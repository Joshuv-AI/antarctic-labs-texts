Abstract
. Following ideas introduced by Beardon-Minda and by Baribeau-Rivard-Wegert in the context of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we use the iterated hyperbolic difference quotients to prove a multipoint Julia lemma. As applications, we give a sharp estimate from below of the angular derivative at a boundary point, generalizing results due to Osserman, Mercer and others; and we prove a generalization to multiple fixed points of an interesting estimate due to Cowen and Pommerenke. These applications show that iterated hyperbolic difference quotients and multipoint Julia lemmas can be useful tools for exploring in a systematic way the influence of higher order derivatives on the boundary behaviour of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disk.



1.
Introduction

The classical Schwarz-Pick lemma
[
26
,
22
,
21
,
6
]
says that every holomorphic self-map of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is a weak contraction for the Poincaré distance
\omega
. More precisely, for every
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
z
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
we have

\omega\bigl(f(z),f(w)\bigr)\leq\omega(z,w)\;,

with equality for some
z_{0}\neq w_{0}
if and only if there is equality everywhere if and only if
f
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
. In particular, if
f\notin\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
then we have

(1)

\omega\bigl(f(z),f(w)\bigr)<\omega(z,w)

for all
z\neq w
.

In the century following the appearance of this result many improvements of (
1
) for non automorphisms have appeared; see, e.g.,
[
10
,
25
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
14
,
5
,
3
]
. Surprisingly, in 2004 Beardon and Minda
[
4
]
found an elegant unified way to recover all these results, and more.

Their idea is based on the
hyperbolic difference quotient

f^{*}\colon\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
associated to a holomorphic self-map
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
, which is defined as follows:

f^{*}(z,w)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)}\bigg/\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}&\mathrm{if}\ z\neq w\;;\\
f^{\prime}(z)\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}&\mathrm{if}\ z=w\;.\end{cases}

It is clear that for every
w\in\mathbb{D}
the map
z\mapsto f^{*}(z,w)
is holomorphic. Beardon and Minda observed that (
1
) is equivalent to saying that, if
f
is not an automorphism, then
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
for every
w\in\mathbb{D}
. But then one can apply the Schwarz-Pick lemma to
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
, obtaining the following 3-point Schwarz-Pick lemma:

Theorem 1.1

(Beardon-Minda, 2004)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
. Then

\omega\bigl(f^{*}(z,v),f^{*}(w,v)\bigr)\leq\omega(z,w)

for all
z
,
v
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore equality holds for some
z_{0}\neq w_{0}
and
v_{0}
if and only if it holds everywhere if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree 2.

Here a
Blaschke product
of
degree

d\geq 1
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
of the form

B(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_{j}}{1-\overline{a_{j}}z}\;,

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{D}
. In particular, the Blaschke products of degree 1 are exactly the automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
.

As mentioned above, Beardon and Minda showed how the apparently innocuous Theorem
1.1
can be used to recover many inequalities improving the original Schwarz-Pick lemma; we refer to their beautiful paper
[
4
]
for details.

A consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that if
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree 2 then
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
is not an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
; therefore its hyperbolic difference quotient again is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
, and hence we can apply the classical Schwarz-Pick lemma to get a 4-point Schwarz-Pick lemma — and then, iterating the procedure, a
n
-point Schwarz-Pick lemma for any
n\geq 2
.

This idea has been explored by Baribeau, Rivard and Wegert
[
2
]
in the context of the Nevanlinna-Pick interpolation problem, and by Cho, Kim and Sugawa
[
8
]
in more generality; see also
[
23
,
24
]
. To state their results, we need some notations. Given
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
z
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
we define the
iterated hyperbolic difference quotient

\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)
by setting
\Delta_{w_{1}}f(z)=f^{*}(z,w_{1})
and
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)=\Delta_{w_{k}}(\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f)(z)
. Then we have a multi-point Schwarz-Pick lemma:

Theorem 1.2

(Baribeau-Rivard-Wegert, 2009)
.

Given
k\geq 1
, take
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Then

\omega\bigl(\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z),\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w))\bigr)\leq\omega(z,w)

for all
z
,
w
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore equality holds for some
z_{0}\neq w_{0}
and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
if and only if it holds for all
z
,
w
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

Another way of expressing the Schwarz-Pick lemma consists in saying that holomorphic self-maps of the unit disk send disks for the Poincaré distance into disks for the Poincaré distance. Julia
[
13
]
in 1920 noticed that by moving the centers of these disks toward the boundary one can get a boundary version of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, nowadays known as Julia lemma:

Theorem 1.3

(Julia, 1920)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that

\beta_{f}(\sigma):=\liminf_{z\to\sigma}\frac{1-|f(z)|}{1-|z|}=\alpha<\infty\;.

Then there exists a unique
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that

(2)

\frac{|\tau-f(z)|^{2}}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Moreover, equality in (
2
) holds at one point if and only if it holds everywhere if and only if

f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
.

The number
\beta_{f}(\sigma)\in(0,+\infty]
is the
boundary dilation coefficient
of
f
at
\sigma
, and it is the absolute value of the
angular derivative

f^{\prime}(\sigma)
, the non-tangential limit of
f^{\prime}
at
\sigma
, which is known to exist thanks to the Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory theorem. It is well-known that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)>0
always; furthermore, if
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
then the point
\tau
appearing in the statement of Julia lemma is the non-tangential limit of
f
at
\sigma
, that we will denote by
f(\sigma)
.

The geometrical meaning of (
2
) is that if
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
then
f
sends horocycles centered at
\sigma
in horocycles centered at
f(\sigma)
, where
a
horocycle

E(\sigma,R)
of
center

\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and
radius

R>0
is given by

E(\sigma,R)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}\biggm|\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}<R\right\}\;.

Geometrically,
E(\sigma,R)
is an Euclidean disk or radius
R/(R+1)
internally tangent at
\partial\mathbb{D}
in
\sigma
.

The aim of this paper is to obtain a multipoint version of Julia lemma along the lines of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The paper
[
4
]
contains a 3-point Julia lemma, but its statement does not involve the hyperbolic difference quotient, and it is in a slightly different spirit. Closer to our aims is
[
18
]
*Proposition 4.1; but its (Euclidean) statement is quite involved and not easy to use (see Remark
3.8
).

Our idea then is to obtain a version of Theorem
1.3
involving the iterated hyperbolic difference quotients. The main difference between the Schwarz-Pick lemma and the Julia lemma is that the latter works only for maps with finite boundary dilation coefficient. So the main result allowing our approach to start is the following (see Proposition
3.4
):

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Then

\beta_{\Delta_{w}f}(\sigma)=\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|\sigma-w|^{2}}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. In particular
\beta_{\Delta_{w_{0}}f}(\sigma)<+\infty
for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\beta_{\Delta_{w}f}(\sigma)<+\infty
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.

So if the boundary dilation coefficient is finite for
f
it remains finite for all the iterated hyperbolic difference quotients of
f
. This allows us to obtain a multi-point Julia lemma (see Theorem
4.2
):

Theorem 1.5
.

Given
k\geq 1
, take
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Then

(3)

\frac{|\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for all
z
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, equality occurs for some
z_{0}
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if it occurs everywhere if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

We now describe two applications of this theorem. We mentioned before that the boundary dilation coefficient is always strictly positive. In some instances it is useful to have a more explicit bound from below, like the classical one

(4)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}\;.

In Section
4
we shall prove a much more precise estimate (see Theorem
4.3
):

Theorem 1.6
.

Given
k\geq 0
let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Take
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
with
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Then

(5)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}

for every
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
, where
\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f=f
when
h=0
. Furthermore we have equality in (
5
) for some
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if we have equality for all
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

In particular we have the following corollary (see Corollary
4.7
):

Corollary 1.7
.

Given
k\geq 0
let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Take
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
with
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Then

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{1-|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|}{1+|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|}\;,

where
\mathbf{O}_{h}=(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{D}^{h}
is the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{h}
, and
\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f=f
when
h=0
. Furthermore we have equality if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)=|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(\sigma)

for all
h=0,\ldots,k
.

These results when
k=0
recover (
4
) and when
k\geq 1
improve previous estimates due to Ünkelbach
[
27
,
28
]
, Komatu
[
15
]
, Frovlova et al.
[
11
]
, Osserman
[
20
]
and
Mercer
[
19
]
.

When
f(0)=0
the estimate (
4
) implies that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq 1
. In 1982, Cowen and Pommerenke
[
9
]
proved that if moreover
f(\sigma)=\sigma
this estimate can be improved to

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq 1+\frac{|1-f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\;.

More precisely, they obtained a sharp estimate valid when
f
has a fixed point inside and
n
fixed points on the boundary:

Theorem 1.8

(Cowen-Pommerenke, 1982)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
be such that
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Assume there exist
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points with
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty
and
f(\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

(6)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-1}\leq\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|^{2}}{|1-f^{\prime}(z_{0})|^{2}}\;.

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+1
.

In Section
3
we shall show (see Proposition
3.11
) how to obtain this result as a consequence of our Theorem
1.5
for
k=1
.
More interestingly, in Section
4
we shall generalize the estimate (
6
) to the case of multiple fixed points (see Theorem
4.12
):

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
. Given
k\geq 1
, assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k\geq 1
and that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}

such that
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})=\ldots=f^{(k-1)}(z_{0})=0
.
Take
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty
and

f(\sigma_{j})=\frac{\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}+z_{0}}{1+\overline{z_{0}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}}\;,

for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\left(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\right)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\leq\frac{1-\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(z_{0})}{k!}(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{k-1}\right|^{2}}{\left|1-\frac{f^{(k)}(z_{0})}{k!}(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{k-1}\right|^{2}}\;,

with equality if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+k
.

The proof in the general case is a bit delicate and requires the full force of our multipoint Julia lemma. However,
the case
z_{0}=0
has a simpler statement, and actually a much easier proof (see Corollary
4.13
):

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
. Given
k\geq 1
, assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
and that

f(0)=\cdots=f^{(k-1)}(0)=0\;.

Take
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty
and
f(\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}^{k}

for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\leq\frac{1-\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\right|^{2}}{\left|1-\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\right|^{2}}\;,

with equality if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+k
.

Summing up, these applications show that iterated hyperbolic difference quotients and multipoint Julia lemmas can be an useful tool for exploring in a systematic way the influence of higher order derivatives on the boundary behaviour of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disk.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect a few known results that shall be useful later on.

2.1.
Blaschke products

Definition 2.1
.

A (finite)
Blaschke product

B\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
continuous up to the boundary
with
B(\partial\mathbb{D})\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}
. Since a Blaschke product
B
cannot vanish in a neighbourhood of
\partial\mathbb{D}
it must have a finite number
d\geq 0
of zeroes in
\mathbb{D}
, counted with respect to their multiplicity. The number
d
is the
degree
of
B
. We shall denote by
\mathcal{B}_{d}
the set of Blaschke products of degree
d\geq 1
, and by
\mathcal{B}_{0}
the set of constant functions of modulus 1.

Lemma 2.2
.

A function
B\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is a Blaschke product of degree
d\geq 0
if and only if there are
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{D}
such that

(7)

B(z)=e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_{j}}{1-\bar{a_{j}}z}\;.

In particular, if
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
then
B\circ\gamma
and
\gamma\circ B
are still Blaschke products of the same degree
d
.

Proof.

Since

\frac{\sigma-a}{1-\overline{a}\sigma}=\overline{\sigma}\frac{\sigma-a}{\overline{\sigma}-\overline{a}}\in\partial\mathbb{D}

for all
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, it is clear that all maps of the form (
7
) are Blaschke products of degree
d
, with zeroes in
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
.

Conversely, assume that
B
is a Blaschke product of degree
d\geq 0
. If
d=0
then
the maximum principle applied to
1/B
implies that
|B|\equiv 1
, and hence
B\equiv e^{i\theta}
for a suitable
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Assume
d\geq 1
, let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{D}
be the zeroes of
B
, listed accordingly to their multiplicities, and put

B_{0}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_{j}}{1-\bar{a_{j}}z}\;.

Then
B/B_{0}
is holomorphic without zeroes in
\mathbb{D}
and
|B/B_{0}|=|B_{0}/B|\equiv 1
on
\partial D
. By the maximum principle we get
|B/B_{0}|
,
|B_{0}/B|\leq 1
,
and thus
B/B_{0}
is a constant of modulus 1, as required.

If
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
then
B\circ\gamma
obviously is a Blaschke product of the same degree. On the other hand, clearly
\gamma\circ B
is still a Blaschke product. Moreover, if
a=\gamma^{-1}(0)
then
(\gamma\circ B)(z)=0
if and only if
B(z)=a
if and only if

e^{i\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(z-a_{j})=a\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1-\bar{a_{j}}z)\;.

This is a polynomial equation of degree exactly
d
; thus
\gamma\circ B
has exactly
d
zeroes, counted with multiplicities, and we are done.
∎

In particular, the Blaschke products of degree 1 are exactly the automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
, that is
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
. If
w\in\mathbb{D}
we shall denote by
\gamma_{w}
the automorphism

\gamma_{w}(z)=\frac{z-w}{1-\bar{w}z}\;.

Later on we shall need the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be distinct points,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{d}
for some
n\geq 1
and
d\geq 0
. If
B\not\equiv 1
define
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}+\frac{1+B(z)}{1-B(z)}\;.

Then
h\in\mathcal{B}_{n+d}
.

Proof.

A quick computation yields

h(z)=\frac{2B(z)+\bigl(1-B(z)\bigr)S(z)}{2+\bigl(1-B(z)\bigr)S(z)}\;,

where

S(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\;;

in particular
h
is a rational function of degree
n+d
because numerator and denominator have no common factors.

When
z\in\mathbb{D}
we have

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}+\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{1+B(z)}{1-B(z)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z|^{2}}+\frac{1-|B(z)|^{2}}{|1-B(z)|^{2}}>0\;;

this yields
h(z)\in\mathbb{D}
, and hence
h(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
.

If
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
is different from
\sigma_{j}
we have

\frac{\sigma_{j}+\sigma}{\sigma_{j}-\sigma}=2i\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits(\sigma\overline{\sigma_{j}})}{|\sigma_{j}-\sigma|^{2}}\in i\mathbb{R}\;;

thus if
\sigma\neq\sigma_{1},\ldots\sigma_{n}
we have
S(\sigma)=ia\in i\mathbb{R}
and hence setting
B(\sigma)=e^{i\phi}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
we have

h(\sigma)=\frac{2e^{i\phi}+(1-e^{i\phi})ia}{2+(1-e^{i\phi})ia}\in\partial\mathbb{D}\;.

To deal with
\sigma=\sigma_{j}
we write

h(z)=\frac{a_{j}(\sigma_{j}+z)\bigl(1-B(z)\bigr)+(\sigma_{j}-z)\bigl(S_{j}(z)+2B(z)\bigr)}{a_{j}(\sigma_{j}+z)\bigl(1-B(z)\bigr)+(\sigma_{j}-z)\bigl(S_{j}(z)+2\bigr)}

where
S_{j}(\sigma_{j})=0
; therefore we get
h(\sigma_{j})=1
, also when
B(\sigma_{j})=1
.

Summing up, we have proved that
h
is a Blaschke product; being a rational function of degree
n+d
we get
h\in\mathcal{B}_{n+d}
, and we are done.
∎

2.2.
The hyperbolic difference quotient

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be a holomorphic self-map of the unit disk. The
hyperbolic derivative

f^{h}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
of
f
is given by

f^{h}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\,f(z)|^{2}}\!\bigg/\!\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}=f^{\prime}(z)\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|\,f(z)|^{2}}\;.

The
hyperbolic difference quotient

f^{*}\colon\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

f^{*}(z,w)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)}\bigg/\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}&\mathrm{if}\ z\neq w\;;\\
f^{h}(z)&\mathrm{if}\ z=w\;.\end{cases}

It is easy to check that for every
w\in\mathbb{D}
the function
z\mapsto f^{*}(z,w)
is holomorphic. Furthermore, the Schwarz-Pick lemma implies that
|f^{*}(z,w)|\leq 1
always,
and that there exists
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
such that
|f^{*}(z_{0},w_{0})|=1
if and only if
|f^{*}|\equiv 1
if and only if
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
. In particular, if
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
is given by

\gamma(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar{a}z}

then it is easy to check that

\gamma^{*}(z,w)=e^{i\theta}\frac{1-\bar{a}w}{1-a\bar{w}}\;.

This can be seen as a particular case of the following result:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
d\geq 1
. Then
f\in\mathcal{B}_{d}
if and only if
f^{*}(\cdot,w)\in\mathcal{B}_{d-1}
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f^{*}(\cdot,w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}_{d-1}
for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

By definition we have

(8)

\gamma_{w}(z)f^{*}(z,w)=\gamma_{f(w)}\bigl(f(z)\bigr)

for all
z
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
.
If
f^{*}(\cdot,w_{0})\in\mathcal{B}_{d-1}
for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
then
B=\gamma_{w_{0}}f^{*}(\cdot,w_{0})
is a Blaschke product of degree
d
, and thus Lemma
2.2
implies that
f=\gamma^{-1}_{f(w_{0})}\circ B
is a Blaschke product of degree
d
.

Conversely, if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
d
, then for any
w\in\mathbb{D}
we have that
\gamma_{f(w)}\circ f
is a Blaschke product of degree
d
vanishing at
w
.
Therefore
\gamma_{w}
is a factor of
\gamma_{f(w)}\circ f
, and (
8
) implies that
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
is a Blaschke product of degree
d-1
.
∎

2.3.
The classical Julia lemma

Definition 2.6
.

The

horocycle

E(\sigma,R)\subset\mathbb{D}
of
center

\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and

radius

R>0
is given by

(9)

E(\sigma,R)=\biggl\{z\in\mathbb{D}\biggm|\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}<R\biggr\}\;.

Geometrically,
E(\sigma,R)
is
the euclidean disk of radius
R/(R+1)
internally
tangent to
\partial\mathbb{D}
in
\sigma
.

Definition 2.7
.

Given
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma
,
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, set

(10)

\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\biggl\{\frac{|\tau-f(z)|^{2}}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\biggm/\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\biggr\}\;.

The
boundary dilation coefficient
of
f
at
\sigma
is given by

(11)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)=\inf_{\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}}\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)\in[0,+\infty]\;.

Remark 2.8
.

By definition

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall R>0\quad\qquad$}\hfil f\bigl(E(\sigma,R)\bigr)\subseteq E\bigl(\tau,\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)R\bigr)\;.

In particular, for every
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
there is at most
one point
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)
is finite. Indeed, if we had
\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau_{j})<+\infty
for two distinct points
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}\in\partial\mathbb{D}

we would get a contradiction choosing
R
so small that

E(\tau_{1},\beta R)\cap E(\tau_{2},\beta R)=\mathord{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle/\hfil$\crcr$\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\textstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\textstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}}\;,

where
\beta=\max\{\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau_{1}),\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau_{2})\}
.

The following well-known result gives us an alternative way to compute the boundary dilation coefficient (for a proof see, e.g.,
[
1
]
*Proposition 1.2.6):

Proposition 2.9
.

Take
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then

\beta_{f}(\sigma)=\liminf_{z\to\sigma}\frac{1-|f(z)|}{1-|z|}\;.

Furthermore (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
*Lemma 1.2.4):

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be holomorphic. Then

(12)

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall z\in\mathbb{D}\quad\qquad$}\hfil\frac{1-|f(z)|}{1-|z|}\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}>0\;.

In particular, for all
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
we have

(13)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}>0\;.

Moreover, equality in (
12
) holds at one point
z_{0}\neq 0
(and hence everywhere)
if and only if
f(z)=e^{i\theta}z
for a suitable
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

We can now state the classical Julia lemma
[
13
]
:

Theorem 2.11

(Julia lemma)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
, and choose
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
so that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Let
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be the unique point of
\partial\mathbb{D}
such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)<+\infty
. Then

(14)

\frac{|\tau-f(z)|^{2}}{1-|f(z)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\;,

that is

(15)

\hbox{}\hfilneg\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\forall R>0\quad\qquad$}\hfil f\bigl(E(\sigma,R)\bigr)\subseteq E(\tau,\beta_{f}(\sigma)R)\;.

Moreover, equality in (
14
) holds at one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if

f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
.

As a consequence we have another way for computing the boundary dilation coefficient:

Corollary 2.12
.

Take
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then

\beta_{f}(\sigma)=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\;.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.9
we have

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\leq\liminf_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}\;;

in particular the first equality is proven when
\beta_{f}(\sigma)=+\infty
. Assume then that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. An easy computation shows that

r\sigma\in\partial E\bigl(\sigma,\frac{1-r}{1+r}\bigr)
; therefore Theorem
2.11
yields
f(r\sigma)\in\overline{E\bigl(\tau,\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-r}{1+r}\bigr)}

for a suitable
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Since
E(\tau,R)
is an Euclidean disk of radius
R/(R+1)
internally tangent to
\partial\mathbb{D}
in
\tau
it follows that

1-|f(r\sigma)|\leq|\tau-f(r\sigma)|\leq 2\frac{\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-r}{1+r}}{1+\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-r}{1+r}}\;.

Therefore

\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}\leq\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{2\beta_{f}(\sigma)}{1+r+\beta_{f}(\sigma)(1-r)}=\beta_{f}(\sigma)

and the first equality is proved.

To prove the second equality, first of all notice that

(16)

\frac{2}{1+r}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}\geq\frac{1+|f(r\sigma)|}{1+r}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}=\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{1+r}\frac{1-|f(r\sigma)|}{1-r}\;.

From this the second equality immediately follows when
\beta_{f}(\sigma)=+\infty
. If
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
then
|f(r\sigma)|\to 1
as
r\to 1^{-}
,
and thus the assertion follows again from (
16
).
∎

Definition 2.13
.

Given
\tau\in\partial\mathbb{D}
and
M>0
, the
Stolz region

K(\tau,M)
of
vertex

\tau
and
amplitude

M
is

(17)

K(\tau,M)=\biggl\{z\in\mathbb{D}\biggm|\frac{|\tau-z|}{1-|z|}<M\biggl\}\;.

Note that
K(\tau,M)=\mathord{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle/\hfil$\crcr$\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\textstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\textstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptscriptstyle/\hfil$\crcr$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrel{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.8pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bigcirc$}}}$\crcr}}}}
if
M\leq 1
, for
|\tau-z|\geq 1-|z|
.

Definition 2.14
.

We say that a function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
has
non-tangential
(or
angular
)
limit
c\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
at
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
if
f(z)\to c
as
z
tends to
\sigma

within
K(\sigma,M)
for any
M>1
. When this happen we shall write

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}f(z)=c

and denote
c
by
f(\sigma)
.

We end this preliminary section recalling the famous Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory theorem
[
29
,
7
]
; for a proof see, e.g.,
[
1
]
*Theorem 1.2.7.

Theorem 2.15

(Julia-Wolff-Carathéodory; 1926)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\tau
,
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then

(18)

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}\frac{\tau-f(z)}{\sigma-z}=\tau\bar{\sigma}\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)\;.

If this non-tangential limit is finite then
\beta_{f}(\sigma)=\beta_{f}(\sigma,\tau)<+\infty
, the function
f
has non-tangential limit
\tau
at
\sigma
and

(19)

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}f^{\prime}(z)=\tau\bar{\sigma}\beta_{f}(\sigma)\;.

3.
2-point Julia lemma

As anticipated in the introduction, our 2-point Julia lemma will be obtained by applying the classical Julia lemma to the function
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
. To do so we need to compute the boundary dilation coefficient of the hyperbolic difference quotient; this is done in the next two results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
f
has non-tangential limit
f(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
at
\sigma
. Then

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}f^{*}(z,w)=\frac{f(\sigma)-f(w)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(\sigma)}\bigg/\frac{\sigma-w}{1-\overline{w}\sigma}=\overline{f(\sigma)}\sigma\,\frac{f(\sigma)-f(w)}{\overline{f(\sigma)}-\overline{f(w)}}\,\frac{\overline{\sigma}-\overline{w}}{\sigma-w}\in\partial\mathbb{D}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from the definition of
f^{*}
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
f
has non-tangential limit
f(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
at
\sigma
. Given
w\in\mathbb{D}
we set

f^{*}(\sigma,w)=\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}f^{*}(z,w)\;;

in particular,
|f^{*}(\sigma,\cdot)|\equiv 1
.

Remark 3.3
.

For the sake of completeness, we remark that

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{w\to\sigma}f^{*}(z,w)=\frac{f(z)-f(\sigma)}{1-\overline{f(\sigma)}f(z)}\bigg/\frac{z-\sigma}{1-\overline{\sigma}z}\equiv f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, (
18
) yields

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{w\to\sigma}f^{*}(\sigma,w)=\overline{f(\sigma)}\sigma\frac{f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}\beta_{f}(\sigma)}{\overline{f(\sigma)}\sigma\beta_{f}(\sigma)}=f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}\;.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Denote by
f(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
the non-tangential limit of
f
at
\sigma
. Then

(20)

\displaystyle\liminf_{z\to\sigma}\frac{1-|f^{*}(z,w)|}{1-|z|}

\displaystyle=\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|\sigma-w|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}\left[\beta_{f}(\sigma)-\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}{1-|f(w)|^{2}}\bigg/\frac{|\sigma-w|^{2}}{1-|w|^{2}}\right]

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, the left-hand side vanishes for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if it vanishes for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

First of all we have

\displaystyle 1-|f^{*}(z,w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\left|\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)}\right|^{2}}{\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|^{2}}=\frac{\left(1-\left|\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)}\right|^{2}\right)-\left(1-\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|^{2}\right)}{\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{(1-|f(z)|^{2})(1-|f(w)|^{2})}{|1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)|^{2}}-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2}}}{\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1-|z|^{2})\frac{\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|1-\overline{f(w)}f(z)|^{2}}\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{2}}}{\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|^{2}}

Recalling Corollary
2.12
and the fact that
f
has non-tangential limit
f(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
at
\sigma
we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{z\to\sigma}\frac{1-|f^{*}(z,w)|}{1-|z|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{1-|f^{*}(r\sigma,w)^{2}|}{1-r^{2}}=\frac{\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|1-\overline{f(w)}f(\sigma)|^{2}}\beta_{f}(\sigma)-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{w}\sigma|^{2}}}{\left|\frac{\sigma-w}{1-\overline{w}\sigma}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|\sigma-w|^{2}}\;,

and (
20
) is proved.

Finally, by Theorem
2.11
the right-hand side in (
20
) vanishes at one point if and only if it vanishes identically if and only if
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
,
and we are done.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Given
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
we put

\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;w)=\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|\sigma-w|^{2}}\in[0,+\infty]

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. In particular,
\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma,w_{0})=+\infty
for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;\cdot)\equiv+\infty
if and only if
\beta_{f}(\sigma)=+\infty
,
and
\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma,w_{0})=0
for some
w_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;\cdot)\equiv 0
if and only if
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
.

As a first hint of how it is possible to use this kind of results we show how to improve (
13
):

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Then

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(w)|^{2}}\geq\frac{|w|-\left|\frac{f(w)-f(0)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(0)}\right|}{|w|+\left|\frac{f(w)-f(0)}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(0)}\right|}+\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|\sigma-w|^{2}}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
and

(21)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{2}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(0)|^{2}}{1-|f(0)|^{2}}\geq\frac{2}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}\;.

Proof.

If we apply (
13
) to
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
we get

\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma,w)\geq\frac{1-|f^{*}(0,w)|}{1+|f^{*}(0,w)|}

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Since

f^{*}(0,w)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(w)-f(0)}{w}\frac{1}{1-\overline{f(w)}f(0)}&\mathrm{if}\ w\neq 0\;,\\
f^{h}(0)&\mathrm{if}\ w=0\;,\end{cases}

we immediately get the first inequality for
w\neq 0
. When
w=0
we get

(22)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|f(0)|^{2}}{|f(\sigma)-f(0)|^{2}}\geq\frac{1-|f^{h}(0)|}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}+1=\frac{2}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}

and we are done.
∎

Notice that when
f\notin\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(D)
we have
|f^{h}(0)|<1
and thus (
21
) is strictly stronger than (
13
).

The inequality (
21
) was already known (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
); however, in the next section we shall substantially improve it (see Theorem
4.3
and its corollaries).

We can now state and prove our 2-point Julia lemma:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Then

(23)

\frac{|f^{*}(\sigma,w)-f^{*}(z,w)|^{2}}{1-|f^{*}(z,w)|^{2}}\leq\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;w)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for all
z
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, equality in (
23
) occurs for some
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
if and only if it occurs everywhere if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree 2.

Proof.

The inequality (
23
) follows from Theorem
2.11
applied to
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
. If we have equality in (
23
)
for some
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
again Theorem
2.11
implies that
f^{*}(\cdot,w_{0})\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
, and then Proposition
2.5
implies that

f\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
. Conversely,
f\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
implies that
f^{*}(\cdot,w)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
, and thus we have equality in (
23
)
for all
z
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

It turns out that (
23
) implies a (not very illuminating) Euclidean statement, originally proved by Mercer
[
18
]
, that in our notations can be expressed as follows: let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Take
w\in\mathbb{D}
and set
\Lambda=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\sigma-z|^{2}}
,
\hat{\beta}=\beta_{f}^{*}(\sigma;w)
,
\phi_{w}(z)=\frac{w-z}{1-\overline{w}z}
and

L=\frac{1-|\overline{f(w)}\phi_{w}(z)|^{2}}{|1-\overline{f(w)}f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)|^{2}}\;.

Then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
we have
|f(z)-c_{w}(z)|<r_{w}(z)
, where

\displaystyle c_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=\overline{f(\sigma)}f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{\bigl(1-\overline{f(w)}f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)\bigr)^{2}}\frac{\hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}L+\Lambda}

\displaystyle+\overline{f(\sigma)}\phi_{f(w)}\bigl(f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)\bigr)\;,

\displaystyle r_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=|\phi_{w}(z)|\frac{1-|f(w)|^{2}}{\bigl|1-\overline{f(w)}f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)\bigr|^{2}}\frac{\hat{\beta}}{\hat{\beta}L+\Lambda}\;.

This can be recovered as follows. Theorem
3.7
says that
f^{*}(z,w)
belongs to the horocycle of center
f^{*}(\sigma,w)
and radius
\beta^{*}/\Lambda
, which is an Euclidean disk of center
\frac{\Lambda}{\beta^{*}+\Lambda}f^{*}(\sigma,w)
and radius
\frac{\beta^{*}}{\beta^{*}+\Lambda}
. Since
\phi_{w}(z)f^{*}(z,w)=\phi_{f(w)}\bigl(f(z)\bigr)
,
it follows that
\phi_{f(w)}\bigl(f(z)\bigr)
belongs to the Euclidean disk
D
of center

\frac{\Lambda}{\beta^{*}+\Lambda}f^{*}(\sigma,w)\phi_{w}(z)
and radius
\frac{\beta^{*}}{\beta^{*}+\Lambda}|\phi_{w}(z)|
. Using the fact that
\phi_{f(w)}^{-1}=\phi_{f(w)}
we get that
f(z)
belongs to the
Euclidean disk
\phi_{f(w)}(D)
; computing center and radius of this latter disk we get the assertion.

Applying Theorem
2.15
to
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
we get the next corollary:

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Then

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}\frac{f^{*}(\sigma,w)-f^{*}(z,w)}{\sigma-z}=f^{*}(\sigma,w)\overline{\sigma}\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;w)

and

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma}\frac{\partial f^{*}}{\partial z}(z,w)=f^{*}(\sigma,w)\overline{\sigma}\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;w)\;.

As mentioned in the introduction, specializing Theorem
3.7
to the case
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
we can recover an estimate due to Cowen and Pommerenke. The main step is contained in the following

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Assume that
f(0)=0
. Then

(24)

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma}-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{f(z)}{z}\right|^{2}}\leq\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\bigr)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

if
z\neq 0
and

(25)

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\;.

In particular, if furthermore
f(\sigma)=\sigma
we get

(26)

\frac{|1-f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\;.

Moreover, equality occurs in (
24
) at some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
or in (
25
) if and only if it always occurs if and only
f
is a Blaschke product of degree 2.

Proof.

If
f(0)=0
then

(27)

f^{*}(z,0)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(z)}{z}&\mathrm{if}\ z\neq 0\;,\\
f^{\prime}(0)&\mathrm{if}\ z=0\;;\end{cases}

moreover,
f^{*}(\sigma,0)=f(\sigma)/\sigma
and
\beta^{*}_{f}(\sigma;0)=\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1
. The assertions then follow from Theorem
3.7
.
∎

When
f(\sigma)=\sigma
(
26
) can be restated as

\frac{1}{\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1}\leq\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{|1-f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{1+f^{\prime}(0)}{1-f^{\prime}(0)}\;.

Recalling that
f^{\prime}(\sigma)=\beta_{f}(\sigma)
when
f(\sigma)=\sigma
, where
f^{\prime}(\sigma)
is the non-tangential limit of
f^{\prime}
at
\sigma
(see Theorem
2.15
), using (
24
) we can now recover a result due to Cowen and Pommerenke
[
9
]
, saying that a similar estimate still holds when there are several fixed points in the boundary:

Proposition 3.11

(Cowen-Pommerenke, 1982)
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
be such that
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Assume there exist
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points with
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty
and
f(\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

(28)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-1}\leq\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|^{2}}{|1-f^{\prime}(z_{0})|^{2}}\;.

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+1
.

Proof.

First of all, let
\phi_{z_{0}}(z)=(z_{0}-z)/(1-\overline{z_{0}}z)
. Then
\tilde{f}=\phi_{z_{0}}\circ f\circ\phi_{z_{0}}
satisfies
\tilde{f}(0)=0
and
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime}(z_{0})
. Moreover
if we put
\tilde{\sigma}_{j}=\phi_{z_{0}}(\sigma_{j})
then we have
\tilde{f}(\tilde{\sigma}_{j})=\tilde{\sigma}_{j}
and
\beta_{\tilde{f}}(\tilde{\sigma}_{j})=\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})
. Therefore in the proof without loss
of generality we can assume
z_{0}=0
.

For
j=1,\ldots,n
set
\beta_{j}=\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})
.
We would like to prove, by induction on
n
, that

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1-\left|f(z)/z\right|^{2}}{\left|1-f(z)/z\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{1+f(z)/z}{1-f(z)/z}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z|^{2}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, with equality at one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
. Clearly, when
z=0
the expression
f(z)/z
is replaced by
f^{\prime}(0)
, and thus the theorem follows taking
z=0
.

For
n=1
(
29
) follows from Corollary
3.10
.
Assume it is true for
n-1
. In particular we have

(30)

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\frac{1+f(z)/z}{1-f(z)/z}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\right]\geq 0\;,

with equality at one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
Therefore we can find
h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
with
h(\mathbb{D})\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
so that

(31)

\frac{1+f(z)/z}{1-f(z)/z}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}=\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}\;.

Notice that
h(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
unless in (
30
) we have equality at one point (and hence everywhere); in that case
h\equiv e^{i\theta}
for a suitable
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
h\equiv e^{i\theta}
, Lemma
2.3
shows that
f(z)=zB(z)
, where
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
. But then
f
, being rational of degree
n
, can have at most
n
fixed points, whereas we are assuming that it has
n+1
fixed points, contradiction. Thus
h
cannot be a constant, and we have the strict inequality in (
30
).

A quick computation shows that

h(z)=\frac{2\frac{f(z)}{z}-\left(1-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)S(z)}{2-\left(1-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)S(z)}\;,

where

S(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\;,

with the usual convention of replacing
f(z)/z
by
f^{\prime}(0)
when
z=0
.

Put
g(z)=zh(z)
. Then
g\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
,
g(0)=0
and
g(\sigma_{n})=\sigma_{n}
, because
h(\sigma_{n})=1
. Furthermore we have

h^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{\left(2-\left(1-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)S(z)\right)^{2}}\left[\frac{4}{z}\left(f^{\prime}(z)-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)+O\left(1-\frac{f(z)}{z}\right)\right]\;,

and thus

\mathop{\hbox{$K$-$\lim$}}\limits_{z\to\sigma_{n}}h^{\prime}(z)=\overline{\sigma_{n}}(\beta_{n}-1)\;.

Since
g^{\prime}(z)=h(z)+zh^{\prime}(z)
we get
g^{\prime}(\sigma_{n})=\beta_{n}
. Since
h
is not a constant we can apply Corollary
3.10
to
g

obtaining

(32)

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\beta_{n}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{\sigma_{n}+z}{\sigma_{n}-z}\right)

which recalling the definition of
h
gives exactly (
29
).

If we have equality in one point in (
29
) we must have equality in one point
in (
32
), and this happens if and only if
g
is a Blaschke product of degree 2, again by Corollary
3.10
. But this occurs if and only if
h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
; putting this in (
31
)
we get that
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
by Lemma
2.3
.

To prove the converse, assume that
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}
with
f(0)=0
. Then
f(z)=zB(z)
, where
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
are the
n
distinct solutions of
B(z)=1
. Let
F\colon\mathbb{C}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be defined by

F(z)=\frac{1+B(z)}{1-B(z)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\;.

Then
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits F|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\equiv 0
; this implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits F(0)=0
, which is exactly

\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{|1-f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}-1}=0\;,

and we are done.
∎

4.
Multipoint Julia lemmas

Our 2-point Julia lemma has been obtained by applying the classical Julia lemma to the hyperbolic difference quotient
f^{*}(\cdot,w)
, which is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
as soon as
f
is not an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
. But if we also assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree 2 then by Proposition
2.5

f^{*}(\cdot,w)
is not
an automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
, and so
its
hyperbolic difference quotient is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
to which we may apply the classical Julia lemma, obtaining a 3-point Julia lemma.

Clearly this procedure can be iterated; to do so let us introduce some notations.

Definition 4.1
.

Given
k\geq 1
and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
the
hyperbolic
k
-th difference quotient

\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f
of
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is defined by induction by setting
\Delta_{w_{1}}f(z)=f^{*}(z,w_{1})
and

\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)=\Delta_{w_{k}}(\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f)(z)

for
k\geq 2
.

Proposition
2.5
ensures that
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
as soon as
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Moreover, if
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
is such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
by applying repeatedly Proposition
3.4
we see that
\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)
is finite. More precisely,
\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)
can be recursively computed by

\displaystyle\beta_{\Delta_{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}}f}

\displaystyle(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{k})|^{2}}{|\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{k})|^{2}}-\frac{1-|w_{k}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{k}|^{2}}\;,

and the non-tangential limit
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)
is inductively given by

\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)=\overline{\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)}\sigma\frac{\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{k})}{\overline{\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)}-\overline{\Delta_{w_{k-1},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{k})}}\frac{\overline{\sigma}-\overline{w_{k}}}{\sigma-w_{k}}\;.

In particular we have a multipoint Julia lemma:

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
k\geq 1
, take
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Then

(33)

\frac{|\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f(z)|^{2}}\leq\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for all
z
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, equality occurs for some
z_{0}
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if it occurs everywhere if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

Proof.

The inequality (
33
) follows from Theorem
2.11
applied to
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f
. If we have equality in (
33
)
for some
z_{0}
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
again Theorem
2.11
implies that
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
, and then Proposition
2.5
implies that

f\in\mathcal{B}_{k+1}
. Conversely,
f\in\mathcal{B}_{k+1}
implies that
\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
for all
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
, and thus we have equality in (
33
)
for all
z
,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
.
∎

The idea is that we can use this multipoint approach to improve known estimates by involving higher order derivatives. We shall show two examples of this: a strengthened version of Corollary
3.6
and a generalization of Proposition
3.11
.

We begin with a reformulation of Theorem
4.2
which gives a far-reaching generalization of Corollary
3.6
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Given
k\geq 0
, let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
be not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
. Take
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
with
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Then

(34)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}

for every
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
, where
\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f=f
when
h=0
. Furthermore we have equality in (
34
) for some
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if we have equality for all
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

Proof.

One way to prove the assertion is to obtain by induction a formula for
\beta_{\Delta_{w_{k},\ldots,w_{1}}f}(\sigma)
applying repeatedly Proposition
3.4
, and then to show that, with this formula, (
34
) is equivalent to (
33
). For the sake of variety we shall describe a different proof, relying on the
classical Julia lemma.

We proceed by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
is

(35)

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{1}|^{2}}\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(w_{1})|^{2}}{1-|f(w_{1})|^{2}}

which is exactly the classical Julia inequality (
14
). In particular, we have equality for some
w_{1}\in\mathbb{D}
(and hence for all
w_{1}\in\mathbb{D}
) if and only if
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
.

Assume that (
34
) holds for
k-1
, and take
w_{1}\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
, by Proposition
2.5

\Delta_{w_{1}}f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k-1
. So by the inductive hypothesis we have

\displaystyle\beta_{\Delta_{w_{1}}f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=1}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{2}}(\Delta_{w_{1}}f)(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{2}}(\Delta_{w_{1}}f)(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{2}}(\Delta_{w_{1}}f)(w_{h+1})|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=1}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}

for all
w_{2},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
, with equality for some (and hence all)
w_{2},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
\Delta_{w_{1}}f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k
, that is,
by Proposition
2.5
, if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

Now Proposition
3.4
yields

\beta_{f}(\sigma)=\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(w_{1})|^{2}}{1-|f(w_{1})|^{2}}\left[\frac{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{1}|^{2}}+\beta_{\Delta_{w_{1}}f}(\sigma)\right]\;;

therefore

\displaystyle\beta_{f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(w_{1})|^{2}}{1-|f(w_{1})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\!\Biggl[\frac{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{1}|^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=1}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}\;,

with equality for some (and hence all)
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}\in\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
, and we are done.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It is easy to see that
the estimate (
34
) becomes better and better as

increases.

has a number of corollaries that it is worthwhile to state.

.

Given

, let

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})

\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}

(38)

\displaystyle\beta_{f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-|w_{j+1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{j+1}|^{2}}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{1-|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|}{1+|\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f(w_{h+1})|}

for any sequence
\{w_{h}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
, where
\Delta_{w_{h},\ldots,w_{1}}f=f
when
h=0
as usual. In particular,

\displaystyle\beta_{f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\prod_{h=0}^{j}\frac{1-|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|}{1+|\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{h}}f(0)|}\;.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.3
, Corollary
4.7
and Remark
4.4
.
∎

A natural question, that we leave open, is whether the first inequality in (
38
) actually is an equality, at least when
f
is an infinite Blaschke product.

To give an idea of the actual content of (
34
), let us reformulate it for small values of
k
and particular values of
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k+1}
.

For
k=0
we get (
35
), that we already noticed to be equivalent to the classical Julia lemma.

For
k=1
we get

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(w_{1})|^{2}}{1-|f(w_{1})|^{2}}\left[\frac{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{1}|^{2}}+\frac{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}{|\sigma-w_{2}|^{2}}\frac{|\Delta_{w_{1}}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{w_{1}}f(w_{2})|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{w_{1}}f(w_{2})|^{2}}\right]\;.

Since
\Delta_{0}f(0)=f^{h}(0)
and

(39)

\Delta_{0}f(\sigma)=\overline{f(\sigma)}\overline{\sigma}\frac{f(\sigma)-f(0)}{\overline{f(\sigma)}-\overline{f(0)}}\;,

putting
w_{1}=w_{2}=0
we obtain

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(0)|^{2}}{1-|f(0)|^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\left|\overline{f(\sigma)}\overline{\sigma}\frac{f(\sigma)-f(0)}{\overline{f(\sigma)}-\overline{f(0)}}-f^{h}(0)\right|^{2}}{1-|f^{h}(0)|^{2}}\right]\;,

which is a slightly more precise version of (
21
). If moreover
f(0)=0
we find again (
25
).

The case
k=2
with
w_{1}=w_{2}=w_{3}=0
yields

(40)

\displaystyle\beta_{f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|f(\sigma)-f(0)|^{2}}{1-|f(0)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\frac{\left|\overline{f(\sigma)}\overline{\sigma}\frac{f(\sigma)-f(0)}{\overline{f(\sigma)}-\overline{f(0)}}-f^{h}(0)\right|^{2}}{1-|f^{h}(0)|^{2}}\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{0,0}f(\sigma)-(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|^{2}}{1-|(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|^{2}}\right)\right]\;,

where we have used the equality
\Delta_{0,0}f(0)=(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)
.
To compute
(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)
first all we notice that

\displaystyle(\Delta_{w_{0}}f)^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)(z-w_{0})-\bigl(f(z)-f(w_{0})\bigr)}{(z-w_{0})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1-\overline{w_{0}}z}{1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(z)}

\displaystyle+\frac{f(z)-f(w_{0})}{z-w_{0}}\cdot\frac{-\overline{w_{0}}\bigl(1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(z)\bigr)+(1-\overline{w_{0}}z)\overline{f(w_{0})}f^{\prime}(z)}{\bigl(1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(z)\bigr)^{2}}\;,

and so

\displaystyle(\Delta_{w_{0}}f)^{h}(0)=\frac{1}{1-|\Delta_{w_{0}}f(0)|^{2}}\biggl[

\displaystyle\frac{f(w_{0})-f(0)-f^{\prime}(0)w_{0}}{w_{0}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(0)}

\displaystyle+\frac{f(w_{0})-f(0)}{w_{0}}\frac{\overline{f(w_{0})}f^{\prime}(0)-\overline{w_{0}}\bigl(1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(0)\bigr)}{\bigl(1-\overline{f(w_{0})}f(0)\bigr)^{2}}\biggr].

In particular putting
w_{0}=0
we get

(41)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-|f^{h}(0)|^{2}}\biggl[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\bigl(1-|f(0)|^{2}\bigr)}+\frac{\overline{f(0)}f^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{\bigl(1-|f(0)|^{2}\bigr)^{2}}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-|f^{h}(0)|^{2}}\biggl[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\bigl(1-|f(0)|^{2}\bigr)}+\overline{f(0)}f^{h}(0)^{2}\biggr]\;.

Applying (
39
) to
\Delta_{0}f
we also get

\Delta_{0,0}f(\sigma)=f(\sigma)\frac{\overline{f(\sigma)}-\overline{f(0)}}{f(\sigma)-f(0)}\frac{\Delta_{0}f(\sigma)-f^{h}(0)}{\overline{\Delta_{0}f(\sigma)}-\overline{f^{h}(0)}}\;,

and we have all the terms appearing in (
40
). In particular,

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}\left[1+\frac{1-|f^{h}(0)|}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}\frac{2}{1+|(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|}\right]\;,

and thus if
f(0)=0
we obtain

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq 1+\frac{2(1-|f^{\prime}(0)|)}{1+|f^{\prime}(0)|+\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{2(1-|f^{\prime}(0)|)}}

that improves (
25
).
If moreover
f^{\prime}(0)=0
we also get

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq 1+\frac{2}{1+\frac{1}{2}|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}\geq 2

because
\frac{1}{2}|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|=|\Delta_{0,0}f(0)|\leq 1
.

As a final example, we record that a similar argument with
k=3
and
w_{1}=w_{2}=w_{3}=w_{4}=0
yields

\beta_{f}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1-|f(0)|}{1+|f(0)|}\biggl[\frac{1-|f^{h}(0)|}{1+|f^{h}(0)|}\left(\frac{1-|(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|}{1+|(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|}\frac{2}{1+|(\Delta_{0,0}f)^{h}(0)|}+1\right)+1\biggr]\;,

where

(\Delta_{0,0}f)^{h}(0)=\frac{1}{1-|(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)|^{2}}\biggl[\frac{(\Delta_{0}f)^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\bigl(1-|f^{h}(0)|^{2}\bigr)}+\overline{f^{h}(0)}(\Delta_{0}f)^{h}(0)^{2}\biggr]\;,

with

(\Delta_{0}f)^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{1-|f(0)|^{2}}\biggl[\frac{1}{3}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)+2\overline{f(0)}f^{h}(0)f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\overline{f(0)}^{2}f^{h}(0)^{2}f^{\prime}(0)\biggr]\;.

We now proceed toward the promised generalization of Proposition
3.11
.
Let us start with the following reformulation of the case
k=2
of Theorem
4.2
valid when
f(0)=0
:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
, not a Blaschke product of degree at most 2, and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
.
Assume that
f(0)=0
. Then

(42)

\frac{\left|\frac{\overline{\sigma}\bigl(f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\bigr)}{1-\overline{f^{\prime}(0)}f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}}-\frac{\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}-f^{\prime}(0)\right)}{1-\overline{f^{\prime}(0)}\frac{f(z)}{z}}\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{f(z)}{z}-f^{\prime}(0)\right)}{1-\overline{f^{\prime}(0)}\frac{f(z)}{z}}\right|^{2}}\leq\left[\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\bigr)-1\right]\!\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for
z\neq 0
, and

(43)

\frac{\left|\frac{\overline{\sigma}\bigl(f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\bigr)}{1-\overline{f^{\prime}(0)}f(\sigma)\overline{\sigma}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\bigl(1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\bigr)}\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\bigl(1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\bigr)}\right|^{2}}\leq\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\bigr)-1\;.

Moreover, equality occurs in (
42
) at some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
or in (
43
) if and only if it always occurs if and only
f
is a Blaschke product of degree 3.

Proof.

We would like to apply Theorem
4.2
with
k=2
and
w_{1}=w_{2}=0
.

First of all

\beta_{\Delta_{0,0}f}(\sigma)=\beta_{\Delta_{0}f}(\sigma)\frac{1-|\Delta_{0}f(0)|^{2}}{|\Delta_{0}f(\sigma)-\Delta_{0}f(0)|^{2}}-1=\frac{1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma}-f^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\bigr)-1\;,

where we used
f(0)=0
and
\Delta_{0}f(0)=f^{h}(0)=f^{\prime}(0)
. Next recalling (
27
) we get

\Delta_{0,0}f(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{\frac{f(z)}{z}-f^{\prime}(0)}{z}\frac{1}{1-\overline{f^{\prime}(0)}\frac{f(z)}{z}}&\mathrm{if}\ z\neq 0\;,\\
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2(1-|f^{\prime}(0)|^{2})}&\mathrm{if}\ z=0\end{cases}

where we used (
41
). The assertion then follows from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Notice that (
26
) is equivalent to saying that the right-hand side of (

) is non-negative; so (
43
) is an improvement of (
26
).

If
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
(

) and (
43
) simplify becoming

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{2}}\leq\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-2\bigr)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for

and

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)\right|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)-2\;.

These formulas suggest the following

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
be such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma)<+\infty
. Given
k\geq 1
, assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k\geq 1
,
and that
f(0)=\cdots=f^{(k-1)}(0)=0
. Then

(44)

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma^{k}}-\frac{f(z)}{z^{k}}\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{f(z)}{z^{k}}\right|^{2}}\leq\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-k\bigr)\frac{|\sigma-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for
z\neq 0
and

(45)

\frac{\left|\frac{f(\sigma)}{\sigma^{k}}-\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(0)\right|^{2}}{1-\left|\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(0)\right|^{2}}\leq\beta_{f}(\sigma)-k\;.

Moreover, equality occurs in (
44
) at some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
or in (
45
) if and only if it always occurs if and only
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

Proof.

By induction it is easy to prove that

(46)

\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{k}}f(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(z)}{z^{k}}&\mathrm{if}\ z\neq 0\;,\\
\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(0)&\mathrm{if}\ z=0\;,\end{cases}

and that
\beta_{\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{k}}f}(\sigma)=\beta_{f}(\sigma)-k
. The assertion then follows from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

In particular, if
f(\sigma)=\sigma^{k}
the left-hand sides of (
44
) and (
45
) become independent of
\sigma
. This suggests that we might obtain
a generalization of Proposition
3.11
with multiple fixed points. It turns out that this is easy when the multiple fixed point is the origin (see Corollary
4.13
below), but the statement
and the proof of the general result when the multiple fixed point is not the origin are considerably harder:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
. Given
k\geq 1
, assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
and that there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}

such that
f(z_{0})=z_{0}
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})=\ldots=f^{(k-1)}(z_{0})=0
.
Take
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points such that
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty
and

(47)

f(\sigma_{j})=\frac{\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}+z_{0}}{1+\overline{z_{0}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}}\;,

for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

(48)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\left(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\right)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\leq\frac{1-\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(z_{0})}{k!}(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{k-1}\right|^{2}}{\left|1-\frac{f^{(k)}(z_{0})}{k!}(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{k-1}\right|^{2}}\;,

with equality if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+k
.

Proof.

The fact that
z_{0}
is multiple fixed point of
f
of order
k
is equivalent to saying that we can write

f(z)=z_{0}+\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(z-z_{0})^{k}+O\bigl((z-z_{0})^{k+1}\bigr)\;.

We claim that then

(49)

\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f(z)=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{h-1}(z-z_{0})^{k-h}+O\bigl((z-z_{0})^{k-h+1}\bigr)

for all
h=1,\ldots,k
, where
(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{0})\in\mathbb{D}^{h}
. We proceed by induction. For
h=1
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{z_{0}}f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(z)-z_{0}}{z-z_{0}}\frac{1-\overline{z_{0}}z}{1-\overline{z_{0}}f(z)}=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(z-z_{0})^{k-1}\bigl[1+O(z-z_{0})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(z-z_{0})^{k-1}+O\bigl((z-z_{0})^{k}\bigr)\;,

as claimed. Assume that (
49
) holds for
1\leq h-1\leq k-1
. Then
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f(z_{0})=0
yields

\displaystyle\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f(z)}{z-z_{0}}(1-\overline{z_{0}}z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{h-2}(z-z_{0})^{k-h}\bigl[1-|z_{0}|^{2}+O(z-z_{0})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{h-1}(z-z_{0})^{k-h}+O\bigl((z-z_{0})^{k-h+1}\bigr)\;,

as wanted.

Now we claim that

(50)

\beta_{\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f}(\sigma_{j})=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\left[\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\bigl(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\bigr)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-h\right]

for all
h=1,\ldots,k
and
j=1,\ldots,n
. We again proceed by induction on
h
. For
h=1
Proposition
3.4
yields

\displaystyle\beta_{\Delta_{z_{0}}f}(\sigma_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\bigl(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1\bigr)+\left(\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}-\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\left(\beta_{f}(\sigma)-1+\frac{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}-|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\left[\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\bigl(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\bigr)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-1\right]\;,

as claimed. Assume that (
50
) holds for
1\leq h-1\leq k-1
. Using again the fact that
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f(z_{0})=0
we get

\displaystyle\beta_{\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f}(\sigma_{j})

\displaystyle=\beta_{\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f}(\sigma_{j})-\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\left[\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\bigl(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\bigr)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-h\right]\;,

and we are done.

We need one more preliminary computation. We claim that

(51)

\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f(\sigma_{j})=\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}\sigma_{j}^{h}\left(\frac{\overline{\sigma_{j}}-\overline{z_{0}}}{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}\right)^{h}\frac{f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}}{\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}-\overline{z_{0}}}

for
j=1,\ldots,n
and
h=1,\ldots,k
. As always, we argue by induction on
h
. For
h=1
we have

\Delta_{z_{0}}f(\sigma_{j})=\frac{f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}f(\sigma_{j})}\frac{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}=\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}\sigma_{j}\frac{\overline{\sigma_{j}}-\overline{z_{0}}}{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}\frac{f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}}{\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}-\overline{z_{0}}}

as claimed. Assume that (
51
) holds for
1\leq h-1\leq k-1
. Recalling that
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f(z_{0})=0
we obtain

\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h}}f(\sigma_{j})=\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{h-1}}f(\sigma_{j})\frac{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}=\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}\sigma_{j}^{h}\left(\frac{\overline{\sigma_{j}}-\overline{z_{0}}}{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}\right)^{h}\frac{f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}}{\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}-\overline{z_{0}}}\;,

and (
51
) is proved. In particular, we have
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(\sigma_{j})=1
if and only if

\frac{1-\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}z_{0}}{\overline{f(\sigma_{j})}-\overline{z_{0}}}=\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}

if and only if

f(\sigma_{j})=\frac{\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}+z_{0}}{1+\overline{z_{0}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}\right)^{k}}\;.

In other words, condition (
47
) is just another way of writing
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(\sigma_{j})=1
.

We can now apply Theorem
4.2
. Recalling the assumption
\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(\sigma_{j})=1
we get

(52)

\frac{|1-\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(z)|^{2}}{1-|\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}\left[\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\bigl(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\bigr)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k\right]\frac{|\sigma_{j}-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
, where

\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(z)=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(z_{0})(1-|z_{0}|^{2})^{k-1}+O(z-z_{0})

by (
49
). Furthermore, equality in (
52
) holds in one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
k+1
.

We now claim that

(53)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\biggl(

\displaystyle\frac{1+\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(z)}{1-\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f(z)}\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\left(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\right)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\right)\;,

with equality in one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+k
.

We argue by induction on
n
. For
n=1
(
53
) is exactly equivalent to (
52
).
Assume that (
53
) holds for
n-1
. In particular we have

(54)

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left[\frac{1+g(z)}{1-g(z)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}\right]\geq 0\;,

with equality in one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+k-1}
, where
g=\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f
and

a_{j}=\frac{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\left(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\right)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}=\frac{1}{\beta_{\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f}(\sigma_{j})}>0\;.

Therefore we can find
h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
with
h(\mathbb{D})\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}
so that

\frac{1+g(z)}{1-g(z)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}+z}{\sigma_{j}-z}=\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}\;.

Notice that either
h(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
or
h\equiv e^{i\theta}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, and the latter case occurs if and only if we have equality in (
54
).

If
h\equiv e^{i\theta}
Lemma
2.3
implies that
g\in\mathcal{B}_{n-1}
. So
g
is a rational function of degree
n-1
; but we are assuming that the equation
g(z)=1
has at least
n
distinct solutions, contradiction.

So
h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
. Since
g(\sigma_{n})=1
,
\beta_{g}(\sigma_{n})=\frac{1}{a_{n}}
and
g^{\prime}(\sigma_{n})=\overline{\sigma_{n}}/a_{n}
, where the latter equality follows from (
19
), a quick computation yields
h(\sigma_{n})=1
and
h^{\prime}(\sigma_{n})=\overline{\sigma_{n}}/a_{n}
.

Put
\tilde{h}(z)=zh(z)
. Then we have
\tilde{h}(0)=0
,
\tilde{h}(\sigma_{n})=\sigma_{n}
and
\beta_{\tilde{h}}(\sigma_{n})=\frac{1}{a_{n}}+1
. So we can apply Corollary
3.10
to
\tilde{h}
obtaining

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{1+h(z)}{1-h(z)}\right)\geq a_{n}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\left(\frac{\sigma_{n}+z}{\sigma_{n}-z}\right)\;,

with equality in one point (and hence everywhere) if and only if
h\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
. Recalling (
54
) and Lemma
2.3
we see that we have proven
(
53
), with equality in one point (and hence everywhere) implying that
g
is a Blaschke product of degree
n
, and thus that
f
is a
Blaschke product of degree
n+k
, by Proposition
2.5
.

In particular, (
48
) follows taking
z=z_{0}
in (
53
), and equality there implies that
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+k}
.

To prove the converse, assume that
f\in\mathcal{B}_{n+k}
, so that
g=\Delta_{(\mathbf{z_{0}})_{k}}f\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
are the
n
distinct solutions of
g(z)=1
. Let
F\colon\mathbb{C}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be defined by

F(z)=\frac{1+g\circ\phi_{z_{0}}(z)}{1-g\circ\phi_{z_{0}}(z)}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}+\phi_{z_{0}}(z)}{\sigma_{j}-\phi_{z_{0}}(z)}\;,

where
\phi_{z_{0}}(z)=(z_{0}-z)/(1-\overline{z_{0}}z)
; notice that
g\circ\phi_{z_{0}}
is still a Blaschke product thanks to Lemma
2.2
.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits F|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\equiv 0
; this implies
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits F(0)=0
, which gives exactly

\frac{1-|g(z_{0})|^{2}}{|1-g(z_{0})|^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{1-|z_{0}|^{2}}{|\sigma_{j}-z_{0}|^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(1+2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\frac{\left(f(\sigma_{j})-\sigma_{j}\right)\overline{z_{0}}}{|f(\sigma_{j})-z_{0}|^{2}}\right)\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\;,

and we are done.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hol}}\nolimits(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
. Given
k\geq 1
, assume that
f
is not a Blaschke product of degree at most
k
and that
f(0)=\cdots=f^{(k-1)}(0)=0
.
Take
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
distinct points such that
f(\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}^{k}
and
\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})<+\infty

for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-k}\leq\frac{1-\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\right|^{2}}{\left|1-\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}\right|^{2}}\;,

with equality if and only if
f
is a Blaschke product of degree
n+k
.

Proof.

It immediately follows from Theorem
4.12
applied with
z_{0}=0
.

Alternatively, we can apply directly Proposition
3.11
to
g=\Delta_{\mathbf{O}_{k-1}}f
. Indeed, (
46
) shows that
g(z)=f(z)/z^{k-1}
for
z\neq 0

and
g(0)=0
; in particular,
g^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(0)
. Moreover,
g(\sigma_{j})=\sigma_{j}
and
g^{\prime}(\sigma_{j})=\beta_{f}(\sigma_{j})-(k-1)
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
; hence the assertion follows immediately from (
28
).
∎

Notice that when
k=1
the condition (
47
) becomes

f(\sigma_{j})=\frac{\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}+z_{0}}{1+\overline{z_{0}}\frac{\sigma_{j}-z_{0}}{1-\overline{z_{0}}\sigma_{j}}}=\sigma_{j}\;.

So (
48
) reduces to (
28
), and thus Theorem
4.12
for
k=1
recovers exactly Proposition
3.11
.

Remark 4.14
.

We have seen that Proposition
3.11
for a generic fixed point
z_{0}
followed immediately from the case
z_{0}=0
, just replacing the map
f
by the composition

\phi_{z_{0}}\circ f\circ\phi_{z_{0}}
. Such an approach however does not allow to easily deduce Theorem
4.12
from Corollary
4.13
because the boundary dilation coefficient depends in a complicated way on the higher order derivatives, and so we need the iterated hyperbolic difference quotients to keep everything under control.

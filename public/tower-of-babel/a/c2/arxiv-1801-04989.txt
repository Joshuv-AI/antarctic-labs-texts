m m -cluster tilted algebras of Euclidean type
Elsa Fernández, Ana Garcia Elsener and Sonia Trepode

Abstract
. We consider m m -cluster tilted algebras arising from quivers of Euclidean type and we give necessary and sufficient conditions for those algebras to be representation finite. For the case 𝔸 ~ \tilde{\mathbb{A}} , using the geometric realization, we get a description of representation finite type in terms of ( m + 2 ) (m+2) -angulations. We establish which m m -cluster tilted algebras arise at the same time from quivers of type 𝔸 ~ \tilde{\mathbb{A}} and 𝔸 \mathbb{A} . Finally, we characterize representation infinite m m -cluster tilted algebras arising from a quiver of type 𝔸 ~ \tilde{\mathbb{A}} , as m m -relations extensions of some iterated tilted algebra of type 𝔸 ~ \tilde{\mathbb{A}} .

The representation infinite case - Realization as
m
-relation extensions

In this section we are going to characterize the
m
-cluster tilted algebras of infinite representation type arising
from an
m
-cluster category of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
as being iterated tilted algebras or
m
-relation extensions of iterated tilted algebras of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
.

We start recalling some results. We recall from
[
Gub
]
that if a connected algebra is an
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
then it has a description in terms of quiver with relations. Combining
this with
[
BuR
]
, we get:

Remark 5.10
.

A connected algebra
A=KQ/I
is an
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from
Q
of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
of infinite representation type if and only if
(Q,I)
is a gentle bound quiver satisfying the following conditions:

a)

It contains a unique non-saturated root cycle
\mathfrak{C}
without zero relations.

b)

If the quiver contains more cycles, then all of them are saturated cycles.

c)

Outside of a saturated cycle it can contain at most
m-1
consecutive relations.

Recall that the trivial extension of
B
by
M

is the algebra
B\begin{picture}\qbezier{}{2}{0.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{6.5}\qbezier{}{2}{6.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{0.5}\put(2.0,0.5){\line{0}{1}{6.0}}\end{picture}M
with underlying vector space
B\oplus M
, and multiplication given by
(a,m)(a^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(aa^{\prime},am^{\prime}+ma^{\prime})
, for any
a,\;a^{\prime}\in B
and
m,\;m^{\prime}\in M
. For further
properties of trivial extensions we refer the reader to

[
FGR
]
.

Let
B
be an algebra of global dimension at most

m+1
and
DB={\rm Hom}_{k}(B,k)
. The trivial extension

\mathcal{R}_{m}(B)=B\begin{picture}\qbezier{}{2}{0.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{6.5}\qbezier{}{2}{6.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{0.5}\put(2.0,0.5){\line{0}{1}{6.0}}\end{picture}\Ext^{m+1}_{B}(DB,B)
is called
the
m
-relation extension of
B
.

This concept is the
m
-ified

analogue of the notion of relation extension introduced in

[
ABS
]
. In this work the authors proved that an algebra
{C}

is a cluster tilted algebra if and only if it is the relation
extension of some tilted algebra
B
.

We are now in a position to state our result.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
A
be a connected algebra of infinite representation type. The following are equivalent.

(a)

A
is an
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from a quiver of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
.

(b)

Is one of the following algebras

(i)

A
is an iterated tilted algebra of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
, or

(ii)

A=\mathcal{R}_{m}(B)=B\begin{picture}\qbezier{}{2}{0.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{6.5}\qbezier{}{2}{6.5}{5}{3.5}{8}{0.5}\put(2.0,0.5){\line{0}{1}{6.0}}\end{picture}\Ext^{m+1}_{B}(DB,B)
, is the
m
-relation extension of the algebra
B
, where
B=\End_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(H)}(T)
is an iterated tilted algebra of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
of global dimension
m+1
, with
T
a tilting complex in the fundamental domain
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

b)
implies
a)
. It follows from
[
FPT
, Theorem 2.5]
.

a)
implies
b)
.
Let
A
be a representation infinite
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from a quiver of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
. Then, by Remark
5.10
,
A
has a unique root cycle
\mathfrak{C}
without zero relations an the other cycles are all saturated.

If
A
has non saturated cycles, then
A
has a root cycle and branches satisfiyng the same conditions given in
[
AS
]
, by Remark
5.10
. Then
A
is an iterated tilted algebra of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
by
[
AS
]
.

Let
A
be a representation infinite
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from a quiver of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}
. Then, by Remark
5.10
,
A
has a unique root cycle
\mathfrak{C}
without zero relations an the other cycles are all saturated. Then, if we kill the root cycle we get a product of
m
-cluster tilted algebras arising from
m
-cluster categories of type
\mathbb{A}
, see
5.10
and
[
Mu
]
. More precisely,
B=A/<\mathfrak{C}>=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{n}
where each
B_{i}
an
m
-cluster tilted algebra arising from from a quiver of type
\mathbb{A}
. Now, we cut exactly one arrow of each saturated cycle of
B_{i}
. Let
\alpha_{i_{1}},\cdots,\alpha_{i_{n}}
be the deleted arrows in each saturated cycle of
B_{i}
.

More precisely we make an admissible cut in the sense of
[
BFPPT
, Definition 4.6]
. In this way, we obtain
C_{i}
, an iterated tilted algebra of type
\mathbb{A}
of global dimension at most
m+1
. In fact, by
5.10
each
C_{i}
is a gentle algebra which is a tree with at most
m-1
consecutive relations, then by
[
AH
]
we get that
C_{i}
is an iterated tilted algebra of type
\mathbb{A}
. Using the description given by
[
Mu
]
and
[
FPT
, Remark 4.3]
, we have that
B_{i}=\mathcal{R}_{m}(C_{i})
.

Finally, we make the admissible cut,
A/<\alpha_{i_{j}}>=B
. Using the description from
[
Gub
, Definition 7.5]
of the quiver with relations of the algebra
A
and how the algebras
B_{i}
are glued with the root cycle, in internal or external way in the sense of
[
AS
]
. We get that
B
is a representation infinite iterated tilted algebra of type
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}

by
[
AS
, Theorem A]
, since the algebras
C_{i}
are iterated tilted algebras of type
\mathbb{A}
(branches in the sense of Assem and Skowronski). Observe that global dimension of
B
is
m+1
. Considering
\mathcal{R}_{m}(B)
we recover
A
since in each branch
C_{i}
we recover
B_{i}
, and the relations outside saturated branches are at most
m-1
. Then
A=\mathcal{R}_{m}(B)
.∎

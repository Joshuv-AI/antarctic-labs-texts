Modulational instability of ion-acoustic waves and associated envelope solitons in a multi-component plasma Journal: “Physics of Plasmas"
S. Banik ∗,1,2 , N.M. Heera ∗∗,1 , T. Yeashna ∗∗∗,1 , M.R. Hassan †,1 , R.K. Shikha ‡,1 , N.A. Chowdhury §,3 , A. Mannan ‡‡,1,4 , and A.A. Mamun §§,1

Abstract
A generalized plasma model having warm ions, iso-thermal electrons, super-thermal electrons and positrons is considered to theoretically investigate the modulational instability (MI) of ion-acoustic waves (IAWs). A standard nonlinear Schrödinger equation is derived by applying reductive perturbation method to study the MI of IAWs. It is observed that the MI criteria of the IAWs are significantly modified by various plasma parameters. The present results should be useful in understanding the conditions for MI of IAWs which are relevant to both space and laboratory plasma system.

Governing Equations

We consider a four component unmagnetized plasma model consisting of
warm ions (with charge

\kappa

The overall charge neutrality condition for this plasma model can be written as

Z_{+}n_{+0}+n_{p0}=n_{e10}+n_{e20}

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\frac{\partial n_{+}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(n_{+}u_{+})=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\frac{\partial u_{+}}{\partial t}+u_{+}\frac{\partial u_{+}}{\partial x}+\alpha n_{+}\frac{\partial n_{+}}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x},

(2)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}+n_{+}=\mu_{1}n_{e1}+\mu_{2}n_{e2}-(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1)n_{P},\

(3)

ion can be written as
P_{+}=P_{+0}(N_{+}/n_{+0})^{\gamma}
with

\mu_{1}=n_{e10}/Z_{+}n_{+0}
,
\mu_{2}=n_{e20}/Z_{+}n_{+0}

The expression for number density of super-thermal electron following the

\kappa
-distribution
[
3
]

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{e1}=\left[1-\frac{\phi}{(\kappa-3/2)}\right]^{(-\kappa+1/2)}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=1+F_{1}\phi+F_{2}\phi^{2}+F_{3}\phi^{3}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(4)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{1}=(2\kappa-1)/(2\kappa-3),

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{2}=[(2\kappa-1)(2\kappa+1)]/2(2\kappa-3)^{2},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{3}=[(2\kappa-1)(2\kappa+1)(2\kappa+3)]/6(2\kappa-3)^{3}.\

The expression for number density of super-thermal positron following the

\kappa
-distribution
[
3

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{p}=\Big[1+\frac{\delta\phi}{(\kappa-3/2)}\Big]^{(-\kappa+1/2)}

(5)

where
F_{4}=F_{1}\delta
,
F_{5}=F_{2}\delta^{2}

, and

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{e2}=\exp(\lambda\phi)=1+\lambda\phi+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{6}\phi^{3}+\cdot\cdot\cdot,

(6)

where

4

3

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{7}=\mu_{1}F_{1}+\mu_{2}\lambda+(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1)F_{4},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{8}=\mu_{1}F_{2}+\mu_{2}\lambda^{2}/2+(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1)F_{5},

3
Derivation of the NLSE

We can employ the RPM to derive the NLSE and hence to study the MI of IAWs.
So, the stretched co-ordinates can be written as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\xi=\epsilon(x-v_{g}t),

(8)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\tau=\epsilon^{2}t,

(9)

where
v_{g}
and
\epsilon
are denoted as the group speed and smallness parameter, respectively.
So, the dependent variables can be written as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\Pi(x,t)=\Pi_{0}+\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}\epsilon^{(m)}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\Pi_{l}^{(m)}(\xi,\tau)~\mbox{exp}[il(kx-\omega t)],

(10)

where
\Pi_{l}^{(m)}
=
[n_{+l}^{(m)}
,
u_{+l}^{(m)}
,
\phi_{l}^{m}]
,
\Pi_{0}
=
[1,0,0]^{T}
,
and
\omega
is the angular frequency. The carrier wave number is represented by
k
.
The derivative operators can be written as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\epsilon v_{g}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\epsilon^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau},

(11)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\epsilon\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}.

(12)

Now, by substituting Eqs. (
8
)-(
12
), into Eqs. (
1
), (
2
),
and (
7
), and selecting the terms containing
\epsilon
, the first order (
m=1

and
l=1
) reduced equations can be written as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptiku_{+1}^{(1)}-i\omega n_{+1}^{(1)}=0,

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptik\phi_{1}^{(1)}+ik\alpha n_{+1}^{(1)}-i\omega u_{+1}^{(1)}=0,

(14)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{+1}^{(1)}-k^{2}\phi_{1}^{(1)}-F_{7}\phi_{1}^{(1)}=0,

(15)

these equations provide the dispersion relation for IAWs

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\omega^{2}=\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+F_{7}}+k^{2}\alpha,

(16)

For second order harmonics, equations can be found from the next order of
\epsilon
(with
m=2
and
l=1
) as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{+1}^{(2)}=\frac{k^{2}}{(\omega^{2}-\alpha k^{2})}\phi_{1}^{(2)}+\frac{2ik\omega(v_{g}k-\omega)}{(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi_{1}^{(1)}}{\partial\xi},

(17)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptu_{+1}^{(2)}=\frac{k\omega}{(\omega^{2}-\alpha k^{2})}\phi_{1}^{(2)}+i\frac{(v_{g}k-\omega)(\omega^{2}+\alpha k^{2})}{(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi_{1}^{(1)}}{\partial\xi},

(18)

with the compatibility condition, we have obtained the group speed of IAWs as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptv_{g}=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}=\frac{\omega^{2}-(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}}{k\omega},

(19)

when
m=2
with
l=2

\epsilon
in terms of
|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2}
as

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{+2}^{(2)}=F_{10}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptu_{+2}^{(2)}=F_{11}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(21)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\phi_{2}^{(2)}=F_{12}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(22)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{10}=\frac{k^{2}\{\alpha k^{4}+3\omega^{2}k^{2}+2F_{12}(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}\}}{2(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{3}},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{11}=\frac{F_{10}(\omega^{5}-2\omega^{3}k^{2}\alpha+\omega k^{4}\alpha^{2})-\omega k^{4}}{k(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{12}=\frac{k^{4}(3\omega^{2}+\alpha k^{2})-2F_{8}(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{3}}{2(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}[4\omega^{2}k^{2}-4\alpha k^{4}+F_{7}\omega^{2}-\alpha F_{7}k^{2}-k^{2}]}.

Now, we consider the third-order expression for
m=3
with
l=0
and
m=2
with
l=0
that leads to zeroth harmonic modes. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptn_{+0}^{(2)}=F_{13}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(23)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptu_{+0}^{(2)}=F_{14}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(24)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866pt\phi_{0}^{(2)}=F_{15}|\phi_{1}^{(1)}|^{2},

(25)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{13}=\frac{k^{2}(2\omega v_{g}k+\alpha k^{2}+\omega^{2})+F_{15}(\omega^{4}-2\omega^{2}k^{2}\alpha+k^{4}\alpha^{2})}{(v_{g}^{2}-\alpha)(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{14}=\frac{F_{13}(v_{g}\omega^{4}-2\omega^{2}k^{2}v_{g}\alpha+v_{g}k^{4}\alpha^{2})-2\omega k^{3}}{(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptF_{15}=\frac{k^{2}(2\omega v_{g}k+\alpha k^{2}+\omega^{2})-2F_{8}(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}(v_{g}^{2}-\alpha)}{(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)^{2}[F_{7}(v_{g}^{2}-\alpha)-1]}.

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98866ptP=\frac{(\omega^{2}-k^{2}\alpha)(4\alpha k^{2}\omega-3v_{g}k\omega^{2}-v_{g}k^{3}\alpha)}{2\omega^{2}k^{2}},

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt-k^{2}\{(\alpha k^{2}+\omega^{2})(F_{10}+F_{13})-2\omega k(F_{11}+F_{14})\}].

(28)

4
Modulational instability

The stable and unstable parametric regimes of IAWs are
organised by the sign of
P
and
Q
of Eq. (
26
)
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.
When
P
and
Q
have the same sign (i.e.,
P/Q>0
),
the evolution of IAWs amplitude is modulationally
unstable in the presence of external perturbations.
On the other hand, when
P
and
Q
have opposite signs
(i.e.,
P/Q<0
), the IAWs are modulationally stable in
the presence of external perturbations. The plot of
P/Q

against
k
yields stable and unstable parametric regimes
of the IAWs. The point, at which the transition of
P/Q

curve intersects with the
k
-axis, is known as the threshold
or critical wave number
k~(=k_{c})

[
23
,
24
,

,
26
,

,

7
Conclusion

We have studied an unmagnetized realistic space
plasma system consists of warm ions, iso-thermal electrons,
\kappa
-distributed
electrons and positrons. The RPM is used to derive the NLSE.
The existence of both stable and unstable regions of IAWs has been found,
and the interaction between the
k_{c}
with various plasma parameters (i.e.,
\alpha
,
\mu_{1}
,

\delta
, and
\kappa
, etc.) has also been observed.
Finally, these results may be applicable
in understanding the conditions of the MI of IAWs
and associated envelope solitons in astrophysical environments.

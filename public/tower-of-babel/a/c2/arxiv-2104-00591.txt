Abstract
. We give a classification of the dual graphs of the exceptional divisors on the minimal resolutions of log canonical foliation singularities on surfaces. For an application, we show the set of foliated minimal log discrepancies for foliated surface triples satisfies the ascending chain condition and a Grauert-Riemenschneider type vanishing theorem for foliated surfaces with good log canonical foliation singularities.

Primary 32S65, Secondary 32M25, 14B05, 14J99.

Introduction

Singularities play an important role in many areas of algebraic geometry.
For instance, singular varieties naturally appear in the minimal model program and in the study of moduli spaces, where it often happens that smooth objects degenerate to singular ones.
However, many classes of singularities that naturally occur are mild and can sometimes be understood in detail.
One of the natural classes of singularities is (log) canonical singularities.
These singularities have been extensively studied, especially in the case of surfaces.
In particular, we have a full classification of log canonical surface singularities.
(For a reference, see
[
Wat80
]
,
[
KM98
, Section 4.1]
, or
[
Har98
, Appendix]
.)

When studying foliations, one expects that similar results may hold.
In this direction,
McQuillan
introduces in
[
McQ08
]
a notion of log canonical foliation singularities, which is a natural generalization of log canonical singularities.
Moreover, he also gives a classification for the canonical foliation singularities on surfaces.
(See
[
McQ08
, Corollary I.2.2 and Fact I.2.4]
.)

In this paper, we first give a full list of all possibilities of the dual graphs of the exceptional divisors on the minimal resolutions of log canonical foliation singularities on surfaces.
(See also Theorem
2.4
.)
This is achieved with the help of the work of Brunella, Camacho, and Sad on the indices and a theorem on separatrices.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
be a germ of a foliated surface.
Assume that
p
is a log canonical singularity of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Let
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be the minimal resolution (see subsection
1.3
) for
(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
with exceptional divisors
E=\cup E_{i}
.
Then
E
belongs to one of the following types:

(1)

A
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain.

(2)

A chain of three invariant curves
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup E_{3}
where
E_{1}
and
E_{3}
are
(-1)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves with self-intersection
-2
and
E_{2}
is a bad tail.

(3)

A chain of
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves.

(4)

A dihedral singularity.
More precisely, two
(-1)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves with self-intersection
-2
joined by a bad tail which itself connects to a chain of
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves.

(5)

An elliptic Gorenstein leaf.

(6)

A chain
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
with exactly one non-invariant curve
E_{\ell}
with
1\leq\ell\leq r
. Moreover,
E_{\ell}
has tangency order zero and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell-1}E_{i}
and
\bigcup_{i=\ell+1}^{r}E_{i}
are
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains.

(7)

The dual graph is star-shaped with a non-invariant center
[E_{0}]
. Moreover,
E_{0}
has tangency order zero, all branches are
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains, and all first curves of
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains have intersection number one with
E_{0}
.

Note that type (1) is terminal, and types (1) - (5) are canonical.

Inspired by the work in
[
Ale93
]
on the ascending chain condition (ACC) for the set of the minimal log discrepancies of surface singularities, we show the set of foliated minimal log discrepancies satisfies the ACC.
(See also Definition
3.2
and Theorem
4.7
.)

Theorem 0.2
.

For any set
B
satisfying the descending chain condition, the set

\textnormal{MLD}(2,B):=\{\textnormal{mld}_{x}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)|\,\mbox{$(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)$ is a foliated triple with $x\in X$ and $\Delta\in B$}\}

satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC).

Finally, we prove a Grauert-Riemannschneider type vanishing theorem for foliated surfaces with
good
log canonical foliation singularities by using the method in
[
Kol13
, Theorem 10.4]
.
(See Definition
5.1
and Theorem
5.3
.)
This is a generalization of
[
HL21
, Theorem 6.1]
in which
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
is assumed to have only canonical foliation singularities.

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
f:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a proper birational morphism where
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
is a foliated surface with good log canonical foliation singularities and
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
is a foliated surface with only reduced singularities.
Then
R^{i}f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(K_{\mathscr{G}})=0
for
i>0
.

1.
Preliminaries

In this paper, we always work over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
By a surface, we mean a normal algebraic space of dimension two.
In this section, we recall several definitions and results which will be used later.

1.1.
Foliations on surfaces

A
foliation

{\mathscr{F}}
on a surface
X
is a rank 1 saturated subsheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
of the tangent sheaf
T_{X}
of
X
.
So we have the following short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow T_{X}\rightarrow T_{X}/{\mathscr{F}}\rightarrow 0

with
T_{X}/{\mathscr{F}}
torsion-free.

The point
p
on
X
is called a
singular
point of the foliation if either a singular point of
X
or a point at which the quotient
T_{X}/{\mathscr{F}}
is
not
locally free.
Since
X
is normal, the foliation singularities are isolated.

Definition 1.1
.

A
foliated surface
is a pair
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
consisting of a surface
X
and a foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
.
A
foliated triple
is a triple
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
consisting of a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
\Delta=\sum a_{i}D_{i}
.

Notice that
T_{X}\cong\textnormal{Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(\Omega_{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
is reflexive.
Thus,
{\mathscr{F}}
is also reflexive and therefore we can define the
canonical divisor

K_{\mathscr{F}}
of the foliation as a Weil divisor on
X
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathscr{F}})\cong{\mathscr{F}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface.
Given any birational map between normal surfaces
f:Y\dashrightarrow X
and a foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
, then we define the
pullback foliation

f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
as follows:

Let
U
be an open subset such that
V:=f^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U
is an isomorphism.
Note that
{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\subset T_{U}\cong T_{V}
.
By
[
Har77
, Exercise II.5.15]
, we have a coherent subsheaf
{\mathscr{G}}
of
T_{Y}
such that
{\mathscr{G}}|_{V}={\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\subset T_{V}
.
Then the pullback foliation
f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
is defined to be the saturation of
{\mathscr{G}}
.
By
[
HL21
, Lemma 1.8]
, this definition is well-defined.

Also if
{\mathscr{G}}
is a foliation on
Y
, then we can define the
pushforward foliation

f_{*}{\mathscr{G}}
by taking the saturation of the image of the composition

f_{*}T_{\mathscr{G}}\rightarrow f_{*}T_{Y}\rightarrow(f_{*}T_{Y})^{**}=T_{X}.

Definition 1.3

(Invariant curves)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
U
be the non-singular locus of
X
.
A curve
C
on
X
is called
{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant
if the inclusion map

T_{{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}}|_{C}\rightarrow T_{U}|_{C}

factors through
T_{C}|_{U}
.

Definition 1.4

(
[
McQ08
, Definition I.1.5]
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated triple and
f:Y\rightarrow X
be a proper birational morphism.
For any divisor
E
on
Y
, we define the
discrepancy
of
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
along
E
to be
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=\textnormal{ord}_{E}(K_{f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}}-f^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta))
.
We say

(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\mbox{ is }\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\textnormal{terminal}&\mbox{ if }a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)>0\\
\textnormal{canonical}&\mbox{ if }a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\geq 0\\
\textnormal{log terminal}&\mbox{ if }a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)>-\varepsilon(E)\\
\textnormal{log canonical}&\mbox{ if }a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\geq-\varepsilon(E)\end{array}\right.\mbox{ for every divisor $E$ over $X$}

where
\varepsilon(E)
is defined to be
0
if
E
is
f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant, and
1
otherwise.

1.2.
Indices on foliated surfaces

Most definitions in this subsection follow from
[
Bru15
]
with some generalizations.

Let
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\backslash\textnormal{Sing}(X)
. That is,
p
is a smooth point on
X
but a singular point of the foliation.
Let
v
be the vector field around
p
generating
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Since
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})
, we have
v(p)=0
.
Then we can consider the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
of
(Dv)|_{p}
, which do not depend on the choice of
v
.

Definition 1.5
.

If one of the eigenvalues is non-zero, say
\lambda_{2}
, then we say
p
is
semi-reduced
and define the eigenvalue of the foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
to be

\lambda:=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}.

For
\lambda\neq 0
, this definition is unique up to reciprocal
\lambda\sim\frac{1}{\lambda}
.

If
\lambda=0
, then
p
is called a
saddle-node
; otherwise, we say
p
is
non-degenerate
.
If
\lambda\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
, then
p
is called a
reduced
singularity of
{\mathscr{F}}
.

Reduced singularities arise naturally.
Indeed, blowing up a smooth foliation point will introduce a reduced singularity with
\lambda=-1
.

Theorem 1.6

(Seidenberg’s theorem)
.

Given any foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
with
X
smooth.
There is a sequence of blowups
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
such that
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
has only reduced singularities.

Proof
.

See
[
Sei68
]
,
[
MM80
, Appendix]
, or
[
Bru15
, Theorem 1.1]
. ∎

Definition 1.7

(Separatrices)
.

Let
p
be a singular point of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
A
separatrix
of
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
is a holomorphic (possibly singular) irreducible
{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant curve
C
on a neighborhood of
p
which passes through
p
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a normal surface
X
and
C\subset X
be a connected, compact
{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant curve such that

(1)

all the singularities of
{\mathscr{F}}
on
C
are reduced.

(2)

the intersection matrix of
C
is negative definite and the dual graph is a tree.

Then there exists at least one point
p\in C\cap\textnormal{Sing}{\mathscr{F}}
and a separatrix through
p
not contained in
C
.

Proof
.

See
[
Cam88
]
,
[
Seb97
]
, or
[
Bru15
, Theorem 3.4]
. ∎

1.2.1.
Non-invariant curves

We first consider the non-invariant curves and define the tangency order for them.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
C
be a
non-invariant
reduced curve.
Let
p\in C\backslash\textnormal{Sing}(X)
and
v
be the vector field generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
Let
f
be the local defining function of
C
at
p
.
We define the
tangency order
of
{\mathscr{F}}
along
C
at
p
to be

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}}{\langle f,v(f)\rangle}.

Note that
\mbox{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)\geq 0
and is independent of the choices of
v
and
f
.
Moreover, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is transverse to
C
at
p
, then
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0
.
Therefore, if
C
is compact, then we can define

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C):=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p).

Proposition 1.10

(
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
, and
C
be a non-invariant curve on
X
.
Then we have

K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C)-C^{2}.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
C
be a non-invariant curve on a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
If
C
is contained in the smooth locus of
X
, then we have
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+C)\cdot C\geq 0
.

1.2.2.
Invariant curves

Now we study the invariant curves.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
C
be an invariant curve.
Let
p\in C\backslash\textnormal{Sing}(X)
and
\omega
be a
1
-form generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
If
C
is an invariant curve and
f
is the local defining function of
C
at
p
, then we can write

g\omega=hdf+f\eta

where
g
and
h
are holomorphic functions,
\eta
is a holomorphic
1
-form, and
h,f
are relatively prime functions.

We define the index
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
to be the vanishing order of
\frac{h}{g}|_{C}
at
p
.
Also we define the Camacho-Sad index
\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
to be the residue of
\frac{-1}{h}\eta|_{C}
at
p
.
These two definitions are independent of the choices of
f,g,h,\omega,\eta
.
(For a reference, see
[
Bru15
, page 15 in Chapter 2 and page 27 in Chapter 3]
.)

Note that if
p\not\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0=\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
.
Therefore, if
C
is compact, then we can define

\displaystyle\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p),\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)

where the sums are taken over only finitely many points.

Definition 1.13
.

We define the
virtual Euler characteristic

\chi(C)
of
C
on a normal surface
X
to be
\chi(C)=-K_{X}\cdot C-C^{2}
.

Theorem 1.14

(
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
, and
C
be an invariant curve on
X
.
Then we have

(1)

K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)-\chi(C)
, and

(2)

C^{2}=\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C)
. This identity is called Camacho-Sad formula.

Lemma 1.15
.

Given a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
and
p
a reduced singularity.

(1)

If
p
is non-degenerate, assume
\omega=\lambda y(1+o(1))dx-x(1+o(1))dy
generates
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
Then

\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\frac{1}{\lambda},\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=\lambda,\mbox{ and }\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=1.

(2)

If
p
is a saddle-node, assume
\omega=y^{k+1}dx-(x(1+\nu y^{k})+yo(k))dy
generates
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
where
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then

\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=0\mbox{ and }\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},y=0,p)=1.

Suppose there exists a weak separatrix, then

\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=\nu\mbox{ and }\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},x=0,p)=k+1.

Proof
.

This is done by direct computation.
For a reference, see
[
Bru15
, page 30-31 in Chapter 3]
.
∎

1.3.
Minimal resolutions of foliated surfaces

Definition 1.16
.

A morphism
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of foliated surfaces is called a
resolution
if
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
has only reduced foliation singularities.

A resolution
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
is called
minimal
if any resolution
\phi:(Z,{\mathscr{H}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of the foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
factors through
\pi
.
That is, there is a morphism
\psi:(Z,{\mathscr{H}})\rightarrow(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
with
{\mathscr{H}}=\psi^{*}{\mathscr{G}}
such that
\phi=\pi\circ\psi
.

Proposition 1.17
.

Any foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
has a unique minimal resolution up to isomorphism.

Proof
.

Taking a resolution of
X
, and then by Seidenberg’s theorem
1.6
, we have a resolution
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of the foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
After blowing down
{\mathscr{G}}
-exceptional curves, we may assume that
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
has no
{\mathscr{G}}
-exceptional curves.
We will show that this
\pi
gives a minimal resolution of the foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.

Given any resolution
\phi:(Z,{\mathscr{H}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of the foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
Let
W
be the minimal resolution of singularities of
Z\times_{X}Y
, and
{\mathcal{E}}
be the pullback foliation on
W
.
So we have the following diagram of foliated surfaces

\textstyle{(W,{\mathcal{E}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{(Z,{\mathscr{H}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{(X,{\mathscr{F}}).}

We may assume
(W,{\mathcal{E}})
is minimal in the sense that there is no birational morphism of foliated surfaces
\theta:(W,{\mathcal{E}})\rightarrow(W^{\prime},{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
, which is not an isomorphism,
such that both
\alpha
and
\beta
factor through
\theta
.

Suppose
\alpha
is not an isomorphism, then there is a (last)
\alpha
-exceptional curve
\widetilde{C}
on
W
with
\widetilde{C}^{2}=-1
, which is also
{\mathcal{E}}
-exceptional.
By the minimality of
(W,{\mathcal{E}})
,
\widetilde{C}
is not contracted by
\beta
.
Let
C\subset Y
be the curve
\beta(\widetilde{C})
.
Since
p_{a}(\widetilde{C})=0
, we have that
p_{a}(C)=0
.
Note that
C
is contracted by
\pi
because
\widetilde{C}
is contracted by
\phi\circ\alpha=\pi\circ\beta
.
Hence
C^{2}\leq-1
.
Also,
C^{2}\geq\widetilde{C}^{2}=-1
and thus,
C^{2}=-1
.
Moreover,
\beta
is isomorphic around
\widetilde{C}
.
Therefore,
C
is
{\mathscr{G}}
-exceptional.
But this is impossible since
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
has no
{\mathscr{G}}
-exceptional curves.
∎

Remark 1.18
.

In general, for any minimal resolution
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
of the foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
, the morphism
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
of surfaces is not the minimal resolution of

.

1.4.
Dual graphs

Definition 1.19
.

Let
C=\bigcup C_{i}
be a collection of proper curves on the smooth locus of a surface
X
.
Then the (weighted)
dual graph

\Gamma=\Gamma(C)
of
C
is defined as follows:

(1)

The vertices of
\Gamma
are the curves
C_{i}
. We will use
[C_{i}]
to indicate the vertex of
\Gamma
corresponding to the curve
C_{i}
.

(2)

Two vertices
[C_{i}]
and
[C_{j}]
for
i\neq j
are connected with
C_{i}\cdot C_{j}
edges.

(3)

The weight
\textnormal{w}([C_{i}])
of vertex
[C_{i}]
is given by
-C_{i}^{2}
.

Definition 1.20
.

For any (dual) graph
\Gamma
, we have the following definitions:

(1)

A
cycle
is a graph whose vertices and edges can be ordered as
[C_{1}],\ldots,[C_{m}]
and
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
where
m\geq 2
such that edge
e_{i}
connects vertices
[C_{i}]
and
[C_{i+1}]
for
i=1,\ldots,m
where
[C_{m+1}]:=[C_{1}]
.

(2)

We say
\Gamma

has simple edges
if any two vertices are connected by at most one edge.

(3)

A
tree
is a connected graph which has no cycle as its subgraph.

(4)

The
degree
, denoted by
\textnormal{deg}\,[C_{i}]
, of the vertex
[C_{i}]
is the number of edges connecting to
[C_{i}]
.

(5)

The vertex
[C_{i}]
is called a
leaf
of
\Gamma
if the degree of
[C_{i}]
is 1.

(6)

The vertex
[C_{i}]
is called a
fork
of
\Gamma
if the degree of
[C_{i}]
is at least 3.

(7)

A tree without forks is called a
chain
.

(8)

The connected components of a tree minus a fork are called the
branches
of the fork.

(9)

A tree is
star-shaped
if there is exactly one fork.

(10)

We define
\Delta(\Gamma)
to be the absolute value of the determinant of the intersection matrix for
\Gamma
.
We set
\Delta(\emptyset)=1
.

By direct computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.21
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a tree with simple edges.

(1)

For any vertex
[C]
of
\Gamma
of weight
w([C])
, we have

\Delta(\Gamma)=\textnormal{w}([C])\Delta\big(\Gamma\backslash\{[C]\}\big)-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Delta\big(\Gamma\backslash\{[C],[C_{i}]\}\big)

where
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
are all vertices adjacent to
C
.

(2)

For any two vertices
[C_{i}]
and
[C_{j}]
of
\Gamma
, the
(i,j)
-cofactor of the intersection matrix
A
of
\Gamma
is

A_{i,j}=(-1)^{n+1}\Delta\big(\Gamma\backslash\{\textnormal{path from }[C_{i}]\textnormal{ to }[C_{j}]\}\big)

where
n=|\Gamma|
.

2.
Classification

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.4
which gives a full list of all possibilities of the dual graphs of the exceptional divisors on the minimal resolutions of log canonical foliation singularities on surfaces.

In order to describe the exceptional divisor over a foliation singularity, we recall the following definitions.
(See also
[
Bru15
, Definition 5.1 and 8.1]
and
[
McQ08
, Definition III.0.2 and III.2.3]
.)

Definition 2.1
.

Given a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
with
X
smooth.

(1)

Before proving the main theorem, we recall the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C=\bigcup_{i}C_{i}
be a set of proper curves on a smooth surface.
Assume that the intersection matrix
(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})_{i,j}
is negative definite.
Let
A=\sum a_{i}C_{i}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear combination of the curves
C_{i}
’s.
If
A\cdot C_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
, then

(1)

a_{i}\leq 0
for all
i
.

(2)

If
C
is connected, then either
a_{i}=0
for all
i
or
a_{i}<0
for all
i
.
Moreover, if
A\cdot C_{j}>0
for some

for all

.

Proof
.

For a reference, see
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.41]
.

Remark 2.3
.

The exceptional divisor
E
of a resolution of a singularity is connected by Zariski’s main theorem.
Moreover, it is well-known that the intersection matrix of
E
is negative definite. (For a reference, see
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.40]
.)

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
be a germ of a foliated surface.
Assume that
p
is a log canonical singularity of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Let
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}})\rightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be the minimal resolution (see subsection
1.3
) for
(X,{\mathscr{F}},p)
with exceptional divisors
E=\cup E_{i}
.
Then
E
belongs to one of the following types:

(1)

A
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain.

(2)

A chain of three invariant curves
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup E_{3}
where
E_{1}
and
E_{3}
are
(-1)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves with self-intersection
-2
and
E_{2}
is a bad tail.

(3)

A chain of
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves.

(4)

A dihedral singularity.
More precisely, two
(-1)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves with self-intersection
-2
joined by a bad tail which itself connects to a chain of
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curves.

(5)

An elliptic Gorenstein leaf.

(6)

A chain
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
with exactly one non-invariant curve
E_{\ell}
with
1\leq\ell\leq r
. Moreover,
E_{\ell}
has tangency order zero and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell-1}E_{i}
and
\bigcup_{i=\ell+1}^{r}E_{i}
are
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains.

(7)

The dual graph is star-shaped with a non-invariant center
[E_{0}]
. Moreover,
E_{0}
has tangency order zero, all branches are
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains, and all first curves of
{\mathscr{G}}
-chains have intersection number one with
E_{0}
.

Note that type (1) is terminal, and types (1) - (5) are canonical.

Proof
.

Let
\Gamma
be the dual graph of exceptional divisors
E=\cup E_{i}
, which is connected by Zariski’s main theorem.
Let
\Delta
be the sum over all non-invariant exceptional curves
E_{i}
.
Then we write
K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta=\pi^{*}K_{\mathscr{F}}+\sum a_{i}E_{i}
where
a_{i}\geq 0
since
p
is a log canonical singularity of
{\mathscr{F}}
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

(1)

Claim.
For any vertex
[E_{i}]
with
E_{i}
invariant, we have
(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}\geq\deg\,[E_{i}]-2
.

Proof
.

Let
d=\deg\,[E_{i}]
.
Suppose there are exactly
m
edges of
[E_{i}]
connecting to the vertices corresponding to
invariant
curves.
Since the intersections of two invariant curves are reduced singularities and at most two separatrices pass through any reduced singularity, there are at least
m
distinct foliation singularities on
E_{i}
.
Thus, we have that
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{i})\geq m
.

The other
d-m
edges of
[E_{i}]
connect to the vertices corresponding to
non-invariant
curves, which are in the support of
\Delta
.
Thus, we have that
\Delta\cdot E_{i}=d-m
by the definition of edges.
Therefore, by Theorem
1.14
, we have

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{i})+2p_{a}(E_{i})-2+\Delta\cdot E_{i}\geq m-2+(d-m)=d-2.

(2)

Claim.
Suppose
\Gamma=\{[E_{1}]\}
is a one-vertex graph. Then we have the following two possibilities.

(a)

If
E_{1}
is non-invariant, then the tangency order of
E_{1}
is zero.

(b)

If
E_{1}
is invariant, then
E_{1}
is a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain, a
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curve, or a rational curve with only one node.

Proof
.

If
E_{1}
is non-invariant, then we have
\Delta=E_{1}
and, by Theorem
1.10
,

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{1}=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+E_{1})\cdot E_{1}=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})\geq 0.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
a_{1}\leq 0
and
a_{1}=0
if and only if
(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{1}=0
.
Since
a_{1}\geq 0
, we have
a_{1}=0
and hence

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+E_{1})\cdot E_{1}=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{1}=\pi^{*}K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot E_{1}=0.

If
E_{1}
is invariant, then we hvae
\Delta=0
and, by Theorem
1.14
,

K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})+2p_{a}(E_{1})-2\geq-2.

•

If
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}=-2
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})=0
and
p_{a}(E_{1})=0
.
So
E_{1}
is smooth with no foliation singularity, which is impossible by Theorem
1.8
.

•

If
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}=-1
, then
\mbox{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})=1
and
p_{a}(E_{1})=0
.
So
E_{1}
is a smooth rational curve with exactly one reduced singularity.
Since
\pi
is minimal, we have
E_{1}^{2}\leq-2
, and thus
E_{1}
is a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain.

•

If
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}\geq 0
, then by Lemma
2.2
, we have
a_{1}\leq 0
and
a_{1}=0
if and only if
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}=0
.
Since
a_{1}\geq 0
, we have
a_{1}=0
and
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot E_{1}=0
.
Thus we have
p_{a}(E_{1})=0
or
1
.
If
p_{a}(E_{1})=0
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})=2
, which is a
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curve.
If
p_{a}(E_{1})=1
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},E_{1})=0
.
So
E_{1}
is not smooth, otherwise
E_{1}^{2}=0
by Theorem
1.14
, which is impossible.
Thus,
E_{1}
is a rational curve with only one node.

(3)

From now on, we assume that
\Gamma
has at least two vertices.

Claim
.

For any vertices
[E_{i}]
, we have

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}\geq-1
and the equality holds if and only if
E_{i}
is invariant and
[E_{i}]
is a leaf.

Proof
.

Since
\Gamma
is connected with at least two vertices, we have

for any vertex
[E_{i}]
. If
E_{i}
is invariant, then we have the required inequality by step (
1
).

If
E_{i}
is non-invariant, then we have

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}\geq(K_{\mathscr{G}}+E_{i})\cdot E_{i}=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{G}},E_{i})\geq 0

by Theorem
1.10
.

(4)

We call
[E_{i}]
a
special leaf
if
E_{i}
is invariant with
(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}=-1
.
We will denote
L:=E_{i}
if
[E_{i}]
is a special leaf.

Note that, by Theorem
1.14
, we have

-1=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot L=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},L)+2p_{a}(L)-2+\Delta\cdot L\geq-2.

Hence, we have
p_{a}(L)=0
, and thus
L
is a smooth rational curve.
Since
L^{2}\leq-1
, we have
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C)\geq 1
, and hence
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C)=1
and
\Delta\cdot L=0
.
Then by the minimality of
\pi
, we have
L^{2}\leq-2
.
Thus
L
itself is a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain.
Therefore, we consider the chain
C_{L}
of maximal length

starting from

, which is a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain and disjoint from
\Delta
.

(5)

Claim.
Fix a special leaf
[L]
. Other than those curves in

, there is at most one curve in

connecting to the last curve
C_{L,N_{L}}
of
C_{L}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{L}}C_{L,i}
with

Precisely, if the dual graph of

is not

, then such curve exists and is called the tail of

, denoted by

.
Moreover, it is invariant with

\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})\geq 2\mbox{ and }(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot C_{L,N_{L}+1}\geq 1.

Proof
.

If the dual graph of
C_{L}
is
\Gamma
, then the statement is clear.
If not, then such vertex
[V]
exists because
\Gamma
is connected.
Since
C_{L}
is disjoint from
\Delta
, so the curve

must be invariant.
Since
C_{L}
is a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain, there is precisely one such curve

.
Besides, this curve

must connect to the last curve

.
So we denote it by

.
Note that
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})\geq 1

intersects

.

(a)

If
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})=1

\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{L}+1}C_{L,j}
contradicts to Theorem
1.8
.

(b)

If
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})=2
, then
\Delta\cdot C_{L,N_{L}+1}\geq 1

.
Thus, by Theorem

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot C_{L,N_{L}+1}=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})-2+\Delta\cdot C_{L,N_{L}+1}\geq 1.

(c)

If
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},C_{L,N_{L}+1})\geq 3

.

(6)

From now on, we assume that
\Gamma
is not a dual graph of a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain.
So by step (
5
),
C_{L,N_{L}+1}
always exists for any special leaf
[L]
.

(7)

Claim.
For two distinct special leaves
[L]
and

, two chains

and

are disjoint.

Proof
.

If not, then two

-chains intersect at the last curves, and

forms a chain which contradicts Theorem

.

(8)

Now for any special leaf
[L]
, we define a divisor

D_{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{L}}\frac{N_{L}+1-j}{N_{L}+1}C_{L,j}.

Let
D=\sum D_{L}
where the sum is over all special leaves
[L]
.

(9)

Claim.
There is a vertex
[V]
of
\Gamma
such that the coefficient of
V
in
D
is zero.

Proof
.

Suppose not, then the support of
D
, which is a union of
C_{L}
’s where
[L]
’s are special leaves, is the same as the support of
E
.
Since
\Gamma
is connected and any two distinct

and

are disjoint by step (

), we conclude that
\Gamma
is the dual graph of
C_{L}
for one special leaf
[L]
.
Therefore,
\Gamma
is the dual graph of a
{\mathscr{G}}
-chain, which contradicts step (
6
).

(10)

Claim.
We have
D\cdot L\leq-1=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot L
for any special leaf
[L]
.
Moreover,
D\cdot L=-1
if and only if

.

Proof
.

If
N_{L}=1
, then

D\cdot L=\frac{1}{2}L^{2}\leq-1

and the equality holds if and only if
L^{2}=-2
.

If
N_{L}\geq 2
, then

D\cdot L=\frac{N_{L}}{N_{L}+1}L^{2}+\frac{N_{L}-1}{N_{L}+1}C_{L,2}\cdot L\leq\frac{-2N_{L}}{N_{L}+1}+\frac{N_{L}-1}{N_{L}+1}=-1

and the equality holds if and only if

.

(11)

Claim.
For any special leaf
[L]
, we have

D\cdot C_{L,j}\leq 0=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot C_{L,j}

for
2\leq j\leq N_{L}
.

Proof
.

Since
C_{L,j}
is a
(-2)
-
{\mathscr{G}}
-curve and
C_{L}
is disjoint from
\Delta
, we have
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot C_{L,j}=0
and
\Delta\cdot C_{L,j}=0
.
Therefore, we have
(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot C_{L,j}=0
.

For

, we have

\displaystyle D\cdot C_{L,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{L}+2-j}{N_{L}+1}+\frac{N_{L}+1-j}{N_{L}+1}C_{L,j}^{2}+\frac{N_{L}-j}{N_{L}+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{N_{L}+2-j}{N_{L}+1}+\frac{N_{L}+1-j}{N_{L}+1}(-2)+\frac{N_{L}-j}{N_{L}+1}=0.

(12)

Claim.
For any fork
[F]
, we have

D\cdot F=\sum\frac{1}{N_{L}+1}

where the sum is over all special leaves
[L]
such that
C_{L,N_{L}+1}=F
.
Also we have
D\cdot F\leq(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot F
.

Proof
.

It is clear that

D\cdot F=\sum\frac{1}{N_{L}+1}C_{L,N_{L}}\cdot F=\sum\frac{1}{N_{L}+1}

where the sum is over all special leaves
[L]
such that
C_{L,N_{L}+1}=F
.

•

If
F
is non-invariant, then
D\cdot F=0
since

is always invariant by step (
5
).
Also, by Theorem
1.10
, we have
(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot F\geq(K_{\mathscr{G}}+F)\cdot F\geq 0
.

•

If
F
is invariant, then
D\cdot F\leq m/2
where

is the number of special leaves
[L]
such that

.
Also, by Theorem

Moreover, by step (

), we have

Hence, we have

(13)

Claim.
For any vertex
[V]
of
\Gamma
, we have

.

Proof

The only vertices we have not checked yet are vertices

If

.
Also, by step (

.

If

Thus, by step (

.

(14)

Claim.

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot V=D\cdot V

[V]
of
\Gamma

Suppose there is a vertex

.
By steps (

), (

), and Lemma

(15)

Claim.
For any special leaf
[L]
, we have
N_{L}=1
and

must be a bad tail which has

\deg\,[C_{L,2}]=2\mbox{ or }3

.

.

Let

and

be all special leaves such that

for

. So we have
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},V)\geq s
.

If
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},V)=s

contradicts to Theorem

.
Thus, we have

(1)

\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},V)\geq s+1

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta)\cdot V\geq s-1
.

For the case when

, we have
D\cdot V=\frac{1}{N_{L}+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}

Thus, by step (

.

(16)

Claim.
Let
[F]
be a fork. Then

Assume that

.

.

If

Hence,

(17)

Claim.
All vertices
[V]
of degree
\leq 2
belong to one of the following types:

(a)

A non-invariant curve

with tangency order zero.

(b)

A special leaf.

(c)

A bad tail
V
.

(d)

An invariant curve

with

,
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},V)=1
, and
\Delta\cdot V=1
.

(e)

An invariant curve

,
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{G}},V)=2
, and
\Delta\cdot V=0
.

Proof
.

If
V

), we have

.

).

3.
Foliated minimal log discrepancy

In this section, we define the foliated minimal log discrepancy and show some of its properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated triple.
For any divisor
E
over
X
, we define the
foliated log discrepancy
of
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
along
E
to be
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)
where
\varepsilon(E)=0
if
E
is invariant under the pullback foliation and
\varepsilon(E)=1
otherwise.

Definition 3.2
.

Given
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
a foliated triple.
Let
(Y,{\mathscr{G}})
be the minimal resolution of
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
We define the
foliated minimal log discrepancy
of
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
as

\textnormal{mld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta):=\inf\{a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)\neq 0|\,\mbox{$E$ is a divisor over $X$}\}

and the
partial log discrepancy
as

\textnormal{pld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta):=\min\{a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)\neq 0|\,\mbox{$E$ is a divisor on $Y$}\}.

Also, for any fixed
x\in X
, we define

\displaystyle\textnormal{mld}_{x}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\{a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)\neq 0|\,\mbox{the center of $E$ on $X$ is $x$}\}\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\textnormal{pld}_{x}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min\{a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)\neq 0|\,\mbox{$E$ is a divisor on $Y$ over $x$}.\}

From now on, we make the convention that
\min\emptyset=\inf\emptyset=0
.

Remark 3.3
.

By Corollary
3.6
, if
\textnormal{mld}_{x}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\geq 0
, then it is indeed a minimum, that is, there is a divisor
E
over
X
that computes the
\textnormal{mld}_{x}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\textnormal{mld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)<0
, then
\textnormal{mld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=-\infty
.

Proof
.

Suppose
\textnormal{mld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)<0
, then there is a divisor
E
over
X
such that

a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E)<0.

If
E
is non-invariant, that is
\varepsilon(E)=1
, then blowing up a general point
p
on
E
introduces an invariant exceptional divisor
E^{\prime}
with
a(E^{\prime},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+\varepsilon(E^{\prime})\leq a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+1<0
.
Therefore, we may assume that
E
is invariant.

Let
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=-c
for some positive number
c
.
Now blowing up a general point
p
on
E
introduces an invariant exceptional divisor
E_{1}
with

a(E_{1},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\leq 1+a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=1-c.

Notice that the proper transform of the support of
\Delta
doesn’t contain the intersection of
E_{1}
and the proper transform of
E
.

Next we blow up the intersection of
E_{1}
and the proper transform of
E
, then we have an invariant exceptional divisor
E_{2}
with

a(E_{2},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)+a(E_{1},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\leq 1+2a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=1-2c.

Then we blow up the intersection of the proper transform of
E
and
E_{2}
to have an invariant exceptional divisor
E_{3}
with

a(E_{3},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\leq 1+3a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=1-3c.

Repeating the process, by inductively, we have an invariant exceptional divisor
E_{n}
over
X
with
a(E_{n},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\leq 1-nc
.
This shows that
\textnormal{mld}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=-\infty
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated triple with only log canonical foliation singularities, that is

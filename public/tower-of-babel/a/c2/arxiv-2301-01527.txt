1.
Introduction

1.1.
The model

Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\big(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\big)^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional torus (
d=2,3
). The convective Brinkman-Forchheimer (CBF) equations describe the motion of incompressible fluid flows in a saturated porous medium (
[
44
,
45
]
). A porous medium or a porous material is defined as a solid (often called a matrix) permeated by an interconnected network of pores (voids) filled with a fluid (e.g., air or water). We consider a porous medium that is fully saturated with a fluid (
[
58
]
). With control applications in mind, we consider the following CBF equations with potential (perturbed by a subdifferential, see Hypothesis
2.2
below):

(1.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{y}}{\partial t}-\mu\Delta\boldsymbol{y}+(\boldsymbol{y}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{y}+\alpha\boldsymbol{y}+\beta|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y}+\nabla p+\Psi(\boldsymbol{y})&\ni\boldsymbol{g},\ \text{ in }\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{y}&=0,\ \text{ in }\ \mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{y}(0)&=\boldsymbol{y}_{0}\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{T}^{d},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{y}(x,t):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents the velocity field at time
t
and position
x
,
p(x,t):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure field,
\boldsymbol{g}(x,t):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an external forcing and
\Psi(\cdot)\subset\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is a multi-valued map. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{y}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
p(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\boldsymbol{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the following periodic boundary conditions:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}(x+e_{i},\cdot)=\boldsymbol{y}(x,\cdot),\ p(x+e_{i},\cdot)=p(x,\cdot)\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{g}(x+e_{i},\cdot)=\boldsymbol{g}(x,\cdot),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i=1,\ldots,d,
where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The constant
\mu>0
denotes the
Brinkman coefficient
(effective viscosity), the positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
represent the
Darcy
(permeability of porous medium) and
Forchheimer
(proportional to the porosity of the material) coefficients, respectively. The term
\beta|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y}
with
r\geq 1
is known as the absorption or damping term and
r
is known as the absorption exponent (cf.
[
3
]
). The exponent
r=3
is called the
critical exponent
. The critical homogeneous CBF equations (system (
1.1
) without potential,
r=3
and
\boldsymbol{g}=\mathbf{0}
) have the same scaling as Navier-Stokes equations (NSE) only when
\alpha=0
(
[
30
]
). We refer the case
r<3
as
subcritical
and
r>3
as
supercritical
(or fast growing nonlinearities). The model is accurate when the flow velocity is too large for Darcy’s law to be valid, and apart from that the porosity is not too small (
[
38
]
). If one considers system (
1.1
) without potential and if
\alpha=\beta=0
, then we obtain the classical NSE, and if
\alpha,\beta>0
, then it can be considered as damped NSE. For 3D case, the authors in
[
38
]
introduced the extra term
\beta_{1}|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r_{1}-1}\boldsymbol{y}
in system (
1.1
) to model a pumping, when
\beta_{1}<0
by opposition to the damping modeled through the term
\beta|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y}
when
\beta>0
. For
\beta>0
and
\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, the existence of weak solutions were obtained by assuming
r>r_{1}\geq 1
, and the continuous dependence on the data as well as the existence of strong solutions were established for
r>3
. As we are working on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
r_{1}<r
, by modifying some calculations accordingly, similar results of this paper holds true for the model proposed in
[
38
]
as well.

1.2.
Literature survey

In the literature, CBF equations are also known as tamed NSE or NSE modified with an absorption term, cf.
[
3
,
50
]
etc., and references therein. The damping
\alpha\boldsymbol{y}+\beta|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y}
arises from the resistance to the motion of the flow, which describes several physical phenomena such as drag or friction effects, porous media flow, some dissipative mechanisms, cf.
[
17
,
30
,
38
,
59
]
etc., and references therein. The continuous data assimilation problem for 3D CBF model is described in
[
38
]
. The global solvability results for CBF model (for fast growing nonlinearities in 3D) can be accessed from
[
3
,
30
,
32
,
38
,
40
]
, etc. Similar to 3D NSE, the existence of a unique global (in time) weak solution of 3D CBF equations with
r\in[1,3)
(for any
\beta,\mu>0
) and
r=3
(for
2\beta\mu<1
) is also an open problem.

The theory of monotone operators is an important tool in the study of nonlinear operator equations, we refer the readers to
[
5
,
6
,
19
,
31
,
39
]
, etc., for more details. When the operator has some kind of monotonicity properties, then one can pass to the limit in the Galerkin and Faedo-Galerkin approximations of the original equation, with a-priori estimates that are in general weaker than those necessary in the compactness methods (
[
22
]
). In particular, monotone operators are suitable tools for studying variational inequalities (
[
6
]
). Local and global solvability of NSE and Boussinesq equations with potential (or perturbed by a subdifferential) is established in
[
33
,
35
]
, respectively.

In a standard Faedo-Galerkin discretization scheme under a Gelfand triplet
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}\equiv\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
(all are Hilbert spaces), we approximate the infinite-dimensional operator equation by a finite-dimensional one, whose well-posedness can be ensured by some classical existence and uniqueness results or some fixed point arguments. Then we find some uniform bounds of these approximate solutions (a-priori estimates) and use the Banach-Alaoglu compactness argument to find a subsequence which converges weakly. Using some compactness results like Aubin-Lions, one can extract a further subsequence which converges strongly, so that the weak limit provides us the solution of the original equation (cf.
[
55
]
). Whereas, the monotone operators is a class of nonlinear operators which provide a broad framework for the analysis of infinite-dimensional problems. Of special interest are the so-called maximal monotone operators (or
m
-accretive operators on Hilbert spaces identified with its own dual), which exhibit remarkable surjectivity properties. Further, for inclusion problems, once the operator becomes maximal, then one can use the Yosida approximation scheme, which is an important regularization technique for solving the evolution systems (
[
5
,
16
]
, etc.). In this way, one can avoid the tedious Faedo-Galerkin approximation scheme also.

Control of ordinary/partial differential equations associated with fluid flow motions have numerous applications in science, engineering and technology. Behavior and control of turbulent flows are some of the most difficult problems in fluid mechanics. By control of turbulent flows, we meant to determine an optimal action which
minimizes the turbulence
inside the flow, (cf.
[
1
,
25
,
29
,
53
]
). One of the interesting control problem is the flow invariance preserving feedback controllers for fluid flows. The authors in
[
13
]
developed a procedure to design feedback controllers that ensure the resultant dynamics of turbulence preserve some prescribed physical constraints such as enstrophy, helicity, etc. Flow invariance of controlled flux sets with respect to NSE is discussed in
[
12
]
. The existence problem of the variational inequality for Stokes equations and NSE with constraints of obstacle type is considered in
[
26
,
28
]
, respectively. An another interesting feedback control problem is the time optimal control problem, where one finds a control of bang-bang type to reach a fixed state from an arbitrary state in minimal time (cf.
[
6
,
57
]
). As far as the time optimal control of fluid flow models are concerned, the time optimal control problem for 2D NSE, Boussinesq equations, 3D Navier-Stokes-Voigt equations, 2D CBF equations with
r\in[1,3],
and 3D NS-
\alpha
model is considered in
[
2
,
7
,
36
,
43
,
52
]
, respectively.
Stabilization of NSE is dealt to stabilize the equilibrium solution of NSE by using finite-dimensional feedback controllers having support either in interior or on the boundary of the domain (cf.
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, etc.). The internal stabilizability of NSE (with slip and non-slip Dirichlet boundary conditions) is developed in
[
11
,
14
]
. The author in
[
34
]
discussed the feedback stabilization of NSE preserving the invariance of a given convex set.

\mathbb{V}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p^{\prime}}
are Banach spaces. Moreover, we have the continuous embedding
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\cong\mathbb{H}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p^{\prime}}
.

2.2.
Linear operator

Let
\mathcal{P}_{p}:\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p},

p\in[1,\infty)
be the Helmholtz-Hodge (or Leray) projection (cf.
[
4
,
24
]
, etc.). Note that
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is a bounded linear operator and for
p=2
,
\mathcal{P}:=\mathcal{P}_{2}
is an orthogonal projection (
[
49
, Section 2.1]
). We define the Stokes operator

\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}:=-\mathcal{P}\Delta\boldsymbol{y},\;\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}):=\mathbb{V}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

Note that
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
can also be written as
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})=\big\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{y}=0\big\}
. It should be noted that
\mathcal{P}
and
\Delta
commutes in a torus (
[
49
, Lemma 2.9]
). For the Fourier expansion
\boldsymbol{y}(x)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}\boldsymbol{y}_{k},
we have by using Parseval’s identity

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{y}_{k}|^{2}\ \text{and}\ \|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=(2\pi)^{4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|k|^{4}|\boldsymbol{y}_{k}|^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|k|^{4})|\boldsymbol{y}_{k}|^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Moreover, by the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
, we have
\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\geq\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
and hence it is immediate that
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{A})=\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

2.3.
Bilinear operator

Let us define the
trilinear form

b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
by

b(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{y}(x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{z}(x)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}(x)\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{z}_{j}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x.

If
\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}
are such that the linear map
b(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbb{V}
, the corresponding element of
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})
. We also denote
\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})=\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{y})=\mathcal{P}[(\boldsymbol{y}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{y}]
.
An integration by parts yields

(2.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} b(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w})&=-b(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{z}),\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{V},\\
b(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{z})&=0,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{V}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

2.4.
Nonlinear operator

Let us now consider the operator
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\mathcal{P}(|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y})
. It is immediate that
\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{y}\rangle=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}
and the map
\mathcal{C}(\cdot):\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{r+1}{r}}
is Gateaux differentiable with Gateaux derivative

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{z}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{z}),&\text{ for }r=1,\\
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{P}(|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{z})+(r-1)\mathcal{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{y}}{|\boldsymbol{y}|^{3-r}}(\boldsymbol{y}\cdot\boldsymbol{z})}}\right),&\text{ if }\boldsymbol{y}\neq\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{0},&\text{ if }\boldsymbol{y}=\mathbf{0},\end{array}}}\right.&\text{ for }1<r<3,\\
\mathcal{P}(|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{z})+(r-1)\mathcal{P}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-3}(\boldsymbol{y}\cdot\boldsymbol{z})),&\text{ for }r\geq 3,\end{array}}}\right.

for all
\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
.
From
[
37
, pp. 104]
(also see
[
42
, Subsection 2.4]
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\||\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\||\boldsymbol{z}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{r-1}}\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\geq 0,

for
r\geq 1
. This shows that
\mathcal{C}(\cdot)
is monotone operator.

Remark 2.1
.

In periodic domain (cf.
[
42
, Subsection 3.5]
), we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{3(r+1)}}^{r+1}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{2}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{r-1}\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

for
d=3
and
r\geq 1
. Also, from
[
48
, Lemma 2.2]
, we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p(r+1)}}^{r+1}=\||\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla|\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r+1}{2}}|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x\ {\leq}\ C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{2}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{r-1}\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

for
d=2
and for all
p\in[2,\infty).

2.5.
Main results

The main objective of this work is to establish the solvability results of the inclusion problem (
1.1
) and discuss their applications in the context of control problems. Since
\alpha
is not playing a major role in this work, so we fix
\alpha=0
in the rest of the paper. Let us state the main results of this work for the problem (
1.1
) in an abstract framework (see (
3.74
) below). We will prove these results in the subsequent sections. Let us denote
\boldsymbol{f}=\mathcal{P}\boldsymbol{g}
and
\Phi(\cdot)=\mathcal{P}\Psi(\cdot)
, where
\mathcal{P}
is the Helmholtz-Hodge (or Leray) projection. The following assumption is imposed on
\Phi(\cdot)
to achieve our goals which is similar to the work
[
33
]
.

Hypothesis 2.2
.

Let
\Phi
be a maximal monotone operator on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
satisfying the following hypothesis:

(H.1)

\Phi=\partial\varphi
, where
\varphi:\mathbb{H}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function.

(H.2)

\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathrm{D}(\Phi)
.

(H.3)

For some
\gamma\geq 0
and
\varsigma\in(0,\frac{1}{\mu})
(when
d=2
with
r\in[1,\infty)
and
d=3
with
r\in[1,5)
), and for some
\gamma\geq 0
and
\varsigma=0
(when
d=3
with
r\in[5,\infty)
), the following inequality hold:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y},\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}))\geq-\gamma(1+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2})-\varsigma\|\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

for all
\lambda>0
and
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
, where
\Phi_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathrm{I}-(\mathrm{I}+\lambda\Phi)^{-1}):\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is the Yosida approximation of
\Phi
.

We observe from the above expression that
\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y})\in\Phi((\mathrm{I}+\lambda\Phi)^{-1})(\boldsymbol{y})),

for every
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{H}
and
\lambda>0.
Let us discuss one example which satisfies the Hypothesis
2.2
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a closed and convex subset of
\mathbb{H}
satisfying

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{I}+\lambda\mathrm{A})^{-1}\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}.

We consider the indicator function
\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathbb{H}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
(
[
6
]
) by

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{0},&\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{K},\\
+\infty,&\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{x}\notin\mathcal{K},\end{cases}

whose subdifferential is given by

\displaystyle\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\begin{cases}\varnothing,&\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{x}\notin\mathcal{K},\\
\{\boldsymbol{0}\},&\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{K}),\\
N_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\text{if}\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{bdy}(\mathcal{K}),\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{int}(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathrm{bdy}(\mathcal{K})
denote the interior and boundary of
\mathcal{K}
, respectively. Then a regularization of
\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}}
is given by (see
[
6
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.2]
)

(\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\boldsymbol{x}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

and its Gateaux derivative

(\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})),

where
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to\mathcal{K}
is the projection operator of
\boldsymbol{x}
onto
\mathcal{K}
which is equal to the resolvent
(\mathrm{I}+\lambda\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}.
It implies that the above derivative is equal to the Yosida approximation of
\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}},
that is,

(\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}-(\mathrm{I}+\lambda\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}(\boldsymbol{x})),\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{H}.

Then from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1, Chapter 2]
, we observe that the multi-valued operator
\Phi:=N_{\mathcal{K}}
is a maximal monotone operator with
\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathrm{D}(\Phi)=\mathrm{D}(\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})=\mathcal{K}.
Also, from
[
6
, Chapter 1, pp. 30]
since
\mathrm{A}
is single-vlaued maximal monotone operator in
\mathbb{H}
, thus from
[
5
, Chapter IV, Proposition 1.1, part(iv)]
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y},(\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}})_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}))\geq 0,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}),\ \lambda>0.

Thus the multi-valued operator
N_{\mathcal{K}}=\partial\mathrm{I}_{\mathcal{K}}
satisfies all the assumptions (H1)-(H3) of Hypothesis
2.2
(see Subsections
5.2
-
5.3
for further examples).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T>0
and assume that
\Phi\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
satisfies Hypothesis
2.2
. Let
\boldsymbol{y}_{0}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})\cap\mathrm{D}(\Phi)
and
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
. For
d=2,3
with
r\in[3,\infty)
and
d=r=3
with
2\beta\mu\geq 1
, there exists a unique strong solution

(2.10)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}\in{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H})}\cap\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{V})\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{r+1}(0,T;\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{3(r+1)})\cap\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(0,T;\mathbb{V}),

such that in
\mathbb{H}

(2.11)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{y}(t)}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}(t)+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))+\Phi(\boldsymbol{y}(t))&\ni\boldsymbol{f}(t),\ \text{ a.e. }\ t\in[0,T],\\
\boldsymbol{y}(0)&=\boldsymbol{y}_{0}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

.

1.) Theorem
2.4
holds true if one replaces

by

in

.

2.) Using condition

3.
Abstract results

In this section, we first prove the
m
-accretivity of a single-valued and multi-valued operator. Then, we show abstract result (Proposition
3.5
) by using the abstract theory available in
[
5
,
6
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
d=2,3
with
r>3
, define the operator
\mathcal{G}(\cdot):\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{H}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\cdot)=\mu\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}(\cdot)+\beta\mathcal{C}(\cdot),

where
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{G})=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}:\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{H}\}.
Then
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is
m
-accretive in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
for some
\kappa\geq\varrho,
where

(3.12)

\displaystyle\varrho=\frac{r-3}{2\mu(r-1)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{\beta\mu(r-1)}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r-3}}.

Proof.

We shall first show that
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is a monotone operator for
\kappa\geq\varrho>0
. Then we will show that
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is coercive and demicontinuous, which imply the
m
-accretivity of the operator
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
. The proof is divided into the following four steps:

Step I:

\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is monotone for some
\kappa>0
.
We estimate
\langle\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}-\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle
by using an integration by parts as

(3.13)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}-\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle=\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Note that
\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle=0
which implies along with Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities that

(3.14)

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\boldsymbol{z}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

We take the term
\|\boldsymbol{z}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
from (
3.14
) and use Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities to estimate it as (see
[
30
]
also)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{2}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)-\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{2}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)-\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{\frac{4}{r-1}}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)-\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{\frac{2(r-3)}{r-1}}\/\mathrm{d}\/x

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{r-1}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)-\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r-1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{y}(x)-\boldsymbol{z}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x}}\right)^{\frac{r-3}{r-1}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq{\beta\mu}\||\boldsymbol{z}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{r-3}{r-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{2}{\beta\mu(r-1)}}}\right]^{\frac{2}{r-3}}\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

for
r>3
. Using (
3
) in (
3.14
), we find

(3.16)

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\||\boldsymbol{z}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\varrho\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

where
\varrho=\frac{r-3}{2\mu(r-1)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{2}{\beta\mu(r-1)}}}\right]^{\frac{2}{r-3}}.
From (
2.4
), we easily have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\beta\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\||\boldsymbol{z}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Combining (
3.13
) and (
3.16
)-(
3.17
), we conclude that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y})-(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+(\kappa-\varrho)\|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

for
\kappa\geq\varrho
and
r>3
. Thus
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is monotone.

Step II:

\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is demicontinuous.
Let us take a sequence
\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\to\boldsymbol{y}
in
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1},
so that
\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For any
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
, we consider

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{F}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-(\mathcal{F}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\mu\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle-\beta\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle+\kappa(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}-\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z}).

Note that

\displaystyle|\mu\langle\mathrm{A}(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}-\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle+\kappa(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}-\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z})|\leq\mu\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{y}_{n}-\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\kappa\|\boldsymbol{y}_{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
, since
\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\to\boldsymbol{y}
strongly in
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
. We estimate the term
|\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|
by using the Hölder’s and interpolation inequalities

\displaystyle|\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n},\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{2(r+1)}{r-1}}}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{2(r+1)}{r-1}}}}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{r-3}{r-1}}\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{\frac{2}{r-1}}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{r-3}{r-1}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{\frac{2}{r-1}}}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\to 0,\ \text{ as }\ n\to\infty,

since
\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\to\boldsymbol{y}
strongly in
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
and
\boldsymbol{y}^{n},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
. We estimate the term
|\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|
using the Taylor’s formula (
[
18
, Theorem 7.9.1]
) as

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\theta<1}r\|(\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y})|\theta\boldsymbol{y}^{n}+(1-\theta)\boldsymbol{y}|^{r-1}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{r+1}{r}}}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}

\displaystyle\leq r\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|\boldsymbol{y}^{n}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}}}\right)^{r-1}\|\boldsymbol{z}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty,

since
\boldsymbol{y}_{n}\to\boldsymbol{y}
strongly in
\mathbb{V}\cap{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}
and
\boldsymbol{y}_{n},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}
. From the above convergences, it is immediate that
\langle(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}^{n})-(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{z}\rangle\to 0
, for all
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
.
Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}:\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}\to\mathbb{V}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{r+1}{r}}
is demicontinuous and hence it is hemicontinuous.

Step III:

\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is coercive.
We consider

\displaystyle\frac{\langle(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{y}\rangle}{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\beta\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}+\kappa\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{2}}}\geq\frac{\min\{\mu,\beta,\kappa\}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}-1}}\right)}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{2}}},

where we have used the fact that
x^{2}\leq x^{r+1}+1,
for
x\geq 0
and
r\geq 1.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}\to\infty}\frac{\langle(\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I})(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{y}\rangle}{\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}}=\infty,

and it shows that the operator
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is coercive.

Step IV:

\mathcal{F}(\cdot):=\mathcal{G}(\cdot)+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is
m
-accretive in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
. Let us define an operator

\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})+\kappa\boldsymbol{y},

where
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F})=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}:\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})\in\mathbb{H}\}.
Note that the space
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
is reflexive. Since
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is monotone, hemicontinuous and coercive from
\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}
to
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}+\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\frac{r+1}{r}}
, then by an application of
[
16
, Example 2.3.7]
, we obtain that
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is maximal monotone in
\mathbb{H}
with domain
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F})\supseteq\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}).
In fact, we shall prove that
\mathcal{F}
is
m
-accretive for
\kappa
sufficiently large with
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}).
Let us consider the operators for some
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
as

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{1}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\mu(1-\delta_{1})\mathrm{A}+\beta(1-\delta_{2})\mathcal{C}(\cdot),

(3.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\mu\delta_{1}\mathrm{A}+\mathcal{B}(\cdot)+\beta\delta_{2}\mathcal{C}(\cdot)+\kappa\mathrm{I},

where
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F}^{1})=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}:\mathcal{F}^{1}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{H}\}
and
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F}^{2})=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}:\mathcal{F}^{2}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{H}\}.
Taking the inner product with
\boldsymbol{y}
in (
3.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu(1-\delta_{1})\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\beta(1-\delta_{2})\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\leq(\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y}),\boldsymbol{y})\leq\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}},

so that

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

Taking the inner product with
\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}
in (
3.20
) and using equality (
1.3
), we get

\displaystyle\mu(1-\delta_{1})\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\beta(1-\delta_{2})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\||\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{4(r-1)}{(r+1)^{2}}\||\nabla|\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r+1}{2}}|\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}}\right]=(\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y}),\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}).

Therefore, we have

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\ \text{ which implies }\ \mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F}^{1})\subseteq\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}).

Moreover, by using Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2r}
, we infer

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(1-\delta_{1})\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+C\beta(1-\delta_{2})\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{2r}}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(1-\delta_{1})\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+C\beta(1-\delta_{2})\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(1-\delta_{1})\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+C\beta(1-\delta_{2})\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{r}+C\beta(1-\delta_{2})\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{r},

which gives
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F}^{1})\supseteq\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
and therefore
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})=\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F}^{1}).

Similarly, taking the inner product with
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})
in (
3.20
), we find

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta(1-\delta_{2})}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

For
r>3
, similar to (
3
), we estimate
\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}
using Hölder’s inequality as follows:

(3.25)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\||\boldsymbol{y}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{4}{r-1}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{2(r-3)}{r-1}}.

Note that
(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(-\Delta\boldsymbol{y}(x))\cdot|\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{y}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x
. Using the estimate (
3.22
) and the equality (
1.3
) in (
3.25
), we find

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}),\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y})}}\right]^{\frac{2}{r-1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{1}{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]^{\frac{r-3}{r-1}}.

Therefore, we estimate
\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}
as

(3.26)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\|\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{1}{r-1}}\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{1}{r-1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{1}{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]^{\frac{r-3}{2(r-1)}}.

Using the estimates (
3.23
)-(
3.24
) in (
3.26
), then using Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{\beta\mu(1-\delta_{1})(1-\delta_{2})}}}\right]^{\frac{1}{r-1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}}{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}}}\right]^{\frac{r-3}{2(r-1)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\delta_{1})}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{1}{\beta(1-\delta_{2})}}}\right]^{\frac{1}{r-1}}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{r+1}{2(r-1)}}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\frac{r-3}{2(r-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta_{1}}{1-\delta_{1}}\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}+C_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\mu,\beta}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}},

where
C_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\mu,\beta}=\frac{r-3}{2(r-1)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1-\delta_{1}}{\mu^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\beta(1-\delta_{2})}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{r-3}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{r+1}{2\delta_{1}(r-1)}}}\right)^{\frac{r+1}{r-3}}
.
Now using the estimates (
3.23
)-(
3.24
) and (
3
) in (
3.21
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{2}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\delta_{1}\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\beta\delta_{2}\|\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\kappa\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{2\delta_{1}}{1-\delta_{1}}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{2}}}}\right]\|\mathcal{F}^{1}(\boldsymbol{y})\|_{\mathbb{H}}+(C_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\mu,\beta}+\kappa)\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

Let us choose
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
in such a way that
\rho=\frac{2\delta_{1}}{1-\delta_{1}}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{2}}<1,
for example, one can choose

,
\delta_{2}=\frac{1}{5}
, so that

Then by the well-known perturbation theorem for nonlinear

-accretive operators (
[
5
, Chapter II, Theorem 3.5]
), we conclude that the operator
\mathcal{F}^{1}+\mathcal{F}^{2}
with the domain
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
is
m
-accretive in
\mathbb{H}
. Since
\mathcal{F}^{1}+\mathcal{F}^{2}=\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
, the operator
\mathcal{G}+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is
m
-accretive in
\mathbb{H}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

1. For

with

and

2. For

We estimate

Combining (
3.13
), (

) and (

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{y})-\mathcal{G}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2\mu}}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{z}(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{z})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\geq 0,

provided

. Moreover, other properties like demicontinuity and coercivity can be proved in similar way as
r>3
case (see the proof of Proposition

).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Phi\subset\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
be a maximal monotone operator satisfying Hypothesis
2.2
. Define the multi-valued operator
\mathfrak{A}:\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{A})\to\mathbb{H}
by

\mathfrak{A}(\cdot)=\mu\mathrm{A}+\mathcal{B}(\cdot)+\beta\mathcal{C}(\cdot)+\Phi(\cdot)+\kappa\mathrm{I},

with the domain
\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{A})=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{H}:\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{A}(\boldsymbol{y})\subset\mathbb{H}\}
. Then
\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{A})=\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})\cap\mathrm{D}(\Phi)
and
\mathfrak{A}
is a maximal monotone operator in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H},
where
\kappa
is as in Proposition
3.1
.

Furthermore, the following estimates holds

(3.30)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{w}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{w})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{w})+\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{w})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2})^{\vartheta},

for every
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}),\lambda>0
and

(3.31)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{w}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{w})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{w})+\xi\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2})^{\vartheta},

for every
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})\cap\mathrm{D}(\Phi)
and
\xi\in\Phi(\boldsymbol{w}),
where

(3.32)

\displaystyle\vartheta=\begin{cases}r,&\text{when}\ d=2\ \text{with}\ r\in(3,\infty),\\
\frac{r+3}{5-r},&\text{when}\ d=3\ \text{with}\ r\in(3,5),\\
3,&\text{when}\ d=r=3\ \text{with}\ 2\beta\mu\geq 1,\\
1,&\text{when}\ d=3\ \text{with}\ r\in[5,\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

It has been shown in Proposition
3.1
that the operator
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)=\mu\mathrm{A}+\mathcal{B}(\cdot)+\beta\mathcal{C}(\cdot)+\kappa\mathrm{I}
is maximal monotone with domain
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
in
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}.
Note that
\mathfrak{A}=\mathcal{F}+\Phi
implies
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})\cap\mathrm{D}(\Phi)\subseteq\mathrm{D}(\mathfrak{A})
and since
\mathfrak{A}
is the sum of two monotone operators, it is monotone. In order to prove
\mathfrak{A}
is maximal monotone, we need to show that

(3.33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{I}+\mathfrak{A})=\mathbb{H}.

Step I:

Well-posedness of the Yosida approximated problem.
Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{H}
be arbitrary but fixed. We approximate the inclusion problem

(3.34)

\boldsymbol{y}+\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y})+\Phi(\boldsymbol{y})+\kappa\boldsymbol{y}\ni\boldsymbol{f},

by the equation

(3.35)

\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}+\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\kappa\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{f},

where
\Phi_{\lambda}
is the Yosida approximation of
\Phi
.
By the properties of Yosida approximation,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is demicontinuous and monotone (see
[
5
, Chapter 2, Proposition 1.3]
). Therefore the sum
\mathcal{F}(\cdot)+\Phi_{\lambda}(\cdot)
is maximal monotone (see
[
6
, Chapter 2, Corollary 1.1]
). This guarantees the existence of a solution
\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A})
for (
3.35
).
Let
\widetilde{\kappa}=\kappa+1.
Then (
3.35
) can be written as

(3.36)

\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\widetilde{\kappa}\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{f}.

We shall now prove the uniqueness. Let
\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}
be two solutions of the equation (
3.36
) with the same data
\boldsymbol{f}
and let
\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}.
Then we have

(3.37)

\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda})+\beta(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}))+\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda})+\widetilde{\kappa}\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{0}.

Taking the inner product with
\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}
in (
3.37
), we get

\displaystyle\mu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}),\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda})+\widetilde{\kappa}\|\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

(3.38)

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}),\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda})-\beta(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}),\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}).

By similar calculations as in (
3.16
) and (
2.4
), we obtain

(3.39)

\displaystyle-(\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda})-\beta(\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})+\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda})),\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda})\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\varrho\|\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},

By
[
6
, Chapter 2, Proposition 1.3, part (i)]
, we know that
\Phi_{\lambda}
is monotone, so that
(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})-\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}),\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda})\geq 0
for any

. Therefore, we conclude from (

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{\kappa}-\varrho}}\right)\|\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq 0.

Since

, we get
\boldsymbol{w}_{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{0}
and thus
\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{z}_{\lambda}.

Step II:

Uniform bounds for
\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}
.
Let us take the inner product with

in (

) to get

since
(\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}),\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})=0
. As the operator

is monotone with

By applying Young’s inequality and by

, we have

-(\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{0}),\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})\leq\|\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{0})\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{\kappa}}\|\Phi(\boldsymbol{0})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}}{4}\|\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Then equation (
3.40
) yields

(3.43)

\|\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}),\ \text{ for all }\ \lambda>0,

does not depend on
\lambda
. Taking the inner product of (

\mathrm{A\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}},
we get

(3.44)

By

For

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\||\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}|\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\varrho\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2})+\begin{cases}\varsigma\|\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},&\text{ for }d=2\text{ with }r\in(3,\infty)\text{ and }d=3\text{ with }r\in(3,5),\\

Uniform bounds for

Taking the inner product with

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\widetilde{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})).

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}),\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda})|

\displaystyle=|b(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda},\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda},\Phi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{y}_{\lambda}))|

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz, interpolation and Young’s inequalities, (

) and (

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}(t)=

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{f}(t)+\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}(t)+\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))

\displaystyle-(-\boldsymbol{f}(t)+\mu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}(t)+\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))+\beta\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{y}(t))+N_{\mathcal{K}}(\boldsymbol{y}(t)))^{0},\ \ \ \ \text{for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T],

The enstrophy of the flow is an important quantity which determines the rate of dissipation of kinetic energy (see

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x.

For the incompressible flow, one can express the enstrophy in the following form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{y}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{\omega}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

where
\boldsymbol{\omega}:=\nabla\times\boldsymbol{y}
is the vorticity vector. We consider the constraint set

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{V}:\|\nabla\times\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}=\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\varpi\}.

Let
\boldsymbol{g}
be any arbitrary element of
\mathcal{K}
such that

\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}

\displaystyle\|\nabla\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\lambda\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\mathbb{H}

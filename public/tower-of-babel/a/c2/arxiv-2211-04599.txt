Stability with respect to domain of the low Mach number limit of compressible heat-conducting viscous fluid Thanks: This research was supported by the grant Sonata Bis UMO-2020/38/E/ST1/00469, National Science Centre, Poland
Aneta Wróblewska-Kamińska

Abstract
. We investigate the asymptotic limit of solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with the Mach number proportional to a small parameter ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , the Froude number proportional to ε \sqrt{\varepsilon} and when the fluid occupies large domain with spatial obstacle of rough surface varying when ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . The limit velocity field is solenoidal and satisfies the incompressible Oberbeck–Boussinesq approximation. Our studies are based on weak solutions approach and in order to pass to the limit in a convective term we apply the spectral analysis of the associated wave propagator (Neumann Laplacian) governing the motion of acoustic waves.

Introduction and formulation of the problem

The Oberbeck–Boussinesq approximation is a mathematical model of a stratified flow, where the fluid is assumed to be incompressible and yet convecting a diffusive quantity creating positive and negative buoyancy force. Then the system of equations reads:

(OB

(OB

(OB
3

2.
Primitive system

In the beginning of this section let us introduce some standard notation.
We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

By

The Sobolev space of functions which derivatives are integrable up to order

We start our considerations with a ”primitive system” - the rescaled Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with a small Mach and Froude number which consists of: the continuity equation (conservation of mass), the momentum equation, the entropy balance and the total energy balance respectively

(NSF
{}_{\varepsilon}^{1}
)

\partial_{t}\varrho_{\varepsilon}+{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})=0,

(NSF
{}_{\varepsilon}^{2}
)

\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})+{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(\varrho_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\nabla_{x}p(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})={{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}F_{\varepsilon},

(NSF
{}_{\varepsilon}^{3}
)

\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon}))+{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(\varrho_{\varepsilon}s(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})+{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{q}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon})}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\sigma_{\varepsilon},

(NSF
{}_{\varepsilon}^{4}
)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}e(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\varrho_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}\right)dx=0.

Where the viscous stress tensor satisfies the Newton rheological law and heat flux is determined by the Fourier law:

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})=\mu(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\left(\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}^{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{2}{3}{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Id}}\right)+\eta(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}){{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{Id}}

\boldsymbol{q}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon})=-\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}

with a positive heat coefficient
\kappa
and for the entropy production rate holds:

(2.1)

\quad\sigma_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\left({\varepsilon^{2}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}_{\varepsilon}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}_{\varepsilon}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\right).

The unknowns are the fluid mass density
\varrho_{\varepsilon}=\varrho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
, the velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}(t,x):(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

and absolute temperature
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}=\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(t,x):(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The pressure
p
, the specific internal energy

e
and the specific entropy
s
are given scalar valued functions of
\varrho
and
\vartheta
which are related through
Gibbs’ equation
\vartheta Ds=De+pD({1}/{\varrho}).

The system is supplemented with complete slip boundary conditions for velocity field and the boundary of physical space is thermally isolated, i.e.

(2.2)

\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=0,\quad[\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon},\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})\boldsymbol{n}]\times\boldsymbol{n}=0,\quad\quad\boldsymbol{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}|_{\partial{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}=0.

Small parameter

2.1.
Structural restrictions

In order to be able to use the existence result of
[
7
]
and later to build uniform estimates,
we need to impose structural restrictions on the thermodynamical functions
p
,
e
,
s
as well as on
the transport coefficients
\mu
,
\eta
,
\kappa
. Following
[
7
]
(where the reader can find more detailed description and physical motivations) we set

(2.3)

\begin{split}&p(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})=\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{5/2}P\left(\frac{\varrho_{\varepsilon}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{3/2}}\right)+\frac{a}{3}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{4},\ a>0,\mbox{ where }P\in C^{1}[0,\infty)\cap C^{2}(0,\infty),\\
&P(0)=0,\quad P^{\prime}(Z)>0\mbox{ for all }Z\geq 0,\end{split}

(2.4)

\begin{split}&0<\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-P^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}<c\mbox{ for all }Z>0,\quad\lim\limits_{Z\to\infty}\frac{P(Z)}{Z^{5/3}}=P_{\infty}>0,\\
&\mbox{ and }\partial_{\varrho}p(\varrho,\vartheta)>0.\end{split}

Accordingly to Gibbs’ relation, the specific internal energy and the entropy can be written in the following forms

(2.5)

e(\varrho,\vartheta)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\vartheta^{5/2}}{\varrho}P\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{3/2}}\right)+a\frac{\vartheta^{4}}{\varrho},\quad\partial_{\vartheta}e(\varrho,\vartheta)>0,\quad\mbox{ is positive and bounded,}

(2.6)

s(\varrho,\vartheta)=S\left(\frac{\varrho}{\vartheta^{3/2}}\right)+\frac{4}{3}a\frac{\vartheta^{3}}{\varrho},\quad\quad S^{\prime}(Z)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{5}{3}P(Z)-ZP^{\prime}(Z)}{Z^{2}}\mbox{ for all }Z>0.

The transport coefficients:
\mu
- shear viscosity,
\eta
- bulk viscosity and
\kappa
- heat conductivity are assumed to be continuously differentiable
functions of the temperature
\vartheta\in[0,\infty)
satisfying the following growth conditions for all
\vartheta\geq 0
and some positive constants
\underline{\mu}
,
\overline{\mu}
,
\overline{\eta}
,
\underline{\kappa}
,
\overline{\kappa}
:

(2.7)

\begin{split}&0<\underline{\mu}(1+\vartheta)\leq\mu(\vartheta)\leq\underline{\mu}(1+\vartheta),\quad\quad 0\leq\eta(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\eta}(1+\vartheta),\\
&0<\underline{\kappa}(1+\vartheta^{3})\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\overline{\kappa}(1+\vartheta^{3}).\end{split}

2.2.
Equilibrium state and ill-prepered initial data

Let us assume that outer force
F
is defined on whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and is independent of
\varepsilon
. The so-called equilibrium state (static state) for each scaled NSF
ε
system consist of static density
\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}
and constant temperature distribution
\overline{\vartheta}

satisfying (for a convenience we consider a static density
\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}
defined on the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
)

\nabla_{x}p(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon},\overline{\vartheta})=\varepsilon\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}F_{\varepsilon}\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\quad\mbox{ where }\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\overline{\varrho}.

Hence we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\varrho}=\frac{\varepsilon}{P^{\prime}(\overline{\varrho})}F+\varepsilon^{2}h_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon},\mbox{ with }P^{\prime}(\varrho)=\frac{1}{\varrho}\partial_{\varrho}p(\varrho,\overline{\vartheta}),\ \|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}<c\\
&\mbox{ and }|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\varepsilon c|\nabla_{x}F_{\varepsilon}(x)|\mbox{ for }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\end{split}

(notice that the above properties gives closeness of static density
\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}
and constant state
\overline{\varrho}
).
Since we work with weak solutions based on energy estimates and control of entropy production rate we need to assume that initial data are close to equilibrium state.
Namely initial density and initial temperature are of the following form

(2.9)

\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}=\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)},\quad\quad\vartheta_{0,\varepsilon}=\overline{\vartheta}+\varepsilon\vartheta_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)}

where

\overline{\vartheta}>0
is positive constants characterising the static distribution
of the absolute temperature and

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\|\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq c,\ \int\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\,{\rm d}x=0,\ \|\vartheta_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq c,\ \int\vartheta_{0,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\,{\rm d}x=0,\\
&\ \|\boldsymbol{u}_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq c\mbox{ for all }\varepsilon\in(0,1].\end{split}

The above uniform bounds will allow to control right hand side of total dissipation balance which is a source of uniform estimates needed to perform the limit system.
Nevertheless, such a choice allow to consider nontrivial dynamics but on the other hand it causes oscillations in acoustic equation. Those will be eliminated by dispersive estimates.

Main result

We say that functions
\boldsymbol{U}

\begin{split}{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\boldsymbol{U}&=0\mbox{ a.e. on }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\

-\int_{\Omega}\overline{\varrho}\boldsymbol{U}_{0}\cdot\varphi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}x+&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}:\nabla_{x}\varphi-r\nabla_{x}F\cdot\varphi\right)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

for any

, where
{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,\varphi=0
and

Moreover

\begin{split}\overline{\varrho}c_{p}(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta})\left[\partial_{t}\Theta+{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(\Theta\boldsymbol{U})\right]&-{{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(\kappa(\overline{\vartheta})\nabla_{x}\Theta)-\overline{\varrho}\overline{\vartheta}\alpha(\overline{\varrho},\overline{\vartheta}){{\mathrm{div}}_{x}}\,(F\boldsymbol{U})=0\\
&\mbox{ a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega\\

3.1.
Uniform bounds

All uniform bounds stated below may be seen as a direct consequence of total dissipation balance and more detailed reasoning may be found in

To begin with, according to these references, we introduce essential and residual part of a measurable function

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\rm{res}}}=(0,\infty)^{2}\setminus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\rm{ess}}}.

The total dissipation balance reads then

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)(t)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})-(\varrho_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\frac{\partial H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon},\overline{\vartheta})}{\partial\varrho}-H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon},\overline{\vartheta})\right)(t)\,{\rm d}x\\
&+\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sigma_{\varepsilon}\left[[0,t]\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\right]\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}|\boldsymbol{u}_{0,\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x\\
&+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon},\vartheta_{0,\varepsilon})-(\varrho_{0,\varepsilon}-\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\frac{\partial H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon},\overline{\vartheta})}{\partial\varrho}-H_{\overline{\vartheta}}(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon},\overline{\vartheta})\right)\,{\rm d}x,\end{split}

where

Two variational problems in Kähler geometry
László Lempert

Abstract
. On a Kähler manifold ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) we consider the problems of maximizing/minimizing Monge–Ampère energy over certain subsets of the space of Kähler potentials. Under suitable assumptions we prove that solutions to these variational problems exist, are unique, and have a simple characterization. We then use the extremals to construct hermitian metrics on holomorphic vector bundles, and investigate their curvature.

Introduction

The pair of variational problems studied in this paper goes back to two sources. One has to do with
constructing hermitian metrics on holomorphic vector bundles; we will discuss this aspect in section 8.
The other source is certain problems in convex geometry: For example, given a
bounded set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

0

[
J48
]
, see also
[
B97
]
. The result has
also been associated with Löwner’s name. There is a dual problem as well, maximizing the volume
of ellipsoids inscribed in a convex set; either problem can be easily reduced to the other.

The problems have natural complex variants. For example, consider a convex
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
and among all hermitian ellipsoids—images of the unit ball under complex linear transformations—that
are contained in

problem, and Arocha, Bracho, and Montejano in
[
A+22
]

solution. Related earlier work includes Gromov’s, who in
[

]
already dealt with the problem when

[

]
by Rabotin and Ma.
It is noteworthy, though, that a variant of the

Above we mentioned in passing that hermitian ellipsoids in

[

of Killing vector fields that vanish somewhere.) By results of Lichnerowicz and Kobayashi,
[

]

to which we will come shortly, such actions always admit a moment map. In conclusion, the
variational problems for constant scalar curvature potentials are special cases of (1.2), (1.3).

If ellipsoids and Kähler metrics of constant scalar curvature are analogous, what corresponds in the

Background

This section reviews notions that will be needed further down.

Spaces of plurisubharmonic functions.
Suppose
N

\Omega

real
(1,1)
form,
d\Omega=0

on every open

\Omega
can be written as
i\partial\bar{\partial}w

smooth,
the function

w+f
is plurisubharmonic. The collection of such functions is denoted Psh
(\Omega)
.
For continuous functions
f

\Omega
-plurisubharmonicity is equivalent to
\Omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}f\geq 0

as a current.

Suppose
(M,\omega)
is an
n

\text{Psh}(\omega)
and its subspaces (such as

in (1.1)) have a rich geometry, see

[
D15
,
D17
,
D19
]
. A central notion
is geodesics. After Berndtsson and Darvas, they are defined as follows
[
B18
,
D19
]
.
Let
a<b
be real numbers,
S_{ab}=\{s\in\mathbb{C}:a<\text{Re}\,s<b\}

\pi:S_{ab}\times M\to M
the projection.

Definition 2.1
.

(a) A path (i.e., a continuous map)
\varphi:(a,b)\to\text{Psh}(\omega)
is a subgeodesic if
\Phi:S\times M\to[-\infty,\infty)

\Phi(s,x)=\varphi(\text{Re}\,s)(x)

(b) The geodesic
\varphi:(a,b)\to\text{Psh}(\omega)

is

(2.1)

\varphi=\sup\{\psi\mid\psi:(a,b)\to\text{Psh}(\omega)\text{ is a subgeodesic, }\lim_{a}\psi\leq u,\lim_{b}\psi\leq v\}.

In (2.1) the pointwise limits exist because for subgeodesics

are convex functions for all

\varphi(a)=u

\varphi(b)=v

The geodesics of relevance to this paper are all smooth maps into
{\mathcal{H}}\subset\text{Psh}(\omega)

This class of geodesics had been introduced by Mabuchi in
[

]
rather differently:

{\mathcal{H}}\subset C^{\infty}(M)

Mabuchi defines a Riemannian metric

\text{Vol}=\int_{M}\omega^{n}

(=\int_{M}\omega_{u}^{n}

g_{M}(\xi,\eta)=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{M}\xi\eta\,\omega_{u}^{n},\qquad\xi,\eta\in T_{u}{\mathcal{H}}\approx C^{\infty}(M).

The metric determines a unique Levi–Civita connection

\dot{\varphi}:[a,b]\to T{\mathcal{H}}

\nabla_{\dot{\varphi}(t)}\dot{\varphi}(t)=0

That Mabuchi’s geodesics are geodesics in the sense of Definition 2.1 follows from Semmes’ work

[
S92
]
. Semmes rediscovered Mabuchi’s connection, and proved that a smooth
\varphi:[a,b]\to{\mathcal{H}}

is a geodesic for this connection if and only if the function

\Phi:\bar{S}_{ab}\times M\ni(s,x)\mapsto\varphi(\text{Re}\,s)(x)\in\mathbb{R}

satisfies the Monge–Ampère equation

(\pi^{*}\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi)^{n+1}=0,\qquad\text{or, equivalently,}\qquad\text{rk}\,(\pi^{*}\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi)\equiv n.

Setting

If

Suppose a geodesic, or even a subgeodesic

Then the associated function

Proposition 2.3
.

Indeed, the length of the path

Mabuchi further introduced a closed smooth 1-form, say

The zero level set

is a bijection between

Proposition 2.4
.

The map

{\mathcal{H}}\ni u\mapsto\big(u-E(u),E(u)\big)\in{\mathcal{K}}\times\mathbb{R}

is a Riemannian isometry if

is endowed with the Euclidean metric (see

.

The only geodesics needed in the paper are of the following type. Let
{\mathcal{L}}
stand for Lie derivative, and
let
J:TM\to TM
be the almost complex structure tensor of
M
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose a real vector field
Y
on
M
vanishes somewhere and generates a one parameter group

g_{t}
of biholomorphisms. If

{\mathcal{L}}_{JY}\omega=0
,
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfies

\int_{M}h\,\omega^{n}=0
,
then with any

(2.5)

\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}h\,d\tau\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi(t)=\varphi(t)+ct

\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{K}}
,
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

First observe that

(2.6)

i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(t)=i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}i\partial\bar{\partial}h\,d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}_{Y}\omega\,d\tau=g_{t}^{*}\omega-\omega.

The differential of Monge–Ampère energy evaluated on the velocity vector

Hence

for all

\varphi(t)\in{\mathcal{K}}
. Mabuchi
and Donaldson already
studied paths in

[

implies that

[

is also

The moment map.
Now let
(M,\omega)
be a connected compact symplectic manifold of dimension

2n
. Contraction with tangent vectors

(2.7)

TM\ni\theta\mapsto-\iota(\theta)\omega\in T^{*}M,\quad\text{resp.}\quad\mathbb{C}TM\ni\theta\mapsto-\iota(\theta)\omega\in\mathbb{C}T^{*}M,

induces isomorphisms between the tangent
and cotangent bundles of
M
on the one hand, and their complexifications on the other. The
symplectic gradient of a smooth
h:M\to\mathbb{C}
is the complex vector field
\text{sgrad}\,h=\text{sgrad}_{\omega}\,h

that corresponds to

dh
under (2.7). Vector fields that arise in this way are called Hamiltonian. If
h
is real valued,

\text{sgrad}\,h
is a real vector field whose flow preserves
\omega
.

Consider a smooth left action of a Lie group
G
on
M

(2.8)

G\times M\ni(g,x)\mapsto gx\in M.

The differential of the above map at

\mathfrak{g}
, the Lie
algebra of
G
, to Vect
(M)
,
the Lie algebra of smooth real vector fields on
M
. Denote the vector field that corresponds to

by
X_{M}
. If
G
acts by symplectomorphisms, the flow of these vector fields preserves

\omega
,
0={\mathcal{L}}_{X_{M}}\omega=d\iota(X_{M})\omega
. Assuming that
\iota(X_{M})\omega
is not
only closed but exact, we can write
-\iota(X_{M})\omega=dh_{X}
, or
X_{M}=\text{sgrad}\,h_{X}
with some

h_{X}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, that is unique under the condition
\int_{M}h_{X}\,\omega^{n}=0
. If such
h_{X}

exists for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, the moment map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is defined by

\langle\mu(x),X\rangle=h_{X}(x),\qquad x\in M,\quad X\in\mathfrak{g};

it is equivariant in the sense that
\mu\circ g=\text{Ad}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\,\mu
, see e.g.
[
GS84’
, pp. 186–187]
.

Assume now that
(M,\omega)
is even Kähler, and the action of

acts by a holomorphic isometry, and there is a good criterion,
due to Lichnerowicz and Kobayashi, for the existence of the moment map. Denote by

J
the complex structure tensor of

can be decomposed

(1,0)

(2.9)

Y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(Y-iJY),\quad Y^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(Y+iJY);\quad\text{if }Y\text{ is real, }Y=2\text{Re}\,Y^{\prime}=2\text{Re}\,Y^{\prime\prime}.

That the vector fields
X_{M}
generate one parameter groups of holomorphic isometries means that

is a Killing vector field, and

is a holomorphic section of the
(1,0)
tangent bundle

[

Proposition 2.6
.

A holomorphic
(1,0)
vector field

on

with some smooth

if and only if
\sigma(Z)=0
for all holomorphic
(1,0)
-forms
\sigma
on
M
.
\text{Re}\,Z
is Killing if and
only if

can be chosen real; then
\text{Re}\,Z=\text{sgrad}\,h/2
.

Proof.

See
[
L67
]
,
[
K72
, pp. 94–95]
. In Kobayashi’s notation the
(0,1)
-form
\zeta

corresponding to the vector field

is an imaginary constant times
\iota(Z)\omega
.

Since
\sigma(Z)
above is a holomorphic function on

, hence constant, it follows that
\sigma(Z)=0

whenever
Z
(or
\text{Re}\,\,Z
) vanishes somewhere. Conversely, if
\text{Re}\,Z=\text{sgrad}\,h
with a real
h
, then

Z=0
where
h
is maximal. (A fuller result of this sort is
[
LS94
, Theorem 1]
by LeBrun and Simanca.) Hence:

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose a connected Lie group
G
acts by holomorphic isometries on a connected compact Kähler
manifold
M
. The action admits a moment map if and only if the vector fields
X_{M}
vanish somewhere,
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
or,
equivalently, for a family of
X\in\mathfrak{g}
that spans
\mathfrak{g}
.

For example,
\text{SU}(n+1)
acts naturally by holomorphic isometries on

, endowed with the
Fubini–Study metric. The vector fields

are real parts of holomorphic sections of

,
and have zeros. We conclude that the action has a moment map. By contrast, if

is a torus with
translation invariant Kähler form, the only holomorphic section of

that has a zero is the
zero section. Indeed, it is easily seen directly that only trivial actions will have a moment map (
\equiv 0
).

Assume the
G
-action on the connected
compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
, by holomorphic isometries,
admits a moment map
\mu
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and

, then

(2.10)

i\partial\bar{\partial}h={\mathcal{L}}_{JX_{M}}\omega.

Proof.

By definition,
X_{M}=-\text{sgrad}\,h/2

dh/2=\iota(X_{M})\omega=\iota(X^{\prime}_{M})\omega+\iota(X_{M}^{\prime\prime})\omega

Separating
(1,0)

\bar{\partial}h=2\iota(X_{M}^{\prime})\omega

\displaystyle 0=d\big(2\iota(X_{M}^{\prime})\omega-\bar{\partial}h\big)

\displaystyle=d\,\iota(X_{M})\omega-\sqrt{-1}d\,\iota(JX_{M})\omega-\partial\bar{\partial}h

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{X_{M}}\omega-\sqrt{-1}{\mathcal{L}}_{JX_{M}}\omega-\partial\bar{\partial}h.

Since

, (2.10) follows.

Complexifications.

Assume
G
is compact and connected. Then the action (2.8) extends to a holomorphic action

G^{\mathbb{C}}\times M\ni(g,x)\mapsto gx\in M

of the complexification of

We will need the following structure theorem:

Theorem 2.9
.

G^{\mathbb{C}}
is a Stein manifold and the map

G\times\mathfrak{g}\ni(g,X)\mapsto g\exp iX\in G^{\mathbb{C}}

is a diffeomorphism.

Proof.

We can assume that
G
is a subgroup of a unitary group
{\rm U}(N)
. In the construction of

in
[

]
we can take

the restriction of the standard representation
{\rm U}(N)\to\text{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})

and the discussion after (8.2) there then identifies

with an affine variety, the zero set

V(I)\subset\text{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})\subset\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
of

of holomorphic polynomials. In particular,

is Stein. Since

is maximally real in

and invariant under the operation of passing to the

is also invariant.
Hence the second statement of the theorem is a special case of
[

.

Lemma 2.10
.

If the
G
-action on
(M,\omega)
admits a moment map
\mu
, then
X_{M}
has a zero for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

If
X=Y+iZ
with

, then
X_{M}^{\prime}=\text{sgrad}\,^{\prime}\big(\langle\mu,Y\rangle+i\langle\mu,Z\rangle\big)
.
By
[
LS94
, Theorem 1]
, this implies that
X^{\prime}_{M}
vanishes somewhere, and so does

.

Proposition 2.11
.

Assume that the
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
M
is effective:
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
X_{M}=0
imply
X=0
. Assume also that
the
G
-action on
(M,\omega)
has a moment map. If
\gamma\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
\gamma^{*}\omega=\omega
, then

\gamma\in G
.

Proof.

Write
\gamma=g\exp iX
with
g\in G
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
(Theorem 2.9). Thus
(\exp iX)^{*}\omega=\omega
.
By Proposition 2.10
X
vanishes at some
y\in M
, hence
y
is fixed by all
g_{s}=\exp sX
,
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Choose
local coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
centered at

so that

\omega=i\sum_{j}dx_{j}\wedge d\bar{x}_{j}
at

. Passing to the Jacobian matrix defines a homomorphism

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\partial g_{t}/\partial(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|_{0}\in\text{U}(n).

The induced homomorphism of Lie algebras associates with

an element
\tilde{X}\in\mathfrak{u}(n)

arrange the coordinates so that
\tilde{X}
is diagonal, diag
(i\lambda_{1},\dots,i\lambda_{n})

, first for

whence each

. But then

.

3.
Existence of extremals

From now on
(M,\omega)
is an
n
dimensional connected compact Kähler manifold, on which a connected compact
Lie group
G
acts by holomorphic isometries. The action admits a moment map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and extends
to a holomorphic action of the complexification
G^{\mathbb{C}}
on
M
. Without loss of generality
we assume that the induced action of

\mathfrak{g}
is effective, if
X_{M}=0
for some
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
X=0
. The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given a bounded
v_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
, among admissible
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
v\leq v_{0}
, there is at least
one which maximizes
Monge–Ampère energy
E(v)
; and among admissible
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
v\geq v_{0}
, there is at least one
that minimizes
E(v)
.

We need two auxiliary results.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, and
\omega_{v}=(\exp iX)^{*}\omega
. Let
g_{t}=\exp itX
and

h=-2\langle\mu,X\rangle
. Then

\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}h\,d\tau\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi(t)=\varphi(t)+E(v)t

define geodesics
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{K}}
,
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{H}}
. For each
t
,
\varphi(t),\psi(t)
are admissible and
\psi(1)=v
.

Proof.

All claims but the last follow by combining Propositions 2.5, 2.8, and (2.6). As to the last, (2.6) gives

\omega_{\psi(1)}=(\exp iX)^{*}\omega=\omega_{v}
, whence
\psi(1)=v+\text{const}
. The constant has to
be
0
because
E\big(\psi(1)\big)=E(v)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle K=

\displaystyle\{g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}:g^{*}\omega=\omega_{v}\text{ with }v\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ such that }a\leq E(v),\,\max_{M}v\leq b\},

(3.2)

\displaystyle L=

\displaystyle\{g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}:g^{*}\omega=\omega_{v}\text{ with }v\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ such that }a\leq\min_{M}v,\,E(v)\leq b\}

are compact subsets of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

We start by defining a norm on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Delta=2\,\text{tr}_{\omega}i\partial\bar{\partial}
denote the Laplacian on

C^{\infty}(M,\omega)
. If
X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h_{X}=-2\langle\mu,X\rangle
, by Proposition 2.8

i\partial\bar{\partial}h_{X}={\mathcal{L}}_{JX_{M}}\omega
. Taking trace with respect to
\omega
, we obtain

\Delta h_{X}=2\,\text{tr}_{\omega}{\mathcal{L}}_{JX_{M}}\omega
, hence
with the Green operator
\Gamma:C^{\infty}(M,\omega)\to C^{\infty}(M,\omega)

(3.3)

h_{X}=\Gamma(2\,\text{tr}_{\omega}{\mathcal{L}}_{JX_{M}}\omega).

Let

||X||=\int_{M}|h_{X}|\omega^{n}.

This is indeed a norm, for if
||X||=0
, then
h_{X}
and
X_{M}=-\text{sgrad}\,h_{X}/2
vanish, whence
X=0
.

Now to the compactness of
K,L
. Write an arbitrary
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
as
g=\gamma\exp iX
,
\gamma\in G
,

X\in\mathfrak{g}
(Theorem 2.9). Thus
g^{*}\omega=(\exp iX)^{*}\omega
. If
\omega_{v}=g^{*}\omega
then
X
,
i.e.
h_{X}
, can be estimated in terms of
\min_{M}v
,
\max_{M}v
, and
E(v)
as follows. Let
g_{t}=\exp itX
,
and with

h_{X}
defined in (3.3),
\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}h_{X}\,d\tau+E(v)t
. By Proposition 3.2 this is a geodesic.
In particular,
\psi(\cdot)(x)
is convex for
x\in M
, whence

h_{X}+E(v)=\dot{\psi}(0)\leq\psi(1)-\psi(0)\leq\dot{\psi}(1)=g_{1}^{*}h_{X}+E(v).

Since
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(1)=v
by Proposition 3.2, we obtain

(3.4)

h_{X}\leq v-E(v)\leq g_{1}^{*}h_{X}.

Suppose
g\in K
. Then (3.4) implies
h_{X}\leq b-a
; with
h^{+}=\max(h_{X},0)

||X||=\int_{M}|h_{X}|\,\omega^{n}=\int_{M}(2h^{+}-h_{X})\,\omega^{n}=\int_{M}2h^{+}\omega^{n}\leq 2(b-a)\int_{M}\omega^{n}.

Similarly, if
g\in L
, and
h^{-}=\max(-h_{X},0)
, then (3.4) gives
\min_{M}h_{X}=\min_{M}g_{1}^{*}h_{X}\geq a-b
, and

||X||=\int_{M}|h_{X}|\,\omega^{n}=\int_{M}(2h^{-}+h_{X})\,\omega^{n}=\int_{M}2h^{-}\omega^{n}\leq 2(b-a)\int_{M}\omega^{n}.

These estimates show that the closed sets
K,L\subset G^{\mathbb{C}}
are contained in the compactum

\Big\{\gamma\exp iX:\gamma\in G,\,X\in\mathfrak{g},\,||X||\leq 2(b-a)\int_{M}\omega^{n}\Big\},

therefore themselves are compact.

Proof of Proposition 3.1.

Since
G^{\mathbb{C}}
leaves the de Rham class of
\omega
invariant, for each
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
there is a unique

u=U(g)
that solves

(3.5)

g^{*}\omega-\omega=i\partial\bar{\partial}u,\qquad\int_{M}u\,\omega^{n}=0.

As in the previous proof, the map
U:G^{\mathbb{C}}\to C^{\infty}(M)
can be expressed through the Green operator

\Gamma
as

U(g)=\Gamma\big(2\,\text{tr}_{\omega}(g^{*}\omega-\omega)\big),

which shows that
U
is continuous. Let
m(g)=\min_{M}\big(v_{0}-U(g)\big)
. Suppose

g^{*}\omega=\omega_{v}
. Then
v\leq v_{0}
if and only if
v=U(g)+m(g)+c
with a constant
c\leq 0
. As,
in general,
E(w+c)=E(w)+c
, maximizing
E(v)
among admissible
v\leq v_{0}
reduces to maximizing

(3.6)

E\big(U(g)+m(g)\big),

a continuous function, over
G^{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
a=\inf_{M}v_{0}
and
b=\sup_{M}v_{0}
. Since the constant
function
a\leq v_{0}
is admissible, maximizing (3.6) over
G^{\mathbb{C}}
amounts to the same as maximizing over

K
, see (3.1); and of course, the maximum over this compact set
is attained. The existence of the minimizer
in the second statement follows similarly.

4.
Characterization of the extremals

We continue with the setup of section 3. To investigate extremals of problems (1.2), (1.3),
it will be convenient to be able to change the Kähler form
\omega
to
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{w}
with

w\in{\mathcal{H}}
. We start the section by discussing how notions involved in Theorems 1.2, 1.3 transform as
we pass from
\omega
to
\omega^{\prime}
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{H}}(\omega^{\prime})=\{u\in C^{\infty}(M):\omega^{\prime}_{u}=\omega^{\prime}+i\partial\bar{\partial}u>0\}
. Consider the map

(4.1)

{\mathcal{H}}\ni v\mapsto v^{\prime}=v-w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}.

One checks that
\omega^{\prime}_{v^{\prime}}=\omega_{v}
, and that (4.1) is an isometry in Mabuchi’s metric. Denote
Monge–Ampère energy on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
by
E^{\prime}:{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
. Given
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
, let
\psi:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{H}}
be a smooth
path connecting
w
with
v
, and let
\psi^{\prime}(t)=\psi(t)-w
. Continuing to denote
t
-derivatives by a dot,

(4.2)

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(v^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dE^{\prime}\big(\dot{\psi}^{\prime}(t)\big)\,dt=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\dot{\psi}^{\prime}(t)\big(\omega^{\prime}_{\psi^{\prime}(t)}\big)^{n}\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\dot{\psi}(t)\omega_{\psi(t)}^{n}\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}dE\big(\dot{\psi}(t)\big)\,dt=E(v)-E(w).

Hence finding extremals of
E
and
E^{\prime}
is one and the same thing.

Next suppose
w\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible,
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{w}=\gamma^{*}\omega
with some

\gamma\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
.
The action

(4.3)

G^{\mathbb{C}}\times M\ni(g,x)\mapsto\gamma^{-1}g\gamma\,x\in M,

and its restriction to
G
, will be referred to
as the modified action. If
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible, i.e.,
\omega_{v}=g^{*}\omega
with
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
, then

\omega^{\prime}_{v^{\prime}}=\omega_{v}=(\gamma^{-1}g)^{*}\omega^{\prime}
shows that
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}

is also admissible; and
the converse holds as well.

The map
\gamma:(M,\omega^{\prime})\to(M,\omega)
is an isomorphism of Kählerian
G^{\mathbb{C}}
-spaces, if

(M,\omega^{\prime})
is endowed with the modified action (4.3)
(and
(M,\omega)
with the original action).
In particular, the modified action of
G
is by isometries of
(M,\omega^{\prime})
. It follows that the moment map
of the modified action is
\mu^{\prime}=\mu\circ\gamma
. If
\gamma\in G
, then
\mu^{\prime}=\text{Ad}_{g^{-1}}^{*}\,\mu
by equivariance.
To summarize:

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
w\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible,
\omega_{w}=\gamma^{*}\omega
,
\gamma\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
. The map

{\mathcal{H}}\ni v\mapsto v-w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is an isometry that sends Monge–Ampère energy
E
on
{\mathcal{H}}
to Monge–Ampère
energy
E^{\prime}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
, plus a constant. Let

v_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
and
v^{\prime}_{0}=v_{0}-w
. An admissible
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
maximizes Monge–Ampère energy
E(v)

among admissible
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
v\leq v_{0}
, if and only if
u^{\prime}=u-w
maximizes
E^{\prime}(v^{\prime})
among
admissible
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}\leq v_{0}^{\prime}
. For minimizers of
E
over
\{v\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ admissible }:v\geq v_{0}\}

a corresponding statement holds.

Furthermore, the moment maps
\mu:(M,\omega)\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\mu^{\prime}:(M,\omega^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for the original and modified

G
-actions (see (4.3)) are related by
\mu^{\prime}=\mu\circ\gamma
. If
\gamma^{*}\omega=\omega
, then

\gamma\in G
(by Proposition 2.11), and

\mu^{\prime}=\text{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}}^{*}\,\mu
.

Now suppose
v_{0}\in C(M)
. If
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
, let
C_{u}=\{x\in M:u(x)=v_{0}(x)\}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible,
\omega_{u}=\gamma^{*}\omega
with
\gamma\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
. If
u=v

maximizes
E(v)
among admissible
v\leq v_{0}
, or if
u=v
minimizes
E(v)
among admissible

v\geq v_{0}
, then the convex hull of
\mu(\gamma C_{u})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
contains
0
.

Proof.

Consider first the case when
u=0
. When
u
minimizes,
v_{0}\leq 0
, when
u
maximizes,
v_{0}\geq 0
.
Since
\omega=\omega_{u}=\gamma^{*}\omega
, by Proposition 4.1

\mu\circ\gamma=\text{Ad}_{\gamma^{-1}}^{*}\,\mu
. Hence the convex hull of

\mu(\gamma C_{u})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
contains
0

if and only if the convex hull of
\mu(C_{u})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
contains
0
.
Suppose this latter does not contain
0
. Then there is a linear form

l:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
l\big(\mu(x)\big)<0
for all
x\in C_{0}
. Such a form
l
is evaluation at some

X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus

(4.4)

h=-2\langle\mu,X\rangle>0\qquad\text{on }C_{0},

and in fact
U=\{x\in M:h(x)>\varepsilon\}
is a neighborhood of
C_{0}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Let
g_{t}=\exp itX
and

\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}h\,d\tau
. By Proposition 3.2

E\big(\varphi(t)\big)=0
and
\varphi(t)
is admissible for all
t
. Fix a compact neighborhood
K\subset U
of
C_{0}
.
Since
h|U>\varepsilon
, for small
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in K

\varphi(t)(y)\geq\varepsilon t\text{ if }t>0,\qquad\varphi(t)(y)\leq\varepsilon t\text{ if }t<0.

If
u
minimizes (maximizes), then

\varphi(0)(x)=0>v_{0}(x)
, respectively
\varphi(0)(x)=0<v_{0}(x)
, when

x\in M\setminus\text{int}\,K
. It follows that for small
t
and on all of
M

\displaystyle\varphi(t)>v_{0},\quad\text{ if }t>0\text{ and }u=0\text{ minimizes},

\displaystyle\varphi(t)<v_{0},\quad\text{ if }t<0\text{ and }u=0\text{ maximizes.}

The inequalities continue to hold if a small constant
c
is added to
\varphi(t)
. But this contradicts
the extremality of
u=0
, since
\varphi(t)+c
is also admissible, and
E\big(\varphi(t)+c\big)=c=E(u)+c
.

Second, consider a general extremal
u
. Let
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{u}
,

v_{0}^{\prime}=v_{0}-u
, and
E^{\prime}:{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
the Monge–Ampère energy as in Proposition 4.1 (but with

). Since
the contact sets
\{u=v_{0}\}
and
\{0=v_{0}^{\prime}\}
agree, Proposition 4.1 and the special case we just proved
imply the necessity of the condition in Proposition 4.2 in general.

Next we prove sufficiency.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible,
\omega_{u}=\gamma^{*}\omega
with
\gamma\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
; and the

\mu(\gamma C_{u})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}

contains
0
. If
u\leq v_{0}
, then
u=v
maximizes
E(v)
among all admissible
v\leq v_{0}
. If
u\geq v_{0}

and
v_{0}\in\text{Psh}(\omega)
, then

minimizes

among all admissible

.

The convex hull condition is equivalent to the existence of a Borel probability measure

(even one with finite

such that

(4.5)

\int_{C_{u}}(\mu\circ\gamma)\,dm=0.

Proof.

Again it suffices to prove under the assumption
u=0
and
\gamma=e
; the general case will follow in
light of Proposition 4.1. Suppose
v\in{\mathcal{H}}
is another admissible potential,
\omega_{v}=g^{*}\omega
.
By Theorem 2.9
g
can be taken of form
g=\exp iX
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.
With
h=-2\langle\mu,X\rangle
, by Proposition 3.2

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\exp i\tau X)^{*}h\,d\tau+E(v)t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,

defines a geodesic connecting
0
with
v
. By convexity (Proposition 2.2), if

then

\psi(t)\leq v_{0}
; if
0,v\geq v_{0}
then
\psi(t)\geq v_{0}
, for

. Also
\psi(0)(x)=0
when

.

In the first case of the proposition it follows that
0\geq\dot{\psi}(0)(x)=h(x)+E(v)
for
x\in C_{0}
. But (4.5) implies

\int_{C_{0}}h\,dm=0
, and so

E(u)=0\geq\int_{C_{0}}\big(h+E(v)\big)\,dm=E(v),

as claimed. In the second case we have
0\leq\dot{\psi}(0)(x)=h(x)+E(v)
for
x\in C_{0}
.
Therefore

E(u)=0\leq\int_{C_{0}}\big(h+E(v)\big)\,dm=E(v),

which completes the proof.

5.
Uniqueness of extremals

Let
(M,\omega)
, the
G
- and
G^{\mathbb{C}}
-actions, and the moment map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be as in sections 3, 4.
With
X\in\mathfrak{g}
put

N_{X}=\{x\in M:\langle\mu,X\rangle\text{ vanishes to second order at }x\},

and introduce the assumption

(5.1)

\text{If }X\in\mathfrak{g}\setminus\{0\},\text{ the convex hull of }\{\mu(x):x\in N_{X}\}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}\text{ avoids }0.

This latter is the same as requiring that under some linear map
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
the image of
\mu(N_{X})
be
on one side of
0\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus (5.1) is equivalent to

(5.2)

\text{If }X\in\mathfrak{g}\setminus\{0\},\text{ there is a }Y\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ such that }\langle\mu,Y\rangle>0\text{ on }N_{X}.

Here are two examples. We use dagger
†
to indicate adjoint of a matrix.
The standard action of
G=\text{SU}(n+1)
on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
descends to an action
on projective space
M=\mathbb{P}_{n}=\big(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\big)/\sim
, preserving the Fubini–Study form

\omega
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
consists of traceless skew adjoint matrices
X
,
X^{\dagger}=-X
. By
Kirwan
[
K84
, Lemma 2.5]
,
for example, the pull back
\tilde{\mu}
of the moment map
\mu:\mathbb{P}_{n}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the projection

\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}_{n}
is

(5.3)

\langle\tilde{\mu}(z),X\rangle=-i\dfrac{z^{\dagger}Xz}{z^{\dagger}z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\text{ a column vector},\,X\in\mathfrak{g},

up to a positive constant factor that is determined by the normalization of the Fubini–Study metric.

Assumption (5.2) is satisfied. It suffices to verify this when
X
is diagonal with diagonal entries

ia_{0},\dots,ia_{n}\in i\mathbb{R}
,
not all
0
. The simultaneous vanishing of
\langle\tilde{\mu}(z),X\rangle=\sum a_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}/\sum|z_{j}|^{2}
and

d\langle\tilde{\mu}(z),X\rangle(JX_{M})=

\partial_{t}|_{t=0}\langle\tilde{\mu}\big((\exp itX)z\big),X\rangle=2\,\frac{\sum a_{j}^{2}|z_{j}|^{2}\sum|z_{j}|^{2}-(\sum a_{j}|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}{(\sum|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}

implies
a_{j}z_{j}=0
for all
j
. Say,
a_{0}\neq 0
. Then
x=(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n})\in N_{X}
implies
x_{0}=0
, and
so the diagonal matrix
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
with entries
-in,i,\dots,i
satisfies
\langle\mu,Y\rangle>0
on
N_{X}
.

As a second example, consider the unit sphere
S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with the inherited conformal, hence
complex, structure and rotation invariant Kähler form (=Fubini–Study form, if
S^{2}
is identified
with
\mathbb{P}_{1}
). Let
M=(S^{2})^{n}
and
\omega
the product Kähler form on
M
. The group

G=\text{SO}(3)
acts on
M
diagonally by rotations. If
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is suitably
identified with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the moment map is

\mu(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\approx\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad(x_{j})\in M,

see
[
K84
, 2.8]
, and
\langle\mu,X\rangle
is the composition of
\mu
with a linear
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
. Its critical
points occur where each
x_{j}\in S^{2}
is an extremum point of

. This means each

must agree
with one of a fixed pair of antipodal points in
S^{2}
. If

is odd then

on the critical set,

, and (5.1) is satisfied. If

is even then

is nonempty, and along with any

x
it contains

as well. Since
\mu(x)+\mu(-x)=0
, (5.1) fails.

Now to the uniqueness in problems (1.2), (1.3). Let
v_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume (5.1) (or equivalently (5.2)). There is a unique
v
that maximizes

among
admissible
v\leq v_{0}
. If
v_{0}\in C^{2}(M)
and

v
that minimizes

among admissible

.

Later we will need that in the minimization problem a more liberal if slightly technical condition also

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume (5.1). Consider a

,
\omega_{u}=\gamma^{*}\omega
with some

Proof of Propositions 5.1, 5.2.

In Proposition 5.1 the existence of extremals is the content of Proposition 3.1. As to uniqueness, the statement of
Proposition 5.1 about minimizers follows from Proposition 5.2. Indeed, Proposition 4.2 implies that if in the scenario of
Proposition 5.1 an admissible

minimizes energy, then there is a Borel probability measure
m
on
C_{u}

everywhere,
Proposition 5.2 applies and gives uniqueness.

This leaves us with proving uniqueness of maximizers in Proposition 5.1, and Proposition 5.2. We will refer to the former
as Case A, to the latter as Case B. In what follows, let
u,\bar{u}\leq v_{0}
be two maximizers in Case A,

E(u)=E(\bar{u})
; or
u
as in
Case B and
\bar{u}\geq v_{0}
admissible,

.

In light of Proposition 4.1 we can assume

and, in case B,
\gamma=e
.
Let
\omega_{\bar{u}}=g^{*}\omega
,
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
. By Theorem 2.9 we can also assume

g=\exp iX
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. With
g_{t}=\exp itX
and
h=-2\langle\mu,X\rangle
,

(5.4)

\psi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\tau}^{*}h\,d\tau+tE(\bar{u}),\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,

defines a geodesic in
{\mathcal{H}}
,

and
\psi(1)=\bar{u}
(Proposition 3.2).
By convexity, Proposition 2.2,
\psi(t)\leq v_{0}
in Case A and
\psi(t)\geq v_{0}
in Case B.
In Case A

m
on

.

Let us start with Case A. With any

In case B, if
y\in C_{0}

\psi(\cdot)(y)
has a minimum at

, so

for

–almost every

.
Pick a smooth function

,

, solves

in a neighborhood of
(0,y)\in\bar{S}_{01}\times M
.
The leaf of the associated Monge–Ampère foliation
[
BK77
]
through

contains the graph
of a smooth function

, where

is a connected (relative) neighborhood of

(w+v_{0})\big(f(s)\big)\leq w\big(f(s)\big)+\Psi\big(s,f(s)\big)=(\pi^{*}w+\Psi)\big(s,f(s)\big),

But then (5.5), with equality instead of inequality, gives

With the claim proved, we compute

In particular,

For example:

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that for some nonzero

the convex hull of

.
Then there is a

.

Proof.

Write

.

Since

According to Atiyah, Guillemin, and Sternberg

[

6.
Stability of extremals, extrema

We continue in the setup of section 5. In view of Proposition 5.1, with any
v_{0}\in C(M)
one can associate
the unique admissible
u_{-}=U_{-}(v_{0})
that
maximizes
E(v)
among admissible
v\leq v_{0}
. Similarly, if we denote by
C_{\text{unique}}\subset C(M)
the
set of
v_{0}
such that
E
has a unique minimizer among admissible
v\geq v_{0}
, with each
v_{0}\in C_{\text{unique}}

we can associate this unique minimizer
u=U(v_{0})
. For example, if the assumptions of Proposition 5.1 are
met,
{\mathcal{H}}\subset C_{\text{unique}}
.
First we record a stability property of
u_{-},u
; then of the minimum value in (1.3).

Proposition 6.1
.

The maps

U_{-}:C(M)\to{\mathcal{H}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad U:C_{\text{unique}}\to{\mathcal{H}}

are continuous, when their domains are endowed with the topology of uniform convergence and
{\mathcal{H}}\subset C^{\infty}(M)

with its natural Fréchet topology.

Proof.

Since the proofs for
U_{-}
and
U
are almost identical, we will write out the proof only for
U
. Suppose

v_{0},v_{1}\in C_{\text{unique}}
, and

\max_{M}|v_{0}-v_{1}|=\delta
. Thus
U(v_{1})\geq v_{1}\geq v_{0}-\delta
, whence the admissible potential

U(v_{1})+\delta
is
\geq v_{0}
. Therefore

E\big(U(v_{1})\big)+\delta=E\big(U(v_{1})+\delta\big)\geq E\big(U(v_{0})\big).

Exchanging the roles of
v_{0},v_{1}
, we obtain
\big|E\big(U(v_{0})\big)-E\big(U(v_{1})\big)\big|\leq\delta
, and so

E\circ U
is continuous.

Now take a sequence of
v_{j}\in C_{\text{unique}}
,
j=1,2,\dots
, converging uniformly to
v_{0}\in C_{\text{unique}}
. Thus

\omega_{U(v_{j})}=g_{j}^{*}\omega
with some
g_{j}\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
,
j=0,1,\dots
. To prove
\lim_{j}U(v_{j})=U(v_{0})

we first assume
g_{j}\to g_{0}
. By Theorem 2.9
g_{j}=\gamma_{j}\exp iX_{j}
with
\gamma_{j}\in G
,
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}
, and

X_{j}\to X_{0}
. Since
(\exp iX_{j})^{*}\omega=\omega_{U(v_{j})}
, by Proposition 3.2

U(v_{j})=-2\int_{0}^{1}(\exp i\tau X_{j})^{*}\langle\mu,X_{j}\rangle\,d\tau+E\big(U(v_{j})\big).

This indeed shows that
U(v_{j})\to U(v_{0})
.

Without assuming
g_{j}\to g_{0}
we can argue like this. Suppose
U(v_{j})\not\to U(v_{0})
. Thus
U(v_{0})

has a neighborhood

{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
that infinitely many
U(v_{j})
avoid. By compactness, Proposition 3.3, at the price of passing to a
subsequence, we can arrange that the corresponding
g_{j}
converge. By what we have already proved, this implies
that the
U(v_{j})
converge to some admissible
u\neq U(v_{0})
. However,
u\geq\lim_{j}v_{j}=v_{0}
and

E(u)=\lim_{j}E\big(U(v_{j})\big)=E\big(U(v_{0})\big)
; this contradicts uniqueness of the minimizer.

In section 9 we will need a coarser stability property of the minimum value itself in (1.3). For any upper bounded

v_{0}:M\to[-\infty,\infty)
let

I(v_{0})=\inf\{E(v):v\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ is admissible, }v\geq v_{0}\}\in[-\infty,\infty).

Proposition 6.2
.

If a sequence of upper semicontinuous
v_{k}:M\to[-\infty,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, decreases to
v_{0}
, then

I(v_{k})
decrease to
I(v_{0})
.

Proof.

The definition implies that
I(v_{k})\geq I(v_{0})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, form a decreasing sequence; call its limit
L\geq I(v_{0})
.
Suppose
u\geq v_{0}
is admissible. Then the upper semicontinuous functions
\max(v_{k},u)
decrease to
u
,
perforce uniformly by Dini’s theorem. Thus, if
\varepsilon>0
there is a
k
such that
v_{k}\leq u+\varepsilon
, whence

L\leq I(v_{k})\leq E(u+\varepsilon)=E(u)+\varepsilon
.
This being true for all
\varepsilon>0
and for all admissible
u\geq v_{0}
,
L\leq I(v_{0})
follows, and the proof is complete.

7.
Polarized manifolds

With
(M,\omega)
,
G
, and
G^{\mathbb{C}}
as before we now consider a holomorphic line bundle
L\to M
with a smooth
hermitian metric
h^{0}
. The curvature
\Theta(h^{0})
is a closed form, and we assume
\omega=i\Theta(h^{0})
. We also assume
that the action of each
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
on
M
can be lifted to
\tilde{g}:L\to L
; thus
\tilde{g}
is holomorphic and maps
each
L_{x}
linearly onto
L_{gx}
. This section is about a reformulation of the variational problems (1.2), (1.3) in this
setting. The material, when
L
is the hyperplane section bundle over projective space, will be needed in sections 8, 10.

Any two lifts
L\to L
of
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
are multiples of one another by a holomorphic function on
M
, hence by a nonzero
constant. All lifts
of all
g\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
form a group
H^{\mathbb{C}}
, an extension of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
by
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, the multiplicative
group of nonzero complex numbers. While
H^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of an extension
H
of
G
by
S^{1}
, we will not need
this fact. We can let
H^{\mathbb{C}}
act on
M
as well, via the projection
H^{\mathbb{C}}\to G^{\mathbb{C}}
.

For each
g\in H^{\mathbb{C}}
there is a
W(g)\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(7.1)

g^{*}h^{0}=e^{-W(g)}h^{0}.

(In this formula, as usual,
W(g)
is viewed as a function on
L
, constant on the fibers.) Thus

0<i\Theta(g^{*}h^{0})=i\Theta(h^{0})+i\partial\bar{\partial}W(g)=\omega_{W(g)},

W(g)\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and
W
is a smooth map
H^{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
. Since
i\Theta(g^{*}h^{0})=ig^{*}\Theta(h^{0})=g^{*}\omega
, it follows
that
W(g)
is admissible in the sense of Definition 1.1. The converse is also true:

Proposition 7.1
.

v\in{\mathcal{H}}
is admissible if and only if it is of form
W(g)
, where
g\in H^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

What remains to prove is the “only if” statement. Note that if
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\subset H^{\mathbb{C}}
then

e^{-W(\lambda g)}h^{0}=(\lambda g)^{*}h^{0}=g^{*}\lambda^{*}h^{0}=|\lambda|^{2}g^{*}h^{0}=|\lambda|^{2}e^{-W(g)}h^{0}.

Hence
W(\lambda g)=W(g)-\log|\lambda|^{2}
. Suppose that
v
is admissible,
\omega_{v}=g_{0}^{*}\omega

with some
g_{0}\in G^{\mathbb{C}}
. Choose
g_{1}\in H^{\mathbb{C}}
that projects to
g_{0}
. By (7.1)

\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}v=g_{1}^{*}\omega=i\Theta(g_{1}^{*}h^{0})=i\Theta(e^{-W(g_{1})}h^{0})=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}W(g_{1}),

whence
W(g_{1})-v=c
is constant. It follows that
W(e^{c/2}g_{1})=W(g_{1})-c=v
.

Definition 7.2
.

We say that hermitian metrics
h,h^{0}
on
L
are similar, or are in the same similarity class, if
h=g^{*}h^{0}
with some

g\in H^{\mathbb{C}}
. In this case we put
{\mathcal{E}}(h^{0},h)=E\big(W(g)\big)=E\big(\log(h_{0}/h)\big)
.

An arbitrary metric
\bar{h}
on
L
is of form
e^{-\bar{v}}h^{0}
, with
\bar{v}

Lemma 7.5

E\circ W:H^{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R}

Proof.

If

W(g_{1}g_{2})=W(g_{2})+g_{2}^{*}W(g_{1}).

Now consider
g\in H^{\mathbb{C}}
and a smooth path
\gamma:[0,1]\to H^{\mathbb{C}}
, starting at the neutral element. Let

\varphi(t)=W\big(\gamma(t)\big)
and
\psi(t)=W\big(\gamma(t)g\big)=W(g)+g^{*}W\big(\gamma(t)\big)
.
Thus
\dot{\psi}(t)=g^{*}\dot{\varphi}(t)
as functions on
M

\dfrac{d}{dt}E\big(W(\gamma(t)g)\big)=dE\big(\dot{\psi}(t)\big)=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{M}\dot{\psi}(t)\omega_{\psi(t)}^{n}\\
=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{M}\big(g^{*}\dot{\varphi}(t)\big)\big(\gamma(t)g\big)^{*}\omega^{n}=\dfrac{1}{\text{Vol}}\int_{M}\dot{\varphi}(t)\gamma(t)^{*}\omega^{n}=\dfrac{d}{dt}E\big(W(\gamma(t))\big).

is constant. Setting

Since

8.
Hermitian metrics on vector bundles

In the Introduction we suggested that one of the motivations
for the variational problems (1.2), (1.3) had to do with hermitian metrics on vector bundles. This comes from a
fundamental construction in geometry that associates with a, say, holomorphic vector bundle

F\to B
a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)\to\mathbb{P}F
. Here we use the convention that

\mathbb{P}F=\bigcup_{b\in B}\mathbb{P}F_{b}
is the set of one
codimensional subspaces
\Sigma
in various fibers of
F

F
induce the
structure of a complex manifold fibered over
B
; and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)
is a holomorphic line bundle whose fiber over

\Sigma\subset F_{b}
is
F_{b}/\Sigma
. Thus
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)
restricts to
\mathbb{P}F_{b}
as the hyperplane section bundle.
In this section we will write

\pi:\mathbb{P}F\to B

for the fibering.

A hermitian metric on a vector space
V
induces a hermitian metric on any quotient of
V
. Applying this construction
fiberwise, we can associate to a hermitian metric
h_{F}
on the vector bundle
F
a hermitian metric
h_{\mathbb{P}}
on the
line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)
. We will refer to
h_{\mathbb{P}}
as induced by
h_{F}
. Note that the restrictions
h_{\mathbb{P}}|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)
for
b\in B

are of Fubini–Study type, hence positively curved.
The question is whether there is a natural converse
construction, that associates a hermitian metric on
F
to a hermitian metric
k
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)
. We will be interested
only in
k
whose restrictions to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)
are positively curved.

One such construction is as follows. All hermitian metrics on
F
are induced from one another: If
h^{0}_{F},h_{F}
are such, and

b\in B
, then
h_{F}|F_{b}=g^{*}h^{0}_{F}|F_{b}
with some

. The corresponding metrics
h^{0}_{\mathbb{P}},h_{\mathbb{P}}

on
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)
are related by the induced action of

on
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)

\Sigma\subset F_{b}
is a hyperplane, the fiber of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)
over
\Sigma
, i.e.,
F_{b}/\Sigma
, consists of translates

\Sigma^{\prime}\subset F_{b}
of
\Sigma
. Since

h_{\mathbb{P}}(\Sigma^{\prime})=\min_{\zeta\in\Sigma^{\prime}}h_{F}(\zeta)=\min_{\zeta\in\Sigma^{\prime}}h^{0}_{F}(g\zeta)=\min_{\zeta\in g\Sigma^{\prime}}h^{0}_{F}(\zeta)=h^{0}_{\mathbb{P}}(g\Sigma^{\prime}),

we have

(8.1)

h_{\mathbb{P}}|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)=g^{*}h^{0}_{\mathbb{P}}|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1),\qquad g\in\text{GL}(F_{b}).

We are in the situation of section 7, with
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)\to M=\mathbb{P}F_{b}
,
G=\text{SU}(h^{0}_{F}|F_{b})
and
G^{\mathbb{C}}=\text{SL}(F_{b})
acting
on
\mathbb{P}F_{b}
,

acting on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)

and

are similar in the sense of Definition 7.2, and clearly the converse is also
true: Given a metric

on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F}(1)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}F_{b}}(1)

,

on

. Moreover,

is uniquely determined by

: If

\zeta\in F_{b}
and

is a hyperplane, write

for the equivalence class of

mod
\Sigma

) to

Hence, as established in section 7, given a smooth hermitian metric

This association

\displaystyle=-1+\log\dfrac{e^{3}|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}}{|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}}.

In these formulae
x=(x_{0}:x_{1})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}

z\in A

Proposition 10.2
.

u_{1},u_{2}
are
\omega
-subharmonic on
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
u_{0}
is strictly
p^{*}\omega
-plurisubharmonic on
D_{0}
.

Proof.

With the projection
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}_{1}
, the first question is whether
\pi^{*}u_{1},\pi^{*}u_{2}
are

\pi^{*}\omega
-plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
\pi^{*}\omega=i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\big(|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}\big)
,
the question is whether the functions

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{3}\log\dfrac{|x_{1}|^{2}}{2|x_{0}|^{2}}+\log\dfrac{3|x_{1}|^{2}}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3}\log(2|x_{0}|^{2})+\log\dfrac{3|x_{1}|^{4/3}}{2}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle-1+\log(e^{3}|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2})

are plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
: they obviously are.

To discuss
u_{0}
, we start with a formula for smooth functions
r,s
:

\partial\bar{\partial}\log(e^{r}+e^{s})=\dfrac{e^{r}\partial\bar{\partial}r+e^{s}\partial\bar{\partial}s}{e^{r}+e^{s}}+e^{r+s}\dfrac{\partial(r-s)\wedge\bar{\partial}(r-s)}{(e^{r}+e^{s})^{2}}.

Since
x_{0}\neq 0
on
D_{0}
, we can use the local coordinate
\xi=x_{1}/x_{0}
. At least when
\xi\neq 0

u_{0}(z,x)=-|z|^{2}+\log\dfrac{e^{3|z|^{2}}|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}}{|x_{0}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}}=-|z|^{2}+\log(e^{r}+e^{s})-\log(1+|\xi|^{2}),

where
r=3|z|^{2}
,
s=\log|\xi|^{2}
. Hence

\displaystyle p^{*}\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}u_{0}

\displaystyle=-idz\wedge d\bar{z}+\dfrac{3ie^{3|z|^{2}}\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}}{e^{3|z|^{2}}+|\xi|^{2}}+ie^{r+s}\dfrac{\partial(r-s)\wedge\bar{\partial}(r-s)}{(e^{r}+e^{s})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{i(2e^{3|z|^{2}}-|\xi|^{2})}{e^{3|z|^{2}}+|\xi|^{2}}dz\wedge d\bar{z}+ie^{r}\dfrac{\alpha\wedge\bar{\alpha}}{(e^{r}+e^{s})^{2}},

where
\alpha=3\bar{z}\bar{\xi}\,dz-d\xi
. By continuity, this formula holds when
\xi=0
, too. Above, the coefficients of

i\,dz\wedge d\bar{z}
and
i\alpha\wedge\bar{\alpha}
are positive in
D_{0}
, and the forms
dz
and
\alpha
are
everywhere linearly independent. It follows that
p^{*}\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}u_{0}
is indeed positive.

Proposition 10.4

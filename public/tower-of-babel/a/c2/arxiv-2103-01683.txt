Renormalization in a Landau-to-Coulomb interpolating gauge in Yang-Mills theory
A Andraši, J C Taylor

Abstract
The Coulomb gauge in QCD is the only explicitly unitary gauge. But it suffers from energy-divergnces which means that it is not rigorously well-defined. One way to define it unambiguously is as the limit of a gauge interpolating between the Landau gauge and the Coulomb gauge. This interpolating gauge is characterized by a parameter θ \theta and the Coulomb gauge is obtained in the limit θ → 0 \theta\rightarrow 0 . We study the renormalization of this θ \theta -gauge for all values of θ \theta , and note some special features of it. Pacs numbers: 11.15.Bt; 03.70.+k Keywords QCD, Coulomb gauge, renormalization



1
Introduction

The Coulomb gauge in non-abelian gauge theory deserves attention for a number of reasons. It is the only explicitly unitary gauge. It may be convenient for the study of heavy bound states. It has been used in investigations of confinement, see for example
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and references therein.

But, in perturbation theory at least, it suffers from ’energy divergences’, that is Feynman integrals which are divergent over the
time-components of the momenta, while the spacial components are held fixed.
The simplest such energy divergences occur at one loop. For pure YM theory, these are quite easily cancelled by combining Feynman
diagrams appropriately, but they are automatically removed by using the Hamiltonian, rather than the Lagrangian formalism
[
4
]
, so we use the Hamiltonian formalism in this paper.

More subtle, logarithmic, divergences appear first at two loop order. The cancellation of these was proved by Doust
[
6
]
(see also
[
7
]

and generalized in
[
5
]
).
The origin of these divergences was linked by Christ and Lee
[
8
]
with the problem of correctly ordering the factors in the
Coulomb potential in the Hamiltonian (see also
[
9
]
). But in this paper we consider only ordinary momentum-space Feynman perturbation theory,
in the manner of
[
6
]
.

Pure energy-divergences occur
at 2-loop order only, not at higher order (see
[
6
]
). But if ordinary UV divergences are combined with energy divergences, new problems occur at 3-loop order, from the insertion of UV divergent loops into
two-loop graphs
[
10
]
.
A difficulty in studying these matters is to be sure that the divergent integrals we are manipulating are well-defined.
To overcome this problem, we make use of a ’flow gauge’, which interpolates between a covariant gauge
and the Coulomb gauge. A flow gauge is characterized by a parameter
\theta
,
\theta=1
is the covariant
gauge, and the Coulomb gauge is defined by the limit
\theta\rightarrow 0
. For nonzero
\theta
, there are no energy divergences
in any Feynman integral.
In a previous paper
[
11
]
, we studied the flow gauge interpolating between the Feynman gauge and the Coulomb gauge. Let us call this the FC-flow gauge. The purpose of the present paper is to do the same for
a flow gauge interpolating between the Landau gauge and the Coulomb gauge: the LC-flow gauge.
This gauge has been used by some authors, for example
[
3
]
.

As in
[
11
]
, we use the Hamiltonian (phase space) formalism, as this eliminates linear divergences to one-loop order. The renormalization of the Lagrangian formalism is obtained as a special case (see section 6 below).
The renormalization for the LC-flow gauge is obtained by field scaling and mixing, just as in
the FC-flow gauge
[
11
]
. In the present paper we derive the values of the renormalization constants
in the LC-flow gauge, which are in general different from those in the FC-flow gauge for
\theta\neq 0
.

In section 2 we define the LC-flow gauge, and state the Feynman rules. In section 3 we state the general form of renormalization, by scaling and mixing of fields and of sources. In section 4 we give the values of the renormalization constants to one-loop order, and in section 5 we show an example of their derivation.
In section 6 we discus the transition from the first order to the second order formalism.
Section 7 is a summary.

2
The flow gauge

The total Lagrangian density for the Hamiltonian form of Yang-Mills theory is

L_{TOTAL}=L+L_{GF}+L_{G}=L_{C}+L_{GF}+L_{G}+L_{S},

(1)

being the classical, gauge-fixing, ghost and source parts.

L_{C}=-E^{ai}F^{a}_{0i}-H

(2)

where
a
is a colour index,
i
is a 3-vector index, and

F^{a}_{0i}=\partial_{0}A^{a}_{i}-\partial_{i}A^{a}_{0}+gf^{abc}A^{b}_{0}A^{c}_{i},

(3)

and
E^{ai}(x)
(the ’colour electric field’) is the conjugate momentum field to
A^{a}_{i}(x)
.

The Hamiltonian density is

H=-\frac{1}{2}E_{i}.E^{i}+\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}.F^{ij}

(4)

where we have suppressed colour indices, and use upper and lower indices although it is not covariant
(which explains the minus sign). The Lagrangian formalism would be obtained by expressing
L
in terms of the free field

\tilde{E}_{i}=E_{i}-F_{0i}.

(5)

The gauge-fixing term which we use in this paper is

L_{GF}=-\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}(\partial_{i}A^{i}+\theta^{2}\partial_{0}A^{0})^{2}.

(6)

The limit
\alpha\rightarrow 0
imposes the constraint that the following bracket is zero.
The parameter
\theta
dictates the flow, with
\theta=1
giving the Landau gauge and
\theta=0

the Coulomb gauge.

The terms involving the ghost
c
and anti-ghost
c^{*}
are

L_{G}=\partial_{i}c^{*}.[\partial^{i}c+gA^{i}\wedge c)]+\theta^{2}\partial_{0}c^{*}(\partial_{0}c+gA_{0}\wedge c).

(7)

The source part of the Lagrangian is

L_{S}=u^{i}.[\partial_{i}c+g(A_{i}\wedge c)]+u_{0}.[\partial_{0}c+g(A_{0}\wedge c)]+gv^{i}.(E_{i}\wedge c)-\frac{1}{2}gK.(c\wedge c),

(8)

Here
u^{i},u_{0},v^{i},K
are source fields used to impose the BRST
conditions. Except for
K
, these are fermionic (anti-commuting) like
c,c^{*}
. Colour indices are suppressed, and we use the abbreviation

(X\wedge Y)^{a}\equiv f^{abc}X^{b}Y^{c}.

(9)

The BRST condition is

\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta u^{i}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta A_{i}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta u^{0}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta A_{0}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta v^{i}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta E_{i}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta c(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta K(x)}\right]

\equiv\gamma*\gamma=0,

(10)

where

\gamma=\int d^{d}yL(y).

(11)

We will use the notation that the momentum
k=(k_{0},\textbf{K})
, K=|
K
|,
k^{2}=k_{0}^{2}-K^{2}
, and

\bar{K}^{2}\equiv\textbf{K}^{2}-\theta^{2}k_{0}^{2}.

(12)

We use
g_{\mu\nu}
for the Minkowski metric with
g_{00}=1
. In the gauge given by (6), the Coulomb propagator is

D_{00}=-\frac{K^{2}}{(\bar{K}^{2})^{2}},

(13)

and the ghost propagator is

D=+\frac{1}{\bar{K}^{2}}.

(14)

The transverse part of the
A_{i}

the propagator is

D^{T}_{ij}=\frac{1}{k^{2}+i\eta}\left[g_{ij}+\frac{K_{i}K_{j}}{K^{2}}\right]

(15)

and the longitudinal part is

D^{L}_{ij}=-\frac{\theta^{4}k_{0}^{2}}{(\bar{K}^{2})^{2}}\frac{K_{i}K_{j}}{K^{2}},

(16)

Since we use the Hamiltonian formalism, we require also propagators involving the electric field
\bf{E}
. It is

D^{mn}=\frac{K^{2}}{k^{2}+i\eta}\left[g^{mn}+\frac{K^{m}K^{n}}{\textbf{K}^{2}}\right],

(17)

which is transverse.
(We use indices
i,j,...
for the potential
\bf{A}
and indices
m,n,...
for
\bf{E}
).
There are also off-diagonal propagators. That between
E^{m}
and
A_{j}
has a transverse part

D_{j}^{Tm}=\frac{ik_{0}}{k^{2}+i\eta}\left[g^{m}_{j}+\frac{K^{m}K_{j}}{\bar{K}^{2}}\right],

(18)

and a longitudinal part

D^{Lm}_{j}=i\theta^{2}\frac{k_{0}}{\bar{K}^{2}}\frac{K^{m}K_{j}}{K^{2}}.

(19)

The off-diagonal propagator between
E_{m}
and
A_{0}
is

D^{m}_{0}=\frac{iK^{m}}{\bar{K}^{2}}.

(20)

Finally there is an off-diagonal propagator between
A_{i}
and
A_{0}

D_{0i}=-\frac{\theta^{2}k_{0}K_{i}}{(\bar{K}^{2})^{2}}.

(21)

In addition to the usual Yang-Mills vertex involving the spatial components
A_{i}
, there is an

E^{ma}A_{0}^{b}A_{i}^{c}
vertex

-gf^{abc}g^{i}_{m}.

(22)

Most of the above rules are the same as for the FC-flow gauge, as in our previous paper
[
11
]
.
The three exceptions are
D_{ij}^{L}
in (16),
D_{j}^{Lm}
in (19) and
D_{0i}
in (21).
(In general, these rules should be supplemented by a factor
[(2\pi)^{4}i]^{-1}
for each propagator and a factor
(2\pi)^{4}i
for each vertex; but these factors cancel in 1-loop graphs.)

Because of the constraint imposed by the gauge-fixing term (6), the propagators obey the following equations:

P^{i}D_{ij}+\theta^{2}p^{0}D_{0j}=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,P^{j}D_{0j}+\theta^{2}p_{0}D_{00}=0,

(23)

P^{i}D^{n}_{i}+\theta^{2}p^{0}D^{n}_{0}=0.

(24)

3
Renormalization

The structure of renormalization is as follows. Let the part
L
of the Lagrangian, defined in (1), be the function

L(A_{i},A_{0},E^{n};c,u^{i},u^{0},v_{n},K;g).

(25)

This is the physical, finite Lagrangian. Then the renormalized (bare) Lagrangian is the function

L_{R}=L(A_{R}^{i},A_{R}^{0},E_{R}^{n},c_{R},u_{R}^{i},u_{R}^{0},v_{R}^{n},K_{R};g_{R}),

(26)

where the renormalized fields and sources have the forms

A_{R}^{i}={Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{1/2}A^{i},\,\,\,\,A_{R}^{0}={Z^{\prime}}_{6}^{1/2}A_{0},\,\,\,\,c_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{7}^{-1/2}c,\,\,\,\,v^{i}_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{8}^{-1/2}v^{i},

u^{i}_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{-1/2}\left[u^{i}+{Y^{\prime}}_{10}\partial_{0}v^{i}-{Y^{\prime}}_{11}v^{i}\wedge A^{0}\right],

u_{R}^{0}={Z^{\prime}}_{6}^{-1/2}\left[u_{0}+{Y^{\prime}}_{9}\partial_{i}v^{i}+{Y^{\prime}}_{11}v^{i}\wedge A_{i}\right],

E^{i}_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{8}^{1/2}\left[E^{i}+{Y^{\prime}}_{9}\partial^{i}A_{0}+Y^{\prime}_{10}\partial_{0}A^{i}+Y^{\prime}_{11}A_{0}\wedge A^{i}+Y^{\prime}_{12}v^{i}\wedge c\right],

K_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{7}^{1/2}\left[K+\frac{1}{2}Y^{\prime}_{12}v^{i}\wedge v_{i}\right],\,\,\,\,g_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{0}^{1/2}g.

(27)

This structure was derived in our previous paper
[
11
]
, and applies to the Hamiltonian formalism of
gauge theories, irrespective of the gauge-fixing. But the values of the coefficients
Z^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}

depend on the gauge fixing, and are denoted by primed letters in the LC-flow gauge to distinguish
them from the FC-flow gauge.
It can be proved (see
[
12
]
Appendix D,
[
11
]
Appendix B) that
L_{R}
obeys the same BRST condition (10) as
L
, that is

\gamma_{R}*\gamma_{R}=0,

(28)

where the
*
composition is defined in (10), and

\gamma_{R}=\int d^{d}yL_{R}(y).

(29)

.

The important point about (25) is that, for
E
,
u_{i}
and
u_{0}
, there is mixing as well as scaling.
This implies that the renormalized Lagrangian in (26) does not have a pure Hamiltonian form:
it contains second order time derivatives of
A_{i}
.

Given
L_{R}
in (26), the renormalized ghost part,
L_{GR}
, is determined by the equation

\frac{\delta{\gamma_{GR}}}{\delta c^{*}(x)}=-{Z^{\prime}_{5}}^{-1/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\left[\frac{\delta\gamma_{R}}{\delta u_{iR}(x)}\right]-{Z^{\prime}_{6}}^{-1/2}\theta^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}\left[\frac{\delta\gamma_{R}}{\delta u_{0R}(x)}\right],

(30)

where

\gamma_{GR}=\int d^{d}yL_{GR}(y).

(31)

Then

L_{GR}=L_{G}(A_{iR}A_{0R},c_{R},c^{*}_{R},\theta_{R};g_{R}),

(32)

where, in addition to (27),

c^{*}_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{-1/2}c^{*},\,\,\,\,\theta_{R}^{2}=(Z^{\prime}_{5}/Z^{\prime}_{6})^{1/2}\theta^{2}.

(33)

Finally, the gauge-fixing term in (6),
L_{GF}
, is unchanged by renormalization. If expressed in terms of the renormalized quantities (27), (33), it may be written

L_{GF}(A_{i},A_{0},\theta,\alpha)=L_{GF}(A_{iR},A_{0R},\theta_{R},\alpha_{R}),

(34)

where

\alpha_{R}^{2}=Z_{5}\alpha^{2}.

(35)

Since
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, (35) makes no difference.

4
Renormalization at one-loop order

To one-loop order we write

{Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{1/2}=1+a^{\prime}_{5},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,Y^{\prime}_{9}=a^{\prime}_{9},

(36)

etc. By calculating the divergent parts of a sufficient number of one-loop graphs, we have evaluated
the
a^{\prime}
s. In terms of the quantity (using dimensional regularization with
d
dimensions of space-time)

c=\frac{g^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}C_{G}\frac{1}{4-d},

(37)

we find:

a^{\prime}_{0}=a^{\prime}_{7}=-\frac{11}{6}c,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,a^{\prime}_{12}=0,

(38)

a^{\prime}_{5}=\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{12}\theta+\frac{4}{3}\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}\right]c,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,a^{\prime}_{6}=\left[\frac{11}{6}-\frac{3}{4}\theta\right]c,

(39)

a^{\prime}_{8}=\left[-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{\theta}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right]c,

(40)

a^{\prime}_{9}=\left[\frac{1}{6}+\frac{2}{3}\theta-\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{\theta}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right]c,

(41)

a^{\prime}_{10}=\left[-\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{3}\theta+\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta}-\frac{5}{3}\frac{\theta}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right]c,

(42)

a^{\prime}_{11}=\left[-\frac{1}{6}-\frac{2}{3}\theta+\frac{2\theta}{1+\theta}-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\theta}{(1+\theta)^{2}}\right]c.

(43)

Some comments about these values may be useful. As expected,
a^{\prime}_{0}
is gauge-independent. The zero value of
a^{\prime}_{12}
and the equality between

a^{\prime}_{0}
and
a^{\prime}_{7}
are consequences of equations (23) and (24) for the
v_{j}A_{i}c
and
Kcc
amplitudes respectively.
Some combinations of constants have the same values as in the FC-flow gauge, for example

a^{\prime}_{9}+a^{\prime}_{10}=a_{9}+a_{10}
. This is because the
v_{i}c
graphs involve only the propagators (14), (18), (19) and (20) which are the same as in the FC-flow gauge. In the Landau gauge (
\theta=0
),
a^{\prime}_{5}=a^{\prime}_{6}=(13/12)c
,
the expected values (see for example
[
13
]
equation (12-113)).
In the Coulomb gauge (
\theta=0
),
a^{\prime}_{9}=-a^{\prime}_{10}=-a^{\prime}_{11}=1/6
, as found in the FC-flow gauge
[
11
]
.
We do not know the reason for this simplification.

We will now argue that the relation
a^{\prime}_{0}=a^{\prime}_{7}
in (34) generalizes to all orders, that is

Z^{\prime}_{0}=Z^{\prime}_{7}.

(44)

Consider the renormalization of the
-gK.(c\wedge c)/2
term in (8). The relevant divergent graphs
have two external ghost fields and a
K
source.These graphs are overall logarithmically divergent
by dimensional power counting. (We assume that any divergencies in subgraphs have been cancelled by counter-terms).Take any one of the external ghost lines. It must attach to
a ghost-antighost-gluon vertex, say

igp^{i}c^{*}(p).(A_{i}(p^{\prime})\wedge c(q)),\,\,\,\,\text{or}\,\,\,\,i\theta^{2}gp^{0}c^{*}(p).(A_{0}(p^{\prime})\wedge c(q)),

(45)

where the momenta are indicated,
q
being an external momentum. The
A
fields in the above equations are the ends of gluon propagators as in (15), (16), (19), (20), (21). Because of the properties (23) and (24),

p^{\prime}
effectively gives zero, and so the internal momentum
p
may be replaced by the external one
q
.
Then by power counting the graph is overall convergent. Thus the counter-terms available to cancel
the divergence must themselves cancel as in (38).

An example of the derivation of the above results (38)…(43) is given in the next section.

5
An example

To illustrate our methods, we describe the calculation of the divergent parts of the
A_{0}A_{0}
amplitude.
The graphs involved are shown in Fig.1.
The individual graphs are, by power counting, quadratically divergent. We expect two powers of the external momentum to come out, reducing it to a logarithmic divergence. After that, the internal momenta
may be put equal to get the divergent part.

Figures (a) and (b) are individually quadratically divergent, but, having carried
out the
p_{0}
integration, the sum gets a factor
(P-P^{\prime})^{2}
, reducing the degree of divergence to logarithmic. The contribution is
icQ^{2}/3
.
This is the full result in the Coulomb gauge (
\theta=0
).

In each of graphs (c) and (d), the transversality of the lower lines results in a factor
Q^{2}
.
For (c), this leads to the integral

g^{2}C_{G}Q^{2}\frac{2}{3}\int d^{d-1}P\int dp_{0}\frac{\theta^{4}p_{0}^{2}}{(\bar{P^{2}})^{2}p^{2}},

(46)

giving a divergent part

\frac{2}{3}\frac{\theta^{3}}{(1+\theta)^{2}}icQ^{2}.

(47)

Similarly (d) (together with the distinct graph
q\leftrightarrow-q
) give

-\frac{8}{3}\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}icQ^{2}.

(48)

There is partial cancellation between graphs (e) and (f), and the sum gives
(2/3)ic\theta Q^{2}

The sum of the above divergent parts determines the value of the counter-term
a^{\prime}_{9}
in (41).

6
The transition to the Lagrangian formalism

Before discussing the transition to the renormalized second order formalism, let us discuss the unrenormlized case, just using
L
in (1). We can define new variables

\tilde{E}_{i}=E_{i}-F_{0i}.\,\,\,\,\tilde{u}_{0}=u_{0}+D_{i}v^{i},\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{u}_{i}=u_{i}-D_{0}v_{i}.

(49)

(Where
D_{0}=\partial_{0}+gA_{0}\wedge
and
D_{i}=\partial_{i}+gA_{i}\wedge
are covariant derivatives.)
In terms of this

L_{C}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}^{i}-\frac{1}{4}[F_{ij}F^{ij}+2F_{0i}F^{0i}],

(50)

The BRST condition (10) has the same form in terms of the new variables, that is

\gamma*\gamma=

\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta\tilde{u}^{i}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta A_{i}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta\tilde{u}^{0}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta A_{0}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta v^{i}(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta\tilde{E}_{i}(x)}+\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta c(x)}.\frac{\delta\gamma}{\delta K(x)}\right].

(51)

This is because the transformation to the new variables (49) is formally the same as a special case of the transformation of the renormalized fields (27), which is known to satisfy (28).

Then, in (50),
\tilde{E}
and
A
are separated and the dependence on
\tilde{E}
is trivial. However,

\tilde{E}
still occurs in
L_{S}
. But since
\tilde{E}
occurs trivially in (50), we can omit the source
v_{i}

in
L_{S}
. Then (51) still controls the BRST invariance of the second term in (50).

To make the transition to the second order formalism from the renormalized Lagrangian (26), we can proceed in the same way but using the renormalized fields in (27), finally obtaining

L_{CR}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{E}_{Ri}\tilde{E}^{i}_{R}-\frac{1}{4}[F_{Rij}F_{R}^{ij}+2F_{R0i}F_{R}^{0i}],

(52)

and

L_{SR}=u^{i}_{R}.[\partial_{i}c_{R}+g(A_{Ri}\wedge c_{R})]+u_{R0}.[\partial_{0}c_{R}+g(A_{R0}\wedge c_{R})]-\frac{1}{2}g_{R}K_{R}.(c_{R}\wedge c_{R}),

(53)

where the renormalized fields and sources are now just

A_{R}^{i}={Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{1/2}A^{i},\,\,\,\,A_{R}^{0}={Z^{\prime}}_{6}^{1/2}A_{0},\,\,\,\,c_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{7}^{-1/2}c,

u^{i}_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{5}^{-1/2}u^{i},\,\,\,\,u_{R}^{0}={Z^{\prime}}_{6}^{-1/2}u_{0},\,\,\,\,\,K_{R}={Z^{\prime}}_{7}^{1/2}K,\,\,\,\,g_{R}={Z^{\prime}}^{1/2}_{0}g,

(54)

with the property (44) satisfied.
The renormalization constants
Z_{8},Z_{9},Z_{10},Z_{11}
are of course irrelevant to the second order formalism.

It may be noted that the source part of the action has the same form in terms of the new variables (49)
as in terms of the original ones. That is, if we define

\tilde{L}_{S}=\tilde{u}^{i}.[\partial_{i}c+g(A_{i}\wedge c)]+\tilde{u}_{0}.[\partial_{0}c+g(A_{0}\wedge c)]+gv^{i}.(\tilde{E}_{i}\wedge c)-\frac{1}{2}gK.(c\wedge c),

(55)

then

\int d^{d}x\tilde{L}_{S}-\int d^{d}xL_{S}=

\int dx\left[-v^{i}.(D_{0}D_{i}c)+v^{i}.(D_{i}D_{0}c)+gv^{i}(F_{0i}\wedge c)\right]=0.

(56)

7
Summary and comments

We have studied, in Hamiltonian non-Abelian gauge theory, the flow gauge interpolating between the Landau and Coulomb gauges. We have described the general form of renormalization and calculated the one-loop renormalization constants as functions of the flow parameter
\theta
. We make some comparisons with
the flow gauge interpolating between the Feynman and Coulomb gauges
[
11
]
.
We prove an all-orders equality between the ghost renormalization and the beta function (equation (44)),
which has previously been noted in
[
3
]
. We demonstrate how the transition from the Hamiltonian to the Lagrangian formalism can be made.

The renormalization of the Hamiltonian form of gauge theories has a lot in common with that of
the covariant first-order formalism
[
12
]
, involving mixing as well as scaling of fields.

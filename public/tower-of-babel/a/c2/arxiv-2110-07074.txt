Finite-dimensional Quantum Observables are the Special Symmetric † \dagger -Frobenius Algebras of CP Maps
Stefano Gogioso

Abstract
We use purity , a principle borrowed from the foundations of quantum information, to show that all special symmetric † \dagger -Frobenius algebras in CPM ⁡ ( fHilb ) \operatorname{CPM}\left({\operatorname{fHilb}}\right) —and, in particular, all classical structures—are canonical , i.e. that they arise by doubling of special symmetric † \dagger -Frobenius algebras in fHilb {\operatorname{fHilb}} .

Corollary 7

(CP
\dagger
-SSFAs are all canonical)
.

The
\dagger
-SSFAs in
\operatorname{CPM}\left({\operatorname{fHilb}}\right)
are all canonical, i.e. they all arise by doubling of
\dagger
-SSFAs in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
.

Proof.

Now let
(\Delta,E,\Delta^{\dagger},E^{\dagger})
be a
\dagger
-SSFA in
\operatorname{CPM}\left({\operatorname{fHilb}}\right)
.
Because
(\Delta,E)
is a comonoid which is isometric and satisfies the snake equations, we now know that
\Delta
and
E
are pure, i.e. that we can find linear maps
\delta
and
\epsilon
in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
such that
\Delta=\operatorname{CPM}(\delta)
and
E=\operatorname{CPM}(\epsilon)
arise by doubling.
We now wish to conclude that
(\delta,\epsilon,\delta^{\dagger},\epsilon^{\dagger})
form a
\dagger
-SSFA in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
, but this doesnt immediately follow from the equations in
\operatorname{CPM}\left({\operatorname{fHilb}}\right)
: while
\delta
is certainly an isometry in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
, the associativity law, unit laws, symmetry law and Frobenius law for
\delta
and
\epsilon
are only guaranteed to hold up to phase.
In fact,
(\delta,\epsilon,\delta^{\dagger},\epsilon^{\dagger})
is not, in general, a
\dagger
-SSFA in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
.
However, it is easy to show (cf. Lemma
6
below) that
(\delta,e^{-i\lambda}\epsilon,\delta^{\dagger},e^{i\lambda}\epsilon^{\dagger})
is always a
\dagger
-SSFA in
{\operatorname{fHilb}}
, where
e^{i\lambda}
is the phase associated to the identity in the left unit law.
Because
\operatorname{CPM}(e^{-i\lambda}\epsilon)=\operatorname{CPM}(\epsilon)
, this is enough to prove our result.

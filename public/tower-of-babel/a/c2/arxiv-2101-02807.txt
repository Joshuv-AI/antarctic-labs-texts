Complex Hyperbolic Triangle Groups of Type [ m , m , 0 ; n 1 , n 2 , 2 ] [m,m,0;n_{1},n_{2},2]
Sam Povall, Anna Pratoussevitch

Abstract
. In this paper we study discreteness of complex hyperbolic triangle groups of type [ m , m , 0 ; n 1 , n 2 , 2 ] [m,m,0;n_{1},n_{2},2] , i.e. groups of isometries of the complex hyperbolic plane generated by three complex reflections of orders n 1 , n 2 , 2 n_{1},n_{2},2 in complex geodesics with pairwise distances m , m , 0 m,m,0 . For fixed m m , the parameter space of such groups is of real dimension one. We determine the possible orders for n 1 n_{1} and n 2 n_{2} and also intervals in the parameter space that correspond to discrete and non-discrete triangle groups.

1.
Introduction

Complex hyperbolic triangle groups are groups of isometries of the complex hyperbolic plane
generated by three complex reflections in complex geodesics. We will focus on the case of ultra-parallel groups, that is, the case where the complex geodesics are pairwise disjoint.

Unlike real reflections, complex reflections can be of arbitrary order. Work on higher order reflections has been discussed by Parker and Paupert
[
11
]
and Pratoussevitch
[
14

n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
in complex geodesics
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}

C_{k-1}
and
C_{k+1}
equal to
m_{k}
for

[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3};n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}]
.

In this paper, we will study discreteness of ultra-parallel complex hyperbolic triangle groups of type
[m,m,0;n_{1},n_{2},2]

n_{1}

n_{2}

2

n_{2}

) and the other two distances between fixed point sets coincide (

[m,m,0;2,2,2]

Background

2.1.
Complex hyperbolic plane:

Let
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
be the
3
-dimensional complex vector space
equipped with a Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
of signature
(2,1)
,
e.g.

\langle z,w\rangle=z_{1}\bar{w}_{1}+z_{2}\bar{w}_{2}-z_{3}\bar{w}_{3}.

If
z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
then we know that
\langle z,z\rangle
is real.
Thus we can define subsets
V_{-}
,
V_{0}
and
V_{+}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
as follows

\displaystyle V_{-}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}\,\,\big|\,\,\langle z,z\rangle<0\},

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}\backslash\{0\}\,\,\big|\,\,\langle z,z\rangle=0\},

\displaystyle V_{+}

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}\,\,\big|\,\,\langle z,z\rangle>0\}.

We say that
z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
is
negative
,
null
or
positive
if
z
is in
V_{-}
,
V_{0}
or
V_{+}
respectively. Define a projection map
\mathbb{P}
on the points of
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
with
z_{3}\neq 0
as

\mathbb{P}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z=\begin{bmatrix}z_{1}\\
z_{2}\\
z_{3}\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}/z_{3}\\
z_{2}/z_{3}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{2,1}).

That is, provided
z_{3}\neq 0
,

z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\mapsto[z]=[z_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z_{3}]=\left[\frac{z_{1}}{z_{3}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\frac{z_{2}}{z_{3}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1\right].

The
projective model
of the complex hyperbolic plane is defined to be the collection of negative lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}

and its boundary is defined to be the collection of null lines.
That is

H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}=\mathbb{P}(V_{-})\quad\text{and}\quad{\partial H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}=\mathbb{P}(V_{0}).

The metric on
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, called the
Bergman metric
, is given by the distance function
\rho
defined by the formula

\operatorname{cosh}^{2}\left(\frac{\rho([z],[w])}{2}\right)=\frac{\langle{z,w\rangle}\langle{w,z\rangle}}{\langle{z,z\rangle}\langle{w,w\rangle}},

where
[z]
and
[w]
are the images of
z
and
w
in
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
under the projectivisation map
\mathbb{P}
.
The group of holomorphic isometries of
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with respect to the Bergman metric
can be identified with the projective unitary group
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)
.

2.2.
Complex geodesics:

A
complex geodesic
is a projectivisation of a 2-dimensional complex subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{2,1}
.
Any complex geodesic is isometric to

\{[z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1]\,\,\big|\,\,z\in\mathbb{C}\}

in the projective model.
Any positive vector
c\in V_{+}
determines a two-dimensional complex subspace

\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}\,\,\big|\,\,\langle c,z\rangle=0\}.

Projecting this subspace we obtain a complex geodesic

\mathbb{P}\left(\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2,1}\,\,\big|\,\,\langle c,z\rangle=0\}\right).

Conversely, any complex geodesic is represented by a positive vector
c\in V_{+}
,
called a
polar vector
of the complex geodesic.
A polar vector is unique up to multiplication by a complex scalar.
We say that the polar vector
c
is
normalised
if
\langle c,c\rangle=1
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be complex geodesics with normalised polar vectors
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
respectively.
We call
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

ultra-parallel
if they have no points of intersection in
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\cup{\partial H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
,
in which case

|\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle|=\operatorname{cosh}\left(\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(C_{1},C_{2})\right)>1,

where
\operatorname{dist}(C_{1},C_{2})
is the distance between
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
We call
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

ideal
if they have a point of intersection in
{\partial H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
,
in which case
|\langle c_{1},c_{2}\rangle|=1
and
\operatorname{dist}(C_{1},C_{2})=0
.

2.3.
Complex reflections:

For a given complex geodesic
C
, a
minimal complex hyperbolic reflection of order
n
in
C

is the isometry
\iota_{C}
in
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)
of order
n
with fixed point set
C
given by

\iota(z)=-z+(1-\mu)\frac{\langle z,c\rangle}{\langle c,c\rangle}c,

where
c
is a polar vector of
C
and
\mu=\exp(2\pi i/n)
.

2.4.
Complex hyperbolic triangle groups:

A
complex hyperbolic triangle
is a triple
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
of complex geodesics in
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
A triangle
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
is a
complex hyperbolic ultra-parallel
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangle

if the complex geodesics are ultra-parallel at distances
m_{k}=\operatorname{dist}(C_{k-1},C_{k+1})
for
k=1,2,3
.
We will allow
m_{k}=0
for some or all
k
.
A
complex hyperbolic ultra-parallel
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3};n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}]
-triangle group

is a subgroup of
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)
generated by complex reflections
\iota_{k}
of order
n_{k}
in the sides
C_{k}

of a complex hyperbolic ultra-parallel
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangle
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
.

2.5.
Angular invariant:

For each fixed triple
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
the space of
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangles is of real dimension one.
We can describe a parametrisation of the space of complex hyperbolic triangles in
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
by means of an angular invariant
{\alpha}
.
We define the
angular invariant

{\alpha}
of the triangle
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
by

{\alpha}=\arg\left(\prod_{k=1}^{3}\langle c_{k-1},c_{k+1}\rangle\right),

where
c_{k}
is the normalised polar vector of the complex geodesic
C_{k}
.
We use the following proposition, given in
[
14
]
, which gives criteria for the existence of a triangle group
in terms of the angular invariant.

Proposition 2.1
.

An
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangle in
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is determined uniquely up to isometry
by the three distances between the complex geodesics and the angular invariant
{\alpha}
.
For any
{\alpha}\in[0,2\pi]
, an
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangle with angular invariant
{\alpha}
exists if and only if

\cos({\alpha})<\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+r_{3}^{2}-1}{2r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}},

where
r_{k}=\operatorname{cosh}(m_{k}/2)
.

For
m_{3}=0
we have
r_{3}=1
and the right hand side of the inequality in Proposition
2.1
is

\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}\geqslant 1,

so the condition on
{\alpha}
is always satisfied,
i.e. for any
{\alpha}\in[0,2\pi]
there exists an
[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
-triangle with angular invariant
{\alpha}
.

2.6.
Heisenberg group:

The boundary of complex hyperbolic space can be identified with the one-point compactification of the
Heisenberg group

\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}=\{(\zeta,\nu)\,\,\big|\,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{C},\nu\in\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{\infty\}.

One homeomorphism taking
{\partial H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
to
\mathcal{N}
is given by the stereographic projection:

[z_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z_{3}]\mapsto\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}+z_{3}},\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{2}+z_{3}}\right)\right)\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ z_{2}+z_{3}\neq 0,\quad[0\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}-z]\mapsto\infty.

The
Heisenberg group
is the Heisenberg space
\mathcal{N}
with the group law

(\xi_{1},\nu_{1})*(\xi_{2},\nu_{2})=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+2\operatorname{Im}(\xi_{1}\bar{\xi_{2}})).

The centre of
\mathcal{N}
consists of elements of the form
(0,\nu)
for
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
The Heisenberg group is not abelian but is
2
-step nilpotent.
To see this, observe that

[(\xi_{1},\nu_{1}),(\xi_{2},\nu_{2})]=(\xi_{1},\nu_{1})^{-1}*(\xi_{2},\nu_{2})^{-1}*(\xi_{1},\nu_{1})*(\xi_{2},\nu_{2})=(0,4\operatorname{Im}(\xi_{1}\bar{\xi_{2}})).

Therefore the commutator of any two elements of
\mathcal{N}
lies in the centre.

An alternative description of the Heisenberg group
\mathcal{N}
is as the group of upper triangular matrices

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x&y\\
0&1&z\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,\,\big|\,\,x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

with the operation of matrix multiplication.
For any integer
k\neq 0
, the subgroup
N_{k}
generated by the matrices

a=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad b=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad c=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\frac{1}{k}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

is a uniform lattice in
\mathcal{N}
with the presentation

N_{k}=\langle a,b,c\,\,\big|\,\,[b,a]=c^{k},\penalty\ [c,a]=[c,b]=1\rangle.

Moreover, any uniform lattice in
\mathcal{N}
is isomorphic to
N_{k}
for some integer
k\neq 0
, see section 6.1 in
[
2
]
.

2.7.
Chains:

A complex geodesic in
H_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is homeomorphic to a disc,
its intersection with the boundary of the complex hyperbolic plane is homeomorphic to a circle.
Circles that arise as the boundaries of complex geodesics are called
chains
.

There is a bijection between chains and complex geodesics. We can therefore, without loss of generality, talk about reflections in chains instead of reflections in complex geodesics.

Chains can be represented in the Heisenberg space, for more details see
[
3
]
.
Chains passing through
\infty
are represented by vertical straight lines defined by
\zeta=\zeta_{0}
.
Such chains are called
vertical
.
The vertical chain
C_{\zeta_{0}}
defined by
\zeta=\zeta_{0}
has a polar vector

c_{\zeta_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-\bar{\zeta_{0}}\\
\bar{\zeta_{0}}\end{bmatrix}.

A chain not containing
\infty
is called
finite
.
A finite chain is represented by an ellipse whose vertical projection
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a circle in
\mathbb{C}
.
The finite chain with centre
(\zeta_{0},\nu_{0})\in\mathcal{N}
and radius
r_{0}>0
has a polar vector

\begin{bmatrix}2\zeta_{0}\\
1+r_{0}^{2}-\zeta_{0}\bar{\zeta_{0}}+i\nu_{0}\\
1-r_{0}^{2}+\zeta_{0}\bar{\zeta_{0}}-i\nu_{0}\end{bmatrix}

and consists of all points
(\zeta,\nu)\in\mathcal{N}
satisfying the equations

|\zeta-\zeta_{0}|=r_{0},\quad\nu=\nu_{0}-2\operatorname{Im}(\zeta\bar{\zeta}_{0}).

2.8.
Heisenberg isometries:

We consider the space
\mathcal{N}
equipped with the
Cygan metric
,

\rho_{0}\left((\zeta_{1},\nu_{1}),(\zeta_{2},\nu_{2})\right)=\Big|\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\right\rvert}^{2}-i(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})-2i\operatorname{Im}(\zeta_{1}\bar{\zeta_{2}})\Big|^{1/2}.

A
Heisenberg translation

T_{(\xi,\nu)}
by
(\xi,\nu)\in\mathcal{N}
is given by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto(\zeta+\xi,\omega+\nu+2\operatorname{Im}(\xi\bar{\zeta}))=(\xi,\nu)*(\zeta,\omega)

and corresponds to the following element in
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)

\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi&\xi\\
-\bar{\xi}&1-\frac{|\xi|^{2}-i\nu}{2}&-\frac{|\xi|^{2}-i\nu}{2}\\
\bar{\xi}&\frac{|\xi|^{2}-i\nu}{2}&1+\frac{|\xi|^{2}-i\nu}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

A special case is a vertical Heisenberg translation
T_{(0,\nu)}
by
(0,\nu)\in\mathcal{N}
given by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto(\zeta,\omega+\nu).

A
Heisenberg rotation

R_{\mu}
by
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
,
|\mu|=1
, is given by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto(\mu\cdot\zeta,\omega)

and corresponds to the following element in
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)

\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

A minimal complex reflection
\iota_{C_{\varphi}}
of order
n
in a vertical chain
C_{\varphi}
with polar vector

c_{\varphi}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-\bar{\varphi}\\
\bar{\varphi}\end{bmatrix}

is given by

(\zeta,\omega)\mapsto\left(\mu\zeta+(1-\mu)\varphi,\omega-2|\varphi|^{2}\operatorname{Im}(1-\mu)+2\operatorname{Im}((1-\mu)\bar{\varphi}\zeta)\right)

and corresponds to the following element in
\operatorname{PU}(2,1)

\begin{pmatrix}-\mu&-(1-\mu)\varphi&-(1-\mu)\varphi\\
-(1-\mu)\bar{\varphi}&(1-\mu)|\varphi|^{2}-1&(1-\mu)|\varphi|^{2}\\
(1-\mu)\bar{\varphi}&-(1-\mu)|\varphi|^{2}&-(1-\mu)|\varphi|^{2}-1\end{pmatrix},

where
\mu=\exp(2\pi i/n)
.
The complex reflection
\iota_{C_{\varphi}}
can be decomposed as a product of a Heisenberg translation and a Heisenberg rotation:

\iota_{C_{\varphi}}=R_{\mu}\circ T_{(\xi,\nu)}=T_{(\mu\xi,\nu)}\circ R_{\mu},

where

\xi=(\bar{\mu}-1)\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad\nu=-2|\varphi|^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Im}(1-\mu)=2|\varphi|^{2}\sin(2\pi/n).

Heisenberg translations, Heisenberg rotations and complex reflections are isometries with respect to the Cygan metric.
The group of all Heisenberg translations is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}
.
The group of all Heisenberg rotations
\{R_{\mu}\,\,\big|\,\,\mu\in\mathbb{C},\penalty\ |\mu|=1\}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{U}(1)
.
The group of their products
\mathcal{N}\hbox{\msbm\char 111\relax}\operatorname{U}(1)
contains all complex reflections.

Products of reflections in chains:

What effect does the minimal complex reflection of order
n
in the vertical chain
C_{\zeta}
have on another vertical chain,
C_{\xi}
, which intersects
\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}
at
\xi
?

We calculate

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\mu&-(1-\mu)\zeta&-(1-\mu)\zeta\\

-\bar{\xi}\\

-(1-\mu)\bar{\zeta}+\bar{\xi}\\

Recall the Heisenberg group

Let

Proof.

From the Heisenberg group

By this, we have that

Note that

Therefore, the group

We can see that

We next need to show that

\mathbb{C}

For a contradiction, suppose not.
For a sequence to converge on the plane
\mathbb{C}
,
we are able to bound their corresponding elements in

which exists due to

This implies that there is a convergent subsequence to an element

This is the required contradiction since

∎

Abstract
. We study the generalized Kähler-Ricci flow with initial data of symplectic type, and show that this condition is preserved. In the case of a Fano background with toric symmetry, we establish global existence of the normalized flow. We derive an extension of Perelman’s entropy functional to this setting, which yields convergence of nonsingular solutions at infinity. Furthermore, we derive an extension of Mabuchi’s K K -energy to this setting, which yields weak convergence of the flow.



1.
Introduction

Generalized Kähler (GK) structures first appeared through investigations into supersymmetric sigma models
[
29
]
, and were rediscovered in
a purely mathematical context in the work of Gualtieri
[
35
]
and Hitchin
[
44
]
. They have recently
attracted interest in both the physics and mathematical communities as natural generalizations of Kähler structures. We will focus here entirely on the so called
biHermitian
description of generalized Kähler geometry (cf.
[
5
,
29
]
). Thus, a
generalized Kähler manifold is a smooth manifold
M
with a triple
(g,I,J)
consisting
of two integrable almost-complex structures,
I
and
J
, together with a
Riemannian metric
g
which is Hermitian with respect to both, such that the Kähler forms
\omega_{I}
and

\omega_{J}
satisfy

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\omega_{I}=H=-{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\omega_{J},\qquad{\mathrmsl d}H=0,

where the first equation defines
H
, and
{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}=\sqrt{-1}(\overline{\partial}_{I}-\partial_{I}),\,{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}=\sqrt{-1}(\overline{\partial}_{J}-\partial_{J})
.

The generalized Kähler-Ricci flow (GKRF) was introduced by the second named author and Tian in
[
61
]
as a tool for constructing canonical metrics and understanding existence and moduli problems in generalized Kähler geometry, extending the deeply developed theory in Kähler geometry. Prior global existence and convergence results for GKRF have appeared in for instance
[
6
,
54
,
56
]
. In this paper we consider
normalized generalized Kähler Ricci flow
(NGKRF), expressed as a flow of
{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}_{J}^{c}
-closed positive definite
(1,1)
-forms
\omega_{J}
on the complex manifold
(M,J)
, together with a flow of
I
, as (cf.
[
61
]
)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega_{J}=&\ -2\Big(\rho^{B}_{J}\Big)^{1,1}+2\lambda\omega_{J},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}I={\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I,\end{split}

where
\lambda
is a fixed real constant and
\Big(\rho_{J}^{B}\Big)^{1,1}
denotes the
(1,1)
-part with respect to
J
of the Bismut-Ricci form
\rho_{J}^{B}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
of
(\omega_{J},J)
. By
[
61
]
, as far as a solution to (
1.2
) exists,
the triple
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
defines a GK structure in the sense of (
1.1
). Notice that if the initial GK structure
(g,I,J)
was Kähler (i.e.
{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\omega_{J}=-H=0
and
I=J
in (
1.1
)), the Bismut-Ricci form
\rho^{B}
is just the usual Kähler-Ricci form of the Kähler manifold
(g,J)
, and (
1.2
) reduces to the normalized Kähler Ricci flow. In this setting Cao showed
[
12
]
smooth global existence. The convergence at infinity is a very subtle issue, and many results have appeared in recent years
[
14
,
20
,
51
,
65
,
66
,
67
,
68
]
.

In this paper, we will study NGKRF in the case when
(M,J)
is a smooth toric Fano variety of complex dimension
m
, assuming that the initial GK structure
(g,I,J)
is compatible with a symplectic form
F\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
, and is invariant under the action of a maximal compact torus
{\mathbb{T}}
in the automorphism group
\mathrmsl{Aut}(M,J)
of
(M,J)
. More precisely, suppose that

F
is a symplectic
2
-form on
M
which tames
J
, i.e. whose
(1,1)
-part
\omega_{J}:=\big(F\big)^{1,1}_{J}
is positive definite. We then define a Riemannian metric
g
, a
2
-form
b
and an almost complex structure
I
by

(1.3)

\displaystyle g:=-(FJ)^{\rm sym},\qquad b:=-(FJ)^{\rm skew},\qquad I:=-F^{-1}J^{*}F.

One can show (cf. §
2
) that when
I
is also integrable,
(g,I,J)
is a GK structure with
H=db
. Furthermore, in this case we say that
(g,I,J)
is an
F
-
compatible
GK structure, or a GK structure of

, referring to the fact
[
37
]
that one of the corresponding generalized complex structures on

is determined completely by the symplectic
2
-form
F
.

To better understand these structures, first recall that a key feature of GK geometry, observed by Hitchin
[
45
]
(cf.
[
5
,
52

for the

the tensors

\displaystyle\sigma=[I,J]g^{-1},\qquad\sigma_{I}=\sigma-\sqrt{-1}I\sigma,\qquad\sigma_{J}=\sigma-\sqrt{-1}J\sigma

define respectively a real Poisson structure and holomorphic Poisson structures with respect to
I
and
J
. Conversely, given a compact Kähler manifold
(M,J,{\overline{g}},\overline{\omega})
and a non-trivial holomorphic Poisson structure

[
46
,
32
,
34
,
38

producing non-Kähler GK structures
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
with holomorphic Poisson structure
\sigma_{J_{t}}=t\mu
for

. This takes a particularly nice and explicit form on a Kähler toric variety
(M,J,{\mathbb{T}})
. Recent work of Boulanger
[
11
]
constructs invariant toric GK structures by deforming the complex structure

using

deformations of type
A

[

constructed toric GK structures by deforming

-equivariant diffeomorphism, any

-invariant GK structure

of symplectic type on
(M,J)

2

{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler structure

Building upon this, we show in §
2
that NGKRF interacts with the associated Poisson structures in a predictable way, and thus is a natural tool for investigating questions on the global moduli of generalized Kähler structures on, as well as the underlying Poisson geometry of, Fano varieties. We formulate certain precise conjectures coming from the formal picture of NGKRF in §
2
. As a fundamental first step in this direction, we establish the sharp global existence of the flow in the case of toric symmetry.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,J,{\mathbb{T}})
be a smooth toric Fano variety and
F\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant symplectic
2
-form which defines a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
of symplectic type on
M
. Then, the solution of normalized generalized Kähler Ricci flow (
1.2
) with this initial data exists on
[0,\infty)
.

The key geometric observation behind Theorem
1.1
is that in the case of a Fano background with toric symmetry, the normalized generalized Kähler Ricci flow (
1.2
) reduces to a flow of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
Kähler
metrics
\overline{\omega}_{t}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
. Using the Abreu–Guillemin reduction
[
2
,
41
]
, these are then determined by a family of strictly convex smooth functions
\phi_{t}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi=\log{\det}\Big(\big({\rm Hess}\ \phi\big)^{-1}+\sqrt{-1}e^{-2t}B\Big)^{-1}+2\phi,

where
B
is a skew-symmetric matrix corresponding to the
B
-type deformation described above. Interestingly, this reduced scalar equation is independent of
A
-type deformations. Also, when
B=0
, it coincides with the PDE describing the reduction of the Kähler Ricci flow on a smooth toric Fano variety, studied in
[
74
]
. We emphasize here that the scalar reduction in the Kähler-Ricci flow case is an adaptation to the toric setting of the general scalar reduction, itself a consequence of the transgression formula for the Ricci curvature. In our setting the derivation of this scalar reduction is more subtle. In GK geometry, the local Ricci potential relies on delicate constructions in generalized geometry (cf.
[
28
]
Definition 8.15), and is not always easy to make explicit. In §
3
, after a careful buildup of the differential geometry of toric generalized Kähler structure, we derive the Bismut-Ricci potential. However, the relevant formula involves the evolving complex structure
I
, and therefore a scalar reduction of the flow is not immediate. Again subtle structure of toric GK geometry enters to give the scalar reduction to (
1.5
) in Proposition
4.2
. Given this scalar reduction, through a series of estimates based on the maximum principle we derive a priori
L^{\infty}
estimates for the associated metric in terms of a
C^{0}
bound for
\phi

C^{2,\alpha}
/Calabi-Yau
C^{3}
estimates for pluriclosed flow (
[
48
,
55
]
), we obtain
C^{\infty}
estimates for
\phi
in terms of a
C^{0}
estimate for
\phi
. Since
\phi
grows at worst exponentially by the maximum principle, we thus conclude the long time existence.

One expects the convergence at infinity to be a delicate issue, and we prove two partial results in this direction. In the toric KRF setting the convergence was established by Zhu
[
74
]
using delicate estimates derived using Perelman’s entropy, Mabuchi’s
K
-energy and estimates from convex geometry. Outside of the toric setting, convergence assuming the existence of a Kähler-Ricci soliton was shown by Tian-Zhu
[
67
,
68
]
. First we prove convergence of nonsingular solutions without the toric symmetry hypothesis, where so far there is not a scalar reduction of the flow. The key point is a Perelman-type entropy monotonicity, which yields that the only smooth self-similar limits of NGKRF are in fact Kähler-Ricci solitons, and also shows a uniform
\kappa
-noncollapsing result for NGKRF on Fano manifolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler structure of symplectic type with respect to
F
. Let
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
denote the solution to NGKRF with this initial data. Then

(1)

The metric
g_{t}
is uniformly
\kappa
-noncollapsed for all times the flow exists.

(2)

Suppose
(M,J)
is Fano,
F\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
. Suppose the solution exists on
[0,\infty)
with uniformly bounded curvature. Then any sequence of times
\{t_{j}\}\to\infty
admits a subsequence such that
(g_{t_{j}},I_{t_{j}},J)
converges in the
C^{\infty}
-Cheeger-Gromov topology to
(g_{KRS},J_{\infty},J_{\infty})
, where
g_{KRS}
is a Kähler-Ricci soliton.

In the convergence statement above, the complex structure
J_{\infty}
may not be in general biholomorphic to
J
, and as is the case in Kähler-Ricci flow one expects this subtle issue to be related to
K
-stability of
(M,J)
. In the case when the initial structure
(g,I,J)
has a
toric symmetry
, the scalar reduction (
1.5
) of the GKRF and the results in
[
74
]
motivate us to conjecture that
J_{\infty}\cong J
(see Conjecture
2.12
below).

Returning to the setting of toric symmetry, we are able to prove a certain kind of weak, but unconditional, convergence. First, adapting ideas from general GIT theory, we are able to define an extension of the Mabuchi
K
-energy to toric generalized Kähler structures, which we show is monotone along NGKRF. Using this monotonicity and exploiting the variational approach of

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,J,{\mathbb{T}})
be a smooth toric Fano variety which admits a Kähler-Einstein metric, and

-invariant symplectic

The precise definitions for the convergence are given in Section

2.1.
Curvature identities for Hermitian metrics

In this subsection we recall the definitions of the Bismut-Ricci and the Chern-Ricci forms on a Hermitian manifold
(M,g,J)
, extending the notion of the Kähler-Ricci form of a Kähler manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g,J)
be a Hermitian manifold with fundamental
2
-form
\omega_{J}=gJ
. The
Bismut connection
is the Hermitian connection defined
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{B}_{X}Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\ g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\omega_{J}(X,Y,Z),

where
\nabla
stands for the Riemannian connection of
g
. The
Chern connection
is the Hermitian connection defined
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{C}_{X}Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\ g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\omega_{J}(X,JY,JZ).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(M,g,J)
be a complex
manifold with a Hermitian metric
g
. The
Bismut-Ricci curvature
and

Chern-Ricci curvature
are defined by

\displaystyle\rho^{B}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle\ \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}R^{B}(X,Y,e_{i},Je_{i}),\qquad\rho^{C}(X,Y)=\ \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}R^{C}(X,Y,e_{i},Je_{i}),

where
R^{B}
and
R^{C}
are the curvature tensors associated to the
Bismut
[
9
]
and Chern connections respectively, and
\{e_{i}\}
is an orthonormal
basis for
(TM,g)
. Furthermore we define the
Bismut scalar curvature
as

\displaystyle s^{B}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m}\rho^{B}(e_{i},Je_{i}).

Using the formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),
one can check (see e.g.
[

\nabla^{B}
and

(2.3)

\nabla^{B}_{X}=\nabla^{C}_{X}+i\theta_{J}(JX),

where

\theta_{J}:=\left<\omega_{J},{\mathrmsl d}\omega_{J}\right>=-\omega_{J}^{-1}\righthalfcup{\mathrmsl d}\omega_{J}

Lee form
of
(g,J)
. It follows from (

(2.4)

\rho^{B}=\rho^{C}+{\mathrmsl d}(J^{\sharp}\theta_{J}),

where for a
1

(J^{\sharp}\alpha)(X):=-(J^{*}\alpha)(X)=-\alpha(JX)
for the induced action of

where

2.2.
Generalized Kähler structures of symplectic type

Definition 2.3
.

Given a smooth manifold
M
, a
generalized Kähler structure
is a triple
(g,I,J)
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
, and two integrable complex structures
I
and
J
compatible with
g
such that

\displaystyle{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\omega_{I}=H=-{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\omega_{J},\qquad{\mathrmsl d}H=0.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a smooth manifold
M
, a generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
is of
symplectic type
if

(2.7)

\displaystyle\det(I+J)\neq 0

everywhere on
M
. In this setting we define

\displaystyle F=-2g(J+I)^{-1},\qquad b=-g(J+I)^{-1}(I-J),\qquad F^{\sharp}=Fg^{-1}=-2(J+I)^{-1}.

It follows
[
34
]
that
F
is non-degenerate and tames both
J
and
I
, and
b
is a skew-symmetric tensor which further satisfies
b\in\Wedge^{2,0+0,2}_{I}\cap\Wedge^{2,0+0,2}_{J}
. Furthermore, direct computations yield the basic relationship

(2.8)

\displaystyle-FJ=g+b,\qquad-FI=g-b.

This decomposition yields the further useful inequality

(2.9)

\displaystyle F^{m}\geq\omega_{J}^{m}=m!dV_{g}.

Several differential operators are relevant for these structures. In particular, we set

\displaystyle d^{c}_{I}=IdI,\qquad d^{c}_{J}=JdJ,\qquad d_{F}=F^{\sharp}dF^{\sharp}.

Using the integrability conditions for generalized Kähler structures it furthermore follows that (cf.
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
)

\displaystyle{\mathrmsl d}F=0,\qquad H={\mathrmsl d}b.

Remark 2.5
.

It turns out (cf.
[
26
]
Proposition 1) that the data above is equivalently described by fixing first a symplectic form
F
, then asking for an integrable complex structure
J
which is tamed by
F
, and furthermore the almost complex structure

\displaystyle I=-F^{-1}J^{*}F

is integrable. Given these conditions, the formulas in Definition
2.4
can be inverted to derive
g
and
b
. This in turn allows for an explicit derivation of the associated
generalized complex structures
(cf.
[
35
,
36
]
), one of which is of ‘symplectic type,’ determined explicitly by the global closed pure spinor
F
.

Kähler metrics are naturally interpreted as symplectic-type generalized Kähler, setting
I=J
. Furthermore, given a hyperKähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
, the triple
(g,I,J)
is GK of symplectic type, in fact is of
nondegenerate
type (cf.
[
54
]
,
[
6
]
). Hamiltonian deformations of these structures are examples of non-Kähler symplectic-type GK structures (
[
5
]
), as are Hitchin’s Poisson deformations of Kähler metrics on Fano surfaces
[
46
]
. We further record here a basic further identity:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler structure of symplectic type and denote by
\theta_{J}=J\delta^{g}\omega_{J}
and
\theta_{I}:=I\delta^{g}\omega_{I}
the Lee forms. Then we have

\frac{1}{2}d\log\det(I+J)=-(b\righthalfcup db)=\theta_{J}+\imath_{\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}b+\delta^{g}b=\theta_{I}-\imath_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}}b-\delta^{g}b.

Proof.

This follows directly from
[
6
]
Lemmas 2.10 and 2.13.

2.3.
The Bismut-Ricci forms for symplectic-type GK structures

Observe that in the context of generalized Kähler geometry we have two Bismut connections, defined via

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{B,I}_{X}Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\ g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\omega_{I}(X,Y,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}H(X,Y,Z),

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{B,J}_{X}Y,Z)=

\displaystyle\ g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\omega_{J}(X,Y,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)+\tfrac{1}{2}H(X,Y,Z).

For a generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
, we denote by
\rho^{C}_{J},\rho^{C}_{I}
and
\rho^{B}_{J},\rho^{B}_{I}
the Chern and Bismut-Ricci forms of the Hermitian structure
(g,J)
and
(g,I)
, respectively. We next derive a relationship between the two associated Bismut-Ricci forms for GK structures of symplectic type.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler structure of symplectic type. Then

\rho_{J}^{B}(X,Y)-\rho_{I}^{B}(X,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp}}F)(X,Y).

Proof.

An important identity
[
28
, Lemma 9.27]
reads as

(2.10)

g((\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I)X,Y)=\rho_{J}^{B}(X,[J,I]Y),

where the upper script
\sharp
stands for the bundle map
g^{-1}:T^{*}M\to TM
.
Using this together with the analogous identity involving
\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}J
we have

\begin{split}g((\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I)X,Y)=\rho_{J}^{B}(X,[J,I]Y),\qquad g((\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}J)X,Y)=\rho_{I}^{B}(X,[J,I]Y).\end{split}

Next, using (
2.6
), we find

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}g\right)(X,Y)=\rho_{I}^{B}(X,IY)+\rho_{I}^{B}(Y,IX)-\rho_{J}^{B}(X,JY)-\rho_{J}^{B}(Y,JX).

Combining the above for
F=-2g(I+J)^{-1}
, we find:

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}(&\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}F)(X,Y)\\
=&\ -\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}g((I+J)^{-1}X,Y)+g((I+J)^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}(I+J)(I+J)^{-1}X,Y)\\
=&\ \rho_{J}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,JY)+\rho_{J}^{B}(Y,J(I+J)^{-1}X)-\rho_{I}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,IY)-\rho_{J}^{B}(Y,I(I+J)^{-1}X)\\
&\ \qquad-\rho_{J}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,[J,I](I+J)^{-1}Y)-\rho_{I}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,[J,I](I+J)^{-1}Y)\\
=&\ \rho_{J}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,IY)+\rho_{J}^{B}(Y,J(I+J)^{-1}X)-\rho_{I}^{B}((I+J)^{-1}X,JY)-\rho_{J}^{B}(Y,I(I+J)^{-1}X)\\
=&\ -\rho_{J}^{B}(X,Y)+\rho_{I}^{B}(X,Y),\end{split}

where we used that
[J,I](I+J)^{-1}=(J-I)
and that
\rho_{J}^{B}
(resp.
\rho_{I}^{B}
) is
I
invariant (resp.
J
-invariant), see
[
28
, Lemma 9.26]
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler structure of symplectic type and
v_{\Theta}=(\sqrt{-1})^{m}\Theta\wedge\overline{\Theta}
a local holomorphic volume form of
(M,J)
, associated to a local non-vanishing section
\Theta
of the canonical line bundle
\Omega^{m,0}(M,J)
. Then the smooth function

\displaystyle\Phi_{J,\Theta}:=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{v_{\Theta}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)\right]

is a potential for
\rho^{B}_{I}
, i.e.

\rho^{B}_{I}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\Phi_{J,\Theta}
.

Proof.

Using (
2.5
) and
F^{[m]}/\omega_{J}^{[m]}=(2^{m})\det(I+J)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we get

\begin{split}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\Phi_{J,\Theta}=&\ \rho^{C}_{J}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}_{J}^{c}\log\det(I+J)=\rho^{C}_{J}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}\left(J(\theta_{J}+\theta_{I})+Jb(\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp})\right)\\
=&\ \rho^{C}_{J}+{\mathrmsl d}(J\theta_{J})+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}\big(F(\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp})\big)=\ \rho^{B}_{J}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}F=\ \rho^{B}_{I},\end{split}

where for passing from the first to the second line we have used Lemma
2.6
, for passing from the second to the third line we have used (
2.8
), and for passing from the third to the fourth line we have used Proposition
2.7
and (
2.4
).
∎

2.4.
The generalized Kähler-Ricci flow

The generalized Kähler-Ricci flow (GKRF) is a solution
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
of the equation

(2.11)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega_{J}=&\ -2\Big(\rho^{B}_{J}\Big)_{J}^{1,1},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}I=\ {\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I.\end{split}

We refer the reader to
[
28
,
58
,
59
,
61
]
for the basic properties of the above equation. The expression given here is the equivalent expresion for GKRF in the “
J
-fixed gauge,” (cf.
[
28
]
Definition 9.22). Our first goal is to show that the condition of being symplectic type is preserved, which follows by recasting the GKRF in terms of
F
and
b
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
denote a solution to generalized Kähler-Ricci flow (
2.11
) which is symplectic-type for all times. Then the associated one-parameter families of two-forms satisfy

(2.12)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F_{t}=-2\rho^{B}_{J}(\omega_{t}),\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}b_{t}=\Delta_{g_{t}}b_{t}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{t}^{\sharp}}b_{t}.

Proof.

By (
2.6
), using that
H={\mathrmsl d}b
and Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

(2.13)

\begin{split}(-2J\rho^{B}_{J})^{\rm skew}=&\ -\delta^{g}H+{\mathrmsl d}\theta-\imath_{\theta^{\sharp}}H=\Delta_{g}b-\mathcal{L}_{\theta^{\sharp}}b.\end{split}

As
\pi_{\Wedge^{1,1}_{J}}F=\omega_{J}
and
(-FJ)^{\rm skew}=b
, equation (
2.13
) shows that the two equalities in the lemma are equivalent. Using the identity (
2.10
) and equations (
2.11
)
we compute below the evolution of
F=-2g(I+J)^{-1}
:

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(X,Y)=\ 2\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g\right)(X,(I+J)^{-1}Y)+2g\left(X,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(I+J)^{-1}Y\right)\\
&\ =2\left(\rho^{B}_{J}(JX,(I+J)^{-1}Y)-\rho^{B}_{J}(X,J(I+J)^{-1}Y)\right)+2g\left((I+J)^{-1}X,(\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I)(I+J)^{-1}Y\right)\\
&\ =2\left(\rho^{B}_{J}(JX,(I+J)^{-1}Y)-\rho^{B}_{J}(X,J(I+J)^{-1}Y)\right)-2\rho^{B}\Bigl([J,I](I+J)^{-1}X,(I+J)^{-1}Y\Bigr)\\
&\ =-2\left(\rho^{B}_{J}(X,J(I+J)^{-1}Y)+\rho^{B}_{J}(X,I(I+J)^{-1}Y)\right)\ =-2\rho^{B}_{J}(X,Y),\end{split}

where for passing to the last line we have used that
\rho^{B}_{J}
is
I
-invariant, see
[
28
, Lemma 9.26]
. ∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
denote a solution to generalized Kähler-Ricci flow such that
(g_{0},I_{0},J)
is symplectic-type. Then
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
is symplectic-type for all
t
for which the flow is defined.

Proof.

If the maximal interval on which the data is symplectic type is
[0,T),T<\infty
, but the solution exists smoothly on
[0,T]
. By integrating the evolution equations over
[0,T]
it follows that the family of endomorphisms
J+I_{t}
extends smoothly across time
T
. Using the evolution equation for
F
from Lemma
2.9
and integrating in time we also conclude that

\displaystyle(J+I_{t})^{-1}=-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{t}^{-1}F_{t}

extends smoothly across time
T
. It follows directly that
J+I_{T}
is well-defined and invertible, contradicting maximality of
T
.
∎

2.5.
The normalized generalized Kähler Ricci flow in the Fano case

As explained in the introduction, in the special case when the symplectic form
F
of a symplectic type generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
belongs to
2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
, we can consider the
normalized
GKRF

(2.14)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega_{J}=&\ -2\Big(\rho^{B}_{J}\Big)_{J}^{1,1}+2\omega_{J},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}I=\ {\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{I}^{\sharp}-\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}I.\end{split}

To get a sense of the possible global existence behaviour of this in the Fano case, we exhibit the behavior of some basic quantities.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(M^{2n},J)
be a Fano manifold, and suppose
(g_{t},I_{t},J)
is a solution of (
2.14
) of symplectic type such that
[F_{0}]=2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
. Then for all
t
, the cohomology class
[F_{t}]
and the Poisson tensor
\sigma_{t}=[I_{t},J]g_{t}^{-1}
(see
1.4
) satisfy

(1)

[F_{t}]=2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
,

(2)

\sigma_{t}=e^{-2t}\sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

The solutions
(g_{t},I_{t})
and
(\tilde{g}_{t},\tilde{I}_{t})
of (
2.11
) and (
2.14
) are related by a reparametrisation

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2t}g_{(1-e^{-2t})},\qquad\tilde{I}_{t}=I_{(1-e^{2t})}.

It follows directly from Lemma
2.9
that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{F}=-2\rho^{B}_{J}(\tilde{g})+2\tilde{F},

proving claim (1). To see claim (2), we first note that along a solution of (
2.11
) one has that
\sigma
is fixed in time (
[
31
, Corollary 1.5]
cf. also
[
28
, Proposition 9.29]
). Claim (2) then follows easily from the reparameterization relationships above.
∎

Thus the symplectic form
F
remains in the canonical class, while the tensor
\sigma
, which measures the deviation of a symplectic-type GK structure from being Kähler, decays exponentially. Based on this, as well as the monotonicity formulae described below in §
5
, one might expect the normalized GKRF (
2.14
) to behave similarly to the much studied normalized Kähler-Ricci flow on Fano manifolds. In particular, by a result of Cao
[
12
]
the solution of the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow exists for all time, and, by results of Tian-Zhu
[
67
,
68
]
, the global solution of the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow composed by automorphisms of
(M,J)
converges to a Kähler-Ricci soliton, provided that the latter exists. Furthermore, by
[
74
]
, in the toric case the reduced flow will converge at the level of normalized potentials. It is natural to ask whether or not similar results hold true for solutions of (
2.14
). More precisely we have:

Conjecture 2.12
.

Suppose
(g,I,J)
is a symplectic type generalized Kähler structure on a Fano manifold
(M,J)
, with
F\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
. Then

•

The solution of (
2.14
) with this initial data exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
.

•

If
(M,J)
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric then the global solution of (
2.14
) converges, in the
C^{\infty}(M)
topology, to a Kähler-Einstein metric.

•

If
(M,J)
admits a Kähler-Ricci soliton, with a soliton vector field
K
, and
(g,I,J)
is invariant by the torus action generated by
K
, then there exists a smooth family of complex automorphisms
\tau_{t}
of
(M,J)
such that the global solution
g_{t}
of (
2.14
) pulled-back by
\tau_{t}
converges, in the
C^{\infty}(M)
topology, to a Kähler-Ricci soliton metric in
c_{1}(M,J)
with soliton vector field
K
.

•

If
(M,J)
is a toric Fano variety and
(g,I,J)
is invariant under the maximal torus
{\mathbb{T}}
, then the reduced equation (
1.5
) has a global solution
\phi_{t}(y)
defined on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and there are families of points
y_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and real constants
c_{t}
, such that
\tilde{\phi}_{t}(y):=\phi_{t}(y+y_{t})+c_{t}
converges in
C^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
to a smooth convex function
\tilde{\phi}_{\infty}(y)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
which defines a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler Ricci soliton on
M
.

3.
Toric generalized Kähler structures

3.1.
Toric Kähler structures

In this section we recall fundamental material on toric Kähler structures going back to
[
2
,
41
]
; we refer to
[
3
,
25
]
for comprehensive surveys. Let
(M,\omega_{0})
be a smooth compact symplectic manifold of real dimension
2m
, endowed with an effective Hamiltonian action of a compact
m
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}
. We denote by
{\mathfrak{t}}
the Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{T}}
, and by
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
the dual vector space. Let
\mu:M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
be a fixed momentum map for the action. Delzant’s theorem
[
18
]
tells us that the image of
\mu(M)
is a Delzant polytope
{\mathrm{P}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, which comes equipped with a minimal set of defining hyperplanes

{{\mathrm{P}}}=\{x\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}:L_{j}(x)=\langle v_{j},x\rangle+\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\,j=1,\ldots,d\},

where
v_{j}
are primitive elements of the lattice
\Lambda\subset{\mathfrak{t}}
of circle subgroups of
{\mathbb{T}}
, i.e.
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathfrak{t}}/2\pi\Lambda
. We denote by
{{\mathrm{L}}}=\{L_{1}(x),\ldots,L_{d}(x)\}
the set of the defining affine-linear functions of
{\mathrm{P}}
as above, which sometimes is referred to as a
labelling
of
{\mathrm{P}}
, see
[
49
]
. According to
[
18
]
, the data
({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
in turn identify
(M,\omega_{0})
, up to a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant symplectomorphism, with the Kähler reduction of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
(endowed with its flat Kähler structure) by a real torus of dimension
d-m
. This gives rise to a
canonical

\omega_{0}
-compatible
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler structure
(g_{c},J_{c},\omega_{0})
on
M
.

We next describe the set
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant,
\omega_{0}
-compatible complex structures on
(M,\omega_{0})
, or equivalently, the set of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics
(g,J,\omega_{0})
with fixed Kähler form
\omega_{0}
. Notice that
J_{c}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
by construction. On the union
M^{0}:=\mu^{-1}(\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}})
of the generic orbits of the
{\mathbb{T}}
-action (where
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}
denotes the interior of
{\mathrm{P}}
), any Kähler metric
g
determined by an element
J
in
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})

has a general expression due to V. Guillemin
[
41
]
. In this description, the
momentum map
\mu:M^{0}\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is supplemented by angular coordinates (depending on
J
)

\theta:M^{0}\to{\mathfrak{t}}/2\pi\Lambda
, such that the kernel of
{\mathrmsl d}\theta
is
orthogonal to the tangent space of the torus orbits. These momentum-angular coordinates
(\mu,\theta)

identify each tangent space to
M^{0}
with
{\mathfrak{t}}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, and the
symplectic form
\omega_{0}
and a compatible Kähler metric

on
M^{0}
have the form

(3.1)

\omega_{0}=\langle{\mathrmsl d}\mu,{\mathrmsl d}\theta\rangle,\qquad g=\langle{\mathrmsl d}\mu,{\mathbf{G}}(\mu),{\mathrmsl d}\mu\rangle+\langle{\mathrmsl d}\theta,{\mathbf{H}}(\mu),{\mathrmsl d}\theta\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes contraction of
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
,
{\mathbf{G}}(\mu)
is (the pull back by
\mu
) of a positive definite
S^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
-valued function on
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}

(with
S^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
denoting the symmetric tensor product of
{\mathfrak{t}}
),

{\mathbf{H}}
is its point-wise inverse in
S^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the
point-wise contraction
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times S^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{R}}
or the
dual contraction. The corresponding complex structure
J
is defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle J{\mathrmsl d}\theta=-\langle{\mathbf{G}}(\mu),{\mathrmsl d}\mu\rangle,\qquad J{\mathrmsl d}\mu=\langle{\mathbf{H}}(\mu),{\mathrmsl d}\theta\rangle,

and
J
is integrable if and only if
{\mathbf{G}}={\rm Hess}(u)
is the Hessian of a
smooth strictly convex function
u(x)
on
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}
, called
symplectic potential
of
(g,J,\omega_{0})
(cf.
[
2
,
41
]
). This applies in particular to the canonical complex structure
J_{c}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
coming from the Delzant construction, giving rise to canonical angular coordinates
\theta^{c}
and symplectic potential
[
41
]

(3.3)

u_{c}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}L_{j}(x)\log L_{j}(x).

Necessary and sufficient conditions for the symplectic potential

to come from a globally defined Kähler metric
(g,J)
on
M
are obtained in
[
2
,
4
,
23
]
. Here we
mention the conditions established in
[
23
]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

A smooth strictly convex function

on
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}
is a symplectic potential of a globally defined
\omega_{0}
-compatible Kähler metric in
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
if and only if

satisfies the following boundary conditions:

•

is smooth and strictly convex in the interior of
{\mathrm{P}}
, and extends as a continuous function on
{\mathrm{P}}
which is smooth and strictly convex on the interior of each face of
{\mathrm{P}}
;

•

u(x)-u_{c}(x)
extends to a smooth function over
{\mathrm{P}}
, where
u_{c}(x)
is given by (
3.3
).

Definition 3.2
.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
the space of functions
u
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
3.1
.

A subtle point in the above description is the fact that the angular coordinates
\theta
depend upon
J
, but one can show
[
4
, Lemma 3]
that by pulling back
J
by a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant symplectomorphism of
(M,\omega_{0})
, we can assume that
\theta=\theta^{c}
, a normalization we shall implicitly use below without further notice.
It follows that the space
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
modulo pull-backs by
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant symplectomorphisms can be identified with the space
\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{\mathrm{L}})
, modulo additions with affine-linear functions. Because of this correspondence, for any
u\in\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{\mathrm{L}})
, with a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
(g_{u},J_{u})
the corresponding
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric on
M
(where we implicitly use the canonical angular coordinates
\theta=\theta^{c}
to define
(g_{u},J_{u})
).

It is a basic fact of the theory (see e.g.
[
49
]
) that any two elements of
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
are biholomorphic under a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant diffeomorphism, which acts trivially on the cohomology class of
[\omega_{0}]
, i.e. for any
J\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
, there exists a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant diffeomorphism
\Phi
, such that
\Phi\cdot J=J_{c}
and
\Phi^{*}\omega_{0}
is the Kähler form of a
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metric in the Kähler class
[\omega_{0}]
on the fixed complex manifold
(M,J_{c})
. The converse is also true by the equivariant Moser lemma. These two equivalent descriptions are often referred to as
symplectic
versus
complex
point of view, respectively, and will be used in our study below. We thus give next an explicit description of this correspondence, based on an observation from
[
41
]
.

For any
u\in\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
, we let

(3.4)

\displaystyle y=\nabla u,\qquad\phi(y)+u(x)=\langle y,x\rangle

be the Legendre transform of the strictly convex smooth function
u
on
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}
. Using the compactness of
\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}
and the strict convexity of
u
, one can see that

y=\nabla u:\mathring{{\mathrm{P}}}\to{\mathfrak{t}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

is a diffeomorphism whereas
\phi(y)
is a strictly convex smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{m})
the space of such functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and let

{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{S}({{\mathrm{P}}},{{\mathrm{L}}})\mapsto\mathcal{C}({{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}),\qquad\mathcal{T}(u(x))=\phi(y)

be the induced map on the corresponding Frechét spaces. Notice that we can recover
u(x)
from its image
\phi(y)
by letting
x=(\nabla\phi)(y)

and using (
3.4
). A simple computation using the definition (
3.4
) shows that the differential of
\mathcal{T}
is given by

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\delta_{u}\mathcal{T})(\dot{u})=-\dot{\phi}.

It turns out that the Legendre transform
\mathcal{T}
underlines the geometric correspondence between the symplectic and complex points of view mentioned above. To see this,
for any
J=J_{u}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
, we shall implicitly identify the abstract variable
y=\nabla u\in{\mathfrak{t}}
with the function
y:M\to{\mathfrak{t}}
given by the composition

, and use
\{y(\mu),\theta^{c}\}
as a system of coordinates on
M^{0}
. Let us introduce a basis
\{K_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\}
for
{\mathfrak{t}}
(usually given by the generators of circle groups of
{\mathbb{T}}
) and write
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})
with respect to this and the dual basis. It is easily seen that the
1

\{{\mathrmsl d}y_{1},\ldots,{\mathrmsl d}y_{m},{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{1},\ldots,{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{m}\}
form a dual basis (at each point of

) of the basis of commuting real holomorphic vector fields
\{-JK_{1},\ldots,-JK_{m},K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}\}
. We can thus consider two systems of holomorphic coordinates on
(M^{0},J)
:
\{y_{1}+\sqrt{-1}\theta_{1},\ldots,y_{m}+\sqrt{-1}\theta_{m}\}
and

\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
with
z_{j}:=e^{y_{j}+\sqrt{-1}\theta_{j}}
. Geometrically, the action of
{\mathbb{T}}
gives rise to a holomorphic action of the complex torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\cong({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}
on
(M,J)
, which is the linear in the coordinates
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
on
M^{0}
. In other words, letting
x_{u}\in{\mathrm{P}}^{0}
be the pre-image of

under
\nabla u
(or, equivalently, the point of minima of
u
) and

the point in

such that

and
\theta(p_{u})=0
, we have the identification
(M^{0},J)=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}\cdot p_{u}
with the orbit of
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
at

, with

being the natural complex coordinate on the

-th factor

(reflecting the fact that
z_{j}(p_{u})=1
). The key observation in
[
41
]
is that the Kähler form
\omega_{0}
is written on
(M^{0},J)
as

(3.6)

\omega_{0}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\phi(y(\mu))=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\phi_{,pq}(y){\mathrmsl d}y_{p}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{q},

where

and, by Legendre duality,
\big(\phi_{,pq}(y)\big)=\big(u_{ij}(x)\big)^{-1}={\bf G}^{-1}(x)={\bf H}(x)
.

The above description holds on
M^{0}
in complex coordinates with respect to
J=J_{u}
. To relate it to the canonical complex structure
J_{c}
of
M
, let
z^{c}=(z_{1}^{c},\ldots,z_{m}^{c})
be the respective holomorphic coordinates on
(M^{0},J_{c})
, obtained from the canonical potential

defined by (

). We let

be the corresponding point in

so that
(M^{0},J_{c})=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}\cdot p_{0}
with
(z_{1}^{c},\ldots,z_{m}^{c})
being the standard coordinates on
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}
. Then, using the boundary conditions for

in Proposition

, the following correspondence is established in
[
24
, pp. 395-396]
:

Proposition 3.3
.

The Legendre transform
\mathcal{T}
defines a bijective map from the space of symplectic potentials
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
to the space
\mathcal{K}({\mathbb{R}}^{m},\omega_{0})
of strictly convex smooth functions

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, satisfying the following conditions:

•

the function defined on
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}\cdot p_{0}=(M^{0},J_{c})
by

\phi(z):=\phi\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\log|z^{c}_{1}|^{2},\ldots,\tfrac{1}{2}\log|z^{c}_{m}|^{2}\big)

gives rise to a Kähler metric
\omega_{\phi}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\phi
on
(M,J_{c})
.

•

\phi(z^{c})-\phi_{c}(z^{c})
extends to a smooth function on
(M,J_{c})
.

In particular,
\mathcal{T}
gives rise to a bijection between the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant
\omega_{0}
-compatible Kähler metrics on
M
, modulo the action of
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant symplectomorphisms of
(M,\omega_{0})
, and the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics in the Kähler class
[\omega_{0}]
on
(M,J_{c})
, modulo the action of
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}
.

3.2.1.
Toric generalized Kähler deformations of type
A

Following
[
11
]

{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant almost complex structure

The integrability of the almost complex structure

Furthermore,

g:=-(\omega_{0}J)^{\rm s}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big(({\bf\Psi}^{\rm s})_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{i}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}+({\bf\Psi}^{-1})^{\rm s}_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{i}{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j}\Big)

(3.10)

\begin{split}&{\bf\Psi}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm s}{\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}={\bf\Psi}^{\rm s},\,\,{\bf\Psi}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm a}{\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}=-{\bf\Psi}^{\rm a};\\
&{\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm a}+{\bf\Psi}^{\rm a}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm s}=0,\,\,{\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm s}+{\bf\Psi}^{\rm a}\big({\bf\Psi}^{-1}\big)^{\rm a}={\rm Id},\end{split}

where
{\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}
stands for the transposed matrix. It follows from (

is positive definite over

if and only if

is positive definite over

A special case when the almost complex structures

(Canonical biholomorphism between

3.2.2.

Following
[
70
]
, let
(g_{A},I_{A},J_{A})
be a generalized Kähler deformation of type
A
on
(M,\omega_{0},{\mathbb{T}})
, defined via (

and

(3.15)

It is observed in

It will be also convenient in this case to express the geometry in terms of the

\phi(y)+u(\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}y_{j}
, we have on
(M^{0},J)
(see (
3.6
)):

\overline{\omega}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\phi=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big({\rm Hess}(\phi(y))\Big)_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge d\theta_{j},

whereas the symplectic
2
-form
F
and the fundamental
2
-form
\omega=F^{1,1}
are given by:

(3.24)

\begin{split}F&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big(\phi_{,ij}{\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j}+\Big[\big({\rm Hess}(\phi)\big)B\big({\rm Hess}(\phi)\big)\Big]_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}y_{j}\Big)\\
&=\overline{\omega}+\overline{\omega}\Pi\overline{\omega};\\
\omega&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big(\phi_{,ij}{\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\Big[\big({\rm Hess}(\phi)\big)B\big({\rm Hess}(\phi)\big)\Big]_{ij}({\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}y_{j}+{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j})\Big)\\
&=\overline{\omega}+\overline{\omega}\left(\Pi^{1,1}\right)\overline{\omega}.\end{split}

In this case,
(y_{j},\overline{\theta}_{j})
are pluriharmonic coordinates of
I
(see (
3.17
)), so similar formulae hold with respect to
I
(in which one should replace
B
by
-B
and
\theta_{j}
by
{\overline{\theta}}_{j}
). We also notice that

(3.25)

{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\phi=\overline{\omega}_{J},\qquad{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\phi=\overline{\omega}_{I},

so, by (
3.21
), we get

(3.26)

F=\tfrac{1}{2}({\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\phi+{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\phi).

3.2.4.
The holomorphic Poisson structure of a toric generalized Kähler structure

The next result gives a geometric meaning of the parameters
A,B\in\Wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
. In particular, they completely determine the associated holomorphic Poisson structures.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(g,I,J)
be a toric GK structure associated to the data
(u,A,B
). Then the corresponding holomorphic Poisson structure on
(M,J)
given by (
1.4
) is

\sigma_{J}=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\big(A_{ij}+\sqrt{-1}B_{ij}\big)\big(K_{i}-\sqrt{-1}JK_{i}\big)\wedge\big(K_{j}-\sqrt{-1}JK_{j}\big),

where
K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}
are the fundamental vector fields associated to a basis of
{\mathfrak{t}}
, and
A_{ij}
and
B_{ij}
are the corresponding components of
A
and
B
in this basis.

Proof.

The above formula follows by straightforward but somewhat tedious matrix computations, using that
K_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}
, (
3.16
), the identification (which in turn follows from (
3.18
))

g^{-1}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}&0\\
0&{\rm Id}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbf{X}}&{\mathbf{Y}}\\
-{\mathbf{Y}}&{\mathbf{X}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf\Psi}&0\\
0&{\rm Id}\end{array}\right),

where

{\mathbf{X}}:=\Big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}+B\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\big)^{-1}B\Big)^{-1},\,\ {\mathbf{Y}}:=-\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\big)^{-1}B{\mathbf{X}},

and the identities (
3.10
). ∎

3.3.
The Bismut-Ricci form of a toric generalized Kähler structure

M. Abreu
[
1
]
obtained the expression of the Ricci form
\rho_{J}
of a Kähler metric
(g,J)\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
in terms of the momentum/angular coordinates and the corresponding
u\in\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
as follows

(3.27)

\rho_{J}=\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\Big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u)\big)(\mu)\Big)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{m}({\bf G}^{-1})_{ij,ik}(\mu){\mathrmsl d}\mu_{k}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j}.

In this subsection we derive the Bismut-Ricci forms
\rho^{B}_{I},\rho^{B}_{J}
associated to a toric generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
, using momentum-angular coordinates of
\omega_{0}
, thus extending (
3.27
).

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(g,I,J)
be a toric generalized Kähler structure, associated to the data
(u,A,B)
. Then, the Bismut-Ricci forms
\rho_{I}^{B},\rho^{B}_{J}
of
(g,J)
are given on
M^{0}
by

\begin{split}\rho^{B}_{I}=&\ \tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\Big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u)\big)(\mu)+\sqrt{-1}B\Big),\\
\rho^{B}_{J}=&\ \tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}\Big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u)\big)(\mu)+\sqrt{-1}B\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

We are going to use Proposition
2.8
with respect to the holomorphic section (defined on
M^{0}
)

\Theta_{J}:=\left((K_{1}-\sqrt{-1}JK_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(K_{m}-\sqrt{-1}JK_{m})\right)^{-1}.

To this end, recall that
K_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}
and, by (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), we have (in momentum/angular coordinates)

(3.28)

\omega_{J}\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\bf\Psi}^{-1}B\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}&-{\bf\Psi}^{-1}{\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\\
{\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}&B\end{array}\right),

so we compute, by using the above together with (
3.18
), (
3.28
), (
3.10
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2^{-m}\left(\frac{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}{v_{\Theta}}\right)&=\det\Big(g(K_{i},K_{j})-\sqrt{-1}\omega_{J}(K_{i},K_{j})\Big)\\
&=\det\Big(\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big({\bf\Psi}^{\rm s}-\sqrt{-1}B\big){\bf\Psi}^{-1}\Big)=\frac{\det\big({\rm Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}B\big)}{\big(\det{\bf\Psi}\big)^{2}},\end{split}

As
\omega_{0}^{[m]}=F^{[m]}
(see (
3.7
) and (
3.15
)), using (
3.7
) and (
3.28
), we also get

\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)=\left(\frac{\omega_{0}^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)=\frac{\det{\bf\Psi}}{\det\left(\mathrm{Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}B\right)},

and therefore

(3.29)

\begin{split}\left[\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{v_{\Theta}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)\right]&=\left[2\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}{v_{\Theta}}\right)\right]\\
&=-\log\det\left(\mathrm{Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}B\right)+\mbox{const}.\end{split}

This yields the formula for
\rho^{B}_{I}
via Proposition
2.8
. Using the symmetry (
3.20
) and that
\det({\rm Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}B)=\det({\rm Hess}(u)-\sqrt{-1}B)
, we get the formula for
\rho^{B}_{J}
. ∎

We now restrict to the special case when the compact toric symplectic manifold
(M,\omega_{0},{\mathbb{T}})
satisfies the (topological) Fano condition
2\pi c_{1}(M,J_{u})=[\omega_{0}]
for any
J_{u}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
. It is well-known that this is equivalent to the assumption that
({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
satisfies

(3.30)

\displaystyle L_{j}(0)=1,\quad\,j=1,\ldots d.

We refer to a Delzant polytope
({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
satisfying (
3.30
) as
barycentred
. In this setting, by (
3.27
) and (
3.6
), we have for any
\omega_{0}
-compatible toric Kähler structure
(g_{u},J_{u})

(3.31)

\rho_{J_{u}}-\omega_{0}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u(\mu))\big)-(-u(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}u_{,i}(\mu))\Big).

Using the boundary conditions for
u
in Proposition
3.1
(which imply that
\det{\rm Hess}(u)=\frac{\delta(x)}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}L_{j}(x)}
for a positive smooth function
\delta
on
{\mathrm{P}}
, see
[
2
]
) and the Fano condition (
3.30
), the function

(3.32)

h_{u}(\mu):=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u(\mu))\big)-(-u(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}u_{,i}(\mu))

appearing at the RHS of (
3.31
) is smooth on
M
. It thus follows that
\rho_{J_{u}}-\omega_{0}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J_{u}}h_{u}

globally
on
M
.

Similar to the Kähler toric Fano case, a generalized Kähler toric structure corresponding to data
(u,A,B)
also admits a relative Ricci potential:

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose
({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
is a barycentred Delzant polytope corresponding to a toric Fano variety. Then, for any toric GK structure
(g,I,J)
corresponding to the data
(u,A,B)
, the function

\begin{split}{{h}}_{u,B}(x)&:=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\left({\rm Hess}(u(x))+\sqrt{-1}B\right)-\big(-u(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}u_{,j}(x)\big)\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\log\det\left(\big({\rm Hess}(\phi(y))\big)^{-1}+\sqrt{-1}B\right)-\phi(y)\end{split}

pulls-back by the momentum map
\mu
of
F
to a smooth function on
M
. Furthermore, if
A=0
we have

\begin{split}&\rho^{B}_{J}-\overline{\omega}_{I}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}(h_{u,B}(\mu)),\qquad\rho^{B}_{I}-\overline{\omega}_{J}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}(h_{u,B}(\mu)),\\
&\tfrac{1}{2}(\rho^{B}_{J}+\rho^{B}_{I})-F=\tfrac{1}{2}\big({\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{I}(h_{u,B}(\mu))+{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}(h_{u,B}(\mu))\big).\end{split}

Proof.

The smoothness of
{{h}}_{u,B}(\mu)
follows from the smoothness of
h_{u}(\mu)
in (
3.32
), and the fact that
\det({\rm Hess}(u))/\det({\rm Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}B)
is smooth and positive on
M
as it computes the ratio
F^{m}/\omega_{J}^{m}
, where
(F,\omega_{J})
is the GK structure obtained by a
B
-transformation of the Kähler structure
({\overline{g}}_{u},\omega_{0})
. The Bismut-Ricci curvature formulas follow from Lemma
3.10
, (
3.25
), and (
3.26
). ∎

4.
The normalized generalized Kähler-Ricci flow on a toric Fano variety

4.1.
Scalar Reduction

We now describe the NGKRF (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
, starting from a toric generalized Kähler structure
(g,I,J)
on a smooth toric Fano manifold
(M,\omega_{0})
. We suppose that the initial symplectic form
F
belongs to
2\pi c_{1}(M,J_{0})
, and that
(g,I,J)
satisfies Convention
1
(so we have
\overline{\omega}=\omega_{0}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
as well). The solution of (
1.2
) is defined in terms of Hermitian metrics
(g_{t},J)
on a fixed complex manifold
(M,J)
, whereas Convention
1
normalizes (via the action of a
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant diffeomorphism) the toric GK structures on
M
to be obtained from a Kähler structure
({\overline{g}}_{u},{\overline{J}}_{u})
compatible with the fixed symplectic form
\omega_{0}
and canonical angular coordinates via deformations of type
A
and
B
. This is similar to the symplectic versus complex viewpoints in the Kähler case (see Proposition
3.3
), and the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant diffeomorphism in Lemma
3.6
connects the two points of view in the generalized Kähler case. The next proposition makes this explicit.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(g_{0},I_{0},J_{0})
be a toric GK structure on a Fano toric variety, corresponding to the data
(u_{0},A_{0},B_{0})
with respect to a symplectic form
\omega_{0}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J_{0})
.
Denote by
(g_{t},I_{t},J_{t})
the family of GK structures corresponding to the solution
(u_{t},A_{t},B_{t})
of

(4.1)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=&\ \log\Big({\det}\big({\rm Hess}(u)+\sqrt{-1}e^{-2t}B_{0}\big)\Big)-2\Big(-u+\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}u_{,j}\Big),\\
A_{t}=&\ e^{-2t}A_{0},\qquad B_{t}=e^{-2t}B_{0}.\end{split}

Then
\Phi_{t}^{*}(g_{t},I_{t},J_{t})
solves (
1.2
), where
\Phi_{t}^{*}
is the canonical biholomorphism between
(M,J_{0})
and
(M,J_{t})
from Lemma
3.6
.

Proof.

We shall do computations on
M^{0}
, using by Lemma
3.11
that for
u_{t}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
the RHS of (
4.1
) is a smooth function on
{\mathrm{P}}
, so (
4.1
) is a flow of elements
u_{t}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathrm{P}},{{\mathrm{L}}})
defined for
t\in[0,T)
, thus giving rise to a smooth path of GK structures
(g_{t},I_{t},J_{t}),t\in[0,T)
on
M
, obtained as deformations of type
A
and
B
of the Kähler structures
(g_{u_{t}},J_{u_{t}})
in
\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
with the
2
-vectors
A_{t},B_{t}\in\Wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
.

We denote by
J_{t}
the complex structure defined by the deformation of type
A
of the complex structures
J_{u_{t}}\in\mathcal{K}^{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
with the
2
-vector
A_{t}=e^{-2t}A_{0}\in\Wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
, and by
F_{t}=\omega_{0}+e^{-2t}\langle{\mathrmsl d}\mu,B_{0},d\mu\rangle
the symplectic form of
(g_{t},J_{t},I_{t})
, with
I_{t}
being the
F_{t}
-conjugate of the almost complex structure
J_{t}
. Denote by
\tilde{y}_{t}
the
J_{t}
-pluriharmonic variables (
3.12
), and consider the
{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant diffeomorphism
\Phi_{t}
defined in Lemma
3.6
, which satisfies
\Phi_{t}(\tilde{y}_{t})=\tilde{y}_{0}
and
\Phi_{t}^{*}(J_{t})=J_{0}=:J
. We let
\tilde{F}_{t}:=\Phi_{t}^{*}(F_{t})
, so that
\tilde{F}_{t}
is a symplectic form on
(M,J)
which gives rise to
a GK structure
(\tilde{g}_{t},\tilde{I}_{t},J)
compatible with
\tilde{F}_{t}
. We will check that with
(u_{t},A_{t},B_{t})
satisfying (
4.1
), the symplectic forms
\tilde{F}_{t}
satisfy

(4.2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{F}_{t}=-2\rho_{J}^{B}(\tilde{g}_{t})+2\tilde{F}_{t}.

Taking the
(1,1)
part with respect to
J
of (
4.2
) yields (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
.

For a toric GK structure
(g,I,J)
, corresponding to the data
(u,A,B)
, we consider the pluriharmonic variables
\tilde{y}
with respect to
J
, defined in (
3.12
), and a function
\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{y})=\phi(y)
, introduced on
M^{0}
by

(4.3)

\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{y}):=-u(\mu)+\langle\mu,\tilde{y}\rangle=-u(\mu)+\langle\mu,y\rangle=\phi(y),

where
y_{j}=u_{,j}(\mu)
, and
\phi(y)
is the Legendre transform (
3.4
) of
u
. Using (
3.16
)
we obtain the following formula (the detailed verification of which we leave to the reader)

(4.4)

{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\tilde{\phi}=F+{\mathrmsl d}J\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}A_{ij}\mu_{i}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}\Big)-{\mathrmsl d}\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}B_{ij}\mu_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}\Big).\\

Using (
4.4
), we have that

\begin{split}\tilde{F}_{t}=&\ \Phi_{t}^{*}\Big({\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J_{t}}{\tilde{\phi}}_{t}({\tilde{y}}_{t})-{\mathrmsl d}J_{t}\big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\big(A_{t})_{ij}\mu_{i}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}\big)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(B_{t})_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}\Big)\\
=&\ {\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}{\tilde{\phi}}_{t}({\tilde{y}}_{0})-{\mathrmsl d}J\big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\big(A_{t})_{ij}\mu^{t}_{i}{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{j}\big)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(B_{t})_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{j}\Big),\end{split}

where
\mu^{t}:=\mu\circ\Phi_{t}
.
Using (
4.1
), we get

(4.5)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{F}_{t}=&\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big({\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\tilde{\phi}_{t}({\tilde{y}}_{0})-{\mathrmsl d}J\big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(A_{t})_{ij}\mu_{i}^{t}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}^{t}\big)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(B_{t})_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{j}\Big)\\
=&\ {\mathrmsl d}J{\mathrmsl d}\Big(\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{t}}{\partial t}(\tilde{y}_{0})\Big)+2{\mathrmsl d}J\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(A_{t})_{ij}\mu_{i}^{t}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}^{t}\Big)-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}(B_{t})_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\mu^{t}_{j}\\
&\ \qquad-{\mathrmsl d}J\Big[\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big((A_{t})_{ij}\big(\frac{\partial{\mu}_{i}^{t}}{\partial t}\big){\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}^{t}+(A_{t})_{ij}{\mu}_{i}^{t}{\mathrmsl d}\big(\frac{\partial{\mu}_{j}^{t}}{\partial t}\big)\Big)\Big]\\
&\ \qquad+{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Big((B_{t})_{ij}\big(\frac{\partial{\mu}_{i}^{t}}{\partial t}\big){\mathrmsl d}\mu_{j}^{t}+(B_{t})_{ij}{\mu}_{i}^{t}{\mathrmsl d}\big(\frac{\partial{\mu}_{j}^{t}}{\partial t}\big)\Big)\Big].\end{split}

We thus need to compute the
t
derivatives of
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
and
\tilde{\mu}^{t}
. We start with computing the
t
derivative of
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
. By (
3.12
), (
4.3
) and (
4.1
), we first compute

(4.6)

\frac{\partial\tilde{y}_{r}^{t}}{\partial t}=\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}-2\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)_{r\ell}\mu_{\ell}.

Therefore, we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}_{t}}{\partial t}(\tilde{y}_{t})=&\ -\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{\partial{\tilde{\phi}}_{t}}{\partial{\tilde{y}}_{r}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial\tilde{y}_{r}}{\partial t}\Big)-\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial t}(\mu)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mu_{r}\Big(\frac{\partial\tilde{y}_{r}}{\partial t}\Big)\\
=&\ -\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial t}(\mu)-\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(A_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}\mu_{k}\\
=&\ -\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)+2\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y}_{t})\\
&\ \qquad-\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(A_{t}{\big(\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}\mu_{k},\end{split}

where for passing from the first line to the second we have used (
4.6
) and the fact that
A_{ij}
is skew-symmetric, and for passing from the second line to the third we have used (
4.1
) and the fact that
\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{y}_{t})=\phi_{t}(y_{t})
(see (
4.3
)).

We now compute the derivative of
\mu^{t}=\mu\circ\Phi_{t}
. Using that
\mu_{j}^{t}(\tilde{y}_{t})=\mu_{j}
and (
4.6
), we get

(4.8)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial{\mu}_{j}^{t}}{\partial t}(\tilde{y}_{t})&=2\mu_{j}-\sum_{r=1}^{m}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}_{jr}\big(\log{\det}\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}.\end{split}

Thus, substituting back in (
4.5
) and using (
4.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\big(\Phi_{t}^{*})^{-1}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{F}_{t}\Big)\\
&\qquad={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J_{t}}\Big[2\tilde{\phi}_{t}-\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)-\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(A_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\mu_{k}\big(\log\big(\det{\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad-2{\mathrmsl d}J_{t}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}(A_{t})_{kr}\mu_{k}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{r}\Big]+2{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}(B_{t})_{kr}\mu_{k}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{r}\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad-{\mathrmsl d}J_{t}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(A_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{k}\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad+{\mathrmsl d}J_{t}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\mu_{k}{\mathrmsl d}\Big(\big(A_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}\Big)\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad+2{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(B_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{k}\Big].\end{split}

Furthermore, using Lemma
3.10
and (
3.16
), we finally have

\begin{split}&\big(\Phi_{t}^{*})^{-1}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{F}_{t}\Big)=2F_{t}-{\mathrmsl d}J_{t}{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad-2{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\sum_{\ell,r=1}^{m}\Big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{-1}A_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{\ell r}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{\ell}\Big)\Big]\\
&\qquad\quad+2{\mathrmsl d}\Big[\sum_{k,r=1}^{m}\big(B_{t}\big({\bf\Psi}_{t}^{\rm T}\big)^{-1}\big)_{kr}\big(\log\det\big({\rm Hess}(u_{t})+\sqrt{-1}B_{t}\big)\big)_{,r}{\mathrmsl d}\mu_{k}\Big]\\

as required.

4.2.
Evolution equations

Here we determine the evolution of various quantities along NGKRF. To begin we recall the Chern Laplacian and recall the explicit form for its action on
(p,0)
-forms.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(M,g,J)
denote a Hermitian manifold, with associated Chern connection. Let
\Delta^{C}_{g}:\Gamma(\Wedge^{p,q}(M))\to\Gamma(\Wedge^{p,q}(M))
, denote the
Chern Laplacian
, defined by

\Delta^{C}_{g}\psi=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\nabla^{C})^{2}_{E_{i},\overline{E_{i}}}\psi,

where
E_{i}:=e_{i}-\sqrt{-1}Je_{i},\,i=1,\ldots m
for a
J
-adapted orthonormal frame
\{e_{i},Je_{i}\}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Fix a Hermitian manifold
(M^{2n},g,J)
with Chern connection
\nabla^{c}
and let
H=-d^{c}\omega
. For
\psi\in\Wedge^{p,0}
,
\partial\psi=0
, and
Z_{1},\ldots Z_{p}\in T^{1,0}M
, one has

\displaystyle(\Delta^{C}_{g}\psi)(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p})=\Big(\Delta_{g}\psi+H\star({\mathrmsl d}\psi)+{\mathrmsl d}\left(H\star\psi\right)-{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta^{\sharp}}\psi\Big)_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p}},

where for a
(q+1)
-form
\psi
, we let

(H\star\psi)_{X_{1},\ldots X_{q}}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,k=1}^{2m}\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{j}H(e_{i},e_{k},X_{j})\psi(e_{i},e_{k},X_{1},\ldots,\hat{X}_{j},\ldots,X_{q}).

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward computation left as an exercise.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\overline{\omega}_{\phi}={\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\phi
be a toric Kähler metric on
(M,J)
, described by its toric Kähler potential
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, associated to a toric GK structure
(g_{\phi},I_{\phi},J)
corresponding to
A=0,B\in\Wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{t}}
. Then, for a variation
\phi_{s}=\phi_{0}+s\dot{\phi}
of
\phi
one has

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}\log\det\left(\left({\rm Hess}(\phi_{s})\right)^{-1}+\sqrt{-1}B\right)^{-1}=\Delta^{C}_{g_{\phi}}\dot{\phi}.

Proof.

We denote by
{\bf H}:={\rm Hess}(\phi(y))=\big({\rm Hess}(u(x))\big)^{-1}
and notice that

\big({\bf H}^{-1}+\sqrt{-1}B\big)^{-1}={\mathbf{X}}+\sqrt{-1}{\mathbf{Y}}

with
{\mathbf{X}}=\big({\bf H}^{-1}+B{\bf H}B\big)^{-1},\,\,{\mathbf{Y}}=-{\bf H}B{\mathbf{X}}
.
Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\delta_{\phi}\Big(\log\det\big({\bf H}^{-1}+\sqrt{-1}B\big)\Big)(\dot{\phi})=-{\rm tr}\Big(\big({\mathbf{X}}+\sqrt{-1}{\mathbf{Y}}\big){\bf H}^{-1}\dot{\bf H}{\bf H}^{-1}\Big)\\
&\,=-{\rm tr}\Big({\bf H}^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}{\bf H}^{-1}{\rm Hess}(\dot{\phi})\Big)=-{\rm tr}\Big(\big[{\bf H}[{\bf H}^{-1}+B{\bf H}B]{\bf H}\big]^{-1}{\rm Hess}(\dot{\phi})\Big)\\
&\,=-{\rm tr}\Big(\big[{\bf H}+{\bf H}B{\bf H}B{\bf H}\big]^{-1}{\rm Hess}(\dot{\phi})\Big).\ \end{split}

Notice that for any
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant smooth function
f
on
M
, viewed as a function
f(y)
in the holomorphic coordinates
(y_{j}+\sqrt{-1}\theta_{j})
, we have

{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}f(y)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\big({\rm Hess}(f(y))\big)_{ij}{\mathrmsl d}y_{i}\wedge{\mathrmsl d}\theta_{j}.

whereas the inverse of
\omega_{\phi}
is computed from (
3.24
) to be

(\omega_{\phi})^{-1}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tilde{{\mathbf{X}}}_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}_{ij}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\Big)

with
\tilde{{\mathbf{X}}}=\big[{\bf H}+{\bf H}B{\bf H}B{\bf H}\big]^{-1},\,\tilde{{\mathbf{Y}}}=B{\bf H}\tilde{{\mathbf{X}}}
.
We thus get

\Delta^{C}_{g_{\phi}}f=-{\rm tr}\Big(\big[{\bf H}+{\bf H}B{\bf H}B{\bf H}\big]^{-1}{\rm Hess}(f(y))\Big)

and the claim follows. ∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be a solution of (
4.12
). Then one has

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\varphi}_{t}&=\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\dot{\varphi}_{t}+2\dot{\varphi}_{t}-2|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}.\end{split}

Proof.

Notice that by Proposition
3.3
,
\varphi_{t}(y)=\phi_{t}(y)-\phi_{0}(y)
extends to a smooth function on
M
, thus so does
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=\dot{\phi}_{t}
. We take derivative with respect to
t
in (
4.9
) and use Lemma
4.7
to compute

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\phi}_{t}=&\ \Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\dot{\phi}_{t}+2{\rm tr}\big[B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\mathbf{X}}_{t}\big]+2\dot{\phi}_{t}=\ \Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\dot{\phi}_{t}+2m-2{\rm tr}\big[{\bf H}_{t}^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}_{t}\big]+2\dot{\phi}_{t}\\
=&\ \Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\dot{\phi}_{t}+2m-2{\rm tr}\big[{\rm Id}+{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}\big]^{-1}+2\dot{\phi}_{t},\end{split}

where we have set
B_{t}=e^{-2t}B_{0}
, and
{\bf H}_{t}
and
{\mathbf{X}}_{t}
are the objects defined in the proof of Lemma
4.7
with respect to
\phi_{t}
.

As
F_{t}J=-g_{t}-b_{t}
, we have

|F_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}=\tfrac{1}{2}|F_{t}J|^{2}_{g_{t}}=m+|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}},

noting that, by our convention, the norm of a
2
-form is half the norm of the corresponding tensor.
Using that
F_{t}=-2{g}_{t}(J+I_{t})^{-1}
we obtain

2m+2|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}=4|(J+I_{t})^{-1}|^{2}_{g_{t}}=-4{\rm tr}(I_{t}+J)^{-2}

whereas by (
3.16
) (recalling that
A=0
and
{\bf H}={\bf\Psi}^{-1}
in our case) we compute

(J+I_{t})^{2}\sim-4\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Id}+B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}&0\\
0&{\rm Id}+B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}\end{array}\right),

so that

m+|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}={\rm tr}\big[{\rm Id}+B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}\big]^{-1}.

The claim follows. ∎

Using Lemma
4.6
, we can refine the result of Lemma

, giving a useful evolution equation for the

, which yields a key differential inequality.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(M^{2n},g_{t},I_{t},J)
be a solution to GKRF with symplectic-type initial data, and set
\beta_{J}=\pi_{\Wedge^{2,0}_{J}}b
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\beta_{J}=

\displaystyle\ \Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\beta_{J},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left|\beta_{J}\right|^{2}=\ \Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\left|\beta_{J}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla\beta_{J}\right|-\left|\overline{\partial}\beta_{J}\right|^{2}-\left|\beta_{J}\righthalfcup H^{2,1}\right|^{2}.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.6
to
\beta_{J}
. In this case,
H={\mathrmsl d}b
and
{\mathrmsl d}\beta=H^{2,1}
, so that the term
(H\star d\beta)_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}
is zero. By Lemma

, the term

is exact, and thus
({\mathrmsl d}(H\star\beta))_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}=(d(b\righthalfcup H))_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}=0
. We conclude that

\Delta^{C}_{g}\beta_{J}=\Big(\Delta_{g}\beta_{J}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}\beta_{J}\Big)^{2,0}.

As
\Delta_{g}-{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{J}^{\sharp}}
is a real operator, it follows from Lemma
2.9
that the evolution equation of
\beta_{t}
under the GKRF is

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\beta_{J}=\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\beta_{J},

which is the formula obtained in
[
55
]
, noticing that in this context
\beta_{J}=\partial\alpha
and
b=\Re(\partial\alpha)
in the notation of
[
55
]
. The evolution of
\left|\beta_{J}\right|^{2}
then follows from the established evolution for
\beta_{J}
and a Bochner formula (cf.
[
56

Lemma 4.7).
∎

4.3.
Long-time existence

In this subsection we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We first derive an estimate for the tensor
b
which holds without any symmetry hypotheses. Next we derive two key a priori estimates for the potential
\varphi
in the toric setting. Using these and further maximum principle arguments we prove the long time existence.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(M^{2n},g_{t},I_{t},J)
be a solution to GKRF with symplectic-type initial data defined on
[0,T)
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times[0,T)}\left|b\right|^{2}\leq\sup_{M\times\{0\}}\left|b\right|^{2}.

Proof.

This follows by applying the maximum principle to the second relation in Proposition
4.9
, noting that
b=\Re(\beta_{J})
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
denote a solution to (
4.12
) on a toric Fano manifold. There exists a constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times\{t\}}\left(\left|\varphi\right|+\left|\dot{\varphi}\right|\right)\leq Ce^{2t}.

Proof.

Applying the maximum principle directly to (
4.14
) yields the upper bound
\dot{\varphi}\leq Ce^{2t}
. By Proposition
4.10
), we have
|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}\leq C
. Using this and applying the maximum principle to (
4.14
) yield the lower bound
\dot{\varphi}\geqslant-Ce^{2t}
. Using the estimate of
\dot{\varphi}
the bound for
\varphi
follows by integration in time.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.8
the given invariant data is equivalent to a triple
(u_{0},A_{0},B_{0})
. By Corollary
4.3
the solution to NGKRF is described by a triple
(u_{t},0,e^{-2t}B_{0})
, where
u_{t}
is a solution to (
4.1
). Taking Legendre transform, by Proposition
4.4
this solution is equivalently described by
\varphi_{t}
which solves (
4.12
). We will prove the long time existence of the flow
\varphi_{t}
which in turn proves the long time existence of (
1.2
) by the discussion above.

Equation (
4.12
) is strictly parabolic for
\varphi
, thus the short-time existence is guaranteed by general theory. To establish global existence, we first aim to establish uniform parabolicity of the equation, i.e. uniform equivalence of the time-varying metrics. To obtain the upper bound
we first recall that by
[
6
, Lemma 6.9]
, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{{g}_{t}}\bigg)\log\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\overline{\omega}_{0}}\omega_{t}\leq|T^{c}_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}+C\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\omega_{t}}(\overline{\omega}_{0}),

where
T^{c}
is the torsion of the Chern connection. To control the two terms on the right hand side of this inequality, we will augment our test function with two auxiliary terms. We first observe that by Proposition
4.9
, using that
db=H
and dropping some negative terms we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\right)\left|b_{t}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}\leq-\left|T^{c}\right|^{2}_{g_{t}}.

Furthermore, observe that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\bigg)\varphi=&\ \dot{\varphi}-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\omega_{t}}{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}_{J}\varphi=\ \dot{\varphi}-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\omega_{t}}\overline{\omega}_{t}+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\omega_{t}}\overline{\omega}_{0}.\end{split}

Notice that

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\omega_{t}}(\overline{\omega}_{t})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\big[{\rm Id}+{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}{\bf H}_{t}B_{t}\big]^{-1}=m+|b_{t}|_{g_{t}}^{2}\leq C,

where the last inequality follows from Proposition
4.10
. Using this together with Proposition
4.11
in (
4.17
) yields

(4.18)

\displaystyle-C+\tr_{\omega_{t}}\overline{\omega}_{0}\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g_{t}}^{C}\right)\varphi\leq C+\tr_{\omega_{t}}\overline{\omega}_{0}.

Now set

\Psi_{1}:=\log\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\overline{\omega}_{0}}\omega_{t}+A\Big(|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}+\varphi\Big).

For
A
chosen sufficiently large, it follows from (
4.15
), (
4.16
), and (
4.18
) that

\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\bigg)\Psi_{1}\leq CA.

Applying the maximum principle, and using that
\left|\varphi\right|
is already bounded by Proposition
4.11
we conclude that for any
T>0
there is a constant
C
so that

\sup_{M\times[0,T]}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{\overline{\omega}_{0}}\omega_{t}\leq C.

To obtain the lower bound we get a lower bound for the volume form. First, by
[
6
, Lemma 6.7]
, we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\right)\log\frac{\omega_{t}^{m}}{\overline{\omega}_{0}^{m}}\geqslant-C\tr_{\omega_{t}}\overline{\omega}_{0}.

Now set

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}=\log\frac{\omega_{t}^{m}}{\overline{\omega}_{0}^{m}}+A\varphi.

By (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta^{C}_{g_{t}}\right)\Psi_{2}\geqslant-C.

Applying the maximum principle establishes the lower bound for the determinant. It follows that
C^{-1}g_{0}\leq g_{t}\leq Cg_{0}
on any finite time interval. As
|b_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}
is bounded, we can now apply
[
48
, Theorem 1.2]
to obtain the higher order regularity and hence the long time existence.
∎

5.
Monotone functionals and behavior at infinity

In this section we analyze the behavior of NGKRF at infinity. First we derive an extension of Perelman’s shrinker entropy to the GKRF (without toric symmetry), and use it to derive the uniform
\kappa
-noncollapsing estimate and convergence of nonsingular solutions of Theorem
1.2
. Next we derive an extension of the Mabuchi
K
-energy to the GK setting, and show that it is monotone along the GKRF with toric symmetry. This monotonicity is the key point behind the convergence statements of Theorem
1.3
.

5.1.
Entropy monotonicity

In this subsection we observe that a modification of Perelman’s entropy functional
[

]
is monotone along generalized Kähler-Ricci flow for structures of symplectic type. Our discussion is a direct adaptation of
[
28
, Chapter 6]
, (cf. also
[
60
]
). There the monotonicity formulae and applications are contingent upon a further monotone quantity dubbed a torsion-bounding subsolution. In the setting of generalized Kähler structures of symplectic type the torsion potential
b
plays this role, and we make this explicit below

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(M^{2m},g,I,J)
be a generalized Kähler structure of symplectic type.
Fix

and
\tau>0
. The

associated to this data is

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(g,H,f,\tau):=\int_{M}\left[\tau\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}+R-\frac{1}{12}\left|H\right|^{2}\right)-\left|b\right|^{2}+f-2m\right](4\pi\tau)^{-m}e^{-f}dV.

Furthermore, let

\displaystyle\mu\left(g,H,\tau\right):=\inf_{\left\{f\ |\int_{M}(4\pi\tau)^{-m}e^{-f}dV=1\right\}}\mathcal{W}\left(g,H,f,\tau\right),

Definition 5.2
.

5.2.
The extended Mabuchi functional

In this section we give an extension of the Mabuchi functional to the setting of GK structures of symplectic type on a Kähler background. To begin we first observe that it is possible to canonically associate a Kähler metric to every symplectic-type GK structure on a Kähler manifold. First, using Hodge decomposition, the deRham class
\alpha=[F]
defines an element
\overline{\alpha}\in H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Using that
F
tames
J
and a result of Demailly-Paun
[
19
]
, it follows that
\overline{\alpha}
is a Kähler class of
(M,J)
. By Yau’s theorem
[
73
]
, we can find a unique Kähler metric
\overline{\omega}\in\overline{\alpha}
with the property

(5.1)

\overline{\omega}^{[m]}=cF^{[m]},\qquad c=\frac{\overline{\alpha}^{[m]}}{[\alpha]^{[m]}}.

Definition 5.6
.

Given
(M,J)
Kähler and
\alpha\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
, let
{\bf GK}(M,J,\alpha)
be the space of symplectic-type GK structures
(g,I,J)
such that
[F]=\alpha
. Given
(g,I,J)\in{\bf GK}(M,J,\alpha)
, the
Kähler reduction
is the unique Kähler metric
\overline{\omega}
satisfying (
5.1
).

Remark 5.7
.

Kähler reductions exist in smooth families along a solution to GKRF of symplectic type. In general it might not be possible to derive an explicit formula for the evolution of
\overline{\omega}
, but in the toric Fano case this is what is achieved in Proposition
4.4
.

We now recall the definitions of the Aubin-Mabuchi functionals
{\bf I}
and
{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}}
which are defined on the space of
\overline{\omega}_{0}
-relative Kähler potentials
\varphi
by

(5.2)

\begin{split}(\delta_{\varphi}{\bf I})(\dot{\varphi})&=\int_{M}\dot{\varphi}\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]},\hskip 34.14322pt\qquad{\bf I}(0)=0,\\
(\delta_{\varphi}{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}})(\dot{\varphi})&=\int_{M}\dot{\varphi}\rho_{0}\wedge\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m-1]},\qquad{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}}(0)=0\end{split}

where
\rho_{0}
is the Ricci form of
\overline{\omega}_{0}
. As
\rho_{0}\in 2\pi c_{1}(M,J)
,
letting
a:=4\pi\frac{c_{1}(M,J)\cdot[\overline{\alpha}]^{[m-1]}}{[\overline{\alpha}]^{[m]}}
the expression

a{\bf I}(\varphi)-2{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}}(\varphi)
does not change if we add to

a real constant, i.e. it introduces a functional, denoted by
\left(a{\bf I}-2{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}}\right)(\overline{\omega}_{\varphi})
, acting on the Kähler metrics
\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}\in\overline{\alpha}
.

Definition 5.8
.

Fix
(M,J)
Kähler and
\alpha\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
\overline{\alpha}\in H^{1,1}(M,\mathbb{R})
is a Kähler class with representative
\overline{\omega}_{0}\in\overline{\alpha}
. Fix
(g,I,J)\in{\bf GK}(M,J,\alpha)
and let
\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}
denote its Kähler reduction. Define the

by

(5.3)

\begin{split}{\bf M}(F)&:=\left(\frac{\overline{\alpha}^{m}}{[\alpha]^{m}}\right)\int_{M}\left[\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\overline{\omega}_{0}^{[m]}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{F^{[m]}}{\omega_{J}^{[m]}}\right)\right]F^{[m]}+\left(a{\bf I}-2{\bf I}^{\rho_{0}}\right)(\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}),\\
a&:=4\pi\frac{c_{1}(M,J)\cdot\overline{\alpha}^{[m-1]}}{\overline{\alpha}^{[m]}}.\end{split}

Remark 5.9
.

In the case when
\alpha=\overline{\alpha}
is a Kähler class, one has that
\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]}=F^{[m]}\geq\omega_{J}^{[m]}
. This implies the basic inequality

where

is the usual Mabuchi energy of the Kähler metric

[
13
]
.

Given a one-parameter family of symplectic potentials

Lemma 5.12

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}{\bf M}(F_{t})=&\ 2\int_{M}\tr_{\omega_{t}}\left(\rho^{B}_{J}(g_{t})-\overline{\omega}_{t}\right)\overline{\omega}_{t}^{[m]}+\int_{M}\big(\dot{\varphi}_{t}-2\varphi_{t}+2h_{0}\big)({\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\dot{\varphi}_{t})\wedge\overline{\omega}_{t}^{[m-1]}\\

Lemma 5.13

Suppose

is a compact GK manifold of symplectic type. Then

\int_{M}\left[\tr_{\omega_{J}}(\rho^{B}_{J})-\tr_{F}(\rho^{B}_{J})\right]F^{[m]}=-\frac{1}{8}\int_{M}\left[\Big|{\mathrmsl d}\log\det(I+J)-\theta_{I}-\theta_{J}\Big|^{2}_{g}+|\theta_{I}-\theta_{J}|^{2}_{g}\right]F^{[m]}.

Proof.

We denote by
s_{J}^{B}
and

the Bismut-Ricci scalar curvatures of
(g,J)
and
(g,I)
, respectively. By Proposition

\rho^{B}_{J}-\rho^{B}_{I}=\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{(\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp})}F,\qquad s_{J}^{B}-s_{I}^{B}=-\delta^{g}(\theta_{J}-\theta_{I}).

Using that

and the above formula, we compute

\begin{split}&2\left[\tr_{\omega_{J}}(\rho^{B}_{J})-\tr_{F}(\rho^{B}_{J})\right]=s_{J}^{B}-\big\langle\rho^{B}_{J},\omega_{J}+\omega_{I}\big\rangle_{g}=\tfrac{1}{2}s_{J}^{B}-\big\langle\rho^{B}_{J},\omega_{I}\big\rangle_{g}\\
&\qquad=\tfrac{1}{2}(s_{J}^{B}-s_{I}^{B})-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{(\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp})}F,\omega_{I}\Big\rangle_{g}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{g}(\theta_{J}-\theta_{I})-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{(\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp})}F,\omega_{I}\Big\rangle_{g}.\end{split}

We next note that, by (

\begin{split}2\left[\tr_{\omega_{J}}(\rho^{B}_{J})-\tr_{F}(\rho^{B}_{J})\right]&=-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{g}(\theta_{J}-\theta_{I})-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big\langle{\mathrmsl d}\left(I\left((\theta_{J}-\theta_{I})+b(\theta_{J}^{\sharp}-\theta_{I}^{\sharp})\right)\right),\omega_{I}\Big\rangle_{g}\\

\begin{split}2\left[\tr_{\omega_{J}}(\rho^{B}_{J})-\tr_{F}(\rho^{B}_{J})\right]&=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-\Delta^{g}\log\det(I+J)-\delta^{g}(\theta_{I}+\theta_{J})+\big\langle-2\theta_{I}+{\mathrmsl d}\log\det(I+J),\theta_{I}\big\rangle_{g}\right)\\

and integration by parts to get

\begin{split}&\int_{M}\left[\tr_{\omega_{J}}(\rho^{B}_{J})-\tr_{F}(\rho^{B}_{J})\right]F^{[m]}\\

\mathrmsl{Aut}_{0}(M,J)
. We first recall what this precisely means. Denote by

{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})=\left\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)\,|\,\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}:=\overline{\omega}_{0}+{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\varphi>0\right\}

the space of
\overline{\omega}_{0}
-relative Kähler potentials on
M
, and consider the Aubin-Mabuchi functional
{\bf I}
on
{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
, defined in (
5.2
). Notice that
{\bf I}(\varphi+c)={\bf I}(\varphi)+c\Vol(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
, so we can introduce the slice

\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}):={\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\cap{\bf I}^{-1}(0),

and consider the induced action of
{\mathbb{G}}:=\mathrmsl{Aut}_{0}(M,J)
on
\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
via the natural action of
{\mathbb{G}}
on the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant Kähler metrics in
[\overline{\omega}_{0}]
by pullbacks, i.e. for any
\varphi\in\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
and any
\tau\in{\mathbb{G}}
, we let
\tau[\varphi]
denote the uniquely determined
\overline{\omega}_{0}
-relative Kähler potential in
\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
of the Kähler metric
\tau^{*}(\omega_{\varphi})
. We thus have

\tau[\varphi]=\tau[0]+\varphi\circ\tau.

We next consider the
d_{1}
-distance on
{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
, introduced via the
L^{1}
-length of a smooth curve
\varphi(t)\in{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
:

l_{1}(\varphi(t))=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}|{\dot{\varphi}}(t)|\,(\overline{\omega}_{\varphi(t)})^{[m]}.

By the results in
[
16
]
,

d_{1}(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}):=\inf\Big\{l_{1}(\varphi(t))\,\Big|\,\varphi(t,x)\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times M),\,\varphi(t,\cdot)\in{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}),\,\varphi(0)=\varphi_{0},\,\varphi(1)=\varphi_{1}\Big\}

defines a distance.
We then have

Theorem 5.15
.

[
17
]
If
(M,J,[\overline{\omega}_{0}])
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric, then the Mabuchi energy
{\bf M}
is
{\mathbb{G}}
-invariant and
{\mathbb{G}}
-coercive on
{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
, i.e. there are uniform constants
\lambda>0,\delta
such that

{\bf M}(\varphi)\geq\lambda\inf_{\tau\in{\mathbb{G}}}d_{1}(0,\tau[\varphi])-\delta,\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}).

An important ingredient in proving the above result is the weak compactness, established in
[
7
,
8
]
. To state it, we denote by

\mathrm{PSH}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
the space of
\overline{\omega}_{0}
-relative plurisubharmonic functions on
M
, i.e. the usc functions
\varphi\in L^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
such that
\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}=\overline{\omega}_{0}+{\mathrmsl d}{\mathrmsl d}^{c}\varphi\geq 0
in the sense of currents. Furthermore, we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\subset\mathrm{PSH}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
the subspace of elements of
full Monge-Ampère mass
, i.e.

{\mathcal{E}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})=\Big\{\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\,\Big|\,\int_{M}\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]}=\int_{M}\overline{\omega}_{0}^{[m]}=\Vol(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\Big\}.

By the results in
[
40
,
10
]
, each
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
can be weakly approximated (as a current) by a sequence of smooth functions
(\varphi_{j})_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
and for any such sequence
\lim_{j\to\infty}\overline{\omega}_{\varphi_{j}}^{[m]}=\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]}
in the sense of measures. We can take the latter as a definition of
\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]}
when
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
. Following
[
40
]
, one further introduces the sub-space of elements with
full Monge-Ampère mass and finite energy

{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})=\Big\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\,\Big|\,\int_{M}|\varphi|\,\overline{\omega}_{\varphi}^{[m]}<\infty\Big\}.

The central fact
[
16
]
in this theory is that
d_{1}
extends to define a
complete
distance on
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
, such that
({\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}),d_{1})
is a complete geodesic space in which
{\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
is densely embedded, and the
d_{1}
-convergence on
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}),d_{1})
is stronger than both the
L^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
and the weak convergence of
(1,1)
-currents. Furthermore,
{\bf M}
naturally extends to a continuous and lsc functional on
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
. There is a key compactness principle:

Theorem 5.16
.

[
7
,
8
]
If
(\varphi_{j})_{j}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
is a sequence with

d_{1}(0,\varphi_{j})<C,\qquad{\bf M}(\varphi_{j})<C,

then there exists a subsequence
(\varphi_{j_{k}})_{k}
which converges with respect to
d_{1}
to an element
\varphi_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
.

In the toric case considered in this paper, we shall rather work with the subspace

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}):={\mathcal{H}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\cap C^{\infty}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M)

of
{\mathbb{T}}
-invariant relative Kähler potentials, and consider instead of
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathrmsl{Aut}_{0}(M,J)
the complex torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
. The coercivity principle of
[
17
, Theorem 3.4]
still applies in the
{\mathbb{T}}
-relative setting (see
[
43
,
75
]
):

Theorem 5.17
.

If
(M,J,[\overline{\omega}_{0}],{\mathbb{T}})
admits a Kähler-Einstein metric, then the Mabuchi energy
{\bf M}:{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-invariant and
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-coercive, i.e. there are uniform constants
\lambda>0,\delta
such that

{\bf M}(\varphi)\geq\lambda\inf_{\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}d_{1}(0,\tau[\varphi])-\delta,\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0}).

We next use Theorem
5.17
to obtain weak convergence of the global solution of NGKRF in the toric Kähler-Einstein case.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\varphi_{t}:=\phi_{t}-\phi_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\omega_{0})
be the relative Kähler potentials along the Kähler reduction (
4.12
) of the NGKRF, and
\mathring{\varphi}_{t}\in\mathring{{\mathcal{H}}}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})={\bf I}^{-1}(0)
the corresponding normalized
\overline{\omega}_{0}
-relative Kähler potentials. Using Theorem
5.17
, Corollary
5.14
and (
5.4
), we have along the Kähler reduction (
4.12
) of the normalized generalized Kähler Ricci flow,

\lambda\inf_{\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}d_{1}(0,\tau[\mathring{\varphi}_{t}])\leq{\bf M}(\overline{\omega}_{\varphi_{t}})+\delta\leq{\bf M}(F_{\varphi_{t}})+\delta\leq{{\bf M}}(F_{0})+\delta.

Furthermore, by
[
17
, Prop. 6.8]
(using that
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is reductive), for each
t\in[0,\infty)
there exists
\tau_{t}\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
such that

\inf_{\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}d_{1}(0,\tau[\mathring{\varphi}_{t}])=d_{1}(0,\tau_{t}[\mathring{\varphi}_{t}]).

We thus get uniform estimates

d_{1}(0,\tau_{t}[\mathring{\varphi}_{t}])\leq C,\qquad{\bf M}(\tau_{t}[\mathring{\varphi}_{t}])={\bf M}(\varphi_{t})<C

which, by Theorem
5.16
and the fact that
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{T}}}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})
is
d_{1}
-closed as a subset of
{\mathcal{E}}^{1}(M,\overline{\omega}_{0})

[
17
]
, proves the theorem.

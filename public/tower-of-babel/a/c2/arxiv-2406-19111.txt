On decay and asymptotic properties of solutions to the Intermediate Long Wave equation
Felipe Linares, Gustavo Ponce

Abstract
. We consider solutions to the initial value problem associated to the intermediate long wave (ILW) equation. We establish persistence properties of the solution flow in weighted Sobolev spaces, and show that they are sharp. We also deal with the long time dynamics of large solutions to the ILW equation. Using virial techniques, we describe regions of space where the energy of the solution must decay to zero along sequences of times. Moreover, in the case of exterior regions, we prove complete decay for any sequence of times. The remaining regions not treated here are essentially the strong dispersion and soliton regions.

Introduction

This work is concerned with the Intermediate Long Wave (ILW) equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{T}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u+\frac{1}{\delta}\partial_{x}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,\qquad(x,t)\in

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

where
u=u(t,x)
is a real-valued function,

\mathcal{T}_{\delta}f(x):=-\,\frac{1}{2\delta}\,\hbox{p.v.}\int\coth\left(\frac{\pi(x-y)}{2\delta}\right)f(y)dy,

(1.2)

and
\delta>0
is a parameter.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
is a Fourier multiplier of order zero, in the sense that
\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
is the multiplier with symbol

\sigma(\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{\delta})=\widehat{\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{\delta}}(\xi)=-2\pi\xi\,\hbox{coth}\,(2\pi\delta\xi),

(1.3)

so

\sigma\Big(\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{T}_{\delta}+\frac{\partial_{x}}{\delta}\Big)=-i\Big((2\pi\xi)^{2}\coth(2\pi\delta\xi)-\frac{2\pi\xi}{\delta}\Big):=-i\Omega_{\delta}(2\pi\xi).

(1.4)

The ILW equation (
1.1
) describes long internal gravity waves in a two layer stratified fluid, the lower layer having a large finite depth represented by the parameter

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u+u\partial_{x}u=0,

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\int\limits_{|y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{f(x-y)}{y}\,dy=(-i\,{\rm sgn}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}(x).

Our first result addresses the persistence properties of solutions of the IVP associated to (
1.1
) in polynomial weighted Sobolev spaces:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Let
u\in C([-T,T]:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to the IVP associated to (
1.1
)
obtained in
[
19
]
. If
\;|x|^{\alpha}u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\alpha\leq s
, then

|x|^{\alpha}u\in C([-T,T]:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

(1.13)

Roughly, Theorem
1.1
tells us that the
L^{2}
-decay of the data is preserved if it has at least the same amount of regularity in
L^{2}
. Also, it shows that the solution flow of the ILW preserves the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
as does the solution flow of the KdV equation.

The following theorems show that for large values of the parameter, measuring the
L^{2}
-decay, the result in Theorem
1.1
is optimal :

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u\in C([-T,T]:H^{1/6}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to the IVP associated to (
1.1
).
If there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in[-T,T]
,
t_{1}<t_{2}
, such that for
\alpha\in[1/6,1/2]

|x|^{\alpha}u(x,t_{j})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\enskip j=1,2,

(1.14)

then

u\in C([-T,T]:H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})).

(1.15)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s>3/2
. Let
u\in C([-T,T]:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to the IVP associated to (
1.1
).
If there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in[-T,T]
,
t_{1}<t_{2}
, such that for some
\alpha>s

|x|^{\alpha}u(x,t_{j})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\enskip j=1,2,

(1.16)

then

u\in C([-T,T]:H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})).

(1.17)

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

In the above theorems, the time interval
[-T,T]
can be taken to be arbitrarily large, see
[
19
]
.

(2)

In these theorems the relationship between the smoothness,
s
, and the decay,
\alpha

\Gamma=x-t\Omega^{\prime}(\partial_{x})
and the linear part of the ILW equation

(3)

The gap between the result in Theorem

and those in Theorem

, i.e.

\alpha>1/2
this is not the case. One needs to rely on an iterative argument which at each step increases in
1/2
the desired regularity. This is coming from the form of the local smoothing effect. This provides a gain of a
1/2
-derivative (described by a nonlocal operator). To establish this smoothing effect, one has to estimate terms involving fractional derivatives and fractional weights. In our arguments, it is crucial to have the quantity
\underset{t}{\sup}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{\infty}
controlled

. This explains the hypothesis

in Theorem

.

(4)

For the

-generalized KdV

similar results to those in Theorem

were proven in

]
, and for

]
and
[

]
. Theorems

-

were deduced in

]
and
[

(5)

As in the case for the BO equation, see
[
43
]
, one should not expect a well-posedness result based on solely the contraction principle.

(6)

For the BO equation, similar results to those in Theorems

do not hold, see

]

(7)

From the well-posedness result obtained in

Preliminary Results

This section contains several estimates to be used in the proof of the results stated inn the introduction.

Classical
\Psi
.d.o’s in
S^{{}^{m}}_{1,0}

The symbol class

\begin{split}S^{{}^{m}}_{1,0}=\Big\{&a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})\,:\;\forall\alpha,\beta\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{n}\\
&\hskip 15.0pt\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)\big|\leq c_{\alpha,\beta}\big(1+|\xi|\big)^{{}^{m-|\beta|}}\Big\}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

If
a\in S^{{}^{0}}_{1,0}
. Then for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and for any
\rho\in\mathbb{R}

a(x,D):L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\,dx)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\,dx)

(2.2)

with
\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)

The case
p=2
and
\rho>0
was proven in
[
49
]
(see also
[
29
]
).

(2)

We recall that if

T_{a}f(x)=a(x,D)f(x)=c\int e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi,

with
a\in S^{{}^{0}}_{1,0}
, then for any
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists

such that

\|T_{a}f\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\,\|f\|_{p},

see
[

]
.

Proof.

To simplify the exposition we fix
n=1
. It suffices to prove it for
\rho=2pl
with
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since

a(x,D)f(x)=c\int e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi,

to prove (

) we need to show that

\int|a(x,D)f(x)|^{p}\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\,dx\leq c_{\rho}\int|f(x)|^{p}\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\,dx

,

Thus, if

, we need to show

\int\Big|a(x,D)\Big(\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}\Big)\Big|^{p}\langle x\rangle^{2pl}\,dx\leq c_{\rho}\int|g(x)|^{p}\,dx,

\big\|\int e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\langle x\rangle^{2l}\,d\xi\big\|_{p}^{p}\leq c_{\rho}\|g\|_{p}^{p}.

Case 1:

l>0
.

Observe that

\langle x\rangle^{2l}e^{ix\xi}=\underset{j=0}{\overset{l}{\sum}}c_{jl}\,\partial_{\xi}^{2j}\big(e^{ix\xi}\big)\quad\text{with\enskip}c_{jl}=(-1)^{j}\begin{pmatrix}l\\

Hence

\begin{split}&\int\langle x\rangle^{2l}e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\,d\xi\\
&=\underset{j=0}{\overset{l}{\sum}}c_{jl}\,\int\partial_{\xi}^{2j}\big(e^{ix\xi}\big)a(x,\xi)\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\,d\xi\\
&=\underset{j=0}{\overset{l}{\sum}}c_{jl}\,\int e^{ix\xi}\,\partial_{\xi}^{2j}\Big(a(x,\xi)\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\Big)\,d\xi\end{split}

with

\begin{split}&\partial_{\xi}^{2j}\Big(a(x,\xi)\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\Big)\\
&=\underset{m=0}{\overset{2j}{\sum}}\begin{pmatrix}2j\\
m\end{pmatrix}\partial_{\xi}^{2j-m}a(x,\xi)\partial_{\xi}^{m}\Big(\widehat{\frac{g(x)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}}\Big)(\xi)\\
&=\underset{m=0}{\overset{2j}{\sum}}\begin{pmatrix}2j\\

with

.

Collecting the information above, using that
\partial_{\xi}^{r}a(x,D)\in S^{{}^{0}}_{1,0}
and

Case 2:

We have that

\begin{split}&\int e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)\,\widehat{\langle x\rangle^{2l}g}(\xi)\,\frac{d\xi}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}\\

&=\int(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{2l}\Big(\frac{e^{ix\xi}a(x,\xi)}{\langle x\rangle^{2l}}\Big)\,\widehat{g}(\xi)\,d\xi.\end{split}

Since

We start this section by establishing a useful commutator estimate which is fundamental in our analysis.

Suppose
\varphi(\cdot)
is a weight (smooth) with
\varphi(x)\simeq\langle x\rangle^{m}
, for some
\;m>0
.

Let
Q(D)
be a
\Psi
.d.o. in
S^{l}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R})
given by

Q(D)f(x)=c\int e^{ix\xi}Q(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

(2.15)

In particular,
Q(\xi)
is smooth.

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider the operator
Q
defined in (
2.15
). Then for
k>l
and
k\geq m
it holds that

[Q(\partial_{\xi});\varphi(x)]f(x)=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\varphi^{(j)}Q^{(j)}(\partial_{\xi})f+\mathcal{R}_{k}f

(2.16)

where

\|\mathcal{R}_{k}f\|_{2}\leq c\|\varphi^{(k)}\|_{1,2}\|f\|_{2}.

(2.17)

Proof.

We notice first that

\begin{split}&\Big([Q(\partial_{\xi});\varphi(x)]f(x)\Big)^{\widehat{\hskip 3.0pt}}(\xi)\\
&=Q(\xi)\widehat{\varphi f}(\xi)-\widehat{\varphi(x)Q(\partial_{\xi})f}(\xi)\\
&=Q(\xi)(\widehat{\varphi}*\widehat{f})(\xi)-(\widehat{\varphi}*\widehat{Q(\partial_{\xi})f})(\xi)\\
&=\int Q(\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\,d\eta-\int Q(\eta)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\,d\eta\\
&=\int\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\eta)\big(Q(\xi)-Q(\eta)\big)\widehat{f}(\eta)\,d\eta.\end{split}

(2.18)

By a Taylor expansion we find that

\begin{split}Q(\xi)-Q(\eta)&=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}c_{j}\,Q^{(j)}(\eta)(\xi-\eta)^{j}\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+c_{k+1}Q^{(k+1)}(\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)(\xi-\eta)^{k+1}\end{split}

with
\theta\in[0,1]
,
\theta=\theta(\xi,\eta)
. Inserting the identity above in the last expression
on the right hand side of (
2.18
) we get

\begin{split}&\Big([Q(\partial_{\xi});\varphi(x)]f(x)\Big)^{\widehat{\hskip 3.0pt}}(\xi)\\
&=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}c_{j}\int(\xi-\eta)^{j}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\eta)Q^{(j)}(\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\,d\eta\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+c_{k+1}\int Q^{(k+1)}(\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)(\xi-\eta)^{k+1}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\,d\eta\\
&=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}c_{j}\int\widehat{\partial_{x}^{j}\varphi}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\big(Q^{(j)}(\partial_{\xi})f\big)}(\eta)\,d\eta+\mathcal{R}_{k+1}f\\
&=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}c_{j}\widehat{\big(\partial_{x}^{j}\varphi\cdot Q^{(j)}(\partial_{\xi})f\big)}(\xi)+\mathcal{R}_{k+1}f.\end{split}

(2.19)

Thus

\begin{split}[Q(\partial_{\xi});\varphi(x)]f(x)=\underset{j=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}c_{j}\partial_{x}^{j}\varphi\,Q^{(j)}(\partial_{\xi})f+\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{k+1}f}\end{split}

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{R}_{k+1}f\|_{2}&\leq c\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}\|\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi}\|_{1}\\
&\leq c\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}\varphi\|_{1,2}\|f\|_{2}.\end{split}

b\leq\frac{2}{2+q}<\frac{2}{3}
and the last one is bounded by the proof of Lemma
7.2
. This
completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

7.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Since the function
\frac{1}{t\log t}\notin L^{1}(B^{c}_{r}(1))
, from the previous lemma, we can ensure that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})\to\infty
, such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{(t_{n})}^{b}}u^{2}(x,t_{n})\,dx=0.

Therefore,
0
is an accumulation point and using that
u^{2}\geq 0
we can conclude the result.

8.
Sketch Proof of Theorem
1.6

Lemma 8.1
.

Let u
\in C(\mathbb{R}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}:H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}))
the solution
of the IVP associated to (
1.1
) given in
[
19
]
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\{t\gg 1\}}\frac{1}{t\log t}\int_{B_{t^{b}}(0)}|q(\partial_{x})u(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C,

(8.1)

where
q(\partial_{x})
is defined in (
2.10
).

Proof.

Consider the functional

{\mathcal{J}}(t):=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx.

(8.2)

where
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
were defined in (
7.3
).

Differentiating (
8.2
) yields

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{J}}(t)&=-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu^{2}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\partial_{t}u(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{\mu_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)\,\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\left(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\,dx\\
&=A(t)+B(t)+C(t).\end{split}

(8.3)

Combining the properties
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
, the conservation of mass, and using (
7.3
), it follows that

|A(t)|+|C(t)|\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{t^{2-b}\log^{2}t}.

(8.4)

Thus, the terms
A(t)
,
C(t)
are integrable in
\{t\gg 1\}
.

Regarding
B(t)
, we use the equation in (
1.1
) and integrate by parts to write

\begin{split}B(t)&=\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\,\big(\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\partial_{x}u\big)\,\psi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt+\frac{2}{3\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)dx\\
&=B_{1}(t)+B_{2}(t).\end{split}

(8.5)

Using that the symbol of the operator
\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})
is even and integration by parts it follows that
that

\begin{split}B_{1}(t)&=\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\Big(u\psi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\Big)\,\partial_{x}u\,dx\\
&=-\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\partial_{x}\Big(u\psi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\,u\,dx\\
&=-\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int u\psi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\partial_{x}u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{2}{\mu(t)}\int u\,[\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\partial_{x};\psi_{\sigma}]u\,dx.\end{split}

(8.6)

Hence

\begin{split}B_{1}(t)&=-\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int u\,[\mathcal{L}(\partial_{x})\partial_{x};\psi_{\sigma}]u\,dx.\end{split}

(8.7)

From Lemma
6.1

\begin{split}B_{1}(t)&=-\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\,\int u\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\,\Omega_{\delta}^{\prime}(\partial_{x})u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{c}{\mu(t)}\,\int uR_{1}(\cdot)u\,dx\\
&=-\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\,\int u\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\,q(\partial_{x})q(\partial_{x})u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{c}{\mu(t)}\,\int uR_{1}(\cdot)u\,dx\\
&=-\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\,\int q(\partial_{x})\Big(u\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\Big)\,q(\partial_{x})u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{c}{\mu(t)}\,\int uR_{1}(\cdot)u\,dx\\
&=-\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\,\int\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\,q(\partial_{x})u\,q(\partial_{x})u\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)}\,\int u\,(q(\partial_{x})\,[q(\partial_{x});\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)]u)\,dx\\
&\hskip 15.0pt-\frac{c}{\mu(t)}\,\int uR_{1}(\cdot)u\,dx\\
&=B_{1,1}(t)+B_{1,2}(t)+B_{1,3}(t).\end{split}

(8.8)

We observe that
B_{1,1}(t)
is the term we want to estimate.

To estimate

and

\begin{split}|B_{1,3}(t)|&\leq\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|^{1/2}_{2}\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{(\log t)^{1/2}}{t^{1+\frac{3}{2}b}}\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\}).\end{split}

(8.9)

Employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
6.2
, the conservation of mass, and the properties of
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)

we obtain

\begin{split}|B_{1,2}(t)|&\leq\frac{c}{\mu(t)\mu_{1}(t)^{2}\mu_{1}(t)^{1/2}}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\|\phi_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{1/2}\Big(\|\phi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{1/2}+\|\phi_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{2}^{1/2})\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma}\frac{(\log t)^{1/2}}{t^{1+\frac{3}{2}b}}\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\}).\end{split}

(8.10)

Finally, notice that by Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{3}\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)dx

\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|u\,\zeta_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big(\sup_{x\in[n,n+1]}\phi_{\sigma}\Big(\frac{x}{\mu_{1}(t)}\Big)\Big).

8.1.
Sketch Proof of Corollary
1.5

Sketch Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

The proof of this result follows closely the argument employed to establish Theorem
1.4
. Thus we will
describe only the new elements in the proof.

We consider the functional

\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int u(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\lambda}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx

where
\rho(t)=\pm t^{m}
,
m
as in the statement of the corollary,
\mu(t)
and
\mu_{1}(t)
defined as in (
7.3
), and

\psi_{\sigma}
and
\phi_{\lambda}
defined as above.

Same analysis as in the proof of Lemma
7.1
yields

\sup_{t\gg 1}|\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)|<\infty.

We then get a similar inequality as (
7.8
) in Lemma
7.2
, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int u^{2}(x,t)\,\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 4\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(t)+h_{\rho}(t),

where
h_{\rho}(t)\in L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
. In addition to the terms previously handle in the proof of Lemma
7.2
, we must estimate two new extra terms,

-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\lambda}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx=A(t)

and

-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,t)\psi_{\sigma}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}(t)}\right)\phi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-\rho(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)}\right)\,dx=B(t).

This can be done by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the mass conservation to lead to

\begin{split}|A(t)+B(t)|&\leq\left|\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{1/2}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\quad+\left|\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\mu_{1}^{q}(t)\mu(t)}\right|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{\sigma,\lambda,m}\frac{1}{t^{(4-2m-b)/2}\log^{3/2}t}+\frac{1}{t^{(4-2b-2m+bq)/2}\log^{(4-q)/2}t}.\end{split}

We note that the last terms are in
L^{1}(\{t\gg 1\})
since for the first term
m\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}
and for the second one
m\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}<1-\frac{b(1-q)}{2}
, respectively.

From this point on the utilised argument to establish Theorem
1.4
can be applied to complete the proof of Corollary
1.5
.

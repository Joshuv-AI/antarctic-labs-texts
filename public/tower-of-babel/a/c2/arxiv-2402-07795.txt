Finite and infinite order differential properties of the reduced Mittag–Leffler polynomials
Predrag M. Rajković,

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper deals with the Mittag-Leffler
polynomials (MLP) by extracting their essence which consists of
real polynomials with fine properties. They are orthogonal on the
real line instead of the imaginary axes for MLP. Beside recurrence
relations and zeros, we will point to the closed form of its
Fourier transform. The most important contribution consists of the
new differential properties, especially the finite and infinite
differential equation.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010)
: 33C45, 11B83

Key Words
: Generating function, Polynomial sequence,
Recurrence relation, Orthogonality

1
Introduction

The Mittag–Leffler polynomials
\{g_{n}(x)\}
are coefficients in
the expansion

\Bigl(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\Bigr)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)t^{n}\qquad(|t|<1).

They were introduced by Mittag-Leffler in a study of the integral
representations. Their main properties were discovered by H.
Bateman (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
). He noticed that
they occur as coefficients in the closed-form expressions for a
several families of integrals. Also, they were used in deriving
some expansions for the Euler Gamma function and the Riemann Zeta
function
[
13
]
. Truncated Exponential-Based
Mittag-Leffler Polynomials were examined in
[
16
]
. They
were noticed in the solutions of heat diffusion equation of
Fokker-Plank type and researched by G. Dattoli and his coauthors

[
5
]
-
[
6
]
. They are connected with the Sheffer
polynomials in
[
7
]
and Riordan arrays in
[
11
]
. Their
generalizations were considered in
[
14
]
and

[
12
]
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present the
preliminaries for the Mittag-Leffler polynomials. Since the
imaginary unit is present in the orthogonality relation, we have
noticed that we can reduce them to the real polynomials whose
examination is more obvious and much easier. They are the subject
in Section 3. In the next section, we find the closed form of
their Fourier transform. The main contributions of the paper are
the differential properties exposed Section 5. Finally, the paper
is concluded in Section 6 with observation on the quasi-monomial
property of the reduced Mittag-Leffler polynomials.

2
Preliminaries

The Mittag–Leffler polynomials
\{g_{n}(x)\}
can be represented
over hypergeometric function like

g_{n}(x)=2x\;{}_{2}F_{1}\Bigl({{1-n,\ 1-x}\atop{2}}\Bigm|2\Bigr)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

(1)

They can be viewed a special case of the Meixner-Pollaczek
polynomials

P^{(\lambda)}_{n}(x;\phi)=\frac{(2\lambda)_{n}}{n!}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}n\phi}\;{}_{2}F_{1}\Bigl({{-n,\ \lambda+{\rm i}x}\atop{2\lambda}}\Bigm|1-{\rm e}^{-2{\rm i}\phi}\Bigr)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N};\ {\rm i}^{2}=-1)

(2)

for
\lambda=1
and
\phi=\pi/2
:

g_{n}(x)=2\frac{{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}n\pi/2}}{n}\,xP^{(1)}_{n-1}({\rm i}x;\pi/2)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

(3)

Also, they can be considered as a special case of Meixner
polynomials

M_{n}(x;\beta,c)=\;{}_{2}F_{1}\Bigl({{-n,\ -x}\atop{\beta}}\Bigm|1-\frac{1}{c}\Bigr)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N})

for
\beta=2
and
c=-1
(see
[
9
]
). Namely, their relation
is given by

g_{n}(x)=2xM_{n-1}(x-1,2,-1).

The lack of this connection is the fact that orthogonality of the
Meixner polynomials is assured only with the constraint
0<c<1
.

Finally, the Mittag–Leffler polynomials
g_{n}(x)
are connected
with Pidduck polynomials
[
15
]
by the expression

P_{n}(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left({\rm e}^{D}+1\right)g_{n}(x),

where the series for the exponential function is used and
D
is
understood as differentiation.

The Mittag–Leffler polynomials
\{g_{n}(x)\}
satisfy recurrence
relation

(n+1)g_{n+1}(x)-2xg_{n}(x)+(n-1)g_{n-1}(x)=0,

(4)

with initial values

g_{0}(x)=1,\quad g_{1}(x)=2x.

They also satisfy difference relation

xg_{n}(x+1)-2ng_{n}(x)-xg_{n}(x-1)=0.

The Mittag–Leffler polynomials
\{g_{n}(x)\}
satisfy
recurrence-difference relation

g_{n}(x+1)-g_{n-1}(x+1)=g_{n}(x)+g_{n-1}(x).

The orthogonality relation is given by

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}g_{n}(-{\rm i}x)g_{m}({\rm i}x)\frac{{\rm d}x}{x\sinh(\pi x)}=\frac{2}{n}\delta_{mn}\qquad(n,m\in\mathbb{N}).

(5)

Notice that the corresponding monic sequence are

\hat{g}_{n}(x)=\frac{(n)!}{2^{n}}\ g_{n}(x)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Let remind that the central difference operator is

\delta f(z)=f(z+1/2)-f(z-1/2).

Theorem 2.1

The Mittag–Leffler polynomials
\{g_{n}(x)\}
satisfy the
Rodrigues formula

g_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{n!}\frac{x}{w(x,1)}\delta^{n}w(x,n),

where

w(x,n)=\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}-x\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+x\right).

Proof
. It is based on the connection (
3
)
and the Rodrigues formula for the Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials in
[
9
]
, pp. 37.
\Box

3
The reduced Mittag–Leffler polynomials

In spite of the fact that the Mittag-Leffler polynomials are real,
in some relations, as in (
2
), and
especially in their orthogonality relation (
5
) they
are considered as the complex functions. That is why we believe
that is better to extract the following sequence.

Let us consider
reduced Mittag-Leffler polynomials

defined by (see
[
14
]
)

\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{g_{n+1}({\rm i}x)}{{\rm i}^{n+1}\ x}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

The successive members of sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfy the three–term recurrence relation

\displaystyle(n+2)\varphi_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x\varphi_{n}(x)-n\ \varphi_{n-1}(x)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N})

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2,\qquad\varphi_{1}(x)=2x.

The generating function of sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is given by

\mathcal{G}(t,x)=\frac{\exp(2x\arctan t)-1}{tx}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)t^{n}.

Notice that

\varphi_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n}\ \varphi_{n}(x)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

The polynomials of sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

satisfy following orthogonality relation:

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\varphi_{m}(x)\frac{x}{\sinh(\pi x)}\ {\rm d}x=\frac{2}{n+1}\ \delta_{mn}\qquad(n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Notice that

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{n}\;{\rm d}x}{\sinh x}=\frac{(1-(-1)^{n})(2^{n+1}-1)}{2^{n}}\;n!\;\zeta(n+1)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

where
\zeta(n)
is the Riemann zeta function.

The reduced Mittag-Leffler polynomials

\{\varphi_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are real polynomials and,
because of orthogonality, they have all real zeros.

The monic sequence

\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\frac{(n+1)!}{2^{n+1}}\ \varphi_{n}(x)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})

(6)

satisfies three term recurrence relation

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\frac{n(n+1)}{4}\ \hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

(7)

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\qquad\hat{\varphi}_{1}(x)=x.

Remark 3.1

Notice that
\varphi_{n}(x)
is the
Meixner-Pollaczeck polynomial for
\lambda=1
and
\phi=\pi/2
,
i.e.

\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\frac{n!}{2^{n}}\;P_{n}^{(1)}(x;\pi/2)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Hence the difference relation

(x+{\rm i})\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x+{\rm i})-2(n+1){\rm i}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-(x-{\rm i})\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x-{\rm i})=0

is valid.

Example 3.1

The first members of the sequence

\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are

\hat{\varphi}_{0}(x)=1,\quad\hat{\varphi}_{1}(x)=x,\quad\hat{\varphi}_{2}(x)=x^{2}-\frac{1}{2},\quad\hat{\varphi}_{3}(x)=x^{3}-2x,

\hat{\varphi}_{4}(x)=x^{4}-5x^{2}+\frac{3}{2},\quad\hat{\varphi}_{5}(x)=x^{5}-10x^{3}+\frac{23}{2}x.

The largest zeros are

x^{(2)}_{2}\approx 0.707,\quad x^{(3)}_{3}\approx 1.414,\quad x^{(4)}_{4}\approx 2.163,\quad x^{(5)}_{5}\approx 2.945.

Using conclusions from the paper
[
8
]
, we conclude that the zeros
\{x_{k}^{(n)}\}
of the
polynomial
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
are bordered in the next manner:

|x_{k}^{(n)}|<\sqrt{(n-1)n}\qquad(k=1,2,\ldots,n).

Theorem 3.1

The sequence
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfies
the Turan’s inequality

\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x),x\right)=-\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)&\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\\
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)&\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x)\end{vmatrix}\geq 0\qquad(\forall x\in\mathbb{R};\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}).

Proof
. We will prove by the mathematical induction as in the
paper
[
10
]
. Obviously,

\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{0}(x),x\right)=1\geq 0
. Suppose
that
\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x),x\right)\geq 0
.

Let be
c_{n}=n(n+1)/4
. Consider the expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x),x\right)-c_{n}\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x),x\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n+1}(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\hat{\varphi}_{n+2}(x)-c_{n}\left(\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n}(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n+1}(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\left(x\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x)-c_{n+1}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\right)-c_{n}\hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x)-c_{n}\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n}(x).

Applying the recurrence relation (
7
), the last
expression reduces to

\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x),x\right)-c_{n}\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x),x\right)=\left(c_{n+1}-c_{n}\right)\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n}(x)=\frac{n+1}{2}\hat{\varphi}^{2}_{n}(x)\geq 0.

We conclude that

\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(x),x\right)\geq c_{n}\mathcal{T}\left(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x),x\right)\geq 0
.
\Box

4
Fourier transform

Let us remind that the Fourier transform is defined by

\mathfrak{F}\left[f(t)\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\;e^{{\rm i}st}\;{\rm d}t\;=F(s).

(8)

The Fourier transform of the first members of the sequence

\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(t)w(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, where
w(t)=\frac{t}{\sinh(\pi t)}
, is:

\mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{0}w\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\;\frac{1}{1+\cosh s}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{\sinh^{2}(s/2)}{\sinh^{2}s},\ \mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{1}w\right]=2\;{\rm i}\;\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{\sinh^{4}(s/2)}{\sinh^{3}s}.

Theorem 4.1

The Fourier transform of the sequence

\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(t)w(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, where
w(t)=\frac{t}{\sinh(\pi t)}
, is:

\Phi_{n}(s)=\mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{n}w\right]={\rm i}^{n}(n+1)!\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\frac{\sinh^{2n+2}(s/2)}{\sinh^{n+2}s}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

(9)

Proof
. We will apply the mathematical induction. It is
obviously true for
n=1
and a few others. Suppose that it is
valid for every
k\leq n
. We will multiply the recurrence relation
(
7
) with
w(t)
and apply the Fourier transform
on it:

\mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{n+1}(t)w(t)\right]=\mathfrak{F}\left[t\hat{\varphi}_{n}(t)w(t)\right]-\frac{n(n+1)}{4}\ \mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(t)w(t)\right].

(10)

The Fourier transform (
8
) has the property

\mathfrak{F}\left[t^{m}g(t)\right](s)=(-{\rm i})^{m}\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}\mathfrak{F}\left[g(t)\right](s)\quad(m\in\mathbb{N}).

It able us to write

\mathfrak{F}\left[t\varphi_{n}(t)w(t)\right]=(-{\rm i})\frac{d}{ds}\mathfrak{F}\left[\varphi_{n}(t)w(t)\right](s)=-{\rm i}\;\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(s).

Hence the relation (
10
) obtains the form

\Phi_{n+1}(s)=-{\rm i}\;\hat{\Phi}^{\prime}_{n}(s)-\frac{n(n+1)}{4}\ \Phi_{n-1}(s).

Deriving (
9
), we find

\Phi^{\prime}_{n}(s)={\rm i}^{n}(n+1)!\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\;\bigl(n-2\sinh^{2}(s/2)\bigr)\frac{\sinh^{2n+2}(s/2)}{\sinh^{n+3}s}\;.

Finally, using the assumed expression for
\Phi_{n-1}(s)
, we see
that
\Phi_{n+1}(s)
satisfies the relation (
9
),
wherefrom the statement follows.
\Box

Remark 4.1

Having in mind relations between hyperbolic functions, relation
(
9
) can be rewritten in the form

\Phi_{n}(s)=\mathfrak{F}\left[\hat{\varphi}_{n}w\right]=\dfrac{{\rm i}^{n}(n+1)!}{2^{n}\;\sqrt{2\pi}}\;\tanh^{n}(s/2)\left(1-\tanh^{2}(s/2)\right)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

5
Differential properties

The exponential generating function of sequence

\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is (see
[
14
]
)

\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=\frac{4\exp\bigl(2x\;\arctan(t/2)\bigr)}{t^{2}+4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(11)

Theorem 5.1

The exponential generating function
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)
has
the property

\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)\;\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)\right)^{2}.

Proof.
This follows directly from relations

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=2\arctan\frac{t}{2}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)\;,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=4\arctan^{2}\frac{t}{2}\cdot\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)\;.\;\Box

Corollary 5.1

The sequence
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfies
the recurrence-differential equation

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}_{k}(x)\hat{\varphi}_{n-k}(x)-\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{k}(x)\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n-k}(x)}{k!(n-k)!}=0\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Theorem 5.2

Any polynomial
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
satisfies
n^{\rm th}
order
differential equation of the form

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}x)\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{(k)}_{n}(x)}{k!}-n\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=0,

(12)

where
\alpha_{k}=\cos\frac{k\pi}{2}
and
\beta_{k}=\sin\frac{k\pi}{2}
.

Proof
. The sequence
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

is a Sheffer sequence since its generating function has the form

\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=\frac{1}{g\left(\hat{f}(t)\right)}{\rm e}^{x\hat{f}(t)},

where
\hat{f}(t)=2\arctan\frac{t}{2}
. It is the compositional
inverse of
f(t)=2\tan\frac{t}{2}
. Also, here is
g\left(\hat{f}(t)\right)=1+\frac{t^{2}}{4}.
Hence
g(t)=\cos^{2}\frac{t}{2}
.

According to
[
17
]
,
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

satisfies the differential equation of the form
(
12
), where

\beta_{k}=\left(\frac{f(t)}{f^{\prime}(t)}\right)^{(k)}\Big|_{t=0}\;,\qquad\alpha_{k}=\left(-\frac{f(t)}{f^{\prime}(t)}\cdot\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\right)^{(k)}\Big|_{t=0}\;.

Hence

\beta_{k}=\left(\sin t\right)^{(k)}\Big|_{t=0}=\sin\frac{k\pi}{2}\;,\qquad\alpha_{k}=\left(1-\cos t\right)^{(k)}\Big|_{t=0}=\cos\frac{k\pi}{2}\;.\Box

Example 5.1

The polynomial

\hat{\varphi}_{4}(x)=x^{4}-5x^{2}+\frac{3}{2},

satisfies the following differential equation:

\frac{1}{24}\hat{\varphi}^{(4)}_{4}(x)-\frac{1}{6}x\hat{\varphi}^{(3)}_{4}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}_{4}(x)+x\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{4}(x)-4\hat{\varphi}_{4}(x)=0.

(13)

Theorem 5.3

Any polynomial
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
satisfies differential equation
of the form

\left(\cos D+x\sin D-(n+1)I\right)\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=0\qquad\left(D=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\right).

The polynomial
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
is the eigenfunction of the
operator
\mathcal{F}=\cos D+x\sin D-I
with the eigenvalue

n
.

Proof
. Let
I
be the identity operator. Since

\alpha_{k}=\cos\frac{k\pi}{2}=\frac{{\rm i}^{k}+(-{\rm i})^{k}}{2}\;,\qquad\beta_{k}=\sin\frac{k\pi}{2}=\frac{{\rm i}^{k}-(-{\rm i})^{k}}{2{\rm i}}\;,

we can write (
12
) in the form

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{{\rm i}^{k}+(-{\rm i})^{k}}{2}+x\frac{{\rm i}^{k}-(-{\rm i})^{k}}{2{\rm i}}\right)\frac{D^{k}}{k!}-nI\right)\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=0.

(14)

Since
D^{m}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\equiv 0
for every
m>n
, we can
write

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rm i}^{k}\frac{D^{k}}{k!}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm i}D)^{k}}{k!}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\left({\rm e}^{{\rm i}D}-I\right)\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x).

Hence the formula (
14
) becomes

\left(\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm e}^{{\rm i}D}+{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}D}-2I\right)+\frac{x}{2{\rm i}}\left({\rm e}^{{\rm i}D}-{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}D}\right)-nI\right)\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=0.

The statement follows from the Euler identity for the complex
functions.
\Box

Example 5.2

Since

\cos D=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{D^{2k}}{(2k)!}\;,\qquad\sin D=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{D^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\;,

the polynomial
\hat{\varphi}_{4}(x)
satisfies

\left(\left(I-\frac{D^{2}}{2}+\frac{D^{4}}{4!}\right)+x\left(D-\frac{D^{3}}{3!}\right)-4I\right)\hat{\varphi}_{4}(x)=0,

what is the same as (
13
).

Theorem 5.4

The sequences
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and

\{\varphi_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
have the following
differential properties:

\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{2k+1}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}}\hat{\varphi}_{n-2k}(x),

(15)

\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(x)=2\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\varphi_{n-2k}(x).

(16)

Proof.
By differentiation the generating function
(
11
) over
x
, we get

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{4\exp\bigl(2x\ \arctan(t/2)\bigr)}{t^{2}+4}\ 2\arctan(t/2).

Knowing that
\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{0}(t)=0
and using the expansion

2\arctan\dfrac{t}{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}t^{2k+1}\qquad\left(\Big|\dfrac{t^{2}}{2}\Big|<1\right),

we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}t^{2k+1}\right).

Hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)}{(n+1)!}t^{n+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)}{n!}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}t^{n+2k+1},

i.e.,

t\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)}{(n+1)!}t^{n}=t\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\dfrac{\hat{\varphi}_{n-2k}(x)}{(n-2k)!}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}t^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients by
t^{n}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})
, we find

\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)}{(n+1)!}=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\dfrac{\hat{\varphi}_{n-2k}(x)}{(n-2k)!}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}.

(17)

By rearrangement of summands, we have formula (
15
).

Formula (
16
) can be obtained by (
17
) and
(
6
).
\quad\Box

6
Quasi-monomiality

According to
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
, the exponential
generating function
{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)
is of the Boas-Buck type
if

{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=A(t)B(xC(t)),

where

B^{(k)}(0)\neq 0\quad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}),\quad A(0)C^{\prime}(0)\neq 0,\quad C(0)=0.

Considering the exponential generating function of the reduced
Mittag-Leffler polynomials
\{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\}
, we can denote
with

A_{\hat{\varphi}}(t)=\frac{4}{4+t^{2}},\quad B_{\hat{\varphi}}(t)={\rm e}^{t},\quad C_{\hat{\varphi}}(t)=2\arctan\frac{t}{2}.

Here, it is

C^{-1}_{\hat{\varphi}}(t)=2\tan\frac{t}{2}\;.

Theorem 6.1

The sequence
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
is quasi-monomial under the
lowering operator
\mathcal{L}_{x}=2\tan\left(D_{x}/2\right)
,
i.e.

\mathcal{L}_{x}\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)=n\;\hat{\varphi}_{n-1}(x)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Proof.
We start with the Taylor series

\tan x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{2k-1}x^{2k-1},\quad{\rm where}\quad\theta_{2k-1}=(-1)^{k-1}4^{k}(4^{k}-1)\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}\;.

Here,
B_{n}
is the
n
-the Bernoulli number. Since

D_{x}^{2k-1}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)=\frac{4}{t^{2}+4}\exp\Bigl(2x\,\arctan\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)\;\bigl(2\,\arctan\frac{t}{2}\bigr)^{2k-1},

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{x}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{2k-1}\left(\frac{D_{x}}{2}\right)^{2k-1}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{t^{2}+4}\exp\Bigl(2x\,\arctan\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{2k-1}\Bigl(\arctan\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)^{2k-1},

wherefrom

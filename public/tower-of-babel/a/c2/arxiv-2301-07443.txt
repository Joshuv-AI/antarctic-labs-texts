Isospin sum rules in charmed baryon weak decays
Jin-Feng Luo 1, Di Wang 1

Abstract
Isospin symmetry is the most precise flavor symmetry. The effective Hamiltonian of charm quark weak decay is zero under the isospin lowering operators I − n I_{-}^{n} , which permits us to generate isospin sum rules through several master formulas. In this work, we derive the master formulas of isospin sum rules for the two- and three-body non-leptonic decays of singly and doubly charmed baryons. Hundreds of isospin sum rules are derived to test of isospin symmetry and provide hints for the new decay modes. The isospin sum rules for multi-body decays are not broken by the intermediate resonances and hence can be used to study the isospin partners of exotic hadrons.

II
Theoretical framework

Taking the two-body
D
meson decays as examples, the basic idea of generating isospin sum rules by
I_{-}^{n}
is shown as follows.
In the
SU(3)
picture, the effective Hamiltonian of charm decay can be written as
Wang:2020gmn

\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}H^{ij}_{k}O^{ij}_{k},

(1)

where
O^{ij}_{k}
denotes the four-quark operator and
H
is a
3\times 3\times 3
coefficient matrix.
The initial and final states of charm decay can be written as

\displaystyle|M_{\alpha}\rangle=(M_{\alpha})^{i}_{j}|M^{i}_{j}\rangle,

(2)

where
|M^{i}_{j}\rangle
is the quark composition of the meson state,
|M^{i}_{j}\rangle=|q_{i}\bar{q}_{j}\rangle
, and
(M_{\alpha})
is the coefficient matrix.
For instance, the
|M_{\pi^{0}}\rangle
state is written as

\displaystyle|M_{\pi^{0}}\rangle=(M_{\pi^{0}})^{i}_{j}|M^{i}_{j}\rangle,\qquad\text{and}\qquad(M_{\pi^{0}})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right).

In this way, the information of initial/final state is absorbed into the coefficient matrix
(M_{\alpha})
and
|M^{i}_{j}\rangle
is universal under the flavor symmetry.
Decay amplitude of the
D_{\gamma}\to M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}
mode is constructed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(D_{\gamma}\to M_{\alpha}M_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\langle M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}|\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}|D_{\gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle~=\sum_{\omega}\,(M_{\alpha})^{n}_{m}\langle M^{n}_{m}|(M_{\beta})^{s}_{r}\langle M^{s}_{r}||H^{jk}_{l}O^{jk}_{l}||(D_{\gamma})_{i}|D_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle~~=\sum_{\omega}\,\langle M_{m}^{n}M^{s}_{r}|O^{jk}_{l}|D_{i}\rangle\times(M_{\alpha})_{m}^{n}(M_{\beta})_{r}^{s}H^{jk}_{l}(D_{\gamma})_{i}

\displaystyle~~~=\sum_{\omega}X_{\omega}(C_{\omega})_{\alpha\beta\gamma},

(6)

where
X_{\omega}=\langle M_{m}^{n}M^{s}_{r}|O^{jk}_{l}|D_{i}\rangle
is the reduced matrix element and
(C_{\omega})_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=(M_{\alpha})_{m}^{n}(M_{\beta})_{r}^{s}H^{jk}_{l}(D_{\gamma})_{i}
is the Clebsch-Gordan (CG) coefficient
Eckart30
;
Wigner59
.

The isospin lowering operator
I_{-}
is

\displaystyle I_{-}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right).

If the effective Hamiltonian (
1
) is fully invariant under
I_{-}^{n}
, i.e.,
I_{-}^{n}\,H=0
, it follows that

\langle M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}|I_{-}^{n}\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}|D_{\gamma}\rangle=\sum_{\omega}\,\langle M_{m}^{n}M^{s}_{r}|O^{jk}_{l}|D_{i}\rangle\times(M_{\alpha})_{m}^{n}(M_{\beta})_{r}^{s}(I_{-}^{n}\,H)^{jk}_{l}(D_{\gamma})_{i}=0.

(11)

One can apply
I_{-}^{n}
to the initial/final states and expand the results using the initial/final states as bases.
The LHS of Eq. (
11
) is then expressed as a sum of several decay amplitudes and the RHS of Eq. (
11
) remains zero.
The sum of decay amplitudes generated by
I_{-}
for the two-body decays of
D
mesons is written as
Wang:2023pnb

\displaystyle{SumI_{-}}\,[\gamma,\alpha,\beta]=\sum_{\mu}\left[\{[I_{-}]_{M_{8}}\}_{\alpha}^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\to\mu\beta}+\{[I_{-}]_{M_{8}}\}_{\beta}^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\to\alpha\mu}+\{[I_{-}]_{D}\}_{\gamma}^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu\to\alpha\beta}\right],

(12)

in which
[I_{-}]_{M_{8}}
and
[I_{-}]_{D}
are the coefficient matrices found in Ref.
Wang:2023pnb
, and
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\to\mu\beta}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\to\alpha\mu}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\mu\to\alpha\beta}
are the decay amplitudes of
D_{\gamma}\to M_{\mu}M_{\beta}
,
D_{\gamma}\to M_{\alpha}M_{\mu}
,
D_{\mu}\to M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}
respectively.
It was found in Ref.
Wang:2023pnb
that the effective Hamiltonian of the Cabibbo-favored (CF) transition is zero under
I_{-}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
.
And the effective Hamiltonian of the singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) and doubly Cabibbo-suppressed (DCS) transitions is zero under
I_{-}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
.
Thus, we can use Eq. (
12
) to generate isospin sum rules for the CF decays directly.
For instance, choosing
\{\gamma,\alpha,\beta\}=\{D^{0},\pi^{+},\overline{K}^{0}\}
in Eq. (
12
) yields the isospin sum rule of the
D\to K\pi
system,

\displaystyle{SumI_{-}}\,[D^{0},\pi^{+},\overline{K}^{0}]=-\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\overline{K}^{0})-\mathcal{A}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}K^{-})+\mathcal{A}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\overline{K}^{0})=0.

(13)

Isospin sum rules for the SCS and DCS transitions are generated by operating Eq. (
12
) at least twice.
For example, choosing
\{\gamma,\alpha,\beta\}=\{D^{0},\pi^{+},\pi^{+}\}
yields the isospin sum rule of the
D\to\pi\pi
system,

\displaystyle{SumI_{-}^{2}}\,[D^{0},\pi^{+},\pi^{+}]

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\,{SumI_{-}}\,[D^{0},\pi^{+},\pi^{0}]-\sqrt{2}\,{SumI_{-}}\,[D^{0},\pi^{0},\pi^{+}]+{SumI_{-}}\,[D^{+},\pi^{+},\pi^{+}]

\displaystyle=4\,\big[\mathcal{A}(D^{0}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0})-\mathcal{A}(D^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-})-\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(D^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\big]=0.

(14)

It is stressed that the equations
I_{-}\,H_{\rm CF}=0
,
I_{-}^{2}\,H_{\rm SCS}=0
and
I_{-}^{2}\,H_{\rm DCS}=0
are derived without approximating the CKM matrix
Wang:2023pnb
.
So the isospin sum rules generated through Eq. (
12
) do not rely on any approximation of the CKM matrix.
In the rest of this paper, we extend this approach to the two- and three-body non-leptonic decays of singly and doubly charmed baryons.
The advantage of our method is that it avoids the Wigner-Eckart invariants.
It provides a simple and systematic method for generating isospin sum rules.

\displaystyle=\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,\pi^{+}\,\pi^{+}|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,I_{-}\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle+\langle\,(I_{-}\,\Lambda^{0})\,\pi^{+}\,\pi^{+}|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle~~~~+\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})\,\pi^{+}|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle+\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,\pi^{+}\,(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})-\sqrt{2}\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=0.

(151)

It is not correct to assume that

\displaystyle\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})\,\pi^{+}|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle+\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,\pi^{+}\,(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle=2\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,\pi^{+}(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})\,|\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle,

(152)

and then Eq. (
IV
) is changed to be

\displaystyle{SumI_{-}}\,[\Lambda^{+}_{c},\Lambda^{0},\pi^{+},\pi^{+}]

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{2}\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=0.

(153)

The reason is that if there is a resonance, for instance
\Sigma^{*0}
, contributes to the first term of isospin sum rule (
IV
) with
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*0}[\,\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}]\pi^{+}
, there must be an isospin parter
\Sigma^{*+}
, which contributes to the second term of isospin sum rule (
IV
) with
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*+}[\,\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}]\pi^{0}
.
Analogous to Eq. (
147
), it is equivalent to inserting
|\Sigma^{*0}\rangle\langle\Sigma^{*0}|+|\Sigma^{*+}\rangle\langle\Sigma^{*+}|
between two
\pi^{+}
states of
\langle\,\Lambda^{0}\,\pi^{+}\,\pi^{+}|\,I_{-}\,\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}\,|\,\Lambda^{+}_{c}\rangle
.
Under the narrow width approximation, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*+,0}[\,\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+,0}]\pi^{0,+})=\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*+,0}\pi^{0,+})\times\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{*+,0}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+,0})

(154)

Under the isospin symmetry, we have

\displaystyle I_{-}\langle\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}|\Sigma^{*0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}|I_{-}\,\Sigma^{*0}\rangle+\langle(I_{-}\,\Lambda^{0})\pi^{+}|\Sigma^{*0}\rangle+\langle\Lambda^{0}(I_{-}\,\pi^{+})|\Sigma^{*0}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\langle\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}|I_{-}\,\Sigma^{*+}\rangle+0-\sqrt{2}\langle\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}|\Sigma^{*0}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}[\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{*+}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+})+\mathcal{A}(\Sigma^{*0}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0})]=0.

(155)

Here we have used the fact that
I_{-}\,H_{\rm strong}=0
since the strong interaction is flavor conservative.
Under the narrow width approximation, isospin sum rule (
IV
) for three-body decays degrades into isospin sum rule (
169
) for two-body decays,

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*+}\pi^{0})+\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*0}\pi^{+})=0.

(156)

Exchanging
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{0}
in
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
within Eq. (
IV
) results in an interchange of
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{0}
in
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{*0}[\,\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}]\pi^{+}
, violating isospin sum rule (
IV
) and (
156
).
Moreover, not only the first two particles of the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+}
can generate form a resonance particle, but also the first and third particles, and the last two particles can generate form resonance states.
If the first and third particles of
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+}
decay,
\Lambda^{0}
and
\pi^{+}
, forming a resonance
\Sigma^{*+}
, the first and third particles of
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decay,
\Lambda^{0}
and
\pi^{0}
, will form a resonance
\Sigma^{*0}
.
The analysis of this case is similar to the last case.
If the last two particles of the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+}
decay receive contributions from decay chains such as
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+}[\,\to\pi^{0}\pi^{+}]
,
the last two particles of the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decay will receive contribution from decay chain

\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+}[\,\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0}]
.
According to the chiral Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{VPP}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}g_{VPP}Tr[V^{\mu}[P,\partial_{\mu}P]],

(157)

the amplitudes
\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
and
\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
are opposite because of the commutator in Eq. (
157
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=-\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{+}).

(158)

Under the narrow width approximation, isospin sum rule (
IV
) degrades into

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+})-\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+})=0.

(159)

This equation is trivial without physical result.
Exchanging
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{0}
in
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
in Eq. (
IV
) is equivalent to setting
\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{0})=\mathcal{A}(\rho^{+}\to\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
and then
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+})=0
.
It is in conflict with the recent BESIII measurements in which the contribution of
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\rho^{+})
is dominated in the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Lambda^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
decay
BESIII:2022udq
.
According to the above analysis, an isospin sum rule for three-body decays involves several isospin sum rules for two-body decays in the narrow width approximation in which the three-body decay can be factorized into several underlying two-body decays.

The isospin sum rules for three-body decays with two particles within one isospin multiplet can not be tested by data of total branching fractions without performing a partial wave analysis.
For example, the isospin sum rule (
A.3
) given

\displaystyle{SumI_{-}}\,[\Lambda^{+}_{c},\Sigma^{0},\pi^{+},\pi^{+}]

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}\big[\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})+\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle~~~~~-\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})\big]=0

(160)

cannot be tested by measuring the total branching fractions of
\mathcal{B}r(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
and
\mathcal{B}r(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})
.
however, Eq. (
IV
) can be tested through partial wave analysis.
The Hamiltonian is symmetric and anti-symmetric if the two mesons are in relatively even and odd angular momentum
L
.
In the case of
L=0,2,...
, amplitudes
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
and
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
are the same.
Then Eq. (
IV
) becomes

\displaystyle 2\,\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})_{L=0,2,...}-\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})_{L=0,2,...}=0.

(161)

In the case of
L=1,3,...
, amplitudes
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
in sign, and
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{0}\pi^{+})
are opposite and
\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})=0
.
Eq. (
IV
) reduces to a trivial equation of
0=0
.
Above discussion can be applied to the other isospin sum rules listed in Appendices
A
and
B
.
The isospin equations given by Ref.
Savage:1989qr
, such as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{0})_{L=1,3,...}-\mathcal{A}(\Lambda^{+}_{c}\to\Sigma^{+}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})_{L=1,3,...}=0,

(162)

are re-derived in different manner.
In fact, the discussion about
\rho^{+}
resonance in the isospin sum rule (
IV
) is a practical example for
L=1
.
As a systematic approach, many new isospin sum rules for cases of even and odd

L
can be given according to Appendices
A
and
B
.
These could be tested in future experiments and provide useful information in the search for new decay modes.

V
Summary

Flavor symmetry is a powerful tool to analyze the charmed baryon weak decays.
In this work, we derive the isospin sum rules for the two- and three-body non-leptonic decays of singly and doubly charmed baryons in a systematic approach.
Hundreds of isospin sum rules are derived to test the isospin symmetry and study the isospin partners of intermediate resonances in the charmed baryon decays.

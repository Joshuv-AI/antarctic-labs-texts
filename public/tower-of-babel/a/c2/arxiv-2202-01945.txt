Finite-temperature plasmons, damping and collective behavior for α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} model
Andrii Iurov 1 , Liubov Zhemchuzhna 1,2 , Godfrey Gumbs 2,3 , Danhong Huang 4 , Dipendra Dahal 5 , Yonatan Abranyos 2

Abstract
We have conducted a thorough theoretical and numerical investigation of the electronic susceptibility, polarizability, plasmons, their damping rates, as well as the static screening in pseudospin-1 Dirac cone materials with a flat band, or for a general α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} model, at finite temperatures. This includes calculating the polarization function, plasmon dispersions and their damping rates at arbitrary temperatures and obtaining analytical approximations the long wavelength limit, low and high temperatures. We demonstrate that the integral transformation of the polarization function cannot be used directly for a dice lattice revealing some fundamental properties and important applicability limits of the flat band dispersions model. At k B ​ T ≪ E F k_{B}T\ll E_{F} , the largest temperature-induced change of the polarization function and plasmons comes from the mismatch between the chemical potential and the Fermi energy. We have also obtained a series of closed-form semi-analytical expressions for the static limit of the polarization function of an arbitrary α − 𝒯 3 \alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3} material at any temperature with exact analytical formulas for the high, low and zero temperature limits which is of tremendous importance for all types of transport and screening calculations for the flat band Dirac materials.

II
Polarization function in
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials at finite temperature: general formalism

The low-energy Hamiltonian in
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
is written in the following form

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}(\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\,|\,\tau,\phi)=\hbar v_{F}\,\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&k^{\tau}_{-}\cos\phi&0\\
0&0&k^{\tau}_{-}\sin\phi\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right]+h.c.\ ,

(1)

where the geometric phase
\phi=\tan^{-1}\alpha
and
k^{\tau}_{\pm}=k^{\tau}_{x}\pm ik^{\tau}_{y}
are obtained from the components of the electron wave vector
{\bf k}
.

One of the most important features in connection with plasmons at zero and finite temperatures is
its dispersion relations, i.e., dependence of the plasmon frequency
\omega
on wave number
q
. Physically, these complex relations can be determined from the zeros of a dielectric function
\epsilon_{T}(q,\,\omega)
given by

\epsilon_{\phi,T}(q,\,\omega)=1-v(q)\,\Pi_{T}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu(T))=0\ ,

(2)

where
v(q)=2\pi\alpha_{r}/q\equiv e^{2}/2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}q
is the 2D Fourier-transformed Coulomb potential,

\alpha_{r}=e^{2}/4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{r}
, and
\epsilon_{r}
represents the dielectric constant of the host material.

The dielectric function introduced in Eq. (
2
) is determined directly by the finite-temperature
polarization function
,
or
polarizability
,
\Pi_{T}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu(T))
, which is, in turn, related to its
zero-temperature counterpart
,
\Pi_{0}(q,\omega\,|\,E_{F})
, by an integral convolution with respect to different Fermi energies
Maldague 1978
, given by

\Pi_{T}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu(T))=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\eta\,\frac{\Pi_{0}(q,\omega\,|\,\eta)}{1+\cosh\left[(\mu-\eta)/k_{B}T\right]}\ ,

(3)

where the integration variable
\eta
stands for the electron Fermi energy at
T=0
. This equation is derived for electron doping with
\eta=E_{F}>0
. We note that, in order to evaluate this integral, one needs to know in advance how the chemical potential
\mu(T)
varies with temperature
T
.

In RPA, the polarization function for a pseudospin-1 Hamiltonian of
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
model is given by

\Pi_{0}(q,\omega\,|\,\phi,\mu(T))=\frac{g}{4\pi^{2}}\,\int d^{2}{\bf k}\sum\limits_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}=0\,\pm 1}\,\mathbb{O}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}+{\bf q}})\,\frac{n_{F}[\epsilon_{\gamma}(k),\mu]-n_{F}[\epsilon_{\gamma^{\prime}}(|{\bf k}+\bf{q}|),\mu]}{\left(\hbar\omega+i0^{+}\right)+\epsilon_{\gamma}(k)-\epsilon_{\gamma^{\prime}}(|{\bf k}+\bf{q}|)}\,,

(4)

where
n_{F}[\epsilon_{\gamma}(k),\mu(T)]=\left\{1+\texttt{exp}[(\gamma\,\hbar v_{F}k-\mu)/(k_{B}T)]\right\}^{-1}
are Fermi-Dirac distribution functions and the wave function overlaps
\mathbb{O}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}+{\bf q}})
are given by
Huang et al. 2019

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}_{\pm 1,1}(\phi\,|\,\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\mathbb{O}_{1,\pm 1}(\phi\,|\,\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{4}\,\left(1\pm\cos\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\cos^{2}(2\phi)\,\sin^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{O}_{0,\pm 1}(\phi\,|\,\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\mathbb{O}_{\pm 1,0}(\phi\,|\,\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{2}\,\sin^{2}(2\phi)\,\sin^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}

Interestingly, an intra-band element corresponding to the transitions inside the flat band
0\leftrightarrow 0
exists and is equal to

\mathbb{O}_{\pm 0,0}(\phi\,|\,\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\cos^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}+\cos^{2}(2\phi)\sin^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}
. However, these two transitions do not contribute to the polarization function because the difference of the energies and the
corresponding distribution functions on the flat band are always zero disregarding of the temperature
which corresponds to a very limited effect of the presence of a flat band, close to graphene.

III
Long wavelength limit of
\Pi_{\phi,T}^{(0)}(q,\omega)
at zero and finite temperatures

In this section, our aim is to find the long wavelength limit (
q\longrightarrow 0
) of the polarization function and the plasmon dispersions which are often used to evaluate a number of crucial properties of the material and the plasmon behavior in various heterostructures
Gumbs et al. 2015
;
Iurov et al. 2017c
Our results for
T=0
and
q\ll E_{F}/(\hbar v_{F})
are based on analytical expressions for the polarization function demonstrated in Ref. [
Malcolm and Nicol 2016
]. Strictly speaking, our results for an arbitrary
\alpha
-T
3
case is an interpolation (not a rigorous derivation): we presented the expressions for a dice lattice as the corresponding result for graphene plus additional non-graphene terms and interpolated these extra terms
\backsim\mathcal{B}_{\phi}=1/2\,\sin^{2}(2\phi)
for all
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials. However, we strongly believe that this result is precise because exactly the same
\phi-
dependence was rigorously obtained in our thorough static limit derivation in Section
IV
.

Following our derivation in Appendix
B
, we write

16\pi\hbar^{2}g^{-1}\,\Pi_{\phi}^{(0)}(q,\omega)=2E_{F}\left\{\frac{2}{\omega^{2}}-\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\phi}(\hbar\omega)^{2}}{\left[4E_{F}^{2}-(\hbar\omega)^{2}\right]^{2}}\right\}\,q^{2}-(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\,\frac{q^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\,\ln\left[\frac{2E_{F}+\hbar\omega}{2E_{F}-\hbar\omega}\right]\,.

(6)

Now we are assured that the plasmon branch varies as
\backsim\sqrt{q}
, as it should be for any two-dimensional Dirac cone lattice. This means that for
q\longrightarrow 0
, the plasmon is located at an energy
\Omega_{p}\backsim\sqrt{q}\ll E_{F}
and Eq. (
33
) could be further simplified as

16\pi g^{-1}\,\text{Re}\,\Pi_{\phi,T=0}(q,\omega)=\left\{\frac{4E_{F}}{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}-\left(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\frac{2}{E_{F}}-\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\frac{(\hbar\omega)^{2}}{(2E_{F})^{3}}\right\}\,q^{2}\,.

(7)

The analytical expression for the real part of the zero-temperature polarization function could be found only for finite and sufficiently large doping level
E_{F}
. The polarization function for finite doping cannot be presented as intrinsic
\text{Re}\,\Pi_{\phi,T=0}(q,\omega)
plus an additional term proportional to the Fermi energy. Therefore, the integral transformation in Eq. (
3
) cannot be applied to the real part of the polarization function
7
and the analytical expression for the temperature-induced modification of the polarization function needs to be generalized in the presence of a flat band. We would, however, estimate the finite-temperature real polarizability using the fact that at
T=0
K the flat band has only very limited effect on the polarization function (next order of
\hbar\omega
) and the plasmon branch in the long wavelength limit. Apart from that, at high temperature
k_{B}T\gg E_{F}
and the long wavelength limit
q\ll k_{F}
the situation is such that the finite electron doping
E_{F}
has a negligible effect on the polarization function and the plasmon branch,
Sarma and Li 2013
i.e., at high temperature the extrinsic and intrinsic materials’ behavior is almost identical. Therefore, in the lowest-order approximation, we can assume that the polarization function is the same as for undoped graphene.
Sarma and Li 2013

\text{Re}\,\Pi_{\phi,T\gg E_{F}}(q,\omega)=\frac{g\ln 2}{2\pi}\,k_{B}T\,\frac{q^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\,

(8)

In the lowest order for which a coefficient
\mathcal{B}_{\phi}=1/2\sin^{2}(2\phi)
would not vanish, the zero-temperature plasmon
branch for an
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
lattice is given as

\Omega_{p}^{2}(q,T=0\,|\,E_{F},\phi)=\frac{2gE_{F}^{2}\,q}{4E_{F}+g\left(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\,q}\backsimeq\frac{gE_{F}}{2\hbar^{2}}\,q-\left(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\frac{g^{2}}{8\hbar^{2}}\,q^{2}+...\,.

(9)

The imaginary part of the polarization function for an
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
model is calculated as (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T=0}(q,\omega)\backsimeq-\frac{g\,q^{2}}{16\,\hbar\omega}\Big\{4\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\Theta(\omega-E_{F})+(1-2\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\,\Theta\left[\omega-2E_{F}\right]\Big\}\,.

(10)

The imaginary part of the finite-temperature polarization function in Eq. (
10
) could be easily calculated as

\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T\gg T_{F}}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)\backsimeq\frac{g}{16}\,\frac{q^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\,\left\{4\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\,\tanh\left(\frac{\omega-\mu}{2k_{B}T}\right)+(1-2\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\,\tanh\left[\frac{\omega-2\mu}{4k_{B}T}\right]+(1+2\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\,\tanh\left[\frac{\mu}{2k_{B}T}\right]\right\}\,.

(11)

At high temperature
k_{B}T\gg\mu
or
k_{B}T\gg\omega
, we can approximate
\tanh x\backsimeq x
so that

\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T\gg T_{F}}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)\backsimeq-\frac{g}{64}\,\frac{q^{2}}{k_{B}T}\,\left(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\,.

(12)

The effect of doping on the imaginary part of the polarizability is only seen in the next-order corrections:

\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T\gg T_{F}}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)\backsimeq-\frac{g}{64}\,\frac{q^{2}}{k_{B}T}\,\left\{1-\frac{1}{48}\,\left(\frac{\omega}{k_{B}T}\right)^{2}+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\,\left[1-\frac{5}{48}\,\left(\frac{\omega}{k_{B}T}\right)^{2}\right]\right\}\,.

(13)

Here, we assumed that at high temperature
k_{B}T\gg E_{F}
we can use
\omega\backsim k_{B}T\gg\mu(T)
.

The decay rate
\Gamma_{\phi,T}[q,\Omega_{p}(q)\,|\,\mu]
of the obtained plasmon branches for the case of low damping could be approximated in the first order of
\Gamma(q,\Omega_{p})/\Omega_{p}(q)
as

\Gamma_{\phi,T}[q,\Omega_{p}(q)\,|\,\mu]=\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)\,\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega}\text{Re}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)\Big|_{\omega=\Omega_{p}(q)}\right\}^{-1}\,.

(14)

Such an approximation works particularly well at a large temperature
k_{B}T\gg E_{F}
and a small wave vector
q\ll k_{F}
when the real part grows
\backsim T
and imaginary part is reduced as
\backsim 1/T
. For the intermediate part, the validity of approximation
14
is not obvious and must be verified by the effect of the next-order terms
\backsim\Gamma^{2}
etc.

Substituting the obtained expressions (

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}(q,T)=\sqrt{\alpha_{r}g\log(2)\,\hbar v_{F}q\,k_{B}T}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi,T}[q,\Omega_{p}(q)]=\frac{g\pi}{32}\,\sqrt{\log(2)}\,\frac{(\alpha_{r}\,\hbar v_{F}q)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k_{B}T}}\,\left(1+6\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\,.

. In the long wavelength limit, we see that the dispersions of the plasmon branches only decrease in the second order of

q
, this decrease becomes more substantial, but the most important difference is between a dice lattice (and any

which makes the plasmon branch strongly damped at any temperature.

At high temperature, the plasmon branches corresponding to phases

(almost graphene) and
\phi_{2}
(almost a dice lattice) are located closer to each other meaning that the effect of the flat band is largely suppressed, but for a large phase the plasmons become highly damned at lower frequencies irrespective of the temperature. The decay rate of the plasmons is also strongly affected by the
\omega
- derivative, or how closely the branch is located to the main diagonal
\omega=v_{F}q
. Thus, we see that for graphene-like materials the damping is stronger for small wave vector values (black curves in Figure

Additionally, it seems interesting to investigate the so-called plasmon pinching effect meaning that all the plasmon dispersions corresponding to different relative dielectric constants
\alpha_{r}
intersect at
\hbar\omega=\hbar v_{F}q=E_{F}

\alpha_{r}
are located at lower frequencies closer to the main diagonal which increases the
\omega

V
Concluding remarks and Summary

In this work, we have investigated the polarization function, plasmons and their damping rates in
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials. The calculations were carried out at finite temperature with the main focus on the temperature dependence of the effect of the presence of a flat band in the electron bandstructure.

The particle-hole modes, determined by the finite imaginary part of the polarization function, demonstrate completely different behaviors dependent on their location in the
(q,\omega)
-plane. In the long wavelength limit, the high-temperature behavior of the plasmon and its damping is qualitatively similar to graphene, i.e.,
\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T\gg T_{F}}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)
falls off on
1/T
. On the other hand, the real part of the polarization function increases as
\backsim T
so that the plasmon is well defined as long as
k_{B}T/\gg E_{F}
. However, the actual values of
\text{Im}\,\Pi^{(0)}_{\phi,T\gg T_{F}}(q,\omega\,|\,\mu)
and
\Gamma_{\phi,T}[q,\Omega_{p}(q)]
differing substantially from that in graphene being significantly larger at any finite temperature.

We have obtained a closed-form analytical expression for the long wavelength limit of the polarization function and plasmon dispersions for all
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials. Additionally, we have demonstrated that this long wavelength behavior is nearly similar to the case of graphene
(\phi=0)
and the effect of the flat band is only revealed in the next order in
q
as
\backsim q^{2}
. The plasmon pinching effect is suppressed at any finite temperature so that the plasmon branches corresponding to different dielectric constants
\alpha_{r}
would no longer intersect at one point. We have also obtained semi-analytical expressions for the Lindhard function, or the static limit of the polarization function. These results were obtained for various temperatures with completely analytical approximations for the high, low and zero temperatures for an arbitrary
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
material with
0<\alpha<1
.

The static polarization function is of special importance and is is often highlighted for the role it plays in collective phenomena. This is so because the static dielectric function is the key component for the temperature-dependent conductivity in the presence of charged impurities, finite-temperature screening and, more generally, Boltzmann transport. It is also employed in evaluating the screened potential of a point charge, Friedel oscillations and the effect of an electric or magnetic impurity on the electron response, as well as the RKKY interaction. Our convenient, handy and ready-to-use semi-analytical expressions are expected to motivate researchers leading to a new wave of research of the many-body properties of
\alpha-\mathcal{T}_{3}
materials.

In brief, we have discovered some important information on the plasmons and collective behavior of pseudospin-1 Dirac materials at finite temperatures. We are confident that these results will find numerous applications in both fundamental research of these innovative systems and their applications. Specifically, we believe that our crucial analytical expressions will be useful to experimentalists for predicting the behavior of flat-band materials at experimentally accessible high and room temperatures.

\xi^{(0)}_{\phi}(q,T)
is equal to zero since
n_{F}(0,k)=n_{F}(0,k^{\prime})=\left[1+\texttt{exp}[-\mu/(k_{B}T)\right]^{-1}
is independent of wave vector
k
or
k^{\prime}
, and
\mathbb{O}_{\pm 1,0}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=\mathbb{O}_{0,\pm 1}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})=1/2\,\sin^{2}(2\phi)\,\sin^{2}(\beta_{\,{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})
.

Also, we will often use the fact that a simultaneous change of variables
{\bf q}\longrightarrow-{\bf q}
and
{\bf k}\longrightarrow-{\bf k}
does not affect any of the overlaps
\mathbb{O}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})
since
\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}=\measuredangle({\bf k},{\bf q})=\pi-\measuredangle({\bf k},-{\bf q})=\pi-\measuredangle(-{\bf k},{\bf q})=\measuredangle({\bf-k},{\bf-q})
so that

\displaystyle\frac{k+(-{\bf q})\cdot{\bf k}}{k|-{\bf q}+{\bf k}|}\longrightarrow\frac{(-k)^{2}+{\bf q}\cdot(-{\bf k})}{k|{\bf q}-{\bf k}|}\,,

(46)

which leads us to

\displaystyle\cos\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}-{\bf q}}=\cos\beta_{-{\bf k},-{\bf k}+{\bf q}}\,.

(47)

Condition (
47
) clearly holds true for
\cos^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}
and for
\sin^{2}\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}
. The substitution
k\rightarrow-k
, equivalent to
\theta\longrightarrow\pi+\theta
, could always be used since we perform an angular integration
\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi
. To summarize, we write

\mathbb{O}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})\xrightarrow[\gamma_{1}\leftrightarrow\gamma_{2}]{{\bf k}\leftrightarrow{\bf k}^{\prime}}\mathbb{O}_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}(\beta_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}})\,,

(48)

i.e., there is a complete reciprocity in any transitions
\gamma_{1}\leftrightarrow\gamma_{2}
and for
{\bf k}\leftrightarrow{\bf k}^{\prime}
.

The angular integration yields (in units of
2\pi/(\hbar v_{F})
)

\displaystyle\,\chi^{(+)}_{\phi}(q,T)=-\mu(T)-k_{B}T\,\log\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{-\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}+\hbar v_{F}\int\limits_{0}^{q/2}\,dk\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k-\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}

(49)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1+2\mathcal{B}_{\phi}-(2k/q)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-(2k/q)^{2}}}+2\hbar v_{F}\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\int\limits_{0}^{q/2}dk\,\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k-\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}\,[q/(2k)]^{2}\,\left\{1-\left[1-(2k/q)^{2}\right]^{-1/2}\right\}

\displaystyle+2\hbar v_{F}\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\int\limits_{q/2}^{\infty}dk\,\left(\frac{q}{2k}\right)^{2}\,\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k-\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}\,,

where
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}=1/2\,[1+\cos^{2}(2\phi)]
ranges between
\mathcal{A}_{0}=1
for graphene and
\mathcal{A}_{1}=1/2
for a dice lattice, and
\mathcal{B}_{\phi}=1/2\,\sin^{2}(2\phi)=1-\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
. Also,

\displaystyle\,\chi^{(-)}_{\phi}(q,T)=-(1+4\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\frac{\pi}{8}\,\hbar v_{F}q-k_{B}T\,\log\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{-\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}+\hbar v_{F}\int\limits_{0}^{q/2}\,dk\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k+\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}

(50)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1+2\mathcal{B}_{\phi}-(2k/q)^{2}}{\sqrt{1-(2k/q)^{2}}}+2\hbar v_{F}\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\int\limits_{0}^{q/2}dk\,\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k+\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}\,[q/(2k)]^{2}\,\left\{1-\left[1-(2k/q)^{2}\right]^{-1/2}\right\}

\displaystyle+2\hbar v_{F}\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\int\limits_{q/2}^{\infty}dk\,\left(\frac{q}{2k}\right)^{2}\,\left\{1+\texttt{exp}\left[\frac{\hbar v_{F}k+\mu}{k_{B}T}\right]\right\}^{-1}\,,

while

\chi^{(0)}_{\phi}(q,T)=0\,.

(51)

Here, we used the following identities

\mathbb{I}_{1}^{(\beta)}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,\frac{\cos\phi\,d\phi}{c_{1}+c_{2}\,\cos\phi}=\frac{1}{c_{2}}\left[2\pi-c_{1}\,\mathbb{I}_{0}^{(\beta)}\right]\,,\hskip 7.22743ptc_{2}>0\,,

(52)

as well as

\mathbb{I}_{2}^{(\beta)}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,\frac{\cos^{2}\phi\,d\phi}{c_{1}+c_{2}\,\cos\phi}=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}^{2}}\left[-2\pi+c_{1}\,\mathbb{I}_{0}^{(\beta)}\right]\,,\hskip 7.22743ptc_{2}>0\,,

(53)

and for a general power
n

\mathbb{I}_{n}^{(\beta)}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,\frac{\cos^{n}\theta\,d\phi}{c_{1}+c_{2}\,\cos\phi}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{c_{2}}\,\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\right)^{n-1}\,\left[2\pi-c_{1}\,\mathbb{I}_{0}^{(\beta)}\right]\,,\hskip 7.22743ptc_{2}>0\,,

(54)

expressed through a well-known integral

\mathbb{I}_{0}^{(\beta)}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\,\frac{d\phi}{c_{1}+c_{2}\,\cos\phi}=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}}}\,\Theta\left(c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}^{2}\right)\,\text{sign}(c_{1}-c_{2})\,,

(55)

which was widely used for similar calculations in graphene
Gonçalves and
Peres 2016
. Here, we need to ensure that
|c_{1}|>|c_{2}|
meaning that there are no poles on the real axis inside the unit circle. If this condition is not satisfied, the principal value of the integral is zero whereas it is actually divergent.

At zero temperature,

\displaystyle\,\chi^{(+)}_{\phi}(q,T\rightarrow 0)=-E_{F}+(1+4\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\frac{\pi}{8}\,\hbar v_{F}q-\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\frac{(\hbar v_{F}q)^{2}}{2E_{F}}\,,

(56)

for
q<2k_{F}
and

\displaystyle\chi^{(+)}_{\phi}(q,T\rightarrow 0)=

\displaystyle-E_{F}+\frac{E_{F}}{2}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{2k_{F}}{q}\right)^{2}}+\hbar v_{F}q\,\left(\frac{1}{4}+\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right)\,\arcsin\left(\frac{2k_{F}}{q}\right)

(57)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\frac{(\hbar v_{F}q)^{2}}{2E_{F}}\left[1-\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{2k_{F}}{q}\right)^{2}}\,\right]\,

for
q>2k_{F}
. Thus,
\chi^{(+)}_{\phi}(q,T)
could be presented as a single expression

\displaystyle\chi^{(+)}_{\phi}(q,T\rightarrow 0)=

\displaystyle-E_{F}+(1+4\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\frac{\pi}{8}\,\hbar v_{F}q-\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\frac{(\hbar v_{F}q)^{2}}{2E_{F}}+\left\{\frac{1}{2}\,\left(E_{F}+\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\,\frac{q^{2}}{E_{F}}\right)\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{2k_{F}}{q}\right)^{2}}\right.

(58)

\displaystyle-\left.\hbar v_{F}q\,\left[\frac{1}{4}+\mathcal{B}_{\phi}\right]\,\arccos\left(\frac{2k_{F}}{q}\right)\,\right\}\,\Theta(q-2k_{F})\,.

The hole contribution term
\chi^{(-)}_{\phi}(q,T)
at
T\rightarrow 0
becomes

\displaystyle\,\chi^{(-)}_{\phi}(q,T\rightarrow 0)=-(1+4\mathcal{B}_{\phi})\frac{\pi}{8}\,\hbar v_{F}q\,,

(59)

which does not have a special point (discontinuity of its derivative) at
q=2k_{F}
.

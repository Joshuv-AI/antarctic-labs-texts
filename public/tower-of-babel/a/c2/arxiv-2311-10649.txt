Computable and Faithful Lower Bound on Entanglement Cost
Xin Wang, Mingrui Jing, Chengkai Zhu

Abstract
Quantifying the minimum entanglement needed to prepare quantum states and implement quantum processes is a key challenge in quantum information theory. In this work, we develop computable and faithful lower bounds on the entanglement cost under quantum operations that completely preserve the positivity of partial transpose (PPT operations), by introducing the generalized divergence of k k -negativity, a generalization of logarithmic negativity. Our bounds are efficiently computable via semidefinite programming and provide non-trivial values for all states that are non-PPT (NPT), establishing their faithfulness for the resource theory of NPT entanglement. Notably, we find and affirm the irreversibility of asymptotic entanglement manipulation under PPT operations for full-rank entangled states. Furthermore, we extend our methodology to derive lower bounds on the entanglement cost of both point-to-point and bipartite quantum channels. Our bound demonstrates improvements over previously known computable bounds for a wide range of quantum states and channels. These findings push the boundaries of understanding the structure of entanglement and the fundamental limits of entanglement manipulation.



Introduction.
—
Quantum entanglement, a phenomenon at the heart of quantum mechanics, has been widely recognized as a valuable resource for performing tasks impossible with classical systems alone
Horodecki et al. 2009
. The correlation between entangled quantum systems has led to various applications in quantum computation
Bruß and Macchiavello 2011
;
Jozsa and Linden 2003
, communication
Bennett et al. 1999
, sensing
Degen et al. 2017
, and cryptography
Ekert 1991
. Developing a comprehensive theory of entanglement quantification is a priority in quantum information
Wilde 2017
;
Watrous 2018
;
Hayashi 2017
and quantum resource theory
Chitambar and Gour 2019
.

To harness the full potential of quantum entanglement in various applications, one crucial aspect is quantifying the minimum amount of entanglement needed to prepare a given target state
Bennett et al. 1996a
;
Hayden et al. 2001
or quantum operation
Berta et al. 2013
. This plays a critical role in optimizing the efficiency of quantum computation and communication. For example, the quantum teleportation protocol
Bennett et al. 1993
uses one ebit (entanglement bit), measured by the maximally entangled state (MES)
Gisin and Bechmann-Pasquinucci 1998
, to simulate an identity channel that perfectly transfers any quantum state between two parties. Further, efficiently estimating entanglement cost is extremely important for understanding the quantumness of implementing quantum algorithms
Chen et al. 2022a
, performing quantum error correction
Brun et al. 2006
, enhancing quantum sensing
Xia et al. 2023
, and simulating quantum information processing
Wang and Wilde 2020
. Therefore, a computable and faithful estimation method is a commonly pursued endeavor in the field of entanglement resource theory.

Figure 1:
General framework of entanglement cost. Given access to copies of ebits, these golden ‘coins’ are utilized to realize a target quantum state
\rho
or quantum channel
{\cal N}
through a process that cannot generate any maximally entangled states (MES) itself. The minimal rate of MES required to achieve this process perfectly is called the entanglement cost of the specific object. This concept plays a crucial role in quantifying entanglement in the studies of resource irreversibility, quantum communication, dynamical entanglement, cryptography, and quantum sensing.

For the case of state preparation, despite the known challenges in evaluating the entanglement cost via regularizing the entanglement of formation
Wootters 1998
;
Winter 2016
;
Hastings 2009
, numerous attempts have yielded to get valuable bounds. The squashed entanglement
Christandl and Winter 2004
is a faithful lower bound, yet it is hardly computable. Two other semidefinite programming (SDP) lower bounds
Wang and Duan 2017a
;
Lami and Regula 2023
are not faithful, thus can not estimate the entanglement cost of general quantum states precisely. Only very few instances of states were nearly solved due to their special symmetries
Audenaert et al. 2002a
. Beyond that, little was known about estimating the entanglement cost of dynamic quantum processes, which requires both efficient and accurate solutions to the static entanglement cost. Very few quantum channels were investigated with their dynamical cost estimations
Wilde 2018
;
Gour and Scandolo 2020
. The faithful bound of the cost to implement general quantum channels or even bipartite channels can be extremely difficult
Berta et al. 2013
;
Wilde 2018
.

In this paper, we develop the first efficiently computable and faithful lower bound on the entanglement cost under quantum operations that completely preserve the positivity of partial transpose (PPT operations)
Audenaert et al. 2003
;
Audenaert et al. 2002b
;
Wang and Duan 2017a
. Technically, we introduce a sequential classification of quantum sub-states called the
k
-hierarchy of positive partial transpose (
{\text{PPT}}_{k}
) and combine it with the quantum Rényi divergence to arrive at an entanglement quantifier for any bipartite state. This is the first SDP lower bound on the entanglement cost that is also faithful for the NPT entanglement theory. The advantages of our bounds are shown by comparing them with previously known ones on random states. Notably, we demonstrate that entanglement manipulation could be irreversible for fully non-degenerate states asymptotically, a phenomenon not previously observed. We further apply our methodology to the dynamical entanglement cost of both point-to-point and bipartite quantum channels, improving the previous lower bound on the entanglement cost of the Werner-Holevo channel for specific dimensions and providing computable lower bounds on the entanglement cost for more general bipartite quantum channels such as noisy Hamiltonian evolutions.

Static entanglement cost.
—
Let
{\cal H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Consider two parties, Alice and Bob, with associated Hilbert spaces
{\cal H}_{A}
and
{\cal H}_{B}
, respectively, where the dimensions of
{\cal H}_{A}
and
{\cal H}_{B}
are denoted as
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
. We denote the set of all linear operators on system
A
as
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{A})
and the set of all density operators as
\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{A})
. The maximally entangled state of dimension
d\otimes d
is denoted as
\Phi_{AB}^{+}(d)=1/d\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}|ii\rangle\!\langle jj|
. The trace norm of
\rho
is denoted as
\|\rho\|_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho^{\dagger}\rho})
.
A bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
is called a PPT state if it admits positive partial transpose, i.e.,
\rho_{AB}^{T_{B}}\geq 0
where
T_{B}
denotes taking partial transpose over the system
B
. Note that any state that is not PPT is called NPT, which will be a resource in the resource theory of NPT entanglement
Gour and Scandolo 2020
. A quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is a linear transformation from
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{A})
to
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{B})
that is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP).

In the task of entanglement dilution, Alice and Bob share a large supply of Bell pairs and try to convert
rn
Bell pairs to
n
high fidelity copies of the desired state
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
using suitable operations. The
entanglement cost

E_{C,\Omega}
of a given bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
quantifies the optimal rate
r
of converting
{rn}
Bell pairs to
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
with arbitrarily high fidelity in the limit of large
n
. The concise definition of the entanglement cost using
\Omega
operations is given as follows:

E_{C,\Omega}(\rho_{AB})=\inf\{r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\Lambda\in\Omega}\|\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}-\Lambda(\Phi^{+}_{AB}(2^{rn}))\|_{1}=0\},

where
\Omega\in\{{\text{1-LOCC}},{\text{LOCC}},{\text{SEP}},{\text{PPT}}\}
and we write
E_{C,{\text{LOCC}}}(\cdot)=E_{C}(\cdot)
for simplification. For the case with an exact transformation rather than the vanishing error, the rate refers to the exact entanglement cost
Audenaert et al. 2003
;
Wang and Wilde 2020
.

To advance our understanding of static entanglement, we draw inspiration from the Rains set
{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}=\{\sigma_{AB}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sigma_{AB}\succeq 0,\|\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1\}
, initially proposed to estimate the distillable entanglement
Rains 1999
(see, e.g.,
Christandl and Winter 2004
;
Leditzky et al. 2017
;
Wang and Duan 2016
;
Wang and Duan 2017b
;
Fang et al. 2019
;
Kaur et al. 2019
for other methods). We generalize the set to a sequence of sub-state sets
{\text{PPT${}_{k}$}}(k\geq 2)
as

\displaystyle{\text{PPT${}_{k}$}}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\Big\{\omega_{1}\in\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega_{1}\succeq 0,~\exists\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=2}^{k},~\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle|\omega_{i}^{T_{B}}|_{*}\preceq\omega_{i+1},\forall i\in[1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k-1],~\big\|\omega_{k}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1\Big\},

where
|X|_{*}\preceq Y
denotes
-Y\preceq X\preceq Y
.
In particular,
{\text{PPT}}_{1}
reduces to the Rains set
{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}
. Moreover,
{\text{PPT}}_{k}
is related to the quantity
\chi_{p}
developed in
(
Lami et al. 2025
, Eq. (6))
for the computable zero-error PPT entanglement cost, i.e.,
{\text{PPT}}_{k}=\{\omega_{AB}\succeq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\chi_{k-1}(\omega_{AB})\leq 1\}
. By construction, any sub-state in
{\text{PPT}}_{k+1}
can be shown as an element in
{\text{PPT}}_{k}
, and the Rains set
{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}
serves as the largest boundary set within this hierarchical structure,
i.e.,
{\text{PPT}}\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{k}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{2}\subseteq{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}
.
Based on the hierarchy, we introduce the
logarithmic fidelity of binegativity
of any bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
as follows.

E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB})=-\log\max_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT${}_{2}$}}}F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}),

(1)

where
F(\rho,\sigma)
is the fidelity between
\rho
and
\sigma
. We call it logarithmic fidelity of binegativity because
{\text{PPT}}_{2}
has a similar spirit of binegativity by recursively taking the partial transpose and finally utilizing the negativity (see Appendix
II
for the generalized divergence of
k
-negativity and our alternative SDP bound). Our first main result is that the logarithmic fidelity of binegativity actually gives a faithful and efficiently computable lower bound on the entanglement cost under PPT operations.

Theorem 1

(Lower bound on the static entanglement cost)

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{AB})
, its entanglement cost is lower bounded by

E_{C}(\rho_{AB})\geq E_{C,{\text{PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})\geq E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB}).

(2)

Sketch of proof.
Since
\alpha\mapsto D_{\alpha}(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})
for given
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
is a monotonically non-decreasing function for the sandwiched Rényi relative entropy in
\alpha

Müller-Lennert et al. 2013
;
Wilde et al. 2014
,
we have

D(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})\geq D_{1/2}(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})=-\log F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}).

Notice that
{\text{PPT}}\subsetneq{\text{PPT${}_{2}$}}
, we have

E_{R,{\text{PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})\geq-\max_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}}\log F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})\geq E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB}),

where
E_{R,{\text{PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})
is the PPT relative entropy of entanglement
Audenaert et al. 2002a
;
Plenio and Virmani 2007
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle E_{C,{\text{PPT}}}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}E_{R}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})

(3)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})=E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB}),

where the first inequality follows from Ref.
(
Hayashi 2016
, p. 421)
and the equality is a consequence of the additivity of
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\cdot)
proved in Appendix
II
.

To the best of our knowledge, the entanglement cost lower bound given in Theorem
1
is the first faithful lower bound in the frame of NPT entanglement theory, and can also be efficiently computed via SDP. Notably, it establishes a vital connection between the entanglement cost of a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
and its distance to the sub-state set
{\text{PPT}}_{2}
. In essence, states that exhibit a considerable separation from any sub-state with negative logarithmic binegativity are associated with a correspondingly higher entanglement cost. More details and proofs can be found in Appendix
II
.

We compared our bound with previous known ones such as the bound
E_{\eta}
introduced in Ref.
Wang and Duan 2017a
, and the
tempered negativity

E_{N}^{\tau}

Lami and Regula 2023
. We generate random bipartite states of fixed rank with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt measure
Życzkowski et al. 2011
and compare the corresponding values of
E_{\eta}
and
E_{N}^{\tau}
with
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
. In Fig.
2
, it can be observed that higher-rank states tend to have diminishing values of
E_{\eta}
and
E_{N}^{\tau}
, and
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
could provider tighter estimation in most of the cases. More comparisons among our bound and existing bounds have been illustrated in Appendix
III
.

E_{N,2}^{1/2}
with
E_{\eta}
and (c-d) Compare
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
with
E_{N}^{\tau}
. Each dot corresponds to one of
500
randomly generated states according to the Hilbert-Schmidt measure. The red line indicates states
\rho_{AB}
for which two measures give the same bound value. The dots above the red line (resp. below) indicate the states for which
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
is tighter (resp.
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
is looser).

Our analysis particularly highlights the effectiveness of the bound
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
in evaluating the entanglement cost of general noisy states, which are not adequately addressed by
E_{\eta}
. For any full-rank, NPT bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, our findings show that
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{AB})
is always greater than
E_{\eta}(\rho_{AB})
, primarily because a full-rank state make
E_{\eta}(\rho_{AB})
exactly zero. This also demonstrates the faithfulness of our new bounds
E_{N,k}
, as they remain positive for all full-rank NPT states.

As a notable application, in the following, we apply our bounds to affirm the irreversibility of asymptotic entanglement manipulation under PPT operations for full-rank quantum states. The reversibility of entanglement manipulation has been an essential topic in quantum information theory. The asymptotic entanglement manipulation is reversible for pure states
Bennett et al. 1996a
, which makes the entanglement entropy a unique entanglement measure of pure states
Plenio and Virmani 2007
;
Horodecki et al. 2000
. However, the manipulation is inherently irreversible for general mixed states under LOCC or PPT operations
Vidal and Cirac 2001
;
Vidal et al. 2002
;
Vollbrecht et al. 2004
;
Cornelio et al. 2011
;
Yang et al. 2005
;
Wang and Duan 2017a
;
Lami and Regula 2023
, unlike the conservation of resources in thermodynamics. This irreversibility highlights the impossibility of establishing a single measure that governs all entanglement transformations, and establishing reversibility requires a deeper understanding of entanglement manipulation. Whether there is a reversible entanglement theory under some larger class of operations, e.g., asymptotically entanglement non-generating operations, has been intensively studied recently
Fang et al. 2021
;
Berta et al. 2023
;
Hayashi and Yamasaki 2024
;
Lami 2025
.

To investigate the irreversibility of entanglement manipulation, we introduce the PPT-undistillable entanglement
E_{\text{U,PPT}}(\rho_{AB})
of a bipartite quantum state as the difference between the entanglement cost and the distillable entanglement of the state regarding the PPT operations. A positive PPT-undistillable entanglement indicates the irreversibility of the entanglement manipulation under PPT operations. This irreversibility has been observed in rank-two states supporting supporting the
3\otimes 3
antisymmetric subspace
Wang and Duan 2017a
, exemplified by states like
\rho_{v}=\frac{1}{2}(|v_{1}\rangle\!\langle v_{1}|+|v_{2}\rangle\!\langle v_{2}|)
where

|v_{1}\rangle=1/\sqrt{2}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle),\ |v_{2}\rangle=1/\sqrt{2}(|02\rangle-|20\rangle).

(4)

These states are pivotal in quantum information theory, aiding in the exploration of differences between LOCC and separable operations, such as in quantum state discrimination
Cohen 2007
and entanglement transformation
Chitambar and Duan 2009
.

The extent to which state makes
E_{\text{U,PPT}}(\rho_{AB})
positive remains questioned. Such states are crucial to understanding the equilibrium states of quantum many-body systems, which are typically non-degenerate. We consider the case where
\rho_{v}
is affected by the depolarizing noise, resulting in a state
\widehat{\rho}_{v}=(1-p)\rho_{v}+pI/3
, where
p
is the depolarizing rate. Our comparative analysis of the logarithmic fidelity of binegativity and the Rains bound
Rains 2000
;
Audenaert et al. 2002a
in low-noise scenarios reveals a discernible gap between these metrics (see Fig.
3
). This study corroborates previous findings and marks the irreversibility of entanglement for the first time in full-rank entangled states under PPT operations.

Comparison between
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
and the Rains bound for a two-qutrit full-rank state
\widehat{\rho}_{v}
. The
x
-axis represents the depolarizing noise parameter
p
. The red line represents
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\widehat{\rho}_{v})
, serving as a lower bound on the entanglement cost, while the dashed blue line represents the Rains bound, providing an upper bound on the distillable entanglement. The gap between the two bounds for noise parameter
p\in[0,0.015]
indicates the irreversibility of asymptotic entanglement manipulation of
\widehat{\rho}_{v}
.

Dynamical entanglement cost.
—
We further extend our theory of static entanglement to the dynamic realms of quantum channels, recognizing their critical role in quantum communication and error correction
Wilde 2018
. The concept of distillable entanglement for point-to-point channels (quantum channels with single input and output systems) was initially explored in Ref.
Bennett et al. 1996b
, and has evolved through continued research
Devetak and Winter 2005
;
Takeoka et al. 2014
, laying the groundwork for understanding entanglement’s operational utility. Furthermore, the entanglement cost of quantum channels
Berta et al. 2013
, quantifies the minimum ebits required to simulate multiple channel uses in the presence of free classical communication. Followed by Theorem
1
and the result in
(
Berta et al. 2013
, Corollary 17)
, we have the following lower bound on the entanglement cost of a point-to-point quantum channel.

Proposition 2

For a point-to-point quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
, its entanglement cost is lower bounded by

E_{C}\left({\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}\right)\geq\widehat{E}_{N,2}^{1/2}({\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B})\geq E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J_{{\cal N}}),

(5)

where
\widehat{E}_{N,2}^{1/2}({\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B})\coloneqq\max\limits_{\psi_{AA^{\prime}}}E_{N,2}^{1/2}(({\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}\otimes{\cal I}_{A^{\prime}})(\psi_{AA^{\prime}}))
and
J_{{\cal N}}
is the Choi state of
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
.

Proposition
2
provides an efficiently computable lower bound
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J_{{\cal N}})
on the entanglement cost of a point-to-point quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
. Note that a channel is PPT if and only if its Choi state is a PPT state. Thus,
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J_{{\cal N}})
is also a faithful bound for
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
, i.e.,
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J_{{\cal N}})=0
if and only if
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is a PPT channel. This lower bound indicates the relationship between the asymptotic cost of Bell pairs to simulate a quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
and the distance of its Choi state
J_{{\cal N}}
to any sub-state with non-positive logarithmic binegativity. If
J_{{\cal N}}
is far from any of such sub-states, the asymptotic cost of Bell pairs for the simulation of
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
increases.

Recent studies have expanded the entanglement theory to bipartite channels where a variety of measures for dynamic entanglement have significantly enriched the scope of entanglement theory in quantum dynamics
Wang et al. 2018
;
Gour and Winter 2019
;
Gour and Scandolo 2020
;
Gour and Scandolo 2021
. Consider a bipartite quantum channel
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
. There are two main strategies to define the dynamical entanglement cost of such channels, i.e., the parallel and adaptive simulation
Berta et al. 2013
;
Bäuml et al. 2019
;
Wilde 2018
;
Gour and Scandolo 2021
. In the parallel approach, it is defined as the minimal rate
r
to simulate
n\gg 1
simultaneous uses of
{\cal N}
with the LOCC channel by consuming
rn
ebits, up to an error of
\varepsilon
based on the diamond norm
Kitaev 1997
. Formally, the entanglement cost of simulating one use of
{\cal N}
with error
\varepsilon
can be expressed as,

\displaystyle E_{C,\varepsilon}^{(1)}({\cal N})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\log k\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\Big\|{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}-

(6)

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle L}_{AB\bar{A}\bar{B}\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\big(\cdot\otimes\Phi_{\bar{A}\bar{B}}^{+}(2^{k})\big)\Big\|_{\diamond}\leq\varepsilon\Big\},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and the minimization ranges over all LOCC operations
{\cal L}_{AB\bar{A}\bar{B}}
between Alice and Bob. The asymptotic entanglement cost of
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
is then defined as
Gour and Scandolo 2020
,

E_{C}({\cal N})\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}E_{C,\varepsilon}^{(1)}({\cal N}^{\otimes n}).

(7)

This definition builds upon the entanglement cost for point-to-point channels
Berta et al. 2013
by taking subsystems
B
and
A^{\prime}
to be trivially one-dimension.

In our study, we primarily explore the parallel framework for assessing entanglement costs (see adaptive version in
Bäuml et al. 2019
;
Gour and Scandolo 2021
). Our methodology on static entanglement can be naturally generalized to bipartite quantum channels by establishing the relationship between the dynamical entanglement cost of
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
and the static entanglement cost of its Choi state relying on the NPT entanglement theory.

Proposition 3

For a bipartite quantum channel
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
, its entanglement cost is lower bounded by

E_{C}\left({\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\right)\geq E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J^{{\cal N}}_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}),

(8)

where
J^{{\cal N}}_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
is the Choi state of
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
with the bipartite partition
AA^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}BB^{\prime}
.

The proof is based on the fact that
E_{C}({\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\geq E_{C}(J_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}^{{\cal N}})
, where
J^{{\cal N}}_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
is the Choi state of
{\cal N}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
and the bipartite cut is settled between systems
AA^{\prime}
and
BB^{\prime}
for calculating
E_{C}(J_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}^{{\cal N}})
. More details can be found in Appendix
.

Examples.
—
We apply our bounds to different channels of interest. First, we consider the Werner-Holevo channel
{\cal W}_{A\rightarrow B}^{(d)}(\rho)\coloneqq\frac{1}{d-1}(I-\rho^{T})
, which provides a counterexample to the multiplicativity of maximal output purity
Werner and Holevo 2002
and is the dual of the state that disproves the additivity of the relative entropy of entanglement
Vollbrecht and Werner 2001
. The Choi state of
{\cal W}_{A\rightarrow B}^{(d)}
is
J_{{\cal W}}=\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\big(I_{AB}-d(\Phi^{+}_{AB})^{T_{B}}\big)
. Previous lower bounds on the entanglement cost of Werner-Holevo channels rely on the antisymmetric subspace, yielding
E_{C}({\cal W}^{(d)})\geq E_{C}(\alpha_{d})\geq\log(4/3)

Christandl et al. 2012
;
Wilde 2018
. Our bound improves upon this for
d=5
, with
E_{C}(J_{\cal W})\geq E_{N,2}^{1/2}(J_{{\cal W}})>0.4854>\log(4/3)
.

Second, we investigate the entanglement cost in a noisy Hamiltonian simulation scenario, which is crucial for understanding the dynamics of quantum systems
Leimkuhler and Reich 2004
;
Ding et al. 2024
;
Clinton et al. 2021
and has applications in quantum chemistry and material science
Berry et al. 2015
;
Low and Chuang 2017
;
Low and Chuang 2019
. We consider a two-body time-independent Heisenberg-XXZ model with Hamiltonian
H=J_{x}XX+J_{y}YY+J_{z}ZZ
, where
J_{x}=J_{y}=-1/2
and
J_{z}=-1
, and single-qubit thermal damping in the state
|1\rangle
at a rate of
0.1
. We estimate the entanglement cost of implementing
e^{-iHt}
at each small time step using
E_{N,2}^{1/2}
and compare it with other lower bounds. The results are depicted in Fig.
4
. Our bound provides more precise estimations throughout the evolution, exhibiting a wavy-shaped tendency with a maximum at
t\approx 1.57
. This suggests that at least two ebits are required to simulate the evolution operator at any time in the presence of amplitude damping on one of the system qubits.

Concluding remarks.
—
In this study, we have established efficiently computable lower bounds on the entanglement cost of quantum states and channels under PPT operations, which is faithful for the NPT entanglement theory. The key ingredient in our work is the generalized divergence of
k
-negativity, which generalizes the concept of Rains set and logarithmic negativity to a family of sub-state sets and entanglement quantifiers that are also related to
Lami et al. 2025
.
Our work settles an important question in quantum information theory by demonstrating the irreversibility of asymptotic entanglement manipulation for full-rank entangled states. Furthermore, our bounds on the entanglement cost of quantum channels pave the way for quantifying the entanglement needed for tasks such as quantum communication and channel simulation. As future directions, it would be interesting to generalize our results beyond the PPT setting, and to explore the applications of logarithmic
k
-negativity in other resource-theoretic frameworks. The proposed bounds on the entanglement cost may also be useful for the recent studies on computational entanglement theory
Arnon-Friedman et al. 2023
;
Leone et al. 2025
and the complexity of distributed quantum computing
Brenner et al. 2023
;
Piveteau and Sutter 2024
;
Harrow and Lowe 2025
;
Jing et al. 2025
.

I
Notations and preliminaries

Let
{\cal H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space. Consider two parties, Alice and Bob, with associated Hilbert spaces
{\cal H}_{A}
and
{\cal H}_{B}
, respectively, where the dimensions of
{\cal H}_{A}
and
{\cal H}_{B}
are denoted as
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
. We use
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{A})
to represent the set of linear operators on system
A
, and
\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{A})
to represent the set of Hermitian and positive semidefinite operators. A linear operator
\rho_{A}\in\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{A})
is considered a density operator of a quantum state if its trace is equal to one. We denote the set of all density operators on system
A
as
\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{A})
. The maximally entangled state of dimension
d\otimes d
is denoted as
\Phi_{AB}^{+}(d)=1/d\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}|ii\rangle\!\langle jj|
. The trace norm of
\rho
is denoted as
\|\rho\|_{1}=\operatorname{Tr}(\sqrt{\rho^{\dagger}\rho})
.
A bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
is called a PPT state if it admits positive partial transpose, i.e.,
\rho_{AB}^{T_{B}}\geq 0
where
T_{B}
denotes taking partial transpose over the system
B
.
Note that quantum states with non-positive partial transpose are called NPT states, considered resourceful in the NPT entanglement theory
Gour and Scandolo 2021
. A quantum channel
{\cal N}_{A\rightarrow B}
is a linear transformation from
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{A})
to
\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{B})
that is
completely positive and trace preserving
(CPTP).

In the task of entanglement dilution, Alice and Bob share a large supply of Bell pairs and try to convert
rn
Bell pairs to
n
high-fidelity copies of the desired state
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
using suitable operations. The
entanglement cost

E_{C,\Omega}
of a given bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
quantifies the optimal rate
r
of converting
{rn}
Bell pairs to
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
with arbitrarily high fidelity in the limit of large
n
. The concise definition of the entanglement cost using
\Omega
operations is given as follows:

\displaystyle E_{C,\Omega}(\rho_{AB})=\inf\Big\{r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big[\inf_{\Lambda\in\Omega}\big\|\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}-\Lambda(\Phi^{+}_{AB}(2^{rn}))\big\|_{1}\big]=0\Big\},

(S1)

where
\Omega\in\{{\text{1-LOCC}},{\text{LOCC}},{\text{SEP}},{\text{PPT}}\}
and we write
E_{C,{\text{LOCC}}}(\cdot)=E_{C}(\cdot)
for simplification. For the case with an exact transformation rather than the vanishing error, the rate refers to the exact entanglement cost
Audenaert et al. 2003
;
Wang and Wilde 2020
.

For the entanglement cost under LOCC, Hayden, Horodecki, and Terhal
Hayden et al. 2001
proved that

\displaystyle E_{C}(\rho_{AB})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}E_{F}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}).

(S2)

Here
E_{F}(\rho_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\big\{\sum_{i}p_{i}S(\operatorname{Tr}_{A}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|\big\}
is the entanglement of formation
Bennett et al. 1996b
of
\rho_{AB}
where the minimization ranges over all pure state decompositions.
In particular, for any bipartite pure state
{\psi}_{AB}
, it is known that
Bennett et al. 1996a

\displaystyle E_{C}(\psi_{AB})=E_{D}(\psi_{AB})=S(\operatorname{Tr}_{A}\psi_{AB}),

(S3)

where
S(\rho)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)
is the
von Neumann entropy
of a state
\rho
. From Eq. (
S3
) we can see the reversibility in the asymptotic transformation between any pure states.
However, little is known about the entanglement cost of general quantum states due to the computational challenge of mixed states’ entanglement measures. One important lower bound on the entanglement cost of
\rho_{AB}
is the
asymptotic PPT relative entropy of entanglement

Hayashi 2016
,

E_{C}(\rho_{AB})\geq{E_{{C,\text{PPT}}}}(\rho_{AB})\geq{E^{\infty}_{{R,\text{PPT}}}}(\rho_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}{E_{{R,\text{PPT}}}}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}),

(S4)

where
{E_{{R,\text{PPT}}}}(\rho_{AB})
is the
PPT relative entropy of entanglement

Audenaert et al. 2002a
;
Plenio and Virmani 2007
given as follows.

\displaystyle{E_{{R,\text{PPT}}}}(\rho_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)}D(\rho_{AB}\|\sigma_{AB}),

(S5)

where
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)
is the quantum relative entropy.

Entanglement distillation, on the other hand, captures the highest rate at which one can obtain maximally entangled states from less entangled states using
\Omega
operations. It is defined by

E_{D}(\rho_{AB})=\sup\Big\{r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big[\inf_{\Lambda\in\Omega}\big\|\Lambda(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})-\Phi_{AB}^{+}(2^{rn})\big\|_{1}\big]=0\Big\},

(S6)

where
\Omega\in\{{\text{1-LOCC}},{\text{LOCC}},{\text{SEP}},{\text{PPT}}\}
. There are several upper bounds on distillable entanglement
Rains 2000
;
Christandl and Winter 2004
;
Leditzky et al. 2017
;
Wang and Duan 2016
;
Wang and Duan 2017b
;
Fang et al. 2019
;
Kaur et al. 2019
, and the Rains bound
Rains 2000
is arguably the best-known computable upper bound for the distillable entanglement under two-way LOCC. Its regularization could provide a tighter bound due to its non-additivity
Wang and Duan 2017b
.
Specifically, Rains bound is given by
Rains 2000
;
Audenaert et al. 2002a

R(\rho_{AB})=\min_{\tau_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)}D(\rho_{AB}\|\tau_{AB}),

(S7)

where
{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)
is the Rains set defined as

{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)=\big\{\sigma_{AB}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\sigma_{AB}\succeq 0,\big\|\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1\big\}.

(S8)

II
Properties of Rényi-
\alpha
divergence of
k
-negativity

In the same spirit of the relative entropy of entanglement and the Rains bound, we consider the minimum ‘distance’ between a target state and the sub-state set

{\text{PPT${}_{k}$}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\coloneqq\Big\{\omega_{1}\in\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\exists\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=2}^{k},~\mathrm{s.t.}~|\omega_{i}^{T_{B}}|_{*}\preceq\omega_{i+1},\forall i\in[1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k-1],~\big\|\omega_{k}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1\Big\},

(S1)

where
|A|_{*}\preceq B
denotes
-B\preceq A\preceq B
. Hence, the sub-state set
{\text{PPT}}_{k}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)
can be rewritten as

{\text{PPT${}_{k}$}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\coloneqq\Big\{\omega_{1}\in\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{AB})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\exists\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=2}^{k},~\mathrm{s.t.}~-\omega_{i+1}\preceq\omega_{i}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{i+1},\forall i\in[1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k-1],~\big\|\omega_{k}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1\Big\}.

(S2)

Based on the above definition, we can have a hierarchical structure of sub-state sets encompassing the Rains set as the largest boundary set.

Proposition S4

For a bipartite quantum system
AB
and any positive integer
k\geq 2
it holds that

{\text{PPT}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{k}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{2}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\subseteq{\text{PPT}}^{\prime}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B).

(S3)

Proof.

First, for any fixed
j\in[2\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k]
and any sub-state
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{j}
, we have
-\omega_{2}\preceq\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{2},~-\omega_{3}\preceq\omega_{2}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{3},\cdots,-\omega_{j}\preceq\omega_{j-1}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{j},\|\omega_{j}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1
. Since
-\omega_{j}\preceq\omega_{j-1}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{j}
, by Lemma
S5
, we have

\big\|\omega_{j-1}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq\operatorname{Tr}\omega_{j}.

(S4)

Since
\omega_{j}\succeq 0
, we have

\big\|\omega_{j-1}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq\operatorname{Tr}\omega_{j}=\operatorname{Tr}\omega_{j}^{T_{B}}\leq\big\|\omega_{j}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1,

(S5)

which indicates that
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{j-1}
. Hence, we have shown that
{\text{PPT}}_{j}\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{j-1}
. Second, if
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}
, we have
\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}=\omega_{1}\geq 0
. Then we can construct a sequence of
\omega_{j}
as
\omega_{k}=\rho_{AB}
if
k
is odd;
\omega_{k}=\omega_{1}
if
k
is even. Therefore, it follows that
\|\omega_{k}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1
and
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{k}
. Hence, we complete the proof.

Lemma S5

For hermitian matrices
A
and
B
, if
-B\preceq A\preceq B
, then it holds that
\|A\|_{1}\leq\operatorname{Tr}B
.

Proof.

Denote the spectral decomposition of
A
as
A=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{i}|
and
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d}
as an orthonormal basis where
r
and
d
are the rank and dimension of
A
, respectively. Since
-B\preceq A\preceq B
, we have

-\langle\psi|B|\psi\rangle\leq\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle\leq\langle\psi|B|\psi\rangle,~\forall|\psi\rangle\implies|\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle|\leq\langle\psi|B|\psi\rangle,~\forall|\psi\rangle.

(S6)

It follows that

\|A\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big|\langle\psi_{i}|A|\psi_{i}\rangle\big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle\psi_{i}|B|\psi_{i}\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}B,

(S7)

which completes the proof.

Lemma S6

For any positive integer
k\geq 2
, a bipartite quantum state
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{k}
if and only if
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}
.

Proof.

The ‘if’ part directly follows from Proposition
S4
. For the ‘only if’ part, suppose there is a quantum state
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{2}
and
\sigma_{AB}\notin{\text{PPT}}
. Then there exists some
\omega_{AB}\in\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{AB})
such that
-\omega_{AB}\preceq\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{AB}
and
\|\omega_{k}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1
. Using the same argument in Proposition
S4
, we have

1<\|\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq\operatorname{Tr}\omega_{AB}\leq\|\omega_{AB}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1,

(S8)

a contradiction. Hence, we have shown that there is no such a state
\sigma_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}_{2}
and
\sigma_{AB}\notin{\text{PPT}}
. As
{\text{PPT}}_{k}\subseteq{\text{PPT}}_{2}
for any
k\geq 2
, we complete the proof.

Notably,
{\text{PPT${}_{k}$}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)

{\text{PPT${}_{2}$}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)\coloneqq\Big\{\sigma_{AB}\succeq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\exists\omega_{AB}\in\mathscr{L}({\cal H}_{AB}),~\mathrm{s.t.}~-\omega_{AB}\preceq\sigma_{AB}^{T_{B}}\preceq\omega_{AB},~\big\|\omega_{AB}^{T_{B}}\big\|_{1}\leq 1\Big\}.

(S9)

We remark that the entanglement cost lower bound
E_{\eta}(\rho_{AB})

\rho_{AB}

Consider a generalized divergence
Tomamichel 2016
;
Khatri and Wilde 2020

\mathbf{D}(\cdot\|\cdot)
of a state

\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\rho)=0

\rho

\mathbf{D}(\rho\|c\sigma)=\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\sigma)-\log c

\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq\mathbf{D}({\cal N}(\rho)\|{\cal N}(\sigma))

k

of a given bipartite quantum state

One can immediately show that the quantifier is faithful regarding the

(Faithfulness of
E_{N,k}

For a bipartite quantum state

and

if and only if
\rho_{AB}\in{\text{PPT}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{AB}&-I_{AB}/2\\
-I_{AB}/2&R_{AB}\end{array}\right)\succeq 0.

Note that for any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}
such that
0\leq\delta_{1}<\delta_{2},~\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}\leq\frac{1}{d_{A}d_{B}}
and
\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}>\delta_{3}>0
,
\{M_{AB}=\delta_{2}I_{AB},N_{AB}=\delta_{1}I_{AB},\sigma_{AB}=\delta_{3}I_{AB}\}
is strictly feasible for the SDP (
S17
). The Slater condition for strong duality is satisfied. Notice that by rescaling the variables, the dual SDP becomes,

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{AB}\rho_{AB})+t)

(S20)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle U_{AB}\succeq 0,~V_{AB}\succeq 0,~U_{AB}^{T_{B}}-V_{AB}^{T_{B}}\succeq R_{AB},

\displaystyle-tI_{AB}\preceq U_{AB}^{T_{B}}+V_{AB}^{T_{B}}\preceq tI_{AB},

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{AB}&-I_{AB}\\
-I_{AB}&R_{AB}\end{array}\right)\succeq 0.

Notice that the constraint in Eq. (
S20
) will lead to
Q_{AB},R_{AB}\succeq 0
and
R_{AB}\succeq Q_{AB}^{-1}
. It follows
\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]\geq 0
. We also have
t\geq 0
. By the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we have

\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]+\frac{1}{2}t\geq\sqrt{t\cdot\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]},

(S21)

where the equality holds iff
\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]=t
. In fact, if
\{Q_{AB},t\}
is a feasible solution, we can always find a feasible solution
\{\eta Q_{AB},t/\eta\}
where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
is a scaling factor such that
t=\eta^{2}\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]
. Also,
\{Q_{AB},t\}
and
\{\eta Q_{AB},t/\eta\}
will lead to the same value of
\sqrt{t\cdot\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]}
. Hence, after restricting the constraint to
\operatorname{Tr}[Q_{AB}\rho_{AB}]=t
, we have the following equivalent dual problem.

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{AB})=\min

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{AB}\rho_{AB})

(S22)

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle U_{AB}\succeq 0,~V_{AB}\succeq 0,~U_{AB}^{T_{B}}-V_{AB}^{T_{B}}\succeq R_{AB},

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{AB}\rho_{AB})I_{AB}\preceq U_{AB}^{T_{B}}+V_{AB}^{T_{B}}\preceq\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{AB}\rho_{AB})I_{AB},

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{AB}&-I_{AB}\\
-I_{AB}&R_{AB}\end{array}\right)\succeq 0.

One nice property of the logarithmic fidelity of binegativity is its additivity with respect to the tensor product of states.

Lemma S11

(Additivity w.r.t. tensor product)

For any bipartite quantum states
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\mathscr{D}({\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B})
,

E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})=E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{0})+E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{1}).

(S23)

Proof.

The additivity of
E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\cdot)
can be obtained from the multiplicativity of
\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\cdot)
. First, we are going to prove

\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})\leq\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1}).

(S24)

If we assume the optimal solution to the SDP (
S15
) for
\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})
and
\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1})
are
\{Q_{0},R_{0},U_{0},V_{0}\}
and
\{Q_{1},R_{1},U_{1},V_{1}\}
, respectively. Then, let

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=U_{0}\otimes U_{1}+V_{0}\otimes V_{1},

(S25)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=U_{0}\otimes V_{1}+V_{0}\otimes U_{1}.

It follows

\displaystyle(U+V)^{T_{B}}

\displaystyle=(U_{0}+V_{0})^{T_{B}}\otimes(U_{1}+V_{1})^{T_{B}},

(S26)

\displaystyle(U-V)^{T_{B}}

\displaystyle=(U_{0}-V_{0})^{T_{B}}\otimes(U_{1}-V_{1})^{T_{B}}.

Then we have

-\operatorname{Tr}[(Q_{0}\otimes Q_{1})(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})]I\preceq(U+V)^{T_{B}}\preceq\operatorname{Tr}[(Q_{0}\otimes Q_{1})(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})]I,

(S27)

and
(U-V)^{T_{B}}\geq R_{0}\otimes R_{1}
.
Therefore,
\{Q_{0}\otimes Q_{1},R_{0}\otimes R_{1},U,V\}
is a feasible solution to the SDP of
\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})
which implies

\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})\leq\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{0}\rho_{0})\operatorname{Tr}(Q_{1}\rho_{1})=\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1}).

(S28)

Second, we shall show that

\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})\geq\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1}).

If
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}\in{\text{PPT${}_{2}$}}(A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B)
are the optimal solutions in Eq. (
S16
) for
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
, respectively, then there exists
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}\in\mathscr{P}({\cal H}_{AB})
such that

\displaystyle-\omega_{0}\leq\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\leq\omega_{0},~\|\omega_{0}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1,

(S29)

\displaystyle-\omega_{1}\leq\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}\leq\omega_{1},~\|\omega_{1}^{T_{B}}\|_{1}\leq 1.

It follows that

(\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{0})\otimes(\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{1})\geq 0,~(\omega_{0}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}})\otimes(\omega_{1}-\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}})\geq 0,

(S30)

which gives

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\omega_{1}+\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}\geq 0,

(S31)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}\geq 0.

Summing these two inequalities will yield
\sigma_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}\geq-\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}
. Meanwhile, consider that

(\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{0})\otimes(\omega_{1}-\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}})\geq 0,~(\omega_{0}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}})\otimes(\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{1})\geq 0,

(S32)

which gives

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\omega_{1}+\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}-\omega_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}\geq 0,

(S33)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\sigma_{1}^{T_{B}}+\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}-\sigma_{0}^{T_{B}}\otimes\omega_{1}\geq 0.

Summing these two inequalities will yield
\sigma_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}\leq\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}
. Note that
\|\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}\|_{1}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{1}\|\omega_{1}\|_{1}\leq 1
. Therefore, we have
-\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}\leq\sigma_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}\leq\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{1}
which indicates that
\sigma_{0}\otimes\sigma_{1}
is a feasible solution for
\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1}
. Therefore, we have

\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})\geq\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1}).

(S34)

Combining Eq. (
S28
), Eq. (
S34
), we conclude that

\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})=\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{0})\widehat{F}_{N,2}(\rho_{1}),

which implies

E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{1})=E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{0})+E_{N,2}^{1/2}(\rho_{1}).

(S35)

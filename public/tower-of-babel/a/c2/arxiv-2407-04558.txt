Convex roofs witnessing Kirkwood-Dirac nonpositivity
Christopher Langrenez, David R.M. Arvidsson-Shukur, Stephan De Bièvre

Abstract
Given two observables A A and B B , one can associate to every quantum state a Kirkwood-Dirac (KD) quasiprobability distribution. KD distributions are like joint classical probabilities except that they can have negative or nonreal values, which are associated to nonclassical features of the state. In the last decade, KD distributions have come to the forefront as a versatile tool to investigate and construct quantum advantages and nonclassical phenomena. KD distributions are also used to determine quantum-classical boundaries. To do so, one must have witnesses for when a state is KD nonpositive. Previous works have established a relation between the uncertainty of a pure state with respect to the eigenbases of A A and B B and KD positivity. If this support uncertainty is large, the state cannot be KD positive. Here, we construct two witnesses for KD nonpositivity for general mixed states. Our first witness is the convex roof of the support uncertainty; it is not faithful, but it extends to the convex hull of pure KD-positive states the relation between KD positivity and small support uncertainty. Our other witness is the convex roof of the total KD nonpositivity, which provides a faithful witness for the convex hull of the pure KD-positive states. This implies that the convex roof of the total nonpositivity captures the nonpositive nature of the KD distribution at the underlying pure state level.

Quasiprobability distributions play an important role in the development of quantum mechanics and in quantum information processing. \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{x}^{2}&\sigma_{xp}^{2}\\

\sigma_{x}^{2}+\sigma_{p}^{2}\geq 1

In this paper, we address analogous questions for the Kirkwood-Dirac (KD) distribution, the definition of which we first recall.
The definitions of the Wigner function and of the P function are intimately related to the existence of conjugate variables for the systems considered. The KD distributions, on the other hand, form a versatile family of quasiprobability distributions well adapted to discrete variable systems. They are defined as follows
[
35
,
36
,
37

\left(|a_{i}\rangle\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}

\left(|b_{j}\rangle\right)_{j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket}

\mathcal{H}

\rho

\forall(i,j)\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket^{2},Q_{ij}(\rho)=\langle b_{j}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|\rho|b_{j}\rangle.

(3)

One can, but need not, think of the two bases as being eigenbases of two distinct, typically incompatible, observables
A

B

Each KD distribution is a quasiprobability distribution since its values can be nonreal or real and negative. Nevertheless, it satisfies the following properties:

\sum_{j=1}^{d}Q_{ij}(\rho)=\langle a_{i}|\rho|a_{i}\rangle\in\mathbb{R},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}Q_{ij}(\rho)=\langle b_{j}|\rho|b_{j}\rangle\in\mathbb{R},\quad\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}(\rho)=\mathrm{Tr}\,(\rho)=1.

(4)

Thus, the KD distributions’ marginals correspond to the probabilities of the Born rule when a measurement is made in one or the other of the two bases. In this sense, a KD distribution is akin to a joint probability distribution for the observables
A
and
B

m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}:=\min_{i,j}|\langle a_{i}|b_{j}\rangle|>0

\rho

37

It has been shown in various settings such as direct state tomography, quantum metrology, weak measurements, quantum thermodynamics, quantum scrambling, Leggett-Garg inequalities, and generalised contextuality that nonclassical behaviour of a state
\rho
is associated with the presence of negative or nonreal values in its KD-distribution. (See

37

The situation for KD distributions is considerably more complex. This is because the identification of the pure KD-positive states depends strongly on the two bases

One can phrase the indeterminacy of a pure state
|\psi\rangle
with respect to measurements in the two bases
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
in terms of a quantity called the support uncertainty
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
[see Eq. (
16
)]. It was then shown in
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
that, for pure states, a high support uncertainty implies KD-nonpositivity. The precise statement of these results is recalled in Section
2.3
. While this criterion provides a witness of KD-nonpositivity for pure states, it fails for mixed states, as explained in Section
3
.
In fact, as we shall show, any finite level of depolarisation of a pure KD-positive state renders the aforementioned nonpositivity criterion invalid (Lemma
3.1
). Given that all experiments necessarily include some diversion from pure states, a generalisation of the previous results on pure states to mixed states is necessary for both foundational purposes and for practical applications of the KD distribution.

We show, in Section
3
(Theorem
3.3
), that the convex roof of the support uncertainty, that we denote by
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
, provides a necessary condition for a state to be a mixture of pure KD-positive states:

\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)\Rightarrow n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leq d+1.

(8)

Convex-roof constructions have proven their efficiency as conceptual tools in other similar problems, such as the construction of witnesses and monotones of entanglement (entanglement of formation), of genuine nongaussianity
[
46
]
and of continuous variable P nonpositivity for mixed states
[
23
,
24
]
. We recall that, if a function
s
is defined on the extreme points
K_{\textrm{ext}}
of a convex set
K
, then its convex roof
s^{\textsc{cr}}
is the largest convex function defined on the full convex set
K
, and that coincides with
s
on its extreme points. We recall in Appendix
A
the basic properties of convex roofs needed in the body of the paper.

A natural question then arises: Is it true that

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}\Rightarrow n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leq d+1?

(9)

In other words, do all KD-positive states have low support uncertainty, or, equivalently, is
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
a witness for nonpositivity?
We answer this question in the negative by exhibiting, for a spin-
1
system, a state
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
for which
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>4
. In other words, even for this completely incompatible system (definition in Section
2.2
), not all KD-positive states have minimal support uncertainty.

We further note that, whereas the implication in Eq. (
8
) shows that mixtures of KD-positive pure states have low support uncertainty, the converse implication is not true, as explained in more detail in Section
2.3
. As a result,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
is not a faithful witness of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
.

We show in Section
4
that a faithful witness of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
is provided by the convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
of the total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)

[
47
]
, which is
defined as

{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}|Q_{ij}(\rho)|\geq 1.

(10)

The total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
is completely analogous to the Wigner negative volume, introduced in
[
14
]
and often used as a witness of Wigner negativity.
The total KD nonpositivity is well known to be a faithful witness of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
since
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)>1
if and only if
\rho
is not KD positive and
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=1
otherwise:

{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=1\ \Leftrightarrow\ \rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}.

(11)

The total KD nonpositivity is often thought of as measuring the amount by which the KD distribution of
\rho
fails to be a genuine probability. This viewpoint explains why several authors think of
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
as a measure of nonclassicality. As a result, they refer to
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
as the total KD nonclassicality of
\rho
. As discussed above, we prefer to use the term total KD nonpositivity.

In Section
4
, we will show that the convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
of
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
is a faithful witness of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
. From this, we will infer that, in fact, for states
\rho
belonging to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
but not to
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
,
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
underestimates the nonpositive nature of the state
\rho
and that its convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
is better suited for this purpose.

The geometry of the KD-positive states

2.2
Complete incompatibility

The condition
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
, in Theorem
2.1
, can be interpreted as a weak form of incompatibility for the measurements associated with
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
. It implies that, if a measurement in the
{\mathcal{A}}
basis is made first, then a subsequent measurement in the
{\mathcal{B}}
basis can, with nonvanishing probability, yield any possible outcome. A significantly stronger notion of
complete incompatibility
of two bases is introduced in
[
41
]
. Complete incompatibility occurs in scenarios where, after a first (coarse- or fine-grained) measurement in the
{\mathcal{A}}
basis is made, a subsequent measurement in the
{\mathcal{B}}
basis is sure to perturb the outcome of the first measurement. In mathematical terms, complete incompatibility is equivalent to the requirement that all minors of the transition matrix
U
between the two bases are nonvanishing; we will say that such a unitary matrix, and its underlying bases, are completely incompatible. This implies a strong form of incompatibility: for all
S,T\subseteq\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket
, with
1\leq|S|,|T|<d
here
|X|
denotes the cardinality of
X
), one has that
[{\Pi_{{\mathcal{A}}}}(S),{\Pi_{{\mathcal{B}}}}(T)]\not=0
,
where the projectors
{\Pi_{{\mathcal{A}}}}(S),{\Pi_{{\mathcal{B}}}}(T)
are defined as

{\Pi_{{\mathcal{A}}}}(S)=\sum_{i\in S}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|,\quad{\Pi_{{\mathcal{B}}}}(T)=\sum_{j\in T}|b_{j}\rangle\langle b_{j}|.

Complete incompatibility has been introduced and studied in
[
41
,
42
,
48
]
. When the Hilbert space dimension is
2
or
3
, two bases are completely incompatible if and only if
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
. This is not true in higher dimensions. Nevertheless, one then has the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

In any finite dimension, there exists a dense, open, probability
1
set of transition matrices that are completely incompatible.

The “open and dense” part of this result was shown in
[
42
]
. The “probability one” part is an immediate result of Proposition 2.4 in
[
45
]
, which states that the zero set of a polynomial in the matrix elements of
U
form a set of vanishing Haar measure in the unitary group. Since complete incompatibility requires all minors of
U
to be nonvanishing, the result then follows immediately.

Let us finally point out that two bases whose transition matrix coincides with the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) are completely incompatible if and only if the Hilbert space dimension is prime
[
42
]
.

2.3
Support uncertainty as a nonpositivity witness: pure states

Given a pure state
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
, one defines
n_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)
[respectively
n_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)
] to be the number of nonvanishing components of
|\psi\rangle
on
{\mathcal{A}}
[respectively
{\mathcal{B}}
]:

\displaystyle n_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)=\left|\{i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\mid\langle a_{i}|\psi\rangle\not=0\}\right|,

(14)

\displaystyle n_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)=\left|\{j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\mid\langle b_{j}|\psi\rangle\not=0\}\right|.

(15)

It was proven in
[
41
]
that, if
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
, then

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi):=n_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\psi)+n_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)>d+1

(16)

implies that
|\psi\rangle
is not KD positive. Equivalently, one has

|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\ \Rightarrow\ n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)\leq d+1.

(17)

The
support uncertainty

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)
therefore provides a KD nonpositivity witness for pure states. However,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)
is not a faithful witness: there may exist states
|\psi\rangle
for which
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)\leq d+1
that are not KD positive. Examples of bases
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
for which this occurs are provided in
[
41
,
42
]
. The link between positivity and uncertainty is therefore not as tight for the KD distribution as for the Wigner distribution and the P function. Indeed, for the Wigner distribution,
\det\gamma
[Eq. (
1
)] provides a faithful Wigner nonpositivity witness for pure states and, for the P function,
\Delta X^{2}+\Delta P^{2}
provides a faithful P nonpositivity witness.

Nevertheless, when the bases
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible, more can be said. First, in this case, for all pure states
|\psi\rangle

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)\geq d+1.

(18)

Hence, for all pure KD-positive states,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)=d+1

[
41
]
. This is somewhat analogous to what happens with the Wigner or P functions for systems with two conjugate variables, which are positive for pure states if and only if the state is has minimal uncertainty in the sense explained above. The difference is that, with the KD distribution, even if
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible, it is still possible for states with minimal support uncertainty to be KD nonpositive. In other words,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
is not a faithful KD nonpositivity witness, even for completely incompatible bases.

3
Support uncertainty as a nonpositivity witness: mixed states

Our main result in this section is Theorem
3.3
, that extends to mixed states the relation between KD positivity and support uncertainty shown in
[
41
,
42
]
for pure states and recalled in the previous section. Before doing so, we point out that a naive extension of the notion of support uncertainty to mixed states
\rho
is not satisfactory. Let us define

n_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\rho)=|\{i\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\mid\langle a_{i}|\rho|a_{i}\rangle\not=0\}|,\quad n_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=|\{j\in\llbracket 1,d\rrbracket\mid|\langle b_{j}|\rho|b_{j}\rangle\not=0\}|

and

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho):=n_{{\mathcal{A}}}(\rho)+n_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)\leq 2d.

We observe that, if
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
, and
\rho=\frac{1}{d}{I}_{d}
, then
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=2d>d+1
. Since
\rho=\frac{1}{d}{I}_{d}
belongs to
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, it becomes clear that, if we define
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)
to be the support uncertainty of
\rho
, then Eq. (
17
) does not extend to mixed KD-positive states. In fact, the following lemma generalizes this observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
. Let
\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
. Suppose
\rho
is written in the form

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{j}|b_{j}\rangle\langle b_{j}|,

(19)

with
0\leq\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}\leq 1
.
Then the following statements are true:

(i) If there exist
i,j
for which
\lambda_{i}\not=0\not=\mu_{j}
, then
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=2d
;

(ii) If
\mu_{j}=0
for all
j
, then

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=d+\left|\{i|\lambda_{i}\not=0\}\right|;

(iii) If
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i
, then

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=d+\left|\{j|\mu_{j}\not=0\}\right|.

In particular, if
\rho
belongs to the interior of the convex polytope
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
, then
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)=2d
.

Proof.

(i) Suppose, without loss of generality, that
\lambda_{1}\not=0\not=\mu_{1}
. Then, for any
i,j

\displaystyle\langle a_{i}|\rho|a_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}|\langle a_{i}|a_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\mu_{1}|\langle a_{i}|b_{1}\rangle|^{2}>0,

\displaystyle\langle b_{j}|\rho|b_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}|\langle b_{j}|a_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\mu_{1}|\langle b_{j}|b_{1}\rangle|^{2}>0.

Statements (ii) & (iii) follow from a direct computation using that
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
.
To prove the last statement, we note that it was proven in
[
39
]
(Appendix A) that
\rho
is in the interior of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
if and only if it can be written in the form Eq. (
19
) with

\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{d}\not=0\ \textrm{or}\ \mu_{1}\mu_{2}\dots\mu_{d}\not=0.

(20)

If
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{d}\not=0
and
\mu_{j}\not=0
for at least one value of
j
, then the conclusion follows from (i). If, on the other hand all
\mu_{j}=0
, then it follows from (ii).
∎

The Lemma implies that the minimal value of
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
on the polytope
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)\subset\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
is
d+1
and that this value is reached only on its vertices. In addition, the global maximum of
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
is reached on each point of the interior of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
. On the facets of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
one finds all intermediate integer values. Since all states in
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
are KD positive, this amply demonstrates that there is no link between a small value of
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\rho)
and KD positivity of
\rho
.

To remedy this situation, we provide below a proper definition of the “support uncertainty” of a mixed state using a convex roof construction. This will allow us to show that the results of
[
41
,
42
]
linking KD positivity and support uncertainty extend naturally to mixed states as well.
Our work relies on some technical properties of convex roofs that are provided in Appendix
A
.

For any state
\rho
, we define
D_{\rho}
to be the set of all possible convex decompositions of
\rho
on pure states,
i.e.

D_{\rho}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left\{(\lambda_{i},|\varphi_{i}\rangle)_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\in([0,1]\times\mathcal{H}_{1})^{n}\left|\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\lambda_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|=\rho\right.\right\},

with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\left\{|\psi\rangle,\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1\right\}
denoting the unit sphere in
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\rho
be a density matrix. Then we define the support uncertainty
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
of
\rho
as

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=\inf_{(\lambda_{i},|\varphi_{i}\rangle)_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\in D_{\rho}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\varphi_{i}).

By Lemma
A.4
, the infimum is in fact a minimum. In other words, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
(\lambda_{i},|\varphi_{i}\rangle)_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}
such that
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|
and

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}n_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\varphi_{i}).

We then have the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
. If
\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, then
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leqslant d+1
. If
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible, then
\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
implies that
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=d+1
.

One way to interpret this result is to say that
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
is a witness for
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
: if
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>d+1
, then
\rho\not\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
.
In addition, when the bases
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
reaches its minimum (
d+1
), on the set
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
. In other words, all mixtures of pure KD-positive states then have minimal support uncertainty.

Proof.

As
m_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}>0
, if
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}
, then, by Eq. (
17
),
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)\leqslant d+1
. Now, if
\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|

with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, and
|\psi_{i}\rangle\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. Thus, by definition,

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi_{i})\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(d+1)\leqslant d+1.

This concludes the first part of the proof. Now, if
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible, it follows from Eq. (
18
) that, for all pure
|\psi\rangle
,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)\geqslant d+1
. Thus, if

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|

with
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, and
|\psi_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, it follows that:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi_{i})\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(d+1)=d+1.

As the right hand side is independent of the decomposition, we conclude that:

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\geqslant d+1.

This inequality, together with the first part of the proof, implies that, if
\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, then
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=d+1
when
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are completely incompatible.
∎

In view of this result, the question arises naturally whether
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
is a witness for all KD-positive states, and not only for
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
. In other words, the question is whether the implication

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}\ \Rightarrow\ \{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leq d+1\}

(21)

is true or not for any choice of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
.

To answer this question, we first recall that, according to the third part of Theorem
2.1
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}=\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\right)
holds in any dimension for an open dense set of unitary transition matrices
U_{ij}=\langle a_{i}|b_{j}\rangle
of full Haar measure in the unitary group. In those cases,
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}={\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}
, so that the implication in Eq. (
21
) is satisfied as as consequence of Theorem
3.3
and therefore
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
is a witness for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
with probability
1
in any dimension
d
.
The question is therefore if the implication in Eq. (
21
) holds for all choices of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
. We show that this is not the case by further examining the example of a spin-
1
system in dimension
3
for which it was shown in
[
39
]
that Eq. (
13
) holds for an appropriate choice of two spin observables. We explicitly exhibit, in Appendix
B
, a
d=3
state
\rho
in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
for which
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>4
.

4
The total KD nonpositivity roof

We have seen, in Eq. (
11
), that the total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}
is a faithful KD nonpositivity witness.
We now briefly show, using the results of Appendix
A
, that the convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}
of the nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}
is a faithful witness of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
.

We define
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}
as the convex roof of
{\mathcal{N}}
:

\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=\inf_{(\lambda_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\in D_{\rho}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\mathcal{N}(\varphi_{i}).

(22)

Since
{\mathcal{N}}
is convex, one has for any
\rho
,

{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho).

The total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}
is a continuous function that has
1
as its minimal value. For pure states,
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=1
if and only if
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}
. It thus follows from Proposition
A.3
that

\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)=1\Leftrightarrow\rho\in\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right).

(23)

In other words, whereas
{\mathcal{N}}
is a faithful witness for
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}
is a faithful witness for
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
.
In particular,

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}\setminus\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)\Rightarrow{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=1,\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>1.

(24)

That is, if
\rho
is KD positive but cannot be written as a convex combination of pure KD-positive states, then the convex roof of the total KD nonpositivity is strictly greater than one. Examples of such states are given in Appendix
B
.

Our results invite the following comparison with entanglement theory. Consider a bi-partite state
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
. Its von Neumann entropy is

S\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right)=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^{\textrm{A}}\ln\rho^{\textrm{A}}=-\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^{\textrm{B}}\ln\rho^{\textrm{B}},

(25)

where
\rho^{\textrm{A}}=\mathrm{Tr}\,^{\textrm{B}}\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right)
is the partial trace of
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
over the
B
-subsystem, etc.
S\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right)
is a concave function of
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
. If
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
is pure,
S\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right)
quantifies its entanglement. However, if
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
is mixed,
S(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}})
can be large although there is no entanglement.

The entanglement of formation
E_{\textrm{f}}(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}})
was constructed to quantify entanglement of bi-partite mixed states.
E_{\textrm{f}}(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}})
is defined as the convex roof, over pure states, of the von Neumann entropy:

E_{\textrm{f}}(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}):=\inf_{(\lambda_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\in D_{\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}S\left(\varphi_{i}^{\textrm{A,B}}\right).

(26)

The concavity of the von Neumann entropy implies that

S\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right)\geqslant E_{\textrm{f}}\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right):=S^{\textsc{cr}}\left(\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}\right).

(27)

For example, if
\rho^{\textrm{A,B}}
is the maximally mixed state, then its von Neumann entropy equals
\ln d>0
, while its entanglement of formation vanishes. In this sense, the von Neumann entropy overestimates the entanglement of the state.

The situation is the converse for the total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
, which is a convex function of its argument. Its convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
satisfies

{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho).

(28)

The total nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
can therefore underestimate
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
.
In fact, any state
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}\setminus\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
satisfies

1={\mathcal{N}}(\rho)<{\mathcal{N}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho).

Note that such states cannot be written as convex mixtures of pure KD-positive states: any such mixture must contain at least one pure state that is not KD positive. (See Fig.
1
; examples are given in Ref.
[
39
]
.) This feature of the state
\rho
is not picked up by its total nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)
, but it is by its convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)
.

5
Conclusion and discussion

Convex roofs are notoriously hard to compute and therefore criticized for being of limited practical value. Nevertheless, our results illustrate again their power as conceptual tools in quantum theory. We showed in particular that the convex roof of a quantum state’s support uncertainty
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
provides a witness—be it a nonfaithful one—for the complement of the set
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
. Thus,
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
links KD-positivity to an uncertainty principle: mixtures of pure KD-positive states have low support uncertainty.

The convex roof
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}
of the total KD nonpositivity
{\mathcal{N}}
, on the other hand, is a faithful witness of the set
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
. Since
{\mathcal{N}}
itself is a faithful witness of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
(the set of
all
KD-positive states) these two witnesses together allow one to distinguish
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
from the potentially larger set
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
. In particular, for a KD-positive state
\rho
that is not a mixture of KD-positive pure states,
{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=1
, while
\mathcal{N}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>1
.
These results provide a first insight in the physical interpretation of such states. Note that, in a resource theory of KD-nonpositivity, they would not be considered “free” states: only states in
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
would be.

We point out that the problem of distinguishing
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
from
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
has an analog in the context of the Wigner function. There, the set of states obtained as convex
combinations of pure Gaussian states is the analog of
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, whereas the larger set of all Wigner-positive states is the analog of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
. The identification of these sets through witnesses or
monotones has attracted considerable attention in the last decade in the context of degaussification
[
46
,
32
,
49
,
50
]
. This is because, while convex combinations of Gaussian states are not Gaussian, they are obtained
by a mixing operation which is considered classical or, in the context of resource theories, “free”. We are hopeful that the tools to distinguish
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
from
\mathrm{conv}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\right)
, presented in this article, will aid the construction of analogous tools for the Wigner function.

Let

designates the set of extreme points of

is bounded below. In what follows, we will say

The convex roof

Theorem A.1

(Krein-Milman)
.

In a finite-dimensional vector space, a convex compact set is the convex hull of its extreme points:
K=\mathrm{conv}\left({K_{\mathrm{ext}}}\right)
.

Note that

. It remains to show the other inclusion.
We want to prove that
\mathcal{M}_{s^{\textsc{cr}}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{M}_{s}\right)}
. Thus, let x be in
\mathcal{M}_{s^{\textsc{cr}}}
so that
s^{\textsc{cr}}(x)=0
. By the definition of
s^{\textsc{cr}}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\exists(\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon},x_{i}^{\varepsilon})_{i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket}\in([0,1]\times K_{ext})^{N_{\varepsilon}},\ x=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}x_{i}^{\varepsilon},\ 0=s^{\textsc{cr}}(x)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}s(x_{i}^{\varepsilon})\leqslant\varepsilon

and
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}=1
. Then,

\varepsilon\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}s(x_{i}^{\varepsilon})\geqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}s(x_{i}^{\varepsilon})\geqslant m^{+}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

And so,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{m^{+}}.

(32)

We define

x^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{P_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\text{ where }P_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

(33)

Thus, for all
\varepsilon>0
,
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{M}_{s}\right)
and:

\left|\left|x-x^{\varepsilon}\right|\right|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\left|1-\frac{1}{P_{\varepsilon}}\right|\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\left|\left|x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\right|\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\left|\left|x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\right|\right|\leqslant\left|1-\frac{1}{P_{\varepsilon}}\right|A\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}+A\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}

where A is an upper bound of the norm of the elements of
K_{ext}
which exists because
K
is compact, hence bounded. Finally, we obtain

\left|\left|x-x^{\varepsilon}\right|\right|\leqslant\left|P_{\varepsilon}-1\right|A+A\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant(1-P_{\varepsilon})A+A\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant 2A\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{2A}{m^{+}}\varepsilon

as
P_{\varepsilon}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\llbracket 1,N_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket\\
x_{i}^{\varepsilon}\notin\mathcal{M}_{s}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon}=1
by convex combination and thanks to the bound we had in (
32
). So
x^{\varepsilon}\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}x
which shows that
x\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{M}_{s}\right)}
and concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
A counterexample to (
21
) in a spin-
1
system

In
[
39
]
, an example of a spin-
1
system is given for which Eq. (
13
) holds:

{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}\subsetneq{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{pure}}\subsetneq{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}^{\mathrm{ext}}.

For this spin-
1
system, the minimum of
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}
equals
d+1=4
. We show here that, in that case, the implication in (
21
), which now reads

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}\ \Rightarrow\ \{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)\leq d+1=\min n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}\}

is violated. For that purpose, we give an explicit example of a state
\rho
that belongs to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{KD+}}
but for which
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho)>d+1=4
.

First, we need to explicitly identify those pure states that have minimal support uncertainty
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
.

B.1
Identification of the pure states in
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}

In
[
39
]
, we considered a spin-
1
system taking
{\mathcal{A}}
to be the
S_{z}=e_{z}\cdot\vec{J}
basis and
{\mathcal{B}}
the
S_{z^{\prime}}=e_{z^{\prime}}\cdot\vec{J}
basis,
where
e_{z^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{3}(2,2,-1)^{T}
.
The transition matrix between these two bases is

U=\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&2\\
2&-1&2\\
2&2&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Since this system is completely incompatible, it is known
[
41
,
42
]
that the pure minimal uncertainty states
|\psi\rangle
satisfy
n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}(\psi)=d+1=4
. Using this information, explicit computations permit to identify these states to be the basis states
{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}
and, in addition, 9 other states:

|\phi_{1}\rangle=\frac{|a_{2}\rangle-|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},\ |\phi_{2}\rangle=\frac{|a_{1}\rangle-|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},|\phi_{3}\rangle=\frac{|a_{1}\rangle-|a_{2}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

|\psi_{1}\rangle=\frac{|a_{1}\rangle+2|a_{2}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}},\ |\psi_{2}\rangle=\frac{2|a_{1}\rangle+|a_{2}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}},\ |\psi_{3}\rangle=\frac{|a_{1}\rangle+2|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}},

|\psi_{4}\rangle=\frac{2|a_{1}\rangle+|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}},\ |\psi_{5}\rangle=\frac{|a_{2}\rangle+2|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}}\ \text{ and }|\psi_{6}\rangle=\frac{2|a_{2}\rangle+|a_{3}\rangle}{\sqrt{5}}.

These
15
pure states form the set
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}}
and, according to Lemma
A.4
, they are the extreme points of
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
. It was further shown in
[
39
]
that all pure KD-positive states are given by
|\phi_{1}\rangle
,
|\phi_{2}\rangle
,
|\phi_{3}\rangle
and the basis states
{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathcal{B}}
. In particular, it follows that not all minimal support uncertainty pure states are KD-positive.

We will prove in the next subsection that there exist mixed KD-positive states that do not have minimal uncertainty

B.2
Identifying a KD-positive state not having minimal uncertainty.

As
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}=\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}}\right)
and as there is a finite number of elements in
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}}
, we can numerically exhaust all possible hyperplanes containing states in
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}}
and determine which ones are bounding hyperplanes of
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
. We find that there are
28
bounding hyperplanes for
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
. However, we will study one particular bounding plane that is determined as follows. Consider the self-adjoint operator
F_{\star}
:

F_{\star}=\frac{1}{12}\begin{pmatrix}2&-2&-3\\
-2&6&-1\\
-3&-1&4\end{pmatrix}.

(34)

Note that
F_{\star}
has trace
1
. Moreover, this operator determines a bounding plan for the convex set
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
since any pure state
|\psi\rangle
with minimal support uncertainty satisfies the following inequality:

\langle\psi|F_{\star}|\psi\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\star}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

(35)

Thus, for any state
\rho
in
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}=\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}}\right)
, we have that

\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\star}\rho)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

(36)

This inequality is saturated when
|\psi\rangle
is equal to one of
|a_{2}\rangle,|b_{1}\rangle,|\phi_{1}\rangle,|\phi_{2}\rangle
or
|\phi_{3}\rangle
.
As a result, the affine hyperplane
\left\{\mathrm{Tr}\,(\rho F_{\star})=\frac{1}{2}\right\}
is a bounding hyperplane for
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
.
Note that

\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\star}^{2})=\frac{7}{12}>\frac{1}{2}.

(37)

One easily establishes, through an explict computation, that
Q(F_{\star})
only has real nonnegative entries, so that
F_{\star}
is KD positive.

Now, we consider the following family of operators, for
\lambda\in[0,1]
:

\rho_{\lambda}=\lambda F_{\star}+\frac{(1-\lambda)}{3}(|a_{2}\rangle\langle a_{2}|+|b_{1}\rangle\langle b_{1}|+|\phi_{3}\rangle\langle\phi_{3}|).

(38)

For any
\lambda\in[0,1],

\rho_{\lambda}
is a KD-positive operator as a convex combination of KD-positive operators and it has trace
1
. For
\rho_{\lambda}
to be a state, we need to check if it is a positive operator.
We can compute the eigenvalues of
\rho_{\lambda}
:

\displaystyle r_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{18}\left(7\lambda+2\right),

\displaystyle r_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{36}\left(\sqrt{7\lambda^{2}-32\lambda+160}-7\lambda+16\right),\

\displaystyle r_{3}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{36}\left(-\sqrt{7\lambda^{2}-32\lambda+160}-7\lambda+16\right).

(39)

These eigenvalues are all real and nonnegative if and only if

-7\lambda+16\geqslant\sqrt{7\lambda^{2}-32\lambda+160}.

This inequality is satisfied for
\lambda\in[0,\frac{4}{7}]
implying that for these values
\rho_{\lambda}
is a KD-positive state. Moreover, we have that

\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\star}\rho_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{12}.

Consequently, for
\lambda\in]0,\frac{4}{7}]
,
\rho_{\lambda}
is a KD-positive state satisfying
\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\star}\rho_{\lambda})>\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, for this range of
\lambda
values,
\rho_{\lambda}
does not belong to
\mathcal{M}_{n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}}
, meaning that
\rho_{\lambda}
satisfies

n_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}^{\textsc{cr}}(\rho_{\lambda})>4=d+1.

Thus, we have an example in dimension
3
for which there exist mixed KD-positive states that do not have minimal support uncertainty, meaning that in this case, (
21
) does not hold.

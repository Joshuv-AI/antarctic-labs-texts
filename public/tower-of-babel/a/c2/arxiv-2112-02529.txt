On entire functions of several variables
with derivatives of even order taking integer values

Abstract




Journal of Ramanujan Society of Mathematics and Mathematical Sciences (JRSMAMS)

Vol. 9, No. 1, December 2021

Abstract

We extend to several variables an earlier result of ours, according to which an entire function of one variable of sufficiently small exponential type, having all derivatives of even order taking integer values at two points, is a polynomial. The proof in the one dimensional case relies on Lidstone expansion of the function. For
n
variables, we need
n+1
points, having the property that the differences of
n
of them with the remaining one give a basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The proof is by reduction to the one variable situation.

Keywords

Integer valued entire functions, Lidstone polynomials, exponential type, Pólya’s Theorem, analytic functions of several variables.

The main result

We denote by
{\mathbb{N}}
the set
\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
For
{\underline{z}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
{\underline{t}}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, write

{\underline{z}}^{\underline{t}}=z_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{t_{n}},\quad|{\underline{z}}|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|,\quad\|{\underline{t}}\|=t_{1}+\cdots+t_{n},\quad{\underline{t}}!=t_{1}!\cdots t_{n}!

and

D^{\underline{t}}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{t_{1}}\cdots\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}\right)^{t_{n}}.

For
{\underline{z}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
and
{\underline{w}}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, let
{\underline{w}}{\underline{z}}=w_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+w_{n}z_{n}
.
For
r>0
and for
f
an analytic function in a domain containing
\{{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\;\mid\;|{\underline{z}}|\leq r\}
, set

|f|_{r}=\sup_{|{\underline{z}}|=r}|f({\underline{z}})|.

The order of an entire function
f
is

\varrho(f)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log|f|_{r}}{\log r}

and its exponential type

\tau(f)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log|f|_{r}}{r}\cdotp

Given
\tau>0
,
{\underline{w}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and an entire function
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, we say that
f
has
exponential type
\leq\tau
in the direction
{\underline{w}}
if the function of a single variable
{\underline{z}}\mapsto f({\underline{w}}{\underline{z}})
has order
\leq 1
and exponential type
\leq\tau
, namely

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\log\sup_{|{\underline{z}}|\leq r}|f({\underline{w}}{\underline{z}})|\leq\tau.

It depends not only on
{\underline{w}}/|{\underline{w}}|
but also on
|{\underline{w}}|
.

Let
{\underline{s}}_{0},{\underline{s}}_{1},\dots,{\underline{s}}_{n}
be
n+1
elements in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, such that
\{{\underline{s}}_{1}-{\underline{s}}_{0},\dots,{\underline{s}}_{n}-{\underline{s}}_{0}\}
is a basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Finally, let
{\mathcal{T}}
be the set of
({\underline{t}},i)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\times\{0,\dots,,n\}
satisfying

\|{\underline{t}}\|\text{ even for all $i=0,1,\dots,n$ and }\lx@nobreakspace t_{1},\ldots,t_{i}\text{ even for }i=1,\dots,n.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f
be an entire function of
n
variables having exponential type
\leq\tau
in each of the directions
{\underline{s}}_{i}-{\underline{s}}_{0}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) satisfying

\limsup_{r\to\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-r}\sqrt{r}|f|_{r}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\max\{|s_{0}|,\dots,|s_{n}|\}}.

(1)

Assume

(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{s}}_{i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ for all $({\underline{t}},i)\in{\mathcal{T}}$.}

(2)

Then the set of
({\underline{t}},i)\in{\mathcal{T}}
with
(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{s}}_{i})\not=0
is finite.
Further, if

\tau<\pi,

(3)

then

is a polynomial in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\underline{z}}]
.

The case

of Theorem
1
is Corollary 1.2 of
[
4
]
.

We will see (Corollary
2

is

.

Assumption (
1
) implies that

has exponential type
\leq 1
. Hence, when

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|{\underline{s}}_{i}-{\underline{s}}_{0}|<\pi,

the hypothesis (

Theorem

cannot be improved in general. Here are some examples. Assume

{\underline{s}}_{0}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}),\;{\underline{s}}_{i}={\underline{s}}_{0}+(b_{i}-a_{i}){\underline{e}}_{i}\quad(i=1,\dots,n),

where

and

, while

is the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Our first example is the function

\sin\left(\pi\frac{z_{1}-a_{1}}{b_{1}-a_{1}}+\cdots+\pi\frac{z_{n}-a_{n}}{b_{n}-a_{n}}\right),

which has exponential type
\leq\pi
in each of the directions
{\underline{s}}_{i}-{\underline{s}}_{0}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) and satisfies
(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{s}}_{i})=0
for

and for any
{\underline{t}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with

even.

For our second example, we define

{\underline{\theta}}({\underline{z}})=\left(\frac{z_{1}-a_{1}}{b_{1}-a_{1}},\dots,\frac{z_{n}-a_{n}}{b_{n}-a_{n}}\right).

For

, let

be a polynomial in

be a polynomial in a single variable with complex coefficients.
Consider the entire function of

\varphi({\underline{w}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sin(\pi w_{i})g_{i}(w_{i+1}^{2},\dots,w_{n}^{2})+\sin(\pi w_{n})g_{n}(w_{n-1}^{2}).

Then the function

f({\underline{z}})=\varphi\circ{\underline{\theta}}({\underline{z}})

\leq\pi
in each of the directions
{\underline{s}}_{i}-{\underline{s}}_{0}
(

) and satisfies
(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{s}}_{i})=0
for all

.

For our third example, we keep the same notation for
{\underline{s}}_{i}
and
{\underline{\theta}}
, we assume that

for

, we also assume that the polynomials

have integer coefficients and we set

\psi({\underline{w}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\sinh(w_{i}-b_{i})}{\sinh(a_{i}-b_{i})}g_{i}(w_{i+1}^{2},\dots,w_{n}^{2})+\frac{\sinh(w_{n}-b_{n})}{\sinh(a_{n}-b_{n})}g_{n}(w_{n-1}^{2}).

Then the function

f({\underline{z}})=\psi\circ{\underline{\theta}}({\underline{z}})

has exponential type
\leq 1
in each of the directions
{\underline{s}}_{i}-{\underline{s}}_{0}
(

) and satisfies
(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{s}}_{i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
({\underline{t}},i)\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

Another reason for which Theorem

is optimal is the following. If we relax the assumption (

) by requiring that it holds outside a finite subset of

2.
An extension of a result due to Pólya

The proof of the first part of Theorem
1
rests on Proposition
1
, which is an extension to several variables of Proposition 2.2 of
[
4
]
.

We will use Cauchy’s inequalities for an analytic function of several variables (
[
2
, Theorem 2.2.7 p. 27]
).
Let
r>0
, let
{\underline{t}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with
\|{\underline{t}}\|=T
and let
f
be analytic in a domain containing
\{{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\;\mid\;|{\underline{z}}|\leq r\}
. Then

\frac{|(D^{\underline{t}}f)(0)|}{{\underline{t}}!}r^{T}\leq|f|_{r}.

We deduce that for
{\underline{z}}_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and for
f
analytic in a domain containing

\{{\underline{z}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\;\mid\;|{\underline{z}}|\leq r+|{\underline{z}}_{0}|\},

we have

\frac{|(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{z}}_{0})|}{{\underline{t}}!}r^{T}\leq|f|_{r+|{\underline{z}}_{0}|}.

(4)

We will also use Stirling’s Formula:

N^{N}{\mathrm{e}}^{-N}\sqrt{2\pi N}<N!<N^{N}{\mathrm{e}}^{-N}\sqrt{2\pi N}{\mathrm{e}}^{1/(12N)},

(5)

which is valid for all
N\geq 1
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f
be an entire function in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and let
A\geq 0
. Assume

\limsup_{r\to\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-r}\sqrt{r}|f|_{r}<\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-A}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdotp

(6)

Then there exists
T_{0}>0
such that, for
{\underline{t}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with
\|{\underline{t}}\|\geq T_{0}
, we have

|D^{\underline{t}}f|_{A}<1.

Proof.

From assumption (
6
), it follows that there exists
\eta>0
such that, for
T
sufficiently large, we have

|f|_{T}<(1-\eta)\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{T-A}}{\sqrt{2\pi T}}\cdotp

Let
{\underline{t}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
with
\|{\underline{t}}\|=T
.
We use Cauchy’s inequalities (
4
) with
r=T-A
: since
{\underline{t}}!\leq T!
, for
|{\underline{z}}|\leq A
we have

|(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{z}})|\leq\frac{T!}{(T-A)^{T}}|f|_{T}.

Hence the upper bound for
T!
given by the right hand side of (
5
) yields

\sup_{|{\underline{z}}|\leq A}|(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{z}})|\leq(1-\eta){\mathrm{e}}^{-A+1/(12T)}\left(1-\frac{A}{T}\right)^{-T}.

For
T
sufficiently large, the right hand side is
<1
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
f
be an entire function in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of exponential type
<\pi
in each of the variables. Assume

(D^{\underline{t}}f)({\underline{e}}_{i})=0\text{ for all }({\underline{t}},i)\in{\mathcal{T}}.

(7)

The case

is a result due to Poritsky (cf.
[

]
and
[

2
by induction on

. The proof in the one dimensional case will use the following two well known lemmas dealing with univariate functions.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f
be an entire function in
{\mathbb{C}}
. The two following conditions are equivalent:

(i)
The function
f
is periodic of period
\omega\not=0
;

(ii)
There exists a function
g
analytic in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
such that
f(z)=g({\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega})
.

Proof.

Clearly (ii) implies (i). Assume (i).
The map
z\mapsto{\mathrm{e}}^{\pi{\mathrm{i}}z}
is analytic and surjective.
The condition
{\mathrm{e}}^{\pi{\mathrm{i}}z_{1}}={\mathrm{e}}^{\pi{\mathrm{i}}z_{2}}
implies
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
.
Hence there exists a unique map
g:{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
g({\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z})=f(z)
.

\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}^{{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z}}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}

Let
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
and let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
be such that
w={\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z}
. From
g(w)=f(z)
it follows that
g
is holomorphic, hence analytic, in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.

This proves lemma
1
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

If
g
is an analytic function in
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, if
\omega
is a nonzero complex number and if the entire function
g({\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega})
has an exponential type
<2(N+1)\pi/|\omega|
for some nonnegative integer
N
, then
w^{N}g(w)
is a polynomial of degree
\leq 2N
.

As a consequence, if
g({\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega})
has a type
<2\pi/|\omega|
, then
g
is constant.

Proof.

Assume that the function
f(z)=g({\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega})
has an exponential type
\tau
with
\tau<2(N+1)\pi/|\omega|
.
Let
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. Write
w=|w|{\mathrm{e}}^{i\theta}
with
|\theta|\leq\pi
. Set

z=\frac{\omega}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}(\log|w|+i\theta),

so that
w={\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega}
. For any
\epsilon_{1}>0
, we have

|z|\leq\left(\frac{\omega}{2\pi}+\epsilon_{1}\right)|\log|w||

for sufficiently large
|w|
and also for sufficiently small
|w|
. We deduce

\log|g(w)|=\log|f(z)|\leq(\tau+\epsilon_{2})|z|\leq\left(\frac{\omega\tau}{2\pi}+\epsilon_{3}\right)|\log|w||.

Hence if
\alpha
satisfy
\frac{\tau|\omega|}{2\pi}<\alpha<N+1
,
then
|g|_{r}\leq r^{\alpha}
for sufficiently large
r
and

|g|_{r}\leq r^{-\alpha}

for sufficiently small
r>0
.
Consider the Laurent expansion of
g
at the origin:

g(w)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b_{n}w^{n}.

From

b_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|w|=r}g(w)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{w^{n+1}}

we deduce Cauchy’s inequalities

|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}|g|_{r}.

For
n>N
, we use these inequalities with
r\to\infty
while for
n<-N
, we use these inequalities with
r\to 0
.
We deduce
b_{n}=0
for
|n|\geq N+1
. Hence

g(w)=\frac{1}{w^{N}}A(w)+B(w)

where
A
and
B
are polynomials of degree
\leq N
.
∎

.

We start by proving the case

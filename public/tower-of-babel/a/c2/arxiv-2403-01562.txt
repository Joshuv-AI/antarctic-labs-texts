On Some Extensions of the Boué-Dupuis Variational Formula
Amarjit Budhiraja

Abstract
The Boué-Dupuis variational formula gives a representation for log Laplace transforms of bounded measurable functions of a finite dimensional Brownian motion on a compact time interval as an infimum of a suitable cost over a collection of non-anticipative control processes. This variational formula has proved to be very useful in studying a variety of large deviation problems. In this article we collect some extensions of this basic result that have appeared in disparate venues in studying a broad range of large deviation questions. Some of these results can be found in a unified way in the recent book [ 10 ] , while others, to date, have been scattered at various places in the literature. The latter category includes, in particular, variational representations, when the stochastic dynamical system of interest has in addition to a driving Lévy noise, another source of randomness, e.g. due to a random initial condition; when the functionals of interest depends on infinite-length paths of a Lévy process; when the noise process is a Gaussian process with long-range dependence, e.g. a fractional Brownian motion, etc. The goal of this survey article is to present these diverse variational formulas in a systematic manner. Keywords. Variational representations, fractional Brownian motion, Hilbert space valued Brownian motion, Lévy process, large deviations, Laplace asymptotics. In memory of Professor K.R. Parthasarathy.

(1.1)

In this we article we collect several important extensions of this result which have proved to be useful in a range of problems, including those from infinite dimensional stochastic dynamical systems, stochastic partial differential equations, jump-diffusions and other stochastic systems driven by Lévy processes, Gaussian processes with long-range dependence, mean-field interacting particle systems, stochastic particle systems on discrete lattices, random graph models, etc.
Several of these extensions can be found in the recent book

Our goal in this survey article is to introduce these various representations in a systematic way and explain the different contexts where they have been used.

The representation given in

it is useful to have an extension of the representation which allows for a filtration that is larger than the Brownian filtration. Such a representation is important in the proof of the lower bound in the large deviation principle for the path occupation measure associated with such interacting particle systems. It turns out that an optimal point
\mu
of the rate function, over a given set,
corresponds to the probability law of the state process, in a controlled stochastic system, which apriori is not known to be adapted to the filtration generated by the driving noise. This subtle but crucial issue requires a variational representation that allows for a larger filtration.

An extension in a different direction is motivated by systems driven by infinite dimensional Brownian motions that are the basic models in the field of stochastic partial differential equations. In order to study large deviation properties of such systems, it becomes important to establish a representation that is applicable to the various models of infinite dimensional Brownian motions, such as a Hilbert space valued Brownian motion, a cylindrical Brownian motion, space-time Brownian sheet, etc.

A representation that allows for both of these features was obtained in

4

We begin with some basic definitions. Fix
T<\infty
.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which is given a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfying the usual conditions. Let
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a real separable Hilbert space and let

be a strictly positive, symmetric, trace class operator on

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous
H

(i)

For every nonzero

\langle Qh,h\rangle^{-1/2}\langle W(t),h\rangle

(ii)

For every
h\in H

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-martingale.

Define
H_{0}\doteq Q^{1/2}H

\langle h,k\rangle_{0}\doteq\langle Q^{-1/2}h,Q^{-1/2}k\rangle,\mbox{ for }h,k\in H_{0}.

Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
the collection of all
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-predictable
u:[0,T]\times\Omega\to H_{0}
that satisfy
\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{0}^{2}dt<\infty
. Also denote by
\mathcal{G}_{t}
the
\mathbb{P}
-augmentation of the Brownian filtration
\sigma\{W(s):0\leq s\leq t\}

\mathcal{A}
be the subcollection of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
consisting of
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-predictable
H_{0}

[
6
,
4
,
10

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable map. Then

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right],

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\}
.

Thus the representation in particular says that the infimum on the right side taken over the larger collection
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is same as that over the subcollection
\mathcal{A}
.
In fact the infimum can be taken over a much more smaller subcollection, a fact which is very useful when one would like to use weak convergence arguments in proofs of large deviation principles. To describe this subcollection, let for
M\in(0,\infty)
, define

S_{M}\doteq\left\{u\in L^{2}([0,T]:H_{0}):\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds\leq M\right\}.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{b,M}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b,M}

\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
) consisting of

that take values in
S_{M}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{b}\doteq\cup_{M>0}\mathcal{A}_{b,M}

and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\doteq\cup_{M>0}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b,M}
. Then
[
10
, Theorem 8.3]
shows that

2.1
) in fact holds for any
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\}
.

One can take the infimum over even a further smaller class, namely the collection of simple processes. This fact was crucially exploited in
[
1
]
in the proof of the large deviation principle for a Brownian interacting particle system with local interactions. This work, in the proof of the large deviation upper bound, required certain estimates on Dirichlet forms associated with the controlled state processes, which in turn relied on the smoothness properties of the density functions of the laws of these controlled processes. The proof of these regularity properties given in
[
1
]
made key use of the fact that the controls are piecewise constant. Thus the result that in the variational representation the infimum can be taken over such controls played a central role in the proof.

We now present this result.

Definition 2.2
.

A process

(resp.
\mathcal{A}
) is called simple, if there exists a

,

and

where, for each

,
X_{j}
is a real
\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}_{t_{j}}
) measurable random variable satisfying

.
We denote the collection of all such simple processes as
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}_{s}
).

The following result says that the infimum in (
2.1
) in fact holds for any
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b},\mathcal{A}_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\}
. Although the result follows readily from the results in
[
6
]

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable map. Then

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right],

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A}_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous and bounded map. In that case, from

[
6
, Lemma 3.5]
, we have that

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]=\inf_{v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]

and so the statement in the theorem follows from Theorem
2.1
. Now consider a general real bounded measurable map
F
on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)
. Then there is a sequence of continuous maps
F_{n}:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|F_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
F_{n}\to F

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
v^{*}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v^{*}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)\right]\leq\inf_{v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]+\varepsilon.

Since
v^{*}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
, the law of
W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds
is mutually absolutely continuous with respect to that of
W
and so
F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)
converges a.s. to

F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)
. Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v^{*}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]+\varepsilon.

Using the first part of the proof, choose
v_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{n}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v^{*}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)\right]+n^{-1}.

From
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left|F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)-F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right|=0.

Combining the last three displays we have

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{n}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v^{*}(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v^{*}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|v(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the result follows.

Functionals of Brownian Motions with Additional Randomness

In some situations one is interested in studying the large deviations behavior of a stochastic dynamical system for which, in addition to a random driving noise, there is another source of randomness, coming e.g. from the initial state of the stochastic system. Two such settings were studied in

In order to motivate the extension considered in this section, we describe the setting in

3.1
Brownian particle systems with killing.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. exponential random variables with rate
1

\{B_{i}(t),t\geq 0\}_{i\geq 1}

Define for

\mu^{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{B_{i}(t)}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{X_{i}>\int_{0}^{t}\langle\zeta,\,\mu^{n}(s)\rangle\,ds\right\}}.

(3.1)

Here
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function with sub-quadratic growth.
Since a.s., we can enumerate
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in a strictly increasing order, the unique solution of (
3.1

It can be checked (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
) that
\mu^{n}\doteq\{\mu^{n}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
converges, in the Skorokhod path space
\mathcal{D}\doteq\mathbb{D}([0,T]:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where

is the space of sub-probability measures on

equipped with the weak convergence topology, in probability
to
\mu
where for

,
\mu(t)
has density

given as the solution of

\frac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)-\langle\zeta,\,u\rangle\,u(t,x),\;(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\lim_{t\downarrow 0}u(t,\cdot)=\delta_{0}(\cdot),

(3.2)

Such particle systems are motivated by problems in biology, ecology, chemical kinetics, etc. For example, the simplest case
where
\zeta\equiv 1
corresponds to the case where the killing rate is proportional to the total number of particles alive and models a setting in which particles compete for a common resource. More general functions

is the amount of resource consumed by a particle in state

.
Similar particle systems arise in problems of mathematical finance as models for self exciting correlated defaults.

In studying large deviation properties of the sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}
of
\mathcal{D}
valued random variables one needs to understand the asymptotics of Laplace functionals of the form

-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}[\exp(-nf(\mu^{n}))]

In view of the weak convergence approach to the study of large deviation problems
[
13
,
10
]
, it is then natural to derive a suitable controlled representation for the above Laplace functional and understand tightness and weak convergence properties of the various terms in this representation. Note that

\mathbf{X}^{n}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}[\exp(-nF_{n}(\mathbf{B}^{n},\mathbf{X}^{n}))]

is a suitable real bounded and measurable map on

In the case where

However the setting where the function

For such settings one needs a variational representation that allows for functionals that, in addition to depending on a Brownian motion, depend also on an additional source of randomness. Such a representation was given in

d
-dimensional Brownian motion with respect to the filtration

\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{\tilde{W}}\doteq\{\sigma\{\tilde{W}(s):s\leq t\}\}
.
Let
\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}
be the augmented filtration, namely
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\doteq\sigma\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{\tilde{W}}\cup\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
is the collection of all
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
-null sets.
Define

\tilde{F}(x)\doteq-\log\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-F(\tilde{W},x)\right)\right],\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{S}.

(3.3)

From the independence between
W
and
X
we see that

-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-F(B,X)\right)\right]=-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\tilde{F}(X)\right)\right].

Applying classical results of Donsker–Varadhan (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
) to the right side, we have the following representation formula from the above equality

-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-F(B,X)\right)\right]=\inf_{\Pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S})}\left[R(\Pi\|\rho)+\int_{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{F}(x)\Pi(dx)\right].

(3.4)

Consider the collection of processes

\mathcal{A}\doteq\left\{u:\mbox{the process }u\mbox{ is }\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\mbox{-predictable and }\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|^{2}\,ds<\infty\right\}.

From (
1.1
) we now have the following variational formula

\tilde{F}(x)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{A}}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|^{2}\,ds+F\left(\tilde{W}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u(s)\,ds,x\right)\right],

(3.5)

which together with (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-F(B,X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\inf_{\Pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{S})}\left\{R(\Pi\|\rho)+\int_{\mathbb{S}}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{A}}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|^{2}\,ds+F\left(\tilde{W}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u(s)\,ds,x\right)\right]\Pi(dx)\right\}.

The above representation is inconvenient to use directly in large deviation proofs and the main challenge in using it, in comparison to the representation in Theorem
3.1
is the presence of the infimum under the integral on the right side. Nevertheless, the above representation is the starting point of the proof of Theorem
3.1
which uses discretization and measurable selection arguments to show not only that the integral and the infimum can be interchanged in the representation but also that the minimal filtration
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
can be replaced by a larger filtration with respect to which which
B
is a martingale. The fact that one can allow for a general filtration turns out to be crucial in the proof of the lower bound of the large deviation proof for the collection
\{\mu^{n}\}
described earlier in the section.

4
Infinite Time Horizon

In some problems one needs a variational formula for functionals of the paths of a Brownian motion over an infinite time horizon. We describe one such situation below.

4.1
Empirical Measures of State Processes Driven by a Fractional Brownian Motion.

One situation where the need to consider infinite-length paths of Brownian motion arises naturally is in the study of path empirical measures of ergodic solutions of stochastic differential equations driven by a fractional Brownian motion (fBM). The term ergodicity here needs to be interpreted suitably as the solution processes are not Markov, however there is a natural way to augment the state of the system with an infinite length path of a Brownian motion to get a Markovian state descriptor (see
[
15
]
). The starting point of obtaining such a Markovian description is the Mandelbrot - Van Ness decomposition of a fBM in terms of a two sided Brownian motion. This decomposition is as follows.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space on which is given a real two-sided Wiener process
\{W(t):-\infty<t<\infty\}
. Such a process is characterized by the property that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{W^{t}(s)\doteq W(t-s)-W(t)\}_{s\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
\{W(t+u):u\geq 0\}
. Fix
H\in(0,1)
. Then a real fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
, denoted as
B_{H}(\cdot)
, has the following representation: For
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle B_{H}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{G}(r)(dW(r+t)-dW(r))=\int_{-\infty}^{0}(\mathcal{G}(r+t)-\mathcal{G}(r))dW(r)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(r-t)dW(r),

where
\mathcal{G}(r)=\alpha_{H}r^{H-1/2}
, for
r\geq 0
, and
\alpha_{H}
is a suitable constant.

One is interested in stochastic differential equations (SDE) driven by such a driving noise. For simplicity of presentation, considered the simplest such equation which describes a fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process:

dX(t)=-\alpha X(t)dt+dB_{H}(t).

The ergodicity behavior of
\{X(t),t\geq 0\}
and in fact of much more general processes given as solutions of SDE driven by a fBM have been studied in
[
15
]
and in several subsequent works.
These results, in particular, characterize the law of the limit of the occupation measures
\{L^{T},T>0\}
as
T\to\infty
, where

L^{T}(\cdot)\doteq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta_{X(s)}\,ds,\;T>0.

In order to study the large deviation behavior of the above collection of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
valued random variables, one needs to characterize the asymptotic behavior of Laplace functionals of the following form:

-\frac{1}{T}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-Tf(L^{T})\}

where
f:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded continuous map. Note that
L^{T}
is a measurable map of the infinite path of a standard Brownian motion, namely the collection

\{W^{T}(s)=W(T-s)-W(T):s\geq 0\}
. Thus, for a suitable bounded measurable map
F_{T}:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
,

-\frac{1}{T}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-Tf(L^{T})\}=-\frac{1}{T}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-TF_{T}(W^{T})\}.

Here, for a Polish space
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
denotes the space of continuous functions from
[0,\infty)
to
\mathcal{E}
, equipped with the local uniform topology.
Thus, in order to study asymptotic behavior of such a quantity, it is of interest to develop a variational representation for functionals of infinite-length paths of a standard Brownian motion.

4.2
Representation for Infinte Length Brownian Paths.

We will only provide detailed arguments for a one dimensional Brownian motion. Analogous results can be established for a Brownian motion with values in a separable Hilbert space by combining the arguments here with those in
[
6
]
. We also remark that, in order to study ergodicity properties of
X(\cdot)
and other fractional diffusions,
[
15
]
and subsequent papers view
W^{T}
as an element of a suitable path space
\mathcal{H}_{H}
of Hölder continuous functions. Since the Borel
\sigma
-fields on
\mathcal{H}_{H}
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
are the same, one immediately obtains from Theorem
4.1
below also a representation for real bounded measurable functionals on
\mathcal{H}_{H}
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0})
be a filtered probability space where the filtration satisfies the usual conditions.
Let
\{W(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a standard
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-Wiener process on this space. The
\mathbb{P}
-augmentation of the filtration
\{\sigma\{W(s):0\leq s\leq t\}\}_{t\geq 0}
will be denoted as
\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Note that
\mathcal{G}_{t}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t}
for all
t\geq 0
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
) the collection of all
\mathbb{R}
-valued
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-predictable (resp.
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-predictable) processes
v:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfy

\mathbb{P}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(t)|^{2}dt<\infty\right\}=1.

It will be convenient to consider the following subcollections of
\mathcal{A}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
as well.
Let, for
M\in(0,\infty)
,

S_{M}\doteq\left\{u\in L^{2}([0,\infty):\mathbb{R}):\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(s)|^{2}ds\leq M\right\},

and
\mathcal{A}_{b,M}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b,M}
) be elements of
\mathcal{A}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
) that take values in
S_{M}
a.s.
Define

\mathcal{A}_{b}\doteq\cup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{b,M},\;\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\doteq\cup_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b,M}.

The following is the main variational representation of the section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded and measurable map. Then

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right],

(4.1)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\}
.

In preparation for the proof of the theorem we give sime preliminary results.
For
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
, and
T\in[0,\infty)
, let

L_{\infty}(v)\doteq\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}v(s)dW(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds\right\},\;L_{T}(v)\doteq\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{T}v(s)dW(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|v(s)|^{2}ds\right\}.

The following result is a simple consequence of Girsanov’s theorem.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. Then
\mathbb{E}L_{\infty}(v)=1
. Define the probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
as

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\doteq L_{\infty}(v)=\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}v(s)dW(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds\right\}.

Then

\tilde{W}(t)\doteq W(t)-\int_{0}^{t}v(s)ds,\;t\geq 0

is a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-Wiener process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0})
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. Then there is a
M<\infty
such that
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b,M}
. Since, for every
T\in[0,\infty)
,
\int_{0}^{T}|v(s)|^{2}ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds\leq M
, we have from Novikov’s criterion (cf.
[
19
, Corollary VIII.1.16]
)
\mathbb{E}(L_{T}(v))=1
for all
T\in[0,\infty)
.
Also, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is easy to verify that

\sup_{T\in[0,\infty)}\mathbb{E}(L_{T}(v))^{2}\leq e^{M}<\infty
which shows that
\{L_{T}(v),T\geq 0\}
is a uniformly integrable
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-martingale on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Noting that

L_{T}(v)\to L_{\infty}(v)
in probability, we now see that in fact the convergence holds in
L^{1}
as well and consequently
\mathbb{E}L_{\infty}(v)=1
proving the first statement in the lemma. Also, it follows that, for
T\in[0,\infty)
,
E(L_{\infty}(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})=L_{T}(v)
a.s. By Girsanov’s theorem (cf.
[
17
, Theorem 3.5.1]
) it now follows that

\tilde{W}
is a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-Wiener process on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0})
. This completes the proof of the second statement.
∎

The following collection of simple processes will be useful.

Definition 4.1
.

A process
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}
) is called simple, if there exists a
T\in(0,\infty)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

0=t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots\leq t_{k+1}=T
, such that

v(s,\omega)\doteq\sum_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}(\omega)\mathbf{1}_{(t_{j},t_{j+1}]}(s),\;(s,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times\Omega,

where, for each
j=1,\ldots,k
,
X_{j}
is a real
\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}_{t_{j}}
) measurable random variable satisfying
|X_{j}|\leq N
.
We denote the collection of all such simple processes as
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
(resp.
\mathcal{A}_{s}
).

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{s}\subset\mathcal{A}_{b}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
.

Since a simple process is zero after some finite time horizon, the known results for finite time horizon filtrations (see e.g.
[
10
, Lemma 8.7 and Equation (8.14)]
) give the following result.
For
v\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
, denote
W^{v}(\cdot)\doteq W(\cdot)-\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds
and define the probability measure
\mathbb{Q}^{v}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
as

d\mathbb{Q}^{v}\doteq L_{\infty}(v)d\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
v_{0}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
, a bounded measurable map
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\varepsilon>0
there is a
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{0}(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{0}(s)ds)\right]+\varepsilon.

Furthermore, for every
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
there is a
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
such that
\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}\circ(W^{\tilde{v}},\tilde{v})^{-1}=\mathbb{P}\circ(W,v)^{-1}
.

In the above lemma when talking about the distribiution of
v,\tilde{v}
, these are regarded as random variables in
S_{M}
, for a suitable
M\in(0,\infty)
, where this space is equipped with the weak toplogy inherited from that on the Hilbert space
L^{2}([0,\infty):\mathbb{R})
.

We now complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We begin by proving the upper bound

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}\leq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]

(4.2)

Since
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
is the largest collection, it suffices to show the result with
\mathcal{R}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
.
We will first show the inequality in (
4.2
) (without the infimum) for an arbitrary
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
. Note that for this, in view of the first part of Lemma
4.3
, it suffices to establish the inequality for an arbitrary
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
. Consider now such a
v
.
From the second part of Lemma
4.3
it follows that there is a
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
such that
\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}\circ(W^{\tilde{v}},\tilde{v})^{-1}=\mathbb{P}\circ(W,v)^{-1}
.
From the Donsker-Varadhan variational formula (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.2(a)]
) we have that

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}\leq R(\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}\|\mathbb{P})+\int_{\Omega}F(W)d\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}.

Note that

R(\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}\|\mathbb{P})=\int_{\Omega}\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}}{d\mathbb{P}}\right)d\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{v}(s)dW(s)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{v}(s)|^{2}ds\right)\\
=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{v}(s)dW^{\tilde{v}}(s)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\tilde{v}(s)|^{2}ds\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}v(s)dW(s)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds,

where the fourth equality uses the second part of Lemma
4.3
.
Also, by another application of this result, we have

\int_{\Omega}F(W)d\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}=\int_{\Omega}F(W^{\tilde{v}}+\int_{0}^{\cdot}\tilde{v}(s)ds)d\mathbb{Q}^{\tilde{v}}=\mathbb{E}F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds).

Combining the last two displays, we now have the inequality in (
4.2
) (without the infimum) for any
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
and thus, as discussed previously, also for any
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
.
Now consider a
v\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
. Without loss of generality,
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds<\infty
. Thus, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a
T_{n}<\infty
such that
\mathbb{E}\int_{T_{n}}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds\leq n^{-1}
. Also, we can find (cf.
[
16
, Lemma II.1.1]
]), for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a
v_{n}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
that satisfying
v_{n}(t)=0
for all
t\geq T_{n}
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(t)-v(t)|^{2}dt=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}|v_{n}(t)-v(t)|^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{T_{n}}^{\infty}|v(t)|^{2}dt\leq 2n^{-1}.

(4.3)

Note that

\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(t)|^{2}dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(t)|^{2}dt+4<\infty.

From the fact that the inequality in (
4.2
) holds for any
v\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
, we have

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right].

(4.4)

Note that, from (
4.3
), for any
T<\infty
,

E\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\int_{0}^{t}v_{n}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{t}v(s)ds|^{2}\leq TE\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)-v(s)|^{2}ds\to 0,\;\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Thus we have that
\theta_{n}\doteq\mathcal{L}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\to\mathcal{L}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\doteq\theta
, as
n\to\infty
, as probability measures on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Also, denoting the probability law of
W
by
\theta_{0}
, note that
\theta_{0}=\mathbb{Q}\circ(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)
, where
\mathbb{Q}
is as in Lemma
4.2
with
v
there replaced by
-v_{n}
. Thus

R(\theta_{n}\|\theta_{0})=R(\mathbb{P}\circ(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)^{-1}\|\mathbb{Q}\circ(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)^{-1})\\
\leq R(\mathbb{P}\|\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}v_{n}(s)dW(s)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds\right)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds.

Thus
\sup_{n}R(\theta_{n}\|\theta_{0})<\infty
. This together with
\theta_{n}\to\theta
now implies (see
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
) that

F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\Rightarrow F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)
. Together with the fact
from (
4.3
) that
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(t))|^{2}dt
converges to
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(t))|^{2}dt
, we now have from (
4.4
) that this inequality holds with
v_{n}
replaced by
v
. Since
v\in\mathcal{A}
is arbitrary, this proves the upper bound in (
4.2
) for
\mathcal{R}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and therefore for any
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\}
.

We now establish the complementary lower bound:

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}\geq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]

(4.5)

It suffices to show the inequality for
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{A}_{b}
since that is the smallest collection.
Consider first the case where for some
T\in(0,\infty)
, and a bounded measurable map

F_{T}:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
,
F(w(\cdot))=F_{T}(w(\cdot\wedge T))
for all
w\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
In this case, denoting the subcollection of
\mathcal{A}_{b}
that consists of predictable
v:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfy
v(t,\omega)=0
for
t>T
by
\mathcal{A}_{b,T}
, we have (cf.
[
10
, Section 8.1.4]
) that

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{b,T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F_{T}\left(W(\cdot\wedge T)+\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge T}v(s)ds\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right].

Next, let
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
be a general bounded measurable map.
Let
\{T_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence such that
T_{n}\uparrow\infty
.
Define
\mathcal{G}_{n}\doteq\sigma\{W(s):s\leq T_{n}\}
. Note that by the martingale convergence theorem

F_{n}\doteq\mathbb{E}(F\mid\mathcal{G}_{n})
converges a.s. to
F
as
n\to\infty
. Also

\|F_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F\|_{\infty}
. Furthermore, since
F_{n}
is
\mathcal{G}_{n}
measurable, there is a bounded measurable map
\tilde{F}_{n}:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T_{n}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F_{n}(W(\cdot))=\tilde{F}_{n}(W(\cdot\wedge T_{n}))
a.s. This also says that for any
v\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
,

F_{n}(W(\cdot)+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)=\tilde{F}_{n}(W(\cdot\wedge T_{n})+\int_{0}^{\cdot\wedge T_{n}}v(s)ds)
a.s.
It thus follows that

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F_{n}(W)\}\geq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right]

a.s.
Let
v_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
be
n^{-1}
-optimal for the right side. Then we have

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F_{n}(W)\}\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right]-n^{-1}.

The left side in the above display converges to
-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}
as
n\to\infty
.
Denoting by
\theta_{n}
(resp.
\theta
) the probability law of
W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds

(resp.
W
) on
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}
, we have that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

R(\theta_{n}\|\theta)\leq\frac{1}{2}E\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds\leq 2\|F\|_{\infty}+1.

This shows that (cf.
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)-F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right|=0.

Combining the above

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W)\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F_{n}(W)\}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds+F_{n}(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v_{n}(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v_{n}(s)ds)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{v\mathcal{A}_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|v(s)|^{2}ds+F(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds)\right].

This completes the proof of the lower bound and thus the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, the infimum in Theorem
4.1
can be taken over
\mathcal{A}_{s}
or
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
. Also, a similar representation can be written for an infinite dimensional Brownian motion, e.g. for a
H
-valued Wiener process as in Section
2
.

5
Functionals of Fractional Brownian Motion

A representation similar to (
1.1
) can be obtained for more general Gaussian processes than a Brownian motion. Fractional Brownian motions are an important class of Gaussian processes with long-range dependence that arise in many applications. In this section we present a variational formula for functionals of a fractional Brownian motion given in
[
7
]
that has been used for studying large deviation properties of small noise stochastic differental equations with a fractional Brownian motion. The variational formula relies on a general result from
[
22
]
on a variational representation for random functionals on abstract Wiener spaces. We begin by presenting this latter result and then describe how a representation for functionals of a fractional Brownian motion can be deduced from it.

5.1
Abstract Wiener Space Representation.

For simplicity we consider the time horizon
[0,1]
. The case of a general finite time horizon
[0,T]
can be treated similarly.
Let
(\mathbb{W},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{W}})
be a separable Banach space. and let
(\mathbb{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}})
be a separable Hilbert space densely and continuously embedded in
\mathbb{W}
. Let
\mu
be a centered, unit variance Gaussian measure over
\mathbb{W}
(cf.
[
18
]
). Identifying in the usual manner the dual space
\mathbb{H}^{*}
with itself,
\mathbb{W}^{*}
may be viewed as a dense linear subspace of
\mathbb{H}
and we have that for any
\ell\in\mathbb{W}^{*}
and
h\in\mathbb{H}
,
\ell(h)=\langle\ell,h\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
. Denoting the embedding map from
\mathbb{H}
into
\mathbb{W}
as
i_{\mathbb{H}}
, the collection
(i_{\mathbb{H}},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{W},\mu)
is referred to as an abstract Wiener space and
\mathbb{H}
is called to Cameron-Martin space of this abstract Wiener space (cf.
[
14
]
).

We recall the notion of a filtration on an abstract Wiener space from
[
21
,
20
]
.
A collection of projection operators
\{\pi_{t},t\in[0,1]\}
on
\mathbb{H}
are referred to as a continuous and strictly monotonic resolution of the identity in
\mathbb{H}
if the following hold

(i)

\pi_{0}=0
,
\pi_{1}=\mbox{Id}
, where
Id
is the identity operator,

(ii)

for
0\leq s<t\leq 1
,
\pi_{s}\mathbb{H}
is a strict subset of
\pi_{t}\mathbb{H}
,

(iii)

for any
h\in\mathbb{H}
and
t\in[0,1]
,
\lim_{s\to t}\pi_{s}h=\pi_{t}h
.

By denseness of
\mathbb{W}^{*}
in
\mathbb{H}
we can find for every
h\in\mathbb{H}
a sequence
\{h_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{W}^{*}
such that
h_{n}\to h
. This says that the sequence
\{h_{n}\}
regarded as a sequence of random variables on the probability space
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W}),\mu)
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mu)
and thus must converge to a limit
\delta(h)
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. This limit is referred to as the Skorohod integral of
h
and we occasionally write
\delta(h)(w)=\langle h,w\rangle
for
h\in\mathbb{H}
and
w\in\mathbb{W}
.

Define the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
as
\mathcal{F}_{t}\doteq\sigma\{\delta(\pi_{t}h),h\in\mathbb{H}\}\vee\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the collection of all
\mu
-null sets in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{W})
. We will regard this as a filtration on the
\mu
-complete probability space
(\mathbb{W},\mathcal{F},\mu)
, where
\mathcal{F}\doteq\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Expecteation on this probability space will be denoted as
\mathbb{E}
.

An
\mathbb{H}
valued random variable
v
on the above probability space is said to be
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted if for every
t\in[0,1]
and
h\in\mathbb{H}
,
\langle\pi_{t}h,v\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
measurable.
The collection of adapted
\mathbb{H}
valued square integrable random variables (i.e
\mathbb{E}\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}<\infty
) is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{a}
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by

S_{N}
the ball of radius
N
in
\mathbb{H}
and let

\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b,N}
the subcollection of
\mathcal{H}^{a}
consisting of
v
that take values in
S_{N}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}\doteq\cup_{N>0}\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b,N}
.
For
t\in[0,1]
, let
\mathcal{C}_{t}
be the collection of cylindrical functions of the form

F(w)=g(\langle\pi_{t}h_{1},w\rangle,\cdots,\langle\pi_{t}h_{n},w\rangle),\;g\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;h_{1},\ldots h_{n}\in\mathbb{W}^{*}.

Here
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of real infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the function and all its derivatives bounded.
Note that such a
F
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
measurable.
A
v\in\mathcal{H}^{a}
is said to be simple if it takes values in
S_{N}
for some
N<\infty
and for some
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1
,
\{h_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\subset\mathbb{H}
, and
\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{t_{i}}
,
i=0,1,\ldots n-1
,

v(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\xi_{i}(w)(\pi_{t_{i+1}}-\pi_{t_{i}})h_{i}.

We denote the collection of all such simple
v
as
\mathcal{S}^{a}
.
The following is the main representation from
[
22
]
(see Theorem 3.2 therein).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
F
be a real bounded and measurable function on
\mathbb{W}
. Then

-\log\mathbb{E}(e^{-F})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+F(\cdot+v)\right)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{H}^{a},\mathcal{S}^{a}\}
.

5.2
Fractional Brownian Motion Representation.

We will now use the representation in Theorem
5.1
to provide a more explicit representation for a fractional Brownian motion. We begin by recalling some basic definitions.
For
H\in(0,1)
, a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (fBm)
B^{H}=\{B^{H}_{t}:\,t\in[0,1]\}
with Hurst parameter
H
defined on some complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a centered Gaussian process whose covariance matrix
R_{H}=(R_{H}^{i,j})_{1\leq i,\,j\leq d}
is given by

R_{H}^{i,j}(s,t)=\mathbb{E}(B^{H,i}_{s}B^{H,j}_{t})=\frac{1}{2}(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H})\delta_{i,j},\;s,\,t\in[0,1],

(5.1)

where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta function. When
H=\frac{1}{2}
, the above process is simply a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion.
From the above covariance formula it is immediate that

\mathbb{E}(|B_{t}^{H}-B_{s}^{H}|^{2})=d|t-s|^{2H},\;t,s\in[0,1].

From the above property along with Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion it follows that the sample paths of
B^{H}
are a.s. Hölder continuous of order
\beta
for all
\beta<H
.

In what follows
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
will denote the canonical probability space, where
\Omega=C_{0}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of continuous functions null at time
0
, equipped with topology of uniform convergence,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(C_{0}([0,1]:\,\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra and
\mathbb{P}
is the unique
d
-dimensional probability measure such that the canonical process
B^{H}=\{B^{H}_{t}(\omega)=\omega(t):\,t\in[0,1]\}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
. Consider the canonical filtration given by
\{\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t}:\,t\in[0,1]\}
, where
\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t}=\sigma\{B^{H}_{s}:\,0\leq s\leq t\}\vee\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}
is the set of the
\mathbb{P}
-negligible events.

We now recall a representation for a fBM in terms of a standard Brownian motion and a suitable kernel function (cf.
[
12
]
). Let
F(a,b,c;z)
denote the Gauss hypergeometric function defined for any
a,\,b,\,c,\,z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
and
c\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
by

F(a,b,c;z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}z^{k},

where
(a)_{0}=1
and
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\dots(a+k-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol.

Let
B=\{B_{t}=(B_{t}^{1},\dots,B_{t}^{d}),\,t\in[0,1]\}
be a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion.
Then it is well known (cf.
[
12
]
) that the process

B_{t}^{H}=\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)dB_{s},\;t\in[0,1]

(5.2)

defines a fBm with Hurst parameter
H
, where

K_{H}(t,s)=k_{H}(t,s)\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}(s),

(5.3)

for
0\leq s\leq t

k_{H}(t,s)=\frac{c_{H}}{\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)}(t-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}F\left(H-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-H,H+\frac{1}{2};1-\frac{t}{s}\right),

(5.4)

c_{H}=\left[\frac{2H\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-H\right)\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(2-2H)}\right]^{1/2}
,
and
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function.

Define
\mathcal{H}_{H}=\{(K_{H}\dot{h}^{1},\dots,K_{H}\dot{h}^{d}):\,\dot{h}=(\dot{h}^{1},\dots,\dot{h}^{d})\in L^{2}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
,
where

(K_{H}f)(t)\doteq\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)f(s)ds\mbox{ for }f\in L^{2}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;t\in[0,1].

That is, any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
can be represented as

h(t)=(K_{H}\dot{h})(t)=\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)\dot{h}(s)ds,

for some
\dot{h}\in L^{2}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Define a scalar inner product on
\mathcal{H}_{H}
by

\langle h,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}=\langle K_{H}\dot{h},K_{H}\dot{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}=\langle\dot{h},\dot{g}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{H}
is a separable Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}
. It can be checked (cf.
[
7
, Remark 1]
) that
\mathcal{H}_{H}
is a subset of
\Omega=C_{0}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the embedding map is continuous.
We now present the main variational representation for functionals of fBm from
[
7
]
.
For
0<N<\infty
, let

S_{N}=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{H}:\,\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\leq N\}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the collection of all measurable
v:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}_{H}
such that there is a

\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t}
-predictable
\dot{v}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{v}(t)\|^{2}dt<\infty
and
v\omega)=K_{H}\dot{v}(\omega)
a.s.
The subcollection of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of
v
that take values in
S_{N}
will be denoted as
\mathcal{A}_{b,N}
and, as before,

\mathcal{A}_{b}=\cup_{N>0}\mathcal{A}_{b,N}
.
The following is
[
7
, Proposition 2]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
F
be a real bounded measurable function on
\Omega
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}(e^{-F(B^{H})})=

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{E}\left(f(B^{H}+v)+\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}\right),

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}_{b}\}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Although not discussed here, one can also replace the above infimum by the infimum over the subclass of
\mathcal{A}_{b}
consisting of all simple processes.

We provide a brief proof sketch of this result. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for details. The basic idea is to identify a suitable abstract Wiener space and then apply Theorem
5.1
.

It is easy to verify that functions in
\mathcal{H}_{H}
are
H
-Hölder continuous (cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2]
), in particular we have the following fact:
Any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
is in
C^{H}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}
and
\|h\|_{H}\leq\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{H}}
.

Next, recall that
\Omega=C_{0}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space equipped with the sup-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Let
\Omega^{\ast}
be its topological dual.
We now introduce the abstract Wiener space associated with a fractional Brownian motion.
The following result is taken from
[
12
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

If we identify
L^{2}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})
and its dual, we have the following diagram

\Omega^{\ast}\overset{i_{H}^{\ast}}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\mathcal{H}_{H}^{\ast}\overset{K_{H}^{\ast}}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}L^{2}([0,1]:\mathbb{R}^{d})\overset{K_{H}}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\mathcal{H}_{H}\overset{i_{H}}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\Omega

Where

i_{H}
is the injection from
\mathcal{H}_{H}
into
\Omega
, and
K_{H}^{\ast}
and
i_{H}^{\ast}
are the respective adjoints.

(a)

The injection
i_{H}
embeds
\mathcal{H}_{H}
densely into
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{H}_{H}
is the Cameron-Martin space of the abstract Wiener space
(i_{H},\mathcal{H}_{H},\Omega,\mathbb{P})
in the sense of Gross
[
14
]
.

(b)

The restriction of
K_{H}^{\ast}
to
\Omega^{\ast}
can be represented by

(K_{H}^{\ast}\eta)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)\eta(dt)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)\eta_{1}(dt),\dots,\int_{0}^{1}K_{H}(t,s)\eta_{d}(dt)\right),

for any
\eta=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d})\in\Omega^{\ast}
.

As before, denote for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}
its Skorohod integral by
\delta(h)
.
Recall the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\{\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t}\})

introduced above and recall that

\{B^{H}_{t}\}
is the canonical coordinate process
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
.

Define the family
\{\pi_{t}^{H},\,t\in[0,1]\}
of projection operators in
\mathcal{H}_{H}
by

\pi_{t}^{H}h=\pi_{t}^{H}(K_{H}\dot{h})=K_{H}(\dot{h}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t]}),\;h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}.

(5.5)

The following result is a consequence of

[
12
, Proposition 4.4, Theorems 4.3 and 4.8]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

(a)

For any
t\in[0,1]
,
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{H}=\sigma\{\delta(\pi^{H}_{t}h),\,h\in\mathcal{H}_{H}\}\vee\mathcal{N}
.

(b)

For any
\mathcal{H}_{H}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t},\,t\in[0,1]\}
-adapted stochastic process
u
there is a
\{\mathcal{F}^{H}_{t},\,t\in[0,1]\}
-predictable process
\dot{u}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
u=K_{H}\dot{u}
a.s.

One can now complete the proof of Theorem
5.2
as follows.
The collection
\{\pi_{t}^{H},\,t\in[0,1]\}
introduced in (
5.5
) defines
a continuous and strictly monotonic resolution
of the identity in
\mathcal{H}_{H}
in the sense described previously in the section.
Also, using Lemma
5.5
, the classes
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{b}
in this section are the same as the classes
\mathcal{H}^{a}
and
\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
introduced earlier in the section, when specialized to the abstract Wiener space for the setting of a fBM. The result is now immediate from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6
Lévy Noise.

In this section we present the analogues of the Boué-Dupuis variational formulas for Lévy processes. The results from this section are from
[
5
]
. They can also be found in
[
10
, Section 8.2]
. The statement in Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
that
\mathcal{R}
can be
\mathcal{A}_{s}
or
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
, although does not appear in these references, it can be easily deduced from the arguments there along the lines of the proof of Theorem
2.2
. We omit the details.

A Lévy noise is composed of a two random components, one consisting of a finite or an infinite dimensional Brownian motion and the other described through a suitable Poisson random measure(PRM). We begin by first presenting a representation of functionals of a PRM and then we will give the representation for the more general case where the functional depends on both a PRM and an infinite dimensional Brownian motion.

6.1
Representation for a Poisson Random Measure.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a locally compact Polish space and let
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
denote the space of all locally-finite measures
\nu
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}))
. The space
\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
is equipped with the standard vague convergence topology. Fix
T\in(0,\infty)
and let
\mathcal{X}_{T}=[0,T]\times\mathcal{X}
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space with a filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfying the usual assumptions. Fix
\nu\in\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
and let
\nu_{T}\doteq\lambda_{T}\times\nu
where
\lambda_{T}
is the Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
.

Let
N
be a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
- Poisson random measure with points in
\mathcal{X}_{T}
and intensity measure
\nu_{T}
. Recall this means that
N
is a
\Sigma(\mathcal{X}_{T})
valued random variable with the following properties

(i)

for every
t\in[0,T]
and
A\in\mathcal{B}([0,t]\times\mathcal{X})
,
N(A)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable,

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
and
A\in\mathcal{B}((t,T]\times\mathcal{X})
,
N(A)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
,

(iii)

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
A_{1},\ldots A_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}_{T})
such that
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
when
i\neq j
, and
\nu_{T}(\cup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i})<\infty
, we have
N(A_{1}),\ldots N(A_{k})

are mutually independent Poisson random variables with parameters
\nu_{T}(A_{1}),\ldots\nu_{T}(A_{k})
, respectively.

Fix
\theta>0
. Letting
\mathbb{M}\doteq\Sigma(\mathcal{X})
, we will denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
the unique probability measure on
(\mathbb{M},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{M}))
under which the the canonical map
N:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
defined as
N(m)=m
is PRM with intensity
\theta\nu_{T}
. In typical large deviation problems of interest
\theta
plays the role of scaling parameter and one is interested in behavior of the system as
\theta
approaches some limiting value (e.g.
\infty
or
0
). In such problems one is interested in characterizing the asymptotic behavior of Laplace functionals of the form

-\log\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\exp\{-F(N)\}
, where
F:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is some bounded measurable map.

We will now present the variational representation from
[
5
]
that, together with weak convergence methods, allows one to study such asymptotic behavior in many problem settings (cf. references in
[
10
]
). In formulating this variational representation it will be convenient to work with a PRM that is defined on a larger point space.

Let
\mathcal{Y}\doteq\mathcal{X}\times[0,\infty)
and
\mathcal{Y}_{T}\doteq[0,T]\times\mathcal{Y}
. Let
\bar{\mathbb{M}}
denote the space of locally finite measures on
\mathcal{Y}_{T}
. and let
\bar{P}
be the unique measure on

(\bar{\mathbb{M}},\mathcal{B}(\bar{\mathbb{M}}))
such that the canonical map
\bar{N}:\bar{\mathbb{M}}\to\bar{\mathbb{M}}
defined as
\bar{N}(m)\doteq m
,
m\in\bar{\mathbb{M}}
, is a PRM with points in
\mathcal{Y}_{T}
and intensity
measure
\bar{\nu}_{t}\doteq\lambda_{T}\times\nu\times\lambda_{\infty}
, where
\lambda_{\infty}
is the Lebesgue measure on
[0,\infty)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{t}
denote the augmentation of
\sigma\{\bar{N}((0,s]\times A):0\leq s\leq t,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y})\}
with all
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
null sets in
\mathcal{B}(\bar{\mathbb{M}})
and denote by
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}
the
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-predictable
\sigma
-field on
[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{M}}
.

Recall that the representation for functionals of Brownian motions, given e.g. in (
1.1
), involves
L^{2}
-controls
v
and controlled noise processes
W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}v(s)ds
.
We now introduce the controls and controlled noises that will play the analogous role in the variational representations for PRM.
First the space of controls, which we denote by
\mathcal{A}
will be the collection of all maps
v:[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{M}}\times\mathcal{X}
that are
(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}))\backslash\mathcal{B}([0,\infty))
measurable. For notational ease, for
(t,\omega,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{M}}\times\mathcal{X}
, we will occasionally suppress
\omega
and write
v(t,\omega,x)
as
v(t,x)
.
For
v\in\mathcal{A}
, define a counting process
N^{v}
on
\mathcal{X}_{T}
as

N^{v}((0,t]\times U)\doteq\int_{(0,t]\times U}\int_{(0,\infty)}\mathbf{1}_{[0,v(s,x)]}(r)\bar{N}(ds\times dx\times dr)

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. When, for some
\theta>0
,
v(s,\omega,x)=\theta
for all
(s,\omega,x)\in\mathcal{X}_{T}\times\bar{\mathbb{M}}
, we will write
N^{v}
as simply
N^{\theta}
.
The process
N^{v}
can be thought of as the controlled noise process associated with the control
v
.

Next, recall that the Brownian motion representation involved a quadratic cost term given as a suitable
L^{2}
-norm. In the representation for a PRM the quadratic function is replaced by the following superlinear (but sub-quadratic) function
\ell:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
defined as

\ell(r)\doteq r\log r-r+1,\;r\in[0,\infty).

For
v\in\mathcal{A}
, define

L_{T}(v)\doteq\int_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}\ell(v(t,x))\nu_{T}(dt\times dx).

This is the cost term associated with a control
v
that will replace the quadratic cost term we see in Brownian motion representation formulas, for the case of a PRM.

In the case of the Brownian motion, it was useful to consider the smaller collection of controls than
\mathcal{A}
, namely
\mathcal{A}_{b}
, which consisted of controls for which one can invoke the Girsanov theorem. We will consider an analogous smaller collection of controls here as well. Let
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of compact sets of
\mathcal{X}
such that
\cup_{n\geq 1}K_{n}=\mathcal{X}
.
For
M\in(0,\infty)
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{b,M}

\displaystyle\doteq\{v\in\mathcal{A}:L_{T}(v)\leq M,\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},v(t,x)\in[1/n,n],

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{ and }v(t,\omega,x)=1\mbox{ if }x\in K_{n}^{c},\mbox{ for all }(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\mathbb{M}}\}.

A process in
\mathcal{A}_{b,M}
is called simple if the following holds: There exist
n,l,n_{1},\ldots n_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
; a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{l}=T
; for each
i=1,\ldots,l
a disjoint
measurable partition
\{E_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq n_{i}}
of
K_{n}
;
\mathcal{G}_{t_{i-1}}
- measurable random variables
X_{ij}
,
1\leq i\leq l
,
1\leq j\leq n_{i}
, such that
X_{ij}\in[1/n,n]
; and for

(t,\bar{m},x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathcal{X}

v(t,\bar{m},x)=\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{(t_{i-1},t_{i}]}(t)X_{ij}(\bar{m})\mathbf{1}_{E_{ij}}(x)+\mathbf{1}_{K_{n}^{c}}(x)1_{(0,T)}(t).

We denote the collection all such simple processes as
\mathcal{A}_{s,M}
.
Define
\mathcal{A}_{b}\doteq\cup_{M>0}\mathcal{A}_{b,M}
and
\mathcal{A}_{s}\doteq\cup_{M>0}\mathcal{A}_{s,M}
.
As in the Brownian motion case, when the canonical filtration
\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}
is replaced by a larger filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
under which

\bar{N}
is a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-PRM with the same intensity (in such a case of course
\bar{N}
is defined on a larger probability space than the canonical space
(\bar{\mathbb{M}},\mathcal{B}(\bar{\mathbb{M}}))
), we will denote the classes analogous to
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}_{b}
and
\mathcal{A}_{s}
as
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
, and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
, respectively.
The following result is from
[
5
]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 8.12]
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable map. Then for any
\theta>0

-\log E_{\theta}\exp\{-F(N)\}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\theta L_{T}(v)+G(N^{\theta v})],

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b},\mathcal{A}_{s},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{s}\}
.

6.2
General Lévy Process Representation.

Finally, we present the representation for a general Lévy noise. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfying the usual conditions and assume that this probability space supports the processes introduced below. With
Q
as in Section
2
, let
W
be a a
Q
-Wiener process with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}
.
Let
\nu,\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{T},\mathcal{Y}_{T},\bar{\nu}_{T}
be as introduced above. Let
\bar{N}
be a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-PRM with points in
\mathcal{Y}_{T}
with intensity measure
\nu_{T}
. Assume that for all
0\leq s\leq t<\infty
,
(\bar{N}((s,t]\times\cdot)),W(t)-W(s))
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
. Let
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}
be the
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-predictable
\sigma
-field on
[0,T]\times\Omega
. Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{W}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{W}_{b}
the collections
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
introduced below Defintiion
2.1
. Also, denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{N}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{N}_{b}
the collections
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
introduced earlier in the current section. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\doteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{W}_{b}\times\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{N}_{b}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\doteq\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{W}\times\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{N}
.
For
v=(\psi,\varphi)\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
, let
L_{T}^{W}(\psi)\doteq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(s)\|_{0}^{2}ds
, where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{0}
is as introduced in Section
2
,
and let
L_{T}^{N}(\varphi)\doteq\int_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}\ell(\varphi(t,x))\nu_{T}(dt\times dx)
. Also, set
L_{T}(v)\doteq L_{T}^{W}(\psi)+L_{T}^{N}(\varphi)
.
Next, for
\psi\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{W}
, let
W^{\psi}(t)\doteq W(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(s)ds
,
t\in[0,T]
.

The following is
[
10
, Theorem 8.19]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}_{0}^{T}(H)\times\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable map. Then for
\theta\in(0,\infty)

-\log\mathbb{E}\exp\{-F(W,N^{\theta})\}=\inf_{v=(\psi,\varphi)\in\mathcal{R}}E\left[\theta L^{T}(v)+F(W^{\sqrt{\theta}},N^{\theta\psi})\right],

where
\mathcal{R}\in\{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{b},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\}
.

Remark 6.3
.

In a similar manner as in Section
3
one can give a variational representation for functionals that, in addition to depending on a Brownian motion and a PRM, also depend on another independent random variable. Also, in a manner similar to Section
4
one can obtain a variational representation for functionals that depend on an infinite path of a Brownian motion and a PRM, namely a function of
\{(W(t),N^{\theta}((0,,t]\times\cdot)),0\leq t<\infty\}
. We omit the details.

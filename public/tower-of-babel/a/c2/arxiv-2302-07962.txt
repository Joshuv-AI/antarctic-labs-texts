NuHamil : A numerical code to generate nuclear two- and three-body matrix elements from chiral effective field theory
Takayuki Miyagi aff1,aff2,aff3,em:at

Abstract
The applicability of nuclear ab initio calculations has rapidly extended over the past decades. However, starting research projects is still challenging due to the required numerical expertise in the generation of underlying nuclear interaction matrix elements and many-body calculations. To ease the first issue, in this paper we introduce the numerical code NuHamil to generate the nucleon-nucleon (NN) and three-nucleon (3N) matrix elements expressed in a spherical harmonic-oscillator basis, inputs of many-body calculations. The ground-state energies for the selected doubly closed shell nuclei are calculated with the no-core shell-model (NCSM) and in-medium similarity renormalization group (IMSRG). The code is written in modern Fortran, and OpenMP+MPI hybrid parallelization is available for the 3N matrix-element calculations.

Description of problem:

Nucleon-nucleon (NN) and three-nucleon (3N) matrix elements are essential inputs in nuclear
ab initio
calculations.
However, developing a numerical code to generate the matrix elements is a demanding task.
Preparing the input matrix elements is one of the main barriers to begin studies.

Method of solution:

The
NuHamil
code has the capability to generate both NN and 3N matrix elements expressed in a single-particle harmonic-oscillator (HO) basis, which can be used as inputs for most of the
ab initio
calculation methods. The jobs can be managed by a simple Python script.

Introduction

The dynamics of the atomic nucleus are governed by the strong interaction, whose fundamental theory is described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD).
Since the quarks are tightly confined in a nucleon, it is well established that nuclear Hamiltonians associated with the interactions between nucleons are a good starting point to understand nuclear structure and reactions.
The study of nuclear interaction models has a long story starting from the pion-exchange theory
Machleidt 2017
.
The quantitative understanding of nuclear interactions is an open problem.
In the past decade, interactions based on chiral effective-field theory (EFT)
Epelbaum et al. 2009
;
Machleidt and Entem 2011
have become the standard starting point of
ab initio
many-body calculations.
Chiral EFT-based interactions have several advantages over other interactions such as AV18
Wiringa et al. 1995
and CD-Bonn potentials
Machleidt 2001
.
For example, a systematic expansion is possible by ordering the diagrams according to the power counting, suggesting the possibility of an uncertainty quantification due to the truncation in the expansion
Epelbaum et al. 2015
;
Furnstahl et al. 2015
;
Melendez et al. 2017
;
Melendez et al. 2019
.
Further, many-nucleon interactions naturally appear at higher order, explaining the hierarchy of many-body terms.
In nuclear physics, it is well known that 3N interactions play an important role (see for example Ref.
Hebeler 2021
as a recent review).
With the progress in nuclear interactions and methodological developments in many-body problems, nuclear
ab initio
studies are well motivated.
Nowadays, the applicability extends over the nuclear chart
Hergert 2020
and recently reached to the heaviest known doubly-magic system
208
Pb
Hu et al. 2022
, and further applications are expected.

To perform
ab initio
calculations, the matrix elements of nuclear Hamiltonians (and relevant operators) are essential.
However, developing a numerically efficient code for the matrix-element generation requires expert knowledge and can be a barrier for those entering the field.
The goal of the
NuHamil
code is to provide a simple way to generate NN and 3N matrix elements expressed in a spherical HO basis, applicable for basis-expansion methods, such as the no-core shell model (NCSM)
Barrett et al. 2013
, coupled-cluster method
Hagen et al. 2014
, self-consistent Green’s function method
SomÃ 2020
, in-medium similarity renormalization group
Hergert et al. 2016
approach, and man-body perturbation theory
Tichai et al. 2020
.
The code currently supports the input formats of the open-source
BIGSTICK

Johnson et al.
and
imsrg++

Stroberg
codes for the NCSM and IMSRG calculations, respectively.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
2
, we clarify the input NN and 3N matrix elements for the many-body calculations and briefly show how to compute them with the single-particle product state in the HO basis.
Also, we discuss the free-space similarity renormalization group (SRG) prescription to soften the nuclear interactions in Sec.
3
.
We show some benchmark many-body calculation results with NCSM and IMSRG calculations in Sec.
4
.
The usage of the code and the conclusion are given in Sec.
5
and Sec.
6
, respectively.

2
Matrix elements of Hamiltonian

Here, we review how the NN and 3N matrix elements enter in the many-body problem.
Our numerical goal is to solve the non-relativistic many-body Schrödinger equation
H|\Psi_{n}\rangle=E_{n}|\Psi_{n}\rangle
, with the intrinsic Hamiltonian with up to 3N terms

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{A-1}{A}\sum_{i}\frac{\boldsymbol{p}^{2}_{i}}{2m}+\sum_{i<j}(V^{{\rm NN}}_{ij}-T^{\rm NN}_{ij})+\sum_{i<j<k}V^{3N}_{ijk}.

(1)

Here,
\boldsymbol{p}_{i}
is the momentum vector of
i
th nucleon, and
m
is the nucleon mass.
The factor
(A-1)/A
in the first term and
T^{\rm NN}_{ij}
is from the subtraction of the center-of-mass (cm) kinetic term.
A
is the nucleon number of the system.
The terms
V^{\rm NN}_{ij}
and
V^{\rm 3N}_{ijk}
are the NN and 3N interactions, respectively.

2.1
Second-quantized representation

To proceed with many-body calculations with basis-expansion methods, we begin with the expression by the second quantization.
To this end, we first define creation and annihilation operators for a nucleon in an HO orbit
\tilde{p}
:
c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}}
and
c_{\tilde{p}}
.
The subscript
\tilde{p}
is a collective index specifying the HO orbit and defined as
\tilde{p}=\{n_{p},l_{p},j_{p},m_{p},t_{z,p}\}
.
Here,
n_{p}
,
l_{p}
,
j_{p}
,
m_{p}
, and
t_{z,p}
are the nodal quantum number, orbital angular momentum, total angular momentum,
z
-component of
j_{p}
, and the
z
-component of the isospin distinguishing protons and neutrons, respectively.
Note that proton (neutron) states are labeled as
t_{z}=-1/2

(1/2)
.
In nuclear physics, the use of an HO basis is particularly useful since the coordinate transformation coefficient is well known
Moshinsky 1959
;
Trlifaj 1972
;
Kamuntavičius et al. 2001
, as will be shown in later.
The creation and annihilation operators satisfy the anticommutation relations

\{c_{\tilde{p}},c_{\tilde{q}}\}=0,\,\{c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}},c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}}\}=0,\,\{c_{\tilde{p}},c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}}\}=\delta_{\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}.

(2)

The object
\delta_{\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}
is defined by products of Kronecker’s delta and is written as

\delta_{\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}=\delta_{n_{p}n_{q}}\delta_{l_{p}l_{q}}\delta_{j_{p}j_{q}}\delta_{m_{p}m_{q}}\delta_{t_{z,p}t_{z,q}}.

(3)

Applying the creation operators to the nucleon vacuum state
|0\rangle
, one can define antisymmetrized states.
For example, one-, two-, and three-nucleon states can be written as

\displaystyle|\tilde{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}}|0\rangle,

(4)

\displaystyle|\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}}|0\rangle,

(5)

\displaystyle|\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}\rangle

\displaystyle=c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{r}}|0\rangle.

(6)

Using the creation and annihilation operators, an arbitrary
n
-body operator
O^{[n]}
can be expressed as

O^{[n]}=\left(\frac{1}{n!}\right)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{n}}\sum_{\tilde{p}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}_{n}}O_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{n}\tilde{p}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}_{n}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{n}}c_{\tilde{p}_{n}}\cdots c_{\tilde{p}_{1}}

(7)

The object
O_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{n}\tilde{p}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}_{n}}
is a shorthand notation for the operator matrix element
\langle\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}^{\prime}_{n}|O^{[n]}|\tilde{p}_{1}\cdots\tilde{p}_{n}\rangle
.
In the same way, the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) can be quantized as

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{p}}T_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{p}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}c_{\tilde{p}}

(8)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2!}\right)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}(V^{\rm NN}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}-T^{\rm NN}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}})c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}c_{\tilde{q}}c_{\tilde{p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{3!}\right)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{r}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}}V^{\rm 3N}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{r}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}c^{{\dagger}}_{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}c_{\tilde{r}}c_{\tilde{q}}c_{\tilde{p}},

using the matrix elements of one-body kinetic term
T_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{p}}
, two-body kinetic term
T^{\rm NN}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}
, NN interaction
V^{\rm NN}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}
, and 3N interaction
V^{\rm 3N}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{r}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}}
.

2.2
J
-coupled scheme

The number of matrix elements defined in Eq. (
8
) is greatly reduced by exploiting the rotational symmetry of the Hamiltonian.
To introduce a smaller set of matrix elements, we define the
J
-coupled two- and three-body states using the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient
\mathcal{C}^{j_{1}j_{2}J}_{m_{1}m_{2}m_{1}+m_{2}}
:

\displaystyle|pq:JM\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{1+\delta_{pq}}}\sum_{m_{p}m_{q}}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p}j_{q}J}_{m_{p}m_{q}M}|\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\rangle,

(9)

\displaystyle|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{p}m_{q}m_{r}}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p}j_{q}J_{pq}}_{m_{p}m_{q}m_{p}+m_{q}}\mathcal{C}^{J_{pq}j_{r}J}_{m_{p}+m_{q}m_{r}M}|\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}\rangle.

(10)

Here,
p
,
q
, and
r
are the quantum number set without
m
, i.e.
p=\{n_{p},l_{p},j_{p},t_{z,p}\}
, and an additional Kronecker’s delta product is introduced as

\delta_{pq}=\delta_{n_{p}n_{q}}\delta_{l_{p}l_{q}}\delta_{j_{p}j_{q}}\delta_{t_{z,p}t_{z,q}}.

(11)

Note that the factor
\sqrt{1/(1+\delta_{pq})}
in the two-body state is for the normalization so that we have
\langle pq:J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|pq:JM\rangle=\delta_{J^{\prime}J}\delta_{M^{\prime}M}
.
On the other hand, such normalization factor is not usually included in the three-body state.
For the three-body state, one can define another state by a different angular momentum coupling order, which should be related with the Wigner’s
6j
-symbol.
In this paper, the first and second indices are always coupled first, and then the third index is coupled.
In practical applications, we need permutations of the indices of the states to further reduce the storage requirement.
For the two-body state, it is given by

|qp:JM\rangle=-(-1)^{j_{p}+j_{q}-J}|pq:JM\rangle.

(12)

Likewise, permutations of the indices in the three-body state are given by

\displaystyle|qrp:J_{qr}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{J_{pq}}(-1)^{j_{q}+j_{r}+J_{qr}}\sqrt{[J_{pq}][J_{qr}]}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p}&j_{q}&J_{pq}\\
j_{r}&J&J_{qr}\end{array}\right\}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle,

\displaystyle|rpq:J_{pr}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{J_{pq}}(-1)^{j_{p}+j_{q}+J_{pq}}\sqrt{[J_{pq}][J_{pr}]}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p}&j_{q}&J_{pq}\\
J&j_{r}&J_{pr}\end{array}\right\}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle,

\displaystyle|qpr:J_{pq}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{j_{p}+j_{q}-J_{pq}}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle,

(17)

\displaystyle|rqp:J_{qr}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{J_{pq}}\sqrt{[J_{pq}][J_{qr}]}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p}&j_{q}&J_{pq}\\
j_{r}&J&J_{qr}\end{array}\right\}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle,

\displaystyle|prq:J_{pr}JM\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{J_{pq}}(-1)^{j_{q}+j_{r}+J_{pr}+J_{pq}}\sqrt{[J_{pq}][J_{pr}]}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p}&j_{q}&J_{pq}\\
J&j_{r}&J_{pr}\end{array}\right\}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle.

Here, Wigner’s
6j
-symbol with the standard notation
Varshalovich et al. 1988
and
[x]=2x+1
are introduced.
With the
J
-coupled states, the two- and three-body matrix elements can be introduced as

\langle p^{\prime}q^{\prime}:J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|O^{[2]}|pq:JM\rangle
and
\langle p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}:J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|O^{[3]}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle
, respectively.
Because of the rotational invariance of the Hamiltonian, the Hamiltonian matrix is
M
-independent and diagonal with respect to
J
and
M
.
Therefore, we introduce a shorthand notation for the matrix elements.

\displaystyle T_{p^{\prime}p}

\displaystyle=\langle p|T|q\rangle,

(22)

\displaystyle T^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}

\displaystyle=\langle p^{\prime}q^{\prime}:JM|T^{\rm NN}|pq:JM\rangle,

(23)

\displaystyle V^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}

\displaystyle=\langle p^{\prime}q^{\prime}:J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|V^{\rm NN}|pq:JM\rangle,

(24)

\displaystyle V^{{\rm 3N},J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J_{pq}J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}pqr}

\displaystyle=\langle p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}:J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J^{\prime}M^{\prime}|V^{\rm 3N}|pqr:J_{pq}JM\rangle.

(25)

Since the uncoupled matrix elements with tilde indices can be computed from the
J
-coupled matrix elements, only calculating the
J
-coupled matrix elements is sufficient for many-body calculations.
The relation between the uncoupled and
J
-coupled matrix elements are the following.

\displaystyle T_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{p}}

\displaystyle=T_{p^{\prime}p}\delta_{m_{p^{\prime}}m_{p}},

(26)

\displaystyle T^{{\rm NN}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p^{\prime}}j_{q^{\prime}}J}_{m_{p^{\prime}}m_{q^{\prime}}M}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p}j_{q}J}_{m_{p}m_{q}M}T^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq},

(27)

\displaystyle V^{{\rm NN}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p^{\prime}}j_{q^{\prime}}J}_{m_{p^{\prime}}m_{q^{\prime}}M}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p}j_{q}J}_{m_{p}m_{q}M}V^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq},

(28)

\displaystyle V^{{\rm 3N}}_{\tilde{p}^{\prime}\tilde{q}^{\prime}\tilde{r}^{\prime}\tilde{p}\tilde{q}\tilde{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{JJ_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J_{pq}}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p^{\prime}}j_{q^{\prime}}J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}}_{m_{p^{\prime}}m_{q^{\prime}}M_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}}\mathcal{C}^{j_{p}j_{q}J_{pq}}_{m_{p}m_{q}M_{pq}}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\times\mathcal{C}^{J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}j_{r^{\prime}}J}_{M_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}m_{r^{\prime}}M}\mathcal{C}^{J_{pq}j_{r}J}_{M_{pq}m_{r}M}V^{{\rm 3N},J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J_{pq}J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}pqr}.

(29)

2.3
Matrix elements of kinetic terms

The one-body kinetic matrix element
T_{p^{\prime}p}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{p^{\prime}p}

\displaystyle=\frac{A-1}{A}\delta_{l_{p^{\prime}}l_{p}}\delta_{j_{p^{\prime}}j_{p}}\delta_{t_{z,p^{\prime}}t_{z,p}}\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(2n_{p}+l_{p}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sqrt{n_{p}\left(n_{p}+l_{p}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}-1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sqrt{\left(n_{p}+1\right)\left(n_{p}+l_{p}+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}+1}\right].

Note that the one-body kinetic operator takes the tridiagonal form.
Also, the two-body kinetic matrix element can be computed through the non-antisymmetrized
J
-coupled matrix element
\bar{T}^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{q^{\prime}}+j_{p}+J}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{Am}

(31)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p^{\prime}}&j_{q^{\prime}}&J\\
j_{q}&j_{p}&1\end{array}\right\}\langle p^{\prime}\|\nabla\|p\rangle\langle q^{\prime}\|\nabla\|q\rangle,

with the reduced matrix element of the gradient operator, which is given by

\displaystyle\langle p^{\prime}\|\nabla\|p\rangle=(-1)^{l_{p}^{\prime}+j_{p}+1/2}\frac{1}{b}\sqrt{[j_{p^{\prime}}][j_{p}]}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{p^{\prime}}&j_{p}&1\\
l_{p}&l_{p^{\prime}}&1/2\end{array}\right\}

(32)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sqrt{(l_{p}+1)(n_{p}+l_{p}+3/2)}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}}\delta_{l_{p^{\prime}}l_{p}+1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 20.00003pt+\sqrt{(l_{p}+1)n_{p}}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}-1}\delta_{l_{p^{\prime}}l_{p}+1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 20.00003pt+\sqrt{l_{p}(n_{p}+l_{p}+1/2)}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}}\delta_{l_{p^{\prime}}l_{p}-1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 20.00003pt+\sqrt{l_{p}(n_{p}+1)}\delta_{n_{p^{\prime}}n_{p}+1}\delta_{l_{p^{\prime}}l_{p}-1}\right].

Here, the HO length parameter
b^{2}\equiv\hbar/m\omega
is introduced with the HO frequency
\omega
.
The antisymmetrized matrix element is obtained as

T^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}})(1+\delta_{pq})}}\left[\bar{T}^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}-(-1)^{j_{p}+j_{q}-J}\bar{T}^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}qp}\right],

(33)

with

\delta_{pq}=\delta_{n_{p}n_{q}}\delta_{l_{p}l_{q}}\delta_{j_{p}j_{q}}\delta_{t_{z,p}t_{z,q}}.

(34)

The main tasks remaining are to compute the matrix elements
V^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}
and
V^{{\rm 3N},J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J_{pq}J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}pqr}
.

2.4
Nucleon-nucleon matrix elements

We begin with the NN matrix element.
One might think that the matrix element can be calculated directly from the integral using the single-particle HO wave function.
It is actually done in quantum chemistry.
However, this would be a computationally expensive task since functional forms of NN interactions are complicated.
Instead, the Talmi-Moshinsky transformation is widely used in nuclear physics:

\displaystyle V^{{\rm NN},J}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}pq}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}J^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}S}\sum_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}nl}T^{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}J}_{N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}n^{\prime}l^{\prime}SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}

(35)

\displaystyle\times V^{SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}nl}\ T^{pqJ}_{N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}nlSJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}.

The quantum numbers introduced for the transformation
N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}
,
L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}
,
n
,
l
,
S
, and
J^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}
are the NN cm radial quantum number, NN cm orbital angular momentum, relative radial quantum number, relative orbital angular momentum, total spin, and total angular momentum of the relative motion, respectively.
The transformation coefficient
T^{pqJ}_{N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}nlSJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}
is

\displaystyle T^{pqJ}_{N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}nlSJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}+l+S+J}\sqrt{[j_{p}][j_{q}][S][J^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}]}

(36)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\Lambda}[\Lambda]\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}l_{p}&1/2&j_{p}\\
l_{q}&1/2&j_{q}\\
\Lambda&S&J\end{array}\right\}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}&l&\Lambda\\
S&J&J^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\langle N^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}L^{\rm NN}_{\rm cm}nl:\Lambda|n_{p}l_{p}n_{q}l_{q}:\Lambda\rangle_{1}.

In the above equation, 9
j
-symbol is used with the standard notation
Varshalovich et al. 1988
.
The symbol
\langle NLnl:\Lambda|n_{1}l_{1}n_{2}l_{2}:\Lambda\rangle_{d}
is the HO bracket defined with the notation in Ref.
Kamuntavičius et al. 2001
.
The inner summations in Eq. (
35
) can be performed with an efficient matrix multiplication.
Note that the antisymmetrization is not taken into account here.
However, it is trivial and can be done by multiplying the factor
f_{pq}
to Eq. (
36
):

f_{pq}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&t_{z,p}\neq t_{z,q}\\
\sqrt{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta_{pq})}}[1+(-1)^{l+S}],&t_{z,p}=t_{z,q}\end{array}\right..

(37)

The NN matrix element in the relative HO basis
V^{SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}nl}
can be obtained through the integral:

\displaystyle V^{SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}nl}

\displaystyle=\int d\pi^{\prime}_{1}d\pi_{1}\ \pi^{\prime 2}_{1}\pi^{2}_{1}R_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}}(\pi^{\prime}_{1})R_{nl}(\pi_{1})

(38)

\displaystyle\times V^{SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}_{l^{\prime}l}(\pi^{\prime}_{1},\pi_{1}),

with the radial HO wave function:

\displaystyle R_{nl}(\pi_{1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}b\sqrt{\frac{2b\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+l+3/2)}}

(39)

\displaystyle\times(\pi_{1}b)^{l}e^{-\pi^{2}_{1}b^{2}/2}L^{(l+1/2)}_{n}(\pi^{2}_{1}b^{2}).

The gamma function
\Gamma(x)
and associated Laguerre polynomial
L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
are introduced.
The momentum
\pi_{1}
is
\pi_{1}=|(\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2})|/\sqrt{2}
, consistent with the definition of the HO bracket.
Note that
\pi_{1}
is different from the usual relative momentum definition
p=|\boldsymbol{p}_{1}-\boldsymbol{p}_{2}|/2
.

The
NuHamil
code requires the input file for
V^{SJ^{\rm NN}_{\rm rel}}_{l^{\prime}l}(p^{\prime},p)
stored as a function of
p^{\prime}
and
p
.
Some selected interactions are given in the
input_nn_files
directory.
The available NN interactions are

4
LO with 500 MeV regulator cutoff by Entem–Machleidt–Nosyk
Entem et al. 2017
, N
3
LO with 500 MeV regulator cutoff by Entem–Machleidt
Entem and Machleidt 2003
, N
2
LO
opt

Ekström et al. 2013
, N
2
LO
sat

Ekström et al. 2015
, and
\Delta
-full EFT series by the Gothenburg–Oak Ridge collaboration
Jiang et al. 2020
.

2.5
Three-nucleon matrix elements

For computational reasons, the 3N matrix elements are calculated within the isospin formalism.
The matrix elements with the proton-neutron basis can be obtained through the
JT
-coupled matrix element
V^{{\rm 3N},J_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}J_{pq}J,T_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}T_{pq}T}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}r^{\prime}pqr}
:

Similarity renormalization group

The momentum scales of chiral EFT interactions are significantly lower than those of the other potential models such as AV18
Wiringa et al. 1995
and CD-Bonn
Machleidt 2001
.
However, the momentum scale is not sufficiently low to obtain converged results in the many-body calculations.
To accelerate convergence, one sometimes softens nuclear interactions.
Softening procedures are well summarized for example in Ref.
Bogner et al. 2010
.
Here, we briefly review the widely used similarity renormalization group (SRG) approach.

In the SRG, we consider a unitary transformation depending on a continuous parameter
\alpha
:

H(\alpha)=U^{{\dagger}}(\alpha)HU(\alpha).

(54)

The SRG flow equation can be obtained by differentiating both sides:

\displaystyle\frac{dH(\alpha)}{d\alpha}

\displaystyle=[\eta(\alpha),H(\alpha)],\quad\eta(\alpha)=\frac{dU^{{\dagger}}(\alpha)}{d\alpha}U(\alpha).

(55)

The antihermitian operator
\eta(\alpha)
is known as the generator of the flow equation and can be chosen flexibly
Wegner 1994
.
The most widely used choice is
\eta(\alpha)=[T_{\rm kin},H(\alpha)]
with the kinetic operator
T_{\rm kin}
, which guarantees the suppression of the coupling between low and high momenta.
The flow equation (
55
) is integrated until the coupling is sufficiently suppressed.
Since the unitary transformation does not change the eigenvalues, the SRG can be regarded as a reshuffling of the NN, 3N, and many-body sectors.
In other words, the many-body interactions are induced by the SRG evolution even if the original interaction includes only NN interactions.

In practical applications, we extract the SRG-evolved interactions with the subtraction method (see Ref.
Roth et al. 2014
for example).
For NN, the Hamiltonian
H_{\rm NN}=T_{\rm NN}+V_{\rm NN}
is evolved, and the evolved NN interaction is given as
V_{\rm NN}(\alpha)=H_{\rm NN}(\alpha)-T_{\rm kin}
.
Note that
T_{\rm kin}
and
V_{\rm NN}
are the NN kinetic and interaction operators, respectively.
From the definition of the flow equation,
V_{\rm NN}(\alpha)
reproduces the two-body observables obtained through the original interaction
V_{\rm NN}
.
However, this is not true for three- and many-body observables due to missing induced many-body forces, and the many-body observables have an artificial
\alpha
dependence, showing how much the unitarity of the transformation is broken in the many-body space.
To obtain more
\alpha
-independent result, one has to include induced 3N interaction extracted from the 3N evolution.

For 3N, the starting Hamiltonian is
H_{\rm 3N}=T_{\rm 3N}+V^{[3]}_{\rm NN}
, where
T_{\rm 3N}
is the 3N kinetic operator, and
V_{\rm NN}^{[3]}
is the NN interaction embedded into the 3N space.
Note that one can add an initial 3N term if required.
The induced 3N term can be obtained as
V_{\rm 3N,ind}(\alpha)=H_{\rm 3N}(\alpha)-T_{\rm 3N}-V_{\rm NN}^{[3]}(\alpha)
.
Here,
V_{\rm NN}^{[3]}(\alpha)
is
V_{\rm NN}(\alpha)
obtained from the NN evolution and embedded into 3N space.
As seen in the NN evolution, the 3N evolution preserves three-body observables.
The same procedure can be applied for many-body terms, and the many-body evolution is needed until the
\alpha
-dependence of the many-body observables becomes weak enough.
However, in practice, even the four-body SRG evolution is too expensive to do due to the resulting basis dimension and the cost of antisymmetrizing the basis.
In the
NuHamil
code, the SRG evolution can be performed in the NN and 3N sectors, the current state-of-the-art.

The unitary transformation can be obtained from the flow equation for the transformation operator:

\frac{dU(\alpha)}{d\alpha}=-U(\alpha)\eta(\alpha).

(56)

However, a computationally more moderate way is used in practice, and
the unitary transformation is obtained as

U(\alpha)=\sum_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}(\alpha)|,

(57)

with the eigenstates of the original and evolved Hamiltonians:

\displaystyle H|\psi_{k}\rangle=E_{k}|\psi_{k}\rangle,\quad H(\alpha)|\psi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle=E_{k}|\psi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle.

(58)

Note that the relative phase of
|\psi_{k}\rangle
and
|\psi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle
cannot be determined in general, which affect the sign of the matrix element of the transformation operator.
Since the SRG transformation does not change the wave function drastically, the relative phase is fixed such that
\langle\psi_{k}|\psi_{k}(\alpha)\rangle\geq 0
.
In the same way as for the Hamiltonian, the induced three-body term of an operator can be computed in the

The end point of the flow equation is usually parametrized by the momentum scale

In the code, the SRG evolution is done in a relative-coordinate HO space because the consistent evolution of the other operators is straightforward.
This means that the evolution is done in the truncated HO space, and the

The

N_{\rm max}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\max(2n+l),&\text{NN system}\\
\max(2n_{12}+l_{12}+2n_{3}+l_{3}),&\text{3N system}\end{array}\right..

(59)

The UV momentum scale in the employed

space is roughly estimated as
p_{\rm UV}\sim\sqrt{2N_{\rm max}m\omega}

Furnstahl et al. 2012
, and we expect that the

should be increased until

is sufficiently larger than the cutoff scale of the interaction, typically
500

Although we can take sufficiently large

for the NN evolution
3

, the 3N

6
Summary and future perspective

We introduce the
NuHamil
code to generate NN and 3N matrix elements.
The jobs can be managed with a simple Python script.
The available NN interactions are LO – N
4
LO with 500 MeV regulator cutoff by Entem–Machleidt–Nosyk
Entem et al. 2017
, N
3
LO with 500 MeV regulator cutoff by Entem–Machleidt
Entem and Machleidt 2003
, N
2
LO
opt

Ekström et al. 2013
,
N^{2}
LO
sat

Ekström et al. 2015
, and
\Delta
-full EFT series by Gothenburg–Oak Ridge collaboration
Jiang et al. 2020
.
The code can generate locally regulated 3N interactions
Navrátil 2007
.
Additional input files are needed for non-locally regulated 3N interactions.
The code also supports the free-space NN and 3N SRG evolution, and the consistent evolution of the other operators is implemented.
The output files can be used for the NCSM calculations with the
BIGSTICK
code
Johnson et al.
and IMSRG calculations with the
imsrg++
code
Stroberg
.

For a comprehensive understanding of nuclear structure, interactions between a nucleus and external field should be addressed.
For example the electromagnetic observables are results of the nucleus-photon interaction and are related with the multipole components of the electromagnetic current operators.
Although we know that higher-order contributions are essential, see Ref.
Pastore et al. 2013
;
Gysbers et al. 2019
for example, the LO current is used in most calculations due to the complexity of the matrix element calculations.
As a future development, we plan to implement the higher-order current operators, including the two-body contributions.

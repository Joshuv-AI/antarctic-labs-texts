An infinite class of quantum codes derived from duadic constacyclic codes
Reza Dastbasteh, Josu Etxezarreta Martinez, Andrew Nemec, Antonio deMarti iOlius, Pedro Crespo Bofill

Abstract
We present a family of quantum stabilizer codes using the structure of duadic constacyclic codes over 𝔽 4 \mathbb{F}_{4} . Within this family, quantum codes can possess varying dimensions, and their minimum distances are lower bounded by a square root bound. For each fixed dimension, this allows us to construct an infinite sequence of binary quantum codes with a growing minimum distance. Additionally, we prove that this family of quantum codes includes an infinite subclass of degenerate codes. We also introduce a technique for extending splittings of duadic constacyclic codes, providing new insights into the minimum distance and minimum odd-like weight of specific duadic constacyclic codes. Finally, we provide numerical examples of some quantum codes with short lengths within this family.



Keywords:

quantum code, degenerate code, duadic code, cyclic code, constacyclic code, minimum distance bound.

1
Introduction

Quantum error-correcting codes
, or simply quantum codes, employ multiple physical qubits affected by decoherence to generate stable logical qubits for the purpose of storing and processing quantum information without errors.
Among the most extensively studied quantum codes are the
stabilizer codes
, which are constructed using certain dual-containing classical codes.
Indeed, many families of quantum codes with good properties have been established using the stabilizer approach
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In this paper, we exclusively focus on binary quantum codes.
We will denote the parameters of a binary quantum code that encodes
k
logical qubits into
n
physical qubits and has a minimum distance of
d
as
[\![n,k,d]\!]_{2}
. Such a quantum code is capable of correcting any error of weight at most
\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
.
Stabilizer codes exhibit a quantum unique effect named degeneracy
[
7
]
, which has no analogous concept in classical coding theory.
A quantum stabilizer code is called degenerate if there exists a non-trivial error of weight less than
d
in the stabilizer group that acts trivially on the code
[
8
,
9
]
.

In general, error patterns leading to non-trivial syndromes necessitate active correction of the physical qubits in a quantum code.
This active correction procedure may introduce supplementary errors into the system, due to the inherent noise introduced by quantum gates
[
9
,
10
]
.
Consequently, construction of degenerate codes becomes significant. For example, in a depolarizing channel where low-weight errors are more likely to happen, the occurrence of numerous low-weight errors that act trivially on the code can help mitigate the potential introduction of additional errors during active correction.
Note also that to prevent the accumulation of errors in a quantum code, one must be able to measure the error syndrome frequently enough. This is
accomplished by a syndrome measurement circuit that uses a sequence of quantum operators.
Degenerate codes typically have a shorter-depth syndrome measurement circuit compared to non-degenerate codes with the same parameters, enabling more efficient and reliable syndrome measurement
[
11
]
.
In addition to making up a large portion of the class of quantum LDPC codes
[
12
,
13
]
, degenerate codes are also useful in the constructions of quantum-classical hybrid codes
[
14
,
15
]
, permutation-invariant quantum codes
[
16
]
, and codes for amplitude damping channels
[
17
,
18
]
.

In general, constructing non-degenerate stabilizer codes is less complicated due to their direct connection to classical codes
[
8
]
.
As such, there are not many families of degenerate stabilizer codes that are constructed from classical algebraic codes such as cyclic and constacyclic codes. Another main difficulties of degenerate codes is in determining the true minimum distance or even establishing bounds, especially a lower bound, on the minimum distances of these codes.
One of the main techniques to construct quantum stabilizer codes is
the so-called Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) construction
[
19
,
20
]
. While the CSS construction has proven to be excellent, non-CSS stabilizer codes can be advantageous, for example, for operating over depolarizing channels. This comes from the fact that CSS codes obviate the correlation that exists between Pauli
\mathrm{X}
and
\mathrm{Z}
errors in the form of
\mathrm{Y}
errors. This leads to the fact that such family of codes is limited by the so-called CSS lower bound
[
21
]
.

Motivated by the above observations and previous works in the literature such as
[
10
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
, we design a family of non-CSS quantum stabilizer codes with various interesting features.
Firstly, it generalizes the construction presented in
[
10
]
by producing (degenerate) quantum codes with a varying dimension and growing minimum distances. Secondly, our constructed quantum codes satisfy a square root minimum distance lower bound. Moreover, one can find a sharper lower bound or even compute the true minimum distance of certain codes constructed using our proposed extended splitting method (introduced in Section
4.1
) in this family. Lastly, we provide a list of degenerate and non-degenerate binary quantum codes constructed using this approach that includes a few currently best-known codes.

Our proposed quantum codes are derived from duadic constacyclic and cyclic codes over the quaternary field. These codes are defined similarly to conventional duadic cyclic codes but offer more freedom in the selection of their defining sets.
The rich algebraic structure of constacyclic and cyclic codes can be highly beneficial for the practical applications of quantum codes that are constructed this way.
This is primarily due to the availability of efficient encoding and decoding algorithms for classical cyclic and constacyclic codes, along with quantum cyclic codes that can be adapted for use with our code
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a review of preliminary concepts such as constacyclic, duadic, and quantum stabilizer codes, which will be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we explore various scenarios under which there exists a splitting for Hermitian dual-containing duadic constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.
Moving to Section
4
, we introduce new classes of quantum codes constructed from duadic constacyclic codes. We also outline the generation of degenerate codes by extending splittings of shorter-length duadic codes to longer-length codes.
Additionally, we give a lower bound for the minimum odd-like distance of duadic codes constructed from extended splittings, and provide numerous examples of binary quantum codes constructed using our approach.

Background

Let
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\{0,1,\omega,\omega^{2}\}
be the finite field of four elements, where
\omega^{2}=\omega+1
. We denote the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{4}
by
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
. Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
and
n
be a positive integer.
A
linear code

C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{4}
-vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
. The
parameters
of a linear code of length
n
, dimension
k
, and minimum (Hamming) distance
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
will be denoted by
[n,k,d]_{4}
.
For each two vectors
x=(x_{0},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1})
and

in
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
, the

of

and

is defined by

\langle x,y\rangle_{h}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x_{i}{y_{i}^{2}}.

The
Hermitian dual
of a linear code

is now defined by

(2.1)

C^{\bot_{h}}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle_{h}=0\ \text{for all}\ y\in C\}.

A linear code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is called Hermitian

(respectively

), if
C^{\bot_{h}}\subseteq C
(respectively
C=C^{\bot_{h}}
).

2.1
Quaternary constacyclic codes

Constacyclic codes are an important class of linear codes that generalizes cyclic codes and inherit many desirable properties from them, making them suitable for various practical applications.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
. A linear code
C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is called
a
-constacyclic
if for each codeword
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in C
, we have
(ac_{n-1},c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in C
.

The value
a
is called the
shift constant
of such constacyclic code. A constacyclic code with the shift constant
a=1
is called
cyclic
. Throughout the rest of this paper we assume that
n
is a positive odd integer.

It is well-known that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and ideals of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{4}[x]/\langle x^{n}-a\rangle
.
Thus each
a
-constacyclic code
C
is generated by a unique monic polynomial
g(x)
such that
g(x)\mid x^{n}-a
. The polynomial
g(x)
is called the
generator polynomial
of
C
.
Therefore, to determine all
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, it is essential to find the irreducible factorization of
x^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

We denote the multiplicative order of
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
by
{\rm ord}(a)
. Since
|\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}|=3
, we have
{\rm ord}(a)\in\{1,3\}
. Let
{\rm ord}(a)=t
and
\alpha
be a
(tn)
-th primitive root of unity in a finite field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{4}
such that
\alpha^{n}=a
. Then all different roots of
x^{n}-a
are in the form
\alpha^{s}
, where
s\in\Omega_{a}
and

(2.2)

\Omega_{a}=\{{kt+1}:0\leq k\leq n-1\}.

For any integer
b
such that
\gcd(n,b)=1
, we denote the multiplicative order of
b
modulo
n
by
{\rm ord}_{n}(b)
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the numerical examples of constacyclic codes throughout
this paper, the
(tn)
-th root of unity
\alpha
is fixed as follows. First we find
z={\rm ord}_{tn}(4)
.
Let
\gamma
be the primitive element of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{4^{z}}

chosen by Magma computer algebra system
[
28
]
.
Set
\alpha_{0}=\gamma^{(4^{z}-1)/(tn)}
, which is a primitive
(tn)
-th root of unity. Then there exists an integer
1\leq i\leq t-1
such that
\alpha_{0}^{in}=a
. Now select
\alpha=\alpha_{0}^{i}
.

For each
s\in\Omega_{a}
, the
4
-cyclotomic coset
of
s
modulo
tn
is defined by the set

Z(s)=\{(4^{i}s)\bmod{tn}:0\leq i\leq r-1\},

where
r
is the smallest integer such that
4^{r}s\equiv s\pmod{tn}
. The smallest element of each
4
-cyclotomic coset is called its
coset leader
.
Moreover, there exist coset leaders
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\in\Omega_{a}
such that
\{Z(b_{i}):1\leq i\leq m\}
forms a partition for
\Omega_{a}
. Thus one can find the following irreducible factorization of
x^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
:

x^{n}-a=\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x),

where

f_{i}(x)=\prod_{\ell\in Z(b_{i})}(x-\alpha^{\ell}).

A common and also computationally efficient approach to represent an
a
-constacyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the generator polynomial
g(x)
is by its
defining set
, which is
A\subseteq\Omega_{a}
such that:

g(\alpha^{s})=0
if and only if
s\in A
.

Defining set of an
a
-constacyclic code is a unique (as
\alpha
is already fixed) union of
4
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
tn
.

Note also that
{\rm ord}(\omega)={\rm ord}(\omega^{2})=3
and by
[
29
, Corollary 3]
the families of
\omega
-constacyclic and
\omega^{2}
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are monomially equivalent.
That is, one is obtained from the other by permuting and rescaling the coordinates by a nonzero value.
Monomially equivalent codes share the same parameters and various other identical features, such as the weight distribution.
Therefore, from now on, we only consider
\omega
-constacyclic codes in our discussions.
Monomial equivalence of cyclic and constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is discussed below.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer. If
\gcd(3,n)=1
, then
\omega
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are monomially equivalent.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
30
, Theorem 15]
.

\square

Thus only when
3\mid n
, the families of
\omega
-constacyclic codes and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
can have different parameters. Motivated by this result and the fact that both of the mentioned monomial equivalences preserve the Hermitian inner product, for the rest of this paper, we only consider cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
when
\gcd(3,n)=1
, and we consider both families separately for other lengths.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
and
{\rm ord}(a)=t
. A
multiplier

\mu_{b}
on
\mathbb{Z}/(nt)\mathbb{Z}
is defined by
\mu_{b}(x)=bx\mod{(nt)}
for some integer

such that
\gcd(nt,b)=1
. Multipliers act as special permutations on
\mathbb{Z}/(nt)\mathbb{Z}
.
The following theorem provides criteria for Hermitian dual-containment of constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
be an
a
-constacyclic code with the defining set
A
for some

. Then

if and only if
A\cap\mu_{-2}(A)=\emptyset.

Proof.

The proof of the cyclic case (

) follows from
[
31
, Theorem 4.4.16]
. When
a\in\{\omega,\omega^{2}\}
, the proof follows by choosing
q=4
in
[
32
, Lemma
2.2
]
.

\square

We finish this subsection by recalling the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem (BCH) minimum distance lower bound for
a
-constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
. We denote the minimum distance of a code
C
by

.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
with
t={\rm ord}(a)
. Let

be an
a
-constacyclic code of length

\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the defining set

\{\ell+jt:0\leq j\leq\delta-2\}\subseteq A

This is the case

The BCH bound allows us to simply find a lower bound for the minimum distance of a

2.2
Duadic codes

Duadic codes are an important family of linear codes with rich algebraic and combinatorial properties. Quadratic residue (QR) codes and many examples of linear codes with optimal parameters belong to the family of duadic codes.
Binary duadic codes were first introduced by Leon et al.
[
34
]
, and were later generalized to larger fields by Pless
[
35
,
36
]
.
They are extensively discussed in
[
31
, Chapter 6]
and
[
37
, Section 2.7]
.
There has also been some recent work on the development of duadic constacyclic codes
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
and their applications to construct families of quantum codes with good properties
[
10
,
23
]
.

One way to define duadic codes is using the concept of splitting, see for example
[
31
, Theorem 6.1.5]
and its following discussion. Here, we provide a more tailored definition of splitting (with a few modifications) that is well-suited for our construction of quantum codes and subsequent applications.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
and
X,S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\Omega_{a}
be unions of
4
-cyclotomic cosets such that

1.

X\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2}=\Omega_{a}
,

2.

X\neq\emptyset
,
S_{1}
, and
S_{2}
are disjoint,

3.

and there exists a multiplier
{\mu}_{b}
such that
{\mu}_{b}S_{1}=S_{2}
,
{\mu}_{b}S_{2}=S_{1}
, and
\mu_{b}(Z(s))=Z(s)
for each
s\in X
.

Then the triple
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
is called a
splitting
of
\Omega_{a}
that is given by
\mu_{b}
.

A splitting with
S_{1}=S_{2}=\emptyset
will be called
trivial
, and
non-trivial
otherwise.
Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
such that
{\rm ord}(a)=t
and
X\subseteq\Omega_{a}
. Recall that
\alpha
is a
(tn)
-th primitive root of unity in a finite field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{4}
such that
\alpha^{n}=a
.
A vector
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
will be called
even-like with respect to
X
provided that

c(\alpha^{s})=0\ \text{for each}\ s\in X,

where

c(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x^{i}.

We call an
a
-constacyclic code
even-like with respect to
X
if it has only even-like vectors with respect to
X
. Otherwise we call it an
odd-like code with respect to
X
.
Whenever the set
X
is clear from the context, we simply call the mentioned codes even-like and odd-like.

Note also that almost all our results remain true after change the last condition of Definition
2.6
part 3 to “
\mu_{b}(X)=X
”, except perhaps
the square root minimum distance lower bound (Theorem
4.1
part 5). Given the importance of this square root bound in determining degenerate quantum codes, for the rest of this paper, we adhere to Definition
2.6
as it stands.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer,
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
, and
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
be a non-trivial splitting of
\Omega_{a}
. Then the
a
-constacyclic codes of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the defining sets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
(respectively
S_{1}\cup X
and

) are called odd-like (respectively even-like)

a
-constacyclic

over

.

In the literature, duadic cyclic codes with

have been used to construct infinite families of quantum codes
[
10
,
23
]
with many good properties. In this paper, to avoid reproducing the results of
[
10
,
23
]
, we mainly consider duadic constacyclic codes over

that satisfy

.

Note also that a generalization of duadic codes, called polyadic or

-adic codes, can be defined similarly by considering splittings of

,

2.3
Quantum stabilizer codes

In this section, we briefly recall the stabilizer construction of quantum error correction codes with the concentration on only linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.
Generally, an
[\![n,k,d]\!]_{2}
binary quantum stabilizer code is a
2^{k}
dimensional subspace of the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, and it is capable of correcting each error of weight at most
\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor
. Each stabilizer code is determined by a stabilizer set
[
44
,
8
]
,
\mathcal{S}
, an abelian subgroup of the
n
-qubit Pauli group not containing
-I_{n}

[
45
]
.
The stabilizer code is defined as the simultaneous
+1
-eigenspace of the operators in
\mathcal{S}
.
From a classical coding theory point of view, the stabilizer formalism of quantum codes allows us to use classical additive codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
to construct quantum codes
[
8
]
. A particular case of this, focusing on linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, is stated below.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
C
be a linear
[n,k,d]_{4}
code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
such that
C^{\bot_{h}}\subseteq C
. Then we can construct an
[\![n,2k-n,d^{\prime}]\!]_{2}
binary quantum code, where
d^{\prime}

is the minimum weight in
C\setminus C^{\bot_{h}}
in case
C^{\bot_{h}}\subsetneq C

and
d^{\prime}=d
otherwise.

Proof.

The proof follows from
[
8
, Theorem 2]
.

\square

If the quantum code of Theorem
2.8
has minimum distance
d^{\prime}=d
, then the code is called a
non-degenerate
(pure), otherwise (when
d^{\prime}>d
), it is called
degenerate
(impure). Degenerate stabilizer codes are those that have stabilizer elements of weight less than the minimum distance of the code.
Such non-trivial errors of weight less than
d
are in correspondence to Pauli errors that act trivially on the quantum code
[
9
]
. Degenerate codes have no analogous concept in classical coding theory, since there are no non-trivial errors that preserve the space of a classical code.
The
rate
of an
[\![n,k,d]\!]_{2}
quantum codes is defined by
r=\frac{k}{n}
.
Note that the Hermitian dual-containing requirement comes from the requirement that the elements of a stabilizer subgroup need to commute.
In practice, constructing codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
that meet this condition and have good parameters is typically challenging. An alternative approach is to employ the CSS construction, a special case that involves two binary classical codes, where one is a subset of the other
[
19
,
20
]
.
The codes that will be constructed in this work are non-CSS.

The next construction extends a linear code, which is not necessarily Hermitian dual-containing, to a Hermitian dual-containing linear code of a larger length over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and allows us to construct quantum codes from such linear codes.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
46
]
.
Let
C
be an
{[n,k]_{4}}
linear code and
e=n-k-\dim(C\cap C^{\bot_{h}})
. Then, there exists a
quantum code with parameters
[\![n+e,2k-n+e,d]\!]_{2}
, where

d\geq\min\{d(C),d(C+C^{\bot_{h}})+1\}.

As it is stated in Proposition 2 of
[
46
]
and Theorem 4.5.4 of
[
47
]
, the parameter
e
of Theorem
2.9
, which measures Hermitian dual-containment deficiency of a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, can be easily computed for linear cyclic and constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
using the formula
e=|A\cap\mu_{-2}(A)|
, where
A
is the defining set of such code.
The above results are our main tools in this paper to construct infinite classes of binary quantum codes.

3
Splitting for quaternary duadic constacyclic codes

This section explores the conditions for a duadic constacyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, defined in Definition
2.7
, to be Hermitian dual-containing.
This exploration enables the identification of appropriate duadic constacyclic codes as ingredients for constructing binary quantum codes using the result of Theorem
2.8
.
To enhance the readability of our results, and considering the commonly known nature of the techniques employed in this section, we will defer the proofs and additional details of this section to Appendix
A
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
. Recall that the multiplier
\mu_{-2}
acts as an involution on each
4
-cyclotomic coset inside
\Omega_{a}
. Many of our discussions in this section is based on this fact.
Recall also that
{\rm ord}(a)=t\in\{1,3\}
and

{\rm ord}_{tn}(4)=|Z(1)|
, where the 4-cyclotomic coset
Z(1)
is computed modulo
tn
.
We call a Hermitian dual-containing
a
-constacyclic code
C

minimal
, if there is no other Hermitian dual-containing
a
-constacyclic code
D
such that
D\subsetneq C
.
The next proposition shows that all minimal Hermitian dual-containing
a
-constacyclic codes are duadic codes.

Proposition 3.1
.

An
a
-constacyclic code
C\subsetneq\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
is minimal Hermitian dual-containing if and only if
there exists a non-trivial splitting
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
of
\Omega_{a}
that is given by
\mu_{-2}
and
C
is an odd-like duadic code with respect to
X
.

Inspired by this observation, in the rest of this section, we investigate the existence of a non-trivial splitting for
\Omega_{a}
that is given by
\mu_{-2}
.
The next two lemmas classify when
\Omega_{a}
or one of its 4-cyclotomic cosets is fixed by
\mu_{-2}
, respectively.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
with
{\rm ord}(a)
=t. Then
tn\mid 2^{2j-1}+1
for some
1\leq j\leq r={\rm ord}_{tn}(4)
if and only if
\mu_{-2}(Z(s))=Z(s)
for each
s\in\Omega_{a}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
with
{\rm ord}(a)
=t.
Let
s\in\Omega_{a}
such that
\gcd(n,s)=m
. Then
\mu_{-2}(Z(s))=Z(s)
if and only if
t\frac{n}{m}\mid 2^{2j-1}+1
for some integer
1\leq j\leq r={\rm ord}_{tn}(4)
.

As we mentioned earlier, the families of cyclic and
\omega
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
are monomially equivalent provided that

4
Quantum codes from duadic constacyclic codes

In this section, we construct a new infinite family of quantum codes from duadic constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.
The simple and elegant mathematical structure of duadic constacyclic codes makes it easier to analyze and understand the structure of such quantum codes.
We also establish a square root-type minimum distance lower bound for the quantum codes within this family. A similar square root bound was previously discussed in
[
38
,
40
]
for classical duadic constacyclic codes with
|X|>0
, although we show that it is not stated accurately.
Such square root minimum distance lower bound is only applicable to bound the “minimum odd-like weight” of these codes and does not generally bound the minimum distance of odd-like duadic codes.

Furthermore, we prove that our family of quantum codes encompasses infinite subclasses of degenerate quantum codes with interesting properties.
The quantum codes provided in this section give a generalization of the class of quantum codes presented in
[
10
, Section IV]
, which has a constant dimension of one, i.e. only a single logical qubit is encoded.
We also discuss a method for extending splittings of shorter-length codes to create splittings for longer-length duadic codes. Additionally, we examine the properties of quantum duadic codes constructed using this method.
We also demonstrate numerical examples of quantum codes with good parameters that lie within this family.

To begin, we first provide some properties of duadic constacyclic codes.
The proof closely follows from that of duadic cyclic codes, when
X=\{0\}
,
[
31
, Theorems 4.3.17, 6.1.3, 6.4.2, 6.4.3]
, and we omit certain details.
Let
n
be a positive odd integer,
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
, and
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
be a non-trivial splitting of
\Omega_{a}
.
Let
C
and
D
be odd-like and even-like duadic
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the defining sets
S_{1}
and
S_{1}\cup X
, respectively.
The
minimum odd-like weight
of
C
(with respect to
X
) is defined by

d_{o}(C)=\min\{{\rm wt}(c):c\in C\setminus D\}.

The following theorem summarizes some properties of duadic codes. We specifically focus on Hermitian dual-containing duadic codes, i.e., only duadic codes when
\mu_{-2}
gives the splitting.
Recall that
\mu_{-2}
is a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, allowing it to act on a code

of length

by permuting its coordinates accordingly, which will be denoted as
\mu_{-2}(C)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer,
a\in\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\ast}
, and
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
be a splitting of
\Omega_{a}
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
that is given by
\mu_{-2}
. Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
(respectively

and

) be odd-like (respectively even-like) duadic
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the defining set
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
(respectively
S_{1}\cup X
and
S_{2}\cup X
). Then the following hold for each
1\leq i\leq 2
.

1.

\mu_{-2}(C_{1})=C_{2}
and
\mu_{-2}(D_{1})=D_{2}
(the pair of codes are permutation equivalent).

2.

\dim(C_{i})=\frac{n+|X|}{2}
and
\dim(D_{i})=\frac{n-|X|}{2}
.

3.

C_{1}+C_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}

.

4.

D_{i}^{\bot_{h}}=C_{i}
and
(D_{1}+D_{2})^{\bot_{h}}=C_{1}\cap C_{2}
which is the
a
-constacyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with the defining set
\Omega_{a}\setminus X
.

5.

d_{o}(C_{i})^{2}\geq d(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
{\rm ord}(a)=t
and
\alpha\in K
be an
(nt)
-th primitive root of unity such that
\alpha^{n}=a
, where
K
is a finite field extension of

(1) There exists a parity check matrix for
C_{1}
over

with row vectors in the form

\![1,\alpha^{s},\alpha^{2s},\ldots,\alpha^{ns}]

for each
s\in S_{1}
.
Applying the permutation
\mu_{-2}
to this matrix sends it to a parity check matrix for
C_{2}
. Thus
\mu_{-2}(C_{1})=C_{2}
. A similar proof shows the other case.

(2) Recall that for each
a
-constacyclic code
C
of length
n
with the defining set
A
we have
\dim(C)=n-|A|
. Thus

\dim(C_{i})=n-|S_{i}|=\frac{n+|X|}{2}
and
\dim(D_{i})=n-|S_{i}|-|X|=\frac{n-|X|}{2}
.

(3) The code
C_{1}+C_{2}
has the defining set

, which is also the defining set of the trivial code
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{n}
.
Similarly,
D_{1}\cap D_{2}
has the defining set
(S_{1}\cup X)\cup(S_{2}\cup X)=\Omega_{a}
, which is the defining set of the zero code.

(4) Recall that the Hermitian dual of an
a
-constacyclic code with the defining set

has the defining set

(4.1)

\Omega_{a}\setminus(-2A),

where

is computed modulo
tn
.
Therefore the Hermitian dual of
D_{i}
has the defining set
\Omega_{a}\setminus-2(S_{i}\cup X)=S_{i}
, which is the defining set of
C_{i}
.
Note that
D_{1}+D_{2}
and
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
have the defining sets
(S_{1}\cup X)\cap(S_{2}\cup X)=X
and
S_{1}\cup S_{2}=\Omega_{a}\setminus X
, respectively.
Now applying the relation (
4.1
) shows that
(D_{1}+D_{2})^{\bot_{h}}=C_{1}\cap C_{2}
.

(5) First note that since the pairs
(C_{1},C_{2})
and
(D_{1},D_{2})
are permutation equivalent (part 1) under the same permutation action, and so
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
have the same minimum odd-like weight.
Let
u(x)\in C_{1}
be the minimum odd-like weight vector with respect to
X
. Then
u(x)\not\in D_{1}
and there exists
s\in X
such that for each
\ell\in Z(s)
we have
u(\alpha^{\ell})\neq 0
.
Moreover,

and the fact that

implies that

for each

. Thus

u(x)u^{\prime}(x)
is a non-zero element of
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
and
{\rm wt}(u(x)u^{\prime}(x))\geq d(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
. Moreover,
u(x)u^{\prime}(x)
has at most
d_{o}(C_{i})^{2}
non-zero terms. Therefore,

d_{o}(C_{i})^{2}\geq{\rm wt}(u(x)u^{\prime}(x))\geq d(C_{1}\cap C_{2}).

\square

Note that in the proof of Theorem
4.1
part (5), it is essential to choose
u(x)
to be an odd-like vector (
u(x)\in C_{1}\setminus D_{1}
) as otherwise

and thus
0={\rm wt}(u(x)u^{\prime}(x))\leq d(C_{1}\cap C_{2})
. So this result only lower bounds the minimum odd-like weight and is not applicable for the minimum distance of a general duadic code.
Now we have all the necessary requirements to present our infinite classes of binary stabilizer codes from duadic codes.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer and

with
{\rm ord}(a)=t

1.

There exists a binary quantum stabilizer code with parameters
[\![n,|X|,d]\!]_{2}

There exists a binary quantum stabilizer code with parameters
[\![n+|X|,0,d]\!]_{2}
, where

is even and

.

Proof.

First note that Lemma
3.2
implies that

and consequently one can find a splitting of
\Omega_{a}
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
in the form
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
that is given by

(1) By Theorem

, parts (2) and (4), we have
D=C^{\bot_{h}}\subseteq C
and

over
\mathbb{F}_{4}

to

(2) We apply the result of Theorem

to the code

. First note that

. Thus there exists a quantum stabilizer code with parameters

\square

In general, in order to apply the above constructions, it is essential to find an integer

that such that
tn\nmid 2^{2j-1}+1
for each integer

. Selection of such value of

guarantees the existence of a binary quantum code.
Later we prove that there are infinitely many values of

that satisfy the condition of Theorem

. Furthermore, the linear codes

in Magma computer algebra
[

]
, where

is the generator polynomial of the code and

is the shift constant.
Perhaps the main remaining challenge is the computation of true minimum distance.
Indeed, recently it was demonstrated that computing the true minimum distance of a general quantum stabilizer code is NP-hard
[
48
]
.
Nevertheless, properties specific to cyclic and constacyclic codes can significantly aid in computing the true minimum of quantum codes given in Theorem
4.2
.
This is mainly because:

•

When length of the code is very large, minimum distance lower bounds like the BCH and square root bound provides an initial lower bound for the minimum distance, which can computationally take a considerable amount of time.

•

Using properties like the existence of large cycles in the automorphism groups of cyclic, constacyclic, and quasi-cyclic codes enhances algorithms for computing the minimum distance, outperforming those designed for general codes. Additional details on this can be found, for example, in the minimum distance computation section of
[
28
]
.

The following example serves as a counterexample to this statement.

Example 4.4
.

Let
n=75
. (1) We can partition
\Omega_{\omega}
as

\Omega_{\omega}=Z(1)\cup Z(7)\cup Z(10)\cup Z(55)\cup Z(25).

Let

,

. Then

forms a splitting of
\Omega_{\omega}
that is given by

. Let

be the odd-like duadic code of length

over

with the defining set

. Then our computation in Magma computer algebra
[

]
shows that

.
However, the square root minimum distance bound of
[

does not hold as
d(C)=4<\sqrt{\frac{75}{3}}=5
.

Applying the result of Corollary

to

gives a binary quantum code with parameters
[\![75,3,d_{o}(C)=9]\!]_{2}

The code

has

and
23625
codewords of weight

that all also belong to

, which is its even-like duadic subcode.
In other words, these weights 4 and 8 vectors are in correspondence to degenerate errors that preserve the quantum code.

(2) An easy computation shows that
\Omega_{1}
can be partitioned as

\begin{split}\Omega_{1}&=Z(0)\cup Z(1)\cup Z(2)\cup Z(3)\cup Z(5)\cup Z(6)\cup Z(7)\cup Z(10)\cup\\
&Z(11)\cup Z(15)\cup Z(25)\cup Z(30)\cup Z(35)\cup Z(50)\cup Z(55).\end{split}

Let

. Then
(X,S_{1},S_{2})
forms a splitting for
\Omega_{1}
that is given by
\mu_{-2}
. Let
C
be the odd-like duadic cyclic codes of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.
Then our computation shows that

, but

. Thus there exists a degenerate binary quantum code

with parameters

. The codes
C
and

have 2025, 6300, 9450, and 2700 codewords of weights 8, 10, 12, and 14, respectively that are in correspondence to degenerate errors.

In the case of constacyclic codes over a more general finite field, the same error regarding the statement of square root bound has happened in
[

, Theorem 11 part(8)]
.
One can see a counterexample of that by constructing the odd-like duadic code of length

over

It should be noted that the square root minimum distance lower bound is not generally very tight.
For instance, in Example

The following proposition shows that there are infinitely many integers

Proposition 4.5

Let

Proof.

For each
j\geq 1
we have

if and only if

. This condition is equivalent to
-2
being a quadratic non-residue modulo

. Moreover, by
[

, Article 113]
, we have
-2
is quadratic non-residue modulo all primes in the form

or

. This implies the result.

\square

Hence if

has only prime factors in the form

or
8k+5
, then the assumptions of Corollary

are satisfied.

Next we discuss other features of the above families of quantum codes.
First, it should be mentioned that changing the odd-like duadic code in Theorem

and Corollary
4.3
(using a different splitting that is given by

) can result in a non-equivalent code with a better minimum distance.
Second, these results hold for both cyclic and

-constacyclic codes of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, and, as we observed in Example

, changing the (cyclic-constacyclic) type of code can result in a non-equivalent code with different parameters.

Next, we show that our construction is capable of producing infinite families of good quantum codes in the sense of the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

(1) Let
\delta
be an arbitrary positive integer. Then, there exists an infinite family of quantum duadic codes with minimum distance

and non-zero asymptotic rate.

(2) For each integer

, there exists an infinite family of quantum duadic code with parameters
[\![n,3^{i},d\geq\sqrt{\frac{n}{3^{i}}}]\!]_{2}
.

Proof.

(1) Let

be an integer with prime factors in the form

or
8k+5
.
Then, Proposition
4.5
implies that

satisfies the conditions of Corollary

part (1). Hence, there exists an infinite family of binary quantum codes with parameters

for each

(2) Let

be a fixed integer and

\square

Note also that it is not known whether
the family of duadic cyclic codes with

is asymptotically good or bad. The same holds for the duadic constacyclic codes discussed in this work. Any development in this regard can be used to discuss the asymptotic behaviour of quantum duadic codes.

Theorem 4.7

Theorem 4.8
.

Let

-constacyclic codes of length

It is important to note that, degenerate codes usually require fewer physical qubits, and consequently fewer Pauli operations, for syndrome extraction compared to non-degenerate codes with the same parameters.
Given that it is not possible to completely eliminate the effect of noise using current technologies, codes with fewer operations in the syndrome extraction circuit are more favorable.
Hence, degenerate quantum duadic codes described in Theorem

The quantum code of Example

From the perspective of classical coding theory, it is beneficial to avoid the extended splittings as they produce duadic codes with a small minimum distance.
However, from the standpoint of quantum codes, using the extended splittings can offer codes with degeneracy which is a desirable property for error-correction.
Moreover, as Theorem
4.11
shows, we can bound or compute the true minimum distance of these quantum codes even if the length of the code is very large.

We conclude this section by noting that although we have only explored Hermitian dual-containing constacyclic codes in this paper, a similar construction approach can be applied to binary cyclic codes by employing the quantum CSS construction
[
8
, Theorem 9]
.
Due to space constraints in this paper and the fact that binary CSS codes are exclusively derived from cyclic codes (where binary constacyclic codes are always cyclic), we defer this investigation to a future task.

5
Conclusion and future works

We introduced the family of duadic constacyclic codes over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, enabling us to construct an infinite class of binary quantum stabilizer codes called quantum duadic codes. These quantum codes can possess varying dimensions and their minimum distances are lower bounded by a square root bound.
Additionally, we proved that our quantum codes contains an infinite class of degenerate codes.
We also discussed a technique for extending splittings of duadic constacyclic codes, allowing us to extract new information about the minimum distance and minimum odd-like weight of duadic constacyclic codes.
Finally, we demonstrated parameters of some quantum codes inside this family.

An outstanding open problem is to determine the asymptotic behavior of the quantum codes presented in this paper, which could be closely connected to the asymptotic behavior of the corresponding classical duadic constacyclic codes.

m3^{\ell}n_{1}\equiv\begin{cases}4^{\beta^{\prime}}(3^{\ell}n_{1})\pmod{n}&m\equiv 1\pmod{3}\\
4^{\beta^{\prime}}(2\times 3^{\ell}n_{1})\pmod{n}&m\equiv 2\pmod{3}.\end{cases}

This proves the result.

\square

Remark A.1
.

Note that for each

there exists even number of cyclotomic cosets inside

that are not fixed by
\mu_{-2}
.
For instance, if there are
2k

a

An efficient method to identify many equivalent duadic
a

This technique, as demonstrated in Example 5.5 of

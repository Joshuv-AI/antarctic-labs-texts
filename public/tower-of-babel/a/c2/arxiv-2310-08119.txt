The Existence of ground state solutions for nonlinear p p -Laplacian equations on lattice graphs
Bobo Hua, Wendi Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the nonlinear p p -Laplacian equation − Δ p ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) -\Delta_{p}u+V(x)|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u) with positive and periodic potential V V on the lattice graph ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} , where Δ p \Delta_{p} is the discrete p p -Laplacian, p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . The nonlinearity f f is also periodic in x x and satisfies the growth condition | f ⁡ ( x , u ) | ≤ a ⁡ ( 1 + | u | q − 1 ) |f(x,u)|\leq a(1+|u|^{q-1}) for some q > p q>p . We first prove the equivalence of three function spaces on ℤ N \mathbb{Z}^{N} , which is quite different from the continuous case and allows us to remove the restriction q > p ∗ q>p^{*} in [ SW10 ] , where p ∗ p^{*} is the critical exponent for W 1 , p ​ ( Ω ) ↪ L q ​ ( Ω ) W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega) with Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} bounded. Then, using the method of Nehari [ Neh60 , Neh61 ] , we prove the existence of ground state solutions to the above equation.



1.
introduction

The semilinear Schrödinger type equation of the form

(1)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=f(x,u),\quad u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
V:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a given potential and
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonlinearity,
has been extensively studied in the literature, see, e.g.,
[
BN83
,
Rab92
,
Cao92
,
BW00
,
DT03
,
AFM05
,
LWZ06
,
HZ12
]
and the references therein.
The
p
-Laplacian equation

(2)

-\Delta_{p}u+V(x)|u|^{p-2}u=f(x,u)

is a nonlinear generalization of the problem (
1
) to
p\in(1,\infty)
.
In the note
[
SW10
]
, the authors consider the zero boundary value problem of equation (
2
) with
V(x)=-\lambda>-\lambda_{1}
, where
\lambda_{1}
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian. They obtain ground state solutions to (
2
) via the Nehari method, which was first introduced in
[
Neh60
]
to find a solution to the zero boundary value problem of (
1
) with
V(x)=\lambda\geq 0
and
\Omega=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
. Roughly speaking, the Nehari method is to minimize the corresponding functionals on constrained manifolds and our goal is to use this method to study equation (
2
) on lattice graphs.

In recent years, analysis on graph has attracted the attention because of its importance in applications, see e.g.,
[
BHL+15
,
GLY16a
,
GLY17
,
HSZ20
,
LY22
,
HLS23
]
.
Our work is inspired by the following works. In
[
GLY16b
]
, the authors obtain a positive solution to the Yamabe equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u-\alpha u=|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{ in }\Omega^{0}\\
&u=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega\\
\end{aligned}\right.

on a finite subgraph
\Omega
in a locally finite graph via the mountain pass theorem. They also consider similar problems involving the
p
-Laplacian and poly-Laplacian by the same method. In
[
ZZ18
]
, N. Zhang and L. Zhao prove via the Nehari method that the Schrödinger type equation

-\Delta u+(\lambda a(x)+1)u=|u|^{p-1}u

admits a ground state solution
u_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda>1
and
u_{\lambda}
converges to the solution of a Dirichlet problem as
\lambda\to\infty
. In
[
HS21
]
, X. Han and M. Shao generalize their work from Laplace operator to
p
-Laplace problem,
p\geq 2
, and consider more general nonlinearities.
In our previous work
[
HX23
]
, we prove the existence of ground state solutions of (
1
) with periodic or bounded potential on a lattice graph by the Nehari method. In this paper, we use this method to obtain ground state solutions of the
p
-Laplacian equation
(
2
)
on lattice graphs.

We first introduce the basic setting on graphs. Let
G=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a simple, undirected, locally finite graph, where
\mathbb{V}
denotes a set of vertices and
\mathbb{E}
denotes a set of edges.
We write
x\sim y\,(x\text{ is a neighbour of }y)
if
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}
.
The graph
G
is undirected means that each
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}
is unordered. A graph is called locally finite if each vertex has finitely many neighbours.
We denote by
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
the standard lattice graph with

\mathbb{V}:=\{x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}):x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq N\},

\mathbb{E}:=\Big\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in\mathbb{V},\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}-y_{i}|=1\Big\}.

Let
C(\mathbb{V})
be the set of all functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
be the set of all functions with finite support.
Define the difference operator
\nabla_{xy}:C(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{R}
for any pair
\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{E}
as

\nabla_{xy}u=u(y)-u(x).

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the discrete
p
-Laplacian of
u\in C(\mathbb{V})
is defined as

\Delta_{p}u(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{V},y\sim x}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p-2}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
\mathcal{E}_{p}:C(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{R}
be the
p
-Dirichlet functional on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, defined as

\mathcal{E}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}.

In this paper, we adopt the definition of
\Delta_{p}
in
[
HW20
]
, which is different from that in
[
HS21
]
when
p\neq 2
. This allows us to extend our results to
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
e_{i}
be the unit vector in the
i
-th coordinate.
For T
\in\mathbb{N}
,
g\in C(\mathbb{V})
is called T-periodic if

g(x+Te_{i})=g(x),\quad\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},1\leq i\leq N.

To state our work, we introduce the following assumptions on the nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and the potential
V\in C(\mathbb{V})
for the equation (
2
).

(A_{1})

f(x,u)
is continuous with respect to
u\in\mathbb{R}
and satisfies the growth condition:
\exists\,a>0,q>p
, s.t.

|f(x,u)|\leq a(1+|u|^{q-1}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V}.

(A_{2})

V(x)
and
f(x,u)
are both T-periodic in
x

and
V(x)>0
for each vertex
x
.

(A_{3})

f(x,u)=o(|u|^{p-1})
uniformly in
x
as
u\to 0
.

(A_{4})

u\mapsto\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|^{p-1}}
is strictly increasing on
(-\infty,0)
and
(0,+\infty)
respectively.

(A_{5})

\frac{F(x,u)}{|u|^{p}}\to\infty
uniformly in
x
as
|u|\to\infty
, where
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,s)\,ds
.

Let
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mu),p\in[1,\infty],
be the space of
\ell^{p}
summable functions on
\mathbb{V}
w.r.t. the counting measure
\mu
. We write
\|\cdot\|_{p}
as the
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V},\mu)
norm, i.e.,

\|u\|_{p}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|u(x)|^{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,\\
&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|u(x)|,\quad\quad p=\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

As we know, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
is the completion of
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
under the norm
{\|\cdot\|_{p}}
. Let

W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})=\{u\in C(\mathbb{V}):\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})}<\infty\},

1<p<\infty,
where

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})}

\displaystyle:=\big(\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)+\|u\|_{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|u(x)|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Moreover, from assumption
(A_{2})
, there exist

V_{1},V_{2}>0
such that
0<V_{1}\leq V(x)\leq V_{2}
for each
x\in\mathbb{V}
.
We can define a new norm
\|\cdot\|
on
C_{c}(\mathbb{V})
by

\|u\|:=\Big(\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and set

E=\{u\in C(\mathbb{V}):\|u\|<\infty\}.

The variational functional corresponds to the equation
(
2
)
is

\displaystyle\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\big(\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p}\big)-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,u).

One can check that
\Phi\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
with the derivative given by

\Phi^{\prime}(u)v=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}u)(\nabla_{xy}v)+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p-2}uv-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x,u)v,\quad\forall v\in E.

In particular,

\Phi^{\prime}(u)u=\|u\|^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x,u)u.

Set

\mathcal{N}=\{u\in E\backslash\{0\}:\Phi^{\prime}(u)u=0\}

and

c:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\Phi(u).

\mathcal{N}
is called the Nehari manifold, which contains all nontrivial critical points of
\Phi
. We say that
u
is a ground state solution if it possesses the least positive energy among all solutions for
(
2
)
.
The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f
and
V
satisfy
(A_{1})
-
(A_{5})
.
Then
c>0
,
c
is attainable and each
u\in\mathcal{N}
with
\Phi(u)=c
is a ground state solution for
(
2
)
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some basic properties of function spaces defined on lattice graphs. In Section 3, we construct the framework of the Nehari method, and the proof of Theorem
1.1
is contained in Section 4.

2.
Preliminaries

Lemma 2.1
.

\|\cdot\|_{p}
,
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})}
and
\|\cdot\|
are equivalent norms and
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})=W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})=E
.

Proof.

On one hand,
0<V_{1}\leq V(x)\leq V_{2}
for each
x\in\mathbb{V}
implies

\nu_{1}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq\|u\|\leq\nu_{2}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}},\quad\forall u\in C_{c}(\mathbb{V}),

where
\nu_{1}=\text{min}\{V_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}},1\}
,
\nu_{2}=\text{max}\{V_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}},1\}
.
On the other hand,

\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},y\sim x}\frac{1}{2}|u(y)-u(x)|^{p}\leq C(p,N)\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Hence,

\|u\|_{p}\leq\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq\big(1+C(p,N)\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{p}.

This yields that

\|\cdot\|_{p}\sim\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{V})}\sim\|\cdot\|
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

E
is a reflexive Banach space.

Proof.

Since
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
is a uniformly convex Banach space, it is reflexive.
The reflexive space remains reflexive for an equivalent norm, so both
W^{1,p}(V)
and
E
are reflexive as well.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Integration by parts of
p
-Laplacian)
.

For any
u\in E,v\in C_{c}(V),

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}u\nabla_{xy}v=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}(-\Delta_{p}u)v.

The proof is similar to that of Green’s formula in
[
Gri18
]
.
Based on this lemma, we are ready to define the weak solution of (
2
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
u\in E
. We say that
u
is a weak solution of (
2
) if

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}u\nabla_{xy}v+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|u|^{p-2}uv=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x,u)v,\quad\forall v\in E.

For any given
y\in\mathbb{V}
, by using integration by parts and taking the test function

v(x)=\delta_{y}(x)
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
u
is a weak solution of (
2
), then
u
is a pointwise solution.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{N}=(\mathbb{V},\mathbb{E})
be a lattice graph. Then,

(i)

\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
is embedded into
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V})
for any
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
.

(ii)
For any bounded sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
, there exists
u\in E
such that, up to a subsequence,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{in}\,\,E,\\
&u_{n}(x)\to u(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

(i) is well-known.

(ii) By Lemma
2.2
,
E
is a reflexive Banach space. Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
E
, we have
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
E
after passing to a subsequence. Moreover,
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is also bounded in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
,
1<p<\infty
, and therefore,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{V})
.
It follows that,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}u_{n}\phi=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}u\phi,\quad\forall\phi\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{V}),\,p^{\prime}:=\tfrac{p}{p-1}.

For each vertex
x_{0}\in\mathbb{V}
, taking
\phi=\delta_{x_{0}}
, we have

\lim_{n}u_{n}(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
.
∎

At the end of this section, we give two lemmas which will be used in the subsequent proofs.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
f
satisfies
(A_{1})
and
(A_{3})
. Then, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

|f(x,u)|\leq\epsilon|u|^{p-1}+C_{\epsilon}|u|^{q-1},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{V}.

Proof.

By
(A_{3})
, for any given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that,
\forall|u|<\delta
,

|f(x,u)|\leq\epsilon|u|^{p-1},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{V}.

When
|u|\geq\delta
, choose
C_{\delta}\geq\frac{a}{\delta^{q-1}}
.
Substitute this into
(A_{1})
, we have

|f(x,u)|\leq a(1+|u|^{q-1})\leq(C_{\delta}+a)|u|^{q-1}=:C_{\epsilon}|u|^{q-1}.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
f
satisfies
(A_{3})
-
(A_{4})
. Then,

0<F(x,u)<\frac{1}{p}f(x,u)u,\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

Combining
(A_{3})
with
(A_{4})
, we have
\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|^{p-1}}<0
if
u<0
and
\frac{f(x,u)}{|u|^{p-1}}>0
if
u>0
, which gives
f(x,u)u>0
,
\forall u\neq 0
.
Hence,
F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,t)dt>0
.
If
u>0
, by
(A_{4})
,

F(x,u)=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}t^{p-1}dt<\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\int_{0}^{u}t^{p-1}dt=\frac{1}{p}f(x,u)u.

If
u<0
, the proof is similar.
∎

3.
Existence of ground state solutions

In this section, we always assume that
(A_{1})
-
(A_{5})
are satisfied. We prove the existence of ground state solutions for the equation (
2
) by the Nehari method. Recall that Nehari manifold is defined as

\mathcal{N}=\{u\in E\backslash\{0\}:\Phi^{\prime}(u)u=0\},

which contains all nontrivial critical points of
\Phi
. Denote the unit sphere in
E
by

S=\{u\in E:\|u\|=1\}.

In the following, we first show that
\mathcal{N}
is homeomorphic to
S
. And then, the problem of looking for critical points of
\Phi
on
\mathcal{N}
is reduced to seeking critical points of a new functional
\Psi
on
S
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Set
\alpha_{w}(s)=\Phi(sw)
for
w\in E\backslash\{0\}
,
0<s<\infty
. Then we have the following.

(i)
For each
w\in E\backslash\{0\}
,
\alpha_{w}
admits a unique maximum point
s_{w}\in(0,\infty)
.

(ii)
There exists
\delta>0
such that
s_{w}\geq\delta
for all
w
in
S
.

(iii)
For each compact set
K\subset S
, there exists
C_{K}>0
such that
s_{w}\leq C_{K}
for all
w
in
K
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
w\in E\backslash\{0\}
. By calculation,

\displaystyle\alpha_{w}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\prime}(sw)w=s^{p-1}\|w\|^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x,sw(x))w(x)

\displaystyle=s^{p-1}\Big(\|w\|^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{|f(x,sw)|}{|sw|^{p-1}}|w|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=:s^{p-1}\theta_{w}(s).

According to
(A_{4})
,
\theta_{w}(s)
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover, we know from
(A_{3})
that
\theta_{w}(s)>0
for sufficiently small
s>0
. Lemma
2.8
and
(A_{5})
implies

\theta_{w}(s)<\|w\|^{p}-p\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{F(x,sw)}{|sw|^{p}}|w|^{p}\to-\infty\quad\text{ as }s\to+\infty.

Therefore, there exists unique
s_{w}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3)

\alpha_{w}^{\prime}(s_{w})=\Phi^{\prime}(s_{w}w)w=0

and
\alpha_{w}(s)
increases on
(0,s_{w})
and decreases on
(s_{w},\infty)
.

(ii)
Let
w\in S
and

\theta_{w}(s)=1-\frac{1}{s^{p-1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|f(x,sw)||w|.

By Lemma
2.7
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\theta_{w}(s)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{s^{p-1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\Big(\epsilon|sw|^{p-1}+C_{\epsilon}|sw|^{q-1}\Big)|w|

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\Big(\epsilon|w|^{p}+C_{\epsilon}s^{q-p}|w|^{q}\Big)

\displaystyle=1-\big(\epsilon\|w\|_{p}^{p}+C_{\epsilon}s^{q-p}\|w\|_{q}^{q}\big).

One easily sees that

\|w\|_{q}\leq\|w\|_{\infty}^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\|w\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\|u\|_{p}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\|w\|_{p}\leq V_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}
as
\|w\|=1
.
It follows that

\theta_{w}(s)\geq\big(1-\epsilon V_{1}^{-1}\big)-C_{\epsilon}s^{q-p}V_{1}^{-\frac{q}{p}}.

Take
\epsilon=\epsilon_{{}_{0}}\leq\frac{V_{1}}{2}
and
\delta>0
small enough such that

\theta_{w}(s)\geq\frac{1}{2}-C_{\epsilon_{{}_{0}}}s^{q-p}V_{1}^{-\frac{q}{p}}>0,\quad\forall s\in(0,\delta).

Therefore,

\alpha_{w}^{\prime}(s)=s^{p-1}\theta_{w}(s)>0,\quad\forall s\in(0,\delta),

and then
s_{w}\geq\delta>0
.

(iii)

Since
\alpha_{w}^{\prime}(s)<0
on
(s_{w},\infty)
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{w}(s)>0
on
(0,s_{w})
, it suffices to show the existence of
C_{K}
which guarantees

\alpha_{w}^{\prime}(s)\leq 0\text{ on }[C_{K},\infty),\quad\forall w\in K.

We show this by contradiction. Suppose
\{w_{n}\}\subset K
and
s_{n}:=s_{w_{n}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Note that

(4)

\displaystyle 0<\theta_{w_{n}}(s_{n})=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{f(x,s_{n}w_{n})w_{n}}{s_{n}^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-p\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{F(x,s_{n}w_{n})}{s_{n}^{p}}.

Since
K
is compact, we may assume that
w_{n}\to w\in S
and
w_{n}(x)\to w(x)
as
n\to\infty
for each vertex
x
.
For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{V}

with
w_{n}(x_{0})\to w(x_{0})\neq 0
, by
(A_{5})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{F(x,s_{n}w_{n})}{s_{n}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{F(x,s_{n}w_{n})}{|s_{n}w_{n}|^{p}}|w_{n}(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{F(x_{0},s_{n}w_{n}(x_{0}))}{|s_{n}w_{n}(x_{0})|^{p}}|w_{n}(x_{0})|^{p}\to+\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

This yields a contraction to
(\ref{tri})
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In fact, (
3
) implies
s_{w}w\in\mathcal{N}
. One can see from Lemma
3.1
(i) that, for each
w\in E\backslash\{0\}
, the ray
s\mapsto sw
intersects with
\mathcal{N}
at precisely one point
s_{w}w
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Set
\hat{m}:E\backslash\{0\}\to\mathcal{N}
,
\hat{m}(w):=s_{w}w
and
m=\hat{m}|_{S}
. Then
\hat{m}
is continuous and
m:S\to\mathcal{N}
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

Let
u\in E\backslash\{0\}
. It suffices to show that each sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset E\backslash\{0\}
with
u_{n}\to u\,(u\neq 0)
admits a subsequence such that
\hat{m}(u_{n})\to\hat{m}(u)
. Since
\hat{m}(tu)=\hat{m}(u)
for any
t>0
, we may assume that
\|u_{n}\|=\|u\|=1
. Let
\hat{m}(u_{n})=s_{n}u_{n}
and
\hat{m}(u)=s_{u}u
. By Proposition
3.1
, there exist
\delta>0,C>0
such that
\delta\leq s_{n}\leq C
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. We may assume that
s_{n}\to\bar{s}>0
after passing to a subsequence.
Then
\hat{m}(u_{n})=s_{n}u_{n}\to\bar{s}u
as
n\to\infty
and it suffices to show
\bar{s}u=s_{u}u
.
According to Proposition
3.1
(i) and Remark
3.2
,
\Phi(s_{u}u)\geq\Phi(\bar{s}u)
as
s_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}
. The reverse inequality follows from

\Phi(s_{u}u)=\lim_{n}\Phi(s_{u}u_{n})\leq\lim_{n}\Phi(s_{n}u_{n})=\Phi(\bar{s}u)

since
\Phi
is continuous on
E
.
In fact,

\Phi(w_{n})-\Phi(w)=\frac{1}{p}\big(\|w_{n}\|^{p}-\|w\|^{p}\big)-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\big(F(x,w_{n})-F(x,w)\big),

and

|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\big(F(x,w_{n})-F(x,w)\big)|\leq\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|f(x,\zeta_{n})||w_{n}-w|,

where
\zeta_{n}
is between
w_{n}
and
w
. Take
\epsilon=1
in Lemma
2.7
, we have

(5)

|f(x,\zeta_{n})|\leq|\zeta_{n}|^{p-1}+C_{1}|\zeta_{n}|^{q-1}.

Then, by Hölder inequality,

(6)

\displaystyle|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,w_{n})-F(x,w)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|w_{n}-w|(|\zeta_{n}|^{p-1}+C_{1}|\zeta_{n}|^{q-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|w_{n}-w|^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|\zeta_{n}|^{p}\big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+C_{1}\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|w_{n}-w|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}|\zeta_{n}|^{q}\big)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|w_{n}-w\|_{p}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{p}^{p-1}+C_{1}\|w_{n}-w\|_{q}\|\zeta_{n}\|_{q}^{q-1}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

This yields the continuity of
\hat{m}
.
Now we consider the restriction of
\hat{m}
on the unit sphere
m:S\to\mathcal{N}
.
Let
w_{i}\in S
,
m(w_{i})=s_{i}w_{i},i=1,2
. If
m(w_{1})=m(w_{2})
, i.e.,
s_{1}w_{1}=s_{2}w_{2}
, we have
s_{1}=s_{2}
after taking norm on both sides.
Since
s_{i}\geq\delta>0
, one sees
w_{1}=w_{2}
and
m
is injective.
For each
u\in\mathcal{N}
, we know
\frac{u}{\|u\|}\in S
and
m(\frac{u}{\|u\|})=u
by Proposition
3.1
. Therefore
m
is surjective and thus homeomorphism with its continuous inverse given by
u\mapsto\frac{u}{\|u\|}
, proving the result.
∎

Consider the composite functional
\hat{\Psi}:=\Phi\circ\hat{m}

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}:E\backslash\{0\}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}(w)=\Phi(\hat{m}(w))

The goal is to establish a one-to-one correspondence between the critical points of
\hat{\Psi}|_{S}
and
\Phi|_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

\hat{\Psi}\in C^{1}(E\backslash\{0\},\mathbb{R})
and

\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}(w)z=\frac{\|\hat{m}(w)\|}{\|w\|}\Phi^{\prime}(\hat{m}(w))z,\quad\forall w,z\in E\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

Given
w,z\in E\backslash\{0\}
, take
\gamma>0
small enough such that
w_{t}:=w+tz\neq 0,\forall|t|<\gamma
.
Let
\hat{m}(w_{t})=s_{t}(w_{t})
.
Then
\hat{m}(w)=s_{0}w
and
s_{t}w_{t}\to s_{0}w
as
t\to 0
from Proposition
3.3
.
We calculate that

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}(\hat{\Psi}(w_{t})-\hat{\Psi}(w))=\frac{1}{t}(\Phi(s_{t}w_{t})-\Phi(s_{0}w))\geq\frac{1}{t}(\Phi(s_{0}w_{t})-\Phi(s_{0}w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\Phi^{\prime}\big(s_{0}[w+\eta_{t}(w_{t}-w)]\big)s_{0}(w_{t}-w),\quad\eta_{t}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(s_{0}w)s_{0}z\quad\text{ as }t\to 0,

and

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}(\hat{\Psi}(w_{t})-\hat{\Psi}(w))\leq\frac{1}{t}(\Phi(s_{t}w_{t})-\Phi(s_{t}w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\Phi^{\prime}\big(s_{t}[w+\gamma_{t}(w_{t}-w)]\big)s_{t}(w_{t}-w),\quad\gamma_{t}\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(s_{0}w)s_{0}z\quad\text{ as }t\to 0.

Combining (
7
) with (
8
), we get

\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}(w)z=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\hat{\Psi}(w_{t})-\hat{\Psi}(w)}{t}=\Phi^{\prime}(s_{0}w)s_{0}z=\frac{\|\hat{m}(w)\|}{\|w\|}\Phi^{\prime}(\hat{m}(w))z.

Since
\hat{m}
is continuous and
\Phi\in C^{1}(E\backslash\{0\},\mathbb{R})
,
\hat{\Psi}
admits comtinuous Gateaux derivative on
E\backslash\{0\}
. Thus, by Proposition 1.3 in
[
Wil96
]
,
\hat{\Psi}\in C^{1}(E\backslash\{0\},\mathbb{R})
.
∎

One easily gets the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

\phi(u)=\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}u|^{p}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|u(x)|^{p}.

Then
\phi\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
and

\phi(w)=\frac{1}{p},\quad\phi^{\prime}(w)w=1,\quad\quad\forall w\in S,

where

\phi^{\prime}(w)v=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}w|^{p-2}(\nabla_{xy}w)(\nabla_{xy}v)+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|w(x)|^{p-2}wv,\quad\forall v\in E.

For each
w\in S
, set

T_{w}(S)=\{v\in E:\phi^{\prime}(w)v=0\}=ker(\phi^{\prime}(w)).

We have direct sum decomposition:

E=\mathbb{R}_{w}\oplus T_{w}S.

By Proposition
3.4
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

Set
\Psi=\hat{\Psi}|_{S}
. Then
\Psi\in C^{1}(S)
and

\Psi^{\prime}(w)z=\|m(w)\|\Phi^{\prime}(m(w))z,\quad\forall z\in T_{w}S,

where

is understood as the derivative of a function on Banach manifold.

Proposition 3.7
.

\Phi
is coercive on
\mathcal{N}
, i.e., if
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{N}
with
\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
, then
\Phi(u_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Argue by contradiction. Suppose
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{N}
with
\|u_{n}\|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
and
\Phi(u_{n})\leq d
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}\in S,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|v_{n}\|_{p}<\infty
.
Suppose that
v_{n}\to 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V})
. By Lemma
2.7
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,\lambda v_{n})\big|\leq\frac{1}{p}\big(\epsilon\|\lambda v_{n}\|_{p}^{p}+C_{\epsilon}\|\lambda v_{n}\|_{q}^{q}\big)\leq C\epsilon+o(1),\quad n\to\infty,

for any
\lambda>0
.
Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,\lambda v_{n})\to 0,\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Then Proposition
3.1
gives

(9)

d\geq\Phi(u_{n})\geq\Phi(\lambda v_{n})=\frac{1}{p}\lambda^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,\lambda v_{n})\to\frac{1}{p}\lambda^{p}\text{ as }n\to\infty,\quad\forall\lambda>0.

This is a contradiction. Hence,
v_{n}\nrightarrow 0
in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V})
, i.e.,

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{q}=:A>0.

By the interpolation inequality and
q>p>1
,

\|v_{n}\|_{q}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\leq V_{1}^{-\frac{p}{q}}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}^{\frac{q-p}{q}}.

Hence,

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}>0.

Therefore, there exist subsequences
\{v_{n}\}\subset S
,
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
A^{\prime}>0
such that

|v_{n}(y_{n})|\geq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{n}\|_{\infty}\geq A^{\prime}>0,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Set

\tilde{v}_{n}(\cdot)=v_{n}(\cdot+k_{n}T)
with
k_{n}=(k_{n}^{1},\cdots,k_{n}^{N})\in\mathbb{V}
such that
\{y_{n}-k_{n}T\}\subset\Omega
, where
\Omega=[0,T)^{N}\cap\mathbb{V}
is a bounded set. Then

\|\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq|v_{n}(y_{n})|\geq A^{\prime}>0.

Let
\tilde{u}_{n}(\cdot)=u_{n}(\cdot+k_{n}T)
. By the translation invariance of
\|\cdot\|
and
\Phi
,

\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|=\|u\|\to\infty,\,\tilde{u}_{n}\in\mathcal{N}\text{ and }m(\tilde{v}_{n})=\tilde{u}_{n}.

Since
\{\tilde{v}_{n}\}\subset S
, we may assume up to a subsequence that
\tilde{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
E
and
\tilde{v}_{n}(x)\to v(x)
for each vertex in
\mathbb{V}
. Since there are finitely many vertices in
\Omega
, we have
\tilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})\to v(x_{0})>0
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Then

|\tilde{u}_{n}(x_{0})|=\|\tilde{u}_{n}\||\tilde{v}_{n}(x_{0})|\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty,

where

\tilde{u}_{n}=m(\tilde{v}_{n})\in\mathcal{N}.

Note that by
(A_{5})
,

\frac{F(x_{0},\tilde{u}_{n}(x_{0}))}{|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{p}(x_{0})}\to\infty\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Then

(10)

0<\frac{\Phi(\tilde{u}_{n})}{\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|^{p}}=\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\frac{F(x,\tilde{u}_{n})}{\|\tilde{u}_{n}\|^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{F(x_{0},\tilde{u}_{n}(x_{0}))}{|\tilde{u}_{n}|^{p}(x_{0})}|\tilde{v}_{n}|^{p}(x_{0})\to-\infty\quad

as
n\to\infty
, which is a contradiction. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

\{w_{n}\}_{n}\subset S
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Psi
if and only if
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{N}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Phi
, where
u_{n}=m(w_{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\{w_{n}\}\subset S
with

\sup_{n}|\Psi(w_{n})|=\sup_{n}|\Phi(u_{n})|<\infty
,
where
u_{n}=m(w_{n})\in\mathcal{N}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
On one hand, by Corollary
3.6
,

\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\|_{S}:=\sup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w_{n}}S\\
\|z\|=1\end{array}}\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})z=\|u_{n}\|\sup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w_{n}}S\\
\|z\|=1\\
\end{array}}\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})z,

which implies
\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\|_{S}\leq\|u_{n}\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|
.
On the other hand,

(11)

\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|=\sup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w_{n}}S\\
z+tw_{n}\neq 0\\
\end{array}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})(z+tw_{n})}{\|z+tw_{n}\|}=\sup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}z\in T_{w_{n}}S\\
z+tw_{n}\neq 0\\
\end{array}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})z}{\|z+tw_{n}\|},

where the last equality follows from

\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})w_{n}=\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}=0.

Note that, for each
w\in S,z\in T_{w}S
,

\|z\|\leq\|z+tw\|+|t|,

|t|=|\phi^{\prime}(w)(z+tw)|\leq\|\phi^{\prime}(w)\|\|z+tw\|,

where
\phi(w)=\frac{1}{p}\|w\|^{p}
.
Futhermore, by Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(w)v=

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{xy}w|^{p-2}\nabla_{xy}w\nabla_{xy}v+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|w(x)|^{p-2}wv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}|\nabla_{xy}w|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V},x\sim y}|\nabla_{xy}v|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|w|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\Big(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}V(x)|v|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|w\|^{p-1}\|v\|

for all
v\in T_{w}S
, which implies

\|\phi^{\prime}(w)\|\leq 2

and

(12)

\|z\|\leq 3\|z+tw\|.

Plugging (
12
) into (
11
), we get

\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\leq 3\sup_{z\in T_{w_{n}}S\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})z}{\|z\|}.

It follows that by Corollary
3.6
,

(13)

\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\|_{S}\leq\|u_{n}\|\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\|\leq 3\|\Psi^{\prime}(w_{n})\|_{S}.

Futhermore, by Proposition
3.1
(ii) and Proposition
3.7
, since
\Phi(u_{n})
is bounded,

\delta\leq\|u_{n}\|\leq C,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

for some
0<\delta<C<\infty
, proving the result.

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

.
We first show that

By

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}F(x,u)=o(\|u\|_{p}^{p})=o(\|u\|^{p})\quad\text{ as }\|u\|\to 0.

(i) implies

Taking the infimum over
\mathcal{N}
, we obtain
(\ref{c>0})
.
Note that
\inf_{S}\Psi=\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi=c>0
and
\Psi\in C^{1}(S)
.
Then, by Corollary
3.6

\inf_{\mathcal{N}}\Phi

\Phi
in

. It suffices to prove that

.
It follows from the Ekeland’s variational principle on Banach manifolds that there is a minimizing sequence
\{w_{n}\}\subset S

Let

(15)

\Phi(u_{n})\to c,\,\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty

by Proposition
3.8
. Moreover,

(16)

as
\Phi

\mathcal{N}
.

2.6
(ii), there exists

\Phi^{\prime}(u)=0.

E

Otherwise,
u_{n}(x)\to u(x)=0
for each
x\in\mathbb{V}

Suppose that

in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{V})

2.7
, for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle=\|u_{n}\|^{p}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}f(x,u_{n})u_{n}

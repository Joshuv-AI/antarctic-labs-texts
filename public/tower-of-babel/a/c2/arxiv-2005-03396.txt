A closer look at the non-Hopfianness of B ​ S ​ ( 2 , 3 ) BS(2,3)
Tom Kaiser

Abstract
The Baumslag-Solitar group B ​ S ​ ( 2 , 3 ) BS(2,3) , is a so-called non-Hopfian group, meaning that it has an epimorphism ϕ \phi onto itself, that is not injective. In particular this is equivalent to saying that B ​ S ​ ( 2 , 3 ) BS(2,3) has a non-trivial quotient that is isomorphic to itself. As a consequence the Cayley graph of B ​ S ​ ( 2 , 3 ) BS(2,3) has a quotient that is isomorphic to itself up to change of generators. We describe this quotient on the graph-level and take a closer look at the most common epimorphism ϕ \phi . We show its kernel is a free group of infinite rank with an explicit set of generators. Finally we show how ϕ \phi appears as a morphism on fundamental groups induced by some continuous map. This point of view was communicated to the author by Gilbert Levitt.

We only focus on the Baumslag-Solitar group

•

If

•

Consider the group element

By repeatedly applying the previous item, any word

So suppose

Because of the previous point, there is some

of the commutator path seperates the Bass-Serre tree into two connected components.

Finally consider
\omega
, a good representative for an end-essential nepalese path
p
. Let
\omega\notin\mathcal{O}
. We will show that
\omega
can be written as a product of one (any) of its siblings and paths

such that
c(q)<c(p)
. Consider two triplets
\omega_{1}B\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{1}aBa^{-1}\omega_{2}
, where
B
is a tip. Then

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}B\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}BaB^{-1}a^{-1}\omega_{1}^{-1}\omega_{1}aBa^{-1}\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}[a^{b},a]\omega_{1}^{-1}\omega_{1}(aBa^{-1}\omega_{2})\text{, or}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}B\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{1}a^{-1})aBa^{-1}B^{-1}(a\omega_{1}^{-1})\omega_{1}a^{-1}Ba\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{1}a^{-1})[a^{b},a]^{-1}(a\omega_{1}^{-1})(\omega_{1}a^{-1}Ba\omega_{2}).

Clearly
c(\omega_{1})<c(\omega)
. On the other hand consider two twins
\omega_{1}Vb^{-1}\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{1}a^{-1}Vab^{-1}\omega_{2}
. Note that the valley can either be of the form
b^{-1}ab
or
bab^{-1}
. We suppose it is the former, the argument for the latter is similar. Note also Figure
10(a)
for a visual interpretation. One has that

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}b^{-1}ab\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{1}a^{-1}b^{-1})bab^{-1}aba^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}(ba\omega_{1}^{-1})\omega_{1}a^{-1}b^{-1}aba\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{1}a^{-1}b^{-1})[a^{b},a](ba\omega_{1}^{-1})\omega_{1}a^{-1}b^{-1}aba\omega_{2}.

The case
b^{-1}a^{-1}b
is similar, obtaining a conjugate of
[a^{b},a]^{-1}
in the first term. Since each connected component in
G_{\sim}
is represented in
\mathcal{O}
by some element, each end-essential nepalese path can be written as a product of the designated relative in
\mathcal{O}
and elements with less tips or valleys. One ends the argument by induction on the number of tips and valleys.

Freeness.
We are now ready to prove the given basis is free. Let
\omega\in\mathcal{O}
, then
\omega a^{i}[a^{b},a]a^{-i}\omega^{-1}
does not necessarily represent a geodesic path (we call the path
p
), it may have backtracking when the last edge of
\omega
and the first of
[a^{b},a]
overlap. Similarly it might also overlap on the last edge of
[a^{b},a]
and the first of
\omega^{-1}
. Let
E
be the second edge of
[a^{b},a]
as a subpath of
p
(see Figure
10(b)
), then it seperates the tree in two conncected components (say
E^{-}
and
E^{+}
). We claim that applying any other element
\tilde{\omega}a^{s}[a^{b},a]a^{-s}\tilde{\omega}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{S}
, one will never traverse
E
again. There are several possibilities.

•

Firstly suppose
\tilde{\omega}=\omega
, then this reduces to the fact that
\langle[a^{b},a],a[a^{b},a]a^{-1}\rangle
is free on two generators.

•

Consider the concatenated path
\omega a^{i}[a^{b},a]a^{-i}\omega^{-1}\tilde{\omega}a^{s}[a^{b},a]a^{-s}\tilde{\omega}^{-1}
. Since
\tilde{\omega}
is end-essential and nepalese, this path remain in
E^{+}
, once having crossed

. Let
E_{\tilde{\omega}}
be the second edge in the second commutator
[a^{b},a]
. Suppose
E_{\tilde{\omega}}
is not part of the path defined by
\omega^{-1}
. In particular, to cross
E
, we first need to cross
E_{\tilde{\omega}}
, which means we have merely displaced the problem.

•

So we may suppose

is part of
\omega^{-1}

contains a tip or a valley. In this case the composition
\omega^{-1}\tilde{\omega}[a^{b},a]\tilde{\omega}^{-1}
defines a sibling of

, since for every connected component of the sibling relation we only allowed one path to be contained in
\mathcal{O}
, which is

Remark 9
.

Note that if one is willing to give up the generating set, then there is a quick and clean argument to show that
\Ker(\phi)
acts freely on the Bass-Serre tree. The stabiliser of each vertex is given by a conjugate of
\langle a\rangle
. So to show that
\Ker(\phi)
acts freely, it is enough to show that the intersection with each of the stabilisers is trivial. Given that
\Ker(\phi)
is normal, it suffices to show that its intersection with
\langle a\rangle
is trivial. Since
a^{n}
is mapped to
a^{2n}
this is true.

Furthermore we look at the limit case of applying the morphism
\phi
.

Proposition 10
.

\Ker(\phi^{n})
is normally generated by
\{[b^{m}ab^{-m},a]\;\lvert\;0<m\leq n\}

Proof.

By induction.
∎

Corollary 11
.

BS(2,3)/\bigcup_{n}\Ker(\phi^{n})=\langle a,b\;\lvert\;[b^{m}ab^{-m},a]\text{ where }m\in\mathbb{N}\rangle.

Call this group
L
. Note that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an element
\alpha\in L
, such that
\alpha^{2^{n}}=\overline{a}
. I.e
\overline{a}
has infinitely many roots. More specifically the presentation induced by
\alpha
and
\overline{b}
is the same as the one given in Corollary
11
. One sees that the group generated by
\langle\overline{b^{m}ab^{-m}}\;|\;m\in\mathbb{N}\rangle
is isomorphic to the dyadic rationals
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
.

4
Another interpretation of non-Hopfianness and a continuous map

We present another interpretation on the non-Hopfianness of
BS(2,3)
, which was communicated to the author by Gilbert Levitt. In some sense, this is a more topological version of Remark 6.10 in
[
Lev15
]
. The paper treats generalised Baumslag-Solitar groups, which are fundamental groups of graphs of groups. This is also the setting of Remark 6.10. These groups can be retrieved as the fundamental groups of certain 2-dimensional simplicial complexes (see
[
DRT11
, Section 7]
). We will not introduce graphs of groups and associated concepts, but will rather immediately take the topological point of view adapted to
BS(2,3)
. As a last remark note that the forthcoming example is not particular to the case of
BS(2,3)
, but can easily be altered to apply to other Baumslag-Solitar groups. The same is true for Figure
11
.

First as small detour. We note the following fact: in
BS(2,3)
, the elements
a^{2}
and
a^{3}
are “the same” since they are conjugates. What we mean is that they behave in the same way, which is made precise in the following lemma.

Lemma 12
.

The group
BS(2,3)
has an abstract group presentation, where the generators can be interpreted as either
\{a^{2},b\}
or
\{a^{3},b\}
. This presentation is given by
\langle\lambda,\mu\;|\;\lambda^{2}=\mu\lambda\mu^{-1}\lambda^{-1}\mu\lambda\mu^{-1}\rangle
.

Proof.

Starting from the usual presentation
\langle a,b\;|\;ba^{2}b^{-1}=a^{3}\rangle
, one can apply two sequences of Tietze transformations to obtain the presentation above. We present the calculations for
a^{2}
:

\displaystyle\langle a,b\;|\;ba^{2}b^{-1}=a^{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a,\lambda,b\;|\;\lambda=a^{2},\;ba^{2}b^{-1}=a^{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a,\lambda,b\;|\;\lambda=a^{2},\;ba^{2}b^{-1}=a^{3},\;a=b\lambda b^{-1}\lambda^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda,b\;|\;\lambda=b\lambda b^{-1}\lambda^{-1}b\lambda b^{-1}\lambda^{-1},\;b\lambda b^{-1}=b\lambda b^{-1}\lambda^{-1}\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda,b\;|\;\lambda^{2}=b\lambda b^{-1}\lambda^{-1}b\lambda b^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda,\mu\;|\;\lambda^{2}=\mu\lambda\mu^{-1}\lambda^{-1}\mu\lambda\mu^{-1}\rangle.

We can obtain the same presentation for
a^{3}
by doing similar calculations.
∎

We define
\phi^{\prime}
to be the morphism

BS(2,3)\rightarrow BS(2,3):\begin{cases}a\mapsto a^{3}\\
b\mapsto b\\
\end{cases}.

From a group presentation point of view the following is true: since

and
a^{3}
behave in a completely similar way the morphisms

and

are in fact also the same morphism! As a consequence they have the same kernel.

Some rigidity properties for λ \lambda -self-expanders Thanks: The first author is supported by CAPES of Brasil. The second author is partially supported by CNPq and Faperj of Brazil.
Saul Ancari, Xu Cheng

Abstract
. λ \lambda -self-expanders Σ \Sigma in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} are the solutions of the isoperimetric problem with respect to the same weighted area form as in the study of the self-expanders. In this paper, we mainly extend the results on self-expanders which we obtained in [ 1 ] to λ \lambda -self-expanders. We prove some results that characterize the hyperplanes, spheres and cylinders as λ \lambda -self-expanders. We also discuss the area growths and the finiteness of the weighted areas under the control of the growth of the mean curvature.

1.
introduction

A
\lambda
-self-expander is a hypersurface
\Sigma
immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
whose mean curvature
H
satisfies the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}+\lambda,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is real number,
x
is the position vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
{\bf n}
is the outward unit normal field on
\Sigma
. Such hypersurfaces appear as solutions to the weighted isoperimetric problem of weight
e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}
and are characterized as critical points of the weighted area functional

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma,

for any compact normal variations
F:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which satisfies
\int_{\Sigma}\varphi e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma=0
, where
\varphi=\langle\partial_{t}F(0,x),{\bf n}(x)\rangle
.

On the other hand, they can also be characterized as constant weighted mean curvature hypersurfaces of weight
e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}
(see Section
2
).

In the case that
\lambda=0
,
\lambda
-self-expanders just are self-expanders of the mean curvature flows, which are critical points of the weighted area functional (
1.2
).

In recent years, as the models of singularity for the mean curvature flow, self-shrinkers, self-expanders and translating solitons have been studied very much.
See, for instance
[

,

4

[

,

,
11
,
23
,
30
]
, respectively. We would like to mention that in some sense the research of self-expanders brings out the interest on
\lambda
-self-expanders. The analogous phenomenon has already happened on the self-shrinkers and translating solitons of MCF. There are notions of constant Gaussian-weighted mean curvature hypersurfaces (also called
\lambda
-hypersurfaces) and
\lambda
-translating solitons, which are linked with self-shrinkers and translating solitons, respectively (see, for instance, the work of Q. Cheng and Wei
[
6
]
, McGonagle and Ross
[
25
]
, and López in
[
20
]
). Various results have been obtained on
\lambda
-hypersurfaces and

and proved that if a

In this paper, we study
\lambda
-self-expanders. All hyperplanes, Spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
centered at the origin of radius
r>0
, and cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
, where
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
r>0
are examples of
\lambda
-self-expanders (see the details in Section

). It is well known that there exists no closed self-expanders in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(see, for instance
[
4

). But the situation is different for
\lambda\neq 0
.
All spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
centered at the origin of radius

\lambda

\lambda=\frac{n}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\geq\sqrt{2n}

Throughout the paper, the following convention of notation are used. The notation

A
denotes the second fundamental form of
\lambda
-self-expander
\Sigma
.
\Sigma
is called convex if its second fundamental form
A
is negative definite, that is, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of
A
satisfy
\lambda_{i}\leq 0
, where
A(e_{i})=-\bar{\nabla}_{e_{i}}{\bf n}=\lambda_{i}e_{i}
,
e_{i}
is a local orthonormal frame.
\Sigma
is called mean convex if its mean curvature
H=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}
is nonnegative. We use
x^{\top}
to denote the tangent component of the position vector
x
.

We give the lower bound of
\lambda
and obtain a gap theorem for closed
\lambda
-self-expanders as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

(i)

\lambda\geq\sqrt{2n}
. Moreover, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\sqrt{2n}}(0)
is the only closed
\lambda
-self-expander with
\lambda=\sqrt{2n}
.

(ii)

If

(1.3)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2n},

then
\Sigma
is a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
with
r=\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}
.

Theorem
1.1
(ii) gives a pinching condition of
|A|
such that a compact
\Sigma
is a sphere. This result can be compared with the following theorem proved by Guang
[
12
]
for
\lambda
-hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a closed
\lambda
-hypersurface with
\lambda\geq 0
. If
\Sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+2n}\right)}{2n},

then
\Sigma
is a round sphere with radius
\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+2n}+\lambda.

We also obtain a condition on
\lambda
and the mean curvature
H
so that a complete
\lambda
-self-expander must be a sphere (see Theorem
4.2
). Besides, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
,
n\geq 2
, be a closed mean convex
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If

(1.4)

\displaystyle H\textup{tr}A^{3}+|A|^{4}\leq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

For a convex hypersurface, note that

\displaystyle H\textup{tr}A^{3}+|A|^{4}=-\sum_{i<j}^{n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\leq 0.

Therefore, (
1.4
) holds and a consequence of Theorem
1.3
is as follows.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
,
n\geq 2
, be a closed convex
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then
\Sigma
must be a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Next, we study complete
\lambda
-self-expanders with some integrability condition on the norm of the second fundamental form. Now we recall some related results. In
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
, the second author of the present paper and Zhou proved the uniqueness of hyperplanes through the origin for mean convex self-expander hypersurfaces under some condition on the square of the norm of the second fundamental form.

In
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, Q. Cheng and Wei proved that a complete embedded
\lambda
-hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with polynomial area growth (there exist a constant
C>0
and
r_{0}>0
such that
Area(B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma)\leq Cr^{\alpha}
, for all
r\geq r_{0}
and for some
\alpha>0
) satisfying
H-\lambda\geq 0
and
\lambda((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{2})\leq 0
has to be a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l},

0\leq l\leq n
. Later, the same classification theorem as Q. Cheng and Wei was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 3]
by the first author of the present paper and Miranda, replacing the polynomial area growth by a weaker intrinsic property on the norm of the second fundamental form. Motivated by the above work, we obtain the following result on
\lambda
-self-expanders.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Sigma
,
n\geq 2
, be a complete
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If
H-\lambda\leq 0
,
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lambda((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2})\geq 0,

then
\lambda\geq 0
and
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The equality in (
1.5
) holds if
\Sigma
is either a hyerplane or
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
, but the cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n-1
do not satisfy the property that
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
. This is the reason why the theorem
1.4
does not characterize the cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n-1
.

Recently, Smoczyk
[
27
, Theorem 1]
proved that complete self-expanders
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
H\neq 0
are products of self-expander curves and flat subspaces, if and only if the function
\frac{|A|^{2}}{H^{2}}
attains a local maximum. Motivated by this result, we obtain the following result, that characterizes generalized cylinders as
\lambda
-self-expanders.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\lambda\neq 0
. Then

(i)

the open set
\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
must be non-empty.

(ii)

Further, if the following conditions are satisfied on the non-empty set
\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
,

(a)

\frac{\lambda}{H-\lambda}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right)\leq 0,

(b)

the function
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
attains a local maximum,

then
\Sigma
is either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{l}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

We also prove that

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If there exists
\alpha>0
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H+\frac{1}{2}H^{2}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha+1)^{2}}{8}|x^{\top}|^{2}\right)\leq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Using the Simons’ type equations, we prove Proposition
6.1
, which states that if a complete immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
has constant mean curvature, then it must be either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

In the last Section
7
, we mainly extend some of our previous results on self-expanders in
[
1

to the

deals with finitiness of weighted areas and area growth estimate of

It is interesting to see that unlike self-expanders, hyperplanes not through the origin or a cylinder

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some concepts and basic facts.

Let
\Sigma
denote a hypersurface immersed in the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g_{0})
with the induced metric
g
. We will denote by
d\sigma
the area form of
\Sigma
. In this paper, unless otherwise specified, the notations with a bar, for instance
\bar{\nabla}
and
\bar{\nabla}^{2}
, denote the quantities corresponding the Euclidean metric
g_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. On the other hand, the notations like
\nabla,\Delta
denote the quantities corresponding the intrinsic metric
{g}
on
\Sigma
.

The isometric immersion
i:(\Sigma,g)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},g_{0})
is said to be properly immersed if, for any compact subset
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the pre-image
i^{-1}(\Omega)
is compact in
\Sigma
.

Let
A
denote the second fundamental form of
(\Sigma,g)
. At
p\in\Sigma
,

A(X)=-\bar{\nabla}_{X}{\bf n},

where
X\in T_{p}\Sigma
and
{\bf n}
is the outward unit normal field on
\Sigma
. The mean curvature
H
of
\Sigma
is defined as
the trace of
-A
.

Given a smooth function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, it induces a smooth measure
e^{-f}d\sigma
on
(\Sigma,g)
. Define the weighted mean curvature
H_{f}
of
\Sigma
of weight
e^{-f}
by

H_{f}:=H-\langle\bar{\nabla}f,{\bf n}\rangle.

\Sigma
is called constant weighted mean curvature (or simply by CWMC) hypersurface (of weight
e^{-f}
) if
H_{f}=\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, or equivalently if it satisfies

(2.1)

H=\langle\bar{\nabla}f,{\bf n}\rangle+\lambda.

If
\lambda=0
,
\Sigma
is called
f
-minimal. There are very interesting examples of
f
-minimal hypersurfaces:

Example 2.1
.

If
f=\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}
,
-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}
, and
-\left<x,w\right>
respectively, where
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a constant vector, a
f
-minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker, self-expander and translator for MCF in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
respectively.

Taking
f=-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}
in (
2.1
), a CWMC hypersurface
\Sigma
of weight
e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is just a
\lambda
-self-expander defined by (
1.1
),
that is,
\Sigma
satisfies the equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}+\lambda.

Here are some examples of
\lambda
-self-expanders.

Example 2.2
.

Any hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a
\lambda
-self-expander with
\lambda=\pm\frac{d}{2}
, where
d
denotes the distance from the origin to the hyperplane and the sign depends on the orientation. Indeed, let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a hyperplane. Let
{\bf n}
be the unit normal of
\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle=d\}
or
\Sigma=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle=-d\}
. In both cases,
we have

\displaystyle H=-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}+\lambda.

This implies that
\Sigma
is a
\lambda
-self-expander with
\lambda=\frac{d}{2}
or
-\frac{d}{2}
.

Example 2.3
.

All spheres
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
centered at the origin of radius
r>0
are
\lambda
-self-expanders with
\lambda=\frac{n}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\geq\sqrt{2n}
. For each
\lambda>\sqrt{2n}
, there are two spheres
S^{n}_{r}(0)
of radius
r=\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}
as
\lambda
-self-expanders with this
\lambda
.

Moreover, a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(p)
is
\lambda
-self-expander if and only if its center
p
is the origin. In fact, let
{\bf{n}}
be the outward unit normal of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(p)
. We have
H=\frac{n}{r}
,
\langle x-p,{\bf{n}}\rangle=r
, and
hence
H+\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}=\frac{n}{r}+\frac{r}{2}-\frac{\langle p,\bf{n}\rangle}{2}=\lambda.
Therefore
\langle p,\bf{n}\rangle
must be constant and thus
p
must be the origin.

Example 2.4
.

The cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
, where
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
r>0
, are also
\lambda
-self-expanders with
\lambda=\frac{k}{r}+\frac{r}{2}\geq\sqrt{2k}
. Each
\lambda>\sqrt{2k}
corresponds two cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
of radius
r=\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2k}
.

Analogous to the argument in Example
2.3
, the only cylinders
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{r}(p)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
as
\lambda
-self-expanders are
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Here we mention that
\lambda
-hypersurfaces defined in
[
6
]
and
\lambda
-translating solitons defined in
[
20
]
are CWMC hypersurfaces of weight
e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}
and
e^{\left<x,w\right>}
respectively, where
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a constant vector.

Now we state the equivalent characterization of CWMC hypersurface.

The weighted area of a measurable subset
S\subset\Sigma
with respect to the weigth
e^{-f}
is defined by

(2.3)

\mathcal{A}_{f}(S):=\int_{S}e^{-f}d\sigma.

It is known that an
f
-minimal hypersurface is a critical point of the weighted area functional defined in (
2.3
).
In
[
25
]
, McGonagle-Ross proved that a CWMC hypersurface
\Sigma
of weight
e^{-f}
is also the critical points of the weighted area functional (
2.3
)
but for compact normal variations
F:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
that preserve weighted volume, i.e. for variations
F
which satisfies
\int_{\Sigma}\varphi e^{-f}d\sigma=0
, where
\varphi=\langle\partial_{t}F(0,x),{\bf n}(x)\rangle
.

On the smooth metric measure space
(\Sigma,g,e^{-f})
, there is a very important second-order elliptic operator: the drifted Laplacian

\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\cdot\right\rangle.

It is well known that
\Delta_{f}
is a densely defined self-adjoint
operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{-f}d\sigma)
, i.e. for

u
and
v
in
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
, it holds that

(2.4)

\int_{\Sigma}(\Delta_{f}u)ve^{-f}d\sigma=-\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\right\rangle e^{-f}d\sigma.

In particular, if
\Sigma
is a
\lambda
-self-expander, we denote by
\mathscr{L}
the corresponding drifted Laplacian on
\Sigma
, that is,
\mathscr{L}=\Delta+\frac{1}{2}\left<x,\nabla\cdot\right>
.

3.
Simons’ type equations

In this section, we give some
Simons’ type equations for
\lambda
-self-expanders.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\Sigma
is a
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{H}{2},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}A=-\frac{1}{2}A-|A|^{2}A-\lambda A^{2},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}=2|\nabla A|^{2}-|A|^{2}-2|A|^{4}-2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|=\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}-|\nabla|A||^{2}}{|A|}-\frac{\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}}{|A|}-|A|^{3}-\frac{|A|}{2}.

Proof.

Let us fix a point
p\in\Sigma
, and choose a local orthonormal frame
{e_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, for
\Sigma
such that
\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}(p)=0
. Let
h_{ij}=\langle\bar{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{j},{\bf n}\rangle
. At this point
p
we have

\displaystyle Hess(H)(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e_{j}}\nabla_{e_{i}}(-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}+\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}e_{j}\langle x,-h_{ik}e_{k}\rangle

(3.5)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(-h_{ij}-\langle x,h_{ijk}e_{k}\rangle-\langle x,h_{ik}h_{jk}{\bf n}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle A(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle(\nabla_{x^{\top}}A)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle\langle A^{2}(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle A(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle(\nabla_{x^{\top}}A)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle-(H-\lambda)\langle A^{2}(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle.

This implies that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Delta H=-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\nabla H\rangle-(H-\lambda)|A|^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{H}{2}.

So we have proved (
3.1
).

In order to prove (
3.2
), recall the Simons’ equation, that is

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Delta A=-Hess(H)-HA^{2}-|A|^{2}A.

Combining (
3.5
) with (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta A=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x^{\top}}A-\frac{1}{2}A-|A|^{2}A-\lambda A^{2}.

Hence

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}A=-\frac{1}{2}A-|A|^{2}A-\lambda A^{2},

which is (
3.2
). For (
3.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle\mathscr{L}A,A\rangle+2|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle=2|\nabla A|^{2}-|A|^{2}-2|A|^{4}-2\lambda\langle A^{2},A\rangle.

Further
\langle A^{2},A\rangle=\textup{tr}A^{3}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}=2|\nabla A|^{2}-|A|^{2}-2|A|^{4}-2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3},

which is just (
3.3
).

(
3.4
) follows from (
3.3
) and the following identity

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}=2|A|\mathscr{L}|A|+2|\nabla|A||^{2}.

∎

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\Delta-\frac{\alpha}{2}\langle x,\nabla\cdot\rangle
on the
\lambda
-self-expander
\Sigma
. We get the following equations.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\langle x,\nabla H\rangle,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}A(x^{\top},x^{\top}),

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H^{2}=

\displaystyle-2|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-H^{2}+2\left|\nabla H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}Hx^{\top}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha+1)^{2}}{8}H^{2}|x^{\top}|^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{L}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{H}{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\langle x,\nabla H\rangle.

Take a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
,
i=1,...,n,
for
\Sigma
. From
H=\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle
,

\displaystyle 2\nabla_{e_{i}}H=

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}x,{\bf n}\rangle-\langle x,\nabla_{e_{i}}{\bf n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{ij}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle

and hence

\displaystyle\langle x,\nabla H\rangle=\langle x,e_{i}\rangle\nabla_{e_{i}}H=\frac{1}{2}h_{ij}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle\langle x,e_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}A(x^{\top},x^{\top}).

By this and Equation (
3.9
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H

\displaystyle=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\langle x,\nabla H\rangle

\displaystyle=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}A(x^{\top},x^{\top}).

On the other hand, combining (
3.9
) and the equality
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H^{2}=2H\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H+2|\nabla H|^{2}
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H^{2}=

\displaystyle-2|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-H^{2}-(\alpha+1)H\langle x,\nabla H\rangle+2|\nabla H|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-H^{2}+2\left|\nabla H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}Hx^{\top}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{(\alpha+1)^{2}}{8}H^{2}|x^{\top}|^{2}.

∎

4.
Rigidity of spheres

In this section, we will prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
which characterize the spheres as closed
\lambda
-self-expanders. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need the following

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be an immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|x|^{2}=|x|^{2}-2\lambda\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}|x|^{2}=(2H-\lambda)^{2}+2n-\lambda^{2}-|x^{\top}|^{2},

where the operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}\langle x^{\top},\nabla\cdot\rangle
.

Proof.

Recall that for any hypersurface, we have
\Delta x=-H{\bf n}
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta|x|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\langle x,\Delta x\rangle+2|\nabla x|^{2}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=-2H\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n.

Since
H=\lambda-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|x|^{2}=\Delta|x|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\nabla|x|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2H\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n+|x^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\lambda-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}\right)\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n+|x^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|x|^{2}-2\lambda\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}|x|^{2}=\Delta|x|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\nabla|x|^{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle-2H\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle+2n-|x^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=4H(H-\lambda)+2n-|x^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=4H^{2}-4\lambda H+2n-|x^{\top}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(2H-\lambda)^{2}+2n-\lambda^{2}-|x^{\top}|^{2}.

∎

Using (
4.1
), we prove Theorem
1.1
, that is

Theorem 4.1
.

(Theorem
1.1
)
Let
\Sigma
be a closed
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

(i)

\lambda\geq\sqrt{2n}
. Moreover, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\sqrt{2n}}(0)
is the only closed
\lambda
-self-expander with
\lambda=\sqrt{2n}
.

(ii)

If

(4.4)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2n},

then
\Sigma
is a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
with
r=\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is closed, there exists a point
p\neq 0
where
|x|
achieves its maximum. At
p
,
x
and
{\bf n}
are in the same direction, and
H(p)\geq\frac{n}{|p|}
. This implies that
\lambda=H(p)+\frac{\langle p,{\bf n}(p)\rangle}{2}\geq\frac{n}{|p|}+\frac{|p|}{2}\geq 2\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{|p|}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{|p|}}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2n}
.

If
\lambda=\sqrt{{2n}}
, (
4.1
) implies

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|x|^{2}\geq(|x|-\lambda)^{2}-\lambda^{2}+2n=(|x|-\lambda)^{2}\geq 0.

By the maximum principle,
|x|
must be constant. We conclude that
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\sqrt{2n}}(0)
. So (i) is confirmed.

Now we prove (ii). Since
\Sigma
is closed, we consider the point
p
where
|x|
achieves its minimum. We claim that
p\neq 0
. In fact, if
p=0
, then
H(p)=\lambda
. By (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}=H^{2}(p)\leq n|A|^{2}\leq-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2}.

This implies

0\leq-\frac{n}{2}-\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2},

which is a contradiction. Therefore
p\neq 0
.

Now, since
p\neq 0
, it follows that either
H(p)=\lambda+\frac{|x|(p)}{2}
or
H(p)=\lambda-\frac{|x|(p)}{2}
.

We claim that
H(p)=\lambda-\frac{|x|(p)}{2}
, which is equivalent to say that
\langle p,{\bf n}\rangle=|x|(p)
. In fact, if
H(p)=\lambda+\frac{|x|(p)}{2}
, by (
4.4
) we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda+\frac{|x|(p)}{2}\right)^{2}=H^{2}(p)\leq n|A|^{2}\leq-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2}.

By (i), we know that
\Sigma
satisfies
\lambda\geq\sqrt{2n}
. This property and (
4.6
) imply that

|x|(p)\leq-\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n},

which is a contradiction. Therefore, we have confirmed the claim.

Applying (
4.4
) again, we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left(\lambda-\frac{|x|(p)}{2}\right)^{2}=H^{2}(p)\leq n|A|^{2}\leq n\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}\right)}{2n}\right),

which gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle|x|(p)\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda}(|x|^{2}(p)-2\lambda|x|(p)+2n)+\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}.

Further, since
|x|
achieves its minimum at
p
, by (
4.1
), we have

\mathscr{L}|x|(p)=|x|^{2}(p)-2\lambda|x|(p)+2n\geq 0.

Therefore, (
4.8
) implies

(4.9)

\displaystyle\min_{\Sigma}|x|=|x|(p)\geq\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n}.

Combining (
4.9
) with (
4.1
) yields

\mathscr{L}(|x|^{2})\geq 0.

By the maximum principle,
|x|^{2}=constant
for
\Sigma
. We conclude that
\Sigma
is
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

∎

For complete
\lambda
-self-expanders, (
4.2
) in Lemma
4.1
has the following consequence:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If
\Sigma
satisfies

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\geq 2n+(\lambda-2H)^{2},

then
\Sigma
is a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

By (
4.2
) and hypothesis (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}|x|^{2}=(2H-\lambda)^{2}+2n-\lambda^{2}-|x^{\top}|^{2}\leq 0.

Since
\Sigma
is proper,
|x|^{2}
achieves its minimum. By the maximum principle we conclude that
\Sigma
is
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

The condition (
4.10
) implies that
H
is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}(|\lambda|+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-2n})
. Then, the Gaussian weighted mean curvature
H-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}
of
\Sigma
is
2H-\lambda
and also bounded. In
[
7
]
, the second author of the present paper, Vieira and Zhou (
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
) proved that if
\Sigma
is an immersed hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, with bounded Gaussian weighted mean curvature, then the polynomial area growth is equivalent to the properness of the hypersurface. Therefore, in Theorem
4.2
, it is possible to replace condition of properness of the hypersurface by the polynomial area growth condition.

Now we prove Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

(Theorem
1.3
) Let
\Sigma
,
n\geq 2
, be a closed mean convex
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If

(4.11)

\displaystyle H\textup{tr}A^{3}+|A|^{4}\leq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

Let us consider
w=\frac{\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}{H+\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant. Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=(H+\varepsilon)\mathscr{L}w+w\mathscr{L}H+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle

(4.12)

\displaystyle=(H+\varepsilon)\mathscr{L}w+w(-|A|^{2}H-\frac{1}{2}H+\lambda|A|^{2})+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle.

On the other hand, by (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}\left(\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}-2\left|\nabla\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}\left(|\nabla A|^{2}-\left|\nabla\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}\left(-|A|^{2}-2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}-2|A|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}\left(-|A|^{2}-2|A|^{4}-2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}\right).

Combining this with (
4.12
) yields

\displaystyle(H+\varepsilon)^{2}w\mathscr{L}w\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}-|A|^{4}-\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}{H+\varepsilon}(-|A|^{2}H-\frac{1}{2}H+\lambda|A|^{2})

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle(H+\varepsilon)w

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{-\lambda(H\textup{tr}A^{3}+|A|^{4})}{H+\varepsilon}-\frac{\varepsilon((1+2\lambda)|A|^{2}+2|A|^{4}+2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3})}{2(H+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle(H+\varepsilon)w.

Noting that
\Sigma
is closed, by Theorem
4.1
,
\lambda\geq\sqrt{2n}
. Further, from (
4.11
) we deduce that
\textup{tr}A^{3}\leq 0
and
|A|\leq H
. Therefore

\displaystyle(H+\varepsilon)^{2}w\mathscr{L}w

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\varepsilon((1+2\lambda)|A|^{2}+2|A|^{4})}{2(H+\varepsilon)}-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle(H+\varepsilon)w

\displaystyle\geq-\varepsilon\left(\frac{(1+2\lambda)}{2}|A|+|A|^{3}\right)-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle(H+\varepsilon)w.

Using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(H+\varepsilon)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}(H+\varepsilon)^{2}w\mathscr{L}(w)e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}(H+\varepsilon)w\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{(1+2\lambda)}{2}|A|+|A|^{3}\right)e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma}(H+\varepsilon)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

Let
p\in\mathcal{S}:=\{x\in\Sigma;\mbox{ }H(x)\neq 0\}
, and
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(p)\subset\mathcal{S}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{r}(p)}(H+\varepsilon)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
\frac{|A|}{H}
is constant in
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(p)
. Since
p
is
arbitrary,
\frac{|A|}{H}
must be a constant on each connected component of
\mathcal{S}
. By (
3.1
), (
3.4
) and (
4.11
), we conclude that
|\nabla A|=|\nabla|A||
on
\mathcal{S}
. Now we use an argument similar to the one used by Huisken in
[
14
]
(See, e.g. the proof of Theorem 0.17 in
[
10
]
). Since
\Sigma
is closed, there exists
q\in\mathcal{S}
such that rank
A=n\geq 2
and then
|\nabla A|=0
on
\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the connected component of
\mathcal{S}
containing
q
. In particular,
|A|
and
H
are positive constants on
\mathcal{D}
. Using a continuity argument, we have
\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
and thus
\mathcal{D}=\Sigma
. Hence, on
\Sigma
,
|\nabla A|=0
and
H
is a positive constant. By a Lawson’s result
[
17
, Theorem 4]
,
\Sigma
must be a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.
∎

5.
Rigidity of hyperplanes and spheres

In this section, we will prove some rigidity results that characterize the hyperplanes and spheres as
\lambda
-self-expanders.

First, we prove Theorem
1.4
, which is,

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem
1.4
)
.

Let
\Sigma
,
n\geq 2
, be a complete
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If
H-\lambda\leq 0
,
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
and

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lambda((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2})\geq 0,

then
\lambda\geq 0
and
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\leq 0
, from Lemma
3.1
and the hypothesis
H-\lambda\leq 0
we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H\geq 0.

Since
H-\lambda\leq 0
, by the maximum principle we have either
H-\lambda\equiv 0
or
H-\lambda<0
. If
H-\lambda\equiv 0
,
\Sigma
must be a hyperplane.

For
\lambda>0
and
H-\lambda=0
at some point
p\in\Sigma
, from hypothesis (
5.1
)

\displaystyle-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}=(H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\geq 0

at
p\in\Sigma
. This implies that
|A|(p)=0
, and thus
0=H(p)=\lambda
. But this contradicts the fact that
\lambda>0.

To complete the proof, we only need to consider the case
H-\lambda<0
. Define the function

\displaystyle w=\frac{\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}}{H-\lambda}.

Using
(
3.1
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle w\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)+(H-\lambda)\mathscr{L}w+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\left[-\frac{\lambda}{2(H-\lambda)}-\bigg(|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\right]+(H-\lambda)\mathscr{L}w

\displaystyle+2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle.

Hence

\displaystyle(H-\lambda)^{2}w\mathscr{L}w=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\mathscr{L}\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle w(H-\lambda)

(5.3)

\displaystyle+(|A|^{2}+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\lambda}{2(H-\lambda)}+|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

On the other hand, (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\mathscr{L}\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{L}|A|^{2}-2\left|\nabla\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}-\left|\nabla\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(|A|^{2}+2\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}+2|A|^{4}\right).

Using the inequality
|\nabla A|^{2}-\left|\nabla\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\geq 0
, we obtain

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\mathscr{L}\sqrt{|A|^{2}+\varepsilon}\geq-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}-\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}-|A|^{4}.

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) it follows that

\displaystyle(H-\lambda)^{2}w\mathscr{L}w\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-H}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right)+\varepsilon|A|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon H}{2(H-\lambda)}

(5.5)

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle w(H-\lambda).

Since
H-\lambda<0
and
\lambda\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 0
, (
5.5
) yields

(5.6)

\displaystyle(H-\lambda)^{2}w\mathscr{L}w\geq\frac{\varepsilon H}{2(H-\lambda)}-2\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\rangle w(H-\lambda).

For a nonnegative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
, using integration by part and (
5.6
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}w\mathscr{L}we^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}\left\langle\nabla w,\nabla H\right\rangle w(H-\lambda)e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle\varphi(H-\lambda)\nabla w,w(H-\lambda)\nabla\varphi\right\rangle e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\varepsilon H\varphi^{2}}{2(\lambda-H)}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}w^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Therefore,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\varepsilon H\varphi^{2}}{\lambda-H}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+4\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle+4\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Now, for the following set

\mathcal{S}=\{p\in\Sigma;|A|(p)=0\},

we consider two cases separately:

Case

({\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset})
. Since
H-\lambda<0
and
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, we get
\lambda>0
. First, we claim that
\Sigma
is noncompact. In fact, if
\Sigma
is compact, then taking
\varphi=1
in (
5.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}:=\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{S}
is not empty. Let
q\in\mathcal{D}
and
B_{r}^{\Sigma}(q)\subset\mathcal{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{r}^{\Sigma}(q)}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
\frac{|A|}{H-\lambda}
is constant in
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)
. Since
q
is
arbitrary, it is possible to conclude that
\frac{|A|}{H-\lambda}
is constant on each connected component of
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, using a continuity argument, we conclude that
|A|\equiv 0
, but this contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{D}\neq\emptyset
. Hence
\mathcal{S}=\Sigma
. In particular
\Sigma
must be minimal, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we have confirmed the claim.

Now we assume that
\Sigma
is noncompact. For a fixed point
p\in\Sigma
, choose
\varphi=\varphi_{k}
, where
\varphi_{k}
are nonnegative cut-off functions, such that
\varphi_{k}=1
on
B^{\Sigma}_{k}(p)
,
\varphi_{k}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{k}
for every
k
. Substitute
\varphi
in (
5.7
):

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{k}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}\frac{H\varphi_{k}^{2}}{\lambda-H}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+4\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle+4\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)}\frac{|H|}{\lambda-H}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{k^{2}}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)\setminus B^{\Sigma}_{k}(p)}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+\frac{4\varepsilon}{k^{2}}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{k}\left(\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)}\frac{|H|}{\lambda-H}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\right)^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{k}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}+\frac{4}{k^{2}}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)\setminus B^{\Sigma}_{k}(p)}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+\frac{4}{k^{3}}.

In the above,
w_{k}
denotes
w
in which
\varepsilon
has been substituted.

Since
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{k}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

Given that,
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, consider
\mathcal{D}=\Sigma\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is an open set, let
q\in\mathcal{D}
and
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)\subset\mathcal{D}
. For
k
sufficiently large,
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)\subset\textup{supp}\varphi_{k}
and
\varphi_{k}=1
on
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)
. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

By the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
\frac{|A|}{H-\lambda}
is constant on
B^{\Sigma}_{r}(q)
. Since
q
is arbitrary, it is possible to conclude that
\frac{|A|}{H-\lambda}
is constant on
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, using a continuity argument, we conclude that
|A|=0
on
\Sigma
. We conclude that
\Sigma
is a hyperplane.

Case
(
\mathcal{S}=\emptyset
). Choosing
\varepsilon=0
in (
5.7
), we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq 4\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

In the case
\Sigma
is compact, choose
\varphi=1
. Otherwise, fixing a point
p\in\Sigma
, consider
\varphi=\varphi_{k}
, where
\varphi_{k}
are nonnegative cut-off function, such that
\varphi_{k}=1
on
B^{\Sigma}_{k}(p)
,
\varphi_{k}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{k}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{k}(H-\lambda)^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{k}|^{2}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{k^{2}}\int_{B^{\Sigma}_{2k}(p)\setminus B^{\Sigma}_{k}(p)}|A|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Since
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
, letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.9
) and using the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(H-\lambda)^{2}\left|\nabla\frac{|A|}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

This implies that
|A|=C(H-\lambda)
, for a constant
C<0.
So from (
3.1
), it follows that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|+\left(|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)|A|=-\frac{\lambda|A|}{2(H-\lambda)}.

Combining (
3.4
) and (
5.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}-|\nabla|A||^{2}}{|A|}=\frac{\lambda}{(H-\lambda)}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right).

Further, by hypothesis
\lambda\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 0
, and
H-\lambda<0
. Therefore

|\nabla A|=|\nabla|A||.

Now again, as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we use an argument similar to the Huisken’s in
[
14
]
( See, e.g. the proof of Theorem 0.17 in
[
10
]
). Following the proof in
[
10
]
,
|\nabla A|=|\nabla|A||
implies two possible sub-cases: (I) If the rank of
A
is greater than 2,
\nabla A=0
. By Theorem 4 in
[
17
]
, it follows that
\Sigma
is either
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
or
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
2\leq l\leq n-1
. Since
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
2\leq l\leq n-1
, do not satisfy the hypothesis that
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
, we conclude that
\Sigma
must be
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
. (II) If the rank of
A
is 1, then
\Sigma
is the product of a curve
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a
(n-1)
-dimensional hyperplane, but this contradicts the fact that
|A|\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
.

∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.6
.

Theorem 5.2

(Theorem
1.6
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. If there exists
\alpha>0
such that

(5.11)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H+\frac{1}{2}H^{2}\left(1+\frac{(\alpha+1)^{2}}{8}|x^{\top}|^{2}\right)\leq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
, from (
3.11
) and hypothesis (
5.11
), we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2}\left|\nabla H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}x^{\top}H\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}\varphi H^{2}\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla H^{2}\rangle e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H^{2}|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H^{2}|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}+2\int_{\Sigma}H^{4}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

Therefore

(5.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H^{4}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2}\left|\nabla H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}x^{\top}H\right|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H^{2}|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

On the other hand, from (
5.11
) it follows that
(H-\lambda)H\leq 0
. In particular
|H|\leq|\lambda|.
Thus, by Corollary
7.2
, which will be proved later in this paper , we have that
\int_{\Sigma}H^{4}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}<\infty.
Choose
\varphi=\varphi_{j}
in (
5.12
), where
\varphi_{j}
are nonnegative cut-off functions satisfying that
\varphi_{j}=1
on
B_{j}(0)
,
\varphi_{j}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{j+1}(0)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{j}|\leq 1
. Using the monotone convergence theorem, it follows that

H^{2}\left|\nabla H-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}x^{\top}H\right|^{2}=0\mbox{ and }|\nabla H^{2}|^{2}=0.

This implies that
H
is constant and
H^{4}\left|x^{\top}\right|^{2}=0
. If
H\equiv 0
, then
\Sigma
is a hyperplane. If
H\neq 0
, then
|x^{\top}|=0
,
|x|^{2}=|\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle|^{2}=4(\lambda-H)^{2}
is constant. Thus
\Sigma
is a sphere.
∎

6.
Rigidity of generalized cylinders

In this section, using the maximum principle we will prove Theorem
1.5
that characterizes the generalized cylinders as
\lambda
-self-expander hypersurfaces.

Theorem 6.1

(Theorem
1.5
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\lambda\neq 0
. Then

(i)

the open set
\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
must be non-empty.

(ii)

Further, if the following conditions are satisfied on the non-empty set
\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
,

(a)

\frac{\lambda}{H-\lambda}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right)\leq 0,

(b)

the function
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
attains a local maximum,

then
\Sigma
is either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}_{r}^{l}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

First we confirm
(i)
. In fact, by
contradiction, assume that
H-\lambda\equiv 0
on
\Sigma
. From (
3.1
) we conclude that
\lambda=0
, a contradiction. Therefore, the set
\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
is non-empty.

Now we prove
(ii)
. By hypothesis that the function
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
attains a local maximum on the set
\mathcal{D}=\{x\in\Sigma;H\neq\lambda\}
, there is a point
p\in\mathcal{D}
and a
connected open neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{D}
of
p
such that
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}(p)=\max\limits_{U}\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
.
Computing
\mathscr{L}\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
on
U
we get

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}=2\left\langle\mathscr{L}\frac{A}{H-\lambda},\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right\rangle+2\left|\nabla\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}.

Further

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\frac{A}{H-\lambda}=\frac{1}{H-\lambda}\mathscr{L}A+A\mathscr{L}\frac{1}{H-\lambda}-\frac{2}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\nabla_{\nabla(H-\lambda)}A,

and

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\frac{1}{H-\lambda}=-\frac{\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}+2\frac{|\nabla(H-\lambda)|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}.

Substituting (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) into (
6.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\langle\mathscr{L}A,A\rangle+\frac{2|A|^{2}}{H-\lambda}\left(-\frac{\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}+2\frac{|\nabla(H-\lambda)|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\langle\nabla_{\nabla(H-\lambda)}A,A\rangle+2\left|\nabla\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\langle\mathscr{L}A,A\rangle-\frac{2|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)+\frac{4|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{4}}|\nabla(H-\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\langle\nabla|A|^{2},\nabla(H-\lambda)\rangle+2\left|\nabla\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\langle\mathscr{L}A,A\rangle-\frac{2|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\mathscr{L}(H-\lambda)

(6.4)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}},\nabla(\log(H-\lambda)^{2})\right\rangle+2\left|\nabla\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}.

From (
6.4
), (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) it follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\right)=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}},\nabla\log(H-\lambda)^{2}\right\rangle+2\left|\nabla\frac{A}{H-\lambda}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\lambda}{(H-\lambda)^{3}}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right).

Combining this with the hypothesis
(a)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}\right)+\left\langle\nabla\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}},\nabla\log(H-\lambda)^{2}\right\rangle\geq 0.

Since
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}
attains a local maximum, the maximum principle implies that
\frac{|A|^{2}}{(H-\lambda)^{2}}=c
, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative constant. Since
H-\lambda\neq 0
, either
|A|=\sqrt{c}(H-\lambda)
or
|A|=-\sqrt{c}(H-\lambda)
.
If
c=0
,
U
must be contained in a hyperplane. If
c>0
, from (
3.1
), it follows that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}|A|+\left(|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)|A|=-\frac{\lambda|A|}{2(H-\lambda)}.

Combining (
3.4
) and (
6.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}-|\nabla|A||^{2}=\frac{\lambda}{(H-\lambda)}\left((H-\lambda)\textup{tr}A^{3}-\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}\right).

Since
|\nabla A|^{2}-|\nabla|A||^{2}\geq 0
, using the hypothesis
(a)
and the equality above, we conclude that

(6.6)

\displaystyle|\nabla A|=|\nabla|A||.

Now again, as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, (
6.6
) implies two possibilities: (I) If rank of
A
is greater than 2,
\nabla A=0
. By the result of Lawson in
[
17
]
, it follows that
U
must be contained in
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
2\leq l\leq n
. (II) If rank of
A
is 1, then

\displaystyle H^{2}=|A|^{2}=c(H-\lambda)^{2}.

This implies that
H
and
|A|
are constants. In particular, by (
6.6
),
\nabla A=0
. By the result of Lawson in
[
17
]
,
U
must be contained in
\mathbb{S}^{1}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Therefore, we have proved that
\Sigma
is locally contained in either a hyperplane or a
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
. Applying the unique continuation properties for solutions of elliptic equations, we conclude that
\Sigma
is either a hyperplane or a generalized cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.
∎

Now we prove a result (Proposition
6.1
) on the immersed complete
\lambda
-self-expanders with constant mean curvature.
In
[
3
, Corollary 3.2]
, the first author of the present article and Miranda proved that
a complete
\lambda
-hypersurface is either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
. Since a
\lambda
-self-expander with constant mean curvature is also a
(2H-\lambda)
-hypersurface, Proposition
6.1
can be obtained by Corollary 3.2 in
[
3
]
. In the following, we give a direct proof for
\lambda
-self-expanders for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
\Sigma
is a complete
\lambda
-self-expander immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with constant mean curvature, then
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

If
H=0
, (
3.1
) implies that
\lambda|A|^{2}=0
. Then
\lambda=0
or
|A|^{2}\equiv 0
on
\Sigma
. If
\lambda=0
,
\Sigma
is a self-expander and must be a hyperplane through the origin. If
|A|^{2}=0
,
\Sigma
also be a hyperplane.

If
H\neq 0
, it follows that from (
3.1
),

(6.7)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}H-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)=0.

This implies that
H\neq\lambda
. Then
|A|
is constant and
\lambda\neq 0
. From (
3.3
), we get

(6.8)

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}=\frac{|A|^{2}}{2}+\lambda\textup{tr}A^{3}+|A|^{4}.

On the other hand, by the following Simons’ equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|A|^{2}=|\nabla A|^{2}-\left\langle A,Hess(H)\right\rangle-H\textup{tr}A^{3}-|A|^{4},

it follows that

(6.9)

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}=|A|^{4}+H\textup{tr}A^{3}.

Then, by (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), we have that

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}=|A|^{4}+\frac{|A|^{2}H}{2(H-\lambda)}.

This and (
6.7
) imply that

\displaystyle|\nabla A|=0.

So, by a result of Lawson in
[
17
, Theorem 4]
,
\Sigma
must be a generalized cylinder.

∎

7.
Further discussion

In this section, we first prove the following result which may be compared with Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume that
H^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{N}
and

(7.1)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}((2H-\lambda)^{2}+2n-\lambda^{2})\leq 0.

Then
\Sigma
is either a hyperplane or a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{r}(0)
.

Proof.

From hypothesis (
7.1
) and (
3.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle H\mathscr{L}H

\displaystyle=-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-\frac{H^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-\frac{n|A|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}|A|^{2}(4(H-\lambda)H+2n)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}|A|^{2}((2H-\lambda)^{2}-\lambda^{2}+2n)

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
. Integrating by part, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H^{2\delta-2}\varphi^{2}H(\mathscr{L}H)e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}H^{2\delta-1}\varphi\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla H\rangle e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle-(2\delta-1)\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2(\delta-1)}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{2\delta-1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2(\delta-1)}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{2\delta-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}H^{2\delta}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}},

where
\delta\geq 1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{2\delta-1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}H^{2(\delta-1)}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq\frac{2}{2\delta-1}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}H^{2\delta}e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

Choose
\varphi=\varphi_{j}
, where
\varphi_{j}
are nonnegative cut-off functions satisfying that
\varphi_{j}=1
on
B_{j}^{\Sigma}(p)
,
\varphi_{j}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{2j}^{\Sigma}(p)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{j}
. By the monotone convergence theorem and the hypothesis that
H^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Sigma,e^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma)
, it follows that, on
\Sigma
,

H^{2(\delta-1)}|\nabla H|^{2}=0.

This implies that
H
is constant. If
H=0
,
\Sigma
is a hyperplane. If
H\neq 0
, by Proposition
4.2
,
\Sigma
is at most a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
. However, the integrability hypothesis of
H^{\delta}
implies that
\Sigma
must be a sphere.
∎

In
[
1
]
, by using properties on the finiteness of weighted areas and the area growth upper estimate for self-expanders with some restriction on mean curvature, we proved some theorems that characterize the hyperplanes through the origin as self-expanders (
[
1
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4]
). Here, motivated by the work
[
1
]
we will prove some results that characterize the generalized cylinders as
\lambda
-self-expanders. In order to prove these results, we first prove the following Theorem
7.2
, which deals with finitiness of weighted areas and area growth estimate of
\lambda
-self-expanders.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume that its mean curvature
H
satisfies
|H|(x)\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma
, for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b>0
. Then it holds that, for any
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}},

(i)

\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}d\sigma<\infty.

(ii)

The area of
B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma
satisfies

\displaystyle Area(B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma)\leq C(\alpha)e^{\frac{\alpha}{4}r^{2}},

where
B_{r}(0)
denotes the round ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of radius
r
centered at the origin
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

In particular, if
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
, then the Gaussian weighted area is finite, that is,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\sigma<\infty.

Proof.

Take
h=\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Since
\Sigma
is properly immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
h
is proper on
\Sigma
. Besides, since
H=\lambda-\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}
, by (
4.3
) we have that, on
B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma
,

\displaystyle\Delta h=

\displaystyle-H\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle}{2}+\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(H-\lambda)+\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle ar^{2}+(2ab+a|\lambda|)r+(b^{2}+|\lambda|b+\frac{n}{2}).

In the above, we also has used the hypothesis:
|H|(x)\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma
. We also have that on
B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma
,

\displaystyle\Delta h-\alpha|\nabla h|^{2}+\alpha h=

\displaystyle H(H-\lambda)+\frac{n}{2}+\alpha\frac{\langle x,{\bf n}\rangle^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(H-\lambda)+\frac{n}{2}+\alpha(H-\lambda)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)H^{2}-\lambda(1+2\alpha)H+\frac{n}{2}+\alpha\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\alpha)a^{2}r^{2}+(2ab(1+\alpha)+a|\lambda|(1+2\alpha))r

\displaystyle+((1+\alpha)b^{2}+|\lambda|(1+2\alpha)b+\frac{n}{2}+\alpha\lambda^{2}).

Let
a_{2}=(1+\alpha)a^{2}
,
a_{1}=2ab(1+\alpha)+a|\lambda|(1+2\alpha)
,
a_{0}=(1+\alpha)b^{2}+|\lambda|(1+2\alpha)b+\frac{n}{2}+\alpha\lambda^{2}
and
\beta=\alpha
. For
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
, where
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
, it holds that
a_{2}<\frac{\beta}{4}
. By applying Theorem 3.1 in
[
1
]
, we obtain that
\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}d\sigma<\infty
for all
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
and the area of
B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma
satisfies that, for all
r>0,

\displaystyle Area(B_{r}(0)\cap\Sigma)\leq C(\alpha)e^{\frac{\alpha}{4}r^{2}}.

In the particular case of
a<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
, since
a<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
implies that
\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}<1
, we may take
\alpha=1
.
∎

By an argument analogous to the ones used in the proofs of Theorem 4.1 in
[
8
]
and Theorem 4 in
[
2
]
, we may prove the following result: Let
\Sigma

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\Sigma

\lambda

Another consequence of Theorem

is the following corollary, which deals of the integrable property of the powers of the norm of mean curvature

.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume that
|H|\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma,
for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b>0
. Then for
\delta\geq 0
and
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|H|^{\delta}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}d\sigma<\infty.

Proof.

Since
|H|\leq a|x|+b
, it is easily to see that Theorem
7.2
implies the desired conclusion.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume that its mean curvature
H
satisfies
|H|(x)\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma
, for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b>0
. If there exists
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
such that

(7.4)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H+\frac{H^{2}}{2}+\frac{\alpha+1}{4}A(x^{\top},x^{\top})H\leq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
. From (
3.10
) and hypothesis (
7.4
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(-|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)H-\frac{1}{2}H^{2}-\frac{\alpha+1}{4}A(x^{\top},x^{\top})H\right)\varphi^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}H\varphi^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

Further

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H\varphi^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}H)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Sigma}H\varphi\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla H\rangle e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}+2\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}H^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}\leq 2\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}H^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}.

Choose
\varphi=\varphi_{j}
, where
\varphi_{j}
are nonnegative cut-off functions satisfying
\varphi_{j}=1
on
B_{j}(0)
,
\varphi_{j}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{j+1}(0)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{j}|\leq 1
. By the hypothesis
|H|(x)\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma
, for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b>0
, Corollary
7.2
implies that
\int_{\Sigma}H^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}|x|^{2}}<\infty
, for any
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
. By the monotone convergence theorem it follows that

|\nabla H|=0,

which implies that
H
is constant. By Proposition
6.1
, we get that
\Sigma
must be a generalized cylinder.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete properly immersed
\lambda
-self-expander in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Assume that its mean curvature
H
is bounded from below and satisfies
H(x)\leq a|x|+b
,
x\in\Sigma
, for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b>0
. If there exists
\alpha>\frac{4a^{2}}{1-4a^{2}}
such that

(7.5)

\displaystyle|A|^{2}(H-\lambda)+\frac{H}{2}+\frac{\alpha+1}{4}A(x^{\top},x^{\top})\geq 0,

then
\Sigma
must be either a hyperplane or a cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{l}_{r}(0)\times\mathbb{R}^{n-l}
,
1\leq l\leq n
.

Proof.

Let us fix
C=\inf_{x\in\Sigma}H
. From hypothesis (
7.5
) and (
3.10
) it follows that

(7.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}(H-C)\leq 0.

By the maximum principle, either
H\equiv C
or
H>C.

If
H\equiv C
, by Proposition
4.2
,
\Sigma
is either hyperplane or a generalized cylinder.
If
H>C
, let us consider
u:=\log(H-C)
. A computing yields

(7.7)

\displaystyle\Delta u=-|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\Delta H}{H-C}.

Combining (
7.6
) and (
7.7
), we get

(7.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u\leq-|\nabla u|^{2}.

Let us consider the sequence
\varphi_{j}
of nonnegative cut-off function satisfying that
\varphi_{j}=1
on
B_{j}(0)
,
\varphi_{j}=0
on
\Sigma\setminus B_{j+1}(0)
and
|\nabla\varphi_{j}|\leq 1.

Multiplying (
7.8
) by
\varphi^{2}_{j}
and integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{j}|\nabla u|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{j}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u)e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}2\varphi_{j}\langle\nabla\varphi_{j},\nabla u\rangle e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{j}|\nabla u|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}+2\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

Therefore

(7.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}_{j}|\nabla u|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}\leq 4\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi_{j}|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

From assumption on the mean curvature of
\Sigma
, it follows that
|H|(x)\leq a|x|+b_{1}
, for some constants
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b_{1}>0
. Therefore, Theorem
7.2
implies that
\int_{\Sigma}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}<\infty
. Applying the dominated convergence theorem in (
7.9
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla u|^{2}e^{-\alpha\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}=0.

In particular
H
must be a constant, but this contradicts the assumption that
H>\inf_{x\in\Sigma}H
.
∎

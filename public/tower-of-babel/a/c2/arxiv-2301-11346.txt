Trace methods for coHochschild homology
Sarah Klanderman, Maximilien Péroux

Abstract
. Hochschild homology is a classical invariant of rings that plays an important role because of its connection to algebraic K K -theory via the Dennis trace. At level zero, the Dennis trace is induced by the Hattori–Stallings trace. In this paper, we introduce new algebraic K K -theories of coalgebras and obtain coalgebraic refinements of the Hattori–Stallings trace that connect these algebraic K K -theories to coHochschild homology—the invariant analogous to Hochschild homology but for coalgebras. We employ bicategorical methods of Ponto to show that coHochschild homology is a shadow. Consequently, we obtain that coHochschild homology is Morita–Takeuchi invariant.

Introduction

Trace methods are essential for computations in algebraic
K
-theory. The
Dennis trace

K_{*}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{*}(R)
relates
K
-theory to the more computable
Hochschild homology
(HH). Bökstedt refined this by introducing
topological Hochschild homology
(THH), yielding the trace
K(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{THH}(R)
, which has been instrumental in computations, for instance
[
BHM93
,
Dun97
,
NS18
]
.

CoHochschild homology
(coHH) is the invariant of coalgebras analogous to Hochschild homology that was first introduced by Doi
[
Doi81
]
.
Based on previous work by Hess–Parent–Scott in chain complexes
[
HPS09
]
, Hess–Shipley extended Doi’s definition to the setting of stable homotopy theory and introduced
topological coHochschild homology
(coTHH) as the spectral refinement of coHH in
[
HS21
]
.
Given a connected space
X
, there is an equivalence of spectra

\mathrm{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathcal{L}X,

where
\mathcal{L}X
is the free loop space on
X
, see
[
Lod98
, 7.3.11]
or
[
NS18
, IV.3.3]
.
Building on work of Malkiewich
[
Mal17
, 2.22]
, Hess–Shipley show that
further connectivity conditions on
X
give an equivalence of spectra

\mathrm{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathcal{L}X,

see
[
HS21
, 3.7]
.
These equivalences of spectra have led to new computations of the homology of the free loop space
[
BGS22
]
, using a
coBökstedt spectral sequence
for coTHH constructed in
[
BGH+18
]
.

Given the prominent role played by trace methods in computations of algebraic
K
-theory, it is natural to extend trace methods to coTHH.
Combining the equivalences above with the Dennis trace, Hess–Shipley obtained a new trace to coTHH
[
HS21
]
for
X
simply connected:

K(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{THH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X)\simeq\mathrm{coTHH}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X).

Additionally, work of the second author and Bayındır
[
BP23
, 4.4]
shows that applying Spanier–Whitehead duality on the Dennis trace determines a pairing

K(C^{\vee})\wedge\mathrm{coTHH}(C)\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}
,
that can be reformulated as

(1.1)

K(C^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathrm{coTHH}(C)^{\vee},

where
C^{\vee}
is the Spanier–Whithead dual of a coalgebra spectrum
C
with some finiteness conditions.

However, both of the coTHH traces mentioned above are not internal to the coalgebraic setting; they are directly induced by combining the usual Dennis trace with an identification of coTHH with THH.
Moreover, there has been no clear candidate of an algebraic
K
-theory for coalgebras without using a duality on coalgebras into rings.
In this paper, as a first step towards trace methods in the coalgebraic setting, we introduce new algebraic
K
-theories for coalgebras and construct analogues of the Dennis trace on coHochschild homology at level zero. Our results lay the foundational framework for building higher level traces on coHochschild homology as well as spectral refinements to coTHH.

Review of trace methods

If
A
is a connective ring spectrum, the Dennis trace
K(A)\rightarrow\mathrm{THH}(A)
at level zero is a homomorphism
K_{0}(\pi_{0}(A))\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(\pi_{0}(A))
, induced by
K_{0}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R)
for a (discrete) ring
R
.
In order to obtain trace maps in the coalgebraic setting, we must first determine what happens at level zero for coHochschild homology.
In the algebraic setting, the homomorphism
K_{0}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R)
arises from the usual trace of matrices, which is additive and cyclic.
These properties generalize to endomorphisms on dualizable objects in symmetric monoidal categories
[
DP80
,
PS14
]
. In non-symmetric contexts, Hattori and Stallings define the
Hattori–Stallings trace
, a homomorphism
\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)\rightarrow R/[R,R]
for a finitely generated projective
R
-module
M

[
Hat65
,
Sta65
]
. The group
R/[R,R]=\operatorname{HH}_{0}(R)
serves as the universal target for trace maps
\mathcal{M}_{n}(R)\rightarrow A
. Evaluating this trace on identity endomorphisms induces
K_{0}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R)
.

Ponto generalized this approach by introducing the notion of traces in
shadowed bicategories

[
Pon10
]
.
The key observation is that given (not-necessarily commutative) rings
R
and
S
, an
(R,S)
-bimodule
M
and an
(S,R)
-bimodule
N
, the relative tensor products
M\otimes_{S}N
and
N\otimes_{R}M
are
not
isomorphic.
In fact, they do not even have the same algebraic structure, as the former is an
(R,R)
-bimodule while the latter is an
(S,S)
-bimodule.
However, the two bimodules become equivalent after applying (relative) Hochschild homology:

\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R,M\otimes_{S}N)\cong\mathrm{HH}_{0}(S,N\otimes_{R}M).

Considering instead the derived tensor product shows that higher levels of Hochschild homology
\mathrm{HH}_{*}
are also shadows, and replacing rings by ring spectra shows that THH is a shadow. In fact, THH is the universal cyclic invariant, and the universal shadow in a sense made precise in
[
HR21a
]
.
Every shadowed bicategory comes equipped with a notion of a bicategorical trace that is cyclic, and the bicategorical trace on dualizable bimodules recovers the Hattori–Stallings trace in the underived case.

Results

We dualize the construction of trace maps in the coalgebraic setting, using the framework of bicategorical shadows. In doing so, we are confronted with several obstacles.
For instance, given coalgebras
C
and
D
, the
cotensor product

M\square_{D}N
of a
(C,D)
-bicomodule
M
with a
(D,C)
-bicomodule
N
need not to be a
(C,C)
-bicomodule.
Even worse, the cotensor product is not even associative in general when considering coalgebras over
\mathbb{Z}

[
GP87
, IV.2.5]
.
To remedy this, we assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is a ring of global dimension zero, as this guarantees we have a nice associative relative cotensor product on bicomodules.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero.
The zeroth coHochschild homology
\mathrm{coHH}_{0}
defines a shadow on the bicategory of
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebras and bicomodules with their relative cotensor products.

In particular, given
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebras
C
and
D
, a
(C,D)
-bicomodule
M
and a
(D,C)
-bicomodule
N
, there is a natural isomorphism of
\mathbbm{k}
-modules

\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(M\square_{D}N,C)\cong\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(N\square_{C}M,D).

Given a right dualizable
(C,D)
-bicomodule
M
and an endomorphism
f\colon M\rightarrow M
of
(C,D)
-bicomodules, the fact that
\mathrm{coHH}_{0}
is a shadow induces a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear homomorphism

(1.3)

\mathrm{tr}_{C}^{D}(f)\colon\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)\longrightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(D),

that is cyclic (Proposition
6.2
). Considering either
C=\mathbbm{k}
or
D=\mathbbm{k}
leads to two particularly interesting traces.

Case 1:
D=\mathbbm{k}
in (
1.3
)

In this case, if
M
is a finitely cogenerated and injective left
C
-comodule (see Definition
2.8
), then it is right dualizable as a
(C,\mathbbm{k})
-bicomodule.
Given a
C
-colinear endomorphism
f
of
M
, the assignment
f\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{C}^{\mathbbm{k}}(f)
determines a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear homomorphism that we call the
Hattori–Stallings cotrace
:

{}_{C}\mathrm{End}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C),\mathbbm{k}).

We shall write
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C),\mathbbm{k})
as
\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}
.
The class of finitely cogenerated and injective left
C
-comodules is the dual analogue of the class of finitely generated and projective right
R
-modules considered when computing the algebraic
K
-theory
K(R)
of a ring
R
.
Therefore, this analogy leads us to introduce the following construction.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero and let
C
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebra.
Let
{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}^{\mathrm{fc,inj}}
denote the category of finitely cogenerated and injective left
C
-comodules, viewed as a Waldhausen category whose cofibrations are monomorphisms and whose weak equivalences are isomorphisms.
Let
K^{c}(C)
denote the resulting algebraic
K
-theory spectrum associated to the Waldhausen category
{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}^{\mathrm{fc,inj}}
, as in
[
Wal85
]
or
[
Wei13
, IV.8.5]
.

Just as the Hattori–Stallings trace
\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R)
led to a homomorphism
K_{0}(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R)
of abelian groups by considering the trace on the identity, the Hattori–Stallings cotrace
{}_{C}\mathrm{End}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}
on the identity (i.e., the
corank
) induces the following map.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
2.16
)
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero, and let
C
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebra.
The Hattori–Stallings corank induces a homomorphism of abelian groups

K_{0}^{c}(C)\longrightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}.

The cotrace
K_{0}^{c}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}
is analogous
to the Dennis trace
K_{0}(C^{*})\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}
obtained when
C
is finitely generated as
\mathbbm{k}
-modules, a particular case of (
1.1
) from
[
BP23
]
.
We view Theorem
1.5
as a first step in constructing the Dennis trace entirely in the coalgebraic setting, with no finiteness assumption on
C
.

Case 2:
C=\mathbbm{k}
in (
1.3
)

In this case, relabeling
D
as
C
, if
M
is a right
C
-comodule that is finitely generated as a
\mathbbm{k}
-module, then it is right dualizable as a
(\mathbbm{k},C)
-bicomodule.
Given a
C
-colinear endomorphism
f
on
M
,
the assignment
f\mapsto\mathrm{tr}^{C}_{\mathbbm{k}}(f)(1)

determines a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear homomorphism that we call the
colinear Hattori–Stallings trace
:

\mathrm{End}_{C}(M)\longrightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C).

The class of right
C
-comodules that are finitely generated as
\mathbbm{k}
-modules is similar to classes that have been considered in algebraic
K
-theory before in the literature
[
HS16
,
Pér24
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero, and let
C
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebra. Let
\mathrm{CoMod}_{C}^{\mathrm{fg}}
denote the category of right
C
-comodules that are finitely generated (and automatically projective) as
\mathbbm{k}
-modules.
It is a Waldhausen category whose cofibrations are monomorphisms and whose weak equivalences are isomorphisms.
Let

G^{c}(C)
denote the resulting algebraic
K
-theory spectrum
K(\mathrm{CoMod}_{C}^{\mathrm{fg}})
.

We show in Proposition
2.28
that this algebraic
K
-theory is similar to the
G
-theory of rings.
A particular example of interest is the group ring
\mathbbm{k}\Gamma
for
\Gamma
a finite group, as
G(\mathbbm{k}\Gamma)
is the
K
-theory of representations of
\Gamma
in the category of
\mathbbm{k}
-modules, also known as Swan theory
[
Swa70
]
.
The colinear Hattori–Stallings trace
\mathrm{End}_{C}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)
on the identity defines a colinear rank.
In
[
GPS25
]
, together with Gerhardt and Soré, the second author show that the colinear rank is dual to the character of finite dimensional representations.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
2.27
)
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero, and let
C
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebra.
The colinear Hattori–Stallings rank induces a homomorphism of abelian groups

G^{c}_{0}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C).

In this paper, we also extend our shadow framework of Theorem
1.2
to the derived setting.
Our approach dualizes the arguments which show Hochschild homology is a shadow
[
Pon10
]
, using an adaptation of the so-called Dennis–Waldhausen Morita argument.
However, we are again faced with the following challenges.

First, obtaining model structures on categories of comodules and coalgebras is a difficult problem
[
BHK+15
,
HKRS17
,
GKR20
]
. Even in the case when model structures do exist, they may not be well-behaved
[
PS19
,
Pér22b
]
.
Nevertheless, the second author showed that there is a model structure on connective comodules over simply connected differential graded
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebras up to quasi-isomorphism representing homotopy coherent connective comodules in
H\mathbbm{k}
-spectra
[
Pér25
]
.

Second, constructing a derived cotensor product is challenging but necessary for a shadow structure on derived bicomodules. While the two-sided bar construction models the derived tensor product, a two-sided cobar construction should model the derived cotensor product. However, topological and algebraic cobar constructions each have issues: the former lacks comodule preservation, while the latter fails to be invariant under quasi-isomorphisms, see Remark
3.11
. Restricting to simply connected dg-coalgebras, as in
[
Pér24
,
Pér25
]
, offers a workaround, though subtleties remain, such as totalization not commuting with tensoring, which we resolve here.

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative ring with global dimension zero.
CoHochschild homology
\mathrm{coHH}
defines a shadow on the bicategory of simply connected differential graded
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebras and bicomodules with their derived relative cotensor product.

One of the defining properties of
K
-theory and Hochschild homology is invariance under Morita equivalences — rings that have equivalent (possibly derived) categories of left modules.
The dual notion for coalgebras and comodules is called
Morita–Takeuchi equivalence
.
Despite the pathological nature of model categories of comodules discussed above, we are able to show that coHochschild homology is invariant under Morita–Takeuchi equivalence in the derived context using our shadow framework.

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem
7.4
)
.

Let
C
and
D
be homotopically Morita–Takeuchi equivalent simply connected differential graded
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebras.
Then there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{coHH}(C)\cong\mathrm{coHH}(D).

Vista

The present paper is the first step of a program to extend the Dennis trace
K(R)\rightarrow\mathrm{THH}(R)
to the coalgebraic setting.
Our results propose natural candidates for the algebraic
K
-theory of a coalgebra.
The Hess–Shipley identification
in
[
HS16
]
suggests that these new
K
-theories for coalgebras “deloop” the usual algebraic
K
-theories of rings.
In fact, given any ring spectrum
R
, its bar construction
BR
is a coalgebra and modules over
R
are equivalent to certain comodules over
BR
under a Koszul duality
[
Heu24
]
.
Therefore, obtaining computational tools to determine the algebraic
K
-theories of a coalgebra can shed new light on the usual
K
-theory of rings. Additionally, in forthcoming work with Brazelton, Calle, Chan, and Keenan, the second author intends to extend current methods in this paper to the bicategory of bicomodules over the coalgebra spectrum
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X
.

In
[
GPS25
]
, Gerhardt, Soré, and the second author show how the algebraic
K
-theories
K^{c}(C)
and
G^{c}(C)
relate further to the usual algebraic
K
-theory and
G
-theory of a ring. They also show the compatibility of the corank and colinear rank with the usual Hattori–Stallings trace and the character of finite dimensional representations.
In forthcoming work with Agarwal and Mehrle, the second author intends to extend the trace of Theorems
1.5
to higher level

K_{n}^{c}(C)\longrightarrow(\mathrm{coHH}_{n}(C))^{*},
for all
n\geq 0
.

Organization

We finish this section with notations and definitions that are used throughout this paper.
In section
2
, we construct the Hattori–Stallings cotrace and the colinear Hattori–Stallings trace using only algebraic methods and motivation. This section does not require any knowledge of bicategories, shadows, or homotopy theory.
In order to be a self-contained paper, we will recall all necessary bicategorical definitions in later sections.
We introduce the different bicategories of bicomodules
in Section
3
. Our key result, that coHochschild homology defines a bicategorical shadow, is proved in Section
4
. We then explore the notion of duality in bicomodules in Section
5
, which has been under-documented in the literature thus far.
In Section
6
, we provide a bicategorical description of the traces that appear in Section
2
.
In Section
7
, we show how Morita–Takeuchi equivalences are part of the bicategorical structures. In Appendix
A
, we carefully detail how to represent the derived cotensor product of bicomodules and explore what fibrant bicomodules are.

Notation

We begin by establishing notation that we use throughout this paper.

(1)

The letter
\mathbbm{k}
shall always denote a commutative ring with global dimension zero, i.e., a finite product of fields, or said differently, a commutative semisimple ring. Every
\mathbbm{k}
-module is projective and injective.

(2)

Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
denote the category of
\mathbbm{k}
-modules. We write the relative tensor product

as

.

(3)

Given

2.
Coalgebraic variations of the Hattori–Stallings trace

We introduce the Hattori–Stallings cotrace (Definition

We then obtain trace maps on

We first begin by briefly recalling how the Hattori–Stallings trace is built so that the reader sees the analogies between the algebraic setting and coalgebraic setting.

2.1.
The Hattori–Stallings trace

We recall

]
.
Let

Definition 2.1
.

A

Each map

[

, 1.4,1.5]

, where

.
Therefore we can rephrase a

on

to be a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear homomorphism

such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m\circ\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

If we let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R/{[R,R]}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tr}}

Given

More generally, if

Definition 2.2

Let

The definition above depends on the choice of generators of

. However, the Hattori–Stallings trace can be defined coordinate-free as follows.
Given a finitely generated projective right

and thus the Hattori–Stallings trace is the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes_{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ev}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tr}}

Let

be the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective right

2.2.

We wish to dualize the approach of Hattori and Stallings in the coalgebraic context. Just as the group

is the universal home for traces, we introduce a group

as the universal home for so-called cotraces.
Throughout the rest of the section, let

be a

-coalgebra, not necessarily cocommutative.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

-linear homomorphism

Example 3.2

-bimodules for rings

Example 3.3

When the 0-cells are given by ring spectra and the 1- and 2-cells come from the homotopy category of

-bimodules for ring spectra

Remark 3.11

By the Dold-Kan correspondence, since

from Definition

is quasi-isomorphic to

We first establish the following notation conventions.

•

Given

\mathbbm{k}
-module, we define

Elements in the summands are denoted

.

•

Let

denote the desuspension functor on graded
\mathbbm{k}
-modules where, for

(s^{-1}V)_{i}=V_{i+1}

Given a homogeneous element

for the corresponding element in

.

•

We denote the kernel of the counit

by

, often referred to as the coideal of

.

The conormalized cobar resolution of
C
is the chain complex

\underline{\Omega}C:=(T(s^{-1}\underline{C}),d_{\Omega})

where, if

denotes the differential on

, then

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}(s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pm s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}(dc_{j})|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{(c_{j})}\pm s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}{c_{j}}_{(1)}|s^{-1}{c_{j}}_{(2)}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n},

where

denotes the (reduced) comultiplication of

on the element

using the Sweedler notation. To determine the signs above, one needs to apply the Koszul rule.
More generally, we define the conormalized cobar resolution of

and

over

to be the chain complex

\underline{\Omega}(M,C,N):=(M\otimes T(s^{-1}\underline{C})\otimes N,\delta),

where up to Koszul sign, the differential

\displaystyle\delta(m\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dm\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes n

\displaystyle\pm\sum_{(m)}m_{(0)}\otimes s^{-1}m_{(1)}|s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes n

\displaystyle\pm m\otimes d_{\Omega}\Big(s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\Big)\otimes n

\displaystyle\pm m\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}\otimes dn

\displaystyle\pm\sum_{(n)}m\otimes s^{-1}c_{1}|\cdots|s^{-1}c_{n}|s^{-1}n_{(1)}\otimes n_{(0)},

where

By the Dold-Kan correspondence, there is an isomorphism of categories between cosimplicial objects and non-positive cochain complexes of bicomodules given by the conormalization functor

N^{*}\colon\left({}_{D}\mathrm{CoMod}_{E}({\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}})\right)^{\Delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{coCh}^{\leq 0}\left({}_{D}\mathrm{CoMod}_{E}({\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}})\right).

Therefore, we denote

by

.
A word of warning however: a double complex in general has two possible totalizations, one given using coproducts and one using products (see
[

The product-total complex of

is quasi-isomorphic to the homotopy limit of

(see
[

On the other hand, the coproduct-total complex of

is
\underline{\Omega}(M,C,N)

For instance, when

is simply-connected, then
\underline{\Omega}^{-q}(M,C,N)_{p}=0
for

, and is therefore bounded. Thus when
C
is simply-connected, we obtain a quasi-isomorphism

\Omega(M,C,N)\simeq\underline{\Omega}(M,C,N).

But in general, if

is not simply-connected, no such claim can be made.

Definition 4.1

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle\langle}(M\odot N)\odot P{\rangle\rangle}_{C}}

Example 4.2
.

We can now consider shadows for the bicategories that we introduced in Examples
3.2
and
3.3
. For a ring

, the
0^{th}
Hochschild homology,
\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R,-)
, is a shadow on the bicategory with 1- and 2-cells from the category of
(R,R)
-bimodules to the category of abelian groups
\mathrm{Ab}
:

\displaystyle{\langle\langle}-{\rangle\rangle}_{R}\colon{}_{R}\mathrm{Mod}_{R}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Ab}

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto R\otimes_{R\otimes R^{\mathsf{op}}}M\cong\mathrm{HH}_{0}(R,M).

More generally, a Dennis-Waldhausen Morita argument shows that Hochschild homology,

, is a shadow in the derived setting
[
Wal79
]
. Further,
[
BM12
]
shows that topological Hochschild homology,

\displaystyle{\langle\langle}-{\rangle\rangle}_{R}\colon\textup{Ho}({}_{R}\mathrm{Mod}_{R})

\displaystyle\to\textup{Ho}(\textup{Sp})

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{THH}(R,M).

Since (topological) Hochschild homology provides a bicategorical shadow for the setting of modules, we want to consider the analogue of this construction for the context of comodules.
Work of Doi defines coHochschild homology, denoted
\mathrm{coHH}
, as an invariant of coalgebras analogous to Hochschild homology.

Definition 4.3

(
[
Doi81
]
)
.

For a commutative ring
R
, a coassociative, counital
R
-coalgebra
C
, and a
(C,C)
-bicomodule
M
, build the cochain complex
\mathcal{H}(M,C)
:

\cdots\xlongleftarrow{}M\otimes_{R}C\otimes_{R}C\xlongleftarrow{}M\otimes_{R}C\xlongleftarrow{}M\xlongleftarrow{}0,

as follows. Let
\mathcal{H}^{r}(M,C)=M\otimes_{R}C^{\otimes r}
for

with coboundary map
\delta^{r}\colon\mathcal{H}^{r}(M,C)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{r+1}(M,C)
defined by

\delta^{r}=\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}(-1)^{i}d_{i},

for

d_{i}
given by

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes r}&i=0\\
\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes(r-i)}&1\leq i\leq r\\
\tilde{t}\circ(\lambda\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes r})&i=r+1,\end{cases}

where
\rho\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}C
denotes the right coaction,
\lambda\colon M\rightarrow C\otimes_{R}M
denotes the left coaction, and
\tilde{t}
is the map that twists the first factor to the last. Then the
q^{th}
-
coHochschild homology of

with coefficients in

is given by the cohomology of the cochain complex

\mathrm{coHH}_{q}(M,C):=H^{q}(\mathcal{H}(M,C)).

Remark 4.4
.

We can see that
\mathcal{H}(M,C)=\underline{\Omega}(M,C^{e},C)
as in Remark

, where

, and in particular we get that

In particular

.

Theorem 4.5
.

The

coHochschild homology

\displaystyle\to\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(M,C)

Let

and

Given

(C,D)
-bicomodule and

Using Sweedler notation, we denote the coactions on

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C\otimes M,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M\otimes D

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{(m^{C})}m^{C}_{(1)}\otimes m^{C}_{(0)}

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{(m^{D})}m^{D}_{(0)}\otimes m^{D}_{(1)},

and the coactions on

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D\otimes N,

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\longrightarrow N\otimes C

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{(n^{D})}n^{D}_{(1)}\otimes n^{D}_{(0)}

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{(n^{C})}n^{C}_{(0)}\otimes n^{C}_{(1)}.

Recall that

is defined as the kernel of

The desired isomorphism will be induced by

\displaystyle\tau\colon M\otimes N

\displaystyle\longrightarrow N\otimes M

\displaystyle\longmapsto n\otimes m.

We need to verify that if we restrict

. In other words, we need to verify that if

.
Notice that because

\displaystyle\sum_{(m^{D})}m^{D}_{(0)}\otimes m^{D}_{(1)}\otimes n=\sum_{(n^{D})}m\otimes n^{D}_{(1)}\otimes n^{D}_{(0)}

.

To see that

) above. Therefore we have obtained the desired homomorphism,
\theta
.

is an isomorphism.

Definition 4.6
.

Let

be a symmetric monoidal category with a model structure, and let

be a coalgebra with coassociative comultiplication

and counit

. Further, let

(C,C)

to be the cosimplicial object with

\mathrm{coHH}^{\mathsf{M}}(M,C)^{r}=M\otimes C^{\otimes r}

with coface maps

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes r}&i=0\\
\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes(r-i)}&1\leq i\leq r\\
\tilde{t}\circ(\lambda\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes r})&i=r+1,\end{cases}

where
\tilde{t}
is the map that twists the first factor to the last, and with codegeneracy maps
s_{i}:M\otimes C^{\otimes(r+1)}\to M\otimes C^{\otimes r}
, for

s_{i}=\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes i}\otimes\varepsilon\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C}^{\otimes r-i}.

This gives a cosimplicial object of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M\otimes C\otimes C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M\otimes C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}

The
coHochschild homology in
\mathsf{M}
of the coalgebra
C

M

\mathrm{coHH}^{\mathsf{M}}(M,C)=\mathsf{holim}_{\Delta}\big(\mathrm{coHH}^{\mathsf{M}}(M,C)^{\bullet}\big).

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle\langle}r{\rangle\rangle}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle\langle}r{\rangle\rangle}}

We will prove the left triangle is commutative, but the argument for the right triangle will follow analogously. We apply the definition of
\theta
,
\ell
, and
r
on the bicosimplicial objects:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(P\otimes C^{\otimes\bullet}\otimes C)\otimes C^{\otimes\ast}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(C\otimes C^{\otimes\ast}\otimes P)\otimes C^{\otimes\bullet}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\otimes C^{\otimes\ast}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\otimes C^{\otimes\bullet}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle\langle}r{\rangle\rangle}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\langle\langle}\ell{\rangle\rangle}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Here we kept track of the different gradings by
\bullet
and
\ast
. Therefore, once we apply the homotopy limit, we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.
Dualizable bicomodules

As traces are always defined on dualizable objects, we investigate the notion of duality in our bicategories of bicomodules.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a bicategory. A
1
-cell
M\in\mathcal{B}(C,D)
is
right dualizable
if there is another
1
-cell
M^{\star}
in
\mathcal{B}(D,C)
, with
2
-cells
\eta\colon U_{C}\rightarrow M\odot M^{\star}
and
\varepsilon\colon M^{\star}\odot M\rightarrow U_{D}
, called
coevaluation
and
evaluation
respectively, such that the compositions in
\mathcal{B}(C,D)
and
\mathcal{B}(D,C)
respectively are the identity
2
-cells:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\cong{U_{C}\odot M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(M\odot M^{\star})\odot M\cong M\odot(M^{\star}\odot M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M\odot U_{D}}\cong M,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta\odot\mathsf{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\odot\varepsilon}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\star}\cong{M^{\star}\odot U_{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M^{\star}\odot(M\odot M^{\star})\cong(M^{\star}\odot M)\odot M^{\star}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{D}\odot M^{\star}}\cong M^{\star}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\odot\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon\odot\mathsf{id}}

We call
M^{\star}
the
right dual
of
M
and say that
(M,M^{\star})
form a
dual pair
in
\mathcal{B}
.

For instance, in the bicategory of bimodules, an
(R,S)
-bimodule
M
is right dualizable if and only if it is finitely generated and projective as a right
S
-module, and its dual is given by its linear dual
\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(M,S)
, see
[
PS13
, 6.1]
. We shall obtain a very similar result for bicomodules with the subtlety that our bicategory is not “closed" (see
[
Pon10
, 4.1.4]
), and thus we cannot recognize dualizable objects (as in
[
Pon10
, 4.3.3]
) since we are not provided with an internal hom. Our bicategories will almost be “co-closed" thanks to the introduction of a cohom functor.
We shall make use of the notion of “quasi-finite" comodules, as introduced by Takeuchi in
[
Tak77
]
. More modern reviews, in more general settings, can be found in
[
BW03
]
and
[
Al-02
]
.

Let
{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}
be the category of left
C
-comodules. If
M
is a left
C
-comodule and
V
is a
\mathbbm{k}
-module, then
M\otimes V
is a left
C
-comodule.
This defines a functor:

\displaystyle M\otimes-\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\longmapsto M\otimes V.

Definition 5.2
.

We say a left
C
-comodule
M
is
quasi-finite
if the functor
M\otimes-\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}\rightarrow{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}
is a right adjoint.
In this case, we denote its left adjoint by
h_{C}(M,-)\colon{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, and refer to it as the
cohom
functor.
In other words, given any left
C
-comodule
N
, the cohom
h_{C}(M,N)
is the universal
\mathbbm{k}
-module providing a natural
\mathbbm{k}
-linear isomorphism

(5.3)

\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}\Big(h_{C}(M,N),V\Big)\cong{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}\Big(N,M\otimes V\Big),

for any
\mathbbm{k}
-module
V
. In particular, for
V=\mathbbm{k}
, we obtain the isomorphism

h_{C}(M,N)^{*}\cong{}_{C}\mathrm{Hom}(N,M).

Example 5.4
.

An example of a quasi-finite left
C
-comodule
M
is given by cofree comodules
M=C\otimes V
, where
V
is a finitely generated
\mathbbm{k}
-module.
Since we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}\Big(h_{C}(C\otimes V,N),W\Big)

\displaystyle\cong{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}\Big(N,C\otimes V\otimes W\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}\Big(N,V\otimes W\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbbm{k}}\Big(V^{*}\otimes N,W\Big),

for any
\mathbbm{k}
-module
W
, we obtain
h_{C}(C\otimes V,N)\cong V^{*}\otimes N
.
Choosing
V=\mathbbm{k}^{\oplus n}
so that

, then

.
More generally, any finitely cogenerated comodule (Definition
2.8
) is quasi-finite.

The unit of the adjunction (
5.3
) provides the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear coevaluation

\displaystyle\eta\colon N\longrightarrow M\otimes h_{C}(M,N).

If
D
is another coalgebra, and

is a
(C,D)
-bicomodule, then

is a right
D
-comodule, and the map

is a morphism of

-bicomodules, see
[

, 1.7, 1.19]
. Therefore, for any quasi-finite left
C
-comodule

, we obtain that
h_{C}(M,C)
is a right
C
-comodule, and we have a
(C,C)
-bicomodule homomorphism
\eta\colon C\rightarrow M\otimes h_{C}(M,C)
.

Let
T
be a left
C
-comodule,
M
a quasi-finite left
C
-comodule, and
N
a
(C,C)
-bicomodule.
Denote by
\partial\colon h_{C}(M,N\square_{C}T)\rightarrow h_{C}(M,N)\square_{C}T
the adjoint to the left

-colinear homomorphism

\eta\square 1\colon N\square_{C}T\longrightarrow\Big(M\otimes h_{C}(M,N)\Big)\square_{C}T\cong M\otimes\Big(h_{C}(M,N)\square_{C}T\Big).

It is noted that, in
[
Tak77
, 1.14]
, the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear homomorphism

above is an isomorphism whenever

is an injective quasi-finite left
C
-comodule.
If we choose

and

,
combining the isomorphism with the map

of Definition

, we obtain the evaluation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon\colon h_{C}(M,C)\square_{C}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{C}(M,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

Example 5.5
.

Consider the bicategory
\mathrm{CoMod}
of Definition
3.5
.
A comodule
M
in
{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}_{\mathbbm{k}}
is right dualizable if it is quasi-finite and injective as a left
C
-comodule.
The dual of

is the right
C
-comodule

, together with the coevaluation

and evaluation

defined above. The desired triangle identities follow from the adjunction of the cohom functor (
5.3
).

Example 5.6
.

In Example
5.5
, choose
M=C\otimes V
, where
V
is a dualizable
\mathbbm{k}
-module (i.e. finitely generated).
We obtain
M^{\star}=h_{C}(C\otimes V,C)\cong V^{*}\otimes C
.
We can then explicitly describe the coevaluation and evaluation.
Let us denote the comultiplication and counit of

by
\Delta_{C}\colon C\rightarrow C\otimes C
and
\varepsilon_{C}\colon C\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
respectively.
Similarly, denote the coevaluation and evaluation of the dualizable
\mathbbm{k}
-module

by
\eta_{V}\colon\mathbbm{k}\rightarrow V\otimes V^{*}
and
\varepsilon_{V}\colon V^{*}\otimes V\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}
respectively.
Then the coevaluation
\eta\colon C\rightarrow M\otimes M^{\star}\cong(C\otimes V)\otimes(V^{*}\otimes C)
is the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes C\cong C\otimes\mathbbm{k}\otimes C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes(V\otimes V^{*})\otimes C.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\otimes\eta_{V}\otimes\mathsf{id}}

Explicitly, if we pick
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
for a basis of
V
, and
(e^{*}_{1},\ldots,e_{n}^{*})
for the dual basis of
V^{*}
, and write
\Delta_{C}(c)=\sum_{(c)}c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
, we obtain the formula

\eta(c)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{(c)}c_{(1)}\otimes e_{i}\otimes e^{*}_{i}\otimes c_{(2)}.

The evaluation

is then the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{*}\otimes C\otimes V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\otimes V\cong V^{*}\otimes V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\otimes\varepsilon_{C}\otimes\mathsf{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon_{V}}

Explicitly, we obtain the formula for

:

\varepsilon(f\otimes c\otimes v)=\varepsilon_{C}(c)f(v).

Choosing in particular

so that

and

and

, we get

and the coevaluation can be formulated as the

-bicolinear morphism:

\displaystyle\eta\colon C

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{n}^{c}(C)\otimes C

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{(c)}c_{(1)}\otimes I_{n}\otimes c_{(2)}.

Notice that
M^{\star}\square_{C}M\cong\mathcal{M}_{n}^{c}(C)
as
\mathbbm{k}
-modules, and thus the evaluation can be formulated as a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear homomorphism:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{M}_{n}^{c}(C)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}

\displaystyle c\otimes f

\displaystyle\longmapsto\varepsilon_{C}(c)\mathrm{tr}(f).

Example 5.7
.

We now generalize Example

.
A comodule

in
{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}_{D}
is right dualizable whenever it is quasi-finite and injective as a left

Define

-bicomodule. Indeed, by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes h_{C}(M,N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes D\otimes h_{C}(M,N).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}}

Moreover, by

for any left

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

Example 5.9
.

A particular case of Example

is when

. A bicomodule
M
in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathrm{CoMod}_{D}
is right dualizable when
M
is dualizable as a
\mathbbm{k}
-module (i.e., finitely generated), and its right dual is

, the usual linear dual. This appeared in the proof of Proposition

.
Explicitly, its left

-coaction is given by:

\displaystyle\lambda\colon M^{*}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(M,D)\cong D\otimes M^{*}

\displaystyle\left(M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbbm{k}\right)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\rightarrow}}M\otimes D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha\otimes\mathsf{id}}}{{\rightarrow}}D\right),

where
\rho\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes D
is the right
D
-coaction of
M
.
By
[
BW03
, 10.11]
, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{k}
-modules

.
Therefore, we can interpret the coevaluation
\eta\colon\mathbbm{k}\rightarrow M\square_{D}M^{*}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbbm{k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{D}(M,M)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathsf{id}_{M}.

Notice that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{*}\otimes M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(M,D)\otimes M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{*}\otimes M\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda\otimes\mathsf{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}\otimes\rho}

where the unlabeled maps are defined by evaluating. This defines the

-bicolinear evaluation homomorphism

\varepsilon(\alpha\otimes m)=\sum_{(m)}\alpha(m_{(0)})\otimes m_{(1)}

where we denoted

.

We can further generalize the examples above to the differential graded case. Arguments for quasi-finite comodules are entirely categorical (see
[
Al-02
]
). However, the notion has not been documented before in this context, and therefore we introduce them here.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
C
be a simply connected dg-coalgebra over
\mathbbm{k}
.
A left dg-
C
-comodule
M
is

if the functor

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\longmapsto M\otimes V

admits a left adjoint, denoted
h_{C}(M,-)\colon{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
, called the

.
In other words, we obtain an equivalence

{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}\Big(h_{C}(M,N),V\Big)\cong{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}\Big(N,M\otimes V\Big),

for any left dg-
C
-comodule

and
\mathbbm{k}
-chain complex

.

Example 5.11
.

If
V
is a perfect
\mathbbm{k}
-chain complex, then just as in Example
5.4
, we have that

is quasi-finite, and
h_{C}(C\otimes V,N)\cong V^{*}\otimes N
. If

, then a left dg
C
-comodule is just a chain complex, and quasi-finite comodules are precisely the perfect chain complexes.

If
M
is also endowed with a right
D
-coaction, then

is a right
D
-comodule and we obtain the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{D}\mathrm{CoMod}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{C}(M,-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

Therefore, just as in the discrete case, the right dual of a
(C,D)
-bicomodule

should be the
(D,C)
-bicomodule

.
Notice, even if

is a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule, there is no reason to expect

is fibrant as a
(D,C)
-bicomodule. However, this is not needed. Indeed, by Proposition

, if
M
is a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule, then

\displaystyle M\square_{D}h_{C}(M,C)

\displaystyle\simeq M\square_{D}\Omega(D,D,h_{C}(M,C))

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega(M,D,h_{C}(M,C))

\displaystyle\simeq M\widehat{\square}_{D}h_{C}(M,C).

Similarly, we obtain
h_{C}(M,C)\square_{C}M\simeq h_{C}(M,C)\widehat{\square}_{C}M
.

Just as in the discrete case, the
C
-bicolinear map
\eta\colon C\rightarrow M\square_{D}h_{C}(M,C)
from the unit of the cohom adjunction induces the desired coevaluation on the dual pair
(M,h_{C}(M,C))
.

We now describe how to obtain the evaluation.
Recall that a
\mathbbm{k}
-chain complex
V
is perfect if and only if the natural map
V\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,\mathbbm{k})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,V)
is an isomorphism. Similarly, as in Example

, for any quasi-finite left

-comodule

, we have a natural

-bicolinear map
\partial\colon h_{C}(M,M)\rightarrow h_{C}(M,C)\square_{C}M
. We saw in the discrete case that if

is injective and quasi-finite, then

is an isomorphism.

Definition 5.12
.

A fibrant left

-comodule

is said to be

if it is quasi-finite and the induced map
\partial\colon h_{C}(M,M)\rightarrow h_{C}(M,C)\square_{C}M

Given a coperfect
(C,D)
-bicomodule

, define the evaluation as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon\colon h_{C}(M,C)\square_{C}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{C}(M,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Combining our arguments above, we obtain the desired triangle identities by adjunction of the cohom functor, and thus the following result.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
C
and
D
be simply connected dg-coalgebras.
A (fibrant)
(C,D)
-bicomodule
M
is right dualizable if it is coperfect as a left

-comodule. Its right dual is given by

.

Example 5.14
.

Suppose

, and

, where

is quasi-finite as a left

.
Moreover,

is coperfect because

V^{*}\otimes M\cong h_{C}(C\otimes V,M)\rightarrow h_{C}(C\otimes V,C)\square_{C}M\cong V^{*}\otimes M

.

If

, then if

6.
Bicategorical traces

Every shadowed bicategory defines a notion of traces on its dualizable objects. This extends the notion of traces on symmetric monoidal categories, as reviewed in
[
PS14
]
.
We show the Hattori–Stallings cotrace
{}_{C}\mathrm{End}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)^{*}
(Definition
2.12
) and the colinear Hattori–Stallings trace
\mathrm{End}_{C}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(C)
(Definition
2.23
) are bicategorical traces on the shadowed bicategory
(\mathrm{CoMod},\mathrm{coHH}_{0})
.
We first recall the general definition of a bicategorical trace.

Definition 6.1

(
[
PS13
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},{\langle\langle}-{\rangle\rangle})
be a shadowed bicategory. Let
M\in\mathcal{B}(C,D)
be a right dualizable
1
-cell.
The
trace
of a
2
-cell
f:M\rightarrow M
, denoted
\mathrm{tr}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\langle\langle}U_{C}{\rangle\rangle}}_{C}}

Remark 6.6
.

In recent independent work of Justin Barhite
[
Bar24
]
, Hochschild cohomology
\mathrm{HH}^{0}
is shown to be a so-called
coshadow
on the bicategory of bimodules over
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras, and there are associated
bicategorical cotraces
.
If
C
is a
\mathbbm{k}
-coalgebra that is finitely generated as
\mathbbm{k}
-module, then the bicategory of bimodules over
C^{*}
is intimately related to the bicategory of bicomodules over
C
since

by
[
BW03
, 10.10]
.
This induces an equivalence
\mathrm{coHH}_{0}(N\otimes M,C)\cong\mathrm{HH}^{0}(C^{*},M\otimes N)
.
From our perspective however, our cotrace comes from a shadow and not a coshadow, and we do not assume that our bicategory is closed.
One can build a functor
(\mathrm{CoMod}^{\mathrm{fg}})^{\mathrm{co-op}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}
from the co-opposite bicategory (inverting both 1 and 2 cells) of finitely generated bicomodules to the bicategory of bimodules, and we expect that it sends the shadow
\mathrm{coHH}_{0}
to the coshadow
\mathrm{HH}^{0}
.
Nonetheless, it does not seem that the language of coshadows and bicategorical cotraces is the appropriate vocabulary for the Hattori–Stallings cotrace, nor the colinear Hattori–Stallings trace.

Example 6.7
.

We can generalize the Hattori–Stallings cotrace for chain complexes.

7.
Morita–Takeuchi invariance

In bicategories, Morita equivalence is the natural notion of an equivalence in a bicategory. It extends the usual notion of equivalence of categories and Morita equivalence between rings.

Definition 7.1

(
[
CP19
, 4.1, 4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a bicategory, let
M\in\mathcal{B}(C,D)
be a right dualizable
1
-cell, and denote its right dual by
M^{\star}
.
We say the dual pair
(M,M^{\star})
is a
Morita equivalence
in
\mathcal{B}
if the coevaluation
\eta\colon U_{C}\rightarrow M\odot M^{\star}
and evaluation
\varepsilon\colon M^{\star}\odot M\rightarrow D
maps are isomorphisms in
\mathcal{B}(C,C)
and
\mathcal{B}(D,D)
respectively.
If such a pair exists, we say
C
and
D
are
Morita equivalent
.

Proposition 7.2

(
[
CP19
, 4.5]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{B},{\langle\langle}-{\rangle\rangle})
be a shadowed bicategory. Let
M\in\mathcal{B}(C,D)
be a right dualizable
1
-cell and denote its right dual by
M^{\star}
. If
(M,M^{\star})
is a Morita equivalence, then the Euler characteristic
\chi(M)
is an isomorphism, with inverse
\chi(M^{\star})
. In particular, if
C
and
D
are Morita equivalent, then

{\langle\langle}U_{C}{\rangle\rangle}_{C}\cong{\langle\langle}U_{D}{\rangle\rangle}_{D}.

.

Proposition 7.5

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\star}\square_{C}-\colon{}_{C}\mathrm{CoMod}({\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}})}

Remark A.2
.

Just as in Remark

, given

-coalgebras

Let

Let

We now describe fibrant objects in

, 4.15]

Furthermore, usually the functor

Pér24
, 4.21]

We now show that fibrant objects in the category of bicomodules behave well with respect to the cotensor product.

Consider the general case of a symmetric monoidal category,
(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})

remains abelian, and the forgetful functor

(

Lemma A.8

.

One particularly nice characterizing algebraic property of fibrant comodules is that they are

, i.e., they interplay well with the cotensor product and exact sequences. The cotensor product is left-exact, as it preserves finite products and equalizers. We are interested in knowing the cases in which it preserves exactness, without any cocommutativity requirement.

Remark A.13
.

In fact, the result of

In particular, by Lemma

-bicomodule is always two-sided coflat.

. Then we obtain a left
(C\otimes D)
-coaction on
N
via

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes D\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\otimes C\otimes D\otimes N\cong C\otimes D\otimes C\otimes N.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{C}\otimes\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{C}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D\otimes Y}}

The above coaction, denoted
\widetilde{\lambda}\colon C\otimes N\rightarrow(C\otimes D)\otimes C\otimes N
, is counital and coassociative because the left
D
-coaction on
N
and the coalgebra structure on
C
are both coassociative and counital.

To prove the desired isomorphism, we verify that
M\square_{D}N
satisfies the universal property of the equalizer. From the right
(C\otimes D)
-coaction
\rho\colon M\rightarrow M\otimes C\otimes D
, we obtain the underlying right
C
-coaction
\rho_{C}
on
M
as the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes C\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes C.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{X\otimes C}\otimes\varepsilon_{D}}

Now we obtain a morphism
\alpha\colon M\square_{D}N\rightarrow M\square_{C\otimes D}(C\otimes N)
by functoriality of the equalizers:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes D\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{C\otimes D}(C\otimes N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes(C\otimes N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes(C\otimes D)\otimes(C\otimes N).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{C}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{D}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C\otimes N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\widetilde{\lambda}}

The right unlabeled vertical arrow is induced by applying the functor
-\otimes N
on the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M\otimes C)\otimes D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underbrace{(M\otimes(C\otimes C))\otimes D}_{\cong M\otimes(C\otimes D)\otimes C}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{C}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\Delta_{C}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{D}}

Similarly, by applying the counit map
\varepsilon_{C}\colon C\rightarrow\mathbb{I}
vertically on each
C
, we obtain the dashed map on the equalizers below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{C\otimes D}(C\otimes N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes C\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes(C\otimes D)\otimes C\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes D\otimes N.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho\otimes\mathsf{id}_{C\otimes N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\widetilde{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{D}\otimes\mathsf{id}_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{id}_{M}\otimes\lambda}

The induced morphism
\beta
is the inverse of the morphism
\alpha
defined above. Therefore we obtain the desired isomorphism in
\mathsf{C}
.
∎

Lemma A.15
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\otimes,\mathbb{I})
be a symmetric monoidal abelian category.
Let
C
and
D
be flat coalgebras in
\mathsf{C}
.
Let
M
be a
(C,D)
-bicomodule in
\mathsf{C}
. If
M
is right coflat as a right
(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)
-comodule, then it is two-sided coflat as a
(C,D)
-bicomodule.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is right coflat a
(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)
-comodule.
Let us first show that
M
is right coflat over
D
. Consider a short exact sequence in
{}_{D}\mathrm{CoMod}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N^{\prime\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

By the previous lemma, we obtain that
C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N
,
C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime}
, and
C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime\prime}
are left
(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)
-comodules. Since
C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes-
preserves exactness because it is flat, we obtain a short exact sequence in
{}_{(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)}\mathrm{CoMod}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Since
M
is a right coflat over
(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)
, we obtain that the sequence is exact in
\mathsf{C}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D}C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D}C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D}C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes N^{\prime\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

By the previous lemma, this induces the short exact sequence in
\mathsf{C}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}N^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\square_{D}N^{\prime\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Thus
M
is right coflat over
D
as desired. We prove that
M
is left coflat over
C
in a similar fashion.
∎

Example A.16
.

The converse is not true: a two-sided coflat
(C,D)
-bicomodule need not to be a right coflat
(C^{\mathsf{op}}\otimes D)
-comodule, and thus need not be a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule. This can already be seen for modules over an algebra (even commutative). Indeed, consider the polynomial ring,
\mathbbm{k}[x]
, as a
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra. It is a two-sided flat
\mathbbm{k}[x]
-module, but it is not flat as a right
(\mathbbm{k}[x]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x])
-module. Indeed, note first that
\mathbbm{k}[x,y]\cong\mathbbm{k}[x]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x]
. Consider the following short exact sequence of left
k[x,y]
-modules

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x,y]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x,y]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot y}

If we apply
\mathbbm{k}[x]\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}[x,y]}-
, we obtain the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbbm{k}[x]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The above sequence is not a short exact sequence however, which shows that
\mathbbm{k}[x]
is not flat as a right
\mathbbm{k}[x,y]
-module.

We are now equipped to prove a crucial result in this paper: the cotensor product with a fibrant comodule preserves quasi-isomorphisms.

Proposition A.17
.

Let
C
and
D
be simply connected dg-coalgebras over
\mathbbm{k}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule. Then
M\square_{D}-\colon{}_{D}\mathrm{CoMod}({\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
and
-\square_{C}M:\mathrm{CoMod}_{C}({\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
preserve weak equivalences.

Proof.

We use an Eilenberg–Moore spectral sequence argument.
Let
N
be any left
D
-comodule in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
.
As in Remark
3.11
, the conormalized cobar complex
\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}(M,D,N)
is a second quadrant double chain complex that is bounded. We grade the row cohomologically, but the columns homologically. Hence its two associated spectral sequences converge to the same page, see
[
McC01
, 2.15]
.

The first spectral sequence has its
E^{1}
-page induced by the cohomology of the rows and therefore

E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=H^{q}(\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}(M,D,N))\cong\mathrm{CoTor}^{q}_{D}(M,N).

Since
M
is a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule, then it is coflat as a right
D
-comodule. Thus
E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=0
for all
q\geq 1
, and
E^{1}_{\bullet,0}=M\square_{D}N
.
Hence the spectral sequence collapses onto its second page,
E^{2}_{\bullet,0}=H_{*}(M\square_{D}N)
.

The second spectral sequence has its
E^{1}
-page induced by the homology of its columns and therefore

E^{1}_{\bullet,q}=H_{*}(\underline{\Omega}^{q}({X},D,{Y}))=\underline{\Omega^{q}}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(D),H_{*}(N)).

Thus, as its
E^{2}
-page is given by the cohomology of the induced cochain complex, we obtain

E^{2}_{\bullet,q}=\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(D)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N)).

It converges to the page with trivial columns except its
0^{th}
column, which is given by the cohomology
H_{*}(M\square_{D}N)
.

Combining the arguments above, we obtain a converging Eilenberg–Moore spectral sequence

E^{2}_{\bullet,q}=\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(D)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N))\Rightarrow E^{\infty}_{\bullet,0}=H_{*}(M\square_{D}N),

for any left
D
-comodule
N
. In particular, given a map of left
D
-comodules
N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
that is a quasi-isomorphism, we get

\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(D)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N))\cong\mathsf{CoTor}^{q}_{H_{*}(D)}(H_{*}(M),H_{*}(N^{\prime})).

Thus the map
N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism,
H_{*}(M\square_{D}N)\cong H_{*}(M\square_{D}N^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary A.18
.

Let
C
,
D
and
E
be simply connected coalgebras in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
(D,C)
-bicomodule and
N
a fibrant
(C,E)
-bicomodule.
Then we obtain a quasi-isomorphism of

Corollary A.19
.

Let
C
,
D
, and
E
be simply connected coalgebras in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule, let
N
be a left fibrant
D
-comodule and
P
be a fibrant right
E
-comodule.
Then there is a natural quasi-isomorphism of
(C,E)
-bicomodules

\Omega(M,D,N)\otimes P\simeq\Omega(M,C,N\otimes P).

Proof.

Since
M
and
N
are fibrant, we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega(M,D,N)\otimes P

\displaystyle\simeq(M\square_{D}N)\otimes P

\displaystyle\cong M\square_{D}(N\otimes P)

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega(M,D,N\otimes P).

The isomorphism follows from the fact that
P
is flat in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
, and thus preserves equalizers.
The last quasi-isomorphism follows from the fact that
N\otimes P
remains a fibrant
(D,E)
-bicomodule by Lemma
A.9
.
∎

Remark A.20
.

Corollary
A.19
is not true if the comodules are not fibrant, since in general the tensor product does not preserve infinite homotopy limits.

Corollary A.21
.

Let
C
,
D
,
E
, and
F
be simply connected coalgebras in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
(C,D)
-bicomodule, let
N
be a fibrant
(D,E)
-bicomodule, and let
P
be a fibrant
(E,F)
-bicomodule.
Then we obtain a quasi-isomorphism of
(C,F)
-bicomodules

\Omega\big(\Omega(M,D,N),E,P\big)\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta\times\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N),E,P\big).

Proof.

By the previous Corollary, for any
i\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N)\otimes E^{\otimes i}\otimes P\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N\otimes E^{\otimes i}\otimes P)\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega(M,D,N\otimes E^{\otimes i}\otimes P)

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega(M,D,N)\otimes E^{\otimes i}\otimes P

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N)\big)\otimes E^{\otimes i}\otimes P.

In other words, we have proved that we obtain a quasi-isomorphism between fibrant
(C,F)
-bicomodules (recall that we fixed a model for the homotopy limit):

\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\big(\Omega^{i}(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N),E,P)\big)\simeq\Omega^{i}\big(\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N),E,P\big).

This above equivalence is compatible with cofaces and codegeneracies of the cobar construction, and therefore we obtain by
[
Hir03
, 18.5.2, 18.5.3]
:

\displaystyle\Omega(\Omega(M,D,N),E,P)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}\Big(\Omega(M,D,N),E,P\Big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}\Big(\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}\big(M,D,N\big),E,P\Big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N),E,P\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{\Delta\times\Delta}\Omega^{\bullet}(\Omega^{\bullet}(M,D,N),E,P).\qed

.

Let

,

, and

Let

be a fibrant

Proposition A.23
.

Let
C
be a simply connected in
{\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\geq 0}}
.
Let
M
be a fibrant
(C,C)
-bicomodule.
There is a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{coHH}(M,C)\simeq\Omega(M,C\otimes C^{\mathsf{op}},C).

Proof.

We denote the enveloping coalgebra by
C^{e}=C\otimes C^{\mathsf{op}}
.
Recall that we have the quasi-isomorphism
C\simeq\Omega(C,C,C)
as
(C,C)
-bicomodules. Thus we obtain

M\widehat{\square}_{C^{e}}C\simeq M\widehat{\square}_{C^{e}}\Omega(C,C,C).

Notice that each object in the cosimplicial diagram
\Omega^{\bullet}(C,C,C)
is a fibrant
(C,C)
-bicomodule by Lemma
A.8
.
Thus
\Omega(C,C,C)
is a fibrant
(C,C)
-bicomodule by
[
Hir03
, 18.5.2]
.
Therefore

M\widehat{\square}_{C^{e}}\Omega(C,C,C)\simeq M\square_{C^{e}}\Omega(C,C,C).

Since the functor
M\square_{C^{e}}-
preserves towers that stabilize in each degree, we get

\displaystyle M{\square}_{C^{e}}\Omega(C,C,C)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{holim}\Big({M}\square_{C^{e}}\Omega^{\bullet}(C,C,C)\Big).

We have an isomorphism,
\mathrm{coHH}^{n}(M,C)\cong M\square_{C^{e}}\Omega^{n}(C,C,C)
, for each
n\geq 0
, induced by

\displaystyle M\otimes C^{n}\cong M{\square}_{C^{e}}(C^{e}\otimes C^{n})

\displaystyle\cong M{\square}_{C^{e}}(C\otimes C^{n}\otimes C),

given by the permutation of
C^{op}\cong C
past
C^{n}
.
These isomorphisms are compatible with the cosimplicial structures and thus provide an isomorphism,
\mathrm{coHH}^{\bullet}(M,C)\cong M\square_{C^{e}}\Omega^{\bullet}(C,C,C)
.
∎

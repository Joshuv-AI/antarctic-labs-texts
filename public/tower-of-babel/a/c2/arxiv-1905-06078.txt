Jacob’s ladders and infinite set of transmutations of asymptotic complete hybrid formula on level curves in Gauss’ plane
Jan Moser

Abstract
. In this paper we have obtained new phenomenon lying in the following: every fixed asymptotic complete hybrid formula (we call it as mother formula) generates infinite set of new formulas (transmutations) such that every new formula expresses a close binding between some subset of { | ζ ⁡ ( s ) | } \{|\zeta(s)|\} and subset of moduli of certain integral and meromorphic functions. Dedicated to old alchemists

2.
Jacob’s ladder as basis of secondary complete hybrid formula

2.1.

Let us remind that the Jacob’s ladder

\varphi_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(t)

was introduced in
[
2
]
, (see also
[
3
]
), where the function
\varphi_{1}(t)
is arbitrary solution of the non-linear integral equation (also introduced in
[
2
]
)

\int_{0}^{\mu[x(T)]}Z^{2}(t)e^{-\frac{2}{x(T)}t}{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}Z^{2}(t){\rm d}t,

of course,

\begin{split}&Z(t)=e^{i\vartheta(t)}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right),\\
&\vartheta(t)=-\frac{t}{2}\ln\pi+\im\left\{\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{t}{2}\right)\right\},\end{split}

where each admissible function
\mu(y)
generates a solution

y=\varphi(T;\mu)=\varphi(T);\ \mu(y)>7y\ln y.

We call the function
\varphi_{1}(T)
the Jacob’s ladder as an analogue of the Jacob’s dream in Chumash, Bereishis, 28:12.

Remark 7
.

By making use of the Jacob’s ladders we have shown (see
[
2
]
) that the classical Hardy-Littlewood integral (1918)

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|{\rm d}t

has - in addition to previously known Hardy-Littlewood expression (and other similar) possessing an unbounded error term at
T\to\infty
- the following infinite set of almost exact representations

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|{\rm d}t=\varphi_{1}(T)\ln\{\varphi_{1}(T)\}+(c-\ln 2\pi)\varphi_{1}(T)+c_{0}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln T}{T}\right)

as

T\to\infty,

where
c
is the Euler’s constant and
c_{0}
is the constant from the Titschmarsh-Kober-Atkinson formula.

2.2.

Next, we have obtained (see
[
2
]
, (6.2)) the following formula

(2.1)

T-\varphi_{1}(T)\sim(1-c)\pi(T);\ \pi(T)\sim\frac{T}{\ln T},\ T\to\infty,

where
\pi(T)
is the prime-counting function.

Remark 8
.

Consequently, the Jacob’s ladder
\varphi_{1}(T)
can be viewed by our formula (
2.1
) as an asymptotic complementary function to the function

(1-c)\pi(T)

in the following sense

\varphi_{1}(T)+(1-c)\pi(T)\sim T,\ T\to\infty.

Since Jacob’s ladder is exactly increasing function we have the reversely iterated sequence

\{\overset{k}{T}\}_{k=1}^{k_{0}},\ k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}:\ \varphi_{1}(\overset{k}{T})=\overset{k-1}{T},\ \overset{0}{T}=T>T_{0}>0,\ \overset{k}{T}=\varphi_{1}^{-k}(T),

where
T_{0}
is a sufficiently big (we fix
k_{0}
). Next we have (see
[
4
]
, (2.5) – (2.7), (5.1) – (5.13)) the following basic property generated by the Jacob’s ladder.

Property
.

For every segment

[T,T+U],\ U=o\left(\frac{T}{\ln T}\right),\ T\to\infty

there is the following class of disconnected sets

(2.2)

\Delta(T,U,k)=\bigcup_{r=0}^{k}[\overset{r}{\wideparen{T}},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T+U}}],\ 1\leq k\leq k_{0}

generated by the Jacob’s ladder
\varphi_{1}(t)
.

Remark 9
.

Disconnected set (
2.2
) has the following properties (see
[
4
]
, (2.5) – (2.7)):

(a)

lengths of its components are given by

(2.3)

|[\overset{r}{\wideparen{T}},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T+U}}]|\sim o\left(\frac{T}{\ln T}\right),\ T\to\infty,

(b)

lengths of adjacent segments are given by

(2.4)

|[\overset{r-1}{\wideparen{T+U}},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T}}]|\sim(1-c)\frac{T}{\ln T},\ T\to\infty,

i.e. for distance
\rho
of two consecutive components we have

(2.5)

\rho\{[\overset{r-1}{\wideparen{T}},\overset{r-1}{\wideparen{T+U}}];[\overset{r}{\wideparen{T}},\overset{r}{\wideparen{T+U}}]\}\sim(1-c)\frac{T}{\ln T},\ T\to\infty,\ r=1,\dots,k.

Remark 10
.

Formula (
1.4
) follows from (
2.5
) for
r=1
and
T=\pi L
.

Remark 11
.

Asymptotic behavior of the components of the disconnected set (
2.2
) is as follows: at
T\to\infty
these components recede unboundedly each from other and all together are receding to infinity. Hence, at
T\to\infty
the set of the components of (
2.2
) behaves as one-dimensional Friedman-Hubble universe.

d d -dimensional extension of a penalization method for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions: a boundary layer approach and numerical experiments (extended version)
Bouchra Bensiali, Jacques Liandrat

Abstract
This paper studies the d d -dimensional extension of a fictitious domain penalization technique that we previously proposed for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions. We apply Droniou’s approach for non-coercive linear elliptic problems to obtain the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the penalized problem, and we derive a boundary layer approach to establish the convergence of the penalization method. The developed boundary layer approach is adapted from the one used for Dirichlet boundary conditions, but in contrast to the latter where coercivity enables a straightforward estimate of the remainders, we reduce the convergence of the penalization method to the existence of suitable supersolutions of a dual problem. These supersolutions are then constructed as approximate solutions of the dual problem using an additional formal boundary layer approach. The proposed approach results in an advection-dominated problem, requiring the use of appropriate numerical methods suitable for singular perturbation problems. Numerical experiments, using upwind finite differences, validate both the convergence rate and the boundary layer thickness, illuminating the theoretical results.

d\geq 4
. The bilinear form associated with the penalized problem (
14
) is not always coercive for
\varepsilon
small enough.

Proof.

The bilinear form associated with the penalized problem reads:

a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}(\nabla u\cdot n\,u+\alpha uv)

(15)

for
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus

a(u,u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}\nabla u\cdot n\,u+\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}u^{2}.

(16)

Using Green’s theorem (when it is valid, for instance if
n\in C^{1}(\overline{\omega})
), for all
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\omega}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot n\,\varphi=-\int_{\omega}(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi\cdot n+u_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{div}(n)\,\varphi)-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u_{\varepsilon}\varphi

(17)

thus

\int_{\omega}\nabla u\cdot n\,u=-\int_{\omega}u\,\nabla u\cdot n-\int_{\omega}u^{2}\mathrm{div}(n)-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}

(18)

then

\displaystyle a(u,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u^{2}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}u^{2}\mathrm{div}(n)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}u^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\biggl[\int_{\omega}\bigl(2\alpha-\mathrm{div}(n)\bigr)u^{2}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\biggl[\int_{\omega}\bigl(2\alpha-\mathrm{div}(n)\bigr)u^{2}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}\biggr].

We are going to show that the dominant term (in front of
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
) can be negative for a given
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, which will contradict the coercivity of the bilinear form
a
.

Assuming
\mathrm{div}(n)\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
(which holds if
n\in C^{1}(\overline{\omega})
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\bigl(2\alpha-\mathrm{div}(n)\bigr)u^{2}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}-\int_{\partial\mathcal{U}}u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega)}-\|\gamma_{0}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\omega)},

and the result of non-coercivity is related to the non-continuity of the trace operator from
(H^{1}(\omega),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\omega)})
to
(L^{2}(\partial\omega),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\partial\omega)})
. Indeed, let us show the following in our case (domains
\mathcal{U}
,
\omega
and
\Omega
as in Fig.
1
):

\displaystyle\forall C>0,\exists u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ \|\gamma_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\omega)}>C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Let us assume
\omega
of class
C^{1,1}
then using Theorem
7
, there exists a neighborhood of
\partial\omega
in which the distance to the boundary is
C^{1,1}
. Let us define the following sequence of functions, for
j
big enough:

f_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}\text{ solution to}\begin{cases}-\Delta v=0&\text{in $\mathcal{U}$}\\
v=1&\text{on $\partial\mathcal{U}$}\end{cases}&\text{if }x\in\mathcal{U}\\
\frac{1}{1+jd(x,\partial\mathcal{U})}&\text{if }x\in\omega_{j}=\Bigl\{x\in\omega,\ d(x,\partial\mathcal{U})<\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}\Bigr\}\\
\text{ solution to}\begin{cases}-\Delta v=0&\text{in $\omega\setminus\overline{\omega_{j}}$}\\
v=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\\
v=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{j}}&\text{on $\partial\omega_{j}$}\end{cases}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(19)

Clearly,
f_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, since
{f_{j}}_{|\mathcal{U}}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{U})
,
{f_{j}}_{|\omega\setminus\overline{\omega}_{j}}\in H^{1}(\omega\setminus\overline{\omega}_{j})
, and
{f_{j}}_{|\omega_{j}}\in H^{1}(\omega_{j})
(since
d(.,\partial\mathcal{U})\in C^{1,1}(\omega_{j})
and Lipschitz continuous thus
f_{j}
and its first order partial derivatives are bounded, allowing to show
f_{j}
and
\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}
in
L^{2}(\omega_{j})
), and we have by construction continuity of the traces through the interfaces
\partial\mathcal{U}
and
\partial\omega_{j}
. In addition, we have

\begin{cases}f_{j}(x)\mathds{1}_{\omega_{j}}(x)\to 0\quad\text{everywhere in $\omega$}\\
\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}},0\leq f_{j}(x)\mathds{1}_{\omega_{j}}(x)\leq 1\quad\text{everywhere in $\omega$}\end{cases}

(20)

thus, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we get

\|f_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega_{j})}\to 0.

(21)

In
\omega\setminus\overline{\omega_{j}}

of class

for

, so that

is also of class

then

by elliptic regularity and

\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}},0\leq f_{j}(x)\mathds{1}_{\omega\setminus\overline{\omega_{j}}}(x)\leq 1\quad\text{everywhere in $\omega$}\end{cases}

thus, again by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we get

If

\displaystyle\exists C>0,\forall u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ \|\gamma_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},

then we would have for

which is in contradiction with
\mathcal{U}

.

In conclusion, we exhibited special cases for

3.3
Existence and uniqueness using Droniou’s approach

Due to the lack of coercivity, we cannot use the Lax-Milgram theorem in order to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the penalized problem. Instead, we are going to see that our penalized problem (
14
) falls within the framework of a theorem already established by J. Droniou
[
14
]
for non-coercive elliptic linear problems. The proof relies on a dual problem, that we will also encounter in our study of convergence of the penalization method. The dual problem (where the convection terms are in a conservative form) has the following form:

\begin{cases}\text{Find $r_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$ such that $\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$}\\
\int_{\Omega}(\nabla r_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+r_{\varepsilon}\varphi)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\omega}(r_{\varepsilon}\,n\cdot\nabla\varphi+\alpha r_{\varepsilon}\varphi)=\langle L,\varphi\rangle,\end{cases}

(25)

where
L\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and we recall
\alpha\geq 0
.

Theorem 9
(Existence and uniqueness of the solution of the penalized problem and of the solution of the dual problem).

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\Omega
be bounded Lipschitz domains of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(with
\Omega\supset\overline{\mathcal{U}}
),
\chi
be the characteristic function of the extended domain
\omega:=\Omega\setminus\overline{\mathcal{U}}
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{U})
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
g\in H^{1}(\omega)
and
n\in H^{1}(\omega)
. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to the penalized problem (
14
) and a unique solution
r_{\varepsilon}
to the dual problem (
25
), in dimension
d=2
or
3
. If one can choose the lifting
n\in L^{d}(\omega)
then the result holds also in any dimension
d\geq 4
.

Proof.

The penalized problem and its dual form fall within the framework of a theorem already established by J. Droniou for non-coercive linear elliptic problems. Indeed, our penalized problem (
14
) is of the following form (Eq. (4) of
[
14
]
), where the convection terms are in a non-conservative form:

\begin{cases}\text{Find $v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)$ such that $\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)$}\\
\int_{\Omega}A^{T}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega}\varphi{\bf v}\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}bv\varphi+\int_{\Gamma_{f}}\lambda v\varphi\,d\sigma=\langle L,\varphi\rangle_{(H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega))^{\prime},H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)},\end{cases}

(26)

while the dual problem (
25
) is of the following form (Eq. (3) of
[
14
]
), where the convection terms are in a conservative form:

\begin{cases}\text{Find $u\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)$ such that $\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)$}\\
\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega}u{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega}bu\varphi+\int_{\Gamma_{f}}\lambda u\varphi\,d\sigma=\langle L,\varphi\rangle_{(H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega))^{\prime},H^{1}_{\Gamma_{d}}(\Omega)},\end{cases}

(27)

[Theorem 2.1 (Droniou’s article
[
14
]
)] thus gives the existence and uniqueness of the solutions
v
and
u
under some assumptions ((8)–(14) in
[
14
]
) that are verified by our penalized problem and its dual form as detailed in Appendix
B
.
∎

are also of the form
\sum_{j=0}^{2}B_{j}^{i}(x)z^{j}e^{-c(x)z}
with suitable regularity, thus
\forall x\in\omega

\displaystyle\biggl|\theta(x)\Delta\widetilde{W}^{1}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)+2\theta(x)\,\nabla\widetilde{W}^{2}_{z}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\theta(x)\,\Delta\varphi(x)\,\widetilde{W}^{2}_{z}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)-\theta(x)\,\nabla\widetilde{W}^{2}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)\cdot n(x)\biggr|\leq C,

which estimates cannot be refined to
C\varepsilon
, and

\displaystyle\biggl|\varepsilon\theta(x)\Delta\widetilde{W}^{2}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)-\varepsilon\theta(x)\widetilde{W}^{2}\biggl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\biggr)\biggr|\leq C\varepsilon.

Also,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\varepsilon{\Delta\overline{W}^{2}}(x)-\varepsilon\overline{W}^{2}(x)\Bigr|\leq C\varepsilon.

The remaining terms are of the form
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\Delta\theta(x)\,\widetilde{W}^{i}\bigl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)
or
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot\nabla\widetilde{W}^{i}\bigl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)
or
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,\widetilde{W}^{i}_{z}\bigl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)
or
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot n(x)\,\widetilde{W}^{i}\bigl(x,\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\bigr)
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The terms under study are thus of the from

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\Delta\theta(x)\sum_{j=0}^{2}A_{j}^{i}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{j}e^{-c(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}}

or

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{2}B_{j}^{i}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{j}e^{-c(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}}

or

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\sum_{j=0}^{2}B_{j}^{i}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{j}e^{-c(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}}

or

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{k}}\nabla\theta(x)\cdot n(x)\sum_{j=0}^{2}A_{j}^{i}(x)\Bigl(\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{j}e^{-c(x)\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varepsilon}}

Since
\theta\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
, these terms are zero if
\varphi(x)\leq\delta_{0}
for a well-chosen
\delta_{0}>0
. They are also zero outside of the
\omega_{1}
, the support of
\theta
. The
j^{th}
terms are thus bounded by

C\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{j+k}}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\frac{\delta_{0}}{\varepsilon}}\leq C\varepsilon,

since for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varepsilon^{\ell}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\frac{\delta_{0}}{\varepsilon}}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

In conclusion, we have the following estimate

\|R^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{obst}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq C.

(62)

In the following, we will need a finer estimate: since
R^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{obst}}
reduces to
\varepsilon\Delta{\overline{W}^{2}}-\varepsilon\overline{W}^{2}
outside of the support of
\theta
, we have

\displaystyle\|R^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{obst}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C

(63)

\displaystyle\|R^{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{obst}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega\setminus\omega_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon,

(64)

where we recall

.

5.1.3
Condition number

The penalization introduces terms of order
\varepsilon^{-1}
in the matrix
A_{h}
, which leads to an ill-conditioned matrix problem for small values of the penalization parameter
\varepsilon
. Indeed, the condition number in the infinity norm is

\displaystyle\kappa(A_{h})=\|A_{h}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|A_{h}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\|A_{h}\|_{\infty},

(98)

using the stability already established (Lemma
4
). On the other hand

\displaystyle\sum_{m}|A_{nm}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{h^{2}}+1+\frac{\chi_{n}}{\varepsilon}\alpha+2\frac{\chi_{n}}{\varepsilon h}(|(n_{x})_{n}|+|(n_{y})_{n}|)\leq\frac{8}{h^{2}}+1+\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon}+\frac{4}{R\varepsilon h},

(99)

thus

\displaystyle\|A_{h}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{n}\Bigl(\sum_{m}|A_{nm}|\Bigr)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\alpha+\frac{4}{Rh}\Bigr)+\Bigl(1+\frac{8}{h^{2}}\Bigr).

(100)

For a fixed space step
h
, one thus has

\kappa(A_{h})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{=}O(\varepsilon^{-1}),

(101)

which holds in every matrix norm by the equivalence of norms in finite dimension (fixed space step
h
).

Applying a diagonal preconditioner,
C=\mathrm{diag}(A_{h})
, allows to obtain a condition number almost independent of
\varepsilon
as shown in Figure

Special treatment of discontinuities:

We recall that

\chi(x,y)=\chi_{\mathcal{\omega}}(x,y)

(106)

is the characteristic function of

and hence is a discontinuous function, and that

[

where second order convergence was recovered when we impose

on the boundary of the initial domain, we do the same in the two-dimensional case by forcing

Consider for example the upper left corner

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})+u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})+\frac{\chi(x_{c},y_{c})}{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x}(x_{c},y_{c})n_{x}(x_{c},y_{c})+\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial y}(x_{c},y_{c})n_{y}(x_{c},y_{c})+\alpha u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})\Bigr)

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})+u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Bigl(\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x}(x_{c},y_{c})n_{x}(x_{c},y_{c})+\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial y}(x_{c},y_{c})n_{y}(x_{c},y_{c})+\alpha u_{\varepsilon}(x_{c},y_{c})\Bigr)

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the extension to higher dimensions of a previously suggested penalization method for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions
[
7
]
. Our results include the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the penalized problem and the convergence with respect to the penalization parameter towards the solution. The existence and uniqueness have been established using the method of Droniou for non-coercive linear elliptic problems
[
14
]
. To prove the convergence, we developed a boundary layer approach, assuming enough regularity, for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions, adapted from the Dirichlet approach
[
9
]
. The primary challenge in applying the boundary layer approach to Neumann or Robin boundary conditions is that, in contrast to Dirichlet boundary conditions, the penalized problem is not coercive, making it more challenging to derive essential estimates on the remainders. To this aim, in order to derive such estimates, a thorough examination of the dual problem turned out to be pertinent: the convergence result was reduced to finding suitable supersolutions to the dual problem. While the construction of these supersolutions might be explicit in special cases: one-dimensional case, spherical symmetric case, it is more tricky in general settings.
We proposed to conduct an additional formal boundary layer approach on the dual problem in order to construct theses supersolutions as approximated solutions of the dual problem. Finally, we have derived numerical experiments in two dimensions, using adequate finite difference schemes suitable for advection-dominated problems. Even if finite differences are not the best choice of discretization given the discontinuity of the coefficients introduced by the penalization, these numerical simulations allowed to illustrate the theoretical results and also suggest that the convergence result may be valid in less regular settings. It would thus be interesting to weaken the regularity assumptions (ours are not necessarily sharp) in the context of the boundary layer approach or using weak convergence approaches.

Perspectives of this work include different directions:

•

extension of the proposed method to other model problems of interest in fluid mechanics: Stokes, Navier-Stokes equations, …as it has been performed for penalized Dirichlet boundary conditions
[
9
]
,

•

practical extension of the various data on the boundary to the complementary domain,

•

improvement of numerical approximations using stabilized finite elements suitable for singular perturbation problems
[
19
,
24
]
, more suitable for our penalized problem,

•

application to problems raised on moving domains such as those associated with the simulation of population dynamics under climate change
[
8
,
28
]
.

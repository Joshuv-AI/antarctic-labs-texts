Introduction

Long time asymptotics of integrable nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) can be studied by means of the Riemann–Hilbert (RH) approach. In this approach, which has been successfully applied to several initial value problems on the line, both for decaying and nondecaying initial data, a RH problem is associated to the equation and the asymptotic behavior is computed with the aid of Deift–Zhou nonlinear steepest descent techniques.

For initial boundary value problems on the half-line, the RH approach involves additional steps compared to the case on the line, because, in general, not all boundary values are known for a well-posed problem. For instance, if one assumes that the Dirichlet data are given, then the Neumann value has to be computed. This is often referred to as the Dirichlet to Neumann map.

In the case of decaying boundary data, Antonopoulou and Kamvissis
[
1
]
showed for the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation that if the Dirichlet data have sufficient decay as
t\to\infty
, then the Neumann value also decays, thus successfully characterizing the large
t
limit of the Dirichlet to Neumann map for decaying boundary conditions.

In the setting of nondecaying boundary data, however, less is known. In this paper, we consider the special case of asymptotically periodic boundary values. More specifically, we consider solutions
q(x,t)
in the quarter plane
\big\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,x\geq 0,\,t\geq 0\big\}
whose boundary values satisfy

\displaystyle q(0,t)\sim\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},\qquad q_{x}(0,t)\sim c{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},\qquad t\to\infty,

(1.1)

where
\alpha>0
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
are three parameters.

For the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle{\rm i}q_{t}+q_{xx}+2|q|^{2}q=0

(1.2)

Boutet de Monvel and coauthors
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
were able to show that equation (
1.2
) has a solution with boundary values satisfying (
1.1
) and with decay as
x\to\infty
, if and only if the parameters
(\alpha,\omega,c)
satisfy either

\displaystyle c=\pm\alpha\sqrt{\omega-\alpha^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega\geq\alpha^{2}

(1.3)

or

\displaystyle c=i\alpha\sqrt{|\omega|-2\alpha^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega\leq-6\alpha^{2}.

(1.4)

They also computed the long time asymptotics of any such solution using the Deift–Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method.

The first step in the study of initial boundary value problems whose leading order long-time behaviour is described by a single exponential consists of determining those triples
(\alpha,\omega,c)
which are admissible.
Here we call a triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
admissible if there is a solution of the corresponding initial boundary value problem with boundary values of the form (
1.2
) (see Definition
2.2
for the precise definition).
In the case of the focusing NLS equation the admissible parameter triples are precisely those determined by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

The defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle{\rm i}q_{t}+q_{xx}-2|q|^{2}q=0

with boundary values satisfying (
1.1
) has been studied by Lenells
[
12
]
and Lenells and Fokas
[
13
,
14
]
. In
[
12
]
it was shown that every admissible parameter triple belongs to one of five families. Note that the corresponding result for the focusing case only leads to two admissible families (cf. (
1.3
) and (
1.4
)). Thus the defocusing case seems to be richer, although it is still unclear if all of the five families determined in
[
12
]
are indeed admissible.

In this paper we aim to implement the first step in the program initiated by Boutet de Monvel and coauthors described above for the Gerdjikov–Ivanov (GI) equation
[
10
]

\displaystyle{\rm i}q_{t}+q_{xx}+{\rm i}q^{2}\bar{q}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}|q|^{4}q=0.

(1.5)

Equation (
1.5
) is related to the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) equation

\displaystyle{\rm i}u_{t}+u_{xx}-{\rm i}\big(|u|^{2}u\big)_{x}=0

(1.6)

via the invertible gauge transformation

\displaystyle u(x,t)=q(x,t)\exp\left({\rm i}\int_{x}^{\infty}|q(y,t)|^{2}{\rm d}y\right).

(1.7)

The initial boundary value problem for (
1.5
) in the quarter plane
\big\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,x\geq 0,\,t\geq 0\big\}
is overdetermined in the sense that the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary values at
x=0

cannot
both be independently prescribed for a well-posed problem. Indeed, in
[
9
]
it was shown that the Dirichlet initial boundary value problem for (
1.5
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}([0,\infty))
for any
s\in\big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}\big)
,
s\neq\frac{3}{2}
, with given initial data
q(x,0)=g(x)
and Dirichlet boundary data
q(0,t)=h(t)
.
In particular, for any
g\in H^{s}([0,\infty))
and
h\in H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}([0,\infty))
satisfying
g(0)=h(0)
, there exists a
T=T\big(\|g\|_{H^{s}([0,\infty))},\|h\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}([0,\infty))}\big)
such that this problem has a distributional solution

\displaystyle q\in C_{t}^{0}H_{x}^{s}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}})\cap C_{x}^{0}H_{t}^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}({\mathbb{R}}\times[0,T]).

We will not give a complete classification of the admissible parameter triples for (
1.5
) but instead focus on two particularly interesting families of parameters.
The first family arises as a generalization of a two-parameter family of stationary solitons.
The second family arises from the plane wave solutions

\displaystyle q^{b}(x,t)=\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t+{\rm i}bx}

for suitable parameters
\alpha>0
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Within each of these families we give necessary conditions for admissibility.

The proof is inspired by the proof of the corresponding results in
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
.

2
Main result

Before stating our main result, we give the definition of an admissible triple (see Definition 1.2 in
[
5
]
or Definitions 2.1–2.3 in
[
12
]
) and introduce two special families of parameters.
Let
\mathcal{S}([0,\infty))
denote the Schwartz space

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}([0,\infty))=\big\{u\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))\,|\,\sup_{x\geq 0}\big|x^{n}u^{(m)}(x)\big|<\infty\text{ for all }n,m=0,1,\dots\big\}.

Definition 2.1
.

A solution of the GI equation in the quarter plane
\big\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\,|\,x\geq 0,\,t\geq 0\big\}
is a smooth function
q\colon[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{C}}
with
q(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{S}([0,\infty))
for each
t\geq 0
, which satisfies (
1.5
) for
x>0
and
t>0
.

Definition 2.2
.

A parameter triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
with
\alpha>0
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
, is admissible for the GI equation if there exists a solution
q(x,t)
of (
1.5
) in the quarter plane such that

\displaystyle q(0,t)-\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t}\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{x}(0,t)-c{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t}\to 0\text{ sufficiently fast as }t\to\infty.

(2.1)

Remark 2.3
.

We need a certain order of decay in (
2.1
) to show that certain solutions of Volterra equations are well defined and analytic. For example, it is enough to assume that the order of decay is
O\big(t^{-5/2}\big)
.

2.1
The soliton solution

Equation (
1.6
) admits a two-parameter family of solitons
[
11
]
(see also for example (1.2) in
[
7
]
)

\displaystyle u_{\omega,d}(x,t)=\varphi_{\omega,d}(x+dt)\exp\left({\rm i}\omega t-{\rm i}\frac{d}{2}(x+dt)-\frac{3{\rm i}}{4}\int_{x+dt}^{\infty}\varphi_{\omega,d}(y)^{2}{\rm d}y\right),

where
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\omega>d^{2}/4
, and

\displaystyle\varphi_{\omega,d}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{4\omega-d^{2}}{\omega^{1/2}\left(\cosh\big(\sqrt{4\omega-d^{2}}x\big)-\frac{d}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)}}.

Letting
d=0
, applying the gauge transform (
1.7
), and multiplying the resulting function by
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\pi/4}
, we obtain a one-parameter family of solutions of the GI equation with periodic boundary values.
More precisely, we obtain that for every
\omega>0
, the function

\displaystyle q_{\omega}(x,t)=\phi_{\omega}(x){\rm e}^{-\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{4}+\frac{{\rm i}}{4}\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi_{\omega}(y)^{2}{\rm d}y}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t}=\phi_{\omega}(x){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\arctan(\tanh(\omega^{1/2}x))}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},

with

\displaystyle\phi_{\omega}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{4\omega}{\omega^{1/2}\cosh\big(\sqrt{4\omega}x\big)}},

is a solution of (
1.5
) (in the sense of Definition
2.1
) with boundary values

\displaystyle q_{\omega}(0,t)=\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},\qquad(q_{\omega})_{x}(0,t)=c{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},

where

\displaystyle\alpha=2\omega^{1/4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c=-2\omega^{3/4}{\rm i}.

In particular, it follows that the family of parameters

\displaystyle\big\{\big(\alpha=2\omega^{1/4},\,\omega,\,c=-2\omega^{3/4}{\rm i}\big)\,\big|\,\omega>0\big\}=\left\{\left(\alpha,\,\omega=\frac{\alpha^{4}}{16},\,c=-\frac{\alpha^{3}}{4}{\rm i}\right)\bigg|\,\alpha>0\right\}

(2.2)

is admissible for the GI equation.

We note that the parameters associated with the soliton solution
q_{\omega}
satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha^{6}-2\alpha^{2}\omega+2|c|^{2}+4\alpha^{3}\im(c)=0.

(2.3)

2.2
The plane wave

Equation (
1.5
) admits the plane wave solution

\displaystyle q^{b}(x,t)=\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t+{\rm i}bx},

(2.4)

where
\alpha>0
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha^{4}-2b^{2}+2\alpha^{2}b-2\omega=0.

(2.5)

The boundary values of (
2.4
) are given by

\displaystyle q^{b}(0,t)=\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},\qquad q^{b}_{x}(0,t)=c{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t},

where

\displaystyle c=\alpha b{\rm i}.

Substituting the latter expression into (
2.5
), we find that the parameters associated with the plane wave satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle\re(c)=0,\qquad\im(c)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\omega=\frac{\alpha^{6}}{2}+\alpha^{3}\im(c).

(2.6)

Note that the plane wave (
2.4
) itself does not decay as
x\to\infty
and hence is not a solution of (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

2.3
Statement of the result

The following theorem classifies all potentially admissible parameter triples within the families corresponding to the stationary soliton and the plane wave given in (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), respectively.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

(a)

Any admissible triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
which satisfies (
2.3
) belongs to the family

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\alpha,\,\omega,\,c=\pm{\alpha}\sqrt{\omega-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{16}}-\frac{\alpha^{3}}{4}{\rm i}\right)\!\Bigg|\,\alpha>0,\,\omega\geq\frac{\alpha^{4}}{16}\right\}\cup\left\{\left(\alpha,\,-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{4},\,-\frac{\alpha^{3}}{2}{\rm i}\right)\!\bigg|\,\alpha>0\right\}.

(b)

Any admissible triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
which satisfies (
2.6
) belongs to one of the two families

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\alpha,\,\omega,\,c=\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{3\alpha^{4}}{4}-\omega}\right){\rm i}\right)\Bigg|\,\alpha>0,\,\omega\leq-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{4}\right\},

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\alpha,\,\omega,\,c=\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{3\alpha^{4}}{4}-\omega}\right){\rm i}\right)\Bigg|\,\alpha>0,\,\omega\leq-\frac{1}{2}\big(6\sqrt{6}+15\big)\alpha^{4}\right\}.

Remark 2.5
.

The parameter triples determined in Theorem
2.4
are only potentially admissible, i.e., the conditions imposed on a parameter triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
by one of the families derived in Theorem
2.4
are necessary but may not be sufficient for the existence of a solution of (
1.5
) with boundary values satisfying (
2.1
).
It is yet to be determined which of the parameter triples are actually admissible.
In the case of the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation this was done by constructing an appropriate solution with the help of an associated RH problem
[
2
,

,

,

,

3
Eigenfunctions

Equation (
1.5
) is the compatibility condition of the Lax pair

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mu_{x}+{\rm i}k^{2}[\sigma_{3},\mu]=U\mu,\\
\mu_{t}+2{\rm i}k^{4}[\sigma_{3},\mu]=V\mu.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Here
k\in{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the spectral parameter,
\mu(x,t,k)
is a
(2\times 2)
-matrix valued eigenfunction and

\displaystyle U=-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}|q|^{2}\sigma_{3}+kQ,\qquad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\
\bar{q}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle V=-{\rm i}k^{2}|q|^{2}\sigma_{3}+2k^{3}Q-{\rm i}kQ_{x}\sigma_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(q_{x}\bar{q}-q\bar{q}_{x})\sigma_{3}+\frac{{\rm i}}{4}|q|^{4}\sigma_{3}.

The above Lax pair arises from the Lax pair for the DNLS equation discovered by Kaup and Newell
[
11
]
by applying the gauge transformation (
1.7
) (for details see for instance the appendix of
[
15
]
).
Occasionally it is convenient to consider the rescaled Lax-pair

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{x}+{\rm i}k^{2}\sigma_{3}\phi=U\phi,\\
\phi_{t}+2{\rm i}k^{4}\sigma_{3}\phi=V\phi,\end{cases}

(3.2)

which arises from (
3.1
) through the transformation
\phi=\mu{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(k^{2}x+2k^{4}t)\sigma_{3}}
.

For the remainder of the paper let
(\alpha,\omega,c)
be an admissible triple and let
q(x,t)
be an associated solution of the GI equation in the quarter plane satisfying (
2.1
).

3.1
The background eigenfunction

Consider the background
t
-part equation

\displaystyle\phi^{b}_{t}+2{\rm i}k^{4}\sigma_{3}\phi^{b}=V^{b}\phi^{b},

(3.3)

where the matrix
V^{b}
is given by
V
with
q
and
q_{x}
replaced by
\alpha{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t}
and
c{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t}
, respectively. We define a solution
\phi^{b}(t,k)
of (
3.3
) by

\displaystyle\phi^{b}(t,k)={\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{2}t\sigma_{3}}E(k){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\Omega(k)t\sigma_{3}},

where
\Omega(k)
and
E(k)
are defined by

\displaystyle\Omega(k)=\sqrt{4k^{8}+2\omega k^{4}-\frac{\alpha^{6}-2\alpha^{2}\omega+2|c|^{2}+4\alpha^{3}\im(c)}{2}k^{2}+\frac{\big(\alpha^{4}+4\alpha\im(c)-2\omega\big)^{2}}{16}},

\displaystyle E(k)=\sqrt{\frac{2\Omega-H}{2\Omega}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\dfrac{H}{k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)}\\
-\dfrac{H}{k\big({c}-2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)}&1\end{pmatrix},

(3.4)

with

\displaystyle H(k)=\Omega(k)-2k^{4}+\alpha\im(c)-\alpha^{2}k^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{4}}{4}-\frac{\omega}{2}.

We view the functions
\Omega
and
\sqrt{{(2\Omega-H)}/(2\Omega)}
as being defined on the cut complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{2}
, respectively, were
\mathcal{X}_{i}
contains the branch cuts connecting the zeroes and poles of the respective function.

We have that
\det E(k)=1
for
k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}
and that
E(k)
approaches the identity matrix as
k\to\infty
.
Furthermore, the identity

\displaystyle(2\Omega-H)H=-k^{2}\big(2\alpha k^{2}-{\rm i}\bar{c}\big)\big(2\alpha k^{2}+{\rm i}{c}\big)

implies that zero is not a branch point of
\sqrt{{(2\Omega-H)}/(2\Omega)}
so that
E(k)
is analytic near zero, assuming
0\not\in\mathcal{X}
.

Assumption 3.1
.

We will assume that
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
are invariant under the involutions
k\mapsto-k
and
k\mapsto\bar{k}
, that
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{X}_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, is connected and that the branch cuts only intersect transversely in at most finitely many points.

We will see that in our case the above assumptions are always satisfied.

We fix the branches of
\Omega
and
\sqrt{{(2\Omega-H)}/(2\Omega)}
by their asymptotics as
k\to\infty
as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega(k)=2k^{4}+\frac{\omega}{2}+O\big(k^{-2}\big),\qquad\sqrt{\frac{2\Omega-H}{2\Omega}}=1+O\big(k^{-2}\big),\qquad k\to\infty.

The symmetries of the branch cuts together with the asymptotics of
\Omega
at infinity imply that
\Omega
satisfies the identities

\displaystyle\Omega(k)=\Omega(-k),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{1},

and

\displaystyle\Omega(k)=\Omega^{*}(k),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{1},

where
\Omega^{*}(k):=\overline{\Omega(\bar{k})}
denotes the Schwartz conjugate of
\Omega(k)
.
Similar identities are valid for
\sqrt{{(2\Omega-H)}/{(2\Omega)}}
on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{2}
.
In particular, we find

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}E(k)^{*}\sigma_{1}=E(k),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{X},

where
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{1}\cup\mathcal{X}_{2}
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

3.2
Eigenfunctions

We define an action
\hat{\sigma}_{3}
on a
2\times 2
matrix
A
by
\hat{\sigma}_{3}A=[\sigma_{3},A]
, so that
{\rm e}^{\hat{\sigma}_{3}}A={\rm e}^{\sigma_{3}}A{\rm e}^{-\sigma_{3}}
.
We further define three solutions
\{\phi_{j}(x,t,j)\}_{j=1}^{3}
of (
3.2
) by

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x,t,k)=\mu_{1}(x,t,k){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(k^{2}x+(\Omega(k)-\frac{\omega}{2})t)\sigma_{3}},

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(x,t,k)=\mu_{j}(x,t,k){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(k^{2}x+2k^{4}t)\sigma_{3}},\qquad j=2,3,

where
\mu_{j}
are
(2\times 2)
-matrix valued solutions of the Volterra integral equations

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(x,t,k)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}k^{2}x\hat{\sigma}_{3}}\bigg\{\mathcal{E}(t,k)-\mathcal{E}(t,k)\int_{t}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(\Omega(k)-\frac{\omega}{2})(t^{\prime}-t)\hat{\sigma}_{3}}\big[\mathcal{E}^{-1}(t^{\prime},k)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mu_1(x,t,k)=}{}\times\big(V-V^{b}\big)(0,t^{\prime},k)\mu_{1}(0,t^{\prime},k)\big]{\rm d}t^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{x}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}k^{2}x^{\prime}\hat{\sigma}_{3}}[U(x^{\prime},t)\mu_{1}(x^{\prime},t,k)]{\rm d}x^{\prime}\bigg\},

\displaystyle\mu_{j}(x,t,k)=I+\int_{(x_{j},t_{j})}^{(x,t)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}[k^{2}(x^{\prime}-x)+2k^{4}(t^{\prime}-t)]\hat{\sigma}_{3}}W_{j}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime},k),\qquad j=2,3,

(3.5)

with
(x_{2},t_{2})=(0,0)
,
(x_{3},t_{3})=(\infty,t)
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,k)={\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{2}t\hat{\sigma}_{3}}E(k),\qquad W_{j}=(U{\rm d}x+V{\rm d}t)\mu_{j},\quad j=2,3.

Finally, we define domains
D_{j}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, by

\displaystyle D_{1}=\big\{k\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\im k^{2}>0,\,\im\Omega(k)>0\big\},\qquad

\displaystyle D_{2}=\big\{k\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\im k^{2}>0,\,\im\Omega(k)<0\big\},

\displaystyle D_{3}=\big\{k\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\im k^{2}<0,\,\im\Omega(k)>0\big\},\qquad

\displaystyle D_{4}=\big\{k\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\im k^{2}<0,\,\im\Omega(k)<0\big\},

and let
D_{+}=D_{1}\cup D_{3}
and
D_{-}=D_{2}\cup D_{4}
.

Next we will collect some properties of the eigenfunctions
\{\mu_{j}(x,t,k)\}_{j=1}^{3}
:

•

The first (resp. second) column of
\mu_{1}(0,t,k)
is defined and analytic for
k\in D_{-}\setminus\mathcal{X}
(resp.
D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
).
Furthermore, the second column of
\mu_{1}
has a continuous extension to the boundary of
D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
, in the sense that away from the branch points the limits from the right and left onto every branch cut in
D_{+}
and onto each part of the boundary of
D_{+}
exist and are continuous.
Note that if a branch cut can be approached from both right and left from within
D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
, then the right and left limits are, in general, different.

•

\mu_{2}(x,t,k)
is defined and analytic for all
k\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

•

The first (resp. second) column of
\mu_{3}(x,t,k)
is defined and analytic for
\im k^{2}<0
(resp.
\im k^{2}>0
) with a continuous extension to
\im k^{2}\leq 0
(resp.
\im k^{2}\geq 0
).

•

The
\mu_{j}
’s are normalized so that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}[\mu_{1}(0,t,k)-\mathcal{E}(t,k)])=0,\qquad k\in(D_{-}\setminus\mathcal{X},D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}),

\displaystyle\mu_{2}(0,0,k)=I,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{C}},

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\mu_{3}(x,0,k)=I,\qquad k\in\big(\big\{\im k^{2}\leq 0\big\},\big\{\im k^{2}\geq 0\big\}\big),

where
k\in(A_{1},A_{2})
indicates that the first and second columns are valid for
k\in A_{1}
and
k\in A_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

The proof is standard, see for instance
[
8
]
or
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
.
The key argument in the proof can be summarized as follows. The first (resp. second) column of the integrand under the
t
-integral appearing in (
3.5
) contains the exponential

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\Omega(k)(t^{\prime}-t)}\qquad\big(\text{resp. }{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\Omega(k)(t^{\prime}-t)}\big),

which is bounded in
D_{-}\setminus\mathcal{X}
(resp.
D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
).
Furthermore, by assumption (
2.1
) the term
\big(V-V^{b}\big)(0,t^{\prime},k)
decays as
t\to\infty
.
Standard arguments for Volterra integral equations now imply that the first (resp. second) column of
\mu_{1}(0,t,k)
is defined and analytic for
k\in D_{-}\setminus\mathcal{X}
(resp.

The remaining statements follow in a similar fashion.

Spectral functions

We define the spectral functions

and

\displaystyle s(k)=\mu_{3}(0,0,k)=\phi_{3}(0,0,k),\qquad S(k)=\mu_{1}(0,0,k)=\phi_{1}(0,0,k).

In view of the identities
\sigma_{1}{\mu_{j}^{*}}\sigma_{1}=\mu_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,
we may write

\displaystyle s(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{a(\bar{k})}&b(k)\\
\overline{b(\bar{k})}&a(k)\end{pmatrix},\qquad S(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{A(\bar{k})}&B(k)\\
\overline{B(\bar{k})}&A(k)\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\displaystyle\phi_{3}(x,t,k)=\phi_{2}(x,t,k)s(k),\qquad\phi_{1}(x,t,k)=\phi_{2}(x,t,k)S(k).

Note that the analyticity properties of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
carry over to
s
and
S
and thus to
a
,
b
,
A
and
B
.
In particular, the functions
A
and
B
are defined and analytic in
D_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
with a continuous extension to
\bar{D}_{+}\setminus\mathcal{X}
. Furthermore, away from the branch cuts they also have continuous extensions onto any branch cut intersecting
\bar{D}_{+}
. The functions
a
and

are defined and analytic in
\im k^{2}>0
with a continuous extension to
\im k^{2}\geq 0

6
Inadmissible triples

The global relation leads to the following lemma, which is the basis for the proof of Theorem
2.4
(see
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
for the corresponding result for the focusing and defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation, respectively).

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is an unbounded connected component of
D_{1}\setminus\mathcal{X}
and assume that there exists an open set
U\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
such that one of the four branch cuts connecting the eight zeroes of
\Omega^{2}(k)
intersects
U
.
Then the triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
is inadmissible.

The proof is standard, see for example
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
for the proof of the corresponding result in the case of the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For the convenience of the reader however, we will present it here as well.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
be an open set and
C
be a branch cut of
\Omega^{2}(k)
intersecting
U
.
Note that on
C
we have
\Omega_{+}=-\Omega_{-}
, where
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
denote the limits of
\Omega
onto
C
from the left and right, respectively.
Furthermore, since
U\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{1}\subseteq\bar{D}_{1}
, we also have
\im\Omega_{\pm}\geq 0
on
C\cap U
. Hence
\im\Omega_{\pm}=0
on
C\cap U
.
Thus we may define functions
(\mu_{1}(0,t,k))_{\pm}
on
C\cap U
according to (
3.5
) by replacing
\mathcal{E}(t,k)
and
\Omega(k)
with
\mathcal{E}(t,k)_{\pm}
and
\Omega(k)_{\pm}
, respectively.
We further define eigenfunctions
\nu_{\pm}(t,k)
by

\displaystyle(\mu_{1}(0,t,k))_{\pm}=\nu_{\pm}(t,k)\mathcal{E}_{\pm}(t,k),\qquad k\in C\cap U.

(6.1)

In view of (
3.5
) it follows that
\nu_{\pm}
satisfies the integral equation

\displaystyle\nu_{\pm}(t,k)=I-\int_{t}^{\infty}\phi^{b}(t,k)\big(\phi^{b}\big)^{-1}(t^{\prime},k)\big(V-V^{b}\big)(0,t^{\prime},k)\nu_{\pm}(t^{\prime},k)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nu_\pm(t,k) =}{}\times\phi^{b}(t^{\prime},k)\big(\phi^{b}\big)^{-1}(t,k){\rm d}t^{\prime},

(6.2)

where
\phi^{b}(t,k)\big(\phi^{b}\big)^{-1}(t^{\prime},k)
and its inverse are entire functions of
k
.
The latter statement can be verified directly by computation or one may observe that
V^{b}
is polynomial in
k
.
Assumption (
2.1
) yields that
V-V^{b}=O\big(t^{-5/2}\big)
, which implies that the Volterra equation (
6.2
) has a unique solution for
k\in C\cap U
.
Thus
\nu_{+}=\nu_{-}=:\nu
.

Let us consider the second column of equation (
6.1
), evaluated at
t=0
, which reads as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}B(k)\\
A(k)\end{pmatrix}_{\pm}=\nu(0,k)\begin{pmatrix}E_{12}(k)\\
E_{22}(k)\end{pmatrix}_{\pm}.

If we write
\nu(0,k)=(\nu_{ij}(k))_{i,j=1,2}
and use the definition (
3.1
) of
E(k)
, the last equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{B(k)}{A(k)}\right)_{\pm}=\frac{\nu_{11}H_{\pm}-k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)\nu_{12}}{\nu_{21}H_{\pm}-k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)\nu_{22}}.

Using that
H_{+}-H_{-}=\Omega_{+}-\Omega_{-}=2\Omega_{+}
on
C
and
\det\nu=1
(which follows from
\det E(k)=1
), we find that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{B(k)}{A(k)}\right)_{+}-\left(\frac{B(k)}{A(k)}\right)_{-}=\frac{-2k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)\Omega_{+}}{\big(\nu_{21}H_{-}-k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)\nu_{22}\big)\big(\nu_{21}H_{+}-k\big(\bar{c}+2{\rm i}\alpha k^{2}\big)\nu_{22}\big)}\neq 0.

Thus the quotient
B(k)/A(k)
is discontinuous across
C\cap U
.
Since
a(k)
and
b(k)
are continuous in
\bar{D}_{1}\cup\bar{D}_{2}
, this contradicts the global relation (
5.1
). Hence the triple
(\alpha,\omega,c)
is inadmissible.
∎

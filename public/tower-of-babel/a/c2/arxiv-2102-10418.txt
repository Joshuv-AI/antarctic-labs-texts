Thermal lensing-induced soliton molecules in β \beta -phase Gallium Oxide Publication type: Letters
Alain M. Dikandé*, E. Aban Chenui, A. Sunda-Meya, Chenui Aban E, Dikandé Alain M, A. Sunda Meya

Abstract
In recent years, beta gallium oxide ( β \beta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} ) has become the most investigated isomorph of gallium oxide polymorphs, due to the great potential it represents for applications in optoelectronics and photonics for solar technology, particularly in blind ultraviolet photodetector solar cells (SBUV) designs. To optimize its use in these applications, and to identify possible new features, knowledge of its fundamental properties is relevant. In this respect, optical, thermal and electronic properties of β \beta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} have been studied expriementally, providing evidence of a wide-band inorganic and transparent semiconductor with a Kerr nonlinearity. Thermo-optical properties of the material, probed in SBUV sensing experiments, have highlighted a sizable heat diffusion characterized by a temperature gradient along the path of optical beams, quadratic in beam position and promoting a refractive-index change with temperature. The experimentally observed Kerr nonlinearity together with the thermally induced birefringence, point unambiguously to a possible formation of soliton molecules during propagation of high-intensity fields in β \beta - Ga 2 ​ O 3 \text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . To put this conjecture on a firm ground we propose a theoretical analysis, based on the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in 1+1 spatial dimension, in which thermal lensing creates an effective potential quadratic in the coordinate of beam position. Using the non-isospectral inverse-scattering transform method, the exact one-soliton solution to the propagation equation is obtained. This solution features a bound state of entangled pulses forming a soliton molecule, in which pulses are more or less entangled depending on characteristic parameters of the system.

Propagation equation and exact one-soliton solution

Consider an electromagnetic field
\mathcal{E}(x,z,t))
propagating on the
xy
plane of a thin
\beta
-
\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
crystal, assuming that its envelope changes very slowly along
x
. In the paraxial approximation we can pick
\mathcal{E}(x,z,t))=u(x,z)e^{-(kz-i\omega t)}
, where
u(x,z)
is the optical field envelope,
k
is the wave number and
\omega
is the carrier frequency. In the paraxial approximation, the envelope
u(x,z)
of the optical field propagating in the transparent medium with Kerr nonlinearity, obeys the following nonlinear Helmholtz equation in (1+1) dimensions
[
27
]
:

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+2ik\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}+\frac{2k^{2}}{n_{0}}\left[n_{K}|u|^{2}+\Delta n(T)\right]\,u=0,

(5)

where
n_{K}
denotes the Kerr nonlinearity coefficient and
n_{0}
is the unperturbed refractive index (defined as
n_{0}=kc/\omega
, with
c
the speed of light). Replacing
\Delta T(x)
given by eq. (
4
) in the refractive index correction eq. (
1
), and substituting the latter quantity in the (1+1) dimensional Helmholtz equation (
5
), we obtain:

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+2ik\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}+2g\left[|u|^{2}-V(x)\right]u=0,

(6)

where we defined:

g=\frac{n_{K}}{n_{0}}k^{2},

(7)

and;

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu}{2}x^{2}+\mu_{0}\,x,

(8)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\beta}Q}{\kappa b\,n_{K}},\hskip 8.5359pt\mu_{0}=\frac{\tilde{\beta}\gamma}{n_{K}}.

(9)

Equation (
6
) is a perturbed cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation, where the perturbation is introduced by the external potential
V(x)
which depends on the spatial variable
x
. Unlike the homogeneous cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation which is known to be exactly integrable
[
28
,
29
]
, in general the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with an external potential does not admit exact soliton solutions for arbitrary expressions of
V(x)
. Notably, for the particular form of
V(x)
given by formula (
8
), we can introduce the following new variables:

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sqrt{g},\hskip 8.5359pt\tau=\frac{g}{2k}\,z,

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{2}}{2}\,s^{2}+L_{0}\,s,

(10)

\displaystyle L^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{g},\hskip 8.5359ptL_{0}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{g}}.

(11)

Also we redefine the field envelope using a different parameter i.e.
q(s,\tau)\equiv u(x,z)
, such that the inhomogeneous cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
6
) becomes:

iq_{\tau}+q_{ss}+2\left[|q|^{2}-F(s)\right]q=0.

(12)

Equation (
12
) is closely similar to the inhomogeneous cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation considered by Balakrishnan in ref.
[
30
]
, for which the author proposed a non-isospectral inverse-scattering transform approach for the associated exact one-soliton solution. Instructively the non-isospectral inverse-scattering transform
[
30
]
rests essentially on mapping the inhomogeneous cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
12
), onto a pair of linear eigenvalue equations similar to the Lax pair
[
28
,
29
]
, for which exact eigenvalues and the associated eigen functions exist. If they indeed exist the Lax-pair eigenvalue problem is said to be exactly integrable, and so is the generic inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Balakrishnan coined out
[
30
]
that because of the particular quadratic dependence of the external potential
F(s)
in the spatial coordinate
s
, eigenvalues of the Lax pair cannot be constant but will depend on
\tau
. In other words the Lax pair in this particular case is a non-isospectral eigenvalue problem
[
31
,
32
]
.

In the specific case of eq. (
12
), the non-isospectral inverse-scattering transform
[
30
]
assumes an initial beam
q(s,\tau=0)
which is intented to also serve as the scattering potential. The appropriate choice of reflectioness potential for the Lax pair associated with eq. (
12
), is the following sech-shaped initial field envelope
[
30
]
:

q(s,0)=U_{0}\vartheta(s,0)\,\text{sech}\left[2\int_{0}^{s}{\vartheta(y,\tau=0)\,dy}\right],

(13)

where
U_{0}
is a normalization constant and
\vartheta(s,\tau)
is the eigenvalue of the spectral problem. As stressed the eigenvalue
\vartheta(s,\tau)
is not homogeneous, but depends on the variables
s
and
\tau
consistently with the non-isospectral feature of the Lax pair associated with the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
12
). Most generally the eigenvalue
\vartheta(s,\tau)
will be determined by solving the following nonlinear partial differential equation:

\frac{\partial\vartheta}{\partial\tau}=-2\frac{\partial(i\vartheta)^{2}}{\partial s}+\frac{\partial F(s)}{\partial s},

(14)

in ref.
[
30
]
it was shown that the corresponding solution can be expressed as a factor of two functions of different variables, namely:

\vartheta(s,\tau)=G(s)H(\tau).

(15)

The two functions of distinct variables
G(s)
and
H(\tau)
are solutions to the following independent equations:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G^{2}}{\partial s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\,G,\hskip 8.5359pt\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}=\lambda_{0}\,G,

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{1}H^{2}+\lambda_{0},

(17)

with
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{0}
being two arbitrary (we assume so for now) real constants. The set of two ordinary differential equations (
16
) leads to the following solution for
G(s)
:

G(s)=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\,s+G(0),

(18)

provided with the constraint:

F(s)=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{0}}{4}\,s^{2}+\lambda_{0}G(0)\,s.

(19)

Quite remarkably the quantities
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{0}
in eq. (
16

16
) and (
10
). This gives:

G(0)=L_{0},\hskip 8.5359pt\lambda_{1}=-2L^{2},\hskip 8.5359pt\lambda_{0}=1,

(20)

where
L
and
L_{0}
are defined in eq. (
11
). Having the analytical expression of
G(s)
let us turn to the one for
H(\tau)
, which is found by solving the ordinary differential equation (
17
):

\displaystyle H(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(0)\left[\frac{1+\alpha\tanh\left(\Omega\tau\right)}{1+\alpha^{-1}\tanh\left(\Omega\tau\right)}\right],

(21)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2L,\hskip 8.5359pt\alpha=\frac{1}{\Omega\,H(0)}.

(22)

Knowing the analytical expression of the eigenvalue
\vartheta(s,\tau)
, to generate the complete solution

to the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
12

[

]
, consists in reconstructing the complete solution

from the initial profile (

q(s,\tau)=q_{0}(s,\tau)\,\text{sech}\left[\Phi(s,\tau)\right]\exp\left[-i\theta(s,\tau)\right],

(23)

with:

\displaystyle q_{0}(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{0}G(s)H_{i}(\tau),

\displaystyle H_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{sech}^{2}\left(\Omega\tau\right)}{1+\Omega^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\Omega\tau\right)},

(24)

\displaystyle\Phi(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16L_{0}\int_{0}^{\tau}{H_{r}(t)H_{i}(t)dt}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2H_{i}(\tau)F(s)-\ln\left[2H_{i}(\tau)\right],

\displaystyle H_{r}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\frac{(1+\Omega^{2})\tanh\left(\Omega\tau\right)}{1+\Omega^{2}\tanh^{2}\left(\Omega\tau\right)},

(25)

\displaystyle\theta(s,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8L_{0}\int_{0}^{\tau}{\left[H_{r}^{2}(t)-H_{i}^{2}(t)\right]dt}

(26)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2H_{r}(\tau)F(s).

Our final comment in this section is devoted to a relevant feature of the analytical solution (

\gamma

\gamma

implies that the probability of heat flow in the two directions along the axis of beam propagation is the same, whereas an asymmetric external potential

means that one direction will be priviledged. Choosing
\gamma

In fact these behaviours, and specifically the generation of symmetric or asymmetric soliton-molecule structures, are characteristic manifestations of thermo-optical phenomena occuring in the Kerr nonlinear material concomitantly with the propagation of heat-carrying high-intensity optical beams.

From a more general standpoint, birefringence of optical media induced by thermal lensing is a quite common phenomenon in optoelectronic and photonic materials with sizable heat conductivity. This is for instance the case of optoelectronic devices, in which thermally induced birefringence has been shown to enable manipulations of polarization states of solid-state lasers
[

,

self-mode-locked lasers, it was experimentally established
[
33
]
that thermal lensing effects create an effective refractive index that varies quadrically with the laser focus position along the axis of propagation of the laser. Changes in optical indices of photonic waveguides, due to thermo-optical effects, have also been reported recently in several experimental as well as theoretical studies
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,

. Thermal-lensing phenomena in optical media are therefore a subject of great current interest, their manifestations in
\beta
-
\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
that emerged from the present study, offer new perspective for the design of photodetector solar cells extending up to high-power and ultrashort optical fields in the ultraviolet
[
40
]
.

3
Conclusion

In recent years there has been a great deal of interest in characteristic properties of
\beta
-
\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, an inorganic semiconducting isomorph of gallium oxide with outstanding thermo-optical properties. It is thus well established
[
14
]
that
\beta
-
\text{Ga}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is a transparent material with a Kerr optical nonlinearity, on the other hand its thermo-optical properties, probed via measurements of thermo-optical effects caused by optical beam propagation along the axis of high thermal conductivity, point to a linear temperature dependence of its refractive index as suggested e.g. in ref.
[
16
]
. Taking these experimental evidences into consideration, we proposed a theory aimed at predicting the appropriate shape profiles of high-intensity pulses that might propagate along the axis of heat flow in

Asymptotic expansions for the linear PDEs with oscillatory input terms; Analytical form and error analysis
Karolina Kropielnicka, Rafał Perczyński

Abstract
Partial differential equations with highly oscillatory input terms are hardly ever solvable analytically and their numerical treatment is difficult. Modulated Fourier expansion used as an ansatz is a well known and extensively investigated tool in asymptotic numerical approach for this kind of problems. Although the efficiency of this approach has been recognised, its error analysis has not been investigated rigorously for general forms of linear PDEs. In this paper, we start such kind of investigations for a general form of linear PDEs with an input term characterised by a single high frequency. More precisely we derive an analytical form of such an expansion and provide a formula for the error of its truncation. Theoretical investigations are illustrated by computational simulations.



1
Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the linear PDEs featured by highly oscillatory input term
f(x,t)
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\mathcal{L}u(x,t)+f(x,t)u(x,t),\qquad t\in[0,t^{\star}],\ x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

where
\Omega
is a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
while
\mathcal{L}
is a differential operator of degree
p
acting in the following way

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v(x)=\sum_{|\textbf{p}|\leq p}a_{\textbf{p}}(x)\frac{\partial^{|\mathbf{p}|}v(x)}{\partial x_{1}^{p_{1}}\dots\partial x_{m}^{p_{m}}},\quad\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m}),\ p>0,\ \quad x\in\Omega.

(1.2)

The type of the boundary conditions does not need to be specified for the purpose of this paper and depends on the applications. We require, however, the whole initial boundary problem of considerations to be well-posed. The main assumption is the oscillatory in time nature of the input term, more precisely in this manuscript we assume that

f(x,t)=\alpha(x){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t},\quad\omega\gg 1,

(1.3)

where parameter
\alpha(x)
is independent of
\omega
.
Let us observe that the presented theory is also applicable to function
f(x,t)=\beta(x)+\alpha(x){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t}
, which can be obtained by incorporating (also independent of
\omega
)
\beta(x)
into the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
.

The aim of this paper is to express the solution of (
1.1
) as an asymptotic series

u(x,t)\thicksim p_{0,0}(x,t)+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega^{r}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{r,n}(x,t){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}n\omega t},

(1.4)

which is also known as
modulated Fourier expansion
.
In this paper we make the first step towards the derivation of an asymptotic expansion of the solution of (
1.1
) for a more general highly oscillatory function
f(x,t)
of the following form

f(x,t)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\alpha_{n}(x,t){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega_{n}t},\quad|\omega_{n}|\gg 1,\quad N\in\mathbb{N},

(1.5)

where
\alpha_{n}(x,t)
are independent of
\omega_{m}
,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and where possibly occurring resonances will require additional attention.

The numerical-asymptotic approach based on (
1.4
) for problem of type (
1.1
) with (
1.5
) has been widely applied to many differential problems. For example paper
[
Condon et al.(2009)Condon, Deaño, and Iserles
]
engages this methodology for problems in electronic engineering,
[
Condon et al.(2010)Condon, Deaño, and Iserles
]
for systems of differential equations with extrinsic oscillation,
[
Condon et al.(2012)Condon, Deaño, Iserles, and
Kropielnicka
]
for ODEs with time delay, while wave-like problems were threated in
[
Faou and Schratz(2014)
]
,
[
Condon et al.(2019)Condon, Iserles, Kropielnicka, and
Singh
]
and
[
Condon et al.(2021)Condon, Kropielnicka, Lademann, and
Perczyński
]
. This methodology bases on establishing the ansatz solution of type (
1.4
) for the investigated problem. Incorporating the ansatz to the differential equation allows for establishing recurrent or simple, non-oscillatory differential relations for the coefficients
p_{r,n}(x,t)
. Final calculations of the coefficients often require numerical treatment of simple, non-oscillatory PDEs. For this reason methods based on the described approach are called
numerical-asymptotic
solvers. Numerical-asymptotic methods stem from the theory described in
[
Hairer et al.(2010)Hairer, Lubich, and Wanner
]
, where the theoretical fundaments has been presented for slightly simpler case of expansions (where summation over powers of magnitude
\omega^{-r}
is not taken into account).

The novelty of the paper is twofold: We derive (
1.4
) purely analytically (presenting the formulas for
p_{r,n}
) and we provide the formula for the error of the approximation. Unlike in the cited publications we do not assume that ansatz (
1.4
) solves (
1.1
) but derive it making use of Duhamel formula, Neumann series, and of integration by parts.

To present the error of the approximation we observe that in practice instead of an infinite series (
1.4
) we rather resort to partial sums:

u(x,t)=p_{0,0}(x,t)+\sum_{r=1}^{R}\frac{1}{\omega^{r}}\sum_{n=0}^{N}p_{r,n}(x,t){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}n\omega t}+\mathcal{R}_{R,N},\qquad t\in[0,t^{\star}],\ x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},

(1.6)

and are interested in expression
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
. According to our knowledge no papers concerned with the numerical-asymptotic solvers based on modulated Fourier expansions provide neither formula
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
nor its error bounds. All that has been concluded by now is that the leading error term (not the error!) of such truncation scales like
\omega^{-R-1}
. This is however misleading, because the error constant depends on the differential operator
\mathcal{L}
, on the initial condition
u_{0}(x)
and on the parameters
\alpha_{n}(x,t)
. Let us emphasize that the error constant of the leading error term has never been investigated and the form of dependence on the aforementioned parameters has not been known. In this manuscript we provide analytical formulas not only for functions
p_{r,n}(x,t)
, but also for the error term
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
. In contrast to previous research we investigate the whole error term of the truncation (not only the leading error term). Moreover knowledge of the exact formula
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
allows for the realistic estimate of the error. This is important, because it is widely known that modulated Fourier expansions do not have to be convergent. This may happen once error constant
C_{R}
of the error term
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
exceeds the magnitude of
\omega^{-R-1}
. For this particular reason the knowledge of the formula
\mathcal{R}_{R,N}
is so important.

Modulated Fourier expansions of type (
1.4
) have been successfully applied to the problems, where oscillatory nature of solutions has intrinsic origin rather than extrinsic, see for example
[
Ernst and Christian(2001)
,
Gauckler et al.(2018)Gauckler, Hairer, and Lubich
,
Wang and Zhao(2021)
,
Wang and Zhao(2023)
]
. For obvious reasons our study does not cover these problems.

In Section
2
we present solution of the underlying problem with the Duhamel formula and will present it as a series of multivariate integrals. Section
3
is devoted to the asymptotic expansion of these multivariate integrals derived. The main theorem on the asymptotic expansion of the solution to (
1.1
) is presented in Section
4
. Numerical simulations are presented in Section
5
.

2
The solution as a series of the multivariate oscillatory integrals

In this section we will show that function
u\in C^{1}([0,t^{\star}),H^{p}(\Omega))
solving problem (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
can be represented as an infinite sum of multivariate highly oscillatory integrals which, as will be mentioned in Remark
2.2
and later claimed in Theorem
3.1
, scale like
\mathcal{O}(\omega^{-d}),\ d=0,1,2,\dots
.

Let us recall that for the sake of generality of applications of the presented methodology we do not impose any boundary conditions, but require well possedness of the problem under considerations. The following assumption is necessary for reasonings presented in this section.

Assumption 1

We assume that problem (
1.1
) equipped with suitable boundary conditions is well posed and seek solutions
u[t]:=u(\cdot,t)\in C^{1}([0,t^{\star}],H^{p}(\Omega))
while
u_{0}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
. Moreover we assume that the linear, differential operator
\mathcal{L}:D(\mathcal{L})\supseteq H^{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
defined with (
1.2
) is a generator of
C_{0}
(strongly continuous) semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, that
f[t]:=f(\cdot,t)\in C^{1}([0,t^{\star}),L^{\infty}(\Omega))
and that the functions
a_{\textbf{p}}\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
|\textbf{p}|\leq p
, meaning that their all derivatives are bounded on
\Omega

Remark 2.1

Some boundary conditions may enforce additional assumptions on the domain of the operator
\mathcal{L}
. For example zero boundary conditions would require
H^{p}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
instead of
H^{p}(\Omega)
in Assumption
1
. Careful changes of the domains of operators and/or coefficients, however, would not affect the suggested methodology.

Having Assumption
1
satisfied, application of the Duhamel principle (also known as variation-of-constants) to equation (
1.1
) results in its equivalent integral form

\displaystyle u[t]={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f[\tau]u[\tau]\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(2.7)

Let us define space
Y:=C([0,t^{\star}];L^{2}(\Omega))
with standard norm
\|v\|_{Y}=\max_{0\leq t\leq t^{\star}}\|v[t]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Then for function
f
and operator
\mathcal{L}
satisfying Assumption
1
we can define operator
K(u)[t]:Y\rightarrow Y
as an integral

K(u)[t]=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f[\tau]u[\tau]\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad 0\leq t\leq t^{\star},

(2.8)

and analyse its multiple compositions

\displaystyle K^{d}(u)[t]=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau_{1})\mathcal{L}}f[\tau_{1}]\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\mathcal{L}}f[\tau_{2}]\dots\int_{0}^{\tau_{d-1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{(\tau_{d-1}-\tau_{d})\mathcal{L}}f[\tau_{d}]u[\tau_{d}]\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{d}\dots\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}.

(2.9)

Highly oscillatory nature of function
f
, see definition (
1.3
), indicates that these are
d
times nested highly oscillatory integrals.
Obviously equation (
2.7
) can be expressed as

u[t]={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+Ku[t],\quad t\in[0,t^{\star}],

(2.10)

and, as often happens, solved in means of the iterative approximations

u^{[n]}[t]={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+Ku^{[n-1]}[t],\ {\rm where}\ u^{[0]}={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}.

(2.11)

We can easily observe that formula (
2.11
) leads to the well recognised and widely applied truncated sums of Neumann series, also known as Dyson series or
Time Ordering
operator

u^{[n]}[t]=\sum_{d=0}^{n}K^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0},\ {\rm where}\ K^{0}={\rm Id}.

(2.12)

The following theorem is crucial for further investigations as it not only justifies forthcoming expansions but also clearly establishes error bounds for approximation in means of Neumann series for the particular form of (
1.1
), thus of (
2.7
) as they are equivalent in our setting.

Theorem 2.1

Let us make Assumption
1
. Then the elements of an infinite sequence
\left\{u^{[n]}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in Y
defined as in (
2.12
) with the operator
K
given with (
2.8
) converge in norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{Y}
to the unique solution
u^{\star}
of (
1.1
) as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Moreover, the following estimate holds

\qquad\|u^{\star}-u^{[n]}\|_{Y}\leq C_{t^{\star}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\exp\Big(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}{t^{\star}}\Big)\left\|K^{n+1}\right\|_{Y},\\

(2.13)

where
C_{t^{\star}}=\max_{0\leq t\leq{t^{\star}}}\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Proof
Let us start with the observation that there exists
C_{t^{\star}}
such that
\|{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{t^{\star}}
,
t\in[0,{t^{\star}}]
, and that

\displaystyle\|K^{n}\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}\right)^{n}\int_{0}^{t^{\star}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{\tau_{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{n}\dots\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\leq\frac{(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}{t^{\star}})^{n}}{n!}.

(2.14)

This means that
\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\left\|K^{d}\right\|_{Y}\leq\exp\Big(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}{t^{\star}}\Big)
and that
\left\{u^{[n]}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in Y
forms a Cauchy sequence with its limit defined as

\displaystyle u^{\star}[t]:=\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{[n]}[t]=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}K^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}.

(2.15)

Now we can notice that
u^{\star}
solves (
1.1
). Indeed, since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|K^{n}\|_{Y}=0
we obtain

(I-K)u^{\star}[t]=(I-K)\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}K^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}=\left(I-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}K^{n}\right){\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0},

which coincides with the fixed point of (
2.10
) thus solves (
2.7
) and (
1.1
).
Observation that

u^{\star}[t]-u^{[n]}[t]=K^{n+1}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}K^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}

(2.16)

together with (
2.14
) results in the estimate (
2.13
) which completes the poof.

\Box

Remark 2.2

Estimates (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) lead to

\left\|u^{\star}-u^{[n]}\right\|_{Y}\leq C_{t^{\star}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\exp\Big(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}{t^{\star}}\Big)\frac{\left(C_{t^{\star}}\|f\|_{Y}{t^{\star}}\right)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

(2.17)

which obviously specifies certain rate of convergence. Given, however that
f
is highly oscillatory in time, i.e.
f(x,t)={\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t}\alpha(x),\ \omega\gg 1
this estimate is suboptimal. It is well known that integrals of highly oscillatory functions are of small magnitude. To illustrate it let us consider the integral of a continuously differentiable function
g(s)=c(s){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega s}
,
s\in[0,t]
. One can easily observe that

\left|\int_{0}^{t}c(s){\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\bar{C}\min\{t,\omega^{-1}\},

where
\bar{C}
depends on the coefficient
c(s)
and on its derivative.

Following this argumentation and observing that estimate (
2.13
) utilises multivariate integral of highly oscillatory functions, see (
2.9
), we can deduce that under stronger assumptions on our problem the estimate of the rate of convergence in (
2.17
) may be improved to

\left\|u^{\star}-u^{[n]}\right\|\leq C^{n}\cdot\omega^{-n-1}.

One needs to keep in mind, however that the constant is independent of
\omega
but might get dangerously large (even larger then
\omega^{-n-1}
!) as it depends on other few factors, namely
C^{n}(t^{\star},\mathcal{L},u_{0},\alpha)
. For this reason we are interested in the formula describing the error of truncation.

3
Multivariate oscillatory integral as an asymptotic expansion

In this section we are concerned with the operator (
2.8
) where
u={\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
and
f(x,t)=\alpha(x){\mathrm{e}}^{i\omega t}
,
\omega\gg 1
. To distinguish this form we will use letter
T
instead of
K
:

T^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau_{1})\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega\tau_{1}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega\tau_{2}}\dots\int_{0}^{\tau_{d-1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{(\tau_{d-1}-\tau_{d})\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t_{d}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\tau_{d}\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{d}\dots\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}

(3.18)

We are interested in an asymptotic expansion of integrals
T^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0},d=1,\dots n
. More specifically for given
d
we will present
T^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
as a finite series and an error term related to the asymptotic expansion. This will lead us towards the expansions of
n
-th approximations of (
2.7
). Indeed, it is enough to observe that
u^{[n]}
is defined, see (
2.12
), as an infinite sum of integrals
T^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
. Let start with necessary assumption and auxiliary lemmas to which we will refer to in the further part of this manuscript.

Assumption 2

We assume that problem (
1.1
) equipped with suitable boundary conditions is well posed and seek solutions
u[t]:=u(\cdot,t)\in C^{n}([0,t^{\star}],H^{np}(\Omega))
while

u_{0}\in H^{np}(\Omega)
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed number. Moreover we assume that the linear, differential operator
\mathcal{L}:D(\mathcal{L})\supseteq H^{np}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
defined with (
1.2
) is a generator of a differential (in strong operator topology) semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, that

\alpha\in W^{np,\infty}(\Omega)

and that the functions
a_{\textbf{p}}\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
|\textbf{p}|\leq p
, meaning that their all derivatives are bounded on
\Omega

Lemma 3.1

Let us make Assumption
2
. The

-th derivative of expression
{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}}
can be presented in the following way

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{k}\left({\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}}\right)={\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\underbrace{\Big[\dots\big[[\alpha,\mathcal{L}],\mathcal{L}\big],\dots\Big]}_{\mathcal{L}\ appears\ k\ \text{times}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}}=(-1)^{k}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{k}\big(\alpha\big){\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}},\quad k\leq n,

where
\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}\big(\alpha\big)=\alpha,\quad\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{k}\big(\alpha\big)=\big[\mathcal{L},\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{k-1}\big(\alpha\big)\big]
and
[X,Y]\equiv XY-YX
is the commutator of
X
and
Y
.

Lemma 3.2

Let us make Assumption
2
. The following integral can be expressed as a sum of
n+1
components scaling like
\omega^{-m},\quad m=1,\ldots n.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega\tau}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\alpha{\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{({\mathrm{i}}\omega)^{m+1}}\Big[{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{m}\big(\alpha\big){\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}-{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{m}\big(\alpha\big)\Big]u_{0}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\hskip 108.12054pt+\frac{1}{({\mathrm{i}}\omega)^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega\tau}{\mathrm{e}}^{(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\big(\alpha\big){\mathrm{e}}^{\tau\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

Lemma
3.1
can be easily proved by induction while Lemma
3.2
by
n
-timeas integration by parts. Let us observe that in case

and
\alpha\in W^{(n+1)p,\infty}(\Omega)
, the last component of expansion (

To derive the formula for the asymptotic series of the multivariate highly oscillatory integral
T^{d}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
, we will heavily utilise Lemma
3.2
. We will start with the asymptotic expansion of the innermost integral, then we will expand asymptotically each successive integrals up to the outermost. Integration by parts of each integral will result in appearance of the two terms
\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{({\mathrm{i}}\omega)^{m+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{{\mathrm{i}}\omega t}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{m}\big(\alpha\big){\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
and
\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{-1}{({\mathrm{i}}\omega)^{m+1}}{\mathrm{e}}^{t\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{ad}\,_{\mathcal{L}}^{m}\big(\alpha\big)u_{0}
. As a consequence, the number of components in the series of expansion of

will grow exponentially in

, therefore to facilitate the notation we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 3.1

Let us define multi-index

Equality of magnetization and edge current for interacting lattice fermions at positive temperature
Jonas Lampart, Massimo Moscolari, Stefan Teufel, Tom Wessel

Abstract
We prove that the magnetization is equal to the edge current in the thermodynamic limit for a large class of models of lattice fermions with finite-range interactions satisfying local indistinguishability of the Gibbs state, a condition known to hold for sufficiently high temperatures. Our result implies that edge currents in such systems are determined by bulk properties and are therefore stable against large perturbations near the boundaries. Moreover, the equality persists also after taking the derivative with respect to the chemical potential. We show that this form of bulk-edge correspondence is essentially a consequence of homogeneity in the bulk and locality of the Gibbs state. An important intermediate result is a new version of Bloch’s theorem for two-dimensional systems, stating that persistent currents vanish in the bulk.

Introduction

We show that extended fermion systems subject to homogeneous magnetic fields exhibit a form of bulk-edge correspondence in the thermodynamic limit, namely exact equality of magnetization and edge current, at positive temperatures.
Roughly speaking, our assumptions are finite-range interactions, homogeneity in the bulk of the Hamiltonian, and local indistinguishability of the Gibbs state.
The first two are explicit assumptions on the Hamiltonian, the last is known to hold for sufficiently high temperatures
[
17
]
and expected to hold much more generally.

In
[
6
]
a similar result was established for non-interacting fermion systems.
There it is also shown, how this result relates to the better known bulk-edge correspondence of the transport coefficients: Under the assumption of a gapped ground state and in the zero temperature limit, the derivative of the magnetization with respect to the chemical potential converges to the Hall conductivity and the derivative of the edge current with respect to the chemical potential converges to the edge conductance.
In this paper we establish the differentiability of the magnetization with respect to the chemical potential also for interacting systems and thus also the equality of the corresponding derivatives.

Let us now be more specific.
We consider a system of interacting fermions modelled by a sequence of finite-range Hamiltonians
(H_{L}(b))_{L}
defined on boxes
\varLambda_{L}=\{-L,\dotsc,L\}\times\{0,\dotsc,2L\}
and dependent on a homogeneous magnetic field
b
perpendicular to the plane.
We think of
\varLambda_{L}
as a subset of the upper half plane of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and consider a strip
\{-L,\dotsc,L\}\times\{0,\dotsc,D-1\}
of fixed width
D
as the edge region and its complement as the bulk.
In the bulk we assume translation invariance of the Hamiltonian with respect to magnetic translations.

For inverse temperature
\beta>0
, chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and magnetic field
b\in\mathbb{R}
the Gibbs, or thermal, state is defined as

\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta(H_{L}(b)-\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{L})}}{\mathcal{Z}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)},

(1)

where
\mathcal{N}_{L}
is the number operator and
\mathcal{Z}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=\tr\bigl({\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta(H_{L}(b)-\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{L})}\bigr)
is the partition function.
In the absence of interactions,
\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
is naturally a local object, namely it has an integral kernel in which it is possible to identify a bulk and an edge region, see e.g.
[
6
]
.
However, in the interacting setting, the locality of
\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
is a delicate issue, which has been investigated e.g. in
[
8
,
7
,
26
,
17
,
15
]
, see also the more recent
[
5
]
.
In the present work locality of the Gibbs state in the form of
local indistinguishability
is one of the crucial assumptions: Let
X\subset\varLambda^{\prime}\subset\varLambda_{L}
, then we assume that the expectation value of an observable
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
can be approximated by the Gibbs state of the Hamiltonian restricted to
\varLambda^{\prime}
,

\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\bigr)\approx\tr\bigl(\rho_{\varLambda^{\prime}}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\bigr),

up to terms that vanish in the distance of
X
to the boundary
\partial\varLambda^{\prime}
of
\varLambda^{\prime}
.
A subtle point here is that the definition of
\partial\varLambda^{\prime}
depends on whether we consider
\varLambda^{\prime}
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
or as a subset
\varLambda_{L}
.
In the first case
\partial\varLambda^{\prime}
could include parts of the physical edge
\{-L,\ldots,L\}\times\{0\}
and local indistinguishability is only demanded for
X
in the bulk of the system.
In the second case local indistinguishability is also required for
X
located at the edge of the system.
For this reason we speak of
local indistinguishability in the bulk
for the former case and
local indistinguishability everywhere
for the latter.
Note that in our setting a sufficient condition implying local indistinguishability everywhere is a sufficiently high temperature
[
17
]
.
However, for systems with short-range interactions, one may generally expect local indistinguishability to hold away from critical points, i.e. whenever the system has a unique thermal state in the thermodynamic limit.
Such a state has decaying correlations
[
24
, chapter 4]
, which implies local indistinguishability, at least if the decay is sufficiently fast
[
5
]
.

The magnetization is defined as the derivative of the grand canonical pressure
p_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=-\lvert\varLambda_{L}\rvert^{-1}\,\beta^{-1}\ln\bigl(\mathcal{Z}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\bigr)
with respect to the magnetic field
b
, namely

m_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=\frac{\partial}{\partial b}\,p_{L}(\beta,\mu,b).

(2)

Our main result states that whenever the family of finite volume Gibbs states satisfies
local indistinguishability in the bulk
then the magnetization approximately equals the bond current
I^{\mathrm{edge}}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
through an orthogonal line of length
L
at the lower edge of the sample, see Figure
2
and equation
(
9
)
,

\bigl\lvert m_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)-I^{\mathrm{edge}}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\bigr\rvert=\mathcal{O}(L^{-1}).

(3)

Moreover, this current is very well localized near the edge and thus called
edge current
.
Both statements are contained in Theorem
I
and depicted in Figure
2
.
Furthermore, if the finite volume Gibbs state satisfies
local indistinguishability everywhere
, then we show that the thermodynamic limit
m(\beta,\mu,b):=\lim_{L\to\infty}m_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
exists and obtain an equality between the edge current
I^{\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b)
and the magnetization
m(\beta,\mu,b)
in the infinite volume system, see Theorem
II
.
And while the orbital magnetization
m_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
and the edge current
I^{\mathrm{edge}}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
of the finite systems in principle depend on the bulk and edge part of the system, we show that the limits
m(\beta,\mu,b)
and
I^{\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b)
are independent of the specific shape of the interactions at the edge of the system.

However, since we obtain the infinite volume magnetization
m(\beta,\mu,b)
from a limit of finite systems with edges converging to a system on the upper half plane with an infinite edge, one might ask whether
m(\beta,\mu,b)
can be considered a pure bulk quantity.
To answer this question in the affirmative, we show in Theorem
IV
that the magnetization obtained from any KMS state at
(\beta,\mu)
for the translation invariant bulk Hamiltonian defined on the entire plane coincides with
m(\beta,\mu,b)
.

Finally, in Theorem
III
we establish the differentiability of
m(\beta,\mu,b)
with respect to
\mu
.
By comparison to the non-interacting setting, one would expect that, in the presence of a spectral gap and with weak interactions, the zero temperature limit of
\partial_{\mu}m(\beta,\mu,b)
converges to the quantized Hall conductivity.
While this result is not present in the literature and out of the scope of the paper, we show here a preliminary regularity result of
m(\beta,\mu,b)
with respect to
\mu
.

m_{L}\approx I^{\mathrm{edge}}_{L}

Let us end the introduction with a few more comments on the literature.
As already mentioned, analogous mathematical results relating magnetization in the bulk with edge currents for non-interacting systems were obtained in
[
6
]
, with predecessors e.g. in
[
1
,
19
,
18
]
.
Notice that the equality between edge current and magnetization in two-dimensional systems can be also interpreted as a quantum mechanical, microscopic version of Ampère’s law, as it is sometimes addressed in the physics literature, see for instance
[
22
]
, where the effect of a time-dependent magnetic field on the magnetization of localized states is analyzed in a discrete, non-interacting setting.
The existence and properties of edge states of magnetic Schrödinger operators were studied e.g. in
[
11
,
9
]
.
The mathematical literature on bulk-edge correspondence for transport coefficients is vast but concerns almost exclusively non-interacting systems at zero temperature and with a gap in the bulk, e.g.
[
23
,
10
]
.
In
[
13
,
12
,
14
]

the authors derive, starting from the assumption of an incompressible bulk, effective actions for the bulk and the edge system.
While they do not start from a many-body fermion model as we do, they are able to derive much more far-reaching consequences for quantum Hall systems from a seemingly innocuous assumption about the bulk.
In microscopic models of interacting fermions the bulk-edge correspondence of transport coefficients was established at zero-temperature for weakly interacting gapped systems in
[
16
,
20
]
.

2
Mathematical framework and main results

2.1
The Hamiltonian

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,\infty)
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, both equipped with the
1
-metric
\dist(x,y):=\lvert x_{1}-y_{1}\rvert+\lvert x_{2}-y_{2}\rvert
.
For any finite subset
X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
let
\mathfrak{h}_{X}:=\ell^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}^{s})
be the one-body space and
\mathfrak{F}_{X}:=\mathfrak{F}^{-}(\mathfrak{h}_{X})
the corresponding fermionic Fock space.
By
\mathcal{A}_{X}
we denote the algebra of all bounded operators in
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{F}_{X})
that commute with the number operator
\mathcal{N}_{X}:=\sum_{x\in X}a^{*}_{x}\,a_{x}:=\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{j=1}^{s}a^{*}_{x,j}\,a_{x,j}
and by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}:=\bigcup_{X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{X}
the algebra of all local observables that preserve particle number.
Its closure

\mathcal{A}:=\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}}^{\lVert\cdot\rVert}

is a
C^{*}
-algebra and called the quasi-local algebra.

We consider sequences
(H_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
of Hamiltonians defined on boxes
\varLambda_{L}:=\bigl([-L,L]\times[0,2L]\bigr)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that are of the form

\displaystyle H_{L}(b)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\sum_{x,y\in\varLambda_{L}}a^{*}_{x}\,T^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{b}(x,y)\,a_{y}+\sum_{X\subset\varLambda_{L}}\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}(X)\\
&+\sum_{x,y\in\varLambda_{L}}a^{*}_{x}\,T^{\mathrm{edge}}_{b}(x,y)\,a_{y}+\sum_{X\subset\varLambda_{L}}\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}(X)\end{aligned}

(4)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{x,y\in\varLambda_{L}}a^{*}_{x}\,T_{b}(x,y)\,a_{y}+\sum_{X\subset\varLambda_{L}}\varPhi(X).

We sometimes need to restrict this Hamiltonian to other finite sets
\varLambda^{\prime}\subset\varLambda_{L}
.
In this case we write
H_{L}(b)\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}
which means that the sums in
(
4
)
only run over
x,y\in\varLambda^{\prime}
and
X\subset\varLambda^{\prime}
, respectively.

The Hamiltonian is split into a “bulk” part, which is invariant under magnetic translations and an “edge” part, which lives on the lower edge.
Each consists of two contributions:
The kinetic terms

T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}_{b}(x,y):={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}\xmathstrut{.2}}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}\,T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(x,y)

(5)

are a Peierls phase times a hopping amplitude
T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\ordinarycolon}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{s})
, which is uniformly bounded
\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\bigl\lVert T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(x,y)\bigr\rVert\leq C
and satisfies
T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(x,y)={T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(y,x)}^{*}
.
The interactions

\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\ordinarycolon}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\{X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}},\quad X\mapsto\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(X)\in\mathcal{A}_{X}

are self-adjoint, the terms are uniformly bounded,
\sup_{X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\lVert\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(X)\rVert\leq C
, and the corresponding operators
\sum_{X\subset\varLambda_{L}}\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(X)
are assumed to commute with all local number operators
\mathcal{N}_{\{z\}}
for
z\in\varLambda_{L}
.
The last condition is satisfied, e.g., for density-density interactions or external potentials.

Furthermore, all terms are assumed to be of finite range
R\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.
T^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(x,y)=0
if
\dist(x,y)>R
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk/edge}}(X)=0
if
\diam(X)>R
.
As mentioned above, the bulk contributions are assumed to be invariant under magnetic translations, i.e.
T^{\mathrm{bulk}}(x-z,y-z)=T^{\mathrm{bulk}}(x,y)
only depends on the difference
x-y
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}
satisfies
(
20
)
.
And the edge contributions are supported on a strip of fixed width
D
along the lower edge, i.e.
T^{\mathrm{edge}}(x,y)=0
unless
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1,\dotsc,D-1\}
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}(X)=0
unless
X\subset\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1,\dotsc,D-1\}
.
Without loss of generality we choose
D\geq R
, since the presence of the boundary already modifies the Hamiltonian in
\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1,\dotsc,R-1\}
.

A canonical example of a magnetic Hamiltonian with interactions is the Hofstadter-Hubbard model, i.e. the second quantization of the discrete magnetic Laplacian together with an on-site density-density interaction.
More precisely, for the Hofstadter-Hubbard model we have

\mathfrak{h}_{\{x\}}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

T^{\mathrm{bulk}}(x,y)={\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\cdot\delta_{\lvert x-y\rvert=1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}(X)={a^{*}_{x,1}\,a_{x,1}\,a^{*}_{x,2}\,a_{x,2}\cdot\delta_{X=\{x\}}},

which leads to

H_{L}^{\mathrm{HH}}(b)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\varLambda_{L}:\\
\lvert x-y\rvert=1\end{subarray}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}\xmathstrut{.2}}{2}b(x_{1}-y_{1})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\{1,2\}\end{subarray}}a^{*}_{x,j}\,a_{y,j}+\sum_{x\in\varLambda_{L}}a^{*}_{x,1}\,a_{x,1}\,a^{*}_{x,2}\,a_{x,2}.

Near the edge, one could, for example, add an external potential
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}(X)=\phi(x)\,\mathcal{N}_{\{x\}}\,\delta_{X=\{x\}}
or effectively remove individual sites by subtracting all hoppings connected to them.

For a finite subset
\varLambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}
, a Hamiltonian
H\in\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}
, inverse temperature
\beta>0
, chemical potential
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and magnetic field

we denote the grand canonical partition function by

\mathcal{Z}_{\varLambda}[H](\beta,\mu):=\tr\bigl({\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta(H-\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{\varLambda})}\bigr),

and the Gibbs state by

\rho_{\varLambda}[H](\beta,\mu):=\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta(H-\mu\,\mathcal{N}_{\varLambda})}}{\mathcal{Z}_{\varLambda}[H](\beta,\mu)}.

When we consider truncated Hamiltonians in the proofs, we drop the index and informally write
\rho\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr](\beta,\mu)\equiv\rho_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr](\beta,\mu)
.
On the boxes
\varLambda_{L}
we abbreviate

\mathcal{N}_{L}:=\mathcal{N}_{\varLambda_{L}},\quad\mathcal{Z}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=\mathcal{Z}_{\varLambda_{L}}\bigl[H_{L}(b)\bigr](\beta,\mu)\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b):=\rho_{\varLambda_{L}}\bigl[H_{L}(b)\bigr](\beta,\mu).

3.1
Bloch’s theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of Bloch’s theorem, namely Proposition
5
.
The proof is based on the bulk homogeneity of the system and on the continuity equation for the current provided by Lemma
6
, together with the local indistinguishability assumption for the Gibbs state.
The homogeneity in the bulk of the system is encoded in the invariance under magnetic translation, which we briefly recall in the next section.

3.1.1
Magnetic translations

On the one-particle Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
of the full lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, for
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
b>0
the magnetic translation
U_{y}(b)
is defined by its action on
\psi\in\mathfrak{h}
as

\bigl(U_{y}(b)\,\psi\bigr)(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}by_{2}x_{1}}\,\psi(x-y)\quad\text{with adjoint}\quad\bigl(U_{y}^{*}(b)\,\psi\bigr)(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}by_{2}(x_{1}+y_{1})}\,\psi(x+y).

Then, in second quantization, for which we use the same symbol, we obtain

U_{y}^{*}(b)\,a_{x}^{*}\,U_{y}(b)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}y_{2}bx_{1}}\,a_{x-y}^{*}.

With this definition, the kinetic part of the bulk Hamiltonian
(
4
)
is invariant under magnetic translations since

\displaystyle U_{z}^{*}(b)\,a^{*}_{x}\,T^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{b}(x,y)\,a_{y}\,U_{z}(b)

\displaystyle=U_{z}^{*}(b)\,a_{x}^{*}\,U_{z}(b)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}b\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}\xmathstrut{0.2}}{2}(x_{1}-y_{1})}\,T^{\mathrm{bulk}}(x,y)\,U_{z}^{*}(b)\,a_{y}\,U_{z}(b)

\displaystyle=a^{*}_{x-z}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}b\frac{x_{2}+y_{2}\xmathstrut{0.2}}{2}\,(x_{1}-y_{1})-\mathrm{i}b\frac{2z_{2}x_{1}\xmathstrut{0.2}}{2}+\mathrm{i}b\frac{2z_{2}y_{1}\xmathstrut{0.2}}{2}}T^{\mathrm{bulk}}(x-z,y-z)\,a_{y-z}

\displaystyle=a^{*}_{x-z}\,T^{\mathrm{bulk}}_{b}(x-z,y-z)\,a_{y-z}.

Moreover, we assume that the bulk interaction
\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}
is invariant under magnetic translations, namely

U_{z}^{*}(b)\,\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}(X)\,U_{z}(b)=\varPhi^{\mathrm{bulk}}(X-z).

(20)

Hence, the complete bulk part of the Hamiltonian
(
4
)
is invariant under magnetic translations, and

U_{z}^{*}(b)\,H_{L}(b)\big|_{X}\,U_{z}(b)=H_{L}(b)\big|_{X-z}

for
X\subset\varLambda_{L}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\dist(X,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\varLambda_{L})
and
\dist(X-z,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\varLambda_{L})>D
.
This property carries over to the local Gibbs state in the sense, that

U_{z}^{*}(b)\,\rho\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{X}\bigr](\beta,\mu)\,U_{z}(b)=\rho\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{X-z}\bigr](\beta,\mu).

Moreover, the above calculation also shows that
U_{z}^{*}(b)\,J_{k}^{y}(b)\,U_{z}(b)=J_{k}^{y-z}(b)
for all
y_{2},z_{2}\geq R+D
, due to the simple structure of
J_{k}^{y}(b)
.
Then, it follows that

\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{X}\bigr](\beta,\mu)\,J_{1}^{y}(b)\bigr)=\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}(b)\big|_{X-z}\bigr](\beta,\mu)\,J_{1}^{y-z}(b)\bigr)

(21)

if
X
,
y
and

fulfill all mentioned conditions.

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\bigl\lvert\rho_{\infty}(\beta,\mu,b)\bigl(J_{1}^{z}(b)\bigr)\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\lvert\rho_{\infty}(\beta,\mu,b)\bigl(J_{1,L}^{z}(b)\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,J_{1,L}^{z}(b)\bigr)\bigr\rvert+\bigl\lvert\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,J_{1,L}^{z}(b)\bigr)\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}\bigl(\dist(z,\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}\setminus\varLambda_{L})-R\bigr)+C_{\mathrm{B}}\,\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}\Bigl(\bigl[\dist(z,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\varLambda_{L})-D-R\bigr]_{+}\Bigr),

for

For the convergence in

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert I^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)-I^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b)\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq d\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}(L-R).\qed

3.3.3
Independence of the specific edge Hamiltonian
T_{b}^{\mathrm{edge}}
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
II
it remains to show that the thermodynamic limit of the magnetization and the current does not depend on the specific edge contributions
T_{b}^{\mathrm{edge}}
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}
.
We prove this by showing that the edge currents of two finite systems with different edge Hamiltonians are asymptotically equivalent and then taking the thermodynamic limit.

Taking into account the differentiability result with respect to
\mu
of Theorem
III
, this also implies the insensitivity to boundary perturbations of the
\mu
-derivatives in the thermodynamic limit.

Proposition 12
.

Let
\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}\in\ell^{1}
,
\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}
tend to zero, and let
(H_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\tilde{H}_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
be two families of Hamiltonians of the form
(
4
)
that only differ in the definition of the edge contributions
T_{b}^{\mathrm{edge}}
,
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}
and
\tilde{T}_{b}^{\mathrm{edge}}
,
\tilde{\varPhi}^{\mathrm{edge}}
.
Assume that both satisfy local indistinguishability of the Gibbs state at
(\beta,\mu,b)
with
\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}
-decay in the bulk and
\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}
-decay everywhere in the sense of Definition
1
.
Then, denoting the quantities of
\tilde{H}_{L}(b)
with an additional tilde,

I^{\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b)=\tilde{I}^{\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b).

This result shows, that a local perturbation near the edge might change, where exactly the edge current flows, but it does not change the total net current near the boundary.
In this sense, the edge current
I^{\mathrm{edge}}(\beta,\mu,b)
is dictated by the bulk.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to use current conservation and
Proposition 5

(Bloch’s Theorem)
.
to show that the current along the lower and upper edge are the same up to a sign (for both interactions).
Using local indistinguishability we can then prove that the currents along the upper edge are almost the same for both edge interactions.
Thus, also the currents along the lower edge almost agree.

As in section
3.2.2
, we abbreviate
j_{k,L}^{(m,n)}:=\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,J_{k,L}^{(m,n)}(b)\bigr)
.
And for better readability, we drop the arguments
(\beta,\mu,b)
in the following.
The current flowing in the upper edge of the box can be written as

I^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}:=\sum_{n=0}^{d-1}j_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}.

By current conservation (see edge set (a) in Figure
10
) and
Proposition 5

(Bloch’s Theorem)
.
we find

\Bigl\lvert I^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}+I^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}\Bigr\rvert\leq\sum_{n=d}^{2L-d}\bigl\lvert j_{1,L}^{(0,n)}\bigr\rvert\leq 2\,C_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{n=d-R-D}^{\infty}\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}(n)

for all
L\geq d\geq D+R
.
Then, using local indistinguishability for
\varLambda^{\prime}=B_{L}^{(0,2L-n)}(2L-n-D)
, with
0<n<d-1
, which is chosen such that
T_{b}^{\mathrm{edge}}
and
\varPhi^{\mathrm{edge}}
vanish on
\varLambda^{\prime}
, we find

\Bigl\lvert j_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}-\tr\Bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]\,J_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert\leq\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}(2L-n-R-D).

And the same also holds for the corresponding properties of
\tilde{H}_{L}
denoted by an additional tilde.
Due to the choice of
\varLambda^{\prime}
,
H_{L}\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}=\tilde{H}_{L}\big|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}
, and we also have

J_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}=\tilde{J}_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}
for
2L-n>D+R
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert I^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}-\tilde{I}^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\lvert I^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}-\tilde{I}^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}\bigr\rvert+\bigl\lvert I^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}+I^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}\bigr\rvert+\bigl\lvert\tilde{I}^{{d\,{\mathrm{up.}}}\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}+\tilde{I}^{d\,\mathrm{edge}}_{L}\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{d-1}\Bigl\lvert j_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}-{\tilde{\jmath}}_{1,L}^{(0,2L-n)}\Bigr\rvert+4\,C_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{n=d-R-D}^{\infty}\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}(n)

\displaystyle\leq 2\,d\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}(2L+1-d-D-R)+4\,C_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{n=d-R-D}^{\infty}\zeta^{\mathrm{bulk}}(n).

(33)

The statement now follows from Proposition
10
.
∎

3.4

In this section, we discuss the

Defining

(34)

(35)

To prove Theorem

(Bloch’s Theorem)
.
.
Therefore, we start by proving a similar statement to

(Bloch’s Theorem)
.

A key point in the proof of

(Bloch’s Theorem)

Let us start by showing that

As a starting point, note that local indistinguishability everywhere implies decay of correlations, a property needed in the following.

Lemma 13
.

Let
(H_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of Hamiltonians of the form
(
4
)

(\beta,\mu,b)

\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}
-decay in the sense of Definition

\dist(X,Y)>R

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)}(A,B):=\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\,B\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\bigr)\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,B\bigr)

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)}(A,B)\bigr\rvert\leq 3\,g\bigl(\lvert X\rvert+\lvert Y\rvert\bigr)\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\dist(X,Y)-R-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right).

Proof.

Let
\ell=\bigl\lfloor\bigl(\dist(X,Y)-R-1\bigr)/2\bigr\rfloor
and
\varLambda^{\prime}=X_{\ell}\cup Y_{\ell}
, with the
\ell
-neighbourhoods
X_{\ell}:=\bigl\{\,z\in\varLambda_{L}\,\big|\,\dist(z,X)\leq\ell\,\bigr\}
and
Y_{\ell}
.
For better readability we again drop the arguments
(\beta,\mu,b)
.
Then, for all

\Bigl\lvert\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}\,Q\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]\,Q\bigr)\Bigr\rvert\leq g\bigl(\lvert X\rvert+\lvert Y\rvert\bigr)\,\lVert Q\rVert\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}(\ell).

Moreover,
\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]=\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{X_{\ell}}\bigr]\otimes\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{Y_{\ell}}\bigr]

\dist(X_{\ell},Y_{\ell})>R

\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]\,A\,B\bigr)=\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]\,A\bigr)\,\tr\bigl(\rho\bigl[H_{L}|_{\varLambda^{\prime}}\bigr]\,B\bigr)

The statement then follows from the triangle inequality.
∎

This allows us to prove convergence in the thermodynamic limit of the expectation value in the state
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)
of observables that may be supported near the boundary.
We denote by

B_{+}^{x}(\ell):=\bigl\{\,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}\,|\,\dist(x,y)\leq\ell\,\bigr\}

\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}

\varLambda_{L}

Lemma 14
.

Let
n\mapsto n^{2}\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}(n)\in\ell^{1}

(H_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}

(
4
)
.

(
38

(H_{L}(b))_{L\in\mathbb{N}}

\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}
-decay in the sense of Definition
1

x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}

and

\Bigl\lvert\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}}(\beta,\mu,b)\,A\bigr)\Bigr\rvert\leq\beta\,\lVert A\rVert\,\xi\bigl(\dist(x,\varLambda_{L}\setminus\varLambda_{L^{\prime}})-R\bigr).

(36)

For better readability we drop the arguments
(\beta,\mu,b)

Then

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Bigl(\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L}\,A\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}}\,A\bigr)\Bigr)/\beta

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} ={}&\sum_{z\in Z}\tr\bigl(\rho_{L}\,A\,\mathcal{N}_{z}\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\rho_{L^{\prime}}\,A\,\mathcal{N}_{z}\bigr)\\

(37)

Using

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Bigl\lvert\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L}\,A\bigr)-\tr\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}}\,A\bigr)\Bigr\rvert\Big/\bigl(\beta\,\lVert A\rVert\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{aligned} &3\,\sum_{z\in Z}\,\zeta^{\mathrm{edge}}\bigl(\dist(X\cup\{z\},\varLambda_{L}\setminus\varLambda_{L^{\prime}})\bigr)\\

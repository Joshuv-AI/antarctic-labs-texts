Abstract
. Given a compact doubling metric measure space X X that supports a 2 2 -Poincaré inequality, we construct a Dirichlet form on N 1 , 2 ​ ( X ) N^{1,2}(X) that is comparable to the upper gradient energy form on N 1 , 2 ​ ( X ) N^{1,2}(X) . Our approach is based on the approximation of X X by a family of graphs that is doubling and supports a 2 2 -Poincaré inequality (see [ 18 ] ). We construct a bilinear form on N 1 , 2 ​ ( X ) N^{1,2}(X) using the Dirichlet form on the graph. We show that the Γ \Gamma -limit ℰ \mathcal{E} of this family of bilinear forms (by taking a subsequence) exists and that ℰ \mathcal{E} is a Dirichlet form on X X . Properties of ℰ \mathcal{E} are established. Moreover, we prove that ℰ \mathcal{E} has the property of matching boundary values on a domain Ω ⊆ X \Omega\subseteq X . This construction makes it possible to approximate harmonic functions (with respect to the Dirichlet form ℰ \mathcal{E} ) on a domain in X X with a prescribed Lipschitz boundary data via a numerical scheme dictated by the approximating Dirichlet forms, which are discrete objects.

1.
Introduction

The idea of approximating a compact doubling metric space by graphs now is well-established, with the work of
Christ
[
13
]
containing a prototype of such an idea. This idea blossomed into the construction of
hyperbolic fillings of compact doubling spaces to obtain a Gromov hyperbolic space whose boundary is
(homeomorphic to) the given space (see for instance
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
18
]
). Under the
additional condition that the compact doubling metric space is equipped with a doubling measure that supports a
Poincaré inequality, much can be said about the approximating graphs, see
[
18
]
. In the non-smooth
setting of metric measure spaces equipped with a doubling measure supporting a Poincaré inequality,
it is now known that there is a rich theory of harmonic functions (see for instance
[
5
,
6
,
29
]
).
What is missing in the current literature is a construction of a Dirichlet form which
is compatible with the upper gradient structure on the underlying metric
measure space and, in addition, a way of approximating the Dirichlet form via its discrete couterparts
defined on graphs approximating
the metric space so that solutions to a Dirichlet boundary value problem for functions that are harmonic with respect to the limit
Dirichlet form can be approximated by solutions to Dirichlet boundary value problem for the
approximating sequence of graph-Dirichlet forms. The goal of this present note is to demonstrate one way of
constructing such an approximation.

The notion of Dirichlet forms has its roots in the work of Beurling and Deny
[
3
,
4
]
. The theory of
Dirichlet forms has been fleshed out in the textbook
[
17
]
, and an excellent exposition of
the notions of
\Gamma
-convergence
and Mosco-convergence of Dirichlet forms can be found for instance in
[
9
,
15
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
. Of these expositions, the works of
[
9
,
15
,
27
]
consider sequences of Dirichlet forms
on Euclidean spaces, while the works
[
25
,
26
]
consider sequences of Dirichlet forms,
each defined on a (potentially) different metric measure space, converging in a sense akin to measured Gromov-Hausdorff
convergence (see
[
12
]
for more on Gromov-Hausdorff convergence) of Dirichlet forms.

The paper
[
25
]
discusses convergence of graphs to graphs and the
convergence of corresponding
Dirichlet forms, while
[
26
]
studies Gromov-Haudorff convergence of function classes
and Dirichlet forms.
The papers
[
24
,
1
]
construct a Dirichlet form that is equivalent to the Korevaar-Schoen energy,
but their approximations are not discrete and hence do not lend themselves to numerical schemes.
Neither of them work for us as we consider graphs that approximate a potentially non-graph metric
measure space, and we do not know Gromov-Hausdorff convergence of corresponding Sobolev classes. Hence we have
instead used graphs
X_{r}
,
r>0
, to construct a family of Dirichlet forms
\mathcal{E}_{r}
on
N^{1,2}(X)
, see (
3.2
). We then show that
this family of Dirichlet forms has a subsequence that
\Gamma
-converges to a Dirichlet form on
X
so that the domain
of this limit form coincides with the Newton-Sobolev class
N^{1,2}(X)
, but with Dirichlet energy that is comparable to the
upper gradient energy therein. The definition of
\Gamma
-convergence is given in Definition
3.15
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a doubling measure on the complete metric space
(X,d)
so that
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
2
-Poincaré inequality.
Then for forms
\mathcal{E}_{r}
defined by (

), the following is true for every

and its minimal

2
-weak upper gradient

:

(1)

There is a constant

such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\,\sup_{r>0}\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]\leq\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq C\,\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}[u].

(2)

There is a sequence
\{r_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
\lim_{k}r_{k}=0
, such that
\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}

\Gamma
-converges to
a Dirichlet form

on
X

. Moreover, for each

\frac{1}{C}\,\mathcal{E}[u]\leq\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq C\,\mathcal{E}[u].

(3)

The form

closed bilinear form on

This above theorem enables us to have a way of numerically computing Dirichlet energies on a compact
doubling metric measure space supporting a
2
-Poincaré inequality, and in so-doing, we improve on the goal set in
[
16
]

In this goal, we rely on the type of Poincaré inequality on graphs as found in
[
18
]

note. While our construction of approximating Dirichlet forms is based on the work of
[

]

The primary reason for considering this specific construction of a Dirichlet form

Here, given the boundary data

Observe that given a function

with

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and

If there exist

in

and
\mathcal{E}[u]=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}[v_{j}]

then there exists a sequence

\mathcal{E}[u]=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}[u_{j}]

u-u_{j}\in N^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

2.
Background

In this note
(X,d,\mu)
will denote a complete geodesic
metric space
(X,d)
, equipped with a Radon measure
\mu
supported on
X
,
such that
\mu
is
doubling
; that is, there is a constant
C_{d}\geq 1
such that for all
x\in X
and
r>0
we have

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{d}\,\mu(B(x,r)).

Here,
B(x,r):=\{y\in X\,:\,d(x,y)<r\}
is the open metric ball, and
\overline{B}(x,r):=\{y\in X\,:\,d(x,y)\leq r\}
is the closed
ball; note that the topological closure of
B(x,r)
is contained in
\overline{B}(x,r)
, but need not equal.

Definition 2.1
.

With
D\subset L^{2}(X)
a vector subspace,
a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{E}:D\times D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be a Dirichlet form on
D
if

(1)

D
is dense in
L^{2}(X)
in the norm of
L^{2}(X)
,

(2)

with
\mathcal{E}_{1}(u,v):=\mathcal{E}(u,v)+\int_{X}u\,v\,d\mu
for
u,v\in D
,
(D,\mathcal{E}_{1})
is a Hilbert space (in
[
17
]
this
property identified as the closedness property of
\mathcal{E}
, see Definition
4.3
below),

(3)

\mathcal{E}(u_{T},u_{T})\leq\mathcal{E}(u,u)
whenever
u\in D
and
u_{T}:=\max\{0,\min\{u,1\}\}
(in
[
17
]
this
property is identified as the Markovian property).

Associated to the Dirichlet form there is an energy form, also denoted
\mathcal{E}
, with
\mathcal{E}[u]:=\mathcal{E}(u,u)
for
each
u\in D
.

While
[
17
]
sets a Dirichlet form to be
\mathcal{E}:L^{2}(X)\times L^{2}(X)\to[-\infty,\infty]
and then
sets
D
to be the collection of all functions
u\in L^{2}(X)
for which
\mathcal{E}(u,u)<\infty
, this is not necessary and recent papers
on Dirichlet forms do not require
\mathcal{E}
to act on all of
L^{2}(X)
, see for instance
[
25
, Section 5]
,
[
11
]
,
or
[
30
, page 7, Section 2C]
.

Since the doubling measure is supported on
X
, it follows that
(X,d)
is itself a
doubling metric space
; that is,
there is some positive integer
N_{d}
such that for each

x\in X
and
r>0
, there are at most
N_{d}
number of points in
B(x,r)
that are separated by a distance at least
r/2
from
each other; that is, whenever
A\subset B(x,r)
such that for
x,y\in A
we have
d(x,y)\geq r/2
or
x=y
, we must have that

A
has at most
N_{d}
number of elements. It follows that for each
r>0
we can find a set
X_{r}\subset X
such that

(a)

d(x_{1},x_{2})\geq r
whenever
x_{1},x_{2}\in X_{r}
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
,

(b)

X=\bigcup_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}B(\bar{x},r)
.

The set
X_{r}
can be given the structure of a graph, with elements of
X_{r}
being the vertices of the graph and
two vertices
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
neighbors if
\bar{x}\neq\bar{y}
and
B(\bar{x},2r)\cap B(\bar{y},2r)\neq\emptyset
. We denote the
neighborhood relationship by
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
. By gluing an interval of length
r
to the two neighboring vertices
\bar{x},\bar{y}
,
we obtain a metric graph, equipped with the path-metric
d_{r}
. Note that because
X
is a connected metric space, necessarily
we must also have
X_{r}
be a connected metric space. We refer the interested reader to
[
20
]

for details regarding this construction.

Note that
\bar{x}\in X_{r}
now has two identities; one as a point in the graph
X_{r}
, and one as a point in the metric space
X
.
The following lemma compares the two metrics on
X_{r}
, namely the metrics
d_{r}
and
d
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a doubling
geodesic metric space and
(X_{r},d_{r})
be defined as above. Then

\frac{1}{2}d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq 3d(\bar{x},\bar{y})

for all
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
.

Proof.

For
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
with
\bar{x}\neq\bar{y}
, we have that

d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y}):=Nr,

where
N
is the smallest number for which there is a collection of points
\{\bar{x}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
so that
\bar{x}_{0}=\bar{x}
,

\bar{x}_{N}=\bar{y}
and
\bar{x}_{i-1}\sim\bar{x}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,N
. Such a collection is called an
X_{r}
–chain between
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
.
Given the shortest such chain, we see that

d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}d(\bar{x}_{i-1},\bar{x}_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}2r=2d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

Since
X
is a geodesic space, we also
have a reverse relationship between
d
and
d_{r}
as well.

Let
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
be two distinct points, connected in
X
by a geodesic
\gamma
.
If
d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=r
, then
r\leq d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq 4r
. If
d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\geq 2r
,
then
d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\geq 4r
, and we can find points
z_{0}:=\bar{x},z_{1},\dots,z_{N-1},z_{N}:=\bar{y}
on
\gamma
with the property that

d(z_{N_{0}-1},z_{N_{0}})\leq r,\qquad d(z_{i-1},z_{i})=r,\text{ for }i=1,\dots,N_{0}-1,

and
r\leq(N_{0}-1)r\leq d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq N_{0}r
with
N_{0}\geq 3
. Furthermore, each
z_{i}
lies in the ball
B(\bar{z}_{i},r)
, for some
\bar{z}_{i}\in X_{r}

for
i=1,\cdots,N_{0}-1
, where we set
\bar{z}_{0}=\bar{x},\bar{z}_{N_{0}}=\bar{y}
. Then

d(\bar{z}_{i-1},\bar{z}_{i})\leq d(\bar{z}_{i-1},z_{i-1})+d(z_{i-1},z_{i})+d(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})<3r
, and
so
d_{r}(\bar{z}_{i-1},\bar{z}_{i})\leq r
(with the value either
0
, if
\bar{z}_{i-1}=\bar{z}_{i}
, or
r
otherwise).
Thus

d_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}d_{r}(\bar{z}_{i-1},\bar{z}_{i})\leq N_{0}r\leq 3(N_{0}-1)r\leq 3d(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

∎

The graph metric space
X_{r}
plays the role of a discrete approximation
of the metric space
(X,d)
at scale
r
. By equipping
X_{r}
with a “lift” of the measure
\mu
, given by

\mu_{r}(A)=\sum_{\bar{x}\in A}\mu(B(\bar{x},2r))

whenever
A\subset X_{r}
, we obtain a metric measure space
(X_{r},d_{r},\mu_{r})
with the measure
\mu_{r}
a doubling Radon
measure on
X_{r}
. For more on the approximation nature of
(X_{r},d_{r},\mu_{r})
we refer the reader to
[
18
]
.

Given a function
u:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that a non-negative Borel measurable function
g
on
X
is an
upper gradient
of
u

if for every non-constant, compact, rectifiable curve
\gamma
in
X
we have

|u(y)-u(x)|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

where
x
and
y
denote the two end points of
\gamma
. If
u
is a locally Lipschitz-continuous function on
X
, then
the local Lipschitz-constant function
\Lip u
, given by

\Lip u(x)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\,\sup_{x\neq y\in B(x,r)}\,\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{d(y,x)}

is an upper gradient of
u
.

For
1<p<\infty
, we say that
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
if
u
is measurable with
\int_{X}|u|^{p}\,d\mu<\infty
such that
u
has
an upper gradient
g\in L^{p}(X)
. We refer the reader to
[
21
]
for more on upper gradients and
the Sobolev class
N^{1,p}(X)
.

In this note we will also assume that
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality
, that is, there is a constant

C_{P}\geq 1
such that for each ball
B\subset X
and each function-upper gradient pair
(u,g)
on
X
we have

\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|\,d\mu\leq C_{P}\,\rad(B)\,\left(\fint_{B}g^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

A more general version of Poincaré inequality would have the existence of a constant
\Theta\geq 1

such that the right-hand side of the above inequality has
\Theta B
rather than
B
. However, when
X
is a geodesic
space (and a biLipschitz change in the metric does provide a geodesic space), it is possible to choose
\Theta=1
at
the expense of increasing the value of
C_{P}
, and this is what we choose to do here. We refer the interested reader
to
[
19
,
21
]
for more on this.
A theorem of Cheeger
[
12
]
tells us that there is a linear differential structure on such
X
, commensurate with a notion
of Taylor approximation by Lipschitz charts on
X
, and a measurable inner product structure on this differential structure,
so that the inner product norm of the differential of a Lipschitz function

on

is comparable to
\Lip u
. However,
this proof, as given in
[
12
]
, is that of existence.

In the rest of the paper we consider only the case

. The goal of this note is to give a construction of a Dirichlet form
on
X
, by using the ideas of
[
16
,
18
]
, together with a modification of the notion of Mosco convergence,
by considering the natural Dirichlet forms on the approximating graphs.

Remark 2.3
.

It was shown in

that

Proposition 2.4

(Gill-Lopez
[

]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space and
(X_{r},d_{r},\mu_{r})
be defined as above.
If
(X,d,\mu)
admits a Poincare inequality with constants

and
\lambda\geq 1
, then

(X_{r},d_{r},\mu_{r})
admits a Poincare inequality in the Holopainen-Soardi sense: there are constants

independent of

such that for any
\bar{x}_{0}\in X_{r}
,

and

defined on

, we have

\sum_{\bar{x}\in G_{R}}|\bar{u}(\bar{x})-(\bar{u})_{G_{R}}|\frac{\mu_{r}(\bar{x})}{\mu_{r}(G_{R})}\leq C_{HS}\cdot R\left[\sum_{\bar{x}\in G_{\theta R}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\left|\frac{\bar{u}(\bar{x})-\bar{u}(\bar{y})}{r}\right|^{p}\frac{\mu_{r}(\bar{x})}{\mu_{r}(G_{\theta R})}\right]^{1/p},

where

.

Given the graph
X_{r}
as above, the graph energy of a function

is given by

(2.5)

\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|u_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x}).

We straddle three realms here; the graph energy on the graph

, the energy
\mathcal{E}_{r}
induced on

by the graph energy (see (

) below), and the

-limit energy

on

is also derived from a Dirichlet form. The energy

, however, is not known to be from a Dirichlet form, though it
is derived from a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form.

3.
Constructing approximating energy forms
\mathcal{E}_{r}

In this section we use the structure of
X_{r}
to construct an approximating Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}_{r}:N^{1,2}(X)\times N^{1,2}(X)\to[-\infty,\infty]
on
X
as follows. For functions
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
and
\bar{x}\in X_{r}
, we set
u_{r}:X_{r}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

(3.1)

u_{r}(\bar{x}):=\fint_{B(\bar{x},r/4)}u\,d\mu,

and set the Dirichlet form by the formula

(3.2)

\mathcal{E}_{r}(u,v):=\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\,\frac{[u_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{x})][v_{r}(\bar{x})-v_{r}(\bar{y})]}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

whenever
u,v\in N^{1,2}(X)
. It is clear that
\mathcal{E}_{r}
is a bilinear form on the Hilbert space
N^{1,2}(X)
.
We set
\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]:=\mathcal{E}_{r}(u,u)
.

Remark 3.3
.

The form
\mathcal{E}_{r}
is a bilinear energy form but is not known to satisfy the Markov property, and so should, according
to the definition of
[
3
,
17
]
, not be called a Dirichlet form.
Moreover,
\mathcal{E}_{r}
need not be closed as it acts only on
N^{1,2}(X)
, but it is closable in
L^{2}(X)
. The point here
is that we can have a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
of functions in
N^{1,2}(X)
that converges to a function in
L^{2}(X)\setminus N^{1,2}(X)

and so that
\mathcal{E}_{r}(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m})\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
.
However,
\mathcal{E}_{r}
is induced by a bilinear energy form
on the graph
X_{r}
which is indeed a Dirichlet form, and so we allow ourselves the liberty of calling
\mathcal{E}_{r}
also a Dirichlet
form as this does not give rise to any confusion. The
\Gamma
-limit energy form on
N^{1,2}(X)
obtained from
the forms
\mathcal{E}_{r}
will indeed be a Dirichlet form, see Proposition
4.8
and Lemma
4.6
below.

We are also concerned with the natural energy form on
X
inherited from the upper gradient structure; for
u\in N^{1,p}(X)

we set

E[u]:=\inf_{g}\int_{X}g^{2}\,d\mu,

where the infimum is over all upper gradients
g
of
u
. From the results in
[
21
]
we know that there is a minimal

2
-weak upper gradient,
g_{u}
, associated with each
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
, such that

E[u]=\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu.

When
u
is locally Lipschitz on
X
, it follows from
[
12
]
together with
[
21
]
that
g_{u}=\Lip u
.
For functions
u\in L^{2}(X)
that do not have (a modification on a set of measure zero) representative in
N^{1,2}(X)
we set

E[u]:=\infty
.

The following proposition gives us a comparison between the family
\mathcal{E}_{r}
and
E
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a doubling metric measure space admitting a
2
-Poincaré inequality. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]\leq CE[u]

for all
r>0
and
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
. Moreover, whenever
\Lip\,u
is continuous and belongs to
L^{2}(X,d\mu)
,we have

E[u]\leq C\liminf_{r\to 0+}\mathcal{E}_{r}[u].

The constant of comparison,
C
, is dependent
solely on the doubling constant
C_{d}
associated with
\mu
, the metric doubling constant
N_{d}
associated with the doubling
metric space
(X,d)
, and the scaling constant
\theta
from Proposition
2.4
; in particular, it is independent of
u

and
r
.

This proposition will be proved using Lemma
3.5
below.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
be a locally Lipschitz function such that
\Lip u
is a continuous function on
X
.
Then for any
r_{0}>0
and any
x\in X

there exists a ball
B_{x}
, centered at
x
and
of radius
r(B_{x})\leq r_{0}
, such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\rad(B_{x})/36
we have

(3.6)

\Lip u(x)\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x},\bar{y}\in E(x,r(B_{x}))}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r(B_{x})}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})}{\mu(B_{x})^{2}},

where we set

E(x,r(B_{x}))=\{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}:B(\bar{x};\varepsilon)\subset B_{x}\}.

Proof.

We fix
r_{0}>0
and
x\in X
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\Lip u(x)>0
. Then by the continuity of

\Lip u
, there is some positive number
r_{1}\leq r_{0}
such that

(3.7)

\sup_{y\in B(x,r_{1})}|\Lip u(y)-\Lip u(x)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Lip u(x).

Next, by the definition of
\Lip u(x)
, there exists a positive number
r_{2}\leq r_{1}/2
and
a point
y\in B(x,r_{2})
such that

(3.8)

\sup_{x^{\prime}\in B(x,r_{2})}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(x)|}{d(x,x^{\prime})}\leq\frac{3}{2}\Lip u(x)

and

(3.9)

\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{r_{2}}\geq\frac{2}{3}\Lip u(x).

To see this claim, we argue as follows.
By the definition of
\Lip u(x)
we know that there is some
y\in B(x,r_{1}/2)
such that
y\neq x
and

\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{d(y,x)}\geq\frac{2}{3}\Lip u(x).

Setting
r_{2}=d(x,y)
, we obtain (
3.9
). By (
3.7
) we know that

\sup_{w\in B(x,r_{1})}\,\Lip u(w)\leq\frac{3}{2}\Lip u(x),

and as for each
x^{\prime}\in B(x,r_{2})
we know that
B(x^{\prime},r_{2})\subset B(x,r_{1})
, we know that
u
is
\frac{3}{2}\Lip u(x)
-Lipschitz
continuous on
B(x,r_{2})
, and so (
3.8
) follows.

We set
B_{x}:=B(x,r_{2})
and will show that (
3.6
) holds for
\varepsilon\leq r_{2}/36
.
In order to do this, consider the balls
B_{1}=B(x,r_{2}/9)
and
B_{2}=B(y,r_{2}/9)
. Then by the choice of

r_{2}
and by (
3.8
), for all
x^{\prime}\in B_{1}

we have

|u(x^{\prime})-u(x)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\Lip u(x)\,d(x,x^{\prime})<\frac{r_{2}}{6}\,\Lip u(x).

Therefore

\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(x)|}{r_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{6}\Lip u(x).

Similarly, because
X
is a geodesic space and
\Lip u
is an upper gradient of the Lipschitz function
u

(see
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
) and by (
3.7
) we see that
for all
y^{\prime}\in B_{2}
,

|u(y^{\prime})-u(y)|\leq\sup_{z\in B(y,r_{2})}\Lip u(z)\ d(y,y^{\prime})\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\Lip u(x)\ \frac{r_{2}}{9}=\frac{r_{2}}{6}\,\Lip u(x).

Note that
d(x,y)=r_{2}
, and so

E(x,r_{2}/9):=\left\{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}:B(\bar{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{1}\right\}\subset E(x,r_{2}),

E(y,r_{2}/9):=\left\{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}:B(\bar{x},\varepsilon)\subset B_{2}\right\}\subset E(x,r_{2}).

For
\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)
and
\bar{y}\in E(y,r_{2}/9)
we have

\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u(x)|}{r_{2}}\leq\fint_{B(\bar{x},\varepsilon/4)}\frac{|u(x^{\prime})-u(x)|}{r_{2}}\,d\mu(x^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{6}\Lip u(x)

and

\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})-u(y)|}{r_{2}}\leq\fint_{B(\bar{y},\varepsilon/4)}\frac{|u(y^{\prime})-u(y)|}{r_{2}}d\mu(y^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{6}\Lip u(x).

Hence by (
3.9
), for all
\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)
and
\bar{y}\in E(y,r_{2}/9)
,

(3.10)

\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{3}\Lip u(x).

As the balls
B(\bar{x},\varepsilon/2)
are disjoint in
X
and the measure
\mu
is doubling, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9))=\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}\mu(B(\bar{x},\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}\mu(B(\bar{x};\varepsilon/2))

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B_{1}).

Similarly we obtain

\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9))\lesssim\mu(B_{2}).

On the other hand, as
\varepsilon\leq r_{2}/36
and
X_{\varepsilon}
is an
\varepsilon
-net, we have that

\tfrac{1}{2}B_{1}\subset\bigcup_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}B(\bar{x};\epsilon)\ \text{ and }\ \tfrac{1}{2}B_{2}\subset\bigcup_{\bar{y}\in E(y,r_{2}/9)}B(\bar{y};\epsilon).

Therefore

\displaystyle\mu(B_{1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu(\tfrac{1}{2}B_{1})\leq\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}\mu(B(\bar{x};\varepsilon))=\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9)),

\displaystyle\mu(B_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu(\tfrac{1}{2}B_{2})\leq\sum_{\bar{y}\in E(y,r_{2}/9)}\mu(B(\bar{y};\varepsilon))=\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9)).

Combining the above two sets of comparisons, we obtain

\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9))\approx\mu(B_{1})\ \text{ and }\ \mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9))\approx\mu(B_{2}),

and as
r(B_{1})=r(B_{2})=r(B_{x})/9
with the centers
x
and
y
, of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
respectively, in
B_{x}
, we obtain
from the doubling property of
\mu
that

\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9))\approx\mu(B_{x})\ \text{ and }\ \mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9))\approx\mu(B_{x}).

Finally, by (
3.10
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Lip u(x)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x,r_{2}/9)}\sum_{\bar{y}\in E(y,r_{2}/9)}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9))}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9))}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{\bar{x},\bar{y}\in E(x,r(B_{x}))}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(x,r_{2}/9))}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(E(y,r_{2}/9))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x},\bar{y}\in E(x,r(B_{x}))}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})}{\mu(B_{x})^{2}}.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
3.4
.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Let
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
be a locally Lipschitz continuous function on
X
such that its local Lipschitz constant
function
\Lip u
is also continuous on
X
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\int_{X}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu
is positive, for otherwise
u
is constant and all
the relevant Dirichlet energies are zero.
Now we introduce a large parameter
\lambda>1
that we will fix at the end (indeed, it can be
any choice of
\lambda>12\theta+1
with

\theta
from the Poincaré inequality given in Proposition
2.4
above).
Let
K
be a closed ball of sufficiently large radius so that

\int_{X}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\leq 2\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu.

Note that as
X
is complete and doubling, it is proper; hence
K
is compact.
By uniform continuity of
\Lip u
on
10\lambda K
, we can find
r_{1}\in(0,1)
small enough so that

2r_{1}
is less than the radius of
K
and

(3.11)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in 10\lambda K\\
d(x,y)<2r_{1}\end{subarray}}|(\Lip u(x))^{2}-(\Lip u(y))^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\fint_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu.

Here we have fixed a number
\Lambda>2
, to be chosen later.

For each
x\in K
we find a ball
B_{x}
satisfying the conclusions of Lemma
3.5

with the choice of
r_{0}=r_{1}/(5\lambda)
. Such a family of balls is a cover of the compact set
K
, and so we can
obtain a a finite subcover of
K
. Moreover, by the
5r
-covering lemma
(see e.g.
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
21
, page 60]
), we can further find a
subcollection of balls
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that

(3.12)

\{\lambda B_{i}\}\text{ are disjoint and }\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}5\lambda B_{i}\supset K.

With this subcover, we get

\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\fint_{5\lambda B_{i}}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\right)\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i}),

where the balls
B_{i}
satisfy the conclusions of Lemma
3.5
. We denote the centers of these balls by
x_{i}
.
Since the radii of each
5\lambda B_{i}
is no more than
r_{1}
and the center of
5\lambda B_{i}
lies in
K
,
it follows from (
3.11
) that

\fint_{5\lambda B_{i}}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\leq(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\fint_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu.

We now obtain from the above observations that

\displaystyle\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i})+\left(\fint_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu\right)\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu(5\lambda B_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i})+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\fint_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu(B_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i})+\frac{\mu(2K)}{\Lambda}\,\fint_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i})+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu.

The last few steps in the above argument was arrived at by using the fact that the balls
B_{i}
are pairwise disjoint and are
contained in
2K
. The comparison constant associated with the above series of inequalities,
C_{*}
, depends solely on
the doubling constant of
\mu
and the choice of
\lambda
.
We choose
\Lambda\geq 2C_{*}
. It follows that
C_{*}/\Lambda\leq 1/2
, and so

\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu\leq 2\,C_{*}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Lip u(x_{i}))^{2}\cdot\mu(5\lambda B_{i}).

Recall that in Lemma
3.5
, the parameter
\varepsilon_{0}
depended on the radius of the ball
B_{x}
.
We will indicate this dependence by denoting
\varepsilon_{0}
by
\varepsilon_{0}(x)
.
Therefore, choosing any
\varepsilon\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\varepsilon_{0}(x_{i})
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\sum_{\bar{x},\bar{y}\in{E(x_{i},r(B_{i}))}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|}{r(B_{i})}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})}{\mu(B_{i})^{2}}\right]^{2}\mu(B_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\sum_{\bar{x}\in E(x_{i},r(B_{i}))}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-(u_{\varepsilon})_{G(x_{i},r(B_{i}))}|}{r(B_{i})}\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}{\mu(B_{i})}\right]^{2}\mu(B_{i}),

where, as in Lemma
3.5
, we have

\sum_{\bar{y}\in E(x_{i},r(B_{i}))}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})\approx\mu(B_{i})

and

E(x_{i},r(B_{i}))=\{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}\,:\,B(\bar{x};\varepsilon)\subset B_{i}\}\subset G(x_{i},r(B_{i}))=\{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}\,:\,d_{\varepsilon}(x_{i},\bar{x})\leq r(B_{i})\}.

By the doubling property of
\mu
again, we have that
\mu_{\varepsilon}(G(x_{i},r(B_{i})))\approx\mu(B_{i})
.
Now we apply Proposition
2.4
, to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\sum_{\bar{x}\in G(x_{i},\theta r(B_{i}))}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}{\mu(B_{i})}\right]\mu(B_{i})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\sum_{\bar{x}\in G(x_{i},\theta r(B_{i}))}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\,\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{G(x_{i},\theta r(B_{i}))}(\bar{x})\right]\,\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}),

where we used Tonelli’s theorem to obtain the last inequality. Since
\lambda>12\theta+1
and the balls
\lambda B_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,N
are
pairwise disjoint, and as for each
i
we have that
G(x_{i},\theta r(B_{i}))\subset 2\theta B_{i}\subset\lambda B_{i}
, we have that

\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\,\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})-u_{\varepsilon}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})=\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}[u].

The above holds for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, and so we have that

\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{X}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu\leq 2\,\int_{K}(\Lip u)^{2}\,d\mu\lesssim\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}[u],

which verifies the claimed latter inequality in the statement of the proposition.

We now complete the proof of the proposition by verifying the first inequality stated in the claim.
To this end, let
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
. We fix
r>0
such that
r<\text{diam}(X)/2
.
Recall that for distinct
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
with
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
we have
d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in[r,4r)
. Thus

B(\bar{y};r)\subset B(\bar{x};5r)\subset B(\bar{y};9r).

As
\mu
is doubling, it follows that

\frac{\mu(B(\bar{x},5r))}{\mu(B(\bar{y},r))}\leq\frac{\mu(B(\bar{y},9r))}{\mu(B(\bar{y},r))}\leq C_{d}^{4}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\mu(B(\bar{x},5r))}{\mu(B(\bar{x},r))}\leq C_{d}^{3}.

Hence, if
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
, then

\displaystyle|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B(\bar{x};r/4)}\fint_{B(\bar{y};r/4)}|u(x)-u(y)|d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{B(\bar{x};5r)}\fint_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|u(x)-u(y)|d\mu(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|u(x)-u_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|d\mu(x).

So

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\sum_{\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}}\left|\frac{u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})}{r}\right|^{2}\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{d}\cdot\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\left|(5r)^{-1}\fint_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|u(x)-u_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|d\mu(x)\right|^{2}\mu_{r}(\bar{x}),

where
N_{d}
is the doubling constant of the metric space
(X,d)
.
Next, applying the Poincaré inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\fint_{B(\bar{x};5r)}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu\cdot\mu(B(\bar{x};r))

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\int_{B(\bar{x};5r)}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\leq N_{d}^{3}\,\int_{X}(\Lip u)^{2}\ d\mu,

where we have used the fact that two balls
B(\bar{x},5r)
and
B({\bar{x}}^{\prime},5r)
intersect with
\bar{x}\neq{\bar{x}}^{\prime}
if and only if

r\leq d(\bar{x},{\bar{x}}^{\prime})<5r
.
The constants of comparison in the above inequalities all depend only on the doubling constant
C_{d}
of
the measure
\mu
, and in particular are independent of
u
and
r
.
∎

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
X
be a doubling metric measure space admitting a
2
-Poincaré inequality.
Then there is a constant
C>0
such that for each
u\in N^{1,2}(X)

we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\,\sup_{r>0}\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]\leq E[u]=\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq C\,\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}[u].

Moreover, if
u_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is a sequence of functions in
N^{1,2}(X)
with
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
, then

\lim_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}]=\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]
for each
r>0
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows immediately from Proposition
3.4
, and so only the second inequality needs verification.
That is the focus of this proof.

Let
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
, and
(u_{k})_{k}
be a sequence of compactly supported Lipschitz functions on
X
with compactly
supported Lipschitz upper gradients, such that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,p}(X)
; see
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
(note that in
[
12
]
,
the continuous upper gradient
\hat{v}_{k,j(k)}
used to construct the approximating function
u_{k}=\hat{f}_{k,j(k)}
,
by the construction of
u_{k}
, necessarily has the property that
\Lip u_{k}=\hat{v}_{k,j(k)}
).
By passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

\|u-u_{k}\|_{N^{1,2}(X)}\leq 1/k
.
Then by the first part of Proposition
3.4
again, we have that

\mathcal{E}_{r}[u-u_{k}]\leq C\,\int_{X}g_{u-u_{k}}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\frac{C}{k^{2}}.

Note that the constant
C
independent of
r
.
Therefore, for each
\varepsilon>0
we can find a positive integer
k_{\varepsilon}
such that whenever
k_{\varepsilon}\leq k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
for all
r>0
we have
1/k<\varepsilon
and

\mathcal{E}_{r}[u-u_{k}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

Now by the last inequality in the statement of Proposition
3.4
, there is some

r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for each
0<r<r_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\int_{X}g_{u_{k}}^{2}\,d\mu\leq C\,\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}].

For such
r
and
k
, we have by triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}\geq\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}]^{1/2}-\mathcal{E}_{r}[u-u_{k}]^{1/2}\geq\frac{1}{C}\left(\int_{X}g_{u_{k}}^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}-\varepsilon.

The desired conclusion follows from letting
r\to 0^{+}
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We now show that if
(u_{k})_{k}
is a sequence
in
N^{1,2}(X)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
, then for each
r>0
we have that
\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
We fix
r>0
and consider the balls
B_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, related to the construction of
X_{r}
; namely the balls of the
form
B(\bar{x},r)
,
\bar{x}\in X_{r}
. Note that
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
if and only if
B(\bar{x};2r)\cap B(\bar{y};2r)
is nonempty.
Now setting
v_{k}=u_{k}-u
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]=\mathcal{E}_{r}[v_{k}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\in X_{r}\,:\,\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}}\frac{|(v_{k})_{B(\bar{x};r/4)}-(v_{k})_{B(\bar{y};r/4)}|^{2}}{r^{2}}\mu(B(\bar{x};r))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{r^{2}}\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\in X_{r}\,:\,\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}}\left((|v_{k}|)_{B(\bar{x};r/4)}^{2}+(|v_{k}|)_{B(\bar{y};r/4)}^{2}\right)\mu(B(\bar{x};r))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{r^{2}}\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\,\sum_{\bar{y}\in X_{r}\,:\,\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}}\left(\fint_{B(\bar{x};r/4)}|v_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu+\fint_{B(\bar{y};r/4)}|v_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu\right)\mu(B(\bar{x};r)).

Note that if
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
, then
B(\bar{y};r)\subset B(\bar{x};5r)
; moreover, there is at most
N
such
\bar{y}\in X_{r}
that are
neighbors of
\bar{x}
, with
N
independent of
\bar{x}
and
r
(in fact, it depends solely on the metric doubling constant
N_{d}
).
Therefore by the doubling property of
\mu
we have

\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]=\mathcal{E}_{r}[v_{k}]\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\int_{B(\bar{x};5r)}|v_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{X}|v_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu,

where in the last step we have used the bounded overlap property
\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\chi_{B(\bar{x};5r)}\leq K
on
X

with
K
depending solely on the doubling constant
N_{d}
. Thus, if

u_{k}\to u
in
L^{2}(X)
, that is,
v_{k}\to 0
in
L^{2}(X)
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]\to 0
as well as
k\to\infty
.
Note that

\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}-\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]^{1/2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}]^{1/2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]^{1/2}.

This completes the proof of the theorem; though note that the convergence of
\mathcal{E}_{r}[u_{k}-u]
to zero is not
uniform across
r>0
as we have
r^{2}
in the denominator of the above estimate.
∎

Definition 3.15
.

A sequence
\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}
of Dirichlet forms on the Hilbert space
N^{1,2}(X)

is said to
\Gamma
-converge to a Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}
if
the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

Whenever
(u_{k})_{k}
is a sequence in
N^{1,2}(X)
and
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
,
we must have
\mathcal{E}[u]\leq\liminf_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]
;

(ii)

For each
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
there is a sequence
(u_{k})_{k}
in
N^{1,2}(X)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
and

\mathcal{E}[u]\geq\limsup_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]
.

Given the first condition above, the second condition is equivalent to knowing the existence of a sequence
(u_{k})_{k}
in

N^{1,2}(X)
such that
\mathcal{E}[u]=\lim_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]
and
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
.

Proposition 3.16
.

There exists a
\Gamma
-convergent subsequence
\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}
of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
.

Proof.

From
[
2
]
we know that
N^{1,2}(X)
is separable.
Therefore, by
[
14
, Theorem 8.5]
,
there exists a
\Gamma
-convergent subsequence
\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}
of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
.
From
[
14
, Proposition 6.8]
we know that the
\Gamma
-limit is a closable bilinear energy form.
∎

We denote the
\Gamma
-limit of
\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}
from the previous proposition by
\mathcal{E}
. We now show that the
energy form induced by the
\Gamma
-limit
\mathcal{E}
is comparable to the Newton-Sobolev energy form, thus proving
Condition (2) of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.17
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that the following is true for every
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
and its minimal

2
-weak upper gradient
g_{u}\in L^{2}(X)
:

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\,\mathcal{E}[u]\leq\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq C\,\mathcal{E}[u].

Proof.

If
u_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is the sequence guaranteed by the
\Gamma
-limit so that

\lim_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]=\mathcal{E}[u]
and
\|u_{k}-u\|_{N^{1,2}(X)}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, then we have that
\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu=\lim_{k}\int_{X}g_{u_{k}}^{2}\,d\mu
. By
[
14
, Proposition 6.8]
, we know that the
\Gamma
-limit is lower semicontinuous. Then by Theorem
3.13
we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[u]\leq\liminf_{k}\mathcal{E}[u_{k}]\leq C\liminf_{k}\int_{X}g_{u_{k}}^{2}\,d\mu=C\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu,

and so
\mathcal{E}[u]\leq C\,\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu
, which gives the left side of (
3.18
). On the other hand, by Theorem
3.13
, we have

\mathcal{E}[u]^{1/2}=\lim_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]^{1/2}\geq\liminf_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u]^{1/2}-\limsup_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u-u_{k}]^{1/2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{C}\int_{X}g_{u}^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2},

where we have used the fact that (Again from Theorem
3.13
),

(3.19)

0\leq\limsup_{k}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u-u_{k}]\leq\limsup_{k}\int_{X}g_{u_{k}-u}^{2}\,d\mu=0.

This proves Claim (2) of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

One of the key properties needed with respect to the
\Gamma
-convergence is that if the function being approximated
vanishes outside a bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
with
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0
, then the sequence approximating
this function in the
\Gamma
-convergence condition (ii) also vanishes outside the approximating domain. This property
is essential in the discussion on approximating solutions to the Dirichlet problem on
\Omega
, and is called the

matching boundary values
in
[
9
, Section 4.2.1]
.
However, the method of De Giorgi, as outlined in
[
9
]
, uses a decomposition of the domain
\Omega
into suitable
annular rings and constructing a partition of unity subordinate to this decomposition.
An alternate method is to construct different
\Gamma
-limits for different boundary data as in
[
14
, Chapter 21]
,
but this again is computationally involved (see
[
14
, Theorem 21.1]
).
In the second main theorem, Theorem
1.2
,
we give a more direct proof of the existence of optimal sequences with matching boundary
values in the sense of
[
9
]
. We now prove this theorem.

proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
x\in X
set
w(x)=\dist(x,X\setminus\Omega)
. For
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
let

u_{j}=\min\{\max\{u-w,v_{j}\},u+w\}.

Then,

u_{j}-u=\min\{\max\{-w,v_{j}-u\},w\},

and therefore
u_{j}-u\in N^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Further, note that as
w\geq 0
, when
x\in X
we have that
u_{j}(x)-u(x)>0
if and only if
v_{j}(x)-u(x)>0
, and in
which case we also have
u_{j}-u(x)\leq v_{j}-u(x)
. If
u_{j}(x)-u(x)\leq 0
, then necessarily
v_{j}(x)-u(x)\leq 0

and
u_{j}-u(x)\geq v_{j}-u(x)
. Thus,
|u_{j}(x)-u(x)|\leq|v_{j}(x)-u(x)|
for all
x\in X
. Therefore, as
v_{j}\to u
in
L^{2}(X)
, we have

u_{j}\to u
in
L^{2}(X)
.

To see the
N^{1,2}
-convergence, we use
[
21
, Proposition 6.3.23]
.
Using this proposition and the fact that
g_{w}\leq\chi_{\Omega}
, we get

g_{u_{j}-u}\leq\chi_{\{|v_{j}-u|>w\}\cap\Omega}+g_{v_{j}-u}\cdot\chi_{\{|v_{j}-u|\leq w\}\cap\Omega}.

Note that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\int_{X}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{\Omega}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}d\mu=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}d\mu+\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}d\mu,

where
\Omega_{\epsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\dist(x,X\setminus\Omega)>\epsilon\}
. So we get

\int_{X}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\mu(\{|v_{j}-u|>\epsilon\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon})+\int_{\{|v_{j}-u|\leq w\}\cap\Omega}g_{v_{j}-u}^{2}d\mu+\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}).

Note that

\mu(\{|v_{j}-u|>\epsilon\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon})\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}|v_{j}-u|^{2}\,d\mu.

Since
v_{j}\to u
in
L^{2}(X)
, it follows that
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mu(\{|v_{j}-u|>\epsilon\}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon})=0
,
and moreover, as
v_{j}\to u
in the Hilbert space
N^{1,2}(X)
, it also follows that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}g_{v_{j}-u}^{2}\,d\mu=0
. Therefore we have

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}\,d\mu\leq\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}).

As
\epsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small, and
\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}
is empty,
it follows that
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon})=0
, and so

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}g_{u_{j}-u}^{2}\,d\mu=0.

Thus we have that
u_{j}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
.

Finally, note that
|\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}^{1/2}[u_{j}]-\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}^{1/2}[v_{j}]|\leq\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}^{1/2}[u_{j}-v_{j}]
. By
Theorem
3.13
we therefore have that

|\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}^{1/2}[u_{j}]-\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}^{1/2}[v_{j}]|\leq C\,\int_{X}g_{u_{j}-v_{j}}^{2}\,d\mu=C\,\int_{\Omega}g_{v_{j}-u_{j}}^{2}\,d\mu\to 0\text{ as }j\to\infty.

It follows that
\mathcal{E}[u]=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}[v_{j}]=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{j}}[u_{j}]
as desired.
∎

4.
Properties of
\mathcal{E}

In this section we will consider some key basic properties of the limit form
\mathcal{E}
.
There is a property of
\mathcal{E}
that would be essential in order to call
\mathcal{E}
a Dirichlet form; namely, that

\mathcal{E}
is Markovian. In this section we will also prove that
\mathcal{E}
is Markovian and that it is local.
The first lemma follows
from
[
14
, Proposition 11.9(e) and Theorem 11.10]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a symmetric bilinear form from a Hilbert space
H
to
[-\infty,\infty]
. Let

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
be a dense linear subspace of
H
such that
\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric bilinear form
mapping
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then we say
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
is the
domain
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
C_{c}(X)
be the collection of all real-valued continuous functions with compact support on
X
. A
core
of a
symmetric biliear form
\mathcal{E}
is a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})\cap C_{c}(X)
such that
\mathcal{C}
is
dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
under the norm
\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}+\mathcal{E}(\cdot)}
and dense in
C_{c}(X)
under the uniform norm.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is
closed
if for any
(u_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
satisfying

(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m})_{H}+\mathcal{E}[u_{n}-u_{m}]\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
, there exists
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
such that

(u_{n}-u,u_{n}-u)_{H}+\mathcal{E}[u_{n}-u]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

In our setting, we have chosen the Hilbert space
H=N^{1,2}(X)
, and consequent
to Proposition
3.17
(or Theorem
1.1
), we also have that

N^{1,2}(X)=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
. This is because the
comparability of
\mathcal{E}
and the energy form on
N^{1,2}(X)
(Proposition
3.17
) guarantees
that
\mathcal{E}
should always be finite on
N^{1,2}(X)
.

We can choose the core
\mathcal{C}
to be the collection of all Lipschitz functions on
X
.
Since the measure
\mu
on
X
is doubling and supports a
2
-Poincaré inequality, it follows that Lipschitz
functions are dense in
N^{1,2}(X)
, see for instance
[
21
, Section 8.2]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

\mathcal{E}
is a symmetric bilinear form on
N^{1,2}(X)
.

The above lemma follows from
[
14
, Proposition 11.9 and Theorem 11.10]
, where symmetric bilinear forms are called quadratic forms.

Lemma 4.5
.

The form
\mathcal{E}
is a local form on
N^{1,2}(X)
. Moreover, for each
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
we have that

\mathcal{E}[u]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u].

Proof.

We first prove the locality property of
\mathcal{E}
.
Let
u,v\in N^{1,2}(X)

such that the support
K_{u}
of
u
and the support
K_{v}
of
v
are disjoint. Then, as
X
is compact, there is
some
\rho>0
such that
\text{dist}(K_{u},K_{v})\geq 10\rho
.
By the bilinearity and symmetry properties of
\mathcal{E}
, we have
\mathcal{E}[u+v]=\mathcal{E}[u-v]+4\mathcal{E}(u,v)
.
To show that
\mathcal{E}(u,v)=0
(the definition of locality for a symmetric bilinear form), it suffices to show
that
\mathcal{E}[u+v]=\mathcal{E}[u-v]
.
Note that
u+v,u-v
are both zero in
X\setminus(K_{u}\cup K_{v})
. Let
(\widehat{f_{k}})_{k}\subset N^{1,2}(X)
be an
approximating sequence for
u+v
as guaranteed by the
\Gamma
-convergence. Then, thanks to
Theorem
1.2
, we can also assume that each
\widehat{f_{k}}=0
on
X\setminus(K_{u}\cup K_{v})
.
Then for each positive integer
k
, the functions
f_{k}=\widehat{f_{k}}\,\chi_{K_{u}}
and
h_{k}=\widehat{f_{k}}\,\chi_{K_{v}}

both belong to
N^{1,2}(X)
and
\widehat{f_{k}}=f_{k}+h_{k}
.
Indeed, as there is a positive distance between
K_{u}
and
K_{v}
, we can find two compactly supported
Lipschitz functions
\varphi_{u}
and
\varphi_{v}
so that
\varphi_{u}=1
on
K_{u}
,
\varphi_{v}=1
on
K_{v}
, and

f_{k}=\varphi_{u}\widehat{f_{k}}
,
h_{k}=\varphi_{v}\widehat{f_{k}}
. Thus
f_{k},h_{k}\in N^{1,2}(X)
, as seen
from
[
21
, Proof of Proposition 7.1.35]
. Moreover,
f_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
and

h_{k}\to v
in
N^{1,2}(X)
. It follows that
f_{k}-h_{k}\to u-v
in
N^{1,2}(X)
.
It follows that

\mathcal{E}[u-v]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[f_{k}-h_{k}].

On the other hand, when
r_{k}<\rho
, we have that

\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[f_{k}-h_{k}]=\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[f_{k}]+\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[h_{k}]=\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[f_{k}+h_{k}],

and so as
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[\widehat{f_{k}}]=\mathcal{E}[u+v]
,
we have that

\mathcal{E}[u-v]\leq\mathcal{E}[u+v].

Replacing
v
with
-v
in the above argument also gives that
\mathcal{E}[u+v]\leq\mathcal{E}[u-v]
, and so we have that

\mathcal{E}[u+v]=\mathcal{E}[u-v]
. It follows that
\mathcal{E}
is a local form.

Next, for
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
, let
(u_{k})_{k}
be a sequence in
N^{1,2}(X)
such that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)

and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]=\mathcal{E}[u]
; existence of such sequence is guaranteed by the definition
of
\Gamma
-convergence. Then

\mathcal{E}[u]^{1/2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u]^{1/2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u-u_{k}]^{1/2}+\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]^{1/2}\right).

By Theorem
3.13
and by the fact that
u_{k}\to u
in
N^{1,2}(X)
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u-u_{k}]=0
, see for example (
3.19
).
It follows that

\mathcal{E}[u]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u_{k}]=\mathcal{E}[u]
, from which
the desired conclusion now follows.
∎

In
[
27
, page 372]
, it is shown
that the
\Gamma
-limit of bilinear forms on
L^{2}(X)
is closed. In our setting, we consider a

\Gamma
-convergence with the ambient Hilbert space itself being
N^{1,2}(X)
. Therefore
for completeness of the exposition, we include our proof here.

Lemma 4.6
.

The symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{E}
is closed.

Proof.

We first show that if
(f_{n})_{n}
is a sequence in
N^{1,2}(X)
and
f\in L^{2}(X)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}(X)
and

\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}]\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
, then by modifying
f
on a set of
\mu
-measure zero on
X
if necessary, we have that

f\in N^{1,2}(X)
and
\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{N^{1,2}(X)}\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
. Indeed, as
\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}]\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
,
it follows from Theorem
3.13
(or more specifically, from its limit version given in (2) of Theorem
1.1
),
we have that
\int_{X}g_{f_{n}-f_{m}}^{2}\,d\mu\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
. It follows that
(f_{n})_{n}
is Cauchy in the Banach space
N^{1,2}(X)
,
and so such modification of
f
exists.

Now let
(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
be a sequence such that
\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{N^{1,2}(X)}+\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}]\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
.
Then necessarily
(f_{n})_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
N^{1,2}(X)
, and as
N^{1,2}(X)
is a Banach space (see
[
28
]
),
there exists
f\in N^{1,2}(X)=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\|f_{n}-f\|_{N^{1,2}(X)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. By
Lemma
4.5
, we know that
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})
.
To complete the proof of closedness of
\mathcal{E}
we now show that
\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

By
[
14
, Proposition 6.8]
, we know that the
\Gamma
-limit is lower semicontinuous.
Therefore, for each fixed
n
, using the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f]\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}].

Hence, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle 0\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f]\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}]=\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}[f_{n}-f_{m}]=0.

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

The bilinear form
\mathcal{E}
is Markovian.

Proof.

To show that the form is Markovian, it suffices to show that if
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
, setting
v=\max\{0,\min\{1,u\}\}
,
we have
\mathcal{E}[v]\leq\mathcal{E}[u]
. Since
(X,d,\mu)
is doubling and supports a
2
-Poincaré inequality, we know that
Lipschitz functions form a dense subclass of
N^{1,2}(X)
; hence it suffices to prove this property for the case that

u
is Lipschitz continuous.

So let
u\in N^{1,2}(X)
be Lipschitz on
X
, and set
E_{-}=\{u<0\}
,
E_{+}=\{u>1\}
, and
E_{0}=\{0\leq u\leq 1
.
Then as
X
is compact, and as
\overline{E_{-}}
and
\overline{E_{+}}
are disjoint, we have that

\rho:=\text{dist}(\overline{E_{-}},\ \overline{E_{+}})>0
. So if
0<r<\rho/16
, then we have that for
\bar{x}\in X_{r}

and
\bar{y}\in X_{r}
with
\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}
,
the set
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
cannot intersect both
\overline{E_{+}}
and
\overline{E_{-}}
.

Now let
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in X_{r}
with
\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
. If
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)\subset\overline{E_{-}}

or
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)\subset\overline{E_{+}}
or
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)\subset E_{0}
, then
we have
|v_{r}(\bar{x})-v_{r}(\bar{y})|\leq|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|
. If either

B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
intersects both
E_{-}
and
E_{0}
(the case that

B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
intersects both
E_{+}
and
E_{0}
will be handled in s very similar manner).
In this case, we have

|v_{r}(\bar{x})-v_{r}(\bar{y})|\leq|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|+|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|+|u_{r}(\bar{y})-v_{r}(\bar{y})|.

For such
\bar{x}
, we have to estimate
|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|
and
|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|
. Note that

\displaystyle|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\fint_{B(\bar{x},r/4)}(u-v)\,d\mu\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B(\bar{x},r/4))}\int_{B(\bar{x},r/4)\cap E_{-}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu

\displaystyle|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\fint_{B(\bar{y},r/4)}(u-v)\,d\mu\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu(B(\bar{y},r/4))}\int_{B(\bar{y},r/4)\cap E_{-}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu.

Setting
L
to be a Lipschitz constant of
u
and that
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
intersects

E_{-}
and
E_{0}
, and
d(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq 4r
, necessarily
|u|\leq 5Lr
on
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
.
Hence

|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}\leq 25L^{2}r^{2}\ \text{ and }\ |v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}\leq 25L^{2}r^{2}.

Similar estimates hold if
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
intersects both
E_{+}
and
E_{0}
, now with
|u|^{2}
replaced by

|u-1|^{2}
. Note also that when
B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)
intersects both
E_{-}
and
E_{0}
and

\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
, necessarily we must have

B(\bar{x},r/4)\cup B(\bar{y},r/4)\subset\bigcup_{z\in\partial E_{-}}B(z,6r)
because
X
is a geodesic space and
u
is
continuous. We set

(\partial E_{-})_{r}=\bigcup_{z\in\partial E_{-}}B(z,6r),\qquad\text{ and }\qquad(\partial E_{+})_{r}=\bigcup_{z\in\partial E_{+}}B(z,6r),

and let

X_{r}[v]:=\{\bar{x}\in X_{r}\,:\,B(\bar{x},r/4)\subset(\partial E_{-})_{r}\cup(\partial E_{+})_{r}\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[v]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-v_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{\left(|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|+|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|+|u_{r}(\bar{y})-v_{r}(\bar{y})|\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}\setminus X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[v]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})+\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|\cdot|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|\cdot|u_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|\cdot|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x}).

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{r}[v]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]+\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})+\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}\cdot\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}\cdot\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\left[\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})\right)\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})\right)\right]^{1/2}.

It now suffices to show that both the term
\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

and the term
\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})
tend to
0
as
r\to 0
.
From the discussion prior to the above estimates, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq N\,\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{x})-u_{r}(\bar{x})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq N\,25L^{2}\,\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\mu_{r}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq C\,25L^{2}\,\mu((\partial E_{-})_{r}\cup(\partial E_{+})_{r}),

where we have used the fact that for each
\bar{x}\in X_{r}
there are at most
N
number of vertices
\bar{y}\in X_{r}
such that

\bar{x}\sim\bar{y}
, with
N
depending only on the doubling constant associated with
\mu
; and we also used
fact that there is at most
bounded overlap property of the balls
B(\bar{y},r)
,
\bar{y}\in X_{r}
, to obtain the last inequality.
A similar argument also gives

\sum_{\bar{x}\in X_{r}[v]}\sum_{\bar{y}\sim\bar{x}}\frac{|v_{r}(\bar{y})-u_{r}(\bar{y})|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\mu_{r}(\bar{x})\leq C\,25L^{2}\,\mu((\partial E_{-})_{r}\cup(\partial E_{+})_{r}).

It follows that

\mathcal{E}_{r}[v]\leq\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]+10C\,L\,\mathcal{E}_{r}[u]^{1/2}\mu((\partial E_{-})_{r}\cup(\partial E_{+})_{r})^{1/2}+25\,C\,L^{2}\,\mu((\partial E_{-})_{r}\cup(\partial E_{+})_{r}).

So if

\mu(\partial E_{-})+\mu(\partial E_{+})=0
, then we have that

\mathcal{E}[v]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[v]\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{r_{k}}[u]=\mathcal{E}[u].

The last equality follows from Lemma
4.5
above.
The assumption of
\mu(\partial E_{-})+\mu(\partial E_{+})=0
is not a serious obstacle, as we know that for
almost every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have that
\mu(\{u=t\})=0
, and so we can approximate
0
from below by such
t_{-}<0
,
and approximate
1
from above by such
t_{+}>1
, and consider
v_{t_{-},t_{+}}=\max\{t_{-},\min\{t_{+},u\}\}

and obtain the Markov property for these, and then let
t_{-}\to 0
,
t_{+}\to 1
and note that

v_{t_{-},t_{+}}\to v
in
N^{1,2}(X)
; we know that
\mathcal{E}
is bounded
(from Theorem
3.13
above) bilinear form on
N^{1,2}(X)
.
The fact that
\mathcal{E}_{r}[v_{t_{-},t_{+}}]
converges to
\mathcal{E}_{r}[v]
can be obtained from
the closability of
\mathcal{E}
(lemma
4.6
above) and Theorem
3.13
, together with the fact that

v_{t_{-},t_{+}}\to v
in
N^{1,2}(X)
as
t_{-}\to 0
and
t_{+}\to 1
.
∎

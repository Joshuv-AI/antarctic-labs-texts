Introduction

Let

\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(g)=\int_{M^{n}}R_{g}dM_{g},

where

It is well-known that the formal

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}^{*}_{g}(f):=-(\Delta_{g}f)g+Hess_{g}f-fRic_{g},

An Euler-Lagrange equation is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}^{*}_{g}(f)=Ric_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{n}g.

A

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be an
n
-dimensional compact, orientable Riemannian manifold.
In what follows, we adopt, without further comment, the moving frame notation.
For any
p\in M^{n}
, we choose
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
as a local orthonormal frame field at
p
,
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
as its dual coframe field,
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
.
Here and hereafter the Einstein convention of summing over the repeated indices will be adopted.

The
decomposition of the Riemannian curvature tensor into irreducible components yields

(2.1)

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{ijkl}+\frac{1}{n-2}(R_{ik}\delta_{jl}-R_{il}\delta_{jk}+R_{jl}\delta_{ik}-R_{jk}\delta_{il})

\displaystyle-\frac{R}{(n-1)(n-2)}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}),

where
R_{ijkl}
are the components of the Riemannian curvature tensor,
W_{ijkl}
are the components of the Weyl tensor,
R_{ij}:=\sum_{kl}R_{ikjl}g_{kl}
are components of Ricci curvature tensor and
R:=\sum_{ij}R_{ij}g_{ij}
is the scalar curvature of
M^{n}.

Let
\phi=\sum_{i,j}\phi_{ij}\omega_{i}\otimes\omega_{j}
be a symmetric (0,2)-type tensor defined on
M^{n}.
By letting
\phi_{ij,k}:=\nabla_{k}\phi_{ij},

\phi_{ij,kl}:=\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}\phi_{ij},
where
\nabla
is the operator of covariant differentiation on
M^{n},

we have the following Ricci identities

(2.2)

\displaystyle\phi_{ij,kl}-\phi_{ij,lk}=\phi_{mj}R_{mikl}+\phi_{im}R_{mjkl}.

The norm of a
(0,4)
-type tensor
T
is defined as

|T|^{2}=|T_{ijkl}|^{2}=T_{ijkl}T_{ijkl}.

By the second Bianchi identity

R_{imkl,j}+R_{imlj,k}+R_{imjk,l}=0,

we have

(2.3)

R_{ij,k}-R_{ik,j}=R_{likj,l}

and

(2.4)

R_{ik,i}=\frac{1}{2}R_{,k}.

A Cotton tensor
C_{ijk}
is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle C_{ijk}=R_{jk,i}-R_{ik,j}-\frac{1}{2(n-1)}(R_{,i}\delta_{jk}-R_{,j}\delta_{ik}).

From Definition
1
we know that a
CPE
metric
(M^{n},g,\tilde{f})
satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle-(\Delta\tilde{f})\delta_{ij}+\tilde{f}_{,ij}-\tilde{f}R_{ij}=Ric-\frac{R}{n}\delta_{ij},

where
R
is a constant.
Replacing
\tilde{f}
by
f-1
, we can rewrite (
2.6
) as

(2.7)

\displaystyle-(\Delta f)\delta_{ij}+f_{,ij}-fR_{ij}=-\frac{R}{n}\delta_{ij},

where
-\frac{R}{n}
is a constant. Obviously, (
2.7
) is a V-static equation. Then we can unify the equation of
CPE
metric, Miao-Tam critical metric and vacuum static space into the following form:

(2.8)

\displaystyle-(\Delta f)\delta_{ij}+f_{,ij}-fR_{ij}=\kappa\delta_{ij},

where
\kappa
is a constant. By tracing the above formula, we can get

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Delta f=\frac{fR+n\kappa}{1-n}.

Substituting (
2.9
) into (
2.8
), we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle fR_{ij}-f_{,ij}-\frac{fR}{n-1}\delta_{ij}=\frac{\kappa}{n-1}\delta_{ij}.

Now we present four lemmas as the preparation for proving our main theorems.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
f
be a smooth solution to (
2.8
). Then
g
has constant scalar curvature.

Proof.

As the proof of Theorem 7 in Miao-Tam
[
30
]
, differenting (
2.8
), we get

(2.11)

\displaystyle-f_{,kkj}\delta_{ij}+f_{,ijj}-f_{,j}R_{ij}-fR_{ij,j}=0.

From Ricci identity and (
2.4
), we can derive that scalar curvature
R
is constant.
Hence we complete the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
f
be a smooth solution to (
2.8
). Then

(2.12)

\displaystyle fC_{kij}=f_{,h}R_{hjik}+\frac{R}{n-1}(f_{,k}\delta_{ij}-f_{,i}\delta_{kj})-(f_{,k}R_{ij}-f_{,i}R_{kj}).

Remark 2.3
.

As in
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
, we can derive

(2.13)

\displaystyle fC_{kij}=T_{kij}+W_{kijs}f_{,s}

from (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.2
, where
T_{kij}
is defined as

\displaystyle T_{kij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n-2}(R_{jk}f_{,i}-R_{ij}f_{,k})-\frac{R}{n-2}(\delta_{jk}f_{,i}-\delta_{ij}f_{,k})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n-2}(\delta_{jk}R_{is}f_{,s}-\delta_{ij}R_{ks}f_{,s}).

Proof.

Differenting (
2.10
), we get

(2.14)

\displaystyle fR_{ij,k}=f_{,ijk}+\frac{Rf_{,k}}{n-1}\delta_{ij}-f_{,k}R_{ij}.

Combining this equation with Ricci identity, we have

\displaystyle fC_{kij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(R_{ij,k}-R_{kj,i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{,ijk}-f_{,kji}+\frac{R}{n-1}(f_{,k}\delta_{ij}-f_{,i}\delta_{kj})-(f_{,k}R_{ij}-f_{,i}R_{kj})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{,h}R_{hjik}+\frac{R}{n-1}(f_{,k}\delta_{ij}-f_{,i}\delta_{kj})-(f_{,k}R_{ij}-f_{,i}R_{kj}).

Hence we complete the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold,
f
be a smooth solution to (
2.8
). Then

(2.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{ij}(fC_{kij})_{,k}-R_{ij}f_{,k}C_{kij}

\displaystyle R_{ij}\left[f_{,h}R_{hjik}+\frac{R}{n-1}(f_{,k}\delta_{ij}-f_{,i}\delta_{kj})-(f_{,k}R_{ij}-f_{,i}R_{kj})\right]_{,k}

\displaystyle-R_{ij}f_{,k}C_{kij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{ij}f_{,hk}R_{hjik}+R_{ij}f_{,h}R_{hjik,k}+\frac{R^{2}}{n-1}\Delta f-\frac{R}{n-1}f_{,ij}R_{ij}

From (

\displaystyle fR_{ij}C_{kij,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{ij}(fR_{hk}-\frac{fR}{n-1}\delta_{hk}-\frac{\kappa}{n-1}\delta_{hk})R_{hjik}+R_{ij}f_{,h}C_{jhi}

\displaystyle+\frac{R^{2}}{n-1}\frac{fR+n\kappa}{1-n}-\frac{R}{n-1}(fR_{ij}-\frac{fR}{n-1}\delta_{ij}-\frac{\kappa}{n-1}\delta_{ij})R_{ij}

\displaystyle-\frac{fR+n\kappa}{1-n}|Ric|^{2}-R_{ij}f_{,k}R_{ij,k}+R_{ij}(fR_{ik}-\frac{fR}{n-1}\delta_{ik}

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{n-1}\delta_{ik})R_{kj}-R_{ij}f_{,k}C_{kij}.

That is

\displaystyle fR_{ij}C_{kij,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fR_{ij}R_{jk}R_{ki}-fR_{ij}R_{hk}R_{ikjh}-\frac{1}{2}<\nabla f,\nabla|Ric|^{2}>

\displaystyle-2R_{ij}f_{,k}C_{kij}+\frac{n\kappa}{n-1}|Ric|^{2}-\frac{R^{2}\kappa}{n-1}.

Thus we complete the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature. Then

\displaystyle{6{R_{ij}}{R_{hj}}{R_{hi}}-5R{{\left|{Ric}\right|}^{2}}+{R^{3}}}\geq 0.

Proof.

For any fixed point
p\in M
, we choose an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
in
T_{p}M
such that

R_{ij}=\rho_{i}\delta_{ij},\ \ \ R=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
0\leq\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{2}\leq\rho_{3}
.

Similar to the proof of Lemma 2.2 in
[
21
]
, we can compute that

\displaystyle{6{R_{ij}}{R_{hj}}{R_{hi}}-5R{{\left|{Ric}\right|}^{2}}+{R^{3}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i}^{3}-5\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\rho_{j}^{2}+(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i})^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 9\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i}^{3}-6\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\rho_{j}^{2}+(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{i})^{3}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(3\rho_{i}^{3}-\rho_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\rho_{j}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}\rho_{i}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{j})(\rho_{i}-\rho_{k})-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\rho_{i}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq k}\rho_{i}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{j})(\rho_{i}-\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k\neq}\rho_{i}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{j})(\rho_{i}-\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2[\rho_{1}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{3})+\rho_{2}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3})

\displaystyle+\rho_{3}(\rho_{3}-\rho_{1})(\rho_{3}-\rho_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2[\rho_{1}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{3})

\displaystyle+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3})^{2}(\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}-\rho_{1})]\geq 0.

Thus we complete the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3.
Proof of main theorems

(M^{n},g)(n\geq 3)
be an
n
-dimensional compact, oriented, connected Riemannian manifold,
f
be a smooth solution to (
2.8
). From a direct computation and the definition of

\displaystyle div\left({f^{2}\nabla{{\left|{Ric}\right|}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle 2f<\nabla f,\nabla|Ric|^{2}>+f^{2}\Delta|Ric|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f<\nabla f,\nabla|Ric|^{2}>+2f^{2}|\nabla Ric|^{2}+2f^{2}R_{ij}R_{ij,kk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f<\nabla f,\nabla|Ric|^{2}>+2f^{2}|\nabla Ric|^{2}+2f^{2}R_{ij}(C_{kij}+R_{jk,i})_{,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f<\nabla f,\nabla|Ric|^{2}>+2f^{2}|\nabla Ric|^{2}+2f^{2}R_{ij}C_{kij,k}

\displaystyle+2f^{2}R_{ij}R_{jk,ik}.

Combining equation above with Lemma
2.4
and Ricci identity, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle div\left({f^{2}\nabla{{\left|{Ric}\right|}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4f^{2}R_{ij}R_{jk}R_{ki}-4f^{2}R_{ij}R_{hk}R_{ikjh}-8fR_{ij}f_{,k}C_{kij}

\displaystyle+\frac{4n\kappa f}{n-1}|\mathring{Ric}|^{2}-4f^{2}R_{ij}C_{kij,k}+2f^{2}|\nabla Ric|^{2}

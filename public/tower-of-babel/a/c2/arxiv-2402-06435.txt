Abstract
. In this paper we obtain the existence of a weak global attractor for the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, that is, a weakly compact set with an invariance property, that uniformly attracts solutions, with respect to the weak topology, for initial data in bounded sets. To that end, we define this weak global attractor in terms of limits of solutions of the globally modified Navier-Stokes equations in the weak topology. We use the theory of semilinear parabolic equations and ϵ \epsilon -regularity to obtain the local well posedness for the globally modified Navier-Stokes equations and the existence of a global attractor and its regularity.

1.
Introduction

The understanding of the asymptotic behavior of solutions for the
three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations is still a big challenge. In
particular, the existence of the global attractor with respect to the strong
topology of the phase space is up to now an open problem. The main
difficulties in proving such results come from the facts that it is not
known whether the weak solutions are continuous and also if uniqueness of
the Cauchy problem is true. As proved in
[
4
]
as a conditional
result, if all the weak solution were continuous, then they would define a
multivalued semiflow possessing a global attractor with respect to the
strong topology (an extension of this to the stochastic framework is
[
18
]
). Later on, in
[
14
]
this result was extended by showing
that if strong solutions were globally defined, then at least one weak
solution would be continuous, ensuring then the existence of a multivalued
semiflow having a global attractor, which is again a conditional theorem.

Another approach that has been helpful in order to handle this problem is to
use the theory of trajectory attractors (see
[
7
,
8
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
for
instance, also
[
9
]
for the nonautonomous case, and
[
10
]
for the stochastic equation). The idea behind these
types of attractors is to avoid the problem of the lack of continuity by
using a weaker topology, namely, the topology of square integrable functions
on finite intervals of time. The drawback of this kind of attractor is that
the connection with the original physical phase space is lost.

The existence of the global attractor has been obtained when we
consider the weak topology of the phase space. In view of the lack of
uniqueness, the proper way to do that is to define a multivalued semiflow
and prove at least the existence and negative invariance of the attractor.
This question is fraught with important difficulties. If we use the
Leray-Hopf solutions satisfying the energy inequality almost everywhere for
positive moments of time, but not for the initial one, then a multivalued
semiflow is defined but we cannot obtain a bounded absorbing set. Therefore,
we are not able to prove the existence of a global attractor in this
situation. On the other hand, if we add the Leray-Hopf solutions satisfying
the energy inequality at the initial moment of time, then a bounded
absorbing set exists, but the translation of solutions fails to be a
solution, so we cannot define a multivalued semiflow. A detailed discussion
of these difficulties is given in
[
3
]
.

The first result in this direction was published in
[
12
]
(see

[
11
]
as well). The so-called universal attractor,
consisting of the bounded complete weak solutions, was defined. This set was
proved to attract uniformly in the weak topology of the phase space the
Leray-Hopf solutions starting at a bounded set and which satisfy the energy
inequality at the initial moment of time. In such a situation, as remarked
before, a semiflow cannot be defined and the attractor was shown to be
positively invariant but not negatively invariant. This problem was overcome
in
[
14
]
(see also
[
9
]
) by restricting the phase
space to a suitable ball, proving the existence of the weak global attractor
in both the autonomous and nonautonomous cases. However, the question about
the convergence of the solutions starting outside this ball to the global
attractor remained open.

In this paper, in order to define a weak global attractor in the whole phase
space, we follow the approach of
[
6
]
by taking suitable
approximations. In this way, we are able to define a global attractor which
uniformly attracts, with respect to the weak topology, the solutions
starting at any bounded set. Moreover, the solutions define a multivalued
semiflow in the attractor (although not in the whole space) and this set is
negatively and positively invariant with respect to it.

In order to be more specific about the results proved here we consider the
Navier-Stokes equations (NSE)

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p+f,&t>0,\ x\in\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\ u=0,&t>0,\ x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&t>0,\ x\in\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary

\partial\Omega

is the unknown velocity field of the
fluid,
\nu>0
is a given constant kinematic viscosity,
p
is the unknown
pressure,
u_{0}
is a initial velocity field and
f
is a constant external
force taken in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
.

We also consider the globally modified Navier-Stokes equations (GMNSE)

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+F_{N}(u)(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p+f,&t>0,\ x\in\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\ u=0,&t>0,\ x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&t>0,\ x\in\partial\Omega\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
N
is a fixed positive number and
F_{N}(u)=f_{N}(\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})
, where
f_{N}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

(1.3)

which were
introduced in
[
5
]
with the term
f_{N}(\|u\|_{V})
instead of
f_{N}(\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)})
. It follows in a similar way as in
[
5
,
19
]
, for the case

, that
problem (
1.2
) generates a family of continuous semigroups

in
H
given by
S_{N}(t)u_{0}=u_{N}(t,u_{0})
,
where
u_{N}(\cdot,u_{0})
is the unique classical solution to (
1.2
). We will give a different approach to obtain the
semigroup

and the existence of its global
attractors, which will provide additional regularity of solutions and of the
global attractors.

Following
[
17

21

22
]

\mathcal{V}=\{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{3}\colon\mathrm{div}\ u=0\},

with inner product

where, for

,

(u,v)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega}u_{j}v_{j}.

and

with scalar product
((\cdot,\cdot))
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
where, for

((u,v))=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}.

Consider the Leray projection
P\colon L^{2}\to H
and

the Stokes operator (we have

]

is closed and densely

Since

(1.4)

H_{-\alpha}=(H_{\alpha})^{\ast}\quad\hbox{ for }\alpha\in[0,1].

In this way, we have found

, with

,

(1.5)

satisfying

With this setting, we prove the following result concerning the local well

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
Pf\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}}

Let

We say that

With this we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
Pf\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The set

is a weak global attractor for the

, which means that:

(a)

(b)

Local existence and regularity results for abstract semilinear

\displaystyle+h)-g(s)-g(t+h)+g(t)\|_{X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|g(s+h)-g(s)\|_{X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}}+\|g(t+h)-g(t)\|_{X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}}\leqslant K(s)h^{\delta}+K(t)h^{\delta},

and

\displaystyle\|g(s+h)-g(s)-g(t+h

Choosing

Thus

\displaystyle\|H

\displaystyle\leqslant M\int_{0}^{h}K(s)(t+h-s)^{-\mu+\delta-1}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt+Mh^{\delta(1-\omega)}\int_{0}^{t}(K(s)+K(t))^{1-\omega}(K(s+h)+K(s))^{\omega}(t-s)^{-\mu+\delta\omega-1}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant MCt^{-\mu+\delta-1}h^{\varphi}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt+Mh^{\delta(1-\omega)}\int_{0}^{t}(K(s)+K(t))^{1-\omega}(K(s+h)+K(s))^{\omega}(t-s)^{-\mu+\delta\omega-1}ds.

Since

for

fixed, and the map

∎

Lastly, we will need the following singular version of the Grönwall

Lemma 2.6

(Singular Grönwall Inequality)
.

Let

,

With these lemmas, we can present the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

\epsilon

Very weak formulation of the globally modified Navier-Stokes
equation

In this section we show that the nonlinearities of the GMNSE are
\frac{3}{8}
-regular maps, obtain the very weak formulation of the GMNSE, prove Theorem
1.1
, and guarantee the existence of the global attractor in
H
for
the GMNSE.

3.1.
Nonlinearity of the GMNSE

We begin with some results involving the map
f_{N}
defined in (
1.3
),
to study the nonlinear term
F_{N}(u)(u\cdot\nabla u)
of the GMNSE. We note
that, from
[
5
, Lemma 4]
, for every
N>0
and
s,t\geqslant 0
with
s+t>0
we have

(3.1)

|f_{N}(s)-f_{N}(t)|\leqslant\frac{1}{\max\{s,t\}}|s-t|.

Writing, for
r\geqslant 1
,
L^{r}=L^{r}(\Omega)^{3}
, to simplify the notation, we point out that for each
N>0
and
u\in L^{4}
we have

(3.2)

F_{N}(u)\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant N,

and for any
N>0
and
u,v\in L^{4}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}>0
we have

|F_{N}(u)-F_{N}(v)|\leqslant\frac{1}{\max\{\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)},\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\}}\|u-v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)},

just using (
3.1
) and noting that
|\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}-\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}|\leqslant\|u-v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}
.

Before continuing, we note that the tensor product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
will be of great importance and we recall it here. The
tensor product
between
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}),\ v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the
3\times 3
real matrix given by

u\otimes v=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}v_{1}&u_{1}v_{2}&u_{1}v_{3}\\
u_{2}v_{1}&u_{2}v_{2}&u_{2}v_{3}\\
u_{3}v_{1}&u_{3}v_{2}&u_{3}v_{3}\end{pmatrix}=(u_{i}v_{j})_{i,j=1}^{3}.

Clearly, with the following properties hold:

•

(u+v)\otimes w=u\otimes w+v\otimes w;

•

|u\otimes v|_{M_{3}({\mathbb{R}})}\leqslant|u|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\,|v|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}};

•

u\otimes v=(v\otimes u)^{t}
, where
A^{t}
denotes the transposed
matrix of
A\in M_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
u,v\in L^{4}
, we have

\|F_{N}(u)u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant 3N\|u-v\|_{L^{4}},

and

\|F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant N\|u\|_{L^{4}}.

Proof.

Note that

\|F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant F_{N}(u)\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant N\|u\|_{L^{4}},

where in the last inequality we used (
3.2
).

Now we deal with the first estimate. Firstly, we treat the case where
\|u\|_{L^{4}},\|v\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant N
. In this case, we have
F_{N}(u)=F_{N}(v)=1
and

\displaystyle\|F_{N}(u)

\displaystyle u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}=\|u\otimes u-v\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|u\otimes(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(u-v)\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}+\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}\|v\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant(\|u\|_{L^{4}}+\|v\|_{L^{4}})\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant 2N\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}.

Now we assume that
\|v\|_{L^{4}},\|u\|_{L^{4}}\geqslant N
. We can
assume, without loss of generality, that
\|v\|_{L^{4}}\geqslant\|u\|_{L^{4}}
. If this case, we have

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle F_{N}(u)u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}=N\Big\|\frac{u\otimes u}{\|u\|_{L^{4}}}-\frac{v\otimes v}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant N\frac{\|u\otimes(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}}{\|u\|_{L^{4}}}+N\|u\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}\Big|\frac{1}{\|u\|_{L^{4}}}-\frac{1}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}\Big|+N\frac{\|u\otimes(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant N\frac{\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}}{\|u\|_{L^{4}}}+N\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|v\|_{L^{4}}\frac{|\|u\|_{L^{4}}-\|v\|_{L^{4}}|}{\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|v\|_{L^{4}}}+N\frac{\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 3N\|u-v\|_{L^{4}}.

For the last case, we can assume, without loss of generality, that

\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant N\leqslant\|v\|_{L^{4}}
. Hence

\displaystyle F_{N}

\displaystyle(u)u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v=\frac{1}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}\big(\|v\|_{L^{4}}u\otimes u-Nv\otimes v\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|v\|_{L^{4}}}\big(\|v\|_{L^{4}}u\otimes(u-v)+(\|v\|_{L^{4}}-N)u\otimes v+N(u-v)\otimes v\big).

Estimating the
L^{2}
norm and using the fact that
\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant N
and that

\|v\|_{L^{4}}-N\leqslant\|v\|_{L^{4}}-\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leqslant\|u-v\|_{L^{4}},

we obtain

\|F_{N}(u)u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant 3N\|u-v\|_{L^{4}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

We recall the following Sobolev type embeddings for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Given
s>0
we consider
m
the smallest integer greater than or equal to
s
. Then

[\,L^{p}(\Omega)^{3},W^{m,p}(\Omega)^{3}\,]_{s/m}=H^{s,p}(\Omega)^{3}.

Additionally, for
1\leqslant p\leqslant q=3p/(3-sp)
, we have

(3.3)

H^{s,p}(\Omega)^{3}\subset L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}.

Hence, we arrive at the following result without having to know explicitly
H_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
q=\frac{6}{3-4\alpha}
, we have
H_{\alpha}\subset H^{2\alpha,2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
H_{\alpha}\subset L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}
.

Proof.

By construction
D(A)\subset W^{2,2}(\Omega)^{3}
and since
A
is
sectorial, the inclusion holds with equivalent norms. Interpolating these
spaces we arrive at
H_{\alpha}\subset H^{2\alpha,2}(\Omega)^{3}
and apply (
3.3
).
∎

Now we define the spaces
X^{1}
and
X^{0}
that we use for the rest of the
paper. Set

(3.4)

X^{1}=H\quad\hbox{ and }\quad X^{0}=H_{-1}.

Hence, choosing

(3.5)

\epsilon=\frac{3}{8}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\gamma(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2},

we have

X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}=X^{\frac{1}{2}}=H_{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad X^{1+\epsilon}=X^{\frac{11}{8}}=H_{\frac{3}{8}}.

Note that from Proposition
3.2
we obtain

X^{1+\epsilon}=H_{\frac{3}{8}}\subset L^{4}.

Also, we point out that, from (
1.4
),
X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}=H_{-\frac{1}{2}}=(H_{\frac{1}{2}})^{\ast}=V^{\ast}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For each
N>0
, the map
G_{N}\colon X^{1+\epsilon}\to X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}
given by

(3.6)

G_{N}(u)\phi=\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi\quad\hbox{ for
}u\in X^{1+\epsilon}\hbox{ and }\phi\in V,

is a well-defined
\frac{3}{8}
-regular map relative to the pair
(X^{1},X^{0})
,
and we can choose any
\rho\in(1,\frac{4}{3}]
.

Proof.

Since
X^{1+\epsilon}\subset L^{4}
, for
u\in X^{1+\epsilon}
and
\phi\in V
, using Lemma
3.1

we have

\displaystyle|G_{N}(u)\phi|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi\Big|\leqslant\|F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi\|_{V}

\displaystyle\leqslant N\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|\phi\|_{V}\leqslant Nk\|u\|_{H_{\frac{3}{8}}}\|\phi\|_{V},

where

is the embedding constant of

into

. Then, we obtain

|G_{N}(u)\phi|\leqslant Nk\|u\|_{X^{1+\epsilon}}\|\phi\|_{V},

and thus

\|G_{N}(u)\|_{X^{\gamma(\epsilon)}}\leqslant Nk\|u\|_{X^{1+\epsilon}}.

Again, from Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle|G_{N}(u)\phi-G_{N}(v)\phi|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Omega}(F_{N}(u)u\otimes u-F_{N}(v)v\otimes v)\cdot\nabla\phi\Big|

3.2.
The very weak formulation of the GMNSE

Let us reformulate the GMNSE in a
very weak context
. In the
framework of scales of Banach spaces, that consists basically in a
translation to the extrapolated scale.

Projecting the first equation of (
1.2
) into
H
,
formally multiplying it by a smooth function
\phi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
with compact support in
\Omega
and
\mathrm{div}\phi=0
, and
integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}Au\cdot\phi-\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\phi+\int_{\Omega}f_{\sigma}\cdot\phi,

where
f_{\sigma}=Pf
.
We have

\int_{\Omega}Au\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\Delta\phi=\int_{\Omega}u\cdot A\phi.

Now we relate the tensor product
u\otimes u
with
(u\cdot\nabla)u
. To
that end, we define

\mathrm{div}(u\,u)=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}(u_{1}u)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(u_{2}u)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}(u_{3}u)=(\mathrm{div}(u_{1}u),\mathrm{div}(u_{2}u),\mathrm{div}(u_{3}u)),

and note that, if
\mathrm{div}\,u=0
, we have

(3.7)

(u\cdot\nabla)u=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}(u_{1}u)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}(u_{2}u)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}(u_{3}u)-\underbrace{\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}u_{1}+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}u_{2}+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}u_{3}\right)u}_{=0}=\mathrm{div}(u\,u).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u,\phi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be smooth
functions with compact support in
\Omega
and
\mathrm{div}\ \phi=\mathrm{div}\ u=0
. Then

\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\phi=-\int_{\Omega}u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi,

and the same holds for
u\in V
. In the last term, we mean the scalar product for matrices.

Proof.

By (
3.7
) and the Divergence Theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}(u\,u)\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}(\mathrm{div}(u_{1}u),\mathrm{div}(u_{2}u),\mathrm{div}(u_{3}u))\cdot(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\big(u_{1}u\cdot\nabla\phi_{1}+u_{2}u\cdot\nabla\phi_{2}+(u_{3}u)\cdot\nabla\phi_{3}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\partial\Omega}\underbrace{\big(\phi_{1}(u_{1}u)_{\partial\Omega}+\phi_{2}(u_{2}u)_{\partial\Omega}+\phi_{3}(u_{3}u)_{\partial\Omega}\big)}_{=0}d\sigma

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}[u_{1}u\ \ u_{2}u\ \ u_{3}u]\cdot[\nabla\phi_{1}\ \ \nabla\phi_{2}\ \ \nabla\phi_{3}]

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}u_{1}&u_{1}u_{2}&u_{1}u_{3}\\
u_{2}u_{1}&u_{2}u_{2}&u_{2}u_{3}\\
u_{3}u_{1}&u_{3}u_{2}&u_{3}u_{3}\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{3}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{3}}{\partial x_{2}}\\
\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{3}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{2}}{\partial x_{3}}&\frac{\partial\phi_{3}}{\partial x_{3}}\end{bmatrix}=-\int_{\Omega}u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi,

where, for each
i=1,2,3
, the term
(u_{i}u)_{\partial\Omega}
is the
outward normal component of
u_{i}u
in
\partial\Omega
. The last claim
follows from a simple density argument.
∎

From this lemma, formally we have

-\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi,

and, in this way, we obtain the
very weak formulation
of the
globally modified Navier-Stokes equation

(3.8)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}u\cdot A\phi+\int_{\Omega}F_{N}(u)u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi+\int_{\Omega}f_{\sigma}\cdot\phi,\quad t>0.

Therefore, (
1.2
) can be written in
H_{-1}
as

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}=A_{-1}u+G_{N}(u)+f_{\sigma,-1},\quad t>0\\
&u(0)=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
A_{-1}
is defined in (
1.5
),
G_{N}
is defined in (
3.6
) and
f_{\sigma,-1}\in H_{-1}
is the functional

H_{1}\ni\phi\mapsto f_{\sigma,-1}\phi=\int_{\Omega}f_{\sigma}\cdot\phi.

Once we arrive at this point, the local well posedness of (
1.2
) in
H
follows from the fact that
G_{N}
is a
\frac{3}{8}
-regular map relative to the pair
(X^{1},X^{0})=(H,H_{-1})
, given that
f_{\sigma,-1}
is a fixed vector in
H_{-1}
.

Observe that, for each
u_{0}\in H
the
\frac{3}{8}
-regular mild solution
u(\cdot,u_{0})
obtained in Theorem
2.1
satisfies the variation of
constants formula associated to (
3.9
), that is

u(t,u_{0})=e^{At}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}G_{N}(u(s,u_{0}))ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-s)}f_{\sigma,-1}ds.

We make the following assumption

(3.10)

f_{\sigma,-1}\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}},

and note that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|u(t,u_{0})

\displaystyle\|_{H_{\frac{3}{8}}}\leqslant\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H,H_{\frac{3}{8}})}\|u_{0}\|_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{A(t-s)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H_{-\frac{1}{2}},H_{\frac{3}{8}})}\|G_{N}(u(s,u_{0})\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{A(t-s)}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H_{-\frac{1}{2}},H_{\frac{3}{8}})}\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}ds,

\displaystyle\leqslant Mt^{-\frac{3}{8}}\|u_{0}\|_{H}+8Mt^{\frac{1}{8}}\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}+MNk\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{7}{8}}\|u(s,u_{0})\|_{H_{\frac{3}{8}}}ds.

Hence, it follows by the singular Grönwall inequality (Lemma
2.6
) that
u(\cdot,u_{0})
does not blow up in finite time in
H_{\frac{3}{8}}
and, in particular, it must exist for all time
t\geqslant 0
.

Therefore, for each
N>0
, provided (
3.10
) holds, problem (
3.9
) defines a semigroup
S_{N}=\{S_{N}(t)\colon t\geqslant 0\}
in
H
, given by
S_{N}(t)u_{0}=u(t,u_{0})
where, for each
u_{0}\in H
,
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto u(t,u_{0})\in H
is the unique
\frac{3}{8}-
regular
solution of (
3.9
). Moreover, from the above estimate,
S_{N}(t)
a compact map for each
t>0
.

Hence, from Theorems
2.1
and
2.7
, the above
computations and Lemma
3.5
below, we obtain Theorem
1.1
.

3.3.
Global attractor in
H
for the GMNSE

To prove existence of a global attractor
\mathcal{A}_{N}
for the semigroup

S_{N}
associated with (
3.9
), it suffices to show that there
exists a bounded absorbing set in
H
, since
S_{N}(t)
is compact for each
t>0
. This has already been proved in
[
5
]
when
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
. When
f_{\sigma,-1}\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}}
it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\nu\|u\|_{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\langle u,f_{\sigma,-1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}},

which implies

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\nu\|u\|_{V}^{2}\leqslant\|u\|_{V}\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}\leqslant\frac{\nu}{2}\|u\|_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Consequently

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\nu}{2}\|u\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|u\|_{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},

where
\lambda_{1}>0
is the first eigenvalue of the Stokes operator
A
. This expression immediately gives us

(3.12)

\|S_{N}(t)u_{0}\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant\|u_{0}\|_{H}^{2}e^{-\nu\lambda_{1}t}+\frac{\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\nu^{2}},

for every
N>0
. Hence, the ball

(3.13)

B_{0}=\left\{u\in H\colon\|u\|_{H}^{2}\leqslant 1+\frac{\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\nu^{2}}\right\}

uniformly absorbs bounded subsets of
H
under the action of the semigroup
S_{N}
, and we point out that
B_{0}
is independent of
N
. More precisely,
we obtain immediately the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
f_{\sigma,-1}\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then, for any bounded
set
B\subset H
there exists a time
T_{B}>0
such that

S_{N}(t)u_{0}\in B_{0}\quad\hbox{ for all }t\geqslant T_{B},\ u_{0}\in B\hbox{ and }N>0.

Therefore, for each
N>0
, the semigroup
S_{N}
has a global attractor
\mathcal{A}_{N}\subset B_{0}
.

The regularity of the solutions (uniformly in bounded sets, see (
3.11
)) also promptly gives a bounded absorbing set in
H_{\frac{3}{8}}
, but the latter depends on
N
. The global attractor
\mathcal{A}_{N}
attracts bounded subsets of
H
in the
H_{\frac{3}{8}}
-norm, or even
stronger norms.

4.
Weak global attractors in
H
for the
Navier-Stokes equation

We will assume throughout this section that
f_{\sigma,-1}\in H_{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Let us recall the definition of the set
\mathcal{A}\subset H
given in the
introduction.

\mathcal{A}=\left\{\begin{aligned} y\in H\colon\hbox{ there are sequences }&t_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty,\ \{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}\\
\hbox{ and }N_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty&\text{ such that }S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\to y\text{ weakly in }H.\end{aligned}\right\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{A}
is nonempty, since for any given sequences
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

j_{1}\in\mathbb{N}

for

such
that, up to a subsequence,
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}y
weakly in

. Thus,

.
Moreover, it is clear that

.

Definition 4.1

(Weak solution of (
1.1
))
.

We say that the function

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;V)\quad\hbox{ with }\quad\frac{du}{dt}\in L^{1}(0,T;V^{*})

is a weak solution of (

) on
(0,T)
, if

(4.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\phi=\int_{\Omega}A^{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi+\int_{\Omega}u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\phi+\int_{\Omega}f_{\sigma}\cdot\phi,\quad\hbox{ for all }\phi\in V\hbox{ and }t>0.

We recall that the set of all weak solutions of (
1.1
) for all times
will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}
.

For any
u_{0}\in H
and
f_{\sigma}\in L^{2}(0,T;V^{*})
there exists at least one
weak solution of (
1.1
) (see
[
21
]
). We recall the following lemma, which is proved exactly as in
[
5
, Theorem 13]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any sequence
u_{0}^{j}\to u_{0}
weakly in
{H}
, there
exists a sequence
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
and a
u\in\mathcal{K}
such that, along a subsequence, for all
T>0
we have

\displaystyle S_{N_{j}}(\cdot)u_{0}^{j}

\displaystyle\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(\cdot)\text{
weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,T;{H}),

\displaystyle S_{N_{j}}(\cdot)u_{0}^{j}

\displaystyle\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(\cdot)\text{
weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H_{\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle S_{N_{j}}(\cdot)u_{0}^{j}

\displaystyle\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(\cdot)\text{
strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;{H}).

We can easily extend this result.

Lemma 4.3
.

The subsequence obtained in Lemma
4.2
also
satisfies

(4.2)

S_{N_{j}}(s_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t_{0})\hbox{ weakly
in }H\hbox{ when }s_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t_{0},\hbox{
with }\{s_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,\infty).

Proof.

In a standard way (see
[
15
, p.1494]
) we obtain
S_{N_{j}}(\cdot)u_{0}^{j}\to u(\cdot)
in
C([0,T],H_{-\frac{1}{2}})
for any
T>0
. Combining this
with inequality (
3.12
), the statement follows.
∎

From Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
it follows that for any
u_{0}\in H
there exists
u\in\mathcal{KN}
such that
u(0)=u_{0}
(the subclass
\mathcal{KN}
of
\mathcal{K}
was introduced in Definition
1.2
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Each
u\in\mathcal{KN}
satisfies the energy inequality

(4.3)

V(u(t))\leqslant V(u(s))\hbox{ for a.e. }s>0\hbox{ and all }t\geqslant s,

where

V(u(r))=\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|u(r)\|_{H}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{r}\|u(\xi)\|_{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}d\xi-\int_{0}^{r}\langle u(\xi),f_{\sigma,-1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}d\xi.

Moreover, if
u(0)\in{H}\setminus\mathcal{A}
, then (
4.3
) is true for
s=0

as well.

Proof.

Let us first consider
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
. Then there exist
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}

and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that

v_{j}(t):=S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)\text{
weakly in }{H},\text{ for all }t\geqslant 0.

The functions
v_{j}
satisfy, for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t
, the energy
equality:

\frac{1}{2}\|v_{j}(t)\|_{{H}}^{2}+\nu\int_{s}^{t}\|v_{j}(\xi)\|_{{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}}^{2}d\xi-\int_{s}^{t}\langle v_{j}(\xi),f_{\sigma,-1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\|v_{j}(s)\|_{{H}}^{2}.

By the convergences in Lemmas
4.2
-
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\ \|v_{j}(t)\|_{H}^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|u(\xi)\|_{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{s}^{t}\|v_{j}(\xi)\|_{{H_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2}}}^{2}d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}(u(\xi),f_{\sigma,-1})d\xi

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{s}^{t}\langle v_{j}(\xi),f_{\sigma,-1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}d\xi\quad\hbox{ for all }t\geqslant s\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v_{j}(s)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\quad\hbox{ for a.e. }s>0.

Hence for a.e.
s>0
and all
t\geqslant s
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{{H}}^{2}+\nu\int_{s}^{t}\|u(\xi)\|_{{H_{\frac{1}{2}}}}^{2}d\xi-\int_{s}^{t}\langle u(\xi),f_{\sigma,-1}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}d\xi\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|u(s)\|_{{H}}^{2},

so (
4.3
) follows.

Now let
u(0)\in H\setminus\mathcal{A}
. Then there exists
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that

v_{j}(t)=S_{N_{j}}(t)u(0)\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)\text{ weakly
in }{H},\text{ for all }t\geqslant 0.

Then, arguing as before, we obtain (
4.3
). Also,
as
v_{j}(0)=u(0)
for all
j
, the inequality is true for
s=0
as well.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The ball
B_{0}
is absorbing for the solutions in
\mathcal{KN}
, that is, for any bounded set
B
in
{H}
there exists
T_{B}\geqslant 0
such that

u(t)\in B_{0}\quad\hbox{ for all }t\geqslant T_{B},

where
u\in\mathcal{KN}
and
u(0)\in B
.

Proof.

We begin by fixing a bounded subset
B
of
{H}
. From Lemma
3.5

there exists
\tilde{T}=T_{B,B_{0}}\geqslant 0
such that

S_{N}(t)u_{0}\in B_{0}\quad\hbox{ for all }u_{0}\in B\cup B_{0},\ t\geqslant\tilde{T}\hbox{ and }N>0.

Now let
u\in\mathcal{KN}
. If
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
and
t\geqslant\tilde{T}
, there exists sequences
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that
S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)
weakly in

. Hence

\|u(t)\|_{H}\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\|_{H}\leqslant 1+\frac{\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\nu^{2}},

since
S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\in B_{0}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
u(t)\in B_{0}
.

If
u(0)\in{H}\setminus\mathcal{A}
and
t\geqslant\tilde{T}
, there exists
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that
S_{N_{j}}(t)u(0)\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)
weakly in
{H}
. Thus,

\|u(t)\|_{H}\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|S_{N_{j}}(t)u(0)\|_{H}\leqslant 1+\frac{\|f_{\sigma,-1}\|_{H_{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\nu^{2}},

since
S_{N_{j}}(t)u(0)\in B_{0}
for

. Hence,
u(t)\in B_{0}

and the proof is complete.
∎

We observe that the weak topology of
{H}
is metrizable in the ball
B_{0}
.
We denote this metric by
\rho_{w}
. Since all the solutions starting in a
bounded subset
B
of
{H}
enter
B_{0}
and remain there after some time
T_{B}\geqslant 0
, we can use this metric to study the asymptotic behavior of
the solutions. For
A,B\subset B_{0}
we denote by

(4.4)

dist_{w}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\rho_{w}(a,b)

the Hausdorff semidistance from
A
to
B
in the weak topology.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

(a)
Let
\{y_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{A}
. Hence, there exist
sequences
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

such that

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j},y_{j})<\frac{1}{j},

where we can assume that
j
is sufficiently large so that
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\in B_{0}
. Hence, up to a subsequence,
S_{N_{n}}(t_{n})u_{0}^{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}z
weakly in
H

for some
z\in H
(clearly
z\in\mathcal{A}
). It follows that
y_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}z
, and proves that
\mathcal{A}
is
weakly compact.

(b)
Let
y\in\mathcal{A}
,
t\geqslant 0
,
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}

and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
be such that
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\to y
weakly in
H
. Since
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
, we can assume that
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j}-t)u_{0}^{j}\in B_{0}
for
all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence, up to a subsequence
z_{0}^{j}:=S_{N_{j}}(t_{j}-t)u_{0}^{j}\to z
weakly in
H
for some
z\in H
. From
Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, there exists
u\in\mathcal{K}

such that if
[0,\infty)\ni s_{j}\to t_{0}
, up to a subsequence, we have

S_{N_{j}}(s_{j})z_{0}^{j}\to u(t_{0})\hbox{ weakly in }H.

In particular
u(t)\leftarrow S_{N_{j}}(t)z_{0}^{j}=S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\to y
, and
hence
u(t)=y
. Also, for any
t_{0}\geqslant 0
and
[0,\infty)\ni s_{j}\to t_{0}

we have

(4.5)

u(t_{0})\leftarrow S_{N_{j}}(s_{j})z_{0}^{j}=S_{N_{j}}(t_{j}-t+s_{j})u_{0}^{j}.

When
t_{0}=0
, we obtain
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j}-t)u_{0}^{j}\to u(0)
which proves that
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
, from (
4.5
) it
follows that
u\in\mathcal{KN}
.

(c)
Since
u\in\mathcal{KN}
and
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
then
given
t\geqslant 0
we have
S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\to u(t)
weakly in
H
as
j\to\infty
. Hence
u(t)\in\mathcal{A}
, since
t+t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
.

(d)
If that is not the case, there exist
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{KN}
with
u_{j}(0)\in B
,
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.6)

dist_{w}(u_{j}(t_{j}),\mathcal{A})\geqslant\varepsilon\quad\hbox{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Up to subsequences, we can distinguish two cases:
\{u_{j}(0)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
and
\{u_{j}(0)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H\setminus\mathcal{A}
.

When
\{u_{j}(0)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
, by a diagonalization
process we can construct sequences
r_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}}(r_{j}+t_{j})u_{0}^{j},u_{j}(t_{j}))<\frac{1}{j}\quad\hbox{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Also, up to a subsequence, we can assume that
S_{N_{j}}(r_{j}+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}z
weakly in
H
for some
z\in H
. Clearly
z\in\mathcal{A}
and
u_{j}(t_{j})\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}z

weakly in
H
. Taking the limit when
j\to\infty
in (
4.6
) we
obtain a contradiction.

It remains to consider the case
\{u_{j}(0)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H\setminus\mathcal{A}
. We can construct a sequence
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{j}(0),u_{j}(t_{j}))<\frac{1}{j}.

Again, we can assume that, up to a subsequence,
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{j}(0)\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}z
weakly in
H
for some
z\in H
. Since
\{u_{j}(0)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
, for
j
sufficiently large,
t_{j}\geqslant T_{B}
(where
T_{B}\geqslant 0
is given in Lemma
4.5
) and
t_{j}-T_{B}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Hence
S_{N_{j}}(t_{j})u_{j}(0)=S_{N_{j}}(t_{j}-T_{B})S_{N_{j}}(T_{B})u_{j}(0)
and since
S_{N_{j}}(T_{B})u_{j}(0)\in B_{0}
, we obtain
z\in\mathcal{A}
. Again, taking the
limit when
j\to\infty
in (
4.6
) we obtain a contradiction and
prove the result.
∎

The solutions in
\mathcal{KN}
satisfy the translation property on
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 4.6

(Translation property)
.

For any
u\in\mathcal{KN}
such that
u(0)\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
u_{s}(\cdot):=u(\cdot+s)\in\mathcal{KN}
for any
s>0
.

Proof.

For each
s\geqslant 0
, clearly
u_{s}\in\mathcal{K}
. From Theorem
1.3
, we know that
u_{s}(0)\in\mathcal{A}
. If
t_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
are such that

S_{N_{j}}(t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)\hbox{ weakly
in }H\hbox{ for all }t\geqslant 0,

we have

S_{N_{j}}(s+t+t_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t+s)=u_{s}(t)\hbox{ weakly in }H\hbox{ for all }t\geqslant 0,

which shows that
u_{s}\in\mathcal{KN}
.
∎

We are not able to prove the translation property for
u\in\mathcal{KN}

when
u(0)\in H\setminus\mathcal{A}
. The reason is that when we take the
translation of the approximative solutions the initial conditions are a
sequence, and not a constant value equal to the initial value of the limit
problem (as given in the definition of
\mathcal{KN}
). Indeed, if
u\in\mathcal{KN}
and
u(0)\in H\setminus\mathcal{A}
then, for
s>0
, we define

v(\cdot)=u(\cdot+s)
. We choose
s>0
such that
u(s)\in H\setminus\mathcal{A}
. For
t_{0}\geqslant s
and
[s,\infty)\ni s_{j}\to t_{0}
we have

S_{N_{j}}(s_{j})u(0)=S_{N_{j}}(s_{j}-s)S_{N_{j}}(s)u(0)=S_{N_{j}}(\widetilde{s}_{j})u_{0}^{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t_{0})=v(t_{0}-s)=v(\widetilde{t}_{0}),

where
\widetilde{s}_{j}=s_{j}-s
,
u_{0}^{j}=S_{N_{j}}(s)u(0),\ \widetilde{t}_{0}=t_{0}-s
. However, as remarked before, the initial conditions are not equal to
u(s)
, so we do not have the convergence

S_{N_{j}}(\widetilde{s}_{j})u(s)\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}v(\widetilde{t}_{0}).

As a consequence of this property the map
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{A}\to P(\mathcal{A})
, where
P(\mathcal{A})
is the set of all
nonempty subsets of
\mathcal{A}
, given by

G(t,u_{0})=\{u(t)\colon u\in\mathcal{KN}\hbox{ and }u(0)=u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}\}

is a multivalued semiflow, that is,
G(0,\cdot)
is the identity map and
G(t+s,u_{0})\subset G(t,G(s,u_{0}))
for all
t,s\geqslant 0
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
, as shown in the next result.

Proposition 4.7
.

G
is a multivalued semiflow.

Proof.

Fix
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
. If
y\in G(t+s,u_{0})
then there exists
u\in\mathcal{KN}
with
u(0)=u_{0}
such that
y=u(t+s)
. We have
y=u_{s}(t)
and
from the translation property we obtain
u_{s}\in\mathcal{KN}
and
u_{s}(0)=u(s)
. Hence
y\in G(t,u(s))\subset G(t,G(s,u_{0}))
. Therefore,
G(t+s,u_{0})\subset G(t,G(s,u_{0}))
.
∎

We have not been able to prove that
G
is a strict multivalued semiflow,
that is, that the equality
G(t+s,u_{0})=G(t,G(s,u_{0}))
is satisfied for all
t,s\geqslant 0
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
. The reason is that we do not know
whether the concatenation of solutions from
\mathcal{KN}
remains in
\mathcal{KN}
(even when
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
), that is, we do not know that
if given
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{KN}
such that
u_{2}(0)=u_{1}(s)
for some
s>0
,
the function

u(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}u_{1}(t)&\hbox{ for }0\leqslant t\leqslant s,\\
u_{2}(t-s)&\hbox{ for }t>s,\end{array}\right.

belongs to
\mathcal{KN}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
u_{0}^{n}\to u_{0}
weakly in
H
, where
\{u_{0}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{A}
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
. For any sequence
u_{n}\in\mathcal{KN}
with
u_{n}(0)=u_{0}^{n}
there exists a subsequence (renamed the
same) and
u\in\mathcal{KN}
with
u(0)=u_{0}
such that

u_{n}(s_{n})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)\hbox{ weakly in }H\hbox{ for all }[0,\infty)\ni s_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist sequences
t_{j}^{n}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
,
\{u_{0}^{j,n}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{0}
and
N_{j}^{n}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
such that for any given

T>0
we have

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}^{n}}(t+t_{j}^{n})u_{0}^{j,n},u_{n}(t))\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}0\quad\hbox{ uniformly in }[0,T].

Thus, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose
j_{n}>n
such that

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}^{n}}(t+t_{j_{n}}^{n})u_{0}^{j_{n},n},u_{n}(t))<\frac{1}{n}\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

Clearly, we can choose the sequence
\{j_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in such a
way that it is strictly increasing. By Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
there exists
u\in\mathcal{K}
such that, up to a subsequence,
we have

\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}^{n}}(t+t_{j_{n}}^{n})u_{0}^{j_{n},n},u(t))\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\quad\text{ uniformly in }[0,T].

Hence,
u\in\mathcal{KN}
and, if
[0,T]\ni s_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t
, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
n\geqslant n_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{w}

\displaystyle(u_{n}(s_{n}),u(t))

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}^{n}}(s_{n}+t_{j_{n}}^{n})u_{0}^{j_{n},n},u(t))+\rho_{w}(S_{N_{j}^{n}}(s_{n}+t_{j_{n}}^{n})u_{0}^{j_{n},n},u_{n}(s_{n}))\leqslant\varepsilon+\frac{1}{n},

so
u_{n}(s_{n})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u(t)
weakly in
H
.
Clearly
u(0)=u_{0}
and, by a diagonalization argument, the conclusion holds
for any
t\geqslant 0.

∎

We obtain now a standard characterization of the weak global attractor
\mathcal{A}
. The function
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to H
is said to be a

complete trajectory
of (
1.1
) in
\mathcal{KN}
if

\phi(\cdot+s)\!\mid_{[0,\infty)}\ \in\mathcal{KN}\quad\hbox{ for any }s\in\mathbb{R}.

It is said to be a
bounded complete trajectory
in
\mathcal{KN}
if
it is a complete trajectory in
\mathcal{KN}
and
\phi(\mathbb{R})
is a
bounded set in
H
.

Lemma 4.9
.

If
\phi
is a bounded complete trajectory in
\mathcal{KN}
, then
\phi(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Denote
B=\phi(\mathbb{R})
and fix
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Denote
u_{\tau}(\cdot)=\phi(\cdot+t_{0}-\tau)
for
\tau\geqslant 0
. Then, as
\phi(t_{0})=u_{\tau}(\tau)
, we deduce by the attracting property of
\mathcal{A}
that

\displaystyle dist_{w}(\phi(t_{0}),\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=dist_{w}(u_{\tau}(\tau),\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{u\in\mathcal{KN},\ u(0)\in B}dist_{w}(u(\tau),\mathcal{A})\overset{\tau\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Therefore,
\phi(t_{0})\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is
arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

The weak global attractor
\mathcal{A}
can be characterized
by the union of all bounded complete trajectories of (
1.1
) in
\mathcal{KN}
, that is,

\mathcal{A}=\{\phi(0)\colon\phi\hbox{ is a bounded complete trajectory of
\eqref{eq:NS} in }\mathcal{KN}\}.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathcal{A}
be arbitrary. We take a sequence
t_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is
negatively invariant, there exist
u_{n}\in\mathcal{KN}
such that
u_{n}(t_{n})=z
and
u^{n}(0)\in\mathcal{A}
. We define the functions
v_{n}^{0}(\cdot)=u_{n}(\cdot+t_{n})
, which belongs to
\mathcal{KN}
by the
translation property. As the attractor is positively invariant,
v_{n}^{0}(t)\in\mathcal{A}
for all
t\geqslant 0
. In view of Lemma
4.8
, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}^{0}(s_{n})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}v^{0}(t)\in\mathcal{A}
weakly in
H
for all
t\geqslant 0
and
[0,\infty)\ni s_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t
, where
v^{0}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{KN}
. In addition,
v^{0}(0)=z
. Consider now the
sequence
v_{n}^{1}(\cdot)=u_{n}(\cdot+t_{n}-1)
, which again belongs to
\mathcal{KN}
and satisfies
v_{n}^{1}(t)\in\mathcal{A}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Using again Lemma
4.8
, up to a subsequence we have
v_{n}^{1}(s_{n})\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}v^{1}(t)\in\mathcal{A}
weakly in
H

for all
t\geqslant 0
and
[0,\infty)\ni s_{n}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}t
, where
v^{1}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{KN}
. It follows that
v^{1}(t+1)=v^{0}(t)
for all
t\geqslant 0
as well. In this way, we
construct inductively a sequence of solutions from
\mathcal{KN}
, denoted
by
\{v^{j}(\cdot)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
v^{j}(t)\in

\mathcal{A}

and
v^{j}(t+1)=v^{j-1}(t)
for all
t\geqslant 0
and
j
. Define
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to H
to be at any
t
the common value of the functions
v^{j}
,
that is,

\phi(t)=v^{j}(t+j)\text{ if }t\geqslant-j.

Then
\phi
is a bounded complete trajectory of (
1.1
) in
\mathcal{KN}
. Thus,

\mathcal{A}\subset\{\phi(0)\colon\phi\hbox{ is a bounded complete trajectory
of \eqref{eq:NS} in }\mathcal{KN}.\}

The converse inclusion follows from Lemma
4.9
, and the result is
proved.
∎

Finally, we will prove that the global attractors
\mathcal{A}_{N}
of (
3.9
) behave upper semicontinuously with respect to the weak
global attractor
\mathcal{A}
of (
1.1
).

Proposition 4.11
.

If
\mathcal{A}_{N}
is the global attractor of
S_{N}

then

dist_{w}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{A})\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Proof.

If
N_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
and
u_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{N_{j}}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\cup_{N>0}\mathcal{A}_{N}\subset B_{0}
, there exists
u_{0}\in{H}
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{j}\to u_{0}

weakly in
{H}
. For each
u_{j}
there exists a global solution
\xi_{j}
of
the semigroup
S_{N_{j}}
with
\xi(0)=u_{j}
and
\xi(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{A}_{N_{j}}
. Hence

S_{N_{j}}(j)\xi_{j}(-j)=\xi_{j}(0)=u_{j}\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}u_{0}\hbox{ weakly in }{H}.

This shows that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence,
dist_{w}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{A})\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
∎

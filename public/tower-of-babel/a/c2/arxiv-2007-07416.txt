On metric spaces with given transfinite asymptotic dimensions
Yan Wu, Jingming Zhu, Taras Radul

Abstract




Abstract.

For every countable ordinal number
\xi
, we construct a metric space
X_{\xi}
whose transfinite asymptotic dimension and complementary-finite asymptotic dimension are both
\xi
.

Keywords
Asymptotic dimension, Transfinite asymptotic dimension, Complementary-finite asymptotic dimension;

1
Introduction

In coarse geometry, asymptotic dimension of a metric space is an important concept which was defined
by Gromov for studying asymptotic invariants of discrete groups
[
1
]
. This dimension can be considered
as an asymptotic analogue of the Lebesgue covering dimension. As a large scale analogue of W.E. Haver’s
property
C
in dimension theory, A. Dranishnikov introduced the notion of asymptotic property
C
in
[
2
]
. It is
well known that every metric space with finite asymptotic dimension has asymptotic property
C
. But the
inverse is not true, which means that there exists some metric space
X
with infinite asymptotic dimension
and asymptotic property
C
. Therefore how to classify the metric spaces with infinite asymptotic dimension
into smaller categories becomes an interesting problem.

In
[
3
]
, T. Radul defined the transfinite asymptotic dimension (trasdim) which can be viewed as a transfinite extension of the asymptotic dimension and proved that for a metric space
X
, trasdim
(X)<\infty
if and only if
X
has asymptotic property
C
. He also gave examples of metric spaces with trasdim
=\infty
and with trasdim
=\omega
, where
\omega
is the smallest infinite ordinal number (see
[
3
]
). But whether there is a metric space
X
with
\omega<
trasdim
(X)<\infty
(stated as“omega conjecture”in
[
4
]
by M. Satkiewicz) was unknown until recently. A metric space
X
with trasdim
X=\omega+1
was constructed in
[
5
]
. Some examples of metric spaces with higher trasdim were constructed in
[
6
]
. Let us remark that trasdim takes only countable values
[
3
]
.
In this paper, for every countable ordinal number
\xi
, we construct a metric space
X_{\xi}
with trasdim
(X_{\xi})=\xi
and coasdim
(X_{\xi})=\xi
, which generalized the results in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
. Let us remark that this result combined with a recent result in
[
7
]
gives an answer to a problem stated by
[
8
]
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we recall some definitions and properties of transfinite asymptotic dimension and complementary-finite asymptotic dimension. In
Section 3, we introduce a concrete metric space
X_{\xi}
with trasdim
(X_{\xi})=\xi
and coasdim
(X_{\xi})=\xi
for every countable ordinal number
\xi
.

2
Preliminaries

Our terminology concerning the asymptotic dimension follows from
[
9
]
and for undefined terminology we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
U,V\subseteq X
, let

\text{diam}~U=\text{sup}\{d(x,y)~|~x,y\in U\}\text{ and }d(U,V)=\text{inf}\{d(x,y)~|~x\in U,y\in V\}.

Let
R>0
and
\mathcal{U}
be a family of subsets of
X
.
\mathcal{U}
is said to be
R
-bounded
if

\text{diam}~\mathcal{U}\doteq\text{sup}\{\text{diam}~U~|~U\in\mathcal{U}\}\leq R.

In this case,
\mathcal{U}
is said to be
uniformly bounded
.
Let
r>0
, a family
\mathcal{U}
is said to be

r
-disjoint
if

d(U,V)\geq r~\text{for every}~U,V\in\mathcal{U}\text{~with~}U\neq V.

In this paper, we denote

\bigcup\{U~|~U\in\mathcal{U}\}
by
\bigcup\mathcal{U}
, denote
\{U~|~U\in\mathcal{U}_{1}\text{~or~}~U\in\mathcal{U}_{2}\}

by
\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\mathcal{U}_{2}
.
Let
A
be a subset of a metric space
X
and
\epsilon>0
. We denote
\{x\in X~|~d(x,A)<\epsilon\}
by
N_{\epsilon}(A)

and denote
\{x\in X~|~d(x,A)\leq\epsilon\}
by
\overline{N_{\epsilon}(A)}
.
We denote
\{N_{\epsilon}(U)~|~U\in\mathcal{U}\}
by
N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{U})
and
denote
\{\overline{N_{\epsilon}(U)}~|~U\in\mathcal{U}\}
by
\overline{N_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{U})}
.

Definition 2.1.

(
[
1
]
)
A metric space
X
is said to have
finite asymptotic dimension
if
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for every
r>0
,
there exists a sequence of uniformly bounded families

\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
of subsets of
X

such that the family

\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{U}_{i}
covers
X
and each
\mathcal{U}_{i}

is
r
-disjoint for
i=0,1,\cdots,n
. In this case, we say that
the
asymptotic dimension
of
X
less than or equal to
n
, which is denoted by
asdim
(X)\leq n
.

We say that asdim
(X)=n
if asdim
(X)\leq n
and asdim
(X)\leq n-1
is not true.

T. Radul generalized asymptotic dimension of a metric space
X
to transfinite asymptotic dimension which is denoted by trasdim
(X)
(see
[
3
]
). We will need the set-theoretical function Ord from
[
11
]
which classifies families of finite non-empty subsets of a set
T
. We will consider only the particular case when
T=\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2.2.

(
[
11
]
)
Let
Fin\mathbb{N}
denote the collection of all finite, nonempty
subsets of
\mathbb{N}
and let
M\subseteq Fin\mathbb{N}
. For
\sigma\in\{\varnothing\}\cup Fin\mathbb{N}
, let

M^{\sigma}=\{\tau\in Fin\mathbb{N}~|~\tau\cup\sigma\in M\text{ and }\tau\cap\sigma=\varnothing\}.

Let
M^{a}
abbreviate
M^{\{a\}}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
the ordinal number
Ord
M
inductively as follows:

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle M=\varnothing,

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall~a\in\mathbb{N},~\text{Ord}M^{a}<\alpha,

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M=\alpha

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M\leq\alpha\text{ and }\text{Ord}M<\alpha\text{ is not true},

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M=\infty

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\text{Ord}M\leq\alpha\text{ is not true for every ordinal number }\alpha.

We call a family
M\subseteq Fin\mathbb{N}

inclusive
if and only if for each
\sigma,\tau\in Fin\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau\subseteq\sigma
and

\sigma\in M
, we have
\tau\in M
. We consider only inclusive families in the following.

The following lemmas are particular cases of the corresponding lemmas from
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
11
]
)

Let
M\subseteq Fin\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then

Ord
M\leq n
if and only if
|\sigma|\leq n
for every
\sigma\in M
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
11
]
)

If

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[

]
)

If

\text{Ord}(M\cup N)\leq\text{max}\{\text{Ord}M,\text{Ord}N\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
11
]
)

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be sets. Let
FinL
and
FinL^{\prime}
denote collections of all finite, nonempty
subsets of
L
and
L^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let
M\subseteq FinL
,
M^{\prime}\subseteq FinL^{\prime}
and

\phi:L\to L^{\prime}
be a function such that for every
\sigma\in M
, we have
\phi(\sigma)\in M^{\prime}
and

. Then Ord
M\leq
Ord
M^{\prime}
.

Let

and

be an infinite subset of
\mathbb{N}
. Then there is a standard bijection
\varphi
from
\mathbb{N}
to

which keeps the order. We define
M[K]=\Big\{\{\varphi(k_{1}),\varphi(k_{2}),\cdots,\varphi(k_{m})\}~\Big|~\{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}\}\in M\Big\}
.
Note that
M=M[\mathbb{N}]
.
By Lemma

, we obtain the following result.

.

Let
M\subseteq Fin\mathbb{N}
and
K

M[K]
.

Definition 2.3.

(
[
3
]
)
Given a metric space
X
, define the following collection:

\begin{split}A(X,d)=\{\sigma\in Fin\mathbb{N}~|~&\text{ there are no uniformly bounded families }\mathcal{U}_{i}\text{ for }i\in\sigma\\
&\text{ such that each }\mathcal{U}_{i}\text{ is }i\text{-disjoint and }\bigcup_{i\in\sigma}\mathcal{U}_{i}\text{~covers~}X\}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}A_{2}(X,d)=\{\sigma\in Fin\mathbb{N}~|~&\text{ there are no uniformly bounded families }\mathcal{V}_{i}\text{ for }i\in\sigma\\
&\text{ such that each }\mathcal{V}_{i}\text{ is }2^{i}\text{-disjoint and }\bigcup_{i\in\sigma}\mathcal{V}_{i}\text{ covers }X\}.\end{split}

The
transfinite asymptotic dimension
of
X
is defined as trasdim
(X)=
Ord
A(X,d)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Ord
A(X,d)=
Ord
A_{2}(X,d)
.

Proof.

Since
A(X,d)\subseteq A_{2}(X,d)
, Ord
A(X,d)\leq
Ord
A_{2}(X,d)
by Lemma
2.2
.
The inverse inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
by considering the exponential function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
defined by
\phi(n)=2^{n}
.
∎

Definition 2.4.

(
[
12
]
)
Every ordinal number
\gamma
can be represented as
\gamma=\lambda(\gamma)+n(\gamma)
, where
\lambda(\gamma)
is the limit ordinal or
0
and
n(\gamma)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
X
be a metric space, we define
complementary-finite asymptotic dimension
of
X
(
coasdim
(X)
) inductively as follows:

•

coasdim
(X)=-1

\Leftrightarrow

X=\emptyset
,

•

coasdim
(X)\leq\lambda(\gamma)+n(\gamma)

\Leftrightarrow
for every
r>0
, there exist
r
-disjoint uniformly bounded families
\mathcal{U}_{0},\cdots,\mathcal{U}_{n(\gamma)}
of subsets of
X
such that
coasdim
(X\setminus\bigcup\bigcup_{i=0}^{n(\gamma)}\mathcal{U}_{i})<\lambda(\gamma)
,

•

coasdim
(X)=\gamma

\Leftrightarrow

coasdim
(X)\leq\gamma
and
coasdim
(X)\leq\beta
is not true
for any
\beta<\gamma
,

•

coasdim
(X)=\infty

\Leftrightarrow

coasdim
(X)\leq\gamma
is not true for any ordinal number
\gamma
.

X
is said to have
complementary-finite asymptotic dimension
if coasdim
(X)\leq\gamma
for some ordinal number
\gamma
.

Remark 2.1.

It is easy to see that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, coasdim
(X)\leq n
if and only if asdim
(X)\leq n
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(
[
12
]
)

Let
X
be a metric space with
X_{1},X_{2}\subseteq X
. Then

coasdim
(X_{1}\cup X_{2})\leq\text{max}\{\text{coasdim}(X_{1}),\text{coasdim}(X_{2})\}
.

Main result

3.1
Families with given Ord

For each
\tau=\{k_{0},k_{1},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in Fin\mathbb{N}
, we choose an indexation such that
k_{0}<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{s}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let

K(\tau,n)=\{k_{n+1},k_{n+2}\cdots,k_{s}\}
if
n<s
and
K(\tau,n)=\varnothing
if
n\geq s
,

and let

i(\tau,n)=\text{max}\{k_{0},k_{1}\cdots,k_{n}\}=k_{n}
if
n\leq s
and
i(\tau,n)=k_{s}
if
n>s
.

For each limit ordinal
\alpha
, we fix an increasing sequence
\{\zeta_{i}(\alpha)+i\}
of ordinals such that

each
\zeta_{i}(\alpha)
is a limit ordinal or 0 and
\alpha=\text{sup}_{i}(\zeta_{i}(\alpha)+i)
.

When
\alpha=\text{sup}_{i}(\beta+i)
for some limit ordinal
\beta
, we put
\zeta_{i}(\alpha)=\beta
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each countable ordinal number
\xi
, we write
\xi=\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)
, where
\gamma(\xi)
is a limit ordinal or 0 and

n(\xi)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
We build a family
S_{\xi}\subseteq Fin\mathbb{N}
by induction.

Definition 3.5.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\xi
be a countable ordinal number , we define

•

S_{n}=\{\sigma\in Fin\mathbb{N}~|~|\sigma|\leq n\}
.

•

S_{\xi}=S_{\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)}=\Big\{\sigma\in Fin\mathbb{N}~\Big|~K(\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing~\text{~for~some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\sigma,n(\xi))\}\Big\}.

Remark 3.2.

•

It follows from the definition of the family
S_{\xi}
that
\sigma\in S_{\xi}
for each
\sigma\in Fin\mathbb{N}
with
|\sigma|\leq n(\xi)+1
.

•

Note that if
\sigma=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi}
, then
\{l,k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi+1}
for every
l<k_{0}
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\xi=\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)
be an infinite ordinal number and
\tau=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi}
. Then we have

\tau\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+n(\xi)+1}\text{ for some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\tau,n(\xi))\}.

Proof.

Since
\tau=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi}
, we have
K(\tau,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing~\text{~for~some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\tau,n(\xi))\}
.

•

If
K(\tau,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l},

then
\tau=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{n(\xi)},k_{n(\xi)+1},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+n(\xi)+1}
by Remark 3.2.

•

If
K(\tau,n(\xi))=\varnothing
, then

K(\tau,l+n(\xi)+1)\subseteq K(\tau,n(\xi))
implies
K(\tau,l+n(\xi)+1)=\varnothing
. So
\tau\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+n(\xi)+1}
.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The family
S_{\xi}
is inclusive for each countable ordinal
\xi
.

Proof.

•

It follows from the definition of
S_{\xi}
that the result is true when
\xi
is finite.

•

Assume that the result holds for every infinite ordinal number
\xi<\alpha
.
Now for
\xi=\alpha
, let
\tau=\{k_{j_{0}},k_{j_{1}},\cdots,k_{j_{t}}\}\subseteq\sigma=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi}
.
Note that
t\leq s
and
K(\tau,n(\xi))\subseteq~K(\sigma,n(\xi))
. If
t\leq n(\xi)
, then we have
\tau\in S_{\xi}
by definition.
Consider the case
t>n(\xi)
. Then we have
s>n(\xi)
and

K(\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}~\text{~for~some }1\leq l\leq i(\sigma,n(\xi))=k_{n(\xi)}\leq k_{j_{n(\xi)}}=i(\tau,n(\xi)).

By inductive assumption, we have
K(\tau,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}
.
So
\tau\in S_{\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)}
.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

S_{\gamma+n}\subseteq S_{\gamma+m}
, where
\gamma
is a limit ordinal and
m\in\mathbb{N},n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
such that
n<m
.

Proof.

For every
\sigma=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\gamma+n}
.

•

If
s\leq m
, then we have
\sigma\in S_{\gamma+m}
by definition.

•

If
s>m
, then
s>n
. It follows that

K(\sigma,n)\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma)+l}~\text{~for~some }1\leq l\leq i(\sigma,n)=k_{n}<k_{m}=i(\sigma,m).

Since
n<m
, we have

K(\sigma,m)\subseteq K(\sigma,n).

Since
S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma)+l}
is inclusive by Lemma
3.2
,
we have

K(\sigma,m)\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma)+l}\text{~for~some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\sigma,m)\}.

Hence
\sigma\in S_{\gamma+m}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For each countable ordinals
\xi
, Ord
S_{\xi}\leq\xi
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, the result is true when
\xi
is finite. Assume that the result holds for every
\xi<\alpha
. Now
\xi=\alpha
.
For every
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in S_{\xi}^{a}
,
\{a\}\sqcup\sigma\in S_{\xi}.
It follows that

K(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing~\text{for~some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))\}.

•

When
n(\xi)\in\mathbb{N}
.

–

If
i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))>a
, then
K(\sigma,n(\xi)-1)=K(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing
.
Note that

1\leq l\leq i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))=i(\sigma,n(\xi)-1)\text{~which implies that~}\sigma\in S_{\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)-1}.

–

If
i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi))\leq a
, then

l\leq a\text{ and }K(\sigma,n(\xi))\subseteq K(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,n(\xi)).

Since
S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}
is inclusive,
K(\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing
.
So by Remark 3.2,
\sigma\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+n(\xi)+1}
for some
l\in\{1,2,\cdots,a\}
.

Therefore,

S_{\xi}^{a}\subseteq S_{\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)-1}\cup(\bigcup_{l=1}^{a}S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+n(\xi)+l+1}).

Then by Lemma
2.3
and inductive assumption,

\text{Ord}(S_{\xi}^{a})\leq\gamma(\xi)+n(\xi)-1<\xi.

•

When
n(\xi)=0
.

–

If
i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,0)=a
, then
\sigma=K(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,0)\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing
, for some
l\in\{1,2,\cdots,a\}
.

–

If
i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,0)<a
, then

K(\sigma,0)\subseteq K(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,0)\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l}\cup\varnothing~\text{for~some }l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\{a\}\sqcup\sigma,0)\}
,

which implies
\sigma\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+1}
for some
l<a
, by Lemma
3.2
.

So by Lemma
3.3
,

S_{\xi}^{a}\subseteq\bigcup_{l=1}^{a}S_{\zeta_{l}(\gamma(\xi))+l+1}.

Then by Lemma
2.3
and inductive assumption,

\text{Ord}(S_{\xi}^{a})\leq\zeta_{a}(\gamma(\xi))+a+1<\xi.

So in both cases, we have Ord
S_{\xi}\leq\xi
.

∎

Let
\sigma=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in Fin\mathbb{N}
and
\tau=\{p_{0},\cdots,p_{s}\}\in Fin\mathbb{N}
.
We denote
\sigma\leq\tau
if for each
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,s\}
,
k_{i}\leq p_{i}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\xi
be an countable ordinal number and
\sigma\in S_{\xi}
. Then for each
\tau\in\text{Fin}\mathbb{N}
such that

\sigma\leq\tau
, we have
\tau\in S_{\xi}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see the result is true when
\xi
is finite. Assume that the result holds for every countable ordinal number
\xi<\alpha
. For
\xi=\alpha
, let
\sigma=\{k_{0},\cdots,k_{s}\}\in S_{\xi}
and
\tau=\{p_{0},\cdots,p_{s}\}
such that
k_{i}\leq p_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,s\}
.

•

If
s\leq n(\xi)
, then
\tau\in S_{\xi}
by Remark 3.2.

•

If
s>n(\xi)
, then
K(\sigma,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\xi)+l}
for some
1\leq l\leq i(\sigma,n(\xi))=k_{n(\xi)}\leq p_{n(\xi)}=i(\tau,n(\xi))
.
Note that
K(\sigma,n(\xi))\leq K(\tau,n(\xi))
. By inductive assumption,

K(\tau,n(\xi))\in S_{\zeta_{l}(\xi)+l}
for some
l\in\{1,2,\cdots,i(\tau,n(\xi))\}
.

So
\tau\in S_{\xi}
.

∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Ord
S_{\xi}=\xi
for each countable ordinal number
\xi
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, it is easy to obtain that the result is true when
\xi
is finite. Assume that the result holds for every countable ordinal number
\xi<\alpha
. For
\xi=\alpha
.
By Lemma
3.4
, it suffices to show that Ord
S_{\xi}\geq\xi
.

•

If
n(\xi)=0
, then it means that
\xi
is a limit ordinal. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)=\{\tau\in S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}\mid t>k
for each
t\in\tau\}
.

Then for each
\tau\in S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)
,
\{k\}\sqcup\tau\in S_{\xi}
by definition. Since
S_{\xi}
is inclusive by Lemma
3.2
,
\tau\in S_{\xi}
. So
S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)\subseteq S_{\xi}
.

Note that

\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}=\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k).

Indeed, by Lemma
2.2
,
S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)\subseteq S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}
implies
\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)\leq\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}.
There is a function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
defined by
\phi(i)=i+k
. Then by Lemma
3.5
,

\text{ $\phi(\sigma)\in S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k)$ whenever $\sigma\in S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}$ and $|\phi(\sigma)|=|\sigma|$}.

By Lemma
2.4
,
\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}\leq\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k).
So
\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}=\text{Ord}S_{\zeta_{k}(\xi)+k}(k).

By Lemma

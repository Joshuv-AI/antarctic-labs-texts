Ghosts and congruences for p s p^{s} -approximations of hypergeometric periods
Alexander Varchenko ⋆ and Wadim Zudilin ⋄

Abstract
. We prove general Dwork-type congruences for constant terms attached to tuples of Laurent polynomials. We apply this result to establishing arithmetic and p p -adic analytic properties of functions originating from polynomial solutions modulo p s p^{s} of hypergeometric and KZ equations, solutions which come as coefficients of master polynomials and whose coefficients are integers. As an application we show that the simplest example of a p p -adic KZ connection has an invariant line subbundle while its complex analog has no nontrivial subbundles due to the irreducibility of its monodromy representation.

1.
Introduction

In the seminal work
[
Dw
]
Dwork laid the foundation of the theory
of
p
-adic hypergeometric differential equations.
His principal working example was the differential equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle x(1-x)I^{\prime\prime}+(1-2x)I^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}I=0,

whose analytic at the origin solution

(1.2)

{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;x\Big)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}(t-1)^{-1/2}(t-x)^{-1/2}dt=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{k}^{2}x^{k}

encodes periods of the Legendre family
y^{2}=t(t-1)(t-x)
.
Dwork used the approximations

\displaystyle F_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{s}-1}\binom{-1/2}{k}^{2}x^{k}\qquad\text{for}\quad s=1,2,\dots,

which are nothing but truncations of the infinite sum in (
1.2
)
and clearly converge to it in the disk
D_{0,1}=\{x\mid|x|_{p}<1\}
,
to show that the uniform limit
F_{s+1}(x)/F_{s}(x^{p})
as
s\to\infty
exists in a larger domain

\mathfrak{D}^{\text{Dw}}
and this limit, dubbed as the “unit root”, corresponds to a root of the
local zeta function of the
x
-fiber in the family.
Dwork’s work boosted the whole body of research in the area; we limit ourselves to
mentioning some recent contributions on the theme
[
AS
,
BV
,
LTYZ
]
.

Interestingly enough, Dwork himself indicates in
[
Dw
]
that he adopts a
similar point of view in the
p
-adic case to the one Igusa had in
[
Ig
]

for modulo
p
solution of (
1.1
).
Namely, the cycles of the elliptic curve
y^{2}=t(t-1)(t-x)
for a given
x
can
be thought of as the local at
x
analytic solutions of the differential equation
(
1.1
). At the same time, Igusa’s modulo
p
solution

\displaystyle g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{(p-1)/2}{k}^{2}x^{k}

of (
1.1
), though indeed coinciding with Dwork’s
F_{1}(x)
modulo
p
,
hints at a somewhat different way for approximating (
1.2
)
p
-adically
through
natural
truncations of the sum.
This recipe seems to escape its own development until very recently
Schechtman and Varchenko gave solutions to general Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov (KZ)
equations modulo
p
in
[
SV2
]
, recovering Igusa’s polynomial as a particular case.
It has been realized in
[
V4
]
that this approach goes in parallel with that of Dwork in

[
Dw
]
; explicitly, the polynomial solutions of hypergeometric equation and of a certain
class of KZ equations modulo
p^{s}
are shown in
[
V4
]
to give rise to
p
-adic
approximations of the corresponding unit roots.
The principal goal of this paper is to prove such expectations, at least for the cases in
which the technicality of proofs do not overshadow the beauty of outcomes.

In this paper we study certain
p^{s}
-approximation polynomials of hypergeometric periods.
We consider an integral of hypergeometric type like in (
1.1
) without specifying the cycle of integration.
For any positive integer
s
we replace the integrand by a polynomial
\Phi_{s}(t,x)
with integer coefficients
called the master polynomial and define the
p^{s}
-approximation polynomial as the coefficient of

t^{p^{s}-1}
in the master polynomial.
This is our
p^{s}
-analog of the initial integral.
In the example of (
1.1
) the master polynomial is

\Phi_{s}(t,x)=t^{(p^{s}-1)/2}(t-1)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}(t-x)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}
and the
p^{s}
-approximation polynomial
is

(1.3)

\displaystyle P_{s}(x)=(-1)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{s}-1)/2}\binom{(p^{s}-1)/2}{k}^{2}x^{k}.

We prove the Dwork-type congruence,

(1.4)

\displaystyle P_{s+1}(x)P_{s-1}(x^{p})\equiv P_{s}(x)P_{s}(x^{p})\pmod{p^{s}},

in Theorem
4.2
. More general
p^{s}
-approximation
constructions are discussed in
[
SV2
,
V4
,
RV1
,
RV2
]
.

In Section
6
we consider the simplest example of the KZ connection. The KZ connections are objects
of conformal field theory, representation theory, enumerative geometry, see
[
KZ
,
EFK
,
MO
]
.
In our example, the KZ connection is identified with the Gauss–Manin connection of
the family
of elliptic curves
y^{2}=(t-z_{1})(t-z_{2})(t-z_{3})
. We study the
p^{s}
-approximation polynomials to the
elliptic period
\int\!dt/y
and show that the
p
-adic KZ connection of our example
has an invariant line subbundle. This is a
p
-adic feature since the corresponding complex KZ connection
has no proper invariant subbundle due to the irreducibility of its monodromy representation.

The invariant subbundles of the KZ connection over
{\mathbb{C}}
usually are related to some additional conformal block
constructions, for example see
[
FSV
,
SV2
,
V3
]
. Apparently our subbundle is of a
different
p
-adic nature.

The results above require proving
p
-adic convergence,
which in turn rests upon establishing certain special congruences.
Considering more general hypergeometric series
F(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)x^{n}

and its
p^{s}
-truncations
F_{s}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{p^{s}-1}A(n)x^{n}
,
Dwork showed that

(1.5)

\displaystyle F_{s+1}(x)F_{s-1}(x^{p})\equiv F_{s}(z)F_{s}(x^{p})\pmod{p^{s}}\quad\text{for}\;s=1,2,\dots,

in
[
Dw
, Theorem 2]
; this allowed him to conclude
the existence of the
p
-adic limit

F_{s+1}(x)/F_{s}(x^{p})
as
s\to\infty
in
[
Dw
, Theorem 3]
.
As an auxiliary component of Dwork’s argument the other set of congruences,
a là Lucas, was used for the sequence of coefficients
A(n)
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{A(n+mp^{s})}{A([n/p]+mp^{s-1})}\equiv\frac{A(n)}{A([n/p])}\pmod{p^{s}}\quad\text{for}\;m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}\;\text{and}\;s=1,2,\dots

(see Corollary 1(ii) on p. 36 in
[
Dw
]
).
These two different-looking families of congruences (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)
are both known as Dwork congruences, and to distinguish between the two
we dub them type I and type II, respectively.
Our Igusa-inspired
p^{s}
-aproximations of solutions of hypergeometric equation, like (
1.3
) above,
are of dual nature. Although congruences (
1.4
) look like congruences (
1.5
) of type I
we may view the sequence
(P_{s}(x))_{s\geqslant 1}
as a subsequence of a suitable
polynomial sequence
A(n;x)
depending
on the extra parameter
x
and satisfying

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{A(n+mp^{s};x)}{A([n/p]+mp^{s-1};x^{p})}\equiv\frac{A(n;x)}{A([n/p];x^{p})}\pmod{p^{s}}\qquad\text{for}\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}\quad\text{and}\quad s=1,2,\dots\,.

Then the restriction of these type II Dwork congruences to the
subsequence
(P_{s}(x))_{s\geqslant 1}
reads as
type I congruences for the
p^{s}
-aproximation polynomials
in parameter
x
.
(We make this explicit in the remark after Theorem
4.4
.)
To summarize, our principal tool for establishing the existence of
p
-adic
convergence are Dwork-type congruences (
1.7
), of which
required type I congruences are particular instances.
General theorems towards Dwork’s congruences of type II
were given by Mellit in
[
Me
]
and, independently, by Samol and van Straten in
[
SvS
]
.
As these results are insufficiently general and only for (
1.6
) rather than (
1.7
),
we extend them further using the method from Mellit’s unpublished preprint
[
Me
]

(see also
[
MV
]
for another application).
Our result based on Mellit’s elegant approach is displayed in Section
2
,
and its power is illustrated by the congruences (
1.4
) from Theorem
4.2

and by several other quite different applications in later sections.

In Section
7
we conjecture some stronger congruences for the polynomials

P_{s}(x)
.

2.
On ghosts

In this paper
p
is an odd prime.

2.1.
Mellit’s theorem

Let
\Lambda(t)
be a Laurent polynomial in variables
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})

with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and constant term
\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda)
.
Assume that the Newton polytope of
\Lambda(t)
contains only one interior point
\{0\}
.

For a tuple
a=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{l-1})
, denote by
l(a)=l
its length.
For two tuples
a
and
b
, the concatenation product
a*b
is the tuple
of length
l(a)+l(b)
obtained by gluing
a
and
b
together.
For
a=(a_{0}
, …,
a_{l-1})
of length
l
, denote by

a^{\prime}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{l-1})
the “derivative” tuple of length
l-1
.
If
a
is a tuple of numbers, denote
|a|=\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}a_{i}
.

For a tuple
m=(m_{0},\dots,m_{l})
of integers from
\{1,\dots,p-1\}

denote by
\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda^{m})
the constant term of the Laurent polynomial

\Lambda(t)^{m_{0}+m_{1}p+m_{2}p^{2}+\dots+m_{l-1}p^{l-1}}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Anton Mellit, 2009,
[
Me
]
, unpublished)
.

Let
a,b,c
be tuples of integers from
\{1,\dots,p-1\}
, where
b,c,a^{\prime}
can be empty, that is, of length 0.
Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda^{a*b})\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda^{a^{\prime}*c})\equiv\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda^{a^{\prime}*b})\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda^{a*c})\pmod{p^{l(a)}}.

We modify the statement and three-page Mellit’s proof of Theorem
2.1

and prove Theorem
2.9
below.

2.2.
Convex polytopes

Given a positive integer
r
we consider convex polytopes, which are convex hulls of finite subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A tuple
(N_{0},N_{1},\dots,N_{l-1})
of convex polytopes is called
\operatorname{admissible}
if for any

0\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant l-1
we have

\displaystyle\big(N_{i}+pN_{i+1}+\dots+p^{j-i}N_{j}\big)\cap p^{j-i+1}{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}=\{0\}.

2.3.
Definition of ghosts

Let
\Lambda(t,z)
be a Laurent polynomial in some variables

t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
,
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{r^{\prime}})

with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. We
define the ghost terms
R_{m}(\Lambda)
,
m\geqslant 0,

as the unique sequence of Laurent polynomials in
t,z
satisfying the following two properties:

(i)

For each
m
we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(t,z)^{p^{m}}=R_{0}(\Lambda)(t^{p^{m}},z^{p^{m}})+R_{1}(\Lambda)(t^{p^{m-1}},z^{p^{m-1}})+\dots+R_{m}(\Lambda)(t,z).

(ii)

For each
m
the coefficients of
R_{m}(\Lambda)(t,z)
are divisible by
p^{m}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Properties (
i
)–(
ii
) recursively determine the polynomials
R_{m}(\Lambda)(t,z)
.
Namely,

(2.2)

\displaystyle R_{m}(\Lambda)(t,z)=\Lambda(t,z)^{p^{m}}-\Lambda(t^{p},z^{p})^{p^{m-1}}\,,\quad R_{0}(\Lambda)(t,z)=\Lambda(t,z).

Let
F(t,z)
be a Laurent polynomial in
t,z
. Let
N(F)
be the Newton polytope of
F(t,z)
with respect to
the
t

variables only
. Clearly, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle N(R_{m}(\Lambda))\subset p^{m}N(\Lambda).

2.4.
Composed ghosts

Let
\lambda=(\Lambda_{0}(t,x),\dots,\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))
be a tuple of Laurent polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
We decompose the product

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}(t,z):=\Lambda_{0}(t,x)(\Lambda_{1}(t,z))^{p}\cdots(\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))^{p^{l-1}}

into the sum of ghost terms of
\Lambda_{0},\dots,\Lambda_{l-1}
. As the result we obtain that

\tilde{\lambda}
is the sum of the products

\displaystyle R_{m,\lambda}(t,z)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle R_{m_{0}}(\Lambda_{0})(t,z)\cdot R_{m_{1}}(\Lambda_{1})(t^{p^{1-m_{1}}},z^{p^{1-m_{1}}})\cdot R_{m_{2}}(\Lambda_{2})(t^{p^{2-m_{2}}},z^{p^{2-m_{2}}})\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots R_{m_{l-1}}(\Lambda_{l-1})(t^{p^{l-1-m_{l-1}}},z^{p^{l-1-m_{l-1}}}),

where
m=(m_{0},\dots,m_{l-1})
runs over the set of all
l
-tuples of integers satisfying
0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant i
.
Clearly, we have

\displaystyle R_{m,\lambda}(t,z)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{|m|}}

and

\displaystyle N(R_{m,\lambda}(t,z))\subset N(\Lambda_{0}(t,z))+pN(\Lambda_{1}(t,z))+\dots+p^{l-1}N(\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z)).

2.5.
Indecomposable tuples

Denote by
S_{k}
the set of all
k
-tuples
m=(m_{0},\dots,m_{k-1})
of integers
such that
0\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant i
.
Put
S=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k}
. A tuple
m\in S
is called

indecomposable
if it cannot be presented as
m^{\prime}*m^{\prime\prime}
for

m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\in S
. Denote by
S_{k}^{\operatorname{ind}}
the set of all indecomposable
k
-tuples and put

S^{\operatorname{ind}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k}^{\operatorname{ind}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
m\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{ind}}
, then
|m|\geqslant k-1
.

Proof.

If
m
is indecomposable, then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
there exists

j\geqslant i
such that
m_{j}>j-i
, i.e.
j\geqslant i>j-m_{j}
. The number
of such
i
for a given
j
is
m_{j}
. The total number of
i
is
k-1
, therefore the sum of
m_{j}
is at least
k-1
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For each
m\in S
, there exist unique indecomposable
m^{1},\dots,m^{r}
such that

m=m^{1}*\dots*m^{r}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
l(m)
. If
l(m)=1
, then
m=(m_{0})=(0)
and
m
is indecomposable.
Let us prove the induction step. Let

\displaystyle m=m^{1}*\dots*m^{r}=n^{1}*\dots*n^{s}

be two decompositions into indecomposable factors. We may assume that
l(n^{s})\geqslant l(m^{r})
.
If
l(n^{s})=l(m^{r})
, then
n^{s}=m^{r}
. In this case we can conclude that

m^{1}*\dots*m^{r-1}=n^{1}*\dots*n^{s-1}
, and the statement follows from the induction hypothesis.
If
l(n^{s})>l(m^{r})
, then the sequence
n^{s}
contains the sequence
m^{r}
as its last

l(m^{r})
-part. This contradicts to the indecomposability of
n^{s}
. The lemma is proved.
∎

2.6.
Polynomials
I_{\lambda}

For an
l
-tuple
\lambda=(\Lambda_{0}(t,z),\Lambda_{1}(t,z),\dots,\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))
of Laurent polynomials with coefficients
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
define

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(t,z)=\sum_{m\in S_{l}^{\operatorname{ind}}}R_{m,\lambda}(t,z).

We have

(2.4)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}(x,z)\equiv 0\qquad\pmod{p^{l-1}}

by Lemma
2.3
and

\displaystyle N(I_{\lambda}(t,z))\subset N(\Lambda_{0}(t,z))+pN(\Lambda_{1}(t,z))+\dots+p^{l-1}N(\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))\,.

Lemma 2.5
.

We have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}(t,z)=\sum_{\lambda=\lambda^{1}*\dots*\lambda^{s}}I_{\lambda^{1}}(t,z)I_{\lambda^{2}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}})\cdots I_{\lambda^{s}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}}),

where the sum is over the set of all possible decompositions of the tuple
\lambda
into a product of tuples.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}(t,z)=\sum_{m\in S_{l}}R_{m,\lambda}(t,z).

For any
m\in S_{l}
, let
m=m^{1}*\dots*m^{s}
be its unique idecomposition into
indecomposable factors. Let
\lambda=\lambda^{1}*\dots*\lambda^{s}
be the corresponding factorization of
the sequence
\lambda
. Then

(2.6)

\displaystyle R_{m,\lambda}(t,z)=R_{m^{1},\lambda^{1}}(t,z)R_{m^{2},\lambda^{2}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}})\cdots R_{m^{s},\lambda^{s}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}}).

This product contributes to the the expansion of the product

(2.7)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda^{1}}(t,z)I_{\lambda^{2}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}})\cdots I_{\lambda^{s}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}})

into the sum, and conversely each summand in the expansion of (
2.7
)
comes from (
2.6
) for a unique indecomposable factorization
m=m^{1}*\dots*m^{s}
.
∎

2.7.
Admissible tuples of Laurent polynomials

Definition 2.6
.

A tuple
\lambda=(\Lambda_{0}(t,z),\Lambda_{1}(t,z),\dots,\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))
of Laurent polynomials
is called
\operatorname{admissible}
if the tuple

(N(\Lambda_{0}(t,z)),N(\Lambda_{1}(t,z)),\dots,N(\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z)))
of its Newton polytopes with respect to variables
t

is admissible.

Denote by
\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda)(z)
the constant term of the Laurent polynomial
\Lambda(t,z)
with respect to
the variables
t
.
The constant term
\operatorname{CT}_{t}(\Lambda)(z)
is a Laurent polynomial in
z
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\lambda=(\Lambda_{0}(t,z),\Lambda_{1}(t,z),\dots,\Lambda_{l-1}(t,z))
be an admissible tuple of Laurent polynomials
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
\lambda=\lambda^{1}*\dots*\lambda^{s}
. Then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}_{t}\!\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{s}I_{\lambda^{i}}\big(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}}\big)\bigg)\!(z)=

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa}=\,\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(I_{\lambda^{i}}(t,z)\big)(z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle N(I_{\lambda^{1}}(t,z))\subset N(\Lambda_{0}(t,z))+pN(\Lambda_{1}(t,z))+\dots+p^{l(\lambda^{1})-1}N(\Lambda_{l(\lambda^{1})-1}(t,z)).

Hence

\displaystyle N(I_{\lambda^{1}}(t,z))\cap p^{l(\lambda^{1})}{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}=\{0\}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}_{t}\!\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{s}I_{\lambda^{i}}(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}})\Big)\!(z)=

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(I_{\lambda^{1}}(t,z)\big)(z)\operatorname{CT}_{t}\!\Big(\prod_{i=2}^{s}I_{\lambda^{i}}\big(t^{p^{l(\lambda^{2})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}},z^{p^{l(\lambda^{2})+\dots+l(\lambda^{i-1})}}\big)\Big)\big(z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}}\big).

Thus by induction on
s
we prove the statement.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

We have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(\tilde{\lambda}\big)(z)=\sum_{\lambda=\lambda^{1}*\dots*\lambda^{s}}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{\lambda^{1}})(z)\cdot\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{\lambda^{2}})(z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})}})\cdots\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{\lambda^{s}})(z^{p^{l(\lambda^{1})+\dots+l(\lambda^{s-1})}}),

where the sum is over the set of all decompositions of
\lambda
into a product of tuples.

2.8.
Dwork congruence for tuples of Laurent polynomials

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
a,b,c
be tuples of Laurent polynomials in
t,z
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
where
b,c,a^{\prime}
can be empty.
Assume that the tuples
a*b,a*c,a^{\prime}*b,a^{\prime}*c
of Laurent polynomials are
admissible. Then

(2.10)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(\widetilde{a*b}\big)\!(z)\,\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(\widetilde{a^{\prime}*c}\big)\!(z^{p})\,\equiv\,\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(\widetilde{a^{\prime}*b}\big)\!(z^{p})\,\operatorname{CT}_{t}\big(\widetilde{a*c}\big)\!(z)\pmod{p^{l(a)}}.

Proof.

The left-hand side and right-hand side of (
2.10
) are

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{{a*b=x^{1}*\dots*x^{q}\atop a^{\prime}*c=y^{1}*\dots*y^{s}}}\prod_{i=1}^{q}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{x^{i}})(z^{p^{l(x^{1})+\dots+l(x^{i-1})}})\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{y^{j}})(z^{p^{1+l(y^{1})+\dots+l(y^{j-1})}})

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{{a^{\prime}*b=x^{1}*\dots*x^{q}\atop a*c=y^{1}*\dots*y^{s}}}\prod_{i=1}^{q}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{x^{i}})(z^{p^{1+l(x^{1})+\dots+l(x^{i-1})}})\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{y^{j}})(z^{p^{l(y^{1})+\dots+l(y^{j-1})}}),

respectively.

\displaystyle l(a)+l(b)+l(c)\geqslant q+s+1.

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{q}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{x^{i}})(z^{p^{l(x^{1})+\dots+l(x^{i-1})}})\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{CT}_{t}(I_{y^{j}})(z^{p^{1+l(y^{1})+\dots+l(y^{j-1})}}),

the summand corresponding to the presentation

,
a^{\prime}*c=y^{1}*\dots*y^{s}

.

There exist indices

and

such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle l(x^{1})+\dots+l(x^{i})=l(y^{1})+\dots+l(y^{j})+1\leqslant l(a).

Proof.

If
l(x^{1})=1
, then

and
j=0
are the required indices.

Assume that

and the required

,

do not exist.
Then each number in

cannot be represented simultaneously as

and

.
Therefore the sum of the total number of

, such that

, and the total number of

, such that

is at most

. The number of remaining

is at most

and the number of remaining

and

∎

Now we return to the remaining summand (

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}a^{\prime}*b=y^{1}*\dots*y^{j}*x^{i+1}*\dots*x^{q},\qquad a*c=x^{1}*\dots*x^{i}*y^{j+1}*\dots*x^{s},

3.
p^{s}
-Approximation of a hypergeometric integral

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
be rational numbers with
|\alpha|_{p}=|\beta|_{p}=|\gamma|_{p}=1
.
Consider a hypergeometric integral

(3.1)

\displaystyle I^{(C)}(x)=\int_{C}t^{\alpha}(t-1)^{\beta}(t-x)^{\gamma}dt

where
C\subset{\mathbb{C}}-\{0,1,x\}
is a contour on which the integrand takes its initial value when
t
encircles
C
.
The function
I^{(C)}(x)
satisfies the hypergeometric differential equation

(3.2)

\displaystyle x(1-x)I^{\prime\prime}+((\alpha+\beta+2\gamma)x-(\alpha+\gamma))I^{\prime}-\gamma(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)I=0.

This follows from Stokes’ theorem and the following identity of differential forms. Denote

\Phi(t,x)=t^{\alpha}(t-1)^{\beta}(t-x)^{\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=x(1-x)\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+((\alpha+\beta+2\gamma)x-(\alpha+\gamma))\frac{d}{dx}-\gamma(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1).

Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle d_{t}\Big(\gamma\frac{t(t-1)}{t-x}\Phi(t,x)\Big)=\mathcal{D}\,\Phi(t,x)dt.

The differential equation (
3.2
) turns into the standard hypergeometric differential equation

(3.4)

\displaystyle x(1-x)I^{\prime\prime}+(c-(a+b+1)x)I^{\prime}-abI=0

if
\alpha=a-c
,
\beta=c-b-1
,
\gamma=-a
. For a suitable choice of
C
and multiplication of the integral by a constant,
the integral in (
3.1
) can be expanded as a power series

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;x)={}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\begin{matrix}a,\,b\\
c\end{matrix}\biggm|x\biggr)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{k!(c)_{k}}x^{k}\,.

Here

(a)_{n}=\Gamma(a+n)/\Gamma(a)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(a+k)
stands for Pochhammer’s symbol.

We consider the following
p^{s}
-approximation of the integral in (
3.1
).
Given a positive integer
s
, let
1\leqslant\alpha_{s},\beta_{s},\gamma_{s}\leqslant p^{s}
be the unique positive integers
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}\equiv\alpha,\quad\beta_{s}\equiv\beta,\quad\gamma_{s}\equiv\gamma\qquad\pmod{p^{s}}.

Define the

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(t,x)=t^{\alpha_{s}}(t-1)^{\beta_{s}}(t-x)^{\gamma_{s}}

and the
p^{s}
-approximation polynomial

as the coefficient of
t^{p^{s}-1}

in the master polynomial
\Phi_{s}(t,x)
. Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle I_{s}(x)=(-1)^{\alpha_{s}+\beta_{s}+\gamma_{s}-p^{s}+1}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=\alpha_{s}+\beta_{s}+\gamma_{s}-p^{s}+1}\binom{\beta_{s}}{k_{1}}\binom{\gamma_{s}}{k_{2}}x^{k_{2}}.

The polynomial
I_{s}(x)
has integer coefficients.

Theorem 3.1
.

The polynomial
I_{s}(x)
is a solution of the hypergeometric differential equation
(
3.2
) modulo
p^{s}
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}I_{s}(x)\,\in\,p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]\,.

Proof.

The theorem follows from formula (

).
∎

For

For

Denote

6.
KZ equations

6.1.
KZ equations

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple Lie algebra with an invariant scalar product.
The
Casimir element
is

\displaystyle\Omega={\sum}_{i}\,h_{i}\otimes h_{i}\ \ \in\ {\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},

where
(h_{i})\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is an orthonormal basis.
Let
V=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
be
a tensor product of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
a nonzero number.
The
KZ equations
is the system of differential
equations on a
V
-valued function
I(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\kappa}\,{\sum}_{j\neq i}\,\frac{\Omega_{i,j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\Omega_{i,j}:V\to V
is the Casimir operator acting in the
i
th and
j
th tensor factors,
see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
.

This system is a system of Fuchsian first order
linear differential equations.
The equations are defined on the complement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
to the union of all diagonal hyperplanes.

The object of our discussion is the following particular case. We consider
the following system of differential and algebraic equations
for a column
3
-vector
I=(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})
depending on variables
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{\Omega_{12}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}+\frac{\Omega_{13}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}\Big)I,\qquad\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{2}}={\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{\Omega_{21}}{z_{2}-z_{1}}+\frac{\Omega_{23}}{z_{2}-z_{3}}\Big)I,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{\Omega_{31}}{z_{3}-z_{1}}+\frac{\Omega_{32}}{z_{3}-z_{2}}\Big)I,\qquad\quad 0=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where
\Omega_{ij}=\Omega_{ji}
and

\displaystyle\Omega_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\
1&-1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\Omega_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad\Omega_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&-1&1\\
0&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Denote

For

and

denote

\displaystyle f_{s}(z)=T_{s}(z)\Big/T_{s-1}(z^{p}),\qquad\eta^{(i)}_{s}(z)=\frac{\partial T_{s}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\Big/T_{s}(z),\qquad\eta^{(ij)}_{s}(z)=\frac{\partial^{2}T_{s}}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}(z)\Big/T_{s}(z).

Theorem 6.8
.

For
s\geqslant 1
the rational functions

\eta^{(i)}_{s}(z)
and
\eta^{(ij)}_{s}(z)
are regular on
\mathfrak{D}
.
The sequences of rational functions

(\eta^{(i)}_{s}(z))_{s\geqslant 1}
and
(\eta^{(ij)}_{s}(z))_{\geqslant 1}

converge uniformly on

to analytic functions.
If
\eta^{(j)}
and
\eta^{(ij)}
denote the corresponding limits, then

(6.21)

\displaystyle\eta^{(1)}+\eta^{(2)}+\eta^{(3)}=0,

(6.22)

\displaystyle\eta^{(j1)}+\eta^{(j2)}+\eta^{(j3)}=0,\qquad j=1,2,3,

(6.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\eta^{(i)}=\eta^{(ji)}-\eta^{(i)}\eta^{(j)}.

Proof.

Denote

\delta_{i}=z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
. By Theorem
6.7
the sequence
(f_{s})
uniformly converges to
the analytic function

on
\mathfrak{D}

Therefore, the sequence of the derivatives

Hence the sequence

to the function

At the same time

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{i}f_{s}}{f_{s}}(z)=\frac{\delta_{i}T_{s}}{T_{s}}(z)-p\,\frac{\delta_{i}T_{s-1}}{T_{s-1}}(z^{p})

and, more generally,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{i}f_{s-k}}{f_{s-k}}(z^{p^{k}})=\frac{\delta_{i}T_{s-k}}{T_{s-k}}(z^{p^{k}})-p\,\frac{\delta_{i}T_{s-k-1}}{T_{s-k-1}}(z^{p^{k+1}})\qquad\text{for}\quad k=0,1,\dots,s.

\Phi_{s}(t+z_{3},z)=\big((t-(z_{1}-z_{3}))(t-(z_{2}-z_{3}))t\big)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}
.
We have
U_{1}(z)=T_{1}(z)

\pmod{p}
, by Lucas’ theorem.
Similarly to Theorem
6.5
we conclude that

(6.27)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}U_{s+1}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\,U_{s-1}(z_{1}^{p},z_{2}^{p},z_{3}^{p})\,\equiv\,U_{s}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\,U_{s}(z_{1}^{p},z_{2}^{p},z_{3}^{p})\pmod{p^{s}}.

Hence the sequence
\big(\frac{U_{s+1}(z)}{U_{s}(z^{p})}\big)_{s\geqslant 1}
uniformly converges to an analytic function
on the domain
\mathfrak{D}
defined in (
6.18
).
The vector-valued polynomial

\nabla U_{s}(z)=\big(\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{1}},\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{2}},\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{3}}\big)

is a solution modulo
p^{s}
of the KZ equations (
6.1
); see
[
V4

,
cf. the proof of Theorem
6.3
. Consider the function

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}=(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\mu^{(3)}):=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\nabla U_{s}}{U_{s}}

defined on the same domain
\mathfrak{D}
.
Similarly to the proofs of Theorems
6.8
–
6.11
we conclude that the function

\vec{\mu}(z)
is nonzero on
\mathfrak{D}
and its values span an analytic line subbundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\bigcup\nolimits_{a\in\mathfrak{D}}\,\mathcal{M}_{a}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}

of the trivial bundle
W\times\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}
; here
\mathcal{M}_{a}\subset W

\vec{\mu}(a)

The line subbundle
\mathcal{M}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}

Theorem 6.12
.

The line bundles
\mathcal{M}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}
coincide.

Proof.

The proof rests on the two lemmas.

Lemma 6.13
.

The line bundles
\mathcal{M}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathcal{L}\,\to\,\mathfrak{D}
coincide,
if there is
a\in\mathfrak{D}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{L}_{a}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{L}_{a}
for some
a\in\mathfrak{D}
. Then

\mathcal{M}_{z}=\mathcal{L}_{s}
in some neighborhood of
a
,
since locally the subbundles are generated by the values of the solutions with the same
initial condition at
z=a
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{z}=\mathcal{L}_{z}
on
\mathfrak{D}
.
∎

Lemma 6.14
.

For
i=1,2,3
the functions

\frac{\partial T_{s}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\big/T_{s}(z)
and
\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\big/U_{s}(z)

are equal on the line

,

.

Hence
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{M}_{z}=\mathcal{L}_{z}

.
∎

6.8.2.
Line subbundle
\mathcal{N}\to\hat{\mathfrak{D}}

Let
\omega(x)=F^{\prime}(x)/F(x)
, where
F(x)={}_{2}F_{1}(1/2,1/2;1;x)

We have

\omega(x)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{P_{s}^{\prime}(x)}{P_{s}(x)}
on

Introduce new variables

(6.28)

\displaystyle u_{1}=z_{1}-z_{3},\qquad u_{2}=\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}},\qquad u_{3}=z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3},

and a vector-valued function

(6.29)

\displaystyle\vec{\omega}(u)=\frac{1}{u_{1}}\big(\!-1/2-\omega(u_{2})u_{2},\,\omega(u_{2}),\,1/2+\omega(u_{2})(u_{2}-1)\big)\,.

Define

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{0}=\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{3}\ |\ z_{i}\neq z_{j}\,\ \forall i\neq j\}.

For any
\sigma=(i,j,k)\in S_{3}
define

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaa}\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{1}^{\sigma}=\Big\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{0}\ \Big|\ \ \frac{z_{j}-z_{k}}{z_{i}-z_{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ \Big|g\Big(\frac{z_{j}-z_{k}}{z_{i}-z_{k}}\Big)\Big|_{p}=1\Big\},

\displaystyle\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{2}^{\sigma}=\Big\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{0}\ \Big|\ \frac{z_{i}-z_{k}}{z_{j}-z_{k}}\in\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{1}^{\sigma}\Big\},\qquad\hat{\mathfrak{D}}^{\sigma}=\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{1}^{\sigma}\cup\hat{\mathfrak{D}}_{2}^{\sigma},\qquad\hat{\mathfrak{D}}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}\hat{\mathfrak{D}}^{\sigma},

where
g(\lambda)
is the Igusa polynomial in (
4.20
).

Using Dwork’s results in
[
Dw
]
,
it is shown in
[
V4
, Appendix]
that the values of the analytic continuation of the function
\vec{\omega}(u)

generate a line bundle
\mathcal{N}\to\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
invariant with respect to the KZ connection.

Theorem 6.15
.

The line bundles
\mathcal{M}\to\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathcal{N}\to\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
coincide on

\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
.

Thus we identified the line bundles

\mathcal{L}\to\mathfrak{D}
,
\mathcal{M}\to\mathfrak{D}
, and
\mathcal{N}\to\hat{\mathfrak{D}}

over
\mathfrak{D}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{D}}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle U_{s}(z)=(z_{1}-z_{3})^{(p^{s}-1)/2}P_{s}\big(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}\big),\qquad\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{2}}=(z_{1}-z_{3})^{(p^{s}-1)/2-1}P_{s}^{\prime}\big(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}\big)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{1}}=\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}\,\operatorname{const}\,(z_{1}-z_{3})^{(p^{s}-1)/2-1}P_{s}\big(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}\big)-\operatorname{const}\,(z_{1}-z_{3})^{(p^{s}-1)/2-1}\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}P_{s}^{\prime}\big(\frac{z_{2}-z_{3}}{z_{1}-z_{3}}\big).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{U_{s}(z)}\Big(\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{1}},\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{2}},\frac{\partial U_{s}}{\partial z_{3}}\Big)=\frac{1}{u_{1}}\Big(\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-u_{2}\frac{P_{s}^{\prime}(u_{2})}{P_{s}(u_{2})},\,\frac{P_{s}^{\prime}(u_{2})}{P_{s}(u_{2})},\,-\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}+(u_{2}-1)\frac{P_{s}^{\prime}(u_{2})}{P_{s}(u_{2})}\Big).

Clearly the limit of this vector equals
\vec{\omega}(u)
as

7.

7.1.
Conjectural stronger congruences for
\bar{P}_{s}(x)

By Theorem
4.2
we have for polynomials
\bar{P}_{s}(x):=(-1)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}P_{s}(x)
:

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{4}(x)\bar{P}_{2}(x^{p})-\bar{P}_{3}(x)\bar{P}_{3}(x^{p})\equiv 0\pmod{p^{3}}.

In particular, for the coefficient of
x^{N_{0}+N_{1}p+N_{2}p^{2}+N_{3}p^{3}}

in
\bar{P}_{4}(x)\bar{P}_{2}(x^{p})-\bar{P}_{3}(x)\bar{P}_{3}(x^{p})
we have

(7.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\scriptstyle k_1+l_1=N_1}{\scriptstyle k_2+l_2=N_2}}\bigg(\binom{(p^{4}-1)/2}{N_{0}+k_{1}p+k_{2}p^{2}+N_{3}p^{3}}^{2}\binom{(p^{2}-1)/2}{l_{1}+l_{2}p}^{2}-

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaaaa}-\binom{(p^{3}-1)/2}{N_{0}+k_{1}p+k_{2}p^{2}}^{2}\binom{(p^{3}-1)/2}{l_{1}+l_{2}p+N_{3}p^{2}}^{2}\,\bigg)\equiv 0\pmod{p^{3}}.

Computer experiments show that this sum can be split into subsums with at most
four terms so that each a subsum is divisible by
p^{3}
.
More precisely, let
0\leqslant a,b,\,c,c^{\prime},d,d^{\prime}\leqslant p-1
be integers.
Define

\displaystyle A(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d,d^{\prime})=\binom{(p^{4}-1)/2}{a+cp+dp^{2}+bp^{3}}^{2}\binom{(p^{2}-1)/2}{c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}p}^{2}-\binom{(p^{3}-1)/2}{a+cp+dp^{2}}^{2}\binom{(p^{3}-1)/2}{c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}p+bp^{2}}^{2}

and

(7.2)

\displaystyle B(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d,d^{\prime})=\operatorname{Sym}A(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d,d^{\prime}):=

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}:=A(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d,d^{\prime})+A(a,b;c^{\prime},c;d,d^{\prime})+A(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d^{\prime},d)+A(a,b;c^{\prime},c;d^{\prime},d).

We expect that
the integer
B(a,b;c,c^{\prime};d,d^{\prime})
is divisible by
p^{3}
.

More generally, define

\displaystyle k=(k^{(1)},\dots,k^{(s)}),\qquad k^{(i)}=(k^{(i)}_{1},k^{(i)}_{2}),

\displaystyle A(a,b;k)=\binom{(p^{s+2}-1)/2}{a+\sum_{i=1}^{s}k^{(i)}_{1}p^{i}+bp^{s+1}}^{2}\binom{(p^{s}-1)/2}{\sum_{i=1}^{s}k^{(i)}_{2}p^{i-1}}^{2}-\binom{(p^{s+1}-1)/2}{a+\sum_{i=1}^{s}k^{(i)}_{1}p^{i}}^{2}\binom{(p^{s+1}-1)/2}{\sum_{i=1}^{s}k^{(i)}_{2}p^{i-1}+bp^{s}}^{2}.

Set

\displaystyle B(a,b;k)=\operatorname{Sym}A(a,b;k),

where
\operatorname{Sym}
denotes the symmetrization with respect to the index
j

in
k^{(i)}_{j}
in each group
k^{(i)}=(k^{(i)}_{1},k^{(i)}_{2})
. Thus the symmetrization has
2^{s}
summands; the case
s=2
of this symmetrization is displayed in (
7.2
).

Conjecture 7.1
.

The integer
B(a,b;k)
is divisible by
p^{s+1}
.

This conjecture is supported by computer experiments and is checked for
s=1
using
[
Gr
]
.

The soaring kite: a tale of two punctured tori Preprint: UUITP–03/24 BONN-TH-2024-01
Mathieu Giroux, Andrzej Pokraka, Franziska Porkert, Yoann Sohnle

Abstract
We consider the 5-mass kite family of self-energy Feynman integrals and present a systematic approach for constructing an ε \varepsilon -form basis, along with its differential equation pulled back onto the moduli space of two tori. Each torus is associated with one of the two distinct elliptic curves this family depends on. We demonstrate how the locations of relevant punctures, which are required to parametrize the full image of the kinematic space onto this moduli space, can be extracted from integrals over maximal cuts. A boundary value is provided such that the differential equation is systematically solved in terms of iterated integrals over g g -kernels and modular forms. Then, the numerical evaluation of the master integrals is discussed, and important challenges in that regard are emphasized. In an appendix, we introduce new relations between g g -kernels.

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\psi_{1}}\int_{e_{1}}^{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}\quad\text{mod}\quad\Lambda_{(1,\tau)}

\displaystyle=\pm\left[e^{i[\arg(x-e_{1})-\arg(x-e_{2})]}\frac{F(\sqrt{u_{x}},k^{2})}{2K(k^{2})}+\frac{\tau}{2}\right]\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathbbm{R}\,,

(17)

where

\displaystyle u_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{x-e_{2}}{x-e_{1}}\frac{e_{1}-e_{3}}{e_{2}-e_{3}}\,,

(18)

and
F
is the incomplete elliptic integral of first kind

F\left(\arcsin\sqrt{u},k^{2}\right)=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{u}}\dfrac{\text{d}t}{\sqrt{(1-t^{2})(1-k^{2}t^{2})}}\,.

(19)

It is also important to note that one needs to use a branch choice

\displaystyle\frac{y}{|y|}=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}-1&x\leq e_{1}\text{ or }x>e_{4}\,,\\
-i&e_{1}<x_{1}\leq e_{2}\,,\\
1&e_{2}<x_{1}\leq e_{3}\,,\\
i&e_{3}<x_{1}\leq e_{4}\,,\end{array}\right.

that is compatible with the root ordering
e_{i}<e_{i+1}

when comparing the integral definition of Abel’s map to the definition in terms of incomplete elliptic integrals.

Now, because
\tau
is not fixed and there are multiple punctures, the functions and differential forms that appear in this work are actually defined on the
moduli space
of the torus with
n
marked points, denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}
. Roughly speaking, each point in
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}
corresponds to a tuple
(\tau,\mathbf{z})
, where
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, which parameterizes a genus-1 surface with
n
marked points. However, the proper definition of this space is quite involved and is not essential to understand the discussion that follows; hence, it is not defined here. Interested readers are referred to
(
weinzierl2022feynman
, app. F)
for more details.

It is often useful to organize functions (and differential forms) on
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}
by
modular weight
.
A function
f(z,\tau)
of modular weight
k+2
is defined to be one such that

f(z,\tau)\mapsto(c\tau+d)^{k+2}f(z,\tau)\,,

(24)

under the action of
\text{SL}(2,\mathbbm{Z})
on
\mathbbm{T}
, which takes
\tau\mapsto\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
and
z\mapsto\frac{z}{c\tau+d}
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbbm{Z}
and
ad-bc=1
.
In particular,
\text{d}\tau
and
\text{d}z|_{\tau=\text{cte}}
are modular forms of weight 0 and 1 respectively.
Quasi-modular forms are a slight generalization of modular forms that will appear in later sections of this text.
A quasi-modular form of weight
k
and depth
p
is a function whose image under a modular (
\text{SL}(2,\mathbbm{Z})
) transformation takes the form

\displaystyle f(z,\tau)\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{p}(c\tau+d)^{k+2}\left(\frac{cz}{cz+d}\right)^{i}f_{i}(z,\tau)\,,

(25)

where the functions
f_{i}
are holomorphic on the upper-half plane
\mathbbm{H}
.

Review on differential forms on the torus

In this section, we outline the definitions and additional conventions essential for the discussion that follows. We start with the aforementioned
Kronecker-Eisenstein form

;
weinzierl2022feynman

\omega_{k}(z,\tau)=(2\pi)^{2-k}\Big(g^{(k-1)}(z,\tau)\text{d}z+(k-1)g^{(k)}(z,\tau)\frac{\text{d}\tau}{2\pi i}\Big)\,,

;

Importantly, the

It is not hard to see that this implies that

is a quasi-modular form of weight

(according to our definition of modular weight in (

we have the following useful expansions around

g^{(4)}(z,\tau)&=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{45}-\frac{16\pi^{4}}{3}\cos(2\pi z)q^{2}+\mathcal{O}(q^{4})\,.\end{split}

Clearly, for

In fact, one can show that

Finally, for some entries of

\displaystyle\eta_{4}(\tau)

5.2
All poles from the diagonal dlog terms and connection to Landau

In order to pull
\mathbf{B}
back to the torus, we need to construct an ansatz of differential 1-forms and know where the entries of
\mathbf{B}
can diverge on the torus.
Our strategy is to identify a (optimally
minimal
) set of matrix elements
B_{ij}
that contains all possible poles in a way that is easily mapped to the torus.
In our case, we find that this is a property satisfied by the diagonal dlog terms of
\mathbf{B}
.
7
7

7

This observation is motivated by the naive expectation that the diagonal should “know” about all off-diagonal singularities, which could, in principle, be made more precise using the language of spectral sequences.

The dlog terms are modular invariant objects that often pull back to specific linear combinations of
\omega_{2}(z,\tau)
. An important example is

\Omega_{2}(z,\tau)=\omega_{2}(z,\tau)-2\omega_{2}(z,2\tau)\,.

(107)

Note that the
g
-kernels with the argument
2\tau
(hidden in
\omega_{2}(z,2\tau)
) are required to make the above expressions modular invariant and do not change the location of any singular points on the torus.
Although
g^{(k)}(z,2\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\left[g^{(k)}(\frac{z}{2},\tau)+g^{(k)}(\frac{z+1}{2},\tau)\right]
(see
(
weinzierl2022feynman
, §13)
; alternatively, this can be extracted from the formula described in appendix
C
) these
g
-kernels are singular only on a subset of the points where
g
-kernels with argument
\tau
are singular.

To determine what
z
is, given a dlog entry of
\mathbf{B}
, it is instructive to compare the
q
-expansion of
\omega_{2}(z,\tau)
with that of the entry. On one hand, we have

\omega_{2}(z,\tau)=\text{d}\log\dfrac{\theta_{1}(\pi z,\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}=\text{d}\log\sin(\pi z)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})\,,

(108)

where
\eta(\tau)
is the Dedekind eta function. This allows us to rewrite any linear combination of
\omega_{2}
’s at leading order in
q
as

\sum_{j}c_{j}\,\omega_{2}(f_{j}(\boldsymbol{z}),\tau)=\text{d}\log\Big(\prod_{j}\sin^{c_{j}}\pi f_{j}(\boldsymbol{z})\Big)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})\,,

(109)

where
c_{j}\in\mathbbm{Q}+i\mathbbm{Q}
. On the other hand, the kinematic dlog entries we wish to pull back factorize as

\text{d}\log\Big(\dfrac{A_{i_{1}}\ldots A_{i_{n}}}{A_{j_{1}}\ldots A_{j_{n}}}\Big)=\text{d}\log A_{i_{1}}+\ldots+\text{d}\log A_{i_{n}}-\text{d}\log A_{j_{1}}-\ldots-\text{d}\log A_{j_{n}}\,.

(110)

Here, each algebraic function
A_{i}
can be pulled back separately onto the torus (i.e., expressed as 0-forms in terms of
\tau
and the
z
variables) before being expanded in
q
. Then, expressing the leading-order terms of these expansions as the dlog of a product of sines gives an efficient way of reading off the
Z
-arguments
f_{j}(\boldsymbol{z})
appearing in (
109
).

Crucially, we observe that applying this procedure to all diagonal dlog terms is
sufficient
to find all the arguments needed to express the matrix
\mathbf{B}
completely in terms of modular and Kronecker-Eisenstein forms.

Landau analysis of the
{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
-sunrise subtopology

For an integral family such as the sunrise, all of the elliptic structure appears in the top sector.
When the kinematics is localized to the Landau variety of the top sector,
the elliptic geometry necessarily degenerates, meaning that
\tau^{\alpha}\to 0
or
i\infty
(with
\alpha={{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}},{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(345)}}
).
In fact, it is fairly easy to show that the elliptic modulus

and its complement
1-k^{2}_{\alpha}
are rational functions of the polynomials that define the Landau variety for the corresponding sunrise diagram; when the kinematics is restricted to a subvariety in
\mathcal{K}
defined by any of these polynomials,

k^{2}_{\alpha}\to 0
or
1
and
\tau^{\alpha}\to i\infty
or
0
.
In the following, we focus (without loss of generality) on
\alpha={{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
and suppress this label on the variables.

Near any (
{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
-sunrise) Landau singularity where
\tau\to i\infty
, the polynomials in the original kinematic variables

that describe this Landau singularity are given by the leading term in the corresponding

-series expansion.
For example, we have

\displaystyle X_{0}

\displaystyle=16q^{2}\sin^{2}(\pi z_{1})\sin^{2}(\pi z_{2})\to 0\,,

(111a)

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}(\pi z_{1})\sin^{-2}\big(\pi(z_{1}+z_{2})\big)\,,

(111b)

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}(\pi z_{2})\sin^{-2}\big(\pi(z_{1}+z_{2})\big)\,,

(111c)

when
\tau\to i\infty
(or equivalently

q\to 0
).
Recalling that
z_{3}=1-z_{1}-z_{2}\simeq z_{1}+z_{2}\text{ mod }\Lambda_{(1,\tau)}
, we see that the subleading Landau equations
X_{i=1,2}=0,\infty
are satisfied whenever the
{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
-punctures vanish in the

In summary, we find that the subleading Landau singularities

of the sunrise integral are associated to the points
(\tau,z_{i=1,2})\to(i\infty,0)
in
\mathcal{M}_{1,3}^{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
.

The above analysis of the
\tau\to i\infty
limit is the first step towards constructing an ansatz for the pullback of a kinematic quantity to
\mathcal{M}_{1,3}^{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
.
For example, from (

), we know that the form
\text{d}\log(X_{1})
should have simple poles whenever

and

vanish.
Therefore, we conclude that

and
z_{1}+z_{2}
should appear in the arguments of the
g
-kernels in our ansatz.
Indeed, one finds

\displaystyle\text{d}\log(X_{1})=2\Omega_{2}(z_{1}+z_{2},\tau)-2\Omega_{2}(z_{1},\tau)\,,

(112)

where each

is modular invariant.

In contrast to the subleading Landau singularities, the (pseudo-)normal threshold singularities
(\pm\pm\pm)=1\pm\sqrt{X_{0}}\pm\sqrt{X_{1}}\pm\sqrt{X_{2}}=0
(c.f., (
90

\tau\to 0
(or equivalently

)

Since this limit defines the boundary of the analyticity domain of

, studying the (pseudo-)normal thresholds on
\mathcal{M}_{1,3}^{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
is, strictly speaking, only possible after analytic continuation of

via a modular

-transformation (
\tau\mapsto-1/\tau
).
8
8

8

Since the (psudo-)normal thresholds correspond to

However, even if we cannot access these singularities without doing a

is modular invariant.
For example, consider the complementary elliptic modulus

.
It is a rational function of all the polynomials defining the normal and pseudo-normal thresholds:

\displaystyle 1-k^{2}=\frac{(--+)(-+-)(+--)(+++)}{(---)(-++)(+-+)(++-)}\,.

(113)

Multiplying the numerator and the denominator of (

-sunrise.
By pulling back

are embedded into
\mathcal{M}^{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}_{1,3}

As outlined earlier, to pull back this differential form, we start by analyzing the

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to i\infty}T=\left[2\sin(\pi z_{1})\sin(\pi z_{2})\sin^{-1}\big(\pi(z_{1}+z_{2})\big)\right]^{4}.

This suggests that we should use the

,
z_{2}
and

Fixing the coefficients using the PSLQ algorithm, we find

where

-part.
It is interesting that the

and

In the end, the

The major difference between

and

Indeed, the subleading Landau singularities occur at

Landau analysis of the
(1234)
-eyeball (and kite)

For integral families such as the eyeball and kite diagrams, the elliptic structure is inherited from a subtopology.
Thus, it is no longer obvious that the pullback of the entire Landau variety corresponds to the degenerate limits
\tau^{\alpha}\to i\infty
or
\tau^{\alpha}\to 0
; components not associated with the elliptic subtopology could be missing.
This opens up the interesting possibility that part of the Landau variety pulls back to
finite
(nonzero)
\tau^{\alpha}
, which we explore further below.

While we will focus on the Landau singularities of the
(1234)
-eyeball here, the discussion carries over straightforwardly to the kite.
Once again, we suppress the label
{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}
for readability.

Even if there are some components of the Landau variety that are not associated with the
\tau^{\alpha}\to i\infty
limit, the
q^{\alpha}
-series is still extremely useful for determining the location of Landau singularities as illustrated in (
115
).
For example, using the
\tau\to i\infty
limit of
X_{4}
in (
123
), we find that the subleading Landau equation
X_{4}=0
is associated with the vanishing of
z_{1}
,
z_{1}+z_{2}\pm z_{4}
and
z_{1}\pm z_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\text{d}\log X_{4}

\displaystyle=2\omega_{2}\left(z_{1},2\tau\right){+}2\omega_{2}\left(z_{2},2\tau\right){-}2\omega_{2}\left(z_{1},\tau\right){+}\omega_{2}\big(z_{1}{+}\tfrac{z_{2}+z_{4}}{2},\tau\big){+}\omega_{2}\left(z_{1}{+}\tfrac{z_{2}-z_{4}}{2},\tau\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\omega_{2}\left(\tfrac{z_{2}-z_{4}}{2},\tau\right)-\omega_{2}\left(\tfrac{z_{2}+z_{4}}{2},\tau\right)-2\omega_{2}\left(z_{1}+z_{2},2\tau\right)-2\eta_{2}\left(\tau\right)\,.

(117)

Note that the expression above is manifestly modular invariant on the left-hand side, even though it is not expressed in terms of
\Omega_{2}
’s.

At this stage, one may wonder why we pulled back the complicated polynomial

in (

.
Although we can use PSLQ to match the

is needed to match the

We report that naively adding Kronecker-Eisenstein forms evaluated at rational points

to the PSLQ ansatz does not seem to solve this issue.
In fact, it might be impossible to pull back the
\text{d}\tau
-part of
\text{d}\log(\pm\pm\pm)
using only rational coefficients in the ansatz.
Therefore, it currently appears that only the dlog of
special
polynomials can be easily pulled back to
\mathcal{M}^{{\color[rgb]{0.72,0,0}(123)}}_{1,3}

Interestingly, we can also pull back functions or 0-forms.
For example, using (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X_{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial z_{4}}{\partial X_{4}}\bigg[g^{(1)}(z_{2}{+}z_{4},\tau){-}g^{(1)}(z_{2}{-}z_{4},\tau){+}g^{(1)}(z_{1}{+}\tfrac{z_{2}{+}z_{4}}{2},\tau){-}g^{(1)}(z_{1}{+}\tfrac{1}{2}(z_{2}{-}z_{4}),\tau)\bigg]\,.

could be predicted simply by requiring modular invariance; one would find that the coefficients of a

-kernel ansatz needs to transform like the derivative of a puncture.
Furthermore, note that while

and

appear as

Z
-arguments in (

This suggests that either much of the singularity structure of the

or that there are some nontrivial cancellations in the pullback of

Moreover, the pullback of 0-forms in terms of

That is, we are unaware of other explicit results that convert rational functions of kinematics

into linear combinations of
g

It would be interesting if such expressions could help sharpen statements about the pullback of the Landau variety.

In principle, the list of

This is essentially what we have done since the singularities appearing in the pullback of

However, there is an important advantage to the way the Landau singularities are organized in the dlog-forms on the diagonal of

Identifying and factorizing the square-root singularities of a Feynman integral is a well-known and difficult problem that is the subject of ongoing research (see

where

in (

Interestingly, we find that it is much harder to derive the pullback of

Of course, we can simply square both sides of

.

On the other hand, it is not too difficult to construct the pullback of

\displaystyle\text{d}\log(\lambda_{134})

\displaystyle=2\Omega_{2}(z_{1},\tau)+\Omega_{2}(z_{2},\tau)-2\Omega_{2}(z_{1}{+}z_{2},\tau)+\Omega_{2}(z_{4},\tau)-\omega_{2}(z_{2},\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad-\omega_{2}(z_{4},\tau)+2\omega_{2}(\tfrac{z_{2}{+}z_{4}}{2},\tau)+2\omega_{2}(\tfrac{z_{2}{-}z_{4}}{2},\tau)-4\eta_{2}(\tau)\,.

In this regard, potential generalizations to

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the first analytic results for the 5-mass kite family of Feynman integrals near two space-time dimensions using the method of differential equations.

Our approach involved investigating the relationship between two elliptic curves associated with the sunrise subsectors of the 5-mass kite family of Feynman integrals. In particular, we introduced a method to construct an
\varepsilon

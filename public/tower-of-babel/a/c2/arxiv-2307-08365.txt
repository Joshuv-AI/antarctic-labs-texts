Statistical Mechanics of Learning via Reverberation in Bidirectional Associative Memories
Martino Salomone Centonze, Ido Kanter, Adriano Barra

Abstract
We study bi-directional associative neural networks that, exposed to noisy examples of an extensive number of random archetypes, learn the latter (with or without the presence of a teacher) when the supplied information is enough : in this setting, learning is heteroassociative –involving couples of patterns– and it is achieved by reverberating the information depicted from the examples through the layers of the network. By adapting Guerra’s interpolation technique, we provide a full statistical mechanical picture of supervised and unsupervised learning processes (at the replica symmetric level of description) obtaining analytically phase diagrams, thresholds for learning, a picture of the ground-state in plain agreement with Monte Carlo simulations and signal-to-noise outcomes. In the large dataset limit, the Kosko storage prescription as well as its statistical mechanical picture provided by Kurchan, Peliti, and Saber in the eighties is fully recovered. Computational advantages in dealing with information reverberation, rather than storage, are discussed for natural test cases. In particular, we show how this network admits an integral representation in terms of two coupled restricted Boltzmann machines, whose hidden layers are entirely built of by grand-mother neurons, to prove that by coupling solely these grand-mother neurons we can correlate the patterns they are related to: it is thus possible to recover Pavlov’s Classical Conditioning by adding just one synapse among the correct grand-mother neurons (hence saving an extensive number of these links for further information storage w.r.t. the classical autoassociative setting).

2
The dataset, the neural network model and the training protocols

Let us briefly introduce the Bidirectional Associative Memories (BAM)
[
28
]
.

Consider a shallow Hebbian network made of just two layers of neurons, the former built of by
N
binary neurons
\sigma_{i},\ i\in(1,...,N)
the latter by
\overline{N}
binary neurons
\tau_{k},\ k\in(1,...,\overline{N})
whose Cost function
H_{N}(\sigma,\tau|W)
admits a Hamiltonian representation where the two layers of neurons mutually interact by the following prescription

H_{N}(\sigma,\tau|W)=-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}w_{ik}\sigma_{i}\tau_{k}.

(1)

The synaptic matrix
w_{ik}
is symmetric (i.e.
w_{ik}=w_{ki}
) and aims to store couples of
N
-bit and
\overline{N}
-bit long patterns
\xi^{\mu},\ \bar{\xi}^{\mu}
(respectively) solely within bidirectional interactions among neurons of different layers such that
W
reduces to a linear combination of
K
factorized terms, i.e. the
patterns
, whose the explicit expression reads

w_{ik}\propto\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\xi^{\mu}_{i}\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{k},

(2)

where the proportionality relation omits a normalization constant that ensures the Hamiltonian to be linearly extensive in the number of neurons. Eq.
2
constitutes the natural generalization of the Hebb’s rule for storing single patterns to couples of patterns.

It is instructive to rephrase the above Cost function
H_{N}(\sigma,\tau|W)
in terms of a standard Loss function of typical usage in Machine Learning:

H_{N}(\sigma,\tau|W)=-\sqrt{N\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu}\left(1-\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\mu}\right)

(3)

where the Loss function
\mathcal{L}_{N,\bar{N}}^{\mu}
is defined in terms of the functions
L_{\sigma}^{\mu}
and
\overline{L}_{\tau}^{\mu}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N,\bar{N}}^{\mu}=L_{\sigma}^{\mu}+\overline{L}_{\tau}^{\mu}-L^{\mu}_{\sigma}\overline{L}_{\tau}^{\mu},

(4)

\displaystyle L_{\sigma}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\left(\xi^{\mu}-\sigma_{i}\right)^{2},

(5)

\displaystyle\overline{L}_{\tau}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\left(\overline{\xi}^{\mu}-\tau_{k}\right)^{2}.

(6)

We emphasize that, historically within the statistical mechanical formalization of neural networks, by writing the Cost function
H_{N}(\sigma,\tau|W)
we keep the focus on neural dynamics, e.g. to inspect the pattern recognition properties of the network, while when writing
\mathcal{L}_{N,\bar{N}}(W|\sigma,\tau)
the focus is on the learning properties as emerging from synaptic dynamics.

2.1
The datasets and their conditional entropies

Extending the route paved in
[
32
,
33
]
for the standard Hopfield model, in this paper we propose a theory for bidirectional Hebbian learning, both in the supervised and unsupervised scenarios (namely in presence -or absence- of a teacher): roughly speaking this means that the network is no longer exposed directly to the patterns (that it never experiences), rather, solely noisy examples of these patterns are provided to the network that has thus to infer correctly the archetypes hidden in these corrupted versions once
enough information
has been collected.

In this context, the terms
\xi^{\mu},\bar{\xi}^{\mu}
, as defined in Eq.
2
, are promoted to play as
archetypes
and used to generate a dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{\eta^{a},\bar{\eta}^{a}\>|\>a=1,..,M\}
of related
examples
: the index
a=1,..,M
runs over the number of examples available for each archetype, that is assumed to be the same for all of them.

We define the
examples

\eta
and
\overline{\eta}
as the noisy version of the
archetypes

\xi
and
\overline{\xi}
by randomly flipping fractions of the original pixels via

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu a}_{i}=\xi^{\mu}_{i}\chi^{\mu a}_{i},

(7)

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}=\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{k}\bar{\chi}^{\mu b}_{k}.

(8)

Following the standard assumptions of AGS theory
[
1
]
, the
archetypes

\xi\>(\bar{\xi})
are random vectors whose entries assume values
\{\pm 1\}^{N}
(
\{\pm 1\}^{\overline{N}}
respectively) with probability
1/2
. The
\chi,\overline{\chi}
flipping-noises distribute according to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\chi_{a})=p\delta(\chi_{a}-1)+(1-p)\delta(\chi_{a}+1),

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\bar{\chi}_{a})=\bar{p}\delta(\bar{\chi}_{a}-1)+(1-\bar{p})\delta(\bar{\chi}_{a}+1).

(10)

such that the first momentum of the distributions of
\chi,\bar{\chi}
reads

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\chi\right\}_{\chi}=2p-1\equiv r,\>\>\mathbf{E}\left\{\bar{\chi}\right\}_{\bar{\chi}}=2\bar{p}-1\equiv\bar{r}.

(11)

Hence, in the limit of
r,\bar{r}\to 1
(i.e.
p,\bar{p}\to 1
), the examples
\eta,\overline{\eta}
collapse to the archetypes
\xi,\overline{\xi}
(and consequently, the entropy of the related dataset
\mathcal{D}
vanishes), while, in the other limit
r,\bar{r}\to 0
(i.e.,
p,\bar{p}\to 1/2
), the examples become random vectors totally uncorrelated to their archetypes (and consequently, the entropy of the related dataset is therefore maximal).

Notice that, at difference with standard (autoassociative) Hebbian learning, in the present context, mixed situations -where the noise level of the dataset is not the same in the examples
\eta
and
\overline{\eta}
- can arise
4
4

4

This is the typical situation in datasets collected from fluorescence microscopy where the emission of the fluorescence molecules tackling the signals have different intensities
[
46
,
47
]
.
.

The following statistical properties of the
examples

\eta,\bar{\eta}
can be consequently traced:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\right\}_{\eta}=0,

(12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{i}\right\}_{\bar{\eta}}=0,

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\right)^{2}\right\}_{\eta}=r^{2}+\frac{1-r^{2}}{M}\equiv R,

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\right)^{2}\right\}_{\bar{\eta}}=\bar{r}^{2}+\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M}\equiv\bar{R},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\right)\right\}_{\eta,\bar{\eta}}=r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{M}=r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}\left(1+\rho\right).

(16)

In the last equation, we have introduced the parameter

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}},

(17)

which will be central in the analysis of the unsupervised model.

The information content of the original message carried by the archetypes that is retained by the examples is quantified for each pattern
\mu
and digit
i,k
,
(\xi^{\mu}_{i}
,
\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{k})
, and block
(\eta_{i}^{\mu},\bar{\eta}_{k}^{\mu})=\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\eta_{i}^{\mu a},\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\eta}_{k}^{\mu a}\right)
, through the conditional entropy
S(\xi^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{i}|\eta^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\eta}^{\mu}_{i})
:

\displaystyle S(\xi^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{i}|\eta^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\eta}^{\mu}_{i})=\mathbf{E}_{\sim P(\xi^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{i},\eta^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\eta}^{\mu}_{i})}\log P(\xi^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{i}|\eta^{\mu}_{i},\bar{\eta}^{\mu}_{i}).

(18)

In the following, we avoid repeating the indices
i,k,\mu
in the equations in order to keep the notation minimal.
Notice that the pair of examples
(\eta,\bar{\eta})
are statistically independent, as they are only related to the independent archetypes
\xi
and
\bar{\xi}
(i.e.,
P(\xi,\bar{\xi})=P(\xi)P(\bar{\xi})
thus
P(\eta,\bar{\eta})=P(\eta)P(\bar{\eta})
). This consideration allows us to express the joint probability as
P(\xi,\bar{\xi},\eta,\bar{\eta})=P(\xi,\eta)P(\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta})
. From this follows that the conditional entropy defined above is the sum of two independent terms, one per layer, that is

\displaystyle S(\xi,\bar{\xi}|\eta,\bar{\eta})=S(\xi|\eta)+S(\bar{\xi}|\bar{\eta}).

(19)

Consider the computation of
S(\xi|\eta)
. The examples
\eta
are given by eq. (
7
) and are therefore determined by the quantity
\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a}
. One can distinguish between two cases, depending on the sign of the product
\xi\eta=sgn(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a})=\pm 1
. Hence, the conditional entropy
S(\xi|\eta)
can be written as

\displaystyle S(\xi|\eta)=-P_{+}\log P_{+}-P_{-}\log P_{-},

(20)

where

\displaystyle P_{+}=\mathcal{P}(sgn(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a})=1)=\mathcal{P}(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a}\geq 0)\sim\frac{1}{2}(1+\erf(1/\sqrt{2\rho_s}),\>\>\>M\to\infty,

(21)

\displaystyle P_{-}=\mathcal{P}(sgn(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a})=-1)=\mathcal{P}(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi^{a}<0)\sim\frac{1}{2}(1-\erf(1/\sqrt{2\rho_s}),\>\>\>M\to\infty.

(22)

The last step is justified by virtue of the Central Limit Theorem (CLT).

Note that the conditional entropy only depends on the parameter
\rho_{s}
, defined as:

\displaystyle\rho_{s}=\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}},

(23)

and it is an increasing function of
\rho_{s}
. The computation of
S(\bar{\xi}|\bar{\eta})
is identical, and naturally leads to the definition of
\bar{\rho}_{s}
:

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{s}=\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}.

(24)

Hence, the parameters
\rho_{s},\bar{\rho}_{s}
uniquely determine the conditional entropy (
19
) of the dataset: at
(\rho_{s},\bar{\rho}_{s})=(0,0)
, the conditional entropy of the dataset is zero, therefore the examples coincide with the archetypes; at
(\rho_{s},\bar{\rho}_{s})\to(\infty,\infty)
the conditional entropy is maximal and the examples are no longer representative of the original archetypes.

As a consequence, w.r.t. the standard AGS theory on Hebbian storing, here we have more control parameters, namely
M,\bar{M}
and
r,\bar{r}
(that are the control parameters related to the datasets, quantifying its
quantity
and
quality
), or we can consider as effective control parameters directly the entropies of the datasets that they ruled, that is
\rho_{s}
and
\bar{\rho_{s}}
. The remaining (standard) control parameters, related to the network’s load and noise, are introduced in the next section.

2.2
The neural networks and their learning protocols

The direct consequence of dealing with examples rather than patterns is that the Hebbian storage rule for reverberation
[
35
]
has to be extended toward a generalized Hebbian learning rule for reverberation and this gives rise to two scenarios: the unsupervised and the supervised settings.

In the case of unsupervised Hebbian learning, the examples are directly summed up in the Hamiltonian of the model, resulting in the following form of the related synaptic matrix:

w_{ik}^{unsup}\propto\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}.

(25)

This expression implements the idea that a teacher -able to distinguish
a priori
the different categories (i.e. the different archetypes in our jargon) the examples belong to- is not available, hence the best we can do is to supply directly to the network the whole available information as a linear superposition of all of the examples.

In the supervised counterpart instead, due to the help of a teacher, we do distinguish among different categories (i.e. different archetypes) before supplying the collected information to the network: the natural choice for the synaptic matrix is thus

w_{ik}^{sup}\propto\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{M}\bar{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}.

(26)

In the following we investigate the information processing capabilities of the BAM in both cases, tackling the problem by a statistical mechanics perspective.
In order to do so, hereafter we share the basic definitions that are in order: first, we explicitly introduce the two Cost functions that contextualize the BAM within an unsupervised (supervised) protocol.

The Cost function of the BAM network trained without the presence of a teacher -that is, under an unsupervised protocol- is given by

H_{N,M}^{unsup}(\sigma,\tau|\eta)=-\frac{1}{ML\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu,a=1}^{K,M}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,k=1}^{N,\overline{N}}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\sigma_{i}\tau_{k},

(27)

while the Cost function of the BAM network trained under the help of a teacher -that is, within a supervised protocol- reads as

H_{N,M}^{sup}(\sigma,\tau|\eta)=-\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,k=1}^{N,\overline{N}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{M}\bar{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\right)\sigma_{i}\tau_{k},

(28)

where we have chosen the geometric mean
L=\sqrt{N\overline{N}}
as the normalization coefficient (instead of the total number of neurons
N+\overline{N}
), preserving notation consistency w.r.t.
[
35
]
.

Note that, while standard artificial learning in e.g. restricted Boltzmann machines
[
36
]
(i.e. trained within the
contrastive divergence
class of algorithms
[
39
]
), feed-forward networks
[
48
]
(i.e. trained via the
back propagation
class of chain rules
[
49
]
), etc. typically involves evaluation of the gradients of the Loss functions w.r.t. the weights (as discussed in the incipit of this Section), hence it works trough evaluation of derivatives, at contrary
Hebbian learning -that is an oversimplified representation of biological learning- takes place by the integral path, that is by taking explicit summations over the examples (as directly coded in the related Cost functions) so to take advantage of concentration of measure arguments as those provided by the classical convergence dictat of CLT.

Back to the introduction of the standard statistical mechanical package of definitions, once provided the Hamiltonians (i.e the Cost functions) and introduced the fast noise (i.e, the
temperature
T) in the network ruled by the control parameter
\beta\equiv T^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}^{+}
, the associated partition function
Z_{N,\bar{N},M,\bar{M}}(\beta)
reads

Z_{N,\bar{N},M,\bar{M}}(\beta)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\{\sigma\}\{\tau\}}^{2^{N},2^{\overline{N}}}e^{-\beta H},

(29)

and allows to define the main observable, namely the quenched free energy
-\beta f(\beta)=A(\beta)
, where the quenching is performed by the average
\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\overline{\eta}}
over the variables
\eta,\overline{\eta}
after taking the logarithm of the partition function:

A(\beta)=\lim_{N,\bar{N}\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\overline{\eta}}\ln Z_{N,\bar{N},M}(\beta).

(30)

We aim to obtain an explicit expression of the quenched free energy in terms of the natural order parameters of the theory: these are the archetype magnetizations
m^{\mu}_{\sigma}
and
m^{\mu}_{\tau}
and the two-replica overlaps
q^{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{q}^{\alpha\beta}
(one per layer):

\displaystyle m^{\mu}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\xi^{\mu}_{i}\sigma_{i},

(31)

\displaystyle m^{\mu}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\bar{\xi}^{\mu}_{k}\tau_{k},

(32)

\displaystyle q^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}^{\alpha}\>\sigma_{i}^{\beta},

(33)

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\tau_{k}^{\alpha}\>\tau_{k}^{\beta},

(34)

where the indices
\alpha,\beta
are the replica indices. Further, in the unsupervised setting, we have also the magnetizations relative to the examples
\eta^{a},\bar{\eta}^{a}
, indicated as
n^{\mu a}_{\sigma}
and
n^{\mu a}_{\tau}
, whose definitions are

\displaystyle n^{\mu a}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{r\sqrt{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}}\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\sigma_{i},

(35)

\displaystyle n^{\mu a}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\bar{r}\sqrt{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}}\frac{1}{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k,a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu}_{k}\tau_{k},

(36)

while, in the supervised counterpart, the relevant example’s magnetization order parameters are relative to the average examples
\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\eta^{\mu a}
and
\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}

(37)

\displaystyle n^{\mu}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\bar{r}\sqrt{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}}\frac{1}{\overline{N}M}\sumop\displaylimits_{k,a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\tau_{k}.

(40)

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}=\lim_{\overline{N}\to\infty}K/\overline{N}=\alpha\gamma^{2},

(41)

\displaystyle\gamma=\lim_{N,\overline{N}\to\infty}\sqrt{N/\overline{N}},

(42)

where

account for the network’s load per layer while

tunes the asymmetry in the network’s layer sizes.
We define also the ‘global’ load of the network

\displaystyle\lambda=\lim_{L\to\infty}K/L=\sqrt{\alpha\bar{\alpha}}=\alpha\gamma.

(43)

The plan is to achieve an explicit expression of the quenched free energy in terms of the control and order parameters, so to extremize the former w.r.t. the latter: this standard procedure results in a series of (self-consistent) equations that rule the evolution of the order parameters in the space of the control parameters, whose study allows to draw phase diagrams, namely plots in the space of the control parameters where different regions depict different emergent computational capabilities collectively shown by these assemblies of neurons and synapses.

A first remark here is that, while in the classical domain of usage of statistical mechanics (i.e., when the Hamiltonian represents a physical system) this plan guarantees thermodynamics to hold, in this extension of statistical mechanics to neural network, it can be seen as the extremization of the cost function under the maximum entropy inferential perspective, as developed by Jaynes
[
50
]
: in these regards the cost function acts as a Lagrangian multiplier to the maximum entropy inferential criterion and the whole free energy can be seen as a complex constrained optimization problem
[
51
,
52
]
.

A second remark consists in observing that phase diagrams, the standard final output of the statistical mechanical journey in the neural network’s world, may play nowadays a pivotal role
en route
toward Sustainable and Optimized AI (SAI
\&
OAI)
[
53
]
because the a-priori knowledge of the regions in the control parameters where the network loses some information processing capabilities allows to save energy and CPU time badly assigned to mismatched problems and, at contrary, forcing the network in the regions of the phase diagram where the desired properties can be achieved allows a conscious usage of neural networks
5
5

5

One could still argue that the bulk of analytical calculations required to obtain phase diagrams is performed on random data-sets, while real data-sets contain structure. While this is certainly true, yet the Shannon compression argument guarantees that if there is a not-empty region where a given computational capability is spontaneously shown by the network, such a region would eventually enlarge (due to potential compression of structured information), but not diminish, hence the random setting is universal and works as a lower-bound picture for structured scenarios, that have to be addressed one-by-one.
.

In the following two Sections we investigate supervised and unsupervised learning protocols (one per Section) achieved by reverberation in the BAM networks.

3
Replica Symmetric Theory of Supervised Learning via Reverberation

As the couples of archetypes share all the same importance (a straight consequence of the RS scenario we are working in), we focus (with no loss of generality) solely on the first couple (and their related examples): this represent the signal, while all the remaining information constitutes the (slow) noise playing against its learning, storage and retrieval.

To handle the post-synaptic potential it is convenient to rely upon the mean field nature of these networks: this allows to apply generalized Carmona-Wu universality theorems regarding the quenched noise in spin glasses

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\sim M\bar{R}^{1/2}\bar{\lambda}^{\mu}_{k},\>\>\bar{\lambda}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(45)

Keeping in mind the distinction between the signal (

to allow the retrieval of couples of patterns, instead of single patterns as in standard Hebbian settings (e.g. the Hopfield model).

It is thus remarkable that, in order to correlate two patterns, while performing Classical Conditioning solely on visible neurons implies the update of an extensive number of synapses (the longer the patterns to relate, the larger the amount of required synapses to handle their correlation)
[

3.1
Supervised Guerra’s interpolation

We are now ready to tackle the explicit calculation of the quenched free energy. To this task we aim to apply Guerra’s interpolation technique (see e.g.
[
58
]
for its genesis in spin glasses and
[
59
]
for its applications to neural networks): the standard plan when using Guerra’s approach is to introduce an interpolating parameter
t\in(0,1)
and an interpolating free energy whose extrema lie between the original model (already split in a signal and a noise term, as they appear in eq. (
47
)) -that we achieve by setting
t=1
- and a linear combination of factorized one-body terms, that we code in the auxiliary function
\phi(t)
, whose analytical treatment is feasible and that we achieve by setting
t=0
. We then solve for the latter, namely we evaluate the free energy at
t=0
, and then propagate back the solution at
t=1
via the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. The whole is reported in the following scheme:

\displaystyle A(\beta)=A(\beta;t=1)=A(\beta;t=0)+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}dt^{\prime}\frac{d}{dt^{\prime}}A(\beta;t^{\prime}),

(53)

\displaystyle A(\beta;t)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{E}\left\{\ln Z(\beta;t)\right\},

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}A(\beta;t)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\langle\frac{d}{dt}Z(\beta;t)\rangle_{t},

(55)

where the brackets represent
\langle.\rangle=\mathbb{E}\{\omega(.)\}
, namely the quenched
\mathbb{E}
expectation of the Boltzmann average
\omega(.)
.

We use to define a generalized interpolating partition function that reads as

\displaystyle Z_{N,\bar{N},M}(\beta;t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\{\sigma\}\{\tau\}}\intop\nolimits Dz^{\dagger}Dz\>e^{-\beta W(t)}.

(56)

Notice that the
e^{-\beta W}
measure automatically defines the modified Boltzmann and quenched averages as, considering a generic function of the neurons
f(\sigma,\tau)
, we can write

\displaystyle\omega_{t}(f(\sigma,\tau))\equiv\sumop\displaylimits_{\{\sigma\}\{\tau\}}\intop\nolimits Dz^{\dagger}Dz\>f(\sigma,\tau)\>e^{-\beta W(t)},

(57)

\displaystyle\langle f(\sigma,\tau)\rangle_{t}\equiv\mathbf{E}\left\{\omega_{t}(f(\sigma,\tau))\right\},

(58)

where
\omega_{t}(.)
denotes the generalized Boltzmann average, which depends on
t
and reduces to the standard one of statistical mechanics whenever evaluated at
t=1
. Likewise,
\langle f(\sigma,\tau)\rangle:=\mathbf{E}\left\{\omega(f(\sigma,\tau))\right\}
.

The details of the computation are shown in Appendix

\displaystyle-\beta\sqrt{R\overline{R}}\>n_{\sigma}n_{\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}\alpha\gamma}{2}\left(p(1-q)+\bar{p}(1-\bar{q})\right)+

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha\gamma}{2}\ln\left(1-\beta^{2}(1-q)(1-\bar{q})\right)+\frac{\alpha\gamma\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{q(1-\bar{q})+\bar{q}(1-q)}{1-\beta^{2}(1-q)(1-\bar{q})}+

\displaystyle+\gamma\mathbf{E}_{\chi}\intop\nolimits Dx\>\ln\cosh\left(\beta\sqrt{\alpha p}\>x+\beta\bar{r}n_{\tau}\gamma^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\chi_{a}\right)\right)+

\displaystyle+\gamma^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{\bar{\chi}}\intop\nolimits Dx\>\ln\cosh\left(\beta\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}\bar{p}}\>x+\beta rn_{\sigma}\gamma\left(\frac{1}{M}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\bar{\chi}_{a}\right)\right),

5
Signal to Noise analysis

Once provided an exhaustive statistical mechanical picture we inspect the quality of the ground state solutions by the signal-to-noise analysis to have an independent confirm of the previous findings (as these have been derived under the replica symmetric approximation). The core idea of this technique is rather simple: once assumed
(\sigma,\tau)
to be a stable configuration corresponding to a stored archetype, the stability request for this configuration simply reads as
\sigma_{i}h_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in(1,...,N)
(for one layer) and
\tau_{k}h_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\in(1,...,\bar{N})
(for the other layer), where we called
h
the post-synaptic fields acting on these neurons.

Hereafter we derive the conditions by which the given neuron
\sigma_{i}
(and similarly
\tau_{i}
), subjected to the internal field
h_{i}
, is aligned to archetype
\xi^{1}
(and, similarly,
h_{k}
for
\bar{\xi}^{1}
) that we want the network to retrieve, splitting the cases of supervised and unsupervised protocols in the next two subsections.

As we assume that the configuration
(\sigma,\tau)
is a retrieval one, the analysis may be implemented following the one-step update prescription of Hinton for fast MC sampling
[
60
]
. We begin by specifying the dynamics of the model at
\beta\to\infty
, as expressed through the update equations at time
t+1
, keeping in mind that the goal is to evaluate the state of the neuron
\sigma
at time
t=1
starting from the initial configuration
\sigma^{(t=0)}=\xi^{1},\tau^{(t=0)}=\bar{\xi}^{1}
:

\displaystyle\sigma^{(t+1)}=\sign(h^{(t)}_{i}\sigma^{(t)}_{i}),

(104)

\displaystyle\tau^{(t+1)}=\sign(\overline{h}^{(t)}_{k}\tau^{(t)}_{k}).

(105)

The neuronal fields
h_{i}
and
\bar{h}_{k}
are the internal fields felt by the neurons
\sigma_{i}
and
\tau_{k}
; they are derived from the definition of the Hamiltonian of the model. In particular, in the the supervised setting from eq. (
28
) follows:

\displaystyle h^{sup}_{i}=\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1^{K}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1^{M}}\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\right)\tau_{k},

(106)

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{sup}_{k}=\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{M}\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\right)\sigma_{i};

(107)

while in the unsupervised case, from eq. (
27
) we have

\displaystyle h^{unsup}_{i}=\frac{1}{L}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\overline{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\tau_{k},

(108)

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{unsup}_{k}=\frac{1}{L}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}_{i}\overline{\eta}^{\mu a}_{k}\sigma_{i}.

(109)

After deriving the state of the network at time
t=1
,
\sigma^{(t=1)},\tau^{(t=1)}
, the overlap between this state and the patterns
\xi^{1},\overline{\xi}^{1}
is given by the Mattis magnetizations
m^{1}_{\sigma},m^{1}_{\tau}
as follows:

\displaystyle m^{1}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\xi^{1}_{i}\sigma^{(t=1)}

(110)

\displaystyle m^{1}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\overline{\xi}^{1}_{k}\tau^{(t=1)}.

(111)

These quantities can be evaluated in the large number of examples limit
M\to\infty
(big data scenario), by replacing the algebraic average
\langle.\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i}(.)
with a Gaussian integral over a stochastic variable
z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, by virtue of the CLT, as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\xi^{1}_{i}\sigma^{(t=1)}=\intop\nolimits Dz\>\xi^{1}\sign(\mu+sz)

(112)

where we have expressed
\sigma^{(t=1)}
through its update rule eq. (
104
), and introduced the mean and variance
\mu,s
of the argument of the sign function, given by:

\displaystyle\mu=\mathbf{E}\left[h^{(t=0)}\xi^{1}\right],

(113)

\displaystyle s=\mathbf{E}\left[\left(h^{(t=0)}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right]-\mu^{2},

(114)

where now formally
\xi^{1}
is a binary scalar variable,
\xi^{1}=\pm 1
,
and
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_{\xi,\overline{\xi},\chi,\overline{\chi}}
. Notice that, by simply rescaling
z\to\xi^{1}z
, we can rewrite eq. (
112
) as

\displaystyle m_{\sigma}^{1}=\intop\nolimits Dz\>\sign(\mu+\sqrt{s}z)=\erf\left(\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{2s}}\right).

(116)

In the following we outline the computation of
m_{\sigma}^{1}
through eq. (
116
), in the supervised and unsupervised cases. Equivalently, the computation can be similarly done for deriving
m_{\tau}^{1}
.

5.1
Signal-to-noise for the Supervised setting

Firstly we start by computing
\mu
. It is given by

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\mathbf{E}\left[\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{M}\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\right)\overline{\xi}_{k}^{1}\xi^{1}\right].

(117)

It can be divided in two terms, the first for
\mu=1
and the second for
\mu>1
:

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\mathbf{E}\left[\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\chi^{1a}\overline{\chi}^{1b}_{k}\right]+\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\mathbf{E}\left[\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu>1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\overline{\xi}^{\mu}_{k}\overline{\xi}^{1}_{k}\chi^{\mu a}\overline{\chi}^{\mu b}_{k}\xi^{1}\xi^{\mu}\right].

(118)

The last term is zero given that
\mathbf{E}\left[\overline{\xi}_{k}^{\mu}\xi_{k}^{\nu}\right]=\delta^{\mu\nu}
and
\mu\neq 1
in this case.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{LM^{2}\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\mathbf{E}_{\chi}\left[\chi^{1a}\right]\mathbf{E}_{\overline{\chi}}\left[\overline{\chi}^{1b}_{k}\right]=\frac{\overline{N}r\bar{r}}{L\sqrt{R\overline{R}}}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}}{\sqrt{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}},

(119)

given the definition of
R
,
\overline{R}
,
\rho_{s}
and
\bar{\rho}_{s}
.

In order to compute
s
, we need to derive
\mathbf{E}\left[\left(h^{(t=0)}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(h^{(t=0)}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{M}\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\right)\overline{\xi}_{k}^{1}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right],

(120)

which can be again decomposed in two contributions,
\mu=1
and
\mu>1
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\chi^{1a}\overline{\chi}^{1b}_{k}\right)^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu>1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\chi^{\mu a}\bar{\chi}^{\mu b}_{k}\>\bar{\xi}_{k}^{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{k}^{1}\xi^{\mu}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right].

(121)

The first term can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k,l}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b,c,d}\mathbf{E}\left[\chi^{1a}\chi^{1c}\overline{\chi}^{1b}_{k}\overline{\chi}^{1d}_{l}\right]=\frac{\overline{N}^{2}M^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}(M^{2}r^{2}+M(1-r^{2}))=\frac{\gamma^{-2}}{1+\bar{\rho}_{s}}.

(122)

Carefully inspecting the second term, it can be seen that only the term of order
\mathcal{O}(\overline{N}K)
survives; it simply reads:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu>1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b,c,d}\mathbf{E}\left[\chi^{\mu a}\chi^{\mu c}\bar{\chi}^{\mu b}_{k}\bar{\chi}^{\mu d}_{k}\right]=\frac{K\overline{N}M^{4}r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}{L^{2}M^{4}R\overline{R}}=\alpha.

(123)

Collecting all the terms, we arrive at the following expression for
s
:

\displaystyle s=\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{-2}}{1+\bar{\rho}_{s}}-\frac{\gamma^{-2}}{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}=\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{-2}\rho_{s}}{(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})}.

(124)

Hence,
m^{1}_{\sigma}
finally reads:

\displaystyle m^{1}_{\sigma}=\erf\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\left(\rho_{s}+\bar{\alpha}(1+\rho_{s})(1+\bar{\rho}_{s})\right)}}\right).

(125)

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\sigma
of the pattern
\xi^{1}
is then

\displaystyle\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<1.

(126)

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\tau
of the pattern
\bar{\xi}^{1}
is then

\displaystyle\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}+\alpha\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<1.

(127)

As shown in Figure
9
, the above conditions -eq.s (
126
) and (
127
)- provide a recipe to guarantee, before starting to train the network, if we have enough information to provide to it to ensure the correct learning, storage and successive retrieval of the archetypes over both the layers: in Figure

we report the growth of the magnetizations of the two archetypes under retrieval as more and more examples are supplied to the network.

5.2
Signal-to-noise for the Unsupervised setting

Again, we start by computing
\mu
. It is given by

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{E}\left[\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\eta^{\mu a}\overline{\eta}^{\mu b}_{k}\overline{\xi}_{k}^{1}\xi^{1}\right].

As in the supervised case, only the term relative to
\mu=1
survives;
\mu
then reads:

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{L}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\mathbf{E}_{\chi}\left[\chi^{1a}\right]\mathbf{E}_{\overline{\chi}}\left[\overline{\chi}^{1a}_{k}\right]=\frac{1}{L}M\overline{N}r\bar{r}=\gamma^{-1}Mr\bar{r}.

(129)

We now compute
s
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(h^{(t=0)}\xi^{1}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu=1}^{K}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\overline{N}}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{M}\xi^{1}\xi^{\mu}\bar{\xi}_{k}^{1}\bar{\xi}_{k}^{\mu}\chi^{\mu a}\overline{\chi}^{\mu a}_{k}\right)^{2}\right],

which can be again decomposed in two contributions,
\mu=1
and
\mu>1
. The first term (
\mu=1
) then reads:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k,l}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\mathbf{E}\left[\chi^{1a}\chi^{1b}\overline{\chi}^{1a}_{k}\overline{\chi}^{1b}_{l}\right]=\frac{\overline{N}^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(M^{2}\bar{r}^{2}r^{2}+M(1-r^{2})\bar{r}^{2}\right)=\gamma^{-2}M^{2}\bar{r}^{2}r^{2}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}\right).

(131)

In the second term (
\mu>1
), the only relevant contribution is that of order
\mathcal{O}(\overline{N}K)
, which reads:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu>1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{a}\xi^{\mu}\xi^{1}\bar{\xi}^{1}_{k}\bar{\xi}^{1}_{k}\chi^{\mu a}\bar{\chi}^{\mu a}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu>1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b}\mathbf{E}_{\chi}\left[\chi^{\mu a}\chi^{\mu b}\right]\mathbf{E}_{\bar{\chi}}\left[\bar{\chi}^{\mu a}\bar{\chi}^{\mu b}\right].

(132)

It can be easily computed, hence it follows

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{N}K}{L^{2}}(M^{2}r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}+M(1-r^{2})\bar{r}^{2}+Mr^{2}(1-\bar{r}^{2})+M(1-r^{2})(1-\bar{r}^{2}))=\alpha M^{2}\bar{r}^{2}r^{2}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}\right).

(133)

Collecting all the terms, we arrive at the following expression for
s
:

\displaystyle s=\gamma^{-2}M^{2}\bar{r}^{2}r^{2}\left[\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}\right)\right]

(134)

Hence,
m^{1}_{\sigma}
finally reads:

\displaystyle m^{1}_{\sigma}=\erf\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\left(\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}\right)\right)}}\right).

(135)

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\sigma
of the pattern
\xi^{1}
is then:

\displaystyle\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<1.

(136)

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\tau
of the pattern
\bar{\xi}^{1}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}+\alpha\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}{Mr^{2}\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<1.

(137)

6
Conclusions and outlooks

In this work we performed a statistical mechanical analysis of the Bidirectional Associative Memories provided with a generalisation of the synaptic matrix that extends hetero-associative Hebbian storage toward hetero-associative Hebbian learning, both in the presence of a teacher (i.e., via a supervised protocol) as well as in its absence (i.e., via an unsupervised protocol).

There is a number of conclusions that we can state from this analysis:

•

The integral representation of the partition function related to the BAM network (see Figure
1
and eq. (
47
)) shows that this machine can be represented by two Restricted Boltzmann machines, whose visible layers are the visible layers of the original BAM, while their hidden layers are made solely by grand-mother cells (that is neurons that selectively fires when a given pattern is presented to the visible layer) in binary communication among the two hidden layers.

•

Thanks to this duality we can appreciate how retrieval of couples of patterns is achieved, namely we can explain
reverberation
by a Machine Learning perspective: as the first RBM is provided with an example -say
\eta^{\nu,a}
- and it is able to recognize the archetype hidden behind the noise (that is
\xi^{\nu}
), its grand-mother cell starts to fire (i.e.
z_{\nu}>>1
), triggering its coupled grand-mother cell in the hidden layer of the other RBM, hence
z_{\nu}^{{\dagger}}>>1
too, that in turn will succeed in retrieving the correct related archetype
\bar{\xi}^{\nu}
on the other visible layer.

•

The above explanation suggests also that synapses do not share all the same importance as, if we would store couples of patterns in an autoassociative network (i.e. in the Hopfield model) so to account for standard Pavlov’s Classical Conditioning, the amount of links to vary would be extensive in the volume, while connecting directly the grand-mother cells allows to save all these synapses (that can thus be used for other tasks).

•

We derived explicitly the thresholds for learning, namely we know explicitly and
a priori
, given the amount of archetypes that we want the network to handle and the noise at which the network operates, if we have the required information in the datasets to undergo a successful training of the machine or we do not. Furthermore the critical scalings in the number of examples to provide to the network are power-laws, resulting in
M_{c}\sim r^{-2}
in the supervised regime and in
M_{c}\sim r^{-4}
in the unsupervised counterpart (in the symmetric case, asymmetric cases are slightly more subtle).

•

The BAM can use these extended resources (provided by two visible layers) to retrieve an archetype on a layer even if provided with a highly entropic dataset by taking advantage of the other layer (if provided with enough information, i.e., if supplied with a low-entropy dataset).

•

We obtained phase diagrams in the space of the control parameters, that are
\rho,\bar{\rho}
(or equivalently,
r,M
and
\bar{r},\bar{M}
) for the dataset and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
for the network’s load, noise and architecture: the knowledge of the retrieval region (that is, knowing
a priori
for which control parameter values the network will operate as an associative memory), constitutes valuable information for an optimized theory of AI as it prevents useless or conceptually wrong training.

Further research extensions of this work would be dedicated to inspect higher order generalizations of these machines, as -for instance- a TAM, namely a three-directional associative memory that stores and retrieves triplets of patterns: we plan to report soon on this topic.

Appendix C
Appendix: the threshold for learning

In this appendix we provide the detailed calculations that lead to the critical thresholds for learning in the two protocols.

Supervised setting

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\sigma
of the pattern
\xi^{1}
is:

\displaystyle\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}+\bar{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<x_{1}=\frac{1}{2\left(\erf^{-1}(\theta_{1})\right)^{2}}.

(C.1)

The stability condition for the retrieval by the layer
\tau
of the pattern
\bar{\xi}^{1}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}+\alpha\left(1+\frac{1-r^{2}}{Mr^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1-\bar{r}^{2}}{M\bar{r}^{2}}\right)<x_{2}=\frac{1}{2\left(\erf^{-1}(\theta_{2})\right)^{2}}.

(C.2)

The critical values of
r_{c}
and
\bar{r}_{c}
are given by:

\displaystyle r_{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\rho_{0}M}},

(C.3)

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\bar{\rho}_{0}M}},

(C.4)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=-\frac{A}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{4}-b},

(C.5)

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{0}=-\frac{\bar{A}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\bar{A}^{2}}{4}-\bar{b}},

(C.7)

\displaystyle\bar{A}=\bar{a}+\bar{c},\>\>\bar{a}=\gamma^{-2}x_{2}-x_{1},\>\>\bar{c}=\bar{\alpha}^{-1}+\gamma^{-2}+1;

\displaystyle b=\gamma^{2}x_{2}-x_{1}+\gamma^{2}-\alpha^{-1}x_{1}=a+\gamma^{2}-\alpha^{-1}x_{1};

(C.9)

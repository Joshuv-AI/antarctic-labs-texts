On pentagon identity in Ding-Iohara-Miki algebra
Yegor Zenkevich

Abstract
We notice that the famous pentagon identity for quantum dilogarithm functions and the five-term relation for certain operators related to Macdonald polynomials discovered by Garsia and Mellit can both be understood as specific cases of a general ‘‘master pentagon identity’’ for group-like elements in the Ding-Iohara-Miki (or quantum toroidal, or elliptic Hall) algebra. We perform some checks of this remarkable identity and discuss its implications.

of Macdonald operators. It also preserves the central charges of the
Fock representation. One can then guess that
\mathcal{S}^{-1}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{S}
can be realized as an operator
\tau
on the
Fock space, diagonal in the basis of Macdonald polynomials:

f(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{-1}(e_{\vec{\gamma}}))=\tau^{-1}f(e_{\vec{\gamma}})\tau.

(49)

In fact it is enough to verify its action on the generators
e_{(0,\pm 1)}
,
e_{(\pm 1,0)}
to find

\tau M_{\lambda}^{(q,t)}(p_{n})=\left(\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}q^{j-1}t^{1-i}\right)M_{\lambda}^{(q,t)}(p_{n}).

(50)

Thus we have

f(T_{(1,1)}(uv))=f(\mathcal{S}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{-1}(T_{(1,0)}(uv)))=\tau^{-1}f(T_{(1,0)}(uv))\tau.

(51)

To make contact with the work of Garsia and Mellit
[
25
]
we notice
that their parameters
q_{\mathrm{GM}}
,
t_{\mathrm{GM}}
and the power
sums
p_{n}^{\mathrm{GM}}
are related to ours as follows:

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{GM}}

\displaystyle=q,

(52)

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{GM}}

\displaystyle=t^{-1},

(53)

\displaystyle p_{n}^{\mathrm{GM}}

\displaystyle=(1-t^{n})p_{n}.

(54)

They also define operators
\tau_{v}^{\mathrm{GM}}
,

\Delta^{\prime\mathrm{GM}}_{u}
and
\nabla_{\mathrm{GM}}
, which are
related to our
T_{(1,0)}
,
T_{(0,1)}
and
\tau
respectively:

\displaystyle\tau_{v}^{\mathrm{GM}}

\displaystyle=f(T_{(0,1)}(-v/t)),

(55)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime\mathrm{GM}}_{u}

\displaystyle=f(T_{(1,0)}(-u)),

(56)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathrm{GM}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}\tau,

(57)

where
d
counts the total degree of polynomial in

\mathcal{F}_{q_{1},q_{2}}^{(1,0)}
. We also notice that

(-1)^{d}f(T_{(1,1)}(uv))(-1)^{d}=f(T_{(1,1)}(-uv)).

(58)

Combining all these changes of variables and shifting
v\to tv
in

T_{(0,1)}
we find that the DIM pentagon identity under the action of

f
reduces to

(\Delta_{u}^{\prime\mathrm{GM}})^{-1}\tau_{v}^{\mathrm{GM}}\Delta_{u}^{\prime\mathrm{GM}}(\tau_{v}^{\mathrm{GM}})^{-1}=\nabla^{-1}\tau_{uv}^{\mathrm{GM}}\nabla,

(59)

which is precisely what is proven in
[
25
]
.

This is another nontrivial check of the DIM pentagon identity: in the
Fock space case, unlike in sec.
3.1
, there are no
additional multiplicative relations
e_{\vec{\alpha}}e_{\vec{\beta}}\sim e_{\vec{\alpha}+\vec{\beta}}
for
f(e_{\gamma})
.

3.3
Direct checks

One can try to verify some lower orders in the expansion of the
pentagon identity (
3
) in
u
and
v
. At first one gets some
identities which can be derived from the commutation relations without
deformation parameters
q_{i}
making any appearance:

\displaystyle u^{1}v^{1}

\displaystyle:\qquad e_{(0,1)}e_{(1,0)}=e_{(1,0)}e_{(0,1)}+e_{(1,1)},

(60)

\displaystyle u^{2}v^{1}

\displaystyle:\qquad\frac{1}{2}e_{(0,1)}\left(e_{(1,0)}^{2}-e_{(2,0)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(e_{(1,0)}^{2}-e_{(2,0)})e_{(0,1)}+e_{(1,0)}e_{(0,1)},

(61)

However, the check at the order
u^{2}v^{2}
requires the evaluation of
the commutator
[e_{(2,0)},e_{(0,2)}]
which corresponds to a
non-admissible triangle. After sequential application of the
commutation relations for admissible triangles we find

5

There
is an error in the first coefficient in the r.h.s. in this

]
.

[e_{(2,0)},e_{(0,2)}]=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2}e_{(1,1)}^{2}+\left(2-\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2\kappa_{1}}\right)e_{(2,2)},

4
Applications and implications

One can evaluate the identity (
3
) in other representation of
the DIM algebra, producing some interesting identities for operators
in representation spaces. Here we list a couple of preliminary
applications of the DIM pentagon identity along these lines.

4.1
Pentagon identity for spherical double affine Hecke
algebra from tensor product of vector representations

There is a homomorphism from
U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})
to spherical double
affine Hecke algebra (sDAHA)
\mathbb{H}_{n}
with
n=1,2,\ldots

The case
n=1
is just the Reineke’s character, or vector
representation
\mathcal{V}_{q_{i}}
discussed in
sec.
3.1
. For higher
n
the homomorphism can be
described by noting that DIM algebra carries a coproduct structure (we
have not discussed it, since we did not use it for our checks of the
pentagon relation). This allows one to take a tensor product of
n

copies of vector representation
\mathcal{V}_{q_{i}}
. This produces an
action of
U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})
on
functions of
n
coordinates
w_{i}
, involving Ruijsenaars-Schneider
difference operators. For example, in this representation we have

\rho_{i}^{\otimes n}(E_{k}(e_{(m,0)}))\sim H_{k}^{\mathrm{RS}},

(63)

where
H_{k}^{\mathrm{RS}}
are Ruijsenaars-Schneider Hamiltonians

H_{k}^{\mathrm{RS}}=\sum_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},|I|=k}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\backslash I}\frac{tw_{i}-w_{j}}{w_{i}-w_{j}}q^{\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\partial_{w_{i}}}

(64)

and
E_{k}
are complete symmetric functions.

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
one recovers the Fock representation
and the identity of Garsia-Mellit with

p_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w_{i}^{n}

(65)

being power sum variables.

4.2
Pentagon identity for Macmahon operators

There is an interesting representation
m
of
U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})
on a vector space

\mathcal{M}
spanned by plane partitions (or
3d
Young diagrams),
which is called the Macmahon representation. Most of the generators,
which we have described for Fock representation in
sec.
3.2
can also be written for Macmahon
representation. For example:

m(e_{(n,0)})|\pi\rangle=\left(-\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}+\sum_{(i,j,k)\in\pi}q_{1}^{n(j-1)}q_{2}^{n(i-1)}q_{3}^{n(k-1)}\right)|\pi\rangle,\qquad n\geq 1

(66)

where
\pi
is a plane partition. The expressions for
m(e_{(0,n)})

are more involved, but can also be found using certain analogues of
Pieri rules for plane partitions. It would be interesting to write
down and check the pentagon relation in this case.

5
Conclusions and comments

Let us recapitulate. We have conjectured an analogue of pentagon
relation between certain generating series in the DIM algebra. This
identity is very nontrivial, since the commutation relations of the
algebra are complicated with some of them defined implicitly by
recursion. We have performed a set of tests of the identity,
evaluating it in two families of representations and have found that
it reduces to previously known (and also nontrivial) identities in
these cases. We have also verified lower orders of the expansion in
the generating parameters using commutation relations directly,
without any reference to representations. Of course a formal proof of
Conjecture
1
would be desirable.

There is one puzzling fact about the identity (
3
). The
generating series
T_{\vec{\gamma}}
look like elements of a group and
therefore it would be logical to expect that they would have some
group-like property, i.e.

\Delta(T_{\vec{\gamma}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}T_{\vec{\gamma}}\otimes T_{\vec{\gamma}}

(67)

where
\Delta
is the coproduct on a DIM algebra. However, a problem
arises immediately: exactly which coproduct features in
Eq. (
67
). In fact the algebra
U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})
has an infinite number of
coproduct structures labelled by an irrational slope in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

lattice. It turns out that Eq. (
67
) holds only for the
coproduct associated to the slope
\vec{\gamma}+0
, where
+0
means
the ‘‘closest irrational slope’’ to the rational vector

\vec{\gamma}
. For other choice of coproduct Eq. (
67
) is
manifestly wrong. How then would the identity involving

T_{\vec{\gamma}}
hold if some of the terms are group-like, while
others are not? For
q_{3}\to 1
this problem does not arise, since the
coproducts are all equivalent and trivial. One hope might be that
coproducts for different slopes
s
and
s^{\prime}
are related by nontrivial
Drinfeld twists
F_{s,s^{\prime}}\in U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})\otimes U_{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{1})
:

\Delta_{s}=F_{s,s^{\prime}}\Delta_{s^{\prime}}F_{s,s^{\prime}}^{-1}.

(68)

The form of the Drinfeld twist
F_{s,s^{\prime}}
is known more or less
explicitly:

F_{s,s^{\prime}}\sim\prod_{s<\vec{\gamma}<s^{\prime},\,\gcd(\vec{\gamma})=1}\exp\left[\sum_{n\geq 1}\kappa_{n}e_{n\vec{\gamma}}\otimes e_{-n\vec{\gamma}}\right],

(69)

however, the commutation relations of
F_{(1,0),(0,1)}
with

T_{(1,0)}
and
T_{(0,1)}
seem to be complicated. Thus, the mystery
remains: how it happens that the coproduct preserves the pentagon
relation (
3
)?

Finally, it would be interesting to find if similar identities
involving
T_{\vec{\gamma}}
with non-coprime
\vec{\gamma}
hold. In
the limit
q_{3}\to 1
there are plenty of such identities each
corresponding to a motivic wall-crossing
formula
[
24
]
. One could wonder if any of them
survives
q_{3}
-deformation.

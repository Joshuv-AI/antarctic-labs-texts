Detectability Conditions and State Estimation for Linear Time-Varying and Nonlinear Systems Thanks: This manuscript was accepted for publication in a future issue of the SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization.
Markus Tranninger, Richard Seeber, Martin Steinberger, Martin Horn, Christian Pötzsche

Abstract
This work proposes a detectability condition for linear time-varying systems based on the exponential dichotomy spectrum. The condition guarantees the existence of an observer, whose gain is determined only by the unstable modes of the system. This allows for an observer design with low computational complexity compared to classical estimation approaches. An extension of this observer design to a class of nonlinear systems is proposed and local convergence of the corresponding estimation error dynamics is proven. Numerical results show the efficacy of the proposed observer design technique.

1.1
Related Work and Contribution

A key concept in data assimilation is the assimilation on the unstable subspace (AUS), which was introduced by A. Trevisan and collaborators
[
58
]
for nonlinear stochastic discrete time systems.
The resulting algorithms are modifications of the extended Kalman filter, where the computation of the error covariances and the Kalman gain is confined to the unstable and neutral tangent subspace of the solutions of the full order filter
[
58
,
17
]
.
With this approach, the solution of the filter Riccati equation has to be computed only for the unstable and neutral tangent subspace in order to obtain the filter gain.
Usually, this subspace is of significantly lower dimension than the system’s state space and hence the computational complexity of the resulting estimation algorithm can be drastically reduced compared to the classical extended Kalman filter (EKF).
Since the initial investigations
[
58
]
, algorithms for continuous-time
[
26
]
and discrete-time systems
[
17
,
43
]
were presented and analyzed.

In
[
26
]
, a data assimilation scheme based on the numerical approximation of regular Lyapunov exponents for continuous time nonlinear systems is presented.
The resulting observer design is numerically very efficient, because it does not require solving a differential Riccati equation to compute the observer feedback gain.
The underlying existence condition is, however, conservative.
It requires the number of linearly independent measurements to be at least equal to the number of non-negative Lyapunov exponents.
Hence, this condition is not fulfilled, for example, by the simple observable linear time invariant double integrator system where the first integrator state is measured.

For linear and nonlinear stochastic discrete time systems, AUS algorithms are discussed in
[
58
,
17
,
16
,
11
,
42
]
.
Such algorithms are very popular, because many numerical studies showed that AUS algorithms are at least as efficient but simpler to implement and computationally less demanding than their original EKF counterparts
[
43
]
.
Based on the AUS ideas, a detectability condition is introduced in
[
29
,
11
]
.
There, the convergence of the error covariance matrices of the AUS Kalman filter is investigated for linear systems in the presence of bounded additive model errors.
The proposed detectability condition has some similarity to the results presented in
[
57
,
55
]
for the deterministic continuous time setting.
Albeit numerical
[
54
,
17
]
and theoretical investigations, cf.
[
57
,
29
,
11
]
, have shown the validity of the underlying AUS ideas, a formal proof of the estimation error convergence for nonlinear systems is still missing to the best of the authors’ knowledge.

This work presents a detectability criterion and an observer design strategy for systems of the form (
1
).
It is a significant extension of the ideas presented in
[
55
,
57
]
and relies on numerically stable algorithms to compute the dichotomy spectrum of dynamical systems
[
20
]
.
The spectral intervals of the dichotomy spectrum are associated with the manifolds comprising solutions with a common exponential growth rate
[
52
]
.
For linear time invariant systems, the proposed detectability definitions and conditions reduce to well known criteria
[
53
]
.
Based on ideas similar to AUS strategies, the observer feedback gain is computed only on a reduced order state space, which corresponds to the unstable and neutral tangent space of the observer trajectory.
Compared to the deterministic extended Kalman-Bucy filter, this results in a numerically efficient observer for systems with a large system order.
The existence conditions for the observer are shown to be fulfilled for a class of uniformly observable nonlinear systems.
Moreover, the present work is a step towards closing the gap between theory and applications of AUS algorithms, because it provides a convergence proof of the resulting observer in a deterministic continuous time framework.

3
Two Important Spectra for Linear Time-Varying Systems

The Lyapunov spectrum and the exponential dichotomy spectrum are two important generalizations of the eigenvalue spectrum to the time-varying case.
A negative Lyapunov spectrum guarantees exponential stability
[
6
, p. 6]
, whereas a negative exponential dichotomy spectrum guarantees uniform exponential stability.
Both spectra are introduced in the following based on
[
23
]

Moreover, it holds that
\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{1}\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{i}}_{1}\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{s}}_{1}\leq\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{1}
and therefore
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathrm{ED}}
.

3.3
Numerical Approximation

For simplicity, it is assumed that system (
5
) is regular.
The non-regular case is treated in
[
23
]
.
In a first step, system (
5
) is transformed to an upper triangular form using a Lyapunov transformation.
For systems with a real coefficient matrix
\mathbf{A}(t)
,
[
2
, Theorem 3.3.1 and Remark 3.3.2]
state that this change of coordinates
\mathbf{R}(t)=\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{X}(t)
can be achieved using an orthogonal Lyapunov transformation matrix
\mathbf{Q}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
.
This transformation can be obtained numerically by means of a continuous QR decomposition
[
23
]
.
In this case,
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{R}
are the solutions of

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{R}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{R},\;\quad\mathbf{B}(t)=\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{Q}(t)-\mathbf{S}(t)

(15a)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{S}(t).\;

(15b)

The coefficients
s_{ij}(t)
of the skew symmetric
n\times n
matrix
\mathbf{S}(t)
are given by
s_{ij}(t)=\mathbf{q}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{q}_{j}(t)
for
i>j
, where
\mathbf{q}_{i}(t)
denotes the
i
-th column of
\mathbf{Q}(t)
.

In a first step, it is assumed that the initial conditions are obtained by a QR decomposition of an ordered normal Lyapunov basis
4
4

4

The numerical implementation is discussed in Section
3.4
.
, i.e.,
\mathbf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{0}
.
Under the assumption that all diagonal elements of
\mathbf{R}_{0}
are non-negative, this decomposition is unique.
For regular systems (
5
) transformed to an upper triangular form

\dot{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{z}
with
\mathbf{z}(t)=\mathbf{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{x}(t)
, the Lyapunov exponents can be obtained by time-averaging of the diagonal elements according to

\lambda_{i}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}{{b_{ii}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}{{\mathbf{q}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}(\tau)\mathbf{A}({\tau})\mathbf{q}_{i}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}},

(16)

see
[
41
]
.
To obtain the Lyapunov exponents, merely solving the orthogonal differential equation (
15b
) and not the (possibly unbounded) solution of (
15a
) is required.

Independent of the system’s regularity, the exponential dichotomy spectrum can always be obtained from the diagonal elements of
\mathbf{B}
, see
[
24
]
.
To that end, let the two real functionals

\beta^{\mathrm{i}}(b)=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}t-t_{0}\rightarrow\infty\\
t_{0}\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+t}{{b(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta^{\mathrm{s}}(b)=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t-t_{0}\rightarrow\infty\\
t_{0}\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+t}{{b(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}

(17a)

be introduced for a scalar function
b:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}
.
Applied to the diagonal entries of
\mathbf{B}
, these functionals provide the lower and upper Bohl exponents
\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i}=\beta^{\mathrm{i}}(b_{ii})
and
\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}=\beta^{\mathrm{s}}(b_{ii})
, respectively, of the scalar systems

\dot{\eta}_{i}=b_{ii}(t)\eta_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n.

According to
[
24
, Proposition 5]
, the dichotomy spectrum is then obtained as

\Sigma_{\mathrm{ED}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{i}\quad\text{with}\quad\Lambda_{i}=\left[\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}\right].

(18)

Note that the
n
intervals
\Lambda_{i}
are not necessarily disjoint and the obtained Bohl exponents are not necessarily sorted.
In many cases, however, a sorting similar to the Lyapunov exponents can be expected.
From the relations in (
16
) and (
17
) it follows that
\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}
and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathrm{ED}}
, i.e., the Lyapunov spectrum is contained in the exponential dichotomy spectrum.
This is also true in the non-regular case
[
23
]
.
For periodic and time-invariant systems, both spectra coincide, i.e.,
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{ED}}
.
In the general time-varying case, however, this is not true as demonstrated in
[
24
]
.

The numerical approximation of the Bohl exponents turns out to be more difficult than the approximation of (regular) Lyapunov exponents, because
t_{0}
and
t-t_{0}
tend to infinity in (
17
).
For the approximation of the Bohl exponents, let

\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i}=\inf_{t_{0}\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}}\frac{1}{H}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+H}{{b_{ii}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}=\sup_{t_{0}\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}}\frac{1}{H}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+H}{{b_{ii}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}

(19)

be introduced with a scalar parameter
H>0
.
It is stated in
[
22
, Theorem 8.4]
that for any
H>0
,
\Lambda_{i}=[\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}]\subseteq[\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}]
.
It is furthermore claimed that for
H>0
sufficiently large it holds that

[\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}]\subseteq[\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}]
and hence
\Lambda_{i}=[\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i},\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}]
.
The latter statement is not true as shown by the following counterexample.

Example 9
.

Consider the scalar system
\dot{x}=\frac{1}{1+t}x
which admits the fundamental solution
X(t)=(1+t)
.
The exponential dichotomy spectrum is
\Sigma_{\mathrm{ED}}=\{0\}
and hence
\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{1}=\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{1}=0
.
The computation of
\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}
gives

\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{1}=\sup_{t}\frac{1}{H}\int_{t}^{t+H}{{\frac{1}{1+\tau}}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}=\sup_{t}\frac{1}{H}\ln(1+\frac{H}{1+t})=\frac{1}{H}\ln(1+H)

(20)

with a supremum at
t=0
.
Hence, for any finite
H>0
it holds that
\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{1}>0=\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{1}
.

The correct relation between the approximation (
19
) and the bounds of the spectral intervals (
17
) is summarized in the following

Theorem 10
(approximation of the spectral intervals).

For every
H>0
, it holds that

\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i}\leq\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i}\leq\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}\leq\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}.

(21)

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
H_{0}>0
such that for all
H\geq H_{0}
it holds that

\beta^{\mathrm{i}}_{i}-\varepsilon\leq\beta^{H,\mathrm{i}}_{i}\text{ and }\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{i}\leq\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}+\varepsilon.

(22)

The proof is given in the appendix.
Averaging the
b_{ii}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, over a sufficiently large time interval
H
allows to approximate the spectral intervals theoretically with arbitrary accuracy.
In particular, Theorem
10
implies that in the limit, it holds that

\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{i}=\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t_{0}}\frac{1}{H}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+H}{{b_{ii}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}{\tau}}.

Detailed considerations and numerical examples for the approximation of the Lyapunov exponents (also for non-regular systems) and the exponential dichotomy spectral intervals can be found in
[
23
]
.

3.4
Reduced QR Decomposition

If not all spectral intervals are of interest, it may suffice to compute only a part of the spectrum.
This allows a reduction of the computational complexity by utilizing properties of the QR-decomposition
[
57
]
.
For the reduced QR decomposition, only the first
k\leq n
columns of an ordered normal Lyapunov basis
\mathbf{X}
are considered.
This results in the differential equations for the reduced QR decomposition

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)\mathbf{R}_{1},\quad\mathbf{R}_{1}(0)\in\mathds{R}^{k\times k},

(23a)

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}})\mathbf{A}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{S}_{1}(t),\quad{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(0)\in\mathds{R}^{n\times k},\quad\text{with}}

(23b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{A}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)-\mathbf{S}_{1}(t).

(23c)

The elements
s_{ij}
of the skew-symmetric
k\times k
-Matrix
\mathbf{S}_{1}
are given by

s_{ij}(t)=\bar{\mathbf{q}}_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{A}(t)\bar{\mathbf{q}}_{j}(t)

for
i>j

[
26
,
57
]
.

Again, only the solution of (
23b
) is required for the approximation of
k
spectral intervals via the diagonal entries of
\mathbf{B}_{1}
.
For this, it is crucial to preserve orthogonality of the columns of
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
.
This can be achieved by a projected integration algorithm, which is a standard integration scheme (e.g., a fourth-order Runge-Kutta algorithm) in combination with an orthogonalization procedure based, e.g., on the modified Gram-Schmidt algorithm as presented in
[
21
]
.

If the initial condition
\bar{\mathbf{X}}(0)=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(0)\mathbf{R}_{1}(0)
is chosen as the first
k
columns of an ordered normal Lyapunov basis, the
k
largest Lyapunov exponents can be obtained from the diagonal of
\mathbf{B}_{1}
.
Typically, such a basis is not known a priory and determining such a basis may be cumbersome
[
20
,
23
]
.
Hence, it is proposed in
[
20
]
to choose
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(0)
as random orthogonal matrix in practice.
This choice proved to be successful in many numerical investigations
[
26
,
57
,
54
]
and is theoretically supported by
[
7
]
.

4
Uniform Detectability of Linear Time-Varying Systems

This section deals with the existence of an observer for systems of the form (
5
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{x},\quad\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{x}

(24)

with the output
\mathbf{y}(t)\in\mathds{R}^{p}
.
The output matrix function
\mathbf{C}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{p\times n}
is assumed to be continuous and bounded.
The goal is to design an observer

\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}=\mathbf{A}(t)\hat{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{L}(t)\left[\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{C}(t)\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right],

(25)

where
\mathbf{L}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{n\times p}
is a design parameter.
The estimation error
\mathbf{e}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
is governed by

\dot{\mathbf{e}}=\left[\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{L}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}.

(26)

The following detectability definition introduced in
[
60
,
48
]
, is strongly related to the stability properties of (
26
).

Definition 11
.

System (
24
) is called uniformly exponentially detectable, if there exists a bounded
\mathbf{L}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{n\times p}
such that the estimation error dynamics (
26
) is uniformly exponentially stable.

In other words, uniform exponential detectability or short
detectability
is equivalent to the existence of an observer with a uniformly exponentially stable estimation error dynamics.
A stronger concept which also guarantees the existence of an observer is uniform complete observability.
It is characterized by the constructibility Gramian

\mathbf{N}(t_{1},t_{0})=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{{\mathbf{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}}(s,t_{1})\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(s)\mathbf{C}(s)\mathbf{\Phi}(s,t_{1})}\,\mathrm{d}{s}}

(27)

according to

Definition 12
(uniform complete observability).

System (
24
), or equivalently the pair
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{C}(t))
, is called uniformly completely observable, if there exist positive constants
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
and
{T}
, such that

\alpha_{1}\mathbf{I}_{n}\preceq\mathbf{N}(t_{0}+{T},t_{0})\preceq\alpha_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}

holds for all
t_{0}\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}
.

It is shown in
[
15
]
that uniform complete observability implies the existence of a unique and uniformly bounded positive definite solution of the observer Riccati equation

\dot{\mathbf{P}}=\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{G}(t).

(28)

Here,
\mathbf{P}(0)\succ 0
and
\mathbf{G}(t)=\mathbf{G}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)
with
g_{1}\mathbf{I}_{n}\preceq\mathbf{G}(t)\preceq g_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}
and
0<g_{1}\leq g_{2}
.
Moreover, choosing the feedback gain in (
26
) as
\mathbf{L}(t)=\mathbf{P}(t)\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)
renders the error dynamics uniformly exponentially stable
[
14
]
and hence, uniform complete observability is sufficient for uniform exponential detectability.

Uniform complete observability is a strong system requirement and solving the differential Riccati equation (
28
) might be computationally demanding, especially for systems with a large system order.
If the system, however, possesses only a few unstable modes, i.e., only a small number of non-negative upper Bohl exponents, it is reasonable to modify only these modes in the estimation error dynamics.
This allows to solve the Riccati equation on a reduced order subspace.
This strategy is also pursued in
[
43
,
57
]
for non-negative (regular) Lyapunov exponents and is the underlying idea of the following detectability condition and observer design technique.

Theorem 13
(uniform detectability).

Consider the linear time-varying system (
24
).
Let
\mathbf{Q}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
be the solution to (
15b
) of the continuous QR-decomposition of a corresponding fundamental solution
\mathbf{X}
with
\mathbf{X}(t_{0})=\mathbf{Q}(t_{0})\mathbf{R}(t_{0})
.
Let
j^{\star}
be the first integer
0\leq j^{\star}\leq n
such that
\beta^{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{q}_{i}(t)^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{q}_{i}(t))<0
for
i=j^{\star}+1,\ldots,n
and let
\mathbf{Q}(t)
be partitioned according to
\mathbf{Q}(t)=\left[\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\;\;\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}(t)\right]
, where
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\in\mathds{R}^{n\times j^{\star}}
.

System (
24
) is detectable, if the pair
(\mathbf{B}_{1}(t),\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t))
with
\mathbf{B}_{1}
as in (
23c
) and
k=j^{\star}
is uniformly completely observable.
In particular, there exists a bounded positive definite solution to the differential Riccati equation

\dot{\mathbf{P}}{{}_{1}}=\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}+\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)-\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)+\mathbf{G}{{}_{1}}(t),

(29)

with the
j^{\star}\times j^{\star}
matrix
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t_{0})\succ 0
and positive constants
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
such that
g_{1}\mathbf{I}_{j^{\star}}\preceq\mathbf{G}{{}_{1}}(t)\preceq g_{2}\mathbf{I}_{j^{\star}}
.
Moreover, the error system (
26
) with

\mathbf{L}(t)=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{{}_{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)

(30)

is uniformly exponentially stable.

Proof.

Based on the Lyapunov transformation
\mathbf{e}(t)=\mathbf{Q}(t)\mathbf{e}_{z}(t)
, the error system (
26
) can be transformed to the block upper triangular form

\begin{bmatrix}\dot{\mathbf{e}}_{z,1}\\
\dot{\mathbf{e}}_{z,2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)&\mathbf{B}_{12}(t)\\
\bm{0}&\mathbf{B}_{2}(t)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{z,1}\\
\mathbf{e}_{z,2}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{L}_{1}(t)\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{C}(t)\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)&\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}(t)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{e}_{z,1}\\
\mathbf{e}_{z,2}\end{bmatrix}

(31)

with
\mathbf{L}_{1}(t)=\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)
.
The subsystem
\dot{\mathbf{e}}_{z,2}=\mathbf{B}_{2}(t)\mathbf{e}_{z,2}
is uniformly exponentially stable, because all upper Bohl exponents are negative.
Hence,
\mathbf{e}_{z,2}(t)
can be seen as a uniformly exponentially vanishing perturbation in the system

\dot{\mathbf{e}}_{z,1}=\left[\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)-\mathbf{L}_{1}\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}_{z,1}+\left[\mathbf{B}_{12}(t)-\mathbf{L}_{1}\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}_{z,2}

(32)

Uniform complete observability of the pair
(\mathbf{B}_{1}(t),\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t))
implies that there exists a uniformly bounded positive definite solution to (
29
) and also implies that
\dot{\mathbf{e}}_{z,1}=\left[\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)-\mathbf{L}_{1}\mathbf{C}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}_{z,1}
is uniformly exponentially stable.
Uniform exponential stability of the overall error system and hence detectability then follows from
[
61
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 14
.

For the practical implementation of the observer (
25
) with the gain (
30
), it suffices to solve the differential equation (
23b
) for some
k\geq j^{\star}
instead of the (full order) differential equation (
15b
).
This could drastically reduce the complexity, if
j^{\star}
is small compared to the system order
n
.
The parameter
k
determines the number of upper Bohl exponents, which are modified by the observer feedback gain.
If system (
24
) is uniformly completely observable,
k
can be regarded as a tuning parameter and allows a trade-off between the computational complexity and the convergence speed of the error system
[
57
]
.
In particular,
k
determines how many spectral intervals of the system are modified in the observer error dynamics.

In general, the detectability condition in
Theorem

13
is not necessary, even if
\mathbf{X}_{0}
is an ordered normal Lyapunov basis, as demonstrated by an example provided in
[
56
]
where the upper Bohl exponents are not ordered.
In the numerical simulations, it turned out that a certain ordering of the upper Bohl exponents similar to the Lyapunov exponents can be expected as shown in
Section

6
.
Moreover, if the system has an exponential dichotomy and
\mathbf{X}_{0}
is an ordered normal Lyapunov basis, the detectability condition in
Theorem

13
turns out to be necessary and sufficient
[
55
]
.

It is now shown, that the reduced order Riccati equation (
29
) can be obtained from a projection of the full order Riccati equation with properly chosen
\mathbf{P}(t_{0})
and
\mathbf{G}(t)
.
To that end, it is assumed that system (
5
) is transformed to an upper triangular form with
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{Q}(t)\mathbf{z}
and partitioned according to
\mathbf{Q}(t)=\left[\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\;\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}(t)\right]
.
The full-order Riccati equation is then given by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{1}&\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{12}\\
\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}_{1}&\mathbf{B}_{12}\\
\bf 0&\mathbf{B}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}&\mathbf{P}_{12}\\
\mathbf{P}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\mathbf{P}_{2}\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{P}(t)}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}&\mathbf{P}_{12}\\
\mathbf{P}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\mathbf{P}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}_{1}&\bf 0\\
\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}_{12}&\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{T}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}

(33)

\displaystyle-\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}&\mathbf{P}_{12}\\
\mathbf{P}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\mathbf{P}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}\\
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}^{\mathsf{T}}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{C}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}&\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{\perp}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}&\mathbf{P}_{12}\\
\mathbf{P}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\mathbf{P}_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{G}_{1}&\mathbf{G}_{12}\\
\mathbf{G}_{12}^{\mathsf{T}}&\mathbf{G}_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where the time indices are omitted for the sake of readability.

Lemma 15
(Reduced Riccati equation).

Let
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
be the solution of the differential Riccati equation (
29
) and

\mathbf{P}(t_{0})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}(t_{0})&\bf 0\\
\bf 0&\bf 0\end{bmatrix},\;\mathbf{G}_{12}={\bf 0}\;\text{and}\;\;{\mathbf{G}}_{2}=\bf{0}.

(34)

Then, the solution of (
33
) is given by

\mathbf{P}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for all }t\geq t_{0}.

(35)

Proof.

The statement follows by substituting
\mathbf{P}(t)
into (
33
) and using (
29
) and (
34
).

5
Nonlinear Observer Design

This section extends the observer design approach presented in
Section

4
to nonlinear systems.
As the Riccati differential equation is solved on a reduced state-space only, the ideas for the convergence proof of the extended Kalman-Bucy filter cannot be applied in the present setting.
However, the local stability result utilizes the Lyapunov function from
Proposition

3
and hence, under some assumptions stated subsequently, local uniform exponential stability of the observer error dynamics can be guaranteed.

In the following, the nonlinear system (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}),\,t\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}},\;\mathbf{x}(t_{0})=\mathbf{x}_{0},

(36a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{x}

(36b)

is considered with the output equation (
36b
).
Again, it is assumed that the output matrix function
\mathbf{C}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{p\times n}
is bounded.
It should be remarked that the case of a linear measurement equation is used to demonstrate the key idea.
An extension to a nonlinear output map is possible by a straightforward application of the concepts presented in
[
50
]
.

5.1
Extended Kalman-Bucy Filter as a Deterministic Observer

The idea of the deterministic interpretation of the extended Kalman-Bucy filter is to obtain the time-varying matrices
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{C}(t)
by a linearization along the estimated trajectory
[
50
]
.
The algorithm can be summarized as

\displaystyle{\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u})+\mathbf{P}(t)\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\left[\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{C}(t)\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right],\quad\hat{\mathbf{x}}(0)=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0},

(37a)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{P}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)+\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{G}(t),\quad\mathbf{P}(t)\in\mathds{R}^{n\times n}

(37b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial{\phantom{.}}}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))\right|_{(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))}.

(37c)

The initial condition
\mathbf{P}_{0}
is chosen as a positive definite matrix and the matrix
\mathbf{G}(t)
is a positive definite tuning parameter.

Local stability results for the dynamics of the estimation error
\mathbf{e}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
are presented in
[
50
,
39
,
12
]
.
A key assumption of these stability proofs is the existence of positive constants
p_{1},\,p_{2}
such that
p_{1}\mathbf{I}_{n}\preceq\mathbf{P}(t)\preceq p_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}
holds for (
37b
).
This assumption can be guaranteed by uniform complete observability of the observer trajectory, which, however, might not be a trajectory of the original system in general.
This assumption cannot be dropped easily as extensively discussed in
[
12
,
37
]
.

5.2
Extended Subspace Observer

Based on the ideas proposed in
Theorem

13

The proposed algorithm is summarized as

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u})+\mathbf{L}(t)\left[\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{C}(t)\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right],

(38a)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{P}}{{}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}+\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}\mathbf{B}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)-\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}\bar{\mathbf{C}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\bar{\mathbf{C}}(t)\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{G}{{}_{1}}(t),\;\quad\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)\in\mathds{R}^{k\times k}

(38b)

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{I}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right]\mathbf{A}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{S}_{1}(t),\quad\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\in\mathds{R}^{n\times k},

(38c)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)\bar{\mathbf{C}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t),\quad\bar{\mathbf{C}}(t)=\mathbf{C}(t)\mathbf{Q}(t),

(38d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial{\phantom{.}}}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))\right|_{(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))},\quad\mathbf{B}_{1}(t)=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathsf{T}}(t)\mathbf{A}(t)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(t)-\mathbf{S}_{1}(t).

(38e)

The elements
s_{ij}

The number of columns in

In the following, a convergence result for this observer is provided.

5.3
Convergence Analysis

The estimation error
\mathbf{e}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
is governed by

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{L}(t)\left[\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{C}(t)\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right].

(39)

By substituting
\mathbf{x}
with
\mathbf{x}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{e}
in (
39
) and performing a Taylor series expansion of
\mathbf{f}(\cdot,\cdot)
around
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{0}
one obtains

\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{e},\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u})+\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{e}+\bm{\eta}(\mathbf{e},\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u}),
where
\mathbf{A}(t)=\left.\frac{\partial{\phantom{.}}}{\partial{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\right|_{(\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))}

and
\bm{\eta}
is the remainder of the Taylor series truncated after the linear term.
Hence, the estimation error dynamics can be stated as

\dot{\mathbf{e}}=\left[\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{L}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}+\bm{\eta}(\mathbf{e},\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u}).

(40)

The following additional assumptions are standard assumptions in the convergence analysis of the extended Kalman-Bucy filter, see, e.g.
[
50
]
.

(a1)

The matrix function
\mathbf{A}:{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}\rightarrow\mathds{R}^{n\times n}
is bounded

(a2)

For
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)
in (
38b
), there exist positive constants
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
such that
p_{1}\mathbf{I}_{k}\preceq\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)\preceq p_{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}
holds for all
t\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}
.

(a3)

There exist positive constants
\varepsilon
and
\kappa
such that

\|\bm{\eta}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\|\leq\kappa\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|^{2}

holds for all
t\in{\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}}
,
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{e}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{u}\in\mathds{R}^{m}
with
\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|\leq\varepsilon
.

Remark 16
.

Assumption
(a1)
is e.g. fulfilled if
\mathbf{f}
is globally Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbf{x}
.
The lower bound on
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
in
(a2)
is fulfilled for a properly chosen
\mathbf{G}_{1}(t)
as shown in the proof of
Theorem

20
.
For a class of uniformly observable systems, the upper bound on
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
moreover holds independently of the specific observer trajectory.
This result is stated in detail in
Section

5.4
.
Assumption
(a3)
is fulfilled, e.g., if
\mathbf{f}
is at least two times continuously differentiable and the corresponding Hessian matrix of each component of
\mathbf{f}
is bounded, see
[
50
,
26
]
. Let the components of
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})&\cdots&f_{n}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
be denoted by
f_{i}
,
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.
Then,
\kappa
is given by

\kappa=\frac{1}{2}\max_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x}\in\mathds{R}^{n}\\
\mathbf{u}\in\mathds{R}^{m}\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{H}_{f_{i}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\|

(41)

with
\mathbf{H}_{f_{i}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})
as the Hessian matrix of
f_{i}
.

To show local stability of the estimation error dynamics, the standard approach typically used in the stability analysis for the extended Kalman-Bucy filter, see, e.g.
[
50
]
, cannot be applied, because the Riccati equation is solved only on a reduced-order subspace.
However, a local convergence result is obtained by utilizing the Lyapunov function for linear time-varying systems from
Proposition

3
.

Theorem 17
(extended subspace observer).

Let a system be given by (
36
) and the observer for this system by (
38
).
Moreover, let the assumptions (a1) to (a3) hold.
Then, the estimation error dynamics (
40
) resulting from this observer is locally uniformly exponentially stable.

Proof.

According to
Theorem

13
and due to assumptions (a1) and (a2), it follows that the linear time-varying system

\dot{\mathbf{e}}=\left[\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{L}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)\right]\mathbf{e}

is uniformly exponentially stable with the feedback gain
\mathbf{L}(t)
as in (
38d
).
The state transition matrix of this (unperturbed) system can then be bounded by

\|\mathbf{\Phi}(t_{1},t_{0})\|\leq Ke^{-\gamma(t_{1}-t_{0})}

(42)

for some positive constants
K\geq 1
and
\gamma>0
.
Moreover, according to
Proposition

3
, there exists a Lyapunov function
V(t,\mathbf{e})=\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)\mathbf{e}
with positive constants
\bar{p}_{1}
and
\bar{p}_{2}
and a positive definite
n\times n
matrix
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)
such that
\bar{p}_{1}\mathbf{I}_{n}\preceq\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)\preceq\bar{p}_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}
.
The matrix
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)
is the unique positive definite solution of

\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{L}}+\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}_{\mathrm{e}}(t)\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}+\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{e}}(t)+\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)=\mathbf{0}

(43)

with
\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{e}}(t)=\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{L}(t)\mathbf{C}(t)
and
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)
as any positive definite matrix bounded by positive constants
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
such that
q_{1}\mathbf{I}_{n}\preceq\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)\preceq q_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}
.

The function
V(t,\mathbf{e})=\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)\mathbf{e}(t)
is now used as a Lyapunov function candidate for the perturbed error system (
40
).
Let
\varepsilon
be given as in assumption (a3).
Then, for
\|\mathbf{e}\|\leq\varepsilon
, the time derivative along the trajectory can be obtained according to

\displaystyle\dot{V}(t,\mathbf{e})

\displaystyle=\dot{\mathbf{e}}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\dot{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{L}\dot{\mathbf{e}}=-\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{e}+2\bm{\eta}(\mathbf{e},\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{u})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{e}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq-q_{1}\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}+2\bar{p}_{2}\|\bm{\eta}\|\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|\leq-q_{1}\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}+2\bar{p}_{2}\kappa\|\mathbf{e}\|^{3}\leq(-q_{1}+2\bar{p}_{2}\kappa\|\mathbf{e}\|)\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}

For
\|\mathbf{e}\|\leq\min\left(\frac{q_{1}}{4\bar{p}_{2}\kappa},\varepsilon\right)
, it holds that

\dot{V}(t,\mathbf{e})\leq-\frac{1}{2}q_{1}\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}.

(45)

According to
[
38
, Theorem 4.10]
, the error dynamics is locally uniformly exponentially stable because of (
45
) and
\bar{p}_{1}\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}\leq V(t,\mathbf{e})\leq\bar{p}_{2}\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}
.

To further investigate the norm constraint on the initial error, it is now assumed for simplicity that
\varepsilon\geq\frac{q_{1}}{4\bar{p}_{2}\kappa}
and that
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)=q\mathbf{I}_{n}
, i.e.,
q_{1}=q_{2}=q
.
Then, item
(iv)
of
Proposition

3
, i.e.,

\bar{p}_{2}\leq\frac{K^{2}q}{2\gamma}

with
K
and
\gamma
as in (
42
) can be used to simplify the constraint on the initial error according to

\|\mathbf{e}(t_{0})\|\leq\frac{q_{1}}{4\bar{p}_{2}\kappa}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2K^{2}\kappa}

(46)

Here,
\gamma
is the exponential decay rate.
For the proposed observer,
\gamma
is bounded by the
k
-th upper Bohl exponent
\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{k}
such that
\gamma<\beta_{k}
, because the
(k+1)
-th exponent is not modified by the observer gain.
This suggests that the region of convergence can be increased by taking more exponents into account, i.e., by increasing the number of columns in
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
, if the system is not merely detectable but has stronger observability properties.
This effect is also demonstrated in the numerical simulation examples.

Remark 18
.

In general, the number of non-negative upper Bohl exponents
j^{\star}
depends on the specific observer trajectory.
The Bohl exponents, however, are robust with respect to bounded vanishing perturbations, see
[
19
, Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.5]
.
Because the trajectories of the nonlinear system (
36
) are also trajectories of the observer (for zero initial error), it hence suffices to obtain
j^{\star}
from simulation studies of the original system.
If the initial estimation error is small, the observer trajectory can then be regarded as perturbed trajectory of the true system trajectory with the same
j^{\star}
.
The Bohl exponents of the linearizations along the trajectories in general depend on the specific trajectory.
For the special class of ergodic measure preserving systems, Oseledet’s multiplicative ergodic theorem guarantees that the Lyapunov exponents are independent of the specific trajectory, see
[
4
, p. 8-9]
or
[
35
, Theorem 2.1]
.
To the authors’ knowledge, a similar result regarding the Bohl exponents or the exponential dichotomy spectrum is still missing.
A step towards this direction is provided in
[
35
]
, where the relationship between Lyapunov exponents and the so-called dynamical spectrum is investigated.
The dynamical spectrum is a generalization of
Definition

7
to nonlinear systems and is independent of the specific linearization.
It is hard to compute in practice, however.

5.4
A Class of Uniformly Observable Systems

For special classes of nonlinear systems, the assumptions
(a1)
to
(a3)
are guaranteed to hold and verifiable conditions depending only on the system properties can be stated.
In particular, this section considers nonlinear systems which are diffeomorphic to

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{x}+\bar{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})

(47a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{x}

(47b)

where the state
\mathbf{x}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
is partitioned according to

\mathbf{x}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}^{(1)}\\
\mathbf{x}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{x}^{(p)}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{with }\mathbf{x}^{(k)}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{k,1}\\
x_{k,2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{k,l_{k}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathds{R}^{l_{k}},\,k=1,\ldots,p,\;\text{ and }\;\sum_{k}l_{k}=n.

(47c)

The rest of the system is characterized by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{A}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\left({\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{1},\,\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{2},\,\ldots,\,\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{p}}\right),\;\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
&&&\ddots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}_{(l_{k}\times l_{k})}

(47d)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{diag}\left({\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{1},\,\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{2},\,\ldots,\,\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{p}}\right),\;\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}_{(1\times l_{k})}.

(47e)

The nonlinear function
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is structured according to

\bar{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{f}}^{(1)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\\
\bar{\mathbf{f}}^{(2)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\\
\vdots\\
\bar{\mathbf{f}}^{(p)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\end{bmatrix}\text{ with }\bar{\mathbf{f}}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})=\begin{bmatrix}f_{k,1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\\
f_{k,2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\\
\vdots\\
f_{k,l_{k}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})\\
\end{bmatrix}

(47f)

and

\frac{\partial f_{i,j}}{\partial x_{v,w}}=0
for all
i,v\leq p
and all
w>j
.

Moreover,
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is assumed to be globally Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbf{x}
and at least two times continuous differentiable, where the corresponding Hessian matrix of each component of
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is bounded.

It is well known that for general nonlinear systems, the input may destroy the system’s observability.
Systems diffeomorphic to (
47
) are called uniformly observable for any input
[
39
]
, because observability does not depend on the particular input.
Systems in the form (
47
) play an important role in the observer design for nonlinear systems, see, e.g.
[
27
,
32
,
25
,
31
,
28
,
44
]
.
A lot of work has been devoted to determining existence conditions for transformations to the form (
47
) or more general upper triangular forms, see
[
44
, Sec. 6]
and the references therein for a good overview on existing procedures.
It is worth to mention that system (
47
) is a generalization of the phase variable form
[
8
]
.

For system (
47
), Krener
[
39
]
shows that the extended Kalman-Bucy filter (
37
) is a local exponential observer.
In particular, the proof of
[
39
, Theorem 1.1.1]
establishes the boundedness of
\mathbf{P}(t)
in (
37b
) independently of the observer trajectory.
This important result is summarized in

Lemma 19
(boundedness of
\mathbf{P}(t)
).

Assume that the nonlinear system is in the form (
47
).
Moreover
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and at least twice continuously differentiable and the corresponding Hessian matrix of each component of
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is bounded.
Then, for all
\mathbf{P}(t_{0})\succeq\bm{0}
and all bounded
\mathbf{G}(t)\succeq\bm{0}
, there exists a constant
p_{2}>0
such that for the solution
\mathbf{P}(t)
of the corresponding Riccati equation (
37b
) it holds that
\mathbf{P}(t)\leq p_{2}\mathbf{I}_{n}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof for the bound with
\mathbf{P}(t_{0})\succ 0
is given in the proof of
[
39
, Theorem 1.1.1]
.
Remarkably, this bound is independent of the observer trajectory and hence it holds for any trajectory.
The bound is established via the design of a suboptimal high-gain observer, which gives an upper bound on the cost of the optimal filtering problem and hence an upper bound on the solution of the Riccati equation (
37b
).
The boundedness for all
\mathbf{P}(t_{0})\succeq\bm{0}
then follows from the order preserving property of the Riccati differential equation, see
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.4 and Corollary 4.1.5]
.

The previous result together with the specific system structure guarantees that the assumptions
(a1)
to
(a3)
are fulfilled.
Hence, the existence of the extended subspace observer proposed in Section
5.2
is guaranteed for this system class.
This is summarized in the following

Theorem 20
(existence of extended subspace observer).

For systems of the form (
47
), where
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and at least twice continuously differentiable and the corresponding Hessian matrix of each component of
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is bounded, the assumptions
(a1)
–
(a3)
are fulfilled for all
\mathbf{P}_{1}(0)\succ\bm{0}
and all
g_{1}\mathbf{I}\preceq\mathbf{G}_{1}(t)\preceq g_{2}\mathbf{I}
with arbitrary constants
0<g_{1}\leq g_{2}
.

Proof.

Assumptions
(a1)
and
(a3)
are guaranteed by the global Lipschitz assumption and the boundedness assumption on the Hessian, see also
Remark

16
.
Hence it remains to show that
(a2)
holds.
The upper bound on
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
follows straightforwardly from
Lemma

15
and
Lemma

19
for any positive definite and bounded
\mathbf{G}_{1}(t)
.
The proof that
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
is also bounded from below follows from continuity arguments.
Under the assumption that
\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
becomes positive semidefinite for some
t=t_{1}>t_{0}
, there exists a non-trivial vector
\mathbf{v}
such that
\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{1}(t_{1})\mathbf{v}=0
.
Multiplying the Riccati equation (
38b
) with
\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}
from the left and
\mathbf{v}
from the right and evaluating the derivative at
t=t_{1}
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{1}\mathbf{v}\big|_{t=t_{1}}=\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{G}_{1}(t_{1})\mathbf{v}\succ\bm{0}.

(48)

Due to continuity and since
\mathbf{v}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}_{1}(0)\mathbf{v}\succ>0
, the solution of (
38b
) must remain positive definite and lower bounded according to
p_{1}\mathbf{I}\preceq\mathbf{P}_{1}(t)
with some
p_{1}>0
.

Theorem

20
guarantees the existence of the extended subspace observer for systems in the form of (
47
).
The global Lipschitz continuity of
\bar{\mathbf{f}}
is also required in high-gain observer designs, see, e.g.,
[
25
]
.
This condition is restrictive since the Lipschitz condition is usually satisfied only locally.
If, however, the state
\mathbf{x}(t)
of system (
47
) lies in a bounded set, it is possible to relax this assumption using prolongation techniques, see
[
25
, Remark 2.1]
and the references therein.

6
Numerical results

The nonlinear Lorenz’96 (L’96) model introduced in
[
40
]
is widely used as a benchmark of data assimilation algorithms
[
58
,
17
,
26
]
.
It is a system of nonlinear differential equations recursively defined by

\dot{z}_{i}=(z_{i+1}-z_{i-2})z_{i-1}-z_{i}+F,\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(49)

with the notational convention
z_{-1}=z_{n-1}
,
z_{0}=z_{n}
, and
z_{n+1}=z_{1}
.
The state vector is
\mathbf{z}=[z_{1}\;\cdots\;z_{n}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.
For
F=8
, this model exhibits a chaotic behavior.
With this configuration, the model is used as a benchmark examples for data assimilation in
[
26
,
43
,
10
]
.

The following simulations are carried out for a model of order
n=18
.
The output of the model is chosen as
\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{C}_{p}\mathbf{z}(t)
with a constant
p\times n
matrix
\mathbf{C}_{p}
.
The rows of
\mathbf{C}_{p}
are chosen such that only one state is measured per row and the “sensors” are distributed with equal distance over all state variables.
The matrix is given by

\mathbf{C}_{p}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{b}_{1}&\mathbf{b}_{d+1}&\cdots&\mathbf{b}_{(p-1)d+1}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}},

(50)

where
\mathbf{b}_{i}
is the i-th standard basis vector and
d=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p}\right\rfloor
, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes rounding to the nearest integer towards zero.

The initial condition is chosen as
z_{i,0}=\sin\left(\frac{i-1}{n}2\pi\right)
at initial time
t_{0}=0
.
For evaluation purposes, also different initial conditions were chosen.
The obtained results were quantitatively similar and in particular the approximated Lyapunov exponents and the upper and lower Bohl exponents were approximately the same.

First, the spectral intervals of the system are investigated.
All differential equations are solved using fixed step 4th order Runge-Kutta integration with a step-size of
T_{s}=0.005
.
For solving the differential equation (
23b
) to obtain
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
, a projected integrator
[
21
]
is implemented, where the orthonormalization is carried out after each simulation time step.

The results for all
n=18
spectral intervals are given in
Table

1
in
Appendix

B
.
The final time of this simulation is chosen as
T_{f}=1500
in order to be able to choose the averaging window length
H
for the approximation of the exponential dichotomy spectral intervals sufficiently large.
For a window length of
H=300
, the
7^{\text{th}}
approximated upper Bohl exponent is positive whereas for an increased window length
H=800
, this value is negative.
The spectral intervals
\Lambda_{7}
to
\Lambda_{18}
are thus negative and moreover all intervals are disjoint.
The results show that the number of non-negative Lyapunov exponents
k^{\star}
is equal to the number of non-negative upper Bohl exponents
j^{\star}
.

Now, the design of the extended subspace observer is carried out for
p=5
as the dimension of the output vector
\mathbf{y}(t)
.
The initial estimate is chosen as
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{0}+\bm{\xi}_{0}
with
\bm{\xi}_{0}
as a random perturbation.
The components
\xi_{0,i}
of
\bm{\xi}_{0}
are chosen to be uniformly distributed on an interval
(-\delta,\delta)
with
\delta>0
as a simulation parameter.
For
k\leq 5<j^{\star}
, no convergence of the estimation error could be achieved independently of the size of the initial perturbation.
This coincides with the observations from the approximated spectral intervals.
Hence, in a first step,
k=k^{\star}=j^{\star}=6
is chosen in the observer design and an ensemble simulation with
N=50
simulation runs is carried out.
The matrix
\mathbf{G}_{{1}}(t)
in the differential Riccati equation (
38b
) is chosen as a constant matrix
\mathbf{G}_{{1}}=10\mathbf{I}_{k}
in the following simulations.

The (point-wise) minimum and maximum estimation errors together with the median and the 80%-quantile are depicted in Fig.
1
.
The latter quantity is a (point-wise) upper bound for 80% of the estimation errors.
The expected minimum convergence rate of the observer error, i.e., the approximated 7-th upper Bohl exponent is also depicted.
This indicates that the estimation error converges exponentially with the rate
\beta_{7}^{800}\approx-0.0320
.
This convergence rate is very small and, as predicted by theory, the stability of the nonlinear error system is very sensitive to the size of the initial estimation error
\mathbf{e}_{0}
.
This behavior can be seen in the simulations and hence the interval bound for the uniform distribution of the initial error is chosen as
\delta=10^{-4}
.

In order to increase the convergence speed and to decrease sensitivity with respect to the magnitude of the initial perturbation, the number of columns in
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
was increased to
k=7
.
The results of this ensemble simulation is depicted in Fig.
2
.
The resulting exponential convergence is now achieved at an approximate rate of
\beta_{8}^{800}\approx-0.2760
, because this is the first Bohl exponent which is not modified by the observer feedback gain.
The convergence is faster compared to
k=6
and moreover the robustness with respect to the initial perturbation is improved. Hence, the size of the initial perturbation could be increased to
\delta=10^{-3}
.
Towards the end of the simulation, the estimation error norm saturates around
10^{-13}
due to numerical integration and floating point error.

Taking into account one more spectral interval to be modified via the observer gain, the convergence speed can again be increased as depicted in Fig.
3
for
k=8
.
The size of the initial perturbation was again increased to
\delta=10^{-2}
and a convergence of the estimation error was achieved for all simulation runs.

In order to analyze the detectability properties of the Lorenz’96 model, the observer was implemented for
k=j^{\star}=6
with the matrix
\mathbf{G}_{{1}}(t)=\mathbf{0}
in (
38b
).
Then, the obtained differential Riccati equation is a differential equation for the inverse of the constructibility Gramian on the considered subspace.
This inverse, i.e.,
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t_{0})
, cannot be initialized correctly, because the constructibility Gramian is zero at the initial time.
However, independently of the positive definite initialization, all solutions of
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)
converge to each other and hence to the true inverse asymptotically, see
[
49
, Sec. 3]
.

This suggests that if
\mathbf{P}_{{1}}(t)
is upper bounded, the constructibility Gramian is lower bounded and the linearization along the estimated trajectory is uniformly completely constructible on the considered subspace.
The largest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)
for different measurement configurations is depicted in Fig.
4
.
This result indicates that the considered trajectory is uniformly completely observable on the unstable subspace for any number of measurements.

An additional insight can be obtained by the investigation of the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{P}{{}_{1}}(t)
.
The number of measurements was now again chosen to be
p=5
.
If one considers only the unstable modes in the observer gain, i.e.
k=6
, the smallest eigenvalue is uniformly bounded from below.
However, if one takes into account an additional mode in the observer gain by choosing
k=7
, the smallest eigenvalue tends to zero, see Fig.

.
This indicates that the observer gain is “losing strength” in the already uniformly asymptotically stable directions.
This is avoided by choosing
\mathbf{G}(t)
as a positive definite matrix as proposed in the present observer design.
A similar effect is also discussed for a deterministic interpretation of the Kalman-Bucy filter in
[
49
, Remark 3.2]
and is also well recognized in data assimilation on the unstable subspace
[
58
,
11
]
.

Conclusions and Outlook

This work proposes a new observer design strategy for linear time-varying and nonlinear systems.
Stability proofs for the estimation error dynamics show global exponential stability in the time-varying case and local exponential stability in the nonlinear case.
The feedback gain is computed only for the unstable modes of the estimation error dynamics, which allows to reduce the computational complexity of the observer compared to classical approaches like the extended Kalman-Bucy filter.
Simulation results moreover suggest that additionally including stable modes in the design may increase the robustness in the nonlinear case.
In future research, the assignment of the exponential dichotomy spectrum will be investigated.
This may foster the development of efficient observer designs, which do not require the solution of a (computationally costly) differential Riccati equation.
Moreover, the conditions for the existence of the proposed observer will be investigated in more detail.
For this purpose, it is important to guarantee the upper bound of the observer Riccati equation independent of the observer trajectory.
For systems which are fully observed on the unstable subspace, it may be possible to guarantee this upper bound by adapting the bound from
[
36
]
.

Proof of
Theorem

10

The proof for relation (
21
) can be found in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 8.4]
.
Relation (
22
) is proven for one upper Bohl exponents
\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}
.
First, assume an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.
The scalar system
\dot{\eta}(t)=(b_{jj}(t)-\lambda)\eta(t)
with
\lambda=\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon
has an exponential dichotomy with
P=1
and, equivalently, there exist reals
\alpha>0
and
d\geq 0
such that

-\ln\|\mathbf{\Phi}(t,t_{0})\|=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{{\left(\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon-b_{jj}(s)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}{s}}\geq\alpha(t-t_{0})-d

(51)

holds for all
t_{0}\in\mathds{J}
and all
t\geq t_{0}
.
For a sufficiently large
H_{0}>0
such that
\alpha-d/H_{0}>\alpha/2
, it follows that

\frac{1}{H}\int_{t}^{t+H}{{\left(\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon-b_{jj}(s)\right)}\,\mathrm{d}{s}}\geq\alpha-\frac{d}{H}>\frac{\alpha}{2}

(52)

holds for all
H\geq H_{0}
.
Splitting the integral in (
52
) yields

\frac{1}{H}\int_{t}^{t+H}{{\left(\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon\right)}\,\mathrm{d}{s}}-\frac{1}{H}\int_{t}^{t+H}{{b_{jj}(s)}\,\mathrm{d}{s}}=\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon-\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{j}>\frac{\alpha}{2},

(53)

and hence
\beta^{\mathrm{s}}_{j}+\varepsilon>\beta^{H,\mathrm{s}}_{j}
for all
H\geq H_{0}
.
The proof for the remaining upper and lower Bohl exponents follows analogously.

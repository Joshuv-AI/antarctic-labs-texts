Erroneous proofs of the wildness of some automorphisms of free metabelian Lie algebras


Abstract
. The well known Bachmuth-Mochizuki-Roman’kov Theorem [ 2 , 31 ] states that every automorphism of the free metabelian group of rank ≥ 4 \geq 4 is tame. In 1992 Yu. Bahturin and S. Nabiyev [ 3 ] claimed that every nontrivial inner automorphism of the free metabelian Lie algebra M n M_{n} of any rank n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 over a field of characteristic zero is wild. More examples of wild automorphisms of M n M_{n} of rank n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 were given in 2008 by Z. Özcurt and N. Ekici [ 27 ] . The main goal of this note is to show that both articles contain uncorrectable errors and to draw the attention of specialists to the fact that the question of tame and wild automorphisms for free metabelian Lie algebras M n M_{n} of rank n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 is still widely open.

The automorphism groups of commutative and associative algebras generated by three elements are much more complicated.
The well-known Nagata automorphism
[
25

\displaystyle\sigma=(x+2y(zx-y^{2})+z(zx-y^{2})^{2},y+z(zx-y^{2}),z)

[
46

The Nagata
automorphism gives an example of a wild

It is well known

In 1981 Shafarevich

and in 1983 Anick

independently proved that every automorphism of the polynomial algebra

In 1964 P. Cohn proved

Later this result was extended to free algebras of Nielsen-Schreier varieties

The varieties of all non-associative algebras

]

Some other examples of Nielsen-Schreier

It was recently shown

In particular, every automorphism of a free right-symmetric and a free Lie-admissable algebra of finite rank is tame. The tameness of automorphisms of free right-symmetric algebras in two variables over any field was proven in

One of the classical and well studied varieties of algebras is the variety of metabelian Lie algebras. Let

Obviously, the group of tame automorphisms of

In 1992, V. Drensky

In 1992 Yu. Bahturin and S. Nabiyev

2.
On the paper by Bahturin and Nabiyev

2.1.
The structure of
M_{n}

Let
M_{n}
be the free metabelian Lie algebra over a field
K
freely generated generated by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. We give a description of
M_{n}
using Shmel’kin’s wreath products
[
39
]
. Let
Y_{n}
be the abelian Lie algebra with a linear basis
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}
. The universal enveloping algebra
U(Y_{n})
is the polynomial algebra
U(Y_{n})=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Let
T_{n}
be the free (left)
U(Y_{n})
-module with a basis
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
. Consider the direct sum

\displaystyle M=Y_{n}\oplus T_{n}.

Turn
M
into a Lie algebra by

\displaystyle[a+t,b+s]=as-bt

where
a,b\in Y_{n}
and
t,s\in T_{n}
. Then the subalgebra of
M
generated by

\displaystyle x_{i}=y_{i}+t_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

is the free metabelian Lie algebra
M_{n}
with the free variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

[
39
]
.

2.2.
The Jacobian matrix of an endomorphism of
M_{n}

Let
f\in M_{n}
be an arbitrary element
M_{n}
. Then
f
is uniquely represented as

\displaystyle f=y+t,y\in Y_{n},t\in T_{n}.

Moreover,
t
is uniquely represented as

\displaystyle t=d_{1}t_{1}+\ldots+d_{n}t_{n},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]=U(Y_{n}).

We call
d_{i}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
the Fox derivatives of
f
(see
[
43
]
). Set

\displaystyle\partial(f)=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})=\big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\big).

Also set
Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})^{t}
, where
t
means transpose. There is a well known statement (
[
43
, Lemma 5]
) that says
f\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
if and only if

(1)

\displaystyle\partial(f)Y=0.

Let
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
be an arbitrary endomorphism of
M_{n}
such that
\phi(x_{i})=f_{i}
for all
i
. In particilar,
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the identity automorphism. We define the Jacobian matrix of
\phi
by

\displaystyle J(\phi)=[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial(f_{1})\\
\ldots\\
\partial(f_{n})\\
\end{array}\right].

If
\phi=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
and
\psi=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
then

\displaystyle\phi\circ\psi(x_{i})=g_{i}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. The corresponding Chain Rule gives
[
43
]
that

(3)

\displaystyle J(\phi\circ\psi)=\overline{\phi}(J(\psi))J(\phi),

where
\overline{\phi}
denotes the endomorphism of
K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
, first induced by
\phi
to
Y_{n}
and then extended to
U(Y_{n})
.

Denote by
\mathrm{IAut}\,M_{n}
the group of all automorphisms of
M_{n}
that induces the identity automorphism on
M_{n}/[M_{n},M_{n}]
.

Lemma 1
.

The map

\displaystyle J:\mathrm{IAut}(M_{n})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(U(Y_{n}))

is an injective antimorphism of groups.

This is a direct corollary of (
3
) since
\overline{\phi}=\mathrm{id}
.

Remark 1
.

The matrix
E+QA_{n}
in
[
3
, Theorem 2]
is the Jacobian matrix of an automorphism.
It is mentioned (without proof) in the middle of
[
3
, Theorem 2]
that
J
is an isomorphism. Sometimes it depends on the definition of composition of automorphisms.

2.3.
Tame automorphisms

The group of tame automorphisms
\mathrm{TAut}\,M_{n}
is generated by all linear automorphisms and the automorphisms of the form

\displaystyle\phi_{f}=(x_{1}+f(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),f\in[M_{n},M_{n}].

Notice that

\displaystyle J(\phi_{f})=E+\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\partial(f)\\
0\\
\ldots\\
0\\
\end{array}\right]=E+e_{1}\partial(f),

where
E
is the identity matrix and
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)^{t}
, since
\partial(f)=(0,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}})
. Notice that
\partial(f)e_{1}=0
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\alpha
be an arbitrary linear automorphism of
M_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle J(\alpha\phi_{f}\alpha^{-1})=E+\Phi\Psi,

where
\Phi
is a column vector and
\Psi
is a row vector such that

\displaystyle\Psi\Phi=0,\Psi Y=0.

Proof.
Let
J(\alpha)=A
. Using (
3
) we get

\displaystyle J(\alpha\phi_{f}\alpha^{-1})=\overline{\alpha}(J(\phi_{f}\alpha^{-1})J(\alpha)=\overline{\alpha}(\overline{\phi_{f}}(J(\alpha^{-1}))J(\phi_{f}))A

\displaystyle=\overline{\alpha}(A^{-1}J(\phi_{f}))A=A^{-1}\overline{\alpha}(J(\phi_{f}))A=A^{-1}\overline{\alpha}(E+e_{1}\partial(f))A

\displaystyle=A^{-1}(E+e_{1}\overline{\alpha}(\partial(f)))A=E+A^{-1}e_{1}\overline{\alpha}(\partial(f))A=E+\Phi\Psi,

where
\Phi=A^{-1}e_{1}
and
\Psi=\overline{\alpha}(\partial(f))A
. We have

\displaystyle\Psi\Phi=\overline{\alpha}(\partial(f))e_{1}=\overline{\alpha}(\partial(f)e_{1})=0.

Obviously,

\displaystyle\alpha\phi_{f}\alpha^{-1}=X+\alpha(f,0,\ldots,0)^{t}\alpha^{-1}=X+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})^{t}

for some
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
. Consequently,

\displaystyle J(\alpha\phi_{f}\alpha^{-1})=E+\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\partial(f_{1})\\
\ldots\\
\partial(f_{n})\\
\end{array}\right]

and

\displaystyle\Phi\Psi=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial(f_{1})\\
\ldots\\
\partial(f_{n})\\
\end{array}\right].

By (
1
) we get

\displaystyle\Phi\Psi Y=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial(f_{1})\\
\ldots\\
\partial(f_{n})\\
\end{array}\right]Y=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\partial(f_{1})Y\\
\ldots\\
\partial(f_{n})Y\\
\end{array}\right]=0.

Notice that
\Psi Y
, the product of a row and a column, belongs to
U(Y_{n})
. Since
\Phi=A^{-1}e_{1}
is a nonzero column it follows that
\Psi Y=0
.
\Box

2.4.
Inner automorphisms

Let
0\neq z\in[M_{n},M_{n}]
. Then
\mathrm{ad}\,z:M_{n}\to M_{n}(m\mapsto[z,m])
is a locally nilpotent derivation and
(\mathrm{ad}\,z)^{2}=0
. The
exponential
automorphism of
M_{n}

\displaystyle\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,z)=\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{ad}\,z=(x_{1}+[z,x_{1}],\ldots,x_{n}+[z,x_{n}])^{t}.

is called

.

Notice that
\partial([z,x_{1}])=-y_{1}\partial(z)
since
z\in[M_{n},M_{n}]\subseteq T_{n}
and
[z,x_{1}]=[z,y_{1}+t_{1}]=-y_{1}z
. Consequently,

\displaystyle J(\exp(\mathrm{ad}\,z))=E-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{1}\partial(z)\\
\ldots\\
y_{n}\partial(z)\\
\end{array}\right]=E-Y\partial(z).

2.5.
Wild automorphisms

Theorem 1
.

[
3

Every nontrivial inner automorphism

Proof.
Suppose that

is tame. Then

\displaystyle(E+\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1})\ldots(E+\Phi_{n}\Psi_{n})=E-Y\partial(z).

From now on we deal only with the matrix multiplication. Recall that

are columns and

(12)

\displaystyle(E+\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1})(E+\Phi_{2}\Psi_{2})(E+\Phi_{3}\Psi_{3})=E-Y\partial(z).

Set

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1}+\Phi_{2}\Psi_{2}+\Phi_{3}\Psi_{3}+\Phi_{1}\lambda_{12}\Psi_{2}+\Phi_{1}\lambda_{13}\Psi_{3}+\Phi_{2}\lambda_{23}\Psi_{3}

(13)

\displaystyle+\Phi_{1}\lambda_{12}\lambda_{23}\Psi_{3}=-Y\partial(z).

Notice that

\displaystyle\lambda_{12}\Psi_{2}+\lambda_{13}\Psi_{3}+\lambda_{12}\lambda_{23}\Psi_{3}=0.

2.6.
The mistake

\displaystyle(E+\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1})(E+\Phi_{2}\Psi_{2})(E-\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1})(E+\Phi_{1}\Psi_{1})(E+\Phi_{3}\Psi_{3})

Odd Dimensional Nonlocal Liouville Conformal Field Theories
Amitay C. Kislev, Tom Levy, Yaron Oz

Abstract
We construct Euclidean Liouville conformal field theories in odd number of dimensions. The theories are nonlocal and non-unitary with a log-correlated Liouville field, a 𝒬 {\cal Q} -curvature background, and an exponential Liouville-type potential. We study the classical and quantum properties of these theories including the finite entanglement entropy part of the sphere partition function F F , the boundary conformal anomaly and vertex operators’ correlation functions. We derive the analogue of the even-dimensional DOZZ formula and its semi-classical approximation.

II
Classical Nonlocal Liouville CFT

II.1
Weyl Symmetry

Consider the Liouville CFT defined by the action (
1

g_{ab}\rightarrow e^{2\sigma}g_{ab}\ ,

(5)

S_{L}(\phi-Q\sigma,e^{2\sigma}g)=S_{L}(\phi,g)-S_{CG}(Q\sigma,g)\ ,

(9)

Equation (

{\cal P}_{g}\sigma+{\cal{Q}}_{g}={\cal{Q}}_{e^{2\sigma}g}e^{d\sigma}\ .

(10)

If we take

to be a real constant

From equation (

When

III
Quantum Nonlocal Liouville CFT

In this section we study the quantum aspects of the odd-dimensional nonlocal Liouville CFT. We study the A-type conformal anomaly, find the finite part of the free energy in odd dimensions (entanglement entropy) and construct the correlation functions and structure constants of the theory.

III.1
Conformal Anomaly

We begin by studying the conformal anomaly of the free Coulomb gas theory
S_{CG}
. As for local CFTs, there is only a boundary quantum anomaly and no quantum bulk one. The bulk contribution to the trace anomaly is the classical contribution from the background charge.
The calculation of the anomaly follows

Dowker:2010qy
;
Dowker:2013mba
.

Preforming the Gaussian integration of (
1
) for
\mu=0

W=-\log(Z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\det\left(\frac{d}{\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}\sqrt{g}\mathcal{P}_{g}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}\right)^{2}Q^{2}\mathcal{Q}_{g}D\mathcal{Q}_{g}\ ,

(28)

where
D
is the propagator of the free theory:

(\sqrt{g}\mathcal{P}_{g})_{x}D(x,y)=\frac{\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}{d}\delta^{d}(x,y)\ ,

(29)

and we use the notation:

\mathcal{Q}_{g}D\mathcal{Q}_{g}\equiv\int d^{d}xd^{d}y\left(\sqrt{g}\mathcal{Q}_{g}\right)_{x}D(x,y)\left(\sqrt{g}\mathcal{Q}_{g}\right)_{y}\ .

(30)

The second term in equation (
28

9
). This equation shows that at the classical level the action shifts by a geometric quantity under Weyl transformation. The classical term in (
28

We can explicitly calculate the geometric quantity

for the sphere of radius

We can now calculate

III.2
Entanglement Entropy

The sphere partition function of a conformal field theory in odd number of dimensions,
d=2n+1
, generally has an expansion of the form:

F_{S^{2n+1}}\equiv-\log Z_{S^{2n+1}}=c_{2n+1}(R\Lambda_{UV})^{2n+1}+c_{2n-1}(R\Lambda_{UV})^{2n-1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+c_{1}(R\Lambda_{UV})+F_{2n+1}\ ,

(41)

where
R
is the radius of the sphere and
\Lambda_{UV}
is the UV cutoff.
The coefficients
c_{k}
are regularization dependent, however, the universal finite number
F_{2n+1}
is a physical observable
that can be interpreted as the entanglement entropy for a spherical entangling hypersurface.

In equations (
28
), (
32
) we saw that for the Liouville field theory, the sphere free energy also includes a logarithmic term in
R
, which is not accounted for in (
41
). This term, together with an additional constant term, are proportional to the square of the background charge
Q^{2}
, and originate from a classical geometric term in the quantum action. The constant term proportional to
Q^{2}
is ambiguous as a result of the classical logarithmic term, and its value is dependent on the non-physical re-scaling parameter
\rho
in (
23
). Thus, it cannot contribute to the physical observable
F_{2n+1}
, which is therefore independent of the background charge. The quantum contribution to
F_{2n+1}
which is independent of
Q
is well-defined and can be calculated.

In this section we will calculate finite part of the free energy
F_{2n+1}
for the odd-dimensional Liouville conformal field theory.
Conformal invariance implies that
F_{2n+1}
does not depend on marginal couplings of a conformal field theory
Gerchkovitz:2014gta
. In the case of nonlocal Liouville theory that means that
F_{2n+1}

\mu

The sphere partition function of the Gaussian theory is formally given by

F=-\log\left(Z\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log\det\left(\mathcal{P}_{g\left(S^{d}\right)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}K_{d,l^{\prime}}\log\Lambda_{l^{\prime}},

(42)

F=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}K_{d,l^{\prime}}\log\Lambda_{l^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{l=n+1}^{\infty}l^{2}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\log\left(l\prod_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n}\left(l^{2}-k^{\prime 2}\right)\right).

(45)

(46)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\frac{\pi}{(2n)!}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(t^{2}-j^{2}\right)\cot(\pi t)+\frac{t}{n^{2}-t^{2}}\right)\ ,

III.3
Multiplicative Anomaly

In the previous section we’ve calculated the functional determinant (
42
) of the nonlocal GJMS operator
\mathcal{P}_{g}
operator on the sphere. To do so, we’ve written the corresponding zeta function and calculated its derivative at zero, as detailed in appendix
A
.

As can be seen in (
43
), the eigenvalues on the sphere can be factored into a product of simple terms, each linear in the summation variable. If we could distribute the logarithm of the eigenvalues into a sum of logarithms of linear terms, then the calculation would be significantly simpler. However, such naive factorization is not always possible, since in general for two pseudo-differential operators
A,B
we can have
\det(AB)\neq\det(A)\det(B)
. This is known as the multiplicative anomaly (for a review see e.g.
Elizalde:1997nd
), and is a result of regularization.

Nevertheless, as it turns out in our specific case the multiplicative anomaly exactly vanishes, a fact which is proved in appendix
B
. This allows us to preform an alternative computation of the sphere partition function, which results in a different equivalent mathematical expression for the finite part of the free energy
F_{2n+1}
.

Knowing that there is no multiplicative anomaly, we can freely reorganize and manipulate the free energy sum,

F=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}K_{d,l^{\prime}}\log\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(l^{\prime}+k\right)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}K_{d,l^{\prime}}\log\left(l^{\prime}+k\right)\ .

(47)

This sum can be further simplified by defining
l=l^{\prime}+k
and noting that for
-d+1<l^{\prime}<0
the formal expression for the degeneracy (
18
) vanishes, i.e.
K_{d,l^{\prime}}=0
, in addition to
K_{d,0}=1
. This allows us to extend the sum to
l^{\prime}\geq-k+1\implies l\geq 1
, by subtracting the additional terms,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{2n}K_{d,l-k}\right)\log(l)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}\sum_{l=1}^{k}K_{d,l-k}\log(l)

(48)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{2n}K_{d,l-k}\right)\log(l)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left((2n)!\right)\ ,

where again we’ve written the odd dimension as
d=2n+1
. We’ll define the inner sum as a function of
l
, which by explicit calculation using (
44
) is given by:

K_{n}(l)\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{2n}K_{d,l-k}=\frac{2n+1+2l}{2n+1}{{2n+l}\choose{2n}}+\frac{2n+1-2l}{2n+1}{{l-1}\choose{2n}}\ .

(49)

The function
K_{n}(l)
is even with respect to
l
so we can expand it as a polynomial with even powers only,
K_{n}(l)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}k_{j,n}l^{2j}
. Using
K_{n}(0)=2
we get:

F=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{k_{j,n}l^{2j}}\log(l)+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\log(l)-\frac{1}{2}\log((2n)!)\ .

(50)

We can now use zeta regularization, which gives us an alternative expression for
F_{2n+1}
, the finite part of the sphere partition function:

F_{2n+1}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left(-1\right)^{j+1}\frac{\left(2j\right)!}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2j}}k_{j,n}\zeta\left(2j+1\right)}+\frac{1}{2}\log{\frac{2\pi}{\left(2n\right)!}}\ .

(51)

Here we’ve used zeta regularization to calculate the sum in the first line. We have verified that this new expression numerically agrees with (
46
) for the values presented in the table, which demonstrates the lack of multiplicative anomaly.

III.4
Correlation Functions

We study the theory (
1
) on the sphere, and using the Weyl invariance of the action we preform a Weyl transformation to flat space. This transformation (
23
) is singular, and concentrates all of the non-trivial curvature at infinity.

To regularize the action, we introduce an IR cutoff and study the theory on the ball
B^{d}
with radius
R_{B}\to\infty
. The non-trivial curvature at infinity is then homogeneously spread on the boundary sphere
\partial B_{d}
. The action is defined as follows:

\displaystyle S_{L}=\frac{d}{2\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}\int_{B_{d}}d^{d}x\,\phi(-\Delta)^{\frac{d}{2}}\phi+\frac{dQ}{\Omega_{d-1}}\int_{\partial B_{d}}d^{d-1}\Omega\,\phi+\mu\int_{B_{d}}d^{d}xe^{db\phi}\ .

(52)

In this setting, the relevant eigenfunctions for the spectral decomposition are therefore the ball eigenfunctions (
19
).
The propagator in the Coulomb gas theory can be obtained with the help of IR regularization. Using the spectral decomposition for the nonlocal operator
(-\Delta)^{\frac{d}{2}}
, we get the following propagator,

\displaystyle\langle\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle=\mathcal{P}^{-1}\delta(x-y)\approx\frac{2}{d}\int_{\frac{\pi}{L_{\infty}}}^{\infty}\frac{\cos{p|x-y|}}{p}dp=\frac{2}{d}\log\frac{L_{\infty}}{|x-y|}+O(1),

(53)

where
L_{\infty}
is a the IR cutoff in length dimension.

Just as in the even dimensional theories, the vertex operators are defined as:

\displaystyle V_{\alpha}(x)=e^{d\alpha\phi(x)}\ .

(54)

These are the primary conformal operators of the theory, with dimension given by:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}=d\alpha(Q-\alpha).

(55)

Requiring that the potential term in the action
e^{db\phi}
has the correct dimension,
\Delta_{b}=d
, determines the background charge:

\displaystyle Q=b+\frac{1}{b},

(56)

and in the classic limit
b\rightarrow 0
we go back to the classical value
Q\to\frac{1}{b}
.
In order to obtain the correlation functions of the C.G. theory, we use the 2-point function (
53
), for vertex operators satisfying the condition of charge conservation
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}=Q
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{i}V_{\alpha_{i}}\left(x_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{C.G.}=\prod_{i\neq i^{\prime}}\left|x_{i}-x_{i^{\prime}}\right|^{2d\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i^{\prime}}}\ .

(57)

With the help of this result and by using the decomposition of the path integral measure into a zero mode
\phi_{0}
and non-zero modes, i.e.
D\phi=D\phi\,d\phi_{0}\delta\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\sqrt{g}d^{d}x\phi-\phi_{0}\right)
, we can calculate a formal expression for the correlation functions in nonlocal Liouville theory,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle&\equiv\int D\phi e^{-S}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{d\alpha_{i}\phi(x_{i})}\\
=\frac{\Gamma(-s)\mu^{s}}{db}&\left\langle\left(\int d^{d}ye^{db\phi\left(y\right)}\right)^{s}\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{d\alpha_{i}\phi\left(x_{i}\right)}\right\rangle_{CG},\end{aligned}

(58)

where we have defined,

\displaystyle s=\frac{Q-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}}{b},

(59)

and
\left\langle\dots\right\rangle_{CG}
refers to expectation value in the Coulomb Gas theory without zero-mode.
Thus, we see that the correlation function have poles at
s=n
for non-negative integer
n
. We can write the residues at these poles as integrals over free field correlation functions:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \underset{s=n}{\mathrm{Res}}\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle&=\\
=\frac{(-\mu)^{n}}{n!}&\int d^{d}y_{1}\cdots d^{d}y_{n}\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{b}\left(y_{i}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{N}V_{\alpha_{k}}\left(x_{k}\right)\right\rangle_{CG}\\
=\frac{(-\mu)^{n}}{n!}&\prod_{k>l=1}^{N}\left|x_{k}-x_{l}\right|^{-2d\alpha_{k}\alpha_{l}}\\
&\int d^{nd}y\prod_{i>j=1}^{n}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{-2db^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left|y_{i}-x_{j}\right|^{-2db\alpha_{j}}\ .\end{aligned}

(60)

Note that the requirement
s=n
can now be translated to a conservation of the background charge in the Coulomb Gas theory, i.e.
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}+nb=Q
.

Of particular interest in a CFT are the 3-point correlation functions, which by conformal invariance are constrained to have the form,

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)V_{\alpha_{2}}\left(x_{2}\right)V_{\alpha_{3}}\left(x_{3}\right)\right\rangle=\frac{C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}}|x_{2}-x_{3}|^{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-\Delta_{1}}|x_{3}-x_{1}|^{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}}}.

(61)

Here the coefficients
C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
are the structure constants of the theory, and they play a key role in determining the properties of the CFT.

In appendix
C
we detail the calculation of the 3-point residue integrals following
Furlan:2018jlv
, which also preformed the analytic continuation required to get the exact structure constants,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)=&\,\left(-\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}\mu\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b\right)^{-d(1+b^{2})}\right)^{\frac{Q-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}}{b}}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{d}}\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)^{\prime}}(0)}{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}\left(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-Q\right)}\\
&\times\frac{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(2\alpha_{1})\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(2\alpha_{2})\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(2\alpha_{3})}{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3})\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{1})\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\ .\end{aligned}

(62)

As is further detailed in appendix
C
, the special function
\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)
is a
d
-dimensional generalization of the Upsilon function
\Upsilon_{b}(x)
, used in 2-dimensional Liouville CFT. To write its expression, we first define the special function
\Gamma_{w}(x)\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{2}(x|w,w^{-1})}{\Gamma_{2}(w/2+w^{-1}/2|w,w^{-1})}
, where
\Gamma_{2}(x|w_{1},w_{2})
is the Double Gamma function. We can now define the
d
-dimensional generalization of the Upsilon function
\Upsilon_{b}
, namely

\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}x\right)\Gamma_{\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}(Q-x)\right)}\ .

(63)

This definition satisfies
\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)=\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(Q-x)
, and in the two-dimensional case we return to the usual Upsilon function,
\Upsilon_{b}^{(2)}(x)=\Upsilon_{b}(x)
.

III.5
Semi-Classical Approximation

The semi-classical limit of Liouville CFT is given by
b\rightarrow 0
. In this limit the background charge (
56
) approaches its classical value and the path integral defining the correlation functions (
58
) is dominated by the saddle point of the action. In this limit we can use the saddle point approximation to calculate the semi-classical value of the correlators.
In order to simplify the calculations, we define the classical Liouville field
\phi_{c}=b\phi
.
The action on the sphere (
52
) can then be written in this limit as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{L}(\phi_{c})=\frac{d}{2\Omega_{d}(d-1)!b^{2}}\int_{B^{d}}d^{d}x\,\phi_{c}\mathcal{P}_{g}\phi_{c}+\mu\int_{B^{d}}e^{d\phi_{c}}+\frac{d}{\Omega_{d-1}b^{2}}\int_{\partial B^{d}}d^{d-1}\Omega\phi_{c}+O(b^{0}).\end{aligned}

(64)

In this section we will calculate the semi-classical limit of correlation functions of ”light” vertex operators (
54
), defined as operators
V_{\alpha_{i}}
with
\alpha_{i}=O(b)
. As we have shown in the even dimensional case
Levy:2018bdc
, in these conditions the corresponding path integral has no real saddle points. To overcome this, we consider correlation functions with fixed area
A
, which is defined as
A=\int d^{d}xe^{db\phi}
, by inserting into the path integral the identity element
1=\int_{0}^{\infty}dA\delta\left(A-\int d^{d}xe^{db\phi}\right)
. This insertion gives us:

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dA}{A}e^{-\mu A}\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle_{A}

(65)

\displaystyle=(\mu A)^{s}\Gamma(-s)\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle_{A}\ .

Here
\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle_{A}
is the fixed area correlation function, and we have used equation (
58
). The fixed area path integral for light vertex operators does have real saddle points, which allows us to calculate the structure constants (
61
) in the semi-classical approximation. The saddle points are given by solutions to the fixed area equation of motion, which can be obtained by using a Lagrange multiplier:

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{d/2}\phi_{c}=\frac{\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}{A}e^{d\phi_{c}}.

(66)

Thus saddle points correspond to Weyl factors that describe manifolds with positive constant
\mathcal{Q}
-curvature and finite area. As we have shown in equations (
23
), (
26
), one such solution is the Weyl factor of the sphere with radius
A
:

\displaystyle\phi_{c}=-\log\left(\frac{\rho^{2}+r^{2}}{\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{d}\log\left(\frac{2^{d}A}{\Omega_{d}}\right)\ .

(67)

Following the calculation done previously in
Levy:2018bdc
, by the conformal invariance of the theory, all other relevant saddle points are given by conformal transformations of the solution (
67
). The semi-classical approximation of the correlation functions is then given by summing over all saddle points, i.e. integrating over the moduli space of saddle points, which is the conformal group modulo its subgroup that leaves (
67
) invariant. When changing the path integral into an integral over this moduli space we introduce some
b
-dependent Jacobian
\mathcal{A}(b)
, which can be shown to satisfy
\log(\mathcal{A}(b))=O(\log b)
. Finally, explicitly preforming the integration for the 3-point function of light vertex operators, we get the semi-classical approximation of the structure constants for
\alpha_{i}=O(b)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\approx&\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{2}\mathcal{A}(b)\Gamma\left(\frac{\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}-Q}{b}\right)\left(\frac{\Omega_{d}}{2^{d}}\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}}{b}}e^{-\frac{S_{bulk}}{b^{2}}}\Gamma\left[\frac{d}{2b}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})\right]\\
&\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2b}(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{1})\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2b}(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2b}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3})\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{b}\alpha_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{b}\alpha_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{b}\alpha_{3}\right)}\ .\end{aligned}

(68)

Here
S_{bulk}
is the value of the bulk action defined by,

S_{bulk}=\frac{d}{2\Omega_{d}(d-1)!}\int_{B^{d}}d^{d}r\phi_{c}(-\Delta)^{d/2}\phi_{c},

(69)

which is evaluated for the solution (
67
) for the unit sphere, i.e.
A=\Omega_{d}
. Preforming this calculation gives us the value:

S_{bulk}=\frac{2^{d-1}d}{\Omega_{d}}\int d^{d}r\left(\frac{-\log(1+r^{2})}{(1+r^{2})^{d}}\right)=-\frac{d}{2}\left(\psi^{(0)}(d)-\psi^{(0)}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)\right)\ .

(70)

IV
Discussion

Liouville conformal field theories provide examples of solvable interacting CFTs in dimensions higher that two. In this work we considered these theories in odd number of dimensions where they are nonlocal.
We studied their classical and quantum properties and found that they exhibit similar structures to that of the local even-dimensional Liouville CFTs.

There are various directions to follow.
We derived the exact DOZZ formula (
62
),
and independently its semi-classical approximation (
68
).
Matching the sum over saddle points calculation and the limit
b\rightarrow 0
of the exact calculation is an interesting open problem that requires a generalization of the methods used for the even-dimensional case in
Furlan:2018jlv
.

The stress energy tensor of these theories is nonlocal. It will be important to construct it,
understand the structure of the nonlocal generators of the conformal algebra and calculate the charges such as
C_{T}
of these theories. In fact, a general classification of possible anomaly terms of these nonlocal field theories is still lacking.

Renormalization group flow properties of higher-dimensional Liouvllile theories are largely unexplored.
Being non-unitary in dimensions higher than two, it is not clear whether there are monotonicity theorems
corresponding to anomaly and
F
charges.
Finally, adding boundaries and defects to these theories may shed new light on the structure of these theories.

Appendix A
Zeta Regularization of the Sphere Partition Function

In this section we compute the finite part of the formal infinite sum (
45
),

F=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{\infty}K_{d,l}\log\left(l\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\right)\ ,

(71)

by employing the technique of zeta regularization. We begin by writing the identity,

F=-\frac{1}{(2n)!}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\sum_{l=n+1}^{\infty}l^{2}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\left(l\prod_{k^{\prime}=1}^{n}\left(l^{2}-k^{\prime 2}\right)\right)^{-s}.

(72)

Written this way, we note that for
s<1
the expression we sum over actually vanishes for
l=0,1,\dots,n-1
. Therefore, we can replace the sum over
l\geq n+1
with a sum over
l\neq n
.

We can then expand the eigenvalues (
43
) and degeneracies (
44
) as a polynomial in
l
using,

\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[l^{2}-k^{2}\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j,n}l^{2j},\quad a_{j,n}=\left(-1\right)^{j+n}\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\ldots<k_{j}\leq n}\frac{\left[n!\right]^{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}^{2}}\ ,

(73)

and use this expression to simplify
F
. We express each term in the sum over eigenvalues as:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{l}^{-s}

\displaystyle=l^{-s}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j,n}l^{2j}\right)^{-s}

(74)

\displaystyle=l^{-(2n+1)s}\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{0}+\dots+m_{n-1}=M}{-s\choose M}{M\choose m_{0},\dots,m_{n-1}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(a_{j,n}l^{-2(n-j)}\right)^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\sum_{j}m_{j}=M}{-s\choose M}{M\choose m_{0},\dots,m_{n-1}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j,n}^{m_{j}}\right)l^{-(2n+1)s-2\sum_{j}(n-j)m_{j}},

where we used
a_{n,n}=1
and the generalized multinomial theorem, with multinomial coefficients defined as:

{M\choose m_{0},\dots,m_{n-1}}\equiv\frac{M!}{m_{0}!m_{1}!\cdots m_{n-1}!}.

(75)

Inserting this into the free energy, and denoting
m\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)m_{j}
, we get:

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2n)!}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\sum_{l=0,l\neq n}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}l^{2(i+1)}\Lambda_{l}^{-s}

(76)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2n)!}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{M\geq 0,\sum_{j}m_{j}=M}{-s\choose M}{M\choose\{m_{j}\}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j,n}^{m_{j}}\right)a_{i,n-1}\sum_{l=0,l\neq n}^{\infty}l^{-(2n+1)s-2m+2(i+1)}.

Using the identity
{-s\choose M}=\delta_{M,0}+(-1)^{M}\frac{1}{M}s+O(s^{2})
, we can compute the derivative at
s=0
,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n+1}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}\left(\zeta^{\prime}(-2(i+1))+n^{2(i+1)}\log n\right)+

(77)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{M\geq 1,\sum_{j}m_{j}=M}\frac{(-1)^{M}}{M}{M\choose\{m_{j}\}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j,n}^{m_{j}}\right)a_{i,n-1}\left(\zeta\left(2(m-i-1)\right)-n^{-2(m-i-1)}\right).

While the second line seems complicated, it can be made significantly simpler by noting some basic identities. Looking at equation (
74
), one can write,

-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\Lambda_{l}^{-s}=(2n+1)\log l-\sum_{M=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\sum_{j}m_{j}=M}\frac{(-1)^{M}}{M}{M\choose\{m_{j}\}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j,n}^{m_{j}}\right)l^{-2m}.

(78)

On the other hand, using
\log\Lambda_{l}=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\Lambda_{l}^{-s}
, we have

\displaystyle\log\Lambda_{l}

\displaystyle=\log\left(l\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\right)

(79)

\displaystyle=(2n+1)\log l-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\left(1-k^{2}l^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=(2n+1)\log l-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2m}}{m}l^{-2m}.

Expressions (
78
) and (
79
) are both analytic functions in
l^{-1}
(discarding the identical log term) since in these calculations we kept
l
arbitrary. Thus, the coefficients of the two power series must match, and we get

\sum_{M=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\sum_{j}m_{j}=M}\frac{(-1)^{M}}{M}{M\choose\{m_{j}\}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j,n}^{m_{j}}\right)=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{2m}}{m}.

(80)

Applying this identity to (
77
), we get for the finite part:

\displaystyle F_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n+1}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}\zeta^{\prime}(-2(i+1))-\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\log(n)

(81)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2m}}{m}\left(\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}\zeta\left(2(m-i-1)\right)-\frac{1}{2}n^{-2m}\right),

where we used
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}n^{2(i+1)}=n^{2}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(n^{2}-k^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(2n)!
.

Equation (
81
) demonstrates that even when using zeta regularization the order of summation can be rearranged such that:

\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{\infty}K_{d,l}\log\left(l\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\right)=\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{\infty}K_{d,l}\log(l)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=l_{0}}^{\infty}K_{d,l}\log\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)\ .

(82)

As explained in subsection
III.3
, this is a highly non-trivial result, which shows the multiplicative anomaly vanishes for this decomposition of the eigenvalues into simpler products. In appendix
III.3
we prove that we can further decompose into liner terms with no multiplicative anomaly.

To simplify (
81
), we will use the following values of the derivative of the zeta function,

\zeta^{\prime}(-2j)=(-1)^{j}\frac{(2j)!}{2(2\pi)^{2j}}\zeta(2j+1),\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\ .

(83)

Note, that we have the following generating function for the positive even integer values of the zeta function,

-\frac{\pi}{2}t\cot(\pi t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\zeta(2j)t^{2j},

(84)

while for negative even integer we have
\zeta(-2j)=0,\;j\in\mathbb{N}
. We can now write an integral expression for the infinite sum remaining in
F_{2n+1}
for each
k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2m}}{m}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}n^{-2m}-\frac{1}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}\zeta\left(2(m-i-1)\right)\right)

(85)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}t^{2m-1}n^{-2m}-\frac{2}{2n!}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}t^{2m-1}\zeta\left(2(m-i-1)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\frac{t}{n^{2}-t^{2}}-\frac{2}{(2n)!}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i,n-1}t^{2i+1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\zeta(2(m-i))t^{2(m-i)}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\frac{t}{n^{2}-t^{2}}+\frac{\pi}{(2n)!}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(t^{2}-j^{2}\right)\cot(\pi t)\right)\ .

Combing this result with equation (
81
) gives us our final result (
46
).

We note that the zeta regularization technique employed here agrees with previous results when applied to simpler operators. Specifically, we have checked that when applying this exact method to the conformal Laplacian, we get the same results for the finite part of the free energy as in
Klebanov:2011gs
.

Appendix B
No Multiplicative Anomaly for the GJMS Operator

In this appendix we show that the multiplicative anomaly vanishes when expanding the logarithm of the eigenvalues in the infinite sum (
42
) into logarithms of linear terms. During our derivation in appendix
A
, we have shown that multiplicative anomaly vanishes for the partial decomposition (
82
). What remains to be shown is that this infinite sum can be further decomposed by writing the terms inside the logarithm as
l^{2}-k^{2}=(l+k)(l-k)
, with no multiplicative anomaly.

The degeneracy
K_{d,l}
is an even polynomial in
l
, so in fact we can prove a stronger statement by replacing it with a general even power of
l
, i.e.
l^{2N}
for a non-negative integer
N
. We define the quantities:

F_{\pm}=\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}{l^{2N}\log{\left(l\pm k\right)}},\quad F_{0}=\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}{l^{2N}\log{\left(l^{2}-k^{2}\right)}},\quad\Delta_{k}=F_{0}-F_{+}-F_{-}\ .

(86)

To show that the multiplicative anomaly vanishes we need to prove
\Delta_{k}=0
and we shall do that by induction on
k
. Trivially the base case holds,
\Delta_{0}=0
. Shifting the summation index in
F_{\pm}
we get:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{+}+F_{-}&=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(l-k)^{2N}\log(l)+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(l+k)^{2N}\log(l)-\sum_{l=-k+1}^{k}l^{2N}\log(l+k)=\\
&=-2\sum_{j=0}^{N}{2N\choose 2j}k^{2(N-j)}\zeta^{\prime}(-2j)-\sum_{l=1}^{k}l^{2N}\log(l+k)-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}l^{2N}\log(k-l)\ ,\end{aligned}

(87)

where we used
\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{\alpha}\log(l)=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{\alpha-s}=-\zeta^{\prime}(-\alpha)
. Similarly, we can express
F_{0}
as follows,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} F_{0}&=\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}l^{2N}\log\left(l^{2}\right)+\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}{l^{2N}\log{\left(1-k^{2}l^{-2}\right)}}\\
&=-2\zeta^{\prime}(-2N)-2\sum_{l=1}^{k}l^{2N}\log(l)+\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}{l^{2N}\log{\left(1-k^{2}l^{-2}\right)}}\ .\end{aligned}

(88)

Using a very similar derivation as in (
85
), we can compute the remaining infinite sum,

\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}{l^{2N}\log{\left(1-k^{2}l^{-2}\right)}}=\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\pi t^{2N}\cot{\left(\pi t\right)}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}\frac{l^{2N}}{l-t}\right)-\sum_{l=1}^{k}l^{2N}\log\left(\frac{l+k}{l}\right)\ .

(89)

Note, that the integrand is finite in the integration interval, as the poles of the two terms cancel each other. We can simplify the expression by explicitly integrating the second term and obtain for
F_{0}
:

\displaystyle F_{0}=-

\displaystyle 2\zeta^{\prime}\left(-2N\right)+\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\pi t^{2N}\cot{\left(\pi t\right)}+\frac{k^{2N}}{k-t}\right)-k^{2N}\log 2-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}l^{2N}\log\left(k^{2}-l^{2}\right)\ .

(90)

This integration step introduces poles into the integration interval, so when applicable the integral should be understood as the Cauchy principal value.

The multiplicative anomaly,
\Delta_{k}=F_{0}-F_{+}-F_{-}
, reads

\Delta_{k}=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{2N\choose 2j}k^{2(N-j)}\zeta^{\prime}(-2j)+\int_{0}^{k}dt\left(\pi t^{2N}\cot{\left(\pi t\right)}+\frac{k^{2N}}{k-t}\right)+k^{2N}\log(k)\ .

(91)

In order to prove that
\Delta_{k}=0
for any
k
we assume that it vanishes for a particular value of
k
and consider the difference

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_{k}=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{p=2j+1}^{2N}{k^{2N-p}\frac{(2N)!}{(2j)!(p-2j)!(2N-p)!}}\zeta^{\prime}\left(-2j\right)

(92)

\displaystyle+\int_{k}^{k+1}dt\left(\pi t^{2N}\cot{\left(\pi t\right)}+\frac{\left(k+1\right)^{2N}-k^{2N}}{k+1-t}\right)\ .

Note that since
\Delta_{k}=0
, by our assumption, we have
\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{k+1}
. The first term can be recast, using the re-summation
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{p=2j+1}^{2N}...=\sum_{p=1}^{2N}\sum_{j=0}^{{\left\lfloor(p-1)/2\right\rfloor}}...
.

Furthermore, the integral can be simplified by the integration parameter change
u=t-k
, which finally gives the result,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}=\sum_{p=1}^{2N}{{2N\choose p}}k^{2N-p}\left(2\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{p-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{{p\choose 2j}}\zeta^{\prime}\left(-2j\right)+\int_{0}^{1}du\left(\pi u^{p}\cot{\left(\pi u\right)}+\frac{1}{1-u}\right)\right)\ .

(93)

The sum of two terms in the parentheses vanishes for each value of
p
by an integral identity, leaving us with
\Delta_{k}=0
for all non-negative integer
k
by induction. This completes the proof that the multiplicative anomaly vanishes for this decomposition.

Appendix C
Exact Correlation Functions by Relation to a Free Field

In order to calculate the exact correlation function we first need to calculate it’s residues using the relation to a free field (
60
). To solve the relevant conformal integrals, we can use a mathematical integral identity of Liouville CFT. This section is a review of the work done in
Furlan:2018jlv
.

The starting point for solving these integrals is utilising the reflection identity:

V_{Q-\alpha}=R(\alpha)V_{\alpha},

(94)

where the function
R(\alpha)
is called the reflection coefficient. We can then look at the possible residues of the correlation function (
60
) and the same correlation function with all operators
V_{\alpha}
replaced by their reflected operators
V_{Q-\alpha}
. If a pole exists in both versions then we can require:

\underset{Q-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}=nb}{\mathrm{Res}}\left\langle V_{\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}R(\alpha_{i})^{-1}\underset{Q-\sum_{i}(Q-\alpha_{i})=mb}{\mathrm{Res}}\left\langle V_{Q-\alpha_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)\cdots V_{Q-\alpha_{N}}\left(x_{N}\right)\right\rangle,

(95)

for some non-negative integers
n,m
. This in turn implies the conformal integral identity:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{(-\mu)^{n}}{n!}\prod_{k>l=1}^{N}\left|x_{k}-x_{l}\right|^{-2d\alpha_{k}\alpha_{l}}\int d^{nd}y\prod_{i>j=1}^{n}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{-2db^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2db\alpha_{k}}=\\
&\frac{(-\mu)^{m}}{m!}\prod_{i=1}^{N}R(\alpha_{i})^{-1}\prod_{k>l=1}^{N}\left|x_{k}-x_{l}\right|^{-2d(Q-\alpha_{k})(Q-\alpha_{l})}\int d^{md}y\prod_{i>j=1}^{m}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{-2db^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2db(Q-\alpha_{k})}.\end{aligned}

(96)

For the two residues to exist, we have the background charge conservation requirements:

Q-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=nb,\quad Q-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Q-\alpha_{i})=mb\ .

(97)

Summing these two equations we get a relation which determines the number of external points
N
in the correlation function:

N=-\frac{b}{Q}(n+m)+2.

(98)

Since
n,m
can be any non-negative integers, then for
N
to be a positive integer
(-Q/b)
must be a non-negative integer. To fulfil this requirement we are going to analytically continue the Liouville CFT to a specific imaginary value of
b
. This procedure will in turn provide us with a conformal integral identity which can be used for the general
b
case.

To get an identity for all possible
N
-point function, i.e. all integer
N\leq 2
, we must choose
(-Q/b)=1
, which using
Q=1/b+b
implies:

Q=-b,\quad b^{2}=-\frac{1}{2},\quad N=n+m+2.

(99)

Inserting these specific values into the analtically continued equation (
96
) and taking the limit
x_{N}\to\infty

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{(-\mu)^{n}}{n!}\int d^{nd}y\prod_{i>j=1}^{n}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2db\alpha_{k}}=\frac{(-\mu)^{m}}{m!}\prod_{i=1}^{n+m+1}R(\alpha_{i})^{-1}R(\alpha_{N})^{-1}\times\\
&\prod_{k>l=1}^{n+m+1}\left|x_{k}-x_{l}\right|^{-2d(Q^{2}-2Q(\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{l}))}\int d^{md}y\prod_{i>j=1}^{m}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2db(Q-\alpha_{k})}.\end{aligned}

(100)

Finally, lets define
D_{s}(y)\equiv\prod_{i>j=1}^{s}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|
,
a_{i}\equiv db\alpha_{i}
and
r(a_{i})=\left.R(\alpha_{i})^{-1}\right|_{b^{2}=-1/2}
. Using charge conservation we have
\alpha_{N}=Q-nb-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\alpha_{i}\implies a_{N}=d(n+1)/2-\sum_{i=1}^{n+m+1}a_{i}
, and we can rewrite the integral identity as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{(-\mu)^{n}}{n!}\int d^{nd}yD_{n}^{d}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2a_{k}}=\frac{(-\mu)^{m}}{m!}\prod_{i=1}^{n+m+1}r(a_{i})r\left(d(n+1)/2-\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+m+1}a_{i}\right)\times\\
&\prod_{k<l}^{n+m+1}|x_{k}-x_{l}|^{d-2a_{k}-2a_{l}}\int d^{md}yD_{m}^{d}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-d+2a_{k}}.\end{aligned}

(101)

The only thing left to obtain the full identity is to find the function
r(a_{i})
, which can be found by looking at the 3-point function
n=1,m=0,N=3
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int d^{d}y\left|y-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}}\left|y-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{2}}&=(-\mu)^{-1}r(a_{1})r(a_{2})r\left(d-a_{1}-a_{2}\right)\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{d-2a_{1}-2a_{2}}\\
&=\pi^{d/2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-a_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-a_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(a_{1}+a_{2}-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(a_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(a_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(d-a_{1}-a_{2}\right)}\ .\end{aligned}

(102)

Choosing the value
\mu=-\pi^{-d/2}
we get the reflection coefficient:

r(a_{i})=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-a_{i}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{i})}\equiv\frac{1}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{i})}=\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(d/2-a_{i}).

(103)

Finally, the full conformal integral identity is:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int dy_{n}D_{n}^{d}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-2a_{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n+m+1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{i})}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(d(n+1)/2-\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+m+1}a_{i}\right)}\times\\
&\prod_{k<l}^{n+m+1}|x_{k}-x_{l}|^{d-2a_{k}-2a_{l}}\int dy_{m}D_{m}^{d}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{k=1}^{n+m+1}\left|y_{i}-x_{k}\right|^{-d+2a_{k}},\end{aligned}

(104)

where for simplicity we’ve defined the measure
dx_{n}\equiv d^{nd}x/\left(\pi^{nd/2}n!\right)
.
To return to the general residue integral (
60
), which includes the factor
D_{n}^{-2db^{2}}(y)
, we will look at the identity (
104
) with
n\to n-1,m=0
and
a_{i}=d(1+b^{2})/2
. We get:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{n}^{-2db^{2}}(y)&=D_{n}^{d}(y)D_{n}^{-d-2db^{2}}(y)\\
&=D_{n}^{d}(y)\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-n\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)^{n}}\int du_{n-1}D_{n-1}^{d}(u)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left|y_{i}-u_{k}\right|^{-d(1+b^{2})}.\end{aligned}

(105)

The last identity we need is the result of (
104
) for
m=0
,
a_{i}=d(1+b^{2})/2
for
i=3,\dots,n-1
, which we rewrite as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int dy_{n}D_{n}^{d}(y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|y_{i}-u_{k}\right|^{-d(1+b^{2})}\left|y_{i}-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}}\left|y_{i}-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{2}}\\
&=\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)^{n-1}D_{n-1}^{-d-2db^{2}}(u)\left|x_{12}\right|^{d-2a_{1}-2a_{2}}}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{1})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{2})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(d-a_{1}-a_{2}-(n-1)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|u_{i}-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}-db^{2}}\left|u_{i}-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{1}-db^{2}}.\end{aligned}

(106)

Going back to the original integral for the 3-point function (
60
), with
x_{3}\to\infty
, we have:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{n}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\equiv&\int dy_{n}D_{n}^{-2db^{2}}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left|y_{i}-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}}\left|y_{i}-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{2}}\\
=&\,\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-n\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)^{n}}\int du_{n-1}D_{n-1}^{d}(u)dy_{n}D_{n}^{d}(y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|y_{i}-u_{k}\right|^{-d(1+b^{2})}\left|y_{i}-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}}\left|y_{i}-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{2}}\\
=&\,\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-n\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\left|x_{12}\right|^{d-2a_{1}-2a_{2}}}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{1})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{2})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(a_{3}+(n-1)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}\\
&\times\int du_{n-1}D_{n-1}^{-2db^{2}}(u)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|u_{i}-x_{1}\right|^{-2a_{1}-db^{2}}\left|u_{i}-x_{2}\right|^{-2a_{1}-db^{2}}\\
=&\,\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-n\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\left|x_{12}\right|^{d-2a_{1}-2a_{2}}}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{1})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(a_{2})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(a_{3}+(n-1)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}I_{n}\left(a_{1}+\frac{d}{2}b^{2},a_{2}+\frac{d}{2}b^{2},a_{3}\right),\end{aligned}

(107)

where we’ve applied (
105
) to get from the first equality to the second, and equation (
106
) to get from the second to the third. We’ve also used the condition
a_{3}=d-(n-1)db^{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}
. This equation translates to a recursion relation on the residues of the structure constants:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C_{n}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})=&\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-n\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(db\alpha_{1})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}(db\alpha_{2})\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(db\alpha_{3}+(n-1)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}C_{n-1}\left(\alpha_{1}+\frac{b}{2},\alpha_{2}+\frac{b}{2},\alpha_{3}\right)\\
=&\left(\frac{-\pi^{d/2}\mu}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(-\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}\right)^{n}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left((k-n)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(db\alpha_{1}+k\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(db\alpha_{2}+k\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(db\alpha_{3}+k\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}.\end{aligned}

(108)

Given the residues, we can now preform analytic continuation to find the general expression for the structure constants. To do so, we use the special function
\Gamma_{w}(x)\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{2}(x|w,w^{-1})}{\Gamma_{2}(w/2+w^{-1}/2|w,w^{-1})}
, where
\Gamma_{2}(x|w_{1},w_{2})
is the Double Gamma function. The function
\Gamma_{w}(x)=\Gamma_{w^{-1}}(x)
then satisfies

\frac{\Gamma_{w}(x+w)}{\Gamma_{w}(x)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{w^{wx-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(wx)}.

(109)

We can now define the
d
-dimensional generalization of the Upsilon function
\Upsilon_{b}
, namely

\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{\Gamma_{\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}x\right)\Gamma_{\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}(Q-x)\right)}\ .

(110)

Following (
109
), this function then satisfies the relation
\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)=\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(Q-x)
and the functional relation

\frac{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x+b)}{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b\right)^{d/2-dbx}\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(\frac{d}{2}bx\right).

(111)

We can then write the following products,

\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(db\alpha+k\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b\right)^{\frac{d}{2}nb\left(Q-4\alpha-nb\right)}\frac{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(2\alpha)}{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(2\alpha+nb)}\ .

(112)

Using the recursion relation, we can also compute the residues of
\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)

\underset{x=-nb}{\mathrm{Res}}\frac{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(b)}{\Upsilon_{b}^{(d)}(x)}=\frac{\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}b}\right)^{d/2-1}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\left(\sqrt{\frac{d}{2}}b\right)^{\frac{d}{2}nb(Q+nb)}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma_{\frac{d}{2}}\left((k-n)\frac{d}{2}b^{2}\right)\ .

(113)

Finally, preforming the analytic continuation by replacing
n
with the continuous variable
s=\frac{Q-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}}{b}
we get the structure constants (
62
).

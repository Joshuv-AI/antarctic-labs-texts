Abstract
. We address partition regularity problems for homogeneous quadratic equations. A consequence of our main results is that, under natural conditions on the coefficients a , b , c a,b,c , for any finite coloring of the positive integers, there exists a solution to a ​ x 2 + b ​ y 2 = c ​ z 2 ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2} where x x and y y have the same color (and similar results for x , z x,z and y , z y,z ). For certain choices of ( a , b , c ) (a,b,c) , our result is conditional on an Elliott-type conjecture. Our proofs build on and extend previous arguments of the authors dealing with the Pythagorean equation. We make use of new uniformity properties of aperiodic multiplicative functions and concentration estimates for multiplicative functions along arbitrary binary quadratic forms.

1.1.
Partition regularity results

A central problem in arithmetic Ramsey theory is to determine when a polynomial equation is
partition regular
– this means that in every partition of
{\mathbb{N}}
into finitely many cells there is a solution to the equation with all variables in the same cell.
A still unsolved special case of this problem is to decide whether the Pythagorean equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
is partition regular.
In the previous paper
[
11
]
we studied this and more general quadratic equations of the form

(1.1)

where
a,b,c
are arbitrary integers. Additional results about partition regularity of quadratic equations can be found in
[

While our methods, in the current form, do not allow us to establish full partition regularity for equations of the form (

Given nonzero

1.2.
Density regularity results

To prove the partition regularity results formulated above, we establish stronger density versions.
To this end, we recall some standard notions.
A
multiplicative Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence
\Phi=(\Phi_{K})_{K=1}^{\infty}
of finite subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
that is asymptotically invariant under dilation, in the sense that

\forall x\in{\mathbb{N}},\qquad\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\big|\Phi_{K}\cap(x\cdot\Phi_{K})\big|}{|\Phi_{K}|}=1.

An example of a multiplicative Følner sequence is given by

\Phi_{K}:=\Big\{\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}\colon 0\leq a_{p}\leq K\Big\},\quad K\in{\mathbb{N}}.

The
upper multiplicative density
of a set
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{N}}
with respect to a multiplicative Følner sequence
\Phi=(\Phi_{K})_{K=1}^{\infty}
is the quantity

\bar{d}_{\Phi}(\Lambda):=\limsup_{K\to\infty}\frac{\big|\Phi_{K}\cap\Lambda\big|}{|\Phi_{K}|}.

Definition 1.8
.

Given
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we say that the equation (
1.1
) is
density regular with respect to
x,y
if for every multiplicative Følner sequence
\Phi
and every set
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{N}}
with
\bar{d}_{\Phi}(\Lambda)>0
, there exist
distinct
x,y\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with the same color, and
z\in{\mathbb{N}}
that satisfy (
1.1
). Similarly we define the density regularity of (
1.1
) with respect to the variables
x,z
and
y,z
.

A finite coloring of
{\mathbb{N}}
always contains a monochromatic cell with positive multiplicative density, so density regularity implies partition regularity.
Theorem
1.2
thus follows from the following stronger density statement.

Theorem 1.9
.

If
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are nonzero and
ac
or
bc
is a square, then (
1.1
) is density regular with respect to
x,y
.

Likewise, Theorem
1.3
follows from the following stronger density statement.

Theorem 1.10
.

Suppose that
2
holds. If
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are nonzero and
(a+b)c
is a square (perhaps zero), then (
1.1
) is density regular with respect to
x,y
.

1.3.
Parametric form

It will be convenient to use the parametric form of the solution set of the given equations and to work with the respective notion of density regularity. Two cases to keep in mind are our working examples, the equation

x=k\,c(am^{2}-bn^{2}),\quad y=k\,2acmn,\quad z=k\,d(am^{2}+bn^{2}),

2.
Roadmap to the proofs

We shall follow the general strategy developed in our previous work

A function

f(mn)=f(m)\cdot f(n)\quad\text{ whenever }(m,n)=1.

Whenever necessary, we extend

Statement of main result

Our goal is to prove

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
that the multiplicative functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s},g\in{\mathcal{M}}
are extended to even sequences in
{\mathbb{Z}}
and that

is aperiodic. Let

be linear forms in two variables with integer coefficients and suppose that either

.
Finally, let

that is homogeneous, i.e.
(x,y)\in K

(kx,ky)\in K

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,{\bf 1}_{K}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}(L_{j}(Qm+a,Qn+b))\cdot g(P(Qm+a,Qn+b))=0.

Remark
.

If

are as in case (

) of

, then

is an irreducible binary quadratic form and

where

are pairwise independent linear forms. Applying

3.1
for

,

,

, and

, we get the conclusion of

2.5
.

In the case of completely multiplicative functions,

Theorem

3.1
generalizes
[
10
, Theorem 9.7]
that covered the case where

for

and

for

with

see also

for a quantitative aspect of this result for linear forms.
The case of binary quadratic forms with negative discriminant can be reduced to the previous cases by a simple trick explained in Section

. On the other hand, when the binary quadratic form

3.3.
Quadratic number fields - Notation

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be a square-free integer. If
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is
the ring of integers (i.e. solutions of monic polynomials with coefficients in
K
) of a quadratic number field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
, then there exists
\tau_{d}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that every element
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
can be uniquely expressed in the form
m+n\tau_{d}
for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. In fact, we can take

(3.1)

\tau_{d}:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{-d},\quad&\,\text{ if }d\equiv 1,2\!\pmod{4}\\
\frac{1+\sqrt{-d}}{2},&\,\text{ if }d\equiv 3\!\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

We let

(3.2)

R_{N}:=\{z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\colon z=m+n\tau_{d},m,n\in[N]\}.

If
z=m+n\sqrt{-d}
we let
\overline{z}:=m-n\sqrt{-d}
and
\mathcal{N}(z):=z\cdot\overline{z}=m^{2}+dn^{2}
. Note that if
z=m+n\tau_{d}
, then

\mathcal{N}(z)=P_{d}(m,n)
where

(3.3)

P_{d}(m,n):=\begin{cases}m^{2}+dn^{2},\quad&\,\text{ if }d\equiv 1,2\!\pmod{4}\\
m^{2}+mn+\frac{d+1}{4}n^{2},&\,\text{ if }d\equiv 3\!\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

3.4.
Variant of the Daboussi-Kátai orthogonality criterion

An important fact used in the proof of the Daboussi-Kátai orthogonality criterion on
{\mathbb{Z}}

[
6
,
17
]
is that for
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
the cardinality of the
set
r^{-1}[N]:=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon rn\in[N]\}
is approximately
N/r
.
A similar statement holds for Gaussian integers, namely, if
R_{N}=\{m+in\colon m,n\in[N]\}
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}[i]
is nonzero, then

z^{-1}R_{N}=\{w\in{\mathbb{Z}}[i]\colon zw\in R_{N}\}\subset R_{C\cdot\mathcal{N}(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}N}

for some
C>0
.
Similar inclusions also hold for all other quadratic fields with negative discriminant,
but this is not the case for quadratic number fields with positive discriminant, which have infinitely many units (elements
\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\mathcal{N}(\epsilon)=1
).
In this case, every element
z\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
has infinitely many associates
z\epsilon
, all of which have the same norm.
While not all of them satisfy the required property, Sun proved in
[
25
]
that at least one of the associates of any given element does.
We state here the result for quadratic number fields, since that is all we need.

Definition 3.3

(Definition 2.12 in
[
25
]
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
be the ring of integers of a quadratic number field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})
and
C>0
. We say that
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
,
z\neq 0
, is
C
-regular
if for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

z^{-1}R_{N}\subset R_{C|\mathcal{N}(z)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}N},

where
R_{N}
is as in (
3.2
).

We will use the following special case of a result from
[
25
]
.

Lemma 3.4

(Theorem 2.13 in
[
25
]
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
be the ring of integers of a quadratic number field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})
. Then there exists
C>0
, depending on
d
only, such that for every
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
there exists a unit
\epsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
\epsilon z
is
C
-regular.

Using this, the following extension of the Daboussi-Kátai orthogonality criterion was established in
[
25
]
(in the setting of general number fields, but we only need it for quadratic number fields).

Lemma 3.5

(Lemma 2.20 in
[
25
]
)
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be square-free,
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
be the ring of integers of the quadratic number field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})
, and
a\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a bounded sequence. Let also
C>0
and
{\mathcal{P}}
be a collection of
C
-regular prime elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
such that

\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{N}}(p)|}=+\infty.

Suppose that for all
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}
with coprime norms we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{z\in R_{N}}\,a(pz)\,\overline{a(qz)}=0.

Then for every completely multiplicative function
f\in\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]\to{\mathbb{U}}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{z\in R_{N}}\,a(z)\,f(z)=0.

Remark
.

The case
d>0
was previously covered by
[
10
, Proposition 9.5]
.

We will use this lemma in the form of the following corollary that is better suited for our purposes.

Corollary 3.6

(Daboussi-Kátai orthogonality criterion for quadratic number fields)
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be square-free,
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
be the ring of integers of the quadratic number field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})
,
a\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a bounded sequence, and
P_{0}>0
.
Suppose that for all
\alpha,\beta
that are prime elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
with norm greater than
P_{0}
and such that all elements
\alpha,\overline{\alpha},\beta,\overline{\beta}
are non-associates,
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{z\in R_{N}}\,a(\alpha z)\,\overline{a(\beta z)}=0.

Then for every completely multiplicative function
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]\to{\mathbb{U}}
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{z\in R_{N}}\,a(z)\,f(z)=0.

Proof.

For each integer prime
p
let
\tilde{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
be a prime that divides
p
.
Using
Lemma

3.4
we can assume that each
\tilde{p}
is
C
-regular, for the constant
C
given in
Lemma

3.4
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}
denote the set of all
\tilde{p}
for
p>P_{0}
such that
\tilde{p}
is not associated to its conjugate or to an integer.

Since
{\mathcal{N}}(\tilde{p})=p
the primes
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{q}
are not associate whenever
p\neq q
.
If
\tilde{p}
and its conjugate are associate, then
p
is divisible by
\tilde{p}^{2}
, and hence
\tilde{p}
is a ramified prime, of which there are only finitely many.
If
\tilde{p}
is associated to an integer, then
p
itself is a prime in
{\mathbb{Z}}[\tau_{d}]
and hence it is an inert prime.
By
[
23
, Theorem 7.4.2]
,
\tilde{p}\in{\mathcal{P}}
if and only if
p>P_{0}
and
-d
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
.
By the quadratic reciprocity and Dirichlet’s theorem, it follows that
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{P}}}\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha)|}=\infty
.

We have shown that
{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies the conditions of
Lemma

3.5
, and hence the conclusion follows by applying that lemma.
∎

.
Hence, it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[CN]}\,{\bf 1}_{A_{d,N}}(m,n)\cdot{\bf 1}_{K}(\tilde{L}_{d}(m,n))\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{s}f_{j}(L_{j,d}^{\prime}(m,n))\cdot g(P_{d}(m,n))=0,

where
C:=2|\alpha|+|\beta|+2r
,
A_{d,N}:=A_{d}([N]\times[N])
, and we used that
A_{d,N}
is a subset of
[CN]\times[CN]
and

A_{d}
is an injective map.
Note also that
{\bf 1}_{K}(\tilde{L}_{d}(m,n))={\bf 1}_{K_{d}}(m,n)
where

K_{d}:=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}\colon\tilde{L}_{d}(x,y)\in K\}
is a convex subset of
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
.
Since our linear independence assumption for the linear forms
L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}
passes to the linear forms
L_{1,d}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{s,d}^{\prime}
, the last vanishing property follows from (
3.4
).

4.
Concentration estimates - Statements and preliminary results

The main goal of this section and the next one is to prove the concentration estimates for irreducible binary quadratic forms as stated in Propositions
4.8
and
4.9
below. Some of our intermediate results apply without additional difficulty to arbitrary homogeneous polynomials, and we present them in this broader context.

4.1.
Preliminaries and notation

We will need some notation in order to state the main concentration estimates.

Definition 4.1
.

If
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
is a homogeneous polynomial we let

(4.1)

{\mathcal{P}}_{P}:=\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}\colon P(m,n)\equiv 0\!\!\pmod{p}\text{ has a solution with }n\not\equiv 0\!\!\pmod{p}\}

and note that because the polynomial
P
is homogeneous we have

{\mathcal{P}}_{P}=\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}\colon P(m,1)\equiv 0\!\!\pmod{p}\text{ has a solution}\}.

We remark that if
P
is an irreducible binary quadratic form with discriminant
D
, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
is the set of primes such that

D
is a quadratic residue
\!\!\mod{p}
. Thus, if the square-free parts of the discriminants of two binary quadratic forms
P,Q
coincide, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}={\mathcal{P}}_{Q}
.

Definition 4.2
.

If
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
is a homogeneous polynomial and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let

(4.2)

\omega_{P}(r):=\big|\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}\colon P(n,1)\equiv 0\!\!\pmod{r}\}\big|.

(Note that
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}=\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}\colon\omega_{P}(p)>0\}
.)

Remarks
.

\bullet
We trivially have
\omega_{P}(r)\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
for every
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
we have
\omega_{P}(p)\leq\deg(P)
.
If we assume that the greatest common divisor of the coefficient of
P
is
1
, then
\omega_{P}(p)\leq\deg(P)
for all
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
. Note, however, that this is not the case for composite
r
, for instance,
if
P(m,n)=m^{2}
we have
\omega_{P}(p^{2})=p
for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
.

\bullet
If
P(m,n)=m^{2}+dn^{2}
and
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
is odd such that
p\nmid d
, then
\omega_{P}(p)=2
if
-d
is a quadratic residue
\!\!\mod{p}
and
\omega(p)=0
if
-d
is not a quadratic residue
\!\!\mod{p}
.

The following basic property is a consequence of the Chinese remainder theorem

(4.3)

\omega_{P}(rs)=\omega_{P}(r)\,\omega_{P}(s),\quad\text{whenever }(r,s)=1.

In other words
\omega_{P}
is a multiplicative function on
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
P
be an irreducible binary quadratic form with discriminant
D
and suppose
d
is the unique square-free integer satisfying
D=dr^{2}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
we have
\omega_{P}(p)=2
or
0
depending on whether
d
is a quadratic residue
\!\!\mod{p}
or not.

Proof.

Let
P(m,n)=\alpha m^{2}+\beta mn+\gamma n^{2}
for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}
not all of them
0
.
Since the form is irreducible we have
\alpha\neq 0
and
D:=\beta^{2}-4\alpha\gamma
is not a square. Note that

4\alpha P(m,1)=(2\alpha m+\beta)^{2}-dr^{2}
.
It follows that if
p\nmid 2\alpha dr
, then the number of solutions
\!\!\pmod{p}
to the congruence
P(m,1)\equiv 0\!\!\pmod{p}
is equal to the number of solutions to the congruence
m^{2}\equiv d\!\!\pmod{p}
; hence, it is
2
or
0
, depending on whether
d
is a quadratic residue
\!\!\mod{p}
or not.
∎

We also introduce some quantities that will appear in our main concentration estimates. If
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
is a homogeneous polynomial,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\chi
is a Dirichlet character, we let

(4.4)

G_{P,N}(f,K):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<p\leq N,\\
p\in{\mathbb{P}}\end{subarray}}\,\frac{\omega_{P}(p)}{p}\,(f(p)\cdot\overline{\chi(p)}\cdot n^{-it}-1);

(we suppress the dependence on
t
and
\chi
)
and

(4.5)

{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g;x,y)^{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq y,\\
p\in{\mathbb{P}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\omega_{P}(p)}{p}\,(1-\Re(f(p)\cdot\overline{g(p)}));

(4.6)

{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g):={\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g;1,+\infty).

If
P(m,n)=n
, in which case
\omega_{P}(p)=1
for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
, we let

(4.7)

{\mathbb{D}}(f,g;x,y):={\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g;x,y),\qquad{\mathbb{D}}(f,g):={\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g).

Recall that by Definition
2.2
, if

P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
is a homogeneous polynomial we say that
the multiplicative function
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
is
P
-pretentious if
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,\chi\cdot n^{it})<+\infty
for some
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and Dirichlet character
\chi
. Note that
since for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
we have
\omega_{P}(p)\leq\deg(P)
,

f
is
P
-pretentious if and only if

\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}}\frac{1}{p}\,(1-\Re(f(p)\cdot\overline{\chi(p)}\cdot p^{-it}))<+\infty,

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
is the set defined in (
4.1
).
In this case we write
f\sim_{P}\chi\cdot n^{it}
. If
P(m,n)=n
, then
\omega_{P}(p)=1
for all
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
and we recover the usual notion of pretentiousness in the sense of Granville and Soundararajan
[
14
]
. More generally we have the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

For every bounded sequence
c\colon{\mathbb{P}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
we let

{\mathbb{D}}_{c}(f,g;x,y)^{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq y,\\
p\in{\mathbb{P}}\end{subarray}}\frac{c(p)}{p}\,(1-\Re(f(p)\cdot\overline{g(p)})).

If
{\mathbb{D}}_{c}(f,g;1,+\infty)<+\infty
we write
f\sim_{c}g
.
Note that if
c_{1}(p)\leq c_{2}(p)
for all but finitely many
p
, then the following implication holds
f\sim_{c_{2}}g\Rightarrow f\sim_{c_{1}}g
.

It can be shown (as in
[
13
]
or
[
14
, Section 2.1.1]
) that
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
satisfies the triangle inequality

(4.8)

{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,g)\leq{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,h)+{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(h,g)

for all
f,g,h\colon{\mathbb{P}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
.
Also, for all
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\colon{\mathbb{P}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
, we have (proved as in

[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
)

(4.9)

{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f_{1}f_{2},g_{1}g_{2})\leq{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f_{1},g_{1})+{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f_{2},g_{2}).

It follows from (
4.9
) that if
f_{1}\sim_{P}g_{1}
and
f_{2}\sim_{P}g_{2}
, then
f_{1}f_{2}\sim_{P}g_{1}g_{2}
. Similar estimates also hold with
{\mathbb{D}}_{c}
in place of
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
where
c\colon{\mathbb{P}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is any bounded sequence.

If
f\sim\chi\cdot n^{it}
, it is known that the value of
t
is unique. A similar fact holds if
f\sim_{P}\chi\cdot n^{it}
and
P
is an irreducible binary quadratic form. We will prove a more general fact in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be two irreducible binary quadratic forms, let
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
be a multiplicative function,
and suppose that for
j=1,2
we have
f\sim_{P_{j}}\chi_{j}\cdot n^{it_{j}}
for some
t_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and Dirichlet characters
\chi_{j}
. Then

t_{1}=t_{2}
and
\chi_{1}(p)=\chi_{2}(p)
for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}
.

Proof.

First, we show that
t_{1}=t_{2}
. Using
Lemma

4.3
and quadratic reciprocity, we can find
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\omega_{P_{1}}(p)=\omega_{P_{2}}(p)=2
for all but finitely many primes
p\in\mathcal{G}:=1+a{\mathbb{N}}
.
Indeed, if
d_{1},d_{2}
are the square-free parts of the discriminants of
P_{1},P_{2}
, and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}
are the prime factors of
d_{1},d_{2}
, for any prime
p
such that
q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}
are all quadratic residues
\pmod{p}
, both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are quadratic residues
\pmod{p}
and hence by
Lemma

4.3

\omega_{P_{1}}(p)=\omega_{P_{2}}(p)=2
.
Using quadratic reciprocity we get that any prime
p
satisfying
p\equiv 1\pmod{8\,q_{1}\cdots q_{k}}
works, so it suffices to take
a:=8\,q_{1}\cdots q_{k}
.

So let
c(p):=2
if
p\in\mathcal{G}
and
c(p):=0
otherwise, so that
c(p)\leq\omega_{P_{j}}(p)
for
j=1,2
and all but finitely many
p
.
Therefore
f\sim_{c}\chi_{1}\cdot n^{it_{1}}
and
f\sim_{c}\chi_{2}\cdot n^{it_{2}}
.
It follows
from (
4.9
), with
{\mathbb{D}}_{c}
in place of
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
, that
\chi_{1}\cdot n^{it_{1}}\sim_{c}\chi_{2}\cdot n^{it_{2}}
.
Note also that
there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\chi_{j}^{k}(p)=1
,
j=1,2
, for all but finitely many primes
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
. Using the previous facts and (
4.9
) again, we deduce that
n^{ikt_{1}}\sim_{c}n^{ikt_{2}}
, hence

n^{ik(t_{1}-t_{2})}\sim_{c}1
.
Whenever
\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1-\Re(p^{it})}{p}<\infty
, the prime number theorem on arithmetic progressions implies
t=0.
Consequently, we have
t_{1}=t_{2}
.

Next note that if
P
is an irreducible quadratic form with discriminant
D
, then

{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
consists of those primes
p
that satisfy
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=1
.
Using quadratic reciprocity we deduce that there exists
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a non-empty finite set
Q_{0}
, consisting of positive integers relatively prime to
Q
, such that

(4.10)

{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}=\{p\equiv j\!\!\pmod{Q}\colon j\in Q_{0}\}.

By enlarging
Q
if necessary, we can assume that both
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
have period
Q
.
Let
\omega(p)=2
for
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}
and
0
otherwise.
Since
\omega_{P_{1}}(p)=\omega_{P_{2}}(p)=2
for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}
, we have
f\sim_{\omega}\chi_{j}\cdot n^{it}
, for
j=1,2
, and we deduce using (
4.8
), with
{\mathbb{D}}_{c}
in place of
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
, that

(4.11)

\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}}\,\frac{1}{p}\,(1-\Re(\chi_{1}(p)\cdot\overline{\chi_{2}(p)}))<\infty.

Since
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
have period
Q
and take finitely many values on primes, if

\chi_{1}(p_{0})\neq\chi_{2}(p_{0})
for some
p_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{1}}\cap{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{2}}
which does not divide
Q
, we deduce from (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) that

\sum_{p\equiv p_{0}\!\!\!\pmod{Q}}\,\frac{1}{p}<\infty.

This contradicts Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions and completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
The case
P(m,n)=m^{2}+dn^{2}

For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
square-free we let

P_{d}(m,n)=m^{2}+dn^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{d}:={\mathcal{P}}_{P_{d}},

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{P_{d}}
is given by (
4.1
).
Then

(4.12)

{\mathcal{P}}_{d}=\Big\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}\colon\left(\frac{-d}{p}\right)=1\Big\}.

For example,

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}:=\{p\equiv 1\!\pmod{4}\}
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{2}:=\{p\equiv 1,3\!\pmod{8}\}
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{-2}:=\{p\equiv 1,7\!\!\pmod{8}\}
.
It can be shown that for square-free
d
the set
{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
has relative density
1/2
on the primes and it always consists of the set of primes that belong to finitely many congruence classes. For example, using quadratic reciprocity, we get that if
q
is an odd prime, then

{\mathcal{P}}_{q}=\{p\equiv\pm k^{2}\!\!\pmod{4q}\colon k\in[4q]\text{ is odd}\}.

In the case where
P=P_{d}
the expressions (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) used in the statements of
Proposition

4.8
and
Proposition

4.9
take the form

(4.13)

G_{d,N}(f,K):=2\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<p\leq N,\\
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d}\end{subarray}}\,\frac{1}{p}\,(f(p)\cdot\overline{\chi(p)}\cdot n^{-it}-1)

and

(4.14)

{\mathbb{D}}_{d}(f,g;x,y)^{2}:=2\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq y,\\
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\,(1-\Re(f(p)\cdot\overline{g(p)})).

4.3.
Statements of linear and quadratic concentration estimates

We will make use of the following “linear” concentration estimate, which is an immediate consequence of
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Definition 4.6
.

Throughout, if
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
is a multiplicative function we extend it to
{\mathbb{Z}}
by letting
f(-n)=f(n)
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We do a similar thing for additive functions
h\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

For each
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
let

(4.15)

\Phi_{K}:=\Big\{\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}\colon K<a_{p}\leq 2K\Big\}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
be a multiplicative function such that
f\sim\chi\cdot n^{it}
for some
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and Dirichlet character
\chi
with period
q
. Let
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi_{K}
be the set described in (
4.15
).
Suppose that
K
is sufficiently large so that
q
divides all elements of
\Phi_{K}
.
Then

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K},a\in A_{Q}}{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N]}|f(Qn+a)-\chi(a)\cdot(Qn)^{it}\cdot\exp\big(F_{N}(f,K)\big)|\\
\ll{\mathbb{D}}(f,\chi\cdot n^{it};K,\infty)+K^{-1/2},

where for given
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
the set
A_{Q}
consists of all
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|a|\leq Q
and
(a,Q)=1
, the implicit constant is absolute,
{\mathbb{D}}
is as in (
4.7
), and

(4.16)

F_{N}(f,K):=\sum_{K<p\leq N}\frac{1}{p}\,\big(f(p)\cdot\overline{\chi(p)}\cdot p^{-it}-1\big).

(We suppress the dependence on
t
and
\chi
).

Our main goal in this section is to prove an analog of this result for irreducible binary quadratic forms. The case where
Q(m,n)=m^{2}+n^{2}
was essentially covered in
[
11
]
. We follow a similar general strategy, but the more general setting we want to cover presents some additional challenges that we need to address. In particular, unlike in
[
11
]
, we need to invoke several results from algebraic number theory and also work over real quadratic extensions.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be an irreducible binary quadratic form,

A,K,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
be an even multiplicative function,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\chi
be a Dirichlet character with period
q,

Q=\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}
for some
a_{p}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let also
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
-A\leq a,b\leq A
,
c:=(P(a,b),Q)
, and
suppose that
q
and
\prod_{p\leq K}p
divide
Q/c
. Then there exist constants
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that if
K
is sufficiently large, depending only on
P
, and
N
is sufficiently large, depending only on
A
,
P
,
Q
, we have

(4.17)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|f\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-\chi(P_{c}(a,b))\cdot(P_{c}(Qm,Qn))^{it}\cdot\exp\big(G_{P,N}(f,K)\big)\big|\\
\ll_{P}({\mathbb{D}}_{P}+{\mathbb{D}}_{P}^{2})(f,\chi\cdot n^{it};K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot\mathbb{D}_{P}(f,\chi\cdot n^{it};\sqrt{N},C_{A,P,Q}N^{2})+K^{-1/2},

where
P_{c}:=P/c
and
G_{P,N},{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
are as in (
4.4
), (
4.5
).

Remarks
.

\bullet

The uniformity in the parameters is important for our applications. In particular, it is crucial that the implicit constant depends only on
P
and not on
A,K
, and that
\exp\big(G_{P,N}(f,K)\big)
is the same for all
Q
that are divisible by
\prod_{p\leq K}p
.

\bullet
In the proof of part (
i
) of
Theorem

2.4
we apply this result and the next one for

in which case some of our assumptions hold trivially. The full strength of
Proposition

4.8
, for varying
a,b
that depend on
Q
, is needed in the proof of part (

) of
Theorem

2.4

to cover the case where the irreducible polynomials
P_{1},P_{2}
have different discriminants.

\bullet
If
P
is reducible, then
P=L_{1}\cdot L_{2}
, where
L_{1},L_{2}
are linear forms, and we get concentration estimates using
Proposition

4.7
.

\bullet
The averaging over both variables
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
is crucial for our argument and allows to overcome issues with large primes that cause concentration estimates along single-variable quadratic polynomials to fail in general.
On the other hand, in
[
26
]
Teräväinen proved a version of the concentration estimates for values of
f(P(Qn+a))
, where
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is arbitrary and the multiplicative functions
f
satisfies
f(p)=p^{it}\chi(p)
for
p>N
. In our setting, however, we cannot afford to make such assumptions on

.

For
P
-pretentious multiplicative functions we deduce the following result.

.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]

. Let also

, and so that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_{Q\in\Phi_{K},a,b\in S_{K}}{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|f\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-\\

,

,

.

It is important for our argument that

4.4.
Preliminary results I

We will need the following elementary result.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be an irreducible homogeneous polynomial
and
\omega_{P}
be as in
Definition

4.2
.
Then
\omega_{P}(p^{2})\leq\deg(P)
for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{P}(n):=P(n,1)
.
By Hensel’s lemma, if
\tilde{P}(n)\equiv 0\!\pmod{p^{2}}
has more than
\deg(\tilde{P})
solutions, then either
\tilde{P}(n)\equiv 0\!\pmod{p}
has more than
\deg(\tilde{P})
solutions (which can only happen for finitely many
p
), or the polynomials
\tilde{P}
and
\tilde{P}^{\prime}
have a common root
\!\!\!\pmod{p}
.
Since
\tilde{P}
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
, it shares no common factors with
\tilde{P}^{\prime}
and hence there are polynomials
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[n]
such that
P_{1}\tilde{P}+P_{2}\tilde{P}^{\prime}=k
for some non-zero
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Therefore, if
\tilde{P}
and

Proposition

4.9
. Although the results and proofs presented are similar to those in [5], which cover the case where
P(m,n)=m^{2}+n^{2}
and
c=1
, there are also a number of technical differences in the argument that necessitate giving it in full detail in order to facilitate proper understanding.

5.1.
Concentration estimates for additive functions

We start with a concentration estimate for additive functions, which will eventually get lifted to a concentration estimate for multiplicative functions.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
h\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is
additive
, if it satisfies

h(mn)=h(m)+h(n)
whenever
(m,n)=1
. We extend additive functions to the integers by
h(-n):=h(n)
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Turán-Kubilius inequality for quadratic forms)
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be an irreducible binary quadratic form and
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
be as in (
4.1
).
Let
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be sufficiently large (depending only on
P
) and let
N
be sufficiently large (depending only on
K
). For any
A\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
-A\leq a,b\leq A
,
Q=\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}
for some
a_{p}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
c\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
c\mid(Q,P(a,b))
, there exists
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that for any additive even function
h\colon\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that is bounded by
1
on primes and satisfies

(i)

h(p)=0
for all primes
p\leq K
and
p>N
;

(ii)

h(p)=0
for all primes
p\notin{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
;

(iii)

h(p^{k})=0
for all primes
p
and
k\geq 2
,

we have

(5.1)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-H_{N}(h,K)\big|^{2}\ll_{P}\\
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-1},

where
P_{c}:=P/c
,

(5.2)

H_{N}(h,K):=2\,\sum_{K<p\leq N}\,\frac{h(p)}{p}

and

(5.3)

{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h;K,N):=\sum_{K<p\leq N}\frac{|h(p)|^{2}}{p}.

Proof.

Henceforth, we take
K
sufficiently large, depending only on
P
, so that it exceeds all elements of the finite set

F^{\prime}
considered in
Corollary

4.12
and such that
\omega_{P}(p)=2
for every
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
with
p>K
(for this it suffices to ensure that
p
does not divide the discriminant of
P
, which is nonzero since the
P
is irreducible). Note also that since
p\mid Q
implies
p\leq K
, for
p>K
we have
p\notin F
where
F
is the finite set considered in
Corollary

4.12
.

We consider the additive functions
h_{1},h_{2}
, which are the restrictions of
h
on the primes
K<p\leq\sqrt{N}
and
\sqrt{N}<p\leq N
.
More precisely,

h_{1}(p^{k}):=\begin{cases}h(p),\quad&\text{if }\ k=1\text{ and }K<p\leq\sqrt{N}\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and

h_{2}(p^{k}):=\begin{cases}h(p),\quad&\text{if }\ k=1\text{ and }\sqrt{N}<p\leq N\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases},

and we extend
h_{1},h_{2}
to even functions in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
h=h_{1}+h_{2}
.
We also define

(5.4)

H_{N}(h_{i},K):=2\,\sum_{K<p\leq N}\,\frac{h_{i}(p)}{p},\quad i=1,2,

and the technical variant

(5.5)

H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K):=\sum_{K<p\leq N}\,w_{P,Q,N}(p)\cdot h_{1}(p),

where

(5.6)

w_{P,Q,N}(p):=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in[N],\\
p\,\mid\mid\,P(Qm+a,Qn+b)\end{subarray}}1.

(Note that
w_{P,Q,N}(p)=w_{P,Q,N}(p,p)
where
w_{P,Q,N}(p,q)
is as in (
4.18
).)
Note that as shown in (
4.21
)
of
Lemma

4.11
for
p\nmid Q
, which holds for
p>K
, we have

w_{P,Q,N}(p):=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in[N],\\
p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\end{subarray}}1.

The reason for introducing
H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)
is that it gives the mean value of
h_{1}
along
integers of the form
P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)
. Indeed,
using the properties (
i
)-(
iii
), and since
h_{1}
is additive and even, we have

(5.7)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))={\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P(Qm+a,Qn+b)}h_{1}(p)\\
=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{K<p\leq N}h_{1}(p)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in[N],\\
p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\end{subarray}}1=H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K).

Using the estimate (
4.27
) of
Corollary

4.12
(which holds since
p\notin F
for
p>K
), and the fact that
\omega_{P}(p)=2
for every
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
with
p>K
and
h_{1}(p)=0
if
p\notin{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
or
p>\sqrt{N}
and
|h_{1}(p)|\leq 1
for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
, we get

(5.8)

|H_{N}(h_{1},K)-H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)|\ll\sum_{K<p\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\leq\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}.

Hence, in order to prove (
5.1
),
it suffices to estimate

(5.9)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\big|h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))-H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)\big|^{2}

and

(5.10)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\big|h_{2}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big|^{2}+|H_{N}(h_{2},K)|^{2}.

First, we deal with the expression (
5.9
).
Using (
5.7
) and expanding the square below we get

(5.11)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)-H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)\big|^{2}=\\
{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big|^{2}-|H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)|^{2}.

To estimate this expression, first note that since
h_{1}
is additive, even, and
h_{1}(p^{k})=0
for
k\geq 2
, we have

(5.12)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))\big|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\Big|\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)}h_{1}(p)\Big|^{2}.

Expanding the square, using the fact that
h_{1}(p)=0
unless
K<p\leq\sqrt{N}
, and the definition of
w_{Q,N}(p,q)
given in (
4.18
), we get that the right side is equal to

\sum_{K<p\leq\sqrt{N}}|h_{1}(p)|^{2}\cdot w_{P,Q,N}(p,p)+\sum_{K<p,q\leq\sqrt{N},\,p\neq q}h_{1}(p)\cdot\overline{h_{1}(q)}\cdot w_{P,Q,N}(p,q).

Using the estimate (
4.27
) of
Corollary

4.12
,
we get that the first term is
at most

\sum_{K<p\leq\sqrt{N}}\frac{|h_{1}(p)|^{2}}{p}+O_{P}(N^{-1/2}).

Using (
4.28
) of
Corollary

4.12
we get that the second term is equal to (crucially we use the bound
p,q\leq\sqrt{N}
here and the prime number theorem)

\sum_{K<p,q\leq\sqrt{N},\,p\neq q}h_{1}(p)\cdot\overline{h_{1}(q)}\cdot w_{P,Q,N}(p,p)\cdot w_{P,Q,N}(q,q)+O((\log{N})^{-2})\leq\\
(H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K))^{2}+O((\log{N})^{-2}),

where to get the last estimate we added to the sum the contribution of the diagonal terms
p=q
(which is non-negative), used (
5.5
), and the fact that
h_{1}(p)=0
for
p>\sqrt{N}
.
Combining (
5.11
) with the previous estimates, we are led to the bound

(5.13)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h_{1}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)-H^{\prime}_{N}(h_{1},Q,K)\big|^{2}\ll_{P}\\
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{1};K,\sqrt{N})+O((\log{N})^{-2}).

Next we estimate the expression (
5.10
). Since
h_{2}
is additive, even, and satisfies the properties (
i
)-(
iii
), we get by using (
5.12
) (with
h_{2}
instead of
h_{1}
) and expanding the square

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h_{2}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))\big|^{2}=\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p,q\leq N}h_{2}(p)\,\overline{h_{2}(q)}\,w_{P,Q,N}(p,q).

Since
h_{2}(p)\neq 0
only when
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
(by property (
ii
)),
using
Lemma

4.15
for
k=1,2
, we get that the right side is bounded by

\ll_{A,P,Q}\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p,q\leq N}\frac{|h_{2}(p)|\,|h_{2}(q)|}{pq}+\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{|h_{2}(p)|^{2}}{p}=\\
\Big(\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{|h_{2}(p)|}{p}\Big)^{2}+{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{2};\sqrt{N},N)\ll\\
\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{|h_{2}(p)|^{2}}{p}\cdot\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{1}{p}+{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{2};\sqrt{N},N)\ll{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{2};\sqrt{N},N),

where we used crucially the estimate

\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{1}{p}\ll 1.

Similarly we find

(H_{N}(h_{2},K))^{2}=4\,\Big(\sum_{\sqrt{N}<p\leq N}\frac{|h_{2}(p)|}{p}\Big)^{2}\ll{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{2};\sqrt{N},N).

Combining the previous estimates, we get the following bound for the expression in (
5.10
)

(5.14)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\big(h_{2}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)^{2}+(H_{N}(h_{2},K))^{2}\ll_{A,P,Q}{\mathbb{D}}^{2}(h_{2};\sqrt{N},N).

Combining the bounds (
5.8
), (
5.13
), (
5.14
), we get the asserted bound (
5.1
), which completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Concentration estimates for multiplicative functions

Next we use Lemma
5.2
to get a variant that deals with multiplicative functions.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be an irreducible binary quadratic form
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
be as in (
4.12
),
A,K,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
-A\leq a,b\leq A
,
c\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
c\mid(Q,P(a,b))
, and
f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{U}
be an even multiplicative function such that

(i)

f(p)=1
for all primes
p\leq K
and
p>N
;

(ii)

f(p)=1
for all primes
p\notin{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
;

(iii)

f(p^{k})=1
for all primes
p
and
k\geq 2
.

Let also
Q=\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}
with
a_{p}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then there exists
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that if
K
is sufficiently large, depending only on
P
,
and
N
is sufficiently large, depending only on
K
, we have

(5.15)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|f\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-\exp\big(G_{N}(f,K)\big)\big|\ll_{P}\\
({\mathbb{D}}+{\mathbb{D}}^{2})(f,1;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}(f,1;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
P_{c}:=P/c
,
{\mathbb{D}}
is as in (
5.3
), and

(5.16)

G_{N}(f,K):=2\,\sum_{K<p\leq N}\,\frac{1}{p}\,(f(p)-1).

Proof.

Henceforth, we take
K
sufficiently large, depending only on
P
, so
that it exceeds all elements of the finite set

F^{\prime}
considered in
Corollary

4.12
, and also so that
Lemma

5.2
holds and
\omega_{P}(p)\leq 2
for
p>K
.

Let
h:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be the additive function given on prime powers by

h(p^{k}):=f(p^{k})-1,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Note that due to our assumptions on
f,
the properties (
i
)-(
iii
) of Lemma
5.2
are satisfied.

Using that
z=e^{z-1}+O(|z-1|^{2})
for
|z|\leq 1
and property (
iii
), we have

\displaystyle f(P_{c}(m,n))=\prod_{p^{k}\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(m,n)}f(p^{k})=\prod_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(m,n)}\big(\exp(h(p))+O(|h(p)|^{2})\big).

Applying the estimate
|\prod_{j\leq k}z_{j}-\prod_{j\leq k}w_{j}|\leq\sum_{j\leq k}|z_{j}-w_{j}|
, which holds if
|z_{j}|\leq 1
and
|w_{j}|\leq 1
for
j=1,\ldots,k
, we deduce that for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\displaystyle f(P_{c}(m,n))=\exp(h(P_{c}(m,n)))+O\Big(\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(m,n)}|h(p)|^{2}\Big).

Using this and since
G_{N}(f,K)=H_{N}(h,K)
, where
H_{N}(h,K)
is given by (
5.2
), we get

(5.17)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|f\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-\exp\big(G_{N}(f,K)\big)\big|\ll\\
{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}|\exp\big(h(P_{c}(m,n))\big)-\exp(H_{N}(h,K))|+\\
{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(m,n)}|h(p)|^{2},

where the implicit constant is absolute.
Next, we use the inequality
|e^{z_{1}}-e^{z_{2}}|\leq|z_{1}-z_{2}|
, which holds for
\Re z_{1},\Re z_{2}\leq 0
, to bound the last expression by

(5.18)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}|h((P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))-H_{N}(h,K)|+{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)}|h(p)|^{2}.

To bound the first term in (
5.18
) we use Lemma
5.2
. It gives that there exist
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that for all sufficiently large
N
, depending only on
K
, we have

(5.19)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|h\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-H_{N}(h,K)\big|\ll_{P}\\
{\mathbb{D}}(h;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}(h;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll\\
{\mathbb{D}}(f,1;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}(f,1;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where for the last bound we used that
|h(p)|^{2}\leq 2-2\,\Re(f(p))
, which holds since
|f(p)|\leq 1
.
To bound the second term in (
5.18
), we note that
using the properties (
i
)-(
iii
) of Lemma
5.2
, we have

(5.20)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\sum_{p\,\mid\mid\,P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)}|h(p)|^{2}=\sum_{K<p\leq N}\ |h(p)|^{2}\,w_{P,Q,N}(p)\ll_{P}\\
\\
\sum_{K<p\leq N}\frac{|h(p)|^{2}}{p}+O((\log{N})^{-1})\ll_{P}\mathbb{D}^{2}(f,1;K,N)+O((\log{N})^{-1}),

where
w_{P,Q,N}(p)
is as in (
5.6
) and we used equation (
4.27
) of
Corollary

4.12
(which holds since
p>K
implies
p\nmid Q
and
p\notin F^{\prime}
), the bound
\omega_{P}(p)\leq 2
(which holds for
p>K
), and the prime number theorem to get the first bound.
Combining (
5.17
)-(
5.20
) we get the asserted bound. ∎

We use the previous result to derive the following improved version.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[m,n]
be an irreducible binary quadratic form,

{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
be as in (
4.1
),

A,K,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{U}
be an even multiplicative function such that
f(p)=1
for all primes
p>N
with
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
. Let also
Q=\prod_{p\leq K}p^{a_{p}}
with
a_{p}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exists
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that if
K
is sufficiently large, depending only on
P
,
and
N
is sufficiently large, depending only on
K
,
then for all
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
-A\leq a,b\leq A
and such that
\prod_{p\leq K}p
divides
Q/c
where
c:=(P(a,b),Q)
, we have

(5.21)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}\,\big|f\big(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\big)-\exp\big(G_{P,N}(f,K)\big)\big|\ll_{P}\\
({\mathbb{D}}_{P}+{\mathbb{D}}_{P}^{2})(f,1;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,1;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where

(5.22)

G_{P,N}(f,K):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K<p\leq N\end{subarray}}\,\frac{\omega_{P}(p)}{p}\,(f(p)-1),

{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,1;x,y)^{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\omega_{P}(p)}{p}\,(1-\Re(f(p))).

(Note that both sums are supported on the primes in
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
and
\omega_{P}(p)=2
for
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
with
p>K
as long as
K
is sufficiently large.)

Proof.

Let
P(m,n)=\alpha m^{2}+\beta mn+\gamma n^{2}
. Note that since
P
is irreducible we have
\alpha\neq 0
. We can assume that
K
is greater than
|\alpha|
and that
\omega_{P}(p)\leq 2
for all
p>K
; this holds as long as
p
does not divide the discriminant of
P
, which is nonzero since
P
is irreducible. We also assume that
K
is sufficiently large for
Lemma

5.3
to hold.

First, we define the multiplicative function
\tilde{f}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
on prime powers as follows

\tilde{f}(p^{k}):=\begin{cases}f(p^{k}),\quad&\text{if }\ p>K,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
1,\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Since by assumption every prime
p\leq K
divides
Q/c
and since
(P(a,b),Q)=c
,
we have

(5.23)

p\nmid P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)\text{ for every }p\leq K\text{ and }m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
f
is multiplicative we deduce that

f(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))=\tilde{f}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))\quad\text{for every }m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Note also that
G_{P,N}(\tilde{f},Q)=G_{P,N}(f,Q)
and
{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(\tilde{f},1;K,N)={\mathbb{D}}_{P}(f,1;K,N)
.
It follows that in order to establish (
5.21
), it is enough to show that there exist
C_{A,P,Q}>0
such that for all sufficiently large
N
, depending only on
A
and
K
, we have

(5.24)

{\mathbb{E}}_{m,n\in[N]}|\tilde{f}(P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b))-\exp(G_{P,N}(\tilde{f},Q))|\ll_{P}\\
({\mathbb{D}}_{P}+{\mathbb{D}}_{P}^{2})(\tilde{f},1;K,\sqrt{N})+C_{A,P,Q}\cdot{\mathbb{D}}_{P}(\tilde{f},1;\sqrt{N},N)+K^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

In order to establish (
5.24
) we
make a series of further reductions that will eventually allow us to apply Lemma
5.3
. By the definition of
{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
(see (
4.1
)), for each
p\not\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
that does not divide
\alpha

(recall that
K>|\alpha|
so
p>K
implies that
p\nmid\alpha
), we have that
p\mid P(m,n)
implies that
p\mid m
and
p\mid n
.
Using this and (
5.23
), we get that the contribution to the average in (
5.24
) of those
m,n\in[N]
for which
P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)
is divisible by some prime
p\not\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
is

\ll\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{K<p\leq N}\left[\frac{N}{p}\right]^{2}\ll\frac{1}{K},

which is acceptable.

Next we show that the contribution to the average in (
5.24
) of those
m,n\in[N]
for which
P_{c}(Qm+a,Qn+b)
is divisible by
p^{2}
for some prime
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}
with
p>K
(hence
p\nmid Q
) is also acceptable. Note that this contribution is maximized for
c=1
, hence it is sufficient to check this for
c=1
, that is, for the polynomial
P
instead of
P_{c}
, assuming that
(P(a,b),Q)=1
. For
fixed
n\in[N]
such that
p\nmid Qn+b
there are at most
\omega_{P}(p^{2})\cdot([N/p^{2}]+1)
values of
m\in[N]
such that
p^{2}\mid P(Qm+a,Qn+b).
On the other hand, if
p>K
satisfies
p\mid Qn+b
and
p\mid P(Qm+a,Qn+b)
, then also
p\mid Qm+a
(recall that
p>K
implies that
p\nmid\alpha
). Since
P(n,1)
is an irreducible quadratic polynomial we have
\omega_{P}(p^{2})\leq 2
for all but finitely many
p\in{\mathbb{P}}
.
So the contribution to the average in (
5.24
) of those
m,n\in[N]

for which
P(Qm+a,Qn+b)
is divisible by
p^{2}
for some prime
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{P}

is bounded by (note again that our assumptions imply that
P(Qm+a,Qn+b)
is only divisible by primes
p>K
)

\ll_{P}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\Big(\sum_{K<p\leq N}\Big(\left[\frac{N}{p^{2}}\right]+1\Big)N+\sum_{K<p\leq N}\left[\frac{N}{p}\right]^{2}\Big)\ll_{P}\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{\log{N}},

where

\text{a quadratic nonresidue mod }q&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\bmod 4\text{ and }q\in F_{2}\\
\text{a quadratic nonresidue mod }q&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\bmod 4\text{ and }q\in F_{1}\\
\text{a quadratic residue mod }q&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\bmod 4\text{ and }q\in F_{2}\end{cases}

and furthermore assume that
i_{q}
is chosen so that
(i_{q},q)=1
.
Also let
i=7
if
2\in F_{1}
and
i=3
otherwise.
Using the Chinese remainder theorem and the Dirichlet theorem we can find a prime
p
satisfying
p\equiv i\bmod 8
and
p\equiv i_{q}\bmod q
for every odd prime
q\in F_{1}\cup F_{2}
.
In view of quadratic reciprocity, such
p
will satisfy the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
A,B,C\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and assume that none of
A,B,C,ABC
is a perfect square.
Then there exists a prime
p
such that none of
A,B,C
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
.

Proof.

Dividing
A,B,C
by any square factors does not change either the hypothesis or the conclusion of the lemma, so we may assume that
A,B,C
are all squarefree numbers.
If
A=B=C=-1
, then take
p=3
.
Otherwise, the assumption that
ABC
is not a perfect square and
A,B,C
are square-free implies that there exists a prime
p_{0}
that either divides all of
A,B,C
or divides exactly one of them.

In the first case, apply
Lemma

7.2
with
F_{2}=\{p_{0}\}
and
F_{1}
the set of all primes that divide
ABC
other than
p_{0}
, together with
-1
.

Suppose now that we are in the second case, where
p_{0}
divides (say)
A
but not
B
nor
C
.
We further separate into two cases: if there exists a prime
p_{1}
that divides both
B
and
C
(but not
A
), apply
Lemma

7.2
with
F_{2}=\{p_{0},p_{1}\}
and
F_{1}
the set of all primes that divide
ABC
other than
p_{0},p_{1}
, together with
-1
.

In the final case there exist primes
p_{1}
dividing
B
but not
C
and
p_{2}
dividing
C
but not
B
. Both
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are allowed to divide
A
.
In this case we apply
Lemma

7.2
with the following sets. If exactly one of
p_{1},p_{2}
divides
A
, let
F_{2}=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
, otherwise let
F_{2}=\{p_{0},p_{1},p_{2}\}
. In either case, let
F_{1}
be the set of all primes that divide
ABC
other than those in
F_{2}
, together with
-1
.
∎

Proposition

1.15
follows by
putting together the lemmas above (with
A=ac
,
B=bc
and
C=(a+b)c
).

7.2.
Other necessary conditions

Finally, we proof Propositions
1.16
and
1.17
.

Proof of
Proposition

1.16
.

The assumptions imply that
c
is even.
Since the equation
ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}
is partition regular with respect to
x,y
if and only if
ax^{2}+by^{2}=c(2z)^{2}
is (as the color of
z
is irrelevant), we may assume that
c\equiv 2\bmod 4
.

Let
\chi:{\mathbb{N}}\to\{-1,1\}
be the completely multiplicative function defined by
\chi(2)=-1
and
\chi(p)=1
for any other prime
p
.
Suppose, for the sake of a contradiction, that
x,y,z
satisfy (
1.1
) and
\chi(x)=\chi(y)
.
Since
a,c
are even and
b
is odd, it follows that
y
is even.
If
z
is even, then
x
must be even as well; in this case
(x/2,y/2,z/2)
is another solution with
\chi(x)=\chi(y)
, so we may reduce to the case when
z
is odd.
Similarly, we can assume that
x
is odd.
Together with
\chi(x)=\chi(y)
this implies that
y
is a multiple of
4
.

Analysing the equation (
1.1
) modulo
16
yields
ax^{2}\equiv cz^{2}
.
Since odd squares are congruent to
1\bmod 8
, after dividing by
2
we have
(a/2)\equiv(c/2)\bmod 8
.
This implies that the odd perfect square
S:=(a/2)b(a+b)(c/2)
satisfies
1\equiv S\equiv ab+b^{2}\equiv ab+1\bmod 8
, and hence that
ab+1
is a multiple of
8
.
This contradicts our assumptions on
a,b
and we conclude that in this case (
1.1
) is not partition regular with respect to
x,y
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

1.17
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2^{d}||a+b
.
From the assumptions it follows that
d
is odd and that
s:=ab(a+b)c/2^{d+1}
is an odd perfect square.
Let
\ell=d+3
and consider the coloring
\chi:{\mathbb{N}}\to\{1,3,\dots,2^{\ell}-1\}
described recursively by
\chi(n)=n\bmod 2^{\ell}
if
n
is odd, and
\chi(n)=\chi(n/2)
if
n
is even.

Suppose
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy (
1.1
) and
\chi(x)=\chi(y)
.
If both
x,y
are even, then
ax^{2}+by^{2}
is a multiple of
4
, forcing
z
to be even.
In that case, dividing all of
x,y,z
by
2
we still obtain a solution with
\chi(x)=\chi(y)
, so we may assume without loss of generality that
x,y
are not both even.
Analysing mod
2
shows that both
x
and
y
must be odd, and hence
x\equiv y\bmod 2^{\ell}
.

If follows that
ax^{2}+by^{2}\equiv(a+b)x^{2}\bmod 2^{\ell}
and hence
2^{d}
is the largest power of
2
that divides
ax^{2}+by^{2}
.
Using (
1.1
) and the fact that
2||c
it follows that
2^{d-1}||z^{2}
, and hence that
z^{\prime}:=z^{2}/2^{d-1}
is an odd perfect square.
Reducing the equation modulo
2^{\ell}
we have

2^{d}\frac{a+b}{2^{d}}x^{2}\equiv 2^{d}\frac{c}{2}\cdot z^{\prime}\bmod 2^{\ell}\qquad\iff\qquad\frac{a+b}{2^{d}}x^{2}\equiv\frac{c}{2}\cdot z^{\prime}\bmod 8.

Recalling that
s=ab(a+b)c/2^{d+1}
is an odd perfect square, as well as
x^{2}
and
z^{\prime}
, and hence are all equal to
1\bmod 8
, multiplying both sides above by the odd number
abc/2
we get that
1\equiv abc^{2}/4\bmod 8
. Since
c^{2}/4
is another odd square, this yields the desired conclusion
ab\equiv 1\bmod 8
.
∎

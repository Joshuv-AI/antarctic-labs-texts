Three-component link homotopy
scott Stirling

Abstract
. In 2019, Schneidermann and Teicher showed that the Kirk invariant classifies two-component link maps of two-spheres in the four-sphere up to link homotopy. In this paper, we construct a three-component link homotopy invariant. We construct two link maps where each component has the same image, and apply our invariant to prove that nevertheless they are not link homotopic. We develop tools to help distinguish between three-component link maps. We then construct a similar invariant for three-component annular link maps. Towards the end of the paper we discuss how to generalise to an n n -component link map invariant.

1.1.
Organisation of paper

In Section
2
, we define the equivariant intersection form, geometric intersections invariant, and establish their relationship to each other.

In Section
3
, three types of homotopy are defined which allow us to understand link homotopy in terms of a more rigid structure. We also discuss the results which have been achieved in the two-component case.

In Section
4
, Milnor groups are defined and we revise commutator calculus. We then show that the Milnor group of the free group on two generators,
F/F_{3}
, is isomorphic to the integral Heisenberg group of matrices and compute its group homology. We then calculate the homology of the complement of generically immersed two-spheres in the four-sphere and show that the second homology is generated by Clifford tori around each self-intersection. Using Dwyer’s theorem we show that the Milnor group of the fundamental group of the complement of two generically immersed disjoint spheres is isomorphic to
F/F_{3}
.

In Section
5
, we give a link homotopy invariant for based three-component link maps of oriented two-spheres. We then remove the based restriction to create
\widetilde{\sigma}^{3}
. We then develop tools for being able to differentiate between values of
\widetilde{\sigma}^{3}
since our invariant takes values in a quotient space. We show there exist examples of link maps with the same image in
S^{4}
but are distinct. Additionally, we develop an invariant which highlights how analogous our invariant is to the triple linking number. We then construct an invariant which measures the size of the values of our invariant.

In Section
6
, we construct an invariant for annular link maps and show how one can use annular link maps to create link maps and establish the existence of an annular link map invariant which records induced automorphism information and the intersection information.

In Section
7
, we discuss generalising
\widetilde{\sigma}^{3}
to establish an
n
-component link map invariant.

2.1.
The intersection form

Let

is given a left

\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}\pi
; where

has a involution coming from extending the involution
\overline{g}:=g^{-1}
on

linearly. With homology denoted by
H_{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)
.

We define the cochain complex by

C^{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)\colon=\Hom_{\rit-\Lambda}\left(\overline{C_{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)},\Lambda\right),

where
\overline{C_{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)}
is the involuted chain complex, with corresponding cohomology denoted

. This is a left
\Lambda
-module with the action of

defined by

(r\cdot f)(\sigma)\colon=rf(\sigma),

where

,
f\in C^{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)
, and
\sigma\in\overline{C_{*}(X,Y;\Lambda)}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Define
\kappa:C^{n}(X,Y;\Lambda)\rightarrow\overline{\Hom_{\lef-\Lambda}\left(C_{n}(X,Y;\Lambda),\Lambda)\right)}
to be the map

f\mapsto\left(\sigma\mapsto\overline{f(\sigma)}\right)\text{.}

We denote the evaluation map by

p:H^{n}\left(\overline{\Hom_{\lef-\Lambda}\left(C_{*}(X,Y;\Lambda),\Lambda\right)}\right)\rightarrow\overline{\Hom_{\lef-\Lambda}\left(H_{n}(X,Y;\Lambda\right),\Lambda)}

and define
\ev\colon:=p\circ\kappa
. We often refer to

as the evaluation map, however we will be specify which map if it is unclear.

Let

be a compact orientable connected four-manifold with

which is equipped with Poincaré duality maps

PD:H^{n}(W;\Lambda)\rightarrow H_{4-n}(W,\partial W;\Lambda)\text{ and }PD:H^{n}(W,\partial W;\Lambda)\rightarrow H_{4-n}(W;\Lambda)\text{.}

Consider the following composition:

\Phi:H_{2}(W;\Lambda)\xrightarrow{q}H_{2}(W,\partial W;\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\PD^{-1}}H^{2}(W;\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\ev}\overline{\Hom_{\lef-\Lambda}\left(H_{2}(W;\Lambda),\Lambda\right)}\text{.}

.

The

on

\displaystyle H_{2}(W;\Lambda)\times H_{2}(W;\Lambda)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Lambda

\displaystyle(a,b)

\displaystyle\mapsto\Phi(b)(a)\text{.}

From the definition, it is clear this form is sesquilinear and it is well known that the intersection form is hermitian

Definition 2.3
.

We define the

to be

\lambda_{\text{geo}}(f,g)=\sum_{x\in f(S^{2})\cap g(S^{2})}\varepsilon_{x}g_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}\pi,

where
\varepsilon_{x}
is the sign of the intersection at

.

The geometric intersection is independent of the choice of paths on each sphere as

is simply connected. However, if we consider

with a different choice of basing path this may change the value of the geometric intersection. Hence, it is only well defined on based maps. The geometric intersection form agrees with the equivariant intersection form
[
16
]
. Hence, we will use
\lambda
to refer to both the geometric and the equivariant intersection forms.

Let
f^{+}\colon S^{2}\rightarrow M
be a normal push-off, using a section of the normal bundle of

which is transverse to the
0

\lambda_{geo}(f,f)
we define:

\lambda_{\text{geo}}(f,f):=\lambda(f,f^{+}).

This is independent of the choice of normal push off.

We define a self-intersection number related to
\lambda
. Let
I
be the set of all double points of

and let
p\in I
. Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in f^{-1}\left(p\right)
with

and let
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}:[0,1]\rightarrow S^{2}
be paths from

and

We could have swapped the roles of
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
and this would give us the loop

. To resolve this we take values in the quotient

. This is a group quotient where we view

as an abelian group.

Definition 2.4
.

The

of a generic (based) immersion

is defined to be

\mu(f)\colon=\sum_{p\in I}\varepsilon_{p}g_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}\widetilde{\pi}\text{,}

where
\varepsilon_{p}

.

We can relate the self-intersection number and geometric intersection number by the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f:S^{2}\rightarrow M
be a generic immersion and let
\iota:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\pi
be the ring homomorphism where

. Then the following equation holds

\lambda\left(f,f\right):=\mu(f)+\overline{\mu(f)}+\iota\left(\chi(f)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}\pi,

where
\overline{\mu(f)}
is given by taking the representatives we use to describe

and sending each group element to its inverse, and
\chi(f)
is the Euler number of the normal bundle of

.

It is easily shown from this that
\mu
is not a homotopy invariant. Cusp homotopies (defined in the next section) change the Euler number of the normal bundle by
\pm 2
but
\lambda
remains fixed as it is a homotopy invariant. Therefore must
\mu
must change for
\lambda
to remain constant.

3.
Homotopy of surfaces and the Kirk invariant

3.1.
The structure of link homotopy in dimension four

Definition 3.1
.

A map

f=f_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup f_{n}:\coprod_{i=1}^{n}S^{2}_{i}\rightarrow S^{4},

where
S^{2}_{i}\cong S^{2}
, is called a
link map
if components disjoint in the domain are disjoint in the image, i.e
f_{i}(S^{2}_{i})\cap f_{j}(S^{2}_{j})=\phi
for
i\neq j
. A
link homotopy
is a homotopy through link maps. We denote the set of
n
-component link maps up to link homotopy by
\LM_{\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{n}}^{4}
. If each of our spheres comes equipped with a basing path from the basepoint in
S^{4}
to
f_{i}(S^{2}_{i})
we call
f
a
based link map
. The set of based link maps up to link homotopy is denoted
\LM_{\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{n}*}^{4}
.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

A generic (link-)homotopy between two generic immersions is (link-)homotopic to concatenations of isotopies, finger moves, Whitney moves, and cusp homotopies.

Hence, we can think of a link homotopy between two link maps as a combination of isotopy, Whitney moves, finger moves and cusp homotopies.

3.2.
Two-component link maps and the Kirk invariant

For this subsection all link maps will have two components unless stated otherwise. In 1988, Koschorke showed that, up to link homotopy, connect sum is well defined on two-component link maps
[
12
]
. This turns
\LM_{2,2}^{4}
an abelian monoid. In 1999 Bartels and Teichner showed that each element in
\LM_{2,2}^{4}
has an inverse, establishing that
\LM_{2,2}^{4}
is a group
[
2
]
. Two-component link maps have an invariant defined by Paul Kirk in 1988, which we now call the Kirk invariant. In 2018, Schneiderman and Teichner showed that the Kirk invariant is a complete invariant of
\LM_{2,2}^{4}

[
18
]
. We define this invariant since this is the inspiration for our three-component invariant.

Let
f=f_{1}\sqcup f_{2}:S^{2}_{1}\coprod S^{2}_{2}\rightarrow S^{4}
be a link map and define

A divergence-free finite element method for the Stokes problem with boundary correction Thanks: This work was supported in part by the National Science Foundation through grant number DMS-2011733.
Haoran Liu and Michael Neilan and M. Baris Otus

Abstract
. This paper constructs and analyzes a boundary correction finite element method for the Stokes problem based on the Scott-Vogelius pair on Clough-Tocher splits. The velocity space consists of continuous piecewise quadratic polynomials, and the pressure space consists of piecewise linear polynomials without continuity constraints. A Lagrange multiplier space that consists of continuous piecewise quadratic polynomials with respect to boundary partition is introduced to enforce boundary conditions as well as to mitigate the lack of pressure-robustness. We prove several inf-sup conditions, leading to the well-posedness of the method. In addition, we show that the method converges with optimal order and the velocity approximation is divergence free.

65N30,65N12,76M10

1.
Introduction

Boundary correction methods are a broad class of unfitted finite element methods, i.e.,
methods in which the computational mesh does not conform to the physical domain
\Omega
.
In contrast to, e.g., isoparametric methods, in which a domain is approximated
via curved elements, boundary correction methods generally solve a PDE
in a polytopal interior domain and transfer boundary conditions in a way such
that the scheme still maintains optimal order convergence.
This polytopal approximation is, in general, not an
O(h^{2})
approximation
to the physical domain and in particular, the polytope’s vertices are not necessarily
on the boundary of
\Omega
.
This approach can be advantageous for, e.g., dynamic problems with moving boundaries,
as remeshing is not needed at each time step.
Another feature of boundary correction methods, in contrast to other unfitted schemes, is the absence of ‘cut elements’
which may require special quadrature formula and algebraic stabilization.
Boundary correction methods were first
introduced and analyzed nearly
50
years ago
[
6
]

for the Poisson problem, and the technique has been
improved and refined recently resulting in practical and robust implementations

[
12
,
28
,
24
,
2
,
3
,
4
]

(see also
[
7
,
17
]
for variants).

In this article, we construct
a boundary correction finite element method for the Stokes problem
based on the Scott-Vogelius pair on Clough-Tocher (or Alfeld) splits. The velocity approximation is sought
in the space of continuous piecewise quadratic polynomials, whereas
the pressure space is approximated by piecewise linear polynomials
without continuity constraints. From their definitions, we see that the divergence operator maps
the velocity space into the pressure space, and therefore, the scheme
yields divergence-free velocity approximations. As far as we are aware
this is the first
H^{1}
-conforming divergence–free finite element method for incompressible flow
on unfitted meshes.

The construction and analysis of divergence-free methods
is an active area of research, and many
schemes have been proposed
[
21
,
1
,
33
,
18
,
15
,
14
]
.
These schemes have several inherent advantages, e.g., exact conservation laws
for any mesh size and long-time stability
[
10
,
5
]
. Another feature of these schemes
is pressure-robustness; similar to the continuous setting, modifying the source term in the Stokes problem
by a gradient field only affects the pressure approximation. This feature leads
to a decoupling in the velocity error, with abstract estimates independent of the viscosity.
Thus, divergence-free schemes may be advantageous for high Reynold number
flows and/or flows with large pressure gradients
[
31
,
32
,
23
]
. Except for the recent work
[
25
]
, where
isoparametric methods are introduced and studied, all of these divergence–free methods are applied to PDEs
on polytopal domains.

Let us describe the scheme in more detail and briefly summarize the context
of our results. The method starts with a background mesh enveloping the domain
\Omega
,
and the computational mesh simply consists of those
elements fully contained in
\bar{\Omega}
. The method
is based on a standard Nitsche-based formulation,
where the Dirichlet
boundary conditions are enforced via penalization.
As the computational domain does not conform
to
\Omega
, boundary conditions are corrected
via simple applications of Taylor’s theorem to reduce
the inconsistency of the scheme.

The procedure
described so far is relatively standard for the Poisson problem (cf.
[
6
,
24
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
),
but leads to some pressing issues for the Stokes equations.
First, because the computational domain explicitly depends on the mesh parameter
h
,
inf-sup stability of the Stokes pair is not immediately obvious.
As explained in
[
16
]
,
the standard proof of inf-sup stability in the continuous setting (which
is needed for the discrete result) is based on a decomposition of the computational domain
into a finite number of strictly star shaped domains; the number
of star shaped domains is generally unbounded as
h\to 0
.
This issue can be circumvented with pressure-stabilization
[
24
,
3
]
, but at the price
of additional consistency errors and poor conservation properties.
We address this stability issue by designing the computational mesh
such that it inherits a macro element structure and
applying the framework developed in
[
16
]

for Stokes pairs on unfitted domains. Doing so, we show
that the resulting pair is uniformly stable on the unfitted domain with respect
to the discretization parameter.

The second difficulty of a boundary correction
method for the Stokes problem is its lack of pressure-robustness.
This feature is not due to the boundary correction per se, but rather
due to the weak enforcement of boundary conditions via penalization.
In particular, a divergence-free method for the Stokes problem
with weak enforcement of the boundary conditions is
not
pressure robust.
We mitigate the lack of pressure robustness in the scheme by introducing
an additional Lagrange multiplier that enforces the boundary conditions of the normal component of the velocity.
The Lagrange multiplier space consists of continuous piecewise quadratic polynomials
with respect to the boundary partition, and the Lagrange multiplier is an approximation to the pressure (modulo
an additive constant) restricted to the computational boundary.
The Lagrange multiplier ameliorates the lack of pressure robustness of the method
and leads to a weakly coupled velocity error estimate; the velocity error’s dependence on the viscosity
is compensated by a higher-order power of the discretization parameter
h
.
We remark that Lagrange multipliers within boundary correction schemes have been proposed and studied in
[
9
,
11
]

for the Poisson problem.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, we state the Stokes problem,
the computational mesh, and the boundary transfer operator.
In Section
3
, we state the finite element method
and show that the scheme yields exactly divergence–free velocity approximations.
Section
4
proves several inf-sup conditions and
the well-posedness of the method.
In Section
5
, we prove optimal order convergence provided
the exact solution is sufficiently smooth. Finally, in Section
6

we perform some numerical experiments which verify the theoretical results,
and give some concluding remarks in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries

For a two-dimensional bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we consider the Stokes problem

(2.1a)

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle={\bm{f}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.1b)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\,\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle={\bm{g}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }{\partial}\Omega,

where
\nu>0
is the viscosity, assumed to be constant.
For simplicity in the presentation, and without loss of generality, we assume
that
{\bm{g}}=0
. The extension to non-homogeneous boundary conditions
is relatively straight-forward
[
19
]
.

We assume the domain has smooth boundary
{\partial}\Omega
with outward unit normal

\bm{n}
.
We denote by
\phi
the signed distance
function of
\Omega
such that
\phi(x)<0
for
x\in\Omega

and
\phi(x)\geq 0
otherwise, so that
\bm{n}=\nabla\phi/|\nabla\phi|

on
{\partial}\Omega
. For a positive number
\tau
,
denote by
\Gamma_{\tau}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\phi(x)|\leq\tau|\}

the tubular region around
{\partial}\Omega
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 14.16]
,
there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such
the closest point projection
{\bm{p}}:\Gamma_{\tau_{0}}\to{\partial}\Omega

is well defined and satisfies

{\bm{p}}(x)=x-\phi(x)\bm{n}({\bm{p}}(x))
for all
x\in\Gamma_{\tau_{0}}

[
9
]
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a polygon
such that
\Omega\subset S
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{h}

be a quasi-uniform triangulation of
S
that consists of shape regular triangles.
We define the computational mesh
as

\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{T\in\mathcal{S}_{h}:\ \bar{T}\subset\bar{\Omega}\},

and set

\Omega_{h}={\rm int}\Big(\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bar{T}\Big)\subset\Omega

to be the associated domain.
We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
the Clough-Tocher refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, obtained
by connecting the vertices of each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
to its barycenter.
The set of boundary of edges of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, which is also the set of boundary
edges of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
, is denoted by
\mathcal{E}^{B}_{h}
.
With an abuse of notation, for a piecewise smooth function
q
(with respect to
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
), we
write

\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}q\,ds=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}q\,ds.

We use
\bm{n}_{h}
to denote the outward unit normal with respect
to the computational boundary
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
.
For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
, we set
h_{K}={\rm diam}(K)

and
h=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}}h_{K}
.
Likewise, for
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
, we set
h_{e}={\rm diam}(e)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{h}^{ct}
the Clough-Tocher refinement
of the background mesh
\mathcal{S}_{h}
.
We emphasize that
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}\subset\mathcal{S}_{h}^{ct}
, however,

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}\neq\{K\in\mathcal{S}_{h}^{ct}:\ \bar{K}\subset\bar{\Omega}\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
inherits
the macro-element structure needed to prove
the stability of the Scott-Vogelius pair.

2.1.
Boundary transfer operator

The main component of boundary correction methods
is a well-defined mapping
M:{\partial}\Omega_{h}\to{\partial}\Omega
that assigns
each point on the computational boundary to physical one in order to “transfer” the boundary
information on
{\partial}\Omega
to
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
.
With such a mapping in hand, we can define
the transfer direction as

\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}({x})=(M-I){x}\qquad x\in{\partial}\Omega_{h},

and transfer length

(2.2)

\delta(x)=|\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}(x)|.

Several choices of the mapping
M

and corresponding transfer directions have appeared in
the literature. A common choice
(and arguably the most natural)
is to take
M
to be the closest point projection, i.e.,
M={\bm{p}}
.
In this case, assuming
\Omega_{h}
approximates

\Omega
well enough, the distance vector
\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}
defined above coincides (up to a multiplicative constant)
with the outward unit normal vector
\bm{n}
of the original boundary
\partial\Omega
.
In particular, there holds
\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}(x)=\phi(x)\bm{n}({\bm{p}}(x))
and
\delta(x)=|\phi(x)|
.
Another common choice is to take the transfer direction to be parallel to the outward unit normal
of the computational boundary, i.e.,
\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}/\delta=\bm{n}_{h}
.
In this case, we have
\delta(x)\geq|\phi(x)|
with possible large discrepancies between
\delta(x)
and
|\phi(x)|
, but it leads
to a simpler implementation in the numerical method.

In the definition and analysis of the method
below, we do not explicitly define the mapping
M
;
rather, our main requirement
for the mapping
M
is to satisfy the Assumption (
A
) below.
In particular, and similar to
[
6
,
28
,
9
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, the stability and convergence analysis
only assumes that the transfer distance
\delta(x)
is sufficiently
small relative to the mesh parameter
h
.
In the numerical experiments provided in Section
6
,
we take
M
to be an approximation to the closest point projection.

Set
{\bm{d}}=\mathfrak{{\bm{d}}}/{\delta}
,
for
x\in\partial\Omega_{h}
, and define the boundary transfer operator

(S_{h}{\bm{v}})(x)={\bm{v}}(x)+\delta(x)\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}(\delta(x))^{2}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}^{2}}(x).

Note that
(S_{h}{\bm{v}})(x)
is the second-order Taylor expansion of the function
{\bm{v}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Throughout this paper, the constants
C
and
c
(with or without subscripts) denote some positive constants that are independent of the mesh parameter
h
and the viscosity.

3.
A divergence–free finite element method

For
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
denote by
\EuScript{P}_{k}(D)
the space of polynomials
of degree
\leq k
with domain
D
.
Analogous vector-valued spaces are denoted in boldface.
We define the lowest-order Scott-Vogelius finite element pair
with respect to the Clough-Tocher triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{1}(\Omega_{h}):\ {\bm{v}}|_{K}\in\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{2}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct},\ \int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\,ds=0\},

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle=\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega_{h}):\ q|_{K}\in\EuScript{P}_{1}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}\},

and the analogous spaces with boundary conditions

\displaystyle\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}

\displaystyle={\bm{V}}_{h}\cap{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h}),\qquad\mathring{Q}_{h}=Q_{h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{h}).

We further introduce a Lagrange multiplier space

\displaystyle X_{h}=\{\mu\in C({\partial}\Omega_{h}):\ \mu|_{e}\in\EuScript{P}_{2}(e)\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}\},

and its variant,

\mathring{X}_{h}=\{\mu\in X_{h}:\ \int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\mu\,ds=0\}.

We define the bilinear form

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle=\nu\Big(\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla\bm{u}:\nabla{\bm{v}}\,dx-\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\frac{{\partial}\bm{u}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,ds+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\cdot(S_{h}\bm{u})\,ds,

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}(S_{h}\bm{u})\cdot(S_{h}{\bm{v}})\,ds\Big),

where
\sigma>0
is a penalty parameter.

Remark 3.1
.

The bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is based on a standard “Nitsche bilinear form” associated
with the Laplace operator, but with boundary correction
[
26
,
30
]
.
Note that the bilinear form
is based on a non-symmetric version of Nitsche’s method
due to the positive sign in front of the third term
in the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.
However, boundary correction methods based on the symmetric version
of Nitsche’s method still yield a non-symmetric
bilinear form
[
6
,
24
]
.
The non-symmetric version allows less restrictions on the penalty parameter
\sigma
to ensure
stability if the extension direction coincides with the outward unit
normal of
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
.
In particular, if
{\bm{d}}=\bm{n}_{h}
,
a standard argument shows that the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
{\bm{V}}_{h}

for any
\sigma>0
; cf. Lemma
4.4
.

We define two bilinear forms associated with the continuity equations, one without and one with
boundary correction:

\displaystyle b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds,

\displaystyle b^{e}_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}((S_{h}{\bm{v}})\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds.

We consider the method of finding

(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h},\lambda_{h})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}
such that

(3.1a)

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},{\bm{v}})+b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h},\lambda_{h}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},

(3.1b)

\displaystyle b^{e}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},(q,\mu))

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Remark 3.2
.

The zero mean-value constant
defined in the Lagrange multiplier space
\mathring{X}_{h}

mods out constants, and is due to the condition
\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\,ds=0

in the definition of the discrete velocity space
{\bm{V}}_{h}
.
If this constraint is not imposed in the Lagrange multiplier space,
then in general (
3.1
) is ill-posed since

b_{h}({\bm{v}},(0,1))=0\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}.

On the other hand, the constraint
\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\,ds=0

is needed to ensure that method (
3.1
) yields
a divergence-free solution, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 3.3

(Divergence–free property)
.

If
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h},\lambda_{h})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}

satisfies (
3.1
), then
{\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h}\equiv 0
in
\Omega_{h}
.

Proof.

The definition of the Stokes pair
{\bm{V}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}

shows
{\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h}\in\mathring{Q}_{h}
.
Then, letting
q={\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h}
and
\mu=0
in (
3.1b
) yields

0=b^{e}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},({\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h},0))=-\|{\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

Thus,
{\rm div}\,\bm{u}_{h}\equiv 0
.
∎

4.
Stability and Continuity estimates

In our stability and convergence analysis,
we make an assumption regarding the distance
between the PDE domain
\Omega

and the computational domain
\Omega_{h}
.
To state this assumption, we define for a boundary edge
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
,

\delta_{e}:=\max_{x\in\bar{e}}\delta(x).

We make the assumption

(A)

\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\delta_{e}\leq c_{\delta}<1,\qquad\text{for }c_{\delta}\text{ sufficiently small.}

Remark 4.1
.

Assumption (
A
) essentially states
that the distance between
{\partial}\Omega
and
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
is of order
h
, i.e.,

\delta=O(h)
with (hidden) constant
sufficiently small. Similar assumptions, in the context
of boundary correction methods, are made in, e.g.,

[
6
,
27
,
24
,
2
,
3
]
.

We define three
H^{1}
-type norms on
{\bm{V}}_{h}
:

\displaystyle\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2},

\displaystyle\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2},

\displaystyle|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle=\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}.

In addition, we define a
H^{-1/2}
-norm on the Lagrange multiplier space
\mathring{X}_{h}
:

\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}^{2}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}.

Finally, we define the norm on
\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}
as

\|(q,\mu)\|:=\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}.

Lemma 4.2
.

There holds for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\leq Cc_{\delta}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}^{2},

provided that
c_{\delta}
in (
A
) is sufficiently small.
In particular,
\|\cdot\|_{h}
,
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
, and
|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}
are equivalent on
{\bm{V}}_{h}
.

Proof.

By trace and inverse inequalities, the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and (
A
), there holds for
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}|\delta|^{2j}\big|\frac{{\partial}^{j}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}^{j}}\big|^{2}\,ds\leq C\delta_{e}^{2j}h_{e}^{-2j}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{e})}^{2}\leq Cc_{\delta}^{2j}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{e})}^{2}\quad j=1,2,

where
T_{e}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
satisfies
e\subset{\partial}T
. It then follows from the definition of
S_{h}
and

\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\sum_{j=0}^{2}h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}|\delta|^{2j}\big|\frac{{\partial}^{j}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}^{j}}\big|^{2}\,ds\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}^{2}.

This inequality immediately yields
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}
.
Moreover, standard arguments involving the trace and inverse inequalities
show
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}\leq|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}
on
{\bm{V}}_{h}
.
Thus, to complete the proof,
it suffices to show that
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}
.

To this end, we once again use (
4.2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+C\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{e}|\delta|^{2j}\big|\frac{{\partial}^{j}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}^{j}}\big|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+C\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

This inequality implies
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{h}
.

∎

4.1.
Continuity and coercivity estimates of bilinear forms

Lemma 4.3
.

There holds

(4.3)

\displaystyle|a_{h}({\bm{v}},\bm{w})|

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}(1+\sigma)\nu|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{w}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{\bm{v}},\bm{w}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}+H^{3}(\Omega_{h}),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\big|b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\|(q,\mu)\|\quad

\displaystyle\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big|b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))-b_{h}^{e}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))\big|

\displaystyle\leq Cc_{\delta}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\|(q,\mu)\|\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},\ \forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Proof.

The proof of the continuity estimate of (
4.3
)
is given in
[
2
, Proposition 1]
(with superficial modifications).
The continuity estimate of
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
(
4.4
) follows directly
from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

This third estimate (
4.5
) follows from the definition of the forms,
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
4.2
):

\displaystyle\big|b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))-b_{h}^{e}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\big(({\bm{v}}-S_{h}{\bm{v}})\cdot\bm{n}_{h}\big)\mu\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}|\delta|^{2j}\big|\frac{{\partial}^{j}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}{\bm{d}}^{j}}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}

\displaystyle\leq Cc_{\delta}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that Assumption (
A
) is satisfied for
c_{\delta}

sufficiently small.
Then there exists
\sigma_{0}>0
such that, for
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
,

c_{1}{\nu}\|{\bm{v}}\|^{2}_{1,h}\leq a_{h}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}

for
c_{1}>0
independent of
h
and
\nu
.
If the extension direction
{\bm{d}}
coincides with the outward unit normal
of
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
, i.e., if
{\bm{d}}=\bm{n}_{h}
, then coercivity is satisfied
for any positive penalty parameter
\sigma>0
.

Proof.

The proof the result under
assumption (
A
)
follows exactly from the arguments in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
6
, Lemma 6]
),
so the proof is omitted.

If
{\bm{d}}=\bm{n}_{h}
, then by definition of the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
,

\displaystyle a_{h}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle=\nu\Big(\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\Big(\int_{e}\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\cdot(S_{h}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}})\,ds+\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\nu\Big(\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\Big(\int_{e}\delta\big|\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\big|^{2}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{e}\delta^{2}\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}^{2}_{h}}\,ds+\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)\Big).

We then use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, standard trace and inverse estimates,
and Assumption (
A
) to get

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\delta^{2}\frac{{\partial}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}_{h}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\bm{v}}}{{\partial}\bm{n}^{2}_{h}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq(\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\delta_{e}\big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h^{3}_{e}\|D^{2}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Cc_{\delta}^{2}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}.

Thus, we find

\displaystyle a_{h}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle\geq\nu\Big((1-Cc_{\delta}^{2})\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}\|S_{h}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)\Big)\geq C\nu\|{\bm{v}}\|^{2}_{h}\geq C\nu\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}^{2}

for
c_{\delta}
sufficiently small and for
\sigma>0
.

∎

4.2.
Inf-Sup Stability I

In this section we prove
the discrete inf-sup (LBB) condition for the Stokes pair

\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}

with stability constants independent of
h
.
In the case of a fixed polygonal domain,
the LBB stability for this pair is well-known (cf.
[
1
,
29
,
14
]
);
however, the extension of these results to the unfitted domain
\Omega_{h}
is not immediate.
In particular, the proofs in
[
1
,
29
,
14
]
(directly or indirectly)
rely on the Nečas inequality:

\mathfrak{c}_{h}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{h})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\qquad\forall q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{h})

for some
\mathfrak{c}_{h}>0
depending on the domain
\Omega_{h}
.
As explained in
[
16
]
, it is unclear if the constant
\mathfrak{c}_{h}
in this inequality
is independent of
h
.

Our approach is to simply combine the local
stability of the Scott-Vogelius
pair with the stability of the
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{2}\times\EuScript{P}_{0}
pair.
For a (macro) element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the local
spaces with boundary conditions

\displaystyle{\bm{V}}_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(T):\ {\bm{v}}|_{K}\in\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{2}(K)\ \forall K\subset T,\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}\},

\displaystyle Q_{0}(T)

\displaystyle=\{q\in L^{2}_{0}(T):\ q|_{K}\in\EuScript{P}_{1}(K)\ \forall K\subset T,\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}\}.

We state a local surjectivity
of the divergence operator acting on these
spaces. The proof is found in, e.g.,
[
14
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every
q\in Q_{0}(T)
, there exists

{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{0}(T)
such that
{\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}=q

and
\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq\beta^{-1}_{T}\|q\|_{L^{2}(T)}
.
Here, the constant
\beta_{T}>0
depends only
on the shape-regularity of
T
.

Next, we state the recent stability result of the
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{2}\times\EuScript{P}_{0}

pair on unfitted domains (cf.
[
16
, Theorem 1, Section 6.3, and Remark 1]
).

Lemma 4.6
.

Define the space of piecewise constants
with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\mathring{Y}_{h}=\{q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{h}):\ q|_{T}\in\EuScript{P}_{0}(T)\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\subset\mathring{Q}_{h}.

There exists
\beta_{0}>0
and
h_{0}>0

such that for
h\leq h_{0}
, there holds

\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\geq\beta_{0}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\qquad\forall q\in\mathring{Y}_{h}.

Combining Lemmas
4.5
–
4.6

yields the following stability result for the
\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}

Stokes pair.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists
\beta_{1}>0
independent of
h
such that

\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}\geq\beta_{1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\qquad\forall q\in\mathring{Q}_{h}.

for
h\leq h_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof essentially follows from Lemmas
4.5
–
4.6

with the arguments in
[
1
,
29
,
14
]
. We provide
the proof for completeness.

Let
q\in\mathring{Q}_{h}
, and let
\bar{q}\in\mathring{Y}_{h}
be its piecewise average, i.e.,
\bar{q}|_{T}=|T|^{-1}\int_{T}q\,dx
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
We then have
(q-\bar{q})|_{T}\in Q_{0}(T)
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
and therefore, by Lemma
4.5
, there exists
{\bm{v}}_{1,T}\in{\bm{V}}_{0}(T)

such that
{\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}_{1,T}=(q-\bar{q})|_{T}
and
\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq\beta_{T}^{-1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(T)}
.
Defining
{\bm{v}}_{1}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}
by
{\bm{v}}_{1}|_{T}={\bm{v}}_{1,T}\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
we have
{\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}_{1}=(q-\bar{q})
in
\Omega_{h}
and
\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\beta_{*}^{-1}\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}
,
where
\beta_{*}=\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\beta_{T}
.

With this result, and by Lemma
4.6
, we conclude

\displaystyle\beta_{0}\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})\bar{q}\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}+\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\beta_{*}^{-1})\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|q-\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\big(\beta^{-1}_{*}+\beta_{0}^{-1}(1+\beta^{-1}_{*})\big)\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx}{\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}}.

Setting
\beta_{1}=\big(\beta^{-1}_{*}+\beta_{0}^{-1}(1+\beta^{-1}_{*})\big)^{-1}
completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Inf-Sup Stability II

The following lemma proves
inf-sup stability for the Lagrange multiplier part
of the bilinear form
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

There holds

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n})\mu\,ds}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}\geq\beta_{2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathring{X}_{h}.

for some
\beta_{2}>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

We label the boundary edges
as
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
,
and denote the boundary vertices by
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}=\mathcal{V}_{h}^{B}
, labeled
such that
e_{j}
has vertices
a_{j}
and
a_{j+1}
, with the convention that
a_{N+1}=a_{1}
.
Define the set of boundary edge midpoints
\mathcal{M}_{h}^{B}=\{m_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}

with
m_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{j}+a_{j+1})
.
Let
\bm{n}_{j}
be the normal vector of
{\partial}\Omega_{h}
restricted to the edge
e_{j}
,
and let
\bm{t}_{j}
be the tangent vector obtained by rotating
\bm{n}_{j}

90
degrees clockwise.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\bm{t}|_{e_{j}}

is parallel to
a_{j+1}-a_{j}
.
We further denote by
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{C}
the set of boundary corner vertices, i.e.,
if
a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{C}
, then the outward unit normals
\bm{n}_{j},\bm{n}_{j-1}

of the edges touching
a_{j}
are linearly independent.
The set of flat boundary vertices are defined as
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{F}=\mathcal{V}_{h}^{B}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{C}
.
Note that
\bm{n}_{j}=\bm{n}_{j-1}
and
\bm{t}_{j}=\bm{t}_{j-1}
for
a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{F}
.

Given
\mu\in\mathring{X}_{h}
, we define
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
by the conditions

(4.7)

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})(a_{j})=h\mu(a_{j}),\quad

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1})(a_{j})=h\mu(a_{j})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{C},

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})(a_{j})=h\mu(a_{j}),\quad

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{t}_{j})(a_{j})=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{F},

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})(m_{j})=h\mu(a_{j}),\quad

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{t}_{j})(m_{j})=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall m_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}^{B}.

All other (quadratic Lagrange) degrees of freedom of
{\bm{v}}
are set to zero, i.e.,

{\bm{v}}(a)=0
at all interior vertices and interior edge midpoints in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
.

Since
({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}-h\mu)|_{e_{j}}
is a quadratic
on each
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}
, and
{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}=h\mu
at three distinct points on
e_{j}
, we have that
{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}-h\mu|_{e_{j}}=0
.
Moreover, using quasi uniformity, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n})\mu\,ds\geq C\|\mu\|^{2}_{-1/2,h}.

It remains to show that
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\leq C\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}
to complete the proof.

For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{K}^{B},\mathcal{V}_{K}^{C},\mathcal{V}_{K}^{F},\mathcal{M}_{K}^{B}

be the sets of elements in
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{B},\mathcal{V}_{h}^{C},\mathcal{V}_{h}^{F},\mathcal{M}_{h}^{B}

contained in
\bar{K}
, respectively.
By a standard scaling argument and (
4.7
), we get (
m=0,1
)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{m}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{B}\cup\mathcal{M}_{K}^{B}}h_{e_{j}}^{2-2m}|{\bm{v}}(a_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle=C\Big(\sum_{a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{C}}h_{e_{j}}^{2-2m}|{\bm{v}}(a_{j})|^{2}+\sum_{a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{F}\cup\mathcal{M}_{K}^{B}}h_{e_{j}}^{4-2m}|\mu(a_{j})|^{2}\Big).

Claim:
|{\bm{v}}(a_{j})|\leq Ch|\mu(a_{j})|
for all
a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{C}
, where
C>0
is
uniformly bounded and independent of
h
,
\bm{n}_{j}
and
\bm{n}_{j-1}
.

Proof of the claim
: Assume that
\mathcal{V}_{K}^{C}
is non-empty
for otherwise the proof is trivial.
For
a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{C}
, we write
{\bm{v}}(a_{j})
in terms
of the basis
\{\bm{t}_{j},\bm{t}_{j-1}\}
, use (
4.7
),
and apply some elementary vector identities:

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}(a_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bm{t}_{j-1}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j})(a_{j})\bm{t}_{j-1}+\frac{1}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1})(a_{j})\bm{t}_{j}

\displaystyle=h\mu(a_{j})\Big(\frac{1}{\bm{t}_{j-1}\cdot\bm{n}_{j}}\bm{t}_{j-1}+\frac{1}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}\bm{t}_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=h\mu(a_{j})\Big(\frac{\bm{t}_{j}-\bm{t}_{j-1}}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}\Big).

Write
\bm{t}_{j}=(\cos(\theta_{j}),\sin(\theta_{j}))^{\intercal}
.
We then compute
\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}=\sin(\theta_{j-1}-\theta_{j})
, and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{t}_{j}-\bm{t}_{j-1}}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\cos(\theta_{j})-\cos(\theta_{j-1}),\sin(\theta_{j})-\sin(\theta_{j-1}))^{\intercal}}{\sin(\theta_{j-1}-\theta_{j})}.

Since

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta_{j}\rightarrow\theta_{j-1}}\frac{(\cos{\theta_{j}}-\cos{\theta_{j-1}},\sin{\theta_{j}}-\sin{\theta_{j-1}})^{\intercal}}{\sin{(\theta_{j-1}-\theta_{j})}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\theta_{j}\rightarrow\theta_{j-1}}\frac{(-\sin{\theta_{j}},\cos{\theta_{j}})^{\intercal}}{-\cos{(\theta_{j-1}-\theta_{j})}}

\displaystyle=(\sin{\theta_{j-1}},\cos{\theta_{j-1}})^{\intercal},

we conclude that
\big|\frac{\bm{t}_{j}-\bm{t}_{j-1}}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}\big|
is bounded
for
|\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}|\ll 1
, i.e., for “nearly flat boundary vertices”.
This conclusion and the shape regularity of the mesh
shows
\big|\frac{\bm{t}_{j}-\bm{t}_{j-1}}{\bm{t}_{j}\cdot\bm{n}_{j-1}}\big|\leq C

for some
C>0
independent of
h
and
\{\bm{n}_{j-1},\bm{n}_{j}\}
.
This concludes the proof of the claim.

Applying the claim to (
4.9
) and a scaling argument yields

\displaystyle\|{\bm{v}}\|_{H^{m}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{B}\cup\mathcal{M}_{K}^{B}}h_{e_{j}}^{4-2m}|\mu(a_{j})|^{2}\leq C\mathop{\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}}_{a_{j}\in\bar{e}:\ a_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{B}}h_{e}^{3-2m}\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}.

Therefore, by an inverse inequality and shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
,

\displaystyle\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\frac{1}{h_{e}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}^{2}+C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}}h_{K}^{-2}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq C\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}^{2}.

Combining this estimate with (
4.8
)
yields the desired inf-sup condition (
4.6
).
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The proof of Lemma
4.8
, and in particular
the proof of the claim, relies on the continuity properties
of the Lagrange multiplier space at nearly flat corner vertices.

4.4.
Main Stability Estimates

Combining Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8

yields inf-sup stability for the bilinear form
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.
We also show that this result implies inf-sup
stability for the bilinear form with boundary correction
b_{h}^{e}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Then there exists
\beta>0
depending only on
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
such that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\beta\|(q,\mu)\|\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}\qquad\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Proof.

We use Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
and
follow the arguments in
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Fix
(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}
.
The statement (
4.6
)
implies the existence of
{\bm{v}}_{2}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}

such that
\|{\bm{v}}_{2}\|_{1,h}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}_{2}\cdot\bm{n})\mu\,ds\geq\beta_{2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}.

By Lemma
4.7
, there exists
{\bm{v}}_{1}\in\mathring{{\bm{V}}}_{h}
satisfying
\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}=\|{\bm{v}}_{1}\|_{1,h}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}_{1})q\geq\beta_{1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Set
{\bm{v}}=c{\bm{v}}_{1}+{\bm{v}}_{2}
for some
c>0
, so that
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\leq(1+c)
,
and

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})q\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c\beta_{1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}-\|{\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\geq c\beta_{1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}-\sqrt{2}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\geq c\beta_{1}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}-\sqrt{2}\|{\bm{v}}_{2}\|_{1,h}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle=\big(c\beta_{1}-\sqrt{2}\big)\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Because
{\bm{v}}_{1}|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds=\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}_{2}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds\geq\beta_{2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle b_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))

\displaystyle\geq\big(c\beta_{1}-\sqrt{2}\big)\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\beta_{2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}

\displaystyle\geq(1+c)^{-1}\Big(\big(c\beta_{1}-\sqrt{2}\big)\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\beta_{2}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\Big)\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}.

We now choose
c>0
sufficiently large to obtain the desired result.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Provided
c_{\delta}
in Assumption (
A
) is sufficiently small, there exists

\beta_{e}>0
independent of
h
such that
there holds

(4.11)

\displaystyle\beta_{e}\|(q,\mu)\|\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}^{e}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}\qquad\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Proof.

Combining Theorem
4.10
and Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\beta\|(q,\mu)\|\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b^{e}_{h}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}+Cc_{\delta}\|(q,\mu)\|\qquad\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

This result implies (
4.11
) for
c_{\delta}
sufficiently small
with
\beta_{e}=\beta-Cc_{\delta}
.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h},\lambda_{h})\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\times\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}

satisfy (
3.1
).
Then, provided
c_{\delta}
in Assumption (
A
)
is sufficiently small, there holds

(4.12)

\nu\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|(p_{h},\lambda_{h})\|\leq C\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h},

where
\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h}=\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}
.
Consequently, there exists a unique solution to (
3.1
).

Proof.

Setting
{\bm{v}}=\bm{u}_{h}
in (
3.1a
),
(q,\mu)=(p_{h},\lambda_{h})
in (
3.1b
),
and subtracting the resulting expressions yields

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot\bm{u}_{h}\,dx+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\big((S_{h}\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u}_{h})\cdot\bm{n}_{h}\big)\lambda_{h}\,ds.

We apply the coercivity result in Lemma
4.4
,
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
4.1
) to get

(4.13)

\displaystyle\nu c_{1}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}+Cc_{\delta}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\lambda_{h}\|_{-1/2,h}.

On the other hand, we use inf-sup stability (
4.10
) to conclude

\displaystyle\beta\|(p_{h},\lambda_{h})\|_{-1/2,h}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h},\lambda_{h}))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},{\bm{v}})}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}.

Using the continuity estimate (
4.3
) gets

(4.14)

\displaystyle\beta\|\lambda_{h}\|_{-1/2,h}\leq\beta\|(p_{h},\lambda_{h})\|\leq\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h}+c_{2}(1+\sigma)\nu\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

Inserting this estimate into (
4.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nu\big(c_{1}-Cc_{\delta}c_{2}\beta^{-1}(1+\sigma)\big)\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\leq(1+Cc_{\delta}\beta^{-1})\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h}.

Thus,
\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\leq C\nu^{-1}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{-1,h}
for
c_{\delta}
sufficiently small.
This, combined with (
4.14
), yields the desired stability result (
4.12
).

∎

5.
Convergence Analysis

In this section, we show that
the solution to the the finite element
method (
3.1
) converges with optimal order
provided the exact solution is sufficiently smooth.
As a first step, we derive some consistency
estimates for the boundary correction
operator and the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

5.1.
Consistency Estimates

The following lemma bounds
the boundary correction operator acting on the exact velocity function.
The result is essentially an estimate on the Taylor polynomial remainder
and follows directly from the arguments in
[
4
, Proposition 3]
(also see
[
6
]
).
For this reason, its proof is omitted.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\bm{u}\in{\bm{H}}^{3}(\Omega)\cap{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}^{-1}\int_{e}\big|S_{h}\bm{u}\big|^{2}\,ds\leq Ch^{4}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Lemma 5.2
.

There holds for all
\bm{u}\in{\bm{H}}^{3}(\Omega)\cap{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Big|-\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{u},{\bm{v}})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}.

If
{\rm div}\,\bm{u}=0
in
\Omega
, then

\displaystyle\big|b_{h}^{e}(\bm{u},(q,\mu))\big|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|(q,\mu)\|\qquad\ \forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Proof.

We integrate-by-parts to write

\Big|-\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{u},{\bm{v}})\Big|=\nu\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial\bm{n}_{h}}(S_{h}\bm{u})\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}(S_{h}\bm{u})(S_{h}{\bm{v}})\,ds\Big|.

Next, we estimate the two terms on the right hand side of the above equality by using
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, trace and inverse inequalities, along with Lemmas
4.2
and
5.1
as follows:

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial\bm{n}_{h}}(S_{h}\bm{u})\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h_{e}\int_{e}\big|\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial\bm{n}_{h}}\big|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h^{-1}_{e}\int_{e}|S_{h}\bm{u}|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h},

and

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\frac{\sigma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}(S_{h}\bm{u})(S_{h}{\bm{v}})\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h^{-1}_{e}\int_{e}|S_{h}\bm{u}|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}h^{-1}_{e}\int_{e}|S_{h}{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}.

Thus, the first estimate (
5.1
) holds.

Similarly, another use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with Lemma
5.1
yields

\big|b_{h}^{e}(\bm{u},(q,\mu))\big|=\Big|\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}(S_{h}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds\Big|\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{-1/2,h},

and this completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Approximation Properties of the Kernel

We define the discrete kernel as

\bm{Z}_{h}=\{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}:\ b_{h}^{e}({\bm{v}},(q,\mu))=0,\ \forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}\}.

Note that if
{\bm{v}}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
, then
{\rm div}\,{\bm{v}}=0
in
\Omega_{h}
(cf. Lemma
3.3
),
and

(5.2)

\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}((S_{h}{\bm{v}})\cdot\bm{n}_{h})\mu\,ds=0\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathring{X}_{h}.

In this section, we show
that the kernel
\bm{Z}_{h}
has optimal order approximation
properties with respect to divergence-free smooth functions.
To this end, we define the orthogonal complement of
\bm{Z}_{h}
as

\bm{Z}_{h}^{\perp}:=\{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}:\ ({\bm{v}},\bm{w})_{1,h}=0\ \ \forall\bm{w}\in\bm{Z}_{h}\},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{1,h}
is the inner product on
{\bm{V}}_{h}

that induces the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

There holds

\displaystyle{\beta}_{e}\|\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\leq\sup_{(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}^{e}(\bm{w},(q,\mu))}{\|(q,\mu)\|}\qquad\forall\bm{w}\in\bm{Z}_{h}^{\perp}.

Proof.

The result follows from Corollary
4.11

and standard results in mixed finite element theory (cf.
[
8
, Lemma 12.5.10]
).
∎

The following theorem states
the approximation properties of the discrete kernel.

Theorem 5.4
.

For any
\bm{u}\in{\bm{H}}^{3}(\Omega)\cap{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

with
{\rm div}\,\bm{u}=0
, there holds

(5.3)

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{w}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-\bm{w}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
be arbitrary.
By Corollary
4.11
, there exists

{\bm{y}}\in\bm{Z}^{\perp}_{h}
such that

b_{h}^{e}({\bm{y}},(q,\mu))=b_{h}^{e}(\bm{u}-{\bm{v}},(q,\mu))\qquad\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h},

and
\|{\bm{y}}\|_{1,h}\leq C{\beta}_{e}^{-1}\|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}
.
We then let
\bm{z}\in\bm{Z}_{h}^{\perp}
satisfy

b_{h}^{e}(\bm{z},(q,\mu))=-b_{h}^{e}(\bm{u},(q,\mu))\qquad\forall(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}.

Then
\bm{w}:={\bm{v}}+{\bm{y}}+\bm{z}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
, and

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}+\|{\bm{y}}\|_{1,h}+\|\bm{z}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C{\beta}_{e}^{-1})\|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}+\|\bm{z}\|_{1,h}.

By Lemmas
5.3
and
5.2
,

\displaystyle\beta_{e}\|\bm{z}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(q,\mu)\in\mathring{Q}_{h}\times\mathring{X}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}^{e}(\bm{u},(q,\mu))}{\|(q,\mu)\|}\leq Ch^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)},

and so, by Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-\bm{w}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+C\|{\bm{v}}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\leq C\big(|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\beta_{e}^{-1})\big(|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+\|\bm{u}-{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}+h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\big)\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}.

Taking
{\bm{v}}
to be the nodal interpolant of
\bm{u}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose that the solution to (
2.1
)
has regularity
(\bm{u},p)\in{\bm{H}}^{3}(\Omega)\cap{\bm{H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)

for some
1\leq s\leq 3
. Furthermore, without loss of generality, assume that
p|_{\Omega_{h}}\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{h})
.
Then,

(5.4a)

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\nu^{-1}\inf_{\mu\in X_{h}}\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big),

(5.4b)

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\inf_{\mu\in X_{h}}\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}+\inf_{q_{h}\in\mathring{Q}_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

(5.4c)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}\|_{-1/2,h}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big)\qquad\forall\mu\in X_{h},

where
\mathring{\mu}:=\mu-\frac{1}{|{\partial}\Omega_{h}|}\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\mu\,ds
.
In particular there holds

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\nu^{-1}h^{s}\|p\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}\big),

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+h^{\min\{2,s\}}\|p\|_{H^{\min\{2,s\}}(\Omega)}\big).

Proof.

Let
\bm{w}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
be arbitrary.
We then have, for all
{\bm{v}}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
and
\mu\in{X}_{h}
,

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}-a_{h}(\bm{w},{\bm{v}})-b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h},\lambda_{h}))

\displaystyle=-\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{w},{\bm{v}})-\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(p-\lambda_{h})\,ds

\displaystyle=-\nu\int_{\Omega_{h}}\Delta\bm{u}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{w},{\bm{v}})-\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(p-\mu)\,ds+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\,ds,

where
\mathring{\mu}=\mu-\frac{1}{|{\partial}\Omega_{h}|}\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\mu\,ds\in\mathring{X}_{h}
.

Therefore by Lemma
5.2
,
the continuity of
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
(cf. (
4.3
)),
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w},{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big)\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w},{\bm{v}})+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\nu(1+\sigma)|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-\bm{w}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big)\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}+\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\,ds.

We then use (
5.2
) and
(
4.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\,ds=\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}\big(({\bm{v}}-S_{h}{\bm{v}})\cdot\bm{n}_{h}\big)(\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\,ds\leq Cc_{\delta}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}\|\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}\|_{-1/2,h}.

Setting
{\bm{v}}=\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}
, applying the coercivity of
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and Theorem
5.4
, we obtain

(5.5)

c_{1}\nu\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\leq C\big(\nu(1+\sigma)h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}+c_{\delta}\|\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}\|_{-1/2,h}\big)

for
\bm{w}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
satisfying (
5.3
).

Next, let
P_{h}\in\mathring{Q}_{h}
be the
L^{2}
-projection of
p
and note that,
due to the definitions of the finite element spaces,
\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})(p-P_{h})\,dx=0

for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
.
This identity, along with the inf-sup stability estimate given in Theorem
4.10
yields

\displaystyle\beta\|(p_{h}-P_{h},\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h}-P_{h},\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}=\sup_{{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h}-p,\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}))}{\|{\bm{v}}\|_{1,h}}.

Using Lemma
5.2
, we write the numerator as

\displaystyle b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h}-p,\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu}))

\displaystyle=b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p_{h},\lambda_{h}))-b_{h}({\bm{v}},(p,\mathring{\mu}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}{\bm{f}}\cdot{\bm{v}}\,dx-a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},{\bm{v}})+\int_{\Omega_{h}}({\rm div}\,{\bm{v}})p\,dx-\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h}){\mu}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},{\bm{v}})-\int_{{\partial}\Omega_{h}}({\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{n}_{h})(\mu-p)\,ds.

By continuity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\beta\|(p_{h}-P_{h},\lambda_{h}-\mathring{\mu})\|\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\nu(1+\sigma)|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\big(\nu h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\nu(1+\sigma)\big(|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|\bm{u}-\bm{w}|\hskip-0.85355pt|\hskip-0.85355pt|_{h}+\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\big)+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\big(\nu(1+\sigma)h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\nu(1+\sigma)\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big).

Inserting this estimate into (
5.5
), we get

(5.7)

\displaystyle\nu\big(c_{1}-C\beta^{-1}c_{2}(1+\sigma)c_{\delta}\big)\|\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}\|_{1,h}\leq C\nu(1+\sigma)h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}.

Using he approximation properties of the discrete kernel once again(cf. Theorem
5.4
), and for
c_{\delta}
sufficiently small,

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\leq C\big(h^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\nu^{-1}\inf_{\mu\in X_{h}}\|p-\mu\|_{-1/2,h}\big).

This establishes the velocity estimate (
5.4a
).

To obtain the estimate for the pressure approximation (
5.4b
), we
use the triangle inequality and the approximation properties of the
L^{2}
-projection:

\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|p_{h}-P_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}+\inf_{q_{h}\in\mathring{Q}_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}.

Inserting (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) into the right-hand side
yields the desired
bound for the pressure. Likewise, combining (
5.6
)
and (
5.7
) yields (
5.4c
).
∎

7.
Concluding Remarks

This paper constructed
a uniformly stable and divergence-free method
for the Stokes problem on unfitted meshes
using a boundary correction approach. While the method
is not pressure-robust, a Lagrange multiplier
enforcing the normal boundary conditions
is included to mitigate the affect of the pressure
contribution in the velocity error. Theoretical results and numerical experiments
show that the method converges with optimal order.

The presentation is confined
to the two dimensional setting, however
many of the results extend to 3D as well.
For example, the proof of inf-sup stability given in Lemma

4.7

applies mutatis mutandis to the
the three-dimensional Scott-Vogelius pair.
On the other hand, inf-sup stability
of the velocity-Lagrange multiplier pairing (cf. Lemma
4.8
),
and its dependence on the geometry of the computational mesh
is less obvious. We plan to address this issue
in the near future.

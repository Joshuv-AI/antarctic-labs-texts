Coarse-graining of a discrete model for edge dislocations in the regular triangular lattice Thanks: August 8, 2026
R. Alicandro, L. De Luca, G. Lazzaroni, M. Palombaro, M. Ponsiglione

Abstract
. We consider a discrete model of planar elasticity where the particles, in the reference configuration, sit on a regular triangular lattice and interact through nearest neighbor pairwise potentials, with bonds modeled as linearized elastic springs. Within this framework we introduce plastic slip fields, whose discrete circulation around each triangle detects the possible presence of an edge dislocation. We provide a Γ \Gamma -convergence analysis, as the lattice spacing tends to zero, of the elastic energy induced by edge dislocations in the energy regime corresponding to a finite number of geometrically necessary dislocations. Keywords: Dislocations, Topological singularities, Plasticity, Discrete to continuum limits, Gamma-convergence. MSC2020: 74C05, 58K45, 70G75, 74G65, 49J45.



Introduction

Dislocations are line defects in the periodic structure of crystals and are considered the main microscopic mechanism of plastic flow.
Idealized straight dislocations are classified into two types,
edge
and
screw
, while in real crystals dislocations are actually curved lines of mixed type
[
12
,
11
]
.
From a mathematical viewpoint, dislocations can be seen as
topological line singularities around which the elastic strain
has non trivial circulation given by a vector of the underlying lattice and referred to as
Burgers vector
.

Here we focus on planar elasticity, where the relevant dislocations are of edge nature and can be seen as
topological point singularities of the elastic strain.
Specifically, we study the discrete elastic energy induced by a finite system of edge dislocations in a finite portion
\Omega\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
of the regular triangular lattice
\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
, where
\varepsilon
is the lattice spacing.

As customary in the linearized framework, we adopt the additive decomposition of the
discrete deformation gradient

\mathrm{d}u
, defined on pairs of nearest neighbors, into an elastic and a plastic part.
The latter is represented by an additional variable
\sigma
, referred to as
slip
, defined on pairs of nearest neighbors and taking values in the set of lattice vectors. In this way we identify dislocations as points around which the discrete circulation of the plastic slip
\sigma
, or, equivalently, the discrete circulation of
\mathrm{d}u-\sigma
, is non trivial. Such a procedure agrees with the formalism of the eigenstrains considered in
[
2
]
.

We focus on the simple case where
nearest neighbors interact through linearized pairwise potentials;
specifically, in absence of singularities, the energy functional is defined by

G_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i,j\in\Omega\cap\ep\Tl}{|i-j|=\ep}}\big(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)\cdot(j-i)\big)^{2}\,.

The above energy can be formally derived by linearization of suitable nonlinear frame invariant functionals (see Remark
1.2
); further rigorous linearization results in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence are provided in

[
1
,
4
,
16
]
. In the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0

the energy
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}G_{\varepsilon}
approximates,
up to a pre-factor,
the continuum isotropic elastic energy with Lamé parameters both equal to
1
(see Remark
3.1
).

In presence of a plastic slip field
\sigma
, the energy functional should depend only on the elastic strain
\mathrm{d}u-\sigma
, and hence the resulting elastic energy reads as

F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i,j\in\Omega\cap\ep\Tl}{|i-j|=\ep}}\big((\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)\big)^{2}\,.

It is well known that the energy induced by an isolated edge dislocation, both in the discrete and in the continuum setting, is of order
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, while short dipoles of opposite dislocations induce a much smaller energy of order
\varepsilon^{2}
.
The aim of the present paper is to determine the asymptotic behavior (as
\varepsilon\to 0
) of
F_{\varepsilon}
in the energy regime
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
.
This corresponds to a finite distribution of
geometrically necessary edge dislocations
, i.e., to the superposition of a finite number of isolated dislocations plus
clusters of singularities with total Burgers vector equal to zero (usually referred to as
statistically stored dislocations
).

Our results are obtained within the rigorous formalism of
\Gamma
-convergence and consist in a compactness property for the dislocation measures and in the derivation of the effective limit energy induced by dislocations.
Specifically,
our analysis shows that the discrete dislocation density generated by the plastic slip
\sigma
converges, in the sense of flat convergence (
1.7
), to a finite sum of Dirac deltas
\sum_{k=1}^{K}b^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}

with
b^{k}\in\mathbb{T}
. The effective energy (namely the
\Gamma
-limit) “counts” the limiting singularities with a coefficient
\varphi(b^{k})
given by the explicit formula (
2.13
). These results rely on a similar analysis performed
in
[
8
,
6
]
in a
semi-discrete
setting within the so-called core radius approach.

We restrict our analysis to configurations of plastic slips inducing dislocations with minimal mutual distance larger than or equal to
\sqrt{3}\varepsilon
. Roughly speaking, this means that
two neighboring triangles cannot both contain a dislocation.
Such a
mild separation assumption (
MS
) guarantees that each dislocation induces a core energy of order
\varepsilon^{2}
; the latter estimate is essential also in the core radius approach, and represents the starting point in the so-called ball construction technique
[
15
]
,
which is devised to provide sharp lower bounds.
This is not a mere technical assumption; indeed, removing it, one can exhibit unphysical configurations with zero stored elastic energy where all triangles contain a dislocation (see Remark
3.5
).
In this respect, it seems that our linearized model (without assuming (
MS
)) fails to describe the core energy stored in a single triangle and induced by the presence of a dislocation. On the other hand, under the mild separation assumption (
MS
), each dislocation is surrounded by an annulus of
“elastically deformed”
triangles where a finite amount of energy is stored, as it follows from an application of Korn’s inequality.

Although our model provides a good description of “non-pathological” dislocation configurations, it exhibits some degeneracies, due to the discrete linearized framework relying on a reference configuration, as well as to the presence of the slip variable (see Section
4
).
A natural way to rule out such degeneracies could be to include kinematic constraints on the slip fields mimicking (in a discrete framework) pure shear/deviatoric stress conditions that are typically assumed in (continuum) elasto-plasticity. In this respect, in Section
4
, we discuss a possible constraint on the slip fields, based on the (formal) linearization of the volume preserving condition on dislocation-free
\varepsilon
-triangles; we remark that our
\Gamma
-convergence analysis still holds true under such a constraint.

Finally, some comments are in order. First, we do believe that a similar discrete-to-continuum analysis could be developed in the
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}
regime, which corresponds to a limit diffused distribution of dislocations and to an effective energy accounting for both elastic and plastic effects (see
[
9
]
for an analogous result within the core-radius approach). Second, it would be interesting to look at the coarse-graining for the energy
F_{\varepsilon}
in even higher regimes, such as that of the grain boundaries (see the recent papers
[
10
,
13
,
7
]
). Third,
a challenging issue would be
to deal directly with nonlinear models built on the deformed configurations. An intriguing intermediate attempt in this direction could be to consider
hybrid models

combining the mathematical efficiency of linearized theories together with the mechanical understanding of dislocations in terms of interpenetrating pentagon-heptagon pairs usually observed in the deformed configurations.

For every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we denote by
M^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
the transpose matrix of
M
, i.e., such that
M^{*}_{ij}=M_{ji}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=1,\ldots,m
.
In what follows the elements
(x_{1},x_{2})
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
will be also identified (whenever it is convenient) with column vectors
\left(\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\\

.
We denote by
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
e_{1}=(1,0)
and
e_{2}=(0,1)
.
Given two vectors
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\,,\ b=(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we set
a\wedge b:=a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}
;
moreover,
we set
a^{\perp}:=(-a_{2},a_{1})
.

For every open set
U
with
\partial U
smooth and for every
s\in\partial U
, we denote by
\tau(s)
the tangent vector to
\partial U
at
s
defined as
\tau(s)=n^{\perp}(s)
, where
n(s)
denotes the outer normal unitary vector to
\partial U
at
s
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for every
0<r<R
we define the annulus
A_{r,R}(p):=B_{R}(p)\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(p)
, where, for every
\rho>0
,
B_{\rho}(p)
and
\overline{B}_{\rho}(p)
denote the open and the closed ball centered at
p
with radius
\rho
, respectively.

Moreover, for every bounded open set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for every
\beta\in L^{2}(A;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
the symbol
\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta
denotes the row-by-row distributional curl of
\beta
; formally,

\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta=(\partial_{x_{1}}\beta_{12}-\partial_{x_{2}}\beta_{11},\partial_{x_{1}}\beta_{22}-\partial_{x_{2}}\beta_{21})\,.

Analogously, the symbol
\mathrm{Div}\,\beta
will denote the row-by-row distributional divergence of
\beta
, formally given by

(\mathrm{Div}\,\beta)_{i}=\partial_{x_{1}}\beta_{i1}+\partial_{x_{2}}\beta_{i2}\,,\qquad i\in\{1,2\}\,.

Finally,
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the class of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
valued Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

1.
The model and the main result

In this section we introduce our model and state the main result.

Reference lattice.

We set

\nu:=\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_{2}
and
\eta:=-\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_{2}
.
Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathrm{span}\,_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},\nu\}
and set

T^{+}:=\mathrm{conv}\{0,e_{1},\nu\}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad T^{-}:=\mathrm{conv}\{0,e_{1},-\eta\}\,,

where, for every
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the set
\mathrm{conv}\{a,b,c\}
denotes the convex envelope of the points
a
,
b
,
c
, i.e., the (closed) triangle with vertices at
a
,
b
,
c
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
the family of the triangles
T_{\varepsilon}
of the form
i+\varepsilon T^{\pm}
, with
i\in\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary, representing the domain of definition of the relevant fields in the model.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, we set

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega):=\{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}\,:\,T_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\}

and
we define

\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\bigcup_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}T_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, we set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}:=\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
and we denote by
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
the family of nearest neighbor bonds in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}:=\{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,:\,|i-j|=\varepsilon\}
. Trivially,
(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
if and only if
(j,i)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
.

In the following we will generalize the notation introduced above to general subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(not necessarily open). In particular, for every triangle
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}=\{(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T})\times(T_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T})\,:\,i\neq j\}\,.

For every map
V:(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define the
discrete circulation
of
V
on the “boundary of
T_{\varepsilon}
” as

(1.1)

\mathrm{d}V(T_{\varepsilon}):=V(i,j)+V(j,k)+V(k,i)\,,

where
(i,j,k)
is a triple of counter-clockwise oriented vertices of
T_{\varepsilon}
.

Displacement, plastic slip, dislocation measure.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
the class of displacements
u:\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
; moreover, for every
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
we define the
discrete gradient

\mathrm{d}u:\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
u
as
\mathrm{d}u(i,j):=u(j)-u(i)
for every
(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
.

We define the class of
plastic slips

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
as

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega):=\Big\{\sigma:\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\varepsilon\mathbb{T}\,:\,\sigma(i,j)=-\sigma(j,i)\textrm{ for every }(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\Big\}\,.

For every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
we define the discrete dislocation measure associated to
\sigma
as

\mu[\sigma]:=-\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\sigma(T_{\varepsilon})\delta_{x_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma
is defined in (
1.1
) and
x_{T_{\varepsilon}}
denotes the barycenter of the triangle
T_{\varepsilon}
.
Notice that for every
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
and for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
it holds

\mu[\sigma]=\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}u-\sigma)(T_{\varepsilon})\delta_{x_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\,.

The class of
admissible dislocation measures
, denoted by
X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
, is the family of measures of the form
\mu=\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}b(T_{\varepsilon})\delta_{x_{T_{\varepsilon}}}
with
b(T_{\varepsilon})\in\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
and satisfying the following
mild separation
property:

(MS)

for every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with
\mu(T_{\varepsilon})\neq 0
, we have

\partial T_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\emptyset
and
\mu(T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})=0
for every
T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with
\partial T^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial T_{\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
.

Finally,
we set

X(\Omega):=\Big\{\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}b^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\,:\,K\in\mathbb{N}\,,\,b^{k}\in\mathbb{T}\,,\,x^{k}\in\Omega\Big\}\,.

The energy functional and the main result.

We are now in a position to define the energy functionals
F_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
as

(1.2)

F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}}[(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)]^{2}\,,

where
\cdot
denotes the standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We will consider also localized versions of the functional
F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma)
in (
1.2
). More specifically, for every set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;A):\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(A)\times\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(A)\to[0,+\infty)
as

(1.3)

F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;A):=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{(i,j)\in A_{\varepsilon}^{1}}[(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)]^{2}\,,

so that
F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;\Omega)=F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma)
.

Since in our analysis the relevant parameter is the dislocation measure
\mu
associated to
\sigma
, we let the energy functionals depend only on (the admissible measures)
\mu
, by defining
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}:X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
as

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(u,\sigma)\in\AD_\ep(\Omega)\times\AS_{\ep}(\Omega)}{\mu[\sigma]=\mu}}F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma)\,.

For every
b\in\mathbb{T}
, we set

(1.4)

\varphi(b):=\frac{1}{3\pi}\min\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{3}|z_{i}|\,:\,z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad b=z_{1}e_{1}+z_{2}\nu+z_{3}\eta\bigg\}\,.

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness) Let
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
. If
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, then, up to a subsequence,
\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{flat}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) for some
\mu\in X(\Omega)
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) For every
\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}b^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in X(\Omega)
and for every
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
and such that
\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{flat}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) it holds

(1.5)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi(b^{k})\,.

(iii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) For every
\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}b^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in X(\Omega)
there exists
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{flat}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) and

(1.6)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi(b^{k})\,.

The convergence appearing in Theorem
1.1
is the
flat convergence
, that is the convergence with respect to the
flat norm
, defined by

(1.7)

\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat}}:=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\phi\in C^{0,1}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)}{\|\phi\|_{C^{0,1}}\le 1}}\Big|\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big|\,,\qquad\textrm{for every }\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\,,

where
C^{0,1}(\Omega)
is the space of Lipschitz continuous functions endowed with the norm

\|\phi\|_{C^{0,1}}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\phi(x)|+\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{x,y\in\Omega}{x\neq y}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{|x-y|}\,,

and
C^{0,1}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
is the subspace of
C^{0,1}
functions compactly supported in
\Omega
.

Remark 1.2
.

We illustrate how to formally derive (
1.2
) from a nonlinear frame invariant model with nearest neighbor pairwise interaction potentials, in the case where no dislocation is present, i.e.,
\sigma\equiv 0
.
We assume that, given a deformation
v\colon\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the total interaction energy is

E_{\varepsilon}(v)=\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\psi\Big(\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}v(i,j)}{\varepsilon}\Big|\Big)\,,

where
\psi
is a
C^{2}
function such that
\psi(1)=\psi^{\prime}(1)=0
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)>0
, so that the identity is an equilibrium configuration. Expressing the energy in terms of the displacement

u(x)=\frac{v(x)-x}{\delta}
, with
\delta>0
, and rescaling by
\delta^{2}
, the total
interaction energy reads as

E^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x+\delta u)=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\psi\Big(\Big|\frac{j-i}{\varepsilon}+\delta\,\frac{\mathrm{d}u(i,j)}{\varepsilon}\Big|\Big)\,.

A second order Taylor expansion of
E^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x+\delta u)
with respect to
\delta
about
the point
\delta=0
gives

E_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(x+\delta u)=F_{\varepsilon}(u)+\mathrm{o}_{\delta}(1)\,,

where

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}}[\mathrm{d}u(i,j)\cdot(j-i)]^{2}\,,

which coincides with (
1.2
) up to a multiplicative constant when
\sigma\equiv 0
.
We refer to
[
10
]
for a formal derivation of (
1.2
) starting from a nonlinear model with discrete plastic slips.

2.
The continuum case

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
we will use the corresponding result in the continuum setting
[
6
]
, that we briefly recall here.
Let
A
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Lipschitz continuous boundary. For every
\mu\in X(A)
we set

(2.1)

A_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=A\setminus\bigcup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,(\mu)}\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)

and we define the set
\mathscr{A}\!\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
of admissible strains associated to
\mu
as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}\!\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=

\displaystyle\Big\{\beta\in L^{2}(A_{\varepsilon}(\mu);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\,:\,\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta=0\,\textrm{ in }A_{\varepsilon}(\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial U}\beta\,\tau\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\mu(U)\textrm{ for every open set }U\subset A\textrm{ with }\partial U\subset A_{\varepsilon}(\mu)\textrm{ smooth}\Big\}\,.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be an elasticity tensor, i.e., a linear operator from
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
into itself satisfying the following property: There exist two constants
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
such that

c_{1}|\beta^{\mathrm{sym}}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta\leq c_{2}|\beta^{\mathrm{sym}}|^{2}\qquad\textrm{for every }\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\,,

where
\beta^{\mathrm{sym}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\beta+\beta^{*})
.

The elastic energy of a field
\beta\in\mathscr{A}\!\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
in the body
A
is given by

E_{\varepsilon}(\beta;A_{\varepsilon}(\mu)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{\varepsilon}(\mu)}\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

and the energy induced by the dislocation distribution
\mu
in the body
A

is defined by

(2.3)

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mu;A):=\inf_{\beta\in\mathscr{A}\!\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)}E_{\varepsilon}(\beta;A_{\varepsilon}(\mu))+|\mu|(A)\,,\qquad\textrm{for every }\mu\in X(A)\,.

In (
2.3
) , the first addendum on the right hand side is the elastic energy induced by the dislocation measure
\mu
, whereas the second addendum plays the role of a plastic core energy.

In order to introduce the self-energy of an edge dislocation, for every
b\in\mathbb{T}
we first define the strain field
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{b,\mathbb{C}}
satisfying the circulation condition

\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta=b\delta_{0}\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}

and the equilibrium equation

\mathrm{Div}\,\mathbb{C}\beta=0\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\,.

As shown in
[
6
]
, the strain field
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{b,\mathbb{C}}
is given, in polar coordinates, by

(2.4)

\beta^{b,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\rho,\theta):=\frac{1}{\rho}\big(f^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)\otimes(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)+g^{b,\mathbb{C}}\otimes(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\big)\,,

where the constant
g^{b,\mathbb{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the function
f^{b,\mathbb{C}}\in C^{0}([0,2\pi];\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with
f(0)=f(2\pi)
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}f^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega=b
, are uniquely determined by the vector
b
and the tensor
\mathbb{C}
.

The corresponding displacement
u^{b,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
(i.e., such that
\nabla u^{b,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=\beta^{b,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
)
is computed explicitly in the literature (see, for instance,
[
3
, formula 4.1.25]
) and, in polar coordinates,
is given by

(2.5)

u^{b,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\rho,\theta)=F^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)+g^{b,\mathbb{C}}\log\rho\,,

where
F^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)=\int_{0}^{\theta}f^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega
, for
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.
Note that the displacement above is uniquely determined up to a constant.

For every
b\in\mathbb{T}
we set

(2.6)

where we have set

for every

.

[

, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness)

(

there exists a sequence of measures
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset X(A)
such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{flat}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu
(as

The proof of Theorem

can be obtained by arguing verbatim as in the proof of
[

First, in

the admissible strains

Such a condition can be always enforced in view of the invariance of the elastic energy with respect to translations.
Second, in
[
6
, Theorem 4]
the compactness property is stated enforcing (

) only for the optimal
\beta_{\varepsilon}
.
Third, in
[

Now, we specialize the functions

and

More specifically, let

be the isotropic elasticity tensor with Lamé parameters both equal to

(2.11)

\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta:=|\mathrm{tr}\,\beta|^{2}+2|\beta^{\mathrm{sym}}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6\pi}b^{\perp}\,,

\displaystyle f^{b,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)=

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
. Let moreover
u:T_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\sigma:(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
be such that
\sigma(i,j)=-\sigma(j,i)
for every
(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}
.
If
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
satisfies

\beta\,(j-i)=\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j)\qquad\textrm{for every }(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1},

then, by straightforward computations, we have that

(3.1)

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;T_{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Big(|e_{1}^{*}\,\beta\,e_{1}|^{2}+|\nu^{*}\,\beta\,\nu|^{2}+|\eta^{*}\,\beta\,\eta|^{2}\Big)=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{3}{8}\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int_{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

where
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;T_{\varepsilon})
is defined in (
1.3
) and
\mathbb{C}
is given in (
2.11
).

In the next lemma we construct, far from the singularities, a (continuous) strain field
\beta
that is compatible with a given distribution of dislocations and whose (continuous) energy behaves like the discrete energy
F_{\varepsilon}
. Such an estimate, together with a bound on the total variation,
will allow us to deduce the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality in Theorem
1.1
(ii) directly from the analogous statement in the continuous setting (Theorem
2.1
(ii)).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
(u,\sigma)\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
.
Let moreover

(3.2)

\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}:=\{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\,:\,\mu[\sigma](T_{\varepsilon})=0\}\,,\qquad K_{\varepsilon}:=\bigcup_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}T_{\varepsilon}\,.

Then, there exists a piecewise constant (namely, constant on each triangle
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
) field
\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\in L^{2}(K_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
such that:

(a)

\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j)=\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\,(j-i)
for every
i,j\in T_{\varepsilon}
with
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
;

(b)

F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;K_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int_{K_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}:\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j)\in K^1_\ep}{i,j\in\partial K_\ep}}|(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)|^{2}
,
where
F_{\varepsilon}
is defined in (
1.3
) and
\mathbb{C}
is defined in (
2.11
) ;

(c)

\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}=0
in
K_{\varepsilon}
and
\int_{\partial U}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\tau\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\mu[\sigma](U)
for every smooth open set
U\subset\Omega
such that
\partial U\subset K_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Let
T_{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{conv}\{i,j,k\}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
and assume that the triple
(i,j,k)

is counterclockwise oriented. Let
v_{T_{\varepsilon}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(T_{\varepsilon})
be defined by

v_{T_{\varepsilon}}(i)=0\,,\quad v_{T_{\varepsilon}}(j)=\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j)\,,\quad v_{T_{\varepsilon}}(k)=v_{T_{\varepsilon}}(j)+\mathrm{d}u(j,k)-\sigma(j,k)\,,

and notice that the discrete gradient
\mathrm{d}v_{T_{\varepsilon}}
of
v_{T_{\varepsilon}}
agrees with
\mathrm{d}u-\sigma
.
For every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
let
\tilde{v}_{T_{\varepsilon}}:T_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the piecewise affine interpolation of
v_{T_{\varepsilon}}
and let
\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}:K_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be the map defined by

\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}:=\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{D}\tilde{v}_{T_{\varepsilon}}\,\chi_{T_{\varepsilon}}\,,

where
\mathrm{D}\tilde{v}_{T_{\varepsilon}}
denotes the (continuous) gradient of the function
\tilde{v}_{T_{\varepsilon}}
.
Clearly
\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\in L^{2}(K_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.
Moreover, by construction, we have that property (a) is satisfied; furthermore, since for every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
and for every
(i,j)\in T_{\varepsilon}^{1}
it holds

\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\,(j-i)=\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j)\,,

by Remark
3.1
we get

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;K_{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}F_{\varepsilon}(u,\sigma;T_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j)\in K^1_\ep}{i,j\in\partial K_\ep}}|(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int_{K_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}:\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j)\in K^1_\ep}{i,j\in\partial K_\ep}}|(\mathrm{d}u(i,j)-\sigma(i,j))\cdot(j-i)|^{2}\,,

i.e., property (b).

Finally, we prove that also (c) is satisfied. To this end, we notice that if
T_{\varepsilon}^{1}
and
T_{\varepsilon}^{2}
are two triangles sharing one edge
(i,j)
, then, by construction,
\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{T_{\varepsilon}^{1}}}\,(j-i)=\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}}\,(j-i)
so that
\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}=0
on
K_{\varepsilon}
.
Now, since for every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
,

\int_{\partial T_{\varepsilon}}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\tau\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=0\,,

by using Stokes’ Theorem, for every smooth open set
U\subset\Omega
such that
\partial U\subset K_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\int_{\partial U}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\tau\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\int_{U\cap\partial K_{\varepsilon}}\beta^{u,\sigma,\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}}\,\tau\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=-\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\subset U}\mathrm{d}\sigma(T_{\varepsilon})=\mu[\sigma](U)\,,

which concludes the proof of property (c) and of the whole lemma.
∎

4.
Degeneracies of the model and possible kinematic constraints

We highlight that the functional (
1.2
) exhibits some degeneracies that are typical of linearized energies as well as of discrete models built on a reference configuration; the presence of the plastic slip field
\sigma
actually makes the system even less rigid and produces even more unphysical configurations.

As an example, let
\Omega:=[-1,1]^{2}
and define
u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
as

(4.1)

u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\varepsilon\nu&\textrm{if }i_{2}\leq 0\\
0&\textrm{elsewhere in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

Notice that
u_{\varepsilon}^{-}
can be interpreted as a microscopic crack, whereas
u_{\varepsilon}^{+}
can be seen as an unphysical crack which produces interpenetration of matter
generating the superposition of two lines of atoms.
It is easy to check that there exist
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
such that
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm},\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})=0
.
Moreover, up to slightly modifying
u_{\varepsilon}^{-}
, one can construct a function
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
such that the crack does not disconnect
\Omega
and has two ending points which in our model are classified as dislocations with opposite Burgers vector. In particular, what in our model is identified as a plastic slip,
in some cases should be rather understood as a microscopic crack opening.

Furthermore, we observe that the energy in (
1.2
) is invariant with respect to integer dilations of the lattice. In order to see this, it is enough to fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
and to take the displacement
u_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}
and the slip
\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}
defined by

(4.2)

u_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(i):=\lambda\varepsilon i\,\qquad\qquad\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(i,j):=\varepsilon\lambda(j{-}i)\,.

Trivially,
\mathrm{d}u_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(i,j)\equiv\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(i,j)
and hence
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda},\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda})\equiv 0
.

In view of the degeneracies pointed out above, one may ask whether additional suitable kinematic constraints on the plastic
slips could prevent such unphysical behaviors.
In the present framework, it seems natural to incorporate linearized constraints on the plastic slips, mimicking deviatoric/pure shear volume preserving conditions on each
\varepsilon
-triangle
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
.

Let us discuss now a possible way to introduce such a kinematic constraint in our model.
In order to
(formally) linearize the nonlinear volume preserving constraint, consider
deformation gradients
\mathrm{Id}+\delta z
close to the identity, where
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
represents a plastic deformation gradient,
\delta>0
is a small parameter with respect to which the linearization is performed and
\mathrm{Id}
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.
Enforcing that the deformed configuration
(\mathrm{Id}+\delta z)(T_{\varepsilon})
has the same (oriented) area of
T_{\varepsilon}
we obtain

[(k{-}i)+\delta\,z(k{-}i)]\wedge[(j{-}i)+\delta\,z(j{-}i)]=(k{-}i)\wedge(j{-}i)\,,

for every triple
(i,j,k)
of counterclockwise oriented vertices of
T_{\varepsilon}
.
Neglecting lower order terms, this yields

(4.3)

z(k{-}i)\wedge(j{-}i)-z(j{-}i)\wedge(k{-}i)=0\,,

for every triple
(i,j,k)
as above.
Summing (
4.3
) over two triples of the type
(i,j,k)
,
(j,k,i)
, one can see that condition (
4.3
) is equivalent to the well known trace free constraint on
z
;
we refer the interested reader to
[
14
]
for a rigorous derivation via
\Gamma
-convergence of such a kind of trace free constraints starting from continuous nonlinear models in plasticity, relying on multiplicative rather than additive decompositions of the deformation gradient.

We now rewrite (
4.3
) in terms of the (linear) slip field
\sigma
, which is, by construction, given by

(4.4)

\sigma(i,j)=z(j{-}i)\qquad\qquad\textrm{for every bond }(i,j)\textrm{ of the triangle }T_{\varepsilon}\,.

Condition (
4.3
) becomes

(4.5)

\sigma(i,k)\wedge(j{-}i)-\sigma(i,j)\wedge(k{-}i)=0\,,

for every triple
(i,j,k)
of counterclockwise oriented vertices of
T_{\varepsilon}
.
As a consequence of (
4.4
), we have that
\mu[\sigma](T_{\varepsilon})=0
, and hence (
4.5
) is justified by the reasoning above only on dislocation-free triangles.
On the other hand, one can easily check that the only condition (
4.5
) (assumed for every triple
(i,j,k)
of counterclockwise oriented vertices of
T_{\varepsilon}
) implies that
\mu[\sigma](T_{\varepsilon})=0
.
Summarizing, in our framework, a reasonable linearization of the volume preserving constraint seems to be provided by condition (
4.5
) assumed on all dislocation-free
\varepsilon
-triangles
T_{\varepsilon}
.

Notice that (
4.5
) rules out the degeneracies pointed out by the examples above. More precisely, one can check that, given a displacement as in (
4.1
) or(
4.2
), the minimum energy in a dislocation-free triangle among all slip fields fulfilling (
4.5
) is strictly positive. On the other hand (
MS
) guarantees that dislocation-free triangles are necessarily present. Finally, our
\Gamma
-convergence result still holds true under the additional constraint (
4.5
),
which is satisfied by the recovery sequence constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii).

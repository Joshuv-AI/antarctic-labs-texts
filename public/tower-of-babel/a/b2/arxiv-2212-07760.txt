Choquard equation involving mixed local and nonlocal operators
G.C. Anthal , J. Giacomoni and K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In this article, we study an elliptic problem involving an operator of mixed order with both local and nonlocal aspects and in the presence of critical nonlinearity of Hartree type. To this end, we first investigate the corresponding Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and detect the optimal constant. Using variational methods and a Pohožaev identity we then show the existence and nonexistence results for the corresponding subcritical perturbation problem. Key words: Local-nonlocal operators, critical Choquard nonlinearity, Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, existence reusults, variational methods, Pohožaev identity. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35A01, 35A15, 35B33, 35B65.

Introduction

The aim of the present article is to study the following problem consisting of combination of local and nonlocal operators along with critical Choquard nonlinearity

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\right)|u|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u+\lambda u^{p}~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u\equiv 0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,~u\geq 0~\text{in}~\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{1,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\lambda
is a real parameter,
p\in[1,2^{*}-1)
,
n\geq 3
,
0<\mu<n
,
2_{\mu}^{\ast}=(2n-\mu)/(n-2)
and
2^{*}=2n/(n-2)
. The mixed operator
\mathcal{L}
in (
1.1
) is given by

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}~\text{for some}~s\in(0,1).

(1.2)

The word ”mixed” refers to the type of the operator combining both local and nonlocal features and to the differential order of the operator. The operator
\mathcal{L}
is obtained by the superposition of the classical Laplacian
(-\Delta)
and the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, which for a fixed parameter
s\in(0,1)
, is defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=C(n,s)P.V.\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy.

The term
"P.V"
stands for the Cauchy’s principal value and
C(n,s)
is a normalizing constant, whose explicit expression is given by

C(n,s)=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-\cos(z_{1})}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz\right)^{-1}.

The study of the mixed operators of the form
\mathcal{L}
in (
1.2
) is motivated by the wide range of applications. Indeed these operators arise naturally in the applied sciences, to study the role of the impact caused by a local and a nonlocal change in a physical phenomenon. These operators model diffusion patterns with different time scales (loosely speaking, the higher order operator leading the diffusion for small scales times and the lower order operator becoming predominant for large times) and they arise for instance in bi-modal power law distribution processes, see
[
27
]
. Further applications arise in the theory of optimal searching, biomathematics and animal forging, see
[
14
,
15
]
and the references therein. See also
[
23
,
24
,
28
]
and the references therein for further applications.

Due to these applications and mathematical interest, the study of elliptic problems involving mixed type of operators having both local and nonlocal features is attracting a lot of attention. The current research has specifically focused on several problems in the existence and regularity theory. In the following, we present a short literature review regarding existence and regularity of solutions of the problem of the type

-\Delta_{q}u+(-\Delta)_{q}^{s}u=f~\text{in}~\Omega,

is some domain,

,
s\in(0,1)
,

and

Laplacian and fractional

In the nonlinear setting

The stirring motivation for the present paper comes from the study of nonlinear problems with critical exponents. These problems are usually modelled as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}u=\mathcal{W}(u)+\lambda\mathcal{Y}(u)\in\Omega,

(1.3)

with some appropriate conditions on

. Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain,
\mathcal{J}
is some operator (local/nonlocal/mixed),
\mathcal{W}
is a critical nonlinearity (notion of criticality changes from problem to problem and relies on the limit of compactness inherited by the problem),
\mathcal{Y}
is subcritical nonlinearity and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter. In these problems mostly we address the issue of existence and multiplicity of the solutions with respect to the parameter
\lambda

1.3

The case of critical Choquard nonlinearity namely
\mathcal{W}=\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\right)|u|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u
with linear and sublinear or superlinear perturbations were studied by Gao and Yang in their works

respectively and obtain the existence and multiplicity of solutions depending on some range of

\lambda

is in accordance with well known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality which states as under:

Proposition 1.1

Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
Let
r,q>1
and
0<\mu<n
with
1/r+1/q+\mu/n=2
,
g\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),h\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, there exist a sharp constant
C(r,q,n,\mu)
independent of
g
and
h
such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(r,q,n,\mu)|g|_{r}|h|_{q}.

(1.5)

In particular, let
g=h=|u|^{t}

then by Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we see that,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)^{t}|u(y)|^{t}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy

is well defined if
|u|^{t}\in L^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\nu=\frac{2n}{2n-\mu}>1
.
Thus, from Sobolev embedding theorems, we must have

\frac{2n-\mu}{n}\leq t\leq\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}.

From this, for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}\leq C(n,\mu)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}|u|_{2^{*}}^{2}.

We denote
S_{H,L,C}
to denote the best constant associated to

\displaystyle S_{H,L,C}=\inf\limits_{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}}.

(1.6)

The case of critical Choquard nonlinearity i.e., when

•

Mixed operator case i.e., when
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{L}
: The critical problems involving operators of mixed type are very little explored. In this regard we can quote
[
5
, Biagi et al]
. Here the authors first investigated the mixed Sobolev inequality and showed that the Sobolev constant in the mixed case is actually equal to the classical Sobolev constant. They then used this information to obtain the existence results both for linear and superlinear subcritical perturbation.

Motivated by the above discussion, in the present work we considered the problem (
1.1
). As far as we know there is no result involving the mixed local and nonlocal operator with a critical nonlocal term as the Choquard nonlinearity. We aim to bridge this gap in the present paper. As we know the study of problems with Choquard nonlinearity is linked to the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, we first investigated the inequality for the mixed operator case. More precisely, we consider a fractional exponent
s\in(0,1)
, an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, not necessarily bounded or connected, and all functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which vanish outside
\Omega
, accounting for a mixed Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality of the type

\displaystyle S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy.

Here above, the constant
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)
is taken to be the largest possible one for which such an inequality holds true. Using (
1.6
) it is easy to see that

S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\geq S_{H,L,C}.

In principle, one may suspect in fact a strict inequality occurs (because, for instance,
S_{H,L,C}
is independent of
\Omega
, as well as of
s
), but this is not the case according to the following result we prove:

Theorem 1.2

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open set. Then we have
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)=S_{H,L,C}
.

Our next result answers the question whether or not the optimal constant
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)
is achieved. It states as under:

Theorem 1.3

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open set. Then the optimal constant
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)
is never achieved.

Finally, we study the existence theory for the problem (
1.1
) with respect to the parameter
\lambda
. Let us briefly recall the main strategy used in
[
18
]
in the case
p=1
.

Due to lack of compactness caused by the critical exponent, an idea borrowed from
[
18
]
consists in proving that the Palais Smale level lies in the range
c\in\left(0,\displaystyle\frac{n+2-\mu}{4n-2\mu}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{n+2-\mu}}\right)
. In order to do so, the crucial step is to show that

S(\lambda):=\inf\left\{\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2}:u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)~\text{and}~\|u\|_{HL}=1\right\}<S_{H,L,C}.

Now, the strict inequality
S(\lambda)<S_{H,L,C}
is obtained in
[
18
]
via the following approach:

First, taking into account that the minimizers in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality are given by
Aubin-Talenti functions, one consider the function

u_{\epsilon}=\frac{\psi}{(\epsilon^{2}+|x|^{2})^{(n-2)/2}},~\epsilon>0.

where
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
.
Then, using
u_{\epsilon}
as a competitor function, one gets (at least for
n\geq 5
) that

\frac{\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}=S_{H,L,C}-d\lambda e^{2}+O(\epsilon^{n-2})~\text{as}~\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

where
d>0
is a suitable constant. From this, choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small, one immediately conclude that
S(\lambda)<S_{H,L,C}
. A similar approach works for
1<p<2^{*}-1
as well, and it has also been used in the nonlocal framework
[
26
]
.

In our mixed setting the situation is quite different. In fact, in trying to repeat the above argument as it obtained in
[
31
]
, one is led to consider the following minimization problem

S_{H,L}(\lambda):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{G}(u)^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2}:u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)~\text{and}~\|u\|_{HL}=1\right\}

and to prove that

S_{H,L}(\lambda)<S_{H,L,C}.

(1.10)

Now since, we know from Theorem
1.2
that
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)=S_{H,L,C}
, in order to prove (
1.10
), it is natural to consider the test function
u_{\epsilon}
defined above. However, the presence of the nonlocal term
[u_{\epsilon}]_{s}^{2}
gives in this case

\frac{\mathcal{G}(u_{\epsilon})^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}=S_{H,L,C}+O(\epsilon^{2-2s})-d\lambda e^{2}+O(\epsilon^{n-2})~\text{as}~\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

and the term
O(\epsilon^{2-2s})
is not negligible when
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
In order to get the existence of solutions we borrowed ideas from
[
5
]
and study the properties of the map
\lambda\mapsto S_{H,L}(\lambda)
to obtain the inequality (
1.10
).

All that being said, in the linear case
p=1
we obtain that the problem (
1.1
) does not admit solution both in the range of ”small” and ”large” values of
\lambda

Theorem 1.11

Let

be an open and bounded set and

\lambda^{*}\in[\lambda_{1,s},\lambda_{1})
such that the problem (

Moreover the following nonexistence results also hold:

there do no exist solutions to problem (

2.

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|u\|_{HL}\leq S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n-2}{4-\mu}}\}.

Regarding the case of superlinear subcritical perturbation, we can follow the variational argument in

2
Preliminaries

In this section we shall give the functional settings required to study the problem (
1.1
) and also collect the notations and preliminary results required in the rest of the paper.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For a measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define

[u]_{s}=\left(\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

the so-called Gagliardo seminorm of
u
of order
s
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary non-empty open set, not necessarily bounded. We define the space
\Pi(\Omega)
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the global norm

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(u):=\left(\|u\|^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where we denote
\|u\|^{2}=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}
.

Remark 2.1

A few remarks are in order:

1.

The norm
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
is induced by the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla vdx+\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy,

where
\cdot
denotes the usual scalar product in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\Pi(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space.

2.

Note that in the definition of
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
the
L^{2}
-norm of
\nabla u
is considered on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in spite of
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(identically vanishes outside
\Omega
). This is to point out that the elements in
\Pi(\Omega)
are functions defined on the entire space and not only on
\Omega
. The benefit of having this global functional setting is that these functions can be globally approximated on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the norm
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
by smooth functions with support in
\Omega
.

In particular, when
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will see that this global definition of
\mathbb{G}(\cdot)
implies that the functions in
\Pi(\Omega)
naturally satisfy the nonlocal Dirichlet condition prescribed in problem (
1.1
), that is,

u\equiv 0~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega~\text{for every}~u\in\Pi(\Omega).

(2.1)

In order to verify (
2.1
), we distinguish between two cases.

Case A
: When
\Omega
is bounded. In this case, we know (see
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
)
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(with
s\in(0,1)
)
i.e.
there exists a constant
k=k(s)>0
such that, for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
one has

[u]_{s}^{2}\leq k(s)\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=k(s)(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}).

(2.2)

This, together with the classical Poincaré inequality, implies that
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
and the full
H^{1}-norm
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are actually equivalent in the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, and hence

\displaystyle\Pi(\Omega)=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\arrowvert_{\Omega}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)~\text{and}~u\equiv 0~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

Case B
: When
\Omega
is unbounded. In this case, even if the embedding inequality (
2.2
) holds, the Poincaré inequality does not hold in general. Hence, the norm
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
is no more equivalent to the full norm
H^{1}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\Pi(\Omega)
is not a subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

On the other hand, by the classical Sobolev inequality we infer the existence of a constant
S>0
, independent of the set
\Omega
, such that

S\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq\mathcal{G}(\cdot)^{2}~\text{for every}~u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

(2.3)

where
2^{*}=2n/(n-2)
is the critical Sobolev exponent. Now (
2.3
) implies that every Cauchy sequence in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\mathcal{G}(\cdot)
is also a Cauchy sequence in the space
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As a consequence, since the functions in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
identically vanish out of
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\equiv 0~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,~\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})~\text{and}~[u]_{s}<\infty\}.

3
Mixed Hardy-Littlewood- Sobolev inequality

Here we are interested to find the sharp constant in the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality defined by

\displaystyle S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)=\inf\limits_{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathcal{G}(u)^{2}}{\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}}.

(3.1)

Recall that for any
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the best constant in the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality is given as

\displaystyle S_{H,L,C}(\Omega)=\inf\limits_{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}}.

(3.2)

Let us first recall properties of
S_{H,L,C}(\Omega)
. For a proof of these properties we refer to
[
18
]

Remark 3.1

1.

For
N\geq 3
and for every open set
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
S_{H,L,C}(\Omega)=S_{H,L,C}
.

2.

S_{H,L,C}(\Omega)
is never achieved except when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.

If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
S_{H,L,C}
is achieved by the family of functions

\mathcal{A}=\{V_{t,x_{0}}=t^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\mathcal{U}((x-x_{0})/t):t>0,~x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

where
\mathcal{U}(y);=c(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{2-n}{2}}
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
From the definition of
\mathcal{G}(u)
, the inequality
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\geq S_{H,L,C}
is clear. Next we prove the reverse inequality. First note that
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)
is translation invariant and so we can assume without loss of generality that
0\in\Omega
. Now let
r>0
be such that
B_{r}(0)\subseteq\Omega
. Then for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, there exists
k_{0}(u)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\text{supp}(u)\subseteq B_{kr}(0),~\text{for any}~k\geq k_{0}.

Now setting
u_{k}:=k^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u(kx)
, for
k\geq k_{0}
we see that

\displaystyle\text{supp}(u_{k})\subseteq B_{r}(0)\subseteq\Omega.

Now by definition of
S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)
we have for every
k\geq k_{0}
that

\displaystyle S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\mathcal{G}(u_{k})^{2}}{\|u_{k}\|_{HL}^{2}}=\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}+k^{2s-2}\frac{[u]_{s}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}.

Let
k\rightarrow\infty
and using

, we obtain

S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}.

By the arbitrariness of

and the fact that
S_{H,L,C}
is independent of the set
\Omega
, we finally infer that

S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)\leq S_{H,L,C}.

Hence we have proved the required equality.
\square

We are now in the position of proving Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:
Suppose on the contrary that there exists a nonzero function
v\in\Pi(\Omega)
such that
\|v\|_{HL}=1
and

\mathcal{G}(v)=S_{H,L,M}(\Omega)=S_{H,L,C}.

Noting that
\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we infer that

\displaystyle S_{H,L,C}\leq\|v\|^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}+[v]^{2}=S_{H,L,C},

which implies that

. As a consequence, the function
u_{0}
must be constant in
\Omega
, which contradicts the fact that

.
\square

Remark 3.2

Even if Theorem

show that the constant

Hence,

for every

and

\displaystyle\nu\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}|\psi(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}-2}\psi(x)\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy+\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}\psi\varphi~\text{for all}~\varphi\in\Pi(\Omega).

(4.31)

Using
\psi
as test function in (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\nu=\nu\|\psi\|_{HL}^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}=\mathcal{G}(\psi)^{2}-\lambda\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(\psi)=S_{H,L}(\lambda)>0.

As a consequence, setting
u=S_{H,L}(\lambda)^{\frac{(n-2)}{2n+4-2\mu}}\psi
, we see that
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and for every
\varphi\in\Pi(\Omega)
using (
4.2
) we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}

\displaystyle dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}}|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{*}-2}u(x)\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy+\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}u\varphi.

This completes the proof.
\square

As we see in Lemma
4.4
, sign of the real number
S_{H,L}(\lambda)
plays an important role to study the solvability of problem (
1.1
). The following result combined with Remark
4.5
provide further informations in this regard:

Lemma 4.6

For every
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{1,s}
, we have

S_{H,L}(\lambda)=S_{H,L,C}>0.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1,s})
be given. Using Remark
4.5
, we already know that
S_{H,L}(\lambda)\leq S_{H,L,C}
. Now to prove the reverse inequality, using the definition of
\lambda_{1,s}
in (
4.28
), for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(u)=\|u\|^{2}+([u]_{s}^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2})\geq\|u\|^{2}+(\lambda_{1,s}-\lambda)|u|_{2}^{2}\geq\|u\|^{2}.

As a consequence, since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
\Pi(\Omega)
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{H,L}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\inf\{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(u):u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}(\Omega)\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf\{\|u\|^{2}:u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{H}(\Omega)\}=S_{H,L,C}.

Thus we get the desired result.
\square

By combining Lemma
4.6
and Remark
4.5
we have the following

1.

S_{H,L}(\lambda)=S_{H,L,C}
for every
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{1,s}
;

2.

S_{H,L}(\lambda)\geq 0
for every
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
;

3.

S_{H,L}(\lambda)<0
for every
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
.

Lemma 4.7

The mapping
\lambda\mapsto S_{H,L}(\lambda)
is continuous from left on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

To prove the left continuity, let
\lambda_{0}>0
and
\epsilon>0
be given. By definition of
S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})
there exists
v=v_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}}\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})\leq\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(v)<S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})+\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

From this, using the monotonicity of
S_{H,L}(\cdot)
, for every
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle 0<S_{H,L}(\lambda)-S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(v)-S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})=(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}(v)-S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0}))+(\lambda_{0}-\lambda)|v|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}+(\lambda_{0}-\lambda)|v|_{2}^{2}.

As a consequence, setting
\delta_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon/(2|v|_{2}^{2})
, we conclude that

0<S_{H,L}(\lambda)-S_{H,L}(\lambda_{0})<\epsilon~\text{for every}~\lambda_{0}-\delta_{\epsilon}<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}.

This proves that
S_{H,L}(\cdot)
is continuous from the left at
\lambda_{0}
.
\square

In the light of results obtained so far, we can prove Theorem
1.11
.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
:
To begin with, we define

\lambda^{*}:=\sup\{\lambda>0:S_{H,L}(\mu)=S_{H,L,C}~\text{for all}~0<\mu<\lambda\}.

On account of Lemma
4.6
, we see that
\lambda_{1,s}\leq\lambda^{*}
. Again since
S_{H,L}(\lambda_{1})=0
(see Remark
4.3
) and using Lemma
4.7
, we conclude that
\lambda^{*}\in[\lambda_{1,s},\lambda_{1})
. We treat the following three cases separately.

Case 1
:
0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{1,s}
. Arguing by contradiction, let us suppose there exists a solution to problem (
1.1
) such that
u\in\mathcal{B}
. Now set
w=\displaystyle\frac{u}{\|u\|_{HL}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(w)=\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}_{HL}}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}_{HL}}(\mathcal{G}(u)^{2}-\lambda|u|_{2}^{2})=\|u\|_{HL}^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}\leq S_{H,L,C}.

As a consequence of Lemma
4.6
, we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(w)\leq S_{H,L,C}=S_{H,L}(\lambda).

This implies that
S_{H,L}(\lambda)
is achieved at
w
. Now using
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1,s}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{H,L,C}\leq\|w\|^{2}=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(w)-([w]_{s}^{2}-\lambda|w|_{2}^{2})\leq\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(w)=S_{H,L,C}

which shows that
S_{H,L,C}
is achieved.

On the other hand, since
\Omega
is a bounded domain we know that
S_{H,L,C}
is never achieved and so we have a contradiction.

Case 2
:
\lambda*<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
. Then owing to the definition of
\lambda^{*}
, we can find
0<\mu<\lambda
such that
S_{H,L}(\mu)<S_{H,L,C}
and so from Remark
4.5
-
(ii)

S_{H,L}(\lambda)<S_{H,L,C}
. Then noting that Brezis Lieb Lemma type hold for
\|\cdot\|_{HL}
(see
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
) and following proof of
[
11
, Lemma 1.2]
we see that
S_{H,L}(\lambda)
is achieved.
Next we show that
S_{H,L}(\lambda)>0
. Now after renormalization of
u
, we have
S_{H,L}(\lambda)=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(u)
. In particular, since
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, we obtain

S_{H,L}(\lambda)=|u|_{2}^{2}\left[\frac{\mathcal{G}(u)^{2}}{|u|_{2}^{2}}-\lambda\right]\geq|u|_{2}^{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)>0.

Now we have proved that
S_{H,L}(\lambda)
is strictly positive and is achieved, we are then entitled to apply Lemma
4.4
, ensuring there exists a solution to (
1.1
).

Case 3
:
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
. Arguing by contradiction, let us assume that there exists a solution to (
1.1
). Then by Maximum principle (see Proposition
1.6
), we have
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega
. By Remark
4.3
we know there exists
w_{0}\in\Pi(\Omega)
such that
w_{0}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
and

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla w_{0}\nabla\varphi dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}w_{0}\varphi dx~\text{for all}~\varphi\in\Pi(\Omega).

Taking
\varphi=u
above, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}w_{0}u=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla w_{0}\nabla\varphi dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|w_{0}(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|w_{0}(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}w_{0}(x)u(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dydx+\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}uw_{0}dx

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}uw_{0}dx

but this is a contradiction to
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
. This concludes the proof.
\square

4.3
The superlinear case

In this subsection we study the case
1<p<2^{*}-1
and prove Theorem
1.12
. We will employ Mountain Pass Theorem to obtain the existence of a solution. As in the purely local case (see
[
17
]
) the main difficulty to apply Mountain Pass Theorem consists in proving the validity of a
(PS)_{c}
condition at a level
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Precisely, we have to prove that the Palais Smale condition holds for any
c
strictly below the Mountain Pass first critical level (given in Lemma (
4.10
)).

In the next lemma, we show that
J_{\lambda}
possesses the Mountain Pass geometry.

Lemma 4.8

If
1<p<2^{*}-1
and
\lambda>0
, then the functional
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the following properties:

1.

There exists
\alpha
,
\sigma>0
such that
J_{\lambda}(u)\geq\alpha
for
\mathcal{G}(u)=\sigma
.

2.

There exists
e\in\Pi(\Omega)
with
\mathcal{G}(e)>\sigma
such that
J_{\lambda}(e)<0
.

Proof.

The proof is standard and is thus omitted.
\square

Lemma 4.9

Let
1<p<2^{*}-1
,
\lambda>0
. If
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
(PS)_{c}
sequence of
J_{\lambda}
, then
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded. Let
u_{0}\in\Pi(\Omega)
be the weak limit of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, then
u_{0}
is a weak solution of problem (
1.1
). Moreover,
J_{\lambda}(u_{0})\geq 0
.

Proof.
First note that
J_{\lambda}(|u|)\leq J_{\lambda}(u)
,
u\in\Pi(\Omega)
, without loss generality we assume that
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of nonnegative functions.
Since
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
(PS)_{c}
sequence, we can easily find
M>0
such that

|J_{\lambda}(u_{k})|\leq M,~|\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{k}),u_{k}/\mathcal{G}(u_{k})\rangle|\leq M.

From here it is easy standard to show that
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
\Pi(\Omega)
(see for instance
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
).
Since
\Pi(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space, we can assume that up to a subsequence, there exists
v\in\Pi(\Omega)
,
v\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
such
u_{k}\rightharpoonup v
weakly in
\Pi(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\rightarrow v
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Rest of the proof follows similarly as the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 2.2]
.
\square

In the next lemma, we give the threshold value below which the energy functional
J_{\lambda}^{+}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition. This will play an important role in applying the critical point theorems.

Lemma 4.10

Assume that
1<q<2^{*}-1
and
\lambda>0
. If
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
(PS)_{c}
sequence of
J_{\lambda}
with

c<\frac{n+2-\mu}{4n-2\mu}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{n+2-\mu}},

(4.32)

then
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has a convergent subsequence.

Proof.

The proof is similar as the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
.
\square

Lemma 4.11

There exists
w_{\epsilon}
such that

\sup\limits_{s\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(sw_{\epsilon})<\frac{n+2-\mu}{4n-2\mu}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{n-\mu+2}}

(4.33)

provided that either

1.

n>\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{2(p+3)}{p+1},2+\frac{\mu}{p+1},2\left(1+\frac{2-2s}{p-1}\right)\right\},\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\right\}
and
\lambda>0
, or

2.

n\leq\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{2(p+3)}{p+1},2+\frac{\mu}{p+1},2\left(1+\frac{2-2s}{p-1}\right)\right\},\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\right\}
and
\lambda
is sufficiently large.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle V(x)=\frac{[n(n-2)]^{\frac{n-2}{4}}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}
. Then we know from
[
18
]
that
V
is a minimizer of
S_{H,L,C}
. Assume that
B_{\delta}\subset\Omega\subset B_{2\delta}
and let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta(x)=1
in
B_{\delta}
and
\eta(x)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. We define, for
\epsilon>0
,

V_{\epsilon}(x):=\epsilon^{\frac{2-n}{2}}V\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right)~\text{and}~v_{\epsilon}(x):=\eta(x)V_{\epsilon}(x).

(4.34)

Then from
[
5
, p22]
and
[
17
, Lemma 2.5]
, we know that as
\epsilon\to 0

\|v_{\epsilon}\|^{2}=C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{n-2}),

(4.35)

\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|v_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\geq C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}}),

(4.36)

and defining
\nu_{s,n}=\min\{n-2,2-2s\}

[v_{\epsilon}]_{s}=O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}}).

(4.37)

Now we argue as in
[
17
]
and consider the following two cases:

Case 1.

n>\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{2(p+3)}{p+1},2+\frac{\mu}{p+1},2\left(1+\frac{2-2s}{p-1}\right)\right\},\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\right\}.

First by the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
, since
p>1
and
n>\frac{2(p+1)}{p}
, we know
n<(n-2)(p+1)
and then as
\epsilon\to 0

|v_{\epsilon}|_{p+1}^{p+1}=O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})+O(\epsilon^{\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})=O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}}).

(4.38)

Using the estimates in (
4.35
), (
4.36
), (
4.37
) and (
4.38
), we have

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(sv_{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}}{2}\mathcal{G}(v_{\epsilon})^{2}-\frac{\lambda s^{p+1}}{p+1}|v_{\epsilon}|_{p+1}^{p+1}-\frac{s^{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}\|v_{\epsilon}\|_{HL}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})\right)-\frac{\lambda s^{p+1}}{p+1}O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})

\displaystyle-\frac{s^{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle f(s).

Note that
f(s)\rightarrow-\infty
as
s\rightarrow\infty
and
f
is increasing near
0
. Thus there exists
s_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\sup\limits_{s>0}f(s)
is achieved at some
s_{\epsilon}
. Then clearly
s_{\epsilon}
satisfies the following

s_{\epsilon}<\left(\frac{C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})}{C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}}:=S_{H,L,C}(\epsilon)

and it is easy to see that there exists
s_{0}>0
independent of
\epsilon
such that for all
\epsilon>0
small enough
s_{\epsilon}>s_{0}
.
Notice that the function

s\rightarrow\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})\right)-\frac{s^{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\max\limits_{s\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(sv_{\epsilon})\leq

\displaystyle f(s_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s_{\epsilon}^{2}}{2}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})\right)-\frac{s_{\epsilon}^{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{2\cdot 2_{\mu}^{\ast}}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{S^{2}_{H,L,C}(\epsilon)}{2}\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n+2-\mu}{4n-2\mu}\left(\frac{C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}\cdot\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{n}{2}}+O(\epsilon^{\nu_{s,n}})}{\left(C(n,\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{2}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{\mu}{2}})\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-\mu}}}\right)^{\frac{2n-\mu}{n+2-\mu}}-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})

\displaystyle\frac{n+2-\mu}{4n-2\mu}S_{H,L,C}^{\frac{2n-\mu}{n+2-\mu}}+O(\epsilon^{\min\{\nu_{s,n},n-\frac{\mu}{2}\}})-O(\epsilon^{n-\frac{(n-2)(p+1)}{2}})

Case 2.

n\leq\max\left\{\min\left\{\frac{2(p+3)}{p+1},2+\frac{\mu}{p+1},2\left(1+\frac{2-2s}{p-1}\right)\right\},\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\right\}.

Again since for any fixed

)

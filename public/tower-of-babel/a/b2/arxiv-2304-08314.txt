The Hilbert Polynomial of Quandles and Colorings of Random Links
Ariel Davis, Tomer M. Schlank

Abstract
Given a finite quandle Q Q , we study the average number of Q Q -colorings of the closure of a random braid in B n B_{n} as n n varies. In particular we show that this number coincides with some polynomial P Q ∈ ℚ ⁡ [ x ] P_{Q}\in\mathbb{Q}[x] for n ≫ 0 n\gg 0 . The degree of this polynomial is readily computed in terms of Q Q as a quandle and these invariants are computed for all quandles with | Q | ≤ 4 |Q|\leq 4 . Additionally we show that the methods in this paper allow to improve on the stability results of [ EVW15 ] from “periodic stability” to “stability”.

Introduction

A quandle is an algebraic structure particularly well-suited for producing numerical invariants of oriented links: For a finite quandle
Q
and oriented link
L
in
S^{3}
, one counts
\textrm{Col}_{Q}(L)
- the set of “
Q
-colorings” of
L
.
Our main goal in this paper is to study the average size
\textrm{Col}_{Q}(L)
where
L
is chosen randomly due to a certain family of distributions. Informally we will say that we are interested in the average size of
\textrm{Col}_{Q}(L)
where
L
is taken to be the closure
\overline{\sigma}
of a random braid
\sigma\in B_{n}
of
n
-strands. The formal meaning is not immediately clear as
B_{n}
is a countable discreet group. However it is not hard to show (See
Proposition

2.24
) that the function
c_{Q}\colon B_{n}\to\mathbb{N}

\sigma\in B_{n}
to
c_{Q}(\sigma)\coloneqq|\textrm{Col}_{Q}(\overline{\sigma})|\in\mathbb{N}
extends uniquely to a continues function
c_{Q}\colon\widehat{B_{n}}\to\mathbb{N}
from the profinite completion of
B_{n}
. Since
\widehat{B_{n}}
is compact and Hausdorff we get a well defined average
\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{B_{n}}}c_{Q}=\int\limits_{\widehat{B_{n}}}c_{Q}d\mu
. The main result of this paper is:

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

2.26
)
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Then there exist integer-valued polynomials
P_{Q}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
s.t.

\int\limits_{\widehat{B_{n}}}c_{Q}d\mu=P_{Q}(n)

for all
n\gg 0
.

We then give a give a simple formula for
\deg P_{Q}
in term of the number of connected components of sub-quandles of
Q
(
proposition

4.5
).

Theorem

1.1
is reminiscent of the existence of Hilbert polynomial for graded modules in commutative algebra. Indeed, the main technical step in the proof is
Proposition

3.36
that gives a generalization of Hilbert polynomial to graded modules over certain class of well behaved non-commutative graded rings.
Proposition

3.36
can be use also to give a slight improvement on the stability result in
[
EVW15
]
. Specifically
[
EVW15
, Thm 6.1]
gives that dimension of the homology groups
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{i}(\textrm{Hur}_{G,n}^{c},\mathbb{C})
of certain sequence of Hurwitz spaces is eventually periodic in
n
,
Proposition

3.36
gives that this sequence is in fact eventually
constant
(see
Remark

3.38
).

The Contents of this Paper

In section

In section

In section

Definition 2.1
.

A

is a set

Q\times Q\to Q\;,\;\;\;\;(x,y)\mapsto{}^{x}{y}

(Q1)

for every

{}^{x}{x}=x

(Q2)

for every

is a bijection.

(Q3)

for every

{}^{x}{({}^{y}{z})}={}^{({}^{x}{y})}{({}^{x}{z})}
.

A
morphism
of quandles is a function

s.t. for all

f({}^{x}{y})={}^{f(x)}{f(y)}.

All quandles and quandle morphisms naturally
form a category, denoted here by
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
and let

.
By
\varphi_{x}\colon Q\to Q
we denote the function

\varphi_{x}(y)={}^{x}{y}
. By axioms (Q2) and (Q3) of

2.1
, for all
x\in Q

\varphi_{x}\in\textrm{Aut}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q).

We denote by
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}\subseteq{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
the full subcategory of finite quandles, that is, those

with finite underlying set.

Example 2.2

(
[
Joy82
, §1]
)

Let

be a group.
Then there is a canonical quandle structure on

There is a functor

\textrm{Conj}\colon{\mathscr{G}\textrm{rp}}\to{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}\;\;,\;\;\;\textrm{Conj}(G)=(G,{}^{g}{h}=ghg^{-1})\;,\;\;\textrm{Conj}(f\colon G\to H)=f.

and let

is a quandle with structure induced from conjugation in

The following notion of a trivial quandle can be found in many sources, for example
[

.

\varphi_{a}(b)\cdot a=a\cdot b=b\cdot\varphi_{b}^{-1}(a)=c.

Thus if
a

c
from the left, then
a
divides
c
from the right, and if
b
divides
c
from the right then
b
divides
c
from the left.

:

\mathscr{A}_{Q}=\coprod_{R\leq Q}\mathscr{A}_{R}^{\textrm{dom}}.

Thus for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|=\sum_{R\leq Q}|\mathscr{A}_{R,n}^{\textrm{dom}}|.

(3)

By
definition

3.37
, for all
R\leq Q
,

|\mathscr{A}_{R,n}^{\textrm{dom}}|=P_{R}^{\textrm{dom}}(n)

for
n\gg 0
, the threshold depending on
R
. The sum in (
3
) runs over finitely-many sub-quandles
R\leq Q
, therefore there is a common threshold, and

P_{Q}(n)=|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|=\sum_{R\leq Q}|\mathscr{A}_{R,n}^{\textrm{dom}}|=\sum_{R\leq Q}P_{R}^{\textrm{dom}}(n)

for
n\gg 0
. Since
P_{Q},\;\displaystyle{\sum_{R\leq Q}P_{R}^{\textrm{dom}}}
coincide for input
n\gg 0
, they must be equal:

P_{Q}=\sum_{R\leq Q}P_{R}^{\textrm{dom}}\in\mathbb{Q}[x].

∎

We proceed to calculate
\deg P_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
and let
a\in\mathscr{A}_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}
. Then

\langle\{\varphi_{b}\;|\;b\in\mathscr{A}_{Q},\;b|a\}\rangle_{\mathscr{G}\textrm{rp}}={\textrm{Inn}}(Q).

Proof.

Denote by
G_{a}:=\langle\{\varphi_{b}\;|\;b\in\mathscr{A}_{Q},\;b|a\}\rangle\leq{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
and let
S\subseteq Q
denote

S_{a}=\{x\in Q\;|\;\varphi_{x}\in G_{a}\}.

In fact
S\leq Q
is a sub-quandle: Let
x,y\in S_{a}
. Then by
proposition

2.8

\varphi_{x},\varphi_{y}\in G_{a}\;\Longrightarrow\;\varphi_{{}^{x}{y}}=\varphi_{x}\varphi_{y}\varphi_{x}^{-1}\in G_{a}\;\Longrightarrow\;{}^{x}{y}\in S_{a}.

Therefore
S_{a}\leq Q
is a sub-quandle.
Write
a=x_{1}\dots x_{n}
for
x_{i}\in\mathscr{A}_{Q,1}\simeq Q
.
For all
i
,
\varphi_{x_{i}}\in G_{a}
, therefore
x_{i}\in S_{a}
, hence

a=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\mathscr{A}_{S_{a}}.

But
a\in\mathscr{A}_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}
, it follows that
S_{a}=Q
. Therefore
\varphi_{x}\in G_{a}
for all
x\in Q
. Thus

G_{a}\leq\langle\{\varphi_{x}\;|\;x\in Q\}\rangle_{\mathscr{G}\textrm{rp}}={\textrm{Inn}}(Q),

and

\langle\{\varphi_{b}\;|\;b\in\mathscr{A}_{Q},\;b|a\}\rangle={\textrm{Inn}}(Q).

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
and let

. Then for all
x\in Q
and all

x^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a=\psi(x)^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a.

Proof.

Let
b\in\mathscr{A}_{Q}
s.t.

3.23
, for all

x^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a=x^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot b\cdot c=b\cdot x^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot c=\varphi_{b}(x^{\exp_{Q}})\cdot b\cdot c=\varphi_{b}(x)^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a.

By

4.2
,
{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
is generated as a group by
\varphi_{b}
for all

in
\mathscr{A}_{Q}
. Thus

x^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a=\psi(x)^{\exp_{Q}}\cdot a

for all
x\in Q
and all
\psi\in{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

s.t.
|c|\in N\mathbb{Z}
and

\deg P_{C}=k-1.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq C
be a finite set of generators. Define a graded monoid
A^{\prime}
and graded
A^{\prime}

as follows:

A^{\prime}_{n}=A_{nN}\;\;\;\;,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;C^{\prime}_{n}=C_{nN}.

Then for all

|C^{\prime}_{n}|=|C_{nN}|=P_{C}(nN).

Therefore
C^{\prime}
admits a Hilbert polynomial
P_{C^{\prime}}
satisfying

\deg P_{C}=\deg P_{C^{\prime}}.

The
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathbb{C}[A^{\prime}]
is graded, abelian, freely generated by
k
elements of degree
1
. Consider
\mathbb{C}[C^{\prime}]
, a graded
\mathbb{C}[A^{\prime}]
-module. On one hand
\mathbb{C}[C^{\prime}]
is finitely-generated, therefore
\deg P_{C^{\prime}}\leq k-1
. On the other hand
\mathbb{C}[C^{\prime}]
contains a free sub-module, namely

\deg P_{C^{\prime}}\geq k-1
. Altogether,

\deg P_{C}=\deg P_{C^{\prime}}=k-1.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
f\colon R\to Q
be a surjective morphism of quandles, and let
x,y\in Q
be such that
[x]_{\sim}=[y]_{\sim}\in\pi_{0}(Q)
. Then there exists
\psi\in{\textrm{Inn}}(R)
s.t.

\psi\colon f^{-1}(x)\xrightarrow{\sim}f^{-1}(y)

is an isomorphism of quandles.

Proof.

Since
[x]_{\sim}=[y]_{\sim}\in\pi_{0}(Q)
, let
\psi_{0}\in{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
be s.t.

\psi_{0}(x)=y.

Since
f
is surjective, by
proposition

5.4
we can lift
\psi_{0}
to some
\psi\in{\textrm{Inn}}(R)
s.t.

f\circ\psi=\psi_{0}\circ f,

which also implies that

\psi_{0}^{-1}\circ f=f\circ\psi^{-1}.

This
\psi
maps
f^{-1}(x)
bijectively onto
f^{-1}(y)
: Let
z\in f^{-1}(x)
. Then

f(\psi(z))=\psi_{0}(f(z))=\psi_{0}(x)=y\;\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\;\psi(z)\in f^{-1}(y).

For
z\in f^{-1}(y)
,

f(\psi^{-1}(z))=\psi_{0}^{-1}(f(z))=\psi_{0}^{-1}(y)=x\;\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\;\psi^{-1}(z)\in f^{-1}(x).

By
proposition

5.3
,
f^{-1}(x),f^{-1}(y)
are sub-quandles of
R
, and since
\psi\in{\textrm{Inn}}(R)

is a quandle automorphism of
Q
, it induces a quandle isomorphism

\psi\colon f^{-1}(x)\xrightarrow{\sim}f^{-1}(y).

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f\colon R\to Q
be a surjective morphism of finite quandles. Then

|\pi_{0}(R)|\leq\sum_{[x]\in\pi_{0}(Q)}|\pi_{0}(f^{-1}(x))|.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq Q
be a set of representatives for
\pi_{0}(Q)
. For every
x\in Q
, let
\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\in{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
be s.t.

\mathfrak{m}_{x}:=\widetilde{\psi}_{x}(x)\in\mathfrak{m}.

By
proposition

5.4
, we can lift these
\widetilde{\psi}_{x}\in{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)
to some
\psi_{x}\in{\textrm{Inn}}(R)
, s.t.

\psi_{x}\colon f^{-1}(x)\xrightarrow{\sim}f^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}).

Therefore the map

\coprod_{[x]\in\pi_{0}(Q)}f^{-1}(x)\to{\textrm{Inn}}(Q)

is surjective. Moreover, for every
[x]\in\pi_{0}(Q)
, the composition

f^{-1}(x)\to R\to Q\to\pi_{0}(Q)

factors through
f^{-1}(x)\to\pi_{0}(f^{-1}(x))
. Thus the map

\coprod_{[x]\in\pi_{0}(Q)}\pi_{0}(f^{-1}(x))\longrightarrow\pi_{0}(Q)

is surjective. Hence the desired inequality.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let

to the projection map
Q\times R\to Q
:

|\pi_{0}(Q\times R)|\leq\sum_{[x]\in\pi_{0}(Q)}|\pi_{0}(\{x\}\times R)|=|\pi_{0}(Q)|\cdot|\pi_{0}(R)|.

All sets involved are finite because
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
, therefore the natural map

\pi_{0}(Q\times R)\to\pi_{0}(Q)\times\pi_{0}(R)

is a bijection.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
be finite quandles. Then

\dim_{Q\times R}=\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

Proof.

We first show that

\dim_{Q\times R}\geq\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

Let
Q^{\prime}\leq Q,R^{\prime}\leq R
. Then
Q^{\prime}\times R^{\prime}\leq Q\times R
, and by
proposition

5.7
,

|\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime}\times R^{\prime})|=|\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime})|\cdot|\pi_{0}(R^{\prime})|.

Therefore

\dim_{Q\times R}=\max_{D\leq Q\times R}|\pi_{0}(D)|\geq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q^{\prime}\leq Q\\
R^{\prime}\leq R\end{subarray}}|\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime}\times R^{\prime})|=

=\max_{Q^{\prime}\leq Q}|\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime})|\cdot\max_{R^{\prime}\leq R}|\pi_{0}(R^{\prime})|=\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

Next we show that

\dim_{Q\times R}\leq\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

Let
D\leq Q\times R
. Consider the composition

f\colon D\to Q\times R\xrightarrow{proj_{Q}}Q.

Denote by
Q^{\prime}=f(Q)
, a sub-quandle of
Q
from
proposition

2.5
. By definition,

|\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime})|\leq\dim_{Q}.

For all
x\in Q^{\prime}
, denote by

D_{x}:=f^{-1}(x)=\{x\}\times R_{x}

for some subset
R_{x}\subseteq R
. Since the quandle structure on
D
is induced by
Q\times R
, and by
proposition

5.3
we know that
D_{x}\leq D
, it follows that
R_{x}\leq R
, and

|\pi_{0}(D_{x})|=|\pi_{0}(R_{x})|\leq\dim_{R}.

We invoke
lemma

5.6
with the surjective
f\colon D\to Q^{\prime}
and find that

|\pi_{0}(D)|\leq\sum_{\pi_{0}(Q^{\prime})}|\pi_{0}(D_{x})|\leq\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

This holds for all
D\leq Q\times R
, therefore

\dim_{Q\times R}=\max_{D\leq Q\times R}|\pi_{0}(D)|\leq\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

We have the desired equality:

\dim_{Q\times R}=\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}.

∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is an equality of random variables on
\widehat{B_{n}}
:

c_{Q}\cdot c_{R}=c_{Q\times R}

Proof.

By
proposition

5.2
,
Q\times R
is the direct product in
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
. Therefore for any oriented link
L
,

\textrm{Col}_{Q\times R}(L)=\textrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q_{L},Q\times R)\simeq

\simeq\textrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q_{L},Q)\times\textrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q_{L},R)=\textrm{Col}_{Q}(L)\times\textrm{Col}_{R}(L).

So for every braid
\sigma\in B_{n}
,

\textrm{col}_{Q\times R}(\overline{\sigma})=\textrm{col}_{Q}(\overline{\sigma})\cdot\textrm{col}_{R}(\overline{\sigma}).

It follows that the random variables
c_{Q\times R}
and
c_{Q}\cdot c_{R}
- both continuous - coincide on
B_{n}\subseteq\widehat{B_{n}}
, which is dense. It follows that

c_{Q\times R}=c_{Q}\cdot c_{R}

∎

Remark 5.10
.

A consequence of
theorem

2.26
and
proposition

5.9
is that more refined measurements of such
c_{Q,n}
also coincide with a polynomial for
n\gg 0
. These include higher moments of
c_{Q,n}
, or covariances
\textrm{Cov}(c_{Q,n},c_{R,n})
. The threshold for
n
where these measurements coincide with a polynomial is not universal.

Example 5.11
.

A finite nonempty quandle
Q
is said to be
stable
if the sequence
|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|
is constant for
n\gg 0
, that is if
\deg P_{Q}=0
. By
theorem

4.6
,
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
is stable iff
\dim P_{Q}=1
. By
theorem

5.8
, the product of stable quandles is again stable, since

\dim_{Q}=\dim_{R}=1\Longrightarrow\dim_{Q\times R}=\dim_{Q}\cdot\dim_{R}=1.

6
Disjoint Unions of Quandles

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
be quandles. The disjoint union
Q\sqcup R
has a natural quandle structure given by

x,y\in Q\sqcup R\;\;:\;\;\;{}^{x}{y}\;\;=\;\;\begin{tabular}[]{|c||c|c|}\hline\cr${{}^{x}{y}}$&$x\in Q$&$x\in R$\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr$y\in Q$&${}^{x}{y}$&$y$\\
\hline\cr$y\in R$&$y$&${}^{x}{y}$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}

Proof.

Quandle axioms (Q1) and (Q2) clearly hold. As for (Q3):

{}^{z}{({}^{x}{y})}={}^{({}^{z}{x})}{({}^{z}{y})}.

If
x,y,z\in Q
or
x,y,z\in R
, this holds from (Q3) in
Q
and
R
separately.

If
x,y\in Q
and
z\in R
, or likewise if
x,y\in R
and
z\in Q
, then

{}^{z}{({}^{x}{y})}={}^{x}{y}={}^{({}^{z}{x})}{({}^{z}{y})}.

If
x\in Q
and
y\in R
, or likewise if
x\in R
and
y\in Q
, then

{}^{z}{({}^{x}{y})}={}^{z}{y}={}^{({}^{z}{x})}{({}^{z}{y})}.

∎

The following lemma about monoids is fairly straightforward. The proof is omitted.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}
be monoids given by generators and relations:

A=\mathbb{N}\langle S_{A}\;|\;R_{A}\rangle\;,\;\;B=\mathbb{N}\langle S_{B}\;|\;R_{B}\rangle.

Then the free product
A\star B
, which is the coproduct in
{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}
, is given by

A\star B\simeq\mathbb{N}\langle S_{A}\sqcup S_{B}\;|\;R_{A}\sqcup R_{B}\rangle

and the product
A\times B
is given by

A\times B\simeq\mathbb{N}\langle S_{A}\sqcup S_{B}\;|\;R_{A}\sqcup R_{B}\sqcup\{xy=yx\;|\;\forall x\in S_{A},y\in S_{B}\}\;\rangle.

The natural map
A\star B\to A\times B
in
{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}
from coproduct to product, that comes from the fact that
{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}
is a pointed category, is seen here by taking further quotients in RHS.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
. Then there is a natural isomorphism

\mathscr{A}_{Q}\times\mathscr{A}_{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}

in
{{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}_{gr}}
.

Proof.

From the natural embeddings of
Q
and
R
into
Q\sqcup R
we get morphisms

\mathscr{A}_{Q}\to\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}\;,\;\;\mathscr{A}_{R}\to\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}

in
{{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}_{gr}}
.
The coproduct in
{{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}_{gr}}\simeq{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}_{/\mathbb{N}}
is the coproduct in
{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}
, which is the free product. Thus we get

\mathscr{A}_{Q}\star\mathscr{A}_{R}\to\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}

in
{{\mathscr{M}\textrm{on}}_{gr}}
.

\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}=\mathbb{N}\langle Q\sqcup R\;|\;\forall x,y\in Q\sqcup R\;,\;\;x\cdot y={}^{x}{y}\cdot x\rangle=

=\mathbb{N}\left\langle Q\sqcup R\;\Bigg|\;\begin{matrix}\forall x,y\in Q&x\cdot y={}^{x}{y}\cdot x\\
\forall x,y\in R&x\cdot y={}^{x}{y}\cdot x\\
\forall x\in Q,y\in R&x\cdot y=y\cdot x\\
\forall x\in R,y\in Q&x\cdot y=y\cdot x\end{matrix}\;\right\rangle.

By
lemma

6.2
, this is naturally isomorphic to

\mathbb{N}\langle Q\;|\;\forall x,y\in Q,\;x\cdot y={}^{x}{y}\cdot x\;\rangle\times\mathbb{N}\langle R\;|\;\forall x,y\in R,\;x\cdot y={}^{x}{y}\cdot x\;\rangle=\mathscr{A}_{Q}\times\mathscr{A}_{R}.

This isomorphism preserves generators, all of degree
1
:

\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}\supseteq Q\sqcup R\;\longleftrightarrow\;\left(Q\times\{1_{\mathscr{A}_{R}}\}\sqcup\{1_{\mathscr{A}_{Q}}\}\times R\right)\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{Q}\times\mathscr{A}_{R},

thus the isomorphism is one of graded monoids.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. We define the
generating function
for
Q
as

\eta_{Q}(t)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|\cdot t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[t].

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Then

\eta_{Q\sqcup R}(t)=\eta_{Q}(t)\cdot\eta_{R}(t).

Proof.

By
proposition

6.3
,
\mathscr{A}_{Q}\times\mathscr{A}_{R}
and
\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R}
are isomorphic as graded monoids. For all
n
we therefore have

A_{Q\sqcup R,n}\simeq\left(\mathscr{A}_{Q}\times\mathscr{A}_{R}\right)_{n}\simeq\coprod_{i+j=n}\mathscr{A}_{Q,i}\times\mathscr{A}_{R,j}.

Therefore

\eta_{Q}(t)\cdot\eta_{R}(t)=\left(\sum_{i}|\mathscr{A}_{Q,i}|t^{i}\right)\left(\sum_{j}|\mathscr{A}_{R,j}|t^{j}\right)=

=\sum_{n}\sum_{i+j=n}|\mathscr{A}_{Q,i}|\cdot|\mathscr{A}_{R,j}|\cdot t^{n}=\sum_{n}|\mathscr{A}_{Q\sqcup R,n}|\cdot t^{n}=\eta_{Q\sqcup R}(t).

∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Then

\eta_{Q}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,(1-t)^{-1}]\;,\;\;{\textrm{ord}}_{(1-t)}(\eta_{Q})=-\dim_{Q}.

Proof.

If
Q=\emptyset
, then
\eta_{Q}(t)=1
. Otherwise since
P_{Q}(x)
is an integer-valued polynomial of degree
\dim_{Q}-1
, there exist
a_{1},\dots a_{\dim_{Q}}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a_{\dim_{Q}}\neq 0
s.t.

P_{Q}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim_{Q}}a_{k}\cdot{x+k-1\choose k-1}.

Thus

\sum_{n}P_{Q}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim_{Q}}a_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{n+k-1\choose k-1}t^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim_{Q}}\frac{a_{k}}{(1-t)^{k}}.

Since
|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|=P_{Q}(n)
for all
n\gg 0
, it follows that

\eta_{Q}(t)-\sum_{k=1}^{\dim_{Q}}\frac{a_{k}}{(1-t)^{k}}=\eta_{Q}(t)-\sum_{n}P_{Q}(n)t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|-P_{Q}(n)\right)t^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[t].

Thus
\eta_{Q}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,(1-t)^{-1}]
. Since
a_{\dim_{Q}}\neq 0
,
{\textrm{ord}}_{(1-t)}(\eta_{Q}(t))=-\dim_{Q}
.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

As an added bonus, one recovers from
\eta_{Q}(t)
the threshold beyond which
|A_{Q,n}|
agrees with
P_{Q}(n)
:

\min\{n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\;|\;\forall n>n_{0},\;|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|=P_{Q}(n)\}=-{\textrm{ord}}_{t=\infty}\eta_{Q}(t).

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
Q,R\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Then

\dim_{Q\sqcup R}=\dim_{Q}+\dim_{R}.

Proof.

By
proposition

6.6
, we can recover
\dim_{Q}
from
\eta_{Q}
via:

\dim_{Q}=-{\textrm{ord}}_{(t-1)}(\eta_{Q}(t)).

By
proposition

6.5
,

\eta_{Q\sqcup R}(t)=\eta_{Q}(t)\cdot\eta_{R}(t).

Thus

\dim_{Q\sqcup R}=-{\textrm{ord}}_{(t-1)}(\eta_{Q\sqcup R}(t))=-{\textrm{ord}}_{(t-1)}(\eta_{Q}(t)\cdot\eta_{R}(t))=

=-{\textrm{ord}}_{(t-1)}(\eta_{Q}(t))-{\textrm{ord}}_{(t-1)}(\eta_{R}(t))=\dim_{Q}+\dim_{R}.

∎

Example 6.9
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
. Let
Q_{+}\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
denote
Q_{+}:=Q\sqcup*
, with
*:=T_{1}
.
Then

\eta_{Q_{+}}(t)=\eta_{Q}(t)\cdot\frac{1}{1-t}\;\Longrightarrow\;\eta_{Q}(t)=(1-t)\cdot\eta_{Q_{+}}(t).

Therefore for
n\gg 0
,

P_{Q}(n)=|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}|=|\mathscr{A}_{Q_{+},n}|-|\mathscr{A}_{Q_{+},n-1}|=P_{Q_{+}}(n)-P_{Q_{+}}(n-1).

Let
\eta_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]]
denote the series

\eta_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}^{\textrm{dom}}\right|t^{n}.

Then

\mathscr{A}_{Q_{+}}^{\textrm{dom}}\simeq\mathscr{A}_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}\times\mathscr{A}_{T_{1}}^{\textrm{dom}}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\eta_{Q_{+}}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)=\eta_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)\cdot\frac{t}{1-t}

Therefore for
n\gg 0
,

P_{Q}^{\textrm{dom}}(n)=|\mathscr{A}_{Q,n}^{\textrm{dom}}|=|\mathscr{A}_{Q_{+},n+1}^{\textrm{dom}}|-|\mathscr{A}_{Q_{+},n}^{\textrm{dom}}|=P_{Q_{+}}(n+1)-P_{Q_{+}}(n).

6.1
Twisted Pointed Quandles

In this section,
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}^{\textrm{fin}}
is a finite quandle and
\psi\in\textrm{Aut}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q)
is such that for all
x\in Q
,

\varphi_{\psi(x)}=\varphi_{x}.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
Q
and
\psi
be as above. Then the quotient
Q/\psi
as a set inherits the quandle structure on
Q
, s.t.

Q\to Q/\psi
is a morphism in
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in Q
,

{}^{\psi(x)}{y}={}^{x}{y}\;\;,\;\;\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\;\;{}^{x}{\psi(y)}=\psi\left({}^{\psi^{-1}(x)}{y}\right)=\psi({}^{x}{y}).

Hence the quandle structure on
Q
is well defined modulo
\psi
.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
Q\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
and
\psi\in\textrm{Aut}_{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}(Q)
be as above. Then

{{}^{x}{y}}

x=*

x\in Q

y=*

*

*

y\in Q

\psi(y)

{}^{x}{y}

is a quandle structure on the set
Q\sqcup*

Q\sqcup_{\psi}*\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}

Q
into
Q\sqcup_{\psi}*
is a morphism in
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
.

Proof.

Axioms (Q1) and (Q2) are easily verified. As for (Q3): let

. Then

z=*\;\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\;{}^{x}{({}^{y}{*})}=*={}^{({}^{x}{y})}{({}^{x}{*})}.

x=*\neq y,z\;\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\;{}^{*}{({}^{y}{z})}=\psi({}^{y}{z})={}^{\psi(y)}{\psi(z)}={}^{({}^{*}{y})}{({}^{*}{z})}.

x=y=*\neq z\;\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\;{}^{*}{({}^{*}{z})}={}^{({}^{*}{*})}{({}^{*}{z})}.

∎

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
Q
and
\psi

7.1
Trivial Quandles

For

,
\mathscr{A}_{\emptyset}=\{1\}
. Since
\emptyset
has no proper sub-quandles,
\mathscr{A}_{\emptyset}^{\textrm{dom}}=\{1\}
as well. therefore

\eta_{T_{0}}(t)=\eta_{T_{0}}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)=1.

For
\alpha\geq 1
,
T_{\alpha}=(T_{\alpha-1})_{+}
, therefore

\eta_{T_{\alpha}}(t)=\eta_{T_{\alpha-1}}(t)\cdot\frac{1}{1-t}\;\;,\;\;\;\;\eta_{T_{\alpha}}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)=\eta_{T_{\alpha-1}}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)\cdot\frac{t}{1-t}.

By induction on

\eta_{T_{\alpha}}(t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}\;\;,\;\;\;\;\eta_{T_{\alpha}}^{\textrm{dom}}(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{(1-t)^{\alpha}},

7.3
Dihedral Quandles

There is a functor from abelian groups to quandles -

{D}\colon{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}\to{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}\;\;,\;\;\;\;M\mapsto{D_{M}},

where
D_{M}
has underlying set
M
, and quandle structure

{}^{x}{y}=2x-y.

A morphism
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
in
{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}
yields a morphism
{D_{f}}\colon{D_{M}}\to{D_{M^{\prime}}}
in
{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
:

\forall x\in M\;\;:\;\;\;\;f({}^{x}{y})=f(2x-y)=2f(x)-f(y)={}^{f(x)}{f(y)}.

For clarity in computations we distinguish between elements of
M\in{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}
and
{D_{M}}\in{\mathscr{Q}\textrm{dl}}
thusly:

x\in M\;,\;\;[x]\in{D_{M}}.

For every
M\in{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}
there is a natural function

\textrm{T}_{M}\colon\mathscr{A}_{{D_{M}}}\to M\;\;,\;\;\;\;\textrm{T}_{M}([x_{0}]\cdots[x_{n}])=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\cdot x_{i}.

This is the unique function
\mathscr{A}_{{D_{M}}}\to M
satisfying

\forall x\in M\;,\;\;\textrm{T}_{M}([x])=x\;,

\forall\;a,b\in\mathscr{A}_{{D_{M}}}\;,\;\;\textrm{T}_{M}(a\cdot b)=\textrm{T}_{M}(a)+(-1)^{|a|}\textrm{T}_{M}(b).

For
M=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\in{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}
these
{D_{M}}
are called
dihedral quandles
, denoted by

{D_{\ell}}:={D_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}}}.

For all
M\in{\mathscr{A}\textrm{b}}
, the quandle
{D_{M}}
is naturally isomorphic to the sub-quandle of the dihedral group
M\rtimes\{\pm 1\}
consisting of all reflections, via

{D_{M}}\to M\rtimes\{\pm 1\}\;\;,\;\;\;\;m\mapsto(m,1).

The aim here is to compute
P_{D_{\ell}}(x),P_{D_{\ell}}^{\textrm{dom}}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and
\eta_{D_{\ell}}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}(t)
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. We need some preliminary statements:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

and let

. Then the sub-quandles

Isoparametric functions on Finsler space forms
∗

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we prove that transnormal functions are isoparametric functions on a Finsler space form
(N(c),F)
under certain conditions, which generalize Theorem B in
[
5
]
. Further more, we obtain a local isoparametric function constructed by a distance function and give a global isoparametric function on a standard Finsler sphere.

Key words:

Finsler space form; transnormal function; isoparametric function; umbilic; distance function.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
53C20, 53C60, 53C42.

1
Introduction

The study on isoparametric theory has a long history. A smooth function
f
defined on a connected complete Riemannian manifold
(N,g)
is called an
isoparametric function
if there exists a smooth function
a
and a continuous function
b
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|\nabla f\|^{2}=\tilde{a}(f),\\
&\Delta f=\tilde{b}(f).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

E. Cartan began to study isoparametric hypersurfaces in real space forms with constant sectional curvature
c
systematically
[
1
]
. Then many mathematicians started working on isoparametric functions and the classification of isoparametric hypersurfaces in a real space form
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. If
f
only satisfies the first equation of (
1.1
),
f
is called a
transnormal function
.

The fundamental structural result in a general Riemannian manifold
N
was given by Q. M. Wang
[
5
]
. It shows that the focal varieties of a transnormal function
f
, which is the preimage if the maximum (resp. minimum), are smooth submanifolds of
N
and each regular level set of
f
is a tube over either of the focal varieties. Particularly, he obtained the following startling theorems,

Theorem A
.

[
5
]

Transnormal functions on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\mathbb{E}^{n}
are isoparametric.

However, no proof was given in
[
5
]
. Later,
[
6
]
supplied the proof of Theorem A and constructed an example to point out there is an essential difference between transnormal functions and isoparametric functions in hyperbolic spaces.

In Finsler geometry,
[
7
]
introduced the concept of isoparametric functions and transnormal functions. Let
(N,F)
be an

n
-dimensional Finsler manifold. A
C^{1}
non-constant function
f

on
N
is called
isoparametric
if it is smooth on
N_{f}=\{x\in N\mid df(x)\neq 0\}
and there exist a smooth function
a(t)
and a continuous function
b(t)
defined on
J=f(N_{f})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &F^{2}(\nabla f)=a(f),\\
&\Delta f=b(f).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Each regular level hypersurface
M_{t}=f^{-1}(t)
is called an
isoparametric hypersurface
. The function satisfies the first equation in (
1.2
) is called a
transnormal function
.

As in Riemannian geometry, we call a complete and simply connected Finsler manifold with constant flag curvature a
Finsler space form
. There are many kinds of special Finsler space forms, such as Minkowski spaces (with zero flag curvature) and standard Finsler spheres (with positive flag curvature and vanishing
\mathbf{S}_{BH}
-curvature)
[
8
]
. If a Finsler manifold is only forward (resp. backward) complete, we call it
forward
(resp.
backward
)
Finsler space form
.
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
classified isoparametric hypersurfaces in some kinds of Finsler space forms.
[
12
]
studied isoparametric hypersurfaces in Finsler space forms with vanishing reversible torsion.

The fundamental structural result in
[
5
]
was generalized to Finsler geometry in
[
13
]
. Based on the structural result given in
[
13
]
, in this paper, we try to generalize Theorem A to Finsler geometry. Unlike the Riemannian case, the geodesic are not necessarily reversible and the regularity of
f
,
a(t)
and
b(t)
in (
1.2
) are very poor, which causes more difficulties for the research.

For a connected Finsler manifold
(N,F)
, set
\overline{J}=f(N)
. Denote the focal variety of
f
by
M_{\pm}
, which means the set where
f
attains its global maximum value or global minimum
value. Let
f
be a
C^{2}
transnormal function satisfying
F(\nabla f)^{2}=a(f)
, where
a(t)\neq 0
for
t\in J
and
a^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
for
t\in\partial\overline{J}
. We called such
f
an
appropriate transnormal function
. Let
g
be the number of distinct principal curvatures for an isoparametric hypersurface. Then we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(N(c),F)
be a Finsler space form. Suppose that
f
and
-f
are both appropriate transnormal functions. Then
f
is isoparametric when
c\geq 0
, or
c<0
and all principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
of a regular level hypersurface satisfying
|\kappa_{i}|\geq\sqrt{-c}
,
1\leq i\leq g
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
M_{\pm}
be submanifolds and
f
has no critical value in
J
.
f
and
-f
are both appropriate transnormal functions in Theorem
1.1
can be weaken to
f
and
-f
are transnormal functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(N(c),F)
be a connected forward Finsler space form and
f
be a transnormal function on
N
. If some level hypersurface of
f
is umbilic, then
f
is isoparametric.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(N(c),F)
be a forward Finsler space form and
r(x)=d_{F}(p,x)
be a distance function from any
p\in N
. Then
f=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}(x)
is a local isoparametric function with
g=1
on the cut-domain of
p
. Particularly,
f=-\cos(\sqrt{c}r)
is a global isoparametric function on a standard Finsler sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{n}(c),F)
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we will give some definitions and lemmas that will be used in the proof of our main results. Here and from now on, we will use the following convention of index ranges unless other stated,

1\leq i,j,\dots\leq n,\ \ \ \ 1\leq a,b,\dots\leq n-1.

2.1
Finsler manifolds

Let
(N,F)
be an
n
-dimensional manifold and let
TN=\cup_{x\in N}T_{x}N
be the tangent bundle over
N
with local coordinates
(x,y)
, where
x=(x^{i})
and
y=y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
. The fundamental form
g
of
(N,F)
is given by

g=g_{ij}(x,y)dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j},~~~~~~~g_{ij}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}[F^{2}]_{y^{i}y^{j}}.

The geodesics
\gamma=\gamma(t)
of
F
in local coordinates
(x^{i})
are characterized by

\displaystyle\frac{\textmd{d}^{2}x^{i}}{\textmd{d}t^{2}}+2G^{i}(x,\frac{\textmd{d}x}{\textmd{d}t})=0,

(2.1)

where
(x^{i}(t))
are the coordinates of
\gamma(t)
and
G^{i}=G^{i}(x,y)
are defined by

G^{i}=\frac{g^{il}}{4}\{[F^{2}]_{x^{k}y^{l}}y^{k}-[F^{2}]_{x^{l}}\},

which are called the
geodesic coefficients
.

Let
\pi:TN\rightarrow N
be the natural projection. There exists the unique

Berwald connection

\nabla
with
\nabla\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=\Gamma^{i}_{jk}dx^{k}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}
on the pull-back bundle
\pi^{\ast}TN
, which satisfies

\Gamma^{i}_{jk}=\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{j}\partial y^{k}}=\frac{\partial N^{i}_{j}}{\partial y^{k}}
. Let
\frac{\delta}{\delta x^{i}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-N^{j}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}
. The Riemannian curvature tensor is defined by

R^{i}_{k}:=y^{j}R^{i}_{j~kl}y^{l},\ \ \ \ R_{jk}:=g_{ij}R^{i}_{k}=y^{i}R_{ijkl}y^{l},

where

R^{i}_{j~kl}=\frac{\delta\Gamma^{i}_{jl}}{\delta x^{k}}-\frac{\delta\Gamma^{i}_{jk}}{\delta x^{l}}+\Gamma^{i}_{km}\Gamma^{m}_{jl}-\Gamma^{i}_{lm}\Gamma^{m}_{jk}.

The non-Riemannian curvature tensor is defined by

\displaystyle P^{i}_{j~kl}=-F\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{jk}}{\partial y^{l}}=-F(G)^{i}_{y^{j}y^{k}y^{l}}.

(2.2)

For a vector
v=v^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
satisfying
g_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}=1
and
g_{ij}y^{i}v^{j}=0
, the flag curvature of
(N,F)
is defined by

K(y,v):=F^{-2}R_{jk}v^{j}v^{k}.

For
X=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in\Gamma(TN)
, the covariant derivative of
X
along
v=v^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in T_{x}N
with respect to a reference vector
w\in T_{x}N\setminus\{0\}
is defined by

\nabla^{w}_{v}X(x)=\{v^{j}\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}(x)+\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(w)v^{j}X^{k}(x)\}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}.

Then (
2.1
) can be expressed by
\nabla^{\dot{\gamma}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}\equiv 0
. If a vector field
J(s)
along
\gamma(s)
satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{\dot{\gamma}}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{\dot{\gamma}}J+\textbf{R}_{\dot{\gamma}}(J)=0,

(2.3)

then
J(s)
is called the
Jacobi field
along
\gamma(s)
, where
\textbf{R}_{y}:=R^{i}_{k}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\otimes dx^{k}
. Let
(N,F)
be a forward complete Finsler space. For
y\in S_{x}N=\{y\in TN\setminus\{0\}\big|F(y)=1\}
, define
\textbf{c}_{y}>0
to be the first number
d>0
such that there is a Jacobi field
J(s)
along
\gamma(s)=\exp_{x}(sy)
,
0<s<d
, satisfying
J(0)=0=J(d)
.
\textbf{c}_{y}
is called the
conjugate value
of
y
. From Cartan-Hadamard Theorem, if
K\leq 0
,
\textbf{c}_{y}=\infty
. Let
\textbf{i}_{y}
be the supremum of
r>0
such that
\gamma(s)
is minimizing on
[0,r]
. We call
\mathcal{D}_{x}:=\{\exp_{x}(ty),0\leq t<\textbf{i}_{y},y\in S_{x}N\}
the
cut-domain
of
x

[
14
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}:TN\rightarrow T^{\ast}N
denote the
Legendre transformation
, satisfying
{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda y)=\lambda{\mathcal{L}}(y)
for all
\lambda>0,~y\in TN
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\xi)=F^{*}(\xi)[F^{*}]_{\xi^{i}}(\xi)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\ \ \forall\xi\in T^{*}N\setminus\{0\},\ \ \mathcal{L}^{-1}(0)=0,

where
F^{*}
is the dual metric of
F
. For a smooth function
f:N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the
gradient vector
of
f
at
x
is defined as
\nabla f(x)={\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(df(x))\in T_{x}N
. Set
\nabla^{2}f(x)=\nabla^{\nabla f}(\nabla f)(x)
for
x\in N_{f}
. The Hessian of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle\textrm{Hes}f(X,Y):=g_{\nabla f}(\nabla^{2}f(X),Y)=X(Yf)-(\nabla^{\nabla f}_{X}Y)f,

(2.4)

where
X,Y\in T_{x}N
,
\nabla^{2}f(x)=\nabla^{\nabla f}_{X}\nabla f\in T_{x}^{*}N\otimes T_{x}N
for
x\in N_{f}
.
The
Laplacian
of
f
can defined
by

\hat{\Delta}f=\textmd{tr}_{g_{{}_{\nabla f}}}(\nabla^{2}f).

(2.5)

And
Laplacian
of
f
with respect to the volume form
d\mu=\sigma(x)dx=\sigma(x)dx^{1}\wedge dx^{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}
can be represented as

\Delta_{\sigma}f=\textmd{div}_{\sigma}(\nabla f)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(\sigma g^{ij}(\nabla f)f_{j})=\hat{\Delta}f-\textbf{S}(\nabla f),

(2.6)

where

\textbf{S}(x,y)=\frac{\partial G^{i}}{\partial y^{i}}-y^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}(\ln\sigma(x))

is the
\mathbf{S}
-curvature

[
14
]
.

2.2
Anisotropic hypersurfaces

Let
(N,F)
be an
n
-dimensional Finsler manifold and
\phi:M\to(N,F)
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional immersion.
Set
(x,y)\in TN
with local coordinate
(x^{i},y^{i})
and
(u,v)\in TM
with local coordinate
(u^{a},v^{a})
, we have

\displaystyle x^{i}=\phi^{i}(u),\ \ \ \ y^{i}=\phi^{i}_{a}v^{a},\ \ \ \ \phi^{i}_{a}=\frac{\partial\phi^{i}}{\partial u^{a}}.

(2.7)

For simplicity, we will denote
d\phi(x)
by
x
and
d\phi X
by
X
. Define

\mathcal{V}(M)=\{(x,\xi)~|~x\in M,\xi\in T_{x}^{*}N,\xi(X)=0,\forall X\in T_{x}M\},

which is called the

normal bundle
of
M

[
15
]
. Set
\tau\in\mathcal{N}M={\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(\mathcal{V}(M))\subset TN
, which satisfies

for any

.
\tau
is called a

of

. Since

is a hypersurface in

, there exists a global unit normal vector field

is the unit normal bundle. In general,
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(-\nu)\neq-\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\nu)
. We call the Riemannian manifold
(M,\hat{g}=\phi^{*}g_{\textbf{n}})
an
anisotropic hypersurface
of
(N,F)
.

The shape operator
{A}_{\textbf{n}}:T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
is defined by

\displaystyle{A}_{\textbf{n}}X=-\nabla^{\textbf{n}}_{X}\textbf{n}.

(2.8)

We call the eigenvalues of
{A}_{\textbf{n}}
,
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1}
, the principal curvatures of
M
with respect to
n
. If
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\cdots=\kappa_{n-1}
, we call

an

Let
\rho
be a
C^{\infty}
distance function on an open subset

and
M_{s}:=\rho^{-1}(s)\subset U
such that
M_{0}=M\cap U\neq\varnothing
. Let
\gamma(s)
be a unit speed normal geodesic of the regular level hypersurface
M_{s}
. Denote
T(s)=\nabla\rho\in\mathcal{N}^{0}M_{s}
and
A_{T(s)}
as the shape operator of
M_{s}
. For
X\in TM_{s}
, define

\dot{A}_{T(s)}X=\nabla^{T(s)}_{T(s)}(A_{T(s)}X)-A_{T(s)}(\nabla^{T(s)}_{T(s)}X).

From
[
14
]
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{A}_{T(s)}=A_{T(s)}^{2}+\textbf{R}_{T(s)}.

(2.9)

(
2.9
) is called the
Riccati equation
.

3.1
Transnormal functions and focal varieties

For

M_{-}
)

(resp.

(resp.

(resp.

4.1
The number of distinct principal curvatures

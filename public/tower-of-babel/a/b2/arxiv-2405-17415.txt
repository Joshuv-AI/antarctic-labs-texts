Splitting aspects of holomorphic distributions with locally free tangent sheaf
Raphael Constant da Costa

Abstract
. In this work, we mainly deal with a two-dimensional singular holomorphic distribution 𝒟 \mathcal{D} defined on M M , in the two situations M = ℙ n M=\mathbb{P}^{n} or M = ( ℂ n , 0 ) M=(\mathbb{C}^{n},0) , tangent to a one-dimensional foliation 𝒢 \mathcal{G} on M M , and whose tangent sheaf T 𝒟 {T_{\mathcal{D}}} is locally free. We provide sufficient conditions on 𝒢 \mathcal{G} so that there is another one-dimensional foliation ℋ \mathcal{H} on M M tangent to 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , such that their respective tangent sheaves satisfy the splitting relation T 𝒟 = T 𝒢 ⊕ T ℋ {T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}} . As an application, we show that if ℱ \mathcal{F} is a codimension one holomorphic foliation on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} with locally free tangent sheaf and tangent to a nontrivial holomorphic vector field on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} , then T ℱ {T_{\mathcal{F}}} splits. Some division results for vector fields and differential forms are also obtained.

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a complex manifold of dimension
n
and
1\leq k<n
. A singular holomorphic distribution
\mathcal{D}
of dimension
k
(or codimension
n-k
) can be defined by a family
\{(U_{j},\omega_{j})\}_{j\in J}
where:

(1)

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is an open covering of
M
.

(2)

\omega_{j}\not\equiv 0
is a holomorphic (
n-k
)-form defined on the open set
U_{j}
, which is locally decomposable outside the singular set
\sing(\omega_{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{z\in U_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega_{j}(z)=0\}
. This means that for each point
z\in U_{j}
, where
\omega_{j}(z)\neq 0
, there exist a neighborhood
V\subset U_{j}
of
z
and holomorphic
1
-forms
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}
on
V
such that

(1.1)

{\omega_{j}}_{|V}=\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{n-k}\,.

(3)

If
U_{ij}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
, there is a nonzero holomorphic function defined on
U_{ij}
,
h_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}(U_{ij})
, satisfying

(1.2)

{\omega_{i}}_{|U_{ij}}=h_{ij}{\omega_{j}}_{|U_{ij}}\,.

(4)

\codim(\sing(\omega_{j}))\geq 2,\forall j\in J
.

The singular set of
\mathcal{D}
is the analytic subset of
M
given by

\sing(\mathcal{D})=\bigcup_{j}\{z\in M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega_{j}(z)=0\}\,.

Note that the definition of
\sing(\mathcal{D})
is possible due to (
1.2
). If
\sing(\mathcal{D})=\emptyset
we say that the distribution
\mathcal{D}
is regular.

If
T_{p}M
denotes the holomorphic tangent space of
M
at
p\in M
, it follows that we can define on
M\setminus\sing(\mathcal{D})
a distribution of
k
-planes

\mathcal{D}(p)=\{v\in T_{p}M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i_{v}\omega_{j}(p)=0\},

whenever
p\in U_{j}\setminus\sing(\mathcal{D})
. In fact, if
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n-k}
are as in (
1.1
), we have

\mathcal{D}(p)=\bigcap_{1\leq l\leq n-k}\Ker(\eta_{l}(p))\,.

About decomposition (
1.1
), if additionally for every
j\in J
the following Frobenius integrability condition is satisfied on
V
:

(1.3)

d\eta_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}=0,i=1,\ldots,n-k,

we say that the distribution
\mathcal{D}
is integrable, and call it a holomorphic singular foliation of dimension
k
on
M
. In this case we usually denote such a distribution by
\mathcal{F}
.

A foliation
\mathcal{F}
provides a decomposition of
M\setminus\sing(\mathcal{F})
into a union of disjoint connected immersed submanifolds
\{\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
of dimension
k
, called the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, in such a way that if
p\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
, then the tangent space to
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
at
p
is given by
T_{p}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{D}(p)
.

In the case
k=1
, it is well known that a one-dimensional distribution is always integrable, see also Example
2.5
below. Alternatively, it can be given by a collection of vector fields
v_{j}
defined on the open sets
U_{j}
belonging to an open covering
\mathcal{U}
of
M
, with
\codim(\sing(v_{j}))\geq 2
, and nonzero holomorphic functions
g_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}(U_{ij})
satisfying

{v_{i}}_{|U_{ij}}=g_{ij}{v_{j}}_{|U_{ij}}\,.

The integral curves of all vector fields
v_{j}
are glued together forming the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 1.1
.

A meromorphic vector field
v
on a complex manifold
M
, possibly with codimension one zeros, defines a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
as follows: For every
p\in M
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
such that
v=f\tilde{v}_{p}
, where
f
is a meromorphic function and
\tilde{v}_{p}
is a holomorphic vector field, both defined on
U_{p}
, and
\codim(\sing(\tilde{v}_{p}))\geq 2
. The one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
is given by
\{(U_{p},\tilde{v}_{p})\}_{p\in M}
. Of course,
v
defines a one-dimensional (singular) foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
M\setminus\left(\supp(D_{0})\cup\supp(D_{\infty})\right)
, where
\supp(D_{0})
and
\supp(D_{\infty})
denote the support of the divisor of zeros and poles of
v
, respectively. We say that
\mathcal{G}
is the extension of
\mathcal{H}
to
M
.

Denote by
{T_{M}}
the tangent sheaf of
M
. The tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
, is the subsheaf of
{T_{M}}
whose sections over an open set
U\subset M
is given by

{T_{\mathcal{D}}}(U)=\{v\in{T_{M}}(U)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i_{v}\omega_{j}=0\text{ on }U\cap U_{j}\}\,.

Note that
({T_{\mathcal{D}}})_{p}
, the stalk of
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
at
p\in M
, consists of germs of vector fields at
p
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Recall that a theorem due to Oka guarantees that a holomorphic sheaf on
M
is coherent if it is given locally as the kernel of a morphism between free holomorphic sheaves. After writing the definition of
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
in local coordinates, we see that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is given locally by a finite intersection of the kernels of morphisms between free holomorphic sheaves. This implies that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is coherent, since it is given locally by a finite intersection of coherent sheaves. From the definition of
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
, a straightforward verification shows that the coherent sheaf
{T_{M}}/{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is torsion-free.

For a
k
-dimensional distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, there is an important discrete invariant called the degree of the distribution, denoted by
\deg(\mathcal{D})
. By definition it is the degree of the locus of tangency of
\mathcal{D}
with a generic linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{n-k}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. The distribution
\mathcal{D}
can be defined in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
by a homogeneous polynomial (
n-k
)-form

\Omega=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-k}\leq n}A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-k}}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i_{n-k}},

which is locally decomposable outside the singular set
\sing(\Omega)
, satisfying
i_{R}\Omega=0
, where

R=x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\cdots+x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}

is the radial vector field,
A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-k}}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\deg(\mathcal{D})+1
and
\codim(\sing(\Omega))\geq 2
, see for example
[
9
]
. The integrability of
\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to the integrability of
\Omega
.

The singular set of
\mathcal{D}
is the image, under the canonical projection
\Pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
, of the singular set of
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. Similarly, the
k
-planes of the distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
are the images, under the same map, of the (
k+1
)-planes on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
induced by
\Omega
. If
\mathcal{D}
is integrable, a similar result holds for its leaves.

When
k=1
, alternatively a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
can be defined in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
by the vector field

V=V_{0}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}+\cdots+V_{n}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}},

where
V_{i}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
,
i=0,\ldots,n
, is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\deg(\mathcal{F})
. Although necessary, the condition
\codim(\sing(V))\geq 2
is not sufficient to define such a one-dimensional foliation. In fact,
\sing(\mathcal{F})
is the image, under
\Pi
, of the set

\{p\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V(p)\wedge R(p)=0\}\,.

1.1.
Notation

We will denote by:

-

M
: a complex manifold. Most of the time we will have
M=\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.

-

(M,p)
: the germ of
M
at a point
p\in M
. Most of the time we will deal with
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
.

-

\Omega^{k}(U)
and
\mathcal{X}(U)
: the sets of holomorphic
k
-forms and holomorphic vector fields on
U
, respectively, where
U
is
(M,p)
or an open set of
M
.

-

\mathcal{O}_{M,p}
: the ring of germs of holomorphic functions defined at a point
p\in M
.

1.2.
Distributions on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with split tangent sheaf

Given a distribution
\mathcal{D}
of dimension
k
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, we say that the tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{D}
splits if

{T_{\mathcal{D}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(e_{i}),

for some integers
e_{i}
. This is equivalent to
\mathcal{D}
being induced by the homogeneous (
n-k
)-form on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

(1.4)

\Omega=i_{X_{1}}\cdots i_{X_{k}}i_{R}(dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}),

where for
i=1,\ldots,k
,
X_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial vector field on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
of degree
d_{i}=1-e_{i}
. Note that
X_{i}
defines a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}_{i}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
with
\deg(\mathcal{G}_{i})=d_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
. In particular,
\deg(\mathcal{D})=\deg(\mathcal{G}_{1})+\cdots+\deg(\mathcal{G}_{k})
. The reader can find more details in
[
9
]
, where it deals with foliations, but the same procedures apply to distributions.

In general, the tangent sheaf of a foliation is not locally free. Given a foliation with locally free tangent sheaf, a very interesting question is to determine whether its tangent sheaf splits or not. The importance of this matter can be seen in
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
, where it is proved that some foliations with a special type of singular set and split tangent sheaf are stable under small deformations. This make it possible to obtain irreducible components of the space of foliations on projective spaces, which can also be found in
[
17
]
and
[
10
]
.

For codimension one foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, in
[
14
]
it was proved that the tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{F}
is locally free if and only if
\sing(\mathcal{F})
(considered as a scheme) is a curve. Furthermore,
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits if and only if
\sing(\mathcal{F})
is an
arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay
curve. These results were generalized later in
[
8
]
for higher dimensions.

The following question was posed in
[
1
]
:

Problem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, where
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
is locally free. Does
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
split?

In
[
4
]
, the authors provide sufficient conditions for a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with locally free tangent sheaf to split. They use the relation between the Kupka scheme and the unfoldings ideal associated to a codimension one foliation. They also show that if
\mathcal{F}
is a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
\sing(\mathcal{F})
is smooth of dimension
(n-2)
, then
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits (in fact it is a degree
0
rational foliation), generalizing
[
3
, Theorem 3.11]
. In
[
2
]
, the authors show that if
\mathcal{D}
is a two-dimensional distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{4}
of degree at most
2
with locally free tangent sheaf such that
\sing(\mathcal{D})
(considered as a scheme) has pure dimension one, then
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
splits.

Regarding Problem
1
, it is well known that there are nonintegrable distributions whose tangent sheaf is locally free and does not split. We can take, for example, a regular distribution of codimension one and degree
0
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, i.e., a holomorphic contact structure on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Very recently, it was given in
[
13
]
an answer (in the negative) to Problem 1.

1.3.
Statement of the main results

We know that there are foliations
\mathcal{F}
on projective spaces such that
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
is locally free but
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
does not split, see
[
13
]
. Given a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with locally free tangent sheaf, we would like to find extra conditions for
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
to split. Although part of our original analysis was for codimension
1
foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, it naturally extends to distributions of dimension
2
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Most of the time we deal with the following:

Problem 2
.

Let
M
represent
\mathbb{P}^{n}
or
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. Given a two-dimensional distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
M
with locally free tangent sheaf, and a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
M
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
, under what conditions can we guarantee that there is another one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
M
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
so that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}}
?

We start by approaching Problem 2 in local terms.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
v
be a germ of holomorphic vector field defined at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Assume that
0
is a singularity of
v
, i.e.,
v(0)=0
. We define the linear rank of
v
(at
0
) as the rank of the linear transformation
Dv(0)
.

If
v=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\hat{v}_{i}
,
\deg(v_{i})=i
, denotes the Taylor expansion of
v
, observe that the linear rank of
v
is at most
1
if and only if
\hat{v}_{1}=fu
, where
f
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree one in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
u
is a germ of constant vector field defined at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a one-dimensional foliation on a complex manifold
M
and
p\in\sing(\mathcal{G})
. Assume that
V\subset M
is an open set containing
p
,
v
is a holomorphic vector field defining
\mathcal{G}
on
V
and
(U,\varphi)
is a holomorphic chart containing
p
such that
\varphi(p)=0
. We define the linear rank of
\mathcal{G}
at
p
as the rank of
(\varphi_{|U\cap V})_{*}v_{|U\cap V}
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
\sing(\mathcal{G})\neq\emptyset
, we also define the linear rank of
\mathcal{G}
as the least possible value of the rank of
\mathcal{G}
at
p
for
p\in\sing(\mathcal{G})
.

The definition of the linear rank of
\mathcal{G}
at
p\in\sing(\mathcal{G})
is independent of the vector field defining
\mathcal{G}
on an neighborhood of
p
and the holomorphic chart centered at
p
.

Remark 1.5
.

If
M=U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a Stein open set, in particular if it is a polydisc, since
H^{1}(U,\mathcal{O}^{*})=0
a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
U
can be defined by a single (
n-k
)-form
\omega
on
U
. When we deal with germs of distributions defined at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, such germs can be represented by a (
n-k
)-form
\omega
defined on a sufficiently small polydisc
U
containing
0
. By a similar reason, a germ of one-dimensional foliation can be represented by a single vector field on a sufficiently small polydisc containing
0
.

The following results provide answers to Problem 2, the first one being in local terms.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a germ of two-dimensional distribution at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with locally free tangent sheaf, defined by the germ of (
n-2
)-form
\omega
. Let also
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a germ of vector field such that
X(0)=0
, tangent to
\mathcal{D}
and with linear rank at least
2
. Then exactly one of the following assertions holds:

(1)

There exist germs of vector field
Y
and
n
-form
\Theta
defined at
0
such that
\omega=i_{X}i_{Y}(\Theta)
, which is equivalent to
\sing(X)\subset\sing(\mathcal{D})
.

(2)

The germ of distribution
\mathcal{D}
is regular. In this case the linear rank of
X
is
2
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a two-dimensional distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, with locally free tangent sheaf. Assume that
\mathcal{D}
is tangent to a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
with linear rank at least
2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Every irreducible component of
\sing(\mathcal{G})
intersects
\sing(\mathcal{D})
.

(2)

Every irreducible component of
\sing(\mathcal{G})
of dimension
n-2
intersects
\sing(\mathcal{D})
.

(3)

\sing(\mathcal{G})\subset\sing(\mathcal{D})
.

(4)

There is a one dimensional foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{D}
is a two-dimensional distribution with locally free tangent sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{n},n\geq 4
, such that
\dim(\sing(\mathcal{D}))\geq 2
and
\mathcal{D}
is tangent to a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
with linear rank at least
2
, then
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
splits.

Regarding Theorem
B
, there is a stronger version if we assume that the linear rank of
\mathcal{G}
is at least
3
, see Theorem
4.7
below. As an application of the results obtained, we have the following theorem.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one singular holomorphic foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, with locally free tangent sheaf and tangent to a nontrivial holomorphic vector field on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Then
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits.

For a codimension one distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with locally free tangent sheaf, we also show that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
splits, provided that
\deg(\mathcal{D})\geq 1
and
\mathcal{D}
is tangent to a nonnilpotent holomorphic vector field on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, see Corollary
6.4
.

In section
5
, we generalize some of the results obtained for two-dimensional distributions. The reason there is a separate section is that in some cases it is simpler to write the proofs for two-dimensional distributions, and in the generalizations we have essentially the same ideas. Furthermore, most of the results for two-dimensional distributions imply that their tangent sheaves split, and the same does not happen for other cases. Compare, for example, Theorems
4.7
and
5.4
. Finally, our computations lead us to some division results involving vector fields and differential forms. See Corollaries
4.4
and
5.2
and Propositions
5.5
and
5.7
. They are easily verifiable, in the sense that it is equivalent to finding the rank of a linear transformation.

2.
Distributions with locally free tangent sheaf

In this section we present some results related to distributions with locally free tangent sheaf. We believe they are well known, but we are not aware of any reference for them, at least as they are stated.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k
-dimensional distribution on a polydisc
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, defined by the (
n-k
)-form
\omega
on
U
. Let also
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
be vector fields on
U
, defining the one-dimensional foliations
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{k}
, respectively. Then the following are equivalent:

a)

{T_{\mathcal{D}}}=T_{{\mathcal{G}_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{{\mathcal{G}_{k}}}
.

b)

\omega=i_{X_{1}}\cdots i_{X_{k}}(\Theta)
, for some
n
-form
\Theta
on
U
.

Proof.

Assume first that b) holds. Given
p\in U
, let
Z
be a germ of vector field around
p
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
, defined on a sufficiently small open set
V\subset U
. As in particular

\omega=i_{X_{1}}\cdots i_{X_{k}}(\Theta)

on
V
, from
i_{Z}\omega=0
we conclude that there unique holomorphic functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
defined on
V\setminus\sing(\omega)
such that

Z=f_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+f_{k}X_{k}\,.

Since
\codim(\sing(\omega))\geq 2
, it follows from Hartog’s Theorem that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
can be extended to
V
. We conclude that

({T_{\mathcal{D}}})_{p}=(T_{{\mathcal{G}_{1}}})_{p}\oplus\cdots\oplus(T_{{\mathcal{G}_{k}}})_{p}\,.

As
p\in U
is arbitrary, we obtain that

{T_{\mathcal{D}}}=T_{{\mathcal{G}_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{{\mathcal{G}_{k}}}\,.

Now, assume that a) holds. Given a
n
-form
\Lambda
on
U
, it follows that

(2.1)

i_{X_{1}}\cdots i_{X_{k}}(\Lambda)=h\omega,

for some holomorphic function
h
on
U
. We claim that
h
has no zeros in
U
, and we conclude by setting
\Theta=\Lambda/h
. In fact, if
h
has some zero in
U
, since
\codim(\sing(\omega))\geq 2
we can take
p\in U
such that
h(p)=0
e
\omega(p)\neq 0
. It follows from (
2.1
) that

X_{1}(p)\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k}(p)=0,

which contradicts
\omega(p)\neq 0
, since the germs of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
at
p
are the generators of
({T_{\mathcal{D}}})_{p}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Arguing in a similar way to the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we can conclude that given a complex manifold
M
,
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathcal{X}(M)
and
\Theta\in\Omega^{n}(M)
, by setting
\omega=i_{X_{1}}\cdots i_{X_{k}}(\Theta)
, if
\codim(\sing(\omega))\geq 2
and
X\in\mathcal{X}(M)
is such that
i_{X}(\omega)=0
, then there are unique holomorphic functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}
on
M
such that
X=f_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+f_{k}X_{k}
. We will use this fact a few times in the sequel.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k
-dimensional distribution on a complex manifold
M
. Then the following are equivalent:

a)

{T_{\mathcal{D}}}

3.
Some division properties

Classically, given germs of holomorphic forms
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, we say that
\alpha
is divisible by
\omega
if there exists
\beta\in\Omega^{k-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
\alpha=\omega\wedge\beta
, and it is clearly necessary that
\omega\wedge\alpha=0
. One might ask when this condition is also sufficient, which leads to the following:

division property by
k
-forms
We say that
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
satisfies the division property by
k
-forms, if for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
\omega\wedge\alpha=0
then there exists
\beta\in\Omega^{k-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
\alpha=\omega\wedge\beta
(cf.
[
20
]
and
[
7
]
).

The following result is known as the De Rham-Saito Division Lemma for
1
-forms. A more general version can be found in
[
22
]
and
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a germ of
1
-form such that
\codim(\sing(\omega))\geq p+1
, where
p
is a nonnegative integer. Then
\omega
satisfies the division property by
k
-forms for
k\leq p
.

Lemma
3.1
has a dual version in terms of vector fields. In a similar way, we say that
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
has the division property by
k
-forms if for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
i_{X}(\alpha)=0
then there exists
\beta\in\Omega^{k+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
\alpha=i_{X}(\beta)
. Making use of the Hodge star operator, we have the following result, see
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a germ of vector field such that
\codim(\sing(X))\geq p+1
, where
p
is a nonnegative integer. Then
X
satisfies the division property by
k
-forms for
n-p\leq k\leq n-1
. In particular, if
\codim(\sing(X))\geq 3
then
X
satisfies the division property by
k
-forms for
k=n-2
and
k=n-1
.

The next definition is simply a complement to the property of vector field division by
k
-forms.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be germs of
k
-form and vector field, respectively. We say that
X
divides
\omega
if there is
\Theta\in\Omega^{k+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
\omega=i_{X}(\Theta)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{D}
be germs of one-dimensional foliation and
k
-dimensional distribution, both defined on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, given by
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{n-k}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, respectively. If
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{n},0}
are germs of holomorphic functions satisfying

, it follows that
X
divides
\omega
if and only if
fX
divides
g\omega
. If so, this is the reason why we will also say that
\mathcal{G}
divides
\mathcal{D}
, or
X
divides
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a singular distribution on a complex manifold
M
, and
\mathcal{G}
a one-dimensional foliation (resp. a holomorphic vector field

) on

\mathcal{D}
. We say that
\mathcal{G}
(resp.

) locally divides

at

if for some (any) holomorphic chart

containing

we have

(resp.
\varphi_{*}(v_{|U})
) divides
\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{D}_{|U})
at

. If
\mathcal{G}
(resp.
v
) locally divides
\mathcal{D}
at every

we say that
\mathcal{G}
(resp.
v
) locally divides
\mathcal{D}
.

Given a holomorphic vector field
v
on a complex manifold

, let
\mathcal{G}
be the one-dimensional foliation on
M

, see Remark
1.1
. For a singular distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
M
, note that

locally divides

if and only if

and

locally divides

at

denotes the germ of

at

.

From now on we will mainly deal with two-dimensional distributions.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{n-2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
and
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be germs of (
n-2
)-form and vector field at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose that
X
divides
\omega
. As every element
\Theta\in\Omega^{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
is of the form
\Theta=i_{Y}(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})
, for some
Y\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, it follows that
\omega
is of the form
\omega=i_{X}i_{Y}(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})
. In particular, given a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
and a two-dimensional distribution
\mathcal{D}
, both defined on a complex manifold
M
, if
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
then it follows from Proposition
2.3
that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is locally free.

It is well known that a rank two vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, contains no proper subbundles, unless it splits. See, for example,
[
21
, Lemma 2.2.4]
. The analog for two-dimensional distributions and one-dimensional subfoliations would be the following result, also inspired by
[
11
, Lemma 4.15]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a two-dimensional distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Assume that there exists a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which locally divides
\mathcal{D}
. Then there is a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
such that the tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{D}
splits as
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
, by Remark
3.5
it follows that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is a locally free subsheaf of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Denote by
e
the degree of the one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{G}
, in such a way that
{T_{\mathcal{G}}}\cong\mathcal{O}(1-e)
. Note that
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
at
p\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
means that

({T_{\mathcal{D}}})_{p}=({T_{\mathcal{G}}})_{p}\oplus({T_{\mathcal{G}_{1}}})_{p},

where
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is a one-dimensional foliation tangent to
\mathcal{D}
defined on a neighborhood of
p
, which implies that
({T_{\mathcal{D}}}/{T_{\mathcal{G}}})_{p}
is free. By our hypothesis, it follows that the inclusion of
{T_{\mathcal{G}}}
into
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
has locally free cokernel. Making no distinction between a vector bundle and the associated locally free sheaf, at this point we could conclude that
{T_{\mathcal{G}}}
can be seen as a subbundle of
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
, and then apply
[
21
, Lemma 2.2.4]
. Instead, we make the following computation for a sake of completeness. Therefore we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-e)\to{T_{\mathcal{D}}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(h)\to 0\,.

Since
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-h+1-e))=0
(no line-bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
has intermediate cohomology), this sequence splits showing that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-e)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(h)
. The factor
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(h)
corresponds to a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{H}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
satisfying
\deg(\mathcal{H})=1-h
. In particular
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}}
.
∎

In the situation of Proposition
3.6
, note that if
d=\deg(\mathcal{D})
and
e=\deg(\mathcal{G})
, then
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-e)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1+e-d)
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a two-dimensional distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and
\mathcal{G}
a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
. If
\codim(\sing(\mathcal{G}))\geq 3
then
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
. In particular
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}
splits.

Proof.

The distribution
\mathcal{D}
is given locally by (
n-2
)-forms. By the particular case of Corollary
3.2
, we have that
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
. From Proposition
3.6
the result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a two-dimensional distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and
\mathcal{G}
a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
. If
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
, then
\deg(\mathcal{G})\leq\deg(\mathcal{D})
. In particular if
\codim\sing(\mathcal{G})\geq 3
then
\deg(\mathcal{G})\leq\deg(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
locally divides
\mathcal{D}
, by Proposition
3.6
there is a is a one-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
tangent to
\mathcal{D}
such that
{T_{\mathcal{D}}}={T_{\mathcal{G}}}\oplus{T_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Hence

implies that
\deg(\mathcal{G})\leq\deg(\mathcal{D})
. If
\codim\sing(\mathcal{G})\geq 3

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{D}
and the result follows.
∎

Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a germ of two-dimensional distribution at

n-2
)-form

\mathcal{D}

(2)

The germ of distribution
\mathcal{D}

.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, there are

Next we denote by

If

or

\mathcal{D}
and it is clear that

of course

Recall that the singular set of a coherent sheaf

\sing(\mathcal{T})=\{p\in M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{T}_{p}\text{ is not }\mathcal{O}_{M,p}\text{-free}\},

at a point

is an analytic subset of

. Given a two-dimensional holomorphic distribution
\mathcal{D}
on

\mathcal{G}

the set of points

does not locally divide

at

\mathcal{S}
is a closed subset of

(clearly its complement is an open set). Next, we observe that it is an analytic subset of

locally divides

, which implies that

locally divides
\mathcal{D}
at

is torsion-free (because

Lemma 4.8

For a germ of vector field

Proof.

If

, where

Denote by

\mathcal{V}_{1}

\mathcal{V}

Since the germs of

at

(4.1)

f_{i}=h_{i1}g_{1}+\cdots+h_{ir}g_{r},i=1,\ldots,m\,.

Theorem C
.

\mathcal{F}

Let

R=\sum_{i=0}^{3}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},

is the image, under the canonical projection

be a polynomial
1
-form defining
\mathcal{F}
on an affine coordinate system
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that the hyperplane at infinity corresponds to such a hyperplane invariant by
\mathcal{F}
. The decomposition of
\omega
into homogeneous
1
-forms is given by

\omega=\hat{\omega}_{0}+\hat{\omega}_{1},\deg(\hat{\omega}_{i})=i,i=0,1\text{ and }\hat{\omega}_{1}\neq 0\,.

In this case, it is easy to produce a polynomial vector field
X\neq 0
on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
with degree at most
1
satisfying
i_{X}(\omega)=0
, which gives rise to
v\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},{T_{\mathcal{F}}})-\{0\}
.

If (2) holds, assume first that
\mathcal{F}
is in a logarithmic component. Then
\mathcal{F}
is a pencil of quadrics or it has an invariant hyperplane, see again
[
11
, Proposition 3.7]
. In the former case, the result follows from Theorem D of
[
13
]
, and in the latter case, let
\omega
be a polynomial
1
-form defining
\mathcal{F}
on an affine coordinate system as in (1). Since
\deg(\mathcal{F})=2
, we have

\omega=\hat{\omega}_{0}+\hat{\omega}_{1}+\hat{\omega}_{2},\deg(\hat{\omega}_{i})=i,i=0,1,2\text{ and }\hat{\omega}_{2}\neq 0\,.

The integrability of
\omega
is equivalent to
i_{Y}(\omega)=0
, where
Y
is the polynomial vector field satisfying

d\omega=i_{Y}(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz)\,.

If
Y\neq 0
, we have that
h_{0}(\mathcal{F})\geq 1
. Otherwise, it follows that
\omega=df
, where
f(x,y,z)
is a polynomial of degree
3
. There are three possibilities for
f
:

i)

f
is irreducible.

ii)

f=ph
,
p
is irreducible,
\deg(p)=2
and
\deg(h)=1
.

iii)

f=h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}
,
\deg(h_{i})=1,i=1,2,3
, and
h_{i}
and
h_{j}
are relatively prime, for
i\neq j
. The last condition is due to
\deg(\mathcal{F})=2
.

Note that item i) is excluded by our hypothesis. If ii) holds, after an affine transformation we can assume that
h=x
, then

\omega=pdx+xdp\,.

Since
p
is irreducible, we have that
p_{y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\neq 0
or
p_{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}\neq 0
. Then

X=p_{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-p_{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

provides an element in
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},{T_{\mathcal{F}}})-\{0\}
. Similarly, we can produce an element in
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},{T_{\mathcal{F}}})-\{0\}
if
f
is as in iii).

If
\deg(\mathcal{F})=2
and
\mathcal{F}
is not in a logarithmic component, then
\mathcal{F}
is a linear pull-back from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or it belongs to the exceptional component. In the latter case, using the notation of
[
6
]
, the exceptional component is the closure of the orbit of
\overline{\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)}
under
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{3})
. Since
h_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)})\geq 1
we conclude that
h_{0}(\mathcal{F})\geq 1
.

Finally, if (3) holds, due to the structure theorem for degree three foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
of
[
18
]
, we have that either
\mathcal{F}
is a linear pull-back from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, or there is a degree one foliation by algebraic curves tangent to
\mathcal{F}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We can conclude from Corollary
6.5
that if
\mathcal{F}
is a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with locally free tangent sheaf and not admitting an invariant hypersurface, then
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits provided that
\deg(\mathcal{F})\leq 3
.

So far, the known examples of codimension one foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
whose tangent sheaf is locally free and does not split can be found in Theorem E of
[
13
]
: For every
k\geq 0
there are rational foliations of type
(k+3,k+3)
satisfying such property. Thus all such examples have a rational first integral, and for
k=0
the respective foliation has degree
4
. Based on this and Corollary
6.5
, we finish by presenting the following problem:

Problem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
is locally free. If
\mathcal{F}
does not have an invariant hypersurface, is it true that
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits? More generally, if
\mathcal{F}
does not admit a rational first integral, is it true that
{T_{\mathcal{F}}}
splits?

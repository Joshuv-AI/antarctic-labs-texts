Quantum Inception Score
Akira Sone, Akira Tanji, Naoki Yamamoto

Abstract
Motivated by the great success of classical generative models in machine learning, enthusiastic exploration of their quantum version has recently started. To depart on this journey, it is important to develop a relevant metric to evaluate the quality of quantum generative models; in the classical case, one such example is the (classical) inception score (cIS). In this paper, as a natural extension of cIS, we propose the quantum inception score (qIS) for quantum generators. Importantly, qIS relates the quality to the Holevo information of the quantum channel that classifies a given dataset. In this context, we show several properties of qIS. First, qIS is greater than or equal to the corresponding cIS, which is defined through projection measurements on the system output. Second, the difference between qIS and cIS arises from the presence of quantum coherence, as characterized by the resource theory of asymmetry. Third, when a set of entangled generators is prepared, there exists a classifying process leading to the further enhancement of qIS. Fourth, we harness the quantum fluctuation theorem to characterize the physical limitation of qIS. Finally, we apply qIS to assess the quality of the one-dimensional spin chain model as a quantum generative model, with the quantum convolutional neural network as a quantum classifier, for the phase classification problem in the quantum many-body physics.

Second, the proposed qIS demonstrates that measurements on the quantum classifier can be one factor leading to quality degradation due to the destruction of quantum coherence preserved by the classifier.
Third, the entanglement of the generator’s output could lead to the further quality enhancement, which is coming from the the potential superadditivity of the communication capacity of the quantum classifier channel

II
Inception score

Let us first review the concept of IS based on Refs.
Barratt and Sharma
;
Salimans et al. 2016
.
Let
\mathcal{X}\equiv\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\cdots,x_{r-1}\}
be a dataset produced from an unknown probability distribution.
The aim of generative modeling is to construct a model
p_{\rm{in}}(x)
that approximates the unknown probability distribution producing
\mathcal{X}
.
Given a data
x_{i}
, we also aim to construct a classifier that produces a relevant label
y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}\equiv\{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{\ell-1}\}
, where
\ell
denotes the number of the labels; we model the classifier via the conditional probability
q_{\rm{out}}(y|x)
, which is usually constructed via a neural network. Note here that, in the generative models, we always have
\ell\leqslant r
.
This is because we usually assume that the number of labels characterizing the patterns of the encoded dataset is smaller than that of the number of the input data.
The marginal probability for the label data,
p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})
, is given by
p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})
.
Therefore, the
input
and
output
of the classifier are
p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})
and
p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})
, respectively.

Then, the IS of
p_{\rm{in}}(x)
relative to
q_{\rm{out}}(y|x)
is defined by

\displaystyle{\xi\equiv\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})D(q_{\rm{out}}(y|x_{i})\,\|\,p_{\rm{out}}(y))\right),}

(1)

where

\displaystyle{D(q_{\rm{out}}(y|x_{i})\,\|\,p_{\rm{out}}(y))\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})\ln\frac{q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})}{p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})}}

(2)

is the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence of the conditional label probability
q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})
with respect to
p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})
. Note that
\ln\xi
can be expressed as
\ln\xi=\sum_{i}p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})\sum_{j}q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})\ln q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})-\sum_{j}p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})\ln p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})
, that is, the sum of the expected negative Shannon entropy of
q_{\rm{out}}(y|x)
and the entropy of
p_{\rm{out}}(y)
.
The former quantifies the accuracy of the label assigned to the data and the latter does the diversity of the data, implying that a generative model with bigger IS may cast as a high-quality data generator.
Therefore, in this scenario, the primary objective is to construct generative models, with the help of relevant design of the classifier, that achieves the higher IS; for this reason, IS has been often used for GAN.

III
Quantum inception score

We consider a general setup of quantum generative model with a system described by
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{S}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S})
denote the set of density operators acting on
\mathcal{H}_{S}
.
Suppose that the generator encodes a classical data (or a latent variable)
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
to a quantum state
\rho_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S})
, for
i=0,1,2,\cdots,(r-1)
. Here, note that
\{\rho_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})\}_{i=0}^{r-1}
are the input states of the quantum classifier.

As in the usual machine learning scenario that tries to solve problems by encoding data into a larger dimensional space, we assume
d\geqslant r
.
The encoded quantum states are then processed by a completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime}
denotes the
d^{\prime}
-dimensional Hilbert space of the output system.
Thus the output state
\rho_{\rm{out}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime})
is related to the input as
\rho_{\rm{out}}=\Phi(\rho_{\text{in}})
.

Now, let us write the input state
\rho_{\text{in}}
as an ensemble of
\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S})
encoding the classical input data
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
sampled from a probability distribution
p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{in}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})}\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i}).

(3)

Note that in general
\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})
does not necessarily commute with
\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i^{\prime}})
when
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Then, the output state becomes

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm{out}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})}\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\,,

(4)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\equiv\Phi(\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})).

(5)

Let us write the ensemble of the output state as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\equiv\left\{{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})},\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\right\}_{i=0}^{r-1}\,.

(6)

Because the supports of
\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}
and
\rho_{\rm{out}}
satisfy
\text{supp}(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)})\subseteq\text{supp}(\rho_{\rm{out}})
, the quantum relative entropy
S(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\,\|\,\rho_{\rm{out}})\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\ln\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}]-{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\ln\rho_{\rm{out}}]
takes a finite value.
Then the Holevo information
\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})
of the output ensemble is defined as
Holevo 1973
;
Schumacher and Westmoreland 2001

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})}S(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\,\|\,\rho_{\rm{out}})\,.

(7)

The Holevo-Schumacher-Westmoreland (HSW) capacity (or Holevo capacity)
Hausladen et al. 1996
;
Schumacher and Westmoreland 1997
;
Holevo 1998
;
King and Ruskai 2001
;
Holevo et al. 1999
;
Siudzińska 2020
;
Lee et al. 2015
is defined as the maximization of the Holevo information over all the possible
p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})
and
\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})
:

\displaystyle\chi_{\max}(\Phi)\equiv\max_{\{{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})},\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})\}}\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\,.

(8)

In the most generic scenario, the maximal transmittable amount of classical information through the quantum channel
\Phi
is measured by the regularized classical capacity
C(\Phi)

Holevo et al. 1999
;
Siudzińska 2020
;
Lee et al. 2015
;
Gyongyosi et al. 2018
;
Hastings 2009

\displaystyle C(\Phi)\equiv\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\chi_{\max}(\Phi^{\otimes n})\,,

(9)

which is asymptotically achievable with infinite copies of
\Phi
with joint measurement, and we always have
Siudzińska 2020
;
Lee et al. 2015
;
Gyongyosi et al. 2018
;
Hastings 2009

\displaystyle C(\Phi)\geqslant\chi_{\max}(\Phi)\,.

(10)

From these facts, as our
first main result
, we define the qIS as follows.

Definition 1

(Quantum Inception Score)
.

The quantum inception score (qIS) is defined as

\displaystyle\xi_{q}\equiv\exp\left(\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\right)\,.

(11)

Additionally, the achievable maximum qIS is defined as

\displaystyle\Xi_{q}\equiv\exp(C(\Phi))\,,

(12)

which we refer to as the regularized qIS.

Suppose now that the task of quantum generator is to generate the optimal set of
\{{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})},\rho_{\text{in}}(x_{i})\}
to maximize
\xi_{q}
.
Then, because the asymptotically achievable maximal qIS is given by
\exp(C(\Phi))
, from Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
),
\exp(C(\Phi))
is regarded as an indicator of the best achievable quality by the quantum generator.
Adopting an example of the image recognition, these results are consistent to the intuition that having a greater volume of information enables the generator to achieve better balanced accuracy and diversity. Particularly, in the asymptotic setup,
when entangled input
\overline{\rho}_{\rm in}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\otimes n})
is allowed, there exists a CPTP map
\Phi
leading to the superadditivity of the classical capacity
C(\Phi)>\chi_{\max}(\Phi)

Hastings 2009
.
From Eq. (
12
), this results in the quantum advantage in the generative modeling achieved by using the entanglement as a resource.

IV
Relation between the qIS and cIS

Here, we explore the relation between the qIS and cIS.
By introducing projective measurements, we can recover the cIS as follow.
To classify the data into
\ell
labels, we assume
\ell=d^{\prime}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime})
. Let
\{|y_{j}\rangle\}_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime}
. Then, the rank-1 projective measurement onto the orthogonal state
|y_{j}\rangle
is given by
\Pi_{j}\equiv|y_{j}\rangle\!\langle y_{j}|
. Then, we have
\Pi_{j}\Pi_{k}=\Pi_{j}\delta_{jk}
with
\delta_{jk}
the Kronecker’s
\delta
. Also, the completeness relation
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Pi_{j}=\openone
holds, where
\openone
denotes the identity operator acting on
\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime}
.
Regarding the dimensions of the Hilbert spaces, hence, the meaningful setup is
d\geqslant r\geqslant\ell
. Therefore,
\Phi
belongs to the class of tree-like quantum classifiers composed of the hierarchical quantum circuits
Grant et al. 2018
;
Cong et al. 2019
;
Li and Deng 2022
;
Hur et al. 2022
;
Wrobel et al.
;
Chen et al. 2023
, where the compressed states are expected to carry the features of the encoded data
\mathcal{X}
.

For a state
\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{S}^{\prime})
, let us write the post-projection-measurement state as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\rho)\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Pi_{j}\rho\Pi_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\langle y_{j}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pty_{j}\rangle|y_{j}\rangle\!\langle y_{j}|\,,

(13)

which is also a dephasing map generating an incoherent state diagonal in the basis
\{|y_{j}\rangle\}_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
.
Then, the probability that the output state takes
|y_{j}\rangle
is given by
p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}\Pi_{j}]=\langle y_{j}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho_{\rm{out}}|\hskip 1.0pty_{j}\rangle
.
From Eqs. (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
), we have
{q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})}\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}\Pi_{j}]=\langle y_{j}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}|\hskip 1.0pty_{j}\rangle
.
Because
\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)})
and
\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}})
have the identical eigenbasis
\{|y_{j}\rangle\}_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
, we have
S(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)})\,\|\,\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}}))={D(q_{\rm{out}}(y|x_{i})\,\|\,p_{\rm{out}}(y))}
, meaning that the cIS in Eq. (
1
) can be recovered from Eq. (
11
) by projective measurements. Therefore, when we write the Holevo information of the projected output ensemble due to
\mathcal{P}
as

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})}S(\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)})\,\|\,\mathcal{P}(\rho_{\rm{out}}))\,,

(14)

the cIS dependent on the choice of
\mathcal{P}
can be written as

\displaystyle\xi_{c}(\mathcal{P})\equiv\exp\left(\chi_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\right)\,.

(15)

Extending to the positive operator-valued measures (POVMs)
\mathcal{M}\equiv\{E_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
, where
E_{j}\geqslant 0
is a POVM element satisfying
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}E_{j}=\openone
. Also, note that with
j
th POVM element, the probabilities are
{p_{\rm{out}}(y_{j})}\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}E_{j}]
and
{q_{\rm{out}}(y_{j}|x_{i})}\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}E_{j}]
. The maximum cIS can be given by using the
accessible information

Holevo 1973
;
Jozsa et al. 1994
;
Davies 1978
;
Watrous 2018
;
Vedral 2002

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{\rm{acc}}&(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\\
&\equiv\max_{\{\mathcal{M}\}}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i}){\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}E_{j}]\ln{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(i)}E_{j}]\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}E_{j}]\ln{\rm Tr}[\rho_{\rm{out}}E_{j}]\Bigg]\\
&=\max_{\{\mathcal{M}\}}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})D_{\mathcal{M}}(q_{\rm{out}}(y|x_{i})\,\|\,p_{\rm{out}}(y))\right]\,,\end{split}

(16)

which is the maximization of the classical mutual information over all possible POVMs
\{\mathcal{M}\}
. Here, we intentionally write
D_{\mathcal{M}}
to emphasize the dependence of the KL divergence on the choice of the POVMs. The Holevo theorem states
Holevo 1973

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\geqslant I_{\rm{acc}}(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})

(17)

with the equality if and only if

\displaystyle[\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(n)},\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(m)}]=0~(\forall n,\forall m)\,,

(18)

implying that
\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(n)}
and
\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(m)}
can be simultaneously diagonalized.

By defining the
accessible IS
as

\displaystyle\xi_{\rm{acc}}\equiv\exp(I_{\rm{acc}}(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}}))\,,

(19)

we can obtain our second main result as follows

Theorem 1
.

The inception scores,
\xi_{q}
,
\xi_{\rm{acc}}
, and
\xi_{c}(\mathcal{P})
, satisfy

\displaystyle\ell\geqslant\xi_{q}\geqslant\xi_{\rm{acc}}\geqslant\xi_{c}(\mathcal{P})~(\forall\mathcal{P})\,.

(20)

Proof.

First,
\xi_{q}\geqslant\xi_{\rm{acc}}
is the Holevo theorem itself. Second, because the projective measurements belong to the POVMs, from Eq. (
16
), we must have
\xi_{\rm{acc}}\geqslant\xi_{c}(\mathcal{P})
. Finally, from
\ln\ell\geqslant\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})
and Eq. (
11
), we have
\ell\geqslant\xi_{q}
. Therefore, we can obtain Eq. (
20
).
∎

Theorem
1
implies that performing the measurements on the classifier is a primary factor causing the difference between qIS and cIS.

V
Quantum coherence and quality

From Theorem
1
, we can intuitively infer that the difference between qIS and cIS arises due to the destruction of quantum coherence by the measurements. Indeed, this inference is correct.
In the following, we employ the resource theory of asymmetry (RTA) to provide a rigorous and formal analysis.

V.1
Review of resource theory of asymmetry

First, we briefly review the RTA
Lostaglio et al. 2015a
;
Lostaglio et al. 2015b
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
;
Marvian 2020
;
Marvian et al. 2016
;
Takagi 2019
;
Yamaguchi and Tajima 2023
;
Ahmadi et al. 2013
;
Gour and Spekkens 2008
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2013
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2014b
;
Gour et al. 2009
;
Marvian and Lloyd
;
Vaccaro et al. 2008
;
Gour and Spekkens 2008
, where the quantum coherence is regarded as a resource of breaking the group symmetry. Let
G
be a symmetry group, and
g
be the group element with its unitary representation
U_{g}:G\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
acting on a
D
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{G}(\mathcal{H})\equiv\{\sigma\,|\,U_{g}\sigma U_{g}^{\dagger}=\sigma,\forall g\in G,\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\}

(21)

be the set of the free states in the RTA, which are invariant under any unitary operations with respect to
g
. The free state satisfies the commutation relation
[\rho,U_{g}]=0~(\forall g)
and is called the
symmetric
state with respect to
G
, and
asymmetric
state otherwise, which becomes a resource state in the RTA.

A relevant set of free operations are the covariant operations
\Lambda

Bartlett and Wiseman 2003
with respect to
G
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\Lambda(U_{g}\rho U_{g}^{\dagger})=U_{g}\Lambda(\rho)U_{g}^{\dagger}~~(\forall g\in G,\forall\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))\,.

The covariant operation cannot generate the asymmetric states from the symmetric state and transform one asymmetric state to the other.

.

Proof.

We prove by taking the contraposition of the following statement

\xi_{q}=\xi_{\rm{acc}}\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{S}

For the second part, We consider a set of time translations

{p_{\rm{in}}(x_{i})}>0~(\forall i)

\displaystyle\langle A(\rho_{\rm{out}};\rho_{\rm{out}}^{(k)})\rangle=0~(\forall k)\Longrightarrow\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{S}\,.

This theorem demonstrates that the qIS is larger than the maximum cIS (i.e., the accessible IS) if and only if

has an asymmetry with respect to the

This theorem not only proves that this quality degradation occurs due to the destruction of quantum coherence by measurements, but it also captures the specific characteristics of this quantum coherence. It shows that the coherence destroyed by the optimal POVM, resulting in minimal quality degradation, is characterized by the asymmetry of
\rho_{\rm{out}}
with respect to the constituent states

Here we remark that we are not comparing quantum generative models with and without measurements.

VI
Impact of decoherence on quality

Finally, we discuss another primary factor causing the quality degradation of the quantum generative models.
From Theorem
2
, we can also expect that the pure dephasing (or decoherence) contributes to degrading the quality.
Here, we demonstrate that the quality degradation mechanism can be captured by the information-theoretic fluctuation theorems

;
Sone et al. 2023
;
Kafri and Deffner 2012

;

Fujitani and Suzuki 2010

is a random variable whose average is

is called quantum efficacy
2
2

2

For the origin of

regarding the Holevo information, refer to Eqs. (11) – (14) in Ref.
Kafri and Deffner 2012
.
and satisfies

.

From the Jensen’s inequality, we have
\chi(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})-\chi_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{E};\rho_{\rm{out}})\geqslant-\ln\gamma
.
Here, note that
\gamma
is strictly dependent on
\Phi
and the choice of the projective measurement

. Then, we can obtain our

as follows.

Theorem 3
.

The quantum inception score can be lower bounded by using quantum efficacy

\displaystyle\xi_{q}\geqslant\frac{\xi_{c}(\mathcal{P})}{\gamma}~~(0<\gamma\leqslant 1)\,.

(33)

Proof.

By definitions, we have

∎

\rho_{\rm{out}}

This analysis also enables us to interpret

VIII
Conclusion

We have proposed the quantum inception score as a quality measure of quantum generative models, and obtained the following three main claims by connecting to the Holevo information.
First, the performing measurements is one of the primary factors resulting in the quality
difference of the quantum generative models in QQ protocol in Fig.
1
because of the destruction of the quantum coherence preserved in the quantum classifier.
Second, the best quality can be further achieved with the entanglement of the generator’s output due to the potential superadditivity of the communication capacity. This can be regarded as the quantum advantage in the generative modeling by using the entanglement as a resource.
Third, the quality difference of quantum generative models is due to the decoherence in the classifier,
which can be quantified by the quantum efficacy emerging from the quantum fluctuation theorem.
We also show examples of utilizing the quantum inception score to evaluate the quality of the 1D spin-
1/2
chain as a generator, for the 2-class and 3-class classification of the quantum phase in the quantum many-body physics.

Finally we remark that, in the classical regime, inception score is not currently a widely used measure for assessing generative models because of its potential constraints in facilitating useful model comparison as pointed out in Ref.
Barratt and Sharma
.
These constraints could extend to the qIS in the similar manner.
Nonetheless, the results obtained in this paper based on qIS enable us to emphasize
the significance of further investigation of characterizing the quantum machine learning protocols from the fundamental perspectives, such as quantum information transmission and information thermodynamics.

Appendix A
Derivation of Eq. (
25
)

In the Appendix, we provide the proof details of Eq. (
25
). We discuss two cases: nondegenerate and degenerate
H
.

A.1
Nondegenerate
H

First, let us focus on the case that
H
is nondegenerate.
H
can be diagonalized as

\displaystyle H=\sum_{n=1}^{D}\omega_{n}|\omega_{n}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{n}|\,,

(48)

where
D
denotes the dimension of the Hilbert space and
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=1}^{D}
are all different from each other. Here,
\{|\omega_{n}\rangle\}_{n=1}^{D}
is an orthonormal basis of
H
. Defining,

\displaystyle\Delta\omega_{nm}\equiv\omega_{n}-\omega_{m},

(49)

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2T}&\int_{-T}^{T}dte^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt}\\
=&\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{n,m}\int_{-T}^{T}dte^{-i\Delta\omega_{nm}t}\langle\omega_{n}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{m}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{n}\rangle\langle\omega_{m}\hskip 1.0pt|\\
=&\sum_{n,m}\text{sinc}(\Delta\omega_{nm}T)\langle\omega_{n}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{m}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{n}\rangle\langle\omega_{m}\hskip 1.0pt|\\
=&\sum_{n=1}^{D}\langle\omega_{n}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{n}\rangle|\omega_{n}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{n}|\\
&+\sum_{n\neq m}\text{sinc}(T\Delta\omega_{nm})\langle\omega_{n}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{m}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{n}\rangle\langle\omega_{m}\hskip 1.0pt|\,,\end{split}

(50)

where we used
\text{sinc}(0)=1
. Then, by utilizing
\lim_{T\to\infty}\text{sinc}(T\Delta\omega_{nm})=0~(\Delta\omega_{nm}\neq 0)
, the second term vanishes when
T\to\infty
; therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)&=\lim_{T\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}dte^{-itH}\rho e^{itH}\right]\\
&=\sum_{n=1}^{D}\langle\omega_{n}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{n}\rangle|\omega_{n}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{n}|=\sum_{n=1}^{D}\Pi_{n}\rho\Pi_{n}\,,\end{split}

(51)

where
\Pi_{n}\equiv|\omega_{n}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{n}|
is a rank-1 projector onto the eigenstate
|\omega_{n}\rangle
of
H
.
This implies that
\mathcal{G}_{H}
corresponds to the dephasing map transferring
\rho
into a fully incoherent state
Baumgratz et al. 2014
diagonal in the eigenbasis
\{|\omega_{n}\rangle\}_{n=1}^{D}
of
H
. Then, the relative entropy of asymmetry
A(\rho;H)\equiv S\left(\rho\,\|\,\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)\right)
coincides with the relative entropy of coherence
Baumgratz et al. 2014
.

A.2
Degenerate
H

Next, let us discuss the case that
H
is degenerate. Again,
H
can be diagonalized as Eq. (
48
). Suppose that
H
has
\lambda
degenerate eigenvalues

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha_{1}},\omega_{\alpha_{2}},\cdots,\omega_{\alpha_{\lambda}}\,.

(52)

For each degenerate eigenvalue
\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}
, suppose that we have
k_{\mu}
orthonormal eigenbasis

\displaystyle\{|\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(1)}\rangle,|\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(2)}\rangle,\cdots,|\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(k_{\mu})}\rangle\}\,.

(53)

Defining

\displaystyle\overline{K}\equiv\sum_{\mu=1}^{\lambda}k_{\mu}\,,

(54)

the number of distinct nondegenerate eigenvalues is given by

\displaystyle K=D-\overline{K}\,.

(55)

Then,
L=K+\lambda<D
is the total number of distinct eigenvalues of
H
, and the Hilbert space can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{L}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\,,

(56)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
denotes the subspace spanned by the eigenstates of
H
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\omega_{\alpha}

\omega_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\Pi_{\omega_{\alpha}}\equiv|\omega_{\alpha}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{\alpha}|\,.

(57)

For the degenerate eigenvalue

\displaystyle\Pi_{\alpha_{\mu}}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{k_{\mu}}|\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu)}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu)}|\,,

so that the rank of the projector is
\text{rank}(\Pi_{\alpha_{\mu}})=k_{\mu}>1

\displaystyle\Omega\equiv\{\omega_{\alpha}\,|\,\omega_{\alpha}\neq\omega_{\beta},~\alpha\neq\beta\}

and the set of degenerate eigenvalues as

\displaystyle\Gamma\equiv\{\omega_{\alpha}\,|\,\omega_{\alpha}=\omega_{\beta},\alpha\neq\beta\}\,.

(60)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2T}&\int_{-T}^{T}dte^{-iHt}\rho e^{iHt}\\

&+\sum_{\omega_{\alpha},\omega_{\beta}\in\Omega}\text{sinc}(T\Delta\omega_{\alpha\beta})\langle\omega_{\alpha}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\beta}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\omega_{\beta}\hskip 1.0pt|\\
=&\sum_{\omega_{\alpha}\in\Omega}\langle\omega_{\alpha}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha}\rangle|\omega_{\alpha}\rangle\!\langle\omega_{\alpha}|\\
&+\sum_{\mu=1}^{\lambda}\sum_{\nu=1}^{k_{\mu}}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}=1}^{k_{\mu}}\langle\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu)}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu^{\prime})}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu)}\rangle\langle\omega_{\alpha_{\mu}}^{(\nu^{\prime})}\hskip 1.0pt|\\
&+\sum_{\omega_{\alpha},\omega_{\beta}\in\Omega}\text{sinc}(T\Delta\omega_{\alpha\beta})\langle\omega_{\alpha}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\beta}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\omega_{\beta}\hskip 1.0pt|\\

&+\sum_{\omega_{\alpha},\omega_{\beta}\in\Omega}\text{sinc}(T\Delta\omega_{\alpha\beta})\langle\omega_{\alpha}\hskip 1.0pt|\rho|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\beta}\rangle|\hskip 1.0pt\omega_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\omega_{\beta}\hskip 1.0pt|\,,\end{split}

(61)

where the third term vanishes as

\displaystyle\{\Pi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{L}\in\{\Pi_{\omega_{\alpha}}\}_{\omega_{\alpha}\in\Omega}\cup\{\Pi_{\alpha_{\mu}}\}_{\mu=1}^{\lambda}

onto the subspace of the eigenbasis of

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{H}(\rho)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}dte^{-itH}\rho e^{itH}\right]=\sum_{n=1}^{L}\Pi_{n}\rho\Pi_{n}\,,

(63)

which is a block diagonal state. In this case, the relative entropy of asymmetry

Åberg

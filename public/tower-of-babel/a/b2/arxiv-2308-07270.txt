Quivers and curves in higher dimension
Hülya Argüz, Pierrick Bousseau

Abstract
. We prove a correspondence between Donaldson–Thomas invariants of quivers with potential having trivial attractor invariants and genus zero punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of holomorphic symplectic cluster varieties. The proof relies on the comparison of the stability scattering diagram, describing the wall-crossing behavior of Donaldson–Thomas invariants, with a scattering diagram capturing punctured Gromov–Witten invariants via tropical geometry.



0.
Introduction

0.1.
Overview

Donaldson–Thomas (DT) invariants are counts of stable objects in a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
which is Calabi–Yau of dimension 3
[
34
,
49
,
52
,
70
]
, and have important applications in physics, in geometry and in representation theory. In physics, in particular in quantum field theory and string theory, DT invariants play an important role as counts of BPS states and D-branes
[
11
]
. From the geometric point of view, particularly interesting situations are when
\mathcal{C}
is the derived category of coherent sheaves
[
70
]
or the Fukaya category of a Calabi–Yau 3-fold
[
48
,
71
]
. In the context of representation theory, quivers with potentials
[
32
]
provide a natural source of examples of Calabi–Yau categories of dimension three
[
39
,
50
]
.
Due to its more algebraic nature, DT theory of quivers with potentials provides an ideal framework to study and explore many questions, which are also of interest in the geometric counterpart of DT theory.

Stable objects in a triangulated category are defined with respect to a Bridgeland stability condition on this category
[
21
]
, and
DT invariants are piecewise-constant with respect to the choice of stability condition:
they are constant in the complement of
countably many real codimension one loci in the space of stability conditions called walls, but they
jump discontinuously in general when the stability condition crosses a wall. The jumps of DT invariants across walls in the space of stability conditions are given by a universal wall-crossing formula due to Joyce–Song
[
49
]

and
Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
52
]
.

Remarkably, a very similar wall-crossing formula appears in the a priori very different context of counting holomorphic disks in mirror symmetry
[
12
,
45
,
52
]
. This observation leads to the surprising expectation that in many cases DT invariants may be equal to counts of holomorphic curves, such as log and punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of Abramovich–Chen–Gross–Siebert
[
1
,
4
,
46
]
, which appear in the algebro-geometric mirror construction of Gross–Siebert
[
47
]
. Therefore, one naturally expects DT invariants to be related in some situations to counts of holomorphic curves in holomorphic symplectic varieties
[
17
,
53
,
56
]
.
We show this expectation holds in the context of DT invariants of quivers with potentials. In particular, we prove a correspondence between quiver DT invariants and punctured log Gromov–Witten invariants of holomorphic symplectic cluster varieties.

0.2.
Background and main result

We state our main result after a quick review of quiver DT and punctured Gromov–Witten invariants.

0.2.1.
Quiver DT Invariants

A quiver with potential
(Q,W)
is given by a finite oriented graph
Q
, and a finite formal linear combination
W
of oriented cycles in
Q
.
Given a quiver with potential
(Q,W)
, with set of vertices
Q_{0}
, one can define
a DT invariant
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}\in\mathbb{Z}
for every dimension vector
\gamma\in N_{Q}:=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{Z}s_{i}
and every stability parameter

\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}:=\operatorname{Hom}(N_{Q},\mathbb{R})\,,

as reviewed in §
1.1
. The dependence of
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
on the stability parameter
\theta
is captured by a universal wall-crossing formula
[
49
,
52
]
.
It follows from the wall-crossing formula that general DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
are determined by particular DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}
called
attractor DT invariants
, which are defined for a stability parameter
\theta
close to the attractor point
\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}=\omega_{Q}(\gamma,-)\in\gamma^{\perp}
, where
\omega_{Q}
is the skew-symmetric form on
N_{Q}
obtained by skew-symmetrization of the Euler form – see (
1.4
)
[
5
,
53
,
63
]
.

When
\Omega_{s_{i}}^{+,\star}=1
for all
i\in Q_{0}
, and
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}=0
unless
\gamma=s_{i}
for some
i
or
\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
, we say that
(Q,W)
has
trivial attractor DT invariants
.
As reviewed in Examples
1.11
-
1.12
, this condition is known to hold for many quivers with potentials of interest in representation theory and geometry. It is also expected to be a general property of quivers with potentials describing the derived category of coherent sheaves on non-compact Calabi–Yau 3-folds.

Throughout this paper we frequently consider rational DT invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
, which are a repackaging of the integer DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
defined by a universal formula – see (
1.3
) for details.

0.2.2.
Punctured Gromov–Witten Invariants

Given a pair
(X,D)
consisting of a smooth projective variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
and a normal crossing divisor on
X
, punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of
(X,D)
are virtual counts of curves in
X
with prescribed tangency conditions along
D

[
4
]
. In general, such curves might have components contained in
D
and logarithmic geometry is needed to make sense of the contact orders with
D

[
1
,
46
]
. For general log Calabi-Yau pairs
(X,D)
, Gross–Siebert define particular genus zero punctured Gromov–Witten invariants, which play an important role in their general construction of mirrors, and which can be viewed as an algebro-geometric definition of counts of Maslov index zero holomorphic disks in the non-compact Calabi-Yau variety
U=Y\setminus D

[
47
]
.

We consider these invariants for particular log Calabi-Yau pairs
(X,D)
. A
symplectic seed

\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
consists of a finite rank abelian group, finitely many elements
e_{i}\in N
, and a skew-symmetric form
\omega
on
N
, which is non-degenerate over
\mathbb{Q}
. We further assume that
v_{i}:=\iota_{e_{i}}\omega\in M
is primitive – see (
2.2
).
Given a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}
, one first considers a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
with fan
\Sigma
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
containing the rays
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{i}
, and then one defines a pair
(X,D)
, where
X
is the blow up of
X_{\Sigma}
along the loci of equation
1+z^{e_{i}}
in the toric divisor corresponding to the ray
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{i}
, and where
D
is the strict transform of the toric boundary divisor of
X_{\Sigma}
. Under an appropriate assumption – see (
2.4
),
X
is smooth, and so
(X,D)
is a log Calabi-Yau pair. Moreover,
\omega
induces an holomorphic symplectic form on the complement
U=Y\setminus D
. We refer to
U
as the
cluster variety
defined by
\mathbf{s}
, an to
(X,D)
as a
log Calabi-Yau compactification of the cluster variety
.

Following
[
47
]
, one can define a genus zero punctured Gromov–Witten invariant
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}\in\mathbb{Q}
of
(X,D)
for every curve class
\beta
and every combinatorial choice of a so-called
wall type
.
The invariant
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}
is a virtual count of rational curves in
(X,D)
of class
\beta
and whose combinatorics of intersections with the strata of
D
is constrained by
\tau
– see §
2.2
for details.

0.2.3.
Main result

We prove a correspondence between the DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
of a quiver with potential
(Q,W)

having trivial attractor DT invariants, and the punctured Gromov–Witten invariants
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}
of a log Calabi-Yau compactification of the cluster variety defined by a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}
“compatible” with
(X,D)
. Here, the compatibility
between a quiver
Q
and a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
is the data of linear map
\psi:N_{Q}\rightarrow N
such that
\psi(s_{i})=e_{i}
,
\omega_{Q}=\psi^{\star}\omega
, and

\psi\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is surjective – see §
3.1
for details. If
Q
and
\mathbf{s}
are compatible, then the holomorphic symplectic cluster variety
U=X\setminus D
is a finite quotient of a symplectic fiber of the Poisson
\mathcal{X}
cluster variety defined by
Q

[
37
,
40
]
. In particular, the dimension of
U
and
X
is equal to the rank of the skew-symmetrized Euler form
\omega_{Q}
of
Q
.
Our main result, Theorem
3.6
, states:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants. Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
be a symplectic seed and
(X,D)
a log Calabi-Yau compactification of the corresponding cluster variety satisfying assumptions (
2.2
) and (
2.4
).
Let
\psi\colon N_{Q}\rightarrow N
be a compatibility data between
\mathbf{s}
and
Q
.
Let
\gamma\in N_{Q}\setminus\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
be a dimension vector such that
\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Then, for every general stability parameter
\theta\in\psi^{\vee}(M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\gamma^{\perp}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
and point
x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\psi^{\vee}(x)=\theta
, there exists a set of wall types
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{x}
and a curve class
\beta_{\gamma}^{x}
described in §
3.3
, such that we have the correspondence

(0.1)

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\frac{1}{|\gamma|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{x}}k_{\tau}N_{\tau,\beta_{\gamma}^{x}}^{(X,D)}\,,

between the rational DT invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
of
(Q,W)
, and the genus zero punctured Gromov–Witten invariants

N_{\tau,\beta_{\gamma}^{x}}^{(X,D)}
of
(X,D)
, where
|\gamma|
is the divisibility of
\gamma
in
N_{Q}
,
and
the coefficient
k_{\tau}
is a positive integer defined in (
2.8
).

The proof of Theorem
0.1
is based on a comparison between the stability scattering diagram for DT invariants
[
22
]
and the explicit description, obtained by Mark Gross and the first author
[
10
]
, of the canonical scattering diagram capturing punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of log Calabi-Yau pairs obtained as blow-ups of toric varieties.

Using Theorem
0.1
,
one can interpret the integer DT invariants

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
as BPS invariants underlying the rational punctured Gromov–Witten invariants
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}
. We will also show, under the assumptions of Theorem
0.1
, that the DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}

are non-negative integers – see Theorem
1.8
. Both the integrality and the positivity of
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
are highly non-trivial from the point of view of punctured Gromov–Witten invariants.

While Theorem
0.1
is about DT invariants of quivers with potentials, it can sometimes be applied to geometrically defined DT invariants when the derived category of coherent sheaves of a Calabi-Yau 3-fold admits a description in terms of a quiver with potential. We give an explicit example of such an application to the DT invariants of local
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem
0.1
relates all DT invariants of a quiver with potential, and so of a Calabi-Yau category of dimension 3, to punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of a log Calabi–Yau compactification of a holomorphic symplectic cluster variety of dimension equal to the rank of the skew-symmetrized Euler form. In particular, this result is very different from the MNOP correspondence
[
57
]
which relates DT counts of ideal sheaves and Gromov–Witten invariants of the same 3-fold.

0.3.
Related works

The tropical vertex

The first example of the general correspondence given in Theorem
0.1
was obtained by Gross–Pandharipande
[
43
]
, following previous work of Gross–Pandharipande–Siebert on the tropical vertex
[
44
]
: they proved a correspondence between DT invariants of the
m
-Kronecker quivers, consisting of two vertices connected by
m
arrows, and log Gromov–Witten invariants of log Calabi–Yau surfaces.
This correspondence was generalized by Reineke–Weist to the case of complete bipartite quivers under the name of refined Gromov–Witten/Kronecker correspondence
[
66
,
67
]
, and then to arbitrary acyclic quivers with skew-symmetrized Euler forms of rank two by the second author
[
15
, §8.5]
. Theorem
0.1
generalize these results to higher dimension. Actually, even in dimension two, Theorem
0.1
is broader than previously known results as it can be applied to non-acyclic quivers of rank two having trivial attractor DT invariants. We give examples of such application in
§
4.1
-
4.2
.
There also exist a refined DT/ higher genus GW generalization of the Gromov–Witten/Kronecker correspondence
[
15
, §8.5]
and extensions to different geometries in dimension two
[
16
,
68
]
, and it is an interesting question to find out if they admit higher dimensional generalizations.

Finally, there should exist analogues of Theorem
0.1
in the context of geometric DT invariants of non-compact Calabi–Yau 3-folds. Such a correspondence is proved in
[
14
]
for geometric DT invariants of the local projective plane.

Knots-quivers correspondence

An a priori different relation between quiver DT invariants and open Gromov–Witten theory has been explored in the context of the knots-quivers correspondence
[
36
]
. This correspondence involves DT invariants of symmetric quivers, and so in particular with zero skew-symmetrized Euler form, whereas Theorem
0.1
deals with the complementary case of dimension vectors which are not in the kernel of the skew-symmetrized Euler form.
It is a very interesting question to understand what is the precise relation between Theorem
0.1
and the correspondence in
[
36
]
.

Quivers, flow trees and log curves

A correspondence between quiver DT invariants and counts of tropical curves has been established by Cheung–Mandel
[
26
]
and by the authors in their proof of the flow tree formula expressing general DT invariants in terms of attractor DT invariants
[
7
]
.
Using a tropical/log Gromov–Witten correspondence, it was then proved by the authors that the universal coefficients appearing in the flow tree formula are log Gromov–Witten invariants of toric varieties
[
9
]
. For quivers with potentials having trivial attractor DT invariants, the compatibility between Theorem
0.1
and the main result of
[
9
]
should have a geometric interpretation as a degeneration formula in Gromov–Witten theory for the degeneration of the log Calabi–Yau compactification of the cluster variety to a toric variety and other simpler pieces, as studied in

[
10
,
44
]
. In the two-dimensional case, this degeneration formula is studied in
[
44
]
and its interpretation in terms of quiver DT invariants is discussed in
[
66
]
.

1.
Donaldson–Thomas invariants of quivers

1.1.
Quiver DT invariants

A
quiver

Q
is a finite oriented graph. We denote by

Q_{0}
the set of vertices of
Q
, and
Q_{1}
the set of oriented edges of
Q
, referred to as arrows.
We set

N_{Q}:=\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{Z}s_{i}\,.

We denote by
M_{Q}:=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
the dual lattice to
N_{Q}
, and

M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}:=M_{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=\operatorname{Hom}(N_{Q},\mathbb{R})\,,

the associated dual vector space.

Definition 1.1
.

A
representation
of a quiver
Q
, denoted by

V=(\{V_{i}\}_{i\in Q_{0}},\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in Q_{1}})\,,

is an assignment of a finite-dimensional vector space
V_{i}
over
\mathbb{C}
for each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, and a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
f_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Hom}(V_{i},V_{j})
for each arrow
(\alpha:i\rightarrow j)\in Q_{1}
. The
dimension vector
associated to a quiver representation is the vector

\gamma=(\gamma_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in N_{Q}\,,

where
\gamma_{i}:=\dim V_{i}
.

We have the following notion of stability due to King
[
51
]
.

Definition 1.2

(King’s stability)
.

Let
V
be a quiver representation with associated dimension vector
\gamma\in N_{Q}
. A
stability parameter
for
\gamma
is a point

\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}:=\{\theta\in M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\,,\theta(\gamma)=0\}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\,.

The representation
V
is
\theta
-
stable
(resp.
\theta
-
semistable
) if for all
non-zero strict subrepresentation
V^{\prime}
of
V
we have
\theta(\mathrm{dim}(V^{\prime}))<0
(resp.
\theta(\mathrm{dim}(V^{\prime}))\leq 0
).

For a dimension vector
\gamma\in N_{Q}
, we say a stability parameter
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
is
\gamma
-general
if
\theta(\gamma^{\prime})=0
implies
\gamma^{\prime}
collinear with
\gamma
. For
\gamma\in N_{Q}
and a
\gamma
-general stability parameter
\theta\in M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
of S-equivalence classes of
\theta
-semistable quiver representations of
Q
dimension
\gamma
is a quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
,
which is constructed via geometric representation theory – see
[
51
]
. When
Q
is acyclic,
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
is projective. In general, the choice of a
potential

W=\sum_{c}\lambda_{c}c
, that is, of a finite linear combination of oriented cycles
c
in
Q
and with coefficients
\lambda_{c}\in\mathbb{C}
, defines a
trace function

\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}:\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
on each of the moduli spaces

\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
: if
c
is an oriented cycle of arrows
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
of
Q
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(c)_{\gamma}^{\theta}:\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle V=(V_{i},f_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathrm{Tr}(f_{\alpha_{n}}\circ\ldots\circ f_{\alpha_{1}})

and we set

\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}=\sum_{c}\lambda_{c}\mathrm{Tr}(c)_{\gamma}^{\theta}\,\ .

Quiver DT invariants of a quiver with potential
(Q,W)
are then defined as follows. Fix a dimension vector
\gamma\in N
, and a

\gamma
-general stability parameter
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
. The DT (Donaldson-Thomas) invariant, denoted by

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}\in\mathbb{Z},

is an integer corresponding to the virtual count of the critical points of the trace function
\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}
on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
of
\theta
-semistable representations of dimension
\gamma
. If the
\theta
-stable locus in
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
is empty, we have
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=0
.
Else,
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
is
defined as

(1.1)

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}:=\sum_{i,n}(-1)^{i+n}\dim\operatorname{Gr}_{n}^{W}H^{i}(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta},\phi_{\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}}(IC))\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

where
IC
is the intersection cohomology sheaf on
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
normalized to be the constant sheaf in degree
0
if
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
is smooth,
\phi_{\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}}
is the vanishing cycle functor defined by the trace function, and
\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{n}
are the graded pieces with respect to the weight filtration

[
29
,
30
,
58
]
.
Note that if the cohomology groups in (
1.1
) are pure and concentrated in even degrees, then

(1.2)

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=e(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta},\phi_{\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}}(IC))\,,

where
e(-)
is the Euler characteristic.
This is for example the case if
Q
is acylic (and so
W=0
) by
[
58
]
.
The data of DT invariants

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
can be repackaged into the
rational DT invariants

(1.3)

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\in N_{Q}\\
\gamma=k\gamma^{\prime},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}\in\mathbb{Q}\,,

which are often more convenient to work with, particularly when calculating them using wall structures
[
7
]
. Rational DT invariants can also be defined using the motivic Hall algebra
[
49
,
52
,
64
,
65
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the literature on DT invariants, it is more common to work with DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{\theta}
defined by

\Omega_{\gamma}^{\theta}:=(-1)^{\dim\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}}\,e(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta},\phi_{\mathrm{Tr}(W)_{\gamma}^{\theta}}(IC))\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

and the rational DT invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
defined
by

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{\theta}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\in N_{Q}\\
\gamma=k\gamma^{\prime},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{\theta}\in\mathbb{Q}\,.

In the notation of
[
7
, §5.2]
, the DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
are obtained from the refined DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{\theta}(y,t)
by setting
y=1
and
t=1
, whereas
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
are obtained by setting
y=-1
and
t=1
.
When (
1.2
) holds, we simply have

\Omega_{\gamma}^{\theta}=(-1)^{\dim\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}^{\theta}}\,\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}\,.

In this paper, we work with the DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
as they have better positivity properties – see Theorem
1.8
.

1.2.
Attractor DT invariants

The DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
of a quiver with potential
W
are locally constant functions of the
\gamma
-general stability parameter
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
. Their change across the loci of non-
\gamma
-general stability parameters can be computed by the wall-crossing formula of Joyce–Song
[
49
]
and Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
52
]
.
Using the wall-crossing formula, DT invariants can be expressed in terms of the simpler
attractor DT invariants
, which are quiver DT invariants at specific values of the stability parameter, defined as follows.

The skew-symmetrized Euler form
\omega_{Q}\colon N_{Q}\times N_{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is defined by

(1.4)

\omega_{Q}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}):=\sum_{i,j\in Q_{0}}(a_{ij}-a_{ji})\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}^{\prime}\,,

where
a_{ij}
is the number of arrows in
Q
from the vertex
i
to the vertex
j
.
For every
\gamma\in N_{Q}
, the specific point

\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}:=\omega_{Q}(\gamma,-)\in\gamma^{\perp}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}

is called the
attractor point
for
\gamma

[
5
,
63
]
. In general, the attractor point is not
\gamma
-general and we define
the attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{*}
by

(1.5)

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,*}\coloneqq\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta_{\gamma}}\,,

where
\theta_{\gamma}

is a small
\gamma
-general perturbation of

\omega_{Q}(\gamma,-)
in
\gamma^{\perp}

[
5
,
63
]
. The integer
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,*}
is independent of the choice of the small perturbation

[
5
,
63
]
. Thus, for a fixed dimension vector
\gamma\in N
, we have a well-defined attractor DT invariant
\Omega_{\gamma}^{\star}
– for detailed discussion of these invariants see
[
5
,
53
,
63
]
.
By iterative applications of the wall-crossing formula, general DT invariants are uniquely determined in terms of the attractor DT invariants.
This reconstruction of the general DT invariants from the attractor DT invariants can be made explicit using either the flow tree formula
[
7
]
or the attractor tree formula
[
62
]
.

We denote by
I\subset Q_{0}
the set of vertices
i
of
Q_{0}
such that
\iota_{s_{i}}\omega_{Q}\neq 0
, that is,
s_{i}\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
. In this paper, we will mainly consider quivers with potentials having a very simple set of attractor DT invariants, as made precise in the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

A quiver with potential
(Q,W)
has
trivial attractor DT invariants
if

(i)

\Omega_{s_{i}}^{+,\star}=1
for all
i\in I
, and

(ii)

\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}=0
for all
\gamma\in N_{Q}
such that
\gamma\neq s_{i}
for all
i\in Q_{0}
, and
\gamma\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
.

Remark 1.5
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the only decompositions of
ks_{i}
in
N_{Q}
with positive coefficients contain only multiples of
s_{i}
. As
\omega_{Q}(s_{i},s_{i})=0
, it follows from the wall-crossing formula that
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\theta}
does not depend on
\theta
. In particular, if
\Omega_{s_{i}}^{+,\star}=1
as in Definition
1.4
(i), then we have
\Omega_{s_{i}}^{+,\theta}=1
for all
\theta\in s_{i}^{\perp}
. Similarly, if
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\star}=0
for all
k>1
as in Definition
1.4
(i), then we have
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\theta}=0
for all
k>1
and
\theta\in s_{i}^{\perp}
.

Remark 1.6
.

If
\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
, then, by the wall-crossing formula,
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
does not depend on
\theta
, and so in particular we have
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}
for all
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
. It also follows from the wall-crossing formula that these invariants do not play a role in any wall-crossing of other invariants
\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}
. In particular, if
\gamma^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
, then
\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}
can be recovered from the attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
with
\gamma\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
.

Remark 1.7
.

A closely related notion has been introduced in
[
28
, Def 7.3]
: a quiver is called
genteel
if
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}=0
unless
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for some
i\in Q_{0}
. However, to be genteel and to have trivial attractor DT invariants are slightly different in general. For example, we allow
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\star}\neq 0
if
\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
in Definition
1.4
. On the other hand, for a genteel quiver as in
[
28
, Def 7.3]
, to have
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\star}\neq 0
for
k>1
is allowed, whereas it is not for a quiver with trivial DT invariants as in Definition
1.4
.

DT invariants of a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants have particularly nice positivity properties, as illustrated by the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants. Then, for every
\gamma\in N_{Q}\setminus\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
and every
\gamma
-general stability parameter
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
, the DT invariant
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
is a non-negative integer.

Proof.

By Remark
1.6
, DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
with
\gamma\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
can be reconstructed using the wall-crossing formula from attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\star}
with
\gamma^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
.
For a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants, the attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\star}
with
\gamma^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
are either
0
or
1
, and so in particular are positive. The result follows because positivity of DT invariants is preserved under wall-crossing by the proof of
[
41
, Thm 1.13]
(see also
[
28
]
).

Definition 1.9

A quiver with potential

Theorem 1.10

Let

1.3.
Stability scattering diagram

Given a quiver
Q
, we denote

N_{Q}^{\oplus}:=\{\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}s_{i}\in N_{Q}\,|\,\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}\,,

and

N_{Q}^{+}:=N_{Q}^{\oplus}\setminus\{0\}\,.

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}[N_{Q}^{\oplus}]
the monoid
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of
N_{Q}^{\oplus}
, that is the algebra of polynomials

\sum_{\gamma\in N_{Q}^{\oplus}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}

with
c_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[N_{Q}^{\oplus}]
, generated by the monomials
z^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in N_{Q}^{+}
. We denote by
\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{\oplus}]\!]
the completion of
\mathbb{Q}[N_{Q}^{\oplus}]
with respect to this maximal ideal: it is the algebra of formal power series

\sum_{\gamma\in N_{Q}^{\oplus}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}

with for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
finitely many coefficients
c_{\gamma}\neq 0
with
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}s_{i}
and
\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}\leq k
.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
Q
be a quiver with
d
vertices.
A
Q
-wall
is a pair
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})
, where

(i)

\mathfrak{d}
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional convex rational polyhedral cone in
M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
contained in an hyperplane of the form
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}^{\perp}
, with
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}\in N_{Q}^{+}
primitive.

(ii)

f_{\mathfrak{d}}\in\mathbb{Q}[\![z^{\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}}]\!]\subset\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{\oplus}]\!]
is a formal power series in
z^{\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}}
with constant term
1
, that is of the form

(1.7)

f_{\mathfrak{d}}=1+\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}

with
c_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Remark 1.15
.

Note that
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}
in Definition
1.14
(i) is uniquely determined by
\mathfrak{d}
.

Definition 1.16
.

A
Q
-wall
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})
is
incoming

if
\iota_{\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}}\omega_{Q}\in\mathfrak{d}
, that is, if
\mathfrak{d}
contains the attractor point for
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}
.

Definition 1.17
.

A
Q
-scattering diagram
is a set
\mathfrak{D}=\{(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\}
of
Q
-walls such that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exist finitely many walls
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\in\mathfrak{D}
with
f_{\mathfrak{d}}\neq 1\mod\mathfrak{m}^{k}
.

We define the
support
of
a
Q
-scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}=\{(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\}
in
M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
by

(1.8)

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}\mathfrak{d}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\,,

and its

singular locus
by

(1.9)

\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}\partial\mathfrak{d}\cup\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}}\mathfrak{d}\cap\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\,,

where
\partial\mathfrak{d}
denotes the boundary of a wall
\mathfrak{d}
, and the last union is over the pairs of walls
\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{codim}\mathfrak{d}\cap\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\geq 2
.
For every point
x\in M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})
, we define

(1.10)

f_{\mathfrak{D},x}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}\\
x\in\mathfrak{d}\end{subarray}}f_{\mathfrak{d}}\,,

where the product is over all the walls of
\mathfrak{D}
containing
x
.
Two
Q
-scattering diagrams
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
are called
equivalent
if

(1.11)

f_{\mathfrak{D},x}=f_{\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},x}

for all
x\in M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\setminus(\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})\cup\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}))
.

Given a path

\displaystyle\alpha:[0,1]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\longmapsto\alpha(\tau)\,

intersecting transversally
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
, and
\tau\in[0,1]
such that
\alpha(\tau)\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
, we define an automorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha,\tau}:\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{\oplus}]\!]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{\oplus}]\!]

\displaystyle z^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha(\tau)}^{\epsilon_{\alpha,\tau}\,\omega_{Q}(\gamma_{\alpha,\tau},\gamma)}z^{\gamma}\,,

where
\gamma_{\alpha,\tau}
is the unique primitive element in
N_{Q}^{+}
such that
\mathfrak{d}\subset\gamma_{\alpha,\tau}^{\perp}
for all walls
\mathfrak{d}
containing
\alpha(\tau)
, and
\epsilon_{\alpha,\tau}\in\{\pm 1\}
is the sign such that
\epsilon_{\alpha,\tau}\,\omega_{Q}(\gamma_{\alpha,\tau},\alpha^{\prime}(\tau))<0
.
The path ordered automorphism

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha}
is the product of automorphisms

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha,\tau}
for all
\tau\in[0,1]
such that
\alpha(\tau)\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
, and ordered following increasing values of
\tau
.
Finally, a scattering diagram is called
consistent

if for any path
\alpha
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})
with
\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)

the associated path ordered automorphism

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha}
is the identity.

Definition 1.18
.

A
Q
-wall
\mathfrak{d}
is called
central
if
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}\in\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
.

Given a
Q
-scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}
, there is a natural way to produce a
Q
-scattering diagram
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}
without central walls:
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}
is simply the set of non-central walls of
\mathfrak{D}
.

Lemma 1.19
.

If a
Q
-scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
is consistent, then the corresponding
Q
-scattering diagram
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{Q}
without central walls is also consistent.

Proof.

It follows from (
1.12
) that the automorphism
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha,\tau}
is the identity if
\gamma_{\alpha,\tau}\in\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
. Hence, removing the central walls does not change the automorphisms
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha}
: we have
\mathfrak{p}_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}},\alpha}=\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha}
for all paths
\alpha
. In particular, this implies that, if
\mathfrak{D}
is consistent, then
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}
is also consistent.

how DT invariants of a quiver with potential can be organized into a scattering diagram called the stability stability scattering.

Definition 1.20
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential. The

Following
[

, §11.4]
, we review the notion of a cluster scattering diagram.

Definition 1.21
.

Let

be a quiver. The

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q,\mathrm{in}}

-scattering diagram with set of walls

(1.14)

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q,\mathrm{in}}:=\{(s_{i}^{\perp},1+z^{s_{i}})\}_{i\in I}\,.

Note that all walls of

are incoming as
\iota_{s_{i}}\omega_{Q}\in s_{i}^{\perp}
for all

.

By
[

(see also
[

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q}

and such that every wall
\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q}\setminus\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q,\mathrm{in}}
is non-incoming. We refer to
\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q}

.

Theorem 1.22
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants as in Definition
1.4
.
Then, the scattering diagram
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)}
without central walls corresponding to
the stability scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)}
and the cluster scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q}
are equivalent.

Proof.

By Definition
1.4
of trivial attractor DT invariants, we have
\Omega_{s_{i}}^{+,\star}=1
and
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\star}=0
for all
i\in I
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
.
By Remark
1.5
, it follows that, for all
\theta\in s_{i}^{\perp}
,
i\in I
, and

, we have
\Omega_{s_{i},\theta}=1
and
\Omega_{ks_{i}}^{+,\theta}=0
.
Applying (
1.3
), we obtain

\overline{\Omega}_{ks_{i}}^{\theta}=\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}
for all

, and so, by (

), we have

f_{\mathfrak{D}_{(Q,W)}^{\mathrm{st}},\theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}k\,\,\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k^{2}}z^{ks_{i}}\right)=1+z^{s_{i}}\,,

for all

. Moreover, for

, we have

, and so we have
f_{\mathfrak{D}_{(Q,W)}^{\mathrm{st}},\theta}=f_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{(Q,W)}^{\mathrm{st}},\theta}
for all

\overline{\mathfrak{D}}
of the equivalence class of

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q,\mathrm{in}}\subset\overline{\mathfrak{D}}

by (

By Definition

\overline{\mathfrak{D}}
does not have any other incoming walls apart from

for

\overline{\mathfrak{D}}\setminus\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q,\mathrm{in}}
are non-incoming. Therefore,

is equivalent to the cluster scattering diagram
\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{Q}
by the uniqueness part of
[

, Prop 3.3.2]
(see also
[

, Thm 1.21]

2.

2.1.
Cluster varieties

Definition 2.1
.

A
symplectic seed

\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
is the data of a finite rank free abelian group
N
, a collection of elements
e_{i}\in N
indexed by a finite set
I
, and an integral skew-symmetric form

\omega\colon N\times N\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\,,

such that
\operatorname{ker}\omega=0
, that is, such that
\omega\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is non-degenerate.

Given a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
, we denote
M:=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes\mathbb{R}=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb{R})
, so that
\omega\in\bigwedge^{2}M
. As
\operatorname{ker}\omega=0
, the map

(2.1)

\displaystyle\nu:N

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\longmapsto\iota_{\gamma}\omega:=\omega(\gamma,-)

is injective and has finite cokernel. In particular, the image
\mathrm{Im}(\nu)
is of finite index in
M
.
For every
i\in I
, we denote

v_{i}:=\nu(e_{i})=\iota_{e_{i}}\omega=\omega(e_{i},-)\in M\,.

In addition, we will make the following assumption:

(2.2)

|v_{i}|=1\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i\in I\,,

where
|v_{i}|
is the divisibility of
v_{i}
in
M
.

We review the construction of a cluster variety starting from a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
satisfying (
2.2
). Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
containing the rays
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{i}
for all
i\in I
, and such that the corresponding toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is smooth and projective. Such a fan always exists by toric resolution of singularities
[
27
, Thm 11.1.9]
and the toric Chow lemma

[
27
, Thm 6.1.18]
.
For every
i\in I
, we denote by
\overline{D}_{i}
the toric divisor of
X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to the ray

of

For every

Up to refining

.
Moreover,
we assume that for every

Note that if

Let

be the blow-up of

along the codimension two locus

and let

be the strict transform of the toric boundary divisor

of

. As

is also smooth and

For every

of

.
We refer to the complement

defined by the symplectic seed

, and

of the cluster variety. As we will always consider the pair

up to locally trivial deformations, we suppress the parameters

from the notation and from the terminology. The skew-symmetric form

Remark 2.2
.

In the usual theory of cluster varieties
[
37
,
40
]
,
a (skew-symmetric)

consists a finite abelian group
\tilde{N}
, a basis
(\tilde{e}_{i})_{i\in I}
of

, and a skew-symmetric form
\tilde{\omega}
.
Given a symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
as in Definition

, one can define a seed
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}=(\tilde{N},(\tilde{e}_{i})_{i\in I},\tilde{\omega})
by
\tilde{N}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\tilde{e}_{i}
, and
\tilde{\omega}=\pi^{\star}\omega
, where

\displaystyle\pi:\tilde{N}\longrightarrow N

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{i}\longmapsto e_{i}\,.

If

is exactly the quotient of

by the kernel

. In this case,

is a symplectic fiber of the Poisson

cluster variety defined by the seed
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}

[
37
,
40
]
.
In general,

contains

as a sublattice of finite index, and

is a quotient by a finite group of a symplectic fiber of the Poisson
\mathcal{X}
cluster variety defined by
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}
.

2.2.
Punctured Gromov–Witten invariants

Given a log Calabi-Yau pair, Gross–Siebert define in
[
47
]

punctured Gromov–Witten invariants indexed by wall types. Using these invariants, they define the canonical scattering diagram which can be use to construct the mirror geometry.
In this section, we briefly review the definition of these punctured Gromov–Witten invariants in the particular setting of log Calabi-Yau compactifications of cluster varieties satisfying assumptions (
2.2
)-(

) of §

Let

be a

)-(

) of §

Let

is the topological space obtained by gluing together the cones of
\mathscr{P}
according to the incidence relations between strata. By construction, one can view
\mathscr{P}
as a decomposition of

into a union of cones.
Let
\Delta\subset B
be the union of codimension
2
cones of
\mathscr{P}
. Then, as reviewed in
[
10
, (2.4)]
, there is a natural integral affine structure

1

As described in
[

on the complement
B_{0}:=B\setminus\Delta
of
\Delta
in
B
. For every cone
\sigma\in\mathscr{P}
, we denote by
\Lambda_{\sigma}
the space of integral tangent vectors to
\sigma
, and for every point

, we denote by

the space of integral tangent vectors to

at

Following

Definition 2.3
.

A
tropical type
is the data of a triple
\tau=(G,\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{u})
, where:

(i)

G
is a graph, with set of vertices
V(G)
, set of edges
E(G)
, and set of legs
L(G)
,

(ii)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is a map

\boldsymbol{\sigma}:V(G)\cup E(G)\cup L(G)\longrightarrow\mathscr{P}\,,

such that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\subset\boldsymbol{\sigma}(E)
if
v
is a vertex of an edge or leg

,

(iii)

\mathbf{u}
is a map

\mathbf{u}:\{(V,E)\,|\,V\in V(G)\,,E\in E(G)\cup L(G)\,,V\in\partial E\}\longrightarrow\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathscr{P}}\Lambda_{\sigma}\,,

such that
\mathbf{u}(V,E)\in\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(E)}
for all
(V,E)
such that
V\in\partial E
, and
\mathbf{u}(V_{2},E)=-\mathbf{u}(V_{1},E)
if
E\in E(G)
and
\partial E=\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

An
abstract tropical curve
is a pair
(G,\ell)

consisting of a graph
G
and of length functions
\ell:E(G)\cup L(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
\ell(E)\neq\infty
for all
E\in E(G)
.

We view an abstract tropical curve
(G,\ell)
as a metric graph, with edges
E\in E(G)
identified with the line segment
[0,\ell(E)]
, and with legs
L\in L(G)
identified with the line segment
[0,\ell(L)]
if
\ell(L)\neq\infty
, and with the half-line
[0,+\infty)

if
\ell(L)=\infty
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\tau=(G,\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{u})
be a tropical type. A
tropical map
to
(B,\mathscr{P})
of type
\tau
is the data of an abstract tropical curve
(G,\ell)
and of a map
h\colon G\rightarrow B
such that

(i)

h(v)\in\operatorname{Int}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v))
for all
v\in V(G)

(ii)

for all
E\in E(G)\cup L(G)
,
h(\operatorname{Int}(E))\in\operatorname{Int}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(E))

and
h|_{E}:E\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\sigma}(E)
is an affine linear map.

(iii)

for all
E\in E(G)
with
\partial E=\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
, we have
h(V_{2})-h(V_{1})=\ell(E)\mathbf{u}(V_{1},E)
.

(iii)

for all
L\in L(G)
with
\partial L=\{V\}
, we have
h(E)=V+[0,\ell(L)]u(V,L)=\boldsymbol{\sigma}(E)\cap(V+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u(V,L))
if
\ell(L)\neq\infty
, and
h(E)=V+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u(V,L)
if
\ell(L)=\infty
.

Definition 2.6
.

A tropical type
\tau
is
realizable
if there exists a tropical map to
(B,\mathscr{P})
of type
\tau
.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a realizable tropical type
\tau=(G,\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{u})
, the
basic monoid
of
\tau
is the monoid
Q_{\tau}:=\operatorname{Hom}(Q_{\tau}^{\vee},\mathbb{N})
, where

Q_{\tau}^{\vee}:=\{((p_{V})_{V},(\ell_{E})_{E})\in\prod_{V\in V(G)}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\prod_{E\in E(G)}\mathbb{N}\,|\,p_{V_{2}}-p_{V_{1}}=\ell_{E}\mathbf{u}(V_{1},E)\,,\forall E\in E(\mathsf{G})\}

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the set of integral points of the cone
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)
and
\partial E=\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
.

By construction, the cone
Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}:=\operatorname{Hom}(Q_{\tau}^{\vee},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
parametrizes the tropical maps to
(B,\mathscr{P})
of type
\tau
: for every
s=((p_{V})_{V},(\ell_{E}))\in Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
, the corresponding tropical map is defined by
\ell_{s}(E):=\ell_{E}
and
h_{s}(V)=p_{V}
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
wall type
is a realizable tropical type
\tau=(G,\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{u})
such that:

(i)

the graph
G
is connected of genus zero, with a single leg:
L(G)=\{L_{\mathrm{out}}\}
.

(ii)

\dim Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}=d-2
and
\dim\cup_{s\in Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}}h_{s}(L_{\mathrm{out}})=d-1
, where
h_{s}:G\rightarrow B
are the universal tropical maps indexed by
s\in Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
.

(iii)

\tau
is balanced: for every vertex
V\in V(G)
such that
\dim\boldsymbol{\sigma}(V)=d
or
d-1
, with incident edges or legs
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
, then, for every point
x\in\operatorname{Int}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(V))
, one can view
u(V,E_{1}),\dots,u(V,E_{m})
as elements of
\Lambda_{x}
, and we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}u(V,E_{i})=0
in
\Lambda_{x}
.

Given a wall type
\tau
, we denote

(2.5)

u_{\tau}:=u(V_{\mathrm{out}},L_{\mathrm{out}})\in\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(L_{\mathrm{out}})}\,,

where
V_{\mathrm{out}}
is the vertex adjacent to the leg
L_{\mathrm{out}}
.
It follows from Definition
2.8
(ii) that
u_{\tau}\neq 0
.

We refer to
[
2
]
for the details of the general theory of punctured maps. A punctured map to
(X,D)
is a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

in the category of log schemes, where

is equipped with the divisorial log structure defined by

, the base

is a log point, and

is a puncturing of log curve

as in
[

is called stable if the underlying morphism of schemes

gives a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma(X)=(B,\mathscr{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma(W)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma(f)}

2.5
, whose generic tropical type

is called the type of

\mathbf{\sigma}

\Sigma(W)=Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
and

Let

be the abelian group of curve classes in

[

of

By

The corresponding punctured Gromov–Witten invariant is

(2.6)

N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}:=\deg[\mathscr{M}_{\tau}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in\mathbb{Q}\,.

Let
\Lambda_{\tau_{\mathrm{out}}}
be the space of integral tangent vectors to the
(d-1)

(2.7)

\tau_{\mathrm{out}}:=\bigcup_{s\in Q_{\tau,\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}}L_{\mathrm{out}}

formed by the universal family of legs

over

. The derivative of the universal tropical map

induces a map

h_{*}:\Lambda_{\tau_{\mathrm{out}}}\rightarrow\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(L_{\mathrm{out}})}

coefficient

is defined as the order of the finite torsion subgroup of the quotient

(2.8)

k_{\tau}:=|\left(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(L_{\mathrm{out}})}/h_{*}(\Lambda_{\tau_{\mathrm{out}}})\right)_{\mathrm{tors}}|\,.

2.3.
HDTV scattering diagram

For any log Calabi-Yau pair
(X,D)
, Gross–Siebert
[
47
]
showed that the punctured Gromov–Witten invariants defined by (

) naturally define a canonical scattering diagram in the
tropicalization
(X,D)
of

.
In joint work of the first author with Gross
[
10
]
, it was shown that, for log Calabi-Yau pairs obtained as blow-ups of toric varieties, and so in particular for the log Calabi–Yau compactifications of cluster varieties as in §

HDTV scattering diagram
in what follows.
In this section, we review the definition of the HDTV scattering diagram. The main result of
[
10
]
relating punctured Gromov–Witten invariants and the HDTV scattering diagram will be reviewed in the next section.

Let
N
be a finite rank free abelian group and
(e_{i})_{i\in I}
a finite collection of elements

indexed by a finite set

. As in §

Let
\mathbb{Q}[M][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]
be the algebra of power series in variables

indexed by
i\in I
and with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}[M]
. We denote by
\mathfrak{m}
the ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[M][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]
generated by the variables

for all
i\in I
.
We recall below the notion of scattering diagram in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
following
[
10
, §5]

Definition 2.9
.

Fix a primitive

A

of direction
-m_{0}

(i)

primitive such that

.

(ii)

f_{\mathfrak{d}}\in\mathbb{Q}[z^{m_{0}}][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]\subset\mathbb{Q}[M][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]
with

, that is,

is a power series of the form

(2.9)

f_{\mathfrak{d}}=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}m_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{A}=(a_{i})_{i}\\
\in\mathbb{N}^{I}\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}c_{m,\mathbf{A}}z^{m}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{a_{i}}

, only finitely many

Definition 2.10
.

A wall
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
of direction
-m_{0}
is
incoming
if

m_{0}\in\mathfrak{d}
.

Definition 2.11
.

A
scattering diagram
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a set
\mathfrak{D}=\{(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\}
of walls in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exist only finitely many walls
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\in\mathfrak{D}
with
f_{\mathfrak{d}}\neq 1\mod\mathfrak{m}^{k}
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}=\{(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\}
be a scattering diagram in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. We define the
support
of
\mathfrak{D}
by

(2.10)

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}\mathfrak{d}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

and the

singular locus
of
\mathfrak{D}
by

(2.11)

\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}}\partial\mathfrak{d}\cup\bigcup_{\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}}\mathfrak{d}\cap\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

where
\partial\mathfrak{d}
denotes the boundary of a wall
\mathfrak{d}
, and the last union is over the pairs of walls
\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{codim}\mathfrak{d}\cap\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}\geq 2
.
For every point
x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})
, we define

(2.12)

f_{\mathfrak{D},x}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{d}\in\mathfrak{D}\\
x\in\mathfrak{d}\end{subarray}}f_{\mathfrak{d}}\,,

where the product is over all the walls of
\mathfrak{D}
containing

.
Two scattering diagrams
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
are called
equivalent
if

(2.13)

f_{\mathfrak{D},x}=f_{\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},x}

for all

.

Given a path

\displaystyle\alpha:[0,1]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\mathrm{Sing}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\longmapsto\alpha(\tau)\,

intersecting transversally

, and

such that

, we define an automorphism of

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[M][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]

\displaystyle z^{m}

\displaystyle\longmapsto f_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha(\tau)}^{\langle n_{\alpha,\tau},m\rangle}z^{m}\,,

where

is the unique primitive element in

such that
\mathfrak{d}\subset n_{\alpha,\tau}^{\perp}
for all walls
\mathfrak{d}
containing
\alpha(\tau)
, and

\langle n_{\alpha,\tau},\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)\rangle<0
.
The path ordered automorphism

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha}
is the product of automorphisms

\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D},\alpha,\tau}
for all

such that
\alpha(\tau)\in\operatorname{Supp}(\mathfrak{D})
, and ordered following increasing values of
\tau
.
Finally, a scattering diagram is called

if for any path

Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
be a symplectic seed and

For every

the fan in

It is the fan of the toric variety

.
For every codimension one cone

, there exists a unique codimension one cone

such that

.
Finally, we denote by

the set of codimension one cones

such that

.

The

\mathbf{s}
and a fan

, and such that every wall

3.
DT/punctured GW correspondence

3.1.
Quiver and cluster compatibility

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
be a symplectic seed with index set
I=\{i\in Q_{0}\,|\,\iota_{s_{i}}\omega_{Q}\neq 0\}
as in §
2.1
.
A
compatibility data
between
Q
and
\mathbf{s}
is a linear map

(3.1)

\psi\colon N_{Q}\longrightarrow N

with finite cokernel, that is, with
\psi\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is surjective,
such that
\psi(s_{i})=e_{i}
for all
i\in I
, and
\psi^{*}\omega=\omega_{Q}
, that is,
\omega(e_{i},e_{j})=\omega_{Q}(s_{i},s_{j})
for all
i,j\in I
.

Remark 3.2
.

By Definition
2.1
of a symplectic seed,
\omega\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is non-degenerate, and so the rank of
\omega
is equal to the rank of
N
. As
\psi\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is surjective, and
\psi^{\star}\omega=\omega_{Q}
, it follow that the rank of
N
is equal to the rank of
\omega_{Q}
. In particular, the cluster variety defined by the symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}
has dimension equal to the rank of the skew-symmetrized Euler form
\omega_{Q}
.

In what follows we provide examples of compatibility data.

Example 3.3
.

Let
Q
be a quiver.
Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)

be the symplectic seed defined by
N=N_{Q}/\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
,
e_{i}=\pi(s_{i})
for all
i\in I
, where
\psi:N_{Q}\rightarrow N
is the quotient map, and
\omega
the non-degenerate skew-symmetric form on
N
induced by
\omega_{Q}
. Then,
\psi
is a compatibility data between
Q
and
\mathbf{s}
.

3.2.
Comparison of scattering diagrams

Let
Q
be a quiver,
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
a symplectic seed, and
\psi:N_{Q}\rightarrow N
a compatibility data between
\mathbf{s}
and
Q
as in (
3.1
). We denote by

(3.2)

\psi^{\vee}:M_{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}

the dual map of
\psi
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
be a consistent
Q
-scattering diagram in
M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
without central walls, as in Definitions
1.17
-
1.18

We define a scattering diagram
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q}=\{(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})\}
in

in the sense of Definition
2.11
as follows:
for every wall
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})
of

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}}):=((\psi^{\vee})^{-1}(\mathfrak{d}),(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\mathfrak{d}})

Here,

(\psi^{\vee})^{-1}(\mathfrak{d})\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the preimage by
\psi
of
\mathfrak{d}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}

Note that as
(\mathfrak{d},f_{\mathfrak{d}})
is not central, the primitive direction
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}\in N^{+}_{Q}

is not in

\psi(n_{\mathfrak{d}})\neq 0
, and so, as
(\psi^{\vee})^{-1}(\mathfrak{d})
is contains in

(\psi^{\vee})^{-1}(\mathfrak{d})
is of codimension one in

if non empty.

Now to define
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\mathfrak{d}}

(3.3)

f_{\mathfrak{d}}=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}z^{k\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}}

with

\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}\in N_{Q}^{+}
in the basis

\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}s_{i}

with
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

Note that as
\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
is without central walls, it follows from Remark

that if

, then
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}=0
if

, and so
\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}}=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}s_{i}
, and
\psi(\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}})=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}e_{i}
.
Finally, we set

(3.4)

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\mathfrak{d}}:=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}z^{k\iota_{\psi(\gamma_{\mathfrak{d}})}\omega}\left(\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{k\gamma_{\mathfrak{d},i}}\right)\,.

Note that the finiteness condition in Definition
2.11
for
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
follows from the finiteness condition in Definition
1.17
for
\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
. Hence,
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
is indeed a scattering diagram.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
be a consistent
Q
-scattering diagram in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
without central walls. Then

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q}
is also consistent.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}((\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q})
be a continuous path intersecting
\operatorname{Supp}((\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q})
transversally. Then
\psi^{\vee}\circ\alpha
is a path in
M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(\mathfrak{D}_{Q})
intersecting
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathfrak{D}_{Q})
transversally.
In order to prove Lemma
3.4
, it is enough to show that if the automorphism
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{D}_{Q},\psi^{\vee}\circ\alpha}
of
\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{+}]\!]
defined in (
1.12

\mathfrak{p}_{(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\mathfrak{D}_{Q},\alpha}

of
\mathbb{Q}[M][\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]

defined in (
2.14
) is also the identity.
It is sufficient to prove this result for every finite truncation with respect to powers of the maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{Q}[\![N_{Q}^{+}]\!]
and
\mathbb{Q}[\![(t_{i})_{i\in I}]\!]

Denote by

the image in

\displaystyle N_{Q}^{+}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\longmapsto\iota_{\psi(\gamma)}\omega\,.

\displaystyle N_{Q}^{+}

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential having trivial attractor DT invariants. Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{i\in I},\omega)
be a symplectic seed with index set

I=\{i\in Q_{0}\,|\,\iota_{s_{i}}\omega_{Q}\neq 0\}

and
(X,D)
a log Calabi-Yau compactification of the corresponding cluster variety satisfying assumptions (
2.2
) and (
2.4
).
Let
\psi\colon N_{Q}\rightarrow N
be a compatibility data between
\mathbf{s}
and
Q
as in (
3.1
).
Let
\gamma\in N_{Q}\setminus\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
be a dimension vector such that
\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Then, for every general stability parameter
\theta\in\psi^{\vee}(M_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\gamma^{\perp}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
and point
x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\psi^{\vee}(x)=\theta
, we have

(3.7)

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\frac{1}{|\gamma|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{x}}k_{\tau}N_{\tau,\beta_{\gamma}^{x}}^{(X,D)}\,,

where
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
is the rational DT invariant of
(Q,W)
as in (
1.3
), and
N_{\tau,\beta_{\gamma}^{x}}^{(X,D)}
are the punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of
(X,D)
as in (
2.6
).

Proof.

Denote
\gamma_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{|\gamma|}
.
By (
1.13
), we have

f_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)},\theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\gamma_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}|\,\,\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}z^{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\,.

Applying the definition (
3.4
) of
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}
, we obtain

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)},\theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\gamma_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}|\,\,\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}z^{\iota_{\gamma^{\prime}}\omega}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\gamma^{\prime}_{i}}\right)\,.

Using
\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}s_{i}
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}s_{i}
, we have
\iota_{\gamma^{\prime}}\omega=\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}v_{i}
and so

(3.8)

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)},\theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\gamma^{\prime}_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}|\,\,\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}z^{\sum_{i\in I}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\gamma^{\prime}_{i}}\right)\,.

By Theorem
3.5
, we have
(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{(Q,W)}^{\mathrm{st}}=\mathfrak{D}_{\mathbf{s}}
and so

(\psi^{\vee})^{\star}f_{\overline{\mathfrak{D}}^{\mathrm{st}}_{(Q,W)},\theta}=f_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathbf{s}},x}=f_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathbf{s}},x}^{\mathrm{in}}f_{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathbf{s}},x}^{\mathrm{out}}\,.

Combining (
3.8
) with Theorem
2.13
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\gamma_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}|\,\,\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{+,\theta}z^{\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}^{\prime}v_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\gamma^{\prime}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I,\rho\in\Sigma_{i}\\
x\in\rho\end{subarray}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}k_{\rho}N_{\rho,\ell[E_{i}]}^{(X,D)}z^{\ell v_{i}}t_{i}^{\ell[E_{i}]}+\sum_{\mathbf{A}=(a_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{x}}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{A}}^{x}}k_{\tau}N_{\tau,\beta_{\mathbf{A}}^{x}}^{(X,D)}z^{\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}v_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{a_{i}}

It remains to isolate on both sides the terms proportional
z^{\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}v_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}
. As we are assuming that
\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for all
i\in I
, it follows that
z^{\sum_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}v_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}t_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}
is not equal to
z^{\ell v_{i}}t_{i}^{\ell[E_{i}]}
for any
i\in I
and
\ell\geq 1
. Hence, we conclude that

|\gamma|\,\,\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{x}}k_{\tau}N_{\tau,\beta_{\gamma}^{x}}^{(X,D)}\,,

and so (
3.7
) follows.

4.
Examples: local
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and cubic surfaces

In this section, we give two examples to illustrate Theorem
3.6
.

4.1.
The local projective plane

Let
Q
be the
3
-node quiver in Figure
4.1
. The skew-symmetric form
\omega_{Q}
on
N_{Q}=\mathbb{Z}s_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}s_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}s_{3}
satisfies

\omega_{Q}(s_{1},s_{2})=\omega_{Q}(s_{2},s_{3})=\omega_{Q}(s_{3},s_{1})=3\,.

Moreover,
\omega_{Q}
is of rank two and its kernel is given by
\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}=\mathbb{Z}(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3})
.

Figure 4.1.
The quiver attached to the local projective plane.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 3},\omega)
be the symplectic seed defined by
N=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

e_{1}=(1,1)\,,e_{2}=(-2,1)\,,e_{3}=(1,-2)\,,

and
\omega(-,-)=\det(-,-)
.
Then, the map
\psi:N_{Q}\rightarrow N
defined by
\psi(s_{i})=e_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
is a compatibility data in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
To construct the corresponding cluster variety, note that
\omega
is non-degenerate, and so we can use it to identify
M
with
N
and
v_{i}=\iota_{e_{i}}\omega
with
e_{i}
. Therefore, we consider the fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
consisting of the three rays
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{1}
,

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{2}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{3}
. The corresponding toric surface
X_{\Sigma}
has three toric
A_{2}
cyclic quotient singularities, which can be resolved torically if one insists on having
X_{\Sigma}
smooth and projective as in §
2.1
. Let
X
be the surface obtained by blowing-up a point on each of the toric divisors of
X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to the rays
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{1}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{2}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}e_{3}
, and
D
be the strict transform of the toric boundary divisor of
X_{\Sigma}
. Then,
U=X\setminus D
is the cluster variety defined by the symplectic seed
\mathbf{s}
, and
(X,D)
is a log Calabi–Yau compactification of
U
. As
\operatorname{coker}\psi\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, the holomorphic symplectic cluster variety
U
is a
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
-quotient of a symplectic fiber of the Poisson
\mathcal{X}
cluster variety defined by
Q
.

Then, as reviewed for example in

[
20
]
, there exists a potential
W
on
Q
and an equivalence of triangulated categories

(4.1)

\Phi:D^{b}\mathrm{Rep}(Q,W)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})

between the derived category of representations of
(Q,W)
and the derived category of coherent sheaves on the non-compact toric Calabi–Yau 3-fold given by the local projective plane
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-3)
, sending the three simple representations of
Q
to the spherical objects
E_{1}=\iota_{\star}\mathcal{O}[-1]
,
E_{2}=\iota_{\star}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1))
,
E_{3}=\iota_{\star}(\mathcal{O}(-1))[1]
in
D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
, where
\iota:\mathbb{P}^{2}\hookrightarrow K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
is the inclusion of the zero section.

By
[
20
, Thm 1]
,
(Q,W)
has trivial attractor DT invariants, and so Theorem
3.6
applies.
Theorem
3.6
computes
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
for
\theta\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\subset M_{Q,\mathbb{R}}
, that is, for
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
such that
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}=0
. For every
\gamma\in N_{Q}\setminus\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
such that
\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
, the intersection
\gamma^{\perp}\cap M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a line separated into two chambers: the attractor chamber
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}
, and the anti-attractor chamber
-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}
. If
\theta
is in the attractor chamber, then the set of wall types
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
is empty, and so, by Theorem
3.6
, we have

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{\theta}=0
. If
\theta
is in the anti-attractor chamber, then the set
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
contains a single wall type
\tau_{\gamma}^{\theta}
, given by the half-line coming out of the origin and passing by
\Upsilon(\theta)
in the tropicalization
B
of
(X,D)
– see
[
47
, Ex 3.14]
. Moreover, we have
k_{\tau}=|\iota_{\psi(\gamma)}\omega|
–
Therefore, by Theorem
3.6
, we have

(4.2)

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\frac{|\iota_{\psi(\gamma)}\omega|}{|\gamma|}N_{\tau_{\gamma}^{\theta},\beta_{\gamma}^{\theta}}^{(X,D)}\,.

Using the equivalence (
4.1
), one can deduce a result comparing geometric DT invariants of
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}

and punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of
(X,D)
. For every
v=(r,d,\chi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, one can define a geometric DT invariant
\overline{\Omega}_{v}^{+}
using the moduli space of Gieseker semistable coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of rank
r
, degree
d
, and Euler characteristic
\gamma
– see for example
[
18
]
.
By
[
20
, (5.4)]
, under the equivalence
(
4.1
), a coherent sheaf of class
v
is mapped to an object of
D^{b}\mathrm{Rep}(Q,W)
of dimension vector

\gamma(v):=(-\chi,r+d-\chi,r+2d-\chi)\,.

While there are in general walls in the space of Bridgeland stability conditions on
D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
separating the quiver DT invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma(v)}^{+,\theta}
and the geometric DT
\overline{\Omega}_{v}^{+}
, these walls are absent for
normalized
coherent sheaves, that is, with slope
\mu=\frac{d}{r}
satisfying
-1<\mu\leq 0
:
we have

(4.3)

\overline{\Omega}_{v}^{+}=\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma(v)}^{+,\theta}

for
\theta
in the anti-attractor chamber
-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma(v)}\omega_{Q}
.
Indeed, in this case, moduli spaces of Gieseker semistable sheaves coincide with moduli spaces of
\theta
-semistable quiver representations by

[
35
, Prop 2.3]
and using
[
20
, §5.2, proof of Thm 1]
to compare representations of
Q
with representations of the Beilinson quiver obtained by removing from
Q
all the three arrows between a given pair of vertices. A pictorial proof can also be obtained by looking at the scattering diagram
\mathcal{D}_{\psi}
of
[
20
]
for
\psi=0
: the rays of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
going out from the orbifold point, where the quiver description is valid, go directly to the large volume region, where the geometric description is valid, without any further scattering.
Therefore, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
v=(r,d,\chi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with
\mu:=\frac{d}{r}
satisfying
-1<\mu\leq 0
, we have the following correspondence between geometric DT invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{v}^{+}
of the local projective plane
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
and the punctured Gromov–Witten invariants
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}
of the log Calabi–Yau surface
(X,D)
:

\overline{\Omega}_{v}^{+}=\frac{|\iota_{\psi(\gamma(v))}\omega|}{|\gamma(v)|}N_{\tau_{\gamma(v)}^{\theta},\beta_{\gamma(v)}^{\theta}}^{(X,D)}\,,

where
\theta
is a point in the anti-attractor chamber
-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma(v)}\omega_{Q}
.

Proof.

The result follows by combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).

Remark 4.2
.

One can check that the classes
v
such that
\Omega_{v}^{+}=1
are exactly the classes of the exceptional vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and that the corresponding curve classes
\beta^{\theta}_{\gamma(v)}
on
(X,D)
are exactly the curve classes coming from exceptional curve classes of toric models of
(X,D)
. Moreover, under the correspondence
v\mapsto\beta^{\theta}_{\gamma(v)}
, the mutations of exceptional vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}

[
35
]
correspond to the mutations of the toric models of
(X,D)

[
40
]
.

Theorem
4.1
is compatible with the heuristic picture of
[
14
, §7]
describing DT invariants of
K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
in terms of holomorphic curves via mirror symmetry and hyperkähler rotation. In particular, the holomorphic symplectic cluster variety
U
is exactly the mirror of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)
, where
E
is a smooth elliptic curve, when we obtain
X
by blowing up three points whose sum is linearly equivalent to zero on the toric boundary divisor of
X_{\Sigma}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Using
[
15
]
, one can refine Theorem
4.1
to a refined DT/higher genus Gromov–Witten correspondence.

4.2.
Cubic surfaces

Let
Q
be the octahedral quiver in Figure
4.2
. This quiver is mutation equivalent to the elliptic Dynkin
D_{4}^{(1,1)}
quiver.
Let
\mathbf{s}=(N,(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 3},\omega)
be the symplectic seed defined by
N=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

e_{1}=e_{2}=(1,0)\,,e_{3}=e_{4}=(0,1)\,,e_{5}=e_{6}=(-1,-1)\,,

and
\omega(-,-)=\det(-,-)
. Then, one checks that the map
\psi:N_{Q}\rightarrow N
defined by
\psi(s_{i})=e_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 6
is a compatibility data in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
In particular, this shows that
\omega_{Q}
is of rank two.

Figure 4.2.
The quiver whose cluster variety is the cubic surface.

A log Calabi–Yau compactification
(X,D)
of the corresponding cluster variety is obtained by blowing-up two general points on each of the the three toric divisors of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In particular,
X
is a cubic surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
D
is an anticanonical triangle of lines on
X
, and the holomorphic symplectic cluster surface
U=X\setminus D
is an affine cubic surface. As
\psi
is surjective,
U
is isomorphic to a symplectic fiber of the Poisson
\mathcal{X}
cluster variety defined by
Q
.

The quiver
Q
can be obtained from an ideal triangulation of the 4-punctured sphere – see for example
[
23
, Fig 9]
.
In particular, as reviewed in Example
1.11
, it admits a quiver with potential
W

[
54
]
such that
(Q,W)
has trivial attractor
DT invariants, and so we can apply Theorem
3.6
. As in §
4.1
, for every
\gamma\in N_{Q}\setminus\operatorname{ker}\omega_{Q}
such that
\gamma\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}s_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 6
, the intersection
\gamma^{\perp}\cap M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the union of the attractor chamber
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}
and of the anti-attractor chamber
-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma}\omega_{Q}
.
If
\theta
is in the attractor chamber, then
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{\theta}=0
. If
\theta
is in the anti-attractor chamber, then
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}^{\theta}
contains a single wall
\tau_{\gamma}^{\theta}
, and so, by Theorem
3.6
, we have

(4.4)

\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}=\frac{|\iota_{\psi(\gamma)}\omega|}{|\gamma|}N_{\tau_{\gamma}^{\theta},\beta_{\gamma}^{\theta}}^{(X,D)}\,.

This example is particularly interesting because all the punctured Gromov–Witten invariants
N_{\tau,\beta}^{(X,D)}
have been explicitly computed in
[
42
]
– see also
[
19
]
. In particular, it follows that every non-zero DT invariant
\overline{\Omega}_{\gamma}^{+,\theta}
is equal to either one or two.

Moreover, the collection of these DT invariants has a physics interpretation as the BPS spectrum of the 4-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric
SU(2)
gauge theory with
N_{f}=4
flavors. The quiver with potential
(Q,W)
is the BPS quiver of this theory – see for example
[
6
, §4.7]
and
[
24
]
where the two mutated versions of
Q
are respectively mentioned. The BPS quiver has a natural origin from the class
\mathcal{S}
construction of the gauge theory by compactification of the 6-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)

A_{1}
superconformal field theory on a 4-punctured sphere

[
38
, §10.7]
, whereas the description of BPS states in terms of punctured Gromov–Witten invariants of
(X,D)
arises from the realization of the gauge theory on the worldvolume of a M5-brane wrapping a torus Lagrangian in
U=X\setminus D
, as reviewed in
[
19
, §1.3]
.

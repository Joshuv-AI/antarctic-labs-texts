The semidirect product case, which is important for applications in Hamiltonian mechanics, is also discussed.

Introduction

For many physical systems the configuration space is the total space of a principal bundle
P(M,G)
over a base manifold
M
which parametrizes the external degrees of freedom of the system under consideration. That is, there is a symmetry group
G
which acts freely on
P
with a quotient manifold
P/G=M
and the projection
P\rightarrow M
is locally trivial. In this situation the action of
G
lifts to the phase space
T^{*}P
with quotient manifold
T^{*}P/G
. The action of
G
on
T^{*}P
is by symplectomorphisms and so the symplectic structure descends to a Poisson structure on
T^{*}P/G
. This quotient space and its Poisson structure encode the mechanics and the symmetry of the original system.

The typical situation is when the Hamiltonian of the system is invariant with respect to the cotangent lift of the action of
G
on
P
. Then the quotient manifold
T^{*}P/G
becomes the reduced phase space of the system. For example the above happens in rigid body mechanics
[
6
]
or when one formulates the equation of motion of a classical particle in a Yang–Mills field: the electromagnetic field is a particular case
[
10
,
11
]
.

When the action of
G
is lifted to
TP
the quotient manifold
TP/G
is the Atiyah algebroid of
P(M.G)
, or equivalently the Lie algebroid of the associated gauge groupoid. There is a natural structure of vector bundle on
TP/G
with base
M
, and sections of this correspond to vector fields on
P
which are invariant under the group action; these are closed under the Lie bracket and define a bracket on the sections of
TP/G\rightarrow M
.

There are interesting geometric objects which include
TP/G
and
T^{*}P/G
as their crucial components. In this paper we will consider the Atiyah sequence (
3.14
) and the dual Atiyah sequence (
3.15
) as well as the dual pair of Poisson manifolds (
3.11
). From the dual Atiyah sequence we also construct a short exact sequence of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids (
4.6
) over the gauge groupoid
\frac{P\times P}{G}\rightrightarrows P/G
. The concept of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid
[
8
]
is recalled in Section
2
. The short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids which we construct and study (see in particular the diagrams (
4.6
) and (
4.10
)) relate the various algebraic and Poisson structures inherent in the situation.

Even the case
P=G
is very productive from the point of view of the applications in Hamiltonian mechanics as well as Lie groupoid theory. Note here that the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows T_{e}^{*}G
is the symplectic groupoid of the Lie–Poisson structure of the dual
T^{*}_{e}G=T^{*}G/G
of the Lie algebra
T_{e}G
of
G
, see
[
1
]
. As we will see, for a general principal bundle
P
the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows T^{*}_{e}{G}
is replaced by the symplectic groupoid
T^{*}\big(\frac{P\times P}{G}\big)\rightrightarrows T^{*}P/G
which one obtains by the dualization (in the sense of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid dualization
[
5
,
8
]
) of the tangent prolongation groupoid
T\big(\frac{P\times P}{G})\rightrightarrows T(P/G\big)
of the gauge groupoid.

We now describe the contents of each section in detail.

Section
2
is concerned with aspects of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids: their duality, principal actions of a Lie group
G
on a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid, and short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids. The main results are presented in Proposition
2.6
and Proposition
2.12
, which show that for
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids, and for short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids, the operations of dualization and quotient, commute.

In Section
3
we recall the definitions of Atiyah sequence and dual pair of Poisson manifolds and describe the relationship between these notions. Using this relationship we describe in detail the ‘double fibration’ structure of the symplectic leaves of
T^{*}P/G
; see Theorem
3.5
.

In Section
4
, we extend the Atiyah sequence (
3.14
) and its dual (
3.15
) to short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids over the gauge groupoid, see (

) and (

T^{*}P/G
and
\frac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}

T^{*}\big(\frac{P\times P}{G}\big)\rightrightarrows T^{*}P/G
and
\frac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}\rightrightarrows T^{*}P/G

The case when
P
is a group

G=N

[

2
Short exact sequences of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}}
-groupoids

The groupoids with which the paper will be chiefly concerned are the tangents and cotangents of gauge groupoids, and certain groupoids related to them. For any groupoid
\mathscr{G}
the tangent bundle
T\mathscr{G}
has both a Lie groupoid and a vector bundle structure and these are related in a natural way. Owing to the relations between these structures, the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\mathscr{G}
also has a natural Lie groupoid structure, and indeed is a symplectic groupoid with respect to the canonical cotangent symplectic structure
[
2
]
.

The relations between the groupoid and vector bundle structures on
T\mathscr{G}
were abstracted by Pradines
[
8
]
to the concept of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid, and
[
8
]
showed that for a general
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid
\Omega
, dualizing the vector bundle structure on
\Omega
leads to a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid structure on
\Omega^{*}
. We recall this construction in Sections
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
. For further detail, see
[
5
, Chapter 11]
.

In Section
2.4
we show that, under a natural condition, short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids may be dualized; see Proposition
2.6
. We then show that when the base of a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid is the total space of a principal bundle, the group of which acts on, and preserves the structure of, the
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid, the quotient spaces form another
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid (Theorem
2.7
). Finally in Section
2.5
we show that the two processes – taking the quotient and taking the dual – commute (Proposition
2.12
).

2.1
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}}
-groupoids

We consider manifolds with both a groupoid structure and a vector bundle structure. The compatibility condition between the groupoid and vector bundle structures can be described succinctly by saying that the groupoid multiplication must be a morphism of vector bundles and that the vector bundle addition must be a morphism of groupoids. In fact these conditions are expressed by a single equation. In order to formulate the equation several conditions on the source, target and bundle projections are necessary.

\Omega

B

\mathscr{G}

is to be a vector bundle on base
P
and

is to be a Lie groupoid on the same base

. (At present

is an arbitrary manifold; later it will denote a principal bundle.) These structures are shown in Fig.

\widetilde{1}_{-b}=-\widetilde{1}_{b}

\displaystyle\widetilde{1}_{b_{1}+b_{2}}=\widetilde{1}_{b_{1}}+\widetilde{1}_{b_{2}},\qquad(\xi+\eta)^{-1}=\xi^{-1}+\eta^{-1}.

2.2
The core of a
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}}
-groupoid

A necessary ingredient in the duality of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids is the concept of core. The
core

K
of a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid
(\Omega;B;\mathscr{G};P)
is the intersection of the kernel of the bundle projection
\widetilde{\lambda}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
with the kernel of the source
\widetilde{\textbf{s}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow B
.

Equivalently,
K
is the preimage under
(\widetilde{\textbf{s}},\widetilde{\lambda})
of the closed submanifold
\{(0_{p},1_{p})\,|\,p\in P\}
. Since
(\widetilde{\textbf{s}},\widetilde{\lambda})

is assumed to be a surjective submersion,
K
is a closed submanifold of
\Omega
.

Equivalently again,
K
is the pullback of
\ker(\widetilde{\lambda})
along the unit map
P\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
. This equips
K
with a natural structure of vector bundle with base
P
.

Example 2.2
.

For any Lie groupoid
\mathscr{G}\rightrightarrows P
, the groupoid
T\mathscr{G}\rightrightarrows TP
obtained by applying the tangent functor to the structure of
\mathscr{G}
, is a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid,
(T\mathscr{G};TP,\mathscr{G};P)
. The core is the Lie algebroid
A\mathscr{G}
.

When
\mathscr{G}
is a Lie group
G
, the multiplication, denoted
\bullet
, in the tangent group
TG
obtained by applying the tangent functor to the multiplication in
G
, is given by the explicit formula

\displaystyle X_{g}\bullet Y_{h}=TL_{g}(h)Y_{h}+TR_{h}(g)X_{g},

(2.4)

where
L_{g}(h):=gh
and
R_{h}(g):=gh
. In particular for
X_{e},\ Y_{e}\in T_{e}G
and zero elements
0_{g}\in T_{g}G
and
0_{h}\in T_{h}G
one has

\displaystyle X_{e}\bullet Y_{e}=X_{e}+Y_{e},\qquad 0_{g}\bullet 0_{h}=0_{gh},\qquad 0_{g}\bullet X_{e}=TL_{g}(e)X_{e}.

(2.5)

The group
TG
is isomorphic to the semi-direct product
G\ltimes T_{e}G
with respect to the adjoint action.

For general groupoids
T\mathscr{G}\rightrightarrows TP
is not a groupoid semi-direct product.

2.3
The dual of a
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}}
-groupoid

Since
\widetilde{\lambda}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
is a vector bundle, it has a dual bundle
\widetilde{\lambda}_{*}\colon\Omega^{*}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
. Somewhat surprisingly, this has a natural Lie groupoid structure with base
K^{*}
and these structures make
(\Omega^{*};K^{*},\mathscr{G};P)
a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid
[
8
]
.

Take
\Phi\in\Omega^{*}_{\gamma}
where
\gamma=\widetilde{\lambda}(\Phi)\in\mathscr{G}
has source
p=\textbf{s}(\gamma)
and target
q=\textbf{t}(\gamma)
. Then the target and source of
\Phi
in
K^{*}_{q}
and
K^{*}_{p}
respectively are defined to be

\displaystyle\big\langle{\widetilde{\textbf{t}\mkern 6.0mu}\mkern-6.0mu}{}_{*}(\Phi),k\big\rangle=\big\langle\Phi,k\widetilde{0}_{\gamma}\big\rangle,\quad k\in K_{q},\qquad\langle\widetilde{\textbf{s}}_{*}(\Phi),k\rangle=\big\langle\Phi,-\widetilde{0}_{\gamma}k^{-1}\big\rangle,\quad k\in K_{p}.

The lack of symmetry is unavoidable: in defining the core one must take either the kernel of
s
or of
t
.

For the composition, take
\Psi\in\Omega^{*}_{\delta}
with
\widetilde{\textbf{s}}_{*}(\Psi)={\widetilde{\textbf{t}\mkern 6.0mu}\mkern-6.0mu}{}_{*}(\Phi)
. To define
\Psi\Phi\in\Omega^{*}_{\delta\gamma}
we must define the pairing of
\Psi\Phi
with any element of
\Omega_{\delta\gamma}
. Now any element
\zeta
of
\Omega_{\delta\gamma}
can be written as a product
\eta\xi
where
\eta\in\Omega_{\delta}
and
\xi\in\Omega_{\gamma}
. We claim that

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\Phi,\eta\xi\rangle=\langle\Psi,\eta\rangle+\langle\Phi,\xi\rangle

(2.6)

is well defined. Any other choice of
\eta
and
\xi
must be
\eta\tau^{-1}
and
\tau\xi
for some
\tau
with
\widetilde{\textbf{s}}(\tau)=\widetilde{\textbf{s}}(\eta)={\widetilde{\textbf{t}\mkern 6.0mu}\mkern-6.0mu}{}(\xi)
. Also,
\tau
must project to
1_{q}\in\mathscr{G}
where
q=\textbf{s}(\delta)
, since
\eta\tau^{-1}
must lie in the same fibre over
\mathscr{G}
as
\eta
.

Write
b=\widetilde{\textbf{s}}(\tau)
. Then
\tau-\widetilde{1}_{b}
is defined and is a core element, say
k
. Now we have
\eta\tau^{-1}=\eta(\widetilde{1}_{b}+k)^{-1}=(\eta+\widetilde{0}_{\delta})(\widetilde{1}_{b}+k^{-1})=\eta+\widetilde{0}_{\delta}k^{-1}
so

\displaystyle\big\langle\Psi,\eta\tau^{-1}\big\rangle=\big\langle\Psi,\eta\rangle+\langle\Psi,\widetilde{0}_{\delta}k^{-1}\big\rangle.

Proceeding in the same way, we likewise find that

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,\tau\xi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\xi\rangle+\big\langle\Phi,k\widetilde{0}_{\gamma}\big\rangle.

From
\widetilde{\textbf{s}}_{*}(\Psi)={\widetilde{\textbf{t}\mkern 6.0mu}\mkern-6.0mu}{}_{*}(\Phi)
we know that
-\langle\Psi,\widetilde{0}_{\delta}k^{-1}\rangle=\langle\Phi,k\widetilde{0}_{\gamma}\rangle
, so (
2.6
) is well-defined.

We now define the identity element of
\Omega^{*}
at
\chi\in K^{*}_{p}
. To do this we need to pair
\widetilde{1}_{\chi}
with elements of
\Omega
which project to
1_{p}\in\mathscr{G}
. Take such an element
\xi
and write
b=\widetilde{\lambda}(\xi)
. Then
\xi-\widetilde{1}_{b}
is a core element
k
and we define

\displaystyle\big\langle\widetilde{1}_{\chi},\widetilde{1}_{b}+k\big\rangle=\langle\chi,k\rangle.

It is now straightforward to check the proof of the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoid
(\Omega,B,\mathscr{G};P)
, the construction above yields a

-groupoid

.

Given

, we identify it with the element

defined by

\displaystyle\big\langle\overline{\omega},\widetilde{1}_{b}+k\big\rangle=\big\langle\omega,b+{\widetilde{\textbf{t}\mkern 6.0mu}\mkern-6.0mu}{}(k)\big\rangle.

Example 2.4
.

Given a Lie groupoid

, the dual of the

-groupoid
(T\mathscr{G};\mathscr{G},TP;P)
is

is a symplectic groupoid with respect to the canonical symplectic structure on

2.4
Short exact sequences of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}}
-groupoids and their duals

The main constructions of the paper rely on dualizing short exact sequences of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids and in this subsection we give the basic results for this process. First we need the concept of morphism.

A
morphism of
\mathcal{V}\!\mathcal{B}
-groupoids

(\Omega;B,\mathscr{G};P)\rightarrow(\Omega^{\prime};B^{\prime},\mathscr{G}^{\prime};P^{\prime})

It follows that

For the purposes of this paper we only need to consider the dualization of morphisms which preserve

3
The dual Atiyah sequence

The Lie algebroid of a gauge groupoid is the Atiyah algebroid of the corresponding principal bundle
P
, and this Lie algebroid is the central term of a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids, the Atiyah sequence. Accordingly, the dual of this Lie algebroid is the central term of a short exact sequence of vector bundles with Poisson structures. In this section we set up the notation needed for the study of this dual sequence. We also investigate the fibre structure of the symplectic leaves of the Poisson manifold
T^{*}P/G
using the notion of dual pair together with that of Atiyah sequence. The main results are collected in Theorem
3.5
. Throughout the section,
P
denotes a principal bundle unless otherwise specified.

3.1

We consider a principal
G
-bundle

with the notation of Section

and obtain the action

\displaystyle T\kappa\colon\ TP\times TG\rightarrow TP

2.4

\displaystyle\kappa(p,g)=\kappa_{p}(g)=\kappa_{g}(p)=pg

(iv)

of symplectic forms, where

.

.

If

Proposition 4.5
.

The action of the symplectic groupoid
T^{*}\big(\frac{P\times P}{G}\big)\rightrightarrows\frac{T^{*}P}{G}
on the symplectic realization
\textbf{t}\colon J_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G\rightarrow\frac{T^{*}P}{G}
is a symplectic action; that is, the graph of the action

\displaystyle T^{*}\big(\tfrac{P\times P}{G}\big)\times_{T^{*}P/G}J_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G\rightarrow J_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G

(4.16)

is Lagrangian and
\textbf{t}\colon J_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G\rightarrow\frac{T^{*}P}{G}
is a Poisson map which is equivariant with respect to this action and the natural action of
T^{*}\big(\tfrac{P\times P}{G}\big)
on its base. Corresponding results with signs reversed hold for the action of
T^{*}\big(\frac{P\times P}{G}\big)\rightrightarrows\frac{T^{*}P}{G}
on
\textbf{s}\colon J_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G\rightarrow\frac{T^{*}P}{G}

Proof.

First consider the multiplication

\displaystyle\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}\times_{T^{*}P/G}\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}\rightarrow\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}

(4.17)

in the groupoid
\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}\rightrightarrows T^{*}P/G
. This is the quotient over
G
of the groupoid
T^{*}P\times T^{*}P\rightrightarrows T^{*}P
(with the structure from (
4.9
)), which is a symplectic groupoid with respect to the form
d(\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\gamma+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\gamma)
, where
\gamma
is the canonical 1-form on

G
preserves the symplectic structure,
\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}\rightrightarrows T^{*}P/G
is a Poisson groupoid. In particular, the graph of the multiplication (

\overline{\mathscr{G}}\times\mathscr{G}\times\mathscr{G}
where we write
\mathscr{G}=\tfrac{T^{*}P\times T^{*}P}{G}
for brevity.

The map (
4.16
) is a restriction of the multiplication (
4.17
). By (

T^{*}\big(\tfrac{P\times P}{G}\big)
is
(\iota_{2}^{*})^{-1}(0)
and

is a morphism, it follows that the groupoid multiplication of an element of

Further, since

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\langle((T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}),T\pi^{*}((T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}))T(T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\langle((T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}),T(\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}))X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\langle\theta_{k}\circ T\mu(h)+\chi_{u}\circ TL_{\sigma(k)^{-1}}(h)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\circ(\text{id}_{T_{h}H}-T(R_{\iota(u)}\circ\sigma\circ\mu)(h)),T(\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}))X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\langle\theta_{k},T(\mu\circ\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\langle\chi_{u},TL_{\iota(u)}(e)\circ\alpha\circ T(\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma)(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle,

(5.16)

where for the last equality we used (
5.4
). Now we observe that

\displaystyle\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma=\Sigma\circ((\pi^{*}_{K}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K}^{*})\times(\pi_{N}^{*}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{*}))

(5.17)

and

\displaystyle\nu\circ\pi^{*}\circ T^{*}\Sigma=\pi^{*}_{K}\circ\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{K}.

(5.18)

Substituting (
5.17

5.18
) into (
5.16

\displaystyle\langle((T^{*}\Sigma)_{*}\gamma_{H})(\theta_{K},\chi_{u}),X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle=\langle\theta_{k},T(\pi^{*}_{K}\circ\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{K})(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\langle\chi_{u},TL_{\iota(u)}(e)\circ\alpha\circ T(\Sigma\circ((\pi^{*}_{K}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K}^{*})\times(\pi_{N}^{*}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{*})))(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times(\pi_{N}^{*}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{N}^{*})))_{*}(\theta_{k},\chi_{u}),X_{(\theta_{k},\chi_{u})}\rangle,

which gives (
5.15

The first term in (
5.15
) is the pullback of
\gamma_{K}
and the second one,
called the “magnetic term”, is the pullback of the connection form
\alpha
(superposed with
\chi_{u}\circ TL_{\iota(u)}(e)\in T^{*}_{e}N)
on the product

as a section of

T^{*}K

T^{*}K\times T^{*}N
. Similarly, the magnetic term is linear on the fibres of
T^{*}N
, constant on the fibres of
T^{*}K
, but not constant on the base
K\times N
of
T^{*}K\times T^{*}N
.

Since

on

\displaystyle\{e\}\times N\ni(e,w)\mapsto\big(\text{id}\times\Ad^{-1}_{w^{-1}}\big)\in\operatorname{Aut}(T^{*}K\times T^{*}_{e}N).

defines a Hamiltonian system on the Poisson manifold

In this case the coadjoint representation
\Ad^{*}\colon N\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}T^{*}_{e}N

\Ad^{*}=\text{id}

\displaystyle J^{-1}(a)/N\cong(J\circ T^{*}\Sigma)^{-1}((0,a))/N\cong T^{*}K\times J_{N}^{-1}(a)/N\cong T^{*}K\times\{0\}\cong T^{*}K.

Shunsuke Kitamura, Katsuaki Morisawa

Abstract
This paper is devoted to the initial value problems for semilinear wave equations of derivative type with spatial weights in one space dimension. The lifespan estimates of classical solutions are quite different from those for nonlinearity of unknown function itself as the global-in-time existence can be established by spatial decay.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the initial value problems;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\frac{|u_{t}|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}&\mbox{in}\quad{\bf R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),&x\in{\bf R},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p>1
,
a\in{\bf R}
,
f
and
g
are given smooth functions of compact support
and a parameter
{\varepsilon}>0
is “small enough”.
We are interested in the estimate of the lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
,
the maximal existence time,
of classical solutions of (
1.1
).
Our result is the following;

\begin{array}[]{l}T({\varepsilon})\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(-a)}&\mbox{for}\ a<0,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{for}\ a=0,\end{array}\right.\\
T({\varepsilon})=\infty\qquad\mbox{for}\quad a>0.\end{array}

(1.2)

Here we denote the fact that there are positive constants,

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, independent of
{\varepsilon}
satisfying
A({\varepsilon},C_{1})\leq T({\varepsilon})\leq A({\varepsilon},C_{2})

by
T({\varepsilon})\sim A({\varepsilon},C)
.
We note that (
1.2
) is established for classical solutions when
p\geq 2
,
while we have to consider
C^{1}
solutions of associated integral equations to (
1.1
)
in case of
1<p<2
.
When
a=-1
, the upper bounds in (
1.2
) are already obtained by Zhou
[
11
]
,
while the lower bounds are verified only for integer
p
by general theory
which is studied by Li, Yu and Zhou
[
6
,
7
]
.
We see that (
1.2
) is similar to the one for the time-weighted nonlinear terms of
unknown function itself by Kato, Takamura and Wakasa
[
3
]

in sense that there is a possibility to obtain the global-in-time existence in spite of one dimension.
For such an equation, the lifespan estimates are classified into two cases according to
the value of the total integral of the initial speed.
But (
1.2
) has no classification whatever it is.
This is due to the fact that Huygens’ principle is always available
for the time derivative of the solution of the free wave equation.

In fact, let us compare (
1.1
) with

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\frac{|u|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}&\mbox{in}\quad{\bf R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),&x\in{\bf R}.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

In our previous work
[
4
]
, it is established that

T({\varepsilon})\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{for}\ a<0,\\
\phi^{-1}(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)})&\mbox{for}\ a=0,\\
C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}&\mbox{for}\ a>0\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{if}\ \int_{\bf R}g(x)dx\neq 0,

(1.4)

where
\phi^{-1}
is an inverse function of
\phi
defined by

\phi(s):=s\log(2+s),

and

T({\varepsilon})\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)/(1-pa)}&\mbox{for}\ a<0,\\
\psi_{p}^{-1}(C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)})&\mbox{for}\ a=0,\\
C{\varepsilon}^{-p(p-1)}&\mbox{for}\ a>0\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{if}\ \int_{\bf R}g(x)dx=0,

(1.5)

where
\psi_{p}^{-1}
is an inverse function of
\psi_{p}
defined by

\psi_{p}(s):=s\log^{p}(2+s).

We remark that the quantities in all the cases of (
1.4
) are smaller than
those of (
1.5
).
This work in
[
4
]
is an extension of the case of
a=-1
by Zhou
[
10
]
,
and inspired by non-compactly supported case by Suzuki
[
8
]
,
Kubo, Osaka and Yazici
[
5
]
and Wakasa
[
9
]
.
See Introduction in
[
4
]
for details.

Finally, we note that our result cannot be valid with our method in this paper
if
|u_{t}|^{p}
in (
1.1
) is replaced with
|u_{x}|^{p}
even in the non-weighted case,
a=-1
,
because the blow-up part requires a positiveness of the nonlinear terms
in the level of the
x
-derivative of the unknown function.
But one can find immediately that it is impossible by the expression of
u_{x}

in (
2.9
).
On contrast, it is possible to obtain the same lifespan estimate from below, the existence part,
along with our method.

This work was almost completed when the first and second authors were in the master course of Mathematical Institute, Tohoku University
and the third author had the second affiliation with
Research Alliance Center of Mathematical Sciences, Tohoku University.
This paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, (
1.2
) is divided into two theorems,
and the preliminaries are introduced.
Section 3 is devoted to the proof of the existence part of (
1.2
).
The main strategy is the iteration method in the weighted
L^{\infty}
space
which is originally introduced by John
[
1
]
.
In Section 4, we prove a priori estimate.
Finally, we prove the blow-up part of (
1.2
)
employing the method by Zhou
[
11
]
in Section 5.

2
Preliminaries and main results

Throughout of this paper, we assume that the initial data

(f,g)\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}_{0}({\bf R})
satisfies

\mbox{\rm supp }f,\ \mbox{supp }g\subset\{x\in{\bf R}:|x|\leq R\},\quad R\geq 1.

(2.1)

Let
u
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) in the time interval
[0,T]
.
Then the support condition of the initial data, (
2.1
), implies that

\mbox{supp}\ u(x,t)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}.

(2.2)

For example, see Appendix in John
[
2
]
for this fact.

It is well-known that
u
satisfies the following integral equation;

u(x,t)={\varepsilon}u^{0}(x,t)+L_{a}(|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t),

(2.3)

where
u^{0}
is a solution of the free wave equation with the same initial data;

u^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f(x+t)+f(x-t)\}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}g(y)dy,

(2.4)

and a linear integral operator
L_{a}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is Duhamel’s term defined by

L_{a}(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{x-t+s}^{x+t-s}\frac{v(y,s)}{(1+y^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}dy.

(2.5)

Then, one can apply the time-derivative to (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) to obtain

u_{t}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}(x,t)+L_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)

(2.6)

and

u_{t}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)+g(x-t)\},

(2.7)

where
L_{a}^{\prime}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{a}^{\prime}(v)(x,t):=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x+t-s,s)}{\{1+(x+t-s)^{2}\}^{(1+a)/2}}ds\\
&+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x-t+s,s)}{\{1+(x-t+s)^{2}\}^{(1+a)/2}}ds.\end{array}

(2.8)

On the other hand, applying the space-derivative to (
2.3
) and (
2.4
),
we have

u_{x}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}(x,t)+\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)

and

u_{x}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)-g(x-t)\},

where
\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(v)(x,t):=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x+t-s,s)}{\{1+(x+t-s)^{2}\}^{(1+a)/2}}ds\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x-t+s,s)}{\{1+(x-t+s)^{2}\}^{(1+a)/2}}ds.\end{array}

(2.9)

Therefore,
u_{x}
is expressed by
u_{t}
.
Moreover, one more space-derivative to (
2.6
) yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+pL_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,t)-(1+a)L_{a+2}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

(2.10)

and

u_{tx}^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)+g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

(2.11)

Similarly, we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tt}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)+\frac{|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}\\
&\displaystyle+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,t)-(1+a)\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

(2.12)

and

u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)-g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

Therefore,
u_{tt}
is expressed by
u_{tx}
and
u_{t}
, so is
u_{xx}
because of

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{xx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,t)-(1+a)\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)=u^{0}_{tt}(x,t).

First, we note the following fact.

Proposition 2.1

Assume that
(f,g)\in C^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}({\bf R})
.
Let
u_{t}
be a
C^{1}
solution of (
2.6
).
Then,

w(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}u_{t}(x,s)ds+{\varepsilon}f(x)

(2.13)

is a classical solution of (
1.1
).

Proof.
It is trivial that
w
satisfies the initial condition and

w_{t}=u_{t},\quad w_{tt}=u_{tt}.

(2.14)

Then, (
2.10
) yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{x}(x,t)&\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}u_{tx}(x,s)ds+{\varepsilon}f^{\prime}(x)\\
&\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\{pL_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,s)-(1+a)L_{a+2}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,s)\}ds\\
&\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}(x,s)ds+{\varepsilon}f^{\prime}(x)\\
&\displaystyle=\overline{L^{\prime}_{a}}(|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)+{\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}(x,t)\end{array}

because of

pL_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,s)-(1+a)L_{a+2}^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,s)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\overline{L^{\prime}_{a}}(|u_{t}|^{p})(x,s).

Therefore we obtain that

\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{xx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx})(x,t)-(1+a)\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

which implies, together with (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), the desired conclusion,

w_{tt}-w_{xx}=\frac{|u_{t}|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}=\frac{|w_{t}|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{(1+a)/2}}.

\Box

) is splitted into the following two theorems.

)

(2.16)

a solution

\mbox{\rm supp}\ u_{t}(x,t)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}

.

satisfying
\|U\|,\|U_{x}\|\leq 2M{\varepsilon}

follows from

C_{0}{\varepsilon}^{p-1}E_{a}(T)\leq 1.

Therefore we obtain Theorem
2.1
for
p\geq 2
.

For
1<p<2
,
X
and
M
in the proof for
p\geq 2
above are replaced
with
Y
and
N
respectively, where

Y:=\{U\in C({\bf R}\times[0,T]):\ \mbox{supp}\ U\subset\{|x|\leq t+R\}\}

equipping

\|U\|_{Y}:=\|U\|=\sup_{{\bf R}\times[0,T]}|U(x,t)|

and

N:=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf R})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf R})},

respectively.
It is trivial that the convergence of
\{U_{j}\}
in the closed subspace
Y

satisfying
\|U\|\leq 2N{\varepsilon}
follows from (
3.6
) and (
3.8
),
so that the proof of Theorem
2.1
is completed now
by taking
{\varepsilon}
small enough.

\Box

4
Proof of Proposition
3.1

In this section, we prove a priori estimate (
3.3
).
Recall the definition of
L^{\prime}_{a}
in (
2.8
).
From now on, a positive constant
C
independent of
T
and
{\varepsilon}

may change from line to line.
Since

\frac{1}{2}(1+|x|)\leq(1+x^{2})^{1/2}\leq(1+|x|),

we have that

|L_{a}^{\prime}(|U|^{p})(x,t)|\leq C\|U\|^{p}\{I_{+}(x,t)+I_{-}(x,t)\},

where the integrals
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
are defined by

I_{\pm}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\chi_{\pm}(x,t;s)}{(1+|t-s\pm x|)^{1+a}}ds

and the characteristic functions
\chi_{+}
and
\chi_{-}
are defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi_{\pm}(x,t;s)&:=\chi_{\{s:|t-s\pm x|\leq s+R\}}\\
&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{when $s$ satisfies }|t-s\pm x|\leq s+R,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\end{array}

respectively.
First we note that it is sufficient to estimate
I_{\pm}
for
x\geq 0
due to its symmetry,

I_{+}(-x,t)=I_{-}(x,t).

Hence it follows from
0\leq x\leq t+R
as well as

|t-s+x|\leq s+R\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0\leq s\leq t

that

\frac{t+x-R}{2}\leq s\leq t,

so that

I_{+}(x,t)\leq\int_{(t+x-R)/2}^{t}\frac{1}{(1+t-s+x)^{1+a}}ds.

When
a<0
, we have

I_{+}(x,t)\leq C\left(1+t+x-\frac{t+x-R}{2}\right)^{-a}\leq C(T+2R)^{-a}.

When
a=0
, we have

I_{+}(x,t)\leq\log\frac{1+t+x-(t+x-R)/2}{1+x}\leq\log(T+2R).

When
a>0
, we have

I_{+}(x,t)\leq C(1+x)^{-a}\leq C.

Therefore we obtain

I_{+}\leq CE_{a}(T)\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T].

On the other hand, the estimate for
I_{-}
is divided into two cases.
If
t-x\geq 0
, then
|t-s-x|\leq s+R
yields that

I_{-}(x,t)\leq\int_{(t-x-R)/2}^{t-x}\frac{1}{(1+t-s-x)^{1+a}}ds+\int_{t-x}^{t}\frac{1}{(1-t+s+x)^{1+a}}ds

follows.
When
a<0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)\leq C(1+t-x-(t-x-R)/2)^{-a}+C\leq C(T+2R)^{-a}.

When
a=0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)=\log(1+t-x-(t-x-R)/2)+\log(1+x)\leq 2\log(T+2R).

When
a>0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)\leq C.

Therefore we obtain

I_{-}\leq CE_{a}(T)\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T]\cap\{t-x\geq 0\}.

If
(-R\leq)t-x\leq 0
,
|t-s-x|\leq s+R
yields that

I_{-}(x,t)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(1+s-t+x)^{1+a}}ds.

When
a<0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)\leq C(1+x)^{-a}\leq C(T+2R)^{-a}.

When
a=0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)\leq\log\frac{1+x}{1-t+x}\leq\log(T+2R).

When
a>0
, we have

I_{-}(x,t)\leq C(1-t+x)^{-a}\leq C.

Therefore we obtain

I_{-}\leq CE_{a}(T)\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T]\cap\{-R\leq t-x\leq 0\}.

Summing up all the estimates for
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
, we have

|L_{a}^{\prime}(|U|^{p})|\leq C\|U\|^{p}E_{a}(T)\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T].

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

\Box

5
Proof of Theorem
2.2

In this section, a positive constant
C
independent of
T
and
{\varepsilon}

may change from line to line.
Let
U\in C({\bf R}\times[0,T])
with

\mbox{supp}\ U(x,t)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}

(5.1)

be a solution of the integral equation (
2.6
),
namely

U={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}+L^{\prime}_{a}(|U|^{p}).

Then it is easy to see by simple integration that

V(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}U(x,s)ds+{\varepsilon}f(x)

satisfies a integral equation,

V={\varepsilon}u^{0}+L_{a}(|V_{t}|^{p}).

Set
t=x+R,\ x\geq R
. Then, inverting the order of the
(y,s)
-integral and
diminishing its domain, we have that

L_{a}(|V_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\geq C\int_{R}^{x}\frac{1}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy\int_{y-R}^{y+R}|V_{t}(y,s)|^{p}ds.

Hence Hölder’s inequality yields that

L_{a}(|V_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\geq C\int_{R}^{x}\frac{1}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy\left|\int_{y-R}^{y+R}V_{t}(y,s)ds\right|^{p}

which implies that, due to (
5.1
),

L_{a}(|V_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\geq C\int_{R}^{x}\frac{|V(y,y+R)|^{p}}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy.

On the other hand, it follows from the assumption on the support of the data that

u^{0}(x,x+R)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=:G>0\quad\mbox{for}\ x\geq R.

Hence
V
satisfies

V(x,x+R)>G{\varepsilon}+C\int_{R}^{x}\frac{|V(y,y+R)|^{p}}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy\quad\mbox{for}\ x\geq R.

(5.2)

We note that the equality in the inequality above can be removed without loss of generality
by taking slightly smaller
G
.

Now we employ the comparison argument
with a solution of the related ordinary differential equation.
Let
W
be a solution of

W(x)=G{\varepsilon}+C\int_{R}^{x}\frac{|W(y)|^{p}}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy\quad\mbox{for}\ x\geq R.

(5.3)

Then we have

V(x,x+R)>W(x)\quad\mbox{for}\ x\geq R.

(5.4)

Because
V(R,2R)>W(R)
and the continuity of
V,W
yield that
(
5.4
) holds in the neighborhood of
x=R
.
If there exists a point

x_{0}:=\inf\{x\geq R:V(x,x+R)=W(x)\},

we immediately reach to a contradiction,

0=V(x_{0},x_{0}+R)-W(x_{0})=C\int_{R}^{x_{0}}\frac{|V(y,y+R)|^{p}-|W(y)|^{p}}{(1+y)^{1+a}}dy>0.

Hence (
5.4
) is true and implies that
the existence time of
V(x,x+R)
is less than the blow-up time of
W(x)
.

Therefore the conclusion of Theorem
2.2
follows by solving
the initial value problem for ordinary differential equations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle W^{\prime}=\frac{C|W|^{p}}{(1+x)^{1+a}}\quad\mbox{in}\ [R,\infty),\\
W(R)=G{\varepsilon},\end{array}\right.

which is equivalent to (
5.3
).
In fact, the blow-up time
X
of
W
has to satisfy

(G{\varepsilon})^{1-p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{-a}C\left\{(1+X)^{-a}-(1+R)^{-a}\right\}&\mbox{for}\ a<0,\\
\displaystyle(p-1)C\log\frac{1+X}{1+R}&\mbox{for}\ a=0.\end{array}\right.

Since (
5.4
) implies that the blow-up time
T
of
W

has to satisfy the inequality
T\geq X+R
,
we have the blow-up condition (
2.17
) by taking
{\varepsilon}
small enough.
To see this, if
a<0
, one can estimate
X+R
as

\begin{array}[]{ll}X+R&\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{-a}{(p-1)C}(G{\varepsilon})^{1-p}+(1+R)^{-a}\right\}^{1/(-a)}-1+R\\
&\displaystyle\leq\left\{\frac{-a}{(p-1)C}(G{\varepsilon})^{1-p}+2(1+R)^{-a}\right\}^{1/(-a)}.\end{array}

Therefore
{\varepsilon}_{2}
in Theorem
2.2
should be defined by

\frac{-a}{(p-1)C}(G{\varepsilon}_{2})^{1-p}=2(1+R)^{-a}

because it makes

X+R\leq\left\{\frac{2(-a)G^{1-p}}{(p-1)C}\right\}^{1/(-a)}{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(-a)}\quad\mbox{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{2}.

Similarly, if
a=0
, one can estimate
X+R
as

\begin{array}[]{ll}X+R&\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\frac{(G{\varepsilon})^{1-p}}{(p-1)C}+\log(1+R)\right\}-1+R\\
&\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{\frac{(G{\varepsilon})^{1-p}}{(p-1)C}+2\log(1+R)\right\}.\end{array}

Therefore
{\varepsilon}_{2}
in Theorem
2.2
should be defined by

\frac{(G{\varepsilon}_{2})^{1-p}}{(p-1)C}=2\log(1+R)

because it makes

X+R\leq\exp\left\{\frac{2G^{1-p}}{(p-1)C}{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right\}\quad\mbox{for}\ 0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{2}.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is now completed.

\Box

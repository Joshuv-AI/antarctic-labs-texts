Logarithmic Refinements of a Power Weighted Hardy–Rellich-Type Inequality Thanks: To appear in Ann. Funct. Anal.
Fritz Gesztesy, Michael M. H. Pang, Jonathan Stanfill

Abstract
. The principal purpose of this note is to prove a logarithmic refinement of the power weighted Hardy–Rellich inequality on n n -dimensional balls, valid for the largest variety of underlying parameters and for all dimensions n ∈ ℕ n\in{\mathbb{N}} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35A23, 35J30; Secondary: 47A63, 47F05.

1.
Introduction

In the recent paper
[
9
]
we reconsidered the following sharp inequality, first derived by Caldiroli and Musina
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-4}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,&\\
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},\;f\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;n\in{\mathbb{N}},\,n\geq 2,&\end{split}

where

(1.2)

C_{n,\gamma}=\min_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}-\frac{(\gamma-2)^{2}}{4}+j(j+n-2)\bigg)^{2}\bigg\}.

In addition, we also derived the sharp inequality (sometimes called the Hardy–Rellich inequality),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma}|(\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq A_{n,\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,&\\
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},\;f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\;n\in{\mathbb{N}},\,n\geq 2,&\end{split}

where

(1.4)

A_{n,\gamma}={\min}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\{\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}\},

with

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{0}}&=\alpha_{n,\gamma,0}=4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2},\\
\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}&=\big[4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)(n-\gamma)+\lambda_{j}\big]^{2}\Big/\big[4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)^{2}+\lambda_{j}\big],\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{split}

In the unweighted case
\gamma=0
this simplifies to the known fact,

(1.6)

A_{n,0}=\begin{cases}n^{2}/4,&n\geq 5,\\
3,&n=4,\\
25/36,&n=3,\\
0,&n=2.\end{cases}

In the special case where
C_{n,\gamma}
in (
1.1
), or
A_{n,\gamma}
in (
1.3
), vanishes, the resulting inequality is rendered trivial (e.g., there is no nontrivial inequality of the type (
1.3
) in the case
n=2
,
\gamma=0
) and hence one wonders about the possibility of logarithmically refining these inequalities to prevent them from becoming insignificant.

In this connection we recall that logarithmic refinements of (
1.1
) were already known. Indeed, as discussed in
[
4
]
, whenever,
4^{-1}\big[(\gamma-2)^{2}-(n-2)^{2}\big]
equals one of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
(i.e., one of the numbers
j(j+n-2)
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
), then
C_{n,\gamma}
vanishes, rendering inequality (
1.1
) trivial. In this context we recall the following result from
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-4}|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad+\big\{\big[(n-\gamma)^{2}+(n+\gamma-4)^{2}\big]\big/16\big\}\\
&\qquad\times\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-4}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/|x|)]^{-2}\Bigg)|f(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,\\
&\,R\in(0,\infty),\;\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},\;N\in{\mathbb{N}},\;\eta\in[e_{N}R,\infty),\;f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{n}(0;R)\backslash\{0\}),\end{split}

which yields an appropriate logarithmic refinement even if
C_{n,\gamma}
vanishes. Here
B_{n}(0;R)
denotes the open ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, centered at the origin
0
of radius
R\in(0,\infty)
, the iterated logarithms
\operatorname{ln}_{k}(\,\cdot\,)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are given by

(1.8)

\operatorname{ln}_{1}(\,\cdot\,)=\operatorname{ln}(\,\cdot\,),\quad\operatorname{ln}_{k+1}(\,\cdot\,)=\operatorname{ln}\big(\operatorname{ln}_{k}(\,\cdot\,)\big),\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

and the iterated exponentials
e_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are introduced via

(1.9)

e_{0}=0,\quad e_{j+1}=e^{e_{j}},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Given the logarithmic refinement (
1.7
) of (
1.1
), it is natural to ask if a corresponding analogous logarithmic refinement of (
1.3
) exists that prevents it from becoming insignificant if
A_{n,\gamma}
vanishes. Answering this question in the affirmative is the principal purpose of this note. In particular, we will prove the following inequality in Theorem
2.3
:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq A_{n,\gamma}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad+4^{-1}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/|x|)]^{-2}\Bigg)|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\\
&\quad+4^{-1}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-4}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/|x|)]^{-2}\Bigg)|(\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,\\
&\,R\in(0,\infty),\;\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}},\;N,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\,n\geq 2,\;\eta\in[e_{N}R,\infty),\;f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{n}(0;R)\backslash\{0\}).\end{split}

Once again, this inequality remains meaningful even if
A_{n,\gamma}
vanishes.

2.
A logarithmically Modifed Hardy–Rellich-type Inequality

We begin by recalling the following simplified version of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1
(iii)
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R\in(0,\infty)
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\eta\in[e_{N}R,\infty)
, and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,R))
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,dr&\geq 4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha-2}|f(r)|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad\,+4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha-2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)|f(r)|^{2}\,dr,\end{split}

where the iterated logarithms
\operatorname{ln}_{k}(\,\cdot\,)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are given by

(2.2)

\operatorname{ln}_{1}(\,\cdot\,)=\operatorname{ln}(\,\cdot\,),\quad\operatorname{ln}_{k+1}(\,\cdot\,)=\operatorname{ln}\big(\operatorname{ln}_{k}(\,\cdot\,)\big),\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

and the iterated exponentials
e_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are introduced via

(2.3)

e_{0}=0,\quad e_{j+1}=e^{e_{j}},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Proof.

As the current investigation came about while studying factorizations in
[
9
]
, we provide a factorization proof of this lemma in the spirit of
[
9
]
(see also
[
6
]
for related higher dimensional unweighted factorizations with log refinements).

Given
R\in(0,\infty)
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\eta\in[e_{N}R,\infty)
, one defines the differential expression

(2.4)

T_{N,\alpha}=r^{\alpha/2}\frac{d}{dr}+\frac{\alpha-1}{2}r^{(\alpha-2)/2}+\frac{1}{2}r^{(\alpha-2)/2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-1},\quad r\in(0,R).

Then, after applying appropriate integration by parts and combining similar terms, one confirms that for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,R))
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\int_{0}^{R}|(T_{N,\alpha}f)(r)|^{2}\,dr&=\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\,dr-4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha-2}|f(r)|^{2}\,dr\\
&\quad\,-4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha-2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)|f(r)|^{2}\,dr,\end{split}

proving (
2.1
).
∎

Before deriving our next result, we recall some standard notation and facts.
Let
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
denote the
(n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ n\geq 2,
with
d^{n-1}\omega:=d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)
the usual volume measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We denote by
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
the nonnegative, self-adjoint Laplace–Beltrami operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1};d^{n-1}\omega)
. Let

(2.6)

\lambda_{j}=j(j+n-2),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

be the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
, that is,
\sigma(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}})=\{j(j+n-2)\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, of multiplicity

(2.7)

m(\lambda_{j})=(2j+n-2)(j+n-2)^{-1}{j+n-2\choose n-2},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

with corresponding eigenfunctions
\varphi_{j,\ell},\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ell\in\{1,\dots,m(\lambda_{j})\}
. We may (and will) assume that
\{\varphi_{j,\ell}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ell\in\{1,\dots,m(\lambda_{j})\}}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)
, and let

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)=(\varphi_{j,\ell},f(r,\,\cdot\,))_{L^{2}({{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}};d^{n-1}\omega)}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\overline{\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)}f(r,\theta)\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta),&\\
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),\quad r>0,\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \ell\in\{1,\dots,m(\lambda_{j})\}.&\end{split}

Finally, let
B_{n}(0;R)
denote the open ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, centered at the origin
0
of radius
R\in(0,\infty)
.

We are now in the position to prove the following lemma which will be combined with Lemma
2.1
to prove our main result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R\in(0,\infty)
,
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{n}(0;R)\backslash\{0\})
, and
g\in C((0,R))
satisfy
g(r)>0
for all
r\in(0,R)
. Then

(2.9)

\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}g(|x|)|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\big[|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}r^{n-1}+\lambda_{j}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}r^{n-3}\big]\,dr.

Proof.

We begin by recalling that

(2.10)

|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}=|(\partial f/\partial r)(r,\theta)|^{2}+r^{-2}|(\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f(r,\,\cdot\,))(\theta)|^{2},

where
\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
denotes the gradient operator on
{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
.
Thus applying (
2.10
) and
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}g(|x|)|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x=\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|(\nabla f)(r,\theta)|^{2}\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\,r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\big[|(\partial f/\partial r)(r,\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\hskip 76.82234pt+r^{-2}|(\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f(r,\,\cdot\,))(\theta)|^{2}\big]\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\,r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\Bigg\{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Bigg|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)\Bigg|^{2}\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)

\displaystyle\quad\hskip 51.21504pt+r^{-2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\overline{(-\Delta_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)(r,\theta)}f(r,\theta)\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\Bigg\}\,r^{n-1}dr

(2.11)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\Bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\,+r^{-2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}\overline{F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad\hskip 59.75095pt\times\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)\Bigg]\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\Bigg\}\,r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\big[|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}+\lambda_{j}r^{-2}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\big]\,r^{n-1}dr,

proving (
2.9
)
∎

Explicitly, (
2.11
) yields

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|(\partial f/\partial r)(r,\theta)|^{2}\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\,r^{n-1}dr\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{0}^{R}g(r)|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,r^{n-1}dr,\end{split}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{R}g(r)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}r^{-2}|(\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f(r,\,\cdot\,))(\theta)|^{2}\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\,r^{n-1}dr\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\lambda_{j}\int_{0}^{R}g(r)r^{-2}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,r^{n-1}dr.\end{split}

The previous results now allow us to prove the main result in this note in the form of the following Hardy–Rellich-type inequality with logarithmic refinements.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
R\in(0,\infty)
,
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
N,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with
n\geq 2
,
\eta\in[e_{N}R,\infty)
, and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{n}(0;R)\backslash\{0\})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq A_{n,\gamma}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-2}|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

(2.14)

\displaystyle\quad+4^{-1}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/|x|)]^{-2}\Bigg)|(\nabla f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad+4^{-1}\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma-4}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/|x|)]^{-2}\Bigg)|(\nabla_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x,

where

(2.15)

A_{n,\gamma}={\min}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\{\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}\},

with

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{0}}&=\alpha_{n,\gamma,0}=4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2},\\
\alpha_{n,\gamma,\lambda_{j}}&=\big[4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)(n-\gamma)+\lambda_{j}\big]^{2}\Big/\big[4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)^{2}+\lambda_{j}\big],\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{split}

Excluding the cases
(\alpha)

n=2,\ \gamma=2
and
(\beta)

n=3,\ \gamma=1
, the constant
A_{n,\gamma}
on the right-hand side of inequality (
2.14
) is optimal.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Eq. (A.25)]
and
[
8
, Lemmas 2.3 and B.3
(i)
]
one has

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-1}\big|-r^{1-n}\big[d/dr\big(r^{n-1}F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big)\big]+\lambda_{j}r^{-2}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

(2.17)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-1}\big|F^{\prime\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+[2\lambda_{j}+(n-1)(1-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(\gamma+n-4)(2-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,dr\Bigg\}.

Furthermore, note that (
2.15
) implies

(2.18)

\lambda_{j}A_{n,\gamma}\leq\big[4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)(n-\gamma)+\lambda_{j}\big]^{2}-4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)^{2}A_{n,\gamma},\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

or equivalently,

(2.19)

\lambda_{j}A_{n,\gamma}\leq 4^{-1}(n+\gamma-4)^{2}\big[4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2}+2\lambda_{j}-A_{n,\gamma}\big]+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(n+\gamma-4)(2-\gamma)],\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
and (
2.19
) to (
2.17
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}(0;R)}|x|^{\gamma}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}\,d^{n}x\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\Bigg\{4^{-1}(2-n-\gamma)^{2}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+[2\lambda_{j}+(n-1)(1-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(\gamma+n-4)(2-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,dr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\Bigg\{\big[4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2}+2\lambda_{j}\big]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(\gamma+n-4)(2-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,dr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\Bigg\{A_{n,\gamma}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\big[4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2}+2\lambda_{j}-A_{n,\gamma}\big]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(\gamma+n-4)(2-\gamma)]\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,dr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\Bigg\{A_{n,\gamma}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{-1}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-3}\big|F^{\prime}_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\big\{4^{-1}(\gamma+n-4)^{2}\big[4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2}+2\lambda_{j}-A_{n,\gamma}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\lambda_{j}[\lambda_{j}+(\gamma+n-4)(2-\gamma)]\big\}\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{-1}\big[4^{-1}(n-\gamma)^{2}+2\lambda_{j}-A_{n,\gamma}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\int_{0}^{R}r^{\gamma+n-5}\big|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\prod_{p=1}^{k}[\operatorname{ln}_{p}(\eta/r)]^{-2}\Bigg)\,dr\Bigg\}

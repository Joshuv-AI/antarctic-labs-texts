Alina Chertock, Shaoshuai Chu, Michael Herty, Alexander Kurganov

Abstract
We propose novel less diffusive schemes for conservative one- and two-dimensional hyperbolic systems of nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs). The main challenges in the development of accurate and robust numerical methods for the studied systems come from the complicated wave structures, such as shocks, rarefactions and contact discontinuities, arising even for smooth initial conditions. In order to reduce the diffusion in the original central-upwind schemes, we use a local characteristic decomposition procedure to develop a new class of central-upwind schemes. We apply the developed schemes to the one- and two-dimensional Euler equations of gas dynamics to illustrate the performance on a variety of examples. The obtained numerical results clearly demonstrate that the proposed new schemes outperform the original central-upwind schemes.



Key words:
Local characteristic decomposition; central-upwind schemes; hyperbolic systems of conservative laws; Euler equations of gas
dynamics

AMS subject classification:
76M12, 65M08, 76N15, 35L65, 35L67.

1
Introduction

This paper focuses on numerical solutions of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws, which in the two-dimensional (2-D) case, read as

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},

(1.1)

where
x
and
y
are spatial variables,
t
is the time,
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a vector of unknowns,
\bm{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\bm{G}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the
x
- and
y
-flux functions, respectively.

It is well-known that the solutions of (
1.1
) may develop complicated wave structures including shocks, rarefactions, and contact
discontinuities even for infinitely smooth initial data, and thus developing highly accurate and robust shock-capturing numerical methods
for solving (
1.1
) is a challenging task.

Since the pioneering works
[
7
,
22
,
9
]
, a large number of various methods had been introduced; see, e.g., the monographs and
review papers
[
12
,
23
,
15
,
37
,
40
,
2
]
and references therein. Here, we focus on finite-volume (FV) methods, in
which solutions are realized in terms of their cell averages and evolved in time according to the following algorithm. First, a piecewise
polynomial interpolant is reconstructed out of the given cell averages, and then the evolution step is performed using the integral form of
(
1.1
). To this end, a proper set of time-space control volumes has to be selected. Depending on this selection, one may distinguish
between two basic classes of finite-volume methods: upwind and central schemes. In upwind schemes, the spatial part of the control volumes
coincides with the FV cells and therefore, one needs to (approximately) solve (generalized) Riemann problems at every cell interface; see,
e.g.,
[
9
,
40
,
2
]
and references therein. This helps the upwind schemes to achieve very high resolution. At the same time, it
might be quite complicated and even impossible to solve the (generalized) Riemann problems for general systems of conservation laws. Central
schemes offer an attractive simple alternative to the upwind ones. In staggered central schemes, first proposed for one-dimensional (1-D)
systems in
[
30
]
and then extended to higher order
[
24
,
29
]
and multiple number of dimensions

[
25
,
13
,
1
]
, the control volumes use the staggered spatial parts so that the FV cell interfaces remain inside the
control volumes. This allows to avoid even approximately solving any Riemann problems, which makes staggered central schemes easy to
implement for a wide variety of hyperbolic systems. The major drawback of staggered central schemes is, however, their relatively large
numerical dissipation as they basically average over the Riemann fans rather than resolving them.

In order to reduce the amount of excessive numerical dissipation present in central schemes, a class of central-upwind (CU) schemes have
been proposed in
[
17
,
19
]
. These schemes are based on nonuniform control volumes, whose spatial size taken to be
proportional to the local speeds of propagation. This allows one to minimize the area over which the solution averaging occurs still
without (approximately) solving any (generalized) Riemann problems. The upwind features of the CU schemes can be seen, for example, when
they are applied to simpler systems. For instance, the CU scheme from
[
17
]
reduces to the upwind one when it is applied to a
system whose Jacobian contains only positive (only negative) eigenvalues. Another advantage of the CU schemes is related to the fact that
unlike the staggered central schemes, they admit a particularly simple semi-discrete form. This observation is the basis of the
modifications of the CU schemes we propose in this paper.

Even though the CU schemes from
[
17
,
19
]
are quite accurate, efficient and robust tools for a wide variety of hyperbolic
systems, higher resolution of the numerical solutions can be achieved by further reducing numerical dissipation. This can be done in a
number of different ways, for example: (i) by introducing a more accurate evolution procedure, which leads to a “built-in” anti-diffusion
term
[
18
]
; (ii) by implementing a numerical dissipation switch to control the amount of numerical dissipation present in the CU
schemes
[
16
]
; (iii) by obtaining more accurate estimates for the one-sided local speeds of propagation using the discrete
Rankine-Hugoniot conditions
[
8
]
.

Another way to control the amount of numerical dissipation present in the CU schemes or any other FV methods is by adjusting the nonlinear
limiting mechanism used in the piecewise polynomial reconstruction. It is well-known that sharper reconstructions may lead to larger
numerical oscillations and a way to reduce these oscillations is to reconstruct the characteristic variables rather than the conservative
ones; see, e.g.,
[
33
]
. This can be done using the local characteristic decomposition (LCD); see, e.g.,

[
3
,
14
,
28
,
31
,
33
,
37
,
42
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we modify the CU schemes from
[
17
]
by applying LCD to the numerical diffusion part of the schemes. The
obtained new LCD-based CU schemes contain substantially smaller amount of numerical dissipation, which leads to a significantly improved
resolution of the computed solution compared with the original CU schemes. As observed above, the key idea is that the new LCD-based CU
scheme reduces to the upwind scheme when applied to 1-D linear hyperbolic systems

\bm{U}_{t}+A\bm{U}_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.2)

where
A
is a constant matrix (disregarding the sign of the eigenvalues of
A
). This feature suggests that the new CU schemes have more
built-in upwinding compared with their predecessors.

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we briefly describe the 1-D second-order FV CU scheme from
[
17
]
. In
§
3
, we introduce the proposed new 1-D LCD-based CU scheme and show that the developed 1-D scheme reduces to the upwind scheme
when applied to the linear system (
1.2
). In §
4
, we construct the 2-D LCD-based CU scheme. Finally, in §
5
, we test
the proposed schemes on a number of 1-D and 2-D numerical examples for the Euler equations of gas dynamics. We demonstrate high accuracy,
efficiency, stability, and robustness of the new LCD-based CU schemes, which clearly outperform the second-order CU scheme from

[
17
,
20
]
.

2
1-D Central-Upwind Scheme: A Brief Overview

In this section, we consider the 1-D hyperbolic system of conservation laws

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(2.1)

and describe the second-order semi-discrete CU scheme from
[
17
]
. To this end, we assume that the computational domain is
covered with the uniform cells
C_{j}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]
of size
\Delta x
centered at
x_{j}=(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2
and denote by
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t)
cell
averages of
\bm{U}(\cdot,t)
over the corresponding intervals
C_{j}
, that is,

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t):\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int\limits_{C_{j}}\bm{U}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x.

We also assume that at certain time
t\geq 0
, the cell average values
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
are available and from here on we suppress the
time-dependence of all of the indexed quantities for the sake of brevity.

According to
[
17
]
, the computed cell averages (
2.1
) are evolved in time by solving the following system of ordinary
differential equations (ODEs):

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(2.2)

where
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the CU numerical fluxes given by

\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}+\frac{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}\left(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(2.3)

Here,
\bm{F}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}:=\bm{F}\big(\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the right/left-sided values reconstructed out of the given set of
cell averages
\{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}\}
. The one-sided local speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are estimated using the largest and the smallest
eigenvalues of the Jacobian
A(\bm{U}):=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})
, which we denote by

\lambda_{1}(A(\bm{U}))\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{d}(A(\bm{U}))
. This can be done, for example, by taking

\displaystyle a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\left\{\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{d}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),0\right\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\left\{\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\lambda_{1}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),0\right\}.

The (formal) order of the scheme (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) is determined by the accuracy the point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are reconstructed with
and the order of the ODE solver used to integrate the ODE system (
2.2
) in time. In this paper, we use the LCD-based second-order
piecewise linear reconstruction described in §
2.1
and the three-stage third-order strong stability preserving (SSP) Runge-Kutta
solver; see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
]
.

2.1
LCD-Based Piecewise Linear Reconstruction

In order to ensure at least second-order of accuracy of the CU scheme (
2.2
)–(
2.4
), one has to use a second-order piecewise
linear reconstruction, which will be non-oscillatory provided the numerical derivatives are computed using a nonlinear limiter. A library of
different limiters are available; see, e.g.,
[
26
,
30
,
39
,
2
,
12
,
23
,
40
]
. The limiters can be classified
as dissipative, compressive or overcompressive; see
[
26
]
. When applied to the conservative variables
\bm{U}
, dissipative limiters may
introduce excessive numerical dissipation, which may results in oversmeared solution discontinuities. On the other hand, compressive and
overcompressive limiters tend to lead to quite large oscillations near discontinuities and artificial sharpening smooth parts of the
solution (compressive limiters may lead to kinks, that is, jumps in the first derivatives, while the overcompressive limiters may lead to
jumps in the solution itself); see, e.g.,
[
26
]
. Part of these difficulties can be overcome by reconstructing local characteristic
variables as it was demonstrated in
[
33
]
in the context of higher-order WENO reconstructions.

In this paper, we use the minmod limiter, which is, according to
[
26
]
, the most compressive out of dissipative limiters. As this
limiter typically leads to quite large oscillations when applied to the conservative variables, we implement it in the LCD framework.
Specifically, we first introduce the matrix
\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=A(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
, where
A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})
is
the Jacobian and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is either a simple average
(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1})/2
or another type of average of the

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}
states.

As long as the system (
1.1
) is strictly hyperbolic, we compute the matrices
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
such that

R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is a diagonal matrix and introduce the local characteristic variables
\bm{\Gamma}_{k}
in the neighborhood
of
x=x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
:

\bm{\Gamma}_{k}=R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{k},\quad k=j-1,\ldots,j+2.

Equipped with the values
\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1}
,
\bm{\Gamma}_{j}
,
\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}
and
\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2}
, we reconstruct
\bm{\Gamma}
in the cell

C_{j}
by computing

(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j}={\rm minmod}\left(2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1}}{2\Delta x},\,2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j}}{\Delta x}\right),

(2.5)

and

(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j+1}={\rm minmod}\left(2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j}}{2\Delta x},\,2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}}{\Delta x}\right),

(2.6)

where the minmod function, defined as

{\rm minmod}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots):=\begin{cases}\min_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}>0\quad\forall\,j,\\
\max_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}<0\quad\forall\,j,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2.7)

is applied in the component-wise manner. The slopes (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) allow to evaluate

\bm{\Gamma}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{\Gamma}_{j}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{\Gamma}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j+1},

and obtain the corresponding point values of
\bm{U}
by

\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Gamma}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.8)

Remark 2.1

In Appendix
A
, we provide a detailed explanation on how the average matrix
\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and the corresponding matrices

R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are computed in the case of the Euler equation of gas dynamics.

3
1-D LCD-Based Central-Upwind Scheme

In this section, we introduce a new 1-D LCD-based CU scheme, in which the amount of numerical dissipation is substantially reduced compared
with the original CU scheme (
2.2
)–(
2.3
). To this end, we first rewrite the numerical flux (
2.3
) of the original CU scheme in
the following form:

\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}+\bm{D}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(3.1)

where
\bm{F}_{j}:=\bm{F}(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j})
and
\bm{D}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is the numerical diffusion given by

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]-\frac{a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right),

which, in turn, can be rewritten with the help of the matrix
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
introduced in §
2.1
and using (
2.8
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left[\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}M_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left[\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]

(3.2)

\displaystyle+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\Gamma}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Here,
P_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
M_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, and
Q_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the diagonal matrices

\displaystyle P_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((P_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(P_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad M_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((M_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(M_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),

(3.3)

\displaystyle Q_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((Q_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(Q_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

with

\big((P_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(M_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(Q_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)=\frac{1}{a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

(3.4)

The main idea of the new the 1-D LCD-based CU scheme is to replace the constant entries (
3.4
) in the diagonal matrices
P_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,

M_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, and
Q_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
with the corresponding characteristic entries of the diagonal matrix
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
. This is
motivated by the fact that in the linear case with
\bm{F}(\bm{U})=A\bm{U}
, the local characteristic speed might be different for each
(diagonalized) component
\bm{\Gamma}
. Hence, a diffusion that depends on the corresponding local speed of the each component may lead to a
sharper resolution of possible discontinuities.

Proposed modifications lead to the semi-discrete scheme

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(3.5)

where the numerical fluxes
\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the LCD modifications of (
3.1
):

\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}+\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(3.6)

with the following LCD modification of the numerical diffusion term (
3.2
)–(
3.4
):

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left[\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left[\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j}+\bm{F}_{j+1}}{2}\right]

(3.7)

\displaystyle+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{\Gamma}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Here,

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(P^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((M^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(M^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((Q^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

with

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\big((\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)&&\mbox{if}~(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

and

\displaystyle(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},

\displaystyle(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},

for
i=1,\ldots,d
.

Finally,
\varepsilon
in (
3.8
) is a very small desingularization constant, taken
\varepsilon=10^{-10}
in all of the numerical
examples reported in §
5
.

It is crucial to note that the proposed 1-D LCD-based CU scheme reduces to the second-order semi-discrete upwind scheme when applied to a
linear hyperbolic system (
1.2
) with constant coefficients. This is proven in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

If

\bm{F}(\bm{U})=A\bm{U},

(3.9)

where
A
is a constant matrix, then the scheme (
3.5
)–(
3.7
) becomes the second-order semi-discrete upwind scheme.

Proof.
We note that in the linear case with constant coefficients, the corresponding matrix composed of the right eigenvectors of
A

is also a constant matrix, that is,
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv R
, and matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
reduce to

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv P={\rm diag}\left(\max\{{\rm sign}(\lambda_{1}),0\},\ldots,\max\{{\rm sign}(\lambda_{d}),0\}\right),

(3.10)

\displaystyle M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv M={\rm diag}\left(\min\{{\rm sign}(\lambda_{1}),0\},\ldots,\min\{{\rm sign}(\lambda_{d}),0\}\right),

while the matrix
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv 0
as
\lambda^{+}_{i}\lambda^{-}_{i}=\max(\lambda_{i},0)\cdot\min(\lambda_{i},0)=0
.

Taking into account the above simplifications and substituting (
3.9
), (
3.10
) into (
3.7
), yields the following expression for
the numerical diffusion term:

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=RPR^{-1}A\left[\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}}{2}\right]-RMR^{-1}A\left[\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}}{2}\right]

(3.11)

\displaystyle=RPR^{-1}AR\left[R^{-1}\left(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}}{2}\right)\right]-RMR^{-1}AR\left[R^{-1}\left(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=R\Lambda^{+}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j}+\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}}{2}\right)+R\Lambda^{-}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j}+\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}}{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}

\displaystyle=PR^{-1}AR=P\Lambda={\rm diag}\left(\max\{\lambda_{1},0\},\ldots,\max\{\lambda_{d},0\}\right),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-}

\displaystyle=MR^{-1}AR=M\Lambda={\rm diag}\left(\min\{\lambda_{1},0\},\ldots,\min\{\lambda_{d},0\}\right).

Finally, combining (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) together and including them into (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}A\left(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}\right)+R\Lambda^{+}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j}+\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}}{2}\right)+R\Lambda^{-}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j}+\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1}}{2}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}(A^{+}+A^{-})\left(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}\right)+A^{+}\left(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{U}_{j}+\bm{U}_{j+1}}{2}\right)+A^{-}\left(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\bm{U}_{j}+\bm{U}_{j+1}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=A^{+}\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+A^{-}\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

where
A^{\pm}=R\Lambda^{\pm}R^{-1}
. This confirms that the scheme (
3.5
), (
3.12
) is the second-order semi-discrete upwind scheme.

\hfill\blacksquare

4
2-D LCD-Based Central-Upwind Scheme

In this section, we generalize the 1-D LCD-based CU scheme introduced in §
3
for the 2-D hyperbolic system of conservation laws
(
1.1
). We design the 2-D LCD-based CU scheme in a “dimension-by-dimension” manner, so that it reads as

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y},

where

\bm{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{\bm{F}_{j,k}+\bm{F}_{j+1,k}}{2}+\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\quad\bm{\mathcal{G}}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\bm{G}_{j,k}+\bm{G}_{j,k+1}}{2}+\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\bm{F}_{j,k}:=\bm{F}(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k})
,
\bm{G}_{j,k}:=\bm{G}(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k})
, and
\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are
the numerical diffusion terms defined by

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle=R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\left[\bm{F}^{\rm E}_{j,k}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j,k}+\bm{F}_{j+1,k}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\left[\bm{F}^{\rm W}_{j+1,k}-\frac{\bm{F}_{j,k}+\bm{F}_{j+1,k}}{2}\right]+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\left(\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm W}_{j+1,k}-\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm E}_{j,k}\right),

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\bm{G}^{\rm N}_{j,k}-\frac{\bm{G}_{j,k}+\bm{G}_{j,k+1}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle-R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\bm{G}^{\rm S}_{j,k+1}-\frac{\bm{G}_{j,k}+\bm{G}_{j,k+1}}{2}\right]+R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm S}_{j,k+1}-\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm N}_{j,k}\right].

The matrices
R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the matrices such that

R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\widehat{B}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are diagonal. Here,

\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=A(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})
,
\widehat{B}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=B(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})
with

A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}(\bm{U})}{\partial\bm{U}}
,
B(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{G}(\bm{U})}{\partial\bm{U}}
, and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are either simple averages
(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k})/2
,
(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1})/2
or
another type of averages of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}
,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}
,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}
states, respectively. The
numerical fluxes
\bm{F}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}
and
\bm{G}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}
are defined by
\bm{F}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k})
,

\bm{G}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}:=\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k})
, and the details of reconstructing the point values
\bm{U}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}
and

\bm{U}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}
,
\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}
and
\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}
for the 2-D Euler equations of gas dynamics are provided
in Appendix
B
. Finally, the diagonal matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are defined by

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

where for all
i=1,\ldots,d

\displaystyle\left((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}\left((\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}>\varepsilon,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\left((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}\left((\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},-(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}>\varepsilon,\\
&0&&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}:=(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}:=(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and

\displaystyle(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\max\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{\rm E}_{j,k})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{\rm W}_{j+1,k})\big),\,0\right\},

\displaystyle(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\min\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{\rm E}_{j,k})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{\rm W}_{j+1,k})\big),\,0\right\},

\displaystyle(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\max\left\{\mu_{i}\big(B(\bm{U}^{\rm N}_{j,k})\big),\,\mu_{i}\big(B(\bm{U}^{\rm S}_{j,k+1})\big),\,0\right\},

\displaystyle(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\min\left\{\mu_{i}\big(B(\bm{U}^{\rm N}_{j,k})\big),\,\mu_{i}\big(B(\bm{U}^{\rm S}_{j,k+1})\big),\,0\right\},

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the Jacobians
A(\bm{U})
and
B(\bm{U})
:
\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{d}(A)
and

\mu_{1}(B)\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{d}(B)
, respectively.

Appendix A
LCD Matrices for the 1-D Euler Equations of Gas Dynamics

The 1-D Euler equations of gas dynamics read as the system (
2.1
) with

\bm{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\rho u\\
E\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{F}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
u(E+p)\end{pmatrix},

where
\rho
,
u
,
p
, and
E
are the density, velocity, pressure and total energy, respectively. The system is completed through the
following equations of state:

E=\rho e+{\frac{1}{2}}\rho u^{2},\quad e=\frac{p}{(\gamma-1)\rho},

where
e
is the specific internal energy, and
\gamma
is the parameter representing the specific heat ratio.

We first compute the Jacobian

\displaystyle A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
\dfrac{\gamma-3}{2}u^{2}&(3-\gamma)u&\gamma-1\\[6.45831pt]
-\dfrac{\gamma u\hat{E}}{\rho}+(\gamma-1)u^{3}&H-(\gamma-1)u^{2}&\gamma u\end{pmatrix},

where
H=\frac{E+p}{\rho}
is the total specific enthalpy. We then introduce the matrices

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
\dfrac{\gamma-3}{2}\hat{u}^{2}&(3-\gamma)\hat{u}&\gamma-1\\[6.45831pt]
-\dfrac{\gamma\hat{u}\hat{E}}{\hat{\rho}}+(\gamma-1)\hat{u}^{3}&\hat{H}-(\gamma-1)\hat{u}^{2}&\gamma\hat{u}\end{pmatrix},

where
\hat{(\cdot)}
stands for the following averages:

\hat{\rho}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}}{2},~\hat{u}=\frac{u_{j}+u_{j+1}}{2},~\hat{p}=\frac{p_{j}+p_{j+1}}{2},~\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{E}+\hat{p}}{\hat{\rho}},~\hat{E}=\frac{\hat{p}}{\gamma-1}+{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}\hat{u}^{2},

where

u_{j}=\frac{(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho u$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{j}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{j}=(\gamma-1)\left[\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$E$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}-{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{j}u_{j}^{2}\right].

Notice that all of the
\hat{(\cdot)}
quantities have to have a subscript index, that is,
\hat{(\cdot)}=\hat{(\cdot)}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, but we omit
it for the sake of brevity for all of the quantities except for
\widehat{A}
. We then compute the matrix
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
composed of the right
eigenvectors of
\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and obtain

R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
\hat{u}-\hat{c}&\hat{u}&\hat{u}+\hat{c}\\
\hat{H}-\hat{u}\hat{c}&\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}}{2}&\hat{H}+\hat{u}\hat{c}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\dfrac{1}{\hat{\phi}}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{\hat{u}\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{u}-\dfrac{\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&1\\[5.16663pt]
2\hat{\phi}-{2\hat{H}}&{2\hat{u}}&-2\\
\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{\hat{u}\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{u}+\dfrac{\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\hat{\phi}=2\hat{H}-\hat{u}^{2}
and
\hat{c}=\sqrt{\gamma\hat{p}/\hat{\rho}}
.

Appendix B
LCD-Based Piecewise Linear Reconstruction for the 2-D Euler Equations of Gas Dynamics

The 2-D Euler equations of gas dynamics read as (
1.1
) with

\bm{U}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\rho u\\
\rho v\\
E\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{F}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\rho uv\\
u(E+p)\end{pmatrix},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{G}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho v\\
\rho uv\\
\rho v^{2}+p\\
v(E+p)\end{pmatrix},

(B.1)

where
\rho
is the density,
u
and
v
are the
x
- and
y
-velocities,
p
is the pressure, and
E
is the total energy. The system is
completed through the following equations of state:

E=\rho e+\frac{\rho}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2}),\quad e=\frac{p}{(\gamma-1)\rho}.

(B.2)

where, as in the 1-D case,
e
is the specific internal energy, and
\gamma
is the parameter representing the specific heat ratio.

In what follows, we discuss the reconstruction of the point values
\bm{U}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}
and
\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm E,W}_{j,k}
(the point values

\bm{U}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}
and
\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm N,S}_{j,k}
can be computed in a similar manner and we omit the details for the sake of brevity).
To this end, we first compute the Jacobian

\displaystyle A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
\dfrac{\gamma-3}{2}u^{2}+\dfrac{\gamma-1}{2}v^{2}&(3-\gamma)u&(1-\gamma)v&\gamma-1\\
-uv&v&u&0\\
-\dfrac{\gamma uE}{\rho}+(\gamma-1)u(u^{2}+v^{2})&H-(\gamma-1)u^{2}&(1-\gamma)uv&\gamma u\end{pmatrix},

where, as in the 1-D case,
H=\frac{E+p}{\rho}
. We then introduce the matrices

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
\dfrac{\gamma-3}{2}\hat{u}^{2}+\dfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\hat{v}^{2}&(3-\gamma)\hat{u}&(1-\gamma)\hat{v}&\gamma-1\\
-\hat{u}\hat{v}&\hat{v}&\hat{u}&0\\
-\dfrac{\gamma\hat{u}\hat{E}}{\hat{\rho}}+(\gamma-1)\hat{u}(\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2})&\hat{H}-(\gamma-1)\hat{u}^{2}&(1-\gamma)\hat{u}\hat{v}&\gamma\hat{u}\end{pmatrix},

where
\hat{(\cdot)}
stands for the following averages:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}}{2},\quad\hat{u}=\frac{u_{j,k}+u_{j+1,k}}{2},\quad\hat{v}=\frac{v_{j,k}+v_{j+1,k}}{2},\quad\hat{p}=\frac{p_{j,k}+p_{j+1,k}}{2},

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{E}+\hat{p}}{\hat{\rho}},\quad\hat{E}=\frac{\hat{p}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{\hat{\rho}}{2}(\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}),

where

u_{j,k}=\frac{(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho u$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{j,k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}},\quad v_{j,k}=\frac{(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho v$\kern 0.0pt}}})_{j,k}}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{j,k}=(\gamma-1)\left[\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$E$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\frac{\rho_{j,k}}{2}\left(u_{j,k}^{2}+v_{j,k}^{2}\right)\right].

As in Appendix
A
, all of the
\hat{(\cdot)}
quantities have to have a subscript index, that is,

\hat{(\cdot)}=\hat{(\cdot)}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
, which we omit for the sake of brevity for all of the quantities except for
\widehat{A}
. We then
compute the matrix
R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
composed of the right eigenvectors of
\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and obtain

R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&1\\
\hat{u}-\hat{c}&\hat{u}&0&\hat{u}+\hat{c}\\
\hat{v}&\hat{v}&1&\hat{v}\\
\hat{H}-\hat{u}\hat{c}&\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}}{2}&\hat{v}&\hat{H}+\hat{u}\hat{c}\end{pmatrix}

and

R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\dfrac{1}{\hat{\phi}}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{\hat{u}\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{u}-\dfrac{\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{v}&1\\[5.16663pt]
2\hat{\phi}-2\hat{H}&2\hat{u}&2\hat{v}&-2\\
-\hat{v}\hat{\phi}&0&\hat{\phi}&0\\
\dfrac{\hat{u}^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{\hat{u}\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{u}+\dfrac{\hat{\phi}}{2\hat{c}}&-\hat{v}&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\hat{\phi}=2\hat{H}-\hat{u}^{2}-\hat{v}^{2}
and
\hat{c}=\sqrt{\gamma\hat{p}/\hat{\rho}}
.

We then introduce the local characteristic variables in the neighborhood of
(x,y)=(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
:

\bm{\Gamma}_{\ell,k}=R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{\ell,k},\quad\ell=j-1,\ldots,j+2.

Equipped with the values
\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1,k}
,
\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}
,
\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}
, and
\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2,k}
, we compute

(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j,k}={\rm minmod}\left(2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1,k}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j-1,k}}{2\Delta x},\,2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}}{\Delta x}\right),

and

(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j+1,\,k}={\rm minmod}\left(2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}}{2\Delta x},\,2\,\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{j+2,k}-\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}}{\Delta x}\right),

where the minmod function, defined in (
2.7
), is applied in the component-wise manner. We then use these slopes to evaluate

\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm E}_{j,k}=\,\bm{\Gamma}_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j,k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm W}_{j+1,k}=\bm{\Gamma}_{j+1,k}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{\Gamma}_{x})_{j+1,k},

and finally obtain the corresponding point values of
\bm{U}
by

\bm{U}^{\rm E,W}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{\Gamma}^{\rm E,W}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}.

Structure of the nucleon at leading and subleading twist in the covariant parton model
S. Bastami 1 , A. V. Efremov 2 , P. Schweitzer 1 , O. V. Teryaev 2 , P. Zavada 3

Abstract
The covariant parton model is generalized to describe quark correlators in a systematic way. Previous results are reproduced for the T-even leading-twist transverse momentum dependent parton distribution functions (TMDs), and for the first time all T-even twist-3 TMDs are evaluated in this model. We apply the approach to evaluate the fully unintegrated quark correlator which allows us to understand the model-specific relations between different TMDs. We verify the consistency of the approach, present numerical results and compare to available TMD parametrizations.

III
Formulation of the covariant parton model

We define the quark correlator in the CPM as follows

\Phi^{q}(p,P,S)_{ij}=2P^{0}\,\Theta(p^{0})\,\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\;\bar{u}_{j}(p)u_{i}(p)\times\begin{cases}\,\mathcal{G}^{q}(pP)\;&\mbox{unpolarized partons,}\\
\mathcal{H}^{q}(pP)\;&\mbox{polarized partons.}\end{cases}

(6)

The prefactor
2P^{0}
is due to the covariant normalization

\langle N(P^{\prime},S)|N(P,S)\rangle=2P^{0}\,(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{P}^{\,\,\prime}-\vec{P}^{\,})

of the nucleon states in Eq. (
1
).
The onshell condition of the quarks in the CPM is implemented in
terms of the Lorentz-invariant function
\Theta(p^{0})\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})
.

\mathcal{G}^{q}(pP)
describes the covariant momentum distribution
of unpolarized quarks of flavor
q=u,\,d,\,\dots
inside the nucleon, while

\mathcal{H}^{q}(pP)
describes the covariant distribution of polarized quarks.

In the CPM the quarks are on-shell which allows us
to evaluate the bispinor expressions as

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p)\,\Gamma\,u(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\big[\frac{1}{2}\,(\not{p}+m)\,\big(1+\gamma^{5}\,\not{\omega}\big)\,\Gamma\big]\,,

(7)

Here
\omega^{\mu}
is the quark polarization vector which satisfies

\omega^{2}=-1
and
p\cdot\omega=0
and can be expressed in the CPM
in terms of
p^{\mu}
,
P^{\mu}
,
S^{\mu}
as follows
Zavada:2001bq

\omega^{\mu}=-\frac{M}{m}\,\frac{p\cdot S}{p\cdot P+mM}\,p^{\mu}-\frac{p\cdot S}{p\cdot P+mM}\,P^{\mu}+S^{\mu}.

(8)

A more general expression for
\omega^{\mu}
was given in

Zavada:2011cv
which coincides with (
8
) for
massless quarks. In this work we will use Eq. (
8
) for

\omega^{\mu}
and explore the more general representation for
\omega

from
Zavada:2011cv
elsewhere.

By exploring Eq. (
7
) we obtain the following compact
expression

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}[\Phi^{q}(p,P,S)\,\Gamma]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{0}\,\Theta(p^{0})\,\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\;{\rm Tr}\big[(\not{p}+m)\,\bigl(\mathcal{G}^{q}(pP)+\mathcal{H}^{q}(pP)\gamma^{5}\,\not{\omega}\bigr)\,\Gamma\big]\,.

(9)

We recall that the covariant function
{\cal G}^{q}(pP)
is positive and has
a partonic interpretation within the CPM: when interpreted in the nucleon
rest frame it describes the momentum distribution of unpolarized quarks in
the nucleon. Similarly
{\cal H}^{q}(pP)
describes the momentum distribution
of polarized
q
in a nucleon polarized in its rest frame along

S^{\mu}=(0,\vec{S})

Zavada:1996kp
;
Zavada:2001bq
;
Zavada:2002uz
.
The unpolarized covariant function satisfies
G^{q}(Pp)\geq 0
, and the
polarized one
|H^{q}(Pp)|\leq G^{q}(Pp)
which reflects the partonic
interpretation.

The Eqs. (
6
–
9
) can be viewed as a
definition of the CPM and describe how to evaluate in the model
quark correlation functions.
In the remainder of this work, we will compute all twist-2 and
twist-3 T-even TMDs of quarks on the basis of
Eqs. (
6
–
9
). Hereby we will
reproduce results known from previous works, derive many new
results (especially for twist-3 TMDs), and demonstrate the internal
theoretical consistency of the approach.

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}p^{-}\mathrm{d}p^{+}\;\frac{P^{0}\,\mathcal{G}^{q}(pP)}{P^{+}}\;\Theta(p^{0})\,\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\;\;\delta(x-\frac{p^{+}}{P^{+}})\;\bar{u}(p)\,\gamma^{+}u(p)\,,

In the nucleon rest frame

V
Relations among TMDs

In QCD the different TMDs are all independent of each other, and
describe different aspects of the nucleon structure. Due to the simpler
dynamics or additional symmetries, different TMDs can be related to each
other in quark models. The goal of this section is to discuss the
relations among TMDs in the CPM.

V.1
Equation-of-motion relations

An important consistency test of the model results is provided by the
following relations which can be derived by making use of the QCD
equations of motion (EOM) and are given by

\displaystyle xe^{q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{e}^{q}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T}),

(18a)

\displaystyle xf^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{f}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})+f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T}),\phantom{\frac{1}{1}}

(18b)

\displaystyle xg_{L}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{g}_{L}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})+g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,h_{1L}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T}),

(18c)

\displaystyle xg_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})+g_{1T}^{\perp(1)q}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,h_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T}),

(18d)

\displaystyle xg_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{g}_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})+g_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T}),

(18e)

\displaystyle xh_{L}^{q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{h}_{L}^{q}(x,p_{T})-2\,h_{1L}^{\perp(1)q}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T}),

(18f)

\displaystyle xh_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{h}_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})-h_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})-h_{1T}^{\perp(1)}(x,p_{T})+\frac{m}{M}\,g_{1T}^{\perp}(x,p_{T}),

(18g)

\displaystyle xh_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\tilde{h}_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})+h_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})-h_{1T}^{\perp(1)}(x,p_{T}),\phantom{\frac{1}{1}}

(18h)

where the transverse moment
n

f^{q}(x,p_{T})

is defined as follows

The EOMs (

–

or “interaction dependent terms” which are denoted by tilde-functions

but
define
the respective genuine twist-3 tilde contributions.

In quark models in general the tilde terms are non-zero and arise from
the model interactions due to the pertinent model equations of motion.
The EOM of the CPM is the free Dirac equation which

Eqs. (

14

16

17

This is an important consistency test for our new results

V.2
Lorentz invariance relations

In models with no gluonic degrees of freedom, such as the CPM,
T-odd TMDs are absent
Pobylitsa:2003ty
and the quark correlator can
be decomposed in terms of 9 T-even
A_{i}
-amplitudes while it gives rise to
14 T-even TMDs, see Sec.
II
.
As there are 14 TMDs and 9 linearly independent amplitudes, this implies
5 relations among T-even TMDs. These relations are referred to as
Lorentz-invariance relations (LIRs) and are not valid in QCD

Goeke:2003az
but must hold in all models which preserve Lorentz
symmetry and exhibit no gauge degrees of freedom. The LIRs are given by

Mulders:1995dh

\displaystyle g_{T}^{q}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm LIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;g_{1}^{q}(x)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}g^{\perp(1)q}_{1T}(x)\,,

(20a)

\displaystyle h_{L}^{q}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm LIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;h_{1}^{q}(x)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}h^{\perp(1)q}_{1L}(x)\,,

(20b)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{q}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm LIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}h^{\perp(1)q}_{1T}(x)\,,

(20c)

\displaystyle g_{L}^{\perp q}(x)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}g_{T}^{\perp(1)q}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm LIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;0\,,

(20d)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})-h_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm LIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;h^{\perp q}_{1L}(x,p_{T})\,,

(20e)

and connect twist-3 TMDs (on left-hand sides of the above equations)
with twist-2 TMDs (if any, on right hand sides). The CPM satisfies all LIRs.
This is an important consistency test for the model.
The Eqs. (
20a
–
20d
) can be proven using
the methods developed in App. C of Ref.
Efremov:2009ze
,
while (
20e

relation (

V.6
Independent TMDs in the model

The previous section has shown that there are two independent amplitudes:
one unpolarized and one polarized. Consequently, all unpolarized TMDs are
related to each other, and all polarized TMDs are related to each other.
Thus, it is possible to choose one unpolarized and one polarized TMD,
and express all other TMDs in terms of them.

In the unpolarized case we have 3 T-even TMDs
f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
,

e^{q}(x,p_{T})
,
f^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})
. We can choose the well-known
unpolarized twist-2 TMDs as basis function. The other unpolarized
T-even TMDs are then given by

\displaystyle xe^{q}(x,p_{T})\;

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm CPM}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{m}{M}\,f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})

(29a)

\displaystyle xf^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\;

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm CPM}}{{=}}

\displaystyle f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})

(29b)

Notice that
e^{q}(x,p_{T})
vanishes if we one neglects current quark
mass effects.

In the polarized T-even sector we have 11 TMDs. At first glance it would seem
natural to express all polarized TMDs in terms of the relatively well-known
helicity distribution
g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
. This is possible, but not ideal for
the following reason. In the CPM,
g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
exhibits
a node at

(neglecting current quark masses)

.
Consequently TMDs without nodes would be expressed in terms

divided by a prefactor which is singular at
p_{T}=x\,M
in order to remove
the node present in
g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
. This is impractical for phenomenological
applications.

It is clearly an advantage to choose a TMD without a node to express the
other TMDs. A convenient choice for a basis function for polarized TMDs
is transversity
h_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
which exhibits no node in the model and is,
after
g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})
, the currently best known T-even TMD.
It is convenient to neglect current quark mass effects which make the
explicit expressions quite bulky and can be safely expected to be small
in phenomenological applications.

It is convenient to quote the results in 3 groups in which the
polarized TMDs have structurally similar expressions. We can express

g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})

\displaystyle\,,

(30a)

VI.1
Covariant functions and input PDFs

The covariant functions

\displaystyle{\cal G}^{q}(p^{0})\biggl|_{p^{0}=\frac{1}{2}xM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi M^{3}}\frac{d\;}{dx}\,\biggl[\frac{f_{1}^{q}(x)}{x}\biggr],

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{q}(p^{0})\biggl|_{p^{0}=\frac{1}{2}xM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi M^{3}x^{2}}\Biggl[2\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}\,g_{1}^{q}(y)+3g_{1}^{q}(x)-x\frac{d\;}{dx}\,\biggl[\frac{g_{1}^{q}(x)}{x}\biggr]\Biggr].

Several comments are in order. First, the model relates different TMDs

Second, in the case of TMDs we strictly speaking deal with a

The second scale, associated with the removal of rapidity divergences,
can be also chosen to be
\mu^{2}=2.5\,{\rm GeV}^{2}
. The dependence on
this second scale is governed by the CSS evolution equations.
Third, an important feature of the parton model is the partonic interpretation.
In QCD the partonic interpretation is strictly speaking only justified at
leading-order (LO) (and for PDFs of the nucleon, but e.g. not for
nuclei

VII
Conclusions

In this work we have generalized the CPM which was originally formulated
to describe PDFs accessible in DIS through an intuitive modeling of the
hadronic tensor. We have shown that the new formulation of the CPM allows
one to reproduce all results for the T-even twist-2 TMDs

f_{1}^{q}
,
g_{1}^{q}
,
h_{1}^{q}
,
g_{1T}^{\perp q}
,
h_{1L}^{\perp q}
,
h_{1T}^{\perp q}

and the twist-3
g_{T}^{q}
known from prior studies. The advantage of
the new formulation is that it allows one to evaluate systematically
quark correlators in the CPM. We have demonstrated this by deriving
the model expressions for all twist-3 T-even TMDs.

We have checked the consistency of the model by showing that the
QCD equations-of-motion relations are valid in the CPM with
tilde-terms consistently being zero which is to be expected in a parton
model approach. The model also complies with Lorentz-invariance
relations which are valid in quark models which respect
Lorentz symmetry but lack explicit gauge field degrees of freedom.
We have investigated the relations among TMDs in the CPM. Some of
these relations were known from prior studies in other models, but
most of them are specific to our model. The relations among TMDs
may constitute one of the most interesting predictions of the CPM
and they will allow one to test the underlying model concepts
quantitatively in future when more information about TMDs will
become available.

We presented numerical predictions for the T-even TMDs and compared
to studies in other models or lattice QCD, and confronted them with
TMD parametrizations available for transversity and pretzelosity
(the latter with very large uncertainties). No extractions are available
for other TMDs and the model predictions await phenomenological tests
in those cases.
One interesting advantage is that the results from the CPM refer to a
high renormalization scale where the partonic interpretation may be
assumed to be valid, while to the best of our knowledge the results
from other quark model approaches refer to very low hadronic scales

Appendix A
Model results for TMDs in compact notation

In order to have a better overview, it is convenient to
introduce the compact notation for the integration measures

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{d}p^{\scriptscriptstyle 1}_{{\cal G}q}\}=\frac{\mathrm{d}p^{1}}{p^{0}}\,\frac{\mathcal{G}^{q}(p^{0})}{(p^{0}+m)}\,\delta(x-\frac{p^{0}+p^{1}}{M})

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{d}p^{\scriptscriptstyle 1}_{{\cal H}q}\}=\frac{\mathrm{d}p^{1}}{p^{0}}\,\frac{\mathcal{H}^{q}(p^{0})}{(p^{0}+m)}\,\delta(x-\frac{p^{0}+p^{1}}{M})\,.

(33)

Then the model results for the leading twist T-even TMDs
can be summarized as follows

\displaystyle f_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p_{{\cal G}q}^{1}\}\,\biggl[Mx(p^{0}+m)\biggr]

(34a)

\displaystyle g_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\biggl[Mx(p^{0}+m)-\vec{p}_{T}^{\,2}\biggr]

(34b)

\displaystyle g_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\biggl[M(Mx+m)\biggr]

(34c)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{a}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\biggl[Mx(p^{0}+m)-\frac{\vec{p}_{T}^{\,2}}{2}\biggr]

(34d)

\displaystyle h_{1L}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\biggl[-M(Mx+m)\biggr]

(34e)

\displaystyle h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\biggl[-M^{2}\biggr]\,.

(34f)

and the twist-3 T-even TMDs are given by

\displaystyle f^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal G}q}\}\biggl[M(p^{0}+m)\biggr]

(35a)

\displaystyle e^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal G}q}\}\biggl[m(p^{0}+m)\biggr]

(35b)

\displaystyle g_{L}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[M(Mx-p^{0})\biggr]

(35c)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[m(p^{0}+m)+\frac{1}{2}\vec{p}_{T}^{\;2}\biggr]

(35d)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[M^{2}\biggr]

(35e)

\displaystyle h_{L}^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[m(p^{0}+m)+\vec{p}_{T}^{\,2}\biggr]

(35f)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[M(p^{0}-Mx)\biggr]\,.

(35g)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\perp q}(x,p_{T})\,=

\displaystyle\,\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\biggl[M(p^{0}+m)\biggr]

(35h)

The model expressions can be rewritten by exploring
the onshellness of partons which gives rise to identities like

(p^{0}+p^{1})(p^{0}+m)-\frac{1}{2}p_{T}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(xM+m)^{2}
and can be used to
express transversity e.g. as

h_{1}^{q}(x,p_{T})=\int\{\mathrm{d}p^{1}_{{\cal H}q}\}\,\frac{1}{2}(xM+m)^{2}

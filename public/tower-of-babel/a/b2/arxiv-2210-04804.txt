Smooth linearization of nonautonomous dynamics under polynomial behaviour
Lucas Backes, Davor Dragičević, Wenmeng Zhang

Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to formulate new conditions for smooth linearization of nonautonomous systems with discrete and continuous time. Our results assume that the linear part admits a nonuniform polynomial dichotomy and that the associated polynomial dichotomy spectrum exhibits appropriate spectral gap and spectral band conditions. This is in sharp contrast to most of the previous results in the literature which assumed that the linear part admits an exponential dichotomy. Our techniques exploit the relationship between polynomial and exponential dichotomies via a suitable reparametrization of time. Keywords : Linearization, dichotomy spectrum, nonuniform polynomial dichotomy, nonuniform exponential dichotomy AMS (2020) subject classification: 37C15, 37D25



1.
Introduction

One of the most important notions in the theory of dynamical systems is that of (uniform) hyperbolicity. It plays a major part in many situations like in the study of stability, chaos and bifurcations. The counterpart of this notion in the case of nonautonomous dynamical systems is given by the notion of
exponential dichotomy
, whose study dates back to the pioneering work of Perron
[
32
]
. In the discrete time case, we say that the nonautonomous dynamics defined by the sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of invertible linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits an exponential dichotomy
if there exist constants
{K},\lambda>0
and a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m>n
,
A_{n}P_{n}=P_{n+1}A_{n}
and

\displaystyle\|A_{m-1}\cdots A_{n}P_{n}\|\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)},\quad\|A^{-1}_{n}\cdots A^{-1}_{m-1}(\text{\rm Id}-P_{m})\|\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}.

(1.1)

As for hyperbolicity in the case of automomous dynamical systems, the notion of an exponential dichotomy also has wide applications like in the study of invariant manifolds, invariant foliations and normal forms
[
8
,
33
,
37
]
, and there exists an extensive literature involving this notion for both finite and infinite dimensional systems (see for instance
[
16
,
26
,
31
,
43
]
).

Even though the notion of an exponential dichotomy is an important concept, there are many important classes of systems that do not fit in this framework. For example, Coppel
[
17
, p.12]
gave an example
of a planar nonautonomous system without bounded growth that does not admit an exponential dichotomy even though its restriction to the stable and unstable subspaces are contractions and expansions, respectively. As observed in
[
44
, p. 683 and Example 2]
, what fails to be satisfied for this example to admit an (uniform) exponential dichotomy is that the amount of time that we need to wait until we see actual contraction (or expansion) depends on the initial time. In terms of condition (
1.1
) this means that the coefficient
K
may depend on
n
(or
m
) and that it can go to infinite whenever
n
(or
m
) vary.
This gives rise to the notion of
nonuniform
exponential dichotomy introduced by Barreira and Valls
[
8
]
, which turns out to be ubiquitous in the context of the ergodic theory.

On the other hand, due to the flexibility of the nonautonomous setting, it is easy to construct broad classes of systems which admit a splitting into stable and unstable directions, but with non-exponential rates of contraction and expansion. In the present paper we will be concerned with systems which combined both of the previous features: the rates of contraction and expansion along stable and unstable directions are nonuniform and non-exponential. Among many meaningful possibilities, we will consider the notion of a
nonuniform polynomial dichotomy
, which was introduced independently (and in a slightly different manner) by Bento and Silva
[
11
,
12
]

and Barreira and Valls
[
9
]
(see also
[
7
]
). We recall that a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of invertible linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a
nonuniform polynomial dichotomy
if there exist
{K},\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
and a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n
,
A_{n}P_{n}=P_{n+1}A_{n}
and

\displaystyle\|A_{m-1}\cdots A_{n}P_{n}\|\leq{K}(m/n)^{-\lambda}n^{\varepsilon},\quad\|A^{-1}_{n}\cdots A^{-1}_{m-1}(\text{\rm Id}-P_{m})\|\leq{K}(m/n)^{-\lambda}m^{\varepsilon}.

As emphasized in
[
7
]
, this notion arises quite naturally under non-vanishing of appropriate polynomial Lyapunov exponents.

In what follows, we are interested in studying the problem of smooth linearization of nonautonomous systems whose linear part admits a nonuniform polynomial dichotomy. We will consider systems with both discrete and continuous time. This problem consists of finding a time dependent smooth change of coordinates sending the solutions of the nonlinear system into the solutions of the linear one.
In our main results, we first formulate smooth linearization results for one-sided systems whose linear part admits a nonuniform exponential dichotomy, which are deduced from some existing results for two-sided systems
[
22
,
23
]
. Then, these results are applied to the setting of a nonuniform polynomial dichotomy to obtain smooth linearization results when the associated
polynomial dichotomy spectrum
exhibits appropriate spectral gap and spectral band conditions. Our techniques rely on the relationship between polynomial and exponential dichotomies via a suitable reparametrization of time. A key concept that is used in our results is given by the polynomial dichotomy spectrum and an important part of the paper is devoted to study the properties of this set. We emphasize that our results are in sharp contrast to most of the previous results in the literature which assumed that the linear part admits an (uniform/nonuniform) exponential dichotomy.

The linearization problem described above is one of the most fundamental ones in the theory of dynamical systems and has a long history (of which we do not attempt to give a complete survey). In the context of autonomous dynamics, the problem of formulating sufficient conditions under which the conjugacy exhibits higher regularity properties was first considered in the pioneering works of Sternberg
[
39
,
40
]
who proved in the 1950s that
for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
there is an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
C^{k}
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with non-resonance conditions up to order
k
admit a
C^{r}
linearization.
We also mention the fundamental works of Hartman
[
27
]
and Grobman
[
25
]
who proved, independently, in the 1960s that
C^{1}
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms can be
C^{0}
linearized near the hyperbolic ﬁxed point. It shows that if one is only interested in the case where
r=0
in the Sternberg’s theorem, then one can take
k=1
and, moreover, no non-resonance condition is needed (see Section
3
for more details). We note that infinite dimensional versions of this result were obtained independently by Palis (
[
29
]
) and Pugh (
[
34
]
).

The first nonautonomous version of the Grobman-Hartman theorem was established by Palmer
[
30
]
for the case of continuous time dynamics. A discrete time version of Palmer’s result was formulated in
[
2
]
. Both these results were obtained by assuming that the associated linear system admits a uniform exponential dichotomy.
The problem of smooth linearization for nonautonomous systems was considered only recently. To the best of our knowledge, the ﬁrst results in this direction
were obtained in
[
13
,
14
]
, in the uniformly/nonuniformly exponentially stable case. Moreover, in
[
18
]
the authors have established a Sternberg-type theorem in the setting of
a uniform exponential dichotomy with continuous time.
More relevant to our context are the works
[
22
,
23
]
(see also
[
21
]
) where
C^{1}
, differentiable (at 0) and Hölder linearization results were obtained, respectively, in the setting of a nonuniform exponential dichotomy under some spectral gap and spectral band conditions. These results will play a major part in our proofs, as we already emphasized.

Finally, we mention recent results
[
3
,
15
,
28
]
dealing with the smooth linearization of nonautonomous systems in the absence of any kind of dichotomy, non-resonance or spectral gap assumptions. However, as observed in
[
3
]
, such results in the setting of
[
22
]
(and consequently also in the setting of the present paper) can fail to be applicable or could yield weaker results.

This paper is organized as follows: we start our study focusing in discrete time dynamical systems (Sections
2
-
4.3
). In Section
2
we recall some notions of dichotomy and highlight some relations between them. Moreover, we introduce and study a notion of dichotomy spectrum for systems admitting a nonuniform polynomial dichotomy. In Section
3
we present results about
C^{1}
, differentiable (at 0) and Hölder linearization under the assumption of nonuniform exponential dichotomy on the half line. Section
4
is devoted to establish
C^{1}
, differentiable (at 0) and Hölder linearization results under the assumption of nonuniform polynomial dichotomy. At the end of Section
4
, we explain how the results from the previous section can be extended to the infinite dimensional setting. Finally, in Section
5
we obtain continuous time versions of the results obtained in Section
4
. Appendix A is devoted to strengthen the properties of the conjugacies given in
[
22
]
, while in Appendix B we prove Proposition
4
.

2.
Polynomial dichotomy and dichotomy spectrum

In this section we introduce several notions of dichotomies and dichotomy spectra and present some useful properties and relations between these notions. We start by recalling some definitions and introducing some notations.

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all natural numbers,
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers,
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
the set of nonnegative integers and
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
the set of nonpositive integers. Given
J\in\{\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}^{-},\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
and a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in J}
of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
m,n\in J
let us consider

\mathcal{A}(m,n):=\begin{cases}A_{m-1}\ldots A_{n},&m>n;\\
\text{\rm Id},&m=n;\\
A_{m}^{-1}\ldots A_{n-1}^{-1},&m<n,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
Id
denotes the identity operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.1.
Nonuniform polynomial and exponential dichotomies

We introduce the notion of a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy.

Definition 1
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a
nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy
if there exist constants
{K}>0
,
a\geq\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
and a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following properties hold:

•

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

A_{n}P_{n}=P_{n+1}A_{n};

(2.2)

•

for
m\geq n
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}\|\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}n^{\varepsilon},\quad\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}\|\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}m^{\varepsilon},\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

(2.3)

where
Q_{m}:={\rm Id}-P_{m}
;

•

for
m\geq n
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)\|\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}n^{\varepsilon},\qquad\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)\|\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}m^{\varepsilon}.

(2.4)

In particular, when
\varepsilon=0
we say that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a
uniform strong polynomial dichotomy
.

We also recall the notion of a nonuniform strong exponential dichotomy.

Definition 2
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in J}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that
(A_{n})_{n\in J}
admits a
nonuniform strong exponential dichotomy

if there exist constants
{K}>0
,
a\geq\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
and a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following properties hold:

•

for
n,n+1\in J
, (
2.2
) holds;

•

for
m\geq n
,
m,n\in J
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}(m,n)P_{n}\|\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)+\varepsilon|n|},\quad\|{\mathcal{A}}(n,m)Q_{m}\|\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)+\varepsilon|m|},\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

(2.5)

where
Q_{m}:=\text{\rm Id}-P_{m}
;

•

for
m\geq n
,

\|{\mathcal{A}}(m,n)\|\leq{K}e^{a(m-n)+\varepsilon|n|},\qquad\|{\mathcal{A}}(n,m)\|\leq{K}e^{a(m-n)+\varepsilon|m|}.

(2.6)

In particular, when
\varepsilon=0
we say that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a
uniform strong exponential dichotomy
.

2.2.
Polynomial and exponential dichotomies with respect to a sequence of norms

Definition 3
.

Let
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We say that a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
admits a
strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to

(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
if there exist constants
{K}>0
,
a\geq\lambda>0
and a sequence of projections
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following properties hold:

•

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (
2.2
) holds;

•

for
m\geq n
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|_{m}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n},\\
&\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}x\|_{n}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{m},\end{split}

(2.7)

where
Q_{m}:={\rm Id}-P_{m}
;

•

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)x\|_{m}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n},\quad\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)x\|_{n}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{m}.\!\!\!

(2.8)

Remark 1
.

Observe that in the above Definitions
1
-
3
, it is allowed that
P_{n}=\text{\rm Id}
or
P_{n}={\bf 0}
(the nul-operator), which correspond to the contractive case and the expansive case, respectively.

The following result gives the relationship between nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomies and polynomial dichotomies with respect to a sequence of norms.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy;

(2)

(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with the property that there exist
C>0
and
\delta\geq 0
such that

\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{n}\leq Cn^{\delta}\|x\|,\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(2.9)

Proof.

Assume that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that (
2.9
) holds with
C>0
and
\delta\geq 0
. Observe that (
2.7
) and (
2.9
) imply that

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|\leq\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|_{m}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n}\leq C{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}n^{\delta}\|x\|,

and

\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}x\|\leq\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}x\|_{n}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{m}\leq C{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}m^{\delta}\|x\|

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Simialrly, (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) give that

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)x\|\leq C{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}n^{\delta}\|x\|,\quad\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)x\|\leq C{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}m^{\delta}\|x\|

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, we conclude that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy.

Suppose now that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\|x\|_{n}:=\|x\|_{n}^{s}+\|x\|_{n}^{u},

where

\|x\|_{n}^{s}:=\sup_{m\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{m<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg),

and

\|x\|_{n}^{u}:=\sup_{m<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{m\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg).

Observe that

\|x\|\leq\|P_{n}x\|+\|Q_{n}x\|\leq\|x\|_{n}^{s}+\|x\|_{n}^{u}=\|x\|_{n},

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
. Moreover, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) imply that

\|x\|_{n}^{s}\leq Kn^{\varepsilon}\|x\|+Kn^{\varepsilon}\|P_{n}x\|\leq({K}+{K}^{2})n^{2\varepsilon}\|x\|,

and similarly,

\|x\|_{n}^{u}\leq({K}+{K}^{2})n^{2\varepsilon}\|x\|

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We conclude that (
2.9
) holds with
C=2({K}+{K}^{2})>0
and
\delta=2\varepsilon\geq 0
.

Moreover, for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|_{m}=\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|^{s}_{m}\\
&=\sup_{k\geq m}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{k<m}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&\leq\sup_{k\geq m}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{n\leq k<m}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\sup_{k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&\leq 2\sup_{k\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&=2\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\sup_{k\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{n}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=}+\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-a}\sup_{k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{n}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg),\\
&\leq 2\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n},\end{split}

(2.10)

establishing the first inequality in (
2.7
). On the other hand, for
m\leq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|_{m}=\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|^{u}_{m}\\
&\leq\sup_{k\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+\sup_{m\leq k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\sup_{k<m}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&\leq\sup_{k\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)+2\sup_{k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{m}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&=\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{\lambda}\sup_{k\geq n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{k}{n}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg)\\
&\phantom{=}+2\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{a}\sup_{k<n}\bigg(\|\mathcal{A}(k,n)P_{n}x\|\bigg(\frac{n}{k}\bigg)^{-a}\bigg)\\
&\leq 2\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n}.\end{split}

(2.11)

Similarly, one can establish that

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}x\|_{m}\leq 2\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n}\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $m\leq n$,}

(2.12)

and

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}x\|_{m}\leq 2\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n},\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $m\geq n$.}

(2.13)

In particular, the second estimate in (
2.7
) follows from (
2.12
). Moreover, (
2.10
) and (
2.13
) imply that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)x\|_{m}&\leq\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}x\|_{m}+\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}x\|_{m}\\
&\leq 2\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n}+2\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n}\leq 4\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n},\end{split}

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, the first inequality in (
2.8
) holds. Similarly, using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) one can establish the second estimate in (
2.8
). The proof is completed.

;

•

(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{2^{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. It follows from (
2.2
) that

P_{2^{n+1}}B_{n}=P_{2^{n+1}}\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},2^{n})=\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},2^{n})P_{2^{n}}=B_{n}P_{2^{n}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

By (
2.7
), we have that

\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)P_{2^{n}}x\|_{2^{m}}=\|\mathcal{A}(2^{m},2^{n})P_{2^{n}}x\|_{2^{m}}\leq{K}e^{-\lambda\log 2(m-n)}\|x\|_{2^{n}},

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Similarly,

\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)Q_{2^{n}}x\|_{2^{m}}\leq{K}e^{-\lambda\log 2(n-m)}\|x\|_{2^{n}},

for
m\leq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
Q_{2^{n}}:=\text{\rm Id}-P_{2^{n}}
. Finally, (
2.8
) implies that

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)x\|_{2^{m}}\leq{K}e^{a\log 2(m-n)}\|x\|_{2^{m}},\\
&\|\mathcal{B}(n,m)x\|_{2^{n}}\leq{K}e^{a\log 2(m-n)}\|x\|_{2^{m}},\end{split}

(2.18)

for
m\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We conclude that
(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{2^{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
.

Assume now that
(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{2^{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and projections
\tilde{P}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Namely, there exist
{K}^{\prime},\lambda>0
such that

\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)\tilde{P}_{n}x\|_{2^{m}}\leq{K}^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|x\|_{2^{n}},\quad\text{for $m\geq n$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

(2.19)

\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)\tilde{Q}_{n}x\|_{2^{m}}\leq{K}^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(n-m)}\|x\|_{2^{n}},\quad\text{for $m\leq n$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

(2.20)

where
\tilde{Q}_{n}:=\text{\rm Id}-\tilde{P}_{n}
,
and

\tilde{P}_{n+1}B_{n}=B_{n}\tilde{P}_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(2.21)

Take an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there exists a unique
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
2^{n}\leq k<2^{n+1}
, which enables us to set

P_{k}:=\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k).

Observe that
P_{k}
is a projection for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We claim that (
2.2
) holds. In fact, when
2^{n}\leq k<k+1<2^{n+1}
, we have

\displaystyle P_{k+1}A_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(k+1,2^{n})\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k+1)A_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)=A_{k}P_{k}

and, when
2^{n}\leq k<k+1=2^{n+1}
, we have (using (
2.21
)) that

\displaystyle P_{k+1}A_{k}=\tilde{P}_{n+1}A_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(k+1,2^{n+1})\tilde{P}_{n+1}\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},k+1)A_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n+1})\tilde{P}_{n+1}\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},k)

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\mathcal{A}(2^{n},2^{n+1})\tilde{P}_{n+1}\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},2^{n})\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})B_{n}^{-1}\tilde{P}_{n+1}B_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)

\displaystyle=A_{k}\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)=A_{k}P_{k}.

Take now arbitrary
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\geq l
, and choose
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
2^{m}\leq k<2^{m+1}
and
2^{n}\leq l<2^{n+1}
. Obviously,
m\geq n\geq 0
. Moreover, observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k,l)P_{l}=\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},l)=\mathcal{A}(k,2^{m})\mathcal{B}(m,n)\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},l).

(2.22)

Hence, by (
2.8
) and (
2.19
) we have that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(k,l)P_{l}x\|_{k}&=\|\mathcal{A}(k,2^{m})\mathcal{B}(m,n)\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},l)x\|_{k}\\
&\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{k}{2^{m}}\bigg)^{a}\|\mathcal{B}(m,n)\tilde{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(2^{n},l)x\|_{2^{m}}\\
&\leq KK^{\prime}2^{a}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(2^{n},l)x\|_{2^{n}}\\
&\leq KK^{\prime}2^{a}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}{K}\bigg(\frac{l}{2^{n}}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{l}\\
&\leq{K}^{2}{K}^{\prime}4^{a}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|x\|_{l}={K}^{2}{K}^{\prime}4^{a}e^{-\lambda\left(\lfloor\frac{\log k}{\log 2}\rfloor-\lfloor\frac{\log l}{\log 2}\rfloor\right)}\|x\|_{l}\\
&\leq{K}^{2}{K}^{\prime}4^{a}e^{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{k}{l}\bigg)^{-\frac{\lambda}{\log 2}}\|x\|_{l},\end{split}

for
k\geq l
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This proves the first inequality in (
2.7
). Similarly, one can establish the second inequality in (
2.7
). Then, (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) imply that

(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

3.
Smooth linearization under exponential dichotomy

We emphasize that
C^{0}
conjugacy in general does not preserve important dynamical properties
such as characteristic directions and smoothness of invariant manifolds. Preserving these properties needs at least
C^{1}
conjugacy. In 1970s, an effort was made by Belitskii in
[
10
]
to establish a
C^{1}
linearization
of
C^{2}
hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
under certain second order non-resonance conditions. This result requires much weaker conditions on smoothness and non-resonance than the Sternberg’s theorem in
C^{1}
case. Moreover, Hartman’s example given in
[
27
]
shows that Belitskii’s non-resonance conditions cannot be avoided.

Since 1990s, two independent research directions emerged. In one direction, the goal was to extend Belitskii’s result to the infinite-dimensional setting, while the other direction was concerned with differentiable (at the fixed point
0
) linearization in the absence of non-resonance conditions.
Concerning the first direction of research, we mention the work
[
42
]
, which established
C^{1}
linearization result on Banach spaces under appropriate spectral gap and spectral band conditions for the linear part. This result was further extended to the nonautonomous setting in
[
22
]
. In the second direction, van Strien
[
41
]
claimed the linearization result for
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
without any non-resonance conditions. The conclusion was that there exists a conjugacy which is simultaneously differentiable at 0 and Hölder
continuous near 0 (weaker than
C^{1}
smoothness). However, van Strien’s proof was found to be incorrect (see
[
35
]
). In
[
23
]
, the authors gave a correct proof of
van Strien’s result by using different methods.

In what follows, we present two linearization results for nonautonomous dynamics under the assumption that the linear part admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms. The first result (see Theorem
1
) gives conditions under which the conjugacies are
C^{1}
, while the second result (see Theorem
2
) is concerned with the case when the conjugacies are differentiable at
0
and locally Hölder
continuous. In contrast to the existing result in the literature, the novelty is that Theorems
1
and
2
are concerned with one-sided dynamics (which will make them applicable to the problem of linearization under polynomial behaviour).

3.1.
C^{1}
linearization under exponential dichotomy

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbb{B}=(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
.
Suppose that
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}
has the form (
2.23
), where

a_{1}\leq b_{1}<\ldots a_{k}\leq b_{k}<1<a_{k+1}\leq b_{k+1}<\ldots a_{r}\leq b_{r}

(3.1)

and

\begin{cases}a_{k+1}/b_{k}>\max\{b_{r},a_{1}^{-1}\},\\
b_{i}/a_{i}<b_{k}^{-1},\ \forall i=1,\ldots,k,\ b_{j}/a_{j}<a_{k+1},\forall j=k+1,\ldots,r.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Moreover, let
f_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, be a sequence of
C^{1}
maps such that

f_{n}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad Df_{n}(0)=0,

(3.3)

and

\|Df_{n}(x)v\|_{n+1}\leq\eta\|v\|_{n},\quad\|(Df_{n}(x)-Df_{n}(y))v\|_{n+1}\leq L\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n},

(3.4)

with constants
L,\eta>0
for
x,y,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, provided that
\eta
is sufficiently small, there exists a sequence of
C^{1}
-diffeomorphisms
h_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, such that

•

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

h_{n+1}\circ(B_{n}+f_{n})=B_{n}\circ h_{n};

•

there exist
M,\rho>0
such that

\|Dh_{n}(x)v\|_{n}\leq M\|v\|_{n},\quad\|Dh_{n}^{-1}(x)v\|_{n}\leq M\|v\|_{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\|x\|_{n}\leq\rho
.

Remark 5
.

Theorem
1
is a consequence of Theorem
7
whose proof will be given in Appendix A. In fact, Theorem
7
deals with the case of
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, while Theorem
1
deals with the case of
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Moreover, observe that
1\notin\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}
since
\mathbb{B}
admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Set
f_{n}:=0
for all
n<0
. Then, (
3.4
) holds for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Choose
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r+1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

0<c_{1}<a_{1}\leq b_{1}<c_{2}<a_{2}\leq b_{2}<\ldots a_{r}\leq b_{r}<c_{r+1}.

For each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r+1\}
, set

S_{i}:=\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{c_{i}^{n}}\|\mathcal{B}(n,0)x\|<+\infty\bigg\}.

Then (see the proof of Proposition
4
), for each
i\in\{1,2\ldots,r+1\}
, the sequence
(\frac{1}{c_{i}}B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a nonuniform exponential dichotomy with respect to projections
\tilde{P}_{n}^{i}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, where
\Ima\tilde{P}_{0}^{i}=S_{i}
. Moreover, the following holds true:

•

S_{i}
does not depend on the particular choice of
c_{i}
;

•

S_{1}=\{0\}\subsetneq S_{2}\ldots\subsetneq S_{r}\subsetneq S_{r+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Next, choose subspaces
V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\mathbb{R}^{d}=S_{r}\oplus V_{r},\ S_{r}=S_{r-1}\oplus V_{r-1},\ldots,S_{3}=S_{2}\oplus V_{2}.

Hence,

\mathbb{R}^{d}=V_{r}\oplus V_{r-1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{2}\oplus S_{2}.

Define an operator
B
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle B\rvert_{S_{2}}=a_{1}\text{\rm Id}_{S_{2}},\ B\rvert_{V_{2}}=a_{2}\text{\rm Id}_{V_{2}},\ldots,B\rvert_{V_{r}}=a_{r}\text{\rm Id}_{V_{r}},

(3.5)

where
\text{\rm Id}_{V}
denotes the identity map on a subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover,
set
B_{n}:=B
and
\|\cdot\|_{n}=\|\cdot\|
for
n<0
.

Set
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:=\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,
\mathbb{B}^{\prime}:=(B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and let
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
be defined as in the Definition
6
.
We now claim that
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}=\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
. As mentioned above,
S_{i}
does not depend on
c_{i}
. Thus, for any

\tilde{c}_{1}\in(0,a_{1}),~~\tilde{c}_{i}\in(b_{i-1},a_{i}),~~i\in\{2,...,r\},~~\tilde{c}_{r+1}\in(b_{r},\infty),

we have that the sequence
(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}_{i}}B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
also admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\mathcal{S}
and projections
\tilde{P}_{n}^{i}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that

\Ima\tilde{P}_{0}^{i}=S_{i}
. Moreover, it is easy to see from (
3.5
) that the sequence
(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}_{i}}B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}^{-}
with respect to the sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and projections
\tilde{P}_{n}^{i}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
\Ker\tilde{P}_{0}^{-}=V_{i}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{r}
. Since,

\mathbb{R}^{d}=S_{i}\oplus V_{i}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{r},

we conclude from the proof of
[

that
(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}_{i}}B_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}
with respect to the sequence of norms
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
This implies that
(0,\infty)\setminus\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}\subset(0,\infty)\setminus\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
, i.e.
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\subset\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}
. On the other hand, we trivially have that
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}\subset\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
, proving that the two spectra coincide, i.e.,

\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}=\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{B},\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}[a_{i},b_{i}].

The conclusion of the theorem now follows readily from Theorem
7
given in the Appendix A (which is a corollary of
[
22
, Theorem 2]
).
∎

4.1.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy and let
\mathcal{S}=\{{\|\cdot\|_{n}};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the sequence of norms given by Proposition
1
.
Suppose that
\Sigma_{PD,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}
has the form (
2.24
), where the numbers
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
satisfy (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).
Moreover, let
g_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence of
C^{1}
maps with the following properties:

•

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

g_{n}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad Dg_{n}(0)=0;

(4.1)

•

there exists
c>0
such that

\|Dg_{n}(x)\|\leq\frac{c}{(n+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};

(4.2)

•

there exists
L>0
such that

\|Dg_{n}(x)-Dg_{n}(y)\|\leq\frac{L}{(n+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.3)

where
\varepsilon\geq 0
is as in Definition

.

Then, provided that
c
is sufficiently small, there exists a sequence of

- diffeomorphisms
\psi_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

(4.4)

Moreover, there exist
\tilde{M},\tilde{\rho}>0
such that

(4.5)

for

and

Proof.

We begin by recalling that (

) holds with

(see the proof of Proposition
1
).
Setting
G_{n}:=A_{n}+g_{n}
for

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(m,n):=\begin{cases}G_{m-1}\circ\ldots\circ G_{n},&m>n;\\
\text{\rm Id},&m=n,\end{cases}

we easily verify that

\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)=\mathcal{A}(m,n)x+\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)g_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x))

(4.6)

for

and

(4.7)

Observe that (

) and (

\|Dg_{n}(x)v\|_{n+1}\leq C(n+1)^{2\varepsilon}\|Dg_{n}(x)v\|\leq\frac{cC}{n+1}\|v\|\leq\frac{cC}{n+1}\|v\|_{n}

(4.8)

and therefore

\begin{split}&\|D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)v\|_{m}\\

&\leq K\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|v\|_{n}+K\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\frac{cC}{j+1}\|D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)v\|_{j}\end{split}

by (

),
which is equivalent to

\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{a}\|D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)v\|_{m}\leq K\|v\|_{n}+K\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\frac{cC}{j+1}\bigg(\frac{n}{j}\bigg)^{a}\|(D\mathcal{G}(j,n))(x)v\|_{j}

for
m\geq n
and
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We need the following discrete-version of Gronwall’s lemma (see e.g.

Lemma 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
. Suppose that
(u_{m})_{m\geq n}
and
(z_{m})_{m\geq n}
are two nonnegative sequences satisfying

u_{m}\leq K\{u_{0}+\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}z_{j}u_{j}\}

for
m\geq n
.
Then

\displaystyle u_{m}\leq Ku_{0}\,e^{\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}(Kz_{j})},\quad\forall m\geq n.

By the above lemma, we find that

\bigg(\frac{n}{m}\bigg)^{a}\|D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)v\|_{m}\leq K\|v\|_{n}e^{\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\frac{cCK}{j+1}}.

Since
{\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\frac{1}{j+1}}\leq\log m-\log n
,
we obtain that

\|D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)v\|_{m}\leq K\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a+cCK}\|v\|_{n},

(4.9)

for
m\geq n\geq 1
and
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Furthermore, we see from (
4.7
) that

\displaystyle D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(y)

\displaystyle=\phantom{=}\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)

\displaystyle=\phantom{=}\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x))(D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)(Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))-Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y).

(4.10)

In addition, (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
4.2
) imply that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x))(D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))v\bigg\|_{m}

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\frac{cC}{j+1}\|(D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))v\|_{j}.

(4.11)

Furthermore, by (
2.8
), (
2.9
), (
4.3
) and (
4.9
) we have that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\mathcal{A}(m,j+1)(Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))-Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)v\bigg\|_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}K\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\|(Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))-Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)v\|_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}KC\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}(j+1)^{2\varepsilon}\|(Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))-Dg_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)))D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)v\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}(j+1)^{2\varepsilon}\frac{KLC}{(j+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}}\|\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)\|\cdot\|D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)v\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\frac{KLC}{j+1}\|\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)\|_{j}\cdot\|D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y)v\|_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\frac{KLC}{j+1}K^{2}\bigg(\frac{j}{n}\bigg)^{2a+2cCK}\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq K^{3}LC\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{2a+2cCK}~\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\frac{1}{j+1}\,\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq K^{3}LC\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{2a+2cCK+1}\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n}.

(4.12)

Hence,
from (
4.1
), (
4.1
) and (
4.1
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&\|(D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(y))v\|_{m}\\
&\leq K\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}\bigg(\frac{m}{j+1}\bigg)^{a}\frac{cC}{j+1}\|(D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(j,n)(y))v\|_{j}\\
&\phantom{\leq}+K^{3}LC\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{2a+2cCK+1}\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n}.\end{split}

This together with Lemma
1
implies that

\|(D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(x)-D\mathcal{G}(m,n)(y))v\|_{m}\leq\tilde{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{\tilde{a}}\|x-y\|_{n}\cdot\|v\|_{n},

(4.13)

for
m\geq n\geq 1
and
x,y,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\tilde{K}:=K^{3}LC>0
and

.

On the other hand, observe that (

\mathcal{G}(2^{m},2^{n})(x)=\mathcal{B}(m,n)x+\sum_{j=2^{n}}^{2^{m}-1}\mathcal{A}(2^{m},j+1)g_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,2^{n})(x)),

for

and

. For

and

, set

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{G}(2^{n+1},2^{n})(x)-\mathcal{B}(n+1,n)x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}-1}\mathcal{A}(2^{n+1},j+1)g_{j}(\mathcal{G}(j,2^{n})(x)).

Then,

\begin{split}\|(Df_{n}(x)-Df_{n}(y))v\|_{2^{n+1}}&=\|(D\mathcal{G}(2^{n+1},2^{n})(x)-D\mathcal{G}(2^{n+1},2^{n})(y))v\|_{2^{n+1}}\\

and

.
This proves the second inequality of (

) is proved.

Applying Theorem

to

,

Moreover, there exist

such that

for

and

.
Hence,

(4.16)

Letting

and

be such that

(4.17)

and assert that (

) holds. For this purpose, we first assume that

\begin{split}\psi_{k+1}\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})&=\mathcal{A}(k+1,2^{n})\circ h_{n}\circ\mathcal{G}(2^{n},k+1)\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})\\
&=A_{k}\circ\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})\circ h_{n}\circ\mathcal{G}(2^{n},k)\\
&=A_{k}\circ\psi_{k},\end{split}

and thus (

) holds. Let us now suppose that

. Then, using (

) we have that

\begin{split}A_{k}\circ\psi_{k}&=\mathcal{A}(k+1,2^{n})\circ h_{n}\circ\mathcal{G}(2^{n},k)\\

&=h_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{G}(2^{n+1},2^{n})\circ\mathcal{G}(2^{n},k)\\
&=h_{n+1}\circ\mathcal{G}(k+1,k)=h_{n+1}\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})=\psi_{k+1}\circ(A_{k}+g_{k}),\end{split}

where the last equality holds because

Consequently, (

), (

), (

) and (

\begin{split}\|D\psi_{k}(x)v\|&\leq\|D\psi_{k}(x)v\|_{k}\leq K2^{a+cCK}\|Dh_{n}(\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)x)\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)v\|_{2^{n}}\\
&\leq K^{2}M2^{2a+cCK}\|v\|_{k}\leq CK^{2}M2^{2a+cCK}k^{2\varepsilon}\|v\|\end{split}

whenever

since it implies

\|\mathcal{A}(2^{n},k)x\|_{2^{n}}\leq K2^{a}\|x\|_{k}\leq CK2^{a}k^{2\varepsilon}\|x\|\leq\rho.

Hence, the first estimate in (
4.5
) holds. Similarly, one can establish the second estimate in (
4.5
). The proof of this theorem is completed.

We now apply Theorem
3
to a very simple example, which is not covered by previously known results.

Example 1
.

Let us consider the sequences of linear operators
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
acting on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

A_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{n}{n+1}&0\\
0&\frac{n+1}{n}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad P_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then,

\mathcal{A}(m,n)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{n}{m}&0\\
0&\frac{m}{n}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for every }m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to see that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy with constants
K=a=\lambda=1
,
\varepsilon=0
. Furthermore, it admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{n}=\|\cdot\|
is the Euclidean norm. It is easy to see that
\Sigma_{PD,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}=\{-1,1\}
. In particular,
r=2
,
a_{1}=b_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
and
a_{2}=b_{2}=2
. Thus, conditions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are satisfied.

Consider now
\xi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\xi(x)=x^{2}e^{-x^{2}}
. Then,
D\xi(x)=2xe^{-x^{2}}(1-x^{2})
and, consequently,
|D\xi(x)|\leq 1
and
|D\xi(x)-D\xi(y)|\leq 2|x-y|
. Thus, taking
g_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

g_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{c}{n+1}(\xi(x_{1}),\xi(x_{2})),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
c>0
is a constant, it follows that conditions (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) are satisfied. In particular, Theorem
3
may be applied whenever
c>0
is small enough. On the other hand, it is easy to see that the sequence
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not admit a nonuniform strong exponential dichotomy. Therefore, previously known results such as the one in
[
22
]
can not be applied to this example.

4.2.
Differentiable and Hölder linearization under polynomial dichotomy

In the next result we remove the spectral gap condition from our hypothesis (i.e., the first inequality of (
3.2
)), and obtain a linearization which is simultaneously differentiable (at 0) and Hölder continuous in a neighborhood of 0.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

and

) and (

) hold.
Let

such that (

)-(

and

is sufficiently small, there exists a sequence of homeomorphisms
\psi_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

) holds.

(4.18)

as

and

\|\psi_{n}(x)-\psi_{n}(y)\|\!\leq\!L^{\prime}n^{2\varepsilon\alpha_{1}}\|x-y\|^{\alpha_{1}},\quad\|\psi_{n}^{-1}(x)-\psi_{n}^{-1}(y)\|\!\leq\!\ L^{\prime}n^{2\varepsilon\alpha_{1}}\|x-y\|^{\alpha_{1}}

for

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
3
. By Theorem
2
, there exist a sequence of homeomorphisms

,

as

(4.21)

for

satisfying

Next, we may construct a sequence of homeomorphisms
\psi_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

, exactly as in the proof of Theorem
3
(see (

)) such that (

) holds. Moreover, using (

), (

), (
4.9
), (

\begin{split}\|\psi_{k}(x)-\psi_{k}(y)\|&=\|\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(x))-\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(y))\|\\
&\leq\|\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(x))-\mathcal{A}(k,2^{n})h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(y))\|_{k}\\
&\leq K2^{a}\|h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(x))-h_{n}({\mathcal{G}}(2^{n},k)(y))\|_{2^{n}}\\

\begin{split}\psi_{k+1}-\text{\rm Id}&=A_{k}\circ\psi_{k}\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})^{-1}-\text{\rm Id}\\

\begin{split}&\|A_{k}\circ({\psi}_{k}-\text{\rm Id})\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})^{-1}(x)\|_{k+1}\\
&\leq K2^{a}\|({\psi}_{k}-\text{\rm Id})\circ(A_{k}+g_{k})^{-1}(x)\|_{k}\\
&=o(\|(A_{k}+g_{k})^{-1}(x)\|_{k})=o(\|x\|_{k+1})\end{split}

as
\|x\|_{k+1}\to 0
.
From the last two estimates we conclude that (
4.22
) holds for
k+1
. By induction, we obtain that (
4.22
) holds for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, recalling (
2.9
) we have that

\|\psi_{k}(x)-x\|\leq\|\psi_{k}(x)-x\|_{k}=o(\|x\|_{k}^{1+\varrho})=k^{2\varepsilon(1+\varrho)}o(\|x\|^{1+\varrho})

as
\|x\|\to 0
, which proves the first estimate of (
4.18
). The second one of (
4.18
) can be proved similarly and the proof is completed.
∎

4.3.
The case of infinite-dimensional dynamics

The purpose of this short subsection is to indicate how one can extend our results to the infinite-dimensional setting. Let
X
be an arbitrary Hilbert space and
\mathcal{B}(X)
be a space of all bounded linear operators on
X
.

One can introduce all relevant notions of dichotomy (as in Definitions
1
-
4
) for sequences of invertible operators in
\mathcal{B}(X)
. Moreover, one can establish versions of Propositions
1
,
2
and
3
in the infinite-dimensional setting by arguing exactly as we did in the finite-dimensional setting. Indeed, note that in the proofs of these results finite dimensionality was never used.

Moreover, for a sequence
\mathbb{A}
of invertible operators in
\mathcal{B}(X)
and a sequence of norms
\mathcal{S}
on
X
, one can introduce
\Sigma_{PD,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}
and
\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}
as in Definitions
5
and
6
. Then, Corollary
1
remains valid. However, Proposition
4
(and consequently also Corollary
2
) fails to hold in the infinite-dimensional setting. Hence, one can formulate versions of Theorems
3

and
4
in the infinite-dimensional case by assuming an additional condition that
\Sigma_{PD,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}
has the form (
2.24
) (which in the finite-dimensional setting is automatically satisfied).

In particular, we note that the preparatory results Theorem
1
and Theorem
7
can be established in exactly the same manner. We only emphasize that the assumption that
X
is a Hilbert space would be used in the construction of (closed) subspaces
S_{i}
appearing in the proof of Theorem
1
.

5.
The case of continuous time

In this section we present versions of Theorems
3
and
4
in the case of continuous time dynamical systems.

Let us consider a linear nonautonomous equation

x^{\prime}=A(t)x\quad t\geq 1,

(5.1)

where
A:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a continuous map acting on
[1,\infty)
and with values in the family of linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By
T(t,s)
we will denote the evolution family associated with (
5.1
).
Moreover, given a continuous function
f\colon[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider the following semilinear differential equation

x^{\prime}=A(t)x+f(t,x),\quad t\geq 1.

(5.2)

5.1.
Polynomial dichotomies for continuous time dynamics

The main notions of dichotomy we are going to use in this section are the following.

Definition 7
.

We say that (
5.1
) admits a
nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy
if there exist
K>0
,
a\geq\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
and a family of projections
P(t)
,
t\geq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following properties hold:

•

for
t\geq s\geq 1
,

P(t)T(t,s)=T(t,s)P(s);

(5.3)

•

for
t\geq s
,

\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{-\lambda}s^{\varepsilon},

(5.4)

and

\|T(s,t)Q(t)\|\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{-\lambda}t^{\varepsilon},

(5.5)

where
Q(t)=\text{\rm Id}-P(t)
;

•

for
t\geq s
,

\|T(t,s)\|\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{a}s^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T(s,t)\|\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{a}t^{\varepsilon}.

(5.6)

In particular, when
\varepsilon=0
we say that (
5.1
) admits a uniform strong polynomial
dichotomy.

Definition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
be a family of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We say that (
5.1
) admits a
strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
if there exist
K>0
,
a\geq\lambda>0
and a family of projections
P(t)
,
t\geq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following properties hold:

•

(
5.3
) is satisfied for every
t\geq s\geq 1
;

•

for
t\geq s\geq 1
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|T(t,s)P(s)x\|_{t}\leq K\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{s}

(5.7)

and

\|T(s,t)Q(t)x\|_{s}\leq K\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{t},

(5.8)

where
Q(t)=\text{\rm Id}-P(t)
;

•

for
t\geq s\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|T(t,s)x\|_{t}\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T(s,t)x\|_{s}\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{t}.

(5.9)

As in the discrete time case, it turns out that the previous notions are strongly related.

Proposition 5
.

The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

(
5.1
) admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy;

(2)

(
5.1
) admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to a family of norms
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
with the property that there exist
C>0
and
\delta\geq 0
such that

\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{t}\leq Ct^{\delta}\|x\|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t\geq 1.

(5.10)

Proof.

Assume (
5.1
) admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy. For
t\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set

\|x\|_{t}:=\|x\|^{s}_{t}+\|x\|^{u}_{t}

where

\begin{split}\|x\|^{s}_{t}&=\sup_{r\geq t}\left(\|T(r,t)P(t)x\|\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{\lambda}\right)+\sup_{r\leq t}\left(\|T(r,t)P(t)x\rVert\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)^{-a}\right)\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|x\|^{u}_{t}&=\sup_{r\leq t}\left(\|T(r,t)Q(t)x\|\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)^{\lambda}\right)+\sup_{r\geq t}\left(\|T(r,t)Q(t)x\rVert\left(\frac{r}{t}\right)^{-a}\right).\end{split}

Then, proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
1
we conclude that (2) is satisfied. Similarly, if (2) is satisfied then arguing as in the proof of Proposition
1
we conclude that (1) is satisfied.
∎

In the next proposition we present a relationship between the dynamics of (
5.1
) and a suitable discretization of it.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
be a family of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that there exist
K,a>0
such that the evolution family
T(t,s)
of (
5.1
) satisfies (
5.9
).
Then, the following properties are equivalent:

•

(
5.1
) admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
;

•

the sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where

A_{n}=T(n+1,n),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.11)

Proof.

Considering
\mathcal{A}(m,n)
as in (
2.1
) with
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by (
5.11
) we have that

\mathcal{A}(m,n)=T(m,n),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.12)

Assume that (
5.1
) admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
and projections
P(t)
,
t\geq 1
. By (
5.7
) and (
5.12
), we have that

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P(n)x\|_{m}\leq K\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{n},\quad m\geq n\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Similarly, (
5.8
) and (
5.12
) imply that

\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q(m)\|_{n}\leq K\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{m},\quad m\geq n\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, by (
5.9
) and (
5.12
) we get that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
m\geq n
,

\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)x\|_{m}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)x\|_{n}\leq{K}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{a}\|x\|_{m}.

Consequently, we conclude that the sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and projections
P(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assume now that the sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect the sequence of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and projections
P_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, there exist
K>0
and
a\geq\lambda>0
such that (
2.2
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) holds.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[n,n+1)
, we set

P(t)=T(t,n)P_{n}T(n,t).

One can easily verify that
P(t)
is a projection for each
t\geq 1
.
Take
t,s\geq 1
and assume
t\geq s
. We choose
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\leq t<m+1
and
n\leq s<n+1
. Clearly,
m\geq n
. Then,

\begin{split}T(t,s)P(s)&=T(t,s)T(s,n)P_{n}T(n,s)\\
&=T(t,n)P_{n}T(n,s)=T(t,m)\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}T(n,s)\\
&=T(t,m)P_{m}\mathcal{A}(m,n)T(n,s)=T(t,m)P_{m}T(m,t)T(t,s)\\
&=P(t)T(t,s).\end{split}

In particular, (
5.3
) holds.

Moreover, the previous calculations
also show that for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\begin{split}\|T(t,s)P(s)x\|_{t}&=\|T(t,m)\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}T(n,s)x\|_{t}\\
&\leq K\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{a}K\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{-\lambda}K\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{a}\|x\|_{s}\\
&\leq K^{3}4^{a}\bigg(\frac{m}{n}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{s}\leq K^{3}4^{a}2^{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{-\lambda}\|x\|_{s}\\
\end{split}

by (
2.7
) and (
5.9
),
which yields (
5.7
). Similarly, one can establish (
5.8
). Noting that (
5.9
) is satisfied by the hypothesis, it follows that (
5.1
) admits a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
as claimed. This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Polynomial dichotomy spectrum

Given a family of norms
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let us consider the set
\Sigma_{PD,A(\cdot),\mathcal{S}}
which consists of all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that the equation

x^{\prime}=\bigg(A(t)-\frac{\tau}{t}\text{\rm Id}\bigg)x,\quad t\geq 1

(5.13)

does not admit a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 6
.

Observing that the evolution family of (
5.13
) is given by

T_{\tau}(t,s)=\bigg(\frac{t}{s}\bigg)^{-\tau}T(t,s),

we have that

T_{\tau}(n+1,n)=\bigg(\frac{n+1}{n}\bigg)^{-\tau}T(n+1,n),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Using this together with Proposition
6
, we see that if
T(t,s)
satisfies (
5.9
) with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
, then the following properties are equivalent:

•

the sequence
((\frac{n+1}{n})^{-\tau}T(n+1,n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not admit a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
;

•

(
5.13
) does not admit a strong polynomial dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\mathcal{S}
.

The following result is a direct consequence of the above remark.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
be a family of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

and consider
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Assume that there exist
K,a>0
such that (
5.9
) holds. Then,

\Sigma_{PD,A(\cdot),\mathcal{S}}=\Sigma_{PD,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}},

where the sequence
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by (
5.11
). In particular,
\Sigma_{PD,A(\cdot),\mathcal{S}}
is given by (
2.24
) with
1\leq r\leq d
and
0<a_{1}\leq b_{1}<a_{2}\leq\ldots<a_{r}\leq b_{r}
.

5.3.
Smooth linearization

The following is a version of Theorem
3
in the continuous time setting.

Theorem 5
.

Let (
5.1
) admit a nonuniform strong polynomial dichotomy and let
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{t};\ t\geq 1\}
be a family of norms given by Proposition
5
. Assume that
\Sigma_{PD,A(\cdot),\mathcal{S}}
is given by (
2.24
), where
a_{i},b_{i}
satisfy (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
Furthermore, suppose that
f\colon[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
C^{1}
map satisfying the following conditions:

•

for
t\geq 1
,

f(t,0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad D_{x}f(t,0)=0;

(5.14)

•

there exists
\eta>0
such that

\|D_{x}f(t,x)\|\leq\frac{\eta}{t^{1+4\varepsilon}},\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$;}

(5.15)

•

there exists
L>0
such that

\|D_{x}f(t,x)-D_{x}f(t,y)\|\leq\frac{L}{t^{1+5\varepsilon}}\|x-y\|,\quad\text{for $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

(5.16)

where
\varepsilon\geq 0
is given in Definition
7
.

Then, provided that
\eta
is sufficiently small, there exist
C^{1}
maps
H,G\colon[1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the following properties:

i)

if
t\mapsto x(t)
is a solution of (
5.2
), then
t\mapsto H(t,x(t))
is a solution of (
5.1
);

ii)

if
t\mapsto x(t)
is a solution of (
5.1
), then
t\mapsto G(t,x(t))
is a solution of (
5.2
);

iii)

for
t\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

H(t,G(t,x))=x\quad\text{and}\quad G(t,H(t,x))=x;

iv)

there exist
R,\zeta>0
such that

\|D_{x}H(t,x)\|\leq Rt^{4\varepsilon}\text{ and }\|D_{x}G(t,x)\|\leq Rt^{4\varepsilon},

for every
t\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\|x\|\leq\frac{\zeta}{t^{3\varepsilon}}
.

Proof.

The general idea of the proof consists of using the discretization of (
5.1
) given by (
5.11
) in order to obtain Theorem
5
as a consequence of Theorem
3
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by (
5.11
). Combining our assumptions with Proposition
6
and Corollary
3
, we understand that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
3
. Let
t\mapsto\varphi(t,t_{0};x_{0})
denote the solution of (
5.2
) satisfying
x(t_{0})=x_{0}
. Hence, by the variation of constants formula, we have that

\displaystyle\varphi(t,n;x)=T(t,n)x+\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))\,dr.

(5.17)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set

g_{n}(x)=\varphi(n+1,n;x)-A_{n}x=\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))\,dr.

(5.18)

We will now verify that this sequence of
C^{1}
maps
g_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies all assumptions of Theorem
3
.

It follows from (
5.14
) that
\varphi(t,t_{0};0)=0
, and thus
g_{n}(0)=0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,

\begin{split}Dg_{n}(0)&=\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)D_{x}f(r,0)D_{x}\varphi(r,n;0)\,dr=0,\end{split}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We conclude that (
4.1
) holds.

Observe that

D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)=T(t,n)+\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\,dr

(5.19)

for
t\geq n
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular, from (
5.6
) and (
5.15
) we obtain that for every
t\in[n,n+1]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\begin{split}\lVert D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)\rVert&\leq K\bigg(\frac{t}{n}\bigg)^{a}n^{\varepsilon}+\int_{n}^{t}K\bigg(\frac{t}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{\eta}{r^{1+4\varepsilon}}\lVert D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\rVert\,dr\\
&\leq K2^{a}n^{\varepsilon}+K\eta 2^{a}\int_{n}^{t}\lVert D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\rVert\,dr.\end{split}

Hence, it follows from Gronwall’s lemma that

\lVert D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)\rVert\leq K2^{a}n^{\varepsilon}e^{\int_{n}^{t}K\eta 2^{a}\,dr},

and therefore

\lVert D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)\rVert\leq\hat{M}n^{\varepsilon}

(5.20)

for every
t\in[n,n+1]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

\hat{M}:=K2^{a}e^{K\eta 2^{a}}.

On the other hand, note that

Dg_{n}(x)=\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\,dr.

(5.21)

Then, combining (
5.6
), (
5.15
), (
5.20
) with (
5.21
) we get

\lVert Dg_{n}(x)\rVert\leq\int_{n}^{n+1}K\bigg(\frac{n+1}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{\eta}{r^{1+4\varepsilon}}\hat{M}n^{\varepsilon}\,dr\leq\frac{K\hat{M}2^{a+1+2\varepsilon}\eta}{(n+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}},

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We conclude that (
4.2
) holds with
c:=K\hat{M}2^{a+1+2\varepsilon}\eta>0
, which can be sufficiently small by taking
\eta
small enough.

Next, we observe that (
5.19
) implies that

\begin{split}&D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(t,n;y)\\
&=\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\,dr\\
&\phantom{=}-\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;y))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\,dr\\
&=\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))(D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y))\,dr\\
&\phantom{=}+\int_{n}^{t}T(t,r)(D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))-D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;y)))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\,dr.\end{split}

Hence, it follows from (
5.6
), (
5.15
), (
5.16
) and
(
5.20
) (together with the
mean-value theorem) that

\begin{split}&\lVert D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(t,n;y)\rVert\\
&\leq\int_{n}^{t}K\bigg(\frac{t}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{L}{r^{1+5\varepsilon}}\lVert\varphi(r,n;x)-\varphi(r,n;y)\rVert\hat{M}n^{\varepsilon}\,dr\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\int_{n}^{t}K\bigg(\frac{t}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{\eta}{r^{1+4\varepsilon}}\lVert D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\rVert\,dr\\
&\leq KL2^{a}\hat{M}^{2}\|x-y\|+K2^{a}\eta\int_{n}^{t}\lVert D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\rVert\,dr,\end{split}

for
t\in[n,n+1]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By Gronwall’s inequality again, one can conclude that there exists
\check{M}>0
such that

\lVert D_{x}\varphi(t,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(t,n;y)\rVert\leq\check{M}\lVert x-y\rVert,

(5.22)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in[n,n+1]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Moreover, since

\begin{split}&Dg_{n}(x)-Dg_{n}(y)\\
&=\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)\,dr\\
&\phantom{=}-\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;y))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\,dr\\
&=\int_{n}^{n+1}T(n+1,r)D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))(D_{x}\varphi(r,n;x)-D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y))\,dr\\
&\phantom{=}+\int_{n}^{n\!+\!1}T(n+1,r)(D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;x))\!-\!D_{x}f(r,\varphi(r,n;y)))D_{x}\varphi(r,n;y)\,dr,\\
\end{split}

we obtain from (
5.15
), (
5.16
), (
5.20
) and (
5.22
) (together with the mean-value
theorem) that

\begin{split}&\lVert Dg_{n}(x)-Dg_{n}(y)\rVert\\
&\leq\int_{n}^{n+1}K\bigg(\frac{n+1}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{\eta}{r^{1+4\varepsilon}}\check{M}\lVert x-y\rVert\,dr\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\int_{n}^{n+1}K\bigg(\frac{n+1}{r}\bigg)^{a}r^{\varepsilon}\frac{L}{r^{1+5\varepsilon}}\hat{M}n^{\varepsilon}\lVert x-y\rVert\hat{M}n^{\varepsilon}\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{K\check{M}2^{a+1+2\varepsilon}\eta}{(n+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}}\|x-y\|+\frac{KL\hat{M}^{2}2^{a+1+2\varepsilon}}{(n+1)^{1+2\varepsilon}}\|x-y\|,\\
\end{split}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Lemma 2
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,

and projections

,

Proof.

It follows from the first inequality in (
2.14

\sup_{m\geq n}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)v\|_{m}<+\infty,

(7.1)

for each

\sup_{m\geq n}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)Q_{n}v\|_{m}<+\infty.

(7.2)

On the other hand, the second estimate in (

From (

, and therefore

.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
be a sequence of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to a sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and projections
P_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Futhermore, let
(P_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
be a sequence of projections on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

P_{n+1}^{\prime}A_{n}=A_{n}P_{n}^{\prime},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(7.3)

Then,
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and projections
P_{n}^{\prime}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if and only if

\Ima P_{0}=\Ima P_{0}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

If
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and projections
P_{n}^{\prime}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, it follows from Lemma
2
that

\Ima P_{0}=\Ima P_{0}^{\prime}=\bigg\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup_{m\geq 0}\|\mathcal{A}(m,0)v\|_{m}<+\infty\bigg\}.

Suppose now that
\Ima P_{0}=\Ima P_{0}^{\prime}
. Then,

P_{0}P_{0}^{\prime}=P_{0}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{0}^{\prime}P_{0}=P_{0},

and consequently

P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime}=P_{0}(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})=(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})Q_{0},

where
Q_{0}=\text{\rm Id}-P_{0}
.
By (
2.14
), we have that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(n,0)(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})v\|_{n}&=\|\mathcal{A}(n,0)P_{0}(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})v\|_{n}\\
&\leq{K}e^{-\lambda n}\|(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})Q_{0}v\|_{0}\\
&\leq KLe^{-\lambda n}\|Q_{0}v\|_{0}\\
&=KLe^{-\lambda n}\|\mathcal{A}(0,m)\mathcal{A}(m,0)Q_{0}v\|_{0}\\
&=KLe^{-\lambda n}\|\mathcal{A}(0,m)Q_{m}\mathcal{A}(m,0)v\|_{0}\\
&\leq K^{2}Le^{-\lambda n-\lambda m}\|\mathcal{A}(m,0)v\|_{m},\end{split}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

L:=\max_{\|v\|_{0}\leq 1}\|(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})v\|_{0}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)P_{m}^{\prime}v\|_{n}&\leq\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)P_{m}v\|_{n}+\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)(P_{m}-P_{m}^{\prime})v\|_{n}\\
&=\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)P_{m}v\|_{n}+\|\mathcal{A}(n,0)(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})\mathcal{A}(0,m)v\|_{n}\\
&\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(n-m)}\|v\|_{m}+K^{2}Le^{-\lambda n-\lambda m}\|v\|_{m}\\
&\leq K^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(n-m)}\|v\|_{m},\end{split}

for
n\geq m
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
K^{\prime}=K+K^{2}L>0
. Similarly, setting
Q_{m}^{\prime}=\text{\rm Id}-P_{m}^{\prime}
we obtain that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}^{\prime}v\|_{n}&\leq\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{m}v\|_{n}+\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)(P_{m}-P_{m}^{\prime})v\|_{n}\\
&\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|v\|_{m}+\|\mathcal{A}(n,0)(P_{0}-P_{0}^{\prime})\mathcal{A}(0,m)v\|_{n}\\
&\leq{K}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|v\|_{m}+K^{2}Le^{-\lambda n-\lambda m}\|v\|_{m}\\
&\leq K^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|v\|_{m},\end{split}

for
m\geq n
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We conclude that
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits a strong exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and projections
P_{n}^{\prime}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
∎

P_{n}
,

. Furthemore, let

Let us now fix an arbitrary sequence
\mathcal{S}=\{\|\cdot\|_{n};\ n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
of norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a sequence
\mathbb{A}=(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
of invertible operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that (
2.15
) holds with some
K,a>0
.
For
r>0
and

, let

S_{r}(n):=\bigg\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sup_{m\geq n}(r^{-(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)v\|_{m})<+\infty\bigg\}.

Clearly,

A_{n}S_{r}(n)=S_{r}(n+1)\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ and $r\in(0,\infty)$,}

which implies that
\dim S_{r}(n)
does not depend on

we have that

Lemma 4
.

The set
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{ED,\mathbb{A},\mathcal{S}}
is closed. Moreover, for
r\in(0,\infty)\setminus\Sigma
we have

S_{r}(n)=S_{r^{\prime}}(n),

(7.5)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
r^{\prime}
in some open interval around
r
.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary
r\in(0,\infty)\setminus\Sigma
. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
r=e^{\mu}
. Hence, there exist
K^{\prime},\lambda>0
and a sequence of projections
P_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
satisfying (
2.2
) and such that

e^{-\mu(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}v\|_{m}\leq K^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|v\|_{n},

and

e^{\mu(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{n}v\|_{n}\leq K^{\prime}e^{-\lambda(m-n)}\|v\|_{m},

for
m\geq n
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, for

we have that

e^{-\mu^{\prime}(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(m,n)P_{n}v\|_{m}\leq{K^{\prime}}e^{-(\lambda-\mu+\mu^{\prime})(m-n)}\|v\|_{n},

e^{\mu^{\prime}(m-n)}\|\mathcal{A}(n,m)Q_{n}v\|_{n}\leq{K^{\prime}}e^{-(\lambda+\mu-\mu^{\prime})(m-n)}\|v\|_{m},

for
m\geq n
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We conclude that
(\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to the sequence of norms

,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and projections
P_{n}
,

whenever

2

Lemma 6

,

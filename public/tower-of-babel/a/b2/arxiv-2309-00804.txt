Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we introduce a discrete version of weighted Morrey spaces, and discuss the inclusion relations of these spaces. In addition, we obtain the boundedness of discrete weighted Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces by establishing a discrete Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for weighted
l^{1}
-sequences. Furthermore, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Morrey spaces are discussed. Particularly, necessary and sufficient conditions are also discussed for the discrete power weights.

1
Introduction

In 1938, the classical Morrey spaces were introduced by Morrey in
[
16
]
to investigate the local behavior of solutions to second order elliptic partial differential equations. In 1987, Chiarenza and Frasca
[
5
]
showed the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on the Morrey spaces. In 2009, Komori and Shirai
[
14
]
defined a weighted Morrey space and investigated the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on this space.

Let
m=(m_{1},\,\cdots,\,m_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
S_{m,N}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\|k-m\|_{\infty}\leq N\}
, where as usual
\|(m_{1},\,\cdots,\,m_{d})\|_{\infty}:=\max\{|m_{i}|:1\leq i\leq d\}
for every
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Then
\left|S_{m,N}\right|=(2N+1)^{d}
-
the cardinality of
S_{m,N}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
. The
discrete Morrey space
is defined by
l^{p}_{q}=l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}})
the set of sequences
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
taking values in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\|x\|_{l^{p}_{q}}:=\sup\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}|S_{m,N}|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}|x(k)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

In 2018, Gunawan, Kikianty and Schwanke
[
10
]
studied the discrete Morrey spaces and their generalizations. In addition, discrete Morrey spaces can be used to study the properties of discrete operators and the properties of the spaces themselves. For more results on discrete Morrey spaces, we lead the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
12
,
13
,
25
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence. The
discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

\mathcal{M}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}x(m):=\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|,\quad m\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

In 2019, Gunawan and Schwanke
[
11
]
discussed the boundedness of the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete Morrey spaces of arbitrary dimension.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
. For all
x\in l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
we have
\mathcal{M}x\in l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|\mathcal{M}x\|_{l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}\leq C\|x\|_{l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}
holds for all
x\in l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
.

On the other hand, it is very important to study weighted estimates for all kinds of discrete operators in harmonic analysis. On the discrete weighted
l^{p}
spaces, in 2021, when
{\mathbb{Z}}
is restricted to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, the boundedness of the discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators was obtained via Hardy operator by Saker and Agarwal
[
18
]
. For more studies of discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
8
,
19
,
20
]
. However, discrete weighted Morrey spaces have not been studied.

Thus, we generate the following natural question:

Q).

Is it possible to prove Theorem
1.3
for weighted sequences in the discrete weighted Morrey spaces when
d=1
?

Our aim is to give an affirmative answer to the question which promotes the development of the applications of discrete Muckenhoupt weights and discrete Morrey spaces on discrete harmonic analysis.

This article is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we introduce the discrete weighted Morrey space, discrete
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
weight and discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Then we prove some inclusion relations of discrete weighted Morrey spaces and discrete

weights. In Sect.

, inspired by the continuous Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for weighted

of Garcia-Cuerva and Rubio de Francia
[

]
, we further give the discrete Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for weighted

(see Theorem

below). As applications, in Sect.

, we obtain strong type and weak type inequalities for the discrete weighted Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces, and the proofs are quite different from the one restricted to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
(

while the discrete Calderón-Zygmund decomposition plays an important role for the bounded results on

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce the discrete weighted Morrey space and discrete Muckenhoupt class. Then we discuss some properties of these spaces and Muckenhoupt classes. Let us begin with the definition of discrete weighted Morrey space.

A discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence
\omega=\{\omega(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of positive real numbers.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
. For two discrete weights
\omega
and
v
, we define the
discrete weighted Morrey space

l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}=l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}({\mathbb{Z}})
to the space of all sequences
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
for which

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}:=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}(v(S_{m,N}))^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where
v(S_{m,N}):=\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}v(k)
. Particularly, when
p=q
,

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}=\|x\|_{l^{p}_{\omega}}:=\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|x(k)|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 2.2
.

When
\omega=v\equiv 1
,
{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}({\mathbb{Z}})
coincides with discrete Morrey space
l^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{Z}})
. In addition, since
\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}}:=\sup\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|S_{m,N}\right|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}
is a norm on
l{{}^{p}_{q}}
(see
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
). Similarly, we can obtain
\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}
is also a norm on
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}
. Furthermore,
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}
is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

(i)

When discrete weight
v=\omega
,
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}
reduces to
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
:

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}:=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}(\omega(S_{m,N}))^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(ii)

When
1\leq p<q=\infty
,
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}
reduces to
l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega,v)}
:

\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega,v)}}:=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}(v(S_{m,N}))^{-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proposition
2.4
will be useful in studying the relations between two discrete weighted Morrey spaces.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\omega
and
v
be discrete weights.

(i)

If
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
\omega(k)\leq Cv(k)
for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(v,\omega)}\subseteq l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}
with
\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega,v)}}\leq C\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(v,\omega)}}
for every
x\in l{{}^{p}_{q}}_{(v,\omega)}
.

(ii)

If
1\leq p<q=\infty
and
v=\omega
, then
l^{\infty}\subseteq{l^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}
with
\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}}\leq\|x\|_{l^{\infty}}
for any
x\in l^{\infty}
.

(iii)

If
1<p<q<\infty
and
v=\omega
, then
{l^{q}_{\omega}}\subseteq{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
and
\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}\leq\|x\|_{{l^{q}_{\omega}}}
for any
x\in{l^{q}_{\omega}}
.

Proof.

\rm(i)
Since
\omega(k)\leq Cv(k)
for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have
\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\leq C\left|x(k)\right|^{p}v(k)
for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain

\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}v(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.1)

In addition, by
\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\leq 0
, we have

\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}v(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.2)

According to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}v(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}v(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This proves
\rm(i)
.

\rm(ii)
Let
1\leq p<q=\infty
. If
v=\omega
and
x\in l^{\infty}
, then for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\omega(S_{m,N})^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\omega(S_{m,N})^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|x\|_{l^{\infty}}\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|x\|_{l^{\infty}},

which implies that
\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}}\leq\|x\|_{l^{\infty}}
and hence
l^{\infty}\subseteq{l^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}
.

Since the proof of
\rm(iii)
is derived immediately from Hölder’s inequality, we omit it.
We finish the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

For
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, we defined the
weighted weak type discrete Morrey space

{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
to be the set of sequences
x=\{x(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
taking values in
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\|x\|_{{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}<\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}
is given by

\|x\|_{{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}:=\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}},\lambda>0}\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\lambda\omega(\{k\in S_{m,N}:|x(k)|>\lambda\})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that when
\omega=1
,
{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}=:wl^{p}_{q}
, which is a weak type discrete Morrey space (see
[
10
, Page 4]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
\omega
be a discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}\subseteq{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
with
\|x\|_{{wl^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}\leq\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}
for every
x\in{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
.

Proof.

For
x\in{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\lambda\omega(\{k\in S_{m,N}:|x(k)|>\lambda\})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N},|x(k)|>\lambda}|x(k)|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}|x(k)|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Taking the supremum over
m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lambda>0
, we finish the proof of Proposition
2.6
.
∎

In the following, referring to the definition of discrete Muckenhoupt class restricted to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
(see
[
21
, Page 2]
), we give the definition of discrete Muckenhoupt class on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

By interval
J
, we mean a finite subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
consisting of consecutive integers, i.e.,
J=\{a,a+1,\dots,a+n\}
,
a,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\left|J\right|
stands for its cardinality.

Definition 2.7
.

A discrete weight
\omega
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}={\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})
if

\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})}:=\sup\limits_{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\left(\frac{1}{\inf\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)\right)<\infty.

For
1<p<\infty
, a discrete weight
\omega
is said to belong to the
discrete Muckenhoupt class

{\mathcal{A}}_{p}={\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})
if

\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})}:=\sup\limits_{J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{J}\omega^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where
\|\omega\|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})}
denotes the norm of weight
\omega
and
J
is any bounded interval in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Define
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}:=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{1\leq p<\infty}{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
.

Some basic properties of discrete
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
weights are given as follows: Their proofs are the same as corresponding results on the real line
[
6
]
. For the sake of the completeness, some of the proofs are included.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\omega
be a discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
;

(ii)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)\right)\|\omega^{-1}\|_{l^{\infty}(J)}\leq C
, for every bounded interval
J\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
. Here,
\|\omega\|_{l^{\infty}(J)}:=\max\limits_{k\in J}\left|\omega(k)\right|
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\omega
be a discrete weight on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}

(1<p<\infty)
;

(ii)

\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\omega(J)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
,
x\in l{{}^{p}_{\omega}}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
\rm(i)\Rightarrow\rm(ii)
. By Hölder’s inequality and the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}

(1<p<\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}\left|x(k)\right|{\omega(k)}^{\frac{1}{p}}{\omega(k)}^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\left|x(k)\right|}^{p}{\omega(k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\left|x(k)\right|}^{p}{\omega(k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{1}{\omega(J)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\left|x(k)\right|}^{p}{\omega(k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Next, let us show
\rm(ii)\Rightarrow\rm(i)
. Let
x(k)={\omega(k)}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
. According to
\rm(ii)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\omega(k)}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}&\leq&C\left(\frac{1}{\omega(J)}\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\omega(k)}^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{array}

\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in J}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in J}{\omega(k)}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq C

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{k\in J}\omega(k)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{\left|J\right|}\sum_{k\in J}\omega(k)^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\bigg)^{p-1}\leq C

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}.

We finish the proof of Proposition
2.9
.
∎

From Propositions
2.8
and
2.9
, we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}

(1\leq p<\infty)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lambda S_{m,N}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\left|k-m\right|\leq\lambda N\}
. For each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, consider the collection of disjoint intervals of cardinality
2^{N}
,

I_{N}:=\{I_{N,j}\}:=\{\{(j-1)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}.

For any interval
I\in I_{N}
and
n\geq 2
we define

\displaystyle nLI:=\{(j-n)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\},\quad\left|nLI\right|=n2^{N};

\displaystyle nRI:=\{(j-1)2^{N}+1,\cdots,(j+n-1)2^{N}\},\quad\left|nRI\right|=n2^{N};

\displaystyle(2n-1)I:=\{(j-n)2^{N}+1,\cdots,(j+n-1)2^{N}\},\quad\left|(2n-1)I\right|=(2n-1)2^{N}.

Then the following holds:

(i)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{k\in\lambda S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}C\right)^{p}\lambda^{p}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k).

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in nLI}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{p}\sum_{k\in I}\omega(k),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in nRI}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{p}\sum_{k\in I}\omega(k),

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in(2n-1)I}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq C(2n-1)^{p}\sum_{k\in I}\omega(k).

Proof.

\rm(i)
When
p=1
, according to Definition
2.7
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|}\sum_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\leq C\inf_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\leq C\inf_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|\inf\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle=C\frac{\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\left|S_{m,N}\right|\inf\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|}{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle=C\frac{2\lambda N+1}{2N+1}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}C\lambda\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k).

When
p>1
, let
x(k):=\mathcal{X}_{S_{m,N}}(k)
. By Proposition
2.9
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|}\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\mathcal{X}_{S_{m,N}}(k)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\omega(\lambda S_{m,N})}\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\left|\mathcal{X}_{S_{m,N}}(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\left|S_{m,N}\right|}{\left|\lambda{S_{m,N}}\right|}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\frac{{2{\lambda}N+1}}{2N+1}\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\frac{3}{2}\lambda\left(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\lambda}S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}C\right)^{p}\lambda^{p}\sum_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k).

The proof of
\rm(ii)
is similar to that of
\rm(i)
, so the details are omitted. This finishes the proof of Proposition
2.10
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

If
\omega\equiv C
, then
C\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}

(p\geq 1)

Similar to the definitions of discrete
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}({\mathbb{Z}})
(
1\leq p<\infty
) weights, replacing
{\mathbb{Z}}
by
{\mathbb{N}}
, we can also give the definitions of discrete

The Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for weighted sequences

Swarup and Alphonse
[
22

discussed the discrete Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for

-sequences
(1\leq p<\infty)
. Garcia-Cuerva and Rubio de Francia
[
9
]
discussed the continuous Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for weighted

-functions. Combining the methods of
[

]

[

]

-sequences, i.e., the following Theorem

, which will be used to obtain the boundedness of discrete weighted Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Lebesgue spaces in Sect.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
and
x\in l^{1}_{\omega}
. For every
t>0
, there exist a constant
C
only depending on
p
and a sequence of disjoint intervals
\mathcal{I}_{t}=\mathcal{I}_{t}(x,\omega)
, consisting of those maximal dyadic over which the average of
|x|
relative to
\omega
is greater than
t
, such that

(i)

t<\frac{1}{\omega(I)}\sum\limits_{k\in I}\left|x(k)\right|\omega(k)\leq Ct
,
\forall

I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
;

(ii)

|x(n)|\leq t
,
\forall

n\notin\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}I
;

(iii)

If
t_{1}>t_{2}
, then every dyadic interval in
\mathcal{I}_{t_{1}}
is a subinterval of some dyadic interval in
\mathcal{I}_{t_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let

I_{N,j}=\{(j-1)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\},\quad N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

The set of all intervals which are of the form
I_{N,j}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are called
dyadic intervals. We denote by
\mathcal{I}_{t}=\mathcal{I}_{t}(x,\omega)
the collection formed by the maximal dyadic intervals
I
satisfying the condition

t<\frac{1}{\omega(I)}\sum\limits_{k\in I}|x(k)|\omega(k).

(3.1)

Every dyadic interval satisfying (
3.1
) is contained in some member of
\mathcal{I}_{t}
. Pick a maximal dyadic interval
I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
. Then there exist some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and some
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
I=I_{N,j}
. Furthermore, there exists dyadic interval
I_{N+1,m}\supset I_{N,j}
such that

\frac{1}{\omega(I_{{N+1},m})}\sum\limits_{I_{{N+1},m}}|x(k)|\omega(k)\leq t.

By simple geometric observation, we have
I_{{N+1},m}=2LI_{N,j}
or
I_{{N+1},m}=2RI_{N,j}
, where

\displaystyle 2LI_{N,j}:=\{(j-2)2^{N}+1,\cdots,j2^{N}\},

\displaystyle 2RI_{N,j}:=\{(j-1)2^{N}+1,\cdots,(j+1)2^{N}\}.

From Proposition
2.10
, it follows that
\omega(I_{{N+1},m})=\omega(2LI_{N,j})\leq C\omega(I_{N,j})
or
\omega(I_{{N+1},m})=\omega(2RI_{N,j})\leq C\omega(I_{N,j})
. Therefore

\frac{1}{\omega(I_{N,j})}\sum\limits_{k\in I_{N,j}}|x(k)|\omega(k)\leq\frac{C}{\omega(I_{{N+1},m})}\sum\limits_{k\in I_{N+1,m}}|x(k)|\omega(k)\leq Ct.

Thus, for every
I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
, we obtain

t<\frac{1}{\omega(I)}\sum\limits_{k\in I}|x(k)|\omega(k)\leq Ct.

This proves
\rm(i)
.

\rm(ii)
Now all elements not included in
\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}I
form a set
S
such that for every
n\in S
, we have
|x(n)|\leq t
. Otherwise, if
|x(n)|>t
for some
n\in S
, then we have

|x(n)|=\frac{1}{\omega(\{n\})}\sum\limits_{k\in\{n\}}|x(k)|\omega(k)>t,

thus
\{n\}\subset\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}I
, which contradict with
n\in S
. This proves
\rm(ii)
.

\rm(iii)
If
t_{1}>t_{2}>0
, then for any maximal dyadic interval
I=I^{t_{1}}_{N,j}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{1}}
, it holds true that

t_{2}<t_{1}<\frac{1}{\omega(I^{t_{1}}_{N,j})}\sum\limits_{k\in I^{t_{1}}_{N,j}}|x(k)|\omega(k),

which implies that
I^{t^{1}}_{N,j}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{2}}
or
I^{t_{1}}_{N,j}
is a subinterval of some maximal dyadic interval in
\mathcal{I}_{t_{2}}
.
∎

\displaystyle\leq

which implies (

) holds true.

Furthermore, by (

) and Proposition

(ii), we have

(4.2)

\rm(ii)

4.2
Estimate for discrete Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators on discrete weighted Morrey spaces

The
(l^{p}_{q},l^{\infty})

[

, Lemma 3.1]
. The following Theorem

shows the
(l^{p}_{\infty},l^{\infty})
-boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
. If
x\in l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}
, then
\mathcal{M}x\in l{{}^{\infty}}
and there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\|\mathcal{M}x\|_{l^{\infty}}\leq C\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}
and
m^{*}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then by Hölder’s inequality and the definition of discrete weight
{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}x(m^{*})=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{2N+1}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m^{*},N}}\left|x(k)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{2N+1}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|\omega(k)^{\frac{1}{p}}\omega(k)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{1}{2N+1}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}},N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\left|x(k)\right|^{p}\omega(k)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\omega(k)\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|x\|_{l{{}^{p}_{\infty}}_{(\omega)}}.

We finish the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

\displaystyle\omega(S_{m,N})^{\frac{p}{q}-1}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\mathcal{M}x_{2}(k)^{p}\omega(k)\sim\frac{1}{(|m|^{\beta}(2N+1))^{1-\frac{p}{q}}}\sum\limits_{k\in S_{m,N}}\mathcal{M}x_{2}(k)^{p}|k|^{\beta}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(|m|^{\beta}(2N+1)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}-1}|m|^{\beta}(2N+1)(2N^{\prime}+1)^{-\frac{p}{q}(1+\beta)}\|x\|^{p}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{|m|}{2N^{\prime}+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}\beta}\left(\frac{2N+1}{2N^{\prime}+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\|x\|^{p}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{|m|}{2N^{\prime}+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}\beta}\left(\frac{3|m|}{2N^{\prime}+1}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\|x\|^{p}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|x\|^{p}_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}.

Taking the supremum in (
4.10
) for all symmetric interval of type
\rm{II}
, then we have

\|\mathcal{M}x_{2}\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}\leq C\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}.

(4.11)

If
S^{\prime}
is a symmetric interval of type
\rm{III}
, i.e.,
|m^{\prime}|\geq 1
and
N^{\prime}>\lfloor{\frac{|m^{\prime}|}{2}}\rfloor
, then we have
S_{m^{\prime},N^{\prime}}\subset S_{0,|m^{\prime}|+N^{\prime}}\subset S_{0,3N^{\prime}}
and

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}x_{2}(k)<\frac{1}{|S_{m^{\prime},N^{\prime}}|}\sum\limits_{j\in S_{m^{\prime},N^{\prime}}}x(j)\leq\frac{|S_{0,3N^{\prime}}|}{|S_{m^{\prime},N^{\prime}}|}\frac{1}{|S_{0,3N^{\prime}}|}\sum\limits_{j\in S_{0,3N^{\prime}}}x(j)\leq\frac{C}{|S_{0,3N^{\prime}}|}\sum\limits_{j\in S_{0,3N^{\prime}}}x(j),k\in S.

Therefore, repeating the estimate of (
4.11
) with the fact that
S_{0,3N^{\prime}}
is a symmetric interval of type
\rm{I}
, we may obtain
\|\mathcal{M}x_{2}\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}\leq C\|x\|_{{l^{p}_{q}}_{(\omega)}}
and hence finish the proof of Theorem
4.11
.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

For another different weighted Morrey spaces as follows: Let
\omega\in L_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weight,
p\in(0,\infty)
and
\lambda\in[0,n)
be two real parameters. The weighted Morrey space
L^{p,\lambda}_{\omega}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the set of
f\in L^{p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p,\lambda}_{\omega}}=\sup\limits_{Q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\left(\frac{1}{l(Q)^{\lambda}}\int_{Q}|f(x)\omega(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Tanaka
[
23
]
and Nakamura-Sawano-Tanaka
[
17
]
discussed the necessary and sufficient conditions for the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, the fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and the fractional integral operator on weighted Morrey spaces
L^{p,\lambda}_{\omega}
. We will consider whether these conclusions still hold true under discrete setting in our next article.

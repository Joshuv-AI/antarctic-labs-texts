Notes on limits of accessible categories
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. Let κ \kappa be a regular cardinal, λ < κ \lambda<\kappa be a smaller infinite cardinal, and 𝖪 \mathsf{K} be a κ \kappa -accessible category where colimits of λ \lambda -indexed chains exist. We show that various category-theoretic constructions applied to 𝖪 \mathsf{K} , such as the inserter and the equifier, produce κ \kappa -accessible categories 𝖤 \mathsf{E} again, and the most obvious expected description of the full subcategory of κ \kappa -presentable objects in 𝖤 \mathsf{E} in terms of κ \kappa -presentable objects in 𝖪 \mathsf{K} holds true. In particular, if 𝖢 \mathsf{C} is a κ \kappa -small category, then the category of functors 𝖢 ⟶ 𝖪 \mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K} is κ \kappa -accessible, and its κ \kappa -presentable objects are precisely all the functors from 𝖢 \mathsf{C} to the κ \kappa -presentable objects of 𝖪 \mathsf{K} . We proceed to discuss the preservation of κ \kappa -accessibility by conical pseudolimits, lax and oplax limits, and weighted pseudolimits. The results of this paper go back to an unpublished 1977 preprint of Ulmer. Our motivation comes from the theory of flat modules and flat quasi-coherent sheaves.

\mathsf{A}
and
B
is
a
\kappa
-presentable object of
\mathsf{B}
.

It remains to apply the joint equifier construction of
Section
3
and Remark
3.3
to
the family of two pairs of parallel natural transformations

(\mathrm{id},\>\theta^{\prime}\circ\theta^{\prime\prime})
and
(\mathrm{id},\>\theta^{\prime\prime}\circ\theta^{\prime})
of
functors
\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
in order to produce the full subcategory

\mathsf{D}\subset\mathsf{E}
of all quadruples
(A,B,\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\prime})
such that

\theta^{\prime}
and
\theta^{\prime\prime}
are mutually inverse isomorphisms

\Theta_{\mathsf{A}}(A)\simeq\Theta_{\mathsf{B}}(B)
.
Then Theorem
3.1
tells that the category
\mathsf{D}

is
\kappa
-accessible and describes its full subcategory of

\kappa
-presentable objects, as desired.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Alternatively, one can consider what we would call
the
isomorpher
construction for two parallel functors between
two categories
P
,
Q\colon\mathsf{H}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{G}
.
(It appears in the literature under the name of
the “iso-inserter”
[
13
, Section 4]
,
[
5
, Section 1]
.)
The isomorpher category
\mathsf{D}
consists of all pairs
(H,\theta)
,
where
H\in\mathsf{H}
is an object and
\theta\colon P(H)\simeq Q(H)
is
an isomorphism in
\mathsf{G}
.

One can observe that the pseudopullback and the isomorpher
constructions are actually equivalent, in the sense that they can be
reduced to one another.
Given a pair of functors
\Theta_{\mathsf{A}}\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
and
\Theta_{\mathsf{B}}\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
, one can put
\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}
and
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{C}
, and
denote by
P\colon\mathsf{H}\longrightarrow\mathsf{G}
and
Q\colon\mathsf{H}\longrightarrow\mathsf{G}
the compositions

\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{A}\times\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
.
In this context, the two constructions of the category
\mathsf{D}
agree.

Conversely, given a pair of parallel functors
P
,
Q\colon\mathsf{H}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{G}
, put
\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{C}=\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{G}
.
Let the functor
\Theta_{\mathsf{A}}\colon\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
take an object
H^{\prime}\in\mathsf{H}

to the pair
(H^{\prime},P(H^{\prime}))\in\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{G}
and the functor
\Theta_{\mathsf{B}}\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}
take an object
H^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{H}
to the pair

(H^{\prime\prime},Q(H^{\prime\prime}))\in\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{G}
.
Then an isomorphism
\Theta_{\mathsf{A}}(H^{\prime})\simeq\Theta_{\mathsf{B}}(H^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathsf{C}

means a pair of isomorphisms
H^{\prime}\simeq H^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathsf{H}
and

P(H^{\prime})\simeq Q(H^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathsf{G}
.
Up to a category equivalence, the datum of two objects
H^{\prime}
,
H^{\prime\prime}\in\mathsf{H}
endowed with such two isomorphisms is the same thing as
a single object
H\in\mathsf{H}
together with an isomorphism
P(H)\simeq Q(H)

in
\mathsf{G}
.
Thus, in this context, the two constructions of the category
\mathsf{D}

agree as well.

Assume that the categories
\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{G}
are
\kappa
-accessible
with colimits of
\lambda
-indexed chains (for a regular
cardinal
\kappa
and a smaller infinite cardinal
\lambda<\kappa
).
Assume further that the functors
F
and
G
preserve

\kappa
-directed colimits and colimits of
\lambda
-indexed chains,
and that they take
\kappa
-presentable objects to

\kappa
-presentable objects.
Then it follows from Proposition
2.1

and Corollary
5.1
that the isomorpher
category
\mathsf{D}
is
\kappa
-accessible, and the
\kappa
-presentable
objects of
\mathsf{D}
are precisely all the pairs
(H,\theta)
with

H\in\mathsf{H}_{<\kappa}
.

6.
Diagram Categories

In this section, we discuss two constructions: the category of
functors
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
and the category of
k
-linear functors

\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
.
The former one is of interest to the general category theory, while
the latter one is relevant for additive category theory, module
theory, complexes in additive categories, etc.

Let us start with the nonadditive case.
Given a small category
\mathsf{C}
and a category
\mathsf{K}
, we denote by

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
the category of functors
\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
.

Recall that a category
\mathsf{K}
is called
locally

\kappa
-presentable

[
1
, Definitions 1.9 and 1.17]

if
\mathsf{K}
is
\kappa
-accessible and all colimits exist in
\mathsf{K}
.
The following theorem is a generalization of
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]

from the case of locally
\kappa
-presentable categories to the case
of
\kappa
-accessible categories with colimits of
\lambda
-indexed
chains (for some fixed infinite cardinal
\lambda<\kappa
).
It is also a correct version of
[
18
, Lemma 5.1]
(which was
shown to be erroneous in full generality in
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
).

A category
\mathsf{C}
is said to be
\kappa
-small
if
the cardinality of the set of all objects and morphisms in
\mathsf{C}

is smaller than
\kappa
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal and
\lambda<\kappa
be a smaller
infinite cardinal.
Let
\mathsf{C}
be a
\kappa
-small category.
Let
\mathsf{K}
be a
\kappa
-accessible category in which all

\lambda
-indexed chains (of objects and morphisms) have colimits.
Then the category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
is
\kappa
-accessible.
The full subcategory
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})
is precisely
the full subcategory of all
\kappa
-presentable objects in

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof Corollary
5.1
,
the point is that the diagram category can be constructed as
a combination of products, inserters, and equifiers.
Let
\mathsf{K}^{\prime}=\prod_{c\in\mathsf{C}}\mathsf{K}
be the Cartesian product of copies of
the category
\mathsf{K}
indexed by the objects of the category
\mathsf{C}
,
and let
\mathsf{L}^{\prime}=\prod_{(c\to d)\in\mathsf{C}}\mathsf{K}
be the similar product of
copies of
\mathsf{K}
indexed by the morphisms of the category
\mathsf{C}
.
Proposition
2.1
tells that the categories

\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
are
\kappa
-accessible, and describes their full
subcategories of
\kappa
-presentable objects.

Define a pair of parallel functors
F
,
G\colon\mathsf{K}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
as
follows.
The functor
F
assigns to a collection of objects

(K_{c}\in\mathsf{K})_{c\in\mathsf{C}}\in\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
the collection of objects

(L_{c\to d})_{(c\to d)\in\mathsf{C}}\in\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
given by the rules

L_{c\to d}=K_{c}
for any morphism
c\longrightarrow d
in
\mathsf{C}
.
Similarly, the functor
G
assigns to a collection of objects

(K_{c}\in\mathsf{K})_{c\in\mathsf{C}}\in\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
the collection of objects

(L_{c\to d})_{(c\to d)\in\mathsf{C}}\in\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
given by the rules

L_{c\to d}=K_{d}
for any morphism
c\longrightarrow d
in
\mathsf{C}
.

Then the related inserter category
\mathsf{E}
from
Section
4
(cf. Remark
4.6
)
is the category of all “nonmultiplicative functors”

\mathsf{C}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
.
An object
E\in\mathsf{E}
is a rule assigning to every object
c\in\mathsf{C}

an object
E_{c}\in\mathsf{K}
and to every morphism
c\longrightarrow d
in
\mathsf{C}

a morphism
E_{c}\longrightarrow E_{d}
in
\mathsf{K}
.
The conditions of compatibility with the compositions of morphisms
and with the identity morphisms are
not
imposed.
Morphisms of “nonmultiplicative functors” (i. e., the morphisms
in
\mathsf{E}
) are similar to the usual morphisms of functors; so
the desired functor category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
is a full subcategory
in
\mathsf{E}
.

Theorem
4.1
tells that the category
\mathsf{E}
is

\kappa
-accessible, and describes its full subcategory of

\kappa
-presentable objects.
Now the desired full subcategory
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})\subset\mathsf{E}
can be
produced as a joint equifier category, as in
Section
3
and Remark
3.3
.
There are two kinds of pairs of parallel natural transformations
to be equified.

Firstly, for every composable pair of morphisms
b\longrightarrow c\longrightarrow d

in
\mathsf{C}
, we have a pair of parallel functors
F_{b\to c\to d}
,

G_{b\to c\to d}\colon\mathsf{E}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{K}
and a pair of parallel
natural transformations
\phi_{b\to c\to d}
,
\psi_{b\to c\to d}\colon F_{b\to c\to d}\rightrightarrows G_{b\to c\to d}
.
The functor
F_{b\to c\to d}
takes an object
E\in\mathsf{E}
to
the object
E_{b}\in\mathsf{K}
, and the functor
G_{b\to c\to d}
takes
an object
E\in\mathsf{E}
to the object
E_{d}\in\mathsf{K}
.
The natural transformation
\phi_{b\to c\to d}
acts by the composition
of the morphisms
E_{b}\longrightarrow E_{c}\longrightarrow E_{d}
in
\mathsf{K}
assigned to
the morphisms
b\longrightarrow c
and
c\longrightarrow d
by the datum of
the object
E
.
The natural transformation
\psi_{b\to c\to d}
acts by the morphism

E_{b}\longrightarrow E_{d}
assigned to the composition of the morphisms
b\longrightarrow c\longrightarrow d
in
\mathsf{C}
by the datum of the object
E
.

Secondly, for every object
c\in\mathsf{C}
, we have a pair of parallel
functors
F_{c}=G_{c}\colon\mathsf{E}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
and a pair of parallel natural
transformations
\phi_{c}
,
\psi_{c}\colon F_{c}\rightrightarrows G_{c}
.
The functor
F_{c}=G_{c}
takes an object
E\in\mathsf{E}
to
the object
E_{c}\in\mathsf{K}
.
The natural transformation
\phi_{c}
acts by the morphism

E_{c}\longrightarrow E_{c}
in
\mathsf{K}
assigned to the identity morphism
\mathrm{id}_{c}

in
\mathsf{C}
by the datum of the object
E
; while
\psi_{c}
is
the identity natural transformation.

The resulting joint equifier is the functor category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
.
Theorem
3.1
tells that this category is

\kappa
-accessible, and provides the desired description of its
full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
∎

Now let
k
be a commutative ring.
A
k
-linear category

\mathsf{A}
is a category enriched in

k
-modules.
This means that, for any two objects
a
and
b\in\mathsf{A}
, the set of
morphisms
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(a,b)
is a
k
-module, and the composition
maps
\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(b,c)\times\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(a,b)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathsf{A}}(a,c)
are

k
-bilinear.

Suppose given a set of objects
a
and, for every pair of objects

a
,
b
, a
generating set
of morphisms
\Gen(a,b)
.
Then one can construct the
k
-linear category
\mathsf{B}
on the given
set of objects
freely generated
by the given generating sets
of morphisms.
For every pair of objects
a
,
b
, the free
k
-module

\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,b)
has a basis consisting of all the formal compositions

g_{n}\dotsm g_{1}
,
n\geq 0
, where
g_{i}\in\Gen(c_{i},c_{i+1})
,

c_{1}=a
,
c_{n+1}=b
.

Furthermore, suppose given a
set of defining relations

\Rel(a,b)\subset\Hom_{\mathsf{B}}(a,b)
for every pair of objects
a
,
b
.
Then one can construct the two-sided ideal of morphisms
\mathsf{J}\subset\mathsf{B}
generated by all the relations, and pass to the
k
-linear
quotient category
\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
by the ideal
\mathsf{J}
.

Abusing terminology, we will say that a
k
-linear category
\mathsf{A}

is
\kappa
-presented
if it has the form
\mathsf{A}=\mathsf{B}/\mathsf{J}
as
per the construction above, where the set of objects
\{a\}
, the set
of all generators
\coprod_{a,b}\Gen(a,b)
, and the set of all
relations
\coprod_{a,b}\Rel(a,b)
all have the cardinalities
smaller than
\kappa
.
In another terminology, one could say that
\mathsf{A}
is “the path
category of a
\kappa
-small quiver with a
\kappa
-small set of
relations”.

A
k
-linear category
\mathsf{K}
is said to be
\kappa
-accessible

if it is
\kappa
-accessible as an abstract category.
Given a small
k
-linear category
\mathsf{A}
and a
k
-linear category

\mathsf{K}
, we denote by
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
the (
k
-linear) category of

k
-linear functors
\mathsf{A}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
.
The following theorem is a
k
-linear version of
Theorem
6.1
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal and
\lambda<\kappa
be a smaller
infinite cardinal.
Let
k
be a commutative ring, let
\mathsf{A}
be a
\kappa
-presented

k
-linear category, and let
\mathsf{K}
be a
\kappa
-accessible

k
-linear category in which all
\lambda
-indexed chains have
colimits.
Then the category
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
is
\kappa
-accessible.
The full subcategory
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})
is precisely
the full subcategory of all
\kappa
-presentable objects in

\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to the proof of
Theorem
6.1
, with the only difference
that one works with the generating morphisms and defining relations
in
\mathsf{A}
instead of all morphisms and all compositions in
\mathsf{C}
.
Let
\mathsf{K}^{\prime}=\prod_{a\in\mathsf{A}}\mathsf{K}
be the Cartesian product of copies
of the category
\mathsf{K}
indexed by the objects of the category
\mathsf{A}
,
and let
\mathsf{L}^{\prime}=\prod_{a,b\in\mathsf{A}}\prod_{(a\to b)\in\Gen(a,b)}\mathsf{K}

be the similar product of copies of
\mathsf{K}
indexed by the set of
generating morphisms
\coprod_{a,b}\Gen(a,b)
.
Proposition
2.1
tells that the categories

\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
are
\kappa
-accessible, and describes their full
subcategories of
\kappa
-presentable objects.

Define a pair of parallel functors
F
,
G\colon\mathsf{K}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
as
follows.
The functor
F
assigns to a collection of objects

(K_{a}\in\mathsf{K})_{a\in\mathsf{A}}\in\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
the collection of objects

(L_{a\to b})_{(a\to b)\in\Gen(a,b),\,a,b\in\mathsf{A}}\in\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
given by
the rules
L_{a\to b}=K_{a}
for any generating morphism

(a\to b)\in\Gen(a,b)
.
Similarly, the functor
G
assigns to a collection of objects

(K_{a}\in\mathsf{K})_{a\in\mathsf{A}}\in\mathsf{K}^{\prime}
the collection of objects

(L_{a\to b})_{(a\to b)\in\Gen(a,b),\,a,b\in\mathsf{A}}\in\mathsf{L}^{\prime}
given by
the rules
L_{a\to b}=K_{b}
for any generating morphism

(a\to b)\in\Gen(a,b)
.

Then the related inserter category
\mathsf{E}
from
Section
4
(cf. Remark
4.6
) is
naturally equivalent to the category
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{B},\mathsf{K})
, where
\mathsf{B}

is the “path category of the quiver without relations” constructed
in the discussion preceding the formulation of the theorem.
Theorem
4.1
tells that the category
\mathsf{E}
is

\kappa
-accessible, and defines its full subcategory of

\kappa
-presentable objects.
The category
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
we are interested in is a full
subcategory in
\mathsf{E}=\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{B},\mathsf{K})
consisting of all the “quiver
representations in
\mathsf{K}
for which the relations are satisfied”.
The full subcategory
\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})\subset\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{B},\mathsf{K})
can be
produced as a joint equifier category, as in
Section
3
and Remark
3.3
.

The pairs of parallel natural transformations to be equified are
indexed by elements of the set of defining relations

\coprod_{a,b}\Rel(a,b)
.
Given a defining relation

, we have a pair of
parallel functors
F_{r}
,
G_{r}\colon\mathsf{E}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{K}
and a pair of
natural transformations
\phi_{r}
,
\psi_{r}\colon F_{r}\rightrightarrows G_{r}
.
The functor
F_{r}\colon\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{B},\mathsf{K})\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
takes a functor

E\colon\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{K}
to the object
E(a)\in\mathsf{K}
, and the functor

G_{r}
takes the functor
E
to the object
E(b)\in\mathsf{K}
.
The natural transformation
\phi_{r}
acts by the morphism

E(r)\colon E(a)\longrightarrow E(b)
.
The natural transformation
\psi_{r}
acts by the zero morphism

0\colon E(a)\longrightarrow E(b)
in the
k
-linear category
\mathsf{K}
.

The resulting joint equifier is the category of
k
-linear functors

\mathsf{Fun}_{k}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{K})
.
Theorem
3.1
tells that this category is

\kappa
-accessible, and provides the desired description of its
full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects.
∎

7.
Brief Preliminaries on
2
-Categories

The aim of this section is to provide a very brief and mostly
terminological preliminary discussion for the purposes of the next
two Sections
8
–
9
.
The reader can find the details by following the references.

Throughout the three sections, for the most part we adopt the policy
of benign neglect with respect to set-theoretical issues of size
(i. e., the distinction between sets and classes).
When specific restrictions on the size matter, we mention them.

In the terminology of higher category theory, the prefix “
2
-”
means strict concepts, while the prefix “bi-” refers to relaxed ones.
So
2
-categories are strict, while bicategories are
relaxed
[
3
]
.

A
2
-category
is a category enriched in the category of
categories
\mathsf{Cat}
(with the monoidal structure on
\mathsf{Cat}
given by
the Cartesian product)
[
14
]
.
In particular, there is the important
2
-category of
categories

\mathbf{Cat}
: categories are the objects, functors are
the
1
-cells, natural transformations are the
2
-cells.

In the terminology of the bicategory theory, one speaks of

morphisms of bicategories
(which are multiplicative and unital
on
1
-cells up to coherent families of
2
-cells) or

homomorphisms of bicategories
(which are multiplicative and
unital on
1
-cells up to coherent families of
invertible

2
-cells)
[
3
, Section 4]
.
Even when one is only interested in
2
-categories, the notion of
a
2
-functor may be too strict, and one may want to relax it by
considering morphisms of
2
-categories (known as
lax functors
),
or homomorphisms of
2
-categories (known as
pseudofunctors
).

Let
\Gamma
and
\Delta
be two
2
-categories.
Then
2
-functors
\Gamma\longrightarrow\Delta
form a
2
-category

[\Gamma,\Delta]
.
The objects of
[\Gamma,\Delta]
are the
2
-functors
\Gamma\longrightarrow\Delta
, the
1
-cells of
[\Gamma,\Delta]
are the
2
-natural
transformations, and the
2
-cells of
[\Gamma,\Delta]
are called

modifications

[
14
, Section 1.4]
.
A
2
-functor
\Gamma\longrightarrow\Delta
is a rule assigning to every
object of
\Gamma
an object of
\Delta
, to every
1
-cell of

\Gamma
a
1
-cell of
\Delta
, and to every
2
-cell of
\Gamma

a
2
-cell of
\Delta
.
A
2
-natural transformation is a rule assiging to every object
of
\Gamma
a
1
-cell in
\Delta
.
A modification is a rule assigning to every object of
\Gamma

a
2
-cell in
\Delta
.

2
-categories and
2
-functors form the
3
-category of

2
-categories
:
2
-categories are the objects,
2
-functors are
the
1
-cells,
2
-natural transformations are the
2
-cells,
and modifications are the
3
-cells.

Even when one is only interested in
2
-functors rather than
the more relaxed concepts of lax functors or pseudofunctors, the notion
of a
2
-natural transformation may be too strict, and one may want
to relax it.
Then one can consider
lax natural transformations
(compatible
with the action of the
2
-functors on
1
-cells in
\Gamma
up
to a coherent family of
2
-cells in
\Delta
) or
pseudonatural
transformations
(compatible with the action of the
2
-functors on
the
1
-cells in
\Gamma
up to a coherent family of
invertible

2
-cells in
\Delta
).
In the terminology of
[
19
, §4.1]
, lax natural
transformations are called “transformations”, pseudonatural
transformations are called “strong transformations”, and

2
-natural transformations are called “strict transformations”.
The
2
-category of
2
-functors
\Gamma\longrightarrow\Delta
,
pseudonatural transformations, and modifications is denoted by

\mathsf{Psd}[\Gamma,\Delta]
in
[
4
]
,
[
13
, Section 5]
,

[
5
, Section 2]
.

In connection with the “lax” notions, the choice of the direction
of the (possibly noninvertible)
2
-cells providing the relaxed
compatibility becomes important.
When the direction is reversed, the correspoding notions are called
“oplax”.
For “pseudo” notions, the compatibility
2
-cells are assumed to be
invertible, and so the choice of the direction in which they act
no longer matters.

8.
Conical Pseudolimits, Lax Limits, and Oplax Limits

We denote by
\mathbf{Cat}
the
2
-category of small categories and
by
\mathbf{CAT}
the
2
-category of locally small categories (i. e.,
large categories in which morphisms between any fixed pair of objects
form a set).
So the categories of morphisms in
\mathbf{CAT}
need not be even locally
small; this will present no problem for our constructions.

Let
\Gamma
be a small
2
-category and

H\colon\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbf{CAT}
be a
2
-functor.
The (
conical
)
lax limit
of
H
is a category
\mathsf{L}

whose objects are the following sets of data:

(i)

for every object
\gamma\in\Gamma
, an object
L_{\gamma}\in H(\gamma)
of the category

10.1.
Modules and flat modules

Let

be an associative ring.
We denote by

the abelian category of left

-modules

the full subcategory of flat

-modules.

The following two propositions are fairly standard.

Proposition 10.1
.

For any ring
R

\kappa

of
R
-modules

is locally
\kappa
-presentable.
The
\kappa

are precisely all
the left
R
-modules that can be constructed as the cokernel of
a morphism of free left

-modules with less than
\kappa

Proposition 10.2
.

For any ring
R
and any regular cardinal
\kappa
, the category
of flat
R
-modules

is
\kappa
-accessible.
All directed colimits exist in

the ones in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
The
\kappa
-presentable objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
are precisely
all those flat left
R

\kappa
-presentable
as objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

Proof.

The connection between the present proposition and the previous one
fits into the setting described in

.
The assertions for

are corollaries of the classical

,
16
]
characterizing the flat

R
-modules as the directed colimits of finitely generated projective
(or free)
R
-modules.
The general case of an arbitrary regular cardinal

deduced by applying
[

, Theorem 2.11 and Example 2.13(1)]
.
∎

For a version of Proposition

for

[

, Corollary 5.2]
.

10.3.
Categories of epimorphisms

For any category
\mathsf{K}
, we denote by
\mathsf{K}^{\to}
the category of
morphisms in
\mathsf{K}
(with commutative squares in
\mathsf{K}

as morphisms in
\mathsf{K}^{\to}
).
The following lemma is not difficult.

Lemma 10.6
.

For any regular cardinal
\kappa
and
\kappa
-accessible
category
\mathsf{K}
, the category of morphisms
\mathsf{K}^{\to}
is

\kappa
-accessible.
The full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{K}^{\to}

is the category
(\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})^{\to}
of morphisms of

\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{K}
.

Proof.

One has
\mathsf{K}^{\to}=\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{K})
for the obvious finite category
\mathsf{C}

with no nonidentity endomorphisms; so the result
of
[
2
, Exposé I, Proposition 8.8.5]
,
[
20
, page 55]
,
or
[
12
, Theorem 1.3]
is applicable.
∎

For any category
\mathsf{K}
, let us denote by
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}\subset\mathsf{K}^{\to}

the full subcategory whose objects are all the epimorphisms in
\mathsf{K}
.

Lemma 10.7
.

For any regular cardinal
\kappa
and any locally
\kappa
-presentable
abelian category
\mathsf{K}
, the category of epimorphisms
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}
is
locally
\kappa
-presentable.

\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}

(\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})^{\mathsf{epi}}
of epimorphisms between

\kappa
-presentable objects in
\mathsf{K}

Proof.

Notice first of all that a morphism in
\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa}
is
an epimorphism in
\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa}
if and only if it is an epimorphism
in
\mathsf{K}
(because the full subcategory
\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa}
is closed
under cokernels in
\mathsf{K}

[
1
, Proposition 1.16]
).
Furthermore, the full subcategory
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}
is closed under colimits
in the locally presentable abelian category
\mathsf{K}^{\to}
; so all colimits
exist in
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}
.
In view of Lemma
10.6
and according to
Proposition
1.2
, in order to prove the lemma
it suffices to check that any morphism from an object of

(\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})^{\to}
to an object of
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}
factorizes through
an object of
(\mathsf{K}_{<\kappa})^{\mathsf{epi}}
in
\mathsf{K}^{\to}
.

Indeed, consider a commutative square diagram in
\mathsf{K}

Remark 10.8
.

It follows immediately from the first assertion of

that the category

\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{mono}}
of monomorphisms in

is also locally

\kappa

In fact, the categories
\mathsf{K}^{\mathsf{epi}}

In particular, if

.

Given a ring
R
and a full subcategory
\mathsf{L}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, we
denote by
\mathsf{L}^{\mathsf{surj}}\subset\mathsf{L}^{\to}
the full subcategory whose

\mathsf{L}
.

Lemma 10.9
.

For any associative ring
R
and any regular cardinal
\kappa
,
the category of surjective morphisms of flat
R
-modules

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}
is
\kappa
-accessible.

\kappa
-presentable objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}
are
the surjective morphisms of
\kappa
-presentable flat
R
-modules.

Proof.

The argument is similar to the proof of
Lemma
10.7
.
In view of Proposition
10.2
,
Lemma
10.6
is applicable to
\mathsf{K}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
;
so the category of morphisms of flat
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\to}

is
\kappa
-accessible and the category of morphisms of

\kappa
-presentable flat
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl},<\kappa}^{\to}

is the full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\to}
.
According to Proposition
1.2
, in order
to prove the lemma it suffices to check that any morphism from

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl},<\kappa}^{\to}
to an object of

R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}
factorizes through an object of

(R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl},<\kappa})^{\mathsf{surj}}

Following the proof of Lemma

,
one needs to observe that if

-module.
Indeed, the kernel

of the morphism

is a flat

-module, too.
The images
V_{\xi}
of the morphisms

in the ambient abelian category
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

\mathsf{K}
.
∎

.

Alternatively, one can drop the assumption that the category

.

10.4.
Short exact sequences of flat modules

Now we can deduce the following three corollaries of

.

Corollary 10.11
.

Let
R
be an associative ring and
\kappa
be a regular cardinal.
Then any surjective morphism of
\kappa
-presentable flat

-modules is a direct summand of a
\kappa
-small directed colimit
of surjective morphisms of finitely generated projective

-modules
(in the category

).

This follows from Lemma

in view
of
[

, proof of Theorem 2.11 (iv)
\Rightarrow
(i)]
for

\mathsf{K}=R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}
,
\lambda=\aleph_{0}
, and

.
The Govorov–Lazard characterization of flat modules
[

,
16
]

implies that all finitely presentable flat

-modules are projective.
By Lemma
10.9
, the category of surjective
morphisms of flat
R
-modules is finitely accessible, and
its finitely presentable objects are the surjective morphisms of
finitely generated projective
R
-modules.
So all surjective morphisms of flat
R
-modules are directed
colimits of surjective morphisms of finitely generated projective

R
-modules.

Let
\mathsf{A}
denote the set of all
\kappa
-small directed colimits
of surjective morphisms of finitely generated projective

R
-modules.
Following the argument
in
[
1
, proof of Theorem 2.11 (iv)
\Rightarrow
(i)]

and
[

, Example 2.13(1)]
, all the objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}

are
\kappa
-directed colimits of objects from
\mathsf{A}
.
Thus all the
\kappa
-presentable objects of
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{surj}}

are direct summands of objects from
\mathsf{A}
.
∎

The next corollary is a generalization of
[

, Lemma 4.1]
.

.

Let
R
be an associative ring and
\kappa
be a regular cardinal.
Then the kernel of any surjective morphism of
\kappa
-presentable

-modules is a
\kappa
-presentable flat
R
-module.

Proof.

Follows from Corollary
10.11
,
as the kernel of any surjective morphism of finitely generated

-modules is a finitely generated projective
R
-module.
For another proof, see
[

, Corollary 4.7]
.
∎

R
and a full subcategory
\mathsf{L}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, let us

the category of all short exact sequences
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
with the terms belonging to
\mathsf{L}
.

.

For any associative ring
R
and any regular cardinal
\kappa
,

is
\kappa
-accessible.
The full subcategory of
\kappa
-presentable objects of

By Corollary

, the obvious equivalence

10.5.
Pure acyclic complexes of flat modules

Finally, we can present our “toy application” of
Corollary
5.1
.
An acyclic complex of flat
R
-modules is said to be

pure acyclic
if its modules of cocycles are flat.

The following corollary is essentially a weaker version of
the result of
[
9
, Theorem 2.4 (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)]

or
[
21
, Theorem 8.6 (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)]
.
Our argument produces it as an application of general
category-theoretic principles.
See
[
27
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
23
, Corollaries 4.5 and 11.4]

for a quasi-coherent sheaf, a comodule, and a contramodule version.

Corollary 10.14
.

Let
R
be an associative ring and
\kappa
be an uncountable
regular cardinal.
Then any pure acyclic complex of flat
R
-modules is
a
\kappa
-directed colimit of pure acyclic complexes of

\kappa
-presentable flat
R
-modules.

Proof.

The point is that a pure acyclic complex of flat
R
-modules
F^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is the same thing as a collection of short exact sequences of flat

R
-modules
0\longrightarrow G^{n}\longrightarrow F^{n}\longrightarrow H^{n}\longrightarrow 0
together
with a collection of isomorphisms
H^{n}\simeq G^{n+1}
,

.
This means that the category of pure acyclic complexes of flat

R
-modules can be constructed from the category of short exact
sequences of flat
R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{ses}}
using Cartesian
products (as in Section
2
) and the isomorpher
construction from Remark
5.2
.

Specifically, put
\mathsf{H}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}^{\mathsf{ses}}
and

\mathsf{G}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{fl}}
.
Let
P\colon\mathsf{H}\longrightarrow\mathsf{G}
be the functor taking a collection of
short exact sequences
(0\to G^{n}\to F^{n}\to H^{n}\to 0)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
to
the collection of modules
(H^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and let

Q\colon\mathsf{H}\longrightarrow\mathsf{G}
be the functor taking the same collection of short
exact sequences to the collection of modules
(G^{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then the resulting isomorpher category
\mathsf{D}
is the category of
pure acyclic complexes of flat
R
-modules.
Given the results of Proposition
10.2

and Corollary
10.13
, it follows from
Proposition
2.1
and Remark
5.2

that the category
\mathsf{D}
is
\kappa
-accessible and the pure acyclic

Superfluid density, Josephson relation and pairing fluctuations in a multi-component fermion superfluid
Yi-Cai Zhang

Abstract
In this work, a Josephson relation is generalized to a multi-component fermion superfluid. Superfluid density is expressed through a two-particle Green function for pairing states. When the system has only one gapless collective excitation mode, the Josephson relation is simplified, which is given in terms of the order parameters and the trace of two-particle normal Green function. In addition, it is found that the two-particle Green function is directly related to the pairing fluctuations of order parameters. Further more, in the presence of inversion symmetry, the superfluid density is given in terms of the pairing fluctuation matrix. The results of the superfluid density in Haldane model show that the generalized Josephson relation can be also applied to a multi-band fermion superfluid in lattice.

The superfluid density
\rho_{s}

\displaystyle\rho_{s}=-{\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{n_{0}m}{q^{2}G(\textbf{q},0)},

The above equation indicates that the Green function diverges as wave vector

Such a divergence of

The above Josephson relation in superfluid system can be viewed as a manifestation of Bogoliubov’s “

It also has close connections with the absence of long ranged order (e.g., condensation) at finite temperature in one and two dimensions

Its possible generalization in two-component fermion

Using auxiliary-field approach, Dawson, et al. also derived a Josephson relation which is suitable for both bosonic and fermion superfluid

\displaystyle\rho_{s}=-{\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{4\Delta^{2}m}{q^{2}G_{II}(\textbf{q},0)},

(2)

\Delta=\langle\psi_{\downarrow}(\textbf{r})\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})\rangle

is two-particle Green function for pairing states,
\Delta_{\textbf{q}}=\sum_{k}\psi_{\downarrow\textbf{q+k}}\psi_{\uparrow\textbf{-k}}
is fluctuation operator of order parameter and
\psi_{\sigma\textbf{k}}
is field operator in momentum space.
\omega_{n0}=E_{n}-E_{0}
,
E_{n}

are system eigenenergy and eigenstates, respectively.
It is found that the superfluid density is determined by superfluid order parameter and the behaviors of two-particle Green function at long wave length limit. In comparison with bosonic superfluid, the above formula shows that the two-particle Green function replace the corresponding single-particle Green function of bosonic superfluid. In addition,

Inspired by above studies, in this paper we investigate the superfluid density (or superfluid weight) for multi-component fermion superfluid. A generalized formula of Josephson relation for multi-component bosons has been given in Ref.
zhangyicai2018

In this paper, we would give a generalized Josephson relation for a multi-component fermion superfluid or superconductor system. It is found that we can take a similar method as done in bosonic system to get the general results for fermions.
In specific, the superfluid density can be expressed in terms of two-particle Green functions. Further more, when there is only one gapless collective mode, the superfluid density is determined by the superfluid order parameter and the trace of two-particle Green function. In addition, in the presence of inverse symmetry, the two-particle Green function is directly related to fluctuation matrix of order parameters. Further more, it is found that the generalized Josephson relation can be also applied to multi-band lattice system.

\displaystyle\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}\textbf{r}[e^{i(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{{r}}-\omega t)+\epsilon t}\xi\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\downarrow}(\textbf{r})\!\!+\!\!e^{-i(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}-\omega t)+\epsilon t}\xi^{*}\psi_{\downarrow}(\textbf{r})\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})],

where

We assume initially the system is in the ground state
|0\rangle
, and then slowly turn on perturbation
H^{\prime}
, the wave function can be written as

\displaystyle\psi(t)=\sum_{n}a_{n}(t)e^{-iE_{n}t}\psi_{n},

where

Using perturbation theory, we get

\displaystyle\psi(t)\simeq\psi_{0}e^{-iE_{0}t}+\sum_{n\neq 0}a_{n}(t)e^{-iE_{n}t}\psi_{n},

\displaystyle a_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{i}\int_{-\infty}^{t}d\tau H^{\prime}_{n0}(\tau)e^{i\omega_{n0}\tau}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\![\frac{\xi\langle n|\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}|0\rangle e^{-i(\omega+i\eta-\omega_{n0})t}}{\omega+i\epsilon-\omega_{n0}}-\frac{\xi^{*}\langle n|\Delta_{\textbf{q}}|0\rangle e^{i(\omega-i\eta+\omega_{n0})t}}{\omega-i\epsilon+\omega_{n0}}],

The changes of order parameter

\displaystyle=\!\!\xi e^{-i(\omega+i\epsilon)t}[\frac{\langle 0|\psi_{\downarrow}\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})|n\rangle\langle n|\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}|0\rangle}{\omega+i\epsilon-\omega_{n0}}\!\!-\!\!\frac{\langle 0|\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}|n\rangle\langle n|\psi_{\downarrow}\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})|0\rangle}{\omega+i\epsilon+\omega_{n0}}]

\displaystyle\!\!+\!\!\xi^{*}e^{i(\omega-i\epsilon)t}[\frac{\langle 0|\Delta_{\textbf{q}}|n\rangle\langle n|\psi_{\downarrow}\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})|0\rangle}{\omega-i\epsilon-\omega_{n0}}\!\!-\!\!\frac{\langle 0|\psi_{\downarrow}\psi_{\uparrow}(\textbf{r})|n\rangle\langle n|\Delta_{\textbf{q}}|0\rangle}{\omega-i\epsilon+\omega_{n0}}],

where density fluctuation operator

III
General Josephson relation for multi-component fermions

For a multi-component (or multi-band) fermions, the superfluid order parameter
\Delta_{\alpha\beta}
can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\psi_{\alpha}(\textbf{r})\psi_{\beta}(\textbf{r})\rangle,

(25)

where
\psi_{\alpha(\beta)}
is the field operator for
\alpha(\beta)
-th component.
The above equation indicates that the number of superfluid order parameter can be an arbitrary integer
m\geq 1
(
m\in Z
) in a multi-component fermion superfluid. In such a case, we need a general perturbation Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H^{\prime}=\int d^{3}r\{e^{i(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}-\omega t+\epsilon t)}\Delta^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{r}).\xi+e^{-i(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{ r}-\omega t+\epsilon t)}\xi^{{\dagger}}.\Delta(\textbf{r})\},

\displaystyle=\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}.\xi e^{-i\omega t+\epsilon t}+\xi^{{\dagger}}.\Delta_{\textbf{q}}e^{i\omega t+\epsilon t},

where we relabel the order parameter with

and introduce column vectors

Similarly, using perturbation theory, we can get the variation of order parameter

where

\displaystyle G_{II\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\textbf{q},\omega+i\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}[\frac{\langle 0|\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\textbf{q}}|n\rangle\langle n|\Delta_{\sigma,-\textbf{q}}|0\rangle}{\omega-i\epsilon-\omega_{n0}}-\frac{\langle 0|\Delta_{\sigma,-\textbf{q}}|n\rangle\langle n|\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime},\textbf{q}}|0\rangle}{\omega-i\epsilon+\omega_{n0}}],

When
\omega\pm i\epsilon=0
,

\displaystyle\delta\langle\Delta(\textbf{r})\rangle=e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}}G_{II}(\textbf{q},0).\xi+e^{-i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}}F_{II}^{\textbf{t}}(\textbf{q},0).\xi^{*},

\displaystyle\delta\textbf{j}(\textbf{r})=e^{i\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}}\textbf{B}(\textbf{q},0).\xi+h.c.

Similarly, using continuity equations and assuming

\displaystyle\textbf{B}(\textbf{q},0)=-\frac{2\textbf{q}}{q^{2}}\{\Delta^{*}_{1},\Delta^{*}_{2},...,\Delta^{*}_{m}\}.

Introducing

\delta\Delta(\textbf{r})=\{\delta\Delta_{1}(\textbf{r}),\delta\Delta_{2}(\textbf{r}),...,\delta\Delta_{m}(\textbf{r})\}^{\textbf{t}}

\displaystyle\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}\delta\langle\Delta(\textbf{r})\rangle\\
\delta\langle\Delta(\textbf{r})\rangle^{*}\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\!\!=\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}G_{II}(\textbf{q},0)&F_{II}^{\textbf{t}}(\textbf{q},0)\\

\end{array}\!\!\!\right)_{2m\times 2m}.\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}x\\

\end{array}\!\!\!\right)

\end{array}\!\!\!\right)=2i\delta\theta\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta\\

\end{array}\!\!\!\right)

and

We should remark that even though Eq.(

IV
Discussions

In the above derivation, we get the superfluid density in terms of two-particle Green function (the Josephson relation) Eq.(
71
). In this section, we will show that for some cases, the above equation can be further simplified. In particular, if the system has only one gapless collective mode, the superfluid density can be give by the trace of two-particle Green function.
When the inversion symmetry is present, the superfluid density can be expressed in terms of the fluctuation matrix of superfluid order parameters.

IV.1
only one gapless collective mode

When a system has unique gapless excitation near ground state, e.g., phonon, the Josephson relation can be generalized to multi-component system through a phase operator method as done in bosonic case
zhangyicai2018
.
Here we know near the ground state, the phonon’s excitation corresponds to total density oscillation. Due to the presence of superfluid order parameter, the density oscillation would couple phase oscillation of order parameter
Ambegaokar1961
. Furthermore, all the superfluid order parameters should share a common phase variation, i.e.,
\delta\theta_{\sigma}(\textbf{r})=\delta\theta(\textbf{r})
. On the other hand, near the ground state, similarly as Eq.(
5
), the fluctuation operator of superfluid order parameters may be expressed in terms of phase operator
\hat{\theta}

Lifshitz

\displaystyle\delta\hat{\Delta}_{\sigma}(\textbf{r})\simeq 2i\Delta_{\sigma}\delta\hat{\theta}(\textbf{r}),

(72)

where
\Delta_{\sigma}
is the
\sigma-th
superfluid order parameter in ground state. Under perturbation
H^{\prime}
(see eq.(
26
)), the variations of order parameters can be obtained by averaging eq.(
72
) with respect to the perturbed ground state.
Consequently, the variations for order parameters are
\delta\Delta_{\sigma}=i\Delta_{\sigma}\delta\theta(\textbf{r})
with
\delta\theta(\textbf{r})=\langle\delta\hat{\theta}(\textbf{r})\rangle
.

From the above equation, we get the fluctuation operators of order parameter in momentum space

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sigma,\textbf{q}}=2i\Delta_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}_{\textbf{q}},

\displaystyle\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma,\textbf{q}}=-2i\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}=-2i\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}_{-\textbf{q}}.

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sigma,-\textbf{q}}=2i\Delta_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}_{-\textbf{q}},

\displaystyle\Delta^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma,-\textbf{q}}=-2i\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}^{{\dagger}}_{-\textbf{q}}=-2i\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}\hat{\theta}_{\textbf{q}},

(73)

where we use
\hat{\theta}^{{\dagger}}_{\textbf{q}}=\hat{\theta}_{-\textbf{q}}
for real phase field
\theta(\textbf{r})
(
\textbf{q}\neq 0
) .
From definitions of the
G_{II}
and
F_{II}
in eq.(
28
), we get

\displaystyle G_{II\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\textbf{q},0)=-4Z\Delta_{\sigma}\Delta^{*}_{\sigma^{\prime}},

\displaystyle F_{II\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(\textbf{q},0)=4Z\Delta_{\sigma^{\prime}}\Delta_{\sigma},

(74)

where
Z\equiv\sum_{n}[\frac{|\langle 0|\hat{\theta}_{\textbf{q}}|n\rangle|^{2}}{\omega_{n0}}+\frac{|\langle 0|\hat{\theta}_{-\textbf{q}}|n\rangle|^{2}}{\omega_{n0}}]>0
is a real number. When the number of superfluid order parameters is one and
\Delta
is real, the relation
F_{II}(\textbf{q},0)=-G_{II}(\textbf{q},0)
is obtained as
q\rightarrow 0
in usual two-component fermion superfluid.

From eqs.(
III
) and (
III
), we get

\displaystyle 2i\delta\theta(\textbf{r})=-4Z\sum_{\sigma}[\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}x_{\sigma}-\Delta_{\sigma}x^{*}_{\sigma}],

\displaystyle=-4Z\sum_{\sigma}2i\alpha_{\sigma}sin(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}+\phi_{\sigma}),

\displaystyle\delta\textbf{j}(\textbf{r})=-\frac{2\textbf{q}}{q^{2}}\sum_{\sigma}[\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}x_{\sigma}+\Delta_{\sigma}x^{*}_{\sigma}],

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\textbf{q}}{q^{2}}\sum_{\sigma}[2\alpha_{\sigma}cos(\textbf{q}\cdot\textbf{r}+\phi_{\sigma})]

\displaystyle=\frac{\vec{\nabla}\delta\theta(\textbf{r})}{q^{2}Z}=\frac{\delta\textbf{v}_{s}}{q^{2}Z},

(75)

where we take
\Delta^{*}_{\sigma}\xi_{\sigma}\equiv\alpha_{\sigma}e^{i\phi_{\sigma}}
with amplitude
\alpha_{\sigma}
, phase
\phi_{\sigma}
.
So the superfluid density is

\displaystyle\rho_{s}=\frac{1}{q^{2}Z}.

(76)

On the other hand, we know

\displaystyle trG_{II}(\textbf{q},0)\equiv\sum_{\sigma}G_{\sigma\sigma}(\textbf{q},0)=-4Z\sum_{\sigma}|\Delta_{\sigma}|^{2}.

(77)

So finally we get the Josephson relation

\displaystyle\rho_{s}=-{\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{4\sum_{\sigma}|\Delta_{\sigma}|^{2}}{q^{2}trG_{II}(\textbf{q},0)},

(78)

where
G_{II}(\textbf{q},0)
is two-particle normal Green function (matrix) at zero-frequency. When the number of order parameters
n=1
, the above equation is reduced to the Josephson relation for usual two component fermion superfluid
Taylor
;
Dawson
.

IV.2
superfluid density and Pairing fluctuations matrix

In this section, we would give a connection between the two-particle Green function and the pairing fluctuation matrix based on BCS mean field theory and Gaussian fluctuation approximation
Taylor
.
Further more, if the system has spatial inversion symmetry, the coefficient matrix
\textbf{G}_{\textbf{II}}
is directly proportional to the inverse of pairing fluctuation matrix.

Fist of all, we discuss the results for usual two-component fermion superfluid.
In the following, we assume the pairing gap can be decomposed as the mean field value
\Delta
and small fluctuation
\delta\Delta(x)
(
\Delta(x)=\Delta+\delta\Delta(x)
).
Next we consider the fluctuations about the mean-field results. Expanding action
S
to second order of
\delta\Delta
,
the partition function
Engelbrecht1997
;
Hu2006
;
Diener2008

\displaystyle Z\approx e^{-S_{0}}\int D\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}D\eta_{q}e^{-\delta S},

(79)

where
S_{0}
is the mean-field contribution and Gaussian fluctuation part

\displaystyle\delta S=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{q},n}\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}M(q)\eta_{q},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\textbf{q},n}\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}M^{o}_{11}(q)&M^{o}_{12}(q)\\
M^{o}_{21}(q)&M^{o}_{22}(q)\end{array}\right)\eta_{q},

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{q},n>0}\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}M^{o}_{11}(q)&M^{o}_{12}(q)\\
M^{o}_{21}(q)&M^{o}_{22}(q)\end{array}\right)\eta_{q}

with pairing fluctuation fields
\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}=[\Delta^{*}_{q},\Delta_{-q}]
and
q=(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})
. The
fluctuation matrix
M
is
2\times 2
matrix

\displaystyle M_{11}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\textbf{k},n^{\prime}}G_{11}(k+q)G_{22}(k)+\frac{1}{g},

\displaystyle M_{12}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\textbf{k},n^{\prime}}G_{12}(k+q)G_{12}(k),

\displaystyle M_{21}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=M_{12}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n}),

\displaystyle M_{22}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=M_{11}(-\textbf{q},-i\omega_{n}),

(85)

where
G_{ij}(k)
is matrix element of Nambu-Gorkov Green function and
g
is the interaction strength between particles.
\omega_{n}=2n\pi/\beta
(
n\in Z
) is Matsubara frequency,
\beta=1/T
is inverse temperature and
k=(\textbf{k},i\omega_{n^{\prime}})
.
The collective modes are given by zeros of determinant
Det|M(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega+i0^{+})|=0
. As
q\rightarrow 0
, the collective mode is the Anderson-Bogoliubov phonon, which characterizes the density oscillations of superfluid.
With the action
\delta S
(Gaussian weight), the correlation function in imaginary time (average values of quadratic terms) can be calculated
yicai2017
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle\Delta^{*}_{q}\Delta_{q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{11},

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{-q}\Delta_{q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{12},

\displaystyle\langle\Delta^{*}_{q}\Delta^{*}_{-q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{21},

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{-q}\Delta^{*}_{-q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{22}.

(86)

On the other hand, we know that the above correlation function in imaginary time has one extra minus sign comparing with Green function.
So the two-particle Green functions can be obtained

\displaystyle-G_{II}(q)=\langle\Delta^{*}_{q}\Delta_{q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{11},

\displaystyle-F_{II}(q)=\langle\Delta_{-q}\Delta_{q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{12},

\displaystyle-F^{*}_{II}(q)=\langle\Delta^{*}_{q}\Delta^{*}_{-q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{21},

\displaystyle-G_{II}(-q)=\langle\Delta_{-q}\Delta^{*}_{-q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{22}.

(87)

In addition, if the system has inversion symmetry, i.e.,

\displaystyle G_{II}(-\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=G_{II}(\textbf{q},i\omega_{n}),

\displaystyle F_{II}(-\textbf{q},i\omega_{n})=F_{II}(\textbf{q},\omega_{n}),

(88)

further more, according to Eq.(
28
), when
\omega\pm i\epsilon=0
, the Green function satisfy

\displaystyle G^{*}_{II\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\textbf{q},0)=G_{II\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(\textbf{q},0),

\displaystyle F_{II\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\textbf{q},0)=F_{II\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(-\textbf{q},0),

(89)

then the coefficient matrix of Green function
\textbf{G}_{\textbf{II}}
can be expressed in terms of the inverse of
M

\displaystyle\textbf{G}_{\textbf{II}}=\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}G_{II}(q)&F_{II}(q)\\
F^{*}_{II}(q)&G^{*}_{II}(q)\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right),

\displaystyle=\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}G_{II}(q)&F_{II}(q)\\
F^{*}_{II}(q)&G_{II}(-q)\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right)=-M^{-1}.

So the superfluid density is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{s}

\displaystyle={\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{-4}{q^{2}}(\Delta^{t*},\Delta^{t}).\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&0\\
0&-I\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right).\textbf{G}_{\textbf{II}}^{-1}.\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&0\\
0&-I\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\!\!.\!\!\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta\\
\Delta^{*}\\
\end{array}\!\!\right),

\displaystyle={\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{4}{q^{2}}(\Delta^{t*},\Delta^{t}).\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&0\\
0&-I\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right).M(q).\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&0\\
0&-I\\
\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\!\!.\!\!\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta\\
\Delta^{*}\\
\end{array}\!\!\right).

Next, for the case of several superfluid order parameters, the proof is similar.
Assuming the number of superfluid order parameters are arbitrary
m
, then Gaussian fluctuation part

\displaystyle\delta S=\sum_{\textbf{q},n>0}\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}M(q)\eta_{q},

(111)

where
M(q)
is a
2m\times 2m
fluctuation matrix, and pairing fluctuation fields
\eta^{{\dagger}}_{q}=[\Delta^{*}_{1q},\Delta^{*}_{2q},...,\Delta^{*}_{mq},\Delta_{1,-q},\Delta_{2,-q},...,\Delta_{m,-q}]

With the action
\delta S
(Gaussian weight), the correlation functions (and the matrix elements of Green function) in imaginary time can be calculated, i.e.,

\displaystyle-G_{II,ij}(q)=\langle\Delta^{*}_{jq}\Delta_{iq}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{i,j},

\displaystyle-F_{II,ij}(q)=\langle\Delta_{j,-q}\Delta_{iq}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{i,m+j},

\displaystyle-F^{*}_{II,ji}(q)=\langle\Delta^{*}_{jq}\Delta^{*}_{i,-q}\rangle=(M^{-1})_{m+i,m+j},

Further more, in the presence of inverse symmetry,
the Green function coefficient matrix can be obtained through the fluctuation matrix, i.e.,

So the superfluid density is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{s}\!\!=\!\!{\rm lim_{q\rightarrow 0}}\frac{4}{q^{2}}(\Delta^{t*},\Delta^{t}).\!\!\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&0\\

VI
summary

In conclusion, we investigate the Josephson relation for a general multi-component fermion superfluid.
It is found that the superfluid density is given in terms of two-particle Green functions. When the superfluid has only one gapless collective excitation, the Josephson relation can be simplified, which is given in terms superfluid order parameters and trace of Green function.
Within BCS mean field theory and Gaussian fluctuation approximation, the matrix elements of Green function can be given in terms of pairing fluctuation matrix elements.
Further more, in the presence of inversion symmetry, it is shown that the two-particle Green function is directly proportional the inverse of pairing fluctuation matrix. The formulas for superfluid density is quite general, which can be also applied to multi-band superfluid with complex spectra in lattice.

Josephson relation for multi-component fermion superfluid provides a general method for calculations on superfluid densities in terms of two-particle Green functions and fluctuation matrix. Our work would be useful for investigations on the superfluid properties of multi-component (or multi-band) superfluid system with complex pairing structures.

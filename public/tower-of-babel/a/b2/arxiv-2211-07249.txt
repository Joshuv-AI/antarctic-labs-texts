Abstract




A hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method for nonlocal hyperbolic partial differential equations

Abstract
: In this paper, we propose a hybrid collocation method based on finite difference and Haar wavelets to solve nonlocal hyperbolic partial differential equations. Developing an efficient and accurate numerical method to solve such problem is a difficult task due to the presence of nonlocal boundary condition. The speciality of the proposed method is to handle integral boundary condition efficiently using the given data. Due to various attractive properties of Haar wavelets such as closed form expression, compact support and orthonormality, Haar wavelets are efficiently used for spatial discretization and second order finite difference is used for temporal discretization. Stability and error estimates have been investigated in order to ensure the convergence of the method. Finally, numerical results are compared with few existing results and it is shown that numerical results obtained by the proposed method is better than few existing results.

Keywords
Haar wavelet, Nonlocal condition, Finite difference, Collocation method, Stability.

Introduction

Numerical techniques for solving nonlocal hyperbolic partial differential equations have received enormous attention over the last few years. These nonlocal hyperbolic PDEs are used to describe the dynamics of ground water (see
[

In this article, we consider the non local hyperbolic problem given by

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}=\phi(x,t),\penalty\ x\in(0,1),\penalty\ t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

with initial conditions

u(x,0)=f(x),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 0\leq x\leq 1,

(1.2)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,0)=g(x),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 0\leq x\leq 1,

(1.3)

Dirichlet boundary condition

u(0,t)=h(t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 0<t\leq T,

(1.4)

and nonlocal condition

\int_{0}^{1}u(x,t)dx=\nu(t),\penalty\ \penalty\ 0<t\leq T,

(1.5)

where
\phi,f,g,h
and
\nu
are known functions. It is assumed that

f(x)\in C[0,1]\cap C^{2}[0,1]\penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ g(x)\in C[0,1]\cap C^{1}[0,1].

Further, we assume that
\phi
is sufficiently smooth in order to obtain a smooth solution
u
. It should be noted that
f(x)
and
g(x)
satisfy the following compatibility conditions

f(0)=h(0),\int_{0}^{1}f(x)dx=\nu(0),

g(0)=h^{{}^{\prime}}(0),\int_{0}^{1}g(x)dx=\nu^{{}^{\prime}}(0).

The existence, uniqueness and stability results for the given problem (

[

Numerical techniques for the solution of nonlocal hyperbolic equations have been proposed by several researchers. Ang
et al.

[
1

proposed a numerical method based on integro-differential equation and local interpolating functions to solve the nonlocal hyperbolic PDEs. Dehghan and his collaborators investigated nonlocal hyperbolic PDEs using several numerical methods, e.g. finite difference method based on cubic B-spline scaling functions
[

, variational iterative method

In the last few years, Haar wavelet based collocation methods are extensively used for the numerical solution of partial differential equations. Because of various attractive properties of Haar wavelet such as closed form expression, compact support and orthonormality, it is widely used in various areas of science and engineering. The main drawback of Haar wavelet is its discontinuity. Therefore, we cannot express the solution in terms of Haar wavelet basis directly. To come out with this difficulty, either we can regularize the Haar wavelets with interpolating splines (see
[
5
]
) or expand the highest derivatives in terms of Haar wavelet basis and integrate it out to get the desired expressions (see
[
6
]
). We have used later approach to handle the difficulty coming from discontinuity of the wavelet. Second order finite difference scheme is used for the temporal discretization whereas Haar wavelet basis is used for the spatial discretization. Stability and error analysis have been rigorously studied in order to ensure the convergence of the method. The obtained numerical results are compared with the numerical results provided in the paper
[
9
]
by Dehghan
et al.
In paper
[
9
]
, authors reformulated the problem in such a way that the integral boundary condition is converted into a periodic boundary condition. We have dealt with integral boundary condition directly using the given data which is more accurate.

The content of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we review some basic background of Haar wavelet. In section 3, we propose a hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method (HHWCM) for nonlocal hyperbolic PDEs. In section 4, Stability and error analysis have been studied. Numerical results are analyzed in section 5. A brief conclusion is presented in section 6.

Basic Background

In this section, we review some basics of Haar wavelet which will be used for the proposed numerical method.

2.1
Haar wavelet

For
i\geq 2
, Haar wavelet family
\{h_{i}(x)\}
is defined as

h_{i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}1,&\quad\text{for $\frac{k}{m}\leq x<\frac{k+0.5}{m}$},\\
\\
-1,&\quad\text{for $\frac{k+0.5}{m}\leq x<\frac{k+1}{m}$},\\
\\
0,&\quad\text{elsewhere},\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
m=2^{j},\penalty\ j=0,1,2,...,J
,
k=0,1,\cdots,m-1
and
i=m+k+1
.
i
and
J
denote the wavelet number and maximum resolution level respectively. For simplicity, we have considered
x\in[0,1]
.

It is to be noted that
h_{1}(x)
correspond to Haar scaling function defined by

h_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}1,&\forall x\in[0,1),\\
0,&\text{elsewhere}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Haar wavelets are orthogonal functions i.e.

\int_{0}^{1}h_{\alpha}(x)h_{\beta}(x)dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}2^{-j},&\quad\text{if $\alpha=\beta,$}\\
0,&\quad\text{if $\alpha\neq\beta.$}\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

The wavelet approximation of a function
u\in L^{2}[0,1)
is given by

u_{J}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}h_{i}(x),

(2.4)

where
a_{i}
is the Haar wavelet coefficient.

In order to solve PDEs of any order, we need to compute the following integrals

P_{\beta,i}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{x}...\int_{0}^{x}h_{i}(t)dt^{\beta}=\frac{1}{(\beta-1)!}\ \int_{0}^{x}(x-t)^{\beta-1}h_{i}(t)dt,

(2.5)

where
\beta=1,2,\cdots,n
and
i=1,2,3,\cdots,2M
.
Using the definition of Haar wavelets, these integrals are calculated as follows:

P_{\beta,i}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}0,&x<\frac{k}{m},\\
\frac{1}{\beta!}\Big(x-\frac{k}{m}\Big)^{\beta},&x\in\Big[\frac{k}{m},\frac{k+0.5}{m}\Big),\\
\frac{1}{\beta!}\Big[\Big(x-\frac{k}{m}\Big)^{\beta}-2\Big(x-\frac{k+0.5}{m}\Big)^{\beta}\Big],&x\in\Big[\frac{k+0.5}{m},\frac{k+1}{m}\Big),\\
\frac{1}{\beta!}\Big[\Big(x-\frac{k}{m}\Big)^{\beta}-2\Big(x-\frac{k+0.5}{m}\Big)^{\beta}+\Big(x-\frac{k+1}{m}\Big)^{\beta}\Big],&x\geq\frac{k+1}{m}.\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

In the special case, when
\beta=1
and 2, we obtain

P_{1,i}(1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}1,&\quad\text{for $i=1,$}\\
0,&\quad\text{for $i\neq 1.$}\par\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

and

P_{2,i}(1)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}0.5,&\quad\text{for $i=1,$}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4m^{2}},&\quad\text{for $i\neq 1.$}\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

Let us define

C_{1,i}=\int_{0}^{1}P_{1,i}(x)dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}0.5,&\quad\text{for $i=1,$}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4m^{2}},&\quad\text{for $i\neq 1.$}\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

and

C_{2,i}=\int_{0}^{1}P_{2,i}(x)dx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\frac{1}{6},&\quad\text{for $i=1,$}\\
\displaystyle\frac{2m-2k-1}{8m^{3}},&\quad\text{for $i\neq 1.$}\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

The grid points are given by

y_{l}=l\Delta y,\penalty\ l=0,1,2,\cdots,2M.

where
\Delta y=\frac{1}{2M}
.

The collocation points are given as

x_{l}=\frac{y_{l}+y_{l-1}}{2},\penalty\ l=1,2,\cdots,2M.

Next, we introduce Haar matrix,
H,
and Haar integral matrices
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
which are square matrices of size
2M\times 2M.
The elements of these matrices are
H(i,l)=h_{i}(x_{l}),P_{1}(i,l)=P_{1,i}(x_{l})
and
P_{2}(i,l)=P_{2,i}(x_{l})

Temporal discretization
:
Let
T_{final}
be the final time where we want to compute the solution. The temporal discretization is given by:

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2},\dots,t_{r},\dots<t_{N}=T_{final}

where
r=0,1,2,\dots,N
and
t_{r}=rdt
where
dt=\frac{T_{final}}{N}
.

3
A hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method for nonlocal hyperbolic partial differential equation

In this section, we propose a hybrid wavelet collocation method based on Haar wavelets and second order finite difference method to solve the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.5
). We assume that
u_{J}
be the wavelet approximation of
u.

Let us assume

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u_{J}}{\partial x^{2}}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)h_{i}(x)

(3.1)

Integrating equation (
3.1
) from
0
to
x
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{J}}{\partial x}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)P_{1,i}(x)+\frac{\partial u_{J}}{\partial x}(0,t)

(3.2)

Integrating equation (
3.2
) from
0
to
1
, we get

\displaystyle u_{J}(1,t)-u_{J}(0,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)C_{1,i}+\frac{\partial u_{J}}{\partial x}(0,t)

(3.3)

where

C_{1,i}=\int_{0}^{1}P_{1,i}(x)dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{J}}{\partial x}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)\Big(P_{1,i}(x)-C_{1,i}\Big)+u_{J}(1,t)-u_{J}(0,t)

(3.4)

Integrating equation (
3.4
) from
0
to
x
, we get

\displaystyle u_{J}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-xC_{1,i}\Big)+x[u_{J}(1,t)-u_{J}(0,t)]+u_{J}(0,t)

(3.5)

Using nonlocal condition (
1.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)\Big(C_{2,i}-\frac{1}{2}C_{1,i}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}[u_{J}(1,t)-u_{J}(0,t)]+u_{J}(0,t)=\nu(t)

(3.6)

After simplification, we get

\displaystyle u_{J}(1,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)\Big(C_{1,i}-2C_{2,i}\Big)+2\nu(t)-h(t)

(3.7)

Thus, from equation (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle u_{J}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}(t)\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)+2x[\nu(t)-h(t)]+h(t)

(3.8)

Using second order finite difference scheme for temporal discretization and Haar wavelets for spatial discretization, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{u_{J}(x,t_{n+1})-2u_{J}(x,t_{n})+u_{J}(x,t_{n-1})}{\Delta t^{2}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}h_{i}(x)+\phi(x,t_{n})

(3.9)

From the given boundary condition (
1.3
) and using central difference formula, we get

\displaystyle\frac{u_{J}(x,t_{1})-u_{J}(x,t_{-1})}{2\Delta t}=g(x)

(3.10)

This implies

\displaystyle u_{J}(x,t_{-1})=u_{J}(x,t_{1})-2\Delta tg(x)

(3.11)

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.11
), we obtain the following equation at
t_{0}=0

\displaystyle u_{J}(x,t_{1})=u_{J}(x,t_{0})+\Delta tg(x)+\frac{(\Delta t)^{2}}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}h_{i}(x)+\frac{(\Delta t)^{2}}{2}\phi(x,t_{0})

(3.12)

Using equation (
3.8
) and (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}h_{i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)+2x[\nu(t_{1})-h(t_{1})]+h(t_{1})

\displaystyle=u_{J}(x,t_{0})+\Delta tg(x)+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}\phi(x,t_{0})

(3.13)

At
t=t_{n}
, we obtain the following discretized scheme,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-\Delta t^{2}h_{i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)+2x[\nu(t_{n+1})-h(t_{n+1})]+h(t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\Delta t^{2}\phi(x,t_{n})+2u_{J}(x,t_{n})-u_{J}(x,t_{n-1})

(3.14)

Equation (
3
) at the collocation points
x_{l},l=1,2,3,\dots,2M
is given by

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x_{l})-\Delta t^{2}h_{i}(x_{l})-2x_{l}C_{2,i}\Big)+2x_{l}[\nu(t_{n+1})-h(t_{n+1})]+h(t_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\Delta t^{2}\phi(x_{l},t_{n})+2u_{J}(x_{l},t_{n})-u_{J}(x_{l},t_{n-1})

(3.15)

Finally, we obtain a matrix system at
t=t_{n}

Ba = c

where
\textbf{B}=\{b_{li},\penalty\ 1\leq l,i\leq 2M\}
and
\textbf{c}=\{c_{l},\penalty\ 1\leq l\leq 2M\}
. The expression for
b_{li}
and
c_{l}
is given by

b_{li}=\Big(P_{2,i}(x_{l})-\Delta t^{2}h_{i}(x_{l})-2x_{l}C_{2,i}\Big)

and

c_{l}=\Delta t^{2}\phi(x_{l},t_{n})+2u_{J}(x_{l},t_{n})-u_{J}(x_{l},t_{n-1})-2x_{l}[\nu(t_{n+1})-h(t_{n+1})]-h(t_{n+1})

At each time step, we calculate the wavelet coefficients
a
and obtain the required solution.

4
Stability and error analysis

4.1
Stability analysis

In this subsection, we will study stability analysis for the hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method.

Equation (
1.1
) can be written as follows:

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(x,t)=\mathcal{L}u(x,t)+\phi(x,t)

(4.1)

where
\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}
is the differential operator. Following the temporal discretization using finite difference technique, we obtain

u^{n+1}-2u^{n}+u^{n-1}=(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H}u^{n+1}+(\Delta t)^{2}\phi(x,t_{n})

(4.2)

\implies u^{n+1}=2(I-(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H})^{-1}u^{n}-(I-(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H})^{-1}u^{n-1}+(I-(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H})^{-1}(\Delta t)^{2}\phi(x,t_{n})

(4.3)

where
I
is the identity matrix and
\mathcal{H}
is the Haar matrix corresponding to the differential operator
\mathcal{L}
.

Since equation involves two time levels, we add one identity equation in order to make single time level. We proceed as follows:

\displaystyle u^{n+1}

\displaystyle=B_{1}u^{n}+B_{2}u^{n-1}

(4.4)

\displaystyle u^{n}

\displaystyle=u^{n}

where
B_{1}=2(I-(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H})^{-1}
and
B_{2}=-(I-(\Delta t)^{2}\mathcal{H})^{-1}.
Equation (
4.4
) can be written in the matrix form as follows

\begin{bmatrix}B_{1}&B_{2}\\
I_{2M}&\boldsymbol{0}_{2M}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u^{n}\\
u^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u^{n+1}\\
u^{n}\end{bmatrix}

(4.5)

We know that the eigenvalues of identity matrix is always 1. The stability of the numerical scheme will depend upon the eigenvalues of the matrix
B
where

B=\begin{bmatrix}B_{1}&B_{2}\\
I_{2M}&\boldsymbol{0}_{2M}\end{bmatrix}

(4.6)

The proposed numerical scheme will be stable if all the eigenvalues of the matrix
B
is less than or equal to 1.

The eigenvalues of the matrix
B
for hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method for different
\Delta t
and
J
are given below

and

4.2
Error Analysis

In this subsection, we will study error analysis for the proposed numerical method.
From equation (
3.8
), we have the approximate representation of function
u
given by

\displaystyle u_{J}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2M}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)+2x(\nu(t)-h(t))+h(t)

(4.7)

and the exact representation of
u
is given by

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)+2x(\nu(t)-h(t))+h(t)

(4.8)

Hence, the error term is given by

\displaystyle E_{J}=u-u_{J}=\sum\limits_{i=2M+1}^{\infty}a_{i}\Big(P_{2,i}(x)-2xC_{2,i}\Big)

(4.9)

In terms of resolution level, equation (
4.9
) can be written as

\displaystyle E_{J}=\sum\limits_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}a_{2^{j}+k+1}\Big(P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\Big)

(4.10)

Lemma 4.2.1
.

[
12
]

Let us assume that
u
is a Lipschitz continuous function in [0, 1]. Then, the wavelet coefficient satisfy the following:

|a_{2^{j}+k+1}|\leq\frac{L}{2^{j+1}}

(4.11)

where
L\geq 0
is the Lipschitz constant and

a_{2^{j}+k+1}=2^{j}\int_{0}^{1}u(x)h_{2^{j}+k+1}(x)dx

(4.12)

Lemma 4.2.2
.

Let
u
be the Lipschitz continuous function in the unit square. Then, for fixed
t
, the proposed method is convergent and order of convergence is 2 in spatial variable i.e.

||E_{J}||_{2}=||u-u_{J}||_{2}=\mathcal{O}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J}+1}\Big)^{2}\Big]

(4.13)

Proof
From equation (
4.10
) and definition of
L^{2}
norm, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{J}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}a_{2^{j}+k+1}\Big(P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\Big)\bigg|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}\sum_{{j_{1}}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{2^{j_{1}}-1}a_{2^{j}+k+1}a_{2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}\Big(P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\Big)\times

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\Big(P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}\Big)\bigg|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}\sum_{{j_{1}}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{2^{j_{1}}-1}|a_{2^{j}+k+1}||a_{2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}|\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\Big)\times

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\Big(P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}\Big)\bigg|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{j}-1}\sum_{{j_{1}}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{2^{j_{1}}-1}|a_{2^{j}+k+1}||a_{2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}|\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)

\displaystyle-2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)-2xC_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)+4x^{2}C_{2,2^{j+k+1}}C_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}\Big)dx\bigg|

(4.14)

By the above lemma

|a_{2^{j}+k+1}|\leq\frac{A_{1}}{{2^{j+1}}},

(4.15)

and

|a_{2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}|\leq\frac{A_{2}}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}.

(4.16)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\|P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)dx\bigg|\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

(4.17)

Similarly

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}2xC_{2,2^{j}+k+1}P_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}(x)dx\bigg|\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}2xC_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}P_{2,2^{j}+k+1}(x)dx\bigg|\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}4x^{2}C_{2,2^{j}+k+1}C_{2,2^{j_{1}}+k_{1}+1}dx\bigg|\leq\frac{4}{3}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

Substituting the above estimates (
4.15
–
4.20
) in (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|E_{J}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{13}{3}A_{1}A_{2}\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{{j_{1}}=J+1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{3}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{3}2^{j}2^{j_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\sum_{{j_{1}}=J+1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j_{1}+1}}}\bigg)^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{where}\quad(A=\frac{13}{12}A_{1}A_{2})

\displaystyle\leq A\bigg[\sum_{{j}=J+1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j+1}}}\bigg)\bigg]^{4}

\displaystyle\leq A\frac{1}{2^{4}}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\sum_{{j}=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j}}}\bigg)\bigg]^{4}

\displaystyle\leq A\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\bigg)^{4}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\mbox{since}\sum_{{j}=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{j}}}\bigg)=2.)

Hence

\displaystyle\|E_{J}\|\leq K\bigg(\frac{1}{{2^{J+1}}}\bigg)^{2}

where
\displaystyle K=\sqrt{A}
.

Hence the order of convergence of the Haar wavelet method in spatial variable is given by

\|\mbox{E}_{J}\|_{L_{2}}=\mathcal{O}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]

Theorem 4.2.3
.

Let
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}
and
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}
exist and bounded in
[0,1]\times[0,T].
Then the error estimate for the fully discretized hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method is given by

\|\mbox{E}\|_{L_{2}}=\mathcal{O}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\bigg)^{2}+{\Delta t^{2}}\bigg].

Proof. From the above lemma

||E_{J}||_{2}=\mathcal{O}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{J}+1}\Big)^{2}\Big]

As we have used second order finite difference method for the temporal discretization, the error estimate for the fully discretized numerical method is given by

\|E\|_{L_{2}}=\mathcal{O}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{J+1}}\bigg)^{2}+{\Delta t^{2}}\bigg].

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have developed a hybrid Haar wavelet collocation method for the numerical solution of nonlocal hyperbolic partial differential equations. Instead of reformulating the original problem into periodic problem, we dealt with the integral boundary condition directly using the given data which is more accurate. For the spatial discretization, Haar wavelets are used whereas second order finite difference is used for temporal discretization. Stability analysis based on eigenvalue properties is carried out. We have derived error estimate for the proposed method. Finally, numerical results are presented and it is shown that our method is better than few existing method. This method can easily be generalized to higher dimensional problems.

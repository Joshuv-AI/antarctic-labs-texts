Pointwise Weyl Laws for Schrödinger operators with singular potentials
Xiaoqi Huang, Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. We consider the Schrödinger operators H V = − Δ g + V H_{V}=-\Delta_{g}+V with singular potentials V V on general n n -dimensional Riemannian manifolds and study the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions under this perturbation. These singular potentials appear naturally in physics, most notably the Coulomb potential | x | − 1 |x|^{-1} . Sogge and the first author [ 14 ] proved the sharp Weyl laws for these H V H_{V} with potentials in the Kato class, which is the minimal assumption to ensure that H V H_{V} is essentially self-adjoint and bounded from below and the eigenfunctions of H V H_{V} are bounded. Later, Frank-Sabin [ 9 ] studied the problem on the pointwise Weyl laws for these H V H_{V} in three dimensions by extending the method of Avakumović [ 2 ] , while it is unknown how to reconstruct this argument in other dimensions. In this paper, we completely solve this problem in any dimensions by using a different argument. First, we establish the pointwise Weyl law for potentials in the Kato class on any n n -dimensional manifolds. This extends the 3-dimensional results of Frank-Sabin [ 9 ] by a different method. Second, we prove that the pointwise Weyl law with the standard sharp error term O ⁡ ( λ n − 1 ) O(\lambda^{n-1}) holds for potentials in L n ​ ( M ) L^{n}(M) . This extends the classical results for smooth potentials by Avakumović [ 2 ] , Levitan [ 18 ] and Hörmander [ 12 ] to critically singular potentials. In three dimensions, this L 3 L^{3} condition also naturally appears in Boccato-Brennecke-Cenatiempo-Schlein [ 6 ] on the ground state energy of the Hamilton operator in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime. These two results are sharp, and our proof exploits Li-Yau’s heat kernel bounds and Blair-Sire-Sogge’s eigenfunction estimates.

The purpose of this paper is to study the
pointwise
Weyl Law for the Schrödinger operators
H_{V}=-\Delta_{g}+V
on compact
n
-dimensional
(n\geq 2)
Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
without boundary. We shall assume throughout that the potentials
V
are real-valued. Moreover, we shall assume that
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, which is the Kato class. Recall that
\mathcal{K}(M)
is all
V
satisfying

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{x\in M}\int_{d_{g}(y,x)<\delta}|V(y)|W_{n}(d_{g}(x,y))dy=0,

where

W_{n}(r)=\begin{cases}r^{2-n},\quad\quad\quad\quad n\geq 3\\
\log(2+r^{-1}),\ \ n=2\end{cases}

and
d_{g}
,

denote geodesic distance, the volume element on
(M,g)
.
For later use, note that
L^{p}(M)\subset\mathcal{K}(M)\subset L^{1}(M)
for all
p>\frac{n}{2}
. The Kato class
\mathcal{K}(M)
and
L^{n/2}(M)
share the same critical scaling
behavior, while neither one is contained in the other one for
n\geq 3
. For instance, singularities of the type
|x|^{-\alpha}

\alpha<2

|x|^{-1}

[
23

As was shown in
[
5
]
(see also
[
23
]
)

V
is in the Kato class is needed

H_{V}
is essentially self-adjoint and bounded from below, and the eigenfunctions of
H_{V}
are bounded, which is an obvious requirement for a pointwise Weyl law to hold. Although the Schrödinger operators with potentials in
L^{n/2}(M)
are also self-adjoint and bounded from below for
n\geq 3
, the eigenfunctions for these potentials need not be bounded. Moreover, for potentials in the Kato class, the associated
eigenfunctions are continuous by the heat kernel estimates of Li-Yau
[
20
]
and Sturm
[
29
]
. Since
M
is compact, the spectrum of
H_{V}
is discrete. Assuming, as we may, that

H_{V}
is a positive operator, we shall write the spectrum
of
\sqrt{H_{V}}
as

(0.1)

\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},

where the eigenvalues,
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\cdots

\tau_{k}
there is an

(the domain of
H_{V}
) so that

(0.2)

H_{V}e_{\tau_{k}}=\tau^{2}_{k}e_{\tau_{k}}.

We shall always assume that the eigenfunctions are

L^{2}

\int_{M}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

After possibly adding a constant to

we may, and shall, assume throughout that

is bounded below by one, i.e.,

(0.3)

\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\langle\,H_{V}f,\,f\,\rangle,\quad f\in\text{Dom }(H_{V}).

Also, to be consistent, we shall let

(0.4)

H^{0}=-\Delta_{g}

be the unperturbed operator. The corresponding eigenvalues and associated

-normalized
eigenfunctions are denoted by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

\{e^{0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

(0.5)

H^{0}e^{0}_{j}=\lambda^{2}_{j}e^{0}_{j},\quad\text{and }\,\,\int_{M}|e^{0}_{j}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

Both
\{e_{\tau_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

\{e^{0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

L^{2}(M)
. Recall (see e.g.
[
27
]

N^{0}(\lambda)

H^{0}

N^{0}(\lambda):=\#\{j:\,\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\}=(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\text{Vol}_{g}(M)\,\lambda^{n}\,+\,O(\lambda^{n-1}),

where
\omega_{n}=\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}/\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

[

1

Recall that

N^{0}(\lambda):=\#\{j:\,\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda\}=\int_{M}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\,|e_{j}^{0}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

The Weyl law (

\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}^{0}(x)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}).

It is due to Avakumović

]

[

]

]

Recently, Huang-Sogge

(0.9)

N_{V}(\lambda):=\#\{k:\,\tau_{k}\leq\lambda\}=(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(M)\,\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}).

See also

]

N_{V}(\lambda):=\#\{k:\,\tau_{k}\leq\lambda\}=\int_{M}\sum_{\tau_{k}\leq\lambda}\,|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

For

with smooth potentials, the pointwise Weyl law of the form (

Let

and
P_{V}=\sqrt{H_{V}}

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(\tau)

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})(x,x)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}^{0}(x)|^{2},\ \ {\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)=\sum_{\tau_{k}\leq\lambda}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}.

When

, Frank-Sabin
[
9
]
proved that if
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, then as
\lambda\to\infty
and uniformly in

(0.11)

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\omega_{3}\lambda^{3}+o(\lambda^{3}).

They pointed out that the error term can not be replaced by
O(\lambda^{3-\delta})
, for any
\delta>0
. Moreover,
[
9
]
proved that if

satisfies a stronger condition, the sharp pointwise law may hold. Indeed, if

satisfies for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

(0.12)

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{d_{g}(y,x)<\varepsilon^{\prime}}\frac{|V(y)|}{d_{g}(y,x)^{2}}dy<\infty,

(0.13)

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\omega_{3}\lambda^{3}+O(\lambda^{2}).

Note that the condition (

V\in L^{q}(M)
, for any
q>3
. For comparison, for any
q<3
, they showed that the sharp pointwise Weyl law (

scale. The proof of
[
9
]
extends the method of Avakumović

2
]
, which relies on Tauberian theorems and parametrix estimates. To our knowledge, it is unknown how to reconstruct this argument in other dimensions, see
[
9

In this paper, we completely solve this open problem in any dimensions. Our proof extends the wave equation method in
[
14
]
,
[

]
to get around the difficulties in
[

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
. Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\Lambda(\varepsilon,V)<\infty
such that for
\lambda>\Lambda(\varepsilon,V)
, we have

\sup_{x\in M}|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)-(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}|\leq C_{V}\varepsilon\lambda^{n}.

Here
C_{V}>0
is a constant independent of
\lambda
and
\varepsilon
.

So for potentials in the Kato class, as
\lambda\to\infty
and uniformly in
x\in M
,

(0.14)

\sum_{\tau_{k}\leq\lambda}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}+o(\lambda^{n}).

The 3-dimensional case is due to Frank-Sabin
[
9
]
, while other dimensions of Theorem
1
are new.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
V\in L^{n}(M)
. Then for
\lambda\geq 1

\sup_{x\in M}|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)-(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}|\leq C_{V}\lambda^{n-1}.

Here
C_{V}>0
is a constant independent of
\lambda
.

In other words, for potentials in
L^{n}(M)
, uniformly in
x\in M
,

(0.15)

\sum_{\tau_{k}\leq\lambda}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{-n}\omega_{n}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}).

Theorem
2
is new in all dimensions. In three dimensions, either (
0.12
) or
L^{3}(M)
can ensure the error term is
O(\lambda^{n-1})
, while neither of them can imply the other one. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that this
L^{3}
condition also naturally appears in the study of the ground state energy of the Hamilton
operator in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime, see Boccato-Brennecke-Cenatiempo-Schlein
[
6
]
.

These two theorems are sharp, by the explicit examples studied in the recent work
[
17
]
of the authors. Specifically, the sharpness of Theorem
1
means the error term
o(\lambda^{n})
cannot be replaced by
O(\lambda^{n-\delta})
for any
\delta>0
. The sharpness of Theorem
2
means that the condition
L^{n}(M)
cannot be replaced by
L^{p}(M)
for any
p<n
. In
[
17
]
, we consider the singular potentials

V(x)=\rho(d_{g}(x,x_{0}))d_{g}(x,x_{0})^{-2+\eta},\ 0<\eta<1,

where
x_{0}\in M
is fixed,
d_{g}
is the Riemannian distance function on
(M,g)
, and
\rho
is a smooth cutoff function nonvanishing at zero. This
V
is clearly in
\mathcal{K}(M)
, and it belongs to
L^{q}(M)
, for all
q<\frac{n}{2-\eta}
. The pointwise Weyl law for
H_{V}
with these
V
is expected to have a sharp error term
\approx\lambda^{n-\eta}
. In
[
17
]
, we proved this sharp bound on the flat torus
M=\mathbb{T}^{n}
for any dimensions

. See also Frank-Sabin
[
9
]
for another different proof for the sharpness in three dimensions. Recall that
L^{p}(M)\subset\mathcal{K}(M)\subset L^{1}(M)
for all
p>\frac{n}{2}
, and that the Kato class can ensure the boundedness of eigenfunctions, while
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
cannot (see
[
5
]
,
[
23
]
).
From the discussion above, we can see that Kato class is exactly the border for the existence of the pointwise Weyl law, and
p=n
is the threshold for the validity of the sharp pointwise Weyl law (with the error term
O(\lambda^{n-1})
) on the
L^{p}
scale. If
V\in L^{p}(M)
(
\frac{n}{2}<p<n
), one can easily modify the argument in the proof of Theorem
2
to obtain the sharp error term
O(\lambda^{n-2+\frac{n}{p}})
.

The main strategy of the proof is using Fourier analysis and the wave equation techniques to estimate the difference between the classical kernel
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})(x,x)
and the Schrödinger kernel
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)
. We reduce it to estimating the difference between their smooth approximations, by the Fourier inversion formula, Duhamel’s principle and Sogge’s
L^{p}
-spectral projection bounds. To deal with the difference of two kernels, the main difficulty is to handle the “mixed terms” with two kinds of frequencies from
P^{0}
and
P_{V}
. To get around this, we must design new efficient frequency decompositions, and estimate the terms carefully by Li-Yau’s heat kernel bounds and the theory of pseudo-differential operators.

It is likely that the sharp pointwise Weyl laws for

can be improved under some global geometric conditions on the manifolds (see e.g.

[

[

]
,
[

]
for

Let

\lambda\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
. Fix an even real-valued function
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

Lemma 3
.

There exists a
\Lambda(\varepsilon,V)<\infty

There exists a

Lemma 5

(Spectral projection bounds,
[

For

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1)}(P^{0})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(n,p)},\ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

[

]

Lemma 6

(Heat kernel bounds,
[
20
]

[

]

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,x)=\frac{\omega_{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}).

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{n}(M)}\cdot\lambda^{-2}\lambda^{n/2}\cdot\lambda\cdot(\sum_{\tau_{k}\in[1,\lambda/2)}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{n}(M)}\cdot\lambda^{-2}\lambda^{n/2}\cdot\lambda\cdot\lambda^{n/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{n}(M)}\lambda^{n-1}.

So we finish the proof of (
4.9
).

Next, we prove the middle-frequency estimate (

K_{\tau_{k},\ell}^{+}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{h(\lambda_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\approx 2^{\ell})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

R_{\tau_{k},\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\approx 2^{\ell})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

K_{\tau_{k},\infty}^{+}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{h(\lambda_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\gtrsim\lambda)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

R_{\tau_{k},\infty}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\gtrsim\lambda)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

K_{\tau_{k},\ell}^{-}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{h(\lambda_{j})-1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\approx 2^{\ell})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

K_{\tau_{k},\infty}^{-}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{h(\lambda_{j})-1}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\gtrsim\lambda)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)

When
\tau_{k}\in(\lambda,10\lambda]
, we decompose

K_{\tau_{k}}=K_{\tau_{k},0}+\sum_{2^{\ell}\leq\lambda/100}(K_{\tau_{k},\ell}^{+}-R_{\tau_{k},\ell}h(\tau_{k}))+K_{\tau_{k},\infty}^{+}-R_{\tau_{k},\infty}h(\tau_{k})

\tau_{k}\in[\lambda/2,\lambda]

Moreover, we decompose

\{\lambda_{j}\geq 2\tau_{k}\}

(4.16)

\tilde{K}_{\tau_{k},\infty}^{-}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{h(\lambda_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\gtrsim\lambda)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y)\\
-\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\frac{\eta(\lambda_{j}\lesssim\tau_{k})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}\beta(|\lambda_{j}-\tau_{k}|\gtrsim\lambda)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(y).

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

, let for

(4.17)

By the spectral projection estimates and the rapid decay property of

, we have the following lemma.

.

,

Finally, we have for

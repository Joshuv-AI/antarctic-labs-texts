Layer skyrmions for ideal Chern bands and twisted bilayer graphene
Daniele Guerci, Jie Wang, Christophe Mora

Abstract
Ideal C = 1 C=1 Chern bands exhibit a Landau level correspondence: they factorize as a lowest Landau levels and a spinor wavefunction that spans the layer index. We demonstrate that, in single Dirac moiré models, the spinor develops generally a Skyrme texture in real space with an associated Berry phase which compensates exactly the magnetic phase of the Landau level. For ideal bands with higher Chern numbers C > 1 C>1 , we find that C C color Landau levels are carried by C C spinors with Skyrme textures. We identify a SU(C) gauge symmetry in the color space of spinors and an emergent non-Abelian connection in real space intimately linked to the Pontryagin winding index of the layer skyrmions. They result in a total real-space Chern number of − 1 -1 , screening the magnetic phase, irrespective of C C and of the number of layers. The topologically robust Skyrme texture remains remarkably intact in twisted bilayer graphene, even far from the chiral limit, and for realistic values of corrugation, making it an experimentally testable feature. We verify our predictions at the first magic angle of twisted bilayer, trilayer, and monolayer-bilayer graphene.

A rotation in color space defines new layer skyrmions with a different real space profile and periodicities. However, the Wilson loop eigenvalues are independent of this gauge fixing and the total winding is always
-1
to screen the fictitious magnetic fields in the ideal band wavefunction. We also find a similar skyrmion texture in monolayer-bilayer graphene

Conclusions —

Our analysis highlights the layer Skyrme texture of ideal bands to screen the magnetic phase within the Landau level correspondence.
For higher Chern numbers, the skyrmion decomposition involves a real-space SU(C) Berry connection, associated with a gauge fixing choice of a unit cell. The non-trivial topological winding of layer skyrmions has a direct consequence in single Dirac models: ideal bands are stabilized only when the number of layers exceeds the band Chern number,
i.e.
L>C
. Ideal flat bands emerging at magic angles are spanned by

[

provide opportunities for predicting new topological phases and excitations beyond quantum Hall analogs

Layer skyrmion: connecting real space Berry curvature with the skyrmion winding

II.1
Real space boundary conditions and Chern number

We first discuss the case of a single color, relevant for an ideal band with Chern number
C=1
. We employ the notation adopted by Ref.
[
47
,
46
]
which consists of defining the real space Berry connection as
\bm{\mathcal{A}}=-i\bm{\chi}^{\dagger}\nabla_{\bm{r}}\bm{\chi}
from the normalized spinor wavefunction
\bm{\chi}(\bm{r})
, with
\bm{\chi}^{\dagger}\bm{\chi}=1
.
\bm{\chi}(\mathbf{r})
arises from the decomposition of the ideal band given as Eq. (
1
) in the main text. The real-space Chern number
C_{R}
is defined from the contour integral

C_{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\cal C}d\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\cal S}d^{2}r\,\Omega

(S2)

with the oriented path
\cal C
following the boundaries of the unit cell spanned by
\mathbf{a}_{1}

and
\mathbf{a}_{2}
and
\cal S
is the corresponding area. We used Stoke’s theorem in Eq. (

) to relate the line integral to the surface integration of the real-space Berry curvature
\Omega=\nabla_{\bm{r}}\times\bm{\mathcal{A}}

{\mathcal{A}}
and
\Omega
characterize the real-space structure of the spinor: they are intrinsically different from the (momentum-space) usual Berry phase of the ideal band.

The path
\cal C
consists of four straight lines connected two by two by the vectors
\mathbf{a}_{1}
,
\mathbf{a}_{2}
and traversed in opposite directions. The boundary conditions on the gauge field
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
are deduced from those of the spinor wavefunctions given in the main text and repeated here for clarity

\bm{\chi}(\bm{r}+\bm{a}_{j})=e^{i\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}(\bm{r})}\bm{\chi}(\bm{r}),\qquad\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}(\bm{r})=(\bm{r}\times\mathbf{a}_{j})/(2l^{2}_{B}),\qquad\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{r}+\mathbf{a}_{j})=\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{r})+\nabla_{\bm{r}}\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}(\bm{r}).

(S3)

The evaluation of the real-space Chern number
C_{R}
in Eq. (
S2

\nabla_{\bm{r}}\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{j}}(\bm{r})
, with the result

C_{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{1}_{0}dl[\mathbf{a}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{r}}\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}(\bm{r})-\mathbf{a}_{1}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{r}}\varphi_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}(\bm{r})]=\frac{\mathbf{a}_{2}\times\mathbf{a}_{1}}{2\pi l^{2}_{B}}=-1

(S4)

We thus find that the screening boundary conditions on the spinor wavefunction, imposed by the LLL factorization, automatically implies a real-space Chern number of
-1
.

The above derivation extends trivially to the case of mutiple colors. The non-Abelian Berry connection is introduced in Sec.
IV
, Eq. (
S39
), and its trace satisfies the real-space boundary conditions

{\rm Tr}\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{r}+\mathbf{a}_{j})={\rm Tr}\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{r})+C\,\frac{\mathbf{a}_{j}\times\bm{z}}{2l^{2}_{B}},

(S5)

and the real-space Chern number evaluated over the unit cell
(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2})
is given by

C_{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\cal C}d\bm{l}\cdot{\rm Tr}\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{C(\mathbf{a}_{2}\times\mathbf{a}_{1})}{2\pi l^{2}_{B}}=-1.

(S6)

II.2
Single-component wavefunction

We analyze the case where
\chi(\mathbf{r})
has a single component (or layer) and show by contradiction that the LLL decomposition imposes that
\chi(\mathbf{r})
has to vanish somewhere in the moiré unit cell. Let us rewrite the LLL decomposition as

\psi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{r})=\chi(\bm{r})\,e^{-K(\mathbf{r})}\,\Phi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{r})

(S7)

where
\Phi_{\bm{k}}(\bm{r})
is a LLL wavefunction with a homogeneous magnetic field of one flux quantum per unit cell. The fictitious magnetic field inhomogeneity is separated and included in the real (Kähler) potential
K(\bm{r})
in such a way that
\chi(\bm{r})
is normalized
|\chi(\bm{r})|^{2}=1
at each position in the unit cell. The wavefunction
\chi(\bm{r})
must be non-vanishing before its normalization by the Kähler potential. In addition,
K(\bm{r})
is periodic over the moiré lattice with
K(\bm{r}+\mathbf{a}_{j})=K(\bm{r})
. The compatibility between the Bloch and magnetic periodities of
\Phi_{\bm{k}}
and
\psi_{\bm{k}}
implies that
\chi(\mathbf{r})\equiv e^{i\theta_{0}(\mathbf{r})}
transforms under lattice translations as Eq. (
S3
) with the result

\theta_{0}(\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{a}_{j})=\theta_{0}(\mathbf{r})+\frac{\bm{r}\times\mathbf{a}_{j}}{2l^{2}_{B}}.

(S8)

The winding of the phase
\theta_{0}(\mathbf{r})
along the boundary of the unit cell is
-2\pi
, as calculated from Eq. (
S8
), which is consistent with the single valuedness of
\chi(\mathbf{r})
. However, this vortex configuration for the phase leads to a contradiction since the path can be contracted to a single point, and
\chi(\mathbf{r})
is of modulus one everywhere on the plane.

In summary, we have proven that the envelope spinor function
\bm{\chi}(\mathbf{r})
, assuming the absolute value does not vanish anywhere in the plane, must consist of at least two components.

Layer spinor Skyrme texture and Pontryagin index

We consider the single color spinor wavefunction

or

x

T_{jk}(\bm{r})={\rm Tr}\left[\partial_{j}P(\bm{r})\,\{1-P(\bm{r})\}\,\partial_{k}P(\bm{r})\right].

(S10)

If the spinor
\chi

P(\bm{r})=\frac{1+\bm{n}(\bm{r})\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}}{2}

(S11)

(S13)

which describes the rotation of the Bloch vector

as the position

\mathcal{W}_{\chi}=\int_{{\cal S}\equiv UC}d^{2}r\,Q(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{UC}d^{2}\bm{r}\,\,\bm{n}\cdot(\partial_{x}\bm{n}\times\partial_{y}\bm{n})

(S14)

which must be an integer. Using the relation between the Pontryagin index and the real-space Berry curvature, Eq. (
S12
), and inserting Eq. (
S13

S2

\mathcal{W}_{\chi}

are identical. The screening
\mathcal{W}_{\chi}=C_{R}=-1

\bm{n}
imposed by the Landau level decomposition.

components obtained from
\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{r})=\langle\bm{\chi}(\bm{r})|\lambda_{\alpha}|\bm{\chi}(\bm{r})\rangle

S12

as well as Eq. (

\bm{n}\cdot(\partial_{x}\bm{n}\times\partial_{y}\bm{n})=f_{abc}n_{a}(\bm{r})\partial_{x}n_{b}(\bm{r})\partial_{y}n_{c}(\bm{r})

III
Color Landau levels

We build a basis of lowest Landau levels, with Bloch (pseudo-)periodicity, adapted to describe ideal bands with Chern number
C

C
unit cells. We thus define the extended magnetic cell

along with the commutation of
T_{C\mathbf{a}_{1}}
and

T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}=e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{b}_{2}}{C}\cdot\mathbf{R}}\qquad T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}=e^{i\frac{\mathbf{b}_{1}}{C}\cdot\mathbf{R}}

(S18)

\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{r}-\frac{\mathbf{z}\crossproduct\mathbf{\Pi}}{eB}\qquad\qquad\mathbf{\Pi}=-i\hbar\mathbf{\nabla}-e\mathbf{A}

(S19)

the reciprocal basis vectors
\mathbf{b}_{1}
and
\mathbf{b}_{2}
and the vector potential
\mathbf{A}
.
The Bloch magnetic states

and

which satisfies the magnetic periodicity

T_{C\mathbf{a}_{1}}U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})=U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r}),\qquad\qquad T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})=U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r}),

(S21)

The subsequent color wavefunction are obtained by simply applying the magnetic translation operator
T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}

U^{z}_{k,n}(\bm{r})=T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}U^{z}_{k,n-1}(\bm{r}),

(S22)

corresponding to
C
different colors, due to
the cyclic condition
T_{C\mathbf{a}_{1}}U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})=(T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{C}U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})=U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})
. The action of the magnetic translation
T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}
on this color basis is readily obtained from the commutation property Eq. (

T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}U^{z}_{k,n}(\bm{r})=e^{i\frac{2\pi(n-1)}{C}}U^{z}_{k,n}(\bm{r})

(S23)

Building a vector with color components,

\bm{U}^{z}_{k}(\bm{r})=\begin{pmatrix}U^{z}_{k,1}(\bm{r})\\
\vdots\\
U^{z}_{k,C}(\bm{r})\end{pmatrix}

(S24)

The representation of the two magnetic translation operators are

,
T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}=\tau
, with
\sigma
and
\tau
given in the main text,
corresponding to the algebra of matrices in the clock or parafermion models,

\sigma^{C}=\tau^{C}=1\qquad\qquad\sigma\tau=e^{2i\pi/C}\tau\sigma

(S25)

Remarkably,
\sigma
and
\tau
play a symmetric role. The eigenvalues of
\sigma
,
\tau
and
\sigma^{\dagger}
are the same. We define the
x
axis by a rotation in the color basis which diagonalizes

T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\bm{U}^{x}_{k}(\bm{r})=\tau\bm{U}^{x}_{k}(\bm{r})\qquad\qquad T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}\bm{U}^{x}_{k}(\bm{r})=\sigma\bm{U}^{x}_{k}(\bm{r})

The magnetic translation along the third symmetric direction

is obtained from the product

T_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}T_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}T_{\mathbf{a}_{3}}=e^{i\phi/2}=e^{i\pi/C}

imposed by the flux piercing the oriented triangle formed by

\mathbf{a}_{2}

and the rotation to the

They are sufficient to determine the Chern number for a color LLL over the Brillouin zone

IV.1

Employing the basis

where
\lambda_{j}
are the generators of SU(C) and
\lambda_{0}
the identity.
\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(\mathbf{r})
is periodic over the moiré lattice contributing with a spatially modulated field
\nabla_{\bm{r}}\times\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
with zero average. We observe that the first two contributions constitute the conventional Abelian part, while the third term arises from the SU(C) non-Abelian structure associated with the internal color degree of freedom.

IV.2
Proof of
C\leq L-1

For ideal bands in single Dirac models, the number of layers
L
must exceed the Chern number
C
,
L>C
. This result can be rigorously proven under two key assumptions:

1.

the layer spinors
\bm{\chi}_{n}(\bm{r})
are normalized at each
\bm{r}
. This requires that their unnormalized counterparts
\bm{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{r})
must not vanish anywhere in the moiré unit cell.

2.

The subspace generated by the spinors
\bm{\chi}_{n}(\bm{r})
must have a rank of
C
at each
\bm{r}
. In other words, the spinors must be linearly independent.

They are satisfied for single Dirac models but not always for double Dirac models, see Sec.
VIII
and Refs.
[
108
,
109
]
. We now proceed with the proof by contradiction and assume that the number of layers equals the Chern number
L=C
.
We employ the ket notation

\ket{\bm\chi_n}
for the
L=C
layer spinor
\bm{\chi}_{n}
with
n=1,\cdots,C
, and define the projector:

P=\sum_{n=1}^{C}\ket{\bm\chi_n}\bra{\tilde{\bm\chi}_n},

(S40)

satisfying
P^{2}=P
.
The assumption 2. implies that the set
\{\ket{\bm\chi_{1}},\dots,\ket{\bm\chi_{C}}\}
is linearly independent and thus form a basis for any wavefunction
\ket{\psi}
, expressed as
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{n=1}^{C}\lambda_{n}\ket{\bm\chi_{n}}
. Applying
P
gives
P\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
or
P=\mathbf{1}
, the projector is the identity. We use this result in the expression of the Berry curvature

\begin{split}\Omega(\bm{r})&=\Tr[\nabla_{\bm{r}}\times\bm{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{r})]=-i\sum_{n}[\innerproduct{\partial_x\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_y{\bm\chi}_n}-\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_n}]=-i\sum_{n}[\innerproduct{\partial_x\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\mathbf 1|\partial_y{\bm\chi}_n}-\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_n}]\\
&=-i\sum_{n}[\innerproduct{\partial_x\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{ P|\partial_y{\bm\chi}_n}-\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_n}]=-i\sum_{n}\left[\sum_{m}\innerproduct{\partial_x\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{{\bm\chi}_m}\innerproduct{\tilde{\bm\chi}_m}{\partial_y{\bm\chi}_n}-\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_n}\right]\\
&=-i\sum_{n}\left[\sum_{m}\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_m}{{\bm\chi}_n}\innerproduct{\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_m}-\innerproduct{\partial_y\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_x{\bm\chi}_n}\right]=0,\end{split}

(S41)

where, in the last expression, we used the identity
\innerproduct{\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{{\bm\chi}_m}=\delta_{nm}\implies\innerproduct{\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{\partial_a{\bm\chi}_m}=-\innerproduct{\partial_a\tilde{\bm\chi}_n}{{\bm\chi}_m}
.
Alternatively, we arrive at the same conclusion by readily using the definition Eq. (
S10
) for the quantum geometric tensor which we find to be identically vanishing when
P=1
. A vanishing Berry curvature throughout the moiré unit cell implies that the real-space Chern number
C_{R}
is zero which is fundamentally incompatible with the screening of the magnetic phase detailed in Sec.
II
and in the main text.
Therefore, we have proven by contradiction that
L=C
is not possible and thus
L-1\geq C
.

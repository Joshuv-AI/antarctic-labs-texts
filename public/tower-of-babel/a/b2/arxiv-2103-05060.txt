The c-map as a functor on certain variations of Hodge structure
Mauro Mantegazza, Arpan Saha

Abstract
We give a new manifestly natural presentation of the supergravity c-map. We achieve this by giving a more explicit description of the correspondence between projective special Kähler manifolds and variations of Hodge structure, and by demonstrating that the twist construction of Swann, for a certain kind of twist data, reduces to a quotient by a discrete group. We combine these two ideas by showing that variations of Hodge structure give rise to the aforementioned kind of twist data and by then applying the twist realisation of the c-map due to Macia and Swann. This extends previous results regarding the lifting of general isomorphisms along the undeformed c-map, and of infinitesimal automorphisms along the deformed c-map. We show in fact that general isomorphisms can be naturally lifted along the deformed c-map.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background

Quaternionic Kähler manifolds are notable as their holonomy group
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
is the only entry in Berger’s classification of Riemannian holonomy groups that corresponds to Einstein but not Ricci-flat metrics.
The construction of (complete) Einstein metrics has been a question of long-standing interest among geometers.
The rigidity offered by the special holonomy of the quaternionic Kähler manifolds allows one to import tools from various other areas of mathematics, such as representation theory and algebraic geometry, to construct interesting examples of Einstein manifolds.

This paper is concerned with one such way of constructing families of complete inhomogeneous quaternionic Kähler metrics with negative scalar curvature that has its origin in the literature on supergravity and string theory. The negativity of the scalar curvature is actually crucial, as there is considerable evidence for a conjecture due to LeBrun and Salamon
[
LS94
]
that there are no non-symmetric examples of complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds with positive scalar curvature. For example, this was shown to be the case in dimension
4
in
[
Hit81
,
FK82
]
, in dimension
8
in
[
PS91
]
, and in dimensions
12
and
16
in
[
BWW20
]
. Moreover, it was shown in
[
DS99
]
that there are no cohomogeneity
1
examples in any dimension. In fact, LeBrun and Salamon themselves showed in
[
LS94
]
that there are, up to rescaling, only finitely many examples of complete quaternionic Kähler manifolds with positive scalar curvature in each dimension.

In physics, quaternionic Kähler manifolds with negative scalar curvature can be thought of as manifolds parametrising the scalar fields in a
3
-dimensional supergravity theory defined on a “spacetime” manifold
X
of dimension
3
. One way to produce such a theory is by
dimensional reduction
. Briefly, one starts with an appropriate
4
-dimensional supergravity theory on
X\times\mathbf{S}^{1}
and then takes a limit in which the radius of the circle
\mathbf{S}^{1}
goes to zero. The manifold parametrising vector multiplet scalar fields in the
4
-dimensional supergravity theory has a natural
projective special Kähler structure
, a notion that will be recalled later on. Thus, the result of this physical procedure is a map from the set of projective special Kähler manifolds to the set of quaternionic Kähler manifolds. This is the
undeformed c-map
.

In the case of supergravity theories arising as low-energy descriptions of string theory, perturbative quantum corrections arising from the genus expansion of string theory give rise to a deformation of the above quaternionic Kähler metric parametrised by a discrete integer parameter
k
. When
k\neq 0
, the resulting map from the set of projective special Kähler manifolds to the set of families of quaternionic Kähler manifolds
N_{2k}
is the
deformed c-map
. These two cases will be collectively referred to as the
supergravity c-map
. The adjective “supergravity” is to distinguish it from a similar construction in the absence of gravity called the
rigid
c-map, that we shall also be encountering later.

The supergravity c-map was described in geometric terms in
[
CHM12
]
and
[
CDS17
]
as a bundle construction over the projective special Kähler manifold.
Note that, in these works, the deformation parameter was continuous, as the authors work only up to local isometry.
In fact, up to local isometry, there are only three cases, characterised by the sign of the parameter.
When the given projective special Kähler metric is complete, it was proven in
[
CDS17
]
, that the resulting quaternionic Kähler metric is also complete for
k\geq 0
. This is in fact false for
k<0
(see
[
ACDM15
, Proposition 4 (p. 287)]
), and so we henceforth exclude this case from our discussion of the supergravity c-map.

Projective special Kähler manifolds and (families of) quaternionic Kähler manifolds have natural notions of isomorphism, giving rise to groupoids (i.e. categories with only invertible arrows) of projective special Kähler and (families of) quaternionic Kähler manifolds.
So, one may ask whether the map of sets underlying the supergravity c-map actually descends to a map of isomorphism classes.
It was shown in
[
CDS17
]
that this is indeed the case.
However, the construction was still formulated in terms of non-canonical data, so it is not clear from this approach whether the supergravity c-map is a functor between the relevant groupoids.
In other words, what we are asking is whether isomorphisms of projective special Kähler manifolds
canonically
lift to isomorphisms (i.e. isometries
[
ACDV03
, (p. 529)]
) of quaternionic Kähler manifolds.

Partial results had been previously established.
It had been shown by independent methods in
[
CHM12
]
,
[
MS15
]
and
[
MS20
]
that in the case of the undeformed c-map, the quaternionic Kähler manifold forms a trivial bundle over the projective special Kähler manifold.
In particular, the lifting of automorphisms is well-defined
[
CDJL18
, Appendix]
.
In the case of the deformed c-map, it was shown in
[
CST21
]
that there is a well-defined lifting of infinitesimal automorphisms.
The question of whether general automorphisms and isomorphisms naturally lift in the deformed case hitherto remained open.

1.2
Main results

In this paper, we fill in the gap and prove that isomorphisms of projective special Kähler manifolds canonically lift to global isometries of the quaternionic Kähler manifolds under the supergravity c-map, for non-negative integer deformation parameters.

There are two main ingredients in our approach. The first is a reformulation of the notion of projective special Kähler structure as a particular case of (abstract) variations of Hodge structure of weight
3
.
We will define these in greater detail later, but for now, we will just mention that they are an abstraction of the bundle
E\rightarrow M
, whose base
M
is the moduli space of complex structure deformations of a compact simply connected Calabi–Yau
3
-fold
K_{m\in M}
, and whose fibres
E_{m}
are the cohomology groups
H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K_{m})
.
Accordingly, we have a Hodge decomposition

E_{\mathbb{C}}=E^{3,0}\oplus E^{2,1}\oplus E^{1,2}\oplus E^{0,3}.

(1.1)

In the case we are interested in,
E^{3,0}
will be a holomorphic line bundle
L
with hermitian structure
h_{L}
, and
E^{2,1}
the bundle
L\otimes T^{1,0}M
.
The other two summands
E^{1,2}
and
E^{0,3}
are defined to be the conjugates of
E^{2,1}
and
E^{3,0}
respectively.
Moreover,
E^{3,0}
has a canonical
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action that preserves
h_{L}
.

The correspondence between projective special Kähler manifolds and abstract variations of Hodge structure was first noted and proved by Cortés in
[
Cor98
]
.
A more intrinsic formulation was then given by Freed in
[
Fre99
]
.
We however give slightly different statements and proofs in Propositions
3.22
and
3.25
that describe the correspondence more explicitly.
This correspondence was also addressed in
[
HHP10
]
via a more local approach.
Our approach differs from theirs in that we give a global description of this structure, and in terms of a Hodge decomposition rather than a filtration.
The result is that we get a much clearer description of the relation, including an explicit expression for the Gauß–Manin connection.

The other ingredient is due to Macia and Swann
[
MS15
,
MS20
]
, building on a prior work by Haydys
[
Hay08
]
.
In their work, the c-map is interpreted as a special case of a more general construction called the
twist
.
Introduced by Swann in
[
Swa10
]
, the twist construction takes as its input a manifold
S
with certain
twist data
, that includes a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action, and produces as output another manifold
S^{\prime}
with a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action.
In Lemma
4.2
, we establish that for certain choices of twist data, the twist construction is equivalent to taking a quotient by a finite subgroup
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
acting on
S
.
This observation is new.

The description of projective special Kähler structures in terms of variations of Hodge structure turns out to be particularly well-suited for applying the twist construction.
Putting these ingredients together, we obtain the following result.

Theorem
.

Let
E
be the variation of Hodge structure associated to a projective special Kähler manifold
M
and let
\widetilde{M}_{>2k}
denote the subset of the line bundle
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E^{3,0}
of points
u
whose fibrewise
h_{L}
-norm satisfies
h_{L}(u,u)>2k
. Then, the quaternionic manifold
N_{2k}
given by the deformed c-map is the pullback of the real part of
E
to the quotient
\widetilde{M}_{>2k}/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is a subgroup of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
whose action preserves
h_{L}
.

In the above, we have omitted the explicit expression for the quaternionic Kähler metric as it requires a large number of prerequisites. The complete statement is given and proved in Theorem
5.4
later on.

Presented in this form, the deformed c-map construction is manifestly functorial, and elucidates the isometric action on the resulting quaternionic Kähler manifold by a semidirect product of the group of automorphisms of the projective special Kähler manifold with a Heisenberg group, cf. Remark
5.14
.
As it turns out, the functorial lift of automorphisms of projective special Kähler manifolds reproduces the lift of infinitesimal automorphisms constructed in a very different way in
[
CST21
]
.
We prove this in Proposition
5.12
.
Furthermore, in Remark
5.13
, we provide evidence that our method for canonically lifting isomorphisms through the c-map cannot be extended to non-integer
k
.

Aside from the manifest functoriality, what is also notable about the above description of the supergravity c-map is that it suggests certain natural generalisations applicable to a much wider class of variations of Hodge structure.
The resulting manifold is not quaternionic Kähler in general, as its real dimension may not even be divisible by
4
.
Interpreting these resulting manifolds is a question we leave for future investigation.

1.3
Outline

Since our major motivation is to prove the naturality of certain constructions, we need to set up some notation.
Thus, we start with a detailed preliminary discussion in §
2
.
Here, we introduce our notation and recollect certain elementary constructions in differential geometry that we make use of later.

Following this, in §
3
, we set up the basic definitions of projective special Kähler geometry, establish useful formulas regarding them, introduce variations of Hodge structure, and realise projective special Kähler structures as certain instances of them.

Next, in §
4
, we introduce Swann’s twist construction and prove a useful lemma regarding it.

In §
5
, we finally put all of this together to give a new description of the supergravity c-map and discuss some of its properties, such as functoriality and the existence of a Heisenberg action on the quaternionic Kähler manifold.

Data availability

Data sharing is not applicable to this article as no new data were created or analysed in this study.

2
Preliminaries, notation, and conventions

We begin by first recounting some elementary notions from differential geometry and fixing our notation.

2.1
Complex vector spaces

Let
W
be a real vector space.
We denote the complexification
W\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
as
W_{\mathbb{C}}
.
If
W
is endowed with a
complex structure
, i.e.
I\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(W,W)
such that
I^{2}=-\mathrm{id}_{W}
, then we have the decomposition
W_{\mathbb{C}}=W^{1,0}\oplus W^{0,1}
into eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalues
\mathrm{i}
and
-\mathrm{i}
respectively.
Similarly, we have a decomposition
W^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}=W^{*}_{1,0}\oplus W^{*}_{0,1}
given by the transpose of
I
.
We define the space
W^{*}_{p,q}
to be the subspace of
(W^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})^{\otimes(p+q)}
built out of
p
copies of
W^{*}_{1,0}
and
q
copies of
W^{*}_{0,1}
.
An element of
W^{*}_{p,q}
will be said to be of type
(p,q)
.

Now let
V
be a complex vector space. Following
[
Sal89
]
, we denote by
V_{\mathbb{R}}
its underlying real vector space and by
[V]
its real part with respect to an antilinear involution
\kappa
, also known as
real structure
, i.e.

[V]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{v\in V\mid\kappa(v)=v\}.

(2.1)

This is a real vector space whose real dimension is equal to the complex dimension of
V
.

Notice that for any complex vector space
V
, not necessarily one equipped with a real structure, we may define another complex vector space
\overline{V}
, which has the same underlying real vector space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
, but on which the complex scalar multiplication
\mathbb{C}\times V_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow V_{\mathbb{R}}
is given by

(\lambda,v_{\mathbb{R}})\longmapsto(\overline{\lambda}v)_{\mathbb{R}},

(2.2)

where
v_{\mathbb{R}}\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the vector
v\in V
regarded as a vector in
V_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The identity map on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
then becomes an antilinear map
V\rightarrow\overline{V}
, which in turn gives a canonical real structure on
V\oplus\overline{V}
.
In terms of this real structure, we may then define

\llbracket V\rrbracket\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[V\oplus\overline{V}].

(2.3)

Putting all of these together, we have the canonical isomorphisms

[V]_{\mathbb{C}}\cong V,\qquad\llbracket V\rrbracket_{\mathbb{C}}\cong V\oplus\overline{V},\qquad\llbracket V\rrbracket\cong V_{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.4)

The first two are isomorphisms of complex vector spaces, whereas the last one is an isomorphism of real vector spaces.

The same notation will be used accordingly for vector bundles.

2.3
Connections

The most general notion of connection is an Ehresmann connection, which is applicable to any (smooth) fibre bundle.
This is just a choice of a right inverse to the map
(p^{A}_{B})_{*}\colon TA\rightarrow(TB)_{A}
.
The image of such a right inverse is a “horizontal” subbundle
\mathcal{H}
of
TA
, complementary to the vertical subbundle and isomorphic to
(TB)_{A}
.
In other words, the data of an Ehresmann connection is precisely the choice of a splitting
TA\cong\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}
.

The right inverse of
(p^{A}_{B})_{*}\colon TA\rightarrow(TB)_{A}
maps the pullback
X_{A}
associated to a vector field
X
on
B
, to a vector field
X^{\mathcal{H}}
on the total space
A
.
We call
X^{\mathcal{H}}
the
horizontal lift
of
X
.

Morally, an Ehresmann connection gives us a way of parallel-transporting elements of a fibre of
A\rightarrow B
to other fibres of the bundle.
Given a curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow B
, we have an induced map of bundles
\gamma^{\prime}\colon A_{\gamma}\rightarrow A
.
The image of the differential
\gamma_{*}^{\prime}\colon T(A_{\gamma})\rightarrow(TA)_{\gamma^{\prime}}
intersects
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma^{\prime}}
in a distribution that has rank at most
1
, and thus is integrable.
The integral curves of the distribution on
A_{\gamma}
are said to be horizontal lifts of the curve
\gamma
.
Since each of these curves intersects every fibre of
A_{\gamma}
at exactly one point, they provide a notion of parallel transport on the bundle
A\rightarrow B
along
\gamma
.

[
KMS93
]
). For instance, in the case of a

on a principal

are two linear connections such that
\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}=(p^{A}_{B})^{*}\theta
and
\nabla-\nabla^{\prime}=C
, then the difference of the horizontal lifts is given by

\displaystyle X^{\varphi}-X^{\varphi^{\prime}}=-\theta(X)^{\circ},

\displaystyle X^{\nabla}-X^{\nabla^{\prime}}=-\mathrm{vert}(C_{X}(\Phi_{A})).

(2.12)

A linear connection on a complex vector bundle with a real structure is called
real
when it maps real sections to real sections.
Finally, we will denote the Levi-Civita connection on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
as
\nabla^{g}
.

2.4
Complete lifts

Suppose we are given a vector field
X
on
B
. Let
\psi_{t}
be its flow. Then, we may define a
1
-parameter subgroup of diffeomorphisms on the tangent bundle
TB
by
(u,X_{u})\mapsto(\psi_{t}(u),(\psi_{t})_{*}X_{u})
. This is the flow of a vector field
X^{T}
on
TB
, which is a lift of
X
. The vector field
X^{T}
is said to be the
complete
lift of
X
cf.
[
YI66
]
. We would like to relate it to the horizontal lift with respect to a connection.

To do this, we first make some general remarks about vector fields on the total space of a vector bundle
A\rightarrow B
. These can be regarded as derivations on the ring of smooth functions on
A
.
By Taylor’s theorem, a derivation on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!{({A})}
is completely specified by its action on the ring of functions on
A
, which are polynomial along the fibres.
Such functions may be identified with sections of the symmetrised dual tensor bundle
\mathrm{Sym}_{\bullet}A^{*}\rightarrow B
, where

\mathrm{Sym}_{\bullet}A^{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}(A^{*\otimes j}).

(2.13)

A section
\Xi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Xi_{j}
of
\mathrm{Sym}_{\bullet}A^{*}
corresponds to the function on the total space
A
obtained by

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\Xi_{j})_{A}(\Phi_{A},\dots,\Phi_{A}).

(2.14)

A vector field on
A
can thus be described in terms of its action as a derivation on sections of
\mathrm{Sym}_{\bullet}A^{*}
.

Given a section
s
of
A_{A}
, the associated vertical vector field
\mathrm{vert}(s)
corresponds to the derivation
\Xi_{j}\mapsto j\iota_{s}\Xi_{j}=j\Xi_{j}(s,\dots)
for
\Xi_{j}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}(A^{*\otimes j}))
.
In particular,
\mathrm{vert}(\Phi_{A})\in\mathfrak{X}{\left({{A}}\right)}
corresponds to the derivation acting on
\mathrm{Sym}(A^{*\otimes j})
as the scalar multiplication by
j
. Note that this is the derivation generated by the identity map on fibrewise linear functions
\alpha
, i.e. sections of
A^{*}
. More generally, the derivation generated by
\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ\phi
for
\phi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(A)_{A})
may be identified with the vertical vector field
\mathrm{vert}(\phi(\Phi_{A}))
.

Let us now consider the horizontal lift
X^{\nabla}
of a vector field
X
on
B
with respect to a linear connection
\nabla
. Its flow corresponds to the parallel transport along the flow lines of
X
. This in turn defines on sections of
\mathrm{Sym}_{\bullet}(A^{*})
a derivation
\Xi\mapsto\nabla_{X}\Xi
.

Finally, let us go back to the case where
A
is the tangent bundle
TB\rightarrow B
.
Here, the complete lift
X^{T}
of a vector field
X
on
B
, acts by Lie derivative on the fibres, and this corresponds to the derivation
\Xi\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{X}\Xi
.

Now, we see that when
\nabla
is torsion-free, the difference
X^{T}-X^{\nabla}
acts on a fibrewise linear function on
TB
, identified with a
1
-form
\alpha
on
B
, as

\alpha\longmapsto\mathcal{L}_{X}\alpha-\nabla_{X}\alpha=\alpha(\nabla X).

(2.15)

As we have seen above, the derivation generated by this corresponds to the vector field
\mathrm{vert}((\nabla X)(\Phi_{TB}))
, where
\nabla X
is interpreted as an endomorphism field. Thus, we have

X^{T}=X^{\nabla}+\mathrm{vert}((\nabla X)(\Phi_{TB})).

(2.16)

This equation can also be generalised to connections with torsion
T^{\nabla}
by writing
\nabla X+\iota_{X}T^{\nabla}
instead of
\nabla X
.
A similar relation was obtained for cotangent bundles in
[
CST21
, Lemma 3.12 (p. 111)]
by working in local coordinates.

3
Special Kähler geometry

In this section, we explicitly describe how to translate the data of a projective special Kähler manifold to that of a variation of Hodge structure of a certain type (Proposition
3.22
) and vice versa (Proposition
3.25
).
In order to accomplish this, we will need to establish a few technical lemmata.
We do this in §
3.2
.

3.1
Basic definitions

We begin by reviewing the definitions of affine special Kähler, conic special Kähler, and projective special Kähler manifolds.

Definition 3.1
.

An
affine special Kähler
(ASK) manifold is the data of a pseudo-Kähler manifold
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g},I,\widetilde{\omega})
with a flat, torsion-free, symplectic connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
such that
(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y=(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}I)X
for all vector fields
X,Y
on
\widetilde{M}
.

Remark 3.2
.

A consequence of torsion-freeness and
(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y=(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}I)X
is that
\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}Y,Z)
and

\begin{split}\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}(IY),Z)&=\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y,Z)+\widetilde{\omega}(I(\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}Y,Z)\\
&=\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y,Z)-\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}Y,IZ)\end{split}

(3.1)

are symmetric under the exchange
X\leftrightarrow Y
.
Meanwhile, the symplectic condition
\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{\omega}=0
implies that
\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}Y,Z)
is symmetric also under the exchange
Y\leftrightarrow Z
, and hence fully symmetric.
This, in turn, implies full symmetry for
\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}(IY),Z)
as well, since we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}(IY),Z)&=\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{IY}X,Z)=\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{IY}Z,X)\\
&=\widetilde{\omega}((\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Z}(IY),X).\end{split}

(3.2)

In particular, by replacing
X
with
IX
in (
3.1
) and using the symmetries just proved, we deduce

(\widetilde{\nabla}-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{X}Y=-\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{IX}I)Y=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{1,0}_{X}I)Y+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{0,1}_{X}I)Y.

(3.3)

We shall henceforth denote the
(1,0)
part of the above as
\widetilde{\eta}_{X}Y
.
This defines a section
\widetilde{\eta}
of
T^{*}_{1,0}M\otimes TM\otimes T^{*}M
.
Note that the symmetry property means
\widetilde{\eta}
is in fact a section of
\sharp_{2}S_{3,0}\widetilde{M}
, where
S_{3,0}\widetilde{M}
denotes the subbundle of
(T^{*}M)^{\otimes 3}
of symmetric tensors of type
(3,0)
, and
\sharp_{2}
indicates that the second index is raised using the metric
\widetilde{g}
.

The flatness of
\widetilde{\nabla}
implies that
\widetilde{\eta}
satisfies
\overline{\partial}^{\widetilde{g}}\widetilde{\eta}=0
, where
\overline{\partial}^{\widetilde{g}}
is the
(0,1)
part of the exterior covariant derivative with respect to
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
, i.e.

\overline{\partial}^{\widetilde{g}}\colon\Omega^{1,0}\left(\widetilde{M},T^{0,1}\widetilde{M}\otimes T^{*}_{1,0}\widetilde{M}\right)\longrightarrow\Omega^{1,1}\left(\widetilde{M},T^{0,1}\widetilde{M}\otimes T^{*}_{1,0}\widetilde{M}\right).

(3.4)

Since
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
respects the
(1,0)
,
(0,1)
decomposition, this is a Dolbeault operator.
We shall thus refer to
\widetilde{\eta}
as the
holomorphic difference tensor field
.
For later applications, it will be useful to consider
\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)
, which hence is a holomorphic symmetric tensor of type
(3,0)
.

It is possible to give a characterisation of ASK manifolds in terms of
\widetilde{\eta}
,
[
Fre99
, Proposition 1.34 (p. 39)]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
conic special Kähler
(CSK) manifold is the data of a pseudo-Kähler manifold
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g},I,\widetilde{\omega})
with a flat, symplectic connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
and a vector field
\xi
such that

1.

\xi
is nowhere vanishing;

2.

\widetilde{g}
is negative definite on
\left\langle{\xi,I\xi}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
and positive definite on its orthogonal complement;

3.

\widetilde{\nabla}\xi=\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\xi=\mathrm{id}
;

4.

\widetilde{\nabla}(I\xi)=\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}(I\xi)=I
.

We adopt the convention
\widetilde{\omega}=\widetilde{g}(I\cdot,\cdot)
.

Remark 3.4
.

Conical special Kähler manifolds are automatically affine special Kähler. Torsion-freeness follows from the computation

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y-\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X-[X,Y]=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\xi-\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\xi-\widetilde{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}\xi=R^{\widetilde{\nabla}}(X,Y)\xi=0.

(3.5)

Meanwhile,
(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y=(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}I)X
follows from the computation

\begin{split}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}I)Y-(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}I)X&=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}(IY)-I\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y-\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}(IX)+I\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}X\\
&=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}(IY)-\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}(IX)-I[X,Y]\\
&=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}(I\xi)-\widetilde{\nabla}_{Y}\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}(I\xi)-\widetilde{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}(I\xi)\\
&=R^{\widetilde{\nabla}}(X,Y)I\xi=0.\end{split}

(3.6)

Thus, Definition
3.3
implies Definition 3 in
[
CHM12
]
if we take
-\widetilde{g}
as the metric.
The opposite implication follows from
[
Man21
, Lemma 2.3 (p. 2647)]
.

Remark 3.5
.

The conic special structure may also be described in terms of the vector fields

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{1}{2}(\xi-\mathrm{i}I\xi),

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta}=\frac{1}{2}(\xi+\mathrm{i}I\xi),

(3.7)

in the complexified tangent bundle.
In particular, the defining properties 3 and 4 of CSK manifolds become

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\zeta=\nabla_{X}^{\widetilde{g}}\zeta=X^{1,0},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\zeta}=\nabla_{X}^{\widetilde{g}}\overline{\zeta}=X^{0,1}.

(3.8)

Remark 3.6
.

The holomorphic difference tensor field
\widetilde{\eta}
of a CSK manifold is horizontal, since

\begin{split}\widetilde{\eta}_{Y}\zeta&=\widetilde{\nabla}^{1,0}_{Y}\zeta-(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}^{1,0}\zeta=Y^{1,0}-Y^{1,0}=0,\\
\widetilde{\eta}_{Y}\overline{\zeta}&=\widetilde{\nabla}^{1,0}_{Y}\overline{\zeta}-(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}^{1,0}\overline{\zeta}=0-0=0.\end{split}

(3.9)

In this paper, we will only consider CSK manifolds for which
\xi
and
I\xi
generate a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action. This property allows us to take a symplectic reduction with respect to the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action generated by
I\xi
.

Definition 3.7
.

A
projective special Kähler
(PSK) manifold is a Kähler manifold
M
endowed with a principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
with
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g},I,\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\nabla},\xi)
conic special Kähler such that
\xi
and
I\xi
are the fundamental vector fields associated to
1,\mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{C}
respectively and
M
is the Kähler quotient with respect to the induced
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action.
In this case, we say that
M
has a projective special Kähler structure.
We call
\xi
the
Euler vector field
.

Definition 3.8
.

A
PSK isomorphism
i.e. an isomorphism between PSK manifolds
(\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M,\widetilde{g},I,\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\nabla},\xi)
and
(\pi^{\prime}\colon\widetilde{M^{\prime}}\rightarrow M^{\prime},\widetilde{g}^{\prime},I^{\prime},\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime},\widetilde{\nabla}^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
is a pair
(\widetilde{\psi},\psi)
consisting of (bijective) Kähler isometries
\widetilde{\psi}\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
and
\psi\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{M}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\psi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

(3.10)

Moreover,
\widetilde{\psi}
is compatible with the connections and the Euler vector fields.
Stated in a more explicit manner,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}^{\prime}_{\widetilde{\psi}}\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{*}=\widetilde{\psi}_{*}\circ\widetilde{\nabla},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{*}\xi=\xi^{\prime}_{\widetilde{\psi}}\ .

(3.11)

A PSK isomorphism is called a
PSK automorphism
when the PSK data of the two PSK manifolds coincide.

For brevity, we will often denote a PSK manifold as simply
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
and suppress the remaining structure.

Remark 3.9
.

The bundle
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
has a principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-connection
\chi
induced by the metric
\widetilde{g}
via

\chi=\frac{\widetilde{g}(\xi,\cdot)+\mathrm{i}\widetilde{g}(I\xi,\cdot)}{\widetilde{g}(\xi,\xi)}.

(3.12)

Therefore, in terms of the principal connection, the conditions
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\xi=\mathrm{id}
and
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}(I\xi)=I
are equivalent to the condition that the metric
\widetilde{g}
is of the form

\widetilde{g}=-\widetilde{g}(\xi,\xi)(\pi^{*}g_{M}-\overline{\chi}\chi)=-\widetilde{h}(\overline{\zeta},\zeta)(\pi^{*}g_{M}-\overline{\chi}\chi),

(3.13)

where
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{g}+\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}
.

Note that
\widetilde{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Im}(\chi)
restricts to a principal connection on the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
S\rightarrow M
obtained by taking the level set
g(\xi,\xi)=-1
.

If we further let
r^{2}=-\widetilde{g}(\xi,\xi)
, then
\widetilde{g}
is a conical metric on the cone
S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
over
S
, that is

\widetilde{g}=r^{2}g_{M}-r^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}-\mathrm{d}r^{2}.

(3.14)

Remark 3.10
.

From the bundle
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
, we can build the complex line bundle associated to the standard
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-representation on
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.

L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{M}\times_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow M.

(3.15)

Since
\pi
is holomorphic,
L
inherits a holomorphic structure.
Moreover,
L
also has a hermitian structure
h_{L}
associated to
r^{2}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}
.

Notice that, using the map
u\in\widetilde{M}\mapsto[u,1]\in L
, we can regard
\widetilde{M}
as
L
with the zero section removed.
In this interpretation, given a local section
s
of
L
, the function
h_{L}(\overline{s},s)
is the pullback of
r^{2}
along
s
.

Finally, the Chern connection for
L
is precisely the linear connection
\nabla^{\chi}
associated to the principal connection
\chi
, i.e. the one defined by

\nabla^{\chi}\Phi_{L}=\chi\otimes\Phi_{L}.

(3.16)

3.2
Formulas and properties

In this section, we establish certain formulas and properties of PSK manifolds that we shall be making use of later.

Lemma 3.11
.

The principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-connection on a PSK manifold
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
defined in Remark
3.9
satisfies

\widetilde{\nabla}\chi=\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\chi=-\chi^{2}-\pi^{*}h_{M},

(3.17)

where
h_{M}=g_{M}+\mathrm{i}\omega_{M}
.

Proof.

We first write
\chi
as

\chi=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,(\widetilde{\omega}(I\xi,\cdot)-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}(\xi,\cdot)).

(3.18)

Taking its covariant derivative, we get

\begin{split}\widetilde{\nabla}\chi&=\widetilde{\nabla}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(I\xi,\cdot)-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}(\xi,\cdot)}{r^{2}}\right)=-2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\,\otimes\chi+\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(\widetilde{\nabla}(I\xi),\cdot)-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}(\widetilde{\nabla}\xi,\cdot)}{r^{2}}\\
&=-(\chi+\overline{\chi})\otimes\chi+\frac{\widetilde{\omega}(I,\cdot)-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}}{r^{2}}=-(\chi+\overline{\chi})\otimes\chi-\frac{\widetilde{h}}{r^{2}}\\
&=-\chi^{2}-\frac{\widetilde{h}+r^{2}\overline{\chi}\otimes\chi}{r^{2}}=-\chi^{2}-\pi^{*}h_{M}.\end{split}

(3.19)

The formula for
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
is deduced similarly.
∎

Since
\widetilde{\nabla}
is torsion-free, the alternating part of
2\widetilde{\nabla}\chi
is
\mathrm{d}\chi
.
Thus, as a corollary of the above computation we have
\mathrm{d}\chi=-2\mathrm{i}\pi^{*}\omega_{M}
.
The factor of
-2\mathrm{i}
may seem strange when this is compared to the curvature of a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-connection, but the
\mathrm{i}
is due to the fact that the (infinitesimal)
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action is given by the imaginary part of the (infinitesimal)
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action, while the
-2
is due to the choice of level set in the Kähler quotient by which
\omega_{M}
is defined.

Lemma 3.12
.

The principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-connection on a PSK manifold
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\chi=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\chi=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\overline{\chi}=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\overline{\chi}=0.

(3.20)

Proof.

The formulas for
\chi
follow from the Cartan formula, i.e.

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\chi&=\mathrm{d}(\iota_{\zeta}\chi)+\iota_{\zeta}\mathrm{d}\chi=\mathrm{d}(1)-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{\zeta}\pi^{*}\omega=0,\\
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\chi&=\mathrm{d}(\iota_{\overline{\zeta}}\chi)+\iota_{\overline{\zeta}}\mathrm{d}\chi=\mathrm{d}(0)-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{\overline{\zeta}}\pi^{*}\omega=0.\end{split}

(3.21)

The formulas for
\overline{\chi}
follow similarly.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Given a PSK manifold
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
, the Lie derivatives of the connections
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
on
\widetilde{M}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{\nabla}=-\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{\nabla}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}I.

(3.22)

Proof.

First of all, we derive an expression for the Lie derivative of a torsion-free connection:

\begin{split}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla)_{Y}Z&=\mathcal{L}_{X}(\nabla_{Y}Z)-\nabla_{\mathcal{L}_{X}Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}(\mathcal{L}_{X}Z)\\
&=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{\nabla_{Y}Z}X-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{Z}X\\
&=R^{\nabla}(X,Y)Z+\nabla^{2}_{Y,Z}X.\end{split}

(3.23)

In particular, the Lie derivative of a torsion-free connection is tensorial.

In the case of
\widetilde{\nabla}
, the curvature
R^{\widetilde{\nabla}}
vanishes, so we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{\nabla}&=\widetilde{\nabla}(\widetilde{\nabla}\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}(\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}I)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}I,\\
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{\nabla}&=\widetilde{\nabla}(\widetilde{\nabla}\overline{\zeta})=\frac{1}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}(\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}I)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}I.\end{split}

(3.24)

In the case of
\widetilde{\nabla}
, we instead make use of the fact that
\zeta
and
\overline{\zeta}
are conformal Killing vector fields, as we can see by computing

\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{g}=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{g}.

(3.25)

We may now use the fact that the Levi-Civita connection preserves the metric to deduce that, for any vector field
X
such that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\widetilde{g}=c\widetilde{g}
for some constant
c
, the action of
\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
as a derivation on
\widetilde{g}
is

(\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})\widetilde{g}=\mathcal{L}_{X}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\widetilde{g})-\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\widetilde{g})=\mathcal{L}_{X}(0)-c\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\widetilde{g}=0.

(3.26)

More explicitly, this means that for any conformal Killing vector field
X
, the tensor field
\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
satisfies

\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}Z,W\right)+\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}W,Z\right)=0,

(3.27)

for arbitrary vector fields
Y
,
Z
,
W
.
Meanwhile, by torsion-freeness,
(\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}Z
is symmetric in
Y
and
Z
.
Combining these observations, we see that

\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}Z,W\right)=-\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}W,Z\right)=-\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{W}Y,Z\right).

(3.28)

This is a circular permutation in
Y
,
Z
, and
W
, which changes the overall sign.
Applying this permutation thrice, we obtain

\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}Z,W\right)=-\widetilde{g}\left((\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}})_{Y}Z,W\right)=0.

(3.29)

By non-degeneracy of
\widetilde{g}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{L}_{X}\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}=0
.
∎

As a corollary of the above result, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{\eta}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}^{1,0}I=\widetilde{\eta},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{\eta}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\widetilde{\nabla}^{1,0}I=-\widetilde{\eta}.

(3.30)

In particular, we can compute the Lie derivatives of the tensor field
\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat}
, obtaining

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}(\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat})&=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}(\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot))=(\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{g})(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)+\widetilde{g}(\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)=2\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)=2\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}(\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat})&=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}(\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot))=(\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{g})(\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)+\widetilde{g}(\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\widetilde{\eta},\cdot)=0.\end{split}

(3.31)

Thus
\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat}
varies quadratically with respect to the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\widetilde{M}
. We have already seen that it is horizontal.
As a consequence, we get a (linear) bundle map
L\otimes L\rightarrow S_{3,0}M
. Raising the second index using the metric
g_{M}
on
M
then gives us a map

\eta\colon L\otimes L\longrightarrow\sharp_{2}S_{3,0}M.

(3.32)

This map was constructed in
[
Man21
, Proposition 6.5 (p. 16)]
, where it was denoted by
\widehat{\gamma}
, and referred to as the
intrinsic deviance
. Explicitly, we have

\widetilde{\eta}^{\flat}=\pi^{*}(g_{M}(\eta(\Phi_{L},\Phi_{L}),\cdot)).

(3.33)

Lemma 3.14
.

Given a PSK manifold
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
and vector fields
X,Y
on
\widetilde{M}
, we have the equation

\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y=\pi_{*}\big(\chi(X)Y^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(X)Y^{0,1}+\chi(Y)X^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(Y)X^{0,1}\big)

(3.34)

holding on the pullback bundle
(TM)_{\pi}\to\widetilde{M}
.

Proof.

Notice that both sides of (
3.34
) are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!{({\widetilde{M}})}
-linear in
X
and
Y
, so it is enough to verify the formula pointwise.
Consider thus the tensor

2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big),

(3.35)

for a vector field
Z^{\prime}
on
M
.
Let
Z=(Z^{\prime})^{\chi}
be the horizontal lift of
Z^{\prime}
, then

\begin{split}&2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)=2\pi^{*}g\big(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y),Z\big)-2g\big(\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\qquad=2\,(r^{-2}\widetilde{g}+\overline{\chi}\chi)(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y,Z)-2g(\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime})\\
&\qquad=\frac{2}{r^{2}}\,\widetilde{g}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y,Z)-2g(\nabla^{g}_{\pi_{*}X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}).\end{split}

(3.36)

Being tensorial,
X
and
Y
can be assumed to be
\pi
-related to vector fields on
M
.
So let
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}{\left({{M}}\right)}
be such that
\pi_{*}X=X^{\prime}_{\pi}
and
\pi_{*}Y=Y^{\prime}_{\pi}
.
We now obtain

2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)=\frac{2}{r^{2}}\,\widetilde{g}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y,Z)-2g(\nabla^{g}_{X^{\prime}}Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})_{\pi}.

(3.37)

By the Koszul formula for
\widetilde{g}
and
g
, this expression can be written as

\begin{split}&\frac{2}{r^{2}}\big(X(\widetilde{g}(Y,Z))+Y(\widetilde{g}(Z,X))-Z(\widetilde{g}(X,Y))\\
&\quad+\widetilde{g}([Z,X],Y)+\widetilde{g}([X,Y],Z)-\widetilde{g}([Y,Z],X)\big)\\
&\quad-X^{\prime}(g(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}))_{\pi}-Y^{\prime}(g(Z^{\prime},X^{\prime}))_{\pi}+Z(g(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))_{\pi}\\
&\quad-g([Z^{\prime},X^{\prime}],Y^{\prime})_{\pi}-g([X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}],Z^{\prime})_{\pi}+g([Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}],X^{\prime})_{\pi}.\end{split}

(3.38)

The last terms can be simplified thanks to the equalities

\displaystyle\begin{split}X^{\prime}(g(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}))_{\pi}&=\mathrm{d}(g(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}))_{\pi}(X^{\prime}_{\pi})=\pi^{*}\mathrm{d}(g(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime}))(X)\\
&=\mathrm{d}(g(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})_{\pi})(X)=\mathrm{d}(\pi^{*}g(Y,Z))(X)=X(\pi^{*}g(Y,Z)),\\
g([X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}],Z^{\prime})_{\pi}&=g_{\pi}(\pi_{*}[X,Y],\pi_{*}Z)=\pi^{*}g([X,Y],Z),\end{split}

(3.39)

and similarly for the other terms.
Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}&2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\quad=2\,\frac{X(\widetilde{g}(Y,Z))+Y(\widetilde{g}(Z,X))-Z(\widetilde{g}(X,Y))}{r^{2}}+\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{g}}{r^{2}}-\pi^{*}g\bigg)([Z,X],Y)\\
&\quad\quad+\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{g}}{r^{2}}-\pi^{*}g\bigg)([X,Y],Z)-\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{g}}{r^{2}}-\pi^{*}g\bigg)([Y,Z],X)\\
&\quad\quad-X(\pi^{*}g(Y,Z))-Y(\pi^{*}g(Z,X))+Z(\pi^{*}g(X,Y)).\end{split}

(3.40)

Using the expression
\widetilde{g}=r^{2}(\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)
, and the
\chi
-horizontality of
Z
, we deduce

\begin{split}&2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\quad=2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(X)(\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(Y,Z)+2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(Y)(\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(Z,X)\\
&\quad\quad-2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(Z)(\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(X,Y)+X((\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(Y,Z))\\
&\quad\quad+Y((\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(Z,X))-Z((\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(X,Y))\\
&\quad\quad-\overline{\chi}\chi([Z,X],Y)-\overline{\chi}\chi([X,Y],Z)+\overline{\chi}\chi([Y,Z],X)\\
&\quad\quad-X(\pi^{*}g(Y,Z))-Y(\pi^{*}g(Z,X))+Z(\pi^{*}g(X,Y))\\
&\quad=2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(X)\pi^{*}g(Y,Z)+2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(Y)\pi^{*}g(Z,X)-2\,\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}(Z)(\pi^{*}g-\overline{\chi}\chi)(X,Y)\\
&\quad\quad+Z(\overline{\chi}\chi(X,Y))-\overline{\chi}\chi([Z,X],Y)+\overline{\chi}\chi([Y,Z],X).\end{split}

(3.41)

We know that
\mathrm{d}r/r
is the real part of
\chi
, thus

\begin{split}&2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\qquad=(\chi+\overline{\chi})(X)\pi^{*}g(Y,Z)+(\chi+\overline{\chi})(Y)\pi^{*}g(Z,X)+0\\
&\quad\quad+Z(\overline{\chi}\chi(X,Y))-\overline{\chi}\chi([Z,X],Y)+\overline{\chi}\chi([Y,Z],X)\\
&\qquad=(\chi+\overline{\chi})(X)\pi^{*}g(Y,Z)+(\chi+\overline{\chi})(Y)\pi^{*}g(Z,X)+\mathcal{L}_{Z}(\overline{\chi}\chi)(X,Y).\end{split}

(3.42)

We now need the Lie derivatives of
\chi
,
\overline{\chi}
, and
\overline{\chi}\chi
.
By the Cartan formula,

\mathcal{L}_{Z}\chi=\iota_{Z}d\chi+d\iota_{Z}\chi=-2\mathrm{i}\iota_{Z}\pi^{*}\omega,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{Z}\overline{\chi}=\iota_{Z}d\overline{\chi}+d\iota_{Z}\overline{\chi}=2\mathrm{i}\iota_{Z}\pi^{*}\omega.

(3.43)

The Lie derivative for
\overline{\chi}\chi
then follows from a short computation:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{Z}(\overline{\chi}\chi)(X,Y)&=(\mathcal{L}_{Z}\overline{\chi}\chi+\overline{\chi}\mathcal{L}_{Z}\chi)(X,Y)\\
&=(2\mathrm{i}(\iota_{Z}\pi^{*}\omega)\chi-2\mathrm{i}\overline{\chi}(\iota_{Z}\pi^{*}\omega))(X,Y)\\
&=\mathrm{i}\pi^{*}\omega(Z,X)\chi(Y)+\mathrm{i}\pi^{*}\omega(Z,Y)\chi(X)\\
&\quad-\mathrm{i}\overline{\chi}(X)\pi^{*}\omega(Z,Y)-\mathrm{i}\overline{\chi}(Y)\pi^{*}\omega(Z,X))\\
&=-\mathrm{i}(\chi-\overline{\chi})(X)\pi^{*}g(IY,Z)-\mathrm{i}(\chi-\overline{\chi})(Y)\pi^{*}g(IX,Z).\end{split}

(3.44)

Substituting the Lie derivatives we just computed into (
3.42
), we get

\begin{split}&2g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{X}Y)-\nabla^{g}_{X}\pi_{*}Y,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\qquad=(\chi+\overline{\chi})(X)\pi^{*}g(Y,Z)+(\chi+\overline{\chi})(Y)\pi^{*}g(X,Z)\\
&\qquad\quad-\mathrm{i}(\chi-\overline{\chi})(X)\pi^{*}g(IY,Z)-\mathrm{i}(\chi-\overline{\chi})(Y)\pi^{*}g(IX,Z)\\
&\qquad=\chi(X)\pi^{*}g(Y-\mathrm{i}IY,Z)+\overline{\chi}(X)\pi^{*}g(Y+\mathrm{i}IY,Z)\\
&\qquad\quad+\chi(Y)\pi^{*}g(X-\mathrm{i}IX,Z)+\overline{\chi}(Y)\pi^{*}g(X+\mathrm{i}IX,Z)\\
&\quad\quad=2\pi^{*}g\big(\chi(X)Y^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(X)Y^{0,1}+\chi(Y)X^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(Y)X^{0,1},Z\big).\end{split}

(3.45)

Hence,

\begin{split}&g\big(\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}X)-\nabla^{g}_{\pi_{*}Y}\pi_{*}X,Z^{\prime}\big)\\
&\qquad=g\big(\pi_{*}\big(\chi(X)Y^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(X)Y^{0,1}+\chi(Y)X^{1,0}+\overline{\chi}(Y)X^{0,1}\big),Z^{\prime}).\end{split}

(3.46)

This proves the lemma, since
g
is non-degenerate and
Z^{\prime}
arbitrary.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
be a PSK manifold with special connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
.
Then, the
\widetilde{\nabla}
-horizontal lifts of the fundamental vector fields
\xi
and
I\xi
to
T\widetilde{M}
generate a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
T\widetilde{M}
.
Moreover, a vector field
X
on
\widetilde{M}
is invariant under this action if and only if

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}X=\widetilde{\nabla}_{I\xi}X=0.

(3.47)

Proof.

Let us denote the principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\widetilde{M}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{\lambda}(u)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}u\cdot\lambda,

\displaystyle\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

(3.48)

Now consider the following principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
T\widetilde{M}
:

X_{u}\longmapsto\lambda^{-1}(\rho_{\lambda})_{*}X_{u}\in(TM)_{\rho_{\lambda}(u)}.

(3.49)

This action is generated by vector fields
Y^{1}
and
Y^{\mathrm{i}}
given by

\begin{split}Y^{1}_{X_{u}}&=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(e^{-t}(\rho_{e^{t}})_{*}X_{u})\right|_{t=0}=\left.\left((\rho_{e^{t}})_{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(e^{-t}X_{u})+e^{-t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}((\rho_{e^{t}})_{*}X_{u})\right)\right|_{t=0}\\
&=-\mathrm{vert}(\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}})_{X_{u}}+\xi^{T}_{X_{u}},\\
Y^{\mathrm{i}}_{X_{u}}&=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(e^{-\mathrm{i}t}(\rho_{e^{\mathrm{i}t}})_{*}X_{u})\right|_{t=0}=\left.\left((\rho_{e^{\mathrm{i}t}})_{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(e^{-\mathrm{i}t}X_{u})+e^{-\mathrm{i}t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}((\rho_{e^{\mathrm{i}t}})_{*}X_{u})\right)\right|_{t=0}\\
&=-\mathrm{vert}(I\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}})_{X_{u}}+(I\xi)^{T}_{X_{u}}.\end{split}

(3.50)

In the previous equation, we implicitly exploited the fact that (
3.49
) is the composition of two commuting actions: a scalar multiplication and a pushforward.
Meanwhile, by the splitting of the complete lift in (
2.16
), we have

\begin{split}\xi^{T}&=\xi^{\widetilde{\nabla}}+\mathrm{vert}\big((\widetilde{\nabla}\xi)\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}}\big)=\xi^{\widetilde{\nabla}}+\mathrm{vert}\big(\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}}\big),\\
(I\xi)^{T}&=(I\xi)^{\widetilde{\nabla}}+\mathrm{vert}\big((\widetilde{\nabla}(I\xi))\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}}\big)=(I\xi)^{\widetilde{\nabla}}+\mathrm{vert}\big(I\Phi_{T\widetilde{M}}\big).\end{split}

(3.51)

Thus, we find that
Y^{1}=\xi^{\widetilde{\nabla}}
and
Y^{\mathrm{i}}=(I\xi)^{\widetilde{\nabla}}
, so the horizontal lifts indeed generate a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
T\widetilde{M}
.

Moreover, from our earlier discussion on connections in §
2.3
, we know that the flows of the horizontal lifts of
\xi
and
I\xi
are precisely the parallel transport along the integral curves of
\xi
and
I\xi
. A section
X
of
TM
is invariant under such parallel transport if and only if
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}X
and
\widetilde{\nabla}_{I\xi}X
vanish.
∎

3.3
Variations of Hodge structure

We will now build up the definition of variation of polarised Hodge structure in several steps. Although the definitions are motivated by the cohomology groups of a non-singular complex variety and their deformation theory, we take an abstract differential-geometric approach along the lines of
[
Sim88
]
. In particular, we relax the integrality condition that is often included as part of the definition of Hodge structures.

Definition 3.16

(Hodge structure)
.

A Hodge structure on a real vector space
V
of weight
d\in\mathbb{Z}
is a decomposition of its complexification

V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q=d}V^{p,q}

(3.52)

satisfying
\overline{V^{p,q}}=V^{q,p}
.

We will only be considering Hodge structures of positive weight such that
V^{p,q}=0
whenever either
p
or
q
is negative.

Example 3.17
.

Let
K
be a compact Kähler manifold. Then the Dolbeault theorem tells us that the
d
-th de Rham cohomology
H^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K)
admits a Hodge structure of weight
d
, namely

H^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K;\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=d}H^{q}(K;\Omega^{p})=\bigoplus_{p+q=d}H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(K),

(3.53)

where
\Omega^{p}
denotes the sheaf of holomorphic
p
-forms and
\overline{\partial}
denotes the Dolbeault operator.

Definition 3.18

(Polarised Hodge structure)
.

A polarised Hodge structure of weight
d
on a real vector space
V
, denoted by
(V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus V^{p,q},Q)
, is the data of a Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus V^{p,q}
of weight
d
on
V
together with a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form
Q
on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

\displaystyle Q(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}Q(v_{2},v_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ for }v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{\mathbb{C}};

\displaystyle Q(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ for }v_{1}\in V^{p,q},v_{2}\in V^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},p\neq q^{\prime};

(3.54)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}^{p-q}Q\left(\overline{v},v\right)

\displaystyle>0

\displaystyle\text{ for }v\in V^{p,q},\ v\neq 0.

Example 3.19
.

Let the compact Kähler manifold
K
considered in Example
3.17
be of complex dimension
n
.
Then the cohomology group
H^{d}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K)
, with
d\leq n
, has a natural bilinear pairing given by

Q(\alpha,\beta)=\int_{K}\frac{1}{(n-d)!}\omega^{n-d}\wedge\beta\wedge\alpha.

(3.55)

The Lefschetz operator is compatible with this pairing, and therefore it induces a bilinear pairing on the subspace of primitive cohomology.
Notice that the primitive cohomology inherits a Hodge decomposition, and the induced bilinear pairing
Q
on it satisfies all three conditions in (
3.18
) (cf. §7.1.2 of
[
Voi07
]
), the last one being a consequence of the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations.

In particular, when
K
is a simply connected Calabi–Yau manifold of complex dimension
3
, all
3
-forms are primitive, so the whole of
H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K)
admits a polarised Hodge structure with the aforementioned pairing.

Next we will consider families of such polarised Hodge structures that are parametrised by some complex manifold.

Definition 3.20

(Variation of polarised Hodge structure)
.

A variation of polarised Hodge structure (VPHS)
(E_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus E^{p,q},Q,\nabla)
of weight
d
on a complex manifold
M
is a real vector bundle
E\rightarrow M
together with a flat connection
\nabla
, a
\nabla
-parallel bilinear form
Q\in\Gamma(E^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes E^{*}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and a decomposition of complex vector bundles

E_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q=d}E^{p,q},

(3.56)

such that the fibre
E_{m}
over a point
m\in M
carries a polarised Hodge structure
((E_{m})_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus E^{p,q}_{m},Q_{m})
of weight
d
and
\nabla
gives a map

\nabla\colon\Gamma(E^{p,q})\longrightarrow\Omega^{0,1}(E^{p+1,q-1})\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(E^{p,q})\oplus\Omega^{0,1}(E^{p,q})\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(E^{p-1,q+1}).

(3.57)

The connection
\nabla
is called
Gauß–Manin connection
, while (
3.57
) is called
Griffiths transversality condition
.

Example 3.21
.

Consider a holomorphic fibre bundle
K\to M
such that fibres are (simply connected, compact) Calabi–Yau
3
-folds.
This can be regarded as a family
\{K_{m}\}_{m\in M}
of Calabi–Yau
3
-folds holomorphically parametrised by points
m
of
M
.
The de Rham cohomology groups
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{3}(K_{m})
, then produce a bundle of polarised Hodge structures of weight
3
.

Moreover, for any contractible
U\subseteq M
, we can choose a family of submanifolds
C^{i}\subseteq K|_{U}
such that
C^{i}_{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}C^{i}\cap K_{m}
is a compact submanifold of
K_{m}
of real dimension
3
, and furthermore,
\{[C^{i}_{m}]\}_{i}
is a basis of homology classes of the free part of
H_{3}(K_{m})
.
This gives a dual basis of
3
-forms
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i}
in
H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K_{m})
.

Two different choices of basis
\{C^{i}_{m}\subset K_{m}\}_{i}
and
\{C^{\prime i}_{m}\subset K_{m}\}_{i}
are related by an integral (and hence locally constant) linear transformation.
Thus, the associated dual bases of
3
-forms
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i}
are also related by a locally constant linear transformation.
This then defines a flat connection on the bundle
\{H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K_{m})\}_{m\in M}
that preserves the natural polarisation
Q
.
Deducing the Griffiths transversality condition requires a bit more work, cf. §10.2.2 of
[
Voi07
]
.

On a variation of Hodge structure with odd weight, there are two natural complex structures
I_{\mathrm{G}}
and
I_{\mathrm{W}}
, respectively called the
Griffiths
and
Weil
complex structures, defined on a section
\sigma\in\Gamma(E^{p,q})
by

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{G}}X=\mathrm{sign}(p-q)\mathrm{i}X,

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{W}}X=\mathrm{i}^{p-q}X.

(3.58)

These, in turn, give rise to two Hermitian structures
h_{\mathrm{G}}
and
h_{\mathrm{W}}
respectively called the
Griffiths
and
Weil
hermitian forms defined by

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{G}}=Q(\cdot,I_{\mathrm{G}}\cdot)+\mathrm{i}Q,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{W}}=Q(\cdot,I_{\mathrm{W}}\cdot)+\mathrm{i}Q.

(3.59)

Notice that both forms have
Q
as imaginary part.

We will now show that the notion of PSK structure has an equivalent reformulation in terms of certain VPHS of weight
3
. We mainly follow the chain of reasoning given by Freed in
[
Fre99
, Proposition 4.6 (p. 46)]
, but work out the correspondence much more explicitly.

We first show that PSK structures
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
give rise to a certain VPHS of weight
3
on
M
.

Proposition 3.22
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
be a PSK manifold and let
L
be the holomorphic line bundle associated to the principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
described in Remark
3.10
.
Then
M
carries a VPHS of weight
3
given by the direct sum

L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)\oplus(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L}.

(3.60)

The Gauß–Manin connection is given by

\begin{split}&\nabla_{Y}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z+\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}+\overline{\varsigma}_{1})\\
&\qquad=\nabla_{Y}^{\chi}\sigma_{1}+h_{M}(Y^{0,1},Z)\sigma_{2}\\
&\qquad\quad+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+\sigma_{1}\otimes Y^{1,0}+(\overline{h}_{L}{}^{-1}\circ\overline{\eta})_{Y^{0,1}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})\\
&\qquad\quad+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi},g_{M}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})+\overline{\varsigma}_{1}\otimes Y^{0,1}+(h_{L}^{-1}\circ\eta)_{Y^{1,0}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)\\
&\qquad\quad+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi}}\overline{\varsigma}_{1}+h_{M}(W,Y^{1,0})\overline{\varsigma}_{2},\end{split}

(3.61)

where
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are sections of
L
;
Z
is a section of
T^{1,0}M
;
\overline{W}
is a section of
T^{0,1}M
;
\overline{\varsigma}_{1},\overline{\varsigma}_{2}
are sections of
\overline{L}
; and
Y
is a vector field on
M
.

Proof.

A PSK manifold
M
may be given in terms of a principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
which is CSK and so equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
.
Let
E
be the quotient of
T\widetilde{M}
by the
\widetilde{\nabla}
-horizontal lift of the principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\widetilde{M}
(cf. Lemma
3.15
).
The complexification
E_{\mathbb{C}}
is then the corresponding quotient of
T\widetilde{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
and forms a complex vector bundle over
M
.
We shall show that this is equipped with the Hodge decomposition as in (
3.60
).

In order to do so, we first need to describe the bundle
E_{\mathbb{C}}
more explicitly.
First of all, we prove that there is a bundle isomorphism

\phi\colon T\widetilde{M}_{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\left(L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)\oplus(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L}\right)_{\pi}.

(3.62)

Recall that we can identify
\widetilde{M}
with
L\smallsetminus\{0\}
, cf. Remark
3.10
.
With the above identification, it makes sense to talk of the tautological section
\Phi_{L}
of the pullback bundle
L_{\pi}
. We define
\phi
separately on the holomorphic and antiholomorphic part by the maps

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi^{1,0}=\chi\otimes\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}\colon T^{1,0}\widetilde{M}&\longrightarrow(L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M))_{\pi},\\
\phi^{0,1}=\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}+\overline{\chi}\otimes\overline{\Phi}_{L}\colon T^{0,1}\widetilde{M}&\longrightarrow((\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L})_{\pi},\end{split}

(3.63)

so that
\phi=\phi^{1,0}+\phi^{0,1}
.
Explicitly,

\phi(X)=\chi(X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{1,0}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{0,1}+\overline{\chi}(X)\overline{\Phi}_{L}.

(3.64)

We will now describe an inverse for
\phi^{1,0}
.
The tautological section
\Phi_{L}
is a global one for the bundle
L_{\pi}
, so, in order to describe a map from
(L\oplus L\otimes TM^{1,0})_{\pi}
to
T^{1,0}\widetilde{M}
, it is enough to see how it acts on
f\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes X
with
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!{({\widetilde{M},\mathbb{C}})}
and
X
a section of
(T^{1,0}M)_{\pi}
.
The inverse of
\phi^{1,0}
is given by

f\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes X\longmapsto f\zeta+X^{\chi}.

(3.65)

Analogously, we may show that
\phi^{0,1}
has the inverse

f\overline{\Phi}_{L}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes X\longmapsto f\overline{\zeta}+X^{\overline{\chi}}.

(3.66)

Thus,
\phi
is invertible.

We will now prove that
\phi
descends through the quotient to an isomorphism

E_{\mathbb{C}}\cong L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)\oplus(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L}.

(3.67)

Let us recall that two vector bundles are isomorphic if and only if their sheaves of sections are.
By Lemma
3.15
, we know that sections of
E
are in bijective correspondence with sections
X\in\Gamma(T\widetilde{M})
such that
\widetilde{\nabla}X
vanishes on the vertical vector fields
\xi
and
I\xi
. Equivalently,

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\zeta}X=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\overline{\zeta}}X=0.

(3.68)

This, in turn, is equivalent to saying that we have the Lie derivatives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}X=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\zeta}X-\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\zeta=-X^{1,0},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}X=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\overline{\zeta}}X-\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\zeta}=-X^{0,1}.

(3.69)

Applying
\pi_{*}
to these equations, we obtain for all
p\in M
, a system of differential equations for
\pi_{*}X
interpreted as a function
\widetilde{M}_{p}\to T_{p}M
.
By solving them, we can say that, given
u\in\widetilde{M}_{p}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we have

(\pi_{*}X)_{\lambda u}=\lambda^{-1}(\pi_{*}X^{1,0})_{u}+\overline{\lambda}{}^{-1}(\pi_{*}X^{0,1})_{u}.

(3.70)

The identification of
\widetilde{M}
as a subbundle of
L
also gives

(\Phi_{L})_{\lambda u}=\lambda u=\lambda(\Phi_{L})_{u}.

(3.71)

If (
3.69
) holds, then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}(\chi(X))=-\chi(X^{1,0})=-\chi(X)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}(\chi(X))=-\chi(X^{0,1})=0
, which imply

\chi(X)_{\lambda u}=\lambda^{-1}\chi(X)_{u}.

(3.72)

Similarly, with
\overline{\chi}
we obtain

\overline{\chi}(X)_{\lambda u}=\overline{\lambda}{}^{-1}\overline{\chi}(X)_{u}.

(3.73)

Combining (
3.72
), (
3.73
), (
3.71
) and (
3.70
), we get

\phi(X)_{\lambda u}=\phi(X)_{u}.

(3.74)

The same can be said for
\phi^{1,0}(X)
and
\phi^{0,1}(X)
.
In other words,
\phi(X)
is the pullback of some section of

L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)\oplus(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L}.

(3.75)

Thus, we may identify this bundle with
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
In particular, we get a Hodge decomposition for
E_{\mathbb{C}}
given by

E^{3,0}=L,\quad E^{2,1}=L\otimes T^{1,0}M,\quad E^{1,2}=\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M,\quad E^{0,3}=\overline{L}.

(3.76)

Next, we describe in terms of
\widetilde{\omega}
the bilinear form
Q
, which for a VPHS of degree
3
is skew-symmetric.
Note that the symplectic form
\widetilde{\omega}
is
\widetilde{\nabla}
-parallel, so it descends to a skew-symmetric bilinear form on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
We define
Q
to be this skew-symmetric bilinear form.

Let
h_{L}
and
h_{M}
be the Hermitian forms defined on
L
and
TM_{\mathbb{C}}
respectively.
Then,
-h_{L}+h_{L}\otimes h_{M}
may be regarded as a bilinear form on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Notice that

\begin{split}&(-h_{L}+h_{L}\otimes h_{M})(\phi(X),\phi(Y))\\
&\qquad\qquad=-\overline{\chi}(X)\chi(Y)h_{L}(\overline{\Phi}_{L},\Phi_{L})+h_{L}(\overline{\Phi}_{L},\Phi_{L})\pi^{*}h_{M}(X^{0,1},Y^{1,0})\\
&\qquad\qquad=-r^{2}\overline{\chi}(X)\chi(Y)+r^{2}\pi^{*}h_{M}(X,Y)=\widetilde{h}(X,Y).\end{split}

(3.77)

Thus,
\mathrm{Alt}(-h_{L}+h_{L}\otimes h_{M})
corresponds to
\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}
, from which we infer

Q=\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{Alt}(h_{L}-h_{L}\otimes h_{M}).

(3.78)

Since
h_{M}
and
h_{L}
are positive-definite, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}^{3-0}Q(\overline{s}_{1},s_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\,h_{L}(\overline{s}_{1},s_{1})>0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}^{2-1}Q(\overline{s}_{2},s_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\,(h_{L}\otimes h_{M})(\overline{s}_{2},s_{2})

(3.79)

for all local non-vanishing sections
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
of
L
and
L\otimes T^{1,0}M
respectively.

Finally, we describe the Gauß–Manin connection and show that it satisfies the Griffiths transversality condition. To describe the Gauß–Manin connection
\nabla
, we first show that any connection
\nabla^{\prime}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\nabla^{\prime}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\nabla^{\prime}I,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\nabla^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\,\nabla^{\prime}I,

(3.80)

is the pullback of some connection on
E
.

Let
X
be a vector field on
\widetilde{M}
descending to a section of
E
, and hence satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}X=-X^{1,0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}X=-X^{0,1}
.
Let
Y
be a vector field on
\widetilde{M}
such that
\pi_{*}Y
is the pullback of some section of
TM
, and thus
\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}Y=\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}Y=0
.
As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}X)&=(\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\nabla^{\prime})_{Y}X+\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}Y}X+\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}X)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}I)X-\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}X^{1,0}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}[\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}I,\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}]X-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}((\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}I)X)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}I)(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(X))=-(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(X))^{1,0},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}X)&=(\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}\nabla^{\prime})_{Y}X+\nabla^{\prime}_{\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}Y}X+\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(\mathcal{L}_{\overline{\zeta}}X)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}I)X-\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}X^{0,1}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}[\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}I,\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}]X-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}((\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}I)X)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{i}I)(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(X))=-(\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}(X))^{0,1}.\end{split}

(3.81)

So, the vector field
\nabla^{\prime}_{Y}X
also descends to a section of
E
.
Thus,
\nabla^{\prime}
is a pullback connection.

As both the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
as well as the special connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
satisfy the property (
3.80
), they are pullbacks of connections
\widehat{\nabla}
and
\nabla
on
E
.
As a local basis of
\widetilde{\nabla}
-parallel vector fields on
\widetilde{M}
descends to a local basis of sections of
E
which are
\nabla
-parallel, we conclude that
\nabla
is a flat connection. Moreover, as
Q
descends from
\widetilde{\omega}
, which is
\widetilde{\nabla}
-parallel, it follows that
Q
is
\nabla
-parallel.

The only thing remaining to be checked is that
\nabla
satisfies the Griffiths transversality condition. For this, we need to obtain an explicit expression for
\nabla
. In fact, it is more convenient to first obtain an explicit expression for
\widehat{\nabla}
. Note that by Lemma
3.11
and Lemma
3.14
, we have

\begin{split}&\phi^{1,0}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}X)\\
&=\chi(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}(\nabla_{Y}^{\widetilde{g}}X)^{1,0}\\
&=\mathrm{d}(\chi(X))(Y)\Phi_{L}-(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}\chi)(X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}(\nabla_{Y}^{\widetilde{g}}X)^{1,0}\\
&=\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}(\chi(X)\Phi_{L})-\chi(X)\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\Phi_{L}-(\nabla_{Y}^{\widetilde{g}}\chi)(X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}X^{1,0})\\
&=\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}(\chi(X)\Phi_{L})+(-\chi(X)\chi(Y)+\chi(Y)\chi(X)+\pi^{*}h_{M}(Y,X))\Phi_{L}\\
&\quad+\Phi_{L}\otimes\left(\nabla^{g_{M}}_{Y}\pi_{*}X^{1,0}+\pi_{*}\left(\chi(Y)X^{1,0}+\chi(X)Y^{1,0}\right)\right)\\
&=\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}(\chi(X)\Phi_{L})+\pi^{*}h_{M}(Y,X)\Phi_{L}+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\Phi_{L}\otimes X^{1,0})+\chi(X)\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}Y^{1,0},\end{split}

(3.82)

where
\nabla^{\chi,g_{M}}
is the tensor product of
\nabla^{\chi}
and
\nabla^{g_{M}}
.
Thus, if we define a connection
\widehat{\nabla}^{1,0}
on
L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)
to be given by

\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}^{1,0}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)=\nabla_{Y}^{\chi}\sigma_{1}+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+h_{M}(Y,Z)\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}\otimes Y^{1,0},

(3.83)

with
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(L)
and
Z\in\Gamma(T^{1,0}M)
being holomorphic sections, then we have

\phi^{1,0}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}_{Y}X)=\widehat{\nabla}^{1,0}_{Y}(\phi^{1,0}(X)).

(3.84)

This is therefore the
\phi^{1,0}
part of the connection
\widehat{\nabla}
induced on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
.
A similar computation on the
\phi^{0,1}
part tells us that the full connection
\widehat{\nabla}
is given by

\begin{split}&\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z+\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}+\overline{\varsigma}_{1})\\
&\qquad=\nabla_{Y}^{\chi}\sigma_{1}+h_{M}(Y,Z)\sigma_{2}+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+\sigma_{1}\otimes Y^{1,0}\\
&\qquad\quad+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi},g_{M}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})+\overline{\varsigma}_{1}\otimes Y^{0,1}+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi}}\overline{\varsigma}_{1}+h_{M}(W,Y)\overline{\varsigma}_{2}.\end{split}

(3.85)

We know that the holomorphic difference tensor
\widetilde{\eta}
descends to the intrinsic deviance
\eta\colon L\otimes L\rightarrow\sharp_{2}S_{3,0}M
. Using the Hermitian form
h_{L}^{-1}
on
L^{*}
to make an identification
L^{*}\cong\overline{L}
, we obtain maps

\begin{split}h_{L}^{-1}\circ\eta\in\Omega^{1,0}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(L\otimes T^{1,0}M,\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)),\\
\overline{h}_{L}{}^{-1}\circ\overline{\eta}\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M,L\otimes T^{1,0}M)).\end{split}

(3.86)

Thus, the Gauß–Manin connection
\nabla
on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
is given by

\begin{split}&\nabla_{Y}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z+\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}+\overline{\varsigma}_{1})\\
&\qquad=\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z+\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}+\overline{\varsigma}_{1})\\
&\qquad\quad+(h_{L}^{-1}\circ\eta)_{Y}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+(\overline{h}_{L}{}^{-1}\circ\overline{\eta})_{Y}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}).\end{split}

(3.87)

The fact that
\nabla
satisfies the Griffiths transversality condition may now be made manifest by rearranging the terms of the above expression as

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\otimes Z+\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W}+\overline{\varsigma}_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=\nabla_{Y}^{\chi}\sigma_{1}+h_{M}(Y^{0,1},Z)\sigma_{2}+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+\sigma_{1}\otimes Y^{1,0}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi},g_{M}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})+\overline{\varsigma}_{1}\otimes Y^{0,1}+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi}}\overline{\varsigma}_{1}+h_{M}(W,Y^{1,0})\overline{\varsigma}_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(h_{L}^{-1}\circ\eta)_{Y^{1,0}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+(\overline{h}_{L}{}^{-1}\circ\overline{\eta})_{Y^{0,1}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})

(3.88)

\displaystyle\qquad=\nabla_{Y}^{\chi}\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{1}\otimes Y^{1,0}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+h_{M}(Y^{0,1},Z)\sigma_{2}+\nabla_{Y}^{\chi,g_{M}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)+(h_{L}^{-1}\circ\eta)_{Y^{1,0}}(\sigma_{2}\otimes Z)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+(\overline{h}_{L}{}^{-1}\circ\overline{\eta})_{Y^{0,1}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi},g_{M}}(\overline{\varsigma}_{2}\otimes\overline{W})+h_{M}(W,Y^{1,0})\overline{\varsigma}_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\overline{\varsigma}_{1}\otimes Y^{0,1}+\nabla_{Y}^{\overline{\chi}}\overline{\varsigma}_{1}.\qed

A physical interpretation of the decomposition (
3.60
) was given in
[
BCOV94
, Equation (2.21) (p. 328)]
.
Moreover, (
3.61
) is equivalent to the Picard–Fuchs equations described in
[
CDF+97
, Equation (1.4) (p. 283)]
.

Remark 3.23
.

By the isomorphism (
3.62
), we have also proven that
T\widetilde{M}\cong E_{\pi}
.

Remark 3.24
.

On this VPHS, the Griffiths and Weil Hermitian forms are

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{G}}=-h_{L}+h_{L}\otimes h_{M},

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{W}}=\overline{h}_{L}+h_{L}\otimes h_{M}.

(3.89)

As expected, the imaginary part of the two forms are the same, whereas the real part of
h_{\mathrm{W}}
corresponds to the real part of
h_{\mathrm{G}}
via a signature change that makes it positive definite.

Now that we know how to obtain variations of polarised Hodge structure from PSK structures, we next show the converse, i.e. that VPHS of the above type give rise to a PSK structure
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
.

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
M
be a Kähler manifold with an integral Kähler form
\omega_{M}
equipped with a VPHS
(E_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus E^{p,q},Q,\nabla)
of weight
3
of the form

E_{\mathbb{C}}=L\oplus(L\otimes T^{1,0}M)\oplus(\overline{L}\otimes T^{0,1}M)\oplus\overline{L},

(3.90)

where
L
is a complex line bundle with curvature
-2\mathrm{i}\omega_{M}
.
Furthermore, suppose
\nabla
is real with respect to the canonical real structure and let the projection of
\nabla_{Y}\sigma
onto
L\otimes T^{1,0}M
be
\sigma\otimes Y^{1,0}
for any sections
\sigma
and
Y
of
L
and
TM
respectively.
Then,
M
admits a PSK structure.

Proof.

First of all, notice that by Griffiths transversality and the integrability of
I
on
M
, the exterior covariant derivative associated to
\nabla
splits as

\mathrm{d}^{\nabla}\colon\Omega^{p,q}(E_{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q}(E_{\mathbb{C}})\oplus\Omega^{p,q+1}(E),

(3.91)

for all
p,q
.
The flatness of
\nabla
implies that the anti-holomorphic projection
\overline{\partial}_{E}\colon\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}(E_{\mathbb{C}})\to\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet+1}(E_{\mathbb{C}})
squares to zero, making
\overline{\partial}_{E}
a Dolbeault operator on
E_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Moreover, again by (
3.57
), the restriction of
\overline{\partial}_{E}
to
L
-valued forms has image in
\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}(L)
, and thus, it defines a Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}_{L}
on
L
, giving it the structure of a holomorphic line bundle.
Furthermore, the bilinear form
-2\mathrm{i}Q
restricted to
\overline{L}\otimes L
gives a (positive-definite) hermitian form
h_{L}
on
L
.

Let
\nabla^{\chi}
be the linear connection on
L
induced by restricting
\nabla
to
L
.
In other words,
\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\sigma
is the restriction of
\nabla_{Y}\sigma
to
L
, for all
Y\in\mathfrak{X}{\left({{M}}\right)}
and
\sigma\in\Gamma(L)
.
By definition,
\nabla^{\chi}
is compatible with the holomorphic structure on
L
, and, since
\nabla
preserves
Q
, the connection
\nabla^{\chi}
preserves
h_{L}
.
In fact, for
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Gamma(L)
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}(h_{L}(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\sigma_{2}))=-2\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}(Q(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\sigma_{2}))=-2\mathrm{i}Q(\overline{\nabla_{Y}\sigma_{1}},\sigma_{2}))-2\mathrm{i}Q(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\nabla_{Y}\sigma_{2})\\
&\quad=h_{L}(\overline{\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\sigma_{1}},\sigma_{2})+h_{L}(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\sigma_{2})=h_{L}(\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\overline{\sigma}_{1},\sigma_{2})+h_{L}(\overline{\sigma}_{1},\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\sigma_{2}).\end{split}

(3.92)

The connection
\nabla^{\chi}
is thus the Chern connection on
L
.
From the hypotheses, we also have

\nabla_{Y}\sigma=\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\sigma+\sigma\otimes Y^{1,0}.

(3.93)

We now take
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
to be
L\rightarrow M
with the zero section removed and show that it has all the requisite structure.
Note that
\widetilde{M}
can be thought of as the frame bundle for
L
and so it is a principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle associated to the line bundle
L
. Let
\zeta
be its fundamental vector field.
Moreover, the Chern connection
\nabla^{\chi}
on
L
gives a principal connection
\chi
on the principal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle
\widetilde{M}
so that

\nabla^{\chi}_{Y}\Phi_{L}=\chi(Y)\Phi_{L}.

(3.94)

Consider the map
\phi\colon T\widetilde{M}\rightarrow E_{\pi}

given by the explicit formula

\begin{split}&\phi(X)=\chi(X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{1,0}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{0,1}+\overline{\chi}(X)\overline{\Phi}_{L}.\end{split}

(3.95)

This has an inverse

\phi^{-1}\colon E_{\pi}\rightarrow T\widetilde{M}

given by

\begin{split}&\phi^{-1}(f\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes Y^{1,0}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes Y^{0,1}+\overline{f}\overline{\Phi}_{L})\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=f\zeta+(Y^{1,0})^{\chi}+(Y^{0,1})^{\overline{\chi}}+\overline{f}\overline{\zeta}.\end{split}

(3.96)

Thus, we have an identification of vector bundles
T\widetilde{M}\cong E_{\pi}
over
\widetilde{M}
.
Under this identification, the bilinear form
Q
and connection
\nabla
define a
2
-form
\widetilde{\omega}
on
\widetilde{M}
and a connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
preserving it. Moreover, we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\zeta&=\phi^{-1}(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{L})=\phi^{-1}(\nabla^{\chi}_{X}\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{1,0})\\
&=\phi^{-1}(\chi(X)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}X^{1,0})=\chi(X)\zeta+(\pi_{*}X^{1,0})^{\chi}=X^{1,0}.\end{split}

(3.97)

A similar argument shows
\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\zeta}=X^{0,1}
.

Finally,
\widetilde{M}
can be given the following hermitian structure

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{g}+\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}=q(\pi^{*}h_{M}-\overline{\chi}\otimes\chi),

\displaystyle q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}h_{L}(\overline{\Phi}_{L},\Phi_{L}).

(3.98)

In order to simplify the imminent calculations, we compute

\mathrm{d}q=h_{L}(\overline{\nabla^{\chi}\Phi_{L}},\Phi_{L})+h_{L}(\overline{\Phi}_{L},\nabla^{\chi}\Phi_{L})=h_{L}(\overline{\Phi}_{L},\Phi_{L})(\overline{\chi}+\chi)=q(\overline{\chi}+\chi).

(3.99)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\omega}&=\mathrm{d}(q(\pi^{*}\omega_{M}+\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\chi}\wedge\chi))\\
&=q(\overline{\chi}+\chi)\wedge(\pi^{*}\omega_{M}+\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\chi}\wedge\chi)+q(-\pi^{*}\omega_{M}\wedge\chi-\pi^{*}\omega_{M}\wedge\chi)=0,\\
\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{g}&=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}(q(\pi^{*}g_{M}-\overline{\chi}\chi))=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}q(\pi^{*}g_{M}-\overline{\chi}\chi)=\iota_{\zeta}\mathrm{d}q(\pi^{*}g_{M}-\overline{\chi}\chi)=\widetilde{g},\\
\mathrm{d}\iota_{\zeta}\widetilde{g}&=-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}(q\overline{\chi})=-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}q\wedge\overline{\chi}+q\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\chi})=-\frac{1}{2}\,q(\chi\wedge\overline{\chi}+2\mathrm{i}\pi^{*}\omega_{M})=-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}.\end{split}

(3.100)

Using the version of the Koszul formula in terms of the Lie derivative, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\widetilde{g}(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\zeta,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\widetilde{g}+\mathrm{d}\iota_{\zeta}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{g}-\mathrm{i}\widetilde{\omega}=\widetilde{g}(\mathrm{id}-\mathrm{i}I,\cdot)=2\widetilde{g}(\pi^{1,0},\cdot).

(3.101)

This implies
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\zeta=\pi^{1,0}
, and similarly we obtain
\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\overline{\zeta}=\pi^{0,1}
.

This then gives
\widetilde{M}
the structure of a CSK manifold.
Hence, we have a PSK structure
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
. ∎

The VPHS
(E_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus E^{p,q},Q,\nabla)
is completely determined by the choice of a Kähler structure
(g_{M},I,\omega_{M})
on
M
, a line bundle
L\rightarrow M
with Hermitian form
h_{L}
such that the curvature of its Chern connection is
-2\mathrm{i}\omega_{M}
, and an intrinsic deviance
\eta\in\Gamma(L^{*}\otimes L^{*}\otimes\sharp_{2}S_{3,0}M)
. The intrinsic deviance goes into the data of the Gauß–Manin connection
\nabla
through (
3.61
). The flatness condition then is equivalent to the following equations:

\displaystyle R^{g_{M}}+R_{\mathbf{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}+\langle h^{-1}_{L}\otimes h^{-1}_{L},[\eta\wedge\overline{\eta}]\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\chi,g_{M}}\eta=0.

(3.102)

Here,
\langle h^{-1}_{L}\otimes h^{-1}_{L},[\eta\wedge\overline{\eta}]\rangle
is to be interpreted as
(h_{L}^{-1}\otimes h_{L}^{-1}\otimes[\cdot\wedge\cdot])(\overline{\eta}\otimes\eta)
,

\mathrm{d}^{\chi,g_{M}}\colon\Omega^{1}(M,L^{*}\otimes L^{*}\otimes T^{0,1}M\otimes T^{*}_{1,0}M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{2}(M,(L^{*}\otimes L^{*}\otimes T^{0,1}M\otimes T^{*}_{1,0}M)

(3.103)

is the exterior covariant derivative with respect to
\nabla^{\chi,g_{M}}
, and
R_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
is formally the Riemann curvature of the complex projective space.
This is more or less the characterisation of PSK manifolds given in of
[
Man21
, Theorem 7.6 (p. 21)]
. Strictly speaking, the equations
D1
and
D2
appearing there are the pullback of the above equations along some section of
L
with unit norm.

Example 3.26
.

Let
M
be the complex hyperbolic space
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
(for a detailed definition see e.g.
[
Man21
, §9]
) and let
L
be its tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1)
(not to be confused with tautological pullback bundles). The curvature
R^{g_{M}}
is then
-R_{\mathbf{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}
, leaving us with the following equation for the intrinsic deviance
\eta
:

\displaystyle\langle h^{-1}_{L}\otimes h^{-1}_{L},[\eta\wedge\overline{\eta}]\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\chi,g_{M}}\eta=0.

(3.104)

Notice that
\eta=0
is a solution of the above (in fact the only one, see e.g.
[
Man21
, Proposition 9.3 (p. 31)]
). The resulting special connection
\nabla
pulls back to
E_{\pi}\cong T\widetilde{M}
to give a connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
that coincides with the Levi-Civita connection associated to the standard flat indefinite metric of signature
(2n,2)
on
\widetilde{M}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

Let us work this out more explicitly in terms of the standard coordinate chart
z=(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. We have the following:

\begin{split}&\widetilde{M}=\{(z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\mid 0<|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}<|z_{0}|^{2}\},\\
&\widetilde{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathrm{d}z_{i}|^{2}-|\mathrm{d}z_{0}|^{2},\quad\zeta=\sum_{i=0}^{n}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}},\quad\chi=\frac{\overline{z}_{0}\mathrm{d}z_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\mathrm{d}z_{i}}{|z_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.105)

The special connection
\widetilde{\nabla}=\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}
on
T\widetilde{M}
admits a basis of globally defined parallel sections
\{\partial_{z_{i}},\partial_{\overline{z}_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{n}
.
This induces an explicit basis of sections parallel with respect to the connection
\nabla
on
E
(which characterises the connection).

First of all, let us note that since
z_{0}
is nowhere vanishing on
\widetilde{M}
, we can define a global complex coordinate chart
X=(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}=(z_{i}/z_{0})_{i=1}^{n}
on
M=\widetilde{M}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Furthermore, the section
z_{0}^{-1}\Phi_{L}
of
L_{\pi}
may be seen to be the pullback of some section of
L
.
Let us define a section
\sigma
of
E_{\mathbb{C}}
so that

\sigma_{\pi}=\frac{z_{0}^{-1}}{\sqrt{1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|^{2}}}\,\Phi_{L}=\frac{z_{0}^{-1}}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}}\,\Phi_{L}.

(3.106)

The point of including the extra factor before
\Phi_{L}
is so that we have
h_{L}(\overline{\sigma},\sigma)=1
.
In terms of the section
\sigma
and coordinates
X_{i}
, the
\widetilde{\nabla}
-parallel sections
\{\partial_{z_{i}},\partial_{\overline{z}_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{n}
of
T\widetilde{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
correspond to the following
\nabla
-parallel sections of
E_{\mathbb{C}}
:

\begin{split}&s_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}}\,\sigma-\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}\,\sigma\otimes\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}\partial_{X_{j}},\\
&s_{j}=-\frac{\overline{X}_{j}}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}}\,\sigma+\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}\,\sigma\otimes\partial_{X_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,\\
&\overline{s}_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}}\,\overline{\sigma}-\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}\,\overline{\sigma}\otimes\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{X}_{j}\partial_{\overline{X}_{j}},\\
&\overline{s}_{j}=-\frac{X_{j}}{\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}}\,\overline{\sigma}+\sqrt{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}\,\overline{\sigma}\otimes\partial_{\overline{X}_{j}},\qquad j=1,\dots,n.\end{split}

(3.107)

Notice that these sections satisfy
Q(\overline{s}_{i},s_{j})=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}(-1)^{\delta_{i,0}}\delta_{ij}
, and for the Griffiths hermitian form we have

h_{\mathrm{G}}(\overline{s}_{i},s_{j})=Q(\overline{s}_{i},I_{\mathrm{G}}s_{j})+\mathrm{i}Q(\overline{s}_{i},s_{j})=2\mathrm{i}Q(\overline{s}_{i},s_{j})=(-1)^{\delta_{i,0}}\delta_{ij}.

(3.108)

We can finally write the Kähler structure on
M
with respect to the coordinate system
X=(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{M}&=\frac{\widetilde{h}}{-\widetilde{h}(\overline{\zeta},\zeta)}+\overline{\chi}\otimes\chi\\
&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}z_{i}-\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{0}\otimes\mathrm{d}z_{0}}{|z_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}|^{2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{(z_{0}\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i})\otimes(\overline{z}_{0}\mathrm{d}z_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{j}\mathrm{d}z_{j})}{(|z_{0}|^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}|^{2})^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{X}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}X_{i}}{1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{X_{i}\mathrm{d}\overline{X}_{i}\otimes\overline{X}_{j}\mathrm{d}X_{j}}{\left(1-\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.109)

4
The twist construction

In this section, we will show that under proper circumstances, the twist construction reduces to a quotient by the action of a finite cyclic group (Lemma
4.2
).
In addition, we explicitly determine how the pushforward of a vector field through the quotient map relates to its twist (Lemma
4.4
).

The
twist construction
was introduced by Swann in order to unify and generalise several differential-geometric constructions arising from T-duality in physics.
The construction takes as input the following
twist data
on a manifold
W
: a vector field
Z
generating a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action, an integral closed
2
-form
\omega
with respect to which
Z
is Hamiltonian, and a choice of a Hamiltonian function
f
that is nowhere vanishing.
Its output is another manifold
W^{\prime}
with a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action, together with a bijective correspondence between
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant tensor fields on
W
and
W^{\prime}
.

Very roughly, the way this is achieved is by building a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
P\rightarrow W
on top of
W
, lifting the given
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
W
to a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
(different from the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action), and then quotienting
P
by this lifted
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action.
The principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
then descends to the quotient
W^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
Swa10
]
Propositions 2.1, 2.3,
[
MS15
]
§4)
.

Given twist data
(Z,\omega,f)
, there is a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
p^{P}_{W}\colon P\rightarrow W
with principal connection
\theta_{P}
having curvature
\omega
and fundamental vector field
X_{P}
such that the lift

Z_{P}=Z^{\theta_{P}}+fX_{P}

(4.1)

of
Z
, generates a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
and so gives a well-defined quotient map

p^{P}_{W^{\prime}}\colon P\longrightarrow W^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}P/\langle Z_{P}\rangle,

(4.2)

such that the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
descends to a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
W^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, the
\theta_{P}
-horizontal lift of any
Z
-invariant vector field on
W
and the pullback of any
Z
-invariant function on
W
to
P
descend to a well-defined
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant vector field and a well-defined
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant function on
W^{\prime}
respectively.

The twist correspondence assigns to a
Z
-invariant vector field
X
on
W
the well-defined vector field
X^{\prime}
on
W^{\prime}
such that
(p^{P}_{W^{\prime}})_{*}X^{\theta_{P}}=X^{\prime}
.
The correspondence also assigns to a
Z
-invariant function
h
on
W
a unique function
h^{\prime}
on
W^{\prime}
such that the pullbacks of
h
and
h^{\prime}
to
P
agree.
We call
X^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}

twists
of
X
and
h
respectively. We write this as

\displaystyle\mathrm{tw}(X)=X^{\prime},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tw}(h)=h^{\prime}.

(4.3)

By stipulating compatibility with tensor products and contractions, this map can be extended to arbitrary
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant tensor fields. In particular, for differential forms
\alpha
on
W
and
\alpha^{\prime}
on
W^{\prime}
, we have
\mathrm{tw}(\alpha)=\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if
(p^{P}_{W})^{*}\alpha-(p^{P}_{W^{\prime}})^{*}\alpha^{\prime}
vanishes on
\theta_{P}
-horizontal vector fields.

Since we aim to show that for a certain class of twist data we can obtain the twisted manifold as a quotient by a discrete group, we will briefly recall some facts on this matter.

Given a principal
G
-bundle
\pi_{S}\colon S\to B
, and a normal subgroup
H\subseteq G
, the quotient
S/H
has an induced structure of a principal
G/H
-bundle.

We specialise to
G=\mathrm{U}(1)
and
H=\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, regarded as the group of
k
-th roots of unity in
\mathrm{U}(1)
. In this case, the quotient
\mathrm{U}(1)/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{U}(1)
itself via the isomorphism
[x]\mapsto x^{k}
. So,
S_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S/\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is also a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
\pi_{k}\colon S_{k}\to B
with the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on it given by

q(u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t}=q(u\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t/k}).

(4.4)

Here,
u
is a point in
S
and
q\colon S\rightarrow S_{k}
is the quotient map.

Notice also that a principal connection
\varphi
on
S
induces a unique principal connection
\varphi_{k}
on
S_{k}
, that is, it has the property that
q_{*}
maps horizontal vector fields to horizontal ones.
We now want to compare
\varphi
and
\varphi_{k}
, and the fundamental vector fields
Z
and
Z_{k}
on these two principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundles using the differential of the quotient map
q
.
Recall that the fundamental vector fields
Z
on
S
and
Z_{k}
on
S_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle Z_{u}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(u\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t})\right|_{t=0},

\displaystyle(Z_{k})_{q(u)}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(q(u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t})\right|_{t=0}.

(4.5)

Therefore, under the action of the differential
q_{*}
, we have

\begin{split}(q_{*}Z)_{u}&=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\,q(u\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t})\right|_{t=0}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(q(u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}kt})\right|_{t=0}=k\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}(q(u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}})\right|_{t^{\prime}=0}\\
&=k(Z_{k})_{q(u)}.\end{split}

(4.6)

In conclusion, we get

q_{*}Z=k(Z_{k})_{q},\qquad q^{*}\varphi_{k}=k\varphi,\qquad q_{*}Y^{\varphi}=(Y^{\varphi_{k}})_{q}\textrm{ for }Y\in\mathfrak{X}{\left({{B}}\right)},

(4.7)

where the second equation follows from
\varphi(Z)=1=\varphi_{k}(Z_{k})
, and the third from

\varphi_{k}(q_{*}(Y^{\varphi}))=q^{*}(\varphi_{k})(Y^{\varphi})=k\varphi(Y^{\varphi})=0.

(4.8)

In order to simplify the following statements, we extend the definition of
S_{k}
for
k=0
by declaring it to be the trivial bundle

\displaystyle S_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B\times\mathrm{U}(1),

\displaystyle\pi_{0}=\mathrm{pr}_{B}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B\times\mathrm{U}(1)\longrightarrow B.

(4.9)

Moreover, we also extend the objects

q(s)=(\pi_{S}(s),1)\in S_{0},\qquad Z_{0}=\partial_{t},\qquad\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{d}t,

(4.10)

where
t=t_{\pi_{0}}
is (the pullback of) the standard coordinate chart on
\mathrm{U}(1)
such that the elements of
\mathrm{U}(1)
are
e^{\mathrm{i}t}
.
Notice that the equalities (
4.7
) still hold and
q_{*}
still preserves horizontal vector fields.

We now consider the following instance of the twist construction, that we will make use of later.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi_{S}\colon S\rightarrow B
be a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle with connection
1
-form
\varphi
and fundamental vector field
Z
. Let
k
be a non-negative integer, and let
f
be a nowhere vanishing function and
\beta
be a
1
-form, both defined on
B
. Then the twist of
S
with respect to the twist data

\begin{split}(Z,\omega,\pi_{S}^{*}f):\!&=(Z,\mathrm{d}((\pi_{S}^{*}f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta),\pi_{S}^{*}f)\\
&=(Z,\mathrm{d}((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta),f)\end{split}

(4.11)

is the principal bundle
\pi_{k}\colon S_{k}\to B
.
Moreover,

\begin{split}&\mathrm{tw}(\pi_{S}^{*}\alpha)=\pi_{k}^{*}\alpha,\qquad\qquad\mathrm{tw}(\varphi)=-\frac{1}{f}(\varphi_{k}+\pi_{k}^{*}\beta),\\
&\mathrm{tw}(X^{\varphi})=X^{\varphi_{k}}-\beta(X)Z_{k},\qquad\qquad\mathrm{tw}(Z)=-fZ_{k},\end{split}

(4.12)

for all differential forms
\alpha
and vector fields
X
on
B
.

Proof.

As
\omega
is exact, the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
P
may be taken to be the trivial bundle
\mathrm{pr}_{S}\colon S\times\mathrm{U}(1)\rightarrow S
.
The connection form
\theta_{P}
and the fundamental vector field
X_{P}
may then be taken to be

\displaystyle\theta_{P}=\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)+\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle X_{P}=\partial_{\tau},

(4.13)

where
\tau
is the standard coordinate chart on
\mathrm{U}(1)
.
Since
P
is a product space, we have an identification

TP\cong(TS)_{\mathrm{pr}_{S}}\oplus(T\,\mathrm{U}(1))_{\mathrm{pr}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}}.

(4.14)

In terms of this identification, we may write the horizontal lift
Z^{\theta_{P}}
of
Z
as

Z^{\theta_{P}}=Z-\theta_{P}(Z)\partial_{\tau}=Z-(f+k)\partial_{\tau}.

(4.15)

Thus, the twisted lift
Z_{P}
is given by

Z_{P}=Z^{\theta_{P}}+fX_{P}=Z-k\partial_{\tau}.

(4.16)

Now we see that our choice of bundle
P
was the correct one, since
Z_{P}
generates a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P=S\times\mathrm{U}(1)
, namely

\left(s,u\right)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau}=\left(s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau},ue^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau}\right).

(4.17)

We now define a map
\varpi_{k}\colon P\rightarrow S_{k}
by
\varpi_{k}(s,u)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q(s)\cdot u
and show that it is a quotient map for the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action generated by
Z_{P}
.
When
k=0
, the quotient is a trivial bundle, and its quotient map is
\pi_{S}\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}=\varpi_{0}
.
When
k\neq 0
, we can say that
\varpi_{k}
is surjective and
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant by the equation

\varpi_{k}((s,u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau})=q(s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau})\cdot ue^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau}=q(s)\cdot u=\varpi_{k}(s,u).

(4.18)

Moreover, the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action is transitive on the fibres of
\varpi_{k}
.
Suppose now that
\varpi_{k}(s,u)=\varpi_{k}(s^{\prime},u^{\prime})
, and consider the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\times\mathrm{U}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pr}_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varpi_{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{k}}

(4.19)

The points
s
and
s^{\prime}
belong to the same fibre of
\pi_{S}
, and so
s^{\prime}=s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}q(s)\cdot u&=\varpi_{k}(s,u)=\varpi_{k}(s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau},u^{\prime})=q(s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau})\cdot u^{\prime}=q(s)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}k\tau}u^{\prime},\end{split}

(4.20)

and since the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
S_{k}
is principal, we infer that
u^{\prime}=ue^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau}
, so

(s^{\prime},u^{\prime})=(s\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau},ue^{-\mathrm{i}k\tau})=(s,u)\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau}.

(4.21)

Thus,
\varpi_{k}
is a quotient map, and
\pi_{k}\colon S_{k}\to B
is the twisted bundle.

Next, we describe the twist correspondence explicitly. It will be convenient to do this first for differential forms and then use the compatibility with contractions to obtain the correspondence for vector fields.

By (
4.19
), we have
\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\pi^{*}_{S}\alpha=\varpi_{k}^{*}\pi^{*}_{k}\alpha
for any form
\alpha
on
B
.
So, we automatically have
\mathrm{tw}(\pi^{*}_{S}\alpha)=\pi^{*}_{k}\alpha
.

In order to compute
\mathrm{tw}(\varphi)
, observe that
\varpi_{k}
is the composition of
q\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}
with the action map
\rho\colon S_{k}\times\mathrm{U}(1)\to S_{k}
, and moreover
\rho^{*}\varphi_{k}=\mathrm{pr}_{S_{k}}^{*}\varphi_{k}+\mathrm{pr}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}^{*}\mathrm{d}\tau
(cf.
[
Tu17
, Example 27.4 (p. 326)]
).
Therefore, we have

\varpi_{k}^{*}\varphi_{k}=\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}q^{*}\varphi_{k}+\mathrm{pr}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}^{*}\mathrm{d}\tau=k\,\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\varphi+\mathrm{pr}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}^{*}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(4.22)

Consider now the
Z_{P}
-invariant
1
-form

\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\varphi-\frac{1}{f}\theta_{P}=-\frac{1}{f}(k\,\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\varphi+\mathrm{pr}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}^{*}\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)=\varpi_{k}^{*}\left(-\frac{1}{f}(\varphi_{k}+\pi_{k}^{*}\beta)\right).

(4.23)

This agrees with
\mathrm{pr}_{S}^{*}\varphi
on
\theta_{P}
-horizontal vector fields, yielding

\mathrm{tw}(\varphi)=-\frac{1}{f}(\varphi_{k}+\pi_{k}^{*}\beta).

(4.24)

The twist correspondence for vector fields is determined by the fact that contractions with
1
-forms are preserved under the twist correspondence. We may thus check that

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}_{S}\alpha)(X^{\varphi})=\alpha(X)=(\pi^{*}_{k}\alpha)(X^{\varphi_{k}}-\beta(X)Z_{k}),

(4.25)

\displaystyle\varphi(X^{\varphi})=0=-\frac{1}{f}(\varphi_{k}(X^{\varphi_{k}}-\beta(X)Z_{k})+(\pi_{k}^{*}\beta)(X^{\varphi_{k}}-\beta(X)Z_{k})),

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}_{S}\alpha)(Z)=0=(\pi^{*}_{k}\alpha)(-fZ_{k}),

\displaystyle\varphi(Z)=1=-\frac{1}{f}(\varphi_{k}(-fZ_{k})+(\pi^{*}_{k}\beta)(-fZ_{k})).\qed

Remark 4.3
.

If
k\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary, one could still construct the bundle
P
so that the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action generated by
Z
lifts to a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
(generated by
Z_{P}
), but that bundle wouldn’t be trivial in general
[
Swa10
]
.
In case
k=\frac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
, with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, when
\frac{1}{b}\mathrm{d}\varphi
corresponds to an integral class, we have a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
S_{\frac{1}{b}}
such that
S
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{b}
-quotient of
S_{\frac{1}{b}}
.
If we now interpret
S_{\frac{1}{b}}
as a principal
\mathbb{Z}_{b}
-bundle over
S
, we can define
P
as the associated bundle
S_{\frac{1}{b}}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{b}}\mathrm{U}(1)
.
This exhibits
P
as a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle over
S
.
Furthermore,
Z_{P}
generates a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-action on
P
given by

[s,u]\cdot e^{\mathrm{i}\tau}=[se^{\mathrm{i}\tau},ue^{-\mathrm{i}a\tau}].

(4.26)

With respect to this action, the orbit containing an arbitrary element
[s,e^{\mathrm{i}\tau}]
, also contains the elements of the form
[se^{\frac{\mathrm{i}\tau}{a}},1]
.
Notice that we have the embedding

\displaystyle S_{\frac{1}{b}}\longrightarrow P,

\displaystyle s\longmapsto[s,1].

(4.27)

The image of this embedding then intersects the orbits of the action generated by
Z_{P}
in exactly
a
points, on which the
Z_{P}
-action induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{a}
-action.
Hence, the quotient
P/\langle Z_{P}\rangle
can be identified with the
\mathbb{Z}_{a}
-quotient of
S_{\frac{1}{b}}
, denoted
S_{\frac{a}{b}}=:S_{k}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\pi_{S}\colon S\rightarrow B
be a principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle with connection
1
-form
\varphi
and fundamental vector field
Z
.
Let
(Z,\omega,f)
and
\beta
be as in Lemma
4.2
, and furthermore assume that
\omega
is non-degenerate.
Let
Y
be a vector field on
S
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}f=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}\varphi=\mathcal{L}_{Y}\pi_{S}^{*}\beta=0.

(4.28)

Then,
Y
is
\omega
-Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function

f_{Y}=((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)(Y).

(4.29)

Moreover,
Y
is
Z
-invariant and its twist is given by

q_{*}Y=(\mathrm{tw}(Y)+f_{Y}Z_{k})_{q}.

(4.30)

Proof.

By the Cartan formula, we have

\begin{split}\iota_{Y}\omega&=\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}((f+k)\varphi+\pi^{*}_{S}\beta)=(\mathcal{L}_{Y}-\mathrm{d}\circ\iota_{Y})((f+k)\varphi+\pi^{*}_{S}\beta)\\
&=-\mathrm{d}\big(((f+k)\varphi+\pi^{*}_{S}\beta)(Y)\big)=-\mathrm{d}f_{Y}.\end{split}

(4.31)

Thus,
Y
is
\omega
-Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function
f_{Y}
.

Since the Lie derivative commutes with the exterior derivative, we also have

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega&=\mathcal{L}_{Y}\mathrm{d}((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)\\
&=\mathrm{d}\circ\mathcal{L}_{Y}((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)=0,\\
\mathcal{L}_{Y}(\iota_{Z}\omega)&=-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\mathrm{d}f=-\mathrm{d}\circ\mathcal{L}_{Y}f=0.\end{split}

(4.32)

In particular, we get

\omega(\mathcal{L}_{Y}Z,\cdot)=\mathcal{L}_{Y}(\iota_{Z}\omega)-(\mathcal{L}_{Y}\omega)(Z,\cdot)=0.

(4.33)

Since
\omega
is assumed to be non-degenerate, this implies
\mathcal{L}_{Z}Y=-\mathcal{L}_{Y}Z=0
.

In order to take its twist, we decompose
Y
into its vertical and
\varphi
-horizontal parts.
Since
Y
is
Z
-invariant, we can find a vector field
Y_{B}
on
B
such that

Y=Y_{B}^{\varphi}+\varphi(Y)Z.

(4.34)

Then, by Lemma
4.2
we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{tw}(Y)&=\mathrm{tw}(Y_{B}^{\varphi})+\mathrm{tw}(\varphi(Y)Z)=Y_{B}^{\varphi_{k}}-\beta(Y_{B})Z_{k}-f\varphi(Y)Z_{k}\\
&=Y_{B}^{\varphi_{k}}+k\varphi(Y)Z_{k}-((f+k)\varphi+\pi_{S}^{*}\beta)(Y)Z_{k}\\
&=Y_{B}^{\varphi_{k}}+k\varphi(Y)Z_{k}-f_{Y}Z_{k}.\end{split}

(4.35)

Note that the pullback of the vector field
Y_{B}^{\varphi_{k}}+k\varphi(Y)Z_{k}
along the quotient map
q
is precisely
q_{*}Y
. The statement to be proved thus follows.
∎

5
The geometry of the c-map

In this section, we prove the main theorem of this paper (Theorem
5.4
), which describes the supergravity c-map as a natural construction on variations of Hodge structure.
Using this description, we furthermore construct the vertical Killing vector fields on the resulting quaternionic Kähler manifold (Proposition
5.7
), and show that they form the Heisenberg algebra (Corollary
5.10
).
Finally, in §
5.4
, we use the naturality of our c-map construction to interpret it as a functor from the category of certain variations of Hodge structure to the one of quaternionic Kähler manifolds.
As a last result (Proposition
5.12
), we show that our lifting of isomorphisms reproduces the one of infinitesimal automorphisms described in
[
CST21
]
.

5.1
Rigid c-map

The rigid c-map assigns a hyperkähler manifold to an affine special Kähler manifold
\widetilde{M}
. The hyperkähler structure may be defined on either the tangent or cotangent bundle. The two descriptions are related by the symplectic form
\widetilde{\omega}
interpreted as a map
T\widetilde{M}\rightarrow T^{*}\widetilde{M}
. Note that, since
\widetilde{\omega}
is
\widetilde{\nabla}
-parallel, the differential
\widetilde{\omega}_{*}
preserves the splitting of
TT\widetilde{M}
induced by
\widetilde{\nabla}
. In terms of the splitting, we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{\omega}_{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}&0\\
0&\widetilde{\omega}\end{pmatrix}\colon\left(T\widetilde{M}\oplus T\widetilde{M}\right)_{T\widetilde{M}}&\longrightarrow\left(T\widetilde{M}\oplus T^{*}\widetilde{M}\right)_{T^{*}\widetilde{M}}.\end{split}

(5.1)

On either of the tangent or cotangent bundle, there are several equivalent choices of hyperkähler structure that we may make.
In
[
CST21
, Equation 6 (p. 106)]
, the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\widetilde{M}
was used, on which the hyperkähler structure was chosen to be

\widehat{g}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{g}&0\\
0&\widetilde{g}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad I_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I^{*}\end{pmatrix},\quad I_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\widetilde{\omega}^{-1}\\
\widetilde{\omega}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad I_{3}=I_{1}I_{2}.

(5.2)

We will instead be working with the corresponding hyperkähler structure on the tangent bundle
T\widetilde{M}
, that is

\widehat{g}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{g}&0\\
0&\widetilde{g}\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&-I\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathrm{id}\\
\mathrm{id}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_{3}=I_{1}I_{2}.

(5.3)

In fact, it will be convenient to make use of the identification of
T\widetilde{M}
with
E_{\pi}
, cf. Remark
3.23
, which allows us to identfy the vertical subbundle
\mathcal{V}\subseteq TT\widetilde{M}
with the pullback of
E
to
E_{\pi}
by (
2.9
). Then, we can define the metric on the total space of
E_{\pi}
to be
\widetilde{g}
on the horizontal subbundle
\mathcal{H}
, and to be the real part of the Griffiths hermitian form
h_{\mathrm{G}}
on the vertical subbundle
\mathcal{V}
.
We can write this in terms of the tautological section
\Phi_{E}
, using
\nabla\Phi_{E}\colon T(E_{\pi})\to E_{\pi}
as the projection to the vertical part (cf. §
2.3
).

\begin{split}\widehat{g}&=\widetilde{g}+\operatorname{Re}(h_{\mathrm{G}})(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})\\
&=r^{2}g_{M}-dr^{2}-r^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}+(-g_{L}+g_{L}\otimes g_{M}-\omega_{L}\otimes\omega_{M})(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E}).\end{split}

(5.4)

The hyperkähler complex structures
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
in (
5.3
) are then given by

\begin{split}I_{1}X&=\bigg(I\left(p^{E_{\pi}}_{\widetilde{M}}\right)_{*}X\bigg)^{\nabla}-\mathrm{vert}(I_{\mathrm{G}}\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E}),\\
I_{2}X&=\mathrm{vert}\circ\phi\bigg(\!\left(p^{E_{\pi}}_{\widetilde{M}}\right)_{*}X\bigg)-\bigg(\phi^{-1}\left(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E}\right)\bigg)^{\nabla},\\
I_{3}X&=I_{1}I_{2}X.\end{split}

(5.5)

5.2
Supergravity c-map

The supergravity c-map assigns to a PSK manifold
M
a family of quaternionic Kähler manifolds
N_{2k}
parametrised by an integer
k
(we write
2k
instead of
k
for consistency with the notation in
[
CST21
]
).
The
k=0
case will be called the
undeformed c-map
, while the
k>0
case will be called the
deformed c-map
.
One can also define the c-map for
k<0
, but only the non-negative case is complete (cf.
[
CDS17
, Theorem 13]
and
[
ACDM15
, Remark 9 (p. 287)]
).
Therefore, in this section, we will present an intrinsic formulation of the c-map where
k
is a non-negative integer.
For our description of the c-map, we will adopt the twist approach of
[
MS15
]
.

In order to introduce the supergravity c-map metric, we first need to define the following two tensor fields, given in terms of the
\nabla
-horizontal lift of
-I\xi
, which we call
Z
:

\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{H}Z}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widehat{g}|_{\left\langle{Z,I_{1}Z,I_{2}Z,I_{3}Z}\right\rangle},

\displaystyle g_{\perp}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widehat{g}|_{\left\langle{Z,I_{1}Z,I_{2}Z,I_{3}Z}\right\rangle^{\perp}}.

(5.6)

Lemma 5.1
.

In terms of the VPHS data, we have

\begin{split}g_{\mathbb{H}Z}&=-\mathrm{d}r^{2}-r^{2}\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}-\operatorname{Re}{h_{L}}(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E}),\\
g_{\perp}&=r^{2}g_{M}+\operatorname{Re}(h_{L}\otimes h_{M})(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E}).\end{split}

(5.7)

Proof.

Using (
5.5
), we can explicitly describe
\mathbb{H}Z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle Z,I_{1}Z,I_{2}Z,I_{3}Z\rangle
, via

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z&=-(I\xi)^{\nabla},\\
I_{1}Z&=\xi^{\nabla}-\mathrm{vert}(I_{\mathrm{G}}\nabla_{-I\xi^{\nabla}}\Phi_{E})=\xi^{\nabla},\\
I_{2}Z&=-\mathrm{vert}\circ\phi(I\xi)+0=\mathrm{vert}(-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{L}+\mathrm{i}\overline{\Phi}_{L}),\\
I_{3}Z&=I_{1}\mathrm{vert}(-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{L}+\mathrm{i}\overline{\Phi}_{L})=-\mathrm{vert}(\Phi_{L}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}).\end{split}

(5.8)

We can see that
Z
and
I_{1}Z
span the vertical part of
\widetilde{M}\to M
, whereas
I_{2}Z
and
I_{3}Z
span
\llbracket L\rrbracket\subseteq E
.
The description of
g_{\mathbb{H}Z}
and
g_{\perp}
follows.
∎

In order to define the supergravity c-map, we make use of its description in terms of the twist.
Let
c\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define
\widetilde{M}_{>c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{u\in\widetilde{M}\mid-\widetilde{g}_{u}(\xi,\xi)>c\}
.

Proposition 5.2

(
[

]
, Lemma 5.2 (p. 1347))
.

The supergravity c-map metric is the twist of a constant scalar multiple of the tensor field

(5.9)

with respect to the twist data

(Z,\omega_{\mathrm{H}},f_{\mathrm{H}})=\left(-(I\xi)^{\nabla},\begin{pmatrix}-\widetilde{\omega}&0\\
0&-\widetilde{\omega}\end{pmatrix},-\frac{1}{2}(r^{2}+2k)\right).

(5.10)

Following
[

]
and
[

as the

of the hyperkähler metric
\widehat{g}

Remark 5.3
.

Note that

is given in terms of the tautological section

\omega_{\mathrm{H}}=-\widetilde{\omega}-Q(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})=-\widetilde{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}(Q(\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})).

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}(Q(\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E}))(X,Y)\\

&\qquad\qquad=Q(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E})-Q(\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E})\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad-Q(\Phi_{E},([\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]-\nabla_{[X,Y]})\Phi_{E})\\
&\qquad\qquad=2Q(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E}).\end{split}

(5.12)

.

The supergravity c-map metric on

is a constant multiple of

Proof.

.

(5.30)

upon taking

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathrm{CDS}}=r^{2}-2k,\qquad w^{\mathrm{CDS}}_{i}=\frac{(-1)^{\delta_{i,0}}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\overline{w}_{i},\qquad t^{\mathrm{CDS}}=4t.

(5.31)

This was originally derived by physicists in
[

,
FS90

When

5.3
Heisenberg action

While there is always a local basis of
\nabla
-parallel sections of
E
, a global basis may fail to exist because of the presence of monodromy.
We can however get around this issue by considering the universal cover of
M
and pulling back
E
along the covering map.
This gives us a VPHS whose Gauß–Manin connection has no monodromy.
We will show that in the absence of monodromy, the supergravity c-map admits the action of the Heisenberg group.

In particular, we would like to obtain the Killing vector fields on
N_{2k}
that generate the Heisenberg action.
In order to do this, we begin by constructing vertical Killing vector fields on the
rigid
c-map space.
The following proposition will show that the bundle map
\mathrm{vert}\colon E_{\pi}\to T(E_{\pi})
maps pullbacks of parallel sections of
E
to Killing vector fields of
E_{\pi}
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
s\in\Gamma(E)
be such that
\nabla s=0
.
Then, the vertical vector field
v_{s}\in\Gamma(T(E_{\pi}))
corresponding to
s
is Killing and
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function

f_{s}=Q(s,\Phi_{E}).

(5.32)

Moreover, it preserves
Z
,
f_{\mathrm{H}}
,
I_{j}
, and
\widehat{\omega}_{j}=\widehat{g}(I_{j}\cdot,\cdot)
, for
j=1,2,3
as defined in (
5.10
) and (
5.5
).

Proof.

First of all, notice that since
s
is
\nabla
-parallel,
\nabla(\nabla_{v_{s}}\Phi_{E})=\nabla s=0
.
We now compute the Lie derivative of
\widehat{g}
along
v_{s}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}\widehat{g}(X,Y)=\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}\big(\widetilde{g}+\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})\big)(X,Y)\\
&\qquad=\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}\big(\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}(\nabla\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})\big)(X,Y)\\
&\qquad=v_{s}\left(\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E})\right)\\
&\qquad\quad-\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}(\nabla_{[v_{s},X]}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E})-\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{[v_{s},Y]}\Phi_{E})\\
&\qquad=\operatorname{Re}(\nabla_{v_{s}}h_{\mathrm{G}})(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E})\\
&\qquad\quad+\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}\left([\nabla_{v_{s}},\nabla_{X}]\Phi_{E}-\nabla_{[v_{s},X]}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E}\right)\\
&\qquad\quad+\operatorname{Re}h_{\mathrm{G}}\left([\nabla_{v_{s}},\nabla_{Y}]\Phi_{E}-\nabla_{[v_{s},Y]}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E}\right)\\
&\qquad=\operatorname{Re}(\nabla_{v_{s}}h_{\mathrm{G}})(\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E},\nabla_{Y}\Phi_{E}).\end{split}

(5.33)

The last expression vanishes because
\nabla_{v_{s}}h_{G}
is to be interpreted as
\nabla_{(\pi_{E})_{*}v_{s}}h_{G}
by the definition of a pullback connection, and
v_{s}
is a vertical tangent vector on the total space of the bundle
\pi_{E}\colon E\to M
.

We can now check that
f_{s}
is a Hamiltonian function of
v_{s}
by computing

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f_{s}

\displaystyle=\nabla(Q(s,\Phi_{E}))=Q(s,\nabla\Phi_{E})=Q(\nabla_{v_{s}}\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E})=-\iota_{v_{s}}\omega_{\mathrm{H}}.

(5.34)

The function
r
is defined on the base, and therefore it is invariant along vertical vector fields.
In particular
\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}f_{\mathrm{H}}=0
.

The vector field
Z
is also invariant, as
\widetilde{\varphi}=r^{-2}\widehat{g}(Z,\cdot)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}\widetilde{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\iota_{v_{s}}\widetilde{\varphi}+\iota_{v_{s}}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\varphi}=0-2\iota_{v_{s}}\widetilde{\omega}=0.

(5.35)

The invariance of
I_{k}
is equivalent to that of
\widehat{\omega}_{k}
.
Note that
\widehat{\omega}_{1}=2\widetilde{\omega}+\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is a sum of invariant
2
-forms.
Their invariance follows from the observation that
\widetilde{\omega}
is horizontal, and
v_{s}
is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian with
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
closed.

Meanwhile, for
\widehat{\omega}_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{v_{s}}\widehat{\omega}_{2}&=\mathrm{d}\iota_{v_{s}}\widehat{\omega}_{2}=\mathrm{d}\big(\widehat{g}(I_{2}v_{s},\cdot)\big)=-\mathrm{d}\Big(\widehat{g}\big((\phi^{-1}(s))^{\nabla},\cdot\big)\Big)\\
&=-\mathrm{d}\Big(\widetilde{g}\big(\phi^{-1}(s),\cdot\big)\Big)=-2\mathrm{Alt}\Big(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}\widetilde{g}\big(\phi^{-1}(s),\cdot\big)\Big)\\
&=-2\mathrm{Alt}\Big(\widetilde{g}\big(\nabla^{\widetilde{g}}(\phi^{-1}(s)),\cdot\big)\Big)=4\mathrm{Alt}\Big(\widetilde{g}\big(\operatorname{Re}\widetilde{\eta}_{\cdot}(\phi^{-1}(s)),\cdot\big)\Big).\end{split}

(5.36)

This quantity vanishes because
\operatorname{Re}\widetilde{\eta}
is symmetric.
The invariance of
I_{3}=I_{1}I_{2}
follows.
∎

Killing vector fields on a hyperkähler manifold satisfying the properties resulting from Proposition
5.7
can be used to obtain Killing vector fields on the corresponding quaternionic Käher manifold via the following proposition.

Proposition 5.8

(
[
CST21
]
Proposition 3.5 (p. 108))
.

Let
X
be a Killing vector field on
(E_{\pi},\widehat{g})
which is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function
f_{X}
, and which preserves the hyperkähler structure
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
and the twist data
Z
,
f_{\mathrm{H}}
.
Then, the vector field

X^{\mathrm{Q}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{tw}\left(X-\frac{f_{X}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}Z\right)

(5.37)

is Killing with respect to
g_{2k}
.

Let
s
thus be a section of
E
such that
\nabla s=0
, and let
v_{s}
be the corresponding vertical vector field on
E_{\pi}
.
We obtain a Killing vector field

w_{s}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{tw}\left(v_{s}-\frac{f_{s}}{f_{\mathrm{H}}}Z\right).

(5.38)

Notice that
-\widetilde{\varphi}(v_{s})=0
, so by
Z
-invariance,
v_{s}
is the
(-\widetilde{\varphi})
-horizontal lift of some vector field on
\mathbb{R}_{>2k}\times M
.
Let
v^{\prime}_{s}
be the
\varphi_{k}
-horizontal lift of said vector field on
N_{2k}
.
The twist of
v_{s}
is then
v^{\prime}_{s}-\beta(v_{s})Z_{k}
, and that of
Z
is
-f_{\mathrm{H}}Z_{k}
, where
Z_{k}
is the fundamental vector field of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle
N_{2k}\to E_{\mathrm{pr}_{M}}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{s}&=v^{\prime}_{s}-\beta(v_{s})Z_{k}+f_{s}Z_{k}=v^{\prime}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\,Q(\Phi_{E},\nabla_{v_{s}}\Phi_{E})Z_{k}+Q(s,\Phi_{E})Z_{k}\\
&=v^{\prime}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\,Q(\Phi_{E},s)Z_{k}+Q(s,\Phi_{E})Z_{k}=v^{\prime}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\,Q(s,\Phi_{E})Z_{k}.\end{split}

(5.39)

Now, in order to see the Heisenberg action, we make use of the following result relating commutators of Killing vector fields on the quaternionic Kähler manifold to those on the hyperkähler manifold.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be Killing vector fields on
(E_{\pi},\widehat{g})
as in Proposition
5.8
with Hamiltonian functions

Then the corresponding Killing vector fields

and

satisfy

[X_{1}^{\mathrm{Q}},X_{2}^{\mathrm{Q}}]=\mathrm{tw}([X_{1},X_{2}])+\mathrm{tw}(\omega_{\mathrm{H}}(X_{1},X_{2}))Z_{k}.

(5.40)

Proof.

See
[
CST21
, Theorem 3.8 (p. 109)]
combined with Proposition
5.8
.

Corollary 5.10

The vertical Killing vector fields
w_{s}
associated to
\nabla
-parallel sections
s
of

satisfy the Heisenberg algebra relations

\displaystyle[w_{s},Z_{k}]=0,

\displaystyle[w_{s_{1}},w_{s_{2}}]=Q(s_{1},s_{2})Z_{k}.

(5.41)

Remark 5.11
.

The vertical Killing vector field
w_{s}
associated to a
\nabla
-parallel section
s
of

can be readily integrated to yield an isometry
\psi_{s}
of

, given by

\psi_{s}(p,x_{p},u_{p})=\left(p,x_{p}+s_{p},u_{p}\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\,Q_{p}(s_{p},x_{p})\bigg)\right).

(5.42)

Here,
p
is a point on
M\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}
, while
x_{p}
and

are points in the fibres over
p
of the respective pullbacks of
E\rightarrow M
and
S_{k}\rightarrow M
to
M\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}
.

We can now explicitly see that this gives an action of the Heisenberg group:

\begin{split}\psi_{s_{1}}\circ\psi_{s_{2}}(p,x_{p},u_{p})&=\psi_{s_{1}+s_{2}}\bigg(p,x_{p},u_{p}\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\,Q(s_{1},s_{2})_{p}\bigg)\bigg).\end{split}

(5.43)

5.4
Functoriality

The supergravity c-map is a natural construction. This means that isomorphisms of PSK manifolds lift to isometries of quaternionic Kähler manifolds in a natural way. In this subsection, we describe this lifting of isomorphisms explicitly.

Consider the PSK isomorphism
(\widetilde{\psi},\psi)
from
(\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M,\widetilde{g},I,\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\nabla},\xi)
to
(\pi^{\prime}\colon\widetilde{M^{\prime}}\rightarrow M^{\prime},\widetilde{g}^{\prime},I^{\prime},\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime},\widetilde{\nabla}^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
.
In the following discussion, any construction associated to the PSK manifold
M
will have a primed counterpart associated to the PSK manifold
M^{\prime}
.
Using the structure of
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{M}\times_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\mathbb{C}
and
L^{\prime}
as associated fibre bundles and the isomorphism
\widetilde{\psi}\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
, we can produce a complex vector bundle isomorphism
\psi^{L}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{\psi}\times_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}\colon L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
covering
\psi
.
This can be regarded as an extension of
\widetilde{\psi}
.
Using
\psi^{L}
, we can then construct the maps
\psi^{E_{\mathbb{C}}}\colon E_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow E^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\psi_{\mathrm{pr}}^{E}\colon E\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}\rightarrow E^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}
via

\begin{split}\psi^{E_{\mathbb{C}}}&=\psi^{L}+\psi^{L}\otimes(\psi^{1,0}_{*})_{\psi^{-1}}+\overline{\psi}^{L}\otimes(\psi^{0,1}_{*})_{\psi^{-1}}+\overline{\psi}^{L},\\
\psi_{\mathrm{pr}}^{E}&=\left.\psi^{E_{\mathbb{C}}}\right|_{E}\times\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{>2k}}.\end{split}

(5.44)

Since
\widetilde{\psi}
is compatible with the Kähler structures on
\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\prime}
, the map
\psi^{L}
is compatible with the Hermitian forms
h_{L}
and
h_{L^{\prime}}
, and with the Chern connections
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
(whose imaginary parts are
\widetilde{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\prime}
respectively).
Since
\widetilde{\psi}_{*}
maps
\zeta
to
\zeta^{\prime}
, the map
\psi^{L}
sends the unit circle bundle
S\subset L
to
S^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
.
So, we obtain a map
\psi^{S}_{k}\colon S_{k}\rightarrow S^{\prime}_{k}
pulling back the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-connection
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
to
\varphi_{k}
via the codifferential
(\psi^{S}_{k})^{*}
.

By Theorem
5.4
, the quaternionic Kähler manifold
N_{2k}
obtained from
M
via the supergravity c-map is the fibred product of the bundles
E\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}\rightarrow M
and
S_{k}\rightarrow M
.
An analogous statement holds for
N^{\prime}_{2k}
.
Therefore, the maps
\psi_{\mathrm{pr}}^{E}
and
\psi^{S}_{k}
(both of which cover
\psi
) canonically induce a map
\psi^{N}\colon N_{2k}\rightarrow N^{\prime}_{2k}
covering
\psi
.

As the map
\widetilde{\psi}
relates
\widetilde{\omega}
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
to
\widetilde{\omega}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\nabla}^{\prime}
, the induced map
\psi_{\mathrm{pr}}^{E}
relates the (pullbacks of the) bilinear form
Q
and the connection
\nabla
on the bundle
E\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}
, to
Q^{\prime}
and
\nabla^{\prime}
on
E^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}\rightarrow M^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}_{>2k}
.
Similarly, the Kähler structure of
M
and the coordinate
r
are related to the corresponding counterparts on
M^{\prime}
.
Therefore, the map
\psi^{N}
is an isometry between the quaternionic Kähler manifolds
(N_{2k},g_{2k})
and
(N^{\prime}_{2k},g^{\prime}_{2k})
.
As a result of naturality, the assignment
(\psi,\widetilde{\psi})\mapsto\psi^{N}
is a functor.
This provides us with the desired functorial lift of PSK isomorphisms.

In particular, we can consider
1
-parameter subgroups of PSK automorphisms and take the differential of the action at the identity to get vector fields that act as infinitesimal automorphisms.
Lifts of infinitesimal PSK automorphisms to infinitesimal isometries (i.e. Killing vector fields) of the supergravity c-map metric were constructed in a different way in
[
CST21
, Theorem 3.15 (p. 112)]
. Unsurprisingly, the above functorial construction reproduces the same result.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
be a PSK manifold and let
\widetilde{X}
and
X
be vector fields on
\widetilde{M}
and
M
respectively, which generate a
1
-paramater family of PSK automorphisms
(\widetilde{\psi}_{t},\psi_{t})
on
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
. Then, the complete lift
\widetilde{X}^{T}
on
T\widetilde{M}\cong E_{\pi}
is
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function

f^{T}_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f_{\widetilde{X}^{T}}=-\Big(f_{\mathrm{H}}+k\Big)\widetilde{\varphi}(X)-\frac{1}{2}\,Q(\Phi_{E},\nabla_{X}\Phi_{E}).

(5.45)

Moreover, the functorial lift
\psi_{t}^{N}
of
(\widetilde{\psi}_{t},\psi_{t})
is generated by the Killing vector field
(\widetilde{X}^{T})^{\mathrm{Q}}
on
N_{2k}
arising from
\widetilde{X}^{T}
and the choice of
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
-Hamiltonian function

, in accordance with (
5.37
).

Proof.

In order to prove the first part, our strategy will be to use Lemma
4.4
. We already know
\omega_{\mathrm{H}}
is non-degenerate. We need to show that
\widetilde{X}^{T}
, interpreted as vector field tangent to
E_{\pi}
, preserves

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{H}}=-\frac{1}{2}(r^{2}+2k),\qquad\varphi=-\widetilde{\varphi},\qquad\beta=-\frac{1}{2}\,Q(\Phi_{E},\nabla\Phi_{E}).

(5.46)

For this purpose, we will describe the flow of
\widetilde{X}^{T}
on
E_{\pi}
and show that it preserves these objects.

Consider first a PSK automorphism
(\widetilde{\psi},\psi)
.
By the universal property of the pullback we get a map
\psi^{(E_{\mathbb{C}})_{\pi}}\colon(E_{\mathbb{C}})_{\pi}\to E_{\pi}
, which in turn restricts to a map
\psi^{E_{\pi}}\colon E_{\pi}\to E_{\pi}
, as
\psi^{E_{\mathbb{C}}}
preserves the real structure.
Given
Y\in T\widetilde{M}_{>2k}
, we determine the element of
E_{\pi}
corresponding to

to be

\begin{split}\phi(\widetilde{\psi}_{*}Y)&=\chi(\widetilde{\psi}_{*}Y)\Phi_{L}+\Phi_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}\widetilde{\psi}_{*}Y^{1,0}+\overline{\Phi}_{L}\otimes\pi_{*}\widetilde{\psi}_{*}Y^{0,1}+\overline{\chi}(\psi_{*}Y)\overline{\Phi}_{L}\\

(5.47)

Here, we used the fact that
\chi
and
\overline{\chi}
are invariant under the action of the diffeomorphism
\widetilde{\psi}
, and that
\widetilde{\psi}
is a map of the bundle
\pi\colon\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
covering
\psi

We know that the tautological section
\Phi_{L}

\psi^{L}
.
Therefore,
\phi(\widetilde{\psi}_{*}Y)

\psi^{(E_{\mathbb{C}})_{\pi}}(\phi(Y))

\psi^{E_{\pi}}\circ\phi=\phi\circ\widetilde{\psi}_{*}

The vector field
\widetilde{X}^{T}

\phi_{*}(\widetilde{X}^{T}{}_{Y})=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi\left((\widetilde{\psi}_{t})_{*}Y\right)\right|_{t=0}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\psi_{t})^{E_{\pi}}\left(\phi(Y)\right)\right|_{t=0}.

(5.48)

It follows that
(\psi_{t})^{E_{\pi}}
is the
1

\widetilde{X}^{T}
in
E_{\pi}
.
Since
\widetilde{\psi}_{t}

\widetilde{\varphi}=\operatorname{Im}(\chi)
,
\widetilde{\omega}
, and
\widetilde{\nabla}

\widetilde{X}^{T}

By combining (
4.30
) and (

.
Therefore, in order to prove the last part, it is enough to show that given some PSK automorphism

Remark 5.13
.

The integrality of
k
is crucial in our functorial lifting of general PSK isomorphisms.
For instance, if
k=\frac{1}{2}
, by Remark
4.3
, the twist of
E_{\pi}
can be identified with a double cover of itself, and in general, automorphisms cannot be lifted to a double cover in a canonical way.
In
[
CST21
]
, the integrality of
k
was not assumed, but this is nevertheless consistent with the above, as infinitesimal automorphisms can still be lifted for general
k
, in a canonical way.

Remark 5.14
.

The action of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{PSK}}(M)
of the PSK manifold
\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M
on the space of parallel sections
s
of
E
induces an action on the Heisenberg isometries
\psi_{s}
.
This action is compatible with the conjugation by the functorial lift of PSK automorphisms.
Therefore, the subgroup of isometries generated by the functorial lifts of PSK automorphisms and the Heisenberg isometries is a semidirect product

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{PSK}}(M)\ltimes\mathrm{Heis}_{\mathrm{rk}(E)+1}\leq\mathrm{Isom}(N_{2k}).

(5.49)

This was shown for
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
in a different manner in
[
CRT21
, §3]
.
For the
n=0
case, it was shown in
[
CST21
, Theorem 4.5 (p. 116)]
that this semidirect product is in fact the full group of isometries.
An advantage of our functorial approach is that it makes the action of the semidirect product on
N_{2k}
transparent and explicit in general.

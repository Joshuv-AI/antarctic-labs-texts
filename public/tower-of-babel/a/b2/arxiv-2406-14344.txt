On the homogenization of a Signorini-type problem in a domain with inclusions
Sara Monsurrò *†, Carmen Perugia, Federica Raimondi *†

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the effect of a Signorini-type interface condition on the asymptotic behaviour, as ε \varepsilon tends to zero, of problems posed in ε \varepsilon -periodic domains with inclusions. The Signorini-type condition is expressed in terms of two complementary equalities involving the jump of the solution on the interface and its conormal derivative via a parameter γ \gamma . Our problem models the heat exchange in a medium hosting an ε \varepsilon -periodic array of thermal conductors in presence of impurities distributed on some regions of the interface. Different limit problems are obtained according to different values of γ \gamma . † † Keywords: Homogenization, Domains with inclusions, Imperfect interface, Signorini-type condition, Variational inequality, Unfolding method. † † MSC: 35B27, 35J25, 35J20, 35J60, 35R35.

Introduction

The Signorini problem was presented for the first time in
[
39
]
in the context of linear elasticity, and, eventually, it was given a rigorous mathematical justification in
[
22
]
. It concerns the equilibrium configuration of an elastic body on a rigid frictionless surface, subject only to its mass forces.

Signorini-type boundary conditions appear in many problems in applied mathematics deriving from engineering and physics. We can think, for instance, to lubrication and filtration processes, hydrodynamics, plasticity, crack theory, optimal control problems, etc.

The weak formulations of such kind of problems involve variational inequalities corresponding to nonlinear free boundary-value problems. For mathematical and historical backgrounds we refer to Duvaut and Lions
[
19
]
, Kinderleherer and Stampacchia
[
27
]
and Brezis
[
4
]
. The unique solvability of these inequalities has been investigated in
[
29
]
and
[
40
]
(see also
[
28
]
).

Successively, this type of problems have been studied in different geometrical settings.
For instance, the homogenization of boundary value problems involving Signorini boundary conditions has been treated in
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
30
]
in domains with oscillating boundaries in both linear and nonlinear cases. In
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
37
]
perforated domains have been considered.
Cracks and layers were taken into account in the works of
[
10
]
and
[
25
]
.

In our paper we consider an open bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
made up of two disjoint components
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{2}
separated by a Lipschitz-continuous interface
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter tending to zero. In this domain we deal with the following problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{2})=f&\hbox{ in }\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}=-(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{2})\cdot\nu_{2}^{\varepsilon}&\hbox{ on }\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle[u^{\varepsilon}]\geq 0,\quad(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u^{\varepsilon}]\geq 0&\hbox{ on }\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle[u^{\varepsilon}]((A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u^{\varepsilon}])=0&\hbox{ on }\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{1}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\nu_{i}^{\varepsilon}
is the unit external normal vector to
\Omega_{i}^{\varepsilon}
,
i=1,2
and
[\cdot]
is the jump of the solution on the interface. In (
1.1
), the coefficients
A^{\varepsilon}
and
h^{\varepsilon}
satisfy suitable hypotheses of uniform ellipticity, boundedness and periodicity,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Problem (
1.1
) can modelize the heat exchange in a medium hosting an
\varepsilon
-periodic array of thermal conductors. Actually, in nature the presence of impurities distributed in some regions of the interface may give rise to a discontinuity of the temperature field. This is why the Signorini interface condition in the fourth and fifth lines of (
1.1
) seems more realistic to describe such real phenomena. Indeed, it means that one can distinguish two a priori unknown subsets of
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
where the couple
u^{\varepsilon}=(u^{\varepsilon}_{1},u^{\varepsilon}_{2})
satisfies the two alternative equalities:

[u^{\varepsilon}]=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u^{\varepsilon}]=0.

From a physical point of view there exist a zone of
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
of perfect conduction i.e. absence of jump, and one characterized by an imperfect contact transmission condition. Here, the external thermal flux is proportional to the jump through a coefficient depending on the period
\varepsilon
and on the parameter
\gamma
representing the order of magnitude of the resistance with respect to
\varepsilon
.

Our aim is to investigate the effect of the Signorini interface condition on the asymptotic behaviour, as
\varepsilon
tends to zero, of problem (
1.1
).

We prove that the thermal properties of the limit problem vary according to the different values of the parameter
\gamma
.
For
\gamma<-1
and
-1<\gamma<1
the homogenized problems are linear and standard (see
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
31
]
) meaning that the Signorini-type interface condition in the
\varepsilon
-problem does not affect the limit behaviour.

More precisely, if
\gamma<-1
, we get the classical homogenized problem of Bensoussan-Lions-Papanicolau in a fixed domain, see
[
3
]
.
Actually, the limit problem behaves as in presence, at
\varepsilon
-level, of the only transmission condition on the interface of type
[u^{\varepsilon}]=0
. Hence, the part of
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
where the two temperature fields differ is such small that it does not affect the asymptotic behaviour and we do not have a variational inequality anymore.

On the contrary, when
\gamma\in]-1,1[
, we obtain the same limit behaviour of
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
where, at
\varepsilon
-level, the only condition
(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u^{\varepsilon}]=0
is assumed on the interface. More in particular, we get the classical homogenized problem of Cioranescu-S.J. Paulin in the case of a perforated domain with Neumann condition on the boundary of the holes, see
[
12
]
.
Indeed, the flux related to
u^{\varepsilon}_{2}
asymptotically vanishes and the effective diffusion of the first phase is the one obtained when there is no material occupying
\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{2}
. However, in the limit problem one has
f
and not
\theta_{1}f
(
\theta_{1}
being the proportion of the material occupying
\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon}
) meaning that the heat exchange takes into account also the source term inside the inclusions.

The most interesting cases are
\gamma=-1
and
\gamma=1
since the two regions of
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
where the Signorini conditions are satisfied at
\varepsilon
-level are both significant at the limit.

For
\gamma=-1
, we obtain a nonlinear homogenized problem (cf. (
2.10
)) where the effective diffusion term takes into account the presence of the contact barrier. Indeed, the associated nonlinear operator is defined through the solution of a cell problem consisting in a variational inequality with a jump on the interface
\Gamma
(cf. (
2.15
)-(
2.16
)). Here, the nonnegativity constraint of the jump on
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
gives rise to a nonnegative jump on the interface
\Gamma
in the cell problem.

At last, when
\gamma=1
, the Signorini-type interface condition at
\varepsilon
-level leads to an obstacle-type homogenized problem (cf. (
2.23
)). Unlike previous cases, at the limit we get two different temperature fields. In some sense, as in the case
\gamma\in]-1,1[
, the part of the interface where the condition
(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\cdot\nu_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u^{\varepsilon}]=0
is assumed prevails on the one where
[u^{\varepsilon}]=0
holds. However, the gap between the two temperatures on
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
is so big that,
even if the flux related to
u^{\varepsilon}_{2}
asymptotically vanishes as for
\gamma\in]-1,1[
, the material occupying the inclusions contributes to the heat exchange. Indeed, the initial jump between the two temperature fields does not vanish but, at the limit, it reflects on the whole domain. As a consequence, the homogenized problem consists in a variational inequality with a lower order term depending on the physical properties of the interface and the gap between the two temperature fields.

The main difficulty of this paper consists in the choice of appropriate test functions allowing to identify the different limit problems.
Indeed, only for
\gamma<-1
it is possible to use quite standard arguments.
For
\gamma=-1

Finally, in the cases
\gamma\in]-1,1[
and
\gamma=1
we need to consider more regular test functions together with opportune density results. Moreover, when
\gamma=1
, we take advantage of lower semi-continuity arguments again.

Further problems involving two-component domains with jump conditions have been treated in

The paper is organized into several sections. In Section 2 we set the problem by describing the two component domain

Setting of the problem and main result

2.1
The two-component domain

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, with a Lipschitz-continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
. We denote by
Y\doteq\prod_{i=1}^{N}[0,l_{i}[

l_{i}>0,i=1,...,N

Y\doteq Y_{1}\cup\overline{Y_{2}}

Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are two disjoint connected open sets such that
Y_{2}\neq\emptyset
,
\overline{Y_{2}}\subset Y
and the common boundary
\Gamma\doteq\partial Y_{2}

For any

k_{l}\doteq(k_{1}l_{1},...,k_{N}l_{N})
and

Y^{k}\doteq k_{l}+Y,\qquad Y^{k}_{i}\doteq k_{l}+Y_{i},\qquad i=1,2.

Given a positive parameter
\varepsilon
taking values in a sequence converging to zero, we set:

J_{\varepsilon}\doteq\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}|\ \varepsilon\overline{Y^{k}_{2}}\subset\Omega\}

and

\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon}\doteq\bigcup_{k\in J_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon Y^{k}_{2},\qquad\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon}\doteq\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon}},\qquad\Gamma^{\varepsilon}\doteq\partial\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon}.

Therefore, one has
\Omega=\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon}\cup\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon}\cup\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, for the sake of simplicity, we suppose that the second component doesn’t meet the boundary of the domain.

2.3
The functional framework and the weak formulation

Let
V^{\varepsilon}\doteq\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon})\ |\ v=0\hbox{ on }\partial\Omega\}
endowed with the norm

\|v\|_{V^{\varepsilon}}\doteq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon})}.

It is known (see for instance
[

holds with a constant

\varepsilon

(2.2)

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{1})}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{1})}\quad\forall v\in V^{\varepsilon}.

Consequently, the norm in

via a constant independent of

Let

H^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}\doteq\{v=(v_{1},v_{2})|\ v_{1}\in V^{\varepsilon},\ v_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon})\},

, is a Hilbert space
equipped with the norm

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}}\doteq\|\nabla v_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1}^{\varepsilon})}+\|\nabla v_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2}^{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|[v]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}.

(5.48)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times Y_{1}}A(y)(\nabla u_{1}+\nabla_{y}\widehat{u}_{1})(\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla u_{1}-\nabla_{y}\widehat{u}_{1})\,dxdy+\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times\Gamma}h(y)(u_{1}-u_{2})(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\,dxdy\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\geq\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{1}-u_{1})\,dx+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{2}-u_{2})\,dx+\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times\Gamma}h(y)(u_{1}-u_{2})^{2}\,dxdy.\end{array}

If we write (
5.44
) for
\Phi_{1}=\widehat{u}_{1}
and we add it to (
5.48
), we get

(5.49)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times Y_{1}}A(y)(\nabla u_{1}+\nabla_{y}\widehat{u}_{1})(\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla u_{1})\,dxdy+\frac{1}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega\times\Gamma}h(y)(u_{1}-u_{2})((\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})-(u_{1}-u_{2}))\,dxdy\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\geq\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{1}-u_{1})\,dx+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{2}-u_{2})\,dx.\end{array}

We observe that

(5.50)

\displaystyle u_{1}\geq u_{2}\quad\hbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Indeed, since
u^{\varepsilon}_{1}\geq u^{\varepsilon}_{2}
a.e. on
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
, one deduces that
\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(u^{\varepsilon}_{1})\geq\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon}_{2}(u^{\varepsilon}_{2})
a.e. in
\Omega\times\Gamma
, hence (
5.50
) follows from convergences (
5.43
).

Hence, in view of (
5.45
), (
5.50
) and the fact that
A^{0}_{1}
is given by (
2.19
), set
K\doteq\{(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\,|\,\varphi_{1}\geq\varphi_{2}\hbox{ in }\Omega\}
, we get that
(u_{1},u_{2})
is solution of the following problem:

(5.51)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\hbox{ Find }(u_{1},u_{2})\in K\hbox{ s.t. }\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A^{0}_{1}\nabla u_{1}(\nabla\varphi_{1}-\nabla u_{1})\,dx+\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(h)}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}-u_{2})[(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})-(u_{1}-u_{2})]\,dx\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\geq\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{1}-u_{1})\,dx+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}f(\varphi_{2}-u_{2})\,dx,\quad\forall(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in K.\end{array}\right.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{A}
the operator

\mathcal{A}:K\rightarrow K^{\prime}

given by

(5.52)

\left\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\right\rangle_{K^{\prime},K}\doteq\int_{\Omega}A^{0}_{1}\nabla u_{1}\nabla v_{1}\,dx+\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(h)}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dx.

By
(\textbf{A}4)
and the fact that
A^{0}_{1}\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha},\Omega)
(see
[
13
]
), one has

(5.53)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}(w)-\mathcal{A}(v),w-v\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}A^{0}_{1}\nabla(w_{1}-v_{1})\nabla(w_{1}-v_{1})\,dx+\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(h)}{|Y|}\int_{\Omega}[(w_{1}-w_{2})-(v_{1}-v_{2})]^{2}\,dx\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|w_{1}-v_{1}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}+h_{0}\|(w_{1}-w_{2})-(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}>0,\quad\hbox{ if }w\neq v.\end{array}

From this and
[
28
, Thereom 8.3 (pag.
248
)]
, we deduce that the couple
(u_{1},u_{2})
is unique. Hence, by (
5.45
), convergences (
5.43
) hold for the whole sequences.

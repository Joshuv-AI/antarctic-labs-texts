Abstract
. We explicitly construct the rank one primitive Stark (equivalently, Kolyvagin) system extending a constant multiple of Flach’s zeta elements for semi-stable elliptic curves. As its arithmetic applications, we obtain the equivalence between the p p -indivisibility of the constant multiple and the minimal modularity lifting theorem, and we also discuss the cyclicity of the adjoint Selmer groups. In particular, we give an affirmative answer to a question of Mazur and Rubin. Our Stark system construction yields a more refined interpretation of the collection of Flach’s zeta elements than the “geometric Euler system” approach due to Flach, Wiles, Mazur, and Weston.

Introduction

Flach’s zeta elements

As explained in the Introduction of his celebrated work
[
Wil95
]
, A. Wiles first tried to prove the semi-stable Shimura–Taniyama conjecture and Fermat’s last theorem via the construction of an Euler system for symmetric squares of semi-stable elliptic curves extending the cohomology classes constructed by M. Flach
[
Fla92
]
, which we call
Flach’s zeta elements
in this article. However, the construction of the Euler system was incomplete, and it was regarded as a gap.

After that, Wiles, together with R. Taylor, found a completely different approach towards the semi-stable Shimura–Taniyama conjecture, which is now known as the Taylor–Wiles system argument
[
TW95
]
, to fill the gap.
See
[
Kur93
,
Kol94
,
RS94
,
Dar95
]
for the survey articles written before the gap was filled, and see
[
Cal23
]
for the far-reaching development of the Taylor–Wiles system argument.

This Euler system approach is often known as the “geometric Euler system” strategy, and it was further developed mainly by B. Mazur and T. Weston
[
Maz
,
MW
,
Wes01
,
Wes02
]
. However, it was still incomplete to obtain the exact bound of Selmer groups since it could proceed only the first step of the Euler system argument.

The main goals of this article are to investigate the behavior of the collection of Flach’s zeta elements beyond the scope of this geometric Euler system strategy and to deduce certain structural results for adjoint Selmer groups.
However, it is well-known that the construction question of an
Euler
system extending Flach’s zeta element is extremely difficult (see
[
Rub00
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.6]
,
[
Wes01
, pp. 359–360]
,
[
MR04
, Rem. 6.3.4]
, and
[
LZ19
, Intro.]
for example).

What is the new idea to overcome this notorious difficulty?
We are greatly benefited from various remarkable and significant developments of the theory of Euler systems.
In particular, it turns out that Kolyvagin systems are more essential than Euler systems to obtain the exact bound of the corresponding dual Selmer groups
[
MR04
]
. Also, the notion of Stark systems is developed by generalizing the units predicted by Stark-type conjectures
[
MR16
]
.
The key observation is that the collection of Flach’s zeta elements forms a ‘‘Stark system of Gauss sum type”
1
1

1

No Gauss sums actually appear in this article. Since we initially have only
finitely many
cohomology classes forming a Stark system, we call them “Gauss sum type”.
.
Since the module of Stark systems (equivalently, Kolyvagin systems) is free of rank one, the Gauss sum type Stark system naturally extends to the
bona fide
one.
In other words,
we construct derivative (so ramified) classes defined over
\mathbb{Q}
directly from Flach’s zeta elements by using the framework of Stark systems
.

Since there is a certain constant multiple subtlety in our Stark system approach, our result does not give a new proof of the semi-stable Shimura–Taniyama conjecture or Fermat’s last theorem yet.
Indeed, we prove that the minimal modularity lifting theorem is equivalent to showing that the constant multiple is a
p
-adic unit.
Also, we do not know whether our Stark system actually comes from an Euler system
2
2

2

We are not sure whether it is possible to recover an Euler system from the Kolyvagin system associated to the Euler system.
.

The cyclicity of adjoint Selmer groups

The cyclicity question has a long history in Iwasawa theory. This is just a quick summary of the preface of Hida’s book
[
Hid22a
]
and we strongly recommend the reader to read it.

K. Iwasawa himself was interested in the structure of various classical Iwasawa modules including the cyclicity over the Iwasawa algebra.
In
[
Iwa69
]
, he obtained the cyclicity of certain classical Iwasawa modules over the Iwasawa algebra (as well as the main conjecture) under the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture.
Even without assuming the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture, he proposed several interesting questions on the cyclicity around 1980
[
Iwa88
,
Iwa14
]
.

H. Hida explored the non-abelian analogue of this nature, and
[
Hid86
]
was written with this purpose in mind.
In particular, he studied the cyclicity question of the adjoint Selmer groups over the universal deformation ring in a series of his papers
[
Hid
,
Hid20
,
Hid22b
,
Hid22a
]
.
In
[
Hid22a
, Chap. 7]
, he proved many cyclicity results for the adjoint Selmer groups of Artin representations.
As far as we understand, the elliptic curve case seems quite open
[
Hid22a
, pp. 287–288 and 339]
unless the length of adjoint Selmer groups is
\leq 1
. As an application of the above Stark system construction, we obtain a cyclicity result for the adjoint Selmer groups of elliptic curves (Corollary
1.5
).

Comparison with other recent work

In recent years, some groups of mathematicians have developed new approaches towards the construction of symmetric square and adjoint Euler systems from different sources
[
LZ19
,
Urb21
,
LZ
,
SS
]
.
We provide brief remarks on their constructions and explain why they are all independent of ours.

In
[
LZ19
]
,
the Beilinson–Flach Euler system for the Rankin–Selberg product of the same
p
-ordinary modular form is studied
and is constructed from Beilinson–Flach’s elements and bounds Selmer groups of the symmetric square representations of an ordinary modular form twisted by a non-trivial Dirichlet character. This twist is inevitable to have the correct Euler system relation.
In
[
Urb21
]
, an Euler system for adjoint representations of ordinary Hilbert modular forms is constructed directly from congruence modules, but the
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem is an ingredient of the construction.
Also, the corresponding explicit reciprocity law depends on a conjecture on the Fitting ideals of some equivariant congruence modules for abelian base change.
In
[
LZ
]
, an Euler system for adjoint representations of ordinary modular forms is constructed from Asai–Flach classes and is used to prove one-sided divisibility of the main conjecture for the symmetric square Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
up to a power of
p
.
In
[
SS
]
, an Euler system for the symmetric square representation of an ordinary modular form twisted by an odd Dirichlet character is initially constructed by utilizing the pull-back of Eisenstein series on
\mathrm{SO}(3,2)
. Then by using Hida theory (among others), an Euler system for the symmetric square representation (with no extra twist) is obtained. As its consequence, the
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem follows.
The
p
-ordinary condition is assumed in all the work mentioned above.

Our Stark system also detects the precise upper bound of the adjoint Selmer groups, so the direct connection with the modularity lifting theorem is obtained.
More precisely, our construction yields an equivalence statement as mentioned earlier.
It appears that our approach is the most akin to Wiles’ original method since we work with Flach’s zeta elements as Wiles did, so it might have some historical meaning.
Also, our argument works for any good reduction prime.
In addition, the structural refinement like the cyclicity is not observed in other approaches.

3.7.1.
Canonical Selmer structure

We first compute the core rank for the “canonical” Selmer structure for
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)
.
Indeed, the notions of the canonical Selmer structures in
[
MR04
, Def. 3.2.1]
and
[
MR16
, Def. 5.1]
are slightly different at
p
.
It is the relaxed condition at
p
in the former, but the universal norm subgroup is involved in the latter.
By
[
MR16
, Cor. 5.3]
, they become equivalent if
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))
has finite length.
The following lemma confirms that our case satisfies this condition.

Lemma 3.3
.

For a non-CM elliptic curve
E
and a good reduction prime
p>2
, we have

\mathrm{cork}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))=0.

Since we are working on semi-stable elliptic curves, the non-CM hypothesis is automatic.

Proof.

Let
V_{p}E=T_{p}E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Since

\mathrm{cork}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(V_{p}E)),

it suffices to show that

\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V_{p}E)\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
For convenience, we write
V
for
V_{p}E
as a representation of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

When
E
has good ordinary reduction at
p
, the non-CM hypothesis implies that
V
is isomorphic to a non-split extension of two distinct characters as a local Galois representation at
p
:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{+}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{-}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Since
F^{+}V
and
F^{-}V
are distinct, there is no non-zero
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-equivariant homomorphism between
F^{+}V
and
F^{-}V
, so every
\psi\in\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)
preserves
F^{+}V
.
Thus, the restriction of
\psi
to
F^{+}V
and the induced map on
F^{-}V
are both scalar multiplication maps.
Suppose that both scalars are different, say
c_{+}
and
c_{-}
with
c_{+}\neq c_{-}
.
For this
\psi
, define another
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-equivariant endomorphism

\psi^{\prime}=\dfrac{\psi-c_{-}\cdot\mathrm{id}}{c_{+}-c_{-}}.

Since
\psi^{\prime}
becomes the identity map on
F^{+}V
and the zero map on
F^{-}V
,

V
splits into the direct sum of
F^{+}V
and
\mathrm{ker}(\psi^{\prime})
as a
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-representation.
Since this violates the non-CM hypothesis, we have
c_{+}=c_{-}
, just say
c
.
Then the restriction of
\psi-c\cdot\mathrm{id}
to
F^{+}V
is the zero map and
the projection of the image of
\psi-c\cdot\mathrm{id}
to
F^{-}V
also becomes the zero map.
In other words, the image of
\psi-c\cdot\mathrm{id}
lies in
F^{+}V
.
Since
\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(F^{-}V,F^{+}V)=0
, we have

\psi=c\cdot\mathrm{id}

on
V
. This implies that
\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

We now suppose that
E
has supersingular reduction at
p
.
Since
V
is irreducible, every non-zero
\psi\in\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)
has trivial kernel; thus, it is an automorphism. In other words,
\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)
is a division algebra.
Consider Fontaine’s de Rham functor

\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}})
and
then the de Rham comparison gives us an isomorphism of filtered
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector spaces

\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{p})^{\vee}

where
(-)^{\vee}
is the dual with respect to the de Rham pairing.
Considering the Hodge filtration and its dual filtration, we have

\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\simeq\left(\frac{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{p})}{\mathrm{H}^{0}(E,\Omega^{1})}\right)^{\vee}

and

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=1
.
For a
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-equivariant endomorphism
\psi
on
V
, the restriction of
\psi\otimes\mathrm{id}
on
V\otimes\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}
to
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
yields an endomorphism on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
.
Since the filtration comes from the
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}
-factor, this map preserves the filtration; thus, we have the endomorphism
\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi)
on
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
.
It defines a homomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebras

\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)

by sending
\psi
to
\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi)
.
Now suppose that
\psi
is non-zero, so we also have the (
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-equivariant) inverse
\psi^{-1}
.
The functoriality of Fontaine’s de Rham functor yields that

(\psi\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\psi^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id})=(\psi^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\psi\otimes\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}

on
\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
.
Thus,
\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi)\circ\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi^{-1})=\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi^{-1})\circ\mathbf{D}^{0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\psi)=\mathrm{id}

on
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
.
Therefore, the homomorphism

\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)

is injective.
Since
\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)
is one-dimensional,

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Thus, we have
\mathrm{End}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(V)\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
∎

Recall the Cartier dual of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)
:

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1))\simeq\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]).

Since the complex conjugation acts by
-1
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)
,
if a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear map

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)

is invariant under complex conjugation, it must vanish on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{+}
and can be arbitrary on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{-}
.
Since
p>2
, the
\pm
-parts split integrally. Thus, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{-},\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, so we have

\mathrm{cork}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))=\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{-}.

Thus, the notions of core ranks between
[
MR04
, Thm. 5.2.15]
and
[
MR16
, Thm. 5.4]
are compatible.
Since the complex conjugation has eigenvalues
+1
,
-1
on
T_{p}E
, so it has eigenvalues
+1,-1,+1
on

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)
; we have

\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E)^{-}=1.

This confirms that the core rank
\chi(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E),\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{can}})
is one.

3.7.2.
Bloch–Kato Selmer structure

By using the global Poitou–Tate global duality computation (e.g.
[
MR04
, Proof of Thm. 5.2.15]
,
[
MR16
, Proof of Thm. 5.4]

The local Euler characteristic formula and local Tate duality yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))

\displaystyle=3+\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))+\mathrm{cork}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{\infty}]))

\displaystyle=3+\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))

where

BK90

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\frac{\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))}{\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))}+\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V_{p}E))

4.1.
A lemma

Let
q
be a prime with

.
Since
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(T_{p}E)
is unramified at

G_{\mathbb{Q}_{q}}

\mathrm{ad}^{0}(T_{p}E)
factors through its quotient by the inertia subgroup
\mathcal{I}_{q}
at

We write

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(T_{p}E))=\mathrm{H}^{0}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{q}}/\mathcal{I}_{q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(T_{p}E))

Lemma 4.1
.

For a prime
q
with
(q,Np)=1
,
we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{/f}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{p}E))\simeq\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(T_{p}E)).

If
q
is a
k
-Flach prime, then we have isomorphism

\psi_{q}:\mathrm{H}^{1}_{/f}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

See
[
Wes01
, Lem. 10.9]
for the first statement and the mod
p^{k}
version also holds by the same reasoning. Since
[
Wes01
, Lem. 10.9]
concerns the complex conjugation, we give the detail for the latter.
Since
q
is a
k
-Flach prime,
we can choose a basis
x
,
y
of

such that

\mathrm{Fr}_{q}(x)=\alpha\cdot x
and

\mathrm{Fr}_{q}(y)=\alpha^{-1}\cdot q\cdot y
.
Then
x\otimes x
,
x\otimes y+y\otimes x
,

forms a basis of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}])
.
Since
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}])(-1)=\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}])
,

\mathrm{Fr}_{q}
acts on the induced basis of
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}])
by multiplication by
\alpha^{2}\cdot q^{-1}
,

, and
\alpha^{-2}\cdot q
, respectively.
Since

and

5.5.

5.5.1.

For every

5.6.
The constant multiple

We compare

constructed in §§

Under the local Tate pairing, the global Poitou–Tate duality

[

identifies
\frac{\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}]))}{\mathrm{Sel}_{\ell\textrm{-}\mathrm{str}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}]))}

with the cokernel of the singular quotient of the localization of
\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}_{\ell\textrm{-}\mathrm{str}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}]))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}]))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{/f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))}{\mathrm{loc}^{s}_{\ell}\left(\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))\right)}\right)^{\vee}}

We also have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1}&=\epsilon^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1}\\

&\in\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))\\

where the last isomorphism is non-canonical and follows from

\langle\kappa^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}

Since

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{/f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))}
\textstyle{\kappa^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

we have equality

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\kappa^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1}\right)+\mathrm{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{/f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))}{\mathrm{loc}^{s}_{\ell}\left(\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{\ell}_{\mathrm{BK}}}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E[p^{k}]))\right)}\right)^{\vee}=\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\mathrm{loc}^{s}_{\ell}(\kappa^{\mathrm{Flach},\ell}_{1})\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(E[p^{k}]))&=\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\mathrm{loc}^{s}_{\ell}\left(\kappa^{\mathrm{prim},\ell}_{1}\right)\right)\\

Therefore, the Bloch–Kato conjecture (Corollary

-adic unit.

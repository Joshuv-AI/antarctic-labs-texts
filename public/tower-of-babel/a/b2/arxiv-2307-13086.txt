Reconstruction techniques for complex potentials
Vladislav V. Kravchenko

Abstract
An approach for solving a variety of inverse coefficient problems for the Sturm-Liouville equation − y ′′ + q ⁡ ( x ) ​ y = ρ 2 ​ y -y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\rho^{2}y with a complex valued potential q ⁡ ( x ) q(x) is presented. It is based on Neumann series of Bessel functions representations for solutions. With their aid the problem is reduced to a system of linear algebraic equations for the coefficients of the representations. The potential is recovered from an arithmetic combination of the first two coefficients. Special cases of the considered problems include the recovery of the potential from a Weyl function, inverse two-spectra Sturm-Liouville problems, as well as the inverse scattering problem on a finite interval. The approach leads to efficient numerical algorithms for solving coefficient inverse problems. Numerical efficiency is illustrated by several examples.

Our approach to solving Problem A is based on two new developments. Most
importantly, in the present work we develop a new procedure for solving
Problem A based on simple relations between solutions of (
1.1

satisfying certain prescribed initial conditions at the opposite endpoints. It
allows us to convert the information on the characteristic functions of
certain regular Sturm-Liouville problems into the information on
q(x)
on the
whole interval. This procedure works equally well both for real and for
complex-valued potentials. The numerical solution of Problem A reduces to a
successive solution of two systems of linear algebraic equations. Additionally
and to the difference from the previous work
[
28
]
and

[
29
]

(NSBF) representations for solutions of (

[

) consists in a
necessity of a double numerical differentiation, which is required in a last
step for recovering
q(x)
. The new NSBF representations allow us to recover

q(x)
without any differentiation. The potential is recovered from an
arithmetic combination of the first two coefficients of the representation.
This considerably improves the final accuracy of the result. However, the use
of the new NSBF representations is restricted to smooth potentials,
q\in C^{1}\left[0,L\right]
, that reduces their applicability. Thus, in the
present work, a new method for solving Problem A for complex-valued
continuously differentiable potentials is developed. It is easy for
implementation, direct, accurate and applicable to a large variety of inverse problems.

2
Neumann series of Bessel functions representations for solutions

By
\varphi(\rho,x)
and
S(\rho,x)
we denote the solutions of the equation

-y^{\prime\prime}(x)+q(x)y(x)=\rho^{2}y(x),\quad x\in(0,L)

(2.1)

satisfying the initial conditions

\varphi(\rho,0)=1,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(\rho,0)=0,

S(\rho,0)=0,\quad S^{\prime}(\rho,0)=1.

In
[
30
]
(see also
[
27
, Sect. 9.2]
) convenient series
representations for the solutions
\varphi(\rho,x)
and
S(\rho,x)
of
(
2.1
) were obtained.

Theorem 2.1
(
[
30
]
)

Let
q\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(0,L)
. The solutions
\varphi(\rho,x)
and
S(\rho,x)
admit the
following series representations

\displaystyle\varphi(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\rho x\right)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}g_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\rho x),

(2.2)

\displaystyle S(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}s_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho x),

(2.3)

where
\mathbf{j}_{k}(z)
stands for the spherical Bessel function of order

k
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
). The coefficients
g_{n}(x)
and

s_{n}(x)
can be calculated following a simple recurrent integration
procedure (see
[
30
]
or
[
27
, Sect. 9.4]
), starting with

g_{0}(x)=\varphi(0,x)-1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{0}(x)=3\left(\frac{S(0,x)}{x}-1\right).

(2.4)

For every
\rho\in\mathbb{C}
the series converge pointwise. For every

x\in\left[0,L\right]
the series converge uniformly on any compact set of
the complex plane of the variable
\rho
, and the remainders of their partial
sums admit estimates independent of
\operatorname{Re}\rho
.

This last feature of the series representations (the independence of the
bounds for the remainders of
\operatorname{Re}\rho
) is a direct consequence
of the fact that the representations are obtained by expanding the integral
kernels of the transmutation operators (for their theory we refer to

[
37
]
,
[
38
]
,
[
47
]
) into
Fourier-Legendre series (see
[
30
]
and
[
27
, Sect. 9.2]
). In
particular, it means that for
\varphi_{N}(\rho,x):=\cos\left(\rho x\right)+\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}g_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\rho x)
and
S_{N}(\rho,x):=\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}s_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho x)
the estimates hold

\left|\varphi(\rho,x)-\varphi_{N}(\rho,x)\right|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{N}(x)\,\sinh(Cx)}{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|S(\rho,x)-S_{N}(\rho,x)\right|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{N}(x)\,\sinh(Cx)}{C}

(2.5)

for any
\rho\in\mathbb{C}
belonging to the strip
\left|\operatorname{Im}\rho\right|\leq C
,
C\geq 0
, where
\varepsilon_{N}(x)

is a positive function tending to zero when
N\rightarrow\infty
. That is,
roughly speaking, the approximate solutions
\varphi_{N}(\rho,x)
and

S_{N}(\rho,x)
approximate the exact ones equally well for small and for
large values of
\operatorname{Re}\rho
. Detailed estimates for the series
remainders depending on the regularity of the potential can be found in

[
30
]
.

Note that formulas (
2.4
) indicate that the potential
q(x)
can be
recovered from the first coefficients of the series (
2.2
) or
(
2.3
). We have

q(x)=\frac{g_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{g_{0}(x)+1}\quad\text{and\quad}q(x)=\frac{\left(xs_{0}(x)\right)^{\prime\prime}}{xs_{0}(x)+3x}.

(2.6)

We recall that any series of the type
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}J_{\nu+n}(z)

is called a Neumann series of Bessel functions (NSBF) (see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
49
, Chapter XVI]
,
[
50
]
).

Together with the solutions
\varphi(\rho,x)
and
S(\rho,x)
we will also
need to consider a solution
T(\rho,x)
of (
2.1
) which satisfies the
initial conditions at
x=L
:

T(\rho,L)=0\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\prime}(\rho,L)=1.

(2.7)

Note that

T(\rho,x)=\varphi(\rho,L)S(\rho,x)-S(\rho,L)\varphi(\rho,x).

(2.8)

Indeed, the first condition in (
2.7
) is obviously fulfilled by the
function (
2.8
), and to verify the second one, from (
2.8
) we have

T^{\prime}(\rho,L)=\varphi(\rho,L)S^{\prime}(\rho,L)-S(\rho,L)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho,L)=W\left[\varphi,S\right]\left(L\right),

where
W\left[\varphi,S\right]\left(L\right)
stands for the Wronskian
of
\varphi(\rho,x)
and
S(\rho,x)
at the point
x=L
. Since the Wronskian
of two solutions of (
2.1
) is constant (see, e.g.,
[
21
, p.
327]
), we have that

W\left[\varphi,S\right]\left(L\right)=W\left[\varphi,S\right]\left(0\right)=\varphi(\rho,0)S^{\prime}(\rho,0)-S(\rho,0)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho,0)=1,

and thus the solution (
2.8
) satisfies both initial conditions
(
2.7
).

Theorem 2.2

Let
q\in C^{1}\left[0,L\right]
. Then the solutions

\varphi(\rho,x)
,

and
T(\rho,x)
of (

(2.9)

S(\rho,x)=\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho}-\frac{\cos\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{2}}\omega(x)+\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{3}}q^{+}(x)-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sigma_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho x),

(2.10)

\displaystyle T(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sin\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\rho}+\frac{\cos\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\rho^{2}}\omega_{L}(x)-\frac{\sin\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\rho^{3}}q_{L}^{+}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\theta_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho\left(L-x\right)),

(2.11)

where

\omega(x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(s)ds,\quad\omega_{L}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{L}q(s)ds,

q^{\pm}(x):=\frac{q(x)\pm q(0)}{4}-\frac{\omega^{2}(x)}{2},\quad q_{L}^{+}(x):=\frac{q(x)+q(L)}{4}-\frac{\omega_{L}^{2}(x)}{2}.

For every
x\in\left[0,L\right]
the series converge uniformly on any
compact set of the complex plane of the variable
\rho
, and for any
\rho\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
the remainders of their partial
sums admit the estimates

\left|\varphi(\rho,x)-\varphi_{N}(\rho,x)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{N}(x)\,}{\left|\rho\right|^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh(2\operatorname{Im}\rho x)}{\operatorname{Im}\rho}},

\left|S(\rho,x)-S_{N}(\rho,x)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{N}(x)\,}{\left|\rho\right|^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh(2\operatorname{Im}\rho x)}{\operatorname{Im}\rho}},

\left|T(\rho,x)-T_{N}(\rho,x)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{N}(x)\,}{\left|\rho\right|^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh(2\operatorname{Im}\rho x)}{\operatorname{Im}\rho}},

(2.12)

where the subindex

indicates that in (
2.9
)-(
2.11
) the sum is taken up to
N
, and
\varepsilon_{N}(x)
is a
positive function tending to zero when
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

This theorem is a direct corollary of Theorem 10 from

[
32
]
. The representations (
2.9
) and
(
2.10
) are obtained from
[

by
considering the relations
\varphi(\rho,x)=\left(u(\rho,x)+u(-\rho,x)\right)/2
and

with

being the solution considered in
[

, formula (23)]
, which satisfies the conditions
u(\rho,0)=1
and
u^{\prime}(\rho,0)=i\rho
. The representation (
2.11
) is obtained from
(

) by flipping the interval. The estimates follow from

[

.
\blacksquare

Remark 2.3

In

]
it was shown that

\varphi_{0}(x)=q^{-}(x)

\sigma_{0}(x)=3\left(q^{+}(x)-\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\right).

(2.13)

Thus, the series representations (

) and (

)
can be written in a more convenient form by regrouping the terms. We have that

\frac{\cos\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{j}_{0}(\rho x)}{\rho^{2}}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\left(\cos\left(\rho x\right)-\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho x}\right)=-\frac{x\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho},

where we used the explicit forms of the spherical Bessel functions

Thus,

\varphi(\rho,x)=\cos\left(\rho x\right)+\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho}\omega(x)-\frac{x\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho}q^{-}(x)-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\varphi_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\rho x).

(2.14)

Similarly, taking into account (
2.13
), we can regroup the second,
third and fourth terms in (

):

\displaystyle-\frac{\cos\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{2}}\omega(x)+\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{3}}q^{+}(x)-\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho^{3}}\left(q^{+}(x)-\frac{\omega(x)}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega(x)\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho^{3}x}-\frac{\cos\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{2}}\right)+q^{+}(x)\left(\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho^{3}}-\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho^{3}}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle S(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\rho x\right)}{\rho}+\frac{\omega(x)}{\rho^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)}{\rho x}-\cos\left(\rho x\right)\right)+\frac{q^{+}(x)}{\rho^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\rho x\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho x)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sigma_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho x).

(2.15)

Analogously,

\displaystyle T(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sin\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\rho}-\frac{\omega_{L}(x)}{\rho^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho\left(L-x\right))}{\rho\left(L-x\right)}-\cos\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{L}^{+}(x)}{\rho^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\rho\left(L-x\right)\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho\left(L-x\right))\right)+\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\theta_{n}(x)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho\left(L-x\right)).

(2.16)

Remark 2.4

Unlike the representations from Theorem
2.1
, which are applicable in
a general case
q\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(0,L)
(and in fact even for
q
from a larger class
W_{2}^{-1}(0,L)
,
see
[
35
]
and references therein), the representations
(
2.14
), (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) are applicable to
q\in C^{1}\left[0,L\right]
. However, in this case the potential can be recovered without
any differentiation in the last step, as we explain further, in subsection

4.2
, that leads to a higher accuracy in recovering
q(x)
.

Thus, in the present work, for solving inverse coefficient problems we use the
NSBF representations (
2.14
)-(
2.16
), while for computing the input
data (direct problem) we use the NSBF representations from Theorem

2.1
.

3
Computation of
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)
from the input data
(
1.4
)

Here we adapt the procedure from
[
29
]
to the new NSBF
representations (
2.14
), (
2.15
). Denote
a_{k}:=a(\rho_{k})
and

b_{k}:=b(\rho_{k})
. We have
u(\rho_{k},x)=a_{k}\varphi(\rho_{k},x)+b_{k}S(\rho_{k},x)
, and hence

a_{k}\varphi(\rho_{k},L)+b_{k}S(\rho_{k},L)=\ell_{k}.

Substitution of (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) into this equality gives us the
equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{k}\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)}{\rho_{k}}+\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)}{\rho_{k}L}-\cos\left(\rho_{k}L\right)\right)\right)\omega(L)-\frac{a_{k}L\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)}{\rho_{k}}q^{-}(L)-\frac{a_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\varphi_{n}(L)\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\rho_{k}L)

\displaystyle+\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)\right)q^{+}(L)-\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\sigma_{n}(L)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho_{k}L)=\ell_{k}-a_{k}\cos\left(\rho_{k}L\right)-\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}}\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)

for all
\rho_{k}
. This leads to a finite system of linear algebraic
equations for computing the NSBF coefficients
\omega(L)
,
q^{-}(L)
,

q^{+}(L)
,
\varphi_{n}(L)
,
\sigma_{n}(L)
,
n=1,\ldots,N
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a_{k}\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)}{\rho_{k}}+\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)}{\rho_{k}L}-\cos\left(\rho_{k}L\right)\right)\right)\omega(L)-\frac{a_{k}L\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)}{\rho_{k}}q^{-}(L)-\frac{a_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(-1)^{n}\varphi_{n}(L)\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\rho_{k}L)

\displaystyle+\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\rho_{k}L)\right)q^{+}(L)-\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(-1)^{n}\sigma_{n}(L)\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\rho_{k}L)=\ell_{k}-a_{k}\cos\left(\rho_{k}L\right)-\frac{b_{k}}{\rho_{k}}\sin\left(\rho_{k}L\right)

(3.1)

for
k=1,\ldots,K
.

Remark 3.1

Here and below we do not look to deal with square systems of equations. In
computations a least-squares solution of an overdetermined system gives
satisfactory results and allows us to make use of all available data, while
keeping the number of the coefficients relatively small (in practice
N=4\
or

5
may result sufficient). Thus,
K\geq 2N+3
.

Remark 3.2

The value
\omega(L)
is an important parameter,
that arises in the second term of the asymptotics of the eigenvalues of the
Sturm-Liouville problems for (
2.1
). Many available in bibliography
numerical techniques for solving inverse Sturm-Liouville problems require its
prior knowledge as, e.g.,
[
44
]
or
[
23
]
. Here
we obtain it immediately, directly from the input data of the problem, as well
as the parameters
q^{-}(L)
and
q^{+}(L)
. This gives us additionally the
values of the potential
q(x)
at the end points:

q(0)=2\left(q^{+}(L)-q^{-}(L)\right)\quad\text{and\hskip 10.22217pt}q(L)=2\left(q^{+}(L)+q^{-}(L)+\omega^{2}(L)\right).

The knowledge of the coefficients
\omega(L)
,
q^{-}(L)
,
q^{+}(L)
,

\varphi_{n}(L)
,
\sigma_{n}(L)
,
n=1,\ldots,N
allows us to compute the
functions
\varphi_{N}(\rho,L)
and
S_{N}(\rho,L)
for any value of
\rho
.
Due to the estimates from Theorem
2.2
we know that the accuracy
of the approximation of the exact solutions
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)

by these approximate ones does not deteriorate for large values of
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
and even improves. So, in fact, we deal with the problem of
converting the knowledge of
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)
into the
knowledge of
q(x)
on the whole interval.

4
Recovery of
q(x)
from
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)

4.1
Main system of linear algebraic equations

Consider the identity (
2.8
), in which
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)

are already known. We use (
2.8
) to construct the main system of linear
algebraic equations. Assume
\varphi(\rho,L)
and
S(\rho,L)
to be computed
on a countable set of points
\left\{\gamma_{k}^{2}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. Denote

S_{k}:=S(\gamma_{k},L)\quad\text{and\quad}F_{k}:=\varphi(\gamma_{k},L).

(4.1)

Substitution of the NSBF representations (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and
(
2.16
) into (
2.8
) leads to a system of linear algebraic equations
for the NSBF coefficients.

Theorem 4.1

Let
q\in C^{1}\left[0,L\right]
. Then for all
x\in\left(0,L\right)

the functions

\omega(x),\qquad Q(x):=\frac{q(x)}{4}-\frac{\omega^{2}(x)}{2},\qquad q_{0}:=\frac{q(0)}{4},

\omega_{L}(x)
,
q_{L}^{+}(x)
and
\left\{\varphi_{n}(x),\,\sigma_{n}(x),\,\theta_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfy the system of linear
algebraic equations

A_{k1}(x)\omega(x)+A_{k2}(x)Q(x)+A_{k3}(x)q_{0}+A_{k4}(x)\omega_{L}(x)+A_{k5}(x)q_{L}^{+}(x)

+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{kn}(x)\varphi_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{kn}(x)\sigma_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{kn}(x)\theta_{n}(x)

=-\frac{\sin\left(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\gamma_{k}}+S_{k}\cos\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)-\frac{F_{k}\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

(4.2)

where

A_{k1}(x):=-\frac{S_{k}\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}}+\frac{F_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\gamma_{k}x)}{\gamma_{k}x}-\cos\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)\right),

A_{k2}(x):=\frac{F_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\gamma_{k}x)\right)+\frac{S_{k}x\mathbf{j}_{1}\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}},

A_{k3}(x):=-\frac{S_{k}x\mathbf{j}_{1}\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}}+\frac{F_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\gamma_{k}x)\right),

A_{k4}(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right))}{\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)}-\cos\left(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)\right)\right),

A_{k5}(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{3}}\left(\sin\left(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)\right)-3\mathbf{j}_{1}(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right))\right),

\displaystyle B_{kn}(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(-1\right)^{n}\frac{S_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{2}}\mathbf{j}_{2n}(\gamma_{k}x),\quad C_{kn}(x):=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\frac{F_{k}}{\gamma_{k}^{3}}\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\gamma_{k}x),

\displaystyle D_{kn}(x)

\displaystyle:=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{3}}\mathbf{j}_{2n+1}(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)).

Proof.

The proof of (
4.2
) consists in
substituting the series representations (
2.14
)-(
2.16
) into
(
2.8
) and grouping coefficients at
\omega(x)
,
Q(x)
and
q_{0}
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.2

Note that
q_{0}=q(0)/4
is computed in the first step, when solving system
(
3.1
), see Remark
3.2
. Thus, in the
system (
4.2
) the term
A_{k3}(x)q_{0}
can be treated as known
and moved to the right-hand side. Moreover, we have the following relations

\omega_{L}(x)=\omega(L)-\omega(x)

and

q_{L}^{+}(x)=\frac{q(x)+q(L)}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega(L)-\omega(x)\right)^{2}=\omega(L)\omega(x)+Q(x)+\frac{q(L)}{4}-\frac{\omega^{2}(L)}{2}.

The values of
\omega(L)
and
q(L)
are also computed in the first step, see
Remark
3.2
. Thus, the corresponding terms in
(
4.2
) can also be moved to the right-hand side, and
(
4.2
) takes the form

\displaystyle\left(A_{k1}(x)-A_{k4}(x)+\omega(L)A_{k5}(x)\right)\omega(x)+\left(A_{k2}(x)+A_{k5}(x)\right)Q(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{kn}(x)\varphi_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{kn}(x)\sigma_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{kn}(x)\theta_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sin\left(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\gamma_{k}}+S_{k}\cos\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)-\frac{F_{k}\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}}

\displaystyle-A_{k3}(x)q_{0}-A_{k4}(x)\omega(L)+\frac{A_{k5}(x)}{2}\left(\omega^{2}(L)-\frac{q(L)}{2}\right),\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

4.2
Final step: recovery of
q(x)

From the data obtained after solving system (
4.2
) the potential

q(x)
can be recovered in the following ways.

1. Differentiation of
2\omega(x)
gives us
q(x)
. Moreover, since typically
the numerical differentiation provides less accurate results at the endpoints
of the interval, it is convenient to use the values
q(0)
and
q(L)
which
are obtained in the first step by solving system (
3.1
), see
Remark
3.2
. Thus, the potential
q(x)
is obtained
from
q(x)=2\omega^{\prime}(x)
, and at the endpoints its previously computed
values are used.

2. The potential
q(x)
can be obtained without differentiating, directly from

Q(x)
and
\omega(x)
, which are computed from (
4.2
). We have

q(x)=4Q(x)+2\omega^{2}(x).

Again, the values
q(0)
and
q(L)
are obtained in the first step by
solving system (
3.1
), see Remark
3.2
.

Both options give similar numerical results.

5
Summary of the method

As it is seen from the previous pages, solution of the considered inverse
coefficient problem reduces to the solution of the main system of linear
algebraic equations (
4.2
) in its reduced form

\left(A_{k1}(x)-A_{k4}(x)+\omega(L)A_{k5}(x)\right)\omega(x)+\left(A_{k2}(x)+A_{k5}(x)\right)Q(x)

+\sum_{n=1}^{N}B_{kn}(x)\varphi_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}C_{kn}(x)\sigma_{n}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}D_{kn}(x)\theta_{n}(x)

=-\frac{\sin\left(\gamma_{k}\left(L-x\right)\right)}{\gamma_{k}}+S_{k}\cos\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)-\frac{F_{k}\sin\left(\gamma_{k}x\right)}{\gamma_{k}}-A_{k3}(x)q_{0}

-A_{k4}(x)\omega(L)+\frac{A_{k5}(x)}{2}\left(\omega^{2}(L)-\frac{q(L)}{2}\right),\quad k=1,\ldots,m.

(5.1)

Having solved it, one can compute
q(x)
easily, as explained in subsection

4.2
.

The proposed algorithm for solving Problem A can be summarized as follows.

1. For a given set of input data (
1.4
) solve (
3.1
).
This gives us
\omega(L)
,
q^{-}(L)
,
q^{+}(L)
,
\left\{\varphi_{n}(L),\,\sigma_{n}(L)\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
. As explained in Remark

3.2
, we obtain
q(0)
and
q(L)
.

2. Choose a set of points
\left\{\gamma_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
and
compute (
4.1
).

3. Solve (
5.1
).

4. Recover
q(x)
as explained in subsection
4.2
.

Note that the choice of the distribution of points
\gamma_{k}
as well as
their total number are arbitrary. The estimates for the remainders of the NSBF
series involved suggest that it is convenient to choose
\gamma_{k}
in a
strip
\left|\operatorname{Im}\rho\right|\leq C
, with
C\geq 0

being not too large. Numerical experiments show that a uniform distribution of

\gamma_{k}
can be less advantageous than, e.g., a logarithmically equally
spaced points distribution, with more densely distributed points near the
origin and less densely far from it.

7
Conclusions

An approach for the numerical solution of a variety of inverse coefficient
problems for the Sturm-Liouville equation
-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\rho^{2}y

with a complex valued potential
q(x)
is presented. It reduces the solution
of a problem to the solution of two systems of linear algebraic equations for
the coefficients of the Neumann series of Bessel functions representations for
solutions of the Sturm-Liouville equation, and the potential
q(x)
is
recovered from the first two components of the solution vector. The method is
easy for implementation, direct, accurate and applicable to a large variety of
inverse problems. Its performance is illustrated by numerical
examples.

R R -matrix for the XX spin chain in a staggered magnetic field
P. N. Bibikov

Abstract
We suggest the elliptic 16 × 16 16\times 16 R R -matrix for the X ​ X XX spin chain in a staggered magnetic field. This integrable model is a special case of the more general one, studied long ago within the alternative approach by V. M. Kontorovich and V. M. Tsukernik. The Yang-Baxter equation is verified with the full rigorous only in the two special degenerate trigonometric cases. For the general one, studying the first terms of the Taylor expansions for the R R -matrix entries, we give the strong evidence for implementation of the Yang-Baxter equation.



1
Introduction

The XX model, suggested in
[
1
]
, and related to the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=\sum_{n}H_{n,n+1},

(1)

where

H_{n,n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}\Big),

(2)

is one of the most studied integrable spin chains (see
[
2
]
and references therein). Here
{\bf S}^{j}_{n}
(
j=+,-,z
) are the spin-1/2 operators
associated with the
n
-th site of the chain.

The integrability of (
1
), (
2
) has been first demonstrated within the Jordan-Wigner transformation to the free-fermion model

[
1
]
and then was confirmed by various alternative approaches
[
2
]
. Namely, the Hamiltonian density
4\times 4
matrix

H=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}\otimes{\bf S}^{-}+{\bf S}^{-}\otimes{\bf S}^{+}\Big),

(3)

related to (
2
), may be represented in the form

H=\frac{1}{2}\frac{dR(\lambda)}{d\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=0},

(4)

where the
R
-matrix

R(\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\cos{\lambda}&0&0&0\\
0&1&\sin{\lambda}&0\\
0&\sin{\lambda}&1&0\\
0&0&0&\cos{\lambda}\end{array}\right),

(5)

satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation (in the Braid group form)
[
4
]

R_{12}(\lambda-\mu)R_{23}(\lambda)R_{12}(\mu)=R_{23}(\mu)R_{12}(\lambda)R_{23}(\lambda-\mu),

(6)

(as usual,
R_{12}(\lambda)\equiv R(\lambda)\otimes I_{2}
and
R_{23}(\lambda)\equiv I_{2}\otimes R(\lambda)
) and the initial condition

R(0)=I_{4}.

(7)

Here by
I_{m}
we denote the
m\times m
identity matrix.

Within the generalized version of the
[
1
]
approach, it has been proved
[
3
]
, that the model (
1
), (
2
) remains integrable even under addition of a staggered longitudinal magnetic field, when (
2
) is replaced by

H_{n,n+1}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}+h_{\rm st}[(-1)^{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n}+(-1)^{n+1}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}]\Big).

(8)

It is desirable to confirm this result by presentation of the corresponding
R
-matrix.
Since, however, (
8
) is invariant only under even-steps translations (
H_{n,n+1}=H_{n+2,n+3}\neq H_{n+1,n+2}
), the approach based on a
4\times 4
Hamiltonian density matrix needs a modification.
Namely, (
1
) should be replaced by

\hat{H}(h_{\rm st})=\sum_{n}{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3},

(9)

where the
16\times 16
matrix
{\cal H}
has the form

{\cal H}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(H^{(+)}\otimes I_{4}+I_{4}\otimes H^{(+)}\Big)+I_{2}\otimes H^{(-)}\otimes I_{2},

(10)

and

H^{(\pm)}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[{\bf S}^{+}\otimes{\bf S}^{-}+{\bf S}^{-}\otimes{\bf S}^{+}\pm h_{\rm st}\Big({\bf S}^{z}\otimes I_{2}-I_{2}\otimes{\bf S}^{z}\Big)\Big].

(11)

The direct calculation shows, that the matrix (
10
) satisfies the Reshetikhin condition
[
4
]

[{\cal H}\otimes I_{4}+I_{4}\otimes{\cal H},[{\cal H}\otimes I_{4},I_{4}\otimes{\cal H}]]=I_{4}\otimes{\cal K}-{\cal K}\otimes I_{4},

(12)

(the
16\times 16
matrix
{\cal K}
may be readily calculated), which is the strong evidence for an existence of the the corresponding

16\times 16

R
-matrix
{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})
.

Since the entries of all
known

R
-matrices depend only on rational, trigonometric (hyperbolic) or elliptic functions, it is natural to suppose,
that this also should be implemented for
{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})
.

Under this assumption, one may try to guess the entries of
{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})
, analyzing the first terms of the series expansions for their ratios. The corresponding method has been first suggested
in
[
5
]
, and then successfully used in
[
6
,
7
]
. In the next Section we present
{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})
, obtained with the use of the developed version of the approach
[
5
]
(the details of calculations will be published elsewhere).

\alpha=\alpha(\lambda)
,
\gamma=\gamma(\lambda)
and

\kappa\equiv\sqrt{1+h^{2}_{\rm st}}-h_{\rm st},\qquad\xi\equiv\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{h^{2}_{\rm st}}}-\frac{1}{h_{\rm st}},\qquad\eta=\sqrt{1+h_{\rm st}^{2}},\qquad\zeta=\frac{h_{\rm st}}{\xi}.

(20)

It may be readily checked, that

h_{\rm st}=\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{2\kappa},\qquad\xi=\frac{1-\kappa}{1+\kappa},\qquad\eta=\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{2\kappa},\qquad\zeta=\frac{(1+\kappa)^{2}}{2\kappa}.

(21)

As it is shown in the Appendix,

{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})=I_{16}+4{\cal H}\lambda+o(\lambda).

(22)

3
Verification of the result

In the two special cases,

h_{\rm st}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha(\lambda)=\tan{\lambda},\qquad\gamma(\lambda)=0,\qquad\theta(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\cos{\lambda}},

(23)

and

h_{\rm st}=i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha(\lambda)=\tanh{\lambda},\qquad\gamma(\lambda)=i\tanh{\lambda},\qquad\theta(\lambda)=1,

(24)

the verification of (
6
) may be done in straightforward (but cumbersome) way. In order to economize the reader’s time, we have presented the corresponding MAPLE 7 program.

In the general, elliptic, case the complete rigorous verification of (
6
) seems to be far from the elementary theory of elliptic functions
[
8
]
, so that the corresponding machinery is unclear for the author.
Nevertheless a strong evidence for implementation of
(
6
) may be obtained by its verifications up to the orders
o(\lambda^{j}\mu^{m-j})
with
m=0,\dots,M
and rather big
M
. The corresponding MAPLE 7 programs are also presented in the Appendix. As a supplementary result we present (up to the orders
o(\lambda^{j}\mu^{m-j})
) the proofs of the following relations between
\alpha(\lambda)
,
\gamma(\lambda)
and
\theta(\lambda)

\displaystyle\gamma(1+\alpha^{2})=h_{\rm st}\alpha(1-\gamma^{2}),

(25)

\displaystyle\gamma\theta^{2}=h_{\rm st}\alpha,

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{d\lambda}=1+\alpha^{2}+2h_{\rm st}\alpha\gamma,

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{d\gamma}{d\lambda}=h_{\rm st}(1-\gamma^{2})-2\alpha\gamma.

(28)

The rigorous proofs for these formulas also are unclear for the author. The values of the governing parameter
M
in the files ”YBellip” and ”relations” are adapted to the characteristics of the authors personal computer. The reader may increase them.

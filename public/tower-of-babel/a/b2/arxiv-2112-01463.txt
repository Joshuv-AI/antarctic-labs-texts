On the Gaussian surface area of spectrahedra
Srinivasan Arunachalam, Oded Regev, Penghui Yao

Abstract
We show that for sufficiently large n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and d = C ​ n 3 / 4 d=Cn^{3/4} for some universal constant C > 0 C>0 , a random spectrahedron with matrices drawn from Gaussian orthogonal ensemble has Gaussian surface area Θ ⁡ ( n 1 / 8 ) \Theta(n^{1/8}) with high probability.



1
Introduction

A
spectrahedron

S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a set of the form

S={\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{(i)}\preceq B\right\}}\;,

for some
d\times d
symmetric matrices

A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)},B\in\mathrm{Sym}_{d}
.
Here we will be concerned with the
Gaussian surface area
of
S
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{GSA}{\left(S\right)}=\liminf_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}^{n}{\left(S_{\delta}^{\mathrm{out}}\right)}}{\delta}\;,

(1)

where
S_{\delta}^{\mathrm{out}}={\left\{x\notin S:\mathrm{dist}(x,S)\leq\delta\right\}}
denotes the outer
\delta
-neighborhood of
S
under Euclidean distance
and
\mathcal{G}^{n}(\cdot)
denotes the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose density is
(2\pi)^{-n/2}\exp(-\|x\|^{2}/2)
.
Ball showed that the
\mathsf{GSA}
of any convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
O(n^{1/4})

[
Bal93
]
, which was later shown to be tight by Nazarov
[
Naz03
]
. Moreover, Nazarov
[
KOS08
]
showed that the
\mathsf{GSA}
of a
d
-facet polytope
1
1

1

A
d
-facet polytope is the special case of a spectrahedron when the matrices,

A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)},B
are
diagonal
.
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
O(\sqrt{\log d})
and this fact has found application in learning theory and constructing pseudorandom generators for polytopes
[
KOS08
,
HKM13
,
ST17
,
CDS19
]
. We refer the interested reader to
[
KOS08
,
HKM13
]
for more details. Motivated by recent work
[
AY21
]
, this raises the question of whether the
\mathsf{GSA}
of spectrahedra is also small. In this note we
answer this question in the negative.
Recall that a matrix
\bm{A}
drawn from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble is a symmetric matrix whose entries
{\left\{\bm{A}_{i,j}\right\}}_{i\leq j}
are all independent normal random variables of mean
0
having variance
1
if
i<j
and variance
2
if
i=j
.

Theorem 1
.

For a universal constant
C>0
and any integers
n,d\geq 1
satisfying
d\leq n/C
the following hold.
If
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. drawn from the
d\times d
Gaussian orthogonal ensemble, then the spectrahedron

\mathcal{T}=\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{i}x_{i}\bm{A}^{(i)}\preceq 2\sqrt{nd}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\Big\}

(2)

satisfies
\mathsf{GSA}(\mathcal{T})\geq c\cdot\sqrt{n/d}
for some absolute constant
c>0
with probability at least
1-C\exp(-dn^{-3/4}/C)
.
Moreover, for any integer
d
satisfying
d\leq n/C
,

\mathsf{GSA}(\mathcal{T})\leq 2\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{\pi d})

holds with probability at least
1-\exp(-n/50)
.

The theorem shows the existence of spectrahedra with
\mathsf{GSA}
of
\Omega(n^{1/8})
. (In fact, a random spectrahedron as above satisfies this with constant probability). This lower bound can be contrasted with the
\mathsf{GSA}
upper bound of Ball
[
Bal93
]
of
O(n^{1/4})
for
arbitrary
convex bodies.
Moreover, the lower bound shows that in contrast to the case of polytopes, the
\mathsf{GSA}
of spectrahedra can depend polynomially on
d
.
A natural open question is how large the
\mathsf{GSA}
of arbitrary spectrahedra can be; can spectrahedra with small
d
(say, polynomial in
n
) achieve a
\mathsf{GSA}
of
\Theta(n^{1/4})
?

2
Preliminaries

For a matrix
A
,
\lambda_{\max}(A)
is the maximum eigenvalue of
A
. We use
\bm{g},\bm{x},\bm{A}
to denote random variables.
We let
\mathcal{G}(0,\sigma^{2})
be the normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{d}
the
d\times d
Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE). Namely,
\bm{A}\sim\mathcal{H}_{d}
if
it is a symmetric matrix with entries
{\left\{\bm{A}_{i,j}\right\}}_{i\leq j}
independently distributed satisfying
\bm{A}_{i,j}\sim\mathcal{G}(0,1)
for
i<j
and
\bm{A}_{i,i}\sim\mathcal{G}(0,2)
.
To keep notations short, for
b\geq 0
we use
[a\pm b]
to represent the interval
[a-b,a+b]
. For every
c\geq 0
, we use
c\cdot[a\pm b]
to represent the interval
[ac\pm bc]
. We denote the set of
n
-dimensional unit vectors by
S^{n-1}
. Finally, we let
\chi_{n}
be the
\chi
distribution with
n
degrees of freedom, which is the square root of the sum of the squares of
n
independent standard normal variables. The following are some simple facts about the
\chi
distribution.

Fact 2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
h(\cdot)
be the pdf of
\chi_{n}
. Then the following hold.

1.

h(x)\geq c
for
x\in[\sqrt{n}\pm c]
, where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

2.

h(x)\leq\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{\pi}\cdot|x|)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Recall that by definition

h(x)=\frac{1}{2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}x^{n-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}

for
x\geq 0
, where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the gamma function, and
h(x)=0
otherwise.
By elementary calculus,
x^{n-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}
monotonically increases for
0\leq x<\sqrt{n-1}
and monotonically decreases for
x>\sqrt{n-1}
. We therefore have

x^{n-1}e^{-x^{2}/2}\geq\min{\left\{(\sqrt{n}+c)^{n-1}e^{-(\sqrt{n}+c)^{2}/2},(\sqrt{n}-c)^{n-1}e^{-(\sqrt{n}-c)^{2}/2}\right\}}

(3)

for
0<c\leq 1
and
x\in[\sqrt{n}\pm c]
.
Item 1 now follows from Eq. (
3
) and the fact that
\Gamma(z)\leq\sqrt{2\pi}z^{z-1/2}e^{-z+1/(12z)}
for all
z>0

[
AAR99
,
Jam15
]
.

Item 2 is trivial for
x\leq 0
. For
x>0
, it follows from the inequalities
\Gamma(z)\geq\sqrt{2\pi}z^{z-1/2}e^{-z}
for all
z>0

[
AAR99
,
Jam15
]
and
x^{n}e^{-x^{2}/2}\leq n^{n/2}e^{-n/2}
, which follows from the same argument as above.
∎

Lemma 3

(
[
LM00
, comment below Lemma 1]
)
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\bm{r}
be a random variable distributed according to
\chi_{n}
. Then for every
x>0
, we have

\Pr{\big[n-2\sqrt{nx}\leq\bm{r}^{2}\leq n+2\sqrt{nx}+2x\big]}\geq 1-2e^{-x}\;.

For our purposes, it will be convenient to use an alternative definition of Gaussian surface area in terms of the
inner
surface area. Namely, for

S_{\delta}^{\mathrm{in}}={\left\{x\in S:\mathrm{dist}(x,S^{c}\right\}}\leq\delta)
where
S^{c}
is the complement of the body
S
, we define,

\displaystyle\mathsf{GSA}{\left(S\right)}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{G}^{n}{\left(S^{\text{in}}_{\delta}\right)}}{\delta}\;.

(4)

It follows from Huang et al.
[
HXZ21
, Theorem 3.3]
that this definition is equivalent to the one in Eq. (
1
) when
S
is a convex body that contains the origin, which is sufficient for our purposes.

To prove our main theorem, we use the following facts, starting with a well known bound on the size of an
\varepsilon
-net of the
n
-dimensional sphere.

Fact 4

(
[
Tao12
, Lemma 2.3.4]
)
.

For every
d\geq 1
and any
0<\varepsilon<1/2
there exists an
\varepsilon
-net of the sphere
S^{d-1}
of cardinality at most

(3/\varepsilon)^{d}
.

The following claim gives a formula for the pdf of the product of two real-valued random variables.

Claim 5

(
[
RS15
, Page 134, Theorem 3]
)
.

Let
\bm{x},\bm{y}
be two real-valued random variables and
f
be the pdf of
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
. Then the pdf of
\bm{z}=\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}
is given by

g{\left(z\right)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f{\left(x,\frac{z}{x}\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{|x|}dx.

Theorem 6

(
[
LR10
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\bm{A}\sim\mathcal{H}_{d}
. For every
0<\eta<1
,
it holds that

\Pr\Big[{\lambda_{\max}{\left(\bm{A}\right)}\in 2\sqrt{d}{\big[1\pm\eta\big]}}\Big]\geq 1-C\cdot e^{-d\eta^{3/2}/C},

for some absolute constant
C>0
.

3
Proof of main theorem

The core of the argument is in the following lemma, bounding
q(2\sqrt{nd})
where
q
is the pdf of the largest eigenvalue of the matrix showing up in Eq. (
2
). We will later show that this value is essentially the same as
\mathsf{GSA}(\mathcal{T})
, where
\mathcal{T}
is the spectrahedron in the statement of the theorem.

Lemma 7
.

For
n,d\geq 1
and
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}\in\mathrm{Sym}_{d}
, let
q(\cdot)
be the probability density function of

\lambda_{\max}{\left(\sum_{i}\bm{x}_{i}A^{(i)}\right)}\;,

where
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n})
is a random vector and each entry is i.i.d. drawn from
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
.
If
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. drawn from the
d\times d
Gaussian orthogonal ensemble, then

q(2\sqrt{nd})\geq~c\cdot\sqrt{1/d}
with probability at least
1-C\exp(-dn^{-3/4}/C)
(over the choice of
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
) where
c,C>0
are universal constants. Moreover, for any integer
d
and any
d\times d
matrices
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
,
q(2\sqrt{nd})\leq 1/(2\sqrt{\pi d})
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{y}\sim S^{n-1}
be chosen uniformly from the unit sphere
and for matrices
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
, denote by
p
the pdf of
\lambda_{\max}{\left(\sum_{i}\bm{y}_{i}A^{(i)}\right)}
.
Let
\bm{r}\sim\chi_{n}
and notice that
\bm{r}\bm{y}
is distributed like
\bm{x}
(since both are spherically symmetric and by definition, have equally distributed norms). Denote by
h
the pdf of
\bm{r}
.
By Claim
5
, we have

\displaystyle q{\left(2\sqrt{nd}\right)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h{\left(2\sqrt{nd}/z\right)}p{\left(z\right)}\frac{1}{|z|}dz\;.

(5)

Using Item 2 of Fact
2
,

h(2\sqrt{nd}/z)/|z|\leq 1/(2\sqrt{\pi d})
for all
z
.
Hence Eq. (
5
) can be bounded as

(1/(2\sqrt{\pi d}))\cdot\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p{\left(z\right)}dz=1/(2\sqrt{\pi d})
, establishing the claimed upper bound on
q
.

To prove the lower bound on
q
, let
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}\sim\mathcal{H}_{d}
be
n
matrices chosen i.i.d. from the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble.
Observe that by Theorem
6
, we have

\displaystyle\Pr\Big[\lambda_{\max}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{y}_{i}\bm{A}^{(i)}\right)}\in I\Big]\geq 1-C\exp(-dn^{-3/4}/C)\;,

(6)

where

I=2\sqrt{d}\cdot[1\pm c/\sqrt{n}]\;,

for some universal constants
C,c>0
.
Define the set of matrices

G={\left\{{\left(A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}\right)}:\Pr\Big[\lambda_{\max}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{y}_{i}A^{(i)}\right)}\in I\Big]\geq\frac{1}{2}\right\}}.

Then, using the definition of
G
and Eq. (
6
), we have

\Pr\Big[{\left(\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}\right)}\in G\Big]\geq 1-2C\exp(-dn^{-3/4}/C)\;.

Now fix any
(A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)})\in G
. By definition of
G
,

\int_{I}p{\left(z\right)}dz\geq 1/2
, and therefore the right-hand side of Eq. (
5
) is at least

\displaystyle\int_{I}h{\left(2\sqrt{nd}/z\right)}p{\left(z\right)}\frac{1}{z}dz\geq c\cdot\int_{I}p{\left(z\right)}\frac{1}{z}dz\geq\frac{c}{2\sqrt{d}(1+c/\sqrt{n})}\cdot\int_{I}p(z)dz\geq\frac{c}{5\sqrt{d}}\;,

(7)

for some absolute constant
c>0
, where we used Item 1 of Fact
2
to conclude that
h(2\sqrt{nd}/z)\geq c
for all
z\in I
.
∎

We next relate
q(2\sqrt{nd})
to
\mathsf{GSA}(\mathcal{T})
.
For a vector
v\in S^{d-1}
, and
d\times d
symmetric matrices
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
, define the vector

W_{v}=\big(v^{T}A^{(1)}v,v^{T}A^{(2)}v,\ldots,v^{T}A^{(n)}v\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;.

(8)

Notice that
\mathcal{T}
can be written as

\mathcal{T}=\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{(i)}\preceq 2\sqrt{nd}\cdot{\mathbb{I}}\Big\}=\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\forall v\in S^{d-1},~\langle x,W_{v}\rangle\leq 2\sqrt{nd}\Big\}\;.

We say that
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
are
good
if

\forall v\in S^{d-1},~\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\leq\mbox{$\|{W_{v}}\|$}\leq 2\sqrt{n}\;.

Lemma 8
.

There exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that
for all integers
n
and
d\leq n/C
,
random matrices
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
drawn i.i.d. from
\mathcal{H}_{d}
are good with probability at least
1-\exp{\left(-n/50\right)}
.

Proof.

For a fixed
v\in S^{d-1}
, we claim that

\displaystyle\Pr[{n\leq\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{v}}\|$}^{2}\leq 3n}]\geq 1-2\exp{\left(-n/40\right)}.\;

(9)

To see this, observe that by definition of the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble, for
\bm{A}\sim\mathcal{H}_{d}
and unit vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
v^{T}\bm{A}v=\sum_{i,j}v_{i}v_{j}\bm{A}_{i,j}

is distributed according to

{\left(4\sum_{i<j}v_{i}^{2}v_{j}^{2}+2\sum_{i}v_{i}^{4}\right)}^{1/2}\cdot\mathcal{G}(0,1)=\sqrt{2}\cdot\mathcal{G}(0,1).

Therefore, each entry in
\bm{W}_{v}
is distributed according to
\mathcal{G}(0,2)
, and
Lemma
3
implies Eq. (
9
). We next prove that with high probability (over the
\bm{A}^{(i)}
s), for
every
unit vector
z
,
\|\bm{W}_{z}\|
is large. First, by Fact
4
, there exists a set

{\mathcal{V}}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{10^{5d}}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

of unit vectors that form a
10^{-4}
-net of the unit Euclidean sphere. Applying a union bound on
{\mathcal{V}}
, we have

\Pr[{\forall v\in{\mathcal{V}}:n\leq\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{v}}\|$}^{2}\leq 3n}]\geq 1-2\exp{\left(-n/40\right)}\cdot 10^{5d}\geq 1-\exp{\left(-n/50\right)}\;,

(10)

here we used that
d\leq n/C
for a sufficiently large
C
.

To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that if
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are such that

\forall v\in{\mathcal{V}},~n\leq\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{v}}\|$}^{2}\leq 3n\;,

then also

\forall z\in S^{d-1},~\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{z}}\|$}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\;.

Let
\bm{b}_{\max}=\max_{z\in S^{d-1}}\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{z}}\|$}
and
\bm{b}_{\min}=\min_{z\in S^{d-1}}\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{z}}\|$}
. Let
\bm{z}_{\max}
and
\bm{z}_{\min}
be the vectors achieving the maximum and the minimum respectively. Let
\bm{v}_{\max}
and
\bm{v}_{\min}
be the vectors in
{\mathcal{V}}
that are closest to
\bm{z}_{\max}
and
\bm{z}_{\min}
, respectively.
For any vectors
z,v\in S^{d-1}
with
\mbox{$\|{z-v}\|$}\leq 10^{-4}
, applying the spectral decomposition of
zz^{T}-vv^{T}
, there exist unit vectors
u_{1},u_{2}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{100}
such that

zz^{T}-vv^{T}=\lambda\cdot{\left(u_{1}u^{T}_{1}-u_{2}u_{2}^{T}\right)}\;.

(11)

Hence

\displaystyle\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{z}-\bm{W}_{v}}\|$}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\left(z^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}z-v^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}v\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\left(\mbox{\rm Tr}{\left(\bm{A}^{(i)}{\left(zz^{T}-vv^{T}\right)}\right)}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\left(u_{1}^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}u_{1}-u_{2}^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}u_{2}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{5000}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\left({\left(u_{1}^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}u_{1}\right)}^{2}+{\left(u_{2}^{T}\bm{A}^{(i)}u_{2}\right)}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bm{b}_{\max}^{2}}{2500}\;.

Choosing
z=\bm{z}_{\max}
and
v=\bm{v}_{\max}
, we have

\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{\bm{z}_{\max}}}\|$}\leq\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{\bm{v}_{\max}}}\|$}+\frac{\bm{b}_{\max}}{50}\;.

Now, since
\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{\bm{z}_{\max}}}\|$}=\bm{b}_{\max}
, we have

\bm{b}_{\max}\leq\frac{50}{49}\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{\bm{v}_{\max}}}\|$}\leq\frac{50}{49}\sqrt{3n}\leq 2\sqrt{n}\;.

Similarly, we set
z=\bm{z}_{\min}
and
v=\bm{v}_{\min}
and obtain

\bm{b}_{\min}\geq\mbox{$\|{\bm{W}_{\bm{v}_{\min}}}\|$}-\frac{\bm{b}_{\max}}{50}\geq\sqrt{n}-\frac{1}{25}\sqrt{n}>\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\;.

This concludes the result.
∎

For the following claim, we define the inner and outer shells of
\mathcal{T}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\Big\{x:\lambda_{\max}\Big(\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{(i)}\Big)\in\sqrt{n}\cdot{\big[2\sqrt{d}-\delta,2\sqrt{d}\big]}\Big\}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{out}}_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\Big\{x:\lambda_{\max}\Big(\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{(i)}\Big)\in\sqrt{n}\cdot{\big[2\sqrt{d},2\sqrt{d}+\delta\big]}\Big\}\;.

Also recall the inner and outer neighborhoods of
\mathcal{T}
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle={\left\{x\in\mathcal{T}:\exists y\notin\mathcal{T}:\mbox{$\|{x-y}\|$}\leq\delta\right\}}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle={\left\{x\notin\mathcal{T}:\exists y\in\mathcal{T}:\mbox{$\|{x-y}\|$}\leq\delta\right\}}\;.

Claim 9
.

For sufficiently small
\delta>0
and any good
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
, we have
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\delta}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{4\delta}^{\mathrm{in}}
and

\mathcal{T}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{out}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{out}}_{2\delta}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\delta}
, let
v
be a unit eigenvector of
\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{(i)}
with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{i}x_{i}A^{{\left(i\right)}})
.
Therefore,

\langle x,W_{v}\rangle=v^{T}\Big(\sum x_{i}A^{(i)}\Big)v\geq(2\sqrt{d}-\delta)\sqrt{n}\;.

Setting
y=2\delta\sqrt{n}W_{v}/\mbox{$\|{W_{v}}\|$}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x+y,W_{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x,W_{v}\rangle+2\delta\sqrt{n}\geq{\left(2\sqrt{d}-\delta\right)}\sqrt{n}+2\delta\sqrt{n}={\left(2\sqrt{d}+\delta\right)}\sqrt{n}\;,

and so
x+y\notin\mathcal{T}
.
Moreover, since
A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(n)}
are good,

\|y\|=2\delta\sqrt{n}/\mbox{$\|{W_{v}}\|$}\leq 4\delta

and therefore
x\in\mathcal{T}_{4\delta}^{\mathrm{in}}
, as desired.
For the other containment, let
x\in\mathcal{T}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{out}}
.
Then for any unit vector
v
, by Cauchy-Schwarz and using
\|W_{v}\|\leq 2\sqrt{n}
,

\langle x,W_{v}\rangle\leq 2\sqrt{nd}+2\delta\sqrt{n}\;,

implying that
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{out}}_{2\delta}
, as desired.
∎

We now prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

By Lemmas
7
and
8
,
if
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. drawn from the
d\times d
Gaussian orthogonal ensemble, then
with probability at least
1-C\exp(-dn^{-3/4}/C)
,
we have that
q(2\sqrt{nd})\geq~c\cdot\sqrt{1/d}
(where
q(\cdot)
is as defined in Lemma
7
) and
that
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are good,
where
c,C>0
are some constants.
Since
q(\cdot)
is continuous, the former
implies that
\mathcal{G}^{n}({\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{in}}_{\delta})\geq c\delta\sqrt{n/(2d)}

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Thus,

\mathcal{G}^{n}(\mathcal{T}_{4\delta}^{\mathrm{in}})\geq c\delta\sqrt{n/(2d)}

by Claim
9
. By definition of
\mathsf{GSA}(S)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{G}^{n}(S^{\mathrm{in}}_{\delta})/\delta
, we obtain the desired lower bound on
\mathsf{GSA}(\mathcal{T})
.
Similarly, by Lemmas
7
and
8
,
if
\bm{A}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{A}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. drawn from the
d\times d
Gaussian orthogonal ensemble, then
with probability at least
1-\exp{\left(-n/50\right)}
,

\mathcal{G}^{n}({\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{out}}_{\delta})\leq\delta\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{\pi d})

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Thus,

\mathcal{G}^{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\text{out}}_{\delta/2})\leq\delta\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{\pi d})

by Claim
9
. We complete the proof using
\mathsf{GSA}(S)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{G}^{n}(S^{\text{out}}_{\delta}})/\delta
.
∎

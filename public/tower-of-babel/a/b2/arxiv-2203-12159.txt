Abstract
. We reveal a new and refined application of (a weaker statement than) the Iwasawa main conjecture for elliptic curves to the structure of Selmer groups of elliptic curves of arbitrary rank. For a large class of elliptic curves, we obtain the following arithmetic consequences: • Kato’s Kolyvagin system is non-trivial. It is the cyclotomic analogue of Kolyvagin’s conjecture. • The structure of Selmer groups of elliptic curves over the rationals is completely determined in terms of certain modular symbols. It is a structural refinement of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. • The rank zero p p -converse, the p p -parity conjecture, and a new upper bound of the ranks of elliptic curves are obtained. • The conjecture of Kurihara on the semi-local description of mod p p Selmer groups is confirmed. • An application of the p p -adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture to the structure of Iwasawa modules is discussed.

1.1.
Overview

1.1.1.

In modern number theory, one of the most important themes is to understand the arithmetic meaning of special values of
L
-functions by developing the connection with the
size
of arithmetically interesting groups.
We go beyond this philosophy by giving a description of the
structure
of Selmer groups of elliptic curves in terms of a certain discrete variation of their special
L
-values.

Let
p
be a prime and
E
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Without a doubt, the Selmer group
\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^{\infty}])
of the
p
-power torsion points of
E
plays a central role in studying the arithmetic of elliptic curves.
The Selmer group encodes the information of the Mordell–Weil group
E(\mathbb{Q})
and the Tate–Shafarevich group
\textrm{{SH}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
via the fundamental exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^{\infty}])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\textrm{{SH}}(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

The celebrated Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture predicts that the rank of an elliptic curve
E
equals the vanishing order of the complex
L
-function of
E
at
s=1
and the leading term of the
L
-function knows the size of
\textrm{{SH}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
, which is conjecturally finite, and other arithmetic invariants.

The
p
-adic BSD conjecture à la Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum
[
MTT86
]
also predicts that
the rank of an elliptic curve equals the vanishing order of its
p
-adic
L
-function at the trivial character and the sizes of similar arithmetic invariants are also encoded in the leading term of the
p
-adic
L
-function.

Here, the key common observation is that a certain
complex or
p
-adic variation of
L
-values
detects the rank of an elliptic curve and the leading term of the Taylor expansion of the variation is related to the size of the Tate–Shafarevich group and other arithmetic invariants.

In this article, we focus on the
discrete variation of
L
-values
naturally arising from Kato’s Kolyvagin systems and establish the corresponding refined BSD type conjecture, which determines the structure of Selmer groups.
We call the discrete variation the
collection of Kurihara numbers
, which are explicitly built out from modular symbols (as defined in
\lx@sectionsign
1.4
) and are also realized as the image of Kato’s Kolyvagin system under a refinement of the dual exponential map.
The collection of Kurihara numbers exactly plays the role of
L
-functions in the context of
refined
Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves à la Kurihara
[
Kur14a
,
Kur14b
]
.

1.2.
Kato’s Kolyvagin systems and the Iwasawa main conjecture

We quickly review the notion of Kato’s Kolyvagin systems and the Iwasawa main conjecture for elliptic curves.

1.2.1.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of conductor
N
and
p\geq 3
a prime.
Let
T
be the
p
-adic Tate module of

and denote by

\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{Kato}}=\left\{z^{\mathrm{Kato}}_{F}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,T)\right\}_{F}

Kato’s Euler system for

where

\mathbb{Q}
.
See
\lx@sectionsign
2.3.1
for the precise convention.

1.2.2.

Let

be an integer.
Let

\mathcal{P}_{k}=\left\{\ell,\textrm{ a prime}:(\ell,Np)=1,\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}},a_{\ell}(E)\equiv\ell+1\pmod{p^{k}}\right\}

and
\mathcal{N}_{k}

\mathcal{P}_{k}
.
For

I_{n}=\sum_{\ell|n}(\ell-1,a_{\ell}-\ell-1)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

1.2.3.

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
,

\mathbb{Q}_{m}

In particular, we have

Of course, this element lies in the first Iwasawa cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbb{Q},T)=\varprojlim_{m}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{m},T)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},T\otimes\Lambda)

Denote by

1.3.
The non-triviality of Kato’s Kolyvagin systems

1.3.1.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a height one prime ideal of
\Lambda

We first recall the following concepts from the theory of Kolyvagin systems
[

]
.

•

\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{\mathrm{Kato},\infty}

\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{\mathrm{Kato},\infty}

•

These play essential roles in the new and refined applications of Kolyvagin systems to the structure of Selmer groups and the Iwasawa main conjecture, which are not observed directly from the theory of Euler systems

1.3.2.

1.4.
Modular symbols and Kurihara numbers

In order to give a precise statement of Theorem

1.4.1.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime and
E
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
such that the residual representation
\overline{\rho}
is irreducible and the Manin constant is prime to
p
.
The Manin constant assumption becomes vacuous when
E
has semi-stable reduction at
p

[
Maz78
, Cor. 4.1]
.

Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(E)q^{n}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be the newform corresponding to
E

[
BCDT01
]
.
For each
\dfrac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
, the modular symbol
\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{+}
is defined by equality

2\pi\cdot\int^{\infty}_{0}f(\dfrac{a}{b}+iy)dy=\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{+}\cdot\Omega^{+}_{E}+\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{-}\cdot\sqrt{-1}\cdot\Omega^{-}_{E}

where
\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{+}
and
\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{-}
are rational numbers
[
MT87
, (1.1)]
.
Especially, the real Néron period
\Omega^{+}_{E}
of

is taken as the absolute value of the integral of an invariant differential of a global minimal Weierstrass model of
E
over
E(\mathbb{R})
. (cf.
[
MT87
, (1.1)]
.)
Under our assumptions, we have
\left[\dfrac{a}{b}\right]^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, i.e. the modular symbols are
p
-integral.

1.4.2.

2.

We summarize some materials in

Let
E

2.1.
Selmer structures and Selmer groups

2.1.1.

Following
[
MR04
,
\lx@sectionsign
1]
, we recall the standard local conditions for Selmer groups of
T/p^{k}T
(with
k\geq 1
) and review their properties. Since
p
is odd, we ignore the infinite place.

Let
K
be a non-archimedean local field. We recall the local conditions we use.

•

the finite condition:

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(K,T/p^{k}T)=E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
via the Kummer map.

•

the relaxed condition:

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{rel}}(K,T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,T/p^{k}T)
.

•

the strict condition:

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{str}}(K,T/p^{k}T)=0
.

•

the unramified condition:

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(K,T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K,\mathrm{H}^{0}(K^{\mathrm{ur}},T/p^{k}T))
.

•

the transverse condition (when
K=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p^{k}}

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{\ell}),T/p^{k}T)).

2.1.6.

We recall two natural Selmer structures on

.

The

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is defined by

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},T/p^{k}T)
, the image of of

under the Kummer map
for every prime

so that we have

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mathbb{Q},T/p^{k}T),}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^{k}])=\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^{k}]).}

Thus, the classical Selmer structure recovers classical Selmer groups.

The

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{can}}

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{can}}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},T/p^{k}T)

We write

\textstyle{\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathbb{Q},T/p^{k}T)=\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{can}}}(\mathbb{Q},T/p^{k}T),}

In other words, the canonical Selmer structure defines the
p
-relaxed Selmer group and the dual canonical Selmer structure defines the
p
-strict Selmer group.

For

, we write

\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathrm{rel},n}=\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{can}}(n)}

\mathrm{Sel}_{n}=\mathrm{Sel}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{cl}}(n)}

When

are defined by the projective limits of Selmer groups of

3.
A refined explicit reciprocity law and Kurihara numbers

The goal of this section is to describe the precise connection between

and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
.
We first investigate when the local torsion

5.

1.8

The goal of this section is to give a complete proof of Theorem

.
In the Kolyvagin system argument (Proposition

), it is essential to change the local conditions of
p
-strict Selmer groups at carefully chosen Kolyvagin primes in order to reduce the number of generators of
p
-strict Selmer groups.
We develop a similar reduction argument for classical Selmer groups by using the comparison with the argument for
p
-strict Selmer groups.

We summarize the strategy of the proof before going through the details.
We first measure the difference between
n
-transverse Selmer groups and
n
-transverse
p
-strict Selmer groups for every

by using the global Poitou–Tate duality as in (

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{i}S_{\mathfrak{P}_{M}}/p^{M}S_{\mathfrak{P}_{M}}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{i}S_{\mathfrak{P}}/p^{M}S_{\mathfrak{P}}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Sel}_{0}(\mathbb{Q},(T/p^{M}T\otimes S_{\mathfrak{P}})^{*})^{\vee}

up to a finite abelian group whose size is independent of
M
.
Thus, we have

\mathrm{Sel}_{0}(\mathbb{Q},(T\otimes S_{\mathfrak{P}})^{*})^{\vee}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}S_{\mathfrak{P}}

up to a finite abelian group whose size is independent of
M
again, so we have

\mathrm{cork}_{S_{\mathfrak{P}}}\mathrm{Sel}_{0}(\mathbb{Q},(T\otimes S_{\mathfrak{P}})^{*})=\sum_{i}1.

We now put
\mathfrak{P}=X\Lambda
and assume Conjecture
1.14
.
Then by Proposition
7.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}1

\displaystyle=\mathrm{cork}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{Sel}_{0}(\mathbb{Q},E[p^{\infty}])

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ord}_{X\Lambda}\left(\mathrm{char}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathrm{Sel}_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])^{\vee}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}m_{i}.

Thus, Theorem
1.15
follows.

Remark 7.2
.

It seems that the same argument works for the classical Selmer groups with
p
-adic
L
-functions or signed
p
-adic
L
-functions when
E
has good ordinary reduction or good supersingular reduction at
p
, respectively.

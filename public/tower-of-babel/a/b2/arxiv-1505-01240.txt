Congruence classes of points in quaternionic hyperbolic space
Wensheng Cao

Abstract




Abstract
An important problem in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry is to classify ordered
m
-tuples of pairwise distinct points in the closure of quaternionic hyperbolic n-space,
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
,
up to congruence in the holomorphic isometry group
{\rm PSp}(n,1)
of
{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. In this paper we concentrate on two cases:
m=3
in
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
and
m=4
on
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.
New geometric invariants and several distance formulas in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry are introduced and studied for this problem.
The congruence classes are completely described by quaternionic Cartan’s angular invariants and the distances between some geometric objects for the first case. The moduli space is constructed for the second case.

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2000)
57M50, 53C17, 32M15.

Keywords
Quaternionic cross-ratio; Quaternionic Cartan’s angular invariant; Gram matrix; Congruence class; Moduli space.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
denote the real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
, the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
or the quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}
.
Let
{\bf H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space over
{\mathbb{F}}
with the boundary
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Let
\langle,\rangle
be the Hermitian product in
{\mathbb{F}}^{n+1}
of signature
(n,1)
. For
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathbb{H}}
,
the linear groups which act as the isometries in
{\bf H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, are denoted by
{\rm PU}(n,1)
and
{\rm PSp}(n,1)
respectively.
An important problem in hyperbolic geometry is to classify ordered
m
-tuples of pairwise distinct points in the closure of hyperbolic n-space
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}}

up to congruence in the holomorphic isometry group of
{\bf H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. This
problem is trivial for
m=1,2
. To deal with the cases of
m\geq 3
, one need to develop some geometric invariants or geometric tools.

The cross-ratio of a quadruple of points in
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
was defined by Cao and Waterman
[
5
]
, which coincides with the classical cross-ratio of the complex plane when
n=3
and the quaternionic cross-ratio of a quadruple of quaternions
[
3
]
when
n=5
. These classical cross ratios are useful tools in real hyperbolic geometry.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
be an ordered quadruple of pairwise distinct points in
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The classical cross-ratio was generalized to
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by Korányi and Reimann
[
16
]
as the following complex number:

{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{X}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})=\frac{\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{4},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle}{\langle{\bf p}_{4},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle},

(1)

where
{\bf p}_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n,1}
are null lifts of
p_{i}
.
This complex cross-ratio is closely related to Cartan’s angular invariant and other geometric invariants. The Cartan’s angular invariant
[
7
,
13
]
is an angle associated to a triple
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
of points in
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Such an angle
{\mathbb{A}}({\mathfrak{p}})
is defined to be the following argument:

{\mathbb{A}}({\mathfrak{p}})=\arg(-\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle)\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2],

(2)

where
{\bf p}_{i}
are the null lifts of
p_{i}
, and

\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle=\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle.

It is a shape invariant, originally used in detecting whether the corresponding triple lies on a chain or on an
{\mathbb{R}}
-circle.

The Cartan’s angular invariant
{\mathbb{A}}({\mathfrak{p}})
and some distance formulas between some geometric objects are the exact geometric invariant and tools to study congruence classes of triples
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in(\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}})^{3}
. The Cartan’s angular invariant
{\mathbb{A}}({\mathfrak{p}})
of a tripe
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in(\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})^{3}
determines its congruence class in
{\rm PU}(n,1)

[
7
,
13
]
.
The moduli space of such triples can be described as the interval
[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
. If such a triple
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
is in
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
, then its congruence class in
{\rm PU}(n,1)
is described by the three distance
\rho(p_{i},p_{j})
and Brehm’s shape invariant
[
4
]
. Here
\rho(,)
is the Bergman metric on
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
. The distance formula from a point in
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
to a complex geodesic is needed for the general congruence class problem in
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

For
n=2
and
m=4
this problem was considered by Falbel, Parker and Platis
[
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
]
. The main tool is the complex cross-ratio variety determined by three complex cros-ratios. The moduli space of ordered quadruples of pairwise distinct points in
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
was described by Cunha and Gusevskii
[
9
]
with the tool of Gram matrix. A Gram matrix associated to
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
in
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
with lift
{\bf p}=({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4})
is the Hermitian matrix

G=G({\bf p})=(g_{ij})=(\langle{\bf p}_{i},{\bf p}_{j}\rangle).

Gram matrix is an important tool in complex hyperbolic geometry
[
9
,
13
,
14
]
because its entries
\langle{\bf p}_{i},{\bf p}_{j}\rangle
are base material for other geometric invariants. We can read off it almost all the geometric information concerning the relative geometric positions of
p_{i}
.

This technique is also used to construct the invariants which describe
uniquely the
{\rm PU}(n,1)
-congruence class of an ordered
m
-tuple of pairwise distinct points in

\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and describe the corresponding moduli
space for any
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 4

[
10
]
.

The main aim of this paper is concerned with the important problem mention above in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry. We concentrate ourself on two cases:
m=3
in
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
and
m=4
on
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
. We will consider the description of congruence classes for the first case. We also will obtain a construction of the moduli space of ordered quadruples of pairwise distinct points in
\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

For this purpose we need to develop some geometric invariants and tools in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry.
We will extend the quaternionic Cartan’s angular invariant and quaternionic cross-ratio to
\overline{{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
. Several distance formulas between some geometric objects are obtained. We will introduce the Gram matrix in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry. We remark that some generalizations are new even in complex hyperbolic geometry and the introduced geometric invariants deserve further research in quaternionic hyperbolic geometry.

and

\Re(\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle)\leq 0.

(3)

By Propositions

(4)

where
{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}

For

{\bf u}
and

, respectively, we define the quaternionic line spanned by

Let

One of our main results is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
and
{\mathfrak{q}}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})

\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
. Then there exists an isometry

h\in{\rm Sp}(n,1)
such that
h(p_{1})=q_{1},h(p_{2})=q_{2},h(p_{3})=q_{3}
if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\in\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
and
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{q}})
.

(ii)

{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\in\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{q}})
and
\rho(L_{p_{1}p_{2}},p_{3})=\rho(L_{q_{1}q_{2}},q_{3})
.

(iii)

{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\in\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
,
\rho(L_{p_{1}p_{2}},p_{3})=\rho(L_{q_{1}q_{2}},q_{3})
,
\rho(L_{p_{1}p_{3}},p_{2})=\rho(L_{q_{1}q_{3}},q_{2})
and
\rho(p_{2},p_{3})=\rho(q_{2},q_{3}).

(iv)

{\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{q}}\in{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}\times{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}({\mathfrak{q}})
,
\rho(p_{1},p_{2})=\rho(q_{1},q_{2})
,
\rho(p_{1},p_{3})=\rho(q_{1},q_{3})
and
\rho(p_{2},p_{3})=\rho(q_{2},q_{3}).

For two quaternions
a=a_{0}+a_{1}{\bf i}+a_{2}{\bf j}+a_{3}{\bf k}
and

{\bf j},&\hbox{provided}\ a_{1}<0;\\

(6)

\sigma(a,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\frac{a_{2}+a_{3}{\bf i}}{\sqrt{a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}}},&\hbox{provided}\ a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}\neq 0;\\

Preliminaries

We briefly recall some necessary material on quaternionic hyperbolic geometry here and we refer to
[
1
,
8
,

We recall that a quaternion is of the form
a=a_{0}+a_{1}{\bf i}+a_{2}{\bf j}+a_{3}{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{H}}

where
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\bf i}^{2}={\bf j}^{2}={\bf k}^{2}={\bf i}{\bf j}{\bf k}=-1.
Let
\overline{a}=a_{0}-a_{1}{\bf i}-a_{2}{\bf j}-a_{3}{\bf k}
and
|a|=\sqrt{\overline{a}a}=\sqrt{a_{0}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}}
be the conjugate and modulus of
a
, respectively. We define
\Re(a)=(a+\overline{a})/2
and
\Im(a)=(a-\overline{a})/2
. For
a,b\in{\mathbb{H}}
, we have

\Re(ab)=\Re(ba)=\Re(\bar{a}\bar{b})=\Re(\bar{b}\bar{a}).

Two quaternions
a
and
b
are similar, denoted by
a\sim b
, if there exists nonzero
\lambda\in{\mathbb{H}}
such that
b=\lambda a\lambda^{-1}
. We mention that
a\sim b
if and only if
\Re(a)=\Re(b)
and
|a|=|b|
. Let

{\mathbb{S}}=\{\nu=\nu_{1}{\bf i}+\nu_{2}{\bf j}+\nu_{3}{\bf k}:\nu_{1}^{2}+\nu_{2}^{2}+\nu_{3}^{2}=1,v_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Every unit quaternion
\nu
can be written as

\nu=\exp(\theta{\bf I}):=\cos\theta+{\bf I}\sin\theta=\cos(-\theta)+(-{\bf I})\sin(-\theta)\,\,\,\,\ \ \mbox{
for some}\ \theta\in[0,\pi]\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;{\bf I}\in{\mathbb{S}}.

It is useful to view
{\mathbb{H}}
as
{\mathbb{H}}={\mathbb{C}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}{\bf j}
. Therefore any quaternion
a=a_{0}+a_{1}{\bf i}+a_{2}{\bf j}+a_{3}{\bf k}
can be uniquely expressed as

a=(a_{0}+a_{1}{\bf i})+(a_{2}+a_{3}{\bf i}){\bf j}=c_{1}+c_{2}{\bf j}=c_{1}+{\bf j}\bar{c_{2}}.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}^{n,1}
be the vector space with the Hermitian form of signature
(n,1)
given by

\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf w}\rangle={\bf w}^{*}J{\bf z}=\overline{w_{1}}z_{n+1}+\overline{w_{2}}z_{2}+\cdots+\overline{w_{n}}z_{n}+\overline{w_{n+1}}z_{1}

with matrix

J=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
0&I_{n-1}&0\\
1&0&0\\
\end{array}\right).

We define

{\rm Sp}(n,1)=\{g\in GL(n+1,{\mathbb{H}}):A^{*}JA=J\}.

Let
g\in{\rm Sp}(n,1)
. Then
g
and
g^{-1}
are of the following forms:

g=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&\zeta^{*}&b\\
\alpha&A&\beta\\
c&\delta^{*}&d\\
\end{array}\right),\ \ g^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\overline{d}&\beta^{*}&\overline{b}\\
\delta&A^{*}&\zeta\\
\overline{c}&\alpha^{*}&\overline{a}\\
\end{array}\right),

(18)

where
a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{H}}
,
A
is an

(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix over
{\mathbb{H}}
, and
\alpha,\beta,\zeta,\delta
are column vectors in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n-1}
.

Following Section 2 of
[
8
]
, let

\displaystyle V_{0}=\Bigl\{{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n,1}\setminus\{0\}:\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf z}\rangle=0\Bigr\},\,\,V_{-}=\Bigl\{{\bf z}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n,1}:\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf z}\rangle<0\Bigr\}.

Let

{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathbb{H}}^{n,1}\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{H}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be the right projection onto
{\mathbb{H}}
-projective space.
If
z_{n+1}\neq 0
then

{\mathbb{P}}
is given by

{\mathbb{P}}(z_{1},\,\ldots,\,z_{n},z_{n+1})^{T}=(z_{1}z_{n+1}^{-1},\cdots,z_{n}z_{n+1}^{-1})^{T}\in{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}.

We also define

{\mathbb{P}}(z_{1},0,\,\ldots,\,0,0)^{T}=\infty,\ \ {\mathbb{P}}(0,0,\,\ldots,\,0,z_{n+1})^{T}=o.

The Siegel domain model of the quaternionic hyperbolic
n
-space is defined to be
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}={\mathbb{P}}(V_{-})
with the boundary
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}={\mathbb{P}}(V_{0})
.
We mention that
g\in{\rm Sp}(n,1)
acts on
{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\cup\partial{\bf H}_{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
as
g(z)={\mathbb{P}}g{\mathbb{P}}^{-1}(z)
. The Bergman metric on

{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
is given by the distance formula

\cosh^{2}\frac{\rho(z,w)}{2}=\frac{\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf w}\rangle\langle{\bf w},\,{\bf z}\rangle}{\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf z}\rangle\langle{\bf w},\,{\bf w}\rangle},\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ z,w\in{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n},\ \ {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{-1}(z),{\bf w}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{-1}(w).

The holomorphic isometry group of
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
is
{\rm PSp}(n,1)={\rm Sp}(n,1)/\pm I_{n+1}
.

The
standard lift
of a finite point
z\in\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
is

\hat{\bf z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\
1\\
\end{array}\right)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{-1}(z)

and the
standard lift
of
\infty
is

\hat{\bf\infty}=(-1,0,\cdots,0)^{T}.

(19)

We will follow the above convention without any other statements in the sequel.

We close this section with the proof of Propositions
1.1
,
1.2
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.
Since
{\rm Sp}(n,1)
acts transitively on
{\mathbb{H}}
-lines in
V_{-}
and doubly transitively on
{\mathbb{H}}
-lines in
V_{0}
, and each isometry of
{\rm Sp}(n,1)
preserves the Hermitian form, we only need to consider the following two cases.

Firstly, we assume that
{\bf z}=(0,\cdots,0,1)^{T}\in V_{0}
and
{\bf w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{n+1})^{T}\in V_{0}\cup V_{-}
. Then

\langle{\bf w},\,{\bf w}\rangle=\overline{w_{1}}w_{n+1}+\overline{w_{n+1}}w_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}|w_{i}|^{2}\leq 0
and
\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf w}\rangle=\overline{w_{1}}
. If
w_{1}=0
then we see
w_{i}=0
for
i=2,\cdots,n
and so
{\bf w}={\bf z}w_{n+1}
.

Secondly, we assume that
{\bf z}=(-1,0,\cdots,0,1)^{T}\in V_{-}
. Then
\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf w}\rangle=\overline{w_{1}}-\overline{w_{n+1}}
. Since
\langle{\bf w},\,{\bf w}\rangle\leq 0
we have
\overline{w_{1}}\neq\overline{w_{n+1}}
, which implies that
\langle{\bf z},\,{\bf w}\rangle\neq 0
.
\square

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.
By properties of quaternions, we can verify that

\langle{{\bf p}_{1}}\lambda_{1},{{\bf p}_{2}}\lambda_{2},{{\bf p}_{3}}\lambda_{3}\rangle=|\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}|^{2}\bar{\lambda_{1}}\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle\lambda_{1},

\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle)=\Re(\langle{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle)=\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}},{{\bf p}_{1}}\rangle)=\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{3}},{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}}\rangle)

and

\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle)=\Re(\overline{\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle})=\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle).

These equalities imply that the sign of
\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle)
is independent of the choice of the lifts
{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}
and invariant under permutations of the points
{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}
. Due to fact that the sign of
\Re(\langle{{\bf p}_{1}},{{\bf p}_{2}},{{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle)
is invariant under the action of isometries in
{\rm Sp}(n,1)
, by the transitivity of
{\rm Sp}(n,1)
on
\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
, we need only to consider the following two cases.

Case (1): One of
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
lies on
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
.

We may assume that
p_{1}=\infty
and
p_{2}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})^{T},\ p_{3}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})^{T}\in\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
.
Note that

\langle{\hat{{\bf p}_{1}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{2}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{3}}}\rangle=r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\bar{u_{i}}r_{i}

and
-\Re(u_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}|u_{i}|^{2}
and
-\Re(r_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}|r_{i}|^{2}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\Re(\langle{\hat{{\bf p}_{1}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{2}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{3}}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re(u_{1})+\Re(r_{1})+\Re(\sum_{i=2}^{n}\bar{u_{i}}r_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Re(u_{1})+\Re(r_{1})+\sqrt{\sum_{i=2}^{n}{|u_{i}|^{2}}\sum_{i=2}^{n}{|r_{i}|^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Re(u_{1})+\Re(r_{1})+2\sqrt{\Re(-u_{1})\Re(-r_{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Bigr(\sqrt{-\Re(u_{1})}-\sqrt{-\Re(r_{1})}\Bigl)^{2}\leq 0.

Case (2): All three points
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
lie in
{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
.

We may assume that
p_{1}=(-1,0,\cdots,0)^{T}
and
p_{2}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})^{T},p_{3}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})^{T}
. Then

-\Re(u_{1})>\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}|u_{i}|^{2},\ -\Re(r_{1})>\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}|r_{i}|^{2}

and

\langle{\hat{{\bf p}_{1}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{2}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{3}}}\rangle=(u_{1}-1)(r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\bar{u_{i}}r_{i})(\bar{r_{1}}-1).

For simplicity, let
\lambda=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\bar{u_{i}}r_{i}
. Then we have

|\lambda|\leq 2\sqrt{\Re(-u_{1})\Re(-r_{1})}

and

\displaystyle\Re((r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}})(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))=-|r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}}|^{2}+(|r_{1}|^{2}+1)\Re(u_{1})+(|u_{1}|^{2}+1)\Re(r_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}}|^{2}+4\Re(u_{1})\Re(r_{1})+(|r_{1}|^{2}-2\Re(r_{1})+1)\Re(u_{1})+(|u_{1}|^{2}-2\Re(u_{1})+1)\Re(r_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|r_{1}-u_{1}|^{2}+|\bar{r_{1}}-1|^{2}\Re(u_{1})+|u_{1}-1|^{2}\Re(r_{1}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\Re(\langle{\hat{{\bf p}_{1}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{2}}},{\hat{{\bf p}_{3}}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re((r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}}+t)(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re((r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}})(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))+\Re(t(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Re((r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}})(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))+|t||(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Re((r_{1}+\bar{u_{1}})(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1))+2\sqrt{\Re(-u_{1})\Re(-r_{1})}|(\bar{r_{1}}-1)(u_{1}-1)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-|r_{1}-u_{1}|^{2}-\Bigr(|\bar{r_{1}}-1|\sqrt{\Re(-u_{1})}-|u_{1}-1|\sqrt{\Re(-r_{1})}\Bigl)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

\square

3
Quaternionic geometric invariants

3.1
Quaternionic cros-ratios

The complex cross-ratio given by (
1
) is independent of the choice of
{\bf p}_{i}
. This property enables one to use the cross-ratios either in
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
or
V_{0}
freely. In an inner product space, the concept of cross-ratio always stems from its inner products. Due to the non-commutativity of quaternions,
\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
and
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
are two different worlds for the concept of quaternionic cross-ratio. In this subsection, we define two quaternionic cross-ratios. The first one is defined on
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
and the second one is defined on
\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
.

Let
{\bf p}=({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4})
be a quadruple of points in
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
, we define

{\mathbb{P}}({\bf p})=\big({\mathbb{P}}({\bf p}_{1}),{\mathbb{P}}({\bf p}_{2}),{\mathbb{P}}({\bf p}_{3}),{\mathbb{P}}({\bf p}_{4})\big)\in(\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}})^{4}.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\bf p}=({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4})
be a quadruple of points in
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
such that
{\mathbb{P}}({\bf p})
are pairwise distinct points in
\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
. The quaternionic cross-ratio of
{\bf p}=({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4})
in
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
is defined as

{\mathbb{X}}({\bf p})={\mathbb{X}}({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4}):=\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle^{-1}\langle{\bf p}_{4},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle\langle{\bf p}_{4},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle^{-1}.

(20)

Proposition
1.1
implies that the definition above is well defined. We mention that the quaternionic cross-ratio above has been used by Platis to obtain the Ptolemaean inequality in the quaternionic hyperbolic space
[
20
]
.

Observe that, for nonzero quaternions
\lambda_{i},i=1,\cdots,4
,

{\mathbb{X}}({\bf p}_{1}\lambda_{1},{\bf p}_{2}\lambda_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\lambda_{3},{\bf p}_{4}\lambda_{4})=\overline{\lambda}_{1}{\mathbb{X}}({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4})\overline{\lambda}_{1}^{-1}.

(21)

The above equality is dominant among the properties of quaternionic cross-ratio in
V_{0}\cup V_{-}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
be any quadruple of pairwise distinct points in

\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
. The quaternionic cross-ratio of
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
in
\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
is defined as

{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{X}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}):={\mathbb{X}}(\hat{\bf p}_{1},\hat{\bf p}_{2},\hat{\bf p}_{3},\hat{\bf p}_{4}),

(22)

where
\hat{\bf p}_{i}
is the standard lift of
p_{i}
.

We mention that the restriction of Definition
3.2
on
\partial{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}}
is identical with the Korányi-Reimann complex cross-ratio
[
16
]
. We are now ready to obtain some properties of quaternionic cross-ratios.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
be any quadruple of pairwise distinct points in

\overline{{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}}
with lifts
{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3},{\bf p}_{4}
and
h\in{\rm Sp}(n,1)
. Then

(i)

\Re\big({\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})\big)=\Re\big({\mathbb{X}}({\bf p})\big)=\Re\big({\mathbb{X}}(h(p_{1}),h(p_{2}),h(p_{3}),h(p_{4}))\big);

(ii)

|{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})|=|{\mathbb{X}}({\bf p})|=|{\mathbb{X}}(h(p_{1}),h(p_{2}),h(p_{3}),h(p_{4}))|.

Proof.

Noting that
{\bf p}_{i}=\hat{\bf p}_{i}\lambda_{i}
for some
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
and the standard lift of
h(p_{i})
can be expressed as
h\hat{\bf p}_{i}\mu_{i},\mu_{i}\neq 0,
we have the following two equations:

{\mathbb{X}}({\bf p})={\mathbb{X}}(\hat{\bf p}_{1}\lambda_{1},\hat{\bf p}_{2}\lambda_{2},\hat{\bf p}_{3}\lambda_{3},\hat{\bf p}_{4}\lambda_{4})=\overline{\lambda}_{1}{\mathbb{X}}(\hat{\bf p}_{1},\hat{\bf p}_{2},\hat{\bf p}_{3},\hat{\bf p}_{4})\overline{\lambda}_{1}^{-1}=\overline{\lambda}_{1}{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})\overline{\lambda}_{1}^{-1},

{\mathbb{X}}(h(p_{1}),h(p_{2}),h(p_{3}),h(p_{4}))={\mathbb{X}}(h\hat{\bf p}_{1}\mu_{1},h\hat{\bf p}_{2}\mu_{2},h\hat{\bf p}_{3}\mu_{3},h\hat{\bf p}_{4}\mu_{4})=\overline{\mu_{1}}{\mathbb{X}}({\mathfrak{p}})\overline{\mu}_{1}^{-1}.

The two equations above conclude the proof.
∎

We mention that those properties of quaternionic cross-ratio has been used in
[

15
]
to obtain the generalized Jørgensen’s inequalities in the quaternionic hyperbolic geometry.

The following proposition displays the relationships of quaternionic cross-ratios of a given quadruple under permutations of its points. One should compare them with analogous results in
[

and
[
20

Proposition 3.2
.

(ii)

\displaystyle 1.

Proposition 3.6

5.1

Given a quadruple

It is obvious that

Let

Thus, to each quadruple

\omega_{3}=g_{13}=\frac{\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle}{|\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\rangle|},\ \omega_{1}=g_{14}=\frac{\langle{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{1}\rangle{\mathbb{X}}_{2}({\bf p})\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle}{|\langle{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2}\rangle|^{2}},\ \omega_{2}=g_{24}=\omega_{3}{\mathbb{X}}_{3}({\bf p}).

A matrix of the form (
72
) is called a
semi-normalised Gram matrix
. We represent such a Hermitian matrix by

V({\bf m})=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}).

Let
G
and
\tilde{G}
be two semi-normalised Gram matrix represented by
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
and
(\tilde{\omega}_{1},\tilde{\omega}_{2},\tilde{\omega}_{3})
, respectively. By Proposition
5.4
(i),
\tilde{G}
and
G
are equivalent if and only if there exists a matrix

D=\mu I_{4},\ \mu\in{\mathbb{H}},\ |\mu|=1

such that

\tilde{G}=D^{*}GD.

That is

(\tilde{\omega}_{1},\tilde{\omega}_{2},\tilde{\omega}_{3})=\bar{\mu}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\mu=(\bar{\mu}\omega_{1}\mu,\bar{\mu}\omega_{2}\mu,\bar{\mu}\omega_{3}\mu).

Based on the above observation, we define an equivalent relation in
{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
by the above equality and denote it by

(\tilde{\omega}_{1},\tilde{\omega}_{2},\tilde{\omega}_{3})\simeq(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}).

Let
{\rm M}
be the set of representations
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
. Then the configuration space
\mathcal{M}(n)
can be viewed as the quotient
of
{\rm M}
under this equivalent relation. That is
\mathcal{M}(n)=M/\simeq
. The description of
{\rm M}
is left as an exercise for the reader.

We revert to the original topic of this subsection. The following theorem provide a criterion on classification of congruence class of two quadruples of pairwise distinct points in
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
, which somewhat can be viewed as a description of the above equivalent relation by geometric invariants.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
and
{\mathfrak{q}}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
be two
quadruple of pairwise distinct points in
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}.
Then there exists an isometry

h\in{\rm Sp}(n,1)
such that
h(p_{1})=q_{1},h(p_{2})=q_{2},h(p_{3})=q_{3},h(p_{4})=q_{4}
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(i)

{\mathbb{X}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})\sim{\mathbb{X}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
,
{\mathbb{X}}(p_{1},p_{4},p_{3},p_{2})\sim{\mathbb{X}}(q_{1},q_{4},q_{3},q_{2})
and
{\mathbb{X}}(p_{2},p_{4},p_{3},p_{1})\sim{\mathbb{X}}(q_{2},q_{4},q_{3},q_{1})
;

(ii)

{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
,
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{4})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{4})
and
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
.

We need the following properties of quaternions to prove Theorem
5.2
. The proof of them is by direct computations.

Proposition 5.5
.

(i)

For any quaternions
a
and
b
we have

\Re(ab)=\Re(a)\Re(b)+\Re\big(\Im(a)\Im(b)\big);

(ii)

For
a=a_{1}{\bf i}+a_{2}{\bf j}+a_{3}{\bf k}
and
b=b_{1}{\bf i}+b_{2}{\bf j}+b_{3}{\bf k}
,
a_{i},b_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the angle
\phi
between vectors
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
and
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
is

\phi=\arccos\big(\frac{\Re(a\bar{b})}{|ab|}\big)=\arccos\big(\frac{-\Re(ab)}{|ab|}\big).

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.
The necessity is clear. Since
{\rm Sp}(n,1)
acts transitively on
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
we may assume that
p_{1}=q_{1}=o
and
p_{2}=q_{2}=\infty
. For sufficiency we need to find an
h\in G_{o,\infty}
such that

h(p_{3})=q_{3},\ h(p_{4})=q_{4}

under the conditions (i) and (ii).
For convenience, we set

p_{3}=(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}),\ p_{4}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}),\ q_{3}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}),\ q_{4}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n}).

The condition
{\mathbb{X}}(o,\infty,p_{3},p_{4})\sim{\mathbb{X}}(o,\infty,q_{3},q_{4})
implies that

\frac{|r_{1}|}{|s_{1}|}=\frac{|z_{1}|}{|w_{1}|},\ \ \frac{\Re(r_{1}\bar{s_{1}})}{|r_{1}||s_{1}|}=\frac{\Re(z_{1}\bar{w_{1}})}{|z_{1}||w_{1}|}.

(73)

The condition
{\mathbb{X}}(o,p_{4},p_{3},\infty)\sim{\mathbb{X}}(o,q_{4},q_{3},\infty)
implies that

\frac{|r_{1}|}{|\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle|}=\frac{|z_{1}|}{|\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle|},\ \ \frac{\Re(r_{1}\overline{\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle})}{|r_{1}||\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle|}=\frac{\Re(z_{1}\overline{\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle})}{|z_{1}||\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle|}.

(74)

The condition
{\mathbb{X}}(\infty,p_{4},p_{3},o)\sim{\mathbb{X}}(\infty,q_{4},q_{3},o)
implies that

\frac{|s_{1}|}{|\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle|}=\frac{|w_{1}|}{|\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle|},\ \ \frac{\Re(s_{1}\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle)}{|s_{1}||\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{4}},{\hat{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle|}=\frac{\Re(w_{1}\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle)}{|w_{1}||\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{4}},{\hat{\bf q}_{3}}\rangle|}.

(75)

The conditions
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
,
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{4})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{4})
and

{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{H}}}(q_{2},q_{3},q_{4})
imply that

\frac{\Re(r_{1})}{|r_{1}|}=\frac{\Re(z_{1})}{|z_{1}|},\ \ \frac{\Re(s_{1})}{|s_{1}|}=\frac{\Re(w_{1})}{|w_{1}|},\ \frac{\Re(\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{4}},{\hat{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle)}{|\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{4}},{\hat{\bf p}_{3}}\rangle|}=\frac{\Re(\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{4}},{\hat{\bf q}_{3}}\rangle)}{|\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{4}},{\hat{\bf q}_{3}}\rangle|}.

(76)

By Proposition
5.5
and (
73
)-(
76
), we know that the three angles between pairs of the vectors

\Im\big(\frac{r_{1}}{|r_{1}|}\big),\Im\big(\frac{s_{1}}{|s_{1}|}\big),\Im\big(\frac{\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle}{|\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle|}\big)

equal the three angles between the corresponding pairs of the vectors

\Im\big(\frac{z_{1}}{|z_{1}|}\big),\Im\big(\frac{w_{1}}{|w_{1}|}\big),\Im\big(\frac{\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle}{|\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle|}\big),

respectively.

Let

\kappa=\frac{|r_{1}|}{|z_{1}|}=\frac{|s_{1}|}{|w_{1}|}=\frac{|\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle|}{|\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle|}.

Due to the action of
{\rm Sp}(1)
by conjugation in
{\mathbb{H}}
coincides with the action of
{\rm SO}(3)
, therefore there exists a
\mu\in{\rm Sp}(1)
such that

r_{1}=\kappa\bar{\mu}z_{1}\mu,\ \ s_{1}=\kappa\bar{\mu}w_{1}\mu,\ \ \langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle=\kappa\bar{\mu}\langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle\mu.

Noting that

\langle{\hat{\bf p}_{3}},{\hat{\bf p}_{4}}\rangle=r_{1}+\bar{s_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}{\bar{s_{i}}r_{i}},\ \langle{\hat{\bf q}_{3}},{\hat{\bf q}_{4}}\rangle=z_{1}+\bar{w_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}{\bar{w_{i}}z_{i}},

we have that

\sum_{i=2}^{n}{\bar{s_{i}}r_{i}}=\kappa\bar{\mu}\big(\sum_{i=2}^{n}{\bar{w_{i}}z_{i}}\big)\mu.

Since
p_{3},p_{4}
and
q_{3},q_{4}
are on
\partial{\bf H}_{{\mathbb{H}}}^{n}
, we have that

\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}|r_{i}|^{2}}{\sum_{i=2}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}}=\frac{\sum_{i=2}^{n}|s_{i}|^{2}}{\sum_{i=2}^{n}|w_{i}|^{2}}=\kappa

and so that we can find an
A\in{\rm Sp}(n-1)
such that

A(r_{2},\cdots,r_{n})^{T}=\sqrt{\kappa}(z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})^{T}\mu,\,A(s_{2},\cdots,s_{n})^{T}=\sqrt{\kappa}(w_{2},\cdots,w_{n})^{T}\mu.

Hence
h={\rm diag}(\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{\kappa}},A,\sqrt{\kappa}\mu)
is the desired isometry.
\square

Some Variants of Apéry-Type Series and Level Four Colored Multiple Zeta Values
Ce Xu and Jianqiang Zhao[1mm] a. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University, Wuhu 241002, PRCb. Department of Mathematics, The Bishop’s School, La Jolla, CA 92037, USA

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we study Apéry-type series involving the central binomial coefficients

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n_{1}}}\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

and its variations where the summation indices may have mixed parities and some or all “
>
” are replaced by “
\geq
”, as long as the series are defined. These sums have naturally appeared in the calculation of massive Feynman integrals by the work of Jegerlehner, Kalmykov and Veretin. We show that all these sums can be expressed as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of the real and/or imaginary parts of the colored multiple zeta values at level four, i.e., special values of multiple polylogarithms at fourth roots of unity. We also show that the corresponding series where
{\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}}/4^{n_{1}}
is replaced by
{\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}}^{2}/16^{n_{1}}
can be expressed in a similar way except for a possible extra factor of
1/\pi
.

Keywords
: Apéry-type series, central binomial coefficient, colored multiple zeta value, multiple polylogarithm, iterated integral.

AMS Subject Classifications (2020):
Primary 11M32; Secondary 11B65, 11B37, 44A05, 33B30.

Introduction

Set
a_{0}=a_{0}(x)=1
,
a_{n}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}x^{2n}
and
a_{n}=a_{n}(1)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the first part of this series
[
25
]
, we considered the following Apéry-type inverse binomial series

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}^{-1}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}\quad

for all positive integers
s_{1}\geq 2,s_{2},\dots,s_{d}\geq 1
, and their variants with some or all of

n_{j}
’s replaced by
2n_{j}\pm 1
and some or all of “
>
” replaced by “
\geq
”, as long
as the series are defined. By generalizing Akhilesh’s ideas in
[
1
,
2
]

we showed that the above sum can be expressed as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of the real and/or
the imaginary parts of some colored multiple zeta values of level 4, i.e., multiple polylogarithms evaluated at 4th roots of unity.
We also proved similar results after replacing
a_{n_{1}}^{-1}
by
a_{n_{1}}^{-2}
.

In this paper, we will turn to another class of Apéry-type series defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}^{2}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

(1.1)

and similar variants with the summation indices having mixed parities.
We call these
Apéry-type (central) binomial series
(not inverse type).
Instances of this type already appeared in Leshchiner’s work
[
18
]
extending Apéry’s formula.
These sums have also appeared in the calculation of massive Feynman integrals
[
19
]
.

Recall that for any positive integers
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}
and
N
th roots of unity
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{d}
the

colored multiple zeta values
(CMZVs) of level
N
are defined by

\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots z_{d}^{n_{d}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots n_{d}^{s_{d}}},

(1.2)

which converge if
(s_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
(see
[
22
]
and
[
27
, Ch. 15]
).
Starting from the 1990s,
mathematicians and theoretical physicists have been attracted to the study of
colored multiple zeta values due to their frequent and
sometimes unexpected appearance in quite a few different branches of mathematics and physics,
in particular, the computation of many Feyman diagrams (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
).

The CMZVs can be expressed using Chen’s iterated integrals (see
[
27
, Sec. 2.1]
):

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{z}}})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\texttt{a}}^{s_{1}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{a_{1}}\cdots{\texttt{a}}^{s_{d}-1}{\texttt{x}}_{a_{d}},

where
{\texttt{a}}=dt/t
,
{\texttt{x}}_{a}=dt/(a-t)
, and
a_{j}=1/(z_{1}\cdots z_{j})
for all
j=1,\dots,d
. The theory of iterated integrals was developed firstly by K.T. Chen in the 1960’s. It has played important roles in the study of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry in the past half century. Its simplest form is

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}(t)dtf_{2}(t)dt\cdots f_{p}(t)dt=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}(t)dt\circ f_{2}(t)dt\circ\cdots\circ f_{p}(t)dt

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int\limits_{1>t_{1}>\cdots>t_{p}>0}f_{1}(t_{1})f_{2}(t_{2})\cdots f_{p}(t_{p})dt_{1}dt_{2}\cdots dt_{p}.

One can extend these to iterated integrals over any piecewise smooth path on the complex plane via pull-backs.
We refer the interested reader to Chen’s original work
[
14
,
15
]
for more details.

The key idea to compute the infinite sums (
1.1
) and their odd-indexed variations is to derive some recursive formulas for the “tails” of a variable version of these series, which are given by the following (see also (
5.18
)–(
5.21
) and (
3.12
)).

Recursive Formulas.
For all
n\geq 0
,
s\geq 1
, and
y\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{m}(\sin y)}{(2m)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\cot t\,dt)^{s-1}(1-\csc t\,dt\circ\sec t)a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\,dt,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{m}(\sin y)}{(2m+1)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\csc y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\cot t\,dt)^{s-1}(1-dt\circ\csc t\sec t)a_{n}(\sin t)\sin t\tan t\,dt,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{m}(\sin y)}{(2m+1)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\csc y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\cot t\,dt)^{s-1}(\csc t-dt\circ\sec t)a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\,dt,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{m}(\sin y)}{2m-1}=

\displaystyle\,\cos y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\sec t\,dt,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{m}(\sin y)}{(2m-1)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\sin y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\cot t\,dt)^{s-2}(\cot^{2}t\,dt)a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\sec t\,dt\quad(s\geq 2).

Here we have used a notation generalizing that for the iterated integrals.
See the beginning of the next section for details.

We remark that a particular type of odd variations already appeared implicitly
in
[
19
, (A.25)]
. Indeed, it is easy to
verify that
(2n)a_{n}=(2n-1)a_{n-1}
so that for any function
S

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{(n+1)^{c}}S(n)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n-1}}{(2n)n^{c-1}}S(n-1)=\displaystyle\sum_{n>0}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{(2n-1)n^{c-1}}S(n-1).

(1.3)

We now summarize the content of this paper.
In the next three sections, we will repeatedly apply the recursive formulas in the above to
obtain the iterated integral expressions of three general Apéry-type binomial series. In Section 5,
we will analyze these iterated integrals carefully to prove the main result of this paper relating these
sums to the CMZVs of level 4. We then apply a beta integral to derive the iterated integral expressions
for the corresponding Apéry-type series in which

appears. In the last section, we

[

and provide a few enlightening examples.
We have computed many other examples and attach them as two appendices to this paper.

2
Apéry-type central binomial series

In this section, we will consider the Apéry-type series defined in (
1.1
).
We will need to extend Chen’s iterated integrals by combining 1-forms and functions as follows.
For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, 1-forms
f_{1}(t)\,dt,\dots,f_{r+1}(t)\,dt
and functions
F_{1}(t),\dots,F_{r}(t)
,

G_{1}(t),\dots,G_{r}(t)
, we extend the definition of iterated integrals by setting recursively

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r}{}\hskip-10.11804pt{\scriptstyle\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt\Big[F_{j}(t)+f_{j}(t)\,dt\circ G_{j}(t)\Big]\bigg)\circ f_{r+1}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}{}\hskip-10.11804pt{\scriptstyle\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt\Big[F_{j}(t)+f_{j}(t)\,dt\circ G_{j}(t)\Big]\bigg)\circ\big(F_{r}(t)f_{r+1}(t)\big)\,dt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}{}\hskip-10.11804pt{\scriptstyle\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt\Big[F_{j}(t)+f_{j}(t)\,dt\circ G_{j}(t)\Big]\bigg)\circ f_{r}(t)\,dt\circ G_{r}(t)f_{r+1}(t)\,dt,

where

\prod_{j=1}^{r}{}\hskip-10.11804pt{\scriptstyle\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt{\alpha}_{j}={\alpha}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ{\alpha}_{r}.

Our first result concerns the tails of the Apéry-type series (
1.1
). To be consistent with all the major theorems
of this paper, we formulate it as an even-indexed variation. For
s\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\displaystyle p_{s}(t):=

\displaystyle\,\tan t\,dt\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(1-\csc t\,dt\circ\sec t).

(2.4)

Theorem 2.1
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
y\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
we have

\displaystyle{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\sin y)_{n}:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}(\sin y)}{(2n_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d})^{s_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\cot y\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}p_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ p_{s_{d}}\circ a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\,dt.

Here if
y=\pm\pi/2
then the right-hand side of the above is defined to be the limit as
y\to\pm\pi/2
.

We will again call the sum
|{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}|:=s_{1}+\dots+s_{d}
the weight and
d
the depth of the series
{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\sin y)
, respectively.

Proof.

The proof is in the same spirit as that of
[
2
, Thm. 4]
. Define

\displaystyle u_{n}(y)=\tan y\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m-1}y}{2m-1},\quad v_{n}(y)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}t}{4^{n}}\tan^{2}t\,dt.

We claim that
u_{n}(y)=v_{n}(y)
. Indeed, first we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\Big(u_{n-1}(y)-u_{n}(y)\Big)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}y\tan y}{(2n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}y}{4^{n}}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{(2n-1)+\sec^{2}y}{2n-1}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\Big(v_{n-1}(y)-v_{n}(y)\Big)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-3}y}{4^{n}}\tan^{2}t\left(4\binom{2n-2}{n-1}-{\binom{2n}{n}}\sin^{2}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}y}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\sec^{2}y\left(\displaystyle\frac{2n}{2n-1}-\sin^{2}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}y}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\sec^{2}y\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n-1}+\cos^{2}y\right).

Hence for all
n\geq 1

\displaystyle u_{n-1}(y)-u_{n}(y)=v_{n-1}(y)-v_{n}(y)

since clearly the two sides both vanish when
y=0
.
Further, for all
y\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(y)=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(y)=0

since by Stirling’s formula

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\sim\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

By telescoping we see immediately that
u_{n}(y)=v_{n}(y)
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m-1}y}{2m-1}=\cot y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}t}{4^{n}}\tan^{2}t\,dt.

(2.5)

Differentiating (
2.5
) we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}a_{m}\sin^{2m-2}y\cos y={\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}y}{4^{n}}\sec y-\csc^{2}y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\sec t\,dt.

(2.6)

Multiplying this by
\sin y
and integrating, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m}y}{2m}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(1-\csc t\,dt\circ\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\,dt.

Repeatedly multiplying these by
\cot y
and integrating, we have for all
s\geq 1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m}y}{(2m)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(1-\csc t\,dt\circ\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\,dt.

(2.7)

The general depth cases of the theorem follow readily from the above depth one cases by iteration. We leave the details
to the interested reader.
∎

Example 2.2
.

If
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{d}=1
then we see that

\displaystyle{\alpha}(1;\sin y):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}y}{2n}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left.\log\displaystyle\frac{\csc t}{\csc t+\cot t}\right|_{0}^{y}=\log 2-\log(1+\cos y).

Thus
{\alpha}(1;1)=\log 2
. When
d=2
we see that

\displaystyle{\alpha}(1,1;\sin y):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\Big(\csc t\,dt\circ\sec t-\cot t\,dt\Big)(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt+\csc t\,dt\circ(\sec t-1)(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt.

Note that

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}(\sec t-1)(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\displaystyle\frac{(1-\cos t)^{2}\,dt}{\sin t\cos t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\displaystyle\frac{(\cos t-1)\,d\cos t}{(1+\cos t)\cos t}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\left(\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+\cos t}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos t}\right)d\cos t=\log\displaystyle\frac{(1+\cos u)^{2}}{4\cos u}.

Setting
a=\cos y
we have

\displaystyle{\alpha}(1,1;\sin y):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\csc t-\cot t)\,dt\Big)^{2}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\log\displaystyle\frac{(1+\cos t)^{2}}{4\cos t}\csc t\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+a}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\cos^{2}t}\log\displaystyle\frac{(1+\cos t)^{2}}{4\cos t}d\cos t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+a}+\displaystyle\int_{a}^{1}\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u^{2}}\log\displaystyle\frac{(1+u)^{2}}{4u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+a}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Li_{2}(1-a)-\Li_{2}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Li_{2}(-a)

\displaystyle\,+\log 2\log(1+a)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log(a)\log(1+a)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}2-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}(1+a)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\zeta(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Li_{2}(1-a)-\Li_{2}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Li_{2}(-a)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log(a)\log(1+a)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\zeta(2).

Applying the identity
\Li_{2}(t)+\Li_{2}(1-t)+\log t\log(1-t)=\zeta(2)
with
t=1/2
we see that

\displaystyle{\alpha}(1,1;1):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}2+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\zeta(2)\approx 0.65146002.

3
An odd variant

In this section, we turn to the Apéry-type series similar to (
1.1
) but with
summation indices restricted to odd numbers only. Set

\displaystyle\theta_{s}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\tan t\,dt\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(1-dt\circ\csc t\,\sec t),

(3.8)

\displaystyle q_{s}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\sec t\,dt\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(\csc t-dt\circ\sec t).

(3.9)

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
y\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
we have

\displaystyle{\beta}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\sin y)_{n}:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}(\sin y)}{(2n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d}+1)^{s_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\csc y\cot y\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\theta_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\theta_{s_{d}}\circ a_{n}(\sin t)\sin t\tan t\,dt,

\displaystyle{\beta}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\sin y)_{n}:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{d}\geq n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}(\sin y)}{(2n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d}+1)^{s_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\cot y\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}q_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ q_{s_{d}}\circ a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\,dt.

Here when
y=\pm\pi/2
we understand the right-hand side of the above as the limits
y\to\pm\pi/2
.

Proof.

Recall that from (
2.6
) we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}a_{m}\sin^{2m-2}y\cos y={\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}y}{4^{n}}\sec y-\csc^{2}y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\sec t\,dt.

(3.10)

Multiplying this by
\sin^{2}y
and integrating, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m+1}y}{2m+1}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(1-dt\circ\csc t\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n+1}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\,dt.

Repeatedly multiplying this by
\cot y
and integrating, we have for all
s\geq 1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m+1}y}{(2m+1)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(1-dt\circ\csc t\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n+1}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\,dt.

(3.11)

On the other hand, multiplying (
3.10
) by
\sin^{2}y

and adding
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}\sin^{2n}y\cos y
on both sides we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\sin^{2m}y\cos y={\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}y}{4^{n}}\sec y-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n}t}{4^{n}}\tan t\sec t\,dt.

Integrating we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m+1}y}{2m+1}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(\csc t-dt\circ\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n+1}t}{4^{n}}\sec t\,dt.

Repeatedly multiplying these by
\cot y
and integrating, we have for all
s\geq 2

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m+1}y}{(2m+1)^{s}}=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-1}(\csc t-dt\circ\sec t){\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n+1}t}{4^{n}}\sec t\,dt.

(3.12)

The general cases of the theorem follow readily from the above depth one cases by iteration.
∎

Another odd variant

In this section, we study another variant of the Apéry-type series similar to (
1.1
). Even though these series look very similar to those in the previous section, they are fundamentally different and therefore require different approaches to handle them. Set

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{s}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tan t\,dt,&\hbox{if $s=1$;}\\
\tan^{2}t\,dt\circ(\cot t\,dt)^{s-2}\circ\cot^{2}t\,dt,&\hbox{if $s\geq 2$.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
{\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
y\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
we have

\displaystyle{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{\sl{s}}};\sin y)_{n}:=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{d}>n}\displaystyle\frac{a_{n_{1}}(\sin y)}{(2n_{1}-1)^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d}-1)^{s_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\cos y\cot y\displaystyle\frac{d}{dy}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\varphi}_{s_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ{\varphi}_{s_{d}}\circ a_{n}(\sin t)\tan t\sec t\,dt.

Here when
y=\pm\pi/2
we understand the right-hand side of the above as the limits
y\to\pm\pi/2
.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m-1}y}{2m-1}=\cot y\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}t}{4^{n}}\tan^{2}t\,dt.

For any
s\geq 2
we can repeatedly multiply this equation by
\cot y
and integrate to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m>n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{m}}{\binom{2m}{m}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2m-1}y}{(2m-1)^{s}}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan t}\bigg)^{s-2}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{\tan^{2}t}{\binom{2n}{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2n-1}t}{4^{n}}\tan^{2}t\,dt.

(4.15)

This proves the case of
d=1
in the theorem. The general depth case now follows easily by repeating this procedure.
We leave the details to the interested reader.
∎

Example 4.2
.

If
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{d}=1

8
Conclusion

In this paper, by using iterated integrals we have demonstrated that the Apéry-type series

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}}{4^{n_{1}}(2n_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d})^{s_{d}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{{\binom{2n_{1}}{n_{1}}}^{2}}{4^{2n_{1}}(2n_{1})^{s_{1}}\cdots(2n_{d})^{s_{d}}}

can be expressed as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of the real and/or imaginary parts of the
colored multiple zeta values of level 4, with an extra factor of
1/\pi
for the squared version.
The same claim still holds if some or all the indices
2n_{j}
are replaced by
2n_{j}\pm 1

and “
>
” replaced by “
\geq
” as long as the series converge.

From numerical evidence, it seems
that similar results can be obtained if
4^{n_{1}}
is replaced by
8^{n_{1}}
,
12^{n_{1}}
or
16^{n_{1}}
,
however, the level must be increased significantly. Currently, our method can only
show that these series can be expressed in terms of multiple polylogarithms at suitable algebraic points.
In our next paper
[
26
]
, we will study the alternating versions of the series treated
in this paper and those series in which the binomial coefficients appear on the denominators.

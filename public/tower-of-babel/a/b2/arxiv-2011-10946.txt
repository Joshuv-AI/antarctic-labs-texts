Convergence of a Godunov scheme for degenerate conservation laws with BV spatial flux and a study of Panov type fluxes
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, John D. Towers, Ganesh Vaidya

Abstract
In this article we prove convergence of the Godunov scheme of [ 16 ] for a scalar conservation law in one space dimension with a spatially discontinuous flux. There may be infinitely many flux discontinuities, and the set of discontinuities may have accumulation points. Thus the existence of traces cannot be assumed. In contrast to the study appearing in [ 16 ] , we do not restrict the flux to be unimodal. We allow for the case where the flux has degeneracies, i.e., the flux may vanish on some interval of state space. Since the flux is allowed to be degenerate, the corresponding singular map may not be invertible, and thus the convergence proof appearing in [ 16 ] does not pertain. We prove that the Godunov approximations nevertheless do converge in the presence of flux degeneracy, using an alternative method of proof. We additionally consider the case where the flux has the form described in [ 21 ] . For this case we prove convergence via yet another method. This method of proof provides a spatial variation bound on the solutions, which is of independent interest. We present numerical examples that illustrate the theory.

When

5

\displaystyle A(x,u)
has infinitely many discontinuities, the existence of traces is not guaranteed. Thus in order to formulate
a definition of solution in this case, an entropy condition that is independent of traces is required. One of the major developments in this direction is the notion of
adapted entropy solutions
which generalizes the classical Kruzhkov theory to a certain class of fluxes having possibly infinite discontinuities. This theory has the advantage that it does not require the existence of
traces at the location of the spatial flux discontinuities. In this direction,
an adapted entropy inequality was proposed in
[
7
]
for monotone and unimodal flux functions by introducing a certain class of steady state solutions in (
1.3

[
21

to other class of functions which are of the form
\displaystyle A(x,u)=g(\beta(x,u)),
where
\displaystyle g\in C(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\beta(x,\cdot)

it requires that
\displaystyle u\mapsto A(x,u)
are of same type for all
\displaystyle x
in some sense. For the fluxes which are unimodal away from the degeneracies, this notion of adapted entropy was extended to obtain the uniqueness of solutions in
[
17
]

[
22

via wave front tracking, assuming that the fluxes are convex. Numerically, the existence of the solutions with monotone fluxes, without the assumption of convexity of flux
\displaystyle A(x,u)
was established in
[

[

\displaystyle A(x,\cdot)
admits degeneracies, well-posedness was settled in
[
17
]

This article focuses on the existence of the adapted entropy solution in the case of degenerate fluxes, via a convergent Godunov type numerical scheme. Compactness has always been an issue when it comes to convergence of numerical schemes for conservation laws with discontinuous flux, due to the blow up of total variation in finite time. A well known method to tackle this issue is the use of singular maps. However, the classical technique of singular maps cannot be applied in this context as singular map are not invertible when the fluxes are degenerate. In
[
17
]
, this obstacle of non invertibility of the singular maps was overcome by using the augmented mapping
\displaystyle u\mapsto\Psi(x,u)+\pi(x,u)
, which is invertible and existence was shown via wave front tracking algorithms.

Another important question regarding conservation law with discontinuous flux is the spatial total variation of the solutions. Though the solution operator forms an
\displaystyle L^{1}
contractive semigroup,
\displaystyle L^{1}
contractivity does not imply that solutions are TVD unlike in the homogeneous case i.e.
\displaystyle A(x,u)=f(u)
because solutions do not satisfy the translation invariance property when the fluxes heterogeneous. Thus in general BV bounds on the solutions do not exist.
For the case of a single flux discontinuity, existence and non existence of TV bounds are studied in
[
2
,
18
,
19
]
using the explicit Lax-Oleinik formulae for conservation laws with discontinuous flux derived in
[
4
]
. These results are not true in general when the fluxes admit infinitely many spatial discontinuities as pointed out in
[
17
]
. Nevertheless, existence of BV bounds were proved via front tracking in
[
17
]
for certain class of initial data under the assumption that the fluxes are uniformly convex. Since degenerate fluxes are not uniformly convex, the question remains as to whether there exists sufficient conditions which assure the existence of TV bounds for the solutions when initial data is in BV and fluxes are allowed to be degenerate. The BV estimate obtained in this article indeed gives an affirmative answer to this question, for a certain class of degenerate fluxes
\displaystyle A(\cdot,\cdot).

In the current article we establish existence of the solution via convergence of a Godunov type scheme and show that limit of the finite volume approximation satisfies the adapted entropy inequality obtained in
[
17
]
. Convergence is proved for two class of flux functions. For the first class, convergence is established by proving BV bounds away from the points of spatial discontinuity and for the second class by proving BV bounds on
\displaystyle\beta(\cdot,u(\cdot,t))
. In the recent years, there has been a considerable developments in the study of BV regularity of conservation laws with discontinuous flux. However, most of the BV results either assume uniform convexity or monotonicity of the flux function
\displaystyle A(x,\cdot)
. To the best of our knowledge this is the first BV result for conservation laws with discontinuous flux, which requires neither monotonicity nor uniform convexity of
\displaystyle u\mapsto A(x,u)

As in
[
16

1

We simply apply the Godunov interface flux at every grid cell boundary. At cell boundaries where there is no flux discontinuity, the interface flux automatically reverts to the classical Godunov flux, as desired.

3
The case of a Panov-type flux

For the case where
\displaystyle A(x,u)
is unimodal (
\displaystyle u_{M}^{-}(x)=u_{M}^{+}(x)=u_{M}(x)

(3.1)

where
\displaystyle u\mapsto\beta(x,u)
is strictly increasing, and
\displaystyle g
is continuous and unimodal. Based on this observation,
well-posedness (in the sense of Audusse-Perthame entropy solutions) was established in
[
21
]

(for the unimodal case).

In this section, we assume that the flux has the form (
3.1

the possibility that
\displaystyle g
may be degenerate, i.e., not unimodal.
It turns out that due to the special form (
3.1

BV
bound for the Godunov approximations (and thus the limit), whether or not the flux
is degenerate. At
the same time, this approach makes it possible to obtain compactness/existence

(
B-1
)

\displaystyle u_{M}^{\pm}\in\textrm{BV}(\mathbb{R})
(Assumption
(
B-3
)

previous of results of
[
18

19

where it was noted that

is independent of
\displaystyle x

\displaystyle L\leq L_{g}L_{\beta}
, and so (
2.15
) also holds.

Lemmas
2.3
(monotonicity)
and
2.4
(
\displaystyle u_{j}^{n}
bounded) of Section
2

are applicable with the more restrictive CFL condition (
3.21
) in effect.

For the remainder of the convergence analysis we make use of the fact that
the scheme can be written in terms of the
\displaystyle\beta
variable.
It is clear from Assumption
(
C-2
)
that

\displaystyle g
is nonincreasing on
\displaystyle(-\infty,0)
and nondecreasing on
\displaystyle(0,\infty)
. As a consequence
the Godunov flux
\displaystyle\bar{g}

consistent with
\displaystyle g
can be expressed as follows:

\bar{g}(p,q)=\max\{g(\max(p,0)),g(\min(q,0))\}.

(3.22)

Lemma 3.4
.

The following relationship between the Godunov fluxes
\displaystyle\bar{A}
and
\displaystyle\bar{g}
holds:

\bar{A}(u,v,x,y)=\bar{g}(\beta(x,u),\beta(y,v)).

(3.23)

Proof.

We claim that

\beta(x,\max(u,u_{M}(x)))=\max(\beta(x,u),0),\quad\beta(y,\min(v,u_{M}(y)))=\min(\beta(y,v),0).

(3.24)

To verify the first part of the claim, note that since
\displaystyle u\mapsto\beta(x,u)
is nondecreasing,

\begin{split}\beta(x,\max(u,u_{M}(x)))&=\max(\beta(x,u),\beta(x,u_{M}(x)))\\
&=\textrm{$\displaystyle\max(\beta(x,u),\beta(x,\beta^{-1}(x,0))$), using $\displaystyle u_{M}(x)=\beta^{-1}(x,0)$}\\
&=\max(\beta(x,u),0).\end{split}

(3.25)

The second assertion of (
3.24
) is verified in a similar manner.

Next, starting from (
2.6
) and then using
\displaystyle A(x,u)=g(\beta(x,u))
, along with (
3.24
) and
(
3.22
), we find that

\begin{split}\bar{A}(u,v,x,y)&=\max\{A(x,\max(u,u_{M}(x))),A(y,\min(v,u_{M}(y)))\}\\
&=\max\{g(\beta(x,\max(u,u_{M}(x)))),g(\beta(y,\min(v,u_{M}(y))))\}\\
&=\max\{g(\max(\beta(x,u),0)),g(\min(\beta(y,v),0)\}\\
&=\bar{g}(\beta(x,u),\beta(y,v)).\end{split}

(3.26)

∎

Let
\displaystyle\beta_{j}^{n}=\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n})
.
Lemma
3.4
makes it possible to write the marching formula (
2.5
) in the
equivalent form

u_{j}^{n+1}=u_{j}^{n}-\lambda\Delta_{-}\bar{g}(\beta_{j}^{n},\beta_{j+1}^{n}),\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N,

(3.27)

which we abbreviate as

\textrm{$\displaystyle u_{j}^{n+1}=u_{j}^{n}-\lambda\Delta_{-}\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n}$,
where $\displaystyle\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n}=\bar{g}(\beta_{j}^{n},\beta_{j+1}^{n})$}.

(3.28)

Lemma 3.5
.

The Godunov scheme is TVD with respect to
\displaystyle\{\beta_{j}^{n}\}
in the following sense:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n+1}-\beta_{j}^{n+1}\right|\leq\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|,

(3.29)

and for some
\displaystyle\Delta
-independent constant
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{5}>0
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{5}.

(3.30)

Proof.

First, by combining Lemmas
2.4
and
3.3
,
we have

\left|\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{P},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},\quad n\geq 0.

(3.31)

Thus
\displaystyle L_{g}
serves as a Lipschitz constant for
\displaystyle g(\cdot)
, and also
for both arguments of the Godunov numerical flux
\displaystyle\bar{g}(\cdot,\cdot)

in the calculations that follow.
Next, we apply

\displaystyle\beta(x_{j},\cdot)
to both sides of (
3.28
), which
yields

\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n+1})=\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n}-\lambda\Delta_{-}\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n}).

(3.32)

The right side of (
3.32
) can be expressed as

\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n}-\lambda\Delta_{-}\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n})=\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n})-\lambda\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}\Delta_{-}\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n},

(3.33)

where, using
\displaystyle u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}=-\lambda\Delta_{-}\bar{g}_{j+1/2}^{n}
,

\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}=\begin{cases}{\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n+1})-\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n})\over u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}},\quad&u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}\neq 0,\\
0,\quad&u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}=0.\end{cases}

(3.34)

Thus we have

\beta_{j}^{n+1}=\beta_{j}^{n}-\lambda\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}\Delta_{-}\bar{g}^{n}_{j+1/2},

(3.35)

and it is clear that

0\leq\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}\leq L_{\beta}.

(3.36)

Next we write (
3.35
) in incremental form:

\beta_{j}^{n+1}=\beta_{j}^{n}+\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n}\Delta_{+}\beta_{j}^{n}-\mathcal{D}_{j-1/2}^{n}\Delta_{-}\beta_{j}^{n},

(3.37)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n}&=\begin{cases}-\lambda\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}\left({\bar{g}(\beta_{j}^{n},\beta_{j+1}^{n})-\bar{g}(\beta_{j}^{n},\beta_{j}^{n})\over\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}}\right),\quad&\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\neq 0,\\
0,\quad&\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}=0,\end{cases}\\
\mathcal{D}_{j-1/2}^{n}&=\begin{cases}\lambda\theta_{j}^{n+1/2}\left({\bar{g}(\beta_{j}^{n},\beta_{j}^{n})-\bar{g}(\beta_{j-1}^{n},\beta_{j}^{n})\over\beta_{j}^{n}-\beta_{j-1}^{n}}\right),\quad&\beta_{j}^{n}-\beta_{j-1}^{n}\neq 0,\\
0,\quad&\beta_{j}^{n}-\beta_{j-1}^{n}=0.\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.38)

Recalling (
3.36
) and that

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a monotone numerical flux
[
13
,
20
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n},\mathcal{D}_{j-1/2}^{n}\geq 0
.
Moreover, due to the CFL condition (
3.21
),
along with the fact that
\displaystyle L_{g}
serves as a Lipschitz constant for

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
we also have

\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n},\mathcal{D}_{j-1/2}^{n}\leq\lambda L_{g}L_{\beta}\leq 1/2.

(3.39)

Thus
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n}+\mathcal{D}_{j+1/2}^{n}\leq 1
,
and we can apply Harten’s lemma
[
20
, Theorem 6.1]
, which yields

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n+1}-\beta_{j}^{n+1}\right|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|,

(3.40)

and verifies the claim that the scheme is TVD with respect to
\displaystyle\{\beta_{j}^{n}\}
.
It follows from (
3.40
) that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{0}-\beta_{j}^{0}\right|.

(3.41)

We estimate the terms in the sum on the right side of (
3.41
):

\begin{split}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{0}-\beta_{j}^{0}\right|&=\left|\beta(u_{j+1}^{0},x_{j+1})-\beta(u_{j}^{0},x_{j})\right|\\
&\leq\left|\beta(u_{j+1}^{0},x_{j})-\beta(u_{j}^{0},x_{j})\right|+\left|\beta(u_{j+1}^{0},x_{j+1})-\beta(u_{j+1}^{0},x_{j})\right|\\
&\leq\mathcal{K}_{3}(x_{j})\left|u_{j+1}^{0}-u_{j}^{0}\right|+\mathcal{K}_{4}(u_{j+1}^{0})\left|\alpha(x_{j+1})-\alpha(x_{j})\right|.\end{split}

(3.42)

In light of (
3.42
), along with the assumption that
\displaystyle u_{0}
is compactly supported,
\displaystyle u_{0}\in\textrm{BV}(\mathbb{R})
,
and Assumption
(
C-5
)
,
we have
for some
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{5}>0
independent of the mesh size
\displaystyle\Delta
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{0}-\beta_{j}^{0}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{5}.

(3.43)

Finally, in view of (
3.41
) and (
3.43
) we obtain the spatial variation bound (
3.30
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
\displaystyle A(x,u)
is unimodal, then (
C-1
) is satisfied with
\displaystyle g(u)=\left|u\right|
and
\displaystyle\beta(x,u)=\Psi_{A}(x,u).
In
[
16
]
, convergence was established by showing that
\displaystyle\Psi_{A}(\cdot,u^{\Delta x}(\cdot,t))
is TVB. The above lemma is stronger in the sense that, it implies
\displaystyle\Psi_{A}(\cdot,u^{\Delta x}(\cdot,t))
is not only TVB but also in addition TVD.

Lemma 3.6
.

For some
\displaystyle\Delta
-independent constant
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{6}>0
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|u_{j+1}^{n}-u_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{6}.

(3.44)

Proof.

We employ the reverse triangle inequality to obtain

\begin{split}\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|&\geq\left|\beta(x_{j},u_{j+1}^{n})-\beta(x_{j},u_{j}^{n})\right|-\left|\beta(x_{j+1},u_{j+1}^{n})-\beta(x_{j},u_{j+1}^{n})\right|\\
&\geq\mathcal{K}_{2}\left|u_{j+1}^{n}-u_{j}^{n}\right|-\left|\beta(x_{j+1},u_{j+1}^{n})-\beta(x_{j},u_{j+1}^{n})\right|.\end{split}

(3.45)

Here we have used Assumption
(
C-6
)
. From (
3.45
) we have

\left|u_{j+1}^{n}-u_{j}^{n}\right|\leq{1\over\mathcal{K}_{2}}\left(\left|\beta_{j+1}^{n}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|+\left|\beta(x_{j+1},u_{j+1}^{n})-\beta(x_{j},u_{j+1}^{n})\right|\right).

(3.46)

The proof is completed by summing over
\displaystyle j\in\mathbb{Z}
and invoking (
3.30
)
and Assumption
(
C-5
)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

We have the following discrete time continuity estimates:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j}^{n+1}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{5},

(3.47)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{7},

(3.48)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{7}>0
is independent of the mesh size
\displaystyle\Delta
.

Proof.

From (
3.37
) and (
3.39
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\beta_{j}^{n+1}-\beta_{j}^{n}\right|&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{j+1/2}^{n}\left|\Delta_{+}\beta_{j}^{n}\right|+\mathcal{D}_{j-1/2}^{n}\left|\Delta_{-}\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left({1\over 2}\left|\Delta_{+}\beta_{j}^{n}\right|+{1\over 2}\left|\Delta_{-}\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\Delta_{+}\beta_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{5}.\end{split}

(3.49)

Here we have used (
3.30
) to obtain the last inequality above.
Using the first inequality of (
3.47
), along with (
3.6
), we find that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}\right|\leq\mathcal{K}_{5}/\mathcal{K}_{2}=:\mathcal{K}_{7}.

(3.50)

(

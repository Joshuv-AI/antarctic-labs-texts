Single-differential top quark pair production cross sections with running mass schemes at NLO
Sven-Olaf Moch

Abstract
Single-differential cross section predictions for top quark pair production are presented at next-to-leading order, using running top quark mass renormalization schemes. The evolution of the mass of the top quark is performed in the MSR scheme m t MSR ​ ( μ ) m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(\mu) for renormalization scales μ \mu below the MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} top quark mass m ¯ t ​ ( m ¯ t ) \overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}) , and in the MS ¯ \overline{\textrm{MS}} scheme m ¯ t ​ ( μ ) \overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu) for scales above. In particular, the implementation of a mass renormalization scale independent of the strong coupling renormalization scale and factorization scale in quantum chromodynamics allows investigating independent dynamical scale variations. Furthermore, the first theoretically consistent extraction of the top quark MSR mass from experimental data is presented.

The top quark mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}
is a fundamental parameter of the Standard Model and has an important role in many predictions, both directly and via higher-order corrections.
Yet, the formal definition of quark masses makes them renormalization scheme dependent quantities. While the pole mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}
suffers from the renormalon ambiguity, an infrared sensitivity of the order of the scale of quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
[
1
]
, short-distance masses e.g. the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass
\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})
and the MSR mass
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)

[
2
]
do not. However, the dependence on the mass renormalization scales
\mu_{m}
and
R
necessitates proper scale setting for the extraction of theoretically well-defined masses from cross section measurements to avoid the appearance of large logarithms.

The pole and
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
masses are related by

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}=\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})\left(1+\sum_{n=1}d^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}_{n}(\mu_{m})(a_{S}(\mu_{m}))^{n}\right)
,
where
a_{S}\equiv\alpha_{S}/\pi
,
and
d^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}_{n}(\mu_{m})
are perturbative coefficients.
The pole and MSR mass relation reads

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}=m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)+R\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d^{\textrm{MSR}}_{n}(a_{S}(R))^{n}
,
i.e.
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
approaches
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}
in the formal limit
R\rightarrow 0
, and the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass at
R\rightarrow\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})
up to a small matching correction. The latter are obtained by integrating out top quark loop corrections at
R\lesssim\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})

[
3
]
.
The
R
-evolution of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
is linear, contrary to the logarithmic
\mu_{m}
evolution of
\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})
, and designed to capture the correct physical logarithms for observables with
m_{\mathrm{t}}
dependence generated at dynamical scales
R<m_{\mathrm{t}}
(e.g. resonances, thresholds, low-energy endpoints)
[
4
]
.
For dynamical scales of order and larger than
m_{\mathrm{t}}
, the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
mass and evolution are used.
Based on Ref.
[
5
]
, the top quark-antiquark (
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
) production cross section as a function of the
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
system invariant mass
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
at next-to-leading order (NLO) reads

\frac{d\sigma}{dm_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}}=\left(a_{S}\right)^{2}\frac{d\sigma^{(0)}}{dm_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}}\big(m,\mu_{r},\mu_{f}\big)+\left(a_{S}\right)^{3}\frac{d\sigma^{(1)}}{dm_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}}\big(m,\mu_{r},\mu_{f}\big)+\left(a_{S}\right)^{3}\tilde{R}d_{1}\frac{d}{dm_{\mathrm{t}}}\left(\frac{d\sigma^{(0)}(m_{\mathrm{t}},\mu_{r},\mu_{f})}{dm_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}}\right)\bigg|_{m_{\mathrm{t}}=m},

(1)

where
\sigma^{(0)}
is the leading order (LO) and
\sigma^{(1)}
the NLO cross section in the pole mass scheme and the derivative term at NLO implements the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
or MSR top mass schemes, the renormalization (factorization) scale
\mu_{r}
(
\mu_{f}
) is independent of the mass renormalization scales
R
or
\mu_{m}
and
a_{S}=a_{S}(\mu_{r})

(m,d_{1},\tilde{R})=\begin{cases}(m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R),~d^{\textrm{MSR}}_{1},~R),&\textrm{in the MSR regime }(R<\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})),\\
(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m}),~d^{\overline{\textrm{MS}}}_{1}(\mu_{m}),~\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\mu_{m})),&\textrm{in the $\overline{\textrm{MS}}$ regime }(R>\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})).\end{cases}

(2)

\mu_{r}

As illustrated in Fig.

A determination of
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(R)
is performed using the single-differential
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
production cross section measured by the CMS Collaboration in
\mathrm{p}{}\mathrm{p}{}
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{Te\hskip-0.80002ptV}

[
8
]
, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
35.9\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
. The cross section is provided in four bins:

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<420\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\in[420,550]\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}\in[550,810]\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
and

m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}>810\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
.
The top quark MSR mass is extracted by fitting
{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
production cross section predictions, computed with the ABMP16 5 flavor PDF
[
9
]
at NLO, to the experimental data.
For the extraction,
R=80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
is assumed. For comparison with previous studies, the resulting
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
is evolved to the reference scale
R=1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, as well as translated to
\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})
. The fit uncertainty is obtained via the
\Delta\chi^{2}=1
tolerance criterion. The uncertainty in the initial
R
choice is estimated by repeating the fits at
R=60\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
and
100\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
, and taking the difference of the masses evolved to the reference scales to the respective results of the
R=80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
fit. The
\mu_{r}
,
\mu_{f}
uncertainty is obtained by multiplying the scales independently by
2^{\pm 1}
, avoiding cases where one scale is multiplied by 2 and the other by 1/2, and constructing an envelope.

With
\mu_{r}=\mu_{f}=m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
throughout the
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}
distribution, evolving the obtained
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})
to
R=1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
yields

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})=173.2\pm 0.6\,\text{(fit)}^{+0.4}_{-0.6}\,\text{($\mu_{r},\mu_{f}$)}^{+0.4}_{-0.5}\,\text{($R$)}\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,
compatible with the Monte Carlo calibration studies performed in Ref.
[
10
]
where
R=1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
was also adopted as the reference scale.
Furthermore, the result translates into

\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}}(\overline{m}_{\mathrm{t}})=163.3^{+0.8}_{-1.0}\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
,
which is in agreement with Ref.
[
8
]
.
However, it disagrees with Ref.
[
11
]
, where

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{pole}}=170.5\pm 0.8\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}

was obtained, which translates into

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})=170.2\pm 0.8\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}

interpreting the pole mass
[
11
]
as the asymptotic pole mass
[
4
]
.

Setting
\mu_{r}
and
\mu_{f}
to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})/2
for
m_{\mathrm{t}{}\overline{\mathrm{t}}}<420\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
and to
m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(80\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})

m_{\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{MSR}}(1\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV})=174.8\pm 0.5\,\text{(fit)}^{+0.2}_{-0.4}\,\text{($\mu_{r},\mu_{f}$)}^{+0.2}_{-0.3}\,\text{($R$)}\,\text{Ge\hskip-0.80002ptV}
.
As expected from the present investigations, the setting increases robustness against scale variations, resulting in small uncertainties.
Though a full understanding of
m_{\mathrm{t}}
extracted from cross section measurements requires the inclusion of threshold Coulomb effects, the results indicate
that the choice of the top quark mass scheme and the use of dynamical renormalization scales have a considerable impact on the phenomenological analysis and need to be investigated thoroughly.

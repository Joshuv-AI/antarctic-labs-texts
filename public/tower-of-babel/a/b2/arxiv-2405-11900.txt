Global-in-time Well-Posedness of the Compressible Navier-Stokes Equations with Striated Density
Xian Liao, Sagbo Marcel Zodji

Abstract
. We first show local-in-time well-posedness of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations, assuming striated regularity while no other smoothness or smallness conditions on the initial density. With these local-in-time solutions served as blocks, for less regular initial data where the vacuum is permitted, the global-in-time well-posedness follows from the energy estimates and the propagated striated regularity of the density function, if the bulk viscosity coefficient is large enough in the two dimensional case. The global-in-time well-posedness holds also true in the three dimensional case, provided with large bulk viscosity coefficient together with small initial energy. This solves the density-patch problem in the exterior domain for the compressible model with W 2 , p W^{2,p} -Interfaces. Finally, the singular incompressible limit toward the inhomogenous incompressible model when the bulk viscosity coefficient tends to infinity is obtained.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we establish the existence and uniqueness of global-in-time weak solutions of compressible viscous flows, and at the same time we investigate the dynamics of density-interfaces in dimension
d\in\{2,3\}
. More precisely, we consider the following compressible Navier-Stokes equations describing the motion of compressible viscous fluids:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\dvg(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\dvg(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P(\rho)=\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg u.\end{cases}

Here
\mu>0
represents the dynamic viscosity, and
\lambda>0
stands for the kinetic viscosity.
In the present paper,
\mu
is some fixed positive constant while the constant
\lambda
may become very large.
For notational simplicity, we introduce the so-called bulk viscosity coefficient

\nu=2\mu+\lambda,

which tends to infinity when
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
We always assume that our fluids are (strictly) viscous:

\nu\geqslant\underline{\nu}>0,

where
\underline{\nu}
is a fixed positive constant.

In the above,
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
denote the time and space variables respectively.
The notations
\rho=\rho(t,x)\geqslant 0
and
u=u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
represent, respectively, the density and velocity
of the compressible fluid, which serve as the unknowns in the problem. Meanwhile,
P=P(\rho)
is a given smooth (in this paper we assume
P\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
) and increasing function of the density (that is,
P^{\prime}(\rho)>0
). The system (
1.1
) is supplemented with initial data

(1.2)

\displaystyle(\rho,\rho u)_{|t=0}=(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0}),

which satisfy

(1.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\geqslant 0,\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,\infty)),\quad\rho_{0}-\widetilde{\rho}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}),\quad u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\widetilde{\rho}>0
is some given positive equilibrium state of the density.

Striated regularity

We assume further striated regularity with respect to a given nondegenerate family of vector fields for the initial density

Definition 1.1

(Nondegeneracy)
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{m})\subset\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a family of
m
vector fields with
m\geqslant d-1
and
p\in(d,\infty)
. We say that
\mathcal{Y}
is
nondegenerate

if it satisfies the following property

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{Y}):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\Upsilon\in\Upsilon^{m}_{d-1}}\bigg|\bigwedge^{d-1}Y_{\Upsilon}(x)\bigg|^{\tfrac{1}{d-1}}>0.

Above
\Upsilon\in\Upsilon^{m}_{d-1}
means that
\Upsilon=(\upsilon_{1},\upsilon_{2},\cdots,\upsilon_{d-1})
with each
\upsilon_{i}\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
and
\upsilon_{i}<\upsilon_{j}
for
i<j
,

Y_{\Upsilon}:=(Y_{\upsilon_{1}},Y_{\upsilon_{2}},\dots,Y_{\upsilon_{d-1}})
, while the symbol
\displaystyle\bigwedge^{d-1}Y_{\Upsilon}
stands for the unique element of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\bigwedge^{d-1}Y_{\Upsilon}\right)\cdot Z=\det(Y_{\upsilon_{1}},Y_{\upsilon_{2}},\cdots,Y_{\upsilon_{d-1}},Z),\quad\forall\,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Definition 1.2

(Striated regularity with respect to a nondegenerate family of vector fields)
.

Let
Y\in\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\in(d,\infty)
be a (single) vector field, and

(1)

A function

is said to be of class

, if

g\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,|\dvg(gY)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

We define the
derivative of the function
g
along
Y
as follows

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}g:=\dvg(gY)-g\dvg Y,

and hence we can equivalently define

\mathbb{L}^{p}_{Y}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,|\,\partial_{Y}g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

(2)

A function
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be of class
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
along the family
\mathcal{Y}
, if

g\in\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\bigcap_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\mathbb{L}^{p}_{Y_{\upsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and we equip the space

with the following norm

\displaystyle\lVert g\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\dfrac{1}{I(\mathcal{Y})}\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\left[\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert Y_{\upsilon}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\dvg(gY_{\upsilon})\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right],

which is equivalent to the norm with

.

We now continue with the assumption of the initial density
\rho_{0}
given in (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) associated with the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). We assume further that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\mathcal{X}_{0}=(X_{0,1},\dots X_{0,m})\subset\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a given
nondegenerate
family of vector fields for some
m\geqslant d-1
and
p\in(d,\infty)

(1)

We establish the local-in-time well-posedness of the system (

(2)

1.2.1.
Local-in-time well-posedness and continuation criterion

We begin by providing the statement of the local-in-time result, which technically further assumes the strict positivity of the initial density function and the compatibility condition on the initial data.

Theorem 1.4

(Local-in-time well-posedness and continuation criterion)
.

We consider the Cauchy problem of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) supplemented with the initial data (
1.2
) verifying (
1.3

1.4

1.2.2.
Definitions of energy functionals

The global-in-time well-posedness result will follow from the above local-in-time well-posedness, continuation criterion and a series of energy estimates.
We define in this subsection the relevant energy functionals.

Recall the positive density equilibrium
\widetilde{\rho}
in (
1.3
).
We define first

(1.10)

\displaystyle\hbox{The pressure equilibrium }\widetilde{P}:=P(\widetilde{\rho}),

(1.11)

\displaystyle\hbox{The $\rho$-dependent functions }H_{l}(\rho)=\rho\int_{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\rho}s^{-2}\lvert P(s)-\widetilde{P}\rvert^{l-1}(P(s)-\widetilde{P})ds,\quad l\in[1,\infty),

(1.12)

\displaystyle\hbox{The pressure deviation }G(t,x):=\bigl(P(\rho)\bigr)(t,x)-\widetilde{P},

(1.13)

\displaystyle\hbox{The effective flux }F(t,x)=\nu(\dvg u)(t,x)-G(t,x).

We define the associated energy function of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
)

(1.14)

E(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\rho\dfrac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}}{2}+H_{1}(\rho)\right](t,x)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\left[\mu\lVert\nabla u(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)\lVert\dvg u(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]dt^{\prime},

which consists of

•

The kinetic energy
\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}
;

•

The potential energy
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(H_{1}(\rho)\bigr)(t,x)dx
with
H_{1}(\rho)
defined in (
1.11
):
\displaystyle H_{1}(\rho)=\rho\int_{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\rho}\frac{P(s)-\widetilde{P}}{s^{2}}ds
;

•

The energy dissipation
\mu\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)\|\dvg u\|_{L^{2}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}
.

The energy
E(t)
is conserved for regular enough solutions of (
1.1
).

Recall the notations in (
1.7
), and we introduce energy functionals of higher order

(1.15)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)=\frac{\mu}{2}\lVert\nabla u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{\mu+\lambda}{2}\lVert\dvg u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}dt^{\prime},\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)=\sigma(t)\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t^{\prime})\left[\mu\lVert\nabla\dot{u}(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu+\lambda}{\nu^{2}}\lVert\dot{F}(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right]dt^{\prime}.\end{array}

The hierarchy of energy functionals
E(t),\mathcal{A}_{1}(t),\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)
encode
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm,
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm for
u(t)
and (time-weighted)
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm for the material derivative
\dot{u}(t)
, respectively.
Although trivially
|\dvg u|\leqslant d|\nabla u|
, we will make efforts to get the (large) viscosity coefficient
\lambda
before
\dvg u
in the definition of
E,\mathcal{A}_{1}
, such that intuitively
\dvg u\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
if

is bounded uniformly in time.
The review of their history can be found in
section

1.3
below.

Recall the initial data (
1.2
) and we denote
G_{0}(x)=G(0,x)=\bigl(P(\rho_{0})\bigr)(x)-\widetilde{P}
.
For notational simplicity we denote

(1.16)

\displaystyle\hbox{The first initial energy }E_{0}:=E(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\rho_{0}\dfrac{\lvert u_{0}\rvert^{2}}{2}+H_{1}(\rho_{0})\right](x)\,dx,

(1.17)

\displaystyle\hbox{The total initial energy }E_{0}^{\nu}:=E_{0}+\mu\lVert\nabla u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\nu\lVert\dvg u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\lVert G_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(1.18)

\displaystyle\hbox{The upper bound of the initial density }\rho_{0}^{\ast}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x).

We observe that for initial data given in (
1.2
)-(
1.3
),

\displaystyle E_{0}\leq C(\rho_{0}^{\ast})\|(\rho_{0}-\widetilde{\rho},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty,\quad E_{0}^{\nu}\leq C(\mu,\underline{\nu},\rho_{0}^{\ast})(E_{0}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})+\nu\|\dvg u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}<+\infty.

We aim to bound
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t),\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)
globally in time in terms of
E_{0}^{\nu},\rho_{0}^{\ast}
, if
\nu\geq\nu_{0}
is large enough (and if the initial energy is small enough for

).
The following quantity
\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)
captures the striated regularity of the density function along the family of vector fields
\mathcal{X}(t)=(X_{\upsilon}(t))_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}
transported by the flow as in (
1.8
)

(1.19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)=\lVert\mathcal{X}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\lVert(\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}\rho)(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)
grows exponentially in
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}}
.
We aim to show that the striated regularity encoded in
\|\log\mathcal{A}_{3}\|_{L^{1}((0,t))}
together with the energy functionals
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t),\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)
controls
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}}
.
Gronwall’s inequality hence implies the exponential-in-time control of
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}}
.

1.3.
Review of known results

Classical solutions for the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) with regular initial data are known to exist, since the work by Nash
[
42
]
, Itaya
[
33
,
34
]
, Solonnikov
[
47
]
, Tani
[
48
]
, just to cite a few examples. These solutions are defined up to a positive time which depends on the (norms of) initial data. The first result addressing the global-in-time well-posedness of classical solutions is provided by Matsumura and Nishida
[
41
]
for small initial data in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Nowadays, global-in-time classical solutions are known to exist under smallness assumption on the initial data in critical Besov space
[
4
,
7
,
26
]
.

Weak solutions and estimates for
E(t)
and
G
.

Similar to the solutions constructed by Leray
[
35
]
for the
incompressible
Navier-Stokes equations, there are well-established results that investigate the existence of global-in-time
weak
solutions for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
), with finite initial energy. The first result was obtained by P.-L. Lions
[
40
]
, followed by Feireisl, Novotný, Petzeltová
[
23
]
, for pressure laws of the form
P(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma}
,
a>0
, with some limitations on
\gamma
.
These weak solutions verify the following classical energy inequality:

(1.26)

\displaystyle E(t)\leqslant E(0)=E_{0},

where the functional
E
has been given in (
1.15
).

The introduction of the (generalized) specific energy
H_{l}(\rho)
,
l\in[1,\infty)
, in (
1.11
) helps (technically) to estimate the pressure deviation
G
.
As observed in e.g.
[
3
]
, the so-defined
H_{l}(\rho)
is non-negative:
H_{l}(\rho)\geqslant 0
, since the pressure
P(\rho)
is an increasing function of the density.

For the classical case
l=1
,
H_{1}(\rho)
appears in the definition of
E(t)
, which is integrable in space uniformly in time due to (
1.26
):
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(H_{1}(\rho)\bigr)(t,x)\,dx\leqslant E(t)=E_{0}
.
Consequently, under the a priori assumption

\rho(t,x)\leqslant\rho^{\ast},

we have the estimates for
G
uniformly in time by the energy
E_{0}
below

(1.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,t]}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\leqslant C^{\ast}\sup_{t^{\prime}\in[0,t]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{1}(\rho(t^{\prime},x))\,dx\leqslant C^{\ast}E_{0},\quad\text{with}\quad q\in[2,\infty),

where the constant
C^{\ast}
depends only on
\rho^{\ast}
and
q
.
In the above, the first inequality follows from the definition of
H_{1}(\rho)
in (
1.11
).

General
H_{l}(\rho)
,
l\geqslant 1
, as a function of
\rho
, satisfies the following ordinary differential equation

\rho H^{\prime}_{l}(\rho)-H_{l}(\rho)=\lvert P(\rho)-\widetilde{P}\rvert^{l-1}(P(\rho)-\widetilde{P}),

and hence, by virtue of the mass equation
\eqref{ep0.1}_{1}
, the function

\bigl(H_{l}(\rho)\bigr)(t,x)
satisfies the following time evolutionary equation

\partial_{t}H_{l}(\rho)+\dvg(H_{l}(\rho)u)+\lvert P(\rho)-\widetilde{P}\rvert^{l-1}(P(\rho)-\widetilde{P})\dvg u=0,

which is in the same spirit of the renormalized continuity equation appearing in e.g.
[
40
]
.
By view of the definitions (
1.12
) and (
1.13
), it is equivalent to

\partial_{t}H_{l}(\rho)+\dvg(H_{l}(\rho)u)+\frac{1}{\nu}|G|^{l+1}=-\frac{1}{\nu}\lvert G\rvert^{l-1}GF.

Consequently, integrating the above in space yields, after Hölder’s inequality, the following

(1.28)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\|H_{l}(\rho)(t,x)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{l+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{l+1}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{\nu}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{l+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{l}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{l+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{l+1},

and hence by Young’s inequality and integration in time,
G
can be controlled by
F
in the following way

(1.29)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\nu}\lVert G\rVert_{L^{l+1}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{l+1}\leqslant 2\lVert H_{l}(\rho_{0})(x)\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{C}{\nu}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{l+1}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{l+1},\quad\forall t\in(0,\infty).

Density patch problem.

In the last three decades, there has been growing interests in exploring the properties of weak solutions to models arising from fluid mechanics that enable tracking down discontinuities of some quantities such as density or vorticity.
We refer to the density patch problem for incompressible models stated in
[
39
]
:

Consider the incompressible model (
1.24
) in two dimension with initial density as the characteristic function of some regular domain
D_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
:
\rho_{0}=\mathbb{1}_{D_{0}}
. The density-patch problem asks whether or not, at positive times, the density is still some characteristic function
\mathbb{1}_{D(t)}
with the domain
D(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
preserving the initial regularity of
D_{0}
.
This problem is almost solved for incompressible models, even for density-dependent viscosity (under some smallness assumption) or higher Sobolev regularity of
D_{0}
, see
[
11
,
12
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
.

However, for a similar problem in the context of compressible fluids, there are not so many results. On one hand, the global classical solutions constructed by Matsumura and Nishida, or in critical Besov space, are too strong in a way that they do not allow for discontinuous solutions. On the other hand, the weak solutions constructed by P.-L. Lions or Feireisl, Novotný, Petzeltová only require that the initial energy is finite, allowing for discontinuous density. However, the velocity is relatively weak, with
\nabla u\in L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and this is insufficient to track down discontinuities in the density.
A natural idea is to construct weak solutions in a class that allows for tracking down the discontinuity of the interface.
The first result addressing this issue is, as far as we know,
[
29
]
by Hoff, where the author considered an initial density with Hölder regularity on both sides of a suitable curve, allowing for jumps across this curve.
The initial curve is transported by the flow of the velocity into a curve that maintains its initial regularity. The density also remains Hölder continuous on both sides of the transported curve, and moreover, its jump through the latter decays exponentially over time. This result pertains only in the case of linear pressure law and
small bulk viscosity.
Recently, these restrictions were removed in
[
49
]
, even in the more
challenging case of density-dependent viscosity.
theorem

1.6
is thus added to this list, in the constant viscosity setting, with domains having Sobolev regularity, and the density can be large in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, unlike the cited results.

1.3.1.
Hoff’s strategy

We review briefly some key concepts in Hoff’s works
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.

Energy functionals.

In
[
27
]
, Hoff introduced the following energy functionals which can be compared with our definitions in (
1.15
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}^{H}(t)=\sup_{[0,t]}\sigma\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t^{\prime})\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}(t^{\prime})\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}dt^{\prime},\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}^{H}(t)=\sup_{[0,t]}\sigma^{d}\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d}(t^{\prime})\lVert\nabla\dot{u}(t^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}dt^{\prime},\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(t)=\sup_{[0,t]}\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},\end{cases}

where the time weight
\sigma
and the material derivative
\dot{u}
are defined as in (
1.7
).
He provides bounds for these functionals by requiring that the initial velocity is small in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
but can be large in
L^{2^{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Additionally, he requires that the initial density is bounded away from zero and bounded from above, along with some technical assumptions.

Effective flux and vorticity.

Hoff’s computations, mainly while propagating the lower and upper bounds of the density, rely strongly on the
effective viscous flux

F
given in (
1.13
):
F=\nu\dvg u-G.

It plays a crucial role by connecting the momentum equations and the mass equation, which was discovered by Hoff and Smoller in
[
31
]
.
It presents its power also in the study of the propagation of oscillations in
[
45
]
, and in the constructions of weak solutions in
[
23
,
28
,
40
]
.

In fact, recall the momentum equations
\eqref{ep0.1}_{2}
, which can be written by virtue of the mass conservation law
\eqref{ep0.1}_{1}
as

\rho\dot{u}-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg u+\nabla\bigl(P(\rho)-\widetilde{P}\bigr)=0.

We apply the divergence operator to it to obtain the Poisson equation for
F
as follows

(1.30)

\displaystyle\Delta F=\dvg(\rho\dot{u}).

Similarly we can apply the curl operator to the momentum equations, to derive the Poisson equation for the vorticity,
\rot u,
as follows

(1.31)

\displaystyle\mu\Delta\rot u=\rot(\rho\dot{u}).

Consequently, the regularity of the material derivative of the velocity
\dot{u}
, as provided by functionals
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{H}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{H}
, allows the effective flux
F
and the vorticity
\rot u
to be regular at positive time, even for rough density. This means that there is some cancellation between the divergence of the velocity and the pressure at positive times. In particular, the fact that
F\in L^{8/3}((1,\infty),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
allows him to propagate the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
estimate for the density.

Thanks to this observation, under smallness condition on the initial data, Hoff proved existence of global weak solutions for the system (
1.1
) with a linear pressure law in a first paper
[
27
]
. He later considered pressure laws of the form
P(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma}
, with
\gamma>1
in a second paper
[
28
]
, in which, again, the effective flux played a crucial role in proving compactness for the density.

Velocity gradient expression involving Riesz operators.

In order to study the dynamics of discontinuous surfaces, Hoff in
[
29
]
used the following decomposition of the velocity gradient:

(1.32)

\displaystyle\mu\nabla u=-(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla(\rho\dot{u})+\dfrac{\mu+\lambda}{\nu}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}F+\dfrac{\mu}{\nu}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}G=:\mu\nabla\widetilde{u}+\mu\nabla u_{G},

which is nothing but a rewriting of the above momentum equations, where
\mathcal{R}_{j}=(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{j})(-\Delta)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
, are the Riesz operators.

By assuming more regularity on the velocity
u_{0}\in H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, he reduces the singularity of time weighs in the definitions of functionals
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{H}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{H}
.
Namely, in dimension two, the time weights
\sigma
and
\sigma^{2}
are replaced, respectively, by
\sigma^{1-\beta}
and
\sigma^{2-\beta}
.
Thus,
\nabla\widetilde{u}
and the effective flux
F
belong to

L^{1}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty),\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
for all
0<\alpha<\beta
.
With the help of the regularity of
F
, Hoff propagated the
piecewise
Hölder regularity of the density, resulting
piecewise
Hölder continuity of
\nabla u_{G}
on both sides of a time-dependent curve. This time-dependent curve is the transport of an initial suitable curve with some geometric assumptions, and only provided with bounded velocity gradient can the structure of the density and of the curve be propagated.

However, since Riesz operators
fail
to be continuous on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, additional regularity must be assumed on the density to obtain
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}G\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In
[
30
]
, Hoff and Santos observed that in the configuration of the previous works (see
[
27
,
28
]
), the rough part of the velocity gradient
\nabla u_{G}
belongs to
L^{\infty}((0,\infty),BMO(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
In this case, the initial interface
\gamma_{0}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is transported to an interface
\gamma_{t}\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha_{t}}
at time
t>0
, with
\alpha_{t}
decaying exponentially to zero in time.

Hence to propagate interface regularity (more than continuity) requires a Lipschitz velocity. For the incompressible model with constant viscosity, this regularity is directly obtained from energy computations and interpolations. In contrast, for the compressible case with discontinuous density, the problem is more delicate, and the issue is to find an appropriate functional space framework such that even-order Riesz operators maps into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1.3.2.
The strategy by use of tangential regularity

Apart from the tools used in

It is referred as tangential/striated regularity space, which goes back to Chemin’s study (see e.g.

To the best of our knowledge, the only one work in the literature is

\displaystyle u=w-\nabla(I_{d}-\Delta)^{-1}G,

solves a parabolic equation with source term in some suitable space

Toward this, we establish local-in-time well-posedness of the system (

This is accomplished through a coupling mechanism that involves the effective flux. By achieving this objective, we propagate the Sobolev regularity of interfaces over time.

2.53

(2.57)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sigma(t)}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant C\int^{\sigma(t)}_{0}\|F\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla F\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{\sigma(t)}\lVert\rho\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\lVert\rho\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\leqslant C^{\ast}\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)^{\tfrac{1}{4}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)^{\tfrac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma(t)}^{t}\lVert F\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{3}\leqslant C\int_{\sigma(t)}^{t}\lVert\rho\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\tfrac{3}{2}}\lVert\rho\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\tfrac{3}{2}}\leqslant C^{\ast}\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)^{\tfrac{1}{4}}\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)^{\tfrac{5}{4}}.

Finally, we get:

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,t]}\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant\lVert\rho_{0}-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\dfrac{C^{\ast}}{\nu^{1/3}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\bigr),

and this ends the proof of
lemma

2.3
.
∎

2.2.5.
Proof of
lemma

2.6

The present section is devoted to the propagation of tangential regularity of the density
together with the derivation of the Lipschitz norm of the velocity, provided with the (time-independent) bounds
C_{0}
for
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t),\mathcal{A}_{2}(t),\rho(t)
.

We recall that the family of vector fields
\mathcal{X}(t)=(X_{\upsilon}(t))_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}
is defined as solution of (
1.8
)

(2.58)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}X_{\upsilon}+u\cdot\nabla X_{\upsilon}=\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}u=(X_{\upsilon}\cdot\nabla)u,\\
{X_{\upsilon}}_{|t=0}=X_{0,\upsilon},\end{cases}

and we can estimate the norms of

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{X}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\|X_{\upsilon}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\bigl(\|X_{\upsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla X_{\upsilon}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\bigr)

by use of the Lipschitz norm of the velocity.
On the other side, these norms will help to bound the Lipschitz norm of the velocity, and in particular the pressure-related part
\nabla u_{G}
in the decomposition (
2.10
) of
\nabla u
:

(2.59)

\displaystyle\nabla u

\displaystyle=\nabla\widetilde{u}+\nabla u_{G}

\displaystyle:=\Bigl(-\dfrac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}(-\Delta)^{-1}\dvg(\rho\dot{u})-\dfrac{1}{\mu}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}(-\Delta)^{-1}\cdot\rot(\rho\dot{u})\Bigr)+\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{R}\mathcal{R}G\Bigr).

Finally we will close the estimates for

\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)=\|\mathcal{X}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\|\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

by Gronwall’s inequality.

Proof.

Step 1. Preliminary estimates for
\|\mathcal{X}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

From (
2.58
) we deduce easily:

(2.60)

\displaystyle\lVert X_{\upsilon}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\lVert{X_{0,\upsilon}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{\upsilon}(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds.

We now take derivatives in (
2.58
) and we obtain:

(2.61)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{k}X_{\upsilon}^{j}+(u\cdot\nabla)\partial_{k}X_{\upsilon}^{j}=\partial_{k}X_{\upsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{j}-\partial_{k}u\cdot\nabla X_{\upsilon}^{j}+\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}\partial_{k}u^{j}.

We take the trace in the above equality and we make use of the expression of the divergence of the velocity

\dvg u=\dfrac{1}{\nu}\left(F+G\right),

in order to obtain the following equation for
\dvg X_{\upsilon}
:

\partial_{t}(\dvg X_{\upsilon})+u\cdot\nabla\dvg X_{\upsilon}=\dfrac{1}{\nu}\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}F.

Hence, it is straightforward to show the following:

(2.62)

\lVert\dvg X_{\upsilon}(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\lVert\dvg X_{0,\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla F\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\dvg u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\dvg X_{\upsilon}(s)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds.

As well, we take the antisymmetric part in (
2.61
) to get the following equation for
\rot X_{\upsilon}
:

\partial_{t}(\partial_{k}X^{j}_{\upsilon}-\partial_{j}X^{k}_{\upsilon})+u\cdot\nabla(\partial_{k}X^{j}_{\upsilon}-\partial_{j}X^{k}_{\upsilon})=\partial_{k}X_{\upsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{j}-\partial_{j}X_{\upsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{k}\\
+\partial_{j}u\cdot\nabla X_{\upsilon}^{k}-\partial_{k}u\cdot\nabla X_{\upsilon}^{j}+\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}\left(\partial_{k}u^{j}-\partial_{j}u^{k}\right),

from which we deduce easily:

(2.63)

\lVert\rot X_{\upsilon}(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\lVert\rot X_{0,\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X_{\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla\rot u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By combining (
2.62
) and (
2.63
), we obtain the following estimate for the vector field gradient:

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla X_{\upsilon}(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\nabla X_{0,\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla X_{\upsilon}(s)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\nabla u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds

(2.64)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert X_{\upsilon}(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigl\|\Bigl(\frac{1}{\nu}\nabla F(s),\nabla\rot u(s)\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds.

Step 2. Estimates for
\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)=\lVert\mathcal{X}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\lVert\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

In order to estimate the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm of
\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})
, we combine the equation on the density
\eqref{ep0.1}_{1}
and the equation on the vector field
X_{\upsilon}
(
2.58
) in order to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon}))+\dvg(u\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon}))=0,

from which we deduce the following estimates:

(2.65)

\displaystyle\lVert\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\lVert\dvg(\rho_{0}X_{0,\upsilon})\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\dvg u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})(s)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}ds.

Consequently we can estimate

\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G=P^{\prime}(\rho)\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})-\rho P^{\prime}(\rho)\dvg X_{\upsilon}

by

\lVert\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C_{0}\left(\lVert\dvg(\rho X_{\upsilon})\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\dvg X_{\upsilon}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

We combine (
2.60

2.64
) and (
2.65
) together with (

) to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{3}(0)+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}_{3}(s)\left[\dfrac{1}{\nu}+\lVert\nabla u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\rho\dot{u}(s)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]ds.

Gronwall’s Lemma yields:

(2.66)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{A}_{3}(0)\exp\Bigl(C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+\lVert\nabla u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\rho\dot{u}(s)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]ds\Bigr).

Step 3. Estimates for
\|\nabla u_{G}\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}

We consider
\nabla u_{G}
first.
We use

2.5
to bound the second part

\lVert\nabla u_{G}(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant\dfrac{C}{\nu}\lVert G(t)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\dfrac{C}{\nu}\lVert G(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left[1+\log\left(e+\dfrac{\lVert G(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}(t)}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\lVert G(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\right)\right].

Now we focus on the estimate for

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{1}{I(\mathcal{X}(t))}\left[\lVert G(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lVert\mathcal{X}(t)\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\lVert\partial_{X_{\upsilon}}G(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]\leqslant\dfrac{C_{0}}{I(\mathcal{X}(t))}\mathcal{A}_{3}(t).

From (

We hence have

In the following we continue with the estimates for
\lVert\rho\dot{u}\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where the first integral of the right hand side above is bounded as

This section is devoted to the final step in the proof of the main result,

We recall that we are considering the Cauchy problem associated with equations (

(

Usually the sequence of initial data

This regularization procedure has the unfortunate effect of destroying the density’s structure. As observed in

We are therefore led to prove the local well-posedness of equations (

) stated in

1.4
in
appendix

B
.

Proof of
theorem

1.6
.

In order to apply the local-in-time well-posedness results in
theorem

1.4
, we consider a sequence of initial densities
(\rho_{0}^{\delta})_{\delta}
verifying: for all

:

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\delta}\geqslant\delta,\quad\rho_{0}^{\delta}-\widetilde{\rho}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

, converging strongly to

in

and satisfying the compatibility condition

[

1.4

.

At this level, we obtain a sequence

Appendix A
Interpolation inequality

Lemma A.1

(Density-weighted interpolation inequality)
.

Let
v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\rho\geqslant 0
such that
\sqrt{\rho}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
1<p<\infty
, with
\widetilde{\rho}>0
. Then
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\widetilde{\rho}
and
p
such that the following estimate holds true:

(A.1)

\displaystyle\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant C\left(\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{p}{2}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right).

Moreover, for all
2<q<\infty
, we have:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{2q}}v\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leqslant C\left(\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{2}{q}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\tfrac{1}{q}}+\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{p}{q}(1-\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{2})}\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1-\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\right).

Proof.

In the first step of the proof, we emulate the approach in
[
44
, Proposition A.6]
by expressing:

(A.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}\lvert v\rvert^{2}=(\widetilde{\rho}-\rho)\lvert v\rvert^{2}+\rho\lvert v\rvert^{2}.

Due to the assumption that
\sqrt{\rho}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we only need to compute the integral of the first term of the right hand above. With the help of interpolation, Hölder and Young inequalities, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\widetilde{\rho}-\rho)\lvert v\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p}\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{2}{p^{\prime}}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\widetilde{\rho}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+C_{p,\widetilde{\rho}}\lVert\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2},

and (
A.1
) just follows. Next, for all
2<q<\infty
, Hölder, Gagliardo Nirenberg inequalities yield:

\displaystyle\lVert\rho^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{2q}}v\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1-\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\tfrac{1}{q}}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{1}{q}-\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lVert\sqrt{\rho}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\tfrac{1}{q}}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\tfrac{2}{q}-\tfrac{q^{\prime}}{q}}.

Hence (
A.2
) holds true while replacing the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
norm of the velocity by (
A.1
).
∎

Appendix B
Local well-posedness

In this section, we prove the local well-posedness result in
theorem

1.4
of the Navier-Stokes equations for a compressible fluid with an initial density having tangential regularity. Our method relies on a change of variables into Lagrangian coordinates, followed by the study of the linearized system and the full nonlinear system, in a similar way as in
[
50
]
.
In particular, we do not require the density to be a small perturbation around a constant state in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

More precisely, we consider the Cauchy problem of the system (
1.1
):

(B.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\dvg(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\dvg(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P(\rho)=\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg u,\end{cases}

equipped with initial data (
1.2
):

(B.2)

\displaystyle\rho_{|t=0}=\rho_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad(\rho u)_{|t=0}=\rho_{0}u_{0},

verifying (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.6
):

(B.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt0<\underline{\rho}\leqslant\rho_{0}(x),\quad\rho_{0}-\widetilde{\rho}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mu\Delta u_{0}+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg u_{0}-\nabla P(\rho_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{cases}

Above
\widetilde{\rho}>0
is a constant and
\mathcal{X}_{0}=(X_{0,\upsilon})_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\subset\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d<p<\infty
, is a non-degenerate family of
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
vectors fields, with
m\geqslant d-1
.

Here, we present the main lines of the proof of
theorem

1.4
; detailed computations can be found in
[
50
]
, in the more involved case of density-dependent viscosity.

Step 1. Lagrangian coordinates.

Let
0<T\leqslant\infty
,
u
be a Lipschitz vector field such that
\nabla u\in L^{1}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and let us consider its flow map
\mathscr{X}
given by

\mathscr{X}_{\overline{u}}(t,y)=y+\int_{0}^{t}u(\tau,\mathscr{X}_{\overline{u}}(\tau,y))d\tau=:y+\int_{0}^{t}\overline{u}(\tau,y)d\tau

where, hereafter, for all
g=g(t,x)
, we define
\overline{g}=g(t,y)
by:

\overline{g}(t,y)=g(t,\mathscr{X}(t,y)).

By performing this change of variables, the equations (
B.1
) reads:

(B.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}(\overline{\rho}J_{\overline{u}})=0,\\
\rho_{0}\partial_{t}\overline{u}=\dvg\left(\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{\overline{u}})\{2\mu\mathbb{D}_{A_{\overline{u}}}\overline{u}+(\lambda\dvg_{A_{\overline{u}}}\overline{u}-P(\overline{\rho})+\widetilde{P})\}\right),\end{cases}

where

J_{\overline{u}}=\det(D\mathscr{X}_{\overline{u}}),\quad A_{\overline{u}}=(D\mathscr{X}_{\overline{u}})^{-1},\quad\dvg_{A_{\overline{u}}}w=A^{T}_{\overline{u}}\colon\nabla w=Dw\colon A_{{}_{\overline{u}}},\quad 2\mathbb{D}_{A_{\overline{u}}}w=Dw\cdot A_{\overline{u}}+A^{T}_{\overline{u}}\cdot\nabla w.

Step 2. Well-posedness of the linearized system.

Motivated by (
B.4
), we are led to consider the linear system

(B.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v-\mu\Delta v-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg v=\dvg f,\\
v_{|t=0}=v_{0},\end{cases}

where the source term
f
and the initial datum
v_{0}
belong to the following space:

\displaystyle Y_{T}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(f,v_{0})\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon f,\partial_{t}f,\sigma\partial_{tt}f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d});\right.

\displaystyle\left.\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}f\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\;\mu\Delta v_{0}+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\dvg v_{0}+\dvg f_{|t=0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.

The solution of the linearized system (
B.5
) is constructed in the following space:

\displaystyle Z_{T}:=\left\{v\in L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\colon\right.

\displaystyle\left.\nabla v,\;\partial_{t}v,\;\nabla\partial_{t}v,\;\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{tt}v,\;\sigma\nabla\partial_{tt}v\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d});\;\;\right.

\displaystyle\left.\partial_{t}v,\;\sqrt{\sigma}\nabla\partial_{t}v,\;\sigma\partial_{tt}v\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\right\}.

It is straightforward to check that for
T<\infty
every
v\in Z_{T}
satisfies:

v\in\mathscr{C}([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad\partial_{t}v\in\mathscr{C}((0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

The well-posedness result for the linearized system (
B.5
) reads as follows.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
0<T\leqslant\infty
. For all
(f,v_{0})\in Y_{T}
, there exists a unique solution
v\in Z_{T}
of the Cauchy problem (
B.5
). Moreover, the following estimates holds true for
v
.

(1)

Basic energy estimates:

(B.6)

\sup_{[0,T]}\lVert\bigl(\sqrt{\rho_{0}}v,\;\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}v,\;\nabla v\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\bigl(\nabla v,\;\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}v,\;\nabla\partial_{t}v\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
\lesssim\lVert\bigl(\sqrt{\rho_{0}}v_{0},\;\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\partial_{t}v_{|t=0},\;\nabla v_{0}\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\sup_{[0,T]}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\bigl(f,\,\partial_{t}f\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(2)

Higher energy estimates:

(B.7)

\sup_{[0,T]}\lVert\bigl(\sqrt{\sigma}\nabla\partial_{t}v,\;\sigma\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\partial_{tt}v\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\bigl(\sqrt{\sigma\rho_{0}}\partial_{tt}v,\;\sigma\nabla(\partial_{tt}v)\bigr)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\\
\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\partial_{t}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\sup_{[0,T]}\sigma\lVert\partial_{t}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}\lVert\partial_{tt}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

The constant appearing in the above estimates does not depend on the upper or lower bound of the density
\rho_{0}
.

The proof of
Proposition

B.1
is not part of the classical theory of parabolic systems due to the roughness of the density. However, it can be achieved by a regularization process followed by a compactness argument. We refer, for example, to
[
50
, Theorem 3.1]
for the derivation of estimates (
B.6
) and (
B.7
).

Step 3. Further estimates of the linearized system.

By interpolating the estimates (
B.6
) and (
B.7
), we observe that the following estimates hold true for the velocity gradient and its time derivative.

Corollary B.2
.

The following estimates hold true.

(1)

Assuming that
f\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for
2\leqslant r\leqslant\infty
and
2<p<\infty
if
d=2
or
2<p\leqslant 6
if
d=3
,
we have:

(B.8)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}\lesssim\lVert(f,v_{0})\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}.

The same estimate holds also true if
d=3
, for
6<p<\infty
, and
2\leqslant r\leqslant 4p/(p-6)
.

(2)

For all
2\leqslant r\leqslant\infty
and
2<p<\infty
if
d=2
and
2<p\leqslant 6
if
d=3
we have:

(B.9)

\displaystyle\lVert\sigma^{s}\nabla\partial_{t}v\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}\lesssim\lVert(f,v_{0})\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma^{s}\partial_{t}f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}

where

\displaystyle s=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt1-\dfrac{1}{p}-\dfrac{1}{r}\quad\text{ if }\quad d=2,\\
\dfrac{5}{4}-\dfrac{3}{2p}-\dfrac{1}{r}\quad\text{ if }\quad d=3.\end{cases}

For
d=3
, the same estimate also holds true for all
6<p<\infty
and
2\leqslant r\leqslant 4p/(p-6)
.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{X}_{0}=(X_{0,\upsilon})_{1\leqslant\upsilon\leqslant m}\subset\mathbb{L}^{\infty,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d<p<\infty
, be a non-degenerate family of
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
vectors fields, with
m\geqslant d-1
.

(a)

Assuming that
f\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, with
2\leqslant r\leqslant 8
if
d=2
and
2\leqslant r\leqslant 32/9
if
d=3
, then
\nabla v\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and the following estimate holds true.

(B.10)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}\lesssim\lVert(f,v_{0})\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}.

(b)

Assuming that
f\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, with
2<p<\infty
,
2\leqslant r\leqslant 2p/(p-2)
if
d=2
and
3<p\leqslant 6
,
2\leqslant r\leqslant 4p/(3p-6)
if
d=3
.
Then we have:

(B.11)

\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\nabla v\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\mathcal{X}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lVert(f,v_{0})\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}\\
+\lVert\nabla\mathcal{X}_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{2}.

Let
d=3
and
6<p<\infty
,
2\leqslant r\leqslant\infty
. If
\sigma^{s}f\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
\sigma^{\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{1}{r}}\partial_{t}f\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with

s=\dfrac{3}{4}-\dfrac{1}{r}-\dfrac{3}{2p}

then, from (
2

\sigma^{\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{1}{r}}\nabla\partial_{t}v\in L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

(B.12)

\lVert\sigma^{s}\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\nabla v\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\mathcal{X}_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\left(\lVert(f,v_{0})\rVert_{Y_{T}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma^{\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{1}{r}}\partial_{t}f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}\right)\\
+\lVert\sigma^{s}\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\mathcal{X}_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lVert\sigma^{s}f\rVert_{L^{r}((0,T),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}.

The constant appearing in the above estimates depends on both the lower and upper bounds of the density.

Indeed, all the estimates in
Corollary

B.2
are based in the following expression of the velocity gradient:

\displaystyle\nabla v

\displaystyle=\nabla\mathcal{P}v+\nabla\mathcal{Q}v

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{\mu}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v)-\dfrac{1}{\nu}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{Q}(\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v)

(B.13)

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{1}{\mu}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{P}\dvg f+\dfrac{1}{\nu}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{Q}\dvg f.

The first two terms associated with

L^{r}((0,T),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

on

for all

. These computations lead to (
B.8

The estimate
(
B.10

norm of the terms associated with
\partial_{t}v
can be estimated
by interpolating the estimate (

Proposition

since Riesz operators fail to be continuous on

.

To derive the estimate (
B.9

,

,
to obtain norms for the terms associated with

. The norm of the first two terms, associated with

B.7
).

For the last estimates (
B.11
)
and (

\mathcal{X}_{0}
in (

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\nabla v

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{\mu}\mathcal{X}_{0}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{P}(\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v)-\dfrac{1}{\nu}\mathcal{X}_{0}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{Q}(\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v)

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{1}{\mu}\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{P}\dvg f+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\mathcal{Q}\dvg f.

Once again, the norms of the first two terms are obtained using Hölder’s inequality and by interpolating estimates (
B.6
) and (
B.7
). For the remaining terms, we use Lemma A.1 of
[
10
]
. This completes this step of the study of the
linear system (
B.5
).

Step 4. Final conclusion.

Once we conclude the study of the linear system associated with (
B.4
), the next step is to define a map that is contracting for some small time
T>0
, such that it admits a unique fixed point, which serves as a solution to (
B.1
) after reverting to Eulerian coordinates. With
Proposition

B.1
and
Corollary

B.2
in mind, we can verify that the unique solution of the full nonlinear system can be constructed in

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{T}:=\left\{v\in Z_{T}\colon\nabla v\in L^{2}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\right\}

for
2<p<\infty
if
d=2
and
3<p\leqslant 6
if
d=3
by following the steps outlined in
[
50
, Section 4]
. The
only argument we need to specify is the following lemmas.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
v
be a vector field verifying
\nabla v\in L^{1}((0,t),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\nabla v\in L^{1}((0,t),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
t>0
. Assuming that

(B.14)

\displaystyle V:=\int_{0}^{t}\left[\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\nabla v\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right]<1,

then, there exists a constant
K=K(V)
such that the following estimate holds true:

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{v}(t)),\;\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}A_{v}(t),\;\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}J^{\pm 1}_{v}(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant K\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}Dv\rVert_{L^{1}((0,t),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Moreover, we have for all
Dw\in\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{v}(t))D_{A_{v}(t)}w)-\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}Dw\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{v}(t))\dvg_{A_{v}(t)}w)-\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\dvg w\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leqslant K\left(\lVert Dw\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}Dw\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\lVert Dv\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}Dv\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

Lemma B.4
.

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
two vector fields verifying (
B.14
):
V_{1},V_{2}<1
, and let
\delta v:=v_{2}-v_{1}
. Then, there exists a constant
K=K(V_{1},V_{2})
such that the following estimate holds true:

\lVert\bigl(\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}A_{v_{2}}(t)-\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}A_{v_{1}}(t),\;\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{v_{2}}(t))-\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\adj(D\mathscr{X}_{v_{1}}(t)),\;\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}J_{v_{2}}^{\pm 1}(t)-\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}J_{v_{1}}^{\pm 1}(t)\bigr)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leqslant K\int_{0}^{t}\left(\lVert D\delta v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}D\delta v\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

The particular case of
3<p\leqslant 6
for
d=3
is sufficient for constructing blocks for the global solution of
theorem

1.6
. For
6<p<\infty
in three dimension, the fixed point theorem may be applied in a closed subset of the following space:

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{T}:=\left\{v\in Z_{T}\colon\sigma^{\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{1}{r}}\nabla\partial_{t}v\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d}));\;\sigma^{\tfrac{3}{4}-\tfrac{1}{r}-\tfrac{3}{2p}}\nabla v\in L^{r}((0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\right\}.

This ends the sketchy proof of
theorem

1.4
.

Sharp Weak Bounds for p p -adic Hardy operators on p p -adic Linear Spaces
Amjad Hussain 1,∗, Naqash Sarfraz 1, Ferit Gürbüz 2

Abstract
. In this article, we establish sharp weak endpoint estimates for p p -adic fractional Hardy operator. In addition, sharp weak bounds for p p -adic Hardy operator on p p -adic central Morrey space are also obtained.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number, if a non-zero rational number
x
can be written in the form

x=\frac{s}{t}p^{k},

where the integer
k=k(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}
are not multiples of
p,
then the function:

|\cdot|_{p}:\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

defined as:

|0|_{p}=0,\quad|x|_{p}=p^{-k},

satisfies all the axioms of a field norm with an additional property that:

|x+y|_{p}\leq\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\},

(1.1)

and is commonly known as
p
-adic norm.

Likewise, the completion of field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to ultrametric
p
-adic norm
|\cdot|_{p}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and is known as the field of
p
-adic numbers.
In expanded form, a
p
-adic number
x\neq 0
can be written as (see
[
24
]
):

x=p^{k}(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}p+\alpha_{1}p^{2}+...)

(1.2)

where
\alpha_{i},k\in\mathbb{Z},\alpha_{0}\neq 0,\alpha_{i}\in\{0,1,2,...,p-1\},i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot.
The series (
1.2
) is convergent with respect to
p
-adic norm, since
|p^{k}\alpha_{i}p^{i}|_{p}\leq p^{-k-i}.

The higher dimensional space
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
represents the vector space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which consists of all points
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}),
where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},i=1,2,...,n.
The
p
-adic norm on the very space
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
is:

|\mathbf{x}|_{p}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{p}.

In non-Archimedean geometry, the ball and and its boundary are defined, respectively, as:

B_{k}(\mathbf{a})=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}|_{p}\leq p^{k}\},\ S_{k}(\mathbf{a})=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}|_{p}=p^{k}\}.

We denote
B_{k}(\mathbf{0})=B_{k}
and
\ S_{k}(\mathbf{0})=S_{k}
for convenience. Additionally, for every
\mathbf{a}_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},\ \mathbf{a}_{0}+B_{k}=B_{k}(\mathbf{a}_{0})
and
\mathbf{a}_{0}+S_{k}=S_{k}(\mathbf{a}_{0})
.

The local compactness and commutativity of the group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
under addition implies the existence of Haar measure
d\mathbf{x}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
, such that

\int_{B_{0}}d\mathbf{x}=|B_{0}|=1,

where the notation
|B|
refers to the Haar measure of a measurable subset
B
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.
Also, it is not difficult to show that
|B_{k}(\mathbf{a})|=p^{nk}
,
|S_{k}(\mathbf{a})|=p^{nk}(1-p^{-n}),
for any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}.

Let
w(\mathbf{x})
be a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
and and
w(E)
the weighted measure of measurable subset
E\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n},
that is
w(E)=\int_{E}w(x)dx
respectively. The space of all complex-valued functions
f
with norm conditions:

\|f\|_{L^{q}(w;\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}w(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty,

is denoted by
L^{q}(w,\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}),(0<q<\infty),
and is known as weighted Lebesgue space. Note that
L^{q}(1,\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=L^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}).

We define the weighted
p
-adic weak Lebesgue space
L^{q,\infty}(w;\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
as the set of all measurable functions
f
satisfying:

\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(w,\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda w\bigg(\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|f(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty.

When
w=1,
we get the weak Lebesgue space
L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
defined in
[
26
]
. Next, we give the definition of strong and weak
p
-adic central Morrey spaces, respectively.

Definition 1.1
.

[
28
]
Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
-1/q\leq\lambda<0.
A function
f\in L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is said to belong to central Morrey space if:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{{1+\lambda q}}}\int_{B_{\gamma}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{q}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty.\end{aligned}

When
\lambda=-1/q,
then
\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=L^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}).
It is not hard to see that
\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is reduced to {0} whenever
\lambda<-1/q.

Definition 1.2
.

[
29
]
Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
-1/q\leq\lambda<0.
The
p
-adic weak central Morrey space
W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=\{f:\|f\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}<\infty\},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}}|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}\|f\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})},\end{aligned}

and
\|f\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}
is the local
p
-adic
L^{q}
-norm of
f(x)
restricted to the ball
B_{\gamma}
, that is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=\sup_{\lambda>0}|\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\gamma}:|f(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}|^{1/q}.\end{aligned}

It is clear that if
\lambda=-1/q,
then
W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})=L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}).
Also,
\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})\subseteq W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
for
1\leq q<\infty
and
-1/q<\lambda<0.

The study of
p
-adic analysis is considered important in modern ages, because,
p
-adic analysis is a natural base for development of various models of ultrametric diffusion energy landscape
[
3
]
. It also attracts great deal of interest towards quantum mechanics
[
24
]
, theoretical biology
[
10
]
, string theory
[
25
]
, quantum gravity
[
1
,
6
]
, spin glass theory
[
2
,
22
]
. In
[
3
]
, it was shown that the
p
-adic analysis can be efficiently applied both to relaxation in complex speed systems and processes combined with the relaxation of a complex environment. Besides,
p
-adic analysis has an imperative role in pseudo-differential equations and harmonic analysis, see for example
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
19
]
.

For a non-negative integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{+},
the one dimensional Hardy operator is defined as:

hf(x)=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}f(y)dy,\quad x>0,

(1.3)

Hardy, in
[
17
]
, established the following integral inequality for the Hardy operator:

\|hf\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\leq\frac{q}{q-1}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+})},\quad 1<q<\infty.

(1.4)

Later, Faris in
[
11
]
gave the following extension of one dimensional Hardy operator:

\mathcal{H}f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|^{n}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|\leq|\mathbf{x}|}f(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}.

(1.5)

In
[
7
]
, the authors computed the norm of Hardy operator on the Lebesgue space, whereas Fu et al., in
[
12
]
, obtained the boundedness of
\mathcal{H}
on power weighted Lebesgue space
L^{q}(|\mathbf{x}|^{\alpha})
for
1<q<\infty.
For more details about the boundedness of Hardy operator we included some references
[
5
,
18
,
23
,
33
]
.

On the other hand, the fractional Hardy operator is obtained by merely interchanging
|\cdot|^{n}
with
|\cdot|^{n+\alpha}
(
0\leq\alpha<n
) in (
1.5
). The weak and strong type optimal bounds for the fractional Hardy and adjoint Hardy operator has also spotlighted many researchers in the past, see for example
[
12
,
14
,
15
,
31
,
32
]
.

For
f\in L_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})
and
0\leq\alpha<n,
Wu, in (
[
27
]
), defined the
p
-adic fractional Hardy operator as:

H^{p}_{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}f(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},\qquad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

If
\alpha=0,
the fractional
p
-adic Hardy operator is reduced to
p
-adic Hardy operators, see
[
13
]
. Some other papers showing the boundedness of
p
-adic Hardy type operator include
[
16
,
20
,
21
,
28
]
.

In this article, we give the sharp weak endpoint estimates for
p
-adic fractional Hardy operator on
p
-adic Lebesgue space. Moreover, Sharp weak bounds for
p
-adic Hardy operator on
p
-adic central morrey space are also obtained.

2.
Sharp weak endpoint estimates for
p
-adic fractional Hardy Operator

Our main result for this section read as:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and
n+\gamma>0.
If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}),
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|H^{p}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha),\infty}(|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma};\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq&C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})},\end{aligned}

where the constant

C=\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}

is optimal.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |H^{p}_{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})|=&\bigg|\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}f(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}\bigg|\\
=&\bigg|\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}f(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}\bigg|\\
\leq&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{-(n-\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

(2.1)

Let
C_{1}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})},
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|H^{p}_{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}\subset\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(C_{1}/\lambda)^{1/(n-\alpha)}\}.\end{aligned}

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|H^{p}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha),\infty}(|x|_{p}^{\gamma};\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\\
\leq&\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|H^{p}_{\alpha}f(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
\leq&\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{\big\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<\big(C_{1}/\lambda\big)^{1/(n-\alpha)}\big\}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\int_{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<\big(C_{1}/\lambda\big)^{1/(n-\alpha)}}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\log_{p}\big(C_{1}/\lambda\big)^{1/(n-\alpha)}}\int_{S_{j}}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&(1-p^{-n})^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\log_{p}\big(C_{1}/\lambda\big)^{1/(n-\alpha)}}p^{j(n+\gamma)}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\bigg(\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda}\bigg)\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\beta})}.\end{aligned}

(2.2)

To show that the constant

\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)},

appeared in (
2.2
) is optimal, we employ the idea of use of
power function given in
[
30
]
, hence, we let

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\chi_{\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1\}}(\mathbf{x}),\end{aligned}

then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=1.\end{aligned}

Also,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\alpha}^{p}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=&\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}f_{0}(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}\chi_{\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq 1\}}(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n-\alpha}}\begin{cases}\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}d\mathbf{y},&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1;\\
\int_{|\mathbf{y}|_{p}\leq 1}d\mathbf{y},&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}>1.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

Since
|B_{\log_{p}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}}|_{H}=|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n}|B_{0}|_{H},
therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H^{p}_{\alpha}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\alpha},&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1;\\
|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\alpha-n},&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}>1.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

Now,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|H^{p}_{\alpha}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}=&\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\alpha}>\lambda\}\cup\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}>1:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\alpha-n}>\lambda\}.\end{aligned}

Since
0<\alpha<n,
therefore, when
\lambda\geq 1,
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|H^{p}_{\alpha}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}=\emptyset,\end{aligned}

and when
0<\lambda<1,
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:|H^{p}_{\alpha}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})|>\lambda\}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:(\lambda)^{1/n}<|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(1/\lambda)^{1/n-\alpha}\}.\end{aligned}

Ultimately we are down to:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\alpha}f_{0}\|_{L^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha))),\infty}(|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma};\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\\
=&\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\lambda\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}}\chi_{\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}:(\lambda)^{1/\alpha}<|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(1/\lambda)^{1/(n-\alpha)}\}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\lambda\bigg(\int_{(\lambda)^{1/\alpha}<|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(1/\lambda)^{1/(n-\alpha)}}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&(1-p^{-n})^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\lambda\bigg(\sum_{j=\log_{p}\lambda^{1/\alpha}+1}^{\log_{p}\lambda^{1/(\alpha-n)}}p^{j(n+\gamma)}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&(1-p^{-n})^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\lambda\bigg(\frac{p^{(\log_{p}\lambda^{1/\alpha}+1)(n+\gamma)}-p^{(\log_{p}\lambda^{1/(\alpha-n)}+1)(n+\gamma)}}{1-p^{(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&(1-p^{-n})^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\lambda\bigg(\frac{{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/\alpha}}-{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/(\alpha-n)}}}{p^{-(n+\gamma)}-1}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&(1-p^{-n})^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\bigg(\frac{1-{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/\alpha}}{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha)}}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\bigg(1-{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/\alpha}}{\lambda^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{(n-\alpha)/(n+\gamma)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

(2.3)

We thus conclude from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|{H}^{p}_{\alpha}\|_{{L^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})\rightarrow L^{(n+\gamma)/(n-\alpha),\infty}(|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{\gamma};\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}}=\bigg(\frac{1-p^{-n}}{1-p^{-(n+\gamma)}}\bigg)^{1/q}.\end{aligned}

∎

3.
Sharp bound for
p
-adic Hardy operator

The present section investigates the boundedness of
p
-adic Hardy operator on
p
-adic weak central Morrey spaces. It is shown the constant obtained in this case is also optimal.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty,

-1/q\leq\lambda<0
and if
f\in\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}),
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})},\end{aligned}

and the constant
1
is optimal.

Proof.

Applying Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |H^{p}f(\mathbf{x})|\leq&\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n}}\bigg(\int_{B(0,|\mathbf{x}|_{p})}|f(\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbf{y}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\int_{B(0,|\mathbf{x}|_{p})}d\mathbf{y}\bigg)^{1/q^{\prime}}\\
=&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n\lambda}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

Let
C_{2}=\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.
Since
\lambda<0,
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq&\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{t>0}t|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}\big|\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\gamma}:C_{2}|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{n\lambda}>t\}\big|^{1/q}\\
=&\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{t>0}t|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}\big|\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}\}\big|^{1/q}.\end{aligned}

If
\gamma\leq\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda},
then for
\lambda<0,
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{t>0}\sup_{\gamma\leq\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}}t|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}\big|\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}\}\big|^{1/q}\\
&\leq\sup_{t>0}\sup_{\gamma\leq\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}}tp^{-\gamma n\lambda}\\
&=C_{2}\\
&=\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

If
\gamma>\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda},
then for
\lambda+1/q>0,
we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{t>0}\sup_{\gamma>\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}}t|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}|\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}\}|^{1/q}\\
&\leq\sup_{t>0}\sup_{\gamma>\log_{p}(t/C_{2})^{1/n\lambda}}tp^{-\gamma n(\lambda+1/q)}(t/C_{2})^{1/q\lambda}\\
&=C_{2}\\
&=\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

(3.1)

Conversely, to prove that the constant
1
is optimal, we let

f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\chi_{\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1\}}(\mathbf{x}),

then,

\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{{1+\lambda q}}}\int_{B_{\gamma}}\chi_{\{|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1\}}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{1/q}.

If
\gamma<0,
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\gamma<0\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{{1+\lambda q}}}\int_{B_{\gamma}}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{1/q}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\gamma<0\end{subarray}}p^{-n\gamma\lambda}=1,\end{aligned}

since
\lambda<0.

If
\gamma\geq 0,
then using the condition that
\lambda+1/q>0,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\gamma\geq 0\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{{1+\lambda q}}}\int_{B_{0}}d\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{1/q}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\gamma\geq 0\end{subarray}}p^{-n\gamma(\lambda+1/q)}=1.\end{aligned}

Therefore,

\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=1.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H^{p}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}1,&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}\leq 1;\\
|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{-n},&|\mathbf{x}|_{p}>1,\end{cases}\end{aligned}

which implies that
|H^{p}f_{0}(\mathbf{x})|\leq 1.

Next, in order to construct weak central Morrey norm we divide our analysis into following two cases:

Case 1. When
\gamma\leq 0,
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf_{0}\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t|\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\gamma}:1>t\}|^{1/q}=p^{n\gamma/q},\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf_{0}\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=\sup_{\gamma<0}|B_{\gamma}|_{H}^{-\lambda-1/q}\|f\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=\sup_{\gamma<0}p^{-n\gamma\lambda}=1=\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

Case 2. When
\gamma>0,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf_{0}\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=&\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t|\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{0}}:1>t\}\cup\{1\leq|\mathbf{x}|_{p}<p^{\gamma}:|\mathbf{x}|_{p}^{-n}>t\}|^{1/q}.\end{aligned}

For further analysis, this case is further divided into the following subcases:

Case 2(a). If
1<\gamma<\log_{p}t^{-1/n},
then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf_{0}\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t\{1+p^{n\gamma}-1\}^{1/q}=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}tp^{n\gamma/q}.\end{aligned}

Case 2(b). If
1<\log_{p}t^{-1/n}<\gamma,
then:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|Hf_{0}\|_{WL^{q}(B_{\gamma})}=&\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t(1+t^{-1}-1)^{1/q}=&\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{1-1/q}.\end{aligned}

Now, for
1\leq q<\infty
and
-1/q\leq\lambda<0,
from case 2(a) and 2(b), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|Hf_{0}\|_{W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}\\
&=\max\bigg\{\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\sup_{1<\gamma\leq\log_{p}(1/t)^{1/n}}tp^{-n\gamma\lambda},\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\sup_{1<\log_{p}(1/t)^{1/n}<\gamma}t^{1-1/q}p^{-n\gamma(\lambda+1/q)}\bigg\}\\
&=\max\bigg\{\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{1+\lambda},\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{1+\lambda}\bigg\}\\
&=1=\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}.\end{aligned}

(3.2)

Finally, using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we arrive at:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|H\|_{\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})\rightarrow W\dot{B}^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n})}=1.\end{aligned}

∎

Global existence and finite time blowup for a mixed pseudo-parabolic p p -Laplacian type equation Journal: Journal
Jiazhuo Cheng, Qiru Wang

Abstract
This paper concerns the initial-boundary value problem for a mixed pseudo-parabolic p p -Laplacian type equation. By constructing a family of potential wells, we first present the explicit expression for the depth of potential well, and then prove the existence, uniqueness and decay estimate of global solutions and the blowup phenomena of solutions with subcritical initial energy. Next, we extend parallelly these results to the critical initial energy. Lastly, the existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior of global solutions with supercritical initial energy are proved by further analyzing the properties of ω \omega -limits of solutions.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following semilinear pseudo-parabolic equation with initial-boundary conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{split}&u_{t}-\mathrm{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)-\triangle u_{t}=\left|u\right|^{q-1}u-{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q-1}u\operatorname{d}x,~~&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\times\left(0,T\right),\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\left(x,t\right)=0,&\mathrm{on}\;\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right),\\
&u\left(x,0\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right)\not\equiv 0,~~{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}u_{0}\operatorname{d}x=0,&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,\end{split}\right.

(1.1)

where, (1) the first equation of (
1.1
) is a mixed pseudo-parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equation,
p>1,\;\max\left\{p-1,1\right\}<q<\frac{np}{n-p}-1
,
T\in(0,\infty]
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\left(n\geq 1\right)
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary,
{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}u\operatorname{d}x=\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{d}x
and
{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q-1}u\operatorname{d}x
is to conserve the spatial integral of
the unknown function over time; (2) the second equation of (
1.1
) is Neumann boundary condition,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}
denotes differentiation with respect to the outward normal
\nu
on
\partial\Omega
; (3) the third equation of (
1.1
) is initial boundary condition.

Problem (
1.1
) has attracted much attentions in recent years in the fields of population dynamics and biological sciences
[
3
,
21
,
23
,
6
]
, where the total mass is often conserved or known. Such equations can provide insight into biological and chemical problems where conservation properties predominate. Since in mathematics we do not require

instead of

in the problem (

[

). In addition, when affected by a variety of factors, such as atoms, ions, etc., the

\mathrm{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
.

The pseudo-parabolic equation

u_{t}-u_{xxt}=F\left(x,t,u_{x},u_{xx}\right)

(1.2)

[

u_{t}-\triangle u-\triangle u_{t}=\left|u\right|^{p-1}u-{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p-1}u\operatorname{d}x,

and proved global existence, uniqueness, asymptotic behavior and blowup of solutions with subcritical and critical initial energy

In 2007, Yin and Jin
[

studied the following equation

. In 2013, Li et al.
[

In order to overcome the difficulties encountered in the prior estimation of solutions using Galerkin method, we use the potential well method proposed by Sattinger
[

[

It is interesting to consider (
1.1

, which can not only help the global existence by decaying energy, but also impede blowup and delay the blowup time. In this paper, it is important to deal with the blowup situation and maintain this balance, but it is not easy. Therefore, we combine the theory of potential wells with the Galerkin method to overcome the existing difficulties, and obtain the global existence, uniqueness, asymptotic behavior and finite time blow up of solutions.

The structure of this paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we give some notations, definitions and lemmas about the basic properties of the related functionals and sets. Also, we present the main results of this paper. In Sections 3 and 4, we are devoted to the subcritical initial energy
J(u_{0})<d
and the critical initial energy
J(u_{0})=d

J\left(u_{0}\right)>d
by analyzing the properties of
\omega
-limits of solutions. The conclusion is made in Section 6.

2
Preliminaries and main results

Throughout the whole paper, let
C
represent generic positive constant, which may change from line to line. Let
L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
be the set of all measurable functions on
\Omega
and satisfying
\int_{\Omega}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\operatorname{d}x<\infty
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, denote by
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{p}
the
L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
norm, i.e.,

{\left\|u\right\|}_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\operatorname{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\;\;\;\mathrm{if}\;1\leq p<\infty;\\
\mathrm{ess}\;\underset{x\in\Omega}{\mathrm{sup}}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mathrm{if}\;p=\infty,\end{array}\right.

where
\forall u\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
. For
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, denote

W^{m,p}\left(\Omega\right)=\left\{u\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)|D^{\alpha}u\in L^{p}\left(\Omega\right),\forall\alpha\in Z_{+}^{n},\left|\alpha\right|\leq m\right\},

and

W_{N}^{{m,p}}\left(\Omega\right)=\left\{u\in W^{m,p}\left(\Omega\right)|{\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|}_{\partial\Omega}=0,\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{d}x=0\right\},

where
D^{\alpha}u
represents the
\alpha
order weak derivative of
u
.

When
p=2
, we can write
H^{k}\left(\Omega\right)=W^{k,2}\left(\Omega\right)\left(k=0,1,\cdots\right)
. Let
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
be the inner product in
L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
and
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{H^{1}}
be the norm of
H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
. That is

\left(u,v\right)=\int_{\Omega}u\left(x\right)v\left(x\right)\operatorname{d}x,\;\;\;\forall u,v\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right),

and

{\left\|u\right\|}_{H^{1}}=\left(\left\|u\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\;\;\;\forall u\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right),

then the norm
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{H^{1}}
is equivalent to the norm
{\left\|\nabla\left(\cdot\right)\right\|}_{2}
.

Next, we define the energy functional
J\left(u\right)
and the Nehari functional
I\left(u\right)
as

\begin{array}[]{l}J\left(u\right)=\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1},\\
I\left(u\right)=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1},\end{array}

(2.1)

and define the Nehari manifold as

\mathcal{N}=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|I\left(u\right)=0,{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0\right\}.

By
J\left(u\right)
and
I\left(u\right)
, we set

\begin{array}[]{l}W=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|J\left(u\right)<d,I\left(u\right)>0\right\}\cup\left\{0\right\},\\
V=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|J\left(u\right)<d,I\left(u\right)<0\right\},\end{array}

where

d=\underset{u\in\mathcal{N}}{\mathrm{inf}}J\left(u\right)

is the depth of the potential well.

For
\delta>0
, we introduce a family of potential wells as follows

\begin{array}[]{l}I_{\delta}\left(u\right)=\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1},\\
{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|I_{\delta}\left(u\right)=0,{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0\right\},\\
W_{\delta}=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|J\left(u\right)<d\left(\delta\right),I_{\delta}\left(u\right)>0\right\}\cup\left\{0\right\},\\
V_{\delta}=\left\{\left.u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right|J\left(u\right)<d\left(\delta\right),I_{\delta}\left(u\right)<0\right\},\\
d\left(\delta\right)=\underset{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}}{\mathrm{inf}}J\left(u\right).\end{array}

(2.2)

In what follows, we give some sets and functionals for weak solutions with high energy levels. Set

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{N}}_{+}=\left\{u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\left|I\left(u\right)>0\right.\right\},\\
J^{\alpha}=\left\{u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\left|J\left(u\right)\leq\alpha\right.\right\},\\
\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{N}\cap J^{\alpha}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{N}\left|J\left(u\right)\leq\alpha\right.\right\},\;\;\;\forall\alpha>d,\\
\lambda_{\alpha}=\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\left|u\in\right.\mathcal{N}^{\alpha}\right\},\;\;\;\forall\alpha>d.\end{array}

Obviously,
\lambda_{\alpha}
is non-increasing with respect to
\alpha
.

Integrating the first equation of (
1.1
) with respect to
x
over
\Omega
and then with respect to
t
from
0
to
t
, we obtain
\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{d}x=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\operatorname{d}x=0
. That is, we have the conservation law for problem (
1.1
). Before stating the main theorems, we first give some definitions and lemmas.

As in
[
9
, Page 8]
, “In general, as we shall see, the conservation law has no classical solutions, but

well-posed if we allow for properly defined

. This is all to say that we may be forced by the structure of the particular equation to abandon the search for smooth, classical solutions. We must instead, while still hoping to achieve the well-posedness conditions (a)-(c), investigate a wider class of candidates for solutions. And in fact, even for those PDE which turn out to be classically solvable, it is often most expedient initially to search for some appropriate kind of weak solution."

Definition 2.1
(Weak solution
[
9
]
)

Function
u\left(x,t\right)
is called a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) on
\Omega\times[0,T)
, if
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
,
u_{t}\in L^{2}\left(0,T;W_{N}^{1,2}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
,
u\left(x,0\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right)\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
and satisfies

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left(u_{\tau},\varphi\right)+\left(\nabla u_{\tau},\nabla\varphi\right)+\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\right)\right)\operatorname{d}\tau\\
\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left|u\right|^{q-1}u-{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q-1}u\operatorname{d}x,\varphi\right)\operatorname{d}\tau,\end{array}

(2.3)

for any
\varphi\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\cap W_{N}^{1,2}\left(\Omega\right)
.

Moreover, the following equality

\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{\tau}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\operatorname{d}\tau+J\left(u\right)=J\left(u_{0}\right)

holds for
t\in[0,T)
.

Definition 2.2
(Maximal existence time)

If
u\left(t\right)
is a weak solution of problem (
1.1
), for maximal existence time
T
of
u\left(t\right)
, we have the following definition.

(i)
For
0\leq t<\infty
, if
u\left(t\right)
exists, then
T=+\infty
;

(ii)
For

, if

, then

Lemma 2.1
(Relations between
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)
and
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}
)

Let
u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
.

(i)
If
0<{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}<r\left(\delta\right)
, then
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)>0
;

(ii)
If
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)<0
, then
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}>r\left(\delta\right)
;

(iii)
If
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)=0
and
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0
, then
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\geq r\left(\delta\right)
,

where
r\left(\delta\right)=\left(\frac{\delta}{C_{\ast}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}
,
C_{\ast}
is the imbedding constant for
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{q+1}\left(\Omega\right)
and satisfies

\frac{1}{C_{\ast}}=\underset{u\in W_{N}^{1,p},u\neq 0}{\inf}\frac{{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}}{{\left\|u\right\|}_{q+1}}.

(2.4)

Proof
:
(i) By
0<{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}<r\left(\delta\right)
, we obtain

\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}\leq C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1}=C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1-p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}<\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p},

then
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)>0
.

(ii) From
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)<0
, we know that
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0
. Then

\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}<\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}\leq C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1}=C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1-p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}

i.e.,
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}>r\left(\delta\right)
.

(iii) If
I_{\delta}\left(u\right)=0
,
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0
, we see that

\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}=\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}\leq C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1}=C_{\ast}^{q+1}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{q+1-p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p},

which implies
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\geq r\left(\delta\right)
.

Lemma 2.2
(Properties of
J(\lambda u)
)

Assume
u\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
and
\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}\neq 0
. Then

(i)

J\left(\lambda u\right)
is increasing on
\lambda\in\left[0,\lambda^{\ast}\right]
and decreasing on
\lambda\in[\lambda^{\ast},\infty)
,
\lambda=\lambda^{\ast}
is the maximum point of
J\left(\lambda u\right)
. Furthermore,
\underset{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{\mathrm{lim}}J\left(\lambda u\right)=0,\;\underset{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}J\left(\lambda u\right)=-\infty
;

(ii)

I\left(\lambda u\right)>0
for
0<\lambda<\lambda^{\ast}
and
I\left(\lambda u\right)<0
for
\lambda^{\ast}<\lambda<\infty
, and

I\left(\lambda^{\ast}u\right)=0
, where

\lambda^{\ast}=\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p+1}}.

Proof
:
From the definition of
J(u)
, we can find that

J\left(\lambda u\right)=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{\lambda^{q+1}}{q+1}\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1},

which gives
\underset{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{\mathrm{lim}}J\left(\lambda u\right)=0,\;\underset{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}J\left(\lambda u\right)=-\infty
. On the other hand, we see that

\frac{dJ\left(\lambda u\right)}{d\lambda}=\lambda^{p-1}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\lambda^{q-p+1}\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}\right).

(2.5)

Considering
{\left.\frac{dJ\left(\lambda u\right)}{d\lambda}\right|}_{\lambda=\lambda^{\ast}}=0
, we have

\lambda^{\ast}=\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p+1}}.

Taking into account (
2.5
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{dJ\left(\lambda u\right)}{d\lambda}>0,\;\;\;\mathrm{for}\;0<\lambda<\lambda^{\ast},\\
\frac{dJ\left(\lambda u\right)}{d\lambda}<0,\;\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\lambda^{\ast}<\lambda<\infty.\end{array}

In addition, it is easy to see that

I\left(\lambda u\right)=\lambda\frac{dJ\left(\lambda u\right)}{d\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}>0,\;\;\;0<\lambda<\lambda^{\ast},\\
=0,\;\;\;\lambda=\lambda^{\ast},\\
<0,\;\;\;\lambda^{\ast}<\lambda<\infty.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.3
(Depth
d
of potential well)

Let
p
and
q
satisfy the conditions given by (
1.1
). For the depth
d
of potential well, we have

d=\frac{q+1-p}{p\left(q+1\right)}C_{*}^{-\frac{p(q+1)}{q+1-p}}>0.

Proof
: If
u\in\mathcal{N}
, then
{\left\|\nabla u\right\|}_{p}\neq 0
and
I\left(u\right)=0
(or
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}=0
). It follows that

\displaystyle J\left(u\right)=\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\left\|u\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p},

which says
J\left(u\right)>0
.

From Lemma
2.2
(ii), we see that
u\in\mathcal{N}
must be of the form
u=\lambda^{\ast}v
for
v\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
,
v\neq 0
and
\lambda^{\ast}=\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|v\right\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p+1}}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle d=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}J(u)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{q+1}}{\|u\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\inf_{v\in W_{N}^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right),v\neq 0}\left(\frac{\|\nabla\left(\lambda^{\ast}v\right)\|_{p}^{q+1}}{\|\left(\lambda^{\ast}v\right)\|_{q+1}^{q+1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{q+1-p}{p\left(q+1\right)}C_{*}^{-\frac{p(q+1)}{q+1-p}}.

The proof is complete.

Lemma 2.4

with respect to
t
, there exists a sufficiently small
t_{0}>0
such that
I(u(t_{0}))<0
and
J(u(t_{0}))>0
for
I(u_{0})<0
and
J(u_{0})=d>0
. Considering (
3.22
), it is easy to see that
u_{t}\neq 0
for
0<t\leq t_{0}
. Then, we
have

J(u(t_{0}))=d-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left\|u_{\tau}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\operatorname{d}\tau=d_{0}<d.

Applying Proposition
2.1
and taking
t=t_{0}
as the initial time, we obtain
u(x,t)\in V
for
t>t_{0}
.

The rest is similar to the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

5
Supercritical initial energy
J\left(u_{0}\right)>d

In this section, we shall prove the global existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior of solutions to problem (
1.1
) for the supercritical initial energy
J\left(u_{0}\right)>d
by the properties of
\omega
-limits of solutions.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Global existence and uniqueness
. Consider the basis
u_{m}\left(x,t\right)
as in Theorem
2.1
, we claim that
u_{m}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
.

Assuming that the assertion is not tenable, then there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that
u_{m}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
for
t\in\left(0,t_{0}\right)
and
u_{m}\left(t_{0}\right)\in{\mathcal{N}}
. By (
3.17
), we have
\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{m\tau}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\operatorname{d}\tau\neq 0
for
\Omega\times\left(0,t_{0}\right)
. Further, it follows from (
3.3
) that
u_{m}(t_{0})\in J^{J\left(u_{m}\left(0\right)\right)}
. Hence,
u_{m}(t_{0})\in\mathcal{N}^{J\left(u_{m}\left(0\right)\right)}
. Due to the definition of
\lambda_{J(u_{0})}
, we have

\left\|u_{m}\left(t_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\geq\lambda_{J(u_{0})}.

(5.1)

Combining (
3.17
), by the fact
I\left(u_{m}\right)>0
, gives

\left\|u_{m}\left(t_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}<\left\|u_{m}\left(x,0\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{J(u_{0})},

which contradicts with (
5.1
). Then, for all
t_{0}>0
,
u_{m}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
.

Further, we can get
u_{m}\left(t\right)\in J^{J\left(u_{m}\left(0\right)\right)}\cap{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
. On the other hand, (
3.3
) yields

J\left(u_{m}\left(0\right)\right)\geq J\left(u_{m}\left(t\right)\right)\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\left\|\nabla u_{m}\right\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q+1}I\left(u_{m}\right),

and

J\left(u_{m}\left(0\right)\right)\geq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{m\tau}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\operatorname{d}\tau,

which imply boundedness of
\left\|\nabla u_{m}\right\|_{p}^{p}
and
\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u_{m\tau}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\operatorname{d}\tau
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, then problem (
1.1
) has a unique global weak solution
u
.

Asymptotic behavior
. We denote by
\omega\left(u_{0}\right)=\underset{t\geq 0}{\cap}\overline{\left\{u\left(s\right):s\geq t\right\}}
the
\omega
-limit of
u
. By (
3.3
) and (
3.17
), we have

\left\|\omega\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}<\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{J\left(u_{0}\right)},\;J\left(\omega\right)\leq J\left(u_{0}\right),

for any
\omega\in\omega\left(u_{0}\right)
, which means that
\omega\not\in\mathcal{N}^{J\left(u_{0}\right)}
and
\omega\in J^{J\left(u_{0}\right)}
, i.e.,
\omega\not\in\mathcal{N}
. Then,
\omega\left(u_{0}\right)\cap\mathcal{N}=\varnothing
, which implies
\omega\left(u_{0}\right)=\left\{0\right\}
. Therefore,
u\left(t\right)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Theorem
2.5
is proved.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we’ve studied the global existence and finite time blowup of solutions for a mixed pseudo-parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equation. Compared with the research in
[
27
]
, we replace the
\triangle u
term in the equation with the
\mathrm{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
term according to actual physical background. By combining the Galerkin method and the theory of potential wells, we first present the explicit expression for the depth
d
of potential well, and then overcome the difficulties in a priori estimation of
p
-Laplacian term and nonlinear term
{\mathchoice{{-\mkern-19.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}{{-\mkern-16.0mu\int}}}_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{q-1}u\operatorname{d}x
in the equation. In Section 5, we overcome the limitation of the potential well family method in the
J\left(u_{0}\right)>d
case by further analyzing the properties of
\omega
-limits of solutions. However, there exist many problems to be further studied such as in fractional order equations and others.

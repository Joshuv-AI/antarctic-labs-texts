Halil Ibrahim Kurt, Wenxian Shen

Lemma 2.5
.

For any given

\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx\leq m_{0}^{*}(u_{0}):=\max\Big\{\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\Big(\frac{a_{\sup}}{b_{\inf}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|\Omega|\Big\}\quad\forall\,0<t<T_{\max}^{\sigma}(u_{0})

(2.2)

\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{2+\sigma}(x,s;u_{0})dxds\leq\big(1+a_{\sup}\cdot(t-\tau)\big)m_{0}^{*}(u_{0})\quad\forall\,0<\tau<t<T_{\max}^{\sigma}(u_{0}).

Proof.

First, by integrating the first equation in (
1.3
) with respect to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Delta u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx-\chi\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\Big(\frac{u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})}{v_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})}\nabla v_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})\Big)dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\Big\{a(x,t)u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})-b(x,t)u^{2+\sigma}_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})\Big\}dx

\displaystyle\leq a_{\sup}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx-b_{\inf}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{2+\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx

\displaystyle\leq a_{\sup}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx-\frac{b_{\inf}}{|\Omega|^{1+\sigma}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0})dx\Big)^{2+\sigma}\quad\forall\,t\in(0,T_{\max}^{\sigma}(u_{0})).

Let

for

y^{\prime}(t)\leq a_{\sup}y(t)-\frac{b_{\inf}}{|\Omega|^{1+\sigma}}y^{2+\sigma}(t)\quad\text{for all}\,\,0<t<T_{\max}.

The inequality (

Second, observe that, for any

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b(x,t)u_{\sigma}^{2+\sigma}(x,s;u_{0})dxds

This together with (

Lemma 2.6

\displaystyle 0

Lemma 2.7

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
C(\varepsilon,p)>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p+1}\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{p+1}+C(\varepsilon,p)\Big(\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}\Big)^{p+1}\quad\text{for all}\;\;t\in(0,T_{\max}^{\sigma}).

Proof.

First, multiplying the second equation in (

) by

and integrating over
\Omega

which implies

\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p-1}|\nabla v_{\sigma}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{\nu}{p+1}\right)^{p+1}\left(\frac{p}{\mu}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{p+1}\quad\text{for all}\;\;t\in(0,T_{\max}^{\sigma}).

(2.5)

Then, for any
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p+1}=\int_{\Omega}{(v_{\sigma}^{\frac{p+1}{2}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{\sigma}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}|^{2}+C_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\right)^{2}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,\;\;\text{(by Ehrling's lemma)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}(p+1)^{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p-1}|\nabla v_{\sigma}|^{2}+C_{1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p}\Big)\Big(\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}\Big)\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{(by H\"{o}lder's inequality)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}(p+1)^{2}}{4p}\left(\frac{\nu}{p+1}\right)^{p+1}\left(\frac{p}{\mu}\right)^{p}\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{p+1}+C_{1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}^{p}\Big)\Big(\int_{\Omega}v_{\sigma}\Big)\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\,\text{(by \eqref{aux-new-eq1*})}

for all

and taking

Lemma 2.9

(Reverse Hölder’s inequality)
.

Assume that
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then, for any given Lebesgue measurable functions
f
and
g
on
\Omega
with
g(x)\not\equiv 0
for almost all
x\in\Omega
,

\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\geq\|f\|_{{L^{\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)}}\|g\|_{{L^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(\Omega)}}.

Proof.

An application of Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\Big\||f|^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\Big\||fg|^{\frac{1}{p}}|g|^{\frac{-1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\||fg|^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big\||g|^{\frac{-1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\||g|^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

This implies that

\Big\||f|^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\Big\||g|^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{p-1}.

Therefore,

\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\geq\Big\||f|^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{p}\Big\||g|^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{-(p-1)}.

The lemma follows.

3

In this section, we investigate the mass persistence of positive solutions of (

1.2

Throughout this section, we assume that
(u_{\sigma}(x,t),v_{\sigma}(x,t))=(u_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0}),v_{\sigma}(x,t;u_{0}))

1.3
) on the maximal interval

(x,t)
in

We first present some lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R>0
be such that

R>\begin{cases}\frac{\mu\chi^{2}}{4},&\text{if\,\, $0<\chi\leq 2$}\\
\mu(\chi-1),&\text{if \,$\chi>2$.}\\
\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then there is
\beta>0
,
\beta\not=\chi
such that

\frac{(p+1)\beta\mu}{p}-R<0,

(3.2)

where
p
is given by

p=\frac{4\beta}{(\chi-\beta)^{2}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

First, by (
3.3
), (
3.2
) is equivalent to

\frac{\left(\frac{4\beta}{(\chi-\beta)^{2}}+1\right)\beta\mu}{\frac{4\beta}{(\chi-\beta)^{2}}}-R<0,

which is equivalent to

\mu\beta^{2}+2\mu(2-\chi)\beta+\mu\chi^{2}-4R<0.

Let

f(\beta)=\mu\beta^{2}+2\mu(2-\chi)\beta+\mu\chi^{2}-4R.

(3.4)

Then
f(\beta)=0
if and only if
\beta=\beta_{-}
or
\beta=\beta_{+}
, where

\beta_{\pm}=\chi-2\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{R}{\mu}+1-\chi}.

(3.5)

Next, if
\chi>2
, then
R>\mu(\chi-1)
. This implies that
\beta_{\pm}
are real numbers,
f(\beta)<0
for
\beta\in(\beta_{-},\beta_{+})
, and

\beta_{+}=\chi-2+2\sqrt{\frac{R}{\mu}+1-\chi}>0.

Therefore, there is
\beta\in(0,\beta_{+})
with
\beta\not=\chi
such that (
3.2
) holds.

Now, if
\chi\leq 2
, then
R>\frac{\mu\chi^{2}}{4}
. This
implies that

{\frac{R}{\mu}+1-\chi}>\frac{\mu\chi^{2}}{4}\frac{1}{\mu}+1-\chi=\frac{(\chi-2)^{2}}{4}\geq 0,

and

\beta_{+}=\chi-2+2\sqrt{\frac{R}{\mu}+1-\chi}>\chi-2+2\sqrt{\frac{(2-\chi)^{2}}{4}}=0.

Therefore, there is also
\beta\in(0,\beta_{+})
with
\beta\not=\chi
such that (
3.2
) holds.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>0
. Then,

p\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\sigma}^{-p-1}}{v_{\sigma}}\nabla u_{\sigma}\cdot\nabla v_{\sigma}\leq\mu\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{-p}-\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{-p}\frac{|\nabla v_{\sigma}|^{2}}{v_{\sigma}^{2}}\quad\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,T_{\max}).

(3.6)

Proof.

In this proof, we drop
\sigma
in
(u_{\sigma},v_{\sigma})
and
T_{\max}^{\sigma}
if no confusion occurs.

Multiplying the second equation in (
1.3
) by
\frac{u^{-p}}{v}
and then integrating over
\Omega
with respect to

, we obtain that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{-p}}{v}\cdot\big(\Delta v-\mu v+\nu u\big)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{(-p)u^{-p-1}v\nabla u-u^{-p}\nabla v}{v^{2}}\cdot\nabla v-\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{-p}+\nu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{-p+1}}{v}\quad\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,T_{\max}).

Thus we have,

p\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{-p-1}}{v}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}u^{-p}\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}+\nu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{-p+1}}{v}=\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{-p}\quad\text{for all}\,\,t\in(0,T_{\max}).\vskip 7.22743pt

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p>0
. Then for every
\beta>0

\displaystyle(p+1)\chi\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\sigma}^{-p-1}}{v_{\sigma}}\nabla u_{\sigma}\cdot\nabla v_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}b_{\sup}^{\frac{p_{\alpha}}{1+\sigma}}\Big(\frac{2(p_{\alpha}-1-\sigma)}{p_{\alpha}(a_{\inf}-a_{\chi,\mu,\alpha})}\Big)^{\frac{p_{\alpha}-1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}}\frac{1+\sigma}{p_{\alpha}}|\Omega|,\quad&{\rm if}\quad p_{\alpha}>1+\sigma,\cr b_{\sup}|\Omega|,\quad&{\rm if}\quad p_{\alpha}=1+\sigma,\cr b_{\sup}|\Omega|^{p_{\alpha}-\sigma}(m_{0}^{*})^{1+\sigma-p_{\alpha}},\quad&{\rm if}\quad\sigma<p_{\alpha}<1+\sigma,\cr b_{\sup}^{\frac{2+\sigma}{p_{\alpha}+1}}\frac{p_{\alpha}+1}{2+\sigma}|\Omega|,\quad&{\rm if}\quad p_{\alpha}\leq\sigma.\end{cases}

∎

Now, we prove Proposition

Proof of Proposition

.

(1) First of all, by Lemma

Note that this inequality follows from the similar arguments as those in

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\ln u_{\sigma}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{b_{\sup}(1+\sigma)}{2+\sigma}\Big(1+a_{\sup}(t-\tau)\Big)m_{0}^{*}(u_{0})

for all

(

1.7

(2) In view of the Reverse Hölder inequality, taking

\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}\geq|\Omega|^{\frac{p_{\alpha}+1}{p_{\alpha}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{\sigma}^{-p_{\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}\quad\text{for all}\,\,t\in(\tau,T_{\max}^{\sigma}(u_{0})).

This together with Lemma

∎

\displaystyle 0

(4.3)

(4.4)

Lemma 4.2

(4.6)

) and (
4.6

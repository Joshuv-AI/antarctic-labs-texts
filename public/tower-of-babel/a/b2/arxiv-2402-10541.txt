Cousin complexes in motivic homotopy theory
Andrei Druzhinin, Håkon Kolderup, Paul Arne Østvær

Abstract
. We investigate Cousin (bi-)complexes in the setting of motives. For essentially smooth local schemes, the columns of the Cousin bicomplex with coefficients in any stable motivic homotopy type is shown to be acyclic. On the other hand, we also construct a family of non-acyclic Cousin complexes over any positive dimensional base scheme. Our method of proof employs the notion of extended compactified framed correspondences. Three major motivations for this study are to further our understanding of strict homotopy invariance, motivic infinite loop spaces, and connectivity in stable motivic homotopy theory. As applications of our main results on motivic Cousin complexes, we generalize several fundamental results in these topics to finite dimensional base schemes.

We refer

An analog of Cousin complexes in the context of stable homotopy theory is known as the

When

Deshmukh, Kulkarni and Yadav

[

A fundamental part of the Gersten conjecture is the injectivity of the left-most morphism in (

The Grothendieck-Serre conjecture
[

]
,
[

is an analogous

Theorem 1.1

(

.

For any
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
, there are natural equivalences in
\mathrm{Pre}(B)

(7.8)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Theta_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}X&\simeq&L_{\mathrm{sZar}}\varinjlim_{l}\Omega^{l}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(L_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{tf}}\mathrm{Fr}(-,X_{+}\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\wedge l}))^{\mathrm{gp}}\\
&\simeq&L_{\mathrm{Zar}}\varinjlim_{l}\Omega^{l}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(L_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{tf}}\mathrm{Fr}(-,X_{+}\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\wedge l}))^{\mathrm{gp}}.\end{array}

For any semi-local scheme
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
, there is an equivalence of spaces

(7.9)

(\Theta_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}X)(U)\simeq\varinjlim_{l}\Omega^{l}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(L_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{tf}}\mathrm{Fr}(U,X_{+}\wedge\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\wedge l}))^{\mathrm{gp}}.

Proof.

Proposition

7.13
shows the first equivalence in (
7.8
),
cf.
[

, Theorem 16.6]
),
which in turn implies the equivalence in (
7.9
).
Moreover,
the second equivalence in (
7.8
)
follows from (
7.9
) for local schemes
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
.
∎

Recall that
\pi^{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}_{i,j}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}X_{+})

denotes the presheaf of stable motivic homotopy groups on
\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
for
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
,
see (

).

Theorem 7.15
.

Suppose that
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{B}
,

i<-\dim B+j
, and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for any essentially smooth semi-local

B
-scheme

,
we have

\pi^{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}_{i,j}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}X_{+})(U)=0.

Equivalently,
the presheaf

\pi^{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}_{i,j}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}X_{+})

is semi-Zariski locally trivial.

Proof.

Since by
[

, Proposition 16.10]

the functor

takes connective objects to

-connective ones,
the claim follows from

∎

8.
A family of non-acyclic Cousin complexes

In this section, we aim to show that the Cousin complex over a positive dimensional base is generally non-acyclic.
Throughout,
the base scheme
B
has dimension
d>0
,
and except for in
Theorem

8.6

we assume that
B
is local with closed point
z
.
We will work with

(8.1)

\mathcal{F}=\Omega^{\infty}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(B/(B-z))\in\mathbf{SH}_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}^{s}(B).

We aim to show that the associated Cousin complex
\pi_{l}C^{\bullet}(B,\mathcal{F})
is non-acyclic for
l=\codim_{B}z
.

Given
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
,
a non-empty closed immersion
Z\not\hookrightarrow B
,
with
U_{Z}:=U\times_{B}Z
,
we consider the canonical map of motivic spaces

U/(U-U_{Z})\to B/(B-Z)\to B/(B-z).

We denote by
c_{Z}
the corresponding element in

[\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}U/(U-U_{Z}),\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(B/(B-z))]_{\mathbf{SH}_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(B)}\cong\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{U_{Z}}(U)=\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{Z}(U).

The isomorphism holds by (
8.1
),
and the equality holds because
\mathcal{F}_{Z}=\mathcal{F}_{U_{Z}}
by
Definition

3.10
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
such that
U\times_{B}z\neq\emptyset
,
and non-empty closed subscheme
Z
in
B
,
the element
c_{Z}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{Z}(U)
is non-trivial.

Proof.

Base change along the closed embedding
z\not\hookrightarrow B

takes
c_{Z}
to the class in

[\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(U_{z})_{+},\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}z_{+}]_{\mathbf{SH}_{\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathrm{Nis}}^{s}(z)}

defined by the canonical morphism of schemes
U_{z}\to z
,
which is non-trivial because
U_{z}\neq\emptyset
by assumption.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

For any
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}

and
non-empty closed subscheme
Z
in
B
,
there are stable equivalences of spectra

\mathcal{F}_{Z-z}(U-U_{z})\simeq 0,\quad\mathcal{F}_{z}(U)\simeq\mathcal{F}_{Z}(U).

Proof.

The first equivalence follows because

\varinjlim_{\codim_{B}Z=d-1}\mathcal{F}_{Z-z}(U-U_{z})\simeq\varinjlim_{\codim_{B}Z=d-1}(u^{*}\mathcal{F})_{Z-z}(U-U_{z})\simeq 0,

where
u\colon B-z\to B
,
because

u^{*}\mathcal{F}\simeq 0\in\mathbf{SH}^{s}(B-z)
.
The second equivalence follows from the first
because

\mathcal{F}_{z}(U)\simeq\fib(\mathcal{F}_{Z}(U)\to\mathcal{F}_{Z-z}(U-U_{z})).

∎

Proposition 8.3
.

For any
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
such that
U\times_{B}z\neq\emptyset
,
consider the morphisms

(8.2)

\partial\colon\bigoplus\limits_{\codim_{B}x=d-1}\pi_{l-(d-1)}\mathcal{F}_{x}(U)\to\bigoplus\limits_{\codim_{B}x=d}\pi_{l-d}\mathcal{F}_{x}(U)

defined in (
3.9
).
Then, for
l=d
, there is an isomorphism

(8.3)

\operatorname{Coker}(\partial)\cong\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{z}(U).

The element
c_{z}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{z}(U)
is non-trivial.

Proof.

Since
B
is local, we have the isomorphisms

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\codim_{B}x=d-1}\pi_{l-(d-1)}\mathcal{F}_{x}(U)

\displaystyle\cong\varinjlim_{\codim_{B}Z=d-1}\pi_{l-(d-1)}\mathcal{F}_{Z-z}(U-U_{z}),

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\codim_{B}x=d}\pi_{l-d}\mathcal{F}_{x}(U)

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{l-d}\mathcal{F}_{z}(U).

Thus, the left-hand side of (
8.2
) is trivial
by applying
Lemma

8.2
to
Z=z
,
and (
8.3
) follows.
The second claim follows by applying
Lemma

8.1
to
Z=z
.
∎

Proposition 8.4
.

For any
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
such that
U\times_{B}z\neq\emptyset
,
the morphism

(8.4)

g\colon\pi_{l}\mathcal{F}(U)\to\bigoplus\limits_{\codim_{B}x=0}\pi_{l}\mathcal{F}_{x}(U)

induces, for
l=0
, an isomorphism

(8.5)

\operatorname{Ker}(g)\cong\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U).

The element
c_{B}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U)
is non-trivial
(under the canonical equivalence
\mathcal{F}(U)\simeq\mathcal{F}_{B}(U)
).

Proof.

For
l=0
the left-hand side of (
8.4
) is
\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U)
.
The element
c_{B}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U)
is non-trivial
by
Lemma

8.1
applied to
Z=B
.
The right-hand side of (
8.4
) is trivial
by
Lemma

8.2

applied to all closed subschemes
Z
in
B
of codimension
1
.
∎

For any
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
,
we consider
the
d+2
-term complex of abelian groups

\pi_{l}\mathrm{Cou}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{tf}}(U,\mathcal{F})

concentrated in degrees
-1,\dots,d
,
see
Definitions

3.11
and
3.13
.

Theorem 8.5
.

Suppose
B
is a local scheme of Krull dimension
d>0
with closed point
z
.
For any non-empty
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
,
the cohomology group

H^{i}(\pi_{l}\mathrm{Cou}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{tf}}(U,\mathcal{F}))\cong\begin{cases}\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U),&\text{if }i=-1\text{ and }l=0,\\
0,&\text{if }i=0,\dots,d-1\text{ and }l\in\mathbb{Z},\\
\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{z}(U),&\text{if }i=d\text{ and }l=d.\end{cases}

The element
c_{B}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}(U)
defined by the canonical equivalence
\mathcal{F}(U)\simeq\mathcal{F}_{B}(U)

is non-trivial.
Moreover,

c_{z}\in\pi_{0}\mathcal{F}_{z}(U)
is non-trivial.

Proof.

The claim
for

i=0,\dots,d-1
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}

follows from
Lemma

8.2
.
The other claims
follow from
Propositions

8.4
and
8.3
.
∎

Theorem 8.6
.

Suppose
B
is any base scheme of positive Krull dimension.
Let
U\in\mathrm{EssSm}_{B}
be an essentially smooth local
B
-scheme.
Assume the image
z\in B
of the closed point of
U
has positive codimension in
B
.
Then

H^{i}(\pi_{l}\mathrm{Cou}^{\bullet}(U,\mathcal{F}))\neq 0,\quad i=\codim_{B}z,

where
l=\codim_{B}z
,
\mathcal{F}=\Omega^{\infty}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(B/(B-Z))
, and
Z
is the closure of
z
in
B
.

Proof.

This follows by applying
Theorem

8.5
to the local scheme
B_{(z)}
of
B
at
z
.
∎

On polyhomogeneous symbols and the Heisenberg pseudodifferential calculus.
Nathan Couchet, Robert Yuncken

Abstract
Polyhomogeneous symbols, defined by Kohn-Nirenberg [ 16 ] and Hörmander [ 15 ] in the 60’s, play a central role in the symbolic calculus of most pseudodifferential calculi. We prove a simple characterisation of polyhomogeneous functions which avoids the use of asymptotic expansions. Specifically, if U U is open subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , then a polyhomogeneous symbol on U × ℝ d U\times\mathbb{R}^{d} is precisely the restriction to t = 1 t=1 of a function on U × ℝ d + 1 U\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1} which is homogeneous for the dilations of ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} modulo Schwartz class functions. This result holds for arbitrary graded dilations on the vector space ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . As an application, using the generalisation 𝕋 H ​ M \mathbb{T}_{H}M of A. Connes’ tangent groupoid for a filtered manifold M M , we show that the Heisenberg calculus of Beals and Greiner [ 3 ] on a contact manifold or a codimension 1 1 foliation coincides with the groupoid calculus of [ 29 ] .



Keywords
: pseudodifferential calculus, classical symbol, homogeneous Schwartz function, tangent groupoid of a Heisenberg manifold, filtered manifold, contact manifold, non-commutative geometry.

Introduction: From a theorem on polyhomogeneous symbols to Beal and Greiner’s pseudodifferential calculus on Heisenberg manifold.

The notion of a pseudodifferential operator is rather general. The key idea, due to Kohn and Nirenberg, is to multiply the Fourier transform of a function by a symbol function, then apply the inverse Fourier transform.
In order for this procedure to be well-defined, and further to ensure that the class of pseudodifferential operators has nice properties such as stability under compositions and coordinate changes, one must impose strong restrictions on the class of symbols permitted.

For this reason, one typically restricts to the class of polyhomogeneous symbols, or some generalization.
These are the symbols which admit an asymptotic expansion:

a(x,\xi)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}(x,\xi),

with each function
a_{j}
being smooth and homogeneous in
\xi
of order
m-j
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
,
see
[
15
, Definition 18.1.5]
. The notation
\sim
here signifies that
a
differs from the
N
th partial sum of the series by a symbol-class function of order
m-N-1
outside of a neighbourhood of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
.

The notion of polyhomogeneous symbol is powerful, but somewhat cumbersome.
For instance, the proof that polyhomogeneous pseudodifferential operators are stable under smooth changes of coordinates is relatively technical, and it becomes a serious difficulty when one considers generalizations to more complicated calculi
[
3
,
24
,
18
]
.

The simplest example of a polyhomogeneous symbol is a function which is polynomial in
\xi
, where we have an exact equality:

a(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_{j}(x,\xi).

(1.1)

We have a simple way of converting an arbitrary polynomial into a homogeneous polynomial by weighting each
a_{j}
by an extra variable. This can be stated as follows.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The following are equivalent:

1.

a
is an (inhomogeneous) polynomial in the variable
\xi
of order
\leq m
.

2.

There exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
a homogeneous polynomial of order
m
in the variables
(\xi,t)
, such that
u(x,\xi,1)=a(x,\xi)
.

Proof.

With the notation of Equation (
1.1
), it suffices to put
u(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}t^{j}a_{j}(x,\xi)
.
∎

It turns out that there is an analogous theorem for polyhomogeneous functions. We say that a function
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
is
homogeneous modulo Schwartz
of order
m
in
(\xi,t)
if, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
the function

(x,\xi,t)\mapsto u(x,s\xi,st)-s^{m}u(x,\xi,t)

is Schwartz-class in the variables
(\xi,t)
(see Definition
1.5
for the precise definition).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The following are equivalent:

1.

a
is a polyhomogeneous symbol of order
\leq m
.

2.

There exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
, homogeneous modulo Schwartz of order
m
in the variables
(\xi,t)
, such that
u(x,\xi,1)=a(x,\xi)
.

This theorem, and its generalisation in the context of graded dilations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is the first main result of the present paper. For the statement in the presence of graded dilations, see Theorem
1.12
.

One reason to be interested in Theorem
1.2
is that it can be used to prove that the definition of classical polyhomogeneous pseudodifferential operators, due to Kohn-Nirenberg
[
16
]
and developed notably by Hörmander
[
15
]
, coincides with recent definitions using Connes’ tangent groupoid based on work by Debord-Skandalis
[
6
]
. The groupoid approach has certain advantages over the classical approach, in particular because the stability under compositions and changes of coordinates are obtained naturally from the structure of the tangent groupoid.

The equivalence of the two approaches for the classical unfiltered calculus was proven both by Debord-Skandalis
[
6
]
and Van Erp with the second author
[
29
]
in different ways. In this article, we are aiming to systematise the relationship between symbolic calculi and the groupoid calculus.

As an application of Theorem
1.2
, we will prove the analogous result for the Heisenberg calculus on a contact manifold.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a contact manifold. Beals and Greiner’s algebra of Heisenberg pseudodifferential operators on
M

[
3
]
coincides with the algebra of pseudodifferential operators defined via the Heisenberg tangent groupoid in
[
29
]
.

The Heisenberg tangent groupoid was defined independently by Van Erp
[
26
]
and Ponge
[
21
]
, see also
[
27
,
4
,
12
,
19
]
.

We also obtain the analogous theorem for codimension
1
foliations, see Theorem
5.17
. We expect that the groupoid calculus of
[
29
]
coincides with the Heisenberg calculus of
[
3
]
even when the osculating groups are non-constant, and indeed that it coincides with Melin’s calculus
[
18
]
for arbitrary filtered manifolds. However, the proof in these cases becomes far more technical due to the delicate nature of privileged coordinate systems, see
[
22
,
4
]
. This is outside the scope of the present paper.

. This completes the proof.

∎

5.1
Heisenberg manifold of type
\mathbb{H}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and its exponential charts.

In this section we investigate the Heisenberg calculus. Our goal is to compare the calculus on a Heisenberg manifold from Beals and Greiner
[
3
]
and the groupoidal pseudodifferential calculus of Van Erp and the second author
[
29
]
, developed in the case of filtered manifolds. We will show, see Theorems

[

The next definition is taken from BG
[

Definition 5.1
.

A Heisenberg manifold
M
is a d+1 dimensional
C^{\infty}
manifold equipped with a hyperplane bundle
\mathcal{V}
, that is, a subbundle
\mathcal{V}\subset TM
such that for each
y\in M

\mathcal{V}_{y}
has codimension one in the tangent space
T_{y}M
.

Remark:
In the language of vEY, following the preprint of Melin
[
18
]
from 1982 Definition 2.1, a Heisenberg manifold is a two-step filtered manifold with the following filtration:

H^{0}=\{0\}\leq H^{1}=\mathcal{V}\leq H^{2}=TM.

(5.1)

In the first few sections, we will deal only with the model situation.
We introduce the following vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
:

\begin{cases}X_{0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}\\
X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}b_{jk}x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}},&\forall\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq d,\end{cases}

(5.2)

where

is an anti-symmetric matrix of order

.
Moreover we let
\mathcal{V}
be the hyperplane bundle on

generated by
X_{1},...,X_{d}
.
The vector fields
(X_{j})
are left-invariant for the following group law on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\begin{cases}(x.x^{\prime})_{0}=x_{0}+x_{0}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,j=1}^{d}b_{jk}x_{k}x_{k}^{\prime}\\
(x.x^{\prime})_{j}=x_{j}+x_{j}^{\prime},&\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ j\in\{1,...,d\},\end{cases}

(5.3)

where
x_{j}
denotes the
j
th coordinate of

.

Example 1
.

The case

and

B=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n}&-I_{n}\\
I_{n}&0_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(5.4)

corresponds to the Heisenberg group.
The case where
b_{jk}=0,\forall\penalty\ (j,k)
yields the abelian group

.

From now on,

[

or
[

[

. In this particular case, we can equip this manifold with dilations and exponential charts as follows.

Definition 5.2

We define the following family of dilations, called the Heisenberg dilations. For

\delta_{s}:(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\mapsto(s^{2}v_{0},sv_{1},...,sv_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}.

If

where

More generally, thanks to Theorem

5.4
Application to contact manifolds and foliations

Theorem
5.16
can be easily extended to contact manifolds and foliations of codimension 1 which are both examples of Heisenberg manifolds. Note that CR-manifolds, appearing in the papers
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
of Folland-Stein and in other papers from the 70’s concerning the
\overline{\partial_{b}}
Von-Neumann problem (
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
), are particular cases of contact manifolds. Such applications strongly motivate the desire of exploring and understanding the Heisenberg calculus and subelliptic operators on such manifolds.

Recall that a continuous linear operator
P
on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(M)
is called
pseudolocal
if
\mathrm{singsupp}(Pf)\subseteq\mathrm{singsupp}(f)
for every
f\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(M)
, where
suppsing
denotes the singular support of a distribution.
The two definitions of the Heisenberg calculus in
[
3
]
and
[
29
]
are local in the following sense: a continuous linear operator
P
on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(M)
is a Heisenberg pseudodifferential operator (in the sense of either
[
3
]
or
[
29
]
) if and only if it is pseudolocal and for any open set
U
and smooth function
\chi
supported in
U
, the operator
M_{\chi}\circ P\circ M_{\chi}
is a Heisenberg pseudodifferential operator on
U\subset M
(in the sense of
[
3
]
or
[
29
]
, respectively). Here,
M_{\chi}
denotes the operator of multiplication by
\chi
.

Let
M
be a contact manifold of dimension
2d+1
, that is a smooth manifold with a 1-form
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(M)
such that
w\wedge\underbrace{d\omega\wedge...\wedge d\omega}_{2d\penalty\ times}
is nowhere zero. We write
\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{ker}(\omega)
for the contact hyperplane bundle. By Darboux’s Theorem
[
2
, Appendix 4]
, around any point
p\in M
we can find an open neighbourhood
U
and a contactomorphism
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{H}_{d}
, to the Heisenberg group with its standard contact structure. This means that around any point
p\in M
and for any vector field
X\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\mathcal{V})
:

\varphi_{*}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{2d}f_{i}X_{i},

(5.35)

where
X_{0},...,X_{2d}
are the model fields for
\mathbb{H}_{d}
and
f_{i}
are smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2d+1}
.

Let
P
be a pseudolocal operator on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(M)
. Let
\chi
be a bump function at
p
with support in
U
. Using Theorem
5.16
, the push-forward of
M_{\chi}\circ P\circ M_{\chi}
to
\mathbb{H}_{d}
via
\varphi
is in the Beals-Greiner calculus if and only if it is in the groupoid calculus. Since
p
was arbitrary, we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

The same general argument applies to foliations. If
M
is a
d+1
-dimensional manifold equipped with a codimension
1
foliation, then at every point
p
we can find a foliation chart
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
from a neighbourhood
U
of
p
to the abelian group
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with its standard folation. Applying the same reasoning as above, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.17
.

Let
M
be a manifold equipped with a codimension
1

[
3
]
coincides with the algebra of pseudodifferential operators defined via the Heisenberg tangent groupoid of

[

\mathbb{\Psi}^{m}_{\mathcal{V}}(M)=\mathbb{\Psi}^{m}(\mathbb{T}_{H}M)|_{t=1}.

(5.36)

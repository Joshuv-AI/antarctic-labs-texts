Congruence invariants of matrix mutation
Ahmet I. Seven, İbrahim Ünal

Abstract
. Motivated by the recent work of R. Casals on binary invariants for matrix mutation, we study the matrix congruence relation on quasi-Cartan matrices. In particular, we obtain a classification and determine normal forms modulo 4. As an application, we obtain new mutation invariants, which include the one obtained by R. Casals.

1.
Introduction

Mutation is a well-known operation which defines an equivalence relation, called mutation equivalence, on skew-symmetric integer matrices. It is important for the applications to determine algebraic properties of matrices which are invariant under the mutation equivalence. For example, matrix congruence class of a skew-symmetric matrix is a mutation-invariant property, so any invariant of congruence is also a mutation invariant.
Another basic idea to find mutation invariants is the notion of a quasi-Cartan matrix, which gives a class of
symmetric
matrices naturally associated to a skew-symmetric matrix
[
1
]
.
Therefore, it is a natural problem to classify symmetric quasi-Cartan matrices under the matrix-congruence relation and establish their relation to the mutation equivalence.
In this paper, we obtain a complete solution to this problem over
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
in a more general setup: we classify symmetric integer matrices whose diagonal elements are even numbers and determine normal forms using linear algebraic data.
Furthermore, we determine the congruence classes that correspond to a mutation equivalence class. In particular, we obtain new mutation invariants, which include the one obtained recently in
[
3
]
. We also answer questions that were left open in
[
3
]
.

A
, by “
A
modulo n”, we mean the matrix
A\pmod{n}

n
reductions of the entries of

. We denote by

,
m_{2}
copies of
A_{2}
,…,
m_{k}
copies of

, where

We say that two matrices

\mathrm{diag}(r\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{2}&{1}\\

We denote

q(u+v)=q(u)+q(v)+\Omega(u,v),(\mathrm{for\>\>all}\>u,v\in\overline{V}),

It is clear that the quadratic form

If

It is well known from the theory of quadratic forms that

Two quadratic forms

(ii)

q(\overline{V}_{0})=0,Arf(q)=0

(iii)

q(\overline{V}_{0})=\mathbb{F}_{2}

As a corollary of this result, we obtain the following characterization of the determinants of symmetric matrices:

Corollary 1.3

Let

denote

is

\det(A)=2\pmod{4}

•

In particular, if the rank of

is less than or equal to

Furthermore, for

•

\det(A)=1\pmod{4}

\det(A)=0\pmod{4}

•

\det(A)=-1\pmod{4}
or
\det(A)=0\pmod{4}

according to whether the rank of
\overline{A}
is

or less than

Remark 1.4
.

According to the corollary, for

is a binary function, i.e. it takes only two values. The same property holds if

is odd, because, then the rank of

Let us also note that

An important class of matrices that satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
are symmetric quasi-Cartan matrices
[
1
]
. More precisely, a symmetric
n\times n
matrix
A
is a quasi-Cartan matrix if and only if
A_{i,i}=2
for all
i=1,2,..,n
. These include the well-known (symmetric) generalized Cartan matrices of Lie theory
[
6
]
. In cluster algebra theory, those matrices are considered as companions of skew-symmetric matrices: a
quasi-Cartan companion
of a skew-symmetric matrix
B
is a quasi-Cartan matrix
A
such that
|A_{i,j}|=|B_{i,j}|
for all
i\neq j
. This notion was introduced in
[
1
]
to study properties of the matrix mutation operation.

To be more precise we briefly recall the definition of matrix mutation. This is an operation defined on skew-symmetrizable matrices, which is a class of matrices that include skew-symmetric matrices.

In this paper, we only work with skew-symmetric matrices
, so we don’t recall the definition of a skew-symmetrizable matrix.
For each matrix index
k
, the mutation in direction
k
transforms a skew-symmetric matrix
B
into

B^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(B)
whose entries are given as follows, where we use the notation
[b]_{+}=\max(b,0)
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Equation (1.1)]
):

(1.3)

B^{\prime}_{ij}=\begin{cases}-B_{ij}&\text{if $i=k$ or $j=k$;}\\[3.61371pt]
B_{ij}+[B_{ik}]_{+}[B_{kj}]_{+}-[-B_{ik}]_{+}[-B_{kj}]_{+}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This operation is involutive, so it defines a
mutation-equivalence
relation on skew-symmetric matrices: two matrices are called mutation equivalent if they can be obtained from each other by a sequence of mutations. As discussed in
[
3
, Section 1]
, it is a natural problem to find invariant properties of the matrix mutation. The most basic mutation invariant is the corresponding congruence class: if
B
and
B^{\prime}
are mutation equivalent, then there is a matrix
P
with
\det(P)=\mp 1
such that
P^{T}BP=B^{\prime}
(see, e.g.,
[
3
, Section 1]
).

Another basic mutation invariant, which is not discussed in
[
3
]
, is the corresponding quadratic form
[
8
, Proposition 5.2]
. To be more precise, let us
suppose that
B
is a skew-symmetric matrix and let
\overline{B}
denote the matrix

.
Let
\Omega
be the alternating bilinear form on

defined as follows:

.
Furthermore, the isomorphism class of

.

[

,
[

if

if

2.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.3

By Theorem
2.1
, we can assume that
A\pmod{4}
is of the form

\mathrm{diag}(r\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{2}&{1}\\
{1}&{2}\\
\end{array}\right),\,s\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{0}&{1}\\
{1}&{0}\\
\end{array}\right),\,t\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{0}&{2}\\
{2}&{0}\\
\end{array}\right),\,p\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{2}\\
\end{array}\right),\,m\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{0}\\
\end{array}\right)),

where
r,s,p,t,m
are non-negative integers and
n=2(r+s)+2t+m
; also
\dim(\overline{V}_{0})=2t+p+m
. Then
\det{A}=(-1)^{r+s}\cdot 2^{p}\cdot 0^{2t+m}\pmod{4}
, as a product of the determinants of the blocks, so we have the following:

•

\det{A}\equiv(-1)^{r+s}\pmod{4}
if and only if
{2t+m}=0,p=0
. Then
r+s
is even (resp. odd) if and only if
n=2(r+s)+p+2t+m=2(r+s)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
(resp.

). Thus
\det{A}
is as stated in the corollary.

•

\det{A}\equiv 2\pmod{4}
if and only if
2t+m=0
and
p=1
. This happens, by Lemma
2.7
, if and only if
\dim(\overline{V}_{00})=0
and
\dim(\overline{V}_{0})=1
.

•

\det{A}\equiv 0\pmod{4}
if and only if
2t+m\geq 1
or
p\geq 2
. This happens, by Lemma
2.7
, if and only if
\dim(\overline{V}_{00})\geq 1
.

It follows, in particular, that if the rank of the matrix
A\pmod{2}
is less than or equal to

(i.e.
\dim(\overline{V}_{0})\geq 2
), then
\dim(\overline{V}_{00})\geq\dim(\overline{V}_{0})-1\geq 1
(by Lemma
2.7
), so
\det{A}\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.
This completes the proof of the corollary.

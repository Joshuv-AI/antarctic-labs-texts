Mirror symmetry of height-periodic gradient Gibbs measures of an SOS model on Cayley trees
U.A. Rozikov

Abstract
. For the solid-on-solid (SOS) model with spin values from the set of all integers on a Cayley tree we give gradient Gibbs measures (GGMs). Such a measure corresponds to a boundary law (which is an infinite-dimensional vector-valued function defined on vertices of the Cayley tree) satisfying an infinite system of functional equations. We give several concrete GGMs of boundary laws which are independent from vertices of the Cayley tree and (as an infinite-dimensional vector) have periodic, (non-)mirror-symmetric coordinates. Namely, the particular class of height-periodic boundary laws of period q ≤ 5 q\leq 5 is studied, where solutions are classified by their period and (two-)mirror-symmetry.

Definitions and known results

We consider models where spin-configuration

\omega_{b}

\nabla\omega

[
13
]
,
[
14
]

The gradient spin variables are now defined by
\eta_{\langle x,y\rangle}=\omega(y)-\omega(x)
for each
\langle x,y\rangle
.

The space of

is denoted by
\Omega^{\nabla}
. The measurable structure on the space
\Omega^{\nabla}
is given by
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}^{\nabla}:=\sigma(\{\eta_{b}\,|\,b\in\vec{L}\}).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}^{\nabla}
be the sigma-algebra of gradient configurations outside of the finite volume
\Lambda
is generated by all gradient variables outside of
\Lambda
and the relative height-difference on the boundary of
\Lambda
.

For nearest-neighboring interaction potential
\Phi=(\Phi_{b})_{b}
, where

by

(1.1)

Q_{b}(\omega_{b})=e^{-\big(\Phi_{b}(\omega_{b})+|\partial x|^{-1}\Phi_{\{x\}}(\omega(x))+|\partial y|^{-1}\Phi_{\{y\}}(\omega(y))\big)},

where
\partial x
is the set of all nearest-neighbors of

S

To introduce the notion of

on the Cayley tree

\Gamma^{k}

which is the free group with

a_{i}^{-1}=a_{i}

[

Consider the family of

(

) defined by
T_{g}(a):=ga

This group is used to define translation-invariance of functions defined on the vertices
of the Cayley tree. In particular, the following definition is used

Definition 1
.

The potential
\Phi=(\Phi_{b})_{b}
is called invariant under a subgroup
\hat{G}_{k}\subset G_{k}

translations if for any

\Phi_{gb}(g\omega_{b})=\Phi_{b}(\omega_{b})

,

.

In the case

the potential is called translation-invariant.

Similarly one can define translation-invariant Gibbs measures (see
[
3

Define the Markov (Gibbsian) specification as

\gamma_{\Lambda}^{\Phi}(\sigma_{\Lambda}=\omega_{\Lambda}|\omega)=(Z_{\Lambda}^{\Phi})(\omega)^{-1}\prod_{b\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset}Q_{b}(\omega_{b}).

If for any bond

the transfer operator

is
a function of gradient spin variable

.

Boundary laws

[

) which allow to describe the subset of

Definition 2

•

A family of vectors

•

A boundary law

The gradient Gibbs specification is defined as the family of probability kernels

1.2.
The boundary law equation for the SOS model.

In this subsection for
\sigma:x\in V\mapsto\sigma(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the Hamiltonian
of the SOS model, i.e.,

(1.6)

H(\sigma)=-J\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle:\atop x,y\in V}|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)|,

Then by (

Here

It is clear that each

.

[

[

2.1.
Bounded sequence

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following

Proposition 2

If a strictly positive sequence

2.2.
Periodic solutions

Definition 5
.

•

A sequence
\{u_{i}\}_{i=-1}^{\infty}
is called
q

u_{i+q}=u_{i}\ \ \mbox{for some}\ \ q\geq 1\ \ \mbox{and for all}\ \ i\geq-1;

•

A

u_{i}=u_{q-i},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ i=-1,0,1,\dots,[q/2].

•

A

-periodic sequence is called two-mirror symmetric if there exists

u_{i}=u_{p-i},\ \ i=-1,0,1,\dots,[p/2];

u_{p+j}=u_{q-j},\ \ j=0,1,\dots,[(q-p)/2].

Proposition 3
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
. Any
q
-periodic sequence
\{u_{i}\}_{i=-1}^{\infty}

u_{-1}=u_{1}
,

By (

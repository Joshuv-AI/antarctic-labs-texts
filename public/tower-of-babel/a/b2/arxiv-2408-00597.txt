Comprehensive Study of Dynamical Phase Transitions in One-Dimensional XY Model
Bao-Ming Xu, Xiu-Ying Zhang

Abstract
Short-time transient dynamical phase transition (SDQPT), serving as the theoretical framework on far-from-equilibrium physics of quantum many-body systems, has been experimentally observed recently. However, the existence of different types of SDQPTs and the associated conditions are yet to be elusive. In this paper, we comprehensively classify SDQPTs by considering the one-dimensional transverse field quantum XY model initialized in the ground state. We find that there are only six types of SDQPTs, and three of which are completely new that had not yet been identified previously. The associated conditions for different SDQPTs are discussed in detail, and based on this, the dynamical phase diagrams are given. Our analytical method used to classify SDQPTs in one-dimensional transverse field quantum XY model can be applied to any other two-band models in one- and two-spatial dimensions.

Introduction

The past decades have witnessed the flourishing of dynamical quantum phase transitions (DQPTs), both in experimental and theoretical research, among condensed matter physicists, because quantum simulators have nowadays achieved experimental access to the real-time dynamics of closed quantum many-body systems; see Refs.

DQPTs are the sudden or non-analytic changes in the behaviour of a large quantum system during its time evolution, and generally refer to two largely independent concepts: long-time relaxation DQPT and short-time transient DQPT. It should be noted that the abbreviation of DQPT refers to the dynamical quantum phase transition in general, and does not distinguish whether it belongs to a long-time relaxation dynamical phase transition or a short-time transient dynamical phase transition. Some papers represent long-time relaxation DQPT as DQPT-I, and short-time transient dynamical phase transition as DQPT-II. In this paper, for the convenience of discussion, we use LDQPT to represent long-time relaxation DQPT and SDQPT to represent short-time transient DQPT. LDQPT describes the dynamical relaxation behaviors of two-point correlators

Marino2022

;

SDQPT is defined by the zeros in the Loschmidt echo or the nonanalyticities in its rate function obtained by large deviation principle. It is analogous to the zeros in the partition function and the nonanalyticities in the free energy in equilibrium phase transitions, and therefore the rate function of Loschmidt echo is also called the dynamical free energy per particle. Some SDQPTs obey the dynamical scaling defined by a corresponding out-of-equilibrium analog of the universality class

II
Quenched one-dimensional XY model

The Hamiltonian of the one-dimensional XY model is
Dutta2015

\hat{H}(\lambda,\gamma)=-\frac{J}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\biggl[\frac{1+\gamma}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}\hat{\sigma}_{j+1}^{x}+\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{y}\hat{\sigma}_{j+1}^{y}+\lambda\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{z}\biggr],

(1)

where
J
is longitudinal spin-spin coupling,
\gamma
governs the anisotropic coupling between spins along the
x
and
y
directions,
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter measuring the strength of the transverse field with respect to the longitudinal spin-spin coupling. In this work, we set
J=1
as the overall energy scale without loss of generality.
\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{j}

(\alpha=x,y,z)
is the spin-1/2 Pauli operator at lattice site
j
and the periodic boundary conditions are imposed as
\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{N+1}=\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{1}
. Here we only consider that
N
is even. The model exhibits competitions between anisotropic and magnetic couplings, which results in the existence of multiple phases. The quantum phase transition from the ferromagnetic phase (FM) to the paramagnetic phase driven by the transverse field
\lambda
is called the Ising transition with the quantum critical point
\lambda_{c}=1
. On the other hand, the quantum phase transition between two FMs, with magnetic ordering in the
x
-direction and the
y
-direction, respectively, driven by the anisotropy parameter
\gamma
, is called the anisotropic transition with the critical point
\gamma_{c}=0
. In fact, in the absence of the transverse field, the ground state of the system is in the Luttinger liquid phase at
\gamma_{c}=0
.

The Hamiltonian is integrable and can be mapped to a system of free fermions and therefore be solved exactly. By applying the Jordan-Wigner transformation and the Fourier transformation, the Hamiltonain converts from spin operators into spinless fermionic operators as
Mukherjee2007
;
Bunder1999

\hat{H}(\lambda,\gamma)=\sum_{k}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-k}&\hat{c}_{k}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda+\cos k&-i\gamma\sin k\\
i\gamma\sin k&-\lambda-\cos k\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{c}_{-k}\\
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where
\hat{c}_{k}
and
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}
are respectively fermion annihilation and creation operators for mode
k=(2n-1)\pi/N
with
n=1\cdots N/2
, corresponding to antiperiodic boundary conditions for
N
is even. Each
\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda,\gamma)
acts on a two-dimensional Hilbert space generated by
\{\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}|0\rangle,~|0\rangle\}
, where
|0\rangle
is the vacuum of the Jordan-Wigner fermions
\hat{c}_{k}
and
\hat{c}_{-k}
, and can be represented in that basis by a
2\times 2
matrix

\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda,\gamma)=\mathbf{d}_{k}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{\sigma}}

(3)

with

\mathbf{d}_{k}=(0,\gamma\sin k,\lambda+\cos k).

(4)

The instantaneous eigenvalues are
d^{\pm}_{k}=\pm d_{k}
with

d_{k}=\sqrt{(\lambda+\cos k)^{2}+\gamma^{2}\sin^{2}k}.

(5)

The corresponding eigenvectors are

|d^{+}_{k}\rangle=\biggl[\frac{1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k}}{\sqrt{2(1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k})}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}+i\frac{d_{k}^{y}/d_{k}}{\sqrt{2(1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k})}}\biggr]|0\rangle

(6)

and

|d^{-}_{k}\rangle=\biggl[i\frac{d_{k}^{y}/d_{k}}{\sqrt{2(1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k})}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{-k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}+\frac{1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k}}{\sqrt{2(1+d_{k}^{z}/d_{k})}}\biggr]|0\rangle,

(7)

respectively.

At time
t\leq 0
, the system is prepared in the ground state

|G\rangle=\bigotimes_{k}|d^{-}_{k}\rangle.

(8)

Then at time
t=0
, the transverse field is suddenly changed from
\lambda
to
\lambda^{\prime}
or the anisotropic parameter is suddenly changed from
\gamma
to
\gamma^{\prime}
. We assume that this process is so sudden that the system state has no time to change. After quenching, the dynamics of the system is governed by the evolution operator (
\hbar=1
)

\hat{U}(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}=\bigotimes_{k}e^{-i\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t},

(9)

so that the state
|\psi(t)\rangle
at a time
t
after the quench is given by

|\psi(t)\rangle=\bigotimes_{k}e^{-i\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}|d^{-}_{k}\rangle.

(10)

III
The fundamental theory of SDQPT

The central object within the theory of SDQPTs is the Loschmidt overlap amplitude quantifying the deviation of the time-evolved state from the initial condition. Here, the Loschmidt overlap amplitude is generalized to general density matrices defined as

\mathcal{G}(t)=\langle G|\hat{U}(t)|G\rangle.

(11)

Because different modes are independent with each other, the Loschmidt overlap amplitude can be written as

\mathcal{G}(t)=\prod_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)=\prod_{k}\langle d^{-}_{k}|e^{-i\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}|d^{-}_{k}\rangle.

(12)

The behavior of the momentum-wise Loschmidt overlap amplitude can be visualized by the vector introduced by Ding
Ding2020
:

\vec{R}_{k}=(x_{k},y_{k})=|\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)|e^{i\phi_{k}^{G}(t)},

(13)

where

\phi_{k}^{G}(t)=\phi_{k}(t)-\phi_{k}^{D}(t)

(14)

is the geometric phase of the Loschmidt overlap amplitude with

\phi_{k}(t)=\arg\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)

(15)

being the total phase of
\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)
and

\phi_{k}^{D}(t)=-\langle d^{-}_{k}|\hat{H}_{k}(\gamma^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})|d^{-}_{k}\rangle t.

(16)

being the dynamical phase. At a given time, the winding number of the geometric phase in the first Brillouin zone is defined as
Budich2016

\nu(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\partial\phi_{k}^{G}(t)}{\partial k}dk.

(17)

In general, it takes a quantized jump whenever the evolution of the system passes through a critical time of SDQPT; therefore, it is generally regarded as the dynamical topological order parameter of SDQPT. It is obvious that
\nu(t)
can be understood as the winding number of the trajectory
\vec{R}_{k}
around the origin.

The rate function of the Loschmidt echo, measuring the return probability
|\mathcal{G}(t)|^{2}
, is defined as

r(t)=-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln|\mathcal{G}(t)|^{2}.

(18)

It is the dynamical analogues of the equilibrium free energy, namely the dynamical free energy. In the thermodynamic limit one can derive an exact result for
r(t)
:

r(t)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{Re}\biggl[\int_{0}^{\pi}dk\ln\mathcal{G}_{k}(t)\biggr].

(19)

A SDQPT will occur when
r(t)
exhibits a nonanalyticity at some times
t^{*}
, namely critical times.
A powerful method to analyse the nonanalyticity of
r(t)
is Fisher zeros
Heyl2013
;
Fisher1965
. In order to apply this concept, we should expend time
t
into the complex plane and focus on the boundary partition function
Z(z)=\langle G|e^{-z\hat{H}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})}|G\rangle
. The boundary partition function can be expressed as

Z(z)=\prod_{k}\langle d^{-}_{k}|e^{-z\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})}|d^{-}_{k}\rangle

(20)

where
z\in\mathbb{C}
. For imaginary
z=it
this just describes the overlap amplitude of Eq. (
11
). The Fisher zeros are the values of
z
that make
Z(z)=0
. Due to the multiplicative properties of Eq. (
20
), a zero in
Z(z)
is equivalent to finding at least one mode
k
and one
z
making
\langle d^{-}_{k}|e^{-z\hat{H}_{k}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})}|d^{-}_{k}\rangle=0
. In the thermodynamic limit the zeros of the boundary partition function in the complex plane coalesce to a family of lines labeled by a number
n\in\mathbb{Z}

z_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{2d_{k}^{\prime}}\Biggl[\ln\frac{1-\frac{\mathbf{d}_{k}}{d_{k}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{d}_{k}^{\prime}}{d_{k}^{\prime}}}{1+\frac{\mathbf{d}_{k}}{d_{k}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{d}_{k}^{\prime}}{d_{k}^{\prime}}}+i(2n+1)\pi\Biggr].

(21)

SDQPTs, manifested as the non-analytic behaviors of the dynamical free energy, are therefore contingent upon the existence of purely imaginary zeros of the boundary partition function, corresponding to real-time zeros of the Loschmidt overlap amplitude. From Eq. (
21
), we can obtain the well known condition of SDQPT
\mathbf{d}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{k}^{\prime}=0
, i.e.,

(\lambda+\cos k)(\lambda^{\prime}+\cos k)+\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\sin^{2}k=0.

(22)

In other words, given a set of parameters
(\lambda,\gamma,\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
, a SDQPT can only occur if at least one mode satisfy Eq. (
22
) (or
\mathbf{d}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{k}^{\prime}=0
), this mode is called critical mode
k^{*}
. Conversely, solving Eq. (
22
) yields the critical mode
k^{*}
, and discussing the existence of the solution yields the concrete parameters
(\lambda,\gamma,\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
for SDQPT. Then, there is a series of critical times
t^{*}_{n}

t^{*}_{n}=\frac{1}{2d_{k^{*}}^{\prime}}(2n+1)\pi

(23)

depending on the corresponding critical mode
k^{*}
. Fisher zeros are the key basis for determining whether SDQPT occurs, which have been used to discuss the relation between SDQPTs and equilibrium phase transitions
Vajna2014
. Beyond that, Fisher zeros are also the key basis for determining the type of SDQPT, which will be used to achieve the main goal of this paper: classifying SDQPTs and discussing the classification criteria. In this paper, the modes
k=0
and
k=\pi
is named as the edge modes, and the others (
k\neq 0
or
k\neq\pi
) are named as the edge modes. The critical mode can be either a body or an edge mode. Based on the number and attribution of the critical mode, as well as the properties of Fisher zeros, SDQPTs can be classified into six types, which will be discussed in the next section in detail. SDQPTs in the two-band mode have been classified by the ground state topology of both the initial and final Hamiltonians
Vajna2015
. In contrast, our classification is based on the attribution and number of critical modes, and each resulting type of SDQPTs has its own topological properties.

Physically, the condition of SDQPT can be understood with the help of the Bloch sphere. According to the rate function, SDQPT occurring is equivalent to making
\langle d^{-}_{k^{*}}|e^{-i\hat{H}_{k^{*}}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}|d^{-}_{k^{*}}\rangle=0
, which means a flip of the vector of
|d^{-}_{k^{*}}\rangle
in the Bloch sphere (see Fig.
1
). It is conceivable that the axis of rotation must be perpendicular to a vector in order to flip it (see the green axis in Fig.
1
). Therefore, SDQPT is only possible if the axis corresponding to the evolution operator
\hat{U}_{k^{*}}(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}_{k^{*}}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}
is perpendicular to the initial state
|d^{-}_{k^{*}}\rangle
, otherwise it is impossible (see the blue axis in Fig.
1
). The axis corresponding to the evolution operator can be understood as
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}^{\prime}
and the vector of initial state can be understood as
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}
, therefore the condition of SDQPT can be demonstrated as
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}^{\prime}=0
.

However, it must be noted that
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}^{\prime}=0
is not always effective in causing SDQPT. Specifically, the discussions above have acquiesced
d_{k^{*}}\neq 0
and
d_{k^{*}}^{\prime}\neq 0
, i.e., the energy levels of the pre- and post-quench systems are both non-degenerated. Breaking this acquiescence, i.e.,
d_{k^{*}}=0
or
d_{k^{*}}^{\prime}=0
,
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}^{\prime}=0
does not always imply the perpendicularity, because the orientation of zero vector is uncertain and can no longer be used to represent the orientation of the initial state or the axis of evolution. In this subtle case, we use whether the initial state can be flipped as a criterion to judge whether SDQPT occurs, which is a more fundamental and thoroughgoing approach to determine SDQPT. If
d_{k^{*}}^{\prime}=0
, the initial state
|d^{-}_{k^{*}}\rangle
would not evolve at all, let along undergo SDQPT, because
\hat{U}_{k^{*}}(t)=e^{-i\hat{H}_{k^{*}}(\lambda^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})t}=\mathds{I}
(
\mathds{I}
is an identity matrix). If
d_{k^{*}}=0
but
d_{k^{*}}^{\prime}\neq 0
, the orientation of the evolution operator would still be represented by
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}^{\prime}
, and as long as it is perpendicular to the orientation of the initial state (not
\mathbf{d}_{k^{*}}
), the initial state would be flipped and SDQPT would occur. The analysis above will be concretized in the following sections.

IV
The Classification of Dynamical phase transitions

In this section, we mainly classify SDQPTs and discuss the parameter spaces of different SDQPTs based on Eq. (
22
). First, we give the solution to Eq. (
22
). If
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=1
, Eq. (
22
) tells us that

\cos k=-\frac{1+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}

(24)

with the condition
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}\neq 0
.
If
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\neq 1
, the solutions of Eq. (
22
) are

\cos k_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2(1-\gamma\gamma^{\prime})}\Bigl[-(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})\pm\sqrt{\Delta}\Bigr]

(25)

with the condition

\Delta=(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})^{2}-4(1-\gamma\gamma^{\prime})(\gamma\gamma^{\prime}+\lambda\lambda^{\prime})\geq 0.

(26)

Specially, if
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=0
, these two solutions would be simplified as

\cos k_{1}=-\lambda,~\cos k_{2}=-\lambda^{\prime}.

(27)

And if
\lambda=\pm 1
or
\lambda^{\prime}=\pm 1
, the solutions (
25
) would be reduced to

\cos k_{1}=\mp 1,~\cos k_{2}=\mp\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}+\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}{1-\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}.

(28)

These solutions allow for single, two, or even infinite critical modes to cause SDQPT.

IV.1
SDQPT-1

SDQPT-1 is caused by a single critical body mode, wherein the line of Fisher zeros cuts
\mathrm{Im}(z)
axis. First, we discuss the parameter space of SDQPT-1.

For
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=1
, a critical body mode
k^{*}=\arccos[-\frac{1+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}]
can be found if
|\frac{1+\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}|<1
. And we find that

(29)

|\lambda|>1\rightarrow|\lambda^{\prime}|<1

In summary, we demonstrate that there are only six possible SDQPTs in the one-dimensional XY model, three of which were previously discovered, and the others are entirely new SDQPTs that have not been discovered before. Furthermore, the associated conditions for different SDQPTs are shown in Table
1
. It is important to emphasize that this classification of SDQPT and the associate criteria are universal, holding for any other two-band models in one and two dimensions, including SSH model, Kitaev-chain, Haldane model,
p+ip
superconductor, etc. To be specific, there are two types of the critical mode: the critical body mode (
k^{*}\neq 0
or
k^{*}\neq\pi
) and the critical edge mode (
k^{*}=0
or
k^{*}=\pi
). If only one critical body mode is generated by controlling the parameters, and the line of Fisher zeros cuts Im(z) axis at this mode, SDQPT-1 will take place in which the winding number jumps up or down with an integer step. On the contrary, if two critical body modes are generated SDQPT-2 will occur where the winding number jumps up and down alternately with the integer step. If these two critical body modes merge into one by adjusting the parameters, SDQPT-3 occurs with the singularity of the winding number. A single critical edge mode generates SDQPT-4 where the winding number jumps up or down with the half-integer step. A critical body mode and a critical edge mode commonly cause SDQPT-5 whose topological property is the alternative jumps of the winding number with integer and half-integer steps. If all the modes are critical, SDQPT-6 will be caused. The exact values of the initial and final parameters
\lambda
,
\gamma
,
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\gamma^{\prime}
in given classification criteria do not modify qualitatively the behavior of the corresponding types of SDQPT as a sign of some kind of universality.

Dynamical phase diagram

In one-dimensional XY model, the ground state phase diagram has already been well known

;
Dutta2015
;

). Although the conditions under which SDQPTs occur are listed in Table 1, the dynamical phase diagram allows for a more clear and intuitive understanding.

We should consider six cases:
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}<0
,
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=0
including
\gamma=0\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
and
\gamma\neq 0\rightarrow\gamma^{\prime}=0
,
0<\gamma\gamma^{\prime}<1
,
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=1
and
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}>1
to investigate the dynamical phase diagrams, and plot them in Fig.

. It can be seen that for
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
, i.e., the anisotropic parameters (
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
) before and after quench are both not at the critical point
\gamma_{c}=0
, SDQPT-1 always happen if the transverse field is quenched across quantum critical point
|\lambda_{c}|=1
[see the purple areas in Fig.
8
(a)-(d)]. However, this does not mean SDQPT must depend on the quantum phase transition because SDQPT-2 (the yellow areas) and SDQPT-3 (the red curves) can happen when the transverse field is quenched within the same phases. To be specific, SDQPT-2 and SDQPT-3 can happen at the ferromagnetic phase if
0<\gamma\gamma^{\prime}<1
, but can happen at the paramagnetic phase if
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}>1
[see Fig.

(b) and (c)]. Beyond that, SDQPT-2 and SDQPT-3 can happen at both ferromagnetic and paramagnetic phases, if the anisotropic parameter is quenched across the critical point
\gamma_{c}=0
[see Fig.
8
(d)]. It is worth noting that for a given suitable field
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, only the quench of the anisotropic parameter across the critical point
\gamma_{c}=0
can still cause SDQPT-2 and SDQPT-3, and even SDQPT-1.

At the critical boundaries, the situations are more complicated. If the transverse field is quenched from the quantum critical point
|\lambda_{c}|=1
, SDQPT can always occur, either SDQPT-4 or SDQPT-5 (see the blue and cyan lines in Fig.

\lambda^{\prime}
and
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}
, as shown in Table
1
. On the other hand, if the transverse field is quenched to the quantum critical point
|\lambda^{\prime}|=1

\lambda^{\prime}
and
\gamma\gamma^{\prime}
(see the purple lines in Fig.

\gamma_{c}=0
, SDQPT-1 can always occur when the transverse field is quench to the ferromagnetic phase
|\lambda^{\prime}|<1
, independent of the pre-quenched field
\lambda
[see the purple areas in Fig.

(e)]; on the contrary, if the anisotropic parameter is quenched to the critical point

|\lambda^{\prime}|<1

\lambda^{\prime}\neq\lambda
[see the purple areas in Fig.

Vajna2014
.

In addition to the Ising transition and anisotropic transition, there is another type of transition, namely the commensurate-incommensurate transition in one-dimensional XY model
Makki2022
;
Dutta2015
;
Mukherjee2007
;
Bunder1999
. This transition causes LDQPT (i.e., long-time relaxation dynamical phase transition). To be specific, two distinct power-law relaxation behaviors following a sudden quench can be observed, depending upon whether the quenched Hamiltonian lies in the commensurate or the incommensurate phase
Makki2022
. A natural question arises, does the commensurate-incommensurate transition has any effect on SDQPT? The answer is no, because SDQPT can occur regardless of whether the commensurate-incommensurate transition occurs or not. This result demonstrate that SDQPT is of fundamentally different origin from LDQPT.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have comprehensively investigated SDQPTs by focusing the Fisher zeros, the rate function of Loschmidt echo and the winding number after quenching the strength of transverse field and the anisotropic parameter in the one-dimensional XY model. Accordingly, SDQPTs have been classified into six types based on the attribution and number of critical modes, and each type has its own topological properties. Specifically, SDQPT-1 is caused by a single critical body mode at which the line of Fisher zeros cuts the imagery axis, and its topological parameter order jumps up or down with an integer unit at the critical times. SDQPT-2 happens when two critical body modes exist. In SDQPT-2, the winding number jumps up at one set of critical times determined by one critical mode, but jumps down at another set of critical times determined by another critical mode, the jump heights are all integer units. By adjusting the external field and anisotropic parameter, two critical body mode above can approach each other until they merge. In this case, SDQPT-3 occurs. SDQPT-3 can not be described by the rate function of the Loschmidt echo, but can be described by the singularities of the winding number. SDQPT-4 is caused by a single critical edge mode, wherein the winding number jumps up or down with a half-integer unit. SDQPT-5 is commonly caused by two critical modes: one critical body mode and one critical edge mode. The critical body mode makes the winding number jump with the integer unit, whereas the critical edge mode makes it jump with the half-integer unit. SDQPT-6 is very anomalous that all the modes locate at the imagery axis. Although SDQPT-1, SDQPT-2 and SDQPT-4 have been previously discovered in other papers, SDQPT-3, SDQPT-5 and SDQPT-6 are entirely new and had not been discussed before. Furthermore, the parameter spaces of these six SDQPTs have been determined and the phase diagrams have been given. It is important to emphasize that this classification of SDQPT and the associate criteria are universal, holding for any other two-band models in one- and two-dimensions (e.g. SSH model, Kitaev-chain, Haldane model, p+ip superconductor, etc.).

In equilibrium, continuous phase transitions exhibit scaling and universality, connecting to renormalization group theory and the associated fixed points. To what extent the concepts of scaling and universality can be applied to SDQPTs is of fundamental importance for the theoretical understanding of the out-of-equilibrium dynamics of quantum many-body systems. In Ref.
Heyl2015
, Heyl proved that these concepts can be extended to SDQPTs both in one- and two-dimensions Ising models, showing that SDQPTs are critical points associated with unstable fixed points of equilibrium Ising models. Therefore, these SDQPTs obey scaling and universality. It is important to note that the SDQPTs in Ising mode belongs to SDQPT-1. In other words, SDQPT-1 obeys scaling and universality. Investigating to what extent universality and scaling generalize to the other types of SDQPT (especially those independent of quantum phase transition), and whether genuine nonequilibrium fixed points can also appear, which are not related to the equilibrium criticality, is a pertinent task for future work on the theory of SDQPTs.

Abstract
We study the decays B ¯ 0 → K ¯ 0 ​ X \bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{0}\,X , B − → K − ​ X B^{-}\to K^{-}\,X , B ¯ s 0 → η ⁡ ( η ′ ) ​ X \bar{B}^{0}_{s}\to\eta(\eta^{\prime})\,X , B ¯ 0 → K ¯ ∗ 0 ​ X \bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{*0}\,X , B − → K ∗ ⁣ − ​ X B^{-}\to K^{*-}\,X , B ¯ s 0 → ϕ ​ X \bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to\phi\,X , with X ≡ X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X\equiv X(3872) , from the perspective of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) being a molecular state made from the interaction of the D ∗ ⁣ + ​ D − , D ∗ 0 ​ D ¯ 0 D^{*+}D^{-},D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0} and c . c . c.c. components. We consider both the external and internal emission decay mechanisms and find an explanation for the K ¯ 0 ​ X \bar{K}^{0}\,X and K − ​ X K^{-}\,X production rates, based on the mass difference of the charged and neutral D ∗ ​ D ¯ D^{*}\bar{D} components. We also find that the internal and external emission mechanisms add constructively in the B ¯ 0 → K ¯ 0 ​ X \bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{0}\,X , B − → K − ​ X B^{-}\to K^{-}\,X reactions, while they add destructively in the case of B ¯ 0 → K ¯ ∗ 0 ​ X \bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{*0}\,X , B − → K ∗ ⁣ − ​ X B^{-}\to K^{*-}\,X reactions. This feature explains the decay widths of the present measurements and allows us to make predictions for the unmeasured modes of B ¯ s 0 → η ⁡ ( η ′ ) ​ X ​ ( 3872 ) \bar{B}^{0}_{s}\to\eta(\eta^{\prime})\,X(3872) and B − → K ∗ ⁣ − ​ X ​ ( 3872 ) B^{-}\to K^{*-}\,X(3872) . The future measurement of these decay modes will help us get a better perspective on the nature of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) and the mechanisms present in production reactions of that state.

IV
Conclusions

In this work we have addressed the issue of
B
decays to a pseudoscalar and the
X(3872)
and a vector and
X(3872)
,
from the perspective that the
X(3872)
is a dynamically generated state from the
D\bar{D}^{*}+c.c.
interaction in coupled channels.
In particular we have studied the decays
\bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{0}\,X
,
B^{-}\to K^{-}\,X
,
\bar{B}^{0}_{s}\to\eta(\eta^{\prime})\,X
,
\bar{B}^{0}\to\bar{K}^{*0}\,X
,
B^{-}\to K^{*-}\,X
,
\bar{B}_{s}^{0}\to\phi\,X
, with
X\equiv X(3872)
.
We have seen that the production of the final states requires both the mechanism of external and internal emission.
Although the internal emission mechanism is suppressed with respect to external emission by a color factor,
we have seen that for the vector production the two terms interfere destructively,
while they add constructively for pseudoscalar production.
This has as a consequence that while
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
production,
which proceed via internal emission,
are small compared to
\bar{K}^{0}
of
K^{-}
production,
which proceeds via the sum of the two mechanisms,
\phi
production,
which also proceeds via internal emission, has comparable strength to
K^{*}
production,
where both external and internal emission are at work but add destructively.
The reason for an unexpected factor
2
between
K^{-}
and
\bar{K}^{0}
production is traced back to the composite nature of the
X(3972)
from
the charged and neutral
X_{\rm c},X_{\rm n}
of Eq. (
1
) and the mass difference between
D^{*+}\,D^{-}
and
D^{*0}\bar{D}^{0}
, the same reason exposed in Ref.
ShenWang
.
Indeed, the production of the
X(3872)
requires the propagation of the
D^{*}\bar{D}
components, which is accomplished by means of the loop
G
functions.
The one of the charged components is displaced about
7\,\textrm{ MeV}
to higher energies,
due to the mass difference between the charged and neutral components,
and this has as a consequence that at the pole energy the ratio
|G_{\rm n}/G_{\rm c}|^{2}
is about
2.5
(see Fig.
5
).
This would be the ratio between
K^{-}
and
\bar{K}^{0}
production rates in the absence of internal emission.
The contribution of internal emission reduces this factor to about
2
.
This provides a natural explanation for this apparently strange ratio that naively one would expect to be of the order of unity.
The fact that the rates of vector production also appear fine with the same framework adds extra support to this picture.
In addition we have made predictions for
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
production in the pseudoscalar sector,
and
K^{*-}
in the vector sector.
Future measurements of these decays widths should provide extra support for this picture.

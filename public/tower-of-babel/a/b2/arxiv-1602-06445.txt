On error sums formed by rational approximations with split denominators
Thomas Baruchel, Carsten Elsner

Abstract
In this paper we consider error sums of the form ∑ m = 0 ∞ ε m ​ ( b m ​ α − a m c m ) , \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{m}\Big(\,b_{m}\alpha-\frac{a_{m}}{c_{m}}\,\Big)\,, where α \alpha is a real number, a m a_{m} , b m b_{m} , c m c_{m} are integers, and ε m = 1 \varepsilon_{m}=1 or ε m = ( − 1 ) m \varepsilon_{m}={(-1)}^{m} . In particular, we investigate such sums for α ∈ { π , e , e 1 / 2 , e 1 / 3 , … , log ⁡ ( 1 + t ) , ζ ⁡ ( 2 ) , ζ ⁡ ( 3 ) } \alpha\in\big\{\pi,e,e^{1/2},e^{1/3},\dots,\log(1+t),\zeta(2),\zeta(3)\big\} and exhibit some connections between rational coefficients occurring in error sums for Apéry’s continued fraction for ζ ⁡ ( 2 ) \zeta(2) and well-known integer sequences. The concept of the paper generalizes the theory of ordinary error sums, which are given by b m = q m b_{m}=q_{m} and a m / c m = p m a_{m}/c_{m}=p_{m} with the convergents p m / q m p_{m}/q_{m} from the continued fraction expansion of α \alpha .

Introduction

Let
\alpha
be a real number. We assume that there is a sequence
B:={(b_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
of integers, a sequence

R:={(r_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
of rationals
r_{n}=a_{n}/c_{n}
, say, with
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
c_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and a real number
\omega>1

satisfying

\big|b_{n}c_{n}\alpha-a_{n}\big|\,\ll\,\frac{c_{n}}{\omega^{n}}\qquad(n\geq 0)\,.

(1.1)

This is equivalent with

\big|b_{n}\alpha-r_{n}\big|\,=\,\Big|b_{n}\alpha-\frac{a_{n}}{c_{n}}\Big|\,\ll\,\frac{1}{\omega^{n}}\qquad(n\geq 0)\,.

(1.2)

We consider the fraction
a_{n}/b_{n}c_{n}
as a rational approximation of
\alpha
with split denominator
b_{n}c_{n}
.
Since
\omega>1
, the error sums

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}\big(B,R,\alpha\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\big(b_{m}\alpha-r_{m}\big)\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big(b_{m}\alpha-\frac{a_{m}}{c_{m}}\Big)\,,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big(B,R,\alpha\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\big|b_{m}\alpha-r_{m}\big|\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big|b_{m}\alpha-\frac{a_{m}}{c_{m}}\Big|

(1.4)

exist. Let
{(p_{n}/q_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of convergents of
\alpha
defined by
p_{n}/q_{n}=\langle a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\dots a_{n}\rangle
from the regular continued fraction expansion

\alpha\,=\,\langle a_{0};a_{1},a_{2},\dots\rangle\,=\,a_{0}+\cfrac{1}{a_{1}+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}

of
\alpha
. The error sums of
\alpha
for
B={(q_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
and
R={(p_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
, namely

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}\big(B,R,\alpha\big)\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\big(q_{m}\alpha-p_{m}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big(B,R,\alpha\big)\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\big|q_{m}\alpha-p_{m}\big|\,,

were already studied in some papers
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. We call
\mathcal{E}^{*}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{E}(\alpha)

ordinary error sums
.
Conversely, for
B={(1)}_{n\geq 0}
and
R={(p_{n}/q_{n})}_{n\geq 0}
, until now nobody has found any remarkable approach to the error sums

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}(B,R,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big(\alpha-\frac{p_{m}}{q_{m}}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(B,R,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big|\alpha-\frac{p_{m}}{q_{m}}\Big|\,.

In this paper we focus our interest on the series in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) in the case of particular values of
\alpha
and well-known
rational approximations of the form

0\,<\,\Big|b_{n}\alpha-\frac{a_{n}}{c_{n}}\Big|\,\ll\,\frac{1}{\omega^{n}}\qquad(n\geq 0)\,.

(1.5)

Among others we are going to study the numbers

\alpha\,\in\,\Big\{\,\pi,\,e^{1/l},\,\frac{\log\rho}{\sqrt{5}},\,\log(1+t),\,\zeta(2),\,\zeta(3)\,\Big\}\,,

where

2
Ordinary error sums for values of the exponential function

Ordinary error sums connected with the exponential function are studied in
[
1
,
10
]
. Here, our goal is to express this usual error sums itselves by a non-regular
continued fraction. For this purpose we express the error integral

\mbox{erf\,}(z)\,=\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt

by a hypergeometric series, which again can be transformed into a Gauss-type continued fraction.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
l\geq 2
be an integer, and let
p_{n}/q_{n}
denote the convergents of
e^{1/l}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(e^{1/l}\,)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\big|e^{1/l}q_{n}-p_{n}\big|\,=\,e^{1/l}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{l}}\mbox{erf\,}\Big(\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\,\Big)\,=\,\frac{2e^{1/l}}{\sqrt{l}}\int_{0}^{1/\sqrt{l}}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{1/l}{1/2-\cfrac{1/2l}{3/2+\cfrac{2/2l}{5/2-\cfrac{3/2l}{7/2+\cfrac{4/2l}{9/2-\cfrac{5/2l}{11/2+\ddots\cfrac{{(-1)}^{m}m/2l}{(2m+1)/2+\ddots}}}}}}}\qquad(m\geq 1)\,.

Proof:
The first identity of the theorem expressing
\mathcal{E}(e^{1/l})
by an error integral is already known from

[
1
,
10
]
. In order to prove the continued fraction expansion, we set

f(z)\,:=\,\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}ze^{z^{2}}\mbox{erf\,}(z)\,=\,ze^{z^{2}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt\,.

We express
f(z)
in terms of a hypergeometric function
{}_{1}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;z^{2})
.

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ze^{z^{2}}\int_{0}^{z}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}t^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\,dt\,=\,ze^{z^{2}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}z^{2\nu+1}}{(2\nu+1)\nu!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{2}\left(\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2\mu}}{\mu!}\right)\left(\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}z^{2\nu}}{(2\nu+1)\nu!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{2}\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}z^{2(\nu+\mu)}}{(2\nu+1)\nu!\mu!}\,=\,z^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\mathop{\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}}_{\mu+\nu=k}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}}{(2\nu+1)\nu!\mu!}\Big)z^{2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}}{(2\nu+1)\nu!(k-\nu)!}\Big)z^{2k}\,=\,z^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}{k\choose\nu}}{2\nu+1}\Big)z^{2k}\,.

From
[
15
, p. 68]
, Remark 8.5, we have the following formula (with
k
replaced by
\nu
and
n
replaced by
k
)

\frac{1}{d^{k}{[k]}_{k}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}{k\choose\nu}}{c+\nu d}\,=\,\frac{1}{c(c+d)(c+2d)\cdots(c+kd)}\,,

where
{[k]}_{k}=k!
. Setting
c=1
and
d=2
, it follows that

\frac{1}{k!}\,\sum_{\nu=0}^{k}\frac{{(-1)}^{\nu}{k\choose\nu}}{2\nu+1}\,=\,\frac{2^{k}}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2k+1)}\,=\,\frac{1}{{(3/2)}_{k}}\,.

This gives

f(z)\,=\,z^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k}}{{(3/2)}_{k}}\,=\,z^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(1)}_{k}}{k!{(3/2)}_{k}}z^{2k}\,=\,z^{2}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\big(1,3/2;z^{2}\big)\,.

The function
{}_{1}F_{1}\big(1,3/2;z^{2}\big)
can be expressed by a Gauss-type continued fraction. Using formula (8) on page 123
in
[
16
]
with
\gamma=3/2
and
x=z^{2}
, we have

{}_{1}F_{1}\big(1,3/2;z^{2}\big)\,=\,\cfrac{1/2}{1/2-\cfrac{z^{2}/2}{3/2+\cfrac{2z^{2}/2}{5/2-\cfrac{3z^{2}/2}{7/2+\cfrac{4z^{2}/2}{9/2-\cfrac{5z^{2}/2}{11/2+\ddots\cfrac{{(-1)}^{m}mz^{2}/2}{(2m+1)/2+\ddots}}}}}}}\qquad(m\geq 1)

Hence the continued fraction expansion given by the theorem follows from

\sum_{n\geq 0}\big|e^{1/l}q_{n}-p_{n}\big|\,=\,2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\cdot\frac{e^{1/l}}{\sqrt{l}}\cdot\mbox{erf\,}\big(1/\sqrt{l}\,\big)\,=\,2f\big(1/\sqrt{l}\,\big)\,=\,\frac{2}{l}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\big(1,3/2;1/l\big)\,.

∎

We point out the particular case
z=1
.

Corollary 2.1
.

We have

\displaystyle{}_{1}F_{1}\big(1,3/2;1\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\int_{0}^{1}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{MC}(e)\,=\,e-2+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\sum_{b=1}^{a_{n}}\big|(bq_{n-1}+q_{n-2})e-(bp_{n-1}+p_{n-2})\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{1/2}{1/2-\cfrac{1/2}{3/2+\cfrac{1}{5/2-\cfrac{3/2}{7/2+\cfrac{2}{9/2-\cfrac{5/2}{11/2+\ddots\cfrac{{(-1)}^{m}m/2}{(2m+1)/2+\ddots}}}}}}}\qquad(m\geq 1)\,,

where

(2.1)

Proof:
The formula

follows by (

and the formulas

\displaystyle q_{3m-1}e-p_{3m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{m+1}{(x-1)}^{m}}{m!}e^{x}\,dx\,,

\displaystyle q_{3m}e-p_{3m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{m}{(x-1)}^{m+1}}{m!}e^{x}\,dx\,,

\displaystyle q_{3m+1}e-p_{3m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{m+1}{(x-1)}^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}e^{x}\,dx

due to H. Cohn
[
5
]
. ∎

Let

. Then we know from

\displaystyle B_{n}\log(1+t)-A_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}{n+k\choose k}t^{n-k}\sum_{m=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{m-1}t^{m}}{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{(-1)}^{m}t^{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)}^{k}{n\choose k}{n+k\choose k}\frac{t^{n-k}t^{k}}{m+k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2n+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{n}{(1-x)}^{n}}{{(1+tx)}^{n+1}}\,dx\,.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{1+t(1-t)x+t^{2}x^{2}}

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

4.)
As mentionned at the beginning of Section
5
, some connections between rational coefficients involved in computing the error sums for Apéry’s continued fraction and well-known integer sequences may be noticed. Furthermore some unproved identities have been empirically found for such coefficients.

The Theorems
5.4
and
5.7
rely on two triangles of integer coefficients, namely
a_{\nu,\mu}
for the former and
b_{\nu,\mu}
for the latter. Both can be expressed by binomial sums as follows.

\begin{array}[]{lcl}a_{\nu,\mu}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(-1\right)^{\nu+k}{{\mu-k}\choose{2\mu-\nu-k-i}}{{\mu-k}\choose i}{{\mu-i}\choose{k-i}}\,,\\[20.0pt]

which both lead to non-recurrent formulas for the error sums as quadruple sums.

Several basic properties concerning the coefficients
a_{\nu,\mu}
and
b_{\nu,\mu}
can be noticed, including

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{2\nu}a_{\mu,\nu}=1\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{2\nu}b_{\mu,\nu}=1\qquad\qquad\left(\nu\in\mathbb{N}\right)

and

a_{\nu,\mu}=a_{\mu,\nu}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad b_{\nu,\mu}=b_{\mu,\nu}\,.

More unproved identities come from the theory of generating functions. Both coefficients
a_{\nu,\mu}
and
b_{\nu,\mu}
seem to be the coefficients of degree
2\nu-\mu
in the MacLaurin series expansion of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l}\displaystyle\frac{\left(\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}+1-\sqrt{x^{4}-4x^{3}+2x^{2}+1}}{2x^{3}}\right)^{\mu-\nu}}{\sqrt{x^{4}-4x^{3}+2x^{2}+1}}&\textrm{for $a_{\nu,\mu}$}\\[20.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\left(\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}+2x-1+\sqrt{x^{4}+2x^{2}-4x+1}}{2x^{3}}\right)^{\mu-\nu}}{\sqrt{x^{4}+2x^{2}-4x+1}}&\textrm{for $b_{\nu,\mu}$}\end{array}\right.\quad.

a_{\nu,\mu}
and

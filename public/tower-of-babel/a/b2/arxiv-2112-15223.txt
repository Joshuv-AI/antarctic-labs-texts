Abstract
. We consider partial theta series associated with periodic sequences of coefficients, of the form Θ ⁡ ( τ ) := ∑ n > 0 n ν ​ f ​ ( n ) ​ e i ​ π ​ n 2 ​ τ / M \Theta(\tau):=\sum_{n>0}n^{\nu}f(n)\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi n^{2}\tau/M} , with ν ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 \nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} and an M M -periodic function f : ℤ → ℂ f:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C} . Such a function is analytic in the half-plane { ℑ ⁡ m τ > 0 } \{\mathop{\Im m}\nolimits\tau>0\} and as τ \tau tends non-tangentially to any α ∈ ℚ \alpha\in\mathbb{Q} , a formal power series appears in the asymptotic behaviour of Θ ⁡ ( τ ) \Theta(\tau) , depending on the parity of ν \nu and f f . We discuss the summability and resurgence properties of these series by means of explicit formulas for their formal Borel transforms, and the consequences for the modularity properties of Θ \Theta , or its “quantum modularity” properties in the sense of Zagier’s recent theory. The Discrete Fourier Transform of f f plays an unexpected role and leads to a number-theoretic analogue of Écalle’s “Bridge Equations”. The motto is: (quantum) modularity = = Stokes phenomenon + + Discrete Fourier Transform.

1.
From partial theta series to generating series

Suppose for the moment that
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of complex numbers and there
exists
K>0
such that
\lvert a_{n}\rvert\leq K^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi n^{2}\tau/M}\rvert=\mathrm{e}^{-\pi n^{2}(\mathop{\Im m}\nolimits\tau)/M}
, the
function
\Theta
defined by (
0.1
) is analytic on
\mathbb{H}
and exponentially small
as
\mathop{\Im m}\nolimits\tau\to+\infty
.

Theorem 1
.

Take any
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
large enough so that the sum of the generating
series

(1.1)

F(t)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{-nt}

is bounded on
\{\mathop{\Re e}\nolimits t\geq c\}
and analytic in a neighbourhood of this half-plane.
Then the function

(1.2)

\hat{\phi}(\xi)\coloneqq\pi^{-1/2}\xi^{-1/2}F(C\,\xi^{1/2})\qquad\text{with}\kern 5.0ptC\coloneqq\big(\tfrac{4\pi}{M}\big)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4},

where the branch of
\xi^{1/2}
is specified by

-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}<\arg\xi<{\frac{\pi}{2}}\;\Rightarrow\;-\frac{3\pi}{4}<\arg(\xi^{1/2})<\frac{\pi}{4}
,
is bounded on
\mathcal{P}_{c}\coloneqq\{\,\mathop{\Re e}\nolimits(C\,\xi^{1/2})\geq c\,\}

and analytic in a neighbourhood of this set, and we have

(1.3)

\Theta(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{-1/2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{P}_{c}}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\hat{\phi}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi\quad\text{for all}\kern 5.0pt\tau\in\mathbb{H},

where the parabola
\partial\mathcal{P}_{c}
is oriented anticlockwise—see
Figure
1
—and we choose the branch of the square root
so that
0<\arg(\tau^{1/2})<{\frac{\pi}{2}}
.

Figure 1
.
The oriented parabola
\partial\mathcal{P}_{c}
and the path
{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}
in
the
\xi
-plane.

Notice that when
\xi
is on the parabola
\partial\mathcal{P}_{c}
and

\lvert\xi\rvert\to\infty
,
\arg\xi
tends to
-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}
or
{\frac{\pi}{2}}
so that

\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\rvert
decays exponentially as soon as
0<\arg\tau<\pi
.
If
0<{\varepsilon}<{\frac{\pi}{2}}
then, for
\arg\tau\in({\varepsilon},\pi-{\varepsilon})
, the Cauchy
theorem allows us to replace the integration contour
\partial\mathcal{P}_{c}
with a
contour
{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{P}_{c}
beginning like a half-line of
direction
-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}
and finishing like a half-line of direction

{\frac{\pi}{2}}-{\varepsilon}
, as on Figure
1
:

(1.4)

{\varepsilon}<\arg\tau<\pi-{\varepsilon}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\Theta(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau^{-1/2}\int_{{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\hat{\phi}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi.

Theorem
1
is implicit in
[
12
]
and
[
1
]
. A
direct proof is obtained from the identity

(1.5)

\tau^{1/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{{\sigma}^{2}\tau}=\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{-1/2}\Big(-\int_{0}^{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}(-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon})}\infty}+\int_{0}^{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}({\frac{\pi}{2}}-{\varepsilon})}\infty}\Big)\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\,\xi^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2{\sigma}\xi^{1/2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi

(for any
{\sigma}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
), which follows from Borel-Laplace
summation of the
convergent
Puiseux expansion

\sum\frac{{\sigma}^{2p}}{p!}\tau^{p+\frac{1}{2}}

of the left-hand side at
0

(indeed: the Borel transform of this series happens to be the odd part
of
\Psi(\xi^{1/2})\coloneqq\pi^{-1/2}\xi^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2{\sigma}\xi^{1/2}}
, we thus recover the
left-hand side by applying the Laplace transform (
3.8

\big[\Psi(\xi^{1/2})-\Psi(-\xi^{1/2})\big]/2

in any direction,
e.g.

{\frac{\pi}{2}}\pm{\varepsilon}

\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}}\big[\Psi(-\xi^{1/2})\big]=\mathscr{L}^{-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}}\big[\Psi(\xi^{1/2})\big]
).

2.
Partial theta series associated with periodic sequences

From now on, in (
0.1
) we consider sequences
(a_{n})
of the form

a_{n}=n^{\nu}\,f(n)
with

(2.1)

\nu\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\qquad M\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\qquad f\colon\thinspace\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}\kern 5.0pt\text{$M$-periodic function.}

For each
(\nu,f,M)
, the corresponding partial theta series is

(2.2)

\tau\in\mathbb{H}\mapsto\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{\nu}\,f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi n^{2}\tau/M}

and we are particularly interested in the local behaviour of this
analytic function as
\tau
approaches a point
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
of the
boundary of
\mathbb{H}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, we can write

(2.3)

\Theta(\alpha+\tau;\nu,f,M)=\Theta\big(\tfrac{M_{\alpha}}{M}\tau;\nu,f_{\!\frac{\alpha}{M}},M_{\alpha}\big)\quad\text{with notation}\kern 5.0ptf_{\!\beta}(n)\coloneqq f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi n^{2}\beta}\kern 5.0pt\text{for $\beta\in\mathbb{Q}$},

where
M_{\alpha}
is a period of
f_{\!\frac{\alpha}{M}}
,
for instance
\operatorname{lcm}\!\big(M,2\operatorname{denominator}(\alpha/M)\big)
. Therefore, the local study near
\tau=0
of

\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)

with arbitrary
M
and
f
will give us access to the local behaviour
of
\tau\mapsto\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
near any rational.

Instead of
\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
, we will often write

\Theta(\tau;\nu,f)
or
\Theta(\tau)
, omitting some of the parameters if they are
clear from the context.
The cases
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
will be the most important ones.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that,
for
\mu\geq 0
and
\nu=2\mu
or
2\mu+1
,

(2.4)

\Theta(\tau;2\mu,f,M)=\big(\tfrac{M}{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}\,\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\,}{{\mathrm{d}}\tau}\!\big)^{\!\mu}\,\Theta(\tau;0,f,M),\quad\Theta(\tau;2\mu+1,f,M)=\big(\tfrac{M}{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}\,\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\,}{{\mathrm{d}}\tau}\!\big)^{\!\mu}\,\Theta(\tau;1,f,M).

Consequently, even if we state some results with
\nu=0
or
\nu=1
as

\tau\to 0
only, they entail
results for arbitrary
\nu
with
\tau
approaching any

\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, simply by putting together (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).

A few examples

–

The classical Jacobi theta function appears as

{\theta}_{3}=1+2\Theta(\tau;0,1,1)
.

–

The even primitive quadratic Dirichlet character

f(n)=\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)
(Kronecker symbol), listed as

f=\hrefhttp://www.lmfdb.org/Character/Dirichlet/12/11

in
[
16
]
, gives rise to the Dedekind eta-function

\eta(\tau)=\Theta(\tau;0,f,12)
, and its Eichler integral

\tilde{\eta}(\tau)=\Theta(\tau;1,f,12)
has been investigated particularly
in
[
24
]
and
[
3
]
.

–

The Rogers-Ramanujan identity
[
23
]
involves

{\theta}_{5,j}(\tau)=\Theta(\tau;0,f_{5,j},20)
, where

f_{5,1}
and
f_{5,2}
respectively are the real and imaginary part of

\hrefhttp://www.lmfdb.org/Character/Dirichlet/20/7
,
an even primitive Dirichlet character
[
16
]
.

–

In Chern-Simons theory with gauge group
\operatorname{SU}(2)
or in the
context of Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants for the Poincaré
homology sphere
[
15
,
12
]
, there is a partition function
involving
\Theta(\tau;0,f_{+},60)
with

f_{+}\coloneqq\mathop{\Re e}\nolimits\big(\chi_{60}(23,\,\cdot\,)\big)
, where

\hrefhttp://www.lmfdb.org/Character/Dirichlet/60/23

is an odd primitive Dirichlet character
[
16
]
.

–

More generally, for any oriented Seifert fibered integral
homology sphere with
r\geq 3
exceptional fibers, Andersen and
Mistegård show in
[
1
]
that the “Gukov-Pei-Putrov-Vafa
invariant” is essentially a partial theta series as
in (
0.1
),
whose limit as
\tau\to 1/k
is related to the
level
k
Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant, at least for
k>0

even.
One can check that, in this case,
a_{n}
is a finite sum of odd sequences
of the form
n^{\nu}f^{[\nu]}(n)
, with periodic functions
f^{[\nu]}
of the same period
and
\nu
ranging from
0
to
r-3
.

3.
A resurgent divergent series that has three canonical sums

Let
\nu,M,f
be as in (
2.1
). With a view to applying
Theorem
1
, we observe that the
generating function has an analytic continuation as a rational function of
\mathrm{e}^{-t}
:

F(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{\nu}\,f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-nt}=\big(\!\!-\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\,}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\big)^{\!\nu}F_{0}(t),\kern 5.0ptF_{0}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-nt}=\frac{1}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-Mt}}\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq M}f(\ell)\,e^{-\ell t},

hence we can take
c>0
arbitrarily small
and
F(t)
extends from
\{\mathop{\Re e}\nolimits t\geq c\}
to a meromorphic function
on
\mathbb{C}
, regular on
\mathbb{C}-\frac{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{M}\mathbb{Z}
.
The mean value
\langle f\rangle=\frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}}f(\ell)

controls the regularity at
t=0
:
if
\langle f\rangle\neq 0
, then
0
is a simple pole for
F_{0}(t)
with
residue
\langle f\rangle
;
all other possible poles are of the form
t_{n}\coloneqq 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}n/M
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
.

To compute the function
\hat{\phi}(\xi)=\pi^{-1/2}\xi^{-1/2}F(C\,\xi^{1/2})
of (
1.2
),
we decompose
F
into

(3.1)

F(t)=\frac{\nu!\langle f\rangle}{t^{\nu+1}}+F^{+}(t)+F^{-}(t),\qquad F^{\pm}(t)\in\mathbb{C}\{t\},\qquad F^{+}\kern 5.0pt\text{even},\quad F^{-}\kern 5.0pt\text{odd}.

Defining two functions
\hat{\phi}^{+}
and
\hat{\phi}^{-}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle F^{+}(t)=\pi^{1/2}\,\hat{\phi}^{+}\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{C^{2}}\Big),\qquad F^{-}(t)=\pi^{1/2}\frac{t}{C}\,\hat{\phi}^{-}\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{C^{2}}\Big),

we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(\xi)=\frac{\nu!\langle f\rangle}{\pi^{1/2}C^{\nu+1}}\xi^{-\frac{\nu}{2}-1}+\xi^{-1/2}\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)+\hat{\phi}^{-}(\xi),\qquad\hat{\phi}^{\pm}(\xi)\in\mathbb{C}\{\xi\}.

Notice that
\hat{\phi}^{+}
and
\hat{\phi}^{-}
are meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
and their possible
poles are of the form

(3.4)

\xi_{n}\coloneqq\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{C^{2}}=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi n^{2}}{M}\in{\mathrm{i}}\mathbb{R}_{>0},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}.

The Laplace-like formula (
1.4
) thus gives rise to a sum
of three terms:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)

\displaystyle=\boxed{\,\tfrac{1}{2}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac{\nu+1}{2}\big)\langle f\rangle\big(\tfrac{\pi}{M}\!\cdot\!\tfrac{\tau}{{\mathrm{i}}}\big)\!^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}+\Theta^{+}(\tau;\nu,f,M)+\Theta^{-}(\tau;\nu,f,M)}

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\Theta^{+}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\tfrac{\tau^{-1/2}}{2}\int_{{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\,\xi^{-1/2}\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi,\quad\Theta^{-}\coloneqq\tfrac{\tau^{-1/2}}{2}\int_{{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\,\hat{\phi}^{-}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi,

where the first term in (
3.5
) has been obtained from the
known value of the Hankel-type
Laplace integral of
\xi^{-\frac{\nu}{2}-1}
, which is
2\pi{\mathrm{i}}^{\nu+1}\tau^{-\nu/2}/{\Gamma}(1+\nu/2)
, and the Legendre duplication formula.

Now, the key point is that the Cauchy theorem allows us to raise the integration
contour
{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}
until it touches
0
and to replace it with the two
half-lines
\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}({\frac{\pi}{2}}\pm{\varepsilon})}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
properly oriented,
taking into account the change of branch for
\xi^{-1/2}

from
\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}(-{\frac{3\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon})}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to
\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}({\frac{\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon})}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in the
case of
\Theta^{+}
,
whence

\bullet

​

\frac{\Theta^{+}}{\tau^{-1/2}}
is the average of the Laplace transforms
of
\xi^{-1/2}\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)
in the directions
{\frac{\pi}{2}}\pm{\varepsilon}
:

(3.6)

\Theta^{+}=\tau^{-1/2}\times\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}-{\varepsilon}}+\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}}\big)\big[\xi^{-1/2}\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)\big],

\bullet

​

\frac{\Theta^{-}}{\tau^{-1/2}}
is the difference of the Laplace transforms of
\frac{1}{2}\hat{\phi}^{-}(\xi)
in
the directions
{\frac{\pi}{2}}\pm{\varepsilon}
:

(3.7)

\Theta^{-}=\tau^{-1/2}\times\big(\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}-{\varepsilon}}-\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}}\big)\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\hat{\phi}^{-}\big],

with the notation

(3.8)

\mathscr{L}^{\theta}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}{\theta}}\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\hat{\varphi}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi

in the half-plane
\{\,\arg\tau\in({\theta}-{\tfrac{\pi}{2}},{\theta}+{\tfrac{\pi}{2}})\,\}
.

In the case of
\Theta^{-}(\tau)
, since
\hat{\phi}^{-}(\xi)
is regular at
0

and meromorphic, we can go on raising the integration contour as long
as we do not hit the first pole on
{\mathrm{i}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, which results in an
exponentially small contribution as
\tau\to 0
.

In the case of
\Theta^{+}(\tau)
, it is the Puiseux expansion at
0
of

(3.9)

\hat{\varphi}^{+}(\xi)\coloneqq\xi^{-1/2}\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)\in\xi^{-1/2}\mathbb{C}\{\xi\}

that governs the asymptotics:
the standard properties of the Laplace transform
\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}\pm{\varepsilon}}
give
the asymptotic behaviour as
\tau\to 0

non-tangentially,
i.e.
uniformly in any sector

{\varepsilon}<\arg\tau<\pi-{\varepsilon}
, which we denote by
\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}0
.
A few computations lead to formulas involving the analytic
continuation in
s
of the
L
-function

L(s,f)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}f(n)n^{-s}

for the Taylor coefficients of
\hat{\phi}^{+}(\xi)
(see
[
8
]
for the
details).
The upshot is

Theorem 2
.

\bullet

​

If the mean value
\langle f\rangle
is non-zero, then the dominant term in (
3.5
) is
the first one
and
\lvert\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)\rvert\text{\scalebox{.9}{$\xrightarrow[\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}0]{}$}}\infty
.

\bullet

​

The term
\Theta^{+}(\tau;\nu,f,M)
is the
median sum
in the
direction
{\frac{\pi}{2}}
of the formal series

(3.10)

\tilde{\Theta}(\tau;\nu,f,M)\coloneqq\sum_{p\geq 0}\frac{1}{p!}L(-2p-\nu,f)\Big(\frac{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{M}\Big)^{p}\tau^{p}.

The formal series
\tilde{\Theta}
is
resurgent
in
1/\tau
and its
Borel transform has multivalued analytic continuation in

\mathbb{C}-\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.
In particular
\Theta^{+}\sim_{1}\tilde{\Theta}
as
\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}0
.

\bullet

​

The term
\Theta^{-}(\tau;\nu,f,M)
is exponentially
small as
\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}0
: it is
O(\mathrm{e}^{-c\mathop{\Im m}\nolimits(-1/\tau)})
with any

0<c<\frac{\pi}{M}

(much more will be said on it in Section
5
).

A few words on the terminology.
The concept of resurgence
[
6
]
is based on the Borel transform

(3.11)

\mathscr{B}\colon\thinspace\tilde{\psi}(\tau)=\sum_{p\in\mathscr{N}}\!c_{p}\tau^{p}\mapsto\hat{\psi}(\xi)=\sum_{p\in\mathscr{N}}\!c_{p}\frac{\xi^{p-1}}{{\Gamma}(p)}\quad\text{for}\;\mathscr{N}\!=a+b\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\kern 5.0pt\text{with}\kern 5.0pta,b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

The series
\tilde{\psi}(\tau)
is said to be
resurgent
in
1/\tau

if its Borel transform

\hat{\psi}(\xi)
has positive radius of convergence and has “endless
analytic continuation”—see
[
6
]
, or Chapters 5 and 6 of
[
18
]
.
If
\lvert\hat{\psi}(\xi)\rvert
is bounded by an exponential along a
singularity-free sector
\arg\xi\in[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]
, then its Laplace
transforms
\mathscr{L}^{\theta}\hat{\psi}
in the directions

{\theta}\in[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]
are the analytic continuation of one another and can be glued into one function:

(3.12)

\mathscr{L}^{[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]}\hat{\psi}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}{\theta}}\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/\tau}\hat{\psi}(\xi)\,{\mathrm{d}}\xi,\quad{\theta}\in[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]\kern 5.0pt\text{such that}\;\arg\tau\in({\theta}-{\tfrac{\pi}{2}},{\theta}+{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}),

thus yielding the Borel sum
\mathscr{S}^{[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]}\tilde{\psi}\coloneqq\mathscr{L}^{[{\theta}_{1},{\theta}_{2}]}\mathscr{B}\tilde{\psi}
,
that is analytic in a sectorial neighbourhood of
\tau=0
with
arguments ranging from
{\theta}_{1}-{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}
to
{\theta}_{2}+{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}
, and has

1
-Gevrey asymptotic expansion
\tilde{\psi}(\tau)
in that domain—this
is the meaning of the symbol “
\sim_{1}
” in
Theorem
2
.

If there are singularities along
\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}{\theta}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}
but not in the sectors

\arg\xi\in[{\theta}-{\varepsilon},{\theta})
and
({\theta},{\theta}+{\varepsilon}]
, then one may resort to

median
summation
\mathscr{S}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\tilde{\psi}=\mathscr{L}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\mathscr{B}\tilde{\psi}
,
where
\mathscr{L}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}
is a variant of the Laplace operator introduced by
J. Écalle;
in general,
\mathscr{L}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\hat{\psi}
is not the mere arithmetic average

\frac{1}{2}(\mathscr{L}^{{\theta}-{\varepsilon}}+\mathscr{L}^{{\theta}+{\varepsilon}})\hat{\psi}
but something more
elaborate that involves, in a symmetric way, with well-chosen weights,
the various branches of the analytic continuation of
\hat{\psi}
along

\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}{\theta}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}-\{\text{singular points}\}
;
see §1.4 of
[
7
]
or
[
17
]
.
These weights are such that

\mathscr{S}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\big(\tilde{\psi}_{1}\tilde{\psi}_{2}\big)=\big(\mathscr{S}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\tilde{\psi}_{1}\big)\big(\mathscr{S}^{\theta}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\tilde{\psi}_{2}\big)

Discrete Fourier Transform

The DFT operator
U_{M}
is defined on the space of

functions

This is a unitary operator that respects parity:

\displaystyle\text{$f$ even}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\hat{f}=U_{M}f\kern 5.0pt\text{even},\kern 5.0ptU_{M}^{2}f=f,\kern 5.0ptU_{M}\big(\tfrac{f+\hat{f}}{2}\big)=\tfrac{f+\hat{f}}{2},\kern 5.0ptU_{M}\big(\tfrac{f-\hat{f}}{2}\big)=-\tfrac{f-\hat{f}}{2}

\displaystyle\text{$f$ odd}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\hat{f}=U_{M}f\kern 5.0pt\text{odd},\kern 5.0ptU_{M}^{2}f=-f,\kern 5.0ptU_{M}\big(\tfrac{f+{\mathrm{i}}\hat{f}}{2}\big)=-{\mathrm{i}}\tfrac{f+{\mathrm{i}}\hat{f}}{2},\kern 5.0ptU_{M}\big(\tfrac{f-{\mathrm{i}}\hat{f}}{2}\big)={\mathrm{i}}\tfrac{f-{\mathrm{i}}\hat{f}}{2}.

All eigenvectors are of one of the four previous forms, with eigenvalue

\pm 1
or
\pm{\mathrm{i}}
.

6.
Alien Derivatives and “Bridge Equation”

In this work, the two resurgent series

\tilde{\Theta}(\tau)=\hat{\psi}^{+}(0)+\mathscr{B}^{-1}\big[\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\psi}^{+}}{{\mathrm{d}}\xi\;\;}\big]\in\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]

and
\tau^{-1}\tilde{\psi}^{-}=\mathscr{B}^{-1}\big[\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\psi}^{-}}{{\mathrm{d}}\xi\;\;}\big]\in\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{C}[[\tau]]

have appeared in the decomposition (
3.5
) of
\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
:

\Theta^{+}=\mathscr{S}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\,\tilde{\Theta}=\hat{\psi}^{+}(0)+\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}}_{\text{\scalebox{.8}{{med}}}}\big[\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\psi}^{+}}{{\mathrm{d}}\xi\;\;}\big],\qquad\Theta^{-}=\big(\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}+{\varepsilon}}-\mathscr{L}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}-{\varepsilon}}\big)\big[\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\psi}^{-}}{{\mathrm{d}}\xi\;\;}\big]

(
cf.
Theorem
2
, (
3.14
)
and (
5.1
)).
This has led to two interesting Stokes phenomena, via singularity
computations in the Borel plane.

Écalle’s Resurgence Theory provides us with an efficient tool to
handle such singularity computations:
with each point
\omega
of the Riemann surface of the logarithm
is associated the so-called “alien derivation”
\Delta_{\omega}
.
This is an operator which maps a resurgent series
\tilde{\varphi}
to a resurgent
series whose Borel transform somehow measures the singularities of
certain branches of the analytic continuation of
\mathscr{B}\tilde{\varphi}
at
\omega
;
in particular,
\Delta_{\omega}\tilde{\varphi}=0
if all branches of
\mathscr{B}\tilde{\varphi}
are
regular at
\omega
, which is the case if
\tilde{\varphi}
is a convergent power
series.
The reader is referred to
[
6
,
7
,
18
]
for more details.
In the case of meromorphic singularities, the recipe is motivated
by (
5.5
) and can be illustrated with
\xi_{n}=\frac{\pi n^{2}}{M}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{2}}
for
n\geq 1
: the formulas

(6.1)

\displaystyle\nu=0\qquad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\varphi}^{+}

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}\hat{f}^{\operatorname{od}}(n)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\phi}^{-}

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}\hat{f}^{\operatorname{ev}}(n)

(6.2)

\displaystyle\nu=1\qquad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\varphi}^{+}

\displaystyle=2{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}n\hat{f}^{\operatorname{ev}}(n)\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\phi}^{-}

\displaystyle=2{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}n\hat{f}^{\operatorname{od}}(n)\tau^{-1}

must be understood as a mere rephrasing of (

) and (
5.4
).
For the two resurgent series of our problem, since

\tau^{-1}\tilde{\psi}^{-}=\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{-}/2

(6.3)

\displaystyle\nu=0\qquad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\Theta}

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}\hat{f}^{\operatorname{od}}(n)\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}(\tau^{-1}\tilde{\psi}^{-})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}\hat{f}^{\operatorname{ev}}(n)\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(6.4)

\displaystyle\nu=1\qquad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}\tilde{\Theta}

\displaystyle=2{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}n\hat{f}^{\operatorname{ev}}(n)\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi_{n}}(\tau^{-1}\tilde{\psi}^{-})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{3{\mathrm{i}}\pi}{4}}n\hat{f}^{\operatorname{od}}(n)\tau^{-\frac{3}{2}}

The Stokes phenomena that we have observed just reflect the action of the

{\Delta\hskip-6.49994pt\raisebox{1.20554pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}\hskip 2.5pt_{{\frac{\pi}{2}}}}=\sum\limits_{\arg\omega={\frac{\pi}{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega/\tau}\Delta_{\omega}.

Indeed, for

\displaystyle{\Delta\hskip-6.49994pt\raisebox{1.20554pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle/$}\hskip 2.5pt_{{\frac{\pi}{2}}}}\tilde{\Theta}(\tau;0,f)

The standard action of a general modular transformation

Parabolic Case

The case

(7.1)

is a period of

. We note that

(7.2)

\text{$M$ or $b$ even}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Theta(\tau+b;\nu,f,M)=\Theta\big(\tau;\nu,f_{\!\frac{b}{M}},M\big)

(because one can then take

).
Notice that if the support of

is such that

\text{$\exists n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that, $\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}$,}\kern 5.0ptf(n)\neq 0\;\Rightarrow\;n^{2}=n_{0}^{2}\;\;\textrm{mod}\,2M,

Notice also that, since
\Lambda_{M}^{2M}=1

(indeed:
\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)

-periodic in
\tau
).

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Theta(\tau;0,h,M)\mp\big(\tfrac{c\tau+d}{{\mathrm{i}}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Theta\big(\gamma(\tau);0,f,M\big)

(7.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\mathscr{S}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}\mp{\varepsilon}}\,\tilde{\Theta}(c\tau+d;0,\Lambda_{cM}^{-d}h,cM).

Notice that, in (
7.10
) and (
7.11
),
the right-hand side has analytic continuation through the real axis to the right
or to the left of
\gamma^{-1}({\mathrm{i}}\infty)=-d/c
(according to the
\pm

sign) by the standard properties of Borel-Laplace summation.

Imposing to
\gamma
to belong to a certain congruence subgroup and
making use of classical properties of quadratic Gauss sums, one
obtains a relation between
h
and
f
simple enough to allow us to interpret the left-hand side of (
7.10
) and (
7.11
) as a genuine
modularity obstruction, with appropriate automorphy factor.
We thus get classical modularity from (
7.8
)–(
7.9
) or quantum modularity
from (
7.10
)–(
7.11
) according to the
parity of
\nu
and
f
.

Specifically, if we assume
\gamma\in{\Gamma}_{0}(2M)\coloneqq\{\,c=0\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\,\}
,
then
g(r)=0
in (
7.7
) unless
r=0\;\textrm{mod}\,M
and

(7.12)

g(0)=(cM)^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}{\varepsilon}_{d}^{-1}\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)\quad\text{with}\quad{\varepsilon}_{d}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $d=1\;\textrm{mod}\,4$}\\
{\mathrm{i}}&\text{if $d=3\;\textrm{mod}\,4$}\end{cases}

and
\big(\tfrac{m}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)\coloneqq
Jacobi symbol (notice
that
d
is necessarily odd since
ad=1\;\textrm{mod}\,2M
),
whence

\gamma\in{\Gamma}_{0}(2M)\quad\Rightarrow\quad h(n)=\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,{\varepsilon}_{d}^{-1}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)\Lambda_{M}^{bd}(n)f(dn).

Since here we assume
M
even, it follows that

(7.13)

\gamma\in{\Gamma}(2M)\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)f,

where
{\Gamma}(2M)\coloneqq\{\,a=d=1\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\;\text{and}\;b=c=0\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\,\}
.

If we use the larger subgroup
{\Gamma}_{1}(2M)\coloneqq\{\,a=d=1\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\;\text{and}\;c=0\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\,\}

instead of
{\Gamma}(2M)
, then we still have a relation similar to one of
the results of
[
10
]
under the support assumption (
7.3
):

(7.14)

\gamma\in{\Gamma}_{1}(2M)\;\text{and Assumption~\eqref{assumptsupp} holds}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=\Lambda_{M}^{b}(n_{0})\,\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)f.

Another option available when
f
is a Dirichlet character modulo
M
is to use
{\Gamma}_{0}^{0}(2M)\coloneqq\{\,b=0\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\;\text{and}\;c=0\;\textrm{mod}\,2M\,\}
:

(7.15)

\gamma\in{\Gamma}_{0}^{0}(2M)\;\text{and $f$ Dirichlet character $\!\;\textrm{mod}\,M$}\quad\Rightarrow\quad h=\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,{\varepsilon}_{d}^{-1}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)f(d)f.

The relations (
7.8
)–(
7.11
) thus
give rise to explicit transformation formulas for the function

\Theta(\,\cdot\,;\nu,f,M)
under the action of the congruence
subgroup
{\Gamma}
defined by

(7.16)

{\Gamma}\coloneqq{\Gamma}(2M)\kern 5.0pt\textit{or, if Assumption~\eqref{assumptsupp} holds,}\;{\Gamma}\coloneqq{\Gamma}_{1}(2M)\\[4.30554pt]
\textit{or, if $f$ is a Dirichlet character $\!\;\textrm{mod}\,M$,}\;{\Gamma}\coloneqq{\Gamma}_{0}^{0}(2M).

The Boundary Function
\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}

Let us give more details on the cases

(7.17)

{
\nu=0
and
f
odd}

or

{
\nu=1
and
f
even}

(still with
M
even). Recall that the boundary function

\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}(\alpha)=\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}(\alpha;\nu,f,M)
is defined for
\alpha\in\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}
.

If
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in{\Gamma}
is parabolic, we have

\Theta\big(\gamma(\tau);\nu,f,M\big)=\lambda(\gamma)\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
with

\lambda(\gamma)\coloneqq 1
or
\lambda(\gamma)\coloneqq\Lambda_{M}^{b}(n_{0})
according as which
option holds in (
7.16
); in particular,
\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}
is stable under
\gamma
.

If
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in{\Gamma}
is non-parabolic, then we assume
c>0
and
(
7.13
)–(
7.15
) say that the function
h

of (
7.7
) is
h=\lambda(\gamma)f
with

\lambda(\gamma)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)
or

\lambda(\gamma)\coloneqq\Lambda_{M}^{b}(n_{0})\,\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)
or

\lambda(\gamma)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/4}\,{\varepsilon}_{d}^{-1}\,\big(\tfrac{2Mc}{\lvert d\rvert}\big)f(d)
.
The relations (
7.10
)–(
7.11
) thus
yield

(7.18)

\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)\mp\tfrac{1}{{\mathrm{i}}\lambda(\gamma)}\big(\tfrac{c\tau+d}{{\mathrm{i}}}\big)^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}}\Theta\big(\gamma(\tau);\nu,f,M\big)\\[2.15277pt]
=\tfrac{(cM)^{1/2}}{2\pi\lambda(\gamma)}f(0)\tfrac{{\mathrm{i}}}{c\tau+d}+\mathscr{S}^{{\frac{\pi}{2}}\mp{\varepsilon}}\,\tilde{\Theta}(c\tau+d;\nu,\Lambda_{cM}^{-d}f,cM).

Assuming
f(0)=0
and taking the limit as
\tau
tends
non-tangentially to
\gamma^{-1}({\mathrm{i}}\infty)
(in which case the second term
tends to
0
exponentially fast), or to a rational

\alpha>\gamma^{-1}({\mathrm{i}}\infty)
(in which case we use ‘
-
’), or to a rational

\alpha<\gamma^{-1}({\mathrm{i}}\infty)
(in which case we use ‘
+
’), we get

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
M
is even and
f(0)=0
.
If
f
is even then the domain of definition

\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}
of
\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}
is stable under
{\Gamma}
:
it is a union of cusps, which contains at least the cusp at infinity, and also
the cusp at
0
if
\langle f\rangle=0
.
If
f
is odd then
\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}=\mathbb{Q}
.
In all cases, the function
\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}\colon\thinspace\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}\to\mathbb{C}
is a quantum modular
form with respect to
{\Gamma}
, of weight
\frac{1}{2}
if
{
\nu=0
and
f
is odd
}
,
of weight
\frac{3}{2}
if
{
\nu=1
and
f
is even
}
.

Notice that, as a consequence,
\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}
is dense in
\mathbb{Q}
if

f(0)=0
. Indeed, each cusp of
{\Gamma}
is dense in
\mathbb{Q}

because
{\Gamma}
contains
{\Gamma}(2M)
, which is a normal subgroup of finite
index of
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

Remark 7.1
.

The number

\Theta^{\operatorname{nt}\!}(\alpha)
is just the limit value of

\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
as

\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}\alpha
, but we can also consider the whole asymptotic expansion
of (
7.18
) as
\tau\xrightarrow{\operatorname{n.t.\,}}\alpha
:
the formal series
\big(\tilde{\Theta}_{\alpha}\big)_{\alpha\in\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}}

introduced in Remark
3.2
make up
what is called a
strong quantum modular form
in
[
22
]
.

The case when Assumption (
7.3
) holds has already been
considered (with
M
even or odd) in
[
10
]
, and we get the same
congruence subgroup as them in that case.
A notable example where this assumption is fulfilled is,
following
[
11
]
or
[
1
]
, the partial theta series giving the Gukov-Pei-Putrov-Vafa
invariant for an oriented Seifert fibered integral
homology sphere with
3
exceptional fibers (a Brieskorn
3
-sphere).

Remark 7.2
.

For arbitrary
\nu\geq 2
, since Borel-Laplace (median) summation
commutes with
\tfrac{{\mathrm{d}}\,}{{\mathrm{d}}\tau}
, we can
use (
2.4
) and derive transformation formulas for
\Theta(\tau;\nu,f,M)
,
not as simple as when
\nu=0

or
1
, but rather pertaining to the theory of
higher depth quantum modular
forms

[
2
]
.

Remark 7.3
.

Examples with
f
even and
\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}=\mathbb{Q}
.

Suppose that
\nu=1
and
f
is even.
If Assumption (
7.3
) holds for
f
, then we get

f_{\frac{\alpha+1}{M}}=\Lambda_{M}(n_{0})f_{\frac{\alpha}{M}}
for every

\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, hence

(7.19)

\text{Assumption~\eqref{assumptsupp} holds}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{$\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}$ is invariant under
$T\colon\thinspace\tau\mapsto\tau+1$.}

Using (
5.16
), we also have

(7.20)

\text{$f$ eigenvector of~$U_{M}$ and $\langle f\rangle=0$}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{$\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}$ is invariant under
$S\colon\thinspace\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$.}

Since
\langle S,T\rangle=\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
acts transitively on
\mathbb{Q}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}
,
we deduce that
\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}=\mathbb{Q}
whenever
f
is a zero mean value even
eigenvector of
U_{M}
such that (
7.3
) holds
.
This is what happens with the Eichler integral
\tilde{\eta}(\tau)=\Theta\big(\tau;1,\chi_{12}(11,\,\cdot\,),12\big)
of the
Dedekind
\eta
function.

There are cases where
f
is not an eigenvector of
U_{M}
, but
U_{M}f

is a linear combination of
f
and
\tilde{f}
with
f

satisfying (
7.3
) with a certain
n_{0}
and
\tilde{f}

satisfying (
7.3
) with a certain
\tilde{n}_{0}
, so that

\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cap\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{U_{M}f,M}
is invariant under
T

by (
7.19
).
If moreover
\langle f\rangle=f(0)=0
, then (
5.16
) shows that

\big(\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}\big)\cap\big(\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{U_{M}f,M}\cup\{{\mathrm{i}}\infty\}\big)

is invariant under
S
, whence

\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{f,M}=\overline{\mathscr{Q}}_{U_{M}f,M}=\mathbb{Q}
in thoses cases.
This is what happens with the Eichler integrals
\Theta(\tau;1,f_{5,j},20)

of the two modular forms involved in the Rogers-Ramanujan identity.

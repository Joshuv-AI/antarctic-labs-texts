Extreme ATM skew in a local volatility model with discontinuity: joint density approach
Alexander Gairat, Vadim Shcherbakov

Abstract
This paper concerns a local volatility model in which volatility takes two possible values, and the specific value depends on whether the underlying price is above or below a given threshold value. The model is known, and a number of results have been obtained for it. In particular, option pricing formulas and a power law behaviour of the implied volatility skew have been established in the case when the threshold is taken at the money. In this paper we derive an alternative representation of option pricing formulas. In addition, we obtain an approximation of option prices by the corresponding Black-Scholes prices. Using this approximation streamlines obtaining the aforementioned behaviour of the skew. Our approach is based on the natural relationship of the model with Skew Brownian motion and consists of the systematic use of the joint distribution of this stochastic process and some of its functionals.



Keywords:

Local volatility model, Skew Brownian motion, implied volatility,

at the money skew

1
Introduction

This paper concerns a local volatility model (LVM),
in which volatility takes only two possible values.
If the underlying price is larger or equal to some threshold value
R
, then volatility is equal to
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
, and
if the underlying price is less than
R
, then volatility is equal to
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}
, where
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
and
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}
are given
positive constants.
In what follows we refer to this model as the two-valued LVM.
If
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}=\sigma
, then the model is just the classic log-normal model with constant volatility
\sigma
,
under which the celebrated Black-Scholes (BS) formula for the price of a European option has been obtained.

The two-valued LVM is well known, and a number of results for the model are available
(e.g. see Gairat and Shcherbakov
[
5
]
, Lipton
[
8
]
, Lipton and Sepp
[
9
]
, Pigato
[
10
]
and references therein).
Pricing formulas for European options
have been obtained in Gairat and Shcherbakov
[
5
]

for arbitrary spot
S_{0}

and strike
K
.
In Pigato
[
10
]
option pricing formulas have been
obtained in the case when
S_{0}=R
or
K=R
. The other results in
that paper concern the analysis of the implied volatility surface in the case when
S_{0}=R
.
In particular, it was shown that if strike
K
and
maturity
T
are related
by the equation
K=e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}
(“the central limit regime”),
then implied volatility
\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,K)=\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}})

converges to a smooth function
\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(\gamma)
of
\gamma
, as
T\to 0
,
and Taylor’s expansion of the second order for this limit function was explicitly computed.
Moreover, an exact formula for the at the money (ATM) implied volatility
skew was obtained.
These results were then used to show that the ATM skew
behaves like
T^{-1/2}
, as
T\to 0
.

The result concerning the short term behaviour of the ATM skew
is of particular interest for the following reasons.
On one hand, this reproduces the well-known power law behaviour of
the skew for short term maturities observed in some data (see Pigato
[
10
]
and references therein for more details). On the other hand,
in Guyon and El Amrani
[
4
]
the authors claim that
“our study suggests that the term-structure of equity ATM
skew has a power-law shape for maturities above
1
month but has a different behavior, and in particular may not blow up, for shorter maturities”.
This difference in results and opinions should be taken into account when using the two-valued LVM. In addition, note that
in Foreign Exchange option markets the standard practice is
to quote implied volatility in terms of delta moneyness

\frac{\log(K/F)}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}

(e.g. see Clark
[
1
, Chapter 3]
and references therein).
As a result, the skew asymptotically behaves as

T^{-1/2}
when the implied volatility is quoted in terms of the
moneyness
\log(K/F)
.

The research approach in Pigato
[
10
]
was based on
using the Laplace transform and related research techniques.
In the present paper we demonstrate another approach
to the study of the two-valued LVM.
This alternative method is based on the natural relationship
of the two-valued LVM with Skew Brownian motion (SBM). The latter is
a continuous time Markov process
obtained from standard Brownian motion (BM) by independently choosing with certain fixed probabilities the signs
of the excursions away from the origin.
If these probabilities are equal to
1/2
, then the process is standard BM.
It turns out that if the underlying price follows the two-valued LVM, then the natural logarithm of the price divided by volatility is a special case of SBM with a two-valued
drift (to be explained).
Our approach consists of using the joint distribution of this process and its
functionals, such as the local time at the origin, the last visit to the origin and the occupation time. The distribution was obtained in
Gairat and Shcherbakov
[
5
]
, where it was
applied to option valuation under the two-valued LVM.
Using this distribution allowed to streamline some computations
in a special case of SBM in Gairat and Shcherbakov
[
6
]
.
In the present paper, we give another example of the
application of the joint distribution.

First of all, we use the distribution
to obtain option pricing formulas in the case
S_{0}=R=1

(the assumption
R=1
is just a technical one, as the general case of
R

can be readily reduced to this one by dividing the underlying price by
R
).
As we mentioned above, the option pricing in the general case of the
initial underlying price was considered in
[
5
]

(see Section
3
below for details).
However, the case
S_{0}=R=1

was not explicitly mentioned in that paper.
Although pricing formulas in this case
can be obtained by passing to the limit
S_{0}\to 1

in more general formulas in
[
5
]
,
we derive them here directly by using the aforementioned joint distribution
(as we did in
[
5
]
in the general case). This allows us to once
again demonstrate the proposed method.
Besides, the corresponding computations are simplified in the case when the discontinuity threshold is taken at the money.
We also obtain a new representation of
the option prices in this case by combining the joint distribution with the well-known Dupire’s forward equation.
These new option pricing formulas are in the form of the convolution
of ATM prices with the density of the first passage time to zero of the standard Brownian motion, which is easy to interpret in probabilistic terms.
Furthermore, we use our distributional results
for obtaining the approximation of option prices in the two-valued LVM by the corresponding BS prices (BS approximation).
Briefly, the BS-approximation is as follows. Consider a European option with maturity
T
and strike
K\geq 1
.
Let
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)
be the option price under the two-valued LVM and let
\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},K,T)
be the BS price of
the same option, when volatility is equal to
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.
Then
|\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)-2p\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},K,T)|\leq cT
for all sufficiently small
T
, where
p=\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}
.
A similar approximation holds for prices of European put options.
The BS approximation was already briefly noted in
[
5
]
,
although it was not sufficiently appreciated there.
In the present paper we discuss the approximation in more detail
and apply it to obtain some key results concerning the short term
behaviour of implied volatility.
In addition, we obtain a new form of the approximation
and apply it to estimate implied volatility.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we formally define the two-valued LVM and state the key result concerning the aforementioned joint distribution.
Option pricing formulas and the BS approximation
are stated in Section
3
.
Section
4
concerns the analysis of the implied volatility surface.
Proofs of most of the results are given in Section
5
.
In Section
6
a modification of the BS approximation is discussed.

2
The model

Start with some notations.
Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathsf{P})

be a probability space on which all random variables under
consideration are defined.
The expectation with respect to the probability measure
\mathsf{P}
will be
denoted by
\mathsf{E}
.
Throughout
W_{t}=(W_{t},\,t\geq 0)
denotes standard Brownian motion (BM),
and
{\bf 1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function of a set or an event
A
.

Without loss of generality we assume throughout that the threshold value
R
of the underlying price, where the volatility changes its value, is
R=1
.

In the two-valued LVM that was briefly described in the introduction
the underlying price

S_{t}=(S_{t},\,t\geq 0)
follows the equation

dS_{t}=\sigma(\log S_{t})S_{t}dW_{t},

where the function
\sigma
is given by

\sigma(x)=\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}{\bf 1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}{\bf 1}_{\{x<0\}}

for some constants
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}>0
and
\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}>0
.

Further, consider the process
X_{t}=(X_{t},\,t\geq 0)
defined by

X_{t}=\begin{cases}\frac{\log S_{t}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}&\text{ for }S_{t}\geq 1,\\
\frac{\log S_{t}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}&\text{ for }S_{t}<1.\end{cases}

(1)

By
[
5
, Lemma 1]
), the process
X_{t}
follows the equation

dX_{t}=m(X_{t})dt+(p-q)dL_{t}+dW_{t},

(2)

where

\displaystyle m(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}{\bf 1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2}{\bf 1}_{\{x<0\}},

(3)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}},

(4)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=1-p=\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}},

(5)

and

L_{T}=L^{(X)}_{T}=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{T}{\bf 1}_{\{-\varepsilon\leq X_{u}\leq\varepsilon\}}du

(6)

is the local time of the process at the origin.

The process
X_{t}
is a special case of Skew Brownian motion (SBM) with a two-valued drift. Recall that SBM
(without drift) is
obtained from standard BM by independently choosing with certain probabilities the signs
of the excursions away from the origin.
An excursion is chosen to be positive with a fixed probability
p

and negative with probability
q=1-p
(if these probabilities are equal to
\frac{1}{2}
, then the process is standard BM).
The process
X_{t}
is SBM with probabilities
p
and
q
given by (
4
) and (
5
) respectively,
and the two-valued drift (
3
) (see Appendix
8
).

As we already mentioned in the introduction, a key ingredient
in our analysis of the two-valued LVM
is the use of the joint distribution of the process
X_{t}
and some of its functionals.
These functionals include the local time of the process and
the following quantities.
Namely, given
T>0
let

\displaystyle\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{t\in[0,T]:X_{t}=0\right\},

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=\max\{t\in(0,T]:X_{t}=0\},

(7)

be the first and the last visits to the origin respectively (on the interval
[0,T]
),
and let

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}\geq 0\}}dt

(8)

be the occupation time of the non-negative half line on the interval
[\tau_{0},\tau]
.

The distribution of interest is given in
Theorem
1
below.
Note that the theorem
is a special case of a more general result for SBM
obtained in
[
5
]
(see Theorem
7
in Appendix
8
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
X_{t}
be the process defined in (
1
).
Let
L_{T}
,
\tau
and
V

be quantities of this process defined by equations (
6
), (
7
)
and (
8
) respectively, and let
X_{0}=0
.
Then, the joint density of random variables

\tau,V,X_{T}
and
L_{T}
is given by

f_{T}(t,v,x,\ell)=2\alpha(x)h(v,\ell p)h(t-v,\ell q)h(T-t,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}v+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}(t-v)+\sigma^{2}(x)(T-t)}{8}-\frac{\sigma(x)}{2}x},

(9)

for
0\leq v\leq t\leq T
and
\ell\geq 0
, where

\alpha(x)=p{\bf 1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}+q{\bf 1}_{\{x<0\}},

(10)

probabilities
p
and
q
are defined in (
4
) and (
5
) respectively,
and

h(s,y)=\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{2\pi s^{3}}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2s}},\,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}},s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

is the density of the first passage time to zero of the standard BM starting at
y
.

Remark 1
.

Note that it is convenient to rewrite the density in terms of the variables

u=t-v
and
s=T-t
for
t\in[0,T]
.
It is easy to see that if
X_{0}=0
and
x\geq 0
, then
the variable
u
is the occupation time of the negative
half-line, and the variable
v+s
is the total occupation time of the positive half-line, and
if
X_{0}=0
and
x<0
, then the occupation time of the negative
half-line is
u+s
, and the total occupation time of the positive half-line is
v
.
In these terms we have that

\begin{split}f_{T}(t,v,x,\ell)&=\widetilde{f}_{T}(v,u,s,x,\ell)\\
&=2\alpha(x)h(v,\ell p)h(u,\ell q)h(s,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}v+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}u+\sigma^{2}(x)s}{2}-\frac{\sigma(x)}{2}x},\end{split}

(11)

for
(v,u,s):v,u,s\geq 0,\,v+u+s=T
and
\ell\geq 0
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X_{t}
be the process defined in (
1
).
Let
p(0,x,T)
be the probability density function of
X_{T}
given that
X_{0}=0
.
Then

\displaystyle p(0,x,T)

\displaystyle=2\alpha(x)e^{-\frac{\sigma(x)}{2}x}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(T-s)h(s,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}(x)}{8}s}ds

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2pe^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}x}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(T-s)h(s,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{8}s}ds&\text{ for }x\geq 0,\\
2qe^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2}x}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(T-s)h(s,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}}{8}s}ds&\text{ for }x<0,\\
\end{cases}

where

\begin{split}\phi(t)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})}\Big(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}}{8}t}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{8}t}\Big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})}\bigg(\mathcal{N}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2}\Big)-\mathcal{N}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}\Big)\bigg)\end{split}

(12)

and

\mathcal{N}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{z}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}dx

for
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem
2
is proved in Section
5
.

Remark 2
.

The function
\phi
defined in (
12
) has a natural probabilistic
sense.
Recall that the function
h(s,x)
is the density of the
first passage time to
0
of the standard BM starting at
x
. The distribution of the first passage
time converges, as
x\to 0
, to the distribution concentrated at
0
.
It follows from this fact and Theorem
2
that

\phi(T)=\frac{1}{2p}\lim\limits_{x\downarrow 0}p(0,x,T)=\frac{1}{2q}\lim\limits_{x\uparrow 0}p(0,x,T).

3
Option valuation

Let us briefly recall results of
[
5
]
concerning the option valuation under the two-valued LVM.
Pricing formulas were obtained in that paper for knock-in call options in the cases

R=1,S_{0}\geq 1,K>1
and
R=1,S_{0}<1,K>1
.
By combining these results with the Black-Scholes prices for knock-out call options
one can obtain prices of European call options for other values of
S_{0}
and
K
.
The pricing formula in the case
R=1,S_{0}\geq 1,K>1
in
[
5
]
is given in terms of a single integral,
where an integrand is analytically expressed in terms of the cumulative distribution function (cdf)
of the standard normal distribution
N(0,1
).
In the special case
R=S_{0}=1,K>1
one gets the price of the European option call option.
This case is considered in Theorem
3
below.
The pricing formula in the case
R=1,S_{0}<1,K>1
in
[
5
]
is also given in terms of a single integral
with an integrand analytically expressed in terms of the standard normal distribution and
a bivariate normal distribution.

3.1
Pricing formulas

Let

\psi(a,s,k)=\begin{cases}\int\limits_{k}^{\infty}e^{ax}h(s,x)dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}e^{ak-\frac{k^{2}}{2s}}+a\,e^{\frac{a^{2}}{2}s}\mathcal{N}\Big(\frac{as-k}{\sqrt{s}}\Big)&\text{ for }k\geq 0,\\
\int\limits_{-\infty}^{k}e^{ax}h(s,x)dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}e^{ak-\frac{k^{2}}{2s}}-a\,e^{\frac{a^{2}}{2}s}\mathcal{N}\Big(\frac{k-as}{\sqrt{s}}\Big)&\text{ for }k<0.\end{cases}

(13)

Note that the equation for the function
\psi
can be rewritten as follows

\psi(a,s,k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}e^{ak-\frac{k^{2}}{2s}}+a\,{\rm sgn}(k)e^{\frac{a^{2}}{2}s}\mathcal{N}\bigg({\rm sgn}(k)\frac{as-k}{\sqrt{s}}\bigg)\text{ for }k\in{\mathbb{R}},

where

{\rm sgn}(k)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }k\geq 0,\\
-1&\text{ for }k<0.\end{cases}

Define

F\left(T,a,k\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\phi(T-s)\psi(a,s,k)e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}s}ds,

(14)

where
\phi
is the function defined in (
12
).

Consider European options with strike
K
and time to expiry
T
.
Let
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(S_{0},K,T)
and
\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(S_{0},K,T)
be the price of the call option and
the put option respectively, when the initial value
of the underlying price is
S_{0}
.

Theorem 3
.

If
S_{0}=1
and
K>1
, then

\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)=2p\bigg(F\Big(T,\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2},k\Big)-e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}k}F\Big(T,-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2},k\Big)\bigg),

where
k=\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
.

If
S_{0}=1
and
K<1
, then

\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)=2q\bigg(e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}k}F\Big(T,-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2},k\Big)-F\Big(T,\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2},k\Big)\bigg),

where
k=\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}
.

By Theorem
3
we immediately get the following equation for
the ATM price

\begin{split}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)&=\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,1,T)=2p\bigg(F\Big(T,\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2},0\Big)-F\Big(T,-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2},0\Big)\bigg)\\
&=\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,1,T)=2q\bigg(F\Big(T,-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2},0\Big)-F\Big(T,\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2},0\Big)\bigg),\end{split}

(15)

which still involves the function
F
.
Corollary
1
below shows that the above equation for
the ATM price can be simplified in such a way that
the ATM price is analytically expressed in terms of the error function

{\rm Erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int\limits_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt.

Corollary 1

(
ATM price).

If
S_{0}=K=1
, then

\begin{split}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)&=\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{4(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2})}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{8T}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{8}T}-\frac{\sqrt{8T}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}}{8}T}\bigg.\\
&\bigg.+\Big(\frac{4}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}+T\Big){\rm Erf}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{8}}\Big)-\Big(\frac{4}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}}+T\Big){\rm Erf}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{8}}\Big)\bigg).\end{split}

Proofs of both Theorem
3
and Corollary
1
are given in Section
5
.

Remark 3
.

It should be noted that the option pricing formulas in Theorem
3

differ from those that are given in Pigato
[
10
]
. In both papers
the option price is given by a single integral, but the corresponding integrands
differ. It might be of interest to investigate the relationship between the two variants. At the same time, the ATM price in Corollary
1

is exactly the same as the one in
[
10
]
.
In addition, note that the ATM price can be rewritten as follows

\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)=\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{4(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2})}\big(I(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)-I(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},T)\big),

where

I(x,T)=\frac{\sqrt{8T}}{x\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{8}T}+\Big(\frac{4}{x^{2}}+T\Big){\rm Erf}\Big(\frac{x\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{8}}\Big).

3.2
Option prices and Dupire’s forward equation

In this section we provide (in Theorem
4
below) another
representation of option prices. This representation
gives the price of an in-the-money option in terms
of a weighted integral of the corresponding ATM price over the time
until maturity.
It is based on the well known
Dupire’s forward equation (Dupire
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
),
which we recall below.

If the underlying price follows the local volatility model

dS_{t}=\sigma(t,S_{t})S_{t}dW_{t},

then the price
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)
of a European call option with strike
K
and time to expiry

T
satisfies the equation (the forward equation)

\frac{\partial\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)}{\partial T}=\frac{1}{2}K^{2}\sigma^{2}(T,K)\frac{\partial^{2}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)}{\partial K^{2}}.

It follows from the forward equation that

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}\left(K,T\right)-\left(S_{0}-K\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}\left(K,t\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{K^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}(t,K)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial K^{2}}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}\left(K,t\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{K^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}(t,K)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial K^{2}}\mathsf{E}[\max(S_{t}-K,0)|S_{0}]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{K^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}(t,K)\mathsf{E}[\delta(S_{t}-K)|S_{0}]dt,

where
\delta(\cdot)
is the delta-function.
Noting that
\mathsf{E}[\delta(S_{t}-K)|S_{0}]=p_{S}(S_{0},K,t)
,
where
p_{S}(S_{0},\cdot,t)
, is the probability density function of
S_{t}
given

S_{0}
, we arrive to the equation

\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(K,T)-(S_{0}-K)_{+}=\frac{K^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sigma^{2}(t,K)p_{S}(S_{0},K,t)dt,

(16)

which we are going to use in the proof of Theorem
4
below.

Theorem 4
.

If
S_{0}=1
and
K>1
, then

\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)=\sqrt{K}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)h(s,\log(K)/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})e^{-\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}ds;

if
S_{0}=1
and
K<1
, then

\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)h(s,-\log(K)/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})e^{-\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}s}ds,

where in both cases
\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}
is the ATM price.

The proof of Theorem
4
is given in Section
5
.

3.3
Black-Scholes approximation

In this section we discuss the approximation of option prices in the two-valued LVM
model (i.e.
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)
and
\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)
) by the corresponding BS
prices.

Denote by

\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma,S_{0},K,T)
and
\mathsf{P}_{\rm BS}(\sigma,S_{0},K,T)
the BS prices of
European call option and European put option respectively with
strike
K
and time to maturity
T
,
when volatility of the underlying asset is equal to
\sigma
.

Theorem 5
.

If
K>1
, then

|\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)-2p\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},1,K,T)|\leq cT,

and if
K<1
, then

|\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)-2q\mathsf{P}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},1,K,T)|\leq cT

for some constant
c

4
Implied volatility

In this section we use the BS approximation to obtain
some results from
[
10
]
concerning implied volatility.
Recall that implied volatility

\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,k)
is considered
as a function of maturity
T
and log-moneyness
k=\log(K/S_{0})
.
Note that we have
k=\log K
in the case when
S_{0}=1
.

Start with a remark that is almost verbatim to Remark 3.4 in
[
10
]

concerning the ATM implied volatility
\sigma_{\rm atm}(T):=\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,0)
.
By definition,
\sigma_{\rm atm}(T)
is the solution of the equation

\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\rm atm}(T),1,1,T)=\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T).

Recall that given volatility
\sigma
we have that

\begin{split}\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma,T)&:=\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma,1,1,T)=\mathcal{N}\Big(\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\Big)-\mathcal{N}\Big(-\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}\Big)={\rm Erf}\Big(\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{8}}\Big).\end{split}

(17)

Therefore,

\sigma_{\rm atm}(T)=\frac{\sqrt{8}}{\sqrt{T}}{\rm Erf}^{-1}\big(\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)\big).

Further, using that

{\rm Erf}^{-1}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\Big(x+\frac{\pi}{12}x^{3}\Big)+o(x^{3}),\text{ as }x\to 0,

we obtain the short term expansion for the ATM implied volatility

\sigma_{\rm atm}\left(T\right)=2\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)}-\frac{\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)^{2}\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)^{2}}{12\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)^{3}}T+o(T),\text{ as }T\to 0.

In addition, note that

\sigma_{\rm atm}:=\sigma_{\rm atm}\left(0\right)=2\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)}=2p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}=2q\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}.

4.1
Implied volatility in the central limit regime

In this section we consider the short term behaviour
of implied volatility in the case when strike and maturity
are related by the equation
K=e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}
.
This case was considered in
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
,
where it was called the central limit regime (and we use the same terminology).

One of the results of the aforementioned theorem is the equation

\begin{split}\lim_{T\to 0}\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,\gamma\sqrt{T})&=\frac{2\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\gamma\\
&+\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2\left(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\right)}-{\rm sgn}(\gamma)\Big)\gamma^{2}+o(\gamma^{2}),\text{ as }\gamma\to 0.\end{split}

(18)

In other words, the implied volatility

\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(T,\gamma\sqrt{T})
can be
approximated for short term maturities
T
by a quadratic function of
\gamma
,
which can be computed explicitly. Below we compute this quadratic function
by using the BS approximation derived in Theorem
5
.

For definiteness assume that
\gamma>0
(i.e.
K=e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}>1
) and
use our result for prices of call options.
Let
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T)
be the call price in the two-valued LVM
(Theorem
3
).
The equation for the implied volatility
\sigma_{{\rm BS}}
is

\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}\big(\sigma_{{\rm BS}},1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T\big)=\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}\big(1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T\big),

(19)

where the left hand side is
the BS price of the call option with maturity
T
and the strike
K=e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}
.
Then

\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}\big(\sigma,1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T\big)=\mathcal{N}(d_{1})-e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}\mathcal{N}(d_{0}),

where

d_{1}=-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}+\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}
and
d_{0}=-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}-\frac{\sigma\sqrt{T}}{2}
.
By Taylor’s theorem we have that

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma,1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg)\sqrt{T}+o\big(\sqrt{T}\big),\text{ as }T\to 0.

(20)

Further, combining the BS approximation (Theorem
5
) for the right hand side of (
19
)
with (
20
) gives that

\begin{split}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}\big(1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T\big)&=2p\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}\big(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},1,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}},T\big)\\
&=2p\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg)\sqrt{T}+o\big(\sqrt{T}\big).\end{split}

(21)

Replacing both the left hand and the right hand sides of equation (
19
) by their approximations
(provided by equations (
20
) and (
21
) respectively) we obtain the equation

\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg)\sqrt{T}=2p\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg)\sqrt{T}+o\big(\sqrt{T}\big),

which means that under the assumptions made the implied volatility

\sigma_{{\rm BS}}(\gamma,e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}})

converges to a limit, as
T\to 0
, and, moreover, this limit
can be estimated by the solution of the equation

\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}=p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-p\gamma+\frac{p}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\gamma^{2}.

(22)

It is easy to see that equation (
22
) is basically
a quadratic equation for the unknown
\sigma
with coefficients analytically depending on
\gamma
.
This implies that the solution is
an analytic function
\sigma(\gamma)
of
\gamma
.
Consider Taylor’s expansion of the second order for
this function at
\gamma=0
, that is

\sigma(\gamma)=\bigg(c_{0}+c_{1}\gamma+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\gamma^{2}\bigg)+o\big(\gamma^{2}\big),\text{ as }\gamma\to 0,

where
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
denote the values at
0
of the function itself,
its 1st and 2nd derivatives respectively.
Using this expansion for approximating the left hand side of (
22
)
gives the equation

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}+c_{1}\gamma+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\gamma^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{(c_{0}+c_{1}\gamma+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\gamma^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\left(\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\gamma+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{c_{0}}\right)\gamma^{2}+o(\gamma^{2}),

which, in turn, implies that

\begin{split}\frac{c_{0}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\bigg(\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg)\gamma+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{c_{0}}\bigg)\gamma^{2}&=p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}-p\gamma+\frac{p}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\gamma^{2}+o\big(\gamma^{2}\big).\end{split}

(23)

Equating coefficients at
\gamma^{i},\,i=0,1,2
in (
23
)
we obtain that

c_{0}=2p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},c_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}(1-2p)\text{ and }c_{2}=\frac{4p^{2}-1}{p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}},

and, hence,

\begin{split}\sigma(\gamma)&=2p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}(1-2p)\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\frac{4p^{2}-1}{p\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\gamma^{2}+o(\gamma^{2})\\
&=\frac{2\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\gamma+\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})}-1\bigg)\gamma^{2}+o\big(\gamma^{2}\big).\end{split}

(24)

Alternatively, one can use the put price and repeat the above argument
in the case when
\gamma<0
, i.e.
K=e^{\gamma\sqrt{T}}<1
, to obtain that

\begin{split}\sigma(\gamma)&=2q\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}(2q-1)\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\frac{4q^{2}-1}{q\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\gamma^{2}+o\big(\gamma^{2}\big)\\
&=\frac{2\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}\gamma+\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{2(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})}+1\bigg)\gamma^{2}+o\big(\gamma^{2}\big).\end{split}

(25)

Finally, note that (
24
) and (
25
) are special cases of (
18
)
depending on the sign of
\gamma
.

Proposition 1

For any fixed

the following holds

F(T,a,0)=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{T}+\frac{1}{2}+o\big(\sqrt{T}\big),\text{ as }T\to 0.

Observe that

\displaystyle\psi(a,t,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}+\frac{a}{2}+O\big(\sqrt{t}\big),\text{ as }t\to 0,

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}+\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{8\sqrt{2\pi}}-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}}{4}\bigg)\sqrt{t}+o\big(\sqrt{t}\big),\text{ as }t\to 0.

\displaystyle F\left(T,a,0\right)

The proof of the proposition is finished.
∎

\displaystyle=\sqrt{K}\int\limits_{0}^{T}h\big(s,\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\big)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{8}s}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)ds\text{ for }K>1,

where
\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)
is the ATM price (Theorem
1
) for maturity
T-s
.

5.5
Proof of Theorem
5

We obtain the BS approximation only for the call option price
\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)
, as the case of the put option is similar.

Using equation (
35
) for the density of
X_{T}

gives that

\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)=2p\int\limits_{k}^{\infty}\Big(e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}x}-e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}k}\Big)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}x}A(x,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})dx,

where

\displaystyle A(x,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{0}^{T-s}h(v,\ell p)h(T-v-s,\ell q)h(s,x)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}v+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}(T-v-s)+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}{8}}dvdsd\ell.

For the BS price of the same call option in the case when volatility is equal to

\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}
we have that

\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},1,K,T)=\int\limits_{k}^{\infty}\Big(e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}x}-e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}k}\Big)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}x}A(x,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})dx

Denote

and observe that

\displaystyle\Big|e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}v+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}u+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}{8}}-e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}T}{8}}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|1-e^{-\frac{1}{8}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2})u}\Big|e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}T}{8}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big|e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}v+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}u+\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}{8}}-e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}T}{8}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-}^{2}-\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}|T}{8}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}}{8}T}+o(T),\text{ as }T\to 0.

This gives that

\bigg|\int\limits_{k}^{\infty}\Big(e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}x}-e^{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}k}\Big)e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}}{2}x}\big(A(x,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-})-A(x,\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})\big)dx\bigg|\leq C_{1}T

6
BS approximation revisited

In this section we revisit the BS approximation. In particular,
we obtain another form of the approximation. The new approximation
corrects a certain drawback of the original one (to be explained).
Then we apply the modified BS approximation for estimation
of implied volatility in the two-valued LVM.

Start with noting that

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)

\displaystyle\approx 2p\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},1,K,T)\text{ for }K>1

(37)

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)

\displaystyle\approx 2q\mathsf{P}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},1,K,T)\text{ for }K<1.

(38)

In the ATM case
K=1
the equation for the call gives
that
\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)\approx 2p\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)
, while the equation for
the put gives that
\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)\approx 2q\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},T)
.
Below we obtain another form of the BS approximation in which this discrepancy is eliminated.

Using Theorem
4

we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)&=\sqrt{K}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)h(s,\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})e^{-\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}ds\\
&=\sqrt{K}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T-s)}\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T-s)h\big(s,\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\big)e^{-\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}ds,\end{split}

where
\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},\cdot)
is the BS ATM price (defined in (
17
)).
Approximating the time dependent ratio
\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T-s)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T-s)}

by
\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)}
, i.e.
by its value at a single time moment
T
, gives
the new BS approximation for the price of the call option

\begin{split}\mathsf{C}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)&\approx\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)\sqrt{K}}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T-s)h(s,\log K/\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+})e^{-\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}^{2}s}ds\\
&=\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)}\mathsf{C}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},1,K,T)\text{ for }K>1.\end{split}

(39)

Similarly, we obtain the new approximation for the price of the put option, namely

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\rm lvm}(1,K,T)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},T)}\mathsf{P}_{\rm BS}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle-},1,K,T)\text{ for }K<1.

In addition, note that the new BS approximation implies the approximation given by (
37
) and (
38
). For example,
in the case of the call option we have that

\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)\approx\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)\approx\frac{\sigma_{\rm atm}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=2p\frac{\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+}\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},

so that
\frac{\mathsf{V}_{\rm atm}(T)}{\mathsf{BS}_{\rm atm}(\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle+},T)}\approx 2p,
which
gives (
37
), as claimed.

7
Conclusion

In this paper we consider the LVM in which volatility
takes two possible values.
A particular value depends on the position of the underlying price with respect to a given threshold.
The model is well known, and a number of results have been obtained for the model in recent years. In particular, explicit pricing formulas for European options
have been obtained in Pigato
[
10
]
in the case when the threshold
is taken at the money. These formulas have then been used to establish
that the skew explodes as
T^{-1/2}
, as maturity
T\to 0
,
which reproduces the power law behaviour of the skew observed in some
real data. The research method in Pigato
[
10
]
is based on the Laplace transform and related techniques.

In the present paper we propose another approach to the study
of the two-valued LVM.
Our approach is based on the natural relationship of the two-valued
LVM with SBM and consists of using the joint
distribution of SBM and some of its functionals (
[
5
]
).
We use our distributional results for
obtaining new option pricing formulas and
approximation of option prices in terms of the corresponding BS prices.
The BS approximation is a key
ingredient of our analysis of implied volatility and the skew.
Using this approximation allows to obtain the aforementioned behaviour
of the implied volatility surface by rather
elementary methods (e.g. Taylor’s expansion of the second order).
In addition, we show that the BS approximation can be improved
and used to estimate implied volatility.

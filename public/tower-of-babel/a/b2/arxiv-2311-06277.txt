1.
Introduction and definitions

For a function
\omega
, analytic in the open unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|<1\}
and of the form

(1)

\omega(z)=c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+c_{3}z^{3}+\cdots,\qquad(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots\in{\mathcal{C}})

we say that is Schwartz function if
|\omega(z)|<1
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{0}
the class of all such functions.

In his paper
[
4
]
, Zaprawa gave many different inequalities for the coefficients
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots
for the functions of the class
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

In this paper we study the class of functions
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
of type (
1
) such that
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Since

(2)

z\omega^{\prime}(z)=c_{1}z+2c_{2}z^{2}+3c_{3}z^{3}\cdots,

and
|z\omega^{\prime}(z)|=|z|\cdot|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq|z|<1
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, it means that
z\omega^{\prime}(z)
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{0}
. Also, since
\omega(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\omega^{\prime}(z)dz
, then
|\omega(z)|\leq\int_{0}^{|z|}|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\,d|z|\leq|z|<1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, i.e.,
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
. So,
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, is a sufficient condition for
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is subclass of the class
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

For the functions from
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
we try to find properties for the coefficients
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\ldots
that correspond to the properties for the functions from
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

For our considerations we will need the next lemma originating from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
is given by (
1
). Then

(3)

\begin{array}[]{c}|c_{1}|\leq 1,\qquad|c_{2}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2},\\[11.38109pt]
|c_{3}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\frac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|},\\[11.38109pt]
|c_{4}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2}.\end{array}

We showed that when
\omega
given by (
1
) is in
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, then
z\omega^{\prime}(z)
is in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
. Thus, Lemma
1
, together with (
2
), directly brings

Lemma 2
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is given by (
1
). Then

(4)

\begin{split}|c_{1}|&\leq 1,\qquad|c_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}\right),\\[11.38109pt]
|c_{3}|&\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\frac{4|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right),\\[11.38109pt]
|c_{4}|&\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-4|c_{2}|^{2}\right).\end{split}

2.
Main results

We begin with partly sharp estimate of the modulus
|c_{3}-c_{1}c_{2}|
for functions from
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
with expansion (
1
).

Theorem 1
.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
), then

(5)

|c_{3}-c_{1}c_{2}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{48}(1+|c_{1}|)\left[9|c_{1}|^{2}-16|c_{1}|+16\right],&0\leq|c_{1}|\leq\frac{4}{7}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{5}{6}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}),&\frac{4}{7}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1\end{array}\right..

The estimate is sharp for
|c_{1}|=0
and for
\frac{4}{7}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
.

Proof.

For
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
and
\omega
given by (
1
) we apply the inequalities (
4
):

\begin{split}|c_{3}-c_{1}c_{2}|&\leq|c_{3}|+|c_{1}||c_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\frac{4|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)+|c_{2}||c_{2}|\\
&=-\frac{4}{3(1+|c_{1}|)}|c_{2}|^{2}+|c_{1}||c_{2}|+\frac{1}{3}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}).\end{split}

If we consider the last expression as a function of
|c_{2}|
,
0\leq|c_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})
, then we easily obtain the estimate given by (
5
), depending on its maximum which in the case
0\leq|c_{1}|\leq\frac{4}{7}
is attained for
|c_{2}|=\frac{3}{8}|c_{1}|(1+|c_{1}|)
lying in the interval
\left(0,\frac{1}{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})\right)
, and in the case
\frac{4}{7}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
is attained for
|c_{2}|=\frac{1}{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})
.

For
|c_{1}|=0
and for
\frac{4}{7}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
the result is sharp with extremal functions
\omega_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{3}z^{3}
and

\omega_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{|c_{1}|+z}{1+|c_{1}|z}\,dz=|c_{1}|z+\frac{1}{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{2}-\frac{1}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{3}+\cdots,

respectively.
∎

Remark 1
.

Theorem
1
brings:

\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad|c_{3}-c_{1}c_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{3},

while

\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad|c_{3}-c_{1}c_{2}|\leq 1

follows from
[
4
]
.

Similarly as Theorem
1
we can prove the next theorem.

Theorem 2
.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
) and
\mu\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then

(6)

|c_{3}-\mu c_{1}c_{2}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{48}(1+|c_{1}|)\left[9|\mu|^{2}|c_{1}|^{2}-16|c_{1}|+16\right],&0\leq|c_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{1+3/4|\mu|}\\[5.69054pt]
\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{2}|\mu|\right)|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}),&\frac{1}{1+3/4|\mu|}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1\end{array}\right..

The estimate is sharp for
|c_{1}|=0
, and for
\frac{1}{1+3/4|\mu|}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
when
\mu
is nonnegative real number. The extremal functions are
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, respectively (
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
as defined in the proof of Theorem
1
).

For
\mu=2
in Theorem
2
we receive

Corollary 1
.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
). Then

|c_{3}-2c_{1}c_{2}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{12}(1+|c_{1}|)\left[9|c_{1}|^{2}-4|c_{1}|+4\right],&0\leq|c_{1}|\leq\frac{2}{5}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{4}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}),&\frac{2}{5}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1\end{array}\right..

The estimate is sharp for
|c_{1}|=0
and for
\frac{2}{5}\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
, with extremal functions
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, respectively (
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
as defined in the proof of Theorem
1
).

Next, for the modulus
|c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}^{2}|
we have the following sharp estimate.

Theorem 3
.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
), then the following estimate is sharp

(7)

|c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{42}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})(3+|c_{1}|^{2}),\quad 0\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2
we have

\begin{split}|c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}^{2}|&\leq|c_{1}||c_{3}|+|c_{2}|^{2}\\
&\leq|c_{1}|\cdot\frac{1}{3}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\frac{4|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)+|c_{2}|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2})+|c_{2}|^{2}\cdot\frac{3-|c_{1}|}{3(1+|c_{1}|)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2})+\frac{3-|c_{1}|}{3(1+|c_{1}|)}\cdot\frac{1}{4}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{12}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})(2+|c_{1}|^{2}).\end{split}

The equality in (
7
) is obtained for the function
\omega_{2}(z)
given in Theorem
1
,

\omega_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{|c_{1}|+z}{1+|c_{1}|z}\,dz=|c_{1}|z+\frac{1}{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{2}-\frac{1}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{3}+\cdots.

∎

Remark 2
.

From (
7
) we have taht for every
0\leq|c_{1}|\leq 1
,

|c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{12}\left(3-2|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{1}|^{4}\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}.

As it is shown in

(8)

\frac{z}{f(z)}=1-a_{2}z-z\omega(z),

where
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
and
\omega(z)=c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
. From (
8
) we can express the coefficients
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
, and
a_{5}
, of the function
f
, depending on
a_{2}
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2},
,
c_{3}
,…. After some calculations we receive

|H_{3}(1)(f)|=\left|c_{1}c_{3}-c_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4},

where

[
3
]

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}

Similarly as in Theorem

.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
) and
\mu\in{\mathcal{C}}

(9)

|c_{1}c_{3}-\mu c_{2}^{2}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{3}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}),&|\mu|\leq\frac{4}{3}\frac{|c_{1}|}{1+|c_{1}|}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{1}{12}\left[3|\mu|+2(2-3|\mu|)|c_{1}|^{2}-(4-3|\mu|)|c_{1}|^{4}\right],&|\mu|\geq\frac{4}{3}\frac{|c_{1}|}{1+|c_{1}|}\end{array}\right..

Finally, for the modulus
|c_{4}-c_{2}^{2}|
we have

Theorem 5
.

If
\omega\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{0}
is of form (
1
), then

(10)

|c_{4}-c_{2}^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{4}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})

and the estimate is sharp as the function

\omega(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{|c_{1}|+z^{3}}{1+|c_{1}|z^{3}}\,dz=|c_{1}|z+\frac{1}{4}(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{4}-\frac{1}{6}|c_{1}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2})z^{6}+\cdots.

shows.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2
, we easily get

|c_{4}-c_{2}^{2}|\leq|c_{4}|+|c_{2}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-4|c_{2}|^{2})+|c_{2}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}).

∎

A note on local energy decay results for wave equations with a potential
Ryo Ikehata

Abstract
In this paper we consider the local energy decay result for wave equations with a short-range potential. It is important to note that one never uses a finite speed of propagation property unlike the historical previous papers. The essential parts of analysis are in getting L 2 L^{2} -bound of the solution itself, and deriving the weighted energy estimates. In this paper we only use a simple multiplier method to treat the variable coefficient case, and do not rely on any resolvent estimates.

In this paper, we are concerned with the following Cauchy problem with a potential:

u_{tt}-\Delta u+V(x)u=0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.1)

u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 14.22636ptu_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.2)

u(t,x)=0\quad t>0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,

(1.3)

where
\Omega\subset{\bf R}^{n}
is an exterior domain with smooth compact boundary
\partial\Omega
, and one assumes
0\notin\bar{\Omega}
. Furthermore, let
\rho_{0}>0
be a real number such that

\partial\Omega\subset B_{\rho_{0}}
, and one assumes that

(A-1)
the obstacle
{\cal O}:={\bf R}^{n}\setminus\bar{\Omega}
is star-shaped relative to the origin, that is,
x\cdot\nu(x)\leq 0,\;x\in\partial\Omega
where
\nu(x)
is the unit exterior normal at the point
x\in\partial\Omega.

The potential function
V\in BC^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies
V(x)\geq 0
(

) and

(A-2)

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(x\cdot\nabla V(x)\right)+V(x)\leq 0
for all

.

Note that functions and solutions treated in this paper are all real-valued.

One can present a typical example for

satisfying the assumption

V(x)=V_{0}|x|^{-\alpha},\quad V_{0}>0,

\alpha\geq 2

Example 2.

One defines the total energy for the equation (
1.1
) by

E(t):=\int_{\Omega}e(t,x)dx:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx.

(1.5)

Our main purpose is to study the local energy decay problem of the equation (
1.1

V(x)

E_{R}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}\cap\Omega}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx.

The main result of this paper reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\rho_{0}>0
such that
\partial\Omega\subset{B}_{\rho_{0}}
.
Let
n\geq 2
and assume
(A-1)
and
(A-2)
. Let
R>\rho_{0}
be an arbitrary fixed number. If
(u_{0},u_{1})\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then the unique smooth solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies

E_{R}(t)\leq\frac{CK_{0}}{t-R},\quad t>R,

with some generous constant
C>0
, where

K_{0}:=(u_{1},u_{0})+(u_{1},x\cdot\nabla u_{0})+\sqrt{E(0)}(\|u_{0}\|+\|d_{n}(\cdot)u_{1}\|)

+\int_{\Omega}(1+|x|)\left(|u_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\right)dx.

Remark 1.4

In some sense the assumption (A-2) on the potential
V(x)
is a technical condition, however it includes an important example
\alpha=2
, that is,
V(x)=|x|^{-2}
as a critical potential. An important fact is that such
singular potential is a unique type of potential that the perturbed wave equation retains the property of Huygen’s principle in dimension
n=3
. For the perturbed wave equation with a regular potential, the Huygens’ principle never holds (see
[
5
]
). In that case, if the local energy decays then it decays exponentially in case of the validity of such a principle.

It should be emphasized that the constant
C>0
determined in Theorem
1.1
never depends on any
R>\rho_{0}
, and
R>\rho_{0}
is independent of the size of support of the initial data. This implies that one never relies on the finite speed of propagation property as is usually discussed (cf.,
[
14
]
). This is our essential contribution, and the condition
(u_{0},u_{1})\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
assumed on the initial data is not essential. By density one can discuss the same local energy decay in the framework of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. Indeed, one can obtain the following refinement of Theorem
1.1
. We state it without proof.

Theorem 1.2

Let
n\geq 2
and assume
(A-1)
and
(A-2)
. Let
R>\rho_{0}
be an arbitrary fixed number. If
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, then the unique weak solution
u\in C([0,\infty);H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies

E_{R}(t)\leq\frac{CK_{0}}{t-R},\quad t>R,

with some generous constant
C>0
, and
K_{0}
defined in Theorem
1.1
, provided that

\|d_{n}(\cdot)u_{1}\|<+\infty,\quad\int_{\Omega}|x|\left(|u_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}+|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\right)dx<+\infty.

Remark 1.5

Unfortunately, the constant coefficient case
V(x)=m^{2}
(
m>0
) can not be included as an example. This is the so-called Klein-Gordon equation case, which seems more difficult to be treated by our method. The assumption (A-2) may express a small perturbation from the pure wave equation case with
V(x)\equiv 0
. For the sharp local energy decay of the Klein-Gordon equation by using compactness assumptions on the initial data, one can cite the recent paper

Remark 1.6

In the assumptions on the initial velocity

of Theorem 1.2, it is easy to see that in the case when

Note that the concrete case

\int_{B_{R}\cap\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{2R^{\alpha}}{V_{0}}\frac{CK_{0}}{t-R},\quad t>R.

2
Preliminaries

The following lemma is a kind of Morawetz identity for the equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) obtained by using the multiplier
m(u)=u_{t}+x\cdot\nabla u+\frac{n-1}{2}
. The Morawetz identity is the useful framework to have identities when one needs to obtain estimates on the solutions, at least to hyperbolic equations. In
[
2
]
(Lemma 3.3) the authors obtaines identities with simple generalized multipliers of Morawetz type to study the stabilization of solutions to the system of elastic waves with localized nonlinear dissipation.

Lemma 2.1

Let
n\geq 2
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, the corresponding smooth solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies the following identity: for
t\geq 0
it holds that

tE(t)+\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)u(t,x)dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)(x\cdot\nabla u(t,x))dx

-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}(x\cdot\nabla V(x))+V(x)\right)|u(s,x)|^{2}dxds=J_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\sigma\cdot\nu(\sigma)dS_{\sigma}ds,

where

J_{0}:=\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{1}(x)u_{0}(x)dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{1}(x)(x\cdot\nabla u_{0}(x))dx,

and
\nu(\sigma)
is the unit outward normal vector at each
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
.

Furthermore, one needs the weighted energy estimate below. This is the modified version of that introduced originally by Todorova-Yordanov
[
22
]
(see also the Appendix in
[
12
]
). For this we prepare the following notation for the pointwise total energy and the weight function, respectively.

e(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right),\quad t>0,\quad x\in\Omega,

and
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times\bar{\Omega})
satisfying
\psi_{t}(t,x)\neq 0
for all
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\bar{\Omega}
.

Lemma 2.2

Let
n\geq 2
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, the corresponding smooth solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies the following identity:

0=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\psi(t,x)e(t,x)\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(\psi(t,x)u_{t}(t,x)\nabla u(t,x)\right)-\frac{V(x)}{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}\psi_{t}(t,x)

-\frac{1}{2\psi_{t}(t,x)}\left|\psi_{t}(t,x)\nabla u(t,x)-u_{t}(t,x)\nabla\psi(t,x)\right|^{2}+\frac{|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}}{2\psi_{t}(t,x)}\left(|\nabla\psi(t,x)|^{2}-\psi_{t}(t,x)^{2}\right),\,\,t>0,\,\,x\in\Omega.

To prove the following
L^{2}
-estimate of the solution one may derive itself by a similar method introduced in
[
11
]
(see also
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
) even for the equation (
1.1
) with potential
V(x)
. Since the proof relies on the Hardy inequality in the exterior domains for
n\geq 2
, the weight function
d_{n}(x)
appears in the statement (see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 2.3

Let
n\geq 2
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\times C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then, the corresponding smooth solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) satisfies the following estimate:

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+\|d_{n}(\cdot)u_{1}\|),\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.
Note that the function
v(t,x):=\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}}u(s,x)ds
is the solution of the problem

\displaystyle v_{tt}-\Delta v+V(x)v=u_{1},\quad t>0,\;x\in\Omega,

\displaystyle v(0)=0,\;v_{t}(0)=u_{0}.

Using the multiplier
m(v)=v_{t}
one obtains for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\|v_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{V(\cdot)}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{0}\|^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}u_{1}(x)v(t,x)dx,

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|d_{n}(\cdot)u_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{v^{2}(t,x)}{d_{n}^{2}(x)}dx,\quad t>0.

Applying the Hardy inequality for dimension
n\geq 2
and choosing a suitable
\varepsilon>0
the proof of lemma follows due to
u=v_{t}
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section one proves Theorem
1.1
by using Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
.

One first use assumptions
(A-1)
and
(A-2)
and Lemma
2.1
to get the inequality that

tE(t)+\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)u(t,x)dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)(x\cdot\nabla u(t,x))dx\leq J_{0},

(3.1)

where one has just used the fact that
(A-1)
implies
\sigma\cdot\nu(\sigma)\leq 0
for each
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
. Thus, it suffices to estimate two ingredients included in (
3.1
) such that

I_{1}(t):=\left|\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)u(t,x)dx\right|,

(3.2)

I_{2}(t):=\left|\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(t,x)(x\cdot\nabla u(t,x))dx\right|.

(3.3)

I_{1}(t)
can be estimated by the use of Lemma
2.3
soon, and
I_{2}(t)
can be evaluated by Lemma
2.2
.

(I)
.
Let us first get the bound for
I_{1}(t)
.

Indeed, from the Schwarz inequality one has

I_{1}(t)\leq\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)||u_{t}(t,x)|dx

\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}=\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|\|u(t,\cdot)\|.

From (
1.5
) we see that
\frac{1}{2}\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq E(t)=E(0)
, so that
\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|\leq\sqrt{2E(0)}
. Thus, by combining Lemma
2.3
one has

I_{1}(t)\leq C\sqrt{2E(0)}(\|u_{0}\|+\|d_{n}(\cdot)u_{1}\|)\quad t\geq 0.

(3.4)

(II)
.
Let us treat
I_{2}(t)
to get the decay rate for the local energy.

For this purpose one defines the weight function
\psi(t,x)
which has the same style introduced in
[
12
]
:

\psi(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{(1+|x|-t)}&\qquad|x|\geq t,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle{(1+t-|x|)^{-1}}&\qquad|x|<t,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

Then, it is easy to check that
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times{\bf R}^{n})
satisfies

\psi_{t}(t,x)<0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(3.5)

\psi_{t}(t,x)^{2}-|\nabla\psi(t,x)|^{2}=0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(3.6)

Note that (
3.6
) is the so-called Eikonal equation for (
1.1
). Therefore, it follows from Lemma
2.2
,
V(x)\geq 0
, (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) that

0\geq\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\psi(t,x)e(t,x)\right)-\nabla\cdot\left(\psi(t,x)u_{t}(t,x)\nabla u(t,x)\right),\,\,t>0,\,\,x\in\Omega.

By integrating both sides above on
[0,t]\times\Omega
and using the divergence theorem and (
1.3
), one has the weighted energy estimate such that

\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx

\leq\int_{\Omega}(1+|x|)\left(|u_{1}(x)|^{2}+|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\right)dx.

(3.7)

Now let us estimate
I_{2}(t)
basing on (
3.7
). This is just a modification of
[
12
, Lemma 2.4]
, indeed, let
R>\rho_{0}
be an arbitrary fixed number.
Set
\Omega_{R}:=\Omega\cap B_{R}
. Then, for
t>R
it follows that

I_{2}(t)\leq\int_{\Omega}|x||u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx

\leq R\int_{\Omega_{R}}|u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx+\int_{|x|\geq R}|x||u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx

\leq\frac{R}{2}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{|x|\geq t}|x||u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx+\int_{t\geq|x|\geq R}|x||u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx

\leq\frac{R}{2}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx

+\int_{|x|\geq t}(|x|-t)|u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx+t\int_{|x|\geq t}|u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx

+t\int_{t\geq|x|\geq R}|u_{t}(t,x)||\nabla u(t,x)|dx

\leq\frac{R}{2}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{|x|\geq t}(1+|x|-t)\left(|u_{t}(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx

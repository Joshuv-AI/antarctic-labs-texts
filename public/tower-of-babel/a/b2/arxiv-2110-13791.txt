1.
Introduction

Consider the following 6D steady incompressible Navier-Stokes equations on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}
as follows:

\displaystyle(\rm SNS)\,\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u=-\nabla{\pi}+f,\\
&\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u
represents the fluid velocity field,
\pi
is a scalar pressure.

The
\varepsilon-
regularity analysis of the above equations is started by Struwe’s question in
[
36
,
37
]
, where he obtained partial regularity for
N=5
by regularity methods of elliptic systems (c.f. Morrey
[
30
]
and Giaqinta
[
18
]
) and asked if analogous partial regularity results hold in spacial dimension
N>5.

Later, the result of Struwe was extended to the boundary case by Kang
[
23
]
. Recently interior regularity results in 6D are obtained by Dong-Strain
[
8
]
, and
they proved
0
is regular if

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}r^{-2}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover, similar boundary regularity results are obtained in Dong-Gu
[
7
]
and Liu-Wang
[
29
]
by different methods, respectively.
For more developments, in a series of papers by Frehse and Ruzicka
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, the existence on a class of special regular solutions of (
1.1
) was obtained for the five-dimensional and higher dimensional case. Gerhardt
[
17
]
obtained the regularity of weak solutions under the four-dimensional case. More references, we refer to Li-Yang
[
28
]
for the existence of regular solutions of high dimensional Navier-Stokes equations. At last, we refer to
[
14
]
by Farwig-Sohr for existence and regularity criteria for weak solutions to inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equations.

Recall that these so-called
\varepsilon-
regularity criteria can be traced back to
the well-known work by Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg
[
1
]
for the analysis of suitable weak solutions of the three dimensional time-dependent Navier-Stokes equations, where they showed that the set
\mathcal{S}
of possible interior singular points of a suitable weak solution is one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure zero by improving Scheffer’s results in
[
33
,
34
,
35
]
. More references on simplified proofs and improvements, we refer to Lin
[

]
, Ladyzhenskaya-Seregin
[

[

]
, Seregin
[

[

Motivated by the recent interior regularity by Wolf

in one scale can imply the regularity via pressure decomposition of Stokes equation. Also, we refer to Chae-Wolf

and

“Whether the smallness of the velocity in a ball can ensure the interior or boundary regularity of the 6D steady Navier-Stokes equations?”

In this note, we try to investigate this issue and answer these questions.

After finishing this paper, the authors have become to know that, very recently, Cui

At first,

Definition 1.1

(iii)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\big[|u|^{2}\triangle\phi+u\cdot\nabla\phi(|u|^{2}+2\pi)\big]+2fu\phi dx,

(1.2)

for any nonnegative

test function

The existence of such a suitable weak solution can be found in

2.
Notations and some technical lemmas

Let
(u,\pi)
be a solution to the steady Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). Set the following scaling:

\displaystyle u^{\lambda}(x)={\lambda}u(\lambda x),\quad\pi^{\lambda}(x)={\lambda}^{2}\pi(\lambda x),\quad f^{\lambda}(x)={\lambda}^{3}f(\lambda x),

(2.1)

for any
\lambda>0,
then the family
(u^{\lambda},\pi^{\lambda})
is also a solution of (
1.1
) with
f
replaced by
f^{\lambda}(x)
.
Now define some quantities which are invariant under the scaling (
2.1
):

A(r)=r^{-4}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x)|^{2}dx,\quad C(r)=r^{-3}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x)|^{3}dx;

E(r)=r^{-2}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx;

D(r)=r^{-3}\int_{B_{r}}|\pi-(\pi)_{B_{r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx,\quad(\pi)_{B_{r}}=\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\pi dx;

F(r)=r^{3q-6}\int_{B_{r}}|f(x)|^{q}dx,

where

B_{r}(x_{0})
is the ball of radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
, and
we denote
B_{r}(0)
by
B_{r}
.
Moreover, a solution
u
is said to be regular at
x_{0}
if
u\in L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for some
r>0
.

Let us
introduce Wolf’s pressure decomposition as in
[
43
]
. Given a bounded
C^{2}
-domain
G\subset R^{n}
and
1<s<\infty
, define the operator

E_{G}:W^{-1,s}(G)\rightarrow W^{-1,s}(G)
as follows.
By the
L^{p}-
theory of the steady Stokes system
[
16
]
, for any
F\in W^{-1,s}(G)
there exists a unique pair
(v,\pi)\in W^{1,s}_{0}\times L^{s}_{0}(G)
which solves the steady Navier-Stokes equations in the weak sense

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}-\Delta v+\nabla\pi=F,\quad in\quad G\\
{\rm div}~v=0,\quad in\quad G\\
v=0,\quad on\quad\partial G,\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
\pi\in L^{s}_{0}(G)
denotes

\displaystyle\int_{G}\pi dx=0,\quad\pi\in L^{s}(\Omega).

Then let
E_{G}(F)=\nabla\pi
, where
\nabla\pi
denotes the gradient functional in
W^{-1,s}(G)
defined by

\displaystyle<\nabla p,\psi>=-\int_{G}p\nabla\cdot\psi dx,\quad\psi\in W^{1,s^{\prime}}_{0}(G).

The operator
E_{G}
is bounded from
W^{-1,s}(G)
into itself with
E_{G}(\nabla\pi)=\nabla\pi
for all

\pi\in L^{s}_{0}(G)
, and

\displaystyle\|\pi\|_{L^{s}(G)}\leq C\|F\|_{W^{-1,s}(G)}.

(2.3)

The norm of
E_{G}
depends only on
s
and the geometric properties of
G
, and is independent
of
G
, if
G
is a ball or an annulus, which is due to the scaling properties of the Stokes equation.

3.
Interior regularity: proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Proposition

In details, we shall prove the key inequality (

) in Proposition

by using a strong induction
argument on

be a constant which will be specified at the final moment.
From the definition of a local suitable weak solution the following local energy
inequality holds true for every nonnegative
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})
,

\displaystyle 2\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi dx\leq\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}}\big[|u|^{2}\triangle\phi+u\cdot\nabla\phi(|u|^{2}+2\pi_{1}+2\pi_{2})\big]+2fu\phi dx.

(3.6)

First, we introduce the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1

(Cacciopolli type inequality)
.

Let
(u,\pi)
be a local suitable weak
solution in
B_{1}
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). Then for any
0<R\leq 1
there holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R/2})}^{2}\leq CR^{-2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+CR^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{3}+CR^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}.

(3.7)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

For any
0<R\leq\frac{3}{4}
, choose
\phi=1
in
B_{\tau}
and
\phi=0
on
B_{\rho}^{c}
with
\frac{R}{2}\leq\tau<\rho\leq R
and

\displaystyle\nabla\pi_{1}=-E_{B_{\rho}}(u\cdot\nabla u),\quad\nabla\pi_{2}=E_{B_{\rho}}(\triangle u).

It follows from (
3.6
) and
(
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tau}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\rho-\tau)^{-2}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2}dx+C(\rho-\tau)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{3}dx

\displaystyle+C(\rho-\tau)^{-1}\left(\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{3}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|\pi_{1}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle+C(\rho-\tau)^{-1}\left(\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|\pi_{2}|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\int_{B_{R}}|u||f|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\rho-\tau)^{-2}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2}dx+C(\rho-\tau)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{3}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+C\int_{B_{R}}|u||f|dx.

By a standard iteration argument, the proof is complete.

Similar as Lemma 2.9 in
[
2
]
or Lemma 2.3 in
[
22
]
, we have the decay estimate of the pressure part

.

Lemma 3.2

(The pressure estimate)
.

Let
(u,\pi)
be a local suitable weak
solution in
B_{1}
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). Assume that for any
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
and

there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|u\otimes u-({u\otimes u})_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq CC_{*}^{3}r^{6}\int_{B_{1}}|u|^{3}dx

then for all
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
and
0<r<\frac{1}{8}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq CC_{*}^{3}r^{6}\int_{B_{1}}|u|^{3}dx,

where
\nabla\pi_{1}=-E_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}}(u\cdot\nabla u)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Without loss of generality, let
x_{0}=0
. Assume that
0<\theta<\frac{1}{4}
and
r\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
fixed. Since
\nabla\pi_{1}=-E_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}}(u\cdot\nabla u)
, we can write

\displaystyle-\Delta v+\nabla(\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{r}})=\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{\frac{3}{4}}.

Let

\displaystyle p_{0,r}=\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\Delta}((u_{i}u_{j}-(u\otimes u)_{B_{r}})\zeta),

where
\zeta=1
in
B_{r/2}
,
\zeta=0
on
B_{r}^{c}
, and

\displaystyle p_{h,r}=\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{r}}-p_{0,r}.

Then
\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{r}}=p_{h,r}+p_{0,r}
in
B_{r}
.
Using triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\theta r}}|\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{\theta r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\theta r}}|p_{h,r}-(p_{h,r})_{B_{\theta r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

\displaystyle+C\int_{B_{\theta r}}|p_{0,r}-(p_{0,r})_{B_{\theta r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

\displaystyle:=J_{1}+J_{2}.

For

, it follows from the properties of harmonic functions that

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq C(\theta r)^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{B_{\theta r}}|\nabla p_{h,r}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C\theta^{\frac{15}{2}}\int_{B_{r}}|p_{h,r}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx.

For

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{\theta r}}|p_{0,r}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|p_{0,r}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|(u\otimes u-(u\otimes u)_{B_{r}})\zeta|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{r}}|u\otimes u-(u\otimes u)_{B_{r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx.

Combining these estimates, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\theta r}}|\pi_{1}-(\pi_{1})_{B_{\theta r}}|^{\frac{3}{2}}dx

We clarify that any suitable weak solution to the steady Navier-Stokes equations is a local suitable weak solution.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(u,\pi)
as in Definition
1.1
be a suitable weak solution to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). Then
u
is a local suitable weak solution in the sense of Definition

.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{6}
be a fixed ball. Without loss of generality, we assume that
f=0
. Define

\displaystyle\nabla\pi_{0,B}=E_{B}(-u\cdot\nabla u+\Delta u).

Since

is a bounded operator and
\nabla\pi\in W^{-1,q}(B)
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{B}(\nabla\pi)=E_{B}(-u\cdot\nabla u+\Delta u)=\nabla\pi_{0,B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{B}(-u\cdot\nabla u)+E_{B}(\Delta u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\nabla\pi_{1}+\nabla\pi_{2}.

Since
(u,\pi)
is a suitable weak solution, we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\big[|u|^{2}\triangle\phi+u\cdot\nabla\phi(|u|^{2}+2\pi)\big]dx.

Infinite order phase transition in the slow bond TASEP
Sourav Sarkar, Allan Sly, Lingfu Zhang

Abstract
In the slow bond problem the rate of a single edge in the Totally Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (TASEP) is reduced from 1 to 1 − ε 1-\varepsilon for some small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . Janowsky and Lebowitz [ 22 ] posed the well-known question of whether such very small perturbations could affect the macroscopic current. Different groups of physicists, using a range of heuristics and numerical simulations reached opposing conclusions on whether the critical value of ε \varepsilon is 0. This was ultimately resolved rigorously in [ 8 ] which established that ε c = 0 \varepsilon_{c}=0 . Here we study the effect of the current as ε \varepsilon tends to 0 and in doing so explain why it was so challenging to predict on the basis of numerical simulations. In particular we show that the current has an infinite order phase transition at 0, with the effect of the perturbation tending to 0 faster than any polynomial. Our proof focuses on the Last Passage Percolation formulation of TASEP where a slow bond corresponds to reinforcing the diagonal. We give a multiscale analysis to show that when ε \varepsilon is small the effect of reinforcement remains small compared to the difference between optimal and near optimal geodesics. Since geodesics can be perturbed on many different scales, we inductively bound the tails of the effect of reinforcement by controlling the number of near optimal geodesics and giving new tail estimates for the local time of (near) geodesics along the diagonal.



1
Introduction

In the Totally Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (TASEP) particles on
\mathbb{Z}
move from left to right, jumping according to rate 1 Poisson clocks on each edge, but with moves blocked if there is a particle to its right. Starting with work of Johansson
[
23
]
, methods from integrable probability have provided an increasingly detailed description of the dynamics
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
10
,
16
,
26
,
27
]
.

The current, the rate at which particles cross the origin, is maximized at
\frac{1}{4}
when the particle density is one half. This is a global property of the system, since conservation of particles means that the rate has to be equal everywhere. Janowsky and Lebowitz
[
22
]
asked how this would be affected by a local perturbation, in particular reducing the rate of a single edge at the origin from 1 to
1-\varepsilon
, a so-called
slow bond
. Through heuristic arguments, they predicted a reduction in the current for any
\varepsilon>0
. Later, another group of physicists arrived at the opposite conclusion
[
20
]
. Through numerical simulations and arguments of finite size scaling, they estimated
\varepsilon_{c}\approx 0.2
. This problem remained unresolved until work of Basu et. al.
[
8
]
established that
\varepsilon_{c}=0
, that is that there is always a slowdown. The aim of this paper is to explain why this question was hard to answer heuristically and via simulations.

The movement of particles in TASEP can be mapped to Last Passage Percolation (LPP) with rate 1 exponential weights and it is this setup that we analyse. The time for the
n
-th particle to pass the origin from step initial conditions is given by the passage time from the origin to
(n,n)
which we denote
T_{n}
(see Section
2
for the precise definitions). The passage time grows like
\frac{1}{n}T_{n}\approx 4
which corresponds to the inverse of the current. In the slow bond model, the weights along the diagonal are said to be reinforced, replaced with larger rate
1-\varepsilon
exponentials and we denote the corresponding passage time
T_{n}^{\varepsilon}
. By the Subadditive Ergodic Theorem the limit satisfies a law of large numbers which we denote

\Xi(\varepsilon)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{\varepsilon}]-4.

The results of
[
8
]
show that
\Xi(\varepsilon)>0
for all positive
\varepsilon
. The proof is by a multi-scale argument which shows that the reinforcement tends to attract the geodesic towards staying closer to the diagonal. This happens if the optimal geodesic is close to another near optimal geodesic that spends more time along the diagonal. After reinforcement, the new geodesic is larger. This has a small probability for small
\varepsilon
, but improvements to the geodesic can be made on any scale so the proof makes use of an accumulation of increases to the expected passage time over a series of different scales, which in total show that
T_{n}^{\varepsilon}>4n
for large
n
. Together with the Subadditive Ergodic Theorem this implies the result. No lower bound on this increase is given in
[
8
]
, but when
\varepsilon
is small the accumulation of many scales are needed and it would at best give a lower bound of
\Xi(\varepsilon)\geq e^{-c\varepsilon^{-2}}
, which is very small indeed.

One can ask whether this is an artifact of the proof or if
\Xi
is indeed very small. Here we show that it tends to 0 faster than any polynomial.

Theorem 1
.

For every
C\geq 1
we have that
\Xi(\varepsilon)\leq O(\varepsilon^{C})
.

This, in part, explains the difficulty in resolving the value of
\varepsilon_{c}
numerically. Tending to
0
so quickly, it cannot be easily distinguished from a positive
\varepsilon_{c}
, particularly given
n
of order ten thousand.

We note the heuristics of Lebowitz
[
11
]
suggest that
\Xi(\varepsilon)
is in fact of order
e^{-c\varepsilon^{-1}}
.
We expect that our method, which uses an induction-based multi-scale analysis (and is to be explained shortly), can be further pushed to yield
\Xi(\varepsilon)\leq e^{-(\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))^{C}}
for some
C>1
; although for simplicity of the arguments we choose not to pursue that. However, to establish or refute the suggested order of
e^{-c\varepsilon^{-1}}
, some further ideas would be needed.

1.1
Proof Sketch

In Exponential LPP, without reinforcement along the diagonal the transversal fluctuations of the geodesic scale like
n^{2/3}
(
[
24
]
). It is, therefore, natural to expect then that the optimal geodesic spends about
n^{1/3}
time on the diagonal. We establish such a local time result together with exponential tail bounds. Thus, reinforcing the diagonal increases the original geodesic by order
\varepsilon n^{1/3}
. The fluctuations in the passage time themselves are also of order
n^{1/3}
and Tracy-Widom distributed and so are of the same magnitude. Our proof rests on a comparison between the benefit of reinforcement accumulated on a series of smaller scales and the difference in passage times between geodesics and near geodesics.

On a rectangle, parallel to the diagonal, of length
n
and height
n^{2/3}
, there are
n^{4/3}
geodesics joining pairs of points on the left and right sides. These have a strong tendency to coalesce together forming highways from left to right; and in the middle third of the rectangle only
O(1)
distinct geodesics remain which was established in
[
6
]
to show that there are no non-trivial infinite bigeodesics. The proof makes use of the geometric fact that geodesics cannot cross each other twice and so a large number of distinct geodesics implies the existence of many good non-crossing disjoint paths. Many such paths can be ruled out using a combination of the BK inequality with an entropy argument controlling the number of non-overlapping paths.

Since there are few highways and their placement is random, they are unlikely to spend much time close to the diagonal and benefit from reinforcement. However, reinforcement may make another route preferable so we also need to consider the locations of
near geodesics
, i.e., up-right paths close in passage time to the optimal ones. We cannot perform the same geometric reduction since near geodesics can cross each other multiple times. Instead, we discretize space and for each pair of starting and ending points rank the near geodesics. The rank-
k
best geodesics for each pair of starting and ending points have the property that they cannot cross twice and so we can again apply the approach of
[
6
]
.

This reduces our task to showing that, at an appropriate level of discretization, between a pair of starting and ending points, there are few near geodesics. If we consider the passage time of the best geodesic that passes through a given point on an anti-diagonal parallel to the side of the rectangle, this scales asymptotically to a sum of two Airy processes and is thus locally Brownian. As with Brownian motion, it is unlikely to have many well separated almost-maxima on an interval. By the Robinson-Schensted-Knuth (RSK) correspondence, this process is in fact a sum of two random walk bridges, conditioned to lie above another stochastic process (see e.g.
[
13
,
28
]
for this in slightly different settings). Using random walk estimates and a comparison with Brownian motion we control the tails of the number of near-maxima.

Altogether we show that there are only
O(1)
near geodesics in the middle portion of the rectangle. By considering translations of the field (which also translate the geodesics), we argue that in expectation these near geodesics spend only
n^{1/3}
time close to the diagonal. Moreover, the locations are essentially local and we establish enough independence to show corresponding tail bounds via a multi-scale proof.

The final step in the proof of Theorem
1
is a multi-scale induction controlling the increase in the passage time from reinforcement. On a series of scales
n_{k}=\varepsilon^{-k/200}
we show that with very high probability, the increase from reinforcement is at most
\varepsilon^{1/3}n_{k}^{1/3}(\log(n_{k}))^{50k}
. Then at scale
k+1
, the inductive hypothesis shows that after reinforcement the new geodesic must be close to an original near geodesics. Having shown that the original near geodesics do not spend too much time close to the diagonal we can control the increase in the passage time from reinforcement at level
k+1
. This induction will fail for some large enough
k
(the slow bond results of
[
8
]
guarantee this) but we show that for each fixed
k
it will hold provided
\varepsilon
is small enough. The bounds can then be used to establish Theorem
1
directly.

Organisation of the paper

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we setup notations for the model of Exponential LPP, and record some useful results (of the unperturbed LPP) from the literature.
In Section
3
we prove the estimate of the time spent by geodesics near the diagonal. There we use an estimate on the number of near geodesics between a pair of points, whose proof is the main content of Section
5
.
The multi-scale proof of the main result is given in Section
4
.

2
Notation and Preliminaries

We now formally setup the model of Exponential LPP.
To each vertex
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we associate an independent weight
\xi(v)
with
\Exp(1)
distribution.
For two points

, we say

if

is coordinate-wise less than or equal to
v
.
For such
u,v
and any up-right path
\gamma
from
u
to
v
, we define the
passage time of the path
to be

T(\gamma):=\sum_{w\in\gamma}\xi(w).

Then almost surely there is a unique up-right path from
u
to
v
that has the largest passage time.
We call this path the
geodesic

\Gamma_{u,v}
, and call
T_{u,v}:=T(\Gamma_{u,v})
the
passage time from
u
to
v
.
An up-right path
\gamma
from
u
to
v
is called an
x
-near geodesic
, if
T(\gamma)\geq T_{u,v}-x
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote
\mathbf{n}=(n,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
; in particular we have
\mathbf{0}=(0,0)
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
T_{n}=T_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}
.

The TASEP on
\mathbb{Z}
is mapped to this Exponential LPP in the following way.
Consider the step initial condition, where each non-positive site is occupied by a particle, and each positive site is empty.
For each
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we let
\xi((x,y))
be the waiting time for the particle initially at site
-x
to make its
y+1
-th jump, after it has made the previous jump and the site right next to it (which is site
y-x+1
) becomes empty.
Note that these waiting times would be i.i.d.
\Exp(1)
, from the model definition of TASEP.
Then via a recursive relation,
T_{\mathbf{0},(x,y)}
would be the total time till the particle initially at site
-x
makes the
y+1
-th jump.

In the slow bond model, each jump cross the edge
0-1
is done at a slower rate of
1-\varepsilon
.
Thus for this perturbed model, the corresponding LPP would be on the field
\{\xi^{\varepsilon}(v)\}_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, where all the weights
\xi^{\varepsilon}(v)
are independent, and
\xi^{\varepsilon}((x,y))
has distribution
\Exp(1)
when
x\neq y
, or
\Exp(1-\varepsilon)
(with rate
1-\varepsilon
and mean
(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}>1
) when
x=y
.
We shall call this line
x=y

the diagonal
.
We couple
\{\xi^{\varepsilon}(v)\}_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
with
\{\xi(v)\}_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
as follows. For each
v
not on the diagonal, we let
\xi^{\varepsilon}(v)=\xi(v)
; and for each
v
on the diagonal, we let
\xi^{\varepsilon}(v)=\xi(v)+\varrho(v)\xi^{\prime}(v)
, where
\varrho(v)
is a Bernoulli
(\varepsilon)
random variable and
\xi^{\prime}(v)
is an
\Exp(1-\varepsilon)
random variable with mean
(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}=1+\Theta(\varepsilon)
, independent of each other and both are independent of
\xi(v)
.

For points
u\preceq v
, we let
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{u,v}
and
T^{\varepsilon}_{u,v}
be the geodesic and passage time from
u
to
v
, under the reinforced field
\{\xi^{\varepsilon}(w)\}_{w\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we also denote
T^{\varepsilon}_{n}=T^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}
.

We also set up the following useful notations.
For any
u=(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we denote
d(u)=x+y
, and
ad(u)=x-y
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote
\mathbb{L}_{n}=\{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:d(u)=n\}
.
We shall use the notation
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
to denote discrete intervals, i.e.,
\llbracket a,b\rrbracket
will denote
[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

For merely avoiding the notational overhead of integer parts, we would ignore some rounding issues.
For example, we shall often assume, without loss of generality, that fractional powers of integers i.e.,
k^{2/3}
or rational multiples of integers as integers themselves. It is easy to check that such assumptions do not affect the proofs in any substantial way.

3
Tails of local times of near geodesics

In this section we bound the time that near geodesics spend near the diagonal.

For any
w\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider barriers of length
2w
, each centered and perpendicular to the diagonal, and are spaced
w^{3/2}
apart. That is, they are open line segments joining
(iw^{3/2}-w,iw^{3/2}+w)
and
(iw^{3/2}+w,iw^{3/2}-w)
.
More precisely, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
B_{i}=\{(iw^{3/2}-x,iw^{3/2}+x):|x|<w\}
.
We shall estimate the number of barriers that can be hit by a near geodesic.

For any
u\preceq v
and
x>0
, we let
H^{x}_{u,v}(w)=\max_{\gamma}|\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:B_{i}\cap\gamma\neq\emptyset\}|
,
where the maximum is over all
x
-near geodesic
\gamma
from
u
to
v
, i.e., over all up-right path
\gamma
from
u
to
v
with
T(\gamma)\geq T_{u,v}-x
.

For any real numbers
p<q
, we denote

H^{x}(p,q,w)=\max\{H^{x}_{u,v}(w):i,j\in\mathbb{Z};p\leq iw^{3/2}<jw^{3/2}\leq q;u\in B_{i},v\in B_{j}\},

and we let
H^{x}(n,w)=H^{x}(0,n,w)
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exist constants
C,c>0
, such that

\mathbb{P}[H^{x}(n,w)>M(\log(w))^{32}n^{1/3}w^{-1/2}+1]<Ce^{-cM}

for any
M>0
,
n^{0.99}<w^{3/2}
, and
0\leq x\leq w^{1/2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

A special case of this theorem is when
x=0
, i.e., we can bound the maximum number of barriers hit by a geodesic (rather than a near geodesic).
In this case the condition
n^{0.99}<w^{3/2}
is not necessary, since the only reason to have this condition is that we want to apply Proposition
3.10
below to bound the number of near geodesics between a pair of points; while for geodesics there is only one between any pair of points.
More precisely, for the case of
x=0
and
w=1
, it can be shown that (see
[
18
, Lemma 2.18]
),

\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{a,b\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket,a<b}|\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\mathbf{i}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}\}|>Mn^{1/3}\right]<Ce^{-cM},

(3.1)

for
C,c>0
being constants and any
M>0
.
A proof of this estimate can be found in
[
18
, Appendix A]
, which uses the same method as the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Such estimates on the time that geodesics spend on a diagonal are useful in studying geometries of geodesics. For example, in
[
18
,
19
]
such results are used to construct and prove convergence to geodesic local times in the directed landscape, a scaling limit of the Exponential LPP
[
14
,
15
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

It would also be interesting to get a lower bound for the number of barriers hit by a geodesic.
A lower bound in expectation can be quickly deduced from the following estimate.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
3
, Theorem 2]
)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c>0
, such that for any large enough
n
, and
n^{-2/3}\leq\delta\leq 1
, and
t\in\llbracket\epsilon n,(1-\epsilon)n\rrbracket
, we have

\mathbb{P}[|m_{*}|<\delta n^{2/3}]>c\delta,

where
m_{*}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the number such that
(t-m_{*},t+m_{*})\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}
.

We note that
[
3
, Theorem 2]
is stated to require
n
to be large enough depending on
\delta
; but the proof in
[
3
]
actually allows for more general choices of
\delta
and
n
, as discussed below the statement there.
Then for any
n
large enough and
w\leq n^{2/3}
, by taking
\delta=wn^{-2/3}
and summing over order
nw^{-3/2}
many choices of
t
, we get

\mathbb{E}[|\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:B_{i}\cap\Gamma_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}\neq\emptyset\}|]>cn^{1/3}w^{-1/2},

(3.2)

where
c>0
is a constant. In addition, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) together imply the following: for any small
\delta>0
, there is
c(\delta)>0
such that
\mathbb{P}[|\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\mathbf{i}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}\}|>\delta n^{1/3}]>c(\delta)
for any large enough
n
.

As for near geodesics, we expect a generalization of (
3.2
) as stated below to hold:

\mathbb{E}[|\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:B_{i}\cap\gamma\neq\emptyset,\forall\gamma\text{ up-right path from }\mathbf{0}\text{ to }\mathbf{n},\;T(\gamma)\geq T_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}-w^{1/2}\}|]>cn^{1/3}w^{-1/2}.

To prove this, it suffices to upgrade Proposition
3.4
to (using the notations there):

\mathbb{P}\Big[|m_{*}|<\delta n^{2/3},\sup_{|m|\geq\delta n^{2/3}}T_{\mathbf{0},(t-m,t+m)}+T_{(t-m,t+m),\mathbf{n}}-\xi((t-m,t+m))<T_{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{n}}-\delta^{1/2}n^{1/3}\Big]>c\delta.

To establish this upgrade of Proposition
3.4
, a potential route is to analyze the point-to-line profiles
m\mapsto T_{\mathbf{0},(t-m,t+m)}
and
m\mapsto T_{(t-m,t+m),\mathbf{n}}-\xi((t-m,t+m))
, using the random walk Gibbs property (stated as Lemma
5.3
below). We do not pursue a rigorous proof of this upgrade here.

We also mention that the convergence results in
[
18
]
also contain a lower bound, for the setting of the
(1,1)
-direction semi-infinite geodesic, which is an infinite up-right
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
path
\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}=\{\mathbf{0}=(x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots\}
, satisfying (1)
(x_{i},y_{i})-(x_{i-1},y_{i-1})\in\{(0,1),(1,0)\}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
; (2)
\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}/y_{i}=1
; (3) for any
i<j
,
\Gamma_{(x_{i},y_{i}),(x_{j},y_{j})}
is contained in
\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}
.
The existence and almost sure uniqueness of
\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}
have been established in the literature (see
[
12
,
17
]
).
The main result of
[
18
]
implies that as
n\to\infty
,
2^{2/3}n^{-1/3}|\{i\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket:\mathbf{i}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{0}}\}|
converges to a random variable
L(1)
, where
L:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is the directed landscape geodesic local time constructed in
[
19
]
.
It is shown (as
[
19
, Proposition 7.6]
) that
L(1)>0
almost surely.

We can extend Theorem
3.1
by taking wider barriers, and the next result is what will be used in the next section.

For each
h\geq 1
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
hB_{i}
be the barrier with the same center as
B_{i}
, but length
2hw
; i.e. we let
hB_{i}=\{(iw^{3/2}-x,iw^{3/2}+x):|x|<hw\}
.
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x>0
, we let
H^{x}(n,w;h)
be defined the same way as
H^{x}(n,w)
, except for replacing each
B_{i}
by
hB_{i}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

There exist constants
C,c>0
, such that
\mathbb{P}[H^{x}(n,w;h)>Mh(\log(w))^{32}n^{1/3}w^{-1/2}+1]<Ch^{3/2}e^{-cM}
for any
M>0
,
h\geq 1
,
n^{0.99}<w^{3/2}
,
x\leq w^{1/2}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
h<w^{3}
and
n^{2/3}>hw
, since otherwise
h(\log(w))^{32}n^{1/3}w^{-1/2}\geq nw^{-3/2}
, and the conclusion follows since there is always
H^{x}(n,w;h)\leq nw^{-3/2}+1
.

For each
k=0,\ldots,h^{3/2}-1
, we let
H_{k}
be the maximum number of barriers in
\{hB_{ih^{3/2}+k}:i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, hit by an
x
-near geodesic from
hB_{ih^{3/2}+k}
to
hB_{jh^{3/2}+k}
, for some
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq i(hw)^{3/2}+k<j(hw)^{3/2}+k\leq n
.
From this definition we have
H^{x}(n,w;h)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{h^{3/2}-1}H_{k}
, and by Theorem
3.1
we have
\mathbb{P}[H_{k}>M(\log(w))^{32}n^{1/3}(hw)^{-1/2}]<Ce^{-cM}
for each
0\leq k\leq h^{3/2}-1
(here we use that
(\log(w))^{32}n^{1/3}(hw)^{-1/2}>1
).
Then the conclusion follows from a union bound.
∎

3.1
Inductive proof for Theorem
3.1

We fix
w\in\mathbb{N}
. It suffices to prove for the case where
x=w^{1/2}
, so we will assume
x=w^{1/2}
herein. Thus for simplicity of notations, we also write
H_{u,v}=H_{u,v}^{x}(w)
,
H(p,q)=H^{x}(p,q,w)
, and
H(n)=H^{x}(n,w)
.

We shall prove the following statements:
there exist
C,c>0
and large
A>0
, such that

\mathbb{E}[H(n)]<A^{2/3+0.01}n^{1/3}w^{-1/2}(\log(w))^{32}+1,

(3.3)

and for any

\mathbb{P}[H(n)>MAn^{1/3}w^{-1/2}(\log(w))^{32}+1]<Ce^{-cM}.

(3.4)

We prove these results by induction in
n
.
The base case where

is obvious, since there is always
H(n)\leq nw^{-3/2}+1

Below we prove these results for some

Lemma 3.6
.

There is a universal constant

\mathbb{E}[\overline{H}(p,q)]<C_{1}(\log(w))^{32}((q-p)^{1/3}w^{-1/2}+1)

The following estimate is a key input.

.

For

\displaystyle An^{1/3}w^{-1/2}(\log(w))^{32}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}H_{i}{\mathbf{1}}(H_{i}\geq Ar^{-1}n^{1/3}w^{-1/2}(\log(w))^{32})\,.

Let

We need to prove the following estimate on the local fluctuation of near geodesics.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
2m\leq n
, let
\mathcal{E}_{m,n}
be the following event: there exists some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
0\leq b\leq m
,
|a|\geq(\log(n))^{5}m^{2/3}
, and
|a|\leq b
, and
T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}+T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}\geq T_{(a,-a),\mathbf{n}}-m^{1/3}
.
Then there exists universal constant
c>0
, such that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{m,n}]\leq e^{-c(\log(n))^{3}}
, when
n,m
are large enough.

Proof.

The proof is by a union bound over all choices
a,b
, using Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
.
Fix
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
0\leq b\leq m
, and
(\log(n))^{5}m^{2/3}\leq a\leq b
. The case where
a<0
follows by symmetry.
Let
a^{\prime}=\lfloor a(1-b/n)\rfloor
.
Since
T_{(a,-a),\mathbf{n}}\geq T_{(a,-a),(b+a^{\prime}-1,b-a^{\prime})}+T_{(b+a^{\prime},b-a^{\prime}),\mathbf{n}}
,
if
T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}+T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}\geq T_{(a,-a),\mathbf{n}}-m^{1/3}
, one of the following happens:

1.

T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}-T_{(b+a^{\prime},b-a^{\prime}),\mathbf{n}}>D_{(n-b,n-b)}-D_{(n-b-a^{\prime},n-b+a^{\prime})}+(\log(n))^{7}\sqrt{a^{\prime}}
,

2.

T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}>D_{(b-a,b+a)}+(\log(n))^{3}(b+|a|)^{1/2}(b-|a|+1)^{-1/6}
,

3.

T_{(a,-a),(b+a^{\prime}-1,b-a^{\prime})}<D_{(b+a^{\prime}-a,b-a^{\prime}+a)}-(\log(n))^{3}m^{1/3}
.

Indeed, by elementary computation we have

D_{(n-b,n-b)}-D_{(n-b-a^{\prime},n-b+a^{\prime})}+D_{(b-a,b+a)}-D_{(b+a^{\prime}-a,b-a^{\prime}+a)}<-c{a^{\prime}}^{2}/m,

where
c>0
is a small constant.
Then we have

(D_{(n-b,n-b)}-D_{(n-b-a^{\prime},n-b+a^{\prime})}+(\log(n))^{7}\sqrt{a^{\prime}})+(D_{(b-a,b+a)}+(\log(n))^{3}(b+|a|)^{1/2}(b-|a|+1)^{-1/6})\\
-(D_{(b+a^{\prime}-a,b-a^{\prime}+a)}-(\log(n))^{3}m^{1/3})\\
<-c{a^{\prime}}^{2}/m+(\log(n))^{7}\sqrt{a^{\prime}}+(\log(n))^{3}(b+|a|)^{1/2}(b-|a|+1)^{-1/6}+(\log(n))^{3}m^{1/3}<-m^{1/3},

where the last inequality uses that
|a|\geq(\log(n))^{5}m^{2/3}
.
Thus if none of the three events holds, we must have

T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}-T_{(b+a^{\prime},b-a^{\prime}),\mathbf{n}}+T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}-T_{(a,-a),(b+a^{\prime}-1,b-a^{\prime})}<-m^{1/3},

which contradicts with
T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}+T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}\geq T_{(a,-a),\mathbf{n}}-m^{1/3}
.

We finally bound the probabilities of the three events.
We apply Lemma
2.3
for the first event, Theorem
2.1
(iv) for the second event, and Theorem
2.1
(ii) for the third event.
For these, we note that
a^{\prime}\leq b(1-b/n)\leq(n-b)/2
, and
|a-a^{\prime}|+1\leq ab/n+2\leq b/2+2
, so the slopes
\frac{n-b-a^{\prime}}{n-b+a^{\prime}}
and
\frac{b+a^{\prime}-a+1}{b-a^{\prime}+a}
are in
[1/3,3]
when
a,b
are large enough.
Then the probability of each of the three events is bounded by
e^{-c(\log(n))^{3}}
for some constant
c>0
, when
n,m
are large enough.
Thus we conclude that
\mathbb{P}[T_{(a,-a),(b,b)}+T_{(b,b),\mathbf{n}}\geq T_{(a,-a),\mathbf{n}}-m^{1/3}]<3e^{-c(\log(n))^{3}}
.
Note that there are no more than
n^{2}
choices of
a,b
, so by taking a union bound over all
a,b
the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Given any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 200
, there exists
\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{k}
, and all

Proof.

For the simplicity of notation, we again assume that
\varepsilon^{-1/200}
is an integer. We let
c>0
denote a small universal constant, and its value can change from line to line.

We first prove this theorem for
\ell=1
. Note that

\mathbb{E}[Z^{\varepsilon}_{n_{k}}\mathds{1}[Z^{\varepsilon}_{n_{k}}>n_{k}]]=n_{k}\mathbb{P}[Z^{\varepsilon}_{n_{k}}>n_{k}]+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[Z^{\varepsilon}_{n_{k}}>n_{k}+t]{\rm d}t\leq e^{-cn_{k}},

since
Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}
is at most the sum of
n_{k}
independent random variables, each distributed as
\rho\xi
with
\rho
a Bernoulli(
\varepsilon
) random variable and
\xi
an independent Exp(

) random variable. And

\mathbb{E}[Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\mathds{1}[Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\leq n_{k}^{1/2}]]\leq n_{k}^{1/2},\quad\mathbb{E}[Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\mathds{1}[n_{k}^{1/2}\leq Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\leq n_{k}]]\leq n_{k}\mathbb{P}[Z_{n_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\geq n_{k}^{1/2}].

5
Tail of multiple near-optimums

In this section we prove Proposition
3.10
.
We use a Gibbs property of the point-to-line profile in Exponential LPP, which we state now.

Definition 5.1
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\preceq v
, let
L^{k}_{u,v}=\max_{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}T(\gamma_{i})
, where the maximum is over all (possibly empty) up-right paths
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
, such that they are mutually disjoint, and each is contained in the set
\{w\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u\preceq w\preceq v\}
.
Denote
W^{k}_{u,v}=L^{k}_{u,v}-L^{k-1}_{u,v}
(where we take
L^{0}_{u,v}=0
).
For any
\mathbf{0}\preceq v
we also write
W^{k}_{v}=W^{k}_{\mathbf{0},v}
.

We list some properties of these maximum disjoint weights.

1.

T_{u,v}=W^{1}_{u,v}
.

2.

W^{k}_{u,v}\leq W^{k}_{u,v+(1,0)}
and
W^{k+1}_{u,v+(1,0)}\leq W^{k}_{u,v}
; similarly
W^{k}_{u,v}\leq W^{k}_{u,v+(0,1)}
and
W^{k+1}_{u,v+(0,1)}\leq W^{k}_{u,v}
.

3.

For
u=(u_{1},u_{2})\preceq v=(v_{1},v_{2})
and
k>1+(v_{1}-u_{1})\wedge(v_{2}-u_{2})
, we must have
W^{k}_{u,v}=0
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
I_{n}:=\{(a,b,k):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},a+b=n,k\in\llbracket 1,1+a\wedge b\rrbracket\}
.
We now give the explicit distribution of
\{W_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any non-negative
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
, if it satisfies the following
interlacing condition
:
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\leq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k},p_{(a,b+1)}^{k}
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\geq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k+1},p_{(a,b+1)}^{k+1}
for any
(a,b,k)\in I_{n}
,
we have

\mathbb{P}\left[W_{(a,b)}^{k}\in[p_{(a,b)}^{k},p_{(a,b)}^{k}+{\rm d}p_{(a,b)}^{k}),\;\forall(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}\right]\\
=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n+1}}p_{(a,b)}^{k}+\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}}p_{(a,b)}^{k}\right)\prod_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}{\rm d}p^{k}_{(a,b)},

where
Z
is the partition function.
If the interlacing condition is not satisfied, the left hand side equals zero.

We let
S:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a random walk, where each
S(i)-S(i-1)\sim\LAP(0,2)
, i.e., the difference of two independent
\Exp(1/2)
random variables.
From Theorem
5.2
we immediately get the following Gibbs property of the profile
a\mapsto T_{\mathbf{0},(a,n-a)}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Take any
l<r\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
. Conditioned on
\{W^{2}_{(a,n+1-a)}\}_{l\leq a\leq r+1}
and
T_{\mathbf{0},(l,n-l)},T_{\mathbf{0},(r,n-r)}
, the process
\{T_{\mathbf{0},(l+i,n-l-i)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,n-l)}\}_{i=0}^{r-l}
has the same distribution as the random walk
\{S(i)\}_{i=0}^{r-l}
, conditioned on that
S(r-l)=T_{\mathbf{0},(r,n-r)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,n-l)}
, and
S(i)>W^{2}_{(l+i,n+1-l-i)}\vee W^{2}_{(l+i+1,n-l-i)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,n-l)}
for each
0\leq i\leq l-r
.

This result can be viewed as
[
28
, Corollary 4.8]
or
[
13
, Theorem 5.2]
, in slightly different settings.
For completeness we give the proof of Theorem
5.2
in Appendix

5.1
Random walk bridge estimates

As seen above, the point-to-line profile can be described as a random walk bridge, conditioned on staying above a certain function.
To prove Proposition
3.10
, in this subsection we study such random walk bridges, on the probability for the sum of two having multiple peaks, and each staying above a ‘well-behaved’ function.

Fix arbitrary
C_{0}>0
, and in this subsection the constants
c,C
would depend on
C_{0}
, and the values can change from line to line. We define random walk bridges
\underline{S},\overline{S}
as following.
Take any
g>0
and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\underline{s},\overline{s}\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfying that

1.

|\underline{s}|,|\overline{s}|<C_{0}n
and
|\underline{s}+\overline{s}|<\sqrt{g}
.

2.

n^{0.9}<g
,
gm<n
, and
\log(n)^{2}<m<n^{0.1}
.

Let
\underline{S},\overline{S}:\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket\to\mathbb{R}
be two independent copies of
S
on
\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
, conditioned on that
\underline{S}(n)=\underline{s}
and
\overline{S}(n)=\overline{s}
.

Such random walk bridges could also be defined in the following alternative way.
Denote
\alpha=\overline{s}/n
, then we have
|\alpha|<C_{0}
. Take
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\frac{8\beta}{1-4\beta^{2}}=\alpha
.
Let
X
be a random variable with probability density given by
\mathbb{P}[X\in[x,x+{\rm d}x)]=\frac{1-4\beta^{2}}{4}e^{-|x-\alpha|/2-\beta x}{\rm d}x
; then we have
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
.
We consider a random walk
\overline{S}^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
, where each
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)-\overline{S}^{\prime}(i-1)
has the same distribution as
X
; and we let
\underline{S}^{\prime}
be an independent copy of
-\overline{S}^{\prime}
.
Then
\{\overline{S}(i)-\alpha i\}_{i=0}^{n}
is
\overline{S}^{\prime}
on
\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
conditioned on that
\overline{S}^{\prime}(n)=0
;
and
\{\underline{S}(i)+\alpha i\}_{i=0}^{n}
is
\underline{S}^{\prime}
on
\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
conditioned on that
\underline{S}^{\prime}(n)=\overline{s}+\underline{s}
.
We denote
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{i}(x)=\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)\in[x,x+{\rm d}x)]/{\rm d}x
and
\underline{\mathcal{T}}^{i}(x)=\mathbb{P}[\underline{S}^{\prime}(i)\in[x,x+{\rm d}x)]/{\rm d}x
.

We would need the following bound on the probability of exhibiting multiple peaks for the sum
\underline{S}+\overline{S}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event where
\underline{S}(i)+\overline{S}(i)<\sqrt{g}
for each
0\leq i\leq n
; and there exist integers
0<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}<n
, such that
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
and
\overline{S}(i_{j})+\underline{S}(i_{j})>-\sqrt{g}
for each
j
.
Then we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]<Ce^{-cm}
.

Proof.

We let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
be the event where
\underline{S}(i)+\overline{S}(i)<\sqrt{g}
for each
0\leq i\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, and there exist integers
0<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, such that
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
and
\overline{S}(i_{j})+\underline{S}(i_{j})>-\sqrt{g}
for each
j
;
also let
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be the event where
\underline{S}(i)+\overline{S}(i)<\sqrt{g}
for each
\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq i\leq n
; and there exist integers
\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}<n
, such that
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
and
\overline{S}(i_{j})+\underline{S}(i_{j})>-\sqrt{g}
for each
j
.
Then we have that
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{2}
. We shall now bound
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{1}]
, and the bound for
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{2}]
would follow similarly.

We consider the event
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, where
\underline{S}^{\prime}(i)+\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)<\sqrt{g}
for each
0\leq i\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, and there exist integers
0<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, such that
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
and
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i_{j})+\underline{S}^{\prime}(i_{j})>-\sqrt{g}
for each
j
.
Denote

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\overline{S}^{\prime}(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)\in[x,x+{\rm d}x),\underline{S}^{\prime}(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)\in[y,y+{\rm d}y)]/({\rm d}x{\rm d}y).

We can then write

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{1}]=\frac{\iint\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(x,y)\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2\rceil}(-x)\underline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2\rceil}(\overline{s}+\underline{s}-y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y}{\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(0)\underline{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(\overline{s}+\underline{s})}.

By a local limit theorem, we have that
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(0),\underline{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(\overline{s}+\underline{s})>cn^{-1/2}
, while
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2\rceil}(-x),\underline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2\rceil}(\overline{s}+\underline{s}-y)<Cn^{-1/2}
. Thus we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{1}]<C\iint\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(x,y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y=C\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}^{\prime}].

It now suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]
, the probability of an event on the random walk
\underline{S}^{\prime}+\overline{S}^{\prime}
. We denote
\underline{S}^{\prime}+\overline{S}^{\prime}
as
\hat{S}
from now on, for simplicity of notations.
We use Skorokhod’s embedding of a random walk to a Brownian motion:
take a standard Brownian motion
B:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
, there is a sequence of stopping times
0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, such that
\{B(\tau_{i})\}_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
has the same distribution as
\{\hat{S}(i)\}_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
.
For each
i\in\llbracket 1,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\rrbracket
, the random variables
\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
are i.i.d.; and by the exponential tail of each
\hat{S}(i)-\hat{S}(i-1)
we have
\mathbb{P}[\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}>x]<Ce^{-cx^{1/3}}
.

We consider the following event on
B
.
Let
t_{0}=0
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
t_{j}=\inf\{t>t_{j-1}+cg,B(t)>-\sqrt{g}\}
, which is a stopping time.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\prime}
be the event where
B(t)<2\sqrt{g}
for any
t\in[0,t_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}]
.
For each
j
, conditioned on
t_{j}
and
B(t_{j})
, there is a positive probability that
\max_{t\in[t_{j},t_{j}+cg]}B(t)>2\sqrt{g}
; so we have that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\prime}]<Ce^{-cm}
.

Under
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\prime}
, one of the following events must happen:

•

\mathcal{E}_{spike}^{\prime}
: there is some
i\in\llbracket 1,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\rrbracket
, such that
B(\tau_{i-1}),B(\tau_{i})<\sqrt{g}
and
B(t)>2\sqrt{g}
for some
t\in(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i})
.

•

\mathcal{E}_{narrow}^{\prime}
: there is some
i\in\llbracket 0,\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor g\rfloor\rrbracket
, such that
\tau_{i+\lfloor g\rfloor}-\tau_{i}\leq cg
.

This is because, assuming
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{narrow}^{\prime}
, there exist integers
0<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, such that each
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
, so
\tau_{i_{j+1}}-\tau_{i_{j}}>cg
;
and each
\hat{S}(\tau_{i_{j}})>-\sqrt{g}
.
Thus there is
t_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\leq\tau_{i_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}}
,
and by further assuming that
\mathcal{E}_{spike}^{\prime}
does not hold we have that
B(t)<2\sqrt{g}
for any
t\in[0,t_{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}]
, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\prime}
holds.

We have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{spike}^{\prime}]<\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\mathbb{P}[\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}>\sqrt{g}]+\lfloor n/2\rfloor\mathbb{P}[\max_{t\in[0,\sqrt{g}]}B(t)>\sqrt{g}]<Cne^{-cg^{1/6}},

and

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{narrow}^{\prime}]\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-\lfloor g\rfloor}\mathbb{P}[\tau_{i+\lfloor g\rfloor}-\tau_{i}\leq cg]<Cne^{-cg}.

Thus we have that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\prime}]+\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{spike}^{\prime}]+\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{narrow}^{\prime}]<Ce^{-cm}
(since
n^{0.9}<g
and
m<n^{0.1}
), and the conclusion follows.
∎

Define
R:\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket\to\mathbb{R}
as
R(i)=(\log(n))^{9}\sqrt{i(n-i)/n}
.
The next result we need is on lower bounding the probability of
\{\overline{S}(i)-\alpha i\}_{i=0}^{n}
staying above
R
.

Lemma 5.5
.

We have
\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}(i)-\alpha i\geq R(i),\forall i\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket]>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{19}}
.

For its proof we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k<Cn
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{D}_{k,s}
be the event where
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)>-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k}
for each
i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket
, and
\overline{S}^{\prime}(k)\in[s,s+C\sqrt{k}]
.
We then have that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{k,s}]>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{18}}
, when
0\leq s\leq(\log(n))^{9}\sqrt{k}
.

Proof.

We assume that
n
is large enough, since otherwise the result follows by taking
C
large and
c
small enough.

When
k<C(\log(n))^{18}
, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{k,s}]>\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)\in[s,s+C\sqrt{k}],\forall i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket]>ce^{-Cs-C\sqrt{k}}>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{18}}
, and the conclusion holds.

Now we assume that
k\geq C(\log(n))^{18}
.
Recall that
X
is the random variable such that each
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)-\overline{S}^{\prime}(i-1)
has the same distribution as
X
.
Take
\theta\geq 0
such that
\frac{\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\theta X}]}{\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]}=\frac{s}{k}
.
By taking Taylor expansions, and using that
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
, we have that for
\theta
near zero,
|\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\theta X}]-\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\theta|<C\theta^{2}
, and
|\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]-1|<C\theta^{2}
.
Then since
\frac{s}{k}\leq\frac{(\log(n))^{9}}{\sqrt{k}}\leq c
, and
\frac{\mathbb{E}[Xe^{\theta X}]}{\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]}
is non-decreasing in
\theta
, we have
\frac{cs}{k}<\theta<\frac{Cs}{k}
.
We consider the random variable
Y
, where

\mathbb{P}[Y\in[y,y+{\rm d}y)]=\mathbb{P}[X\in[y,y+{\rm d}y)]e^{\theta y}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]^{-1}.

We then have
\mathbb{E}[Y]=\frac{s}{k}
. Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
be an infinite sequence of independent copies of
Y
, and
S^{*}(i)=\sum_{j=1}^{i}Y_{j}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{k,s}]\geq e^{-\theta(s+C\sqrt{k})}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]^{k}\mathbb{P}[0<S^{*}(k)-s<C\sqrt{k},S^{*}(i)>-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k},\forall i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket].

Using that
|\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]-1|<C\theta^{2}
, we have

e^{-\theta(s+C\sqrt{k})}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]^{k}>e^{-\theta(s+C\sqrt{k})-Ck\theta^{2}}>e^{-Cs^{2}/k-Cs/\sqrt{k}}>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{18}},

where the second inequality is by
\theta<\frac{Cs}{k}
, and the third inequality is by
s\leq(\log(n))^{9}\sqrt{k}
.

It now remains to prove
\mathbb{P}[0<S^{*}(k)-s<C\sqrt{k},S^{*}(i)>-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k},\forall i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket]>c
.
For each
i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket
we have

\mathbb{P}[S^{*}(i)<-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k}]\leq e^{-(\log(n))^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{-S^{*}(i)/\sqrt{k}}]=e^{-(\log(n))^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{-Y/\sqrt{k}}]^{i}.

Like estimating
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta X}]
above, we have
|\mathbb{E}[e^{-Y/\sqrt{k}}]-(1-\mathbb{E}[Y]/\sqrt{k})|\leq C\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]/k<C/k
by taking the Taylor expansion of
e^{-Y/\sqrt{k}}
.
Thus we have
\mathbb{P}[S^{*}(i)<-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k}]<e^{-(\log(n))^{2}-\mathbb{E}[Y]\sqrt{k}+C}<Ce^{-(\log(n))^{2}}
.
By taking a union bound over
i
we have
\mathbb{P}[S^{*}(i)>-(\log(n))^{2}\sqrt{k},\forall i\in\llbracket 0,k\rrbracket]>\frac{7}{8}
, as
k<Cn
and
n
is large enough.
We also have that
\mathbb{P}[0<S^{*}(k)-s<C\sqrt{k}]>1/4
when
C
is large, by a local limit theorem.
Thus the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

The general idea is to do a dyadic decomposition of
\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket
from both ends, and apply Lemma
5.6
.
We assume that
n
is large enough, since otherwise the statement holds obviously.
Let
j_{0}
be the largest integer with
2^{j_{0}+2}<n
, and assume that
j_{0}>1

\overline{S}^{\prime}
:

1.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{l}
denote the following event: for each

there is
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)\geq R(i)
; and for each

2.

Similarly, we denote
\mathcal{D}_{r}
as the event where
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)\leq-R(i)
for each

, and
-10R(2^{j})-C2^{j/2}<\overline{S}^{\prime}(2^{j})<-10R(2^{j})
for each

.

3.

Denote
\mathcal{D}_{c}
as the event where there exists some

such that
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)<-5R(2^{j_{0}}+i)
.

We can now write

\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}(i)-\alpha i\geq R(i),\forall i\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket]>\iint\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{c}^{c},\overline{S}^{\prime}(n-2^{j_{0}+1})+x+y\in[0,{\rm d}z)]/{\rm d}z\\
\times\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{l},\overline{S}^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}})\in[x,x+{\rm d}x)]\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{r},\overline{S}^{\prime}(2^{j_{0}})\in[y,y+{\rm d}y)].

(5.1)

We note that for the integral in the right hand side, it is actually over
x,y
with
x,-y\in(10R(2^{j_{0}}),10R(2^{j_{0}})+C2^{j_{0}/2})
, and
|x+y|<C2^{j_{0}/2}
.
For such
x,y
, by a local limit theorem
(and recall that
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{i}
is the
i

\overline{S}^{\prime}
) we have

\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}^{\prime}(n-2^{j_{0}+1})+x+y\in[0,{\rm d}z)]/{\rm d}z=\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{n-2^{j_{0}+1}}(-x-y)>cn^{-1/2}.

(5.2)

We also write

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{c},\overline{S}^{\prime}(n-2^{j_{0}+1})+x+y\in[0,{\rm d}z)]/{\rm d}z\leq\int\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{c,l},\overline{S}^{\prime}(\lfloor n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rfloor)\in[z,z+{\rm d}z)]\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rceil}(-x-y-z)\\
+\int\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{c,r},\overline{S}^{\prime}(\lceil n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rceil)+x+y\in[z,z+{\rm d}z)]\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lfloor n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rfloor}(-z),

where
\mathcal{D}_{c,l}
denotes the event where there exists some
i\in\llbracket 0,\lfloor n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rfloor\rrbracket
such that
\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)<-5R(2^{j_{0}}+i)
, and
\mathcal{D}_{c,r}
denotes the event where there exists some
i\in\llbracket 0,\lceil n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rceil\rrbracket

.

, we have

\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)<-5R(2^{j_{0}}+i)]\leq e^{-5R(2^{j_{0}}+i)/\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}[e^{-\overline{S}^{\prime}(i)/\sqrt{n}}]=e^{-5R(2^{j_{0}}+i)/\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}[e^{-X/\sqrt{n}}]^{i}<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{9}},

and via a union bound over
i
we get
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{c,l}]<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{9}}
.
Similarly we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{c,r}]<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{9}}
.
By a local limit theorem we have
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lceil n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rceil}(-x-y-z),\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{\lfloor n/2-2^{j_{0}}\rfloor}(-z)<Cn^{-1/2}
for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
We then conclude that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{c},\overline{S}^{\prime}(n-2^{j_{0}+1})+x+y\in[0,{\rm d}z)]/{\rm d}z<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{9}}
.
By plugging this and (

) into (

) we get

\mathbb{P}[\overline{S}(i)-\alpha i\geq R(i),\forall i\in\llbracket 0,n\rrbracket]>cn^{-1/2}\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{l}]\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{r}].

By applying Lemma
5.6
repeatedly, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{l}]>ce^{-Cj_{0}(\log(n))^{18}}>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{19}}
; and by similar arguments we can have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{D}_{r}]>ce^{-C(\log(n))^{19}}
. By plugging these bounds into the previous expression, the conclusion follows.

5.2
Fluctuation of the point-to-line profile

In this subsection we prove Proposition
3.10
, using the Gibbs property (Lemma
5.3
) and the estimates given by Lemma
5.4
,
5.5
.

Proof of Proposition
3.10
.

In this proof the constants
c,C>0
would depend on
\psi
, and (as usual) the values can change from line to line.

We consider the following two events.
We let
\MP_{1}
be the event where there exist
m
points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}
, such that

1.

ad(w_{i+1})-ad(w_{i})>g
, for each
i=1,\ldots,m-1
,

2.

T_{\mathbf{0},w_{i}}+T_{w_{i}+(1,0),u}>\max_{w\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}T_{\mathbf{0},w}+T_{w+(1,0),u}-\sqrt{g}
, for each
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Similarly, we let
\MP_{2}
be the event where there exist
m
points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}
, such that

1.

ad(w_{i+1})-ad(w_{i})>g
, for each
i=1,\ldots,m-1
,

2.

T_{\mathbf{0},w_{i}}+T_{w_{i}+(0,1),u}>\max_{w\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}T_{\mathbf{0},w}+T_{w+(0,1),u}-\sqrt{g}
, for each
i=1,\ldots,m
.

We then have that
\MP(\mathbf{0},u;h,g,2m)\subset\MP_{1}\cup\MP_{2}
.
By symmetry it suffices to bound
\mathbb{P}[\MP_{1}]
.

For each
l<r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote
\MP[l,r]
as the event where there exists
(l,h-l)=w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}=(r,h-r)\in\mathbb{L}_{h}
, satisfying the two conditions in defining the event
\MP_{1}
.
We let
\mathcal{F}
be the
\sigma
-algebra, generated by
\{W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},v}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h+1}}
,
T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}
,
T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}
, and
\{W^{2}_{v,u}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}
,
T_{(l+1,h-l),u}
,
T_{(r+1,h-r),u}
.
Note that
\{W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},v}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h+1}}
is determined by
\{\xi_{v}:d(v)\leq h\}
, and
\{W^{2}_{v,u}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}
is determined by
\{\xi_{v}:d(v)\geq h+1\}
; thus
\{W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},v}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h+1}}
,
T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}
,
T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}
are independent of
\{W^{2}_{v,u}\}_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}
,
T_{(l+1,h-l),u}
,
T_{(r+1,h-r),u}
.

Denote
\underline{A}(i)=\frac{(r-i)T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}+(i-l)T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}}{r-l}
,
we then let
\underline{\mathcal{G}}
be the event where

W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i,h+1-l-i)}\vee W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i+1,h-l-i)}<\underline{A}(i)+(\log(n))^{8}\sqrt{i(r-l-i)/(r-l)},\quad\forall i\in\llbracket 0,r-l\rrbracket.

Also denote
\overline{A}(i)=\frac{(r-i)T_{(l+1,h-l),u}+(i-l)T_{(r+1,h-r),u}}{r-l}
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
be the event where

W^{2}_{(l+i,h-l-i),u}\vee W^{2}_{(l+i+1,h-l-i-1),u}<\overline{A}(i)+(\log(n))^{8}\sqrt{i(r-l-i)/(r-l)},\quad\forall i\in\llbracket 0,r-l\rrbracket.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
be the event where
|T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}+T_{(r+1,h-r),u}-T_{(l+1,h-l),u}|<\sqrt{g}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be the event where

|T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}|,|T_{(r+1,h-r),u}-T_{(l+1,h-l),u}|<C(r-l)
.
Note that
\underline{\mathcal{G}},\overline{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}
are all
\mathcal{F}
measurable, and their definitions rely on
l,r
.

Recall that
S:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
is the random walk where each step
S(i)-S(i-1)\sim\LAP(0,2)
.
Let
\underline{T},\overline{T}:\llbracket 0,r-l\rrbracket\to\mathbb{R}
be two independent copies of
S
on
\llbracket 0,r-l\rrbracket
, conditioned on that
\underline{T}(r-l)=T_{\mathbf{0},(r,h-r)}-T_{\mathbf{0},(l,h-l)}
and
\underline{T}(r-l)=T_{(r+1,h-r),u}-T_{(l+1,h-l),u}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{T}
be the event where
\underline{T}(i)+\overline{T}(i)<\sqrt{g}
for each
0\leq i\leq r-l
, and there exist integers
0=i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}=r-l
, such that
i_{j+1}-i_{j}>g
and
\overline{T}(i_{j})+\underline{T}(i_{j})>-\sqrt{g}
for each
j
.
Via Lemma
5.3
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}[\MP[l,r]|\mathcal{F}]<&\mathds{1}[\mathcal{E}_{1}]\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{T}|\mathcal{F}]\\
&\times\mathbb{P}[\underline{T}(i)\geq W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i,h+1-l-i)}\vee W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i+1,h-l-i)},\forall 0\leq i\leq r-l|\mathcal{F}]^{-1}\\
&\times\mathbb{P}[\overline{T}(i)\geq W^{2}_{(l+i,h-l-i),u}\vee W^{2}_{(l+i+1,h-l-i-1),u},\forall 0\leq i\leq r-l|\mathcal{F}]^{-1}.\end{split}

We now assume that
r-l<(\log(n))^{2}h^{2/3}
.
By Lemma
5.4
, we have
\mathds{1}[\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2}]\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{T}|\mathcal{F}]<Ce^{-cm}
.
By Lemma
5.5
, we have

\mathds{1}[\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2}\cap\underline{\mathcal{G}}]\mathbb{P}[\underline{T}(i)\geq W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i,h+1-l-i)}\vee W^{2}_{\mathbf{0},(l+i+1,h-l-i)},\forall 0\leq i\leq r-l|\mathcal{F}]^{-1}<Ce^{C(\log(n))^{19}},

and

\mathds{1}[\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2}\cap\overline{\mathcal{G}}]\mathbb{P}[\overline{T}(i)\geq W^{2}_{(l+i,h-l-i),u}\vee W^{2}_{(l+i+1,h-l-i-1),u},\forall 0\leq i\leq r-l|\mathcal{F}]^{-1}<Ce^{C(\log(n))^{19}}.

Thus we conclude that
\mathds{1}[\mathcal{E}_{2}\cap\underline{\mathcal{G}}\cap\overline{\mathcal{G}}]\mathbb{P}[\MP[l,r]|\mathcal{F}]<Ce^{-cm}
.

We now take
p
as the largest integer with
\frac{p}{h-p}\leq\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{devi}
be the event where there exist
p-\log(n)h^{2/3}/2<i<j<p+\log(n)h^{2/3}/2
, such that

|(T_{\mathbf{0},(i,h-i)}-D_{(i,h-i)})-(T_{\mathbf{0},(j,h-j)}-D_{(j,h-j)})|>(\log(n))^{7}\sqrt{j-i},

or

|(T_{(i+1,h-i),u}-D_{u-(i+1,h-i)})-(T_{(j+1,h-j),u}-D_{u-(j+1,h-j)})|>(\log(n))^{7}\sqrt{j-i}.

Note that
\mathcal{E}_{devi}^{c}
implies
\mathcal{E}_{2},\underline{\mathcal{G}},\overline{\mathcal{G}}
for each
l<r
with
p-l,r-p<\log(n)h^{2/3}/2
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{trans}
be the event where

\max_{|i-p|\geq\log(n)h^{2/3}/2}T_{\mathbf{0},(i,h-i)}+T_{(i+1,h-i),u}>\max_{w\in\mathbb{L}_{h}}T_{\mathbf{0},w}+T_{w+(1,0),u}-\sqrt{g}.

By summing over all pairs of
l<r
with
p-l,r-p<\log(n)h^{2/3}/2
, and taking the expectation over
\mathcal{F}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\MP_{1}]<\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{devi}]+\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{trans}]+Ce^{-cm}.

Finally, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|i-p|\geq\log(n)h^{2/3}/2
, there is
D_{i,h-i}+D_{u-(i+1,h-i)}<D_{p,h-p}+D_{u-(p+1,h-p)}-c(\log(n))^{2}n^{1/3}
.
By Theorem
2.1
and a union bound, we get
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{trans}]<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{3}}
.
By Lemma
2.3
we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{devi}]<Ce^{-c(\log(n))^{2}}
. Thus the conclusion follows.
∎

Appendix A
Gibbs property via RSK correspondence

In this appendix we prove Theorem
5.2
.
We shall prove an analog of it for LPP with geometric weights, using the RSK correspondence, and then pass to a scaling limit.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
, and we consider i.i.d. geometric random variables
\{\hat{\xi}_{u}\}_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
with odds
\beta
.
For any
u\preceq v
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we let
\hat{W}_{u,v}^{k}
be the analog of
W_{u,v}^{k}
for these
\{\hat{\xi}_{u}\}_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
; and we denote
\hat{W}_{v}^{k}=\hat{W}_{\mathbf{0},v}^{k}
when
\mathbf{0}\preceq v
.

Let
\mathscr{T}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,b):a\geq 0,b\geq 0,a+b\leq n+1\}}
. Let
\mathscr{I}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
be the set consisting of all
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
satisfying
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\leq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k}
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\geq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k+1}
, for any

(a,b,k),(a+1,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}
;
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\leq p_{(a,b+1)}^{k}
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\geq p_{(a,b+1)}^{k+1}
, for any

(a,b,k),(a,b+1,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}
.
We let
F:\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{I}
be the function such that
F:\{\hat{\xi}_{(a,b)}\}_{a\geq 0,b\geq 0,a+b\leq n+1}\mapsto\{\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
.

Lemma A.1
.

The map
F
is a bijection between
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{I}
.

From this lemma, and noting that
\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n+1}}\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}-\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}}\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}=\sum_{a\geq 0,b\geq 0,a+b\leq n+1}\hat{\xi}_{(a,b)}
, we get the distribution function for
\{\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}
.

Theorem A.2
.

For any non-negative
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}}\in\mathscr{I}
,
we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}=p_{(a,b)}^{k},\;\forall(a,b,k)\in I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}\right]=(1-\beta)^{(n+2)(n+3)/2}\beta^{\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n+1}}p_{(a,b)}^{k}-\sum_{(a,b,k)\in I_{n}}p_{(a,b)}^{k}}.

By sending
\beta\to 0
and rescaling, this implies Theorem
5.2
.
Now it remains to prove Lemma
A.1
.

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

For each
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}
, let
I[A]=\{(a,b,k):(a,b)\in A,k\in\llbracket 1,1+a\wedge b\rrbracket\}
, and let
\mathscr{I}[A]
denote the set consisting of all
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[A]}
satisfying the interlacing condition, i.e.,
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\leq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k}
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\geq p_{(a+1,b)}^{k+1}
, for any

(a,b,k),(a+1,b,k)\in I[A]
; and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\leq p_{(a,b+1)}^{k}
and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}\geq p_{(a,b+1)}^{k+1}
, for any
(a,b,k),(a,b+1,k)\in I[A]
.

Take
i\in\llbracket 0,n+1\rrbracket
, let
B_{i}=\{(a,i):0\leq a\leq n+1-i\}\cup\{(a,b):a+b\in\{n,n+1\},0\leq b\leq i\}
.
Then we would have
B_{n+1}=I_{n}\cup I_{n+1}
, and
\mathscr{I}[B_{n+1}]=\mathscr{I}
.
Also let
\mathscr{T}_{i}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,b):a\geq 0,b\geq i+1,a+b\leq n+1\}}
, where we regard
\mathscr{T}_{n+1}=\{0\}
.

We define a sequence of functions
F_{i}:\mathscr{T}\to\mathscr{T}_{i}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}]
, for each
0\leq i\leq n+1
, by

F_{i}:\{\hat{\xi}_{(a,b)}\}_{a\geq 0,b\geq 0,a+b\leq n+1}\mapsto\left(\{\hat{\xi}_{(a,b)}\}_{a\geq 0,b\geq i+1,a+b\leq n+1},\{\hat{W}_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in J_{i}}\right).

(A.1)

We would inductively prove that each of them is a bijection.
For
F_{0}
, it is a bijection since that for any
a\in\llbracket 0,n+1\rrbracket
, we have
\hat{W}_{(a,0)}^{1}=\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a}\hat{\xi}_{(a^{\prime},0)}
.
Now suppose that
F_{i}
is bijection. We will show that the function
F_{i+1}\circ F_{i}^{-1}
is also a bijection.

Denote
B_{i}^{\prime}=\{(a,i):0\leq a\leq n-i\}
and
B_{i}^{\prime\prime}=\{(a,i+1):0\leq a\leq n-i\}\cup\{(n-i,i)\}
. We now define a bijection between
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,i+1)\}_{a=0}^{n-i}}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
and
\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]
.

The function
G_{i}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,i+1)\}_{a=0}^{n-i}}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]\to\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]
.

Take
\{q_{(a,i+1)}\}_{a=0}^{n-i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,i+1)\}_{a=0}^{n-i}}
and
\{p_{(a,i)}^{k}\}_{(a,i,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]}\in\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
.
We define
q_{(a,b)}
for
(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i\rrbracket
:
when
a+b\leq i-1
we let
q_{(a,b)}=0
; let
q_{(a,i-a)}=p_{(a,i-a)}^{a+1}
for any
a\in\llbracket 0,(n-i)\wedge i\rrbracket
; and let
q_{(a,b)}=p_{(a,i)}^{i+1-b}-p_{(a-1,i)}^{i+1-b}
for
a+b\geq i+1
.

For each
(a,b,k)\in I[\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket]
we let
l_{(a,b)}^{k}=\max_{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in\gamma_{j}}q_{u}
, where the maximum is over all mutually disjoint up-right paths
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
contained in
\llbracket 0,a\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket
; and let
p_{(a,b)}^{1}=l_{(a,b)}^{1}
, and
p_{(a,b)}^{k}=l_{(a,b)}^{k}-l_{(a,b)}^{k-1}
for
k\geq 2
.
Then we recover
p_{(a,i)}^{k}
for each
(a,i,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]
.
This is because, from our construction of
\{q_{(a,b)}\}_{(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket}
and the interlacing condition, for any
(a,i,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]
, the
k
mutually disjoint maximum paths can be taken as the
k
rows in
\llbracket 0,a\rrbracket\times\llbracket i+1-k,i\rrbracket
.

Obviously
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]}
satisfies the interlacing condition and is in
\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]
.
We then define

G_{i}:\left(\{q_{(a,i+1)}\}_{a=0}^{n-i},\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]}\right)\mapsto\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]}.

Denote
B_{i}^{+}=\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\{i+1\}\cup\{n-i\}\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket
.
The array
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{+}]}
is also equivalent to a pair of semi-standard Young tableaux
U,V
of the same shape, obtained by applying RSK correspondence to
\{q_{(a,b)}\}_{(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket}
.
For each
(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket
there is a Young diagram of at most
1+a\wedge b
rows, denoted as
Y_{(a,b)}
, where there are
p_{(a,b)}^{k}
boxes in row
k
.
The Young tableaux
U,V
would have shape
Y_{(n-i,i+1)}
.
For each box in
Y_{(n-i,i+1)}
, if
j
is the smallest number such that the box is also in
Y_{(j,i+1)}
, then we write
j+1
in this box in
U
;
if
j^{\prime}
is the smallest number such that the box is also in
Y_{(n-i,j^{\prime})}
, then we write
j^{\prime}+1
in this box in
V
(see e.g.
[
29
]
).
We can also define a Young tableaux
U^{\prime}
with shape
Y_{(n-i,i)}
.
For each box in
Y_{(n-i,i)}
, if
j
is the smallest number such that the box is also in
Y_{(j,i)}
, then we write
j+1
in this box for
U^{\prime}
.
This
U^{\prime}
is also (the first Young tableaux in) the RSK correspondence of
\{q_{(a,b)}\}_{(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i\rrbracket}
.
It is known that from
U^{\prime}
, we can get
U
by doing
q_{(a,i+1)}
times Schensted insertions of
a+1
, sequentially for
a=0,\ldots,n-i
.

The function
H_{i}:\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,i+1)\}_{a=0}^{n-i}}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
.

Take any
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]}\in\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]
. For any
b\in\llbracket 0,i-1\rrbracket
, take

p_{(n-i,b)}^{k}=\begin{cases}p_{(n-i,i)}^{k+i-b},\;&k+i-b\leq 1+(n-i)\wedge i;\\
0,\;&k+i-b>1+(n-i)\wedge i.\end{cases}

(A.2)

The array
\{p_{a,b}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{+}]}
is in
\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{+}]
and can be written as a pair of Young tableaux of the same shape.
Applying the inverse of RSK we get non-negative integers
\{q_{(a,b)}\}_{(a,b)\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,i+1\rrbracket}
. For
(a,i,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]
we let
p_{(a,i)}^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{a}q_{(j,i+1-k)}
.

To define
H_{i}
there are several things we need to verify. First, note that
B_{i}^{\prime\prime}\cap B_{i}^{\prime}=\{(n-i,i)\}
, so we verify that we recover
\{p_{(n-i,i)}^{k}\}_{k=1}^{1+(n-i)\wedge i}
. Second, we verify
\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]}\in\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
.
Define
l_{(n-i,b)}^{k}=\max_{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in\gamma_{j}}q_{u}
, where the maximum is over all
k
mutually disjoint up-right paths contained in
\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,b\rrbracket
.
Then (due to the inverse of RSK) we have
l_{(n-i,b)}^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{(n-i,b)}^{j}
.
From (
A.2
) we have that
l_{(n-i,b-1)}^{b}+l_{(n-i,b+k-1)}^{k}=l_{(n-i,b+k-1)}^{b+k}
, for any
0<b<b+k\leq i+1
. This implies that
l_{(n-i,b+k-1)}^{k}=\sum_{a=0}^{n-i}\sum_{b^{\prime}=b}^{b+k-1}q_{(a,b^{\prime})}
,
for any
0\leq b<b+k\leq i+1
.
Thus
\sum_{j=0}^{n-i}q_{(j,i+1-k)}=l_{(n-i,i)}^{k}-l_{(n-i,i)}^{k-1}
and recovers
p_{(n-i,i)}^{k}
.
Also we get that for any
a\in\llbracket 0,n-i\rrbracket
and
b\in\llbracket 1,i\rrbracket
, there is
\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a-1}q_{(a^{\prime},b)}\geq\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a}q_{(a^{\prime},b-1)}
, implying that
\{p_{(a,i)}^{k}\}_{(a,i,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]}\in\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
.

We then define

H_{i}:\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime\prime}]}\mapsto\left(\{q_{(a,i+1)}\}_{a=0}^{n-i},\{p_{(a,b)}^{k}\}_{(a,b,k)\in I[B_{i}^{\prime}]}\right).

It is straight forward to check that
G_{i}\circ H_{i}
and
H_{i}\circ G_{i}
are identity maps, so
G_{i}
is a bijection.

Finally, for the function
F_{i+1}\circ F_{i}^{-1}
from
\mathscr{T}_{i}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}]
to
\mathscr{T}_{i+1}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i+1}]
, it is in fact
G_{i}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\{(a,i+1)\}_{a=0}^{n-i}}\times\mathscr{I}[B_{i}^{\prime}]
, because it can be realized as the same sequence of Schensted insertions on these coordinates; and it is the identity map on other coordinates.
Thus
F_{i+1}
is also a bijection, and the induction closes. By taking
F=F_{n+1}
the conclusion follows.
∎

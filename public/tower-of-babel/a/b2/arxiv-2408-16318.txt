Quaternary Legendre pairs II
Ilias S. Kotsireas, Christoph Koutschan, Arne Winterhof

Abstract
Quaternary Legendre pairs are pertinent to the construction of quaternary Hadamard matrices and have many applications, for example in coding theory and communications. In contrast to binary Legendre pairs, quaternary ones can exist for even length ℓ \ell as well. It is conjectured that there is a quaternary Legendre pair for any even ℓ \ell . The smallest open case until now had been ℓ = 28 \ell=28 , and ℓ = 38 \ell=38 was the only length ℓ \ell with 28 ≤ ℓ ≤ 60 28\leq\ell\leq 60 resolved before. Here we provide constructions for ℓ = 28 , 30 , 32 \ell=28,30,32 , and 34 34 . In parallel and independently, Jedwab and Pender found a construction of quaternary Legendre pairs of length ℓ = ( q − 1 ) / 2 \ell=(q-1)/2 for any prime power q ≡ 1 mod 4 q\equiv 1\bmod 4 , which in particular covers ℓ = 30 \ell=30 , 36 36 , and 40 40 , so that now ℓ = 42 \ell=42 is the smallest unresolved case. The main new idea of this paper is a way to separate the search for the subsequences along even and odd indices which substantially reduces the complexity of the search algorithm. In addition, we use Galois theory for cyclotomic fields to derive conditions which improve the PSD test.



Keywords. Legendre pair, Hadamard matrix, discrete Fourier transform, power spectral density, autocorrelation, cyclotomic field, Galois theory

Introduction

Two sequences

Preliminaries

2.1
Quaternary Legendre pairs

Let
A=[a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}]
,
B=[b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}]

be a quaternary Legendre pair of even length
\ell
.

Balance

Put

\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}a_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}b_{j}.

By
[
6
, Lemma 1]
we may assume w.l.o.g.

\alpha=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=1+i.

(2)

DFT and the PSD-test

For an integer
n\geq 1
we denote by
\xi_{n}
the primitive
n
th root of unity

\xi_{n}=e^{2\pi i/n}.

Note that we may fix any other primitive
n
th root of unity instead.

The
discrete Fourier transform

\operatorname{DFT}(A,s)
of
A
at
s

is

\operatorname{DFT}(A,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}a_{j}\xi_{\ell}^{js},\quad s=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1,

and the
power spectral density

\operatorname{PSD}(A,s)
of
A
at
s
is

\operatorname{PSD}(A,s)=|\operatorname{DFT}(A,s)|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\operatorname{PAF}(A,j)\xi_{\ell}^{js}.

A quaternary Legendre pair
(A,B)
satisfies

\operatorname{PSD}(A,s)+\operatorname{PSD}(B,s)=2\ell+2,\quad s=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1.

(3)

For efficiency, we search for possible candidate sequences
A
and
B
separately.
An important tool for this separation is the following
PSD-test
. The nonnegativity of the PSD and
(
3
) imply that we must have

\operatorname{PSD}(A,s),\operatorname{PSD}(B,s)\leq 2\ell+2,\quad s=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1,

(4)

which is an analog of the PSD-test for binary Legendre pairs introduced in
[
2
]
. Criterion (
4
) allows us to discard many sequences because they cannot be part of a Legendre pair.
(Note that we have
\operatorname{PSD}(A,\ell-s)=\operatorname{PSD}(\overline{A},s)
which for binary sequences reduces the PSD-test
(\ref{PSDtest})
to
s=1,2,\ldots,\frac{\ell-1}{2}
. However, for quaternary sequences we need to check all
s=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1
.)

2.2
Norms in cyclotomic fields

Let

\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})=\bigl\{a(\xi_{n})=a_{0}+a_{1}\xi_{n}+\dots+a_{\varphi(n)-1}\xi_{n}^{\varphi(n)-1}:a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{\varphi(n)-1}\in\mathbb{Q}\bigr\}

denote the
n
th
cyclotomic field
,
where
\varphi(n)
is Euler’s totient function.
For background on Galois theory and cyclotomic fields see for example
[
7
,
10
]
.

A condition for integers of the form
|\alpha|^{2}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})

We recall the following well-known condition, see for example
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
which follows from
[
9
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
satisfies
|\alpha|^{2}=m
for an integer
m
, and that
p
is a rational prime dividing the square-free part of
m
. Denote by
f
the order of
p
modulo
n
if
\gcd(p,n)=1
. Then either

•

f
is odd or

•

f
is even and
p^{f/2}\not\equiv-1\bmod n
.

For
n=4
we get the sum of two squares theorem.

Lemma 2
.

Let
m=|\alpha|^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[i]
, then every rational prime divisor
p
of the square-free part of
m
is either
p=2
or
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
.

For
n=q>2
a prime, Lemma
1
simplifies to the following condition.

Lemma 3
.

Let
q>2
be a prime.
If
m=|\alpha|^{2}
is an integer for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{q})
, then for every rational prime divisor
p
of the square-free part of
m
we have either
p=q
or the order of
p
mod
q
is odd.

For example, if
q\equiv 3\bmod 4
, then the eligible
p
with
p\not=q
are equivalent to a quadratic residue modulo
q
, and if
q=2^{s}+1
is a Fermat prime, then either
p=q
or
p\equiv 1\bmod q
.

Norms and automorphisms in cyclotomic fields

The
automorphisms

\psi_{j}
of
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
are

\psi_{j}(a(\xi_{n}))=a(\xi_{n}^{j}),\quad\gcd(j,n)=1,

and the group
G_{n}
of automorphisms is isomorphic to the multiplicative group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{*}
of the residue class ring
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
modulo
n
.

The
absolute norm
of
a(\xi_{n})
is

N(a(\xi_{n}))=\prod_{\psi\in G_{n}}\psi(a(\xi_{n}))=\prod_{\textstyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j=1}{\gcd(j,n)=1}}^{n-1}\kern-10.0pta(\xi_{n}^{j})\ \in\mathbb{Q}

and if
a(\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{n}]
we get
N(a(\xi_{n}))\in\mathbb{Z}.

By the
Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory
there is a bijection between the subgroups
U
of
G_{n}
and the subfields
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
:

U\mapsto\mathbb{F}=\operatorname{Fix}(U)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n}):\psi(\xi)=\xi\text{ for all }\psi\in U\}

and

\mathbb{F}\mapsto U=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})=\{\psi\in G_{n}:\psi(\xi)=\xi\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathbb{F}\},

respectively.
The
relative norm
of
a(\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
in
\operatorname{Fix}(U)
is

N_{U}(a(\xi_{n}))=\prod_{\psi\in U}\psi(a(\xi_{n}))\ \in\operatorname{Fix}(U).

In particular, if
n\equiv 0\bmod 4
, then
\mathbb{Q}(i)
is a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})

and for

U_{4}=\{\psi_{4j+1}:\gcd(4j+1,n)=1\}

we get

N_{U_{4}}(a(\xi_{n}))=\prod_{\textstyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j=0}{\gcd(4j+1,n)=1}}^{n/4-1}\kern-16.0pta(\xi_{n}^{4j+1})\ \in\mathbb{Q}(i),

(5)

since
i^{4j+1}=i
.
Moreover, if some prime
p>2
divides
n
, then
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p})
is a subfield of

\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
and for

U_{p}=\{\psi_{jp+1}:\gcd(jp+1,n)=1\}

we get

N_{U_{p}}(a(\xi_{n}))=\prod_{\textstyle\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j=0}{\gcd(jp+1,n)=1}}^{n/p-1}\kern-16.0pta(\xi_{n}^{jp+1})\ \in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p}),

since
\xi_{p}^{jp+1}=\xi_{p}
.

We have
\psi_{n-1}\not\in U_{4}
and
\psi_{n-1}\not\in U_{p}
for any prime divisor
p>2
of
n
since

n-1\equiv-1\bmod 4p
. Note that

\psi_{n-1}(a(\xi_{n}))=\overline{a(\xi_{n})},\quad\mbox{and}\quad a(\xi_{n})\psi_{n-1}(a(\xi_{n}))=|a(\xi_{n})|^{2},

and by the transitivity of the norm we get

N(a(\xi_{n}))=\bigl|N_{U_{4}}(a(\xi_{n}))\bigr|^{2}.

In addition, write

b(\xi_{p})=N_{U_{p}}(a(\xi_{n}))\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p})

to obtain

N(a(\xi_{n}))=\left|\prod_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}b(\xi_{p}^{j})\right|^{2},

that is, the absolute norm of
b(\xi_{p})\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p})
equals the absolute norm of
a(\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{n})
, again by the transitivity of the norm.
Hence, for any
a(\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{n}]
and for any prime divisor
p>2
of
n
, we have

N(a(\xi_{n}))=|\alpha_{4}|^{2}=|\alpha_{p}|^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}

(6)

for some
\alpha_{4}\in\mathbb{Q}(i)
and some
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{p})
.

General ideas for even
\ell

Let
\ell
be even.
Conditions on
\operatorname{PSD}\left(A,\frac{\ell}{2}\right)
and
\operatorname{PSD}\left(B,\frac{\ell}{2}\right)
can be used to separate the search for
[a_{0},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell-2}]
and
[a_{1},a_{3},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}]
, or
[b_{0},b_{2},\ldots,b_{\ell-2}]
and
[b_{1},b_{3},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}]

, and let

\alpha_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{2j},\qquad\alpha_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_{2j+1},

(7)

\beta_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}b_{2j},\qquad\beta_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}b_{2j+1}.

(8)

Obviously, by (
2
) we have

\alpha=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}=0,\qquad\beta=\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}=1+i.

We get

\operatorname{PSD}(A,k)=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}a_{j}(-1)^{j}\right|^{2}=|\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{1}|^{2}=4|\alpha_{0}|^{2}=4|\alpha_{1}|^{2}

and

\operatorname{PSD}(B,k)=|\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}|^{2}=|2\beta_{0}-1-i|^{2}=|2\beta_{1}-1-i|^{2}.

In particular,

\operatorname{PSD}(A,k)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{PSD}(B,k)\quad\mbox{are sums of two integer squares}.

(9)

Let
N_{b}
be the number of elements

in the subsequence
[a_{0},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell-2}]
.
Then

|\alpha_{0}|^{2}=(N_{1}-N_{-1})^{2}+(N_{i}-N_{-i})^{2}\equiv N_{1}+N_{-1}+N_{i}+N_{-i}\equiv k\bmod 2

(10)

and thus

\operatorname{PSD}(A,k)\equiv 4k\equiv 2\ell\bmod 8.

(11)

The conditions
(\ref{sumPSD})
,
(\ref{A_2})
, and
(\ref{4k})
can now be used to construct a list of eligible pairs
\bigl(\operatorname{PSD}(A,k),\operatorname{PSD}(B,k)\bigr)
. This allows us to
separate the search for
[a_{0},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell-2}]
and
[a_{1},a_{3},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}]
as well as
for
[b_{0},b_{2},\ldots,b_{\ell-2}]
and
[b_{1},b_{3},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}]
.

\operatorname{PSD}\left(A,\frac{\ell}{d}\right)=\left|\operatorname{DFT}\left(A,\frac{\ell}{d}\right)\right|^{2}

5
Legendre pairs for
\ell=28,30,32
and
34

In this section, we report some computational findings, namely quaternary Legendre pairs of the previously open lengths
28,30
, and
32
, that were made possible by the theoretical results presented in the previous sections. Our strategy is as follows: we fix an eligible pair
\bigl(\operatorname{PSD}\bigl(A,\frac{\ell}{2}\bigr),\operatorname{PSD}\bigl(B,\frac{\ell}{2}\bigr)\bigr)
, from which we can deduce the possible choices for
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\beta_{0},\beta_{1}
(see Section
3
). Fix one such choice and generate the set
S_{0}
(resp.
S_{1}
) of all
\{+1,-1,+i,-i\}
-sequences of length
\ell/2
whose sum equals
\alpha_{0}
(resp.
\alpha_{1}
). Then
S_{0}\times S_{1}
(with suitable interlacing applied to each member) forms a set of candidate
A
-sequences. The same is done analogously and independently for the
B
-sequences. Certain optimizations can be implemented, such as modding out cyclic shifts and constant multiples of sequences, and precomputing the DFTs of the subsequences in
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
so that the DFT of a member of
S_{0}\times S_{1}
is obtained by a single addition.

The main point of our investigations, and the reason why such searches become feasible at all, is the fact that many of these candidate sequences can be filtered out at an early stage, i.e., before it is tried to find a corresponding partner sequence for a Legendre pair. In particular, we have the following tests at our disposal:

(T1)

the PSD test (
4
),

(T2)

the
\operatorname{PSD}\bigl(\cdot,\frac{\ell}{4}\bigr)
and
\operatorname{PSD}\bigl(\cdot,\frac{3\ell}{4}\bigr)
tests (
13
) in the case that
\ell
is divisible by
4
,

(T3)

a set of product tests that arise from the results of Section
4
.

We store only those candidate
A
- and
B
-sequences that pass all tests, and then try to find matches between them that form Legendre pairs.

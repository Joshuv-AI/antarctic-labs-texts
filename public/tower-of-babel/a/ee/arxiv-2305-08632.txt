Surfaces defined by pairs of polynomials
D. Gvirtz-Chen, A.N. Skorobogatov

Abstract
We prove that the Brauer group of the generic diagonal surface of arbitrary degree is trivial. The same method is applied to surfaces whose equation can be written as the sum of two bilinear forms. This uses a general criterion for the triviality of the transcendental Brauer group of an isotrivial variety. We determine the field of definition of the geometric Picard group of the Fermat surface of arbitrary degree, thus finishing work of Shioda and Aoki.

Introduction

The starting point of the present investigation was our desire to compute the Brauer group
of the
generic
diagonal surface. We have found that for a field
k_{0}
of characteristic zero
and algebraically independent variables
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}

the Brauer group of the surface given by

x_{0}^{d}+t_{1}x_{1}^{d}+t_{2}x_{2}^{d}+t_{3}x_{3}^{d}=0

,

) diagonal surface over a number field

It turns out that our method can be applied to more general surfaces

given by

,
where

and

are homogeneous forms of degree

[

]

[

,

,

Without loss of generality, consider the surface

X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
given by the equation

f(x_{1}/x_{0})x_{0}^{d}=ag(x_{3}/x_{2})x_{2}^{d},

(1)

where

Recall the standard notation

The spectral sequence (

Define the field
L_{f,g}
as the compositum of

and

, where

is given by

.

Although our method works whenever

For a finite group

For

we denote by

We shall use the following notation for an irreducible polynomial

with a root

\xi\in\bar{k}
,

. Let

be the maximal Galois subextension of

Define

.
Then

is the normal closure of

in

.
The abelianisation

of the maximal abelian subextension of

Let

the kernel of the restriction map

{\rm H}^{3}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\rm prim}\cong(G_{f}^{\rm ab}\otimes G_{g}^{\rm ab})^{*}

Let

be the composition of the following maps

{\rm{Hom}}((G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}\otimes(G_{g}/N_{g})^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)\hookrightarrow(G_{f}^{\rm ab}\otimes G_{g}^{\rm ab})^{*}

\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm H}^{3}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\rm prim}\hookrightarrow{\rm H}^{3}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm inf}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\bar{k}^{\times}),

where the last arrow is induced by the map sending

to

Alternatively, one can describe

The Künneth formula for group homology and the universal coefficients formula
give rise to an isomorphism
{\rm H}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{Z}}/d)_{\rm prim}\cong{\rm{Hom}}(G_{f}^{\rm ab}\otimes G_{g}^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)

Then

is the composition

{\rm{Hom}}((G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}\otimes(G_{g}/N_{g})^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)\hookrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}(G_{f}^{\rm ab}\otimes G_{g}^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)

\tilde{\longrightarrow}{\rm H}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{Z}}/d)_{\rm prim}\hookrightarrow{\rm H}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{Z}}/d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm inf}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{2}(k,{\mathbb{Z}}/d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup[a]}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm H}^{3}(k,\mu_{d})\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\bar{k}^{\times}).

The equivalence of the two descriptions of

Let
\varphi\in{\rm{Hom}}((G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}\otimes(G_{g}/N_{g})^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)
.
Using the canonical surjective function
G_{f}/S_{f}\to(G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}
and a similar map for

,
we obtain from
\varphi
a function

\widetilde{\varphi}\colon(G_{f}/S_{f})\times(G_{g}/S_{g})\to{\mathbb{Z}}/d
.
Define

\Pi_{f,g}\subset{\rm{Hom}}((G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}\otimes(G_{g}/N_{g})^{\rm ab},{\mathbb{Z}}/d)

as the subgroup of functions

such that

\sum_{y\in G_{g}/S_{g}}\widetilde{\varphi}(a,y)=\sum_{x\in G_{f}/S_{f}}\widetilde{\varphi}(x,b)=0

for any
a\in G_{f}/S_{f}
and any
b\in G_{g}/S_{g}
.

Theorem 1.1

Let
f
and
g
be irreducible monic polynomials in
k[t]
of degree
d

whose splitting fields are linearly disjoint over
k
.
Let
X
be the surface
(\ref{X})
, where
a\in k^{\times}
is such that
[L_{f,g}(\sqrt[d]{a}):L_{f,g}]=d
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)
We have
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))\cong\Pi_{f,g}
.
If the orders of
(G_{f}/N_{f})^{\rm ab}
and
(G_{g}/N_{g})^{\rm ab}
are coprime, then

{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))=0
.

(ii)
If at least one of
f
and
g
has primitive Galois group,
then
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))=0
except when
d
is an odd prime
and both polynomials have cyclic Galois groups.

(iii)
The differential
d_{1,1}\colon{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))\to{\rm H}^{3}(k,\bar{k}^{\times})

can be identified with
\Delta
,
hence there is an isomorphism
{\rm{Br}}_{1}(X)/{\rm{Br}}_{0}(X)\cong\Pi_{f,g}\cap{\rm{Ker}}(\Delta)
.

It is noteworthy that the proof of this theorem uses neither any knowledge of
the group
{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}})
(like an integral basis or the Galois action), nor of the field
L_{f,g}
.

Recall that a transitive subgroup
H\subset S_{d}
is called
primitive

if the stabiliser of a point is a maximal subgroup of
H
. Thus, under the assumptions
of Theorem
1.1
, we have
{\rm{Br}}_{1}(X)={\rm{Br}}_{0}(X)
if at least one of

G_{f}
and
G_{g}
is the symmetric group
S_{d}
for
d\geq 3
, or the alternating group
A_{d}
for

d\geq 4
.
The same conclusion holds if
d
is prime and at least one of
G_{f}
and
G_{g}
is not isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
, hence singling out the cyclic case as special.

For any
d
, if
G_{f}\simeq G_{g}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/d
, then (i) gives that
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/d
if
d
is odd, and
{\mathbb{Z}}/(d/2)
if
d
is even. This applies to the exceptional
case in (ii).

Diagonal surfaces

As mentioned above, a case of particular interest is that of diagonal surfaces,
where we can take
f(t)=t^{d}+b
and
g(t)=t^{d}+c
for
b,c\in k^{\times}
.
These surfaces can be regarded as twisted forms of
the Fermat surface
F\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{\mathbb{Q}}
:

x_{0}^{d}+x_{1}^{d}+x_{2}^{d}+x_{3}^{d}=0.

(3)

Let
L
be the field of definition of
{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
,
that is, the smallest extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}

such that
{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/L)
acts trivially on
{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
.
We have
L=L_{f,g}
, where
f(t)=g(t)=t^{d}+1
. To make our next result explicit
we determine the field
L
for any

via a Größencharacter computation, thereby completing previous work of Shioda and Aoki

[
Sh81
,
Aok83
,
ASh83
]
, see §
5
.
Consider the diagonal surface
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
given by

x_{0}^{d}+a_{1}x_{1}^{d}+a_{2}x_{2}^{d}+a_{3}x_{3}^{d}=0,

(4)

where
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in k^{\times}
.
Let us denote by
k_{L}
the compositum

, and by
\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\rangle

the subgroup of

generated by the classes of

The following condition describes what we mean by ‘general’ diagonal surfaces:

\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\rangle\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/d)^{3}.

[formula]

Let
[a_{1}]\cup[a_{2}]\cup[a_{3}]\in{\rm H}^{3}(k,\mu_{d}^{\otimes 3})
be the triple cup-product.
Fixing a primitive
d
-th root of unity we get a map

{\rm H}^{3}(k,\mu_{d}^{\otimes 3})\to{\rm H}^{3}(k(\mu_{2d}),\mu_{d}^{\otimes 3})\to{\rm H}^{3}(k(\mu_{2d}),\bar{k}^{*}),

where the first arrow is restriction. Consider the following condition:

the order of the image of
[a_{1}]\cup[a_{2}]\cup[a_{3}]
in

{\rm H}^{3}(k(\mu_{2d}),\bar{k}^{*})
is
d
.

[formula]

Let
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{d})^{[2]}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{d})
be the invariant subfield of
the 2-torsion subgroup
({\mathbb{Z}}/d)^{\times}[2]
of
({\mathbb{Z}}/d)^{\times}\cong{\rm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{d})/{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Write
\chi\colon\Gamma_{k}\to({\mathbb{Z}}/d)^{\times}

for the cyclotomic character of degree

.

We have
2({\mathbb{Z}}/d)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/d
when

is odd and

2({\mathbb{Z}}/d)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/(d/2)
when

Theorem 1.2

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
be the diagonal surface
(\ref{eqX})

satisfying condition
(\star)
.
Then the following properties hold.

(i)

The group
{\rm H}^{1}(k,{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}}))

acts by

(ii)
If

[a_{1}]\cup[a_{2}]\cup[a_{3}]
in

For any

(iii)

The condition

Transcendental Brauer group

For some ‘isotrivial’ surfaces over a function field we can prove the triviality of the
transcendental Brauer group, see Theorem
4.2
. This implies

Theorem 1.4

Let
k_{0}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
f
and
g
be separable
polynomials in
k_{0}[t]
of degree
d
. Let
k=k_{0}(a)

be a purely transcendental extension of
k_{0}
of transcendence degree
1
, and
let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
be the surface
(\ref{X})
.
Then the map
{\rm{Br}}(X)\to{\rm{Br}}(X_{\bar{k}})
is zero. Moreover, if
f
and
g
are irreducible
with splitting fields linearly disjoint over
k_{0}

and at least one of them has primitive Galois group,
then
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(X)
is an isomorphism, except when
d
is an odd prime
and both polynomials have cyclic Galois groups.

Finally, we state the result mentioned in the beginning of the introduction.

Theorem 1.5

Let
k_{0}
be a field of characteristic zero and let
k=k_{0}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

be a purely transcendental extension of
k_{0}
of transcendence degree
3
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
be the diagonal surface
(\ref{eqX})
.
Then the map
{\rm{Br}}(k)\to{\rm{Br}}(X)
is an isomorphism.

In this case both conditions
(\star)
and
(\star\star)
hold.
This can be compared to ‘less generic’ diagonal surfaces, where these conditions
fail, see the examples at the end of §

.

Outline of paper

In §

Moreover, the field of definition of

and

We prove Theorems

and

in §

.

In §

and

Finally, §

contains results about the field of definition of

Reduction to
d^{2}

In this section
f(t)
and
g(t)
are separable monic polynomials of degree
d
with coefficients
in a field
k
of characteristic zero, and
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}_{k}
is the surface
given by (
1
), where
a\in k^{\times}
.
Define
Z\subset X
as the union of closed subsets

and

f(x_{1}/x_{0})x_{0}^{d}=0
. On

, the zero set of

is a smooth irreducible curve, while the zero set of

is the union of

.

Consider the subgroup
\Lambda\subset{\rm{Pic}}(X_{\bar{k}})
generated by the

d^{2}
lines contained in

.
The following lemma gives an explicit description of this Galois module.
In particular, it shows that the Galois module
\Lambda

does not depend on

Write

be the closed subscheme of the affine line

, and similarly

For a 0-dimensional

the free abelian group
whose generators bijectively correspond to the

is a permutational

-module. Let

be the sum of the generators.
We write

and
\sigma_{g}=\sigma_{V_{g}}
.

Lemma 2.1

We have a commutative diagram of

we infer that

{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{2d})\subset L
. By Shioda
[
Sh81
, Thm. 7]
,
{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Q}}
is generated by the classes of these lines if
d\leq 4
or
{\rm gcd}(6,d)=1
.

(ii) Let
S_{\rm ind}\subset S_{\flat}
be the subset of quadruples

(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
such that
a_{i}+a_{j}\neq d
for all
i\neq j
.
Let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})
be the subgroup generated by the
3d^{2}
lines. Then

{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E=(\Lambda^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E)\oplus\bigoplus_{\chi\in S_{\rm ind}}V_{\chi}.

Let
S_{\rm reg}\subseteq S_{\rm ind}
be the subset of quadruples which, possibly after a permutation, can be written in one of the following forms

\displaystyle(i,d/2+i,d-2i,d/2),\

\displaystyle i\neq d/4,

\displaystyle(i,d/2+i,d/2+2i,d-4i),\

\displaystyle i\neq d/3,d/4,d/6,

\displaystyle(i,d/3+i,2d/3+i,d-3i),\

\displaystyle i\neq d/6.

In
[
ASh83
, Thm. 1–3]
, Aoki and Shioda find for each
\chi\in S_{\rm reg}
an explicit equation describing a divisor whose class generates
V_{\chi}
. These divisors are defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{2d},2^{2/d})
(for
d
divisible by
2
) or
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{2d},3^{3/d})
(for
d
divisible by
3
) and have negative self-intersection, hence they are unique effective divisors in their classes.
By
[
Aok83
, Thm. C]
, if
d>180
and
\chi\in S_{\flat}
is primitive, then
\chi\in S_{\rm reg}
.
From the calculations in
[
Sh81
]
it then follows that
if
d
is not divisible by any of the integers between
12
and
180
in the table of

[
Sh81
, p. 727]
,
then
S_{\rm ind}=S_{\rm reg}
. The claim (ii) follows.
\Box

Proposition 5.3

Write
w=|(O_{E}^{\times})_{\rm tors}|
, so that
w=d
if
d
is even, and
w=2d
if
d
is odd. Let
\Delta
be the subgroup of
E^{\times}/E^{\times w}

generated by
O_{E}^{\times}
and the elements
1-\varepsilon^{d/p}

for all prime divisors
p|d
. Then
L
is contained in
K=E(\Delta^{1/w})
.

Proof.
By Proposition
5.2
(ii), it remains to determine the image of the action of
{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
on
V_{\chi}
for all
\chi=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in S_{\rm ind}\setminus S_{\rm reg}
.

It was first shown by Weil
[
Wei52
, Theorem]
that
h_{\chi}
is a Größencharacter of conductor dividing
d^{2}
, hence dividing
c\coloneq 2w^{2}
. Since
{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
acts on
V_{\chi}\subset{\rm{Pic}}(F_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E
through a finite abelian quotient, it follows by
[
Neu99
, Ch. VII, Prop. 6.9]
that
h_{\chi}
factors through a generalised Dirichlet character
h^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\chi}
modulo
c
.

By class field theory, the action of
{\rm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/E)
on
V_{\chi}
thus factors through the Galois group
{\rm{Gal}}(E(c)/E)\simeq{\rm Cl}(c)
of the ray class field
E(c)
of conductor
c
. By Lemma
5.4
below, we have
K\subset E(c)
and hence the Artin map
a_{K/E}:I(c)\to{\rm{Gal}}(K/E)
factors through a homomorphism
a^{\mathrm{fin}}_{K/E}:{\rm Cl}(c)\to{\rm{Gal}}(K/E)
. It remains to show that

{\rm{Ker}}(a^{\mathrm{fin}}_{K/E})\subset{\rm{Ker}}(h^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\chi})

(23)

in the finite ray class group
{\rm Cl}(c)
. For any given
\chi
, the determination of
{\rm{Ker}}(h^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\chi})
is a finite calculation with Jacobi sums which can be performed as in
[
Wat18
, §4.1]
with customary computer algebra packages (see Remark
5.5
); and so is the determination of
{\rm{Ker}}(a^{\mathrm{fin}}_{K/E})
using the characterisation of the Artin map of a Kummer extension from
[
Fie01
, §2]
.

It suffices to verify (
23
) for each primitive
\chi\in S_{\rm ind}\setminus S_{\rm reg}
and all
d
.
By the aforementioned
[
Aok83
, Thm. C]
, such
\chi
may only exist for those
12\leq d\leq 180
that are listed in the table of
[
Sh81
, p. 727]
. Checking this finite list of
\chi
completes the proof of (iii).
\Box

Lemma 5.4

We have
K\subset E(c)
.

Proof.
It is enough to show that if

x\in O_{E}
divides
d\geq 3
, then the conductor of the Kummer extension

E(\sqrt[w]{x})
of
E
divides
c
.
The relative discriminant
\Delta_{E(\sqrt[w]{x})/E}
divides the relative discriminant ideal of the polynomial
t^{w}-x
, which is
({w}^{w}x^{{w}-1})
.
A formula in Hasse’s lectures on class field theory
[
Has67
, Sätze (163), (164)]
says that the conductor of
E(\sqrt[w]{x})
divides

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid{w}}\mathfrak{p}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|{w}}\mathfrak{p}^{\left(v_{p}({w})+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)e(\mathfrak{p}/p)+1},

where both products are taken over all primes
\mathfrak{p}\subset O_{E}
dividing
\Delta_{E(\sqrt[w]{x})/E}
, and
e(\mathfrak{p}/p)
is the ramification index of
\mathfrak{p}
over the underlying prime number
p
.
The first product is vacuous. Now if
v_{p}(w)>1
or
p>2
, then
e(\mathfrak{p}/p)/(p-1)+1\leq v_{p}(w)e(\mathfrak{p}/p)
. If
p=2
and
v_{2}(w)=1
, then
\left(v_{2}({w})+\frac{1}{2-1}\right)e(\mathfrak{p}/2)+1=2v_{2}(w)e(\mathfrak{p}/2)+1
. Thus the conductor of
E(\sqrt[w]{x})
divides

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|{w}}\mathfrak{p}^{2v_{p}(w)e(\mathfrak{p}/p)}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|2}\mathfrak{p},

hence divides
c
.
\Box

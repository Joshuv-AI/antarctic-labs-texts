Affine Noetherian algebras, filtrations and presentations
Be’eri Greenfeld

Abstract
. Resco and Small gave the first example of an affine Noetherian algebra which is not finitely presented. It is shown that their algebra has no finite-dimensional filtrations whose associated graded algebras are Noetherian, affirming their prediction. A modification of their example yields countable fields over which ‘almost all’ (that is, a co-countable continuum of) affine Noetherian algebras lack such a filtration, and an answer to a question suggested by Irving and Small is derived.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16P40

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a field and let
R
be an
F
-algebra with a finite-dimensional filtration
F\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\cdots
, that is, for all
i,j
we have
\dim_{F}\mathcal{F}_{i}<\infty
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}\mathcal{F}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{i+j}
. We always assume that our filtrations are exhaustive, namely,
R=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}_{i}
. It is well known that if the associated graded algebra:

{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=\mathcal{F}_{0}+\frac{\mathcal{F}_{1}}{\mathcal{F}_{0}}+\frac{\mathcal{F}_{2}}{\mathcal{F}_{1}}+\cdots

is Noetherian then
R
is Noetherian as well, but the converse is false. It was asked by McConnell and Robson in 1987 whether every affine Noetherian algebra admits
any
finite-dimensional filtration with respect to which the associated graded algebra is Noetherian (aka ‘Noetherian filtration’), see
[
9
, 8.3.10]
. The first counterexample to this question was given by Stephenson and Zhang
[
15
]
; they proved that graded Noetherian algebras have subexponential growth, and hence any affine Noetherian algebra of exponential growth forms a counterexample to the aforementioned question. Later, counterexamples were found among PI-algebras
[
14
]
, which are thus of polynomially bounded growth.

In 1992, Resco and Small
[
11
]
gave the first example of an affine Noetherian algebra which does not remain Noetherian under a suitable scalar extension; interestingly, over an uncountable algebraically closed field such examples do not exist
[
3
]
(for more on the importance of Noetherian stability under scalar extensions, see
[
2
]
; there are plenty of non-affine examples, for instance, if
K/F
is a non-finitely generated field extension then
K\otimes_{F}K
is not Noetherian
[
16
]
). Resco and Small also proved that their example is non-finitely presented. To the best of our knowledge, the only known examples of affine Noetherian algebras having these two unexpected properties are of the kind of the Resco-Small algebras.
Motivated by the McConnell-Robson problem, Resco and Small computed the associated graded algebra of their ring for various filtrations and asked if it admits a Noetherian filtration. We remark that their algebra has subexponential (in fact, intermediate) growth, so the Stephenson-Zhang argument does not apply.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be an algebra and let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite-dimensional filtration. If
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is Noetherian then
R
is finitely presented.

As a consequence, we affirm the suspicion of Resco and Small and answer their question:

Corollary 1.2
.

The Resco-Small algebra has no finite-dimensional filtration whose associated graded algebra is Noetherian.

In 1986, Irving and Small
[
6
]
investigated necessary conditions for representability of PI-algebras, and as a consequence of their work they proved that over a countable field there are only countably many isomorphism classes of affine Noetherian PI-algebras (in retrospect, this can be deduced also from Anan’in’s representability result
[
1
]
or from the fact that affine Noetherian PI-algebras are finitely presented
[
4
, Theorem 10.1.2]
, both proven later). They suggested that the following question is reasonable: over a countable field, are there only countably many isomorphism classes of affine Noetherian (not necessarily PI) algebras?
Using a further modification of the Resco-Small argument, we resolve this question in the negative:

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a countable field
F
and a continuum of pairwise non-isomorphic affine Noetherian
F
-algebras.

Combined with Theorem
1.1
, we get that over certain fields, ‘almost all’ affine Noetherian algebras do not afford a finite-dimensional Noetherian filtration:

Corollary 1.4
.

There exists a countable field over which there is a continuum of non-isomorphic affine Noetherian algebras without Noetherian filtrations, and only countably many non-isomorphic affine algebras which admit a Noetherian filtration.

This gives (another) resounding negative answer to McConnell-Robson’s question on the existence of Noetherian filtrations.

2.
Graded algebras

We start with a slight generalization of a theorem of Lewin
[
7
, Theorem 17]
. Lewin proved that if
U\triangleleft F\left<x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\right>
is an ideal of a free algebra which is homogeneous with respect to the standard grading and
F\left<x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\right>/U
is Noetherian then
U
is finitely generated as a two-sided ideal. Lewin’s proof works mutatis-mutandis for any grading of the free algebra, as long as it remains connected (that is,
S_{0}=F
):

Theorem 2.1

(Lewin’s Theorem for non-standard connected gradings)
.

Let
S=F+S_{1}+\cdots
be a finitely generated connected graded
F
-algebra and
\dim_{F}S_{i}<\infty
for all
i\geq 1
. If
S
is Noetherian then it is finitely presented.

In fact, we will see that one can omit the connectedness assumption:

Theorem 2.2

(An extended version of Lewin’s theorem)
.

Let
S=S_{0}+S_{1}+\cdots
be a finitely generated graded
F
-algebra and
\dim_{F}S_{i}<\infty
for all
i\geq 0
. If
S
is Noetherian then it is finitely presented.

The following seems to be well-known and definitely proven in various sources for commutative rings, but we give here a proof of this folklore for completeness.

Lemma 2.3
.

A non-negatively graded Noetherian
F
-algebra with finite-dimensional homogeneous components is affine over
F
.

Proof.

Let
R=R_{0}+R_{1}+\cdots
be a graded Noetherian algebra with
\dim_{F}R_{i}<\infty
. Suppose that
I=R_{1}+R_{2}+\cdots
is generated as a left ideal by
R_{1}+\cdots+R_{k}
(this is true for some
k
by Noetherianity). We claim that each
R_{m}\subseteq F\left<R_{0}\cup\cdots\cup R_{k}\right>
. Work by induction. Pick
m>k
and let
\alpha\in R_{m}
. Then
\alpha\in R\cdot\left(R_{1}+\cdots+R_{k}\right)
so we can write:

\alpha=\sum_{i_{1}}a_{1,i_{1}}\cdot r_{1,i_{1}}+\cdots+\sum_{i_{k}}a_{k,i_{k}}\cdot r_{k,i_{k}}

with each
a_{l,i_{l}}\in R
homogeneous and
r_{l,i_{l}}\in R_{l}
. Since
\deg(\alpha)=m
, we may assume that all
a_{l,i_{l}}\in R_{0}\cup\cdots\cup R_{m-1}
, and the claim follows by the induction hypothesis.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Consider
T=F+S_{1}+S_{2}+\cdots\subseteq S
, a connected graded subalgebra. Obviously
S_{0}
is a finite-dimensional subalgebra of
S
, so
T
is a finite-codimensional subalgebra of
S
. By
[
13
, Lemma 1.4]
, it follows that
T
is Noetherian as well.
By Lemma
2.3
, the algebra
T
is also affine, say, generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. As a connected graded affine Noetherian algebra, by Theorem
2.1
it follows that
T
is finitely presented. Since
S
is a finitely generated
T
-module, it is a homomorphic image of an algebra of the form
T^{\prime}=T\left<e_{1},\dots,e_{p}\right>/I
, where
I
is the ideal generated by (finitely many) relations expressing each
e_{i}e_{j}
and
e_{i}t_{j}
as a
T
-linear combination of
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}
(with coefficients given as polynomials in
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
). Then
T^{\prime}
is a finitely presented algebra (recalling that
T
is) and a finitely generated
T
-module, hence Noetherian, so every homomorphic image of it – in particular
S
– is finitely presented as well.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite-dimensional filtration of an algebra
R
. If
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is finitely presented then so is
R
.

Proof.

This is proven in
[
8
]
for filtrations arising from powers of a generating subspace; let us generalize the argument. Fix a set of homogeneous generators of
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
, say,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
of degrees
d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
respectively. Any system of lifts
\hat{a}_{1},\dots,\hat{a}_{n}\in R
of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, whose leading terms with respect to
\mathcal{F}
agree with
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, generates
R
. Let
A=F\left<x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right>
be the
n
-generated free algebra. We have a presentation:

0\rightarrow I\rightarrow F\left<x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right>\xrightarrow{\pi}R\rightarrow 0

given by
\pi(x_{i})=\hat{a}_{i}
. Now grade
A=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}A_{d}
by
\deg(x_{i})=d_{i}
and let
\mathcal{G}
be the corresponding filtration (namely,
\mathcal{G}_{d}=A_{0}+\cdots+A_{d}
).
Notice that
\pi(\mathcal{G}_{d})=\mathcal{F}_{d}
.
Consider the surjective morphism of graded algebras
\varphi\colon A\rightarrow{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
given by
\varphi(x_{i})=a_{i}
. Now
{\operatorname{Ker}}(\varphi)
is a homogeneous ideal of
A
. Let
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\operatorname{Ker}}(\varphi)\cap A_{d}
. Then
f(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is zero in
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
, which means that in
R
it holds that
f(\hat{a}_{1},\dots,\hat{a}_{n})\in\mathcal{F}_{d-1}
(for
d=0
, formally set
\mathcal{F}_{-1}=\mathcal{G}_{-1}=0
); equivalently,
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in I+\mathcal{G}_{d-1}
.
It follows that:

{\operatorname{Ker}}(\varphi)=I\cap\mathcal{G}_{0}+\frac{\left(I+\mathcal{G}_{0}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}_{1}}{\mathcal{G}_{0}}+\frac{\left(I+\mathcal{G}_{1}\right)\cap\mathcal{G}_{2}}{\mathcal{G}_{1}}+\cdots=:{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(I)

hence
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)\cong F\left<x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right>/{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(I)
. The assignment
J\mapsto{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(J)
, carrying ideals of the free algebra to graded ideals is order-preserving and injective on chains (see
[
10
, Proposition 6.7]
). Write
I=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}I_{i}
as an ascending union of finitely generated ideals. Then
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(I)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(I_{i})
, but since
{\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{G}}(I)

is finitely generated (recall that finite presentation holds for any finite set of generators), both chains must stabilize. Hence
I
is finitely generated as well, and
R
is finitely presented.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite-dimensional filtration of
R
. Write
S:={\operatorname{gr}}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=S_{0}+S_{1}+\cdots
and assume that it is Noetherian. By Theorem
2.2
,
S
is finitely presented. By Lemma
2.4
,
R
is finitely presented.
∎

Since the Resco-Small algebra is not finitely presented, it now evidently follows that it has no finite-dimensional filtrations whose associated graded algebras are Noetherian, thereby proving Corollary
1.2
.

3.
A continuum of Noetherian algebras

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Fix a prime number
p
. Let
K=\mathbb{F}_{p}(t_{0},t_{1},\dots)
be the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in countably many algebraically independent variables. For each sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
, set a derivation
\delta=\delta_{\lambda}\colon K\rightarrow K
by:

\delta_{\lambda}(t_{k})=t_{k+1}+\lambda_{k}t_{0}\ \ \text{for}\ k=0,1,\dots

This definition induces a well-defined derivation (e.g. see
[
12
, Page 287, Exercise 5]
).
Consider the associated differential polynomial ring
R=R_{\lambda}=K[x;\delta]
. Observe that
F=K^{p}\subseteq{\operatorname{Ker}}(\delta)
, so
F=\mathbb{F}_{p}(t_{0}^{p},t_{1}^{p},\dots)
is a central subfield of
R
. Obviously
F
is countable. Notice that
R
is
F
-affine, and in fact
R=F\left<t_{0},x\right>
. Indeed, assume by induction that
t_{0},\dots,t_{k}\in F\left<t_{0},x\right>
for some
k\geq 0
; then:

t_{k+1}=xt_{k}-t_{k}x-\lambda_{k}t_{0}\in F\left<t_{0},x\right>.

Moreover, observe that
K
is spanned over
F
by the monomials:

\{t_{i_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{r}}^{d_{r}}\ |\ r\geq 1,\ i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r},\ 0\leq d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\leq p-1\}

as an
F
-vector space. Hence
R=F\left<t_{0},x\right>
. Since
K
is a field, it immediately follows that
R
is a Noetherian ring by
[
5
, Theorem 2.6]
. Hence for each sequence
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
we obtain an affine Noetherian
F
-algebra
R_{\lambda}
as described above.

Fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
. For any
f\in R_{\lambda}
such that
f^{p}=t_{k}^{p}
, write
f=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}x^{i}
with all
c_{i}\in K
. If
c_{m}\neq 0
and
m>0
, it is easy to see that the highest non-zero term in
f^{p}
is
c_{m}^{p}x^{mp}
, so
m=0
and
f
lies in
K
. It is clear that the only
p
-th root of
t_{k}^{p}
in
K
is
t_{k}
itself.

Fix
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
. Let
\phi\colon R_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}R_{\lambda^{\prime}}
be an
F
-algebra isomorphism; in particular,
\phi(t_{k}^{p})=t_{k}^{p}
. By the above argument,
\phi
fixes
K
. Write
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}x^{i}
with all
c_{i}\in K
and
c_{m}\neq 0
. Then:

K[x;\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}]=K+K\phi(x)+K\phi(x)^{2}+\cdots

so we can write
x=\sum_{j=0}^{r}d_{j}\phi(x)^{j}
with all
d_{j}\in K
and
d_{r}\neq 0
. The highest non-zero term of the right hand side of the last equation is then
d_{r}c_{m}^{r}x^{mr}
, so
mr=1
and
m=1
. Now
\phi(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x
where
c_{1}\neq 0
. Then:

[\phi(x),t_{k}]=\phi(t_{k+1}+\lambda_{k}t_{0})=t_{k+1}+\lambda_{k}t_{0}

but:

[\phi(x),t_{k}]=[c_{1}x,t_{k}]=c_{1}xt_{k}-t_{k}c_{1}x=c_{1}\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}(t_{k})=c_{1}t_{k+1}+c_{1}\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t_{0}

Since
\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we always have that
t_{k+1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t_{0}\neq 0
, so:

c_{1}=\frac{t_{k+1}+\lambda_{k}t_{0}}{t_{k+1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t_{0}}

Take
k\gg 1
such that
c_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(t_{0},\dots,t_{k})
, then:

0=\frac{\partial c_{1}}{\partial t_{k+1}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+1}}\left(\frac{t_{k+1}+\lambda_{k}t_{0}}{t_{k+1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t_{0}}\right)=\frac{t_{0}(\lambda^{\prime}_{k}-\lambda_{k})}{(t_{k+1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{k}t_{0})^{2}}

hence
\lambda_{k}=\lambda^{\prime}_{k}
.

Identify two sequences
\lambda\sim\lambda^{\prime}
if they agree for almost all slots. Since
|\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}/\sim~|=2^{\aleph_{0}}
, the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

This is straightforward by Theorem
1.3
and Lemma
2.4
, as over a countable field there are only countably many isomorphism classes of finitely presented algebras.
∎

Nuclear mass and size corrections to the magnetic shielding
Krzysztof Pachucki

Abstract
We derive finite nuclear mass and finite nuclear size corrections to the magnetic shielding in light ions. These corrections are important for the accurate determination of nuclear magnetic moments. We correct several previous formulas for the nuclear mass corrections and present improved results for the magnetic shielding in 1 H, 3 He + , and 3 He. Finally, we obtain an 3 He atomic magnetic moment, which serves as an accurate probe to measure magnetic fields.



I
Introduction

The nuclear magnetic moment in atoms is partially shielded by atomic electrons.
This effect is not very significant: about
10^{-5}
for light elements. Nevertheless, because
nuclear magnetic moments are determined from the Zeeman shift in atomic systems,
the calculation of the magnetic shielding is necessary for their accurate determination.
For example, the recent measurement of the magnetic moment of the
3
He
+
ion
Schneider et al. 2022

together with the calculation of magnetic shielding
Wehrli et al. 2021
;
Wehrli et al. 2022

allowed for the most accurate determination so far of the helion magnetic moment.
A similar measurement is planned for
9
Be
3+
, which will result in an improved determination
of the
9
Be nuclear magnetic moment
Mooser 2021
.
Moreover, accurate values for nuclear magnetic moments are important for the determination of atomic hyperfine splitting (HFS),
testing quantum electrodynamics (QED), and the nuclear structure theory
Karshenboim 2005
.
This is because HFS is very sensitive to the distribution of the magnetic moment within the nucleus.

In this work, we point out two interesting effects that are frequently overlooked in calculations
of nuclear magnetic shielding
Jaszuński et al. 2012
, namely, those due to the finite nuclear mass and the finite nuclear size.
Nuclear mass corrections are as large as relativistic corrections for light atomic systems,
while finite nuclear size effects are much smaller, but they are expected to be significant for heavier elements.
These finite nuclear mass effects have already been the subject of several works
Pachucki 2008
;
Rudziński et al. 2009
.
Here, we rederive them thoroughly, correct some mistakes, and update numerical values for the most relevant cases of
the H,
3
He
+
, and
3
He elements.
The finite nuclear size effects have been studied only numerically and only for hydrogen-like systems
Yerokhin et al. 2011
;
Yerokhin et al. 2012
.
Here, we derive a compact analytic formula in terms of the charge, magnetic, and effective Zemach nuclear radii,
which accounts also for nuclear inelastic effects.

II
Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian with the homogenous magnetic field

To account for finite nuclear mass effects, we have to treat nuclei on an equal footing with all electrons.
Therefore, we consider a system of charged particles, each having its own mass
m_{a}
, charge
e_{a}
, spin
s_{a}
,
and the so-called g-factor
g_{a}
, which is related to the magnetic moment by

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}_{a}=\frac{g_{a}\,e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,\vec{s}_{a}\,.

(1)

These particles are electrons with spin
1/2
and nuclei with an arbitrary spin.
Our derivation employs a Breit-Pauli Hamiltonian with homogenous magnetic field
and with separation of center of mass motion. It closely follows the lines of Ref.
Pachucki 2008
.
Let us therefore introduce the total mass
M

\displaystyle M=\sum_{a}m_{a}\,,

(2)

center of mass variables

\displaystyle\vec{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\frac{m_{a}}{M}\,\vec{r}_{a}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\vec{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\vec{p}_{a}\,,

(4)

and relative coordinates

\displaystyle\vec{x}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{r}_{a}-\vec{R}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\vec{q}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{a}-\frac{m_{a}}{M}\,\vec{P}\,,

(6)

such that

\displaystyle\bigl[x_{a}^{i}\,,\,q_{b}^{j}\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\delta^{ij}\,\biggl(\delta_{ab}-\frac{m_{b}}{M}\biggr)\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\bigl[R^{i}\,,\,P^{j}\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\,\delta^{ij}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\bigl[x_{a}^{i}\,,\,P^{j}\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl[R^{i}\,,\,q_{a}^{j}\bigr]=0\,.

(9)

The Hamiltonian of a bound system of charged particles in an external magnetic field including leading relativistic corrections
and with the separated-out center of mass motion is
Pachucki 2008

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{in}}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}\biggl\{\frac{\vec{\pi}_{a}^{2}}{2\,m_{a}}-\frac{e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,g_{a}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}-\frac{\vec{\pi}^{4}_{a}}{8\,m^{3}_{a}}+\frac{e_{a}}{8\,m^{3}_{a}}\,\Bigl[4\,\vec{\pi}_{a}^{2}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}+(g_{a}-2)\,\bigl\{\vec{\pi}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}\,,\,\vec{\pi}_{a}\cdot\vec{s}_{a}\bigr\}\Bigr]-\frac{e_{a}^{2}}{2}\,\chi_{a}\,\vec{B}^{2}\biggr\}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{a>b,b}\frac{e_{a}\,e_{b}}{4\,\pi}\,\biggl\{\frac{1}{r_{ab}}-\frac{1}{2\,m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,\pi_{a}^{i}\,\biggl(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{ab}}+\frac{r^{i}_{ab}\,r^{j}_{ab}}{r^{3}_{ab}}\biggr)\,\pi_{b}^{j}-\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,\langle r_{Ea}^{2}+r_{Eb}^{2}\rangle\,\delta^{3}(r_{ab})-\frac{2\,\pi\,g_{a}\,g_{b}}{3\,m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{s}_{b}\,\delta^{3}(r_{ab})

\displaystyle\ +\frac{g_{a}\,g_{b}}{4\,m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,\frac{s_{a}^{i}\,s_{b}^{j}}{r_{ab}^{3}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-3\,\frac{r_{ab}^{i}\,r_{ab}^{j}}{r_{ab}^{2}}\biggr)\biggr\}+\sum_{a,b}\frac{e_{a}\,e_{b}}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{1}{2\,r_{ab}^{3}}\biggl[\frac{g_{a}}{m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{r}_{ab}\times\vec{\pi}_{b}-\frac{(g_{a}-1)}{m_{a}^{2}}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{r}_{ab}\times\vec{\pi}_{a}\biggr]\,,

(10)

where
r_{ab}=|\vec{r}_{a}-\vec{r}_{b}|
, and

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}_{a}=

\displaystyle\ \vec{q}_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\,\vec{D}_{a}\times\vec{B}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\vec{D}_{a}=

\displaystyle\ e_{a}\,\vec{x}_{a}+\frac{m_{a}}{M}\,\vec{D}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\vec{D}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a}e_{a}\,\vec{x}_{a}\,.

(13)

For a point spin
s=1/2
particle,
g=2
,
\langle r_{E}^{2}\rangle=3/(4\,m^{2})
, and
\chi=1/(4\,m^{3})
.
For a finite size particle
\langle r_{E}^{2}\rangle
includes the mean square charge radius.
An equivalent Hamiltonian for a system of spin
1/2
point particles was originally obtained by Hegstrom in Ref.
Hegstrom 1973
.
Our Hamiltonian in Eq. (
10
), however, is valid for arbitrary spin particles and has a more compact form.

The magnetic interaction resulting from
H_{\mathrm{in}}
neglecting the terms quadratic in
\vec{B}
is

\displaystyle\delta H=

\displaystyle-\sum_{a}\frac{e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,(\vec{x}_{a}\times\vec{q}_{a}+g_{a}\,\vec{s}_{a})\cdot\vec{B}

\displaystyle+\sum_{a}\,\frac{1}{4\,m_{a}^{3}}\,\Bigl[q_{a}^{2}\,\vec{D}_{a}\times\vec{q}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}+2\,e_{a}\,q_{a}^{2}\,\vec{s}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}

\displaystyle+e_{a}\,(g_{a}-2)\,\vec{q}_{a}\cdot\vec{s}_{a}\,\vec{q}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}\Bigr]+\sum_{a\neq b,b}\,\frac{e_{a}\,e_{b}}{4\,\pi}\biggl[

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\,m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,q_{a}^{i}\biggl(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r_{ab}}+\frac{r_{ab}^{i}\,r_{ab}^{j}}{r_{ab}^{3}}\biggr)\,(\vec{D}_{b}\times\vec{B})^{\,j}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\,r_{ab}^{3}}\,\frac{g_{a}}{m_{a}\,m_{b}}\,(\vec{s}_{a}\times\vec{r}_{ab})\cdot(\vec{D}_{b}\times\vec{B})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\,r_{ab}^{3}}\,\frac{(g_{a}-1)}{m_{a}^{2}}\,(\vec{s}_{a}\times\vec{r}_{ab})\cdot(\vec{D}_{a}\times\vec{B})\biggr].

(14)

This is a general interaction Hamiltonian, which is valid for an arbitrary set of particles.
In particular, one can obtain the bound electron g-factor or the magnetic shielding in
atomic and molecular systems. In the next section we derive the atomic magnetic shielding
with full account of the nuclear mass.

III
Finite nuclear mass corrections

We will derive the magnetic shielding constant for arbitrary ions with the vanishing orbital angular momentum
\vec{L}
.
The interaction of the nuclear spin with the magnetic field is
obtained from Eq. (
14
) as

\displaystyle\delta H=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{e_{N}}{2\,m_{N}}\,g_{N}\,\vec{s}_{N}\cdot\vec{B}

\displaystyle+\frac{e_{N}}{4\,m_{N}^{3}}\,\Bigl[2\,q_{N}^{2}\,\vec{s}_{N}\cdot\vec{B}+(g_{N}-2)\,\vec{q}_{N}\cdot\vec{s}_{N}\;\vec{q}_{N}\cdot\vec{B}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{e_{N}\,e}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{(\vec{s}_{N}\times\vec{r}_{Nb})}{4\,r_{Nb}^{3}}

\displaystyle\ \cdot\biggl[\frac{g_{N}}{m_{N}\,m_{e}}\,\vec{D}_{b}\times\vec{B}-\frac{(g_{N}-1)}{m_{N}^{2}}\,\vec{D}_{N}\times\vec{B}\biggr],

(15)

where we assumed that for nucleus
a=N
, all other particles are electrons, and
\sum^{\prime}
denotes summation over electrons only.
For an electronic state with a spherical symmetry

\displaystyle(\vec{s}_{N}\times\vec{X})\cdot(\vec{Y}\times\vec{B})=-\frac{2}{3}\,\vec{s}_{N}\cdot\vec{B}\,\vec{X}\cdot\vec{Y}\,,

(16)

one introduces the scalar shielding constant
\sigma

\displaystyle\delta H=-\frac{g_{N}\,e_{N}}{2\,m_{N}}\,\vec{s}_{N}\cdot\vec{B}(1-\sigma)\,.

(17)

This
\sigma
is conveniently split into two parts, consisting of the first- and the second-order matrix elements

\displaystyle\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}\,.

(18)

\sigma_{1}
results from the first-order matrix element of
\delta H
in Eq. (
15
)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,g_{N}\,m_{N}^{2}}\,\Bigl[2+\frac{(g_{N}-2)}{3}\Bigr]\,\big\langle q_{N}^{2}\big\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{e_{e}}{4\,\pi}\bigg\langle\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{bN}}{r_{bN}^{3}}\cdot\biggl[\frac{1}{m_{e}}\,\vec{D}_{b}-\frac{(g_{N}-1)}{g_{N}\,m_{N}}\,\vec{D}_{N}\biggr]\bigg\rangle.

Because
\sum_{a}m_{a}\,\vec{x}_{a}=0
, the position
\vec{x}_{N}
of the nucleus with respect to mass center and the dipole operator
\vec{D}

can be expressed in terms of the electron coordinates only

\displaystyle\vec{x}_{N}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{m_{e}}{M}\,\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\,\vec{r}_{aN}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\vec{D}=

\displaystyle\ e_{e}\,\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{aN}\,\Bigl(1+(Z-N_{e})\,\frac{m_{e}}{M}\Bigr)\,,

(21)

where
M=m_{N}+N_{e}\,m_{e}
,
N_{e}
is the number of electrons, and
Z
is the nuclear charge in units of the elementary charge.
Consequently, the shielding constant
\sigma_{1}
takes the form

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{3\,m_{e}}\,\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{1}{r_{a}}\biggr\rangle+\frac{(4+g_{N})}{6\,g_{N}}\,\frac{\langle p_{N}^{2}\rangle}{m_{N}^{2}}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\alpha}{3}\,\bigg\langle\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{bN}}{r_{bN}^{3}}\cdot\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{aN}\bigg\rangle\,\frac{1}{g_{N}}\,\frac{m_{e}}{M}\,

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\frac{(Z-N_{e})}{M}+(1-g_{N})\,\bigg(\frac{1}{m_{e}}+\frac{Z}{m_{N}}\bigg)\biggr]\,,

(22)

where
\vec{p}_{N}=-\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{p}_{a}
, and we used
\vec{P}|\phi\rangle=0
.
Consider now the following matrix element

\displaystyle\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{aN}}{r_{aN}^{3}}\cdot\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{bN}\biggr\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{i\,Z\,\alpha}\,\biggl\langle[\vec{p}_{N}\,,\,H-E]\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{bN}\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{i\,Z\,\alpha}\,\bigl\langle\vec{p}_{N}\,[H-E\,,\,\vec{r}_{bN}]\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \langle p_{N}^{2}\rangle\,\frac{1}{Z\,\alpha}\,\frac{M}{m_{N}\,m_{e}}\,,

(23)

which is used to simplify
\sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{3\,m_{e}}\,\bigg\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{1}{r_{a}}\bigg\rangle+\frac{\langle p_{N}^{2}\,\rangle}{3\,g_{N}\,m_{N}^{2}}\,\biggl[3-\frac{g_{N}}{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{m_{N}}{M}\,\biggl(1-\frac{N_{e}}{Z}\biggr)+(1-g_{N})\,\frac{m_{N}}{Z\,m_{e}}\biggr]\,.

(24)

The
\sigma_{2}
part is given by the second-order interaction coming from the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\delta H=

\displaystyle\ -\sum_{a}\frac{e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,\vec{x}_{a}\times\vec{q}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}\,-\frac{e_{N}\,e_{e}}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{\vec{s}_{N}}{2\,m_{N}}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\frac{\vec{r}_{bN}}{r_{bN}^{3}}\times\biggl[g_{N}\,\frac{\vec{q}_{b}}{m_{e}}-(g_{N}-1)\,\frac{\vec{q}_{N}}{m_{N}}\biggr],

(25)

namely

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\,\biggl\langle\sum_{a}\frac{e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,\vec{x}_{a}\times\vec{q}_{a}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\,\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{e_{e}}{4\,\pi}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{bN}}{r_{bN}^{3}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\frac{\vec{p}_{b}}{m_{b}}-\frac{(g_{N}-1)}{g_{N}}\,\frac{\vec{p}_{N}}{m_{N}}\biggr]\biggr\rangle\,.

(26)

Using

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\frac{e_{a}}{2\,m_{a}}\,\vec{x}_{a}\times\vec{q}_{a}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e_{e}}{2\,m_{e}}\,\vec{L}+\biggl(\frac{e_{e}}{2\,m_{e}}+\frac{Z\,e_{e}}{2\,m_{N}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\frac{m_{e}}{M}\,\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{aN}\times\vec{p}_{N},

(27)

we arrive at

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{3\,M}\,\biggl(1+\frac{Z\,m_{e}}{m_{N}}\biggr)\,\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{aN}\times\vec{p}_{N}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)}\,\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\frac{\vec{r}_{bN}}{r_{bN}^{3}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\frac{\vec{p}_{b}}{m_{e}}-\frac{(g_{N}-1)}{g_{N}}\,\frac{\vec{p}_{N}}{m_{N}}\biggr]\biggr\rangle\,.

(28)

The total shielding constant is
\sigma=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
, where
\sigma_{1}
is given in Eq. (
24
) and
\sigma_{2}
in Eq. (
28
).
For the numerical calculations, it is convenient to apply the expansion in the mass ratio, which takes the form (in a.u.)

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{(2,0)}+\sigma^{(2,1)}+\ldots,

(29)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(2,0)}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha^{2}}{3}\,\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{r_{a}}\biggr\rangle\,,

(30)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(2,1)}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha^{2}}{3}\,\frac{m_{e}}{m_{N}}\biggl[\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\frac{1}{r_{a}}\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,p_{N}^{2}\biggr\rangle+\langle p_{N}^{2}\,\rangle\frac{(1-g_{N})}{Z\,g_{N}}

\displaystyle\ +\biggl\langle\sum_{a}{}^{\prime}\vec{r}_{a}\times\vec{p}_{N}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\sum_{b}{}^{\prime}\,\frac{\vec{r}_{b}\times\vec{p}_{b}}{r_{b}^{3}}\biggr\rangle\biggr],

(31)

where all matrix elements in the above are assumed with infinite nuclear mass,
and
\sigma^{(i,j)}
denotes the expansion term of order
\alpha^{i}\,(m_{e}/m_{N})^{j}
.
The last term in the above differs in sign from that derived previously in Ref.
Pachucki 2008
;
Rudziński et al. 2009
,
see Table I for the updated numerical values.

For the hydrogenic ion in
nS
state the nonrelativistic shielding constant, using Eq. (
24
), is

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{3\,n^{2}}\,\frac{m_{N}}{m_{N}+m_{e}}+\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,m_{e}^{2}}{3\,n^{2}\,g_{N}\,(m_{N}+m_{e})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\biggl[3-\frac{g_{N}}{2}+\frac{m_{N}}{m_{N}+m_{e}}\,\biggl(1-\frac{1}{Z}\biggr)-(g_{N}-1)\,\frac{m_{N}}{Z\,m_{e}}\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{3\,n^{2}\,g_{N}\,(1+x)^{2}}\,\biggl[\biggl(3-\frac{g_{N}}{2}+\frac{x}{1+x}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{x^{2}}{Z}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{1+x}+g_{N}\biggr)\biggr]\,,

(32)

where
x=m_{N}/m_{e}
. It is convenient to define
\delta g_{N}=-g_{N}\,\sigma

\displaystyle\delta g_{N}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{3\,n^{2}\,(1+x)^{2}}\,\biggl[\frac{g_{N}}{2}-3-\frac{x}{1+x}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{x^{2}}{Z}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{1+x}+g_{N}\biggr)\biggr]\,,

(33)

which is in agreement with the known formula for the electron g-factor in the S-state of the hydrogenic ion
Grotch and Hegstrom 1971
;
Pachucki 2008
,

\displaystyle\delta g_{e}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{3\,n^{2}\,(1+x)^{2}}\,\biggl[\biggl(\frac{g_{e}}{2}-3-\frac{x}{1+x}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ -Z\,x^{2}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{1+x}+g_{e}\biggr)\biggr]

(34)

with
x=m_{e}/m_{N}
, which verifies the new formula for the magnetic shielding in hydrogen-like ions.
Its small electron mass expansion takes the following form:

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Z\,\alpha^{2}}{3\,n^{2}}\,\biggl[1+\frac{m_{e}}{m_{N}}\,\bigg(\frac{1}{g_{N}}-2\bigg)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{m_{e}^{2}}{m_{N}^{2}}\,\bigg(\frac{4\,Z-3}{g_{N}}-\frac{Z}{2}+3\bigg)+\ldots\biggr]\,,

(35)

where the quadratic in the mass ratio term differs from that derived previously in Ref.
(
Wehrli et al. 2022
, Eq. (64))

due to the computational mistake.
As seen from Table I, the largest uncertainty for light ions comes from the relativistic
recoil correction
\sigma^{(4,1)}
, but this has not yet been studied in the literature.

IV
Finite nuclear size corrections

Let us pass now to another nuclear correction, which is due to the finite distribution of
the charge and the magnetic moment within the nucleus.
We will study this correction for hydrogenic ions only, but generalization for an arbitrary ion is straightforward.
For light hydrogenic ions this effect is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{fs}}=-\frac{Z\,\alpha^{2}}{3}\,\bigl[2\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,m^{2}\,(r_{C}^{2}+r_{M}^{2})+8\,(Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,m\,\tilde{r}_{Z}\bigr],

(36)

where
m=m_{e}
,
r_{C}
is the charge radius,
r_{M}
the magnetic radius, and
\tilde{r}_{Z}
the effective Zemach radius of the nucleus.
This formula is proved as follows.

The shift of nonrelativistic hydrogenic levels due to
r_{C}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta H=

\displaystyle\ e\,A^{0}-\frac{e}{6}\,r_{C}^{2}\,\vec{\nabla}\vec{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -Z\,\alpha\,\Bigl(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,r_{C}^{2}\Bigr)\,,

(37)

where
e=e_{e}
.
The finite nuclear size affects the nonrelativistic wave function, which in turn affects the
matrix elements for the nuclear magnetic shielding

\displaystyle\sigma_{C}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\frac{\alpha}{3\,m}\,\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,Z\,\alpha\,r_{C}^{2}\,\delta^{3}(r)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -2\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,m^{2}\,r_{C}^{2}\,\frac{Z\,\alpha^{2}}{3}\,.

(38)

To derive the contribution from the magnetic radius of the nucleus, let us rederive the leading shielding
that comes from the
e^{2}\,\vec{A}^{2}/(2\,m)
term in the kinetic energy of the electron

\displaystyle\delta E=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{2\,m}\,\Bigl\langle(\vec{B}\times\vec{r})\cdot\Bigl(\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{B}\,\frac{\alpha}{3\,m}\Bigl\langle\vec{r}\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\Bigr\rangle.

(39)

The shielding
\sigma
is thus given by

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\alpha}{3\,m}\,\Bigl\langle\vec{r}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\Big)\Bigr\rangle.

(40)

The magnetic radius
r_{M}
enters the magnetic interaction similarly to
r_{C}
in Eq. (
37
);
therefore, the shift due to the magnetic radius is

\displaystyle\sigma_{M}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{3\,m}\,\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,r_{M}^{2}\,\Bigl\langle\vec{r}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\Bigl(\delta^{3}(r)\Bigr)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{3\,m}\,\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,r_{M}^{2}\,(-3)\,\langle\delta^{3}(r)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -2\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,m^{2}\,r_{M}^{2}\,\frac{Z\,\alpha^{2}}{3}.

(41)

The calculation of the shift due to the Zemach radius
\tilde{r}_{Z}
is more complicated.

\tilde{r}_{Z}
represents the hyperfine anomaly, namely
(E_{\mathrm{hfs}}^{\mathrm{exp}}-E_{\mathrm{hfs}}^{\mathrm{point}})/E_{F}=-2\,Z\,\alpha\,m\,\tilde{r}_{Z}
, see Eq. (
55
).
If we assume that it comes exclusively from the
charge and magnetic moment distribution, it becomes
r_{Z}
given by Eq. (
56
),
which can only be derived from the Dirac equation.
Let us thus start derivation from the relativistic hyperfine splitting

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfs}}=

\displaystyle\ -e\,\Bigl\langle\psi^{\dagger}\Big|\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}\Big|\psi\Bigr\rangle,

(42)

where, for a point nucleus

\displaystyle\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}_{\mathrm{I}}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}.

(43)

In the nonrelativistic limit
E_{\mathrm{hfs}}
is given by the Fermi formula

\displaystyle E_{F}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{e}{2\,m}\,\big\langle\phi\big|\big\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p}\,,\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}\big\}\big|\phi\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big\langle\phi|\vec{\mu}_{e}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{I}}|\phi\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}\big\langle\phi|\vec{\mu}_{e}\cdot\vec{\mu}_{\mathrm{I}}\,\delta^{3}(r)|\phi\big\rangle.

(44)

We are now ready to consider the leading finite nuclear size correction
E_{Z}
to the hyperfine splitting

\displaystyle E_{Z}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\biggl\langle\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{\dagger}(0)\\
0\end{array}\!\bigg|(-e)\,\vec{\gamma}\cdot\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}\,\frac{1}{\not\!p-m}\,e\,\gamma^{0}\,A^{0}\bigg|\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi(0)\\
0\end{array}\!\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2\,e^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{1}{\vec{p}^{\,2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ \biggl\langle\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{\dagger}(0)\\
0\end{array}\!\bigg|\vec{\gamma}\cdot\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}(-\vec{p})\,(\not\!p+m)\,\gamma^{0}\,A^{0}(\vec{p})\bigg|\!\begin{array}[]{c}\phi(0)\\
0\end{array}\!\biggr\rangle,

where
p^{0}=m
and

\displaystyle A^{0}(\vec{p})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\,e}{\vec{p}^{\,2}}\,G_{E}(\vec{p}^{\,2}),

(53)

\displaystyle\vec{A}_{\mathrm{I}}(\vec{p})=

\displaystyle\ -i\,\vec{\mu}_{\mathrm{I}}\times\frac{\vec{p}}{p^{2}}\,G_{M}(\vec{p}^{\,2}),

(54)

with normalization
G_{E}(0)=G_{M}(0)=1
.
E_{Z}
can be simplified to

\displaystyle E_{\rm Z}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\,Z\,\alpha\,m}{\pi^{2}}\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{p^{4}}\,\biggl[G_{E}(p^{2})\,G_{M}(p^{2})-1\biggr]\,E_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -2\,Z\,\alpha\,m\,r_{\rm Z}\,E_{F}\,,

(55)

where

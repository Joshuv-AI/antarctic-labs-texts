Quantum hydrodynamics from local thermal pure states Preprint: RIKEN-QHP-497
Shoichiro Tsutsui, Masaru Hongo, Shintaro Sato, Takahiro Sagawa

Abstract
We provide a pure state formulation for hydrodynamic dynamics of isolated quantum many-body systems. A pure state describing quantum systems in local thermal equilibrium is constructed, which we call a local thermal pure quantum ( ℓ ​ TPQ \ell\mathrm{TPQ} ) state. We show that the thermodynamic functional and the expectation values of local operators (including a real-time correlation function) calculated from the ℓ ​ TPQ \ell\mathrm{TPQ} state converge to those from a local Gibbs ensemble in the large fluid-cell limit. As a numerical demonstration, we investigate a one-dimensional spin chain and observe the hydrodynamic relaxation obeying the Fourier’s law. We further prove the second law of thermodynamics and the quantum fluctuation theorem, which are also validated numerically. The ℓ ​ TPQ \ell\mathrm{TPQ} formulation gives a useful theoretical basis to describe the emergent hydrodynamic behavior of quantum many-body systems furnished with a numerical efficiency, being applicable to both the non-relativistic and relativistic regimes.

Local thermal pure quantum (
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
) state.
—

We first introduce the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state.
Suppose that the system possesses a set of conserved currents

\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}(t)
satisfying

\partial_{\mu}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}(t)\equiv\partial_{0}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}_{~a}(t)+\partial_{i}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)=0,

(1)

where
\mu,\nu,\cdots
denote spacetime indices, and
a,b,\cdots
label
conserved charges such as energy, momentum, and particle numbers
1
1

1

Summations over the repeated indices for Greek letters (
\mu,\nu,\cdots
)
are assumed throughout the paper.
The repeated indices for Latin letters is assumed to contain the
spatial integral, e.g.

\lambda^{a}\hat{c}_{a}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\lambda^{a}(\bm{x})\hat{c}_{a}(\bm{x})
where
d

denotes the spatial dimension.

.
Throughout the paper, we use the Heisenberg picture, and
Eq. (
1
) gives the equation of motion for conserved charge densities
\hat{c}_{a}(t)\equiv\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}_{~a}(t)
.
Then, we consider the system in local thermal equilibrium, which is described by local thermodynamic parameters
\lambda^{a}
conjugate to average charge densities
\langle\hat{c}_{a}\rangle
.
We assume that each fluid cell contains many degrees of freedom so that thermodynamic parameters are smooth functions of spatial coordinates.
In other words, we identify the current operators
\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}
in Eq. (
1
) as coarse-graining ones over a fluid-cell volume
V_{\mathrm{cell}}
.

To describe the locally thermalized system, we introduce the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state at time
t
by

\ket{\lambda_{t};t}\equiv\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\ket{\alpha_{t}},

(2)

with
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]\equiv\lambda^{a}(t)\hat{c}_{a}(t)
,
any orthonormal basis state vector
\ket{\alpha_{t}}
at time
t
, and
a complex random variable
z_{\alpha}\equiv(z_{\alpha}^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}z_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime})/\sqrt{2}

whose real/imaginary parts are taken from the normal distribution.
Note that we distinguish two arguments in
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]
; one for thermodynamic parameters
\lambda_{t}
, whose suffix denotes its configuration
\lambda_{t}=\lambda(t)
at time
t
, while the second argument
t
represents the time argument of charge densities as Heisenberg operators.
When the system only contains the energy as the conserved charge,

\hat{K}
reads
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\beta(t,\bm{x})\hat{h}(t,\bm{x})

with the local inverse temperature
\beta(t,\bm{x})
and energy density operator

\hat{h}(t,\bm{x})
, for instance.
The normalization of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state is defined by

Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]\equiv\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}
,
whose logarithm is identified as a thermodynamic functional.
Using these, we define the entropy-functional operator for the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state as

\hat{S}[\lambda_{t};t]=\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]+\log Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}],

(3)

and the average value over the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state as

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\equiv\frac{1}{Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]}\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\hat{\mathcal{O}}|\lambda_{t};t}.

(4)

Hydrodynamics and the quantum fluctuation theorem.
—

Based on the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state, we derive hydrodynamics and the quantum fluctuation theorem.
The hydrodynamic equation is regarded as the averaged Eq. (
1
)
over a certain initial density operator
\hat{\rho}_{0}
.
Here, we put a crucial assumption on the initial state: the system is in the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state at initial time
t_{0}
parametrized by
\lambda^{a}_{t_{0}}
.
Starting from this initial
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state, we shall consider the subsequent time-evolution of charge densities described by

\partial_{\mu}\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}(t)\rangle=0\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}(t)\rangle\equiv\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{~a}(t)\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t_{0}}},

(5)

for
t\geq t_{0}
.
To make Eq. (
5
) a closed set of equations,
we need a constitutive relation, which expresses
\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\rangle
by the dynamical variable
\langle\hat{c}_{a}(t)\rangle=\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{0}_{~a}(t)\rangle
.
Thus, our problem is to find the constitutive relation.

The vital point here is that we have a solution to this problem if we replace the initial
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
-state average with the LG-ensemble average over the density operator:

\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\equiv\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}}]}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]},

(6)

where we defined the partition functional for the LG ensemble as
Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}}]\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}
.
We will express the LG average as
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]\hat{\mathcal{O}}\big)
.
Thus, it is enough to show the equivalence between the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
and LG averages for the purpose of deriving hydrodynamics from the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state.

Following the similar treatment of Refs.
Sugiura and Shimizu 2012
;
Sugiura and Shimizu 2013
;
Hyuga et al. 2014
;
Endo et al. 2018
, we can indeed show such equivalence holds as convergence in probability
\xrightarrow{P}
w.r.t. the random variable
z_{a}

Sup
:

\displaystyle Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{P}Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}],

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{P}\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}},

(8)

where we took the large size limit of all fluid cells
V_{\mathrm{cell}}\to\infty
.
Therefore, the LG-based derivation developed in Refs.
Hayata et al. 2015
;
Hongo 2017
;
Hongo 2019
;
Hongo and Hattori 2021
also provides a derivation of hydrodynamics from the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state in the large fluid-cell limit.

We here summarize some consequences of Eqs. (
7
)-(
8
).
Within a first-order derivative expansion supplemented by the Markovian approximation for current-current correlators, the equivalence leads to the constitutive relation

\begin{split}\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\rangle&=\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}+L_{ab}^{ij}(t)\partial_{j}\lambda^{b}_{t}+O(\partial^{2}),\end{split}

(9)

where transport coefficients
L^{ij}_{ab}
is given by the Green-Kubo formula with the
\mathrm{TPQ}
state:

\displaystyle L_{ab}^{ij}(t)

\displaystyle=\beta_{t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\tau\big\langle\widetilde{\delta}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a,\tau}(x)\widetilde{\delta}\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{j}_{~b}(x^{\prime})\big\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\beta_{t}}{4V}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\big\langle\{\widetilde{\delta}\hat{\mathsf{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t),\widetilde{\delta}\hat{\mathsf{J}}^{j}_{~b}(t^{\prime})\}\big\rangle_{\lambda_{t}}^{\mathrm{TPQ}},

(10)

where we defined
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}
as a grandcanonical TPQ average parametrized by
\lambda_{t}

Sugiura and Shimizu 2012
;
Sugiura and Shimizu 2013
;
Hyuga et al. 2014
;
Endo et al. 2018
.
Here we defined a projected operator
\widetilde{\delta}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\equiv(1-\hat{\mathcal{P}})\delta\hat{\mathcal{O}}
using the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
version of Mori’s projection

;
Kawasaki and Gunton 1973

\hat{\mathcal{P}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)\equiv\delta\hat{c}_{a}(t)\frac{\delta}{\delta c_{a}(t)}\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}},

(11)

together with
\delta\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)\equiv\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}

and
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}(t)\equiv\mathrm{e}^{\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]\tau}\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]\tau}

In the second line of Eq. (
10

\hat{\mathsf{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t,\bm{x})

Note that the constitutive relation (
9

the parameter
\lambda_{t}^{a}=\{\beta_{t},\cdots\}
at time

, which is defined by the matching condition
\langle\hat{c}_{a}(t)\rangle=\langle\hat{c}_{a}(t)\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}

Thanks to one-to-one correspondence between
\lambda^{a}_{t}
and
\langle\hat{c}_{a}(t)\rangle

Eq. (
9

\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\rangle
by
\langle\hat{c}_{a}(t)\rangle

Then, once we evaluate
\langle\hat{\mathcal{J}}^{i}_{~a}(t)\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}
following from the thermodynamic functional
\log Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]
(see e.g.,
Hayata et al. 2015

Hongo 2017

Hongo 2019

Hongo and Hattori 2021
) and the Green-Kubo formula (

9

\ell\mathrm{TPQ}

In addition to hydrodynamic equations, we can also show the quantum fluctuation theorem and the second law of thermodynamics for the

\displaystyle G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z)

\displaystyle G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z)

By using a combined parity and time-reversal

We can prove the second law of thermodynamics and

\displaystyle\langle\hat{S}[\lambda_{t};t]-\hat{S}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\rangle

\displaystyle\geq O(\mathrm{e}^{-A_{1}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}),

\displaystyle\left|G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z)-G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i})\right|

Application to spin chain.
—

We now apply the developed formalism to a one-dimensional nonintegrable lattice half-integer spin system, whose Hamiltonian reads

\begin{split}\hat{H}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\big[&J_{z}\sigma_{n}^{z}\sigma_{n+1}^{z}+D(\sigma_{n}^{z}\sigma_{n+1}^{x}-\sigma_{n}^{x}\sigma_{n+1}^{z})\\
&+\Gamma\sigma_{n}^{x}+B\sigma_{n}^{z}\big],\end{split}

(16)

where we impose the periodic boundary condition.
The energy is the only conserved quantity of this system so that we find the energy-current operator satisfies the averaged conservation law

\partial_{t}\langle\hat{h}(t,n)\rangle+\nabla_{x}\langle\hat{J}_{E}(t,n)\rangle=0,

(17)

over the initial
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state
\ket{\beta_{t_{0}};t_{0}}
with

\nabla_{x}\langle\hat{J}_{E}(t,n)\rangle\equiv\langle\hat{J}_{E}(t,n+1/2)\rangle-\langle\hat{J}_{E}(t,n-1/2)\rangle
.

In accordance with our formal derivation, one can expect
the long-time/length behavior of the energy current is governed by the hydrodynamic equation.
In the present setup, the constitutive relation within the leading-order derivative expansion leads to the Fourier’s law:

\langle\hat{J}_{E}(t,n-1/2)\rangle=-\kappa_{\mathrm{Fourier}}(t,n)\nabla_{x}T(t,n-1/2)

(18)

where we defined
\nabla_{x}T(t,n-1/2)\equiv T(t,n)-T(t,n-1)
with

T(t,n)\equiv 1/\beta(t,n)
.
The proportional coefficient
\kappa_{\mathrm{Fourier}}(t,n)
is identified as the thermal conductivity.
On the other hand, we can also compute the thermal conductivity from the Green-Kubo formula:

\hskip-8.0pt\kappa(t,n)=\frac{\beta(t,n)^{2}}{4N}\!\!\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\!\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}\!\!\mathrm{d}t\langle\{\delta\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t),\delta\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t_{0})\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta(t,n)},

(19)

with the total energy current
\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{J}_{E}(t,n)
.
Our derivation tells us that
\kappa_{\mathrm{Fourier}}
should agree with
\kappa
in the large fluid-cell limit.
Thus, we can quantitatively evaluate the validity of the hydrodynamic description
by comparing the values of
\kappa_{\mathrm{Fourier}}(t,n)
and
\kappa(t,n)
, which are independently evaluated from Eqs. (
18
)-(
19
).

Concluding remarks.
—

In this Letter, we have formulated hydrodynamics
based on a special class of random pure quantum states, which we call the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state.
We have shown the equivalence of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
-state formalism
to that of the LG ensemble in the large fluid-cell limit in Eqs. (
7
)-(
8
), from which
we have derived the hydrodynamic equations (
9
)-(
10
), the second law of thermodynamics (
14
), and the quantum fluctuation theorem (
15
).
Using the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state, we have performed the numerical simulation of one-dimensional nonintegrable spin chain model.
From a careful analysis, we have numerically confirmed that the observed thermal diffusion allows the hydrodynamic description with the Fourier’s law and the Green-Kubo formula (Figs.
1
-
2
).
The validity of the second law of thermodynamics and quantum fluctuation theorem has also been confirmed (Fig.
3
).

Let us comment on limitations of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
-state formulation both from the conceptual and practical viewpoints.
First of all, the present formulation employs a
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state mainly motivated by its numerical efficiency.
However, a
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state is a randomly sampled state to reproduce local thermodynamics for isolated quantum systems.
Therefore, a
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state is not directly related to the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH)
Deutsch 1991
;
Srednicki 1994
;
Rigol et al. 2008
;
Steinigeweg et al. 2014b
;
Kim et al. 2014
;
D’Alessio et al. 2016
;
Dymarsky et al. 2018
;
Yoshizawa et al. 2018
;
Deutsch 2018
;
Noh et al. 2020
, as the latter concerns energy eigenstates that are responsible for quantum ergodicity of Hamiltonian dynamics.
We thus believe that it is crucial to construct a pure state formulation based on the ETH in establishing a more solid foundation for hydrodynamics of isolated quantum systems, which is an important future issue.

Another limitation of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
-state formulation is set by the dimension of the Hilbert space of bosonic systems.
Since we do not assume the relativistic or nonrelativistic nature of systems, the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
formulation is, in principle, applicable to any quantum system including the quark-gluon plasma or relativistic/nonrelativistic bose gas.
Although a current computational resource does not allow us to perform the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
simulation for such bosonic systems with a huge degrees of freedom,
it would be of immediate interest to perform the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
simulation for fermionic systems.

There are also interesting prospects that can be clarified along the line of this Letter.
While we performed a
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
simulation for the system with the single conserved quantity, it is interesting to realize the simulation with multiple conserved charges.
In particular, the existence of the momentum density in low-dimensional systems is interersting, because it is expected to drastically change conventional hydrodynamics due to a large hydrodynamic fluctuation
Lepri Ed.
.
Furthemore, it would be an interesting direction to extend our
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
framework so as to apply thermal transports for quantum integrable systems described by generalized hydrodynamics
Castro-Alvaredo et al. 2016
;
Bertini et al. 2016
;
Doyon and Yoshimura 2017
;
De Nardis et al. 2018
;
Ruggiero et al. 2020
.
We left them as future works (some will be reported in the full paper
Tsutsui et al.
).

Appendix A
Proofs of equivalence in the large fluid-cell limit

We here give proofs of equivalence between
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
formulation and
the local Gibbs (LG) ensemble method, which includes a proof of
the second law of thermodynamics and quantum fluctuation theorem in
the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
formulation.

A.1
Proof of Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
)

We here give a proof of Eqs. (
7
)-(
8
),
which is similarly accomplished, as is the case with the global thermal equilibrium
Sugiura and Shimizu 2012
;
Sugiura and Shimizu 2013
;
Hyuga et al. 2014
;
Endo et al. 2018
.
For that purpose, we rely on Chebyshev’s inequality:

\mathrm{Prob}_{z}\Big[\left|\mathcal{O}-\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{z}\right|\geq\epsilon\Big]\leq\frac{\langle\left(\mathcal{O}-\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{z}\right)^{2}\rangle_{z}}{\epsilon^{2}}=\frac{\langle\mathcal{O}^{2}\rangle_{z}-\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{z}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}.

(S1)

Let us first define the average over the random variable
z_{\alpha}=(z_{\alpha}^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}z_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime})/\sqrt{2}
by
\langle\mathcal{O}(z)\rangle_{z}
.
Since both real and imaginary parts are independently sampled from the
standard normal distribution, they satisfy, e.g.,

\langle z_{\alpha}\rangle_{z}=0,\quad\langle z_{\alpha_{1}}^{\ast}z_{\alpha_{2}}\rangle_{z}=\delta_{{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}},\quad\langle|z_{\alpha}|^{4}\rangle_{z}=2,\quad\langle|z_{\alpha_{1}}|^{2}|z_{\alpha_{2}}|^{2}\rangle_{z}=1,\quad\langle z_{\alpha_{1}}^{\ast}z_{\alpha_{2}}^{\ast}z_{\alpha_{3}}z_{\alpha_{4}}\rangle_{z}=0,

(S2)

where
\delta_{{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}
denotes the Kronecker delta, and
the last two equations hold for
{\alpha_{1}}\neq{\alpha_{2}}
.
With the help of Eqs. (
S2
),
we can show the following key identity for a state
\ket{\psi_{0}}\equiv\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{{\alpha}}
:

\Big\langle\left(\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{A}\ket{\psi_{0}}-\big\langle\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{A}\ket{\psi_{0}}\big\rangle_{z}\right)\left(\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{B}\ket{\psi_{0}}-\big\langle\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{B}\ket{\psi_{0}}\big\rangle_{z}\right)\Big\rangle_{z}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\hat{A}\hat{B}\big),

(S3)

where
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
denote arbitrary operators.

Proof of Eq. (
7
)
:
In order to show the convergence of the partition functional (
7
),
we use Chebyshev’s inequality (
S1
) for

\mathcal{O}=Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]/Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}]=\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}/\big\langle\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}
.
Recalling the definition of the partition functional for the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state
and LG ensemble
Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}]\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}
, we find the identity (
S3
) with

\hat{A}=\hat{B}=\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}
provides the right-hand-side of Chebyshev’s
inequality thanks to

\begin{split}\bra{\psi_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\ket{\psi_{0}}&=\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}=Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}],\\
\big\langle\bra{\psi_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\ket{\psi_{0}}\big\rangle_{z}&=\big\langle\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}=Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}].\end{split}

(S4)

As a consequence, we obtain

\Big\langle\left(\frac{\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}}{\big\langle\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}}-1\right)^{2}\Big\rangle_{z}=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}=\exp\left[-2\int_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\beta_{t}\Big(p(\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})-p(2\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})+O(\partial^{2})\Big)\right],

(S5)

where, to show the second equality, we used the leading-order expression
of the Massieu-Planck functional
\log Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}]
for
a parity-preserving fluid obtained in Refs.
Hayata et al. 2015
;
Hongo 2017
;
Hongo 2019
.
Here
\beta_{t}=\beta(t,\bm{x})
denotes the local inverse temperature conjugate to
the energy density,
\mu_{t}^{a}=\mu^{a}(t,\bm{x})
local chemical potentials for charge densities

\hat{J}_{~a}^{0}
attached to internal symmetries of the system labeled by indices
a
.
We explicitly put the size of the fluid cell
\Lambda
.
Substituting this result into Chebyshev’s inequality, we obtain

\mathrm{Prob}_{z}\left[\left|\frac{Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]}{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}]}-1\right|\geq\epsilon\right]\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\exp\left[-2\int_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\beta_{t}\Big(p(\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})-p(2\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})+O(\partial^{2})\Big)\right].

(S6)

The vital point here is that the pressure of a fluid cell
p(\beta,\mu^{a})

is the decreasing function with respect to
\beta
, and satisfies

p(\beta,\mu^{a})-p(2\beta,\mu^{a})>0
.
Therefore, taking the large size limit of the fluid cell

V_{\mathrm{cell}}\equiv\int_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}^{d}x
, we see that the probability
of finding the difference between

Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t}]
and
Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}]
will be exponentially
small.
This completes the proof of Eq. (
7
).

Proof of Eq. (
8
)
:
Let us move on to the proof for Eq. (
8
).
The average value over
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
and LG distribution are
given by

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}=\frac{\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}}{\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}=\frac{\big\langle\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}}{\big\langle\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}}.

(S7)

Noting
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\neq\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}
,
we divide the proof into two steps:
First, we evaluate the difference between
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}
and

\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}
, and second, we evaluate
the difference between
\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}
and

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}
.

As a first step, we use Chebyshev’s inequality for

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}=\bra{\lambda;t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}/\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}
.
In order to evaluate the right-hand-side of that inequality, we expand it as

\begin{split}\Big\langle\Big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}\Big)^{2}\Big\rangle_{z}&=\Bigg\langle\left(\frac{\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}}{\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}}-\Big\langle\frac{\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}}{\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}}\Big\rangle_{z}\right)^{2}\Bigg\rangle_{z}\\
&=\frac{\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}-\frac{2\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\langle\delta f\delta g\big\rangle_{z}}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}+\frac{\big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\big\langle(\delta g)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}+O(\delta^{3}),\end{split}

(S8)

where we defined

\begin{split}\delta f&=\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t}t;t}-\big\langle\bra{\lambda_{t};t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z},\\
\delta g&=\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}-\big\langle\braket{\lambda_{t};t|\lambda_{t};t}\big\rangle_{z}.\end{split}

(S9)

Since we have already evaluated
\big\langle(\delta g)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}

in Eq. (
S5
),
we only need to evaluate the first two terms in Eq. (
S8
);

\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}
and
\big\langle\delta f\delta g\big\rangle_{z}
.

Let us first evaluate
\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}
.
By the use of the identity (
S3
)
with
\hat{A}=\hat{B}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}
, we obtain

\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\right).

(S10)

Since
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]
is an Hermitian operator, taking trace with
its eigenstate
\ket{n}
result in

\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}=\sum_{n,m}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{n}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{m}[\lambda_{t};t]}\bra{n}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{m}\bra{m}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{n}\leq\sum_{n,m}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{n}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{m}[\lambda_{t};t]}\bra{n}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{m}\bra{m}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{n},

(S11)

where
K_{n}[\lambda_{t};t]
denotes the eigenvalue of
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]
,
and we used the completeness for the eigenstate of
\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]

to show the first equality.
The inequality results from the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means:

\sqrt{ab}\leq(a+b)/2
.
The right-hand side of this inequality can be further rewritten as

\sum_{n,m}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{n}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-K_{m}[\lambda_{t};t]}\bra{n}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{m}\bra{m}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\ket{n}=Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}},

(S12)

and we now obtain the following inequality for
\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}
:

\big\langle(\delta f)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}\leq Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}.

(S13)

To evaluate
\big\langle\delta f\delta g\big\rangle_{z}
, we
simply apply the identity
(
S3
) with

\hat{A}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}

and
\hat{B}=\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}
:

\big\langle\delta f\delta g\big\rangle_{z}=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-2\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\right)=Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}.

(S14)

Substituting Eqs. (
S5
), (
S13
) and (
S14
) into
Eq. (
S8
), we eventually obtain an upper bound of the variance as

\begin{split}\Big\langle\Big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}\Big)^{2}\Big\rangle_{z}&\leq\frac{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}\left[\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}-2\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}+\big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\right]\\
&=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}\left[\big\langle\big(\hat{\mathcal{O}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\big\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}+\big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\right],\end{split}

(S15)

which results in the following inequality:

\mathrm{Prob}_{z}\left[\left|\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}\right|\geq\epsilon\right]\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2\int_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\beta_{t}[p(\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})-p(2\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})])}}{\epsilon^{2}}\left[\big\langle\big(\hat{\mathcal{O}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\big\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}+\big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\right].

(S16)

As a consequence, for any operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
whose two variations

\big\langle\big(\hat{\mathcal{O}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}\big\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}

and
\big(\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}\big)^{2}

do not show an exponentially large behavior with respect to
the volume of fluid cells
V_{\mathrm{cell}}
, the inequality
brings about the following result on convergence:

\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\xrightarrow{P}\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z},

(S17)

which gives the first step of the proof.

Thanks to Eq. (
S5
) and (
S14
),
the second step is mostly finished if we evaluate the average
difference by expanding it as follows:

\begin{split}\left|\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\right|&=\frac{1}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}\left|-\big\langle\delta f\delta gf\big\rangle_{z}+\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\langle(\delta g)^{2}\big\rangle_{z}\right|\\
&=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[2\lambda_{t}]}{(Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}])^{2}}\left|\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\right|\\
&=\exp\left(-2\int_{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}^{d}x\beta_{t}[p(\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})-p(2\beta_{t},\mu_{t}^{a})])\right)\left|\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{2\lambda_{t}}-\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}\right|.\end{split}

(S18)

We thus conclude that any operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
not showing the exponentially large
fluctuation, the difference between

\big\langle\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t}}\big\rangle_{z}
and
\langle\hat{\mathcal{O}}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t}}
is exponentially
small.
This completes the proof of Eq. (

Proof of Eq. (

\begin{split}\langle\hat{\Sigma}[t,t_{0};\lambda]\rangle&\equiv\langle\hat{S}[\lambda_{t};t]\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t_{0}}}-\langle\hat{S}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t_{0}}}\\

(S19)

Using the equivalence between the

(a)=O(\mathrm{e}^{-B_{1}V_{\mathrm{cell}}})

(c)\geq 0
(See Refs.

Proof of Eq. (
15
)
:
To prove the quantum fluctuation theorem (

) for the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}

we first recall that for the LG ensemble
Hongo 2019
.
For that purpose, let us introduce the generating function for the entropy production under the forward/backward evolution by

\displaystyle G_{F}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\hat{\mathcal{U}}^{\dagger}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}[\lambda_{t};t_{0}]}\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}\right),

\displaystyle G_{B}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\tilde{\lambda}_{t};t]\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}(t)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}[\tilde{\lambda}_{t_{0}};t]}\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{\dagger}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}[\tilde{\lambda}_{t};t]}\right),

(S21)

where
\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t)
is a time evolution operator.
By using a combined parity and time-reversal
\Theta\equiv\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}

we also defined
\mathsf{P}\mathsf{T}
-transformed time evolution operator and parameter by
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}(t)\equiv\Theta\hat{\mathcal{U}}(t)\Theta^{-1}
and

\tilde{\lambda}_{t}\equiv\epsilon^{a}\lambda^{a}(t,-\bm{x})
with
\epsilon^{a}

\hat{c}_{a}

Here, we introduced the LG distribution for the density operator

\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]
and the entropy functional operator

\hat{S}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]
by

\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]\equiv\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{S}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\hat{S}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t};t]\equiv\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]+\log Z_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t}].

(S22)

The generating function in Eqs. (
S20
) and (
S21

Hongo 2019

G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)=G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i}).

(S23)

In order to show the quantum fluctuation theorem for the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}

\overline{G}_{F}(z)\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[t_{0};\lambda_{t_{0}}]}\right).

(S24)

By the use of this reference generating function with the triangle inequality,
we can evaluate the difference between
G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)
and
G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)
as follows:

\begin{split}\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)\right|&=\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-\overline{G}_{F}(z)-G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)+\overline{G}_{F}(z)\right|\\
&\leq\underbrace{\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-\overline{G}_{F}(z)\right|}_{(A)}+\underbrace{\Big|G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)+\overline{G}_{F}(z)\Big|}_{(B)}.\end{split}

(S25)

Defining

\Delta\Psi[\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t_{0}}]\equiv\log Z[\lambda_{t}]-\log Z[\lambda_{t_{0}}]

for both
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
and LG states, and using the error estimation for
the partition functional, we can estimate the second term in the second line as

\begin{split}(B)&=\left|\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}\right)\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\Delta\Psi_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t_{0}}]}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\Delta\Psi_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t_{0}}]}\right)\right|\\
&=\left|\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{LG}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\Delta\Psi_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t},\lambda_{t_{0}}]}\left(\exp\left[O(\mathrm{e}^{-C_{1}V_{\mathrm{cell}}})\right]-1\right)\right|\\
&=O(\mathrm{e}^{-C_{1}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}).\end{split}

(S26)

Since the first term in Eq. (
S25
) is given by
the difference of the averaged operator over the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state and LG
distribution, we can evaluate it as

\begin{split}(A)&=\left[\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}\rangle^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\lambda_{t_{0}}}-\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t};t]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{S}_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\lambda_{t_{0}};t_{0}]}\rangle^{\mathrm{LG}}_{\lambda_{t_{0}}}\right]\\
&=O(\mathrm{e}^{-C_{2}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}),\end{split}

(S27)

where we assumed that the variation of the averaged operator
does not show the exponentially large behavior.
Substituting Eqs. (
S26
)-(
S27
) into
Eq. (
S25
), we obtain

\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{F}(z)\right|\leq O(\mathrm{e}^{-C_{3}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}),

(S28)

which states the equivalence of the generating function for the entropy
production for the forward evolution.
On the other hand, a similar analysis also works for

G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z)-G_{B}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)
, and thus, we have,

\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i})-G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i})\right|\leq O(\mathrm{e}^{-C_{4}V_{\mathrm{cell}}}),

(S29)

Therefore, combining Eq. (
S23
) with
Eqs. (
S28
)-(
S29
), we can evaluate

\begin{split}&\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i})\right|\\
=&\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-G_{F}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)-\Big(G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(-z+\mathrm{i})-G_{B}^{\mathrm{LG}}(-z+\mathrm{i})\Big)+G_{F}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)-G^{\mathrm{LG}}_{B}(-z+\mathrm{i})\right|\\
\leq&\left|G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)-G_{F}^{\mathrm{LG}}(z)\right|+\left|G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(-z+\mathrm{i})-G_{B}^{\mathrm{LG}}(-z+\mathrm{i})\right|\\
=&O(\mathrm{e}^{-A_{2}}V_{\mathrm{cell}}),\end{split}

(S30)

where the inequality in the third line again follows from the triangle
inequality.
This gives the quantum fluctuation theorem for the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state.

Detail of numrerical implementation

We here summarize a detail of our numerical simulation based on the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
formulation such as (i) the concrete definition of the (smeared) energy density/current operator, (ii) how to prepare the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state and the equation of state,
and (iii) two ways to evaluate the thermal conductivity, and

B.1
Energy density/current operator and their smearing

Let us first write the Hamiltonian in the following form:

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\sum_{n}\hat{h}(n),\quad\hat{h}(n)=\mathcal{H}_{n,n+1}+\mathcal{H}_{n},

(S31)

where the energy density operator
\hat{h}(n)
is decomposed into
the sum of the nearest-neighbor term
\mathcal{H}_{n,n+1}
and the on-site term
\mathcal{H}_{n}
.
Then, we shall derive the energy current operator
\hat{J}_{E}(n-1/2)
.
In order to determine the form of
\hat{J}_{E}(n-1/2)
,
we require that the time evolution of the Hamiltonian density should be written as the discrete divergence of the energy current operator as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{h}(t,n)}{\partial t}=-\big(\hat{J}_{E}(t,n+1/2)-\hat{J}_{E}(t,n-1/2)\big).

(S32)

On the other hand,
the Heisenberg equation for the Hamiltonian density operator reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{h}(t,n)}{\partial t}=\mathrm{i}\big[\hat{H},\hat{h}(t,n)\big]

(S33)

Comparing these two equations, we identify the energy current as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{E}(n-1/2)=\mathrm{i}\left[\mathcal{H}_{n-1,n},\mathcal{H}_{n,n+1}+\mathcal{H}_{n}\right].

(S34)

Thus, the concrete form of the energy density operator
\hat{h}(n)

and energy current operator
\hat{J}_{E}(n-1/2)
for the system defined by the
Hamiltonian (
16
) is given by

\displaystyle\hat{h}(n)=

\displaystyle J_{z}\sigma_{n}^{z}\sigma_{n+1}^{z}+D(\sigma_{n}^{z}\sigma_{n+1}^{x}-\sigma_{n}^{x}\sigma_{n+1}^{z})+\Gamma\sigma_{n}^{x}+B\sigma_{n}^{z}

(S35)

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{E}(n-1/2)=

\displaystyle-2D^{2}\left(\sigma_{n-1}^{x}\sigma_{n}^{y}\sigma_{n+1}^{z}-\sigma_{n-1}^{z}\sigma_{n}^{y}\sigma_{n+1}^{x}\right)+4DJ_{z}\sigma_{n-1}^{z}\sigma_{n}^{y}\sigma_{n+1}^{z}

\displaystyle-2\Gamma J_{z}\sigma_{n-1}^{z}\sigma_{n}^{y}+2D\Gamma\sigma_{n-1}^{x}\sigma_{n}^{y}+2DB\sigma_{n-1}^{z}\sigma_{n}^{y}.

(S36)

Let us then define a smearing of local operators.
To extract the hydrodynamic behavior, we define a smeared operator of
an arbitrary local operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}(n)
by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{O}}(n)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_{n-m}\hat{\mathcal{O}}(m),

(S37)

with an appropriate weight function
w_{n-m}
which is a positive and has a peak at
n=m
.
We also require that
w_{n}
satisfies the periodic boundary condition
w_{n-N}=w_{n}
.
This requirement is needed in our setup to define a smeared energy current operator in a consistent manner.
To see this, we apply Eq. (
S37
) to the local Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{h}(t,n)}{\partial t}=\sum_{m=1}^{N}w_{n-m}\frac{\partial\hat{h}(t,m)}{\partial t}=-\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N}w_{n+1-(m+1/2)}\hat{J}_{E}(t,m+1)-\bar{J}_{E}(t,n-1/2)\right)

(S38)

The first term of the right hand side is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N}w_{n+1-(m+1)}\hat{J}_{E}(t,m+1/2)=\bar{J}_{E}(t,n+1/2)-(w_{n}-w_{n-N})\hat{J}_{E}(t,1/2).

(S39)

Thus, we find that the smeared current operator holds the continuity equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{h}(t,n)}{\partial t}=-\left(\bar{J}_{E}(t,n+1/2)-\bar{J}_{E}(t,n-1/2)\right)

(S40)

thanks to the periodicity
w_{n-N}=w_{n}
.
In our numerical simulations, we choose the weight function
w_{n}
as

\displaystyle w_{n}=\frac{1}{\sum_{m=1}^{N}e^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\cos\frac{2\pi m}{N}}}e^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\cos\frac{2\pi n}{N}},

(S41)

where
\sigma^{2}
is a positive parameter controlling the smearing length.
In the thermodynamic limit with
N\to\infty
, the typical width of this function is given by
1-I_{1}(1/\sigma^{2})/I_{0}(1/\sigma^{2})
,
where
I_{n}(x)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind.
In the
1/\sigma^{2}\to\infty
limit for a fixed
N
,
the weight function converges to the Kronecker delta.
For our purpose, the width of the weight function, regarded as the effective size of the fluid cell
\Lambda
, should be taken so that
1\ll\Lambda\ll N
.
Typical shapes of the weight function is shown in Fig.
S1
.
For instance, the number of spins in the fluid cell is about 5 when
1/\sigma^{2}=5
.

Figure S1:
The weight function Eq. (
S41
).

B.2
How to prepare
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state and equation of state

Construction of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state.

Let us first review a way to implement a canonical TPQ state which is defined by

\displaystyle\ket{\beta}\equiv\exp\left(\frac{-N\beta\hat{h}}{2}\right)\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha},

(S42)

where
\hat{h}=\hat{H}/N
is the Hamiltonian density and
\ket{\alpha}
is a random vector in the Hilbert space.
We expand the canonical TPQ state by microcanonical TPQ states as

\displaystyle\ket{\beta}=\mathrm{e}^{-N\beta l/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}R_{k}\ket{\psi_{k}}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\ket{\psi_{k}}=\frac{\ket{k}}{\braket{k|k}},\quad\ket{k}\equiv(l-\hat{h})^{k}\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad R_{k}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N\beta/2)^{k}\braket{k|k}}{k!},

(S43)

where
l
is a certain number larger than the maximum eigenvalue of
\hat{h}
.
One can show that
R_{k}
has a sharp peak at a certain
k=k^{*}
,
whose magnitude is controlled by the inverse temperature
Sugiura and Shimizu 2012
;
Sugiura and Shimizu 2013
;
Hyuga et al. 2014
;
Endo et al. 2018
.
Thanks to this property,
it is justified to truncate the expansion at a certain order
k=k_{\text{max}}
.
Then, we define
k_{\text{max}}
by

\displaystyle\frac{R_{k_{\text{max}}}}{\max_{k}R_{k}}>10^{-t},

(S44)

for a given tolerance parameter
t
.
In our numerical simulations, we take
t=20
and confirm that the expectation values of observables are well converged.

One can generalize the above procedure in a straightforward manner
to the implementation of
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
states.
For that purpose, we first define

\displaystyle\ket{\{\beta(n)\}}\equiv\exp\left(\frac{-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)\hat{h}(n)}{2}\right)\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha},

(S45)

where
\hat{h}_{n}
is the local Hamiltonian density.
Expanding the exponential operator, we find

\displaystyle\ket{\{\beta(n)\}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\beta(n)l_{n}}\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)(l_{n}-\hat{h}(n))}{2}\right)\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}

(S46)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\beta(n)l_{n}}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)(l_{n}-\hat{h}(n))\right)^{k}\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}

(S47)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\beta(n)l_{n}}\sum_{k}\frac{\bar{\beta}^{k}N^{k}}{2^{k}k!}\left(\frac{1}{N\bar{\beta}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)(l_{n}-\hat{h}(n))\right)^{k}\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha},

(S48)

where
l_{n}
is a certain number larger than the maximum eigenvalue of
\hat{h}
,
and
\bar{\beta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)
is the average inverse temperature.
We note that the state appeared in Eq. (
S48
)

\displaystyle\ket{k,\{\beta(n)\}}\equiv\left(\frac{1}{N\bar{\beta}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\beta(n)(l_{n}-\hat{h}(n))\right)^{k}\sum_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}

(S49)

can be regarded as a generalization of the microcanonical TPQ state
\ket{k}
:
Indeed, we can show
\ket{k,\{\beta(n)\}}=\ket{k}
if
\beta=\beta_{1}=\dots=\beta_{N}
,
l=l_{1}=\dots,l_{N}
.
Then, we obtain the expression the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state as follows:

\displaystyle\ket{\{\beta(n)\}}=e^{-N\beta l/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}R_{k}(\{\beta(n)\})\ket{\psi_{k},\{\beta(n)\}},

(S50)

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\ket{\psi_{k},\{\beta(n)\}}=\frac{\ket{k,\{\beta(n)\}}}{\braket{k,\{\beta(n)\}|k,\{\beta(n)\}}},\quad R_{k}(\{\beta(n)\})=\frac{(N\beta/2)^{k}\braket{k,\{\beta(n)\}|k,\{\beta(n)\}}}{k!}.

(S51)

We confirmed that
R_{k}(\{\beta(n)\})
also has a sharp peak at a certain
k=k^{*}
so that we can truncate the summation over
k
in Eq. (
S50
) in a same manner as the canonical TPQ states.
Equations (
S48
)-(
S51
) define a way to prepare the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state starting from an arbitrary random vector

\ket{a}
in the Hilbert space.

.

(S52)

This relation gives the one-to-one correspondence between the energy and the inverse temperature as long as first order phase transitions do not occur.

\beta=0.0,0.1,\cdots,3.0

.

.

.

B.3
Two ways to evaluate thermal conductivity

Thermal conductivity from the Fourier law.

While the formal arguments rely on the Heisenberg picture,
we perform numerical simulations based on the Schrödinger picture.
The expectation value of an operator
\hat{O}
at time
t
is calculated by

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{O}(t)}=\frac{\braket{\psi(t)|\hat{O}|\psi(t)}}{\braket{\psi(0)|\psi(0)}},

(S53)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}\ket{\psi(t)},\quad\ket{\psi(0)}=\ket{\{\beta(n)\}}.

(S54)

Unless otherwise noted,
the number of spins is taken as
N=20
,
and the coupling constants are
J_{z}=\Gamma=-B=D=0.2
so that the system is nonintegrable.
We set the initial temperature profile of the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state as

\displaystyle\beta(n)=\bar{\beta}+\Delta\beta\sin(2\pi(n-1)/N),\quad n=1,\dots,N,

(S55)

with
\bar{\beta}=1.5
and
\Delta\beta=0.2
.
We numerically solve the Schrödinger equation by the fourth order Runge-Kutta method with a time slice
\Delta t=0.01
,
and calculated the local energy and the energy current.
The smearing is also performed by using the weight function given in Eq. (
S41
) with
1/\sigma^{2}=5
.
The local temperature is estimated from the local energy through the relation in equilibrium shown in Fig.
S2
assuming that derivative corrections are negligible.

If the system is well described by hydrodynamics,
we can extract the thermal conductivity from the Fourier law Eq. (
18
).
We introduce temporally averaged operator by

\displaystyle\tilde{O}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\bar{O}(t^{\prime}).

(S56)

The time evolution of temporally averaged quantities are shown in Fig.
S3
(and Fig.
1
in the main text), where
\tau=10
.
Again, the local temperature is estimated by using the equation of state in equilibrium as
\tilde{T}(t,n)=T(\braket{\tilde{h}(t,n)})
. (In the main text, the tilde is omitted for the simplicity of notation.)

and

.

Thermal conductivity from the Green-Kubo formula.

On the basis of the Green-Kubo formula, the thermal conductivity is given by

\displaystyle\kappa(t,n)=\frac{\beta_{n}(t)}{4N}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\langle\{\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t),\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(0)\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta_{n}}\simeq\frac{\beta}{4N}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\langle\{\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t),\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(0)\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta}

(S57)

where
\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{J}^{n}_{E}(t)
.
In the second equality, we neglect the spatial and temporal dependence of temperature, which gives rise to the higher-order derivative correction.
Conceptually, the Green-Kubo formula does not require a fully microscopic time resolution but only a hydrodynamic one.
Therefore, we also replace the bare correlation function
\langle\{\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t),\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(0)\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta}
with the temporally smeared one, which leads to

\displaystyle G(t)\equiv\frac{1}{t_{h}^{2}}\int_{t}^{t+t_{h}}\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{t_{h}}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\langle\{\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(s),\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(s^{\prime})\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta}.

(S58)

An appropriate time scale
t_{h}
is estimated from the damping time of the bare correlation function
\langle\{\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(t),\hat{\mathsf{J}}_{E}(0)\}\rangle^{\mathrm{TPQ}}_{\beta}
. Below, we set
t_{h}=10
which is numerically computed.
The thermal conductivity is now given by

\displaystyle\kappa(t,n)\simeq\frac{\beta}{2}\left(C(\infty)-C(-\infty)\right)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad C(t)=\frac{1}{2N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}G(t^{\prime}).

(S59)

Since
C(t)
is defined by the symmetric Green’s function, we can show it is equal to
-C(-t)
within a small error, which is actually confirmed numerically.
Thus, we reach the following simple linear response result
in the
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
formulation:

\displaystyle\kappa(t,n)\simeq\beta C(\infty)\equiv\kappa

(S60)

C(t)

C(t)
converges when

.
The value at infinitely late time
C(\infty)
is estimated by the average of
C(t)
from

B.4
Numerical evaluation of generating functions in fluctuation theorem

Let us finally present a numerical method to compute the generating functional.
When the Hamiltonian does not depend on time explicitly,
we can rewrite the generating functions for the total entropy production
Eqs. (
12
)-(
13
) as follows:

\displaystyle G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\log(Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]/Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}])}}{Z_{\text{lTPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]}\Braket{\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}Ht}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\hat{H}t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]}|\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}},

(S61)

\displaystyle G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(\mathrm{i}-z;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z^{*}\log(Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]/Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}])}}{Z_{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]}

\displaystyle\times\Braket{\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Ht}\mathrm{e}^{-\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]z^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\hat{H}t}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]z^{*}}\mathrm{e}^{\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]}|\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}}^{*}.

(S62)

Here
\ket{\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}}
is an
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state, whose temperature profile is chosen to be that at time
t
.
We compute these quantities based on the following steps:

(i)

Make an
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state
\ket{\{\beta(0,n)\}}
whose temperature profile is set by the initial condition (
S55
).

(ii)

Compute
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}Ht}\ket{\{\beta(0,n)\}}
.

(iii)

Compute spatially smeared and temporally averaged local energies
\{\braket{\tilde{h}(t,n)}\}
.

(iv)

Compute local temperatures
\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}
using the energy-temperature relation.

(v)

Make an auxiliary
\ell\mathrm{TPQ}
state
\ket{\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}}
.

Exponential operators like
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(t,n)\}]}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}z\hat{K}[\{\tilde{\beta}(0,n)\}]}
are regarded as a time evolution operator.
Action of these operators to pure states are computed by fourth order Runge-Kutta method.

The entropy production can be computed by three independent ways.
One way is to compute the expectation value of the entropy operator Eq. (
3
) directly.
The others are to compute the gradient of
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{F}(z)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}G^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}_{B}(\mathrm{i}-z)
at
z=0
.
Concretely, they are given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{F}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(z)}{\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{i}z)}\right|_{z=0},\quad\Sigma_{B}\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(\mathrm{i}-z)}{\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{i}z)}\right|_{z=0}.

(S63)

In Fig.
S6
, we demonstrate the estimation of the entropy production from
G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}
.
At each time, we confirm that the generating function behaves as a linear function around the origin, which allows us to evaluate the entropy production
according to Eq. (
S63
).
In the figure, the solid line shows the fitting result using the data at
z=0.00,0.01,\dots,0.03
. The choice of the fitting range is insensitive to the result. The fitting of
G_{B}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}(\mathrm{i}-z)
can be performed in the same manner.

Figure S6:
The generating function
G_{F}^{\ell\mathrm{TPQ}}
at
t=20
.
The solid line stands for the fitting function.

Unlike the standard quantum fluctuation theorem,

\Sigma_{F}
agrees with
\Sigma_{B}

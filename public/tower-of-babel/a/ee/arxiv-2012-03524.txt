The Hausdorff measure of the range and level sets of Gaussian random fields with sectorial local nondeterminism
Cheuk Yin Lee

Abstract
. We determine the exact Hausdorff measure functions for the range and level sets of a class of Gaussian random fields satisfying sectorial local nondeterminism and other assumptions. We also establish a Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm. The results can be applied to the Brownian sheet, fractional Brownian sheets whose Hurst indices are the same in all directions, and systems of linear stochastic wave equations in one spatial dimension driven by space-time white noise or colored noise.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification

60G15, 60G60, 60G17.

1.
Introduction

Consider a centered, continuous,
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued Gaussian random field

v=\{v(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\}
with i.i.d. components, that is,
v(x)=(v_{1}(x),\dots,v_{d}(x))

and
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}
are i.i.d.

The class of Gaussian random fields
v
that we treat in this paper
includes the Brownian sheet and other Gaussian random fields that share a similar
structure with the Brownian sheet, such as the fractional Brownian sheets and the
solution of a system of linear stochastic wave equations.
One of the important properties that they have in common is that they all satisfy sectorial
local nondeterminism (sectorial LND).
The purpose of this paper is to establish a unified and general framework that
incorporates these Gaussian random fields and allows us to study fine properties such
as the Hausdorff measures of the associated random sets and sharp regularity
properties for these random fields.
In the main results of this paper, we identify the exact Hausdorff measure functions for
the range and level sets of
v
.
We also prove a Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm (LIL).
We use these results to outline subtle differences between Gaussian random fields with
sectorial LND and those with stationary increments and strong LND.

It is well known that the fractional Brownian motion satisfies strong LND
[
28
]
.
The exact Hausdorff measure function for the range of
Lévy’s multiparameter Brownian motion was identified by Goldman
[
16
]

and the result was extended by Talagrand
[
33
]
to the case of the
fractional Brownian motion.
For Gaussian random fields with stationary increments and
strong LND, the problem of finding the Hausdorff
measure functions for the range and level sets was studied by Xiao
[
39
,
40
]
.
Baraka and Mountford
[
6
]
improved the result of Xiao
[
40
]

in the case of fractional Brownian motion and determined
the exact value of the Hausdorff measure of the zero set.
The Hausdorff measure function for the range of anisotropic Gaussian random fields
was studied by Luan and Xiao
[
25
]
.

On the other hand, the Brownian sheet does not satisfy strong LND (see
[
4
]
).
Instead, it satisfies a weaker condition called sectorial LND
[
18
,
19
]
.
The Hausdorff measure functions for the range and graph of the Brownian sheet
were determined by Ehm
[
13
]
.
The Brownian sheet is closely related to the additive Brownian motion, and
the exact Hausdorff measure of the zero set of the latter was studied by
Mountford and Nualart
[
27
]
.
In
[
38
]
, Wu and Xiao studied the local times of anisotropic Gaussian random
fields with sectorial LND and obtained a partial result for the Hausdorff measure function
for the level sets.
We point out that the exponent of the logarithmic factor in the Hausdorff measure
function for Gaussian fields with sectorial LND is
typically not the same as those with stationary increments and strong LND,
indicating subtle differences between these two classes of Gaussian fields.

Our framework is inspired by the special structure of the Brownian sheet.
Let us recall the local representation and behaviour of the Brownian sheet,
which has been discussed in the literature, e.g.
[
17
,
8
]
.
Let
B=\{B(t_{1},t_{2}):(t_{1},t_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}\}
be a Brownian sheet on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
.
Fix
s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in(0,\infty)^{2}
and let
I=[s_{1},s_{1}+r]\times[s_{2},s_{2}+r]

be a small interval. Then for all
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in I
, we can write

(1.1)

B(t_{1},t_{2})=B(s_{1},s_{2})+\tilde{B}^{1}(t_{1})+\tilde{B}^{2}(t_{2})+R(t_{1},t_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{1}(t_{1})=B(t_{1},s_{2})-B(s_{1},s_{2}),\qquad\tilde{B}^{2}(t_{2})=B(s_{1},t_{2})-B(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad R(t_{1},t_{2})=B(t_{1},t_{2})-B(t_{1},s_{2})-B(s_{1},t_{2})+B(s_{1},s_{2}).

Note that
\{\tilde{B}^{1}(t_{1}):t_{1}\geq s_{1}\}
and
\{\tilde{B}^{2}(t_{2}):t_{2}\geq s_{2}\}

are two independent Brownian motions (up to scaling).
Since
\mathrm{Var}(R(t_{1},t_{2}))=(t_{1}-s_{1})(t_{2}-s_{2})
, the remainder process
R

is of smaller order compared to
\tilde{B}^{1}
and
\tilde{B}^{2}
.
Now consider the increments of
B
over the interval
I
: for all
t,t^{\prime}\in I
,

(1.2)

B(t)-B(t^{\prime})=[\tilde{B}^{1}(t_{1})-\tilde{B}^{1}(t_{1}^{\prime})]+[\tilde{B}^{2}(t_{2})-\tilde{B}^{2}(t_{2}^{\prime})]+\text{remainder}.

This suggests that the increments of
B
can be approximated by those of

\tilde{B}^{1}
and
\tilde{B}^{2}
.

In this paper, we will consider the examples of fractional Brownian sheets and the
solution of a linear stochastic wave equation (driven by a possibly colored noise),
which share similar structures with the Brownian sheet.
A technical difference for these examples of Gaussian fields is that the
corresponding processes
\tilde{B}^{j}
’s may not have
independent increments or Markov property unlike the Brownian sheet case, where
the
\tilde{B}^{j}
’s are Brownian motions.
Nonetheless, all of these Gaussian fields have certain harmonizable representation,
which is a useful representation to create independence (see
[
11
,
10
]
).
These observations motivate us to introduce the processes
\tilde{v}^{j}
,

j=1,\dots,N
, and the conditions for these processes in Assumption
1.2
below.

Let
0<\alpha<1
be a constant and
T
be a compact interval in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Define
{\|X\|}_{L^{2}}=({\mathbb{E}}|X|^{2})^{1/2}
for a random vector
X
, where

|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm.
We will consider the following assumptions for the Gaussian random field
v
.
The first assumption specifies upper and lower bounds for the increments of
v

in
L^{2}
-norm, and the property of sectorial local nondeterminism.
This assumption appears in
[
41
,
38
]
.

Assumption 1.1
.

There exist positive finite constants
c_{0},c_{1}
such that the following
properties hold:

(a)

For all
x,y\in T
,

c_{0}^{-1}|x-y|^{\alpha}\leq{\|v(x)-v(y)\|}_{L^{2}}\leq c_{0}|x-y|^{\alpha}.

(b)

(Sectorial local nondeterminism) For all integers
n\geq 1
,
for all
x,y^{1},\dots,y^{n}\in T
,

\mathrm{Var}(v_{1}(x)|v_{1}(y^{1}),\dots,v_{1}(y^{n}))\geq c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{0\leq i\leq n}|x_{j}-y^{i}_{j}|^{2\alpha},

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
and
y^{0}=0
.

For any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
r>0
, let

I_{r}(s)
denote the compact interval
T\cap\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
.
Let
\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
denote the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=[0,\infty)
.
The following assumption is a modification of Assumption 2.1 in
[
11
]

(see also
[
10
]
).
Here, this assumption is adapted to the structure of the Brownian sheet
as discussed above so that at the same time we are able to deal with other Gaussian
random fields sharing a similar structure.

Assumption 1.2
.

For every
s\in T
, there exists
0<r_{0}\leq 1
, there exists,
for each
j=1,\dots,N
, an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued Gaussian process

\{\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j}):A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),x_{j}\in[s_{j}-r_{0},s_{j}+r_{0}]\}
,
which has i.i.d. components and is a.s. continuous in
x_{j}
for fixed
A
, and
there exists a finite constant
c_{2}
(depending on
T
but not on
s
) such that
the following properties hold:

(a)

For each
j
and
x_{j}
,
A\mapsto\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued
independently scattered Gaussian measure.
Moreover, whenever
A
and
B
are fixed, disjoint sets,
the
\sigma
-algebra
\sigma\{\tilde{v}^{1}(A,\cdot),\dots,\tilde{v}^{N}(A,\cdot)\}

is independent of
\sigma\{\tilde{v}^{1}(B,\cdot),\dots,\tilde{v}^{N}(B,\cdot)\}
.

(b)

For all
j
, for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
and all
x_{j},y_{j}\in[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
,

{\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha}.

(c)

Let
\tilde{v}(A,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})
.
The process
v(\cdot)-\tilde{v}(A,\cdot)
has i.i.d. components and
there exist constants
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\alpha
,
\gamma_{2}>0
and
a_{0}\geq 0

such that for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
, for all
x,y\in I_{r}(s)
, for all
a_{0}\leq a<b\leq\infty
,

\begin{split}&{\|v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a,b),x)+\tilde{v}([a,b),y)\|}_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\leq c_{2}\left(a^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+r^{\gamma_{1}}b^{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}+r^{\gamma_{2}}|x-y|^{\alpha}\right).\end{split}

The next assumption originates from Assumption 2.4 in
[
11
]
.
Part (a) states a uniform lower bound for the
L^{2}
-norm of
v
, and
part (b) basically states that for any
x
, one can find a reference point
x^{\prime}

(which may depend on
x
) such that for all
y,\bar{y}
in a small neighbourhood of
x
,
the covariances in (
1.3
) are smoother than what one gets from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the upper bound in Assumption
1.1
(a) above.

Assumption 1.3
.

There exists constants
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1
,
c_{3}>0
,

c>0
and
\delta\in(\alpha,1]
such that
the following properties hold:

(a)

For all
x\in T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}
,
{\|v(x)\|}_{L^{2}}\geq c_{3}
,
where
T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}
denotes the
\varepsilon_{0}
-neighbourhood
of
T
in the Euclidean norm.

(b)

For every sufficiently small compact interval
I\subset T
,
and every
0<\rho\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists a finite constant
c_{4}
such that
for any
x\in I
, there exists
x^{\prime}\in I^{(c\rho)}
such that for all
y,\bar{y}\in I^{(c\rho)}

with
|x-y|\leq 2\rho
and
|x-\bar{y}|\leq 2\rho
, for all
i=1,\dots,d
,

(1.3)

|{\mathbb{E}}[(v_{i}(y)-v_{i}(\bar{y}))v_{i}(x^{\prime})]|\leq c_{4}|y-\bar{y}|^{\delta}.

Let
\mathscr{I}(T)
be the collection of all compact intervals in
T
, and
let
\lambda
be the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
For any subset
F
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
let
\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(F)
denote the Hausdorff measure of
F

with respect to the function
\phi
, and
for any interval
J\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, let
L(\cdot,J)
denote the local time of
v
on
J

(see Section 2 for definitions).
The range of
v
on
J
is the random set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
defined by

v(J)=\{v(x):x\in J\}.

For any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the
z
-level set of
v
on
J
is the random set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

defined by

v^{-1}(z)\cap J=\{x\in J:v(x)=z\}.

The following are the main results of the paper, concerning the Hausdorff measure
of the range and level sets of
v
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
T
be a compact interval in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, if
N<\alpha d
, then
there exist positive finite constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(1.4)

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{C_{1}\lambda(J)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(v(J))\leq C_{2}\lambda(J)\text{ for all }J\in\mathscr{I}(T)\Big\}=1,

where
\phi(r)=r^{N/\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{N}
.

We can compare Theorem
1.4
to the case of
a fractional Brownian motion
X
from
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
for which we have

0<\mathscr{H}_{\psi}(X(J))<\infty
, where
\psi(r)=r^{N/\alpha}\log\log(1/r)
;
see Talagrand
[
33
]
.

Also, we remark that by Corollary 5.1 of
[
28
]
,
if
N>\alpha d
and
T
has interior points,
then a.s.
v(T)
has positive Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For the critical case
N=\alpha d
,
the problem of determining the exact Hausdorff measure
function for
v(T)
is open;
see Talagrand
[
34
]
for some results for the fractional Brownian motion.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T
be a compact interval in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Under Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
, if
N>\alpha d
, then
there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any fixed
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

(1.5)

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{CL(z,J)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(z)\cap J)<\infty\text{ for all }J\in\mathscr{I}(T)\Big\}=1,

where
\varphi(r)=r^{N-\alpha d}(\log\log(1/r))^{\alpha d}
.
Moreover, if
N\leq\alpha d
, then
v^{-1}(z)\cap T=\varnothing
a.s.

Baraka and Mountford
[
6
]
proved that if
N>\alpha d

and
X
is a fractional Brownian motion from
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
then
\mathscr{H}_{\psi}(X^{-1}(0)\cap J)=CL(0,J)
,
where
\psi(r)=r^{N-\alpha d}(\log\log(1/r))^{\alpha d/N}
.
We conjecture that Theorem
1.5
can be strengthened to

\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(0)\cap J)=CL(z,J)
for some constant
C
.

Another result of this paper is a Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
T
be a compact interval in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
,
for any fixed
s\in T
, there exists a constant
K
that may depend on
s

such that

(1.6)

\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|v(x)-v(s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}=K\quad\text{a.s.}

and
K_{0}\leq K\leq K_{1}
, where
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
are positive finite constants
depending on
T
.

2.
Preliminaries

Consider the class
\Phi
of nondecreasing, right-continuous functions
\phi:(0,\delta_{0})\to(0,\infty)
for some
\delta_{0}>0
satisfying
\phi(0+)=0

and the condition: there exists
c>0
such that
\phi(2r)\leq c\phi(r)

for all
0<r\leq\delta_{0}/2
.
For
\phi\in\Phi
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
the
\phi
-Hausdorff measure
of
F
is defined by

\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(F)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\inf\Bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\phi(2r_{j}):F\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}B(x_{j},r_{j}),0\leq r_{j}\leq\delta\Bigg\}.

It is known that
\mathscr{H}_{\phi}
is an outer measure and
every Borel set is
\mathscr{H}_{\phi}
-measurable.
We say that
\phi
is the
exact Hausdorff measure function
for
F
if

0<\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(F)<\infty
.

When
\phi(r)=r^{\beta}
, where
\beta>0
,
the
\phi
-Hausdorff measure is written as

\mathscr{H}_{\beta}
and is called the
\beta
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.
The Hausdorff dimension of
F
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim F

\displaystyle=\inf\{\beta>0:\mathscr{H}_{\beta}(F)=0\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\beta>0:\mathscr{H}_{\beta}(F)=\infty\}.

The equality between the
\inf
and the
\sup
is well known.
We refer to
[
14
,
29
]
for basic properties of Hausdorff measure and Hausdorff
dimension.

For any finite Borel measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and any function
\phi\in\Phi
,
the
upper
\phi
-density
of
\mu
at a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined as

\overline{D}^{\phi}_{\mu}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\phi(r)}.

The following lemma can be derived from the results in
[
30
]
(see also
[
35
]
)
and it will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
For completeness, we give a proof for this lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\phi\in\Phi
, any finite Borel measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

and any Borel set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\mu(F)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(F)\sup_{x\in F}\overline{D}^{\phi}_{\mu}(x).

Proof.

Let
M=\sup_{x\in F}\overline{D}^{\phi}_{\mu}(x)
and
\varepsilon>0
.
For each
\delta>0
, let

F_{\delta}=\big\{x\in F:\mu(B(x,r))\leq(M+\varepsilon)\phi(r)\text{ for all }0<r\leq 2\delta\big\}.

Suppose the collection
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of balls with
0\leq r_{i}\leq\delta

is a cover for
F
. In particular it is a cover for
F_{\delta}
.
For each ball
B(x_{i},r_{i})
containing a point
y_{i}
in
F_{\delta}
, we have

B(y_{i},2r_{i})\supset B(x_{i},r_{i})
. By the definition of
F_{\delta}
,
we have
\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))\leq\mu(B(y_{i},2r_{i}))\leq(M+\varepsilon)\phi(2r_{i})
.
Then

\mu(F_{\delta})\leq\sum_{i:B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap F_{\delta}\neq\varnothing}\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))\leq(M+\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\phi(2r_{i}).

The cover
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is arbitrary, so we have

\mu(F_{\delta})\leq(M+\varepsilon)\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(F)
.
Since
F_{\delta}\uparrow F
as
\delta\to 0
and
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
the result follows.

Let us recall the definition and basic properties of local times.
We refer to
[
15
]
for more details.
Let
T
be a Borel set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
The occupation measure of
v
on
T
is defined as

\mu_{T}(A)=\lambda_{N}\{x\in T:v(x)\in A\},\quad A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

where
\lambda_{N}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
If
\mu_{T}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{d}

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we say that the local time of
v
exists on
T
, and the local time is defined as
the Radon–Nikodym derivative

L(z,T)=\frac{d\mu_{T}}{d\lambda_{d}}(z).

It is clear that if the local time exists on
T
, then
it also exists on any Borel subset of
T
.

Theorem 8.1 of
[
41
]
shows that under Assumption
1.1
(a),

v
has a local time
L(\cdot,T)\in L^{2}(\lambda_{d}\times{\mathbb{P}})

if and only if
N>\alpha d
.

Furthermore, by Theorem 8.2 of
[
41
]
, if Assumption
1.1
is satisfied on
a compact interval
T=\prod_{j=1}^{N}[t_{j},t_{j}+h_{j}]
and
N>\alpha d
, then

v
has a jointly continuous local time in the sense that
there exists a version of the local time, still denoted by
L(z,\cdot)
, such that a.s.
L(z,\prod_{j=1}^{N}[t_{j},t_{j}+s_{j}])
is jointly continuous in all variables

(z,s)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\prod_{j=1}^{N}[0,h_{j}]
.

We will use a jointly continuous version of the local time whenever it exists.
If
v
is continuous and has a jointly continuous local time on
T
, then

L(z,\cdot)
defines a Borel measure supported on the level set
v^{-1}(z)\cap T
.
See
[
2
, Theorem 8.6.1]
or
[
15
, p.12, Remark(c)]
.

Next, let us recall a small ball probability estimate for Gaussian processes
that is due to Talagrand
[

.
The following is a reformulation of this result and its proof can be found in
Ledoux
[

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{Z(t):t\in S\}
be a separable, real-valued, centered Gaussian process
indexed by a bounded set
S
with the canonical metric

d_{Z}(s,t)=({\mathbb{E}}|Z(s)-Z(t)|^{2})^{1/2}
.
Let
N_{\varepsilon}(S)
denote the smallest number of
d_{Z}
-balls of radius
\varepsilon

that are needed to cover
S
.
If there is a decreasing function
\psi:(0,\varepsilon_{1}]\to(0,\infty)
such that

N_{\varepsilon}(S)\leq\psi(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
and there are constants

a_{2}\geq a_{1}>1
such that

(2.1)

a_{1}\psi(\varepsilon)\leq\psi(\varepsilon/2)\leq a_{2}\psi(\varepsilon)\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}],

then for all
u\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|Z(s)-Z(t)|\leq u\right\}\geq\exp\big(-K\psi(u)\big),

where
K
is a constant depending on
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
only.

The next lemma is an isoperimetric inequality for Gaussian processes;
see
[
22
, p.302]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is a universal constant
K
such that the following statement holds.
Let
S
be a bounded set and
\{Z(s):s\in S\}
be a real-valued Gaussian process.
Let
D=\sup\{d(s,t):s,t\in S\}
be the diameter of
S
under the
canonical metric
d_{Z}
. Then for all
h>0
,

(2.2)

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{s,t\in S}|Z(s)-Z(t)|\geq K\Big(h+\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N_{\varepsilon}(S)}\,d\varepsilon\Big)\right\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}}{D^{2}}\right).

The last lemma of this section provides a probability estimate for the
uniform modulus of continuity for
v
. It will be needed later in the proofs of Theorems

1.4
and
1.5
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under Assumption
1.1
(a), there exist constants
c
and
C

such that for all
s\in T
, for all
r>0
small, for all
L\geq C
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x,y\in I_{r}(s)}|v(x)-v(y)|\geq Lr^{\alpha}\sqrt{\log(1/r)}\right\}\leq r^{L^{2}/c^{2}}.

Proof.

With the notations in Lemma
2.3
, let
S=I_{r}(s)
and
Z(x)=v(x)
.
By Assumption
1.1
(a),
d_{Z}(x,y)\leq c_{0}|x-y|^{\alpha}
.
It follows that the diameter of
S
under
d_{Z}
is
D\leq Cr^{\alpha}
and

N_{\varepsilon}(S)\leq C\varepsilon^{-N/\alpha}
.
Note that for
0<x<x_{0}
with
x_{0}>0
small, the inequality

\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon\leq Cx\sqrt{\log(1/x)}
holds
and the function
x\sqrt{\log(1/x)}
is increasing.
Then for some constant
C
, for
r>0
small, we have

\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N_{\varepsilon}(S)}\,d\varepsilon\leq Cr^{\alpha}\sqrt{\log(1/r)}.

Therefore, the result now follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

3.
A Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm

The goal of this section is to derive the probability estimate in
Proposition
3.3
,
which is a key ingredient of the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
,
and to provide a proof for the Chung-type LIL of Theorem
1.6
.
First, we need to establish two lemmas.
Recall that
I_{r}(s)=T\cap\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
1.2
,
there exist constants
0<K_{0}<\infty
and
0<\rho_{0}<1
such that
for all compact intervals
I_{r}(s)
in
T
with
r\in(0,\rho_{0})
,
for all
0<\varepsilon<r^{\alpha}
and all
0<a<b<\infty
,

(3.1)

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}|\tilde{v}([a,b),x)-\tilde{v}([a,b),s)|\leq\varepsilon\right\}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{K_{0}r}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}\right).

Proof.

Since
\tilde{v}
has i.i.d. components, we only need to prove the lemma for
d=1
.
Recall that
\tilde{v}([a,b),x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})
.
Fix
s\in T
.
Take
\rho_{0}=r_{0}
from Assumption
1.2
.
We first prove that for each
j
,

(3.2)

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x_{j}\in[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]}|\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),s_{j})|\leq\varepsilon/N\right\}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{Kr}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}\right).

We are going to use Lemma
2.2
to prove this inequality
for fixed
j
,
0<r<\rho_{0}
and
0<a<b
.
Take
S=[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
and
Z(x_{j})=\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})
.
By Assumption
1.2
(a),

\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),\cdot)
and
\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[a,b),\cdot)
are independent,
thus

\begin{split}&{\|\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[a,b),x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[a,b),y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&={\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Then Assumption
1.2
(b) implies that for all
x_{j},y_{j}\in S
,

d_{Z}(x_{j},y_{j})\leq{\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha}.

It follows that for all
\varepsilon>0
small,

N_{\varepsilon}(S)\leq\frac{r}{(c_{2}^{-1}\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}}=C_{c_{2},\alpha}\bigg(\frac{r}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}\bigg).

Then we can take
\psi(\varepsilon)=C_{c_{2},\alpha}({r}/{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}})
.
This function satisfies (
2.1
) with constants
a_{1}=a_{2}=2^{1/\alpha}

which are greater than 1. By Lemma
2.2
, we can find a constant
K

depending on
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
c_{2}
,
\alpha
and
N
such that (
3.2
) is
satisfied for all
j
, for all
0<r<\rho_{0}
and
0<\varepsilon<r^{\alpha}
.

To prove (
3.1
), we use an approximation argument and the
Gaussian correlation inequality. In fact, for each
j
, we can take
a sequence
(t_{ij})_{i=1}^{\infty}
that is dense in
[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
.
Since
\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})
is a.s. continuous in
x_{j}
,
the probability on the left-hand side of (
3.1
) is

\geq{\mathbb{P}}\Bigg(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\bigg\{\sup_{x_{j}\in[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]}|\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),s_{j})|\leq\varepsilon/N\bigg\}\Bigg)=\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigg(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\{X^{m}_{j}\in B\}\Bigg),

where
X^{m}_{j}
is the
m
-dimensional Gaussian vector defined by

X^{m}_{j}=\Big(\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),t_{1j})-\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),s_{j}),\dots,\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),t_{mj})-\tilde{v}^{j}([a,b),s_{j})\Big),\quad j=1,\dots,N,

and
B=[-\varepsilon/N,\varepsilon/N]^{m}
.
Now, for fixed
m
, we apply the Gaussian correlation inequality
[
31
,
20
]
to get that

{\mathbb{P}}\Bigg(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\{X^{m}_{j}\in B\}\Bigg)\geq\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\mathbb{P}}(X^{m}_{j}\in B).

For each
j
, as
m\to\infty
,
{\mathbb{P}}(X^{m}_{j}\in B)
converges to the probability on
the left-hand side of (
3.2
). Therefore, we deduce (
3.1
) with
K_{0}=NK
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
,
there exist positive finite constants
K
,
K^{\prime}
and
A_{0}
such that for any
r>0
small,
for any compact interval
I_{r}(s)
in
T
, for any
0<a<b<\infty
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a,b),x)+\tilde{v}([a,b),s)|\geq h\right\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}}{K^{\prime}A^{2}}\right)

provided that
h\geq KA\sqrt{\log(Kr^{\alpha}/A)}
and
A\leq A_{0}r^{\alpha}
, where

A=a^{1-\alpha}r+r^{\gamma_{1}}b^{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}+r^{\gamma_{2}+\alpha}
,
and
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\alpha
and
\gamma_{2}>0
are the constant in
Assumption
1.2
.

Proof.

Since
v(\cdot)-\tilde{v}([a,b),\cdot)
has i.i.d. components,
we may assume that
d=1
.
With the notations in Lemma
2.3
,
set
S=I_{r}(s)
and
Z(x)=v(x)-\tilde{v}([a,b),x)
.
By Assumption
1.2
(c), the canonical metric of
Z
satisfies

d_{Z}(x,y)\leq C\left(a^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+r^{\gamma_{1}}b^{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}+r^{\gamma_{2}}|x-y|^{\alpha}\right)

for all
x,y\in S
. It follows that the diameter of
S
in
d_{Z}
satisfies

D\leq C\left(a^{1-\alpha}r+r^{\gamma_{1}}b^{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}+r^{\gamma_{2}+\alpha}\right)=CA.

Also, by Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, for all
x,y\in S
,

\displaystyle d_{Z}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}.

This implies that

N_{\varepsilon}(S)\leq C\left(\frac{r^{N}}{\varepsilon^{N/\alpha}}\right)

and hence if
D\leq CA_{0}r^{\alpha}
(which is the case if
A\leq A_{0}r^{\alpha}
), there is some large constant
K
such that

\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N_{\varepsilon}(S)}\,d\varepsilon\leq K\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log(Kr^{\alpha}/\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon.

By the change of variable
\varepsilon=Kr^{\alpha}\exp(-u^{2})
and the
elementary inequality
\int_{x}^{\infty}u^{2}\exp(-u^{2})\,du\leq Cx\exp(-x^{2})

for
x
large, we deduce that

\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N_{\varepsilon}(S)}\,d\varepsilon\leq KD\sqrt{\log(Kr^{\alpha}/D)}.

Since
D\leq CA
and the function
f(x)=x\sqrt{\log(r^{\alpha}/x)}

is increasing for
0<x\ll r^{\alpha}
, the right-hand side is

\leq KA\sqrt{\log(Kr^{\alpha}/A)}

provided
A\leq A_{0}r^{\alpha}
, where
A_{0}
is a sufficiently small constant.
Therefore, the result now follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Under Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
,
there exist constants
0<K_{1}<\infty
and
0<\rho_{0}\leq 1
such that
for any compact interval
I_{r}(s)
in
T
, for any
0<r_{0}\leq\rho_{0}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exists\,r\in[r_{0}^{2},r_{0}],\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)|\leq K_{1}r^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}\geq 1-\exp\left(-\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\,\Big)^{1/2}\right).

Remark 3.4
.

This is a key estimate in the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.
Note that the exponent of the
\log\log\frac{1}{r}
factor above is different from
the one in Proposition 4.1 of
[
33
]
and the one in Proposition 2.3 of
[
11
]
.

Proof.

The proof follows the idea of Talagrand
[
33
]
.
Let
r_{0}>0
. Fix
U>1
. The value of
U
will depend on
r_{0}
and
will be determined later.
For
\ell\geq 1
, define
r_{\ell}=r_{0}U^{-2\ell}
and
a_{\ell}=r_{0}^{-1}U^{2\ell-1}
.
Let
\ell_{0}
be the largest integer such that

(3.3)

\ell_{0}\leq\frac{\log(1/r_{0})}{2\log U}.

Then we have
r_{0}^{2}\leq r_{\ell}\leq r_{0}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0}
.
It suffices to prove that for some constant
K_{1}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exists\,1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0},\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)|\leq K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}\geq 1-\exp\left(-\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\,\Big)^{1/2}\right).

By Lemma
3.1
, if we take
K_{1}=2(4K_{0})^{\alpha}
,
then for all
1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)=\left(\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-1/4}.

By Assumption
1.2
(a), the processes
\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),\cdot)
,

\ell=1,\dots,\ell_{0}
, are independent, so

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exists\,1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0},\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}\\
&=1-\prod_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\left(1-{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}\right)\\
&\geq 1-\bigg(1-\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\Big)^{-1/4}\bigg)^{\ell_{0}}\\
&\geq 1-\exp\left(-\ell_{0}\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\Big)^{-1/4}\right).\end{aligned}

Let
A_{\ell}=a_{\ell}^{1-\alpha}r_{\ell}+a_{\ell+1}^{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}r_{\ell}^{\gamma_{1}}+r_{\ell}^{\gamma_{2}+\alpha}
,
where
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\alpha
and
\gamma_{2}>0
are the constants
given by Assumption
1.2
(c).
Then

A_{\ell}r_{\ell}^{-\alpha}=(a_{\ell}r_{\ell})^{1-\alpha}+(a_{\ell+1}r_{\ell})^{-(\alpha-\gamma_{1})}+r_{\ell}^{\gamma_{2}}.

Note that
a_{\ell}r_{\ell}=U^{-1}
and
a_{\ell+1}r_{\ell}=U
.
Set
\beta=\min\{1-\alpha,\alpha-\gamma_{1}\}>0
. Then

(3.6)

A_{\ell}r_{\ell}^{-\alpha}\leq 3U^{-\beta}

provided

(3.7)

r_{0}^{\gamma_{2}}\leq U^{-\beta}.

In particular,
A_{\ell}r_{\ell}^{-\alpha}\leq A_{0}
if
U
is large enough.
Set
h_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r_{\ell}))^{-\alpha}
.
By Lemma
3.2
, for some constants
K
and
K^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\geq h_{\ell}\right\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}_{\ell}}{K^{\prime}A_{\ell}^{2}}\right)

provided
h_{\ell}\geq Kr_{\ell}^{\alpha}U^{-\beta}\sqrt{\log(KU)}
, that is, provided

(3.8)

U^{\beta}(\log(KU))^{-1/2}\geq\frac{2K}{K_{1}}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{\alpha},

which is possible if
U
is large enough (depending on
r_{0}
).
Then by (
3.6
), we get that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\geq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{U^{2\beta}}{C(\log\log(1/r_{0}))^{2\alpha}}\right).\end{aligned}

Let

\displaystyle F_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\},

\displaystyle G_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{\ell},a_{\ell+1}),s)|\geq\frac{1}{2}K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exists\,1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0},\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)|\leq K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}(F_{\ell}\cap G_{\ell}^{c})\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}F_{\ell}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}G_{\ell}^{c}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}F_{\ell}\right)-{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}G_{\ell}\right).

By (
3.5
),

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}F_{\ell}\right)\geq 1-\exp\left(-\ell_{0}\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\Big)^{-1/4}\right),

and by (
3.9
),

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}G_{\ell}\right)\leq\ell_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{U^{2\beta}}{C(\log\log(1/r_{0}))^{2\alpha}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exists\,1\leq\ell\leq\ell_{0},\sup_{x\in I_{r_{\ell}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)|\leq K_{1}r_{\ell}^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{\ell}}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\exp\left(-\ell_{0}\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\Big)^{-1/4}\right)-\ell_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{U^{2\beta}}{C(\log\log(1/r_{0}))^{2\alpha}}\right).

Therefore, the proof of (
3.4
) will be complete provided

(3.10)

\exp\left(-\ell_{0}\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}}\,\Big)^{-1/4}\right)+\ell_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{U^{2\beta}}{C(\log\log(1/r_{0}))^{2\alpha}}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\,\Big)^{1/2}\right).

Recall that conditions (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) are required for
U
.
Hence, we can take

U=\left(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}.

Then for all
r_{0}
small enough, by (
3.3
),

\ell_{0}>\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{-1}>1.

Therefore, the left-hand side of (
3.10
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}})^{3/4}}{C^{\prime}\log\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}}\right)+\left(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}}{C(\log\log\frac{1}{r_{0}})^{2\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\Big(\log\frac{1}{r_{0}}\Big)^{1/2}\right)

provided
r_{0}
is small enough. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We first prove that for each
s\in T
,
there exists a constant
0\leq K\leq\infty
such that

(3.11)

\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|v(x)-v(s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}=K\quad\text{a.s.}

We claim that the
\liminf
in (
3.11
) is equal to

(3.12)

\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}\quad\text{a.s.}

Indeed, by Assumption
1.2
(c), there exists a constant
C
such that for
r
small,
for all
x,y\in I_{r}(s)
,

{\|v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),y)\|}_{L^{2}}\leq Cr^{\gamma}|x-y|^{\alpha},

where
\gamma=\min\{1-\alpha,\gamma_{2}\}>0
.
Let
S=I_{r}(s)
and
Z(x)=v(x)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)
.
Then the
d_{Z}
-diameter of
S
is

D\leq Cr^{\gamma+\alpha}
, and
by the calculations in Lemma
3.2
, we get that for
r
small,

\int_{0}^{D}\sqrt{\log N_{\varepsilon}(S)}\,d\varepsilon\leq KD\sqrt{\log(Kr^{\alpha}/D)}\leq Kr^{\gamma+\alpha}\sqrt{\log(K/r)}.

By taking
r_{n}=2^{-n}
and using Lemma
2.3
,
we see that provided
K
is large,

{\mathbb{P}}\Bigg\{\sup_{x\in I_{r_{n}}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),s)|\geq Kr_{n}^{\gamma+\alpha}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}\Bigg\}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{K^{2}n}{C^{2}}\right)

and hence by the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in I_{r_{n}}(s)}\frac{|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),s)|}{r_{n}^{\gamma+\alpha}\sqrt{\log(1/r_{n})}}\leq K\quad\text{a.s.}

This implies that

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|v(x)-v(s)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

From this, it follows that the
\liminf
in (
3.11
) is equal to the one in
(
3.12
).

We continue with the proof of (
3.11
). For each
n\geq 0
, set
a_{n}=a_{0}+n
.
By Assumption
1.2
(a),

\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{v}([a_{n},a_{n+1}),x)\quad\text{a.s.}

and the Gaussian fields
\tilde{v}([a_{n},a_{n+1}),\cdot)
,
n\geq 0
, are independent.
Consider the
\sigma
-algebras

\mathscr{F}_{n}=\sigma\{\tilde{v}([a_{m},a_{m+1}),\cdot):m\geq n\}
and the

\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}_{n}
of all tail events.
By Kolmogorov’s zero–one law,
{\mathbb{P}}(A)=0
or
1
for all
A\in\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
.
This will imply (
3.11
) if we can show that
the random variable in (
3.12
) is
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
-measurable.

For each
n\geq 1
, we write
\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)=Y_{n}(x)+Z_{n}(x)
, where

Y_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tilde{v}([a_{i},a_{i+1}),x)=\tilde{v}([a_{0},a_{n}),x)\quad\text{and}\quad Z_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\tilde{v}([a_{i},a_{i+1}),x).

By Assumption
1.2
(c), there is a constant
C
(depending on
n
) such that
for all
x,y\in I_{r}(s)
,

\displaystyle{\|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(y)\|}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{\|v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{0},\infty),y)\|}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+{\|v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a_{n},\infty),x)+\tilde{v}([a_{n},\infty),y)\|}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\gamma}|x-y|^{\alpha},

where
\gamma=\min\{1-\alpha,\gamma_{2}\}
.
Then, as in the first part of the proof, we use Lemma
2.3
to deduce that

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|Y_{n}(x)-Y_{n}(s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

It follows the random variable in (

) is equal to

\liminf_{r\to 0}\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}\frac{|Z_{n}(x)-Z_{n}(s)|}{r^{\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{-\alpha}}\quad\text{a.s.}

Since

is arbitrary, this random variable is

-measurable
and therefore constant a.s. by Kolmogorov’s zero–one law.
Combining with the claim in (

), we obtain (

)
with

.

Finally, we apply Proposition
3.3
with

and
the Borel–Cantelli lemma to deduce the upper bound

, while
the lower bound

follows from Theorem 3.4 of
[
38
]

under Assumption
1.1
.
∎

4.
Sojourn time estimates

In this section, we derive estimates for the sojourn times of
v
.
For each
s\in T
and
r>0
, define

\tau(s,r)=\int_{T}{\bf 1}_{\{|v(x)-v(s)|\leq r\}}dx=\lambda\{x\in T:|v(x)-v(s)|\leq r\},

which is the sojourn time of
v
in the ball centered at
v(s)
with radius
r
.
The following proposition provides upper bounds for the moments of
\tau(s,r)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let Assumption
1.1
hold.
Then there exists a finite constant
K
such that
for all
n\geq 1
, for all
x^{*}\in T
, for all
r>0
,

{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(x^{*},r)^{n}]\leq K^{n}(n!)^{N}r^{nN/\alpha}.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(x^{*},r)^{n}]=\int_{T^{n}}{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|v(x^{i})-v(x^{*})|\leq r\right\}dx^{1}\dots dx^{n}.

For
m=1,\dots,n
, define the event
A_{m}
by

A_{m}=\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|v(x^{i})-v(x^{*})|\leq r\right\}.

Then

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n})={\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bf 1}_{A_{n-1}}{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|v(x^{n})-v(x^{*})|\leq r\,\Big|\,v(x^{*}),v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n-1})\Big\}\right].

Since
v
is Gaussian, so is the conditional distribution of
v(x^{n})
given

v(x^{*}),v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n-1})
which, by
sectorial LND [Assumption
1.1
(b)],
has conditional variance

\mathrm{Var}(v(x^{n})|v(x^{*}),v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n-1}))

\geq c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{i}|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|^{2\alpha}
,
where the
\min_{i}
is taken over
i\in\{\ast,0,\dots,n-1\}
and
x^{0}=0
.
Then by Anderson’s inequality
[
3
]
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{|v(x^{n})-v(x^{*})|\leq r\,\Big|\,v(x^{*}),v(x^{1}),\dots,v(x^{n-1})\Big\}\leq K\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{r^{d}}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{i}{|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|}^{2\alpha}\big)^{d/2}}\Bigg\}.

Note the elementary inequality

(\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j})^{\alpha}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}^{\alpha}

for
0<\alpha<1
and
z_{j}\geq 0
.
Now, let us fix
x^{*},x^{1},\dots,x^{n-1}
and estimate the integral

(4.1)

\int_{T}\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{r^{d}}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{i}{|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|}^{2}\big)^{\alpha d/2}}\Bigg\}\,dx^{n}.

We may assume that
x^{*},x^{1},\dots,x^{n}
are distinct,
because the set of
(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
for which they are not distinct
has Lebesgue measure 0.
Consider the following set consisting of
(n+1)^{N}
points:

Y=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left\{x^{*}_{j},x^{0}_{j},x^{1}_{j},\dots,x^{n-1}_{j}\right\}.

We use these points to produce a partition of
T
into subintervals
S
such that
for each subinterval, there is one and only one
y\in Y
such that

(4.2)

\min_{i}|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|=|x^{n}_{j}-y_{j}|

for all
x^{n}\in S
and all
j=1,\dots,N
.
This can be done by first ordering the points with permutations

\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{N}
on the set
\{\ast,0,\dots,n-1\}
such that for every
j
,

x_{j}^{\Pi_{j}(\ast)}<x_{j}^{\Pi_{j}(0)}<\dots<x_{j}^{\Pi_{j}(n-1)},

and then using the perpendicular bisectors of every two adjacent points to construct
the partition. There are at most
(n+1)^{N}=O(n^{N})
subintervals
S
.
Then the integral in (
4.1
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{S}\int_{S}\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{r^{d}}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{i}{|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|}^{2}\big)^{\alpha d/2}}\Bigg\}\,dx^{n}.

For each fixed subinterval
S
, we use (
4.2
) and polar coordinates

x^{n}-y=\rho\theta
to get that

\displaystyle\int_{S}\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{r^{d}}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}{|x^{n}_{j}-y_{j}|}^{2}\big)^{\alpha d/2}}\Bigg\}\,dx^{n}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{1,{r^{d}}{\rho^{-\alpha d}}\right\}\rho^{N-1}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=K\int_{0}^{r^{1/\alpha}}\rho^{N-1}\,d\rho+K\int_{r^{1/\alpha}}^{\infty}r^{d}\rho^{N-\alpha d-1}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=Kr^{N/\alpha}.

It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(x^{*},r)^{n}]

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{n-1}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n-1})dx^{1}\dots x^{n-1}\int_{T}\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{r^{d}}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\min_{i}|x^{n}_{j}-x^{i}_{j}|}^{2}\big)^{\alpha d/2}}\Bigg\}\,dx^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Kn^{N}r^{N/\alpha}\int_{T^{n-1}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{n-1})dx^{1}\dots dx^{n-1}

\displaystyle=Kn^{N}r^{N/\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(x^{*},r)^{n-1}].

Therefore, the result can be deduced by induction.
∎

From the moment estimates, we can derive the following asymptotic result for the
sojourn times, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under Assumption
1.1
, for all
s\in T
,

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\tau(s,r)}{\phi(r)}\leq K\quad\text{a.s.},

where
\phi(r)=r^{N/\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{N}
and
K
is the constant in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

Consider a constant
C>K
.
By Fubini’s theorem and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(C^{-1/N}r^{-1/\alpha}\tau(s,r)^{1/N})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}C^{-n/N}r^{-n/\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(s,r)^{n/N}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}C^{-n/N}r^{-n/\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}[\tau(s,r)^{n}]^{1/N}.

Then by Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(C^{-1/N}r^{-1/\alpha}\tau(s,r)^{1/N})]\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(K/C)^{n/N}=:A,

where
A
is finite since
C>K
.
It follows that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\tau(s,r)>(1+\varepsilon)^{N}Cr^{N/\alpha}(\log\log(1/r))^{N}\right\}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left\{\exp(C^{-1/N}r^{-1/\alpha}\tau(s,r)^{1/N})>(\log(1/r))^{1+\varepsilon}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{(\log(1/r))^{1+\varepsilon}}.

Set
r_{n}=e^{-n/\log n}
. Then by the Borel–Cantelli lemma, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tau(s,r_{n})}{\phi(r_{n})}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{N}C\quad\text{a.s.}

From this, we can use a monotonicity argument to deduce that

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\tau(s,r)}{\phi(r)}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{N}C\quad\text{a.s.}

Finally, we obtain the result by letting

\varepsilon\to 0
and
C\downarrow K
along rational sequences.
∎

Proof of Theorem

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{C_{1}\lambda(J)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(v(J))\leq C_{2}\lambda(J)\Big\}=1.

) follows by taking sequences

of compact intervals with

The proof of (

[

]

We start with the proof of the lower bound.
Let

be any compact interval in

Define a random Borel measure

\mu(B)=\lambda\{s\in J:v(s)\in B\}

Then by the change of variable formula and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\mu(F)]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{F}(z)\mu(dz)={\mathbb{E}}\int_{J}{\bf 1}_{F}(v(s))\,ds

We turn to the proof of the upper bound in (

The idea is due to Talagrand

For each

\displaystyle R_{p}=\left\{s\in J:\exists\,r\in[2^{-2p},2^{-p}]\text{ such that }\sup_{x\in I_{r}(s)}|v(x)-v(s)|\leq K_{1}r^{\alpha}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{-\alpha}\right\}

Then by Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{p,1}^{c})\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[\lambda(J\setminus R_{p})]}{\lambda(J)\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)}.

By Fubini’s theorem and Proposition

, the numerator is equal to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{J}{\bf 1}_{J\setminus R_{p}}(s)\,ds=\int_{J}{\mathbb{P}}(s\not\in R_{p})\,ds\leq\lambda(J)\exp(-\sqrt{p/2}).

It follows that

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{p,1}^{c})\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)<\infty
.

We will call an interval of the form
\prod_{j=1}^{N}[m_{j}2^{-p},(m_{j}+1)2^{-p})

, a dyadic cube of order
p

Let

that intersect

,
and for convenience, we replace each

by the intersection

.
Let
K_{2}
be a constant and consider the event

Since the number of cubes in

is

,
it follows from Lemma
2.4
that

is large enough.
Let
\Omega_{p}=\Omega_{p,1}\cap\Omega_{p,2}
.
Then with probability 1,
\Omega_{p}
occurs for all
p
large.
Let
\omega\in\Omega_{p}
.
We are going to construct a random cover for

.

We say that

\sup_{x,y\in C}|v(x)-v(y)|\leq 4K_{1}2^{-q\alpha}(\log\log 2^{q})^{-\alpha}.

Then for each

(

(

, define
s_{C}
as
the lower-left vertex of
C
and
r_{C}
as in (
5.3
).
Now, we construct the family
\mathcal{G}_{p}=\mathcal{G}_{p}(\omega)
of cubes
by setting

\mathcal{G}_{p}=\{C\in\mathcal{H}_{p}:\omega\in\Omega_{C}\},

where

\Omega_{C}=\{|v(s_{C})-z|\leq 2r_{C}\}.

We verify that
\mathcal{G}_{p}
covers
v^{-1}(z)\cap J
for
\omega\in\Omega_{p}

and
p
large.
Let
y\in v^{-1}(z)\cap J
. Then
v(y)=z
and
y\in C

for some dyadic cube
C\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
because
\mathcal{H}_{p}
covers
J
.
We need to show that
C
is a member of
\mathcal{G}_{p}
.
Consider the following two cases.

Case 1. If
C
is a good dyadic cube in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
of order
q
,
where
p\leq q\leq 2p
, then condition (
6.3
) is satisfied and thus

|v^{1}(s_{C})-v^{1}(y)|\leq r_{C}.

Since
\omega\in\Omega_{p,3}
, by (
6.2
),

|v^{2}(s_{C})-v^{2}(y)|\leq K2^{-q(\delta-\beta)},

which is
\leq 4K_{1}2^{-q\alpha}(\log\log 2^{q})^{-\alpha}=r_{C}
for
p
large
because
\alpha<\delta-\beta
.
Since
v=v^{1}+v^{2}
, it follows that

|v(s_{C})-z|=|v(s_{C})-v(y)|\leq 2r_{C}.

Hence
\Omega_{C}
occurs and
C\in\mathcal{G}_{p}
.

Case 2. If
C
is a bad dyadic cube in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
, then
C
of order
2p
.
Since
\omega\in\Omega_{p,2}
, we have

|v(s_{C})-z|=|v(s_{C})-v(y)|\leq r_{C}

and hence
C\in\mathcal{G}_{p}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{G}_{p}
covers
v^{-1}(z)\cap J
for
\omega\in\Omega_{p}

and
p
large.

Consider the
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma_{1}=\sigma\{v^{1}(y):y\in J\}
.
In order to estimate
{\mathbb{E}}[\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(z)\cap J)]

using the random cover
\mathcal{G}_{p}
,
we need to first estimate the conditional probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{C}|\Sigma_{1})
.
Note that
\mathrm{Var}(v(y)|v(x^{\prime}))\leq{\mathbb{E}}[(v(y)-v(x^{\prime}))^{2}]
.
Then by the conditional variance formula and
Assumptions
1.1
(a) and
1.3
(a), we get that for all
y\in J
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(v^{2}(y))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}({\mathbb{E}}(v(y)|v(x^{\prime})))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(v(y))-{\mathbb{E}}[\mathrm{Var}(v(y)|v(x^{\prime}))]

\displaystyle\geq c_{3}^{2}-c_{0}^{2}|y-x^{\prime}|^{2\alpha}.

Since
J
has side length
2\rho
and
x^{\prime}\in J^{(c\rho)}
, with
0<\rho\leq\rho_{0}
,
we can choose
\rho_{0}>0
to be small enough so that
the variance of
v^{2}(y)
is bounded below by a positive constant.
It follows that
{\mathbb{P}}(|v^{2}(y)-a|\leq r)\leq Kr^{d}
for all
y\in J
,
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
,
where
K
is a constant.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
and
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
depend on
v^{1}
only, so

r_{C}
is
\Sigma_{1}
-measurable.
Hence, by the independence of
v^{1}
and
v^{2}
,

(6.4)

{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{C}|\Sigma_{1})\leq Kr_{C}^{d}.

Now, we estimate
{\mathbb{E}}[\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(z)\cap J)]
using the cover
\mathcal{G}_{p}
.
Recall that the cubes of
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
are contained in
J\setminus R_{p}
,
and note that on
\Omega_{p,1}
, the set
J\setminus R_{p}
has Lebesgue measure

\leq\lambda(J)\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)
.
It follows that
\Omega_{p}
is contained in the event
\tilde{\Omega}_{p}

that the number of cubes in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
is
at most
\lambda(J)2^{2pN}\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)
.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\Omega_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{G}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C)){\bf 1}_{\Omega_{C}}\Bigg].

Since
\tilde{\Omega}_{p}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}
are measurable with respect to

\Sigma_{1}
,
we can take conditional expectation on
\Sigma_{1}
and use (
6.4
) to
get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C)){\mathbb{P}}(\Omega_{C}|\Sigma_{1})\Bigg]\leq K{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))r_{C}^{d}\Bigg].

Recall that
\varphi(r)=r^{N-\alpha d}(\log\log(1/r))^{\alpha d}
.
If
C\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}
is a good dyadic cube of order
q
, then

\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))r_{C}^{d}\leq K2^{-q(N-\alpha d)}(\log q)^{\alpha d}(2^{-q\alpha}(\log q)^{-\alpha})^{d}\leq K2^{-qN}.

It follows that

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))r_{C}^{d}\Bigg]\leq K\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{p}}\lambda(C)\leq K\lambda(J).

If
C\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
is a bad dyadic cube, then

\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))r_{C}^{d}\leq K2^{-2pN}(\log p)^{\alpha d}p^{d/2}.

Since the number of cubes in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}
is

\leq\lambda(J)2^{2pN}\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)
on
\tilde{\Omega}_{p}
, it follows that

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\tilde{\Omega}_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))r_{C}^{d}\Bigg]\leq K\lambda(J)\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)(\log p)^{\alpha d}p^{d/2}.

Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(z)\cap J)]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[{\bf 1}_{\Omega_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{G}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C))\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left[K\lambda(J)\left(1+\exp(-\sqrt{p}/4)(\log p)^{\alpha d}p^{d/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=K\lambda(J).

This completes the proof of (
6.1
) and establishes (
1.5
).

Consider the case
N\leq\alpha d
.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, the event

\Omega^{\prime}:=\{v^{-1}(z)\cap J\neq\varnothing\}
has positive probability.
Then the event
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Omega^{*}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\bigcap_{p\geq n}(\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{p})

also has positive probability.
Consider the random variable

X=\liminf_{p\to\infty}{\bf 1}_{\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{p}}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{G}_{p}}\varphi(\mathrm{diam}(C)).

By the calculations above,
{\mathbb{E}}(X)\leq K\lambda(J)<\infty
.
On the other hand, for
p
large,
since
\mathcal{G}_{p}
covers
v^{-1}(z)\cap J
on
\Omega_{p}

and
v^{-1}(z)\cap J\neq\varnothing
on
\Omega^{\prime}
,
we see that
\mathcal{G}_{p}
is nonempty on
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{p}
.
Moreover,
N\leq\alpha d
implies that
\varphi(r)\to\infty
as
r\to 0
.
It follows that
X=\infty
on
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Omega^{*}
, which contradicts the fact that

{\mathbb{E}}(X)<\infty
. Therefore, we have
v^{-1}(z)\cap J\neq\varnothing
a.s. for all
sufficiently small compact intervals
J
in
T
.
This implies
v^{-1}(z)\cap T\neq\varnothing
a.s. and
completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

7.
Examples

7.1.
Brownian sheet and fractional Brownian sheets

Let
v=\{v(x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}\}
be a fractional Brownian sheet
from
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with Hurst indices
H_{1}=\dots=H_{N}=\alpha

where
0<\alpha<1
, i.e., a centered, continuous Gaussian random field
with i.i.d. components
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}
and covariance

{\mathbb{E}}[v_{i}(x)v_{i}(y)]=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}(|x_{j}|^{2\alpha}+|y_{j}|^{2\alpha}-|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha}).

When
\alpha=1/2
,
v
is a Brownian sheet from
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

The uniform dimension of the level sets of the Brownian sheet was studied by Adler

[
1
]
, and that of the images was studied by Mountford
[
26
]
and
Khoshnevisan et al.
[
18
]
.
The Hausdorff measures of the range and graph of the Brownian sheet were studied
by Ehm
[
13
]
.
For fractional Brownian sheets, dimension results for the images and level sets
can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
37
]
.

It is known that for any compact interval
T
in
(0,\infty)^{N}
, there exists a
positive finite constant
c_{0}
such that for all
x,y\in T
,

(7.1)

c_{0}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha}\leq{\mathbb{E}}(|v(x)-v(y)|^{2})\leq c_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha},

and
v
satisfies sectorial LND:
there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that for all integers
n\geq 1
,
for all
x,y^{1},\dots,y^{n}\in T
,

(7.2)

\mathrm{Var}(v_{1}(x)|v_{1}(y^{1}),\dots,v_{1}(y^{n}))\geq c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\min_{0\leq i\leq n}|x_{j}-y_{j}^{i}|^{2\alpha},

where
y^{0}=0
. See
[
5
,
37
]
.

Let us recall the
harmonizable representation
for the fractional Brownian sheet

[
10
, §5.1]
.
For any
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have the identity

\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2\alpha}+|y|^{2\alpha}-|x-y|^{2\alpha})=c_{\alpha}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left[\frac{(1-\cos{x\xi})(1-\cos{y\xi})}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+1}}+\frac{\sin{x\xi}\sin{y\xi}}{|\xi|^{2\alpha+1}}\right]\,d\xi,

where
c_{\alpha}
is a constant depending on
\alpha
.
This implies that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,

(7.3)

\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}(|x_{j}|^{2\alpha}+|y_{j}|^{2\alpha}-|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha})=c_{\alpha}^{N}\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{2\alpha+1}}\,d\xi,

where
f_{0}(x)=1-\cos{x}
and
f_{1}(x)=\sin{x}
.
Therefore,
v
has the following representation:

(7.4)

v(x)\overset{d}{=}c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\,W_{p}(d\xi),

where
c=c_{\alpha}^{N/2}
and
W_{p}(d\xi)
,
p\in\{0,1\}^{N}
,
are i.i.d.
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued white noises on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
In what follows, we assume that
v
is defined by this representation,
which allows us to introduce a set variable
A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})

and define

v(A,x)=c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\int_{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in A}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\,W_{p}(d\xi),

where
{|\xi|}_{\infty}=\max\{|\xi_{j}|:1\leq j\leq N\}
.
For each
A
,
x\mapsto v(A,x)
has a continuous version because of

\|v(A,x)-v(A,y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v(x)-v(y)\|_{L^{2}}
and (
7.1
),
and we will use such a version.

Let
T
be a compact interval in
(0,\infty)^{N}
.
Fix
s\in T
.
We are going to verify Assumption
1.2
.
By re-centering and re-scaling, we can consider the intervals

\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j},s_{j}+r]
instead of
\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j}-r,s_{j}+r]
.
This will simplify notations.

Motivated by the representation (
1.1
) for the Brownian sheet,
we define, for each
j
, the Gaussian process

\{\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j}):A\in\mathscr{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),x_{j}\geq s_{j}\}
(depending on
s
)
by

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})

\displaystyle:=v(A,(s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},x_{j},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{N}))-v(A,s)

\displaystyle=c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\int_{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in A}\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(s_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(s_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\,W_{p}(d\xi).

Set
\tilde{v}(A,x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Fix
s\in T
. Take
r_{0}=1
, and let
\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})
and
\tilde{v}(A,x)

be defined as above.
Then there exists a finite constant
c
depending on
T
but not on
s

such that the following statements hold.

(a)

For each
j
and
x_{j}
,
A\mapsto\tilde{v}^{j}(A,x_{j})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued
independently scattered Gaussian measure.
The
\sigma
-algebra

\sigma\{\tilde{v}^{1}(A,\cdot),\dots,\tilde{v}^{N}(A,\cdot)\}
is independent of

\sigma\{\tilde{v}^{1}(B,\cdot),\dots,\tilde{v}^{N}(B,\cdot)\}
whenever

A
and
B
are fixed, disjoint sets.

(b)

For all
j
, for all
x_{j},y_{j}\in[s_{j},s_{j}+r_{0}]
,

{\|\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})\|}_{L^{2}}\leq c\,|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{\alpha}.

(c)

Take
\gamma_{1}=0
,
\gamma_{2}=\alpha
and
a_{0}=0
.
Then for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
, for all
x,y\in\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j},s_{j}+r]
, for
all
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
,

(7.5)

\displaystyle\qquad{\|v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a,b),x)+\tilde{v}([a,b),y)\|}_{L^{2}}\leq c\left(a^{1-\alpha}|x-y|+b^{-\alpha}+r^{\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha}\right).

Proof.

First, (a) is obvious because
W_{p}
,
p\in\{0,1\}^{N}
, are independent white noises,
and (b) follows from (
7.1
) since

\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},x_{j})-\tilde{v}^{j}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},y_{j})=v(s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},x_{j},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{N})-v(s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},y_{j},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{N}).

It remains to prove (c). Let
x,y\in\prod_{j=1}^{N}[s_{j},s_{j}+r]
.
Notice the telescoping sum:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}-\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg).

It follows that

\displaystyle v(x)-v(y)-\tilde{v}([a,b),x)+\tilde{v}([a,b),y)

\displaystyle=c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)W_{p}(d\xi)

\displaystyle\quad+c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in[a,b)}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(s_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)W_{p}(d\xi)

\displaystyle=c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Bigg[\int_{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[a,b)}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)W_{p}(d\xi)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in[a,b)}\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg(\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}-\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(s_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)W_{p}(d\xi)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=:c\sum_{p\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big[u_{p,j}^{1}(x,y)+u_{p,j}^{2}(x,y)\big].

Fix
p\in\{0,1\}^{N}
and
1\leq j\leq N
.
Let us consider
u_{p,j}^{1}
first. Note that

\big\{{|\xi|}_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus[a,b)\big\}=\big\{|\xi_{k}|<a,\forall\,1\leq k\leq N\big\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\big\{|\xi_{k}|\geq b\big\}.

Also,
|f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})|\leq|x_{j}-y_{j}||\xi_{j}|
and

|f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})|\leq 2
for
p_{j}=0,1
.
It follows that

\displaystyle{\|u_{p,j}^{1}(x,y)\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{\{|\xi_{k}|<a,\forall k\}}\frac{|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}}{|\xi_{j}|^{2\alpha-1}}\Bigg(\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad+d\int_{\{|\xi_{j}|\geq b\}}\frac{4}{|\xi_{j}|^{2\alpha+1}}\Bigg(\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad+d\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{\{|\xi_{k}|\geq b\}}\frac{1}{|\xi_{k}|^{2\alpha+1}}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i<j,i\neq k}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j,i\neq k}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi.

By (
7.3
) applied to
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
instead of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\Bigg(\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi\leq C\prod_{i<j}|y_{i}|^{2\alpha}\prod_{i>j}|x_{i}|^{2\alpha}.

Also, using the representation (
7.4
) and the estimate
(
7.1
) for a fractional Brownian sheet on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}_{+}
, we get that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i<j,i\neq k}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j,i\neq k}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi\leq C|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha}.

Then

\displaystyle{\|u_{p,j}^{1}(x,y)\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(a^{2-2\alpha}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2}\prod_{i<j}|y_{i}|^{2\alpha}\prod_{i>j}|x_{i}|^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad+b^{-2\alpha}\prod_{i<j}|y_{i}|^{2\alpha}\prod_{i>j}|x_{i}|^{2\alpha}+b^{-2\alpha}|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(a^{2-2\alpha}|x-y|^{2}+b^{-2\alpha}\right).

For
u^{2}_{p,j}
, we use (
7.1
) in a similar way to deduce that

\displaystyle{\|u^{2}_{p,j}(x,y)\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Bigg(\frac{f_{p_{j}}(x_{j}\xi_{j})-f_{p_{j}}(y_{j}\xi_{j})}{|\xi_{j}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg(\prod_{i<j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(y_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\prod_{i>j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}-\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{f_{p_{i}}(s_{i}\xi_{i})}{|\xi_{i}|^{\alpha+1/2}}\Bigg)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{2\alpha}\bigg(\sum_{i<j}|y_{i}-s_{i}|^{2\alpha}+\sum_{i>j}|x_{i}-s_{i}|^{2\alpha}\bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C|x-y|^{2\alpha}r^{2\alpha}.

Therefore, we have

{\|w_{4}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq Cr|\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}|^{2-\beta}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
7.4
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

If
|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|\leq r
and
|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|\leq r
, then

(7.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{0}^{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|f(\eta,\theta)-f(\eta^{\prime},\theta)-f(\eta,\theta^{\prime})+f(\eta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})|^{2}|\xi|^{\beta-1}d\tau d\xi\\
&\hskip 150.0pt\leq Cr\cdot\min\{|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|^{2-\beta},|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|^{2-\beta}\}.\end{split}

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\eta^{\prime}<\eta
and
\theta^{\prime}<\theta
.
Let
I
denote the integral in (
7.16
).
Then by (
7.13
),

I={\mathbb{E}}(|v(\eta,\theta)-v(\eta^{\prime},\theta)-v(\eta,\theta^{\prime})+v(\eta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})|^{2}).

By (
7.8
) and (
7.14
),

I=\frac{d}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A)|^{2})
,
where
A
is the rotated rectangle with vertices

\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\text{ and }\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta^{\prime}+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big).

Note that
A
is contained in
A^{\prime}
, where
A^{\prime}
is the parallelogram with vertices

\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}-2\eta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\text{ and }\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\eta+\theta-2\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big).

Then by (
7.7
), since
{\bf 1}_{A}\leq{\bf 1}_{A^{\prime}}
and
f\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A)|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A^{\prime})|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\beta}}{2\pi}\int_{\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}}^{\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\xi\,|\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[s-\sqrt{2}\eta,s-\sqrt{2}\eta^{\prime}]}(\xi)|^{2}\,|\xi|^{\beta-1}.

For
a<b
, the Fourier transform of
{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}
is

\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)=\frac{1}{i\xi}(e^{-ia\xi}-e^{-ib\xi})
.
Then by scaling,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathscr{F}{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}(\xi)|^{2}\,|\xi|^{\beta-1}d\xi=(b-a)^{2-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|1-e^{-i\xi}|^{2}\,|\xi|^{\beta-3}d\xi.

Hence

{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A)|^{2})\leq C\int_{\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}}^{\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}}(\eta-\eta^{\prime})^{2-\beta}ds\leq Cr(\eta-\eta^{\prime})^{2-\beta}.

Similarly, by considering
A\subset A^{\prime\prime}
, where
A^{\prime\prime}
is the parallelogram with vertices

\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{2\theta-\eta^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\text{ and }\Big(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{2\theta^{\prime}-\eta-\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\Big),

we can show that

{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A)|^{2})\leq{\mathbb{E}}(|\tilde{W}_{1}(A^{\prime\prime})|^{2})\leq Cr(\theta-\theta^{\prime})^{2-\beta}
.
The proof of (
7.16
) is complete.
∎

[
11
]
, it is shown that the stochastic wave equation
in spatial dimension
k\geq 1
with
\beta\geq 1
satisfies Assumption
1.3
with

\delta=2-\beta
.
We verify this assumption for
k=1

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
T
be a compact rectangle in
D:=\{(\eta,\theta):\eta+\theta>0\}
.
Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1
be such that
T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}\subset\subset D
.
Take
c=1
and
\delta=1
.

(a)

There exists a positive constant
c_{3}
such that

{\|v(\eta,\theta)\|}_{L^{2}}\geq c_{3}
for all
(\eta,\theta)\in T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}
.

(b)

Let
I\subset T
be a compact rectangle and
0<\rho\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.
For
(\eta_{0},\theta_{0})\in I
, take
(\eta_{0}^{\prime},\theta_{0}^{\prime})=(\eta_{0},\theta_{0})
.
Then there is a constant
c_{4}
such that
for all
(\eta_{1},\theta_{1}),(\eta_{2},\theta_{2})\in I^{(\rho)}
with

|\eta_{1}-\eta|+|\theta_{1}-\theta|\leq 2\rho
and

|\eta_{2}-\eta|+|\theta_{2}-\theta|\leq 2\rho
,
for all
i=1,\dots,d
,

(7.17)

|{\mathbb{E}}[(v_{i}(\eta_{1},\theta_{1})-v_{i}(\eta_{2},\theta_{2}))v_{i}(\eta_{0}^{\prime},\theta_{0}^{\prime})]|\leq c_{4}(|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|+|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|).

Proof.

For any
t>0
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, by (
7.7
) and change of variables
(
r=t-s
and then
\zeta=r\xi
), we have

\displaystyle{\|U(t,x)\|}_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}\frac{\sin^{2}((t-s)|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}dr\,r^{2-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\zeta}{|\zeta|^{3-\beta}}\sin^{2}(|\zeta|)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}t^{3-\beta}.

Hence (a) is satisfied provided
t=\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}}\geq c>0
for all

(\eta,\theta)\in T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}
, which is true since
T^{(\varepsilon_{0})}\subset\subset D
.
For (b), write
(t,x)=(\frac{\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta+\theta}{\sqrt{2}})

and
(t_{0}^{\prime},x_{0}^{\prime})=(\frac{\eta_{0}^{\prime}+\theta_{0}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta_{0}^{\prime}+\theta_{0}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}})=(\frac{\eta_{0}+\theta_{0}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{-\eta_{0}+\theta_{0}}{\sqrt{2}})
.
Then by (
7.7
),

\displaystyle h(t,x)

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}[(U_{i}(t,x)U_{i}(t_{0}^{\prime},x_{0}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}e^{-i(x-x_{0}^{\prime})\xi}\sin((t-s)|\xi|)\sin((t_{0}^{\prime}-s)|\xi|).

By considering
t<t_{0}^{\prime}
and
t\geq t_{0}^{\prime}
respectively, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(t,x)=C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{2-\beta}}e^{-i(x-x_{0}^{\prime})\xi}\cos((t-s)|\xi|)\sin((t_{0}^{\prime}-s)|\xi|).

Since
|\cos(z)|\leq 1
and
|\sin(z)|\leq\min\{|z|,1\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{2-\beta}}\min\{(t_{0}^{\prime}-s)|\xi|,1\}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\bigg(\int_{|\xi|\leq(t_{0}^{\prime}-s)^{-1}}(t^{\prime}_{0}-s)\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{1-\beta}}+\int_{|\xi|>(t_{0}^{\prime}-s)^{-1}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{2-\beta}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}(t_{0}^{\prime}-s)^{1-\beta}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime})(t_{0}^{\prime})^{1-\beta},

which is bounded over all possible
(\eta,\theta)\in I^{(\rho)}
and

(\eta_{0},\theta_{0})\in I
by the compactness of
I
.
For the derivative in
x
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}h(t,x)=C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{3-\beta}}i\xi e^{-i(x-x_{0}^{\prime})\xi}\sin((t-s)|\xi|)\sin((t_{0}^{\prime}-s)|\xi|).

Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}h(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t\wedge t_{0}^{\prime}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\xi}{|\xi|^{2-\beta}}\min\{(t_{0}^{\prime}-s)|\xi|,1\},

which is also bounded as shown by the calculations above.
Therefore, by the mean value theorem, there is a finite constant
C
such that for all

(\eta_{1},\theta_{1}),(\eta_{2},\theta_{2})\in I^{(\rho)}
,

|h(t_{1},x_{1})-h(t_{2},x_{2})|\leq C(|t_{1}-t_{2}|+|x_{1}-x_{2}|),

which implies (
7.17
). This completes the proof of Lemma
7.6
.
∎

1.1
.

,

Theorem 7.7
.

Suppose that
T
is a compact rectangle in
\{(\eta,\theta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\eta+\theta>0\}
.
Let
\phi(r)=r^{4/(2-\beta)}(\log\log(1/r))^{2}
and

\varphi(r)=r^{2-d(2-\beta)/2}(\log\log(1/r))^{d(2-\beta)/2}
.

(a)

If
d>4/(2-\beta)
, then
there exist positive finite constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{C_{1}\lambda(J)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\phi}(v(J))\leq C_{2}\lambda(J)\text{ for all }J\in\mathscr{I}(T)\Big\}=1.

In particular,
\dim v(T)=4/(2-\beta)
a.s.

(b)

If
d<4/(2-\beta)
, then there exists a positive finite constant
C
such that
for any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big\{CL(z,J)\leq\mathscr{H}_{\varphi}(v^{-1}(z)\cap J)<\infty\text{ for all }J\in\mathscr{I}(T)\Big\}=1.

In particular,
\dim[v^{-1}(z)\cap T]=2-d(2-\beta)/2
a.s. on the event
\{L(z,T)>0\}
.

If
d\geq 4/(2-\beta)
, then
v^{-1}(z)\cap T=\varnothing
a.s.

Proof.

Note that it suffices to prove the theorem for all sufficiently small rectangles in
T

because we can cover
T
with finitely many small rectangles.
Then, without loss of generality, we can assume that the sides of
T
have length

\leq\delta_{0}/\sqrt{2}
, so that
v
satisfies (
7.11
) on
T
.
Together with (
7.10
), we see that
v
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Moreover, Lemmas
7.4
and
7.6
show that

v
satisfies Assumptions
1.2
and
1.3
respectively.
Hence, the results follow from Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.
∎

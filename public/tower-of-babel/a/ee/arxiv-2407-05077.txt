Abstract
. This paper gives exact formulas for the regularity of powers of edge ideals of an edge-weighted integrally closed cycle.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
.
We write
xy
for an edge of
G
with
x
and
y
as endpoints.
Suppose
{\bf w}:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is an edge weight function on
G
. We write
G_{\bf w}
for the pair
(G,{\bf w})
and call it an
edge-weighted
graph (or simply weighted graph) with the underlying graph
G
. For a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
with the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, its
edge-weighted ideal
(or simply edge ideal), was introduced in
[
23
]
, is the ideal of the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
in
n
variables over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
given by

I(G_{\bf w})=(x^{{\bf w}(xy)}y^{{\bf w}(xy)}\mid xy\in E(G_{\bf w})).

If
{\bf w}
is the constant function defined by
{\bf w}(e)=1
for all
e\in E(G)
, then
I(G_{\bf w})
is the classical edge ideal of the underlying graph
G
of
G_{\bf w}
, which
has been studied extensively in the literature
[
1
,
2
,
12
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
26
,
27
]
.

Recently, there has been a surge of interest in characterizing weights for which the edge ideal of a weighted graph is Cohen-Macaulay. For example,
Paulsen and Sather-Wagstaff in
[
23
]
classified Cohen-Macaulay weighted graphs
G_{\bf w}
where the underlying
graph
G
is a cycle, a tree, or a complete graph. Seyed Fakhari et al. in
[
10
]
continued this study, classifying a
Cohen-Macaulay weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
when
G
is a very well-covered graph. Recently, Diem et al. in
[
8
]
gave a complete characterization of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay edge-weighted graphs. In
[
25
]
, Wei classified all Cohen-Macaulay weighted chordal graphs from a purely graph-theoretic point of view. Hien in
[
15
]

classified Cohen-Macaulay edge-weighted graphs
G_{\bf w}
when
G
has girth at least
5
.

The study of edge ideals of weighted graphs is much more recent and consequently there are fewer results in this direction.
In this paper, we are interested in the regularity of powers of the edge ideal of a weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{n}
. Recall that the regularity is a central invariant associated to a homogeneous ideal
I
. It is well known that
\operatorname{reg}(I^{t})
is asymptotically a linear function for
t\gg 0
, i.e., there exist constants
a
,
b
and a positive integer
t_{0}
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
,
\mbox{reg}\,(I^{t})=at+b
(see
[
7
,
19
]
). In this context, it has been of interest to find the exact form of this linear function and to determine the stabilization index
t_{0}
at which
\operatorname{reg}(I^{t})
becomes linear (see
[
1
,
2
]
). It turns out that even in the case of monomial ideals it is difficult to find the linear function and
t_{0}
(see
[
5
]
).

Integral closure and normality of monomial ideals play an important role in the multiplicity theory and it is also an interesting topic.
In
[
9
]
, Duan and the first three authors of this article gave a complete characterization of an integrally closed edge-weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
and showed that if its underlying
graph
G
is a star graph, a path, or a cycle, then
G_{\bf w}
is normal. The regularity of powers of edge ideals has been computed for some weighted graphs, such as star graphs, integrally closed paths, and integrally closed trees(see
[
28
,
18
]
).
In this article, we will give exact formulas for the regularity of powers of edge ideals of a weighted integrally closed cycle. Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
C_{{\bf w}}^{n}
be an integrally closed
n
-cycle, then

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(C_{{\bf w}}^{n})^{t})=2\omega t+\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor-2

where
\omega=\max\{{\bf w}_{1},\ldots,{\bf w}_{n}\}
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some of essential definitions and terminology needed later.
In Section
3
, by choosing different exact sequences and repeatedly using Lemma
2.2
, we give some exact formulas for the regularity of the edge ideal of a non-trivial integrally closed
n
-cycle.
In Section
4
, when
C_{\bf w}^{n}
is an
n
-cycle with the weight of only one edge is non-trivial, we provide a special order on the set of minimal monomial generators of the powers of its edge ideal.
Using this order and some exact sequences, we give formulas for the regularity of the powers of these edge ideals.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide the definitions and basic facts that will be used throughout this paper.
For detailed information, we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
16
]
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. For any subset
A
of
V(G)
,
G[A]
denotes the
induced subgraph
of
G
on the set
A
, i.e.,
for any
u,v\in A
,
uv\in E(G[A])
if and only if
uv\in E(G)
.
For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, its
neighborhood
is defined as
N_{G}(v)\!:=\{u\in V(G)\mid uv\in E(G)\}
.
A connected graph
G
is called a
cycle
if
\deg_{G}(v)=2
for all
v\in V(G)
. A cycle with
n
vertices is denoted by
C_{n}
. A connected graph with vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, is called a
path
, if
E(G)=\{x_{i}x_{i+1}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
. Such a path is usually denoted by
P_{n}
.

Given a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
, we denote its vertex and edge sets by
V(G_{\bf w})
and
E(G_{\bf w})
, respectively. Any concept valid for graphs automatically applies to weighted graphs.
For example, the
neighborhood
of a vertex
v
in a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
with the underlying graph
G
is defined as
N_{G}(v)\!:=\{u\in V(G)\mid uv\in E(G)\}
. Given a subset
W
of
V(G_{\bf w})
, its
neighborhood
is defined as
N_{G}(W)\!:=\bigcup\limits_{v\in W}N_{G}(v)
.

The
induced subgraph
on the set
A
in
G_{\bf w}
is the graph
G_{\bf w}[A]
with vertex set
A
, and for any
u,v\in A
,

uv\in E(G_{\bf w}[A])
if and only if
uv\in E(G_{\bf w})
, and the weight function
{\bf w}^{\prime}
satisfies
{\bf w}^{\prime}(uv)={\bf w}(uv)
.
A weighted graph is said to be
non-trivial
if there is at least one edge with a weight greater than
1
, otherwise, it is said to be

trivial
. An edge
e\in E(G_{\bf w})
is said to be
non-trivial
if its weight
{\bf w}(e)\geq 2
. Otherwise, it is said to be
trivial
.

For any homogeneous ideal
I
of the polynomial ring
S=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
there exists a finite minimal graded free resolution

0\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(I)}\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}(I)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}(I)}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

where
p\leq n
, and
S(-j)
is the
S
-module obtained by shifting
the degrees of
S
by
j
. The number

\beta_{i,j}(I)
, the
(i,j)
-th graded Betti number of
I
, is
an invariant of
I
that equals the number of minimal generators of degree
j
in the

i
th syzygy module of
I
. The regularity of
I
, denoted by
\mbox{reg}\,(I)
, is defined as

\mbox{reg}\,(I):=\mbox{max}\,\{j-i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\},

which measures the size of the minimal graded
free resolution of
I
.
By looking at the minimal free resolution, it is easy to see that
\operatorname{reg}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(S/I)+1
.

The following lemmas are often used for computing regularity of a module.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
)
Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring and
I\subset S
be a graded ideal with
\dim(R/I)\leq 1
. Then
\operatorname{reg}(IM)\leq\operatorname{reg}(I)+\operatorname{reg}(M)
for any finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
17
, Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1]
)

Let
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact
sequence of finitely generated graded S-modules. Then we have

(1)

\operatorname{reg}(N)\leq max\{\operatorname{reg}(M),\operatorname{reg}(P)\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{reg}(P)\neq\operatorname{reg}(M)-1
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(P)\leq max\{\operatorname{reg}(M)-1,\operatorname{reg}(N)\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{reg}(M)\neq\operatorname{reg}(N)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(I)
denote the unique minimal set of monomial generators of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
and let
u\in S

be a monomial. We set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}\!:x_{i}|u\}
. If
G(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
, we set
\operatorname{supp}(I)=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{supp}(u_{i})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
17
, Lemma 2.2, Lemma 3.2]
)

Let
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{m+1},\dots,x_{n}]
be two polynomial rings over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
S=S_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}S_{2}
and
I\subset S_{1}
,

J\subset S_{2}
be two nonzero homogeneous ideals. Then

(1)

\operatorname{reg}(S/(I+J))=\operatorname{reg}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{reg}(S_{2}/J)
,

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/JI)=\operatorname{reg}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{reg}(S_{2}/J)+1
.

In particular, if
u
is a monomial of degree
d
such that
\mbox{supp}\,(u)\cap\mbox{supp}\,(I)=\emptyset
and
J=(u)
, then
\mbox{reg}\,(J)=d
and
\mbox{reg}\,(JI)=\mbox{reg}\,(I)+d
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
)

Let
I\subseteq S
be a monomial ideal. Then, for any variable
x
,
\operatorname{reg}(I,x)\leq\operatorname{reg}(I)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
28
, Lemma 4.6]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a trivial path with
n
vertices, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P^{n}_{\bf w}))=\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor
and

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P^{n}_{\bf w})^{t})=2(t-1)+\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P^{n}_{\bf w}))
for all
t\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
)
Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
be a regular sequence of homogeneous polynomials in
S
with
\deg\,(u_{1})=\cdots=\deg\,(u_{1})=d
. Let
I=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
. Then we have
\operatorname{reg}\,(I^{t})=dt+(d-1)(m-1)
for all
t\geq 1
.

3.
The regularity of the edge ideal

Calculating or even estimating the regularity of edge ideals of weighted cycles with arbitrary weight functions is a difficult problem. In this section, we will give precise formulas for the regularity of the edge ideal of a non-trivial integrally closed
n
-cycle.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
13
, Definition 1.1]
)
Let
I
be a monomial ideal. If there exist monomial ideals
J
and
K
such that
\mathcal{G}(I)=\mathcal{G}(J)\cup\mathcal{G}(K)
and
\mathcal{G}(J)\cap\mathcal{G}(K)=\emptyset
. Then
I=J+K
is a Betti splitting if

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i,j}(K)+\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)\text{ for all }i,j\geq 0,

where
\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)=0
for
i=0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
13
, Corollary 2.7]
)
Suppose
I=J+K
, where
\mathcal{G}(J)
contains all the generators of
I
that are divisible by some variable
x_{i}
, and
\mathcal{G}(K)
is a nonempty set containing the remaining generators of
I
. If
J
has a linear resolution, then
I=J+K
is a Betti splitting.

Definition
3.1
states that
\operatorname{reg}(I)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(J),\operatorname{reg}(K),\operatorname{reg}(J\cap K)-1\}
, as a result of the Betti splitting.

Definition 3.3
.

(
[
11
, Definition 2.1]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
, where
u_{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{ij}}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
The polarization of
I
, denoted by
I^{\mathcal{P}}
, is a squarefree monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
S^{\mathcal{P}}

I^{\mathcal{P}}=(\mathcal{P}(u_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{P}(u_{m}))

where
\mathcal{P}(u_{i})=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{a_{ij}}x_{jk}
is a squarefree monomial in
S^{\mathcal{P}}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{j1},\ldots,x_{ja_{j}}\mid j=1,\ldots,n]
and
a_{j}=\max\{a_{ij}|i=1,\ldots,m\}
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

A monomial ideal and its polarization share many homological and
algebraic properties. The following is a useful property of the polarization.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
16
, Corollary 1.6.3]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
I^{\mathcal{P}}\subset S^{\mathcal{P}}
be its polarization.
Then

\beta_{ij}(I)=\beta_{ij}(I^{\mathcal{P}})

for all
i
and
j
. In particular,
\operatorname{reg}(I)=\operatorname{reg}(I^{\mathcal{P}})
.

Definition 3.5
.

(
[
16
, Definition 1.4.1]
)
Let
I
be an ideal in a ring
R
.
An element
f\in R
is said to be integral over
I
if there exists an equation

f^{k}+c_{1}f^{k-1}+\dots+c_{k-1}f+c_{k}=0\text{\ \ with\ \ }c_{i}\in I^{i}.

The set
\overline{I}
of elements in
R
that are integral over
I
is the
integral closure
of
I
.
If
I=\overline{I}
, then
I
is said to be integrally closed.
An edge-weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
is said to be integrally closed if its edge ideal
I(G_{\bf w})
is integrally closed.

According to
[
16
, Theorem 1.4.6]
, every trivial graph
G_{\bf w}
is integrally closed. The following lemma gives a complete characterization of a non-trivial graph that is integrally closed.

Lemma 3.6
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.6]
)
If
G_{\bf w}
is a non-trivial graph, then
I(G_{\bf w})
is integrally closed if and only if
G_{\bf w}
does not contain any of the following three graphs as induced subgraphs.

(1)

A path
P_{\bf w}^{3}
of length
2
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

(2)

The disjoint union
P_{\bf w}^{2}\sqcup P_{\bf w}^{2}
of two paths
P_{\bf w}^{2}
of length
1
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

(3)

A
3
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{3}
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

From the above lemma, we can derive

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivial integrally closed cycle with
n
vertices, then if
n=6
, it can have at most three edges with non-trivial weights; otherwise, it can have up to two edges with non-trivial weights.

The following lemmas provide some exact formulas for the regularity of the powers of the edge ideal of a non-trivial integrally closed path.

Lemma 3.8
.

(
[
28
, Theorems 4.8 and 4.9]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivial integrally closed path with
V(P_{\bf w}^{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Let
\omega_{i}=\max\{{\bf w}_{t}\mid{\bf w}_{t}={\bf w}(e_{t})\text{\ and }e_{t}=x_{t}x_{t+1}\text{\ for}\\
\text{each }1\leq t\leq n-1\}
, with
\omega_{i}\geq 2
and
\omega_{i}\geq{\bf w}_{i+2}
if
e_{i+2}\in E({P_{\bf w}^{n}})
. Then,

(1)

if
n\leq 4
, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))=2\omega_{i}-1
;

(2)

if
n\geq 5
, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))=\max\{2\omega_{i}+\lfloor\frac{i-1}{3}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{n-(i+1)}{3}\rfloor,2{\bf w}_{i+2}+\lfloor\frac{i-2}{3}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{n-i}{3}\rfloor\}-1
.

Lemma 3.9
.

(
[
28
, Theorem 4.8]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivial integrally closed path with
V(P_{\bf w}^{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Let
\omega_{i}=\max\{{\bf w}_{s}\mid{\bf w}_{s}={\bf w}(e_{s})\text{\ and }e_{s}=x_{s}x_{s+1}\text{\ for each }1\leq s\leq n-1\}
, with
\omega_{i}\geq 2
and
\omega_{i}\geq{\bf w}_{i+2}
if
e_{i+2}\in E({P_{\bf w}^{n}})
. Then

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=2(t-1)\omega_{i}+\operatorname{reg}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))
for all
t\geq 1
.

The following lemma provides some exact formulas for the regularity of the powers of the edge ideal of a trivial integrally closed cycle.

Lemma 3.10
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
)

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a trivial integrally closed cycle with
n
vertices, then

(1)

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n}))=\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(C^{n}_{\bf w})^{t})=2(t-1)+\operatorname{reg}(S/I(C^{n}_{\bf w}))
for all
t\geq 1
.

Thus, we will now consider a non-trivial integrally closed cycle that satisfies the following conditions.

Remark 3.11
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivial integrally closed
n
-cycle with
V(C_{\bf w}^{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
E(C_{\bf w}^{n})=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, where
e_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}
with
x_{n+1}=x_{1}
and
{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}(e_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
.

We first compute the regularity of the edge ideal of a cycle
C_{\bf w}^{n}
for
3\leq n\leq 6
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be an
n
-cycle as in Remark
3.11
, where
3\leq n\leq 6
. Let
\omega=\max\{{\bf w}_{1},\ldots,{\bf w}_{n}\}
. Then

\operatorname{reg}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n}))=2\omega+\lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor-2.

Proof.

Let
I=I(C_{\bf w}^{n})
and
\omega={\bf w}_{1}
by symmetry. If
n=3
, then
{\bf w}_{2}=1
or
{\bf w}_{3}=1
by Lemma
3.6
. We can assume
\bf w_{3}=1
due to symmetry. So
I:x_{3}=(x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{2}}x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{2}-1},x_{1})
and
(I,x_{3})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3})
. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\operatorname{reg}(S/(I:x_{3}))=2{\bf w}_{2}-2
and
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I,x_{3}))=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
. Applying Lemma
2.2
to the following short exact sequence with
i=3

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}0&\rightarrow&\frac{S}{I:x_{i}}(-1)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{I}&\rightarrow&\frac{S}{(I,x_{i})}&\rightarrow&0,\end{matrix}

we can observe that
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
.

If
n=4
, then
{\bf w}_{2}={\bf w}_{4}=1
by Lemma
3.6
. Thus
(I:x_{4})=(x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{3}}x_{4}^{{\bf w}_{3}-1},x_{1})
and
(I,x_{4})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{2}x_{3},x_{4})
. Let

(I:x_{4})^{\mathcal{P}}
and
(I,x_{4})^{\mathcal{P}}
be the polarizations of ideals
I:x_{4}
and
(I,x_{4})
, respectively. By Lemmas
2.3
,
3.2
and
3.4
,

\operatorname{reg}(S/(I:x_{4}))=\operatorname{reg}(S^{\mathcal{P}}/(I:x_{4})^{\mathcal{P}})=2{\bf w}_{3}-2
and
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I,x_{4}))=\operatorname{reg}(S^{\mathcal{P}}/(I,x_{4})^{\mathcal{P}})=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
.
Using Lemma
2.2
and the short exact sequence (
1
) with
i=4
,
we can see that
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
.

If
n=5
, then
{\bf w}_{2}={\bf w}_{4}={\bf w}_{5}=1
by symmetry and Lemma
3.6
. Thus
(I:x_{5})=(x_{2}x_{3},x_{1},x_{4})
and
(I,x_{5})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{3}}x_{4}^{{\bf w}_{3}},x_{5})
. It follows from Lemmas
2.3
and
3.8
that
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I:x_{5}))=1
and
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I,x_{5}))=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
. This implies that
\operatorname{reg}(S/I)=2{\bf w}_{1}-1
due to
Lemma
2.2
and the short exact sequence (
1
) with
i=5
.

If
n=6
, then
{\bf w}_{1}\geq{\bf w}_{3}\geq{\bf w}_{5}
and
{\bf w}_{2}={\bf w}_{4}={\bf w}_{6}=1
by symmetry and Lemma
3.6
. Thus
(I,x_{6})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{3}}x_{4}^{{\bf w}_{3}},x_{4}x_{5},x_{6})
and
(I:x_{6})=(x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{3}}x_{4}^{{\bf w}_{3}},

x_{4}x_{5},x_{5}^{{\bf w}_{5}}x_{6}^{{\bf w}_{5}-1},x_{1})
. By Lemma
3.8
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I,x_{6}))=2{\bf w}_{1}
. To compute
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I:x_{6}))
, we distinguish
between the following two cases:

(i) If
{\bf w}_{5}=1
, then
(I:x_{6})=(x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{3}}x_{4}^{{\bf w}_{3}},x_{5},x_{1})
. It follows from Lemmas
2.3
and
3.8
that
\operatorname{reg}(S/(I:x_{6}))=2{\bf w}_{3}-1
.

(ii) If
{\bf w}_{5}\geq 2

Submanifolds in Koszul-Vinberg geometryAugust 9, 2026
Abdelhak Abouqateb, Mohamed Boucetta, Charif Bourzik

Abstract
A Koszul-Vinberg manifold is a manifold M M endowed with a pair ( ∇ , h ) (\nabla,h) where ∇ \nabla is a flat connection and h h is a symmetric bivector field satisfying a generalized Codazzi equation. The geometry of such manifolds could be seen as a type of bridge between Poisson geometry and pseudo-Riemannian geometry, as has been highlighted in our previous article [ Contravariant Pseudo-Hessian manifolds and their associated Poisson structures . Differential Geometry and its Applications (2020)]. Our objective here will be to pursue our study by focusing in this setting on submanifolds by taking into account some developments in the theory of Poisson submanifolds.

Introduction

An
n
-dimensional manifold
M
is called an affine manifold when it is equipped with a flat connection
\nabla
on the tangent bundle
TM\rightarrow M
or, equivalently, the manifold
M
is equipped with a maximal atlas such that all transition functions are restrictions of affine transformations of
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
. For such manifolds, there exists around every point an affine coordinates system, i.e. a coordinates system
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
satisfying
\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
(see
[
10
]
). Now let
(M,\nabla)
be an affine manifold of dimension
n
,
h
be a symmetric bivector field on
M
and
h_{\#}:T^{*}M\rightarrow TM
the associated contraction given by
\beta(\alpha^{\#})=h(\alpha,\beta)
where
\alpha^{\#}:=h_{\#}(\alpha)
. We recall (
[
1
]
) that
h
is said to be a
pseudo-Hessian bivector field
on
(M,\nabla)
if it satisfies the
contravariant Codazzi equation

(\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}h)(\beta,\gamma)=(\nabla_{\beta^{\#}}h)(\alpha,\gamma),\quad

(1.1)

for any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. The triple
(M,\nabla,h)
is then called a
contravariant pseudo-Hessian manifold
. This means that around each affine coordinates system
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, we have
(dx_{i})^{\#}(h_{jk})=(dx_{j})^{\#}(h_{ik})
for any
i,j,k=1,\ldots,n
; which could be written as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(h_{il}\dfrac{\partial h_{jk}}{\partial x_{l}}-h_{jl}\dfrac{\partial h_{ik}}{\partial x_{l}}\right)=0,

(1.2)

where
h_{ij}=h(dx_{i},dx_{j})
.

Motivated by the theory of left-symmetric algebroid the authors in
[
12
]
introduce the notion of Koszul-Vinberg structure on a left-symmetric algebroid. Lets clarify briefly this fact: Let
(A,\bullet,\rho)
be a left symmetric algebroid and
h
a symmetric bivetor field on
A
then
h
is called a Koszul-Vinberg structure on
A
if and only if
[h,h]=0
where:

\displaystyle[h,h](\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha^{\#}).h(\beta,\gamma)-\rho(\beta^{\#}).h(\alpha,\gamma)+\prec\alpha,\beta^{\#}\bullet\gamma^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle-\prec\beta,\alpha^{\#}\bullet\gamma^{\#}\succ-\prec\gamma,[\alpha^{\#},\beta^{\#}]_{A}\succ.

The notion contravariant pseudo-Hessian manifolds could be seen as a Koszul-Vinberg structure on the left symmetric algebroid
A=(TM,\nabla,id)
. For this reason all notions introduced under the name contravariant pseudo-Hessian in
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
will be called Koszul-Vinberg.

Keeping in mind the analogies given in
[
1
]
between Koszul-Vinberg manifolds and Poisson manifolds it is natural to ask if we can define an analogue of Hamiltonian vector fields, this leads to a new questions and some important geometric and algebraic proprieties. It is also important to study the maps which preserves this structure which are the analogue of Poisson maps, in particular we show that there is a correspondence between such maps and Poisson maps. Taking into account the study devoted to submanifolds in Poisson geometry see for example the survey paper
[
15
]
we introduce the notion of Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds which are the analogue of Poisson submanifolds, and we show that a Koszul-Vinberg manifold in some sense is the union of the affine leaves which meet such submanifolds. Also we introduce a notion of transversal submanifolds which are the analogue of tranversal submanifolds in Poisson geometry known also under the name cosymplectic submanifolds, and we show that they inherit naturally a Koszul-Vinberg structure. Also we introduce a notion coisotropic Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds which are the analogue of coisotropic submanifolds in Poisson geometry (see
[
13
]
). It is well known that Poisson maps can be characterized using coisotropic submanifolds in Poisson geometry (see
[
13
]
), so we show that the same thing happens in Koszul-Vinberg geometry. Also we know that the conormal bundle of coisotropic submanifolds inherited naturally a Lie algebroid structure, in this direction we show that the conormal bundle of a coisotropic Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds is a left-symmetric algebroid.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
1
we give some preliminaries and basic properties developed in
[
1
]
about Koszul-Vinberg manifolds.
In Section
2
we study Koszul-Vinberg Hamiltonian vector fields and we explore their related properties. Section
3
is devoted to the study of Koszul-Vinberg maps and their relationships with Poisson maps. The study of Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds, as a similarity with Poisson submanifolds, is the main purpose of Section
4
. After we move to the description of Koszul-Vinberg traversals in section
5
. The last section deals with the notion of coisotropic Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds.

2
Preliminaries

Let us recall from
[
1
]
that Koszul-Vinberg manifolds define a subclass of Lie algebroids in the following way: Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be an affine manifold endowed with a symmetric bivector field. We associate to this triple a bracket on
\Omega^{1}(M)
by putting

{[\alpha,\beta]_{h}}:=\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}\beta-\nabla_{\beta^{\#}}\alpha.

(2.1)

and a contravariant connection
\mathcal{D}:\Omega^{1}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)
given by

\displaystyle\prec\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta,X\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}h)(\alpha,\beta)+\prec\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}\beta,X\succ

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X.h(\alpha,\beta)+\alpha^{\#}.\prec\beta,X\succ+\prec\alpha,\nabla_{X}\beta^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle+\prec\beta,\left[X,\alpha^{\#}\right]\succ,

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, satisfying the relations

[\alpha,\beta]_{h}=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta-\mathcal{D}_{\beta}\alpha\text{ and }[\alpha,f\beta]_{h}=f[\alpha,\beta]_{h}+\alpha^{\#}(f)\beta,

(2.3)

where
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Theorem
2.4
in
[
1
]
shows that
(M,\nabla,h)
is a Koszul-Vinberg manifold if and only if
(T^{*}M,h_{\#},[\;,\;]_{h})
is a Lie algebroid. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}
is a connection for the Lie algebroid structure
(T^{*}M,h_{\#},[\;,\;]_{h})
satisfying

(\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta)^{\#}=\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}\beta^{\#}.

(2.4)

Also any leaf
L
of the singular foliation on
M
induced by the Lie algberoid structure support a pseudo-Hessian structure
(g_{L},\nabla^{L})
where
g_{L}(\alpha^{\#},\beta^{\#})=h(\alpha,\beta)
. This foliation will be called affine foliation.

There is a more important geometric structures on the tangent bundle of
TM
. Indeed, associated to
\nabla
there exists a splitting

TTM=V(M)\oplus H(M)

such that for any
u\in TM
,
T_{u}p:H_{u}(M)\rightarrow T_{p(u)}M
is an isomorphism. For any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
we denote by
X^{v}\in\Gamma(V(M))
its vertical lift and by
X^{h}\in\Gamma(V(M))
its horizontal lift. There are given, for any
u\in TM
, by

X^{v}_{u}=\frac{d}{dt}_{|_{t=0}}(u+tX_{p(u)}),\text{ and }T_{u}p(X^{h}_{u})=X_{p(u)}.

(2.5)

The vector
X^{h}_{u}
can also be defined using parallel transport in the following way: Let
\gamma
be a smooth curve on
M
starting at
x
and its derivative at
0
is the vector
X_{x}

X^{h}_{u}=\frac{d}{dt}_{\lvert_{t=0}}\tau^{\gamma}_{0t}(u)

(2.6)

where
\tau^{\gamma}_{0t}:T_{x}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}T_{\gamma(t)}M
is the parallel transport map along
\gamma
.
The
Sasaki almost complex structure

J:TTM\rightarrow TTM
determined by
\nabla
is defined by

J(X^{h})=X^{v}\text{ and }J(X^{v})=-X^{h}.

(2.7)

It is integrable to a complex structure on
TM
if and only if
\nabla
is flat.

The vanishing of the curvature of
\nabla
implies that the Lie bracket on
\Gamma(T(TM))
is determined by:

[X^{h},Y^{h}]=[X,Y]^{h},[X^{h},Y^{v}]=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{v}\text{ and }[X^{v},Y^{v}]=0.

(2.8)

As for the vector fields, for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, we define
\alpha^{v},\alpha^{h}\in\Omega^{1}(TM)
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\prec\alpha^{v},X^{v}\succ=\prec\alpha,X\succ\circ p\\
\prec\alpha^{v},X^{h}\succ=0\end{array}\right.\text{ and }\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\prec\alpha^{h},X^{h}\succ=\prec\alpha,X\succ\circ p\\
\prec\alpha^{h},X^{v}\succ=0,\end{array}\right.

one can see that
\alpha^{h}=p^{*}\alpha
.

The
Sasaki connection

\overline{\nabla}
on
TM
determined by
\nabla
is defined by

\overline{\nabla}_{X^{h}}Y^{h}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{h},\overline{\nabla}_{X^{h}}Y^{v}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{v}\text{ and }\overline{\nabla}_{X^{v}}Y^{h}=\overline{\nabla}_{X^{v}}Y^{v}=0,

(2.9)

where
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. This connection is torsionless and flat and hence defines an affine structure on
TM
. Moreover, the endomorphism vector field
J:TTM\rightarrow TTM
is parallel with respect to
\overline{\nabla}
.

To any pair
(\nabla,h)
we can associate a skew-symmetric bivector field
\Pi
on
TM
by putting

\Pi(\alpha^{v},\beta^{v})=\Pi(\alpha^{h},\beta^{h})=0\text{ and }\Pi(\alpha^{h},\beta^{v})=-\Pi(\beta^{v},\beta^{h})=h(\alpha,\beta)\circ p,

(2.10)

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. So we get that

\Pi_{\#}(\alpha^{v})=-(\alpha^{\#})^{h}\text{ and }\Pi_{\#}(\alpha^{h})=(\alpha^{\#})^{v}.

(2.11)

Theorem
3.3
in
[
1
]
assert that
(M,\nabla,h)
is a Koszul-Vinberg manifold if and only if
(TM,\Pi)
is a Poisson manifold.

3
Koszul-Vinberg Hamiltonian vector fields

In Poisson geometry, Hamiltonian vector fields play an important role (see
[
11
]
) and it is therefore natural to discuss some properties of their analogue in Koszul-Vinberg geometry. Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold. For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
we can associate the vector field
X_{f}:=(df)^{\#}
, which will be called the
K-V Hamiltonian vector field
associated to
f
. The symmetry of the bivector field
h
leads to the following:

X_{f_{1}}(f_{2})=X_{f_{2}}(f_{1})=\prec df_{2},X_{f_{1}}\succ=\prec df_{1},X_{f_{2}}\succ,\;\;\quad\forall f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M).

Contrary to what happens in Poisson geometry the flow of the vector field
X_{f}
does not generally preserve the K-V bivector field
h
, this can be seen through the following example.

Example 3.1

Consider the K-V manifold
M=(\mathbb{R}^{2},\nabla,h)
where
\nabla
is the canonical affine connection and
h=x\partial_{x}\otimes\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}\otimes\partial_{y}
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},(x,y)\mapsto x
, by a direct computation we get that
L_{X_{f}}(h)(dx,dx)=-f
.

More generally, we have:

Proposition 3.2

For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and any
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
we have

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}(h)(\alpha,\beta)=-\nabla_{X_{f}}(h)(\alpha,\beta)+2\prec\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}df,\beta^{\#}\succ.

Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an affine local coordinates system on
M
. Denote by
X_{i}=(dx_{i})^{\#}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}(h)(dx_{i},dx_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{f}.h_{ij}-h\left(\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}dx_{i},dx_{j}\right)-h\left(dx_{i},\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}dx_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X_{f}.h_{ij}+\prec dx_{i},[X_{f},X_{j}]\succ+\prec dx_{j},[X_{f},X_{i}]\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X_{f}.h_{ij}+\prec dx_{i},[df,dx_{j}]^{\#}\succ+\prec dx_{j},[df,dx_{i}]^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X_{f}.h_{ij}+\prec[df,dx_{j}],X_{i}\succ+\prec[df,dx_{i}],X_{j}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X_{f}.h_{ij}+\prec\nabla_{X_{j}}df,X_{i}\succ-\prec\nabla_{X_{f}}dx_{j},X_{i}\succ

\displaystyle+\prec\nabla_{X_{i}}df,X_{j}\succ-\prec\nabla_{X_{f}}dx_{i},X_{j}\succ.

Now since
\nabla dx_{i}=0
and
\prec\nabla_{X}df,Y\succ=\prec\nabla_{Y}df,X\succ
, we get

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}(h)(dx_{i},dx_{j})=-\nabla_{X_{f}}(h)(dx_{i},dx_{j})+2\prec\nabla_{X_{i}}df,X_{j}\succ.

We recall that a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
is affine if
f
is an affine function in local affine coordinates. In what follows, we introduce the following space.

\mathcal{E}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\ /\ \prec\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}df,\beta^{\#}\succ=0,\quad\forall\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)\};

which is the space of smooth functions
f:M\to\hbox{\bb R}
that are affine along the leaves of the affine foliation (i.e. the restriction of
f
to any leaf
L\subset M
is affine).

Example 3.3

Let
(\mathcal{A},.)
be a
n
-dimensional commutative associative algebra. We have seen in
[
1
]
Proposition
4.2
that the dual
\mathcal{A}^{*}
carries a linear K-V structure
(\nabla,h)
where
\nabla
is the canonical affine connection on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
h
is the linear symmetric bivector field on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
given by

h(u,v)(\alpha)=\prec\alpha,u(\alpha).v(\alpha)\succ

where
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
u,v\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{*})=C^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{A})
. Now we take
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the commutative associative product given by
e_{1}.e_{1}=e_{1}
and the others products are zero and denote by
(x,y)
the canonical dual coordinates of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, so we have

(dx)^{\#}=x\partial_{x}\text{ and }(dy)^{\#}=0.

From Proposition
3.2
it follows that for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
which depend only on the
y
-variable

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}(h)=0.

We know that for any K-V manifold
(M,\nabla,h)
the tangent bundle
(TM,\Pi)
is a Poisson manifold. So we can ask the following question: When the vertical lift
X_{f}^{v}
and the horizontal lift
X_{f}^{h}
are Poisson vector fields on
(TM,\Pi)
?

Proposition 3.4

For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

1.

X_{f}^{v}
is the Hamiltonian vector field on
(TM,\Pi)
associated to the function
f\circ p\in C^{\infty}(TM)
.

2.

X_{f}^{h}
is a Poisson vector field on
(TM,\Pi)
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{E}
.

From (
2.10
) we get
X_{f}^{v}=\Pi_{\#}((df)^{h})=\Pi_{\#}(d(f\circ p))
which leads to
1
. For the second assertion we need to compute
\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}(\Pi)
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, by using the formulas proved in
[
1
]
Proposition
3.6
, we get that

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}(\Pi)(\alpha^{v},\beta^{v})=\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}(\Pi)(\alpha^{h},\beta^{h})=0.

Furthermore we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}(\Pi)(\alpha^{v},\beta^{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{f}^{h}.\Pi(\alpha^{v},\beta^{h})-\Pi(\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}\alpha^{v},\beta^{h})-\Pi(\alpha^{v},\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}\beta^{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X_{f}^{h}.(h(\alpha,\beta)\circ p)-\Pi((\nabla_{X_{f}}\alpha)^{v},\beta^{h})-\Pi(\alpha^{v},(\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\beta)^{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(X_{f}.h(\alpha,\beta))\circ p+h(\nabla_{X_{f}}\alpha,\beta)\circ p+h(\alpha,\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\beta)\circ p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-X_{f}.h(\alpha,\beta)+\prec\nabla_{X_{f}}\alpha,\beta^{\#}\succ+\prec\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\beta,\alpha^{\#}\succ\right)\circ p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prec\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}}\beta,\alpha^{\#}\succ-\prec\alpha,\nabla_{X_{f}}\beta^{\#}\succ\right)\circ p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(X_{f}.h(\alpha,\beta)-\prec\beta,[X_{f},\alpha^{\#}]\succ-\prec\alpha,\nabla_{X_{f}}\beta^{\#}\succ\right)\circ p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prec\nabla_{X_{f}}\alpha,\beta^{\#}\succ-\prec\beta,[X_{f},\alpha^{\#}]\succ\right)\circ p.

Now since
[\alpha^{\#},\beta^{\#}]=[\alpha,\beta]_{h}^{\#}
and
\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}\beta-\nabla_{\beta^{\#}}\alpha=[\alpha,\beta]_{h}
, we get

\mathcal{L}_{X_{f}^{h}}(\Pi)(\alpha^{v},\beta^{h})=\prec\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}df,\beta^{\#}\succ\circ p.

Now we consider the two vector spaces:

\mathcal{V}_{h}:=\{X_{f}\in\Gamma(TM)|\text{ }f\in\mathcal{E}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{V}_{\Pi}:=\{X_{f}^{h}\in\Gamma(T(TM))|\text{ }f\in\mathcal{E}\}.

Proposition 3.5

\mathcal{V}_{h}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\Pi}
are two abelian Lie subalgebras of vector fields.

For all
f_{1},f_{2}\in E
we have

[X_{f_{1}},X_{f_{2}}]=([df_{1},df_{2}]_{h})^{\#}=\left(\nabla_{X_{f_{1}}}df_{2}-\nabla_{X_{f_{2}}}df_{f_{1}}\right)^{\#}=0.

The other assumption follows from
[X^{h}_{f_{1}},X^{h}_{f_{2}}]=[X_{f_{1}},X_{f_{2}}]^{h}
.

It is well known that for any affine manifold
(M,\nabla)
, the space of vector fields
\Gamma(TM)
endowed with the product
X\bullet Y=\nabla_{X}Y
is a left symmetric algebra, i.e, for any
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM)
,

\mathrm{ass}(X,Y,Z)=\mathrm{ass}(Y,X,Z),

where
\mathrm{ass}(X,Y,Z)=(X\bullet Y)\bullet Z-X\bullet(Y\bullet Z)
.
It is therefore natural to look at the behavior of the subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{h}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\Pi}
with respect to the left symmetric structures in
\Gamma(TM)
and in
\Gamma(TTM)
.

Theorem 3.6

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold satisfying the following condition:

{}f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{E}\Longrightarrow h(df_{1},df_{2})\in\mathcal{E}.

(3.1)

Then
(\mathcal{V}_{h},\bullet)
and
(\mathcal{V}_{\Pi},\bullet)
are two commutative associative subalgebras of
(\Gamma(TM),\bullet)
and
(\Gamma(T(TM)),\bullet)
respectively. Moreover, the map
X_{f}\mapsto X_{f}^{h}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{V}_{h}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\Pi}
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
. For any
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
we have

\displaystyle\prec\alpha,X_{f_{1}}\bullet X_{f_{2}}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec\alpha,(\mathcal{D}_{df_{1}}df_{2})^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec\mathcal{D}_{df_{1}}df_{2},\alpha^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{\#}.h(df_{1},df_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec\alpha,X_{h(df_{1},df_{2})}\succ.

Hence
X_{f_{1}}\bullet X_{f_{2}}=X_{h(df_{1},df_{2})}
which is in
\mathcal{V}_{h}
by the condition (
3.1
). The remainder of the theorem is obvious.

The class of K-V manifolds satisfying condition (
3.1
) deserves a special study that might be the main subject of another paper; here we only give an example of such class of K-V manifolds.

Example 3.7

Let
(\mathcal{A},.,B)
be a finite dimensional commutative and associative algebra endowed with a symmetric scalar
2
-cycle. We recall from
[
1
]
Proposition
4.2
that the dual
\mathcal{A}^{*}
carries a K-V structure
(\nabla,h)
where
\nabla
is the canonical affine structure on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
h
is the affine symmetric bivector field on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
given by

h(u,v)(\alpha)=\prec\alpha,u(\alpha).v(\alpha)\succ+B(u(\alpha),v(\alpha))

where
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
u,v\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
. We consider the canonical coordinates system
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
associated to a basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
; then

h(dx_{i},dx_{j})=b_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}C^{k}_{ij}x_{k},

where
C^{k}_{ij}=\prec e_{k}^{*},e_{i}.e_{j}\succ
and
b_{ij}=B(e_{i},e_{j})
. Hence we get that a function
f
belongs to
\mathcal{E}
if and only if for all
i,j=1,\cdots,n
we have

{}\sum_{l,k}h_{il}h_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{k}}=0.

(3.2)

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
, then we have

h(df_{1},df_{2})=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{j}}.

By a direct computation we get that:

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l}h_{il}h_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}h(df_{1},df_{2})}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,m,s}h_{il}h_{jk}h_{ms}\frac{\partial^{3}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}+h_{il}h_{jk}h_{ms}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{s}}

\displaystyle+h_{il}h_{jk}C^{k}_{ms}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}+h_{il}h_{jk}h_{ms}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2}}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{s}}

\displaystyle+h_{il}h_{jk}h_{ms}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial^{3}f_{2}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}\partial x_{s}}+h_{il}h_{jk}C^{k}_{ms}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2}}{\partial x_{l}\partial x_{s}}

\displaystyle+h_{il}h_{jk}C^{l}_{ms}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}+h_{il}h_{jk}C^{l}_{ms}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{s}}.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{k}h_{ik}C^{k}_{jm}=\sum_{k}h_{jk}C^{k}_{im}
together with equation (
3.2
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l,m,s}h_{il}h_{jk}h_{ms}\frac{\partial^{3}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}\partial x_{m}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,s}h_{ms}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{m}}\left(\sum_{k,l}h_{il}h_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}\right)-\sum_{k,l,s}h_{jk}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{s}}\left(\sum_{m}h_{ms}C^{m}_{il}\right)

Now we look at the relation between the notion of K-V submanifolds and the affine foliation.

Proposition 5.5

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold with affine foliation
\mathcal{F}
. An affine submanifold
(N,\nabla^{N})\subset(M,\nabla)
is a K-V submanifold if and only if for each leaf
L\in\mathcal{L}
the intersection
L\cap N
is an open subset of
L
. Hence, the affine foliation of
(N,\nabla^{N},h^{N})
consists of the connected components of the intersection
L\cap N
.

An affine submanifold
(N,\nabla^{N})\subset(M,\nabla)
is a K-V submanifold if and only if

\mathrm{Im}(h_{\#}(x))\subset T_{x}N,\text{ }\forall x\in N.

It follows that for a K-V submanifold
N\subset M
, every affine leaf of
(N,\nabla^{N},h^{N})
is also an integral submanifold of
(M,\nabla,h)
. Hence, every affine leaf of
(N,\nabla^{N},h^{N})
is an open subset of an affine leaf of
\mathcal{F}
.

Conversely, if for each affine leaf
L\in\mathcal{F}
the intersection
L\cap N
is an open subset of
L
. Then for any

\mathrm{Im}(h_{\#}(x))=T_{x}L\subset T_{x}N
, where

is the affine leaf passing through

is a K-V submanifold.

Remark 5.6

Let
(M,\nabla,g)
be a pseudo-Hessian manifold. Then the only K-V submanifolds are the open subsets of

.

Finally, we give the relation between K-V maps and the affine foliation.

Proposition 5.7

If
F:(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
is K-V map then for each pseudo-Hessian leaf

(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})

is open in

. In particular, if

(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
.

Let

.

Given

are

\gamma_{2}(t)=F(\gamma_{1}(t))
, for all small enough

.

Corollary 5.8

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold. If

N^{1}\cap N^{2}\subset M

Let

In general, K-V submanifolds don’t have functoriality under K-V maps which are explained by the following example.

Example 5.9

Consider the K-V map

6
Koszul-Vinberg transversals

We recall from (
[
4
]
) that a Poisson transversal of a Poisson manifold
(P,\pi)
is a submanifold
N\subset P
such that, at every point
x\in N
, we have

T_{x}P=T_{x}N+\pi_{\#}(T_{x}N^{\circ}).

Definition 6.1

A Koszul-Vinberg transversal of a K-V manifold
(M,\nabla,h)
is an affine submanifold
N\subset M
such that, at every point
x\in N
, we have

T_{x}M=T_{x}N+h_{\#}(T_{x}N^{\circ}).

(6.1)

Note that the equality
\mathrm{rank}(TN^{\circ})=\mathrm{rank}(T_{N}M)-\mathrm{rank}(TN)
implies that condition (
6.1
) is equivalent to the direct sum decomposition:

T_{N}M=TN\oplus h_{\#}(TN^{\circ}).

(6.2)

The main reason to consider K-V transversals is that they have naturally induced K-V structures. Indeed, let
N
be a K-V tranversal of
(M,\nabla,h)
. Then the decomposition for
T_{N}M
and the dual decomposition for
T^{*}_{N}M
gives a sequence of bundle maps:

T^{*}N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{*}_{N}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{\#}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{N}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}TN.

The resulting bundle map
T^{*}N\rightarrow TN
is symmetric and so it is of the form
h^{N}_{\#}
for a unique symmetric bivector field
h^{N}
on
N
. Hence we get that:

Proposition 6.2

(N,\nabla,h^{N})
is a K-V manifold.

To show this proposition we need the following lemma:

Lemma 6.3

Let
N
be a K-V tranversal of
(M,\nabla,h)
. Then
TN
is a Poisson transversal submanifold of
(TM,\Pi)
.

Let
x\in M
,
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)
and
X\in\Gamma(TM)
such that
\alpha_{x}\in T_{x}N^{\circ}
. Let
u\in T_{x}N
. For all
Z\in\Gamma(TN)

\prec(\alpha^{h})_{u},(Z^{h})_{u}\succ=\prec\alpha_{x},Z_{x}\succ=0,

and

\prec(\alpha^{v})_{u},(Z^{v})_{u}\succ=\prec\alpha_{x},Z_{x}\succ=0,

hence
(\alpha^{h})_{u},(\alpha^{v})_{u}\in T_{u}(TN)^{\circ}
. On the other hand there exist
Y\in\Gamma(TN)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)
such that
\gamma_{x}\in T_{x}N^{\circ}
and
X_{x}=Y_{x}+\gamma_{x}^{\#}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\prec(\beta^{v})_{u},(X^{v})_{u}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec\beta_{x},X_{x}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec\beta_{x},Y_{x}+\gamma_{x}^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec(\beta)^{v}_{u},(Y^{v})_{u}+(\gamma_{x}^{\#})_{u}^{v}\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prec(\beta)^{v}_{u},(Y^{v})_{u}+\Pi_{\#}(\gamma^{h}_{u})\succ.

This implies that
(X^{v})_{u}=(Y^{v})_{u}+\Pi_{\#}(\gamma^{h}_{u})
. Similarly we get that
(X^{h})_{u}=(Y^{h})_{u}-\Pi_{\#}(\gamma^{v}_{u})
. Hence the equality
T_{u}(TM)=T_{u}(TN)+\Pi_{\#}(T_{u}(TN)^{\circ})
follows from the fact that
T_{u}(TM)=\mathrm{span}\{(X^{v})_{u},(X^{h})_{u}|X\in\Gamma(TM)\}
.

Let
\Pi^{N}
be the skew-symmetric bivector field associated to the pair
(\nabla,h^{N})
, one can see also that
\Pi^{N}
coincide with the bundle map given by the following composition maps

T^{*}(TN)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Tp^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{*}_{TN}(TM)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Pi_{\#}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{TN}(TM)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Tp}}{{\longrightarrow}}T(TN).

According to the Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
2.13
in
[
4
]
,
\Pi^{N}
is a Poisson teansor field on
TN
, hence we get that
(\nabla,h^{N})
is a K-V structure on
N
. .

It is important to note that for a K-V transversal
N
in
(M,\nabla,h)
, with induced K-V structure

, the inclusion map
\iota:(N,\nabla,h^{N})\hookrightarrow(M,\nabla,h)
is not a K-V map (Unless is an open set in

). This will be clear in the next example.

Example 6.4

Take

endowed with its canonical affine structure and
h=x\partial_{x}\otimes\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}\otimes\partial_{y}

Clearly

is a K-V structure and

is a K-V transversal, however the induced K-V bivector field on

vanish identically.

K-V transversals behave functorially under pullbacks by K-V maps. This turns out to be a very useful property:

Proposition 6.5

Let
F:(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
be a K-V map and let

be a K-V transversal. Then

is transverse to

is a K-V transversal in

. Moreover,

and
N^{2}
.

Consider

and let
y=F(x)\in N^{2}
. Since

is a K-V map we have for all
\alpha\in T^{*}_{y}M^{2}

\alpha^{\#_{2}}=T_{x}F((T_{x}F^{*}\alpha)^{\#_{1}}).

(6.3)

Therefore
h^{2}_{\#}(T^{*}_{y}M^{2})\subset T_{x}F(T_{x}M^{1})
. Since

is a K-V transversal this implies that

T_{y}M^{2}=T_{y}N^{2}+h_{\#}^{2}(T_{y}^{*}M^{2})=T_{y}N^{2}+T_{x}F(T_{x}M^{1}).

This show that
F
is transverse to
N^{2}
. In particular,

is a submanifold of
M^{1}
.

The affinity of
N^{1}
follows from Theorem 2 in (
[
8
]
). Let

, and decompose

, with

and
\alpha\in(T_{y}N^{2})^{\circ}

is mapped by

to

v=w+(T_{x}F^{*}\alpha)^{\#_{1}}\in T_{x}N^{1}+h_{\sharp}^{1}((T_{x}N^{1})^{\circ}).

Therefore

is a K-V transversal.

According to the Lemma

we get that
TN^{2}\subset(TM^{2},\Pi^{2})

Corollary 6.6

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold, and
(N^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})\subset M
be a K-V transversal and let
(N^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})\subset M
be a K-V submanifold. Then

and

intersect transversally,

is a K-V transversal in

and a K-V submanifold of

, and the two induced K-V structures on

coincide.

Now we give the relation between K-V transversals and the affine foliation.

Proposition 6.7

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold with affine foliation
\mathcal{F}
. An affine submanifold
N\subset M
is a K-V transversal if and only if for all
L\in\mathcal{F}
, the intersection
L\cap N
is a K-V submanifold of
L
. Hence, the affine foliation of
\left(N,\nabla,h^{N}\right)
consists of the connected compenents of the intersection
L\cap N
.

The condition for affine submanifold

to be a K-V transversal is

T_{N}M=TN\oplus h_{\#}\left(TN^{\circ}\right).

This condition is equivalent to have both the following conditions satisfied:

(i)

T_{N}M=TN+h_{\#}\left(TN^{\circ}\right)
.

(ii)

TN\cap h_{\#}\left(TN^{\circ}\right)=0
.

Condition
(i)
says that
N
is transverse to the affine leaves and condition
(ii)
(provided
(i)
is satisfied) says that the kernel of the pullback of
g_{L}
to

is trivial, so

is a K-V submanifold of
L
.

7
Coisotropic Koszul-Vinberg submanifolds

Let
(P,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. A submanifold
N\subset P
is called coisotropic submanifold if and only if
\pi_{\#}(TN^{\circ})\subset TN
. Now we have a similar statement for K-V manifolds, we consider a K-V manifold
(M,\nabla,h)
and
N
be an affine submanifold of
M
.

Proposition 7.1

The following assertions are equivalent

1.

h_{\#}(TN^{\circ})\subset TN
.

2.

TN
is a coisotropic submanifold of
(TM,\Pi)
.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
then
\alpha_{|_{TN}}=0
if and only if
\alpha^{h}_{|_{T(TN)}}=\alpha^{v}_{|_{T(TN)}}=0
. Hence the desired equivalence follows from equation
(1.4)
.

Definition 7.2

A coisotropic K-V submanifold
N\subset M
is an affine submanifold such that
h_{\#}(TN^{\circ})\subset TN
.

Clearly K-V submanifolds is a subclass of coisotropic K-V submanifolds.

Proposition 7.3

Let
(M,\nabla,h)
be a K-V manifold. For an affine closed submanifold
N\subset M
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

N
is a coisotropic K-V submanifold;

(ii)

For every
f,g\in\mathcal{J}(N)
where
\mathcal{J}(N)
is the vanishing ideal we have
X_{f}(g)\in\mathcal{J}(N)
;

(iii)

For every
f\in\mathcal{J}(N)
the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}
is tangent to
N
.

(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
Let
f,g\in\mathcal{J}(N)
clearly for any
x\in N
we have
d_{x}f,d_{x}g\in T_{x}N^{\circ}
. Now let
N\subset M
be a coisotropic K-V submanifold then
h(x)(d_{x}f,d_{x}g)=0
, so
h(df,dg)\in\mathcal{J}(N)
.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
Assume that
\mathcal{J}(N)
is stable under
h
. Let
f,g\in\mathcal{J}(N)
, for all
x\in N

X_{f}(g)(x)=h(x)(d_{x}f,d_{x}g)=0.

The closedness of the submanifold
N
implies that
X_{f}
is tangent to
N
.

(iii)\Longrightarrow(i)
Again the closedness of the submanifold
N
implies that
T_{x}N^{\circ}
is generated by elements
d_{x}f
where
f\in\mathcal{J}(N)
. so we conclude that for any
\alpha,\beta\in TN^{\circ}
,
h(\alpha,\beta)=0
. Therefore,
N
is coisotropic.

Proposition 7.4

Let
F:(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
be a K-V map and assume that
F
is transverse to a coisotropic K-V submanifold
N^{2}\subset M^{2}
. Then
F^{-1}(N^{2})\subset M^{1}
is a coisotropic K-V submanifold.

The result follows by applying Propostion
2.34
in (
[
4
]
) to the Poisson map
TF:(TM^{1},\Pi^{1})\rightarrow(TM^{2},\Pi^{2})
, with the affinity of
F^{-1}(N^{2})
which is garanted by Theorem 2 in (
[
8
]
).

There is one more important property of coisotropic objects and which shows their relevance in K-V geometry. In order to express it, we introduce the following notation: If
(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})
and
(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
are K-V manifolds we denote by
(M^{1}\times\overline{M}^{2},\nabla,h)
the K-V manifold such that the canonicals projection
p_{1}:(M^{1}\times\overline{M}^{2},\nabla,h)\rightarrow(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})
and
p_{2}:(M^{1}\times\overline{M}^{2},\nabla,h)\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2},-h^{2})

Proposition 7.5

For a smooth map
F:(M^{1},\nabla^{1},h^{1})\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2},h^{2})
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

F
is a K-V map.

(ii)

\mathrm{Graph}(F)\subset M^{1}\times\overline{M}^{2}
is a coisotropic K-V submanifold.

To prove this Proposition we need the following lemma which is a generalization of the following fact: A map
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is affine if and only if its graph is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Now let
(M^{1},\nabla^{1})
and
(M^{2},\nabla^{2})
be two affine manifolds and
F:(M^{1},\nabla^{1})\rightarrow(M^{2},\nabla^{2})
is a smooth map.

Lemma 7.6

F
is an affine map if and only if its graph is an affine submanifold of
(M^{1}\times M^{2},\nabla^{1}\oplus\nabla^{2})
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathrm{Graph}(F)
,
\tilde{\gamma}=(\gamma,F(\gamma))
where
\gamma
is a curve on
M^{1}
such that
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
hence for any
u\in T_{p}M^{1}
we have

\tau^{\tilde{\gamma}}(u,T_{p}F(u))=(\tau^{\gamma}(u),\tau^{F(\gamma)}(T_{p}F(u)))

where
\tau^{\tilde{\gamma}}
is the parallel transport along the curve
\tilde{\gamma}
seen as a curve on
M^{1}\times M^{2}
. Hence
T_{q}F(\tau^{\gamma}(u))=\tau^{F(\gamma)}(T_{p}F(u))
if and only if
\tau^{\tilde{\gamma}}(u,T_{p}F(u))\in T_{(q,F(q))}\mathrm{Graph}(F)
. This show that
F
is an affine map if and only if commute with parallel transport if and only if
\mathrm{Graph}(F)
is an affine submanifold of
(M^{1}\times M^{2},\nabla^{1}\oplus\nabla^{2})
.

Follow directly from Lemma
7.6
.

Now let’s explore more proprieties of coisotropic K-V subamanifold. For that we endowed the space of section of the vector bundle
TN^{\circ}
with the product
\bullet

\alpha\bullet\beta=\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta.

Proposition 7.7

Let

be a coisotropic submanifold. Then
(TN^{\circ},N,\bullet,\rho)
is a left symmetric algebroid, where
\rho
is the restriction of

to the subbundle

. Moreover fro any

the vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\ker\rho_{x}
is a commutative associative algebra.

What we need to show is that the product
\bullet
is well defined the other assertions is a direct consequence of this fact. Let
X
be a vector field on

which is tangent to
N
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma(TN^{\circ})
then we have

\displaystyle\prec\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta,X\succ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X.(\prec\alpha,\beta^{\#}\succ)+\prec\alpha,\nabla_{X}\beta^{\#}\succ+\prec\beta,\nabla_{X}\alpha^{\#}\succ

\displaystyle+h_{\#}(\alpha).(\prec\beta,X\succ)-\prec\beta,\nabla_{\alpha^{\#}}X\succ

using the affinity of

together with the condition

we get that
\prec\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta,X\succ=0
hence
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\beta\in\Gamma(TN^{\circ})
.

. A submanifold has clean intersection with a foliation if it intersects cleanly every leaf of the foliation.

Proposition 7.9

Let
N
be an affine submanifold of a K-V manifold
(M,\nabla,h)
which has
clean intersection with its affine foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Then
N
is a coisotropic submanifold of
(M,\nabla,h)
if and only if for each affine leaf
L\in\mathcal{F}
the intersection
L\cap N
is
a coisotropic submanifold of
N
.

Assume that
N\subset M
is an affine submanifold which is transverse to the affine
foliation
\mathcal{F}
. This means that for each
L\in\mathcal{F}
the inclusion
\iota:L\hookrightarrow M
is transverse

N
. Now:

(a)

If
N
is coisotropic in
M
, it follows that
\iota^{-1}(N)=L\cap N
is coisotropic in
N
, since
the inclusion is a K-V map.

(b)

If
L\subset N
is coisotropic in
L
, then we have:

h_{\#}^{L}(T(L\cap N)^{\circ})\subset T(L\cap N)=TL\cap TN,

where the annihilator is in
T^{*}L
. It follows that for for any a
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
such that
\alpha_{|_{T(L\cap N)}}=0
, we have:

\alpha^{\#}\in\Gamma(TN)
.
But
(TL\cap TN)^{\circ}=TL^{\circ}+TN^{\circ}
, so we conclude that
h_{\#}(TN^{\circ})\subset TN
, which means that
N
is coisotropic.

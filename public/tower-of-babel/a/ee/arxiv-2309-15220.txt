Abstract
We present exactly solvable modifications of the two-matrix Zinn-Justin-Zuber model and write it as a tau function. The grand partition function of these matrix integrals is written as the fermion expectation value. The perturbation theory series is written out explicitly in terms of series in strict partitions. The related string equations are presented.

2.1
Standard and modified models of two unitary matrices

In
[
55

the following integral over two unitary matrices was studied

I_{1}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}^{*})=C\int_{\mathbb{U}_{N}\times\mathbb{U}_{N}}e^{c\,\mathrm{tr}\left(U_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{-1}\right)+\sum_{n>0}\frac{1}{n}\left(t_{n}\mathrm{tr}U_{1}^{n}+t^{*}_{n}\mathrm{tr}U_{2}^{n}\right)}d_{*}U_{1}dU_{2}^{*}

(1)

where

\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots),\,\mathbf{t}^{*}=(t_{1}^{*},t_{2}^{*},\dots)
play the role of coupling constants in the model,

CI(0,0)=1

It is shown that

can be written explicitly as a series of the Schur polynomials
as functions in the coupling constants

I_{N}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}^{*})=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t})s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t}^{*})\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}\frac{c}{(N-i+j)!}

(2)

where
(i,j)

\lambda

all Young diagrams of height do not accede
N

Modified family

\tau\left(N;c\,U^{-1}_{1}U^{-1}_{2};f\right):=\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i<j\leq N}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-i+j)s_{\lambda}\left(c\,U^{-1}_{1}U^{-1}_{2}\right)\prod_{i\leq N}f(\lambda_{i}-i+N)

{\rm if}\,\,f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(x+1)}\,\,{\rm then}\,\,\tau\left(N;c\,U^{-1}_{1}U^{-1}_{2};f\right)=e^{c\,\mathrm{tr}\left(U_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{-1}\right)}

(5)

{\rm if}\,\,f(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x+a)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(x+1)}\,\,{\rm then}\,\,\tau\left(N;c\,U^{-1}_{1}U^{-1}_{2};f\right)=\mathrm{det}\left(1-c\,U_{1}^{-1}U_{2}^{-1}\right)^{-a}

\int_{\mathbb{U}_{N}}\tau\left(c\,UU^{-1}_{1}U^{-1}U^{-1}_{2};f\right)d_{*}U=\frac{\mathrm{det}[\tau(1;c\,u^{-1}_{i}v^{-1}_{j};f)]_{i,j\leq N}}{\prod_{i<j\leq N}(u^{-1}_{i}-u^{-1}_{j})(v^{-1}_{i}-v^{-1}_{j})}

(7)

\tau(1;c\,u^{-1}v^{-1};f)]=1+f(1)u^{-1}v^{-1}+f(2)u^{-2}v^{-2}+\cdots

(8)

which allows us writing down

(9)

(10)

which is the Toda lattice
[

3
New models of two unitary matrices

Let us consider a modification of the models mentioned above.
Let us consider the following model:

J_{N}(\mathbf{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{p}^{(2)})=C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{U}_{N}\times\mathbb{U}_{N}}e^{c\,\mathrm{tr}\left(U_{1}^{-2}U_{2}^{-2}\right)+\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}\frac{2}{n}\left(p^{(1)}_{n}\mathrm{tr}U_{1}^{n}+p^{(2)}_{n}\mathrm{tr}U_{2}^{n}\right)}d\mu_{1}(U_{1})d\mu_{2}(U_{2})

(11)

d\mu_{i}(U)=\mathrm{det}\left(U^{-\frac{1}{2}(N^{2}-N)+\kappa_{i}}\right)d_{*}U_{i},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2

(12)

For the sake of simplicity,
we choose
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=0
(all calculations below can be reproduced also in case
\kappa_{i}\neq 0
,
but all formulas take up noticeably more space in this case).
Here, the constant

In this model, the parameters

To study (

\int_{\mathbb{U}_{N}}e^{\mathrm{tr}\left(UAU^{-1}B\right)}d_{*}U=\frac{\mathrm{det}\left[e^{a_{k}b_{l}}\right]_{1\leq k,l\leq N}}{\prod_{k<l}(a_{k}-a_{l})(b_{k}-b_{l})}

J_{N}(\mathbf{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{p}^{(2)})=\tilde{C}_{N}\oint\dots\oint\prod_{i<j}\frac{(u_{i}-u_{j})(v_{i}-v_{j})}{(u_{i}+u_{j})(v_{i}+v_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{c\,\mathrm{tr}\left(u_{i}^{-2}v_{i}^{-2}\right)+\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}\frac{2}{n}\left(p^{(1)}_{n}u_{i}^{n}+p^{(2)}_{n}v_{i}^{n}\right)}\frac{du_{i}}{u_{i}}\frac{dv_{i}}{v_{i}}

13

for the Haar measure

d_{*}U_{1}\sim\prod_{i<j}|u_{i}-u_{j}|^{2}\prod_{k}\frac{du_{k}}{u_{k}}

d_{*}U_{2}\sim\prod_{i<j}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}\prod_{k}\frac{dv_{k}}{v_{k}}

For the next step, we use the following analogue of the Cauchy-Littlewood relation

e^{\sum_{n=1,3,5,\dots}\frac{2}{n}p_{n}\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{n}\right)}=\sum_{\alpha\in DP\atop\ell(\alpha)\leq N}Q_{\alpha}(\mathbf{p})Q_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{p}(X)\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\alpha)}2^{-\alpha_{i}}

where

is the so-called projective Schur function, see Appendix. This is a polynomial
in the variables
p_{1},p_{3},\dots
and

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k}),\,\alpha_{1}>\cdots>\alpha_{k}\geq 0,\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,2,\dots

p_{n}=p_{n}(X):=\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{n}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptn\,{\rm is\,odd}

(16)

Thus, the polynomial
Q_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{p}(X)\right)
is a symmetric function in the eigenvalues of

For the sake of simplicity, we shall write

At last, one can prove (see Appendix

see Appendix. From (

We recall that a non-increasing set of non-negative integers

\lambda_{i}

\lambda

\lambda

[
29

Partitions will be denoted by Greek letters:

\alpha,\beta

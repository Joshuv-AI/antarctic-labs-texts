Separating path systems of almost linear size
Shoham Letzter

Abstract
A separating path system for a graph G G is a collection 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of paths in G G such that for every two edges e e and f f , there is a path in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} that contains e e but not f f . We show that every n n -vertex graph has a separating path system of size O ⁡ ( n ​ log ∗ ​ n ) O(n\log^{*}n) . This improves upon the previous best upper bound of O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n\log n) , and makes progress towards a conjecture of Falgas-Ravry–Kittipassorn–Korándi–Letzter–Narayanan and Balogh–Csaba–Martin–Pluhár, according to which an O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) bound should hold.

Given a set

\mathcal{F}

The notion of weak separation is sometimes referred to, simply, as

. We keep the word ‘weak’ in the definition, as our focus will be on the strong version.
In particular, if
\mathcal{F}

, and if it weakly-separates

The notion of separation was introduced by Rényi

in 1961. It is easy to see that the smallest weakly-separating system for a set of size

(1+o(1))\log_{2}n
(see
[

). The problem of determining the size of a smallest separating system becomes more interesting when one imposes restrictions on the members of the separating system.
For example, separating systems with restrictions on the size of their members were studied in
[

,
41
,
35
,
29
]
, and different extremal questions about separating systems were studied in
[

Another interesting direction is to consider separating systems where the ground set is a graph and the members of the separating system satisfy some graph theoretic properties; see
[

17
,

]
for extremal examples
[
38

39
,
20
]
for algorithmic examples. Our focus will be on

, where the ground set is the set of edges of a given graph

Separating path systems arise naturally in the study of network design (see
[

,
20
,
38
]
).
In the context of extremal graph theory, this variant was proposed by Gyula Katona in the

Define

The study of separating path systems of graphs is related to the well-researched topic of path decompositions. A famous conjecture of Gallai asserts that every connected graph on

Every

By

Proof overview

The main purpose of this section is to give an overview of the proof of
Theorem

1.4
.
We will first describe a general strategy for finding small separating path systems (see
Section

2.1
), we then show how sublinear expanders come into play (
Section

2.2
), and we give a more detailed proof overview in
Section

2.3
. At the end of the section we outline the structure of the paper (see
Section

) and define some notation (in
Section

).

2.1
A general strategy

We start by describing a general strategy for finding a separating path system for a graph
G
on
n
vertices, which was used in
[
13
]
. By
Theorem

1.2
, there is a collection
\mathcal{P}
of at most
n
paths that decomposes
G
; for an edge
e
, denote by
P(e)
the unique path in
\mathcal{P}
that contains
e
. We claim that there is a collection
\mathcal{M}
of at most
3n
matchings that decomposes
G
, such that
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{M}
separates the edges of
G
.

Indeed, take
\mathcal{M}
to be a collection of
3n
pairwise edge-disjoint matchings, whose edges intersect each path in
\mathcal{P}
in at most one edge, and that covers a maximum number of edges under these conditions. Suppose that
e
is an edge in
G
which is not covered by any of the matchings in
\mathcal{M}
. Notice that there are at most
n-1
matchings in
\mathcal{M}
that contain edges of
P(e)
, and at most
2(n-1)
matchings in
\mathcal{M}
that contain an edge that intersects
e
. Hence, there is a matching
M\in\mathcal{M}
that contains neither edges of
P(e)
nor edges that intersect
e
. Replacing
M
by
M\cup\{e\}
, we reach a contradiction to the maximality of
\mathcal{M}
, thus showing that
\mathcal{M}
covers all edges in
G
.
Observe that
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{M}
separates the edges of
G
. Indeed, defining
M(e)
to be the unique matching in
\mathcal{M}
that contains the edge
e
, we see that for any two edges
e
and
f
, one of
P(e)
and
M(e)
contains
e
but not
f
.

Thus, if we could find a path
P_{M}
, for each
M\in\mathcal{M}
, that contains
M
but avoids all other edges of
\bigcup_{e\in M}P(e)
, then we would obtain a separating path system for
G
whose size is
|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{M}|\leq 4n
.

Of course, this strategy does not always work; otherwise we would have been able to improve on our main result,
Theorem

1.4
. Nevertheless, if
G
has expansion properties and we impose additional restrictions on the matchings in
\mathcal{M}
, then this strategy often succeeds.
Indeed, in
[
13
]
it was used to show that, with high probability, the random graph
G(n,p)
has a weakly-separating path system of size
O(n)
for every
p=p(n)
(similar ideas appear in
[
4
]
, where the authors found much smaller strongly-separating path systems when
p\geq\frac{1000}{\log n}
). In this paper we apply it to a more general class of expanders.

2.2
Expanders

The exact notions of expansion that we shall use will be introduced later (see
Sections

4
and
5
). We mention that they are variants of the ‘sublinear expanders’, which were introduced by Komlós and Szemrédi
[
27
,
28
]
, and have since been modified in various ways to prove many interesting results
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
30
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
26
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
8
]
. As their name suggests, such expanders have a fairly weak expansion property. Their strength is in their omnipresence: every graph with a sufficiently large (but possibly still constant) average degree contains a sublinear expander. In fact, Bucić and Montgomery
[
8
]
recently showed that every graph can be decomposed (or almost decomposed, if we want the expanders to be ‘robust’) into sublinear expanders that, on average, cover each vertex a constant number of times. This is very useful for us; it shows that, essentially, it suffices to be able to find separating path systems of linear size in expanders.

With this decomposition-into-expanders result at hand, a rough plan would be to apply it to decompose a given graph into expanders, and then find a linear separating path system for each expander using the strategy above. If this plan could be realised as stated, then we would end up with a linear separating path system for the original graph. However, depending on the density of the expanders we find, we sometimes require them to be ‘robust’ (or ‘somewhat robust’), and then some edges might remain uncovered. Moreover, in some situations, our methods separate almost all, but not all, edges of a given expander.
Instead, we perform
O(\log^{\star}n)
steps, each time significantly decreasing the number of unseparated edges, until remaining with
O(n)
edges that we can separate trivially by individual edges.

2.3
Proof sketch

We now give a more concrete outline of our proof.
Our main task is to prove that, given a graph
G
on
n
vertices and with average degree
d
, there is a subgraph
G_{1}
on
O(n\polylog d)
edges and a collection
\mathcal{P}
of
O(n)
paths in
G
that separates the edges of
G-G_{1}
.

It now essentially suffices to separate the edges of
G_{1}
. Indeed, suppose that
\mathcal{Q}
is a collection of paths in
G
that separates
G_{1}
.
By
Theorem

1.2
, there are collections
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
of at most
n
paths that decompose
G-G_{1}
and
G_{1}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
separates the edges of
G
. To see this, let
e
and
f
be distinct edges in
G
. If both are in
G-G_{1}
, then
\mathcal{P}
has a path containing
e
but not
f
; if both are in
G_{1}
then
\mathcal{Q}
has such a path; if
e
is in
G-G_{1}
and
f
is not, then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
has a path through
e
but not
f
; and finally if
e
is in
G_{1}
but
f
is not then
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
has such a path.

Iterating the argument in the paragraph before last thus yields a separating path system for
G
of size
O(n\log^{\star}n)
.
We complete this task in three steps.

Step 1: dense expanders.

We show (in
Lemma

3.1
) that given a graph
J
on
m
vertices, it has a subgraph
J_{1}
of size
O(m\polylog m)
such that
J-J_{1}
can be separated using
O(m)
paths.
To do so, we decompose
J
into ‘robust’ expanders (with total order
O(m)
) and a remainder of size
O(m\polylog m)
and show that such expanders can be separated using a linear number of paths (in
Corollary

6.4
).

To separate a robust expander
K
, we show that if
V
is a set of vertices in
K
that includes each vertex with probability
1/3
, then, with high probability, the edges of
K-V
can be separated by a linear number of paths. To find these paths, we apply the strategy given in
Section

2.1
to
K-V
, to obtain small collections
\mathcal{P}
of paths and
\mathcal{M}
of matchings that together separate the edges of
K
, while imposing a certain degree condition on the matchings in
\mathcal{M}
. We then partially connect the edges of each
M\in\mathcal{M}
using a subset
V_{1}
of
V
, chosen uniformly at random, thereby forming a path forest whose ends ‘expand well’ into
V-V_{1}
. Now, we adapt a result from
[
8
]
(which in turn uses ideas from a paper of Tomon
[
40
]
) that allows for connecting pairs of vertices, disjointly, via a random set of vertices.

Step 2: separating edges touching large degrees in sparse expanders.

We again start by decomposing a graph
G
on
n
vertices and with average degree
d
into expanders (here the expanders are not required to be robust, and so we have no uncovered edges), whose sum of orders is
O(n)
. For each such expander
H
, we show (in
Lemma

8.1
) that its edges touching high degree vertices can be separated using
O(n+\frac{e(H)}{d})
paths.

Denoting by
L
the set of large degree vertices in
H
, let us explain how to separate the edges in
H[L]
. Writing
k:=n+\frac{e(H)}{d}
, we apply the strategy in
Section

2.1
, while additionally requiring that the paths in
\mathcal{P}
and matchings in
\mathcal{M}
have size at most
d
; it is straightforward to show that there exist appropriate
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{M}
of size
O(k)
. Now, given a matching
M\in\mathcal{M}
, let
F
be a path forest that extends
M
, avoids vertices in
X:=\bigcup_{e\in M}V(P(e))-V(M)
, and under these conditions has as few components as possible, and as few edges as possible (prioritising the former). Suppose, towards a contradiction, that there are at least two components, and let
u
and
v
be leaves of distinct components of
F
. The minimality assumption allows us to show that, roughly speaking, balls around
u
in
H-X
have few neighbours in
F
. Adapting a lemma from Liu–Montgomery
[
32
]
, it follows that more than
|H|/2
vertices in
H
can be reached from
u
in
H-(X\cup V(F)-\{u\})
, exploiting the fact that
u
has a large degree. Since the same holds for
v
, it follows that
F
can be extended by a path from
u
to
v
, a contradiction. Thus
F=F_{M}
is a path that extends
M
and avoids other edges from
\bigcup_{e\in M}P(e)
, and
\{F_{M}:M\in\mathcal{M}\}\cup\mathcal{P}
is a collection of
O(k)
paths separating
G[L]
.

In fact, similar reasoning can be applied to separate edges
xy
where
x\in L
and
y
has at least four neighbours in
L
, by allowing the members of
\mathcal{M}
to contain paths of length
2
whose ends are in
L
.

Step 3: separating almost all remaining edges in
H
.

For each
H
as in the previous step, let
F
be its subgraph of ‘unseparated edges’; so
F
has small maximum degree. We now decompose
F
into ‘somewhat robust’ expanders (with total order
O(|H|)
) and a remainder of
O(\polylog d\cdot|H|)
edges. Consider one of these expanders
J
. If
|J|
is fairly large with respect to
d
(namely, if
|J|\geq 2^{(\log d)^{7})}
) then we show (in
Lemma

8.4
) that its edges can be separated using
O(|J|)
edges.

The proof is similar to the one outlined in Step 2. Here, in addition to requiring the paths and matchings in
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{M}
to have size at most
d
, we also require the edges in
\mathcal{M}
to be far from each other (the assumption that
|J|
is large with respect to
d
is used to prove that appropriate
\mathcal{M}
exists). Defining
M
,
X
and
F
as above, it suffices to show that
F
has just one component. To this end, taking
u
to be a leaf in
F
, we first show that the ball of radius
(\log d)^{5}
around
u
in
J^{\prime}:=J-(X\cup V(F)-\{u\})
is large, exploiting the expansion property being ‘somewhat robust’ and the fact that the other edges of
M
are far from
u
. We then proceed like in the previous step to expand
u
further, and reach a contradiction if
F
has at least two components. As before, this implies the existence of a separating path system for
J
of size
O(|J|+\frac{e(J)}{d})
.

If instead
|J|\leq 2^{(\log d)^{7}}
, we apply the first step to show that all but
O(|J|\polylog|J|)
edges of
J
can be separated using
O(|J|)
paths.

Altogether, we get a collection of
O(n)
paths that separate all but
O(n\polylog d)
edges of the graph
G
that we started with at the beginning of the second step, concluding the iterative step.

Notation

Our notation is mostly standard. Given a graph
G

X

.

All logarithms will be in base

3
Main lemmas

In this section we state two key lemmas and then show how to use them to prove our main result (
Theorem

1.4
).

The first key lemma shows that given any graph on
n
vertices there is a linear collection of paths separating all but
O(n\polylog n)
of its edges.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a graph on
n
vertices. Then there is a subgraph
G_{1}\subseteq G
, with
e(G_{1})\leq n(\log n)^{55}
, and a collection
\mathcal{P}
of at most
1300n
paths in
G
that separates the edges of
G-G_{1}
.

The second key lemma has a more complicated statement; essentially, it shows that for every graph
G
there is a linear collection of paths that separates a subgraph of
G
whose complement in
G
can be decomposed into small subgraphs and a sparse remainder.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
d_{0}
such that the following holds.
Suppose that
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with average degree
d
, where
d\geq d_{0}
.
Then there is a subgraph
G_{1}\subseteq G
, a collection
\mathcal{H}
of subgraphs of
G
and a collection
\mathcal{P}
of paths in
G
with the following properties.

(1)

The graphs in
\mathcal{H}
are mutually edge-disjoint;

(2)

4
Decomposition into expanders

We say that a graph
G
on
n
vertices is an
(\varepsilon,s,t)
-expander
if
|N_{G-F}(X)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|X|}{(\log|X|+1)^{2}}
for every
X\subseteq V(G)
and
F\subseteq E(G)
satisfying
1\leq|X|\leq 2n/3
and
|F|\leq s\cdot\min\{|X|,t\}
.
An
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander
is an
(\varepsilon,s,2n/3)
-expander (namely,
F
is not restricted by
t
). An
\varepsilon
-expander
is an
(\varepsilon,0)
-expander (namely,
F
is always empty).
This definition builds on previously defined notions of expansion: Komlós and Szemerédi
[
28
,
27
]
introduced a notion of sublinear expanders, which are (up to a slight change in parameters)
\varepsilon
-expanders in our notation.
Haslegrave, Kim and Liu
[
23
]
introduced a notion of robust sublinear expanders, which are
(\varepsilon,s)
-expanders in our notation.
We add the parameter
t
, which provides a cut-off in the robustness: vertex sets of size at most
t
expand robustly, whereas larger vertex sets expand less robustly.

The following lemma shows that every graph
G
can be decomposed into expanders, or almost decomposed into robust expanders. This is a variant of Lemma 14 in
[
8
]
and the proof is similar but more technical. The main difference is the introduction of the parameter
t
, which allows us to obtain expanders where small sets of vertices expand robustly, while not leaving too many edges uncovered.
As a sanity check, note that as
s
and
t
increase, the expansion property becomes stronger, and the potential number of uncovered edges grows.

We will apply this lemma with several regimes for
s
and
t
: in
Section

6
we take
s
to be polylogarithmic in
n
(and take
t=2n/3
). In
Section

8
we apply this lemma twice, once with
s=0
(in which case the choice of
t
does not matter), and once with
s
a large constant and
t
being the average degree of the ground graph.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
,
t\geq 1
and let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/48
.
Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then there is a collection
\mathcal{H}
of pairwise edge-disjoint
(\varepsilon,s,t)
-expanders that covers all but at most
48sn(\log t+1)^{2}
edges of
G
and satisfies
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}|H|\leq 2n
.

Proof.

We leave a few simple calculus claims (see
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.5
) until after this proof. Notice that we may assume
t\leq\frac{2n}{3}
, by our definition of expanders.

We prove the statement by induction on
n
, with the following slightly stronger upper bounds:
24sn\cdot(\log t+1)^{2}(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1})
on the number of edges removed, and
n\cdot(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1})
on the sum of orders.
If
G
is itself an
(\varepsilon,s,t)
-expander then we may take
\mathcal{H}=\{G\}
, noting that
n\cdot(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1})\geq n
.

Suppose then that
G
is not an
(\varepsilon,s,t)
-expander. In particular,
n\geq 2
because the singleton graph is an
(\varepsilon,s,t)
-expander. This means that there are subsets
X\subseteq V(G)
and
F\subseteq E(G)
such that
1\leq|X|\leq 2n/3
,
|F|\leq s\cdot\min\{|X|,t\}
, and
|N_{G-F}(X)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon|X|}{(\log|X|+1)^{2}}
. Let
G_{1}:=G[X\cup N_{G-F}(X)]
and let
G_{2}
be the subgraph obtained from
G-X
by removing the edges with both ends in
N_{G-F}(X)
.
Then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are edge-disjoint, together they cover all edges in
G-F
, and they satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}n\leq|G_{1}|+|G_{2}|=n+|N_{G-F}(X)|&\leq n+\frac{\varepsilon|X|}{(\log|X|+1)^{2}}\\
&\leq n+\frac{3\varepsilon|G_{1}|}{(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}\leq n+\frac{|G_{1}|}{16(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}},\end{split}

(1)

where the penultimate inequality follows from
4.3
stated below.

The bulk of the calculations needed for this lemma will be performed in the following claim.

Claim 4.2
.

\displaystyle\,|G_{1}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}\right)+|G_{2}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{2}|+1}\right)+\frac{\min\{|G_{1}|,t\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}\leq n\left(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right).

Proof.

Write
D
for the difference between the left-hand side and the right-hand side.

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=|G_{1}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}\right)+|G_{2}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{2}|+1}\right)-n\left(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right)+\frac{\min\{|G_{1}|,t\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\left(|G_{1}|+|G_{2}|-n\right)-\frac{|G_{1}|}{\log|G_{1}|+1}-\frac{|G_{2}|}{\log|G_{2}|+1}+\frac{n}{\log n+1}+\frac{\min\{|G_{1}|,t\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{|G_{1}|}{8(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}-\frac{|G_{1}|}{\log|G_{1}|+1}-\frac{|G_{2}|}{\log n+1}+\frac{n}{\log n+1}+\frac{\min\{|G_{1}|,t\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,|G_{1}|\left(\frac{1}{8(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}+\frac{1}{\log n+1}+\frac{\min\{1,t/|G_{1}|\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}\right),

where for the first inequality we used (
1
).
We consider two cases:
|G_{1}|\geq t
and
|G_{1}|<t
.
In the first case, using
4.3
, we have

\frac{\min\{1,t/|G_{1}|\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}=\frac{t}{24|G_{1}|(\log t+1)^{2}}\leq\frac{3|G_{1}|}{24|G_{1}|(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{8(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}.

Thus, noting that
|G_{1}|\leq|X|+\frac{\varepsilon|X|}{(\log|X|+1)^{2}}\leq|X|\left(1+\frac{1}{24}\right)\leq\frac{2n}{3}\cdot\frac{25}{24}\leq\frac{3n}{4}
, and using
4.5
,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\leq\,|G_{1}|\left(\frac{1}{4(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}+\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right)\leq 0.

In the second case, by
4.4
, we have
\frac{1}{8(\log|G_{1}|+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}\leq\frac{1}{8(\log t+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log t+1}
. Thus,

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\leq|G_{1}|\left(\frac{1/8+1/24}{(\log t+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log t+1}+\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|G_{1}|\left(\frac{1}{4(\log t+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log t+1}+\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right)\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from
4.5
, using
1\leq t\leq 2n/3
.
Either way,
D\leq 0
, as required.
∎

For
i\in[2]
, apply the induction hypothesis to
G_{i}
to obtain a family
\mathcal{H}_{i}
of pairwise edge-disjoint subgraphs of
G_{i}
that cover all but at most
24s(\log t+1)^{2}|G_{i}|(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{i}|+1})
edges of
G_{i}
and satisfy
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{i}}|H|\leq|G_{i}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{i}|+1}\right)
. Take
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\cup\mathcal{H}_{2}
. By
4.2
, the graphs in
\mathcal{H}
cover all but at most the following number of edges in
G
:

\displaystyle 24s(\log t+1)^{2}\left(|G_{1}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}\right)+|G_{2}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{2}|+1}\right)+\frac{\min\{|G_{1}|,t\}}{24(\log t+1)^{2}}\right)\leq

\displaystyle 24sn(\log t+1)^{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}}|H|\leq\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}|H|+\sum_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{2}}|H|

\displaystyle\leq|G_{1}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{1}|+1}\right)+|G_{2}|\left(2-\frac{1}{\log|G_{2}|+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n\left(2-\frac{1}{\log n+1}\right).

So
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the requirements, proving the induction hypothesis.
∎

.

Claim 4.3
.

\frac{x}{(\log x+1)^{2}}\leq\frac{3y}{(\log y+1)^{2}}

\frac{1}{8(\log x+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log x+1}\leq\frac{1}{8(\log y+1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\log y+1}

Claim 4.5
.

The following holds for

\frac{1}{4x^{2}}-\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{\log n+1}\leq 0.

\displaystyle f(1)

\displaystyle=\log n+1-4(\log n+1)+4=1-3\log n\leq 0.

\displaystyle f(\log(3n/4)+1)

5
Finding vertex-disjoint paths through a random vertex set

In this section we prove
Lemma

5.10
, which allows for connecting pairs of vertices
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{r},y_{r})
in a robust expander through a random set of vertices via vertex-disjoint paths, provided that any subset of the vertices
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
expands well. This is a variant of Theorem 10 in
[
8
]
, which does something very similar, but avoids the expansion assumption on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
and only requires the paths to be edge-disjoint.
Our proof follows
[
8
]
very closely. We note that, as mentioned also in
[
8
]
, some of the ideas that appear in the proof are due to Tomon
[
40
]
.

We will introduce the relevant preliminaries (including a different notion of expansion) in
Section

5.1
, we then prove
Lemma

5.10
in three steps, given in
Sections

5.2
,
5.3
and
5.4
.

5.1
Preliminaries

Say that a graph
G
on
n
vertices is a
weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander
if
|N_{G-F}(U)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|U|}{(\log n)^{2}}
for every
U\subseteq V(G)
and
F\subseteq E(G)
with
1\leq|U|\leq 2n/3
and
|F|\leq s|U|
. In
[
8
]
such a graph is called simply an
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander; we add ‘weak’ to its name to distinguish it from
(\varepsilon,s)
-expanders, which have stronger expansion properties for smaller sets of vertices.
Note that an
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander on at least two vertices is a weak
(\frac{\varepsilon}{2},s)
-expander.

We will use the following two propositions from
[
8
]
, both of which show that every not-too-large vertex set
U
in an expander either ‘expands well’ (i.e. has a large neighbourhood) or ‘expands robustly’ (i.e. there are many vertices in
N(U)
with many neighbours in
U
). We state a slightly simplified version of the propositions, where there is no set of forbidden edges, because we will not use this feature.

Proposition 5.1

(Proposition 12 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, let
0<d\leq s
. Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex
(\varepsilon,s)
-weak expander. Then one of the following holds, for every set
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\leq\frac{2n}{3}
.

•

|N(U)|\geq\frac{s|U|}{2d}
, or

•

\left|\{u\in V(G):|N(u)\cap U|\geq d\}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|U|}{(\log n)^{2}}
.

Proposition 5.2

(Proposition 13 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon\geq 2^{-9}
,
s\geq 8(\log n)^{13}
, and let
n
be large.
Suppose that
G
is an
(\varepsilon,s)
-weak expander,
and let
W\subseteq V(G)
satisfy
|W|\leq 2n/3
. Then
G
contains one of the following

•

a collection of at least
\frac{|W|}{(\log n)^{7}}
pairwise vertex-disjoint stars of size at least
(\log n)^{9}
, whose centre is in
W
and its leaves are in
V(G)-W
,

•

a bipartite graph
H
with parts
W
and
X\subseteq V(G)-W
, such that

–

|X|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|W|}{2(\log n)^{2}}
,

–

every vertex in
X
has degree at least
(\log n)^{4}
and every vertex in
W
has degree at most
2(\log n)^{9}
in
H
.

As in
[
8
]
, we also need the following hypergraph version of Hall’s theorem, due to Aharoni and Haxell.

Theorem 5.3

(Aharoni–Haxell
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s,r\geq 1
be integers, and let

be hypergraphs on the same vertex set whose edges have at most

whose

.

Additionally, we use the following martingale concentration result (see Chapter 7 in
[

]
).
We say that a function

.

Let

be independent random variables, with

taking values in a set

for

5.2
Expansion into a random vertex set

The following lemma shows that, given a sufficiently robust weak expander
G
and a random set of vertices
V
, obtained by including each vertex independently with probability
1/6
, any set
U
expands well in
V
, while avoiding a given small set of vertices
Z
, with quite large probability. This lemma is a variant of Lemma 17 in
[
8
]
, where instead of a forbidden set of edges we have a forbidden set of vertices, and the proof is essentially the same.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
2^{-9}\leq\varepsilon<1
,
s\geq 8(\log n)^{13}
and let
n
be large. Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander and let
U,Z\subseteq V(G)
be sets satisfying
|U|\geq(\log n)^{23}
and
|Z|\leq\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{3}}
. Let
V
be a random subset of
V(G)
, obtained by including each vertex independently with probability
1/6
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right)
,

\left|B^{(\log n)^{4}}_{G[V^{\prime}]}(U\cap V^{\prime})\right|>\frac{|V|}{2},

where
V^{\prime}:=V-Z
.

Proof.

Take
\ell:=(\log n)^{4}
, and let
p
satisfy
1-(1-p)^{\ell}(1-\frac{3}{20})(1-\frac{1}{120})=\frac{1}{6}
, so that

\displaystyle 1-p\ell\leq(1-p)^{\ell}=\frac{1-1/6}{(1-3/20)\cdot(1-1/120)}\leq\frac{99}{100},

which implies that
p\geq\frac{1}{100(\log n)^{4}}
.
Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell},V^{*},V^{**}
be random sets, where
V_{i}
is obtained by including each vertex with probability
p
, independently,
V^{*}
is obtained by including each vertex with probability
\frac{1}{120}
, independently, and
V^{**}
is obtained by including each vertex with probability
\frac{3}{20}
, independently. Notice that each vertex is in
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{\ell}\cup V^{*}\cup V^{**}
with probability
1/6
, so we may think of
V
as the union
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{\ell}\cup V^{*}\cup V^{**}
.

Define
U^{*}=(U\cap V^{*})-Z
. Then, with probability
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(|U|\right)\right)
, we have
|U^{*}|\geq\frac{1}{200}|U|-|Z|\geq\frac{1}{400}|U|
.
Define
B_{0}:=U^{*}
and, for
i\geq 1
, let
B_{i}
be the set of vertices in
G
that can be reached by a path in
G-Z
that starts in
U^{*}
, has length at most
i
, and its interior is in
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{i}
. We emphasise that
B_{i}
is only required to be disjoint from
Z
, and need not be a subset of
V_{i}
. Notice that
B_{i}\subseteq B_{i+1}
for every
i\geq 0
, implying that
|B_{i}|\geq|B_{0}|=|U^{*}|\geq\frac{1}{400}|U|
for
i\geq 0
when
|U^{*}|\geq\frac{1}{400}|U|
.

Claim 5.6
.

If
|B_{i}|\leq\frac{2}{3}n
, then, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right)
,

\left|B_{i+1}-B_{i}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|B_{i}|}{10^{4}(\log n)^{2}}.

Proof.

Notice that a vertex in
N(B_{i})-Z
is in
B_{i+1}-B_{i}
if at least one of its neighbours in
B_{i}
is sampled into
V_{i+1}
.
We consider the two possible outcomes of
Proposition

5.2
for
B_{i}
.

Suppose that the first outcome holds, so there are
\frac{|B_{i}|}{(\log n)^{7}}
pairwise vertex-disjoint stars of size
(\log n)^{9}
with centres in
B_{i}
and leaves in
N(B_{i})
.
By a Chernoff bound, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{p|B_{i}|}{(\log n)^{7}}\right)\right)=1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{11}}\right)\right)
, at least
\frac{p|B_{i}|}{2(\log n)^{7}}
centres are added to

, implying that

\left|B_{i+1}-B_{i}\right|\geq(\log n)^{9}\cdot\frac{p|B_{i}|}{2(\log n)^{7}}-|Z|\geq\frac{|B_{i}|}{200(\log n)^{2}}-\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{3}}\geq\frac{|B_{i}|}{400(\log n)^{2}}.

Now suppose that the second outcome holds, so there is a bipartite subgraph
H\subseteq G
with parts
B_{i}
and
X\subseteq V(G)-B_{i}
, with
|X|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|B_{i}|}{4(\log n)^{2}}
, such that vertices in
X
have degree at least
d:=(\log n)^{4}
in

while vertices in
B_{i}
have degree at most
D:=2(\log n)^{9}
in
H
.
Let

be the set of vertices in
X
that do not have an
H
-neighbour in

. Note that
\mathbb{E}|Y|\leq|X|(1-p)^{d}\leq|X|e^{-pd}\leq\frac{399}{400}|X|
. Note also that
|Y|
is
D
-Lipschitz, since the outcome of the sampling of any single vertex in

affects the outcome of at most
D
vertices in

. Thus, by
Lemma

5.4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|Y|>\frac{799}{800}|X|\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|Y|>\mathbb{E}|Y|+\frac{|X|}{800}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{|X|^{2}}{2\cdot 800^{2}\cdot D^{2}|B_{i}|}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right).

So, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right)
, we have
|B_{i+1}-B_{i}|\geq\frac{|X|}{800}-|Z|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|B_{i}|}{10^{4}(\log n)^{2}}
.
∎

Iterating the claim, with probability at least

1-(\log n)^{4}\cdot\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right)\geq 1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right),

if
|B_{i}|\leq\frac{2}{3}n
and
i\leq(\log n)^{4}
, then,

|B_{i}|\geq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{10^{4}(\log n)^{2}}\right)^{i}|U|,

which implies
|B_{\ell}|\geq\frac{2}{3}n
with probability
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{22}}\right)\right)
.

To complete the proof, note that any vertex in
B_{\ell}
that gets sampled into

(or that is already in

) is in the set
B^{\prime}:=B_{G[V^{\prime}]}^{(\log n)^{4}}(U\cap V^{\prime})
. By Chernoff, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
, at least
\frac{99}{100}\cdot\frac{3}{20}\cdot\frac{2}{3}n=\frac{99}{1000}n
vertices of

are in

, and
|V|\leq\frac{101}{100}\cdot\frac{1}{6}n=\frac{101}{600}n
. If all this holds, then
|B^{\prime}|>\frac{1}{2}|V|
, as required.
∎

The next corollary, which is a variant of Lemma 19 in
[
8
]
, boosts the probability of expansion of

into

, so as to be amenable to an application of the union bound, with the expense of a slightly smaller upper bound on

.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
2^{-9}\leq\varepsilon<1
,
s\geq 2^{11}(\log n)^{50}
, and let
n
be large. Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander, and let
V
be a random subset of
V(G)
, obtained by including each vertex independently with probability at least
1/6
. Then, with high probability, if
U,Z\subseteq V(G)
are sets with
|Z|\leq\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{30}}
, then

\left|B^{(\log n)^{4}}_{G[V^{\prime}]}\left(N(U)\cap V^{\prime}\right)\right|>\frac{|V|}{2},

(2)

where
V^{\prime}:=V-Z
.

Proof.

Say that a set
U\subseteq V(G)

expands well
if
|N(U)|\geq|U|(\log n)^{24}
. Given a (non-empty) set
U
which expands well, and a set
Z
with
|Z|\leq|U|
,
Lemma

5.5
(applied with
U_{\ref{lem:expansion-random}}=N(U)
) tells us that (
2
) holds, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\Omega\left(|U|(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)
.

By a union bound, we conclude that the probability that (
2
) fails for
(U,Z)
, with
U
well-expanding and
|Z|\leq|U|
, is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{u=1}^{n}\binom{n}{u}^{2}\exp\left(-\Omega\left(u(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u=1}^{n}\exp\left(2u\log n-\Omega\left(u(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=1}^{n}\exp\left(-\Omega\left(u(\log n)^{2}\right)\right)=o(1/n).

For the rest of the proof we assume that (
2
) holds for all such pairs
(U,Z)
.
To complete the proof, we will deduce that (
2
) holds for all pairs
(U,Z)
, where
U
need not expand well,
|U|\leq\frac{2}{3}n
, and
|Z|\leq\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{30}}
. Fix such
U,Z
.

Write
d:=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}(\log n)^{26}
. By
Proposition

5.1
, either
|N(U)|\geq\frac{s|U|}{2d}
, or
\left|\{u:|N(u)\cap U|\geq d\}\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|U|}{(\log n)^{2}}
. Notice that the first outcome implies that
U
expands well and so we already know that (
2
) holds. We thus assume that the second one holds, and write
W:=\{u:|N(u)\cap U|\geq d\}
. Let
U^{\prime}
be a subset of
U
of size
\frac{|U|}{d}
, chosen uniformly at random. For a fixed
w\in W
, the probability that
w
has no neighbours in
U^{\prime}
is at most

\frac{\binom{|U|-d}{|U|/d}}{\binom{|U|}{|U|/d}}\leq\left(\frac{|U|-d}{|U|}\right)^{|U|/d}\leq e^{-1}.

It follows that
\mathbb{E}[|W\cap N(U^{\prime})|]\geq(1-e^{-1})|W|\geq\frac{\varepsilon|U|}{2(\log n)^{2}}=|U^{\prime}|(\log n)^{24}
. In particular, there is a subset
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
of size
\frac{|U|}{d}
with
|N(U^{\prime})|\geq|U^{\prime}|(\log n)^{24}
. Since such

expands well, and
|Z|\leq\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{30}}\leq|U^{\prime}|
, Equation (
2
) holds for
(U^{\prime},Z)
and thus for
(U,Z)
.
∎

5.3
A path connection through a random set

Our end goal is to be able to join (disjointly)
r
pairs of vertices through a random set

, provided that they expand well. Here we show how to join one pair of vertices (while avoiding a small set of forbidden vertices). This is a variant of Proposition 8 in
[
8
]
, where in addition to forbidding vertices instead of edges, we also impose the expansion property on the vertices we wish to connect. The proof is essentially the same.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
2^{-9}\leq\varepsilon<1
,
s\geq 2^{11}(\log n)^{50}
, and let
n
be large. Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander, and let
V
be a random subset of
V(G)
, obtained by including each vertex independently with probability
1/6
.
Then, with high probability, the following holds for every
r
: if

are distinct vertices, satisfying

there is an

-path in

whose interior is in

and whose length is at most

.

Proof.

Fix an outcome of
V
such that the following holds, where
V^{\prime}=V-Z
.

•

Equation (
2
) holds for every disjoint

with
|Z|\leq\frac{|U|}{(\log n)^{30}}
,

•

|V|\geq\frac{n}{8}
,

•

|N(X)\cap V^{\prime}|\geq|X|(\log n)^{49}
for every

of size at least

.

By

5.7

Write

.
For a set of vertices

and integer

Let

Claim 5.9
.

|X_{1}|<\frac{r}{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose

. We will show that there is a sequence

\left|R^{i\ell}(X_{i})\right|>\frac{|V|}{2},

(3)

for

.

\left|B^{(\log n)^{4}}_{G[V^{\prime}]}\big(N(U)\cap V^{\prime}\big)\right|>\frac{|V|}{2}.

and (

5.4
Many disjoint path connections through a random set

Finally, we prove that given a sufficiently robust weak expander and pairs
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(x_{r},y_{r})
, such that the set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\}
expands well, the pairs can be connected via vertex-disjoint paths through a random set

. This is a variant of Lemma 10 in
[
8
]
(though their statement looks quite different).

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
2^{-9}\leq\varepsilon<1
,
s\geq 2^{11}(\log n)^{50}
, and let
n
be large. Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander, and let
V
be a random subset of
V(G)
, that includes each vertex independently with probability
\frac{1}{6}
.
Then, with high probability, for every sequence of distinct vertices

satisfying

for every

, there is a sequence of paths

, whose interiors are pairwise vertex-disjoint and in

, such that

is a path from

to

.

Proof.

We assume that the conclusion of

5.8
holds (it holds with high probability, so we are justified in making this assumption).
Let
\mathcal{H}_{i}
be the hypergraph on vertex set
V
with edges the interiors of
P
, for all
(x_{i},y_{i})
-paths

of length at most
\ell:=(\log n)^{6}
with interior in

.

We will use

5.3
. Fix a subset

. We wish to show that there is a matching of size

in

. Suppose no such matching exist, and let

. Then

and every edge in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

are satisfied, showing that there is a matching

∎

6
Proof of
Lemma

3.1
: separating dense expanders

In this section we prove the first key lemma,
Lemma

3.1
, which shows that for any graph
G
, there is a linear (i.e. of size
O(|G|)
) path system that separates a subgraph of
G
, whose complement in
G
has polylogarithmic average degree.
This will follow quite easily from
Lemma

4.1
, that decomposes graphs into expanders, and
Corollary

6.4
that shows that robust weak expanders can be separated by a linear path system.

To prove the latter corollary, given a robust weak expander
G
, we will take a random partition
\{V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\}
of

and show that, with high probability,

can be separated using a linear path system, for

. Taking the union of these collections of paths, as well as a decomposition of

into a linear number of paths, we obtain the desired linear separating paths system for

.

It thus suffices to prove that, with high probability, if
V
is a random set, obtained by including each vertex independently with probability
1/3
, then
G-V
has a linear separating path system. This is done in the following lemma, whose proof proceeds as follows.
First, we decompose
G-V
into at most
n

\mathcal{P}
(using
Theorem

1.2
), and then we show that
G-V
can be further decomposed into

, each of which intersects each path in
\mathcal{P}
in at most one edge, and moreover each matching satisfies a certain degree condition.
Next, we take a random partition
\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
of
V
, taken uniformly at random. We first extend each
M
through
V_{1}
to a path forest

, in such a way that any set of leaves of

expands in
G
, and thus in
V
. This is exactly the property needed for an application of
Lemma

5.10
, so we can use it to connect the leaves of

via paths through
V
, vertex-disjointly. We thus get a path

that extends

and contains no other edges from
G-V
, and so

is a linear collection of paths separating
G-V
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
2^{-9}\leq\varepsilon<1
,
s\geq 2^{11}(\log n)^{51}
, and let
n
be large.
Suppose that
G
is a weak
(\varepsilon,s)
-expander, and let

be a random subset of

, that includes each vertex independently with probability
\frac{1}{3}
. Then, with high probability, there is a collection of
211n

G-V
.

Proof.

Expanding with forbidden sets

As a preparation for the proof of the second key lemma, in this section we prove a variant of Lemma 3.2 from
[
32

Following
[

]
, say that a vertex set

\left|N_{G}(B_{H-X}^{i-1}(A))\cap X\right|\leq ki
for every integer

.
The following lemma is similar to Lemma 3.2 in
[

]

Lemma 7.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

such that the following holds for

is an
n
-vertex

-expander, and let

and

be disjoint subsets of

and

-limited contact with

, for every integer

.

Write

and

for

. Our task is thus to show

For

is the disjoint union of

, we have

\displaystyle|B_{j}|\geq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(\log|B_{j-1}|)^{2}}\right)|B_{j-1}|\geq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(\log|B_{i}|)^{2}}\right)|B_{j-1}|,

using that

.
Iterating this, we get

|B_{i}|\geq\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(\log|B_{i}|)^{2}}\right)^{i}|A|.

(4)

(Notice that this inequality holds for

, as

, since

is assumed to be disjoint of

, allowing us to treat the induction base and step simultaneously.)

We conclude that

. Indeed, if

8.2

The next lemma shows that given a ‘somewhat robust’ expander

The proof is very similar in structure to the proof of

. We first decompose

into paths

and find matchings

\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{M}

Now it remains to show that for each

As before, we take

to be a path forest that extends

to deduce that

(by assumption on

-expansion. Note also that

, by
(\varepsilon,s,d)
-expansion, implying that

has minimum degree at least

. Write

and
\big|N_{G^{\prime\prime}}(B^{r-1}_{G^{\prime\prime}-X_{u}}(A_{u}))\cap X_{u}\big|\leq r+2\leq 3r
for

. Write

3
-limited contact with

. Using that

is large,

7.1
shows
|B_{G^{\prime\prime}-X_{u}^{\prime}}^{r_{0}-2}(A_{u})|\geq 2^{(r_{0}-2)^{1/4}}\geq 2^{(\log d)^{2}}\geq d^{8}

9
), as

.

Next, we follow the proof of

quite closely to show that the set
B_{G^{\prime}-X_{u}}^{r_{0}}(u)
expands.

\left|N_{G}\!\left(B^{r-1}_{G-Y}(B_{u})\right)\cap V(F)\right|\leq d(r+r_{0}+1).

(11)

The proof of this is very similar to that of (

.
Suppose this does not hold for some

and

in

\big|B_{G-Y}^{(\log n)^{4}}(B_{u})\big|>n/2.

For

9
Conclusion

Recall that
\sep^{*}(G)
is the size of a smallest weakly-separating path system for
G
, and
\sep^{*}(n)
is the maximum of
\sep^{*}(G)
over all
n
-vertex graphs
G
;
\sep(G)
and
\sep(n)
are defined analogously for strong separation.

In this paper we proved that
\sep(n)=O(n\log^{\star}n)
. This is significant progress from the easy initial bound of
O(n\log n)
, towards a conjecture of, independently, Falgas-Ravry–Kittipassorn–Korándi–Narayanan and the author
[
13
]
, and Balogh–Csaba–Martin–Pluhár
[
4
]
, that
\sep(n)=O(n)
.

It is plausible that our methods could be used to fully settle this conjecture. For example, one could try to prove a statement like the following, by induction on
n
. Suppose that
G
is an
\varepsilon
-expander and let
H
be a subgraph of
G
on
n
vertices. Then the edges of
H
can be separated using
O(n)
paths in
G
. To accomplish this, it would suffice to find
O(n)
paths in
G
that separated all edges of
H
touching a set
V
of
\Omega(n)
vertices in
H
. An obstruction to achieving this is a graph
H
as follows:
H
has average degree
d
, but almost all vertices in
H
have degree much lower than
d
(say, at most
\log\log d
), and, moreover, there are relatively small sets of low degree vertices in
H
with few low degree neighbours.

In fact, the authors of
[
13
]
suggest that, perhaps,
\sep^{*}(n)=(1+o(1))n
; it is easy to see that this bound would be tight by considering complete graphs. If true, this is likely to be very hard to prove. Indeed, even the restriction to complete graph seems hard. Wickes
[
42
]
proved that
\sep^{*}(K_{n})\leq(\frac{21}{16}+o(1))n
for any integer
n
, and that
\sep^{*}(K_{n})\leq n
when
n=p\text{ or }p+1
for some prime
p
. It would be nice, and perhaps not completely out of reach, to prove that
\sep^{*}(K_{n})=(1+o(1))n
. It also makes sense to study
\sep(K_{n})
.

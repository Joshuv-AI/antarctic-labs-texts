Well-posedness and applications of classical elastohydrodynamics for a swimming filament
Yoichiro Mori, Laurel Ohm

Abstract
. We consider a classical elastohydrodynamic model of an inextensible filament undergoing planar motion in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The hydrodynamics are described by resistive force theory, and the fiber elasticity is governed by Euler-Bernoulli beam theory. Our aim is twofold: (1) Serve as a starting point for developing the mathematical analysis of filament elastohydrodynamics, particularly the analytical treatment of an inextensibility constraint, and (2) As an application, prove conditions on internal fiber forcing that allow a free-ended filament to swim. Our analysis of fiber swimming speed is supplemented with a numerical optimization of the internal fiber forcing, as well as a novel numerical method for simulating an inextensible swimmer.



1.
Introduction

We consider the planar motion of an inextensible, free-ended filament immersed in a Stokes fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The hydrodynamic effects on the filament are described using resistive force theory (or local slender body theory), one of the most fundamental tools for modeling flagellar locomotion in viscous fluids
[
16
,

,

,

. The structure of the filament itself is described by the classical Euler-Bernoulli beam theory, coupled with internal forcing corresponding to a (possibly nonzero) preferred curvature

,

,

Resistive force theory coupled with the elastic response of the fiber has been shown to be remarkably effective at capturing actual filament dynamics, as demonstrated by
[

]
.

Aim 1: Analysis of inextensibility.

A key feature of this model is the inextensibility constraint which prohibits the fiber from growing or shrinking in length over time. Inextensibility features prominently in related models of, for example, vesicle dynamics
[
57
,
56
,
47
]
, but its treatment is relatively underdeveloped from the perspective of mathematical analysis, particularly in the dynamical setting.

We begin by considering the relaxation of the filament with zero preferred curvature and show global-in-time existence and uniqueness of solutions for small initial data, as well as local well-posedness for large initial data. Global well-posedness for large data is hindered by the behavior of the filament tension. However, we show that if the filament’s initial bending energy is small, then a solution exists globally. As a byproduct, we obtain nonlinear stability of the straight filament. The filament evolution in this setting may be regarded as a simpler version of the Peskin problem (immersed boundary method), which has been the subject of many recent PDE works
[
13
,
14
,
29
,
40
,
54
]
. This work also follows a recent program by the authors to place nonlocal slender body theory on firm theoretical footing
[
38
,
39
,
36
,
37
,
44
]
. These previous papers have all considered the static boundary value problem for Stokes flow about a curve at a single instant in time. The form of the integral operator in nonlocal slender body renders the dynamic problem for the curve evolution much more difficult. Using resistive force theory for the force-to-velocity map along the fiber simplifies the curve evolution problem while still remaining relevant from a current modeling perspective (see, for example,
[
1
,
4
,

,

,

Aim 2: Analysis of swimming and applications.

Resistive force theory coupled with fiber elastodynamics has been especially useful for understanding undulatory swimming at low Reynolds number

On the analysis side, we consider the filament dynamics under small-amplitude periodic forcing, corresponding to a nonzero preferred curvature along the fiber. We show existence of a unique periodic solution and derive an expression for the swimming speed of the filament. From the swimming expression, we obtain conditions on the periodic forcing that give rise to propulsion. Our swimming analysis may also be compared to other analysis works on swimming, including
[

,

The swimming expression we obtain allows us to perform a small numerical optimization to determine the internal fiber forcing which gives rise to the fastest swimming for a fixed amount of work and bending energy. The optimal forcing that we find is a type of traveling wave; notably, we do not

Finally, we develop a novel numerical method based on two recent approaches to inextensibility, due to Moreau et al.
[

and Maxian et al.

These reformulations of filament elastohydrodynamics are interesting from an analysis perspective because, as seen in the well-posedness results of this paper, estimates for the filament tension are a limiting factor in the solution theory which hinder us in showing global existence for large initial data. A weaker notion of inextensibility – perhaps related to these reformulations – may overcome this difficulty. Moreover, a broader aim of this paper is to serve as a starting point for a full numerical analysis of the various formulations of fiber inextensibility used in numerical simulations, ranging from penalization methods

to variants of the more recent approaches which avoid solving for the fiber tension altogether

1.1.
Setup and statement of results

We consider an inextensible elastic filament in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with centerline
\bm{X}:[0,L]\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\big(c_{\perp}{\bf I}+(c_{\parallel}-c_{\perp})\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{s}^{\rm T}\big)\big(E(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n})-\tau(s,t)\bm{X}_{s}\big)_{s}

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=1

For a filament of radius

The system (

\displaystyle(\kappa(s,t),\tau(s,t))\mapsto(\lambda\kappa(\lambda s,\lambda^{4}t)\,,\lambda^{2}\tau(\lambda s,\lambda^{4}t))\,.

Norms which are invariant under the above scaling symmetry are known as critical norms and play an important role in the well-posedness theory. Norms which decay upon ‘zooming in’ via the above scaling are known as subcritical. The natural expectation is that the PDE is (locally) well-posed in subcritical spaces and solutions belonging to subcritical cases are in fact smooth. Typically, if we have
a priori

\displaystyle T\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\bigg)=\varepsilon\leq c_{0}\,,\quad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=c_{1}\varepsilon\leq c_{0}\,.

Then a filament whose curvature evolution satisfies (
7
)-(
9

(25)

\bm{V}(t)=U(t)\,\bm{e}_{\rm t}(0,0)+\bm{r}_{\rm v}(t)\,,

where
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert U\right\rvert\leq c\varepsilon^{2}
and
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert\bm{r}_{\rm v}\right\rvert\leq c\varepsilon^{3}
, and
U
is given by

(26)

U(t)=-\gamma\int_{0}^{1}(\kappa_{0})_{s}\overline{\kappa}\,ds\,.

Writing
\kappa_{0}(s,t)=\sum_{m,k=1}^{\infty}\big(a_{m,k}\cos(\omega mt)-b_{m,k}\sin(\omega mt)\big)\psi_{k}(s)

\psi_{k}(s)
are the eigenfunctions of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
(see (

may be written

(27)

\displaystyle\langle U\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{m,k,\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{2}m^{2}}{\omega^{2}m^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\omega m}\big(a_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}-b_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}\big)

\displaystyle+a_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}+b_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{k}(\psi_{\ell})_{s}\,ds\,+r_{\rm u}\,,

where
\lambda_{k}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}
and

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is given in Section
3.3
. We note that the additional

regularity on
\kappa_{0}
is used to ensure that the fiber frame
(\bm{e}_{\rm t},\bm{e}_{\rm n})
is changing very little over time (see equation (

)). As in previous works (see, e.g.,
[
25

), we obtain that an
O(\varepsilon)
forcing results in propulsion at most at
O(\varepsilon^{2})
.
From (

However, from (

are needed to actually generate net movement over the course of one full period. Observations about the swimming speed (
27
) are made more precise in Section
4.1
, but may be summarized as follows:

Observations 1.5
.

(1)

Forcing only the lowest nonzero temporal frequency appears to yield the furthest filament displacement for a given amount of work. Thus in further applications we consider the filament’s preferred curvature to be of the form

(28)

\kappa_{0}(s,t)=F_{1}(s)\cos(\omega\,t)+F_{2}(s)\sin(\omega\,t)\,.

(2)

If
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are both even or both odd about
s=\frac{1}{2}
, the integral
\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{k}(\psi_{\ell})_{s}\,ds
in (
27
) vanishes and the filament does not swim to leading order.

(3)

If either
F_{1}=0
or
F_{2}=0
, or if
F_{1}=\pm F_{2}
, then the term proportional to
\big(a_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}-b_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}\big)
in (
27
) vanishes. The filament may still swim at leading order, but its displacement will be very small, due to the size of
\lambda_{k}
(see (
30
)).

Observation (1), which was also noted in
[
24
]
, comes from numerically solving a simple constrained optimization problem (see (
79
)). The requirements on
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
may be considered in terms of the Scallop Theorem for a swimmer in a linear, time-independent flow: violating observation (2) can be seen numerically to lead to a time-reversible filament deformation (see Section
4.3
), while violating (3) gives rise to a nearly time-reversible fiber deformation.

A waveform fitting the above criteria is a traveling wave propagating along the filament; for example,
F_{1}=\sin(\omega s)
and
F_{2}=\cos(\omega s)
. This is consistent with classical calculations by Pironneau and Katz
[
45
]
which find that a filament should deform as a traveling wave to reach a given swimming speed while minimizing energy expenditure.

A further small numerical optimization problem (see (
80
)) where
\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
is constrained as well yields a
\kappa_{0}
(
28
) in the form of a traveling wave which outperforms the simple prescription
F_{1}=\sin(\omega s)
and
F_{2}=\cos(\omega s)
. A notable feature of this optimal
\kappa_{0}
is that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
look like
\sin(\omega s)
and
\cos(\omega s)
away from the filament ends, but
F_{1}=F_{2}=0
and
F_{1}^{\prime}=F_{2}^{\prime}=0
at
s=0,1
. This is reminiscent of the results of
[
41
]
, which find that an inactive flagellar endpiece enhances swimming movement.

The above observations are explored in more detail in Section
4.1
, and are further supplemented with numerical simulations in Section
4.2
. We implement a reformulated version of equation (

[

and Maxian et al.
[

, which we validate against a direct discretization of (

\kappa_{0}
, we consider combinations of

and

that give rise to non-swimmers, bad swimmers, and good swimmers, and create visualizations of their respective positions over time.

2.1.
Preliminaries

We begin by stating a few useful lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

(Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality)
.

For any function
u\in H^{1}(I)
, the following inequality holds for each
2\leq p\leq\infty
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{\frac{p+2}{2p}}\,.

If
u\in H^{1}_{0}(I)
, then we may replace
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}
in the above estimate with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}
.

In addition to Lemma
2.1
, we will need estimates for the semigroup generated by the operator
\mathcal{L}
(
13
). We first state some properties of the well-studied Dirichlet problem for the bilaplace equation in 1D
(see
[
23
,
61
,
62
]
). In particular, we have explicit expressions for the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}
. First, define an increasing sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}
satisfying

(29)

\cos(\xi_{k})\cosh(\xi_{k})=1\,,\qquad\xi_{0}=0\,.

The first few values of
\xi_{k}
are given by
\xi_{1}\approx 4.730
,
\xi_{2}\approx 7.853
,
\xi_{3}\approx 10.996
.
Note that since
\cosh(\xi)
increases exponentially as
\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert
increases, we have
\xi_{k}\to\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}
as
k\to\infty
. The eigenfunctions
\psi_{k}
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of
\mathcal{L}
are given by

(30)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{\psi}_{k}(s)}{\|\widehat{\psi}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}}\,,\qquad\lambda_{k}=\xi_{k}^{4}\,,\qquad k=1,2,\dots

\displaystyle\text{where }\widehat{\psi}_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\cos(\xi_{k})-\cosh(\xi_{k})\right)\left(\cos(\xi_{k}s)-\cosh(\xi_{k}s)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\sin(\xi_{k})+\sinh(\xi_{k})\right)\left(\sin(\xi_{k}s)-\sinh(\xi_{k}s)\right)\,.

We note in particular that the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
is given by
\lambda_{1}\approx(4.73)^{4}\approx 500
. The first six eigenfunctions
\psi_{k}
are plotted in Figure
1
. Note that from (

), for odd
k
,
\psi_{k}(s)
is even about

, while for even

\psi_{k}(s)

.

.

\mathcal{L}

Using (
30
), we may define the domain of
\mathcal{L}
by

(31)

D(\mathcal{L})=\bigg\{u\in L^{2}(I)\;:\;u=\sum_{k}a_{k}\psi_{k},\;a_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}u(s)\psi_{k}(s)ds;\;\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}a_{k}^{2}<\infty\bigg\}\,.

Furthermore, following
[
50
, Chapter 3.7]
, we may define the domain of fractional powers
\mathcal{L}^{r}
,
0\leq r\leq 1
, by

(32)

D(\mathcal{L}^{r})=\bigg\{u\in L^{2}(I)\;:\;u=\sum_{k}a_{k}\psi_{k},\;a_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}u(s)\psi_{k}(s)ds;\;\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2r}a_{k}^{2}<\infty\bigg\}\,.

Noting the quartic growth of
\lambda_{k}=\xi_{k}^{4}
as
k\to\infty
, we have the following inclusions:

(33)

D(\mathcal{L}^{r})\subseteq H^{4r}(I)\,,\quad 0\leq r\leq 1\,;\quad D(\mathcal{L}^{0})=L^{2}(I)\,.

We will repeatedly use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(Semigroup estimates)
.

For
0\leq m+j\leq 4
we have

(34)

\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\partial_{s}^{j}u)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{m}}\leq c\max\{t^{-(m+j)/4},1\}e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

where
\lambda_{1}=\xi_{1}^{4}
is the first eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}
(
30
).

Proof.

For
u\in L^{2}(I)
, write
u=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\psi_{k}(s)
where
a_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}u(s)\psi_{k}(s)\,ds
. For
0\leq r\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{L}^{r}e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{r}e^{-t\lambda_{k}}a_{k}\psi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sup_{\lambda_{k}}\bigg(\lambda_{k}^{r}e^{-t(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1})}\bigg)e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\psi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{r}\max\{t^{-r},1\}e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,.

Using (
33
), we then have

(35)

\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}}\leq c_{r}\max\{t^{-r},1\}e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,,\qquad 0\leq r\leq 1\,.

It remains to show the smoothing estimate, for which we use a duality argument.
For
u,w\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\partial_{s}^{j}u)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\big(w,e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\partial_{s}^{j}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\big(\partial_{s}^{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}}w,u\big)_{L^{2}}

since
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint. Using (
35
), we then have

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\partial_{s}^{j}u)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\sup_{\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1}\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{j}e^{-t\mathcal{L}}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq c_{j}\max\{t^{-j/4},1\}e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\,.

The same result holds for
u\in L^{2}(I)
by density.
∎

Finally, we will require the following lemma to show local existence for large initial data.

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix
u\in L^{2}(I)
and let
0<r\leq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
T_{\varepsilon}>0
which depends on
u
and satisfies

(36)

\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}]}\,\min\{t^{r},1\}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}(I)}\leq\varepsilon\,.

The smallness in Lemma
2.3
is achieved by approximating
u\in L^{2}(I)
by slightly smoother functions.

Proof.

Consider a sequence of functions
\phi_{j}\in D(\mathcal{L}^{r})
with
\phi_{j}\to u
in
L^{2}
. Since
\phi_{j}\in D(\mathcal{L}^{r})
, by (
32
) we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{L}^{r}e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{r}e^{-t\lambda_{k}}(\phi_{j},\psi_{k})\psi_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2r}(\phi_{j},\psi_{k})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq e^{-t\lambda_{1}}c_{j}

for some constant
0<c_{j}<\infty
. Then, using Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\min\{t^{r},1\}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{t^{r},1\}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}}+\min\{t^{r},1\}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(u-\phi_{j})\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{4r}}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{t^{r},1\}\,c_{j}+c\left\lVert u-\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,.

Taking
j
large enough that
c\left\lVert u-\phi_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and taking
t
small enough that
t^{r}\,c_{j}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, we obtain the bound (
36
).
∎

With Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
, and
2.3
, we may now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
: well-posedness for the unforced system (
10
)–(
12
). First, a remark:

Remark 2.4
.

To highlight the subtlety of the well-posedness theory for the system (
10
)–(
12
), we may consider the following reduced PDE system consisting of the most problematic terms of (
10
)–(
12
):

\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}=-\kappa_{ssss}+(\tau\kappa_{s})_{s}\,,\quad\tau_{ss}=\kappa_{s}^{2}\,.

We may rewrite this simplified system as a single equation:

\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}=-\kappa_{ssss}+\big(\partial_{ss}^{-1}(\kappa_{s}^{2})\kappa_{s}\big)_{s}\,,

where
\partial_{ss}^{-1}
is the inverse second derivative with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions at
s=0,1
. This equation is invariant under the same scaling symmetry as the full system:
\kappa(s,t)\mapsto\lambda\kappa(\lambda s,\lambda^{4}t)
. Again,
\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
is subcritical for this equation. However, to actually make sense of the tension
\tau
, we need
\kappa_{s}\in L^{2}
. Taking initial data
\kappa_{\rm in}\in L^{2}(I)
only, we expect that
\sup_{t}\,t^{1/4}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}<\infty
due to the smoothing effect of
\mathcal{L}
. Thus every
\kappa_{s}
which appears in the nonlinear term ‘costs’
t^{-1/4}
. From Lemma
2.2
, using the smoothing estimate on the semigroup to account for the outside derivative in the nonlinear term also costs
t^{-1/4}
. The nonlinearity then contains three additional powers of
\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
, costing a total of
t^{-3/4}
. Altogether, we must integrate
t^{-1}
in the Duhamel formula, so the full power of the smoothing of
\mathcal{L}
is required to close the contraction.

2.2.
Tension equation

We first consider the elliptic equation (
11
) and derive the following estimates for the tension
\tau
in terms of
\kappa
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
\kappa\in H^{1}(I)
, there exists a unique weak solution
\tau
to equation (
11
) satisfying

(37)

\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}\leq c\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}+1\right)\,.

Given
\kappa,\varphi\in H^{1}(I)
, define
\tau^{\kappa}
,
\tau^{\varphi}
to be the
H^{1}_{0}
solution to (
11
) using
\kappa
,
\varphi
respectively on the right hand side. The difference
\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi}
then satisfies

(38)

\displaystyle\|\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\bigg)

\displaystyle+c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big)\,.

Note that the right hand sides of both (
37
) and (
38
) involve two powers of an
\dot{H}^{1}
norm, and it is not clear that a lower power is possible. This factor of
\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}
presents an obstacle to showing global existence for large data, as demonstrated by Remark
2.4
.

Proof.

Given
\kappa\in H^{1}(I)
, for
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(I)
, we may define

(39)

\mathcal{B}(\tau,\phi):=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\tau_{s}\phi_{s}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{1+\gamma}\tau\phi\bigg)\,ds\,,

which, due to the sign of the potential term (note that
\kappa\in L^{\infty}(I)
) is always bounded and coercive on
H^{1}_{0}(I)
. We may define a weak solution of the tension equation as
\tau
satisfying

(40)

\mathcal{B}(\tau,\phi)=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\bigg(\kappa^{4}\phi+(\kappa_{s})^{2}\phi+(4+3\gamma)\kappa_{s}\kappa\phi_{s}\bigg)\,ds\quad\text{for all }\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(I)\,.

By the Lax–Milgram lemma, there exists a unique weak solution
\tau\in H^{1}_{0}(I)
, and, using Lemma
2.1
,
\tau
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\tau,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{3}\left\lVert\kappa\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\tau_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\kappa\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3/2}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg)\,.

Note that, in terms of regularity, the limiting factor here is the term
\kappa_{s}^{2}\tau
, which requires 2 powers of
\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}
to estimate, while the additional regularity of
\tau
is wasted.

Using Young’s inequality, we then have

(41)

\mathcal{B}(\tau,\tau)\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{4}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)\,,

and therefore, using that
\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq c\left\lVert\tau_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq c\mathcal{B}(\tau,\tau)
, we obtain the bound (
37
).

We next show the Lipschitz estimate (
38
) for
\tau
. Let
\tau^{\kappa}
,
\tau^{\varphi}
be as defined in Lemma
2.5
. The difference
\widehat{\tau}=\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi}
then satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau}_{ss}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{1+\gamma}\tau^{\kappa}-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{1+\gamma}\tau^{\varphi}+\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\bigg(\kappa^{4}-\varphi^{4}+(\kappa_{s})^{2}-(\varphi_{s})^{2}-\frac{4+3\gamma}{2}\big(\kappa^{2}-\varphi^{2}\big)_{ss}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1+\gamma)}\bigg((\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2})\widehat{\tau}+(\kappa-\varphi)(\kappa+\varphi)(\tau^{\kappa}+\tau^{\varphi})\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\bigg((\kappa-\varphi)(\kappa+\varphi)(\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2})+(\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s})(\kappa_{s}+\varphi_{s})-\frac{4+3\gamma}{2}\big((\kappa-\varphi)(\kappa+\varphi)\big)_{ss}\bigg)\,.

Upon multiplying by
\widehat{\tau}
and integrating by parts in
s
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\widehat{\tau}_{s}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2}}{2(1+\gamma)}\widehat{\tau}^{2}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa+\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\|\tau^{\kappa}+\tau^{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\|\widehat{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\kappa+\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\|(\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2})\widehat{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}+\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\|\widehat{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}+\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa+\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\|\widehat{\tau}_{s}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)\bigg)\|\widehat{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{1/2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2})\|(\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2})\widehat{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}})\|\widehat{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}\,.

Here we have used Lemma
2.1
as well as the bound (
37
). Using Young’s inequality, we then have

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\tau}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\widehat{\tau}_{s}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2}}{2(1+\gamma)}\widehat{\tau}^{2}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}})^{2}\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big)^{2}\,.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad+c\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big)\,,

from which we obtain the bound (
38
).
∎

2.3.
Evolution equation

We now consider the evolution equation (
10
) for the curvature
\kappa(s,t)
. To prove the existence of a unique mild solution (
15
) in both the large data and small data settings, we will rely on a contraction mapping argument that makes use of the semigroup estimates of Lemma
2.2
. We will also require Lemma
2.3
in the large data setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin by defining the function spaces
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{0}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(I))\,:\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}<\infty\big\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{1}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in C((0,T];\dot{H}^{1}(I))\,:\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}<\infty\big\}\,,

where the norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}
,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}
, respectively, are given by

\displaystyle\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,,\qquad\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}\,.

We will close a contraction mapping argument in
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{1}
. We consider the intersection of two closed balls in
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
:

\displaystyle B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})=\big\{u\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{1}\,:\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\leq M_{0},\;\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\leq M_{1}\big\}\,.

Given either sufficiently small
T
or sufficiently small initial data, we show that the mild solution formula (
15
) maps
B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
into itself for some choice of
M_{0}>0
and
0<M_{1}<1
.

For any
\varphi\in B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
, let
\Psi
denote the mapping

\displaystyle\Psi[\varphi]:=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathcal{R}[\varphi(s,t^{\prime})]\big)_{s}dt^{\prime}\,.

We start by estimating the nonlinear terms of (
15
). First, using (
37
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

(42)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}[\kappa(\cdot,t)]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{5/2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{3/2}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{1/2})+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)\,.

Here we note that the term

from (

\tau_{s}\kappa+\tau\kappa_{s}=(\tau\kappa)_{s}
and using the smoothing of the semigroup in Lemma

will still lead to the same result). In particular, since we only have the

bound (

, the regularity of
\tau

Using Lemma
2.2
and (
42

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathcal{R}[\kappa(\cdot,t^{\prime})]\big)_{s}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}[\kappa]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1)dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left(e^{-3t^{\prime}\lambda_{1}}\max\{(t^{\prime})^{-3/4},1\}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(e^{-t^{\prime}\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)\right)\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq ce^{-t\lambda_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}\max\{(t^{\prime})^{-3/4},1\}\,e^{-2t^{\prime}\lambda_{1}}\,dt^{\prime}\,\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq c\,\max\{t^{-1/4},1\}e^{-t\lambda_{1}}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)\right)\,.

By an analogous series of estimates, we also have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathcal{R}[\kappa(\cdot,t^{\prime})]\big)_{s}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq ce^{-t\lambda_{1}}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)\right)\,.

Then, using Lemma
2.2
, for
\varphi\in B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
we obtain the following
\mathcal{Y}_{0}

(43)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Psi[\varphi]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+c\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}+cM_{1}^{3}(M_{0}+1)\leq M_{0}\,,

where we have taken

and

small enough that

Furthermore, to obtain a
\mathcal{Y}_{1}

(44)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Psi[\varphi]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+c\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}\left(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+cM_{1}^{3}(M_{0}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\frac{M_{1}}{2}

for

sufficiently small. It remains to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{2}\,,

for which we have two options:

(1)

Small time:
Using Lemma
2.3
, we may choose a time

such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{M_{1}}]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{2}\,.

Note that
T_{M_{1}}
depends on
\kappa_{\rm in}
, particularly on how well
\kappa_{\rm in}\in L^{2}(I)
can be approximated by slightly smoother functions.

(2)

Small data:
Using Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq c\left\lVert\kappa_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,.

For

sufficiently small, we may take

We now show that
\Psi
is a contraction on
B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
. For
\kappa,\varphi\in H^{1}(I)
we first note that

(45)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}[\kappa(\cdot,t)]-\mathcal{R}[\varphi(\cdot,t)]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\left\lVert\kappa^{2}\kappa_{s}-\varphi^{2}\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\tau^{\kappa}_{s}\kappa-\tau^{\varphi}_{s}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\tau^{\kappa}\kappa_{s}-\tau^{\varphi}\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert(\kappa^{2}+\varphi^{2}-\varphi\kappa)(\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert(\kappa-\varphi)(\kappa\varphi_{s}+\varphi\kappa_{s})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\|(\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi})_{s}(\kappa+\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|(\tau^{\kappa}+\tau^{\varphi})_{s}(\kappa-\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi})(\kappa_{s}+\varphi_{s})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\tau^{\kappa}+\tau^{\varphi})(\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s})\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left\lVert\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle+\|(\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi})_{s}\|_{L^{2}}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})+(\|\tau^{\kappa}_{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tau^{\varphi}_{s}\|_{L^{2}})\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|\tau^{\kappa}-\tau^{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\left\lVert\kappa_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})+(\|\tau^{\kappa}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tau^{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}})\left\lVert\kappa_{s}-\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)\right)

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\right)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\right)\right)\bigg)\,.

Here we have used Lemma
2.1
in the third inequality, and in the fourth we used the estimates (
38
) and (
37
) to bound
\tau^{\kappa}
,
\tau^{\varphi}
in terms of
\kappa
,
\varphi
.

Using (
45
), for
\kappa,\varphi\in B_{M_{0}}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\cap B_{M_{1}}(\mathcal{Y}_{1})
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Psi[\kappa]-\Psi[\varphi]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\mathcal{R}[\kappa]-\mathcal{R}[\varphi]\big)_{s}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\,e^{t\lambda_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}\,e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\mathcal{R}[\kappa]-\mathcal{R}[\varphi]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}\max\{(t^{\prime})^{-3/4},1\}\,e^{-2t^{\prime}\lambda_{1}}\,dt^{\prime}\;\bigg(\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{3}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}^{2}+1)\big)+\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1)+\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}^{2}(\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+1)\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\,M_{1}^{3}(M_{0}^{2}+1)\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}+c\,M_{1}^{2}(M_{0}+1)\left\lVert\kappa-\varphi\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\,,

.
Furthermore, in the case of small data, combining the bounds (

Remark 2.6
.

Here we sketch a bootstrapping argument for showing that
\kappa
is in fact smooth for all positive times.
By a calculation analogous to that above equation (
43
), it can be shown that
\kappa(s,t)
in fact belongs to
H^{2}(I)
for any positive time. Using this additional regularity on the right hand side of the equation (
11
) for
\tau
, we may show that
\tau\in H^{2}(I)
as well.
Then, applying
\mathcal{L}^{1-\epsilon/4}
to the Duhamel formula (
15
) for
\kappa
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{1-\epsilon/4}\kappa(s,t)=\mathcal{L}^{1-\epsilon/4}e^{-(t-t_{0})\mathcal{L}}\kappa\big|_{t=t_{0}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathcal{L}^{1-\epsilon/4}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{R}[\kappa(s,t^{\prime})])_{s}\,dt^{\prime}\,,

since
(\mathcal{R}[\kappa(s,t^{\prime})])_{s}\in L^{2}(I)
(see (
14
)), we may use
Lemma
2.2
to obtain that
\kappa\in C([t_{0},T];H^{4-\epsilon})
for any
\epsilon,t_{0}>0
. Note that we can also show that
\kappa\in L^{2}([t_{0},T];H^{4}(I))
by applying the full operator
\mathcal{L}
above and estimating the integral term in
L^{2}
in time.

Passing to higher regularity becomes a bit tricky with the boundary conditions (
12
): if we were instead working on the torus, we could simply apply
\mathcal{L}^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, to the Duhamel formula, and use that
\mathcal{L}
commutes with the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
.
However, on the interval
I
, this requires
(\mathcal{R}[\kappa])_{s}\in D(\mathcal{L})
, which is not necessarily the case since, e.g.,
\tau_{s}\kappa_{s}
does not satisfy the boundary conditions (
12
).

Instead, we can use that
\dot{\kappa}\in L^{2}([t_{0},T];L^{2}(I))
since the right hand side of the
\dot{\kappa}
equation (
10
) now belongs to
L^{2}([t_{0},T];L^{2}(I))
. Taking a time derivative of the equation (
10
) for
\kappa
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\dot{\kappa})

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}\dot{\kappa}+(2+\gamma)(3\kappa^{2}\dot{\kappa})_{ss}+(\tau\dot{\kappa})_{ss}+(2+\gamma)(\dot{\tau}\kappa)_{ss}+(1+\gamma)(\tau_{s}\dot{\kappa}-\dot{\tau}\kappa_{s})_{s}\,.

Here
\dot{\tau}
satisfies the elliptic equation

\displaystyle\dot{\tau}_{ss}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{1+\gamma}\dot{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\bigg(2\kappa\dot{\kappa}\tau+4\kappa^{3}\dot{\kappa}+2((\kappa_{s}\dot{\kappa})_{s}-\kappa_{ss}\dot{\kappa})-(4+3\gamma)(\dot{\kappa}_{s}\kappa+\kappa_{s}\dot{\kappa})_{s}\bigg)\,,

and we have that at each time
t>t_{0}
,
\left\lVert\dot{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}\leq c\big(\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{H^{2}}^{3}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big)
.
Using the Duhamel formula for
\dot{\kappa}
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}

\displaystyle=e^{-(t-t_{0})\mathcal{L}}\dot{\kappa}\big|_{t=t_{0}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\bigg((\mathcal{R}_{1}[\kappa(s,t^{\prime})])_{ss}+(\mathcal{R}_{2}[\kappa(s,t^{\prime})])_{s}\bigg)\,dt^{\prime}\,,

where
\mathcal{R}_{1}=(2+\gamma)3\kappa^{2}\dot{\kappa}+\tau\dot{\kappa}+(2+\gamma)\dot{\tau}\kappa
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}=(1+\gamma)(\tau_{s}\dot{\kappa}-\dot{\tau}\kappa_{s})
. Since we need
\dot{\kappa}\in H^{1}(I)
to make sense of
\dot{\tau}
, we can again obtain estimates for
\dot{\kappa}
in a time-weighted space analogous to
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{1}
. In particular, we can estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\bigg(\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}+\min\{(t-t_{0})^{1/4},1\}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}\bigg)

in a similar way as in estimates (
43
) and (
44
), and the above argument begins to repeat. Repeating this, we can show that
\partial_{t}^{k}\kappa\in L^{2}([kt_{0},T];H^{4}(I))
. To obtain spatial regularity from the time regularity, we may use that, once
\dot{\kappa}\in L^{2}([2t_{0},T];H^{4}(I))
, we have
\mathcal{L}\dot{\kappa}\in L^{2}([2t_{0},T];L^{2}(I))
, and, using the equation (
10
) for
\dot{\kappa}
,
\mathcal{L}(-\mathcal{L}\kappa+(2+\gamma)(\kappa^{3})_{ss}+(2+\gamma)(\tau_{s}\kappa)_{s}+(\tau\kappa_{s})_{s})\in L^{2}([2t_{0},T];L^{2}(I))
, i.e.
\kappa\in L^{2}([2t_{0},T];H^{8}(I))
.

3.
Periodic active forcing and swimming

We now consider the system (
7
)–(
9
) with nonzero
T
-periodic active forcing
\kappa_{0}(s,t)
along the filament, corresponding to a nonzero preferred curvature, as derived in
[
2
,
53
]
.
In particular, at a fixed time
t
, let
\kappa_{0}(s)
denote the preferred curvature of the fiber. For a planar fiber, recalling that
\bm{X}_{ss}=\kappa(s)\bm{e}_{\rm n}(s)
and
(\bm{e}_{\rm n})_{s}=-\kappa(s)\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s)
, we may compute the elastic energy of the fiber as

(46)

\displaystyle G[\bm{X}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\big(\kappa(s)-\kappa_{0}(s)\big)^{2}+\lambda(s)(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}-1)\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\right\rvert^{2}+\lambda(\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}-1)\bigg)\,ds\,,

along with the boundary conditions
(\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n})\big|_{s=0,1}=0
,
(\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n})_{s}\big|_{s=0,1}=0
,
\lambda|_{s=0,1}=0
. The Lagrange multiplier
\lambda(s)
is equal to the tension
\tau
when
\kappa_{0}\equiv 0
. Taking the variation and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}G[\bm{X}+\varepsilon\bm{Y}]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}{\bm{Y}}_{s}\cdot\bigg(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}-\kappa_{0}(\bm{e}_{\rm n})_{s}-\lambda\bm{X}_{s}\bigg)\,ds\,.

Defining the tension of the fiber to be
\tau=\lambda-\kappa\kappa_{0}
, we obtain the form of forcing appearing on the right hand side of (
2
).

3.1.
Existence of solution with force

Given the complicated way that the forcing terms due to
\kappa_{0}
enter the system (
7
)–(
9
), we will first need to establish existence and uniqueness of solutions for
t\in[0,T]
for sufficiently small
\kappa_{0}
. In particular, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Well-posedness with forcing)
.

There exists a constant
0<M<1
such that, given a preferred curvature
\kappa_{0}(s,t)\in C^{1}([0,T];H^{1}(I))
and an initial condition
\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}\in L^{2}(I)
satisfying

\displaystyle cT\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\bigg)=c_{1}M\,,\quad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=c_{2}M\,,\qquad\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=c_{3}M\,,

for
c_{1}>0
,
c_{2}>0
,
c_{3}\geq 0
, and
c_{1}+c_{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
there exists a unique mild solution
\overline{\kappa}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(I))\cap C((0,T];H^{1}(I))
to the forced system (
7
)–(
9
) satisfying

(47)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\bigg(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}+\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}(I)}\bigg)\leq cM\,.

Proof.

\displaystyle+(1+\gamma)\big(\overline{\kappa}(\overline{\kappa}+2\kappa_{0})\big)_{s}\phi_{s}+(2+\gamma)\overline{\kappa}_{s}(\overline{\kappa}+\kappa_{0})\phi_{s}\bigg)\,ds\qquad\forall\,\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(I)\,.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)\left\lVert\overline{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg)\,.

\left\lVert\overline{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq c\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\big)\,.

We also need to amend the Lipschitz estimate of Lemma

to account for the presence of the forcing terms. As before, given
\overline{\kappa}
,
\overline{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I)

\displaystyle\widehat{\tau}_{ss}-\frac{(\overline{\kappa}+\kappa_{0})^{2}+(\overline{\varphi}+\kappa_{0})^{2}}{2(1+\gamma)}\widehat{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}(\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi})(\overline{\kappa}+\overline{\varphi}+2\kappa_{0})(\overline{\tau}^{\kappa}+\overline{\tau}^{\varphi})

\displaystyle\qquad+(\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi})\big(\overline{\kappa}^{3}+\overline{\varphi}^{3}+(\overline{\kappa}\overline{\varphi}+5\kappa_{0}^{2})(\overline{\kappa}+\overline{\varphi})+4\kappa_{0}(\overline{\kappa}^{2}+\overline{\kappa}\overline{\varphi}+\overline{\varphi}^{2})+2\kappa_{0}^{3}\big)

\displaystyle+c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)\,.

Equipped with estimates (
49
) and (
50
), we now consider the forced evolution equation (

Here

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}]

\displaystyle=3(2+\gamma)\overline{\kappa}(\overline{\kappa}+2\kappa_{0})\overline{\kappa}_{s}+(5+3\gamma)\kappa_{0}^{2}\overline{\kappa}_{s}+(5+2\gamma)\overline{\kappa}^{2}(\kappa_{0})_{s}

\displaystyle B_{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{T})=\{u\in\mathcal{Y}_{T}\,:\,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\leq M\}\,,

we have that
\overline{\Psi}
is bounded from

to
B_{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{T})
for some

. In particular, by Lemma
2.2
, the second term of
\overline{\Psi}[\overline{\kappa}]
involving
\dot{\kappa}_{0}
satisfies

(53)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\,\dot{\kappa}_{0}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{m}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-m/4},1\}\,e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\max\{t^{1-m/4},1\}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\bigg)\,.

Furthermore, we may bound the nonlinear terms from
(\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}])_{s}
following similar steps to the unforced estimate (
42
) for
\mathcal{R}[\kappa]
. Using Lemma (
2.1
) and the tension estimate (
49
), we have

(54)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}(\cdot,t)]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert\overline{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\big)\,.

By Lemma
2.2
, we then have

(55)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}(\cdot,t^{\prime})]\big)_{s}dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{m}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-(m+1)/4},1\}\,e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-(m+1)/4},1\}\,e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\big)\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{3}\big)\left(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\max\{t^{-m/4},1\}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{3}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{3}\big)\left(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+1\right)\,.

Combining the estimates (
53
) and (
55
) for
\dot{\kappa}_{0}
and

, we then have

(56)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\overline{\Psi}[\overline{\kappa}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+cT\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\dot{\kappa}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg)+c\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{3}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{3}\big)\left(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}+1\right)

for

, and

\left\lVert\overline{\Psi}[\overline{\kappa}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\leq\left(c_{1}+c_{3}+c(M^{3}+M^{2})\right)M\leq M

for

is a contraction on

\overline{\kappa}
,
\overline{\varphi}\in B_{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{T})
, we first amend the unforced Lipschitz estimate (

) for

\displaystyle\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}(\cdot,t)]-\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\varphi}(\cdot,t)]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)+\left\lVert\overline{\tau}^{\kappa}-\overline{\tau}^{\varphi}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\big)+\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\left\lVert\overline{\tau}^{\varphi}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\bigg(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{3}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\big(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)\big(\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+1\big)\bigg)\,.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}]-\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\varphi}]\right)_{s}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/4},1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}\bigg)e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}]-\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\lambda_{1}}\bigg(\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/4},1\}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\max\{(t-t^{\prime})^{-1/2},1\}\bigg)\max\{(t^{\prime})^{-3/4},1\}\,dt^{\prime}\,\bigg(\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{5}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{5}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{5}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}+\left\lVert\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}\bigg)\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\bigg(M^{5}+M^{2}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{5}+\left\lVert\kappa_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}^{2}\bigg)\,.

Using the bound (
57
) on
\kappa_{0}
, we therefore have

(59)

\left\lVert\overline{\Psi}[\overline{\kappa}]-\overline{\Psi}[\overline{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\leq c(M^{5}+M^{2})\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\,.

Taking

small enough that

, we obtain a contraction on

.

\displaystyle\left\lVert u(\cdot,T)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\,.

Taking
\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}\in B_{M^{\prime}}(L^{2}(I))

) of

, the estimate (

Furthermore, by Lemma

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{-T\mathcal{L}}\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi^{T}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\leq M^{\prime}\,.

Next we need a Lipschitz estimate for
\Phi^{T}
. Given two initial conditions
\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}
,
\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\in L^{2}(I)
, we begin by noting that, by Lemma

, the difference in the linear evolution alone satisfies

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\,e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}e^{t\lambda_{1}}\bigg(\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\min\{t^{1/4},1\}\left\lVert e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in})\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,.

Furthermore, using the estimate (

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\mathcal{L}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\kappa}]-\overline{\mathcal{R}}[\overline{\varphi}]\big)_{s}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\big(\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi}\big)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\,.

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\big(\Phi^{t}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]-\Phi^{t}[\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}]\big)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}(\overline{\kappa}-\overline{\varphi})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\,.

Noting that
\Phi^{t}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}](s)=\overline{\kappa}(s,t)
, we may subtract

from both sides to obtain

(62)

\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\big(\Phi^{t}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]-\Phi^{t}[\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}]\big)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\leq c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,.

In particular, at time

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{T\lambda_{1}}\big(\Phi^{T}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]-\Phi^{T}[\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}]\big)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert e^{t\lambda_{1}}\big(\Phi^{t}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]-\Phi^{t}[\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}]\big)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{T}}\leq c\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\,,

which we may rewrite as

(63)

\left\lVert\Phi^{T}[\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}]-\Phi^{T}[\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\leq ce^{-T\lambda_{1}}\left\lVert\overline{\kappa}_{\rm in}-\overline{\varphi}_{\rm in}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}\,.

Taking the period

of the preferred curvature
\kappa_{0}(s,t)
to be large enough that

As a slight abuse of notation, we now let

The estimate (

Furthermore, the bound (

4.
Observations and numerics

In this section, we begin by exploring the observations
1.5
about the swimming speed predicted by the expression (
27
). We proceed to perform a small numerical optimization of the swimming speed subject to a fixed amount of work performed by the filament. Finally, we outline a numerical method for a reformulation of equation (
2
) and verify the observations numerically.

4.1.
Observations

We first consider the form of the eigenfunctions
\psi_{k}
of
\mathcal{L}
(see (
30
)). Since
\psi_{2k}(s)
is odd and
\psi_{2k-1}(s)
is even about
s=\frac{1}{2}
for each
k=1,2,3,\dots
, we have that eigenfunctions with the same parity do not contribute to net motion:

(75)

\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{2k}(\psi_{2\ell})_{s}\,ds=0\,,\quad\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{2k-1}(\psi_{2\ell-1})_{s}\,ds=0\,,\qquad k,\ell=1,2,3,\dots\,.

If
\kappa_{0}(s,t)
is always odd about
s=\frac{1}{2}
, we may write

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(a_{m,2k}\cos(\omega m\,t)+b_{m,2k}\sin(\omega m\,t)\big)\psi_{2k}(s)\,.

Likewise, if
\kappa_{0}(s,t)
is always even about
s=\frac{1}{2}
, we may write

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(a_{m,2k-1}\cos(\omega m\,t)+b_{m,2k-1}\sin(\omega m\,t)\big)\psi_{2k-1}(s)\,.

In either of the above cases, due to (
75
), we have that the leading order term of the swimming expression (
27
) satisfies

\displaystyle-\gamma\int_{0}^{1}\langle(\kappa_{0})_{s}\overline{\kappa}^{\rm lin}\rangle\,ds=0\,.

Furthermore, writing

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}A_{m}(s)\cos(\omega m\,t)+B_{m}(s)\sin(\omega m\,t)\,,

we see that if
A_{m}=\pm B_{m}
or either
A_{m}=0
or
B_{m}=0
for all
m
, then the first term
b_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}-a_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}
in the swimming expression (
27
) vanishes. Recalling that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}=\xi_{k}^{4}
of
\mathcal{L}
satisfy
\xi_{k}\to\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}
as
k\to\infty
and that the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}\approx(4.73)^{4}\approx 500
(see (
30
)), we note that this initial term has the greatest relative contribution to filament propulsion. (The remaining terms
a_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}+b_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}
become relatively more important for large
m
, but the optimimzation results below prompt us to consider only
m
small).
In particular, if this initial term vanishes, the fiber may still have nonzero net motion over the course of one period, but we expect its displacement to be very small.

We may also consider optimizing the swimming speed (
27
) given certain constraints on the filament deformation, such as a fixed amount of work
[
51
,
25
]
. We begin by calculating the average work done by the swimmer over one period. Starting with the formulation (
2
), we have

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}\cdot\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)_{s}\bigg\rangle\,ds=-\int_{0}^{1}\bigg\langle\frac{\partial\bm{X}_{s}}{\partial t}\cdot\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)\bigg\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\langle\dot{\theta}(\kappa-\kappa_{0})_{s}\rangle\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\dot{\kappa}\overline{\kappa}\rangle\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\dot{\kappa}_{0}\overline{\kappa}\rangle\,ds\,.

Here we have used that
\overline{\kappa}=\kappa-\kappa_{0}
is
T
-periodic. Using Lemma
3.3
, we have that

\displaystyle|W-W^{\rm lin}|\leq c\varepsilon^{3}\,,

where, using the expansion (
73
) and the form of the coefficients (
74
), we may write

(76)

\displaystyle W^{\rm lin}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\dot{\kappa}_{0}\overline{\kappa}^{\rm lin}\rangle\,ds\,dt=\sum_{m,k,\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega m}{2}\bigg(b_{m,\ell}c_{m,k}-a_{m,\ell}d_{m,k}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{k}\psi_{\ell}\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}\frac{\omega^{2}m^{2}}{\omega^{2}m^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\big(a_{m,k}^{2}+b_{m,k}^{2}\big)\,.

Here we seek to numerically optimize the leading order swimming speed (

) over a (small) finite number of spatial and temporal modes
a_{m,k}
,
b_{m,k}
, subject to a fixed amount of work (
76
). For a given

,
k_{\max}
, we aim to construct the preferred curvature

\displaystyle\kappa_{0}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{m_{\max}}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\max}}\big(a_{m,k}\cos(\omega m\,t)-b_{m,k}\sin(\omega m\,t)\big)\psi_{k}(s)

which results in the fastest swimming speed.
Note that we do not necessarily expect the true optimal preferred curvature to be representable by a finite number of spatial modes

\psi_{k}
of
\mathcal{L}
. We instead aim to visualize general trends in the emerging optimal

We define the finite-mode swimming speed and work

\displaystyle U_{m_{\max},k_{\max}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{m_{\max}}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{k_{\max}}\frac{\omega^{2}m^{2}}{\omega^{2}m^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\omega m}\big(a_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}-b_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}\big)

(77)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+a_{m,k}a_{m,\ell}+b_{m,k}b_{m,\ell}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{k}(\psi_{\ell})_{s}\,ds

(78)

\displaystyle W_{m_{\max},k_{\max}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m=1}^{m_{\max}}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\max}}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}\frac{\omega^{2}m^{2}}{\omega^{2}m^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}}\big(a_{m,k}^{2}+b_{m,k}^{2}\big)

and solve the following constrained optimization problem for the coefficients

,

:

\displaystyle\min_{a_{m,k},b_{m,k}}

\displaystyle U_{m_{\max},k_{\max}}

\displaystyle\text{subject to }

\displaystyle W_{m_{\max},k_{\max}}={\rm const.}

\bm{e}_{\rm t}(0,0)=\bm{e}_{x}
, i.e. leftward.

4.2.
Numerical method

Here we numerically verify the swimming predictions of the previous section. We develop a simple numerical method for simulating the motion of an inextensible filament via resistive force theory based on a combination of the methods of
[
32
]
and
[
35
]
. We apply the method to the planar swimmer studied in the previous sections, but note that the formulation itself is applicable more generally.

While the curvature evolution equations (
7
)-(
9
) in terms of
\kappa
and
\tau
are convenient for analysis, a direct numerical implementation can be cumbersome due in part to complicated dependence on the forcing
\kappa_{0}
as well as the need to solve for the actual fiber movement separately (
22
). Numerically, however, we can make use of reformulations that may simplify the computation. As noted, our future goal is to do numerical analysis comparing the many existing formulations of filament inextensibility.

To introduce the numerical method, we return to the original formulation of inextensible fiber dynamics (
2
), which we reiterate here for convenience:

(81)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}(s,t)

\displaystyle=-\big({\bf I}+\gamma\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{s}^{\rm T}\big)\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)_{s}

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle(\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n})\big|_{s=0,1}

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n})\big|_{s=0,1}=0\,.

We enforce inextensibility directly by parameterizing
\bm{X}(s,t)
as

(82)

\bm{X}(s,t)=\bm{X}_{0}(t)+\int_{0}^{s}\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime},t)ds^{\prime}\,,

where
\bm{e}_{\rm t}\in S^{2}
; in particular,
\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}\cdot\bm{e}_{\rm t}=0
.

Along the fiber we may rewrite (
81
) as the coupled system

(83)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-({\bf I}+\gamma\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bm{h}(s)

(84)

\displaystyle\big({\bf I}-\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s)\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s)^{\rm T}\big)\int_{0}^{s}\bm{h}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\big({\bf I}-\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s)\bm{e}_{\rm t}(s)^{\rm T}\big)\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)\,,

where
\bm{h}(s)
is the hydrodynamic force density along the filament, and only the normal components of the integrated force density (
84
) are specified. To satisfy the boundary conditions of (
81
), we must also ensure that the total hydrodynamic force and torque along the fiber vanish:

(85)

\int_{0}^{1}\bm{h}(s)\,ds=0,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}(\bm{X}(s)-\bm{X}(0))\times\bm{h}(s)\,ds=0\,.

The formulation (
83
)-(
84
), combined with total force vanishing (
85
), is equivalent to the formulation in
[
32
]
, which uses slender body theory rather than resistive force theory in equation (
83
). As noted in
[
32
]
, equations (
83
)-(
84
) along with (
85
) are enough to recover the rate of work exerted by the filament on the fluid in (
81
). In particular, the rate of work may be calculated using (
83
)-(
84
) as

\displaystyle\dot{W}=\int_{0}^{1}\bm{h}(s)\cdot\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\bm{h}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}\bigg)\cdot\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n})\cdot\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s)\,ds\,,

where we have integrated by parts and used that
\bm{e}_{\rm t}\cdot\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}=0
. Starting instead from (
81
) and again integrating by parts and using
\bm{X}_{s}=\bm{e}_{\rm t}\perp\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}
, we also obtain

\displaystyle\dot{W}=\int_{0}^{1}\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)_{s}\cdot\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}\,ds=-\int_{0}^{1}\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)\cdot\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}\,ds\,.

Since we are using resistive force theory, equation (
83
) may be directly inverted to solve for
\bm{h}
:

(86)

\bm{h}=-({\bf I}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\bigg)\,,

and this expression may be inserted into (
84
) to condense (
83
) and (
84
) into a single equation along the filament. For a planar fiber, recalling that we may parameterize
\bm{e}_{\rm t}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\rm T}
,
\bm{e}_{\rm n}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^{\rm T}
, and that
\bm{X}_{sss}=-(\theta_{s})^{2}\bm{e}_{\rm t}+\theta_{ss}\bm{e}_{\rm n}
, we may rewrite (
83
) and (
84
) as

(87)

\bm{e}_{\rm n}(s,t)\cdot\int_{0}^{s}({\bf I}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s^{\prime}}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(\overline{s})\,d\overline{s}\bigg)ds^{\prime}=-\theta_{ss}+(\kappa_{0})_{s}\,.

Equation (
87
) will be the main evolution equation for the fiber, for which the unknowns are the basepoint
\bm{X}_{0}
and the tangent angle
\theta(s)
. We combine (
87
) with the total force balance (
85
) to enforce the boundary condition
(-\theta_{ss}+(\kappa_{0})_{s})\big|_{s=1}=0
:

(88)

\int_{0}^{1}({\bf I}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\bigg)\,ds=0.

The torque condition in (
85
) leads to the boundary condition
(\theta_{s}-\kappa_{0})\big|_{s=0,1}=0
, which will be enforced directly in the discretization of
\theta_{ss}
on the right hand side of (
87
).

The fiber is discretized into
N
segments between
N+1
points
\bm{X}_{i}
along the fiber, and
\theta_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, is taken to be the angle between segment
i
and the
x
-axis (see Figure
3
). The equation (
87
) is enforced at the midpoint of each segment at
\bm{X}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{\bm{X}_{i-1}+\bm{X}_{i}}{2}
,
i=1,\dots,N
.

Figure 3
.

Filament discretization used to implement (
90
)–(
93
).

As mentioned, the boundary condition
(\theta_{s}-\kappa_{0})\big|_{s=0,1}=0
is enforced in the discretization of
\theta_{ss}
on the right hand side of (
87
) via the approximation

(89)

\theta_{ss}\big|_{s=0}\approx N^{2}(2\theta_{2}-2\theta_{1})-2N\kappa_{0,1}\,,\qquad\theta_{ss}\big|_{s=1}\approx N^{2}(2\theta_{N-1}-2\theta_{N})+2N\kappa_{0,N}\,.

At the
s=1
endpoint, equations (
87
) and (
88
) coincide to yield the boundary condition
\theta_{ss}\big|_{s=1}=(\kappa_{0})_{s}\big|_{s=1}
, which, using (
89
), yields an equation for
\theta_{N}
:

(90)

\theta_{N}=\theta_{N-1}+\frac{1}{N}\kappa_{0,N}-\frac{1}{2N^{2}}(\kappa_{0})_{s,N}\,.

The full discretized system is then given by

(91)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\theta_{j}\\
\cos\theta_{j}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{j}\bm{M}_{\rm RFT}(\theta_{i})\dot{\bm{X}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=-N^{2}(\theta_{j-1}-2\theta_{j}+\theta_{j+1})+(\kappa_{0})_{s,j}\,,\quad j=2,\dots,N-1

(92)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\theta_{1}\\
\cos\theta_{1}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\bm{M}_{\rm RFT}(\theta_{1})\dot{\bm{X}}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=-N^{2}(2\theta_{2}-2\theta_{1})+2N\kappa_{0,1}

(93)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\bm{M}_{\rm RFT}(\theta_{i})\dot{\bm{X}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=0\,,

along with equation (
90
).
Here the evolution
\dot{\bm{X}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
of each segment midpoint is parameterized as

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{X}}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\dot{x}_{0}\\
\dot{y}_{0}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{2N}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\theta_{i}\\
\cos\theta_{i}\end{pmatrix}\dot{\theta}_{i}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\theta_{k}\\
\cos\theta_{k}\end{pmatrix}\dot{\theta}_{k}\,,\quad i=1,\dots,N\,,

and the
2N\times 2N
matrix
\bm{M}_{\rm RFT}(\theta_{i})
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{\rm RFT}(\theta_{i})=\begin{pmatrix}1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\cos^{2}\theta_{i}&-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\cos\theta_{i}\sin\theta_{i}\\
-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\cos\theta_{i}\sin\theta_{i}&1-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}\end{pmatrix}\,.

The expressions (
91
) and (
92
) include
N-1
equations enforced at
\bm{X}_{\frac{1}{2}},\dots,\bm{X}_{N-\frac{3}{2}}
; the expression (
93
) involves two equations enforced at
\bm{X}_{N-\frac{1}{2}}
; and the equation (
90
) gives one additional equation to uniquely determine the
N+2
unknowns
x_{0}
,
y_{0}
,
\theta_{1}
, …,
\theta_{N}
. We use a standard ODE solver to evolve (
90
)–(
93
) in time.

The formulation (
90
)–(
93
) is very similar to that of Moreau et al.
[
35
]
, which also avoids the need to solve for the unknown tension by projecting away from the tangential direction along the fiber. At the continuous level, the formulation in
[
35
]
is essentially based on the identity

(94)

\bm{X}_{s}\times(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau(s)\bm{X}_{s}-(\kappa_{0})_{s}\bm{e}_{\rm n})=\big(\bm{X}_{s}\times(\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n})\big)_{s}\,.

Integrating (
94
) with respect to arclength from
s
to 1 (
0\leq s\leq 1
) and using the boundary conditions in (
81
), the identity (
94
) yields

(95)

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{s}(s)\times(\bm{X}_{ss}(s)-\kappa_{0}(s)\bm{e}_{\rm n})

\displaystyle=-\int_{s}^{1}\bm{X}_{s^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})\times\big(\bm{X}_{sss}(s^{\prime})-\tau(s^{\prime})\bm{X}_{s}(s^{\prime})-\kappa_{0}(s^{\prime})\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{1}\big(\bm{X}(s^{\prime})-\bm{X}(s)\big)\times\big(\bm{X}_{sss}-\tau\bm{X}_{s}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\big)_{s^{\prime}}\,ds^{\prime}\,.

In the case of a planar filament parameterized by the tangent angle
\theta
, we have
\bm{X}_{s}\times\bm{X}_{ss}=\theta_{s}\bm{e}_{z}
. Combining (
95
) with the definition of
\bm{h}
(
86
), we obtain (in the
\bm{e}_{z}
direction):

(96)

\displaystyle\theta_{s}(s)-\kappa_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{1}\big(\bm{X}(s^{\prime})-\bm{X}(s)\big)\times\bm{h}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\int_{s}^{1}\big(\bm{X}(s^{\prime})-\bm{X}(s)\big)\times\bigg(({\bf I}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s^{\prime}}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(\overline{s})\,d\overline{s}\bigg)\bigg)\,ds^{\prime}\,.

The equation (
96
) is also enforced at each of the
N
midpoints
\bm{X}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
along the discretized fiber (see Figure
3
). To close the discretized system, equation (
96
) is accompanied by the total force constraint

(97)

\int_{0}^{1}({\bf I}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bm{e}_{\rm t}\bm{e}_{\rm t}^{\rm T})\bigg(\dot{\bm{X}}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}\dot{\bm{e}}_{\rm t}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\bigg)\,ds=0\,,

which gives two additional equations.

In Appendix
A
, we validate the method (
90
)–(
93
) against a direct discretization of the classical expression (
81
). A more thorough comparison of different numerical implementations of fiber inextensibility is the intended subject of future work.

clearly integrates to a positive number in space and time, leading to net motion (note that the displacement is negative according to the sign of (
26
)).

Appendix A
Validation of numerical method

We validate the numerical method for the formulation described in Section
4.2
through comparison with a direct discretization of the classical inextensible fiber formulation (
81
).

The implementation of (
81
) requires description. We first rewrite the system (
81
) as follows:

(98)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}(s,t)

\displaystyle=-\big({\bf I}+\gamma\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{s}^{\rm T}\big)\bm{F}_{s}\,,\;\bm{F}=\bm{Q}_{s}-\widehat{\tau}\bm{X}_{s}\,,\;\bm{Q}=\bm{X}_{ss}-\kappa_{0}\bm{e}_{\rm n}\,,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=1\,,

\displaystyle\bm{Q}\big|_{s=0,1}

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\bm{F}\big|_{s=0,1}=0\,.

Our spatial discretization is as in Figure
3
, and we denote the arclength coordinate by
s_{i}=i\triangle s,i=0,\dots,N
where
\triangle s=1/N
.
Let us also define the midpoints
s_{i+1/2}=(i+1/2)\triangle s,\;i=0,\dots,N-1
. Let
\bm{X}_{i}
be the discretization of
\bm{X}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}

be the discretization of
\widehat{\tau}
. As the notation suggests,
\bm{X}_{i}
are seen as residing at
s_{i}
whereas
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}
reside
at the midpoints
s_{i+1/2}
. Our
3N+2
unknowns are
\bm{X}_{i}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}
. First, set:

(99)

\begin{split}\bm{Q}_{i}&=\frac{1}{(\triangle s)^{2}}\left(\bm{X}_{i+1}-2\bm{X}_{i}+\bm{X}_{i-1}\right)-\kappa_{0,i}\bm{e}_{{\rm n},i},\quad i=1,\dots,N-1,\\
\bm{e}_{{\rm n},i}&=\mathcal{R}_{\pi/2}\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i},\;\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i}=\frac{\bm{X}_{i+1}-\bm{X}_{i-1}}{\left\lvert\bm{X}_{i+1}-\bm{X}_{i-1}\right\rvert},\;\mathcal{R}_{\pi/2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad i=1,\dots,N-1,\\
\bm{Q}_{0}&=\bm{Q}_{N}=0.\end{split}

Using the above expressions, define:

\bm{F}_{i+1/2}=\frac{1}{\triangle s}\left(\bm{Q}_{i+1}-\bm{Q}_{i}\right)-\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}\frac{\bm{X}_{i+1}-\bm{X}_{i}}{\triangle s},\;i=0,\dots,N-1.

We thus obtain the following semi-discretized system:

(100)

\begin{split}\frac{d\bm{X}_{i}}{dt}&=-\frac{1}{\triangle s}\left({\bf I}+\gamma\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i}\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i}^{\rm T}\right)(\bm{F}_{i+1/2}-\bm{F}_{i-1/2})\text{ for }i=1,\dots,N-1,\\
\bm{F}_{1/2}&=\bm{F}_{N-1/2}=0,\\
\frac{\left\lvert\bm{X}_{i+1}-\bm{X}_{i}\right\rvert^{2}}{(\triangle s)^{2}}&=1,\;i=0,\dots,N-1.\end{split}

Notice that this is a differential algebraic system for
\bm{X}_{i}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}
, and there are exactly
3N+2
equations matching
the number of unknown functions. We discretize the above equation in time as follows. We let
\triangle t
be the time step. Let
\bm{X}_{i}^{n}

and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}^{n}
denote the values of
\bm{X}_{i}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}
evaluated at time
n\triangle t,n=0,1,2,\dots\,
.
Our time discretization is essentially a backward Euler method
in which some parts of the equation are treated explicitly. Given
\bm{X}_{i}^{n-1}
, we solve the following system of equations to find
\bm{X}_{i}^{n}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}^{n}
:

(101)

\begin{split}\frac{\bm{X}_{i}^{n}-\bm{X}_{i}^{n-1}}{\triangle s}&=-\frac{1}{\triangle s}\left({\bf I}+\gamma\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i}^{n-1}\bm{e}_{{\rm t},i}^{n-1,\rm T}\right)(\bm{F}_{i+1/2}^{n}-\bm{F}_{i-1/2}^{n})\,,\;i=1,\dots N-1\,,\\
\bm{F}_{1/2}^{n}&=\bm{F}_{N-1/2}^{n}=0\,,\\
\bm{F}_{i+1/2}^{n}&=\frac{1}{\triangle s}\left(\bm{Q}_{i+1}^{n}-\bm{Q}_{i}^{n}\right)-\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}^{n}\frac{\bm{X}_{i+1}^{n}-\bm{X}_{i}^{n}}{\triangle s}\,,\;i=0,\dots,N-1\,,\\
\bm{Q}_{i}^{n}&=\frac{1}{(\triangle s)^{2}}\left(\bm{X}^{n}_{i+1}-2\bm{X}_{i}^{n}+\bm{X}_{i-1}^{n}\right)-\kappa_{0,i}^{n}\bm{e}_{{\rm n},i}^{n-1}\,,\quad i=1,\dots,N-1\,,\\
\bm{Q}_{0}^{n}&=\bm{Q}_{N}^{n}=0\,,\\
\frac{\left\lvert\bm{X}_{i+1}^{n}-\bm{X}_{i}^{n}\right\rvert^{2}}{(\triangle s)^{2}}&=1,\;i=0,\dots,N-1\,.\end{split}

In the above,
\bm{e}_{\rm t,i}^{n-1}
and
\bm{e}_{{\rm n},i}^{n-1}
are computed with
\bm{X}_{i}^{n-1}
using the second line of equation (
99
).
Note also that
\kappa_{0}
and hence
\kappa_{0,i}^{n}
are known quantities.
The above constitute
3N+2
equations in the unknowns
\bm{X}_{i}^{n}
and
\widehat{\tau}_{i+1/2}^{n}
. These equations are solved using Newton’s method.

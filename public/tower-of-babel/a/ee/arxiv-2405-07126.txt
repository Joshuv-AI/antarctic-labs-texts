Abstract
. We determine when the irreducible modules L ⁡ ( c p , q , h m , n ) L(c_{p,q},h_{m,n}) over the simple Virasoro vertex algebras Vir p , q {\mathrm{Vir}}_{p,q} , where p , q ≥ 2 p,q\geq 2 are relatively prime with 0 < m < p 0<m<p and 0 < n < q 0<n<q , are classically free. It turns out that this only happens with the boundary minimal models, i.e., with the irreducible modules over Vir 2 , 2 ​ s + 1 {\mathrm{Vir}}_{2,2s+1} for s ∈ ℤ + s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+} . We thus obtain a complete description of the classical limits of these modules in terms of the jet algebra of the corresponding Zhu C 2 C_{2} -algebra. The Andrews-Gordon generalization of the Rogers-Ramanujan identities is used in the proof, and our results in turn provide a natural interpretation of these identities. Keywords. Vertex algebras, boundary minimal models, Rogers-Ramanujan identities, quantum algebra, combinatorics.

Introduction

In
[
24
]
, Li introduced a decreasing filtration
(F_{p}V)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
on an arbitrary vertex algebra
V
.
The associated graded space
\gr_{F}(V)
with respect to this decreasing filtration carries the structure of a vertex Poisson algebra.
Li also introduced a decreasing filtration
(F_{p}M)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
for modules
M
over a vertex algebra
V
and showed that the associated graded space
\gr_{F}(M)
is a module over the vertex Poisson algebra
\gr_{F}(V)
.

Given a commutative algebra
R
, the jet algebra
JR
is constructed as the smallest differential algebra containing
R
.
It was proved by Arakawa in
[
5
, Proposition 2.5.1]
that there exists a natural surjection

JR_{V}\twoheadrightarrow\gr_{F}(V)

of vertex Poisson algebras, where
R_{V}
is the Zhu
C_{2}
-algebra of
V
as defined in
[
30
]
.
When this is an isomorphism, we say
V
is classically free.
For modules
M
over the vertex algebra
V
, we also have a natural surjection

JR_{V}\otimes_{R_{V}}R_{M}\twoheadrightarrow\gr_{F}(M),

and we say
M
is classically free if this is an isomorphism.
One may in turn regard the classically free vertex algebra
V
or
V
-module
M
as a quantization of
JR_{V}
or
JR_{V}\otimes_{R_{V}}R_{M}
, respectively.

The question of classical freeness of the simple Virasoro vertex algebra
{\mathrm{Vir}}_{p,q}
was studied in
[
28
]
, and its answer is very simple.
If
q>p\geq 2
are relatively prime integers, then
{\mathrm{Vir}}_{p,q}
is classically free if and only if
p=2
.
The main objective of this article is to solve this problem for all irreducible modules over the vertex algebras
{\mathrm{Vir}}_{p,q}
, namely
L(c_{p,q},h_{m,n})
for integers
m,n
such that
0<m<p
and
0<n<q
.

It turns out that this problem is deeply connected with the Rogers-Ramanujan
q
-series identities and the Andrews-Gordon generalization
[
18
]
and
[
2
]
, namely

(0.1)

\sum_{k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{s-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{s-1}}\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}kG^{(s)}k^{\top}+kB^{(s)}_{s-i}}}{(q)_{k_{1}}\dots(q)_{k_{s-1}}}=\prod^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\not\equiv 0,\pm i\bmod 2s+1\end{subarray}}(1-q^{n})^{-1},

for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, where

\displaystyle G^{(s)}

\displaystyle=(2\min\{i,j\})_{i,j=1}^{s-1},

\displaystyle B^{(s)}_{j}

\displaystyle=(0,0,\dots,0,1,2,\dots,j)^{\top}\quad\text{for $j=0,1,\dots,s-1$},

and
⊤
denotes the transpose matrix.

The identity (
0.1
) has an interpretation in terms of integer partitions, as explained in
[
3
, §8]
.
We represent a partition
\lambda
either as a finite nonincreasing sequence of positive integers
[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}]
or as an infinite sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}=[1^{f_{1}},2^{f_{2}},\dots]
of nonnegative integers in which only finitely many of the
f_{n}
are nonzero, where
f_{n}
is the number of times
n
appears among the
\lambda_{i}
.
We have a natural partial order
\leq
on partitions given by
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\leq(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
if
g_{n}\leq f_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
A set
P
of partitions is said to be a partition ideal if whenever
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\in P
and
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\leq(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
, then
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\in P
.
We denote by
p(P,n)
the number of partitions of
n
that belong to
P
.
We say two partition ideals
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are equivalent, denoted by
P_{1}\sim P_{2}
, if
p(P_{1},n)=p(P_{2},n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and let
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
Gordon’s generalization of the Rogers-Ramanujan identities asserts

A^{s,i}\sim P^{s,i},

where the partition ideals
A^{s,i}
and
P^{s,i}
are defined by:

\displaystyle A^{s,i}

\displaystyle=\{\text{$(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}$ is a partition}\mid\text{for $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}$, $f_{n}>0$ implies $n\not\equiv 0,\pm i\bmod 2s+1$}\},

\displaystyle R^{s,i}

\displaystyle=\{\text{$\lambda$ is a partition}\mid\text{$\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{s}]$ satisfies $\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{s}\leq 1$ or $\lambda=[1^{i}]$}\},

\displaystyle P^{s,i}

\displaystyle=\text{the set of partitions that do not contain any partition in $R^{s,i}$}.

As explained in
[
3
, §8]
,
A^{s,i}
is a partition ideal of order
1
, while
P^{s,i}
is a partition ideal of order
2
.
It is an open problem to characterize the equivalence classes of partition ideals.
For partition ideals of order
1
, this problem is completely solved (see
[
3
, Theorem 8.4]
).

In the context of representations of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras and vertex algebras, the left-hand side of (
0.1
) is precisely the graded dimension, or character, of the irreducible
{\mathrm{Vir}}
-module
M=L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})
.
Our main theorems concern a refined version of this character and the intimate relation between the structure of
\gr_{F}(M)
and the partition ideals
P^{s,i}
.
The second theorem about a PBW basis of
L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})
was conjectured by Kac in
[
21
]
and proved using different methods in
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
.
Here, we present another proof relying on the refined character formula of the next theorem.

Theorem 0.1
.

The refined character of
L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})
is given by

\ch_{L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})}(t,q)=q^{h_{1,i}}\left(\sum_{k=(k_{1},\dots,k_{s-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{s-1}}t^{kB^{(s)}_{s-1}}\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}kG^{(s)}k^{\top}+kB^{(s)}_{s-i}}}{(q)_{k_{1}}\dots(q)_{k_{s-1}}}\right).

Theorem 0.2
.

The set

\{L_{-\lambda_{1}}L_{-\lambda_{2}}\dots L_{-\lambda_{m}}|c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i}\rangle\mid\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]\in P^{s,i}\}

is a vector space basis of
L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})
.

Theorem 0.3
.

The boundary minimal models
L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i})
are classically free as modules over the simple Virasoro vertex algebra
{\mathrm{Vir}}_{2,2s+1}
for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
.
If
p,q>2
then no irreducible
{\mathrm{Vir}}_{p,q}
-module is classically free.

As a corollary, we obtain an explicit expression for the modules
\gr_{F}(L(c_{2,2s+1},h_{1,i}))
over the vertex Poisson algebra
\gr_{F}({\mathrm{Vir}}_{2,2s+1})
.

1.
Preliminaries and notation

All vector spaces and all algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers, unless otherwise stated.
The set of natural numbers
\{0,1,\dots\}
is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
, the set of integers is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
, and the set of positive integers
\{1,2,\dots\}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

First, we review the theory of representations of the Virasoro Lie algebra following
[
20
, §3]
.
The
Virasoro Lie algebra
is a Lie algebra given by

{\mathrm{Vir}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C.

These elements satisfy the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C\quad\text{for $m,n\in\mathbb{Z}$},

\displaystyle[{\mathrm{Vir}},C]

\displaystyle=0.

Let
(c,h)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
We set
{\mathrm{Vir}}^{\geq}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C
.
We make the subalgebra
{\mathrm{Vir}}^{\geq}
of
{\mathrm{Vir}}
act on
\mathbb{C}
as follows:

\text{$L_{n}1=0$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}$, $L_{0}1=h$ and $C1=c$}.

The
Verma representation
of
{\mathrm{Vir}}
with
highest weight

(c,h)
is defined as

M(c,h)=\Ind^{\mathrm{Vir}}_{{\mathrm{Vir}}^{\geq}}(\mathbb{C})=U({\mathrm{Vir}})\otimes_{U({\mathrm{Vir}}^{\geq})}\mathbb{C},

where
{\mathrm{Vir}}
acts by left multiplication.
We take
|c,h\rangle=1\otimes 1
as
highest weight vector
.

A
composition (of
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
is a sequence
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
such that
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for
i=1,\dots,m
(and
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}=n
).
We also consider the
empty composition

\emptyset
, which is the unique composition of
0
.
Given a composition
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
and a permutation
\sigma\in S_{m}
, we define the composition

\lambda\sigma=[\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\lambda_{\sigma(m)}].

For a composition
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
, we define

L_{\lambda}=L_{-\lambda_{1}}\dots L_{-\lambda_{m}}\in U({\mathrm{Vir}}),

the
length of
\lambda
as

\len(\lambda)=m,

the
weight of
\lambda
as

\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m},

and

p_{\lambda}=L_{-\lambda_{1}}\dots L_{-\lambda_{m}}\in\mathbb{C}[L_{-1},L_{-2},\dots].

For a composition
\lambda
with exactly
m
elements greater than
1
and exactly
n
elements equal to
1
, we define
\lambda^{\prime}
as the composition obtained from
\lambda
by removing all
1
s.
We define the
conformal length of
\lambda
as

\clen(\lambda)=2m+n

and

u_{\lambda}=p_{\lambda^{\prime}}L_{-1}^{n}\in\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}[L_{-2},L_{-3},\dots]L_{-1}^{k}.

A
partition (of
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
is a composition
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
(of
n
) such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m}
.
The notation
\lambda\vdash
means
\lambda
is a partition, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the notation
\lambda\vdash n
means
\lambda
is a partition of
n
.
This notation also tacitly assumes that
\lambda
is a partition and not just a composition.

Given a composition
\lambda
of length
m
, there is a unique partition
\prt(\lambda)
obtained by reordering the entries of
\lambda
in nonincreasing order.

By the PBW theorem, the set

\{L_{\lambda}|c,h\rangle\mid\lambda\vdash\}

is a vector space basis of
M(c,h)
.
The representation
M(c,h)
has a unique maximal proper subrepresentation
J(c,h)
, and the quotient

L(c,h)=M(c,h)/J(c,h)

is the
irreducible highest weight representation
of
{\mathrm{Vir}}
with highest weight
(c,h)
.

2.
Modules over algebras with a filtration

Let
A
be an associative (not necessarily commutative) algebra with unit
1
and filtration
(A^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
of subspaces of
A
such that:

(i)

A^{p}=0
for
p<0
;

(ii)

1\in A^{0}
;

(iii)

A^{0}\subseteq A^{1}\subseteq\dots
;

(iv)

A=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}A^{p}
;

(v)

A^{p}A^{q}\subseteq A^{p+q}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let

\gr(A)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}A^{p}/A^{p-1}

be the associated graded vector space.
The vector space
\gr(A)
is an associative algebra with unit and multiplication given as follows.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in A^{p}
and
b\in A^{q}
, we set

\gamma^{p}(a)\gamma^{q}(b)=\gamma^{p+q}(ab),

where
\gamma^{p}:A^{p}\to\gr(A)
is the
principal symbol map
, which is the composition of the natural maps
A^{p}\twoheadrightarrow A^{p}/A^{p-1}
and
A^{p}/A^{p-1}\hookrightarrow\gr(A)
.
The unit of
\gr(A)
is
\gamma^{0}(1)
.

Let
\partial
be a derivation of
A
respecting the filtration
(A^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e., it satisfies:

(i)

\partial(ab)=\partial(a)b+a\partial(b)
for
a,b\in A
;

(ii)

\partial(A^{p})\subseteq A^{p}
for
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We can define

\displaystyle\partial:\gr(A)

\displaystyle\to\gr(A),

\displaystyle\partial(\gamma^{p}(a))

\displaystyle=\gamma^{p}(\partial(a))\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $a\in A^{p}$},

and it is a derivation of
\gr(A)
.

Example 2.1

(
PBW filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra.
The PBW filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, is given by

U(\mathfrak{g})^{p}=\vspan\{x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{s}\mid s\leq p,x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in\mathfrak{g}\}\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

This filtration clearly satisfies axioms (i)–(v) above, and
\gr(U(\mathfrak{g}))
is naturally isomorphic to
S(\mathfrak{g})
, the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is a polynomial algebra (see
[
11
, §2]
for details).
Furthermore, if
\partial
is a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie algebra, then we can extend
\partial
uniquely to a derivation of
U(\mathfrak{g})
(see
[
11
, Proposition 2.4.9(i)]
), and it respects the PBW filtration.
Thus, it defines a derivation
\partial:\gr(U(\mathfrak{g}))\to\gr(U(\mathfrak{g}))
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module with filtration
(M^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
of subspaces of
M
such that:

(i)

M^{p}=0
for
p<0
;

(ii)

M^{0}\subseteq M^{1}\subseteq\dots
;

(iii)

M=\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}M^{p}
;

(iv)

A^{p}M^{q}\subseteq M^{p+q}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Let

\gr(M)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}M^{p}/M^{p-1}

be the associated graded vector space.
Then
\gr(M)
is a
\gr(A)
-module with operations given as follows.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in A^{p}
and
u\in M^{q}
, we set

\gamma^{p}(a)\gamma^{q}_{M}(u)=\gamma^{p+q}_{M}(au),

where
\gamma^{p}_{M}:M^{p}\to\gr(M)
is the principal symbol map, which is the composition of the natural maps
M^{p}\twoheadrightarrow M^{p}/M^{p-1}
and
M^{p}/M^{p-1}\hookrightarrow\gr(M)
.

Let
H\in\End(M)
be a diagonalizable operator.
Defining the eigenspaces of
M
as

M_{\Delta}=\ker(H-\Delta{\mathrm{Id}}_{M})\quad\text{for $\Delta\in\mathbb{C}$},

we have

M=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{C}}M_{\Delta}.

We say the filtration
(M^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
graded
with respect to the diagonalizable operator
H
if each subspace
M^{p}
is graded, i.e., if

(2.1)

H(M^{p})\subseteq M^{p}\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

We assume we have a diagonalizable operator
H
and a graded filtration
(M^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
By (
2.1
) and
[
20
, Corollary 1.1]
, we have

(2.2)

M^{p}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb{C}}M^{p}\cap M_{\Delta}\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

By property (
2.1
), we can define an operator
H\in\End(\gr(M))
as
H(\gamma^{p}_{M}(u))=\gamma^{p}_{M}(Hu)
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\in M^{p}
.
For
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}
, we define
M^{p}_{\Delta}=M^{p}\cap M_{\Delta}
.
For
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}
, we define
\gr(M)_{\Delta}=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma_{M}^{p}(M^{p}_{\Delta})
.
Then
Hu=\Delta u
for
u\in\gr(M)_{\Delta}
, and the family of subspaces
(\gr(M)_{\Delta})_{\Delta\in\mathbb{C}}
is linearly independent.
By (
2.2
), we have the refined grading

(2.3)

\gr(M)=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{N}\\
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\gamma^{p}_{M}(M^{p}_{\Delta}).

By (
2.3
), when
\dim(M_{\Delta})<\infty
for
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}
, it is natural to define the
character of
M
and the
refined character of
M
as:

\displaystyle\ch_{M}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb{C}}\dim(M_{\Delta})q^{\Delta}=\sum_{\Delta\in\mathbb{C}}\dim(\gr(M)_{\Delta})q^{\Delta},

\displaystyle\ch_{M}(t,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{N}\\
\Delta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\dim(\gamma_{M}^{p}(M^{p}_{\Delta}))t^{p}q^{\Delta}.

The category of modules over
A
with the given filtration
(A^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
is given by modules
M
with filtration
(M^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying conditions (i)–(iv) above.
A homomorphism
f:M\to N
must satisfy
f(M^{p})\subseteq N^{p}
for
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

If
f:M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a homomorphism of
A
-modules, then

\displaystyle\gr(f):\gr(M_{1})

\displaystyle\to\gr(M_{2}),

\displaystyle\gr(f)(\gamma_{M_{1}}^{p}(u))

\displaystyle=\gamma_{M_{2}}^{p}(f(u))\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{N}$ and $u\in M_{1}^{p}$}

defines a homomorphism of
\gr(A)
-modules.
If the filtrations of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}

\displaystyle a(z)_{+}

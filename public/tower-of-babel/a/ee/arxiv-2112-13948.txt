A Gridless Fourth-order Cumulant-Based DOA Estimation Method under Unknown Colored Noise
Jiawen Yuan

Abstract
To reduce the adverse impacts of the unknown colored noise on the performance degradation of the direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation, we propose a new gridless DOA estimation method based on fourth-order cumulant (FOC) in this letter. We first introduce the non-redundancy single measurement vector (SMV) through FOC, which is capable of suppressing the Gaussian colored noise. Next, we analyze the distribution of the estimation error and design an estimation error tolerance scheme for it. We then combine the atomic norm minimization of the non-redundancy SMV with the above constraint scheme. This combination poses the stability of the sparsest solution. Finally, the DOA estimation is retrieved through rotational invariance techniques. Moreover, this method extends the gridless DOA estimation to the sparse linear array. Numerical simulations validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.



I
Introduction

Direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation algorithms have received extensive research attention in many engineering fields such as radar
\left[1\right]
,
\left[2\right]
and wireless communication
\left[3\right]
,
\left[4\right]
. These algorithms commonly assume that the background noise is white Gaussian. However, it is a noteworthy phenomenon that occurs in these fields, where colored noise is output as white noise after passing through linear components. This phenomenon inevitably mismatches the signal model proposed by second-order statistics-based DOA estimation methods
\left[5\right]
,
\left[6\right]
, which is the primary cause of the performance deterioration in practical applications.

Numerous efforts have been made to deal with DOA estimation in unknown colored noise
\left[7\right]
-
\left[14\right]
. Based on the assumption that the noise covariance matrix follows a certain symmetric structure
\left[7\right]
,
\left[8\right]
, it is feasible to alleviate the influence of these noises by obtaining the covariance difference matrix. However, this assumption is hard to satisfy and it fails to work in the circumstance with more sources than antennas. To overcome the above deficiencies, an algorithm based on fourth-order cumulant (FOC)
\left[9\right]
and its variants
\left[10\right]
-
\left[14\right]
have been proposed. All these methods have the ability to expand the effective aperture and to increase the degrees of freedom without any assumption on the structure of the noise covariance matrix. In particular, the FOC matrix-based atomic norm minimization (FOC-ANM)
\left[14\right]
algorithm associates sparsity with the FOC matrix to offer reliable estimates under the uniform linear array (ULA) for it fundamentally eliminates the effect of basis mismatch. Compared with the grid-based DOA estimation methods
\left[4\right]
,
\left[15\right]
, FOC-ANM achieves a satisfactory estimation performance. However, FOC-ANM ignores the influence of the FOC matrix estimation error caused by the limited number of snapshots.

In this letter, we propose a new gridless FOC-based method, named error-tolerant FOC-ANM (ET-FOCANM), to enhance resolution capability and estimation accuracy under unknown colored noise.
Compared with the existing FOC-based methods, we analyze the distribution of the estimation error due to the limited number of snapshots and attach the estimation error tolerance constraint to an ANM-based model for ensuring a stable and sufficiently sparse solution. The merits of the ET-FOCANM include robustness to the Gaussian colored noise, an increased number of detectable sources in the ULA and sparse linear array (SLA) case, expanded virtual array aperture, and no requirement of user-parameter in the estimation error tolerance constraint.

II-B

Definition of reduce-complexity FOC (RC-FOC) matrix

Based on the above assumptions, the received signals are considered as stationary non-Gaussian processes with zero mean. Since the FOC is blind to Gaussian processes
\left[16\right]
, we construct the FOC matrix
\bm{C}_{4}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}\times N^{2}}
of
\bm{y}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
as

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{4}

\displaystyle=E\left\{\left(\bm{Y}\otimes\bm{Y}^{*}\right)\left(\bm{Y}\otimes\bm{Y}^{*}\right)^{H}\right\}-E\left\{\bm{Y}\otimes\bm{Y}^{*}\right\}

(2)

\displaystyle\quad\times E\left\{\bm{Y}\otimes\bm{Y}^{*}\right\}^{H}-E\left\{\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{H}\right\}\otimes E\left\{\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{H}\right\}^{*}.

Then
\bm{C}_{4}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\bm{C}_{4}=\left(\bm{A}\odot\bm{A}^{*}\right)\bm{C}_{\bm{S}}\left(\bm{A}\odot\bm{A}^{*}\right)^{H}=\overline{\bm{B}}\bm{C}_{\bm{S}}\overline{\bm{B}}^{H},

(3)

where
\bm{C}_{\bm{S}}=diag(\bm{\gamma})\in\mathbb{C}^{P\times P}
,
\bm{\gamma}=\left[\gamma_{4s_{p}},...,\gamma_{4s_{P}}\right]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{P}
, and its
p
-th element
\gamma_{4s_{p}}=cum\left(s_{p},s_{p}^{*},s_{p}^{*},s_{p}\right)
.

Due to a large number of redundant elements in
\bm{C}_{4}
, the RC-FOC matrix
\bm{R}_{4}\in\mathbb{C}^{(2N-1)\times(2N-1)}
is designed in
\left[14\right]

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{4}

\displaystyle=\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}\bm{C}_{4}\overline{\bm{H}}\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}

(4)

\displaystyle=\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}\overline{\bm{B}}\bm{C}_{\bm{S}}\overline{\bm{B}}^{H}\overline{\bm{H}}\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}=\bm{B}\bm{C}_{\bm{S}}\bm{B}^{H},

where
\overline{\bm{G}}=\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}\overline{\bm{H}}=diag\left\{1,...,N,N-1,...,1\right\}
and
\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}=\left[\overline{\bm{H}}_{1}^{T},\overline{\bm{H}}_{2}^{T},...,\overline{\bm{H}}_{N}^{T}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{(2N-1)\times N^{2}}
is an orthogonal matrix. Moreover, the definition of
\overline{\bm{H}}_{n}
is represented as

\!\overline{\bm{H}}_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\left[\mathbf{0}_{N\times(N-1)},\bm{I}_{N}\right]}&\!n=1\\
{\left[\mathbf{0}_{N\times(N-n)},\bm{I}_{N},\mathbf{0}_{N\times(n-1)}\right]}&\!\quad 2\!\leq\!n\leq\!\!N\!\!-\!\!1\\
{\left[\bm{I}_{N},\mathbf{0}_{N\times(N-1)}\right]}&\!n=N.\end{array}\right.

(5)

\bm{B}=\left[\bm{b}(\theta_{1}),...,\bm{b}(\theta_{P})\right]\in\mathbb{C}^{(2N-1)\times P}
is the new array manifold matrix with the
p
-th column vector
\bm{b}(\theta_{p})=\left[e^{-j\pi(N-1)\sin\theta_{p}},...,e^{j\pi(N-1)\sin\theta_{p}}\right]^{T}
.

III
The ET-FOCANM Algorithm

III-A

Introduction of non-redundancy SMV

Consider a ULA composed of
N
omnidirectional antennas, since rank(
\bm{R}_{4}
) is tied to the number of sources in the ULA case, the condition
P<(4N-3)
can be taken as a priori information. Given
\bm{R}_{4}
as in
\left(4\right)
, let us define the vector
\overline{\bm{z}}
.

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle=vec\left(\bm{R}_{4}\right)=\left(\bm{B}^{*}\odot\bm{B}\right)\bm{\gamma}

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{p=1}^{P}\gamma_{4s_{p}}\bm{b}\left(\theta_{p}\right)^{*}\odot\bm{b}\left(\theta_{p}\right)=\bm{HD\gamma},

where
\bm{D}=\left[\bm{d}(\theta_{1}),...,\bm{d}(\theta_{P})\right]\in\mathbb{C}^{(4N-3)\times P}
, and
\bm{d}(\theta_{p})=\left[e^{-j\pi(2N-2)\sin\theta_{p}},...,e^{j\pi(2N-2)\sin\theta_{p}}\right]^{T}
. It can be seen that
\overline{\bm{z}}
still has the repeating entries. Therefore, we choose a new orthogonal matrix
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{(2N-1)^{2}\times(4N-3)}
whose form is consistent with
\overline{\bm{H}}
to perform the linear transformation on
\overline{\bm{z}}

\bm{z}=\bm{G}^{-1}\bm{H}^{T}\overline{\bm{z}}=\bm{D\gamma}=\sum\nolimits_{p=1}^{P}\gamma_{4s_{p}}\bm{d}\left(\theta_{p}\right),

(7)

where
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{4N-3}
is the non-redundancy single measurement vector (SMV) and
\bm{G}=\bm{H}^{T}\bm{H}
. As a byproduct, it can generate
O\left\{4N-3\right\}
degrees of freedom from only
O\left\{N\right\}
antennas, which is beneficial to detect more sources.

III-B

Estimation error tolerance scheme

Since finite snapshots inevitably lead to estimation error in the actual situation, it has the potential to relax the above equality constraint
\left(7\right)
and demand instead

\bm{z}=\bm{D}\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\varepsilon}=\bm{x}+\bm{\varepsilon},

(8)

in which
\bm{\varepsilon}
is the estimation error vector from non-redundancy SMV. As shown in
\left[5\right]
, the sparse model
\bm{x}
can be linearly represented by
P
atoms in a set of the continuous atoms, so we make the following optimization to reconstruct
\bm{x}

\hat{\bm{x}}=\underset{\bm{x}}{\arg\min}\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\quad s.t.\quad\|\bm{\varepsilon}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{z}-\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\xi,

(9)

where
\xi
denotes the tolerance of error energy. Unlike the constraint in
\left[14\right]
, this inequality is controlled by
\xi
, where
\xi
is a result of multiple factors, including signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and snapshot. Thus, it is hard to select a proper value of
\xi
for the final DOAs.

To proceed, we innovatively propose a new estimation error tolerance scheme that does not depend on
\xi

\Delta\bm{C}_{4}=\hat{\bm{C}_{4}}-\bm{C}_{4}

\bm{C}_{4}

Inspired by

(10)

AsN(\bm{0},\bm{V})
denotes asymptotic normal distribution with zero mean and the covariance matrix
\bm{V}
.
Meanwhile, the element of
\bm{V}
can be determined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\!\!\!\!cov\left\{\hat{c}_{4}(\tau),\hat{c}_{4}(\rho)\right\}\!\!=\!\!\frac{1}{J}\!\!\left\{Q_{44}(\tau;\rho)-\sum_{v=1}^{3}\!\!\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{l}Q_{42}\left(\tau;k_{1}^{\prime},k_{v1}^{\prime}\right)\!\!E\{k_{v2}^{\prime}k_{v3}^{\prime}\}\!+\!Q_{42}\left(\tau;k_{v2}^{\prime},k_{v3}^{\prime}\right)\!\!E\{k_{1}^{\prime}k_{v1}^{\prime}\}\\

(11)

where
\tau\!=\!\left\{k_{1},\!k_{2},\!k_{3},\!k_{4}\right\}
,
\rho\!\!=\!\!\left\{\!k_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},\!k_{2}^{{}^{\prime}},\!k_{3}^{{}^{\prime}},\!k_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}\!\right\}

,

,
k_{13}\!=\!k_{4}
,
k_{21}\!=\!k_{3}
,

,

,
k_{31}\!=\!k_{4}
,
k_{32}\!=\!k_{3}
,
k_{33}\!=\!k_{2}
and the same as
k^{{}^{\prime}}
.
E\{k_{1}k_{v1}\}
is the expectation of
y_{k_{1}}y_{k_{v1}}
and the same as
E\{k_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}k_{v1}^{{}^{\prime}}\}
,
E\{k_{v2}k_{v3}\}
,
E\{\!k_{v2}^{{}^{\prime}}k_{v3}^{{}^{\prime}}\!\}
,
E\{\!k_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}k_{\mu 1}^{{}^{\prime}}\!\}
,
E\{\!k_{\mu 2}^{{}^{\prime}}k_{\mu 3}^{{}^{\prime}}\!\}
. The definition of

is as follows

\displaystyle\!\!Q_{ij}\!=\!\mathop{\lim}\limits_{J\to\infty}\!\!Jcov\{\hat{m}_{i},\hat{m}_{j}\}\!=\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\xi=-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!cov\{{f_{k}}(t),f_{{k^{{}^{\prime}}}}(t+\xi)\},

(12)

where
{f_{k}}(t)={y_{{k_{1}}}}(t)y_{{k_{2}}}^{*}(t)\cdots{y_{{k_{i}}}}(t)
and
{f_{{k^{{}^{\prime}}}}}(t)={y_{k_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(t)y_{k_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}(t)\cdots{y_{k_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(t)

.
y_{{k_{i}}}(t)
denotes the

-th element of

with

Next, utilizing the orthogonal invariance property of Gaussian random matrix, it can be inferred as

\displaystyle\bm{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{-1}\bm{H}^{T}vec\left(\Delta\bm{R}_{4}\right)=\bm{G}^{-1}\bm{H}^{T}vec\left(\widehat{\bm{R}}_{4}-\bm{R}_{4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{G}^{-1}\bm{H}^{T}\left(\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}\right)\otimes\left(\overline{\bm{G}}^{-1}\overline{\bm{H}}^{T}\right)vec\left(\Delta\bm{C}_{4}\right)

where

Finally, based on the property of

\left\|\bm{W}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta.

(15)

The choice of

in Matlab.

III-C

ANM-based model attached to the above constraint

We propose the error-tolerant problem by substituting the (15) into the constraint term of (9)

\hat{\bm{x}}=\underset{\bm{x}}{\arg\min}\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\quad s.t.\quad\left\|\bm{W}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta.

(16)

This problem must always yield the optimal solution at least as sparse as that in (9).
Further, the result of (16) can be solved by the SDP problem
\left[18\right]

\displaystyle\{\hat{\bm{x}},\hat{q},\bm{T}(\hat{\bm{\mu}})\}=\underset{\bm{x},q,T(\bm{\mu})}{\arg\min}\frac{1}{2}q+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle s.t.\left\|\bm{W}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{T}(\bm{\mu})&\quad\bm{x}\\
\bm{x}^{H}&\quad q\end{array}\right]\geq 0,

where
q
denotes the smallest dilation factor and
\bm{T}\left(\bm{\mu}\right)
represents the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix with its first column
\bm{\mu}=\left[\mu_{1},...,\mu_{4N-3}\right]^{T}
and rank(
\bm{T}\left(\bm{\mu}\right))=P\leq(4N-4)
. Hence, when the convex optimization toolbox called CVX
\left[19\right]
produces the optimal solution
\bm{T}\left(\bm{\mu}\right)
of (17), it is easy to estimate the DOAs through some general techniques such as Vandermonde decomposition
\left[5\right]
, rotational invariance techniques (ESPRIT)
\left[20\right]
, spectral peak search
\left[9\right]
and its variant root-MUSIC
\left[21\right]
. To simplify the calculation, we perform rotational invariance techniques on the ensuing DOA estimation.

IV
The Extension to an SLA

In this subsection, we extend ET-FOCANM method to the SLA case. For simplicity, we mainly focus on one type of the SLA, which is the minimum redundancy array (MRA)
\left[5\right]
,
\left[6\right]
. Therefore, according to the signal model (1) and the definition of
\bm{C}_{4}
, the FOC matrix
\bm{C}_{4\Omega}\in\mathbb{C}^{M^{2}\times M^{2}}
is designed as follows

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\bm{C}_{4\Omega}

\displaystyle=E\left\{\left(\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{*}\right)\left(\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{*}\right)^{H}\right\}\!-\!E\left\{\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{*}\right\}

(20)

\displaystyle\!\quad\!\times\!E\left\{\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\!\otimes\!\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{*}\right\}^{H}\!-\!E\left\{\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{H}\right\}\otimes E\left\{\bm{Y}_{\Omega}\bm{Y}_{\Omega}^{H}\right\}^{*}.

Then we derive
\bm{R}_{4}
under the SLA case

\!\!\!\bm{R}_{4}=\bm{G}_{\Omega}^{-1}\left[\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right]^{T}\!\bm{C}_{4\Omega}\!\left[\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right]\bm{G}_{\Omega}^{-1},

(21)

where
\bm{G}_{\Omega}=\left[\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right]^{T}\left[\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right]
. The acquisition of
\bm{z}
is the same as in (8) and let
\Delta\bm{C}_{4\Omega}=\hat{\bm{C}}_{4\Omega}-\bm{C}_{4\Omega}
be the FOC matrix error in the SLA case.
Since

is equal to
\left[\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\otimes\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\right]vec(\bm{C}_{4})
, which reveals that

. Further, we conclude the link between
\bm{\varepsilon}
and
\Delta\bm{C}_{4\Omega}

\displaystyle\!\!\bm{\varepsilon}=\bm{W}_{\Omega}vec\left(\Delta\bm{C}_{4\Omega}\right)\sim AsN\left(\bm{0},\bm{W}_{\Omega}\bm{V}_{\Omega}\bm{W}_{\Omega}^{H}\right),

(22)

where
\bm{W}_{\Omega}\!\!\!\!=\!\!\!\!\bm{G}^{-1}\bm{\!H}^{T}(\bm{G}_{\Omega}^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right)^{T}\otimes\bm{G}_{\Omega}^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\otimes\bm{\Gamma}_{\Omega}\right)\overline{\bm{H}}\right)^{T})
.
Similarly, we have

\left\|\bm{W}_{\Omega}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\sim As\chi^{2}(4N-3).

(23)

The denoising problem in the SLA case is rewritten as follows:

\widehat{\bm{x}}=\underset{\bm{x}}{\arg\min}\|\bm{x}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\quad s.t.\left\|\bm{W}_{\Omega}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta,

(24)

and the problem (22) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\{\hat{\bm{x}},\hat{q},\bm{T}(\hat{\bm{\mu}})\}=\underset{\bm{x},q,T(\bm{\mu})}{\arg\min}\frac{1}{2}q+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle s.t.\left\|\bm{W}_{\Omega}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\varepsilon}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta,\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{T}(\bm{\mu})&\quad\bm{x}\\
\bm{x}^{H}&\quad q\end{array}\right]\geq 0.

To get an accurate estimate of
\bm{T}(\bm{\mu})
through CVX, we use rotational invariance techniques again to retrieve the DOAs.

VI
Conclusion

In this letter, we propose a gridless FOC-based method, named ET-FOCANM, for DOA estimation in the two linear arrays. After suppressing the colored noise by FOC, ET-FOCANM implements the practicable estimation error tolerance scheme and is converted into an ANM-based form. The experimental results indicate that ET-FOCANM can achieve better angular precision and resolution capability than the traditional algorithms. For future work, it will be worth applying the alternating direction method of multipliers to speed up the solution of the ANM-based form in ET-FOCANM.

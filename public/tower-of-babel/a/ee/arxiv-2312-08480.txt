Abstract
Exact solutions describing trapped modes in a plane quantum waveguide with a small rigid obstacle are constructed in the form of convergent series in powers of the small parameter characterizing the smallness of the obstacle. The terms of this series are expressed through the solution of the exterior Neumann problem for the Laplace equation describing the flow of unbounded fluid past the inflated obstacle. The exact solutions obtained describe discrete eigenvalues of the problem under certain geometric conditions, and, when the obstacle is symmetric, these solutions describe embedded eigenvalues. For obstacles symmetric with respect to the centerline of the waveguide, the existence of embedded trapped modes is known (due to the decomposition trick of the domain of the corresponding differential operator) even without the smallness assumption. We construct these solutions in an explicit form for small obstacles. For obstacles symmetric with respect to the vertical axis, we find embedded trapped modes for a specific vertical displacement of the obstacle.

Introduction

The present paper is devoted to the study of trapped modes in a plane quantum waveguide with a small rigid obstacle. Mathematically, this reduces to the study of the following boundary value problem (see Fig.

\displaystyle-\Delta u=k^{2}u,\quad x,y\in\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u\Big|_{\Gamma_{\pm}}=0,\quad\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\Bigg|_{\gamma}=0,

Problems of type (

[
2
]
-
[
4

The present paper is devoted to the construction of exact explicit solutions in the form of convergent series in

Other geometries were studied by means of asymptotic techniques (for example, slender symmetric obstacle situated across the guide
[
3

Let us analyze these conditions.
Substituting in (

\varepsilon
. Therefore, at least the leading term of (

In the leading term in
\varepsilon
, we have by (

)

\hat{Q}_{3}\hat{L}R=0\quad{\rm at}\quad p=\pm p_{1}.

(5.22)

At the point

Also,

, and hence, up to

(which is in fact

), changing

to

and expanding

Thus (

,

\begin{array}[]{lll}O_{\pm}:=\hat{Q}_{3}\hat{L}R\Big|_{p=\pm p_{1}}&=&\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{\mp ip_{1}^{0}\varepsilon X}\Big(\pm i\displaystyle\frac{2bp_{1}^{0}}{\pi}\dot{Y}\;\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2b}\big(a+\varepsilon Y\big)-\dot{X}\sin\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2b}\big(a+\varepsilon Y\big)\Big)\\
\\
&\times&\Big(\hat{L}\sin\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{b}\big(a+\varepsilon Y\big)\Big)dt=0.\end{array}

Expanding the exponentials and trigonometric functions in
\varepsilon
and taking into account that

\begin{array}[]{lll}O_{\pm}&=&\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon\pi}{b}\Big(2S\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}+\pi\mu\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\Big)\sin\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}\pm 2i\varepsilon\displaystyle\frac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{2b}\pi\nu\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}+O(\varepsilon^{2}\ln\varepsilon).\end{array}

Thus orthogonality condition (
5.21
), in the leading term, means that the first summand in (

O_{\pm}^{r}:=\Big(2S\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}+\pi\mu\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\Big)\sin\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}=0

(5.26)

and

O_{\pm}^{i}:=\nu\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}\cdot\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}=0.

Firstly, let us analyze (

\cos\pi a/b=0\quad{\rm or}\quad\nu=0

(because

).
But, if

(ii)

Let us continue the proof of Theorem
2.3
. We still have to satisfy (

2S\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}+\pi\mu\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}=0

\sin\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}=0.

The first possibility (

and hence there are no trapped modes. Indeed, (

), similarly to (

\sigma
is even smaller than

, i.e.,
\sigma=O(\varepsilon^{2})
(cf. (

)) and, in the leading term,

\sigma=2\varepsilon\hat{Q}_{3}\hat{L}R\Big|_{p=i\sigma}=2\varepsilon\hat{Q}_{3}\hat{L}R\Big|_{p=0}+O(\varepsilon\sigma).

Similarly to Section
4.1
(formula (
4.28
)), by means of simple trigonometric calculations, it is easy to obtain that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sigma&=&\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{b^{3}}\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\;\dot{X}Y-\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\;\dot{X}\hat{L}_{0}Y\Big)dt+O(\varepsilon^{3}\ln\varepsilon)\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{b^{3}}\Big(-\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}\sin^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}+\pi\mu\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\Big)+O(\varepsilon^{3}\ln\varepsilon).\end{array}

By (

\pi\mu=-2S\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{2b}\Big/\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}.

Substituting in (

\sigma=-\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{b^{3}}\;S\Big(1+3\cos\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}+4\cos^{2}\displaystyle\frac{\pi a}{b}\Big)<0,

because the quadratic form in parentheses is positive definite.

a=0
in the leading term. As we have already mentioned, we have to require that

.

Proposition 5.3
.

(Necessary condition for the existence of embedded trapped modes).

Embedded trapped modes for (

), (
1.2

and
\nu=0
.

Unfortunately, we do not know in general when the condition

.

In what follows, we will assume that this symmetry condition is satisfied. Let us begin with item
(iii)
.

5.1

Di-lepton Rapidity Distribution in Drell-Yan Production to Third Order in QCD Preprint: KA-TP-17-2021, ZU-TH 33/21, CERN-TH-2021-110, IPPP/21/13
Xuan Chen, Thomas Gehrmann, Nigel Glover, Alexander Huss, Tong-Zhi Yang, Hua Xing Zhu

Abstract
We compute for the first time the lepton-pair rapidity distribution in the photon-mediated Drell-Yan process to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order ( N 3 \text{N}^{3} LO ) in QCD. The calculation is based on the q T q_{T} -subtraction method, suitably extended to this order for quark-antiquark initiated Born processes. Our results display sizeable QCD corrections at N 3 \text{N}^{3} LO over the full rapidity region and provide a fully independent confirmation of the recent results for the total Drell-Yan cross section at this order.



I
Introduction

Precision physics is becoming increasingly important for the CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC) physics program,
in particular in view of the absence of striking signals for beyond the standard model phenomena. Among the most important precision processes at the LHC is Drell-Yan lepton-pair production through neutral current
Z/\gamma^{*}
or charged current
W^{\pm}
exchanges. It plays a central role in the extraction of standard model parameters and as input to the determination of parton distribution functions (PDFs). The Drell-Yan process is also highly important in new physics searches, both as background to direct signals and as an indirect probe of dynamics beyond the collider energy.

The Drell-Yan process further plays a special role in the development of modern precision theory calculations in particle physics. It was the first hadron collider process for which next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD corrections were calculated
Altarelli et al. 1978
;
Altarelli et al. 1979
, due to its simplicity in kinematics on one hand and its phenomenological importance on the other hand. The large perturbative corrections observed at the NLO also sparked the interest in soft gluon resummation
Sterman 1987
;
Catani and Trentadue 1989
, which subsequently developed into a field of its own. The first calculation of inclusive next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) QCD corrections to a hadron collider process was also performed for the Drell-Yan process
Hamberg et al. 1991
;
Harlander and Kilgore 2002
, followed by the first NNLO rapidity distributions
Anastasiou et al. 2003
;
Anastasiou et al. 2004
, and then fully differential distributions
Melnikov and Petriello 2006a
;
Melnikov and Petriello 2006b
;
Catani et al. 2009
;
Catani et al. 2010
;
Gavin et al. 2011
. Very recently, next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (
\text{N}^{3}
LO
) QCD corrections have been computed for the inclusive Drell-Yan process with an off-shell photon
Duhr et al. 2020a
, and for charged current Drell-Yan production
Duhr et al. 2020b
. With such level of accuracy in perturbative QCD, mixed electroweak-QCD corrections, derived recently
Dittmaier et al. 2016
;
Buccioni et al. 2020
;
Behring et al. 2021
;
Buonocore et al. 2021
;
Bonciani et al. 2022
, become equally important.

For many phenomenological applications of Drell-Yan production, it is more desirable to have differential predictions.
For Higgs production, distributions that are fully differential in the decay products are now available at
\text{N}^{3}
LO

Chen et al. 2021
using the analytic results for the inclusive cross section and rapidity distribution of the Higgs boson at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
as input
Anastasiou et al. 2015
;
Mistlberger 2018
;
Dulat et al. 2019
. Unfortunately, the same approach does not work well for the Drell-Yan process, as the threshold expansion used for the analytic calculation of the rapidity distribution does not converge well for quark-induced processes.

In this Letter, we present for the first time the di-lepton rapidity distribution for Drell-Yan production at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, computed using the
q_{T}
-subtraction method at this order. We focus on the contribution from virtual photon production alone, neglecting the contribution from
Z
boson exchange and from virtual photon-
Z
interference. While the remaining contributions are important, the virtual photon contributions are sufficiently representative to gain knowledge about the size of QCD corrections at this order
Duhr et al. 2020a
, and most importantly sufficient for illustrating the subtraction of infrared singularities at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
.
Upon integration over rapidity, our calculation reproduces the recent
\text{N}^{3}
LO
result
Duhr et al. 2020a
for the
inclusive Drell-Yan coefficient function in a completely independent manner,
thereby establishing the validity and practicality of the
q_{T}
-subtraction method at this order.

II
qT-Subtraction at
\text{N}^{3}
LO

The
\text{N}^{3}
LO
corrections in QCD receive contributions from four types of parton-level sub-processes, each correcting the underlying
Born-level process:
triple real radiation at tree level, double real radiation at one loop, single real radiation at two loops and purely virtual three-loop corrections.
At this order, only very few collider processes have been computed so far,
including inclusive and differential Higgs production from gluon fusion
Anastasiou et al. 2015
;
Mistlberger 2018
;
Dulat et al. 2019
;
Cieri et al. 2019a
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Billis et al. 2021a
, inclusive Drell-Yan production
Duhr et al. 2020a
,
inclusive Higgs production from
b
quark annihilation
Duhr et al. 2020c
, vector boson fusion Higgs production
Dreyer and Karlberg 2016
, di-Higgs production
Chen et al. 2020
, inclusive deep inelastic scattering
Vermaseren et al. 2005
and
jet production in deep inelastic scattering
Currie et al. 2018
;
Gehrmann et al. 2019
.

In this Letter, we focus on the Drell-Yan production through a virtual photon, for which all relevant matrix elements have been available for some
time
Hagiwara and Zeppenfeld 1989
;
Berends et al. 1989
;
Glover and Miller 1997
;
Campbell et al. 1997
;
Bern et al. 1998
;
Garland et al. 2002
;
Baikov et al. 2009
;
Gehrmann et al. 2010
.
After mass factorization of universal initial-state collinear singularities, perturbative predictions for infrared safe observables are finite.
All individual subprocesses with different multiplicities are separately infrared divergent, with divergences in subprocesses with real radiations
residing in phase space integrals. An important part of the recent NLO and NNLO revolution has been the development of convenient and efficient algorithms
for handling these infrared singularities from real emissions. Two among these methods (projection-to-Born
Cacciari et al. 2015
and
q_{T}
-subtraction
Catani and Grazzini 2007
) have been extended to be applied in specific
\text{N}^{3}
LO
calculations
Gehrmann et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Cieri et al. 2019a
;
Billis et al. 2021a

The
q_{T}
-subtraction method
Catani and Grazzini 2007
;
Catani et al. 2009
;
Catani et al. 2010
;
Catani et al. 2012a
was
initially developed for processes with colorless final states.
The key idea is that the most singular phase space configurations are associated with the
small
q_{T}
region of the colorless system, and can be isolated by an artificial
q_{T}
cut. The extension of the
q_{T}
-subtraction
method to
\text{N}^{3}
LO
has been outlined for gluon-induced
Cieri et al. 2019a
;
Billis et al. 2021b
and quark-induced processes
Camarda et al. 2021
;
Billis et al. 2021b
. For Drell-Yan production at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, the double differential cross section in di-lepton invariant mass squared
Q^{2}
and di-lepton rapidity
y
is divided into the unresolved (resolved) part, in which
q_{T}
is bounded by
q_{T}^{\rm cut}
from above (below),

\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{\gamma^{*}}}{dQ^{2}dy}=\int_{0}^{q_{T}^{\rm cut}}\!d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{T}\frac{d^{4}\sigma_{\gamma^{*}}}{d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{T}dQ^{2}dy}+\int_{q_{T}^{\rm cut}}\!d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{T}\frac{d^{4}\sigma_{\gamma^{*}}}{d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{T}dQ^{2}dy}\,.

(1)

The resolved contribution can be regarded as Drell-Yan plus jet production, therefore requiring infrared subtraction only to NNLO. The genuine
\text{N}^{3}
LO
infrared singularities cancel within the unresolved contribution. While the singularities themselves are canceled, they
give rise to large logarithms,
\ln^{m}q_{T}^{\rm cut}/Q
, both in the resolved and the unresolved contribution, which cancel each other when resolved and unresolved contributions are combined.

A major advantage of
q_{T}
-subtraction is that the structure of perturbation theory in the unresolved region is well understood from the development of
q_{T}
resummation
Collins et al. 1985
;
Bozzi et al. 2006
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Chiu et al. 2012
. This allows one to write the unresolved contributions in a factorised form to all orders in perturbation theory, in terms of a hard function
H
, beam functions
B
for the incoming particle beams, and a soft function

S
:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}\sigma_{\gamma^{*}}}{d^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{T}dQ^{2}dy}=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{\mathrm{Born}}}{E_{\mathrm{CM}}^{2}}\int\frac{d^{2}\boldsymbol{b}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{q}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle\times B_{i/A}(x_{A},\boldsymbol{b})B_{\bar{\imath}/B}(x_{B},\boldsymbol{b})S(\boldsymbol{b})H_{i}(Q^{2})

\displaystyle+(i\leftrightarrow\bar{\imath})\bigg)\big[1+\mathcal{O}({q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}})\big]\,,

(2)

where
\sigma_{i}^{\mathrm{Born}}=4\pi Q_{i}^{2}\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{2}/(3N_{c}Q^{2})
,
Q_{i}
is the electric charge,
\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}
is the fine structure constant of QED,
E_{\mathrm{CM}}
is the center of mass energy. The momentum fractions are
fixed by the final-state kinematics as

x_{A}=\sqrt{\tau}e^{y}
,
x_{B}=\sqrt{\tau}e^{-y}
, with
\tau=(Q^{2}+q_{T}^{2})/E_{\mathrm{CM}}^{2}
.
In contrast to the leading-power terms
Ebert et al. 2019
;
Cieri et al. 2019b
;
Inglis-Whalen et al. 2021
, the power corrections are far less well understood but their contribution can be suppressed by choosing a sufficiently small
q_{T}^{\rm cut}
value.
The factorisation structure in Eq. (
II
) is most transparent in Soft-Collinear Effective Theory (SCET)
Bauer et al. 2000
;
Bauer et al. 2001
;
Bauer et al. 2002a
;
Bauer et al. 2002b
;
Beneke et al. 2002
, which also provides a convenient framework for the calculation of the
unresolved contribution beyond NNLO.

The hard function
H
is simply the electromagnetic quark form factor.
The beam function
B_{i/A}(x_{A},\boldsymbol{b})
encodes initial-state collinear radiation.
For a high energy hadron
A
moving in the light-cone direction
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
with four momentum
P_{A}^{\mu}
, the beam function can be written in light-cone gauge and coordinates as

B_{i/A}(x,\boldsymbol{b})=\int\!\frac{db^{-}}{4\pi}e^{ixb^{-}\tfrac{P_{A}^{+}}{2}}\langle A|\overline{\psi}_{i}(0,b^{-},\boldsymbol{b})\frac{\gamma^{+}}{2}\psi_{i}(0)|A\rangle\,.

(3)

This beam function is a priori a non-perturbative matrix element, which can be expressed in terms of perturbatively calculable Wilson coefficients
I_{i/j}

and parton distribution functions
f_{j/A}
using a light-cone operator product expansion:

B_{i/A}(x,\boldsymbol{b})=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\!\frac{d\xi}{\xi}I_{i/j}(\xi,\boldsymbol{b})f_{j/A}(x/\xi)+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}|\boldsymbol{b}|)\,.

(4)

The soft function describes multiple soft gluon radiation with a constraint on the total
\boldsymbol{q}_{T}
. It is given by the vacuum matrix element

S(\boldsymbol{b})=\frac{\mathrm{tr}}{N_{c}}\langle\Omega|\mathrm{T}\{Y_{\bar{n}}^{\dagger}Y_{n}(0,0,\boldsymbol{b})\}\overline{\mathrm{T}}\{Y_{n}^{\dagger}Y_{\bar{n}}(0)\}|\Omega\rangle\,,

(5)

where
Y_{n}(x)=\mathrm{P}\exp(\mathrm{i}g\int_{-\infty}^{0}ds\,A(x+sn))
is a path-ordered semi-infinite lightlike Wilson line.

For
\text{N}^{3}
LO
accuracy, we need the third order corrections to the perturbative beam function
I_{i/j}(x,\boldsymbol{b})
, soft function and hard function. The hard function has been known to three loops for some time
Baikov et al. 2009
;
Lee et al. 2010
;
Gehrmann et al. 2010
. The calculation of the beam and soft function is less straightforward, due to the presence of rapidity divergences
Collins 2008
, which only disappear in physical cross sections. Various approaches for rapidity regularization have been adopted in the literature to obtain the beam and soft function at NNLO
Catani and Grazzini 2012
;
Catani et al. 2012b
;
Gehrmann et al. 2012
;
Gehrmann et al. 2014
;
Echevarria et al. 2016
;
Luo et al. 2019
;
Luo et al. 2020a
;
Gutierrez-Reyes et al. 2019
. At
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, the scale dependence of perturbative beam and soft functions are completely fixed by renormalisation group (RG) evolution in SCET; see, e.g.,
Chen et al. 2019
;
Billis et al. 2021b
. The initial conditions of this RG evolution form the genuine
\text{N}^{3}
LO
contributions, and require
calculation to this order in SCET. Very recently,
this was accomplished in a series of works for the soft function
Li and Zhu 2017
and the beam functions
Luo et al. 2020b
;
Ebert et al. 2020
;
Luo et al. 2021
, using the
rapidity regulator proposed in
Li et al. 2020
. These newly available results provide the key ingredients for applying
q_{T}
-subtraction to processes with colorless final states at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
. The perturbative beam functions are expressed in terms of harmonic polylogarithms
Remiddi and Vermaseren 2000
up to weight 5, which can be
evaluated numerically with standard tools
Gehrmann and Remiddi 2001
.

The resolved contribution above the
q_{T}^{\text{cut}}
for
\text{N}^{3}
LO
Drell-Yan production contains the same ingredients of the NNLO
calculation with one extra jet.
Fully differential NNLO contributions for Drell-Yan-plus-jet production have been computed in
Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2016a
;
Boughezal et al. 2016a
;
Boughezal et al. 2016b
.
The application to
\text{N}^{3}
LO

q_{T}
-subtraction further requires stable fixed-order predictions at small
q_{T}

Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2016b
;
Bizoń et al. 2018
;
Bizon et al. 2019
, enabling the
cancellation of the
q_{T}^{\text{cut}}
between resolved and unresolved contributions to sufficient accuracy.
In this Letter, we employ the antenna subtraction method
Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2005a
;
Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2005b
;
Daleo et al. 2007
;
Currie et al. 2013
to compute
Drell-Yan production above
q_{T}^{\text{cut}}
up to NNLO in perturbation theory,
implemented in the parton-level event generator NNLO

JET

Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2016a
;
Gehrmann-De Ridder et al. 2016b
.
To achieve stable and reliable fixed order predictions down to the
q_{T}\sim 0.4
GeV region, NNLO

JET

has been developing dedicated optimizations of its phase space generation based on the work in
Chen et al. 2019
. This ensures sufficient coverage in
the multiply unresolved regions required for the
q_{T}
-subtraction.

IV
Conclusion and outlook

In this Letter, we calculated for the first time the di-lepton rapidity distribution for Drell-Yan production through virtual photon exchange to third order in perturbative QCD. We employed the
q_{T}
-subtraction method at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, by combining results from NNLO Drell-Yan production at large
q_{T}

and leading-power factorised predictions from SCET at small
q_{T}
. Both contributions are matched at a phase space slicing cut
q_{T}^{\rm cut}
,
and the cancellation of the leading power
q_{T}^{\rm cut}
dependence in the full result provides a strong check.
Our results firmly establish for the first time the applicability of
q_{T}
-subtraction at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, without any input from a previous inclusive calculation. This opens the door for the application of
q_{T}
-subtraction at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
to more complicated final states, either with fiducial final state cuts or for more complex processes.

The newly derived di-lepton rapidity distribution at
\text{N}^{3}
LO
also opens up an alternative route to

\text{N}^{3}
LO
corrections to Drell-Yan type fiducial cross sections. By repeating our calculation for all
Born-type angular coefficients
Collins and Soper 1977
in the Drell-Yan process,
inclusive predictions that are fully differential in the Born-level lepton kinematics can be obtained. These represent the integrated counterterm
contributions for a fully differential projection-to-Born (P2B) calculation
Cacciari et al. 2015
at
\text{N}^{3}
LO

Currie et al. 2018
;
Gehrmann et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2021
.

For total Drell-Yan cross section, our results are in excellent agreement with a previous calculation
Duhr et al. 2020a
. We found that
\text{N}^{3}
LO
corrections are significant over the full rapidity region. They are largely rapidity independent, indicating only very small corrections to distributions that are normalized to the total
cross section. Moreover, perturbative uncertainties estimated from scale variation do not overlap between NNLO and
\text{N}^{3}
LO
, indicating an underestimate of perturbative uncertainties at NNLO.

To apply our results in precision phenomenology, one needs to supplement them by contributions from
Z
boson exchange and
Z
-photon interference. They give rise to new subprocesses taht are infrared finite in the small
q_{T}
limit, and therefore one can apply the
\text{N}^{3}

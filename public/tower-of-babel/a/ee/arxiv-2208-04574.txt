A linear isotropic Cosserat shell model including terms up to O ⁡ ( h 5 ) O(h^{5}) . Existence and uniqueness
Ionel-Dumitrel Ghiba, and, Mircea Bîrsan, and, Patrizio Neff

Abstract
In this paper we derive the linear elastic Cosserat shell model incorporating effects up to order O ⁡ ( h 5 ) O(h^{5}) in the shell thickness h h as a particular case of the recently introduced geometrically nonlinear elastic Cosserat shell model. The existence and uniqueness of the solution is proven in suitable admissible sets. To this end, inequalities of Korn-type for shells are established which allow to show coercivity in the Lax-Milgram theorem. We are also showing an existence and uniqueness result for a truncated O ⁡ ( h 3 ) O(h^{3}) model. Main issue is the suitable treatment of the curved reference configuration of the shell. Some connections to the classical Koiter membrane-bending model are highlighted. Keywords: Cosserat shell, micropolar shell, 6-parameter resultant shell, in-plane drill rotations, isotropy, existence of minimisers, linear theories

Introduction

In this paper we consider the linearised formulation of the geometrically nonlinear Cosserat shell model including terms up to order
O(h^{5})
in the shell-thickness
h
proposed previously in
[
27
]

[
22
,
23
]
. The idea is to model a shell-like body as a deformable two-dimensional surface endowed with directors assigned to every point. In this respect, there are several approaches in literature.
For instance, Green and Naghdi
[
35
]
have elaborated a shell theory using two-dimensional surfaces endowed with a single deformable director also called
Cosserat surfaces
. The theory of Cosserat surfaces has been presented in the monograph
[
44
]
and the linearised theory has been investigated in a number of papers
[
24
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

Another direct approach to shell theory, also called the theory of
simple shells
(or
directed surfaces
), describes the shell-like body as a deformable surface endowed with an independent triad of orthonormal vectors connected to each point of the surface. The triad of directors characterizes the
orientation
of material points and introduces thus the
microrotation tensor
.
The theory of simple shells has been presented by Zhilin and Altenbach in
[
50
,
4
,
5
,
51
]
and a mathematical study of the linearised equations for this model is included in the papers
[
11
,
12
]
.

In
[
27
]
we have established a novel geometrically nonlinear Cosserat shell model including terms up to order
O(h^{5})
in the shell-thickness
h
. The dimensional descent was obtained starting with a 3D-parent Cosserat model and assuming an appropriate 8-parameter ansatz for the shell-deformation through the thickness. This is the derivation approach and it has allowed us to arrive at specific novel strain and curvature measures. In this way, we obtained a kinematical model which is equivalent to the kinematical model of 6-parameter shells. Nevertheless, the theory of 6-parameter shells was developed for shell-like bodies made of Cauchy materials, see the monographs
[
34
,
16
]
or the papers
[
26
,
40
]
, but our model is expressed in terms of the accepted measures for the bending and the change of curvature (cf. the assertion of Acharya
[
1
]
and Anicic and Legér
[
7
]
, respectively, see also
[
45
]
). For recent linear Naghdi shell models
[
32
]
which do not incorporate dedicated Cosserat effects we refer to
[
49
,
48
]
, while for a two-dimensional model of elastic shell-like body derived from the three-dimensional linearized micropolar elasticity by using the asymptotic expansion method we refer to
[
3
]
.

For all our proposed new linearised models we show existence and uniqueness. Existence results for the linearised equations of 6-parameter shells have been proved in
[
25
]
.
We refer to the review paper
[
6
]
for a detailed presentation of various approaches and developments concerning Cosserat-type shell theories and to the books
[
18

19

20

for shell theories in the classical linear elasticity framework.

In the linearised version of our Cosserat shell model we obtain the same linear strain measures as in the theory of 6-parameter shells, due to the fact that the kinematical structure is equivalent, since there is an explicit dependence of the internal energy density on the change of curvature measure and on the bending measure.

\displaystyle\,{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}^{\infty}({\rm sym}\,X)+\mu_{\rm c}\lVert\mathrm{skew}\,X\rVert^{2}\quad\ \forall\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

(2.14)

\displaystyle{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}^{\infty}(S):=

\displaystyle\,\mu\,\lVert S\rVert^{2}+\,\dfrac{\lambda\,\mu}{\lambda+2\,\mu}\,\big[\mathrm{tr}(S)\big]^{2}\qquad\quad\,\forall\,S\,\in{\rm Sym}(3),

it follows that there exists positive constants

\displaystyle C_{1}^{+}\lVert S\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle C_{1}^{+}\lVert{\rm sym}\,X\rVert^{2}+\mu_{\rm c}\,\lVert{\rm skew}\,X\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\,W_{\mathrm{shell}}(X)\geq\,c_{1}^{+}\lVert{\rm sym}\,X\rVert^{2}+\mu_{\rm c}\,\lVert{\rm skew}\,X\rVert^{2}\quad\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

(2.15)

\displaystyle C_{2}^{+}\lVert X\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\,W_{\mathrm{curv}}(X)\geq\,c_{2}^{+}\lVert X\rVert^{2}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}.

Here,
c_{1}^{+}
and

denote the smallest and the largest eigenvalues, respectively, of the quadratic form

3.1
Linearized strain measures in the Cosserat shell model

We express the total midsurface deformation

\displaystyle m(x_{1},x_{2})=y_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})+v(x_{1},x_{2}),

(3.1)

with
v:\omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the infinitesimal shell-midsurface displacement. For the elastic rotation tensor
\overline{Q}_{e,s}\in\rm{SO}(3)
there is a skew-symmetric matrix

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{\vartheta}:={\rm Anti}(\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2},\vartheta_{3}):=\footnotesize\begin{pmatrix}0&-\vartheta_{3}&\vartheta_{2}\\
\vartheta_{3}&0&-\vartheta_{1}\\
-\vartheta_{2}&\vartheta_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{so}(3),\quad\qquad{\rm Anti}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathfrak{so}(3),

(3.2)

where
\vartheta={\rm axl}(\overline{A}_{\vartheta})
denotes the axial vector of
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
, such that

\overline{Q}_{e,s}:=\exp(\overline{A}_{\vartheta})\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\,\overline{A}_{\vartheta}^{k}\;=\;{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta}+\textrm{h.o.t.}

The tensor field
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
is the infinitesimal elastic microrotation. Here, “h.o.t” stands for terms of order higher than linear with respect to
v
and
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
. Then, we can expand

\overline{Q}_{e,s}^{T}\nabla m-\nabla y_{0}=({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta}^{T}+\textrm{h.o.t.})(\nabla v+\nabla y_{0})-\nabla y_{0}=\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}+\textrm{h.o.t.}.

(3.3)

Since
\nabla y_{0}
is given, the first order term in (
3.3
) is linear in
v
and
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
. Correspondingly, we get from the non-symmetric
shell strain tensor
(which characterises both the in-plane deformation and the transverse shear deformation)

\mathcal{E}_{m,s}=(\overline{Q}_{e,s}^{T}\nabla m-\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

the linearization

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{E}_{m,s}^{\rm{lin}}&=&(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}=(\partial_{x_{1}}v-\vartheta\times a_{1}\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}v-\vartheta\times a_{2}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}\;\not\in{\rm Sym}(3).\end{array}

(3.4)

Our aim now is to express all the deformation measures and the linearised models in terms of
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
as well as in terms of its axial vector
\vartheta
. The following definitions are used to express these quantities in terms of
\vartheta
.

For any column vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any matrix
M=(M_{1}|M_{2}|M_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we define the cross-product

\displaystyle q\times M:=

\displaystyle\,(q\times M_{1}\,|\,q\times M_{2}\,|\,q\times M_{3})\qquad\qquad(\mbox{operates on columns})\quad\textrm{and}\vskip 6.0pt

(3.5)

\displaystyle M^{T}\times q^{T}:=

\displaystyle\,-(q\times M)^{T}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\mbox{operates on rows}).

Note that
M
can also be a
3\times 2
matrix, the definition remains the same.
Let us note some properties of these operations: for any column vectors
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any matrices
M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
(or
\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}
) we have

\displaystyle(q_{1}\times M)\,q_{2}=

\displaystyle\,\,q_{1}\times(M\,q_{2})\,,\qquad q_{1}^{T}(q_{2}\times M)=\,(q_{1}\times q_{2})^{T}\,M=(q_{1}^{T}\times q_{2}^{T})\,M=-q_{2}^{T}(q_{1}\times M)

(3.6)

and, more general

\displaystyle(q_{1}\times M)N=

\displaystyle\,q_{1}\,\times(M\,N)\,,\qquad N^{T}(q_{2}\times M)=\,-(q_{2}\times N)^{T}\,M=(N^{T}\times q_{2}^{T})\,M\,.

(3.7)

With these relations, the infinitesimal microrotation
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}
can be expressed as

\overline{A}_{\vartheta}:=\vartheta\times{\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}={\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}\times\vartheta^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}.

(3.8)

It is possible to write the linearised strain measures in a simplified form. The relation
\mathcal{E}_{m,s}^{\rm{lin}}=(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

turns into

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{E}_{m,s}^{\rm{lin}}&=&(\nabla v-\vartheta\times\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}=\big[(\nabla v\,|\,0)-\vartheta\times(\nabla y_{0}\,|\,0)\big]\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}.\end{array}

(3.9)

Doing the same for the elastic
shell bending-curvature tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{e,s}:=\Big(\mbox{axl}(\overline{Q}_{e,s}^{T}\partial_{x_{1}}\overline{Q}_{e,s})\,|\,\mbox{axl}(\overline{Q}_{e,s}^{T}\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{Q}_{e,s})\,|\,0\Big)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}

since

\displaystyle=-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}-\overline{A}_{\vartheta})\nabla[({\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}}_{3}+\overline{A}_{\vartheta})n_{0}]-{\rm II}_{y_{0}}=(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla n_{0}-\nabla(\overline{A}_{\vartheta}n_{0}))

(3.23)

\displaystyle=-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\,n_{0}=-(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\partial_{x_{1}}\vartheta\times n_{0}\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}\vartheta\times n_{0})

and, see (

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}&=&(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(n_{0}\times\nabla\vartheta)\;\qquad\mbox{or}\qquad\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}=-(n_{0}\times\nabla y_{0})^{T}\nabla\vartheta\;.\end{array}

(3.24)

To obtain a comparison with the classical linear Koiter-shell model, let us first present an alternative form of
\mathcal{G}^{\rm lin}

From (

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{G}^{\rm lin}&=&(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0})\;=\;(\nabla y_{0})^{T}(\partial_{x_{1}}u+a_{1}\times\vartheta\;|\;\partial_{x_{2}}u+a_{2}\times\vartheta)\\

(3.25)

Remember that

We rewrite the minimization problem (
3.2

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\,=\big[(\nabla v\,|\,0)-\vartheta\times(\nabla y_{0}\,|\,0)\big]\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\qquad\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)=(\nabla\vartheta\,|\,0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1},

(3.31)

the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}^{\rm{lin}}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}\times\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}\to\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\rm{lin}}((v,\vartheta),(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})):=\int_{\omega}\!\!\Big[

\displaystyle\Big(h+{\rm K}\,\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\Big)\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\,-{\rm K}\,\dfrac{h^{5}}{80}\Big)\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\,{\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\,{\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\big)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{h^{3}}{6}\mathrm{H}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}){\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\big)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{h^{3}}{6}\mathrm{H}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}),\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta){\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\big)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}){\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})){\rm B}_{y_{0}}\big)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{shell}}\big(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}),(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta){\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\,\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)){\rm B}_{y_{0}}\big)

(3.32)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{h^{5}}{80}\,\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{mp}}\big((\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\,{\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)){\rm B}_{y_{0}},(\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\,{\rm B}_{y_{0}}+{\rm C}_{y_{0}}\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})){\rm B}_{y_{0}}\big)

\displaystyle+\,\Big(h-{\rm K}\,\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\Big)\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{curv}}\big(\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\dfrac{h^{3}}{12}\,-{\rm K}\,\dfrac{h^{5}}{80}\Big)\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{curv}}\big(\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta){\rm B}_{y_{0}},\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}){\rm B}_{y_{0}}\,\big)

\displaystyle+\,\dfrac{h^{5}}{80}\,\,\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{curv}}\big(\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta){\rm B}_{y_{0}}^{2},\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}){\rm B}_{y_{0}}^{2}\big)\Big]{\rm det}(\nabla y_{0}|n_{0})da.

and the linear operator

\displaystyle{\Pi}^{\rm lin}:\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\qquad{\Pi}^{\rm lin}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})=\overline{\Pi}^{\rm lin}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}).

(3.33)

Then the weak form of the equilibrium problem of the linear theory of Cosserat shells including terms up to order

(u,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\rm{lin}}((v,\vartheta),(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta}))={\Pi}^{\rm lin}(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\qquad\forall(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{\vartheta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}.

(3.34)

Theorem 3.2
.

[Existence result for the linear theory including terms up to order
O(h^{5})
]

Assume that the linear operator

{\Pi}^{\rm lin}
is bounded and
that the following conditions concerning the initial configuration are satisfied:
\,y_{0}:\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a continuous injective mapping and

\displaystyle{y}_{0}

\displaystyle\in{\rm H}^{1}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\qquad\nabla_{x}\Theta(0)\in{\rm L}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}),\qquad\qquad\det[\nabla_{x}\Theta(0)]\geq\,a_{0}>0\,,

(3.35)

where
a_{0}
is a constant.
Then, for sufficiently small values of the thickness
h
such that

\displaystyle h\max\{\sup_{x\in\omega}|\kappa_{1}|,\sup_{x\in\omega}|\kappa_{2}|\}<\alpha\qquad\text{with}\qquad\alpha<\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(29-\sqrt{761})}\simeq 0.97083

(3.36)

and for constitutive coefficients such that
\mu>0,\,\mu_{\rm c}>0
,
2\,\lambda+\mu>0
,
b_{1}>0
,
b_{2}>0
and
b_{3}>0
, the problem (
3.34
) admits a unique solution

(u,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the Lax-Milgram theorem. Since the thickness
h
satisfies (
3.36
), then due to the results established in
[
28
,
29
]
for the geometrically nonlinear model, the internal energy is automatically coercive in terms of the linearised strain and curvature variables
\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta),\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\in{\rm L^{2}}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
, in the sense that for all
(v,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a constant
a_{1}^{+}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\rm{lin}}((v,\vartheta),({v},{\vartheta}))\geq\,a_{1}^{+}\,\big(\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\,\big),

(3.37)

where

a_{1}^{+}
depends on the given constitutive coefficients.

Since the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}^{\rm lin}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}
, as well as the linear operator
{\Pi}^{\rm lin}
, it remains to prove that the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}^{\rm lin}
is coercive on
\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}
. To this aim, it only remains to prove
that for all
(v,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{A}_{\rm lin}
there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\geq c\,(\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{{\rm H}^{1}_{0}(\omega)}+\lVert\vartheta\rVert^{2}_{{\rm H}^{1}_{0}(\omega)}).

(3.38)

The first step is to use the following alternative form of the linearised strain measures, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)=(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1},\qquad\text{
where}\qquad\overline{A}_{\vartheta}={\rm Anti}\,\vartheta.

(3.39)

The next step is to remark that, because the lifted
3\times 3
quantity

\widehat{\rm I}_{y_{0}}\>\,=\,({\nabla y_{0}}|n_{0})^{T}({\nabla y_{0}}|n_{0})\in{\rm Sym}(3)
is positive definite and also it’s inverse is positive definite, using also the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1},(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T},(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\;\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-T}(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)^{T},(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)^{T}\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigl\langle\widehat{\rm I}_{y_{0}}^{-1}(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)^{T},(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)^{T}\bigr\rangle

(3.40)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{0}\bigl\langle(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0),(\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;|\;0)\bigr\rangle=\lambda_{0}\bigl\langle\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0},\nabla v-\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{0}\Big[\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}+\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}-2\,\bigl\langle\nabla v,\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\;\bigr\rangle\Big]\geq\lambda_{0}\Big[(1-\varepsilon)\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}\Big],

for all
\varepsilon>0
, where
0<\lambda_{0}<1
is the smallest eigenvalue of the positive definite matrix
\widehat{\rm I}^{-1}_{y_{0}}
, over
\omega
.

Similarly, we deduce that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\,\lVert(\nabla\vartheta\,|\,0)\;[\nabla\Theta\,]^{-1}\rVert^{2}\geq\lambda_{0}\,\lVert(\nabla\vartheta\,|\,0)\rVert^{2}=\lambda_{0}\,\lVert\nabla\vartheta\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert{\rm Anti}\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\vartheta\rVert^{2}

(3.41)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}{\rm Anti}\,\vartheta\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}.

Moreover, since
\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\in{\rm H}_{0}^{1}(\omega;\mathfrak{so}(3))
, we deduce the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq c_{p}\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)},

(3.42)

where
c_{p}>0
is the constant depending only on
\omega
coming from the Poincaré inequality. Because the Frobenius norm is sub-multiplicative, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}\,\lVert\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}\sup_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\omega}}\,\lVert\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}

(3.43)

and therefore

\displaystyle\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\leq\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\,\sup_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\omega}}\,\lVert\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}.

(3.44)

Using (
3.41
), (
3.42
) and (
3.44
) we deduce

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}+\,\frac{c_{p}\,\lambda_{0}}{4}\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}+\,\frac{c_{p}\,\lambda_{0}}{4}\frac{1}{\sup_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\omega}}\lVert\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}}\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

(3.45)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}+\frac{c_{p}\,\lambda_{0}}{4\,\sup_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\omega}}{\rm tr}({\rm I}_{y_{0}})}\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{0}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}+\frac{c_{p}\,\lambda_{0}}{8\,\lambda_{M}}\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)},

where
\lambda_{M}>1
is the largest eigenvalue of the given positive definite matrix
\widehat{\rm I}_{y_{0}}
, over
\omega
.
Hence with (
3.3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{0}\Big[(1-\varepsilon)\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{c_{p}}{8\,\lambda_{M}}\right)\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

(3.46)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\lVert\,\partial_{x_{\alpha}}\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\Big]

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Now we are looking for some
\varepsilon>0
such that in parallel

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{c_{p}}{8\,{\rm tr}({\rm I}_{y_{0}})}>0,\qquad\text{i.e.},\qquad 1>\varepsilon>\frac{1}{1+\frac{c_{p}}{8\,{\rm tr}({\rm I}_{y_{0}})}}.

(3.47)

Since,
c_{p}>0
and
\lambda_{M}>0
, such a constant
\varepsilon>0
exists and there is a positive constant
c_{1}>0
such that the following Korn-type inequality, see
[
9
]
for some related Korn-type inequalities in the Cosserat theory with a deformable vector, is satisfied

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\Big[\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\nabla y_{0}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\nabla\overline{A}_{\vartheta}\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\Big[\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\nabla{\rm Anti}\,\vartheta\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\Big],

(3.48)

i.e., a positive constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{E}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\mathcal{K}^{\rm{lin}}(v,\vartheta)\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle\geq c\Big[\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}+\lVert\nabla\vartheta\rVert^{2}_{{\rm L}^{2}(\omega)}\Big]\qquad\forall\ (v,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{A}^{\rm lin}.

(3.49)

Hence, the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}^{\rm lin}
is coercive and the Lax-Milgram theorem leads to the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

While the relation between
\mathcal{G}^{\rm{lin}}
and
\;\mathcal{G}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}
holds in the general case, we are able to find a simple explicit relation between
\;\mathcal{R}^{\rm{lin}}\;
and
\;\mathcal{R}_{\rm{Koiter}}^{\rm{lin}}
only in the case of the constrained Cosserat-shell model. This is not surprising, since only symmetric stress tensors are taken into account in the classical linear Koiter model, i.e., the internal strain energy does not depend on the skew-symmetric part of the considered strain measures (since it is work conjugate to the skew-symmetric part of the stress tensor). In addition, the linear Koiter model does not consider extra degrees of freedom.

(the classical bending strain measure, also known as the change of curvature tensor) does not represent the unique choice and that some other modified expressions of the classical bending tensor may be more suitable in the modelling of a shell.

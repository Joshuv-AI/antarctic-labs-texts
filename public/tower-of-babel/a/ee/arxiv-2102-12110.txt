Unity Product Graph of Some Commutative Rings
Mohammad Hassan Mudaber, Nor Haniza Sarmin & Ibrahim Gambo

Abstract
A graph is an instrument which is extensively utilized to model various problems in different fields. Up to date, many graphs have been developed to represent algebraic structures, particularly rings in order to study their properties. In this article, by focusing on commutative ring R R , we introduce a new notion of unity product graph associated with R R and its complement. In addition, we prove that if the number of vertices of the unity product graph is at least 2, then the graph is disconnected, while its complement graph is connected. Furthermore, it is shown that there are some commutative rings with such as Boolean ring and the Cartesian product of Boolean rings in which their associated unity product graphs are trivial. Consequently, some results are established to determine the number of isolated vertices in unity product graph. We also characterize commutative rings with unity in which their associated unity product and complement unity product graphs are empty graph and complete graph, respectively. Finally, we prove some results on the properties of the unity product graph and its complement in terms of girth, diameter, radius, dominating number, chromatic number and clique number as well as planarity and Hamiltonian. Keywords: Commutative Rings, unity product Graph, Complement unity product Graph

Unity Product Graph of a Ring and Some Examples

In this section, we define the unity product graph and the complement unity product graph associated with a ring
R
and provide some examples. The definition of unity product graph is fist given.

Definition 2.1
.

Unity Product Graph

Let
U(R)
denote the set of unit elements of a ring
R
. Then, the unity product graph associated with
R
, denoted by
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
, is a graph that has the vertex set
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=U(R)
and for any distinct
r_{i},r_{j}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
,
\{r_{i},r_{j}\}
is an edge of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
if and only if
r_{i}\cdot r_{j}=e
.

In the next definition, we introduce the complement unity product graph of a ring
R
.

Definition 2.2
.

Complement Unity Product Graph

Let
U(R)
denote the set of unit elements of a ring
R
. Then, the complement unity product graph associated with
R
, denoted by
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
, is a graph that has the vertex set
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))=U(R)
and for any distinct
r_{i},r_{j}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))
,
\{r_{i},r_{j}\}
is an edge of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
if and only if
r_{i}\cdot r_{j}\neq e
.

Connectivity of Unity Product Graph and Its Complement

In this section, the connectedness property of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
and
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
are investigated by establishing some theorems, propositions and corollaries.
The first theorem states that if
R
is a commutative ring with unity and
|U(R)|=1
, then its unity product graph is a trivial graph.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be the unity product graph associated with ring
R
. If
R\cong B
or
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots\times B
, where
B
is a Boolean ring, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is trivial.

Proof.

For
R\cong B
the proof is obvious. Assume
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots\times B
is given as the Cartesian product of
n
Boolean rings, which contains unity element
e=(1,1,1,\cdots,1)
. Thus,
U(R)=\{r_{i}\in R:r_{i}\cdot r_{j}=(1,1,1,\cdots,1),\ \textrm{where}\ 1\leq i\leq j\leq n\}
. By the definition of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
, the vertex set
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=U(R)=\{e\}
and the edge set
E(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=\emptyset
. Hence,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is a graph containing exactly one vertex, which is a trivial graph.
∎

The result obtained in Theorem 3.1 can be generalized to a unity product graph associated with ring
R
which is isomorphic to the Cartesian product of infinite Boolean ring
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots
as presented in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be the unity product graph associated with ring
R
. If
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots
, where
B
is a Boolean ring, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is trivial.

The results are presented in Corollary 3.2 as a consequence of Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.1 on the complement unity product graph,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
be the complement unity product graph associated with a ring
R
. Then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is trivial if
R
is isomorphic to any of the following Boolean rings:

(1)
R\cong B

(2)
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots\times B

(3)
R\cong B\times B\times B\times\cdots

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be the unity product graph associated with a finite commutative ring
R
with unity. If
|V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))|\geq 2
, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is disconnected.

Proof.

Assume
R
is a finite commutative ring with unity, thus
R
contains finite elements as
R=\{r_{i}:i=1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
. Suppose that
U(R)
denote the set of units of
R
, by the definition of unity product graph,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=U(R)
.
Assume
|V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))|\geq 2
. Since
R
is a commutative ring with unity, thus there exists a vertex
e
in
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
such that
e\cdot r_{i}\neq 1
for all
e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
. This shows that there is no path between the vertex
e
and the vertex
r_{i}
in
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
. Similarly, for every
e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
, there is a unique
r_{j}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
, such that
r_{i}\cdot r_{j}=e
. This shows that
r_{i}
is only adjacent to
r_{j}
. If
r_{m}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
, where
i<m<j
, then
r_{m}
is not connected to
r_{i}
, since
r_{i}\cdot r_{m}\neq e
. Hence,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is disconnected.
∎

The result obtained in Theorem 3.2 can be applied to the unity product graph associated with an infinite commutative ring with unity. This result is stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be the unity product graph associated with an infinite commutative ring
R
with unity. If
|V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))|\geq 2
, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is disconnected.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
be the complement unity product graph associated with a finite commutative ring
R
with unity. If
|V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))|\geq 2
, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is connected.

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is a finite commutative ring with unity, i.e.
R=\{r_{i}:i=1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
. Let
U(R)
denote the set of units of
R
, thus by the definition of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)=U(R)
. According to vertices adjacency property of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
,
\{r_{i},r_{j}\}
is an edge of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
if and only if
r_{i}\cdot r_{j}\neq e
. Since,
e
is an isolated vertex in
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
, thus
e\cdot r_{i}\neq e
for all
e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
. This shows that there is a path between the vertex
e
and the vertex
r_{i}
in
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
. Hence,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is connected.
∎

The result obtained in Theorem 3.3 can be applied to the unity product graph associated with an infinite commutative ring with unity. This result is stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
be the complement unity product graph associated with an infinite commutative ring
R
with unity. If
|V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))|\geq 2
, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is connected.

In the following example, we show that
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is connected and
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is disconnected, for a commutative ring without unity.

Example 3.1
.

Let
R=\{ai:a\in\mathbb{Z},\ i^{2}=-1\}
. Then,
U(R)=\{-i,i\}
. By the definition of unity product graph,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=\{-i,i\}
and
E(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=\{\{-i,i\}\}
. Therefore,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
is connected. However, according to the definition of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
, the vertex set
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))=\{-i,i\}
and the edge set
E(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R))=\emptyset
. Therefore,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}c}(R)
is disconnected.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be a unity product graph associated with a ring
R
. Then,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
contains two isolated vertices, if
R
is isomorphic to any of the following:

(1)
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
p
is an odd prime.

(2)
R\cong\mathbb{F}
, where
\mathbb{F}
is a field with
Char(\mathbb{F})=0
.

Proof.

(1). Assume
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
p
is an odd prime which contains the unity element
e
. Thus
R
can be displayed as
R=\{r_{i}:i=1,2,3,\cdots,p\}
. Since
p
is an odd prime, thus
|U(R)|=p-1
. According to the definition of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=U(R)
.
Since
e\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
, thus
e\cdot r_{i}\neq e
for all
e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
. Therefore
e
is an isolated vertex. Since
p-1
is even, hence there exist
\frac{p-3}{2}
pairs of vertices
r_{i}
and
r_{j}
such that
r_{i}\cdot r_{j}=e
. This implies that
r_{p}
is isolated.

(2). Assume
R\cong\mathbb{F}
, where
\mathbb{F}
is a field with
Char(\mathbb{F})=0
. Thus
R
is represented as
R=\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
. By the definition of a field, every non-zero
r_{i}
has a unique multiplicative inverse, thus
|U(R)|=\infty
. According to the definition of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=U(R)
.
Since
e\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
and
e\cdot r_{i}\neq e
for all
e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
, therefore
e
is an isolated vertex. Similarly, there exists a vertex
-e\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
such that
-e\cdot r_{i}\neq e
for all
-e\neq r_{i}\in V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
. Hence,
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
contains two isolated vertices of
e
and
-e
, respectively.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be a unity product graph associated with the ring
R
. If
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2^{m}}
, where
m\geq 3
, then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
contains four isolated vertices.

Proof.

Suppose
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2^{m}}
, where
m\geq 3
. Thus
|U(R)|=2^{m-1}
. By the definition of unity product graph,
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))=\{r_{i}:r_{i}\cdot r_{j}=e\ \textrm{for all}\ r_{i}\neq r_{j}\}
, which has
2^{m-1}
elements. It follows from Theorem 3.4 (1) that
e
and
r_{2^{m}}
are isolated vertices. Since the number of vertices of
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R
is equal to
2^{m-1}
, which is even for all
m\geq 3
, thus there exist two vertices
r_{k}
and
r_{l}
in
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
such that
\frac{r_{k}+r_{l}}{2}
is the median of all elements in
V(\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R))
and satisfy

Planarity and Hamiltonian of Unity Product Graph and its Complement

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R
be a finite commutative ring with unity and
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)
be the unity product graph associated with
R
. Then
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)

Proof.

Assume
R
is a finite commutative ring with unity, then
R
can be represented as
R=\{r_{i}:i=1,2,3,\cdots,n\}
. By Theorem 3.6, the unity product graph is either
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)=2K_{1}+(m-2)K_{2}

or
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)=mK_{1}

\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(R)

7
Conclusion

In this paper the unity product graph and its complement associated with some commutative rings with unity are investigated. The results obtained in this research show that the unity product graph associated with commutative rings with unity, which has at least two vertices, is always disconnected, while its complement graph is connected. However, if the commutative rings contain no unity element, these results are not always true. Some results are also established which determine the number of isolated vertices in unity product graph. Moreover, some properties of unity product graph and its complement in terms of girth, diameter, radius, dominating number, chromatic number and clique number, planarity and Hamiltonian are presented.

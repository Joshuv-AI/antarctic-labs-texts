Deformation of a planar ferromagnetic elastic ribbon Journal: Journal of Elasticity
G. R. Krishna Chand Avatar, Vivekanand Dabade

Abstract
In this paper we explore the influence of magnetisation on the deformation of planar ferromagnetic elastic ribbons. We begin the investigation by deriving the leading-order magnetic energy associated with a curved planar ferromagnetic elastic ribbon. The sum of the magnetic and the elastic energy is the total energy of the ribbon. We derive the equilibrium equations by taking the first variation of the total energy. We then systematically determine and analyse solutions to these equilibrium equations under various canonical boundary conditions. We also determine the stability of the equilibrium solutions. Comparing our findings with the well-studied Euler’s elastica provides insights into the magnetic effects on the deformation behaviour of elastic ribbons. Our analysis contributes to a deeper understanding of the interplay between magnetisation and the mechanical response of planar ferromagnetic structures, and offers valuable insights for both theoretical and practical applications.

Introduction

Ferromagnetic ribbons and slender structures exhibit complex coupling between magnetism and deformation or elasticity and they offer the potential to achieve substantial deformations using remote external magnetic fields. Novel deformations, unachievable in purely elastic slender structures, find interesting applications in advanced actuators and sensors fabricated from ferromagnetic slender structures
samourgkanidis2020characterization
;
Ramachandran2016
;
lum2016shape
. For instance, the concept of a stowed preloaded structure, which can transition into novel configurations upon the application of a small external magnetic field, holds promise for diverse applications in deployable structures. In this paper, we employ concepts drawn from the theory of micromagnetics and Euler’s elastica to construct a model aimed at analysing the planar deformation of ferromagnetic ribbons.

Motivated by the aforementioned applications and prospects of ferromagnetic slender structures, our study focuses on analysing the interplay between magnetism and deformation in ferromagnetic ribbons. We adopt a variational approach for this problem and begin our analysis by deriving the total energy of the ferromagnetic ribbon undergoing planar deformation. The total energy of a ferromagnetic slender ribbon is given by the sum of the elastic energy, magnetic energy of the ribbon and energy due to mechanical loading device. The elastic energy is equal to the bending energy, as we shall assume that the ribbon is inextensible. The rationale for selecting this particular model for the elastic energy of the ferromagnetic planar ribbon is justified in section
2.1
. The magnetic energy is formulated based on the theory of micromagnetics.

Micromagnetics is a continuum theory for magnetism that has proven to be a robust model capable of explaining and predicting the diverse array of magnetic domain structures observed in ferromagnetic materials
desimone2006recent
;
brown1963micromagnetics
. The magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})
is the primary variable in the micromagnetics functional. The magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})
is a unit-normed vector supported on a ferromagnetic body. The micromagnetics functional is expressed as the sum of exchange, magnetocrystalline anisotropy, magnetostatic (abbreviated as demag.), and Zeeman energy, see
desimone2006recent
and
dabade2019micromagnetics
. The exchange energy is characterized by a material constant known as the exchange constant, denoted as
A
. Similarly, the anisotropy energy is characterized by a material constant known as the anisotropy constant, denoted as
K_{a}
. The demag. and Zeeman energy are characterized by a material constant known as the magnetostatic energy density constant, denoted as
K_{d}
. Thus, the constants
A
,
K_{a}
, and
K_{d}
describe the ferromagnet according to the micromagnetics functional. We refer the reader to Section (
2.2
) for a concise discussion on the micromagnetics functional.

Ferromagnets are commonly categorised into soft and hard ferromagnets, depending on the relative magnitudes of
(K_{a})
and
(K_{d})
. In soft ferromagnets
K_{d}\gg K_{a}
, while in hard ferromagnets
K_{a}\gg K_{d}
. In this study, we investigate both soft and hard ferromagnetic ribbons. In soft ferromagnetic ribbons, the demag. energy influences the deformation of the ribbon. The demag. energy, strongly depends on the shape of the body and is computed by solving Maxwell’s equations of magnetostatics. Due to the non local nature of the demag. energy, calculating it is computationally expensive and challenging for general three dimensional bodies, as it requires the calculation of the magnetic field in the entire space surrounding the body. However, for slender structures, the leading order demag. energy is local in nature and assumes simpler forms, offering an opportunity to explore these problems (semi-) analytically. In hard ferromagnetic ribbons, the Zeeman energy influences the deformation of the ribbon. In such materials, magnetization remains fixed along specific preferred directions known as the easy axis of the material. The Zeeman energy seeks to orient the body such that these preferred directions are aligned with the external magnetic field. Hence, our analysis focuses on the deformation of a slender body resulting from the interplay of demag. energy and Zeeman energy along with elastic energy.

The demagnetization (and Zeeman) energy scales very differently compared to the elastic energy in bulk versus slender ferromagnets. This disparity in scaling leads to significantly larger displacements in slender ferromagnetic structures compared to bulk ferromagnets under an identical external magnetic field. In bulk ferromagnetic materials, the elastic energy density scales as the Young’s modulus
E\penalty\ (\sim 10^{11}J/m^{3})
, whereas the demag. energy scales as
K_{d}\penalty\ (\sim 10^{5}J/m^{3})
. Here,
K_{d}
and
E
represent the magnetostatic energy constant and Young’s modulus of the material, respectively, with both being material parameters. Bulk ferromagnetic solids (Iron, Nickel, Galfenol etc.) are known to deform when magnetised. This phenomenon is known as Joule magnetostriction. However, even under strong external magnetic fields, magnetostriction strains are small, typically in the order of
10^{-5}
. Our work does not consider Joule magnetostriction, we are primarily interested in understanding the deformation in slender ferromagnetic structures due to the effects of demag. energy and Zeeman energy. The elastic energy and magnetic energy are comparable in slender ferromagnetic structures, even though the elastic energy density is typically much greater than the magnetic energy density in bulk ferromagnetic materials. Hence, we can obtain large displacements in ferromagnetic slender structures while the strains in bulk ferromagnetic solids due to Joule magnetostriction are very small.

In slender ferromagnetic structures, the interplay between elastic and magnetic energy becomes significant. The dissimilar scaling of these energies with respect to the aspect ratio allows their magnitudes to become comparable. The leading-order demag. energy of a slender ferromagnetic ribbon scales as
\mathcal{O}(K_{d}atl)
, and the elastic energy scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{EI}{2l}\right)
. Here, thickness, width, and length of the ferromagnetic ribbon are denoted as
t
,
a
, and
l
, respectively, and
I=\frac{at^{3}}{12}
, is the area moment of inertia, see Fig
1
. Hence, ferromagnetic slender structures offer an opportunity to tune the aspect ratio, allowing the interaction between demag. energy and elastic energy. A balance of these energies implies that our analysis is valid for ribbons with an aspect ratio of
\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{E}{24K_{d}}}\right)
.

In this study, we consider a ferromagnetic planar ribbon composed of a ferromagnetic material such as Iron or Nickel. Our focus lies in investigating the influence of magnetization on ribbon deformation as the applied load varies quasi-statically. In particular, we shall study the two following cases:

•

Case 1. (Soft ferromagnet)
: Magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m(x)}
is spatially constant and does not change with deformation. This case characterises soft ferromagnetic materials such as Permalloy, with an external magnetic field large enough to saturate the magnetisation uniformly throughout the deformed planar ribbon.

•

Case 2. (Hard ferromagnet)
: Magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m(x)}
makes a constant angle with respect to the tangent of the deformed planar curve. Here, the tangent vector, and hence the magnetisation vector vary spatially as the slender body undergoes deformation. This case characterises hard ferromagnetic materials such as Neodymium or Samarium-Cobalt.

To recapitulate, the total energy of a one-dimensional ferromagnetic ribbon is the summation of its elastic energy, exchange energy, anisotropy energy, demag. energy, Zeeman energy, and mechanical loading device energy. Calculating the elastic energy, anisotropy energy, Zeeman energy, and mechanical loading device energy are straightforward. However, determining the demag. and exchange energies for a curved ferromagnetic ribbon requires computations to be performed within the curvilinear local material frame. We obtain the leading order demag. energy for a curved ferromagnetic ribbon by employing a local material frame aligned with the tangent line of the deformed curve. Working in the deformed configuration alleviates the need to define a pull-back for the magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m(x)}
and solving the Maxwell’s equations of magnetostatics in the reference configuration. Various pullbacks for magnetisation are defined in the literature, posing challenges in the rational selection of the appropriate one,
James2002
;
Keip2019
. The leading order demag. energy is local in nature and hence amenable for analysis. We also derive the exchange energy for our one dimensional ferromagnetic ribbon. Our exchange energy matches with findings reported in the field of curvilinear micromagnetics
streubel2016magnetism
;
sheka2022fundamentals
to leading order. With the effective energy functional of the ribbon derived, we proceed to the next step of determining the energy-minimizing deformations as the external load is increased in a quasi-static manner.

We determine the deformed configuration by solving the equilibrium equations obtained by taking the first variation of the total energy. The equilibrium equations show that the magnetisation produces a body couple along the length of the curve that is dependent on the local orientation of the curve, in addition to the conventional terms originating from elastic energy, see Eqn.
21
.
The equilibrium equations are solved numerically for various canonical boundary conditions, using
Auto
-07p, a standard software used in continuation and bifurcation problems
Doedel1991a
;
Doedel1991b
. Further, we also perform a stability analysis of our computed solutions. The stability analysis involves casting the second variation of the total energy as a Sturm-Liouville boundary value problem. The eigenvalues of this boundary value problem, as the load is varied continuously, are used to determine the stability of the deformed configurations.

We obtain the equilibrium path and the stability of the first few relevant modes, under quasi-static load control simulation. Our results provide us with a stable deformed state of the ferromagnetic ribbon, beginning from zero load and, in certain instances, a moderate tensile load, and extending to an arbitrarily large compressive load. Before proceeding into the main body of the paper, we compare and contextualize our work with the existing literature. Several researchers have studied the deformation of ferromagnetic ribbons under an external magnetic field, primarily focusing on cantilever configurations, that is with fixed-free boundary conditions. Some of the foundational research related to deformation of planar ferromagnetic ribbons traces its origins back to the 1960s, where Moon et al.
Moon1968
;
moon1984magneto
investigated the onset of buckling instabilities in a cantilevered beam-plate under magnetic loading
Moon1968
;
moon1984magneto
. The distributed magnetic torque is dependent on
\theta
. Here,
\theta
represents the angle formed by the local tangent with respect to the horizontal, see Figure
2
. Our model recovers this limit for
\theta\ll 1
. Furthermore, the scaling for critical magnetic load,
B_{c}\sim\left(\frac{l}{t}\right)^{2}
matches with our prediction (See Section
3.3
for detailed comparison). The buckling of slender ferromagnetic structures has also garnered attention in the recent past. Singh et al.
Singh2013
studied nonlinear elastic deformations in a vertically cantileverd Euler-Bernoulli beam with a permanent magnet attached to the free end. Their work explored supercritical and subcritical bifurcation behaviors under combined magnetic field and hard magnet influence. Gerbal et al.
Gerbal2015
investigated magnetoelastic buckling transition in ferromagnetic cantilevered rods. Similar to
Gerbal2015
, Wang et al.
Wang2020
developed a mathematical model for hard magnetic cantilevered beam and determined critical load and the mode deformation under external magnetic fields varying in intensity and direction. We have recovered the equilibrium equation corresponding to the hard magnetisation distribution of a magnetically loaded cantilevered beam which matches with that derived in
Gerbal2015
;
Wang2020
. Singh et al.
Singh2018
studied the occurrence of magnetically-induced instability in cantilever beams using small deflection assumptions. The main focus of these investigations has been to determine the critical external magnetic field at which the cantilever buckles, while either maintaining a fixed external load or having no load applied. In contrast, our work has centered on maintaining a constant external magnetic field while quasi-statically varying the applied load.

2.1
Mechanical energy

The free energy functional of ferromagnetic ribbon is equal to the sum of its magnetic energy, elastic energy and the loading device energy. Given our assumption that the ribbon is made of an inextensible ferromagnetic material, the primary source of the elastic energy is the bending energy. We denote the sum of the bending energy and the loading device energy as
\mathcal{E}_{\text{elastica}}
which is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{elastica}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l}EI\kappa^{2}(s)ds}_{\text{Elastic (or bending) energy}}+\penalty\ \underbrace{P\left(1-\int_{0}^{l}\cos\theta(s)ds\right)}_{\text{Loading energy}},

(4)

subject to the integral constraint

\begin{split}y(l)=\int_{0}^{l}\sin\theta(s)\penalty\ ds=0.\end{split}

(5)

Here,
\kappa(s)=\derivative{\theta(s)}{s}
is the bending curvature,
E
is the Young’s modulus,
I=\frac{at^{3}}{12}
is the area moment of inertia (
EI
is the bending stiffness of the cross section), and
P
is the horizontal load.

We use Kirchhoff rod theory to describe the deformation of our ferromagnetic ribbon. The Kirchhoff rod theory serves as an appropriate framework for describing the deformation of ribbons, when the dimensionless parameter
\bar{\kappa}\sim\frac{\kappa a^{2}}{t}\ll 1
, as discussed in
Audoly2021
. For a ferromagnetic ribbon that is considered in our work, we can estimate
\bar{\kappa}
as follows:

\bar{\kappa}=\frac{\kappa a^{2}}{t}\ll\frac{\kappa_{c}a^{2}}{t}=\left(\frac{a}{t}\right)^{2}\sqrt{\frac{K_{d}}{E}}
. Here,
\kappa_{c}
is the maximum curvature of the ferromagnetic ribbon and is estimated as
\kappa_{c}\sim\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\frac{K_{d}}{E}}\bigg)
, see section
6.4
for the derivation of
\kappa_{c}
. For a typical ferromagnetic material,
K_{d}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{5}\nicefrac{{J}}{{m^{3}}})
, and
E\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{11}\nicefrac{{J}}{{m^{3}}})
implying that
\frac{K_{d}}{E}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
and thus
\bar{\kappa}<<1
for
\frac{a}{t}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{1})
. Therefore, the Kirchhoff rod model serves well for a typical ferromagnetic ribbon of aspect ratio 10 or smaller.

2.2
Magnetic energy

We now proceed to write down the magnetic energy of the ribbon. We employ the theoretical framework of micromagnetics to formulate the magnetic energy associated with a ferromagnetic ribbon. Recall that the magnetisation vector (
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})
), is the primary variable in the micromagnetics functional. The magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})
is supported on
\Omega
and
|{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})|=1,{\bf\it x}\in\Omega
. In our analysis,
\Omega
represents the deformed planar ferromagnetic ribbon. We now examine the various terms of the micromagnetic functional when applied to our ferromagnetic ribbon. For a detailed exposition on the micromagnetic functional, we refer the reader to
dabade2019micromagnetics
.

The micromagnetic energy functional comprises of the following components

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{magnetic}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{A\int_{\Omega}\absolutevalue{\nabla{\vb*{m}(\vb*{x})}}^{2}d{\bf\it x}}_{\mathcal{E}_{ex}}+\underbrace{K_{a}\int_{\Omega}\phi({\bf\it m})d{\bf\it x}}_{\mathcal{E}_{\text{anisotropy}}}+\underbrace{K_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\vb*{h}_m}^{2}\penalty\ d{\bf\it x}}_{\mathcal{E}_{\text{demag.}}}-\underbrace{2K_{d}\int_{\Omega}{\bf\it h}_{e}\cdot{\bf\it m}d{\bf\it x}}_{\mathcal{E}_{\text{Zeeman}}},

(6)

where
\mathcal{E}_{ex}
denotes the exchange energy,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{anisotropy}}
the magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{demag.}}
is the demag. energy, and
\mathcal{E}_{\text{Zeeman}}
is the Zeeman energy. We now briefly discuss each energy component in the magnetic energy functional.

The Zeeman energy in minimised when magnetisation vector
{\bf\it m}
is parallel and aligned along the external magnetic field
{\bf\it h}_{e}
, and is expressed as follows for the ribbon:

\mathcal{E}_{\text{Zeeman}}=-2K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}{\bf\it h}_{e}\cdot{\bf\it m}(s)ds.

(7)

The magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy governs the favoured orientations for the magnetisation vector within the ferromagnetic sample, and assumes the following form:

\mathcal{E}_{\text{anisotropy}}=K_{a}\int_{\Omega}\left(1-({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it p}(s))^{2}\right)d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds=K_{a}at\int_{0}^{l}\left(1-({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it p}(s))^{2}\right)ds,

(8)

where the material parameter
K_{a}
is known as the anisotropy constant, and
{\bf\it p}(s)
is the easy axis. We have the following two scenarios:

•

Case 1. (soft ferromagnet)
: Here,
K_{a}\ll K_{d}
and the magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy is very small, and the magnetisation has no preferred direction of orientation and aligns parallel with the external magnetic field
{\bf\it h}_{e}
for a moderate external field. Since, the external magnetic field
{\bf\it h}_{e}
is applied along the
{\bf\it e}_{2}
direction, we will assume that
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})={\bf\it m}(s)={\bf\it e}_{2}

•

Case 2. (hard ferromagnet)
: Here,
K_{a}\gg K_{d}
and the magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy is very large and and the magnetisation vector makes a fixed angle with tangent along the length of the ribbon, i.e.,
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})={\bf\it m}(s)={\bf\it p}(s)\in\{{\bf\it t}(s),{\bf\it n}(s)\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ex}=A\int_{\Omega}\absolutevalue{\nabla{\vb*{m}(\vb*{x})}}^{2}d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds=A\int_{s=0}^{l}\int_{\tilde{a}=-a/2}^{a/2}\int_{\tilde{t}=-t/2}^{t/2}\absolutevalue{\nabla{\vb*{m}(\vb*{x})}}^{2}d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds,

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ex}

We now write down the demag. energy of the planar ribbon. The demag. energy associated with planar ribbon is computed by solving Maxwell’s equations of magnetostatics, namely,

\begin{split}\curl{\vb*{h}_m(\vb*{x})}&={\bf\it 0},\\
\divergence(\vb*{h}_m(\vb*{x}) + \vb*{m}(\vb*{x}))&=0.\end{split}

(13)

Here,
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})

{\bf\it h}_{m}({\bf\it x})

{\bf\it h}_{m}({\bf\it x})

The demag. energy is evaluated by computing the square of the

on all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it can be conveniently calculated in terms of Fourier transform of
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})
, as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{demag.}}=K_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\absolutevalue{\vb*{h}_m}^{2}\penalty\ d{\bf\it x}

\displaystyle=K_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\absolutevalue{\widehat{\div{\vb*{m}}}(\vb*{\xi})}^{2}}{\absolutevalue{\vb*{\xi}}^{2}}d{\bf\it\xi},

(14)

where
K_{d}=\frac{M_{s}^{2}}{2\mu_{0}}
is the magnetostatic energy constant,
M_{s}
is the saturation magnetization of the material, and
\mu_{0}
is the permeability of the free space. We carry out the above integration in the material frame
({\bf\it d}_{1}(s),{\bf\it d}_{2}(s),{\bf\it d}_{3}(s))
. In the material frame,
{\bf\it x}\mapsto{\bf\it r}(s)+\tilde{a}{\bf\it d}_{1}(s)+\tilde{t}{\bf\it d}_{2}(s)
, and
s\in[0,l],\penalty\ \tilde{a}\in\left[-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}\right],\penalty\ \tilde{t}\in\left[-\frac{t}{2},\frac{t}{2}\right]
, and
{\bf\it r}^{\prime}(s)={\bf\it d}_{3}(s)
. The Fourier transform of
{\bf\it m}
in the material frame as follows:

\begin{split}\widehat{\divergence{\vb*{m}}}({\bf\it\xi})&=\int(\divergence{\vb*{m}})\exp(-i2\pi\vb*{x}\cdot\vb*{\xi})d{\bf\it x}\\
&=\int\frac{1}{J}\left(\partial_{\tilde{a}}m_{d_{1}}+\partial_{\tilde{t}}m_{d_{2}}+\partial_{s}m_{d_{3}}+\kappa[\tilde{t}\partial_{\tilde{a}}m_{d_{1}}+\tilde{t}\partial_{\tilde{t}}m_{d_{2}}+m_{d_{2}}]\right)\exp(-i2\pi(s\xi_{d_3} + \tilde{a} \xi_{d_1} + \tilde{t}\xi_{d_2}))Jdsd\tilde{a}d\tilde{t}.\end{split}

(15)

Here,
J(=1+\tilde{t}\kappa(s))
is the Jacobian involved in computing the divergence of
{\bf\it m}
and in the change of variables from
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
to
(s,\tilde{a},\tilde{t})
. Furthermore,
m_{d_{i}}={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{i}(s),\penalty\ \xi_{d_{i}}={\bf\it\xi}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{i}(s)
. Since the ribbon under study is narrow, we shall assume
{\bf\it m}({\bf\it x})={\bf\it m}(s)
and in the above computation, the leading order term (
\mathcal{O}(t)
) of magnetostatic or demag. energy simplifies as follows:

\mathcal{E}_{\text{demag.}}=K_{d}at\int_{\mathbb{R}}\absolutevalue{\widehat{m_{d_3}}(\xi_{d_2})}^{2}d\xi_{d_{3}}=K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}(m_{d_{2}})^{2}ds=K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}({\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{2})^{2}ds.

(16)

Details of the above calculation and other computations involved in deriving the leading order demag. energy can be found in Appendix
A
.

2.3
Total energy of ferromagnetic elastic ribbon

The total energy is the sum of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{magnetic}}
(Eqns.
9
,
8
,
16
, and
7
) and
\mathcal{E}_{\text{elastica}}
(Eqn.
4
) and is given as follows:

\mathcal{E}(\theta,{\bf\it m})=\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{l}\left(\derivative{\theta(s)}{s}\right)^{2}ds+P\left(l-\int_{0}^{l}\cos\theta(s)ds\right)+A\int_{s=0}^{l}\int_{\tilde{a}=-a/2}^{a/2}\int_{\tilde{t}=-t/2}^{t/2}\absolutevalue{\nabla{\vb*{m}(s)}}^{2}d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds\\
+K_{a}at\int_{0}^{l}\left(1-({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it p}(s))^{2}\right)ds+K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it n}(s))^{2}ds-2K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}{\bf\it h}_{e}\cdot{\bf\it m}(s)ds.

(17)

Incorporating the integral constraint (Eqn.
5
) with the help of a Lagrange multiplier
R
, the augmented energy functional for magnetoelastic ribbon, here, ferromagnetic ribbon, is expressed as

\mathcal{E}(\theta,{\bf\it m})=\frac{EI}{2}\int_{0}^{l}\left(\derivative{\theta(s)}{s}\right)^{2}ds+A\int_{s=0}^{l}\int_{\tilde{a}=-a/2}^{a/2}\int_{\tilde{t}=-t/2}^{t/2}\absolutevalue{\nabla{\vb*{m}(s)}}^{2}d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds+K_{a}at\int_{0}^{l}\left(1-({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it p}(s))^{2}\right)ds\\
+K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}({\bf\it m}(s)\cdot{\bf\it n}(s))^{2}ds-2K_{d}at\int_{0}^{l}{\bf\it h}_{e}\cdot{\bf\it m}(s)ds+P\left(l-\int_{0}^{l}\cos\theta(s)ds\right)-R\int_{0}^{l}\sin\theta(s)ds.

(18)

Non-dimensionalising the augmented energy functional gives:

\bar{\mathcal{E}}(\theta,{\bf\it m})=\frac{\mathcal{E}(\theta,{\bf\it m})}{EI/l}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\right)^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{A}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\bar{a}=-1/2}^{1/2}\int_{\bar{\tilde{t}}=-1/2}^{1/2}\absolutevalue{\grad{\vb*{m}}(\bar{s})}^{2}d\bar{t}d\bar{a}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{a}\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-({\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\cdot{\bf\it p}(\bar{s}))^{2}\right)d\bar{s}\\
+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}({\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\cdot{\bf\it n}(\bar{s}))^{2}d\bar{s}-2\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}{\bf\it h}_{e}\cdot{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}+\bar{P}\left(1-\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}\right)-\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}.

(19)

where,
\bar{A}
,
\bar{K}_{a}
,
\bar{K}_{d}
,
\bar{P}
and
\bar{Q}
are non-dimensional parameters defined as follows

\bar{A}=\frac{Aat/l}{EI/l}=\frac{12A}{Et^{2}},\quad\bar{K}_{a}=\frac{K_{a}atl^{2}}{EI},\quad\bar{K}_{d}=\frac{K_{d}atl^{2}}{EI}=\frac{12K_{d}}{E}\left(\frac{l}{t}\right)^{2},\quad\bar{P}=\frac{Pl^{2}}{EI}\text{, and}\quad\bar{R}=\frac{Rl^{2}}{EI},

while
\bar{a}=\frac{\tilde{a}}{a}
, and
\bar{t}=\frac{\tilde{t}}{t}
.
Note that
\bar{K}_{d}
and
\bar{K}_{a}
are the two important non-dimensional numbers in our analysis;
\bar{K}_{d}
represents the ratio of the demag. energy and the elastic energy, and
\bar{K}_{a}
denotes the ratio of the anisotropy energy and the elastic energy of the ferromagnetic ribbon. They depend on the material properties and the aspect ratio (
\frac{t}{l}
) of the ferromagnetic ribbon.

3
Equilibrium equations

We will now derive our energy functional for the two following scenarios: 1. soft ferromagnetic ribbon and 2. hard ferromagnetic ribbon.

3.1
Case 1. Soft ferromagnetic ribbon:
\left(K_{a}at\ll K_{d}at\sim\frac{EI}{l}\right)
or
(\bar{K}_{a}\rightarrow 0
and
\bar{K}_{d}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
)

A soft ferromagnet is a ferromagnet for which the anisotropy energy can be neglected,
Dabade2018
. The magnetisation vector has no preferred direction of alignment within the ferromagnetic solid. Thus, the magnetisation vector rotates and aligns along the externally applied magnetic field for sufficiently large fields (Fig.
3
), that is,
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\parallel{\bf\it h}_{e}
, and
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})={\bf\it m}
, a constant vector. For the case when external magnetic field is oriented along
{\bf\it e}_{2}
-axis, that is,
{\bf\it h}_{e}=h_{e}{\bf\it e}_{2}
, we have
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})={\bf\it e}_{2}
. Here,
\it{h_{e}}
denotes the strength of the externally applied magnetic field.
Hence, the total energy of a soft ferromagnetic ribbon is given by

\bar{\mathcal{E}}(\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\right)^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{A}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\bar{a}=-1/2}^{1/2}\int_{\bar{t}=-1/2}^{1/2}\underbrace{\absolutevalue{\grad{\vb*{m}}}^{2}}_{=0}d\bar{t}d\bar{a}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos^{2}\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}\\
+\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-\bar{P}-2\bar{K}_{d}h_{e}.

(20)

For a constant magnetisation
{\bf\it m}
, the exchange energy is zero, and the Zeeman energy is a constant. Consequently, the deformation in this case is driven by the interplay among demag. energy, bending energy, and loading device energy.

We derive the Euler-Lagrange equations, i.e., equilibrium equations, and generic boundary conditions, for a soft ferromagnetic ribbon as follows:

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\bar{K}_{d}\sin 2\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\sin\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{R}\cos\theta(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=0,

(21)

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\eta(\bar{s})\rvert_{\bar{s}=0}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\eta(\bar{s})\rvert_{\bar{s}=1}

\displaystyle=0.

where
\eta(\bar{s})
is the admissible perturbation added to
\theta(s)
, which is subject to the constraint:

\begin{split}\bar{y}(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})\penalty\ d\bar{s}=0.\end{split}

(22)

:
Soft ferromagnetic ribbon under the influence of a transverse external magnetic field.

When
\bar{K}_{d}=0
, we obtain the equilibrium equation for the classical Euler’s elastica as follows:

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\sin\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{R}_{e}\cos\theta(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=0,

(23)

where
\bar{R}_{e}
denotes
\bar{R}
-value for the Euler’s elastica.

We consider three canonical boundary conditions for our analysis, namely

•

fixed-free:
\theta(\bar{s}=0)=0,\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s}=1)=0
,

•

fixed-fixed:
\theta(\bar{s}=0)=0,\theta(\bar{s}=1)=0

•

pinned-pinned:
\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s}=0)=0,\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s}=1)=0

Note that the constraint (Eqn.

3.2
Case 2. Hard ferromagnetic ribbon:
\left(K_{d}at\ll K_{a}at\sim\frac{EI}{l}\sim K_{d}ath_{e}\right)
or (
\bar{K}_{d}\rightarrow 0,\penalty\ \bar{K}_{d}h_{e}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)

and

\bar{K}_{a}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
)

A hard ferromagnet is a ferromagnet with very large magnetic anisotropy energy (
K_{a}\gg 1
). The magnetic anisotropy energy is minimised when the magnetization vector aligns along a preferred direction within the ferromagnetic solid. Thus, in a hard ferromagnetic ribbon, magnetization vector (
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
) makes a constant angle with the tangent (
{\bf\it d}_{3}(\bar{s})
) along the entire length of the curve.
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
varies spatially along the length of the curve to maintain a constant angle with the tangent of the curve as the ribbon deforms. In this case, the magnetization direction reorients as a result of induced deformation due to the presence of
{\bf\it h}_{e}
.

As the magnetisation maintains a constant angle with respect to the tangent to the centerline, the components of
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
in material frame basis remain constant, that is,
\partialderivative{m_{d_i}}{\bar{s}}=0,\penalty\ i=1,2,3
. Incorporating these in Eqn.
12
, we obtain the form of exchange energy as:

\mathcal{E}_{ex}=Aat\int_{0}^{l}\kappa^{2}\left[\left(m_{d_{2}}\right)^{2}+\left(m_{d_{3}}\right)^{2}\right]ds.

(24)

We will consider that
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
is either aligned along the normal (
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\perp{\bf\it d}_{2}(\bar{s})
) or that it is aligned along the tangent (
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\parallel{\bf\it d}_{3}(\bar{s})
) of the curve
{{\bf\it r}}(\bar{s})
, see Figs.
4(a)
and
4(b)
.
Accordingly, the components of
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
are
(m_{d_{1}},m_{d_{2}},m{d_{3}})
=
(0,1,0)
or
(0,0,1)
, for normal or tangential magnetisation distributions respectively. For either case, exchange energy expression reduces to

\mathcal{E}_{ex}=Aat\int_{0}^{l}\kappa^{2}ds.

(25)

Non-dimensionalising it with respect to
EI/l
, in the same manner as Eqn.
19
, results in

\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{ex}(\theta)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{ex}(\theta)}{EI/l}=\bar{A}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\right)^{2}d\bar{s}.

(26)

Also, the magnetisation is aligned along the easy axis, such that
{\bf\it m}(s)={\bf\it p}(s)\in\{{\bf\it t}(s),{\bf\it n}(s)\}

Due to the unity nature of
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
, that is,
\absolutevalue{\vb*{m}(s)}=1
, we adopt the following general expression for
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
in the Cartesian representation:

{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})=(0,\sin\phi(\bar{s}),\cos\phi(\bar{s})),

(27)

where
\phi(\bar{s})
is the angle between
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
and
{\bf\it e}_{3}
at
\bar{s}
. The transverse external magnetic field can be represented as

{\bf\it h}_{e}=h_{e}{\bf\it e}_{2}
or
{\bf\it h}_{e}=-h_{e}{\bf\it e}_{2}
depending on whether the field is applied along positive or negative
{\bf\it e}_{2}
-direction.
Upon substituting the expressions of
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
(Eqn.
27

26

19

\bar{\mathcal{E}}(\theta,\psi)=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\bar{A}\right)\int_{0}^{l}\left(\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s})\right)^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{a}\int_{0}^{1}\underbrace{\left(1-({\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\cdot{\bf\it p}(\bar{s}))^{2}\right)}_{=0}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\underbrace{({\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\cdot{\bf\it n}(\bar{s}))^{2}}_{\text{constant}}d\bar{s}\\
+\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}\mp 2\bar{K}_{d}h_{e}\int_{0}^{1}\cos\phi(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-\bar{Q}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-\bar{P}.

(28)

where
\bar{Q}
is a Lagrange multiplier introduced to distinguish from
\bar{R}
or
\bar{R}_{e}

\bar{R}_{e}
is the vertical reaction for the Euler’s elastica (see Eqn.
23

Note that, in this case
({\bf\it m}(\bar{s})\cdot{\bf\it n}(\bar{s}))^{2}
is constant and hence the demag. energy does not participate in energy minimization. Large
K_{a}
ensures
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})
is parallel and aligned along
{\bf\it p}(\bar{s})
. The contribution of exchange energy is negligible since
\bar{A}\ll 1
. Therefore, the deformation in this case is driven by the interplay among bending energy, loading device energy and the Zeeman energy.

Following similar steps as in soft magnetization scenario, we arrive at the following Euler-Lagrange equations for hard magnetization case as

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\sin\theta(\bar{s})\pm 2\bar{K}_{d}h_{e}\sin(\phi(\bar{s}) - \psi)+\bar{Q}\cos\theta(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=0,

4
Path continuation methodology

We determine numerical solutions to the equilibrium equations as the loading parameter
\bar{P}
is quasi-statically varied for various loading scenarios. We use a continuation method called pseudo arc length-based technique as discussed in
Peletan2014
to determine the deformed configurations. The technique constrains the incremental deformation measure and increment in load parameters (
P,R
) via a hyperspheric constraint.

4.1
Discretization of equilibrium equations

We discretize Eqn.
21
for fixed-fixed boundary conditions. We discretize the domain into
N+1
nodal points as
\bar{s}_{i}=ih;i=0,1,\dots,N+1
where
h=\frac{1}{N+1}
. We utilize second-order accurate central difference scheme to approximate the second-order derivative in Eqn.
21
. The discrete system of equations alongwith the boundary conditions is

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{i-1}-2\theta_{i}+\theta_{i+1}}{h^{2}}+\bar{P}\sin\theta_{i}+\bar{K}_{d}\sin 2\theta_{i}+\bar{R}\cos\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=0;\qquad i=1,2,\dots,N,

(37)

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=0,\theta_{N+1}=0,

where
\theta_{i}
denotes the numerical counterpart of
\theta(\bar{s}=\bar{s}_{i})
.
In matrix-vector form, the discretized system can be written for all three cases (for fixed-free case where
\bar{R}=0
) as

\displaystyle{\bf\it K\theta}+\bar{P}\sin{\bf\it\theta}+\bar{K}_{d}\sin 2{\bf\it\theta}+\bar{R}\cos{\bf\it\theta}={\bf\it 0}\text{ (see Appendix \ref{app:equilibrium} for ${\bf\it K}$ and ${\bf\it\theta}$}).

(38)

Incorporating the discretized form of integral constraint (Eqn.
22
) results in the combined nonlinear system of equations (for fixed-fixed and pinned-pinned conditions)

\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf\it K\theta}+\bar{P}\sin{\bf\it\theta}+\bar{K}_{d}\sin 2{\bf\it\theta}+\bar{R}\cos{\bf\it\theta}\\
\sin\theta_{1}+\dots+\sin\theta_{N}\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}{\bf\it 0}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\\
\text{ or, }\qquad\qquad{\bf\it f}({\bf\it\theta},\bar{R},\bar{P})={\bf\it 0}.&\end{split}

(39)

4.2
Pseudo arc length-based continuation method

We proceed to devise a strategy to solve the nonlinear system of equations (Eqn.
39
) using an arc length-based continuation method. Let us describe
k
-th configuration of the planar-deforming ferromagnetic ribbon as
({\bf\it\theta}_{k},\bar{R}_{k},\bar{P}_{k})
where
k
denotes the configuration index. The algorithm works in two steps: prediction step and correction step.

Prediction step

: We expand the equilibrium equation (Eqn.
39
) at
(k+1)
-th index in terms of Taylor series expansion about
k
as

\displaystyle\underbrace{{\bf\it f}({\bf\it\theta}_{k+1},\bar{R}_{k+1},\bar{P}_{k+1})}_{={\bf\it 0}}=\underbrace{{\bf\it f}({\bf\it\theta}_{k},\bar{R}_{k},\bar{P}_{k})}_{={\bf\it 0}}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\vb*{\theta}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{R}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta\bar{R}_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{P}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta\bar{P}_{k}+\underbrace{\mathcal{O}(\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k}^{2},\Delta\bar{R}_{k}^{2},\Delta\bar{P}_{k}^{2})}_{\text{ignore}},

(40)

\displaystyle\implies\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\vb*{\theta}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{R}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta\bar{R}_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{P}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\Delta\bar{P}_{k}

\displaystyle={\bf\it 0}.

(41)

We define a tangent vector as

\displaystyle{\bf\it e}_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k}\\
\Delta\bar{R}_{k}\\
\Delta\bar{P}_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(42)

whose norm squared is

\displaystyle\norm{\vb*{e}_k}^{2}=(\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k})^{T}(\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k})+(\Delta\bar{R}_{k})^{2}+(\Delta\bar{P}_{k})^{2}.

(43)

We make the following substitution

\Delta\bar{P}_{k}=a_{k},\penalty\ \Delta\bar{R}_{k}=a_{k}\Delta r_{k},\penalty\ \Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k}=a_{k}\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k}.

(44)

Setting the norm of
{\bf\it e}_{k}
to unity, we obtain the hyperspheric constraint which yields an expression for
a_{k}

\displaystyle a_{k}^{2}(\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k})^{T}(\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k})+a_{k}^{2}(\Delta r_{k})^{2}+a_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

(45)

\displaystyle\implies a_{k}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k})^{T}(\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k})+(\Delta r_{k})^{2}+1}}.

(46)

The sign of
a_{k}
is chosen positive if
{\bf\it e}_{k-1}^{T}{\bf\it e}_{k}>0
or else
a_{k}
is taken to be negative. Substituting the expressions for
\Delta{\bf\it\theta}_{k},\Delta\bar{R}_{k}
and
\Delta\bar{P}_{k}
in Eqn.
41
, we have

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\vb*{\theta}}\bigg\rvert_{k}a_{k}\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{R}}\bigg\rvert_{k}a_{k}\Delta r_{k}+\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{P}}\bigg\rvert_{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle={\bf\it 0},

(47)

\displaystyle\implies\begin{bmatrix}\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\vb*{\theta}}\bigg\rvert_{k}&\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{R}}\bigg\rvert_{k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta{\bf\it\phi}_{k}\\
\Delta r_{k}\end{pmatrix}=-\partialderivative{\vb*{f}}{\bar{P}}\bigg\rvert_{k}.

(48)

We use Eqn.
48

5
Bifurcation analysis of equilibrium configuration

We now determine whether a given deformed equilibrium configuration is stable with respect to infinitesimal perturbations. We expect stable perturbations to be physically realizable. This requires evaluation of the second variational derivative of the energy functional at the critical points. The critical points are the solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equations.

We proceed to determine the second variational derivative of the functional,
\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)
for the soft ferromagnetic ribbon when
{\bf\it h}_{e}
is applied along
{\bf\it e}_{2}
-axis such that
{\bf\it m}(\bar{s})={\bf\it e}_{2}
. Introducing first order perturbation in the assumed extremum
\theta
as
\hat{\theta}(\bar{s})=\theta(\bar{s})+\epsilon\eta(\bar{s})
where
\eta(\bar{s})
is a kinematically admissible planar variation and
\epsilon
is a small parameter, and substituting it into Eqn.
19

\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta+\epsilon\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\theta^{\prime}+\epsilon\eta^{\prime})^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos^{2}(\theta+\epsilon\eta)atd\bar{s}+\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\theta+ \epsilon\eta)d\bar{s}\\
-\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\theta+ \epsilon\eta)d\bar{s}+\text{constant}.

(56)

Simplifying the above expansion and taking into account the boundary conditions, we have

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)=\derivative[2]{\mathcal{\overline{E}} (\theta+ \epsilon\eta)}{\epsilon}\bigg\rvert_{\epsilon=0}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}[\eta^{\prime\prime}+2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta\eta+\bar{P}\cos\theta\eta-\bar{R}\sin\theta\eta]\eta d\bar{s},

(57)

for all kinematically admissible functions
\eta(\bar{s})
. The stability criterion states that

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)\begin{cases}>0&\text{ stable},\\
<0&\text{ unstable}.\end{cases}

(58)

Further, we also introduce the first variation of the integral constraint
\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
given as:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\eta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0.

(59)

5.1
Construction of Sturm-Liouville problem

Following
Levyakov2009
;
Bigoni2015
, we construct an equivalent Sturm-Liouville problem for the second variation
\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)
as follows:

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{n}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})=C_{R_{n}}\cos\theta(\bar{s}),

(60)

where
\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues,
\phi_{n}
the corresponding eigenmodes of Eqn.
60
and
L(\bar{s})=(2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\cos\theta(\bar{s})-\bar{R}\sin\theta(\bar{s}))
denotes the weight function.
C_{R_{n}}
is a Lagrange parameter introduced to enforce the isoperimetric constraint (Eqn.
59
). The conditions on
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
are

•

fixed-fixed case:
\phi_{n}(0)=\phi_{n}(1)=0
and
\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
,

•

pinned-pinned case:
\phi_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\phi_{n}^{\prime}(1)=0
and
\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
,

•

fixed-free case:
\phi_{n}(0)=\phi_{n}^{\prime}(1)=0
.

We obtain the following conditions for two arbitrary eigenmodes
\phi_{m}(\bar{s})
and
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
of the Sturm-Liouville problem (Eqn.
60
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}^{2}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{n}^{\prime 2}d\bar{s},

(61)

and the orthogonality condition

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(62)

The details of the above calculation can be found in Appendix
D
.

Spectral decomposition

: Let us use
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
alongwith the weight function
L(\bar{s})
to construct a Fourier series representation (converging in the mean) to the square-integrable function
\eta(\bar{s})
,

\displaystyle\eta(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\phi_{n}(\bar{s}),\qquad c_{n}\text{ are Fourier coefficients}.

(63)

Substituting the above representation in Eqn.
57
and invoking the conditions (Eqns.
61
and
62
), we obtain the stability criterion:

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{2}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\bar{s}))^{2}d\bar{s}\quad\begin{cases}>0\text{ if }\lambda_{n}\notin[0,1]&\text{ stable},\\
<0\text{ if }\lambda_{n}\in[0,1]&\text{ unstable}.\end{cases}

(64)

The detailed bifurcation analysis technique is presented in Appendix
D
.

5.1.1
Numerical bifurcation analysis

We describe the numerical procedure for the fixed-fixed case, which can be easily adapted to pinned-pinned and fixed-free cases. Given, Eqn.
60
as

\displaystyle\phi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{m}L_{m}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=C_{R_{m}}N(\bar{s}),

(65)

where, the coefficient functions are

L_{m}(\bar{s})=2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\cos\theta(\bar{s})-\bar{R}\sin\theta(\bar{s}),\qquad N(\bar{s})=\cos\theta(\bar{s}).

(66)

Eqn.
65
is also subjected to the boundary conditions
\phi_{m}(0)=0,\phi_{m}(1)=0
and the additional constraint

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{m}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(67)

Numerical procedure to compute eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}

: Partition the interval
\bar{s}\in[0,1]
segments of equal length
h=\frac{1}{n+1}
such that the starting points of the segments can be denoted by
\bar{s}_{i-1}=(i-1)h;i=1,2,\dots,n+1
. For
i
-th segment, the functions
L(\bar{s})
and
N(\bar{s})
are approximated by averaging their corresponding values at nodal indices
i
and
i+1
resulting in
L_{i}
and
N_{i}
respectively. Substituting these averaged quantities, Eqn.
65
becomes

\displaystyle\phi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{m}L_{i}\phi_{m}(\bar{s})=C_{R_{m}}N_{i},

(68)

which is an ordinary differential equation (ODE) with constant coefficients. The solution to this ODE is

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=A_{1i}F_{1i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})+A_{2i}F_{2i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})+C_{R_{m}}\frac{N_{i}}{\lambda_{m}L_{i}},

(69)

where
A_{1i}
and
A_{2i}
are constants, and the functions
F_{1i}
and
F_{2i}
are defined as

1.

for
\lambda_{m}L_{i}>0
as

F_{1i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})=\cos a_{i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1}),\quad F_{2i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})=\sin a_{i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})
,

2.

for
\lambda_{m}L_{i}<0
as

F_{1i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})=\cosh a_{i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1}),\quad F_{2i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})=\sin a_{i}(\bar{s}-\bar{s}_{i-1})

with
a_{i}=\sqrt{\absolutevalue{\lambda L_i}}
.

The constants
A_{1i}
and
A_{2i}
can be obtained from the matching conditions

\phi_{m}(\bar{s}_{i-1})=\phi_{m,i-1},\qquad\phi_{m}^{\prime}(\bar{s}_{i-1})=\phi_{m,i-1}^{\prime}

(70)

as

A_{1i}=\phi_{m,i-1}-C_{R_{m}}\frac{N_{i}}{\lambda_{m}L_{i}},\qquad A_{2i}=\frac{\phi_{m,i-1}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}.

(71)

The quantity
\phi_{m}(\bar{s}_{i})=\phi_{m,i}
at the right end of the segment is computed alongwith its derivative as

\begin{split}\phi_{m,i}&=\phi_{m,i-1}F_{1i}(h)+\frac{\phi_{m,i-1}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}F_{2i}(h)+C_{R_{m}}N_{i}\frac{[1-F_{1i}(h)]}{\lambda_{m}L_{i}},\\
\phi^{\prime}_{m,i}&=\phi_{m,i-1}F_{1i}^{\prime}(h)+\frac{\phi_{m,i-1}^{\prime}}{a_{i}}F_{2i}^{\prime}(h)-C_{R_{m}}N_{i}\frac{F_{1i}^{\prime}(h)}{\lambda_{m}L_{i}}.\end{split}

(72)

The general solution of Eqn.
68
can be constructed by using Eqns.
72
, and the continuity requirement of
\phi_{m}
and
\phi_{m}^{\prime}
at the extremities of every integration segment. Since Eqn.
65
is linear, its general solution can be written as a combination of three particular solutions

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=c_{1}\psi_{1}(\bar{s})+c_{2}\psi_{2}(\bar{s})+C_{R_{m}}\psi_{3}(\bar{s}),

(73)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constants.
We use the following initial data

\begin{split}&\psi_{1}(0)=1,\quad\psi_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad C_{R_{m}}=0,\\
&\psi_{2}(0)=0,\quad\psi_{2}^{\prime}(0)=1,\quad C_{R_{m}}=0,\\
&\psi_{3}(0)=0,\quad\psi_{3}^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad C_{R_{m}}=1.\end{split}

(74)

The functions
\psi_{i}(\bar{s}),i=1,2,3
can be constructed separately using the recurrence relations Eqn.
72
.
Using boundary conditions, we have

\begin{split}\phi_{m}(0)=0&\implies c_{1}=0,\\
\phi_{m}(1)=0&\implies c_{2}\psi_{2}(1)+C_{R_{m}}\psi_{3}(1)=0.\end{split}

(75)

Substituting Eqn.
73

67

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{2}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}+C_{R_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{3}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(76)

We construct the following homogeneous system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{2}(1)&\psi_{3}(1)\\
\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{2}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}&\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{3}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}\end{bmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{2}\\
C_{R_{m}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(77)

For non-trivial solution to the above equation, the determinant,

\displaystyle\Delta=

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(1)\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{3}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-\psi_{3}(1)\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{2}(\bar{s})N(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

(78)

must be zero. The integrals are evaluated numerically.

We vary the value of
\lambda_{m}
from 0 to 1 and monitor the changes in
\Delta
.

•

If at least one value of
\lambda_{m}\in[0,1]
exists resulting in
\Delta=0
, then the non-trivial solution of Eqn.
73
exists which also satisfies the boundary conditions. The corresponding equilibrium configuration is unstable.

•

It no
\lambda_{m}\in[0,1]
results in
\Delta=0
, it implies that there exists a trivial solution of Eqn.
73
. The corresponding equilibrium configuration is therefore stable.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented a novel model that combines ideas from Euler’s elastica and continuum theory of micromagnetics to predict the deformation of ferromagnetic ribbons. We have analysed the buckling of a planar inextensible ribbon subjected to a large but constant external magnetic field and a gradually applied quasi-static load. While maintaining fixed magnetisation, we investigate the influence of magnetisation on the deformation of the planar ferromagnetic ribbon. Exploring how deformation influences magnetisation is an area for future research, although it presents a challenging problem. Our analysis commences by deriving and incorporating the magnetostatic energy of a curved structure into the total energy of the system. We derive the equilibrium equations and solve them numerically to obtain the equilibrium path as the load is increased. Further, we also determine the stability of the equilibrium solutions by casting the second variation of the total energy as a Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue problem, which is solved numerically.

We examine ribbons composed of both hard and soft ferromagnetic properties, under a transversely applied external magnetic field and various mechanical boundary conditions. We observe that the critical buckling load is tensile mainly for a soft ferromagnetic ribbon under various canonical boundary conditions. Interestingly, our findings reveal the presence of novel stable configurations in the case of a fixed-fixed setup with a transversally applied external magnetic field. We anticipate that these stable configurations can be observed through meticulously conducted experiments.

Our model is applicable within specific parameter ranges. For a typical ferromagnetic material such as iron (Fe) or nickel (Ni), the Young’s modulus (
E
) and the magnetostatic energy density (
K_{d}
) are approximately
E\sim 10^{11}
and
K_{d}\sim 10^{5}
, respectively. The aspect ratio, defined as the ratio of the width (
a
) to the thickness (
t
), can be determined by ensuring that
\bar{\kappa}<<1
, yielding
\nicefrac{{a}}{{t}}\sim
. The ratio of the thickness to the length of the ribbon is determined by the values of
\bar{K}_{d}
derived from the conducted simulations. Our simulations reveal that in soft ferromagnets, the magnetic effect becomes significant when
\bar{K}_{d}\sim 100
. Hence,
\nicefrac{{l}}{{t}}\sim 10\sqrt{\nicefrac{{E}}{{K_{d}}}}
. Similarly, for hard ferromagnets, magnetic effects are observed when
\bar{K}_{d}h_{e}\sim 100
, resulting in:
\nicefrac{{l}}{{t}}\sim 10\sqrt{\nicefrac{{E}}{{K_{d}h_{e}}}}
. Furthermore, for a hard ferromagnet, the maximum external magnetic field is determined by ensuring the exchange energy remains non-negative, expressed as
\kappa_{c}<\kappa_{max}=\nicefrac{{2}}{{t}}
, consequently leading to a maximum external magnetic field
h_{e}<\sqrt{\nicefrac{{E}}{{K_{d}}}}
.

Our model is valid for the following range of parameters. The Young’s modulus (
E
), the magnetostatic energy density (
K_{d}
) for a typical ferromagnetic material (Fe, Ni) are
E\sim 10^{11}
and
K_{d}\sim 10^{5}

\bar{\kappa}<<1
. That is we obtain
\nicefrac{{a}}{{t}}\sim
. The ratio of the thickness to the length of the ribbon is determined by the values of

obtained from the reported simulations. Finally, for a hard ferromagnet the maximum external magnetic field is evaluated by ensuring that the exchange energy is non negative. That is,
\kappa_{c}<\kappa_{max}=\nicefrac{{2}}{{t}}
, and hence the maximum external magnetic field
h_{e}<\sqrt{\nicefrac{{E}}{{K_{d}}}}
.

Our analysis can be readily extended to understanding the deformation of planar ferromagnetic rods. The magnetostatic energy of planar ferromagnetic rod scales as
\mathcal{O}(K_{d}\pi r^{2}l)

Slastikov2011
. A balance of the elastic energy
\mathcal{O}(E\pi\big(\frac{r^{4}}{8l})\big)

\mathcal{O}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{E}{8Kd}}\bigg)
.

The stability analysis conducted in our study solely examines planar perturbations. It is widely recognized that, in the case of Euler’s elastica, stability under planar perturbations does not guarantee stability of the equilibrium configurations under fully three-dimensional perturbations

\bullet

Explore stability under three-dimensional perturbations, and ascertain the potential role of the vertical reaction,
\bar{R}

\bullet

Investigating the influence of twist and out-of-plane deformation on ferromagnetic ribbons.

\bullet

Design laboratory experiments to observe novel stable deformed configurations of ferromagnetic ribbon structures, as identified in our analysis.

\begin{split}\gradient{\bf\it m}&=\left(\partialderivative{m^a}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it g}_{a}{\bf\it g}^{a}+\left(\partialderivative{m^a}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it g}_{a}{\bf\it g}^{t}+\left(\partialderivative{m^a}{s}\right){\bf\it g}_{a}{\bf\it g}^{s}\\
&+\left(\partialderivative{m^t}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it g}_{t}{\bf\it g}^{a}+\left(\partialderivative{m^t}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it g}_{t}{\bf\it g}^{t}+\left(\partialderivative{m^t}{s}-\kappa(1+\kappa t)m^{s}\right){\bf\it g}_{t}{\bf\it g}^{s}\\
&+\left(\partialderivative{m^s}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it g}_{s}{\bf\it g}^{a}+\left(\partialderivative{m^s}{\tilde{t}}+\frac{\kappa}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}m^{s}\right){\bf\it g}_{s}{\bf\it g}^{t}+\left(\partialderivative{m^s}{s}+\frac{\kappa m^{t}}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\right){\bf\it g}_{s}{\bf\it g}^{s}.\end{split}

(102)

We can write the above expression in terms of orthonormal basis of
({\bf\it d}_{1},{\bf\it d}_{2},{\bf\it d}_{3})
using the physical components of
{\bf\it m}(m_{d_{1}},m_{d_{2}},m_{d_{3}})
as follows:

\begin{split}\gradient{\bf\it m}&=\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it d}_{1}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{1}+\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it d}_{1}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{2}+\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{s}{\bf\it d}_{1}\cdot\\
vb*{d}_{3}\\
&+\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it d}_{1}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{2}+\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it d}_{2}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{2}+\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{s}-\kappa m_{d_{3}}\right){\bf\it d}_{2}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{3}\\
&+\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{\tilde{a}}\right){\bf\it d}_{3}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{1}+\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it d}_{3}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{2}+\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{s}+\kappa m_{d_{2}}\right){\bf\it d}_{3}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{3},\end{split}

(103)

where
{\bf\it g}^{s}=\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}{\bf\it d}_{3}(s),\penalty\ {\bf\it g}_{s}=\left(1+\kappa\tilde{t}\right){\bf\it d}_{3}(s)
. We note that
m_{d_{1}}={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{1}(s)={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it g}^{a},\penalty\ m_{d_{2}}={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{2}(s)={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it g}^{t}=m^{t},\penalty\ m_{d_{3}}={\bf\it m}\cdot{\bf\it d}_{3}(s)={\bf\it m}\cdot(1+\kappa\tilde{t}){\bf\it g}^{s}=(1+\kappa\tilde{t})m^{s}
. Also,

\begin{split}\left(\partialderivative{m^t}{\tilde{t}}\right){\bf\it g}_{t}{\bf\it g}^{t}+\left(\partialderivative{m^t}{s}-\kappa(1+\kappa t)m^{s}\right){\bf\it g}_{t}{\bf\it g}^{s}&=\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{s}-\kappa m_{d_{3}}\right)\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}{\bf\it d}_{2}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{3}\\
\left(\partialderivative{m^s}{\tilde{t}}+\frac{\kappa}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}m^{s}\right){\bf\it g}_{s}{\bf\it g}^{t}&=\left(\partialderivative{\tilde{t}}\left(\frac{m_{d_{3}}}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})^{2}}m_{d_{3}}\right)(1+\kappa\tilde{t}){\bf\it d}_{3}\otimes{\bf\it d}_{2}.\end{split}

(104)

Hence,

[\gradient{\bf\it m}]_{{\bf\it d}_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{\tilde{a}}&\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{\tilde{t}}&\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\partialderivative{m_{d_1}}{s}\\
\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{\tilde{a}}&\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{\tilde{t}}&\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_2}}{s}-\kappa m_{d_{3}}\right)\\
\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{\tilde{a}}&\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{\tilde{t}}&\frac{1}{(1+\kappa\tilde{t})}\left(\partialderivative{m_{d_3}}{s}+\kappa m_{d_{2}}\right)\end{bmatrix}.

(105)

The exchange energy is thus expressed as

\mathcal{E}_{ex}=A\int_{\Omega}\absolutevalue{\grad\vb*{m}}^{2}d\tilde{t}d\tilde{a}ds.

(106)

Appendix D
Bifurcation analysis

We briefly describe the bifurcation analysis for planar deformation of ribbons. We begin with taking the second variation of the total magnetoelastic energy functional, followed by Fourier expansion for kinematically-compatible variations, we establish stability criterion for deformed configuration under various loading scenarios and end conditions. The numerical procedure to implement this criterion has been explained in Section
5
.

D.1
Derivation of stability condition

Let us non-dimensionalize the energy functional for planar-deformed magnetoelastic rods and ribbons. The external magnetic field is applied along the Cartesian
{\bf\it e}_{2}
-axis, that is,
{\bf\it h}_{e}=h_{e}{\bf\it e}_{2}
such that
{\bf\it m}(s)={\bf\it e}_{2}
. Upon substituting the values in Eqn.
18
and non-dimensionalizing the energy functionals appropriately, we have

\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\theta^{\prime}(\bar{s}))^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos^{2}\theta(\bar{s})atd\bar{s}-\underbrace{2\bar{K}_{d}h_{e}at\int_{0}^{1}1\cdot d\bar{s}}_{\text{constant}}-\bar{P}\bigg(1-\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}\bigg)-\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}.

(114)

Introducing first order perturbation to the assumed extremum
\theta
as
\hat{\theta}(\bar{s})=\theta(\bar{s})+\epsilon\eta(\bar{s})
where
\eta(\bar{s})
is a kinematically admissible planar variation and
\epsilon
is a small parameter. Now,

\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta+\epsilon\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\theta^{\prime}+\epsilon\eta^{\prime})^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos^{2}(\theta+\epsilon\eta)atd\bar{s}+\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos(\theta+ \epsilon\eta)d\bar{s}-\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin(\theta+ \epsilon\eta)d\bar{s}+\text{constant}.

(115)

Differentiating the above with respect to
\epsilon
twice and putting
\epsilon=0
results in the second variational of
\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)

\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)=\derivative[2]{\mathcal{\overline{E}} (\theta+ \epsilon\eta)}{\epsilon}\bigg\rvert_{\epsilon=0}=\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{\prime 2}d\bar{s}-2\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos 2\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s}-\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s}.

(116)

Integrating by parts the first term,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\eta^{\prime}\eta\bigg\rvert_{0}^{1}}_{=0}-\int_{0}^{1}\eta^{\prime\prime}\eta d\bar{s}-2\bar{K}_{d}\int_{0}^{1}\cos 2\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s}-\bar{P}\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s}+\bar{R}\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta\eta^{2}d\bar{s},

(117)

\displaystyle\implies\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}[\eta^{\prime\prime}+2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta\eta+\bar{P}\cos\theta\eta-\bar{R}\sin\theta\eta]\eta d\bar{s},

(118)

for all kinematically admissible functions
\eta(\bar{s})
. The stability criterion requires that

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)\begin{cases}>0&\text{ stable}\\
<0&\text{ unstable}.\end{cases}

(119)

Introducing first variation to the integral constraint
\int_{0}^{1}\sin\theta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
results in

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\eta(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0.

(120)

D.2
Construction of Sturm-Liouville problem

A meticulous study of the second variation
\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)
necessitates the construction of the following Sturm-Liouville problem whose non-trivial solutions are
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
:

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{n}(2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta+\bar{P}\cos\theta-\bar{R}\sin\theta)\phi_{n}(\bar{s})=C_{R_{n}}\cos\theta(\bar{s}),

(121)

where
\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues,
\phi_{n}
the corresponding eigenmodes of Eqn.
121
and
L(\bar{s})=(2\bar{K}_{d}\cos 2\theta(\bar{s})+\bar{P}\cos\theta(\bar{s})-\bar{R}\sin\theta(\bar{s}))
denotes the weight function.
C_{R_{n}}
is meant to enforce the isoperimetric constraint (Eqn.
120
). The conditions on
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
are

•

fixed-fixed case:
\phi_{n}(0)=\phi_{n}(1)=0
and
\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
,

•

pinned-pinned case:
\phi_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\phi_{n}^{\prime}(1)=0
and
\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}=0
,

•

fixed-free case:
\phi_{n}(0)=\phi_{n}^{\prime}(1)=0
.

Multiplying both sides of Eqn.
121
by
\phi_{n}
, integrating over the domain and using the conditions stipulated on
\phi_{n}(\bar{s})
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[\phi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{n}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})]\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=C_{R_{n}}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}}_{0},

\displaystyle\implies\lambda_{n}\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}^{2}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{n}^{\prime 2}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}.

(122)

We multiply Eqn.
121
by
\phi_{m}
and integrate over the domain to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[\phi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+\lambda_{n}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})]\phi_{m}d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=C_{R_{n}}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}}_{0},

\displaystyle\implies-\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}^{\prime}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}+\lambda_{n}\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(123)

Similarly, consider Eqn.
60
for
\phi_{m}
, multiply it by
\phi_{n}
and integrate by parts to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{m}^{\prime}(\bar{s})\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}+\lambda_{m}\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(124)

Subtracting the second equation from the first results in

\displaystyle(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(125)

For
n\neq m
,
\lambda_{n}\neq\lambda_{m}
, we obtain the orthogonality condition as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=0.

(126)

Spectral decomposition

Let us use
\phi_{n}(\bar{s}
alongwith the weight function
L(\bar{s})
to construct a Fourier series representation (converging in the mean) to the square-integrable function
\eta(\bar{s})
,

\displaystyle\eta(\bar{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\phi_{n}(\bar{s}),\qquad c_{n}\text{ are Fourier coefficients}.

(127)

Substitute the above representation in Eqn.
118
,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}[c_{n}\phi_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{s})+L(\bar{s})c_{n}\phi_{n}(\bar{s})]c_{m}\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=-c_{m}c_{n}\int_{0}^{1}[C_{R_{n}}\cos\theta(\bar{s})-\lambda_{n}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})]\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}-c_{n}c_{m}\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=-c_{m}c_{n}C_{R_{n}}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\cos\theta(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}}_{=0}+c_{n}c_{m}(\lambda_{n}-1)\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{m}(\bar{s})\phi_{n}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}}_{\text{(use orthogonality condition)}}

\displaystyle=c_{n}^{2}(\lambda_{n}-1)\int_{0}^{1}L(\bar{s})\phi_{n}^{2}(\bar{s})d\bar{s}

\displaystyle=c_{n}^{2}(\lambda_{n}-1)\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\bar{s}))^{2}d\bar{s}.

The stability criterion is

\displaystyle\implies\delta^{2}\mathcal{\overline{E}}(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{2}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}(\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\bar{s}))^{2}d\bar{s}\quad\begin{cases}>0\text{ if }\lambda_{n}\notin[0,1]&\text{ stable}\\
<0\text{ if }\lambda_{n}\in[0,1]&\text{ unstable}.\end{cases}

(128)

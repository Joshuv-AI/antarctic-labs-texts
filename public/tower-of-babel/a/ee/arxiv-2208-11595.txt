A coarse geometric approach to the generalised Green-Julg Theorem
Liang Guo, Qin Wang

Abstract
In this note, we provide a proof of the generalised Green-Julg theorem by using the language of twisted localization algebras introduced by G. Yu. This proof is for those who have interests in coarse geometry but not so familiar with K ​ K KK -theory or E E -theory.

Introduction

The Dirac-dual-Dirac method introduced by G. Kasparov
[
8
]
is one of the most powerful methods when dealing with the strong Novikov conjecture for a countable discrete group. This method has been developed in recent 30 years (see Section 2 in
[

[

[
3

[
4

, N. Higson and G. Kasparov show that the Baum-Connes conjecture with coefficient holds for all a-T-menable groups and the generalised Green-Julg theorem play an important role in the proof.

In
[

, G. Yu introduced a notion of the localization algebra for a proper metric space

whose

-theory play a role of the
K
-homology of

[

, G. Yu adapts and develops a construction of
[
4
]
to a coarse geometric version by using the twisted algebras. This method for the coarse Baum-Connes conjecture is always called the geometric Dirac-dual-Dirac method.

In this paper, we shall prove that the equivariant version of geometric Dirac-dual-Dirac method for cocompact action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is actually identified with the original Dirac-dual-Dirac method. The main result of this paper is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a proper
\Gamma
-

-algebra. The evaluation map

ev_{*}:\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A})^{\Gamma})\to\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A})^{\Gamma})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A})^{\Gamma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces RK^{\Gamma}_{*}(\underline{\mathcal{E}}\Gamma,\mathcal{A})}

Let
\Gamma

by using the formula

(\alpha_{\gamma}(f))(x)=f(\gamma^{-1}x).

for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
x\in X
and

A separable

Definition 2.2

The equivariant Roe algebra with coefficient in

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
T\in\mathbb{C}[X,\mathcal{A}]^{\Gamma}

R>0

\textup{supp}(T^{\prime}(x,y))
is bounded by

\|T-T^{\prime}\|\leq\varepsilon

Proof.

Since
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is cocompact and

is a proper metric space, there exists a bounded subset
K_{0}\subseteq X
such that
\Gamma\cdot K_{0}=X
. Denote

K=\{x\in X\mid d(x,K)\leq\textup{Prop}(T)\}.

By condition (3) in Definition
2.1
, there are only finite
(x,y)\in K\times K
such that
T(x,y)\neq 0
. For any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
(x,y)\in K
, set
T^{\prime}(x,y)\in\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{A}
such that
\textup{supp}(T^{\prime}(x,y))
is bounded and
\|T(x,y)-T^{\prime}(x,y)\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Moreover, if
(x,y)
and
(\gamma x,\gamma y)
are both in
K\times K
, we assume that
T^{\prime}(x,y)=\gamma^{-1}T^{\prime}(\gamma x,\gamma y).
We can always do that because

is dense in
\mathcal{A}
and
T
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Take
R>0
such that
\textup{diam}(\textup{supp}(T^{\prime}(x,y)))\leq R
for all
(x,y)\in K\times K
.

For any
(x,y)\notin K\times K
with
d(x,y)\leq\textup{Prop}(T)
, there must exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\gamma x\in K_{0}
. Then

d(\gamma y,K_{0})\leq d(\gamma y,\gamma x)\leq\textup{Prop}(T),

this means that
(\gamma x,\gamma y)\in K\times K

By

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W})^{\Gamma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(F),\mathcal{A}_{V})^{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(F),\mathcal{A}_{V})^{F}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev_{*}}

Notice that the classifying space for proper
F
-action can be chosen to a single point since
F
is finite. Then
\lim\limits_{d\to\infty}K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(F),\mathcal{A}_{V})^{F})
is isomorphic to
K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(pt,\mathcal{A}_{V})^{F})
, which is isomorphic to
K_{*}(C^{*}(pt,\mathcal{A}_{V})^{F})
by evaluation map (see
[
11
, Lemma 12.4.3]
). The right bottem term of the diagram above is clearly isomorphic to
K_{*}(\mathcal{A}_{V}\rtimes_{r}F)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
W=W_{1}\cup W_{2}
and
W^{\prime}=W_{1}\cap W_{2}
, then for each
d\geq 0
,

(1)

K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{1}})^{\Gamma})+K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{2}})^{\Gamma})=K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W})^{\Gamma})
;

(2)

K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{1}})^{\Gamma})\cap K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{2}})^{\Gamma})=K_{*}(C^{*}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W^{\prime}})^{\Gamma})

K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{1}})^{\Gamma})\cap K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W_{2}})^{\Gamma})=K_{*}(C^{*}_{L}(P_{d}(\Gamma),\mathcal{A}_{W^{\prime}})^{\Gamma})
.

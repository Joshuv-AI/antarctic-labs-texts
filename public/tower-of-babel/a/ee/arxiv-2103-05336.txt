Configuration spaces and directed paths on the final precubical set
Jakub Paliga, Krzysztof Ziemiański

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to prove that the space of directed loops on the final precubical set is homotopy equivalent to the “total” configuration space of points on the plane; by “total” we mean that any finite number of points in a configuration is allowed. We also provide several applications: we define new invariants of precubical sets, prove that directed path spaces on any precubical complex have the homotopy types of CW-complexes and construct certain presentations of configuration spaces of points on the plane as nerves of categories.

1.
Introduction

Directed Algebraic Topology (DAT) is a branch of mathematics that uses topological methods to solve problems arising in computer science, especially in concurrency
[
1
]
. The main objects of interest of DAT are directed spaces (d-spaces)
[
3
]
. A directed space is a topological space
X
with a distinguished family of paths
\vec{P}(X)
, called directed paths or d-paths. Points of
X
represent possible states of a concurrent program, while directed paths indicate how the states can evolve in time. The space of directed paths between two distinguished points of
X
, the initial point
\mathbf{0}
and the final point
\mathbf{1}
, is the space of executions of a bi-pointed directed space
(X,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1})
.

The main source of interesting examples of directed spaces are geometric realizations of precubical sets, also written
\square
–sets for short.
These objects are used in computer science for modeling concurrent automata;
for example Higher Dimensional Automata, introduced by Pratt
[
8
]
, are
\square
–sets enriched with some additional structure.
A
\square
–set
K
is a sequence of sets
(K[n])_{n\geq 0}
of
n
–cubes together with face maps, which determine how these cubes are glued.
We say that
K
is bi-pointed if it is equipped with the initial and the final vertex
\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\in K[0]
.
For a (bi-pointed)
\square
–set
K
we associate its geometric realization
|K|
, which is a (bi-pointed) directed space.
The space
\vec{P}(|K|)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}
of directed paths from
\mathbf{0}
to
\mathbf{1}
will be called the execution space of
K
.
For short, we will usually skip the vertical bars and write
\vec{P}(K)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}
for
\vec{P}(|K|)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}
.

Let
Z
be a
\square
–set with exactly one cube in every dimension.
It is a final object both in the category of
\square
–sets and in the category of bi-pointed
\square
–sets
(the only vertex of
Z
is then regarded as both the initial and the final vertex).
One could expect that the final object does not carry any nontrivial structure,
but this is not the case for
\square
–sets.
Let
\UConf(n,\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the space of unordered configurations of
n
points on the real plane.
The main result of this paper is

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a homotopy equivalence

\vec{P}(Z)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\simeq\coprod_{n\geq 0}\UConf(n,\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Guide to the argument

Following
[
10
]
, we introduce the length of a directed path on (the geometric realization of) a bi-pointed
\square
–set
K
.
The length of a d-path that starts and ends at a vertex is a non-negative integer,
and homotopic d-paths have equal lengths.
As a consequence,
\vec{P}(K)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}
decomposes into the disjoint union of the spaces
\vec{P}(K;n)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}
of d-paths having length
n
.
Then we construct, for every

\square

\square

that induces a homeomorphism

To prove the main theorem,
we need to show that there is a homotopy equivalence
\vec{P}(\tilde{Z}_{n})_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\simeq\UConf(n,\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

A method of calculating the homotopy type of the execution space of

is described in
[
14
]

Namely,

is weakly homotopy equivalent to the nerve of the cube chain category

If

Unfortunately,

Instead, for a fixed set

of permutations of

It turns out that the induced

is free,
and the induced map between execution spaces

\vec{P}(Y^{A})_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\to\vec{P}(\tilde{Z}_{n})_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}

Now we can use the cube chain decomposition

Similarly, the unordered configuration space

To compare these spaces, we introduce double orders.
A double order on a set

We introduce two different (non-strict) partial orders, denoted by

It turns out that the category

Finally, we prove that the spaces

Directed spaces

A d-space

[
3
]
, or
a directed space
, is a topological space
X
with a family
\vec{P}(X)\subseteq P(X)=\map([0,1],X)
of paths, called
d-paths
(or
directed paths
), that contains all constant paths and is closed with respect to concatenations and increasing reparametrizations. The concatenation of paths
\alpha,\beta\in P(X)

\alpha(1)=\beta(0)
, is the path

\alpha*\beta(t)=\begin{cases}\alpha(2t)&\text{for $t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]$,}\\
\beta(2t-1)&\text{for $t\in[\tfrac{1}{2},1]$.}\end{cases}

A continuous map
f:X\to Y
between d-spaces is
a d-map
if it preserves d-paths;
d-spaces and d-maps form a category
\mathbf{dTop}
, which is complete and cocomplete.

A bi-pointed d-space
is a d-space
X
with two chosen points
\mathbf{0}_{X},\mathbf{1}_{X}\in X
.

For
x,y\in X
denote

\vec{P}(X)_{x}^{y}=\{\alpha\in\vec{P}(X)\;|\;\text{$\alpha(0)=x$ and $\alpha(1)=y$}\}.

For
s<t
, a path
[s,t]\to X
will be called a d-path if its linear reparametrization to the unit interval is a d-path. The space of such paths will be denoted
\vec{P}_{[s,t]}(X)
, or
\vec{P}_{[s,t]}(X)_{x}^{y}
if the endpoints are fixed.

Examples of d-spaces are:

•

The directed interval

\vec{I}
, which is the unit interval
I=[0,1]
with increasing continuous maps
I\to I
as d-paths.

•

The directed
n
–cube
\vec{I}^{n}
, which is the
n
–fold Cartesian product of directed intervals
\vec{I}
. A path
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n})\in P(\vec{I}^{n})
is a d-path if all its coordinates
\alpha_{i}
are increasing.

Length of d-paths

Following

•

The length of every bi-pointed d-path is a non-negative integer,

Proposition 4.5

Assume that
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\mathop{\bar{\cup}}{{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sigma}

,
which implies that for all

the preimages

Assume that the functions
h({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}}\sigma)
and

5.
Nerve lemma

Let
G
be a finite group and
let
X
be a right free
G
–space.
We assume that
X
is paracompact and has the
G
–homotopy type of a
G
–CW-complex.
Let
\mathscr{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a finite open cover of
X
.

Definition 5.1
.

A cover
\mathscr{U}
is

(a)

G
–equivariant
if there exists an action of
G
on
I
such that
U_{i}g=U_{ig}
,

(b)

proper
G
–equivariant
if additionally
U_{i}\cap U_{ig}=\emptyset
for every
i\in I
and every
1\neq g\in G
,

(c)

good
if every non-empty intersection of sets in
\mathscr{U}
is contractible,

(d)

complete
if
\mathscr{U}
is closed with respect to non-empty intersections.

The nerve

N\mathscr{U}
of
\mathscr{U}
is the family of subsets
J\subseteq I
such that
\bigcap_{i\in J}U_{i}
is non-empty.
This is a poset with respect to inclusion.
Thus,
\mathscr{U}
can be regarded as a functor

\mathscr{U}:(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)\ni J\mapsto\bigcap_{i\in J}U_{i}\in\mathbf{Top}.

The classical Nerve Lemma states that if
\mathscr{U}
is good, then both maps

(5.1)

|(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)|\overset{p}{\longleftarrow}\hocolim_{(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)}\mathscr{U}\overset{q}{\longrightarrow}\colim_{(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)}\mathscr{U}\cong X

are homotopy equivalences.
Here
p
is induced by the unique transformation of
\mathscr{U}
into the trivial functor on
(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)
,
and
q
is the natural projection from the homotopy colimit into the colimit.

Assume that
\mathscr{U}
is good and proper
G
–equivariant.
The action of
G
on the indexing set
I

induces a
G
–action on
N\mathscr{U}
.
The properness of
\mathscr{U}
implies that this action is free.
As a consequence,
G
acts freely on
|(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)|
.

There is a presentation of the homotopy colimit as a quotient

\hocolim_{(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)}\mathscr{U}\simeq\coprod_{n\geq 0}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{0}\supseteq\dotsm\supseteq J_{n}\\
J_{i}\in N\mathscr{U}\end{subarray}}\Big(\Delta^{n}\times\bigcap_{i\in J_{0}}U_{i}\Big)/\sim.

The induced action of
G
on
\hocolim_{(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)}\mathscr{U}

is given by the formula

(J_{0}\supseteq\dotsm\supseteq J_{n},(t_{0},\dotsc,t_{n}),x)g=(J_{0}g\supseteq\dotsm\supseteq J_{n}g,(t_{0},\dotsc,t_{n}),xg).

Clearly both

and

are
G
–equivariant maps;
as a consequence,
\hocolim_{(N\mathscr{U},\supseteq)}\mathscr{U}

Invariants of directed paths on
\square
–sets

Every bi-pointed
\square
–set

\square

This allows to define some invariants of bi-pointed
\square
–sets, or rather their execution spaces.

(1)

(2)

It turns out that these invariants are trivial if
K
is a sculpture
[
8
]
or a Euclidean complex
[

]
(as shown in
[
14

, these classes are equivalent).

.

Let

be a pointed topological space and let

Elements of

are finite sequences of elements of

and two sequences are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by adding or removing

Namely,

(6.3)

x_{0}
at the last position.
By the classical result of James
[
4
]
, the natural map

is a weak homotopy equivalence.

A sequence of maps

,

and

(see (

Thus, these maps glue to the maps

which are bijective.
Here

stands for the

–skeleton of

.
Indeed, every point of

(respectively

) has a unique presentation as a sequence of at most
n
elements of
(0,1)
(resp.

).
Since the spaces
|Z^{(n)}|
are compact,

By passing to the colimit we obtain the following.

Proposition 6.8

As a consequence, the spaces
|Z|
and
\Omega S^{2}
are homotopy equivalent.
The initial and the final vertex of
Z
are the same.
Thus, we have maps

\vec{P}(Z)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\subseteq\Omega|Z|\cong\Omega J(S^{1})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Omega^{2}(S^{2})=\map_{*}(S^{2},S^{2})

and

\UConf(n,\mathbb{R}^{2})\simeq\vec{P}(Z;n)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\subseteq\vec{P}(Z)_{\mathbf{0}}^{\mathbf{1}}\to\Omega^{2}S^{2}.

This suggests that unordered configurations spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be regarded as “directed” self-maps of
S^{2}
,
although an interpretation of this fact is not clear for the authors.

Presentation of configuration spaces as nerves of certain categories

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite category and let
G
be a finite group.
Assume that
G
acts freely on
\mathcal{C}
from the right, ie.
cg\neq c
for every
1\neq g\in G
and every object
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

The quotient category

\mathcal{C}/G
is the category with objects
\Ob(\mathcal{C}/G)=\Ob(\mathcal{C})/G
and morphisms

(\mathcal{C}/G)(cG,c^{\prime}G)=\left(\coprod_{g\in G}\coprod_{g^{\prime}\in G}\mathcal{C}(cg,c^{\prime}g^{\prime})\right)/G\simeq\coprod_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}(c,c^{\prime}g)\simeq\coprod_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}(cg,c^{\prime}).

The composition of morphisms
\alpha G\in(\mathcal{C}/G)(cG,c^{\prime}G)
and
\beta G\in(\mathcal{C}/G)(c^{\prime}G,c^{\prime\prime}G)

is
(\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime})G
,
where
\alpha^{\prime}\in\coprod_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}(cg,c^{\prime})
(resp.
\beta^{\prime}\in\coprod_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}g)
) is the unique morphism such that
\alpha^{\prime}G=\alpha G
(resp.
\beta^{\prime}G=\beta G
). The projection
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}/G
is a functor.

Remark 6.9
.

The quotient category
\mathcal{C}/G
can be defined for arbitrary, non necessarily free action of
G
, as a colimit of the suitable diagram in the category of small categories. But, in general, there is no guarantee that morphisms in the quotient category
\mathcal{C}/G
are exactly orbits of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

If a group
G
acts freely on a category
\mathcal{C}
, then the map
|\mathcal{C}|/G\to|\mathcal{C}/G|
induced by the projection
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}/G
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}/G
induces an isomorphism of simplicial sets
N(\mathcal{C})/G\to N(\mathcal{C}/G)
.
The conclusion follows.
∎

From the main theorem and Lemma
6.10
follows that there is a homotopy equivalence

\UConf(n;\mathbb{R}^{2})=\OConf(A,\mathbb{R}^{2})/\Sigma_{A}\simeq|(R(A),\sqsupseteq)|/\Sigma_{A}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}|(R(A),\sqsupseteq)/\Sigma_{A}|,

which is a presentation of
\UConf(n;\mathbb{R}^{2})
as a nerve of a category.
Following
[
7
]
, we present below an explicit description of the category
(R(A),\sqsupseteq)/\Sigma_{A}
.
We will use notation introduced in Section
4
without further notice.

Definition 6.11
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{n}
be the category whose objects are subsets of
\{1,\dotsc,n-1\}
.
For

B=\{b_{1}<\dotsm<b_{r-1}\}\supseteq B^{\prime}=\{b^{\prime}_{1}<\dotsm<b^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}-1}\}\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,n-1\},

morphisms from
B
to
B^{\prime}

are permutations
\varphi
of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}

satisfyng the conditions:

(x)

\varphi(\{b^{\prime}_{p-1}+1,\dotsc,b^{\prime}_{p}\})=\{b^{\prime}_{p-1}+1,\dotsc,b^{\prime}_{p}\}
for
p\in\{1,\dotsc,r^{\prime}\}
(we assume
b^{\prime}_{0}=0
,
b^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}=n
).

(y)

\varphi(b_{p-1}+1)<\varphi(b_{p-1}+2)<\dotsm<\varphi(b_{p})
for
p\in\{1,\dotsc,r\}
(again,
b_{0}=0
,
b_{r}=n
).

For
B\not\supseteq B^{\prime}
we assume
\mathcal{E}_{n}(B,B^{\prime})=\emptyset
.
The composition in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is the composition of permutations.

Note that (x) is equivalent to the condition

(x’)

\forall i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}:\;(\exists b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}:\;i\leq b^{\prime}<j)\implies\varphi(i)<\varphi(j)
,

and that
\varphi
satisfies (x) if and only if
\varphi^{-1}
satisfies (x).

We will construct a functor
F:(R(A),\sqsubseteq)\to\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
For a regular double order
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in R(A)

let

F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}})=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r-1}\}\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,n-1\},

where
b_{p}=|\{a\in A\st h({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}})(a)\leq p\}|
,
r=l({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}})
.

Definition 6.12
.

Let
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in R(A)
,
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}})=\{b_{1}<\dotsm<b_{r-1}\}
.

The monotonic numbering
for
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in R(A)
is a presentation
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
such that

a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}a_{j}\iff\exists p:\;i\leq b_{p}<j,\qquad a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}a_{j}\iff\exists p:\;b_{p-1}<i<j\leq b_{p}.

Such a numbering exists and is unique.

For
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\in R(A)
let
A=\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\}
and
A=\{a^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,a^{\prime}_{n}\}
be the monotonic numberings for
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}
and
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}
, respectively.
We define
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}})
to be the unique permutation
\varphi\in\Sigma_{n}
such that
a^{\prime}_{\varphi(i)}=a_{i}
for all
i
.
It is easy to verify that
F
preserves composition.
It is a functor thanks to the following.

Proposition 6.13
.

If
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\in R(A)
, then
\varphi:=F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}),F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}))
.

Proof.

We have
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}}\supseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}
and
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}}\subseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}
.
Let
a_{i}
,
a^{\prime}_{i}
,
b_{p}
,
b^{\prime}_{p}
be as above.

For
i,j\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
we have

\exists p:\;i\leq b^{\prime}_{p}<j\iff a^{\prime}_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a^{\prime}_{j}\implies a^{\prime}_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}a^{\prime}_{j}\iff a_{\varphi^{-1}(i)}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}a_{\varphi^{-1}(j)}\implies\varphi^{-1}(i)<\varphi^{-1}(j)

Thus,
\varphi^{-1}
satisfies (x’) and, therefore,
\varphi
satisfies (x).
Also,

b_{p-1}<i<j\leq b_{p}\implies a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}a_{j}\implies a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a_{j}\iff a^{\prime}_{\varphi(i)}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a^{\prime}_{\varphi(j)}\implies\varphi(i)<\varphi(j)

and then
\varphi
satisfies (y).
∎

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma_{A}
. Then

(a)

F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sigma)=F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}})
for
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in\ R(A)
,

(b)

F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}})=F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sigma\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\sigma)
for
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\in R(A)
.

Proof.

We have
h({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}}\sigma)=h({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}})\sigma
, which implies (a).
Assume that
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
(a^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are monotonic numberings of
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}
and
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}
, respectively,
and that
\varphi=F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}})
.
Then
(\sigma^{-1}(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n})
and
(\sigma^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{n})
are monotonic numberings of
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sigma
and
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\sigma
, respectively.
We have
\sigma^{-1}(a_{i})=\sigma^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{\varphi(i)})

and, therefore,
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sigma\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}\sigma)=\varphi
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
6.14
,
F
induces the quotient functor

\bar{F}:(R(A),\sqsupseteq)/\Sigma_{A}\to\mathcal{E}_{n}.

Proposition 6.15
.

The functor
\bar{F}
is an isomorphism of categories.

Proof.

Let
B=\{b_{1}<\dotsm<b_{r-1}\}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
Every numbering
A=\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\}
is the monotonic numbering of the unique double order
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in R(A)
,
and all double orders
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}
such that
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}})=B
can be obtained this way.
As a consequence,
\bar{F}
is a bijection on objects.

Now let
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(B,B^{\prime})
,
B^{\prime}=\{b^{\prime}_{1}<\dotsm<b^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}\}
,
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\in F^{-1}(B)
and let
A=\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\}
be the monotonic numbering for
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}
.
The double order
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}
given by

a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a_{j}\iff\exists p:\;\varphi(i)\leq b^{\prime}_{p}<\varphi(j),\qquad a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a_{j}\iff\exists p:\;b^{\prime}_{p-1}<\varphi(i)<\varphi(j)\leq b^{\prime}_{p}

is the only possible regular double order such that
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}})=B^{\prime}
and
F({\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}})=\varphi
.
It remains to check that
{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}}\sqsupseteq{\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}}
. We have

a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a_{j}\iff\exists p:\;\varphi(i)\leq b^{\prime}_{p}<\varphi(j)\overset{\text{(x)}}{\implies}\exists p:\;i\leq b^{\prime}_{p}<j\overset{B\supseteq B^{\prime}}{\implies}\exists q:\;i\leq b_{q}<j\iff a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle x$}\vss}}}}a_{j}.

and

a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}a_{j}\iff\exists q:\;b_{q-1}<i<j\leq b_{q}\overset{\text{(x),(y)}}{\implies}\exists p:\;b^{\prime}_{p-1}<\varphi(i)<\varphi(j)\leq b^{\prime}_{p}\iff a_{i}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle y$}\vss}}}}^{\prime}a_{j}.

Thus,
F^{-1}(\varphi)
contains exactly one element for every
\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{<}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle*$}\vss}}}}\in F^{-1}(B)
, ie, it is a single orbit.
Therefore,
\bar{F}
is a bijection on morphisms.
∎

Proposition 6.16

For every

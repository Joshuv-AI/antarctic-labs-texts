Intermediate super-exponential localization with Aubry-André chains
Arindam Mallick, Alexei Andreanov, Sergej Flach

Abstract
We demonstrate the existence of an intermediate super-exponential localization regime for eigenstates of the Aubry-André chain. In this regime, the eigenstates localize factorially similarly to the eigenstates of the Wannier-Stark ladder. The super-exponential decay emerges on intermediate length scales for large values of the winding length —the quasi-period of the Aubry-André potential. This intermediate localization is present both in the metallic and insulating phases of the system. In the insulating phase, the super-exponential localization is periodically interrupted by weaker decaying tails to form the conventional asymptotic exponential decay predicted for the Aubry-André model. In the metallic phase, the super-exponential localization happens for states with energies away from the center of the spectrum and is followed by a super-exponential growth into the next peak of the extended eigenstate. By adjusting the parameters it is possible to arbitrarily extend the validity of the super-exponential localization. A similar intermediate super-exponential localization regime is demonstrated in quasiperiodic discrete-time unitary maps.



I
Introduction

Uncorrelated disorder potentials enforce exponential Anderson localization for all eigenstates in one-dimensional potentials
Anderson 1958
;
Kramer and MacKinnon 1993
.
This holds for any strength of the disorder potential relative to the kinetic energy strength induced e.g. by the hopping
t
between nearest neighbour (n.n.) sites on a one-dimensional chain.
Quasi-periodic potentials instead can be viewed as highly correlated aperiodic ones.
Consequently the one-dimensional model shows a metal-insulator transition with eigenstates changing from extended to exponentially localized upon increasing the quasi-periodic potential strength
\lambda
relative to the hopping
t
.
This was first demonstrated for the Aubry-André chain (AA)
Aubry and André 1980
;
Lahini et al. 2009
.
The AA model and its generalizations proved to be useful in different branches of physics, e.g., many-body localization
Mastropietro 2015
;
Doggen and Mirlin 2019
;
Strkalj et al. 2021
;
Sarkar et al. 2022
, and topological insulators
Zeng et al. 2020a
;
Zeng et al. 2020b
;
Fraxanet et al. 2021
.
Theory predicts
Aubry and André 1980
and simulations confirm
Lahini et al. 2009
the asymptotic exponential decay of the eigenstates
Roati et al. 2008
for strong enough potential
\lambda
.
However the intermediate but potentially long-lasting non-asymptotic decay, i.e. the decay of the probability profile on intermediate length scales can show substantial deviation from the established asymptotic ones.

The AA model has four parameters: hopping strength
t
, potential strength
\lambda
, an irrational
winding length

\mathcal{W}
or spatial quasi-period of the potential, and a phase
\phi
which is usually absorbed by a shift of space origin.
The AA model is known to show asymptotic exponential eigenstate localization in its localized phase
\absolutevalue{\lambda}>2\absolutevalue{t}
.
The localization length
\xi
is depending only on one parameter—the ratio of potential strength to hopping strength (see the section below).
In particular,
\xi
is not depending on neither the winding length, nor on the position of the eigenstate, nor on its eigenenergy.
However, the precise form of the localized eigenstate does depend on the winding length, and also on its position in space relative to the AA potential, and also on its energy.
We show that such eigenstates develop a broad core of nondecaying oscillating parts for large winding length, due to the slow variation of the AA potential and the finite strength of the hopping
t
.
At the same time the decay away from the core is controlled over large length scales of the order of the winding length by faster than exponential—super-exponential—factorial decay in the same regime.
Further details can also depend on the energy and the spatial location of the eigenstate.
Exponential decay is observed on distances larger than the winding length.
Remarkably, these features are also observed in the metallic phase,
\absolutevalue{\lambda}<2\absolutevalue{t}
, for states with energies close to the edge of the spectrum.
The super-exponential decay is similar to the decay of the eigenstates of the Wannier-Stark ladder
Fukuyama et al. 1973
;
Mallick et al. 2021
.
Very high precision and very large lattice sizes are required in this regime in order to see that such eigenstates are actually exponentially localized or extended, as predicted by the theory
Aubry and André 1980
.

We further show that similar results hold for unitary maps as well.
Time evolution under discrete-time unitary maps is usually computationally faster compared to the Hamiltonian evolution
Vakulchyk et al. 2018
;
Vakulchyk et al. 2019
;
Malishava and Flach 2022
.
Therefore identifying a unitary map with properties identical or similar to a given Hamiltonian model is important for the development of fast simulation tools for unitary dynamics.
We consider a unitary map (UM): a split-step discrete-time quantum walk
Hamza et al. 2009
;
Kitagawa 2012
;
Mallick and Chandrashekar 2016
with a spatially varying quasi-periodic phase factor.
Similarly to the AA Hamiltonian model with
\absolutevalue{\lambda}>2\absolutevalue{t}
, the eigenstates of this UM localize exponentially
Cedzich and Werner 2021
.
For smaller phase factor and coin parameters, we identify the intermediate super-exponential localization regime.
This allows in principle to use these maps to approximate Wannier-Stark Hamiltonians in the unitary map setting and achieve faster simulation of their dynamics.

The article is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
we discuss the AA Hamiltonian model and its intermediate localization properties for large winding length
\mathcal{W}
, including analytical derivation of the asymptotic decay of eigenstates for power-law potentials on a chain.
Sec.
III
covers numerical evidences for the intermediate super-exponential localization in the AA chain with large winding lengths.
In Sec.
IV
we construct the quasiperiodic discrete-time unitary map, that features the transient plane wave and the super-exponential localization regimes for fine-tuned parameters, similarly to the AA Hamiltonian.
We conclude in Sec.
V
.

II
Intermediate localization regime in the Aubry-André chain

The AA Hamiltonian with an incommensurate potential on the 1D chain reads
Aubry and André 1980

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}[-t(\outerproduct{n}{n+1}+\outerproduct{n}{n-1})+\lambda\cos(\alpha n + \phi)\outerproduct{n}{n}]\;.

(1)

Here
t\in\mathbb{R}
is the n.n. hopping parameter, while
\alpha/2\pi
is an irrational number ensuring the quasiperiodicity of the potential.
Without losing generality we consider
\alpha>0
.
The potential
\cos(\alpha n + \phi)
almost repeats itself at lattice sites
n
and
n+\lfloor 2\pi/\alpha\rfloor
(or
n+\lceil 2\pi/\alpha\rceil
) for arbitrary
n
.
Therefore we define the
winding length

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\lceil 2\pi/\alpha\rceil\geq 1\;.

(2)

The Hamiltonian (
1
) has
asymptotically
exponentially localized eigenstates for
\absolutevalue{\lambda}>2\absolutevalue{t}
, which are peaked around some eigenstate dependent lattice site
n_{0}
.
The asymptotic localization length

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{1}{\ln\absolutevalue{\frac{\lambda}{2t}}}

(3)

describes the asymptotic decay of all eigenstates
\psi_{n}\sim{\rm e}^{-|n|/\xi}
and is independent of the winding length
\mathcal{W}
and the phase
\phi

Aubry and André 1980
.

II.1
Large winding length

We now demonstrate that there is an
intermediate localization
regime whose span is governed by the winding length
\mathcal{W}
and the phase
\phi
.
In this intermediate regime eigenfunctions decay faster than exponential similarly to the eigenstates of the Wannier-Stark ladder.
For that we consider the case of very small values of
\absolutevalue{\alpha}\ll 1
:
there is a region of the 1D chain, a subset of the chain sites
\mathbb{L}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{L}
and a particular eigenstate peak
n_{0}\in\mathbb{L}
the following condition holds

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\alpha(n - n_0)}\ll 1\;.

(4)

Therefore we can approximate the cosine potential up to the second order expansion in
\alpha(n-n_{0})
as

\displaystyle\cos(\alpha n + \phi)

\displaystyle\approx\cos(\alpha n_0 + \phi)\left[1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}{(n-n_{0})}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\sin(\alpha n_0 + \phi)\left[\alpha(n-n_{0})\right]\;.

(5)

The choice of the phase
\phi
allows to approximate either linear or quadratic potentials.
For
\alpha n_{0}+\phi\approx(2m+1)\pi/2
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the Hamiltonian (
1
) is approximated by the Wannier-Stark Hamiltonian (WS) on a 1D chain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=-t\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\outerproduct{n}{n+1}+\outerproduct{n}{n-1}

\displaystyle-{(-1)}^{m}\lambda\alpha\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(n-n_{0})\outerproduct{n}{n}\;.

(6)

The effective DC field strength is given by
\mathcal{F}=\lambda\alpha
which has to be compared to the hopping strength
t

Mallick et al. 2021
.
Given
\absolutevalue{\alpha}\ll 1
, one needs strong potential strengths
\absolutevalue{\lambda/t}\gg 1
to achieve even moderate field values
\mathcal{F}\approx t
.

For
\alpha n_{0}+\phi\approx m\pi
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
the AA Hamiltonian (
1
) approximates the discrete simple harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian (SHO) on a 1D chain

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=-t\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\outerproduct{n}{n+1}+\outerproduct{n}{n-1}

\displaystyle+{(-1)}^{m}\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[1-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}{(n-n_{0})}^{2}\right]\outerproduct{n}{n}\;.

(7)

The harmonic potential strength is given by
\mathcal{F}=\lambda\alpha^{2}/2
.

II.2
Asymptotic decay of eigenfunctions for power-law potentials on a chain

We start by discussing the localization properties of eigenstates in tight-binding chains with power-law potentials.
A tight-binding lattice Hamiltonian with a simple power potential with exponent
\mu
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[-t(\outerproduct{n}{n+1}+\outerproduct{n}{n-1})+\mathcal{F}n^{\mu}\outerproduct{n}{n}\right]\;.

(8)

The eigenvalue equation for an eigenstate at energy
E
reads as

\displaystyle E\psi_{n}=-t\psi_{n+1}-t\psi_{n-1}+\mathcal{F}n^{\mu}\psi_{n}\;.

(9)

The eigenstates localize factorially, i.e. super-exponentially in
n
for the linear potential
\mu=1
, e.g. the Wannier-Stark case
Fukuyama et al. 1973
;
Mallick et al. 2021
.
This implies that the ratio
\psi_{n}/\psi_{n-1}
decays asymptotically as
n^{-1}
for large
n
, e.g. far away from the peak of the eigenstate.
For larger exponents (
\mu>1
), we expect an even stronger decay with
n
, and we use this observation to approximate the eigenvalue equation for very large
n
, away from the peak of the localized eigenstate.
Hence we neglect
E\psi_{n}
and
t\psi_{n+1}
compared to the other terms in Eq. (
9
) for a finite eigenvalue
E
, a finite hopping strength
t
and a finite field strength
\mathcal{F}
,
and simplify the eigenequation to a simple recursion

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\mathcal{F}}n^{-\mu}\psi_{n-1}\approx\psi_{n}\;.

(10)

This resolves into

\displaystyle\psi_{n+n_{0}}\approx{\left(\frac{t}{\mathcal{F}}\right)}^{n}{(n!)}^{-\mu}\psi_{n_{0}}\;,

(11)

where
n_{0}
is the peak of the eigenstate.
Similar arguments apply for very large negative values of
n
, i.e., on other side of the peak of an eigenstate
\psi_{n}
.

It follows from the normalization condition of the probability distribution that
\psi_{n_{0}}
is a finite constant.
Therefore the eigenstate decays super-exponentially for large positive lattice sites
n
as

\displaystyle{\left(\frac{t}{\mathcal{F}}\right)}^{n}{(n!)}^{-\mu}\approx{\left(\frac{tn^{-\mu}}{\mathcal{F}e^{-\mu}}\right)}^{n}{(2\pi n)}^{-\frac{\mu}{2}}\approx e^{-\mu n\ln(n)}\;.

(12)

Here the Stirling’s approximation
n!\approx\sqrt{2\pi n}{(n/e)}^{n}
for large
n
was used.
Therefore we expect the same super-exponential decay of the eigenstates for any power-law potential
n^{\mu}
, up to the factor
\mu
in the exponent.

We note that this asymptotic behavior is very different from the asymptotic localization properties in the continuous 1D space
\{x\in\mathbb{R}\}
where the eigenvalue equation is

\displaystyle E\psi_{x}=-\frac{d^{2}\psi_{x}}{dx^{2}}+\mathcal{F}(x-x_{0})^{\mu}\psi_{x}\;.

(13)

For the linear case
\mu=1
the wave function
\psi_{x}
takes the form of Airy function which for
x\gg x_{0}
decays as
\left(\mathcal{F}^{1/3}x\right)^{-1/4}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\left(\mathcal{F}^{1/3}x\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}
.
On the other hand for the simple harmonic potential
\mu=2
, the wave function
\psi_{x}
is a product of a Gaussian and Hermite polynomials, their asymptotic decay is controlled by the Gaussian:
e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}}x^{2}}{2}}
.
In both cases the asymptotic decay is faster than that of the discrete cases,
e^{-\mu x\ln(x)}
.

III
Numerical results

We refer to
\absolutevalue{\psi_n}^{2}
(modulus squared of an eigenstate’s amplitude) as probability at a site
n
and talk about probability profile
\{\absolutevalue{\psi_n}^{2}\}
of an eigenstate.
As well we refer to the core of a localized eigenstate as its peak.
We set the hopping strength
t=1
and measure
\alpha
in units of
2\pi\varphi
with the golden ratio
\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\approx 1.61803
.
We consider finite chains with open boundary condition, numerically diagonalize the Aubry-André Hamiltonian (
1
) with
\phi=\pi/2
for different winding lengths
\mathcal{W}
and check the decay of probability profiles of the eigenstates.

To quantify the decay of the probability profiles and discriminate between their exponential and super-exponential decay, we use two metrics.
First, we define the local gradient of the logarithms of the probability with respect to the lattice position

\displaystyle\Delta(n)=\frac{\ln\absolutevalue{\psi_{n+1}}^{2}-\ln\absolutevalue{\psi_{n-1}}^{2}}{2}=\ln\absolutevalue{\frac{\psi_{n+1}}{\psi_{n-1}}}\;.

(14)

We refer to it as simply the
gradient
in the text below.
A constant in
n
value of the gradient indicates exponential localization while the decay with
n
signals faster than exponential decay.
For an exponential decay
e^{-2|n|/\xi}
with a constant localization length
\xi
,
\Delta(n)
is a step function,
with values
\frac{2}{\xi}
to the left and
-\frac{2}{\xi}
to right of the probability peak
n=0
.
Second, for a super-exponential e.g. factorial decay of the eigenstates of the Wannier-Stark ladder the following ratio takes an asymptotically constant value:

\displaystyle R(\mu,n)=\frac{\ln\absolutevalue{\psi_{|n| + n_0}}^{2}-\ln\absolutevalue{\psi_{n_0}}^{2}}{\mu\ln(|n|!)-|n|\ln|t/\mathcal{F}|}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}-2

(15)

as follows from Eq. (
11
).
We use the gradient
\Delta
and the
R
value in our numerical simulations to detect and quantify the super-exponential decay.

In what follows we refer to
localization on intermediate scales
as simply
localization
, while the usual AA exponential localization is referred to as
asymptotic localization
.
In all the other cases the type of localization should be clear from the context.

IV
Intermediate super-exponential localization in discrete-time unitary maps

Figure 6:

Pictorial representation of two time steps,
T=1,2
of the unitary map.
The small red spheres indicate lattice sites.
Blue shaded rectangles indicate the coin operations, steps I and II in Eq. (

),
the yellow shaded squares indicate phase operations, step III in Eq. (

The emergence of the super-exponential localization on intermediate length scales followed by an asymptotic exponential decay in the AA Hamiltonian (
1
) can also be realized in other settings with quasiperiodic potentials.
In particular, we consider a discrete-time unitary map, the linear and non-disordered version of the maps used in Ref.
Hamza et al. 2009
;
Malishava and Flach 2022
.
These maps provide a convenient computational tool for studying long-time dynamics of nonlinear systems
Vakulchyk et al. 2019
;
Malishava and Flach 2022
.
It is therefore important to extend these maps to various settings, e.g. to mimic the effect of a DC field.

We start by defining the unitary map.
For that we consider a single particle on an infinite 1D chain with time evolution governed by the brickwork action of local unitary operators and described by the following three steps depicted schematically in Fig.
6
:
All the sites are split into disjoint pairs of the n.n. sites.

Step I
:
Probability amplitudes on every pair of sites are mixed by applying an orthogonal
\mathrm{SO}(2)
matrix:

\displaystyle a_{2n-1}(T+1)=\cos\theta\psi_{2n-1}(T)+\sin\theta\psi_{2n}(T),

\displaystyle a_{2n}(T+1)=-\sin\theta\psi_{2n-1}(T)+\cos\theta\psi_{2n}(T);

Step II
:
make a cyclic shift of all the sites by one lattice spacing and mix the amplitudes of the pairs again by applying the same
\mathrm{SO}(2)
matrix:

\displaystyle b_{2n}(T+1)=\cos\theta a_{2n}(T+1)+\sin\theta a_{2n+1}(T+1),

\displaystyle b_{2n+1}(T+1)=-\sin\theta a_{2n}(T+1)+\cos\theta a_{2n+1}(T+1);

Step III
:
add local random phases
\varepsilon_{n}
to all the amplitudes

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(T+1)=e^{i\varepsilon_{n}}b_{n}(T+1)\;.

(17)

Combining all the three steps together produces the final unitary map:

\displaystyle\psi_{2n}(T+1)

\displaystyle=e^{i\varepsilon_{2n}}[\cos^{2}\theta\psi_{2n}(T)-\cos\theta\sin\theta\psi_{2n-1}(T)

\displaystyle+\sin\theta\cos\theta\psi_{2n+1}(T)+\sin^{2}\theta\psi_{2n+2}(T)],

\displaystyle\psi_{2n+1}(T+1)

\displaystyle=e^{i\varepsilon_{2n+1}}[\cos^{2}\theta\psi_{2n+1}(T)-\sin\theta\cos\theta\psi_{2n}(T)

\displaystyle+\cos\theta\sin\theta\psi_{2n+2}(T)+\sin^{2}\theta\psi_{2n-1}(T)]\;.

(18)

In the absence of the local phase,
\varepsilon_{n}=0
for all sites
n
, the map is translationally invariant and has extended eigenstates with eigenvalues
e^{-iE}
sitting on the unit circle in the complex plane.
The eigenvalues are parametrized by their angle (phase)
E
which is a function of lattice momentum or wave number
k
:
E=\pm\arccos(\cos^2 \theta+ \cos k \sin^2\theta)

Malishava 2022
.
For our work we consider
0<\theta<\pi
.
The bandwidth is
\Delta E=4\theta
.
For a quasiperiodic local phase
\varepsilon_{n}=-\alpha n
with an irrational
\alpha/\pi
, this unitary evolution displays asymptotic exponential localization with localization length

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{1}{|\ln(|\sin\theta|)|}

(19)

as shown in Ref.
Cedzich and Werner 2021
(Proposition 3.2).
This setup is similar to the case of the electric quantum walk with an irrational phase
Cedzich et al. 2013
;
Arnault et al. 2020
or the aperiodic quantum walks
Ribeiro et al. 2004
.

Similarly to the AA case, we consider
\absolutevalue{\alpha}\ll 1
so that
\absolutevalue{\alpha n}\ll\absolutevalue{n}
for all
n
on a finite chain, so that
e^{-i\alpha n}\approx 1-i\alpha n
is justified.
Furthermore to allow nearest neighbor hopping only (as in the AA Hamiltonian) we also require
\theta\ll 1
, so that
\cos\theta\approx 1
and
\sin\theta\approx\theta
.
Then the
\alpha\theta
and
\theta^{2}
terms are negligible compared to
\alpha
and
\theta
respectively, and the unitary map (
18
) becomes approximately

\displaystyle\psi_{2n}(T+1)

\displaystyle\approx(1-i\alpha(2n))\psi_{2n}(T)

(20a)

\displaystyle-\theta(\psi_{2n-1}(T)-\psi_{2n+1}(T)),

\displaystyle\psi_{2n+1}(T+1)

\displaystyle\approx(1-i\alpha(2n+1))\psi_{2n+1}(T)

(20b)

\displaystyle-\theta(\psi_{2n}(T)-\psi_{2n+2}(T))\;.

The two equations (
20
) are the same up to the relabelling of the lattice sites, and correspond to the discrete time version of the 1D Wannier-Stark Schrödinger Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ap}}\ket{\psi(T)}=i\left[\ket{\psi(T+1)}-\ket{\psi(T)}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ap}}=i\theta\sum_{n}\outerproduct{n}{n+1}-\outerproduct{n}{n-1}+\alpha\sum_{n}n\outerproduct{n}{n}\;.

(21)

From the above expressions we can define the effective DC field strength
\mathcal{F}=\alpha
and hopping parameter
t=i\theta
.
Based on this mapping of the parameters, we expect that the eigenstates of the exact unitary map (
18
) display super-exponential localization on intermediate length scales for
\absolutevalue{\alpha},\theta\ll 1
,
followed by the asymptotical exponential localization, similarly to the Aubry-André case.
We also define the winding length
\mathcal{W}=\lceil 2\pi/\alpha\rceil
, that controls the intermediate length scale behaviour just like in the AA model above.
In analogy to the AA chain reasoning we predict that the span of the core of an eigenstate is
\mathcal{S}\approx\frac{4\absolutevalue{\theta}}{\alpha}\approx\frac{2\absolutevalue{\theta}}{\pi}\mathcal{W}

and super-exponential decay should go uninterrupted over one winding length
\mathcal{W}
.

To verify these predictions numerically, we first transform the unitary map (
18
) into a discrete time quantum random walk
Aharonov et al. 1993
.
The unitary map (
18
) acts differently on odd and even sites, and has consequently two bands in the spectrum.
The unitary map can be viewed as a special case of the split-step discrete-time quantum walk (SS-QW)
Kitagawa 2012
;
Mallick and Chandrashekar 2016
on a 1D chain.
In order to reformulate our unitary map as the quantum walk, each pair of odd and even sites of the original chain are merged into a single site of a new chain with two internal states:
spin up
\uparrow
and spin down
\downarrow
, corresponding to the odd and even sites of the original chain respectively
1
1

1

Alternatively, one can map even and odd sites to spin up and down respectively.

\displaystyle\ket{2n-1}\mapsto\ket{m, \uparrow}=(1~0)^{T}\otimes\ket{m},

\displaystyle\ket{2n}\mapsto\ket{m, \downarrow}=(0~1)^{T}\otimes\ket{m}\;.

(22)

The spin degrees of freedom define the coin space on every site of the new chain.
The new lattice site index
m=2n
takes only even values, in order to match with the minimum distance between unit cells in the original lattice.
A single time step of the SS-QW reads:

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{ss-qw}}=P\cdot S_{2}\cdot C_{2}\cdot S_{1}\cdot C_{1},

(23)

\displaystyle P=\sum_{m\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\varepsilon_{m,\uparrow}}&0\\
0&e^{i\varepsilon_{m,\downarrow}}\\
\end{pmatrix}\otimes\outerproduct{m}{m},

\displaystyle S_{2}=\sum_{m\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}\outerproduct{m+2}{m}&0\\
0&\outerproduct{m}{m}\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{m\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}\outerproduct{m}{m}&0\\
0&\outerproduct{m-2}{m}\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle C_{j}=\sum_{m\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta&(-1)^{j}\cos\theta\\
(-1)^{j+1}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
\end{pmatrix}\otimes\outerproduct{m}{m}\;.

Here
C_{1,2}
are unitary operations in the coin space while
S_{1,2}
are the coin state dependent shift operators on the chain.

P
is the coin and site dependent unitary phase shift.
Note that the conventional discrete-time quantum walk is recovered by setting
C_{2}
to an identity matrix.
In what follows we focus on the localization properties of eigenstates of the evolution operator of this quantum walk (
23
).

V
Conclusion

We have shown that for a general power-law potential on a tight-binding chain all the eigenstates decay factorially i.e., super-exponentially.
This is a slower decay as compared to the case in continuous space.

We further demonstrated that such super-exponential decay emerges on intermediate lengthscales in the 1D AA model.
In the insulating regime of the AA model,
\absolutevalue{\lambda}>2\absolutevalue{t}
, we demonstrated that by fine-tuning the parameters of the Hamiltonian one can obtain an intermediate super-exponential localization regime,
that persists down to extremely small values of the amplitudes of the eigenstates.
This makes the asymptotic exponential localization irrelevant for many practical purposes.
The presence of this regime is controlled by the winding length
\mathcal{W}\approx 2\pi/\alpha
over which the AA potential almost repeats itself.
This behavior is based on the observation that for large winding lengths, much bigger than the lattice spacing or comparable to the system size, the Aubry-André potential is well approximated by a power-law potential,
e.g. the Wannier-Stark (linear) or discrete harmonic oscillator (quadratic) potentials, for extended parts of the chain.

In the metallic regime,
\absolutevalue{\lambda}<2\absolutevalue{t}
, theory predicts extended eigenstates only.
For small
\mathcal{W}
, within our finite system size, all the eigenstates are indeed extended.
For large winding lengths
\mathcal{W}
, the eigenstates close to the center of the spectrum,
E\lesssim 2t
, are extended.
However, surprisingly, the eigenvalues closer to the edges of the spectrum feature super-exponentially localized peaks.
One would have to consider system sizes much larger than the winding length
\mathcal{W}
in order to see that these state are extended.

We also demonstrated analytically and confirmed numerically that such intermediate super-exponential localization preceding the asymptotic exponential localization, is also achievable in unitary maps/discrete-time quantum walks.
This setup could be implemented in state-of-art experimental devices
Alderete et al. 2020
.

Our work suggests a novel regime to explore in the well-known quasi-periodic lattice model which is useful in many branches of physics.
The large winding length parameters can be achieved by the state-of-art experimental devices, e.g. in a one-dimensional bichromatic potential obtained by superimposing two optical lattices having very different wavelengths
Modugno 2009
.

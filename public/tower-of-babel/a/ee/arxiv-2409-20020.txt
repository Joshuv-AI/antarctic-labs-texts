Optimal Infinite-Horizon Mixed H 2 / H ∞ \mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty} Control
Vikrant Malik 1 2 Taylan Kargin 1 2 Joudi Hajar 1 Babak Hassibi 1

Abstract
We study the problem of mixed H 2 / H ∞ \mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty} control in the infinite-horizon setting. We identify the optimal causal controller that minimizes the H 2 \mathit{H}_{2} -cost of the closed-loop system subject to an H ∞ \mathit{H}_{\infty} constraint. Megretski [ 1 ] proved that the optimal mixed H 2 / H ∞ \mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty} controller is non-rational whenever the constraint is active, without giving an explicit construction of the controller. In this work, we provide the first exact closed-form solution to the infinite-horizon mixed H 2 / H ∞ \mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty} control in the frequency domain. While the optimal controller is non-rational, our formulation provides a finite-dimensional parameterization of the optimal controller. Leveraging this fact, we introduce an efficient iterative algorithm that finds the optimal causal controller in the frequency domain. We show that this algorithm is convergent when the system is scalar and present numerical evidence for exponential convergence of the proposed algorithm. Finally, we show how to find the best (in H ∞ H_{\infty} norm) fixed-order rational approximations of the optimal mixed H 2 / H ∞ \mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty} controller, and study its performance.



I
Introduction

Performance and robustness are the two most desired characteristics of a controller. Tackling uncertainty plays a pivotal role in the realm of control, especially within control systems that are frequently exposed to a range of uncertainties including external disruptions, inaccuracies in measurements, deviations in models, and time-varying system dynamics. Ignoring such uncertainties during the development of control policies can significantly undermine performance. The traditional
\mathit{H}_{2}

[
2
]
and
\mathit{H}_{\infty}

[
3
]
control are the two main approaches to address the dichotomy of robustness and performance. While
\mathit{H}_{2}
control aims to achieve optimal average case performance for stochastic white disturbances,
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control guarantees robustness to worst-case deterministic disturbances with bounded energy. They both result in rational controllers which can be efficiently synthesized by solving a set of algebraic Riccati equations.

When it comes to designing a controller that is both robust to worst-case disturbances and optimal in the average case performance, a natural idea is to find the best
\mathit{H}_{2}
-optimal controller among a family of suboptimal
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers. Also known as the mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control, these controllers provide a trade-off between the performance of
\mathit{H}_{2}
and the robustness of the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers. Although both
\mathit{H}_{2}
and central
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers are rational and admit finite-order state-space realizations
[
3
]
, Megretski
[
1
]
showed that the mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller has infinite order whenever the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm constraint is not redundant.

I-B

Contributions

In this work, we study the infinite-horizon mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}
-optimal control with
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm constraints of finite-order, discrete-time, linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. While several past works
[
12
,
13
]
assumed separation of the disturbances into stochastic and deterministic components, we make no such separation assumption. In particular, we consider the full-information setting where the controller can access current and past disturbances. Our major contributions are as follows:

i.

The Exact Stabilizing Optimal Controller:
Moving away from earlier approaches that utilized approximation strategies and auxiliary problems to obtain a finite-dimensional optimization problem, we find the
exact stabilizing optimal controller
in the frequency-domain for the infinite-horizon mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
problem.

ii.

A Finite-Dimensional Characterization:
While we confirm that the optimal controller is irrational as shown in
[
1
]
, we show that a finite-dimensional parameter completely characterizes it.

iii.

An Efficient Numerical Method:
Exploiting the finite-dimensional characterization, we propose an iterative fixed point method to compute the optimal mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller in the frequency domain to arbitrary fidelity.

iv.

Fixed-order Rational Approximations:
Given a finite order, we find the best rational approximation (in
H_{\infty}
norm) to the optimal mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller and obtain a state space structure for the approximate controller. We provide numerical simulations to analyze the performance of the mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller and its rational approximations for different orders.

II-A

Linear-Quadratic Control

We consider a discrete-time linear time-invariant (LTI) dynamical system described by its state-space representation as:

x_{t+1}=Ax_{t}+B_{u}u_{t}+B_{w}w_{t},

(1)

Here, the vectors
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
,
u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
, and
w_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{w}}
denote the state, control input, and exogenous disturbance at time
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. At a given time instance
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, the controller suffers a per stage cost
c_{t}\coloneqq x_{t}^{\intercal}Qx_{t}+u_{t}^{\intercal}Ru_{t}
, where
Q,R\succ 0
. We assume that
(A,B_{u})
and
(A,B_{w})
are stabilizable. For notational convenience, we take
Q=I_{d_{x}}
and
R=I_{d_{u}}
without loss of generality by change of variables
x_{t}\mapsto Q_{t}^{-1/2}x_{t}
and
u_{t}\mapsto R_{t}^{-1/2}u_{t}
so that
c_{t}=\|{x_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}+\|{u_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}
.

Input-Output Formalism

Throughout this paper, we employ an operator-theoretic framework to represent the state-space dynamics in
Eq.

1
as input-output maps. We use
\mathbf{x}\coloneqq\{x_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
\mathbf{u}\coloneqq\{u_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\mathbf{w}\coloneqq\{w_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
to represent the state, control input, and disturbance sequences, respectively. The dynamical relations among these variables dictated by the state-space structure in (
1
) can be captured equivalently and succinctly using input/output transfer operators as:

\mathbf{x}=\mathcal{F}\mathbf{u}+\mathcal{G}\mathbf{w},

(2)

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
symbolize strictly causal
d_{x}\times d_{u}
and
d_{x}\times d_{w}
-block Laurent operators mapping control inputs
\mathbf{u}
and the disturbances
\mathbf{w}
to the states
\mathbf{x}
. The corresponding transfer matrices are given by

F(z)\!=\!C(zI\!-\!A)^{\mathrm{\!-\!1}}B_{u},\quad G(z)\!=\!C(zI\!-\!A)^{\mathrm{\!-\!1}}B_{w}.

(3)

Controller

We consider the full-information setting where the control input
u_{t}
at any time
t\in\mathbb{Z}
has causal access to past disturbances,
\{w_{\tau}\}_{\tau\leq t}
. In particular, we restrict our attention to causal LTI controllers such that
u_{t}=\sum\nolimits_{s\leq t}\widehat{K}_{t-s}w_{s}
where
\{\widehat{K}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the Markov parameters of the controller. We succinctly capture this relationship via a linear mapping

\mathcal{K}:\mathbf{w}\mapsto\mathbf{u}\coloneqq\mathcal{K}\mathbf{w}

where
\mathcal{K}
is a causal
d_{u}\times d_{w}
-block Laurent operator. Furthermore, we seek controllers that map
\ell_{2}
disturbances to
\ell_{2}
control inputs, which makes them bounded. Therefore,
\mathcal{K}
must be a member of
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{d_{u}\times d_{w}}
.

When the underlying system in (
2
) is in a feedback loop with a fixed controller
\mathcal{K}
, the states and the control inputs are determined completely by the disturbances through the closed-loop transfer operator given by:

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathbf{w}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}\\
\mathbf{u}\end{bmatrix}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{G}\\
\mathcal{K}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{w}.

(4)

We use
K(z)
and
T_{K}(z)
to represent the z-domain transfer matrices corresponding to the operators
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
, respectively.

In the remainder of this paper, our focus will primarily be on the quadratic expression
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}\succcurlyeq 0
, which can be expressed in terms of
\mathcal{K}
after a completion-of-squares as

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}=(\mathcal{K}\!-\!\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}})^{\ast}(\mathcal{I}\!+\!\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{F})(\mathcal{K}\!-\!\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}})+\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}.

(5)

Here,
\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\coloneqq-(\mathcal{I}\!+\!\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{F})^{\mathrm{\!-\!1}}\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{G}
denotes the unique non-causal controller such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
for all
\mathcal{K}
,
[
14
]
.

II-B

Mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
Control

This paper explores the problem of mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control, aiming to design a causal controller minimizing the
\mathit{H}_{2}
norm of the closed-loop transfer operator,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
, while ensuring that its
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm is bounded above by a fixed constant
\gamma>0
. This can be stated formally as a constrained optimization problem as follows:

Problem II.1

(Mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
Control)
.

Given an achievable
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm bound
\gamma>0
, find a causal and bounded controller
\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{d_{u}\times d_{w}}
that achieves the minimum closed-loop
\mathit{H}_{2}
norm among all
\gamma
-suboptimal
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers, i.e.,

\inf_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{d_{u}\times d_{w}}}\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\,\mathrm{s.t.}\,\quad\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma.

(6)

We will drop the superscript over
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{H}
spaces whenever the block dimensions are clear. Here, the
\mathit{H}_{2}
norm of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
is defined as

\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{2}\coloneqq\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}})}=\sqrt{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\operatorname{Tr}(T_{K}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})^{\ast}T_{K}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega}))}

(7)

The
\mathit{H}_{2}
criterion can be derived from the infinite-horizon average expected cost when the disturbances constitute a white random process with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[w_{s}w_{t}^{\intercal}]=I_{d_{w}}\delta_{s-t}
, namely,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T\!+\!1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\sum\nolimits_{t=-T}^{T}\|{x_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}+\|{u_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}}\right]=\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(8)

We denote by
\mathcal{K}_{2}\coloneqq\argmin\{\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{2}\mid\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{d_{u}\times d_{w}}\}
the optimal
\mathit{H}_{2}
controller.

Similarly, the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
corresponds to its operator norm as a mapping from
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
to
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
i.e.
,

\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\ell_{2}/\{0\}}\frac{\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}\mathbf{w}}\|_{\text{}}}{\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\text{}}}=\max_{\omega\in[0,2\pi)}\overline{\sigma}(T_{K}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega}))

(9)

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
consists of
2\times 1
block of causal Laurent operators, the corresponding transfer matrix
T_{K}(z)
is analytic outside the unit circle whenever it is bounded on the unit circle. The
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
criterion can be derived from the worst-case infinite-horizon cost incurred by
\mathcal{K}
among all bounded energy (or bounded power) disturbances, namely,

\sup_{\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\text{}}\leq 1}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\|{x_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}\!+\!\|{u_{t}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}=\sup_{\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\text{}}\leq 1}\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}\!+\!\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\text{}}^{2}=\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

We denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\coloneqq\argmin\{\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}\mid\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}^{d_{u}\times d_{w}}\}
the optimal
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller.

If it exists, the optimal mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller achieves the minimum expected cost for stochastic disturbances while guaranteeing a certain degree of robustness against adversarial bounded energy (or bounded power) disturbances.

Let
\gamma_{2}\coloneqq\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\|_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{\infty}\coloneqq\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}}\|_{\infty}
be the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norms of closed-loop transfer operators under the optimal
\mathit{H}_{2}
and
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers, respectively. Note that for
\gamma\geq\gamma_{2}
, the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm constraint in Problem
II.1
is redundant, and the optimal solution coincides with
\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Moreover, for
\gamma<\gamma_{\infty}
, Problem
II.1
is not feasible since no causal controller can achieve
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm less than that of the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controller,
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. For
\gamma=\gamma_{\infty}
, the solution coincides with
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. Thus, we are interested in non-trivial solutions for
\gamma\in(\gamma_{\infty},\gamma_{2})
, which interpolate between the optimal
\mathit{H}_{2}
and
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
controllers.

III
Main Results

In this section, we present the main theoretical results of our paper. In Theorem
III.1
, we formulate the Lagrange dual of Problem
II.1
and establish strong duality. In Theorem
III.4
, we state the optimal controller and argue that it is irrational.

First, we form the Lagrange dual of Problem
II.1
in the following theorem.

Theorem III.1

(Strong Duality)
.

Let
\gamma\in(\gamma_{\infty},\gamma_{2})
be an admissible
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm bound. The infinite-horizon mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control problem (
Problem

II.1
) is equivalent to the following max-min problem:

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Uplambda\in\mathscr{L}_{1},\\
\Uplambda\succcurlyeq 0\end{subarray}}\,\min_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}}\,\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda))-\gamma^{2}\operatorname{tr}(\Uplambda),

(10)

where the dual variable
\Uplambda\in\mathscr{L}_{1}^{d_{w}\times d_{w}}
is a positive definite, self-adjoint,
d_{w}\times d_{w}
-block Laurent operator.

Proof:

The proof of this theorem relies on the Lagrange duality theory for infinite-dimensional optimization on Banach spaces.
Consider Problem
II.1
. The
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm constraint
\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma
is equivalent to the
L_{\infty}
norm constraint
\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{2}
, which in turn is equivalent to the following operator inequality constraint:

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}-\gamma^{2}\mathcal{I}\preccurlyeq 0.

(11)

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}\in\mathscr{L}_{\infty}^{d_{w}\times d_{w}}
is self-adjoint, this operator inequality constraint is, by definition, equivalent to

\operatorname{tr}(\Uplambda(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}-\gamma^{2}\mathcal{I}))\leq 0,\quad\forall\Uplambda\succcurlyeq 0,

(12)

where
\Uplambda\in\mathscr{L}_{1}^{d_{w}\times d_{w}}
is a positive-definite, self-adjoint, block Laurent operator with bounded absolute trace,
i.e.
,
\operatorname{tr}(|{\Uplambda}|)<+\infty
.
This constraint can be reincorporated into the primal problem in (
6
) via Lagrangian, which yields an equivalent min-max problem:

\inf_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{K}}\sup_{\Uplambda\succcurlyeq 0}\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}})+\operatorname{tr}(\Uplambda(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}-\gamma^{2}\mathcal{I})).

(13)

Notice that the Lagrangian objective function is strictly convex (indeed quadratic) in
\mathcal{K}
, and affine in the dual variable
\Uplambda\succcurlyeq 0
. Furthermore,
Slater’s conditions
are satisfied since
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}
and
\|{\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}}\|_{\infty}\!\eqqcolon\!\gamma_{\infty}\!<\!\gamma
. Thus, by
[
15
, Thm. 2.9.2]
, strong duality holds, and the supremum is attained. Moreover, the inner infimum is attained for a fixed
\Lambda\succcurlyeq 0
due to strict convexity wrt
\mathcal{K}
. Rearranging terms, we get (
10
).
∎As opposed to the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm constrained primal problem in (
6
), the max-min problem in (
10
) is more manageable as it is not norm constrained and strictly convex in
\mathcal{K}
. This comes at the expense of an additional maximization step.

Notice that the inner minimization over causal
\mathcal{K}
in (
10
) is nothing but
(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda)
-weighted squared
\mathit{H}_{2}
-norm objective. Therefore, the inner minimization can be considered a stochastic optimal control problem under weakly stationary disturbances with autocovariance operator
\mathcal{I}+\Lambda
. This can be carried out tractably using the Wiener-Hopf method
[
16
]
and canonical spectral factorization of
\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda
as stated in
Lemma

III.2
.

Lemma III.2

(Wiener-Hopf Method)
.

Let
\Uplambda\in\mathscr{L}_{1}
be a positive-definite and self-adjoint block Laurent operator. Consider the problem of finding an optimal causal controller,
\mathcal{K}
, minimizing
(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda)
-weighted
\mathit{H}_{2}
norm, i.e.,

\min_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}}\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda))

(14)

The unique solution to this problem is given by,

\mathcal{K}_{\Uplambda}=\mathcal{K}_{2}+\Delta^{-1}\left\{{\{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\}_{\!-}\mathcal{L}}\right\}_{\!+}\mathcal{L}^{-1},

(15)

where
\Delta
and
\mathcal{L}
are the unique causal and causally invertible canonical spectral factors such that
\Delta^{\ast}\Delta=\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}^{\ast}=\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda
.

Proof:

Inserting the equation (
5
) in place of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}
and replacing
\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda
by their corresponding canonical spectral factors, we rewrite (
15
) as

\min_{\mathcal{K}\in\mathscr{H}_{\infty}}\|{\Delta\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}-\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\mathcal{L}}\|_{2}^{2}+\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda)).

(16)

Notice that the term
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}^{\ast}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}}(\mathcal{I}+\Uplambda))
does not depend on
\mathcal{K}
and, therefore, can be omitted. Inside the
\mathit{H}_{2}
norm,
\Delta\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
is causal for any
\mathcal{K}
whereas
\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\mathcal{L}
is mixed (non-causal). Therefore,
\Delta\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
can only minimize the objective by matching itself to the causal part of
\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\mathcal{L}
,
[
16
,
14
]
. The optimal solution satisfies
\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\Uplambda}\mathcal{L}=\{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\mathcal{L}\}_{\!+}
which yields
\mathcal{K}_{\Uplambda}=\Delta^{-1}\left\{{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\mathcal{L}}\right\}_{\!+}\mathcal{L}^{-1}
. By splitting
\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}=\{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\}_{\!+}+\{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\}_{\!-}
and noting that
\Delta^{-1}\{\Delta\mathcal{K}_{\textrm{nc}}\}_{\!+}
is the
\mathit{H}_{2}
-controller
[
14
]
, we get the desired result.
∎

Remark III.3
.

Notice that

Theorem III.4

(Saddle Point)

Let

VI
Rational Approximation

This section describes a practical approach to devising state-space controllers that serve as approximations to our irrational
\gamma
-optimal controller (
33
). Rather than attempting to approximate the controller directly, we
approximate the power spectrum
N(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
, aiming to reduce the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm of the approximation error through the use of positive rational functions. While the problem of approximating with rational functions typically does not lead to convex formulations, we demonstrate in Theorem
VI.3
that the process of approximating positive power spectra through the use of ratios of positive fixed-order polynomials can indeed be framed as a convex feasibility problem.
Formally, the problem is stated as follows.

Problem VI.1

(Rational Approximation via
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
-norm)
.

For a given positive spectrum
\mathcal{N}
, identify the optimal rational approximation utilizing the
\mathit{H}_{\infty}
norm, with an order not exceeding
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Specifically,

\inf_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\in\mathscr{T}_{+}^{m}}\left\|{{\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}-\mathcal{N}}\right\|_{\infty}\text{ subject to }\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{Q})=1,

(55)

where
\mathscr{T}_{m,+}
is the set of positive symmetric polynomials of order less than or equal to
m
and the constraint
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{Q})=1
is to avoid redundancy in solutions.

Definition VI.2
.

Given an
\epsilon>0
approximation bound, the
\epsilon
-sublevel set of Problem
VI.1
is defined as

\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}\!\coloneqq\!\left\{{(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\,\left|\;\left\|{{\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\!-\!\mathcal{N}}\right\|_{\infty}\!\!\leq\!\epsilon,\;\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{Q})\!=\!1\right.}\right\}

Theorem VI.3

(Feasibility of
\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}
,
[
20
, Thm. 5.5]
)
.

Given an accuracy level
\epsilon\!>\!0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
is a fixed order, the polynomials
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
of order
m
belong to the
\epsilon
-sub-level set, i.e.
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\in\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}
if and only if there exists
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{m+1}
such that
\operatorname{tr}\left({\mathbf{Q}}\right)=1
and for all
\omega\in[0,2\pi)
, .

\displaystyle\vskip-8.53581pt1)\,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{P}\bm{\Theta}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})}\right)\!-\!\left({N(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})\!+\!\epsilon}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{Q}\bm{\Theta}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})}\right)\!\leq\!0,

(56)

\displaystyle 2)\,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{P}\bm{\Theta}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})}\right)\!-\!\left({N(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})\!-\!\epsilon}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left({\mathbf{Q}\bm{\Theta}(\mathrm{e}^{\jmath\omega})}\right)\!\geq\!0,\vskip-17.07164pt

(57)

Although the above equations hold for all frequencies, practical implementation necessitates focusing on a finite selection of frequency samples. To sidestep this limitation, the analysis can be limited to a select set of frequencies, defined as
\Omega_{N}=\{\omega=2\pi k/N|k=1,\ldots,N\}
, where
N
, chosen to be much larger than
m
, provides a dense sampling of the frequency domain. While this approach inherently approximates the full spectrum of frequencies, increasing the number of sampled frequencies
N
allows for an arbitrary improvement in the approximation’s accuracy. Consequently, this method facilitates the transformation of the rational function approximation problem into a convex feasibility problem, addressable through the application of Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs) alongside a finite collection of affine (in)equality constraints.
Upon achieving a rational approximation of
N(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
, we then derive a state-space controller as outlined in (
17
). Note that a canonical factor of the rational approximation of
N(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})
can always be found due to the following lemma.

Lemma VI.4

(Canonical Factorization
[
21
, Lem. 1]
)
.

Given a Laurent polynomial of order
m
,
P(z)=\sum_{k=-m}^{m}p_{k}z^{-k}
, where
p_{k}=p_{-k}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
P(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})>0
, it can be shown that a canonical factor
L(z)=\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}z^{-1}+\ldots+\ell_{m}z^{-m}
exists. This factor satisfies
P(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})=|L(\mathrm{e}^{j\omega})|^{2}
, with all roots of
L(z)
lying inside the unit circle.

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the problem of mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control in the infinite-horizon setting. We provide the exact closed-form solution to the infinite-horizon mixed
\mathit{H}_{2}/\mathit{H}_{\infty}
control in the frequency domain. Despite being non-rational, we show that the optimal controller admits a finite-dimensional parameterization. Leveraging this fact, we introduce an efficient iterative algorithm that finds the optimal causal controller in the frequency domain. We show that this algorithm is convergent when the system is scalar and present numerical evidence for exponential convergence of the proposed algorithm. To obtain a finite order controller, we use a rational approximation method (based on the
H_{\infty}
norm) and present its performance. In future works, we will extend our results to cases when
d_{w}>1
. As mentioned in the paper, numerical results hint that the algorithm is exponentially convergent. Future works will involve analyzing the convergence properties of the proposed iterative algorithm.

Some improved bounds in sampling discretization of integral norms
F. Dai, E. Kosov, V. Temlyakov

Abstract
The paper addresses a problem of sampling discretization of integral norms of elements of finite-dimensional subspaces satisfying some conditions. We prove sampling discretization results under a standard assumption formulated in terms of the Nikol’skii-type inequality. In particular, we obtain some upper bounds on the number of sample points sufficient for good discretization of the integral L p L_{p} norms, 1 ≤ p < 2 1\leq p<2 , of functions from finite-dimensional subspaces of continuous functions. Our new results improve upon the known results in this direction. We use a new technique based on deep results of Talagrand from functional analysis.



Keywords and phrases
: Sampling discretization, Nikol’skii inequality, random vectors, frame.

Primary 65J05; Secondary 42A05, 65D30, 41A63.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the probability measure
\mu
. By
L_{p}
norm,
1\leq p<\infty
, we understand

\|f\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

By
L_{\infty}
norm we understand the uniform norm of continuous functions

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}|f(\mathbf{x})|

and with some abuse of notation we occasionally write
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
for the space
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
of continuous functions on
\Omega
.

By discretization of the
L_{p}
norm we understand a replacement of the measure
\mu
by
a discrete measure
\mu_{m}
with support on a set
\xi=\{\xi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset\Omega
in such a way that the error
|\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}-\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu_{m})}|
is small for functions from a given class. In this paper we focus on discretization of the
L_{p}
norms of elements of finite-dimensional subspaces. Namely, we work on the following problem.

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem.
Let
\Omega
be a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with the probability measure
\mu
. We say that a linear subspace
X_{n}
(the index
n
here, usually, stands for the dimension of
X_{n}
) of
L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, admits the Marcinkiewicz-type discretization theorem with parameters
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
and positive constants
C_{1}\leq C_{2}
if there exists a set

\Big\{\xi^{j}\in\Omega:j=1,\dots,m\Big\}

such that for any
f\in X_{n}
we have

C_{1}\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{p}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

(1.1)

The Marcinkiewicz discretization problem with
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
We write
X_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(m,p,\epsilon)
if (
1.1
) holds with
C_{1}=1-\epsilon
and
C_{2}=1+\epsilon
.

There are known results on the Marcinkiewicz discretization problem proved for subspaces
X_{n}

satisfying some conditions. There are two types of conditions used in the literature: (I) Conditions on the entropy numbers and (II) Conditions in terms of the Nikol’skii-type inequalities. The reader can find a detailed discussion of known results in the very recent survey
[
14
]
. In this paper we only prove some discretization results under conditions (II).
We now describe these conditions in detail.

Nikol’skii inequalities.
Let
q\in[1,\infty)
and
X_{n}\subset L_{\infty}(\Omega)
. The inequality

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{q},\ \ \forall f\in X_{n}

(1.2)

is called the Nikol’skii inequality for the pair
(q,\infty)
with the constant
C
. In this paper
it is convenient for us to write the constant
C
in the form
C=(Kn)^{1/q}
. We obtain here discretization results under the Nikol’skii inequality for the pair
(2,\infty)
. In Section
3
we
prove the following Theorem
1.1
, which is one of the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a positive absolute constant
C
such that for any subspace
X_{n}
of
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
of dimension at most
n

satisfying the Nikol’skii inequality

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{Kn}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{n}

(1.3)

for some probability measure
\mu
,
and for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
there is a finite set of points
\{\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{m}\}\subset\Omega
with

m\leq C\epsilon^{-2}Kn\log n,

which provides the following discretization inequalities for any
f\in X_{n}

{(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)},}

(1.4)

{(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}.}

(1.5)

Theorem
1.1
guarantees good discretization with equal weights under the Nikol’skii inequality for the pair
(2,\infty)
with the bound on the number of points
m\leq C_{1}\epsilon^{-2}Kn\log n
.
This covers the case of simultaneous discretization of the
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
norms. In the case of
simultaneous discretization of the
L_{p}
,
1<p<2
, and
L_{2}
norms the following Theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<2
. There exists a positive constant
C(p)

such that for any subspace
X_{n}
of
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
of dimension at most

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{Kn}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{n}

{(1-\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(\xi^{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)}^{2}.}

Comment 1.4.
We give a brief description of the scheme of our proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Let
X_{n}
be a subspace of
L_{\infty}
of dimension at most
n

and

of Talagrand and Rudelson on the expectations of random vectors to reduce the number of points for good discretization
by half (approximately). Third, we iterate the second step with an appropriate stopping time and finish the proof.

Iteration lemmas

Iteration techniques play an important role in our proofs. In this section we present the corresponding results. The following Lemma
2.1
from
[
26
]
was used in
proving sampling discretization results of the
L_{2}
norm.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
26
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
0<\delta<1/100
, and let
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j},j=0,1,\dots
, be defined inductively

\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}=1,\quad\alpha_{j+1}:=\alpha_{j}\frac{1-5\sqrt{\delta/\alpha_{j}}}{2},\quad\beta_{j+1}:=\beta_{j}\frac{1+5\sqrt{\delta/\alpha_{j}}}{2}.

Then there exist a positive absolute constant
C
and a number
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\alpha_{j}\geq 100\delta\ \ \text{for}\ \ j\leq L,\quad 25\delta\leq\alpha_{L+1}<100\delta,\quad\beta_{L+1}<C\alpha_{L+1}.

We prove a version of Lemma
2.1
, which is convenient for us.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/4)
and
\theta\in(0,1/2]
be such that
\delta<\theta^{2}
. Consider the sequence

\alpha_{0}=1,\qquad\alpha_{j+1}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}(1-(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}),

for
j=0,\dots,s
, where
s:=s(\delta,\theta)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is determined by the condition

\alpha_{s}\geq\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}},\qquad\alpha_{s+1}<\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}}.

(2.1)

Then we have the following inequalities

2^{-s-1}\geq\alpha_{s+1}\geq\frac{\delta}{4\theta^{2}},

(2.2)

\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1+\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\leq\exp(c_{1}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta)

(2.3)

and

\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1-\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\geq\exp(-c_{2}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta)

(2.4)

for every
0<\varkappa<{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{-1}
, every
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,s\}

and some positive absolute constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

Proof.

Consider the function
g(x):=x-(\delta x)^{1/2}
,
x\in(0,1)
. Then
g^{\prime}(x)=1-(\delta/x)^{1/2}/2>0
for
x>\delta/4
. Therefore, using our assumption

\alpha_{s}\geq\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}}\geq 4\delta>\delta/4
we obtain

\alpha_{s+1}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{s}(1-(\delta/\alpha_{s})^{1/2})=\frac{1}{2}g(\alpha_{s})\geq\frac{1}{2}g(\tfrac{\delta}{\theta^{2}})

=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}}-\frac{\delta}{\theta}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}}(1-\theta)\geq\frac{\delta}{4\theta^{2}},

which proves (
2.2
).

Using the trivial inequality
\alpha_{j+1}\leq\alpha_{j}/2
, we obtain from (
2.1
) that

\alpha_{j}\geq\frac{\delta}{\theta^{2}}2^{s-j},\qquad j=0,\dots,s.

(2.5)

This implies for
j=0,\dots,s

1+\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}\leq 1+\varkappa\theta 2^{-(s-j)/2}\leq\exp(\varkappa\theta 2^{-(s-j)/2}).

(2.6)

Next,

\sum_{j=0}^{t}2^{-(s-j)/2}=\sum_{k=s-t}^{s}2^{-k/2}\leq\sum_{k=s-t}^{\infty}2^{-k/2}

=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}-1}\cdot 2^{-(s-t)/2}=c_{1}\cdot 2^{-(s-t)/2},\quad c_{1}:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}-1},

(2.7)

and

\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1+\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\leq\exp(c_{1}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta),

which proves (
2.3
).

Since
1-x=(1+\frac{x}{1-x})^{-1}\geq\exp(-\frac{x}{1-x})\geq\exp(-2x)

for every
x\in(0,1/2]

then
using (
2.5
), we obtain for
0\leq j\leq s

1-\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}\geq 1-\varkappa\theta 2^{-(s-j)/2}\geq\exp(-\varkappa\theta 2^{-(s-2-j)/2}).

Therefore,

\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1-\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\geq\exp(-c_{2}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta),

which proves (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=0}^{s+1}
be from Lemma
2.2
, and let
0<\varkappa<{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{-1}
.
Consider the sequences

a_{0}=b_{0}=1,\quad a_{j+1}=\frac{1}{2}a_{j}(1-\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}),\quad b_{j+1}=\frac{1}{2}b_{j}(1+\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}),

j=0,\dots,s
.
Then

b_{t+1}\leq\exp(c_{3}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta)a_{t+1}

(2.8)

for every
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,s\}

for some positive absolute constant
c_{3}
.
In particular, for
\varkappa=1
and for the sequence

\beta_{0}=1,\quad\beta_{j+1}=\frac{1}{2}\beta_{j}(1+(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2}),\quad j=0,\dots,s

one has

\beta_{t+1}\leq\exp(c_{3}2^{-(s-t)/2}\theta)\alpha_{t+1}

(2.9)

for every
t\in\{0,1,\ldots,s\}

for some positive absolute constant
c_{3}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
b_{j}
we obtain from (
2.3
)

b_{t+1}=2^{-t}\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1+\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\leq 2^{-t}\exp(c_{1}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta).

Further, using the definition of the
a_{j}
and (
2.4
) we get

a_{t+1}=2^{-t}\prod_{j=0}^{t}((1-\varkappa(\delta/\alpha_{j})^{1/2})\geq 2^{-t}\exp(-c_{2}2^{-(s-t)/2}\varkappa\theta).

(2.10)

Combining the above two inequalities, we complete the proof of (
2.8
).
∎

Finally, we prove one simple inequality for a recurrent sequence.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let the sequence
\{m_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of positive numbers satisfy the following conditions:

m_{0}=M,\qquad(m_{j}-(m_{j})^{1/2})/2\leq m_{j+1}\leq m_{j}/2,\ \ j=0,1,\cdots

where
M>0
is a constant.
Then for every integer
k\geq 0
we have

M-2^{k/2}M^{1/2}(\sqrt{2}-1)^{-1}\leq 2^{k}m_{k}\leq M.

Proof.

The right inequality is obvious. For the left inequality we have

2m_{k}\geq m_{k-1}-(m_{k-1})^{1/2}\geq m_{k-1}-(2^{-k+1}M)^{1/2},

which implies

2^{k}m_{k}\geq 2^{k-1}m_{k-1}-2^{(k-1)/2}\sqrt{M}\ \ \text{ for $k=1,2,\cdots$,}

and hence

2^{k}m_{k}\geq M-M^{1/2}2^{k/2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{-j/2}\geq M-2^{k/2}M^{1/2}(\sqrt{2}-1)^{-1}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/4)

be a fixed number and let
\epsilon_{0}=\varkappa_{1}\epsilon

and
\theta=\varkappa_{2}\epsilon

Step 1. Preliminary discretization.
We need the following lemma, which can be deduced by combining certain estimates from

[
5
,
6
,
7

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and
0<\epsilon_{0}<1/4
. Let
X_{n}
be a subspace of
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
of dimension at most
n
satisfying

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{Kn}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)},\ \ \forall f\in X_{n}

for some constant

m\leq C_{p}\epsilon_{0}^{-8}Kn(\log(Kn))^{3}

f\in X_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(x_{j})|^{p}\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p},

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|f(x_{j})|^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(3.3)

For the reader’s convenience, we present a detailed
proof of Lemma
3.1
in Section

.

By Lemma
3.1
, we can find
a finite set

\Lambda_{M}:=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{M}\}\subset\Omega

) and (

) hold with

.
Then (

\sup_{f\in X_{n}}\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{2,\Lambda_{M}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{Kn}}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon_{0}}}\leq\sqrt{2Kn},

Step 2.
We identify each vector in

For

f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
, we define

\|f\|_{p,I}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{j\in I}|f(j)|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ 1\leq p<\infty,\ \ \text{and}\ \ \|f\|_{\infty,I}:=\max_{j\in I}|f(j)|,

where
|I|
denotes the cardinality of the set
I
.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the usual norm of
\ell_{p}^{M}
; that is,

\|f\|_{p}:=M^{1/p}\|f\|_{p,\Omega_{M}}.
Let
B_{p}^{M}:=\{f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}:\ \|f\|_{p}\leq 1\}
.
For each
I\subset\Omega_{M}
, we denote by
R_{I}
the orthogonal projection onto the space spanned by
e_{i},i\in I
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
,
\cdots
,
e_{M}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
is a canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
. Thus, for each
f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
,
(R_{I}f)(j)=f(j)
for
j\in I
, and
(R_{I}f)(j)=0
for
j\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I
.
Throughout this note,
\{\varepsilon_{i}:\ \ i=1,2,\cdots\}
denotes
a sequence of independent Bernoulli random variables taking
values
\pm 1
with probability
1/2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X_{n}
be a subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
of dimension at most
n
satisfying

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{Kn}\|f\|_{2,\Omega_{M}}=\sqrt{\frac{Kn}{M}}\|f\|_{2},\ \ \ \ \forall f\in X_{n}.

(3.4)

Then for
p=1
and
p=2
we have

{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M}}\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\varepsilon_{i}}|f(i)|^{p}\Bigr|\Bigr)\leq C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{Kn\log n}{M}},

(3.5)

where
C_{4}
is a positive absolute constant.

Several remarks on Theorem
3.1
are in order:

(i)

Theorem
3.1
for
p=2
was proved by Rudelson
[
28
, Lemma 1]
.

(ii)

For
p=1
, Theorem
3.1
was essentially proved by Talagrand
[
32
]
, while not explicitly stated there
(see also
[
18
, Proposition 15.16]
and
[
10
, Theorem 13]
).

For completeness, we will include a proof of Theorem
3.1
for
p=1
in Appendix I.

The following lemma, which is a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X_{n}
be a subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}

of dimension at most
n
satisfying (
3.4
)
for some constant
K\geq 1
. Let
J\subset\Omega_{M}:=\{1,2,\cdots,M\}
. Assume that there exist positive constants
\alpha_{J}
,
\beta_{J}
such that for any
f\in X_{n}
we have for both
p=1
and
p=2

\alpha_{J}\|f\|^{p}_{p}\leq\|R_{J}f\|^{p}_{p}\leq\beta_{J}\|f\|^{p}_{p}.

(3.6)

Then there exists a subset
I\subset J
with

\frac{|J|}{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\Bigr)\leq|I|\leq\frac{|J|}{2}

(3.7)

such that for any
f\in X_{n}
we have for both
p=1
and
p=2

\alpha_{I}\|f\|^{p}_{p}\leq\|R_{I}f\|^{p}_{p}\leq\beta_{I}\|f\|^{p}_{p}

(3.8)

where

\alpha_{I}:=\frac{(1-\sigma_{1})\alpha_{J}}{2},\ \ \beta_{I}:=\frac{(1+\sigma_{1})\beta_{J}}{2},\ \ \sigma_{1}:=C_{5}\sqrt{\frac{Kn\log n}{\alpha_{J}M}},

(3.9)

and
C_{5}
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
J=\{1,2,\cdots,M_{1}\}
. By (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), we have

\sup_{f\in X_{n}}\frac{\|R_{J}f\|_{\infty}}{\|R_{J}f\|_{2}}\leq\sup_{f\in X_{n}}\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\|R_{J}f\|_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{K_{J}n}{|J|}},

(3.10)

where

K_{J}:=\frac{K|J|}{\alpha_{J}M}
. Set
X_{n}(I):=\{R_{I}(f)\,:\,f\in X_{n}\}
.
By Theorem
3.1
applied to
\Omega_{M_{1}}=J
and
K=K_{J}
, we obtain for
p=1
and
p=2

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\sup_{f\in X_{n}(J)\cap B_{p}^{M_{1}}}\Bigl|\sum_{j=1}^{M_{1}}{\varepsilon_{j}}|f(j)|^{p}\Bigr|\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{K_{J}n\log n}{|J|}}

\displaystyle=C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{Kn\log n}{\alpha_{J}M}}=:\frac{1}{8}\sigma_{1}.

(3.11)

Next, consider the random set
I:=\{i\in J:\varepsilon_{i}=1\}
. Clearly,

|I|=\sum_{i\in J}\frac{\varepsilon_{i}+1}{2}=\frac{|J|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in J}\varepsilon_{i}.

We use the following result from
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
16
]

If
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}^{2}=1
, then

{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(|\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\varepsilon_{j}|\leq 1\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(3.12)

Using (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{|J|}}{2}\leq|I|-\frac{|J|}{2}\leq 0\Bigr)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(-\sqrt{M_{1}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\varepsilon_{i}\leq 0\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(|\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\varepsilon_{i}|\leq\sqrt{M_{1}}\Bigr)\geq\frac{1}{4}.

This means that with probability
\geq\frac{1}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|J|}{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\Bigr)\leq|I|\leq\frac{|J|}{2}.

(3.13)

Now combining (
3.11
) with (
3.13
), we can find a finite sequence
\{\varepsilon_{j}:\ 1\leq j\leq M_{1}\}\subset\{\pm 1\}

such that (

) with

is satisfied, and such that for every

,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}{\varepsilon_{i}}|f(i)|^{2}\Bigr|\leq\sigma_{1}\|R_{J}f\|^{2}_{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\varepsilon_{i}|f(i)|\Bigr|\leq\sigma_{1}\|R_{J}f\|_{1}.

(3.14)

On the other hand, note that for any

we have for
1\leq q<\infty
,

\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}\varepsilon_{i}|x(i)|^{q}\Bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{M_{1}}(1+\varepsilon_{i})|x(i)|^{q}-\|R_{J}x\|_{q}^{q}\Bigr|=\Bigl|2\|R_{I}x\|_{q}^{q}-\|R_{J}x\|_{q}^{q}\Bigr|.

Thus, we obtain from (
3.14
) that for
p=1,2

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sigma_{1}}{2}\sum_{i\in J}|f(i)|^{p}\leq\|R_{I}f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1+\sigma_{1}}{2}\sum_{i\in J}|f(i)|^{p}.

(3.15)

Combining (
3.15
) with (
3.6
), we obtain the desired estimate (
3.8
).
∎

, and

.

. We begin with

(3.16)

By Lemma
2.3
we obtain

\alpha_{s+1}\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|R_{I_{s+1}}f\|_{p}^{p}\leq e^{c_{3}\theta}\alpha_{s+1}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

5
Discretization and frames

In this section we discuss finite-dimensional subspaces
X_{n}
of the space
{\mathcal{C}}(\Omega)
defined
on a compact set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
equipped with a probability measure
\mu
. For convenience we only consider the case of real functions.

Dirichlet kernel.
For an orthonormal system
\mathcal{U}_{n}:=\{u_{j}(\mathbf{x})\}_{j=1}^{n}
on
(\Omega,\mu)

we define the Dirichlet kernel as follows

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}(\mathbf{x})u_{j}(\mathbf{y}).

Here is a very simple claim that the Dirichlet kernel
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
does not depend on the orthonormal basis of a given subspace
X_{n}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any two orthonormal bases
\mathcal{U}_{n}
and
\mathcal{V}_{n}
of
a given subspace
X_{n}
we have

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})={\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{V}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Proof.

For
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
denote the column vectors
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}):=(u_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,u_{n}(\mathbf{x}))^{T}
,
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}):=(v_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,v_{n}(\mathbf{x}))^{T}
. Then there exists an orthogonal matrix
O
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
we have
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})=O\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})
and, therefore,

\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})^{T}=\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})^{T}O^{T}
. This implies that

{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{V}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})^{T}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})^{T}O^{T}O\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})^{T}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{y})={\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

∎

Proposition
5.1
shows that the Dirichlet kernel
{\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
with
\mathcal{U}_{n}
being an orthonormal basis of
X_{n}
can be seen as a characteristic of the subspace
X_{n}
.
Denote

{\mathcal{D}}(X_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):={\mathcal{D}}_{n}(\mathcal{U}_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

Consider the system
{\mathcal{D}}:=\{{\mathcal{D}}(X_{n},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\}_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}
as a dictionary (not normalized) of
functions on
\mathbf{y}
in the subspace
X_{n}
.

We recall the definition of the
p
-frame of the subspace
X_{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
(see
[
1
]
).

Definition 5.1
.

The system
\Psi:=\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is said to be a
p
-frame
of
X_{n}
with positive constants
A
and
B
if for any
f\in X_{n}
we have

A\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle f,\psi_{j}\rangle|^{p}\leq B\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}^{p}.

Using a well known property of the Dirichlet kernel: For any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup\limits_{f\in B_{1}}\Bigl|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{ik}\langle u_{k},f\rangle\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\Bigl[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{ik}\langle u_{k},f_{\varepsilon}\rangle\Bigr]

For the proof of (
8.24
), we need several lemmas. The first lemma allows us to replace
\{t_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
with a subset of points
t_{\ell}
,
\ell\in V
that are “well” distributed.

Lemma 8.2
.

We can find a subset
V
of
\{1,2,\cdots,N_{k+1}\}
with
|V|>N_{k-2}
and the following properties:

\displaystyle\|R_{I^{c}}(t_{\ell}-t_{\ell^{\prime}})\|_{2}\geq\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\ \ \text{whenever $\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V$ and $\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}$},

(8.25)

\displaystyle\|f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{p}\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\cdot\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ \ \forall\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V,

(8.26)

\displaystyle\|R_{I}(f_{\ell})-R_{I}(f_{\ell^{\prime}})\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\Bigl(\frac{n}{M(\log M)^{3}}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ \ \forall\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V.

(8.27)

Proof.

First, by Lemma
8.1
,

e_{{2^{k-2}}}(X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M};\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C_{p}\Bigl(\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{N}\}\subset X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M}
,
we can partition
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
into at most
N_{k-2}
sets
V_{\alpha,1}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
so that (
8.26
) with
V=V_{\alpha,1}
is satisfied for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

Second, since
\|R_{I}f\|_{p}\leq|I|^{1/p}\|f\|_{\infty}\leq A_{k}^{1/p}\|f\|_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle e_{{2^{k-2}}}\Bigl(R_{I}X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M},\|\cdot\|_{p}\Bigr)\leq A_{k}^{1/p}\cdot e_{2^{k}}\Bigl(R_{I}X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq A_{k}^{1/p}\cdot e_{2^{k}}\Bigl(X_{n}\cap B_{p}^{M},\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Bigr)\leq C_{p}\Bigl(A_{k}\cdot\frac{\log M}{M}\cdot\frac{n}{2^{k}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}=C_{p}\Bigl(\frac{n}{M(\log M)^{3}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus, we can partition
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
into at most
N_{k-2}
sets
V_{\alpha,2}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
so that (
8.27
) with
V=V_{\alpha,2}
is satisfied for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

Third, setting
E:=\{R_{I}t_{\ell}:\ \ \ell=1,2,\cdots,N\}
, we have

E\subset\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|I|}:\ \ \|u-R_{I}t_{0}\|_{2}\leq 4a\}.

Since
|I|\leq A_{k}
,
E
can be covered by at most
32^{|I|}\leq N_{k-2}
Euclidean balls of radius
\frac{a}{4}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{|I|}
.
Thus, we may partition the set
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
into at most
N_{k-2}
sets
V_{\alpha,3}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{3}
such that

\max_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V_{\alpha,3}}\|R_{I}(t_{\ell})-R_{I}(t_{\ell^{\prime}})\|_{2}\leq\frac{a}{4},\ \ \alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{3}.

Since
\|t_{\ell}-t_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{2}\geq a
for any two distinct
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, this in turn implies that (
8.25
) with
V={V_{\alpha,3}}
is satisfied for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{3}
.

Finally, let
\{V_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
denote the partition of
\{1,\cdots,N\}

generated by the above three partitions. Then
\{V_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
contains at most
N_{k-2}^{3}<N_{k}
sets for which (
8.25
), (
8.26
), and (
8.27
)
with
V=V_{\alpha}
are satisfied for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Since
N_{k-2}^{4}=N_{k}<N=N_{k+1}
, we can find a set
V
from this last partition
\{V_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
with
|V|>N_{k-2}
.∎

For the reminder of the proof, we will always use the letter
V
to denote a subset of
\{1,\cdots,N\}
with the properties stated in Lemma
8.2
. Therefore, we will work on the set of points
f_{\ell}
,
\ell\in V
instead.

Our second lemma can be used to obtain useful lower estimates of the quantity
S
.

Lemma 8.3
.

There exists a constant
c_{p}>0
depending only on
p
such that

\min_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V,\ \ \ell\neq\ell^{\prime}}\Bigl[\|f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}\Bigr]\cdot S\geq c_{p}\cdot a^{2}.

(8.28)

In particular, this implies

S\geq c_{p}a2^{k/2}\sqrt{\frac{M}{n\log M}}\frac{1}{\log M}\geq\frac{c_{p}}{\log^{2}M}.

(8.29)

Proof.

We will use the following inequality. If
1\leq p\leq 2
,
a,b>0
and
\eta>0
, then

|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{2}\leq 2^{p}p^{2}(a_{\eta}^{p}+b_{\eta}^{p})|b-a|^{p}+2\eta^{p}(a^{p}+b^{p}),

(8.30)

where
a_{\eta}=a\cdot\chi_{[\eta,\infty)}(a)
and
b_{\eta}=b\cdot\chi_{[\eta,\infty)}(b)
. To show (
8.30
), without loss of generality, we may assume that
a\geq b
.
If
a<\eta
, then

|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{2}\leq 2a^{p}(a^{p}+b^{p})\leq 2\eta^{p}(a^{p}+b^{p}).

If
a\geq\eta
, then
a=a_{\eta}
and

\displaystyle|a^{p}-b^{p}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(pa^{p-1}|b-a|)^{2}=p^{2}a^{2p-2}|b-a|^{p}|b-a|^{2-p}\leq p^{2}(2a)^{p}|b-a|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}p^{2}(|a_{\eta}|^{p}+|b_{\eta}|^{p})|b-a|^{p}.

In either case, we have (
8.30
).

Next, note that (
8.30
) implies that for each
J\subset\Omega_{M}
,
x,y\in B_{p}^{M}
and
\eta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\||R_{J}x|^{p}-|R_{J}y|^{p}\Bigr\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4{\eta^{p}}+C_{p}\|x-y\|_{\infty}^{p}A,

(8.31)

where

A:=\max\Bigl\{\sum_{i\in J}|x(i)|^{p}\chi_{\{|x(i)|\geq\eta\}}(i),\ \sum_{i\in J}|y(i)|^{p}\chi_{\{|y(i)|\geq\eta\}}(i)\Bigr\}.

Thus, setting
\eta=\tau^{1/p}
, and using (
8.25
), we have that for any
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V
with
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I}\Bigl||f_{\ell}(i)|^{p}-|f_{\ell^{\prime}}(i)|^{p}\Bigr|^{2}\leq 4\tau+C_{p}\|f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}S.

(8.32)

Since
a^{2}2^{k}\geq\frac{n}{M\log M}
, we obtain from (
8.9
) with
C=C_{1}
that

4\tau=2^{-k-k_{0}+3}(\log M)^{4}\leq\frac{2^{-k+4}n}{CM(\log M)^{2+\frac{4}{2-p}}}\leq\frac{1}{4}a^{2}.

The estimate (
8.28
) then follows from (
8.32
).

Finally, using (
8.26
), (
8.28
) and the assumption
a^{2}2^{k}\geq\frac{n}{M\log M}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S\geq c_{p}\frac{a^{2}2^{k}m}{n\log M}\geq c_{p}a2^{k/2}\sqrt{\frac{m}{n\log M}}\frac{1}{\log M}\geq\frac{c_{p}}{(\log M)^{2}},

proving (
8.29
).
∎

Our aim is to show (
8.24
), which is an improvement of the first inequality in (
8.29
).
According to (
8.28
), for the proof of (
8.24
), it will suffice to show that there exist two distinct
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V
such that

\|f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{p}\leq C_{p}S\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}.

(8.33)

The idea is to construct a set
U=U_{S}
with the following two properties:

(i)

\{f_{\ell}-f_{\ell_{0}}:\ \ \ell\in V\}\subset U
for some
\ell_{0}\in V
;

(ii)

there exists a partition
\{E_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of
U
such that
|\mathcal{A}|<|V|
and

\text{diam}(E_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C_{p}\Bigl(S\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ \forall\alpha\in\mathcal{A}.

Since
|V|>N_{k-2}
, to ensure (ii), it suffices to prove that

e_{2^{k-2}}(U,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C_{p}\Bigl(S\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

To show (
8.33
), we need two additional lemmas. For
\xi\geq 0
, we define

\displaystyle U(\xi)=\xi B^{M}_{p}+\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}B_{2}^{M}\ \ \text{and}\ \ X_{n}(\xi)=U(\xi)\cap X_{n}.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\xi=(3S)^{1/p}
. Then

\{f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}:\ \ \ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V\}\subset 2X_{n}(\xi).

(8.34)

Proof.

Fix
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V
and set
g:=g_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}=f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\in X_{n}
. Our aim is to show that
g\in 2U(\xi)
.
First, by (
8.27
) and (
8.29
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}|g(i)|^{p}=\sum_{i\in I}|f_{\ell}(i)-f_{\ell^{\prime}}(i)|^{p}\leq\frac{C_{p}n}{M(\log M)^{3}}\leq S,

implying
g\chi_{I}\in S^{1/p}\cdot B_{p}^{M}
.
Second, setting
\eta=\tau^{1/p}
, and using (
8.23
), we have

\displaystyle 2S

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I}|f_{\ell}(i)|^{p}\chi_{\{|f_{\ell}(i)|\geq\eta\}}(i)+\sum_{i\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I}|f_{\ell^{\prime}}(i)|^{p}\chi_{\{|f_{\ell^{\prime}}(i)|\geq\eta\}}(i)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-p}\sum_{i\in\Omega_{M}\setminus I}|g(i)|^{p}\chi_{\{|g(i)|\geq 2\eta\}}(i),

where the last step
uses the inequality,

|u-v|^{p}\chi_{|u-v|\geq 2\eta}\leq 2^{p}\Bigl(|u|^{p}\chi_{|u|\geq\eta}+|v|^{p}\chi_{|v|\geq\eta}\Bigr),\ \ u,v\in{\mathbb{R}}.

This implies that

g\chi_{{}_{\{i\in I^{c}:\ \ |g(i)|\geq 2\eta\}}}\in 2\cdot(2S)^{1/p}B_{p}^{M}.

Finally, we write
g=u+v
, where

\displaystyle v:

\displaystyle=g\chi_{I}+g\chi_{{}_{\{i\in I^{c}:\ \ |g(i)|\geq 2\eta\}}},\ \ \text{and}\ \ u:=g\chi_{\{i\in I^{c}:\ |g(i)|<2\eta\}}.

Clearly,
\frac{1}{2}v\in(3S)^{1/p}\cdot B_{p}^{M}
and

\frac{u}{2}\in(\eta B_{\infty}^{M})\cap B_{p}^{M}\subset\eta^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\cdot B_{2}^{M}.

Thus,
g\in 2U(\xi)
.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\xi>0
be such that

\tau\leq\xi^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr).

(8.35)

Then

e_{j}\Bigl(X_{n}(\xi),\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Bigr)\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{j}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,2^{k}.

(8.36)

Proof.

Note first that
U(\xi)=\xi B_{p}^{M}+\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}B_{2}^{M}
is a symmetric convex body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
.
Let
W
denote the polar of
U(\xi)
; that is,

W:=\Bigl\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}:\ \ \|x\|_{W}:=\max_{y\in U(\xi)}x\cdot y\leq 1\Bigr\}.

Then for each
x\in W
,

\displaystyle\|x\|_{W}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|u\|_{p}\leq\xi}\sup_{\|v\|_{2}\leq\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}}(x\cdot u+x\cdot v)\leq\xi\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}+\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\|x\|_{2}.

By Lemma
8.1
, this implies that for
1\leq j\leq 2^{k}
,

\displaystyle e_{j}(B_{2}^{M}\cap X_{n},\|\cdot\|_{W})\leq\xi\cdot e_{j}(B_{2}^{M}\cap X_{n},\|\cdot\|_{p^{\prime}})+2\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{j}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}+2\tau^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{j}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},

where the last step uses (
8.35
).
By duality (see
[
34
, Theorem 16.8.10]
), we deduce

e_{j}(X_{n}(\xi),\|\cdot\|_{2})\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{j}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,2^{k}.

(8.37)

Finally, using (
8.37
) and Lemma
8.1
,
we have that for
1\leq j\leq 2^{k-1}
,

e_{2j}(X_{n}(\xi),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq 2e_{j}(X_{n}(\xi),\|\cdot\|_{2})e_{j}(X_{n}\cap B_{2}^{M},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{j}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The stated estimate then follows by monotonicity.
∎

Now we are in a position to prove Proposition
8.1
.

Proof of Proposition
8.1
.

Let
\xi=(3S)^{1/p}
.
A straightforward calculation using (
8.29
) and (
8.9
) shows that the condition (
8.35
) is satisfied. Consequently, using Lemma
8.5
, we obtain

e_{2^{k-2}}\Bigl(X_{n}(\xi),\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\Bigr)\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

(8.38)

Thus, we may partition the set
2X_{n}(\xi)
into
N_{k-2}
sets
E_{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

\text{diam}(E_{\gamma},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p},\ \ \ \forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

(8.39)

On the other hand, Lemma
8.4
implies that for each fixed
\ell_{0}\in V
,

\{f_{\ell}-f_{\ell_{0}}:\ \ \ell\in V\}\subset 2X_{n}(\xi).

Since
|V|>N_{k-2}
and
|\Gamma|=N_{k-2}
, we can find two distinct
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V
such that
f_{\ell}-f_{\ell_{0}},f_{\ell^{\prime}}-f_{\ell_{0}}
lie in a same set of the partition
\{E_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, which, using (
8.39
), implies

\|f_{\ell}-f_{\ell^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq C\xi\cdot\Bigl(\frac{n}{2^{k}}\frac{\log M}{M}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

Thus, there exist two distinct
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in V
such that the estimate (
8.33
) holds, which, using (
8.28
), in turn implies the desired estimate (
8.24
).
∎

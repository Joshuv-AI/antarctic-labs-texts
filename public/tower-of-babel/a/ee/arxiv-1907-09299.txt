Thresholds for low regularity solutions to wave equations with structural damping
Tomonori Fukushima and Ryo Ikehata Department of Mathematics, Graduate School of Education, Hiroshima UniversityHigashi-Hiroshima 739-8524, Japan and, Hironori Michihisa Department of Mathematics, Graduate School of Science, Hiroshima UniversityHigashi-Hiroshima 739-8526, Japan

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behavior of solutions to wave equations with a structural damping term u t ​ t − Δ ​ u + Δ 2 ​ u t = 0 , u ⁡ ( 0 , x ) = u 0 ​ ( x ) , u t ​ ( 0 , x ) = u 1 ​ ( x ) , u_{tt}-\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u_{t}=0,\qquad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\,\,u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x), in the whole space. New thresholds are reported in this paper that indicate which of the diffusion wave property and the non-diffusive structure dominates in low regularity cases. We develop to that end the previous author’s research [ 2 ] where they have proposed a threshold that expresses whether the parabolic-like property or the wave-like property strongly appears in the solution to some regularity-loss type dissipative wave equation.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of the following wave equation with the structural damping term

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u_{t}=0,&t>0,\quad x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
n\geq 1
.
This equation was proposed in
[
3
]
and they proved that equation (
1.1
) admits a unique mild solution in the class

u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}(\textbf{R}^{n}))

if the initial data belong to the energy space

\displaystyle[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\textbf{R}^{n}).

(1.2)

Before we investigate equation (
1.1
), we recall previous studies on wave equations with damping terms

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\theta}u_{t}=0,&t>0,\quad x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
\theta\geq 0
.
In the case of
\theta=0
, the classical equation (
1.3
) is known as the damped wave equation.
In the asymptotic sense, the solution to (
1.3
) behaves like a heat kernel.
See, e.g., D’Abbicco-Reissig
[
1
]
, Han-Milani
[
4
]
, Hosono
[
5
]
, Hosono-Ogawa
[
6
]
, Ikehata
[
7
]
, Karch
[
15
]
, Marcati-Nishihara
[
16
]
, Matsumura
[
17
]
, Michihisa
[
18
]
, Narazaki
[
23
]
, Nishihara
[
24
]
, Sakata-Wakasugi
[
26
]
and Takeda
[
28
]
.
The strongly damped wave equation, i.e., equation (
1.3
) with
\theta=1
, was studied by Ponce
[
25
]
and Shibata
[
27
]
in the earlier time.
Results on the asymptotic behavior of the solution can be found in Ikehata
[
8
]
, Ikehata-Onodera
[
12
]
, Ikehata-Natsume
[
11
]
, Ikehata-Takeda
[
13
]
, Ikehata-Todorova-Yordanov
[
14
]
and Michihisa
[
19
,
21
]
.
Roughly speaking, the solution behaves like the convolution of the heat kernel and the solution to the corresponding wave equation.
This is so-called a diffusion wave property which is also seen in the case of
\theta\in[1/2,1]
but not in the lower order damping case
\theta\in[0,1/2)
.
From these observations, we can deduce that larger values of
\theta
give stronger wave properties to solutions of equation (
1.3
).
However, what is common to these cases
\theta\in[0,1]
is that the norm of Fourier transformed solutions in the high-frequency region is exponentially small.
That is, only analysis in the low-frequency region is necessary for this concern, and it can be said that the diffusion structure is dominant.

If
\theta
is even larger, i.e.,
\theta>1
, equation (
1.3
) is of regularity-loss type.
As a typical case, we are dealing with (
1.1
) proposed by Ghisi-Gobbino-Haraux
[
3
]
.
On this model, Ikehata-Iyota
[
9
]
derived asymptotic profiles of the solution to (
1.1
) with some weighted
L^{1}
initial data.
When we consider sufficiently smooth initial data, we can expect the diffusive structure is still dominant even in the case of
\theta>1
.
Difficulties arise in the low regularity case such as (
1.2
), and then analysis in the high-frequency region is also inevitable to understand the asymptotic behavior of the solution.
This is just because the high-frequency part of the solution can no longer be regarded as an error.
Related to the topic, Michihisa
[
20
]
gave higher order asymptotic expansions of the solution to some linear Rosenau-type equation.
There, we can find the function
e^{-t/|\xi|^{2}}
included in terms consisting of the profiles.
With his technique, quite recently, authors
[
2
]
have studied another Rosenau-type equation

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u_{tt}+\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u+u_{t}=0,&t>0,\quad x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\textbf{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{l+1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\times H^{l}(\textbf{R}^{n})
with
l\geq 2
.
This is also of regularity-loss type and they found the leading terms of the solution.
One is the heat kernel appearing in the low-frequency region whose decay order is determined by the spatial dimension
n
, and the other one is the oscillating functions derived from the high-frequency region whose decay order depends on the regularity of the initial data
l
.
Compared with their different decay orders, they discovered a meaningful threshold
l^{*}=n/2-1
that indicates whether hyperbolicity or parabolicity is stronger.
It means that the diffusive structure mainly appears when we impose additional regularity assumptions
l>l^{*}
and vice versa.

In this paper, we give another threshold for such superiority.
Even if the low regularity Cauchy data are given, we can expect to discuss the similar argument as in
[
2
]
after pulling out slowly decaying profiles from the solution.
In this process, we first face the necessity for carrying out higher order expansions of the solution in the high-frequency region as presented in
[
20
]
.

Notation

Here, we introduce some notation.

1.

The set of all positive integers is denoted by
N
and put
\textbf{N}_{0}:=\textbf{N}\cup\{0\}
.

2.

The integer part of
0\leq r\in\textbf{R}
is expressed by
[r]
.
That is,
[r]:=\max\{k\in\textbf{N}_{0}:k\leq r\}
.

3.

The surface area of the unit ball in
\textbf{R}^{n}
is expressed by
\omega_{n}
.

4.

The Fourier transform
\hat{f}(\xi)
of a function
f(x)
is

\hat{f}(\xi):=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx.

5.

Throughout this paper,
L^{p}(\textbf{R}^{n})
represents the usual Lebesgue space and we write its norm as
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

6.

In connection with the above, we also use the Sobolev space
H^{p}(\textbf{R}^{n})
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}}:=\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|^{2p})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad f\in H^{p}(\textbf{R}^{n}).

7.

We define the weighted
L^{1}
space as follows:

L^{1,\gamma}(\textbf{R}^{n}):=\left\{f\in L^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n}):\|f\|_{1,\gamma}:=\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{\gamma}|f(x)|\,dx<\infty\right\},\qquad\gamma\geq 0.

8.

Let
l\geq 0
and
\gamma\geq 0
.
For
H^{l+1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,\gamma}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in H^{l}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,\gamma+1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
, put

I^{l,\gamma}(u_{0},u_{1}):=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{l+1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{l}}+\|u_{0}\|_{1,\gamma}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma+1}.

3
Key functions

First, we confirm the solution formula (see
[
22
]
).
The characteristic equation corresponding to problem (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{4}\lambda+|\xi|^{2}=0

and we put its solutions as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}:=\frac{-|\xi|^{4}+\sqrt{|\xi|^{8}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2},\qquad\lambda_{2}:=\frac{-|\xi|^{4}-\sqrt{|\xi|^{8}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}.

Hence, the Fourier transformed solution is formally given by

\displaystyle\hat{u}(t,\xi)=E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+E_{1}(t,\xi)\left(\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\right),

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle E_{0}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cos\left(\frac{t\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{8}}}{2}\right)},&|\xi|\leq\sqrt[3]{2},\\[22.0pt]
\displaystyle{e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cosh\left(\frac{t\sqrt{|\xi|^{8}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}\right)},&|\xi|\geq\sqrt[3]{2},\end{cases}

\displaystyle E_{1}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\sin\left(\frac{t\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{8}}}{2}\right)\biggr/\frac{\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2}-|\xi|^{8}}}{2}},&|\xi|\leq\sqrt[3]{2},\\[22.0pt]
\displaystyle{e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\sinh\left(\frac{t\sqrt{|\xi|^{8}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}\right)\biggr/\frac{\sqrt{|\xi|^{8}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}},&|\xi|\geq\sqrt[3]{2}.\end{cases}

We define

L_{0}(t,\xi,a):=\cos\left(t|\xi|-t|\xi|^{4}\cdot\frac{a}{4+2\sqrt{4-a^{2}}}\right),

L_{1}(t,\xi,a):=\sin\left(t|\xi|-t|\xi|^{4}\cdot\frac{a}{4+2\sqrt{4-a^{2}}}\right)\biggr/\frac{|\xi|\sqrt{4-a^{2}}}{2},

H_{0}(t,\xi,b)=\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-4b}}\right),

H_{1}(t,\xi,b)=\exp\left(-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-4b}}\right)\biggr/|\xi|^{4}\sqrt{1-4b}.

Note that

L_{0}(t,\xi,0)=\cos(t|\xi|),\qquad L_{1}(t,\xi,0)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|},

and

E_{j}(t,\xi)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}L_{j}(t,\xi,|\xi|^{3}),\qquad j=0,1.

We also see that

H_{0}(t,\xi,|\xi|^{-6})=\frac{1}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}.

For
k\in\textbf{N}_{0}
, we put

\mathcal{L}_{j}^{k}(t,\xi):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}L_{j}}{\partial a^{k}}(t,\xi,0)\cdot|\xi|^{3k},\qquad j=0,1,

m[f]^{k}(\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)\,dx\right)(i\xi)^{\alpha},\qquad f\in L^{1,k}(\textbf{R}^{n}).

To state results of higher order asymptotic expansions in the low-frequency region (see Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
),
we prepare the following profiles.
For
j=0,1
, we define

\displaystyle A_{j}^{k}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}B_{j}^{0}(t,\xi),&k=0,\\[5.0pt]
A_{j}^{k-1}(t,\xi)+B_{j}^{k}(t,\xi),&k\in\textbf{N},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle B_{j}^{k}(t,\xi):=\sum_{p=0}^{[k/3]}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{p}(t,\xi)m[u_{j}]^{k-3p}(\xi),\qquad k\in\textbf{N}_{0}.

That is,

\displaystyle A_{j}^{k}(t,\xi)=\sum_{p=0}^{[k/3]}\left(\mathcal{L}_{j}^{p}(t,\xi)\sum_{q=0}^{k-3p}m[u_{j}]^{q}(\xi)\right).

Next, we prepare the following functions leading to higher order asymptotic expansions of the solution in the high-frequency region (see Theorem
4.6
).
For
k\in\textbf{N}_{0}
, we define

\mathcal{H}_{j}^{k}(t,\xi):=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}H_{j}}{\partial b^{k}}(t,\xi,0)\cdot|\xi|^{-6k},\qquad j=0,1.

For
j=0,1
, we define

\displaystyle C^{k}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}D^{0}(t,\xi),&k=0,\\[5.0pt]
C^{k-1}(t,\xi)+D^{k}(t,\xi),&k\in\textbf{N},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle D^{k}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{k/2}(t,\xi)+\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{k/2}(t,\xi)\right)\widehat{u_{0}}},&k\in 2\textbf{N}_{0},\\[11.0pt]
\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(k-1)/2}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}},&k\in 2\textbf{N}_{0}+1.\end{cases}

That is,

\displaystyle C^{k}(t,\xi):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)+\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}(t,\xi)\right)\widehat{u_{0}}},&k=0,\\[11.0pt]
\displaystyle{\sum_{p=0}^{[k/2]}\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{p}(t,\xi)+\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\widehat{u_{0}}+\sum_{p=0}^{[(k-1)/2]}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}},&k\in\textbf{N}.\end{cases}

4
Results

Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
are results of higher order asymptotic expansions of
E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}
and
E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}
in the low-frequency region, respectively.

Theorem 4.1

Let
n\geq 1
and
u_{0}\in L^{1,\gamma}(\textbf{R}^{n})
with
\gamma\geq 0
.
Then, it holds

\displaystyle\left\|E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}-A_{0}^{[\gamma]}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{1,\gamma}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{\gamma}{4}}

(4.1)

for
t\geq 0
.
Here,
C>0
is a constant independent of
t
and
u_{0}
.
Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{n}{8}+\frac{\gamma}{4}}\left\|E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}-A_{0}^{[\gamma]}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}=0.

(4.2)

Theorem 4.2

Let
n\geq 1
and
u_{1}\in L^{1,\gamma}(\textbf{R}^{n})
with

\displaystyle\gamma>\frac{1}{2}\quad(n=1),\qquad\gamma>0\quad(n=2),\qquad\gamma\geq 0\quad(n\geq 3).

(4.3)

Then, it holds

\displaystyle\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}-A_{1}^{[\gamma]}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\gamma}{4}}

(4.4)

for
t\geq 0
.
Here,
C>0
is a constant independent of
t
and
u_{1}
.
Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{4}}\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}-A_{1}^{[\gamma]}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}=0.

(4.5)

In the following theorems, we can find some optimal estimates in the low-frequency region, where the diffusive structure is strong in the sense that (
4.2
) and (
4.5
) holds.

Theorem 4.3

Let
n\geq 1
and
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
.
Then, it holds

\displaystyle C_{1}^{1}\left|\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right|\sqrt{t}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C_{2}^{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,1})\sqrt{t},

\displaystyle n=1,

\displaystyle C_{1}^{2}\left|\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right|\sqrt{\log t}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C_{2}^{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,1})\sqrt{\log t},

\displaystyle n=2,

\displaystyle C_{1}^{n}\left|\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right|t^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C_{2}^{n}(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,1})t^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}},

\displaystyle n\geq 3,

for sufficiently large
t
.
Here,
C_{1}^{n}>0
and
C_{2}^{n}>0

(n\geq 1)
are constants independent of
t
and the initial data.

Theorem 4.4

Let
n\geq 1
and
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
.
Then, it holds

\displaystyle C_{1}\sqrt{\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}}t^{-\frac{n}{8}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C_{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,1})t^{-\frac{n}{8}}

for sufficiently large
t
.
Here,
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
are constants independent of
t
and the initial data.

Theorem 4.5

Let
n\geq 1
and
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,2}(\textbf{R}^{n})
.
Then, it holds

\displaystyle\left\{C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}+C_{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{k}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+2C_{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}x_{k}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}+C_{3}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\xi_{j}-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cos(t|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{0}\|_{1,1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,2})t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{4}}

for sufficiently large
t
.
Here, we put

C_{1}:=\frac{1}{32}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{\eta_{1}^{4}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta,\quad C_{2}:=\frac{1}{16}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}\eta_{2}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta,\quad C_{3}:=\frac{1}{8n}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}|\eta|^{2}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta

and terms including
\displaystyle{\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}}
are read as zero when
n=1
.

Remark 4.1

Due to the choice of constants
C_{j}

(j=1,2,3)
, the lower bound in the theorem never becomes negative.
If and only if

\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}x_{k}u_{1}(x)\,dx=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{0}(x)\,dx=0\quad\mbox{for all $1\leq j\leq k\leq n$,}

the lower bound vanishes (see the proof in the next section).

Next, we state results on the asymptotic behavior of the solution in the high-frequency region.

Theorem 4.6

Let
n\geq 1
,
l\geq 0
and
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{l+1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in H^{l}(\textbf{R}^{n})
.
For each
k\in\textbf{N}_{0}
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-C^{k}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\geq\sqrt{2})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{l+1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{l}})\,(1+t)^{-\frac{l+3k+4}{2}},\qquad t\geq 0.

(4.6)

Here,
C>0
is a constant independent of
t
and the initial data.

Remark 4.2

1.

When we consider the case of
l=k=0
,
inequality (
4.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{u_{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\geq\sqrt{2})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{2})\,(1+t)^{-2},\qquad t\geq 0.

The profile
e^{-t/|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{u_{0}}
reflects the effect of regularity-loss.
It decays like
O(t^{-1/2})
as
t\to\infty
and thus the above estimate implies that this leading term of the energy solution decays quite slowly.

2.

We can assure

D^{k}(t,\xi)\not\equiv 0

for any
k\in\textbf{N}_{0}
.
This means that
C^{k}(t,\xi)\not\equiv C^{j}(t,\xi)
if
k\not=j
.
To confirm the statement, we first consider the case of
k\in 2\textbf{N}_{0}
.
Put
k^{\prime}=k/2
and

h(b):=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1-4b}}.

The terms in
D^{k}(t,\xi)
including the highest order of
t/|\xi|^{2}
are

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(k^{\prime})!}\left(-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{k^{\prime}}\{h^{\prime}(0)\}^{k^{\prime}}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}}{|\xi|^{6k^{\prime}}}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2(k^{\prime})!}\left(-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{k^{\prime}}\{h^{\prime}(0)\}^{k^{\prime}}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}}{|\xi|^{6k^{\prime}}}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k^{\prime})!}\left(-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{k^{\prime}}\{h^{\prime}(0)\}^{k^{\prime}}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}}{|\xi|^{6k^{\prime}}}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi).

Since
h^{\prime}(0)=1\not=0
, this value is not identically zero.
For the same reason, the statement is apparently true for
k\in 2\textbf{N}_{0}+1
.

The leading term of the solution to (
1.1
) is
A_{1}^{0}(t,\xi)
which arises from the low-frequency region or
C^{0}(t,\xi)
extracted from the high-frequency region.
As for the decay orders, the former depends on the spatial dimension
n
but the latter on the regularity condition
l
.
The next theorem and the subsequent corollary give a detailed look at the relationship between these two quantities and the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.7

Let
n\geq 1
,
l\geq 0
and
u
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in H^{l+1}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}\in H^{l}(\textbf{R}^{n})\cap L^{1,1}(\textbf{R}^{n})
.
Then, it holds

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{u_{0}}\right\|_{2}\leq\begin{cases}CI^{l,0}(u_{0},u_{1})t^{-\frac{l+4}{2}},&0\leq l\leq n/4-9/2\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n\geq 18,\\[7.0pt]
CI^{l,0}(u_{0},u_{1})t^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}},&n/4-9/2<l<n/4-3/2\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n\geq 18,\\[7.0pt]
&\,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\,0\leq l<n/4-3/2\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,7\leq n\leq 17,\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}-e^{-\frac{t}{|\xi|^{2}}}\widehat{u_{0}}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leq CI^{l,0}(u_{0},u_{1})t^{-\frac{n}{8}},\qquad l=n/4-3/2\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n\geq 6,

\displaystyle{\rm(iii)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leq\begin{cases}CI^{l,0}(u_{0},u_{1})t^{-\frac{l+1}{2}},&n/4-3/2<l\leq n/4-1\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n\geq 6,\\[7.0pt]
&\,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\,0\leq l\leq n/4-1\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n=4,5,\\[7.0pt]
CI^{l,0}(u_{0},u_{1})t^{-\frac{n}{8}},&n/4-1<l\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n\geq 4,\\[7.0pt]
&\,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\,0\leq l\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,n=1,2,3,\end{cases}

for
t\geq 1
.
Here,
C>0
is a constant independent of
t
and the initial data.

Lemma 5.1

Let

Then, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\left\|E_{0}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{0}^{p}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{3(k+1)}{4}},

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3(k+1)}{4}},

(5.2)

Proof.

The Taylor theorem gives

E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\frac{\partial^{k+1}L_{1}}{\partial a^{k+1}}(t,\xi,\tau|\xi|^{3})\cdot|\xi|^{3(k+1)}

Thus, for each

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{j}L_{1}}{\partial a^{j}}(t,\xi,a)\right|\leq\frac{C}{|\xi|}\sum_{p=0}^{j}(t|\xi|^{4})^{p}

(5.3)

for
t\geq 0
,
\xi\in\textbf{R}^{n}
and
0\leq a\leq 1
.
Now, we consider
\xi\in\textbf{R}^{n}
with

\displaystyle\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)+\left(E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\right\}\times\left\{\sum_{q=0}^{[\gamma]}m[u_{1}]^{q}(\xi)+\left(\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)-\sum_{q=0}^{[\gamma]}m[u_{1}]^{q}(\xi)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\left(\sum_{q=0}^{[\gamma]}m[u_{1}]^{q}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\left(\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)-\sum_{q=0}^{[\gamma]}m[u_{1}]^{q}(\xi)\right)+\left(E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi).

It follows from (
5.3
) and (
7.3

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\left(\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)-\sum_{q=0}^{[\gamma]}m[u_{1}]^{q}(\xi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\left\|(t|\xi|^{4})^{p}|\xi|^{\gamma-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}\left\||\xi|^{\gamma-1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}t|\xi|^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\gamma}{4}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Here, we used (
4.3
) to assure the following integrability:

\displaystyle\left\||\xi|^{\gamma-1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}t|\xi|^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}|\eta|^{2\gamma-2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n+2\gamma-3}e^{-\frac{1}{2}s^{4}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}},\qquad t\geq 0.

From (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(E_{1}(t,\xi)-\sum_{p=0}^{[\gamma/3]}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{p}(t,\xi)\right)\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3([\gamma/3]+1)}{4}}\|u_{1}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\gamma}{4}}\|u_{1}\|_{1},\qquad t\geq 0.

Later, we deal with the term

Here, we note that

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,[\gamma]}\sum_{p=1}^{[\gamma/3]}\left\|(t|\xi|^{4})^{p}|\xi|^{[\gamma]}e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We see that (
4.4
) with
\gamma=1
and the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{1,1}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{8}},\qquad t\geq 0,

give the upper bound.
So, we prove the lower bound.
First, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}=\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-A_{1}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}-A_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left\|E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}-A_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-\left\|\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-o(t^{-\frac{n}{8}})-o(t^{-\frac{n}{8}})-O(t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{3}{4}}),\qquad t\to\infty.

Here, we used (
4.5
) with
\gamma=1
and (
4.2
) with
\gamma=0
.
Direct calculation shows

\displaystyle B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{0}(t,\xi)m[u_{1}]^{1}(\xi)=-i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\xi_{j},

\displaystyle B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)m[u_{0}]^{0}(\xi)=\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cos(t|\xi|).

If
B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\equiv 0
, then the lower bound in the theorem becomes zero.
Thus, it suffices to show it under
B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\not\equiv 0
.
In this case, one has

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}=\left\|B_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}+\left\|B_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\biggr(\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}\xi_{j}\biggr)^{2}\,d\xi+P_{0}^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\cos^{2}(t|\xi|)\,d\xi.

Here, we put

P_{1,j}:=\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)\,dx,\qquad P_{0}:=\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\,dx.

Since

\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\xi_{j}\xi_{k}\,d\xi=0

for
1\leq j<k\leq n
with
n\geq 2
,
one has

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\biggr(\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}\xi_{j}\biggr)^{2}\,d\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\xi_{j}^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)\,d\xi\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)\,d\eta\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}^{2}\right)t^{-\frac{n}{4}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n}\left(\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{1,j}^{2}\right)t^{-\frac{n}{4}},\qquad t\gg 1,

for all spatial dimensions
n\geq 1
.
Now, we use the following estimate derived by the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma whose origin is in
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\cos(2t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta,\qquad t\gg 1.

Similarly, we have

P_{0}^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\cos^{2}(t|\xi|)\,d\xi\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\xi\right)P_{0}^{2}t^{-\frac{n}{4}},\qquad t\gg 1.

Therefore, we obtain the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We prove the lower bound only.
It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\xi_{j}-\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cos(t|\xi|)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-A_{1}^{1}(t,\xi)-A_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-\left\|E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{1}}-A_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\|E_{0}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}-A_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-\left\|\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{2}E_{1}(t,\xi)\widehat{u_{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}-o(t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{4}})-o(t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{1}{4}})-O(t^{-\frac{n}{8}-\frac{3}{4}}),\qquad t\to\infty.

Here, we used (
4.5
) with
\gamma=2
and (
4.2
) with
\gamma=1
.
For the same reason as in the previous proof, we consider the case of
B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)+B_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)\not\equiv 0
.
By the definition, we have

\displaystyle B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{0}(t,\xi)m[u_{1}]^{2}(\xi)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}m[u_{1}]^{2}(\xi),

\displaystyle B_{0}^{1}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{0}(t,\xi)m[u_{1}]^{1}(\xi)=-i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{0}(x)\,dx\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}t|\xi|^{4}}\cos(t|\xi|)\xi_{j}.

First, we deal with
B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)

Since
B_{1}^{2}(t,c\xi)=c^{2}B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)
for all

, we have

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\big(m[u_{1}]^{2}(\xi)\big)^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq t^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\big(m[u_{1}]^{2}(\eta)\big)^{2}\,d\eta,\qquad t\geq 1.

If
m[u_{1}]^{2}(\eta)\not\equiv 0
, the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma gives

\displaystyle\left\|B_{1}^{2}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq 1)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}t^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\big(m[u_{1}]^{2}(\eta)\big)^{2}\,d\eta,\qquad t\gg 1.

.

On the other hand, the case of

is more complicated.
Since

m[u_{1}]^{2}(\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\eta_{j}^{2}\\
-\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}x_{k}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\eta_{j}\eta_{k},

we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\big(m[u_{1}]^{2}(\eta)\big)^{2}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{\eta_{j}^{4}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{k}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{\eta_{j}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}x_{k}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{\eta_{j}^{2}\eta_{k}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8C_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+8C_{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{k}^{2}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)+16C_{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left(\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}x_{j}x_{k}u_{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{2}.

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot t^{-\frac{3}{4}}=t^{\frac{3}{4}},\qquad t\geq 1,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\eta}\,d\eta\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}/2}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\eta}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}\cdot\frac{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3}{4}}}\right)\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{4}}}{8e},\qquad t\geq 1.

Next, we see that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{e^{-\eta^{4}}}{\eta^{2}}\,d\eta=\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\left(-\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\prime}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\left[-\frac{e^{-\eta^{4}}}{\eta}\right]_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}-4\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\eta^{2}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta\leq t^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\leq t^{\frac{3}{4}},\qquad t\geq 1.

Since

\sin\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad 1\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2},

we have

\sin(t^{\frac{3}{4}}\eta)\geq\frac{1}{2}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\eta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{e^{-\eta^{4}}}{\eta^{2}}\,d\eta=\frac{1}{4}\left[-\frac{e^{-\eta^{4}}}{\eta}\right]_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}-\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\eta^{2}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}t^{\frac{3}{4}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)-\frac{2}{\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{4}}{16t^{3}}\right)\right)-\int_{0}^{2}\eta^{2}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}t^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{c}{t^{3}}\right)\right)-C

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4e}\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)t^{\frac{3}{4}}-C,\qquad t\geq 1,

where

c:=\frac{\pi^{4}}{16}-1>0,\qquad C:=\int_{0}^{2}\eta^{2}e^{-\eta^{4}}\,d\eta.

Thus, one has

I_{2}\geq\frac{1}{8e}\left(1-\frac{2}{\pi}\right)t^{\frac{3}{4}},\qquad t\gg 1.

The proof is now complete.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 7.4

Let
n=2
.
There exist constants
0<C_{1}^{2}<C_{2}^{2}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}^{2}\log t\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi\leq C_{2}^{2}\log t,\qquad t\gg 1.

(7.5)

Proof.

Similarly, we divide the integral as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi=\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}+\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\right)e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta=:I_{3}+I_{4},\qquad t\geq 1.

It follows that

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta\leq t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\,d\eta=\pi,\qquad t\geq 1,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4}\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\,d\eta=\frac{\pi}{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}re^{-r^{4}}\,dr\geq\frac{\pi}{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}r^{3}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\left[-\frac{1}{4}e^{-r^{4}}\right]_{0}^{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}=\frac{\pi}{8}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\right)=\frac{\pi}{8}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}+O(t^{-\frac{9}{2}}),\qquad t\gg 1,

since

\displaystyle 1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{t^{3}}-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)^{k}\geq\frac{1}{t^{3}}-\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\right)\frac{1}{t^{6}}.

Next, we see that

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta=2\pi\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{e^{-r^{4}}}{r}\,dr=2\pi\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}(\log r)^{\prime}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\left[(\log r)e^{-r^{4}}\right]_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}+8\pi\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}(\log r)r^{3}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\left\{\frac{1}{4}(\log t)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t}\right)+\frac{3}{4}(\log t)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\right\}+8\pi\int_{t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{t^{\frac{1}{4}}}(\log r)r^{3}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr,

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\log t+8\pi C_{0},\qquad t\geq 1,

where

C_{0}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\log r|r^{3}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr<+\infty.

Note that the function
e^{-x^{4}}/x^{2}

(x>0)
is monotone decreasing and

\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)\geq\frac{1}{2}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+j\pi\right)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\eta|\leq\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}+j\pi\right)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\quad(j\in\textbf{N}).

The largest number
j\in\textbf{N}
satisfying
(3\pi/4+j\pi)t^{-3/4}\leq t^{1/4}
is
[t/\pi-3/4]
.
Thus, one has

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{[t/\pi-3/4]}\int_{(\frac{\pi}{4}+j\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\eta|\leq(\frac{3\pi}{4}+j\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{(\frac{5\pi}{4}+j\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq|\eta|\leq(\frac{5\pi}{4}+[t/\pi-3/4]\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{\frac{5\pi}{4}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{(\frac{5\pi}{4}+[t/\pi-3/4]\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{e^{-r^{4}}}{r}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\left[(\log r)e^{-r^{4}}\right]_{\frac{5\pi}{4}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{(\frac{5\pi}{4}+[t/\pi-3/4]\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}+2\pi\int_{\frac{5\pi}{4}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}^{(\frac{5\pi}{4}+[t/\pi-3/4]\pi)t^{-\frac{3}{4}}}(\log r)r^{3}e^{-r^{4}}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\log t-\log\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)\right)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)^{4}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\log t-\log\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}+\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]\pi\right)\right)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}+\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]\pi\right)^{4}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)-2\pi C_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\pi}{8}(\log t)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)^{4}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{t^{3}}\right)\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\log\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)^{4}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\pi}{2}\log\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}+\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]\pi\right)\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}+\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]\pi\right)^{4}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)-2\pi C_{0},\qquad t\gg 1,

where

\displaystyle\alpha:=\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}+\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]\pi\right)^{4}-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)^{4}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{t^{3}}\geq\left[\frac{t}{\pi}-\frac{3}{4}\right]^{4}\frac{\pi^{4}}{t^{3}},

there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{t^{3}}\right)\leq e^{-c^{\prime}t},\qquad t\gg 1.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3\pi}{16}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)^{4}\right)(\log t)-\frac{\pi}{2}\log\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)-2\pi C_{0},\qquad t\gg 1.

Therefore, we complete the proof.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 7.5

Let
n\geq 3
.
There exist constants
0<C_{1}^{n}<C_{2}^{n}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}^{n}t^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi\leq C_{2}^{n}t^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad t\gg 1.

(7.6)

Proof.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\,d\xi=t^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta.

We can easily see that

\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta\leq\int_{\textbf{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta=\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n-3}e^{-x^{4}}\,dx<+\infty,\qquad t>0.

On the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\int_{|\eta|\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta\geq\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}e^{-|\eta|^{4}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\eta|\leq 1}\frac{e^{-|\eta|^{4}}}{|\eta|^{2}}\cos(2t^{\frac{3}{4}}|\eta|)\,d\eta,\qquad t\geq 1.

The Riemann-Lebesgue lemma gives the lower bound.

\hfill\Box

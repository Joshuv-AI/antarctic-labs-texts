Exact solution of a lambda quantum system driven by a two-photon wavepacket
W. Lopes 1, D. Valente 1

Abstract
Three-level atoms in lambda configuration find diverse applications in quantum information processing, and a promising way to manipulate their quantum states is with single-photon pulses propagating in a waveguide (which can be theoretically regarded as a highly broadband regime of the Jaynes-Cummings model). Here, we analytically find the non-perturbative dynamics of a lambda atom driven by a two-photon wavepacket, propagating in a one-dimensional electromagnetic environment. As an application, we study the dynamics of a quantum state purification. By comparing our exact model with an approximated model of two cascaded single-photon wavepackets, we show how two-photon nonlinearities and stimulated emission affect the purification.



I
Introduction

Two-level quantum systems can play the role of qbits, i.e., quantum bits of information.
Implementing a logical qbit within the two ground states of a three-level atom in
\Lambda
configuration has the advantage that the excited state provides an energetic barrier, blocking unwanted jumps between the two target states, thus increasing the stability of physical operations.
This motivates various applications of the
\Lambda
atoms, such as in quantum memories
rmp2017
;
molmer
, quantum cloning
dvnjp
;
dv2012
;
qsr
;
csimon1
;
csimon2
, and quantum state purification
qda
;
cp22
.

To achieve efficient manipulation of the atom, one can either employ optical cavities or one-dimensional (1D) waveguides.
Waveguides are especially desirable if one wishes that a propagating single-photon wavepacket interacts with a single atom, for instance if the photon works as a flying qubit making distant atoms in a network to communicate

kimble
;
lodahl
;
nori
;
jmg
.
From a theoretical viewpoint, these engineered 1D environments can be described as the highly broadband regime of the Jaynes-Cummings (JC) model
chen2004
;
fan
;
domokos
.
This has unraveled a rich diversity of effects, including non-Markovianity
nonmarkov
, broadband photon-photon correlations
roy
;
njp17
;
lodahl22
, irreversible stimulated emission
dvnjp
;
continuum2018
;
dv2012
, as well as the application of quantum mirrors in Fabry-Perot
prlfratini
and Mach-Zehnder
nelson
interferometers.

Here, our main motivation is to understand how the type of quantum state purification for a
\Lambda
atom as proposed in Ref.
qda
is affected by a two-photon wavepacket.
To that end, we present an exact non-perturbative solution, in real-space representation, for the dynamics of a two-photon wavepacket interacting with a single
\Lambda
system in a broadband one-dimensional environment.
We describe light-matter interaction by employing the broadband JC model, and analytically compute the quantum state of the composite atom-plus-field system within the two-excitation subspace.
We obtain the transition probability between the two ground states of the atom, depending on the initial two-photon pulse parameters.
In order to highlight the presence of two-photon nonlinearities, we compare our exact results with the effective model of two cascaded (consecutive) single-photon wavepackets.

II
Model

We consider a global time-independent Hamiltonian of the system and its environment,

H=H_{S}+H_{I}+H_{E}.

(1)

We model light-matter interaction via a dipole coupling, in the rotating-wave approximation
domokos
;
chen2004
;
fan
;
dvnjp
;
dv2012
;
nonmarkov
,

H_{I}=-i\hbar\sum_{\omega}(g_{a}a_{\omega}\sigma_{a}^{\dagger}+g_{b}b_{\omega}\sigma_{b}^{\dagger}-\mbox{H.c.}),

(2)

where
\sigma_{k}=|k\rangle\langle e|
(for
k=a,b
) and
H.c.
is the Hermitian conjugate.
Orthogonal polarization modes
\left\{a_{\omega}\right\}
and
\left\{b_{\omega}\right\}
respectively interact with transitions
|a\rangle
to
|e\rangle
and
|b\rangle
to
|e\rangle
.
This is a multi-mode Jaynes-Cummings model, and our interest is in the limit of a broadband continuum of frequencies,

\sum_{\omega}\rightarrow\int d\omega\varrho_{\omega}\approx\varrho\int d\omega,

(3)

where we have defined a constant density of modes
\varrho_{\omega}\approx\varrho
.
Physically, this means that there is no frequency filter in our idealized environment.
The continuum of frequencies allows us to employ a Wigner-Weisskopf approximation to obtain dissipation rates

\Gamma_{a}=2\pi g_{a}^{2}\varrho
and

\Gamma_{b}=2\pi g_{b}^{2}\varrho
.
The environment Hamiltonian reads

H_{E}=\sum_{\omega}\hbar\omega(a_{\omega}^{\dagger}a_{\omega}+b_{\omega}^{\dagger}b_{\omega}).

(4)

The system is described by a three-level Hamiltonian, with states named
|a\rangle
,
|b\rangle
, and
|e\rangle
, so that

H_{S}=\hbar\omega_{a}|e\rangle\langle e|+\hbar\delta_{ab}|b\rangle\langle b|.

(5)

When in
\Lambda
configuration, these energy levels provide an elementary energy barrier (the excited state
|e\rangle
) separating two highly stable states (the two ground states
|a\rangle
and
|b\rangle
).
The energy difference between states
|b\rangle
and
|a\rangle
, given by
\hbar\delta_{ab}=\hbar(\omega_{a}-\omega_{b})
, although relevant for thermal equilibrium considerations, does not affect our results as far as nonequilibrium effects are concerned.

As stated above, our main interest here is in applying our exact solution to the study of quantum state purification.
This is the process where the reduced state of the atom asymptotically goes from a mixed to a pure state, such as

p_{a}^{(0)}|a\rangle\langle a|+p_{b}^{(0)}|b\rangle\langle b|\rightarrow|b\rangle\langle b|,

(6)

after being driven by the photons and dissipated to the environment.
To achieve this kind of dynamics, we focus on the case where the initial state of the field is a two-photon wavepacket polarized at modes
\left\{a_{\omega}\right\}
.
This means that the photons will leave the atom unaltered, if initially at
|b\rangle
.
Thus the reduced state of the atom evolves as

p_{a}^{(0)}\mbox{tr}_{F}[U|a,2_{AA}\rangle\langle a,2_{AA}|U^{\dagger}]+p_{b}^{(0)}\mbox{tr}_{F}[U|b,2_{AA}\rangle\langle b,2_{AA}|U^{\dagger}]=p_{a}^{(0)}\mbox{tr}_{F}[U|a,2_{AA}\rangle\langle a,2_{AA}|U^{\dagger}]+p_{b}^{(0)}|b\rangle\langle b|,

(7)

where
\mbox{tr}_{F}
is the partial trace over the field degrees of freedom,
and
U=\exp(-iHt/\hbar)
.
We thus have to focus on the
|a\rangle\rightarrow|b\rangle
transition probability, defined as

p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)\equiv\langle b|\mbox{tr}_{F}[U|a,2_{AA}\rangle\langle a,2_{AA}|U^{\dagger}]|b\rangle,

(8)

if we wish to study the purification dynamics of the atomic state.
If
p_{a\rightarrow b}(\infty)=1
, the atom will be left at the pure state
|b\rangle
, asymptotically.
See Fig.(
1
).

III
Results

III.1
Quantum state of the global atom-plus-field system in the two-excitation subspace

We take advantage of the number conservation as provided by the JC model in order to restrict ourselves to the two-excitation subspace, spanned by

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\omega}\psi_{\omega}^{A}\hat{a}_{\omega}^{\dagger}|e,0\rangle+\sum_{\omega}\psi_{\omega}^{B}\hat{b}_{\omega}^{\dagger}|e,0\rangle+\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{AA}\hat{a}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\omega_{2}}^{\dagger}|a,0\rangle+2\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{AB}\hat{a}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{\omega_{2}}^{\dagger}|a,0\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{BB}\hat{b}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{\omega_{2}}^{\dagger}|b,0\rangle+2\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{BA}\hat{b}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\omega_{2}}^{\dagger}|b,0\rangle.

(9)

By substituting that into the Schrödinger equation,
i\hbar\partial_{t}|\psi\rangle=H|\psi\rangle
, where
H
is given in Eq.(
1
), we find that

\partial_{t}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)=-i(\omega_{a}+\omega)\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)-2\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}[g_{a}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(t)+g_{b}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(t)].

(10)

\partial_{t}\psi_{\omega}^{B}(t)=-i(\omega_{a}+\omega)\psi_{\omega}^{B}(t)-2\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}[g_{b}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BB}(t)+g_{a}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AB}(t)];

(11)

\partial_{t}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(t)=-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(t)+\frac{1}{2}(g_{a}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)+g_{a}\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}^{A}(t))

(12)

\partial_{t}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BB}(t)=-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BB}(t)+\frac{1}{2}(g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{B}(t)+g_{b}\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}^{B}(t));

(13)

\partial_{t}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AB}(t)=-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AB}(t)+\frac{1}{2}g_{a}\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}^{B}(t);

(14)

\partial_{t}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(t)=-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(t)+\frac{1}{2}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t).

(15)

III.2
Formal solution of the amplitudes

Inspired by the Wigner-Weisskopf theory of the spontaneous emission, we first integrate the field equations,

\displaystyle\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(t)=\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(0)e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})t}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})t^{\prime}}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(16)

and

\displaystyle\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(t)=\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(0)e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t^{\prime}}\left(g_{a}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})+g_{a}\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}^{A}(t^{\prime})\right)dt^{\prime}.

(17)

We then substitute them back into the excited-state amplitudes,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i(\omega_{a}+\omega)\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)-2\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}[g_{a}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(0)e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})t}+g_{b}\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(0)e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})}+

(18)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{a}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})(t-t^{\prime})}g_{a}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}-g_{a}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(\omega+\omega^{\prime})(t-t^{\prime})}g_{a}\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}+

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{b}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})(t-t^{\prime})}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}].

(19)

Because we are interested in the case where the initial state of the atom is
|a\rangle
and the field is prepared with two photons at polarization
A
, we have only shown the solutions that are relevant to this scenario.
The more general case can be easily obtained by performing the same steps as used here.

III.3
Real-space representation

We define the real-space representations of all the amplitudes, namely,

\displaystyle\psi^{A}(r,t)=\sum_{\omega}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t)e^{ik_{\omega}r},

(20)

and

\displaystyle\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)=\sum_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}\phi_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}^{AA}(t)e^{ik_{\omega}r_{1}+ik_{\omega^{\prime}}r_{2}},

(21)

with analogous expressions for the
B
components.
Here, we use a linear approximation for the dispersion relation,
k_{\omega}=\omega/c
, with
c
being the speed of light.
The idea that only
k_{\omega}>0
is considered can be justified in two ways: either with unidirectional waveguides employing chiral couplings between atom and field, or considering that each polarization mode is a symmetric superposition of two counter-propagating modes in a bidirectional waveguide.

In these representations, the equations of motion read

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+c\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right]\psi^{A}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)\psi^{A}(r,t)-2g_{a}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0)

\displaystyle-\Gamma_{a}\psi^{A}(-r,t-r/c)\Theta(r)\Theta\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right),

(22)

where we have defined
\Gamma\equiv\Gamma_{a}+\Gamma_{b}
, as obtained after we employ the Wigner-Weisskopf approximation,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a,b}=\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}2\pi g_{a,b}^{2}\delta(\omega_{a,b}-\omega^{\prime})=2\pi g_{a,b}^{2}\varrho.

(23)

Because the coupling
g_{a}
is not experimentally accessible in this regime (only
\Gamma_{a}
is), we take
g_{a}=\sqrt{\Gamma_{a}/(2\pi\varrho)}
, where
\varrho
is the density of modes defined in Eq.(
3
) (the density of modes
\varrho
cancels out at the end, so no probabilities will depend on it).
Another crucial remark concerning the Wigner-Weisskopf approximation employed here is that, at some point in the calculation, we have an integration of the type

\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\delta(t^{\prime}-\tau)
,
where
\tau=t-r/c
.
Because
0<t^{\prime}<t
, this term amounts to zero, unless
0<\tau<t
, which gives us
0<t-r/c<t
;
hence
0<t-r/c
justifies the Heaviside step function
\Theta(t-r/c)
, whereas
t-r/c<t
gives us
r>0
, so
\Theta(r)
appears.
In Eq.(
22
), we have also set

\phi_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}^{BA}(r-ct,-ct,0)=0
,
since we are considering that no
B
-polarized photons are incident on the atom.

The interpretation of Eq.(
22
) is that of a wave equation propagating towards the positive direction in space.
The damping term accounts for the spontaneous emission of the atom, so the homogeneous solution is

\psi^{A}(r-ct,0)\exp[-(\Gamma+i\omega_{a})t]
.
The second term on the right-hand side of Eq.(
22
) corresponds to a pair of photons driving the atom, with strength proportional to the coupling frequency
g_{a}
.
If
g_{a}=g_{b}=0
, the field freely propagates at speed
c
, so its amplitude will obey to

\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)=\phi^{AA}(r_{1}-ct,r_{2}-ct,0)
, in that case.
This allows us to understand the following reasoning.
The atom is at position
r_{a}=0
, so if there is a chance that a photon starts at position
r_{2}=-ct
, then this photon will reach the atom after a time
t
.
This explains the dependence of the driving amplitude on
-ct
.
The other photon started at
r_{1}=r-ct
, explaining why
\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0)
also depends on
r-ct
.
Finally, the last term on the right-hand side describes stimulated emission.
To see that, we notice that
\Theta(r)
is nonzero only for
r>0
, so
\psi^{A}(-r,t-r/c)
is the excited-state amplitude evaluated at a negative position,
-r<0
, where the presence of a photon (with
A
polarization) can stimulate the emission of the excited atom.

III.4
Solution of the excited-state amplitude in the real-space representation

Because Eq.(
22
) presents a discontinuity due to the Heaviside step functions, we proceed to solve it by a stepwise partitioning the space into three domains, as follows.

•

Region I:
r<0
.

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+c\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right]\psi_{1}^{A}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)\psi_{1}^{A}(r,t)-2g_{a}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0).

(24)

•

Region II:
0<r<ct
.

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+c\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right]\psi_{2}^{A}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)\psi_{2}^{A}(r,t)-\Gamma_{a}\psi_{1}^{A}(-r,t-r/c)

\displaystyle-2g_{a}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0).

(25)

Note that the dynamics in Region II depends on the solution of Region I.

•

Region III:
r>ct
.

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+c\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right]\psi_{3}^{A}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)\psi_{3}^{A}(r,t)-2g_{a}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0).

(26)

To solve for Region I, we look for a solution of the type

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{A}(r,t)=e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\bar{\psi}_{1}^{A}(r,t),

(27)

as motivated by the homogeneous solution.
We thus find that

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{1}^{A}=-2g_{a}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0),

(28)

where we have defined

\hat{\theta}\equiv\partial_{t}+c\partial_{r}
,
so the inhomogeneous solution is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{1}^{A}(r,t)=-2g_{a}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)dt^{\prime}.

(29)

To make sense of this solution, we can see it as describing one photon driving the atom (term
-ct^{\prime}
), while the other photon is located at position
r<0
at time
t>0
.
A formal justification of the solution can be given by assuming a general decomposition such as

\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0)=\sum_{nm}C_{nm}R_{n}(r-ct)T_{m}(-ct)
,
where
C_{nm}
are generic coefficients, with
R_{n}
and
T_{m}
being generic functions.
By noting that

\hat{\theta}R_{n}(r-ct)=0
,
we reobtain the original differential equation.

Region II is far richer in terms of dynamical behavior, since it depends on the solution of Region I.
Again, we first eliminate the homogeneous part by taking the transformation

\psi_{2}^{A}(r,t)=e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\bar{\psi}_{2}^{A}(r,t),

(30)

which implies that

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{2}^{A}(r,t)=-e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\left[\Gamma_{a}\psi_{1}^{A}\left(-r,t-r/c\right)+2g_{a}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct,0)\right],

(31)

where, from Eq.(
29
), we have that

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{A}(-r,t-r/c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2g_{a}e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)(t-r/c)}\int_{0}^{t-r/c}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\phi^{AA}(-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)dt^{\prime}.

(32)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle F(t,t-r/c).

(33)

The purpose of defining
F(t,t-r/c)
above is just to highlight that the solution only depends on
t
and
t-r/c
.
Because both terms on the right-hand side of Eq.(
31
) are functions of
t
and
t-r/c
, we can search for a solution in a similar form as we did in Region I, namely,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{2}^{A}(r,t)=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a}\int_{t-r/c}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}F(t^{\prime},t-r/c)dt^{\prime}-2g_{a}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)dt^{\prime}.

(34)

A crucial remark: the integration in the first term of Eq.(
34
) is starting at
t-r/c
.
Replacing
t-r/c
by
0
in that term also provides a correct solution for Eq.(
31
).
However, our final interest is in solving Eq.(
22
), to which this makes a key difference.
We further explain why by the end of this section.

Region III has precisely the same equation of motion of Region I, so the solution is

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{3}^{A}(r,t)=-2g_{a}e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)dt^{\prime}.

(35)

If one is interested in considering a two-photon wavepacket that initially occupies only the
r<0
region (Region I), as we are here, one can assume that

\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},0)=0
for
r_{1}>0
, which amounts to say that

\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)=0
for
r>ct
(that defines Region III).

Putting all three regions together, and adding the homogeneous solution, we get that

\displaystyle\psi^{A}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{A}(r-ct,0)e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}-2g_{a}e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)+

(36)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{a}g_{a}\Theta(r)\Theta(t-r/c)e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t}e^{-\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)(t-r/c)}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{t-r/c}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{t-r/c}dt^{\prime\prime}e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2}+i\omega_{a}\right)t^{\prime\prime}}\phi^{AA}(-ct^{\prime},-ct^{\prime\prime},0).

The first term corresponds to two simultaneous events, namely, the free evolution of the single-photon packet, and the spontaneous emission of the atom.
The second term corresponds to one photon exciting the atom, while the other photon freely propagates.
And the third term describes both photons driving the atom, since
\phi^{AA}(-ct,-ct,0)
indicates both photons having arrived at the atomic position

after time

has passed.

We can now give a justification for the rather unusual integration of Eq.(

) (from

to

).
Starting from Eq.(

) and substituting it back into Eq.(

), we get a spurious extra term coming from

\partial_{r}\Theta(r)=\delta(r)
.
The lower limit defined as

exactly cancels out that spurious term, since

\delta(r)\int_{t-r/c}^{t}(...)=0
.

III.5
Solution of the field amplitudes in the real-space representation

Our main interest is in finding an expression for

\phi^{BA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)=\sum_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}\phi^{BA}_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}(t)\exp(ik_{\omega}r_{1}+ik_{\omega^{\prime}}r_{2})
.
Analogous to Eq.(
16
), and choosing the initial condition
\phi^{BA}_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}}(0)=0
, we have that

\displaystyle\phi^{BA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})t^{\prime}}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)e^{ik_{\omega}r_{1}+ik_{\omega^{\prime}}r_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(\omega+\omega^{\prime}+\delta_{ab})(t-t^{\prime})}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)e^{ik_{\omega}r_{1}+ik_{\omega^{\prime}}r_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ik_{\omega}[r_{1}-c(t-t^{\prime})]}e^{i\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{c}[r_{2}-c(t-t^{\prime})]}e^{-i\delta_{ab}(t-t^{\prime})}g_{b}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{b}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\omega}\psi_{\omega}^{A}(t^{\prime})e^{ik_{\omega}[r_{1}-c(t-t^{\prime})]}\right)\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{i\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{c}[r_{2}-c(t-t^{\prime})]}e^{-i\delta_{ab}(t-t^{\prime})}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{b}\int_{0}^{t}\psi^{A}(r_{1}-c(t-t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})\sum_{\omega^{\prime}}e^{-i\omega^{\prime}[(t-r_{2}/c)-t^{\prime}]}e^{-i\delta_{ab}(t-t^{\prime})}dt^{\prime}.

We take the continuum limit of the sum and perform a Wigner-Weisskopf approximation once again.
We also have to make sure that
0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
is consistent with such approximation.
These two inequalities are guaranteed if we multiply the solution by

\Theta(r_{2})\Theta(t-r_{2}/c)
.
We finally find that

\displaystyle\phi^{BA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\varrho\Gamma_{b}}}{2}\Theta(r_{2})\Theta\left(t-\frac{r_{2}}{c}\right)e^{-i\delta_{ab}r_{2}/{c}}\psi^{A}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c).

(37)

III.6
Transition probabilities in the real-space representation

The transition probability between the two ground states of the
\Lambda
atom, i.e., from
|a\rangle
to
|b\rangle
is given by

p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)=\langle b|\mbox{tr}_{F}(|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|)|b\rangle,

(38)

provided that
|\psi(0)\rangle=|a,2_{AA}\rangle=\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{AA}(0)\hat{a}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\omega_{2}}^{\dagger}|a,0\rangle
.
Also,
|\psi(t)\rangle=\exp(-iHt/\hbar)|\psi(0)\rangle
,
where
H
is given in Eq.(
1
),
and
tr_{F}
is the partial trace over the field degrees of freedom.

Within the two-excitation subspace, Eq.(
9
), we have that

p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)=\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}|\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{BB}(t)|^{2}+4\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}|\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{BA}(t)|^{2}.

(39)

Here, we are mainly interested in the case where

\phi^{BB}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(0)=0
,
implying that

\phi^{BB}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(t)=0
.
To compute the non-vanishing term, we have to transform it to the real-space representation, finding that

\sum_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}|\phi_{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}^{BA}(t)|^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi^{BA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)\big|^{2}dr_{1}dr_{2}.

(40)

By substituting Eq.(
37
) in the double integration above, and using Eq.(
36
) for the excited-state amplitude, we have the exact solution of the transition probability for a generic initial pulse
\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},0)
.

III.7
Analytically solvable example

Let us compute

\displaystyle p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dr_{1}\ |\phi^{BA}(r_{1},r_{2},t)\big|^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\varrho\Gamma_{b}}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dr_{1}\ |\psi^{A}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)\big|^{2},

(42)

for the case where the atom is initially at ground-state
|a\rangle
, so that
\psi^{A}(r,0)=0
, and the initial photon pair is prepared by means of the spontaneous emission of two hypothetical uncorrelated atoms, arbitrarily far away from our
\Lambda
atom, both polarized at
A
.
We can thus choose

\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},0)=N\bigg[f_{1}(r_{1})f_{2}(r_{2})+f_{1}(r_{2})f_{2}(r_{1})\bigg],

(43)

where

f_{k}(r)=\Theta(-r)\exp[(\Delta_{k}/2+i\omega_{Lk})r/c],

(44)

and
N
is a normalization constant satisfying
\langle\psi(0)|\psi(0)\rangle=1
,
equivalent to

2\sum_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}|\phi^{AA}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(0)|^{2}=1
,
or in the real-space representation,

\frac{2}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi^{AA}(r_{1},r_{2},0)\big|^{2}dr_{1}dr_{2}=1.

(45)

Using Eqs.(
43
) and (
44
), and also assuming resonant photons,
\omega_{L1}=\omega_{L2}
, we find that

N=\pi\varrho\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}{1+r_{12}}},

(46)

where we have defined the ratio

r_{12}\equiv 4\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}/(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})^{2}
.
Note that
r_{12}\leq 1
, the equality holding if
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{2}
.

We now have two terms to solve in Eq.(
36
) for
\psi^{A}(r,t)
.

The first, where the integration of
\phi^{AA}(r-ct,-ct^{\prime},0)
appears, amounts to

\psi^{A1}(r,t)=-2Ng_{a}\Theta(t-r/c)e^{-\frac{\Gamma}{2}t}\bigg[e^{-\frac{\Delta_{1}}{2}(t-\frac{r}{c})}\frac{2}{\Gamma-\Delta_{2}}\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{2}}{2}\right)t}-1\right)+e^{-\frac{\Delta_{2}}{2}(t-\frac{r}{c})}\frac{2}{\Gamma-\Delta_{1}}\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{1}}{2}\right)t}-1\right)\bigg].

(47)

When we compute
\psi^{A1}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)
, we get that
\Theta(t-r_{1}/c)
alters the integration from

\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dr_{1}

to

\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{ct}dr_{1}
.

The second term, where a double integration of
\phi^{AA}(-ct^{\prime},-ct^{\prime\prime},0)
appears, amounts to

\displaystyle\psi^{A2}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8N}{(\Gamma-\Delta_{1})(\Gamma-\Delta_{2})}g_{a}\Gamma_{a}\Theta(r)\Theta(t-r/c)e^{-\frac{\Gamma}{2}t}e^{-\frac{\Gamma}{2}\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)}

(48)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg[\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{1}}{2}\right)t}-e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{1}}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)}\right)\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{2}}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)}-1\right)+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{2}}{2}\right)t}-e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{2}}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)}\right)\left(e^{\left(\frac{\Gamma-\Delta_{1}}{2}\right)\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)}-1\right)\bigg].

In the terms proportional to
\psi^{A2}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)
, we have step functions in the form

\Theta(r_{1}-r_{2})\Theta(t-r_{1}/c)
, altering the integration from

\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dr_{1}

to

\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{r_{2}}^{ct}dr_{1}
.

We now have to compute, with the help of Eqs.(
47
) and (
48
),

\displaystyle p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\varrho\Gamma_{b}}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{ct}dr_{1}\big|\psi^{A1}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\pi\varrho\Gamma_{b}}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{r_{2}}^{ct}dr_{1}\big|\psi^{A2}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\pi\varrho\Gamma_{b}}{(2\pi\varrho c)^{2}}\int_{0}^{ct}dr_{2}\int_{r_{2}}^{ct}dr_{1}2\psi^{A1}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c)\psi^{A2}(r_{1}-r_{2},t-r_{2}/c),

(49)

where the Heaviside step functions have been already considered in the integration limits.

Because the analytical expressions are too cumbersome, in Fig.(
2
) we plot

p_{a\rightarrow b}(\infty)

as a function of
\Delta_{2}
(in log-linear scale), for various
\Delta_{1}
(blue curves).
We consider both
\Gamma_{b}=\Gamma_{a}
and
\Gamma_{b}\neq\Gamma_{a}
scenarios.

For the sake of a better understanding, we compare our exact two-photon wavepacket model with the approximated model where two cascaded (consecutive, uncorrelated, distinguishable) photons drive the atom (orange curves).
In this cascaded model, we have that the probability transition is given by
ganascini

p^{(C)}_{a\rightarrow b}(\infty)=p_{1}+(1-p_{1})p_{2},

(50)

with

p_{k}=r_{\Gamma}/(1+\Delta_{k}/\Gamma)
,
and
r_{\Gamma}\equiv 4\Gamma_{a}\Gamma_{b}/\Gamma^{2}
.
Eq.(
50
) has a simple interpretation: either the first photon drives the transition, with probability
p_{1}
, or it leaves the atom unaltered, with probability
1-p_{1}
, and the second photon drives the transition, with probability
p_{2}
.
Note that

p^{(C)}_{a\rightarrow b}(\infty)=1
can only be obtained if
r_{\Gamma}=1
, which means that equal decay rates,
\Gamma_{a}=\Gamma_{b}
, are a necessary condition for a full purification to be achieved with two photons.

|a\rangle

IV
Conclusions

We have developed a real-space representation of a two-photon wavepacket driving a single three-level atom in
\Lambda
configuration.
We have solved the non-perturbative dynamics, as modeled by the broadband JC model.
We have found analytical expressions both for the excited-state amplitudes (see Eq.(
36
)) and the ground state amplitudes (see Eq.(
37
)).
Because we were interested in calculating the probability
p_{a\rightarrow b}(t)
of transition from
|a\rangle
to
|b\rangle
, we have focused on
\phi^{BA}
, as well as on
\psi^{A}
.
Nevertheless, our method is directly applicable to compute all the other amplitudes as defined within the two-excitation subspace.
We have also worked out an analytically solvable example, consisting of an initial wavepacket of two photons of exponential envelope shape, resonant with the atom
|a\rangle
to
|e\rangle
transition, and analyzed how the probability transition behaves for diverse linewidths of the photons.
We have found that two-photon nonlinearities (taking place when both photons have linewidths comparable to the atomic decay rates) can slightly suppress the transition, due to the non-negligible effect of stimulated emission.

In future studies, we intend to explore further applications of our general solutions.
For instance, we would like to find how a two-photon wavepacket affects quantum cloning processes, such as that from Ref.
qsr
.
Also, we plan to better understand the thermodynamics of this processes.
How to define work in this case, and how does the work relate with the probability transition from
|a\rangle
to
|b\rangle
?
These would mean a generalization of the quantum dissipative adaptation as proposed in Ref.
qda
.

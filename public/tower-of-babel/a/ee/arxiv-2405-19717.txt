Rainbow cycles through specified vertices
Henry Liu

Abstract
An edge-coloured cycle is rainbow if the edges have distinct colours. Let G G be a graph such that any k k vertices lie in a cycle of G G . The k k -rainbow cycle index of G G , denoted by c ​ r ​ x k ​ ( G ) crx_{k}(G) , is the minimum number of colours required to colour the edges of G G such that, for every set S S of k k vertices in G G , there exists a rainbow cycle in G G containing S S . In this paper, we will first prove some results about the parameter c ​ r ​ x k ​ ( G ) crx_{k}(G) for general graphs G G . One of the results is a classification of all graphs G G such that c ​ r ​ x k ​ ( G ) = e ⁡ ( G ) crx_{k}(G)=e(G) , for k = 1 , 2 k=1,2 . We will also determine c ​ r ​ x k ​ ( G ) crx_{k}(G) for some specific graphs G G , including wheels, complete graphs, complete bipartite and multipartite graphs, and discrete cubes. Introduction

with one end-vertex in

. Let

be the bipartite subgraph of

with classes
X
and

, and edge set

. For

, and we may also call

or

, a graph

is

[

The topic of colourings of graphs has a long history which dates back to the famous
Four-colour conjecture
from the 1850s. For edge-colourings, a landmark result is due to Vizing
[
33
]
in 1964 which states that
\chi^{\prime}(G)\in\{\Delta(G),\Delta(G)+1\}
for any graph

, where
\chi^{\prime}(G)
and

are the
edge-chromatic number
(or

) and

of

. A significant direction in the topic, introduced independently in the late 1970s by Vizing
[
34
]
; and by Erdős, Rubin and Taylor
[
18
]
, is

. A famous open problem is the

, which states that
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\chi^{\prime}_{\ell}(G)
for any graph

, where
\chi^{\prime}_{\ell}(G)
is the
list edge-chromatic number
of

. This conjecture first appeared in print in a paper of Bollobás and Harris
[
9
]
in 1985. There is a vast amount of literature about colourings of graphs, and a good starting reference is the book by Jensen and Toft
[
23
]
.

Problems in graph theory concerning cycles through specified vertices have been well-studied. A well-known result of Dirac
[
16
]
from 1960, which can be proved by using Menger’s theorem
[
29
]
, states that for

, any

, any
k
specified vertices in a

-connected graph lie in an

cycle. When considering the degrees of a graph, another famous result of Dirac
[
15
]
from 1952 states that, any graph of order

with minimum degree at least

contains a Hamilton cycle. This theorem has been generalised in many directions. One generalisation is by Bollobás and Brightwell
[

]

and

are such that

vertices of degree at least

of the vertices.

An edge-coloured graph is

if all the edges have distinct colours. Another popular research topic considers the presence of rainbow coloured subgraphs in edge-coloured graphs. One direction is
anti-Ramsey numbers
, which was initiated by Erdős, Simonovits and Sós
[

in 1973. See
[

,

]
for results that involve rainbow cycles. A more recent direction is

, which was proposed by Keevash et al.
[

in 2007. Many problems about rainbow cycles in edge-coloured graphs have also been considered. See for example,
[

,
3
,

,

,
21
,

.

In this paper, we consider the following problem.
For
k\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
denote the family of all graphs
G
with the property that any
k
vertices of
G
lie in a cycle. Consider an edge-colouring of
G\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
such that, any
k
vertices lie in a rainbow cycle. What is the minimum number of colours in such an edge-colouring?
Any edge-colouring satisfying the condition of the problem is called a
k
-rainbow cycle colouring
, and a
1
-rainbow cycle colouring is simply called a

. For

, the minimum number of colours in a

-rainbow cycle colouring of

is denoted by

-rainbow cycle index
of

. Thus,

is well-defined if and only if

. The parameter
crx_{k}(G)
can be considered as a type of edge-chromatic number. Moreover, the problem of the study of

can be classed under both of the “cycles through specified vertices” and “rainbow cycles in graphs” themes.

There are conceivably some real-world applications. For example, suppose that a certain country is served by many different airlines, where some pairs of cities are connected directly by exactly one airline. A travelling salesman wants to attend business meetings in
k
designated cities in no particular order, visiting each city once, and returning to his originating city. Between two consecutive designated cities, he can travel via other cities, and in the entire trip, he never visits any city more than once. What is the minimum number of airlines that the country needs so that, for any choice of
k
designated cities, the travelling salesman can always complete his trip, using a different airline between any two consecutive cities that he visits? This minimum is precisely
crx_{k}(G)
, where
G
is the graph whose vertex set consists of all the cities, and two vertices of
G
are adjacent if and only if the corresponding two cities are connected directly by an airline.

We note that Chartrand, Okamoto and Zhang
[
13
]
considered the

-rainbow index
, which is the analogous parameter with “tree” in place of “cycle”. For a connected graph

are connected by a rainbow tree. Trivially, we also let

for every graph

(not necessarily connected). We remark that the

-rainbow cycle index

has previously been studied by Li et al.
[
28
]
, when they proved some computational complexity results. The problem of the study of the parameter
crx_{k}(G)
was in fact originally introduced by the present author to the authors of
[
28
]
.

The family

contains some important subfamilies of graphs, including all Hamiltonian and pancyclic graphs on at least

-connected. In Proposition

Some initial observations can be made. Clearly, we have

This paper is organised as follows. In Section

2
General graphs

In this section, we shall prove some results about the parameter
crx_{k}(G)
for general graphs
G
. We begin by recalling some well-known concepts about
2
-connected graphs.

A
block
of a graph is a subgraph which is either an isolated vertex, or a bridge (i.e., a cut-edge), or a maximal
2
-connected subgraph (i.e., not strictly contained in another
2
-connected subgraph). Any graph

has a unique

into blocks

for some

, such that the edge sets

form a disjoint union of

, and any two blocks are either vertex-disjoint, or meet at a cut-vertex of

. An

is a block which is incident with at most one cut-vertex. If
G
is connected and is not a block, then
G
has at least two end-blocks, each of which contains exactly one cut-vertex. For further details, see
[
6
]
(Ch. I) and
[
7
]
(Ch. III.2).

A graph

is

k
-connected
if
G
is
k
-connected, and for every edge

, the graph

is not

-connected. The following result describes the structure of minimally
2
-connected graphs.

Theorem 1

(
[
6
]
, Ch. I, Theorem 3.1)
.

Let

be a minimally

-connected graph, and

. Then,

has no isolated vertex and at least two blocks. The blocks of
G-xy
, say
B_{1},\dots,B_{p}
for some

, can be ordered linearly so that
V(B_{i})\cap V(B_{i+1})=\{v_{i}\}
for
1\leq i<p
, where
v_{1},\dots,v_{p-1}
are all the cut-vertices of
G-xy
. The end-blocks of
G-xy
are
B_{1}
and
B_{p}
. Moreover, we have
x\in V(B_{1})\setminus\{v_{1}\}
and
y\in V(B_{p})\setminus\{v_{p-1}\}
. See Figure 1.

Finally, we have the following well-known result about the structure of
2
-connected graphs. See for example
[
14
]

Theorem 2
.

A graph is
2
-connected if and only if it can be obtained from a cycle, followed by successively attaching a non-trivial path by identifying the end-vertices with two distinct vertices of the previous graph.

In Theorem
2

for the

-connected graph.

Now, we present the results. Recall that
crx_{k}(G)
is well-defined if and only if
G\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
. We first give characterisations of the families
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. For

Proposition 3
.

(a)

\mathcal{F}_{1}
is the family of all graphs with the property that, every vertex belongs to a
2
-connected block in the block decomposition.

(b)

\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the family of all
2
-connected graphs.

Proof.

(a) Let
G
be a graph with the property as stated, so that any vertex

belongs to a
2
-connected subgraph. By Dirac’s theorem
[
16
]
,

lies in a cycle. Hence,

.

Conversely, suppose that
G
does not have the stated property. If
G
has an isolated vertex
u
, then

does not lie in a cycle, and

. Otherwise,
G
has a vertex

such that, in the block decomposition of

, every block containing

is a bridge of

. If

lies in a cycle

of

, then the two edges of

does not lie in a cycle of

(b) By Menger’s theorem
[

]
,

is

-connected if and only if any two vertices of

have two internally vertex-disjoint paths connecting them. Equivalently, any two vertices of
G
have a cycle containing them, which is equivalent to
G\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
∎

Next, recall that

Here, we have the following result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph of order

.

(a)

Let

. Then

if and only if

is the cycle

.

(b)

(c)

Let

(equivalently,

if and only if

.

Proof.

(a) If
G=C_{n}
, then clearly we have
crx_{1}(G)=e(G)
.

Conversely, suppose that
G\neq C_{n}
. If
G
is
2
-connected, then by Theorem
2
,

has an ear decomposition with at least one ear. Let
P
be the final ear attached, and
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of

be the end-vertices of

, and

. Let

be a

, and then assign a used colour not in

. We have a rainbow cycle colouring of

lies in a rainbow cycle in

(since

is

-connected), and every vertex of

.

Otherwise,

is not

-connected, so that the block decomposition of

consists of at least two blocks. By Proposition

(a), no block is an isolated vertex of

, and every vertex of

-connected block. We colour the edges of

in such a way that every block is rainbow coloured, and two of the blocks have a colour in common. This is a rainbow cycle colouring with fewer than

(b) Suppose that
G
is not minimally
2
-connected. Then there exists

-connected. We assign a rainbow colouring to

Conversely, suppose that
G
is minimally
2
-connected, and we have a colouring of

with the same colour. By Theorem

, the block decomposition of

, and can be ordered linearly so that

, where

are all the cut-vertices of

, and the end-blocks are
B_{1}
and

. Moreover, we have

, where

.
Let

. Otherwise, we have

-connected. Again by Theorem

, and

are all the cut-vertices of

. Moreover, we have

, where
y_{0}\in V(D_{1})\setminus\{y_{1}\}

. We may assume that

to contain

and

, emanating from

, we must use the edge

and

and

. If

.

(c) If

0\leq q\leq n-1
, then
h_{i}=q
. We may assume that
0=h_{1}<h_{2}<\cdots<h_{p}\leq n-1
. Next, since
C_{n}
is rainbow coloured and there are exactly
n
colours, every edge
vv_{i}
can belong to at most one of
C(I_{1}),\dots,C(I_{p})
. Indeed, for the edge
vv_{0}
, the only possible maximal cycle containing
vv_{0}
, if it exists, is either
C(\{v_{0},\dots,v_{s}\})
for some
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\leq s\leq n-2
, or
C(\{v_{t},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{0}\})
for some
2\leq t\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. A similar argument holds for any other edge
vv_{i}
. It follows that
p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\leq k-1
.

We say that the distinct vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{p}
are
representing vertices
for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
if
u_{i}\in\bar{I}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
. Since
v_{h_{1}},\dots,v_{h_{p}}
are representing vertices for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
, there exists at least one such set of vertices. Let
S
be a set of
k
vertices with
v,u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\in S
. If there is a large rainbow cycle containing
S
, then
S\subset V(C(I_{i}))
for some
1\leq i\leq p
. But this implies
u_{i}\in V(C(I_{i}))
, a contradiction.
Hence, it suffices to show that we can choose a set
S
of
k
vertices, containing
v
and representing vertices for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
, so that no small rainbow cycle can contain
S
.

Case 1.

1\leq p\leq k-2
.

If
p=1
, then recall that
v_{0}\in\bar{I}_{1}
. Let
S
be such that
v,v_{0},v_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},

v_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\in S
. If
2\leq p\leq k-2
, choose a set of representing vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{p}
for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
. We may assume that
u_{1}=v_{0}
and
u_{2}=v_{i}
where
1\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. We further choose
v_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}
(if it is not already among the
u_{j}
), so that
v,u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{p},v_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\in S
. In every case, there is no small cycle containing
S
.

Case 2.

p=k-1
.

We have
p=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, and
n=2k-2
or
n=2k-1
. We claim that, we can choose the representing vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{p}
for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
, so that they do not form a set of consecutive vertices of
V^{\prime}
. For
1\leq i\leq p
, let
u_{i}=v_{h_{i}}
. If
u_{1},\dots,u_{p}
are consecutive vertices of
V^{\prime}
, then we have
u_{i}=v_{i-1}
for all
1\leq i\leq p=k-1
. Note that
u_{p}=v_{k-2}
, and we must have
v_{k-1}\in\bar{I}_{p}
. Otherwise, we have
\bar{I}_{p}=\{v_{k-2}\}
, and since
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
are pairwise non-nested, it is easy to see that we have
\bar{I}_{i}=\{v_{i-1}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
. But this is a contradiction, since
C(I_{1})
and
C(I_{3})
would both contain the edge
vv_{1}
. Thus, we may replace
u_{p}=v_{k-2}
with
u_{p}=v_{k-1}
. We then have
u_{1},\dots,u_{p}
is a set of representing vertices for
\bar{I}_{1},\dots,\bar{I}_{p}
which is not a set of consecutive vertices of
V^{\prime}
, since
k-1\leq n-2
, so that
u_{p}=v_{k-1}
is not adjacent to
v_{0}
in
C_{n}
.

Now let
S=\{v,u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\}
. If
n=2k-2
, then
S
is not contained in a small cycle, since this would require the
u_{j}
to be consecutive vertices of
V^{\prime}
. Let
n=2k-1
and suppose that there is a small cycle containing
S
. We may assume that
u_{1}=v_{0}
,
u_{p}=v_{k-1}
, and
u_{2},\dots,u_{p-1}\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k-2}\}
, so that the only small cycle containing
S
is
C=C(\{v_{0},\dots,v_{k-1}\})
. The edges
vv_{0}
and
vv_{k-1}
are in
C
, and we may assume that
vv_{0}
is contained in a maximal cycle
C^{*}
, so that
C^{*}=C(\{v_{\ell},\dots,v_{n-1},v_{0}\})
for some
2\leq\ell\leq k-1
(since if
C^{*}
contains
S
, then again we obtain a contradiction). It follows that the colour of
vv_{0}
is the same as one of
v_{0}v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell-1}v_{\ell}
, and thus
C
is not a rainbow cycle.

We conclude that we can always find a set
S
of
k
vertices so that no rainbow cycle contains
S
, which is the required contradiction. Hence
crx_{k}(W_{n})\geq n+1
, as required.
∎

Our next task is to determine
crx_{k}(G)
when
G=K_{n}
is a complete graph. We have the following result.

Theorem 9
.

(a)

crx_{1}(K_{n})=crx_{2}(K_{n})=3
for
n\geq 3
.

(b)

For
k\geq 3
, there exists
N=N(k)
such that for
n\geq N
, we have
crx_{k}(K_{n})=2k-1
.

Proof.

(a) It suffices to show that
crx_{2}(K_{n})\leq 3
for every
n\geq 3
. We use induction on
n
to show that there is a
2
-rainbow cycle colouring of
K_{n}
, using three colours. For
n=3
, we take the rainbow coloured
K_{3}
. Let
n\geq 4
, and
u
be a vertex of
G=K_{n}
. By induction, there exists a
2
-rainbow cycle colouring
\phi^{\prime}
for
G-u\cong K_{n-1}
, using three colours. Define a colouring
\phi
for
G
, using three colours, as follows. If
n-1
is odd, take
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in V(G-u)
such that
\phi^{\prime}(x_{1}x_{2})
,
\phi^{\prime}(x_{2}x_{3})
and
\phi^{\prime}(x_{3}x_{1})
are distinct, and let
\phi(ux_{i})=\phi^{\prime}(x_{i+1}x_{i+2})
for
i=1,2,3
(with indices taken modulo
3
). Then (whether
n-1
is odd or even), take a perfect matching
M
on the remaining vertices of
G-u
, and for
vw\in M
, let
\phi(uv)
and
\phi(uw)
be the two colours different from
\phi^{\prime}(vw)
. Now, any two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
lie in a rainbow triangle under
\phi
, where we use induction if
x,y\in V(G-u)
. Hence,
\phi
is a
2
-rainbow cycle colouring of
G
, and we are done by induction.

(b) Recall that for integers
r,\ell\geq 1
, the
Ramsey number

R_{r}(\ell)
is the minimum integer
t
such that, whenever we have an edge-colouring of
K_{t}
with at most
r
colours, there exists a monochromatic copy of
K_{\ell}
. It is well-known that all Ramsey numbers
R_{r}(\ell)
exist. If
n\geq R_{2k-2}(k)
and we have a
(2k-2)
-colouring of
K_{n}
, then there exists a monochromatic copy of
K_{k}
, say with vertex set
S
. Any cycle of length at most
2k-2
containing
S
must contain at least two edges with end-vertices in
S
, and so cannot be rainbow. Hence,
crx_{k}(K_{n})\geq 2k-1
.

Now, we prove that
crx_{k}(K_{n})\leq 2k-1
for
n
sufficiently large. Consider a random
(2k-1)
-colouring of
K_{n}
. For
S\subset V(K_{n})
with
|S|=k
, let
E_{S}
be the event that there is no rainbow cycle containing
S
. We estimate the probability
\mathbb{P}(E_{S})
. Let
U_{1},\dots,U_{t}\subset V(K_{n})\setminus S
be disjoint sets with
t=\lfloor\frac{n-k}{k-1}\rfloor
, and
|U_{i}|=k-1
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Let
H
be the complete subgraph of
K_{n}
induced by
S
. Let
\Phi
be the set of all
(2k-1)
-colourings of
H
with no rainbow cycle
C_{k}
containing
S
. Let
H
be coloured by
\phi\in\Phi
and
1\leq i\leq t
. There is at least one way to colour the
k(k-1)
edges of
E(S,U_{i})
so that we have a rainbow cycle containing
S
, with vertices in
S\cup U_{i}
. Indeed, if
S=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
and
U_{i}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}\}
, then we can assign
2k-1
distinct colours to the cycle
C=v_{1}u_{1}\cdots v_{k-1}u_{k-1}v_{k}v_{1}
, since
v_{1}v_{k}
is the only edge of
H
in
C
. Hence, there are some
\alpha_{\phi}<(2k-1)^{k(k-1)}
colourings of
E(S,U_{i})
so that there is no rainbow cycle containing
S
, with vertices in
S\cup B_{i}
. Let
\alpha=\max\{\alpha_{\phi}:\phi\in\Phi\}<(2k-1)^{k(k-1)}
. Since there are at most
k(k-2)+{n-k\choose 2}
edges not in
E(H)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}E(S,U_{i})
, we have

\mathbb{P}(E_{S})\leq\frac{|\Phi|\alpha^{t}(2k-1)^{k(k-2)+{n-k\choose 2}}}{(2k-1)^{n\choose 2}}\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}\alpha^{n/(k-1)}}{(2k-1)^{kn}}=\gamma_{k}^{\phantom{n}\!}\delta_{k}^{n}=o(n^{-k}),

where
\gamma_{k}>0
and
0<\delta_{k}<1
are constants depending only on
k
.

By the union bound,

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcup_{S\subset V(K_{n}),\,|S|=k}E_{S}\bigg)\leq\sum_{S\subset V(K_{n}),\,|S|=k}\mathbb{P}(E_{S})\leq{n\choose k}o(n^{-k})<1,

for
n
sufficiently large. Hence, there exists a
k
-rainbow cycle colouring of
K_{n}
, using
2k-1
colours, and
crx_{k}(K_{n})\leq 2k-1
.
∎

Next we consider

Theorem 10

(a)

crx_{1}(K_{m,n})=4
for

.

(b)

(i)

crx_{2}(K_{2,n})=2n
for

.

(ii)

. Thus,

(iii)

(c)

Let

.

(i)

\lceil\frac{n}{k-1}\rceil\leq crx_{k}(K_{k,n})\leq kn

.

(ii)

(iii)

Throughout this proof, let

(a) Clearly

. Now, define a

-colouring

on

where
\phi(u_{1}v_{1})=1

\phi(u_{1}w)=2
for

,

is in a rainbow cycle of length

which uses

. Hence,

.

(b)(i) Since

is minimally

-connected for

(b)(ii) Note that

. First, suppose that we have a colouring of

, there exist

, and we cannot have

and

.

Now, we define an

-colouring
\phi

as follows. Let

\phi(u_{1}v_{2})=\phi(u_{2}v_{3})=\phi(u_{3}v_{1})=r

, where

. For

2
-rainbow cycle colouring. Firstly, it is easy to verify that any two vertices of

(c)(ii) Let

\phi

(4k-1)
-colouring of

, using the colours

containing

, then in

We claim that

Before we prove Theorem

of distinct vertices in

such that the end-vertices of

are

and

, for

. Clearly, a

-linked graph is also

-connected, but the converse is not true. Jung
[

]
, and Larman and Mani
[

]
, showed that there exists a function

such that every

-linked, and it was shown in
[

]
that

. Bollobás and Thomason
[

]
then made a significant improvement, when they proved that the result holds for

. Thomas and Wollan
[

]
further improved this to

.

Theorem 15

(
[
32
]
, Corollary 1.3)
.

If
G
is a
10k
-connected graph, then

is k-linked.

Let
S=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
be an ordered
k
-tuple of vertices in a graph

S=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in V(G)^{k}
. An

-subdivided closed walk
is a sequence

such that,

is a

path for

, and no two

and

have an internal vertex in common. If

, then

is trivial, and if
v_{i}\neq v_{i+1}
, then

has length at least

. The indices of the vertices

and the paths

are taken modulo

throughout. By using the above result, we have the following lemma, which says that for

is sufficiently large, so that the vertex connectivity of

is large, then

contains an

Now for

(v_{i},v_{i+1})

and

for

∎

, which gives the existence of a Hadamard matrix for every

H_{1}=(1),\textup{ and }H_{2^{t}}=\begin{pmatrix}H_{2^{t-1}}&H_{2^{t-1}}\\
H_{2^{t-1}}&-H_{2^{t-1}}\end{pmatrix}\textup{ for }t\geq 1.

Remark.
Many other problems concerning the existence of rainbow cycles in edge-coloured discrete binary cubes have also been considered. A result of Faudree et al.
[
20
]
states that, for
n\geq 4
,
n\neq 5
, there exists an edge-colouring of
Q_{n}
with
n
colours such that, every copy of
C_{4}
is rainbow. Since such an edge-colouring of
Q_{n}
must be proper,
n
colours are also necessary. This result was extended by Mubayi and Stading
[
30
]
. They proved that for
\ell\equiv 0
(mod
4
), there exists an edge-colouring of
Q_{n}
with
\Theta_{n}(n^{\ell/4})
colours such that, every copy of
C_{\ell}
is rainbow, and moreover,
\Theta_{n}(n^{\ell/4})
colours are also necessary. These may be considered as edge-chromatic number type results, and they appear to be closely related to our problem of the determination of
crx_{k}(Q_{n})
.

In Theorem
14
, we have only determined the correct order of magnitude of
crx_{k}(Q_{n})
for fixed
k
and large
n
, and we have made no serious attempt to optimise the lower bound on
n
(in terms of
k
), or the terms
c_{k}
and
C_{k}
. We have
c_{k}=\frac{k}{2}
and
C_{k}=2^{22k-1}
, so the gap between
c_{k}
and
C_{k}
, when considered as a function of
k
, is very large. It would be desirable to reduce this large gap. We leave the determination of
crx_{k}(Q_{n})
as an open problem.

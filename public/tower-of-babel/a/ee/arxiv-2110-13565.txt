Static and spherically symmetric solutions in f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity
Wenyi Wang, Hua Chen, Taishi Katsuragawa

Abstract
f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity is the extension of symmetric teleparallel general relativity (STGR), in which both curvature and torsion vanish, and gravity is attributed to nonmetricity. This work performs theoretical analyses of static and spherically symmetric solutions with an anisotropic fluid for general f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity. We find that the off-diagonal component of the field equation due to a coincident gauge leads to stringent restrictions on the functional form of f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity. In addition, although the exact Schwarzschild solution only exists in STGR, we obtain Schwarzschild-like solutions in nontrivial f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity and study its asymptotic behavior and deviation from the exact one.

introduction

Modern observations have confirmed that the expansion of the current Universe is accelerating,
which could be sourced by some unknown components called dark energy (DE)

;
Aghanim et al. 2020

A possible way to explain DE is to modify pure general relativity (GR),

\Lambda

Along this way, one can generalize GR to

.
On the other hand, choosing the torsion

These are the so-called teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (TEGR)

;
Maluf 2013

and symmetric teleparallel general relativity (STGR)

;

;

;

In STGR theory, both the curvature and the torsion vanish, as the nonmetricity describes the gravity.
In this theory, under the teleparallelism constraint, we can always choose the coincident gauge, which restricts the affine connection to disappear and makes the metric tensor the only basic variable.
In analogy to

Although the latter one is relatively less investigated, it has many similar properties to those in

For instance, similar to

;

;

Recently, there have been several applications of

In analogy to

This work aims to investigate possible functional forms of

This paper is organized as follows.

In Sec. III, we derive the nonmetricity scalar

II
f(Q)
gravity

f(Q)
gravity considers a generic metric-affine spacetime,
in which the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
and connection
\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}
are treated independently, and the nonmetricity of the connection is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \alpha\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}-\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \alpha\nu}g_{\mu\lambda}\,.

(1)

The general form of affine connection can be decomposed into the following three independent components:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}=\left\{{}^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\right\}+K^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}+L^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
\left\{{}^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\right\}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection determined by the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,

\displaystyle\left\{{}^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\right\}\equiv\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\beta}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\beta\mu}-\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\right)\,.

(3)

K^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}
is the contortion written as

\displaystyle K^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}T^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}+T_{(\mu\ \nu)}^{\ \lambda}\,,

(4)

with the torsion tensor
T^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}
defined as the antisymmetric part of the affine connection,

T^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\equiv 2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\ [\mu\nu]}
,
and the disformation
L^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}
is defined by

\displaystyle L^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}Q^{\lambda}_{\ \mu\nu}-Q_{(\mu\ \nu)}^{\ \lambda}\,.

(5)

Then let us introduce the following nonmetricity conjugate

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}Q^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{(\mu\ \nu)}^{\ \ \alpha}+\frac{1}{4}\left(Q^{\alpha}-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}\right)g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ (\mu}Q_{\nu)}\,,

(6)

and its two independent traces,

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}\equiv Q_{\alpha\ \mu}^{\ \mu}\,,\quad\tilde{Q}_{\alpha}\equiv Q^{\mu}_{\ \alpha\mu}\,.

(7)

Finally, the nonmetricity scalar is defined as follows

\displaystyle Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}P^{\alpha\mu\nu}\,.

(8)

Supplemented with Lagrange multipliers, one introduces
f(Q)
gravity given by the following action
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018b
:

\displaystyle S=\int\sqrt{-g}~d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}f(Q)+\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ \beta\mu\nu}R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta\mu\nu}+\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}T^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right]\,,

(9)

where
g
is the determinant of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,

f(Q)
is an arbitrary function of the nonmetricity
Q
,

\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ \beta\mu\nu}
is the Lagrange multipliers,
and
{\cal L}_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian density,

Varying the action (
9
) with respect to the metric gives the field equation

\displaystyle-T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f+f_{Q}\left(P_{\mu\alpha\beta}Q_{\nu}^{\ \alpha\beta}-2Q_{\alpha\beta\mu}P^{\alpha\beta}_{\ \ \ \nu}\right)\,,

(10)

where a subscript
Q
stands for a derivative of
f(Q)
with respect to
Q
,

f_{Q}\equiv\partial_{Q}f(Q)
.
The energy-momentum tensor is defined in the standard way,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}\equiv-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\,.

(11)

Varying Eq. (
9
) with respect to the connection, one obtains

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ \nu\mu\rho}+\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}=\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}+H_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}\,,

(12)

where the hypermomentum tensor density is written as

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\delta\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}}\,.

(13)

By taking into account the antisymmetry property of
\mu
and
\nu
in the Lagrangian multiplier coefficients, Eq. (
12
) can be reduced to

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}_{\ \ \alpha}+H_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=0\,.

(14)

Taking
\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}H_{\alpha}^{\ \mu\nu}=0

(see discussion in Ref.
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018b
),
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{Q}P^{\mu\nu}_{\ \ \alpha}\right)=0\,.

(15)

Without curvature and torsion make the affine connection has the following form
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2018a

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial x^{\alpha}}{\partial\xi^{\lambda}}\right)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\lambda}\,.

(16)

We can make a special coordinate choice, the so-called coincident gauge, so that
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\nu}=0
. Then, the nonmetricity reduces to

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(17)

and thereby largely simplifies the calculation since only the metric is the fundamental variable.

However, the cost is that the action no longer remains diffeomorphism invariant, except for STGR
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2020
.
One can utilize the covariant formulation of
f(Q)
gravity to avoid the problem.
Since the affine connection in Eq. (
16
) is purely inertial, one could utilize the covariant formulation by first determining the affine connection in the absence of gravity
Zhao 2021
.
As shown in this work, however, the off-diagonal component of the field equations in the coincident gauge would put strict constraints on
f(Q)
gravity, thereby providing us with nontrivial functional forms of
f(Q)
.

III
Static and Spherically Symmetric Geometry in A Polar Coordinate

The metric ansatz for a generic static and spherically symmetric spacetime is written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{a(r)}dt^{2}+e^{b(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

(18)

where
d\Omega^{2}\equiv d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}
.
By substituting Eq. (
18
) into Eq. (
8
),
the nonmetricity scalar
Q
is written in terms of
r
,

\displaystyle Q(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2e^{-b}}{r}\left(a^{\prime}+\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(19)

where a prime (
′
) denotes a derivative with respect to the radial coordinate
r
.

Corresponding to the spherically symmetric geometry,
the energy-momentum tensor for an anisotropic fluid with spherical symmetry is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=\left(\rho+p_{t}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{t}g_{\mu\nu}+\left(p_{r}-p_{t}\right)v_{\mu}v_{\nu}\,.

(20)

Here,
u_{\mu}
is the four-velocity, and

v_{\mu}
is the unitary spacelike vector in the radial direction satisfying
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
,
v^{\mu}v_{\mu}=1
, and
u^{\mu}v_{\nu}=0
.

\rho(r)
is the energy density,

p_{r}(r)
is the pressure in the direction of
v_{\mu}
(radial pressure),
and
p_{t}(r)
is the pressure orthogonal to
v_{\mu}
(tangential pressure).

For the anisotropic fluid (
20
), independent components of equations of motion (
10
) are listed as follows:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{f}{2}-f_{Q}\left(Q+\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{e^{-b}}{r}(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})\right)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle p_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f}{2}+f_{Q}\left(Q+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle p_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f}{2}+f_{Q}\left\{\frac{Q}{2}-e^{-b}\left[\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{4}+\frac{1}{2r}\right)(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})\right]\right\}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\cot\theta}{2}Q^{\prime}f_{QQ}\,.

(24)

Substituting the nonmetricity scalar Eq. (
19
) into the equations of motion, we have

\displaystyle 2p_{r}^{\prime}+a^{\prime}\left(\rho+p_{r}\right)=f_{Q}\frac{e^{-b}}{r}\left[\frac{2}{r}\left(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)-a^{\prime}\left(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}\right)+\frac{4}{r^{2}}\left(1-e^{b}\right)-2a^{\prime\prime}\right]=0\,,

(25)

where in the last equality we have assumed an isotropic fluid and used
p_{r}-p_{t}=0
in Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
).
Therefore, the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equation is satisfied in general
f(Q)
gravity for a static and spherically symmetric metric with isotropic fluids.

Now looking at the off-diagonal component in Eq. (
24
),
one finds that the solutions to
f(Q)
gravity are constrained to the following two cases:

\displaystyle f_{QQ}

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow f(Q)=a_{0}+a_{1}Q\,,

(26)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow Q=Q_{0}\,,

(27)

where

,

, and

are constant.
This result is similar to that in

gravity

i.e.

The first solution in Eq. (
26

a_{0}/a_{1}
corresponding to cosmological constant
\Lambda
.
Nevertheless, we shall confirm whether the Schwarzschild solution exists in linear
f(Q)
gravity.

In vacuum with
\rho=p_{r}=p_{t}=0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}}{a_{1}}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}}{2}+a_{1}e^{-b}\left[\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{2}+\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{4}+\frac{1}{2r}\right)(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})\right]\,.

The first equation indicates that

As a result, the

The second equation implies a cosmological constant term

Since

Using Eqs. (
19

28

\displaystyle e^{-b}=1+\frac{c_{1}}{r}-\frac{a_{0}}{6a_{1}}r^{2}\,.

Note that, throughout the paper, we shall use

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\left(1+\frac{c_{1}}{r}-\frac{a_{0}}{6a_{1}}r^{2}\right)dt^{2}+\left(1+\frac{c_{1}}{r}-\frac{a_{0}}{6a_{1}}r^{2}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

One recognizes that it represents the Schwarzschild (anti-)de Sitter solution,

In the next section, we shall further discuss the nontrivial solution that

In addition, the constant nonmetricity scalar

could be interpreted as cosmological constant

IV.1
Vacuum solution

We first focus on the vacuum case with

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\frac{e^{-b}}{r}\left(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\right)\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\frac{f\left(Q_{0}\right)}{2}+f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\left(Q_{0}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)=0\,,\ f(Q_{0})=0\,.

37

39

IV.1.1
Case 1:

Equation (

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+\frac{1}{d^{2}_{3}}\frac{1}{A(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,

\displaystyle A(r)=\frac{2d_{1}}{3d_{3}}r^{2}-\frac{2d_{\mu}}{d_{3}r}.

Assuming
B\sim r^{n}
,
the case
0<n<2
suggests the first term in Eq. (
55
) is dominant, which gives
B(r)\sim-Q_{0}r^{2}/2n
, and it is inconsistent with the assumption.
Moreover, the case
n>2
suggests the second term is dominant,
which also gives the inconsistent result
B\sim c_{9}/r
.
Finally, one finds a consistent form
B(r)\sim B_{2}r^{2}
,
where
B_{0}
is a parameter.
In this case, Eq. (
55
) approximates
B^{\prime}\sim-Q_{0}r/2-B/r
.
Therefore, the coefficient
B_{2}
satisfies
2B_{2}=-Q_{0}/2-B_{2}
, to find
B_{2}=-Q_{2}/6
.
Finally, we find the asymptotic form of
b(r)
at
r\sim\infty
:

\displaystyle e^{-b(r)}\sim-\frac{Q_{0}}{6}r^{2}\,.

(57)

The above analysis of the asymptotic behavior suggests that
the solution
e^{-b(r)}
to Eqs. (
52
) and (
33
)
is similar to the Schwarzschild-like solution in Eq. (
42
).
However,
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\neq 0
,
and
a(r)
is determined by Eq. (
33
).

e^{-b(r)}\sim c_{10}/r
at
r\sim 0
,
and then, Eq. (
33
) gives
a^{\prime}\sim-Q_{0}r^{2}/2c_{10}-1/r
.
Because the second term is dominant,
the asymptotic form of
a(r)
at
r\sim 0
is

e^{a(r)}\sim c_{11}/r
.
In the same manner,
e^{-b(r)}\sim-\frac{Q_{0}}{6}r^{2}
at
r\sim\infty
,
and Eq. (
33
) approximates
a^{\prime}\sim 2/r
.
Thus, the asymptotic form of
a(r)
at
r\sim\infty
is

e^{a(r)}\sim c_{12}r^{2}
.

IV.1.3
Case 3:
e^{a}=1-\frac{2M}{r}

In previous case studies, we have confirmed the Schwarzschild-like solution and its asymptotic behavior.
Presupposing that
e^{a}=1-\frac{2M}{r}
,
we further discuss the deviation from the exact Schwarzschild metric,
where the deviation is written only by the
rr
component of metric
e^{b(r)}
.
Under the above assumption, Eq. (
33
) solves
b
as

\displaystyle e^{b}=-\frac{2}{Q_{0}r\left(r-2M\right)}\,,

(58)

where we require
Q_{0}<0
, and the metric reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{-2}{Q_{0}r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(59)

Comparing the above with the Schwarzschild metric, one finds an extra prefactor in the
rr
component.
Taking the limit
r\gg 2M
,
we find the leading term in the
rr
component is proportional to
1/r^{2}
,
which shows the absence of a conventional Newtonian limit.

IV.2
Nonvacuum solutions

We have seen that the vacuum solution for
Q=Q_{0}
is closely related to the solutions of Eq. (
40
),
which provide us with the condition for the nontrivial functional forms of
f(Q)
.
In this subsection, however, we shall focus on the nonvacuum case by imposing conditions on geometric quantities.
In particular, we assume that
f_{Q}(Q_{0})\neq 0
to look for particular solutions of interest.

IV.2.1
Case 1:
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}=0

The solutions for
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}=0
are the same as Eqs. (
42
) and (
43
), while the equations of motion are constrained to

\displaystyle-\rho

\displaystyle=p_{r}=p_{t}+f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,,

(60)

\displaystyle p_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f\left(Q_{0}\right)}{2}+Q_{0}f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\,.

(61)

Note that the energy density and radial pressure obey the inverse-square law with respect to the radius if
f_{Q}(Q_{0})\neq 0
, although the tangential pressure is constant. And since
p_{t}
is a constant in terms of
Q_{0}
, we rewrite it as
p_{t0}
and obtain a particular solution to
f(Q)
:

\displaystyle f(Q)=c_{13}Q_{0}\sqrt{\frac{Q}{Q_{0}}}-2p_{t0}\,.

(62)

IV.2.2
Case 2:
a^{\prime}=0

When
a
is a constant, Eq. (
33
) solves
b(r)
as

\displaystyle e^{-b}=-\frac{Q_{0}}{2}r^{2}\,.

(63)

Then the metric reads

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}-\frac{2}{Q_{0}r^{2}}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

(64)

where we have absorbed
a
into a time coordinate.
The equations of motion are

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=-p_{r}-Q_{0}f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\,,

(65)

\displaystyle p_{r}

\displaystyle=p_{t}+f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,,

(66)

\displaystyle p_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f\left(Q_{0}\right)}{2}+Q_{0}f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\,.

(67)

The values of the pressures are the same as in case 1, so is the particular solution to
f(Q)
.

IV.2.3
Case 3:
b=0

For
b=0
, Eq. (
33
) gives

\displaystyle e^{a}=\frac{c_{14}}{r}\exp\left(-\frac{Q_{0}}{4}r^{2}\right)\,,

(68)

with the metric being

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\frac{c_{14}}{r}\exp\left(-\frac{Q_{0}}{4}r^{2}\right)dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(69)

Then the equations of motion read

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{f\left(Q_{0}\right)}{2}-\frac{Q_{0}f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)}{2}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle p_{r}

\displaystyle=-\rho+f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\left(\frac{Q_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\,,

(71)

\displaystyle p_{t}

\displaystyle=-\rho-f_{Q}\left(Q_{0}\right)\left(\frac{Q_{0}}{4}+\frac{Q_{0}^{2}r^{2}}{16}+\frac{1}{4r^{2}}\right)\,.

(72)

Since
\rho
is a constant in terms of
Q_{0}
, we rewrite it as
\rho_{0}
. Then, a particular solution to
f(Q)
is

\displaystyle f(Q)=c_{15}Q+2\rho_{0}\,,

(73)

which recovers STGR with cosmological constant
\Lambda=\rho_{0}/c_{15}
.

V
Conclusions and Discussion

Different geometric bases allow us to have different descriptions of gravity.
Of particular concern in recent years is the STGR, in which gravity is attributed to the nonmetricity tensor.
As an extension of STGR,
f(Q)
gravity is an intriguing approach in the study of modified gravity.
In this work, we have analyzed the static and spherically symmetric solutions in general
f(Q)
gravity
and have shown the two possible solutions due to the nonzero off-diagonal components,
that is, the linear
f(Q)
function and constant nonmetricity scalar
Q=Q_{0}
.
We have confirmed that the Schwarzschild (anti-)de Sitter solution only exists in linear
f(Q)
gravity,
which recovers STGR and is thereby equivalent to GR.

With a focus on the second solution
Q=Q_{0}
,
we have found that the vacuum solutions
f(Q_{0})=f_{Q}(Q_{0})=0
as in Eq. (
40
),
which puts stringent constraints on the functional form of
f(Q)
gravity.
On the other hand, those solutions do not constrain the metric components and thereby provide us with considerable freedom to investigate the spacetime structure.
As examples, we have discussed three cases trying to find the exact Schwarzschild solution,
only to obtain the Schwarzschild-like solutions.
Those solutions do not exhibit asymptotic flatness; that is,
the spacetime cannot reduce to the Minkowski one at
r\gg 2M
due to the absence of
1
in the metric.
Therefore, the Schwarzschild-like solutions obtained for the case
Q=Q_{0}
cannot recover the Newtonian inverse-square law, which is strictly constrained by observations.
As for the nonvacuum solution for
Q=Q_{0}
, we have started from the geometric assumptions listed as three examples.
By proper conditions on the
tt
component or
rr
component,
we have found some particular solutions to
f(Q)
with constant energy density or constant tangent pressure.

As an application of our findings, we have discussed the implications for the
f(Q)
cosmology from the theoretical viewpoint of model building.
In the case study for
Q=Q_{0}
,
we have seen that
Q_{0}
plays a role of the cosmological constant in the Schwarzschild-like solutions,
and thus, we can expect the cosmological model of
f(Q)
gravity for DE.
The possible functional form of
f(Q)
is constrained by Eq. (
40
), which suggests
f(Q)=\underset{n}{\sum}a_{n}\left(Q-Q_{0}\right)^{n}
as in Eq. (
41
).
Therewith, specifying the coefficients
a_{n}
, one could construct specific models of
f(Q)
gravity.
The simplest example is the polynomial of
Q
, which was actually proposed and investigated as the cosmological model of
f(Q)
gravity
Beltrán Jiménez et al. 2020
.
Moreover, reading Eq. (
41
) as the Taylor expansion,
one can construct the exponential of
Q
or its combinations, for instance, the trigonometric functions.
Those functional forms are known well in cosmological applications of
f(R)
gravity
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Starobinsky 2007
;
Appleby and Battye 2007
,
and thus, the
f(Q)
functions similar to or the same as the known
f(R)
functions can provide us with intriguing cosmology.

In the end, we comment on the gauge fixing and possible analogy to the known results in
f(T)
gravity.
The curvatureless and torsionless conditions correspond to the coincident gauge so that the affine connection is always zero.
However, the choice of the coincident gauge makes the
f(Q)
theory no longer invariant under the general coordinate transformation.
As in the case of
f(T)
gravity,
the covariant formulation of
f(Q)
theory
Zhao 2021
;
Lin and Zhai 2021
;
D’Ambrosio et al. 2021
can allow us to avoid issues caused by the coincident gauge.
In our present analysis, the existence of the constraint equation (
24
) results in two choices for
f(Q)
gravity:
a linear function or constant nonmetricity scalar.
Concerning the gauge fixing and breakdown of diffeomorphism,
the different coordinate systems may allow
f(Q)
gravity to possess the Schwarzschild solution even in the noncovariant approach to
f(Q)
gravity.
As in the
f(T)
theory, an off-diagonal component of the field equations shows up in the polar coordinate,
which prohibits the Schwarzschild spacetime as a solution,
otherwise, the
f(T)
function should be linear to the torsion scalar
T
.
On the other hand,
f(T)
gravity with higher orders of
T
can possess the Schwarzschild solution in the isotropic coordinate
Ferraro and Fiorini 2011
.
By relying on the analogy between
f(T)
and
f(Q)
theories,
it may be worth studying the spherically symmetric solution of
f(Q)
gravity in the isotropic coordinate.
Applying our present analysis to the covariant
f(Q)
gravity or other coordinate systems,
we may be able to obtain the Schwarzschild solution even in the nontrivial
f(Q)
function.

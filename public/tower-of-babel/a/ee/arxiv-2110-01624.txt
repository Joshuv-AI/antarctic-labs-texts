Positivity bounds in the Standard Model effective field theory beyond tree level
Mikael Chala, Jose Santiago

Abstract
Focusing on four-Higgs interactions, we analyse the robustness of tree-level-derived positivity bounds on Standard Model effective field theory (SMEFT) operators under quantum corrections. Among other results, we demonstrate that: (i) Even in the simplest extensions of the Standard Model, e.g. with one new scalar singlet or with a neutral triplet, some positivity bounds are strictly violated; (ii) the mixing of the dimension-eight operators under renormalisation, which we compute here for the first time, can drive them out of their positivity region; (iii) the running of the dimension-eight interactions triggered by solely dimension-six terms respects the positivity bounds. Our results suggest, on one hand, that departures from positivity within the SMEFT, if ever found in the data, do not necessarily imply the breaking of unitarity or causality, nor the presence of new light degrees of freedom. On the other hand, they lead to strong constraints on the form of certain anomalous dimensions.



I
Introduction

Effective-field theories (EFT) are the right tool to describe particle physics in the presence of significant mass gaps. In particular, the Standard Model EFT (SMEFT), see Ref.
Brivio and Trott 2019
for a review, seems to be the most appropriate theory at energies
100\,\text{GeV}\lesssim E\lesssim\text{TeV}
, given that no new resonances have been found in this regime.

The parameters of the SMEFT Lagrangian have been subject of experimental scrutiny for many years. By now, many directions in the SMEFT, although certainly not all, have been severely constrained; see for example Refs.
de Blas et al. 2017
;
Ellis et al. 2021
;
Ethier et al. 2021
.

More recently, though, there has been a huge progress in narrowing the SMEFT landscape purely from theoretical arguments
Zhang and Zhou 2019
;
Bi et al. 2019
;
Remmen and Rodd 2019
;
Gu et al. 2020
;
Bonnefoy et al. 2021
;
Remmen and Rodd 2020a
;
Zhang and Zhou 2020
;
Gu and Wang 2021
;
Fuks et al. 2021
;
Yamashita et al. 2021
;
Remmen and Rodd 2020b
. These rely on the basic principles of quantum mechanics and relativity, and in particular on the analyticity and unitarity of the
S
-matrix
Adams et al. 2006
. The corresponding bounds appear in the form of constraints on the sign of certain combinations of Wilson coefficients, and they are commonly known as
positivity bounds
. Often, they are complementary to current experimental limits
Remmen and Rodd 2019
.

Positivity bounds have been studied mostly under the assumption that all operators involved arise at the same order in perturbation theory. There is also little knowledge on the evolution of these constraints under renormalisation group equations (RGE)
Bellazzini et al. 2021
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
. In this paper, we examine these aspects more closely, focusing on the dimension-eight operators with four Higgs fields. A nice property of these operators is that, contrary to, for example, other quartic gauge boson couplings which have been matter of study within the context of positivity, they can arise at tree level in ultraviolet (UV) completions of the SM
Craig et al. 2020
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
II
, we review briefly the derivation of positivity bounds at tree level, while we dedicate Section
III
to analysing important differences that arise at one loop. We substantiate this discussion with explicit examples of matching of UV models onto the dimension-eight SMEFT in Section
IV
, as well as with the computation of the RGEs of the four-Higgs interactions in Section
V
. We conclude in Section
VI
. Appendix
A
is dedicated to highlighting some technical details of the calculations.

The usual derivation of bounds on the dimension-eight coefficient works as follows. First, we
assume
that in the UV the forward scattering amplitude
\mathcal{A}(s)=\mathcal{A}(s,t=0)

\mathcal{I}=\oint\mathcal{A}(s)/s^{3}

Martin 1963

Altogether, we obtain that the residue of
\mathcal{A}(s)/s^{3}
at the origin is positive, or in other words:

\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{A}(s)}{ds^{2}}\bigg\rvert_{s=0}>0\,.

(4)

This residue can be computed in the EFT. Using Eq. (
3

\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{2}

\mathcal{A}(s)=-2\lambda+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,,

(5)

which gives

c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}>0\,.

(6)

The processes
\phi_{1}\phi_{3}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{3}
and
\phi_{1}\phi_{1}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{1}

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}>0\,,

(7)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}>0\,,

(8)

respectively;
see Ref.
Remmen and Rodd 2019
. Compatible bounds were also obtained in Ref.
Bi et al. 2019

III
Positivity bounds at one loop

In order to analyse the fate of the above results when moving to one loop, we must first notice that the Wilson coefficients of the operators, at the EFT cutoff scale
\Lambda
, admit themselves a perturbative expansion. To make it clear, we introduce a weak coupling
g
and write:

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}

\displaystyle=g\,c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)\,\text{tree}}+g^{2}\,c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)\,\text{loop}}+\cdots\,,

(9)

and similarly for
c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(i)}
and
\lambda
. Thus, the forward amplitude for
\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
scattering to order
\mathcal{O}(g^{2})
in a neighbourhood of
s=0
reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)

\displaystyle\sim-2g\lambda^{\text{tree}}+g^{2}[-2\lambda^{\text{loop}}+\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}(\lambda^{\text{tree}})^{2}\log{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{s}}]

\displaystyle+(g\,c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{tree}}+g^{2}c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{loop}}-\frac{\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}}{2}\log{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{s}})\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,,

(10)

up to finite terms proportional to the tree level Wilson coefficients. The

\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
stands for the
\beta
function of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
, defined by
\mu\,dc_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}/d\mu=\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
. This function receives two contributions, corresponding to the renormalisation triggered by pairs of dimension-six interactions and to that driven by dimension-eight operators via
\lambda
. Schematically:

\frac{1}{g^{2}}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}\sim\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(j)\,\text{tree}}+\gamma_{i}\,\lambda^{\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}\,.

(11)

Several interesting conclusions can be derived from considering Eq. (
III
) in different limits.

To start with, let us assume that none of the effective interactions is generated at tree level, and
\lambda^{\text{tree}}=0
as well. Then,
\mathcal{A}(s)
is simply:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)\sim c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{loop}}\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,.

(12)

Upon making the same reasoning as in Section
II
leading to Eq. (
4
), we obtain
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2),\,\text{loop}}>0
.

Let us now turn our attention to the case in which all effective operators but
\mathcal{O}_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
can arise at tree level, and still
\lambda^{\text{tree}}=0
. The amplitude
\mathcal{A}(s)
near
s=0
is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)\sim g^{2}(c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{loop}}-\frac{\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}}{2}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(j)\,\text{tree}}\log{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{s}})\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,.

(13)

In this case, the branch cut all the way to
s=0
originated by the logarithm prevents using the argument outlined in Section
II
. To circumvent this obstacle, one can include a small mass
m
for the Higgs, thus generating an analytic region around
s=0
. This amounts to deforming the logarithm
\log{\Lambda^{2}/s}\to\log{[\Lambda^{2}/(s+m^{2})]}
. We can subsequently study the limit
m^{2}\to 0
upon expanding the logarithm in powers of
s/m^{2}
; see Refs.
Adams et al. 2006
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
. In such limit, the dominant contribution is:

\mathcal{A}(s)\sim-g^{2}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}}{2}\log{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(j)\,\text{tree}}\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(s^{3})\,.

(14)

A first implication of this result is that
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{loop}}
can have either sign without affecting the positivity of the forward amplitude. Therefore, the bound obtained in Section
II
,
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}>0
, does not necessarily hold in models in which this coefficient arises only in loops, provided that other operators are generated at tree level.

Further, requiring the second derivative of
\mathcal{A}(s)
to be positive at the origin implies very severe constraints on the running of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
triggered by pairs of dimension-six interactions, namely:

\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(j)\,\text{tree}}<0\,.

(15)

Note that, because arbitrary values of the dimension-six Wilson coefficients are compatible with the assumption
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{tree}}=0

Chala et al. 2021
, and given that
\lambda
can be always made zero by just tuning the renormalisable Lagrangian, the bound above is completely general. Moreover, using the exact same reasoning one concludes that this inequality is valid even when including fermionic dimension-six operators, the relevant of which are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H\psi_{R}}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}H)(\overline{\psi_{R}}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{R})\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H\psi_{L}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}H)(\overline{\psi_{L}}\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{L}),

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H\psi_{L}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}i\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}^{I}H)(\overline{\psi_{L}}\gamma^{\mu}\sigma_{I}\psi_{L}),

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{Hud}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{H}iD_{\mu}H)(u\gamma^{\mu}d)+\text{h.c.}\,,

(19)

with
\psi_{R}=e,u,d
and
\psi_{L}=l,q
.

Conversely, the renormalisation of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
driven by
\lambda
can take it out of its positivity region. To show why, let us now consider the limit of negligible dimension-six terms (this is only for simplicity of the exposition) and also
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{tree}}=0
. Upon deforming again the logarithm, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s)\sim\frac{g^{2}}{2}[\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}}(\lambda^{\text{tree}})^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{m^{4}}-\gamma_{i}\lambda^{\text{tree}}c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}\log{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}}]\frac{s^{2}}{\Lambda^{4}}\,.

(20)

In the limit
m^{2}\to 0
, the first term dominates and therefore
\gamma_{i}c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(i)\,\text{tree}}
is not necessarily negative for arbitrary values of the Wilson coefficients. This conclusion still holds if dimension-six operators are not ignored, precisely because they do not contribute to
\mathcal{A}(s)
at tree level, and because they fulfill Eq. (
15
).

All these observations hold still in the presence of gauge couplings, although the proof is less straightforward. (For example, the massless gauge bosons induce poles at
s=0
even at tree level.) Likewise, analyses analogous to the one we just did for
\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
but applied to
\phi_{1}\phi_{3}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{3}
and
\phi_{1}\phi_{1}\to\phi_{1}\phi_{1}
reveal that the bounds in Eq. (
7
)–(
8
) could be also violated at the loop level.

In summary, we can conclude that:

•

If effective interactions can arise at tree level, but either
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
or the combination
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
or
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}
vanishes accidentally at this order, then the constraints in Eqs. (
6
), (
7
) or (
8
) can be broken, respectively.
Interestingly, some popular extensions of the SM,
e.g.
the addition of a scalar neutral singlet, fall in this category.

•

If no operator can be generated at tree level, then all bounds in Eqs. (
6
)–(
8
) are satisfied at one loop.

•

The renormalisation of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
by pairs of dimension-six operators maintain those Wilson coefficients within their (tree-level) positivity region.

•

The renormalisation of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
by relevant couplings (including mixing with other dimension-eight operators) can drive these Wilson coefficients out of their positivity region.

IV
One-loop matching of UV models

In the reminder of this paper, we show that our previous arguments, albeit somewhat heuristic, are in fact realised in minimal extensions of the SM. We refer to Appendix
A
for technical details.

First, let us extend the SM with a heavy scalar neutral singlet
\mathcal{S}
of mass
M=\Lambda
, with interaction Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}=\kappa_{S}\mathcal{S}H^{\dagger}H\,.

(21)

This is obviously not the most generic Lagrangian, but it suffices to illustrate our point.

At tree level, we obtain:

c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)\,\text{tree}}=c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{tree}}=0\,,\quad c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)\,\text{tree}}=2\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,.

(22)

At one loop (see Fig.
2
) and at the matching scale
\mu=M
, we get instead:

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)\,\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{39}{144\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)\,\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{39}{144\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)\,\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{187}{720\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,.

(25)

Therefore,

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{39}{144\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{4}}{M^{4}}<0\,,

(26)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{39}{72\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\mathcal{S}}^{4}}{M^{4}}<0\,,

(27)

and then both Eq. (
6
) and Eq. (
7
) are violated within this model.

Let us now consider the SM extended with a scalar real triplet
\Xi
of mass
M
, too. The relevant Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi}=\kappa_{\Xi}H^{\dagger}\Xi^{I}\sigma^{I}H\,.

(28)

Figure 2:
Example diagrams for integrating out a scalar singlet or triplet (left) or scalar quadruplets (right), giving rise to four-Higgs operators. The thick solid lines represent the heavy fields.

When integrating
\Xi
out up to one loop, we obtain:

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=4\frac{\kappa_{\Xi}^{2}}{M^{2}}-\frac{107}{144\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\Xi}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{61}{144\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\Xi}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\kappa_{\Xi}^{2}}{M^{2}}-\frac{271}{720\pi^{2}}\frac{\kappa_{\Xi}^{4}}{M^{4}}\,.

(31)

The tree and loop contributions are manifest. Once again,
c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)}<0
. Note also that, because there is no tree-level contribution to the four-Higgs operators from mixed diagrams involving heavy triplets and singlets, this bound is broken in any UV completion involving only this type of fields.

In all cases, dimension-six four-Higgs operators are also generated. Their explicit tree-level values can be found in Refs.
de Blas et al. 2015
;
de Blas et al. 2018
.

Let us now turn our attention to three scalar extensions of the SM which
do not
generate any four-Higgs operators at tree level (including those of dimension six). These involve adding a heavy doublet with
Y=1/2
(
\varphi
) and adding heavy quadruplets with
Y=1/2
(
\Theta_{1}
) and
\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,Y=3/2
(
\Theta_{3}
), respectively. We obtain:

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\varphi}|^{2}}{24\pi^{2}}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\varphi}|^{2}}{24\pi^{2}}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\varphi}|^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\,;

(34)

as well as

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\Theta_{1}}|^{2}}{9\pi^{2}}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\Theta_{1}}|^{2}}{36\pi^{2}}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{|\lambda_{\Theta_{1}}|^{2}}{18\pi^{2}}\,;

(37)

and

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(38)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\lambda_{\Theta_{3}}|^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=0\,;

(40)

where
\lambda_{\varphi}
,
\lambda_{\Theta_{1}}
and
\lambda_{\Theta_{3}}
are the unique linear couplings between one heavy field and three

In all these cases, as we already anticipated, the conditions in Eqs. (

V
Renormalisation group evolution

Let us now focus on the running of the Wilson coefficients
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
. The contribution triggered by pairs of dimension-six operators, computed in Ref.
Chala et al. 2021
, reads:

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\bigg[-2(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)})^{2}-\frac{11}{8}(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)})^{2}+4c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\underline{+3c_{Hd}^{2}}\,\,\underline{\underline{+c_{He}^{2}}}\,\,\underline{\underline{\underline{+2(c_{Hl}^{(1)})^{2}}}}-2(c_{Hl}^{(3)})^{2}\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{+6(c_{Hq}^{(1)})^{2}}}}}-6(c_{Hq}^{(3)})^{2}+\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{3c_{Hu}^{2}}}}}}-3c_{Hud}^{2}\bigg]\,,

(41)

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\bigg[-2(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)})^{2}-\frac{5}{8}(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)})^{2}-2c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\underline{-3c_{Hd}^{2}}\,\,\underline{\underline{-c_{He}^{2}}}\,\,\underline{\underline{\underline{-2(c_{Hl}^{(1)})^{2}}}}-2(c_{Hl}^{(3)})^{2}\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{-6(c_{Hq}^{(1)})^{2}}}}}-6(c_{Hq}^{(3)})^{2}\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{-3c_{Hu}^{2}}}}}}\bigg]\,,

(42)

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\bigg[-5(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)})^{2}+\frac{7}{8}(c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)})^{2}-2c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(1)}c_{H^{4}D^{2}}^{(2)}+4(c_{Hl}^{(3)})^{2}+12(c_{Hq}^{(3)})^{2}+3c_{Hud}^{2}\bigg]\,.

(43)

(The Wilson coefficients of the fermionic operators are matrices in flavour space, so
c^{2}
must be interpreted as the trace
\text{Tr}[c^{\dagger}c]
.)

It can be trivially seen, for example, that
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}<0
, implying

c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}(\mu)\sim\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}\log{\frac{\mu}{\Lambda}}>0\,,

(44)

given that
\mu/\Lambda<1
within the region of validity of the EFT.

Likewise, we have that
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}<0
as well as
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}<0
, and therefore all bounds in Eqs. (
6
)–(
8
) are respected by dimension-six quantum corrections at all scales.

For illustration, in the equations above we have marked which positive (fermionic) coefficients in
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}
are canceled by
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
, forcing the second inequality even though it could well be that
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}>0
. Note also that
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}
can be positive in a plethora of cases, for example simply if
c_{Hl}^{(3)}
is the only non-vanishing Wilson coefficient. The same holds for
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}
as well as for
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}
. That is, the cancellations are in place precisely in those combinations given by the positivity bounds.

However, the mixing of the three
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
due to renormalisable terms drive the former away from their positivity region. Indeed, upon a thorough computation based on the observations made in Appendix
A
, including the (in this case dominant) contribution from gauge couplings, we obtain that:

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\bigg[(30c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+41c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+15c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{2}^{2}-(16c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+7c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+15c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+16(3c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})\lambda\bigg]\,,

(45)

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\bigg[(28c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+43c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+15c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{2}^{2}+(14c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+33c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+15c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+16(c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+3c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})\lambda\bigg]\,,

(46)

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\bigg[(36c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+29c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+42c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{2}^{2}+(8c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+2c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+9c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})g_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle-16(3c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}+2c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}+5c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)})\lambda\bigg]\,.

(47)

(We do not include fermionic dimension-eight operators, because they do not arise in the models in which we later use these expressions.)
It is clear that
\beta_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}
is not necessarily negative; likewise for the other positivity relations.

As a matter of example, let us assume that
\,\,\,\,\,\,\,c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}(\mu=M)=0
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(2)}(\mu)\sim

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}\bigg[(28g_{2}^{2}+14g_{1}^{2}+16\lambda)c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(1)}(M)

\displaystyle+(15g_{2}^{2}+15g_{1}^{2}+16\lambda)c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(3)}(M)\bigg]\log{\frac{M}{\mu}}\,.

VI
Conclusions

We have argued that tree-level-derived positivity bounds on the Wilson coefficients
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
of four-Higgs dimension-eight operators within the SMEFT do not necessarily hold at one loop.

First, they can be violated at the matching scale.
We have underpinned this statement with explicit calculations. In particular, we have computed the one-loop matching of some scalar extensions of the SM onto the SMEFT to dimension eight, demonstrating the violation of two of the three positivity bounds.
And second, positivity bounds can be broken by their running triggered by renormalisable interactions such as the Higgs quartic or the gauge couplings.

In this respect, it would be interesting to study modified scale-dependent constraints, relying on
s
-dependent integration contours such as the
arcs
discussed in Ref.
Bellazzini et al. 2021
; see also Section 11 of Ref.
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
.
1
1

1

During the completion of this work, Ref.
Baratella et al. 2021
appeared on the arXiv. It shows that running effects from gravity
do
preserve positivity.

Conversely, we have shown that the renormalisation of
c_{H^{4}D^{4}}^{(j)}
driven by dimension-six terms does not break positivity. This implies strong constraints on the form of the corresponding anomalous dimensions.
In turn, this observation could provide new non-renormalisation results. For example, let us consider the renormalisation of a certain Wilson coefficient
c_{8}
, subject to the bound
c_{8}>0
, by dimension-six terms
c_{6}^{(1)}
and
c_{6}^{(2)}
:

\displaystyle\mu\frac{dc_{8}}{d\mu}=\gamma_{11}(c_{6}^{(1)})^{2}+\gamma_{12}c_{6}^{(1)}c_{6}^{(2)}+\gamma_{22}(c_{6}^{(2)})^{2}\,.

(54)

Let us now assume that either
\gamma_{11}
or
\gamma_{22}
(or both) vanishes trivially (
e.g.
because there are no Feynman diagrams, as it is the case in several examples shown in Ref.
Chala et al. 2021
). Then, provided that
c_{6}^{(1,2)}
can have either sign,
\gamma_{12}
must be necessarily zero, or else
c_{8}
could be negative for appropriate values of those terms. It would be worth exploring this avenue for understanding the surprising non-renormalisation of some dimension-eight bosonic operators
Chala et al. 2021
, resulting from the exact cancellation of physical and redundant counterterms.

An efficient mixed finite element method for nonlinear magnetostatics and quasistatics
Herbert Egger 1,2, Felix Engertsberger 1, Bogdan Radu 2

Abstract
We consider systems of nonlinear magnetostatics and quasistatics that typically arise in the modeling and simulation of electric machines. The nonlinear problems, eventually obtained after time discretization, are usually solved by employing a vector potential formulation. In the relevant two-dimensional setting, a discretization can be obtained by H 1 H^{1} -conforming finite elements. We here consider an alternative formulation based on the H H -field which leads to a nonlinear saddlepoint problem. After commenting on the unique solvability, we study the numerical approximation by H ⁡ ( curl ) H(\operatorname{curl}) -conforming finite elements and present the main convergence results. A particular focus is put on the efficient solution of the linearized systems arising in every step of the nonlinear Newton solver. Via hybridization, the linearized saddlepoint systems can be transformed into linear elliptic problems, which can be solved with similar computational complexity as those arising in the vector or scalar potential formulation. In summary, we can thus claim that the mixed finite element approach based on the H H -field can be considered a competitive alternative to the standard vector or scalar potential formulations for the solution of problems in nonlinear magneto-quasistatics.

Introduction

We consider nonlinear systems of magnetostatics and quasistatics which typically arise in the modeling of electric machines. After eventual time discretization, the governing equations in the relevant two-dimensional setting can be phrased as

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(H)-\operatorname{Curl}a

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}H+\sigma a

\displaystyle=j\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2)

and we impose homogeneous boundary conditions
a=0
on
\partial\Omega

for simplicity.
Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the cross-section of the machine,
H
the parallel component of the magnetic field intensity,
a
the axial component of the magnetic vector potential, and
\operatorname{curl}H=\partial_{y}H_{x}-\partial_{x}H_{y}
and
\operatorname{Curl}a=(\partial_{y}a,-\partial_{x}a)
are the two curl operators arising in two space dimensions.
Furthermore,
g^{\prime}(H)=\nabla_{H}g(H)
is the derivative of the magnetic coenergy density
g
which, in general, may depend on the spatial coordinate as well.
Finally,
j
,
\sigma
are the axial component of the current density and a generalized conductivity, with
\sigma=0
in the static case.
Before we proceed, let us briefly discuss some of the standard approaches for the analysis and numerical solution of the problem
Henrotte97
;
meunier08
.

Magnetic vector potential formulation

We can use (
1
) to express
H=f^{\prime}(\operatorname{Curl}a)
, where
f^{\prime}(\cdot)
is the inverse of
g^{\prime}(\cdot)
, i.e., the derivative of the magnetic energy density
silvester91
. In fact,
f(\cdot)
and
g(\cdot)
are closely linked via convex duality
rockafellar70
.
Inserting this into (
2
) leads to

\displaystyle\sigma a+\operatorname{curl}(f^{\prime}(\operatorname{Curl}a))

\displaystyle=j\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

(3)

Any solution to this problem with zero boundary conditions can be characterized by the following weak formulation: Find
u\in H_{0}(\operatorname{Curl};\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle(\sigma a,v)+(f^{\prime}(\operatorname{Curl}a),\operatorname{Curl}v)

\displaystyle=(j,v)\qquad\forall v\in H_{0}(\operatorname{Curl};\Omega).

(4)

Since
\operatorname{Curl}a=(\nabla a)^{\perp}
, one can see that
H_{0}(\operatorname{Curl};\Omega)\simeq H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
are isomorphic.
Under some general assumptions, the existence of a unique solution can then be proven by a nonlinear version of the Lax-Milgram theorem
Zeidler1990
, and standard finite elements can be used to approximate (
4
) numerically; see e.g.
heise94
;
meunier08
.

Total magnetic field formulation

If
\sigma>0
everywhere, one can use (
2
) to express
a=\rho(j-\operatorname{curl}H)
with modified resistivity
\rho=\frac{1}{\sigma}
. This leads to the pure
H
-field formulation

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(H)+\operatorname{Curl}(\rho\operatorname{curl}H)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Curl}(\rho\,j)\qquad\text{in }\Omega.

(5)

The weak form of this problem reads: Find
H\in H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}(H),w)+(\rho\operatorname{curl}H,\operatorname{curl}w)

\displaystyle=(\rho\,j,\operatorname{curl}w)\qquad\forall w\in H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega).

(6)

Note that the space
H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)
consists of vector fields with tangential continuity. The existence of a unique solution to this nonlinear elliptic problem can be established with similar arguments as before.
Furthermore, Nédélec finite elements can be used for the systematic numerical approximation
Bossavit88
;
Nedelec80
.

Reduced scalar potential formulation

In the static case, we have
\sigma=0
and one can satisfy (
2
) explicitly by decomposing
H=H_{s}+\nabla\psi
, with given source field
H_{s}
such that
\operatorname{curl}H_{s}=j
. By testing equation (
2
) with
\nabla z
, one arrives at the following weak formulation: Find
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}(H_{s}+\nabla\psi),\nabla z)

\displaystyle=0\qquad\forall z\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Here
H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
denotes the space of scalar potentials with zero average.
This is again a nonlinear elliptic problem and the existence of a unique solution can be established as before
Engertsberger23
;
Zeidler1990
. Moreover, standard
H^{1}
-conforming finite elements can be used for the efficient and reliable numerical approximation
Albanese90
;
Engertsberger23
.

Outline and main contributions

In this paper, we consider the mixed formulation (
1
)–(
2
) of magneto-quasistatics with
H
in
H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)
and
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, which works for all
\sigma\geq 0
.
The weak form of this system leads to a nonlinear saddlepoint problem, and we briefly discuss its well-posedness.
Mixed finite elements can be used for the systematic discretization; see e.g.
BarbaMariniSavini1993
;
RogierSegre92
;
GilletteBajaj2011
, where corresponding linear magnetostatic problems were considered.
For the solution of the nonlinear problem, we apply a damped Newton method, and we discuss the efficient solution of the linearized saddlepoint problems arising in every Newton through hybridization. By this one can reduce the linearized problems to symmetric positive definite systems which can be solved efficiently with a computational complexity similar to that of the alternative approaches discussed above.
A detailed comparison with the vector potential formulation, concerning accuracy and computational efficiency, will be given below for a typical test problem arising in electric machine simulation.

2
Dual mixed formulation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a simply connected bounded Lipschitz domain. To avoid technicalities, we further assume that
\partial\Omega
is polygonal.
The weak formulation of the system (
1
)–(
2
) with
a=0
on
\partial\Omega
can then be stated as follows.

Problem 1

Find
(H,a)\in H(\operatorname{curl},\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}(H),v)-(a,\operatorname{curl}v)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v\in H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)

\displaystyle(\operatorname{curl}H,q)+(\sigma a,q)

\displaystyle=(j,q)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{2}(\Omega).

With the usual arguments, one can see that any smooth solution of (
1
)–(
2
) with homogeneous boundary conditions also solves Problem
1
.
The following result clarifies the well-posedness of the nonlinear variational problem above.

Lemma 2

Let
g^{\prime}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be uniformly monotone and Lipschitz, i.e.,

(i)
\langle g^{\prime}(y)-g^{\prime}(z),y-z\rangle\geq\alpha|y-z|^{2}
for all
y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(ii)
|g^{\prime}(y)-g^{\prime}(z)|\leq C_{a}|y-z|
for all
y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

with
C_{a},\alpha>0
.
Then for any
j\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the variational Problem
1
admits a unique solution
(H,a)\in H(\operatorname{curl},\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, which can be bounded by

\displaystyle\|H\|_{H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)}+\|a\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|j\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The constant
C
only depends on
\alpha
and
C_{a}
, and on the domain
\Omega
.

The proof of this result can be accomplished using the surjectivity of the vector-to-scalar
\operatorname{curl}
operator, an extension of the Brezzi lemma
Boffi13
, and
the Zarantonello theorem for monotone operators
Zeidler1990
; details are left to the reader.
Let us note that
g
may additionally depend on the spatial coordinate, in which case the above properties are assumed to hold uniformly for all
x\in\Omega
.

3
Finite element discretization

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a geometrically conforming and shape-regular triangulation of the domain
\Omega
.
By
h=\max_{T}h_{T}
we denote the global mesh size, i.e., the maximal diameter of an element in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
We define the finite element spaces

\displaystyle V_{h}=\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H(\operatorname{curl},\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{h}=P_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

where
X(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\{v:v|_{T}\in X(T)\}
is used to denote functions which are members of
X(T)
on every element
T
. Further
\mathcal{N}_{k}(T)=P_{k}(T)^{2}\oplus\vec{x}\times\mathring{P}_{k}(T)
is the Nedelec finite elements,
P_{k}(T)
are the polynomials of degree
\leq k
, and
\mathring{P}_{k}(T)
contains the corresponding homogeneous polynomials; see
Boffi13
;
Nedelec80
for details.

V_{h}(T)=\mathcal{N}_{0}(T)

Q_{h}(T)=P_{0}(T)

V_{h}(T)=\mathcal{N}_{1}(T)

Q_{h}(T)=P_{1}(T)

Figure 1:
Degrees of freedom for
V_{h}
(arrows) and
Q_{h}
(circles) for order
k=0,1
.

For illustration, the degrees of freedom for the spaces of order
k=0
and
k=1
are depicted in Figure
1
.
To facilitate the implementation of the nonlinear term, we further introduce an approximation
(\cdot,\cdot)_{h}
for the
L^{2}
-scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)
, which is obtained by appropriate numerical integration on each element.
We then consider the following discretization of Problem
1
by an inexact Galerkin method.

Problem 3

Find
(H_{h},a_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}(H_{h}),v_{h})_{h}-(a_{h},\operatorname{curl}v_{h})

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}

\displaystyle(\operatorname{curl}H_{h},q_{h})+(\sigma a_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=(j,q_{h})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

With similar reasoning as for the continuous problem and standard arguments for the analysis of finite element methods, one can show the following result.

Theorem 4

Let
\Omega
,
g^{\prime}(\cdot)
and
j
satisfy the assumption of Lemma
2
, and let
V_{h}
,
Q_{h}
be chosen as above.
Further, let the element quadrature rule underlying
(\cdot,\cdot)_{h}
be exact at least for polynomials of degree
\leq 2k+2
and have strictly positive weights.
Then Problem
3
has a unique solution
(H_{h},a_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
.
Moreover

\displaystyle\|H-H_{h}\|_{H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)}+\|a-a_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{k}(\|H\|_{H^{k}(\operatorname{curl};\mathcal{T}_{h})}+\|a\|_{H^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})})

with a constant
C\neq C(h)
independent of the meshsize
h
.

The space
H^{k}(\operatorname{curl};\mathcal{T}_{h})
consists of functions
v\in H^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{2}
with
\operatorname{curl}v\in H_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
We refer to
Engertsberger23
and
heise94
for detailed proofs of corresponding results for the vector potential and the scalar potential formulations. With minor modifications, the main arguments can be generalized to the problem under consideration.

4
Efficient algebraic solution

The numerical solution of Problem
3
requires a nonlinear solver. We here consider iterative methods of the form a damped Newton iteration, which takes the form

\displaystyle(H_{h}^{n+1},a_{h}^{n+1})=(H_{h}^{n},a_{h}^{n})+\tau^{n}(\delta H_{h}^{n},\delta a_{h}^{n}),\qquad n\geq 0.

(8)

The initial iterate
(H_{h}^{0},a_{h}^{0})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
is given.
The updates
(\delta H_{h}^{n},\delta a_{h}^{n})
for the Newton iteration are defined by the following
linearized
system.

Problem 5

Find
(\delta H_{h},\delta a_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime\prime}(H_{h}^{n})\,\delta H_{h}^{n},v_{h})_{h}-(\delta a_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h})

\displaystyle=-(g^{\prime}(H_{h}^{n}),v_{h})_{k}+(\delta a_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h})

(9)

\displaystyle(\operatorname{curl}\delta H_{h}^{n},q_{h})+(\sigma\delta a_{h}^{n},q_{h})

\displaystyle=(j,q_{h})-(\operatorname{curl}H_{h}^{n},q_{h})-(\sigma a_{h}^{n},q_{h})

(10)

for all test functions
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
.

Under the previous assumptions, the existence of a unique solution can be obtained here by standard saddlepoint theory
Boffi13
.
With an appropriate choice of the step size
\tau^{n}
, the convergence of the damped Newton iteration (
8
) can be established; we refer to
Engertsberger23
for a related analysis.

Hybridization

The realization of the damped Newton iteration (
8
) requires the solution of a linear saddlepoint problem (
9
)–(
10
) in every step. This can be achieved efficiently using hybridization
Boffi13
;
meunier08
.
The key idea is to impose the tangential continuity of the functions in
V_{h}
via Lagrange multipliers.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}=\{E_{ij}:i<j\}
denote the set of all edges
E_{ij}=\partial T_{i}\cap\partial T_{j}
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and consider the spaces

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{h}=\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\qquad Q_{h}=P_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\qquad\widehat{Q}_{h}=P_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})

For a piecewise smooth function on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, set
[\ell]_{E}:=\ell|_{E\cap T_{i}}+\ell|_{E\cap T_{j}}
for any face
E\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then Problem
5
is equivalent to the following system.

Problem 6

Find
(\delta H_{h}^{n},\delta a_{h}^{n},\delta\widehat{a}_{h}^{n})\in\widetilde{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\widehat{Q}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle(g^{\prime\prime}(H_{h}^{n})\,\delta H_{h}^{n},v_{h})_{h,T}

\displaystyle-(\delta a_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h})_{T}-(\delta\widehat{a}_{h}^{n},[n\times v_{h}])_{E}

\displaystyle=-(g^{\prime}(H_{h}^{n}),v_{h})_{k,T}+(\delta a_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h})_{T}+(\delta\widehat{a}_{h}^{n},[n\times v_{h}])_{E}

\displaystyle(\operatorname{curl}\delta H_{h}^{n}+\sigma\delta a_{h}^{n},q_{h})_{T}

\displaystyle=(j-\operatorname{curl}H_{h}^{n}-\sigma a_{h}^{n},q_{h})_{T}

\displaystyle([n\times\delta H_{h}^{n}],\hat{q}_{h})_{E}

\displaystyle=0

for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and for all
v_{h}\in\widetilde{V}_{h}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
and
\hat{q}_{h}\in\widehat{Q}_{h}
.

Let us note that the ters
[n\times v_{h}]
represent the jump of the tangential component of the piecewise smooth vector-valued function
v_{h}\in\widetilde{V}_{h}
.
The last equation thus ensures continuity of the tangential component of
\delta H_{h}^{n}
.
Moreover, one can show that the first two components of the solution of Problem
6
coincide with the unique solution of Problem
5
; we refer to
Boffi13
for details.

Algebraic reduction

With the usual choice of a basis, the implementation of the mixed finite element approximation of Problem
6
leads to an algebraic system of the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{M}&-\mathsf{B}^{\top}&-\mathsf{L}^{\top}\\
\mathsf{B}&\mathsf{C}&0\\
\mathsf{L}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\mathsf{h}\\
\delta\mathsf{a}\\
\delta\widehat{\mathsf{a}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{r}\\
\mathsf{w}\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Since the only coupling between elements occurs via the jump terms in Problem
6
, one can see that
\mathsf{M}
,
\mathsf{C}
, and
\mathsf{B}
are block diagonal, with each block associated with a single element.
This allows the elimination of both
\delta\mathsf{h}
and
\delta\mathsf{a}
locally, leading to a Schur complement system for the hybrid variable
\delta\widehat{\mathsf{a}}
alone, viz.

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{L}\quad 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{M}&-\mathsf{B}^{\top}\\
-\mathsf{B}&-\mathsf{C}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{L}^{\top}\\
0\end{pmatrix}\delta\widehat{\mathsf{a}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{L}\quad 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{M}&-\mathsf{B}^{\top}\\
-\mathsf{B}&-\mathsf{C}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-\mathsf{r}\\
\mathsf{w}\end{pmatrix}

(11)

From the previous considerations, one can infer that the system matrix of this problem is sparse, symmetric, and positive definite, and hence (
11
) can be solved efficiently.
Once the solution component
\delta\hat{\mathsf{a}}
at the edges has been computed, the other components
\delta\mathsf{h},\delta\mathsf{a}
can be obtained by local post-processing; see
Boffi13
for details.
Let us emphasize that all the required computations can be done efficiently on the algebraic level.
Via hybridization, the computation of the Newton step in Problem
5
can thus be achieved by the solution of a global symmetric positive definite system and some local algebraic computations.

5
Numerical illustration

To demonstrate the efficiency and accuracy of the proposed hybridized mixed finite element method for nonlinear magnetostatics, let us briefly report on some numerical results for a typical scenario arising in electric machine computation.
For ease of presentation, we only consider the magnetostatic case
\sigma=0
here, which is automatically covered by our results.

Model problem

The geometry, which is taken from
Gangl2023
, comprises
16
permanent magnets (PMs) and
48
slots.
A linear material law
B=\mu_{0}H
, corresponding to
f(B)=\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}|B|^{2}
resp.
g(H)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|H|^{2}
is used for the slots, the air gap between the stator and rotor, the end pockets of the PMs, and the shaft.
For the permanent magnets, we use
B=\mu_{0}(H+M)
which amounts to
f(B)=\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}|B-\mu_{0}M|^{2}
, respectively, and
g(H)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|H+M|^{2}
.
The core material of the stator and rotor is described by an isotropic model
f(B)=\tilde{f}(|B|)
.
For our numerical tests,
\tilde{f}(|B|)
is chosen as a cubic spline approximation of the Brauer model
Brauer75
, which allows us to guarantee all the required properties; see
Pechstein06
for details.
By convex duality, the coenergy functional then takes the form
g(H)=\tilde{g}(|H|)
with
\frac{\tilde{g}^{\prime}(|H|)}{|H|}=\frac{|B|}{\tilde{f}^{\prime}(|B|)}
. The function
\tilde{g}(|H|)
is again approximated by a cubic spline.
Further details of the geometry and material model can be found in
Bogdan2023
.
We consider the fields generated solely by the permanent magnets, and therefore choose
j=0
for the current density.

Details on the discretization

In our numerical tests, we consider the first and second-order approximations of the vector potential formulation and the mixed
H
-field model.
For the implementation of the nonlinear terms, we use numerical quadrature with the Dunavant rules
Dunavant85
of order
p=2
and
p=4
, respectively.
Both these rules satisfy our condition on the positivity of the weights used in our theorems.
For both formulations, Armijo backtracking
Nocedal
is chosen for determining the step size
\tau^{n}
, which allows to prove global convergence of the damped Newton method for the problems under consideration.

Summary

In this paper, we considered a mixed finite element approach for solving the magneto-quasistatic problem.
The approach is viable for the magnetostatic and the magneto-quasistatic case, and a damped Newton method can be used to solve the nonlinear problem.
Using hybridization, the Newton-systems can be reduced to scalar elliptic problems, whose numerical solution has a similar efficiency as that of the magnetic scalar or vector potential formulation.
In contrast to the scalar potential approach, the mixed formulation is applicable also in the quasistatic case, and in contrast to the vector potential formulation, it offers direct access to the magnetic field intensity.
Further information about the magnetic flux could be obtained by post-processing, which also offers access to a-posteriori error estimates; we refer to
Boffi13
for details.

Counting Schur Rings over Cyclic Groups of Semiprime Order
Joseph Keller, Andrew Misseldine, Max Sullivan

Abstract
In this paper, we continue the enumeration of Schur rings over cyclic groups. Cyclic groups of semiprime order p ​ q pq , where p p and q q are distinct primes, are considered. Additionally, cyclic groups of order 4 ​ p 4p are considered. Keywords : Schur ring, cyclic group, association scheme, lattice of subgroups AMS Classification : 20c05, 05c25, 05e30, 05a15 05e16 20k27

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
\mathbb{Q}[G]
denote the rational group algebra. Let
\mathcal{L}(G)
denote the lattice of subgroups of
G

C\subseteq G
, we may associate to it an element of the group algebra, namely
\sum_{g\in C}g\in\mathbb{Q}[G]
. Such an element is called a
simple quantity
. When the context is clear, we will likewise denote the simple quantity associated to
C
as
C
itself. Define

C^{*}:=\{x^{-1}\mid x\in C\}
for all

\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}\}
be a partition of

\mathfrak{S}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}[G]

\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}\}

\mathfrak{S}
is a

1.

C_{1}=\{1\}
;

2.

For each

j

C_{i}^{*}=C_{j}
;

3.

For each
i
and

C_{i}C_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{ijk}C_{k}
, for
\lambda_{ijk}\in\mathbb{N}
.

The sets
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}
are called the
\mathfrak{S}
-classes
(or
primitive sets
of
\mathfrak{S}
). Note that a Schur ring is uniquely determined by its associated partition of

The proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem

(which proofs can be found in Section
3
and Section
4
, respectively) can be summarized as following. By the Fundamental Theorem of Schur Rings over Cyclic Groups (due to Leung and Man
[
2
,
3
]
), all Schur rings over cyclic groups belong to one of four families, which we call the
traditional Schur rings
: namely, the trivial Schur ring, automorphic Schur rings, direct products of Schur rings, and wedge products of Schur rings (see the next section for definitions). Because these four families often overlap, special care is taken to ensure that an exact count is made. The trivial case, as the name suggests, is easy to consider. The families of wedge and direct products are considered recursively. The automorphic Schur rings are in one-to-one correspondence with the subgroups of
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{n})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{p_{i}^{e_{i}}})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{Z}_{(p_{i}-1)p_{i}^{e_{i}-1}}
, where
n=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
is the prime factorization of
n
.

In
[
8
]
, Ziv-Av enumerates all Schur rings over small finite groups up to order 63. In
[
4
]
, the second author enumerates all Schur rings over cyclic groups up to order 400. In both cases, this was accomplished by computer software. In all instances, the two enumerations agree with the formulas found herein.

2
Traditional Schur Rings

In this section, we remind the reader of important counting techniques introduced in
[
4
]
.

For a Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
over
G
and a subgroup
H\leq G
, we say
H
is a an
\mathfrak{S}
-
subgroup
if
H
can be partitioned using the primitive sets of
\mathfrak{S}
. Then
\mathfrak{S}_{H}:=\mathfrak{S}\cap\mathbb{Q}[H]
is a Schur ring over
\mathcal{H}
and is called a
Schur subring
of
\mathfrak{S}
. We say a Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
is
primitive
if the only
\mathfrak{S}
-subgroups are
1
and
G
.

For any subgroup
\mathcal{H}\leq\Aut(G)
, let
G^{\mathcal{H}}
denote the
automorphic Schur ring
associated to
\mathcal{H}
. In the case
\mathcal{H}=1
,
G^{1}=\mathbb{Q}[G]
, the group algebra itself. For simplicity of notation, this Schur ring, called the
discrete Schur ring
, is simply denoted as
G
as the coefficient ring will provide little consequence. In the case that
G
is abelian and
\mathcal{H}=\langle*\rangle
, we denote
G^{\mathcal{H}}
as
G^{\pm}
.

For any
n
, there is exactly one
trivial Schur ring
over
G
, namely
G^{0}:=\Span\{1,G\smallsetminus 1\}
. The only primitive Schur ring over
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
when
n
is composite is this trivial ring. Thus, the trivial family is disjoint from the other three traditional families and contains exactly one ring. On the other hand, all Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
, where
p
is prime, are primitive. As observed in
[
4
,
6
]
, all Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
are automorphic
\left(\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{0}=\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{p})}\right)
and
\Omega(p)=x
, where
x
is the number of divisors of
p-1
.

If
G=H\times K
,
\mathfrak{S}
is a Schur ring over
H
, and
\mathfrak{T}
is a Schur ring over
K
, then
\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{T}
denotes the
direct product
of
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{T}
. Note that both
H
and
K
are necessarily subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{T}
. In fact,
(\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{T})_{H}=\mathfrak{S}
and
(\mathfrak{S}\times\mathfrak{T})_{K}=\mathfrak{T}
. Let
H
and
K
be groups and let
\mathcal{H}\leq\Aut(H)
and
\mathcal{K}\leq\Aut(K)
. If
G=H\times K
, then we may naturally view
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{K}
as a subgroup of
\Aut(G)
. As observed in
[
4
]
, if
G=H\times K
then
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{S}_{H}\times\mathfrak{S}_{K}
is automorphic if and only if
\mathfrak{S}_{H}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{K}
are automorphic.

A
section

U
of
G
is a pair of subgroups
U=[K,H]
such that
1\leq K\leq H\leq G
and
K\trianglelefteq G
. We say that a section
U=[K,H]
is
proper
if
1<K\leq H<G
. We say that a section is
trivial
if
K=H
. As all subgroups of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
are normal and uniquely determined by their orders, we shall denote the section
[\mathcal{Z}_{d},\mathcal{Z}_{e}]
simply as
[d,e]
.

Given any proper section
U=[K,H]
, a Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
over
H
, and a Schur ring
\mathfrak{T}
over
K
, we form the
wedge product

\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T}
by constructing the common refinement of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{S})
and
\mathcal{D}(\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{T}))
, where
\pi:G\to G/K
is the natural map. To guarantee that
(\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T})_{H}=\mathfrak{S}
and
\pi(\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T})=\mathfrak{T}
, the extra compatibility condition
\pi(\mathfrak{S})=\mathfrak{T}_{H/K}
is required in this construction. When
U=[H,H]
is trivial, compatibility is automatic. We say a Schur ring is
wedge-decomposable
when there exists a proper section such that the Schur ring can be expressed as a wedge product of two other Schur rings. In particular,
H
and
K
are
\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T}
-subgroups. Otherwise, we say the Schur ring is
wedge-indecomposable
.

As explained in
[
4
]
, every Schur ring has a
wedge core
, which is a maximal wedge-indecomposable Schur subring. Let
\Omega(n,\mathfrak{S})
be the number of Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
for which
\mathfrak{S}
is its wedge core. Let
\Omega(n,d)=\Omega(n,\mathcal{Z}_{d})
. Clearly,
\Omega(n)=\sum_{\mathfrak{S}}\Omega(n,\mathfrak{S})
, where the sum ranges over the indecomposable Schur subrings of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
. We note that the indecomposable Schur rings are necessarily trivial, direct products of indecomposable Schur rings, or automorphic (of course, not every automorphic ring is indecomposable). The first case is trivial to count and the second is also enumerated recursively.

Lastly, to count automorphic Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, we note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between subgroups of
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{n})
and automorphic Schur rings. So, it suffices to count the number of subgroups of
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{n})
, that is, compute
|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{n}))|
. The problem of counting the number of subgroups of an abelian group is a well studied problem in the literature (for example, see the references in
[
4
]
). The following lemma due to Călugăreanu
[
1
]
will be useful in our enumeration of automorphic Schur rings.

Lemma 2.1
.

The number of subgroups of
\mathcal{Z}_{p^{k}}\times\mathcal{Z}_{p^{\ell}}
is given as

\left|\mathcal{L}\middle(\mathcal{Z}_{p^{k}}\times\mathcal{Z}_{p^{\ell}}\middle)\right|=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,\ell)}\phi(p^{j})(k-j+1)(\ell-j+1),

where
\phi
denotes Euler’s totient function.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We proceed to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Consider
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
, where
p=\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{k_{i}}+1
and
q=\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{\ell_{i}}+1
are primes. By the Fundamental Theorem, all the Schur rings over this group are traditional. So, we now consider each of these families. As observed above, since
pq
is composite, the trivial Schur ring contributes one to the count of
\Omega(pq)
and does not intersect the other three families. Additionally, as all Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
are necessarily automorphic, all direct product rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
are automorphic and will be counted under the automorphic family.

We consider next the wedge-decomposable Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
. Over
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
, the only possible proper sections are
[p,p]
and
[q,q]
, which are trivial. In the first case, any possible Schur ring over
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
could be wedged with any possible Schur ring over
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. This produces
\Omega(p)\Omega(q)=xy
distinct Schur rings. The subgroups of each of these Schur rings will be exactly
1
,
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
. Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is missing since elements of order
q
and
pq
are fused together in cosets of
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
. Thus, these Schur rings are not automorphic. The second case is similar and produces
xy
distinct Schur rings from the automorphic ring and the wedge products already accounted for. Therefore, there are
2xy
wedge products over
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
.

Note that by the above decomposition of
p
,
p-1
has
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}+1)
divisors. Likewise,
q-1
has
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\ell_{1}+1)
divisors. Considering the wedge-decomposable and trivial families, we have already accounted for
2\prod_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}+1)(\ell_{i}+1)+1
distinct, non-automorphic Schur rings. Thus, to prove Theorem
1.1
it suffices to count the number of distinct automorphic Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
. As these Schur rings are in direct one-to-one correspondence with the subgroups of
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq})
, we see that

\Omega(pq)=|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq}))|+2\prod_{i=1}^{n}(k_{i}+1)(\ell_{i}+1)+1.

(3.1)

For a finite group
G
, let
G=\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}
be its primary decomposition. Then it is well-known that
\mathcal{L}(G)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}(P_{i})
. In the case of
\mathcal{Z}_{pq}
, we see that
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq})\cong\mathcal{Z}_{p-1}\times\mathcal{Z}_{q-1}
. Hence, the primary decomposition for
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq})
is given as

\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\mathcal{Z}_{r_{i}^{k_{i}}}\times\mathcal{Z}_{r_{i}^{\ell_{i}}}).

By Lemma
2.1
,

\left|\mathcal{L}\middle(\mathcal{Z}_{r_{i}^{k_{i}}}\times\mathcal{Z}_{r_{i}^{\ell_{i}}}\middle)\right|=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k_{i},\ell_{i})}\phi(r_{i}^{j})(k_{i}-j+1)(\ell_{i}-j+1).

Therefore,

|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{pq}))|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k_{i},\ell_{i})}\phi(r_{i}^{j})(k_{i}-j+1)(\ell_{i}-j+1).

(3.2)

Finally, Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), which finishes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.6

Let
p=2^{k}a+1
, where
a
is odd. Let
x
denote the number of divisors of
p-1
. Then
x/(k+1)
is the number of divisors of
a
.

The construction of wedge products will be more complicated in the case
n=4p
as wedge-compatibility is not necessarily trivial. There is also the concern that different sections can potentially create the same wedge product, e.g. if
U=[2,4]
and
U^{\prime}=[4,4]
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{4}\wedge_{U}\mathcal{Z}_{6}=\mathcal{Z}_{4}\wedge_{U^{\prime}}\mathcal{Z}_{3}
. Additionally, the wedge product construction is associative, e.g.
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2})\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{3}=\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{3})
. To avoid these issues, we will restrict our attention to wedge-decomposable Schur rings
\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T}
where
U=[K,H]
,
K
is a minimal
\mathfrak{S}
-subgroup, and
\mathfrak{S}
is wedge-indecomposable, that is,
\mathfrak{S}
is the wedge-core of
\mathfrak{S}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T}
.

We will organize Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
according to the order of its wedge-core. We need to consider
\Omega(4p,d)
for the divisors
d=2,p,4,2p
(
d=1,4p
are omitted as these are not proper sections). If
\mathfrak{S}
is primitive, the only possible section would be trivial. As such,
\Omega(n,\mathfrak{S})=\Omega(n/d)
. For example,
\Omega(4p,2)=\Omega(2p)=3x+1
and
\Omega(4p,p)=\Omega(4)=3
. In fact, as all the
x
many Schur rings
\mathfrak{S}
over
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
are primitive,
\Omega(4p,\mathfrak{S})=\Omega(4)
. Hence, there are
\Omega(p)\Omega(4)=3x
many Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
with a core of order
p
.

The three Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
are
\mathcal{Z}_{4}^{0}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
, where the first and last ones are indecomposable.
As
\mathcal{Z}_{4}^{0}
is primitive,
\Omega(4p,\mathcal{Z}_{4}^{0})=\Omega(p)=x
. For
\mathfrak{S}=\mathcal{Z}_{4}
we select the section
U=[2,4]
. Hence,
\Omega(4p,4)
counts the number of Schur rings of the form
\mathcal{Z}_{4}\wedge_{U}\mathfrak{T}
where
\mathfrak{T}
is a Schur ring over
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
is a subring. To count the number of such rings
\mathfrak{T}
we revisit the proof of Theorem
1.1
with the simplification
q=2
, which shows that there are exactly
x
many Schur rings of the form
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathfrak{S}
,
x
many Schur rings of the form
\mathfrak{S}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
,
x
many Schur rings of the form
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}
, and one trivial ring
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}^{0}
. Hence, there are
2x
many Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}
that contain the subring
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
, that is,
\Omega(4p,4)=2x
. Therefore, there are
3x
many Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
with a core of order 4.

It is also observed that there are
x+1
indecomposable Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}
, which
x
have the form
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
and one is trivial. Note
\Omega(4p,\mathcal{Z}_{2p}^{0})=\Omega(2)=1
, that is,
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}^{0}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
is the only option. If
\mathfrak{S}=\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
is the core, then
\mathfrak{S}
has two distinct minimal subgroups, namely,
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
. As such, two proper sections need to be considered,
[2,2p]
and
[p,2p]
. For fixed
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
, there are
2
Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{2p}
which contain
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
as a subring, namely
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. Hence, there are two possibilities for
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})\wedge_{[2,2p]}\mathfrak{T}
. For
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})\wedge_{[p,2p]}\mathfrak{T}
, we count the number of Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
which contain
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
as a subgroup. There are two such rings,
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
. Lastly, there is
\Omega(2)=1
ring of the form
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})\wedge_{[2p,2p]}\mathfrak{T}
, namely
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. Therefore, there are
2+2-1=3
many Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
with
\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
as its core. This accounts for
3x+1
many Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
with a core of order
2p
.

In summary, this accounts for
(3x+1)+3x+3x+(3x+1)=12x+2
many wedge-decomposable Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
. It remains to consider the indecomposable Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
. There is, of course, the trivial Schur ring
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}^{0}
, as well as the indecomposable automorphic Schur rings and those direct products of the form
\mathcal{Z}_{4}^{0}\times\mathfrak{S}
for some Schur rings
\mathfrak{S}
of order
p
. In regard to the automorphic Schur rings, we know the total count is equal to
|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4p}))|
. The sublattice
\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4}))\times\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{p}))
will consist of two lattice-isomorphic copies of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Z}_{p-1})
. The full lattice
\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4p}))
contains these two layers and all the diagonal entries that sit between the top and bottom layers. Those non-diagonal automorphic Schur rings in the top layer have the form
\mathcal{Z}_{4}\times\mathfrak{S}
, for some Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
of order
p
, and are indecomposable. Those non-diagonal automorphic Schur rings in the bottom layer have the form
(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{Z}_{2})\times\mathfrak{S}
and are wedge-decomposable. Notice that these decomposable automorphic Schur rings are in one-to-one correspondence with those indecomposable Schur rings of the form
\mathcal{Z}_{4}^{0}\times\mathfrak{S}
. Thus, if every diagonal automorphic Schur ring over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
is indecomposable, which we claim, then the number of indecomposable Schur rings over
\mathcal{Z}_{4p}
is
1+|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4p}))|
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we see that
\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4p})\cong\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathcal{Z}_{p-1}\cong(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathcal{Z}_{2^{k}})\times\mathcal{Z}_{a}
and

|\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4p}))|=|\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Z}_{2}\times\mathcal{Z}_{2^{k}})||\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Z}_{a})|=(2(k+1)+k)\left(\dfrac{x}{k+1}\right)=\dfrac{3k+2}{k+1}x.

To prove the claim, we introduce the representation
\omega:\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{Z}_{n}]\to\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
which maps a generator of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
to
\zeta_{n}:=e^{2\pi i/n}
. We remind the reader that in
[
4
]
we saw that an automorphic Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
is wedge-decomposable if and only if
\omega(\mathfrak{S})\leq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})
for some proper divisor
d\mid n
(excluding, of course, the case when
n
is prime). By definition, the diagonal automorphic Schur rings are not contained in
\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{4}))\times\mathcal{L}(\Aut(\mathcal{Z}_{p}))
, which implies that their image is not contained in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{4}))
or
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))
. This implies they are all indecomposable, as claimed.

As we have now exhausted all possibilities, we see that

\Omega(4p)=(12x+2)+1+\dfrac{3k+2}{k+1}x=\dfrac{15k+14}{k+1}x+3,

which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The de Rham period map for punctured elliptic curves and the KZB equation
Ben Moore

Abstract
. We demonstrate that the algebraic KZB connection of Levin–Racinet and Luo on a once-punctured elliptic curve represents Kim’s universal unipotent connection, and we observe that the Hodge filtration on the KZB connection has a particularly simple form. This allows us to generalise previous work of Beacom by writing down explicitly the maximal metabelian quotient of Kim’s de Rham period map in terms of elliptic polylogarithms. As far as we are aware this is the first time that the de Rham period map has been written out for an infinite dimensional quotient of the de Rham fundamental group on any curve of positive genus.

Introduction

Let
C/K
be a geometrically smooth complete curve of genus

with good reduction over the residue field
k

for the hyperbolic curve

is a finite subset of cardinality

,
3

]
for understanding hyperbolic curves over number fields via

which associates to each

an element inside a certain moduli space of torsors over

(here,

is the completion of the number field at some finite place of good reduction). The entire Selmer scheme apparatus is controlled by the
K
-prounipotent completion of the motivic fundamental group

of
X
, which is an infinite dimensional
K
-prounipotent group, and the de Rham period map is controlled by the de Rham realisation
U^{dR}
of
U

Previous work
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
has generally focussed on the Selmer schemes induced by certain small finite-dimensional quotients of

, such as its descending central series quotients
U_{n}
for

, but such quotients contain nontrivial information about

only when

exceeds some (increasing) function of the rank of the Jacobian of

U
itself — or more realistically, for algebraically well-behaved infinite-dimensional quotients of

, such as its maximal metabelianisation
W=U/[[U,U],[U,U]]

in the simplest nontrivial case, namely for

a once-punctured elliptic curve.

The target of the de Rham period map is the affine space
F^{0}U^{dR}\backslash U^{dR}
, where
F^{\bullet}
is the
Hodge filtration
on
U^{dR}
. In general, we have
F^{0}U^{dR}=\exp\left(F^{0}\mathfrak{u}\right)
, where
\mathfrak{u}
is the Lie algebra of

and

is a subspace. For the purposes of applications to Selmer schemes, it is necessary to understand the composition of the period map with a coordinate
\iota:F^{0}U\backslash U\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}
. In general, one obtains such a coordinate
\iota
from a splitting
\mathfrak{u}=F^{0}\mathfrak{u}\oplus M
of
K
-vector spaces, with
M
a Lie ideal, simply by observing that the composite

M\xrightarrow{\mathrm{exp}}U\twoheadrightarrow F^{0}U\backslash U

is bijective, and defining
\iota
to be its inverse.

When
X
is the thrice-punctured line,
F^{0}
is trivial, and so
\iota
is simply the logarithm. It is easy to compute the image in
W
of the logarithm of the de Rham period map; however, most authors choose to compute its image in Deligne’s polylogarithmic quotient
[
10
]
, which is even smaller than
W
. For a once-punctured elliptic curve
X=E-\{\infty\}
,

is the free Lie algebra on
A
and

corresponds to an algebraic
1
-form on
E
and

to an algebraic
1
-form on

). Furthermore

is one-dimensional. Choosing a Lie ideal

allows us to define the projection
\pi_{F^{0}\mathfrak{u}}
of
\mathfrak{u}
onto
F^{0}\mathfrak{u}
, and we can check that the map

U\ni x\mapsto\log\left(\exp\left(-\pi_{F^{0}\mathfrak{u}}(\log(x))\right)x)\right)\in M

descends to
F^{0}U\backslash U
and is equal to
\iota
. It is natural to wonder whether our results are contained in the earlier article of Beacom
[
6
]
, which is dedicated to the de Rham period map for elliptic and hyperelliptic curves. The answer is that Beacom describes a recursive algorithm for computing the de Rham period map attached to
U_{n}
from the one attached to
U_{n-1}
, but the algorithm is sufficiently complicated that it is infeasible to use it to directly write down the period map for every
U_{n}
.

We circumvent this difficulty by exploiting the fact, which seems to be generally under-appreciated, that the presentation of
F^{0}U^{dR}
, and hence
\iota
, depends on a choice of representative for the
universal connection
on
X
. Beacom, along with all other writers on the subject, chooses the so-called
naive connection

\nabla_{0}
, for which the
crystalline path torsor
appearing in the period map assumes a particularly simple form. But the drawback to this approach is that the Hodge filtration is harder to describe: for example, at level four, we have

F_{\nabla_{0}}^{0}\mathfrak{u}_{4}=\mathrm{Span}_{K}\left\{B-\lambda[B,[A,B]]+(\mu+\kappa/3)[B,[B,[A,B]]]\right\},\quad\lambda,\mu,\kappa\in K.

However, we choose to employ (a variant of) the algebraic KZB connection
\nabla_{KZB}
of Levin–Racinet and Luo. There is a unique (pointed) isomorphism between these (pointed) connections, but this variant of the KZB connection enjoys a number of highly desirable properties. Among them is the particularly simple form taken by its the Hodge filtration:

F_{\nabla_{KZB}}^{0}\mathfrak{u}=\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{B\}.

Now there is a canonical choice for
M
: namely, the Lie ideal spanned by Lie words of degree at least
1
in
A
. Then
\iota
takes the form

(1)

\iota_{KZB}(x)=\log\left(\exp\left(-\pi_{\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{B\}}(\log(x))\right)x)\right).

Finally, we note that as for the thrice-punctured line, it is very convenient to pass to
W
, in order to make the calculations algebraically tractable. We require descriptions of the flat section of the (adjoint) of the KZB equation, of the logarithm of this flat section, and of the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, and all of these are rather difficult to obtain over
U
.

2.
Definitions

This section is devoted to recalling the definition of the de Rham period map. We make definitions for general smooth
K
-schemes as far as possible, restricting to hyperbolic curves only when necessary. We will use the word “connection” throughout to refer to a vector bundle equipped with a connection.

Let
Z/K
be a scheme over a field of characteristic zero. Let
D
be a divisor of

with normal crossing singularities, and set
Z^{\prime}=Z-D
. Let
\mathrm{UnFC}(Z^{\prime})
denote the Tannakian category of unipotent flat connections over

\mathrm{UnFC}(Z)(\log D)
denote the category of flat unipotent logarithmic connections on

. These are the flat meromorphic connections on

that are holomorphic outside

and have at worst logarithmic singularities within

\nabla
over in
\mathrm{UnFC}(Z^{\prime})
with a meromorphic extension to

has a canonical extension
\overline{\nabla}
to a connection in
\mathrm{UnFC}(Z)(\log D)
. Furthermore, for

, any such logarithmic connection on

induces a connection, called the

, on
T_{P}Z\setminus\{0\}
.

For any
K
-schemes

, let

denote the base-change of

to

, and write
\mathrm{Vect}_{S}
for the category of vector bundles on

be a
K
-rational basepoint of

, meaning that

, or a tangential basepoint at

, in which case

is a rational basepoint, there is an obvious fibre functor
F_{b}(S):\mathrm{UnFC}(Z^{\prime}_{S})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{S}
defined by sending a connection
\mathscr{E}
to its fibre over

. When

is a tangential basepoint at

, we define the fibre functor
F_{b}(S):\mathrm{UnFC}(Z_{S})(\log(D))\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{S}
by sending a connection on
Z_{S}
to the fibre of the residue connection
\mathrm{Res}\left(\mathscr{E}\right)
on

over

When
b
is a rational basepoint, a

unipotent flat connection on

(based at

(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
in
\mathrm{UnFC}(Z^{\prime})
together with an element

. When

is a tangential basepoint, a pointed unipotent flat connection is a connection
(\mathscr{E},\nabla)
in
\mathrm{UnFC}(Z)(\log(D))
together with an element

(\mathscr{E}_{0},\nabla_{0},v_{0})

, there exists a unique isomorphism of connections

\mathscr{E}_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}
of
K

\phi_{b}(v_{0})=v

The

of
Z^{\prime}
based at
b

U^{dR}(Z^{\prime},b)
(or simply

, though the dependence on

is crucial), is defined to be the

-group scheme representing the functor
S\mapsto\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}F_{b}(S)
of tensor-compatible automorphisms. Similarly, for

, the functor
S\mapsto\mathrm{Isom}^{\otimes}(F_{b}(S),F_{z^{\prime}}(S))

, which we call the

. On general grounds,

and

admit a certain filtration, called the

: but we will see below that a lemma of Hadian provides a characterisation of the Hodge filtration which is rather more useful in practice. It is well-known that
U
is a prounipotent group, so it is representable as the inverse limit of its descending central series quotients

, defined by

U^{1}=U,\quad U^{n}=[U,U^{n-1}],\quad U_{n}=U/U^{n+1}.

The construction extends to produce descending central series quotients of its Lie algebra

of its Lie algebra, the de Rham path torsor, and unipotent connections on

. We even have descending central series quotients for all of the quotients of these objects induced by quotients of

A logarithmic filtration

Proposition 2

For a
K

, the map

For each

Definition 3

Let

In practice (for example, when working with Selmer schemes), we work with the composition of the de Rham period map with a Lie algebra-valued coordinate

the free cocommutative Hopf algebra on the symbols

\beta_{j}

and

with the grouplike elements of

\mathfrak{u}
with the primitive elements. If

\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes H

. Fix any representative
(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes H,\nabla)
of the universal unipotent pointed connection with marked element

(which we interpret as the fibre over

\iota_{\nabla}\circ j^{dR}_{\nabla}(x)=\log\left[\exp\left(-p_{\nabla}^{H}(x)\right)p^{cr}_{\nabla}(x)\right].

If

is any trivialised representative of the universal unipotent pointed connection on

We turn now to the problem of determining

. A lemma of Hadian

of logarithmic subbundles (with poles contained in

of

inducing by the de Rham complex of

F^{-j}\left(V^{\otimes n}_{dR}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\right)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Span}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\left\{\text{words }w\text{ in }A_{i}\text{s, }B_{j}\text{s, s.t. }\sum_{i=1}^{g}\mathrm{deg}_{A_{i}}w\leq|j|\right\}&j\leq 0\\

Hadian’s pointed filtration is unique up to a unique isomorphism, and is completely determined by a choice of representative for the isomorphism class of the universal pointed connection. Thus, the Hodge filtration on any representative of the universal unipotent pointed connection is the restriction of this filtration from the logarithmic extension. Based on this observation, Beacom
[

from the Hodge filtration on

for affine elliptic and hyperelliptic curves.

Thus, given a representative

for the universal unipotent pointed connection, the data of the logarithm of its fundamental solution together with its Hodge filtration

is sufficient to define Kim’s de Rham period map. For the naive connection
\nabla_{0}
on

, the fundamental solution is given in terms of iterated integrals as

G(z)=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{ in }A_{i},B_{j},C_{k}\end{subarray}}G_{w}(z)w,\quad G_{A_{i}B_{k}^{2}C_{j}}=\int_{b}^{z}\alpha_{i}\beta_{k}\beta_{k}\gamma_{j}\text{ etc},

so the computation of the de Rham period map with respect to

reduces to the computation of the Hodge filtration, at least when

is a rational basepoint. When

is a tangential basepoint, more work is necessary, but when the endpoint

is an integral point contained in the open set containing

\nabla_{0}
with its logarithmic extension over that open set. In particular there is no need to choose a branch of the
p
-adic logarithm. Beacom’s recursive algorithm for computing the Hodge filtration on descending central series quotients gives rise to a recursive algorithm
[

, Sections 5 and 6]
for computing

from
\left(j^{dR}_{\nabla_{0}}\right)_{n-1}
. Unfortunately, it it appears to be very difficult to use his algorithm to directly write down the full Hodge filtration on

However, any choice of representative for the universal unipotent connection, giving rise to a coordinate

on the period space, is sufficient for applications to Selmer schemes. As we shall demonstrate later, it is quite possible to compute explicitly the logarithm of the crystalline parallel transport operator

(or more truthfully, its image in a certain large quotient of

) for any connection

. Thus, it is natural to seek representatives of the universal connection whose Hodge filtration is as simple as possible. We will demonstrate that when

[

and developed further by Luo

meets this requirement.

Proposition 4

\nabla_{KZB}=d-\omega_{KZB},\quad\omega_{KZB}=\beta B+\alpha\exp\left(-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}P_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{k}}{k}\right)A

on

\displaystyle\nabla_{KZB}^{\prime}=d-\omega_{KZB}^{\prime},\quad\omega_{KZB}^{\prime}=(\beta-df)B+\alpha\exp\left(-f-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}P_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{k}}{k}\right)A

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that the gauge transformation takes

to

\displaystyle\exp\left(-f-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}P_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{k}}{k}\right)

∎

It will be convenient to write the KZB connections in the form

\displaystyle\nabla_{KZB}=d-\beta B-\alpha A-\alpha\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}p_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{k}(A),\quad\nabla_{KZB}^{\prime}=d-\left(\beta-df\right)B-\alpha A-\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{k}(A),

Proposition 5
.

The connection

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\nabla=d+\omega,\quad\omega=\alpha A+\beta B+\sum_{|w|>1}r_{w}w,\quad r_{w}\in\mathcal{O}_{X},

where the sum is over words in

dg=g\omega_{0}-\omega g,

Then the invertible gauge transformation

and extended to

Proposition 6

The Hodge filtration

Define a filtration

\left(F^{\prime}\right)^{j}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\langle\langle A,B\rangle\rangle=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\left\{\text{words }w\text{ in }A,B\text{ s.t. }\mathrm{deg}_{A}w\leq-j\right\}

on

F^{i}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\langle\langle A,B\rangle\rangle(\log(\infty))
F^{i-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}\langle\langle A,B\rangle\rangle\otimes\Omega_{X/K}^{1}
F^{i-1}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\langle\langle A,B\rangle\rangle\otimes\Omega_{Y/K}^{1}\left(\log(\infty)\right)

4.
The de Rham period map

At this stage, the only remaining obstacle to writing down the de Rham period map is the computation of the logarithm of a flat section of
\nabla_{KZB}
. Such a computation would require a basis for
\mathfrak{u}
as a
K
-vector space, but recall that this is quite complicated: a basis for the free Lie algebra on a finite set of generators is given by Lyndon words. In order to obtain a reasonable description of the de Rham period map, we therefore concentrate on working out its image inside the maximal metabelian quotient
W=U/[[U,U],[U,U]]
of
U
. This is in some sense the largest (Galois-equivariant) quotient of
U
which is algebraically comprehensible. In fact, the set

\left\{A,B,\sigma_{r,s}\right\}_{r,s\geq 0},\quad\sigma_{r,s}=\tau_{r,s}[A,B],\quad\tau_{r,s}=\mathrm{ad}_{B}^{r}\mathrm{ad}_{A}^{s}

is a
K
-basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{w}
of
W
. The adjoint map on
\mathfrak{w}
is injective, so there is an isomorphism
\mathfrak{w}\cong\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w})\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{K}(\mathfrak{w})
, which induces an isomorphism
W\cong\mathrm{Ad}(W)\subseteq GL(W)
. If
G
is a flat section of some connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{w})
and
\nabla=d+\omega
where
\omega
acts as left multiplication by a primitive element, then
G
is primitive. Furthermore,
\mathrm{Ad}(G)=\exp\left(\mathrm{ad}_{\log(G)}\right)
is a flat section of the induced connection
\mathrm{Ad}(\nabla)=d+\mathrm{ad}_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathscr{U}(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w}))
. To describe such a section, we require a basis for a
K
-vector space containing
\exp\left(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w})\right)
(not for all of
\mathscr{U}(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w}))
):

\exp\left(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w})\right)\subset\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{\tau_{u,v},\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\geq 0\\
r,s\geq 0\end{subarray}}.

Thus, in order to understand
\log(G)
, it suffices to understand (part of) the flat section
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
of the adjoint KZB connection
\mathrm{Ad}\left(\nabla_{KZB}\right)
together with the exponential map on
\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w})
. We prove a slightly more general result (the functions
p_{1}
is not assumed to be zero) so that we can recycle it for the case where
b
is a tangential basepoint.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathrm{Ad}\left(\nabla_{KZB}\right)=d-\mathrm{ad}_{\omega}
, where

\mathrm{ad}_{\omega}=\beta\tau_{1,0}+\alpha\tau_{0,1}-\alpha\sum_{k=1}p_{k}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{k-1,0}}.

Then the unique flat section of
\mathrm{Ad}\left(\nabla_{KZB}\right)
of the form

G=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\geq 0\\
u+v>0\end{subarray}}G^{*}_{u,v}\tau_{u,v}+\sum_{r,s\geq 0}G_{r,s}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}},\quad G\in\mathscr{U}(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w}))

satisfies

(10)

G^{*}_{1,0}=\int\beta,\quad G^{*}_{0,1}=\int\alpha,

and
G_{r,s}
is the iterated integral of the coefficient of
X^{r}Y^{s}
in

(11)

-\left(1-\alpha Y-\beta X\right)^{-1}\left(1+(1-\beta X)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\beta^{i-k+1}\alpha p_{k}X^{i}\right),

where the “iterated integral” of an element of
\mathcal{O}_{X}\langle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\rangle
is defined in the obvious way.

Proof.

Comparing coefficients of
\tau_{1,0}
and
\tau_{0,1}
in
dG=\mathrm{ad}_{\omega}G
yields (
10
), and an inspection of the coefficient of
\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{i,j}}
in
\mathrm{ad}_{\omega}G
yields

dG_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-\alpha\left(p_{1}+\int\beta\right)&i=0,j=0\\
\beta G_{i-1,0}-\alpha p_{i+1}&i\geq 1,j=0\\
\alpha G_{0,j-1}&i=0,j\geq 1\\
\beta G_{i-1,j}+\alpha G_{i,j-1}&i\geq 1,j\geq 1,\end{cases}

where we have used the identity

\tau_{0,1}\tau_{u,v}=\begin{cases}\tau_{u,v+1}&(u,v)\neq(1,0)\\
-\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{0,0}}-\tau_{1,1}&(u,v)=(1,0).\end{cases}

We obtain
G_{0,0}=-\int\alpha p_{1}-\int\alpha\beta
, then by induction we have

G_{i,0}=\sum_{k=1}^{i}\int\beta^{i-k+1}\alpha p_{k}-\int\beta^{i}\alpha\beta,\quad G_{0,j}=-\int\alpha^{j+1}p_{1}-\int\alpha^{j+1}\beta.

If we let the
F_{i,j}
be elements of the noncommutative ring
\mathcal{O}_{X}\langle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\rangle
satisfying the recurrence relations

F_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-\alpha\left(p_{1}+\beta\right)&i=0,j=0\\
-\alpha p_{i+1}+\beta F_{i-1,0}&i\geq 1,j=0\\
\alpha F_{0,j-1}&i=0,j\geq 1\\
\beta F_{i-1,j}+\alpha F_{i,j-1}&i\geq 1,j\geq 1,\end{cases}

and the boundary conditions

F_{i,0}=-\sum_{k=1}^{i}\beta^{i-k+1}\alpha p_{k}-\beta^{i}\alpha\beta,\quad F_{0,j}=-\alpha^{j+1}p_{1}-\alpha^{j+1}\beta,

then we find the following relation among generating series

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}F_{i,j}X^{i}Y^{j}=-\left(1-\alpha Y-\beta X\right)^{-1}\left(1+(1-\beta X)\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{i}\beta^{i-k+1}\alpha p_{k}X^{i}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we compute the logarithm of any grouplike element in
\mathscr{U}(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w}))
(and we note that it is possible to do this for the maximal metabelian group on any finite number of generators).

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that

H=1+\sum_{u,v\geq 0}H^{*}_{u,v}\tau_{u,v}+\sum_{r,s\geq 0}H_{r,s}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}

is grouplike in
\mathscr{U}(\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{w}))
, with

\log(H)=h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}+h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+\sum_{r,s\geq 0}h_{r,s}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}.

Then we have
h_{A}=H^{*}_{0,1}
,
h_{B}=H^{*}_{1,0}
, and an equality of formal power series

\sum_{r,s\geq 0}h_{r,s}X^{s}Y^{r}=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+n=r\\
v+m=s\end{subarray}}\frac{B_{u+v}h_{B}^{u}h_{A}^{v}H_{r,s}}{u!v!}\right)X^{s}Y^{r}-h_{A}h_{B}T(h_{A}X,h_{B}Y),

Proof.

An inspection of the coefficients of
\tau_{1,0}
and
\tau_{0,1}

reveals the identities for
h_{A}
and
h_{B}
. The rest of the claim is proved by studying the coefficient of
\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}
. To begin, this yields

\displaystyle H_{r,s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\left\{\text{coeff. of }\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}\text{ in }\left(h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}+\sum_{r,s\geq 0}h_{r,s}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}\right)^{\ell}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\left\{\text{coeff. of }\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}\text{ in }\left(\sum_{u,v\geq 0}h_{u,v}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{u,v}}\right)^{\ell}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq r\\
v\leq s\end{subarray}}h_{u,v}\times\text{ coeff. of }\tau_{r-u,s-v}\text{ in }\left(h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}\right)^{\ell-1}\right\}.

But

\left(h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}\right)^{\ell}=\sum_{i+j=\ell}h_{A}^{j}h_{B}^{i}\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{in }A,B\text{ with }\\
jA\text{s, }iB\text{s}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ad}_{w},

where

(12)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{in }A,B\text{ with }\\
jAs,iBs\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ad}_{w}=\binom{i+j}{i,j}\tau_{i,j}+\binom{i+j-1}{i-1,j}\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{i-1,j-1}}.

We prove this via induction by writing

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{in }A,B\text{ with }\\
jAs,iBs\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ad}_{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{in }A,B\text{ with }\\
j-1As,iBs\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ad}_{w}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{words }w\\
\text{in }A,B\text{ with }\\
jAs,i-1Bs\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ad}_{w}\mathrm{ad}_{B},

then substituting in (
12

for
i\geq 1
, with the convention that
\sigma_{i-1,j-1}=0
when

Then the coefficient of
\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma_{r,s}}
in
\left(h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}\right)^{\ell}
is

\binom{r+s+1}{r,s+1}h_{A}^{s+1}h_{B}^{r+1}

if
r+s+2=\ell
, and is otherwise zero. Similarly, the coefficient of
\tau_{r-u,s-v}
in
\left(h_{A}\mathrm{ad}_{A}+h_{B}\mathrm{ad}_{B}\right)^{\ell-1}
is

\binom{r-u+s-v}{r-u,s-v}h_{A}^{r-u}h_{B}^{s-v}

and is otherwise zero. Therefore

(13)

H_{r,s}=\frac{h_{B}^{r+1}h_{A}^{s+1}}{(r+s+2)r!(s+1)!}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u\leq r\\
0\leq v\leq s\end{subarray}}\frac{h_{u,v}h_{B}^{r-u}h_{A}^{s-v}}{(r-u+s-v+1)(r-u)!(s-v)!}.

We note that

\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\frac{X^{s}Y^{r}}{(r+s+2)r!(s+1)!}=\frac{1}{X}\left(\frac{\exp(X+Y)-1}{X+Y}-\frac{\exp(X)-1}{X}\right)

because both sides are holomorphic functions near the origin which satisfy the differential equation

\left(X\partial_{X}+Y\partial_{Y}+2\right)F=\frac{\exp(Y)(\exp(X)-1)}{X}.

The same reasoning applies to prove

\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\frac{X^{s}Y^{r}}{(r+s+1)r!s!}=\frac{\exp(X+Y)-1}{X+Y}

where the differential equation is

\left(X\partial_{X}+Y\partial Y+1\right)F=\exp(X+Y)

Then the proposition is proved upon passing to generating series in (
13
)), and using the fact that

\frac{\exp(X+Y)-1}{X+Y}=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\frac{B_{r,s}}{r!s!}X^{r}Y^{s}.\qed

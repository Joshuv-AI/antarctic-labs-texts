Model

In this section, we set the inflationary model with non-minimal coupling term in metric and Palatini formalism.

2.1
Non-minimal Coupling in metric and Palatini formalisms

As the Einstein-Hilbert action already includes the
(Mass)^{2}
dimensional coupling to the Ricci scalar
R
, we are enforced to include the operators of the form
\hat{\cal O}R
with
[\hat{\cal O}]=(Mass)^{2}
following the effective field theory approach unless a symmetry forbids it. Therefore when we introduce a scalar field
\phi
to our theory, we include an arbitrary (gauge invariant) function
K(\phi)
directly coupling to gravity:

\displaystyle S_{J}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{J}}\left[-\frac{M^{2}+K(\phi)}{2}R+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right],

(2)

with
[K]=(Mass)^{2}
and an arbitrary mass parameter
M
. The potential
V(\phi)
is a function of
[V]=(Mass)^{4}
. In metric formalism, the Ricci scalar
R
is solely determined by the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, but in the Palatini formalism it is also determined by the connection
\Gamma^{\mu}_{\nu\lambda}
which is taken to be independent of the metric. In the present of non-minimal coupling
K(\phi)R
, the two formalisms are not equivalent and predicts different Universe
Bauer:2008zj
.

By performing the Weyl transformation,

\displaystyle g_{E,\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}g_{J,\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}\equiv\frac{M^{2}+K(\phi)}{M_{P}^{2}}

(3)

the action is conveniently transformed into the canonical form of gravity in Einstein frame

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{E}}\left[-\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}R_{E}+\frac{1}{2}\Pi(\phi)(\partial\phi)^{2}-\frac{V(\phi)}{\Omega^{4}}\right],

(4)

where the non-trivial kinetic term is given by

\displaystyle\Pi(\phi)\equiv\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}+\frac{3\zeta}{2M_{P}^{2}}\frac{K^{\prime}(\phi)^{2}}{\Omega^{4}},

\displaystyle\zeta=\begin{cases}1&\text{(Metric) }\\
0&\text{(Palatini) }\end{cases}.

(5)

in metric and Palatini formalism, respectively. The second term (
\propto K^{\prime 2}
) originates from the transformation of the Ricci scalar
R(\Gamma)
, making it absent in the Palatini formalism.
The kinetic term is easily canonicalized by
\sqrt{\Pi(\phi)}\partial h=\partial\phi
or

\displaystyle\frac{dh}{d\phi}=\sqrt{\Pi(\phi)}.

(6)

When the explicit function
K(\phi)
is given, we obtain the canonical scalar
h(\phi)
by integration.

An asymptotically flat, positive potential in the Einstein frame
V_{E}(h(\phi))
should satisfy a general condition at large fields
Park:2008hz
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\phi\to\infty}\frac{V(\phi)}{K(\phi)^{2}}=\text{Const.}>0.

(7)

We request this condition
V\sim K^{2}
or equivalently
K\sim\sqrt{V}
to realize successful slow-roll inflation. We emphasize again that the condition essentially describes the
\alpha
-attractor behavior
Kallosh:2013yoa
.

2.2
Monomial functions:
K(\phi)\sim\phi^{m}\sim\sqrt{V(\phi)}

To explicitly see the inflationary behavior, we choose
K
and
V
to be monomial functions with
m\geq 1

1
1

1

For models with a global
U(1)
symmetry, a
K(\phi)
function with an odd power of
\phi
is usually forbidden.

\displaystyle K(\phi)=\xi M_{P}^{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}\right)^{m},

\displaystyle V=\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{4}}{2m}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}\right)^{2m},

(8)

where
\xi
and
\lambda
are arbitrary dimensionless parameters. In addition, without a (or with a sufficiently small) vacuum expectation value of the field
\phi
, we have
M\simeq M_{P}
to guarantee the canonicalized Einstein frame field (later we define as
h
) and potential
U(\phi(h))
coincide with the Jordan frame field
\phi
and potential
V(\phi)
near the origin. Therefore, we set
M=M_{P}
without losing precision in our predictions. Then the potential in Einstein frame becomes

\displaystyle U(\phi)=\frac{M_{P}^{4}}{\left(M_{P}^{2}+K\right)^{2}}V=\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{4}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}\right)^{2m}}{2m\left(1+\xi\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}\right)^{m}\right)^{2}}.

(9)

The slow-roll parameters are defined in the Einstein frame:

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial U(\phi(h))/\partial h}{U}\right)^{2}=\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2\Pi_{\zeta}(\phi)}\left(\frac{\partial U/\partial\phi}{U}\right)^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle\eta(\phi)

\displaystyle\equiv M_{P}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}U/\partial h^{2}}{U}=\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{\sqrt{\Pi_{\zeta}(\phi)}U}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Pi_{\zeta}(\phi)}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial\phi}\right),

(11)

where
\Pi_{\zeta}
is defined in Eq. (
5
) for metric (
\zeta=1
) and Palatini (
\zeta=0
) cases with
K(\phi)
in Eq. (
8
) and
x=\phi/M_{P}
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\zeta}(\phi=M_{P}x)=\frac{1+\xi x^{m}+\zeta\frac{3m^{2}\xi^{2}}{2}x^{2(m-1)}}{(1+\xi x^{m})^{2}},

\displaystyle\zeta=\begin{cases}1&\text{(Metric) }\\
0&\text{(Palatini) }\end{cases}.

(12)

The explicit form of the slow-roll parameters are obtained

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi=M_{P}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4m^{2}}{2x^{2}\left(\xi x^{m}+1\right)+3\zeta m^{2}\xi^{2}x^{2m}},

(13)

\displaystyle\eta(\phi=M_{P}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4m\left[x^{2}\left(\xi x^{m}+1\right)\left\{(m+2)\xi x^{m}-4m+2\right\}-3\zeta m^{3}\xi^{2}x^{2m}\left(\xi x^{m}-1\right)\right]}{\left[2x^{2}\left(\xi x^{m}+1\right)+3\zeta m^{2}\xi^{2}x^{2m}\right]^{2}}.

(14)

It is noticed that the slow-roll parameters are independent of
\lambda
.

The cosmological time (in Einstein frame)
t_{k}
when the mode corresponding to the pivot scale
k
leaves the horizon is determined by
k=a(t_{k})H(t_{k})
with the scale factor
a
and
H
being the Hubble parameter. In this work, the pivot scale is chosen to be
k=0.05\text{Mpc}^{-1}
. On the other hand, the time
t_{e}
at the end of the inflation is set by
\epsilon(\phi(t_{e}))=1
.

Then, the number of e-foldings during expansion from a pivot scale with
a_{k}=a(t_{k})
to the end of inflation at
a_{e}=a(t_{e})
is given by

\displaystyle N(\phi_{k})\equiv N_{k}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{a_{e}}{a_{k}}=\frac{1}{M_{P}^{2}}\int_{h(\phi_{e})}^{h(\phi_{k})}dh\frac{U}{\partial U/\partial h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\phi_{e}}^{\phi_{k}}\frac{d\phi}{M_{P}}\sqrt{\frac{\Pi_{\zeta}(\phi)}{2\epsilon(\phi)}}=\left[\frac{x^{2}}{4m}+\frac{3}{4}\zeta\left(\xi x^{m}-\ln(M_{P}^{m}+\zeta M_{P}^{m}x^{m})\right)\right]_{\phi_{e}/M_{P}}^{\phi_{k}/M_{P}}

(15)

with
\phi_{k}\equiv\phi(t_{k})
,
\phi_{e}\equiv\phi(t_{e})
, and
H_{k}\equiv H(t_{k})
where we used
dh=d\phi\sqrt{\Pi_{\zeta}}
in the 2nd line.

2.3
\xi\ll 1
(Metric
\approx
Palatini)

We first analyze the
\xi\ll 1

14

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi=M_{P}x)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2m^{2}}{x^{2}}-2m^{2}\xi x^{m-2}+\left(2x^{2}-3m^{2}\zeta\right)m^{2}\xi^{2}x^{2m-4}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}),

(16)

\displaystyle\eta(\phi=M_{P}x)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2m(2m-1)}{x^{2}}-5m^{2}\xi x^{m-2}+\left[5x^{2}+3(2-3m)m\zeta\right]m^{2}\xi^{2}x^{2m-4}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3})

where
\zeta=0,1
each correspond to the Palatini and metric cases, respectively. Note that the
\zeta

, and the observables approximately coincide for

.

The field value where inflation ends for both cases is determined by
\epsilon=1
, hence

\displaystyle\phi_{e}\simeq\sqrt{2}mM_{P}-2^{\frac{m-1}{2}}m^{1+m}M_{P}\xi+2^{m-\frac{5}{2}}m^{2m+1}(2m+3-3\zeta)M_{P}\xi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{3}).

(18)

Consequently, the e-folding number
N_{k}
from the pivot scale
a_{k}
to the end of inflation

then becomes, following Eq. (
2.2
)

\displaystyle N_{k}\simeq\frac{\phi_{k}^{2}-\phi_{e}^{2}}{4mM_{P}^{2}}+\frac{3}{8}\zeta\left[\frac{\phi_{k}^{2m}-\phi_{e}^{2m}}{M_{P}^{2m}}\right]\xi^{2}.

(19)

As
\xi\ll 1
, the field value
\phi_{k}
at pivot scale is represented in terms of
N_{k}
as

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\simeq\sqrt{2m(m+2N_{k})}M_{P}-\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}m^{m+2}}{\sqrt{m(m+2N_{k})}}M_{P}\xi+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})

(20)

leading to the slow-roll parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\phi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{m}{m+2N_{k}}+\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}}\left[m^{m+2}-m^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(m+2N_{k})^{\frac{m}{2}+1}\right]\xi}{(m+2N_{k})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2}),

(21)

\displaystyle\eta\left(\phi_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2m-1}{2N_{k}+m}+\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\left[2m^{m+1}(2m-1)-5m^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(m+2N_{k})^{\frac{m}{2}+1}\right]\xi}{(m+2N_{k})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2}).

(22)

These expressions yield the spectral index
n_{s}
and the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
in this limit as

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle\simeq 1-6\epsilon+2\eta|_{\phi=\phi_{k}}

(23)

\displaystyle\simeq 1-\frac{2(m+1)}{m+2N_{k}}+\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}}\left[m^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(m+2N_{k})^{\frac{m}{2}+1}-2m^{m+1}(m+1)\right]\xi}{(m+2N_{k})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})

(24)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\simeq 16\epsilon|_{\phi=\phi_{k}}\simeq\frac{16m}{m+2N_{k}}+\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}+4}\left[m^{m+2}-m^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(m+2N_{k})^{\frac{m}{2}+1}\right]\xi}{(m+2N_{k})^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{2})

(25)

along with the curvature power spectrum amplitude being

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle\simeq\left.\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}\epsilon}\frac{U}{M_{P}^{4}}\right|_{\phi=\phi_{k}}

(26)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2^{m-4}m^{m-2}(m+2N_{k})^{m+1}\lambda}{3\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2^{\frac{3m}{2}-4}m^{\frac{3m}{2}-2}\lambda\left[(m+1)m^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(m+2N_{k})^{m}+(m+2N_{k})^{\frac{3m}{2}+1}\right]\xi}{3\pi^{2}}

(27)

where
A_{s}\simeq 2.1\times 10^{-9}
is given by Planck and BICEP/Keck 2018 results at the pivot scale
k=0.05\text{Mpc}^{-1}

Planck:2018jri
. The
\zeta
dependent terms are suppressed, with its leading order terms emerging at

order.

Note that, the specific
\xi
range that resembles an approximate minimal coupling case differs for each

from minimal cases, the required

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\delta r}{r}\right|\sim\left|\frac{\delta n_{s}}{n_{s}}\right|\sim(4m{N_{k}})^{\frac{m}{2}}|\delta\xi|

(28)

which is

that shows a reasonable deviation from

In the minimal coupling case

\displaystyle n_{s}\simeq 1-\frac{m+1}{N_{k}},

\displaystyle r\simeq\frac{8m}{N_{k}},

(29)

and these two parameters are correlated as

\displaystyle N_{k}\simeq\frac{m+1}{1-n_{s}}\simeq\frac{8m}{r}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle r\simeq\frac{8m}{m+1}(1-n_{s})\approx\left.\frac{0.32m}{m+1}\right|_{n_{s}\approx 0.96}.

We now turn to the opposite limit and examine the case with a large non-vanishing non-minimal coupling.
Now the predictions from metric and Palatini formalisms drastically differ. Therefore we discuss the metric case and Palatini case below separately.

2.4.1
Metric Formalism

In the large
\xi
limit, slow roll parameters are approximated as

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4}{3\xi^{2}}\left(\frac{M_{P}}{\phi}\right)^{2m}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{-3}),

(31)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{4}{3\xi}\left(\frac{M_{P}}{\phi}\right)^{m}+\frac{4}{3\xi^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{M_{P}}{\phi}\right)^{2m}+\frac{3m-2}{3m^{3}}\left(\frac{M_{P}}{\phi}\right)^{2m-2}\right]+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{-3}).

(32)

Therefore,
\epsilon(\phi_{e})=1
gives

\displaystyle\phi_{e}\simeq\left(\frac{4}{3\xi^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}M_{P}.

(33)

E-folding number and the field value at the pivot scale are obtained

\displaystyle N_{k}\approx\frac{3\xi}{4}\left(\frac{\phi_{k}}{M_{P}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\simeq\left(\frac{4N_{k}}{3\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}M_{P},

\displaystyle(\xi\gg 1,~\text{Metric}).

(34)

Then, the slow roll parameters and cosmological observables are represented by
N_{k}
as

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi_{k})\simeq\frac{3}{4N_{k}^{2}},

\displaystyle\eta(\phi_{k})\simeq-\frac{1}{N_{k}}+\frac{3}{4N_{k}^{2}}+\frac{3m-2}{4N_{k}^{2}m^{3}}\left(\frac{4N_{k}}{3\xi}\right)^{\frac{2}{m}}

(35)

and the spectral index and tensor-to-scalar-ratio are

\displaystyle n_{s}\simeq 1-6\epsilon+2\eta|_{\phi=\phi_{k}}\simeq 1-\frac{2}{N_{k}}-\frac{3}{N_{k}^{2}},

\displaystyle r_{s}\simeq 16\epsilon|_{\phi=\phi_{k}}\simeq\frac{12}{N_{k}^{2}},

(36)

which enjoys the sweet spot of the Planck, WMAP and BICEP/Keck bounds on
(n_{s},r)
for
N_{k}\simeq 50-60
. Prospects from future measurements are discussed in Section.
3
.

From the fact that the potential during inflation approaches a constant value at the large field regime with a large non-minimal coupling,

\displaystyle U(\phi)\xrightarrow{\xi\gg 1,~\phi\gg M_{P}/\xi^{1/m}}U_{\text{inf}}\simeq\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{4}}{2m\xi^{2}}

(37)

we have the normalization of
\lambda/\xi^{2}
as

\displaystyle A_{s}\simeq\left.\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}\epsilon}\frac{U}{M_{P}^{4}}\right|_{\phi=\phi_{k}\gg M_{P}/\xi^{1/m}}\simeq\frac{N_{k}^{2}\lambda}{36m\pi^{2}\xi^{2}}\simeq 2.1\times 10^{-9}.

(38)

2.4.2
Palatini Formalism

The slow-roll parameters in Palatini formalism are approximated as

\displaystyle\epsilon\simeq\dfrac{2m^{2}M_{P}^{m+2}}{\xi\phi^{m+2}},

\displaystyle\eta\simeq-\frac{m(m+2)M_{P}^{2}}{\phi^{2}}.

(39)

By requiring
\epsilon(\phi_{e})=1
,

\displaystyle\phi_{e}=2^{\frac{1}{2+m}}M_{P}\left(\frac{\xi}{m^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2+m}}.

(40)

The e-folding number and the field value at pivot scale are

\displaystyle N_{k}\simeq\frac{1}{4mM^{2}}\left(\phi_{k}^{2}-\phi_{e}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\simeq 2\sqrt{mN_{k}}M_{P},

\displaystyle(\xi\gg 1,~\text{Palatini})

(41)

where we neglect
\phi_{e}\ll\phi_{k}
. Now slow roll parameters and cosmological observables are represented as

\displaystyle\epsilon(\phi_{k})\simeq\frac{m^{1-\frac{m}{2}}}{2^{1+m}N_{k}^{1+\frac{m}{2}}\xi},

\displaystyle\eta(\phi_{k})\simeq-\frac{2+m}{4N_{k}}+\frac{5m^{1-\frac{m}{2}}}{2^{2+m}N_{k}^{1+\frac{m}{2}}\xi}

(42)

and the spectral index and the tensor-to-scalar ratio are

\displaystyle n_{s}\simeq 1-\frac{2+m}{2N_{k}}-\frac{m^{1-\frac{m}{2}}}{2^{1+m}N_{k}^{1+\frac{m}{2}}\xi},

\displaystyle r\simeq\frac{m^{1-\frac{m}{2}}}{2^{m-3}N_{k}^{1+\frac{m}{2}}\xi}.

(43)

One should note that
r\propto 1/\xi
is highly suppressed by
\xi\gg 1
.
In Palatini case, CMB power spectrum normalization determines
\lambda/\xi
as

\displaystyle A_{s}\simeq\left.\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}\epsilon}\frac{U}{M_{P}^{4}}\right|_{\phi\simeq\phi_{k}}\simeq\frac{2^{m-3}m^{\frac{m}{2}-2}N_{k}^{\frac{m}{2}+1}\lambda}{3\pi^{2}\xi}.

(44)

The fact that
\lambda/\xi
is normalized requires a larger order of magnitude of
\xi
for the same
\lambda
compared to metric cases.

and
T_{\text{reh}}
by modeling the reheating epoch to be described with a constant equation of state
w_{\text{reh}}
.
Note that the equation of state
w(t)\equiv p/\rho
, in general, is a time dependent parameter determined by the particle physics details of the reheating process
Podolsky:2005bw
;
Lozanov:2016hid
;
Saha:2020bis
;
DiMarco:2021xzk
. In our approach following Ref.
Cook:2015vqa
, we rather take
w_{\text{reh}}
as an average value over the reheating

\displaystyle w_{\text{reh}}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{\text{reh}}}\int_{N_{k}}^{N_{k}+N_{\text{reh}}}w(N)~dN.

(45)

In this work, we allows a wide range of
w_{\text{reh}}\in[0,1/3]
. Recall that
w=0
for matter domination and
w=1/3
for radiation domination.
2
2

2

In principle, one might take
w_{\text{reh}}\in[-1,1]
depending on the detail of the model.

Now we take the standard assumptions for the late time cosmology after the reheating and get the relations for
T_{\rm reh}
and
N_{\rm reh}
. First, the e-folds for the epoch is expressed as

\displaystyle N_{\text{reh}}\equiv\ln\left(\frac{a_{\text{reh}}}{a_{e}}\right)=\frac{1}{3(1+w_{\text{reh}})}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{\text{reh}}}\right),

(46)

where
N_{\text{reh}}
represents the duration of reheating from the end of inflation,
a_{\text{reh}}
and
a_{e}
is the scale factor at the end of reheating and the end of inflation, respectively, while
\rho_{\text{reh}}
and
\rho_{e}
is the corresponding energy density at the end of reheating and the end of inflation. From
\epsilon_{H}=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=1
at the end of inflation, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{e}=\frac{3}{2}U_{e},

(47)

hence
w(t_{e})=-1/3
. Also, the radiation energy with the relativistic degrees of freedom
g_{\text{reh}}
and the temperature
T_{\text{reh}}
at the end of reheating is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{reh}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{\text{reh}}T_{\text{reh}}^{4}.

(48)

Assuming no additional entropy production after reheating, we can link
T_{\rm reh}
and the temperature of our current universe
T_{0}
as

\displaystyle T_{\text{reh}}=T_{0}\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\text{reh}}}\right)\left(\frac{g_{0}}{g_{\text{reh}}}\right)^{1/3}=\left(\frac{43}{11g_{\text{reh}}}\right)^{1/3}\left(\frac{a_{0}T_{0}}{k}\right)H_{k}e^{-N_{k}}e^{-N_{\text{reh}}}

(49)

where
g_{0}=2+\frac{7}{8}\times N_{\text{eff}}\times\frac{4}{11}
with
N_{\text{eff}}=3.046
in the SM is the relativistic degree of freedom at current universe and
g_{\text{reh}}
is that at the end of reheating, respectively.
In the second line, we take the length of e-folds of radiation dominance,
N_{\rm RD}=\ln(a_{\rm eq}/a_{\rm reh})
with the scale factor at the matter-radiation equality
a_{\rm eq}
, and the ratio
a_{0}/a_{\rm eq}=a_{0}(H_{k}/k)e^{-N_{k}}e^{-N_{\rm reh}}e^{-N_{\rm RD}}
from the pivot scale
k=a_{k}H_{k}
.

Combining the expressions, we obtain
N_{\text{reh}}
as

\displaystyle N_{\text{reh}}=\frac{4}{3(1+w_{\text{reh}})}\left[\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{45}{\pi^{2}g_{\text{reh}}}\right)+\ln\left(\frac{U_{e}^{1/4}}{H_{k}}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\ln\left(\frac{11g_{\text{reh}}}{43}\right)\right.\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\ss\left.+\ln\left(\frac{k}{a_{0}T_{0}}\right)+N_{k}+N_{\text{reh}}\right].

(50)

Taking the standard values
g_{\text{reh}}=106.75
,
k=0.05~\text{Mpc}^{-1}
,
T_{0}=2.725~\text{K}
for
w_{\text{reh}}\neq-1/3
we arrive at

\displaystyle N_{\text{reh}}=\frac{4}{(1-3w_{\text{reh}})}\left[61.6-\ln\left(\frac{U_{e}^{1/4}}{H_{k}}\right)-N_{k}\right],

(51)

and accordingly, inserting this expression to
T_{\text{reh}}
gives

\displaystyle T_{\text{reh}}=\left[\left(\frac{43}{11g_{\text{reh}}}\right)^{1/3}\frac{a_{0}T_{0}}{k}H_{k}e^{-N_{k}}\left(\frac{45U_{e}}{\pi^{2}g_{\text{reh}}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{3(1+w_{\text{reh}})}}\right]^{\frac{3(1+w_{\text{reh}})}{3w_{\text{reh}}-1}}.

(52)

4
Conclusion and Discussions

In this work, we clarified the effects of reheating to predictions of inflation models with non-minimal coupling to gravity, covering (i) both metric and Palatini formalisms, (ii) arbitrary monomial potential with asymptotic flatness in the Einstein frame, and (iii) a wide range of non-minimal coupling
\xi
depending on the power of the potential, from the consistency relations between reheating parameters and inflationary observables
n_{s}
and
r
. We also obtained a range of compatible reheating temperatures for a given equation of state parameter
w_{\text{reh}}
during reheating. This in turn can be used complementarily to further decipher the microscopic particle physics governing the reheating process for each model.

For models in both formalisms, we note that the CMB compatible
\xi
values also allow
\xi\leq 1
, with its predictions deviating from those for large
\xi\gg 1
limits. The specific dependence on
(n_{s},r)
in this particular
\xi
range differ with a definite dependence on the details of the model’s potential.

Especially, for
m=2
in the Palatini formalism, while
\xi\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
are allowed for current CMB
2\sigma
bounds, the dependence on
\xi
indicates that typically considered large
\xi\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{8})
lead to
N_{k}
smaller than 50, which is not preferred within the
1\sigma
limit of current CMB bounds. Current and future CMB observations are able to constrain the particular parameter range of
\xi
s, giving definite lower and upper bounds on the non-minimal coupling, with the constraints being stronger when taking the effects of reheating into account.

We emphasize that our results are applicable to a wider class of models, which exhibit
\alpha
-attractor behavior.
R^{2}
-driven Starobinsky inflation is also transformable to this class, giving the same inflationary dynamics. Application to multi-field inflationary models that are reducible into an effective single-field
\alpha
-attractor case is also straightforward. However, the reheating details of these equivalent inflationary classes differ depending specifically on the model that describes its microscopic nature, and predicts different values of
(T_{\text{reh}},N_{\text{reh}},w_{\text{reh}})
. Therefore, our result could be used as a generic template for the inflationary predictions of wide classes of models, breaking the degeneracy of the various models not caught solely from considering the inflation dynamics. This approach will provide valuable information on deciphering the contents of the post-inflationary universe, which may feature - but most definitely not restricted to - multiple fields encompassing spectator fields, new interactions including higher order gravitational couplings.

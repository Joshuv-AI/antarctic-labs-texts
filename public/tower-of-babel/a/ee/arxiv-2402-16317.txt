Effects of group size and noise on cooperation in population evolution of dynamic groups
Hong-Bin Zhang, Deng-Ping Tang

Abstract
In a large population, the agents temporally form one by one group of the Public Goods Game (PGG), and size of one group is randomly distributed at g ∈ [ g l , g u ] g\in[g_{l},g_{u}] . Players in it have two strategies to be chosen to cooperate, or to defect for playing the PGG. Based on this structure we investigate the evolution of cooperation in public goods games as a function of the noise level underlying strategy adoptions with the group size distribution. In the process of the evolution, the payoffs of cooperators(defectors) have related to the strategy selection implemented by the death-birth process. We consider a particular case of time scales s s between the interactions of the agents and the strategy update, and if s s =1, the time scale of one strategy update is same to that of one interaction of agents engaging in a PGG, where the cooperative behavior can be analytically solved. We find the cooperative effect is affected by the noise level and the group distribution, consequently it induces different dynamic behavior. In this model, when at the little value of the noise ( K = 0.1 K=0.1 ), if the system has different initial concentrations of cooperation, a mixed state with coexistence of cooperators and defectors, and AllC state are observed. While at other values of the noise level, the critical parameter of the multiplication factor r r is obtained, and above the critical value of r c r_{c} it induces a totally cooperative behavior, which is fitted well with analytical expressions. The larger uncertainty with the increase of the noise level is, the more he hesitate and unsure how to choose which strategy for a medium value of the multiplication factor r r . So, with a greater value of noise strength, a larger value of r r induces a cooperative behavior; however a small value of r r leads to the mixed states of cooperators and defectors, and between the two players probably randomly choose to cooperate or to defect in a sphere of unstable solution, where a hysteresis effect occurs for a bigger size distribution of group and greater uncertainty.

2
Model

We consider a large population of
M
players. Some players in the population will be randomly selected to form size-distributed groups so as to play the PGG from time to time. For example, investors hunt for yield by engaging into different competing groups. Unlike the structured population with fixed local opponents, the dynamic interaction between agents will happen in the whole system. In the PGG, the cooperators and the defectors will obtain their payoffs respectively. Suppose that there are
g

n

V_{c}(g,n)=(\frac{{rn}}{g}-1)n,

(1)

Meanwhile, the total payoff to the defectors in the group is given by:

V_{d}(g,n)=\frac{{rn}}{g}(g-n),

(2)

Where

(
>1
) is the multiplication factor. With simplicity and without loss of generality, the cost that a cooperator contributes is taken as

to simulate the strategy selection. The payoffs that the two type agents accumulated decide the reproduction probabilities of their offspring. The detailed evolutionary death-birth process is as follows. In an evolutionary time step, there are two processes: at the first one, the initial payoffs of cooperators and defectors are preseted as
V_{c}
=
V_{d}
=0, and then randomly

being the lower and upper limits of the group sizes. The second process is the death-birth process. A randomly selected agent from the whole population is removed and the two-type agents compete to birth an offspring who inherits his father’s strategy. The reproduction probability

w_{c}
of the cooperators is given by
w_{c}=1/[1+e^{(\tilde{V}_{d}-\tilde{V}_{c})/K}]
where

w_{d}
=1-

(

). So far, after the death-birth process, one evolutionary time step is over.

In this paper,

For a greater value of the noise

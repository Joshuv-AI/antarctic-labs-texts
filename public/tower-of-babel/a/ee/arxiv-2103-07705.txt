Upper and lower bounds for topological indices on unicyclic graphs Thanks: (1) Corresponding author.
Álvaro Martínez-Pérez, José M. Rodríguez (1)

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to obtain new inequalities for a large family of topological indices restricted to unicyclic graphs and to characterize the set of extremal unicyclic graphs with respect to them. This family includes variable first Zagreb, variable sum exdeg, multiplicative second Zagreb and Narumi-Katayama indices. Our main results provide upper and lower bounds for these topological indices on unicyclic graphs, fixing or not the maximum degree or the number of pendant vertices.



Keywords: Variable first Zagreb index, variable sum exdeg index, multiplicative second Zagreb index, Narumi-Katayama index, unicyclic graphs.

2010 AMS Subject Classification numbers: 05C07, 92E10.

1.
Introduction

A topological descriptor is a single number that represents a chemical structure in graph-theoretical terms via the
molecular graph.
They play a significant role in mathematical chemistry especially in the QSPR/QSAR investigations.
A topological descriptor is called a topological index if it correlates with a molecular property.
Topological indices are used to understand physicochemical properties of chemical compounds,
since they capture some properties of a molecule in a single number.
Hundreds of topological indices have been introduced and studied, starting with the
seminal work by Wiener
[
44
]
.
The
Wiener index
of
G
is defined as

W(G)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)}d(u,v),

where
\{u,v\}
runs over every pair of vertices in
G
.

Topological indices based on end-vertex degrees of edges have been
used over 40 years. Among them, several indices are recognized to be useful tools in
chemical researches.
Probably, the best know such descriptor is the Randić connectivity
index (
R
)
[
35
]
.

Two of the main successors of the Randić index are the first and second Zagreb indices,
denoted by
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively, and introduced by Gutman et al. in
[
24
]
and
[
22
]
.
They are defined as

M_{1}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}^{2},\qquad M_{2}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}d_{u}d_{v},\qquad

where
uv
denotes the edge of the graph
G
connecting the vertices
u
and
v
, and

d_{u}
is the degree of the vertex
u
.
See the recent surveys on the Zagreb indices
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
21
]
.

Along the paper, we will denote by

and

and

Miličević and Nikolić defined in
[

Note that

is

is

The concept of variable molecular descriptors was proposed as a new way of

2.
Schur convexity

Given two
n
-tuples
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
{\bf{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
with
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}
and
y_{1}\geq y_{2}\geq\cdots\geq y_{n}
, then
{\bf{x}}

majorizes

{\bf{y}}

(and we write
{\bf{x}}\succ{\bf{y}}
or
{\bf{y}}\prec{\bf{x}}
) if

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i},

for
1\leq k\leq n-1
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}.

A function
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
Schur-convex
if
\Phi({\bf{x}})\geq\Phi({\bf{y}})
for all
{\bf{x}}\succ{\bf{y}}
.
Similarly, the function is
Schur-concave
if
\Phi({\bf{x}})\leq\Phi({\bf{y}})
for all
{\bf{x}}\succ{\bf{y}}
.
We say that
\Phi
is
strictly Schur-convex
(respectively,
strictly Schur-concave
) if
\Phi({\bf{x}})>\Phi({\bf{y}})
(respectively,
\Phi({\bf{x}})<\Phi({\bf{y}})
)
for all
{\bf{x}}\succ{\bf{y}}
with
{\bf{x}}\neq{\bf{y}}
.

If

\Phi({\bf{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i}),

where
f
is a convex (respectively, concave)
function defined on a real interval, then
\Phi
is Schur-convex (respectively, Schur-concave).
If
f
is strictly convex (respectively, strictly concave), then
\Phi
is strictly Schur-convex (respectively, strictly Schur-concave).

Thus,

M_{1}^{\alpha}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{u}^{\alpha},

is strictly Schur-convex if
\alpha\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,\infty)
and strictly Schur-concave if
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

3.
Unicyclic graphs

A
unicyclic
graph is a graph containing exactly one cycle
[
25
, p.41]
.
If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices, then
G
has
n
edges.

Given
n\geq 3
, let
S_{2n}
be the set of
n
-tuples
{\bf{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})
with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=2n
.

Remark 3.1
.

Consider any unicyclic graph
G
with
n
vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n},

ordered in such a way that if

{\bf{x}}={\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is the
n
-tuple where
x_{i}
is the degree of the vertex
v_{i}
, then
x_{i}\geq x_{i+1}
for every
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
By handshaking Lemma, we have that
{\bf{x}}\in S_{2n}
.

Given any function
f:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let us define the index

I_{f}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}f(d_{u}).

Besides, if
f
takes positive values, then we can define the index

II_{f}(G)=\prod_{u\in V(G)}f(d_{u}).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices, then

(2,\dots,2)\prec{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\!\prec(n-1,2,2,1,\dots,1).

Proof.

First of all, note that
(2,\dots,2)
and
(n-1,2,2,1,\dots,1)
belong to
S_{2n}
.

Let us consider
{\bf{x}}={\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Since
G
contains a cycle, we have
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq 2
.

Seeking for a contradiction assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}<2k
for some
1\leq k\leq n-1
.
Thus,
x_{k}=1
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<2k+n-k=n+k<2n,

a contradiction.
Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq 2k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}2,

for every
1\leq k\leq n-1
and

(2,\dots,2)\prec{\bf{x}}.

Since

\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}1=n-k,

for any
3\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=2n-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\leq n+k=n-1+2+2+\sum_{i=4}^{k}1,

for every
3\leq k\leq n-1
(where, as usual, we assume the convention
\sum_{i=4}^{3}1=0
).

If
k=2
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{n}x_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{3}+\sum_{i=4}^{n}x_{i}\geq 2+\sum_{i=4}^{n}1=n-1,

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2}

\displaystyle=2n-\sum_{i=3}^{n}x_{i}\leq 2n-(n-1)=n-1+2.

If
k=1
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{2}+x_{3}+\sum_{i=3}^{n}x_{i}\geq 2+2+\sum_{i=4}^{n}1=n+1,

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=2n-\sum_{i=2}^{n}x_{i}\leq 2n-(n+1)=n-1.

Therefore,

{\bf{x}}\prec(n-1,2,2,1,\dots,1).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
3
vertices then
G=C_{3}
, and we have

I_{f}(G)=3f(2)
and
II_{f}(G)=f(2)^{3}
.
So, it suffices to deal with graphs of at lest
4
vertices.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and
f:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex function, then

nf(2)\leq I_{f}(G)\leq f(n-1)+2f(2)+(n-3)f(1),

and both inequalities are attained.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and
f:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a concave function, then

f(n-1)+2f(2)+(n-3)f(1)\leq I_{f}(G)\leq nf(2),

and both inequalities are attained.

In a similar way, we obtain the following results,
since

\log II_{f}(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\log f(d_{u}),

and the logarithm is an increasing function.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and
f:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a function such that
\log f
is convex, then

f(2)^{n}\leq II_{f}(G)\leq f(n-1)f(2)^{2}f(1)^{n-3}

and both inequalities are attained.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and
f:[1,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a function such that
\log f
is concave, then

f(n-1)f(2)^{2}f(1)^{n-3}\leq II_{f}(G)\leq f(2)^{n},

and both inequalities are attained.

Recall that a vertex in a graph is
pendant
if it has degree
1
.
An edge is
pendant
if it contains a pendant vertex.

Let
U_{n}^{3}
be the unicyclic graph obtained from the cycle

Note that

4.
Unicyclic graphs with maximum degree
\Delta

Let
S_{2n}^{\Delta}
be the set of
n
-tuples
{\bf{x}}\in S_{2n}
such that
x_{1}=\Delta
. Note that if
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta
and
{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}
is its degree sequence, then
{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\in S_{2n}^{\Delta}
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices and maximum degree 2, then
G
is the cycle
C_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
and

{\bf{y}}=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
is such that

•

y_{1}=\Delta
,

•

y_{j}=2
for every
1<j\leq n-\Delta+2
,

•

y_{j}=1
for every
n-\Delta+2<j\leq n,

then

\bf{y}\prec{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}.

Proof.

First, note that
{\bf{y}}\in S_{2n}^{\Delta}
. Suppose
{\bf{x}}={\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Since
G
contains a cycle, we have
\Delta=x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq 2
.

Seeking for a contradiction assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}<\Delta+2(k-1)
for some
2\leq k\leq n-\Delta+2
.
Thus,
x_{k}=1
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<\Delta+2k-2+n-k=\Delta+n+k-2\leq 2n,

a contradiction.
Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq\Delta+2k-2,

for every
2\leq k\leq n-\Delta+2
, and it is immediate to check that

{\bf{y}}\prec{\bf{x}}.

∎

As usual, we denote by
\lfloor t\rfloor
the lower integer part of
t\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e., the greatest integer less than or equal to
t
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
and
q=\big\lfloor\frac{n}{\Delta-1}\big\rfloor
.

If
q=1
or
n=2\Delta-2
, let
s=n-\Delta+1

and then

{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\!\prec{\bf{z}}=(\Delta,s,2,1,\dots,1).

If
q\geq 2
and
n\neq 2\Delta-2
, let
r=n-q(\Delta-1)+1
and

{\bf{z}}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})
be such that

•

z_{j}=\Delta
, for every
1\leq j\leq q
,

•

z_{j}=r
if
j=q+1
,

•

z_{j}=1
for every
q+1<j\leq n
,

and then

{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\prec\bf{z}.

Proof.

Suppose
q=1
or
n=2\Delta-2
.
Note that
(\Delta,s,2,1,\dots,1),{\bf{z}}\in S_{2n}^{\Delta}
.
Since for any
k
with
3\leq k\leq n-1
,

\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}1=n-k,

we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=2n-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\leq n+k=\Delta+s+2+\sum_{i=4}^{k}1,

for every
3\leq k\leq n-1
.
Therefore, since
\Delta=x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq 2
, it is readily seen that

{\bf{x}}\prec(\Delta,s,2,1,\dots,1).

Suppose
q\geq 2
and
n\neq 2\Delta-2
.
Note that
{\bf{z}}\in S_{2n}^{\Delta}
.
Since for any
k
with
q+1\leq k\leq n-1
,

\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}1=n-k,

we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=2n-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\leq n+k=q\Delta+r+\sum_{i=q+2}^{k}1,

for every
q+1\leq k\leq n-1
. Since
\Delta=x_{1}\geq x_{i}
for every

, it is immediate to check that

{\bf{x}}\prec{\bf{z}}.

∎

For any

and

,
let
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\Delta}
be the set of graphs obtained from the cycle

5.
Unicyclic graphs with
p
pendant vertices

Given
n\geq 3
, let
S_{2n,p}
be the set of
n
-tuples
{\bf{x}}\in S_{2n}
such that
x_{j}=1
if and only if
j>n-p
. Note that if
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n
vertices and
p
pendant vertices and
{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}
is its degree sequence, then
{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\in S_{2n,p}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
G
is a unicyclic graph with
n\geq 4
vertices and
1\leq p\leq n-3
pendant vertices,

m=\big\lfloor\frac{2n-p}{n-p}\big\rfloor
,
t=2n-p-m(n-p)
,

{\bf{a}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})
is such that

•

a_{j}=m+1
for every
1\leq j\leq t
,

•

a_{j}=m
for every
t<j\leq n-p
,

•

a_{j}=1
for every
n-p<j\leq n,

and
{\bf{b}}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n})
is such that

•

b_{1}=p+2
,

•

b_{j}=2
for every
1<j\leq n-p
,

•

b_{j}=1
for every
n-p<j\leq n,

then

{\bf{a}}\prec{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\prec{\bf{b}}.

Proof.

First, note that
{\bf{a}},{\bf{b}}\in S_{2n,p}
,
m\geq 2
and
0\leq t\leq n-p
.

Suppose
{\bf{x}}={\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in S_{2n,p}
. Seeking for a contradiction
assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}<k(m+1)

for some
k\leq t
. Then,
x_{k}\leq m
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<k(m+1)+(n-p-k)m+p\leq 2n,

a contradiction. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq k(m+1)

for every
k\leq t
.

Now assume that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}<t(m+1)+(k-t)m=km+t

for some
t<k\leq n-p
. Then,
x_{k}\leq m
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}<km+t+(n-p-k)m+p=2n

a contradiction. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq t(m+1)+(k-t)m

for every
t<k\leq n-p
and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}

for every
1\leq k<n
. Thus,

{\bf{a}}\prec{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}.

.

Since
x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{n-p}=2
and
x_{j}=1
if
j>n-p
,

\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}b_{i}

for every
1\leq k<n
.
Thus,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=2n-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\leq 2n-\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}

for every
1\leq k<n
and

{\bf{x}}_{{}_{G}}\prec{\bf{b}}.

∎

Lemma
5.1
has the following consequences.

Theorem 5.2
.

Lemma
5.1
gives the inequalities.

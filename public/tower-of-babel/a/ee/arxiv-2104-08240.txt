Nonseparable CCR algebras
Ilijas Farah, Najla Manhal

Abstract
Extending a result of the first author and Katsura, we prove that for every UHF algebra A A of infinite type, in every uncountable cardinality κ \kappa there are 2 κ 2^{\kappa} nonisomorphic approximately matricial C*-algebras with the same K 0 K_{0} group as A A . These algebras are group C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras ‘twisted’ by prescribed canonical commutation relations (CCR), and they can also be considered as nonseparable generalizations of noncommutative tori.

Concluding remarks

In the present paper we explored constructions of nonseparable CCR algebras from bicharacters on uncountable direct sums of cyclic groups. Since the set-theoretic study of infinite abelian groups has a long and distinguished history (e.g.,

Another related source of nonseparable

), already used (implicitly) in

(see

For the definitions of classifiable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and Elliott invariant see e.g.,
[
28
]
.

Question 6.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a nuclear, simple, separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\kappa
is an uncountable cardinal.

(1)

Is it true that there are
2^{\kappa}
nonisomorphic simple nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of density character
\kappa
with the Elliott invariant equal to
\Ell(A)
?

(2)

Is it true that there are at least two nonisomorphic simple nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of density character
\kappa
with the Elliott invariant equal to
\Ell(A)
?

(3)

Is it true that there exists a simple nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras of density character
\kappa
with the Elliott invariant equal to
\Ell(A)
?

If
A
is as in Question
6.1
,
D
is a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra (see
[
37
]
, but all we need is that
\bigotimes_{\aleph_{0}}D\cong D
) and
A
tensorially absorbs
D
, then Question
6.1
(
3
) has a positive answer. The algebra
A_{1}=A\otimes\bigotimes_{\kappa}D
has the same Elliott invariant as
A
and density character
\kappa
. This is because by the assumption on
D
club many separable subalgebras of
A_{1}
are isomorphic to
A\otimes D\cong A
, and by the reflection argument as in Lemma
4.1
, the Elliott invariant of
A_{1}
is equal to that of
A
. In particular, Question
6.1
(
3
) has a positive answer for every classifiable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra which absorbs the Jiang–Su algebra
{\mathcal{Z}}
. We don’t know the answer to Question
6.1
(
3
) for Rørdam’s simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with both finite and infinite projections (
[
29
]
) or for Villadsen’s algebras with perforated K-theory groups (see
[
28
]
).

If
A
tensorially absorbs both
{\mathcal{Z}}
and some full matrix algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(e.g., if
A
is a UHF algebra such that for some prime
p
every element of
K_{0}(A)
is divisible by
p
, as in the assumptions of Theorem
4.5
), then Question
6.1
(
2
) has a positive answer, as witnessed by
A\otimes\bigotimes_{\kappa}{\mathcal{Z}}
and
A\otimes\bigotimes_{\kappa}M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(a proof of this is analogous to the proofs in
[
15
, §3]
).

An alternative approach to Question
6.1
(
2
) proceeds by finding a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with the same invariant as a given
{\mathcal{Z}}
-absorbing
A
that is tensorially prime (i.e., not isomorphic to the tensor product of two infinite-dimensional
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras). This was done for some AF and some purely infinite, simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras in
[
36
, Theorem B (1) and (4)]
. In Theorem C of the same paper, a monotracial, tensorially prime, nuclear, simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with uncountable
K_{1}
-group was constructed.
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras constructed in
[
36
]
are associated with iterated wreath products of groups and they even have no nontrivial central sequences. Every infinite-dimensional separable AM algebra has nontrivial central sequences (for much stronger results see
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
). The first examples of (necessarily nonseparable) AM algebras with this property were constructed in
[
12
]
using weakenings of the Continuum Hypothesis. These algebras have the same Elliott invariant as
M_{2^{\infty}}
.

Apparently the simplest instance of Question
6.1
(
1
) not resolved by our Theorem
1
is the case of
\bigotimes_{p\text{ prime}}M_{p}(\mathbb{C})
.
Probably the most interesting case is when
A
is the Jiang–Su algebra
{\mathcal{Z}}
, whose Elliott invariant is equal to that of the complex numbers (see
[
20
]
, also
[
18
]
and
[
30
]
for most recent treatments).

A programme analogous to one given by Question
6.1
was pursued in
[
38
]
and
[
35
]
. In these papers the authors studied the variety of the invariants of counterexamples to Naimark’s Problem (using Jensen’s diamond).

Graph CCR algebras provided the first example of a nuclear, simple
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra with the property that its automorphism group did not act transitively on its pure state space (
[
8
]
, see
[
9
, §9.4]
). It is likely that the CCR algebras associated with bicharacters on uncountable abelian groups will find other uses. For example, every graph CCR algebra is a complexification of an operator algebra on a real Hilbert space, and therefore isomorphic to its opposite algebra. The only known examples of simple, nuclear,
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras not isomorphic to their opposites were constructed using Jensen’s diamond in
[
13
]
. It is possible that, with an appropriately chosen group and a bicharacter, the CCR algebras considered in this paper may provide a ZFC example.

Our examples can also be used to construct
2^{\kappa}
nonisomorphic hyperfinite II
1
factors with predual of density character
\kappa
, but this has already been proved in
[
16
]
(see also
[
36
]
). However, the II
1
factors obtained from our CCR algebras may have other interesting properties. For example, it is not known whether a hyperfinite II
1
factor not isomorphic to its opposite can be constructed in ZFC (see
[
14
]
for a construction using Jensen’s diamond).

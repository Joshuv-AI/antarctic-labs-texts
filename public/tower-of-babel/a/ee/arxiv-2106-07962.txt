Abstract
Let 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} be a finite field of order q q , a prime power integer such that q = e ​ t + 1 q=et+1 where t ≥ 1 , e ≥ 2 t\geq 1,e\geq 2 are integers. In this paper, we study cyclic codes of length n n over a non-chain ring R e , q = 𝔽 q ​ [ u ] / ⟨ u e − 1 ⟩ R_{e,q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]/\langle u^{e}-1\rangle . We define a Gray map φ \varphi and obtain many maximum-distance-separable (MDS) and optimal 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -linear codes from the Gray images of cyclic codes. Among others, we present the conditions for existence of free and non-free LCD codes. Moreover, we obtain many optimal LCD codes as the Gray images of non-free LCD codes over R e , q R_{e,q} .

Introduction

Cyclic codes are one of the most important classes of linear codes and they possess rich algebraic properties. A cyclic code of length
n
over a ring (or field)
R
is defined as an
R
-submodule (resp. subspace) of
R^{n}
closed under the cyclic shift. Over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
they can be easily classified via ideals of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
but usually their structure is non-trivial for the case of finite rings. The algebraic structure such as generator polynomials, generator matrices are useful to obtain their parameters like rank or minimum distances. In the field case the standard notation
[n,k,d]
is used to represent a linear code of length
n
, dimension
k
and distance
d
. There are many well-known theoretical relations among these parameters, and some of them are Singleton bound, Plotkin bound, Gilbert-Varshamov bound, Hamming bound and Griesmer bound, etc. A code attaining any of these bounds is called maximum-distance-separable (MDS) for that bound. In 1997, Kanwar and Lopez-Permouth
[
23
]
determined the structure of cyclic codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
while Pless et al.
[
31
]
obtained self-dual cyclic codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. Later, in 1999, Bonnecaze and Udaya
[
6
]
studied cyclic codes over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{2}+u\mathbb{F}_{2},u^{2}=0
. Also, Blackford
[
5
]
classified all cyclic codes of length
2n
(
n
is odd integer) over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. After that, Abualrub et al.
[
2
]
discussed these codes of length
2^{e}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. Also, in a more general setup, cyclic and negacyclic codes over finite chain rings are extensively studied in
[
10
]
. Later, many papers contributed significant tools and results to obtain the structural properties of these codes over finite rings, see
[
1
,
3
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
37
]
.

Linear complementary dual (shortly LCD) codes were introduced by Massey
[
29
]
, in 1992. An LCD code is defined as a linear code having a trivial intersection with its dual code. These codes have gained serious attention due to their recent successful application in cryptography
[
8
]
. In 2016, Carlet and Guilley
[
8
]
shown that LCD codes with possibly large minimum distances prevent the resistance against side-channel attacks in a cryptosystem. In addition, these codes have a much simpler nearest-codeword decoding algorithm than linear codes, and are widely used in communications systems, data storage, and consumer electronics. In 1994, Yang and Massey
[
35
]
completely classified cyclic LCD codes over finite fields. Later, Sendrier
[
32
]
shown that LCD codes attain the Gilbert-Varshamov bound. In 2009 Esmaeili and Yari
[
13
]
studied quasi-cyclic LCD codes. Later, Li et al.
[
24
]
obtained many optimal and good LCD codes over finite fields. Also, Dougherty et al.
[
11
]
discussed LCD codes along with the linear programming bounds. Further, Galvez et al.
[
14
]
determined many bounds on LCD codes over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Recently, LCD codes over finite chain rings are extensively studied in
[
12
,
25
,
26
]
. They have determined necessary and sufficient conditions of a linear code to be an LCD code and show the existence of asymptotically good LCD codes over finite chain rings. Therefore, it is still open to investigate the structure of LCD codes over finite commutative non-chain rings. Very recently, these codes have been explored over a non-chain ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q}+v\mathbb{F}_{q}
by Yadav et al.
[
34
]
. Here, we consider a class of finite commutative non-chain rings with unity
R_{e,q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]/\langle u^{e}-1\rangle
where
q=et+1
and
e\geq 2,t\geq 1
are integers. Then
R_{e,q}
is a semi-local Frobenius ring of order
q^{e}
. The main concern of this paper is to obtain the structure of cyclic and LCD codes of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
and to construct optimal linear (or LCD) codes from them. With the help of a new Gray map
\varphi
, we derive many optimal and MDS codes from cyclic codes over
R_{e,q}
. Further, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions of cyclic codes to LCD codes. Finally, many optimal LCD codes are presented to validate our obtained results.

Preliminary

Let
p
be an odd prime and
q=p^{m}
such that
q=et+1
where
t,m\geq 1,e\geq 2
are integers. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field of order
q
and denote
R_{e,q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]/\langle u^{e}-1\rangle
. Then it is easy to check that
R_{e,q}
is a finite commutative semi-local, Frobenius and non-chain ring with unity and characteristic
p
. Also
R_{e,q}
can be written as
R_{e,q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}+u\mathbb{F}_{q}+u^{2}\mathbb{F}_{p}+\dots+u^{e-1}\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
u^{e}=1
, thus an arbitrary element
r\in R_{e,q}
can be written as
r=a_{0}+ua_{1}+u^{2}a_{2}+\dots+u^{e-1}a_{e-1}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
0\leq i\leq e-1
. Recall that a nonempty subset
\mathcal{C}\subseteq R^{n}_{e,q}
is said to be a
linear code
of length
n
if it is an
R_{e,q}
-submodule of
R^{n}_{e,q}
and elements in
\mathcal{C}
are called codewords. Also, the rank of a code
\mathcal{C}
is the minimum number of generators for
\mathcal{C}
and the free rank is the rank of
\mathcal{C}
when it is a free module over
R_{e,q}
. A typical linear code of length
n
and rank
k
is denoted by
[n,k]
. The inner product between two vectors
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}),y=(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\in R_{e,q}^{n}
is defined as
x\cdot y=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x_{i}y_{i}
. The orthogonal of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
and defined by
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\{x\in R_{e,q}^{n}\mid x\cdot y=0,~\forall~y\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Note that the orthogonal code
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is also a linear code of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
. The linear code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
self-orthogonal
if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
and
self-dual
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.

Since
e\mid(q-1)
, the polynomial
u^{e}-1
uniquely splits into linear factors over the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]
. Suppose

\displaystyle u^{e}-1=\prod_{i=1}^{e}(u-\alpha_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[u].

Let
G_{i}=u-\alpha_{i}
and
\widehat{G}_{i}=\frac{(u^{e}-1)}{G_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq e
. Since
\gcd(G_{i},\widehat{G}_{i})=1
, there exist
z_{i},h_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]
such that
z_{i}G_{i}+h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}=1
, for
1\leq i\leq e
.
Let
\mu_{i}=h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq e
.

Lemma 1
.

Assuming the same notations discussed above and mod
(u^{e}-1)
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}=1,
and

\mu_{i}\mu_{j}=\begin{cases}\mu_{i},&\text{if $i=j$}\\
0,&\text{if $i\neq j$.}\end{cases}

Therefore,
\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\dots,\mu_{e}\}
is a set of orthogonal idempotent elements in
R_{e,q}
.

Proof.

For
i\neq j
, we have
(u-\alpha_{i})\mid h_{j}\widehat{G}_{j}
and
(u-\alpha_{j})\mid h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}
. Thus,
\mu_{i}\mu_{j}=h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}h_{j}\widehat{G}_{j}=0\pmod{u^{e}-1}
. To prove
\mu_{i}^{2}=\mu_{i}
, it is enough to show
(u-\alpha_{s})\mid(\mu_{i}^{2}-\mu_{i})
for all
s=1,2,\dots,e
. For
i\neq s
, it follows from the construction. If
i=s
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{i}^{2}-\mu_{i}

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}(\mu_{i}-1)

\displaystyle=h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}(h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}-1)

\displaystyle=z_{i}G_{i}(z_{i}G_{i}-1),~(\text{since}~z_{i}G_{i}+h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i}=1).

Therefore,
(u-\alpha_{i})\mid(\mu_{i}^{2}-\mu_{i})
, and hence
\mu_{i}^{2}=\mu_{i}
.
Again,

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{e}(1-\mu_{i})~(\text{since}~\mu_{i}^{2}=\mu_{i},\mu_{i}\mu_{j}=0,i\neq j)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{e}(1-h_{i}\widehat{G}_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{e}z_{i}G_{i}

\displaystyle=0~\pmod{u^{e}-1}.

Hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}=1\pmod{u^{e}-1}
.
∎

Taking into account Lemma
1
and the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle R_{e,q}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}R_{e,q}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Therefore, any element
r\in R_{e,q}
can be uniquely expressed as

. Then the units of

can be classified by the result that

is a unit in

if and only if

is a unit in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for
1\leq i\leq e
.

Let
GL_{e}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the set of all
e\times e
invertible matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
M\in GL_{e}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
MM^{T}=\gamma I_{e}
, where
M^{T}
is the transpose matrix of

,
I_{e}
is the identity matrix of order
e
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
q
is odd, so for the existence of such matrix

, we restrict

such that
\gamma^{e}
is a square in

(see [
[
17
]

\varphi:R_{e,q}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{e}
by

\displaystyle\varphi(r)=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{e})M=\boldsymbol{r}M,

(1)

where
r=\sum_{i=1}^{e}s_{i}\mu_{i}\in R_{e,q}
, for
s_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},1\leq i\leq e
. Here, we use
\boldsymbol{r}
for the vector
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{e})
. The map
\varphi
can be extended to
R_{e,q}^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi:R_{e,q}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{en}

by

\displaystyle\varphi(r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{n-1})=

\displaystyle[(s_{0,1},s_{0,2},\dots,s_{0,e})M,(s_{1,1},s_{1,2},\dots,s_{1,e})M,\dots,(s_{n-1,1},s_{n-1,2},\dots,s_{n-1,e})M]

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{r_{0}}M,\boldsymbol{r_{1}}M,\dots,\boldsymbol{r_{n-1}}M),

(2)

where
r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{e}s_{i,j}\mu_{j}
, for
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

The Hamming weight
w_{H}(c)
is the number of non-zero components of the codeword
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
and the distance between two codewords is given by
d_{H}(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})=w_{H}(c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime})
. The Hamming distance of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as
d_{H}(\mathcal{C})=\min\{d_{H}(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})\mid c^{\prime}\neq c^{\prime\prime},
for all
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\}
. The Gray weight of any element

is
w_{G}(r)=w_{H}(\varphi(r))
and the Gray weight for
\overline{r}=(r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{n-1})\in R_{e,q}^{n}
is
w_{G}(\overline{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}w_{G}(r_{i})
. The Gray distance between any two codewords

is defined as
d_{G}(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})=w_{G}(c^{\prime}-c^{\prime\prime})
and the Gray distance of
\mathcal{C}
is
d_{G}(\mathcal{C})=\min\{d_{G}(c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime})\mid c^{\prime}\neq c^{\prime\prime},c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\}
. Now, we present an example for

. On the other side, let

. Then
r\cdot a_{1}=0
for all
a_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
. Let
z=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}a_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Now,
\mu_{1}r\cdot z=\mu_{1}a_{1}\cdot r=0
, which implies
\mu_{1}r\in\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
. From the unique expression of
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, we have
r\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Hence,
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\perp}=\mathcal{S}_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq e
. Consequently,
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\perp}
.

2.

Further,
\mathcal{C}
is self-dual if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\perp}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq e
.

∎

3
Cyclic codes over
R_{e,q}

In this section, we determine the structure of cyclic codes of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
and use these results to obtain LCD codes in the subsequent section. Note that in
[
27
]
the authors considered this type of codes as codes over an affine algebras with a finite commutative chain coefficient ring where the affine algebra is
R_{e,q}
. However, we will construct them from scratch based on the results before so we get an explicit description of the duality and LCD condition based on Theorem
2
above.

Definition 3.1
.

A linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
is said to be a cyclic code if for any
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
\sigma(c):=(c_{n-1},c_{0},\dots,c_{n-2})\in\mathcal{C}
. Here,
\sigma
is called the cyclic shift operator.

We identify each codeword
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
by a polynomial
c(x)\in R_{e,q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
under the correspondence
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\longmapsto c(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\dots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\pmod{x^{n}-1}
. Under this identification, we have the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a linear code of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is a cyclic code if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is an ideal of the ring
R_{e,q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
. Let
a(x)\in R_{e,q}[x]
and
c(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\dots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then
xc(x)=c_{0}x+c_{1}x^{2}+\dots+c_{n-2}x^{n-1}+c_{n-1}x^{n}=c_{n-1}+c_{0}x+c_{1}x^{2}+\dots+c_{n-2}x^{n-1}{\pmod{x^{n}-1}}=\sigma(c)\in\mathcal{C}
. In similar manner, for any positive integer
i\geq 2
, we have
x^{i}c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is an
R_{e,q}
-submodule,
a(x)c(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}
is an ideal of
R_{e,q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}
be an ideal of
R_{e,q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. Let
c=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
. Now,
\sigma(c)=c_{n-1}+c_{0}x+c_{1}x^{2}+\dots+c_{n-2}x^{n-1}=xc(x)\in\mathcal{C}
. Hence
\mathcal{C}
is a cyclic code of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

[
19
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over

and

where

and

is the reciprocal polynomial of

Theorem 5
.

A linear code
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
of length

is cyclic if and only if

over

, for

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}
be a cyclic code of length

over
R_{e,q}
. Let
a^{i}=(a_{0}^{i},a_{1}^{i},\dots,a_{n-1}^{i})\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
for

for

\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
1\leq i\leq e
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be a cyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
1\leq i\leq e

∎

4.1
Existence of non-free LCD codes over
R_{e,q}

In
[
4
]
, the authors proved that there does not exist any non-free LCD codes over finite commutative local Frobenius ring, or in other words, every LCD code over commutative local Frobenius ring is free. Also, the authors gave the conditions in case of a non-local ring for a code to be LCD. Here we show that both free and non-free LCD cyclic codes exist over the commutative semi-local Frobenius ring
R_{e,q}
in terms of the degree of the polynomials defining them. To do so we use the following result.

Lemma 5
.

The element
\sum_{i\in T}\mu_{i}
is a zero divisor in
R_{e,q}
, for any non-trivial subset
T
of
\{1,2,\dots,e\}
.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{i}(1-\mu_{i})=0
, for
1\leq i\leq e
, then
\sum_{i\in T}\mu_{i}\prod_{i\in T}(1-\mu_{i})=0
, for any non-trivial subset
T
of
\{1,2,\dots,e\}
. Also, as
\mu_{i}\mu_{j}=0
, for
i\neq j
, then
\prod_{i\in T}(1-\mu_{i})=1-(\sum_{i\in T}\mu_{i})\neq 0
. Hence the result.
∎

As we discussed in the previous section a cyclic code of length
n
is given by
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
where
g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}g_{i}(x)
. Since
g_{i}(x)
is a monic polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,
g(x)
is monic if the degrees of all polynomials
g_{i}(x)
, for
1\leq i\leq e
are same. Also, if the degrees of all polynomials
g_{i}(x)
are not same, then by Lemma
5
,
g(x)
is a polynomial having a zero divisor leading coefficient. Therefore,
g(x)
is a non-monic polynomial. Now, for the case when
g(x)
is monic, by using the division algorithm on
R_{e,q}[x]
and a similar argument of Theorem 4 in
[
33
]
, we conclude that
\mathcal{C}
is free. On the other hand, when
g(x)
is non-monic, if possible, let us assume that
\mathcal{C}
is free. Then by similar argument of Theorem 5 in
[
33
]
, we have a monic polynomial
g^{\prime}(x)\in R_{e,q}[x]
such that
\mathcal{C}=\langle g^{\prime}(x)\rangle
and
g^{\prime}(x)\mid x^{n}-1
. Since
g(x)
is non-monic, this contradicts the fact that
g(x)
is the unique polynomial in
R_{e,q}[x]
such that
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
and
g(x)\mid x^{n}-1
. Therefore, under above discussion, we have the following results.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
R_{e,q}
where
g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mu_{i}g_{i}(x)
.

1.

\mathcal{C}
is free if the degrees of
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),\dots,g_{e}(x)
are same.

2.

\mathcal{C}
is non-free if the degrees of
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),\dots,g_{e}(x)
are not same.

Unlike commutative local Frobenius ring, both free and non-free LCD codes exist over
R_{e,q}
and can be constructed by following Corollary
4.1
, Corollary
4.2
and Theorem
10
. Now, we discuss some examples of non-free LCD codes over
R_{e,q}
which present many optimal LCD codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as the Gray images of these codes.

Example 5
.

Let
e=2,q=2\cdot 2+1=5,n=6
and
R_{2,5}=\mathbb{F}_{5}[u]/\langle u^{2}-1\rangle
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cyclic code of length
6
over
R_{2,5}
. Now,

\displaystyle x^{6}-1=(x+1)(x+4)(x^{2}+x+1)(x^{2}+4x+1)\in\mathbb{F}_{5}[x].

Let
g_{1}(x)=x+4
and
g_{2}(x)=(x+1)(x^{2}+x+1)=x^{3}+2x^{2}+2x+1
. Since
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)
are self-reciprocal polynomials, by Corollary
4.2
and Theorem
10
, we have
\mathcal{C}=\langle\mu_{1}g_{1}(x)+\mu_{2}g_{2}(x)\rangle
is a non-free LCD code of length
6
over
R_{2,5}
. Let

M=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&4\\
1&1\end{array}}\right]\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5}),

satisfying
MM^{T}=2I_{2}
. Then by Theorem
9
, the Gray image
\varphi(\mathcal{C})
is an LCD code with parameters
[12,8,4]
and it is an optimal code according to
[
16
]
.

Example 6
.

Let
e=3,q=3\cdot 2+1=7,n=3
and
R_{3,7}=\mathbb{F}_{7}[u]/\langle u^{3}-1\rangle
. Now,

\displaystyle x^{3}-1=(x+3)(x+5)(x+6)\in\mathbb{F}_{7}[x].

Let
g_{1}(x)=x+6,g_{2}(x)=1
and
g_{3}(x)=(x+2)(x+3)=x^{2}+x+1
. Then
\mathcal{C}=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mu_{i}g_{i}(x)\rangle
is a cyclic code of length
3
over
R_{3,7}
. Since
\gcd(4,7)=1
and
g_{i}(x)
is self-reciprocal polynomial for
i=1,2
, by Corollary
4.2
and Theorem
10
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a non-free LCD code. Again, let

M=\left[{\begin{array}[]{cccc}2&1&2\\
5&2&1\\
1&2&5\\
\end{array}}\right]\in GL_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{7}),

satisfying
MM^{T}=2I_{3}
. Then by Theorem
9
, the Gray image
\varphi(\mathcal{C})
is an LCD code with parameters
[9,6,3]
, which is an optimal code by
[
16
]
.

Example 7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\langle\mu_{1}g_{1}(x)+\mu_{2}g_{2}(x)\rangle
be a non-free LCD code of length
n
over
R_{2,q}
where
\gcd(n,q)=1
and
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)
are self-reciprocal factors of
x^{n}-1
. Let

M=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&-1\\
1&1\end{array}}\right]\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

satisfying
MM^{T}=2I_{2}
. Again, by Theorem
9
,
\varphi(\mathcal{C})
is an LCD code. In Table
4
, we present generator polynomials
g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)
in the
3^{rd}
and
4^{th}
column, respectively. Also, their
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-images are included in the
5^{th}
column which presents many optimal codes as per
[
16
]
.

Remark 1
.

1.

In order to present Tables
2
,
3
and
4
briefly, we write the vector consisting coefficients of the polynomial
g_{i}(x)
in decreasing order. For example, we write the vector
(1,3,0,2,4)
to represent the polynomial
x^{4}+3x^{3}+2x+4
.

2.

As we can see in Tables
2
and
4
, the codes of parameters
[16,11,4],[20,15,4],[24,19,4],[32,27,4],[12,8,3],

satisfy
n-k+1-d=2
while the remaining codes satisfy
n-k+1-d=1
. Therefore, although these codes are not MDS (with respect to the singleton bound) they are very close to be MDS, having good parameters.

5
Conclusion

Here, we have obtained many MDS and optimal codes from the Gray images of both cyclic and LCD codes over
R_{e,q}
. To compute the parameters of codes, we have used the Magma computation system
[
7
]
. Apart from the obtained parameters, one can further find more optimal codes using the derived results and the Gray map
\varphi
. The article justifies the fact that LCD codes over non-chain rings are a useful tool to obtain optimal LCD codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We hope our work would motivate researchers to study LCD codes over other non-chain rings to explore more good LCD codes in the future.

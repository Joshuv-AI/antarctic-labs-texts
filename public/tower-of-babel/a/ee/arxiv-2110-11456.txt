A CutFEM divergence–free discretization for the Stokes problem Thanks: The first two authors were supported in part by NSF grant no. DMS-2011733. The third author was supported in part by NSF grants DMS-1953535 and DMS-2011444.
Haoran Liu, Michael Neilan, Maxim Olshanskii

Abstract
. We construct and analyze a CutFEM discretization for the Stokes problem based on the Scott-Vogelius pair. The discrete piecewise polynomial spaces are defined on macro-element triangulations which are not fitted to the smooth physical domain. Boundary conditions are imposed via penalization through the help of a Nitsche-type discretization, whereas stability with respect to small and anisotropic cuts of the bulk elements is ensured by adding local ghost penalty stabilization terms. We show stability of the scheme as well as a divergence–free property of the discrete velocity outside an O ⁡ ( h ) O(h) neighborhood of the boundary. To mitigate the error caused by the violation of the divergence-free condition, we introduce local grad-div stabilization. The error analysis shows that the grad-div parameter can scale like O ⁡ ( h − 1 ) O(h^{-1}) , allowing a rather heavy penalty for the violation of mass conservation, while still ensuring optimal order error estimates.

1.
Introduction

Originating from first principles, mass conservation is a favorable property for numerical solutions to equations governing the motion of fluids. For incompressible viscous fluids there is a recent effort in developing Galerkin methods that conserve the mass through enforcing the discrete velocity to be
divergence–free
[
11
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
36
]
. In many cases however, the exactly divergence–free approximations come at a price of high-order spaces or confined to certain triangulations.
This extra complexity makes it difficult, if at all possible, to apply them for modeling of flows with additional features such as propagating interfaces and free surfaces exhibiting large deformations.
One way to address the difficulty of computing flows with finite elements in heavily deforming volumes is to uncouple the volume triangulation from interface/boundary tracking. For sharp interface representations (e.g. by a level-set method
[
1
]
in contrast to diffuse-interface approach
[
2
]
) this suggests using geometrically unfitted finite element (FE) methods, e.g. XFEM
[
35
]
or cutFEM
[
8
]
. While unfitted variants of equal order pressure-velocity, Taylor–Hood and several other well-known finite element methods were studied recently
[
4
,
7
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
, unfitted mass-conserving elements for primitive-variable formulations of the Stokes or incompressible Navier-Stokes equations are not well developed yet.

This brings our attention to the Scott-Vogelius (SV) finite element pair
[
32
]
of continuous polynomial elements of degree
k
for velocity and discontinuous polynomial elements of degree
(k-1)
for pressure. The SV element is known to be inf-sup stable
[
15
]
for shape-regular triangulations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

k\geq 4
provided the triangulation does not contain nearly singular vertices
[
32
,
19
]
.
For
\mathbb{R}^{3}
the situation is more subtle and for a specific structured family of triangulations,
stability is known for
k\geq 6

[
37
]
.
On triangulations resulting from a barycenteric (or Clough-Tocher/Alfeld) refinement of a macro triangulation the stability, however, follows in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
k\geq d

[
3
,
36
,
17
]
.

Since the divergence of any FE velocity field belong to the pressure FE space, a standard mixed formulation of the Stokes problem using the SV element delivers pointwise divergence-free solutions, providing us with an example of a stable and relatively simple mass conserving finite element method.

A geometrically unfitted variant of the SV finite element in two dimensions was considered for the first time in
[
29
]
. In that paper, the authors
constructed a boundary correction scheme, where the Stokes problem is solved
on a strict interior domain, and boundary data is transferred, within a Nitsche-type formulation, via a Taylor expansion.
Here we take a different approach: A boundary or interface condition on a surface cutting through a background mesh is imposed with the help of the Nitsche method
[
22
]
, while stability with respect to small and anisotropic cuts of the bulk elements is ensured by adding local ghost penalty stabilization terms. This renders our approach as the cut-Scott-Vogelius finite element method. The stability of pressure for cut elements requires an additional ghost penalty term defined on a thin strip of elements in a proximity of the boundary. Since this additional stabilizing term alters the divergence-condition in the mixed formulation, the resulting velocity is not divergence–free, strictly speaking. Nevertheless, we show that pointwise mass conservation holds in the volume occupied by the fluid except in an
O(h)
strip. Furthermore, to minimize the error caused by the violation of the divergence–free condition, we introduce local grad-div stabilization
[
31
]
. Our analysis reveals that the grad-div parameter can scale with
h^{-1}
, allowing a rather heavy penalty for the violation of
{\rm div}\bm{u}=0
in the strip and still ensuring an optimal order error estimate.

The remainder of paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is preparatory and collects necessary preliminaries on meshing and FE spaces definitions. It proceeds with the formulation of finite element method and the proof of an internal mass-conservation property. Section
3
addresses stability of the finite element formulation. In section
4
we prove our main result about the convergence of the method.
We extend our method and analysis to two-dimensional Powell-Sabin splits in section
5
, and give
results of a few numerical examples in section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries and Finite Element Method

Consider the Stokes problem on a bounded, contractible, and open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with smooth boundary:

(2.1a)

\displaystyle-{\Delta\bm{u}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle={\bm{f}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.1b)

\displaystyle{\rm div}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle={0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma:=\partial\Omega.

To formulate an unfitted finite element method for (
2.1
) we embed
\Omega
into an open, polytopal domain
S
, i.e.

\bar{\Omega}\subset S
, and let

\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a simplicial shape-regular triangulation of
S
.
For simplicity, we assume that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is quasi-uniform.
We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
the resulting Clough-Tocher/Alfeld (CT) triangulation
obtained by connecting the vertices of each element in
\mathcal{T}_{h}

with its barycenter
[
28
,
14
]
.
Let

(2.2)

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\subset\Omega\},\qquad\Omega_{h}^{i}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}\bar{T}\Big)

be the set of interior simplices and interior domain, respectively.
We then define the analogous set with respect to the CT refinement (cf. Figure
1
):

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}:=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}:\ K\subset T,\ \exists T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}\}.

Left: Example of an interior Clough-Tocher mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}
(blue)
and boundary Clough-Tocher mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
(red).
Right: The same mesh, but with
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{ct,\Gamma}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
(green)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}
(blue).

Remark 2.1
.

Note that

\Omega_{h}^{i}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}}\bar{K}\Big),

since we include in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}
only those micro-triangles which belong to internal macro-triangles, there can be
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
which are strictly inside
\Omega
but not in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}
, i.e., the inclusion

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}\subset\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}:K\subset\Omega\}

is generally strict.

On the interior domain, we define
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
to be
the set of
(d-1)
-dimensional interior faces of the unrefined triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
;
that is,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
provided there exists two distinct simplices
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}

such that
F=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
.
We also let

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:{\rm meas}_{d-1}(T\cap\Gamma)>0\},\qquad\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\bar{T}\Big)

to be the set of simplices that cut through the interface
\Gamma
and the corresponding domain, respectively.

Define

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}\ {\rm or}\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}\},\qquad\Omega_{h}^{e}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}}\bar{T}\Big)

to be the set of triangles that either are interior or cut through the interface
\Gamma
and the corresponding domain, respectively.
We refer to these quantities as the exterior triangulation and exterior domain, respectively.
The analogous sets with respect to the CT refinement are given by

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}:=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}:\ K\subset T,\ \exists T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}\},

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}:=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}:\ K\subset T,\ \exists T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}\}.

Note that now we have

\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\bar{K}\Big),\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{h}^{e}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}}\bar{K}\Big).

Remark 2.2
.

Note that, in general, there exists

K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
such that
\bar{K}\cap\bar{\Omega}=\emptyset
.
Consequently, in the finite element method presented below,
there exists active basis functions with support strictly outside the physical domain
\Omega
.

We define the sets of faces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle:=\{F:\ F\ {\rm is\ a\ face\ in}\ \mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma},\ F\not\subset\partial\Omega_{h}^{e}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{e}

\displaystyle:=\{F:\ F\ {\rm is\ an\ interior\ face\ in}\ \mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}

\displaystyle:=\{F:\ F\ {\rm is\ a\ face\ in}\ \mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma},\ F\not\subset\partial\Omega_{h}^{e}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,e}

\displaystyle:=\{F:\ F\ {\rm is\ an\ interior\ face\ in}\ \mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}\}.

For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
, we define
K_{\Gamma}=\overline{K}\cap\Gamma
, so that
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}|K_{\Gamma}|=|\Gamma|
.
For a simplex
K
, we set
h_{K}={\rm diam}(h_{K})
.
Note that, because of the quasi-uniformity and shape-regularity assumption, there holds

h_{K}\approx h:=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}

and
h_{F}\approx h
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,e}
.
We denote by
{\bm{n}}
an outward normal of a domain which will be clear from its context.
The constant
C
(with or without subscripts) will denote a generic positive constant that is independent of
h
,
how the boundary
\Gamma
cuts the mesh, or any method-dependent parameters.

For some triangulation
\mathcal{S}_{h}
(e.g.,
\mathcal{S}_{h}=\mathcal{T}^{ct,e}_{h}
),
we define the polynomial spaces:

\displaystyle\EuScript{P}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})=\{v\in L^{2}(D_{h}):\ v|_{K}\in\EuScript{P}_{k}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{S}_{h}\},\quad

\displaystyle\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})=\EuScript{P}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})\cap L^{2}_{0}(D_{h}),

\displaystyle\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{k}^{c}(\mathcal{S}_{h})=[\EuScript{P}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})\cap H^{1}(D_{h})]^{d},\quad

\displaystyle\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k}^{c}(\mathcal{S}_{h})=\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{k}^{c}(\mathcal{S}_{h})\cap\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(D_{h}),

where
D_{h}={\rm int}(\cup_{K\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}\bar{K})
, and
\EuScript{P}_{k}(K)
is the space of scalar polynomials of degree
\leq k
with domain
K
.
For an integer
k\geq d
, define the finite element spaces with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}
:

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{{\bm{v}\in\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{k}^{c}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e})},\ \int_{\partial\Omega_{h}^{i}}\bm{v}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0\},\qquad Q_{h}=\{{q\in\EuScript{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e})},\ \int_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}q=0\},

and the analogous spaces with respect to the interior mesh

\displaystyle{\bm{V}^{i}_{h}=\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}^{c}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}),\qquad Q_{h}^{i}=\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}).}

The definitions of the spaces imply that the divergence maps
\bm{V}_{h}
into

Q_{h}
. Likewise, the divergence maps
\bm{V}_{h}^{i}
into
Q_{h}^{i}
.

2.1.
Finite Element Method

Here, we will define a modified version of the finite element method given in
[
18
, Section 3.2]

based on the Scott–Vogelius pair.
First we define the mesh-dependent bilinear form
with grad-div stabilization:

(2.3)

a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v})+\gamma({\rm div}\bm{u},{\rm div}\bm{v})+s_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})+\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})+\eta j_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v}),

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product over
\Omega
,

\gamma\geq 0
is the grad-div parameter, and

\eta>0
is a Nitsche-type penalty parameter,

\displaystyle s_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}(({\bm{n}}^{\intercal}\nabla\bm{u})\cdot\bm{v}+({\bm{n}}^{\intercal}\nabla\bm{v})\cdot\bm{u}),

\displaystyle j_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\frac{1}{h_{K}}\int_{K_{\Gamma}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{v},

\displaystyle\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}h_{F}^{2\ell-1}\int_{F}[\partial^{\ell}_{n}\bm{u}][\partial^{\ell}_{n}\bm{v}],

where we recall
K_{\Gamma}=\bar{K}\cap\Gamma
.
Here,
\partial_{n}^{\ell}\bm{v}
denotes the derivative of order
\ell
of
\bm{v}
in the direction
\bm{v}
,
and
[\bm{w}]|_{F}
denotes the jump of a function
\bm{w}
across
F
.
The continuity equations are discretized via the bilinear form

(2.4)

b(p,\bm{v}):=-(p,{\rm div}\bm{v})+\int_{\Gamma}(\bm{v}\cdot{\bm{n}})p.

The finite element method reads:
find
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\Big\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+b(p_{h},\bm{v}_{h})=(\bm{f},\bm{v}_{h}),\\
b(q_{h},\bm{u}_{h})-{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}J_{h}(p_{h},q_{h})=0\end{array}

for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, where

J_{h}(q,p)=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}h_{F}^{2\ell+1}\int_{F}[\partial^{\ell}_{n}q][\partial^{\ell}_{n}p].

Remark 2.3
.

Compared to other CutFEM discretizations
[
7
,
8
,
23
,
18
]
,
we have modified the above finite element method by modifying the terms
\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
, and
J_{h}(q,p).

In particular, for
\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
, and
J_{h}(q,p)
, instead of summing over all faces
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, we are now summing over all faces
F
from
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}.

Such faces may be completely outside the physical domain
\Omega
.

Likewise, for the term
j_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
, instead of summing over all triangles
T
from
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}
, we are now summing over all triangles
K
from
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
. However, since
\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}K_{\Gamma}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}T_{\Gamma}
,
j_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
is equivalent to the analogous term in, e.g.,
[
18
]
. Compared to the previous work on unfitted Stokes elements, we also introduce the grad-div stabilization. We show later that it effectively acts only on a narrow boundary strip and allows the control of mass-conservation loss due to ghost penalty stabilization. This mechanism may be of help to boost the accuracy for other unfitted Stokes FE methods.

Remark 2.4
.

An accurate computation of integrals over cut elements and
\Gamma
as they appear in the definition of the bilinear forms is not always feasible. One way to handle this is to introduce a polyhedral approximation of
\Omega
allowing for standard quadrature rules both on cut and uncut elements. Such an approximation, however, leads to second order geometric consistency error which is suboptimal for Scott-Vogelius elements. To ensure a geometric error of the same order or higher than the finite element approximation error, we define numerical quadrature rules for cut elements and boundary integrals using the isoparametric approach proposed in
[
27
]
.

2.2.
Divergence–free property

Notice that, in contrast to the domain-fitted Scott-Vogelius FEM,
the method (
2.1
) does not produce exactly divergence-free solution
due to the stability term
J_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
in the discrete continuity equations.
However, we show that the discrete velocity solution
\bm{u}_{h}
is divergence-free on a mesh-dependent interior domain.

We define the set of all simplices in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}

together with those in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}
that are touching these simplices (cf. Figure
1
):

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}:\ {\rm meas}_{d-1}(\bar{K}\cap\bar{K}^{\prime})>0\ \exists K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}\}.

This set’s complement is given by an interior set of elements:

(2.7)

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i},

and we define the domains

\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\bar{K}\Big),\qquad\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}={\rm Int}\Big(\bigcup_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}}\bar{K}\Big).

Lemma 2.5
.

(Divergence-free property) Suppose that
\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
satisfies (
2.1
).
Then
{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=0
on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}
.

Proof.

We show the result in four steps.

(1)

Fix
K_{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}
, and set

\displaystyle q_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{on }\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma},\\
-(|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|)/|K_{*}|&\text{on }K_{*},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We then have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}q_{1}=\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}\setminus\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}}q_{1}+\int_{K_{*}}q_{1}=(|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|)+|K_{*}|\Big(-(|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|)/|K_{*}|\Big)=0.

Thus,
q_{1}\in Q_{h}
.
We also have
J(p_{h},q_{1})=0
because
q_{1}
is constant on
\widetilde{\Omega}^{ct,\Gamma}_{h}
.
It then follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle-\frac{(|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|)}{|K_{*}|}\int_{K_{*}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}+\int_{\Omega\cap\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}-\int_{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0,

and therefore

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|K_{*}|}\int_{K_{*}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|}\Big(\int_{\Omega\cap\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}.

(2)

Fix
K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}\backslash\{K_{*}\}
, and set

\displaystyle q_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{on }K,\\
-\frac{|K|}{|K_{*}|}&\text{on }K_{*},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
q_{2}\in Q_{h}
, and by (
2.1
), we conclude

\int_{K}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=\frac{|K|}{|K_{*}|}\int_{K_{*}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}.

We then sum this expression over
K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}
to conclude

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=\frac{|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}|}{|K_{*}|}\int_{K_{*}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}.

(3)

We combine (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=-\frac{|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}|}{|\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}|-|\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}|}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}},

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=0.

Using (
2.8
), and noting that
K_{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}
was arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=0\qquad\forall K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}.

(4)

Fix
K,K_{\dagger}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,i}
, and set

q_{3}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}&\text{on }K_{\dagger},\\
c&\text{on }K,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen such that
q_{3}\in Q_{h}
.
Then using (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{K_{\dagger}}|{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}|^{2}=-c\int_{K}{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=0.

Thus,
{{\rm div}\bm{u}_{h}}=0
on
\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}^{ct,i}
.

∎

3.
Stability

In this section, we prove inf-sup stability of the finite element method
and derive a priori estimates. As a first step,
we state an inf-sup stability result with respect to the finite element
spaces with support on the interior domain
\Omega_{h}^{i}
.

To do so, we require
two mesh-dependent norms

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}^{2}&=|\bm{u}|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\eta j_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{u})+\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{u}),\\
\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}^{2}&=\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}^{2}+\gamma\|{\rm div}\bm{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\end{split}

as well as the associated dual norm

\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{({\bm{f}},\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}}.

Remark 3.1
.

By definition, there holds
\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq(1+\gamma)^{1/2}\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}

for all
\bm{z}\in\bm{V}_{h}
.

We first need an inf-sup stability estimate for the SV element in the interior domain
\Omega_{h}^{i}
. The estimate is formulated below in Theorem
3.2
. Note that
\Omega_{h}^{i}
is mesh dependent and (
3.1
) does not follow easily from an “inf-sup-stability” of any finite element pair (see discussion in
[
18
]
). The property (
3.1
) was only
assumed
to hold in earlier publications, e.g.
[
7
,
23
]
, on unfitted FEMs for the Stokes problem, and has been proved for
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{2}-\EuScript{P}_{1}
element in
[
24
]
and for several other FE pairs in
[
18
]
. The latter paper does not cover any divergence-free elements.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists a constant
\theta>0
and a constant
h_{0}>0

such that we have the following result for
h\leq h_{0}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\theta\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{i}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}({\rm div}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}}\qquad\forall q\in Q_{h}^{i}.

The constant
\theta>0
is independent of
h
.

The proof of this theorem in the case
k=d=2
can be found in
[
29
, Section 4.2]
.
The general case
k\geq d
(

) follows verbatim using the
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{d}-\EuScript{P}_{0}

stability result in
[
18
]
: A local inf-sup stability result for
q\in\EuScript{P}_{k-1}(K)
with
\int_{K}q=0
on the CT split of each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
(Theorem 3.1 in
[
17
]
) is applied together with the global inf-sup stability of
\bm{\EuScript{P}}_{d}-\EuScript{P}_{0}
element for the macro-triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}
. These two results are combined by standard arguments, see e.g. Lemma 4.7 in
[

]
or Proposition 6.1 in
[
17
]
, to yield (

). We skip including further details.

Corollary 3.3
.

The following stability is satisfied

(3.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{*}\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mathop{\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}}_{{\rm supp}(\bm{v})\subset\Omega_{h}^{i}}\frac{b(\bm{v},q)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}}+J_{h}^{1/2}(q,q)\qquad\forall\ q\in Q_{h},

where
\theta_{*}>0
is independent of
h
and the position of
\Gamma
in the mesh.

Proof.

Fix some
q\in Q_{h}
.
By
[
30
, Lemma 5.1]
,
for each pair of triangles
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct}
with
\partial K_{1}\cap\partial K_{2}=F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,e}

we have

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{1})}\leq C\Big(\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{2})}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}h_{F}^{2\ell+1}\int_{F}[\partial^{\ell}_{n}q]^{2}\Big).

Iterating this estimate, we conclude

(3.3)

\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{e})}^{2}\leq C\big(\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}^{2}+J_{h}(q,q)\big).

Combining this estimate with Theorem
3.2
,
we conclude that there exists
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
with
{\rm supp}(\bm{v})\subset\Omega_{h}^{i}

such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|q\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}+J_{h}(q,q)\big)\leq C\theta^{-1}\Big(\frac{\int_{\Omega_{h}^{i}}({\rm div}\bm{v})q}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}}+J_{h}(q,q)\Big).

Because
\bm{v}=0
on
\Omega_{h}^{\Gamma}
, we have
j_{h}(\bm{v},\bm{v})=0
and by an inverse estimate,

\displaystyle\bm{{\rm j}}_{h}(\bm{v},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}}_{F\subset\partial\Omega_{h}^{i}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}h_{F}^{2\ell-1}\int_{F}[\partial^{\ell}_{n}\bm{v}]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,i}}\|\nabla\bm{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}.

Thus we have
\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{i})}.
Combining this with (
3.4
), we have (
3.2
).
∎

3.1.
A priori estimates for the finite element method

In this section we derive a priori estimates of the finite element
method, thus showing that the discrete problem (
2.1
) is well–posed.
The techniques to show these results are rather standard, but
we show the proofs here for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists constants
C_{a},C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\quad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}+\bm{H}^{k+1}(\Omega_{h}^{e}),

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}(\bm{v},\bm{v})\

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}.

Proof.

The proof of this result can be found in, e.g.,
[
30
,
18
,
6
]
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
satisfies (
2.1
). Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(1+\gamma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}},\quad\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq C\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}},

for some
C>0
independent of
\gamma
,
h
, and the position of
\Gamma
in the mesh.
Consequently, (
2.1
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

We set
\bm{v}_{h}=\bm{u}_{h}
in the first equation in (
2.1
), and
q_{h}=p_{h}
in the second equation of (
2.1
)
and subtract the resulting expressions:

a_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h})+{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}J_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})=({\bm{f}},\bm{u}_{h}).

By the coercivity of
a_{h}(.,.)
stated in Lemma
3.4
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle C_{0}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}J_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})

\displaystyle\leq({\bm{f}},\bm{u}_{h})\leq\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}},

and so

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{0}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}J_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})\leq\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}^{2}.

By the inf-sup stability estimate (
3.2
) and Remark
3.1
,
there exists
\bm{z}\in\bm{V}_{h}
with
(1+\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}=\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and

\displaystyle\theta_{*}\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq b(\bm{z},p_{h})+J_{h}^{1/2}(p_{h},p_{h})\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(\bm{f},\bm{z})-a_{h}(\bm{z},\bm{u}_{h})+J_{h}^{1/2}(p_{h},p_{h})\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By Lemma
3.4
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{*}\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}+C_{a}\|\bm{z}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\big)+J^{1/2}_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\big((1+\gamma)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}+C_{a}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}})+J^{1/2}_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})\big)\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Dividing by
\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and using (
3.6
),
we conclude

\displaystyle\theta_{*}^{2}\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\big((1+\gamma)(\|{\bm{f}}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}+C^{2}_{a}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}})+J_{h}(p_{h},p_{h})\big)

\displaystyle\leq 3(1+\gamma)\Big(1+C_{a}^{2}C_{0}^{-2}+\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\Big)\|{\bm{f}}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}}^{2}.

This estimate and (
3.6
) yields the desired result (
3.5
).
∎

4.
Convergence Analysis

In this section we assume that the solution to the Stokes problem (
2.1a
) is sufficiently
smooth, i.e.,
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega)
,
p\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, where we recall
k
is the polynomial degree in the definition of finite element spaces.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\rm dist}(\partial S,\partial\Omega)=O(1)
.

Because
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz
there exists an extension of
p
, which we also denote by
p
, such that
p\in H^{k+1}(S)
and (cf.
[
34
]
)

(4.1a)

\|p\|_{H^{\ell}(S)}\leq C\|p\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ for $\ell=0,1,\ldots,k+1$}.

An analogous extension of
\bm{u}
is done in the following manner.
First, write the velocity in terms of potential field

\bm{u}=\text{\bf curl}{\bm{\psi}}
, where we agree to understand
\text{\bf curl}{\bm{\psi}}
for any space dimension
d
as the exterior derivative of
(d-2)
-differential form
{\bm{\psi}}
.
For
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega)
, the form
{\bm{\psi}}
satisfies
{\bm{\psi}}\in\bm{H}^{k+3}(\Omega)

and
\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{H^{\ell+1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}
for
\ell=0,1,\ldots k+2

[
15
,
9
]
.
We extend
{\bm{\psi}}
to
S
in a way such that
\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{H^{\ell}(S)}\leq C\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}
for
\ell=0,1,\ldots,k+3
,
and let
\omega
be a smooth cut-off function with compact support in
S
and
\omega\equiv 1
in
\Omega
.
We then define the velocity extension as
\bm{u}=\text{\bf curl}(\omega{\bm{\psi}})
, so that

\bm{u}
is divergence–free, vanishes on
\partial S
, and

(4.1b)

\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(S)}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ for $\ell=0,1,\ldots,k+2$}.

Remark 4.1

(Consistency)
.

Standard arguments show that the method (
2.1
) is consistent.
In particular, there holds

\displaystyle\Big\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{h}(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})+b(p,\bm{v}_{h})=(\bm{f},\bm{v}_{h}),\\
b(q_{h},\bm{u})-{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}J_{h}(p,q_{h})=0\end{array}

for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
.

The following lemma is a direct application of

[
10
, Lemma 4.10]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}
, define

\displaystyle\omega_{T}=\mathop{\cup_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}}}_{\bar{T}\cap\bar{T}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset}\bar{T}^{\prime}
to be the patch
of neighboring elements of
T
. We further define the
O(h)
strip around
\Gamma
:

\omega_{\Gamma}=\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\omega_{T}.

Then there holds

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\Gamma})}\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{H^{1}(S)}\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}(S).

We also require a trace inequality suitable
for the CutFEM discretization (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
18
]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}
it holds

(4.4)

\|v\|_{L^{2}(K_{\Gamma})}\leq C(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{K}\|v\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}_{K}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)})\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}(K),

with a constant
C
independent of
v
,
T
, how
\Gamma
intersects
T
, and
h<h_{1}
for some fixed
h_{1}>0
.

Consider the finite element subspace of pointwise divergence-free functions:

\bm{Z}_{h}=\{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\,:\,{\rm div}\bm{w}_{h}=0~\text{in}~\Omega_{h}^{e}\}.

This subspace enjoys full approximation properties in the sense of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\bm{u}
, the divergence–free extension of the solution to (
2.1
), it holds

(4.5)

\inf_{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{k}|\bm{u}|_{H^{k+1}(\omega_{T})}\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}.

Consequently, if
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{k+2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq C\big(h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\big).

Proof.

The proof of (
4.5
) is given in the appendix.

To bound
\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}
we use the approximation results from
(
4.5
) and note that
{\rm div}(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})=0
in
\Omega_{h}^{e}
.
The penalty part of
\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}
is estimated using Lemma
4.3
as follows:

(4.6)

\begin{split}j_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h},\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})&=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K_{\Gamma})}\\
&\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\big(h_{K}^{-2}\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq Ch^{2k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\omega_{\Gamma})}^{2}\leq Ch^{2k+1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}^{2},\end{split}

where for the last inequality we used (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.2
.
This yields the bound
\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq C\big(h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\big)
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The following error estimate holds

(4.7)

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}+(1+\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|p-p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Bigg(h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+(1+\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}+\eta^{\frac{1}{2}})h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\\
+(\eta^{-\frac{1}{2}}+{(1+\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}})h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|p\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+(1+\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}h^{k}\|p\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}\Bigg).

Proof.

To show the error bounds, we start with a standard argument.
Let
\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
be a function in the discrete kernel satisfying estimate (
4.5
).
Setting
{\bm{e}}_{I}=\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
we have, thanks to the coercivity result in Lemma
3.4
:

(4.8)

C_{0}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq a_{h}({\bm{e}}_{I},{\bm{e}}_{I}).

Denote by
\widehat{p}_{h}\in Q_{h}
the
L^{2}
-projection of
p
onto
Q_{h}
,
and set
q_{I}=p_{h}-\widehat{p}_{h}
. It follows from (
3.2
) that there exists
{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}}
with
{\rm supp}(\bm{v})\subset\Omega_{h}^{i}

such that

(4.9)

C_{1}\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq b(\bm{v},q_{I})+C_{1}^{-1}J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I}),\quad\text{with}~(1+\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\leq\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{0,h}}=\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
C_{1}=\frac{\theta_{*}}{2}
, and
\theta_{*}
is the inf-sup constant given in Corollary

.

From (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
2.1
), and the consistency identity (
4.1
), we conclude that for any

it holds

(4.10)

\displaystyle C_{0}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+C_{1}\alpha\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big((1+\gamma)^{-1}-\alpha C_{1}^{-1}\big)J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq a_{h}({\bm{e}}_{I},{\bm{e}}_{I})+b(\alpha\bm{v},q_{I})+(1+\gamma)^{-1}J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})

\displaystyle\qquad=a_{h}({\bm{e}}_{I},{\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v})+b({\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v},q_{I})-b({\bm{e}}_{I},q_{I})+(1+\gamma)^{-1}J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})-a_{h}({\bm{e}}_{I},\alpha\bm{v})

\displaystyle\qquad=a_{h}(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h},{\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v})+b({\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v},p-\widehat{p}_{h})-b(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h},q_{I})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(1+\gamma)^{-1}J_{h}(p-\widehat{p}_{h},q_{I})-a_{h}({\bm{e}}_{I},\alpha\bm{v})

\displaystyle\qquad=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

We now estimate the right-hand side of (

Using the continuity result in Lemma
3.4
and the approximation results in Lemma
4.4

(4.11)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\bm{e}}_{I}+\alpha\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}\left(h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\right)

4.9

We now estimate the second term in the right-hand side of (

First, using approximation properties of the

-projection, we get

4.2

(4.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}h_{K}\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(K_{\Gamma})}^{2}\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,\Gamma}}\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq C\big(\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})\big).

\displaystyle I_{3}

)
to (

\displaystyle C_{0}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+C_{1}\alpha\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+((1+\gamma)^{-1}-\alpha C_{1}^{-1})J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\big(\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}+\alpha(1+\gamma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big)\big(h^{k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}+\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{C_{0}}{4}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|_{\bm{V}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{C_{a}^{2}\alpha^{2}(1+\gamma)}{C_{0}}\|q_{I}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality several times and rearrange terms to obtain

\displaystyle C_{0}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+\left(C_{1}\alpha-C{\alpha^{2}(1+\gamma)}\right)\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+((1+\gamma)^{-1}-\alpha C_{1}^{-1})J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\Bigg(\Big(h^{2k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\eta h^{2k+1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)+(\alpha+1)(1+\gamma)^{-1}h^{2k}\|p\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\eta^{-1}h^{2k+1}\|p\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha^{-1}h^{2k+1}\big(\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}^{2}+{(1+\gamma)^{-2}}\|p\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\Bigg).

We now take
\alpha=\widetilde{C}(1+\gamma)^{-1}
, with
\widetilde{C}>0
sufficiently small to obtain

\displaystyle C_{0}\|{\bm{e}}_{I}\|^{2}_{\bm{V}_{h}}+C(1+\gamma)^{-1}\big(\|q_{I}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+J_{h}(q_{I},q_{I})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\Bigg(h^{2k}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}+(1+\gamma)^{-1}h^{2k}\|p\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big(1+\eta+\gamma\big)h^{2k+1}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\eta^{-1}+{(1+\gamma)^{-1}})h^{2k+1}\|p\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}^{2}\Bigg).

Finally, we apply the triangle inequality, the divergence-free property of
\bm{u}
and
\bm{w}_{h}
,
and approximation properties (
4.5
) to obtain the error estimate (
4.7
).

∎

Remark 4.6
.

The pressure dependence in velocity error (
4.7
)
arises from the violation of mass conservation in the boundary strip and the penalty treatment of the boundary condition. The violation of the divergence-free constraint in a boundary strip can be partially mitigated by taking grad-div parameter
\gamma=O(h^{-1})

and Nitsche parameter
\eta=O(h^{-1})
, which seem to be the optimal choice with respect to the error analysis in the energy norm. This can be contrasted to
\gamma=O(1)
for the Taylor-Hood element.

\displaystyle\bm{w}(a)=0\qquad

\displaystyle[\hskip-1.5pt[{\rm div}\bm{v}]\hskip-1.5pt](z)=0\qquad

\displaystyle\int_{T}({\rm div}\bm{v})p=0

there holds by scaling and properties of the Scott-Zhang interpolant,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\bm{v}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(h_{T}^{-2}\Big|\int_{\partial T}(\bm{v}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\cdot{\bm{n}}\Big|^{2}+h_{T}^{2}\mathop{\sum_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{h}^{e}}}_{z\in\bar{T}}\big|[\hskip-1.5pt[{\rm div}(\bm{v}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})]\hskip-1.5pt](z)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathop{\sup_{p\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{0}(T^{ct})}}_{\|p\|_{L^{2}(T)}=1}\Big|\int_{T}({\rm div}(\bm{v}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u}))p\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=C\Big(h_{T}^{-2}\Big|\int_{\partial T}(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\cdot{\bm{n}}\Big|^{2}+\mathop{\sup_{p\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{0}(T^{ct})}}_{\|p\|_{L^{2}(T)}=1}\Big|\int_{T}{\rm div}(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})p\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(h_{T}^{-1}\|\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\partial T)}^{2}+\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h_{T}^{-2}\|\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\big)\leq Ch_{T}^{2}|\bm{u}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{T})}^{2}.

Thus,

\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{v})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla(\bm{v}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}|\bm{u}|_{H^{2}(\omega_{T})}.

∎

With the approximation properties
of the divergence-free subspace established,
we can use the same arguments
in the proof of Theorem
4.5
(with
k=1
)
to derive a first-order error estimate.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}^{ps}\times Q_{h}^{ps}

be the solution to (
5.1
).
Then the estimate (
4.7
) holds with
k=1
.

Appendix A
Proof of (
4.5
)

The approximation property (
4.5
) is derived
by constructing a Fortin operator using the recent results in
[
14
]
.
We consider the three-dimensional case; the analogous 2D arguments are similar (and simpler).

For
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}
, let
T^{ct}
denote the local triangulation of four (sub)tetrahedra, obtained by performing a Clough-Tocher (or Alfeld)
split of
T
.
We also define the smooth space

\mathring{{\bm{M}}}_{k+1}(T^{ct})=\{{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k+1}^{c}(T^{ct}):{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k}^{c}(T^{ct})\}.

Lemma A.1
.

There exists an operator

{\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}:\bm{H}^{1}(T)\to\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k}^{c}(T^{ct})
uniquely determined by the conditions

(A.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{T}({\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}\bm{v})\cdot{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}}=\int_{T}\bm{v}\cdot{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{{\bm{M}}}_{k+1}(T^{ct}),

(A.1b)

\displaystyle\int_{T}({\rm div}({\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}\bm{v}))\kappa=\int_{T}({\rm div}\bm{v})\kappa\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\kappa\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(T^{ct}).

Moreover,

\|\nabla{\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq C\big(\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{T}^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\big)\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(T).

Proof.

The existence of such an operator uniquely determined
by (
A.1
) follows from
[
14
, Lemma 4.16]
.

It remains to show the stability estimate. This is done via a scaling argument.

To ease presentation, set
\bm{v}_{T}={\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}\bm{v}
.
Let
\hat{T}=\{\frac{x}{h_{T}}:\ x\in T\}
be a dilation of
T
,
and define
\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}\in\mathring{\bm{\EuScript{P}}}_{k}(\hat{T}^{ct})
as

\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}(\hat{x})=\bm{v}_{T}(x)
with
x=h_{T}\hat{x}
, so that

\partial\bm{v}_{T}=h_{T}^{-1}\hat{\partial}\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}
.
In particular,
{\rm div}\bm{v}_{T}=h_{T}^{-1}\widehat{\rm div}\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}

and
{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}}=h_{T}^{-1}\widehat{\bf curl}\,\hat{\bm{\kappa}}
.
Using a change of variables and equivalence of norms, we compute

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|\nabla\bm{v}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{\nabla}\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}^{2}\approx\sup_{\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{{\bm{M}}}_{k+1}(\hat{T}^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{\int_{\hat{T}}\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot\widehat{\bf curl}\,\hat{\bm{\kappa}}}{\|\widehat{\bf curl}\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}}\Big|^{2}+\sup_{\hat{\kappa}\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(\hat{T}^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{\int_{\hat{T}}\widehat{\rm div}\hat{\bm{v}}_{T}\hat{\kappa}}{\|\hat{\kappa}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{T})}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{{\bm{M}}}_{k+1}(T^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{h_{T}^{-3}\int_{T}\bm{v}_{T}\cdot(h_{T}{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}})}{h_{T}^{-1/2}\|{\bf curl}{\bm{\kappa}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}}\Big|^{2}+\sup_{\kappa\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(T^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{h_{T}^{-3}\int_{T}(h_{T}{\rm div}\bm{v}_{T})\kappa}{h_{T}^{-3/2}\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}(T)}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=h_{T}^{-3}\sup_{\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\in\mathring{{\bm{M}}}_{k+1}(T^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{\int_{T}\bm{v}\cdot{\bf curl}\,{\bm{\kappa}}}{\|{\bf curl}{\bm{\kappa}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}}\Big|^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\sup_{\kappa\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(T^{ct})\backslash\{0\}}\Big|\frac{\int_{T}({\rm div}\bm{v})\kappa}{\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}(T)}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h_{T}^{-3}\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

∎

Set
{\bm{\Pi}}_{0}:\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega^{e})\to\bm{V}_{h}
such that
{\bm{\Pi}}_{0}|_{T}={\bm{\Pi}}_{0,T}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}
.
Let
{\bm{I}}_{h}:\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\bm{\EuScript{P}}^{c}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e})\subset\bm{V}_{h}
be the
k
th degree Scott-Zhang interpolant which satisfies (
k\geq 3
)
[
33
]

(A.2)

\int_{F}{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{v}=\int_{F}\bm{v}\qquad\text{for all faces in $\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}$}\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{e}).

Finally, we set
{\bm{\Pi}}_{h}:\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{e})\to\bm{V}_{h}
as

{\bm{\Pi}}_{h}={\bm{I}}_{h}+{\bm{\Pi}}_{0}({\bm{1}}-{\bm{I}}_{h}),

where
{\bm{1}}
is the identity operator.

Proposition A.2
.

There holds, for all
\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega^{e})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}({\bm{\Pi}}_{h}\bm{v}))q=\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}\bm{v})q\qquad\forall q\in\EuScript{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e}).

Proof.

Fix
q\in\EuScript{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{ct,e})
, and let
\bar{q}\in\EuScript{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}^{e}_{h})

be its
L^{2}
projection onto
\EuScript{P}_{0}(\mathcal{T}^{e}_{h})
. Note that
(q-\bar{q})|_{T}\in\mathring{\EuScript{P}}_{k-1}(T^{ct})
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}
.
We then write, using the divergence theorem, (
A.1
) and (
A.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{e}}({\rm div}({\bm{\Pi}}_{h}\bm{v}))q

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}({\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{v}))(q-\bar{q})+\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}{\rm div}({\bm{\Pi}}_{0}({\bm{1}}-{\bm{I}}_{h})\bm{v})(q-\bar{q})+\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}({\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{v}))\bar{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}({\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{v}))(q-\bar{q})+\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}{\rm div}(({\bm{1}}-{\bm{I}}_{h})\bm{v})(q-\bar{q})+\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}\bm{v})\bar{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{e}_{h}}({\rm div}\bm{v})q.

∎

The estimate (
4.5
) now follows from the following theorem.

Theorem A.3
.

There holds for all divergence–free
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{h}^{e})

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq C(\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{T}^{-1}\|\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(T)})\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{e}.

Therefore, if in addition
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{k+1}(\Omega_{h}^{e})
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}\leq Ch_{T}^{k}|\bm{u}|_{H^{k+1}(\omega_{T})}.

Proof.

If
\bm{u}
is divergence–free, then
{\bm{\Pi}}_{h}\bm{u}\in\bm{Z}_{h}
. Therefore by the definition of
{\bm{\Pi}}_{h}
and the
\bm{H}^{1}
-stability of this operator,

\displaystyle\inf_{\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{Z}_{h}}\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{w}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{\Pi}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\nabla({\bm{\Pi}}_{0}({\bm{1}}-{\bm{I}}_{h})\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla(\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u})\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{T}^{-1}\|\bm{u}-{\bm{I}}_{h}\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(T)}).

We then use the approximation properties of the Scott-Zhang interpolant to obtain the result.
∎

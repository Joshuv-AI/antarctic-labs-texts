Twisted linear periods and a new relative trace formula
Hang Xue, Wei Zhang

Abstract
. We study the linear periods on GL 2 ​ n \GL_{2n} twisted by a character using a new relative trace formula. We establish the relative fundamental lemma and the transfer of orbital integrals. Together with the spectral isolation technique of Beuzart-Plessis–Liu–Zhang–Zhu, we are able to compare the elliptic part of the relative trace formulae and to obtain new results generalizing Waldspurger’s theorem in the n = 1 n=1 case.

1.2.
The new relative trace formula

Let
v
be an archimedean place of
F
, we let
\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
be the space of
Schwartz functions on
G(F_{v})
, meaning the functions
f
such that
Df
is
bounded on
G(F_{v})
for all algebraic differential operators
D
on
G(F_{v})
.
Let
\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
be the space of Schwartz functions on
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, i.e.
linear combinations of the functions of the form
\prod_{v}f_{v}
where
f_{v}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G(F_{v}))
if
v
is nonarchimedean and
f_{v}\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
is
v

is archimedean. Similar definitions also applies to other groups.

To study the linear period
P_{\chi}
we consider the following relative
trace formulae. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
. We put

K_{f}(g_{1},g_{2})=\int_{Z(F)\backslash Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\sum_{y\in G(F)}f(zg_{1}^{-1}yg_{2})\omega(z)^{-1}\mathrm{d}z.

Define a distribution

(1.2)

J(f)=\iint_{(Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F}))^{2}}K_{f}(h_{1},h_{2})\chi(h_{1}h_{2}^{-1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

This distribution at least formally unfolds geometrically and has a spectral
expansion. Then we obtain a relative trace formula on
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. This
relative trace formula is essentially the same as the one propose
in
[
8
]
, except that a character
\chi
is inserted.

We now propose a relative trace formula to study the
L
-function

L(\frac{1}{2},\pi_{0,E}\otimes\chi)
. This is the main innovation of
this paper.

Let us first recall the relative trace formula proposed by
[
8
]
when

\chi
is trivial. In this case, let
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
and the
relative trace formula results from the geometric and spectral expansion of
the distribution

\iint\sum_{x\in\GL_{2n}(F)}f^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1}xh_{2})\eta(h_{1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2},

where the integration is over
h_{1},h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. The spectral expansion gives both the periods

\int\varphi(h)\mathrm{d}h,\quad\int\varphi(h)\eta(h)\mathrm{d}h,

where
\varphi\in\pi_{0}
and the domain of the integration in both cases
are
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\times\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Thus by the work of Friedberg and
Jacquet
[
7
]
on (split) linear periods these periods give rise to the

L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{0})L(s,\pi_{0}\otimes\eta)
.

It is clear that such an approach cannot be generalized to arbitrary
\chi
,
simply because
L(s,\pi_{0,E}\otimes\chi)
does not factorize in general.
An alternative approach is needed. Assume the central character of
\pi_{0}
is

\omega
. In the case of
n=1
, Jacquet
[
13
]
proposed the
following. Assume
n=1
. Let
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{E}))
be a test function.
Then consider

\iint\sum_{x\in\GL_{2}(E)}f^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1}xh_{2})\chi(h_{1})(\omega\eta)(\lambda(h_{2}))\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

Here the integration is over
h_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1\end{pmatrix}
,

a\in E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
,
h_{2}
is in
\GU(1,1)
where
\GU(1,1)

stands for the quasisplit similitude unitary group in two variables, and

\lambda
is the similitude character. Jacquet’s idea is as follows. The
integration over
h_{1}
gives the central
L
-value, and the period over

\GU(1,1)
ensures that the representations we consider on
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{E})

are all base change from
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Jacquet (re)proved
Conjecture
1.1
in the case
n=1
based on this
relative trace formula.

Thus for general
n
a natural idea is to extend the relative trace formulae
in
[
13
]
, i.e.

(1.3)

\iint\sum_{x\in\GL_{2n}(E)}f^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1}xh_{2})\widetilde{\chi}(h_{1})(\omega\eta)(\lambda(h_{2}))\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2},

with
h_{1}=(h_{11},h_{12})\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})\times\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
,

\widetilde{\chi}(h_{1})=\chi(h_{11}h_{12}^{-1})
, and
h_{2}\in\GU(n,n)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. This is very natural and was indeed our first attempt. But
it does not seem to be the correct approach and we eventually abandoned it
for the following reason. The stabilizers in the geometric expansions of the
distributions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are very different, one
being tori in
\GL_{n}(F)
and the other being tori in the unitary groups. On
the philosophical level it is not expected that two trace formulae can be
compared unless the stabilizers from their geometric side are closely
related, e.g. they are isomorphic or at least one of them is trivial. After
all, in the comparison of the trace formulae, we need to equate the volume of
these stabilizers. Therefore we do not expect a nice comparison between the
geometric expansions of the distributions (
1.2
)
and (
1.3
).

We take an alternative approach in this paper. The starting point is the
following key observation. Let
\Pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic
representation of
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
and
\varphi\in\Pi
. The integration of

\varphi
over
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})\times\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
does not merely tell us
something about
L(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\otimes\chi)
, but also about the
self-duality of
\Pi
. Let us introduction some notation. Let
H^{\prime}=\Res_{E/F}(\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n})
, and
\chi_{H^{\prime}}
is a character of
H^{\prime}

sending
h_{1}=(h_{11},h_{12})
to
\chi(h_{11}\overline{h_{12}})
. Indeed
if

\int\varphi(h_{1})\chi_{H^{\prime}}(h_{1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\not=0

where the integration is over
H^{\prime}
, by
[
7
]
we have
L(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\otimes\chi)\not=0
and
L(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi\chi^{c})
has a pole
at
s=1
where
\chi^{c}(g)=\chi(\overline{g})
. The later implies that

\Pi^{\vee}\simeq\Pi\otimes\chi\chi^{c}
. What we need in the relative trace
formula is to use the second integral over
h_{2}
to separate those
\Pi
with

\Pi\simeq\Pi^{c}
. Under the condition that
\Pi^{\vee}\simeq\Pi\otimes\chi\chi^{c}
, this is equivalent to
\Pi^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{-1}\simeq\Pi^{c}\otimes\chi^{c}
where
\Pi^{c}(g)=\Pi(\overline{g})
, and this later
condition can be detected using the period integral of Flicker and Rallis,
i.e. the integration

\int\varphi(h_{2})\chi\eta(h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{2}

where
h_{2}\in\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Thus our new distribution on

\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
reads the following

\iint\sum_{x\in\GL_{2n}(E)}f^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1}xh_{2})\chi_{H^{\prime}}(h_{1})(\chi\eta)^{-1}(h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2},

where
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})\times\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
and
h_{2}\in\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. The geometric and spectral expansions of this distribution
give the relative trace formula on
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})
. The stabilizer of any
(relatively) regular semisimple orbit is a torus in
\GL_{n}(F)
of the form

\prod_{i}\Res_{F_{i}/F}\GL_{1}
, and hence it matches the stabilizers arising
from the distribution (
1.2
).

The majority of this paper compares the elliptic part of this relative trace
formula with the one on
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Our key local results are the relevant
fundamental lemma and transfer of orbital integrals. With the recent
technique from
[
3
]
to isolate cuspidal spectra, these local results
lead to the main theorems. To remove the unnecessary conditions in those
theorems, one would need to compare the full relative trace formulae, not
just the elliptic part. Nevertheless the current comparison is sufficient for
the purpose of solving the local problems, i.e. the conjecture of Prasad and
Takloo-Bighash. The conjecture of Prasad and Takloo-Bighash in turn appears
to be an indispensable ingredient in the comparison of the full relative
trace formulae. We hope to address these questions in a future work.

If

When a group

The

, or simply

If

Let

is be quadratic field extension. The nontrivial Galois involution

. By a twisted conjugation by

, we mean the map

of

We let

2.
Relative trace formulae: the geometric side

2.1.
Geometric side

Let
E/F
be a quadratic extension of number fields, and
D
a (possibly
split) quaternion algebra over
F
containing
E
. Let
G=\GL_{n}(D)
,
Z=\GL_{1,F}
the center of
G
, and
H=\Res_{E/F}\GL_{n,E}
.

Let us recall from the introduction that we have the following relative trace
formula on
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
. We put

K_{f}(g_{1},g_{2})=\int_{Z(F)\backslash Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\sum_{y\in G(F)}f(zg_{1}^{-1}yg_{2})\omega(z)^{-1}\mathrm{d}z.

Define a distribution

J(f)=\iint_{(Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F}))^{2}}K_{f}(h_{1},h_{2})\chi(h_{1}h_{2}^{-1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

We consider the
H\times H
action on
G
by
(h_{1},h_{2})\cdot y=h_{1}^{-1}yh_{2}
. An element
y\in G(F)
is called regular semisimple if the stabilizer

(H\times H)_{y}
is a torus of dimension
n
over
F
. It is called elliptic
if in addition that this torus is anisotropic modulo the center of
G
. Let

G(F)_{\reg}
and
G(F)_{\el}
be the subsets of regular semisimple and
elliptic elements. These definitions also apply to elements in
G(F_{v})
where

v
is a place of
F
.

Assume that
f=\otimes f_{v}
is decomposable and there is one nonsplit place

v_{1}
of
F
such that
f_{v_{1}}
is supported in the regular elliptic locus.
Then we have

(2.1)

J(f)=\sum_{y\in H(F)\backslash G(F)_{\el}/H(F)}\vol((H\times H)_{y})O^{G}(y,f),

where

O^{G}(y,f)=\int_{(H\times H)_{y}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash(H\times H)(\mathbb{A}_{F})}f(h_{1}^{-1}yh_{2})\chi(h_{1}^{-1}h_{2})^{-1}\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

In these expressions we fix compatible measures on
Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash(H\times H)_{y}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
,
Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash(H\times H)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
(H\times H)_{y}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash(H\times H)(\mathbb{A}_{F})
for each
y\in G(F)_{\el}
and
\vol((H\times H)_{y})
stands for the volume of

Z(\mathbb{A}_{F})(H\times H)_{y}(F)\backslash(H\times H)(\mathbb{A}_{F}).

This integral is absolutely convergent for all regular semisimple
y\in G(F)
. Since the test function
f
is not compactly supported, the absolute
convergence of (
2.1
) needs explanation. This will be
given in Appendix
A
.

For any place
v
of
F
, we define similarly the local orbital integrals,
except we integrate over
(H\times H)(F_{v})
instead.

The orbital integral can be simplified as follows. If
g\in\GL_{2n}
we
define an involution

\theta(g)=\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}\\
&-1_{n}\end{pmatrix}g\begin{pmatrix}1_{n}\\
&-1_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
H
is the group of fixed point of
\theta
. We introduce the symmetric
space

S=\{g\theta(g)^{-1}\mid g\in G\}

and then
H
acts on
S
by conjugation. Put

\widetilde{f}(g\theta(g)^{-1})=\int_{H(\mathbb{A}_{F})}f(gh)\chi(gh)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h.

Then
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{S}(S(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
and

O^{G}(g,f)=O^{S}(s,\widetilde{f})=\int_{H_{s}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\widetilde{f}(h^{-1}sh)\mathrm{d}h.

For any place
v
of
F
, the local orbital integrals can be simplified in a
similar way.

We introduce another relative trace formula with
which (
2.1
) will be compared. While the distribution

J
does not differ much from those in
[
8
]
, this relative trace
formula is the main point of innovation of the present paper.

Let
G^{\prime}=\Res_{E/F}\GL_{2n}
,
H^{\prime}=\Res_{E/F}(\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n})

embedded in
G^{\prime}
as diagonal blocks, and
H^{\prime\prime}=\GL_{2n,F}
. Let
Z^{\prime}\simeq\GL_{1,F}
embedded in
G^{\prime}
diagonally. Let
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{E}))
and

K_{f^{\prime}}(g_{1},g_{2})=\int_{Z^{\prime}(E)\backslash Z^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{E})}\sum_{\gamma\in G^{\prime}(F)}f^{\prime}(g_{1}^{-1}z\gamma g_{2})\omega(z\overline{z})^{-1}\mathrm{d}z.

For
h=\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in H^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
with

h_{1},h_{2}\in(\Res_{E/F}\GL_{n})(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, we put
\chi_{H^{\prime}}(h)=\chi(h_{1}\overline{h_{2}})
. Consider the distribution

2.2.
Matching of test functions

Recall that we fixed

(resp.

\overline{\beta}&\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix},\quad\alpha,\beta\in M_{n}(E).

Then

consists of matrices of the form
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
&\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}
,

Let

(resp.

1-(\alpha_{1}\overline{\alpha_{1}})^{-1}\in\epsilon N\GL_{n}(E),\quad\text{resp.}\quad\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{1}+1)\in N\GL_{n}(E).

where

is a place of

of

F
. With the above fixed measures on their

\vol((H^{\prime}\times H^{\prime\prime})_{x})=\vol((H\times H)_{y}).

Fix an element
\tau\in E^{\times}
such that
\overline{\tau}=-\tau
. For
each place

of

, we define transfer factors on

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\kappa_{v}^{G^{\prime}}(x)=\chi_{v}(\alpha_{4})\widetilde{\eta_{v}}(\tau\alpha_{2}),\quad\kappa_{v}^{G}(y)=\chi_{v}(y_{1}),\quad y^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&\epsilon y_{2}\\
\overline{y_{2}}&\overline{y_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

For each place

of

, we define

\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))_{0}=\{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))\mid O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f_{v}^{\prime})=0\text{
for all $x$ not matching any $y\in G(F_{v})$}\},

and

and

. Two

of

2.3.
Matching at split places

The goal of this subsection is to explain the matching of test functions at

If

is a split place of

G(F_{v})=\GL_{2n}(F_{v})

and
thus we have measures on
G^{\prime}(F_{v})
and on

under this identification.
The character
\eta_{v}
is trivial so
\widetilde{\eta}_{v}
takes the form

(\eta_{0},\eta_{0}^{-1})
where
\eta_{0}
is a character of
F_{v}^{\times}
. The

(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})

\chi_{1},\chi_{2}

are characters of
F_{v}^{\times}
. Regular semisimple elements

(2.6)

Lemma 2.3
.

The functions
f^{\prime}
and
f
match.

Proof.

We write

x_{1}x_{2}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&B_{1}\\
C_{1}&D_{1}\end{pmatrix},\quad x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{2}&B_{2}\\
C_{2}&D_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

A little computation gives that the orbital integral
\kappa^{G^{\prime}}((x_{1},x_{2}))O^{G^{\prime}(F_{v})}((x_{1},x_{2}),f^{\prime})
equals

(2.7)

\displaystyle\eta_{0}(x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})^{-1}\eta_{0}(-B_{1}B_{2}^{-1})\chi_{1}(D_{1})\chi_{2}(D_{2})\chi_{1}(x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle\int f\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
&b_{1}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}x_{1}x_{2}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}\\
&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{1}(a_{1}a_{2}^{-1})\chi_{2}(b_{1}b_{2}^{-1})\mathrm{d}a_{1}\mathrm{d}a_{2}\mathrm{d}b_{1}\mathrm{d}b_{2}.

When
(x_{1},x_{2})
is regular semisimple,
A_{2}
is invertible, and thus

\begin{pmatrix}A_{2}&B_{2}\\
C_{2}&D_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
C_{2}A_{2}^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A_{2}&B_{2}\\
&D_{2}-C_{2}A_{2}^{-1}B_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
D_{1}=(D_{2}-C_{2}A_{2}^{-1}B_{2})^{-1}
and
(\det x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})^{-1}=\det A_{2}\det(D_{2}-C_{2}A_{2}^{-1}B_{2})
. It follows that

\chi_{1}(D_{1})\chi_{2}(D_{2})\chi_{1}(x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})^{-1}=\chi_{1}(A_{2})\chi_{2}(D_{2})
.
Similarly we have
\eta_{0}(x_{1}x_{2}^{-1})=\eta_{0}(-B_{1}B_{2}^{-1})
. In
particular we note that
\kappa^{G^{\prime}}((x_{1},x_{2}))O^{G^{\prime}(F_{v})}((x_{1},x_{2}),f^{\prime})

is independent of the choice of
\widetilde{\eta}
. A straightforward

when

.
∎

and

and

*Chapter 1, Section 5.

From the explicit expressions (

3.1.

&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\lvert\det h_{1}h_{2}^{-1}\rvert^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\chi(\det h_{1}h_{2}^{-1})\xi(\det h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2},

and
\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
stands for the center of

is convergent for all

. By
[

]
*Proposition 2.2 this

is not
identically zero. Assume that this is the case. Then

[

]
*Proposition 2.3 when

\int_{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{E})}V_{\varphi}\left(\begin{pmatrix}g\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi(\det g)\lvert\det g\rvert^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}g.

By
[
7
]
*Theorem 4.1 this integral equals a holomorphic multiple of

L(s,\Pi\otimes\chi)
, and there is a choice of
\varphi
such that it
equals this

We have

for some

V_{\varphi_{v}}(g)=\lambda(\Pi_{v}(g)\varphi_{v}),\quad g\in\GL_{2n}(E_{v}),

and

\mathcal{V}_{v}=\{V_{\varphi_{v}}\mid\varphi_{v}\in\Pi_{v}\}.

The space
\mathcal{V}_{v}
is called the local Shalika model of
\Pi_{v}
. As
in
[
7
]
*Section 3 we define

Z_{v}^{\mathrm{FJ}}(s,V_{v},\chi_{v})=\int_{\GL_{n}(F_{v})}V_{v}\left(\begin{pmatrix}g\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{v}(g)\lvert\det g\rvert^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}g.

Here we note that this implicitly depends on
\psi_{v}
and
\xi_{v}
through the
Shalika model. Then by
[
7
]
*Proposition 3.1, it is a holomorphic
multiple of
L(s,\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})
and there is a
V_{v}\in\mathcal{V}_{v}
such
that it equals this local
L
-function. Moreover
by
[
7
]
*Proposition 3.3 there is a functional equation

\gamma(s,\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\psi_{v})Z_{v}^{\mathrm{FJ}}(s,V_{v},\chi_{v})=Z_{v}^{\mathrm{FJ}}(1-s,\widetilde{V_{v}},\chi_{v}^{-1}),

where

\widetilde{V_{v}}(g)=V_{v}\left(\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}\prescript{t}{}{g}^{-1}\right),

and
\widetilde{V_{v}}
belong to the Shalika model of the contragredient of

\pi
(defined using the characters
\chi_{v}^{-1}
and
\psi_{v}^{-1}
).
Specializing to
s=\frac{1}{2}
we conclude that

(3.1)

\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\psi_{v})Z_{v}^{\mathrm{FJ}}(\frac{1}{2},V_{v},\chi_{v})=Z_{v}^{\mathrm{FJ}}(\frac{1}{2},\widetilde{V_{v}},\chi_{v}^{-1}),

where
\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\psi_{v})
is the local root number of

\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v}
.

3.2.
Functoriality

We return to the setup and notation of Section
2
. Let

\Pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.
We define
\Pi^{\vee}
to be the dual of
\Pi
, and
\Pi^{c}
to be the Galois
conjugate (relative to
E/F
) of
\Pi
, i.e. the automorphic representation
whose space is given by
\{\varphi(\overline{g})\mid\varphi\in\Pi\}
.
Then it is well-known that
\Pi^{c}\simeq\Pi
if and only if
\Pi=\pi_{E}

for some cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
,
cf.
[
2
]
*Chapter 3, Theorem 4.2 and 5.1.

We apply the results recalled the previous subsection to the current
situation. Put

(3.2)

P^{\prime}_{\chi}(\varphi)=\int_{Z^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})H^{\prime}(F)\backslash H^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\varphi\left(\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}\\
&h_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi(h_{1}\overline{h_{2}})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}

Then by Proposition
3.1
, applied to the case
\xi=\chi\chi^{c}
,
the linear form
P^{\prime}_{\chi}
is not identically zero if and only if

L(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\otimes\chi)\not=0
and
L(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi\chi^{c})
has a pole at
s=1
. The later implies that
\Pi^{\vee}\simeq\Pi\otimes\chi\chi^{c}
.

We also consider the Flicker–Rallis period of
\Pi
given by

P^{\prime\prime}_{\chi\eta}(\varphi)=\int_{Z_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})H^{\prime\prime}(F)\backslash H^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\varphi(h)(\chi\eta)(h)\mathrm{d}h.

It is identically zero if and only if the Asai
L
-function
L(s,\Pi\otimes\chi,\As^{-})
has a pole at
s=1
, cf.
[
14
]
. Note that this implies
that
\Pi^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{-1}\simeq\Pi^{c}\otimes\chi^{c}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If neither of
P^{\prime}_{\chi}
and
P^{\prime\prime}_{\chi\eta}
is identically zero, then
there is an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of

\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
such that
\Pi=\pi_{E}
. Moreover,
L(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\otimes\chi)\not=0
, and
L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}})

has a simple pole at
s=1
.

Proof.

The existence of
\pi
follows from the above discussion. To prove the second
assertion, let us observe that

L(s,\Pi\otimes\chi,\As^{-})=L(s,\pi,\Sym^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\eta)L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}).

Indeed this can be checked place by place for almost all places of
F
. If

v
is split in
E
, then the equality clearly holds. Assume
v
is inert and

\Pi_{v}
(and hence
\pi_{v}
) is unramified. Let
\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{2n}

be the Satake parameters of
\pi_{v}
, and
\gamma=\chi_{v}(\varpi_{v})
where

\varpi_{v}
is the uniformizer at
v
. Then the Satake parameters of
\Pi_{v}

are
\beta_{1}^{2},\cdots,\beta_{2n}^{2}
. Thus the left hand side equals

\prod_{1\leq i\leq 2n}(1+\beta_{i}^{2}\gamma q_{v}^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n}(1-\beta_{i}^{2}\beta_{j}^{2}\gamma^{2}q^{-2s})^{-1}.

The right hand side equals

\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq 2n}(1+\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\gamma q_{v}^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n}(1-\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\gamma^{2}q^{-s})^{-1}.

Thus the desired equality holds at the place
v
.

Similar we also have

L(s,\Pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi\chi^{c})=L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}})L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\eta).

If
L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}})
is holomorphic at
s=1
, then both

L(s,\pi,\Sym^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\eta),\quad L(s,\pi,\wedge^{2}\otimes\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\eta)

have a pole at
s=1
, which implies that

L(s,\pi\times\pi\chi|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\eta)

has at least a double pole at
s=1
, which is not possible. This proves the
final assertion.
∎

3.3.
Local spherical charaters

Let
v
be a nonarchimedean nonsplit place. Let
\pi_{v}
be an irreducible
unitary representation of
G(F_{v})
. According to
[
20
]
, the space

\Hom_{H(F_{v})}(\pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\mathbb{C})
is at most one dimensional. We
assume that it is one dimensional and we fix a nonzero element
\ell
in it.
Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
and we consider the spherical character

J_{\pi_{v}}(f)=\sum_{\phi}\ell(\pi_{v}(f)\phi)\overline{\ell(\phi)}

where the sum runs over an orthonormal basis of
\pi
. A test functions of
the form
f=f_{1}*\overline{f_{1}^{\vee}}
is called of positive type.

Lemma 3.3
.

There is a positive type
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))_{0}
such that
J_{\pi_{v}}(f)>0
.

Proof.

Choose
\phi\in\pi_{v}
such that
\ell(\phi)\not=0
. Let
f_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
be the characteristic function of a small open compact subgroup,
then
\ell(\pi(f_{1})\phi)\not=0
. Put
f=f_{1}*\overline{f_{1}^{\vee}}
. Then

f
is supported in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
1\in G(F_{v})
and
thus
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))_{0}
, because every regular semisimple element in the
support of
f
matches some element in
G^{\prime}(F_{v})
. Moreover we have

J_{\pi_{v}}(f)=\sum_{\phi}\ell(\pi_{v}(f_{1})\phi)\overline{\ell(\pi_{v}(f_{1})\phi)}.

Each term in the sum is nonnegative and there is at least one nonzero term.
Therefore
J_{\pi_{v}}(f)>0
.
∎

By
[
9
]
,
J_{\pi_{v}}
is represented by a locally integrable function

\Theta_{\pi_{v}}
on
G(F_{v})
, which is locally constant on the regular
semisimple locus, and which satisfies the invariance property

\Theta_{\pi_{v}}(h_{1}gh_{2})=\chi_{v}(h_{1}h_{2})\Theta_{\pi}(g)
. We say that

\pi_{v}
is
H(F_{v})
-elliptic
\Theta_{\pi_{v}}(y)\not=0
for some elliptic
regular semisimple
y\in G(F_{v})
and
y
matches some
x\in G^{\prime}(F_{v})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
\pi_{v}
is supercuspidal and
\Hom_{H(F_{v})}(\pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\mathbb{C})\not=0
, then
\pi_{v}
is
H(F_{v})
-elliptic.

The argument is standard and very close to the classical theory of
Harish-Chandra. The main part of the proof has also been worked out in a
slightly different setting in
[
27
]
. A detailed proof is quite long,
and it produces a lot of repetitions from the literature and deviates
significantly from the goal of this paper. So we will merely sketch the
argument in Appendix
B
.

3.4.
Involution

We need to consider the local spherical characters arising from the
distribution
I_{\Pi}
. Let us fix a nonsplit nonarchimedean place
v
. Then
we have

\Hom_{H^{\prime}(F_{v})}(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{H^{\prime},v},\mathbb{C})\not=0,\quad\Hom_{H^{\prime\prime}(F_{v})}(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\mathbb{C})\not=0.

We let
\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell^{\prime\prime}
be nonzero elements in these
\Hom
-spaces
respectively. Define the local spherical character

I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime})=\sum_{W}\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(f^{\prime})W)\overline{\ell^{\prime\prime}(W)},\quad f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v})),

where
W
ranges over an orthonormal basis of
\Pi_{v}
.

We say that
I_{\Pi_{v}}
is elliptic if there is an
f^{\prime}
supported in the
elliptic locus of
G^{\prime}(F_{v})
such that
I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime})\not=0
. We expect that
all supercuspidal representations are elliptic. We will address it in a
subsequent paper.

Let
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))
. Put

(3.3)

f^{\prime{\dagger}}(g)=f^{\prime}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})(g),\quad g\in G^{\prime}(F_{v}).

Let
\psi_{v}
be a nontrivial additive character of
E_{v}
trivial on
F_{v}
.
Since
\Pi_{v}^{\vee}\otimes\chi_{v}^{-1}\simeq\Pi_{v}^{c}\otimes\chi_{v}^{c}
, the
local root number
\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\psi_{v})=\pm 1
and is
independent of
\psi_{v}
. We denote it by
\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))
we have

I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})=\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime}).

Proof.

To prove this lemma, we need to choose
\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell^{\prime\prime}
more carefully.
Since different choices differ only by a constant, the validity of the lemma
is independent of such choices.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the Whittaker model of
\Pi_{v}
(defined by the character

\psi_{v}
) and
\mathcal{V}
be the Shalika model of
\Pi_{v}
defined by the characters

\psi_{v}
and
\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c}
. We fix an isomorphism

\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{V},\quad W\mapsto\phi_{W}.

We also denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
the Whittaker model
and Shalika model of
\pi^{\vee}
, defined using the characters
\psi_{v}^{-1}

and
\psi_{v}^{-1}
,
(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}
respectively. We also fix an
isomorphism

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{V}},\quad W\mapsto\phi_{W}.

For any function
\alpha
on
G^{\prime}(F_{v})
we temporarily put

\widetilde{\alpha}(g)=\alpha\left(\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}\prescript{t}{}{g}^{-1}\right).

Then both

W\mapsto\phi_{W},\quad W\mapsto\widetilde{\phi_{\widetilde{W}}}

are isomorphisms between
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{V}
, and hence they differ by a
constant. By rescaling the isomorphisms that we have fixed, we may assume
that this constant equals one. It follows that for any
W\in\mathcal{W}
, we have

\phi_{\widetilde{W}}=\widetilde{\phi_{W}}.

For any
W\in\mathcal{W}
we put
W^{c}(g)=W(\overline{g})
.

Recall from Subsection
3.1
that we have the integral

Z^{\mathrm{FJ}}(s,\phi,\chi_{v})=\int_{\GL_{n}(E)}\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{v}(a)\lvert\det a\rvert^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}a,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{V}.

We choose
\ell^{\prime}\in\Hom_{H^{\prime}(F_{v})}(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v},\mathbb{C})
to be the

W\mapsto\ell^{\prime}(W)=Z^{\mathrm{FJ}}(\frac{1}{2},\phi_{W},\chi_{v}).

For the linear form
\ell^{\prime\prime}
, we take it to be

\ell^{\prime\prime}(W)=\int_{N^{\prime}\cap H^{\prime\prime}(F_{v})\backslash P^{\prime}\cap H^{\prime\prime}(F_{v})}W(h)(\chi_{v}\eta_{v})(h)\mathrm{d}h.

where
P^{\prime}
is the mirabolic subgroup of
G^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
the standard upper
triangular unipotent subgroup of
G^{\prime}
.

If
W\in\mathcal{W}
, since
\Pi_{v}^{\vee}\simeq\Pi_{v}\otimes(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}
, we have
\widetilde{W^{c}}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}\in\mathcal{W}
. We
claim that for any
g\in G^{\prime}(F_{v})
we have

(3.4)

\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})W)(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}(g)=\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(g)(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})).

The right hand side equals

\int\phi_{\Pi_{v}(g)(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{v}(a)\mathrm{d}a

which simplifies to

(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}(g)\int\phi_{\widetilde{\Pi_{v}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})W}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{v}^{-1}(a)\mathrm{d}a

Using the functional equation (
3.1
) of
Z^{\mathrm{FJ}}
we
conclude that this equals

\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}(g)\int\phi_{\Pi_{v}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})W}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a\\
&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi_{v}(a)\mathrm{d}a,

which is precisely the left hand side of (
3.4
).

We now compute
I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})
. By definition we have

\displaystyle\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})W)

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{\prime}(F)}f^{\prime}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(g)W)(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})(g)\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{\prime}(F)}f^{\prime}(g)\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(\prescript{t}{}{\overline{g}}^{-1})W)(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})(g)^{-1}\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})\int_{G^{\prime}(F)}f^{\prime}(g)\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(g)(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})).

Thus we have

\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})W)=\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(f^{\prime})(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})).

By
[
17
]
*Lemma 1.1 (the main part of the proof is
from
[
21
]
*Corollary 7.2) we have

\ell^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})=\ell^{\prime\prime}(W).

Thus we conclude

I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})=\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})\sum_{W}\ell^{\prime}(\Pi_{v}(f^{\prime})(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}))\overline{\ell^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1})}.

When
W
ranges over an orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{W}
,
\widetilde{W}^{c}(\chi_{v}\chi_{v}^{c})^{-1}
ranges over an orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{W}
as well.
Thus

I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime{\dagger}})=\epsilon(\Pi_{v}\otimes\chi_{v})I_{\Pi_{v}}(f^{\prime}).

This proves the lemma.
∎

We now study the relation between matching of test functions and this
involution. Let
\epsilon_{D_{v}}=\eta_{v}(\epsilon)=\pm 1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))_{0}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))_{0}
be matching test
functions. Then
f^{\prime{\dagger}}
and
\eta_{v}(-1)^{n}\epsilon_{D_{v}}^{n}f
match.

Proof.

Let
x\in G^{\prime}(F)
and
s^{\prime}=x\overline{x}^{-1}
. Then
O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime})=O^{S^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},\widetilde{f^{\prime}})
where
\widetilde{f^{\prime}}
is defined
by (
2.3
). Moreover a little computation shows that

O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime{\dagger}})=O^{S^{\prime}}(\prescript{t}{}{s^{\prime}},\widetilde{f^{\prime}}).

We will see below in Section
5.1
that
s^{\prime}
is in the

H^{\prime}
-orbit of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&1\\
1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}&-\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}.

So we may assume that
s^{\prime}
equals it. Then

\prescript{t}{}{s^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}\\
1&-\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&1\\
1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}&-\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that

O^{S^{\prime}}(\prescript{t}{}{s^{\prime}},\widetilde{f^{\prime}})=\eta_{v}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})O^{S^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},\widetilde{f^{\prime}})=\eta_{v}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime}).

This in particular implies that
f^{\prime{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F_{v}))_{0}
.

Suppose
x
matches some
y\in G(F_{v})
. This is equivalent to
1-(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}\in\epsilon N\GL_{n}(E_{v})
, which implies
\eta_{v}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})=\eta_{v}(-1)^{n}\epsilon_{D_{v}}^{n}
. Then

O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime{\dagger}})=O^{G^{\prime}}(x,\eta_{v}(-1)^{n}\epsilon_{D_{v}}^{n}f^{\prime})

and the lemma follows.
∎

3.5.
Multipliers

To analyze the spectral side without truncation, we need to make use of the
techniques from
[
3
]
. Recall that for any (complex) algebra
\mathcal{A}
, a
multiplier is linear map
\mu\star:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
that commutes with both the
left and right multiplication in
\mathcal{A}
. The space of multipliers of
\mathcal{A}
is
denoted by
Mul(\mathcal{A})
.

Let
v
be an archimedean place of
F
,
\mathfrak{t}_{v}
the (complexified) Cartan
subalgebra of
G(F_{v})
,
\mathfrak{t}_{v}^{*}
the dual space, and
\mathcal{Z}_{G(F_{v})}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}_{v}]^{W_{v}}
the center of the universal enveloping algebra of
G(F_{v})
.
Write
\chi_{v}=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
then
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
as a character of

F_{v}^{\times}
defines an element
\underline{a}_{v}=(a_{v},\cdots,a_{v})\in\mathfrak{t}_{v}^{*}
.

In
[
3
]
*Definition 2.8(3), an algebra
\mathcal{M}_{v}
of holomorphic
functions on
\mathfrak{t}_{v}^{*}
is introduced (the notation in
[
3
]
is

\mathcal{M}_{\theta}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{\mathsf{W}}
). We do not need the precise
definition of this space, but only the following property,
cf.
[
3
]
*Theorem 2.14. There is an algebra homomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{v}\to Mul(\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))),\quad\mu\mapsto\mu\star

such that if
\sigma
is an irreducible representation of
G(F_{v})
, and
f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
then

\sigma(\mu\star f)=\mu(\lambda_{\sigma})\sigma(f)

where
\lambda_{\sigma}
is the infinitesimal character of
\sigma
. Let

\iota_{v}
be the involution on
\mathcal{M}_{v}
such that
\iota_{v}(\mu)(z)=\mu(-\underline{a}_{v}-z)
for all
z\in\mathfrak{t}_{v}^{*}
and
\mathcal{M}_{v}^{+}
be the subspace
consisting of elements invariant under this involution. Recall that we have
an involution
f\mapsto f^{\vee}\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
on
\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))
and the space

\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))^{+}
consisting of functions invariant under this involution. If

f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))^{+}
and
\iota_{v}(\mu)=\mu
, then
\mu\star f\in\mathcal{S}(G(F_{v}))^{+}
, i.e.

(3.5)

(\mu\star f)^{\vee}\chi_{1}\chi_{2}=\mu\star f.

To see this, we only need to check that the action of both sides on any
irreducible representation
\sigma
of
G(F_{v})
coincide. The left hand side
equals

(\sigma\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2})(\mu\star f)^{\vee}=\mu(-\lambda_{\sigma\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2}})(\sigma\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2})^{\vee}(f)=\mu(-\underline{a}_{v}-\lambda_{\sigma})(\sigma\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2})^{\vee}(f).

Since
\iota_{v}(\mu)=\mu
we have
\mu(-\underline{a}_{v}-\lambda_{\sigma})=\mu(\lambda_{\sigma})
. Moreover
f^{\vee}\chi_{1}\chi_{2}=f
implies
(\sigma\otimes\chi_{1}\chi_{2})^{\vee}(f)=\sigma(f)
. This proves (
3.5
).

Put
\mathcal{Z}_{G}=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\mathcal{Z}_{G(F_{v})}
,
\lambda=\otimes_{v\mid\infty}\lambda_{v}
the character of
\mathcal{Z}_{G}
,
\mathcal{M}=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\mathcal{M}_{v}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\mathcal{M}_{v}^{+}
.

Let
\mathtt{S}
be a finite set of finite places of
F
such that if
v\not\in\mathtt{S}
then
E_{v}/F_{v}
,
\pi_{v}
and
\chi_{v}
are all unramified.
Let
\mathtt{T}_{0}
be the set of nonsplit finite places of
F
and

\mathtt{T}=\mathtt{T}_{0}\cup\mathtt{S}
. Let

\mathcal{H}_{G}^{\mathtt{T}}=\bigotimes_{v\not\in\mathtt{T}}\mathcal{H}_{v}=\bigotimes_{v\not\in\mathtt{T}}C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathfrak{o}_{F_{v}})\backslash G(F_{v})/G(\mathfrak{o}_{F_{v}}))

be the spherical Hecke algebra away from
\mathtt{T}
. We fix an open compact
subgroup
K=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}K_{v}
such that if
v\not\in\mathtt{S}

then
K_{v}=G(\mathfrak{o}_{F_{v}})
.

All the above objects for
G
have their counterparts for
G^{\prime}
. For each
archimedean place
v
we have an algebra of holomorphic function
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{v}

which is identified with
\mathcal{M}_{v}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{v}
and we put

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

The fundamental lemma

Assume that
E/F
is unramified with odd residue characteristic. Let

\widetilde{\eta}(x)=(-1)^{\val_{E}(x)}
be the unique unramified character
that extends
\eta
to
E^{\times}
. Let
\tau\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}
be a purely
imaginary element which is used in the definition of the transfer factor

\kappa^{S^{\prime}}
(cf. (
6.1
)).

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
s^{\prime}=s^{\prime}(\alpha)\in S^{\prime}(F)
and
g=g(\beta)\in G(F)
be matching
regular semisimple elements. Then

(7.1)

\kappa^{S^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})O^{S^{\prime}}(s^{\prime},\id_{S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})})=\kappa^{G}(g)O^{G}(g,\id_{G(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}).

It is straightforward to see that this theorem implies
Theorem
2.2
. The rest of this section is devoted to prove this
theorem. We distinguish two cases depending on
\overline{\alpha}\alpha

being elliptic or not. The elliptic case will be deduced from the base change
fundamental lemma and the calculations in
[
8
]
. The nonelliptic case
will be treated using the parabolic descent and induction on
n
.

Before we prove the theorem, let us first explain that the validity
of (
7.1
) is independent of the character
\chi
. In fact, the left
hand side equals

\chi(-\overline{\alpha})\int\id_{S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}^{-1}\\
&h_{2}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&1\\
1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}&-\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{h_{1}}\\
&\overline{h_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi(\overline{h_{2}}h_{2}^{-1})\widetilde{\eta}(h_{1}h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

Since
\det\overline{h_{2}}h_{2}^{-1}\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}
and
\chi
is
unramified, the above expression equals

\chi(-\overline{\alpha})\int\id_{S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}^{-1}\\
&h_{2}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&1\\
1-\alpha\overline{\alpha}&-\overline{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{h_{1}}\\
&\overline{h_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right)\widetilde{\eta}(h_{1}h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

The right hand side of (
7.1
) equals

\int\id_{G(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}g^{-1}\\
&\overline{g}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta\\
\overline{\beta}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
&\overline{h}\end{pmatrix}\right)\chi(g^{-1}h)\mathrm{d}g\mathrm{d}h.

Taking determinant we see that

\det(g\overline{g})^{-1}\det(1-\beta\overline{\beta})\det(h\overline{h})\in\mathfrak{o}_{F}^{\times}.

Recall that
s^{\prime}(\alpha)
and
g(\beta)
match if
-(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}
and
\beta\overline{\beta}
have the same characteristic polynomial, which implies that

(1-\beta\overline{\beta})^{-1}
and
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
have the
same characteristic polynomial. As
\chi
is unramified we obtain

\chi(g^{-1}h)=\chi(\alpha)=\chi(-\overline{\alpha})
. Thus the right
hand side of (
7.1
) equals

\chi(-\overline{\alpha})\int\id_{G(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}g^{-1}\\
&\overline{g}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta\\
\overline{\beta}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
&\overline{h}\end{pmatrix}\right)\mathrm{d}g\mathrm{d}h.

Thus from now on we assume that
\chi
is trivial. Under this assumption the
transfer factors on both sides of (
7.1
) are trivial.

7.2.
The elliptic case

Put
r=-(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}
and

x_{r}=\lvert\det(1-r)\rvert=\lvert\det\alpha\overline{\alpha}\rvert^{-1},\quad y_{r}=\lvert\det r\rvert=\lvert\det(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}\rvert.

In this subsection we always assume that
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
and hence

r
are elliptic regular semisimple in
\GL_{n}(F)
in the usual sense. Note
that this in particular covers the base case of the induction
n=1
.

Make a change of variable
h_{1}\mapsto\overline{h_{2}}h_{1}
on the left hand
side of (
7.1
) we obtain

(7.2)

\int\id_{S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}^{-1}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{h_{2}}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}&1\\
h_{2}^{-1}(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)h_{2}&-h_{2}^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\overline{h_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{h_{1}}\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\widetilde{\eta}(\det h_{1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

Here the integration is over
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(E)_{\alpha,\mathrm{twisted}}\backslash\GL_{n}(E)
and
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(E)
.

The first observation is that if
x_{r}<1
, i.e.
\lvert\det\alpha\rvert>1
, then
h_{2}^{-1}\alpha\overline{h_{2}}\not\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
for any
h_{2}

and therefore the
integral vanishes. Moreover under this condition, the right hand side
of (
7.1
) also vanishes by
[
8
]
*(3.9). Thus
Theorem
7.1
holds in this case.

Assume from now on that
x_{r}\geq 1
. By
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.4, either

y_{r}\leq x_{r}=1
or
x_{r}=y_{r}>1
. We distinguish two cases.

(1)

y_{r}\leq x_{r}=1
, i.e.
\det\alpha\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}
. Then
y_{r}=\lvert\det(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)\rvert
.

We need to make use of a lemma of Kottwitz’s,
cf.
[
16
]
*Lemma 8.8, which we recall in a special case for
readers’ convenience. The lemma says that if
\gamma\in\GL_{n}(E)
is
conjugate to an element in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and is regular semisimple,
then we can find an element
\delta\in\GL_{n}(E)
such that
\gamma=\delta\overline{\delta}=\overline{\delta}\delta
and
x^{-1}\gamma x\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
implies
x^{-1}\delta x\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.
Moreover if
\delta
satisfies the above property and
h\in\GL_{n}(E)
,
then
h^{-1}\overline{\delta}\overline{h}\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
if and
only if
h^{-1}\gamma h\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and
h\in\GL_{n}(F)\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.

Let us come back to our setup. If
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
is not
conjugate to an element in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
, by considering the lower
right block of the matrix, we see that (
7.2
) equals zero.
Let us assume that
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
is conjugate to an
element in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. We first apply this lemma to
\gamma=\alpha\overline{\alpha}
. We may further assume that
\alpha=\delta

has the property described in the lemma of Kottwitz’s. We then conclude
that
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(F)\GL(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. Thus we may replace the outer
integral in (
7.2
) by
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha}\backslash\GL_{n}(F)
. Here though
\alpha
might not be in
\GL_{n}(F)
but in

\GL_{n}(E)
, the group
\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha}
stands for all
h\in\GL_{n}(F)
that commutes with
\alpha
. We note that
\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha}=\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
. Indeed as explained
in
[
2
]
*Chapter 1, Proof of Lemma 1.1, as algebraic groups over

F
, the group
\GL_{n}(E)_{\alpha,\mathrm{twisted}}
is an inner form
of
\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
. Since
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
is regular semisimple, both are tori over
F
and
hence are canonically isomorphic, which implies that
\GL_{n}(E)_{\alpha,\mathrm{twisted}}=\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
. This in
particular implies that if
h\in\GL_{n}(E)_{\alpha,\mathrm{twisted}}

then
h\in\GL_{n}(F)
and thus
\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha}=\GL_{n}(E)_{\alpha,\mathrm{twisted}}=\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
. Therefore we
may replace the outer integral by
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}\backslash\GL_{n}(F)
.

Now
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(F)
and we apply the lemma again to
\gamma=h_{2}^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h_{2}
, and
\delta=h_{2}^{-1}\overline{\alpha}h_{2}
. Clearly this
\gamma
and
\delta
again
satisfy the conditions in the lemma of Kottwitz’s. By consider the
upper left corner of the matrix in the integrand, we conclude that
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(F)\GL(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and we may replace the outer integral
in (
7.2
) by
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(F)
.

The integrand in (
7.2
) is thus equivalent to the four
conditions

h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}h_{1},\ h_{2}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E}),\quad h_{1}^{-1},\ h_{2}^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})h_{2}h_{1}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F}).

As
\alpha
satisfies the conditions in Kottwitz’s lemma, the first two
conditions are implied by the other two. To see this, observe that

h_{1}^{-1}
and
h_{2}^{-1}(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)h_{2}h_{1}
being in

M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
implies both

h_{2}^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})h_{2},\quad h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})h_{2}h_{1}

are in
M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. As we have assume that
\det\alpha\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}
, we conclude that

h_{2}^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h_{2},\quad h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h_{2}h_{1}

are in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. That
\alpha
satisfies the conditions in the
lemma of Kottwitz’s implies

h_{1}^{-1}h_{2}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}h_{1},\quad h_{2}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}

are both in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.

The integral (
7.2
) thus simplifies to

\int\id_{M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})\times M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})}(h_{2}^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}h_{2}h_{1},h_{1}^{-1})\eta(\det h_{1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2},

where the domain of integration is
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(F)
and
h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}\backslash\GL_{n}(F)
.

In
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.5, a Hecke function
\Psi_{r}
on
\GL_{n}(F)
is
defined. By the calculation in
[
8
]
*p. 137, under the
assumption that
y_{r}\leq x_{r}=1
, this function equals

g\mapsto\int_{\GL_{n}(F)}\id_{\{X,Y\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F}),\ \lvert\det XY\rvert=y_{r}\}}(gh_{1},h_{1}^{-1})\eta(h_{1})\mathrm{d}h_{1}.

Therefore (
7.2
) equals

(7.3)

\int_{\GL_{n}(F)_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}\backslash\GL_{n}(F)}\Psi_{r}(h_{2}^{-1}rh_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

The right hand side of (
7.1
) is exactly the orbital integral
appearing in
[
8
]
. For any (twisted) elliptic regular semisimple

\beta\in\GL_{n}(E)
, a Heck function
\Phi_{\beta}
on
\GL_{n}(E)
is
defined in
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.6. Under the assumption
y_{r}\leq x_{r}=1
, by the calculation in
[
8
]
*p. 139 the characteristic
function of

\{X\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E}),\lvert\det X\rvert=y_{r}\}.

By
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.6 the right hand side of (
7.1
) equals

(7.4)

\int_{\GL_{n}(E)_{\beta,\mathrm{twisted}}\backslash\GL_{n}(E)}\Phi_{\beta}(h^{-1}\beta\overline{h})\mathrm{d}h.

If
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
is not conjugate to an element in

\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
, then neither is
1-\beta\overline{\beta}
. In this
case the above integral of
\Phi_{\beta}
equals zero so both sides
of (
7.1
) equal zero. Now assume that
\alpha\overline{\alpha}

is conjugate to an element in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. Let
\mathcal{H}(\GL_{n}(E))
and

\mathcal{H}(\GL_{n}(F))
be the spherical Hecke algebra of
\GL_{n}(E)
and

\GL_{n}(F)
respectively, and

\mathrm{bc}:\mathcal{H}(\GL_{n}(E))\to\mathcal{H}(\GL_{n}(F))

the usual base change map. By
[
8
]
*Corollary 3.8, we have

\Psi_{r}=\mathrm{bc}(\Phi_{\beta})
. Thus the desired
equality (
7.1
) is a consequence of the
identities (
7.3
) (
7.4
), and the base change
fundamental lemma
[
2
]
*Chapter 1, Theorem 4.5.

(2)

Let us now assume that
x_{r}=y_{r}>1
, i.e.
\lvert\det\alpha\rvert<1

and hence
\lvert\det(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)\rvert=1
. The integrand
of (
7.2
) is equivalent to the condition that

h_{2}^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\overline{h_{2}},\quad h_{1}^{-1}\overline{h_{2}}^{-1}\alpha h_{2}\overline{h_{1}},\quad h_{2}^{-1}(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)h_{2}\overline{h_{1}},\quad h_{1}^{-1}

are all in
M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. Note that

\lvert\det h_{1}^{-1}\rvert\leq 1,\quad\lvert\det h_{2}^{-1}(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)h_{2}\overline{h_{1}}\rvert\leq 1,

but

\lvert\det(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)\rvert=1.

It follows that
\lvert\det h_{1}\rvert=1
and hence
h_{1}\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.
Moreover since
\lvert\det\alpha\rvert<1
, we have
h_{2}^{-1}\overline{\alpha}\overline{h_{2}}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
implies
h_{2}^{-1}(1-\overline{\alpha}\alpha)h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. It follows that the
integral (
7.2
) reduces to

(7.5)

\int\id_{M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})}(h_{2}^{-1}\alpha\overline{h_{2}})\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

By
[
8
]
*Lemma 3.6, and the calculation
in
[
8
]
*p. 139-140, under the assumption
x_{r}=y_{r}>1
, the
right hand side of (
7.1
) equals

(7.6)

\int\id_{\{X^{-1}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E}),\lvert\det X\rvert_{E}=x_{r}\}}(h_{2}^{-1}\beta\overline{h_{2}})\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

It remains to explain that the
integrals (
7.5
) and (
7.6
)
equal. First the condition
\lvert\det X\rvert_{E}=x_{r}

in (
7.6
) is redundant as twisted conjugation does not
change the absolute value of the determinant. As
E/F
is unramified,

-1\in NE^{\times}
and hence there is a
\delta\in\mathfrak{o}_{E}^{\times}
such
that
\delta\overline{\delta}=-1
. Since
\alpha\overline{\alpha}

is elliptic and
\lvert\det\alpha\overline{\alpha}\rvert<1
, we conclude
the absolute values of its eigenvalues are all strictly less than one
(in a fixed splitting field of
F
). Thus

(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=1-\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}+\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{2}(\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{2}+\cdots

is convergent and gives a well-defined element in
\GL_{n}(F)
. Put

\gamma=\delta(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. By
assumption
\alpha\overline{\alpha}\in\GL_{n}(F)
and therefore

\gamma
commutes with
\alpha
and thus
(\gamma\alpha\overline{\gamma\alpha})^{-1}
and
\beta\overline{\beta}
have the
same characteristic polynomial. Replacing
\beta
by its twisted
conjugate, we may assume that
\beta^{-1}=\gamma\alpha
. Therefore
we need to explain

(7.7)

\eqref{eq:easy_case_simplified}=\int\id_{M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})}(h^{-1}\gamma\alpha\overline{h})\mathrm{d}h.

Assume that
h^{-1}\alpha\overline{h}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. Then
h^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and the absolute values of
all eigenvalues of it are strictly less than one. This implies

h^{-1}\gamma h=\delta\left(1-\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{1}h^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h+\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{2}(h^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h)^{2}+\cdots\right)

is convergent and is in
\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
(note the only denominators in
these binomial coefficients are powers of
2
). Thus
h^{-1}\gamma\alpha\overline{h}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. Conversely if
h^{-1}\gamma\alpha\overline{h}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
, then
h^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.
Since
1+h^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h=h^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})^{-1}h
and
the absolute values of
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
are all strictly less
than one, we have
h^{-1}(1-\alpha\overline{\alpha})h\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and hence
h^{-1}\alpha\overline{\alpha}h\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
. Then as before we conclude that
h^{-1}\gamma h\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
and hence
h^{-1}\alpha\overline{h}\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
.
This proves (
7.7
).

This finishes the proof of Theorem
7.1
when
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
is elliptic.

7.3.
Parabolic descent

To handle the nonelliptic case, we make use of the parabolic descent of the
orbital integrals. In this subsection, we deviate from the setup from
Theorem
7.1
and consider
O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime})
where
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(F))

and
x\in G^{\prime}(F)
is regular semisimple in general.

We fix integers
n_{1},n_{2}
with
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Let
Q=LU
be the
parabolic subgroup of
\GL_{2n}
of the form

L=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{(1)}&&m_{1}^{(2)}\\
&m_{2}^{(1)}&&m_{2}^{(2)}\\
m_{1}^{(3)}&&m_{1}^{(4)}\\
&m_{2}^{(3)}&&m_{2}^{(4)}\end{pmatrix},\quad U=\begin{pmatrix}1&u^{(1)}&&u^{(2)}\\
&1\\
&u^{(3)}&1&u^{(4)}\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix},

where

m_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{i}^{(1)}&m_{i}^{(2)}\\
m_{i}^{(3)}&m_{i}^{(4)}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2n_{i}},\quad\begin{pmatrix}u^{(1)}&u^{(2)}\\
u^{(3)}&u^{(4)}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2n_{1}\times 2n_{2}}.

By definition

O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime})=(\chi\widetilde{\eta})^{-1}(x)\int_{(H^{\prime}\times H^{\prime\prime})_{x}\backslash(H^{\prime}\times H^{\prime\prime})}f^{\prime}(h^{-1}xh^{\prime\prime})\chi_{H^{\prime}}(h)(\chi\eta)^{-1}(h^{\prime\prime})\mathrm{d}h\mathrm{d}h^{\prime\prime}

Suppose
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in L(E)
and
x_{i}\in\GL_{2n_{i}}(E)
,
i=1,2
.
Assume that
x_{i}\overline{x_{i}}^{-1}=s^{\prime}(\alpha_{i})
is regular semisimple in

S^{\prime}_{n_{i}}(F)
. Here we add the subscript to indicate the size of the various
groups and symmetric spaces. Let
\widetilde{r_{1}},\cdots,\widetilde{r_{n_{1}}}
and
\widetilde{s_{1}},\cdots,\widetilde{s_{n_{2}}}
be
the eigenvalues of
\alpha_{1}\overline{\alpha_{1}}
and

\alpha_{2}\overline{\alpha_{2}}
respectively (in some fixed algebraic closure
of
F
). Put

\lambda^{\prime}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq n_{1},1\leq j\leq n_{2}}(\widetilde{r_{i}}-\widetilde{s_{j}})^{-1}.

Then one checks that
\lambda^{\prime}\in F
.

Let
P=MN
be the upper triangular parabolic subgroup of
\GL_{n}(E)

corresponding to the partition
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Here
N
is the unipotent
radical, and
M
is the standard diagonal block Levi subgroup. Write
h=(h_{1},h_{2})
,
h_{1},h_{2}\in\GL_{n}(E)
. We make use of the Iwasawa
decomposition

h_{i}=u_{i}m_{i}k_{i},\ i=1,2,\quad h^{\prime\prime}=u^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}k^{\prime\prime}

where
u_{i}\in N(E)
,
m_{i}\in M(E)
,
k_{i}\in\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
,
u^{\prime}\in U(F)
,
and
k^{\prime}\in\GL_{2n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
. Then
O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime})
equals

(\chi\widetilde{\eta})^{-1}(x)\int f^{\prime}_{K}(m^{-1}u^{-1}xu^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime})\delta_{P(E)}(m)^{-1}\delta_{Q(F)}(m^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\chi_{H^{\prime}}(m)(\chi\eta)^{-1}(m^{\prime\prime})\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{d}m\mathrm{d}m^{\prime\prime},

where the domain of integration is
(m,m^{\prime\prime})\in((M\cap L)(E)\times L(F))_{x}\backslash((M\cap L)(E)\times L(F))
,
u\in N(E)\cap U(E)
,
u^{\prime\prime}\in U(F)
, and

f^{\prime}_{K}(g)=\int_{K_{H^{\prime}}}\int_{K_{H^{\prime\prime}}}f(k_{1}^{-1}gk_{2})\chi_{H^{\prime}}(k_{1})(\chi\eta)^{-1}(k_{2})\mathrm{d}k_{1}\mathrm{d}k_{2},

K_{H^{\prime}}=\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})\times\GL_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{E})
is a maximal open compact
subgroup of
H^{\prime}(F)
and
K_{H^{\prime\prime}}=\GL_{2n}(\mathfrak{o}_{F})
is a maximal open compact
subgroup of
H^{\prime\prime}(F)
.

Let us now show that

\delta_{A}:(N\cap U)(E)\times U(F)\to U(E),\quad(u,u^{\prime\prime})\mapsto u^{-1}xu^{\prime\prime}x^{-1}

is bijective and submersive everywhere. Direct computation gives that the
tangent map at
(u,u^{\prime\prime})
is given by

(\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{u})(E)\times\mathfrak{u}(E)\to\mathfrak{u}(E),\quad(X,Y)\mapsto-u^{-1}Xxu^{\prime\prime}x^{-1}+u^{-1}xu^{\prime\prime}Yx^{-1}.

Since
u
and
u^{\prime\prime}
are both unipotent, the determinant at any
(u,u^{\prime\prime})

equals the determinant at
(1,1)
. At the point
(1,1)
, the tangent can be
more explicitly written as

M_{n_{1}\times n_{2}}(E)\times M_{n_{1}\times n_{2}}(E)\times M_{2n_{1}\times 2n_{2}}(F)\to M_{2n_{1}\times 2n_{2}}(E),

and

(X_{1},X_{2},Y)\mapsto-\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\\
&X_{2}\end{pmatrix}+x_{1}Yx_{2}^{-1}.

Let
\rho(x)
be this map and we put

\Delta(x)=\delta_{Q(E)}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\lvert\det\rho(x)\rvert^{-1}.

This will be computed later. We make a change of variable
u^{-1}xu^{\prime}\mapsto wx
where
w\in U(E)
. Then we conclude that the orbital integral

O^{G^{\prime}}(x,f^{\prime})
equals

(\chi\widetilde{\eta})^{-1}(x)\int\delta_{Q(E)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)\Delta(x)f_{K}^{\prime}(m^{-1}wxm^{\prime\prime})\chi_{H^{\prime}}(m)(\chi\eta)^{-1}(m^{\prime\prime})\delta_{P(E)}(m)^{-1}\delta_{Q(F)}(m^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}m\mathrm{d}m^{\prime\prime}.

We note that
\delta_{P(E)}(m)=\delta_{Q(E)}(m)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore a
change of variable
w\mapsto mwm^{-1}

\widetilde{\Theta}_{\pi}(g)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}c_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}(X).

The case treated in
[
23
]
does not involve the character, but the same
argument goes through without change in our setup.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
v,w\in\pi
, and
f(g)=\langle v,\pi(g)w\rangle
be the matrix
coefficient. Then we have

(B.5)

\kappa^{G}(g)O^{G}(g,f)=\widetilde{\Theta}_{\pi}(g)\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(G)
supported in the
elliptic locus, we have

(B.6)

\int_{G}\varphi(g)\kappa^{G}(g)O^{G}(g,f)\mathrm{d}g=J_{\pi}(\varphi\kappa^{G})\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h.

Though
\kappa^{G}
is not defined on all
G
, as
\varphi
is locally constant
and compactly supported in the elliptic locus,
\varphi\kappa^{G}\in\mathcal{S}(G)

and
J_{\pi}(\varphi\kappa^{G})
makes sense.

Let us first note that because
(H\times H)_{g}
is an elliptic torus modulo
the center of
G
, up to some nonzero constant depending only on the choice
of the measures, the orbital integral of
f
equals

\kappa^{G}(g)\int_{Z\backslash H\times Z\backslash H}f(h_{1}^{-1}gh_{2})\chi(h_{1}^{-1}h_{2})^{-1}\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

As
\varphi
is supported on the elliptic locus, we have

\int_{G}\varphi(g)O^{G}(g,f)\mathrm{d}g=\int_{G}\int_{Z\backslash H\times Z\backslash H}\varphi(g)\kappa^{G}(g)f(h_{1}^{-1}gh_{2})\chi(h_{1}^{-1}h_{2})^{-1}\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}\mathrm{d}g.

The right hand of this integral is absolutely convergent. Thus we may change
the order of integration and conclude that this integral equals

(B.7)

\int_{Z\backslash H\times Z\backslash H}\langle v,\pi(h_{1}^{-1})\pi(\varphi\kappa^{G})\pi(h_{2})w\rangle\chi(h_{1}^{-1}h_{2})^{-1}\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

Since
\pi
is admissible, we may find elements
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
and
w_{1},\cdots,w_{r}
in
\pi
such that

\pi(\varphi\kappa^{G})v=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\langle v,v_{i}\rangle w_{i}.

It then follows by (
B.3
) that

\eqref{eq:integral_matrix_coefficient}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell(v)\overline{\ell(w_{i})}\ell(v_{i})\overline{\ell(w)}.

We also have

J_{\pi}(\varphi\kappa^{G})=\sum_{u}\ell(\pi(\varphi\kappa^{G})u)\overline{\ell(u)}=\sum_{u}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\langle u,v_{i}\rangle\ell(w_{i})\overline{\ell(u)}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell(w_{i})\overline{\ell(v_{i})}.

Thus (
B.6
) follows by another application
of (
B.3
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Let
X
be in a small neighbourhood of
0\in\mathfrak{s}
,
g\in G
,
g\theta(g)^{-1}=\exp X
. The character
expansion (
B.4
) gives

\widetilde{\Theta}_{\pi}(g)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}c_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}(X).

Note that
X
is elliptic if and only if
g
is elliptic. Since
\mathcal{O}=\{0\}

is the only nilpotent orbit with
\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}(tX)=\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}(X)
for all
X\in\mathfrak{s}
and
t\in F^{\times}
, to show
that
\widetilde{\Theta}_{\pi}(g)\not=0
for some elliptic
g\in G
which
sufficiently close to
1
, we only need to show that
c_{0}\not=0
.

We find a matrix coefficient
f
such that

\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h\not=0.

For this
f
we consider the expansion of both sides
of (
B.5
) when
g
is close to
1
and is elliptic. We
have

\sum_{\mathcal{O}}\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}(X)\mu_{\mathcal{O}}(f_{\natural})=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}c_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}(X)\times\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h.

The only terms on both sides of the expansion that are invariant under the
scaling
X\to tX
are those corresponding to
\mathcal{O}=0
. Thus by the
homogeneity property of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}}
and
\widehat{\mu_{\mathcal{O}}}
, we conclude
that

\Gamma_{0}(X)\mu_{0}(f_{\natural})=c_{0}\widehat{\mu_{0}}(X)\times\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h.

By our choice of
f
we have

\mu_{0}(f_{\natural})=\int_{Z\backslash H}f(h)\chi(h)^{-1}\mathrm{d}h\not=0.

If
c_{0}=0
, then
\Gamma_{0}(X)=0
if
X
is elliptic in a neighbourhood of

0
. By Lemma
B.1
,
\Gamma_{0}(Y)=0
if
Y
is not elliptic and
hence is identically zero in a neighbourhood of
0
. This is impossible by
Lemma
B.1
. Therefore
c_{0}\not=0
.
∎

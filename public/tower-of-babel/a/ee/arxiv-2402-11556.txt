Abstract
. We relate polyhedral products of topological spaces to graph products of groups. The loop homology algebras of polyhedral products are identified with the universal enveloping algebras of the Lie algebras associated with central series of graph products. By way of application, we describe the restricted Lie algebra associated with the lower 2 2 -central series of a right-angled Coxeter group and identify its universal enveloping algebra with the loop homology of the Davis–Januszkiewicz space.

Introduction

The polyhedral product space
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}
is defined for a sequence of pairs of topological spaces
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})=((X_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(X_{m},A_{m}))
and a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on a finite set
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
. When each
A_{i}
is a point, the polyhedral product
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\mathcal{K}}=({\textbf{{X}}},\mathit{pt})^{\mathcal{K}}
interpolates between the
m
-fold wedge
X_{1}\vee\cdots\vee X_{m}
and the
m
-fold product
X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{m}
. The study of polyhedral products takes its origin in toric topology
[
BP
]
and has recently developed into an active area within homotopy theory
[
BBC
]
. Important examples are the Davis–Januszkiewicz space
(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}}
, the moment-angle complex
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{\mathcal{K}}
and its real analogue
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}
. The latter is a cubic complex that features in geometric group theory.

The polyhedral product
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\mathcal{K}}
can also be defined for a sequence of objects
{\textbf{{X}}}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
in an arbitrary category
c
with finite products and colimits, or in a symmetric monoidal category with finite colimits. For many categories of algebraic objects, like groups
grp
or associative algebras
ass
, the polyhedral product
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\mathcal{K}}
depends only on the one-skeleton (graph)
\mathcal{K}^{1}=\Gamma
and is referred to as the
graph product
(see Proposition
2.3
). This reflects the fact that a set of pairwise commuting elements of a group (algebra) generates a commutative subgroup (subalgebra). On the contrary, the polyhedral product of topological spaces is not determined by the one-skeleton
\mathcal{K}^{1}
. For example, when
\mathcal{K}=\partial\Delta_{[m]}
, the boundary of simplex, the polyhedral product
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\partial\Delta_{[m]}}
is known in homotopy theory as the
fat wedge
of
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
, and it is different from the
m
-fold product
{\textbf{{X}}}^{\Delta_{[m]}}=X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{m}
.

Graph products are familiar objects in combinatorial and geometric group theory. Well-known examples are the
right-angled Artin group

\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}=F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\big/\bigl(a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}\text{ for }\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}\bigr),

which is the graph product
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
and the
right-angled Coxeter group

\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}=F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\big/\bigl(a_{i}^{2}=1,\;a_{i}a_{j}=a_{j}a_{i}\text{ for }\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}\bigr),

which is the graph product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\mathcal{K}}
. (We denote the group of integers by
\mathbb{Z}
and the group/field of residues modulo prime
p
by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.)

A number of results in homotopy theory of polyhedral products and geometric group theory can be interpreted as the property of some functor to preserve graph products. These include the constructions of the classifying spaces for right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups, the description of their cohomology, and the description of the loop homology algebras of polyhedral products. We give a more detailed account in Section
2
. Group-theoretic and homotopy-theoretic results of this sort usually come in pairs, with similar formulations but rather different proofs. For example, there are very similar descriptions of the commutator subgroup
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
of a right-angled Coxeter group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}

[
PV
]
and the commutator subalgebra
H_{*}(\varOmega\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})
of the Pontryagin algebra (loop homology)
H_{*}(\varOmega(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}})

[
GPTW
]
. Furthermore, when
\mathcal{K}
is a flag complex, the commutator subgroup
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
and the commutator subalgebra
H_{*}(\varOmega\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})
are free if and only if the one-skeleton
\mathcal{K}^{1}
is a chordal graph. There is also a similarity in the criteria for
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
and
H_{*}(\varOmega\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}})
to be a one-relator group and algebra, respectively
[
GIPS
]
. Graph products can also be defined for simplicial groups
[
Ca
]
, and their classifying spaces are related to polyhedral products in the simplicial homotopy-theoretical setting.

In this paper we give evidence that group-theoretical results on graph products and homotopy-theoretical results on polyhedral products can be linked to each other via central series of groups and their associated graded Lie algebras. This manifests itself most directly in the case of right-angled Artin groups
\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Namely, the Lie algebra associated with the lower central series
\gamma(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})=\{\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}=\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\supset\gamma_{2}(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\supset\ldots\}
is the ‘‘partially commutative’’ quotient of a free Lie algebra:

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\cong\mathit{FL}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\big/\bigl([v_{i},v_{j}]=0\;\text{for }\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}\bigr).

This extends the classical result of Magnus on the lower central series of a free group and is proved by a similar method based on the Magnus map
[
DK
,
PS
,
Wa
,
BHS
]
. The universal enveloping algebra of
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is the partially commutative associative algebra. The latter is isomorphic to the loop homology of the polyhedral product of spheres
(S^{2p+1})^{\mathcal{K}}
by the result of
[
BG
]
:

\mathcal{U}(\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}))\cong H_{*}(\varOmega(S^{2p+1})^{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{Z}).

The lower central series of a right-angled Coxeter group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
is more subtle. The associated Lie algebra
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
that is not finitely presented
[
Ve
,
VR
]
. It cannot be readily expressed as a graph product, and there is no explicit connection to loop homology of polyhedral products.
Restricted
Lie algebras associated with
p
-central
series better serve this purpose.

The
lower
p
-central series
of a group
G
is the fastest descending central series

\{G=\gamma^{[p]}_{1}(G)\supset\gamma^{[p]}_{2}(G)\supset\cdots\}

with the property that
\bigl(\gamma^{[p]}_{k}(G)\bigr)^{p}\subset\gamma^{[p]}_{kp}(G)
for
k=1,2,\ldots
. The associated graded abelian group

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma^{[p]}(G)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\gamma^{[p]}_{k}(G)/\gamma^{[p]}_{k+1}(G)

is a
restricted Lie algebra
or
p
-Lie algebra
: it is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module with a Lie bracket and a
p
-power operation
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
. The details are given in Section
4
.

In Theorem
6.4
we give the following explicit presentation for the
2
-Lie algebra associated with the lower
2
-central series of
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
:

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma^{[2]}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\cong\mathit{FL}_{2}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\big/\bigl(u_{i}^{[2]}=0,\;[u_{i},u_{j}]=0\text{ for }\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}\bigr).

The right hand side is the graph product of trivial
2
-Lie algebras
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\langle u\rangle=\mathit{FL}_{2}(u)/(u^{[2]}=0)
with one generator. There is also a generalisation to graph products of elementary abelian
p
-groups for an arbitrary prime
p
(Theorem
6.1
). The proof uses the result of Quillen
[
Qu
]
relating the lower
p
-central series of a group to the filtration of its group algebra by the powers of the augmentation ideal alongside with the construction of an analogue of the Magnus map. The universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}_{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma^{[2]}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}))
is identified with the loop homology of
(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficients (Proposition
6.5
).
As a consequence, we obtain the following relation between the fundamental group of the polyhedral product of
\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}
and the loop homology of the polyhedral product of
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
for flag complexes
\mathcal{K}
:

\mathcal{U}_{2}\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\gamma^{[2]}\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}}\bigr)\cong H_{*}\bigl(\varOmega(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{Z}_{2}\bigl).

Recall that
\pi_{1}((\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})^{\mathcal{K}})=(\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty}))^{\mathcal{K}}=\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

As a functor, the polyhedral product

\displaystyle I\longmapsto{\textbf{{X}}}^{I},

{\textbf{{X}}}^{\mathcal{K}}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{colim}\nolimits^{\text{\sc c}}\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{K}}({\textbf{{X}}})=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{colim}\nolimits^{\text{\sc c}}_{I\in\mathcal{K}}{\textbf{{X}}}^{I}.

The

A

A simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}

Complete subgraphs in a simple graph

Every flag complex

The polyhedral product

of pointed topological spaces

is known as their

Definition 2.2
.

A symmetric monoidal category

{\textbf{{X}}}^{\partial\Delta_{[m]}}\to\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}

The following fat categories will be used throughout the paper:

grp
:

groups (the monoidal product is cartesian, the coproduct is the free product);

tmn

topological monoids (the monoidal product is cartesian, the coproduct is the free product);

ass

In each of these categories, a set of pairwise commuting elements of a group (algebra) generates a commutative subgroup (subalgebra). On the other hand, the category

3.
Central series and associated Lie algebras

A
Lie algebra
over a commutative unitary ring
R
is an
R
-module
L
together with an
R
-linear map
L\times L\to L
,
(x,y)\mapsto[x,y]
, satisfying (1)
[x,x]=0
and (2) the Jacobi identity
[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]=0
for all
x,y,z\in L
. Condition (1) implies
[x,y]=-[y,x]
.

There is a functor
\mathcal{L}\colon\text{\sc ass}\to\text{\sc lie}
that takes an associative
R
-algebra
A
to the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}A
on the same
R
-module with the Lie bracket defined by
[x,y]=xy-yx
. The functor
\mathcal{L}
has a left adjoint,
\mathcal{U}\colon\text{\sc lie}\to\text{\sc ass}
, satisfying

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{\text{\sc ass}}(\mathcal{U}L,A)=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{\text{\sc lie}}(L,\mathcal{L}A).

The associative algebra
\mathcal{U}L
together with the canonical Lie algebra homomorphism
L\to\mathcal{L}\mathcal{U}L
is called the
universal enveloping algebra
of
L
. Explicitly,
\mathcal{U}L
is the quotient of
TL
(the free associate algebra on
L
) by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements
xy-yx-[x,y]
,
x,y\in L
. If
L
is a free
R
-module, then
L\to\mathcal{L}\mathcal{U}L
is injective
[
Se
, Corollary III.1]
.

Let
G
be a group (if necessary, viewed as an object in
tgp
with discrete topology). Given two subgroups
H
and
W
of
G
, denote by
(H,W)
the subgroup generated by all commutators
(h,w)
with
h\in H
and
w\in W
.
In particular,
(G,G)=G^{\prime}
is the commutator subgroup.

A (descending)
central series
or
central filtration
on
G
is a sequence of subgroups
\varGamma(G)=\{\varGamma_{k}(G)\colon k\geq 1\}
such that
\varGamma_{1}(G)=G
,

\varGamma_{k+1}(G)\subset\varGamma_{k}(G)
and
(\varGamma_{k}(G),\varGamma_{l}(G))\subset\varGamma_{k+\ell}(G)
for any
k,\ell
. It follows immediately that each
\varGamma_{k}(G)
is a normal subgroup and the quotient
\varGamma_{k}(G)/\varGamma_{k+1}(G)
is abelian.

The fastest descending central series is the
lower central series

\gamma(G)
, given by
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
and
\gamma_{k}(G)=(\gamma_{k-1}(G),G)
for
k\geq 2
.

The
associated Lie algebra
of a central series
\varGamma(G)
is the associated graded abelian group

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits_{\bullet}\varGamma(G)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\varGamma_{k}(G)/\varGamma_{k+1}(G)

with the Lie bracket defined by

[\overline{g}_{k},\overline{g}_{\ell}]=\overline{(g_{k},g_{\ell})},

where
\overline{g}_{k}
denotes the image of
g_{k}\in\varGamma_{k}(G)
in the quotient group
\varGamma_{k}(G)/\varGamma_{k+1}(G)
.

Given a set
S
, the
free Lie algebra

\mathit{FL}(x_{s}\colon s\in S)
generated by

is defined by the universal property: any set map
S\to L
to a Lie algebra

extends uniquely to a homomorphism
\mathit{FL}(x_{s}\colon s\in S)\to L
of Lie algebras.
By the classical result of Magnus
[

, Theorem 5.12]
, the Lie algebra associated with the lower central series of a free group

is the free Lie algebra

over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Let

be a field of characteristic

over

, is a Lie algebra

-power

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

for

(2)

(\alpha x)^{[p]}=\alpha^{p}x^{[p]}
for

and

;

(3)

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y),

where

is defined by the expansion

[\ldots[[x,\underbrace{\lambda x+y],\lambda x+y],\ldots,\lambda x+y}_{p-1}]=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}is_{i}(x,y)\lambda^{i-1}

for
x,y\in L
and
\lambda\in R
.

For example, if
p=2
then
(x+y)^{[2]}=x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}+[x,y]
, and if
p=3
then

(x+y)^{[3]}=x^{[3]}+y^{[3]}+[[x,y],y]-[[x,y],x]
.

An associative algebra
A
over
R
is a restricted Lie algebra with the commutator bracket
[x,y]=xy-yx
and the

-power operation

. This defines a functor
\mathcal{L}_{p}\colon\text{\sc ass}\to\text{\sc lie}_{p}
to the category of restricted Lie algebras over

. The left adjoint functor
\mathcal{U}_{p}\colon\text{\sc lie}_{p}\to\text{\sc ass}
defines the

\mathcal{U}_{p}L
of

together with the canonical homomorphism

of restricted Lie algebras. Explicitly,
\mathcal{U}_{p}L
is the quotient of the free associatie algebra
TL
by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements

and

,

. If
L
is a free
R
-module (e.g., if

is a field of characteristic

), then

is injective
[
Ja
, Theorem V.12]
.

5.
Group algebras

Given a commutative unitary ring
R
and a group
G
, the
group algebra

R[G]
is the associative algebra
whose underlying
R
-module is the the free module with basis
\{g\in G\}
and multiplication is given on basis elements by the group operation. The group algebra functor
\text{\sc grp}\to\text{\sc ass}
is left adjoint to the group of units functor
\text{\sc ass}\to\text{\sc grp}
:

(5.1)

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{\text{\sc ass}}(R[G],A)=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{\text{\sc grp}}(G,A^{\times}),

where
A^{\times}
denotes the group of invertible elements in an associative algebra
A
. This defines the universal property of the group algebra.

Proposition 5.1
.

The group algebra functor
\text{\sc grp}\to\text{\sc ass}
preserves graph products, that is,
R[{\textbf{{G}}}^{\mathcal{K}}]\cong(R[{\textbf{{G}}}])^{\mathcal{K}}
.

Proof.

The group algebra functor preserves products and, since it is left adjoint, it preserves colimits.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose a group
G
is given by generators and relations:

G=F(a_{i}\colon i\in I)/(r_{j}=1\;\emph{for}j\in J).

Then

R[G]\cong T(v_{i},v_{i}^{-1}\colon i\in I)/(v_{i}v_{i}^{-1}=1\;\emph{for}i\in I,\;R_{j}=1\;\emph{for}j\in J),

where
R_{j}
is the free monomial in
v_{i},v_{i}^{-1}
corresponding to the word

.

Proof.

Let
V
denote the associative algebra on the right hand side of the isomorphism being proved. We need to check that
V
satisfies the universal property given by the adjunction (
5.1
). There is a homomorphism
i\colon G\to V^{\times}
defined on the generators by
i(a_{i})=v_{i}
. We need to show that for any homomorphism

from

to the group of units of an algebra

there is a unique homomorphism

such that

. The latter identity implies that

and

, which defines the homomorphism

uniquely.
∎

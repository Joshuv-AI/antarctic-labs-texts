Abstract
Two generic properties of the Neumann–Poincaré operator are investigated. We prove that non-zero eigenvalues of the Neumann–Poincaré operator on smooth boundaries in three dimensions and higher are generically simple in the sense of Baire category. We also prove that the functions defined by the fundamental solutions to the Laplace operator located at points outside the surface are generically cyclic vectors in the sense that the collection of those points where the functions are not cyclic vectors is of measure zero.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) with the
C^{1,\alpha}
-smooth boundary for some
\alpha>0
. The Neumann-Poincaré (abbreviated to NP) operator
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
(the
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
-1/2
) is defined by

{\mathcal{K}}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}[\psi](x):=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n_{x}}\Gamma(x,y)\psi(y)\;dS_{y},\quad x\in\partial\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\partial_{n_{x}}
denotes the outer normal derivative (with respect to
x
) on
\partial\Omega
,
\Gamma
the fundamental solution to the Laplacian, namely,

\Gamma(x,y)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\log{|x-y|},\quad&d=2,\\
\displaystyle\frac{-1}{d(d-2)c_{d}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2}},\quad&d\geq 3,\end{cases}

c_{d}
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
dS_{y}
the surface element on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}
be the single layer potential on
\partial\Omega
, namely,

{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi](x):=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Gamma(x,y)\psi(y)\;dS_{y},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.2)

If we consider
{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi](x)
for
x\in\partial\Omega
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}
maps
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
into
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. As such an operator,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}
is invertible if
d\geq 3
. If
d=2
, it may not be invertible. But, if it is so, we may dilate
\Omega
and the single layer potential on the dilated surface is invertible. Since the NP spectrum (spectrum of the NP operator) is invariant under dilation, we may assume from the beginning that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}:H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is invertible. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the
H^{-1/2}
-
H^{1/2}
pairing on
\partial\Omega
. Then the bilinear form

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}:=-\langle\varphi,{\mathcal{S}}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi]\rangle

(1.3)

on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
becomes an inner product, and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
is self-adjoint on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
equipped with this inner product.

If
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
as we assume in this paper, the NP operator
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
is compact on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, and hence
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
admits sequences of nonzero eigenvalues of finite multiplicities converging to
0
while
0
may or may not be an eigenvalue. These NP eigenvalues (eigenvalues of the NP operator) may have multiplicities higher than 1. For example,
0
is the only NP eigenvalue (other than
1/2
) on disks. It is shown in
[
10
]
(see also
[
9
]
) that
0
is an NP eigenvalue of infinite multiplicities on lemniscates. If
\Omega
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then NP eigenvalues are
\frac{1}{2(2k+1)}

with the multiplicity
2k+1
(
k=0,1,2,\ldots
). In this paper, we show that the hypersurface with non-simple nonzero NP eigenvalues is rare by showing that nonzero NP eigenvalues are generically simple. Here, the notion ‘generic’ means the collection
E
with such a property in a topological space
X
contains an
intersection of at most countable number of open dense subsets in
X
. The terminology is in general
used when
X
is a Baire space: in such spaces the set
E
is dense and said to be Baire typical. There are many works on generic properties of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions (see
[
15
]
and references therein). We also mention the article
[
1
]
which motivates the study of this paper. There the generic simplicity of eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on compact Riemannian manifolds is proved.

To define the class of boundary surfaces, let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 3
) with the smooth boundary
\partial D
. Fix constants
L>0
and
c>0
(
c
to be chosen), and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=

\displaystyle\{G=(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{d})\mid G\text{ is smooth in a neighborhood of }\partial D,

\displaystyle\quad\|G-I\|_{C^{1}(\partial D)}\leq c,\ \ |G(x)-G(y)|>L|x-y|\text{ for all }x\neq y\in\partial D\},

(1.4)

where
I
stands for the identity mapping. We choose
c
so small that
G(\partial D)
is the boundary of a bounded domain for each
G\in\mathcal{M}
which we denote by
D_{G}
, namely,
\partial D_{G}=G(\partial D)
. We introduce the metric on
\mathcal{M}
:

p_{n}(G)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\max_{x\in\partial D,\,|\alpha|\leq n}|\partial^{\alpha}g_{i}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for
n=0,1,\dots
, where
\alpha
is multi-indices. The distance on
\mathcal{M}
is defined by

\rho(F,G)=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{p_{n}(F-G)}{1+p_{n}(F-G)}.

Note that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is the collection of all smooth functions
G=(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{d})
satisfying

|G(x)-G(y)|\geq L|x-y|

for all
x,y\in\partial D
and
(\overline{\mathcal{M}},\rho)
is a complete metric space.

We have the following theorem regarding generic simplicity of the NP eigenvalues:

Theorem 1.1
.

The set
\mathcal{M}_{s}
of all
G\in\mathcal{M}
such that all non-zero NP eigenvalues on
\partial D_{G}
are simple is Baire typical in
(\overline{\mathcal{M}},\rho)
.

The second generic property is regarding cyclic vectors. Let
K
be a bounded operator on a separable Hilbert space
H
. For
\xi\in H
, we denote by
L_{\xi}
the cyclic subspace generated by
\xi
, namely,

L_{\xi}=\overline{\text{span}\{K^{n}\xi\mid n=0,1,2,\ldots\}}.

(1.5)

The vector
\xi
is called a cyclic vector for
K
if

L_{\xi}=H.

(1.6)

We also consider
N
-cyclic vectors. The
N
-tuple
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{N})
of vectors in
H
is called
N
-cyclic vectors for if

L_{\xi_{1}}+L_{\xi_{2}}+\cdots+L_{\xi_{N}}=H.

(1.7)

It is worth mentioning that
E=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{N})
is called
N
-supercyclic vectors for
K
if
\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}K^{n}(E)
is dense in
H
.

In relation to
N
-cyclic vectors for the NP operator
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
on
\partial\Omega
, we seek a class of explicit functions on
\partial\Omega
which provides
N
-cyclic vectors generically in some sense, under the assumption that the spectral multiplicity of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
is finite. The spectral multiplicity is the maximal multiplicity of the eigenvalues (see
[
7
, Section 51]
). As candidates of such functions, we consider

q_{z}(x):=v\cdot\nabla_{z}\Gamma(z-x),\quad x\in\partial\Omega

(1.8)

for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
where
v
is a constant vector. Actually functions
q_{z}
were used to extract spectral information of the NP operator on polygons numerically in
[
8
]
.

We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) with the
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary for some
\alpha>0
. Suppose that the spectral multiplicity of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is
N<+\infty
. Then
(q_{z_{1}},q_{z_{2}},\ldots,q_{z_{N}})
are
N
-cyclic vectors for
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
for almost all
(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega})^{N}
, namely,

L_{q_{z_{1}}}+L_{q_{z_{2}}}+\cdots+L_{q_{z_{N}}}=H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega).

(1.9)

We emphasize that any
(q_{z_{1}},q_{z_{2}},\ldots,q_{z_{M}})
with
M<N
cannot generate
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
(see Lemma
3.1
). If all eigenvalues are simple,
q_{z}
is a cyclic vector for
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
for almost all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

We also obtain the following theorem with the same proof.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
) with the
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary for some
\alpha>0
. Let
\varphi_{n}
,

, be an orthogonal system of eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
. For almost all

,

satisfies the following property:

\langle q_{z},\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}\neq 0\quad\text{for all $n$}.

(1.10)

The above theorem shows that for almost all

,

contains all eigenmodes. The theorem remains true for any system of functions
\phi
belonging to a
countable
subset
of

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We restrict ourselves to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
even though the argument can be applied to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
.

For a bounded domain
\Omega
with the smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, we define the perturbation
\Omega(ha)
for a smooth function
a
with
\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}|a(x)|\leq 1
and a small real parameter
h
as

\partial\Omega(ha)=\{x+ha(x)n(x)\,;\,x\in\partial\Omega\}.

(2.1)

The proof of this section relies heavily on the following Hadamard variational type formula obtained in
[
6
]
. We also mention
[
13

and references therein for general theory for analytic branches of eigenvalues and eigenvectors for perturbation of a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let
\lambda\neq 0,1/2
be an eigenvalue of the NP operator
\mathcal{K}^{*}
on
\partial\Omega
with the eigenspace
E
.
Then there are
h_{0}>0
and real analytic functions
h\mapsto\lambda^{(i)}(h)
,
h\to e^{(i)}(h)
defined for
|h|<h_{0}
,

, such that

(i)

\lambda^{(i)}(0)=\lambda
for all

and
\{e^{(1)}(0),\dots,e^{(\dim E)}(0)\}
is a basis of
E
,

(ii)

for each
h
the numbers
\lambda^{(i)}(h)
are eigenvalues of
\mathcal{K}_{h}^{*}
(the NP operator on
\partial\Omega(ha)
) with eigenfunctions
e^{(i)}(h)
satisfying

\langle e^{(i)}(h),e^{(i)}(h)\rangle_{\partial\Omega(ha)}=1.

(2.2)

For each analytic branch
\lambda^{(i)}(h)
and
e^{(i)}(h)
, we have

\frac{d\lambda^{(i)}}{dh}(0)=\int_{\partial\Omega}a\left[|\nabla_{\partial}\mathcal{S}[e^{(i)}(0)]|^{2}-c(\lambda)(\partial_{n}\mathcal{S}[e^{(i)}(0)]|_{-})^{2}\right]d\sigma,

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{S}
is the single layer potential on
\partial\Omega
, the subscript

indicates the limit (to

) from
\Omega
,

\nabla_{\partial}u^{(i)}=\nabla u^{(i)}-(\partial_{n}u^{(i)})n

c(\lambda)=\frac{1+2\lambda}{1-2\lambda}.

Some remarks on Theorem
2.1
are in order. The normalization (

) differs from

]
by the constant factor and (

) differs accordingly. There the normalization is given by

one can see

\|\nabla\mathcal{S}_{h}[e^{(i)}(h)]\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega(ha))}^{2}=(1/2-\lambda^{(i)}(h))\langle e^{(i)}(h),e^{(i)}(h)\rangle_{\partial\Omega(ha)}.

The function

is analytic in the sense that there is a sequence of functions
\psi_{0}^{(i)},\psi_{1}^{(i)},\psi_{2}^{(i)},\ldots
in

such that

e^{(i)}(h)=\psi_{0}^{(i)}+h\psi_{1}^{(i)}+h^{2}\psi_{2}^{(i)}+\cdots.

which converges in

Note that the perturbation (

which is under consideration in this paper is given in terms of diffeomorphisms. So, in order to be able to apply Theorem

Lemma 2.2
.

For each
F\in\mathcal{M}
, there is a constant
C>0
such that for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
the following hold:

(i)

If
\rho(F,G)<\epsilon
, then there is

with
\max_{x\in\partial D_{F}}|a(x)|\leq 1

\partial D_{G}=\partial D_{F}(C\epsilon a).

(2.5)

(ii)

If a bounded domain

with the smooth boundary satisfies
\partial\Omega=\partial D_{F}(\epsilon a)
for some

with
\max_{x\in\partial D_{F}}|a(x)|\leq 1

G\in\mathcal{M}
with
\rho(F,G)<C\epsilon
such that

\partial\Omega=\partial D_{G}.

(2.6)

Proof.

(i) Fix
F\in\mathcal{M}
and suppose that

satisfies
\rho(F,G)<\epsilon
. Let
H:=G\circ F^{-1}
. Then,
H(\partial D_{F})=\partial D_{G}

for some

(2.7)

We claim that for each

, there is unique

such that

. Moreover,

. Once the claim is proved, we denote such a

by

. Then it is easy to prove, using implicit function theorem, that

is a smooth function. So, (

) is proved.

To prove the claim, let
x_{0}\in\partial D_{F}
. After rotation and translation if necessary, we may assume that
x_{0}=(0,0,0)
and there is an open neighborhood

of

which is represented as a graph of a smooth function

, namely,

. Here

. In particular,

. Let

\pi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2})

Thus, if

(0,0)\in\pi(H(U))
, which implies that
(0,0,t)\in H(U)\subset\partial D_{G}
for some

. Because of (

),

.

To prove uniqueness of such

, let
\eta_{F}
be a defining function for
\partial D_{F}
, namely,
\eta_{F}
is smooth in a neighborhood

(2.8)

Note that

\partial D_{G}
. Suppose that there are two different

with
|t_{j}|<C\epsilon
such that
(0,0,t_{j})\in\partial D_{G}
. Then

0=\eta_{G}(0,0,t_{1})-\eta_{G}(0,0,t_{2})=\frac{\partial\eta_{G}}{\partial x_{3}}(0,0,t_{*})(t_{1}-t_{2})

for some

. Thus,
\frac{\partial\eta_{G}}{\partial x_{3}}(0,0,t_{*})=0
, and hence
|\frac{\partial\eta_{F}}{\partial x_{3}}(0,0,t_{*})|<C\epsilon
. Thus, if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, then this contradicts (
2.8
).

(ii) Suppose
\partial\Omega=\partial D_{F}(\epsilon a)
. If
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, the mapping

is bijection from

, which can be extended to a tubular neighborhood of

as an injective mapping. In fact, the mapping

|Q(x)-Q(y)|>(1-C\epsilon)|x-y|

for every

. If

. This completes the proof.

The following lemma will be used to prove Lemma
2.4
. We emphasize that estimates in the lemma are not optimal.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(

) with the smooth boundary and let

be a smooth function on

\max_{x\in\partial D_{F}}|a(x)|\leq 1

\|\mathcal{K}^{*}[\varphi]-\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}^{*}[\varphi\circ\Psi^{-1}]\circ\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\epsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\|\mathcal{S}[\varphi]-\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}[\varphi\circ\Psi^{-1}]\circ\Psi\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\epsilon\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

for all sufficiently small

(

\partial\Omega(\epsilon a)

\displaystyle K_{\epsilon}(\Psi(x),\Psi(y))=\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{\epsilon}(T(u))|^{2}}}\frac{(T(u)-T(v))\cdot R^{\epsilon}(u,v)(T(u)-T(v))}{|T(u)-T(v)|^{3}(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,v))^{3/2}},

. So, if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, then
A_{\lambda}B
is invertible, and hence (
3.1
) holds.

Conversely, assume that (
1.7
) does not hold. Then there is a normalized eigenfunction
\varphi
of
K
such that
\varphi\in(L_{\xi_{1}}+\cdots+L_{\xi_{N}})^{\perp}
. Let
\lambda
be the eigenvalue corresponding to
\varphi
and
\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{N_{\lambda}}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the eigenspace of
\lambda
with
\varphi_{1}=\varphi
. Since
\varphi_{1}\in L_{\xi_{k}}^{\perp}
for all
k
, we have
\langle\xi_{k},\varphi\rangle=0
for all
k
. Thus, we have

\text{rank}\,\left(\langle\varphi_{j},\xi_{k}\rangle\right)\leq N_{\lambda}-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We assume
d=3
since the same proof works for the case
d\geq 2
. Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
on
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, and let
N_{\lambda}
be the multiplicity of
\lambda
and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{N_{\lambda}}
be corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions. Let
\psi_{j}=\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\varphi_{j}]
. Then, we have

F_{j}(z):=\langle q_{z},\varphi_{j}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=v\cdot\nabla\int_{\partial\Omega}\Gamma(z-x)\psi_{j}(x)d\sigma(x),

(3.3)

namely,

F_{j}(z)=v\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}](z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

Note that
F_{j}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. We emphasize that
F_{j}
is not identically zero. In fact, if
F_{j}\equiv 0
for some constant vector
v
, we may assume
v=(1,0,0)
by rotation if necessary. There is a harmonic function
u
of two variables such that

\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}](z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=u(z_{2},z_{3})

for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}](z)\to 0
as
|z|\to\infty
, we infer
u(z_{2},z_{3})=0
by sending
z_{1}
to
\infty
. Thus
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}](z)=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}]\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}]\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, it follows from the uniqueness of the solution to Dirichlet’s problem that
\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}](z)=0
for all
z\in\Omega
. By the jump formula (
2.4
), we have

\psi_{j}=\partial_{n}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}]|_{+}-\partial_{n}\mathcal{S}_{\partial\Omega}[\psi_{j}]|_{-}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Thus
\psi_{j}=0
which is absurd. So,
F_{j}
is not identically zero.

Choose
N
different points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}\notin\overline{\Omega}
and let
\xi_{k}:=q_{z_{k}}
. Then, the matrix
A_{\lambda}=(\langle\xi_{k},\varphi_{j}\rangle)
is given by

A_{\lambda}=(F_{j}(z_{k})).

Let
A_{\lambda,m}
,
m=1,2,\ldots,M_{\lambda}:=\begin{pmatrix}N\\
N_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}
, be
N_{\lambda}\times N_{\lambda}
submatrices of
A_{\lambda}
. Let

G_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}):=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\lambda}}(\det A_{\lambda,m})^{2}.

Note that
\text{rank}\,A_{\lambda}<N_{\lambda}
if and only if
G_{j}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=0
.

Let

Z_{\lambda}:=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega})^{N}\mid G_{\lambda}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})=0\}.

Then,
\text{rank}\,A_{\lambda}=N_{\lambda}
for any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
if
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\notin\cup Z_{\lambda}
where the union is taken over all eigenvalues
\lambda
of
\mathcal{K}_{\partial\Omega}^{*}
. Since
F_{j}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
G_{\lambda}
is real analytic and hence
Z_{\lambda}
is of Lebesgue measure zero, and so is
\cup Z_{\lambda}
. Thus (
1.9
) follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots\}
be an orthonormal system of eigenfunctions for
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. Let
Z_{j}
be the zero set of the function
F_{j}
defined by (
3.3
). Then each
Z_{j}
is of measure zero and for any
z\notin\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}Z_{j}
, (
1.10
) holds.
∎

Discussion

It would be interesting to know whether the injectivity of the NP operator is also generic among all smooth, closed hypersurfaces in Euclidean space. There are hypersurfaces where
0
is an NP eigenvalue. For example,
0
is an NP eigenvalue of infinite multiplicities on lemniscates in two-dimensions as mentioned in Introduction. Some oblate spheroids have
0
as its NP eigenvalue
[
14
]
(see also
[
3
]
).

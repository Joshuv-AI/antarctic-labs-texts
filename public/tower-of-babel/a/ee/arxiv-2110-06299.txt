Elliptic Weingarten Hypersurfaces of Riemannian Products
R. F. de Lima and A. K. Ramos and J. P. dos Santos

Abstract
. Let M n M^{n} be either a simply connected space form or a rank-one symmetric space of noncompact type. We consider Weingarten hypersurfaces of M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} , which are those whose principal curvatures k 1 , … , k n k_{1},\dots,k_{n} and angle function Θ \varTheta satisfy a relation W ⁡ ( k 1 , … , k n , Θ 2 ) = 0 , W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})=0, being W W a differentiable function which is symmetric with respect to k 1 , … , k n . k_{1},\dots,k_{n}. When ∂ W / ∂ k i > 0 \partial W/\partial k_{i}>0 on the positive cone of ℝ n , \mathbb{R}^{n}, a strictly convex Weingarten hypersurface determined by W W is said to be elliptic . We show that, for a certain class of Weingarten functions W , W, there exist rotational strictly convex Weingarten hypersurfaces of M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} which are either topological spheres or entire graphs over M . M. We establish a Jellett-Liebmann-type theorem by showing that a compact, connected and elliptic Weingarten hypersurface of either 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} or ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} is a rotational embedded sphere. 1.
Introduction

Amongst the compact surfaces of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3},
round spheres are known to be unique
with respect to several types of curvature constraints. For instance,
they are the only compact connected embedded
surfaces which have either constant mean curvature or constant Gaussian curvature, as attested
by the classical theorems of Alexandrov and Hilbert–Liebmann, respectively.
More generally, these theorems apply to

elliptic Weingarten surfaces
, which
are those whose principal curvature functions
k_{1},k_{2}
satisfy a relation

W(k_{1},k_{2})=0,

where
W
is a symmetric differentiable function on a domain
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

which satisfies the ellipticity condition
\partial W/\partial{k_{i}}>0
on
W^{-1}(0)\subset\Gamma.

In
[
17
]
, Gálvez and Mira improved these results by showing that round
spheres are the only elliptic Weingarten spheres
immersed
in
\mathbb{R}^{3},
which
proved affirmatively a long standing conjecture by Alexandrov.

Elliptic Weingarten surfaces (sometimes called
special Weingarten
surfaces)
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and other three-spaces have been considered in many works

[
5
,
16
,
20
,
25
]
.
More recently, Gálvez and Mira
[
18
]
conducted a thorough
investigation of rotationally invariant elliptic Weingarten surfaces
of homogeneous three-manifolds with isometry group of dimension
4
(which include the Riemannian products

\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
).
There, they
established many deep results regarding existence and uniqueness
of certain rotational spheres in the class of elliptic Weingarten surfaces
of these three-manifolds.

Inspired by the work of Gálvez and Mira, in the present paper
we consider
Weingarten hypersurfaces
of Riemannian products
M^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

They are defined here as those whose principal curvatures
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}

and angle function
\varTheta
satisfy

W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})=0,

where
W
is a differentiable function which
is symmetric with respect to
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}.

Such a hypersurface
\varSigma
is then called a
W
-hypersurface of
M\times\mathbb{R}.

If, in addition,
\varSigma
is strictly convex and

W
satisfies the ellipticity condition
\partial W/\partial k_{i}>0

(for all
i=1,\dots,n
) on the positive cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n},

we say that
\varSigma
is an
elliptic Weingarten hypersurface.

Our work primarily concerns existence and uniqueness (in the elliptic case) of
Weingarten hypersurfaces of
M\times\mathbb{R}

when
M
is either a simply connected space form or a rank-one symmetric space
of noncompact type (i.e., one of the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
).
The reason for considering these particular manifolds
relies on the fact that their geodesic spheres are isoparametric, i.e., have
constant principal curvatures. As we shall see,
this property allows us to construct
Weingarten vertical graphs in
M\times\mathbb{R}
whose level hypersurfaces
are concentric geodesic spheres of
M
(we call such graphs
rotational
).

Given a general Weingarten function
W,
we associate
to it a first order ODE which
involves the principal curvature functions of geodesic spheres of
M.
Then, we
call
W

M
-admissible
if this equation admits a solution

\varrho:[0,\delta)\rightarrow[0,1)
satisfying certain conditions (see Definition
4
).
Then we show that, for such an
M
-admissible
W,
there exists a
rotational complete
W
-hypersurface
\varSigma
of
M\times\mathbb{R}
which is
homeomorphic to either the
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
or Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In the latter case (which does not occur if
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}
),

\varSigma
is an entire graph over
M
, and in the former case,
\varSigma

is obtained from the connected sum of
a graph over a closed ball of
M
with its reflection over a horizontal hyperplane
of
M\times\mathbb{R}.

Next, we study constant scalar curvature hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
where
n\geq 3
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
denotes the simply connected space form
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

of constant sectional curvature
\epsilon=\pm 1
(i.e.,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}
).
Based on the fact that such hypersurfaces are Weingarten,
we establish that, for all
c>\epsilon n(n-1),
there exists a
properly embedded strictly convex rotational
hypersurface
\varSigma
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant scalar curvature
c,

which is, in fact, of constant sectional curvature.
Such a
\varSigma
is either a sphere (if
c>0
) or an entire graph (if
c\leq 0
).
For
c>\epsilon n(n-1),
we also obtain a one-parameter family
of properly embedded Delaunay-type
rotational
n
-annuli in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant
scalar curvature
c,
which are not of constant sectional curvature.
An analogous one-parameter family of
non periodic rotational
n
-annuli in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is obtained as well.

Similar results hold for hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which
are invariant by either parabolic or hyperbolic translations.
Specifically, we show that for any
constant
c\in[-n(n-1),0),

there exist in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
an entire graph over
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(of constant sectional curvature)
and a hypersurface which is symmetric
with respect to a horizontal hyperplane, both of constant scalar curvature
c
and invariant
by parabolic translations. For such values of
c,
we also show that there exists a one parameter
family of hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant scalar curvature
c
which are invariant by
hyperbolic translations. All these translational
hypersurfaces are properly embedded and homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As these results indicate,
hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature appear naturally when we are dealing with
constant scalar curvature hypersurfaces. Considering this fact,
we apply the methods developed here
to provide new proofs for the main theorems by Manfio and Tojeiro
[
22
]

regarding the classification of hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

(On this matter, see also
[
2
,
3
]
, where the case
n=2
was considered.)

Regarding uniqueness of elliptic Weingarten hypersurfaces,
we establish a Jellett-Liebmann type theorem.
Namely, by means of the Maximum Principle, the Alexandrov reflection technique, and
the methods and results in
[
7
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
23
]
,
we show that, for
n\geq 3,
a compact connected strictly convex
elliptic Weingarten hypersurface immersed in

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is necessarily an embedded rotational sphere. It is also shown that,
assuming such a hypersurface to be complete, instead of compact, the same conclusion
holds under the additional assumption that
its height function has a critical point, and that
its least principal curvature is bounded away from zero.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we set some notation and formulae.
In Section
3
, we discuss graphs of Riemannian products
M\times\mathbb{R}
over parallel hypersurfaces of
M.

In Section
4
, we introduce Weingarten hypersurfaces of

M\times\mathbb{R},
establishing a key lemma.
In Section
5
, we consider rotational
Weingarten hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
In Section
6
,
we deal with constant scalar curvature hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which are invariant by
either rotational or translational isometries. In Section
7
, we establish the Jellett–Liebmann type
theorem we mentioned, together with other rigidity results for Weingarten hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

In the concluding Section
8
,
we consider hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

2.
Preliminaries

Given an orientable Riemannian manifold
M^{n},

n\geq 2,
we shall consider the Riemannian product

M\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with its standard metric

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{M}+dt^{2}.

Let
\varSigma
be an oriented hypersurface of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Write

N
for its unit normal field and
A
for its shape operator with respect to

N,
so that

AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\,\,X\in T\varSigma,

where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
M\times\mathbb{R}
and

T\varSigma
stands for the tangent bundle of
\varSigma
.
The principal curvatures of
\varSigma,
that is,
the eigenvalues of the shape operator
A,
will be denoted by
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
.
We shall say that
\varSigma
is
convex
(resp.
strictly convex
) if, with respect to
a suitable orientation
N,

k_{i}\geq 0
(resp.
k_{i}>0
) everywhere on
\varSigma,

i=1,\dots,n.

The
height function

\xi
and the
angle function

\varTheta
of
\varSigma

are defined as

\xi:=\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}|_{\varSigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\varTheta(x):=\langle N(x),\partial_{t}\rangle,\,\,x\in\varSigma,

where
\partial_{t}
denotes the gradient of the projection
\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}
of

M\times\mathbb{R}
on the second factor
\mathbb{R}.

We denote the gradient of
\xi
on
\varSigma
by
T,
which means that the equality

(1)

T=\partial_{t}-\varTheta N

holds on
\varSigma.
The trajectories of
T
on
\varSigma
will be called
T
-trajectories.

Given
t\in\mathbb{R},
the set
P_{t}:=M\times\{t\}
is called a
horizontal hyperplane

of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Horizontal hyperplanes are all isometric to
M
and totally geodesic in

M\times\mathbb{R}.
In this context,
we call a transversal intersection
\varSigma_{t}:=\varSigma\mathrel{\text{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\smash{$-$}\crcr$\pitchfork$\crcr}}}}P_{t}
a
horizontal section

of
\varSigma.
Any horizontal section
\varSigma_{t}
is a hypersurface of
P_{t}
.
So, at any point
x\in\varSigma_{t}\subset\varSigma,
the tangent space
T_{x}\varSigma
of
\varSigma
at
x
splits
as the orthogonal sum

(2)

T_{x}\varSigma=T_{x}\varSigma_{t}\oplus{\rm Span}\{T\}.

The (first factor) manifolds
M
we shall consider here are
the simply connected space forms

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
of constant sectional curvature
\epsilon\in\{0,1,-1\},
that is,
the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n},

the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
\epsilon=1)
and the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(
\epsilon=-1),

as well as the rank-one symmetric spaces of noncompact
type, also known as the general hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}.
Notice that the real hyperbolic
space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is the standard hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
of constant sectional curvature
-1

(see, e.g.,
[
12
]
).

Let us recall that, denoting by
R
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
the curvature tensors of

\varSigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},
respectively,
for
X,Y,Z,W\in T\varSigma
, the Gauss equation reads as

(3)

\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle=\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)Z,W\rangle+\langle AX,W\rangle\langle AY,Z\rangle-\langle AX,Z\rangle\langle AY,W\rangle,

where
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
vanishes identically for
\epsilon=0
and, for
\epsilon=\pm 1,
it is given by (see
[
6
]
)

(4)

\displaystyle\epsilon\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X,Y)Z,W\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,Z\rangle-\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,W\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle W,\partial_{t}\rangle-\langle Y,Z\rangle\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle W,\partial_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle X,W\rangle\langle Y,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle+\langle Y,W\rangle\langle X,\partial_{t}\rangle\langle Z,\partial_{t}\rangle.

3.
Graphs on Parallel Hypersurfaces

Let
M_{0}^{n-1}
and
M^{n}
be two orientable Riemannian manifolds.
Assume that

f:M_{0}^{n-1}\rightarrow M^{n}

is an oriented embedding with unit normal field
\eta,
and
suppose that there is an open interval
I\owns 0

such that, for all
p\in M_{0},
the curve

(5)

\gamma_{p}(s)=\exp_{\scriptscriptstyle M}(f(p),s\eta(p)),\,s\in I,

is a well defined geodesic of
M
without conjugate points. In this setting,
for any fixed
s\in I,
the map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{s}:&M_{0}&\rightarrow&M\\
&p&\mapsto&\gamma_{p}(s)\end{array}

is an embedding of
M_{0}
into
M,
which is said to be
parallel
to
f.

Observe that, given
p\in M_{0}
, the tangent space
f_{s_{*}}(T_{p}M_{0})
of
f_{s}
at
p
is the parallel transport of
f_{*}(T_{p}M_{0})
along

\gamma_{p}
from
0
to
s.
We also remark that, with the induced metric,
we will consider the unit normal
\eta_{s}
of
f_{s}
at
p
given by

\eta_{s}(p)=\gamma_{p}^{\prime}(s).

Having set the notation of the parallel immersions
f_{s}
, we introduce now the concept
of
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph, which will play a fundamental role to prove some of the main results of the paper.

Definition 1
.

Let
\phi:I\rightarrow\phi(I)\subset\mathbb{R}
be an increasing diffeomorphism, i.e.,
\phi^{\prime}>0.

With the above notation, we call the set

(6)

\varSigma:=\{(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))\in M\times\mathbb{R}\,;\,p\in M_{0},\,s\in I\},

the
graph
determined by
\{f_{s}\,;\,s\in I\}
and
\phi,
or
(f_{s},\phi)
-
graph
, for short
(Fig.
1
).

Figure 1
.
An
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph in
M\times\mathbb{R}.

For an arbitrary point
x=(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))
of an

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma,
one has

T_{x}\varSigma=f_{s_{*}}(T_{p}M_{0})\oplus{\rm Span}\,\{\partial_{s}\},\,\,\,\partial_{s}=\eta_{s}+\phi^{\prime}(s)\partial_{t}.

So, a unit normal to
\varSigma
is

(7)

N=\frac{-\phi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\eta_{s}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\partial_{t}\,.

In particular, its angle function is

(8)

\varTheta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\,\cdot

As shown in
[
9
, Theorem 6]
, any
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma

has the
T
-
property
, meaning that

T
is a principal direction at any point of
\varSigma
.
More precisely, one has

(9)

AT=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{(\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}})^{3}}T.

Given an

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma,

let
\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}
be
an orthonormal frame of principal directions of
\varSigma

in which
X_{n}=T/\|T\|.
In this case,
for
1\leq i\leq n-1,

the fields
X_{i}
are all horizontal, that is,
tangent to
M,
and constitute principal directions of the immersions
f_{s}

at corresponding points (cf.
[
9
, Lemma 1]
)).
Therefore, setting

(10)

\varrho:=\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}

and considering (
7
), we have, for all
i=1,\dots,n-1,
that

k_{i}=\langle AX_{i},X_{i}\rangle=-\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X_{i}}N,X_{i}\rangle=\varrho\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X_{i}}\eta_{s},X_{i}\rangle=-\varrho k_{i}^{s},

where
k_{i}^{s}
is the
i
-th principal curvature of
f_{s}\,.
Also,
it follows from (
9
)
that
k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime}.
Thus, the principal curvatures of the
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma

at
(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))\in\varSigma
are

(11)

k_{i}=-\varrho(s)k_{i}^{s}(p)\,\,(1\leq i\leq n-1)\quad\text{and}\quad k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime}(s).

We remark that, up to a constant,
the function
\varrho
defined in (
10
) determines
the function
\phi.

Indeed, it follows from equality (
10
) that

(12)

\phi(s)=\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du+\phi(s_{0}),\,\,\,s_{0},s\in I.

It should also be noticed that, from (
8
) and (
10
),
the unit normal
N
defined in (
7
) can be
written as
N=-\varrho\eta_{s}+\varTheta\partial_{t}
. Hence, the relation

(13)

\varrho^{2}+\varTheta^{2}=1

holds everywhere on any
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma.
In particular,
\varrho=\|T\|
on
\varSigma
.

Definition 2
.

A family
\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}
of parallel
hypersurfaces is called
isoparametric
if,
for each
s\in I,
any principal curvature
k_{i}^{s}
of
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}
is constant (possibly depending on

i
and
s
).
If so, each hypersurface
f_{s}
is also called
isoparametric.

It follows from (
11
) that, if

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}
is isoparametric and

\varSigma
is an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph in
M\times\mathbb{R},
then
all principal curvatures
k_{i}
of
\varSigma
at any point

(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))
are functions of
s
alone.

4.
Elliptic Weingarten Hypersurfaces of
M\times\mathbb{R}

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing the
positive cone

\Gamma_{+}:=\{\mathbf{k}=(k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,;\,k_{i}>0\}.

A symmetric function
W\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)

will be called a
Weingarten function.

If, in addition,
W
satisfies the condition:

(14)

\frac{\partial W}{\partial k_{i}}(\mathbf{k})>0\,\,\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{k}\in\Gamma_{+}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,i=1,\dots,n,

then
W
will be called an
elliptic Weingarten function.

Example 1
.

Two distinguished elliptic Weingarten functions are the
following:

i)

W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}}k_{i_{1}}\dots k_{i_{r}}\,,\quad r\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

ii)

W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+\cdots+k_{n}^{2}}.

The function in (i) is the non normalized
r
-th mean curvature
H_{r}
(notice that

H_{1}=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}
is the
mean curvature
and
H_{n}=k_{1}\dots k_{n}
is the
Gauss-Kronecker curvature
), whereas
the function in (ii) is the
norm of the second fundamental form

\|A\|.

We add that these functions are both homogeneous of degree one.
(Recall that, given
d\in\mathbb{R},
a function
f=f(\mathbf{k})
defined in a cone
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
homogeneous of degree
d

if
f(t\mathbf{k})=t^{d}f(\mathbf{k})
whenever
\mathbf{k}\in\Gamma
and
t>0.
)

Example 2
.

Given
W=W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma_{+}),
define
its
inverse

W^{*}
as

W^{*}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n}):={1}/{W(1/k_{1}\,,\dots,1/k_{n})}.

It is easily checked that
W
is homogeneous elliptic Weingarten if and only if

W^{*}
is homogeneous elliptic Weingarten.

Following
[
18
]
, given an open set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\Gamma_{+}\subset\Gamma,
we say that

W=W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2}),\,\,\,(k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})\in\Gamma\times[0,1],

is a
general Weingarten function
(resp. a
general elliptic Weingarten function
)
if, for any fixed
\varTheta\in[0,1],
the map

(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\Gamma\,\,\,\mapsto\,\,\,W(k_{1},\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})\in\mathbb{R}

is a Weingarten function (resp. an elliptic Weingarten function).

Definition 3
.

We say that a hypersurface
\varSigma
of a Riemannian product
M\times\mathbb{R}
is a
Weingarten hypersurface
if its
principal curvatures
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
, together with its angle function
\varTheta,
satisfy a relation
of the type

(15)

W(k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})=0,

where
W
is a general Weingarten function.
More specifically, we shall say that such a
\varSigma
is a
W
-hypersurface.
If, in addition,
W
is elliptic and

\varSigma
is strictly convex, then
\varSigma
will be called an

elliptic Weingarten hypersurface
.

Hypersurfaces of
M\times\mathbb{R}
with constant mean curvature
H_{r}

are canonical examples of Weingarten hypersurfaces.
In the next section, we shall see that hypersurfaces of constant scalar curvature
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
are also Weingarten hypersurfaces.

Remark 1
.

We point out that the Hopf Maximum Principle applies to elliptic Weingarten hypersurfaces
(see
[
16
]
for a
detailed discussion in the case
n=2
).
The same is true for the Continuation Principle, by the results
in
[
21
,
24
]
.

The following lemma, which plays a fundamental role here,
characterizes Weingarten
(f_{s},\phi)
-graphs (with
\{f_{s}\}
isoparametric)
as those whose associated
\varrho
-functions are solutions of a certain first order
ordinary differential equation. In fact, it follows directly from the definition of Weingarten
hypersurface, the relations (
11
), and
equality (
13
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varSigma
be an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph in
M\times\mathbb{R}
whose associated
family

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}

of parallel hypersurfaces is isoparametric.
Then, given a Weingarten function
W\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma),

we have that
\varSigma
is a
W
-hypersurface of
M\times\mathbb{R}
if and only if
its
\varrho
-function satisfies the equality

(16)

W(-k_{1}^{s}\varrho(s),\dots,-k_{n-1}^{s}\varrho(s),\varrho^{\prime}(s),1-\varrho^{2})=0,

where
k_{1}^{s},\dots,k_{n-1}^{s}
are the principal curvatures of

f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}.

It follows from Lemma
1
and Picard’s
theorem for local existence of solutions of first order ODE’s that,
for any general Weingarten function
W,

there exist local
W
-hypersurfaces in

M\times\mathbb{R},
provided that
M
admits families of isoparametric hypersurfaces.
In this context, it is natural to ask under which conditions we can construct complete

W
-hypersurfaces in
M\times\mathbb{R}.
We shall pursue this question in the next sections.

5.
Rotational Weingarten Hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}

Let
M^{n}
be either a simply connected space form
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
or a rank-one symmetric space of
noncompact type
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}.
Take a point
o\in M
and let
f_{s}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow M
be
the isometric immersion such that
f_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the geodesic sphere of

M
with center at
o
and radius
s>0.
In this setting, define

(17)

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{M})\},

where
\mathcal{R}_{M}
is given by

(18)

\mathcal{R}_{M}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}+\infty&\text{if}&M=\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\text{or}\,\,\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m},\\[4.30554pt]
\pi/2&\text{if}&M=\mathbb{S}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

As is well known,
\mathscr{F}
is isoparametric and each sphere
f_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is
strictly convex (see
[
12
]
and the references therein). In accordance
to the notation of Section
3
, for each
s\in(0,+\infty),

we choose the outward orientation of
f_{s}
,
so that
any principal curvature
k_{i}^{s}
of
f_{s}
is negative
.

In what follows, for
M
and
\mathscr{F}
as above,
we construct complete strictly convex Weingarten hypersurfaces
in
M\times\mathbb{R}
from
(f_{s},\phi)
-graphs,
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}.
Since the elements of

\mathscr{F}
are concentric geodesic spheres, we shall call such a hypersurface

rotational
.

The general idea for this construction is to consider (
16
) as an ODE with variable
\varrho.

From a suitable solution to this equation, we obtain a function
\phi
(using (
12
))
which, by Lemma
1
,
defines a Weingarten graph
\varSigma^{\prime}
in
M\times\mathbb{R}
over an open ball

B_{\delta}(o)\subset M
with
\delta\leq+\infty.
If
\delta=+\infty
,
\varSigma^{\prime}

is complete and we are done. Otherwise,

\partial\varSigma^{\prime}
is an
(n-1)
-sphere in a horizontal hyperplane
P_{t}:=M\times\{t\},
and
the tangent spaces of
\varSigma^{\prime}
along its boundary are all vertical (i.e., parallel to
\partial_{t}
).
Hence, a complete Weingarten
n
-sphere is obtained by “gluing”
\varSigma^{\prime}

with its reflection over
P_{t}
along their common boundary.

As we shall see, the effectiveness of this procedure depends on the existence
of a solution
\varrho
to (
16
) which can be defined at the singular point
s=0.

This fact leads us to introduce the concept of
admissible
Weingarten function, as given below.
(Notice that the principal curvatures
k_{i}^{s}
of the spheres
f_{s}
are not defined at
s=0.
)

Definition 4
.

Let
M
and
\mathscr{F}
be as above.
We say that a general Weingarten function
W

is
M
-
admissible
(or simply
admissible
) if equation (
16
)
has a solution
\varrho
defined in
[0,\delta),

0<\delta\leq\mathcal{R}_{M}
, which satisfies the conditions:

(C1)

\varrho(0)=0.

(C2)

0<\varrho<1
on
(0,\delta).

(C3)

\varrho^{\prime}>0
on
(0,\delta).

(C4)

The limits

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\varrho(s)k_{i}^{s}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\varrho^{\prime}(s)

exist and are finite (recall that

k_{i}^{s}
is the
i
-th principal curvature of
f_{s}
) and,
if
\delta<\mathcal{R}_{M}
, the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\delta}\varrho^{\prime}(s)

also exist and is finite.

We assume that
\delta
is maximal with respect to (C2)–(C3) and call

\varrho:[0,\delta)\rightarrow[0,1)
an
associated function
to
W.

In the case
\delta<\mathcal{R}_{M},
we will write (with a slight abuse of notation):

\varrho(\delta):=\lim_{s\rightarrow\delta}\varrho(s)\quad\text{and}\quad\varrho^{\prime}(\delta):=\lim_{s\rightarrow\delta}\varrho^{\prime}(s).

(Notice that, from the maximality of
\delta,
we must have

\varrho(\delta)=1
or
\varrho^{\prime}(\delta)=0.
)

Remark 2
.

From Lemma
1
and equalities (
11
)
and (
12
), a function
\varrho
satisfying
conditions (C2)–(C3) defines a rotational

W
-graph
\varSigma
over the punctured open ball
B_{\delta}(o)-\{o\}\subset M,

whose function
\phi,
up to a constant, is given by

(19)

\phi(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du\,,\,\,\,\,\,s\in(0,\delta).

Also, the condition (C1) and the finiteness of the two first limits in (C4) (together with (
11
))
imply that
\varSigma
extends
C^{2}
-smoothly to the puncture
o.
Analogously,
when
\delta<+\infty,
the finiteness of the last limit in (C4)
gives that
\varSigma
extends
C^{2}
-smoothly to its boundary
f_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\times\{\phi(\delta)\}.

Now, we are in position to state and prove our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M^{n}
be either a simply connected space form

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
or a rank-one symmetric space of
noncompact type
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}.

Given an
M
-admissible Weingarten function
W,

let
\varrho:(0,\delta)\rightarrow[0,1)
be
its associated function. Then, the following assertions hold:

i)

If
\delta<\mathcal{R}_{M},

\varrho(\delta)=1
and
\varrho^{\prime}(\delta)>0,

there exists an embedded strictly convex rotational
W
-sphere
in
M\times\mathbb{R}
which is symmetric with respect to a horizontal hyperplane.

ii)

If
\delta=+\infty
(so that
M
is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
), there
exists a rotational strictly convex entire
W
-graph in
M\times[0,+\infty)

which is tangent to
M\times\{0\}
at a single point, and whose height function is unbounded above.

In particular, if
W
is elliptic, the
W
-hypersurfaces in (i) and (ii) are elliptic.
Consequently, if (ii) occurs for such a
W,
there is no compact
W
-hypersurface in
M\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Assume the hypotheses in (i), and let
\varSigma^{\prime}
be the rotational
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph defined by
the function
\phi
in (
19
). As we pointed out in
Remark
2
,
\varSigma^{\prime}
is
defined over
B_{\delta}(o)\subset M
and constitutes a
W
-hypersurface
of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Also, by (
11
),

\varSigma^{\prime}
is strictly convex.

Let us show that the function
\phi
defining
\varSigma^{\prime}
is bounded in
(0,\delta).
Indeed,
since
\varrho^{\prime}(\delta)>0,
there exist
a,\delta_{0}>0,

0<\delta-\delta_{0}<\delta,
such that

\varrho^{\prime}(s)\geq a\,\forall s\in(\delta-\delta_{0},\delta).
Besides,

0\leq\varrho<1
and
\varrho(\delta)=1.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\delta-\delta_{0}}^{\delta}\frac{\varrho(s)ds}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(s)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\delta-\delta_{0}}^{\delta}\frac{\varrho^{\prime}(s)ds}{\varrho^{\prime}(s)\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(s)}}\leq\frac{1}{a}\int_{\varrho(\delta-\delta_{0})}^{1}\frac{d\varrho}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arcsin(\varrho(\delta-\delta_{0}))\right)\leq\frac{\pi}{2a}\,,

which implies that
\phi
is bounded. So, we can set
\phi(\delta)
for the limit
of
\phi(s)
as
s\rightarrow\delta.

Since
\varrho(\delta)=1,
we have from (
19
) that
\phi^{\prime}(s)\rightarrow+\infty
as

s\rightarrow\delta.
This gives that,
along
\partial\varSigma^{\prime}=f_{\delta}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\times\{\phi(\delta)\},

the tangent spaces are all parallel to
\partial_{t}
. Therefore,
since
\varSigma^{\prime}
extends
C^{2}
-smoothly to its boundary (see Remark
2
),
if we denote by
\varSigma^{\prime\prime}
the reflection of
\varSigma^{\prime}
with respect to
M\times\{\phi(\delta)\},

we have that

\varSigma:={\rm closure}\,\varSigma^{\prime}\cup{\rm closure}\,\varSigma^{\prime\prime}

is a strictly convex rotational
W
-sphere of
M\times\mathbb{R}.

This proves (i).

Now, let us assume that the hypotheses in (ii) hold.
In this case, since
\delta=+\infty,

the
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
defined by
\phi
in (
19
)
is an entire rotational
W
-graph of

M\times\mathbb{R}.
Also, since
\phi(0)=0
and

\phi(s)>0
for any
s>0,

\varSigma
is contained in the closed half-space

M\times[0,+\infty),
being tangent to
M\times\{0\}
at
o.

It remains to prove that the height function
of
\varSigma
is unbounded above. For that, we fix
\delta_{0}>0

and set
\iota_{\delta_{0}}(s)
for the infimum
of the function
u\mapsto\varrho(u)/\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}
on
[\delta_{0},s)
,
s>\delta_{0}.

It is easily seen that
\iota_{\delta_{0}}(s)

is bounded away from zero. Therefore,

\phi(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du\geq\int_{\delta_{0}}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du\geq\iota_{\delta_{0}}(s)(s-\delta_{0})\,\,\,\,\forall s>\delta_{0},

which gives that
\phi
is unbounded above.

The last assertion regarding the non existence of compact elliptic
W
-hypersurfaces in the occurrence
of (ii) follows from the
Maximum Principle (see Remark
1
).

Remark 3
.

exists and is finite.

First, note that if
\delta_{c}\leq\delta_{0}
, then

\lim_{s\to\delta_{c}}\varrho^{\prime}_{c}(s)=\left({c}/{c_{0}}\right)^{1/d}\varrho^{\prime}_{0}(\delta_{c})<\infty
,
so we may assume that
\delta_{c}>\delta_{0}
.
In this case, set

\lambda_{i}=-k_{i}^{\delta_{c}}\varrho_{c}(\delta_{c}),\,\,\,i\char 16\relax=1,2,\ldots,n-1.

Then, the assumption that

\frac{\partial_{f}}{\partial_{x_{n}}}\geq a>0
implies that

\lim_{x\to\infty}f(\lambda_{1},\,\ldots,\,\lambda_{n-1},x)=\infty
,
hence (
26
) implies that
the function
\varrho_{c}^{\prime}(s)
is uniformly bounded.

Next, we prove that the limit
\lim_{s\to\delta_{c}}\varrho^{\prime}_{c}(s)
exists.
Let
(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be two sequences in

(0,\delta_{c})
with
s_{m},t_{m}\nearrow\delta_{c}
and such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\varrho^{\prime}_{c}(s_{m})=\alpha_{1}\in[0,\infty),\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}\varrho^{\prime}_{c}(t_{m})=\alpha_{2}\in[0,\infty).

Then, (
26
) implies that

f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\alpha_{1})=c=f(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1},\alpha_{2}),

from where we obtain that
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}
, since

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}>a>0
.
Thus,
\lim_{s\to\delta_{c}}\varrho^{\prime}(s)
exists and is finite,
proving that
W_{c}
is
M
-admissible.

.

Given constants

6.
Symmetric Hypersurfaces of Constant Scalar Curvature in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

In this section, we consider hypersurfaces of constant scalar curvature
(CSC) of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

\epsilon\neq 0,
which are
symmetric
, meaning that they are
invariant by elliptic, parabolic or hyperbolic isometries. Any such
isometry is determined by a parallel family
\mathscr{F}
of totally umbilical hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

that is, geodesic spheres (elliptic), horospheres (parabolic) or equidistant hypersurfaces (hyperbolic).
In particular, hypersurfaces invariant by elliptic isometries are the rotational ones.

Remark 4
.

Regarding the notation
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
we will assume from now on that
\epsilon\neq 0,
that is,
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
will refer
only to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
or
\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let us start by showing that CSC hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
are
Weingarten hypersurfaces. Indeed, assuming
n\geq 3,
consider a hypersurface
\varSigma
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

and set
K(X,Y)
for its sectional curvature determined by
X,Y\in T\varSigma.

Given an orthonormal frame
\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}
of principal directions
in
T\varSigma,
it follows from Gauss equation (
3
) that:

(33)

K(X_{i}\,,X_{j})=k_{i}k_{j}+\epsilon(1-\|T_{ij}\|^{2}),\,\,\,i\neq j\in\{1,\dots,n\},

where
k_{i}
is the principal curvature in the direction
X_{i}
, and

T_{ij}
is the orthogonal projection of
T
on the plane of
T\varSigma
determined
by
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
.

In this setting,
we have that the (non normalized)
scalar curvature

S
of
\varSigma
is:

S=\sum_{i\neq j}K(X_{i},X_{j}).

Considering the equality
T=\partial_{t}-\varTheta N
and noticing that

\sum_{i\neq j}\|T_{ij}\|^{2}=2(n-1)\|T\|^{2}=2(n-1)(1-\varTheta^{2}),

we have from equation (
33
) that

(34)

S=2H_{2}+\epsilon(n-1)(2\varTheta^{2}+n-2).

Therefore, given
c\in\mathbb{R},
defining
W_{c}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(35)

W_{c}(k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n},\varTheta^{2})=2H_{2}(k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n})+\epsilon(n-1)(2\varTheta^{2}+n-2)-c,

we have that
W_{c}
is elliptic Weingarten, and also that
a
W_{c}
-hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
has constant scalar curvature
S=c.

Now, choose a family

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\,;\,s\in I\}

of parallel totally umbilical hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},

and write
\alpha(s)

for the principal curvature of
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}.

In this setting, the equalities (
11
)
take the form:

k_{i}=-\alpha\varrho\,\,\,(i=1,\dots,n-1)\quad\text{and}\quad k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime},

which gives

2H_{2}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})=-2(n-1)\alpha\varrho\varrho^{\prime}+(n-1)(n-2)\varrho^{2}\alpha^{2}.

Since
\varTheta^{2}=1-\varrho^{2},

we have that the equality
W_{c}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})=0
for

k_{1},\dots,k_{n}
as above (that is,
the ODE (
16
) for
W=W_{c}
)
is equivalent to

(36)

(n-1)(-2\alpha\varrho\varrho^{\prime}+((n-2)\alpha^{2}-2\epsilon)\varrho^{2}+n\epsilon)=c.

Setting
\uptau:=\varrho^{2},
equation (
36
) becomes

(37)

(n-1)(-\alpha\uptau^{\prime}+((n-2)\alpha^{2}-2\epsilon)\uptau+n\epsilon)=c.

Equation (
33
) also gives that the sectional curvatures
K(X_{i}\,,X_{j})
of
an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
as in Lemma
1
are given by (recall that
T
is a principal direction of
\varSigma
):

(38)

\displaystyle K(X_{i}\,,X_{j})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\varrho^{2}+\epsilon\quad(i\neq j=1,\dots,n-1).

\displaystyle K(X_{i},X_{n})

\displaystyle=-\alpha\varrho\varrho^{\prime}+\epsilon(1-\varrho^{2})\quad(i=1,\dots,n-1).

In particular,
\varSigma
has constant sectional curvature if and only if

\varrho
satisfies

(39)

\alpha\varrho\varrho^{\prime}+(\alpha^{2}+\epsilon)\varrho^{2}=0.

Remark 5
.

Throughout this section,
W_{c}
will always denote
the Weingarten function defined in (
35
). We stress that

\varSigma\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
has constant scalar curvature
c
if and only if

\varSigma
is a
W_{c}
-hypersurface.

6.1.
Rotational CSC hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Setting
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}:=\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}}
, as in (
18
),
we have that
any principal curvature of a geodesic sphere of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
of radius

s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon})
is

\alpha(s)=-\cot_{\epsilon}s.

In this case, equation (
37
) takes the form

(40)

\uptau^{\prime}(s)=a(s)\uptau(s)+b(s),\,\,\,s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}),

where the functions
a
and
b
are given by

(41)

a=-(n-2)\cot_{\epsilon}+2\epsilon\tan_{\epsilon}\qquad\text{and}\qquad b=\left(\frac{c}{n-1}-\epsilon n\right)\tan_{\epsilon}.

The general solution
to (
40
) is as follows. For fixed
s_{0}\in(0,\,+\infty)
and

\uptau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,

(42)

\uptau(s)=\frac{1}{\mu(s)}\left(\uptau_{0}+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}{b(u)}{\mu(u)}du\right),

where
\mu(s)=\exp\left(-\int_{s_{0}}^{s}a(u)du\right)
and
s\in(0,\,+\infty)
.
A direct computation gives

(43)

\uptau(s)=\mathfrak{C}_{n}\frac{\sin_{\epsilon}^{n}(s)-\sin_{\epsilon}^{n}(s_{0})}{\sin_{\epsilon}^{n-2}(s)\cos_{\epsilon}^{2}(s)}+\uptau_{0}\frac{\sin_{\epsilon}^{n-2}(s_{0})\cos_{\epsilon}^{2}(s_{0})}{\sin_{\epsilon}^{n-2}(s)\cos_{\epsilon}^{2}(s)},

where
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
is the constant defined as

(44)

\mathfrak{C}_{n}:={\frac{c-\epsilon n(n-1)}{n(n-1)}}\,\cdot

Notice that there exists a solution
\uptau
which is defined at the singular
point
s_{0}=0
and satisfies
\uptau(0)=0.
Namely,

(45)

\uptau(s)=\mathfrak{C}_{n}\tan_{\epsilon}^{2}(s).

In this case,
since
\uptau
is required to be a nonnegative function,
we must have
\mathfrak{C}_{n}\geq 0,
that is

c\geq\epsilon n(n-1).
If
c=\epsilon n(n-1),
the function

\uptau
, and so
\varrho
, vanishes identically, and the corresponding

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph is nothing but a totally geodesic horizontal
hyperplane of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
So, we shall assume
\mathfrak{C}_{n}>0.

If
c>0
and
\epsilon=-1,
we have that
\mathfrak{C}_{n}>1,
so that

\arctan_{\epsilon}(1/\sqrt{\mathfrak{C}_{n}})
is well defined for these values
of
c
and
\epsilon

(as well as for
c>0
and
\epsilon=1
). On the other hand,

\mathfrak{C}_{n}\leq 1
if
c\leq 0
and
\epsilon=-1.

These facts and equality (
45
) imply that, in any case,

W_{c}
is
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
-admissible and its
associated function
\varrho
is

(46)

\varrho(s)=\sqrt{\mathfrak{C}_{n}}\tan_{\epsilon}(s),\,\,s\in[0,\delta),

where
\delta>0
is given by

\delta:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\arctan_{\epsilon}(1/\sqrt{\mathfrak{C}_{n}})&\text{if}&c>0\\[4.30554pt]
+\infty&\text{if}&c\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

(Notice that the condition (C4) in Definition
4
is easily checked.)
In particular,
\varrho
fulfills the conditions of
Theorem
1
, where case (i) occurs if
c>0,
and
case (ii) occurs if
c\leq 0.
Finally, a direct computation gives that

\varrho
satisfies (
39
).

Summarizing, we have the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Given
n\geq 3,
for all
c>\epsilon n(n-1),
there exists a
properly embedded strictly convex (and so elliptic) rotational

W_{c}
-hypersurface
\varSigma
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

which is necessarily of constant sectional curvature
K=c/(n(n-1))>\epsilon.

Furthermore,
if
c>0,

\varSigma
is a sphere as in Theorem
1
-(i), and if

c\leq 0,

\varSigma
is an entire graph as in Theorem
1
-(ii).

Let us consider equation (
40
) again and look for solutions satisfying

\uptau(\lambda)=1
and
\uptau^{\prime}(\lambda)<0
for suitable values of
s_{0}=\lambda
.
In this case, we must have
a(\lambda)+b(\lambda)<0,
which yields the inequality

(47)

(n\mathfrak{C}_{n}+2\epsilon)\tan_{\epsilon}^{2}(\lambda)<(n-2).

Assume
c>\epsilon n(n-1)
and define
\delta_{\epsilon}(c)\in(0,\,\infty]
as

\delta_{\epsilon}(c)={\rm sup}\{\lambda>0\mid(n\mathfrak{C}_{n}+2\epsilon)\tan_{\epsilon}^{2}(\lambda)<(n-2)\}.

Then, we have:

Theorem 4
.

Given
c>\epsilon n(n-1)
, there exists a one-parameter family

\mathscr{S}\>=\{\varSigma(\lambda)\,;\,\lambda\in(0,\delta_{\epsilon}(c))\}

of properly embedded rotational
W_{c}
-hypersurfaces
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which are all homeomorphic to the
n
-annulus
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}.

In addition, the following assertions hold:

i)

If either
\epsilon=-1
and
\mathfrak{C}_{n}>1,
or
\epsilon=1,

each
\varSigma(\lambda)\in\mathscr{S}
is Delaunay-type, i.e., it
is periodic in the vertical direction, and has unduloids as its
T
-trajectories.

ii)

If
\epsilon=-1
and
\mathfrak{C}_{n}\leq 1
,
each hypersurface
\varSigma(\lambda)\in\mathscr{S}
is symmetric with respect to
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\{0\}

and has unbounded height function.

Proof.

Given
s_{0}=\lambda\in(0,\delta_{\epsilon}(c)),
let
\uptau
be the solution
of (
40
) satisfying
\uptau(\lambda)=1.
Then, from
the definition of
\delta_{\epsilon}(c)
, and equalities (
40
) and (
41
),
it follows that

\uptau^{\prime}(\lambda)<0,
so that
\uptau
is decreasing near
\lambda.
Since
we are assuming
c>\epsilon n(n-1)
, we have that
\mathfrak{C}_{n}
is positive, and
so is
b=n\mathfrak{C}_{n}\tan_{\epsilon}
. This, together with (
42
),
implies that
\uptau
is positive on
(\lambda,\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}).

Also, from (
45
), one has

(48)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}}\uptau(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathfrak{C}_{n}&{\rm if}&\epsilon=-1\\
+\infty&{\rm if}&\epsilon=1.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, if
\epsilon=1
or if
\epsilon=-1
and
\mathfrak{C}_{n}>1,

there exists

\bar{\lambda}>\lambda
such that

Let us see that

Assuming otherwise, we have

so that

(n\mathfrak{C}_{n}+2\epsilon)\tan_{\epsilon}^{2}(\bar{\lambda})=n-2.
In particular,

n\mathfrak{C}_{n}+2\epsilon>0.
However,

\uptau^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\lambda})=a^{\prime}(\bar{\lambda})+b^{\prime}(\bar{\lambda})=\frac{n-2}{\sin_{\epsilon}^{2}(\bar{\lambda})}+\frac{n\mathfrak{C}_{n}+2\epsilon}{\cos_{\epsilon}^{2}(\bar{\lambda})}>0,

which implies that
\bar{\lambda}
is a local minimum of
\uptau.
This is a contradiction, since
\bar{\lambda}
is
a maximum for
\uptau
in
(\lambda,\bar{\lambda}].
Thus,
\uptau^{\prime}(\bar{\lambda})>0
.

It follows from the above considerations that, setting
\uptau_{\lambda}:=\uptau|_{(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})}
,

1
and conclude that

To prove (ii), let us suppose that

In this case, (

6.2.
Translational CSC Hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Now, we consider hypersurfaces of constant negative scalar curvature
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which are invariant by either parabolic or hyperbolic translations.
Recall that, in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n},

parabolic translations are isometries which fix parallel families of horospheres,
whereas hyperbolic translations are those which fix parallel families of equidistant hypersurfaces
to a totally geodesic hyperplane of
\mathbb{H}^{n}.

In the upper half-space model of
\mathbb{H}^{n},
horizontal Euclidean translations along fixed horizontal directions
are parabolic, and Euclidean homotheties
from the origin are hyperbolic.
It is easily seen that any of these isometries
extend to an isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
which fixes the factor
\mathbb{R}
pointwise.

Starting with the parabolic case,
let us first consider
(f_{s},\phi)
-graphs such that

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}\,;\,s\in(-\infty,+\infty)\}

is a parallel family of horospheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}.

Since the principal curvatures of any horosphere
\mathscr{H}_{s}:=f_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
are all equal to
1
,
equation (
37
) becomes

(49)

\uptau^{\prime}(s)=n\uptau(s)-\frac{c+n(n-1)}{n-1}\,\cdot

Consider a constant
c\in[-n(n-1),0)
and write

(50)

b_{c}=-\frac{c+n(n-1)}{n-1},

so that

0\leq-b_{c}/n<1.
In this setting, the constant function

\uptau_{c}(s)=-\frac{b_{c}}{n}\,,\,\,\,s\in(-\infty,+\infty),

is a trivial solution of (
49
) satisfying

0\leq\uptau_{c}<1.
The function
\varrho_{c}=\sqrt{\uptau}_{c}
is also constant, and
so it is a solution of (
39
) (for
\alpha=1
and
\epsilon=-1
).
Hence, defining

\phi_{c}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho_{c}(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho_{c}^{2}(u)}}du=\frac{\varrho_{c}}{\sqrt{1-\varrho_{c}^{2}}}s,\,\,\,s\in(-\infty,+\infty),

we have from Lemma
1
that the
(f_{s},\phi_{c})
-graph
\varSigma_{1}(c)

is entire an has constant sectional curvature

7.
Uniqueness of Rotational Elliptic Weingarten Spheres

As we have pointed out in Remark
1
, the Maximum Principle
applies to
elliptic Weingarten hypersurfaces of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
This fact, together with
the main results in
[
7
]
(see also
[
15
,
23
]
),
allows us to apply the Alexandrov reflection method to provide
uniqueness results for the rotational
elliptic Weingarten spheres of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(
\epsilon\neq 0,n\geq 3
)
we constructed in Section
5
. Similar results
were obtained in
[
8
]
for hypersurfaces of constant
higher order mean curvatures.

Definition 5
.

Let
P_{0}
be a totally geodesic hypersurface of either hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
or
an open hemisphere
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n}.
We call the hypersurface
P:=P_{0}\times\mathbb{R}

a
vertical hyperplane
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Regarding the spherical part of the above definition, we remark that
the Alexandrov method is not
effective for vertical hyperplanes over the whole sphere. However,
it works well for vertical hyperplanes over open hemispheres
(cf.
[
1
]
, pg. 144).

Theorem 7

(Jellett–Liebmann-type theorem)
.

For
n\geq 3,
let
\varSigma
be a compact connected
elliptic Weingarten hypersurface of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then,
\varSigma
is an embedded rotational sphere.

Proof.

Since
\varSigma
is strictly convex and compact,

[
7
, Theorems 1 and 2]
apply and give that

\varSigma
is embedded and homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
In this way, we can perform Alexandrov reflections on
\varSigma
with respect to horizontal hyperplanes

P_{t}:=\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\{t\}
coming down from above
\varSigma.
Then,
a standard argument using the maximum principle shows that

\varSigma
is symmetric with respect to some horizontal plane
P_{t_{0}}
.

For
\epsilon=-1,
we can proceed as above by performing Alexandrov reflections on
\varSigma

with respect to vertical hyperplanes of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
to conclude that it has
vertical symmetries in all directions. Therefore,
\varSigma
is rotational.

For
\epsilon=1,
assuming
t_{0}=0
and identifying

\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{0\}
with
\mathbb{S}^{n},
we have that

\varSigma_{0}:=\varSigma\cap\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the boundary of

\pi(\varSigma)
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and that
\varSigma\setminus\varSigma_{0}

has two connected components, each of which being a graph over

{\rm int}(\pi(\varSigma))
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 1]
, the second fundamental form of
\varSigma_{0}
,
as a hypersurface of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, is
positive definite. In particular,
\varSigma_{0}
is not totally geodesic in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Thus,
by
[
11
, Theorem 1]
,
\varSigma_{0}
is contained in an open hemisphere
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}

of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which implies that the same is true for
\pi(\varSigma).

Indeed, the other option would be

\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\pi(\varSigma)\subset\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, in which case
\varSigma
would
have at least one concave point, a contradiction with the assumption
it is strictly convex.
Hence,
\varSigma\subset\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and again
we may apply Alexandrov reflections on the vertical hyperplanes in

\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
to deduce that
\varSigma
is rotational.
∎

Let us see now that the compactness hypothesis in Theorem
7

can be replaced by completeness if we add conditions on the height function

\xi
of
\varSigma

and on its second fundamental form. This is accomplished by means of the following
general height estimate, obtained in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2
.

[
8

Consider an arbitrary Riemannian manifold
M,
and let
\varSigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
be a
compact vertical graph of a nonnegative function defined on a domain
\Omega\subset M\times\{0\}.

Assume
\varSigma
strictly convex up to
\partial\varSigma\subset M\times\{0\}.
Under these conditions,
the following height estimate holds:

(57)

\xi(x)\leq\frac{1}{\inf_{\varSigma}k_{0}}\,\,\,\forall x\in\varSigma,

where
k_{0}
is the least principal curvature function of
\varSigma
.

Theorem 8
.

Assume
n\geq 3
, and let
\varSigma
be a complete connected
elliptic Weingarten hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

whose height function has a local
extreme point

If the least principal curvature
k_{0}
of
\varSigma

is bounded away from zero, then

\varSigma
is an embedded rotational sphere.

Proof.

As in Theorem
7
,
\varSigma
fulfills the hypotheses of
[
7

, which implies that

\varSigma
is properly embedded and homeomorphic to either

\mathbb{S}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In the former case, the result follows from
Theorem
7
, so we can assume that
\varSigma
is noncompact.
Under this assumption,
[
7
, Theorems 1 and 2]
also give that
the extreme point

(which we assume to be a maximum)
is unique, and that the height function
\xi
of
\varSigma
is unbounded (below).

Given a horizontal hyperplane

with

the part
\varSigma_{t}^{+}
of
\varSigma
which lies above

must be a vertical graph with boundary in

.
If not, for some

would be orthogonal

\varSigma
at one of its points. Then, the

\varSigma_{t^{\prime}}^{+}

It follows from the above that, for

\xi(x)-t>\frac{1}{\inf_{\varSigma}k_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{\inf_{\varSigma_{t}^{+}}k_{0}}\,,

which clearly

∎

Corollary 1
.

Any complete strictly convex CSC hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

of Theorem

In this final section, we apply the methods and results developed in

. There, for
n\geq 3,

the authors construct and classify the constant sectional curvature hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(\epsilon\neq 0).

Their proofs rely on parametrizations of symmetric hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

as introduced in
[
10
]
, as well as
on the main result of that paper. Our proofs, instead, are coordinate-free and more direct.
Another distinction of our approach is the simple way we show that constant sectional curvature
hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with non vanishing
T
-field have the
T
-property. This fact, which is also
proved and nicely used in
[
22
]
, plays a fundamental role here, as we shall see.

First, let us observe that, if
\varSigma:=\varSigma_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
is a symmetric
cylinder over a hypersurface
\varSigma_{0}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n},
then

\varSigma_{0}
is an open set of a geodesic sphere, a horosphere or an
equidistant hypersurface. Thus, from Gauss equation (
33
),
we have that
\varSigma
has constant sectional curvature if and only if

\varSigma_{0}
is contained in a horosphere. If so,
\varSigma
is
necessarily flat. We also point out that, by the first equality in
(
38
),
there is no flat parabolic
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 9
.

Given
n\geq 3,
let
\varSigma
be a connected symmetric hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant sectional
curvature
K.
Then,
K\geq\epsilon
and
\varSigma
is an open set of one of the following
properly embedded hypersurfaces:

i)

One of the rotational hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature
K>\epsilon
of Theorem
3
.

ii)

One of the translational hypersurfaces of constant sectional curvature
K\in(-1,0)
of Theorems
5
-(i)
and
6
-(i).

iii)

A horizontal hyperplane of constant sectional curvature
K=\epsilon
.

iv)

A flat vertical cylinder over a horosphere.

Proof.

Let us suppose that
\varTheta T
never vanishes on
\varSigma.
In this case,

\varSigma
is given by a union of vertical graphs with no critical points.
Let
\varSigma^{\prime}
be one of these graphs.
Then, since
\varSigma
is symmetric, up to a reflection over a horizontal hyperplane,

\varSigma^{\prime}
is an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph over a family
\mathscr{F}
of parallel totally umbilical hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}

(recall that
\phi
is supposed to be increasing).

As we have seen in Section
6
,
the
\varrho
function of
\varSigma^{\prime}
satisfies the ODE

(58)

\alpha\varrho\varrho^{\prime}+(\alpha^{2}+\epsilon)\varrho^{2}=0,

where
\alpha(s)
is the principal curvature of
f_{s}
.
Also, from the first equality in (
38
),
we have that
K>\epsilon
and that any initial condition

\varrho_{0}=\varrho(s_{0})
is determined by
K
and
\alpha(s_{0}).

Since
\varSigma
is connected, it is
either rotational or translational. Assuming the former,
we have that
\varSigma^{\prime}
is rotational, so that

\alpha=-\coth_{\epsilon}
and the solution
\varrho
of
(
58
) is
\varrho(s)=C\tan_{\epsilon}(s)
for some constant
C>0.

However, since
C
is determined by
K
and
\alpha(s_{0}),

this solution must coincide with the one defined
in (
46
), that is,

\varrho(s)=(K-\epsilon)\tan_{\epsilon}(s).

In particular, up to an ambient isometry,
\varSigma^{\prime}
is contained in the properly embedded
CSC hypersurface of Theorem
3
, say
\tilde{\varSigma},
that
has constant sectional curvature
K.
(Notice that
\tilde{\varSigma}
is built on an

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph whose
\varrho
-function is
\tilde{\varrho}(s)=(K-\epsilon)\tan_{\epsilon}(s)=\varrho(s).
)
Since
\varSigma^{\prime}
is arbitrary and
\varSigma
is connected,
we have that
\varSigma\subset\tilde{\varSigma}.

If
\varSigma
is translational, it is either
parabolic or hyperbolic. In any case,
we can argue as in the preceding paragraph
and conclude that
\varSigma
is contained in one
of the translational hypersurfaces of Theorems
5
-(i)
and
6
-(i).

Now, suppose that
T
vanishes on an open set
\mathcal{O}
of
\varSigma.
Thus,

\mathcal{O}
is contained in a (totally geodesic) horizontal hyperplane. Gauss equation
then gives that
\mathcal{O},
and so
\varSigma,
has constant sectional curvature
K=\epsilon.

This implies that
\varSigma=\mathcal{O}.
Otherwise, there would be a point in
\varSigma
at
which
\varTheta T\neq 0.
But, as we have seen,
K>\epsilon
at such a point.

Analogously, assume that
\varTheta
vanishes on an open set
\mathcal{O}
of
\varSigma.

In this case, as we pointed out,
\mathcal{O}
is a flat cylinder over an open set
of a horosphere of
\mathbb{H}^{n}.
In particular,
\varSigma
is flat and parabolic.
Since a parabolic
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph cannot be flat,

\varTheta T
must vanish on
\varSigma,
which implies that
\varSigma=\mathcal{O}.

Finally, let us suppose that neither
T
nor
\varTheta
vanishes on an open set
of
\varSigma.
Under this assumption, the set
\mathcal{O}\subset\varSigma
on which
\varTheta T
never vanishes
is open and dense in
\varSigma.
However, from the first part of the proof,
any connected component of
\mathcal{O}
is contained
in a fixed hypersurface
\tilde{\varSigma}
(from one of the
Theorems
3
,
5
-(i) or
6
-(i)),
which implies that the same is true for
\varSigma.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Given a hypersurface
\varSigma
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, let

\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}\subset T\varSigma
be an orthonormal frame of its principal directions.
It follows from Gauss equation (
3
) that

(59)

{\rm Ric}(X_{i}\,,X_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k}\rangle+H\delta_{ij}k_{i}-\delta_{ij}k_{i}^{2}\,,

where
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
are the corresponding principal curvatures of
\varSigma,

and
{\rm Ric}
denotes its
Ricci tensor
, which we define as

{\rm Ric}(X,Y):={\rm trace}(Z\mapsto R(Z,X)Y),\,\,\,X,Y\in T\varSigma.

The following lemma, which has its own interest, gives a simple characterization
of the hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
having the
T
-property.
(Recall that a hypersurface
\varSigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
is said to have the
T
-property
if
T
is a principal direction at any of its points.)

Lemma 3
.

Given
n\geq 3,
let
\varSigma
be a hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with
non vanishing
T
-field. Then,
\varSigma
has the

T
-property if and only if its principal directions
X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}
diagonalize its Ricci tensor.
Consequently, if
\varSigma
is an Einstein hypersurface (in particular, if
\varSigma
has constant sectional curvature),
then it has the
T
-property.

Proof.

Choosing
i,j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j\neq k\neq i,
it follows
from (
4
) that

\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k})=-\epsilon\langle X_{i},T\rangle\langle X_{j},T\rangle.

Combining this equality with (
59
), we get

{\rm Ric}(X_{i}\,,X_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muR\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(X_{k},X_{i})X_{j},X_{k})=-\epsilon(n-2)\langle X_{i}\,,T\rangle\langle X_{j}\,,T\rangle,

from which the result follows.
∎

Let us show now that,
setting

I_{\epsilon}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}(0,1)&\text{if}&\epsilon=\phantom{-}1\\[4.30554pt]
(-1,0)&\text{if}&\epsilon=-1,\end{array}\right.

for all
K\in I_{\epsilon},

there exists a nonsymmetric constant sectional curvature
hypersurface
\varSigma_{K}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
whose angle function is constant,
and whose sectional curvature is
K
(see also
[
22
, Section 6]
).
For its construction, one has just
to consider a parallel family
\mathscr{F}
of embeddings
f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{3},

s\in(-\delta,\delta),
such
that
f_{0}
is non umbilical and flat with the induced metric, that is,

k_{1}^{0}(p)k_{2}^{0}(p)=-\epsilon
for all
p\in M_{0}
. Then, the classical formula for the principal curvatures of the
parallel hypersurfaces
f_{s}
gives that all
f_{s}
are, in fact, flat and non umbilical, so that

k_{1}^{s}(p)k_{2}^{s}(p)=-\epsilon\,\,\,\forall p\in M_{0},\,\,s\in(-\delta,\delta).

Now, consider the
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma_{K}
with constant

\varrho
-function
\varrho=\sqrt{1-\epsilon K}.
Then,
\varSigma_{K}
has
constant angle
\varTheta=\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}.
Besides,
the principal curvatures of

\varSigma_{K}
are

k_{1}=-\varrho k_{1}^{s},

k_{2}=-\varrho k_{2}^{s},
and
k_{3}=0.
Hence,
from Gauss equation (
33
),

\varSigma_{K}
has indeed constant sectional curvature
K.

Definition 6
.

We shall call such a
\varSigma_{K}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
a
flat-foliated graph.

Our next and final result, together with Theorem
9
, provides
a full classification of the constant sectional curvature hypersurfaces of

\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

(n\geq 3)
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\varSigma
be a connected hypersurface of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant sectional curvature
K.

If
n>3,

\varSigma
is symmetric. If
n=3,

\varSigma
is either symmetric or
a nonsymmetric hypersurface which is locally a
constant angle flat-foliated graph
\varSigma_{K}.

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
9
, let us assume first
that
\varTheta T
is nowhere vanishing on
\varSigma,
in which case
\varSigma
is a union of
vertical graphs with no critical points.

Let
\varSigma^{\prime}\subset\varSigma
be a vertical graph.
By Lemma
3
,
T
is a principal direction of
\varSigma^{\prime}
. Hence,

[
26
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 6]
) applies and
gives that
\varSigma^{\prime}
is an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph over a
family
\mathscr{F}
of parallel hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}.
Thus,
by combining the identities (
11
) with Gauss equation
(
33
), we conclude that
the
\varrho
function of
\varSigma^{\prime}
satisfies

(60)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=k_{i}^{s}(p)k_{j}^{s}(p)\varrho^{2}(s)+\epsilon\quad(i\neq j=1,\dots,n-1).

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=-k_{i}^{s}(p)\varrho(s)\varrho^{\prime}(s)+\epsilon(1-\varrho^{2}(s))\quad(i=1,\dots,n-1).

If
n>3,
it follows easily from the first of the equations in (
60
)
that
k_{i}^{s}(p)
is independent of
i
and
p.
Hence,
each
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}
is totally umbilical, which implies that
\varSigma^{\prime}

is symmetric. Therefore,
\varSigma
is symmetric, since
\varSigma^{\prime}\subset\varSigma
is arbitrary.

Suppose now that
n=3.
If
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}
is totally umbilical for any graph

\varSigma^{\prime}\subset\varSigma,
as above, we have that
\varSigma
is symmetric.
So, assume that there is
\varSigma^{\prime}\subset\varSigma
such that

f_{s}
is non totally umbilical,
so that
\varSigma^{\prime},
and so
\varSigma,
is nonsymmetric.
In this case, the
\varrho
function of
\varSigma^{\prime}
is constant. Otherwise,
from the second equation in (
60
),
f_{s}
would be totally
umbilical. From this same equation, we have that

\varSigma^{\prime}
has constant sectional curvature
K=\epsilon(1-\varrho^{2})=\epsilon\varTheta^{2}.

So, from the first equality in (
60
), we have

k_{i}^{s}(p)k_{j}^{s}(p)=-\epsilon,
which implies that each
f_{s}
is flat, that is,

\varSigma^{\prime}
is a constant angle flat-foliated graph
\varSigma_{K}.

The above reasoning shows that, in a neighborhood of a point at which

\varTheta T\neq 0,

\varSigma
is either a symmetric
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph or a
flat-foliated graph. In the former case, as we know,
K>\epsilon.
In the latter
case,
K=\epsilon\varTheta^{2}\neq\epsilon.
Therefore, we can argue just as in the proof of
Theorem
9
to show that

\varSigma
is contained in a horizontal hyperplane if
T

vanishes on an open set of
\varSigma.

Since neither parabolic
(f_{s},\phi)
-graphs nor flat-foliated graphs are flat,
we can also argue as in the proof of
Theorem
9
to show that

\varSigma
is contained in a cylinder over a horosphere if
\varTheta

vanishes on an open set of
\varSigma.

Finally, if neither
T
nor
\varTheta
vanishes in an open set
\varSigma,
the set

\mathcal{O}\subset\varSigma
of points where
\varTheta T
never vanishes is open and dense
in
\varSigma.
Since the theorem is valid for any connected component of
\mathcal{O},
it follows
that it is valid for
\varSigma
as well.
∎

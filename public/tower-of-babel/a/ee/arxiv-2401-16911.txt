Generalized Reed-Muller codes: A new construction of information sets
José Joaquín Bernal

Abstract
In [ 2 ] we show how to construct information sets for Reed-Muller codes only in terms of their basic parameters. In this work we deal with the corresponding problem for q q -ary Generalized Reed-Muller codes of first and second order. We see that for first-order codes the result for binary Reed-Muller codes is also valid, while for second-order codes, with q > 2 q>2 , we have to manage more complex defining sets and we show that we get different information sets. We also present some examples and associated open problems.

II
Preliminaries

We need to introduce some basic notation and definitions about abelian codes that will be needed all throughout this paper. For our purposes we only need to consider two-dimensional abelian codes so we are giving this introduction just in that case.
First, we will always denote by
\mathbb{F}
the field of
q
elements where
q
is a power of a prime
p
. Then, we denote

\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2}):=\mathbb{F}[X_{1},X_{2}]/\left\langle X_{1}^{r_{1}}-1,X_{2}^{r_{2}}-1\right\rangle

and we consider that any (two-dimensional) abelian code is an ideal in
\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
. We identify the codewords with polynomials
P(X_{1},X_{2})
such that every monomial satisfies that the degree of the indeterminate
X_{i}
is in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{i}}
. We write
n=r_{1}\cdot r_{2}
and we always assume that
r_{i}
and
q
are coprime, for
i=1,2
, that is, we assume that
\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
is a semisimple algebra.

We will base our construction on the study of the so-called defining set of an abelian code, so let us introduce that definition.

Definition 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
be an abelian code. Fixed
\alpha_{i}
a primitive
r_{i}
-th root of unity in some extension of
\mathbb{F}
,
i=1,2
, the defining set of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to
\alpha=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
is

\begin{array}[]{rl}D_{\alpha}\left({\mathcal{C}}\right)=&\left\{(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}\mid\right.\\
&\left.P(\alpha_{1}^{a_{1}},\alpha_{l}^{a_{2}})=0\text{ for all }P\in{\mathcal{C}}\right\}.\end{array}

Remark 2
.

The simply adaptation of the previous definition to the case
\mathbb{A}(n)=\mathbb{F}[X]/\langle X^{n}-1\rangle
coincides with the classical notion of defining set of a cyclic code of length
n
.

It is clear that the defining set of a code depends on the election of the primitive roots of unity and it can be proven that fixed
\alpha
any abelian code is completely determined by its defining set.

In particular, we are working with the description of defining sets as union of some especial subsets of integers. Let us introduce them.

Definition 3
.

Let
a
and
r
be integers. The
q
-cyclotomic coset of
a
modulo
r
is the set

C_{q,r}(a)=\left\{a\cdot q^{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}.

The following definition gives the generalization of
q
-cyclotomic coset to the two-dimensional space
\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
.

Definition 4
.

Given
(a_{1},a_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
, its
q
-orbit modulo
\left(r_{1},r_{2}\right)
is the set

Q_{q}(a_{1},a_{2})=\left\{\left(a_{1}\cdot q^{i},a_{2}\cdot q^{i}\right)\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}.

It is easy to see that, under our assumption
\gcd(r_{1},q)=\gcd(r_{2},q)=1
, the
q
-orbits define a partition in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
; moreover, any defining set is a (disjoint) union of
q
-orbits and conversely, any union of
q
-orbits define a defining set (with respect to a fixed
\alpha
) and so an abelian code. Since we will only consider
q
-cyclotomic cosets and
q
-orbits, for the sake of simplicity, we shall write
C_{r}(a)
and
Q(a_{1},a_{2})
without mention to
q
.

To do the required computations on the elements in
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{C}})
we will select a subset satisfying the following definition.

Definition 5
.

Let
D
be a union of
q
-orbits modulo
(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\overline{D}\subset D
a complete set of representatives. Then
\overline{D}
is called a set of restricted representatives if
\overline{D}_{1}
is a complete set of representatives of the
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
r_{1}
in
D_{1}
(
\overline{D_{1}}
and
D_{1}
the projections of
\overline{D}
and
D
onto
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}
respectively).

To finish this section we need to give the formal notion of information set in our ambient space
\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
.

Definition 6
.

An information set for an abelian code
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
with dimension
k
is a set
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
such that
\left|{\mathcal{I}}\right|=k
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathbb{F}^{k}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{I}}
denotes the projection of the codewords in
{\mathcal{C}}
to the positions corresponding with
{\mathcal{I}}
.

The complementary set
\left({\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}\right)\setminus{\mathcal{I}}
is called a set of check positions for
{\mathcal{C}}
.

III
Information sets for cyclic codes seen as two-dimensional abelian codes

According to the notation that has been introduced in the previous sections we take a cyclic code, denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, with length
n=r_{1}\cdot r_{2}
, and assume that
r_{1},r_{2}>1,\gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1
,
\gcd(r_{i},q)=1
for
i=1,2
. Then let
T:{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
be an isomorphism, and let us denote
T=(T_{1},T_{2})
; that is,
T_{i}(e)
is the projection of
T(e)
onto
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
and any
e\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.

According to Remark
2
, let
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}=D_{\alpha}({\mathcal{C}}^{*})\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
be the defining set of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
with respect to an arbitrary primitive
n
-th root of unity
\alpha
. Then, since there exist integers
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
such that
\eta_{1}r_{1}+\eta_{2}r_{2}=1
, we have that
\alpha_{1}=\alpha^{\eta_{2}r_{2}}
and
\alpha_{2}=\alpha^{\eta_{1}r_{1}}
are primitive
r_{1}
-th and
r_{2}
-th roots of unity respectively. Fix
T
an isomorphism as above and set
T(1)=(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
; observe that
\gcd(\delta_{1},r_{1})=1
and
\gcd(\delta_{2},r_{2})=1
. We define the abelian code
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{({\mathcal{C}}^{*},T)}\leq\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
as the code with defining set
{\mathcal{D}}=D({\mathcal{C}})=T({\mathcal{D}}^{*})
, with respect to
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=(\alpha_{1}^{\delta_{1}^{-1}},\alpha_{2}^{\delta_{2}^{-1}})
. In this situation, we have that
{\mathcal{C}}
is the image of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
by the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(n)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})

\displaystyle\sum a_{i}X^{i}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum b_{jl}X^{j}Y^{l},

where
b_{jl}=a_{i}
if and only if
T(i)=(j,l)
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{I}}
is an information set for
{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
T^{-1}({\mathcal{I}})
is an information set for
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
. Usually, we omit the reference to the original cyclic code and the isomorphism
T
in the notation of the new abelian code, and we will write
{\mathcal{C}}
instead of
{\mathcal{C}}_{({\mathcal{C}}^{*},T)}
; those references will by clear by the context.

For the rest of this section we assume that we have fixed a choice of
\alpha
and
T
(consequently, the roots
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are also fixed).

Definition 7
.

Let
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
be a complete set of representatives of the
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
. Then
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
is said to be a suitable set of representatives if
T(\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}})
is a set of restricted representatives of the
q
-orbits in
T({\mathcal{D}}^{*})
(see Definition
5
).

It can be proved that we will always be able to take a suitable set of representatives in
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
(see
[
2
]
).

The goal of this section is to give an information set for the abelian code
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
just in terms of the defining set of the original cyclic code, this is established in Theorem
10
. To get it we need to introduce some special subsets in
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
on which we will do the computations.

Definition 8
.

Given
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
a suitable set of representatives, we define
\sim
the equivalence relation on
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
given by the rule

a\sim b\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\text{ if and only if }a\equiv b\mod r_{1}.

(1)

We denote by
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
a complete set of representatives of the equivalence classes given by
\sim
. Then, for any
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
we write

\mathcal{O}(u)=\{a\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\mid a\sim u\}.

Remark 9
.

It is easy to see that for any
e\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
there exists a unique
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that
T_{1}(u)=T_{1}(e)
. By abuse of notation, we will write

C_{r_{1}}(e)=C_{r_{1}}(T_{1}(e))=C_{r_{1}}(T_{1}(u))=C_{r_{1}}(u).

Next result gives what we were aiming for in this section.

Theorem 10
.

In the setting described above, let
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\leq\mathbb{A}(n)
be a cyclic code of length
n
,
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
a suitable set of representatives of its defining set, with respect to a given
n
-th root of unity, and
{\mathcal{U}}
as in Definition
8
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{A}(r_{1},r_{2})
be the abelian code
{\mathcal{C}}_{({\mathcal{C}}^{*},T)}
. In this situation we consider the values

\left\{M(u)=\frac{1}{|C_{r_{1}}(u)|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{O}(u)}|C_{n}(v)|\mid u\in{\mathcal{U}}\right\}.

Then we define

f_{1}=\max\limits_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\{M(u)\},\qquad f_{i}=\max\limits_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\{M(u)\mid M(u)<f_{i-1}\}.

which yields the sequence
f_{1}>\dots>f_{s}>0=f_{s+1}
. On the other hand, for any
k=1,\dots,s
we define

g_{k}=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{2}{u\in\U}{M(u)\geq f_k}}|C_{r_{1}}(u)|.

Then, the set

\displaystyle\Gamma({\mathcal{C}})=

\displaystyle\{(i_{1},i_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}\mid\text{ there exists }1\leq j\leq s\text{ with }

\displaystyle f_{j+1}\leq i_{2}<f_{j},\text{ and }0\leq i_{1}<g_{j}\}.

is a set of check positions for
{\mathcal{C}}
. Furthermore, the set
T^{-1}\left(\Gamma({\mathcal{C}})\right)
is a set of check positions for
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

The reader may check that the proof is constructed by using some results in
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
. Furthermore, it can be seen that the proofs do not depend on the values of
q
.

Specifically, we begin by taking Theorem 20 in
[
1
]
which gives us the description of an information set for an arbitrary abelian code from some calculations on the cardinalities of the
q
-orbits in its defining set. Then we use Lemma 18 and Proposition 19 in
[
1
]
which relate the cardinalities of the cyclotomic cosets in the defining set of a cyclic code and the cardinalities of the
q
-orbits for the corresponding abelian code obtained as in the beginning of the previous section. Finally, Theorem 20 in
[
2
]
complete the computations we need.
∎

IV
Generalized Reed-Muller codes

We are seeing GRM codes as affine-invariant codes (and, in particular, as extended cyclic codes) (see
[
5
]
). Let us introduce this family of codes briefly. The ambient space will be the group algebra
\mathbb{F}G
, where
G
is the additive group of the field with
q^{m}
elements, for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. All throughout we fixed
m
and we set
n=q^{m}-1
(this notation is being consistent with that given in the previous sections).

Since
G
is an elementary abelian group of order
|G|=q^{m}
and
G^{*}=G\setminus\{0\}
is a cyclic group, we may take a generator element
\langle\alpha\rangle=G^{*}
(note that
\alpha
is also a primitive
n
-th root of unity in the field extension of
\mathbb{F}
with
q^{m}
elements). Then, we write the elements in
\mathbb{F}G
as

bX^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{i}}\quad(a_{i},b\in\mathbb{F}).

(2)

On the other hand, we consider
S_{G}
, the group of automorphisms of
G
, acting on
\mathbb{F}G
via

\tau\left(bX^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{i}}\right)=bX^{\tau(0)}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\tau(\alpha^{i})}

for any
\tau\in S_{G}
. Then, we define the permutation automorphisms group of a code (ideal)
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{F}G
as

\rm PAut({\mathcal{C}})=\{\tau\in S_{G}\mid\tau({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}\}.

Now, we can give the definition of affine-invariant code.

Definition 11
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{F}G
be a code:

a)

{\mathcal{C}}
is an extended cyclic code if for any
bX^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{i}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
one has that
bX^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{(i+1)}}\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
b+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}=0\in\mathbb{F}
.

b)

{\mathcal{C}}
is an
affine-invariant
code if it is an extended cyclic code and
{\rm GA}(G,G)\subseteq\rm PAut({\mathcal{C}})
where

{\rm GA}(G,G)=\{x\mapsto ax+b\mid x\in\mathbb{F}G,a\in G^{*},b\in G\}.

For any affine-invariant code
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{F}G
we denote by
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
the punctured code at the position
X^{0}
. Then,
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is a
cyclic
code in the sense that it is the projection to
\mathbb{F}G^{*}
of the image of a cyclic code via the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(n)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}G

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{i}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow

\displaystyle\left(-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\right)X^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{i}}.

(3)

To give our new construction of information sets we will work with the so-called defining set of an affine-invariant code. Let us note that this notion is not the same as it was given in Definition
1
; we will clarify the difference below.

Definition 12
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{F}G
be an affine-invariant code and
\langle\alpha\rangle=G^{*}
. For any
s\in\{0,\dots,n=q^{m}-1\}
we consider the
\mathbb{F}
-linear map
\phi_{s}:\mathbb{F}G\rightarrow G
given by

\phi_{s}\left(bX^{0}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\alpha^{i}}\right)=0^{s}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\alpha^{is}

where we assume
0^{0}=1\in\mathbb{F}
by convention. Then the set

D({\mathcal{C}})=\{i\mid\phi_{i}(x)=0\text{ for all }x\in{\mathcal{C}}\}

is called the defining set with respect to
\alpha
of the affine-invariant code
{\mathcal{C}}
.

It is clear that
D({\mathcal{C}})
depends on the election of
\alpha\in G
, however all throughout this paper it will be fixed and an arbitrary one so, for the sake of simplicity, we do not include it in the notation.

It is also well known that any affine-invariant code is totally determined by its defining set (see
[
5
]
). On the other hand, it is easy to see that
D({\mathcal{C}})
is a union of
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
; moreover, since
{\mathcal{C}}
is an extended-cyclic code, one has that
0\in D({\mathcal{C}})
. Conversely, any subset of
\{0,\dots,n\}
which is a union of
q
-cyclotomic cosets and contains
0
defines an affine-invariant code in
\mathbb{F}G
.

It essential for us to note that with regard to (
IV
), we talk about the defining set of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
when we are making reference to that of the corresponding cyclic code in
\mathbb{A}(n)
. So, the defining set of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is
D({\mathcal{C}}^{*})=D({\mathcal{C}})\setminus\{0\}
(according to Definition
1
).

Remark 13
.

In the context of affine-invariant codes, a set
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\{0,\alpha^{0},\dots,\alpha^{n-1}\}
is an information set for a code
{\mathcal{C}}\leq\mathbb{F}G
with dimension
k
, if
\left|{\mathcal{I}}\right|=k
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{I}}=\mathbb{F}^{\left|{\mathcal{I}}\right|}
.

It is also important to note that, since
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is contained in
\mathbb{F}G^{*}
we have that any information set for it will be a subset of
\{\alpha^{0},\dots,\alpha^{n-1}\}
. Furthermore, an information set for
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is an information set for
{\mathcal{C}}
too. However, note that if
\Gamma
is a set of check positions for
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
then a set of check positions for
{\mathcal{C}}
is
\Gamma\cup\{0\}
.

Finally, to give the definition of GRM codes we need to introduce the notion of
q
-weight of a natural number.

Definition 14
.

For any natural number
k
its
q
-ary expansion is the sum

\sum_{r\geq 0}k_{r}q^{r}=k

with
k_{r}\in\{0,1,\dots,q-1\}
. The
q
-weight of
k
is
{\rm wt}_{q}(k)=\sum_{r\geq 0}k_{r}
.

Definition 15
.

Let
0<\rho\leq m(q-1)
and
\alpha
a generator of
G^{*}
. The Generalized Reed-Muller (GRM) code of order
\rho
and length
q^{m}
, denoted by
R_{q}(\rho,m)
, is the affine-invariant code in
\mathbb{F}G
with defining set (with respect to
\alpha
)

D(R_{q}(\rho,m))=\{0\leq i<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(i)<m(q-1)-\rho\}.

In this paper we are interested in GRM codes of first and second-order respectively, for them

D(R_{q}(1,m))=\{0\leq i<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(i)<m(q-1)-1\}.

D(R_{q}(2,m))=\{0\leq i<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(i)<m(q-1)-2\}.

Remarks 16
.

1.

We denote by
R^{*}(\rho,m)
the punctured code of
R_{q}(\rho,m)
at the position
X^{0}
.

2.

It is well-known that the dual codes of
R_{q}(1,m)
and
R_{q}(2,m)
are
R_{q}(m(q-1)-2,m)
and
R_{q}(m(q-1)-3,m)
respectively (see
[
7
]
). This fact will be important for us in the subsequent section.

V
Information sets for Generalized Reed-Muller codes

In this main section, we are applying the results of Section
III
to the (cyclic) punctured code of first and second-order GRM codes seen as two dimensional abelian codes. Recall that we are assuming that
n=q^{m}-1=r_{1}\cdot r_{2},\ r_{1},r_{2}>1,\gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1
. (Note that the condition
\gcd(r_{i},q),i=1,2
holds by definition of
n
). Let use denote
a=Ord_{r_{1}}(q)
, that is, the order of
q
modulo
r_{1}
; then, it is easy to see that
a\mid m
.

Since the given construction of information sets relies on the structure of the defining sets, it is more convenient to look at the dual codes instead of the original ones because they have smaller defining sets and so they are more manageable; that is, for
i=1,2
, instead of considering the code
R_{q}(i,m)
we work with its dual
R_{q}(m(q-1)-i-1),m)
. Then, Theorem
10
will give us a set of check positions for
R_{q}(m(q-1)-i-1),m)
and consequently an information set for
R_{q}(i,m)
.

Let us denote
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}=R^{*}_{q}(m(q-1)-i-1,m),i=1,2,
and define

\displaystyle\Omega(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{0<j<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(j)=K\right\}

then

D^{*}=D({\mathcal{C}}^{*})=\bigcup_{i\in D(R(\rho,m))\setminus\{0\}}C_{n}(i)

where
\rho=m(q-1)-i-1
.

We need to describe completely the structure of
D^{*}
and firstly we have to study how we can take a suitable set of representatives and the corresponding set
{\mathcal{U}}
(see Definition
8
).

As in Section
III
we assume that we have fixed a choice of
\alpha
, a primitive
n
-th root of unity, and
T
an isomorphism from
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
.

V-A

First-order GRM codes

In this case,

D(R_{q}(m(q-1)-2,m))=\{0\leq i<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(i)<2\}

and so
D^{*}=\Omega(1)
.

Lemma 17
.

In the case of
R_{q}(m(q-1)-2,m)
we can take
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}={\mathcal{U}}=\{1\}
.

Proof.

By definition

\Omega(1)=\left\{0<j<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}(j)=1\right\}=\{q^{i}\mid 0\leq i<m\}

So,
\Omega(1)=C_{n}(1)
. Then
D^{*}
has a unique
q
-cyclotomic coset and the result is straightforward.
∎

Therefore, we have the same situation as for (binary) Reed-Muller codes. Namely, from Theorem
10
we get our first main result.

Theorem 18
.

The set
T^{-1}\left(\Gamma\right)
, where

\Gamma=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}\mid 0\leq i_{1}<a,0\leq i_{2}<\frac{m}{a}\right\},

is a set of check positions for

The reader may check that the value of

Given

It follows from the last statement of Theorem

Second-order GRM codes

In this section we will assume

as it is a necessary condition for the results to come until the end of this paper. Therefore, the main results in this case are not valid for (binary) Reed-Muller codes.

For second-order GRM codes we have

D(R_{q}(m(q-1)-3,m))=\{0\leq i<q^{m}-1\mid{\rm wt}_{q}(i)<3\}

and then

D^{*}=\Omega(1)\cup\Omega(2),

more specifically, if we write
B_{1}=\{2q^{i}\mid 0\leq i<m\}
and
B_{2}=\left\{q^{i}+q^{j}\mid 0\leq i<j<m\right\}
, we have
\Omega(2)=B_{1}\cup B_{2}
and so

D^{*}=\Omega(1)\cup B_{1}\cup B_{2}.

(4)

(Note that
B_{1}=C_{n}(2)
.)

Note that in this case we have that the structure of
D^{*}
is more sophisticated. So, in order to be able to do the required computations we will impose the additional condition
r_{1}=q^{a}-1
for some natural number
a
. (We will see in Section
VI
that this additional condition is not a strong restriction.)
Then the restrictions needed are

\displaystyle n=q^{m}-1=r_{1}\cdot r_{2},\ r_{1},r_{2}>1,\ \gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1

(5)

\displaystyle r_{1}=q^{a}-1,\text{ for some }a\in{\mathbb{N}},

Remark 20
.

By definition we have that the order of
q
modulo
r_{1}
is precisely
a
. Note that the notation is consistent with that given at the beginning of this section. Let us write

m=ab.

First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 21
.

If
q>2
(
q
a power of a prime number
p
) and
r=q^{a}-1
for some natural number
a
then

1.

C_{r}(1)\neq C_{r}(2).

2.

|C_{r}(1)|=|C_{r}(2)|=a.

Proof.

To prove statement
1)
suppose that
2\in C_{r}(1)
. Then, there exists
\delta\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
q^{\delta}\equiv 2
mod
r
, that is, there exists
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
q^{\delta}-2=k\cdot(q^{a}-1)
. But, since
q
is a power of a primer number greater than
2
, we have that
q^{a}-1
is even which implies
q^{\delta}
even, a contradiction. So,
2\notin C_{r}(1)
and since the cyclotomic cosets define a partition we get what we wanted.

To prove statement
2)
note first that
|C_{r}(1)|=a
from the definition of
q
-cyclotomic coset modulo
r
. Now, let
\mu=|C_{r}(2)|
, that is,
\mu
is the minimum positive integer such that
2\equiv q^{\mu}\cdot 2
mod
r
. Let us denote
I=\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid 2\equiv q^{i}\cdot 2\text{ mod }r\}
. Then, it is clear that
0,a\in I
. Moreover, given
i,j\in I
we have that
2\equiv 2q^{i}
mod
r
implies (since
q
is a unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{r}
) that
2q^{-i}\equiv 2
mod
r
,
2q^{i+j}\equiv 2q^{i}q^{j}\equiv 2q^{j}\equiv 2
mod
r
, and for any
h\in{\mathbb{Z}},2q^{ih}\equiv 2q^{i}q^{i(h-1)}\equiv 2q^{i(h-1)}\equiv\dots\equiv 2q^{i}\equiv 2
mod
r
. Therefore,
I
is an ideal in
{\mathbb{Z}}
so it is a principal ideal. Then, by definition of
\mu
it is easy to see that
I
is the ideal generated by
\mu
. This implies that
\mu\mid a
. Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
a=\mu s
, then there exists
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
2(q^{\mu}-1)=k\cdot(q^{a}-1)=
so

2(q^{\mu}-1)=k\cdot(q^{\mu}-1)(q^{\mu(s-1)}+q^{\mu(s-2)}+\cdots+q^{\mu}+1)

and hence

2=k\cdot(q^{\mu(s-1)}+q^{\mu(s-2)}+\cdots+q^{\mu}+1)

which is possible only in case
s=1
, that is,
\mu=a
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Now, we can prove that we will always may assume that the essential values
1
and
2
belong to the sets we have to manage in our computations.

Proposition 22
.

Let
D^{*}
be the defining set described in (
4
). Then

1.

We may take
\overline{D^{*}}\subseteq D^{*}
, a suitable set of representatives, such that
1,2\in\overline{D^{*}}
. Moreover,

2.

we may take
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
, with
1,2\in{\mathcal{U}}
, and

3.

\mathcal{O}(2)=\{2\}
.

Proof.

Note first that by Lemma
21
we have that
C_{n}(1)\neq C_{n}(2)
. Now, we can take
1,2
in a suitable set of representatives,
\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}
, if and only if
T(1)
and
T(2)
belong to a restricted set of representatives of the
q
-orbits in
T(D^{*})
if and only if
T(1)
and
T(2)
do not belong to the same
q
-cyclotomic coset modulo
r_{1}
; since
r_{1}=q^{a}-1
, this last condition holds again by Lemma
21
. Furthermore, this also implies that
1\not\equiv 2
mod
r_{1}
and then we can take them as elements in
{\mathcal{U}}
.

Finally, as we have seen,
1\notin\mathcal{O}(2)
and if
e\in{\mathcal{U}}\setminus\{1,2\}
then
e\in B_{2}
, which implies that
{\rm wt}_{q}(e)=2
and so
e\notin C_{r_{1}}(2)
. This means that
e\notin\mathcal{O}(2)
. We can conclude that
\mathcal{O}(2)=\{2\}
.
∎

From the previous result we have that we may treat separately the value
2\in{\mathcal{U}}
. This is a crucial fact in order to be able to use the computations made in
[
2
]
, this will be describe explicitly within the proof of Theorem
24
.

To give our main result we only need one more lemma.

Lemma 23
.

For any election of the set of representatives
{\mathcal{U}}
one has that

\left|{\mathcal{U}}\right|=2+\left|\{C_{r_{1}}(e)\mid e\in{\mathcal{U}}\cap B_{2}\}\right|=2+\left\lfloor a/2\right\rfloor.

Proof.

From the previous results we have that
1,2\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
\mathcal{O}(2)=\{2\}
. On the other hand, note that

\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\setminus\{1,2\}\subseteq B_{2}.

Then, by
[
2
, Lemma 45]
, we have that
\left|\left\{e\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{*}}\setminus\{1,2\}\mid e\notin\mathcal{O}(1)\right\}\right|=\left\lfloor a/2\right\rfloor
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 24
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}=R^{*}_{q}(m(q-1)-3,m)
and suppose that the conditions given in (
5

{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}

{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, with respect to

\gamma_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{r_{2}}
, for

, given by

\begin{array}[]{rl}\gamma_{1}=&\displaystyle\left\{(i_{1},i_{2})\mid 0\leq i_{1}<\frac{a(a-1)}{2}\ \text{ and }\ 0\leq i_{2}<b^{2}\right\},\\
&\\
\gamma_{2}=&\left\{(i_{1},i_{2})\mid\displaystyle\frac{a(a-1)}{2}\leq i_{1}<\frac{a(a+1)}{2}\ \text{ and }\right.\\
&\displaystyle\left.0\leq i_{2}<\frac{b(b+1)}{2}\right\},\\
&\\
\gamma_{3}=&\displaystyle\left\{(i_{1},i_{2})\mid\frac{a(a+1)}{2}\leq i_{1}<\frac{a(a+3)}{2}\ \text{ and }\right.\\
&\displaystyle\left.0\leq i_{2}<b\right\}.\\

Then, the set

where

Let

be a suitable set of representatives of the

-cyclotomic cosets modulo

in

and let

. We want to apply Theorem
10
to the code

. To do it we need to compute the value

.

In

]
we obtain those values for any

Now, let us compute

M(2)=\frac{1}{|C_{r_{1}}(2)|}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{O}(2)}|C_{n}(v)|=\frac{|C_{n}(2)|}{|C_{r_{1}}(2)|}=\frac{m}{a}=b

VII
Conclusions and related open problems

In this work we have dealt with the problem of constructing information sets for GRM codes of first and second order just in term of their basic parameters. We have obtained that while for first-order GRM codes we get the same information sets that was presented in
[
2
]
for (binary) Reed-Muller codes, for second-order GRM codes we get different ones with a more complex structure.

In relation with this results some open problems arise:

•

It seems to be possible to study the approach to the aim of this paper seeing the punctured cyclic codes as multidimensional cyclic codes, that is, in the algebra
\mathbb{A}(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
with
l>2
. Meanwhile, it is clear that this drive us to a considerably increase of the complexity of the computations on the cyclotomic cosets.

•

In
[
3
]
we show how to apply the permutation decoding algorithm to first-order GRM codes and we obtain very relevant improvements in the number of errors to be corrected. So, it becomes a very interesting problem how we can apply that decoding algorithm to second-order GRM codes with respect to the new information sets described in the present work.

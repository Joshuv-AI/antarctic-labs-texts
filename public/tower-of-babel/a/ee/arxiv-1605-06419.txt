Abstract
We study 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 | 1 ) \mathfrak{gl}(2|1) symmetric integrable models solvable by the nested algebraic Bethe ansatz. Using explicit formulas for the Bethe vectors we derive the actions of the monodromy matrix entries onto these vectors. We show that the result of these actions is a finite linear combination of Bethe vectors. The obtained formulas open a way for studying scalar products of Bethe vectors.

Introduction

Algebraic Bethe ansatz is one of the most famous applications of the quantum inverse scattering method. It was developed at the end of the 70s of the last century by the Leningrad school
[
15
,
16
]
. From a mathematical point of view the method consists of the study of the highest weight representations of some Hopf algebra that depends on the model under consideration
[
12
,
22
,
23
]
. This method allows one to find eigenvectors of the transfer matrix (a generating function of the commuting Hamiltonians of the quantum models), leading to a diagonalization of the physical Hamiltonians. The obtained determinant representations for scalar products of the Bethe vectors
[
29
,
45
]
allow then to calculate the form factors in various integrable systems
[
25
,
27
,
30
]
. Using the form factor representations, many interesting physical results were obtained during the last few years
[
10
,
11
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
41
,
42
,
44
]
.

The results listed above mostly concerned the models with
\mathfrak{gl}(2)
symmetry or its
q
-deformation. An important problem remains open: the case of models based on the
\mathfrak{gl}(N)
algebras (
N>2
). These models are quite more involved, therefore they are less studied. The models with higher rank symmetries were considered within the framework of a
nested Bethe ansatz
approach
[
48
,
49
,
53
]
. The algebraic version of this method was developed in the works
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
. Later on, new methods to construct Bethe vectors in the models of higher rank symmetries were proposed in
[
23
,
50
]
. A generalization to the case of superalgebras
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
was given in
[
8
]
.

Recently, the models with
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
symmetries were studied more deeply by the nested algebraic Bethe ansatz in the works

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
51
,

]
. There, determinant representations for scalar products of Bethe vectors and form factors of local operators were obtained. Some of these results were generalized to the models with

-deformed
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
symmetry
[
35
,
36
,
46
]
.

In this paper, we calculate multiple actions of the monodromy matrix entries onto Bethe vectors in the models with
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
symmetry. This is a part of a larger program devoted to the study of quantum integrable models based on
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
superalgebras. This program was initiated in
[
8
]
and continued in
[
40
]
, where the Bethe vectors for these models were constructed. The calculation of the monodromy matrix entries multiple actions onto the Bethe vectors is the next step of our investigation. Further steps that include the calculation of the scalar products and form factors of the monodromy matrix entries will be reported elsewhere. They will lead eventually to determinant representations for form factors of local operators in the models of physical interest. In particular, we expect to obtain compact explicit formulas for form factors of local operators in the supersymmetric
t
-
J

[
1
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
43
,

]
and the

[

,
9

since these models are based on the
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2)
superalgebra. Knowing form factors of local operators, one can then study correlation functions via a summation of their form factor series. We expect to come back on these physical models in further works. We also hope that our results will be of some interest in the context of super-Yang–Mills theories, when studied in the integrable systems framework. Indeed in these theories, the general approach relies on a spin chain based on the
\mathfrak{psl}(2,2|4)
superalgebra, but there are closed subsectors based on the
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2)
or
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
superalgebras. We believe that the present results will be a first step to understand these subsectors, and then the entire theory.

2
Definitions

We consider a normalized rational
R
-matrix

\displaystyle R(x,y)=\mathbb{I}+g(x,y)P,\qquad g(x,y)=\frac{c}{x-y},

(2.1)

where
c
is a constant and
P
is a graded permutation operator
[
34
]
. The
R
-matrix of
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
-based models acts in the tensor product
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space with the grading
[1]=[2]=0
,
[3]=1
.
The
R
-matrix (
2.1
) satisfies the graded Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle R_{12}(x,y)R_{13}(x,z)R_{23}(y,z)=R_{23}(y,z)R_{13}(x,z)R_{12}(x,y)

(2.2)

written in the tensor product of graded spaces
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2|1}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
. The subscripts in (
2.2
) show in which copies of the
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
space the
R
-matrix acts non-trivially. The monodromy matrix
T(u)
is also graded according to the rule
[T_{ij}(u)]=[i]+[j]
. It satisfies an intertwining relation (
RTT
relation):

\displaystyle R(u,v)\bigl(T(u)\otimes\mathbb{I}\bigr)\bigl(\mathbb{I}\otimes T(v)\bigr)=\bigl(\mathbb{I}\otimes T(v)\bigr)\bigl(T(u)\otimes\mathbb{I}\bigr)R(u,v).

(2.3)

Equation (
2.3
) holds in the tensor product of graded spaces
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2|1}\otimes\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space of a Hamiltonian. It implies commutation relations between the monodromy matrix entries:

\displaystyle[T_{ij}(u),T_{kl}(v)\}=(-1)^{[i]([k]+[l])+[k][l]}g(u,v)\big[T_{kj}(v)T_{il}(u)-T_{kj}(u)T_{il}(v)\big],

(2.4)

where we introduced a graded commutator

\displaystyle[T_{ij}(u),T_{kl}(v)\}=T_{ij}(u)T_{kl}(v)-(-1)^{([i]+[j])([k]+[l])}T_{kl}(v)T_{ij}(u).

The graded transfer matrix is defined as the supertrace of the monodromy matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(u)=\mathop{\rm str}T(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(-1)^{[i]}T_{ii}(u).

It defines an integrable system, due to the relation
[\mathcal{T}(u),\mathcal{T}(v)]=0
which is implied by the relation (
2.3
).

In order to make our formulas more compact we use several auxiliary functions and conventions on the notation. In addition to the functions
g(x,y)
we also introduce the functions

\displaystyle f(x,y)=1+g(x,y)=\frac{x-y+c}{x-y},\qquad h(x,y)=\frac{f(x,y)}{g(x,y)}=\frac{x-y+c}{c},

\displaystyle t(x,y)=\frac{g(x,y)}{h(x,y)}=\frac{c^{2}}{(x-y+c)(x-y)}.

The following obvious properties of these functions are useful

\displaystyle g(x,y)=-g(y,x),\qquad h(x,y)=\frac{1}{g(x,y-c)},\qquad f(x-c,y)=\frac{1}{f(y,x)}.

Below, we will permanently have to deal with sets of variables which will be denoted by a bar:
\bar{u}
,
\bar{v}
,
\bar{\eta}
etc. Individual elements of the sets are denoted by subscripts and without a bar:
u_{k}
,
v_{\ell}
,
\eta_{j}
etc. The notation
\bar{u}\pm c
means that all the elements of the set
\bar{u}
are shifted by
\pm c
:
\bar{u}\pm c=\{u_{1}\pm c,\dots,u_{n}\pm c\}
. As a rule, the number of elements in the sets is not shown explicitly; however we give these cardinalities in special comments to the formulas. Subsets of variables are denoted by roman or other subscripts, easily distinguishable from Latin indices used for individual element:
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
,
\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
,
\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}
,
\bar{\xi}_{0}
etc. For example, the notation
\bar{u}\Rightarrow\{\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
means that the set
\bar{u}
is divided into two disjoint subsets
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
. We assume that the elements in every subset are ordered in such a way that the sequence of their subscripts is strictly increasing. For the union of two sets into another one we use the notation
\{\bar{v},\bar{z}\}=\bar{\xi}
. Finally we use a special notation
\bar{u}_{j}
,
\bar{v}_{k}
and so on for the sets
\bar{u}\setminus\{u_{j}\}
,
\bar{v}\setminus\{v_{k}\}
etc.

In order to avoid excessively cumbersome formulas we use shorthand notation for products of functions depending on one or two variables. Namely, whenever such a function depends on a set of variables, this means that we deal with the product of this function with respect to the corresponding set, as follows

\displaystyle h(\bar{u},v)=\prod_{u_{j}\in\bar{u}}h(u_{j},v),\qquad g(x_{k},\bar{\xi}_{\ell})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{j}\in\bar{\xi}\\
\xi_{j}\neq\xi_{\ell}\end{subarray}}g(x_{k},\xi_{j}),\qquad f(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})=\prod_{u_{j}\in\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}}\prod_{u_{k}\in\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}}f(u_{j},u_{k}).

This notation is also used for the product of commuting operators,

\displaystyle T_{ij}(\bar{u})=\prod_{u_{k}\in\bar{u}}T_{ij}(u_{k}),\qquad\text{if}\quad[i]+[j]=0,\quad\mod(2).

(2.5)

One can easily see from the commutation relations (
2.4
) that in this case
[T_{ij}(u),T_{ij}(v)]=0
, and hence, the operator product (
2.5
) is well defined. However, if
[i]+[j]=1
, then
[T_{ij}(u),T_{ij}(v)]\neq 0
, but we can introduce symmetric operator products

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{j3}(\bar{v})=\frac{T_{j3}(v_{1})\cdots T_{j3}(v_{n})}{\prod\limits_{n\geq\ell>m\geq 1}h(v_{\ell},v_{m})},\qquad\mathbb{T}_{3j}(\bar{v})=\frac{T_{3j}(v_{1})\cdots T_{3j}(v_{n})}{\prod\limits_{n\geq\ell>m\geq 1}h(v_{m},v_{\ell})},\qquad j=1,2.

2.1
Bethe vectors

For further calculation we need explicit formulas for
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
Bethe vectors in terms of the monodromy matrix entries
T_{ij}(u)
. We recall that generically Bethe vectors are special polynomials in operators
T_{ij}(u)
with
i\leq j
applied to the pseudovacuum vector
\Omega
. This vector possesses the following properties:

\displaystyle T_{ii}(u)\Omega=\lambda_{i}(u)\Omega,\quad i=1,2,3,\qquad T_{ij}(u)\Omega=0,\quad 3\geq i>j\geq 1.

(2.8)

Here
\lambda_{i}(u)
are some scalar functions depending on a specific model. Below we will also use the ratios of these functions

\displaystyle r_{1}(u)=\frac{\lambda_{1}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)},\qquad r_{3}(u)=\frac{\lambda_{3}(u)}{\lambda_{2}(u)}.

We extend the convention on the shorthand notation to the products of the functions
\lambda_{i}
and
r_{k}
. For example,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})=\prod_{z_{j}\in\bar{z}}\lambda_{2}(z_{j}),\qquad r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})=\prod_{\eta_{j}\in\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}}r_{1}(\eta_{j}).

We denote the Bethe vectors as
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
. They depend on two sets of variables (Bethe parameters)
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{a}\}
and
\bar{v}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{b}\}
, where
a,b=0,1,\dots
. Explicit representations for
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
Bethe vectors were obtained in
2
2

2

The formulas for the Bethe vectors obtained in
[
40
]
differ from (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) by a normalization factor
\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})\lambda_{2}(\bar{u})f(\bar{v},\bar{u})
.

[
40
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\#\bar{u}=a
and
\#\bar{v}=b
define a Bethe vector

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\sum g(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\frac{f(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{u})\lambda_{2}(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})f(\bar{v},\bar{u})}\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})T_{12}(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\Omega.

(2.9)

Here the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{v}\Rightarrow\{\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\bar{u}\Rightarrow\{\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with the restriction
\#\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=n
, where

. We recall also that we use the shorthand notation for the products of all the functions and the operators in (

An alternative formula for the Bethe vector is

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\sum K_{n}(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}|\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\frac{f(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})f(\bar{v},\bar{u})}\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})T_{12}(\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\Omega,

(2.10)

A distinctive feature of the Bethe vectors is that under certain conditions on
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
(Bethe equations), they become eigenvectors of the transfer matrix. In this case we call them

. We will show in Section

) do possess this property.

It is obvious within the framework of the current approach
[
23
]
that the action of any monodromy matrix entry
T_{ij}(u)
on a Bethe vector produces a linear combination of a
finite
number of Bethe vectors. This follows from the presentations of the monodromy matrix elements and Bethe vectors in terms of the Cartan–Weyl or current generators of the Yangian double
{\rm DY}(\mathfrak{gl}(2|1))
and normal ordering of these generators according to certain cyclic ordering
[
13
,
19
]
. In summary, we can rewrite the action formulas as normal ordering problem for the current generators and then translate the result of this ordering back into finite sum of the Bethe vectors. However, it is not so obvious if we deal with the explicit representations (
2.9
), (
2.10
). Furthermore, in spite of the action of
T_{ij}(z)
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
formally can be derived via (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), actually it is a pretty nontrivial problem.

Fortunately, similarly to the
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
case
[
6
]
the
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
Bethe vectors obey recursion relations over the number of the Bethe parameters
[
40
]
. The first recursion has the form

\displaystyle T_{12}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z)f(\bar{v},z)\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u};z\};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{12}(z) \mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}+\sum_{j=1}^{b}g(z,v_{j})g(\bar{v}_{j},v_{j})T_{13}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j}).

(2.11)

The second recursion reads

\displaystyle T_{23}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v},z)f(z,\bar{u})\mathbb{B}_{a,b+1}(\bar{u};\{\bar{v},z\})

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{23}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v}) =}{}+\sum_{j=1}^{a}g(u_{j},z)f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})T_{13}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a-1,b}(\bar{u}_{j};\bar{v}).

(2.12)

We recall that in these formulas
\bar{v}_{j}
and
\bar{u}_{j}
respectively mean
\bar{v}\setminus\{v_{j}\}
and
\bar{u}\setminus\{u_{j}\}
. The shorthand notation for the products of the functions
g
and
f
is also used.

Equations (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) allow us to built recursively Bethe vectors starting with the simplest cases

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a,0}(\bar{u};\varnothing)=\frac{T_{12}(\bar{u})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{u})}\Omega,\qquad\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})=\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})}\Omega.

(2.13)

One can also easily derive the actions of
T_{ij}
onto either
\mathbb{B}_{a,0}(\bar{u};\varnothing)
or
\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})
, and then, using induction over
a
or
b
obtain the action rule in the general case. This will be our main strategy in the derivation of the action formulas.

4
On-shell Bethe vectors

The action formulas (
3.1
)–(
3.9
) are valid for generic complex numbers
\bar{z}
,
\bar{u}
, and
\bar{v}
. In this section we consider them for on-shell Bethe vector
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
, that is when the parameters
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
satisfy a system of Bethe equations (see (
4.6
)).

In order to find explicitly the result of the transfer matrix action onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
one should set
n=1
in (
3.4
)–(
3.6
). Then the subsets
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
consist of one element only. Obviously, there are two essentially different types of partitions of the set
\bar{\eta}=\{z,\bar{u}\}
:

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=z,\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\bar{u},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=u_{j},\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{z,\bar{u}_{j}\},\qquad j=1,\dots,a.

(4.2)

Similarly, there are two different types of partitions of the set
\bar{\xi}=\{z,\bar{v}\}
:

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=z,\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\bar{v},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=v_{k},\qquad

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{z,\bar{v}_{k}\},\qquad k=1,\dots,b.

(4.4)

Thus, the action of
\mathcal{T}(z)
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\tau(z|\bar{u};\bar{v})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})+\sum_{j=1}^{a}\Lambda_{j}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\{z,\bar{u}_{j}\};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathcal{T}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}+\sum_{k=1}^{b}\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\{z,\bar{v}_{k}\})+\sum_{j=1}^{a}\sum_{k=1}^{b}M_{jk}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\{z,\bar{u}_{j}\};\{z,\bar{v}_{k}\}),

where
\tau
,
\Lambda_{j}
,
\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}
, and
M_{jk}
are numerical coefficients. In order to find
\tau(z|\bar{u};\bar{v})
we substitute the partitions (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) into (
3.4
)–(
3.6
). We obtain

\displaystyle\tau(z|\bar{u},\bar{v})=\lambda_{1}(z)f(\bar{u},z)+\lambda_{2}(z)f(z,\bar{u})f(\bar{v},z)-\lambda_{3}(z)f(\bar{v},z),

(4.5)

where we have used
h(z,z)=1
and
K_{1}(z|z+c)=g(z,z+c)=-1
.

In order to find
\Lambda_{j}
we substitute the partitions (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) into (
3.4
)–(
3.6
). We find

\displaystyle\Lambda_{j}=\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v},z)g(\bar{v},z)g(z,u_{j})\left(r_{1}(u_{j})\frac{f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})}{f(\bar{v},u_{j})}-f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})\right).

Similarly, in order to find
\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}
we substitute the partitions (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) into (
3.4
)–(
3.6
). This gives us

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}=\lambda_{2}(z)f(z,\bar{u})g(\bar{v}_{k},v_{k})h(\bar{v}_{k},z)g(z,v_{k})\left(1-\frac{r_{3}(v_{k})}{f(v_{k},\bar{u})}\right).

If
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
is an eigenvector of
\mathcal{T}(z)
, then the coefficients
\Lambda_{j}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}
must vanish for arbitrary
z
. Setting
\Lambda_{j}=0
for
j=1,\dots,a
and
\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}=0
for
k=1,\dots,b
we arrive at a system of equations

\displaystyle r_{1}(u_{j})=\frac{f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}{f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})}f(\bar{v},u_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,a,

\displaystyle r_{3}(v_{k})=f(v_{k},\bar{u}),\qquad k=1,\dots,b.

(4.6)

Let us check that
M_{jk}=0
provided the system (
4.6
) is fulfilled. Substituting the partitions (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) into (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle M_{jk}=\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v}_{k},z)g(\bar{v}_{k},v_{k})\Biggl(\frac{r_{1}(u_{j})f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})}{f(\bar{v},u_{j})}g(v_{k},u_{j})g(z,v_{k})

\displaystyle\hphantom{M_{jk}=}{}+f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})g(u_{j},z)\left[g(z,v_{k})+\frac{r_{3}(v_{k})}{f(v_{k},\bar{u})}g(v_{k},u_{j})\right]\Biggr).

Substituting here
r_{1}(u_{j})
and
r_{3}(v_{k})
from equations (
4.6
), we immediately find that
M_{jk}=0
due to the identity

\displaystyle g(v_{k},u_{j})g(z,v_{k})+g(u_{j},z)g(z,v_{k})+g(u_{j},z)g(v_{k},u_{j})=0.

Thus, the system (
4.6
) can be treated as the system of Bethe equations for the parameters
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
. If (
4.6
) holds,
then the corresponding Bethe vector
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
is on-shell, i.e., it is an eigenvector of the transfer matrix
\mathcal{T}(z)
. The eigenvalue of this on-shell vector is given by (
4.5
). At the same time, it is easy to see that the function
\tau(z|\bar{u},\bar{v})
has no poles in the points
z=u_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,a
, and
z=v_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,b
due to the system (
4.6
).

5

Bethe vectors consist of the elements from the upper triangular part of the monodromy matrix applied to pseudovacuum

5.1
Proof for
\boldsymbol{T_{13}}

For
n=1
equation (
3.1
) follows directly from the definitions of the Bethe vectors. Let us take, for instance, (
2.9
)
and set there
\bar{u}=\{z,\bar{u}^{\prime}\}
and
\bar{v}=\{z,\bar{v}^{\prime}\}
. Then the product
1/f(\bar{v},\bar{u})
vanishes, as it contains
1/f(z,z)
. This zero, however,
can be compensated if and only if
z\in\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
z\in\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
. Indeed, in this case the product
g(\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})
contains a
singular factor
g(z,z)
. Thus, we should consider only such partitions, for which
z\in\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
z\in\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
. Therefore we should set:

\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\{z,\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
and
\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\{z,\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
;
\bar{u}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
and
\bar{v}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\{z,\bar{u}^{\prime}\};\{z,\bar{v}^{\prime}\})=\sum g(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\frac{f(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{u}^{\prime})\lambda_{2}(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})f(\bar{v}^{\prime},\bar{u}^{\prime})}g(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)g(z,\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\lx@text@lbrace z,\bar{u}'\lx@text@rbrace;\lx@text@lbrace z,\bar{v}'\lx@text@rbrace)=}{}\times\frac{f(z,\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z)h(z,\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)}{\lambda_{2}(z)f(\bar{v}^{\prime},z)f(z,\bar{u}^{\prime})}\frac{T_{13}(z)}{h(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)}\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})T_{12}(\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}^{\prime}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})\Omega.

After evident cancellations we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\{z,\bar{u}^{\prime}\};\{z,\bar{v}^{\prime}\})=\frac{T_{13}(z)}{\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v}^{\prime},z)}\mathbb{B}_{a-1,b-1}(\bar{u}^{\prime};\bar{v}^{\prime}),

which coincides with (
3.1
) at
n=1
. The same result arises from the analysis of equation (
2.10
).

Now we use induction over
n
. Assume that (
3.1
) holds for some
n-1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\frac{T_{13}(z_{n})\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{z}_{n})}{h(\bar{z}_{n},z_{n})}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathbb{T}_{13}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})}{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z}_{n})\frac{h(\bar{v},\bar{z}_{n})}{h(\bar{z}_{n},z_{n})}T_{13}(z_{n})\mathbb{B}_{a+n-1,b+n-1}(\{\bar{u},\bar{z}_{n}\};\{\bar{v},\bar{z}_{n}\})

g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})
in the denominator. Hence, we can say that the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
with the restrictions on the cardinalities of the subsets only. Setting
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\}=\bar{\xi}_{0}
we recast (
5.2
) as follows

\displaystyle T_{12}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{z}_{n})}{g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{0})h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})}g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})\mathbb{B}_{a+n,b}(\bar{\eta};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

The sum over partitions
\bar{\xi}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\}
can be computed via Lemma
A.1

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{\xi}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\}}g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{z}_{n})=g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{0})h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n}),

(5.3)

where we took into account that
\#\bar{\xi}_{0}=n
. Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{12}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})}\mathbb{B}_{a+n,b}(\bar{\eta};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

which coincides with (
3.2
) up to a relabeling of the subsets.

5.3
Proof for
\boldsymbol{T_{23}}

Assume that (
3.3
) holds for some
n-1
. Let
\#\bar{z}=n
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\frac{T_{23}(z_{n})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z}_{n})}{h(\bar{z}_{n},z_{n})}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{n-1}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z}_{n})\frac{h(\bar{v},\bar{z}_{n})}{h(\bar{z}_{n},z_{n})}\sum K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})T_{23}(z_{n})\mathbb{B}_{a,b+n-1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}).

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{u}\}
,
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{v}\}
, and the sum runs through the partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=n-1
. Acting with
T_{23}(z_{n})
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{v},\bar{z}_{n})}{h(\bar{z}_{n},z_{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})h(\bar{\xi},z_{n})K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})\mathbb{B}_{a,b+n}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}).

Here already
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{z},\bar{u}\}
,
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}
, and the sum is taken first over partitions
\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{u}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=n-1
, and then over partitions
\{z_{n},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}=1
. Substituting here
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{n}\}
we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{n})}\mathbb{B}_{a,b+n}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}).

Setting
\bar{\eta}_{0}=\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}\}
and using
K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)=-K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|z_{n})/f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},z_{n})
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n-1}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times\sum K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|z_{n})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{0},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{0},z_{n})}\mathbb{B}_{a,b+n}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}).

Now we can compute the sum over partitions
\bar{\eta}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}\}
via (
A.2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{\eta}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}\}}K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|z_{n})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})=-f(\bar{\eta}_{0},z_{n})K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0}+c),

(5.4)

which gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})\sum K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0}+c)f(\bar{\eta}_{0},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})\mathbb{B}_{a,b+n}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}).

This is exactly (
3.3
) up to the labeling of the subsets.

6
Proof of the multiple action of the operator
\boldsymbol{T_{22}}

The proofs for the actions (
3.4
)–(
3.9
) are much more involved than the ones considered in the previous section. Fortunately,
they all are quite similar. Therefore, we only detail one as a typical example, the other actions being proven in the same manner. We focus on the operator
T_{22}(u)
.

The strategy of the proof is the following. First, we prove equation (
3.5
) for
a=\#\bar{u}=0
and
n=\#\bar{z}=1
. This can be done either via the standard consideration of the algebraic Bethe ansatz or using induction over
b=\#\bar{v}
. In both cases we use (
2.13
) and the relation

\displaystyle T_{22}(u)T_{23}(v)=f(v,u)T_{23}(v)T_{22}(u)+g(u,v)T_{23}(u)T_{22}(v),

(6.1)

that follows from (
2.4
).

The next step of the proof is an induction over
a
. We assume that (
3.5
) is valid for
n=1
and some
a
and use recursion (
2.11
).
Hereby, we use some of commutation relations (
2.4
)

\displaystyle T_{22}(u)T_{12}(v)=f(u,v)T_{12}(v)T_{22}(u)+g(v,u)T_{12}(u)T_{22}(v),

(6.2)

\displaystyle[T_{22}(u),T_{13}(v)]=g(u,v)\bigl(T_{12}(v)T_{23}(u)-T_{12}(u)T_{23}(v)\bigr).

(6.3)

Finally, when equation (
3.5
) is proved for
n=1
and arbitrary
a
and
b
we use induction over
n
.

Remark 6.1
.

We begin the proof with the case
n=1
,
a=0
, and arbitrary
b
. However, one could also begin with the case
n=1
,
b=0
, and arbitrary
a
.
For the action of the operator
T_{22}(z)
this is a matter of choice. For other operators these two starting cases could be essentially different. For instance, one can easily see that
T_{21}(z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing,\bar{v})=0
for arbitrary
b
. On the other hand, the action
T_{21}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,0}(\bar{u},\varnothing)
is highly nontrivial, although it is clear that it should coincide with the analogous action in the models with
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
-invariant
R
-matrix. Obviously, in this case it is better to begin the proof with the vector
\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing,\bar{v})
.

6.1
Action of
\boldsymbol{T_{22}(z)}
at
\boldsymbol{a=0}
and
\boldsymbol{z=1}

In the particular case
a=0
and
n=1
equation (
3.5
) turns into

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v},z)\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)}\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.4)

The sum is taken over partitions
\{z,\bar{v}\}=\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
. We prove this action using the standard scheme of the algebraic Bethe ansatz. The vector
\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})
is given by the second equation (
2.13
). Thus, we should move the operator
T_{22}(z)
to the right through the product of the operators
T_{23}(v_{j})
. Using (
6.1
) we easily find

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})=\Lambda\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})+\sum_{j=1}^{b}\Lambda_{j}\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\{\bar{v}_{j},z)),

where
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{j}
are some coefficients to be determined. Obviously, in order to obtain the coefficient of

\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})
one should use only the first term in the r.h.s. of (
6.1
). From this we immediately find

\displaystyle\Lambda=\lambda_{2}(z)f(\bar{v},z).

Then, due to the symmetry of
\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})
over
\bar{v}
it is enough to find
\Lambda_{1}
only. Permuting
T_{22}(z)
with
T_{23}(v_{1})
we should use the second term in the r.h.s. of (
6.1
). We have

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})}\Omega=T_{22}(z)\frac{T_{23}(v_{1})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{1})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})h(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})}\Omega=g(z,v_{1})T_{23}(z)\frac{T_{22}(v_{1})\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{1})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})h(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})}\Omega+UWT,

where
UWT
means
unwanted terms
, i.e., the terms that cannot give a contribution to the coefficient
\Lambda_{1}
. Now, moving
T_{22}(v_{1})
through the product
\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})
we should use only the first term in the r.h.s. of (
6.1
), which gives us

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})}\Omega=g(z,v_{1})g(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})T_{23}(z)\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{1})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})}\lambda_{2}(v_{1})\Omega+UWT,

where we used
g(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})=f(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})/h(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})
. It remains to combine
T_{23}(z)
and
\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v}_{1})
into
\mathbb{T}_{23}(\{z,\bar{v}_{1}\})
and we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\bar{v})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})}\Omega=\lambda_{2}(z)g(z,v_{1})g(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})h(\bar{v}_{1},z)\frac{\mathbb{T}_{23}(\{z,\bar{v}_{1}\})}{\lambda_{2}(\bar{v}_{1})\lambda_{2}(z)}\Omega+UWT,

leading to

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}(z)g(z,v_{1})g(\bar{v}_{1},v_{1})h(\bar{v}_{1},z).

Thus, we eventually obtain

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z)f(\bar{v},z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{22}(z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\bar{v})=}{}+\lambda_{2}(z)\sum_{j=1}^{b}g(z,v_{j})g(\bar{v}_{j},v_{j})h(\bar{v}_{j},z)\mathbb{B}_{0,b}(\varnothing;\{\bar{v}_{j},z\}).

(6.5)

It is easy to see that this formula coincides with (
6.4
). Indeed the first term in (
6.5
) corresponds to the partition
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=z
and
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\bar{v}
in (
6.4
). The other terms arise in the case of the partitions
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=v_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,b
, and
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{z,\bar{v}_{j}\}
. Thus, action (
6.4
) is proved.

6.2
Induction over
\boldsymbol{a}

For
n=1
equation (
3.5
) takes the form

\displaystyle T_{22}(z)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z)h(\bar{v},z)\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z)}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.6)

The sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
. We assume that (
6.6
) is valid for some
a\geq 0
and
b
arbitrary. Then, due to recursion (
2.11
)
we have

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u};z_{2}\};\bar{v})=T_{22}(z_{1})\frac{T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})}{\lambda_{2}(z_{2})f(\bar{v},z_{2})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{22}(z_1)\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\lx@text@lbrace\bar{u};z_2\lx@text@rbrace;\bar{v})=}{}-T_{22}(z_{1})\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac{g(z_{2},v_{j})g(\bar{v}_{j},v_{j})T_{13}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j})}{\lambda_{2}(z_{2})f(\bar{v},z_{2})}.

(6.7)

We see that in order to compute the action of
T_{22}(z_{1})
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{z_{2},\bar{u}\};\bar{v})
we should calculate the successive actions of the operators
T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})
and
T_{22}(z_{1})T_{13}(z_{2})
. This can be done via (
6.2
) and (
6.3
)

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\bigl(f(z_{1},z_{2})T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})+g(z_{2},z_{1})T_{12}(z_{1})T_{22}(z_{2})\bigr)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v}),

(6.8)

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})T_{13}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j})=T_{13}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad{}+g(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl(T_{12}(z_{2})T_{23}(z_{1})-T_{12}(z_{1})T_{23}(z_{2})\bigr)\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j}).

(6.9)

Thus, we have reduced the action
T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u};z_{2}\};\bar{v})
to the calculation of several successive actions. In all of them
the operator
T_{22}
acts either on
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
or on
\mathbb{B}_{a,b-1}(\bar{u};\bar{v}_{j})
, which are known due to the induction assumption. The actions of other operators
T_{ij}
with
i<j
are already known for
a
and
b
arbitrary.

6.2.1
Successive action of
\boldsymbol{T_{12}}
and
\boldsymbol{T_{23}}

We begin our calculation with the successive action of the operators
T_{12}
and
T_{23}
. Using (
3.3
) we have

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{23}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z_{1})h(\bar{v},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}).

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{z_{1},\bar{u}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{z_{1},\bar{v}\}
. The sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}

with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
. Then we use (
3.2
) and find

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{23}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi},z_{2})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.10)

Here already
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}
, however we still have
\bar{\eta}=\{z_{1},\bar{u}\}
. Replacing
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}
by
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
we recast (
6.10
) in the form

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{23}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{1},z_{2})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{z},\bar{u}\}
, and the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
. Note that the condition
z_{2}\notin\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
is ensured automatically. Indeed, if
z_{2}\in\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
, then
1/f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})=0
.

Replacing here
z_{1}\leftrightarrow z_{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{1})T_{23}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{2},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

Thus, we find

\displaystyle g(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl(T_{12}(z_{2})T_{23}(z_{1})-T_{12}(z_{1})T_{23}(z_{2})\bigr)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\left\{\frac{f(z_{1},z_{2})}{g(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}+\frac{f(z_{2},z_{1})}{g(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\right\}.

(6.11)

6.2.2
Successive action of
\boldsymbol{T_{13}}
and
\boldsymbol{T_{22}}

Combining the actions (
6.6
) and (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{13}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\};\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}).

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{u},z_{1}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{v},z_{1}\}
. Replacing
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}
with
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
and
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}
with
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}

we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{13}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{1},z_{2})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})g(z_{2},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})}.\!\!\!

(6.12)

Here already
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{u},\bar{z}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{v},\bar{z}\}
. The sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
.

Now we are able to calculate the successive action
T_{22}(z_{1})T_{13}(z_{2})
on
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
. Indeed, due to (
6.9
) this
successive action is given by a combination of (
6.11
) and (
6.12
). A straightforward calculation leads us to the following representation:

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})T_{13}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{2},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

Remark 6.2
.

Taking into account (
3.1
) we conclude that if the action (
6.6
) is valid on the vector
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
, then it is also
valid on vectors of the special type
\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b+1}(\bar{u}^{\prime};\bar{v}^{\prime})
, if
\bar{u}^{\prime}\cap\bar{v}^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.

6.2.3
Successive action of
\boldsymbol{T_{12}}
and
\boldsymbol{T_{22}}

Using (
6.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z_{1})h(\bar{v},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{u},z_{1}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{v},z_{1}\}
. Applying (
3.2
) to this formula we find

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{1})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2})\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

Here we first have partitions
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{u},z_{1}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{v},z_{1}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
. Then we combine
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
with
z_{2}
and divide this set into new subsets
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
. The restrictions are:
\#\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
,
z_{2}\notin\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
, and
z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
. As we already did before, we replace
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{2}\}
with
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
and use
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{2}\}
. Then

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})g(z_{2},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(6.13)

Setting
\bar{\xi}_{0}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
we recast (
6.13
) as follows

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{g(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})g(z_{2},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{0},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

The sum over partitions of the set
\bar{\xi}=\{z_{1},z_{2},\bar{v}\}
is organized as follows: first, we have partitions
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0}\}
; second we divide
\bar{\xi}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
. The latter sum consists of two terms and can be computed straightforwardly. This leads us to

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(\bar{z},\bar{z})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{g(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

and relabeling
\bar{\xi}_{0}\to\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
,
\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\to\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
we finally obtain

\displaystyle T_{12}(z_{2})T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(\bar{z},\bar{z})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{g(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.14)

Here the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
,
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
. The cardinalities of the subsets are
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
,
\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=2
.

6.2.4
Successive action of
\boldsymbol{T_{22}}
and
\boldsymbol{T_{12}}

Using (
6.8
) and (
6.14
) we are able to calculate the action of
T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
. It is clear that for this we should take the following combination: equation (
6.14
) multiplied with
f(z_{1},z_{2})
and the same equation with
z_{1}\leftrightarrow z_{2}
multiplied with
g(z_{2},z_{1})
. This straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(\bar{z},\bar{z})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.15)

Here the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
,
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
.

.

6.3
Recursion formula

Now everything is ready for the use of recursion (
6.7
). Due to (
5.1
) we can write it as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u},z_{2}\};\bar{v})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}(z_{2})f(\bar{v},z_{2})}\bigl(T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})-\Psi\bigr),

(6.16)

where

\displaystyle\Psi=\lambda_{2}(z_{2})h(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum_{z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}}\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

(6.17)

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{z_{2},\bar{u}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{z_{2},\bar{v}\}
, and we used
h(z_{2},z_{2})=1
. The sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
. One more restriction
z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
is shown explicitly by the subscript of the sum.

Recall that we assume that the action of
T_{22}(z_{1})
on the vectors
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
is given by (
6.6
) at some value of
a\geq 0
and arbitrary
b
. All the vectors in the linear combination (
6.17
) have the form
\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u},z_{2}\};\{\bar{v}_{j},z_{2}\})
, that is
\{\bar{u},z_{2}\}\cap\{\bar{v}_{j},z_{2}\}\neq\varnothing
. Hence, taking into account Remark
6.2
, the action of
T_{22}(z_{1})
on these vectors is known and it is given by (
6.6
):

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\Psi=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum_{z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}}\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{1})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(6.18)

In this formula
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{z},\bar{u}\}
and
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}
. At the first step we have partitions
\{z_{2},\bar{v}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
. Then we obtain additional partitions
\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
and
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
. Hereby
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}=1
. Thus, one can say that the set
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}
is divided into subsets
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
with the restrictions
z_{1}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
. Substituting
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{1}\}
into (
6.18
) we obtain

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\Psi=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum_{z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}}\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},z_{1})}{g(z_{1},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).\!\!\!

(6.19)

Observe that the restriction
z_{1}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
holds automatically due to the factor
g(z_{1},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})^{-1}
. In order to get rid of the restriction
z_{2}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
we present
T_{22}(z_{1})\Psi
as a difference of two terms. The first term is just the sum over partitions in (
6.19
), where no restrictions on the partitions of the set
\bar{\xi}
are imposed. In the second term we simply set
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=z_{2}
. Thus,

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\Psi=\Psi^{\prime}-\Psi^{\prime\prime},

where

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},z_{1})}{g(z_{1},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

(6.20)

and

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},z_{2})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{2})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},z_{1})}{g(z_{1},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(6.21)

In (
6.21
) we have
\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}=\{\bar{v},z_{1}\}
, therefore

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{1})f(\bar{v},z_{2})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{1})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(6.22)

In (
6.20
) we can take the sum over partitions into subsets
\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}
and
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
, because it consists of two terms only

\displaystyle\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{g(z_{1},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{2})}+\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{g(z_{1},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{2})}=\frac{h(z_{1},z_{2})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},z_{2})},

where
\bar{\xi}_{0}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},z_{2})h(z_{1},z_{2})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},z_{1})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},z_{2})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

(6.23)

and extracting the product
h(\bar{\xi},z_{1})
we recast (
6.23
) as follows

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{v},\bar{z})h(\bar{z},\bar{z})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

Here the sum is taken over partitions
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
,
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
with
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=1
,
\#\bar{\xi}_{0}=2
. Comparing this expression with (
6.15
) we see that

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}=T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v}).

Thus, we find from the recursion (
6.16
)

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u},z_{2}\};\bar{v})=\frac{T_{22}(z_{1})T_{12}(z_{2})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})-\Psi^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda_{2}(z_{2})f(\bar{v},z_{2})}=\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda_{2}(z_{2})f(\bar{v},z_{2})}.

Substituting (
6.22
) in this expression, we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{22}(z_{1})\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\{\bar{u},z_{2}\};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(z_{1})h(\bar{v},z_{1})\sum\frac{g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})h(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{1})}\mathbb{B}_{a+1,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}),

where we have relabeled
\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\to\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\to\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}
. Thus, the induction step is completed.

6.4
Induction over
\boldsymbol{n}

Actually, the induction over
n
for the action of
T_{22}(\bar{z})
is a combination of the corresponding proofs for the actions of
T_{12}(\bar{z})
and
T_{23}(\bar{z})
. Assume that (
3.5
) is valid for some
n-1
. Then

\displaystyle T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n-1}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z}_{n})h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z}_{n})

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z}_{n})}K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)T_{22}(z_{n})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}).

Here the sum is taken over partitions
\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{v}\}=\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{u}\}=\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
with
\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=n-1
. Acting with
T_{22}(z_{n})
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI};\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})
we obtain

\displaystyle T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z}_{n})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z}_{n})}K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{n})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{n})}K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

Here already
\bar{\xi}=\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}
and
\bar{\eta}=\{\bar{z},\bar{u}\}
, and we have additional partitions
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{n}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
and
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{n}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}
with
\#\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}=1
. Thus, we can say that we have the sum over partitions
\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
and
\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}
with restrictions
z_{n}\notin\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
z_{n}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
.

Substituting
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{n}\}
,
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{n}\}
and denoting
\bar{\xi}_{0}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
,
\bar{\eta}_{0}=\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
we obtain

\displaystyle T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{0},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z}_{n})h(\bar{\xi}_{0},z_{n})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(6.24)

Observe that the restrictions
z_{n}\notin\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
and
z_{n}\notin\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}
hold automatically due to the factors
f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{n})
and
g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})
in the denominator of (
6.24
). Using
K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}+c)=-K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|z_{n})/f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},z_{n})
we recast (
6.24
) in the form

\displaystyle T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n-1}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{0},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{f(\bar{\eta}_{0},z_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{0})h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\bigl\{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{z}_{n})\bigr\}\bigl\{K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|z_{n})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})\bigr\}\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

The sums over partitions
\bar{\xi}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
and
\bar{\eta}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}\}
(see the terms in braces) were already computed (see (
5.3
) and (
5.4
)). Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{22}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=(-1)^{n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})\sum\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{0},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})}K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

which ends the proof.

7
Induction over
\boldsymbol{n}
for the actions of
\boldsymbol{T_{ij}(\bar{z})}
with
\boldsymbol{i>j}

The action formulas for all other elements of the monodromy matrix can be proved exactly in the same manner. However, it is clear that the technical difficulty of the proofs increases when moving from the right top corner of the monodromy matrix to the left bottom corner. It is due to the form of the recursion formulas and the commutation relations (
2.4
). For example, we have seen that for the derivation of the action of
T_{22}(\bar{z})
one should know the actions of
T_{12}(\bar{z})
and
T_{23}(\bar{z})
onto Bethe vectors. The latest are relatively simple. However, one can easily convince oneself that to get the action of
T_{ij}(\bar{z})
with
i>j
, it is necessary to know the actions of the diagonal elements
T_{ii}(\bar{z})
, which are more involved. Therefore, we omit the detailed proofs of the multiple actions of the operators
T_{11}(\bar{z})
(
3.4
),
T_{33}(\bar{z})
(
3.6
), and the operators
T_{ij}(\bar{z})
from the lower-triangular part of the monodromy matrix (
3.7
)–(
3.9
). However, as an illustration of the method, we prove the multiple action of the operator
T_{21}(\bar{z})
assuming that the action of a single operator
T_{21}(z)
is known.

As previously, the proof goes by induction over
n=\#\bar{z}
. We assume that the action (
3.7
) holds for some
n-1
. Then acting successively with
T_{21}(\bar{z}_{n})
and
T_{21}(z_{n})
on
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
we obtain

\displaystyle T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z}_{n})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI\hskip-1.0ptI})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z}_{n})f(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}+c)K_{n-1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}|\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{n})r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{n})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a-n,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(7.1)

Here
\#\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}=1
and
\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=\#\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\#\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}=n-1
. Originally we have partitions
\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{u}\}=\bar{\eta}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
and
\{\bar{z}_{n},\bar{v}\}=\bar{\xi}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}
. Then we have additional partitions
\{z_{n},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii}\}
and
\{z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}\}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}
. Thus, in equation (
7.1
) we have
\{\bar{z},\bar{u}\}=\bar{\eta}
and
\{\bar{z},\bar{v}\}=\bar{\xi}
. Setting there
\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii}\}\setminus\{z_{n}\}
and
\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\{\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}\}\setminus\{z_{n}\}
we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{n})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\frac{f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},z_{n})h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z}_{n})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},z_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}+c)K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}+c)K_{n-1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}|\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a-n,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(7.2)

Now we set
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}\}=\bar{\eta}_{0}
,
\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}=\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}}
, and
\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\}=\bar{\xi}_{0}
. We also transform
K_{1}(z_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}+c)=-K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}|z_{n})/f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii},z_{n})
and
K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}|\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}+c)=-K_{1}(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})/f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})
. Then (
7.2
) takes the form

\displaystyle T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{0})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{z})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},z_{n})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{0})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},z_{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\big\{K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}|z_{n})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})\big\}\big\{K_{n-1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}|\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\mathbb{B}_{a-n,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}).

(7.3)

The sums over partitions in braces can be computed via (
A.2

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}\}}K_{n-1}(\bar{z}_{n}|\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii}|z_{n})f(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I\hskip-1.0ptI},\bar{\eta}_{\rm ii})=-f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},z_{n})K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}}+c),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{\eta}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{\rm i}\}}K_{n-1}(\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}|\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I}+c)K_{1}(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}|\bar{\eta}_{\rm i})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm i},\bar{\eta}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{K_{n}(\bar{\xi}_{0}|\bar{\eta}_{0})}{f(\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\eta}_{0})}.

Substituting this into (
7.3
) we arrive at

\displaystyle T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\frac{h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})}{h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})}\sum\frac{r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{0})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{0})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{0})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}}+c)K_{n}(\bar{\eta}_{0}|\bar{\xi}_{0}+c)\bigl\{g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{z}_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})\bigr\}\mathbb{B}_{a-n,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

where we again replaced

K_{n}(\bar{\xi}_{0}|\bar{\eta}_{0})
by
K_{n}(\bar{\eta}_{0}|\bar{\xi}_{0}+c)
via (
2.7
). The sum over partitions in braces can be calculated via Lemma
A.1

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{\xi}_{0}\Rightarrow\{\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i}\}}g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{\xi}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm I})h(\bar{\xi}_{\rm i},\bar{z}_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{\rm i})=h(z_{n},\bar{z}_{n})g(z_{n},\bar{\xi}_{0}).

Thus, we finally obtain

\displaystyle T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})h(\bar{\xi},\bar{z})\sum\frac{r_{1}(\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{0})f(\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}},\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii})f(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii},\bar{\eta}_{0})g(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\xi}_{0})}{h(\bar{\xi}_{0},\bar{z})f(\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii},\bar{\eta}_{0})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{T_{21}(\bar{z})\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})=}{}\times K_{n}(\bar{z}|\bar{\eta}_{0^{\prime}}+c)K_{n}(\bar{\eta}_{0}|\bar{\xi}_{0}+c)\mathbb{B}_{a-n,b}(\bar{\eta}_{\rm iii};\bar{\xi}_{\rm ii}),

which coincides with the original formula up to the labeling of the subsets.

8
Conclusion

In this paper we obtained compact expressions for the multiple actions of the operators
T_{ij}
onto the Bethe vectors in the models with
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
symmetry. Formally, our method can be extended to higher rank algebras as well, although the technical complexity of the calculations increases significantly with the rank and can be rapidly untractable.

Comparing the actions of
T_{ij}
onto
\mathbb{B}_{a,b}(\bar{u};\bar{v})
with the analogous actions in the
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
-based models
[
6
]
one can observe that they are quite similar. The main difference is that some of the Izergin determinants in the
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
actions are replaced with the Cauchy determinants in their
\mathfrak{gl}(2|1)
analogs. This is the consequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading that ‘trivializes’ some of the relations and helps to go further in the calculation. It is well possible that there exist some general formulas, which valid for both cases. It would be important to find such formulas, especially for the models with graded algebras of higher rank.

The obtained formulas allow one to tackle the problem of scalar products of the Bethe vectors. For this we can use explicit expressions for the dual Bethe vectors
\mathbb{C}_{a,b}(\bar{u},\bar{v})
obtained in
[
40
]
. Actually, they can be obtained by a transposition

and

of (
2.9
), (
2.10
), for instance,

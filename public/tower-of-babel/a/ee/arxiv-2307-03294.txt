Stationary Coupled KdV Hierarchies and Related Poisson Structures
Allan P. Fordy, Qing Huang

Abstract
In this paper we continue our analysis of the stationary flows of M M component, coupled KdV (cKdV) hierarchies and their modifications. We describe the general structure of the t 1 t_{1} and t 2 t_{2} flows, using the case M = 3 M=3 as our main example. One of our stationary reductions gives N N degrees of freedom, superintegrable systems. When N = 1 N=1 (for t 1 t_{1} ) and N = 2 N=2 (for t 2 t_{2} ), we have Poisson maps, which give multi-Hamiltonian representations of the flows. We discuss the general structure of these Poisson tensors and give explicit forms for the case M = 3 M=3 . In this case there are 3 modified hierarchies, each with 4 Poisson brackets. The stationary t 2 t_{2} flow (for N = 2 N=2 ) is separable in parabolic coordinates. Each Poisson bracket has rank 4, with M + 1 M+1 Casimirs. The 4 × 4 4\times 4 “core” of the Poisson tensors are nonsingular and related by a “recursion operator”. The remaining part of each tensor is built out of the two commuting Hamiltonian vector fields, depending upon the specific Casimirs. The Poisson brackets are generalised to include the entire class of potential, separable in parabolic coordinates. The Jacobi identity imposes specific dependence on some parameters, representing the Casimirs of the extended canonical bracket. This general case is no longer a stationary cKdV flow, with Lax representation. We give a recursive procedure for constructing the Lax representation of the stationary flow for all values of M M , without having to go through the stationary reduction.



Keywords
: Coupled KdV, bi-Hamiltonian, stationary flow, Hamiltonian system, Poisson matrix, super-integrability.

Introduction

In this paper our main interest is the construction of integrable and superintegrable finite dimensional Hamiltonian systems (see
[
16
,
8
,
15
,
7
]
) with multi-Hamiltonian representations and possessing a Lax pair. One such construction is to consider the stationary reductions of completely integrable nonlinear evolution equations (PDEs of soliton type).
The observation that the stationary flows of the KdV hierarchy are themselves integrable, finite dimensional Hamiltonian systems was made by Bogoyavlenskii and Novikov in 1976
[
6
]
. These were later shown to be bi-Hamiltonian in
[
3
]
(see also
[
9
]
). Stationary flows of other “soliton hierarchies” were discovered in
[
10
,
4
]
.

In
[
12
]
we revisited this subject and found bi- and tri-Hamiltonian representations of a number of superintegrable systems. These were associated with the first two nontrivial flows of the
M
component cKdV hierarchies, related to the
energy-dependent Schrödinger equation
(see
[
1
,
2
]
). In
[
12
]
we concentrated on the cases
M=1
and
M=2

In one particular coordinate system (associated with a squared eigenfunction substitution) these flows share interesting common features, which we emphasise in this paper. These features could already be seen in the KdV and DWW hierarchies, but are further illustrated here for the
M=3

In particular, the stationary
t_{1}
flow has a Hamiltonian function of the form

h^{(Q)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(P_{i}^{2}+\frac{2\beta_{i}}{Q_{i}^{2}}\right)+U\left({\bf Q}^{2}\right),

(1a)

where

), they have a Lax formulation, as follows from being stationary reductions. For the case
N=1
, we also give a multi-Hamiltonian formulation.

The stationary

flow has a Hamiltonian function of the form

h^{(Q)}=\frac{1}{2}P_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\left(P_{i}^{2}+\frac{2\beta_{i}}{Q_{i}^{2}}\right)+U\left(Q_{1},\sum_{i=2}^{N}Q_{i}^{2}\right),

. This case generalises the well known Hamiltonians, which are separable in parabolic coordinates (see Equation 2.2.41 in
[

2
Lax Representation of cKdV Equations

In
[
1
,
2
]

[
12
]
we gave a very brief review, which we further reduce here.

This differential operator Lax pair for the
M

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right)_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-u&0\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right)=U\Psi,\;\;\;\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right)_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-P_{x}&2P\\[2.84526pt]
-2uP-P_{xx}&P_{x}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\[2.84526pt]
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right)=V\Psi,

(2a)

where
u=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}u_{i}\lambda^{i}-\lambda^{M}
. Consistency of this pair of equations leads to

U_{t}-V_{x}+[U,V]=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad u_{t}=(\partial_{x}^{3}+4u\partial_{x}+2u_{x})P.

(2b)

For a specific hierarchy, we must substitute the correct form of
u
(by choosing
M
).

The cKdV hierarchy is given by the
polynomial expansion

P^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda^{n-k}P_{k},\quad\mbox{with}\quad P_{0}=1,

(2c)

corresponding to
t=t_{n}
. The corresponding
V
is called
V^{(n)}
, after which (
2a
) takes the form

U_{t_{n}}-V^{(n)}_{x}+\left[U,V^{(n)}\right]=0,\;\;\;\mbox{with}\;\;\;u_{t_{n}}=(\partial_{x}^{3}+4u\partial_{x}+2u_{x})P^{(n)}.

(2d)

Equating coefficients of powers of
\lambda
gives us a recursive formula for
P_{k}
and then formulae for
u_{it_{n}}
, which can be written in matrix form to give the Hamiltonian formulation.

8
Conclusions

In this paper we have considered the general structure of the
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
stationary flows of the
M
component cKdV hierarchy, giving detailed formulae for the case
M=3
. One of our reductions, using a squared eigenfunction representation, gives an
N
component, superintegrable system with a Lax pair. For
N=2
, we presented Poisson maps which led to multi-Hamiltonian representations.

The
t_{2}
stationary flow is separable in parabolic coordinates and the Poisson brackets were generalised to include the entire class of separable potentials. The Jacobi identity imposed a specific dependence on the parameters
\kappa_{i}
. The recursive procedure of Section
7.5
allows us to build the Lax matrices without any reference to the stationary reduction. Similarly, the “recursion operator” of Section
6.4.1
allows us to build higher Poisson tensors, without needing to go through the entire stationary reduction process.

An important open problem is to generalise these Poisson brackets to the case
N\geq 3
. In particular, for
N=3
it would be interesting to find multi-Hamiltonian formulations for the generalisations given in Section 6 of
[
12
]
, which are related to generalised parabolic coordinates and also the rational Calogero-Moser model. This connection first arose in
[
11
]
, in which we also presented a conformally flat extension. Neither a Lax representation nor a multi-Poisson formulation are known for this case.

The matrices of (
2a
) have a polynomial dependence on
\lambda
. In
[
1
,
2
]
we gave a more general formulation, with rational dependence on
\lambda
, which includes the Ito hierarchy. Furthermore, the latter includes both
positive
and
negative
hierarchies. It would be interesting to know whether further cases of the general parabolic potentials of Section
7
can be given a Lax representation in this way.

Stationary flows are related to the
t
translation symmetry of the corresponding PDE. Using the scaling symmetry, it is possible to reduce to Painlevé type equations (see
[
13
,
14
]
). Such reductions would be interesting to investigate.

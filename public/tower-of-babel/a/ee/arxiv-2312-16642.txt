. In this paper we analyze some classical operators in harmonic analysis associated to the multidimensional discrete Laplacian Δ N ​ f ​ ( 𝐧 ) = ∑ i = 1 N ( f ⁡ ( 𝐧 + 𝐞 i ) − 2 ​ f ​ ( 𝐧 ) + f ⁡ ( 𝐧 − 𝐞 i ) ) , 𝐧 ∈ ℤ N . \Delta_{N}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i})-2f(\mathbf{n})+f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i})),\qquad\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}. 1.
Introduction

Our target is to analyze mapping properties of some operators related to the multidimensional discrete Laplacian

\Delta_{N}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{N,i}f(\mathbf{n}),

where

\Delta_{N,i}f(\mathbf{n})=f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i})-2f(\mathbf{n})+f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}),

with
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
being
N
-dimensional vectors each of whose components are all zero, except the
i
-th that equals one. This operator is the natural extension to higher dimensions of the one-dimensional discrete Laplacian studied in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
. We have to observe that

\Delta_{N,i}f(\mathbf{n})=\delta_{i}^{-}\delta_{i}^{+}f(\mathbf{n})

with

\delta_{i}^{+}f(\mathbf{n})=f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i})-f(\mathbf{n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{i}^{-}f(\mathbf{n})=f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i}).

Note that
\delta_{i}^{-}\delta_{i}^{+}=\delta_{i}^{+}\delta_{i}^{-}
.
When we consider sequences of one variable we write
\delta^{+}f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n)
and similarly for
\delta^{-}f
and
\Delta f
.

We will focus in this paper on the study of the heat and Poisson semigroups, the fractional integrals, the Riesz transforms, the fractional powers of the Laplacian, and the
g_{k}
-square functions related to
\Delta_{N}
.

The heat semigroup
W_{t}=e^{t\Delta_{N}}
is the solution of the
N
-dimensional heat equation

\begin{cases}\Delta_{N}u(\mathbf{n},t)=\dfrac{d}{dt}u(\mathbf{n},t),\\[3.0pt]
u(\mathbf{n},t)=f(\mathbf{n}),\end{cases}

and it is given by (see
[
11
]
for the one-dimensional case)

(1)

W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})f(\mathbf{k}),

where, for
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N})
,

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(e^{-2t}I_{n_{k}}(2t)),

with
I_{a}
being the modified Bessel function of first kind and order
a
. This semigroup will be the main tool to analyze our discrete multidimensional operators.

M. Riesz initiated the study of discrete operators in harmonic analysis in
[
19
]
, where he proved the boundedness of the Hilbert transform in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and its discrete analogue in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Later, in
[
4
]
A. P. Calderon and A. Zygmund obtained the boundedness in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
of the discrete analogues of the singular integrals from his result in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Weighted inequalities for the discrete Hilbert and discrete maximal operator in the one-dimensional case were proved by R. Hunt, B. Muckenhoupt, and R. Wheeden in
[
12
]
. In the last few decades, several discrete analogues of continuous operators have been analyzed; some significative contributions are due to I. Arkhipov and K. I. Oskolkov
[
2
]
, J. Bourgain
[
3
]
, and E. M. Stein and S. Wainger
[
24
,
25
]
. In [13], we can find a brief history and a nice exposition on discrete analogues of classical operators in harmonic analysis.

In general, along the paper will work on weighted Lebesgue spaces of sequences on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. More precisely, we define

\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)=\left\{f=\{f(\mathbf{n})\}_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}:\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}<\infty\right\},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

where the weight
w=\{w(\mathbf{n})\}_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}
is a sequence of positive numbers and

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}=\left(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|f(\mathbf{n})|^{p}w(\mathbf{n})\right)^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,

and

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}=\sup\{|f(\mathbf{n})|:\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\}.

When
w(\mathbf{n})=1
for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
we will write
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
only.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we present the main tools to deal with the kernel
G_{t,N}
. Section
3
will be focused on the analysis of the heat and Poisson semigroups. Section
4
contains our results about the fractional integrals and the Riesz transforms. In Section
5
we present some results on the fractional powers of
\Delta_{N}
. Finally, in Section
6
we analyze the mapping properties of the
g_{k}
-square functions.

2.
Some facts about the modified Bessel functions of first kind

The modified Bessel function of first kind and order
a
, with
a\in\mathbb{R}
, is given by

I_{a}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k,a}}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+a},

where

c_{k,a}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a+k+1)},

understanding that for
a\leq-1

c_{k,a}=\frac{(a+k+1)\cdots(a+[-a])}{\Gamma(a+[-a]+1)},\qquad 0\leq k<\lfloor-a\rfloor.

In this section we compile some relevant inequalities for the modified Bessel functions of first kind. They will be the main tools to obtain our results.

2.1.
An AM-GM type inequality for the modified Bessel functions of first kind

In
[
26
]
, we find the identity

(2)

I_{a}(x)I_{b}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{a+b+2k}{k}c_{k,a}c_{k,b}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+a+b},

which can be proved by applying the Cauchy product of two absolutely convergent series. From this fact, we can obtain the next inequalities for the modified Bessel functions.

Proposition 1
.

For
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}>-1
and
x\geq 0
the inequalities

(3)

\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}+1\right)\right)^{n}}{\Gamma(a_{1}+1)\cdots\Gamma(a_{n}+1)}\left(I_{\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}}(x)\right)^{n}\leq I_{a_{1}}(x)\cdots I_{a_{n}}(x)\leq\left(I_{\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}}(x)\right)^{n}

hold.

Proof.

We will provide an
à la Cauchy
proof, obtaining the cases
n=2^{m}
first and extending the result for general
n
.
To prove the case
n=2
we are going to check that

(4)

\frac{c_{0,a_{1}}c_{0,a_{2}}}{\big(c_{0,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}}\leq\frac{c_{k,a_{1}}c_{k,a_{2}}}{\big(c_{k,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}}\leq 1.

From the Weiertrass’ product formula for the Gamma function

\Gamma(z)=\frac{e^{-\gamma z}}{z}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\left(1+\frac{z}{n}\right)^{-1}e^{z/n}\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dots\},

we have

\frac{c_{k,a_{1}}c_{k,a_{2}}}{\big(c_{k,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n+a_{1}+k+1)(n+a_{2}+k+1)}{\left(n+\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+k+1\right)^{2}}.

Then, by using that

\frac{(n+a_{1}+1)(n+a_{2}+1)}{\left(n+\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+1\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{(n+a_{1}+k+1)(n+a_{2}+k+1)}{\left(n+\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+k+1\right)^{2}}\leq 1

the inequalities in (
4
) follow.

Now, from (
2
), (
4
) and the identity

\frac{c_{0,a_{1}}c_{0,a_{2}}}{\big(c_{0,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}}=\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+1\right)\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(a_{1}+1)\,\Gamma(a_{2}+1)},

we have

\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+1\right)\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(a_{1}+1)\,\Gamma(a_{2}+1)}\binom{(a_{1}+a_{2})/2+(a_{1}+a_{2})/2+2k}{k}\big(c_{k,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}\\
\leq\binom{a_{1}+a_{2}+2k}{k}c_{k,a_{1}}c_{k,a_{2}}\leq\binom{(a_{1}+a_{2})/2+(a_{1}+a_{2})/2+2k}{k}\big(c_{k,\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}\big)^{2}

and

\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}+1\right)\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(a_{1}+1)\,\Gamma(a_{2}+1)}\left(I_{\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}(x)\right)^{2}\leq I_{a_{1}}(x)I_{a_{2}}(x)\leq\left(I_{\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}}(x)\right)^{2}.

With an elementary induction process, it can be deduced (
3
) for
n=2^{m}
. When
n
is not a power of two, we can find
m
such that
n<2^{m}
. Taking the values
b_{1}=a_{1}
, …,
b_{n}=a_{n}
, and

b_{k}=\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n},\qquad n+1\leq k\leq 2^{m},

from (
3
) with
n=2^{m}
, we have

\frac{\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{2^{m}}}{2^{m}}+1\right)\right)^{2^{m}}}{\Gamma(b_{1}+1)\cdots\Gamma(b_{2^{m}}+1)}\left(I_{\frac{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{2^{m}}}{2^{m}}}(x)\right)^{2^{m}}\\
\leq I_{b_{1}}(x)I_{b_{2}}(x)\cdots I_{b_{n}}(x)I_{b_{n+1}}(x)\cdots I_{b_{2^{m}}}(x)\leq\left(I_{\frac{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{2^{m}}}{2^{m}}}(x)\right)^{2^{m}}.

Finally, applying that

\frac{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{2^{m}}}{2^{m}}=\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}

and

I_{b_{n+1}}(x)\cdots I_{b_{2^{m}}}(x)=\left(I_{\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}}(x)\right)^{2^{m}-n}

the general case of (
3
) follows.
∎

The result in the previous proposition is a particular case of
[
27
, Theorem 2.7]
. We have included this new proof because it is elementary and to do this paper self-contained.

Taking the normalized modified Bessel functions of the first kind

\mathcal{I}_{a}(x)=\frac{2^{a}\Gamma(a+1)}{x^{a}}I_{a}(x),

that verify
\mathcal{I}_{a}(0)=1
, the left inequality in (
3
) reads

\left(\mathcal{I}_{\frac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}}(x)\right)^{n}\leq\mathcal{I}_{a_{1}}(x)\cdots\mathcal{I}_{a_{n}}(x).

2.2.
Some inequalities for the difference of modified Bessel functions of first kind

It is easy to check that the modified Bessel functions decrease with the order; i.e.,
I_{a}(x)<I_{b}(x)
when
-1<b<a
and
x>0
. We will use this fact sometimes along the paper without explicit mention to it.

In our next result we analyze the difference of two consecutive modified Bessel functions of first kind.

Lemma 2
.

For
a>-1
and
x>0
the inequalities

(5)

0<I_{a}(x)-I_{a+1}(x)<\frac{a+1}{x}I_{a}(x)

hold.

Proof.

The lower bound is a consequence of the monotonicity respect to the order of the functions
I_{a}(x)
. To obtain the upper bound, we use the inequalities
[
22
, Theorem 2]

(6)

f_{a+1}(x)<\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}<f_{a+1/2}(x),

where

f_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{x}{\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+x^{2}}}

and the upper bound holds for
a\geq-1/2
and the lower one for
a\geq-1
. Then, it is clear that

(7)

g_{a+1/2}(x)<1-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}<g_{a+1}(x),

with

g_{\alpha}(x)=1-f_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+x+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+x^{2}}}

and now the upper bound holds for
a\geq-1
and the lower one for
a\geq-1/2
.
Then, by using the upper bound in (
7
), for
a>-1
, we have

I_{a}(x)-I_{a+1}(x)=\left(1-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right)I_{a}(x)<g_{a+1}(x)I_{a}(x)\leq\frac{a+1}{x}I_{a}(x).\qed

Lemma 3
.

For
a\geq-1/2
and
x>0
the inequality

(8)

\left|I_{a}(x)-2I_{a+1}(x)+I_{a+2}(x)\right|<\left(\frac{3}{2x}+\frac{(a+1)(a+2)}{x^{2}}\right)I_{a}(x)

holds.

Proof.

We estimate

\left|1-2\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}+\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right|.

To do this we use the obvious identity

1-2\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}+\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}=S_{1}+S_{2}

with

S_{1}=\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}

and

S_{2}=\left(1-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right)\left(1-\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}\right).

By using (
6
) and that
f_{\alpha}(x)<f_{\beta}(x)
when
\beta<\alpha
, for
a\geq-1/2
we have

|S_{1}|<\max\{f_{a+1}(x)-f_{a+3/2}(x),f_{a+1/2}(x)-f_{a+2}(x)\}=f_{a+1/2}(x)-f_{a+2}(x).

Now, with the bound

(9)

f_{a}(x)-f_{a+p}(x)=\frac{p+\sqrt{x^{2}+a^{2}}-\sqrt{x^{2}+(a+p)^{2}}}{x}\leq\frac{p}{x},\qquad p>0

we have
|S_{1}|<3/(2x)
.

For
S_{2}
we use (
7
) to obtain that

S_{2}<g_{a+1}(x)g_{a+2}(x)\leq\frac{(a+1)(a+2)}{x^{2}}

and the proof is finished.
∎

Finally, we estimate an alternating sum of four consecutive modified Bessel functions of first kind.

Lemma 4
.

For
a\geq-1/2
and
x>0
the inequality

(10)

\left|I_{a}(x)-3I_{a+1}(x)+3I_{a+2}(x)-I_{a+3}(x)\right|<\\
C\left(\frac{a+2}{x^{2}}+\frac{(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)}{x^{3}}\right)I_{a}(x)

holds.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we focus on an appropriate bound for

\left|1-3\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}+3\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right|.

In this time, we have

1-3\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}+3\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3},

with

T_{1}=-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}+2\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a+2}(x)},

T_{2}=\left(1-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right)\left(\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a+2}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}\right)\\
+\left(1-\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}\right)\left(\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a+2}(x)}-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right)

and

T_{3}=\left(1-\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}\right)\left(1-\frac{I_{a+2}(x)}{I_{a+1}(x)}\right)\left(1-\frac{I_{a+3}(x)}{I_{a+2}(x)}\right).

We are going to estimate
|T_{1}|
by using continued fractions. Let

\xi:=\langle q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots\rangle=q_{0}+\frac{1}{q_{1}+\frac{1}{q_{2}+\frac{1}{q_{3}+\cdots}}}

be a continued fraction. If

\langle q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\rangle=\frac{P_{n}}{Q_{n}},

it is known
[
14
, Ch. 2, Theorem 9]
that

(11)

\left|\xi-\frac{P_{n}}{Q_{n}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{Q_{n}Q_{n+1}},\qquad n\geq 0.

In
[
17
, 10.33.1]
, we find the expansion

\frac{I_{a+1}(x)}{I_{a}(x)}=\langle q_{0}(a,x),q_{1}(a,x),q_{2}(a,x),\dots\rangle,\qquad x>0,\quad a>-1,

with
q_{0}(a,x)=0
and
q_{k}(a,x)=2(a+k)/x
for
k\geq 1
. Then, taking
n=2
in (
11
),

|T_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{Q_{2}(a,x)Q_{3}(a,x)}+\frac{2}{Q_{2}(a+1,x)Q_{3}(a+1,x)}+\frac{1}{Q_{2}(a+2,x)Q_{3}(a+2,x)}\\
+\left|\frac{P_{2}(a,x)}{Q_{2}(a,x)}-2\frac{P_{2}(a+1,x)}{Q_{2}(a+1,x)}+\frac{P_{2}(a+2,x)}{Q_{2}(a+2,x)}\right|.

By using that

Q_{2}(a,x)=4(a+1)(a+2)+x^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q_{3}(a,x)=4(a+2)(2(a+1)(a+3)+x^{2})

and applying the inequalities

(a+1)^{2}+x^{2}\leq Q_{2}(a,x)\leq Q_{2}(a+1,x)

and

4(a+2)((a+1)^{2}+x^{2})\leq Q_{3}(a,x)\leq Q_{3}(a+1,x),

for
a>-1
and
x>0
, we have

(12)

\frac{1}{Q_{2}(a,x)Q_{3}(a,x)}+\frac{2}{Q_{2}(a+1,x)Q_{3}(a+1,x)}+\frac{1}{Q_{2}(a+2,x)Q_{3}(a+2,x)}\\
\leq\frac{1}{(a+2)((a+1)^{2}+x^{2})^{2}}.

Moreover, due to
P_{2}(a,x)=2(a+2)x
, for
a\geq-1/2

\frac{P_{2}(a,x)}{Q_{2}(a,x)}-2\frac{P_{2}(a+1,x)}{Q_{2}(a+1,x)}+\frac{P_{2}(a+2,x)}{Q_{2}(a+2,x)}\\
=\frac{16x(4(a+2)(a+3)(a+4)-x^{2}(8+3a))}{Q_{2}(a,x)Q_{2}(a+1,x)Q_{2}(a+2,x)}

and

(13)

\left|\frac{P_{2}(a,x)}{Q_{2}(a,x)}-2\frac{P_{2}(a+1,x)}{Q_{2}(a+1,x)}+\frac{P_{2}(a+2,x)}{Q_{2}(a+2,x)}\right|\\
\leq C\frac{x(a+3)((a+2)(a+4)+x^{2})}{((a+1)^{2}+x^{2})^{3}}\leq\frac{C}{(a+1)^{2}+x^{2}}.

In this way, from (
12
) and (
13
), we obtain that

|T_{1}|\leq\frac{C}{x^{2}},\qquad a\geq-1/2.

Now, from (
6
) and (
7
), it is clear that

|T_{2}|\leq g_{a+1}(x)(f_{a+3/2}(x)-f_{a+3}(x))+g_{a+2}(x)(f_{a+1/2}(x)-f_{a+3}(x))

and applying (
9
) we have

|T_{2}|\leq\frac{C}{x}(g_{a+1}(x)+g_{a+2}(x))\leq C\frac{a+2}{x^{2}}.

Finally, from (
7
), the bound

T_{3}<g_{a+1}(x)g_{a+2}(x)g_{a+3}(x)\leq\frac{(a+1)(a+2)(a+3)}{x^{3}}

can be deduced and the proof of (
10
) is completed.
∎

2.3.
A uniform bound for the modified Bessel functions of first kind

Now, we present a uniform bound respect to the order for the function
I_{a}(x)
. It will be fundamental all along the paper.

Lemma 5
.

For
a>-1/2
, let
\alpha
be a real number such that
-1/2\leq\alpha<a
. Then the inequality

(14)

x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)\leq\frac{C}{(a+1)^{2\alpha+1}}

holds with a constant
C
independent of
a
.

Proof.

From the identity
[
17
, 10.32.2]

I_{a}(x)=\frac{x^{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}2^{a}\Gamma(a+1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-xs}(1-s^{2})^{a-1/2}\,ds,\qquad a>-1/2,

by using the change of variable
x(1+s)=2w
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{a}x^{-\alpha-1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(a+1/2)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2w}w^{a-1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(a+1/2)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\left(\frac{w}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw.

We start with the case
a\geq 1/2
. Using the inequality

z^{p}(1-z)^{q}\leq\frac{p^{p}q^{q}}{(p+q)^{p+q}},\qquad p,q\geq 0,\quad 0<z<1,

where in the cases
p=0
,
q=0
, and
p=q=0
the bound has to be understood as one,
we have

\left(\frac{w}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\leq\frac{C}{a^{\alpha+1/2}},

with
C
depending on
\alpha
but not on
a
,
and

\displaystyle x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{a}}{\Gamma(a+1/2)a^{\alpha+1/2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\,dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Gamma(a+1/2)a^{\alpha+1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}r^{a-1-\alpha}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\frac{\Gamma(a-\alpha)}{\Gamma(a+1/2)a^{\alpha+1/2}}\leq\frac{C}{a^{2\alpha+1}},

where in the last step we have applied that
\Gamma(r+p)/\Gamma(r+q)\sim r^{p-q}
, and this is enough for our purpose.

For
-1/2<a<1/2
we have to prove that
x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)\leq C
. We consider the decomposition

x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)=\frac{2^{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(a+1/2)}\\
\times\left(\int_{0}^{x/2}+\int_{x/2}^{x}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\left(\frac{w}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw\right).

The first integral can be controlled easily. In fact,

\int_{0}^{x/2}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\left(\frac{w}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw\\
\leq\frac{1}{2^{\alpha+a}}\int_{0}^{x/2}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\,dw\leq\frac{\Gamma(a-\alpha)}{2^{2a}}.

For the second integral, with the change of variable
w=x(1-r)
, we have

\int_{x/2}^{x}e^{-2w}w^{a-1-\alpha}\left(\frac{w}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{a-1-\alpha}\int_{x/2}^{x}e^{-2w}\left(1-\frac{w}{x}\right)^{a-1/2}\,dw\\
&=\frac{x^{a-\alpha}}{2^{a-1-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1/2}e^{-2x(1-r)}r^{a-1/2}\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{x^{a-\alpha}}{2^{a-1-\alpha}}e^{-x}\int_{0}^{1/2}r^{a-1/2}\,dr\leq\frac{(a-\alpha)^{a-\alpha}e^{-a+\alpha}}{2^{a-1-\alpha}(a+1/2)},\end{aligned}

where in the last step we have applied that
x^{b}e^{-x}\leq b^{b}e^{-b}
for
x,b>0
. Then, using that
0<a-\alpha<1

x^{-\alpha}e^{-x}I_{a}(x)\leq\frac{2^{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(a+1/2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(a-\alpha)}{2^{2a}}+\frac{(a-\alpha)^{a-\alpha}e^{-a+\alpha}}{2^{a-1-\alpha}(a+1/2)}\right)\leq C

and the proof is finished.
∎

3.
The heat semigroup and beyond

Basic properties of the operator (
1
) can be deduced easily. First, by using (
18
), we have

(21)

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})=1

and, due to the positivity of
G_{t,N}
,

|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})|\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

So
W_{t}
is well defined for any sequence in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Moreover, from (
16
) and (
19
), we deduce the properties

W_{0}f(\mathbf{n})=f(\mathbf{n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad W_{t_{1}}W_{t_{2}}f(\mathbf{n})=W_{t_{1}+t_{2}}f(\mathbf{n})

and the family of operators
W_{t}
is a semigroup. By using again the positive of the heat kernel, we obtain that
W_{t}
is a positive operator (it is positive for positive sequences) and from (
18
) it is possible to prove that it is Markovian; i. e.,

W_{t}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{n})=\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{n}),

where
\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{n})
is the sequence having one in all its entries.

Defining the convolution on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
by

f\ast g(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})g(\mathbf{k}),

we can observe that

W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=G_{t,N}\ast f(\mathbf{n}).

Then, by Young’s inequality

(22)

\|f\ast g\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\|g\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},

with
1/q+1/r=1+1/p
and
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
, we obtain that

(23)

\|W_{t}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty

where in the last step we have applied (
21
).

For a sequence
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, its Fourier transform is given by

\mathcal{F}f(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})e^{2\pi i\langle\mathbf{k},x\rangle},\qquad x\in[-1/2,1/2]^{N}.

It is well known that
\mathcal{F}
can be extended as an isometry from the space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
into
L^{2}([-1/2,1/2]^{N})
with inverse

\mathcal{F}^{-1}f(\mathbf{n})=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle\mathbf{n},x\rangle}\,dx,\qquad f\in L^{2}([-1/2,1/2]^{N}).

Moreover, the Parseval’s identity

\|\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{2}([-1/2,1/2]^{N})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

Now, from the integral representation
[
17
, 10.32.3]

(24)

I_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{t\cos\theta}\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

we get

e^{-t}I_{n}(t)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-2t\sin^{2}(\pi\theta)}e^{-2\pi in\theta}\,d\theta.

and

(25)

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}e^{-4t\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})}e^{-2\pi i\langle\mathbf{n},x\rangle}\,dx.

Then

\mathcal{F}(G_{t,N})(x)=e^{-4t\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})}.

Hence, using

\mathcal{F}(f\ast g)=\mathcal{F}f\cdot\mathcal{F}g,

we deduce that

\lim_{t\to 0}\|W_{t}f-f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}=\lim_{t\to 0}\left\|(e^{-4t\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})}-1)\mathcal{F}f\right\|_{L^{2}([-1/2,1/2]^{N})}=0

and we have the convergence in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
of the heat semigroup. Moreover, due to the structure of the spaces
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, we have the pointwise convergence

W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})\longrightarrow f(\mathbf{n}),\qquad\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

Note that, by using the inequality
1-e^{-s}\leq s
with
s\geq 0
, it is verified that

(26)

|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n})|\leq Ct\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

3.1.
Decay of the heat semigroup

Now we focus on the study of the decay of the heat semigroup in time. This is a natural question when we are deling with this operator. We start with a
\ell^{q}-\ell^{p}
estimate. The analogous for
\mathbb{R}^{N}
can be seen, for example, in
[
5
, Ch. 3, p. 44]
. Our discrete result can be found in
[
13
]
but we include here it to do this paper self-contained and to provide a new and simple proof.

Theorem 6
.

For
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, the inequality

(27)

\|W_{t}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

Proof.

From the Young’s inequality for the convolution, it is enough to prove that

(28)

\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-N/2(1-1/r)}

and to do that we use Littlewood’s inequality

(29)

\|f\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{1/r}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{1-1/r},\qquad 1\leq r\leq\infty,

the identity (
21
) and the bound
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\leq Ct^{-N/2}
.
This last estimate can be deduced from (
3
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=-1/2
. Indeed, using the notations
\mathbf{n}_{+}=(|n_{1}|,\dots,|n_{N}|)
and
|\mathbf{n}|=|n_{1}|+\cdots+|n_{N}|
and taking into account that
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})=G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}_{+})
, it is verified that

(30)

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\leq\left(e^{-2t}I_{|\mathbf{n}|/N}(2t)\right)^{N}=t^{-N/2}\left(t^{1/2}e^{-2t}I_{|\mathbf{n}|/N}(2t)\right)^{N}\leq Ct^{-N/2}.\qed

Remark 1
.

(a) By using (
3
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=0
, it is easy to check that

(31)

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\leq C(1+|\mathbf{n}|)^{-N}\leq C.

Then, for
0<t<T
, with
T
finite, we deduce the bound

(32)

\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C

and the estimate

\|W_{t}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Moreover, combining (
27
) and (
32
), it is possible to obtain the inequality

\|W_{t}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\min\{1,t^{-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\}\|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},\qquad 1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty.

(b) From the previous theorem it is possible to prove that for
t>1

(33)

\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\sim t^{-N/2(1-1/r)},\qquad 1\leq r\leq 2.

The upper bound is given in (
28
). To prove the lower one, we consider the sequence
f(\mathbf{k})=G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})
to obtain

W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n-k})=G_{2t,N}(\mathbf{n}),

where in the las step we have applied (
19
). Then, from (
27
), we deduce that

\|G_{2t,N}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq t^{-N/2(1/r-1/2)}\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},\qquad 1\leq r\leq 2.

In this way, proving that

(34)

\|G_{2t,N}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\geq Ct^{-N/4}

the proof of the lower bound in (
33
) will be completed. From Parseval’s identity

\displaystyle\|G_{2t,N}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}e^{-8t\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})}\,dx=\left(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-8t\sin^{2}(\pi s)}\,ds\right)^{N}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{-1/(2\sqrt{t})}^{1/(2\sqrt{t})}e^{-8t\sin^{2}(\pi s)}\,ds\right)^{N}\geq Ct^{-N/2},

where we have used that
e^{-8t\sin^{2}(\pi s)}\geq K
for
s\in[-1/(2\sqrt{t}),1/(2\sqrt{t})]
, and the proof of (
34
) is finished.

The next result contains the main novelty of this subsection. For the classical heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with an initial data having polynomial decay at infinity, in
[
8
]
the authors study how the mass of the solution is distributed for large value of
t
. In fact, it is proved the inequality

\left\|H_{t}f-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)\,dx\right)G_{t}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-1/2-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\||\cdot|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
H_{t}
is the solution of the heat equation,
G_{t}
its kernel (the Gaussian function),
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q<N/(N-1)
,
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
|\cdot|f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Remember that the mass of the solution
H_{t}
is a conservative quantity; i.e.,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{t}f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)\,dx

Note that (
21
) implies that

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}W_{t}f(\mathbf{k})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k}),

so in the discrete setting the mass of the solution of the heat equation is conservative also.
The discrete analogous of this result is given in
[
13
]
only in the case
q=1
, with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and the case
1<q\leq p
is left as an open problem. Our next result closes this problem completely.

Theorem 7
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q<N/(N-1)
, and
|\cdot|f\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then the inequality

(35)

\left\|W_{t}f-\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})\right)G_{t,N}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-1/2-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\||\cdot|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

To prove our result we need some preliminart lemmas.

Lemma 8
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that

H_{t,N}(z)=\frac{z}{t}\left(G_{t,1}\left(z\right)\right)^{N},\qquad z>0.

\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}

Without lost of generality we suppose that

n_{i}=m_{i}
for
j

\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\cup\mathbf{n}_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\cup\mathbf{m}_{2},

where
\mathbf{n}_{1}
are the common values of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{n}_{2}
and
\mathbf{m}_{2}
are the other components.
Then, if we suppose that the result has been proved for different values, we have

\displaystyle|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m})|

\displaystyle=G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})|G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{n}_{2})-G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{m}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq CG_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})|\mathbf{n}_{2}-\mathbf{m}_{2}|H_{t,N-j}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|}{K}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|H_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{2KN}\right).

To check the last inequality we use (
3
) to have

G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N-j}\\
\leq\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{(N-j)(|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|)/K+|\mathbf{n}_{1}|}{N}\right)\right)^{N},

then the inequality follows applying the monotonicity of the functions
I_{a}
and the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{(N-j)(|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|)}{K}+|\mathbf{n}_{1}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(N-j)(|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|)+|\mathbf{n}_{1}|}{K}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|+2|\mathbf{n}_{1}|}{2K}=\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{2K}.

From (
15
), it is clear that
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})=G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}_{+})
. Moreover, we have to observe that
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})
is invariant under permutations of
\mathbf{n}
. With these previous reductions, we can consider
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{N})
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{N})
with

(37)

An important tool in the proof will be the identity

(38)

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i})\right)\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}G_{t,1}(m_{j})\prod_{k=i+1}^{N}G_{t,1}(n_{k}),

where the empty products has to be understood as one. The proof of this relation we can done induction on
N
. To do that we consider
\tilde{\mathbf{n}}=(\mathbf{n},n_{N+1})
and
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}=(\mathbf{m},m_{N+1})
and we apply the identity

G_{t,N+1}(\tilde{\mathbf{n}})-G_{t,N+1}(\tilde{\mathbf{m}})=(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))G_{t,1}(n_{N+1})\\
+G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m})(G_{t,1}(n_{N+1})-G_{t,1}(m_{N+1})).

For
n_{i}<m_{i}
, applying (
5

\displaystyle|G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n_{i}}^{m_{i}-1}|\delta^{+}G_{t,1}(j)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=n_{i}}^{m_{i}-1}(j+1)G_{t,1}(j)\leq\frac{(m_{i}-n_{i})(m_{i}+n_{i}+1)}{t}G_{t,1}(n_{i}).

Then, in general, we obtain the bound

(39)

|G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i})|\leq\frac{|m_{i}-n_{i}|(m_{i}+n_{i}+1)}{2t}\max\{G_{t,1}(n_{i}),G_{t,1}(m_{i})\}

and, from (
38

(40)

|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m})|\\
\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|m_{i}-n_{i}|(m_{i}+n_{i}+1)}{2t}\max\{G_{t,1}(n_{i}),G_{t,1}(m_{i})\}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}G_{t,1}(m_{i})\kern-2.5pt\prod_{k=i+1}^{N}\kern-4.5ptG_{t,1}(n_{k}).

3

From the condition

, we can deduce that
|\mathbf{n}|/2<|\mathbf{m}|<3|\mathbf{n}|/2
. Then

and if

and for

As conclusion,

\left\|H_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\cdot|}{K}\right)\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\frac{1}{(|\mathbf{n}|+1)^{N+1}}\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\leq C.

For

we consider the decomposition

where

(this notation will used in some points along the paper without explicit mention to it). For

Now, by using (

In this way

\left\|H_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\cdot|}{K}\right)\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq Ct^{-1/2}.

and the result follows from (

∎

Proof of the Theorem

From the restriction

where

Then, by using (

Finally, applying the well known inequality

which is a consequence of the classical Hardy’s inequality for

From (

3.2.
Maximal operators for the heat and the Poisson semigroups

From the well known identity

e^{-|\beta|t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}}e^{-t^{2}\beta^{2}/(4u)}\,du=\frac{t}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t^{2}/(4v)}}{\sqrt{v}}e^{-v\beta^{2}}\frac{dv}{v},

we can define by subordination the Poisson semigroup

(54)

P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}}W_{t^{2}/(4u)}f(\mathbf{n})\,du=\frac{t}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t^{2}/(4v)}}{\sqrt{v}}W_{v}f(\mathbf{n})\frac{dv}{v}.

It is an easy exercise to show that
P_{t}
satisfies the Laplace type equation

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})+\Delta_{N}P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=0.

In this part of the paper we are interested in the maximal operators

W^{\star}f(\mathbf{n})=\sup_{t>0}|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})|

and

P^{\star}f(\mathbf{n})=\sup_{t>0}|P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})|.

In fact, we want analyze weighted inequalities for these operators with weights in the Muckenhoupt class. We say that a positive sequence
w
is a weight in
A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
for
1<p<\infty
when

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in Q}w(\mathbf{n})\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in Q}(w(\mathbf{n}))^{-1/(p-1)}\right)^{p-1}\leq C,

where
Q
is any cube in
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
\mu(A)=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in A}1
for any
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. The weight
w
belongs to
A_{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
when

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in Q}w(\mathbf{n})\right)\sup\left\{(w(\mathbf{n})^{-1}:\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\right\}\leq C.

To obtain our result, we will use the Calderón-Zygmund theory adapts to our setting. We say that
K
, defined on
\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
and taking values in a Banach space
A
, is a standard kernel when

(55)

\|K(\mathbf{n})\|_{A}\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N}}

and

(56)

\|K(\mathbf{n})-K(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m})\|_{A}\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{m}|}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+1}},\qquad|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{m}|.

Let
T
be an operator bounded from
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
to
\ell_{A}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
(where we understand that
f\in\ell_{A}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
when
\|f\|_{A}\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
) for some
1<q<\infty
and for
f\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
with compact support

Tf(\mathbf{n})=K\ast f(\mathbf{n}),\qquad\mathbf{n}\notin\operatorname{supp}f,

where
K
is a standard kernel taking values in
A
, we say that
T
is Calderón-Zygmund operator.

A Calderón-Zygmung operator
Tf
is bounded from
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
into
\ell^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
for
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and from
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
into
\ell^{1,\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
for
w\in A_{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
(see
[
20
,
21
]
). As usual the space
\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
is defined as the space of sequences such that
\|f\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}<\infty
, where

\|f\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\left(\lambda\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in A_{\lambda}}w(\mathbf{n})\right)

and

A_{\lambda}=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:|f(\mathbf{n})|>\lambda\}.

Theorem 13
.

For
T
equal
W^{\star}
or
P^{\star}
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, the inequalities

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\qquad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

and

\|Tf\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

hold.

Proof.

We focus on
W^{\star}
because the result for
P^{\star}
follows from its definition by subordination (
54
). By using that

W^{\ast}f(\mathbf{n})=\|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty))},

we prove that
W^{\star}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator taking values in
L^{\infty}((0,\infty))
. From (
23
), applying Stein’s Maximal Theorem of diffusion semigroups (see
[
23
, Chapter III, Section 2]
), we have

\|W^{\ast}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},\qquad 1<p<\infty.

In particular we have the case
p=2
. To conclude it is enough with the estimates

(57)

\|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty))}\leq\frac{C}{(|\mathbf{n}|+1)^{N}}

and

(58)

\|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty))}\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}},\qquad|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{m}|.

The bound (
57
) has been proved yet in (
31
).

Now, from (
36
) and applying (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
, we deduce that for
|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{m}|
it is verified

\displaystyle|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m})|

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{m}|(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}|)\left(t^{-1/N}G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}}

and the proof of (
58
) is completed.
∎

4.
The fractional integrals and the Riesz transforms

From the identity

\frac{1}{\lambda^{\sigma}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt,

we define the fractional integrals (or negative powers of the Laplacian
\Delta_{N}
) by the relation

(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt.

By using that (see
[
15
]
or take
\alpha=-1/2
in (
20
))

e^{-t}I_{k}(t)\sim t^{-1/2},\qquad t\to\infty,

it is clear that the powers
(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}
are well defined for
0<2\sigma<N
.

Having these operators, for
N\geq 2
we can define the Riesz transforms as

R_{i}f(\mathbf{n})=\delta_{i}^{+}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}f(\mathbf{n}),\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

In
[
6
]
it was analyzed the Riesz transform for
N=1
defining it as a limit. In fact, it was defined as

Rf(n)=\lim_{\sigma\to(1/2)^{-}}\delta^{+}(-\Delta_{1})^{-\sigma}f(n).\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

From this, the identity

Rf(n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{f(n)}{n-k+1/2}

was obtained; i.e., it matches with the discrete Hilbert transform.

In this section, firstly, we show some basic properties of the fractional integrals and we provide a Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in our setting and an extension of it. We continue with the study of multidimensional discrete Schauder inequalities for
(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}
. To conclude we analyze the mapping properties of the Riesz transforms on the Hölder classes and on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)

4.1.
Basic properties of the fractional integrals

In this part, we provide some properties of the fractional integrals, we prove a Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality for them and an extension of it.

In some points a long the paper we will consider the spaces

(59)

\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})=\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w_{\sigma})

with
w_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})=(1+|\mathbf{n}|)^{2\sigma-N}
. Moreover, we will denote
\|f\|_{\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}=\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w_{\sigma})}
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
0<\sigma<N/2
and
f\in\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. We have the pointwise formula

(60)

(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k})

where

K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

and it verifies that

(61)

0<K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})\leq\frac{C}{(|\mathbf{n}|+1)^{N-2\sigma}}.

Proof.

First we are going to check that the heat operator is well defined for
f\in\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
.
Indeed, for each
L>0
with
t
fixed, by using (
3
) and the asymptotic (
20
),

\displaystyle\sum_{{|\mathbf{k}|>L}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})|f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2Nt}N^{N/2}\sum_{{|\mathbf{k}|>L}}\left(\frac{(Net)^{|\mathbf{k}|/N}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{|\mathbf{k}|/N+1/2}}\right)^{N}|f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}e^{-2Nt}N^{N/2}\sup_{{|\mathbf{k}|>L}}\frac{(Net)^{|\mathbf{k}|}(1+|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|)^{N-2\sigma}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{|\mathbf{k}|+N/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})},

where the last constant depends on
t
,
N
,
L
,
\mathbf{n}
, and
\sigma
but not on
f
.

Let us prove (
60
). To do it we can suppose that
f
is a non negative sequence because in other case we can consider its decomposition in positive and negative parts. Then, applying Tonelli’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})G_{t}(\mathbf{k})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k}).

Now, let us go with the proof of (
61
). The positivity of
K_{\sigma}
is a simple consequence of the positivity of
G_{t,N}
.
Now, to obtain the upper bound for the kernel, we consider the decomposition

K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})=I_{1}+I_{2}

with

I_{1}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\qquad\text{ and }\qquad I_{2}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{\sigma-1}\,dt.

We consider
\mathbf{n}\not=\mathbf{0}
, because the bound
K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{0})\leq C
is clear. For
I_{1}
we use (
31
) to have

I_{1}\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N}}\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N-2\sigma}},

and for
I_{2}
we apply the bound in (
30
) to obtain that

I_{2}\leq C\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{N/2+1-\sigma}}=\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N-2\sigma}}.\qed

To prove our next theorem we need the following result that we can see in
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
.

Theorem 15
.

Let be

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{f(\mathbf{k})}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{n-2\sigma}},\qquad 0<2\sigma<N,

and
\langle\mathbf{n}\rangle=(1+|\mathbf{n}|^{2})^{1/2}
. Taking
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and the sets of conditions

(\mathcal{C}_{1})\qquad\begin{cases}s\leq t,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{2\sigma}{N}+\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{s}{N},0\right\}\leq\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{t}{N},0\right\},\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{2\sigma}{N}+\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{s}{N}\leq 1,\\[3.0pt]
\end{cases}

(\mathcal{C}_{2})\qquad\begin{cases}s\leq t=2\sigma-N,q=1,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{s}{N}<0\end{cases}

and

(\mathcal{C}_{3})\qquad\begin{cases}s\leq t,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{2\sigma}{N}+\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{s}{N}=\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{t}{N},\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{p}\leq\dfrac{1}{q},\quad 0<\dfrac{1}{p}+\dfrac{s}{N},\quad\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{t}{N}<1,\end{cases}

it is verified that

\|\langle\mathbf{\cdot}\rangle^{s}\mathcal{I}_{2\sigma}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|\langle\mathbf{\cdot}\rangle^{t}f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

if and only if
(p,q,s,t)
satisfies one of the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{3}
.

Remark 4
.

The previous theorem in its full version includes the case
p,q=\infty
but the definition of the Lebesgue for such value in
[
9
]
is different from ours one, so we omit this particular value from the theorem.

Now, we can present the weighted Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev for the fractional powers
(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
0<2\sigma<N
,
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. If
(q,p,s,t)
verify one of the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{3}
, then

\|\langle\mathbf{\cdot}\rangle^{s}(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq\|\langle\mathbf{\cdot}\rangle^{t}f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Proof.

The result is immediate from the previous theorem because, by (
61
), it is verified that

|(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})|\leq C\left(|\mathcal{I}_{2\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})|+|f(\mathbf{n})|\right).\qed

Taking
t=s=0
in the previous theorem, we recover the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in our setting. However, we can go further.

Corollary 17
.

Let
0<2\sigma<N
,
1<q<p<\infty
with
1/p\leq 1/q-2\sigma/N
, then the inequality

\|(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

Moreover, for
1<q<p\leq 2
the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev implies
1/p\leq 1/q-2\sigma/N

Proof.

We have to proof the second part of the result. To do this, we consider the sequence
f(\mathbf{n})=G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})
with
t>1
.
In this way,

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})G_{s,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})s^{\sigma-1}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{t+s,N}(\mathbf{n})s^{\sigma-1}\,ds\geq\int_{0}^{t}G_{t+s,N}(\mathbf{n})s^{\sigma-1}\,ds\geq G_{2t}(\mathbf{n})t^{\sigma}.

Then, by the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev, we have

t^{\sigma}\|G_{2t,N}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\|G_{t,N}\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

and, applying (
33
), it becomes
t^{\sigma-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\leq C

and this implies
1/p\leq 1/q-2\sigma/N
.
∎

To finish this subsection we show a new inequality extending the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev one.

Theorem 18
.

Let
0<2\sigma<N
,
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
with
1\leq q<N/(N-1)
and
1/p<1/q-(2\sigma-1)/N
, and
|\cdot|f\in\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, then the inequality

\left\|(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f-\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})\right)K_{\sigma}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\||\cdot|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

Proof.

The result follows from Minkowsky integral inequality and (
53
). Indeed,

\left\|(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}f-\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})\right)K_{\sigma}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|W_{t}f-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})G_{t,N}\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\\
&\leq C\||\cdot|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{1,t^{-1/2-N/2(1/q-1/p)}\}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\\
&\leq C\||\cdot|f\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.\end{aligned}

∎

4.2.
Discrete Schauder estimates for the fractional integrals

Now, we prove multidimensional discrete Schauder estimates for the fractional integrals and to do that we need to define the Hölder spaces. Given a sequence
f=\{f(\mathbf{k})\}_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we say that
f
belongs to the Hölder space
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
when

[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}:=\sup_{\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{m})|}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

For a natural number
k
such that
k\geq 1

we say that
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})

when it is verified that

[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}:=\sum_{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{N}=k}[(\delta_{1}^{+})^{m_{1}}(\delta_{2}^{+})^{m_{2}}\cdots(\delta_{N}^{+})^{m_{N}}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}<\infty.

Sometimes it is common write the definition of Hölder classes combining
\delta_{i}^{+}
and
\delta_{i}^{-}
but such definitions are equivalents to the our one due to the relation
\delta_{i}^{-}f(\mathbf{n})=\delta_{i}^{+}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i})
.

The next result is the multidimensional version of
[
7
, Theorem 1.6]
.

Theorem 19
.

Let
k\geq 0
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\sigma<1/2
and
f\in\ell_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
.

a)

If
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and
2\sigma+\alpha<1
then
(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f\in C^{k,2\sigma+\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and

[(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f]_{C^{k,2\sigma+\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

b)

If
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and
2\sigma+\alpha>1
then
(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f\in C^{k+1,2\sigma+\alpha-1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and

[(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f]_{C^{k+1,2\sigma+\alpha-1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

c)

If
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
then
(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f\in C^{0,2\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and

[(-\Delta)^{-\sigma}f]_{C^{0,2\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Before starting with the proof of the Schauder estimates, we need the four following lemmas.

Lemma 20
.

For
N\geq 1
and
0<\sigma<1/2
, we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}((-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}(\mathbf{k}))=0.

Proof.

From the obvious identity

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k}))=0,

we deduce that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k}))t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=0,

Let us see now that, for
0<\sigma<1/2
, we can apply Fubini theorem. From (
21
), it is clear that

\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\leq C|\mathbf{n}|^{2\sigma}<\infty,

so we have to focus on the integral in the interval
(|\mathbf{n}|^{2},\infty)
. To do that we consider

I_{1}=\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq 2|\mathbf{n}|}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

and

I_{2}=\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}|}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt.

For
I_{1}
we use the bound (
41
) to obtain that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}|^{2}\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq 2|\mathbf{n}|}1\right)t^{\sigma-N/2-2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}|^{2+N}\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\sigma-N/2-2}\,dt\leq C|\mathbf{n}|^{2\sigma}<\infty.

To analyze
I_{2}
we apply the identity
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})=G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{n})
, (
36
) and (
44
) to deduce the estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}|\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}|}H_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{k}|}{K}\right)\right)t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}|\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\sigma-3/2}\,dt\leq C|n|^{2\sigma}<\infty.

In this way,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}((-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-(-\Delta_{N})^{-\sigma}(\mathbf{k}))\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k}))t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=0

and the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 21
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
, and
0<2\sigma<N
.
Then the inequality

(62)

|K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})-K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{m})|\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1-2\sigma}}

holds with a constant
C
independent of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

It is clear that

|K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})-K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{m})|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=J_{1}+J_{2},

where

J_{1}=\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

and

J_{2}=\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}|G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m})|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt.

To estimate
J_{1}
we consider Lemma
8
and apply (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
. Indeed,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}\frac{1}{t}\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}}\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1-2\sigma}}.

The bound for
J_{2}
is obtained from (
41
) in the following way

J_{2}\leq|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\sigma-N/2-2}\,dt\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1-2\sigma}}.\qed

Lemma 22
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
, and

Then the inequality

(63)

|\delta_{i}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\mathcal{H}_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{K}\right),\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

holds with constants
C
and
K>1
independent of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

We consider
i=1
because the other cases can be done in the same way. We can prove the result for
n_{i}\not=m_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,N
because this situation implies the general case. In fact, to deduce the general case from this one, as in Lemma
8
, let us suppose that
n_{i}=m_{i}
for
j
values of
i
, with
1<j<N
and consider the decompositions

\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\cup\mathbf{n}_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\cup\mathbf{m}_{2},

where
\mathbf{n}_{1}
are the common values of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{n}_{2}
and
\mathbf{m}_{2}
are the other components. Now, we have to distinguish two possibilities
n_{1}\in\mathbf{n}_{1}
and
n_{1}\notin\mathbf{n}_{1}
. In the first situation, by using (
5
) and Lemma
8
, we have

|\delta_{1}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C\frac{|n_{1}|+1}{t}G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})|G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{n}_{2})-G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{m}_{2})|\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C|\mathbf{n}_{2}-\mathbf{m_{2}}|\frac{(|\mathbf{n}_{1}|+1)(|\mathbf{n_{2}}|+|\mathbf{m_{2}}|)}{t^{2}}G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N-j}\\
&\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N-j}\end{aligned}

and we conclude as in Lemma
8
. When
n_{1}\notin\mathbf{n}_{1}
, if we consider proved the case with different components, it is verified that

\displaystyle|\delta_{1}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|

\displaystyle=G_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})|\delta_{1}^{+}(G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{n}_{2})-G_{t,N-j}(\mathbf{m}_{2}))|

\displaystyle\leq CG_{t,j}(\mathbf{n}_{1})|\mathbf{n}_{2}-\mathbf{m}_{2}|\mathcal{H}_{t,N-j}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{2}|+|\mathbf{m}_{2}|}{K}\right)

and the proof of this case is finished again as in Lemma
8
.

Now, we assume the restrictions (
37
) on
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
as in the proof of Lemma
8
.
Then, using (
38
), we get

(64)

\delta_{1}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))=\delta^{+}(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))\prod_{k=2}^{N}G_{t,1}(n_{k})\\
+\delta^{+}G_{t,1}(m_{1})\sum_{i=2}^{N}(G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i}))\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}G_{t,1}(m_{j})\prod_{k=i+1}^{N}G_{t,1}(n_{k}).

Proceeding as in Lemma
8
and using (
5
), we have

(65)

|\delta^{+}G_{t,1}(m_{1})(G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i}))|\\

\displaystyle|\delta^{+}(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{m_{1}-1}\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{t^{2}}\right)G_{t,1}(k)

In general,

(66)

With (

), and (

.

Remark 5
.

|\delta_{i}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)t^{-N/2},\qquad\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

Lemma 23
.

Let

,
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
, and
0<2\sigma<N
.
Then the inequality

(68)

|\delta_{i}^{+}(K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})-K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2-2\sigma}},\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

holds with a constant
C
independent of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

It is clear that

|\delta_{i}^{+}(K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{n})-K_{\sigma}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\delta_{i}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m}))|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt=Q_{1}+Q_{2},

with

Q_{1}=\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}|\delta_{i}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m}))|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt

and

Q_{2}=\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}|\delta_{i}^{+}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,n}(\mathbf{m}))|t^{\sigma-1}\,dt.

To obtain the required bound for
Q_{1}
we consider Lemma
22
and apply (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
and
\alpha=2/N
. Indeed,

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}\frac{1}{t}\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\right.

\displaystyle\kern 10.0pt\left.+(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{K}\right)\right)^{N}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2}}\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2-2\sigma}}.

The estimate for
Q_{2}
can be deduced from (
67
) in the following way

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\sigma-N/2-2}\,dt+(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}t^{\sigma-N/2-3}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2-2\sigma}}.\qed

Proof of Theorem

.

4.3.
The Riesz transforms

As we have said at the beginning of this section the Riesz transform are well defined,
because the kernel

\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{i}^{+}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{-1/2}\,dt

is absolutely convergent.

Of course the Riesz transforms can be written for
N\geq 2
as

R_{i}f(\mathbf{n})=\mathcal{R}_{i}\ast f(\mathbf{n}),

with

\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{i}^{+}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{-1/2}\,dt.

To present our results about the Riesz transforms we need two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 24
.

Let
N\geq 2
, then the inequalities

(69)

|\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n})|\leq\frac{C}{(|\mathbf{n}|+1)^{N}}

and

(70)

|\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n})-\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{m})|\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}},\qquad|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|,

hold.

Proof.

To prove (
69
) we consider
\mathbf{n}\not=\mathbf{0}
because the bound
|\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{0})|\leq C
is obvious. Now, from (
5
) and (
3
), it is deduced that

|\delta_{i}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})|\leq C\frac{|n_{i}|+1}{t}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}|}{t}\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|}{N}\right)\right)^{N}.

Then, using (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
and
\alpha=-1/2
, we deduce that

|\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n})|\leq C|\mathbf{n}|\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2}}\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}t^{-1/2}\,dt+\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N/2-3/2}\,dt\right)\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N}}.

Now, we decompose the difference in (
70
) in the following way

|\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n})-\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{m})|\leq\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}|\delta_{i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|t^{-1/2}\,dt\\
+\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}|\delta_{i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|t^{-1/2}\,dt:=L_{1}+L_{2}.

To analyze
L_{1}
we apply (
63
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
and
\alpha=2/N
to obtain that

|\delta_{i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2}}

and

L_{1}\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+2}}\int_{0}^{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}t^{-1/2}\,dt\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}}.

From (
67
), we have

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N/2-3/2}\,dt+(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}\int_{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N/2-5/2}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}}

and the proof is finished.
∎

Lemma 25
.

For
N\geq 2
it is verified that

(71)

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{k})=0,\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

and, for a fix value
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

(72)

\left|\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\geq\ell}\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{k})\right|\leq C,\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

Proof.

To prove the result we focus on
i=1
because the other cases are similar. We consider
M\in\mathbb{N}
big enough and the partial sums

S_{M}=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k}).

Now, we observe that

(73)

\mathcal{R}_{1}(-\mathbf{k})=-\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{e}_{1}).

Indeed, it is clear that

\displaystyle\delta_{1}^{+}G_{t,N}(-\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=(G_{t,1}(-k_{1}+1)-G_{t,1}(-k_{1}))\prod_{j=2}^{N}G_{t,1}(-k_{j})

\displaystyle=-(G_{t,1}(k_{1})-G_{t,1}(k_{1}-1))\prod_{j=2}^{N}G_{t,1}(k_{j})=-\delta_{1}^{+}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{e}_{1})

and (
73
) follows immediately. With (
73
) we have

\displaystyle 2S_{M}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k})+\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(-\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k})-\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{e}_{1})=\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k})-\sum_{|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{e}_{1}|\leq M}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k}).

Then, applying (
69
) we get

|S_{M}|\leq C\sum_{M-1\leq|\mathbf{k}|\leq M+1}|\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k})|\leq\frac{C}{M}

and

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathbf{k})=\lim_{M\to\infty}S_{M}=0

proving (
71
).

To analyze (
72
), we have to note that

\left|\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|\geq\ell}\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{k})\right|=\left|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{k})-S_{\ell-1}\right|=|S_{\ell-1}|\leq\frac{C}{\ell-1}

and the result follows for
\ell>1
. The case
\ell=1
is obvious.
∎

First, we present the behaviour of the Riesz transforms on the Hölder classes.

Theorem 26
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<1/2
, and
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then for
1\leq i\leq N
the inequality

[R_{i}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

Proof.

By using Lemma
25
, for
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
it is easy to check that

\displaystyle R_{i}f(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{n}))\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|\leq 2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}(f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{n}))\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle\kern 20.0pt+\sum_{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}(f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{n}))\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle:=S_{1}(\mathbf{n})+S_{2}(\mathbf{n}).

For the difference
S_{1}(\mathbf{n})-S_{1}(\mathbf{m})
, by using that
|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|\leq 2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
implies
|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{k}|\leq 3|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
and the estimate (
69
), it is verified that

|S_{1}(\mathbf{n})-S_{1}(\mathbf{m})|\leq|S_{1}(\mathbf{n})|+|S_{1}(\mathbf{m})|\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\left(\sum_{0<|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|\leq 2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{n}|^{\alpha-N}+\sum_{0<|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{k}|\leq 3|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha-N}\right)\\
&\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}.\end{aligned}

To treat
S_{2}(\mathbf{n})-S_{2}(\mathbf{m})
we add the term
\pm f(\mathbf{m})\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
and apply (
70
) and (
72
) to have

|S_{2}(\mathbf{n})-S_{2}(\mathbf{m})|\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\sum_{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}|f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{m})||\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})-\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{k})|\\
&\kern 15.0pt+|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{m})|\left|\sum_{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}\mathcal{R}_{i}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\right|\\
&\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\left(|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\sum_{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{\alpha-N-1}+|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}\right)\\
&\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}\end{aligned}

and this concludes the proof.
∎

It would be possible to define another Riesz transform by mean

\overline{R}_{i}f(\mathbf{n})=\delta_{i}^{-}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}f(\mathbf{n}).

The identity
\delta_{i}^{-}f(\mathbf{n})=\delta_{i}^{+}f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{i})
proves the following result.

Corollary 27
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
0<\alpha<1/2
, and
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then for
1\leq i\leq N
the inequality

[\overline{R}_{i}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

holds.

To finish this section we give a result about the boundedness with weights of the Riesz transforms.

Theorem 28
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
1\leq i\leq N
. Then the inequalities

\|R_{i}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

and

\|R_{i}f\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

hold.

Proof.

We will prove that each
R_{i}
is Calderón-Zygmund operator.

First, we check that
R_{i}
is bounded from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
into itself.
For
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, we have

\mathcal{F}(\delta_{i}f)(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i})e^{2\pi i\langle\mathbf{n},x\rangle}-\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{n})e^{2\pi i\langle\mathbf{n},x\rangle}=(e^{-2\pi ix_{i}}-1)\mathcal{F}f(x)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}((-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-4t(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k}))}\mathcal{F}f(x)t^{-1/2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\left(4\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{F}f(x).

With this we deduce that
\delta_{i}^{+}
and
(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}
can be seen as multipliers of the functions
e^{-2\pi ix_{i}}-1
and
\left(4\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{-1/2}
, respectively.
Then, the operator
R_{i}
is associated with the multiplier

\frac{e^{-2\pi ix_{i}}-1}{\left(4\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{1/2}};

i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(R_{i}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-2\pi ix_{i}}-1}{\left(4\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{1/2}}\mathcal{F}f

\displaystyle=\frac{-ie^{\pi ix_{i}}\sin(\pi x_{i})}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{1/2}}\mathcal{F}f,\qquad i=1,\dots,N.

In this way, using the bound

\frac{|\sin(\pi x_{i})|}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{k})\right)^{1/2}}\leq 1,

by Plancherel identity we have

\|R_{i}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}=\|\mathcal{F}(R_{i}f)\|_{L^{2}\left([-1/2,1/2]^{N}\right)}\leq C\|\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{2}\left([-1/2,1/2]^{N}\right)}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Finally, the estimates (
55
) and (
56
) can be deduced from (
69
) and (
70
) and we have concluded.
∎

For the Riesz transforms
\overline{R}_{i}
we have the next result.

Corollary 29
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
1\leq i\leq N
. Then the inequalities

\|\overline{R}_{i}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

and

\|\overline{R}_{i}f\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

hold.

5.
Fractional powers of the Laplacian

From the identity

\lambda^{s}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\lambda t}-1)\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}},\qquad 0<s<1,

we define the fractional powers (in fact, positive powers) of
-\Delta_{N}
by

(74)

(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}))\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}).

By using (
21
), it is clear that

(75)

W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})(f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{n})).

Moreover, we can prove that the operators
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}
are well defined in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
by (
74
). Indeed, we consider the decomposition

\int_{0}^{\infty}|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n})|\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}\leq F_{1}+F_{2}

where

F_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n})|\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

and
F_{2}
is the integral on
[1,\infty)
of the same function. For
F_{1}
, applying (
26
), we have

F_{1}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-s}\,dt\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Now, from Theorem
6
with
p=\infty
and
q=2
, we deduce that

|W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n})|\leq C(t^{-N/4}+1)\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

and

F_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\int_{1}^{\infty}(t^{-N/4}+1)t^{-s-1}\,dt\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

In this section we will focus on the study of some aspects of the operators
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}
. Firstly, we will show some basic properties of the fractional powers of the Laplacian, including a maximum principle. In the last part of the section we will deal with the mapping properties of
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}
on the Hölder classes.

5.1.
Basic properties of the fractional powers of the Laplacian

In this section we present some elementary properties of the fractional powers
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}
. They are collected in the next theorem.

Theorem 30
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
0<s<1
.

i)

For
f\in\ell_{-s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, it is verified that

(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}\end{smallmatrix}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{k})),

where

(76)

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}},\qquad\mathbf{n}\not=\mathbf{0},

and
\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{0})=0
.

ii)

For
\mathbf{n}\not=\mathbf{0}
, the inequalities

(77)

0<\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n})\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2s}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{1-s}+\frac{2}{N+2s}\right)

hold with a constant
C
independent of
s
.

iii)

For
f\in\ell_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, it is verified that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})=f(\mathbf{n}).

iv)

For
f\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, it is verified that

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})=-\Delta_{N}f(\mathbf{n}).

Proof.

First, we observe that
W_{t}f
is well defined on the spaces
\ell_{-s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
for
0\leq s\leq 1
. Effectively, for
M>0
big enough, with
t
fix, we have

\sum_{|\mathbf{k}|>M}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\leq C\|f\|_{\ell_{-s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\sup_{|\mathbf{k}|>M}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})(1+|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|)^{N+2s}.

From (
3
) and the asymptotic expansion (
20
), we deduce that

\sup_{|\mathbf{k}|>M}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})(1+|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|)^{N+2s}\\
\leq Ce^{-2Nt}N^{N/2}\sup_{{|\mathbf{k}|>M}}\frac{(Net)^{|\mathbf{k}|}(1+|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{k}|)^{N+2s}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{|\mathbf{k}|+N/2}}\leq C_{t,M,|\mathbf{n}|,s,N}

and we conclude.

To prove i) we use (
75
) to get

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})(f(\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{n}))\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{k}))

where
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is given by (
76
). The proof will be completed justifying the application of Fubini theorem in the second identity. To do this we need the upper bound in ii), so we proceed with its proof.

From (
3
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=1/N
, we obtain that

G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{-1}\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2}}.

Then, with (
30
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2}}\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}t^{-s}\,dt+\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N/2-s-1}\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2s}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{1-s}+\frac{2}{N+2s}\right).

The positivity of the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
is clear.

Let us justify the interchange of the sum and the integral. First, we apply (
77
) and Tonelli’s theorem in the term

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})|f(\mathbf{k})|\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

to have that it is bounded by

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\frac{|f(\mathbf{k})|}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{N+2s}}

and this term is finite for each
\mathbf{n}
because
f\in\ell_{-s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Similarly for the term

|f(\mathbf{n})|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}},

we obtain the bound

|f(\mathbf{n})|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{N+2s}}

and this last sum is finite.

To prove iii) we check that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}N_{1}=1\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}N_{2}=0

where

N_{1}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad N_{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})|f(\mathbf{k})|.

We start with
N_{2}
. From (
77
) and (
30
), we have

N_{2}\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{1-s}+\frac{2}{N+2s}\right)\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\frac{|f(\mathbf{k})|}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{N+2s}}.

Now, using that
f\in\ell_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, applying dominated convergence, we deduce that for each
\mathbf{n}
it is verified that

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\frac{|f(\mathbf{k})|}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{N+2s}}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

and then
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}N_{2}=0
. To treat
N_{1}
, we consider the decomposition
N_{1}=N_{1,1}+N_{1,2}
where

N_{1,1}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\int_{0}^{1}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

and
N_{1,2}
is the same sum but with the integral on the interval
[1,\infty)
. To analyze
N_{1,1}
we apply (
3
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=1/(2N)
and we consider
0<s<1/2
. In this way,

N_{1,1}\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k}|^{N+1}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1/2-s}\,dt\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|(1-2s)}

and
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}N_{1,1}=0
. Now, using that

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{n}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})=1-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{0}),

we obtain that

N_{1,2}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\int_{1}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{0})\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}\right).

With the bound (
30
) we get

\int_{1}^{\infty}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{0})\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}\leq C\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-N/2-s-1}\,dt=\frac{C}{N+2s}

and, with the identity
|\Gamma(-s)|s=\Gamma(1-s)
we conclude that
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}N_{1,2}=1
.

To prove iv) we start observing

(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{e}_{k}))(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{k}))+\mathcal{K}_{s}(-\mathbf{e}_{k}))(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{k})))\\
+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
\mathbf{k}\notin\{\mathbf{0},\pm\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\pm\mathbf{e}_{N}\}\end{smallmatrix}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{k})(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})):=M_{1}+M_{2}.

Taking into account that
\mathcal{K}_{s}(-\mathbf{n})=\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n})
, we have

M_{1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{e}_{k})\Delta_{N,i}f(\mathbf{n}).

Now, using that for any continuous and bounded function on
[0,\infty)
,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}f(t)\frac{dt}{t^{s}}=f(0)

(the identity is clear for polynomials and with the hypotheses on
f
the general result is obtained), taking the function

f(z)=\frac{2I_{1}(z)}{z}(I_{0}(z))^{N-1},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{e}_{i})

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2Nt}\frac{I_{1}(2t)}{t}(I_{0}(2t))^{N-1}\frac{dt}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{(2N)^{s-1}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w}f\left(\frac{w}{N}\right)\frac{dw}{w^{s}}=f(0)=1.

Then,

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}M_{1}=-\Delta_{N}f(\mathbf{n}).

Finally, let us check that
\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}M_{2}=0
. By using that
G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})t^{-3/2}\leq C|\mathbf{n}|^{-N-3}
(which can be deduced from (
3
) and (
14
) with
\alpha=3/(2N)
) and proceeding as in the proof of (
77
), we can obtain the bound

\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{n})\leq\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{N+2s}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\left(\frac{1}{3-2s}+\frac{1}{N+2s}\right).

Then

M_{2}\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\not=\mathbf{0}}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{k}|^{N+2s}}\leq\frac{C}{|\Gamma(-s)|}\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

and
\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}M_{2}=0
.
∎

To conclude this section, we present maximum and comparison principles for the fractional powers which are obvious consequences of the positivity of the kernel
\mathcal{K}_{s}
.

Theorem 31
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
0<s<1
.

i)

(Maximum principle) Let
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
be such that
f\geq 0
and
f(\mathbf{n}_{0})=0
for some
\mathbf{n}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then

(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n}_{0})\leq 0.

Moreover,
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n}_{0})=0
only if
f(\mathbf{n})=0
for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

ii)

(Comparison principle) Let
f,g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
be such that
f\geq g
and
f(\mathbf{n}_{0})=g(\mathbf{n}_{0})
for some
\mathbf{n}_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. Then

(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n}_{0})\leq(-\Delta_{N})^{s}g(\mathbf{n}_{0}).

Moreover,
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n}_{0})=(-\Delta_{N})^{s}g(\mathbf{n}_{0})
only if
f(\mathbf{n})=g(\mathbf{n})
for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

5.2.
The fractional powers of the Laplacian on the Hölder classes

The target of this subsection is the analysis of the behaviour of
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}
in the Hölder classes and it is contained in the next result.

Theorem 32
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
k\geq 0
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<s<1
, and
f\in\ell_{-s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
.

i)

If
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and
2s<\alpha
then
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f\in C^{k,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and

[(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f]_{C^{k,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

ii)

If
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and
\alpha<2s<\alpha+1
then
(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f\in C^{k,\alpha-2s+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
and

[(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f]_{C^{k,\alpha-2s+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{k+1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

Proof.

We will prove both results for
k=0
because the other cases can be deduced by using that
\delta_{i}^{+}(-\Delta_{N})^{s}=(-\Delta_{N})^{s}\delta_{i}^{+}
.

To prove i), we consider the identity

|(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{n})-(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f(\mathbf{m})|=|B_{1}+B_{2}|,

where

B_{1}=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
1\leq|\mathbf{k}|\leq|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\end{smallmatrix}}(f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{m})+f(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k}))\mathcal{K}_{s}(\mathbf{k})

and
B_{2}
is the sum on
|\mathbf{k}|>|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
. First, from the estimate

\displaystyle|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{m})+f(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{k})|+|f(\mathbf{m})-f(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{k}|^{\alpha}

and (
77
), we get

B_{1}\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
1\leq|\mathbf{k}|\leq|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\end{smallmatrix}}|\mathbf{k}|^{\alpha-N-2s}\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha-2s}

and this is appropriate for our purpose. To treat
B_{2}
, we use that

\displaystyle|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{m})+f(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\mathbf{n})-f(\mathbf{m})|+|f(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{k})-f(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{k})|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}

and the bound (
77
), to get

B_{2}\leq[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\
|\mathbf{k}|>|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\end{smallmatrix}}|\mathbf{k}|^{-N-2s}\leq C[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|^{\alpha-2s}

and the proof of i) is completed.

We consider
N\geq 2
, the case
N=1
can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 1.5]
. We consider the identity

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{N})^{s}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{N})^{s-1/2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}(-\Delta_{N})=(-\Delta_{N})^{s-1/2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{-}\delta_{i}^{+}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{N})^{s-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{R}_{i}\delta_{i}^{+}

and we distinguish two cases. For
s-1/2>0
it is verified that
0<\alpha-2(s-1/2)<1
and, applying i) and Corollary
27
, we deduce that

\displaystyle[(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f]_{C^{0,\alpha-2s+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\overline{R}_{i}\delta_{i}^{+}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\delta_{i}^{+}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

In the case
s-1/2<0
we have
0<\alpha+2(-s+1/2)
and we can apply a) in Theorem
19
and Corollary
27
to conclude

\displaystyle[(-\Delta_{N})^{s}f]_{C^{0,\alpha-2s+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

\displaystyle=[(-\Delta_{N})^{-(-s+1/2)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{R}_{i}\delta_{i}^{+}f]_{C^{0,\alpha-2s+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\overline{R}_{i}\delta_{i}^{+}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\delta_{i}^{+}f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}\leq C[f]_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}.

For
s=1/2
the result is a direct consequence of Corollary
27
.
∎

6.
The discrete square functions

In this section we will prove weighted inequalities for the discrete Littlewood-Paley-Stein
g_{k}
-square functions associated to the heat semigroup and for the
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
-square functions associated to the Poisson semigroup. Moreover, we conclude with a result about Laplace type multipliers.

6.1.
The
g_{k}
-square functions associated to the heat semigroup

The discrete
g_{k}
-square functions associated to the heat semigroup are defined by

g_{k}(f)(\mathbf{n})=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-1}\left|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}W_{t}f(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2},\qquad k=1,2,\dots.

Note that taking the Banach space
\mathbb{B}_{k}=L^{2}((0,\infty),t^{2k-1}\,dt)
, we have

g_{k}f(\mathbf{n})=\|P_{t,k}f(\mathbf{n})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}

with

P_{t,k}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})\mathcal{P}_{t,k}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{t,k}(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}e^{-4t\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}F_{t,k}(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dt,

being

F_{t,k}(x)=(-4)^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})\right)^{k}e^{-4t\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}.

Our main result about the
g_{k}
-square functions is the following one.

Theorem 33
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the inequalities

(78)

C_{1}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

hold.

To obtain the proof of the equivalence in the previous theorem, we start proving the boundedness of the
g_{k}
-square functions from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
into itself.

Lemma 34
.

For
N\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it is verified that

(79)

\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{2^{2k}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}.

Proof.

For each sequence in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, we have

P_{t,k}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(F_{t,k}\mathcal{F}f).

Then

\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\|P_{t,k}f(\mathbf{k})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(P_{t,k}f(\mathbf{k}))^{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}^{2}.

By applying Parseval’s identity,

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}(P_{t,k}f(\mathbf{k}))^{2}=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}(F_{t,k}(x))^{2}(\mathcal{F}f(x))^{2}\,dx

and

\displaystyle\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-1}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}(F_{t,k}(x))^{2}(\mathcal{F}f(x))^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}(\mathcal{F}f(x))^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-1}(F_{t,k}(x))^{2}\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{2^{2k}}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}(\mathcal{F}f(x))^{2}\,dx=\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{2^{2k}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}.\qed

Now, we proceed with two reductions to simplify the proof of Theorem
33
.

Lemma 35
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the inequality

\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)

implies

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}).

Proof.

Polarising the identity (
79
), we have

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})h(\mathbf{k})=\frac{2^{2k}}{\Gamma(2k)}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-1}\left(\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}W_{t}f(\mathbf{k})\right)\left(\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}W_{t}h(\mathbf{k})\right)\,dt

and

\left|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})h(\mathbf{k})\right|\leq C\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}g_{k}(f)(\mathbf{k})g_{k}(h)(\mathbf{k}).

In this way, taking
h(\mathbf{k})=w^{1/p}(\mathbf{k})f_{1}(\mathbf{k})
and applying a duality argument the result follows.
∎

Lemma 36
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1<p<\infty
, and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then the inequality

\|g_{1}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}),

implies

\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad k>1,\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}).

Proof.

We use an induction argument to prove the result. Let us suppose that the operator
P_{t,k}
is bounded from
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
into
\ell^{p}_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
. Taking
k=1
and applying Krivine’s theorem (see
[
16
, Theorem 1.f.14]
), we deduce that the operator
\overline{P}_{t,1}:\ell^{p}_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)\longrightarrow\ell^{p}_{\mathbb{B}_{k}\times\mathbb{B}_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
, given by

\{f_{s}(n)\}_{s\geq 0}\longmapsto\{P_{t,1}f_{s}\}_{t,s\geq 0},

is bounded. Moreover,
\overline{P}_{t,1}\circ P_{s,k}
is a bounded operator from
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
into
\ell^{p}_{\mathbb{B}_{k}\times\mathbb{B}_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)
. With the Chapman-Kolmogorov type identity

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{k})G_{s,N}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})=G_{t+s,N}(\mathbf{n}),

which can be deduced from Neumann’s identity (
19
), we obtain that

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(W_{t}\left(\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}W_{s}f\right)\right)=\left.\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial u^{k+1}}W_{u}f\right|_{u=s+t},

and using this we have

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{P}_{t,1}\circ P_{s,k}f\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{k}\times\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}ts^{2k-1}\left|\left.\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial u^{k+1}}W_{u}f\right|_{u=s+t}\right|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}t(r-t)^{2k-1}\left|\left.\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial u^{k+1}}W_{u}f\right|_{u=r}\right|^{2}\,dr\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial r^{k+1}}W_{r}f\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{r}t(r-t)^{2k-1}\,dt\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2k+1)(2k)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2k+1}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial r^{k+1}}W_{r}f\right|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{k+1}(f)}{(2k+1)(2k)}

and the proof is finished.
∎

After the reductions in the previous lemmas, Theorem
33
will be a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 37
.

Let
N\geq 1
and
w\in A_{p}
. Then the inequalities

\|g_{1}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\qquad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})

and

\|g_{1}(f)\|_{\ell^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n},w)},\qquad\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}),

hold.

To prove this theorem we check that
g_{1}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator. With Lemma
34

and the estimates in the following lemma this fact will be clear.

Lemma 38
.

Let
N\geq 1
, then inequalities

(80)

\|\mathcal{P}_{t,1}(\mathbf{n})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}\leq\frac{C}{(|\mathbf{n}|+1)^{N}}

and

(81)

\|\mathcal{P}_{t,1}(\mathbf{n})-\mathcal{P}_{t,1}(\mathbf{m})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}},\qquad|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|

hold.

Before proving the previous lemma, we need another one.

Lemma 39
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
such that
|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|
, and

\mathfrak{H}_{t,N}(z)=\left(\frac{z}{t^{2}}+\frac{z^{3}}{t^{3}}\right)\left(G_{t,1}(z)\right)^{N},\qquad z>0.

Then the inequality

(82)

|\Delta_{N,i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\mathfrak{H}_{t,N}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|}{k}\right),\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

holds with constants
C
and
K>1
independent of
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

Again, it is enough to analyze the case
i=1
because the other ones work similarly.

By using the ideas in Lemma
8
and Lemma
22
, we can reduce the proof to the case where
n_{i}\not=m_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,N
. Moreover, the assumptions (
37
) on
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
in the proof of Lemma
8
can be considered also.

We start with the case
m_{1}\geq 2
. From (
38
) we deduce that

\Delta_{N,1}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))=\Delta(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))\prod_{k=2}^{N}G_{t,1}(n_{k})\\
+\sum_{j=2}^{N}\Delta G_{t,1}(m_{1})(G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i}))\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}G_{t,1}(m_{j})\prod_{k=i+1}^{N}G_{t,1}(n_{k}).

We can control
G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i})
as in the proof of Lemma
8
and, applying (
8
), we have

(83)

|\Delta G_{t,1}(m_{1})(G_{t,1}(n_{i})-G_{t,1}(m_{i}))|\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq C\frac{|n_{i}-m_{i}|(m_{i}+n_{i}+1)}{t}\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)G_{t,1}(m_{1}-1)\max\{G_{t,1}(n_{i}),G_{t,1}(m_{i})\}\\
&\leq C\frac{|n_{i}-m_{i}|(m_{i}+n_{i}+1)}{t}\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)G_{t,1}(m_{1}/2)\max\{G_{t,1}(n_{i}),G_{t,1}(m_{i})\},\end{aligned}

where in the last step we have used that
m_{1}-1\geq m_{1}/2
because
m_{1}\geq 2
.

For
n_{1}<m_{1}
, we can obtain that

\Delta(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))=-\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{m_{1}-1}\Delta(G_{t,1}(k+1)-G_{t,1}(k)))\\
=-\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{m_{1}-1}(G_{t,1}(k+3)-3G_{t,1}(k+2)+3G_{t,1}(k+1)-G_{t,1}(k))

and, applying (
10
),

\displaystyle|\Delta(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{m_{1}-1}\left(\frac{k+2}{t^{2}}+\frac{(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)}{t^{3}}\right)G_{t,1}(k)

\displaystyle\leq C(m_{1}-n_{1})\left(\frac{m_{1}+n_{1}}{t^{2}}+\frac{m_{1}^{3}+n_{1}^{3}}{t^{3}}\right)G_{t,1}(n_{1}).

More generally,

|\Delta(G_{t,1}(n_{1})-G_{t,1}(m_{1}))|\\
\leq C|m_{1}-n_{1}|\left(\frac{m_{1}+n_{1}}{t^{2}}+\frac{m_{1}^{3}+n_{1}^{3}}{t^{3}}\right)\max\{G_{t,1}(n_{1}),G_{t,1}(m_{1})\}.

Now, the proof of this case can be concluded as in Lemma
8
.

To finish we have to analyze the cases
m_{1}=0,1
. To obtain the result in these cases we need an appropriate estimate for
\Delta G_{t,1}(m_{1})
to insert it in (
83
). For
m_{1}=0
, we have

\Delta G_{t,1}(0)=G_{t,1}(1)-2G_{t,1}(0)+G_{t,1}(-1)=2(G_{t,1}(1)-G_{t,1}(0))

and, applying (
5
), it is verified that

|\Delta_{t,1}(0)|\leq\frac{C}{t}G_{t,1}(0)

and this is enough for our purpose. In the case
m_{1}=1
, for
0<t\leq 1
it is clear that

|\Delta_{t,1}(1)|\leq C\leq\frac{C}{t}G_{t,1}(1)

and for
t>1
, applying (
10
) and (
6
), we have

\displaystyle|\Delta_{t,1}(1)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)G_{t,1}(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)\frac{1}{f_{1}(t)}G_{t,1}(1)\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right)G_{t,1}(1),

where in the last step we have used that
1/f_{1}(t)\leq C
for
t>1
.
∎

Remark 6
.

From the proof of the previous result, it is cleat that

(84)

|\Delta_{N,i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))|\leq C|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|}{t^{2}}+\frac{|\mathbf{n}|^{3}}{t^{3}}\right)t^{-N/2},\qquad\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}.

Proof of Lemma
38
.

First, it is easy to check that

\|\mathcal{P}_{t,1}(\mathbf{n})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}=\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}=\left\|\Delta_{N}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\Delta_{N,i}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}.

Now, for
|\mathbf{n}|\geq 1
, by using (
8
), we have

|\Delta_{N,i}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|-1}{N}\right)\right)^{N}.

We consider the decomposition

\left\|\Delta_{N,i}G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})\right\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}^{2}\leq C(D_{1}+D_{2}),

where

D_{1}=\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}t\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|-1}{N}\right)\right)^{2N}\,dt

and
D_{2}
the integral of the same function on

. Applying (

) with

we deduce that

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}(t+|\mathbf{n}|^{2})^{2}\left(t^{-3/(2N)}G_{t,1}\left(\frac{|\mathbf{n}|-1}{N}\right)\right)^{2N}\,dt

For
D_{2}

, we obtain the estimate

D_{2}\leq C\left(\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N-1}\,dt+|\mathbf{n}|^{4}\int_{|\mathbf{n}|^{2}}^{\infty}t^{-N-3}\,dt\right)=\frac{C}{|\mathbf{n}|^{2N}}.

The case
|\mathbf{n}|=0
is clear and the proof of (
80
) is completed.

To obtain (

), it is enough to prove that

\|\Delta_{N,i}(G_{t,N}(\mathbf{n})-G_{t,N}(\mathbf{m}))\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}\leq C\frac{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|}{(|\mathbf{n}|+|\mathbf{m}|)^{N+1}},\qquad|\mathbf{n}|>2|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}|.

6.2.
The
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
-square functions associated to the Poisson semigroup

It is very common to define some square functions in terms of the Poisson semigroup
instead of the heat semigroup. In our case such
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
-square functions are given by

\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)(\mathbf{n})=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2k-1}\left|\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2},\qquad k=1,2,\dots.

By using that

P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})Q_{t}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

with

Q_{t}(\mathbf{n})=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}e^{-2t\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}}\rangle\,dx,

it is clear that

(86)

\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)(\mathbf{n})=\|R_{t,k}f(\mathbf{n})\|_{\mathbb{B}_{k}}

where

R_{t,k}f(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}f(\mathbf{k})Q_{t,k}(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})

being

Q_{t,k}(\mathbf{n})=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}Q_{t}(\mathbf{n})=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}S_{t,k}(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}}\rangle\,dx

and

S_{t,k}(x)=(-2)^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})\right)^{k/2}e^{-2t\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}}\rangle\,dx.

For this family of square functions we have the following result.

Theorem 40
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the inequalities

C_{1}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq\|\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

hold.

To prove the previous result we need the two next lemmas.

Lemma 41
.

For
N\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it is verified that

(87)

\|\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{2^{2k}}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})}^{2}.

The proof of this lemma can be done exactly as the proof of Lemma
34
but using (
86
) so we omit the details.

Lemma 42
.

Let
N\geq 1
,
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the inequality

\|\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}),

implies

\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}).

In this case, the proof of this lemma can be obtained polarising the identity (
87
) and using duality. Again we omit the details.

Finally, to conclude the proof of Theorem
40
we will use the estimate

\|g_{k}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)

and the next result.

Lemma 43
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)(\mathbf{n})\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}A_{k,j}g_{k-j}(f)(\mathbf{n}),

where
A_{k,j}
are some positive constants and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function.

Proof.

First, we observe that

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}h\left(\frac{t^{2}}{4u}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}B_{k,j}\left.\frac{\partial^{k-j}}{\partial s^{k-j}}h(s)\right|_{s=\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}\frac{t^{k-2j}}{(4u)^{k-j}},

for some constants
B_{k,j}
. Then, from (
54
), we have

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}P_{t}f(\mathbf{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}B_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}}\left(\left.\frac{\partial^{k-j}}{\partial s^{k-j}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|_{s=\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}\right)\frac{t^{k-2j}}{(4u)^{k-j}}\,du

and, by Minkowsky’s integral inequality,

\mathfrak{g}_{k}(f)(\mathbf{n})\leq\sum_{j=0}^{[k/2]}B_{k,j}S_{j}(\mathbf{n})

where

S_{j}(\mathbf{n})\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}(4u)^{k-j}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{4k-4j-1}\left(\left.\frac{\partial^{k-j}}{\partial s^{k-j}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|_{s=\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}\right)^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\,du.

Now, by using an appropriate change of variables, we have

\displaystyle S_{j}(\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{\sqrt{u}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2k-2j-1}\left(\frac{\partial^{k-j}}{\partial s^{k-j}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right)^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}g_{k-j}(f)(\mathbf{n})

and the result follows.
∎

6.3.
The Laplace type multipliers

Given a bounded function
M
defined on
[0,4N]
, the multiplier associated with
M
is the operator, initially defined on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, by the identity

T_{M}f(\mathbf{n})=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}M\left(4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})\right)\mathcal{F}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dx.

We say that
T_{M}
is a Laplace type multiplier when

M(x)=x\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}a(t)\,dt,

with
a
being a bounded function.

The Laplace type multipliers were introduced by Stein in
[
23
, Ch. 2]
. There, it is observed that they verify
|x^{k}M^{(k)}(x)|\leq C_{k}
for
k=0,1,\dots
, and then form a subclass of Marcinkiewicz multipliers. For the operators
T_{M}
we have the following result.

Theorem 44
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then,

\|T_{M}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

where
C
is a constant independent of
f
.

From the identity

x^{i\gamma}=\frac{x}{\Gamma(1-i\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}t^{-i\gamma}\,dt,\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{R},

we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 45
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
. Then,

\|(-\Delta_{N})^{i\gamma}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)},\qquad f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})\cap\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w),

where
C
is a constant independent of
f
.

Proof of Theorem
44
.

We only need prove that

(88)

g_{1}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n})\leq Cg_{2}(f)(\mathbf{n}),

since by Theorem
33
we get that

\|T_{M}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|g_{1}(T_{M}f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|g_{2}(f)\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}\leq C\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{N},w)}.

Moreover, it is enough to prove (
88
) for sequences in
c_{00}
, the space of sequences having a finite number of non-null terms. First, we have

T_{M}f(\mathbf{n})=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\,ds,

which is an elementary consequence of the relation

\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}M\left(4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})\right)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dx\\
\begin{aligned} &=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})e^{-4s\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dx\,ds\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{N}}4\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})e^{-4s\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^{2}(\pi x_{i})}e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\mathbf{n}\rangle}\,dx\,ds\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}G_{s,N}(\mathbf{n})\,ds.\end{aligned}

Then, applying the semigroup property of
W_{t}
we obtain

W_{t}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n})=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}W_{s+t}f(\mathbf{n})\,ds

and hence,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}W_{t}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n})=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}W_{s+t}f(\mathbf{n})\,ds=-\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}W_{s+t}f(\mathbf{n})\,ds.

In this way,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}W_{t}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{\infty}s\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|\,\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-1/2}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{2}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,ds\right)^{1/2}.

Finally,

\displaystyle(g_{1}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}W_{t}(T_{M}f)(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{2}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{3}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}W_{s}f(\mathbf{n})\right|^{2}\,ds=C(g_{2}(f)(\mathbf{n}))^{2}

and the proof of (
88
) is completed.
∎

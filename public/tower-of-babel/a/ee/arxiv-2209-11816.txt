Notes on Mitsui’s Prime Number Theorem with Siegel zeros
Wataru Kai

Abstract
. In these notes, we refine Mitsui’s Prime Number Theorem from 1957, which for a number field K K predicts how many prime elements there are in bounded convex sets in K ⊗ 𝐐 𝐑 K\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{R} , by incorporating potential Siegel zeros of Hecke L L -functions. This allows the norm of the modulus 𝐍 ⁡ ( 𝔮 ) \mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{q}) to grow at a pseudopolynomial rate e log ⁡ X / O ⁡ ( 1 ) e^{\sqrt{\log X}/O(1)} with respect to the size X X of the convex set as opposed to powers of log ⁡ X \log X . The extra flexibility and precision will be essential in our future application to the study of linear patterns of prime elements. We also hope that our updated exposition will make Mitsui’s work accessible to a wider mathematical audience.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Siegel–Walfisz prime number theorem

Prime Number Theorem states in one of the several equivalent forms:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{0<p<X}\log(p)=X+o(X)\quad\text{ as }X\to+\infty.

Here the sum
\sum_{0<p<X}
is the sum over prime numbers
p
satisfying
0<p<X
.

A little more generally, the Siegel–Walfisz prime number theorem says for any modulus
1<q<\log^{A}X
(
A\geq 1
is a prescribed constant)
and
a\in(\mathbf{Z}/q\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
, we have the estimate

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<p<X\\
p\equiv a\ \mathrm{mod}\,q\end{subarray}}\log(p)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}X+O_{A}(Xe^{-\sqrt{\log X}/O_{A}(1)}).

Formulated this way, the restriction
q<\log^{A}X

cannot be improved by today’s human technology because the contemporary analytic number theory has not been able to exclude the existence of notorious
Siegel zeros
of Dirichlet
L
-functions.
Siegel zeros are potential real zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions slightly smaller than
1
, which contradicts the Grand Riemann Hypothesis.
This concept will be reviewed in §
2
.

Indeed, a Siegel zero
0<\beta<1
modulo
q
would give rise to a second main term
X^{\beta}/\beta\varphi(q)
to (
1.2
).
While this can be absorbed in the error term if we stay in the realm
q<\log^{A}X
,
we have to take it seriously if we want to go beyond this bound.
Indeed, by incorporating the term
X^{\beta}/\beta\varphi(q)
into the picture,
the modulus
q
is allowed to be at least as large as
e^{\sqrt{\log X}/O(1)}
.
Namely, for all
1\leq q<e^{\sqrt{\log X}/O(1)}
we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<p<X\\
p\equiv a\ \mathrm{mod}\,q\end{subarray}}\log(p)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\left(X-\psi_{\mathrm{Siegel}}(a)\frac{X^{\beta}}{\beta}\right)+O(Xe^{-\sqrt{\log X}/O(1)})

where
\psi_{\mathrm{Siegel}}\colon(\mathbf{Z}/q\mathbf{Z})^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
is the potential Siegel character mod
q
.
See e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 5.27 on p. 122]
.

1.2.
Mitsui’s theorem and our main result

The number field analogue of the Siegel–Walfisz theorem has already been documented.
It is known as Mitsui’s prime number theorem,
which can be formulated as follows:

Theorem 1.2.1

(Mitsui
[
11

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n=[K:\mathbf{Q}]=\dim_{\mathbf{Q}}K
.
Let

be a fixed positive number.
Let

and consider a convex open set

contained in the region
(K\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{R})_{<X}
of size
X
(§
A.4

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{q})<\log^{A}X

and choose any

Then we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in C,\\
\text{prime element, }\\
\pi\equiv\alpha\text{\ in }\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q}\end{subarray}}\log|\mathbf{N}(\pi)|=\frac{w_{K}}{\varphi_{K}(\mathfrak{q})2^{r_{1}}\pi^{r_{2}}h_{K}R_{K}}\operatorname{Vol}(C)+O_{K,A}(X^{n}e^{-\sqrt{\log X}/O_{K,A}(1)}).

Here, there is a canonical

and

\mathbf{R}^{n}

The main purpose of this text is

\beta

\mathfrak{q}

To be specific, let

In our main Theorem

the right hand side of (

+O_{K}(X^{n}e^{-\sqrt{\log X}/O_{K}(1)}).

This modification allows us to relax the restrction (

1.5.3.
Landau’s
O(-)
symbol

The symbol
O_{a,b,c}(1)
denotes a complex value whose absolute value is bounded from above by a constant
C_{a,b,c}>0
depending only on the parameters
a,b,c
.
The specific value can be different from occasion to occasion.
In particular, an
O(1)
means a complex value whose absolute value is bounded by an absolute constant independent of any parameter even if some parameters are at play.

When
f(t)
is a positively valued function of
t
,
the expression
O_{a,b,c}(f(t))
means
O_{a,b,c}(1)\cdot f(t)
. Often
t
will be a positive real parameter, and the bound may be valid only when
t
is large enough.
We allow this threshhold to depend on the parameters
a,b,c
.
We shall indicate it if the threshhold depends on more (or less) parameters.

We make an exception of allowing ourselves to use
O_{K}(-)
even when the bounds depend on additional choices related to
K
,
as long as those choices are independent of the problem at hand and can be made once the number field
K
is given.
Such choices include
an isomoprhism
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cong(\mathbf{Z}/w\mathbf{Z})\times\mathbf{Z}^{r}
and representatives
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
(
\lambda\in\operatorname{Cl}(K)
) for the ideal class group.

The expression
f(t)\ll_{a,b,c}g(t)
means

f(t)=O_{a,b,c}(g(t))
.
The expression
f(t)\asymp_{a,b,c}g(t)
means that
f(t)\ll_{a,b,c}g(t)
and
g(t)\ll_{a,b,c}f(t)
both hold.

It goes without saying that a statement of the form

•

If
A=B/O_{a,b,c}(1)
, then
X=O_{d,e,f}(Y)
, or

•

If
A\ll_{a,b,c}B
, then

should be read as the statement that there are large enough positive constants
C_{a,b,c}>1
and
C_{d,e,f}>1

depending only on the parameters indicated in subscript such that the following holds:

If
|A|<B/C_{a,b,c}
, then
|X|<C_{d,e,f}Y
.

Observe that the larger the constants
C_{a,b,c},C_{d,e,f}
are, the more restrictive the hypothesis is and the weaker the conclusion. So the statement gets more likely to hold.

Appendix B
Fourier analysis on a torus

By a

By so-called Lie’s three theorems,

Of course the isomorphism

B.1.
Approximation of indicator functions of small convex subsets by characters

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a torus and

(B.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{T}}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{cont}}(\mathbf{T},S^{1})\cong\widehat{G}\times\mathbf{Z}^{d}

\mathbf{C}

\mathbf{T}

by
\mathbf{T}\xrightarrow{\xi}S^{1}\subset\mathbf{C}
.
Fourier analysis on
\mathbf{T}
roughly says that functions on
\mathbf{T}
can be written as
\mathbf{C}
-linear combinations of elements of
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}
.
The precise statement we use in this paper is Proposition
B.1.2
below.

Definition B.1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{T}
be a torus whose neutral component
\mathbf{T}^{0}
is equipped with an isomorphism
\mathbf{T}^{0}\cong(S^{1})^{d}
.
A
small convex subset
of
\mathbf{T}
(resp. of size
\leq\delta
,
0<\delta<1/4
) is a connected subset

such that for some

the translate

(B.3)

\displaystyle P-p\subset\mathbf{T}^{0}\cong(S^{1})^{d}\cong(\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z})^{d}

is the projection of an open convex subset
\widetilde{P}\subset[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}]^{d}\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
(resp.
\widetilde{P}\subset[-\delta,\delta]^{d}
).

Proposition B.1.2
.

Let
M,Y>1
be positive parameters. Let
P
be a small convex subset of a torus of size
<1/5
with respect to some identification
\mathbf{T}^{0}\cong(S^{1})^{d}=\mathbf{R}^{d}/\mathbf{Z}^{d}
.
Then the indicator function

can be approximated as:

(B.4)

\displaystyle 1_{P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}c_{\xi}\cdot\xi+G+H,

where

•

is a subset of size
O_{d}(\#\pi_{0}(\mathbf{T})\cdot Y^{d})
,

•

the coefficients

obey bounds

,

•

the values of

have absolute values
\leq 1
and there is another small convex set

satisfying

and
\operatorname{Vol}(P^{\prime}\setminus P)=O_{n}(1/M)
,

•

.

Moreover, for

the coefficient

can be explicitly described:
letting

\mathbf{T}
,

(B.5)

\displaystyle c_{\xi}=\frac{\mu(P)}{\mu(\mathbf{T})}\overline{\xi(P)}+O_{d}(1/M),

where the value

is well defined because
\xi\in\left(\pi_{0}(\mathbf{T})\right)\widehat{\ }
is constant on every connected set.

Proof.

This follows from
[
3
, Corollary A.3]
and
[
2
, Lemma A.9]
.

Note that although
[

, Lemma A.9]
is stated for the connected torus

) (
B.2
).

but
do not affect

∎

C.2.3.
A chart

To produce connected open sets
P
as in Proposition
C.2.2
, it is useful to take the following kind of chart.

To state it, for non-zero ideals
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
let us denote by

(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
the set of elements which is a generator of
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q}
-module.
For
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{a}
, this recovers the set of invertible elements
(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{q})^{\times}

Note that by Chinese Remainder Theorem

\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a}=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}}\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{p}^{n_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathfrak{a}

the condition that
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a}
is in
(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
is equivalent to the condition that
its image in
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{p}^{n_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathfrak{a}
is in
(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{p}^{n_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}
.

Let
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})\in\mathbf{Z}
be the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic valuation of a non-zero (fractional) ideal
\mathfrak{a}
.
Let us quickly recall its definition: the scalar extension
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
-module of rank
1
. Take any generator
\alpha
of
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
and we write
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a}):=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
, which is independent of the choice of
\alpha

Then
(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a})^{\times}
is equivalently defined as the set of residue classes
\overline{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a}
such that some (or any) lift
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}
satisfies

(C.31)

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})\text{ for all }\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}.

For each
\mathfrak{p}
, this condition does not depend on the specific lift
\alpha
.

Consider the map of sets

(C.32)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{a})^{\times}\to\left(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}}K^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{n_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}})\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}}\mathbf{Z}

composed with

for each
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}

and
the valuation

for each
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{q}
.
The map (
C.32

because if

are two elements satisfying
(
C.31
)

\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{q}

Indeed, we have

(C.33)

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varepsilon/\alpha_{2})\geq(v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{q})+v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a}))-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{a})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{q})=n_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Appendix D
Euclid’s algorithm

The following statement is used in §
C.2.1
.

Proposition D.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbf{Z}^{n}
be a subgroup of finite index
\#(\mathbf{Z}^{n}/\Lambda)=D
.
Then there is a basis
\bm{v}_{1},\dots,\bm{v}_{n}
of
\Lambda
whose entries have absolute values
\leq D
.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary basis of
\Lambda
and write it as a set of column vectors.

The assertion is translated to the following assertion on matrices:
let
A\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbf{Z})
be an
n\times n
matrix with positive determinant
D
.
Then there is an invertible matrix
U\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbf{Z})
such that
AU
has entries whose absolute values are
\leq D
.

When
n=1
(or
n=0
), the assertion is trivial.
We proceed by induction on
n
.

Let
d_{1}>0
be the greatest common divisor of the entries in the first row of
A
.
By Euclid’s algorithm, which can be realized as the right multiplication by invertible matrices, the matrix
A
can be transformed into the form

(D.1)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&0\dots 0\\
*&A^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{ with }A^{\prime}\in M_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}(\mathbf{Z}).

This also shows
d_{1}\mid D
.
Set
\det(A^{\prime})=D^{\prime}=D/d_{1}
.

From the induction hypothesis, by the right multiplication of invertible matrices which leaves the first column and row unchanged, one can transform
A^{\prime}
so that it has entries whose absolute values are
\leq D^{\prime}
.

The entries
*
in (
D.1
) might
a priori
have absolute values
>D
, but one can use the entries of the modified
A^{\prime}
to make them smaller than
D^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

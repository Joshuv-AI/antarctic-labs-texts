Critical temperature and thermodynamic properties of a homogeneous dilute weakly interacting Bose gas within the improved Hartree-Fock approximation at finite temperature
Nguyen Van Thu and Pham Duy Thanh

Abstract
By means of Cornwall–Jackiw–Tomboulis effective action approach we investigate a homogeneous dilute weakly interacting Bose gas at finite temperature in vicinity of critical region. A longstanding debate, the shift of critical temperature, is considered and obtained in the universal form Δ ​ T C / T C ( 0 ) = c . ( n 0 ​ a s 3 ) a \Delta T_{C}/T_{C}^{(0)}=c.(n_{0}a_{s}^{3})^{a} with constants c c and a a . The non-condensate fraction is contributed by quantum fluctuations as well as thermal exitations and can be expressed in sum of three terms. These terms correspond to the quantum fluctuations, thermal fluctuations and both. Indeed, the specific heat capacity and critical exponents are calculated and in excellent agreement with those in previous works and experimental data.



I
Introduction

It is well-known that a system of Bose gas undergoes a condensate phase transition when its temperature is lowed to the critical temperature
Bose 1924
;
Einstein 1924
. The result is that a number of atoms in the Bose gas will be condensed and formed a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC). The criterion for the BEC to be formed in macroscopic scale is
Pethick and Smith 2008
,

\displaystyle n_{0}\lambda_{B}^{3}\leq\zeta(3/2),

(1)

in which
n_{0}
is total particle density, which is defined as the number of particle in a unit of volume,
\zeta(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1/n^{x}
is zeta function. The de Broglie wave length associated with a particle with mass of
m
at temperature
T
reads

\displaystyle\lambda_{B}=\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}}{mk_{B}T}\right)^{1/2},

(2)

with
\hbar
and
k_{B}
being the reduced Planck and Boltzmann constants, respectively. In case of the ideal Bose gas, Eq. (
1
) gives the critical temperature

\displaystyle T_{C}^{(0)}=\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}}{mk_{B}}\left[\frac{n_{0}}{\zeta(3/2)}\right]^{2/3},

(3)

which is presented in many relevant textbooks
Huang 1987
;
Pethick and Smith 2008
.

For a weakly interacting Bose gas, the interatomic interaction is assumed to be pairwise and described by repulsive contact pseudo-potential (Lieb–Liniger model)
Lieb and Liniger 1963
;
Lieb 1963
. The strength of this interaction is determined by a coupling constant
g=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{s}/m
with
a_{s}
the scattering length of the
s
-wave. Owing to the repulsive interaction among bosonic atoms, the critical temperature is shifted in compared with that of the ideal Bose gas

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T_{C}}{T_{C}^{(0)}}=c\alpha_{s}^{1/3},

(4)

where
\alpha_{s}=n_{0}a_{s}^{3}
is called the gas parameter and
\Delta T_{C}\equiv T_{C}-T_{C}^{(0)}
. The condition of diluteness of the Bose gas is
\alpha_{s}\ll 1

Andersen 2004
, i.e. the
s
-wave scattering
a_{s}
length is very small in compared with the interparticle distance
n_{0}^{-1/3}
. Value and characteristics of the constant
c
have attracted the attention of many physicists to compute, such as
Toyoda 1982
;
Wilkens et al. 2000
;
Ledowski et al. 2004
;
Davis and Morgan 2003
;
Arnold and Moore 2001
;
Baym et al. 2000
;
de Souza Cruz et al. 2001
;
Stoof 1992
. However, some results showed negative
c
whereas others obtained the positive
c
. This issue still remains a matter of controversy.

Below the critical temperature, the lower the temperature is, the more condensate particles there are. For the ideal Bose gas, when temperature is smaller than the critical temperature
T_{C}^{(0)}
, the condensate density is
Pitaevskii and Stringari 2003
,

\displaystyle n=n_{0}\left[1-\left(\frac{T}{T_{C}^{(0)}}\right)^{3/2}\right].

(5)

For the dilute weakly interacting Bose gas, the non-condensate fraction has been calculated in several different approximations, for instance, Hartree-Fock, Hartree-Fock Bogoliubov, Popov approximations. A typical result is
Shi and Griffin 1998
,

\displaystyle\frac{n_{\rm ex}}{n_{0}}=\frac{8}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\alpha_{s}^{1/2}+\frac{m}{12\hbar^{2}cn_{0}}(k_{B}T)^{2},

(6)

where
c
is speed of sound in the Bose gas.

Theoretically, at zero temperature all of the atoms will stay in the ground state
Pitaevskii and Stringari 2003
. In this situation, the ground state is described by a wave function, which can be found by solving the Gross-Pitaevskii (GP) equation
Gross 1961
;
Pitaevskii 1961
. However, due to interaction-induced quantum fluctuations in the BEC, some particles with nonzero momentum reside in excited states instead of the ground state, even at zero absolute temperature
Pethick and Smith 2008
. These particles are pushed out of the condensate– the phenomenon of quantum depletion. The number of atoms in the remaining condensate fraction due to quantum depletion was first studied by N. N. Bogoliubov
Bogolyubov 1947
in 1947. Whose study was just limited to the order
1/2
in the gas parameter by using the second quantization formalism. The main idea is based on a quantum description, where the particle operators are transformed into quasi-particle operators, yielding an explicit diagonalization of the quantum Hamiltonian. In 1997, using the Bogoliubov theory and the semiclassical approach, the authors of Ref.
Dalfovo et al. 1997
investigated the quantum depletion in a Bose gas confined by a harmonic trap. In the case of a homogeneous Bose gas their result corresponded exactly with Bogoliubov’s result. Within the GP theory, the condensed fraction was reproduced by S. Stringari
Stringari 2018
. Using an effective theory called simplified approach, Carlen
et. al.

Carlen et al. 2021a
verified the Bogoliubov’s result for the condensed fraction of the BEC at low density.

Beyond these theorems, a powerful tool to investigate the Bose gas is Cornwall-Jackiw-Tomboulis (CJT) effective action approach
Cornwall et al. 1974
. Recently, this method has been invoked to consider effect from the quantum fluctuations in the dilute BEC at zero temperature to non-condensate fraction
Van Thu and Berx 2022
;
Thu 2023
as well as the Casimir effect in a series of our previous papers, such as
Thu and Song 2020
;
Van Thu and Theu 2017
;
Van Thu 2022
;
Van Thu 2021
. At finite temperature, CJT effective action approach was employed to study the thermodynamics of a weakly interacting Bose gas
Haugset et al. 1998
. However, calculations in this work were only dealt in the one-loop approximation. As a consequence, an important quantity-the critical temperature-of the weakly interacting Bose gas was pointed out the same as the one associated with the non-interacting Bose gas. The main purpose of the present paper is to investigate the critical temperature and thermodynamic properties of a dilute homogeneous weakly interacting Bose gas in vicinity of critical point within IHF approximation, in which the two-loop diagrams are taken into account.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section
II
we calculate the gap and Schwinger-Dyson (SD) equations for a single weakly interacting Bose gas in the improved Hartree-Fock (IHF) approximation by first recapitulating the regular Hartree-Fock (HF) method and then calculating these expressions for the CJT effective potential with symmetry-restoring terms. The critical temperature and other thermodynamic quantities are investigated in Section
III
. Finally, we present the conclusions and a future outlook in Section
IV
.

II
The equations of state in the IHF approximation

In this Section, we will establish the equations of state for a Bose gas, which consists of the gap and Schwinger-Dyson (SD) equations. To do so, the first our task is to derive the CJT effective potential
Cornwall et al. 1974
in the IHF approximation. We set the stage for our calculations by starting with a dilute Bose gas described by the following Lagrangian density without any external field
Pethick and Smith 2008
,

{\cal L}=\psi^{*}\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\right)\psi-\mu\left|\psi\right|^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\left|\psi\right|^{4},

(7)

wherein
\mu
is the chemical potential. The field operator
\psi(\vec{r},t)
depends on both the coordinate
\vec{r}
and time
t
. According to Ref.
Pethick and Smith 2008
, the interaction potential between the atoms can be chosen as the hard-sphere model. In this model, the strength of the interatomic interaction is determined by the coupling constant
g=4\pi\hbar^{2}a_{s}/m
, which is expressed in terms of the
s
-wave scattering length
a_{s}
by making use of the Born approximation. The thermodynamic stability requires that
g>0
, i.e., the boson interactions are repulsive with positive
s
-wave scattering length. Indeed, from now on, we consider the system in the grand canonical ensemble, i.e the total particle density is fixed.

In tree-approximation, the expectation value
\psi_{0}
of the field operator is independent of both time and coordinate, therefore the GP potential is then taken from (
7
)

V_{GP}=-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}.

(8)

Furthermore, the non-macroscopic part of the condensate moves as a whole so that the expectation value
\psi_{0}
is real and plays the role of the order parameter. The square of the order parameter
\psi_{0}^{2}
is defined as the condensate density. Minimizing the potential (
8
) with respect to the order parameter, one arrives at the gap equation

\psi_{0}(-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2})=0,

(9)

and hence, in the broken phase the non-trivial solution is read

\psi_{0}^{2}=\frac{\mu}{g}.

(10)

In order to proceed within the framework of the HF approximation, the complex field operator
\psi
should first be decomposed in terms of the order parameter
\psi_{0}
and two real fields
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, which are associated with quantum fluctuations of the field
Andersen 2004
, i.e.,

\psi\rightarrow\psi_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\psi_{1}+i\psi_{2}).

(11)

Plugging equation (
11
) into (
7
), the Lagrangian density is readily devided into three parts, namely

- The free Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{0}
, which gives us the free propagator.

- The tree Lagrangian density is associated with the tree-approximation. This part allows us to find the propagator (or Green’s function) in the tree-approximation

D_{0}(k)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E_{\text{(tree)}}^{2}(k)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}&\omega_{n}\\
-\omega_{n}&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}\\
\end{array}\right),

(12)

where
\omega_{n}
is the
n
th Matsubara frequency for bosons. The latter is defined as
\omega_{n}=2\pi nk_{B}T
where
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The
E_{\rm{(tree)}}(k)
is the energy spectrum of the elementary excitation, which is determined by examining poles of the Green’s function
Andersen 2004
;
Orland 1998
. Together with the gap equation (
10
), the result is

E_{\rm{(tree)}}(k)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2g\psi_{0}^{2}\right)}.

(13)

The spectrum Eq. (
13
) was first achieved by Bogoliubov
Bogolyubov 1947
. For small momenta, this equation is gapless, which represents the phonon excitations. This is due on the Goldstone theorem
Goldstone 1961
, which requires that the dispersion relation is linear in the wave vactor whenever a continuous symmetry
U(1)
is spontaneously broken
Anderson 2018
. In the physics of BEC, this theorem is referred to as the Hugenholtz-Pines theorem
Hugenholtz and Pines 1959
at zero temperature and a more general proof for all value of temperature was given by Hohenberg and Martin
Hohenberg and Martin 1965
. More discussion about the gapless excitation spectrum for the BEC at finite temperature were mentioned in Refs.
Hutchinson et al. 1998
;
Griffin 1996
.

- The last one is the interaction Lagrangian density, which describes the interaction between the two real fields of fluctuations. In the HF approximation, keeping up to the cubic and quartic terms this Lagrangian density is obtained

{\cal L}_{\rm int}=\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}\psi_{1}(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})+\frac{g}{8}(\psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})^{2}.

(14)

To continue our discussion, we introduce the CJT effective potential in the HF approximation that can be constructed from the interaction Lagrangian density (
14
) in the manner that was pointed out in
Thu and Song 2020
,

\begin{split}V_{T}^{\text{(CJT)}}=&-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}\mbox{tr}\left[\ln G^{-1}(k)+D_{0}^{-1}(k)G(k)-{1\!\!1}\right]\\
&+\frac{3g}{8}(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2})+\frac{g}{4}P_{11}P_{22}\,,\end{split}

(15)

for which the functions
P_{11}
and
P_{22}
are

P_{11}=\int_{T}G_{11}(k)

(16a)

P_{22}=\int_{T}G_{22}(k)

(16b)

The Matsubara integrals in these expressions are defined as follows

\int_{T}f(k)=k_{B}T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}f(\omega_{n},\vec{k})\,.

(17)

Here
G(k)
is the propagator or Green’s function in the HF approximation, which can be obtained by minimizing the CJT effective potential (
15
) with respect to the elements of the propagator. Performing these calculations result in the following expression for the inverse propagator

G^{-1}(k)=D_{0}^{-1}(k)+\Pi,

(18)

in which

\Pi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Pi_{1}&0\\
0&\Pi_{2}\\
\end{array}\right),

(19)

with the matrix entries
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{1}
being the self-energies that can readily be constructed from (
16a
) and (
16b
), i.e.,

\Pi_{1}=\frac{3g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{g}{2}P_{22}

(20a)

\Pi_{2}=\frac{g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{3g}{2}P_{22}\,.

(20b)

The gap equation in the HF approximation can now be found by minimizing the CJT effective potential (
15
) with respect to the order parameter
\psi_{0}
, i.e.,

-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Pi_{1}=0.

(21)

Combining equations (
18
)-(
21
), one has the propagator in the HF approximation

G(k)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E_{\text{(HF)}}^{2}(k)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Pi_{2}&-\omega_{n}\\
\omega_{n}&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Pi_{1}\\
\end{array}\right),

(22)

and consequently the dispersion relation in this approximation is

E_{\text{(HF)}}(k)=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Pi_{1}\right)\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Pi_{2}\right)}.

(23)

Equations (
21
) and (
23
) show a non-gapless spectrum, which means that the Goldstone theorem is violated in the case of spontaneously broken symmetry within the HF approximation. We now recall a special emphasis on enforcing the consequences of the Goldstone theorem. To do so, we employ the method developed in
Ivanov et al. 2005
. In this way, a phenomenological symmetry-restoring correction term
\Delta V
need to be added into CJT effective potential, which has to contemporaneously satisfy three conditions:

(i) it restores the Goldstone boson in the condensed phase;

(ii) it does not change the HF equations for the mean field and;

(iii) the results in the phase of restored symmetry are not changed.

It is not difficult to readily check that this term has to be in from
Van Thu and Berx 2022
,

\Delta V=-\frac{g}{4}(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2})+\frac{g}{2}P_{11}P_{22},

(24)

Let the propagator in the IHF approximation be denoted as
D_{\text{(IHF)}}(k)
, the CJT effective potential (
15
) now becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{T}^{\rm{(CJT)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}\mbox{tr}\left[\ln D^{-1}_{\rm{(IHF)}}(k)+D_{0}^{-1}(k)D_{\rm{(IHF)}}(k)-{1\!\!1}\right]

(25)

\displaystyle+\frac{g}{8}(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2})+\frac{3g}{4}P_{11}P_{22}.

From the CJT effective potential in the IHF approximation (
25
), one arrives at the gap equation

-\mu+g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{1}=0,

(26)

and the SD equation

M=-\mu+3g\psi_{0}^{2}+\Sigma_{2},

(27)

in which the self-energies
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}

\Sigma_{1}=\frac{3g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{g}{2}P_{22}

(28a)

\Sigma_{2}=\frac{g}{2}P_{11}+\frac{3g}{2}P_{22}\,.

(28b)

Combining equations (
25
)-(
28b
), one can once again calculate the propagator, now in the IHF approximation, i.e.,

D_{\text{(IHF)}}(k)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E_{\text{(IHF)}}^{2}(k)}\left(\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}&\omega_{n}\\
-\omega_{n}&\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+M\\
\end{array}\right).

(29)

Hence, the resulting dispersion relation is

E_{\rm{(IHF)}}(k)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+M\right)}.

(30)

Clearly, the Goldstone boson is restored in this approximation. The gap and SD equations (
26
) and (
27
), together with the momentum integrals (
32
) form the equations of state, which govern the variation of all quantities of the system.

To do further, we first deal with the momentum integrals in the gap and SD equations. Without loss of generality, henceforth we use the abbreviation
E(k)
replacing for
E_{\rm(IHF)}(k)
. In the IHF approximation, the momentum integrals are obtained from equations (
16b
) after replacing

by

. Using the following formula

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{n}^{2}+E^{2}(k)}=\frac{1}{2k_{B}TE(k)}\left[1+\frac{2}{e^{E(k)/k_{B}T}-1}\right],

(31)

one has

\displaystyle P_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E(k)}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{E(k)}\frac{1}{e^{E(k)/k_{B}T}-1}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m},

(32a)

\displaystyle P_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E(k)}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+M\right)+\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{E(k)}\frac{1}{e^{E(k)/k_{B}T}-1}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+M\right).

(32b)

The first terms in right-hand side of (

) and (

) do not explicitly depend on the temperature. These integrals are ultraviolet divergent. This divergence is avoidable by means of the dimensional regularization

and thus, the integrals can be computed. The integral
I_{m,n}
is

\displaystyle I_{m,n}({\cal M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}\kappa}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\kappa^{2m-n}}{(\kappa^{2}+{\cal M}^{2})^{n/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\Lambda^{2\epsilon}{\cal M}^{d+2(m-n)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-n}{2}+m\right)\Gamma\left(n-m-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)},

where

\displaystyle P_{110}

\displaystyle P_{220}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E(k)}\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+M\right)=-\frac{(2m)^{3/2}M^{3/2}}{12\pi^{2}\hbar^{3}}.

III
The critical temperature and thermodynamic properties of a homogeneous dilute Bose gas

Let us now investigate the critical temperature and thermodynamic properties of the dilute weakly interacting Bose gas in the IHF approximation. To this end, we first consider the pressure. The pressure is defined as the negative of the CJT effective potential (
25
) at the minimum, i.e. satisfying both the gap and SD equations

{\cal P}=-\widetilde{V}_{T}\bigg|_{\rm{minimum}}\equiv-\widetilde{{\cal V}}_{T}^{\rm{(CJT)}}.

(35)

Now, substituting equations (
26
) and (
27
) into (
25
), one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal V}}_{T}^{\rm{(CJT)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{g}{2}\psi_{0}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}\mbox{tr}\ln D^{-1}_{\rm{(IHF)}}(k)

(36)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(3g\psi_{0}^{2}-\mu-M)P_{11}+\frac{1}{2}(g\psi_{0}^{2}-\mu)P_{22}

\displaystyle+\frac{g}{8}(P_{11}^{2}+P_{22}^{2})+\frac{3g}{4}P_{11}P_{22}.

The chemical potential is defined as first derivative of the pressure with respect to the condensate density

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{\partial\cal P}{\partial n}.

(37)

Plugging (
36
) into (
37
) one can find the chemical potential beyond the mean-field theory

\mu=gn_{0}+gP_{11}.

(38)

Combining (

\displaystyle-1+x+\frac{1}{2n_{0}}(P_{11}+P_{22})=0,

\displaystyle{\cal M}=-1+3x-\frac{1}{2n_{0}}(P_{11}-3P_{22}),

In sum, at high temperature, the momentum integrals (

\displaystyle x

Finally, it is of interest to investigate the critical exponents of the dilute weakly interacting Bose gas in our model. To do this, we define the reduced temperature as

\displaystyle t=\frac{\left|T-T_{C}\right|}{T_{C}}.

In vicinity of the critical temperature, the reduced temperature is very small, i.e.

IV
Conclusion and outlook

Beyond the standard Bolgoliubov theory, we have explored the CJT effective action approach to investigate the thermodynamic properties at finite temperature of homogeneous dilute interacting Bose gas. After deriving the CJT effective potential in the IHF approximation, where the Goldstone theorem is satisfied, the equations of state are obtained. Based on these equations, the important properties of the dilute Bose gas, namely, the critical temperature, non-condensate fraction, specific heat capacity, critical exponents have been studied. Two main results are in order:

- The critical temperature is found in two-terms: one corresponds to that of the ideal Bose gas and the other depends on the strength of atomic interaction. The presence of the atomic interaction makes a shift in the critical temperature in compared with the one of the ideal Bose gas. This shift can be expressed in the universal form (
4
). It is worthy of interest to write (
4
) in a more general form

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T_{C}}{T_{C}^{(0)}}=c.\alpha_{s}^{a}.

(82)

The two constants
c
and
a
determine the behaviors of the shift of critical temperature of Bose gas at low density. One of the outstanding achievements is that most of works found
a=1/3
as listed in Table
1
, some other values were attained, for example,
a=1/2
in Ref.
Huang 1964
,
a=0.34\pm 0.03
in Ref.
Grüter et al. 1997
. It is worth to know that the CJT effective action approach was employed by Haugset
et al.
to calculate the critical temperature of the dilute weakly interacting Bose gas in one-loop approximation
Haugset et al. 1998
. Their result showed that the critical temperature is the same as the one of the ideal Bose gas. This implies that the contribution from the two-loop diagrams are significantly important. Taking into account the higher-order terms of the gas parameter, our result can be achieved

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T_{C}}{T_{C}^{(0)}}=\left(\frac{8}{9\sqrt{\pi}}+\frac{272}{81\pi}\alpha_{s}^{1/2}\right)\alpha_{s}^{1/2}.

(83)

The condition of diluteness shows that the contribution from the higher-order terms of the gas parameter is negligible on the shift of the critical temperature.

- The non-condensate fraction can be separated into three terms: first one is pure quantum fluctuations, second one described the thermal fluctuations and the last one includes both the quantum and thermal fluctuations. An important thing should be noted is the difference between our result (
50
) and (
6
). In Eq. (
50
), the non-condensate fraction depends on temperature in half-integer power law instead of the integer one in Eq. (
6
), which was first exhibited by Glassgold
et al.

Glassgold et al. 1960
. This half-integer power law of temperature-dependence of the condensate fraction was also found in Ref.
Capogrosso-Sansone et al. 2010
within Beliaev’s diagrammatic technique and recently confirmed again in Ref.
Al-Sugheir et al. 2023
by using static fluctuation approximation. In addition, our result contains the interferential term whereas this term is absence in Eq. (
6
).

- The specific heat capacity is investigated in both regions of temperature. Its step of jump at the critical temperature approximates

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta C_{V}}{(n_{0}k_{B})}\bigg|_{T=T_{C}}\approx 6\zeta(3/2)^{2/3}\alpha_{s}^{1/3}.

(84)

This can be reduced to the well-known result for the ideal Bose gas if the gas parameter vanishes.

- Two critical exponents associated with the order-parameter and specific heat capacity are found via the analytical calculations. Based on that, the remaining critical exponents are also calculated by using four equalities among them.

We would insist again that our system under consideration is investigated in the grand canonical ensemble thus the total particle density is fixed. This implies that our system is connected to a ”reservoirs” of particles while it is confined by a potential, even in the state-of-the-art relevant experiments. Consequently, the number of particles is finite but the grand canonical ensemble is still used
Politzer 1996
.

It is also very interesting to explore these calculations for Bose-Einstein condensate mixtures. The interspecies interaction is expected to produce some novel results, especially to compare with relevant results in
Shi et al. 2000
. Furthermore, this procedure can also be employed to investigate the condensed fraction of a Bose gas confined between two parallel plates and the resulting critical temperature and non-condensate fraction
Thu 2023
.

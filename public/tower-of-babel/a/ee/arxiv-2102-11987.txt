Nonconvex integro-differential sweeping process with applications.
Abderrahim Bouach, Tahar Haddad, Lionel Thibault

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze and discuss the well-posedness of a new variant of the so-called sweeping process, introduced by J.J. Moreau in the early 70’s [ 18 ] with motivation in plasticity theory. In this variant, the normal cone to the (mildly non-convex) prox-regular moving set C ⁡ ( t ) C(t) , supposed to have an absolutely continuous variation, is perturbed by a sum of a Carathéodory mapping and an integral forcing term. The integrand of the forcing term depends on two time-variables, that is, we study a general integro-differential sweeping process of Volterra type. By setting up an appropriate semi-discretization method combined with a new Gronwall-like inequality (differential inequality), we show that the integro-differential sweeping process has one and only one absolutely continuous solution. We also establish the continuity of the solution with respect to the initial value. The results of the paper are applied to the study of nonlinear integro-differential complementarity systems which are combination of Volterra integro-differential equations with nonlinear complementarity constraints. Another application is concerned with non-regular electrical circuits containing time-varying capacitors and nonsmooth electronic device like diodes. Both applications represent an additional novelty of our paper.



Keywords
Moreau’s sweeping process, Volterra integro-differential equation, Differential complementarity systems, Gronwall’s inequality, Prox-regular sets, Differential inclusions.

In the seventies, sweeping processes are introduced and deeply studied by J. J. Moreau

[
18

19

\begin{cases}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))\,\,\,\quad a.e.\,\,\,t\in[T_{0},T]&\\

\begin{cases}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))+f(t,x(t))\,\,\,\quad a.e.\,\,\,t\in[T_{0},T]&\\
x(T_{0})=x_{0}\in C(T_{0})\end{cases}

Actually, diverse approaches for existence of solutions of (

[

), regularization procedure (see, e.g.,
[

[

For the first and second methods, existence and uniqueness of solutions follow, to some extent, from the classical Gronwall inequality and the basic relation

\frac{d}{dt}\|x(t)\|^{2}=2\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)\rangle,

(3)

(whenever meaningful), applied to two solutions or suitable approximate solutions

(P_{0,f_{2}})\begin{cases}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))+\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}f_{2}(s,x(s))ds\,\,\,\quad\text{a.e.}\,\,\,t\in[0,T]&\\

It is also worth mentioning that Colombo and Kozaily say in their

[

of course, existence and uniqueness to

, the integro-differential sweeping process

and so

\overbrace{\begin{pmatrix}-\dot{x}(t)\\

So, in

Now, if the integral involving

To the best of our knowledge, for the problem under consideration in the case of the

In the present paper, we obtain results on the existence and uniqueness of a
solution to the Volterra sweeping process

in

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\dot{x}_{n}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x_{n}(t))+f_{1}(t,x_{n}(t_{k}))+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}f_{2}(t,s,x_{n}(t_{j}))\,ds\\
+\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{k}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x_{n}(t_{k}))\,ds\;\;\text{ a.e. }t\in\left[t_{k},t_{k+1}\right],\\
{x}_{n}(T_{0})=x_{0}\in C(T_{0}),\end{array}\right.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
, we recall some preliminary results that we use throughout. In Section
3
, we prove a new Gronwall-like inequality ( differential inequality). Then, in Section
4
, we present our main existence,
uniqueness, and stability result. In Section

w^{1-\alpha}(t)\leq w^{1-\alpha}(T_{0})\exp(\int_{T_{0}}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau)+\int_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp(\int_{s}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau)b(s)ds.

t\in[T_{0},T]
, one has

\displaystyle\rho(t)\leq\rho(T_{0})\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\,ds,

where
b(t):=\max\{b_{1}(t),b_{2}(t)\}
, a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]
.

Proof
. Put
b(t)=\max\{b_{1}(t),b_{2}(t)\}
, a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]
. Setting
z(t)=\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\rho(s)\,ds\Rightarrow\dot{z}(t)=\rho(t)
,
\ddot{z}(t)=\dot{\rho}(t)
. Then from (
8
)
we see that

\displaystyle\ddot{z}(t)

\displaystyle\leq a(t)+b_{1}(t)\dot{z}(t)+b_{2}(t)z(t)\leq a(t)+b(t)w(t),

where
w(t)=\dot{z}(t)+z(t)
, for all
t\in[T_{0},T]
. Hence, for a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]

\dot{w}(t)=\ddot{z}(t)+\dot{z}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{w}(t)\leq a(t)+(b(t)+1)w(t).

Applying the Gronwall Lemma
3.1
with
w
, one obtains for all
t\in[T_{0},T]

w(t)\leq w(T_{0})\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\,ds,

which gives

\displaystyle\rho(t)\leq\dot{z}(t)+z(t)=w(t)\leq\rho(T_{0})\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}a(s)\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\,ds.

We establish now the following new Gronwall-like lemma.

Lemma 3.3
(Gronwall-like differential inequality)

Let
\rho:[T_{0},T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative absolutely continuous function and let
K_{1},K_{2},\varepsilon:[T_{0},T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be non-negative Lebesgue integrable functions. Suppose for some
\epsilon>0

\dot{\rho}(t)\leq\varepsilon(t)+\epsilon+K_{1}(t)\rho(t)+K_{2}(t)\sqrt{\rho(t)}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(s)}\,ds,\hskip 8.5359pta.e.\,\,t\in[T_{0},T].

(9)

Then for all
t\in[T_{0},T]
, one has

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})+\epsilon}\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds\bigg)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle+2\bigg(\sqrt{\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(s)\,ds+\epsilon}-\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle+2\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\sqrt{\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{s}\varepsilon(\tau)\,d{\tau}+\epsilon}\,\,ds,

where
K(t):=\max\bigg\{\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2},\dfrac{K_{2}(t)}{2}\bigg\}
, a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]
.

Proof
. Set
\lambda(t)=\sqrt{\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(s)\,ds+\epsilon}
and
z_{\varepsilon}(t)=\sqrt{\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t)}
for all
t\in[T_{0},T]
.

From (
9
) we have for a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]

\dot{\rho}(t)\leq\varepsilon(t)+\epsilon+K_{1}(t)(\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t))+K_{2}(t)\sqrt{\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t)}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(s)+\lambda^{2}(s)}\,ds

(10)

and

\dot{z}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\dfrac{\dot{\rho}(t)+2\dot{\lambda}(t)\lambda(t)}{2\sqrt{\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t)}}=\dfrac{\dot{\rho}(t)+\varepsilon(t)}{2z_{\varepsilon}(t)},\quad\text{or equivalently}\;\dot{\rho}(t)=2z_{\varepsilon}(t)\dot{z}_{\varepsilon}(t)-\varepsilon(t),

hence from (
10
)

2z_{\varepsilon}(t)\dot{z}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq 2\varepsilon(t)+\epsilon+K_{1}(t)z_{\varepsilon}(t)^{2}+K_{2}(t)z_{\varepsilon}(t)\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}z_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon(t)}{z_{\varepsilon}(t)}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2z_{\varepsilon}(t)}+\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2}z_{\varepsilon}(t)+\dfrac{K_{2}(t)}{2}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}z_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\leq 2\dot{\lambda}(t)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(K_{1}(t)z_{\varepsilon}(t)+K_{2}(t)\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}z_{\varepsilon}(s)\,ds\right).

Since

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\sqrt{\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(s)\,ds+\epsilon}\leq\sqrt{\rho(t)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(s)\,ds+\epsilon}=\sqrt{\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t)}=z_{\varepsilon}(t),

then

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{z_{\varepsilon}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda(t)},\quad\text{or equivalently}\;\dfrac{\varepsilon(t)}{z_{\varepsilon}(t)}\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon(t)}{\lambda(t)}.

Also we have
\dot{\lambda}(t)=\dfrac{\varepsilon(t)}{2\lambda(t)}
. Then
\dfrac{\varepsilon(t)}{z_{\varepsilon}(t)}\leq 2\dot{\lambda}(t)
, and

\sqrt{\epsilon}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(s)\,ds}=\lambda(t)\leq z_{\varepsilon}(t)
, hence
\dfrac{\epsilon}{2z_{\varepsilon}(t)}\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}
. Letting
K(t):=\max\bigg\{\dfrac{K_{1}(t)}{2},\dfrac{K_{2}(t)}{2}\bigg\}
and applying
the Gronwall Lemma
3.2
with
z_{\varepsilon}
, one obtains for all
t\in[T_{0},T]

\displaystyle z_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\leq z_{\varepsilon}(T_{0})\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)(2\dot{\lambda}(s)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2})\,ds

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})+\epsilon}\exp(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)(2\dot{\lambda}(s)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2})\,ds

or equivalently

\displaystyle z_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})+\epsilon}\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds\big)+2\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\dot{\lambda}(s)\,ds+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\,ds.

On the other hand, from integration by parts, we note that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\dot{\lambda}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=[\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\lambda(s)]_{T_{0}}^{t}+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\lambda(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\lambda(t)-\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\sqrt{\epsilon}+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\lambda(s)\,ds,

which combined with what precedes gives

\displaystyle z_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})+\epsilon}\,\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds\big)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\,ds

\displaystyle+2\lambda(t)-2\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\sqrt{\epsilon}+2\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\lambda(s)\,ds.

Consequently, observing that
\sqrt{\rho(t)}\leq\sqrt{\rho(t)+\lambda^{2}(t)}=z_{\varepsilon}(t)
we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\rho(T_{0})+\epsilon}\,\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\,ds\big)+\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{2}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\,ds+2\lambda(t)

\displaystyle-2\exp\big(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\sqrt{\epsilon}+2\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}(K(s)+1)\exp\big(\int\limits_{s}^{t}(K(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\big)\lambda(s)\,ds,

which completes the proof of the lemma .

4
Main results

In this section, we give and prove our main results in the study of the integro-differential sweeping process
(P_{f_{1},f_{2}})
. They concern the existence,
uniqueness, and continuous dependence of the solution with respect to the initial data.
We state first in the next proposition a result which will be utilized in our development. Clearly, when the sets
C(t)
are bounded, the hypothesis
(\mathcal{H}_{1})
is ensured by the usual Hausdorff variation hypothesis

\mathrm{haus}\big(C(s),C(t)\big)\leq\lvert\upsilon(s)-\upsilon(t)\rvert,\quad\forall\;t,s\in[T_{0},T],

which according to the equality

\mathrm{haus}(S,S^{\prime})=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{y\in H}|d_{S}(y)-d_{S^{\prime}}(y)|

for bounded sets
S,S^{\prime}
, amounts to requiring for the bounded sets
C(s),C(t)
that

\lvert d_{C(s)}(y)-d_{C(t)}(y)\rvert\leq\lvert\upsilon(s)-\upsilon(t)\rvert,\quad\forall\;t,s\in[T_{0},T],\,\,\forall y\in\,H.

(11)

The result is proved in
[
7
,
13
]
under the hypothesis (
11
) but the proof in

[
7
,
13
]
is valid with the hypothesis
(\mathcal{H}_{1})
.
One of the advantages of
(\mathcal{H}_{1})
is that the sets
C(t)
there need not be bounded.

Proposition 4.1

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space, suppose that
C(\cdot)
satisfies
\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}_{1}\mathcal{)}
. Let
h:\left[T_{0},T\right]\longrightarrow H
be a single-valued mapping in
L^{1}(\left[T_{0},T\right],H)
. Then for any
x_{0}\in C(T_{0})
there exists a unique absolutely continuous solution
x(\cdot)
for the following differential inclusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))+h(t)\text{ a.e \ }t\in\left[T_{0},T\right],\\
x(T_{0})=x_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover
x(\cdot)
satisfies the following inequality

\left\|\dot{x}(t)+h(t)\right\|\leq\left\|h(t)\right\|+|\dot{\upsilon}(t)|\;\;\text{a.e. }t\in\left[T_{0},T\right].

(12)

Theorem 4.2

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and assume that
\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}_{1}\mathcal{)}
,
\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}_{2}\mathcal{)}
and
\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}_{3}\mathcal{)}
are satisfied. Then for any initial point
x_{0}\in H,
with
x_{0}\in C(T_{0})
there exists a unique absolutely continuous solution
x:[T_{0},T]\longrightarrow H
of the differential inclusion
(P_{f_{1},f_{2}})
. This solution satisfies:

1.

For   a.e.
t\in[T_{0},T]

\lVert\dot{x}(t)+f_{1}(t,x(t))+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\,ds\rVert\leq\lvert\dot{\upsilon}(t)\rvert+\lVert f_{1}(t,x(t))\rVert+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\lVert f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\rVert\,ds.

(13)

2.

If
\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}\bigg[\beta_{1}(\tau)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{\tau}\beta_{2}(\tau,s)\,ds\bigg]d\tau<\dfrac{1}{4}
, one has

\left\|f_{1}(t,x(t))\right\|\leq(1+M)\beta_{1}(t),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,t\in[T_{0},T],

(14)

\left\|f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\right\|\leq(1+M)\beta_{2}(t,s),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,(t,s)\in Q_{\Delta},

(15)

and for almost all
t\in[T_{0},T]

\left\|\dot{x}(t)+f_{1}(t,x(t))+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\,ds\right\|\leq(1+M)\bigg(\beta_{1}(t)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\beta_{2}(t,s)\,ds\bigg)+|\dot{\upsilon}(t)|,

(16)

where
M:=2\bigg(\left\|x_{0}\right\|+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}|\dot{\upsilon}(\tau)|\,d\tau+\dfrac{1}{2}\bigg)
.

3.

Assume the following strengthened form of
assumption
\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}_{3,1}\mathcal{)}
on the function
f_{2}
holds:

\mathcal{(}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{3,1}\mathcal{)}
: there exist non-negative functions
\alpha(\cdot)\in L^{1}([T_{0},T],\mathbb{R})
and

g(\cdot)\in L^{1}(P_{\Delta},\mathbb{R})
such that

\lVert f_{2}(t,s,x)\rVert\leq g(t,s)+\alpha(t)\lVert x\rVert,\,\,\,\text{for any}\,\,(t,s)\in Q_{\Delta}\,\,\,\text{and any}\,\,\,x\in\underset{t\in[T_{0},T]}{\bigcup}C(t).

Then we have

\displaystyle\lVert f_{1}(t,x(t))\rVert\leq(1+\widetilde{M})\beta_{1}(t),\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,t\in[T_{0},T],

(17)

\displaystyle\lVert f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\rVert\leq g(t,s)+\alpha(t)\widetilde{M},\,\,\,\text{a.e.}\,\,\,(t,s)\in Q_{\Delta},

(18)

and for almost all
t\in[T_{0},T]

\displaystyle\lVert\dot{x}(t)+f_{1}(t,x(t))+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x(s))\,ds\rVert\leq\rvert\dot{\upsilon}(t)\rvert+(1+\widetilde{M})\beta_{1}(t)+\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}g(t,s)\,ds+T\alpha(t)\widetilde{M},

(19)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}:=\lVert x_{0}\rVert\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)+\exp\bigg(\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}(b(\tau)+1)\,d\tau\bigg)\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}\bigg(\rvert\dot{\upsilon}(s)\rvert+2\beta_{1}(s)+2\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}g(s,\tau)\,d\tau\bigg)\,ds,

\text{and}\,\,\,b(t):=2\max\{\beta_{1}(t),\alpha(t)\}\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,t\in[T_{0},T].

Proof
. The proof of existence of solution is divided in several steps.

Step 1.

Discretization of the interval
I=\left[T_{0},T\right].

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, divide the interval
I
into
n
intervals of
length
h=\frac{T-T_{0}}{n}
and define, for all
i\in\left\{0,\cdots,n-1\right\}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}t_{i+1}^{n}:=t_{i}^{n}+h=T_{0}+ih,\\
I_{i}^{n}:=\left[t_{i}^{n},t_{i+1}^{n}\right],\end{array}\right.

(20)

so that

T_{0}=t_{0}^{n}<t_{1}^{n}<\cdots<t_{i}^{n}<t_{i+1}^{n}<\cdots<t_{n}^{n}=T.

(21)

Step 2. Construction of the sequence
x_{n}(\cdot)
.

We construct a sequence of mappings
(x_{n}(\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{C}(I,H)
which converges uniformly to a solution
x(\cdot)
of
(P)
.

Our method consists in establishing a sequence of discrete solutions
(x_{k}^{n}(\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in each interval
I_{k}^{n}:=\left[t_{k}^{n},t_{k+1}^{n}\right](0\leq k\leq n-1
) by using
Proposition
4.1
. Indeed, we proceed as follows.

Given the following problem

(P_{0}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))+f_{1}(t,x_{0})+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x_{0})\,ds\;\;\text{ a.e. \ }t\in\left[T_{0},t_{1}^{n}\right],\\
x(T_{0})=x_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(22)

Then
(P_{0})
is a perturbed sweeping process with the perturbation depending only on time.

Let
h_{0}:[T_{0},t^{n}_{1}]\to H
be defined by
h_{0}(t):=f_{1}(t,x_{0})+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x_{0})\,ds
for all

. We see by integrable linear growth condition that

\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}\|h_{0}(t)\|dt\leq(1+\|x_{0}\|)\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}\beta_{1}(t)dt+(1+\|x_{0}\|)\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{T}\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t}\beta_{2}(t,s)ds\,dt,

\text{ a.e. }t\in\left[T_{0},t_{1}^{n}\right].

(P_{1}):\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\dot{x}(t)\in N_{C(t)}(x(t))+f_{1}(t,x_{0}^{n}(t_{1}^{n}))+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t^{n}_{1}}f_{2}(t,s,x_{0})\,ds\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{1}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{0}(t^{n}_{1}))\,ds\;\text{ a.e. }t\in\left[t_{1}^{n},t_{2}^{n}\right],\\

that the mapping

has a unique absolutely continuous solution denoted by

x_{1}^{n}(\cdot):\left[t_{1}^{n},t_{2}^{n}\right]\longrightarrow H,

(26)

and this solution satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\dot{x}_{1}^{n}(t)+f_{1}(t,x_{0}^{n}(t_{1}^{n}))+\displaystyle\int\limits_{T_{0}}^{t^{n}_{1}}f_{2}(t,s,x_{0})\,ds+\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{1}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{0}(t^{n}_{1}))\,ds\right\|

(27)

Successively, for each

(29)

where for

.

\displaystyle\left\|\dot{x}_{k}^{n}(t)+f_{1}(t,x_{k-1}^{n}(t_{k}^{n}))+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{j}}^{t^{n}_{j+1}}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{j-1}(t^{n}_{j}))\,ds+\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k}))\,ds,\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f_{1}(t,x_{k-1}^{n}(t_{k}^{n}))+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k-1}\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{j}}^{t^{n}_{j+1}}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{j-1}(t^{n}_{j}))\,ds+\displaystyle\int\limits_{t^{n}_{k}}^{t}f_{2}(t,s,x^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k}))\,ds,\right\|+|\dot{\upsilon}(t)|,

(30)

\text{ a.e. }t\in\left[t_{k}^{n},t_{k+1}^{n}\right].

Defining for each

by

for all

.
Clearly, by integrable linear growth conditions we have

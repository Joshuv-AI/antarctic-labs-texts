Positive ideals of multilinear operators
Athmane Ferradi, Abdelaziz Belaada and Khalil Saadi

Abstract
. We introduce and explore the concept of positive ideals for both linear and multilinear operators between Banach lattices. This paper delineates the fundamental principles of these new classes and provides techniques for constructing positive multi-ideals from given positive ideals. Furthermore, we present an example of a positive multi-ideal by introducing a new class, referred to as positive ( p 1 , … , p m , r ) (p_{1},...,p_{m};r) -dominated multilinear operators. We establish a natural analogue of the Pietsch domination theorem and Kwapień’s factorization theorem within this class.

2.
Positive operator ideals

Several classes of positive operators, including positive
p
-summing, Cohen
positive strong
p
-summing, and positive
(p,q)
-dominated linear
operators, have been introduced and studied. However, these classes are not
considered as ideal operators. Therefore, in this section, we attempt to
introduce the concept of positive operator ideals and propose abstract
methods for generating positive ideals of multilinear operators. In the
context of this definition, we use positive linear operators to determine
the ideal property.

2.1.
Positive operator ideal

A positive left ideal (or positive left ideal of linear operators), denoted
by
\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+},
is a subclass of all continuous linear operators
from a Banach space into a Banach lattice such that for every Banach space
X
and Banach lattice
E,
the components

\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;E\right):=\mathcal{L}\left(X;E\right)\cap\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}

satisfy:

(i)

\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;E\right)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{L}\left(X;E\right)
containing the linear mappings of finite rank.

(ii)
The positive ideal property: If
T\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;E\right),u\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y;X\right)
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(E;F\right)
, then
v\circ T\circ u
is in
\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(Y;F\right)
.

If
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}:\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies:

a)
\left(\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;E\right),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a Banach (quasi-Banach) space for all
Banach space
X
and Banach lattice
E
.

b) The linear form
T:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
given by
T\left(\lambda\right)=\lambda
satisfies
\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1
,

c)
T\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;E\right),u\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y;X\right)
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(E;F\right)
then

\left\|v\circ T\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\leq\|v\|\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u\right\|.

The class
\left(\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a positive Banach (quasi-Banach) ideal.

Remark 2.1
.

In condition
(ii)
, because every regular operator is a
difference of positive ones, the set
\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(E;F\right)
can
be replaced by the space
\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E;F\right)
, and condition
(ii)
remains the same.

Analogous to the previous approach, we introduce the
positive right
ideal

linear operators on the right side and linear operators on the left side.
Similarly, we define the
positive ideal

by considering only the positive linear operators, with the composition
occurring on both the left and right sides. It is important to note that any
positive right or left ideal is automatically a positive ideal.

Remark 2.2

Every operator ideal is also positive (right, left) ideal.

u_{1}\downarrow&\nearrow v_{1}&\\

B=v_{1}\circ\pi_{1}+v_{2}\circ\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X\times Y;F\right)
. Let

be a finite-rank operator. It can be expressed as a combination of operators

Let
\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}
be positive Banach left
and right ideals, respectively. If
E
and
F

(2.1)

(2.2)

Indeed, let

We have

We have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\varphi,T\left(x\right)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\varphi,T\left(x\right)\right\rangle\right|\left\|id_{\mathbb{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}=\left\|\left\langle\varphi,T\left(x\right)\right\rangle id_{\mathbb{K}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\circ T\circ B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\left\|B\right\|=\left\|\varphi\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\left\|x\right\|.

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in B_{F^{\ast}}}\left|\left\langle\varphi,T\left(x\right)\right\rangle\right|\leq\sup_{\varphi\in B_{F^{\ast}}}\left\|\varphi\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\left\|x\right\|

consequently,
\left\|T\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}
are
positive Banach left and right ideals, respectively. Then

\left(\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+},\left\|.\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\right)

Proof.

It is straightforward to show that

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}=\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}:T=v\circ u\text{ and }\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1\right\}.

Indeed, consider a representation of
T
as
v_{0}\circ u_{0}.
Then we can
write
T=(\frac{v_{0}}{\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}})\circ(\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}u_{0}),
and we
have

\left\|\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\geq\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}:T=v\circ u\text{ and }\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1\right\}.

This implies

\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\geq\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}:T=v\circ u\text{ and }\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1\right\}

Taking the infimum over all representations
T=

v\circ u,
we find

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\geq\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}:T=v\circ u\text{
and }\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1\right\}.

Now, let
v_{0}\circ u_{0}
be a representation of
T
such that
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1.
Then
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}.
Taking the infimum over all such
representations, we obtain

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\inf\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}:T=v\circ u\text{
and }\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=1\right\}.

Next, let
E
and
F
be Banach lattices. We will verify that
\left\|.\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}
is a quasi
norm; the rest is trivial. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
and
T\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}(E;F).
There exist a Banach space
X
and
elements
u_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}\left(E;X\right),v_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\left(X;F\right)

T=v_{0}\circ u_{0}.
Then

\left\|\lambda T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left\|\lambda v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}=\left|\lambda\right|\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}},

if we take the infimum over all representations of
T,
we find
\left\|\lambda T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left|\lambda\right|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}.
We check the inverse inequality only
for
\lambda\neq 0.
Let
v_{0}\circ u_{0}
be a representation of
\lambda T.
Then

and we have

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left\|\frac{v_{0}}{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\frac{1}{\left|\lambda\right|}\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

and taking the infimum over all representations of
\lambda T,
we find
\left|\lambda\right|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\leq\left\|\lambda T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}.
Now, by
\left(2.2\right)

if
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}=0,
then
T=0.
Let
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}(E;F).
Following a similar approach to the proof of
Proposition
2.3

T_{1}+T_{2}=B\circ A.
We can then establish

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|i_{1}\circ u_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+\left\|i_{2}\circ u_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|i_{1}\right\|\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+\left\|i_{2}\right\|\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}=\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|v_{1}\circ\pi_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}+\left\|v_{2}\circ\pi_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\pi_{1}\right\|\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}+\left\|\pi_{2}\right\|\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}=\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}+\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}.

Now, for each

\displaystyle\left\|T_{1}+T_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\left\|B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}\right)\left(\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}+\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}}\right)

\displaystyle 2\left(\left\|T_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+\left\|T_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}+2\varepsilon\right)

We now provide examples of positive ideals. For each

2.2.
Positive left multi-ideal

We extend the previous concepts to the multilinear case by introducing a new
definition for positive ideals of multilinear operators. This new concept is
an extension of multi-ideals and utilizes the techniques introduced in
[
9
,
18
]
. To begin with, we introduce the notion of positive left
multi-ideal. Moreover, we propose a composition method that allows us to
construct a positive left ideal of multilinear operators from a given
positive left ideal.

Definition 2.5
.

A positive left multi-ideal (or positive left ideal of multilinear
operators), denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+},
is a subclass of all
continuous multilinear operators from Banach spaces into a Banach lattice
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, Banach spaces
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and Banach lattice
F,
the components

\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}(X_{1},...,X_{m};F):=\mathcal{L}(X_{1},...,X_{m};F)\cap\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}

satisfy:

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}(X_{1},...,X_{m};F)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{L}(X_{1},...,X_{m};F)
which contains the
m
-linear mappings of
finite rank.

(ii)
The positive ideal property: If
T\in\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m};F\right),u_{j}\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y_{j};X_{j}\right)

for
j=1,\ldots,m
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(F;E)
, then
v\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right)
is in
\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}\left(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m};E\right)
.

If
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}:\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies:

a)
\left(\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}(X_{1},...,X_{m};F),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a Banach (quasi-Banach) space for all
Banach spaces
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
and Banach lattice
F
.

b) The
m
-linear form
T^{m}:\mathbb{K}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
given
by
T^{m}\left(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}\right)=\lambda^{1}\ldots\lambda^{m}
satisfies
\left\|T^{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}=1
for all
m
,

c)
T\in\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m};F\right),u_{j}\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y_{j};X_{j}\right)
for
j=

1,\ldots,m
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(F;E)
then

\left\|v\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}\leq\|v\|\|T\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{1}\right\|\ldots\left\|u_{m}\right\|

we say that
\left(\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a positive Banach (quasi-Banach) right multi-ideal.
When
m=1
, it specifically corresponds to the case of a positive right
ideal.

Remark 2.6
.

As mentioned in the remark

,
we can substitute

\displaystyle\left(v_{1}-v_{2}\right)\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right)-v_{2}\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right).

By the linearity of
\mathcal{M}_{L}^{+}\left(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m};E\right)

.

Definition 2.11

j=

and Banach space

m

right

Remark 2.12
.

Condition
\left(ii\right)

Consider

\left(j=1,2\right)

\displaystyle v\circ T\circ\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\circ T\circ\left(u_{1}^{1}-u_{1}^{2},u_{2}^{1}-u_{2}^{2}\right)

2.4.
Positive multi-ideals

In this subsection, we introduce the definition of positive multi-ideals.
The construction of these positive multi-ideals is based on techniques

Definition 2.15

A positive multi-ideal (or positive ideal of multilinear operators) is a
subclass

of all continuous multilinear operators between
Banach lattices. It is characterized by the property that for all

is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},...,E_{m};F)
which contains the
m
-linear mappings of finite rank.

(ii)
The positive ideal property: If

for

\mathcal{M}^{+}\left(G_{1},\ldots,G_{m};G\right)

If

satisfies:

a)

F
.

b) The

Definition 3.7

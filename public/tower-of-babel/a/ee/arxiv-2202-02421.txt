The rate of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -convergence for Ricci flows with closed and smooth tangent flows In dedication to the 70 70 th birthday of Professor Peter Li
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
This article is a continuation of [ CMZ21c ] , where we proved that a Ricci flow with a closed and smooth tangent flow has unique tangent flow, and its corresponding forward or backward modified Ricci flow converges in the rate of t − β t^{-\beta} for some β > 0 \beta>0 . In this article, we calculate the corresponding 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -convergence rate: after being scaled by a factor λ > 0 \lambda>0 , a Ricci flow with closed and smooth tangent flow is | log ⁡ λ | − θ |\log\lambda|^{-\theta} close to its tangent flow in the 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -sense, where θ \theta is a positive number, λ ≫ 1 \lambda\gg 1 in the blow-up case, and λ ≪ 1 \lambda\ll 1 in the blow-down case.



1
Introduction

In
[
Bam20a
,
Bam20b
,
Bam20c
]
, the newly proposed and studied notion of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence has greatly expanded the horizon of the field of Ricci flow. Bamler’s notion of
\mathbb{F}
-distance can be regarded as the flow version of the Gromov-
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance studied by Sturm
[
St06a
,
St06b
]
; the latter measures the closeness of two metric spaces endowed with probability measures, whereas the former is a distance between two metric flows (see
[
Bam20b
]
). As an effective version of weak convergence, the success of the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence reveals that the Ricci flow and the Cheeger-Colding theory
[
CC97
,
CC20a
,
CC20b
]
are united on the fundamental level, since manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded from below can be regarded as super Ricci flows, for which Bamler’s
\mathbb{F}
-compactness theorems
[
Bam20b
]
are applicable.

One of the central problems in the field of Cheeger-Colding theory is that of tangent cones, and, in particular, of their uniqueness. For instance, Cheeger-Tian
[
CT94
]
proved the uniqueness of tangent cones at infinity for Ricci flat
manifolds with Euclidean volume growth under certain curvature and integrability assumptions.
This result was later strengthened by Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM14
]
, who also proved an analogous result for the mean curvature flow
[
CM15
]
—that if a tangent flow at a point is a
cylinder, then it is the unique tangent flow at that point. For the uniqueness of tangent cones in the field of minimal surface, refer to
[
AA81
,
S83
,
W83
]
. Recently, Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM21
]
proved that the cylindrical singularities in the Ricci flow are isolated singularities, and that their appearance implies the uniqueness of the blow-up limit.

In the previous paper of the authors
[
CMZ21c
]
, following the methods of
[
Se06
,
Ac12
,
SW15
]
, it is proved that if a Ricci flow has closed and smooth tangent flow, then the tangent flow must be unique, and is the canonical form of a closed shrinking gradient Ricci soliton. Here the tangent flow is either of an ancient solution at infinity, or of a Ricci flow developing a finite-time singularity at the singular time. Furthermore, we have also proved that the corresponding modified forward or backward Ricci flow converges to the shrinker metric at the rate of
t^{-\beta}
as
t\to\infty
; see Section 2 for more details.

The modified Ricci flow is the gradient flow of Perelman’s
\mu
-functional. It differs from the Ricci flow by a
1
-parameter family of self-diffeomorphisms. The convergence of the modified Ricci flow obviously implies the Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton convergence of the scaled sequence of the corresponding Ricci flow, and therefore its convergence in the
\mathbb{F}
-sense (c.f.
[
CMZ21a
, Theorem 6.1]
). Nevertheless,
[
CMZ21a
, Theorem 6.1]
is only a qualitative result, and no comparison between the rates of the two kinds of convergence is made. In this paper, we shall prove that for a Ricci flow with smooth and closed tangent flow, the scaled metric flow has a convergence rate of the form
|\log\lambda|^{-\beta}
, where
\lambda>0
is the scaling factor, and
\lambda\nearrow\infty
or
\lambda\searrow 0
when the tangent flow is a blow-up or blow-down limit, respectively.

[
CMZ21c
]
, which is identical to that of the present article. We consider a metric flow

2.1
Modified Ricci flow

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, the tangent flow
\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
is induced by the canonical form of a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
. We normalize the metric
g_{o}
and the potential function
f_{o}
in the way that

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{o}}+\nabla^{2}f_{o}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{o},

\displaystyle\int(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{o}}dg_{o}

\displaystyle=1.

We have also followed the idea of
[
SW15
]
to consider (backward)
modified Ricci flow
instead. The modified Ricci flow is defined to be the gradient flow of the functional

\mu_{g}:=\inf\left\{\int_{M}(|\nabla f|^{2}+R_{g}+f-n)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg\,\left|\,\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg=1\right\}\right.=\mu(g,1),

where
\mu(\cdot,\cdot)
is Perelman’s
\mu
-functional. Specifically, the modified Ricci flow and its backward version are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\mu_{g_{t}}=-2\left(\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{g_{t}}-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{t}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{t}

\displaystyle=-\nabla\mu_{g_{t}}=2\left(\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{g_{t}}-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{t}\right).

Here (and henceforth) we use
f_{g}
to represent the minimizer of
\mu_{g}
, should it exist and be unique.

In fact, if the minimizer of
\mu_{g}
is unique and depends smoothly on
g
, then one may easily convert a Ricci flow into a modified Ricci flow or a backward modified Ricci flow. More precisely, let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in I}
, where
M
is a closed manifold and
I\subset(-\infty,0]
, be a Ricci flow, then
\overline{g}_{s}
defined as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\tilde{g}_{s}&=\ e^{s}g_{-e^{-s}},\\
\partial_{s}\psi_{s}&=\ -\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{s}}f_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\circ\psi_{s},\\
\overline{g}_{s}&=\ \psi_{s}^{*}\tilde{g}_{s},\end{array}\right.

is a modified Ricci flow, and
\overline{g}_{s}
defined as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\tilde{g}_{s}&=\ e^{-s}g_{-e^{s}},\\
\partial_{s}\psi_{s}&=\ \nabla_{\tilde{g}_{s}}f_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\circ\psi_{s},\\
\overline{g}_{s}&=\ \psi_{s}^{*}\tilde{g}_{s},\end{array}\right.

is a backward modified Ricci flow.

2.2.1
Łojaciewicz inequality

Let us recall the Łojaciewicz inequality in

]

Sung-Wang

(Lemma 3.1 in

]

.

There is a

2.2.2
Rate of convergence

We recall some basic computations related to the Łojaciewicz arguments, which are very useful in the present article. Assume that
\overline{g}_{s}
, where
s\in[0,\infty)
, is an
either forward or backward
modified Ricci flow on a closed manifold
M^{n}
, which converges smoothly to a shrinker metric
g_{o}
. We also assume that
\overline{g}_{s}
is in a small neighborhood of
g_{o}
for all
s\geq 0
. By
[
CMZ21c

, the Ricci flow in Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
can be converted to such a backward or forward modified Ricci flow through (
2.1
) or (
2.1

Since
\overline{g}_{s}
is always in a neighborhood of
g_{o}
, it is always regular, and one may estimate the curvature of
\overline{g}_{s}
in terms of the curvature of
g_{o}
. Then, the standard Shi’s estimates and the regularity of the metric imply that

\left|\nabla^{l}\Rm_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right|_{\overline{g}_{s}}\leq C(l),\quad\left\|\Ric_{\overline{g}_{s}}+\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{2}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\leq C(l),

for all
s\in[0,\infty)
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
; see, for instance, the proof of
[
SW15
, Lemma 3.2]
. Let
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
be the constant in the statement of Theorem
2.1
and fix
\beta\in(2-1/\alpha,1)
. Now we consider the forward and backward cases separately.

(1) Backward case.
The argument below is from the appendix of
[
CMZ21c
]
, which is modeled after
[
SW15
]
. By the standard embedding and interpolation inequality, for any integer
l
, there are integers
p\geq l
and
N(p)\gg p
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\leq C(l)\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{W^{p,2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(l,p)\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}^{\beta}\left\|\Ric_{\overline{g}_{s}}+\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{2}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|^{1-\beta}_{W^{N(p),2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(l)\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}^{\beta},

for all
s\geq 0
. Therefore, by the definition of backward modified Ricci flow and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}

\displaystyle=-2(1-(2-\beta)\alpha)\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{-(2-\beta)\alpha}\|\nabla\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq-C(\beta)\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}^{\beta}.

Then

\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(l)\left\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\leq-C(\beta,l)\frac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}.

(2.7)

Integrating the above inequality from
s
to
\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|\overline{g}_{s}-g_{o}\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(\beta,l)\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}.

(2.8)

On the other hand, we compute using Theorem
2.1
again

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-2\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(1-2\alpha)\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{-2\alpha}\left\|\nabla\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\overline{g}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2(2\alpha-1)\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{-2\alpha}\cdot C\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{2\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2C(2\alpha-1).

Integrating the above inequality, we have

\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\leq C(\alpha)s^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha-1}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s>0.

(2.9)

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|\overline{g}_{s}-g_{o}\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(\beta,l)s^{-\frac{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\in(0,\infty).

(2.10)

(2) Forward case.
The forward case is similar to the backward case; more detailed computations can be found in
[
SW15
]
. Note that in this case we consider

and

\displaystyle\|\overline{g}_{s}-g_{o}\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(\beta,l)s^{-\frac{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}{2\alpha-1}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\in(0,\infty),

where

2.2.3
The derivative of the
P
operator

Recall the map
P
defined in Theorem
2.1
is analytic in a regular neighborhood of a shrinker metric, and hence the minimizer

of

depends on

Recall that in the proof of

L:C^{k,\gamma}(\mathfrak{R}(M))\times C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})

\longrightarrow C^{k-4,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})

,

stand for all the

\displaystyle DL_{(g,f)}(0,h)=

\displaystyle\ 2\Delta^{f}_{g}\left(2\Delta_{g}^{f}h+h\right)-(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}he^{-f}dv_{g}

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a

Proof.

Let us compute

is a variation of

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}\left(2\Delta_{g}f+f-|\nabla_{g}f|^{2}_{g}+R_{g}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\left\langle w,\nabla^{2}_{g}f\right\rangle_{g}-\big\langle\left(2{\rm div}_{g}w-\nabla_{g}\tr_{g}w\right),\nabla_{g}f\big\rangle_{g}+w(\nabla_{g}f,\nabla_{g}f)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}\tr_{g}w+{\rm div}_{g}{\rm div}_{g}w-\langle w,\Ric_{g}\rangle_{g}.

Hence if we let
u:=2\Delta_{g}f+f-|\nabla_{g}f|^{2}_{g}+R_{g}
, then

\begin{split}DL_{(g,f)}(w,0)&=-2\left\langle w,\nabla^{2}_{g}u\right\rangle_{g}-\left\langle\left(2{\rm div}_{g}w-\nabla_{g}\tr_{g}w\right),\nabla_{g}u\right\rangle_{g}+2w(\nabla_{g}f,\nabla_{g}u)\\
&\,+2\Delta_{g}^{f}\Big(-2\left\langle w,\nabla^{2}_{g}f\right\rangle_{g}-\left\langle\left(2{\rm div}_{g}w-\nabla_{g}\tr_{g}w\right),\nabla_{g}f\right\rangle_{g}+w(\nabla_{g}f,\nabla_{g}f)\\
&\,-\Delta_{g}\tr_{g}w+{\rm div}_{g}{\rm div}_{g}w-\langle w,\Ric_{g}\rangle_{g}\Big)+(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{M}\tfrac{1}{2}\tr_{g}w\,e^{-f}\,dv_{g}.\end{split}

(2.13)

Now, by the implicit function theorem we have

DP_{g}(w)=-\left(DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}\circ DL_{(g,P(g))}(w,0).

(2.14)

Since
P:\mathcal{U}\to C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
is analytic, we can find a
C^{k,\gamma}
neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
g_{o}
and a constant
C
depending on
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{U}
but independent of
g\in\mathcal{V}
such that, by (
2.13
),

\|DL_{(g,P(g))}(w,0)\|_{C^{k-4,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C\|w\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad w\in C^{k,\gamma}(\mathcal{S}(M)).

(2.15)

Note that here we have used the fact that if
f=P(g)
, then
\|f\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}
is bounded independent of
g\in\mathcal{V}
.

Claim:

There exists a
C^{k,\gamma}
neighborhood of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
of
g_{o}
such that
DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot)
is also a linear isomorphism from
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
to
C^{k-4,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and

\left\|\left(DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq 2\left\|\left(DL_{(g_{o},f_{o})}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}\right\|\quad\text{ for all }\quad g\in\mathcal{V^{\prime}}.

Proof of the claim.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
be the set of all bounded linear operators from
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
to
C^{k-4,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
endowed with the operator norm, i.e.

\|A\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}=\sup_{u\neq 0}\frac{\|A(u)\|_{C^{k-4,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}}{\|u\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}}.

With the induced topology by this norm,
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is a Banach space. Similarly, we denote by
\mathcal{B}_{2}
and
\mathcal{B}_{3}
, the Banach spaces of all bounded linear operators from
C^{k-4,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
to
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
, and of all bounded linear operators from
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
to
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
, respectively, with the corresponding operator norms. We consider the map
Q:\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{1}
given by

Q(g):=DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot).

Since,
P:\mathcal{U}\to C^{k,\gamma}(M)
is continuous (c.f.
[
J05
, Theorem 10.1]
), it can be seen from (
2.12
) that
Q
is also continuous. For any
g\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\begin{split}Q(g)&=Q(g_{o})+Q(g)-Q(g_{o})\\
&=Q(g_{o})\circ\left[I+Q(g_{o})^{-1}\circ\left(Q(g)-Q(g_{o})\right)\right],\end{split}

(2.16)

where
I
is the identity map
on
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
.
As
Q(g_{o})
is a linear isomorphism, we have that
(Q(g))^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
if and only if
I+Q(g_{o})^{-1}\circ\left(Q(g)-Q(g_{o})\right)
is an invertible bounded linear map from
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
to
C^{k,\gamma}(M;\mathbb{R})
. It suffices to check that for
g
sufficiently close to
g_{o}
,
\|E(g)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{3}}<1
, where
E(g):=Q(g_{o})^{-1}\circ\left(Q(g)-Q(g_{o})\right)
, since in this case
[
J05
, Lemma 7.22]
, we know that
I+E(g)
is invertible and

\left(I+E(g)\right)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(E(g)\right)^{k}\text{ and }\left\|\left(I+E(g)\right)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\left\|E(g)\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{3}}}.

(2.17)

By the continuity of
Q
, there is a
C^{k,\gamma}
neighborhood of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
of
g_{o}
such that

\left\|E(g)\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{3}}\leq\left\|Q(g_{o})^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\left\|Q(g)-Q(g_{o})\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}<\tfrac{1}{2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad g\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}.

Hence on
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
,
\left(I+E(g)\right)
as well as
Q(g)
, are invertible. Moreover by (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we have

\begin{split}\left\|\left(DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}&=\left\|Q(g)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\leq\left\|\left(I+E(g)\right)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{3}}\left\|Q(g_{o})^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\\
&\leq 2\left\|Q(g_{o})^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}=2\left\|\left(DL_{(g_{o},f_{o})}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Hence, by replacing
\mathcal{V}
by
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
if necessary,
there exists a
C
independent of
g\in\mathcal{V}
, such that for all
g\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\left\|\left(DL_{(g,P(g))}(0,\cdot)\right)^{-1}(h)\right\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{C^{k-4,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad h\in\mathcal{C}^{k-4,\gamma}(M).

(2.18)

Finally, by (
2.14
), (
2.15
), and (
2.18
), the theorem follows.

∎

2.3
Definition of the

\mathbb{F}
-distance

The
\mathbb{F}
-distance is a generalization of the Gromov-
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance studied by Sturm
[
St06a
,
St06b
]
. To help the reader grasp the main ingredients of the proof, we include the definition of Bamler’s
\mathbb{F}
-distance in this subsection. Note that Definition
2.3
is slightly simpler than the original definition in
[
Bam20b
, Section 5]
, but it is more appropriate for application and is sufficient for our purpose.

The definition of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence is involved in the notions of coupling and
1
-Wasserstein distance between probability measures. Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(X)
the space of probability measures on
X.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(Y),
we denote by
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
the space of
couplings
between
\mu
and
\nu
, namely, the set of all the probability measures
q\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
satisfying

q(A\times Y)=\mu(A),\quad q(X\times B)=\nu(B),

for any measurable subsets
A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
. The
1
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
is defined to be

{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{q\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}{\rm dist}(x,y)\,dq(x,y).

By the Kantorovich-Rubinstein Theorem, this definition is equivalent to

{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{f}\left(\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu\right),

where the supremum is taken over all bounded
1
-Lipschitz functions
f
on
X.

Let
I
be an interval and
(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu^{i}_{t})),i=1,2,
be metric flow pairs defined over
I
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
J\subset I.

The
\mathbb{F}
-distance between
(\mathcal{X}^{1},(\mu^{1}_{t}))
and
(\mathcal{X}^{2},(\mu^{2}_{t}))
(uniform over
J
), denoted by

{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{F}}^{J}\left((\mathcal{X}^{1},(\mu^{1}_{t})),(\mathcal{X}^{2},(\mu^{2}_{t}))\right),

is defined to be the infimum of number
r>0
with the following properties:
There are a measurable set
E\subset I
, couplings
q_{t}\in\Pi(\mu_{t}^{1},\mu_{t}^{2}),
and embeddings
\phi^{i}_{t}:\mathcal{X}^{i}_{t}\to\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}\right)
for
i=1,2,t\in I\setminus E,
where
\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}\right)
is a complete separable metric space, such that
J\subset I\setminus E
, and

1.

|E|\leq r^{2}
;

2.

For any
s,t\in I\setminus E,s<t
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t}^{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{t}^{2}}{\rm dist}^{Z_{s}}_{W_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{*s}\nu^{1}_{x_{1}|s},\phi^{2}_{*s}\nu^{2}_{x_{2}|s}\right)\,dq_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq r.

The tuple
\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}},\{\phi^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1,2}\right)_{t\in I\setminus E}
is called a
correspondence
.

3
Almost monotonicity of the
1
-Wasserstein distance

As indicated by Definition
2.3
, an important ingredient in the estimate of the
\mathbb{F}
-distance is to compare the corresponding conjugate heat kernels in the metric flows. In this section, we consider the
1
-Wasserstein distance between two conjugate heat kernels on two different Ricci flows sufficiently close to each other. It turns out that an almost monotonicity property similar to
[
Bam20a
, Lemma 2.7]
is available. This almost monotonicity further implies that two Ricci flows close to each other in the
C^{2}
-sense are also close in the
\mathbb{F}
-sense, given that the referntial conjugate heat flows are close to each other.

Throughout this section, we shall let
M^{n}
be a closed
n
-dimensional manifold and
(M^{n},g_{i,t})_{t\in I}
,
i=1,2
, be Ricci flows defined on the same time interval
I
. Let
\overline{g}
be a background metric on
M
. We make the assumption that for all
t\in I
and
i=1,2
, it holds that

\displaystyle C_{0}^{-1}\overline{g}\leq g_{i,t}\leq C_{0}\overline{g},\quad\operatorname{diam}(\overline{g})\leq D,\quad\sup_{M\times I}|{\Rm}_{g_{i}}|\leq\Lambda,

(3.1)

for some positive constants
C_{0}
,
D
, and
\Lambda
. The background metric
\overline{g}
can be taken as, for instance,
g_{1,t_{0}}
for some
t_{0}\in I
. Unless otherwise specified, the tensor norms in this section are always computed using the metric
\overline{g}
, and we shall suppress the metric specification in the norm notations, i.e.,
\|\cdot\|_{C^{2}}=\|\cdot\|_{C^{2}_{\overline{g}}}
, etc.

Let us assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\left\|g_{1,t}-g_{2,t}\right\|_{C^{2}}\leq\varepsilon\ll 1.

(3.2)

Then, following
[
Bam20b
]
, we may easily construct a correspondence
\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}},\{\phi^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1,2}\right)_{t\in I}
as follows. Let
Z_{t}=M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}
for
t\in I
, where
M_{1}=M_{2}=M
and, for notational convenience, we have added the subindex to indicate that
M_{i}
corresponds to the underlying manifold of the Ricci flow
g_{i,t}
. For all
z_{1}\in M_{1},z_{2}\in M_{2},
we define

{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(z_{1},z_{2}):={\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(z_{2},z_{1}):=\inf_{w\in M}\left({\rm dist}_{g_{1,t}}(z_{1},w)+{\rm dist}_{g_{2,t}}(w,z_{2})\right)+\varepsilon.

Then it is straightforward to check that
\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}\right)
is a compact metric space.
Let
\phi^{i}_{t}:\left(M,{\rm dist}_{g_{i,t}}\right)\to\left(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}\right)
be the canonical embedding. Clearly, for any
x\in M
, we have

{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(\phi^{1}_{t}(x),\phi^{2}_{t}(x))=\varepsilon.

(3.3)

By a slight abuse of notations, we still write
M_{i}=\phi^{i}_{t}(M)\subset Z_{t}
,
i=1,2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{i,t})_{t\in I}
be Ricci flows satisfying (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), where
M
is a closed manifold. For all
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
and for all
s\leq s^{\prime}\leq s_{1},s_{2},
where
s,s^{\prime},s_{1},s_{2}\in I,
if

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{x_{2},s_{2}}(s_{2}-s)\geq-Y\quad\text{ or }\quad\mathcal{N}^{1}_{x_{1},s_{1}}(s_{1}-s)\geq-Y,

then

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{Z_{s}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s*}\nu^{1}_{x_{1},s_{1}\,|\,s},\phi^{2}_{s*}\nu^{2}_{x_{2},s_{2}|s}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle(1+C\varepsilon){\rm dist}^{Z_{s^{\prime}}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{1}_{x_{1},s_{1}\,|\,s^{\prime}},\phi^{2}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{2}_{x_{2},s_{2}\,|\,s^{\prime}}\right)+C\varepsilon(1+{s^{\prime}-s}),

where
\mathcal{N}^{i}
and
\nu^{i}
stand for the Nash entropy and the conjugate heat kernel on the Ricci flow
(M,g_{i,t})_{t\in I}
, respectively, and
C
is a constant depending only on
C_{0}
,
D
,
Y
,
\Lambda
, and
n
.
In particular, setting
s_{1}=s_{2}=s^{\prime}=t
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{Z_{s}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s*}\nu^{1}_{x_{1},t\,|\,s},\phi^{2}_{s*}\nu^{2}_{x_{2},t\,|\,s}\right)\leq(1+C\varepsilon){\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(\phi^{1}_{t}(x_{1}),\phi^{2}_{t}(x_{2}))+C\varepsilon(1+{t-s}).

(3.5)

Proof.

The idea of the proof is similar to that of
[
Bam20a
, Lemma 2.7]
. Fix a point
o\in M
and write

d\nu^{i}_{t}:=d\nu^{i}_{x_{i},s_{i}\,|\,t}=v_{i,t}\,dg_{i,t},

where
d\nu^{i}_{x_{i},s_{i}\,|\,t}
is the conjugate heat kernel on the Ricci flow
(M,g_{i,t})
based at the point
(x_{i},s_{i})
. By the symmetry in the statement of this proposition, we may assume that

\mathcal{N}^{2}_{x_{2},s_{2}}(s_{2}-s)\geq-Y.

Let
F:Z_{s}\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary
1
-Lipschitz function. By replacing
F
by
F-F(\phi^{1}_{s}(o))
if necessary, we may assume that
F\circ\phi^{1}_{s}(o)=0
. Then we write
u^{0}_{i}=F\circ\phi^{i}_{s}
and solve the heat equations

\displaystyle\Box_{g_{i,t}}u_{i,t}=0\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times[s,s^{\prime}],

\displaystyle u_{i,s}=u^{0}_{i},

for
i=1
,
2
. In fact, we will not need
u_{2,t}
in the following. Obviously,
u^{0}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are both
1
-Lipschitz, and hence

[
Bam20a
, Lemma 2.5]
implies that
|\nabla u_{i,t}|_{g_{i,t}}\leq 1
for all
t\in[s,s^{\prime}]
and
i=1,2
.
By our assumption on
F
, we have
u^{0}_{1}(o)=0
. Then the parabolic maximum principle and the bound of diameter in (
3.1
) imply

|u^{0}_{1}|\leq{\rm diam}(g_{1,t})\leq C_{0}D

and

\displaystyle\sup_{M}|u_{1,t}|\leq\sup_{M}|u^{0}_{1}|\leq C_{0}D\quad\text{ for }\quad t\in[s,s^{\prime}]\quad\text{ and }\quad i=1,2.

(3.6)

Letting
\bar{s}=(s+s^{\prime})/2
, we clearly have

\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|_{t=s}^{t=s^{\prime}}=\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|_{t=\bar{s}}^{t=s^{\prime}}+\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|^{t=\bar{s}}_{t=s}.

We shall estimate the two terms above separately. First of all, we have

\displaystyle\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|_{t=\bar{s}}^{t=s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{s}}^{s^{\prime}}\int_{M}\left(\Box_{g_{2,t}}u_{1,t}\right)d\nu^{2}_{t}dt.

Note that for
t\in(s,s^{\prime}]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Box_{g_{2,t}}u_{1,t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Box_{g_{2,t}}u_{1,t}-\Box_{g_{1,t}}u_{1,t}\right|=\left|\Delta_{g_{2,t}}u_{1,t}-\Delta_{g_{1,t}}u_{1,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|g_{1,t}-g_{2,t}\right\|_{C^{2}}\cdot\sup_{M}\left(|\nabla^{2}u_{1,t}|_{g_{1,t}}+|\nabla u_{1,t}|_{g_{1,t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\frac{1+\sqrt{t-s}}{t-s},

where
C
depends on
C_{0}
,
D
,
\Lambda
,
n
, and we have applied the standard Bando-Bernstein-Shi estimates for the heat equation (see, e.g.,
[
Bam20b
, Lemma 9.14]
). It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\right|_{t=\bar{s}}^{t=s^{\prime}}\,\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon(1+\sqrt{s^{\prime}-s})\leq C\varepsilon(1+s^{\prime}-s).

(3.7)

On the other hand, for any
t\in[s,s^{\prime}]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}-\int u_{1,t}v_{2,t}\,dg_{1,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}D\int v_{2,t}\,\left|dg_{2,t}-dg_{1,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{0},D)\varepsilon\int v_{2,t}\,dg_{2,t}\leq C\varepsilon,

where we have applied (
3.6
). Hence we have

\displaystyle\left|\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|^{t=\bar{s}}_{t=s}-\left.\int u_{1,t}v_{2,t}\,dg_{1,t}\,\right|^{t=\bar{s}}_{t=s}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon

(3.8)

Next,

\displaystyle\left.\int u_{1,t}v_{2,t}\,dg_{1,t}\,\right|^{t=\bar{s}}_{t=s}

\displaystyle=-\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}u_{1,t}\left(\Box^{*}_{g_{1,t}}v_{2,t}\right)\,dg_{1,t},

(3.9)

and we also have

\displaystyle\left|\Box^{*}_{g_{1,t}}v_{2,t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Box^{*}_{g_{1,t}}v_{2,t}-\Box^{*}_{g_{2,t}}v_{2,t}\right|

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\Delta_{g_{1,t}}v_{2,t}-\Delta_{g_{2,t}}v_{2,t}\right|+|R_{g_{1,t}}-R_{g_{2,t}}|v_{2,t}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left(|\nabla^{2}v_{2,t}|_{g_{2,t}}+|\nabla v_{2,t}|_{g_{2,t}}+v_{2,t}\right).

Writing
v_{2,t}=(4\pi(s_{2}-t))^{-n/2}e^{-f_{t}}
, we have

\nabla v_{2,t}=-v_{2,t}\nabla f_{t},\quad\nabla^{2}v_{2,t}=v_{2,t}\left(\nabla f_{t}\otimes\nabla f_{t}-\nabla^{2}f_{t}\right).

Therefore, applying
[
Bam20c
, Proposition 5.2]
and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla^{2}v_{2,t}|_{g_{2,t}}+|\nabla v_{2,t}|_{g_{2,t}}+v_{2,t}\right)\,dg_{1,t}dt

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla^{2}f_{t}|+|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}+|\nabla f_{t}|+1\right)v_{2,t}\,(1+\varepsilon)dg_{2,t}dt

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}(s_{2}-t)|\nabla^{2}f_{t}|^{2}d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}\frac{1}{s_{2}-t}d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\,C\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}(s_{2}-t)|\nabla f_{t}|^{4}d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}\frac{1}{s_{2}-t}d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\,C\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{s}^{\bar{s}}\int_{M}1\,d\nu^{2}_{t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(s^{\prime}-s)

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle C(Y)(1+\sqrt{s^{\prime}-s}+s^{\prime}-s)\leq C(Y)(1+s^{\prime}-s).

Combining (
3.6
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
), we have

\left|\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|^{t=\bar{s}}_{t=s}\right|\leq C(C_{0},D,Y)\varepsilon(1+{s^{\prime}-s}).

(3.12)

It then follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle\left|\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\right|^{t=s^{\prime}}_{t=s}\right|\leq C\varepsilon\left(1+{s^{\prime}-s}\right).

(3.13)

By (
3.1
) again, we have

\left||\nabla u_{1,s^{\prime}}|^{2}_{g_{2,s^{\prime}}}-|\nabla u_{1,s^{\prime}}|^{2}_{g_{1,s^{\prime}}}\right|\leq C\varepsilon,

and hence
u_{1,s^{\prime}}
is a
(1+C\varepsilon)
-Lipschitz function on
\left(M,{\rm dist}_{g_{2,s^{\prime}}}\right)
. Therefore, we may find a
(1+C\varepsilon)
-Lipschitz function
U
on
Z_{s^{\prime}}
, such that
U\circ\phi^{i}_{s^{\prime}}=u_{1,s^{\prime}}
for both
i=1,2.

It follows that

\int u_{1,s^{\prime}}\,d\nu_{s^{\prime}}^{1}-\int u_{1,s^{\prime}}\,d\nu_{s^{\prime}}^{2}\leq(1+C\varepsilon){\rm dist}^{Z_{s^{\prime}}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{1}_{s^{\prime}},\phi^{2}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{2}_{s^{\prime}}\right).

Recall

\int u_{1,s^{\prime}}\,d\nu_{s^{\prime}}^{1}=\int u_{1}^{0}\,d\nu_{s}^{1},

thus, by (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int u_{1}^{0}\,d\nu^{1}_{s}-\int u_{1}^{0}\,d\nu^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int u_{1,s^{\prime}}\,d\nu^{1}_{s^{\prime}}-\int u_{1,s^{\prime}}\,d\nu^{2}_{s^{\prime}}+\left.\int u_{1,t}d\nu^{2}_{t}\,\right|^{t=s^{\prime}}_{t=s}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle(1+C\varepsilon){\rm dist}^{Z_{s^{\prime}}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{1}_{s^{\prime}},\phi^{2}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{2}_{s^{\prime}}\right)+C\varepsilon\left(1+{s^{\prime}-s}\right).

Since
F
is
1
-Lipschitz on
Z_{s}
and because of (
3.3
), for any
x\in M
we have

\left|u^{0}_{1}(x)-u^{0}_{2}(x)\right|=\left|F(\phi^{1}_{s}(x))-F(\phi^{2}_{s}(x))\right|\leq{\rm dist}^{Z_{s}}(\phi^{1}_{s}(x),\phi^{2}_{s}(x))=\varepsilon.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{s}}F\,d(\phi^{1}_{s*}\nu^{1}_{s})-\int_{Z_{s}}F\,d(\phi^{2}_{s*}\nu^{2}_{s})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{0}_{1}\,d\nu^{1}_{s}-\int_{M}u_{2}^{0}d\nu^{2}_{s}\leq\int_{M}u^{0}_{1}\,d\nu^{1}_{s}-\int_{M}u_{1}^{0}d\nu^{2}_{s}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle(1+C\varepsilon){\rm dist}^{Z_{s^{\prime}}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{1}_{s^{\prime}},\phi^{2}_{s^{\prime}*}\nu^{2}_{s^{\prime}}\right)+C\varepsilon\left(1+{s^{\prime}-s}\right).

Since
F
is arbitrary, the almost monotonicity formula (
3.4
) is proved.

∎

Next, applying Proposition
3.1
, we show the following corollary, which is analogous to
[

, Lemma 5.19]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that the same assumptions of the previous proposition hold.
Let
(\mu^{i}_{t})_{t\in I}
be a conjugate heat flow on
(M,g_{i,t})_{t\in I}
, where
i=1,2
, and let

Then

{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{F}}^{J}\big(((M,g_{1,t})_{t\in I},(\mu^{1}_{t})_{t\in I}),((M,g_{2,t})_{t\in I},(\mu^{2}_{t})_{t\in I})\big)\leq(1+C\varepsilon)r+C\varepsilon(1+|I|),

where
r>0
is any number with the property that there is a measurable subset
E\subset I
such that

and

|E|<r^{2},\quad\sup_{t\in I\setminus E}{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}(\phi^{1}_{t*}\mu_{t}^{1},\phi^{2}_{t*}\mu_{t}^{2})<r.

Proof.

Let
r>0
be a number satisfying the property stated in the proposition.
For any
t\in I\setminus E,
let
q_{t}\in\Pi(\mu_{t}^{1},\mu_{t}^{2})
be a coupling satisfying

\int_{M\times M}{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(\phi^{1}_{t}(x),\phi^{2}_{t}(y))\,dq_{t}(x,y)<r.

Then for any
s,t\in I\setminus E
,
s\leq t
, by applying Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M\times M}{\rm dist}^{Z_{s}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{1}_{s*}\nu^{1}_{x,t|s},\phi^{2}_{s*}\nu^{2}_{y,t|s}\right)\,dq_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+C\varepsilon)\int_{M\times M}{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(\phi^{1}_{t}(x),\phi^{2}_{t}(y))\,dq_{t}(x,y)+C\varepsilon(1+t-s)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\ (1+C\varepsilon)r+C\varepsilon(1+|I|).

The conclusion follows from Definition
2.3
.

4
Convergence rate of the referential conjugate heat flow

It is obvious from Corollary
3.2
that, to estimate the

-convergence rate for the Ricci flows in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we need also to estimate the convergence rate of the referential conjugate heat flow
\mu_{t}
; this is the goal of the present section.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be the Ricci flow in the statement either of Theorem
1.1
, in which case
I=(-\infty,0]
, or, of Theorem
1.2
, in which case
I=(-T,0]
. Let

d\mu_{t}:=u(\cdot,t)dg_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{t}}dg_{t},\quad t\in I

(4.1)

be the referential conjugate heat flow in the statement of Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
. Note that in the case of Theorem
1.1
,
\mu_{t}:=\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,t}
for some arbitrarily fixed
p_{0}\in M
; in the case of Theorem
1.2
,
u
is a singular conjugate heat kernel constructed in
[
MM15
]
. Let
(M,g_{o},f_{o})
be the normalized shrinker that generates the unique tangent flow in Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
(c.f.
[
CMZ21c
, Corollary 1.3, Corollary 1.4]
).

Let
\overline{g}_{s}
, where
s\in[0,\infty)
, be the (backward) modified Ricci flow constructed from
g_{t}
, as demonstrated in Section 2.1. Furthermore,
\overline{g}_{s}
satisfies all the properties listed in Section 2.2.2. For the sake of simplicity, we write

\|\overline{g}_{s}-g_{o}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq Cs^{-\theta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s>0,

(4.2)

where
\theta:=\frac{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}{2\alpha-1}
,
k\gg 1
, and
\gamma\in(0,1)
; in particular, we choose
k
and
\gamma
to be the constants in Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
.

To simplify our argument, we may, by shifting
s
if necessary, without loss of generality, assume that
\overline{g}_{s}
is very close to
g_{o}
, so that it satisfies Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
for all
s\geq 0
. Furthermore, if
\overline{g}_{s}
is very close to
g_{o}
for all
s\geq 0
, we also have that
f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
is very close to
f_{o}
for all
s\geq 0
. Therefore, in view of the fact that
\lambda_{1}\left(-\Delta_{g_{o}}^{f_{o}}\right)>\frac{1}{2}
(c.f.
[
SW15
, Lemma 3.5]
), where

\Delta_{g}^{f}:=\Delta_{g}-\langle\nabla_{g}f,\nabla_{g}\cdot\,\rangle

is the drifted laplacian operator, and
\lambda_{1}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue, we also have that

\lambda_{1}\left(-\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\right)>\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0.

As a consequence, we have the following Neumann type Poincaré inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
s\geq 0
, if
v\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfies
\displaystyle\int_{M}ve^{-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}d\overline{g}_{s}=0
, then

\int_{M}v^{2}e^{-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}d\overline{g}_{s}\leq 2\int_{M}|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}v|^{2}e^{-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}d\overline{g}_{s}.

(4.3)

Next, we also modify the referential conjugate heat flow. Let us define

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{s}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{-e^{s}}&\text{in the backward case}\\
f_{-e^{-s}}&\text{in the forward case}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{s}

\displaystyle:=\tilde{f}_{s}\circ\psi_{s}

where
f_{t}
is the function in (
4.1
), and
\psi_{s}
is the
1
-parameter family of self-diffeomorphism defined by (
2.1
) in the backward case, or by (
2.1
) in the forward case. Perelman’s monotonicity formula obviously implies that

\overline{f}_{s}\longrightarrow f_{o}\quad\text{ smoothly, }\quad\text{when }s\to\infty.

Comsequently, we also have

\left|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right|\to 0\quad\text{ uniformly.}

(4.6)

Our goal is to estimate the rate of these convergences. Now we split our argument into two cases.

(1) Backward case.
It follows from the definition of
u
that

\partial_{s}\tilde{f}_{s}-\Delta_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\tilde{f}_{s}+|\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\tilde{f}_{s}|^{2}-R_{\tilde{g}_{s}}+\frac{n}{2}=0.

(4.7)

We will compare
\overline{f}_{s}
to
f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
. To this end, we start with deriving an evolution equation satisfied by
\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
. By virtue of (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\overline{f}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{s}\tilde{f}_{s}\right)\circ\psi_{s}+\langle\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\tilde{f}_{s},\nabla_{\tilde{g}_{s}}f_{\tilde{g}_{s}}\rangle\circ\psi_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{s}\tilde{f}_{s}\right)\circ\psi_{s}+\langle\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\overline{f}_{s},\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}\overline{f}_{s}-|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\overline{f}_{s}|^{2}+R_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\frac{n}{2}+\langle\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\overline{f}_{s},\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)-\langle\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\overline{f}_{s},\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\rangle+\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}+R_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)-\left|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\right|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr }_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right),

where the norms and inner products are all computed with respect to the evolving metric
\overline{g}_{s}
. Since
f_{g}=P(g)
, we have
\partial_{s}f_{\overline{g}_{s}}=DP_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right)
, where
DP
is the linearization of the analytic map
P:g\mapsto f_{g}
. Hence

\partial_{s}\left(f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\overline{f}_{s}\right)=\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\left(f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\overline{f}_{s}\right)+\left|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\right|^{2}+\left(DP_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr }_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\left(\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right).

(4.8)

Let

v_{s}:=e^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\overline{f}_{s}},\quad\mathcal{E}_{s}:=\left(DP_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\text{tr }_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\left(\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\right),

(4.9)

then (
4.8
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v_{s}=v_{s}\,\partial_{s}\left(f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\overline{f}_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{s}\,\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\left(f_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\overline{f}_{s}\right)+v_{s}\,\left|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}\left(\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)\right|^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{s}v_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}v_{s}+\mathcal{E}_{s}v_{s}.

By (
4.6
), it is clear that
v_{s}\to 1
uniformly. We shall then estimate the rate of this convergence. Let us define

d\bar{\nu}_{s}:=(4\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}\,d\overline{g}_{s},\quad Z(s):=\left(\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.10)

Then
d\bar{\nu}_{s}
is a probability measure with

\partial_{s}d\bar{\nu}_{s}=-\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a positive constant
C_{1}
such that for all
s\geq 0
,

C_{1}^{-1}\leq v_{s}\leq C_{1}.

(4.11)

Proof.

Since the original Ricci flow
g_{t}
is of Type I, applying the gaussian upper and lower estimates of
[
X17
, Theorem 3.5]
, in combination with the radius bound

\operatorname{diam}_{\overline{g}_{s}}(M)\leq 2\operatorname{diam}_{g_{o}}(M)\leq C\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0,

we have that, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
, such that

-C^{\prime}\leq\overline{f}_{s}\leq C^{\prime}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0.

On the other hand, since
\overline{g}_{s}
and
g_{o}
are close and consequently
f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
and
f_{o}
are close, we also have

-C^{\prime}\leq f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\leq C^{\prime}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0.

The lemma then follows from the definition of
v_{s}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
Z(s)
defined in (
4.10
), we have

Z(s)\leq Cs^{-\theta},

for
s>0,
where
\theta:=\frac{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}{2\alpha-1}
is the same constant as in (
4.2
).

Proof.

\displaystyle(Z^{2})^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\int 2(v_{s}-1)\partial_{s}v_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}-\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\int 2(v_{s}-1)\left(\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}v_{s}+\mathcal{E}_{s}v_{s}\right)\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}-\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle=-2\int|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}(v_{s}-1)|^{2}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}+\int(v_{s}^{2}-1)\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq-\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}+\sup_{M}\left(|v_{s}+1|\cdot|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\right)\int|v_{s}-1|\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle=-Z^{2}(s)+C\sup_{M}|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\cdot Z(s),

where we have applied (
4.3
), the fact that
\int v_{s}d\bar{\nu}_{s}\equiv 1
, and Lemma
4.2
. For the
\mathcal{E}_{s}
term, we may estimate using
(
4.9
) and Theorem
2.2
:

\sup_{M}|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\leq C\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\|_{C^{0}_{g_{o}}}\leq-C\tfrac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}.

(4.13)

Consequently, (
4.12
) becomes

Z^{\prime}(s)\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}Z(s)-C\tfrac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}.

(4.14)

Let us define

\zeta(s):=C_{0}\left(\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha},

where
C_{0}
is some large constant depending on
v_{0}
and
g_{o}
to be determined. We may first choose
C_{0}\geq C
, where
C
is the constant in (
4.14
), so that

(Z+\zeta)^{\prime}(s)\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}Z(s).

(4.15)

Recall that (
2.9
) implies that

\zeta(s)\leq C_{0}Cs^{-\theta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s>0.

(4.16)

On the other hand,
\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}-\mu_{g_{o}}
is strictly positive, for otherwise the Ricci flow in question is trivial, so
we may enlarge
C_{0}
such that
Z(0)\leq\zeta(0)/2.

Let us now proceed to estimate
Z(s)
. We fix an arbitrary
s\geq 1
. If
Z(s)\leq\zeta(s)
, then, by (
4.16
), we are done. Let us assume
Z(s)>\zeta(s)
, and define

s_{1}:=\inf\{s^{\prime}>0:Z\geq\zeta\text{ on the interval }[s^{\prime},s]\}.

Since
Z(0)\leq\zeta(0)/2
, we must have
s_{1}\in(0,s)
. Hence,
Z(s_{1})=\zeta(s_{1})
. Then, on the interval
[s_{1},s]
, the fact
Z\geq\zeta
together with (
4.15
) implies

(Z+\zeta)^{\prime}\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}Z\leq-\tfrac{1}{4}(Z+\zeta).

Integrating the above inequality from
s_{1}
to
s
, we have

(Z+\zeta)(s)\leq(Z+\zeta)(s_{1})e^{-\frac{1}{4}(s-s_{1})}=2\zeta(s_{1})e^{-\frac{1}{4}(s-s_{1})}\leq Cs_{1}^{-\theta}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(s-s_{1})},

where we have also applied (
4.16
).
If
s_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}s
, then

Z(s)\leq(Z+\zeta)(s)\leq Cs_{1}^{-\theta}\leq C2^{-\theta}s^{-\theta}.

If
s_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}s
, then

Z(s)\leq 2\zeta(s_{1})e^{-s/8}\leq Cs^{-\theta};

note that
\zeta
is uniformly bounded.
∎

Now we summarize the conclusion in the backward case.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be the ancient Ricci flow in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, where
M
is a closed manifold. Let
d\mu_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)e^{-f_{t}}dg_{t}
be the referential conjugate heat flow. Let
f^{*}_{g_{t}}
be the minimizer of
\mu(g_{t},|t|)
, where
\mu
is Perelman’s
\mu
-functional. Then for any
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,\theta)
, there is a positive constant
C
, such that

\|f_{t}-f^{*}_{g_{t}}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\log|t|)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\leq-1.

Note that the
C^{0}
norm is independent of the choice of the metric.

Proof.

Indeed, we need only to estimate
\left|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right|
. By Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right|\leq\left(\sup_{x\in[C_{1}^{-1},C_{1}]}\left|(\log x)^{\prime}\right|\right)\cdot\left|v_{s}-1\right|\leq C|v_{s}-1|.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{g_{o}}}\leq C\|v_{s}-1\|_{L^{2}_{g_{o}}}\leq C\|v_{s}-1\|_{L^{2}_{\bar{\nu}_{s}}}=CZ(s)\leq Cs^{-\theta}.

Furthermore, since, by the standard parabolic derivative estimates and the regularity of
\overline{g}_{s}
, the higher derivatives of
\overline{f}_{s}
and
f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
are bounded uniformly in
s
, we may apply the standard Sobolev embedding and interpolation formula (c.f.
[
Ham82
]
). Specifically, for any
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,\theta)
, we may find an integer
N\gg 1
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\theta^{\prime})\left\|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right\|^{\theta^{\prime}/\theta}_{L^{2}_{g_{o}}}\cdot\left\|\overline{f}_{s}-f_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right\|^{1-\theta^{\prime}/\theta}_{W^{N,2}_{g_{o}}}\leq Cs^{-\theta^{\prime}}.

The conclusion of the proposition follows from the definition of
\overline{f}_{s}
and
f_{\overline{g}_{s}}
, and the change of variable
s=\log(-t)=\log|t|
.
∎

(2) Forward case.
The forward case is almost identical to the backward one. We will omit most of the computational details and will focus on the points where it is different from the former case. Defining
v_{s}
,
\mathcal{E}_{s}
as in (
4.9
), and
\bar{\nu}_{s}
,
Z(s)
as in (
4.10

\displaystyle\partial_{s}v_{s}=-\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}v_{s}+\mathcal{E}_{s}v_{s},

\displaystyle\partial_{s}d\bar{\nu}_{s}=-\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s},

\displaystyle\int_{M}v_{s}d\bar{\nu}_{s}\equiv 1.

Then, we may compute as in (
4.12
)

\displaystyle(Z^{2})^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\int 2(v_{s}-1)\left(-\Delta_{\overline{g}_{s}}^{f_{\overline{g}_{s}}}v_{s}+\mathcal{E}_{s}v_{s}\right)\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}-\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle=2\int|\nabla_{\overline{g}_{s}}(v_{s}-1)|^{2}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}+\int(v_{s}^{2}-1)\mathcal{E}_{s}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle\geq\int(v_{s}-1)^{2}\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}-\sup_{M}\left(|v_{s}+1|\cdot|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\right)\int|v_{s}-1|\,d\bar{\nu}_{s}

\displaystyle=Z^{2}(s)-C\sup_{M}|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\cdot Z(s),

where we have applied Lemma
4.3
and (
4.11
); obviously, the latter formula is also valid in this case due to the gaussian estimates of
[
MM15
, Proposition 2.7, Proposition 2.8]
. Arguing as in (

) and using (

), we have

\sup_{M}|\mathcal{E}_{s}|\leq C\|\partial_{s}\overline{g}_{s}\|_{C^{0}_{g_{o}}}\leq-C\tfrac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}(s)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}Z(s)+C\tfrac{d}{ds}\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha},

and

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{ds}\left(Z(s)-C\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}\right)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}Z(s)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Z(s)-C\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}\right).

Defining

\xi(s):=Z(s)-C\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha},

we have

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(s)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(s)\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0.

(4.17)

If there is a
s_{0}\geq 0
such that
\xi(s_{0})>0
, then, integrating (

) from
s_{0}
to

\xi(s)\geq\xi(s_{0})\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(s-s_{0})\right)\to\infty\quad\text{ as }\quad s\to\infty;

this obviously is a contradiction.

In conclusion, we have that
\xi(s)\leq 0
for all
s\in[0,\infty)
, and hence

Z(s)\leq C\left(\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{\overline{g}_{s}}\right)^{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}\leq Cs^{-\theta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad s>0,

where
\theta:=\frac{1-(2-\beta)\alpha}{2\alpha-1}\in(0,1)
. The rest of the argument is identical to the forward case. We summarize the above results in the following proposition.

.

(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-T,0)}

]

, where

is Perelman’s

, such that

Note that the

norm is independent of the choice of the metric.

5
\mathbb{F}
-distance estimate

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in this section. The following result due to Bahuaud-Guenther-Isenberg

]
will be applied to convert the closeness between

and

to the closeness between the scaled ancient Ricci flow and the canonical form of the shrinker.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem A in
[
BGI20
]
)
.

Let
M^{n}
be a closed manifold and
(M,g_{0}(t))_{t\in[0,\tau_{0})}
be the maximal solution to the Ricci flow with initial metric
g_{0}(0)=\bar{g}_{0},
where
\tau_{0}\in(0,\infty].

Then for any
\tau\in(0,\tau_{0})
, any integer
k\geq 4
, and any
\gamma\in(0,1),
there are
r>0
and
C<\infty
depending on
\bar{g}_{0}
and
\tau
such that
if
\bar{g}_{1}
is another smooth metric on
M
satisfying

\|\bar{g}_{1}-\bar{g}_{0}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}}\leq r,

then the maximal Ricci flow
g_{1}(t)
starting at
\bar{g}_{1}
exist on
[0,\tau]
and

\|g_{1}(t)-g_{0}(t)\|_{C^{k-2,\gamma}}\leq C\|\bar{g}_{1}-\bar{g}_{0}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}},

for all
t\in[0,\tau].
Here, all the Hölder norms are induced from some fixed background metric on
M.

Note that in their statements in
[
BGI20
]
, the authors used the notation
h^{k,\gamma}
to denote the completion of smooth sections with respect to the Hölder norm
C^{k,\gamma},
which is strictly contained in the usual Hölder space. We shall not make the explicit distinction here as we only need the Hölder norms.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are almost identical, we shall only consider the former. Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be the Ricci flow in the statement of Theorem
1.1
. Let
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
be the Ricci shrinker whose canonical form
(M,g^{o}_{t},f^{o}_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}
is the unique tangent flow at infinity of
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
. Specifically, let
\Phi_{t}
be the group of
1
-parameter family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\nabla_{g_{o}}f_{o},
then we have

g^{o}_{t}=|t|\Phi^{*}_{-\log|t|}g_{o},\quad f^{o}_{t}=\Phi^{*}_{-\log|t|}f.

Let
A>1
be an arbitrarily fixed constant and
I_{A}:=[-A,-1/A].

For any
\lambda\ll A^{-1},
write

g^{\lambda}_{t}:=\lambda^{2}g_{t/\lambda^{2}}

and
s_{-}:=\log(A/\lambda^{2}).
Then, by (
2.10
), we have

\|\Phi_{-\log A}^{*}\psi_{s_{-}}^{*}g^{\lambda}_{-A}-g^{o}_{-A}\|_{C_{g_{o}}^{k,\gamma}}=\|\Phi_{-\log A}^{*}(A\overline{g}_{s_{-}}-Ag_{o})\|_{C_{g_{o}}^{k,\gamma}}\leq C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta},

where
\psi_{s}
is defined in (
2.1
). By Theorem
5.1
, if
\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}(A)
, then we have

\sup_{t\in I_{A}}\|\Psi_{\lambda}^{*}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g^{o}_{t}\|_{C_{g_{o}}^{k-2,\gamma}}\leq C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta},

(5.1)

where
\Psi_{\lambda}:=\psi_{s_{-}}\circ\Phi_{-\log A}
is a diffeomorphism, and we are using
g_{o}
as the fixed background metric.
In the following, we may assume that
\Psi_{\lambda}={\rm id}
by considering the pullback flow.

Let
d\mu_{t}:=u_{t}dg_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)e^{-f_{t}}
be the referential conjugate heat flow in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, and
d\mu^{\lambda}_{t}:=u^{\lambda}_{t}dg^{\lambda}_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)e^{-f^{\lambda}_{t}}dg^{\lambda}_{t}
its scaled version, where
f^{\lambda}_{t}=f_{t/\lambda^{2}}
. Let
d\mu^{o}_{t}:=u^{o}_{t}dg^{o}_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)e^{-f^{o}_{t}}dg^{o}_{t}
be the referential conjugate heat kernel of
(M,g^{o}_{t})
. We shall now compare
\mu^{\lambda}_{t}
and
\mu^{o}_{t}
.

By (
5.1
) (note that
\Psi^{*}_{\lambda}
is taken to be
\operatorname{id}
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|_{C^{k-2,\gamma}_{\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g_{o}}}

\displaystyle=|t|^{-1}\Big\||t|\Phi^{*}_{-\log|t|}(|t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o})\Big\|_{C_{g_{o}}^{k-2,\gamma}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A}.

Now we measure the first norm with the metric
g_{o}
instead.
Since
I_{A}
is a compact interval, we have

C(A)^{-1}g_{o}\leq\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g_{o}\leq C(A)g_{o}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A}.

(5.3)

Let

h:=|t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}
and
\tilde{g}:=\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g_{o}
. By (
5.3
), we have

|h|^{2}_{g_{o}}=g_{o}^{ik}g_{o}^{jl}h_{ij}h_{kl}\leq C(A)^{2}\tilde{g}^{ik}\tilde{g}^{jl}h_{ij}h_{kl}\leq C(A)|h|^{2}_{\tilde{g}}.

Hence, by (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|_{C^{0}_{g_{o}}}

\displaystyle\leq C(A)\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|_{C^{0}_{\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g_{o}}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A}.

Furthermore, by the standard Shi’s estimates for
g^{\lambda}_{t}
and the fact that
I_{A}
is a compact interval, we obviously have

\displaystyle\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|_{C^{l}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(A,l)\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A},

(5.5)

so long as we take
\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}(A)
for
\overline{\lambda}(A)
small enough. Applying the standard interpolation formula with (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we have that, for any
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,\theta)
,

\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(k,\theta^{\prime})\Big\||t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}-g_{o}\Big\|^{\theta^{\prime}/\theta}_{C^{0}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A}.

Taking
\overline{\lambda}(A)>0
to be small enough and letting
\lambda\leq\overline{\lambda}(A)
, we may apply Theorem
2.2
to obtain

\left\|f_{|t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g^{\lambda}_{t}}-f_{o}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A},

and hence

\left\|f^{*}_{g^{\lambda}_{t}}-f^{o}_{t}\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A},

(5.6)

where
f^{*}_{g_{t}^{\lambda}}
is the minimizer of
\mu(g_{t}^{\lambda},|t|)
. Note that the
C^{0}
norm is independent of the choice of the metric, and
f_{o}=f_{|t|^{-1}\Phi^{*}_{\log|t|}g_{t}^{o}}
. Combining (
5.6
) with Proposition
4.4
, we have

\|f^{\lambda}_{t}-f^{o}_{t}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A},

and consequently

\|u^{\lambda}_{t}-u^{o}_{t}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in I_{A}.

(5.7)

Henceforth, we fix a
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,\theta)
.

Finally, we are ready to apply Corollary
3.2
with (
5.1
) and (
5.7
) to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Define
\varepsilon:=C(A,\theta^{\prime})(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}
. Let
\mathfrak{C}=(Z_{t},(\phi^{\lambda}_{t},\phi^{o}_{t}))_{t\in I_{A}}
be the correspondence between
(M,g^{\lambda}_{t},\mu^{\lambda}_{t})_{t\in I_{A}}
and
(M,g^{o}_{t},\mu^{o}_{t})_{t\in I_{A}}
constructed at the beginning of Section 3.
Fix
t\in I_{A}
and let
F:Z_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
be any
1
-Lipschitz function. Write
F_{\lambda}=F\circ{\phi^{\lambda}_{t}}
and
F_{o}=F\circ{\phi^{o}_{t}}
. Since
F
is
1
-Lipschitz, for any
x\in M,
we have

|F_{\lambda}(x)-F_{o}(x)|=|F(\phi^{\lambda}_{t}(x))-F(\phi^{o}_{t}(x))|\leq{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}(\phi_{t}^{\lambda}(x),\phi^{o}_{t}(x))=\varepsilon.

By adding a constant to
F
, we may assume that
F_{\lambda}
vanishes at one point on
M
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{t}}F\,d\phi^{\lambda}_{t*}\mu^{\lambda}_{t}-\int_{Z_{t}}F\,d\phi^{o}_{t*}\mu^{o}_{t}=\int_{M}F_{\lambda}\,d\mu^{\lambda}_{t}-\int_{M}F_{o}\,d\mu^{o}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}F_{\lambda}\,d\mu^{\lambda}_{t}-\int_{M}F_{\lambda}\,d\mu^{o}_{t}+\varepsilon=\int_{M}F_{\lambda}u^{\lambda}_{t}\,dg^{\lambda}_{t}-\int_{M}F_{\lambda}\,u^{o}_{t}\,dg^{o}_{t}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \int_{M}F_{\lambda}(u^{\lambda}_{t}-u^{o}_{t})\,dg^{\lambda}_{t}+\int_{M}F_{\lambda}\,u^{o}_{t}\left(dg_{t}^{\lambda}-dg^{o}_{t}\right)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}}.

Note that the bound of
F_{\lambda}
comes from the fact that
F_{\lambda}
attains
0
at some point on
M
, that
F_{\lambda}
is
1
-Lipschitz, and that
(M,g^{\lambda}_{t})
has uniformly bounded diameter for
t\in I_{A}
. Since
F
is arbitrary, we have

\sup_{t\in I_{A}}{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}}_{{\rm W}_{1}}\left(\phi^{\lambda}_{t*}\mu^{\lambda}_{t},\phi^{o}_{t*}\mu^{o}_{t}\right)\leq C(A)(-\log\lambda)^{-\theta^{\prime}},

and Theorem
1.1
follows from Corollary
3.2
.

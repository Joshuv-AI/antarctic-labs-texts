Abstract
. We record an alternative proof of a recent joint equidistribution result of Blomer and Michel, based on Ratner’s topological rigidity theorem. This approach has the advantage of extending to non-uniform lattices.



Expanding closed horocycles are known to become equidistributed on any non-compact finite-area hyperbolic orbifold, such as, e.g., the low-lying horocycle
\{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})(x+\tfrac{i}{T})\,\mid\,x\in[0,1]\}
on the modular curve
X=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathbf{H}
as
T\to\infty
. Equidistribution also holds for various arithmetically interesting growing discrete subsets of points along expanding closed horocycles (see, e.g.,
[
Hej96
,
COU01
,
MS03
,
SU15
,
BSY22
]
). Most recently Blomer and Michel
[
BM23
]
investigated the joint distribution properties of sets of the shape

(0.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\left(\frac{a+i}{q}\right),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\left(\frac{ab+i}{q}\right)\right)\mid\,a\,({\rm mod}\,q)\right\}\subset X\times X

as
q\to\infty
while
b\in(\mathbf{Z}/q\mathbf{Z})^{*}
varies with
q
in a prescribed manner. In parallel, they consider a "continuous version" of this setup, which establishes the joint equidistribution of two low-lying horocycles of different speeds under a weak diophantine condition; see Theorem
1
below. By Dirichlet’s theorem, given any real numbers
y
and
Q>0
, there is a rational number
\tfrac{a}{q}
so that
|y-\tfrac{a}{q}|<\tfrac{1}{qQ}
and
q\leq Q
. In the following statement,
y
is irrational and
Q
is picked to depend on a growing parameter
T
so that as
T\to\infty
we can find a sequence
q\to\infty
for which
y
has a rational approximation of that order of magnitude.

equidistributes as
T\to\infty
for pairs
(y,T)
with
q\to\infty
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
, let
\Gamma
be a non-uniform lattice in
G
, set
L=G\times G
,
\Lambda=\Gamma\times\Gamma
.
Let
T>1
, and let
y>0
be irrational if
\Gamma
is arithmetic or
y\neq 1
if
\Gamma
is non-arithmetic, and let
I\subseteq\mathbf{R}
be a fixed non-empty interval. Then

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\Gamma u_{x}a_{T}^{-1},\Gamma u_{xy}a_{T}^{-1}\right):x\in I\right\}\subseteq\Lambda\setminus L

equidistributes as
T\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be the composition of the smooth immersion
\iota_{y}:G\to L
,
\iota_{y}(g)=(g,a_{y}ga_{y}^{-1})
and the canonical projection
L\to\Lambda\setminus L
so that for any test function
\varphi\in C_{b}(\Lambda\backslash L)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\pi(u_{x}a_{1/T}))\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\Gamma u_{x}a_{1/T},\Gamma u_{xy}a_{1/T})\,dx,

where we use the standard fact that the expansion (or contraction) of closed horocycles by the geodesic flow is algebraically expressed by
a_{y}u_{x}a_{y}^{-1}=u_{xy}
. A result of Shah
[
Sha96
, Theorem 1.4]
asserts that if
\overline{\pi(G)}=\Lambda\backslash L
then

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\Gamma u_{x}a_{1/T},\Gamma u_{xy}a_{1/T})\,dx=\int_{\Lambda\setminus L}\varphi.

We may introduce a conjugation so that
L=G\times G
,
\Lambda=\Gamma\times a_{y}^{-1}\Gamma a_{y}
and
\pi
corresponds to the diagonal embedding. Let
\Delta(G)
denote the diagonal embedding of
G
in
L
. By Ratner’s topological rigidity theorem
[
Rat91
]
, we know that
\overline{\pi(G)}=\pi(H)
for some closed connected subgroup
H<L
containing
\Delta(G)
, where
H\cap\Lambda
is a lattice in
H
. Since
G
is simple,
\Delta(G)
is a maximal connected subgroup in
L
and so the only options are that
H=L
(in which case
\overline{\pi(G)}=\Lambda\backslash L
and equidistribution follows) or that
H=\Delta(G)
. In the latter case, the requirement that
\Lambda\cap H
be a lattice in
H
is equivalent to asking that
\Gamma_{y}:=\Gamma\cap a_{y}^{-1}\Gamma a_{y}
be a lattice in
G
. This holds if and only if
a_{y}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
, where
{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
is the commensurator of
\Gamma
in
G
, i.e.,

{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)=\{\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{R})\mid\Gamma\cap\alpha^{-1}\Gamma\alpha\text{ has finite index in both }\Gamma\text{ and }\alpha^{-1}\Gamma\alpha\}.

A non-uniform lattice
\Gamma<G
is arithmetic if and only if it is commensurable to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
. Since
{\rm Comm}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}))=\mathbf{R}^{\times}\cdot\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{Q})
, we have
a_{y}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
if and only if
y\in\mathbf{Q}
. If
\Gamma
is non-arithmetic, we claim that
a_{y}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
if and only if
y=1
.

By Margulis’ arithmeticity criterium
[
Mar91
, Thm 1.16, Chapter IX]

{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
contains
\Gamma
as a finite-index subgroup. In particular,
{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
is itself a lattice in
G
and by discreteness, its maximal unipotent subgroup is infinite cyclic. Let
u_{t}
be its generator, i.e.,
u_{t^{\prime}}\not\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
for all
|t^{\prime}|<t
. Let
a_{y}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
; up to replacing
a_{y}
by its inverse, we may assume that
y>1
. Then the fact that
a_{y}^{-1}u_{t}a_{y}=u_{t/y}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
contradicts the minimality of
t
.
∎

Remark 3
.

The statement extends to finitely many factors as follows. If
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathbf{R}
are chosen such that
a_{y_{i}/y_{j}}\not\in\mathrm{Comm}(\Gamma)
for
i\neq j
, then

\displaystyle\{(\Gamma u_{xy_{1}}a_{T}^{-1},\dots,\Gamma u_{xy_{n}}a_{T}^{-1})\mid x\in I\}\subset\Gamma^{n}\backslash G^{n}

equidistributes as
T\to\infty
. This can be compared to
[
Mar07
, Thm 10.2]
and
[
Str04
, Thm 4]
. See
[
Mar10
]
for a higher rank version, which also relies on commensurability.

In the rest of this note, we come back to the case of
X=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})\backslash\mathbf{H}
. By spectral expansion and Weyl’s equidistribution criterion, equidistribution in Theorem
1
holds if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\varphi\left(x+\tfrac{i}{T},xy+\tfrac{i}{T}\right)\,dx=o_{\varphi}(1)

holds for all
\varphi=f_{1}\otimes f_{2}
, where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are non-constant Hecke eigenforms. We will show that when
y\in\mathbf{Q}
(and some additional constraints on
y
) this fails, with a limit that can be expressed as a product of Hecke eigenvalues and matrix coefficients. To proceed we first recall some notation connected to the construction of Hecke operators (see, e.g.,
[
Miy89
]
). For each element
\alpha\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\alpha\Gamma=\sqcup_{m=1}^{M}\Gamma\alpha h_{m}

where the
h_{m}
’s are coset representatives
\Gamma=\sqcup_{m=1}^{M}\Gamma_{\alpha}h_{m}
, with
\Gamma_{\alpha}=\Gamma\cap\alpha^{-1}\Gamma\alpha
. For every function
f
on
\Gamma\backslash G
we set
(f|\Gamma\alpha\Gamma)(g)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}f(\alpha h_{m}g)
. Further, if
\alpha\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{Q})
has integer entries, there is a uniquely determined pair of positive integers
l,m
such that
l\mid m
and

\Gamma\alpha\Gamma=\Gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}l&0\\
0&m\end{smallmatrix}\right)\Gamma.

The Hecke operator arising from the double coset
\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
is then given by

T_{n}f=n^{-1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}lm=n\\
l\mid m\end{subarray}}(f|\Gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}l&0\\
0&m\end{smallmatrix}\right)\Gamma).

We say that
f
is a Hecke eigenform if
f
is an eigenfunction of the Hecke operators
T_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. We denote the corresponding Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{f}(n)
.

By abuse of notation, we view functions on
X
as functions on
G
that are left
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
-invariant and right
\mathrm{SO}(2)
-invariant. We denote by
\sigma_{1}
the usual sum-of-divisors function
\sigma_{1}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
. Let
y=\tfrac{p}{q}
with
p,q
distinct, coprime and squarefree, and let
\varphi=f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\in C_{b}(X\times X)
, where
f_{1},f_{2}
are non-constant Hecke eigenforms. Then

\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi\left(x+\tfrac{i}{T},\tfrac{xp}{q}+\tfrac{i}{T}\right)\,dx=\frac{\lambda_{f_{2}}(pq)\sqrt{pq}}{\sigma_{1}(pq)}\left\langle f_{1},a_{p/q}.f_{2}\right\rangle=\frac{\lambda_{f_{1}}(pq)\sqrt{pq}}{\sigma_{1}(pq)}\left\langle a_{q/p}.f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle,

where
\left\langle f_{1},a_{p/q}.f_{2}\right\rangle=\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}f_{1}(g)f_{2}(ga_{p/q}^{-1})d\mu(g)
and
\mu
is normalized so that
\mu(\Gamma\backslash G)=1
.

Proof.

Set
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z})
. Since
a_{p/q}\in{\rm Comm}(\Gamma)
, the group
\Gamma_{p/q}=a_{p/q}^{-1}\Gamma a_{p/q}\cap\Gamma
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
; in particular
\Gamma_{p/q}<G
is a lattice. Then
\varphi\circ\iota_{p/q}\in C_{b}(\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G)
, and the equidistribution of pieces of expanding closed horocycles on
\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G
yields
[
Str04
]

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{I}\varphi(u_{x}a_{T}^{-1},u_{xp/q}a_{T}^{-1})\,dx=\frac{1}{\mu(\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G)}\int_{\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G}\varphi(g,a_{p/q}ga_{p/q}^{-1})\,d\mu(g),

where the Haar measure
\mu
is normalized so that
\mu(\Gamma\backslash G)=1
.

For
p,q
distinct, coprime and squarefree, we find
\Gamma a_{p/q}\Gamma=\delta_{\sqrt{pq}}^{-1}\Gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&pq\end{smallmatrix}\right)\Gamma
, where
\delta_{x}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&\\
&x\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and
\mu(\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G)=M=\sigma_{1}(pq)

[
Miy89
, Lemma 4.5.6]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a fundamental domain for
\Gamma\backslash G
; then
\sqcup_{m=1}^{M}h_{m}\mathcal{F}
, where the
h_{m}
’s are coset representatives of
\Gamma_{p/q}
in
\Gamma
, is a fundamental domain for
\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G
. We can now compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G)}\int_{\Gamma_{p/q}\backslash G}f_{1}(g)f_{2}(a_{p/q}ga_{p/q}^{-1})\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int_{h_{m}\mathcal{F}}f_{1}(g)f_{2}(a_{p/q}ga_{p/q}^{-1})\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\int_{\mathcal{F}}f_{1}(g)\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_{2}(a_{p/q}h_{m}ga_{p/q}^{-1})\,d\mu(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{pq}}{\sigma_{1}(pq)}\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}f_{1}(g)T_{pq}f_{2}(ga_{p/q}^{-1})d\mu(g).

∎

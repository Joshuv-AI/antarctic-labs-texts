Abstract




Abstract:

The signless Laplacian matrix in graph spectra theory is a remarkable matrix of graphs,
and it is extensively studied by researchers.
In 1981, Cvetković pointed
12
directions in further investigations of graph spectra,
one of which is “classifying and ordering graphs”.
Along with this classic direction,
we pay our attention on the order of the largest eigenvalue of the signless Laplacian matrix of graphs, which is usually called the
Q
-index of a graph. Let
\mathbb{G}(m,g)
(resp.
\mathbb{G}(m,\geq g)
) be the family of connected graphs on
m
edges
with girth
g
(resp. no less than
g
), where
g\geq 3
.
In this paper, we firstly order the first
(\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\rfloor+2)
largest
Q
-indices of graphs in
\mathbb{G}(m,g)
, where
m\geq 3g\geq 12
.
Secondly, we order the first
(\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\rfloor+3)
largest
Q
-indices of graphs in
\mathbb{G}(m,\geq g)
, where
m\geq 3g\geq 12
. As a complement, we give
the first five largest
Q
-indices of graphs in
\mathbb{G}(m,3)
with
m\geq 9
.
Finally, we give the order of the first eleven largest
Q
-indices of all connected graphs with size
m
.

Some lemmas and a new upper bound of

In the section, we give some useful lemmas and then give a new bound of

Lemma 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

If
H
is the subgraph of a connected graph
G
, then
q(H)\leq q(G)
. Particularly, if
H
is proper then
q(H)<q(G)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
u
,
v
be two distinct vertices of a connected graph
G
. Suppose
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{t}

(t\geq 1)
are some vertices of
N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)
and
\mathbf{x}
is the Perron vector of
Q(G)
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-\{vw_{i}\mid i=1,2,\ldots,t\}+\{uw_{i}\mid i=1,2,\ldots,t\}
. If
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
then
q(G)<q(G^{\prime})
.

The following lemma gives an interesting transformation that could increase the

-index. As usual, we call it as the ‘quadrangle’ principle,
which is a useful tool in our proofs.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let

be a graph obtained from a connected graph

by a local switching of edges

and

and

. Let
\mathbf{x}
be the Perron vector of

(x_{a}-x_{c})(x_{d}-x_{b})>0

The equitable partition is a significant tool for graph spectral theory.
The largest eigenvalue of the quotient matrix corresponding to an equitable partition of matrix
M
is the largest eigenvalue of

(see
[
1
]
, Lemma 2.3.1). Let
S_{n,3}
be a graph on

by attaching three pendant paths of length

at the center vertex of

, and

be an unicycle graph of order

(see Fig.
2

It is routine to verify that
\phi_{1}(x,n)
and
\phi_{2}(x,n)
in Lemma
2.4
are respectively the characteristic polynomial of the quotient matrix of
S_{n,3}
and

, naturally we have the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i). The
Q
-index of
S_{n,3}
is the largest root of

\phi_{1}(x,n)=x^{3}-nx^{2}+(3n-8)x-n
.

(ii). The

is the largest root of

\phi_{2}(x,n)=x^{5}-(n+5)x^{4}+(7n+1)x^{3}-(15n-17)x^{2}+(10n-8)x-2n.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be the Perron vector of
Q(G)
with respect to
q(G)
and
x_{u}
the coordinate of
\mathbf{x}
corresponds to vertex
u
(
u\in V(G)
).
By the eigenvalue equation
Q(G)\mathbf{x}=q(G)\mathbf{x}
,
we have
q(G)x_{u}=d(u)x_{u}+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x_{v}
. Thus we can get a upper bound of
x_{u}
for any
u\in V(G)
, which can be used to compare the coordinates of two vertices.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.
For any
u\in V(G)
,
we have

x^{2}_{u}\leq\frac{1}{1+\frac{(q(G)-d(u))^{2}}{d(u)}}.

Proof.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

d(u)\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x^{2}_{v}=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}1^{2}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x^{2}_{v}\geq\left(\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x_{v}\right)^{2}=\left((q(G)-d(u))\cdot x_{u}\right)^{2},

which implies

\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x^{2}_{v}\geq\frac{\left((q(G)-d(u)\right)^{2}x_{u}^{2}}{d(u)}.

Thus,

1=\sum_{i\in V(G)}x^{2}_{i}\geq x_{u}^{2}+\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}x^{2}_{v}\geq x_{u}^{2}+\frac{((q(G)-d(u))^{2}x_{u}^{2}}{d(u)}=\left(1+\frac{((q(G)-d(u))^{2}}{d(u)}\right)x_{u}^{2},

which follows
the result.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then

q(G)\leq\max_{u\in V(G)}\left\{d(u)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}d(v)}{d(u)}\right\},

with equality if and only if
G
is either a semiregular bipartite graph or a regular graph.

We now
give an upper and lower bound of
Q
-index by Lemma
2.6
, from which we can deduce three useful corollaries for the later use.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any connected
G
with size
m\geq 5
, we have

(i) if
\Delta(G)\leq s
and
s\geq\frac{2m}{3}
, then
q(G)\leq s+2
.

(ii) if
s\leq\Delta(G)\leq m-1
, then
q(G)>s+1
.

Proof.

Let
z\in V(G)
such that

d(z)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(z)}d(v)}{d(z)}=\max_{u\in V(G)}\left\{d(u)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}d(v)}{d(u)}\right\}.

If
d(z)=1
, by Lemma
2.6
, we have

q(G)\leq d(z)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(z)}d(v)}{d(z)}\leq 1+\Delta(G)\leq s+1.

It follows the result.
If
d(z)=2
, by Lemma
2.6
we get

q(G)\leq d(z)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(z)}d(v)}{d(z)}\leq 2+\Delta(G)\leq s+2.

It follows the result.
Next we consider the case of
d(z)\geq 3
.
Note that

\displaystyle q(G)\leq d(z)+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_{G}(z)}d(v)}{d(z)}\leq d(z)+\frac{2m-d(z)}{d(z)}=d(z)+\frac{2m}{d(z)}-1.

(1)

Clearly,
3\leq d(z)\leq\Delta(G)\leq s
.
Let
f(x)=x+\frac{2m}{x}
.
Then
f(x)
is decreased in the internal
[3,\sqrt{2m}]
and increased in the internal
[\sqrt{2m},+\infty)
.
Since
s\geq\frac{2m}{3}
, also noticed that
f(3)=f(\frac{2m}{3})
and

\frac{2m}{3}>\sqrt{2m}
due to

,
we have

\displaystyle d(z)+\frac{2m}{d(z)}-1\leq s+\frac{2m}{s}-1\leq s+\frac{2m}{\frac{2m}{3}}-1=s+2.

(2)

Combining (
1
) and (
2
), we have the first result.

If
s\leq\Delta(G)\leq m-1
, then
G
has a
K_{1,s}
as a proper subgraph, and so
q(G)>q(K_{1,s})=s+1
from Lemma
2.1
.
It follows the results.
∎

On the other hand, we know that

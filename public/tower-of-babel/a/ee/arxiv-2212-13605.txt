Abstract
. Let T T be a countable complete first-order theory with a definable, infinite, discrete linear order. We prove that T T has continuum-many countable models. The proof is purely first-order, but raises the question of Borel completeness of T T .



Throughout the paper,

Our goal in this paper is to present a complete, self-contained, elementary proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 1

If

We will also prove that the conclusion of Theorem 1 holds for theories admitting a relatively definable discrete order on the locus of some complete type; that will be useful in the continuation of our work, started in
[

The original Vaught’s conjecture, see

[

for theories with a definable linear order and Skolem functions, Mayer in
[

for

Fact 1.1
.

(a) Any relatively definable linear order has a definable extension. More precisely, if
\Pi(x)
is a partial type over

\phi(x,y)

-formula which relatively defines a linear order on

\theta(x)\in\Pi(x)

(b) Any relatively definable function has a definable extension.

Proof.

Routine compactness, we sketch only proof of part (a). Define:

\lambda(x,y,z):=\lnot\phi(x,x)\land(x\neq y\rightarrow\lnot(\phi(x,y)\leftrightarrow\phi(y,x)))\land(\phi(x,y)\land\phi(y,z)\rightarrow\phi(x,z)).

By compactness there exists a formula

∎

Proof.

Let

(D,<)
is discrete.
First, we will prove that

is consistent for some (equivalently all)

and

To verify the injectivity of

-definable subset of

; it follows that

is injective.
In particular, the inverse function

is relatively definable, so after possibly shrinking

and

, we will assume that
\hat{f}
is a bijection.
It remains to show that

) is order-preserving.
Note that at least one of the formulae

\hat{f}^{-1}(x)\leq x

Suppose, by way of contradiction, that

Abstract
This paper focuses on the stability of solutions for a velocity-tracking problem associated with the two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations. The considered optimal control problem does not possess any regularizer in the cost, and hence bang-bang solutions can be expected. We investigate perturbations that account for uncertainty in the tracking data and the initial condition of the state, and analyze the convergence rate of solutions when the original problem is regularized by the Tikhonov term. The stability analysis relies on the Hölder subregularity of the optimality mapping, which stems from the necessary conditions of the problem.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain and
T>0
and let
u_{a},u_{b}:\Omega\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded functions. We consider the
control set

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(Q)^{2}:\,\,u_{a}(x,t)\leq u(x,t)\leq u_{b}(x,t)\,\,\,\,\text{for a.e. }(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times(0,T)\right\}.

(1)

For each control
u\in\mathcal{U}
, interpreted as a force per unit mass acting on the fluid, there is an associated state
{y_{u}}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is the fluid velocity, satisfying the Navier–Stokes equation

\displaystyle{\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}{y_{u}}-\nu\Delta{y_{u}}+({y_{u}}\cdot\nabla){y_{u}}+\nabla{p_{u}}=u\text{ in }Q,\\
&\operatorname{div}{y_{u}}=0\,\,\,\,\text{in Q},\,\,{y_{u}}=0\,\,\,\,\text{on $\Sigma$},\,\,\,{y_{u}}(\cdot,0)=y_{0}\,\,\,\text{in $\Omega$}.\end{aligned}\right.}

(2)

Here,
y_{0}
denotes the initial velocity field,
\nu>0
is the so-called kinematic viscosity parameter, and
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
the lateral boundary of the open cylinder
Q
.
We consider the classical
velocity tracking problem

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|{y_{u}}(x,t)-y_{d}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

(3)

Optimal control problems often involve a regularizing term that aids in the formulation and analysis of the control strategies and the numerical implementation. However, there exist certain scenarios where this regularizing term is absent, such is the case for affine problems, which leads to a different class of solutions that pose additional challenges. In particular, the optimal control for problem (
1
)-(
3

may satisfy

These kinds of optimal control problems have attracted attention due to their implications for the solution of the underlying dynamical system (

[

The study of bang-bang controls for problems constrained by partial differential equations has not been thoroughly explored. It was only in 2012 that a second-order analysis for a semilinear elliptic equation was presented

This article aims to investigate the effect of perturbations on problem (

Additionally, we consider the classic Tikhonov regularization term as a perturbation of the original problem. We provide Hölder estimates for the rates of convergence. \displaystyle\|\widehat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}\leq\kappa\Big(\|y_{0}-w_{0}\|_{W^{2-\frac{2}{\bar{s}},s}_{0.\sigma}(\Omega)^{2}}+\|y_{d}-w_{d}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\varepsilon\Big)^{\frac{1}{\mu}},

In our analysis, we employ the concept of strong Hölder subregularity of a set-valued mapping associated with the optimality of the optimal control problem
[
24

It is important to note that the low regularity solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations present additional challenges in the analysis, which demand the development of distinct estimates compared to papers such as
[

20
,
35

In addition, we dedicate a self-contained appendix to abstract results in metric subregularity; we formalize the tools used to establish stability in the presence of perturbations. Importantly, these tools are designed to be applicable to a wide range of optimal control problems, extending beyond those constrained by the Navier–Stokes equations.
Previous papers that have addressed the subregularity of the optimality mapping have mainly focused on providing sufficient conditions. However, in this paper, we present an abstract result for necessary conditions, see Theorem
A.5

Furthermore, our result clearly establishes that this type of stability implies the bang-bang nature of optimal controls. Due to the general nature of this result, it can be applied to several previously studied optimal control problems, including those examined in
[

20

35

This article is structured as follows: Section
2

3

For a given real normed space

Sobolev spaces.

For a measurable set

For

Let us recall some embeddings that are vital for the upcoming analyses.
Rellich–Kondrachov embedding theorem gives us the compact embedding

if either of the two cases hold: i.

1\leq s_{2}<2s_{1}/(2-ms_{2})

Solenoidal spaces.

To take into account the incompressibility condition, we consider the following solenoidal spaces

\displaystyle W^{m,s}_{0,\sigma}:=\left\{\psi\in W^{m,s}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}:\,\operatorname{div}\psi=0\text{ in }\Omega\right\},

\displaystyle H_{s}:=\left\{\psi\in L^{s}(\Omega)^{2}:\operatorname{div}\psi=0\text{ in }L^{s}(\Omega),\,\psi\cdot n=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}.

We write
V_{s}:=W^{1,s}_{0,\sigma}
and in the Hilbertian case, we use the notations

and

.

The spaces

form a Gelfand triple

\displaystyle{V\hookrightarrow H\cong H^{*}\hookrightarrow V^{*}}

Auxiliary spatial operators.
The orthogonal complement of
H
can be characterized as
H^{\bot}=\{\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}:\,\psi=\nabla q\,\,\,\,\text{for some $q\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{2}$}\}.

The representation

We define the

, where
\psi_{1}

The Stokes operator

We can also look at the Stokes operator as linear operator from

Analysis of the governing equations

Oseen equations.
As usual, in optimal control problems, one needs to consider the linearization of the systems governing the states. For this reason, for known elements
\overline{y}_{1},\overline{y}_{2}
and external force
v
, we shall consider the Oseen equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}z-\nu\Delta z+(\overline{y}_{1}\cdot\nabla)z+(z\cdot\nabla)\overline{y}_{2}+\nabla q=v\,\,\,\,\text{in }Q,\\
\operatorname{div}{z}=0\,\,\,\,\text{in }Q,\,\,{z}=0\,\,\,\,\text{on }\Sigma,\,\,\,{z}(\cdot,0)=z_{0}\,\,\,\text{in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(9)

We can write the weak formulation of (
9
) as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}z+\nu Az+\widetilde{B}(\overline{y}_{1},\overline{y}_{2})z&=v&&\text{in }L^{2}(I;V^{*}),\\
z(0)&=z_{0}&&\text{in }H.\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

The existence of weak solutions of the linearized system can be proven using the usual Galerkin method just as in the nonlinear system.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
30
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Suppose that

and

such that (
10
) holds. Furthermore, the energy estimate

\displaystyle\|z\|_{W(I)}\leq c\left(\|z_{0}\|_{H}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(I;V^{*})}\right)

holds for some constant

independent of
z_{0}
and

.

Quite similarly as in the nonlinear case, improved regularity of the external force and initial datum leads to a more regular solution.

Theorem 3.5

(
[

)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\partial_{t}w+\nu Aw+\widetilde{B}(\overline{y}_{1},\overline{y}_{2})^{*}w&=v&&\text{in }L^{\alpha}(I;V^{*})\\

(

)

Suppose that

We end this section with the following lemma which was inspired by the
L^{s}
-
L^{1}
stability of solutions proven in
[

, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
20
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
v\in L^{s}(Q)^{2}
for
s>2
, and
\overline{y}\in W^{2,1}_{s}
. If
z_{v}\in W^{2,1}_{s}
is the strong solution of (
11
) and
w_{v}\in W^{2,1}_{s}
is the solution of (
14
) both with
\overline{y}_{1}=\overline{y}_{2}=\overline{y}
.
Then for any
\tilde{s}\in[1,2)
, there exists
c>0
independent on
v
such that

\displaystyle\max\{\|z_{v}\|_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}},\|w_{v}\|_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}\}\leq c\|v\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}.

(15)

Proof.

Since
v\in L^{s}(Q)^{2}
for
s>2
,
z_{v}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{2}
. This implies that
|z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}z_{v}\in L^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2},
where
\tilde{s}^{\prime}=\tilde{s}/(\tilde{s}-1)>2
.
The solution
\mathfrak{w}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\partial_{t}\mathfrak{w}+\nu A\mathfrak{w}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})^{*}\mathfrak{w}+\nabla\mathfrak{r}&=|z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}z_{v}&&\text{in }L^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2}\\
\mathfrak{w}(T)&=0&&\text{in }W_{0}^{2-2/{\tilde{s}^{\prime}},\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{2},\end{aligned}

belongs to
C(\overline{Q})^{2}
, and satisfies — according to
Theorem

3.7
—

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathfrak{w}\|_{C(\overline{Q})^{2}}&\leq c\||z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}z_{v}\|_{L^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2}}\leq c\left(\int_{Q}\left||z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}z_{v}\right|^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}\du x\du t\right)^{1/\tilde{s}^{\prime}}\\
&\leq c\left(\int_{Q}|z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}}\du x\du t\right)^{(\tilde{s}-1)/\tilde{s}}\leq c\|z_{v}\|^{\tilde{s}-1}_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

Using integration by parts, and the definition of the adjoint of the operators, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|z_{v}\|^{\tilde{s}}_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}=\int_{Q}|z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}}\du x\du t=\big(|z_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}z_{v},z_{v}\big)_{Q}=\big(-\partial_{t}\mathfrak{w}+\nu A\mathfrak{w}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})^{*}\mathfrak{w}+\nabla\mathfrak{r},z_{v}\big)_{Q}\\
&=\big(\mathfrak{w},\partial_{t}z_{v}+\nu Az_{v}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})z_{v}+\nabla{q}\big)_{Q}=\big(\mathfrak{w},v\big)_{Q}\leq\|v\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}\|\mathfrak{w}\|_{C(\overline{Q})^{2}}\\
&\leq c\|v\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}\|z_{u}\|^{\tilde{s}-1}_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

The transition from the second line to the third line in the computation above used the fact that
\nabla\cdot z_{v}=\nabla\cdot\mathfrak{w}=0
. This cancels out the term
\mathfrak{r}\nabla\cdot z_{v}
and allows the addition of
q\nabla\cdot\mathfrak{w}
. We thus have the estimate for
z_{v}
in (
15
).

For the solution
w_{v}\in W^{2,1}_{s}
, we also get
w_{v}\in C(\overline{Q})^{2}
from which we get
|w_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}w_{v}\in L^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{2}
whence the solution
\mathfrak{Z}\in C(\overline{Q})^{2}
to the equations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathfrak{Z}+\nu A\mathfrak{Z}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})\mathfrak{Z}+\nabla\mathfrak{q}&=|w_{v}|^{\bar{s}-2}w_{v}&&\text{in }L^{\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(Q)^{2}\\
\mathfrak{Z}(0)&=0&&\text{in }W_{0}^{2-2/{\tilde{s}^{\prime}},\tilde{s}^{\prime}}(\Omega)^{2},\end{aligned}

satisfies — by virtue of
Theorem

3.5
— the estimate
\|\mathfrak{Z}\|_{C(\overline{Q})^{2}}\leq c\|w_{v}\|^{\tilde{s}-1}_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}
. From this, we get the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|w_{v}\|_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}^{\tilde{s}}=\int_{Q}|w_{v}|^{\tilde{s}}\du x\du t=\big(|w_{v}|^{\tilde{s}-2}w_{v},w_{v}\big)_{Q}=\big(\partial_{t}\mathfrak{Z}+\nu A\mathfrak{Z}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})\mathfrak{Z}+\nabla\mathfrak{q},w_{v}\big)_{Q}\\
&=\big(\mathfrak{Z},-\partial_{t}w_{v}+\nu Aw_{v}+B^{\prime}(\overline{y})^{*}w_{v}+\nabla r\big)_{Q}=\big(\mathfrak{Z},v\big)_{Q}\leq\|\mathfrak{Z}\|_{C(\overline{Q})^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}\\
&\leq c\|w_{v}\|^{\tilde{s}-1}_{L^{\tilde{s}}(Q)^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{1}(Q)^{2}}.\end{aligned}

This finally establishes estimate (
15
).
∎

4
The velocity tracking problem

In this section, we give a concise statement of the optimal control problem and specify the data assumptions. We also recall some known results including the first-order necessary conditions and the second-order sufficient condition.

We fix positive numbers
\bar{s}
and
\bar{s}^{\prime}
such that
\bar{s}>2
and
1/\bar{s}+1/\bar{s}^{\prime}=1
. For the optimal control problem (
P
), we will make the following standing assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

The following assumptions hold:

(i)

the initial datum
y_{0}
belongs to
W^{2-\frac{2}{\bar{s}},\bar{s}}_{0.\sigma}(\Omega)^{2}
;

(ii)

the tracking datum
y_{d}
belongs to
L^{\infty}(Q)^{2}
;

(iii)

the functions
u_{a},u_{b}:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
are bounded.

We recall that the control set is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\left\{u=(u^{1},u^{2})\in L^{\infty}(Q)^{2}:\,\,u^{j}_{a}\leq u^{j}\leq u_{b}^{j}\,\,\,\,\text{a.e. in $Q$ for each $j=1,2$}\right\}.

By the box constraints imposed on the controls,
\mathcal{U}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(Q)
. From this we define

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{U}}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}|u|_{L^{\infty}(Q)^{2}}.

(16)

Denoting the
objective functional
by
\mathcal{J}:L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\,|{y_{u}}(t,x)-y_{d}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

we consider the optimal control problem

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{J}(u)\quad\text{ subject to }\eqref{dynsys}.

(P)

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
. We define the minimality radius of
\bar{u}
as

\displaystyle\bar{r}_{\bar{u}}:=\sup\left\{\delta\geq 0:\,\,\mathcal{J}(\bar{u})\leq\mathcal{J}(u)\,\,\,\,\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{{L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}}}\leq\delta$}\right\}.

We say that
\bar{u}
is a local minimizer of problem (
P
) if
\bar{r}_{\bar{u}}<+\infty
and that
\bar{u}
is a global minimizer of problem (
P
) if
\bar{r}_{\bar{u}}=+\infty
.

The existence of at least one global minimizer for problem (
P
) follows from the weak sequential lower semi-continuity of the objective functional and the continuity of the force-velocity operator according to
Theorem

3.3
.

Force-to-velocity mapping.

By
Theorem

3.2
, to each control and each initial datum corresponds a unique state. The mapping
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}:L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\times W^{2-2/\bar{s},2}_{0,\sigma}\to W_{\bar{s}}^{2,1}
given by
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(u,y_{0})=y_{u}
is called
the data-to-velocity mapping
. And for a fixed initial datum
y_{0}\in W^{2-2/\bar{s},2}_{0,\sigma}
we define
\mathcal{S}:L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\to W_{\bar{s}}^{2,1}
as
\mathcal{S}(\cdot):=\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot,y_{0})
which we call the
force-to-velocity
map. Such mapping is infinitely differentiable.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

The force-to-velocity mapping
\mathcal{S}:L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\to W_{\bar{s}}^{2,1}
is of class
C^{\infty}
, i.e., it has Fréchet derivatives of all orders. Moreover, the following statements hold:

(i)

The first order derivative is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(u)v=z_{u,v}\quad\text{for }u,v\in L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2},

where each
z_{u,v}\in W_{\bar{s}}^{2,1}
is the unique
L^{\bar{s}}
-strong solution of the Oseen equations (
9
) with
\overline{y}_{1}=\overline{y}_{2}=y_{u}
and zero initial datum.

(ii)

The second order derivative is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}(u)\big(v_{1},v_{2}\big)=z_{u,(v_{1},v_{2})}\quad\text{for }u,v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2},

where each
z_{u,(v_{1},v_{2})}\in W_{\bar{s}}^{2,1}
is the unique
L^{\bar{s}}
-strong solution of the system

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}z+\nu Az+B^{\prime}(y)z+\nabla q&=-B^{\prime\prime}(y_{u})[z_{u,v_{1}},z_{u,v_{2}}]&&\text{in }L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\\
z(0)&=0&&\text{in }W_{0,\sigma}^{2-2/\bar{s},\bar{s}}(\Omega)^{2}.\end{aligned}

where
z_{u,v_{1}}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(u)v_{1}
and
z_{u,v_{2}}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(u)v_{2}
.

Since
\mathcal{U}\subset L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}
the derivatives are also well-defined on
u\in\mathcal{U}
in the directions
v\in L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\in L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}\times L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}
with
u+v,u+v_{1},u+v_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
, see also the first paragraph after Theorem 2.3 in
[
29
]
.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(u)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2};W^{2,1}_{\bar{s},0})

\bar{v}\in L^{\bar{s}}(Q)^{2}

as the right-hand side under the appropriate spaces.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the well-known velocity tracking problem for the Navier–Stokes equations under the context of optimal control. As the objective functional intended for tracking the velocity contains no regularization for the control, the solution, which is assumed to satisfy a box constraint, can be expected to be of bang-bang type. The study of bang-bang optimal solution is the focus of this paper. Before delving into the main results, we analyzed the necessary and sufficient conditions of the optimization problem. The second-order sufficient condition was established under growth conditions, which as far as we are aware have never been used in the context of the Navier–Stokes equations which implies the bang-bang structure of the optimal controls.

The main results of this article are the sufficient and necessary conditions for the stability of optimal controls under several perturbations. In particular, the original optimization problem is perturbed in terms of the desired velocity, the initial data, and the objective functional itself via the Tikhonov regularization. The tools utilized for the study of the solution stability are owed to the so-called strong Hölder subregularity, which we discussed in the appendix. To be able to apply such tools for the Navier–Stokes equations, we used and, in some cases, improved existing stability estimates concerning the solutions of the nonlinear, the linearized, and the adjoint equations. A noteworthy addition is the
L^{s}-L^{1}
stability for the Oseen equations and its adjoint enabled us to prove that the velocity tracking functional has a changing curvature of order
\mu\in[1,2)
. This result then allowed us to prove the desired solution stability of the optimal controls.

A.1
A first-order variant of the Ekeland principle

The first subsection is of technical nature and is devoted to recalling a few results of variational analysis that will be used later on. In particular, we state a first-order variant of the seminal Ekeland variational principle.

We begin recalling the standard notion of (first-order) Gateaux differentiability. We say that
\mathcal{J}
is Gateaux differentiable at
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
if there exists a linear mapping
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)\in U^{*}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+\varepsilon v)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})}{\varepsilon}\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{U}-u.

Working with functions defined on convex domains has the advantage of simple tangent and normal cone formulations, which in turn implies that the first-order necessary condition also take a simpler form.

The normal cone to
\mathcal{U}
at
\bar{u}
is defined by

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{U}}(\bar{u}):=\left\{\rho\in U^{*}:\text{ }\rho(u-\bar{u})\leq 0\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$}\right\}.

(36)

The first-order necessary condition is well-known for Gateaux differentiable functions, and a lot of the work carried out in optimization and variational analysis relies on it; see
[
23
, pp. 11-13]
. If
{\mathcal{J}}
is Gateaux differentiable at a local minimizer
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
, then
0\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})+N_{\mathcal{U}}(\bar{u})
.
Second-order necessary conditions for optimality are also well known; see, e.g.,
[
5
, Lemma 3.44]
or
[
36
, Theorem 3.45]
.

We give now a technical lemma based on
the celebrated Sion Minimax Theorem.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex lower semicontinuous function. Let
\hat{u}\in U
and
\gamma>0
. There exists
\hat{\rho}\in U^{*}
with
\|\hat{\rho}\|_{U^{*}}\leq\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in M}\Big\{\psi(u)+\gamma\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}\Big\}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left\{\psi(u)-\hat{\rho}(u-\hat{u})\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{*}
be the unit ball of
U^{*}
. Define
f:\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{B}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
by
f(u,\rho):=\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})
. Note that
U^{*}
endowed with the weak* topology is a linear topological space, and
\mathbb{B}^{*}
is weak* compact by Banach-Alaoglu Theorem. The function
f(\cdot,\rho):\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and lower semicontinuous for each
\rho\in U^{*}
. The function
f(u,\cdot):\mathbb{B}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
is weak* continuous and affine for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
. The hypotheses of Sion Minimax Theorem (
[
38
, Corollary 3.3]
) are then satisfied, and hence

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{B}^{*}}\left\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})\right\}=\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{B}^{*}}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})\}.

(37)

Let
h:\mathbb{B}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
h(\rho):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})\}
. Clearly,
h
is weak* upper semicontinuous as it is the infimum of weak* continuous functions; and since
\mathbb{B}^{*}
is weak* compact, there exists
\rho^{*}\in\mathbb{B}^{*}
such that
\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{B}^{*}}h(\rho)=h(\rho^{*})
. This implies

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{B}^{*}}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})\}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho^{*}(u-\hat{u})\}.

(38)

Finally, by (
37
) and (
38
),

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\Big\{\psi(u)+\gamma\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\sup_{\rho\in\mathbb{B}^{*}}\Big\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho(u-\hat{u})\Big\}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\Big\{\psi(u)+\gamma\rho^{*}(u-\hat{u})\Big\}.

The results follow defining
\hat{\rho}:=-\gamma\rho^{*}
.
∎

We can now prove the following variant of Ekeland principle.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
\big(U,\|\cdot\|_{U}\big)
is a Banach space,
\mathcal{U}
is a closed convex subset of
U
, and
{\mathcal{J}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is a lower semicontinuous Gateaux differentiable function. Let
\bar{u}\in U
and
r>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})\leq{\mathcal{J}}(s)\quad\text{for all $s\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|s-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq r$}.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
\varepsilon>0
satisfy
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}<r
and
{\mathcal{J}}(u)\leq{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})+\varepsilon.

Then for every
\lambda\in\big(0,r-\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\big)
there exist
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{U}
and
\hat{\rho}\in U^{*}
such that

(i)

\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}\leq\lambda
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\rho}\|_{U^{*}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}
;

(iii)

\hat{\rho}\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})+N_{\mathcal{U}}(\hat{u})
.

Proof.

Let
S:=\{s\in\mathcal{U}:\|s-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq r\}
. Since
S
is a closed subset of
U
,
S
is a complete metric space endowed with the metric induced from the norm of
U
, and
{\mathcal{J}}|_{S}
is lower semicontinuous. We can apply Ekeland Principle (
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
) to obtain that for every
\lambda\in\big(0,r-\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\big)
there exists
\hat{u}\in S
such that

(a)

\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}\leq\displaystyle\lambda
;

(b)

{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{u})\leq{\mathcal{J}}(u)
;

(c)

\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{u})\leq\hat{\mathcal{J}}(s)
for all
s\in S
, where
\hat{\mathcal{J}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}(s):={\mathcal{J}}(s)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}.

Let
r_{\lambda}:=r-\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}-\lambda
; clearly
r_{\lambda}>0
. If
s\in\mathcal{U}
satisfies
\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}\leq r_{\lambda}
, then

\displaystyle\|s-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}+\|\hat{u}-u\|_{U}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq r_{\lambda}+\lambda+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}=r.

Thus, from item
(c)
,
\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{u})\leq\hat{\mathcal{J}}(s)
for all
s\in\mathcal{U}
with
\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}\leq r_{\lambda}
. We conclude that
\hat{u}
is a local minimizer of
\hat{\mathcal{J}}
.
From this, we get

\displaystyle 0\leq\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\hskip 1.42271pt\frac{\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{u}+t(s-\hat{u}))-\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\hat{u})}{t}=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})(s-\hat{u})+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{U}.

This can be rewritten as

\displaystyle 0=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{U}}\Big\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})(s-\hat{u})+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}\Big\}

By Lemma
A.1
, there exists
\hat{\rho}\in U^{*}
with
\|\hat{\rho}\|_{U^{*}}\leq\varepsilon/\lambda
such that

\displaystyle 0=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{U}}\Big\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})(s-\hat{u})+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}\|s-\hat{u}\|_{U}\Big\}=\inf_{s\in\mathcal{U}}\Big\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})(s-\hat{u})-\hat{\rho}(s-\hat{u})\Big\}\leq\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})(v-\hat{u})-\hat{\rho}(v-\hat{u})

for all
v\in\mathcal{U}
. This implies
\hat{\rho}\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})+N_{\mathcal{U}}(\hat{u})
. Clearly,
\hat{u}
and
\hat{\rho}
satisfy items
(i)
-
(iii)
.
∎

A.2
Strong Hölder subregularity of the optimality mapping

This subsection is devoted to study the behavior of critical points under the presence of perturbations.
We derive necessary and sufficient conditions for stability of the variational inequality describing the first-order necessary condition at critical points. From this point on, we assume that
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is Gateaux differentiable, unless we specify otherwise.

Stability of the first-order necessary conditions.

In the literature, the stability of the first-order necessary conditions is studied as a property of a set-valued mapping encapsulating the (generalized) equation satisfied by local minimizers. This property is known as strong Hölder (metric) subregularity, see
[
24
, Section 3I]
; the property has also appeared in the literature by the name of strong (metric)
\theta
-subregularity, see
[
16
, Section 4]
.

Let us begin giving a suitable notion for the correspondence between solutions of the perturbed variational inequality and the perturbations.
The set-valued mapping
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\twoheadrightarrow U^{*}
given by

\displaystyle\Phi(u):=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)+N_{\mathcal{U}}(u)

is called
the optimality mapping
.
We now give the definition of stability that we wish to analyze, i.e., the so-called
strong (metric) subregularty
.

Definition A.3
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
satisfy
0\in\Phi(\bar{u})
. We say that the optimality mapping
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\twoheadrightarrow U^{*}
is strongly (Hölder) subregular at
\bar{u}
(with exponent
\theta\in(0,\infty)
) if there exist positive numbers
\alpha
and
\kappa
such that the following property holds. For all
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
\rho\in U^{*}
,

\displaystyle\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\rho\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)+N_{\mathcal{U}}(u)\quad\text{imply}\quad\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\kappa\|\rho\|_{U^{*}}^{\theta}.

(39)

We now proceed to state both sufficient and necessary conditions for this notion of stability.

Sufficient conditions.

The proof of the sufficient condition for stability, as shown in the next theorem, follows the arguments presented in
[
22
, Theorem 1]
to the letter, where it was previously proven in the context of optimal control.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
0\in\Phi(\bar{u})
, and
\mu\in(0,\infty)
. Suppose there exist positive numbers
\delta
and
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)(u-\bar{u})\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\delta$.}

(40)

Then the optimality mapping
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\twoheadrightarrow U^{*}
is strongly Hölder subregular at
\bar{u}
with exponent
1/\mu
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
\rho\in U^{*}
be arbitrary satisfying
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\delta
and
\rho\in\Phi(u)
. Then, as
\rho-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)\in N_{\mathcal{U}}(u)
and
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
, we have

\displaystyle 0\geq\big(\rho-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)\big)(\bar{u}-u)=\rho(\bar{u}-u)+\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)(u-\bar{u})\geq-\|\rho\|_{U^{*}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}+c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}.

Hence,
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq c^{-1/\mu}\|\rho\|_{U^{*}}^{1/\mu}.
The result follows defining
\alpha:=\delta
and
\kappa:=c^{-1/\mu}
.
∎

Growth assumption (
40
) appeared first in
[
22
, Assumption 2]
as a natural hypothesis for an affine optimal control problem; see also
[
19
, Proposition 4.3]
, where this kind of growth was proven for an elliptic optimal control problem under a linearized growth hypothesis. A similar assumption of this type appeared in
[
21
, Assumption A2]
, where stability results for an affine optimal control problem were studied. In Proposition
A.9
below, we give further details on growth (
40
) and its linearization.

Necessary conditions.

In order to establish necessary conditions for stability in the form of growth properties of functionals, we will use Ekeland principle in the form of Lemma
A.2
, following the approach used in
[
3
,
4
]
. In those papers, the subregularity property of the subdifferential of convex functions was characterized in terms of quadratic growth conditions; see also
[
34
]
, where a similar approach was used for the limiting subdifferential. In all those three papers only Lipschitz stability and quadratic growth conditions were considered. We make simple refinements in those arguments to consider both Hölder stability and higher-order growth conditions.

In the next theorem, we argue similarly to the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
; see also the proofs of
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
for parallel arguments.

Theorem A.5
.

Suppose
\big(U,\|\cdot\|_{U}\big)
is a Banach space,
\mathcal{U}
a closed convex subset of
U
and
{\mathcal{J}}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
a lower semicontinuous Gateaux differentiable function. Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
be a local minimizer of
{\mathcal{J}}
and
\mu\in(0,\infty)
. Suppose that the optimality mapping
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\twoheadrightarrow U^{*}
is strongly Hölder subregular at
\bar{u}
with exponent
1/\mu
. Then there exist positive numbers
\delta
and
c
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\delta$.}

(41)

Proof.

Let
\alpha
and
\kappa
be positive numbers such that property (
39
) holds. Suppose that (
41
) does not hold. Then there would exist
u\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\{\bar{u}\}
satisfying
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}<2\alpha/3
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})<\frac{1}{2^{2\mu+1}\kappa^{\mu}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}.

(42)

Let
\varepsilon:=2^{-(2\mu+1)}\kappa^{-\mu}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}
and
\lambda:=2^{-1}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}
. Note that

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{3}{2}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}-\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}<\alpha-\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}.

From Lemma
A.2
, we conclude the existence of
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{U}
and
\hat{\rho}\in U^{*}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\rho}\|_{U^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{4^{\mu}\kappa^{\mu}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu}
;

(iii)

\hat{\rho}\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\hat{u})+N_{\mathcal{U}}(\hat{u})
.

Observe that
\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\|\hat{u}-u\|_{U}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq 1/2\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}+\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}<\alpha
.
By subregularity of the optimality mapping at
\bar{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\kappa\|\hat{\rho}\|_{U^{*}}^{1/\mu}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}.

(43)

By item
(i)
, we have
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\|u-\hat{u}\|_{U}+\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq 1/2\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}+\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}
.
This implies
\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq 2\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}
. Combining this with (
43
), we get

\displaystyle\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{u}-\bar{u}\|_{U},

and hence
\hat{u}=u=\bar{u}
. A contradiction to (
42
).
∎

Growth condition (
41
) is well known in optimization. See, for example,
[
14
, Theorem 2.4]
or
[
32
, Theorem III]
in affine optimal control; and
[
31
, Theorem 1]
or
[
33
, Theorem I]
in the quantitative study of eigenvalues stability for the Schrödinger operator.

A.3
Hölder growth of real-valued functions

In this section, we study how to reduce growth conditions (
40
) and (
41
) to linearized versions. This is to facilitate the understanding of their feasibility. We say that
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}
has second variation at
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
if there exists a function
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u}):U\times U\to\mathbb{R}
, positively homogeneous in each variable, such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{u})(v,w)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}(\bar{u}+\varepsilon v)w-{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}(\bar{u})w}{\varepsilon}\quad\forall v,w\in\mathcal{U}-\bar{u}.

From now on, we will assume that
\mathcal{J}
has second variation at every element of
\mathcal{U}
. We abbreviate
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}:=\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})(v,v)
.

A Hölder-type second order condition.

In order to transfer conditions (
40
) and (
41
) from being satisfied by a nonlinear function to a second order polynomial, we will employ the following weakened version of “twice continuously differentiable”.

Definition A.6
.

Let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
and
\mu\in[1,\infty)
. We say that
{\mathcal{J}}
has changing curvature of order
\mu
at
\bar{u}
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u}+v)v^{2}-\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|\leq\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

(44)

for all
v\in U
with
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
and
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
.

Properties like (
44
) have appeared ubiquitously in the optimal control literature of bang-bang controls. See, for example,
[
14
, p. 4207]
, where it appeared as a standard assumption in abstract optimal control; or
[
20
, Lemma 11]
where it appeared as natural property in the context of parabolic optimal control problems.

Proposition A.7
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{J}}
has changing curvature of order
\mu\in[1,\infty)
at
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
, then the following statements hold:

(i)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+v)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|\leq\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

for all
v\in U
satisfying
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
and
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
.

(ii)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u}+v)v-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v-\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|\leq\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

for all
v\in U
satisfying
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
and
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
.

Proof.

For each
v\in U
with
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
, define
h_{v}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by
h_{v}(t)={\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+tv)
. We can apply the Taylor Theorem to conclude that for each
v\in\mathcal{U}-\bar{u}
there exists
t_{v}\in[0,1]
such that
h(1)-h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)+h^{\prime\prime}(t_{v})/2
. That is,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+v)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u}+t_{v}v)v^{2}\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{U}-\bar{u}.

(45)

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. By definition of changing curvature of order
\mu
at a point, we can find
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u}+t_{v}v)(t_{v}v)^{2}-\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})(t_{v}v)^{2}|\leq 2\,\varepsilon\|t_{v}v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

(46)

whenever
v\in\mathcal{U}-\bar{u}
satisfies
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
. Then, combining (
45
) and (
46
), we get

\displaystyle t_{v}^{2}|{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+v)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u}+t_{v}v)v^{2}-\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|\leq t_{v}^{\mu+1}\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

for all
v\in U
satisfying
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
and
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
. Since
\mu\geq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u}+v)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}|\leq t_{v}^{\mu-1}\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\leq\varepsilon\|v\|_{U}^{\mu+1}

for all
v\in U
satisfying
\bar{u}+v\in\mathcal{U}
and
\|v\|_{U}\leq\delta
. Thus, item
(i)
holds.

The proof of item
(ii)
is analogous; it follows defining
k_{v}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by
k_{v}(t):=\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u}+tv)v
for each
v\in\mathcal{U}-\bar{u}
, and applying the Mean Value Theorem to each function
k_{v}
.
∎

Growth of functionals and their differentials.

One easy consequence of Proposition
A.7
is the following characterization of growth condition (
41
) which follows directly from item
(i)
of Proposition
A.7
.

Proposition A.8
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{J}}
has changing curvature of order
\mu\in[1,\infty)
at
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exist positive numbers
\alpha
and
c
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(u)-{\mathcal{J}}(\bar{u})\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\alpha$.}

(ii)

There exist positive numbers
\alpha
and
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})^{2}\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\alpha$.}

The growth in previous proposition has been proved in the literature of optimal control several times, this usually involves using a growth condition on the second variation over a critical cone and the so-called structural assumption; this is a condition one the level sets of the adjoint variable. See
[
6
, Section 3]
and
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
.

On the other hand, another trivial, but important, consequence of Proposition
A.7
is the characterization of growth condition (
40
).

Proposition A.9
.

If
{\mathcal{J}}
has changing curvature of order
\mu\in[1,\infty)
at
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{U}
, then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There exist positive numbers
\alpha
and
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)(u-\bar{u})\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\alpha$.}

(ii)

There exist positive numbers
\alpha
and
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\bar{u})v+\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})v^{2}\geq c\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}^{\mu+1}\quad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{U}$ with $\|u-\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq\alpha$.}

We mention that this characterization has appeared before in PDE-constrained optimization; see
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
or
[
20
, Lemma 12]
. Numerous conditions exist to verify the validity of the growth condition given in Proposition
A.9
; see, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
or
[
19
, Theorem 6.3]
. A comprehensive discussion on the assumptions pertinent to this growth condition is available in
[
19
, Section 6]
. An explicit example where the growth holds for
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
was given in
[
37
, Example 1.2]
(an optimal control problem constrained by ODEs).

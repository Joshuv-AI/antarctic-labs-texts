Local Inaccessibility of Random Classical Information : Conditional Nonlocality demands Entanglement
Subhendu B. Ghosh, Snehasish Roy Chowdhury, Tathagata Gupta, Anandamay Das Bhowmik, Sutapa Saha, Some Sankar Bhattacharya, Tamal Guha

Abstract
Discrimination of quantum states under local operations and classical communication (LOCC) is an intriguing question in the context of local retrieval of classical information, encoded in the multipartite quantum systems. All the local quantum state discrimination premises, considered so far, mimic a basic communication set-up, where the spatially separated decoding devices are independent of any additional input. Here, exploring a generalized communication scenario we introduce a framework for input-dependent local quantum state discrimination, which we call local random authentication (LRA). Referring to the term nonlocality , often used to indicate the impossibility of local state discrimination, we coin the term conditional nonlocality for the impossibility associated with the task LRA. We report that conditional nonlocality necessitates the presence of entangled states in the ensemble, a feature absent from erstwhile nonlocality arguments based on local state discrimination. Conversely, all the states in a complete basis set being entangled implies conditional nonlocality . However, the impossibility of LRA also exhibits more conditional nonlocality with less entanglement. The relation between the possibility of LRA and local state discrimination for sets of multipartite quantum states, both in the perfect and conclusive cases, has also been established. The results highlight a completely new aspect of the interplay between the security of information in a network and quantum entanglement under the LOCC paradigm.

3
Results

We will start by comparing the possibility of LRA with respect to LOCC discrimination of a given set of orthogonal quantum states.

Theorem 1
.

Perfect LOCC discrimination of a set of quantum states implies their perfect local random authentication, while the converse is not true.

Proof.

Suppose the set
\mathcal{S}:=\{\ket{\psi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of orthogonal quantum states can be discriminated under LOCC. Therefore, by identifying the unknown state (say,
\ket{\psi_{k}}
) given from the set
\mathcal{S}
, the separated parties can answer any of the
N
-possible authentication questions. In particular, their answer will be
0
for every
\mathcal{Q}_{j\neq k}
and
1
for
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
.

To show that the converse does not hold true, we will consider the following example:

\displaystyle\ket{E_{1}}:=\ket{\phi^{+}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle\ket{E_{2}}:=\ket{\phi^{-}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle\ket{E_{3}}:=\ket{\psi^{+}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

(1)

It follows from
Ghosh et al. 2001
;
Walgate and Hardy 2002
that these three states can not be distinguished perfectly under LOCC. However, they can be authenticated locally by adopting the strategy:
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\to\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y},\penalty\ \mathcal{Q}_{2}\to\sigma_{x}\otimes\sigma_{x}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{3}\to\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z}
, where
\sigma_{i}\otimes\sigma_{i}
implies the local measurement of the Pauli observable
\sigma_{i}
for both the parties. In particular, the spatially separated parties will answer
y=0
whenever their outcomes are correlated and
y=1
otherwise.
∎

3.1
Necessity of entanglement

As mentioned earlier, all the nonlocal aspects in terms of local state discrimination (known till now) also hold for pure product states. Taken in isolation, one can even locally activate such nonlocal features starting from locally distinguishable product preparations
Li and Zheng 2022
;
Ghosh et al. 2022
. Therefore, such nonlocal features can not certify the presence of entanglement in the preparation device. However, in the following, we will first show that the LRA can be accomplished perfectly for any product state, and as a consequence, we will conclude that any
conditional nonlocal
set must contain entanglement.

Theorem 2
.

Any product state chosen from a set of orthogonal quantum states can be authenticated locally.

Proof.

Let us consider a set of orthogonal quantum states
\mathcal{S}:=\{\ket{\psi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{n}
shared between
N
-spatially separated parties. Moreover, the set contains at least one product state
\ket{\psi_{j}}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\ket{\xi_{k}^{(j)}}
. Now, for the question
\mathcal{Q}_{j}
, i.e., whether the shared state is
\ket{\psi_{j}}
or not, they can simply perform the measurements
M_{B_{k}}^{(j)}:=\{|\xi_{k}^{(j)}\rangle\langle\xi_{k}^{(j)}|,\mathbb{I}-|\xi_{k}^{(j)}\rangle\langle\xi_{k}^{(j)}|\},\penalty\ \forall k\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
on their local constituents.

Since all the states
\{\ket{\psi_{i}}\}_{i\neq j}
are orthogonal to
\ket{\psi_{j}}
, the projector
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}|\xi_{k}^{(j)}\rangle\langle\xi_{k}^{(j)}|
of the measurement
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}M_{B_{k}}^{(j)}
clicks only when they share the state
\ket{\psi_{j}}
. Conversely, no other effects click for this state. Hence, they will answer
1
when
\mathcal{P}_{j}
clicks and
0
otherwise.
∎

From Theorem
2
, one can easily derive the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Any set of orthogonal product states admits complete local authentication.

After establishing the strict necessity of entanglement to exhibit the impossibility of LRA, one may be tempted to ask about the relationship between the complexity associated with the LRA task and the amount of entanglement present in the given ensemble. Without going to a rigorous quantification of the complexity, we will now show that such a monotonic relation does not hold true in general. In particular, we will identify a set
\mathcal{P}
, such that any
\mathcal{S}
containing
\mathcal{P}
, can not be authenticated locally if the elements of the set
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{P}
are product states. However,
\mathcal{S}
allows complete LRA if a particular entangled state is present in
\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{P}
. Mimicking the phrase "
more nonlocality with less entanglement
", used in
Horodecki et al. 2003
, we put the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

The impossibility of LRA exhibits more (conditional) nonlocality with less entanglement phenomenon.

Proof.

Here we will consider a minimalistic scenario:
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of
three
two-qubit states, along with

and

\displaystyle\bra{\phi^{-}}(M_{m}^{(1)})_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B_{2}}\ket{\phi^{+}}=0,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\bra{01}(M_{m}^{(1)})_{B_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B_{2}}\ket{\phi^{+}}=0

(2)

Now considering

\displaystyle M_{m}^{(1)}:=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\

and using Eq. (

On the other hand, if we consider

Notably, the conditions in Eq. (

It is important to mention that the phenomenon of more (conditional) nonlocality with less entanglement for the task LRA is more surprising than the similar results for LOCC discrimination

3.2
Impossibility of Local Random Authentication

In the multipartite settings quantum entanglement possesses various topological intricacies, which demand the existence of infinitely many inequivalent classes under LOCC
Sauerwein et al. 2018
. Depending upon their individual structures, perfect accomplishment of LRA may further require a reorientation of the distant receivers. However, presently we will only focus on their complete spatial separation: none of the receivers can join with any other in the same lab. In such a situation, we will establish a no-go result on the possibilities of even partial LRA, with the help of the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

If a set of orthogonal states can not be discriminated conclusively under LOCC, even with an arbitrarily small probability, then the set does not even allow partial LRA.

Proof.

We will prove the result conversely. Consider a set of orthogonal quantum states
\mathcal{S}:=\{\ket{\psi_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
which allows the accomplishment of partial LRA. This means, there exists at least one
\ket{\psi_{k}}\in\mathcal{S}
which can be authenticated locally, i.e., the question
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
can be answered correctly, using a two-outcome LOCC implementable quantum measurement
\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\{M_{k}^{(0)},M_{k}^{(1)}\}
.

Now, consider a situation of LOCC state discrimination, where an arbitrary state from
\mathcal{S}
is distributed among spatially separated parties. It is obvious from above that they can at least perform the LOCC implementable measurement
\mathcal{M}_{k}
and can conclusively identify the unknown state. In particular, the state will be
\ket{\psi_{k}}
whenever
M_{k}^{(1)}
clicks, otherwise inconclusive. This implies, the set
\mathcal{S}
is conclusively LOCC distinguishable with a probability no less than
\frac{1}{N}
.
∎

Numerically this value exactly matches with the random guessing probability, however, unlike the random guessing, the conclusive state discrimination never makes a wrong identification
Chefles 1998
.

Using the above lemma we will now establish the main theorem regarding the possibilities of LRA for a complete orthonormal basis of multipartite quantum states.

Theorem 3
.

Consider a complete set of orthogonal basis states
\mathcal{S}_{n}:=\{\ket{\psi_{i}}_{B_{1},B_{2},\cdots,B_{n}}\}\in\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}}
. If none of these states is fully product, then the set does not allow even partial LRA.

Proof.

Consider a situation with

2n
numbers of parties
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{n},B_{1},\cdots,B_{n}\}
, where each pair of parties
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
share a maximally entangled state
\ket{\phi^{+}_{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\sum_{l=0}^{d_{k}-1}\ket{l}_{A_{k}}\ket{l}_{B_{k}},

\forall k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
. Evidently, the joint state will take the form,

\ket{\Phi^{+}}_{A_{1}B_{1}\cdots A_{n}B_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}}}\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}(\sum_{l=0}^{d_{k}-1}\ket{l}_{A_{k}}\ket{l}_{B_{k}}).

(3)

It is easy to show that the state in Eq.(
3
) can be effectively treated as a maximally entangled state

\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}}}\sum_{l=0}^{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}-1}\ket{l}_{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n}}\ket{l}_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}

for
(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}})^{\otimes 2}
in the
\mathcal{A}:=\{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{n}\}
vs.
\mathcal{B}:=\{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}\}
bipartition. Finally, using the
\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}^{*}
invariance of
\ket{\Phi^{+}}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
, the state can be rewritten as

\ket{\Phi^{+}}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}}}\sum_{l=0}^{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}-1}\ket{\xi_{l}}_{\mathcal{A}}\ket{\xi_{l}^{*}}_{\mathcal{B}},

(4)

where
\{\ket{\xi_{l}}\}_{l=0}^{d_{1}d_{2}\cdots d_{n}-1}
is any arbitrary orthonormal basis in
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}^{d_{k}}
and
∗
denotes the complex conjugation. Evidently, the orthogonal basis set
\{\ket{\psi}_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}\}
can also be identified as
\{\ket{\xi_{l}}_{\mathcal{B}}\}
.

Therefore, if there is a
\ket{\psi_{m}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
which is not fully separable, i.e., entangled in some
\{B_{1}\cdots B_{k}\}
vs.
\{B_{k+1}\cdots B_{n}\}
bipartition, then by identifying it conclusively under LOCC they can inform
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{n}\}
, who are sharing the state
\ket{\psi_{m}^{*}}
, entangled in the
\{A_{1}\cdots A_{k}\}
vs
\{A_{k+1}\cdots A_{n}\}
bipartition. However, the state in Eq. (
3

4

vs.

must be fully product. In other words, the conclusive local discrimination of

necessitates the presence of at least one product state.

Finally, with the help of Lemma

We note that the above proof can be seen as a generalization of the proof introduced in

In Lemma
1
, we have shown that the complexity associated with conclusive state discrimination, under LOCC, is not higher than that of partial LRA. A natural question arises at this point whether the converse holds true or not. In the following, we will answer it in negative by providing an example even in the simplest bipartite scenario.

Proposition 2
.

The impossibility of partial LRA does not imply the impossibility of conclusive local discrimination.

Proof.

Consider a set
\mathcal{S}=\{\ket{\psi_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{8}
of bipartite entangled states in
\mathbb{C}^{3}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{3}
shared between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, where

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{k}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ket{E_{k}}_{B_{1}B_{2}},\forall k\in\{1,2,3\}

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{4}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\sqrt{2}\ket{\psi^{-}}+\ket{22})_{B_{1}B_{2}},

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{5}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{12}+\ket{21})_{B_{1}B_{2}},

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{6}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{12}-\ket{21})_{B_{1}B_{2}},

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{7}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{02}+\ket{20})_{B_{1}B_{2}},

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{8}}_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{02}-\ket{20})_{B_{1}B_{2}}

(5)

Note that, just by performing the measurement

A closer look on the set

depicts that while it does not allow even the partial LRA, there is a finite probability that some of the members of the set can be authenticated conclusively. For instance, if both the parties perform the measurement

in negative. This readily instigates a possibility of the conclusive LRA for a set of multipartite quantum states. A set of orthogonal multipartite quantum states is said to allow conclusive LRA, if atleast one of the states can be authenticated conclusively with a probability
p>0
. In other words, for the accomplishment of the conclusive LRA, atleast one of the randomly sampled questions

can be conclusively answered with a nonzero probability.

As a consequence of Lemma
1
and Proposition
2
, one may conclude that the impossibility of LRA (even the partial) is a stronger aspect of nonlocality than that of the conclusive LOCC discrimination. However, the comparison between conclusive LRA and conclusive LOCC discrimination follows a relation similar to that of Theorem
1
. First, it is easy to understand that any set of multipartite orthogonal quantum states can accomplish conclusive LRA. This is because, for every quantum state
\ket{\psi_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}}
one can find an orthogonal product state
\ket{\Phi_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}}:=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\ket{\phi_{B_{k}}}
and by performing

in each of the individual lab of
k^{\text{th}}
Bob they can conclusively answer that the state is not
\ket{\psi_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}}
, whenever the projector
|\Phi_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}\rangle\langle\Phi_{B_{1}B_{2}\cdots B_{n}}|
clicks. On the other hand, consider any set of complete orthogonal basis, containing no product states. It is clear from the proof of Theorem
3
that none of them can be conclusively discriminated under LOCC. Notably, whenever such a difference between conclusive LRA and conclusive LOCC discrimination persists, then it is not possible to answer any of the questions
\{\mathcal{Q}_{k}\}_{k}
affirmatively in the LRA settings, with certainty. Moreover, if any of the questions can be conclusively answered in affirmation, then the same LOCC implementable protocol will run for the conclusive LOCC discrimination of those states.

4
Discussions

We have introduced a scenario where the spatially separated parties are asked to locally authenticate a state chosen randomly according to a classical index from a given set of orthogonal quantum states. Notably, the scenario is analogous to the quantum hypothesis testing
tej2018quantum
(also see the Refs. therein), however under causally constrained measurement setup, i.e., LOCC. More precisely, given a quantum state from the orthogonal set of states, the spatially separated parties are asked to test the hypothesis in terms of a binary answered question
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
. The impossibility of such a task necessarily implies the presence of entanglement in the set. The present state discrimination task, to date, is the only known variant which can be perfectly accomplished for product ensembles. However, we have encountered instances where the presence of a product state, instead of entanglement, in a set of states forbids the possibility of its local authentication. Besides sharing different kinds of relations with perfect and conclusive LOCC state discrimination, the task of LRA highlights the question based secrecy in a network. In other words, the impossibility arising from the present task closely resembles a practical way of maintaining secrecy via security questions. For instance, consider an authority wishes to secretly share an
n
-bit string among multiple separated parties, which can be further decoded depending upon the input announced by the authority itself. Depending upon the desired secrecy, the authority may encode the
n
-bit string over a properly chosen set
\mathcal{S}:=\{\ket{\psi_{k}}\}_{k}
of orthogonal quantum states. Then for the
i^{th}
-bit the authority can distribute a state
\ket{\psi_{i}}\in\mathcal{S}
and broadcast the classical input (i.e., the question)
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
to the spatially separated parties. Answering the question correctly under LOCC, the parties are then allowed to generate the
i^{\text{th}}
-bit of the string. Impossibility of which, in turn, gives a stronger secrecy of the bit string if the authority uses a conditional nonlocal set of states as
\mathcal{S}
.

With the introduction of the input-dependent local state discrimination setting in the present work, a number of promising research directions open. First, the type of the input questions considered here, is the simplest one, i.e., about the identity of the state. However, there could be various other possible input questions, viz., parity identification, subspace discrimination etc., which may differ by their local answerable complexities. Second, the present framework only considers the fully local operations along with unrestricted classical communication, both of which can be tuned further depending upon practical situations and causal constraints. More precisely, in the multipartite settings, the various forms of party accumulations, along with specific directions of classical communication may lead to identifications of different resources, viz., bi-separability, genuine entanglement etc. Third, due to the lack of concise mathematical description of LOCC, the question of local state discrimination is often asked in the paradigm of separable measurements
Yu et al. 2012
;
Duan et al. 2009
;
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2015
;
Cohen 2015
or positive partial transpose (PPT)-preserving operations
Yu et al. 2014
. Similarly, exploring the status of the task LRA in such broader classes of operations can be noteworthy. Lastly, it is instructive to note that for the two-qubit case, our results point out the maximum cardinality of a locally authenticable set with all entangled members (see Theorem
1
and
3
), which is exactly
three
. However, the same in general bipartite settings is not known. Besides foundational interest, answering these questions might pave way for a robust security in quantum networks.

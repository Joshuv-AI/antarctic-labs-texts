Abstract
The newly observed hidden-charm tetraquark state Z c ​ s ​ ( 3985 ) Z_{cs}(3985) , together with Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900) and X ⁡ ( 4020 ) X(4020) , are studied in the combined theoretical framework of the effective range expansion, compositeness relation and the decay width saturation. The elastic effective-range-expansion approach leads to sensible results for the scattering lengths, effective ranges and the compositeness coefficients, i . e . i.e. , the probabilities to find the two-charm-meson molecule components in the tetraquark states. The coupled-channel formalism by including the J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi and D ​ D ¯ ∗ / D ¯ ​ D ∗ D\bar{D}^{*}/\bar{D}D^{*} to fulfill the constraints of the compositeness relation and the decay width, confirms the elastic effective-range-expansion results for the Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900) , by using the experimental inputs for the ratios of the decay widths between D ​ D ¯ ∗ / D ¯ ​ D ∗ D\bar{D}^{*}/\bar{D}D^{*} and J / ψ ​ π J/\psi\pi . With the results from the elastic effective-range-expansion study as input for the compositeness, we generalize the discussions to the Z c ​ s ​ ( 3985 ) Z_{cs}(3985) by including the J / ψ ​ K − J/\psi K^{-} and D s − ​ D ∗ 0 / D s ∗ ⁣ − ​ D 0 D_{s}^{-}D^{*0}/D_{s}^{*-}D^{0} , and predict the partial decay widths of the J / ψ ​ K − J/\psi K^{-} . Similar calculations are also carried out for the X ⁡ ( 4020 ) X(4020) by including the h c ​ π h_{c}\pi and D ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D^{*}\bar{D}^{*} , and the partial decay widths of the h c ​ π h_{c}\pi is predicted. Our results can provide useful guidelines for future experimental measurements.

2
Elastic two-charm-meson scattering in the effective range expansion

A featured property shared by many of the recently observed heavy-flavor exotic states is that most of them lie near some specific thresholds of the underlying two hadronic states. The effective range expansion, relying on the three-momentum expansion at the two-body threshold, naturally provides a useful framework to investigate the physics around the threshold energy region. Up to next-to-leading order, there are two parameters in the elastic ERE expansion, namely the scattering length
a
and effective range
r
. Combining the ERE expansion and unitarity, one can uniquely determine
a
and
r
in terms of the mass and the width of the resonance
[
18
,
19
,
21
]
.

For the two-hadron
S
-wave scattering, the single-channel unitarized ERE formula up to next-to-leading order has the form

\displaystyle T(E)=\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{2}r\,k^{2}-i\,k}\,,

(1)

with
k
being the center-of-mass (CM) three-momentum. The nonrelativistic relation between the energy
E
and three-momentum
k
reads

\displaystyle k=\sqrt{2\mu_{m}(E-m_{\rm th})}\,,

(2)

with
\mu_{m}=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
,
m_{\rm th}=m_{1}+m_{2}
and
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
the masses of the scattering states. The elastic ERE amplitude of Eq. (
1
) obeys the unitarity condition,

{\rm Im}\,T(E)^{-1}=-k\,,\quad(E>m_{\rm th})\,.

(3)

The virtual and resonance poles are located in the second Riemann sheet (RS), in which the ERE amplitude is given by

\displaystyle T^{\rm II}(E)=\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{2}r\,k^{2}+i\,k}\,.

(4)

It should be noted that the imaginary part of the
k
should be positive in the formulations (
1
) and (
4
). The resonance pole corresponds to the zeros of the denominator of
T^{\rm II}(E)
.
After straightforward algebraic manipulations, one can derive the following expressions for
a
and
r

in order to reproduce a resonance pole at
E_{R}=M_{R}-i\Gamma_{R}/2

[
19
]
,

\displaystyle a=-\frac{2k_{\rm i}}{k_{\rm r}^{2}+k_{\rm i}^{2}}\,,\qquad r=-\frac{1}{k_{\rm i}}\,,

(5)

where the real (
k_{\rm r}
) and imaginary (
k_{\rm i}
) parts of the pole position in the
k
variable are defined as

\displaystyle k_{R}=\sqrt{2\mu_{m}(E_{R}-m_{\rm th})}\equiv k_{\rm r}+ik_{\rm i}\,,\quad(k_{\rm i}>0)\,.

(6)

The Laurent expansion of
T^{\rm II}(E)
around the pole position is

\displaystyle T^{\rm II}(E)=\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{k-k_{R}}+\cdots\,,

(7)

where the ellipsis corresponds to the regular terms in the
k-k_{R}
expansion. Combining Eqs. (
4
)-(
7
), it is straightforward to derive the relation between the residue
\gamma_{k}^{2}
and the pole position

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{2}=-\frac{k_{i}}{k_{r}}\,.

(8)

The compositeness coefficient
X
, i.e. the probability to find the two-body component in the resonance, comes as an important byproduct from the unitarized ERE amplitude, which turns out to be
[
19
]

\displaystyle X=\gamma_{k}^{2}=-\frac{k_{i}}{k_{r}}~,

(9)

which is
\leq 1
for
M_{R}\geq m_{\rm th}

[
19
]
. We show in the Appendix
A
how the previous formula is connected with the direct calculation of the weight of a channel in a nonrelativistic bound state by calculating the expectation value of the number operator of free particles in the state, following Ref.
[
20
]
. Its extension to resonances is illustrated with a exactly solvable toy-model in Quantum Field Theory by including a source that mimics a decaying resonance.

In terms of
a
and
r
, Eq. (
9
) can be cast as

\displaystyle X=\left(\frac{2r}{a}-1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(10)

According to Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
), the scattering length, effective range and the compositeness coefficient can be obtained, once the mass and width of the resonance are provided. The constraint
0\leq X\leq 1
requires that i)
r/a\geq 1/2
, as it trivially follows from Eq. (
5
), and ii)
r\leq a
(or
|r|\geq|a|
), which implies that
M_{R}\geq m_{\rm th}
as derived in detailed in Ref.
[
19
]
. In the general case when there are other types of poles, such as virtual and bound states,
a
and
r
could take different relative sizes and signs
[
22
]
. Our Equation (
10
) is not intended to cope in such cases that do not enter in our specific study here. However, in the literature one can find different approaches for the virtual and bound states in Refs.
[
5
,
6
,
12
,
21
]
.

Coupled-channel study of the hidden-charm tetraquark candidates

A more realistic study of the near-threshold hidden-charm tetraquark state requires the coupled-channel calculation. Since the
Z_{c}(3900)
and
X(4020)
are experimentally observed in the
J/\psi\pi
,
D\bar{D}^{*}
and
h_{c}\pi
,
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
event distributions, respectively, it is natural to include the
J/\psi\pi
and
D\bar{D}^{*}
coupled channels to study the
Z_{c}(3900)
, and the
h_{c}\pi
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
to address the
X(4020)
. For the
Z_{cs}(3985)
, the
J/\psi K^{-}
and
D_{s}^{-}D^{*0}/D_{s}^{*-}{D}^{0}
channels will be considered.

The coupled-channel formalism adopted here relies on the decay width saturation and the compositeness relation, which has been successfully used to describe the hidden-charm pentaquark candidates
P_{c}(4312)
,
P_{c}(4440)
and
P_{c}(4457)

[
17
,
14
]
, and the fully charmed tetraquark state
X(6900)

[
16
]
. After a proper phase transformation of the
S
matrix, we have showed in Ref.
[
11
]
that the partial compositeness coefficient
X_{j}
contributed by the
j
th channel to a resonance can be written as

\displaystyle X_{j}=|g_{j}|^{2}\,\bigg|\frac{\partial G_{j}^{\rm II}(s_{R})}{\partial s}\bigg|\,,

(11)

where
s=E^{2}
is the usual Mandelstam variable,
s_{R}=E_{R}^{2}
with
\Re s_{R}>m_{\rm th}^{2}
,
and the couplings
g_{j}^{2}
are the residue of the partial-wave amplitude at the
pole position,

\lim_{s\to s_{R}}(s-s_{R})T^{\rm{II}}(s)_{jj}=-g_{j}^{2}
, cf. Eq. (
8
).
The function
G_{j}^{\rm II}(s)
corresponds to the unitarity loop function
G(s)
evaluated for the
j
th channel in the second RS. Due to the presence of the lighter threshold that is distant from the resonance pole, we will adopt the relativistic kinematical relations in the coupled-channel case. An explicit relativistic expression for the
G(s)
function from the dimensional regularization is given by

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left[a_{\rm SC}(\mu^{2})+\log\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-x_{+}\log\frac{x_{+}-1}{x_{+}}-x_{-}\log\frac{x_{-}-1}{x_{-}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle x_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s+m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}{2s}\pm\frac{q(s)}{\sqrt{s}}\,,

(12)

where
q(s)
is the relativistic three-momentum

\displaystyle q(s)=\frac{\sqrt{[s-(m_{1}+m_{2})^{2}][s-(m_{1}-m_{2})^{2}]}}{2\sqrt{s}}\,,

(13)

and
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are the masses of the two particles involved in the channel of interest.
After taking the derivative of the
G(s)
function, the unknown constant
a_{\rm SC}(\mu)
disappears and hence does not enter the compositeness coefficient
X
in Eq. (
11
). The total compositeness relation in the two-channel case has the form

\displaystyle X=X_{1}+X_{2}\equiv|g_{1}|^{2}\,\bigg|\frac{\partial G_{1}^{\rm II}(s_{R})}{\partial s}\bigg|+|g_{2}|^{2}\,\bigg|\frac{\partial G_{2}^{\rm II}(s_{R})}{\partial s}\bigg|\,,

(14)

where the lighter channel is labeled as
1
and the heavier two-charm-meson channel is labeled as
2
.
The coupling
g_{2}^{2}
is related to the residue in Eq. (
8
) via

\displaystyle|g^{2}_{2}|=\frac{16\pi|E_{R}^{2}k_{R}|}{\mu_{m}}~.

(15)

For the lighter channel, whose threshold is distant from the resonance mass, its partial decay width is calculated by the standard formula

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}=|g_{1}|^{2}\frac{q_{1}(M_{R}^{2})}{8\pi M_{R}^{2}}\,,

(16)

with
q_{1}(M_{R}^{2})
the CM three-momentum of the lighter channel. Due to the closeness of the resonance mass to the two-charm-meson threshold, the standard formula for the decay width becomes inadequate.
Instead, the Lorentzian energy distribution for the resonance mass is used to estimate the partial width
\Gamma_{2}
for the two-charm-meson channel

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}=|g_{2}|^{2}\int_{m_{\rm th}}^{M_{R}+n\,\Gamma_{R}}dE\,\frac{q_{2}(E^{2})}{16\pi^{2}\,E^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{R}}{(M_{R}-E)^{2}+\frac{\Gamma_{R}^{2}}{4}}\,,

(17)

which naturally reduces to the standard decay formulation in Eq. (
16
) for the narrow-width resonance, i.e. when taking
\Gamma_{R}\to 0
. In principle the upper integration limit in Eq. (
17
) should be taken to infinity. In Ref.
[
19
]
, the value of
n=8
is estimated for the
Z_{b}(10610)/Z_{b}(101650)
by reproducing the experimental widths using Eq. (
17
), with the coupling from the elastic ERE study. Following the same procedure for the
Z_{c}(3900)
, the value of
n
is found to be quite large around 60. Nevertheless, it is pointed out that the resulting width from Eq. (
17
) with
n=10
is already quite close to the experimental value, with the deviation less than 7 percent. In practice, we will take
n=10
throughout by taking into account the relatively narrow widths of the
Z_{c}(3900)
,
X(4020)
and
Z_{cs}(3985)
. Increasing the values of
n
by 1 or 2 orders of magnitude the resulting integral in Eq. (
17
) changes less than 10 percent, so that the results and conclusions change little by extending the upper integral limit in Eq. (
17
). In particular, they are well inside the estimated error uncertainty in the results given.

The requirement of the resonance width saturation gives

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}=|g_{1}|^{2}\frac{q_{1}(M_{R}^{2})}{8\pi M_{R}^{2}}+|g_{2}|^{2}\int_{m_{\rm th}}^{M_{R}+n\,\Gamma_{R}}dE\,\frac{q_{2}(E^{2})}{16\pi^{2}\,E^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{R}}{(M_{R}-E)^{2}+\frac{\Gamma_{R}^{2}}{4}}\,.

(18)

Combining Eqs. (
14
) and (
18
) allows one to solve
|g_{1}|
and
|g_{2}|
, with which one can obtain the valuable information on the partial decay widths and individual compositeness coefficients
X_{i}
for every channel.
However, in order to solve Eqs. (
14
) and (
18
) we need first to provide the total compositeness coefficient
X
, whose value is difficult to estimate beforehand.
Different ways to solve the equations are proposed in the previous studies.
For example, for the pentaquark candidates
P_{c}
, some arbitrary testing values between 0 and 1 are taken for the
X

[
14
]
.
For the fully charmed tetraquark candidate
X(6900)
, a sophisticated dynamical model respecting unitarity is constructed to fit the experimental line shapes first, so that the couplings
|g_{j}|
are then extracted from the dynamical coupled-channel amplitudes.
It is worth pointing out that the neat recipe of the coupled-channel method of Eqs. (
14
) and (
18
) agrees perfectly with the sophisticated coupled-channel amplitudes
[
16
]
.

Regarding the
Z_{c}(3900)
, the ratio of the decay widths between the
D\bar{D}^{*}
and
J/\psi\pi
is measured to be
6.2\pm 2.9
by the BESIII experiment
[
2
]
.
Indeed the ratio of the decay widths also provides another key input to solve the coupled-channel systems (
14
) and (
18
), so that now it is not necessary to fix the total compositeness
X

a

priori
, and it can be predicted. With the experimental input,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{D\bar{D}^{*}}/\Gamma_{J/\psi\pi}=6.2\pm 2.9~,

(19)

it is easy to obtain from Eq. (
18
) that

\displaystyle|g_{1}|=1.46_{-0.23}^{+0.43}\,,\qquad|g_{2}|=7.89_{-0.44}^{+0.18}\,.

(20)

Hence, by implementing these values into Eq. (
14
) one has that

\displaystyle X_{1}=0.002\pm 0.001\,,\qquad X_{2}=0.436_{-0.047}^{+0.021}\,,\qquad X=X_{1}+X_{2}=0.438_{-0.047}^{+0.021}\,.

(21)

Now it is interesting to compare the coupled-channel predictions in Eq. (
21
) with the elastic ERE result in Table
1
. The total compositeness coefficient

, which is overwhelmingly dominated by the near-threshold
D\bar{D}^{*}/\bar{D}D^{*}
channel, is almost identical as the value from the elastic ERE study. That is, the ERE study reproduces the coupled-channel analysis because the role played by the lightest channel is rather marginal, as reflected by the small values of
|g_{1}|
and
X_{1}
compared to those of
|g_{2}|
and
X_{2}
in Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
), respectively.

An alternative way to estimate the compositeness
X_{2}
is to rewrite the partial decay width (

4
Summary and conclusions

In this work we have studied the three hidden-charm tetraquark candidates
Z_{c}(3900)
,
X(4020)
and the newly observed
Z_{cs}(3985)
. The
D\bar{D}^{*}/\bar{D}D^{*}
,
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
and
D_{s}^{-}D^{*0}/D_{s}^{*-}{D}^{0}
channels are incorporated to investigate the
Z_{c}(3900)
,
X(4020)
and
Z_{cs}(3985)
, respectively, within the elastic effective-range-expansion scheme. The scattering lengths
a
, effective ranges
r
, and compositeness coefficients
X
,
i.e.
, the probabilities to find the two-charm-meson constituents in the resonances, are calculated. The rather large magnitudes of
r
found, between 3 and 4 fm, hint toward the relevance of possible underlying bare CDD poles, which seems consistent with the moderate compositeness
X
obtained.
Therefore, the elastic effective-range-expansion study reveals that both the two-charm-meson molecular components and other degrees of freedom, such as the compact four-quark cores or heavier hadronic components, would play relevant roles in the physical
Z_{c}(3900)
,
X(4020)
and
Z_{cs}(3985)
resonances.

The elastic effective-range-expansion description is then improved by the coupled-channel formalism, which is based on the simultaneous saturation of the compositeness and decay width.
By using the ratio of the decay widths between the
D\bar{D}^{*}/\bar{D}D^{*}
and
J/\psi\pi
channels from the BESIII experiment, we exactly solve the coupled-channel system for the
Z_{c}(3900)
. The compositeness value
X
turns out to be almost the same as the one from the elastic effective range expansion. The coincidence of the elastic study with the coupled-channel one reflects the marginal role played by the lighter channel, as evidenced by its small coupling and the related partial compositeness. Guided by this finding in the
Z_{c}(3900)
study, we then predict the partial widths to the
h_{c}\pi
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channels for the
X(4020)
and similarly for the
Z_{cs}(3985)
into
J/\psi K^{-}
and
D_{s}^{-}D^{*0}/D_{s}^{*-}{D}^{0}
. As in the case of the
Z_{c}(3900)
, the decay widths to the lighter channels are predicted to be much smaller than those to the heavier ones. We also point out that the statistical uncertainty in our results could be improved by more precise measurements of the total widths of the resonances mentioned. We think that our results can provide helpful guidelines for future measurements in relevant experiments.

Calculation of the compositeness for a non-relativistic bound state

In Ref.
[
20
]
, the partial compositeness
X_{A}
for a particle species
A
of a nonrelativistic bound state was shown to be given by the expectation value of the corresponding number operator of free particles of type
A
divided by its maximum number
n_{A}
. For example, in the case of the deuteron the compositeness of nucleons is the average number of nucleons divided by 2. For simplicity in the writing we suppress the subscript
A
and just refer to
X
.

We reproduce here the formula Eq. (39) of Ref.
[
20
]
for calculating
X
in terms of the coupling squared
g^{2}(k^{2})
of the bound state to this channel as a function of the three-momentum
k^{2}
,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mu_{m}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}dk\,k^{2}\frac{g^{2}(k^{2})}{(k^{2}-\kappa^{2})^{2}}~,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{m}E_{B}~,

with
E_{B}
the (negative) binding energy. We next perform a Taylor expansion of the coupling squared around
\kappa^{2}
and calculate the resulting integrations in dimensional regularization, in which, the powerlike divergences vanish. The output of this calculation yields the interesting formula

\displaystyle X=-g^{2}(\kappa)^{2}\frac{\partial G(E_{B})}{\partial E_{B}}-\frac{\partial g^{2}(\kappa^{2})}{\partial\kappa^{2}}\frac{\mu^{2}|\kappa|}{\pi}~,

(A.2)

with the nonrelativistic unitarity loop function
G(E)
given by

\displaystyle G(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dk\,k^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{E-k^{2}/2\mu_{m}}~.

(A.3)

If
\Lambda^{-1}
is the typical range of the finite range interactions, due to the onset of the left-hand cut because of crossed particle exchanges, the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
A.2
) is
{\cal O}(\kappa^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
compared to the first one.
2

2

[

24
]
and/or because of OZI rule suppression of light meson exchanges like in the

[
16
]
.
Thus, for close enough bound states to the threshold we can approximate
X
by just the first term,

\displaystyle X=-g^{2}(\kappa)^{2}\frac{\partial G(E_{B})}{\partial E_{B}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)=i\gamma_{k}^{2}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)~,

(A.4)

which constitutes an alternative general derivation of the well-known Weinberg’s formula for

[

, indicated in this paper by

We would also like to develop a simple toy-model example to illustrate the close relationship between the virtual particles in the cloud of a source at early times and the real ones that are radiated at later times. As a result, we think that this toy model shows that counting the later ones gives then information about the virtual cloud particles in the composition of a resonance. The example is based on an exactly solvable model in Quantum Field Theory
[
25
]
consisting of a source coupled to a scalar field
\phi(x)
. The Lagrangian density is

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}+g\rho(x)\phi~,

(A.5)

where
\rho(x)
is a source (external field). To mimic a decaying resonance of mass
M_{R}
and width
\Gamma
we consider a source that is produced at

and that later experiences damping oscillations. Namely,

\displaystyle\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}g\rho(\mathbf{r})\cos(M_{R}t)e^{-\frac{\Gamma t}{2}}&t>0\\
0&t<0\end{array}\right.

with
g
a constant coupling and the source can be taken without loss of generality to be normalized to one (
\int d^{3}r\rho(\mathbf{r})=1
).
The Fourier transform of this source in space and time is

\displaystyle\rho(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{ig\rho(\mathbf{k})(k^{0}+i\Gamma/2)}{(k^{0}-M_{R}+i\Gamma/2)(k^{0}+M_{R}+i\Gamma/2)}~,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}re^{-i\mathbf{k}\mathbf{r}}\rho(\mathbf{r})~.

Making use of the advanced Green function
\Delta^{\rm adv}(x)
the field
\phi(x)
can be written as

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\rm out}(x)+\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\Delta^{\rm adv}(x,x^{\prime})g\rho(x^{\prime})~,

(A.9)

where
\phi^{\rm out}(x)
is the field at the far future (

of the real free particles.
Performing explicitly the integration over
x^{\prime}
in Eq. (
A.9

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\rm out}(x)+g\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}2w(k)}e^{-ikx}\rho(k)\left(\frac{1}{k^{0}+w(k)-i\alpha}-\frac{1}{k^{0}-w(k)-i\alpha}\right)~,

(A.10)

with

and
\alpha\to 0^{+}
. From this formula one can readily obtain the relationship between

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{k},t=0)

(A.11)

Then, the expression for the total number of virtual particles

3

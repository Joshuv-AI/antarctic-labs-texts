Global existence for the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation in Besov spaces
Belkacem Said-Houari

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem of a model in nonlinear acoustic, named the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation. This equation arises as an alternative model to the well-known Kuznetsov equation in acoustics. We prove global existence and optimal time decay of solutions in Besov spaces with a minimal regularity assumption on the initial data, lowering the regularity assumption required in [ 31 ] for the proof of the global existence. Using a time-weighted energy method with the help of appropriate Lyapunov-type estimates, we also extend the decay rate in [ 31 ] and show an optimal decay rate of the solution for initial data in the Besov space B ˙ 2 , ∞ − 3 / 2 ( ℝ 3 ) \dot{{B}}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) , which is larger than the Lebesgue space L 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) due to the embedding L 1 ( ℝ 3 ) ↪ B ˙ 2 , ∞ − 3 / 2 ( ℝ 3 ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{{B}}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) . Hence we removed the L 1 L^{1} -assumption on the initial data required in [ 31 ] in order to prove the decay estimates of the solution.

1.
Introduction

Nonlinear wave propagation has been proved recently to be interesting in ultrasound imaging and in other medical applications, such as lithotripsy and thermotherapy. See for instance
[
25
,
15
,
26
,
12
]
. One of the classical models in nonlinear acoustics is the Kuznetsov equation

(1.1)

u_{tt}-c^{2}\Delta u-\nu\Delta u_{t}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\dfrac{1}{c^{2}}\dfrac{B}{2A}(u_{t})^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right),

where
v(x,t)=-\nabla u(x,t)
represents the acoustic velocity potential.
Equation (
1.1
) was initially introduced in
[
22
]
and derived from a compressible nonlinear isentropic Navier–Stokes (for
\nu>0
) and Euler (for
\nu=0
) systems by assuming in the equation of the conservation of energy that the heat flux obeys the Fourier law of heat conduction:

(1.2)

\boldsymbol{q}(x,t)=-K\nabla\theta(x,t),

where
K
is the thermal conductivity of the material and
\theta
is the absolute temperature.
The interested reader is referred to the recent paper by Dekkers and Rozanova-Pierrat
[
10
]
where the authors investigated (
1.1
) and proved a global existence result in the viscous case form small initial data of size roughly
\varepsilon\nu^{1/2}
, where
\varepsilon
is a small parameter. On the other hand, for the inviscid case, they proved a blow-up result.

It is well-known that using the Fourier law (
1.2
) may lead to the infinite signal speed paradox of the energy propagation; see
[
4
,
17
]
. To overcome this drawback in the Fourier law, other equations were considered to model the heat transfer. The Cattaneo (or the Maxwell–Cattaneo) law:

(1.3)

\tau\boldsymbol{q}_{t}(x,t)+\boldsymbol{q}(x,t)=-K\nabla\theta(x,t),

leads to a finite speed of propagation. In (
1.3
)

\tau
is a small parameter known as the relaxation time of the heat flux. The use of the Cattaneo law instead of the Fourier law in the governing equations of fluid dynamics leads to the following third order “in time” equation know as the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation:

(1.4)

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau u_{ttt}+u_{tt}-c^{2}\Delta u-\beta\Delta u_{t}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\dfrac{1}{c^{2}}\dfrac{B}{2A}(u_{t})^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right),\\
u(t=0)=u_{0},\qquad u_{t}(t=0)=u_{1}\qquad u_{tt}(t=0)=u_{2},\end{array}\right.

where
x\in\Omega
(
\Omega
is either a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and
t>0

, and where
\tau>0
is a time relaxation parameter,
c
is the speed of sound,
\beta
is the parameter of diffusivity and
A
and
B
are the constants of nonlinearity. The reader is referred to the papers
[
16
,
18
]
for the derivation of (
1.4
). We also mention the recent result
[
20
]
, where different time-fractional (J)MGT models have been derived by considering instead of (
1.3
) a time-fractional heat-flux law.

In recent years there has been a lot of work investigating the JMGT equation and its linearized version; the Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) equation.
The study of the controllability properties of the MGT type equations can be found, for instance in
[
3
,
24
]
.
The MGT equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a power source nonlinearity of the form
|u|^{p}
has been considered in
[
7
]
where some blow up results have been shown for the critical case
\tau c^{2}=\beta
.
The MGT and JMGT equations with a memory term have been also investigated recently. For the MGT with memory, the reader is referred to
[
2
,
35
,
11
]
and to
[
23
,
28
,
29
]
for the JMGT with memory.
The singular limit problem when
\tau\rightarrow 0
has been rigorously justified in
[
21
]
. The authors in
[
21
]
showed that in bounded domains, the limit of (
1.4
) as
\tau\rightarrow 0
leads to the Kuznetsov equation (
1.1
).

For the nonlinear model (
1.4
) in a bounded domain, the first result seems to be the one of Kaltenbacher et
al.
in
[
19
]
. They investigated the equation (
1.4
) in its abstract form and proved that the problem is well-posed for the linear equation with variable viscosity and positive diffusivity and showed the exponential decay of solutions of the linear equation. After that they gave local and global well-posedness and exponential decay for the nonlinear equation in a certain range of the parameters and for small initial data.

The Cauchy problem associated to the MGT equation was first studied by Pellicer and the author of this paper in
[
30
]
, where we showed the well-posedness and investigated the decay rate of the solution in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We used the energy method in the Fourier space to show that under the assumption
\beta>c^{2}\tau
the
L^{2}
-norm of the vector
\mathbf{V}=(u_{t}+\tau u_{tt},\nabla(u+\tau u_{t}),\nabla u_{t}),
and of its higher-order derivatives decay as

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{j}\mathbf{V}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(1+t)^{-{n}/{4-{j}/{2}}}\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+Ce^{-ct}\|\nabla^{j}\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

with
\mathbf{V}_{0}=\mathbf{V}(t=0)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0\leq j\leq s
. In addition, we proved the optimality of the decay rate in (
1.5
) by the eigenvalues expansion method. We also showed the following asymptotic behavior of the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{4}},\qquad

\displaystyle n\geq 3

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{1/2},\qquad

\displaystyle n=2.

It is clear from (
1.6
) that the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution either decays very slowly (for
n\geq 3
) or has a time growth (for
n=2
). This creates a difficulty in the nonlinear problem, since the integrability with respect to
t
of the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution will be lost.
See also the recent paper by Chen and Ikehata
[
6
]
, where the authors proved the optimal growth or optimal decay of the norm
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, depending on the space dimension.

In
[
31
]
, the author together with Racke considered the JMGT problem (
1.4
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(the 3D case) and showed a local existence result in appropriate function spaces by using the contraction mapping theorem and a global existence result for small data, by using the energy method together with some interpolation inequalities such as the classical Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality.
We also proved certain decay rates for the solution for small initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, for
s>\frac{5}{2}=\frac{n}{2}+1.
The proof of the global existence in
[
31
]
does not use the linear decay of the solution. It is based on nonlinear energy estimates.

Our goal in this paper is to consider system (
1.4
) in the three dimensional case
and improve the global existence result and decay estimates in
[
31
]
by reducing the regularity assumption on the initial data. In fact, we show that for small initial data in some appropriate Besov spaces, system (
1.4
) has a unique global solution. These spaces considered here are larger than the Sobolev spaces in
[
31
]
(see the discussion in Section
3.1
). This of course affects the size of the initial data required for the global existence, since the larger the space is, the smaller the initial data are. For instance, in
[
1
, page 30]
the authors considered:

\phi_{\varepsilon}=e^{i\frac{x_{1}}{\varepsilon}}\phi(x)
for some function
\phi
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and showed that
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\approx\varepsilon^{-s}
,
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\approx 1
and
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\varepsilon^{\sigma}
. It is clear that
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,r\geq 2
. Hence, for
\varepsilon
small,
\phi_{\varepsilon}
has a large norm in
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
but a small norm in the larger space
\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
p>d
. For this reason it is always interesting to show small-data global existence in the largest possible space.

To prove the global existence result, we use nonlinear
energy-type estimates by constructing an appropriate energy norm in some Besov spaces with critical regularity and show that this norm
remains uniformly bounded with respect to time. We point out that in the proof of global existence result, we
do not use the linear decay, which is a standard way of proving small data existence for non-linear evolution equations. Our proof here is purely based on energy method.
In order to prove the decay rate for dissipative partial differential equations, it is quite common to make smallness assumption on the
L^{1}
-norm of the initial data and combine it with the
L^{2}
-type energy estimates through Duhamel principle to obtain large time decay estimates. However, in many situations, it is difficult to work with the
L^{1}
-norm since it is not always possible to propagate the
L^{1}
-norm along the time evolution. So, it is of a great interest to prove decay estimates for initial data in
L^{2}
based spaces that contain
L^{1}
. In this paper and inspired by
[
33
]
, we prove decay estimates for the solution of (
1.4
) for initial data in the homogeneous Besov spaces
\dot{{B}}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which contains
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see Lemma
A.7
below).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section
2
contains the necessary theoretical preliminaries, which allow us to rewrite the equation with the corresponding initial data as a first-order Cauchy problem and define the main energy norm with the associated dissipative norm in appropriate Besov-type spaces. In Section
3
, we state and discuss our main result. In Section
4
, we derive the main energy estimate and prove the global existence result.
Section
5
is dedicated to the proof of the decay estimates of the linearized problem, where we extend and improve the result in
[
30
]
. In Section
6
, we prove the decay estimates of the nonlinear problem.
In Appendix
A
, we recall the Littlewood–Paley decomposition theory and give the definition and some useful properties of the Besov spaces.
We also present a useful interpolation inequality in Besov spaces and other integral inequalities that we used in the proofs.

.

3.1.
Discussion of the main result

Before moving onto the proof, we briefly discuss the statements made above in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

1.

The global existence result in
[
31
]
requires
u_{0},v_{0}\in H^{\frac{7}{2}+}
and
w_{0}\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+}
. Since
H^{s_{0}+}=B_{2,2}^{s_{0}+}\hookrightarrow B_{2,1}^{s_{0}}
(see
[
39
, Proposition 2.3]
), the global existence result stated in Theorem
3.1
improves
[
31
]
for the minimal possible regularity. As we said in the introduction, this means initial data which are large in the small space
H^{s_{0}+}
might be small in
B_{2,1}^{s_{0}+}
. (Here
H^{s+}=H^{\delta},B_{p,q}^{s+}=B_{p,q}^{\delta}
for any
\delta>s
).
In the proof of the main result, we took advantages of the embedding
\dot{B}^{n/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Such embedding fails if we replace the Besov space
\dot{B}^{n/p}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{n/p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.

By using the interpolation:

(3.6)

\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{2,1}}\lesssim\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}^{\frac{2}{3}\sigma}\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}^{1-\frac{2}{3}\sigma},

we have from (
3.5
)

(3.7)

\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{2,1}}\lesssim\mathbbmss{E}_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}.

for
0\leq\sigma\leq 3/2
.

3.

The decay estimates obtained in
[
31
]
hold under the assumption that the initial data
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Theorem
3.2
does not require the initial data to be in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In fact, we assume that the initial data
\mathbf{V}_{0}
to be in the Besov space
\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which satisfies
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{{B}}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

4.

In this paper, although we restrict ourselves to the 3D case, the proof of the main results can be modified to include the general spaces dimension
\mathbb{R}^{n},\,n\geq 3
. The case
n=2
would require new estimates.

5.

In Theorem
3.1
, since we do not use the decay estimates of the linearized problem, we do not assume the extra
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for the proof of the global existence result.

The following proposition is crucial in the proof of Theorem

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
0<\tau<\beta
. Then the following estimate holds:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}_{2,1}^{3/2})}+\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{B}_{2,1}^{3/2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}(0)\|_{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}_{2,1}^{3/2}}+\sqrt{\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}_{2,1}^{3/2})}}\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{B}_{2,1}^{3/2})}.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
will be given through several lemmas and uses some ideas and estimates from the work
[
31
]
.
First, we recall the following estimate which has been proved in
[
31
, Estimate (2.39)]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
31
]
)
.

Assume that
0<\tau<\beta
. Then, the following estimate holds:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{2}[\mathbf{U}](t)+\mathcal{D}^{2}[\mathbf{U}](t)\leq\mathcal{E}^{2}[\mathbf{U}](0)+C\big(\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{U}](t)+M_{0}(t)\big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\mathbf{U}](t),

uniformly with respect to
t
.

From (
4.2
) and recalling (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}^{2})}+\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{L}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}(0)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\sqrt{\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}^{2})}+M_{0}(t)}\|\mathbf{U}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{L}^{2})}.

Our goal now is to prove a frequency-localized estimate similar to (

). This is the goal of Section

.

4.1.
Frequency-localized estimates

In this subsection, we employ a frequency-localization method and prove a nonlinear energy estimate in homogeneous Besov-type spaces. Applying the operator
\dot{\Delta}_{q}\,(q\in\mathbb{Z})
to the system (
2.3a
), we get

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{\Delta}_{q}u_{t}=\dot{\Delta}_{q}v,&\\
\dot{\Delta}_{q}v_{t}=\dot{\Delta}_{q}w,&\\
\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w_{t}=\Delta\dot{\Delta}_{q}u+\beta\Delta\dot{\Delta}_{q}v-\dot{\Delta}_{q}w+\mathrm{R}_{q}&\end{array}\right.

with

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{q}=

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Big(\dfrac{B}{A}vw+2\nabla u\nabla v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{B}{A}[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]w+\dfrac{B}{A}v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w+2[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla u]\nabla v+2\nabla u\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v.

We define the frequency-localized energy as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)=\,\sup_{0\leq\sigma\leq t}

\displaystyle\Big(\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}(v+\tau w)(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla(v+\tau w)(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big.\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Delta v(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Delta(u+\tau v)(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big.

\displaystyle+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla(u+\tau v)(\sigma)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w(\sigma)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

and its associated dissipative rate as

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)=

\displaystyle\Big(\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Delta v(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big.

\displaystyle+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Delta\left(u+\tau v\right)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla(v+\tau w)(t)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

We also define

(4.7)

\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma.

Following the same steps as in
[
31
]
, where
\dot{\Delta}_{q}
will play the role of
\nabla^{k}
in
[
31
]
, we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)+\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)\lesssim\mathcal{E}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](0)+\sum_{i=1}^{5}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{i}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma,

with

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{1}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathrm{R}_{q}||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathrm{R}_{q}||\nabla(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{3}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathrm{R}_{q}||\Delta(\dot{\Delta}_{q}u+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}v)|\textup{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{4}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathrm{R}_{q}||\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v|\textup{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{5}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathrm{R}_{q}||\dot{\Delta}_{q}w|\textup{d}x.

Our goal now is to estimate the terms
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{i}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}],\,i=1,\dots,5
.
This will be done by a repeated use of suitable functional inequalities, such as: Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities and some embedding theorems in Besov spaces. These functional inequalities will help to control some commutator estimates.
The proof will be done through several lemmas.

In the following lemma, we estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{4}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

It holds that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]+\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{4}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]\Big)(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\Big(\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}\Big.

\displaystyle+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}\Big)\mathcal{D}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)

\displaystyle+\Big(\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\Big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t).

Proof.

We have by Hölder’s inequality

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)+\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{4}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)\Big)\textup{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathrm{R}_{q}\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\Big(\|\nabla(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\Big).

To estimate
\|\nabla\mathrm{R}_{q}\|_{L^{2}}
,
we write

(4.12)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathrm{R}_{q}=

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla\Big(\dfrac{B}{A}vw+2\nabla u\nabla v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Big(\dfrac{B}{A}v\nabla w+\dfrac{B}{A}\nabla vw+2\nabla^{2}u\nabla v+2\nabla u\nabla^{2}v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{B}{A}[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]\nabla w+\dfrac{B}{A}v\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla w+\dfrac{B}{A}[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla v]w+\dfrac{B}{A}\nabla v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w

\displaystyle+2[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla^{2}u]\nabla v+2\nabla^{2}u\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v+2[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla u]\nabla^{2}v+2\nabla u\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla^{2}v.

Applying (
A.27
), we estimate the commutators in (
4.12
) as

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]\nabla w\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla v]w\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla^{2}u]\nabla v\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla u]\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}.

We point out here and in the sequel that each
(c_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}
has possibly a different form, however we always have
\|c_{q}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq 1.

On the other hand, we estimate the remaining terms in (
4.12
) as

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|v\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla w\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla^{2}v\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}.

Plugging (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) into (
4.11
) and using the embedding

\dot{B}_{2,1}^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then (
4.10
) holds. This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Next, we estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{i}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}],\,i=3,5
. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

It holds that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{3}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]+\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{5}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]\Big)(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\Big(\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}\Big)\mathcal{D}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)

\displaystyle+\Big(\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\Big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t).

Proof.

We have, by recalling (
4.9
) and using Hölder’s inequality

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{3}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)+\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{5}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)\Big)\textup{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{R}_{q}\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\Big(\|\Delta(\dot{\Delta}_{q}u+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}v)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\Big).

Hence, following the same steps as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{R}_{q}\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\Big(\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L_{t}^{2}(L^{2})}\Big).

Therefore, (
4.15
) holds by collecting the above last two estimates and using the embedding

\dot{B}_{2,1}^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which ends the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Now, we need to estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{1}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]
. This will be done in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

It holds that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{1}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{U}](t)+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1}}\big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)

\displaystyle+c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\mathcal{E}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)\Big(\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|\nabla v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}\Big).

Proof.

We have by recalling (
4.5
),

(4.19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{1}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}]\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]w||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla u]\nabla v||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{1}+\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{2}+\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{3}+\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{4}.

Our next goal is to estimate the terms
\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{i},\,i=1,\dots,4
. We begin
by estimating
\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{4}
. We have

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v||\dot{\Delta}_{q}v|\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v||\dot{\Delta}_{q}w|\textup{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{1}+\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{2}.

To estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{1}
, we have
by Hölder’s inequality

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{1}\lesssim\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, applying the interpolation inequality, which holds for
n=3
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2},

we obtain

(4.23)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

This together with the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
yields

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The estimate of
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{2}
is straightforward, so, we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, collecting (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) and using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\big)\Big(\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

Consequently, this yields, by using (
A.18
),

(4.27)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{4}(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{U}](t)+\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t).

Next, we estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{2}
. We have

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v\dot{\Delta}_{q}w||\dot{\Delta}_{q}v|\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v||\dot{\Delta}_{q}w|^{2}\textup{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{3}+\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{4}.

Using (
4.22
),
we obtain

(4.29)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{3}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}v\|_{L^{6}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}})\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

Furthermore, we estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{4}
as

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Collecting (
4.29
) and (
4.30
), we obtain

(4.31)

\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{2}\lesssim\big(\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}+\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\big)\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

This yields, by using Hölder’s inequality and (
A.18
),

(4.32)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{2}(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma\lesssim\big(\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{U}](t)+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\big)\mathcal{D}^{2}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t).

Now, to estimate
\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{1}
, we have

(4.33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]w||(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)|\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]w\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\big\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying (
A.26
), we find

(4.34)

\displaystyle\big\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},v]w\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\|w\|_{\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1}}.

This yields

(4.35)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{1}(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\big\|(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}\|v\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\big\|(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(L^{2})}\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\mathcal{E}[\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{U}](t)\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1})}\|w\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{2}(\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1})},

where we have used (
A.18
).

The term
\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{3}
can be estimated similarly. We have

(4.36)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\|[\dot{\Delta}_{q},\nabla u]\nabla v\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c_{q}2^{-\frac{3q}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}\|\nabla v\|_{\dot{B}^{1/2}_{2,1}}\big\|(\dot{\Delta}_{q}v+\tau\dot{\Delta}_{q}w)\big\|_{L^{2}}.

This yields as above,

(4.37)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{T}}_{3}(\sigma)\textup{d}\sigma\lesssim

5.
Decay estimates of the linear equation

In this section, we show decay estimates for some energy norms for the solution of
linearized system

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\tau u_{ttt}+u_{tt}-\Delta u-\beta\Delta u_{t}=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,

with the initial conditions:

(5.1b)

\displaystyle u(t=0)=u_{0},\qquad u_{t}(t=0)=u_{1}\qquad u_{tt}(t=0)=u_{2},

by assuming additionally that our initial data are in
\dot{B}^{-\rho}_{p,\infty}
with
1-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\rho}{n}
.
This improves the decay estimates given in
[
30
]
, due to the embedding
L^{1}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-\rho}_{p,\infty}
(see Lemma
A.7
). These decay estimates of the linearized problem are crucial in the proof of the decay estimates of the nonlinear problem stated in Theorem
3.2
.
We define the vector
\mathbf{V}=(u_{t}+\tau u_{tt},\nabla(u+\tau u_{t}),\nabla u_{t})
and
show the following decay estimates for the vector solution
\mathbf{V}
, where here we are not restricted to the 3D case.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
0<\tau<\beta
. Suppose that
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\sigma+s>0
, then for all
t\geq 0
, the solution of (
5.1
) has the following decay estimate:

(5.2a)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{V}(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}}(1+t)^{-\frac{\sigma+s}{2}},

Also if
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\sigma\geq 0
and
s>0
, then the solution of (
5.1
) has the following decay estimate:

(5.2b)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{k}\mathbf{V}(t)\|_{B_{2,1}^{\sigma-k}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}}(1+t)^{-\frac{k+s}{2}}

for
0\leq k\leq\sigma
.

The result of Theorem
5.1
provides the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, it holds that for
2\leq p<\infty
and

(5.3)

\|\mathbf{V}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}_{2,1}\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-n(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}}(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}.

Proof of Corollary
5.1
.

Using for
(2\leq p<\infty)
, the estimate

(5.4)

\|\mathbf{V}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}\|_{\dot{H}^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{V}\|_{\dot{B}^{n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}_{2,1}}

we obtain from (
5.2a
) and for
s=n(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}),\,(1\leq q<p)

the estimate (
5.3
).
∎

or
B_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Such a decay rate is necessary to prove the decay estimates of the nonlinear equation.
However, we can obtain it through the bound

(5.32)

\|v\|_{{X_{2,1}^{\sigma}}}\lesssim\|(v+\tau w)\|_{{X_{2,1}^{\sigma}}}+\|w\|_{{X_{2,1}^{\sigma}}}

and (
5.2
), if we have a decay rate for
\|w\|_{{X_{2,1}^{\sigma}}}
. The decay rate of
\|w\|_{{X_{2,1}^{\sigma}}}
is the result of the next proposition.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that
0<\tau<\beta
. If
\mathbf{V}_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
w_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\sigma+s+1>0
, then it holds that

(5.33)

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}}\lesssim\Big(\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma+1}\cap\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}}+\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}}\Big)(1+t)^{-\frac{\sigma+s+1}{2}}

provided that the thermal relaxation
\tau>0
is sufficiently small.

Proof.

For proving the above estimate, we need to employ the decay rates of the Fourier transform of the solution. It has been proved in
[
30
, Estimate 3.6]
that the following estimate holds:

(5.34)

|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\lesssim|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,0)|^{2}\exp{(-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}t)}

for all
t\geq 0
. The constant
\lambda
is positive and independent of
t
and
\xi
. For the linearized problem, it holds from the third equation of (
2.3a
)

(5.35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau\left|w\right|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\,\textup{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta uw\,\textup{d}x+\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta vw\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta^{2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, this yields

(5.36)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tau\left|w\right|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\,\textup{d}x\lesssim\,\|\Delta(u+\tau v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus we know that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}\tau\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\hat{w}|^{2}\lesssim\,|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,t)|^{2}.

By plugging in estimate (
5.34
) for
|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,t)|^{2}
in the above inequality, we obtain

(5.38)

\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{\tau}\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}+C|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,0)|^{2}\exp{\left(-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}t\right)}.

Using the factorization
\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}|\hat{w}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}|\hat{w}|^{2}=e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\frac{\textup{d}}{\,\textup{d}t}(e^{\frac{t}{\tau}}|\hat{w}|^{2})

to arrive at

(5.39)

\displaystyle\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle|\hat{w}_{0}|^{2}\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}t\right)}+C|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,0)|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\exp{\left(-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}s\right)}\,\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}(t-s)\right)}\,\textup{d}s,

which directly leads to

(5.40)

\displaystyle\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\,|\hat{w}_{0}|^{2}\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}t\right)}\\
+C|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,0)|^{2}\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}t\right)}\left(\tfrac{1}{\tau}-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left[\exp{\left(-(\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{\tau})t\right)}-1\right].\end{multlined}

To further bound the right-hand side side, we can use the identity

(5.41)

\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\tau}-\lambda\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}}=\frac{\tau\left(|\xi|^{2}+1\right)}{|\xi|^{2}(1-\lambda\tau)+1}.

Assuming that the thermal relaxation is small enough so that
\tau<\frac{1}{\lambda}
, it holds

(5.42)

\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\tau}-\lambda\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}}\leq\frac{\tau}{1-\lambda\tau}.

Altogether, for small
\tau>0
, we obtain

(5.43)

\left|\hat{w}\right|^{2}\leq|\hat{w}_{0}|^{2}\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}t\right)}+C|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{V}}(\xi,0)|^{2}\exp{\left(-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}t\right)}.

Using the third equation of the linearized system of (
4.4
) (i.e.,
\mathrm{R}_{q}=0
) and following the above steps, we can prove the following estimate (here
|\xi|\approx 2^{q}
)

(5.44)

|\widehat{\dot{\Delta}_{q}w}|^{2}\leq|\widehat{\dot{\Delta}_{q}w}_{0}|^{2}\exp{\left(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}t\right)}+C2^{2q}|\widehat{\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{V}_{0}}|^{2}\exp{\left(-\lambda\tfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}t\right)}.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, for
q\geq 0
, we have from (
5.44
) and Plancherel’s theorem

(5.45)

\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-ct}\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+2^{q}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\Big).

We multiply the above inequality by
2^{q\sigma}
, summing the resulting inequality over
q\geq 0
and using Lemma
A.1
, we get

(5.46)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\geq 0}2^{q\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-ct}\sum_{q\geq 0}\Big(2^{q(\sigma+1)}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+2^{q\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-ct}\big(\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma+1}}+\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}}\big).

Now, for
q<0
, we have as in (
5.15
)

(5.47)

\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}t}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+2^{q}e^{-\eta_{1}(2^{q}\sqrt{t})^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma+s+1>0
, then
we multiply the above inequality by
2^{q\sigma}
, we get

(5.48)

\sum_{q<0}2^{q\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}t}\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\sigma}}+\|\mathbf{V}_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{-s}}(1+t)^{-\frac{\sigma+s+1}{2}}.

Hence, (
5.33
) holds by collecting (
5.46
) and (
5.48
).
∎

6.
Decay of the nonlinear model

Our goal in this section is to extend the decay estimates in Theorem
5.1
to the nonlinear model (
1.4
). We follow the method in

[
37
]
, which is based on a frequency-localization Duhamel principle.

First, taking the Fourier transform of linearized system associated to (
2.3a
), we obtain

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{u}_{t}=\hat{v},\\
\hat{v}_{t}=\hat{w},\\
\hat{w}_{t}=-\dfrac{|\xi|^{2}}{\tau}\hat{u}-\dfrac{\beta|\xi|^{2}}{\tau}\hat{v}-\dfrac{1}{\tau}\hat{w}.\end{array}\right.

We can write the previous system in a matrix form as

(6.2a)

\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{t}(\xi,t)=\Phi(\xi)\hat{\mathbf{U}}(\xi,t),

with the initial data

(6.2b)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{U}}(\xi,0)=\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{0}(\xi),

where
\hat{\mathbf{U}}(\xi,t)=(\hat{u}(\xi,t),\hat{v}(\xi,t),\hat{w}(\xi,t))^{T}
and

(6.3)

\Phi(\xi)=L+|\xi|^{2}A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&0&-\dfrac{1}{\tau}\end{array}\right)+|\xi|^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
-\dfrac{1}{\tau}&-\dfrac{\beta}{\tau}&0\end{array}\right).

We denote by
\mathcal{G}(t,x)
the Green matrix associated with the linearized system and defined through its Fourier transform as

(6.4)

\widehat{\mathcal{G}f}(t,\xi)=e^{t\Phi(\xi)}\hat{f}(\xi).

Hence, the solution
\mathbf{U}
of the nonlinear problem (
2.3a
) can be expressed, using Duhamel’s formula, as

(6.5)

\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{U}^{0}+\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U}),

where
\mathbf{U}^{0}
and
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})
are given by

(6.6)

\mathbf{U}^{0}=\mathcal{G}(t,x)\mathbf{U}_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(t-r,x)\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{U},\nabla\mathbf{U})(r)\textup{d}r.

\mathbf{U}^{0}
satisfies the linear equation

(6.7)

\mathbf{U}_{t}^{0}-\mathcal{A}\mathbf{U}^{0}=0,\qquad\mathbf{U}^{0}\left(x\right)=\mathbf{U}_{0}\left(x\right),

and
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})
satisfies the nonlinear equation with zero
initial data, that is

(6.8)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})-\mathcal{A}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{U})=\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{U},\nabla\mathbf{U}),\qquad\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{U}\right)\left(x,0\right)=0,

with

(6.9)

\mathcal{A}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\\
w\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v\\
w\\
\dfrac{1}{\tau}\Delta(u+\beta v)-\dfrac{1}{\tau}w\end{array}\right)

and
\mathcal{F}
is the nonlinear term

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{U},\nabla\mathbf{U})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
\dfrac{1}{\tau}\dfrac{B}{A}vw+\dfrac{2}{\tau}\nabla u\nabla v\end{array}\right).

In the next lemma, we state the frequency localization principle.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
0<\tau<\beta
. Let
\mathbf{U}=(u,v,w)^{T}
be the solution of (
2.3
). Then, it holds that

(6.11)

\Delta_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}\mathbf{U}(t,x)=\Delta_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}[\mathcal{G}(t,x)\mathbf{U}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}[\mathcal{G}(t-r,x)\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{U},\nabla\mathbf{U})(r)]\textup{d}r

for
q\geq-1
and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(6.12)

\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}\mathbf{U}(t,x)=\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}[\mathcal{G}(t,x)\mathbf{U}_{0}]+\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\Delta}_{q}\Lambda^{\ell}[\mathcal{G}(t-r,x)\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{U},\nabla\mathbf{U})(r)]\textup{d}r,

for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
.

The proof of Lemma
6.1
follows as in
[
37
, Lemma 5.1]
. We omit the details.

We define

(6.13)

\boldsymbol{E}(t)=\sup_{0\leq\sigma\leq t}\|\mathbf{U}(\sigma)\|_{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}_{2,1}^{3/2}}

and inspired by (
5.2b
) and (
5.4
), we define

(6.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(t):=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\ell<3/2}\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}(1+r)^{\frac{\ell}{2}+\frac{3}{4}}\|\Lambda^{\ell}\mathbf{V}(r)\|_{B_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}-\ell}}+\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}(1+r)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{V}(r)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}(1+r)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|w(r)\|_{\dot{B}^{3/2}_{2,1}}+\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}(1+r)^{\frac{3}{4}}\|w(r)\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}+\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}(1+r)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathbf{V}(r)\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{5}{2}}_{2,1}}.

We point out here that
\|\mathbf{V}(r)\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{5}{2}}_{2,1}}
should be expected to decay at the rate
(1+r)^{-2}
, but it seems not clear how to reach this for the nonlinear problem. However, the decay of rate
(1+r)^{-3/2}
is enough to prove our result.

Now, we define the norm

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|\nabla(u+\tau v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\|v+\tau w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{V}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Analytic tools

In this appendix, we introduce the Littlewood–Paley decomposition, define the Besov spaces and list some of their properties. See e.g.
[
1
]
for more details.

A.1.
Littlewood-Paley theory

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing functions. For a given
f

in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define its Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}f=\hat{f}
and its inverse
\mathcal{F}^{-1}\hat{f}
as

(A.1)

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,\textup{d}x,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{-1}\hat{f}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\hat{f}(\xi)\textup{d}\xi.

Let
(\varphi,\chi(\xi))
be a couple of smooth functions valued in
[0,1]
such that
\varphi
is supported in
the annulus
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
,
\chi
is supported in the ball

\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
, such that

(A.2)

\chi(\xi)+\sum_{q\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-q}\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

(A.3)

\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-q}\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Denote by
h:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
and
\widetilde{h}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, define

(A.4)

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{q}f=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-q}D)f=2^{dq}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(2^{q}y)f(x-y)\textup{d}y,\quad q\in\mathbb{Z};

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}f=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-q}D)f=2^{dq}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(2^{q}y)f(x-y)\textup{d}y,\quad q\geq 0;

\displaystyle\Delta_{-1}f=

\displaystyle\chi(D)f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widetilde{h}(y)f(x-y)\textup{d}y,\qquad\Delta_{q}f=0\,\ \text{for}\,\ q\leq-2.

Observe that
\dot{\Delta}_{q}
coincide with
\Delta_{q}
for
q\geq 0
.

Now, denote by
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
the
tempered distribution modulo polynomials
\mathcal{P}
.
It is not hard to see that the space
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}
is exactly the
space of tempered distributions for which we may write

(A.5)

u=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{q}u.

This decomposition is called the homogeneous Littlewood-Paley decomposition.

We recall
the Bernstein inequality, which is the fundamental inequality in the Littlewood–Paley theory. See
[
1
]
.

Lemma A.1

(Bernstein inequality)
.

Let
0<R_{1}<R_{2}
and
1\leq a\leq b\leq\infty
and
let
k
be an integer.

(i):

If
\mathrm{Supp}(\hat{f})\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq R_{1}\lambda\}
, then

(A.6)

\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{a}}\lesssim\lambda^{k+n\big(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\big)}\|f\|_{L^{b}}.

(ii):

If
\mathrm{Supp}(\hat{f})\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:R_{1}\lambda\lesssim|\xi|\leq R_{2}\lambda\}
, then

(A.7)

\|\Lambda^{k}f\|_{L^{a}}\approx\lambda^{k}\|f\|_{L^{a}}.

As a consequence of the above inequality, we have

(A.8)

\|\Lambda^{\ell}f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s+\ell}}\,\quad(\ell\geq 0);\qquad\|\Lambda^{\ell}f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s+\ell}}\quad(\ell\in\mathbb{R}).

A.2.
Besov spaces

Now, we introduce the definition of Besov spaces. These spaces are very
useful in the study of nonlinear PDEs since many embedding theorems which fail in Sobolev spaces are correct in Besov spaces.

Definition 1
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Besov space

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}
is defined as

(A.9)

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}:\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}<\infty\right\},

where

(A.10)

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{qs}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}f\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{r}\bigg)^{1/r},&\qquad r<\infty\\
\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qs}\|\dot{\Delta}_{q}f\|_{L^{p}},&\qquad r=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Similarly, we define the inhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}
as:

Definition 2
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the inhomogeneous Besov
space
B^{s}_{p,r}
is defined as

(A.11)

B^{s}_{p,r}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}<\infty\right\},

where

(A.12)

\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{q=-1}^{\infty}\left(2^{qs}\|\Delta_{q}f\|_{L^{p}}\right)^{r}\bigg)^{1/r},&\qquad r<\infty\\
\displaystyle\sup_{q\geq-1}2^{qs}\|\Delta_{q}f\|_{p},&\qquad r=\infty.\end{array}\right.

We summarize the main properties of Besov spaces in the following lemma. See
[
38
, Lemma 2.2]
for the properties (1)-(5) and
[
8
, Proposition 1.4]
for the properties (6) and (7).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, then

1.

If
s>0
, then
B^{s}_{p,r}=L^{p}\cap\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}
and
\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}\approx\|f\|_{p}+\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}
.

2.

If
\tilde{s}\leq s
, then

B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow B^{\tilde{s}}_{p,r}
.

3.

If
1\leq r\leq\tilde{r}\leq\infty
, then
\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,\tilde{r}}^{s}
and
B_{p,r}^{s}\hookrightarrow B_{p,\tilde{r}}^{s}
.

4.

If
1\leq p\leq\tilde{p}\leq\infty
, then
\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{\tilde{p},r}^{s-n(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\tilde{p}})}
and
B_{p,r}^{s}\hookrightarrow B_{\tilde{p},r}^{s-n(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\tilde{p}})}
.

5.

\dot{B}_{p,1}^{n/p}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{0}
and
B_{p,1}^{n/p}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{0}

(1\leq p<\infty)
, where
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is the space of bounded continuous functions which decay at infinity.

6.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that

(A.13)

C^{-1}\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s-1}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}.

7.

\|\nabla u\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}
.

The Littlewood–Paley decomposition enables us to obtain bounds for each
dyadic block in spaces of the form
L^{\rho}_{T}(L^{p})
. Therefore, to go from
this type of bounds to estimates in
L^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})
we need to
perform a summation in
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
. By doing this, we in
fact do not bound the
L^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})
norm, since the time
integration has been performed before the summation in
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
.
This requires the introduction of the following spaces known as the
Chemin–Lerner spaces. See
[
5
]
.

Definition 3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},\,1\leq p,\,r,\,\theta\leq\infty
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval. Then the homogeneous mixed time-space
Chemin–Lerner space
\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})
is defined by

(A.14)

\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})=\left\{f\in L^{\theta}(0,T;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}):\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}<\infty\right\},

where

(A.15)

\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}({\dot{B}}_{p,r}^{s})}=\left\|2^{sq}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta_{q}f(\tau)\|_{p}^{\theta}d\tau\right)^{1/\theta}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}

with the usual modification if
\theta=\infty
.
Notice here that the
integration in time is taken before the summation in
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We also need the inhomogeneous mixed time-space
\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(B_{p,q}^{s}),s>0
whose norm is defined by

(A.16)

\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(B_{p,q}^{s})}=\|f\|_{L^{\theta}_{T}(L^{p})}+\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s})}.

We further define

(A.17a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(B_{p,q}^{s}):=

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(B_{p,q}^{s})\cap\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{p,q}^{s})

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{1}(B_{p,q}^{s}):=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];B_{p,q}^{s}):\partial_{t}f\in\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T}(B_{p,q}^{s})\},

and for
T=+\infty
, we define

(A.17b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}(B_{p,q}^{s}):=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}+;B_{p,q}^{s}):\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}(B_{p,q}^{s})}<+\infty\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(B_{p,q}^{s}):=

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};B_{p,q}^{s}):\|\partial_{t}f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}(B_{p,q}^{s})}\}

where for notation simplicity, the index
T
will be omitted when
T=+\infty
.

Remark 2
.

Using Minkowski’s inequality, we may easily show that the above spaces
can be linked to the usual space-time mixed spaces
L^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})
through the embedding inequalities

(A.18)

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})},\qquad\text{if}\quad r\geq\theta,\\
\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})},\qquad\text{if}\quad r\leq\theta.\end{array}\right.

In addition the following product estimate holds (see
[
38
, Lemma 2.4]
)

(A.19)

\|fg\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\theta_{1}}_{T}(L^{\infty})}\|g\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta_{2}}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}+\|g\|_{L^{\theta_{3}}_{T}(L^{\infty})}\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta_{4}}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}

whenever
s>0,\,1\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq\theta,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{4}\leq\infty

and

(A.20)

\frac{1}{\theta}=\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{1}{\theta_{3}}+\frac{1}{\theta_{4}}.

As a direct corollary, one has

(A.21)

\|fg\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\lesssim\|g\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta_{1}}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}\|f\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\theta_{2}}_{T}(\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s})}

whenever
sp\geq n
and
1/\theta=1/\theta_{1}+1/\theta_{2}
.

Now, we introduce some useful inequalities such as the Moser-type product estimates, commutator estimates and the Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequalities and some other useful inequalities.

We start with the following characterization of the homogeneous Besov spaces. (See

Next, we recall the following lemma. (see

Lemma A.4

(Product estimate)

Let
s>0
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty

where the hidden constant is depending only on

and

Since

(A.27)

Solving the recoverable robust shortest path problem in DAGs
Marcel Jackiewicz, Adam Kasperski, Paweł Zieliński

Abstract
This paper deals with the recoverable robust shortest path problem under the interval uncertainty representation. The problem is known to be strongly NP-hard and not approximable in general digraphs. Polynomial time algorithms for the problem under consideration in DAGs are proposed.



Keywords
: robust optimization, interval uncertainty, shortest path.

1
Introduction

In the
shortest path problem
(
SP
),
we are given a
multidigraph

G=(V,A)
that
consists
of a finite set of nodes
V
,
|V|=n
, and a finite multiset of
arcs
A
,
|A|=m
. A deterministic cost is associated with each arc, two nodes
s\in V
and
t\in V
are
distinguished as the
starting node
and the

destination
node, respectively. In particular, node
s
is
called
source
if no arc enters
s
and node
t
is called

sink
if no arc leaves
t
. Let
\Phi
be the set of all simple
s
-
t
paths in
G
.
We wish to find
a simple
s
-
t
path in
G

with
the minimum total cost. The shortest path problem can be solved efficiently using several polynomial time algorithms (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
).

In the
recoverable robust
version of
SP
(
Rob Rec SP
for short),
we are given: the
first-stage arc costs

C_{e}\geq 0
,
e\in A
, which are precisely known;
the set
\mathbb{U}
of possible realizations
of uncertain second-stage arc costs, called
scenarios
, represented
in this paper by the Cartesian product of interval costs, namely,

\mathbb{U}=\{S=(c_{e}^{S})_{e\in A}\,:\,c_{e}^{S}\in[\hat{c}_{e},\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e}],e\in A\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|A|}
,
where
\hat{c}_{e}
is a
nominal arc cost
of
e\in A
and
\Delta_{e}\geq 0
is the maximum
deviation of the cost of
e
from its nominal value;
and a

recovery parameter

k
,
0\leq k<|V|
.
A decision process in
Rob Rec SP
is 2-stage and consists in finding a path
X\in\Phi

in the first stage. Then in the second (recovery) stage, after a cost scenario
S=(c_{e}^{S})_{e\in A}
reveals,
the first stage path
X
can be modified by choosing a cheapest path
Y
, under the revealed
cost scenario
Y
, from its
neighborhood

\Phi(X,k)
depending on the recovery parameter
k

(a limited recovery action is allowed). Thus,
the goal is to find a pair of paths
X^{*}\in\Phi
(the first stage path) and
Y^{*}\in\Phi(X^{*},k)

(the second stage path), which minimize the sum of total costs of
X^{*}
and
Y^{*}

at the worst case, i.e.

\textsc{Rob Rec SP}:\;\min_{X\in\Phi}\left(\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\max_{S\in\mathbb{U}}\min_{Y\in\Phi(X,k)}\sum_{e\in Y}c_{e}^{S}\right).

(1)

We examine here
the neighborhood

\Phi(X,k)=\{Y\in\Phi\,:\,|Y\setminus X|\leq k\}
,
called the
arc inclusion neighborhood
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
4
]
).
Note that if
k=0
, then
Rob Rec SP
is equivalent to
SP
problem under the arc costs

C_{e}+\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e}
,
e\in A
. Thus,
from now on, we assume that
k\geq 1
.

2
Recoverable (robust) shortest path problem

We start with a simple observation (see
[
2
]
), which
simplifies considerably the
Rob Rec SP

under scenario set
\mathbb{U}
, namely,

\max_{S\in\mathbb{U}}\min_{Y\in\Phi(X,k)}\sum_{e\in Y}c_{e}^{S}=\min_{Y\in\Phi(X,k)}\sum_{e\in Y}(\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e})
.
Hence
Rob Rec SP
is equivalent
the following problem

\textsc{Rec SP}:\;\min_{X\in\Phi,Y\in\Phi(X,k)}\left(\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\sum_{e\in Y}\overline{c}_{e}\right)

(2)

called

recoverable shortest path problem
, where
\overline{c}_{e}
stands for
\hat{c}_{e}+\Delta_{e}
.
Thus, from now on, we will study the
Rec SP
problem instead of
the
Rob Rec SP
under
\mathbb{U}
.

In
[
2
]
, it has been proved that the
Rec SP
(equivalently
Rob Rec SP
) problem
with
\Phi(X,k)

is strongly
NP-hard and not approximable in general digraphs, even
if the recovery parameter
k=2
.

In this section, we show that
Rec SP
with can be solved in polynomial time if the input graph
G
is an acyclic multigraph. Observe that we can drop the assumption about nonnegativity of the arc costs for this class of graphs. We first assume that
G
is layered, and then we will generalize the result for arbitrary acyclic multigraphs. We finish with
arc series-parallel multidigraphs.

2.1
Layered multidigraph

In a layered multigraph
G=(V,A)
the set of nodes
V
is partitioned into
H
disjoint subsets, i.e.
V=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{H}
, and the arcs go only from nodes in
V_{h}
to nodes in
V_{h+1}
for each

h\in[H-1]
. We can assume that
s\in V_{1}
and
t\in V_{H}
.

Theorem 1
.

Rec SP
with
\Phi(X,k)
in
a layered multidigraph
G=(V,A)
can solved in
O(|A||V|+|V|^{2}k)
time.

Proof.

We show a polynomial transformation from
Rec SP
in a layered multidigraph to
the Constrained Shortest Path Problem (
CSP
for short) in an acyclic multidigraph. In the
CSP
problem,
we are given an acyclic multidigraph
G=(V,A)
with
s\in V
and
t\in V
.
Each arc
e\in A
has a cost
c_{e}
and a transition time
t_{e}
. A positive total transition time limit
T
is specified.
We seek an
s
-
t
path
\pi
in
G
that minimizes the
total cost, subject to not exceeding the transition time limit
T
.
The
CSP
can be solved in
O(|A|T)
time
[
3
]
.

We now build an acyclic multidigraph
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},A^{\prime})
in the corresponding instance of
CSP
as follows.
We fix
V^{\prime}=V
and
A^{\prime}
contains two types of arcs labelled as
e^{(0)}
and
e^{(1)}
. Namely,
for each pair of nodes
i\in V_{h}
and
j\in V_{h+1}
,
h\in[H-1]
, such that there exists at least one arc
from
i
to
j
(note that
G
can be multidigraph),
we add to
A^{\prime}
arc
e^{(0)}=(i,j)
with the cost
c_{e^{(0)}}=\min_{e=(u,v)\in A\,:\,u=i,v=j}\{C_{e}+\overline{c}_{e}\}

and the transition time
t_{e^{(0)}}=0
.
For each pair of nodes
i\in V_{g}
and
j\in V_{h}
,
g,h\in[H]
, such that the node
j
is reachable from
i
and

1\leq h-g\leq k
,
we add to
A^{\prime}
arc
e^{(1)}=(i,j)
with the cost
c_{e^{(1)}}
being the sum
of the cost of a shortest path
X^{*}_{ij}
from
i
to
j
under
C_{e}
,
e\in A
, and
the cost of a shortest path
Y^{*}_{ij}
from
i
to
j
under
\overline{c}_{e}
,
e\in A
.
The the transition time
t_{e^{(1)}}=|Y^{*}_{ij}\setminus X^{*}_{ij}|
. Since
G
is layered

|X^{*}_{ij}|=|Y^{*}_{ij}|=h-g
and thus

|Y^{*}_{ij}\setminus X^{*}_{ij}|\leq h-g
.
Finally, we set
T=k
.
It is not difficult to show that
there is a pair of paths
X\in\Phi
and
Y\in\Phi(X,k)
in
G
,
feasible to
Rec SP
, with the cost
\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\sum_{e\in Y}\overline{c}_{e}\leq UB

if and only if there is a path
\pi\in\Phi
in
G^{\prime}
, feasible to
CSP
,
such that
\sum_{e\in\pi}c_{e}\leq UB
, where
UB\in\mathbb{R}
.
The graph
G^{\prime}
can be constructed in
O(|A||V|)
time.
The number of arcs in
G^{\prime}
is
O(|V|^{2})
, so the corresponding
CSP
problem can be solved in
O(|V|^{2}k)
time. Hence the overall running time is
O(|A||V|+|V|^{2}k)
.
∎

2.2
General acyclic multidigraph

Theorem 2
.

Rec SP
with
\Phi(X,k)
in
an acyclic multidigraph
G=(V,A)
can solved in
O(|V|^{2}|A|k^{2})
time.

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
1
, we give
a polynomial time reduction from
Rec SP
to

CSP
in an acyclic multidigraph.
We need to extend the construction of the graph
G^{\prime}
, since
in general acyclic multidigraphs, disjoint paths
X^{*}_{ij}
and
Y^{*}_{ij}
from node
i
to node
j
can have various cardinalities,
so cheaper recoverable action can require choosing more arcs.
We fix
V^{\prime}=V
. The set of arcs
A^{\prime}
can now contain
k+1
types of arcs labeled as
e^{(0)},\dots,e^{(k)}
.
For each pair of nodes
i,j\in V
,
i\not=j
, such that there exists at least one arc
from
i
to
j
,
we add to
A^{\prime}
the arc
e^{(0)}=(i,j)
with the cost
c_{e^{(0)}}=\min_{e=(k,l)\in A\,:\,k=i,l=j}\{C_{e}+\overline{c}_{e}\}

and the transition time
t_{e^{(0)}}=0
.
Consider a pair of nodes
i,j\in V
,
i\not=j
, such that
j
is reachable from
i
. We first compute a path from
i
to
j
having the minimum number of arcs
L^{*}_{ij}
. If
L_{ij}^{*}>k
, then we do nothing. Otherwise, we find a shortest path
X^{*}_{ij}
from
i
to
j
for the costs
C_{e}
. Then, for each
l=L_{ij}^{*},\dots,k
, we find a shortest path
Y^{*l}_{ij}
for the costs
\overline{c}_{e}
,
e\in A
, subject to the condition that
Y^{*l}_{ij}
has at most
l
arcs. Observe that
Y^{*l}_{ij}
can be found in
O(|A|l)
time by solving
the
CSP
problem with arc transition times equal to 1 and
T=l
.
We add to
A^{\prime}
arc
e^{(l)}=(i,j)
with the cost
c_{e^{(l)}}=\sum_{e\in X^{*}_{ij}}C_{e}+\sum_{e\in Y^{l*}_{ij}}\overline{c}_{e}

and with the transition time
t_{e^{(l)}}=l
.
Finally, we set
T=k
.
It is not hard to show that
there is a pair of paths
X\in\Phi
and
Y\in\Phi(X,k)
in
G
,
feasible to
Rec SP
, with the cost
\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\sum_{e\in Y}\overline{c}_{e}\leq UB

if and only if there is a path
\pi\in\Phi
in
G^{\prime}
, feasible to
CSP
,
such that
\sum_{e\in\pi}c_{e}\leq UB
, where
UB\in\mathbb{R}
.
The graph
G^{\prime}
can be built in
O(|V|^{2}|A|k^{2})
time. The number of arcs in
G^{\prime}
is
O(|V|^{2}k)
and the resulting

CSP
problem can be solved in
O(|V|^{2}k^{2})
time. Thus the overall running time is
O(|V|^{2}|A|k^{2})
.
∎

2.3
Arc series-parallel multidigraph (ASP)

In
[
2
]
, Büsing gave an idea of a polynomial algorithm for

Rec SP
with
\Phi(X,k)
in an
ASP multidigraph together with the theorem resulting from it
[
2
, Theorem 5]
.

We now propose a complete
O(|A|k^{2})
-algorithm for ASP multidigraphs, which turned out to be far from trivial,
with a proof of correctness. The algorithm is as follows.
We first recall that an ASP multidigraph
G
is associated with a rooted binary
tree
\mathcal{T}
called
the binary decomposition tree
of
G
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).
Each leaf of the tree represents an arc in
G
. Each
internal node
\sigma
of
\mathcal{T}
is labeled
\mathtt{S}

or
\mathtt{P}
and corresponds to the series or parallel composition
in
G
. Every node
\sigma
of
\mathcal{T}
corresponds to an
ASP subgraph of
G
, denoted by
G_{\sigma}
, defined by the subtree rooted at
\sigma
.
The root of
\mathcal{T}
represents the input ASP multidigraph
G
.
For each ASP subgraph
G_{\sigma}
, we store the following three crucial pieces of information. Namely,
the first one, the cost of a shortest path from the source to the sink in
G_{\sigma}
with under costs
C_{e}
,
e\in A
:

C_{G_{\sigma}}=\min_{X\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}}\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}.

(3)

Here and subsequently,
\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}
denotes the set of all paths from the source to the sink in
G_{\sigma}
.
The second one, the
k
-element array
\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}
, whose
l
th element
\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]
,
l=1,\ldots,k
,
is the cost of a shortest path from the source to the sink in
G_{\sigma}
under the costs
\overline{c}_{e}
,
e\in A
,
that uses exactly
l
arcs – if such a path exists:

\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\min_{Y\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}\,:\,|Y|=l}\sum_{e\in Y}\overline{c}_{e}&\text{if $\{Y\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}\,:\,|Y|=l\}\not=\emptyset$,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4)

The third, the
(k+1)
-element array
\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}
, whose
l
th element
\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]
,

l=0,\ldots,k
,
stores the cost of an optimal pair
X^{*},Y^{*}\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}
such that
|Y^{*}\setminus X^{*}|=l
–
if such paths exist:

\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\min_{X,Y\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}\,:\,|Y\setminus X|=l}\left(\sum_{e\in X}C_{e}+\sum_{e\in Y}\overline{c}_{e}\right)&\text{if $\{X\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}}\,:$}\\
&\text{$(\exists Y\in\Phi_{G_{\sigma}})(|Y\setminus X|=l)\}\not=\emptyset$}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(5)

For each leaf node
\sigma
of the tree
\mathcal{T}
, in this case, the subgraph
G_{\sigma}
consists of
the single arc
e
,
e\in A
, the initial values in (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) are as follows:

C_{G_{\sigma}}=C_{e}
;

\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}[1]=\overline{c}_{e},\;\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]=+\infty,l=2,\ldots,k
;

\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[0]=C_{e}+\overline{c}_{e},\;\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]=+\infty,l=1,\ldots,k
.
By traversing the tree
\mathcal{T}
from the leaves to the root, we recursively
construct each ASP subgraph
G_{\sigma}
corresponding to the internal node
\sigma
and
compute
the values in (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) associated to
G_{\sigma}
,
depending on the label of the node
\sigma
.
If
\sigma
is marked
\mathtt{P}
, then
we call Algorithm
1
,
otherwise we call Algorithm
2
, on the subgraph
G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}
and
G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}
,
where
\mathrm{left}(\sigma)
and
\mathrm{right}(\sigma)
are children of
\sigma
in
\mathcal{T}
.
If
\sigma
is the root of
\mathcal{T}
, then
G_{\sigma}=G
and the array
\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}

contains information about the cost
of an optimal
pair of paths
X^{*},Y^{*}\in\Phi
,
of the optimal solution to
Rec SP
in
G
, which is equal to
\min_{0\leq l\leq k}\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]
.
For simplicity of the presentation, we have shown only how to compute the costs stored in
C_{G_{\sigma}}
,

\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}
and
\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}
. The associated paths can easily
be reconstructed.

Let
G_{\sigma}
be the
parallel composition of
G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}
and
G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}
;

1

\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[0]\leftarrow\min\{\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}}[0],\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}[0]\}
;

C_{G_{\sigma}}\leftarrow\min\{C_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}},C_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}\}
;

2

for

l=1

to

k

3

\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]\leftarrow\min\{\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}}[l],\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}[l],C_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}[l],C_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}}[l]\}

;

4

\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\sigma}}[l]\leftarrow\min\{\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}}[l],\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}}[l]\}

return

(G_{\sigma},c^{*}_{G_{\sigma}},C_{G_{\sigma}},\overline{c}_{G_{\sigma}})

Algorithm 1

\mathtt{P}(G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)},\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}},\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{left}(\sigma)}},G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)},\boldsymbol{c}^{*}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}},C_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}},\overline{\boldsymbol{c}}_{G_{\mathrm{right}(\sigma)}})

Theorem 3
.

Rec SP
with
\Phi(X,k)
in an

Proof.

We first prove that for each node
\sigma

\mathcal{T}
,

is the ASP subgraph of
G

3
), (
4
) and (

]

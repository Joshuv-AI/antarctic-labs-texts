Semiorthogonal indecomposability of minimal irregular surfaces
Shinnosuke Okawa

Abstract
. We prove a relative version of the fact that semiorthogonal decompositions of the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves are strongly constrained by the base locus of the canonical linear system. As an application we prove that the derived category of minimal surfaces X X with H 1 ​ ( X , 𝒪 X ) ≠ 0 H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0 are semiorthogonally indecomposable.



1.
Introduction

It is impossible to stress enough the significance of the relative point of view for the minimal model theory.
Even if one is only interested in the birational geometry of a variety

X
, in many places it is useful to choose an appropriate morphism

f\colon X\to Y

and consider the geometry of

X

relative to

f

This paper is devoted to bringing this insight into the study of semiorthogonal decomposition (SOD) of the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves and demonstrating its usefulness by showing the semiorthogonal indecomposability of minimal surfaces with positive irregularity.

A guiding principal for the study of SODs of the bounded derived category

\mathbf{D}(X)=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}\operatorname{coh}X

. It asserts that a step of a minimal model program

X\dasharrow Y

(or more generally a

K_{X}>K_{Y}
)
induces an SOD of

\mathbf{D}(X)

by
“
\mathbf{D}(Y)
”
and some other components; since

could be singular even if

X

is nonsingular, part of the hypothesis is to define the category
“
\mathbf{D}(Y)
”
suitably (see, say,
[
Kaw18
]
and references therein). Note that the hypothesis implies that if

\mathbf{D}(X)

is indecomposable, then

X

should be minimal.

On the other hand, the other implication

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SOD}\Rightarrow\mathrm{MMP}

does not always hold in dimensions at least

2
:

Example 1.1
.

Suppose that

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0

for

i>0
.
Then any invertible sheaf on

X

is an exceptional object and hence decomposes

\mathbf{D}(X)
.
There are several examples of minimal surfaces of Kodaira dimensions

or

2

of this sort. They are rich sources of (quasi-)phantom categories (see, say,
[
KO15
,
Kuz14
,
GO13
]
and references therein).

Nevertheless, known results seem to suggest the following bold hypothesis.

Hypothesis 1.2
.

For almost all smooth projective variety

X

which is minimal, the derived category

\mathbf{D}(X)

admits no nontrivial semiorthogonal decomposition.

Making the phrase ‘almost all’ in

as precise as possible is part of the problem.
Note that it is a special case of the following even bolder hypothesis. One can tell from experience, however, that

is the essential part (see
[

, Proposition 5.7]
).

Hypothesis 1.3
.

For almost all smooth projective variety

X

which is
not necessarily
minimal, any SOD of the derived category

\mathbf{D}(X)

comes from MMPs starting with

X

through the DK hypothesis up to autoequivalences of

\mathbf{D}(X)

Namely, for almost all

X

the implication (

) holds (up to autoequivalences).

In this paper we add a new technique for investigating SODs of

2.
Basics of relative semiorthogonal decomposition

The aim of this section is to generalize the rudiments of semiorthogonal decompositions to the relative setting as much as possible.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a regular noetherian scheme of finite dimension. We let

\mathbf{D}(X)

denote the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
.

By the theorem of Serre, the natural embedding

{\mathrm{perf}}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{D}(X)

is full for

as in
Definition

.

We use the Grothendieck duality in an essential way:

Theorem 2.2

(
=
[
Har66
, Chapter VII Corollary 3.4 (c)]
)

Let

(2.1)

\displaystyle f\colon X\to Y

be a proper morphism of finite dimensional noetherian schemes.
Then there is a functor

(2.2)

\displaystyle f^{!}\colon\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\to\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)

which is right adjoint to the derived pushforward functor

(2.3)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\colon\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)\to\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(Y)

in the sense that there exists a bi-functorial isomorphism in

\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(Y)

as follows for any

\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{-}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)

and

\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{+}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
.

(2.4)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{X}(\mathcal{F},f^{!}\mathcal{G})\to\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{Y}(f_{\ast}\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})

The following special case of

is particularly relevant to us (see

3.8
and
3.9
for the notion of dualizing complexes).

Corollary 2.3
.

In the situation of
Theorem

2.2
,
there is a natural isomorphism as follows for any

, where

(2.5)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{X}(G,F\otimes\omega^{\bullet}_{X})\simeq\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{Y}(f_{\ast}\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{X}(F,G),\omega^{\bullet}_{Y})

Definition 2.4
.

Let

•

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\mathop{\mathbb{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits_{X}(b,a)=0

We use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle_{f}

An

-linear semiorthogonal decomposition for the structure morphism

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle.

3.
Relative semiorthogonal indecomposability

3.1.
Relative base locus

Definition 3.1
.

Let

f\colon X\to Y

be a morphism of algebraic spaces, and let
L
be an invertible sheaf on
X
.
The
base locus of
L
relative to
f
or the
(relative) base locus of
L
over
Y
is the closed subspace
\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(L)\hookrightarrow X
whose structure sheaf is defined as follows.

(3.1)

\displaystyle f^{\ast}f_{\ast}L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}L^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(L)}\to 0

We call
\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(\omega_{X})
the
(relative) canonical base locus of
f
or the
canonical base locus of
X
over
Y
(relative to
f
)
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
Y
is affine in
Definition

3.1
, then the first map of (
3.1
) is canonically isomorphic to the map

H^{0}(X,L)\otimes L^{-1}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
and hence

\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(L)

is nothing but the usual base locus

\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}(L)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

In the situation of
Definition

3.1
, the following equality of closed subspaces of
X
holds for any invertible sheaf

M

Y

(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(L)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Bs}}_{f}(L\otimes f^{\ast}M)

Proof.

The projection formula

(3.3)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\left(L\otimes f^{\ast}M\right)\simeq f_{\ast}L\otimes M

implies

(3.4)

\displaystyle f^{\ast}f_{\ast}\left(L\otimes f^{\ast}M\right)\otimes\left(L\otimes f^{\ast}M\right)^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\eqref{equation:projection formula}}}}{{\simeq}}f^{\ast}f_{\ast}L\otimes f^{\ast}M\otimes\left(L\otimes f^{\ast}M\right)^{-1}\simeq f^{\ast}f_{\ast}L\otimes L^{-1}.

The assertion immediately follows from this.

Resonant Parametric Photon Generation in Waveguide-coupled Quantum Emitter Arrays
Egor S. Vyatkin, Alexander V. Poshakinskiy, Alexander N. Poddubny

Abstract
We have developed a theory of parametric photon generation in the waveguides coupled to arrays of quantum emitters with temporally modulated resonance frequencies. Such generation can be interpreted as a dynamical Casimir effect. We demonstrate numerically and analytically how the emission directionality and photon-photon correlations can be controlled by the phases of the modulation. The emission spectrum is shown to be strongly dependent on the anharmonicity of the emitter potential. Single- and double-excited state resonances have been identified in the emission spectrum.



I
Introduction

Waveguide quantum electrodynamics, describing photon interaction with arrays of emitters coupled to the waveguide, is now rapidly developing
Sheremet et al. 2023
;
Roy et al. 2017
;
Chang et al. 2018
. This platform allows controllable generation of quantum light
Prasad et al. 2020
, and control over
lifetimes
Zanner et al. 2022
and entanglement

Kannan et al. 2020
;
Corzo et al. 2019
of coupled atom-photon excitations.
Even more possibilities are opened in the structures with the parameters dynamically modulated in time
Silveri et al. 2017
. This enables Floquet engineering and realization of synthetic dimensions
Ozawa et al. 2019
;
Yuan et al. 2018
as well as control of quantum photon-photon correlations
Ilin et al. 2023
;
Jolin et al. 2023
. Such time modulation has been recently demonstrated, for example, for the superconducting transmon emitter platform
Redchenko et al. 2023
.

One more fundamental physical effect, that becomes possible in the
dynamically modulated structures, is the parametric generation of photon pairs. Such generation can be also interpreted as a dynamical Casimir effect. This effect has been first proposed for the cavity with a moving wall
Moore 1970
. To the best of our knowledge, it has been never directly observed in this setup so far, because
of the extremely low photon generation rate at realistic parameters, see the review
Dodonov 2020
. However, there also exist generalized dynamical Casimir effects, where other electromagnetic properties of the medium are changing instead of physical movement of the mirror in space. For example, parametric photon generation due to electron-hole plasma generated and moving in a semiconductor under the laser pulse excitation has been theoretically considered in Ref.
Lozovik et al. 1995
. A seminal observation of an analogue of the dynamical Casimir effect in a superconducting circuit was made in Ref.
Wilson et al. 2011
. The effective length of the transmission line has been modulated by changing the inductance of a superconducting quantum interference device. A detailed theoretical analysis of such systems has been performed in Refs.
Johansson et al. 2010
;
Macrì et al. 2018
.
Two-photon entanglement in this setup has been experimentally studied in Ref.
Schneider et al. 2020
.
However, the consideration has been limited to the case when the modulated elements were not resonant for the generated photons. Dynamical Casimir effect in the arrays of resonant modulated emitters is not yet explored. The resonant structures could potentially allow one to selectively enhance the photon generation and control the correlations between them. Recently, a theory of dynamical Casimir effect in the general dynamically modulated photonic structures has been put forward in Ref.
Sloan et al. 2021
. However, considered setup did not include two-photon interactions, essential for the emitter platform.

Here, we consider a parametric photon generation by an array of emitters with strongly dynamically modulated resonant frequencies, coupled to the waveguide. In such a system there exist resonances for generated photons and photon pairs near the single- and double-excited levels of the emitters. Our goal is to explore the role of the various collective emitter resonances for the intensity, directionality and the quantum correlations between the emitted photons.

The structure under consideration is schematically illustrated in Fig.

H_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\omega_{0}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\frac{U}{2}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}a_{j}\right)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Re}\nolimits(D_{jk})a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k}

(1)

\omega_{0}

2\omega_{0}+U

U\ll\omega_{0}

D_{jk}=-{\rm i}\gamma_{1\rm{D}}e^{{\rm i}\omega_{0}|x_{j}-x_{k}|/c}\;,

(2)

where
\gamma_{1\rm{D}}

(3)

Here,
g_{j}

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}=-{\rm i}[H,\rho]+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\gamma_{jk}(2a_{j}\rho a_{k}^{\dagger}-a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k}\rho-\rho a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k})\;,

where

Blais et al. 2021
and their parameters satisfy the regime

considered here. For example, the parameters of Ref.
Zanner et al. 2022
correspond to
\omega_{0}\approx 7~
GHz,
U\sim 0.2~
GHz,
\gamma_{\rm 1D}\approx 0.03~
GHz,
\gamma\sim 10^{-3}
GHz.

Hence, we can expect appearance of additional sharp spectral features in

V
Summary

To summarize, we have developed a general theory of parametric photon generation from arrays of emitter coupled to the waveguide, that are modulated in time.
Using the two independent approaches, the master equation for the density matrix and the diagrammatic Green-function technique,
we have studied the dependence of the photon emission spectrum and photon-photon correlation functions on the anharmonicity of the emitter potential
U
, the distance between the neighboring emitters and the relative modulation phase
\varphi
.

The calculated emission spectrum is very sensitive to the emitter anharmonicity parameter. The anharmonicity controls the relative weight of the spectral features around the single- and double-excited emitter resonances. The latter become more prominent with the increase of the anharmonicity. When the number of emitters is
N=4
or larger, additional sharp spectral features, corresponding to the double-excited subradiant states, appear in the spectrum.
We have also shown that the interference between photons emitted from different emitters can be controlled by relative phases of their frequency modulation. Our calculation demonstrates how this can be used to obtain directional photon pair emission, similarly as it happens for non-parametric quantum photon sources
Kannan et al. 2023
;
Redchenko et al. 2023
;
Poddubny and Golub 2021
.

We hope that our results will be useful for engineering the parametric quantum emission from the waveguide-coupled emitter arrays. A potentially interesting future research direction could be the system, where the spatial position of the light emitters, rather than their resonance frequency, oscillates in time. This would mean generalization of our concept of optomechanical Kerker effect
Poshakinskiy and Poddubny 2019
, that is motion-induced direcitonal emission, to the quantum optics regime.

Appendix A
Master equation approach

In order to find a stationary solution for the density matrix, we represent

where the operators

and
\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-{\rm i}(H_{0}\otimes 1-1\otimes H_{0})

(11)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\gamma_{jk}(2a_{j}\otimes a_{k}-a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k}\otimes 1-1\otimes a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{j})\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=-{\rm i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}e^{{\rm i}\varphi_{j}}\left((a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j})^{2}\otimes 1-1\otimes(a_{j}^{\dagger}+a_{j})^{2}\right)\;,

(12)

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}+{\rm i}\Omega)^{-1}\mathcal{V}^{*}\bm{\rho}(0)\;,

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bm{\rho}_{0}=-\frac{1}{4}

The stationary density matrix enables us to calculate the emission intensity. Note that the harmonics with
n=\pm 1
do not contribute to the intensity

\displaystyle I_{1}=\langle a^{\dagger}a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits(\rho(t)a^{\dagger}a)\rangle_{t}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits(\rho_{0}a^{\dagger}a)=(\rho_{0})_{22}+2(\rho_{0})_{33}\;.

This yields Eq. (
5
) in the main text.

A.2
Emission spectrum of a single emitter

We define emission spectrum as

S(\omega)=2\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Re}\nolimits\int_{0}^{2\pi/\Omega}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\;e^{-{\rm i}\omega\tau}\langle a^{\dagger}(t^{\prime}+\tau)a(t^{\prime})\rangle\;.

(19)

The integration over
t^{\prime}
is performed over the period
2\pi/\Omega
since the perturbation is periodic. According to the quantum regression theorem
Carmichael 1993

S(\omega)=2\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Re}\nolimits\int_{0}^{2\pi/\Omega}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\;e^{-{\rm i}\omega\tau}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits(a^{\dagger}\rho^{a}(t^{\prime}+\tau))\;,

(20)

where
\rho^{a}(t)
satisfies Eq. (
10
) with the initial condition
\rho^{a}(t=t^{\prime})=a\rho(t^{\prime})
which can be represented in the integral form as

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}^{a}(t^{\prime}+\tau)

\displaystyle=e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}\bm{\rho}^{a}(t^{\prime})

(21)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}e^{\mathcal{L}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}(t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})\bm{\rho}^{a}(t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})\;,

where the perturbation
\mathcal{F}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{V}e^{{\rm i}\Omega t}-\mathcal{V}^{*}e^{-i\Omega t}\right)
and initial
\rho^{a}(t^{\prime})=a\rho_{0}+a\rho_{-1}e^{{\rm i}\Omega t^{\prime}}+a\rho_{1}e^{-{\rm i}\Omega t^{\prime}}
. Then up to the second order in
g
, it reads

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}^{a}(t^{\prime}+\tau)=e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}\bm{\rho}^{a}(t^{\prime})

(22)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}e^{\mathcal{L}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\mathcal{F}(t^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})e^{\mathcal{L}\tau^{\prime}}\left(\bm{\rho}^{a}_{-1}e^{{\rm i}\Omega t^{\prime}}+\bm{\rho}^{a}_{1}e^{-{\rm i}\Omega t^{\prime}}\right)\>.

Performing averaging over
t^{\prime}
and considering that
\mathcal{V}^{*}=-\mathcal{V}
for a single emitter we get

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\rho}^{a}(

\displaystyle t^{\prime}+\tau)\rangle_{t^{\prime}}=e^{\mathcal{L}\tau}\bm{\rho}_{0}^{a}

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}e^{\mathcal{L}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\mathcal{V}e^{\mathcal{L}\tau^{\prime}}\left(\bm{\rho}^{a}_{-1}e^{-{\rm i}\Omega\tau^{\prime}}+\bm{\rho}^{a}_{1}e^{{\rm i}\Omega\tau^{\prime}}\right)\>.

This enable us to get the emission spectrum

S(\omega)=2\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Re}\nolimits\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\;e^{-{\rm i}\omega\tau}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits(a^{\dagger}\langle\rho^{a}(t^{\prime}+\tau)\rangle_{t^{\prime}})\;.

(24)

The result of the calculation according to Eq. (
24
) is shown in Fig.
2
.

Appendix B
Diagrammatic approach

In case of weak modulation, only the states with small number of excitations are populated. They can be described in the framework of perturbative diagrammatic approach. The consideration generalizes the results of Refs.
Fang et al. 2014
;
Poshakinskiy and Poddubny 2016
;
Ke et al. 2019
and also Ref.
Poddubny et al. 2016
for the structures, modulated in time. Somewhat similar consideration for time-modulated structures is also available in Ref.
Ilin et al. 2023
, but that work does not consider parametric photon generation.

The diagram representing the (not normalized)
wavefunction of the generated photon pair is shown in Fig.
7
(a).
The dashed line represents the modulation that creates a pair of emitter excitations (solid lines). Then, the excitations propagate in the structure, which is described by the Green functions (solid lines). The excitations can either get converted to the photons directly (first diagram) or interact and get converted to photons after that (second diagram). For the case when both photons are emitted in the left direction this yields

\displaystyle\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm 1D}\sum_{ij}s_{i}^{+}(\omega_{1})s_{i}^{+}(\omega_{2})[1+(M\Sigma)_{ij}]g_{j}

\displaystyle\times 2\pi\delta(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\Omega)\,.

(25)

Here, the Green function of single excitation reads

\displaystyle G(\omega)=(\omega-H)^{-1},\qquad H_{ij}=\omega_{0}\delta_{ij}+D_{ij}\,,

(26)

the outer lines of the diagrams correspond to

\displaystyle s_{i}^{+}(\omega)=\sum_{j}G_{ij}(\omega){\rm e}^{{\rm i}qz_{j}}=\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}qz_{1}}}{{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}}\left(\frac{\omega_{0}-H}{\omega-H}\right)_{1i}\,,

(27)

and the propagation of a pair of excitations is described by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ij}(\Omega)={\rm i}\int G_{ij}(\omega)G_{ij}(\Omega-\omega)\,\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,.

(28)

The dressed interaction vertex
M
can be determined from the Dyson-like equation shown diagrammatically in Fig.
7
(b), which yields

\displaystyle M(\Omega)=[U^{-1}-\Sigma(\Omega)]^{-1}\,.

(29)

Substituting this into Eq. (
B
) we finally obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{\rm 1D}}{U}\sum_{ij}s_{i}^{+}(\omega_{1})s_{i}^{+}(\omega_{2})M_{ij}g_{j}

\displaystyle\times 2\pi\delta(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\Omega)\,.

(30)

The integration in Eq. (
28
) can be performed explicitly, which yields a compact expression for the
M
matrix:

\displaystyle M_{ij}(\Omega)=\left[\frac{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H-\mathcal{U}}\,\mathcal{U}\right]_{ii,jj}

(31)

where
(A\otimes B)_{ij,kl}\equiv A_{ik}B_{jl}
,
\frac{A}{B}\equiv AB^{-1}
and we introduced the diagonal
N^{2}\times N^{2}
matrix
\mathcal{U}_{ij,kl}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\delta_{ik}U
.

Figure 7:
(a) Diagrams representing the amplitude of two-photon generation by modulated emitters. Dashed line represents the modulation, solid lines are the Green functions of emitter excitations, wavy lines are the outgoing photons. (b) The diagrammatic equation for the dressed vertex (solid circle) that describes the interaction of two emitter excitations. Open circle represents the bare vertex corresponding to the interaction amplitude
U
.

B.1
Correlation function

The second-order correlation function of the emitted photon pair is calculated as

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\left|\int\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\omega_{1}t-{\rm i}\omega_{2}(t+\tau)}\frac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\right|^{2}\>.

(32)

At zero delay we use the representation Eq. (
31
) to obtain

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(0)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{4}}\left|\sum_{i}\left[\frac{(\omega_{0}-H)\otimes(\omega_{0}-H)}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H-\mathcal{U}}\right]_{11,ii}g_{i}\right|^{2}

(33)

In the limit
U\gg\gamma_{\rm 1D}
and for
|\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U|\lesssim\gamma_{\rm 1D}
, we keep in the denominator of Eq. (
33
) only the diagonal terms and find

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(0)=\dfrac{\left|\sum\limits_{i}g_{i}\,{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}qz_{i}}\right|^{2}}{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\,,

(34)

where we used
(\omega_{0}-H)_{1i}={\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}q(z_{i}-z_{1})}
.

For
|\Omega-2\omega_{0}|\sim\gamma_{\rm 1D}
, the result is

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(0)=\frac{1}{U^{2}}\left|\sum_{ij}\Sigma^{+}_{i}(\Omega)[\Sigma^{-1}(\Omega)]_{ij}g_{j}\right|^{2},

(35)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ij}(\Omega)=\left[\frac{1}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H}\right]_{ii,jj},

(36)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{+}_{i}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\left[\frac{(\omega_{0}-H)\otimes(\omega_{0}-H)}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H}\right]_{11,ii}.

(37)

B.2
Emission intensity

We define the intensity of emission to the left as

\displaystyle I_{-}=\int\left[|\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}+|\psi_{-+}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}\right]\frac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}T(2\pi)^{2}}\>,

(38)

where
T
is the normalization time and
\psi_{-+}
is the wave function of a pair of photons emitted in the opposite directions. The latter is obtained from Eq. (
B
) by replacing the factor
s_{j}^{+}(\omega_{2})
with
s_{j}^{-}(\omega_{2})=\sum_{i}G_{ij}(\omega){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}qz_{i}}
.

Using the representation Eq. (
31
), we get

\displaystyle\int|\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}\tfrac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}}{T(2\pi)^{2}}=\frac{1}{{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\sum_{ij}g_{i}g_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle\Big[

\displaystyle\frac{(\omega_{0}-H_{1})(\omega_{0}-H_{2})(\omega_{0}-H_{3}^{*})(\omega_{0}-H_{4}^{*})}{(\Omega-H_{1}-H_{4}^{*})}

(39)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\Omega-H_{3}^{*}-H_{4}^{*}}{H_{1}-H_{3}^{*}}-\frac{\Omega-H_{1}-H_{2}}{H_{4}^{*}-H_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(\Omega-H_{1}-H_{2}-\mathcal{U}_{12})(\Omega-H_{3}^{*}-H_{4}^{*}-\mathcal{U}_{34})}\Big]_{1111,iijj}

and the corresponding integral of
|\psi_{-+}|^{2}
is obtained in the same way by taking the element
[\ldots]_{1N1N,iijj}
. Here
H_{1}=H\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1
,
H_{2}=1\otimes H\otimes 1\otimes 1
etc.

In the limit
U\gg\gamma_{\rm 1D}
and for
|\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U|\lesssim\gamma_{\rm 1D}
, the result can be simplified:

\displaystyle\int|

\displaystyle\psi_{--}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}\tfrac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}}{T(2\pi)^{2}}=\frac{2{\rm i}}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}[(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}]}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{ij}g_{i}g_{j}^{*}(\omega_{0}-H)_{1i}(\omega_{0}-H^{*})_{1j}{\mathcal{Q}}_{11,ij}\>,

(40)

\displaystyle\int|

\displaystyle\psi_{-+}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|^{2}\tfrac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}}{T(2\pi)^{2}}=\frac{{\rm i}}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}[(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}]}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}g_{i}g_{j}^{*}\Big\{(\omega_{0}-H)_{1i}(\omega_{0}-H^{*})_{1j}\mathcal{Q}_{NN,ij}

(41)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\omega_{0}-H)_{Ni}(\omega_{0}-H^{*})_{Nj}\mathcal{Q}_{11,ij}\Big\}\>,

where

\mathcal{Q}=\frac{(\omega_{0}-H)\otimes(\omega_{0}-H^{*})}{(\omega_{0}-H)\otimes 1-1\otimes(\omega_{0}-H^{*})}\>.

(42)

B.3
Optical theorem

We assume that the modulation of the emitters is performed by an external signal. The back-action of the emitter system on that signal can be accounted by introducing the scattering parameter
s
.
Up to the second order in
g
, it reads

\displaystyle s=1-{\rm i}\sum_{ij}g_{i}^{*}\tilde{\Sigma}_{ij}g_{j},

(43)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{ij}=\left[\frac{1}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H-\mathcal{U}}\right]_{ii,jj}\>.

(44)

In the absence of non-radiative losses, the optical theorem imposes
2|s|^{2}+I_{+}+I_{-}=2
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle I_{+}+I_{-}

(45)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{\gamma_{\rm 1D}}{\rm Im\,}\sum_{ij}g_{i}^{*}g_{j}\left[\frac{1}{\Omega-H\otimes 1-1\otimes H-\mathcal{U}}\right]_{ii,jj}\>.

In the limit
U\gg\gamma_{\rm 1D}
,
|\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U|\lesssim\gamma_{\rm 1D}
, the result is trivial

\displaystyle I_{+}+I_{-}=\frac{8}{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\,\sum_{i}|g_{i}|^{2}\>.

(46)

B.4
Emission spectrum of a single emitter

The emission spectrum can be calculated as

S(\omega)=\int\frac{|\psi_{--}|^{2}+|\psi_{-+}|^{2}}{2\pi T\gamma_{\rm 1D}}d\omega^{\prime}\;.

(47)

In the case of a single emitter the wave function
\psi_{--}=\psi_{-+}
and it can be found from Eq. (
B
)

\displaystyle\psi_{--}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\frac{g\gamma_{\rm 1D}\left(\Omega-2\omega_{0}+2{\rm i}\gamma_{1\rm D}\right)}{\left(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U+2{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}\right)}

(48)

\displaystyle\times\frac{2\pi\delta(\Omega-\omega-\omega^{\prime})}{\left(\omega-\omega_{0}+{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}\right)\left(\omega^{\prime}-\omega_{0}+{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D}\right)}\;.

The integration in Eq. (
47
) yields Eq. (
6
) in the main text.

Appendix C
Two emitters

Here we apply the results of Appendix
B

for the system of two emitters. The general explicit expressions are quite bulky so consider two special cases.

C.1
U\gg\gamma_{\rm 1D}
and
|\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U|\lesssim\gamma_{\rm 1D}

In that limit, we get

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(0)=\frac{\left|g_{1}+g_{2}{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}qd}\right|^{2}}{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}

(49)

and

I_{-}=\frac{1}{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U)^{2}+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}}\\
\times\frac{(7-3\cos 2qd)|g_{1}|^{2}+(5-\cos 2qd)|g_{2}|^{2}+2\sin 2qd\,\text{Im\,}g_{1}g_{2}^{*}}{3-\cos 2qd}\>.

(50)

This yields Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
) in the main text.

C.2
Symmetric modulation,
g_{1}=g_{2}=g

In that case, the two-photon emission is

\displaystyle G^{(2)}_{--}(0)=4|g|^{2}

(51)

\displaystyle\times\left|\frac{(\Omega-2\omega_{0})\cos qd+2\gamma_{\rm 1D}\sin qd}{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}+2{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D})(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U+2{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D})+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}qd}}\right|^{2}.

Note that
G^{(2)}_{--}(0)
vanishes at

\Omega=2\omega_{0}-2\gamma_{\rm 1D}\tan qd
.

The single-photon emission intensity is found easily from the optical theorem

\displaystyle I_{+}=I_{-}=8|g|^{2}

(52)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(\Omega-2\omega_{0}+\gamma_{\rm 1D}\sin 2qd)^{2}+2\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}(3-\cos 2qd)\sin^{2}qd}{\left|(\Omega-2\omega_{0}+2{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D})(\Omega-2\omega_{0}-U+2{\rm i}\gamma_{\rm 1D})+4\gamma_{\rm 1D}^{2}{\rm e}^{2{\rm i}qd}\right|^{2}}\>.

We note that
I_{\pm}
has a minimum at
\Omega\approx 2\omega_{0}-\gamma_{\rm 1D}\sin 2qd
. In particular, for
qd=0
, we have
I_{\pm}(\Omega=2\omega_{0})=0
.

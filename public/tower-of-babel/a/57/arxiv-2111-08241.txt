Abstract
. In this note, notwithstanding the generalization, we simplify and shorten the proofs of the main results of the third author’s paper [ 23 ] significantly. In particular, the new proof for [ 23 , Theorem 1.1] is quite short and, unlike the original proof, does not rely on the properties of the “Marcinkiewicz function”. This allows us to get a precise linear dependence on Dini constants with a subsequent application to Littlewood–Paley operators by well-known techniques. In other words, we relax the log-Dini condition in the pointwise bound to the classical Dini condition. This solves an open problem (see e.g. [ 5 , pp. 37–38] ). Our method can be applied to the multilinear case.

Introduction

We seek a sharp sparse estimate for Littlewood–Paley operators.
The class of integral kernels is wide in the sense that
we allow the moduli
\varphi
and
w
of continuity
to satisfy the Dini condition

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi(t)+w(t)}{t}dt<\infty
.
Here by a modulus of continuity,
we mean a positive increasing function on
(0,1)
.
Write
\Gamma_{\alpha}(x)
for the cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
of aperture
\alpha>1

centered at
x
, that is,
where

(1.1)

\Gamma_{\alpha}(x)=\{(y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}:|x-y|<\alpha t\}.

Let
S_{\alpha,\phi}
be the square function defined
by means of a standard kernel
\phi
as follows:

(1.2)

S_{\alpha,\phi}f(x)=\Big(\iint_{\Gamma_{\alpha}(x)}|f\star\phi_{t}(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\phi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\phi(x/t)
and
\star
refers
to the convolution operation of two functions.

The study on the linear/multilinear square functions has important applications
in PDEs and other fields
of mathematics.
For further details on the theory of linear multilinear square functions and their applications,
we refer to
[
4
,
19
,
18
,
23
]
and the references therein.

In
[
18
]
, Lerner
proved sharp weighted norm inequalities for
S_{\alpha,\phi}f

by applying
intrinsic square functions
introduced in
[
24
]
.
Later on, Lerner himself improved the result—he
obtained the sharp dependence on
\alpha
—in
[
19
]
by using the local mean
oscillation formula.
Motivated by these works,
many authors obtained many important results
(see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
]
).
Recall that
a modulus of continuity is an increasing concave function defined on
(0,1)
.
Let
\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
be a modulus of continuity which satisfies
the Dini condition.
That is,

\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)

is an increasing function such that

[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}:=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}dt+\varphi(1)<\infty
.
In the last years, there have been several advances in the fruitful area of
weighted inequalities concerning the precise determination of the optimal
bounds of the weighted operator norm of linear and bilinear Calderón-Zygmund
operators with a Dini continuous kernel in terms of the
A_{p}
constant
of weights (see e.g.
[
9
,
20
]
and the references therein).
The algorithm to obtain sparse domination is formulated in
[
20
]
in
general
and
can be used to study both standard Calderón-Zygmund operators and square
functions.
However, in order to obtain estimates
for kernels satisfying the Dini condition, the main obstacle is
the endpoint estimate and its bound.
In fact, under the Dini assumptions,
some weak type tricks have not been available until now.

The thrust of relaxing the
\log
-Dini condition to the Dini condition
comes from the works
[
16
,
17
]
.
In fact, in these papers, the authors obtained
the sharp estimates
for singular integral operators whose kernels satisfy
the Dini condition.
Thus, it is natural to ask ourselves whether
a counterpart to the Littlewood–Paley operators
is available.

We would like to point out the difference
between the proofs of the main results
of this paper and

[
23
]
,
where
the authors in

[
23
]

assumed the
\log
-Dini condition.
To do so, we use the operators

defined via the cone
(
1.1
) for
\alpha=1

and

defined via rapidly decaying weights.
We refer to (

)

2.1.
Definition

Let us recall the definition of square functions considered in this paper.

Let
\psi(x,y)
be a
real-valued locally integrable function defined away from the diagonal

x=y
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
Let
\varphi,\,w
be moduli of continuity. We assume that there is a positive
constant
A
so that the following conditions hold
for any
x,y,h\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:

Size condition:

(2.1)

|\psi(x,y)|\leq A{\mathcal{M}}1_{Q(x,1)}(y)w\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\Big).

Smoothness condition:

Whenever
|h|<\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
,

(2.2)

|\psi(x,y)-\psi(x+h,y)|\leq A{\mathcal{M}}1_{Q(x,1)}(y)\varphi\Bigl(\frac{|h|}{1+|x-y|}\Bigr)w\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\Big)

and

(2.3)

|\psi(x,y)-\psi(x,y+h)|\leq A{\mathcal{M}}1_{Q(x,1)}(y)\varphi\Bigl(\frac{|h|}{1+|x-y|}\Bigr)w\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\Big).

For
t>0
we define a linear operator
\psi_{t}
by

\psi_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi\Big(\frac{x}{t},\frac{y}{t}\Big)f(y)dy

.
For

and

and

2.2.
Auxiliary estimates

First, for the case of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we verify how strongly
the operator norm depends on
the aperture
\alpha
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
. Then

\|S_{\alpha,\psi}f\|_{L^{2}}=\alpha^{n/2}\|S_{1,\psi}f\|_{L^{2}}

for all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Then

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,\psi}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\iint_{\Gamma_{\alpha(x)}}|\psi_{t}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}|\psi_{t}f(y)|^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}1_{\{|y-x|<\alpha t\}}(x)dx\right)\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=v_{n}\alpha^{n}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}|\psi_{t}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}.

If we let
\alpha=1
in the above, then we obtain

\|S_{1,\psi}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=v_{n}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}|\psi_{t}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}.

From these two equalities, we obtain the desired result.
∎

We will use the following lemma several times:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
.
Let
w
be a modulus of continuity satisfying the Dini condition.

(a)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{(1+t)^{n}}w\left(\frac{4t}{t+1}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim[w]_{\rm{Dini}}.

(b)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{(t+1)^{n}}w\left((1+\alpha)t\right)\right\}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}^{2}.

(c)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2^{k+1}}\right)\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}.

(d)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}w(t)\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}.

(e)

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}\leq[w^{\frac{1}{m}}]^{m}_{\rm{Dini}}
.

Proof.

(a)

Simply change variables
u=\frac{4t}{t+1}
.
We omit further details.

(b)

Write
I_{1}^{\prime}
for the integral on the left-hand side.
We decompose the integral defining
I^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}
into three parts.

\displaystyle I^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\cdots+\int_{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}^{1}\cdots+\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots

\displaystyle\leq w(1)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\frac{w\left((2+\alpha)t\right)}{t}dt+w^{2}(1)\int_{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}^{1}\frac{dt}{t}+w^{2}(1)\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(t+1)^{2n}}.

\displaystyle\leq w(1)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}+w^{2}(1)\log(2+\alpha)+w^{2}(1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}^{2}.

(c)

Write
I_{2}^{\prime}
for the sum on the left-hand side.
By the integral test, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2^{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq w(1)\log(2+\alpha)+\int_{1}^{\infty}w\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2^{x}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim w(1)\log(2+\alpha)+\int_{0}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt.

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt+w(1)\int_{1}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{t}dt.

\displaystyle\lesssim\log(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}.

(d)

Argue similar to
(c)
.

(e)

We calculate

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w(t)}{t}dt\leq w^{\frac{m-1}{m}}(1)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{w^{\frac{1}{m}}(t)}{t}dt\leq[w^{\frac{1}{m}}]^{m-1}_{\rm{Dini}}[w^{\frac{1}{m}}]_{\rm{Dini}}.

∎

The above estimates concern
how to use the Dini condition
for a
1
-dimensional integral.
Now we show how to use

Lemma 2.6

Let

Proof.

Fix

.
We calculate

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|)^{n}}w\left(\frac{2^{k+m}\ell(Q)}{2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{2^{k}Q}\frac{1}{(2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|)^{n}}w\left(\frac{2^{k+m}\ell(Q)}{2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|}\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+l}Q\setminus 2^{k+l-1}Q}\frac{1}{(2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|)^{n}}w\left(\frac{2^{k+m}\ell(Q)}{2^{k}\ell(Q)+|x-c(Q)|}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2^{k}Q}\frac{1}{(2^{k}\ell(Q))^{n}}w(2^{m})dx+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+l}Q\setminus 2^{k+l-1}Q}\frac{1}{(2^{k+l-2}\ell(Q))^{n}}w\left(\frac{2^{k+m}\ell(Q)}{2^{k+l-2}\ell(Q)}\right)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim w(2^{m})+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}w(2^{m-l+1})

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{\rm Dini}.

Thus, if we add this estimate over
k

after we multiply it by

∎

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathcal{M}}\left[\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\psi}f}\right](x)+\sqrt{\alpha^{n}[w]_{\rm{Dini}}[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}\langle f\rangle_{1,3Q}}.

If we use the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

{\mathcal{M}}
once again, then we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\psi}(f\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\alpha^{n}\log^{\frac{3}{2}}(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}{\mathcal{M}}f(x)}+{\mathcal{M}}\left[\sqrt{S_{\alpha,\psi}f}\right](x).

If we square the above inequality,
we obtain

S_{\alpha,\psi}(f\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x)\lesssim\alpha^{n}\log^{\frac{3}{2}}(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}{\mathcal{M}}f(x)+{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}f(x)

is also arbitrary,
it follows that

{\mathcal{N}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}f(x)\lesssim\alpha^{n}\log^{\frac{3}{2}}(2+\alpha)[w]_{\rm{Dini}}[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}{\mathcal{M}}f(x)+{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}f(x).

Since the operators

{\mathcal{M}}
and

{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}

are weak-
(1,1)
bounded with the norms bounded by constant times

and

\alpha^{n}\log^{\frac{3}{2}}(2+\alpha)([w]_{\rm{Dini}}[\varphi]_{\rm{Dini}}+\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}})

it follows that

{\mathcal{N}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}
enjoys the same
boundedness property as

{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{nm}}{2^{knm}t^{nm}}\int_{Q(x,2^{k}t)^{m}\setminus Q(x,2^{k-1}t)^{m}}w(2^{2-k}m\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(y_{i})|d\vec{y}

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(b_{1},b_{2})(x)\leq\sum_{j_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}}\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(b_{1,j_{1}},b_{2,j_{2}})(x).

Summing
Lemma
3.5

over
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(b_{1},b_{2})(x)\lesssim\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}\|b_{1,j_{1}}\|_{L^{1}}\|b_{2,j_{2}}\|_{L^{1}}\prod_{k=1}^{2}(A_{j_{k}}^{(k)}(x)+B_{j_{k}}^{(k)}(x))=C^{(1)}(x)C^{(2)}(x)

for all
x\in\Omega
.

Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(b_{1},b_{2})(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{k=1}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}C^{(k)}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim([\sqrt{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}+[\sqrt{w}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm{Dini}}[\sqrt{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}})\prod_{k=1}^{2}\|b_{k}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim([\sqrt{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}+[\sqrt{w}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm{Dini}}[\sqrt{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}).

Therefore,
by the Chebychev inequality,

(3.29)

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega:\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(b_{1},b_{2})(x)>\rho\right\}\right|\lesssim\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\sqrt{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}+[\sqrt{w}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\rm{Dini}}[\sqrt{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}),

which means that (
3.29
),
or equivalently, (
3.28
) holds.
Here we have used the fact
[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}\leq[\sqrt{w}]^{2}_{\rm{Dini}}
.

Now, set

\tilde{w}=\frac{w}{[\sqrt{w}]^{2}_{\rm Dini}},\quad\tilde{\varphi}=\frac{\varphi}{[\sqrt{\varphi}]^{2}_{\rm Dini}},\ \text{ and }\ \tilde{A}=A[\sqrt{w}]^{2}_{\rm Dini}(1+[\sqrt{\varphi}]^{2}_{\rm Dini}).

Then the size condition and the smoothness condition for
\psi

in the definition of
S_{\alpha,\psi}
are satisfied with
\tilde{A}
,

\tilde{w}
and
\tilde{\varphi}
in place of
A
,
{w}
and
{\varphi}
,
respectively.
In this case,
[\sqrt{\tilde{w}}]_{\rm Dini}\leq 1
and

[\sqrt{\tilde{\varphi}}]_{\rm Dini}<1
.
Thus, by Lemmas
3.6
,
3.7
,
3.8
,
and
3.9
, we see that

\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)>\rho\right\}\right|\lesssim(A[\sqrt{w}]_{\rm Dini}(1+[\sqrt{\varphi}]_{\rm Dini})+1+\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\to L^{p}})\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}.

A homogeneity argument yields

\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\widetilde{S}_{1,\psi}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)>\rho\right\}\right|\lesssim(A[\sqrt{w}]_{\rm Dini}(1+[\sqrt{\varphi}]_{\rm Dini})+\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\to L^{p}})\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
in the case
m=2

is therefore complete.
∎

3.8.
Sparse domination for multilinear Littlewood–Paley operators

We pass to the multilinear case.
Once again we work in the setting of the
m
-linear case

m\geq 2
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

\alpha\geq 1
,

Q_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Suppose that we have
integrable functions

f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}

supported in
3Q_{0}
.
Then there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(Q_{0})

(
depending on
\vec{f}
)

such that

(3.30)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}\cdot 1_{Q_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+m}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm Dini}(1+[{\varphi}]_{\rm Dini})\bigg[\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{S}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\langle f_{i}\rangle_{1,P}^{2}1_{P}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the linear case, so we only
sketch the proof.
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider

{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}\vec{f}(x):=\sup_{Q}1_{Q}(x)\sqrt{|S^{2}_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}(x)-S_{\alpha,\psi}^{2}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{3Q})(x)|}.

and

{\mathcal{N}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}\vec{f}(x):=\sup_{Q}1_{Q}(x)S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x),

where
Q
moves over all cubes. Fix a cube
Q
so that
x\in Q
.
Let
x^{\prime}\in Q
be arbitrary. Keeping in mind that

|x+z-y_{i}|+(2+\alpha)t\geq|x-y_{i}|+(2+\alpha)t-|z|\geq|x-y_{i}|+t

for all
(z,t)\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(0)
and
y_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We get

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle:=|S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x)-S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{\Gamma_{\alpha}(0)}|\psi_{t}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x+z)-\psi_{t}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x^{\prime}+z)|^{2}\frac{dzdt}{t^{n+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 3Q)^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left\{\frac{\alpha^{nm}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(y_{i})|}{(t+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x-y_{i}|)^{nm}}\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}(2+\alpha)\ell(Q)}{t+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x-y_{i}|}\right)w\left(\frac{(2+\alpha)t}{t+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x-y_{i}|}\right)d\vec{y}\right\}^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle{\leq}\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 3Q)^{m}}\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x-y_{i}|)^{nm}}\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}(2+\alpha)\ell(Q)}{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|x-y_{i}|}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(y_{i})|d\vec{y}.

Here we have used Lemma
2.5
(b) in the last step.
By Lemma
2.5
(c),

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\varphi}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}(2+\alpha)}{2^{k}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{M}}f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle{\lesssim}\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+m}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}[{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{M}}f_{i}(x).

As a result,

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+m}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}[{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{M}}f_{i}(x)+S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}(x^{\prime})+S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{3Q})(x^{\prime}).

By Kolmogorov’s inequality
(see Lemma
2.10
)

\int_{E}S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}(x)^{\frac{1}{2m}}dx\lesssim|E|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\alpha^{nm}\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2m}}.

We write

J:=S_{\alpha,\psi}(\vec{f}\cdot 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 3Q})(x)^{\frac{1}{2m}}.

Taking the average over
Q
against
x^{\prime}

and using the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator

{\mathcal{M}}
twice, we obtain

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4m}}(2+\alpha)\left([{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}[{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{M}}f_{i}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{\frac{n}{2}}\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2m}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}(x).

Since
Q
is also arbitrary,
it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}\vec{f}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+m}(2+\alpha)\left([{w}]_{\rm{Dini}}[{\varphi}]_{\rm{Dini}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{M}}f_{i}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{nm}\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2m}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}(x).

Since

{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2m}}
is bounded
on

L^{\frac{1}{m},\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{\frac{1}{2m}}\circ S_{\alpha,\psi}\vec{f}
is bounded
from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\frac{1}{m},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
So, there exists a constant
D>0
,
independent of
\alpha\geq 1
, such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{N}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}\|_{L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{\frac{1}{m},\infty}}+\|{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{\alpha,\psi}}\|_{L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{\frac{1}{m},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq D(\alpha^{nm}\log^{\frac{1}{2}+m}(2+\alpha)[{w}]_{\rm Dini}[{\varphi}]_{\rm Dini}+\alpha^{nm}\|S_{1,\psi}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{1}\times\cdots\times L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}).

As the last step of the proof, one can
follow the idea in Section
2.5
very closely and use the above estimate.
We omit further details.
∎

We show that

\infty

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
l\in{\mathbb{N}}

Then

|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{l}}\log^{1-\kappa}\left(2+\frac{1}{|\xi|}\right)\quad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Proof.

Since
\kappa>1

\psi,\nabla^{l}\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for any

Thus, denoting by
{\mathcal{F}}

(4.1)

|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{l}}\quad(\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

for any

Let
|\xi|<1

We will seek a finer estimate than
(

{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\sin x_{1}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\kappa}(2+|x|^{2})}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx.

Since
\psi
is an odd function, we have

|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\min(1,|x|\cdot|\xi|)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\kappa}(2+|x|^{2})}dx\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n-1}\min(1,t|\xi|)}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\kappa}(2+t^{2})}dt.

We estimate this integral similar to Lemma
2.5
.
We make a change of variables
t=e^{u}

to have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{nu}\min(1,e^{u}|\xi|)}{(1+e^{2u})^{\frac{n}{2}}(\log(2+e^{2u}))^{\kappa}}du

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\log\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\frac{e^{(n+1)u}|\xi|}{(1+e^{2u})^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\kappa}(2+e^{2u})}du+\int_{\log\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{nu}}{(1+e^{2u})^{\frac{n}{2}}\log^{\kappa}(2+e^{2u})}du.

We decompose the integral into
4
parts:

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|\biggl(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{e^{(n+1)u}\,du}{(1+e^{u})^{n}}+\int_{0}^{\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}}\frac{e^{u}\,du}{(1+u)^{\kappa}}+\int_{\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}}^{\log\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\frac{e^{u}\,du}{(1+u)^{\kappa}}\biggr)+\int_{\log\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\frac{du}{(1+u)^{\kappa}}.

Since

e^{u}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\quad\left(0\leq u\leq\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\right)

and

\frac{1}{(1+u)^{\kappa}}\leq\left(1+\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\right)^{-\kappa}\quad\left(\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\leq u\leq\log\frac{1}{{|\xi|}}\right),

we obtain

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{F}}\psi(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{n}}{1+t^{n}}dt+\sqrt{|\xi|}+\left(1+\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\right)^{-\kappa}+\int_{\log\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\frac{du}{(1+u)^{\kappa}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log^{1-\kappa}\left(2+\frac{1}{|\xi|}\right).

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
h,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume
|h|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}
.
Then
for any

\frac{\min\Big(1,|h|^{\gamma}\Big)}{\log(2+|x|)}\log\Big(2+\frac{1+|x|}{|h|}\Big)\lesssim_{\gamma}1.

That is, there exists a constant
C_{\gamma}
which depends only on
\gamma
such that

\frac{\min\Big(1,|h|^{\gamma}\Big)}{\log(2+|x|)}\log\Big(2+\frac{1+|x|}{|h|}\Big)\leq C_{\gamma}.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
Let

and

\displaystyle\log\Big(2+\frac{1+|x|}{|h|}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\log\Big(\frac{2+|h|+|x|}{|h|}\Big)\leq\frac{2}{\log 2}\log\Big(2+|h|+|x|\Big)\log\frac{1}{|h|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\log 2}\log\Big(2+|h|+|x|\Big)\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\frac{1}{|h|^{\gamma}}\lesssim_{\gamma}\frac{\log(2+|x|)}{|h|^{\gamma}}.

Case 2.

So,
we obtain the desired result.

5.
Concluding remarks

Recall that the Marcinkiewicz function was a key tool
in the paper
[
23
]
. It seems
useful to refine the boundedness property
in the spirit of this paper.
In connection with the Dini condition,
the following general estimate seems of use for further work:

Remark 5.1
.

Let
w
be a modulus of continuity.
Let

F_{w}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}{\mathcal{M}}1_{Q(c_{k},r_{k})}(x)w\left(\frac{r_{k}}{r_{k}+|x-c_{k}|}\right)\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

be
the generalized Marcinkiewicz function corresponding to the cubes

\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with center at
c_{k}
and
side-length
r_{k}
.
Here each
\lambda_{k}
is a positive constant.
We claim that if
w
satisfies
the Dini condition and
1<q<\infty
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F_{w}(x)^{q}dx\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}{}^{q}|Q_{k}|

where
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

is a collection of disjoint cubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In fact,
we choose a non-negative function
g\in L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with

\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}}\leq 1
and we establish

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F_{w}(x)g(x)dx\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}{}^{q}|Q_{k}|

We note that

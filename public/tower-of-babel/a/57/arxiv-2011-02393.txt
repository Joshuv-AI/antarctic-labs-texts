Proof of Kaneko–Tsumura Conjecture on Triple T T -Values
Sasha Berger, Aarav Chandra, Jasper Jain, Daniel Xu

Abstract




Abstract
Many
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations exist between multiple zeta values, the most interesting of which are the weighted sum formulas. In this paper, we generalized these to Euler sums and Kaneko and Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values, a variant of the multiple zeta values, by considering generating functions of the Euler sums. Through this approach we are able to re-prove a few known formulas, confirm a conjecture of Kaneko and Tsumura on triple
T
-values, and discover many new identities.

Keywords
: Euler sums, multiple zeta values, weighted sum formulas, multiple
T
-value.

AMS Subject Classifications (2020):
11M32, 11M06, 11G55.

1
Introduction

In their seminal work
[
4
]
, Kaneko and Tsumura defined the multiple
T
-values (MTVs),
a variation of the multiple zeta values as follows:
for any positive integers
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d}
with
s_{1}>1
, let

\displaystyle T(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d}):=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}>\dotsm>m_{d}>0\\
m_{j}\equiv d-j+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\frac{2^{d}}{m_{1}^{s_{1}}\dotsm m_{d}^{s_{d}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\dotsm>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{(1+(-1)^{n_{1}+d})\dotsm(1+(-1)^{n_{d-1}})(1-(-1)^{n_{d}})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\dotsm n_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

(1.1)

They derived many interesting facts concerning these values with analytical methods and posed some conjectures
when the depth
d=3
. In this short note, we shall prove
the following result which was stated in
[
4
, Conjecture 4.6]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(=Corollary
6.2
)

For all
w\geq 4

\displaystyle\sum_{a+b+c=w}2^{b}(3^{a-1}-1)T(a,b,c)=\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}(w-1)(w-2)T(w).

Observe that all MTVs can be decomposed as linear combinations of Euler sums.
Our strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
is to derive similar weighted sum formulas for each Euler sum involved by using
their generating functions. In the process, we also discover a few other types of weighted sum formulas for Euler sums.
We remark that the idea of
proving identities among MZVs using generating functions first appeared in
[
1
]
.
We also notice that Machide already worked on the MZV case
[
5
,
6
]
using multiple polylogarithms when depth
d\leq 4
. Yuan and the last author also studied these directly by using only finite double shuffle relations in
[
8
]
when
d=3
.

2
Multiple zeta values and Euler sums

The classical
multiple zeta values
(abbr. MZVs) are defined by (see
[
2
,
9
]
)

\displaystyle\zeta(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\dotsb>m_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{1}^{s_{1}}\dotsm m_{d}^{s_{d}}},

for positive integers
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d}
with
s_{1}>1
. We call
s_{1}+\dotsb+s_{d}
and
d
the
weight
and
depth
, respectively. A composition
(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d})
is called
admissible
if
s_{1}>1
.

Euler sums, also known as alternating MZVs, are defined as follows: for
{\bf s}=(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{d})\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
,

\zeta(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d};z_{1},\dotsc,z_{d}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}

(2.2)

which converges if and only if
(s_{1},z_{1})\neq(1,1)
, in which case we call
({\bf s};{\bf z})

admissible
. It is well-known (see, for e.g.,
[
10
, p. 17]
) that it has an iterated integral expression

\zeta(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d};z_{1},\dots,z_{d})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{a_{1}-t}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{d}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{a_{d}-t}.

(2.3)

where
a_{k}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}
for all
k\leq d
.
To save space, we put a bar on top of
s_{j}
if
z_{j}=-1
. For example,

\zeta(\bar{2},3,\bar{1},4)=\zeta(2,3,1,4;-1,1,-1,1).

It is clear that MZVs are just the special case of Euler sums when all
z_{j}=1
; namely, no bars can appear.

One of the most important features of Euler sums is that they satisfy many standard relations
(see, for example,
[
10
, §13.3]
). Among them, the (regularized) double shuffle relations, which are consequences
of the two types of expressions in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),
play a key role in the study of the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations among these values.

3
Regularizations of Euler sums and their generating functions

Let
M
be a large positive integer and
{\varepsilon}>0
be a very small number. For any composition
{\bf s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})

and
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{d}=\pm 1
, we consider two variations of the Euler sums defined by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), respectively. First, set

\zeta^{(M)}({\bf s};{\bf z}):=\displaystyle\sum_{M\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots n_{d}^{s_{d}}}

(3.4)

and

I^{({\varepsilon})}({\bf s};{\bf z}):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1-{\varepsilon}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{1}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{a_{1}-t}\cdots\left(\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{s_{d}-1}\displaystyle\frac{dt}{a_{d}-t}

(3.5)

where
a_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}z_{j}^{-1}
. Then
\zeta^{(M)}
satisfies the stuffle product

\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1};z_{1})\zeta^{(M)}(s_{2};z_{2})=\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1}+s_{2};z_{1}z_{2})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{2};z_{1},z_{2})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{2},s_{1};z_{2},z_{1}).

(3.6)

On the other hand, by shuffle product of the iterated integrals we see that

\displaystyle I^{({\varepsilon})}(s_{1};z_{1})I^{({\varepsilon})}(s_{2};z_{2})=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\geq 1\\
t_{1}+t_{2}=s_{1}+s_{2}\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{2}-1}I^{({\varepsilon})}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\Big(z_{2},\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\Big)+\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1}I^{({\varepsilon})}_{t_{1},t_{2}}\Big(z_{1},\displaystyle\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\Big)\Bigg].

(3.7)

It is well-known that for admissible
({\bf s};{\bf z})
we have

\zeta({\bf s};{\bf z})=\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\zeta^{(M)}({\bf s};{\bf z})=\displaystyle\lim_{{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}}I^{({\varepsilon})}({\bf s};{\bf z}).

Therefore one can derive the so-called double shuffle relations using the two different product structures; namely the stuffle and shuffle products. Then by the usual regularization process one can discover the extremely useful regularized double shuffle relation. Briefly speaking, for every admissible and non-admissible
({\bf s};{\bf z})
of level
N
there are two polynomials of
T
, denoted by
\zeta_{*}({\bf s};{\bf z})
(
*
-regularzed) and
\zeta_{\shuffle}({\bf s};{\bf z})
(
\shuffle
-regularzed), such that

(
DBSF1)

\zeta_{*}({\bf s};{\bf z})\zeta_{*}({\bf s}^{\prime};{\bf z}^{\prime})
can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of
*
-regularized colored MZVs of weight
|{\bf s}|+|{\bf s}^{\prime}|
and level
N
using the stuffle product.

(
DBSF2)

\zeta_{\shuffle}({\bf s};{\bf z})\zeta_{\shuffle}({\bf s}^{\prime};{\bf z}^{\prime})
can be expressed as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of
\shuffle
-regularized colored MZVs of weight
|{\bf s}|+|{\bf s}^{\prime}|
and level
N
using the shuffle product.

(
DBSF3)

There is an explicitly defined
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\rho
such that
\rho\circ\zeta_{*}=\zeta_{\shuffle}
satisfying
\rho(T)=T
and
\rho(T^{2})=T^{2}+\zeta(2).

We will not go into the details of this theory, instead, we would like to refer the interested reader to §13.3.1 of the book
[
10
]
.

For any fixed alternating signs
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{d})
we set
\sgn({\bf z})=(\sgn(z_{1}),\dotsc,\sgn(z_{d}))

and define the generating functions

F_{\sharp}^{\sgn({\bf z})}({\bf x}):=\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},\dotsc,s_{d}\geq 1}\zeta_{\sharp}({\bf s};{\bf z})x_{1}^{s_{1}-1}\dotsm x_{d}^{s_{d}-1},

(3.8)

where
\sharp=*
or
\shuffle
,
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{d})
and
\zeta_{\sharp}
denotes the
\sharp
-regularized value.

4
Depth 2 weighted sum formulas

By multiplying
x^{s_{1}-1}y^{s_{2}-1}
on (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), taking the sum for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, and specializing at
(z_{1},z_{2})=(1,1),(1,-1)
and
(-1,-1)
, respectively, we get after applying the regularization process:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\sharp}(a)\zeta_{\sharp}(b):

\displaystyle\,F_{\shuffle}^{+,+}(x+y,y)+F_{\shuffle}^{+,+}(x+y,x)=F_{*}^{+,+}(x,y)+F_{*}^{+,+}(y,x)+\displaystyle\frac{F_{*}^{+}(x)-F_{*}^{+}(y)}{x-y},

\displaystyle\zeta_{\sharp}(a)\zeta(\bar{b}):

\displaystyle\,F_{\shuffle}^{+,-}(x+y,y)+F_{\shuffle}^{-,-}(x+y,x)=F_{*}^{+,-}(x,y)+F_{*}^{-,+}(y,x)+\displaystyle\frac{F_{*}^{-}(x)-F_{*}^{-}(y)}{x-y},

\displaystyle\zeta(\bar{a})\zeta(\bar{b}):

\displaystyle\,F_{\shuffle}^{-,+}(x+y,y)+F_{\shuffle}^{-,+}(x+y,x)=F_{*}^{-,-}(x,y)+F_{*}^{-,-}(y,x)+\displaystyle\frac{F_{*}^{+}(x)-F_{*}^{+}(y)}{x-y}.

Here we need to remark that
\zeta_{\shuffle}(s)=\zeta_{*}(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now, replacing
(x,y)
by
(xt,yt)
and then comparing the coefficient for
t^{w-2}
(
w\geq 3
) we immediately
derive the following results. To save space, we set

\displaystyle\sum=\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=w,a,b\in\mathbb{N}}\quad\text{or}\quad\displaystyle\sum=\displaystyle\sum_{a+b+c=w,a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}}.

On the other hand,
\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}
means that we remove all those terms with
a=1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=w,a\geq 2}\quad\text{or}\quad\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\sum_{a+b+c=w,a\geq 2}.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any fixed
w\geq 3
, set
f_{w}(x,y)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{w-2}x^{j}y^{w-2-j}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\zeta_{\shuffle}(a,b)(x+y)^{{\tilde{a}}}(y^{{\tilde{b}}}+x^{{\tilde{b}}})=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum\zeta_{*}(a,b)(x^{{\tilde{a}}}y^{{\tilde{b}}}+y^{{\tilde{a}}}x^{{\tilde{b}}})+\zeta(w)f_{w}(x,y),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum(x+y)^{{\tilde{a}}}\big(\zeta_{\shuffle}(a,\bar{b})y^{{\tilde{b}}}+\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})x^{{\tilde{b}}}\big)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum\big(\zeta_{*}(a,\bar{b})x^{{\tilde{a}}}y^{{\tilde{b}}}+\zeta(\bar{a},b)y^{{\tilde{a}}}x^{{\tilde{b}}}\big)+\zeta(\bar{w})f_{w}(x,y),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\zeta(\bar{a},b)(x+y)^{{\tilde{a}}}(y^{{\tilde{b}}}+x^{{\tilde{b}}})=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})(x^{{\tilde{a}}}y^{{\tilde{b}}}+y^{{\tilde{a}}}x^{{\tilde{b}}})+\zeta(w)f_{w}(x,y).

Here and in the rest of this paper, we set
{{\tilde{a}}}=a-1
,
{{\tilde{b}}}=b-1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
w\geq 3
and
v=w-1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,b)=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(w),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(\bar{1},v)-\zeta(\bar{1},\bar{v})+\zeta(\bar{w}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(\bar{a},b)=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(\bar{v},\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{1},\bar{v})-\zeta(\bar{v},1)-\zeta(\bar{1},v)+\zeta(w),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,\bar{b})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(\bar{v},1)-\zeta(\bar{v},\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{w}).

Proof.

We can prove these by taking
x=1,y=0
and
x=0,y=1
in Proposition
4.1
.
∎

This immediately implies the following corollary about double
T
-values.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
w\geq 3
and
v=w-1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=w,a\geq 2}T(a,b)=

\displaystyle\,2\Big(\zeta(w)-\zeta(\bar{w})+\zeta(\bar{v},\bar{1})+\zeta(\bar{1},\bar{v})-\zeta(\bar{v},1)-\zeta(\bar{1},v)\Big).

(4.9)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}T(a,b)=\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\Big(\zeta(a,b)+\zeta(\bar{a},b)-\zeta(a,\bar{b})-\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})\Big)

which reduces to (
4.9
) quickly by Theorem
4.2
.
∎

With this approach we can now easily recover
[
4
, Thm. 3.2]
. Notice that
in the proof, we find the depth 2 weighted sum formulas for Euler sums which are analogs of the MZV formula
first discovered by Ohno and Zulidin
[
7
]
and later generalized to
arbitrary depth by Guo and Xie
[
3
]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For all
w\geq 3
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=w,a\geq 2}2^{a-1}T(a,b)=

\displaystyle\,(w-1)(\zeta(w)-\zeta(\bar{w}))=(w-1)T(w).

Proof.

Set
\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}=\displaystyle\sum{}_{a+b=w,a\geq 2}
. Taking
x=1,y=1
in Proposition
4.1
we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}2^{{\tilde{a}}}\zeta(a,b)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(a,b)+\displaystyle\frac{w-1}{2}\zeta(w)=\displaystyle\frac{w+1}{2}\zeta(w),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}2^{{\tilde{a}}}\big(\zeta(a,\bar{b})+\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})\big)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\big(\zeta(a,\bar{b})+\zeta(\bar{a},b)\big)+(w-1)\zeta(\bar{w})=\zeta(w)+w\zeta(\bar{w}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}2^{{\tilde{a}}}\zeta(\bar{a},b)=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum{}^{\prime}\zeta(\bar{a},\bar{b})+\displaystyle\frac{w-1}{2}\zeta(w)=\zeta(\bar{w})+\displaystyle\frac{w-1}{2}\zeta(w),

using the sum formulas in Theorem
4.2
.
The theorem follows immediately.
∎

5
Depth 3 weighted sum formulas, Part A

As in the depth 2 case, we may derive many identities by the generating functions of the triple Euler sums.
For depth 3, there are two possible ways to produce functional equations of
F_{\sharp}^{\sgn({\bf z})}({\bf x})
.
We may consider either

(
A)

products of double logarithms with single logarithms, denoted by
\zeta^{\sharp}_{s_{1},s_{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})\zeta^{\sharp}_{s_{3}}(z_{3})
, or

(
B)

products of three logarithms,
\zeta^{\sharp}_{s_{1}}(z_{1})\zeta^{\sharp}_{s_{2}}(z_{2})\zeta^{\sharp}_{s_{3}}(z_{3})
.

We start by dealing with case (A) in this section. Observe that

\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{2};z_{1},z_{2})\zeta^{(M)}(s_{3};z_{3})=\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1}+s_{3},s_{2};z_{1}z_{3},z_{2})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{2}+s_{3};z_{1},z_{2}z_{3})\\
\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3};z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{1},s_{3},s_{2};z_{1},z_{3},z_{2})+\zeta^{(M)}(s_{3},s_{1},s_{2};z_{3},z_{1},z_{2})

(5.11)

On the other hand, by shuffle product of iterated integrals we see that

\displaystyle I^{({\varepsilon})}(s_{1},s_{2};z_{1},z_{2})I^{({\varepsilon})}(s_{3};z_{3})=

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}\geq 2,t_{2}\geq 1\\

(5.12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}\geq 2,t_{2},t_{3}\geq 1\\
t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}\\
=s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}\end{subarray}}\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1}\binom{t_{2}-1}{s_{2}-t_{3}}I^{({\varepsilon})}\Big(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3};z_{1},\displaystyle\frac{z_{3}}{z_{1}},\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}z_{2}}{z_{3}}\Big)

(5.13)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}\geq 2,t_{2},t_{3}\geq 1\\
t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}\\
=s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}\end{subarray}}\binom{t_{1}-1}{s_{1}-1}\binom{t_{2}-1}{s_{2}-1}I^{({\varepsilon})}\Big(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3};z_{1},z_{2},\displaystyle\frac{z_{3}}{z_{2}}\Big).

(5.14)

To save space, we set
\zeta_{\sharp}\Big({{\bf s}\atop{\bf z}}\Big):=\zeta_{\sharp}({\bf s};{\bf z})

By the usual regularization process (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{2}\zeta(2)\zeta_{*}\Big({{w-2}\atop{z_{3}}}\Big)z^{w-3}+\displaystyle\sum_{b+c=w}\Bigg[\left(\displaystyle\frac{x^{{\tilde{b}}}-z^{{\tilde{b}}}}{x-z}\right)y^{{\tilde{c}}}\zeta_{*}\Big({{\ \,b\,\ ,\,c\,}\atop{z_{1}z_{3},z_{2}}}\Big)+x^{{\tilde{b}}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{y^{{\tilde{c}}}-z^{{\tilde{c}}}}{y-z}\right)\zeta_{*}\Big({{b\,,\ \,c\,\ }\atop{z_{1},z_{2}z_{3}}}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+(x+z)^{{\tilde{a}}}(y+z)^{{\tilde{b}}}z^{{\tilde{c}}}\zeta_{\shuffle}\Big({{\,a\,,\,b\,,\ \,c\,\ }\atop{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}/(z_{1}z_{2})}}\Big)+(x+z)^{{\tilde{a}}}x^{{\tilde{b}}}y^{{\tilde{c}}}\zeta_{\shuffle}\Big({{\,a\,,\ \,b\,\ ,\,c\,}\atop{z_{3},z_{1}/z_{3},z_{2}}}\Big)\Bigg],

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose

with

. Then for all

we have

\zeta_{\shuffle}(a,b,c)=\zeta_{*}(a,b,c).

Further, for

\zeta_{\shuffle}(1,1,c)=\zeta_{*}(1,1,c)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\zeta(2)\zeta(c).

Proof.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let

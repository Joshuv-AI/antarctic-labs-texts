Abstract
In this paper, we consider Barnes-type Daehee polynomials of the first kind and of the second kind. From the properties of Sheffer sequences of these polynomials arising from umbral calculus, we derive new and interesting identities.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the polynomials
D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
and
\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
called the Barnes-type Daehee polynomials of the first kind and of the second kind, whose generating functions are given by

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{a_{j}}\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

(2)

respectively, where
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\neq 0
.
When
x=0
,
D_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=D_{n}(0|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
and
\widehat{D}_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=\widehat{D}_{n}(0|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
are called the Barnes-type Daehee numbers of the first kind and of the second kind, respectively.

Recall that the Daehee polynomials of the first kind and of the second kind of order
r
, denoted by
D_{n}^{(r)}(x)
and
\widehat{D}_{n}^{(r)}(x)
, respectively, are given by the generating functions to be

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{t}\right)^{r}(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(1+t)\ln(1+t)}{t}\right)^{r}(1+t)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{D}_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

respectively. If
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1
, then
D_{n}^{(r)}(x)=D_{n}(x|\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r})
and
\widehat{D}_{n}^{(r)}(x)=\widehat{D}_{n}(x|\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r})
.
Dahee polynomials were defined by the second author
[
7
]
and have been investigated in
[
5
,
12
,
13
]
.

In this paper, we consider Barnes-type Daehee polynomials of the first kind and of the second kind. From the properties of Sheffer sequences of these polynomials arising from umbral calculus, we derive new and interesting identities.

2
Umbral calculus

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the complex number field and let
\mathcal{F}
be the set of all formal power series in the variable
t
:

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}t^{k}\Bigg|a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\,.

(3)

Let
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{C}[x]
and let
\mathbb{P}^{\ast}
be the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
.

\left\langle L|p(x)\right\rangle
is the action of the linear functional
L
on the polynomial
p(x)
, and
we recall that the vector space operations on
\mathbb{P}^{\ast}
are defined by
\left\langle L+M|p(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle L|p(x)\right\rangle+\left\langle M|p(x)\right\rangle
,
\left\langle cL|p(x)\right\rangle=c\left\langle L|p(x)\right\rangle
, where
c
is a complex constant in
\mathbb{C}
. For
f(t)\in\mathcal{F}
, let us define the linear functional on
\mathbb{P}
by setting

\left\langle f(t)|x^{n}\right\rangle=a_{n},\quad(n\geq 0).

(4)

In particular,

\left\langle t^{k}|x^{n}\right\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k}\quad(n,k\geq 0),

(5)

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

For
f_{L}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left\langle L|x^{k}\right\rangle}{k!}t^{k}
, we have
\left\langle f_{L}(t)|x^{n}\right\rangle=\left\langle L|x^{n}\right\rangle
. That is,
L=f_{L}(t)
.
The map
L\mapsto f_{L}(t)
is a vector space isomorphism from
\mathbb{P}^{\ast}
onto
\mathcal{F}
. Henceforth,
\mathcal{F}
denotes both the algebra of formal power series in
t
and the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
, and so an element
f(t)
of
\mathcal{F}
will be thought of as both a formal power series and a linear functional. We call
\mathcal{F}
the
umbral algebra
and the
umbral calculus
is the study of umbral algebra.
The order
O\bigl(f(t)\bigr)
of a power series
f(t)(\neq 0)
is the smallest integer
k
for which the coefficient of
t^{k}
does not vanish. If
O\bigl(f(t)\bigr)=1
, then
f(t)
is called a
delta series
; if
O\bigl(f(t)\bigr)=0
, then
f(t)
is called an
invertible series
.
For
f(t),g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
O\bigl(f(t)\bigr)=1
and
O\bigl(g(t)\bigr)=0
, there exists a unique sequence
s_{n}(x)
(
\deg s_{n}(x)=n
) such that
\left\langle g(t)f(t)^{k}|s_{n}(x)\right\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k}
, for
n,k\geq 0
.
Such a sequence
s_{n}(x)
is called the
Sheffer sequence
for
\bigl(g(t),f(t)\bigr)
which is denoted by
s_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(g(t),f(t)\bigr)
.

For
f(t),g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
and
p(x)\in\mathbb{P}
, we have

\left\langle f(t)g(t)|p(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle f(t)|g(t)p(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle g(t)|f(t)p(x)\right\rangle

(6)

and

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\langle f(t)|x^{k}\right\rangle\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\langle t^{k}|p(x)\right\rangle\frac{x^{k}}{k!}

(7)

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.2.5]
). Thus, by (
7
), we get

t^{k}p(x)=p^{(k)}(x)=\frac{d^{k}p(x)}{dx^{k}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad e^{yt}p(x)=p(x+y).

(8)

Sheffer sequences are characterized in the generating function (
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.4]
).

Lemma 1

The sequence
s_{n}(x)
is Sheffer for
\big(g(t),f(t)\bigr)
if and only if

\frac{1}{g\bigl(\bar{f}(t)\bigr)}e^{y\bar{f}(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{k}(y)}{k!}t^{k}\quad(y\in\mathbb{C})\,,

where
\bar{f}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
f(t)
.

For
s_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(g(t),f(t)\bigr)
, we have the following equations (
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.7, Theorem 2.3.5, Theorem 2.3.9]
):

\displaystyle f(t)s_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=ns_{n-1}(x)\quad(n\geq 0),

(9)

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\left\langle g\bigl(\bar{f}(t)\bigr)^{-1}\bar{f}(t)^{j}|x^{n}\right\rangle x^{j},

(10)

\displaystyle s_{n}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}s_{j}(x)p_{n-j}(y)\,,

(11)

where
p_{n}(x)=g(t)s_{n}(x)
.

Assume that
p_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(1,f(t)\bigr)
and
q_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(1,g(t)\bigr)
. Then the transfer formula (
[
14
, Corollary 3.8.2]
) is given by

q_{n}(x)=x\left(\frac{f(t)}{g(t)}\right)^{n}x^{-1}p_{n}(x)\quad(n\geq 1).

For
s_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(g(t),f(t)\bigr)
and
r_{n}(x)\sim\bigl(h(t),l(t)\bigr)
, assume that

s_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}C_{n,m}r_{m}(x)\quad(n\geq 0)\,.

Then we have (
[
14
, p.132]
)

C_{n,m}=\frac{1}{m!}\left\langle\frac{h\bigl(\bar{f}(t)\bigr)}{g\bigl(\bar{f}(t)\bigr)}l\bigl(\bar{f}(t)\bigr)^{m}\Bigg|x^{n}\right\rangle\,.

(12)

3
Main results

We now note that
D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
is the Sheffer sequence for

g(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{t}\right)\quad\hbox{and}\quad f(t)=e^{t}-1.

So,

D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\sim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{t}\right),e^{t}-1\right)\,.

(13)

\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
is the Sheffer sequences for

g(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{te^{a_{j}t}}\right)\quad\hbox{and}\quad f(t)=e^{t}-1.

So,

\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\sim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{te^{a_{j}t}}\right),e^{t}-1\right)\,.

(14)

Explicit expressions

Recall that Barnes’ multiple Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
are defined by the generating function as

\frac{t^{r}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(e^{a_{j}t}-1)}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,

(15)

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\neq 0
(
[

Let
(n)_{j}=n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)
(
j\geq 1

(n)_{0}=1

S_{1}(n,m)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)x^{m}\,.

Theorem 1

\displaystyle D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)B_{m}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

(16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{l=j}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,j)D_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\right)x^{j}

(17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}D_{n-m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})(x)_{m}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)B_{m}(x+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{l=j}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,j)\widehat{D}_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\right)x^{j}

(20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\widehat{D}_{n-m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})(x)_{m}\,.

(21)

Since

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{t}\right)D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\sim(1,e^{t}-1)

(22)

and

(x)_{n}\sim(1,e^{t}-1)\,,

(23)

we have

\displaystyle D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{t}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}\right)(x)_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{t}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}\right)x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)B_{m}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,.

So, we get (
16
).

Similarly, by

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{e^{a_{j}t}-1}{te^{a_{j}t}}\right)\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\sim(1,e^{t}-1)

and (

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}\right)(x)_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}\right)x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)e^{(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r})t}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{t}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}\right)x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,m)B_{m}(x+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,.

So, we get (
19

By (
10

D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{j}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle x^{j}\,.

Since

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{j}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\Big|\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{j}x^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\Big|j!\sum_{l=j}^{\infty}S_{1}(l,j)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}x^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=j!\sum_{l=j}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,j)\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\Big|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=j!\sum_{l=j}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,j)\left\langle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}D_{i}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\Big|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

we obtain (

Similarly, by (
10

14

\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{a_{j}}\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{j}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle x^{j}\,.

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{a_{j}}\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{j}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=j!\sum_{l=j}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,j)\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{a_{j}}\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\Big|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=xD_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})-e^{-t}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r}{t}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,i)D_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})x^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,i)D_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})e^{-t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r\right)\frac{x^{i+1}}{i+1}\,.

Since

\displaystyle e^{-t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r\right)x^{i+1}

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}B_{m}a_{j}^{m}}{m!}t^{m}-r\right)x^{i+1}

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{m=0}^{i+1}\binom{i+1}{m}B_{m}(-a_{j})^{m}x^{i+1-m}-rx^{i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{m=1}^{i+1}\binom{i+1}{m}B_{m}(-a_{j})^{m}(x-1)^{i+1-m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{m=0}^{i}\binom{i+1}{m}B_{i+1-m}(-a_{j})^{i+1-m}(x-1)^{m}\,,

(32)

we have

\displaystyle D_{n+1}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=xD_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{m=0}^{i}\frac{1}{i+1}\binom{n}{l}\binom{i+1}{m}S_{1}(l,i)

\displaystyle\qquad\times B_{i+1-m}(-a_{j})^{i+1-m}D_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})(x-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=xD_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=m}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{i+1}\binom{n}{l}\binom{i+1}{m}S_{1}(l,i)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\times B_{i+1-m}(-a_{j})^{i+1-m}D_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\right)(x-1)^{m}\,,

which is the identity (
29
).

Next, by applying (
31
) with (
14
), we get

\widehat{D}_{n+1}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})=x\widehat{D}_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})-e^{-t}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,.

Now,

\displaystyle\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}

\displaystyle=(\ln g(t))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ln(e^{a_{j}t}-1)-r\ln t-\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\biggr)t\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}e^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-\frac{r}{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\,.

By (
20
) we have

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{n+1}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\left(x+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\right)\widehat{D}_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})-e^{-t}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r}{t}\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\left(x+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\right)\widehat{D}_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad-e^{-t}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r}{t}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,i)\widehat{D}_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})x^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\right)\widehat{D}_{n}(x-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{l=i}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(l,i)\widehat{D}_{n-l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})e^{-t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{a_{j}te^{a_{j}t}}{e^{a_{j}t}-1}-r\right)\frac{x^{i+1}}{i+1}\,.

By (

), we have the identity (

).

3.5
Differentiation

Theorem 5

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-l-1}}{l!(n-l)}D_{l}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\widehat{D}_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-l-1}}{l!(n-l)}\widehat{D}_{l}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,.

(34)

We shall use

\frac{d}{dx}s_{n}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{l}\left\langle\bar{f}(t)|x^{n-l}\right\rangle s_{l}(x)

(
Cf.

[
14
, Theorem 2.3.12]
).
Since

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{f}(t)|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\ln(1+t)|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}t^{m}}{m}\Big|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-l}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\left\langle t^{m}|x^{n-l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-l}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}(n-l)!\delta_{m,n-l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-l-1}(n-l-1)!\,,

with (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}D_{n}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{l}(-1)^{n-l-1}(n-l-1)!D_{l}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-l-1}}{l!(n-l)}D_{l}(x|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,,

which is the identity (
33
).
Similarly, with (
14
), we have the identity (
34
).

For

\displaystyle=\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)(1+t)^{y}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{t}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)(1+t)^{y}\right)\Big|x^{n-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\left(\partial_{t}(1+t)^{y}\right)\Big|x^{n-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\left(\partial_{t}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\right)(1+t)^{y}\Big|x^{n-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=yD_{n-1}(y-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\left(\partial_{t}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\right)(1+t)^{y}\Big|x^{n-1}\right\rangle\,.

Observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)

(\geq 1)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\left\langle\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{i}}-1}\right)(1+t)^{y+a_{j}-1}\Big|\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}x^{n}\right\rangle

3.7
Relations including the Stirling numbers of the first kind

Theorem 7

For
n-1\geq m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-m}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(n-l,m)D_{l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-m}\binom{n-1}{l}S_{1}(n-l-1,m-1)D_{l}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-m-1}\binom{n}{l+1}S_{1}(n-l-1,m)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(r\sum_{i=0}^{l+1}\binom{l+1}{i}c_{i}D_{l+1-i}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{l+1}\binom{l+1}{i}a_{j}c_{i}D_{l+1-i}(a_{j}-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r},a_{j})\right)\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-m}\binom{n}{l}S_{1}(n-l,m)\widehat{D}_{l}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-m}\binom{n-1}{l}S_{1}(n-l-1,m-1)\widehat{D}_{l}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-m-1}\binom{n}{l+1}S_{1}(n-l-1,m)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(r\sum_{i=0}^{l+1}\binom{l+1}{i}c_{i}\widehat{D}_{l+1-i}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{i=0}^{l+1}\binom{l+1}{i}a_{j}c_{i}\widehat{D}_{l+1-i}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r},a_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{l=0}^{n-m-1}\binom{n-1}{l}S_{1}(n-l-1,m)\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\widehat{D}_{l}(-1|a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\,.

(38)

We shall compute

\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(\frac{\ln(1+t)}{(1+t)^{a_{j}}-1}\right)\bigl(\ln(1+t)\bigr)^{m}\Big|x^{n}\right\rangle

On the one hand,

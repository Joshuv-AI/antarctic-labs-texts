On the commutativity of the powerspace constructions
Matthew de Brecht \rsuper a, Tatsuji Kawai \rsuper b

Abstract
. We investigate powerspace constructions on topological spaces, with a particular focus on the category of quasi-Polish spaces. We show that the upper and lower powerspaces commute on all quasi-Polish spaces, and show more generally that this commutativity is equivalent to the topological property of consonance. We then investigate powerspace constructions on the open set lattices of quasi-Polish spaces, and provide a complete characterization of how the upper and lower powerspaces distribute over the open set lattice construction.

1.
Introduction

Given a topological space
X
, the
lower powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
is the set of closed subsets of
X
with the lower Vietoris topology, the
upper powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is the set of (saturated) compact subsets of
X
with the upper Vietoris topology, and the
convex
(or
Vietoris
)
powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is the set of lenses in
X
with the Vietoris topology.

If
X
is Hausdorff, then the notion of lens and compact subset become equivalent, and
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is the well-known hyperspace of compact subsets often used by topologists (see Section 4.F of
[
Kec95
]
). This is also the same Vietoris construction used in
[
KKV04
]
(but restricted to Stone spaces) to provide coalgebraic semantics for modal logics.
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
and
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
are generally non-Hausdorff and might seem less familiar to some topologists, but
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
should be easily recognized as the topological space underlying the well-known Effros Borel space (see Section 12.C in
[
Kec95
]
), at least when
X
is countably based.

In domain theory, the upper and lower powerdomains (see Section IV-8 of
[
GHK+03
]
) coincide with the upper and lower powerspaces for continuous dcpos with the Scott topology, although the constructions differ in general for non-continuous dcpos. Under some additional mild assumptions, the Plotkin powerdomain and convex powerspace are also known to coincide. Powerdomains are often used in theoretical computer science to model the semantics of non-deterministic programs.

In this paper, we investigate the powerspace constructions with a focus on the category of quasi-Polish spaces
[
dB13
]
, which is general enough to contain all Polish spaces and all
\omega
-continuous domains. We show that the powerspace constructions preserve the property of being quasi-Polish, and as a result we can view the constructions as monads on the category of quasi-Polish spaces. We will also provide topological characterizations of when the powerspace constructions commute, and investigate the interaction between the powerspace constructions and the contravariant endofunctor
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}
which maps a space
X
to its lattice of open subsets
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
equipped with the Scott-topology.

The locale theoretic versions of the powerspace constructions are known as
powerlocales
and have a very rich theory
[
Joh85
,
Vic89
,
JV91
,
Sch93
,
Vic04
,
VT04
]
. Although the powerspace and powerlocale constructions behave differently in general, some of the more notable differences (such as the failure of the commutativity of upper and lower powerspaces) disappear when one restricts to powerspace constructions on quasi-Polish spaces. As we will further explain below, several of our topological results for the powerspaces on quasi-Polish spaces correspond to well-known results for powerlocales, and in some cases our results can be easily obtained using known results and techniques from locale theory. However, we expect that the topological proofs provided here will be more accessible to those who are unfamiliar with locale theory, even though they come at the expense of using classical logic.

In Sections
3
through
5
we show that the lower, upper, and convex powerspace constructions preserve the property of being quasi-Polish. Although we use straightforward topological proofs, the preservation results for the lower and upper powerspaces can also be obtained using known results in locale theory. R. Heckmann showed in
[
Hec15
]
that every countably presented locale is spatial and that the corresponding spaces are precisely the quasi-Polish spaces. Furthermore, results by S. Vickers showing the geometricity of the lower and upper powerlocales (see Theorems 7.5 and 8.8 in
[
Vic04
]
) demonstrate that the powerlocale constructions preserve the property of having a countable presentation. Finally, it is known (see Propositions 6.26 and 7.39 in
[
Sch93
]
) that if
X
is a sober space, then the lower (or upper) powerspace of
X
is homeomorphic to the space of points of the corresponding powerlocale. Combining these results provides a locale theoretic proof that the lower and upper powerspace of a quasi-Polish space is quasi-Polish.

In Section
6
, we will show that a topological property known as
consonance
(see
[
DGL95
,
NS96
,
CW98
,
Bou99
]

{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))\cong{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
under a naturally defined homeomorphism. Analogous commutativity results are known in both domain theory and locale theory. In domain theory, K.E. Flannery and J.J. Martin showed in
[
FM90
]
that the upper and lower powerdomain constructions commute for all bounded complete algebraic dcpos. R. Heckmann
[
Hec91

later extended this result to all dcpos. In
[
Hec92

[
JV91

that the upper and lower powerlocales commute for all locales. It can be shown that the upper powerlocale of a non-consonant space is not spatial, which explains why the powerlocales can commute even in the cases where the powerspaces do not.

Every quasi-Polish space is consonant, and the functions in Definition

2.
Preliminaries

A topological space is
quasi-Polish
if and only if it is countably based and the topology is induced by a Smyth-complete quasi-metric (see
[
dB13
]
). Several equivalent characterizations of quasi-Polish spaces exist, but for this paper the most relevant characterization is that a space is quasi-Polish if and only if it is homeomorphic to a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subset of an
\omega
-algebraic domain (i.e., a countably based algebraic domain). A subset
A
of a topological space
X
is a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subset
if there exist sequences
{(U_{i})}_{i\in\omega}
and
{(V_{i})}_{i\in\omega}
of open subsets of
X
satisfying

x\in A\text{ if and only if }(\forall i\in\omega)[x\in U_{i}\Rightarrow x\in V_{i}].

We denote the collection of
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subsets of
X
by
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}(X)
. If
X
is quasi-Polish then a subspace
A
of
X
is quasi-Polish if and only if
A\in\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}(X)
.

The
specialization preorder
on a topological space
X
is defined as
x\leq y
if and only if
x
is in the closure of the singleton set
\{y\}
. This is a partial order if and only if
X
is a
T_{0}
-space, in which case it is called the
specialization order
. Given a subset
A\subseteq X
, we define
{\uparrow}A=\{y\in X\mid(\exists x\in A)\,x\leq y\}
and
{\downarrow}A=\{y\in X\mid(\exists x\in A)\,y\leq x\}
. Note that the argument to
{\uparrow}
and
{\downarrow}
is always a set, and when
A=\{x\}
is a singleton we do not abbreviate
{\uparrow}A
by
{\uparrow}x
in order to avoid potential ambiguities when working with powerspaces.

We refer the reader to
[
GHK+03
]
and
[
GL13
]
for background on domain theory and the Scott topology on partially ordered sets. A. Schalk’s thesis
[
Sch93
]
is a valuable source of information on the powerspace constructions for general topological spaces, as well as the corresponding powerdomain and powerlocale constructions.

3.
Lower powerspaces

The
lower powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
is defined to be the set of closed subsets of
X
with the lower Vietoris topology. The lower Vietoris topology is generated by sets of the form
\Diamond U=\{A\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\,|\,A\cap U\not=\emptyset\}
for open
U\subseteq X
. Note that the specialization order on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
is subset inclusion.

It is well known that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}
is a monad on the category of topological spaces (see Section 6.3 in
[
Sch93
]
):

•

Each continuous function

defined as
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(f)(A)=Cl_{X}(f(A))
(the closure of the image of

under

•

The unit
\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}\colon X\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
maps

to
{\downarrow}\{x\}
,

•

The multiplication
\mu^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)

{\mathcal{A}}
to
\bigcup_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}}A

Note that
{(\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X})}^{-1}(\Diamond U)=U

4.
Upper powerspaces

The
upper powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is defined to be the set of compact saturated subsets of
X
with the upper Vietoris topology. A subset of a topological space is
saturated
if and only if it is equal to the intersection of all of its open neighborhoods. The upper Vietoris topology is generated by sets of the form
\Box U=\{K\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)\,|\,K\subseteq U\}
for open
U\subseteq X
. Note that the specialization order on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is
reverse
subset inclusion.

It is well known that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}
is a monad on the category of topological spaces (see Section 7.3 in
[
Sch93
]
):

•

Each continuous function
f\colon Y\to X
maps to
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(f)\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(Y)\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
defined as
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(f)(K)={\uparrow}f(K)
(the saturation of the image of
K
under
f
),

•

The unit
\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}_{X}\colon X\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
maps
x
to
{\uparrow}\{x\}
,

•

The multiplication
\mu^{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}_{X}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
maps
{\mathcal{K}}
to
\bigcup_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}K
.

Note that
{(\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}_{X})}^{-1}(\Box U)=U
and
{(\mu^{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}_{X})}^{-1}(\Box U)=\Box\Box U
and
{({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(f))}^{-1}(\Box U)=\Box f^{-1}(U)
for every open
U\subseteq X
and continuous
f\colon Y\to X
.

The proof of the next proposition was inspired by work by M. Schröder on the Scott topology on the open set lattice of sequential spaces (see, e.g.,
[
Sch15
]
).

Proposition 5
.

If
X
is sober and countably based, then the upper Vietoris topology on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
coincides with the Scott topology (where
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is ordered by reverse subset inclusion).

Proof 4.1
.

It is known that if
X
is sober then
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is sober with respect to the upper Vietoris topology (see Lemma 7.20 in
[
Sch93
]
). It follows that the upper Vietoris topology is contained within the Scott topology on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
.

Conversely, let
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
be any non-empty Scott-open set and fix
K\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Assume for a contradiction that for every subset
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
which is open with respect to the upper Vietoris topology, if
K\in{\mathcal{U}}
then
{\mathcal{U}}\not\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
.
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is countably based because
X
has a countable basis, so there exists a decreasing sequence of upper Vietoris open sets
{({\mathcal{U}}_{n})}_{n\in\omega}
forming a neighborhood basis for
K
in the upper Vietoris topology. By assumption there exists
K_{n}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}
, hence the sequence
{(K_{n})}_{n\in\omega}
converges to
K
with respect to the upper Vietoris topology, but none of the
K_{n}
are in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

For each
k\in\omega
, define
W_{k}=\bigcup_{n\geq k}K_{n}\cup K
. Since the
K_{n}
converge to
K
with respect to the upper Vietoris topology, any covering of
K
by open sets will cover all but finitely many of the
K_{n}
, so it easily follows that each
W_{k}
is a compact saturated subset of
X
. We claim that
K=\bigcap_{k\in\omega}W_{k}
. Clearly
K
is contained in each of the
W_{k}
. Conversely, if
x\in X\setminus K
, then
U=X\setminus{\downarrow}\{x\}
is an open subset of
X
containing
K
, hence all but finitely many
K_{n}
are in
\Box U
. Therefore,
x
is in at most finitely many
W_{k}
, which implies
x\not\in\bigcap_{k\in\omega}W_{k}
.

Since the ordering on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is reverse subset inclusion, the
W_{k}
are an increasing chain in
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
and
K
is the least upper bound of this chain. Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is Scott-open, there must be some
k_{0}
such that
W_{k}
is in
{\mathcal{H}}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. But then
K_{n}
is in
{\mathcal{H}}
for every
n\geq k_{0}
, a contradiction.

Therefore, there must be
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
which is open with respect to the upper Vietoris topology and satisfies
K\in{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
. It follows that the upper Vietoris and Scott topologies coincide on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
.

{exa}

The topology on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is not the weak topology in general, even when

is quasi-Polish. As a counterexample, consider the natural numbers
\omega
with the discrete topology. Then
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(\omega)
consists of all finite subsets of

{\mathcal{U}}_{K}=\{K^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)\mid K^{\prime}\not\supseteq K\}
for

if and only if

is any element of

for each

5.
Convex (Vietoris) powerspaces

The
convex
(or
Vietoris
)
powerspace

{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is defined to be the set of
lenses
in
X
with the Vietoris topology. A subset
L\subseteq X
is a
lens
if and only if there are
A\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
and
K\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
such that
L=A\cap K
. The Vietoris topology is the join of the lower and upper Vietoris topologies, and is generated by sets of the form
\Diamond U=\{L\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)\,|\,L\cap U\not=\emptyset\}
and
\Box U=\{L\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)\,|\,L\subseteq U\}
, where
U
varies over open subsets of
X
.

Theorem 9
.

If
X
is quasi-Polish then
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is quasi-Polish.

Proof 5.1
.

The space
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is quasi-Polish by Theorems
4
and
8
and because quasi-Polish spaces are closed under topological products. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is homeomorphic to the subspace of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
consisting of all pairs
\langle A,K\rangle
satisfying
Cl_{X}(A\cap K)=A
and
{\uparrow}(A\cap K)=K
. We can therefore identify
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
with this subspace of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
and it only remains to show that it is a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subspace.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a countable basis for
X
which is closed under finite unions (hence
{\mathcal{B}}
contains the empty set as the empty union). We define
{\mathcal{S}}
to be the subset of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
of all pairs
\langle A,K\rangle
satisfying the following two conditions for each
U,V\in{\mathcal{B}}
:

(1)

If
A\in\Diamond U
and
K\in\Box V
then
A\in\Diamond(U\cap V)
, and

(2)

If
K\in\Box(U\cup V)
then either
A\in\Diamond U
or else
K\in\Box V
.

Since there are only countably many conditions, and each condition is an implication between open subsets (predicates), it is clear that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subset of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
. We conclude by proving that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is homeomorphic to
{\mathcal{S}}
.

First, let
\langle A,K\rangle
be any pair in
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
satisfying
Cl_{X}(A\cap K)=A
and
{\uparrow}(A\cap K)=K
. Fix any
U,V\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

(1)

If
A\in\Diamond U
then
(A\cap K)\cap U\not=\emptyset
because
Cl_{X}(A\cap K)=A
. If furthermore
K\in\Box V
then
(A\cap K)\subseteq V
, hence
(A\cap K)\cap(U\cap V)\not=\emptyset
. Therefore,
A\in\Diamond U
and
K\in\Box V
implies
A\in\Diamond(U\cap V)
.

(2)

If
K\in\Box(U\cup V)
and
A\not\in\Diamond U
, then clearly
(A\cap K)\subseteq V
, hence
K\in\Box V
because
{\uparrow}(A\cap K)=K
.

It follows that
\langle A,K\rangle\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Conversely, let
\langle A,K\rangle
be any element of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

(1)

We prove that
K
is dense in
A
, hence
Cl_{X}(A\cap K)=A
. Since
K
is a compact saturated set, the assumption that
{\mathcal{B}}
is closed under finite unions implies the set
{\mathcal{D}}=\{V\in{\mathcal{B}}\mid K\subseteq V\}
satisfies
K=\bigcap{\mathcal{D}}
. For each
V\in{\mathcal{D}}
we clearly have
K\in\Box V
, and if
U\in{\mathcal{B}}
is any basic open satisfying
A\in\Diamond U
, then the assumption
\langle A,K\rangle\in{\mathcal{S}}
implies
A\in\Diamond(U\cap V)
. Therefore, each
V\in{\mathcal{D}}
is dense in
A
. The countability of
{\mathcal{B}}
implies
{\mathcal{D}}
is countable, hence
K=\bigcap{\mathcal{D}}
is dense in
A
because
X
is completely Baire.

(2)

If
W
is any open set containing
A\cap K
, then the compactness of
K
and the assumption that
{\mathcal{B}}
is closed under finite unions implies there exist
U,V\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
U\subseteq X\setminus A
and
V\subseteq W
and
K\subseteq(U\cup V)
. Since
\langle A,K\rangle\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
A\not\in\Diamond U
, we must have
K\in\Box V
. Thus, every open set
W
containing
A\cap K
contains
K
, hence
{\uparrow}(A\cap K)=K
.

It follows that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is homeomorphic to
{\mathcal{S}}
, hence
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
is quasi-Polish.

The two sets of conditions used in the above proof to construct
{\mathrm{\mathbf{L}}}(X)
as a
\mathbf{\Pi}^{0}_{2}
-subset of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
correspond to two of the axiom schemas in the locale theoretic presentation of the Vietoris locale as introduced by P.T. Johnstone in
[
Joh85
]
. In particular, these are the two axiom schemas which integrate the
\Diamond
and
\Box
modalities.

6.
Double powerspaces and consonance

In this section we will show that a topological property known as
consonance

[

NS96
,
CW98

Bou99
]

[

[
Bou99

Lemma 10
.

Let
X
be a topological space.

(1)

The topology on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))
is generated by sets of the form

\Diamond\Box U=\{{\mathcal{A}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))\,|\,(\exists K\in{\mathcal{A}})\,K\subseteq U\},

where
U\subseteq X
is open.

(2)

The topology on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
is generated by sets of the form

\Box\Diamond U=\{{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))\,|\,(\forall A\in{\mathcal{K}})\,A\cap U\not=\emptyset\},

where
U\subseteq X
is open.

Proof 6.1
.

In the following,
\{U^{i}_{j}\,|\,i\in I\mbox{ and }j\in J_{i}\}
vary over doubly indexed families of open subsets of
X
with
J_{i}
a finite index set for each
i\in I
.

Part (
1
) easily follows from the fact that the topology of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))
is generated by sets of the form

\Diamond{\mathcal{U}}=\Diamond\bigcup_{i\in I}\bigcap_{j\in J_{i}}\Box U^{i}_{j}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\Diamond\Box\bigcap_{j\in J_{i}}U^{i}_{j},

where the second equality holds because
\Diamond
commutes with unions and
\Box
commutes with finite intersections.

For Part (
2
), first note that the topology of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
is generated by sets of the form

\Box{\mathcal{U}}=\Box\bigcup_{i\in I}\bigcap_{j\in J_{i}}\Diamond U^{i}_{j}.

For any
{\mathcal{K}}\in\Box{\mathcal{U}}
, the compactness of
{\mathcal{K}}
implies there is finite
F\subseteq I
such that

{\mathcal{K}}\in\Box\bigcup_{i\in F}\bigcap_{j\in J_{i}}\Diamond U^{i}_{j}.

Let
S
be the set of all selection functions
s
that map each
i\in F
to some
s(i)\in J_{i}
. Then

\Box\bigcup_{i\in F}\bigcap_{j\in J_{i}}\Diamond U^{i}_{j}=\Box\bigcap_{s\in S}\bigcup_{i\in F}\Diamond U^{i}_{s(i)}

by distributivity. Using again the fact that
\Diamond
commutes with unions and
\Box
with finite intersections, we obtain

{\mathcal{K}}\in\bigcap_{s\in S}\Box\Diamond\bigcup_{i\in F}U^{i}_{s(i)}\subseteq\Box{\mathcal{U}},

which completes the proof.

is well-defined and continuous for every quasi-Polish space
X
.

Proof 9.1
.

We first show that
{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})
is an open subset of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
for each
{\mathfrak{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
. If
A\in{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})
, then
A\cap U\not=\emptyset
for each
U\in{\mathfrak{K}}
, hence
{\mathfrak{K}}\subseteq{\vartriangle}A
. Propositions
23
and
24
together imply
{\mathfrak{K}}
is strongly compact, hence there is finite
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
such that
{\mathfrak{K}}\subseteq{\uparrow}{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\vartriangle}A
. Therefore,
A\in\bigcap_{U\in{\mathcal{F}}}\Diamond U\subseteq{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})
, which implies
{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})
is open.

Next we show that
{\gamma}
is continuous. Since
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
is consonant, it suffices to show that
{\gamma}^{-1}(\triangledown{\mathcal{K}})
is open for each
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
. For
{\mathfrak{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
we have

\displaystyle{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})\in\triangledown{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq\bigcap_{U\in{\mathfrak{K}}}\Diamond U

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall U\in{\mathfrak{K}})\,{\mathcal{K}}\in\Box\Diamond U

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall U\in{\mathfrak{K}})\,U\in{\phi}({\mathcal{K}})\text{ (by definition of ${\phi}$)}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}\in\Box{\phi}({\mathcal{K}}),

where
{\phi}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
is the homeomorphism from Definition
6
.

Lemma 36
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
be an upper set. Then for each
A\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
it holds that
A\in{\mathcal{S}}
if and only if
{\mathcal{S}}\not\subseteq\Diamond(X\setminus A)
.

Proof 9.2
.

Clearly, if
A\in{\mathcal{S}}
then
{\mathcal{S}}\not\subseteq\Diamond(X\setminus A)
. Conversely, if
{\mathcal{S}}\not\subseteq\Diamond(X\setminus A)
then there must be
A^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that
A^{\prime}\not\in\Diamond(X\setminus A)
, which implies
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
. Since
{\mathcal{S}}
is an upper set, we obtain
A\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Lemma 37
.

The function
{\delta}
is well-defined and continuous for every quasi-Polish space
X
.

Proof 9.3
.

In order to prove that
{\delta}
is well-defined we must show that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
for each
{\mathcal{U}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
. It is easy to see that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})
is an upper set, so we only need to show that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})
is compact.

Since
X
is consonant, we only need to consider coverings of
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})
by basic opens of the form
\triangledown K
with
K\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
. So assume
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
is such that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\subseteq\bigcup_{K\in{\mathcal{I}}}\triangledown K
. Define
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))
to be the closure of
{\mathcal{I}}
in
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X)
. Set
{\mathcal{K}}={\sigma}({\mathcal{A}})
, where
{\sigma}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
is the homeomorphism from Definition
6
.

We show that
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}
. If
A\in{\mathcal{K}}
then
A\cap K\not=\emptyset
for each
K\in{\mathcal{I}}
, hence
(X\setminus A)\not\in\bigcup_{K\in{\mathcal{I}}}\triangledown K
, which implies
(X\setminus A)\not\in{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})
. Therefore,
A\in{\mathcal{U}}
by Lemma
36
and the definition of
{\delta}
.

Using the same methods as in the proof of Lemma
10
, it is easy to see that the basic opens of the form
\bigcap_{U\in{\mathcal{F}}}\Diamond U
for finite
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
form a basis for
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
that is closed under finite unions. Therefore, by compactness of
{\mathcal{K}}
there must be
U_{0},\ldots,U_{n}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
such that
{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq\Diamond U_{0}\cap\cdots\cap\Diamond U_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}
. Then for
0\leq i\leq n
, we have
{\sigma}({\mathcal{A}})={\mathcal{K}}\in\Box\Diamond U_{i}
, hence
{\mathcal{A}}\in\Diamond\Box U_{i}
by Lemma
12
. From the definition of
\Diamond
there exist
K_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}\cap\Box U_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n
, and as
{\mathcal{A}}
is the closure of
{\mathcal{I}}
, each
K_{i}
can be chosen from
{\mathcal{I}}
.

Now we show that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\subseteq\triangledown K_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\triangledown K_{n}
. If
U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\Diamond U
then
\Diamond U_{0}\cap\cdots\cap\Diamond U_{n}\subseteq\Diamond U
, and hence
\Box\Diamond U_{0}\cap\cdots\cap\Box\Diamond U_{n}\subseteq\Box\Diamond U
because
\Box
is monotonic and distributes over finite intersections. Lemma
12
yields

\displaystyle\Diamond\Box U_{0}\cap\cdots\cap\Diamond\Box U_{n}

\displaystyle={\sigma}^{-1}(\Box\Diamond U_{0})\cap\cdots\cap{\sigma}^{-1}(\Box\Diamond U_{n})

\displaystyle={\sigma}^{-1}(\Box\Diamond U_{0}\cap\cdots\cap\Box\Diamond U_{n})\subseteq{\sigma}^{-1}(\Box\Diamond U)

\displaystyle=\Diamond\Box U.

Since
K_{i}\in\Box U_{i}
we obtain
{\downarrow}\{K_{0},\ldots,K_{n}\}\in\Diamond\Box U
, hence
K_{i}\subseteq U
for some
i\leq n
. Therefore,
U\in\triangledown K_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\triangledown K_{n}
. It follows that
{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})
is compact, hence
{\delta}
is well-defined.

Finally, we show that
{\delta}
is continuous. Fix
{\mathcal{H}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
. Then for
{\mathcal{U}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
we have

\displaystyle{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\in\Box{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))[\,U\in{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\Rightarrow U\in{\mathcal{H}}\,]

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))[\,{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\Diamond U\Rightarrow{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})\in\Box\Diamond U\,]\text{ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:OOX->KAX})}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))[\,{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})\not\subseteq\Diamond U\Rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}\not\subseteq\Diamond U\,]

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall A\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))[\,{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})\not\subseteq\Diamond(X\setminus A)\Rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}\not\subseteq\Diamond(X\setminus A)\,]

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle(\forall A\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))[\,A\in{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})\Rightarrow A\in{\mathcal{U}}\,]\text{ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:upperset_in_PL})}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}\in\triangledown{\psi}({\mathcal{H}}),

where
{\psi}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
is the homeomorphism from Definition
6
.

Theorem 38
.

If
X
is quasi-Polish then
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
with the upper Vietoris topology is homeomorphic (via
{\gamma}
and its inverse
{\delta}
) with
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
with the Scott topology.

Proof 9.4
.

From the previous lemmas, it suffices to show that
{\delta}\circ{\gamma}
and
{\gamma}\circ{\delta}
are the identity functions.

First, for
{\mathcal{U}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
and
{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
, from the proofs of Lemmas
35
and
37
we have

\displaystyle{\gamma}({\delta}({\mathcal{U}}))\in\triangledown{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\delta}({\mathcal{U}})\in\Box{\phi}({\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}\in\triangledown{\psi}({\phi}({\mathcal{K}}))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}\in\triangledown{\mathcal{K}}\text{ (by Theorem~\ref{thrm:KA_OO_iso})},

hence
{\gamma}\circ{\delta}
is the identity on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
.

Similarly, for
{\mathfrak{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
and
{\mathcal{H}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
we have

\displaystyle{\delta}({\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}))\in\Box{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})\in\triangledown{\psi}({\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}\in\Box{\phi}({\psi}({\mathcal{H}}))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}\in\Box{\mathcal{H}}\text{ (by Theorem~\ref{thrm:KA_OO_iso})},

hence
{\delta}\circ{\gamma}
is the identity on
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
.

The homeomorphisms
{\gamma}
and
{\delta}
determine natural isomorphisms between the contravariant functors
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}\circ{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}
and
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}\circ{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}
when restricted to quasi-Polish spaces. The proof is essentially the same as the proof for
{\alpha}
and
{\beta}
in Section
8
. To prove that
{\delta}_{X}\circ{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(f))={\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))\circ{\delta}_{Y}
for every continuous
f\colon X\to Y
, we first show that both functions in the equation are frame homomorphisms. The non-trivial part of this claim is showing that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))
preserves binary joins.
4
4

4

We will give a direct topological proof here, but alternatively one could use Beck distributivity to show that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))
is a morphism of
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}
-algebras, and then use Schalk’s results (see Section 6.3 of
[
Sch93
]
) to show that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))
preserves joins.
We get an explicit description of binary joins in
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
by using the order isomorphism
{\gamma}
to pass to
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
, where joins are unions, and then pull the result back to
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
via the order isomorphism
{\delta}
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\vee{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\delta}({\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{1})\cup{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)\mid{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{1})\cup{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{2})\subseteq\Diamond U\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)\mid{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{1})\subseteq\Diamond U\text{ \& }{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{2})\subseteq\Diamond U\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)\mid{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{1})\subseteq\Diamond U\}\cap\{U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)\mid{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{2})\subseteq\Diamond U\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\delta}({\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{1}))\cap{\delta}({\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}.

Thus binary joins in
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
correspond to intersections, and similarly for
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(Y))
. The rest of the proof that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))
preserves binary joins is dual to the proof that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))
preserves binary meets, and this time uses the fact that saturated compact sets are upper sets and that
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)
preserves binary joins:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))({\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{K}}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\uparrow}\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U)\mid U\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\uparrow}\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U_{1}\cup U_{2})\mid U_{1}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\text{ \& }U_{2}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\uparrow}\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U_{1})\cup{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U_{2})\mid U_{1}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\text{ \& }U_{2}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\uparrow}\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U_{1})\mid U_{1}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{1}\}\cap{\uparrow}\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(U_{2})\mid U_{2}\in{\mathfrak{K}}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))({\mathfrak{K}}_{1})\cap{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))({\mathfrak{K}}_{2}).

So to prove that the frame homomorphisms
{\delta}_{X}\circ{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(f))
and
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))\circ{\delta}_{Y}
are equal, it suffices to show that they both map each subbasic open
\Diamond U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(Y))
to
{\uparrow}\{f^{-1}(U)\}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
. Indeed,
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(f))(\Diamond U)={{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(f)}^{-1}(\Diamond U)=\Diamond f^{-1}(U)
, which
{\delta}_{X}
then maps to
{\{W\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)\mid\Diamond f^{-1}(U)\subseteq\Diamond W\}={\uparrow}\{f^{-1}(U)\}}
. Going in the other direction,
{\delta}_{Y}(\Diamond U)=\{W\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(Y)\mid\Diamond U\subseteq\Diamond W\}={\uparrow}\{U\}
, and
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f))({\uparrow}\{U\})
equals the saturation of
\{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(f)(W)\mid U\subseteq W\}
, which is just
{\uparrow}\{f^{-1}(U)\}
.

Corollary 39
.

If
X
is quasi-Polish then the function
\bigcap\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
, which maps a compact subset of opens to their set-theoretical intersection, is well-defined and continuous.

Proof 9.5
.

Let
\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}\colon X\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
be the unit for the monad
{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}
. The function
{(\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X})}^{-1}\colon{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))\to{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
, which maps each open
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X)
to its preimage under
\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}
, is a frame homomorphism and therefore Scott-continuous. Given
{\mathfrak{K}}\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X))
we have

\displaystyle x\in{(\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X})}^{-1}({\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}}))

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}(x)\in{\gamma}({\mathfrak{K}})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X}(x)\in\bigcap_{U\in{\mathfrak{K}}}\Diamond U

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle x\in\bigcap_{U\in{\mathfrak{K}}}U.

Therefore,
\bigcap={(\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}_{X})}^{-1}\circ{\gamma}
is well-defined and continuous.

It is also interesting to note that the composition
\gamma\circ\eta^{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}_{{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)}
is the continuous function which maps
U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
to
\Diamond U\in{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}(X))
.

We obtain the following corollary from A. Schalk’s characterization of the algebras of the upper powerspace monad (see Section 7.3 in
[
Sch93
]
).

Corollary 40
.

If
X
is quasi-Polish, then
{\mathrm{\mathbf{O}}}(X)
is an algebra of the upper powerspace monad
{\mathrm{\mathbf{K}}}
, with set-theoretical intersection as the structure map.

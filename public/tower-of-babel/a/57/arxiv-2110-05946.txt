Upper bounds of orders of automorphism groups of leafless metric graphs
Yusuke Nakamura, Song JuAe

Abstract
. We prove a tropical analogue of the theorem of Hurwitz: a leafless metric graph of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 has at most 12 12 automorphisms when g = 2 g=2 ; 2 g ​ g ! 2^{g}g! automorphisms when g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 . These inequalities are optimal; for each genus, we give all metric graphs which have the maximum numbers of automorphisms. The proof is written in terms of graph theory.

1.
Introduction

In classical algebraic geometry, the theorem of Hurwitz says that an algebraic curve of genus
g\geq 2
has at most
84(g-1)
automorphisms (cf.
[
3
, Ex. 2.5. in Chapter IV]
).
In this paper, we will prove a tropical analogue of this theorem.

Tropical geometry is, roughly speaking, an algebraic geometry over the tropical semifield
\boldsymbol{T}:=(\boldsymbol{R}\cup\{-\infty\},{\rm max},+)
(cf.
[
5
]
for an introduction to tropical geometry).
The process of passing from classical arithmetic (resp. classical algebro-geometric objects) to tropical arithmetic (resp. tropical objects) is referred to as tropicalization.

Tropicalizations of algebraic curves have abstract tropical curve structures.
An abstract tropical curve is an extension of a metric graph.
A
metric graph
is defined as the underlying metric space of the pair of an unweighted, undirected, finite, connected nonempty multigraph
G
which may have loops and a length function
l:E_{G}\to\boldsymbol{R}_{>0}
with an identification of each edge
e
of
G
with the closed interval
[0,l(e)]
, where
E_{G}
denotes the set of edges of
G
.
For an abstract tropical curve
\varGamma
, we allow
l
to take the value
\infty
on only edges incident to leaves.
Each point
\infty\in[0,\infty]
must be identified with a leaf, where
[0,\infty]
is the one point compactification of
[0,\infty)
.
If
l
takes the value
\infty
, then
\varGamma
is no longer a metric space, but a topological space (cf.
[
4
]
for more details).

An abstract tropical curve
\varGamma
has a
genus
in the usual topological sense.
It coincides with
\#E_{G}-\#V_{G}+1
for any pair
(G,l)
defining
\varGamma
, where
V_{G}
denotes the set of vertices of
G
.
Nonnegative integer
\#E_{G}-\#V_{G}+k
for a graph
G
with
k
connected components is sometimes called the
(first) Betti number
of
G
(cf.
[
2
]
).

In the category of abstract tropical curves, morphisms between abstract tropical curves are (finite) harmonic morphisms.
An automorphism of a metric graph
\varGamma
, i.e., a finite harmonic morphism
\varGamma\to\varGamma
of degree one is an isometry
\varGamma\to\varGamma
, and vice versa.
For a metric graph
\varGamma
, we write its automorphism group as
\operatorname{Aut}(\varGamma)
, which coincides with the isometry group of
\varGamma
.

The following is our main theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varGamma
be a leafless metric graph of genus
g\geq 2
.
Then the inequality

\#\operatorname{Aut}(\varGamma)\leq\begin{cases}2^{g}g!&\text{if $g\geq 3$,}\\
12&\text{if $g=2$}\end{cases}

holds.
Furthermore, the inequality becomes an equality if and only if
\varGamma
is one of the following:

•

\varGamma
is defined by the pair of
G_{{\rm banana},g}
and some
l
whose image is one point with
g=2,3
.

•

\varGamma
is defined by the pair of
G_{{\rm bouquet},g}
and some
l
whose image is one point with
g\geq 3
.

•

\varGamma
is defined by the pair of
G_{{\rm lollipop},g}
and some
l
such that all
g
loops have the same length and all
g
bridges have the same length with
g\geq 3
.

Here, an abstract tropical curve is
leafless
if it has no one valent points (it is said to be
minimal
in
[
1
]
).
The
valency
of a point
x
of an abstract tropical curve is the minimum number of connected components of
U\setminus\{x\}
with all neighborhoods
U
of
x
.
For
g\geq 1
,
G_{{\rm banana},g}
denotes the multigraph that has only two vertices and
g+1
multiple edges between them.
For
g\geq 1
,
G_{{\rm bouquet},g}
denotes the graph that has only one vertex and
g
loops incident to it.
For
g\geq 2
,
G_{{\rm lollipop},g}
denotes the graph obtained from
G_{{\rm bouquet},g}
by replacing the unique vertex with the star
S_{g}
with
g
leaves.

We note that there are no upper bounds of
\#\operatorname{Aut}(\varGamma)
without the leafless condition in Theorem
1
.
Let
\varGamma
be a metric graph of genus
g
, and let
v\in\varGamma
be a point.
Let
\varGamma^{\prime}
be the metric graph obtained from
\varGamma
by attaching
0
’s of
n
copies of the intervals
[0,1]
to the point
v
.
Then
\varGamma^{\prime}
has genus
g
, and we have
\#\operatorname{Aut}(\varGamma^{\prime})\geq n!
since
\operatorname{Aut}(\varGamma^{\prime})
contains the symmetric group of degree
n
as a subgroup.

In tropical geometry, it is natural to deal only leafless ones.
There is an operation called
tropical modification
which defines an equivalence relation on all abstract tropical curves.
Two abstract tropical curves are equivalent to each other if and only if one of them is obtained from the other by a finite number of retractions that contract a leaf edge to its one point (cf.
[
1
]
).
Hence for an equivalence class, all representatives have the same genus and there exists a unique leafless representative.
For this reason, in tropical geometry, one studies leafless metric graphs for some essential information of their equivalence classes (cf.
[
6
]
for higher dimension cases).

Theorem
1
follows the following combinatorial proposition:

Proposition 2
.

Let
G
be a connected leafless graph of Betti number
g\geq 2
.
Then the inequality

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)\leq\begin{cases}2^{g}g!&\text{if $g\geq 3$,}\\
12&\text{if $g=2$}\end{cases}

holds.
Furthermore, the inequality becomes an equality if and only if
G
is one of the following:

(A)

G
is a graph obtained from
G_{{\rm banana},g}
by subdividing all
g+1
edges into the same number (
\geq 1
) of edges with
g=2,3
.

(B)

G
is a graph obtained from
G_{{\rm bouquet},g}
by subdividing all
g
loops into the same number (
\geq 2
) of edges with
g\geq 3
.

(C)

G
is a graph obtained from
G_{{\rm lollipop},g}
by subdividing all
g
loops into the same number
(
\geq 2
) of edges and all
g
bridges into a same number (
\geq 1
) of edges with
g\geq 3
.

G_{{\rm banana},2}
, the center graph is
G_{{\rm bouquet},3}
and the right graph is
G_{{\rm lollipop},4}
. Black dots (resp. lines) stand for vertices (resp. edges).

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this paper, a
graph

G=(V_{G},E_{G},\varepsilon_{G})
means an undirected finite graph allowing multiple edges and loops.
Here,
V_{G}
is the finite set of vertices, and
E_{G}
is the finite set of edges, and
\varepsilon_{G}:E_{G}\to 2^{V_{G}}

is the map that associates an edge
e\in E_{G}
with its vertices, where
2^{V_{G}}
is the power set of
V_{G}
.
We note that
\#(\varepsilon_{G}(e))\in\{1,2\}
holds for any
e\in E_{G}
.
An edge
e
is called a
loop
when
\#(\varepsilon_{G}(e))=1
.
A
cut vertex
(resp.
bridge
) of a graph
G
is a vertex (resp. an edge)
whose deletion increases the number of connected components of
G
.
The
degree
of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it, where a loop is counted twice.
A vertex of degree one is called a
leaf
.

An
automorphism

f=(f_{V},f_{E})
of a graph
G
is
bijective maps
f_{V}:V_{G}\to V_{G}
and
f_{E}:E_{G}\to E_{G}
satisfying
f_{V}\circ\varepsilon_{G}=\varepsilon_{G}\circ f_{E}
.

\operatorname{Aut}(G)
denotes the set of all automorphisms of
G
.
For a subset
S\subset V_{G}\sqcup E_{G}
,

\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S}
denotes the set of all
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
satisfying

f_{V}(v)=v
and
f_{E}(e)=e
for any
v\in S\cap V_{G}
and
e\in S\cap E_{G}
.
When
S
consists of only one element
x
,
we sometimes write
\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}
instead of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x\}}
.
We note that according to this definition,
\#\operatorname{Aut}(G_{{\rm bouquet},1})=1
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a connected leafless graph.
Let
x
be a vertex and let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
be all the edges incident to
x
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
obtained by removing
x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
from
G
.
Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the maximum subgraph of
G^{\prime}
in which all vertices have degree at least two.
We set

S=\left(E_{G}\setminus E_{G^{\prime\prime}}\right)\sqcup\bigcup_{e\in E_{G}\setminus E_{G^{\prime\prime}}}\varepsilon_{G}(e).

Then the following hold.

(1)

\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}=\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S}
.

(2)

Suppose that
G^{\prime\prime}\not=\varnothing
. Then there exists an injective map

\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\prime\prime})_{y}

for some vertex
y\in V_{G^{\prime\prime}}
.

Proof.

If
G
is trivial, then the assertions are clear.
Assume that
G
is not trivial.

G^{\prime\prime}
is obtained from
G^{\prime}
by repeating the following two operations:

(a)

Removing a vertex
v
of degree zero.

(b)

Removing a leaf
v
and the edge
e
incident to
v
.

Let

G^{\prime}=G_{0}\supset G_{1}\supset\cdots\supset G_{c}=G^{\prime\prime}

be its process.
Then, corresponding to the two operations above, the following hold:

(a)

E_{G_{i+1}}=E_{G_{i}}
and
V_{G_{i}}\setminus V_{G_{i+1}}=\{v\}
.

(b)

E_{G_{i}}\setminus E_{G_{i+1}}=\{e\}
and
V_{G_{i}}\setminus V_{G_{i+1}}=\{v\}
.

We set

S_{i}=\left(E_{G}\setminus E_{G_{i}}\right)\sqcup\bigcup_{e\in E_{G}\setminus E_{G_{i}}}\varepsilon_{G}(e).

Since
G
is connected and nontrivial,
d\geq 1
holds.
Hence
S_{0}\supset\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
.
By the definition of
S_{0}
, we have
\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}=\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{0}}
.
Thus, to prove (1), it is sufficient to show

\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{i}}=\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{i+1}}
for each
0\leq i\leq c-1
.

The assertion is clear for case (a). Suppose (b).
Let
u
be the end point of
e
that is not
v
.
Since all vertices of
G
have degree at least two, it follows that
v\in S_{i}
.
Furthermore, any edge of
G
incident to
v
other than
e
is contained in
S_{i}
.
Therefore any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{i}}
fixes
e
and hence
u
too.
Thus we have
\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{i}}=\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S_{i+1}}
, which completes the proof of (1).

By construction, it follows that
S\cap V_{G^{\prime\prime}}\not=\varnothing
when
G^{\prime\prime}\not=\varnothing
.
Pick any
y\in S\cap V_{G^{\prime\prime}}
.
Then the restriction map
\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{S}\to\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\prime\prime})_{y}
is induced and it is injective.
∎

Let
G
be a graph and
S\subset E_{G}
.
Then the
contraction

G/S
is a graph defined as follows:

V_{G/S}=V_{G}/\sim,\qquad E_{G/S}=E_{G}\setminus S,\qquad\varepsilon_{G/S}:E_{G/S}\to 2^{V_{G/S}},

where

is the minimum relation satisfying

with

, and

\varepsilon_{G/S}
is the composition
E_{G/S}\to E_{G}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{G}}2^{V_{G}}\to 2^{V_{G/S}}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a graph and
S\subset E_{G}
. Let
G/S
be its contraction. Then the following hold.

(1)

If
S
is
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant,
there exists a group homomorphim
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G/S)
which commutes with
the projection
V_{G}\to V_{G/S}
and the inclusion
E_{G/S}\to E_{G}
.

(2)

Let
S
be the set of all bridges of
G
. Then, the Betti number of
G/S
is equal to that of
G
.

(3)

In addtion to (2), we assume that
G
is leafless.
Then the group homomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G/S)
is injective.

Proof.

We shall prove (1).
Let
f=(f_{V},f_{E})\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.
Since
S
is
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant,
f_{V}
and
f_{E}
induce the bijective maps

on

and

, respectively.
Since they satisfy

,

.
Then we obtain a group homomorphim
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G/S);\ f\mapsto f^{\prime}
, which proves (1).

Since

forms the union of trees, we have

, which proves (2).

We shall prove (3).
Let

be an element whose image in

is the identity map.
Let
H
be the subgraph of
G

and

.
By assumption,

holds for

.
Let
v
be a leaf of
H
and let

be the edge of

v
.
First, we shall see that
f
fixes
v
.
Since
v
has degree at least two in
G
, there exists an edge
e_{1}\in E_{G}\setminus E_{H}

such that
e_{1}\not=e
and
v\in\varepsilon_{G}(e_{1})
.
If
e_{1}
is a loop, then
f
fixes
v
because
f
fixes

.
Suppose that
e_{1}
is not a loop.
Let
v^{\prime}
be the end point of
e_{1}
that is not
v
.
Since
f
fixes
e_{1}
, it follows that
f_{V}(v)=v
or
f_{V}(v)=v^{\prime}
.
Since
S
does not contain a circuit, it follows that

.
On the other hand, since the image of

in

is the identity map, we have

.
Therefore
f
fixes
v
.
Since
f
fixes all edges incident to

, it also fixes

.
Let
v^{\prime\prime}
be the end point of

that is not

.
Then
f
also fixes

.
Let
H^{\prime}
be the subgraph of

obtained by removing

.
Then we have showed that

for

.
Since

by induction,

Lemma 5
.

(1)

Let
l
,
m
and
n
be positive integers with
m,n\geq l
.
Then we have

m!n!\leq l!(m+n-l)!.

(2)

Let

be positive integers. Then we have

(n_{1}!)(n_{2}!)\dotsm(n_{c}!)\leq\left(-c+1+\sum_{i=1}^{c}n_{i}\right)!.

3.
Main results

In this section, we will prove the two assertions in Section 1.

The following proposition is valid also for
g=1
in contrast to Proposition
2
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
G
be a connected leafless graph of Betti number
g
and
x
a vertex of
G
.
Then the inequality

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq 2^{g}g!

holds.

Proof.

If
g=0
, then
G
is trivial, and the assertion is clear.
Therefore we may assume that
g\geq 1
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
4
, we may assume that
G
has no bridges.

Let

G\setminus\{x\}=U_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup U_{k}

be the decomposition to the connected components.
Let
G_{i}=U_{i}\cup\{x\}
be the subgraph of
G
.
Let
g_{i}
be the Betti number of
G_{i}
.
Then
g=\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}
and
g_{i}\geq 1
hold.
Furthermore, since
G
has no bridges, each
G_{i}
is leafless.

First, we treat the case when
k=1
.

Claim 7
.

When
k=1
, the inequality

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq 2^{g-d+1}d!(g-d+1)!

holds, where
d
is the number of edges incident to
x
.
In particular, Proposition
6
holds when
k=1
.

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
be all the edges incident to
x
.
Let
S_{d}=S\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
denote the permutation group of
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
.
Then a group homomorphism
s
is defined by

s:\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\to S_{d};\qquad f\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}e_{1}&e_{2}&\cdots&e_{d}\\
f(e_{1})&f(e_{2})&\cdots&f(e_{d})\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Thus

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq\#S_{d}\cdot\#\operatorname{Ker}(s)=d!\cdot\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}

hold.
We define the subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
by removing
x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
from
G
.
Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the maximum subgraph of
G^{\prime}
in which all vertices have degree at least two.
We note that
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
are connected since
k=1
.
Furthermore,
G^{\prime}\not=\varnothing
holds unless
G=G_{{\rm bouquet},1}
.
If
G^{\prime}\not=\varnothing
, then the Betti number of
G^{\prime}
is equal to
g-d+1
.
In particular, we have
g-d+1\geq 0
.

Suppose
d=g+1
.
Then
G^{\prime}
is a tree, and it follows that
G^{\prime\prime}=\varnothing
.
Then by Lemma
3
,

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}=\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{V_{G}\sqcup E_{G}}=1,

and hence

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq d!\cdot\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}=(g+1)!\leq 2^{g}g!.

Suppose that
g-d+1\geq 1
.
If
G=G_{{\rm bouquet},1}
, then we have
\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}=1<2=2^{g-d+1}d!(g-d+1)!
.
In what follows, we suppose
G^{\prime\prime}\not=\varnothing
.
Then we have
d\geq 2
and the Betti number of
G^{\prime\prime}
is equal to
g^{\prime}:=g-d+1
.
Then by Lemma
3
(2), there exists a vertex
y
of
G^{\prime\prime}
such that

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}\to\#\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\prime\prime})_{y};\qquad f\mapsto f|_{G^{\prime\prime}}

is injective.
Thus, we have

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\{x,e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}}\leq\#\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\prime\prime})_{y}.

By induction on the Betti number, we may assume that

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\prime\prime})_{y}\leq 2^{g^{\prime}}g^{\prime}!=2^{g-d+1}(g-d+1)!.

Therefore, we have

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq 2^{g-d+1}d!(g-d+1)!<2^{g}g!.

Here, the second inequality follows from Lemma

and
d\geq 2
.
∎

Suppose
k\geq 2
.
Then by
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}
, each
G_{i}
is mapped to some
G_{j}
.
It induces the group homomorphism

t:\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\to S_{k}=S\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}\};\qquad f\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}G_{1}&G_{2}&\cdots&G_{k}\\
f(G_{1})&f(G_{2})&\cdots&f(G_{k})\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Since the kernel

consists of all

such that

for each

,

\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}\leq\#S_{k}\cdot\#\operatorname{Ker}(t)\leq k!\cdot\#\operatorname{Aut}(G_{1})_{x}\dotsm\#\operatorname{Aut}(G_{k})_{x}

hold.
Then, by induction on the Betti number, we have

(
6
.1)

\displaystyle\#\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{x}

\displaystyle\leq k!(2^{g_{1}}g_{1}!)\dotsm(2^{g_{k}}g_{k}!)

\displaystyle=2^{g}(k!)(g_{1}!)\dotsm(g_{k}!)

(
6
.2)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g}\left(-k-1+1+k+\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}\right)!

\displaystyle=2^{g}g!.

The last inequality follows from Lemma
5
.
We complete the proof of Proposition
6
.
∎

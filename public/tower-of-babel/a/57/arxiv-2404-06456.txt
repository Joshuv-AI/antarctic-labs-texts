Sharp Propagation of Chaos for the Ensemble Langevin Sampler
U. Vaes

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to revisit propagation of chaos for a Langevin-type interacting particle system recently proposed as a method to sample probability measures. The interacting particle system we consider coincides, in the setting of a log-quadratic target distribution, with the ensemble Kalman sampler [ 14 ] , for which propagation of chaos was first proved by Ding and Li in [ 10 ] . Like these authors, we prove propagation of chaos with an approach based on a synchronous coupling, as in Sznitman’s classical argument [ 27 ] . Instead of relying on a boostrapping argument, however, we use a technique based on stopping times in order to handle the presence of the empirical covariance in the coefficients of the dynamics. The use of stopping times to handle the lack of global Lipschitz continuity in the coefficients of stochastic dynamics originates from numerical analysis [ 18 ] and was recently employed to prove mean field limits for consensus-based optimization and related interacting particle systems [ 19 , 16 ] . In the context of ensemble Langevin sampling, this technique enables proving pathwise propagation of chaos with optimal rate, whereas previous results were optimal only up to a positive ε \varepsilon .

Introduction

In a wide variety of applications,
ranging from Bayesian inference to statistical physics and computational biology,
it is necessary to produce samples from high-dimensional probability distributions of the form

\mu=\frac{\e^{-\phi}}{Z},\qquad Z=\int_{\real{}^{d}}\e^{-\phi}.

(1.1)

where
\phi\colon\real^{d}\rightarrow\real
is a given function such that
\e^{-\phi}
is integrable.
In
[
14
]
,
the authors propose to simulate the following interacting particle system in order to generate approximate samples from
\mu
:

\mathrm{d}X^{j}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}=-\cov(\mu^{J}_{t})\nabla\phi(X^{j}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\cov(\mu^{J}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W^{j}_{t},\qquad j\in,

(1.2)

where

(W^{j})_{j\in}
are independent standard Brownian motions in
d
,

\mu^{J}_{t}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{X^{j}_{t}}
is the associated empirical measure,
and
\cov(\mu^{J}_{t})
denotes the covariance under
\mu^{J}_{t}
;
see (
1.7
) below for the precise definition.

If the covariance
\cov(\mu^{J}_{t})
in (
1.2
) was replaced by a constant symmetric positive definite matrix,
then (
1.2
) would describe a system of independent overdamped Langevin diffusions
in an external potential
\phi
,
and the system would be suitable for sampling from
\mu
.
The idea of the method (
1.2
) is to include the covariance as a form of preconditioner,
in order to accelerate convergence to the invariant distribution.
The authors of
[
14
]

also present a gradient-free approximation of (
1.2
)
that is well-suited to those Bayesian inverse problems
for which it is difficult or undesirable to calculate derivatives of the forward model,
examples of which are given in
[
11
]
.
Taking formally the limit
J\to\infty
in (
1.2
),
they conjecture that the mean field limit of the system is given by the following McKean stochastic differential equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\overline{X}_{t}&=-\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
\overline{\rho}_{t}&=\Law(\overline{X}_{t})\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

For background material on mean field limits and propagation of chaos,
we refer to
[
6
,
7
]
.
The mean field equation (
1.3
) is often simpler to analyze mathematically than the interacting particle system (
1.2
).

It is simple to show, notably, that the target probability distribution
\mu
is an invariant measure of (
1.3
).

In the setting where
\phi
is quadratic,
it is
also possible to prove that the law
\overline{\rho}_{t}
converges exponentially as
t\to\infty

to the target distribution
\mu
in the Euclidean Wasserstein metric,
provided that the covariance under
\overline{\rho}_{0}
is nondegenerate.
Furthermore, in this case the convergence rate is independent of the mean and covariance parameters of the target measure
\mu

[
5
,
14
]
,
a property related to the affine invariance of the dynamics
[
15
]
.

Although the interacting particle system (
1.2
) is always simulated with a finite number of particles in practice,
mathematical study of the mean field equation (
1.3
)
enables to gain insight
into the behavior of the particle system for large
J
.

Indeed
, once a quantitative version of propagation of chaos has been established,
error estimates in the finite-ensemble setting can be derived from those at the mean field level via
triangle inequalities
.

This motivates the study of propagation of chaos for (
1.2
)
.

Before focusing on (
1.2
), let us mention that
, in
[
25
]
,
a correction of (
1.2
) is proposed to ensure that the interacting particle system possesses as invariant distribution the tensorized measure
\mu^{\otimes J}

for every value of
J\geqslant d+1
.
The properties of the resulting interacting particle system,
with acronym ALDI,
are analyzed in
[
15
]
.

Summary of previous work

The presence of the empirical covariance,
a quadratic quantity,
is an obstruction for proving propagation of chaos rigorously for (
1.2
),
because McKean’s and Sznitman’s classical arguments assume Lipschitz interactions.
The first proof of propagation of chaos for (
1.2
) was proposed in
[
10
]
by Ding and Li,
who investigated the particular situation where
\phi
is quadratic.

In the setting of Bayesian inverse problems
[
26
]
,
this situation arises when the forward model is linear,
observational noise is Gaussian and the prior distribution is Gaussian.
In order to show convergence,
in an appropriate sense,
of the interacting particle system (
1.2
) to the mean field limit (
1.3
),
Ding and Li employ as a pivot the following
synchronously coupled system,
with the same initial condition and the same Brownian motions:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\overline{X}^{j}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}&=-\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}^{j}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W^{j}_{t},\qquad j\in,\\
\overline{\rho}_{t}&=\operatorname{Law}\left(\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

Using a novel bootstrapping argument,
which is summarized in
[
7
, p. 142]
,
they then prove that for every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
C
independent of
J
such that

\forall J\in\mathbf{N}^{+},\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\left[\bigl\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\bigr\rvert^{2}\right]\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

(1.5)

Combining this bound with Wasserstein convergence estimates for empirical measures formed from i.i.d. samples
[
13
]
,
they then deduce a convergence estimate of the form

\forall J\in\mathbf{N}^{+},\qquad\mathbf{E}\Bigl[W_{2}(\mu^{J}_{T},\overline{\rho}_{T})\Bigr]\leqslant CJ^{-\alpha},

(1.6)

for an appropriate
\alpha>0
.
The derivation of (
1.6
) from (
1.5
) is straightforward and independent of the interacting particle system considered.
In the terminology of
[
6
]
,
estimate (
1.5
) establishes pointwise infinite-dimensional Wasserstein-
2
chaos,
whereas estimate (
1.6
) implies pointwise Wasserstein-
2
empirical chaos.

Contributions of this work

The aim of this note is to revisit propagation of chaos for the interacting particle system (
1.2
).
For simplicity, we consider neither the modification proposed in
[
25
,
15
]
nor gradient-free approximations,
but note that the approach we present generalizes to the ALDI sampler from
[
15
]
in a straightforward manner.
Our contributions are the following:

•

We generalize the work of
[
10
]
by relaxing the assumption that
\phi
is quadratic.
The assumptions made on
\phi
in our main results are similar to those in
[
15
]
.

•

Whereas (
1.5
), in the terminology of
[
7
]
,
may be viewed as a
pointwise
estimate for the Wasserstein-2 norm,
the result we prove is a general
pathwise
estimate for the Wasserstein-
p
norm.

•

We
deploy
an approach based on appropriate stopping times,
which
originates from numerical analysis
[
18
]
but is novel in the context of mean field limits and may prove useful for the analysis of other interacting particle systems.
This leads to an estimate which
is
optimal,
in the sense that (
1.5
) holds with
\varepsilon=0
.

•

Finally, we prove a novel well-posedness result for the mean field dynamics (
1.4
),
under a local Lipschitz continuity assumption on
\nabla\phi
.

Well-posedness results for the interacting particle system and its formal mean field limit are presented in

.
We then present auxiliary results in
Section

3
,
and the main results in
Section

4
.
The appendices contain the proofs of well-posedness results (

Notation

We let
\mathbf{N}:=\{0,1,2,3,\dots\}

and
\mathbf{N}^{+}:=\{1,2,3,\dots\}
.
For a matrix
X\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times d}
,
the notation
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\rm F}
refers to the Frobenius norm.
The set of probability measures over

is denoted by

,
and the notation

refers to the set of probability measures with finite moments up to order

.
For a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

the following notation is used to denote the mean and covariance under

:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}x\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x),\qquad\mathcal{C}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigl(x-\mathcal{M}(\mu)\bigr)\otimes\bigl(x-\mathcal{M}(\mu)\bigr)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

(1.7)

The notation
W_{p}(\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet},\mathord{\color[rgb]{0.67,0.67,0.67}\bullet})
denotes the
p
-Wasserstein distance, see
[
29
, Chapter 6]
.
For a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\real^{d})
and a function
f\colon\real^{d}\to\mathbf{R}
,
we use the short-hand notation

\mu[f]=\int_{E}f(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

By a slight abuse of notation,
we sometimes write
\mu[f(x)]
instead of
\mu[f]
for convenience.
For example, for a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
,
the notation
\mu\left[x^{2}\right]
refers to the second raw moment of
\mu
.
Throughout this note,
the notation
\Omega
refers to the sample space,
and
C
refers to a constant whose exact value is irrelevant in the context and may change from occurrence to occurrence.
Furthermore, in expressions such as
\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert f(X)\bigr\rvert^{p}
,
it is always assumed that the exponent is inside the expectation;
otherwise we write
\left(\mathbf{E}\bigl[f(X)\bigr]\right)^{p}
.
Finally, for convenience,
we let

, so that
|x|_{*}^{a}\leqslant|x|_{*}^{b}
for all

and all

.

Assumption and well-posedness

Throughout this note,
we denote by
\mathcal{A}(\ell)
for
\ell\geqslant 0
the set of negative log-densities
\phi
that satisfy the following assumptions,
with that value of
\ell
.

Assumption H
.

Without loss of generality,
we assume that the function
\phi\colon\real^{d}\to\real
is bounded from below by 1.
We assume furthermore that
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\real^{d})
and
that there are positive constants
c_{\ell},c_{u}
and a compact set
K
such that
the following inequalities are satisfied for all
x\in\real^{d}\setminus K
:

\displaystyle c_{\ell}|x|^{\ell+2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\phi(x)\leqslant c_{u}|x|^{\ell+2},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle c_{\ell}|x|^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lvert\nabla\phi(x)\rvert\leqslant c_{u}|x|^{\ell+1},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle c_{\ell}|x|^{\ell}\I_{d}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\operatorname{D}^{2}\phi(x)\preccurlyeq c_{u}\I_{d}|x|^{\ell}.

(2.1c)

Remark 1
.

A few comments are in order.

•

H
implies that there are constants
\widetilde{c}_{\ell},\widetilde{c}_{u}

such that

\forall x\in\real^{d},\qquad\begin{aligned} \widetilde{c}_{\ell}|x|_{*}^{\ell+2}\leqslant\phi(x)&\leqslant\widetilde{c}_{u}|x|_{*}^{\ell+2},\qquad\bigl\lvert\nabla\phi(x)\bigr\rvert&\leqslant\widetilde{c}_{u}|x|_{*}^{\ell+1},\qquad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\operatorname{D}^{2}\phi(x)\preccurlyeq\widetilde{c}_{u}\I_{d}|x|_{*}^{\ell}.}\end{aligned}

(2.2)

Note that these inequalities are valid for all
x\in\real^{d}
, without a compact set excluded.
We shall henceforth assume that these inequalities are satisfied with the same constants as in
H
.

•

The last bound in (
2.2
),
together with the assumption that
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\real^{d})
,
implies that
\nabla\phi
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Specifically, it holds for some

on the straight segment joining

and

that

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},\qquad\bigl\lvert\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(y)\bigr\rvert\leqslant\left\lVert\operatorname{D}^{2}\phi(z)\right\rVert\bigl\lvert x-y\bigr\rvert\leqslant\widetilde{c}_{u}\left(1+|x|^{\ell}+|y|^{\ell}\right)|x-y|.

(2.3)

•

When the target distribution (
1.1
) is the Bayesian posterior associated to an inverse problem with a linear forward model, Gaussian noise and a Gaussian prior,
the function
\phi
is quadratic.
In this case
H
is indeed satisfied with
\ell=0
.

•

The assumption that
\phi\geqslant 1
is made for convenience,
but it is not required.
This assumption is particularly useful for the proofs in

where it enables to use short Lyapunov functions of the form

Lemma 1

(Bound on the probability of large excursions)

Let

\forall J\in\mathbf{N}^{+},\qquad\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}Z_{j}\geqslant R\right]\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

Proof.

This follows from a generalization of Chebychev’s inequality
[
24

Let

By the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund

\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert X-\mathbf{E}[X]\bigr\rvert^{r}\leqslant C_{\rm MZ}(r)J^{-\frac{r}{2}}\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert Z_{1}-\mathbf{E}[Z_{1}]\bigr\rvert^{r}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[X\geqslant R\right]\leqslant\mathbf{P}\Bigl[\bigl\lvert X-\mathbf{E}[X]\bigr\rvert^{r}\geqslant\bigl(R-\mathbf{E}[X]\bigr)^{r}\Bigr]

which concludes the proof.

4.1

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that

p\in[2,\frac{q}{3}]

\forall J\in\mathbf{N}^{+},\qquad\forall j\in,\qquad\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\Big\rvert^{p}\right]\leqslant CJ^{-\min\left\{\frac{p}{2},\frac{q-p}{2\sqrt{q}},\frac{q-p}{2p},\frac{q-p}{6}\right\}}.

Proof.

Fix

Fix also

\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{r}>\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{r}\right].

2

Consider the stopping times

\displaystyle\tau_{J}(R)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{t\geqslant 0:\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{r}\geqslant R^{r}\right\}=\inf\Bigl\{t\geqslant 0:W_{r}(\mu^{J}_{t},\delta_{0})\geqslant R\Bigr\},

Remark 2
.

A few comments are in order.

•

If

4.2
Extension: sharp propagation of chaos for locally Lipschitz
\nabla\phi

In order to prove sharp propagation of chaos for this case,
we need the following additional auxiliary result.

Lemma 4

(Convexity inequality)
.

If
\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\ell)
for some
\ell\geqslant 0
,
then there are
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in\real^{d}\times\real^{d},\qquad\Bigl\langle y-x,\nabla\phi(y)-\nabla\phi(x)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{1}\left(1+\lvert x\rvert^{\ell}+\lvert y\rvert^{\ell}\right)\lvert y-x\rvert^{2}-c_{2}\lvert y-x\rvert^{2}.

Proof.

By the fundamental theorem,
it holds that

\nabla\phi(y)-\nabla\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{D}^{2}\phi\bigl(x+t(y-x)\bigr)\,(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.17)

Assumption (
2.1c
) implies that
there is
c_{2}>0
such that

\forall x\in\real^{d},\qquad\operatorname{D}^{2}\phi(x)\succcurlyeq c_{\ell}|x|^{\ell}\I_{d}-c_{2}\I_{d}.

Therefore,
taking the inner product with
y-x
on both sides of (
4.17
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle y-x,\nabla\phi(y)-\nabla\phi(x)\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{\ell}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert x+t(y-x)\right\rvert^{\ell}\,\lvert y-x\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t-c_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\lvert y-x\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Suppose without loss of generality that
|y|\geqslant|x|
.
Then, using the triangle inequality,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert x+t(y-x)\right\rvert^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{1}\left\lvert ty+(1-t)x\right\rvert^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}t\geqslant\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{1}\Bigl(t|y|-(1-t)\lvert x\rvert\Bigr)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{1}\Bigl(2t-1\Bigr)|y|^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}t\geqslant C|y|^{\ell}\geqslant\frac{C}{2}\left(|x|^{\ell}+|y|^{\ell}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\left[W_{2}{\left(\mu^{J}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}},\overline{\mu}^{J}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}\right)}^{r}\right]\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\left\lvert X^{j}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}.

Note that,

X^{k}
.
On the other hand,
by the moment bounds in

and
2

it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\overline{\mu}^{J}_{s})}-\sqrt{\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})}\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{r}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left\lVert\cov(\overline{\mu}^{J}_{s})-\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{r}\left\lvert\nabla\phi(X^{j}_{s})\right\rvert^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lVert\cov(\overline{\mu}^{J}_{s})-\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\nabla\phi(X^{j}_{s})\right\rvert^{2r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{u\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{u\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{r}{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left\lvert X^{j}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Grönwall’s inequality,
this implies that

\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{u\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{u\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\right]\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{r}{2}}.

(4.33)

Step B. Bounding the second term in (
4.22
)

We have by
Propositions

2
and
1
that

\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\big\rvert^{q}\right]\leqslant 2^{q-1}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\lvert X^{j}_{t}\big\rvert^{q}\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\lvert\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\big\rvert^{q}\right]\right)\leqslant 2^{q-1}\bigl(\kappa(q)+\overline{\kappa}(q)\bigr).

In order to complete the proof of the theorem,
it remains to bound the probability
\mathbf{P}\left[\theta_{J}(R)\leqslant T\right]
.
By using the same strategy as in (
4.15
) in the proof of
Theorem

3
,
it is simple to show that
there is
C
such that

\mathbf{P}\left[\overline{\tau}_{J}\leqslant T\right]\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{q}{2r(\ell+1)}}.

On the other hand, by the Markov inequality and (
4.33
),
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\mathfrak{t}_{J}\leqslant T\leqslant\overline{\tau}_{J}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}W_{r}{\left(\mu^{J}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}},\overline{\mu}^{J}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}\right)}\geqslant\varepsilon\right]\leqslant\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\geqslant\varepsilon^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\geqslant\varepsilon^{r}\right]\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{r}}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}\right\rvert^{r}\right]\leqslant\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{r}}J^{-{\frac{r}{2}}}.

Given the definitions of

and

it follows that

\Bigl(\mathbf{P}[\theta_{J}\leqslant T]\Bigr)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\leqslant C\Bigl(J^{-\frac{q}{2r(\ell+1)}}+J^{-\frac{r}{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}=C\Bigl(J^{-p}+J^{-p}\Bigr)^{1-\frac{1}{4p(\ell+1)}}\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{p}{2}}.

The proof can then be concluded in exactly the same manner as the proof of
Theorem

3

Remark 3

(Non-quantitative propagation of chaos)
.

A natural alternative approach would have been to define stopping times
similar to those employed in the proof of
Theorem

3
,

\tau_{J}(r,R)=\inf\Bigl\{t\geqslant 0:W_{r(\ell+1)}(\mu^{J}_{t},\delta_{0})\geqslant R\Bigr\},\qquad\overline{\tau}_{J}(r,R)=\inf\Bigl\{t\geqslant 0:W_{r(\ell+1)}(\overline{\mu}^{J}_{t},\delta_{0})\geqslant R\Bigr\},

(4.34)

and to let
\theta_{J}(r,R)=\tau_{J}(r,R)\wedge\overline{\tau}_{J}(r,R)
.
This remark aims at explaining why,
although this approach does enable proving a non-quantitative propagation of chaos estimate,
it does not yield a
good
quantitative result.

With the definition (
4.34
) of the stopping times,
the compensation that we relied on to control the first term in (
4.30
) can no longer be applied.
This leads to the presence,
among the terms needing to be controlled,
of a term similar to (
4.32
) but containing
X^{k}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}
instead of
\overline{X}^{k}_{s\wedge\theta_{J}}
.
This is not a problem,
as this term can be handled by definition of
\theta_{J}
in terms of the stopping times in (
4.34
).
Consequently,
the estimate (
4.33
) still holds,
with a constant
C
depending on
(r,R)
.
The issue with this approach is to obtain a sufficiently good bound on
\mathbf{P}[\tau_{J}(R)\leqslant T]
in the last part of the proof.
Indeed, while it is still possible to show,
similarly to (
4.14
),
that

\mathbf{P}[\tau_{J}\leqslant T]\leqslant\frac{2^{\beta}}{R^{\beta}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Bigl\lvert X^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t\wedge\theta_{J}}\Bigr\rvert^{\beta}\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Bigl\lvert\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\Bigr\rvert^{\beta}\geqslant\frac{R^{\beta}}{2^{\beta}}\right],\qquad\beta=r(\ell+1),

the first term on the right-hand side of this inequality cannot be controlled from the estimate (
4.33
) on the stopped particle system,
unless
\beta=r
, which is the globally Lipschitz setting.
A crude alternative manner of bounding
\mathbf{P}[\tau_{J}\leqslant T]
is to use the Markov inequality,
which together with the moment bound in
Proposition

1
gives that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{J}(r,R)\leqslant T\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{\beta}\geqslant R^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{P}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{\beta}\geqslant R^{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{R^{\beta}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{j}_{t}\right\rvert^{\beta}\right]=\frac{1}{R^{\beta}}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert X^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{\beta}\right]\leqslant\frac{\kappa(\beta)}{R^{\beta}}.

A non-quantitative version of propagation can then be derived as follows.
Take
\varepsilon>0
and
fix
R>0
so that the second term in (
4.22
),
with the stopping times given in (
4.34
),
is bounded from above by
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
This is possible in view of the crude bound on
\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{J}(r,R)\leqslant T\right]
above,
together with a similar bound for
\mathbf{P}\left[\overline{\tau}_{J}(r,R)\leqslant T\right]
.
Then fix
J
sufficiently large such that the first term in (
4.22
) is also bounded from above by
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
,
which is possible by (
4.33
).
Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary,
it follows that

\lim_{J\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\lvert X^{j}_{t}-\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\Big\rvert^{r}\right]=0,

which is indeed a non-quantitative propagation of chaos estimate.
Note that this approach of obtaining a non-quantitative estimate
parallels that in
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
.

To conclude this section,
we mention the following corollary of

and
4
,
which is similar to
[

and useful in the context of sampling.
It states that, for the purpose of calculating the average of an observable

with respect to
\overline{\rho}_{t}
,
the interacting particle system at time

is as good,
in terms of convergence rate of

,
as an estimator constructed from

i.i.d. samples from

.

Corollary 5

(
L^{p}
bound on the sampling error)
.

Suppose that
\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\ell)
for some
\ell>0
,
that
\overline{\rho}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\real^{d})
for all
q>0
and that
\cov(\overline{\rho}_{0})\succ 0
.
Assume additionally that
f\colon\real^{d}\to\real
satisfies the following local Lipschitz continuity assumption:

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},\qquad\bigl\lvert f(x)-f(y)\bigr\rvert\leqslant L\Bigl(1+|x|^{s}+|y|^{s}\Bigr)|x-y|.

(4.35)

Consider the systems (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) with the coefficients given in (
2.5
).
Then for any
p\geqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}
,
there is
C>0
depending on
(p,L,s)
and a finite number of moments of
\overline{\rho}_{0}
such that

\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}f(X^{j}_{t})-\overline{\rho}_{t}[f]\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,
it holds that

\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}f(X^{j}_{t})-\overline{\rho}_{t}[f]\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl(f\bigl(X^{j}_{t}\bigr)-f\bigl(\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\bigr)\Bigr)\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}f(\overline{X}^{j}_{t})-\overline{\rho}_{t}[f]\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.36)

By Jensen’s inequality,
exchangeability,
the local Lipschitz continuity of
f
,
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
the
moment bounds (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)
,
and
Theorem

4
,
the first term satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl(f\bigl(X^{j}_{t}\bigr)-f\bigl(\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\bigr)\Bigr)\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Bigl\lvert f\bigl(X^{j}_{t}\bigr)-f\bigl(\overline{X}^{j}_{t}\bigr)\Bigr\rvert^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert f\bigl(X^{1}_{t}\bigr)-f\bigl(\overline{X}^{1}_{t}\bigr)\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\left(3^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2p-1}}\mathbf{E}\left[1+\left\lvert X^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2ps}+\bigl\lvert\overline{X}^{1}_{t}\bigr\rvert^{2ps}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert X^{1}_{t}-\overline{X}^{1}_{t}\right\rvert^{2p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality and the moment bound in
Proposition

2
,
the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.36

which concludes the proof.
∎

\bigl\lvert\nabla f(x)\bigr\rvert\leqslant C|x|_{*}^{q(\ell+2)-1}
.
Thus, writing (
A.6
) in integral form,
then taking the supremum and using the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality,
we obtain for all

that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigl\lvert f(Y_{s})\bigr\rvert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert f(Y_{0})\rvert^{2}+T\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl\lVert\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{s})\Bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\mathbf{E}\Bigl\lVert\operatorname{D}^{2}f(Y_{s})\Bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+2C_{\rm BDG}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\nabla f(Y_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{R}\bigl(\Gamma(s)\bigr)\nabla f(Y_{s})\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\lvert Y_{0}\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}+C\left(\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{C}([0,T],\mathcal{X})}+1\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\lvert Y_{s}\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)-4}+\mathbf{E}\lvert Y_{s}\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)-2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using the lower bound in (
2.1a
) and rearranging,
we finally obtain

\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigl\lvert Y_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s}}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right]\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\lvert Y_{0}\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}+C\left(\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{C}([0,T],\mathcal{X})}+1\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{u\in[0,s]}\lvert Y_{u}\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s.

From Grönwall’s inequality,
it follows that

\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}T}]}\bigl\lvert Y_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s}}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right]\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C\mathbf{E}\left\lvert Y_{0}\right\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}}

(A.7)

for some
constant
C
depending on
\left\lVert\Gamma\right\rVert_{\mathrm{C}([0,T],\mathcal{X})}
and
T
,
among other parameters
.
In particular, since
\overline{\rho}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell+2}(\real^{d})
,
this inequality with
q=\frac{1}{\ell+2}
together with dominated convergence imply that
the functions
t\mapsto\mathbf{E}[Y_{t}]
and
t\mapsto\mathbf{E}[Y_{t}\otimes Y_{t}]
are continuous.
Furthermore, using (
A.7
) with
q=\frac{1}{2}

we deduce that these functions are also differentiable on
[0,T]
because,
by Itô’s lemma and dominated convergence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\Bigl(\mathbf{E}[Y_{t+h}]-\mathbf{E}[Y_{t}]\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{h}\mathbf{E}\left[\int_{t}^{t+h}\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{u})\nabla\phi(Y_{u})\,\mathrm{d}u\right]\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}-\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{t})\nabla\phi(Y_{t})\Bigr].

(A.8)

Similarly, applying Itô’s formula in the same manner as in (
A.14
) below
,
we obtain

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\Bigl(\mathbf{E}[Y_{t+h}\otimes Y_{t+h}]-\mathbf{E}[Y_{t}\otimes Y_{t}]\Bigr)=-\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{t})\nabla\phi(Y_{t})\bigr)\otimes Y_{t}+Y_{t}\otimes\bigl(\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{t})\nabla\phi(Y_{t})\bigr)\Bigr]+2\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{t}).

(A.9)

Another application of dominated convergence yields continuity of the derivatives.
Finally,
we bound the right-hand side of (
A.8
) as follows:

\displaystyle\Bigl\lvert\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{R}(\Gamma_{t})\nabla\phi(Y_{t})\right]\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lVert\Gamma_{t}\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}\bigl\lvert\nabla\phi(Y_{t})\bigr\rvert\Bigr]\leqslant c_{u}\bigl\lVert\Gamma\bigr\rVert_{\mathrm{C}([0,T],\mathcal{X})}\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert Y_{t}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{\ell+1}.

Employing a similar reasoning for the right-hand side of (
A.9
),
and using (
A.7
),
we finally obtain (
A.5
).
∎

Proof.

The statements (

) and (

) are obvious if

.
The proof for a fixed value of

is the same for all values of

with constants that can easily be fixed independently of

so for simplicity we assume from now on that

Proof of the bound (

)

The proof of this bound is similar to the proof of (

) in

.
Let
f(x)=\phi(x)^{q}

for

.
By Itô’s formula, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f(\overline{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=-\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})^{\mathsf{T}}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla f(\overline{X}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t}):\operatorname{D}^{2}f(\overline{X}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\nabla f(\overline{X}_{t})^{\mathsf{T}}\sqrt{2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}

Writing this equation in integral form,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigl\lvert f(\overline{X}_{t})\bigr\rvert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert f(\overline{X}_{0})\bigr\rvert^{2}+T\int_{0}^{t}\bigl\lVert\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\mathbf{E}\bigl\lVert\operatorname{D}^{2}f(\overline{X}_{s})\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

Using the inequality

A.1
),
and then Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigl\lVert\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\mathbf{E}\bigl\lVert\operatorname{D}^{2}{f(\overline{X}_{s})}\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert^{2}\right)^{2}\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)-4}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert^{2q(\ell+2)}\right)^{\frac{4}{2q(\ell+2)}}\left(\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right)^{\frac{2q(\ell+2)-4}{2q(\ell+2)}}=C\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}.

By a similar reasoning,

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\nabla f(\overline{X}_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\nabla f(\overline{X}_{s})\right\rvert\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}.

Therefore, using the lower bound in (
2.1a
),

\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\lvert\overline{X}_{s}\right\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right]\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\overline{X}_{0}\right\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left[\sup_{u\in[0,s]}\bigl\lvert\overline{X}_{u}\bigr\rvert_{*}^{2q(\ell+2)}\right]\mathrm{d}s.

).

Proof of (
A.12
)

Let
g(x)=x\otimes x
. By Itô’s formula, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}g(\overline{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=-\bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\bigr)\otimes\overline{X}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\overline{X}_{t}\otimes\bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t+2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\overline{X}_{t}\otimes\bigl(\sqrt{2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\bigr)+\bigl(\sqrt{2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}\bigr)\otimes\overline{X}_{t}\,.

(A.14)

Therefore, taking expectations, we deduce that

\mathbf{E}g(\overline{X}_{t})-\mathbf{E}g(\overline{X}_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})-\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{s})\bigr)\otimes\overline{X}_{s}+\overline{X}_{s}\otimes\bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{s})\bigr)\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

On the other hand

\mathbf{E}[\overline{X}_{t}]-\mathbf{E}[\overline{X}_{0}]=-\int_{0}^{t}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{s})\mathbf{E}\left[\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{s})\right]\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

Combining these equations,
and noting that
t\mapsto\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})
is differentiable by
Lemma

5
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})

\displaystyle=2\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})-\mathbf{E}\left[\Bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\overline{X}_{t}-\mathcal{M}(\overline{\rho}_{t})\Bigr)\right]-\mathbf{E}\left[\Bigl(\overline{X}_{t}-\mathcal{M}(\overline{\rho}_{t})\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\Bigr)\right].

Inspired by
[
15
, Lemma A.1]
,
we define the Lyapunov function

\mathcal{V}_{\cov}(\mu)=-\log\det\bigl(\cov(\mu)\bigr).

Using Jacobi’s formula for the determinant,
we deduce that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{V}_{\cov}(\overline{\rho}_{t})=\trace\left(\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\cov(\overline{\rho}_{t})\right)=\trace\Bigl({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}\I_{d}-2\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{t})\otimes\bigl(\overline{X}_{t}-\mean(\overline{\rho}_{t})\bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr).

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\cov}(\overline{\rho}_{t})-\mathcal{V}_{\cov}(\overline{\rho}_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}td+2\sqrt{d}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\left\lVert\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{s})\otimes\bigl(\overline{X}_{s}-\mean(\overline{\rho}_{s})\bigr)\right\rVert_{\rm F}\Bigr]

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}td+2\sqrt{d}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\left\lvert\nabla\phi(\overline{X}_{s})\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\overline{X}_{s}-\mean(\overline{\rho}_{s})\right\rvert\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s,

where we used that
\trace A\leqslant\sqrt{d}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\rm F}
for any matrix
A
.

The integrand can be bounded from (
A.11
) with
p=4\vee(\ell+2)
,
which concludes the proof since for any symmetric positive definite matrix

it holds that

\det(A)\leqslant\lambda_{\min}(A)\lambda_{\max}(A)^{d-1}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\lambda_{\min}(A)\geqslant\frac{\det A}{\lVert A\rVert_{\rm F}^{d-1}},

where
\lambda_{\min}(A)
and
\lambda_{\max}(A)
are the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of

.

Consider the map

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto\Bigl(t\mapsto\cov(\rho_{t})\Bigr),

) with matrix

By

Fixed points of

in the space

,

To this end,

,

By (

This establishes the existence of a fixed point of

.
Furthermore,

whereas (

) in

We first present a simple proof of uniqueness for the case

,
and postpone the general proof to

Let

By definition of

For the third term,

\sqrt{\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}}\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}}&\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{s}^{-1}&-\Gamma_{s}^{-1}\\
-\Gamma_{s}^{-1}&\Gamma_{s}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\trace\left(\Bigl(\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}}-\sqrt{\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}}-\sqrt{\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}}\Bigr)\Gamma_{s}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}^{-1}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}}-\sqrt{\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}}\Bigr)\right\rVert_{\rm F}^{2}\leqslant C\mathbf{E}\left\lvert Y_{t}-\widehat{Y}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}.

where we used the estimate (
A.12
) in
Lemma

6
and
the Wasserstein stability estimate (
3.1b
) from
Lemma

2
in the last inequality.

•

Finally,
it immediately follows from the bounds on
\Gamma_{s}=\cov(\rho_{s})
and
\widehat{\Gamma}_{s}=\cov(\widehat{\rho}_{s})
given
Lemma

6
,
as well as the upper bound (
A.5
) on the time derivative of
\Gamma_{s}
,
that

\left\lvert\frac{1}{2}\bigl(Y_{s}-\widehat{Y}_{s}\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\Gamma_{s}^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Gamma_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Gamma_{s}^{-1}\bigl(Y_{s}-\widehat{Y}_{s}\bigr)^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rvert\leqslant C\left\lvert Y_{s}-\widehat{Y}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}.

Combining all the bounds,
we deduce that

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert Y_{t}-\widehat{Y}_{t}\right\rvert^{2}\leqslant C\mathbf{E}f(Y_{t},\widehat{Y}_{t},t)\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}\left\lvert Y_{s}-\widehat{Y}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Grönwall’s inequality then gives that the left-hand side is 0 for all times,
which concludes the proof.

Appendix B
Proof of auxiliary results

B.1
Proof of
Lemma

2

We prove the statements separately,
using exactly the same approach as in
[
16
]
.

Proof of (
3.1a
)

By the triangle inequality,
it holds that

\bigl\lVert\cov(\mu)-\cov(\nu)\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\bigl\lVert\mu[x\otimes x]-\nu[x\otimes x]\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}+\bigl\lVert\mean(\mu)\otimes\mean(\mu)-\mean(\nu)\otimes\mean(\nu)\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}.

(B.1)

Let
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denote an arbitrary coupling between
\mu
and
\nu
.
By Jensen’s inequality, it holds that

\bigl\lVert\mu[x\otimes x]-\nu[x\otimes x]\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lVert x\otimes x-y\otimes y\rVert_{\rm F}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y).

Recall that for all
(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lVert x\otimes x-y\otimes y\right\rVert_{\rm F}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert x(x-y)^{\mathsf{T}}+(x-y)y^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert x(x-y)^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}+\left\lVert(x-y)y^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert_{\rm F}=\lvert x\rvert\cdot\lvert x-y\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\cdot\lvert x-y\rvert=\bigl(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\bigr)\lvert x-y\rvert.

(B.2)

Therefore, we deduce that

\bigl\lVert\mu[x\otimes x]-\nu[x\otimes x]\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\sqrt{\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigl(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\bigr)^{2}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)}\sqrt{\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)}.

Besides,
by the triangle inequality,
it holds that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\bigl(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert\bigr)^{2}\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y)}\leqslant\sqrt{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert x\rvert^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)}+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert y\rvert^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}y)}=W_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})+W_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0}).

Infimizing over all couplings
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
,
we conclude that

\bigl\lVert\mu[x\otimes x]-\nu[x\otimes x]\bigr\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\Bigl(W_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})+W_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0})\Bigr)W_{2}(\mu,\nu).

Applying a similar reasoning to the second term on the right-hand side of (
B.1
),
we deduce (
3.1a
).

Proof of (
3.1b
)

It is sufficient to check the claim for measures
\mu
and
\nu
of the form

\mu^{J}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{X^{j}},\qquad\nu^{J}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\delta_{Y^{j}},\qquad J\in\mathbf{N}^{+}.

(B.3)

Indeed,
assume that the statement holds for all such pairs of probability measures,
and take
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})
.
By
[
29
, Theorem 6.18]
,
there exists a sequence
\bigl\{(\mu^{J},\nu^{J})\bigr\}_{J\in\mathbf{N}^{+}}
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})

such that
W_{2}(\mu^{J},\mu)\to 0
and
W_{2}(\nu^{J},\nu)\to\penalty\ 0
in the limit as
J\to\infty
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu})-\sqrt{\cov(\nu)}\right\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu^{J})}-\sqrt{\cov(\nu^{J})}\right\rVert_{\rm F}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu)}-\sqrt{\cov(\mu^{J})}\right\rVert_{\rm F}+\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\nu)}-\sqrt{\cov(\nu^{J})}\right\rVert_{\rm F}.

The first term is bounded from above by
\sqrt{2}W_{2}(\mu^{J},\nu^{J})
by the base case,
while the other two terms converge to 0 in the limit as
J\to\infty
by (
3.1a
) in
Lemma

2
.
Taking the limit
J\to\infty
, we deduce that

\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu)}-\sqrt{\cov(\nu)}\right\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\sqrt{2}W_{2}(\mu,\nu).

Proof of the statement for empirical measures

By
[
30
, p.5]
,
the Wasserstein distance between empirical measures
\mu^{J}
and
\nu^{J}
of the form (
B.3
)
is equal to

W_{2}(\mu^{J},\nu^{J})=\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{J}}\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}-Y^{\sigma(j)}\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{J}
denotes the set of permutations in
\{1,\dotsc,J\}
.
Thus, the claim will follow if we can prove that,
for any pair of probability measures
(\mu^{J},\nu^{J})\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\real^{d})
of the form (
B.3
),
it holds that

\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu^{J})}-\sqrt{\cov(\nu^{J})}\right\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\,\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}-Y^{j}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(B.4)

We henceforth drop the superscript
J
in
\mu^{J},\nu^{J}
for simplicity,
and write
\mathbf{X}=\left(X^{1},\dotsc,X^{J}\right)
and
\mathbf{Y}=\left(Y^{1},\dotsc,Y^{J}\right)
.
The proof of (
B.4
) presented below follows the lines of a proof shown to me by N. J. Gerber,
who proved this inequality in preliminary work with F. Hoffmann which eventually lead to the preprint
[
16
]
.
First note that

\cov(\mu)=M_{\mathbf{X}}M_{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathsf{T}},\qquad M_{\mathbf{X}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{J}}\begin{pmatrix}\left(X^{1}-\mean(\mu)\right)&\ldots&\left(X^{J}-\mean(\mu)\right)\end{pmatrix}.

Proceeding in the same manner,
we construct a matrix
M_{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathbf{R}^{d\times J}
such that
\mean(\nu)=M_{\mathbf{Y}}M_{\mathbf{Y}}^{\mathsf{T}}
.
A result by Araki and Yamagami
[
1
]
,
later generalized by Kittaneh
[
21
]
and Bhatia
[
3
]
,
states for any two matrices
A
and
B
with the same shape,
it holds that

\left\lVert\sqrt{A^{\mathsf{T}}A}-\sqrt{B^{\mathsf{T}}B}\right\rVert_{\rm F}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left\lVert A-B\right\rVert_{\rm F}.

See also
[
2
, Theorem VII.5.7]
for a textbook presentation.
This result,
applied with
A=M_{\bf X}
and
B=M_{\bf Y}
,
yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sqrt{\cov(\mu)}-\sqrt{\cov(\nu)}\right\rVert_{\rm F}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Big\lvert\left(X^{j}-\mean(\mu)\right)-\left(Y^{j}-\mean(\nu)\right)\Big\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\min_{a\in\real^{d}}\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Big\lvert X^{j}-Y^{j}-a\Big\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lvert X^{j}-Y^{j}\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which shows (
B.4
) and completes the proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we have that

For the second term,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Bigl\lVert\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})\otimes\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})-\mean(\mu)\otimes\mean(\mu)\Bigr\rVert_{\rm F}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\mathbf{E}\left[\bigl(\left\lvert\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})\right\rvert+\lvert\mean(\mu)\rvert\bigr)^{p}\lvert\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})-\mean(\mu)\rvert^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant CJ^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\bigl(\left\lvert\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})\right\rvert+\lvert\mean(\mu)\rvert\bigr)^{2p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used again the Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality.
The claim follows since
\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\left\lvert\mean(\overline{\mu}^{J})\right\rvert^{2p}\Bigr]\leqslant\mathbf{E}\Bigl[\bigl\lvert\overline{X}^{j}\bigr\rvert^{2p}\Bigr]
by Jensen’s inequality.

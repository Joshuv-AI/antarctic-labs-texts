Abstract
. The notion of p p -summing Bloch mapping from the complex unit open disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} into a complex Banach space X X is introduced for any 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty . It is shown that the linear space of such mappings, equipped with a natural seminorm π p ℬ \pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p} , is Möbius-invariant. Moreover, its subspace consisting of all those mappings which preserve the zero is an injective Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings. Bloch versions of the Pietsch’s domination/factorization Theorem and the Maurey’s extrapolation Theorem are presented. We also introduce the spaces of X X -valued Bloch molecules on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and identify the spaces of normalized p p -summing Bloch mappings from 𝔻 \mathbb{D} into X ∗ X^{*} under the norm π p ℬ \pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p} with the duals of such spaces of molecules under the Bloch version of the p ∗ p^{*} -Chevet–Saphar tensor norms d p ∗ d_{p^{*}} .

Introduction

The known concept of absolutely
p
-summing operator between Banach spaces, introduced by Grothendieck
[
10
]
for
p=1
and by Pietsch
[
18
]
for any
p>0
, can be adapted to address the property of summability in the setting of Bloch mappings from the complex unit open disc
\mathbb{D}
into a complex Banach space
X
as follows.

The study of summability has been addressed for different classes of mappings by some authors. For example, for multilinear operators by Achour and Mezrag
[
1
]
and Dimant
[
8
]
, for Lipschitz mappings by Farmer and Johnson
[
9
]
and Saadi
[
20
]
, and for holomorphic mappings by Matos
[
12
]
and Pellegrino
[
15
]
, among other settings. See also the survey by Pellegrino, Rueda and Sánchez-Pérez
[
16
]
for the summability on multilinear operators and homogeneous polynomials.

If
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
denotes the space of all holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
, let us recall that a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is called
Bloch
if there exists a constant
c\geq 0
such that
(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq c
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

The
Bloch space

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the linear space of all those mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
such that

p_{\mathcal{B}}(f):=\sup\left\{(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}<\infty,

equipped with the
Bloch seminorm

p_{\mathcal{B}}
. The
normalized Bloch space

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the Banach space of all Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
such that
f(0)=0
, equipped with the
Bloch norm

p_{\mathcal{B}}
. In particular, we will write
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
instead of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
. We refer the reader to
[
2
,
21
]
for the scalar-valued theory, and to
[
4
,
5
]
for the vector-valued theory on these spaces.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we say that a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is
p
-summing Bloch
if there is a constant
c\geq 0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad

if

\displaystyle 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\quad

if

\displaystyle p=\infty.

The infimum of all the constants
c
for which such an inequality holds, denoted
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
, defines a seminorm on the linear space, denoted
\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
, of all
p
-summing Bloch mappings
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
. Furthermore, this seminorm becomes a norm on the subspace
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
consisting of all those mappings
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
so that
f(0)=0
.

These spaces enjoy nice properties in both complex and functional analytical frameworks. In the former setting, we show that the space
(\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X),\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p})
is invariant by Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{D}
. In the latter context and in a clear parallelism with the theory of absolutely
p
-summing linear operators (see
[
7
, Chapter 2]
), we prove that
[\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p},\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}]
is an injective Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings whose elements can be characterized by means of Pietsch domination/factorization. Applying this Pietsch domination, we present a Bloch version of Maurey’s extrapolation Theorem
[
13
]
.

On the other hand, the known duality of the Bloch spaces (see
[
2
,
4
,
21
]
) is extended to the spaces
(\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p})
by identifying them with the duals of the spaces of the so-called
X
-valued Bloch molecules on
\mathbb{D}
, equipped with the Bloch versions of the
p^{*}
-Chevet–Saphar tensor norms
d_{p^{*}}
. We conclude the paper with some open problems.

The proofs of some of our results are similar to those of their corresponding linear versions, but a detailed reading of them shows that the adaptation of the linear techniques to the Bloch setting is far from being simple. Our approach depends mainly on the application of some concepts and results concerning the theory on a strongly unique predual of the space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, called
Bloch-free Banach space over
\mathbb{D}
that was introduced in
[
11
]
.

Notation.
For two normed spaces
X
and
Y
,
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
denotes the normed space of all bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
, equipped with the operator canonical norm. In particular, the topological dual space
\mathcal{L}(X,\mathbb{C})
is denoted by
X^{*}
. For
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we will sometimes write
\langle x^{*},x\rangle=x^{*}(x)
. As usual,
B_{X}
and
S_{X}
stand for the closed unit ball of
X
and the unit sphere of
X
, respectively.
Let
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit sphere and the unit open disc of
\mathbb{C}
, respectively.

Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
p^{*}
denote the
conjugate index of
p
defined by

p^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}\infty&\text{if}&p=1,\\
p/(p-1)&\text{if}&1<p<\infty,\\
1&\text{if}&p=\infty.\end{array}\right.

1.
p
-Summing Bloch mappings on the unit disc

This section gathers the most important properties of
p
-summing Bloch mappings on
\mathbb{D}
. From now on, unless otherwise stated,
X
will denote a complex Banach space.

1.1.
Inclusions

We will first establish some useful inclusion relations. See first
[
18
, Satz 5]
.

The following class of Bloch functions will be used throughout the paper. For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function
f_{z}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

f_{z}(w)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})w}{1-\overline{z}w}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}),

belongs to
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})=1=(1-|z|^{2})f_{z}^{\prime}(z)
(see
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
).

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
. Then
\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{q}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
for all
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Moreover,
\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
for all
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. We will first prove the second assertion. Let
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
. For all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f),

hence
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f)
. Conversely, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\leq\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|,

this implies that

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right),

and thus
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

To prove the first assertion, let
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Assume
q<\infty
. Taking
\beta_{i}=\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q/p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{(q/p)-1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\beta_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\beta_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
q/p>1
and
(q/p)^{*}=q/(q-p)
, Hölder Inequality yields

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\beta_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\right)^{q-p}\left(\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and thus we obtain

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{q}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This shows that
f\in\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{q}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
if
q<\infty
. For the case
q=\infty
, note that

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and thus
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)=\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(f)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
.
∎

1.2.
Injective Banach ideal property

Let us recall (see
[
11
, Definition 5.11]
) that a
normalized Bloch ideal
is a subclass
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}
of the class of all normalized Bloch mappings
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
such that for every complex Banach space
X
, the components

\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X):=\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X),

satisfy the following properties:

(I1)

\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is a linear subspace of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(I2)

For every
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
, the mapping
g\cdot x\colon z\mapsto g(z)x
from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(I3)

The
ideal property
: if
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
where
Y
is a complex Banach space, then
T\circ f\circ h
belongs to
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
.

A normalized Bloch ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}
is said to be
normed (Banach)
if there is a function
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
such that for every complex Banach space
X
, the following three conditions are satisfied:

(N1)

(\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}})
is a normed (Banach) space with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(N2)

\|g\cdot x\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
for all
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
,

(N3)

If
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
where
Y
is a complex Banach space, then
\|T\circ f\circ h\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}\leq\|T\|\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
.

A normed normalized Bloch ideal
[\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}]
is said to be:

(I)

Injective
if for any mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, any complex Banach space
Y
and any isometric linear embedding
\iota\colon X\to Y
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}=\left\|\iota\circ f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
whenever
\iota\circ f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
.

We are now ready to establish the following result which can be compared to
[
18
, Satzs 1–4]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

[\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p},\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}]
is an injective Banach normalized Bloch ideal for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
for all
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
by Proposition
1.1
.

We will only prove the case
1<p<\infty
. The cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
follow similarly. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
.

(N1) If
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)=0
, then
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=0
, and so
f=0
. Given
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(f_{1}+f_{2})^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left(\left\|f^{\prime}_{1}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}+\left\|f^{\prime}_{2}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f_{1}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f_{2}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{1})+\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{2})\right)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and therefore
f_{1}+f_{2}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{1}+f_{2})\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{1})+\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{2})
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
. We have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(\lambda f)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left|\lambda\right|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\lambda\right|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and thus
\lambda f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda f)\leq|\lambda|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
. This implies that
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda f)=0=\left|\lambda\right|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
if
\lambda=0
. For
\lambda\neq 0
, we have
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda^{-1}(\lambda f))\leq\left|\lambda\right|^{-1}\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda f)
, hence
\left|\lambda\right|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda f)
, and so
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\lambda f)=\left|\lambda\right|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
. Thus we have proved that
\left(\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X),\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}\right)
is a normed space.

To show that it is a Banach space, it is enough to see that every absolutely convergent series is convergent. So let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
such that
\sum\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{n})
converges. Since
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{n})\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\left(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X),p_{\mathcal{B}}\right)
is a Banach space, then
\sum f_{n}
converges in
\left(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X),p_{\mathcal{B}}\right)
to a function
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{D}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{p}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}f^{\prime}_{i}(z_{k})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\right)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{i})\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and by taking limits with
n\to\infty
yields

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{p}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f^{\prime}_{i}(z_{k})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{i})\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{n})
. Moreover, we have

\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}\left(f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\right)=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}\left(\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}f_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{i})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and thus
f
is the
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}
-limit of the series
\sum f_{n}
.

(N2) Let
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
. Note that
g\cdot x\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
by
[
11
, Proposition 5.13]
. If
g=0
, there is nothing to prove. Assume
g\neq 0
. We have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(g\cdot x)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|x\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|\left(\frac{g}{p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)}\right)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\sup_{h\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and thus
g\cdot x\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(g\cdot x)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
. Conversely, we have

p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(g\cdot x).

(N3) Let
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
,
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
where
Y
is a complex Banach space. Note that
T\circ f\circ h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)

by
[
11
, Proposition 5.13]
. We have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(T\circ f\circ h)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|T(f^{\prime}(h(z_{i}))h^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(h(z_{i}))\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(h(z_{i}))\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|(g\circ h)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|k^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we have used that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(g\circ h)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)
by
[
11
, Proposition 3.6]
. Therefore
T\circ f\circ h\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(T\circ f\circ h)\leq\left\|T\right\|\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
.

(I) Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and let
\iota\colon X\to Y
be a linear (not necessarily surjective) isometry. Assume that
\iota\circ f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},Y)
. We have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|\iota(f^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(\iota\circ f)^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\iota\circ f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and thus
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\iota\circ f)
. The reverse inequality follows from (N3).
∎

1.3.
Möbius invariance

The
Möbius group of
\mathbb{D}
, denoted
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, is formed by all biholomorphic bijections
\phi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
. Each
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
has the form
\phi=\lambda\phi_{a}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, where

\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Given a complex Banach space
X
, let us recall (see
[
3
]
) that a linear space
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
of holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
, endowed with a seminorm
p_{\mathcal{A}}
, is
Möbius-invariant
if it holds:

(i)

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
and there exists
c>0
such that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq cp_{\mathcal{A}}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(ii)

f\circ\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{A}}(f\circ\phi)=p_{\mathcal{A}}(f)
for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

By Proposition
1.1
, each
p
-summing Bloch mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is Bloch with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\pi_{p}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Moreover, following the argument of the proof of (N3) in Proposition
1.2
, it is easy to prove that if
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is
p
-summing Bloch and
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, then
f\circ\phi
is
p
-summing with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f\circ\phi)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
, and using this fact we also deduce that
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}((f\circ\phi)\circ\phi^{-1})\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f\circ\phi)
. In this way we have proved the following.

Proposition 1.3
.

(\Pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X),\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p})
is a Möbius-invariant space for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
\hfill\qed

1.4.
Pietsch domination

We establish a version for
p
-summing Bloch mappings on
\mathbb{D}
of the known Pietsch domination Theorem for
p
-summing linear operators between Banach spaces
[
18
, Theorem 2]
.

Let us recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is a dual Banach space (see
[
2
]
) and therefore we can consider this space equipped with its weak* topology. Let
\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
denote the set of all Borel regular probability measures
\mu
on
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f
is
p
-summing Bloch.

(ii)

(Pietsch domination). There is a constant
c\geq 0
and a Borel regular probability measure
\mu
on
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
such that

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq c\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

In this case,
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
is the infimum of all constants
c\geq 0
satisfying the preceding inequality, and this infimum is attained.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: We will apply an unified abstract version of Piestch domination Theorem (see
[
6
,
17
]
). For it, consider the functions

S\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)\times\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{C}\to[0,\infty[,\qquad S(f,z,\lambda)=\left|\lambda\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|

and

R\colon B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\times\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{C}\to[0,\infty[,\qquad R(g,z,\lambda)=\left|\lambda\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|.

Note first that for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the function
R_{z,\lambda}\colon B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\to[0,\infty[
, given by

R_{z,\lambda}(g)=R(g,z,\lambda),

is continuous. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}S(f,z_{i},\lambda_{i})^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}R(g,z_{i},\lambda_{i})^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and therefore
f
is
R-S
-abstract
p
-summing. Hence, by applying
[
17
, Theorem 3.1]
, there is a constant
c\geq 0
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
such that

S(f,z,\lambda)\leq c\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}R(g,z,\lambda)^{p}\ d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and therefore

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq c\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, we have

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
by taking, for example,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1}=1
,
\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}=0
and
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{n}=z
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\leq c
.
∎

1.5.
Pietsch factorization

We now present the analogue for
p
-summing Bloch mappings of Pietsch factorization theorem for
p
-summing operators (see
[
18
, Theorem 3]
, also
[
7
, Theorem 2.13]
).

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
I_{\infty,p}\colon L_{\infty}(\mu)\to L_{p}(\mu)
and
j_{\infty}\colon C(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})\to L_{\infty}(\mu)
denote the formal inclusion operators. We will also use the mapping
\iota_{\mathbb{D}}\colon\mathbb{D}\to C(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
defined by

\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z)(g)=g^{\prime}(z)\quad\left(z\in\mathbb{D},\;g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\right),

and for a complex Banach space
X
, the isometric linear embedding
\iota_{X}\colon X\to\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}})
given by

\left\langle\iota_{X}(x),x^{*}\right\rangle=x^{*}(x)\quad(x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}},\;x\in X).

The following easy fact will be applied below.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
. Then there exists a mapping
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)=1
such that
h^{\prime}=j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}}
. In fact,
h\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
\pi_{p}^{\mathcal{B}}(h)=1
for any
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
(j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}})^{\prime}=j_{\infty}\circ(\iota_{\mathbb{D}})^{\prime}
, where
(\iota_{\mathbb{D}})^{\prime}(z)(g)=g^{\prime\prime}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
. By
[
11
, Lemma 2.9]
, there exists a mapping
h\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
h(0)=0
such that
h^{\prime}=j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}}
. In fact,
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)=1
since

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|h^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|j_{\infty}(\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z))\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\right\|_{\infty}=1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For the second assertion, given
1\leq p<\infty
, it suffices to note that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|j_{\infty}(\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{i}))\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{i})\right\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}}{(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
.
∎

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

f
is
p
-summing Bloch.

(ii)

(Pietsch factorization). There exist a regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu),\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
and a mapping
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
such that the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{L_{\infty}(\mu)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{I_{\infty,p}}
\textstyle{L_{p}(\mu)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T}
\textstyle{\mathbb{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{X}}
\textstyle{\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}})}

In this case,
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\right\}
, where the infimum is taken over all such factorizations of
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}
as above, and this infimum is attained.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then Theorem
1.4
gives a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
such that

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. By Lemma
1.5
, there is a mapping
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)=1
such that
h^{\prime}=j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}}
. Consider the linear subspace
S_{p}:=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(\mathbb{D})))\subseteq L_{p}(\mu)
and the operator
T_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(S_{p},\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
defined by
T_{0}(I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z)))=\iota_{X}(f^{\prime}(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Note that
\left\|T_{0}\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
since

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}T_{0}(I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z_{i})))\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\iota_{X}(f^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left\|\iota_{X}(f^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|_{\infty}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}d\mu(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\frac{\overline{\alpha_{i}}}{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|=\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|=\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{i})(g)\right|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{i})\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}j_{\infty}(\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z_{i}))\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\left\|I_{\infty,p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right)\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. By the injectivity of the Banach space
\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}})
(see
[
7
, p. 45]
), there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu),\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
such that
\left.T\right|_{S_{p}}=T_{0}
with
\left\|T\right\|=\left\|T_{0}\right\|
. This tells us that
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,p}\circ h^{\prime}
with
\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: By (ii), we have
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,p}\circ h^{\prime}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|\iota_{X}(f^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|T(I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z_{i})))\right\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|I_{\infty,p}(h^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}}{(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)
.
∎

The concept of holomorphic mapping with a relatively (weakly) compact Bloch range was introduced in
[
11
]
. The
Bloch range
of a function
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the set

\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f):=\left\{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}\subseteq X.

A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is said to be
(weakly) compact Bloch
if
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
is a relatively (weakly) compact subset of
X
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

(i)

Every
p
-summing Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is weakly compact Bloch.

(ii)

If
X
is reflexive, then every
p
-summing Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is compact Bloch.

Proof.

(i) Assume first
p>1
. If
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then Theorem
1.6
gives a regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mu),\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
and a map
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
such that
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,p}\circ h^{\prime}
, that is,
(\iota_{X}\circ f)^{\prime}=T\circ(I_{\infty,p}\circ h)^{\prime}
. Since
L_{p}(\mu)
is reflexive, it follows that
\iota_{X}\circ f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
is weakly compact Bloch by
[
11
, Theorem 5.6]
. Since
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(\iota_{X}\circ f)=\iota_{X}(\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f))
, we conclude that
f
is weakly compact Bloch. For
p=1
, the result follows from Proposition
1.1
and from what was proved above.

(ii) It follows from (i) that if
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
X
is reflexive, then
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
is relatively compact in
X
, hence
f
is compact Bloch.
∎

1.6.
Maurey extrapolation

We now use Pietsch domination of
p
-summing Bloch mappings to give a Bloch version of Maurey’s extrapolation Theorem
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
and assume that
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})=\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})
. Then
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},X)=\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\mathbb{D},X)
for every complex Banach space
X
.

Proof.

Lemma
1.5
and Proposition
1.2
assures that for each
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
, there is a mapping
h_{\mu}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
such that
h_{\mu}^{\prime}=j_{\infty}\circ\iota_{\mathbb{D}}
and
I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},L_{q}(\mu))
with
\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})\leq 1
.

We now follow the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.17]
. Since
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})=\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})
and
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{q}\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}
on
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})
by Proposition
1.1
, the Closed Graph Theorem yields a constant
c>0
such that
\pi_{p}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq c\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
for all
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})
. Since
L_{q}(\mu)
is an
\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}
-space for each
\lambda>1
, we can assure that given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, the subspace

E=\mathrm{lin}\left(\left\{I_{\infty,q}(h_{\mu}(z_{1})),\ldots,I_{\infty,q}(h_{\mu}(z_{n}))\right\}\right)\subseteq L_{q}(\mu)

embeds
\lambda
-isomorphically into
\ell_{q}
, that is,
E
is contained in a subspace
F\subseteq L_{q}(\mu)
for which there exists an isomorphism
T\colon F\to\ell_{q}
with
\left\|T\right\|||T^{-1}||<\lambda
.

Since
T\circ I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})=\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{q})
and
(T\circ I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left\|T^{-1}\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|T(I_{\infty,q}(h_{\mu}^{\prime}(z_{i})))\right\|_{\ell_{q}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T^{-1}\right\|c\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(T\circ I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|T^{-1}\right\|\left\|T\right\|\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

therefore
\pi_{p}^{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})\leq c\lambda
for all
\lambda>1
, and thus
\pi_{p}^{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})\leq c
. Now, by Theorem
1.4
, there exists a measure
\widehat{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
such that

\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu)}\leq c\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\ d\widehat{\mu}(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=c\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\widehat{\mu}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{p}(\widehat{\mu})}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In the last equality, we have used that

(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\widehat{\mu}})^{\prime}(z)(g)=I_{\infty,q}(h^{\prime}_{\widehat{\mu}}(z))(g)=h^{\prime}_{\widehat{\mu}}(z)(g)=j_{\infty}(\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z))(g)=\iota_{\mathbb{D}}(z)(g)=g^{\prime}(z)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
.

Take a complex Banach space
X
and let
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{q}(\mathbb{D},X)
. In view of Proposition
1.1
, we only must show that
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Theorem
1.4
provides again a measure
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
such that

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{0}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{0})}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. We claim that there is a constant
C>0
and a measure
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
such that

\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{0}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{0})}\leq C\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\lambda})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\lambda)}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Indeed, define
\lambda=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1/2^{n+1})\mu_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
, where
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is the sequence in
\mathcal{P}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})
given by
\mu_{n+1}=\widehat{\mu_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where the measure
\widehat{\mu_{n}}
is defined using Theorem
1.4
. Since
1<p<q
, there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
p=\theta\cdot 1+(1-\theta)q
, and applying Hölder’s Inequality with
1/\theta
(note that
(1/\theta)^{*}=1/(1-\theta)
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{n})}=\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)(g)\right|^{\theta\cdot 1+(1-\theta)q}\ d\mu_{n}(g)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)(g)\right|\ d\mu_{n}(g)\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)(g)\right|^{q}\ d\mu_{n}(g)\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{\theta}_{L_{1}(\mu_{n})}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{1-\theta}_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Using Hölder’s Inequality and the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n+1})}+\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{0}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})},

we now obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu_{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{\theta}_{L_{1}(\mu_{n+1})}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{1-\theta}_{L_{q}(\mu_{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mu_{n+1})}\right)^{\theta}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n+1})}\right)^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mu_{n+1})}\right)^{\theta}\left(2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})}\right)^{1-\theta}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c^{\frac{1}{\theta}}2^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n+1}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mu_{n+1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c^{\frac{1}{\theta}}2^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}2\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{n}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mu_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=(2c)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\lambda})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\lambda)}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. From above, we deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\mu_{0}})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{q}(\mu_{0})}\leq(2c)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\lambda})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\lambda)}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and this proves our claim taking
C=2(2c)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}
. Therefore we can write

\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq C\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|(I_{\infty,q}\circ h_{\lambda})^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{1}(\lambda)}=C\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|\ d\lambda(g)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Hence
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\pi_{1}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq C\pi_{q}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
by Theorem
1.4
.
∎

2.
Banach-valued Bloch molecules on the unit disc

Our aim in this section is to study the duality of the spaces of
p
-summing Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X^{*}
. We begin by recalling some concepts and results stated in
[
11
]
on the Bloch-free Banach space over
\mathbb{D}
.

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, a
Bloch atom of

\mathbb{D}
is the bounded linear functional
\gamma_{z}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\gamma_{z}(f)=f^{\prime}(z)\qquad(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})).

The elements of
\mathrm{lin}(\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
are called
Bloch molecules of

\mathbb{D}
. The
Bloch-free Banach space over

\mathbb{D}
, denoted
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, is the norm-closed linear hull of
\left\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
. The mapping
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
\Gamma(z)=\gamma_{z}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is holomorphic with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=1/(1-|z|^{2})
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
(see
[
11
, Proposition 2.7]
).

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, it is immediate that the functional
\gamma_{z}\otimes x\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\left(\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right)(f)=\left\langle f^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle\qquad\left(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\right),

is linear and continuous with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\|\leq\left\|x\right\|/(1-|z|^{2})
. In fact, it is immediate that
\left\|\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\|=\left\|x\right\|/(1-|z|^{2})
. Indeed, take any
x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
x^{*}(x)=||x||
and consider
f_{z}\cdot x^{*}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
. Since
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z}\cdot x^{*})=1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left|(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)(f_{z}\cdot x^{*})\right|=\left|\left\langle(f_{z}\cdot x^{*})^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle f_{z}^{\prime}(z)x^{*},x\right\rangle\right|=\left|f_{z}^{\prime}(z)\right|\left|x^{*}(x)\right|=\frac{\left\|x\right\|}{1-\left|z\right|^{2}}.

We now present a tensor product space whose elements, according to
[
11
, Definition 2.6]
, could be referred to as
X
-valued Bloch molecules on
\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. Define the linear space

\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X:=\mathrm{lin}\left\{\gamma_{z}\otimes x\colon z\in\mathbb{D},\,x\in X\right\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})^{*}.

Note that each element
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
is of the form

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes\lambda_{i}x_{i}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
z_{i}\in\mathbb{D}
and
x_{i}\in X
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, but such a representation of
\gamma
is not unique.

The action of the functional
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
on a mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
can be described as

\gamma(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle.

2.1.
Pairing

The space
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
is a linear subspace of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})^{*}
and, in fact, we have:

Proposition 2.2
.

\left\langle\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X,\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\right\rangle
is a dual pair, via the bilinear form given by

\left\langle\gamma,f\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
.

Proof.

Note that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a well-defined bilinear map on
(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X)\times\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
since
\langle\gamma,f\rangle=\gamma(f)
. On one hand, if
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and
\langle\gamma,f\rangle=0
for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
, then
\gamma=0
, and thus
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
separates points of
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. On the other hand, if
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
\langle\gamma,f\rangle=0
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, then
\left\langle f^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle=\left\langle\gamma_{z}\otimes x,f\right\rangle=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, hence
f^{\prime}(z)=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, therefore
f
is a constant function on
\mathbb{D}
, then
f=0
since
f(0)=0
and thus
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
separates points of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
.
∎

Since
\left\langle\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X,\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\right\rangle
is a dual pair, we can identify
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with a linear subspace of
(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X)^{\prime}
(the
algebraic dual of

\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
) by means of the following easy result.

Corollary 2.3
.

For each
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
, the functional
\Lambda_{0}(f)\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X\to\mathbb{C}
, given by

\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, is linear. We will say that
\Lambda_{0}(f)
is the linear functional on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
associated to
f
. Furthermore, the map
f\mapsto\Lambda_{0}(f)
is a linear monomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
into
(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X)^{\prime}
.
\Box

2.2.
Projective norm

As usual (see
[
19
]
), given two linear spaces
E
and
F
, the tensor product space
E\otimes F
equipped with a norm
\alpha
will be denoted by
E\otimes_{\alpha}F
, and the completion of
E\otimes_{\alpha}F
by
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}F
. An important example of tensor norm is the projective norm
\pi
on
u\in E\otimes F
defined by

\pi(u)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\left\|y_{i}\right\|\colon n\in\mathbb{N},\,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E,\,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in F,\,u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all the representations of
u
as above.

It is useful to know that the projective norm and the operator canonical norm coincide on the space
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, we have
\left\|\gamma\right\|=\pi(\gamma)
, where

\left\|\gamma\right\|=\sup\left\{\left|\gamma(f)\right|\colon f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\ p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq 1\right\}

and

\pi(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\colon\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
be a representation of
\gamma
. Since
\gamma
is linear and

\left|\gamma(f)\right|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\frac{\left\|x_{i}\right\|}{1-\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}}

for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
, we deduce that
||\gamma||\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|||x_{i}||/(1-|z_{i}|^{2})
. Since this holds for each representation of
\gamma
, it follows that
||\gamma||\leq\pi(\gamma)
and thus
\left\|\cdot\right\|\leq\pi
on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
.

To prove the reverse inequality, suppose by contradiction that
\left\|\mu\right\|<1<\pi(\mu)
for some
\mu\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. Denote
B=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X\colon\pi(\gamma)\leq 1\}
. Clearly,
B
is a closed convex subset of
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{\pi}X
. Applying the Hahn–Banach Separation Theorem to
B
and
\{\mu\}
, we obtain a functional
\eta\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{\pi}X)^{*}
such that

1=\|\eta\|=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\gamma))\colon\gamma\in B\}<\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\mu)).

Define
F_{\eta}\colon\mathbb{D}\to X^{*}
by

\langle F_{\eta}(z),x\rangle=\eta\left(\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right)\qquad(x\in X,\;z\in\mathbb{D}).

We now show that
F_{\eta}
is holomorphic. By
[
14
, Exercise 8.D]
, it suffices to prove that for each
x\in X
, the function
F_{\eta,x}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

F_{\eta,x}(z)=\eta(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D})

is holomorphic. Let
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
is holomorphic, there exists
D\Gamma(a)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C},\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))
such that

\lim_{z\to a}\frac{\gamma_{z}-\gamma_{a}-D\Gamma(a)(z-a)}{\left|z-a\right|}=0.

Consider the function
T(a)\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

T(a)(z)=\eta(D\Gamma(a)(z)\otimes x)\qquad\left(z\in\mathbb{C}\right).

Clearly,
T(a)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
and since

\displaystyle F_{\eta,x}(z)-F_{\eta,x}(a)-T(a)(z-a)

\displaystyle=\eta(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)-\eta(\gamma_{a}\otimes x)-\eta(D\Gamma(a)(z-a)\otimes x)

\displaystyle=\eta\left((\gamma_{z}-\gamma_{a}-D\Gamma(a)(z-a))\otimes x\right),

it follows that

\lim_{z\to a}\frac{F_{\eta,x}(z)-F_{\eta,x}(a)-T(a)(z-a)}{\left|z-a\right|}=\lim_{z\to a}\eta\left(\frac{\gamma_{z}-\gamma_{a}-D\Gamma(a)(z-a)}{\left|z-a\right|}\otimes x\right)=0.

Hence
F_{\eta,x}
is holomorphic at
a
with
DF_{\eta,x}(a)=T(a)
, as desired.

By
[
11
, Lemma 2.9]
, there exists a mapping
f_{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
f_{\eta}(0)=0
such that
f_{\eta}^{\prime}=F_{\eta}
. Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(1-|z|^{2})\left|\left\langle f_{\eta}^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle\right|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\eta\left(\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right)\right|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|\eta\right\|\pi(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=\left\|x\right\|

for all
x\in X
, and thus
(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f_{\eta}^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq 1
. Hence
f_{\eta}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{\eta})\leq 1
. Moreover,
\gamma(f_{\eta})=\eta(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. Therefore
\left\|\mu\right\|\geq|\mu(f_{\eta})|\geq\mathrm{Re}(\mu(f_{\eta}))=\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\mu))
, so
\left\|\mu\right\|>1
, and this is a contradiction.
∎

2.3.
p
-Chevet–Saphar Bloch norms

The
p
-Chevet–Saphar norms
d_{p}
on the tensor product of two Banach spaces
E\otimes F
are well known (see, for example,
[
19
, Section 6.2]
).

Our study of the duality of the spaces
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
requires the introduction of the following Bloch versions of such norms defined now on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
.

The
p
-Chevet–Saphar Bloch norms

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
are defined on a
X
-valued Bloch molecule
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
as

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\right\},

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}\quad(1<p<\infty),

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{\infty}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\right)\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all such representations of
\gamma
as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
.

Motivated by the analogue concept on the tensor product space (see
[
19
, p. 127]
), we introduce the following.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. A norm
\alpha
on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
is said to be a
Bloch reasonable crossnorm
if it has the following properties:

(i)

\alpha(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\leq\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|\left\|x\right\|
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
,

(ii)

For every
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, the linear functional
g\otimes x^{*}\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=g^{\prime}(z)x^{*}(x)
is bounded on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{\alpha}X
with
\left\|g\otimes x^{*}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|
.

Theorem 2.6
.

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
is a Bloch reasonable crossnorm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

We will only prove it for
1<p<\infty
. The other cases follow similarly.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
be a representation of
\gamma
. Clearly,
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)\geq 0
. Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda\lambda_{i})\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
is a representation of
\lambda\gamma
, we have

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left|\lambda\right|\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
\lambda=0
, we obtain
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)=0=\left|\lambda\right|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)
. For
\lambda\neq 0
, since the preceding inequality holds for every representation of
\gamma
, we deduce that
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)\leq\left|\lambda\right|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)
. For the converse inequality, note that
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)=d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda^{-1}(\lambda\gamma))\leq|\lambda^{-1}|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)
by using the proved inequality, thus
\left|\lambda\right|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)
and hence
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\lambda\gamma)=\left|\lambda\right|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)
.

We now prove the triangular inequality of
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\varepsilon>0
. If
\gamma_{1}=0
or
\gamma_{2}=0
, there is nothing to prove. Assume
\gamma_{1}\neq 0\neq\gamma_{2}
. We can choose representations

\gamma_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{1,i}\gamma_{z_{1,i}}\otimes x_{1,i},\qquad\gamma_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{2,i}\gamma_{z_{2,i}}\otimes x_{2,i},

so that

\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{1,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{1,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{1,i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1})+\varepsilon

and

\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{2,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{2,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|x_{2,i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{2})+\varepsilon.

Fix arbitrary
r,s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and define

\displaystyle\lambda_{3,i}\gamma_{z_{3,i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}r^{-1}\lambda_{1,i}\gamma_{z_{1,i}}&\text{ if }i=1,\ldots,n,\\
s^{-1}\lambda_{2,i-n}\gamma_{z_{2,i-n}}&\text{ if }i=n+1,\ldots,n+m,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle x_{3,i}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}rx_{1,i}&\text{ if }i=1,\ldots,n,\\
sx_{2,i-n}&\text{ if }i=n+1,\ldots,n+m.\end{array}\right.

It is clear that
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\lambda_{3,i}\gamma_{z_{3,i}}\otimes x_{3,i}
and thus we have

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\leq\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left|\lambda_{3,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{3,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left\|x_{3,i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

An easy verification gives

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left|\lambda_{3,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{3,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(r^{-1}\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{1,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{1,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}+\left(s^{-1}\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{2,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{2,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left\|x_{3,i}\right\|^{p}=r^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{1,i}\right\|^{p}+s^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|x_{2,i}\right\|^{p}.

Using Young’s Inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{*}}\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left|\lambda_{3,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{3,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}\left\|x_{3,i}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{-p^{*}}}{p^{*}}\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{1,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{1,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}+\frac{r^{p}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{1,i}\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle+\frac{s^{-p^{*}}}{p^{*}}\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{2,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{2,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right)^{p^{*}}+\frac{s^{p}}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|x_{2,i}\right\|^{p}.

Since
r,s
were arbitrary in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, taking above

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=(d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1})+\varepsilon)^{-\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{1,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{1,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right),

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=(d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{2})+\varepsilon)^{-\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\lambda_{2,i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{2,i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\right),

we obtain that
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1})+d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{2})+2\varepsilon
, and thus
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{1})+d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{2})
by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
. Hence
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
is a seminorm. To prove that it is a norm, note first that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|h^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for any
h\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
, by applying Hölder’s Inequality. Since the quantity
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|
does not depend on the representation of
\gamma
because

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)(h\cdot x^{*})=\gamma(h\cdot x^{*}),

taking the infimum over all representations of
\gamma
we deduce that

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}h^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)

for any
h\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
. Now, if
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)=0
, the preceding inequality yields

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})\gamma_{z_{i}}\right)(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})h^{\prime}(z_{i})=0

for all
h\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
. For each
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
, this implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})\gamma_{z_{i}}=0
, and since
\Gamma(\mathbb{D})
is a linearly independent subset of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
by
[
11
, Remark 2.8]
, it follows that
x^{*}(x_{i})\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, hence
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
since
B_{X^{*}}
separates the points of
X
, and thus
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}=0
.

Finally, we will show that
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
is a Bloch reasonable crossnorm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. Firstly, given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, we have

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\leq\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left\|x\right\|\leq\frac{\left\|x\right\|}{1-|z|^{2}}=\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|\left\|x\right\|.

Secondly, given
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})\right|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left|x^{*}(x_{i})\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|f^{\prime}_{z_{i}}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p^{*}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Taking infimum over all the representations of
\gamma
, we deduce that
\left|(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma)\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\gamma)
. Hence
g\otimes x^{*}\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{p}}}X)^{*}
with
\left\|g\otimes x^{*}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|
.
∎

The next result shows that
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}
can be computed using a simpler formula in the cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
. In fact, the
1
-Chevet–Saphar Bloch norm is justly the projective norm.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, we have

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right\}

and

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{\infty}(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all such representations of
\gamma
as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
be a representation of
\gamma
. We have

\displaystyle\pi(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-\left|z_{i}\right|^{2}}\left\|x_{i}\right\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\left\|x_{i}\right\|=\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right)\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|

and therefore
\pi(\gamma)\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma)
. Conversely, since
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}
is a Bloch reasonable crossnorm, we have

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|\gamma_{z_{i}}\right\|\left\|x_{i}\right\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}\left\|x_{i}\right\|,

and thus
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{1}(\gamma)\leq\pi(\gamma)
.

On the other hand, we have

\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\leq\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right),

and taking the infimum over all representations of
\gamma
gives

\inf\left\{\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\colon\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right\}\leq d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{\infty}(\gamma).

Conversely, we can assume without loss of generality that
x_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and since
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\|x_{i}\right\|\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes(x_{i}/\left\|x_{i}\right\|)
, we obtain

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{\infty}(\gamma)\leq\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\right),

and taking the infimum over all representations of
\gamma
, we conclude that

d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{\infty}(\gamma)\leq\inf\left\{\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|\right)\colon\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right\}.

∎

2.4.
Duality

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
, we will show that the dual of the space
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
can be canonically identified as the space of
p
-summing Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to
X^{*}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
, via the mapping
\Lambda\colon\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
defined by

\Lambda(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. Furthermore, its inverse comes given by

\left\langle\Lambda^{-1}(\varphi)(z),x\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi,\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\rangle

for
\varphi\in(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
.

Moreover, on the unit ball of
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
the weak* topology coincides with the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence.

Proof.

We prove it for
1<p<\infty
. The cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
follow similarly.

Let
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and let
\Lambda_{0}(f)\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X\to\mathbb{C}
be its associate linear functional given by

\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
. Note that
\Lambda_{0}(f)\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
with

\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
since

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}},

and taking infimum over all the representations of
\gamma
, we deduce that
\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}(\gamma)
. Since
\gamma
was arbitrary, then
\Lambda_{0}(f)
is continuous on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
with
\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|\leq\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f)
.

Since
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
is a norm-dense linear subspace of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and
d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}
is a norm on
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\otimes X
, then
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\otimes X
is a dense linear subspace of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\otimes_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
and therefore also of its completion
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
. Hence there is a unique continuous mapping
\Lambda(f)
from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
to
\mathbb{C}
that extends
\Lambda_{0}(f)
. Further,
\Lambda(f)
is linear and
\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|=\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|
.

Let
\Lambda\colon\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
be the map so defined. Since
\Lambda_{0}
is a linear monomorphism from
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
to
(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\otimes X)^{*}
by Corollary
2.3
, it follows easily that
\Lambda
is so. To prove that
\Lambda
is a surjective isometry, let
\varphi\in(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
and define
F_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{D}\to X^{*}
by

\left\langle F_{\varphi}(z),x\right\rangle=\varphi(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\qquad\left(z\in\mathbb{D},\;x\in X\right).

As in the proof of Proposition
2.4
, it is similarly proved that
F_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and there exists a mapping
f_{\varphi}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|
such that
f_{\varphi}^{\prime}=F_{\varphi}
.

We now prove that
f_{\varphi}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists
x_{i}\in X
with
\left\|x_{i}\right\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
such that
\left\langle f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle=\left\|f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|
. It is clear that the map
T\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, defined by

T(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\lambda_{i}\left\|f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|,\quad\forall(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

is linear and continuous on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},||\cdot||_{p^{*}})
with
\left\|T\right\|=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. For any
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
||(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})||_{p^{*}}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|T(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)\right|\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes t_{i}x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|\left(\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|t_{i}x_{i}\right\|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\varphi\right\|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

therefore

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\varphi\right\|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and we conclude that
f_{\varphi}\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|
.

Finally, for any
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, we get

\Lambda(f_{\varphi})(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varphi(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})=\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)=\varphi(\gamma).

Hence
\Lambda(f_{\varphi})=\varphi
on a dense subspace of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
and, consequently,
\Lambda(f_{\varphi})=\varphi
, which shows the last statement of the theorem. Moreover,
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{p}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|=\left\|\Lambda(f_{\varphi})\right\|
.

For the final assertion of the statement, let
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
. Assume
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
weak* in
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
, this means that
(\Lambda(f_{i}))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda(f)
weak* in
(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X)^{*}
, that is,
(\Lambda(f_{i})(\gamma))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda(f)(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}_{d^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p^{*}}}X
. In particular,

(<f^{\prime}_{i}(z),x>)_{i\in I}=(\Lambda(f_{i})(\gamma_{z}\otimes x))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda(f)(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=<f^{\prime}(z),x>

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
. Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle f_{i}(z)-f(z),x\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[0,z]}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w)-f^{\prime}(w),x\right\rangle\ dw\right|

\displaystyle\leq|z|\max\left\{\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w)-f^{\prime}(w),x\right\rangle\right|\colon w\in[0,z]\right\}

\displaystyle=|z|\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w_{z})-f^{\prime}(w_{z}),x\right\rangle\right|

for all
i\in I
and some
w_{z}\in[0,z]
, and thus
(\left\langle f_{i}(z),x\right\rangle)_{i\in I}\to\left\langle f(z),x\right\rangle
. This tells us that
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
converges to
f
in the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence. Hence the identity on
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{p}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
is a continuous bijection from the weak* topology to the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence. On the unit ball, the first topology is compact and the second one is Hausdorff, and so they must coincide.
∎

In particular, in view of Theorem
2.8
and taking into account Propositions
1.1
,
2.4
and
2.7
, we can identify the space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with the dual space of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}X\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})^{*}
.

Corollary 2.9
.

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}X)^{*}
, via the mapping
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}X)^{*}
given by

\Lambda(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\otimes X
. Furthermore, its inverse
is given by

\left\langle\Lambda^{-1}(\varphi)(z),x\right\rangle=\left\langle\varphi,\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\rangle

for
\varphi\in(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\widehat{\otimes}X)^{*}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
.
\hfill\qed

f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)

\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,2}\circ h^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I_{\infty,2}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{2}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}).

Hence
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}
factors in this way through the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mu)
. It would be interesting to introduce and study the class of Bloch mappings whose derivatives factor through a Hilbert space.

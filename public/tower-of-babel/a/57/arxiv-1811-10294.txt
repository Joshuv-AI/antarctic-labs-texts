On starlike functions related with the convex conic domain
R. Kargar and J. Sokół

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study a new subclass ℳ p ​ ( α , β ) \mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta) of p p –valent functions and obtain some inequalities concerning the coefficients for the desired class. Also, by use of the Hadamard product, we define a general operator and find a condition such that it belongs to the class ℳ p ​ ( α , β ) \mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta) .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}_{p}
denote the class of functions
f
of the
form:

f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\quad p\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\},

which are analytic and
p
–valent in the unit disk

\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. Further, we write that

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{A}
. A function
f(z)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
is said to
be in the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, if it satisfies

(1.1)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)<\beta\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-p\right|+p\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta),

for some
\beta\leq 0
and
\alpha>1
.
Note that (
1.1
) follows that

(1.2)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)<p\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta),

because
\beta
is negative or
0
. Moreover, if we write
(
1.1
) in the following equivalent form

\frac{|F(z)-p|}{{\rm Re}\left\{p\alpha-F(z)\right\}}<\frac{1}{-\beta}\quad(z\in\Delta),

where

F(z):=\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta),

then we see that the relation between the distance
F(z)
from the
focus
p
and the distance
F(z)
from the directrix
{\rm Re}\left\{w\right\}=p\alpha
depends on the eccentricity
-1/\beta
.
Therefore, if
f(z)\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, then
F(z)
,

z\in\Delta
lies in a convex conic domain: elliptic when

\beta<-1
, parabolic when
\beta=-1
and hyperbolic when

-1<\beta<0
, or
F(\Delta)
is the half–plane (
1.2
), for

\beta=0
.

The class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
cover many subclasses
considered earlier by various authors
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
7
]
. We remark that the class
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathcal{MD}(\alpha,\beta)

was investigated earlier by J. Nishiwaki and S. Owa
[
4
]
.

In this work we shall be mainly concerned with functions

f\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
of the form

(1.3)

\left(\frac{z^{p}}{f(z)}\right)^{\mu}=1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\quad(\mu>0,z\in\Delta\cup\{1\}),

where
(z^{p}/f(z))^{\mu}
represents principal powers (i.e. the
principal branch of
(z^{p}/f(z))^{\mu}
is chosen).

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some inequalities for the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
. In Section
3
, by use of the Hadamard product and applying the generalized Bessel function and the Gaussian hypergeometric function we introduce a new operator which we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)
. As an application, we prove that the operator
\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)
belongs to the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
.

2.
Main Results

Our first result is contained in the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let the function
f
be in the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)

and let
f
be of the form (
1.3
) for some
b_{k}
such that

b_{k}\geq 0\quad for\quad k\in\{p,p+1,p+2,\ldots\}\quad and\quad\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}<1.

Then

(2.1)

\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}[p\mu(\alpha-1)+k(1-\beta)]b_{k}\leq p\mu(\alpha-1),

where
\beta\leq 0
,
\mu>0
and
\alpha>1
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
. A simple calculation gives us

(2.2)

z\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\left(\frac{z^{p}}{f(z)}\right)^{\mu}=\mu\left[p\left(\frac{z^{p}}{f(z)}\right)^{\mu}-\left(\frac{z^{p}}{f(z)}\right)^{\mu+1}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z^{p-1}}\right].

Thus, by use of the above relation (
2.2
), we have

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)<\beta\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-p\right|+p\alpha

if and only if

{\rm Re}\left(p+\frac{-\frac{z}{\mu}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\left(z^{p}/f(z)\right)^{\mu}}{\left(z^{p}/f(z)\right)^{\mu}}\right)<\beta\left|\frac{-\frac{z}{\mu}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\left(z^{p}/f(z)\right)^{\mu}}{\left(z^{p}/f(z)\right)^{\mu}}\right|+p\alpha.

Since
f
is of the form (
1.3
), the last
inequality may be equivalently written as

(2.3)

{\rm Re}\left(p+\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right)<\frac{\beta}{\mu}\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right|+p\alpha.

Now suppose that
z\in\Delta
is real and tends to
1^{-}
through
reals, then from the last inequality (
2.3
), we get

\mu p+\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}}\leq\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}}\right|+p\mu\alpha

or equivalently

\mu p+\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}}\leq\beta\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}}+p\mu\alpha,

which gives (
2.1
).
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Taking
p=\mu=1
in the above Theorem
2.1
, we get the result that has been obtained recently by
Aghalary et al.
[
1
, Theorem 2.1 with
n=1
]
.

Next we derive the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
be of the form (
1.3
) with
\mu>0
.
If

(2.4)

\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}[p\mu(\alpha-1)+k(1-\beta)]|b_{k}|<p\mu(\alpha-1),

then
f
is in the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, where
\beta\leq 0
and
\alpha>1
.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we saw the following inequality

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)-p\alpha<\beta\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-p\right|,

is equivalent to

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right)-p\mu(\alpha-1)<\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right|.

Thus, to show that
f
is in the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, it
suffices to prove that

(2.5)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right)-\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right|<p\mu(\alpha-1).

Note that from (
2.4
), we obtain that

1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}|b_{k}|>0.

Therefore one can rewrite (
2.4
) in the following equivalent form

(2.6)

\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}k|b_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}|b_{k}|}-\beta\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}k|b_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}|b_{k}|}<p\mu(\alpha-1).

Because
\beta\leq 0
, we have

(2.7)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right)-\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}kb_{k}z^{k}}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}}\right|\leq\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}k|b_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}|b_{k}|}-\beta\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}k|b_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}|b_{k}|}.

Then (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) immediately follow
(
2.5
) and therefore,
f\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
(z/f(z))^{\mu}=1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
with
\mu>0
. If the function
f

satisfies the condition

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}[\mu(\alpha-1)+k(1-\beta)]|b_{k}|<\mu(\alpha-1),

then
f
is in the class
\mathcal{MD}(\alpha,\beta)
, where
\beta\leq 0
and
\alpha>1
.

Remark 2.2
.

The case
p=\mu=1
in Theorem
2.2
has been obtained recently
by Aghalary et al.
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

A function
f
of the form
f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
is in the class

\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, if

(2.8)

\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}[p\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]|a_{k}|<p(\alpha-1).

Proof.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
. Then by use of the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
, we have

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)-p\alpha<\beta\left|\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-p\right|

if and only if

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{p+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}ka_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right)-p\alpha<\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}(k-1)a_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right|.

Thus, it suffices to show that

(2.9)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{p+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}ka_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right)-\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}(k-1)a_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right|<p\alpha.

Note that from (
2.8
), we have

1-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|>0,

thus from (
2.8
) we obtain that

(2.10)

\frac{p+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}k|a_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|}-\beta\frac{\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}(k-1)|a_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|}<p\alpha.

Moreover, we have

(2.11)

{\rm Re}\left(\frac{p+\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}ka_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right)-\beta\left|\frac{\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}(k-1)a_{k}z^{k-p}}{1+\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k-p}}\right|

\leq\frac{p+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}k|a_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|}-\beta\frac{\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}(k-1)|a_{k}|}{1-\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|}.

Therefore, (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) follow (
2.9
). This
completes the proof.
∎

Putting
p=1
in Theorem
2.3
we have:

Corollary 2.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}[\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]|a_{k}|<\alpha-1,

for some
\alpha>1
and
\beta\leq 0
, then
f\in\mathcal{MD}(\alpha,\beta)
.

At the end of this section, by Theorem
2.3
we consider an example for the class
\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
.

Example 2.1
.

Define the function
f\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
as follows

f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{p(p-1)(\alpha-1)e^{i\theta}}{[p\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]k(k-1)}z^{k},

where
\alpha>1
,
\beta\leq 0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the coefficients inequality (
2.8
)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}[p\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]|a_{k}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(p-1)(\alpha-1)\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)(\alpha-1)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle p(\alpha-1),

which shows
f\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
.

3.
Applications

In
[
6
]
Porwal and Dixit considered the function

\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}
defined by the transformation

\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}(z)=2^{d}\Gamma\left(d+\frac{e+1}{2}\right)z^{-d/2}w_{d,e,\delta}(z^{1/2}),

where
w_{d,e,\delta}
is called the generalized
Bessel function of the first kind of order
d
and has the familiar representation

w(z)=w_{d,e,\delta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\delta^{k}}{k!\Gamma(d+k+\frac{e+1}{2})}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{2k+d}\quad z,d,e,\delta\in\mathbb{C}.

It is easy to see the function
\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}
has the following
representation

(3.1)

\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\delta/4)^{k}}{\left(d+\frac{e+1}{2}\right)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

where
d+\frac{e+1}{2}\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots
and and
(x)_{n}
is the
Pochhammer symbol defined by

(x)_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{$(n=0)$;}\\
x(x+1)(x+2)\dots(x+n-1),&\hbox{$(n\in\mathbb{N})$.}\end{array}\right.

We remark that the function

\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}(z)
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}
.

The Gaussian hypergeometric function
F(a,b;c;z)
given by

(3.2)

F(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}(1)_{k}}z^{k}\quad(z\in\Delta),

where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
and
c\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots
.
We note that
F(a,b;c;1)
converges for
{\rm Re}(a-b-c)>0
and is related to the Gamma
function by

F(a,b;c;1)=\frac{\Gamma(c-a-b)\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}\quad{\rm Re}(c-a-b)>0.

Now by Using
(
3.1
) and
3.2
we introduce a new function

\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z):\mathcal{A}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{p}
defined by

\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)=z^{p}(F(a,b;c;z)*\mathcal{U}_{d,e,\delta}(z)),

where
"*"
is the well–known Hadamard product. The function

\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)
has the following
representation

\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{(a)_{k-p}(b)_{k-p}(\delta/4)^{k-p}}{(c)_{k-p}\left(d+\frac{e+1}{2}\right)_{k-p}[(1)_{k-p}]^{2}}z^{k}.

We set
\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(1,e,\delta)(z)\equiv\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(e,\delta)(z)
. Applying Theorem
2.3
we have the following
Theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
e,d\in\mathbb{C}
. Also, assume that

\delta,d+\frac{e+1}{2}>0
and
c
be a real number such that

. Then

\mathcal{I}_{c,d}^{a,b}(p,e,\delta)(z)\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
if

(3.3)

\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}[p\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]\frac{|(a)_{k-p}(b)_{k-p}|(\delta/4)^{k-p}}{(c)_{k-p}\left(d+\frac{e+1}{2}\right)_{k-p}[(k-p)!]^{2}}<p(\alpha-1).

Proof.

Let
f(z)=z^{p}+\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}

Theorem
2.3
, it suffices to show that

\sum_{k=p+1}^{\infty}[p\alpha+\beta+k(1-\beta)]\left|\frac{(-1)^{k-p}(a)_{k-p}(b)_{k-p}(\delta/4)^{k-p}}{(c)_{k-p}\left(d+\frac{e+1}{2}\right)_{k-p}[(1)_{k-p}]^{2}}\right|<p(\alpha-1).

Some reductions give (
3.3
)
∎

Boundedness of spectral projectors on hyperbolic surfaces
Jean-Philippe Anker, Pierre Germain and Tristan Léger

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove L 2 → L p L^{2}\to L^{p} estimates, where p > 2 p>2 , for spectral projectors on a wide class of hyperbolic surfaces. More precisely, we consider projections in small spectral windows [ λ − η , λ + η ] [\lambda-\eta,\lambda+\eta] on geometrically finite hyperbolic surfaces of infinite volume. In the convex cocompact case, we obtain optimal bounds with respect to λ \lambda and η \eta , up to subpolynomial losses. The proof combines the resolvent bound of Bourgain-Dyatlov and improved estimates for the Schrödinger group (Strichartz and smoothing estimates) on hyperbolic surfaces.

Introduction

Consider a (smooth, complete) Riemannian manifold
X

positive
Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta

(1.1)

P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta}=\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda-\eta,\lambda+\eta]}(\sqrt{\Delta})\,.

We are interested in the following question:

which bounds does the operator norm
\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}

In this work, we are concerned with hyperbolic surfaces

[

\mathbb{H}

\text{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})

X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}\,.

We will start by providing some background to this problem, paying special attention to the relation between the bounds of the projectors and the underlying geometry of the manifold.

1.1.
General manifolds

The fundamental theorem due to Sogge
[
38
]
asserts that there exists
\eta_{0}>0
such that, for any
\lambda>1

(1.2)

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\sim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\sim\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}&\text{if }p\geq 6\\
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}&\text{if }2<p\leq 6,\\
\end{cases}

which will be abbreviated as

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\sim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\gamma(p)=\max\bigl\{\tfrac{1}{2}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\tfrac{2}{p}\hskip 1.0pt,\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\tfrac{1}{p}\bigr)\bigr\}.

This statement is classical on compact Riemannian manifolds;
we show in Appendix
A

For the round sphere,
P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta}=P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}

was established as

1.2.
The Euclidean case

The Euclidean plane and its quotients share many features with the hyperbolic plane and its quotients; for this reason, it is helpful to review here what is known and expected in this case.

1.2.1.
The Euclidean plane

For the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, optimal bounds are given by

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\sim\begin{cases}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }p\geq 6\\
\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}&\text{if }2<p\leq 6.\\
\end{cases}

This is equivalent to the Stein-Tomas theorem
[
40
,
43
]

\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}
comes from the “radial example”, where concentration occurs at a point (in physical space), while the term
\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}
stems from the Knapp example, which is concentrated along a geodesic (segment).

1.2.2.
The Euclidean torus

Considering a general torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/(a{\mathbb{Z}}+b{\mathbb{Z}})

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}}\hskip 1.0pt\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}+(\lambda\hskip 1.0pt\eta)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}

up to subpolynomial factors. The first term corresponds to the ”radial example” while the second is a variant of the Knapp example, where concentration occurs along a closed geodesic. See
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
24
,
25

for progress on this conjecture.

1.2.3.
The Euclidean cylinder

For the Euclidean cylinder
\mathbb{R}^{2}/(a{\mathbb{Z}})
or equivalently for the Cartesian product
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, the estimate (
1.3

[

decoupling theorem of Bourgain-Demeter

12

1.3.1.
L^{p}\to L^{q}
bounds for Fourier multipliers

When is a Fourier multiplier
m(D)
bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
? Already in the Euclidean case, more regularity is needed from
m
if
2<p,q<\infty
or
1<p,q<2
than if
p<2<q
, as is illustrated by the classical Bochner-Riesz conjecture or the Fefferman ball multiplier theorem.

This behavior becomes much more striking in the case of the hyperbolic space - and, presumably, also in the case of its quotients. More specifically, it has been shown by Clerc and Stein
[
19
]
that
L^{p}
multipliers (
p\neq 2
) on the hyperbolic space enjoy a bounded analytic continuation of their symbol on a strip containing the real axis. The argument, which naturally extends to
L^{p}\to L^{q}
boundedness with
2<p,q<\infty
or
1<p,q<2
, is recalled in Appendix
C
. See
[
22
]
for positive results on multipliers on locally symmetric spaces.

1.3.2.
L^{2}\to L^{p}
bounds

Since the cases
p,q<2
or
p,q>2
leads to this requirement of analyticity for the symbol of a bounded multiplier, we shall focus in the present article, on the case
p<2<q
, where the phenomenology is very different. We actually further restrict the problem, and ask for optimal bounds for the operator
\|P_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}
, which has symbol
\mathbf{1}_{[\lambda-\eta,\lambda+\eta]}
. This choice is natural if one thinks of
P_{\lambda,\eta}
as a spectral projector; furthermore, understanding its boundedness properties allows to treat general multipliers, see (
1.4
). Finally, since
P_{\lambda,\eta}=P_{\lambda,\eta}^{\hskip 1.0pt2}
, this immediately implies
L^{p}\to L^{q}
bounds as well, with
p<2<q
, though they might not be optimal.

In the case of the hyperbolic plane, optimal
L^{2}\to L^{p}
projector bounds were obtained by Chen-Hassell
[
18
]
, see also
[
23
]
for an alternative proof and the dependence of the constant in
p
.

1.5.
Finite area hyperbolic surfaces

Estimating the operator norm of
P_{\lambda,\eta}
for finite area hyperbolic surfaces is a very hard problem. When a cusp is present, the obstruction pointed out in Appendix
B
is still present; it can be avoided, for instance by focusing on a compact subset of the surface.

Two lines of research have been developed to address the case of finite area hyperbolic surfaces.
The first possibility is to rely on semiclassical analysis and to leverage negative curvature,
see for instance
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
27
,
30
]
.
These tools typically lead to improvements of a power of
\log\lambda
over the universal theorem of Sogge.

The second possibility is to focus on arithmetic surfaces, for which sharper results can be obtained through the use of number theory, as was first observed by Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
34
]
. They conjectured, in the compact case, that an eigenfunction
\phi_{\lambda}
associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
satisfies the bound

\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{\varepsilon}\,\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}},

with a corresponding statement in the non-compact case. See also the review
[
36
]
and further progress in this conjecture in
[
15
,
31
,
32
]
.

In view of Theorem
1.2
, it is tempting to ask whether, if
X
is a (geometrically finite) finite area surface, there might hold

(1.5)

\|P_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\sim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}+1

(up to subpolynomial factors).

The validity of (
1.5
) is supported by some observations. First, notice that the term
1
on the right-hand side is necessary since the
L^{p}
norm of a function on a finite area domain controls its
L^{2}
norm, if
p\geq 2
. Furthermore, this bound matches the universal lower bound proved in Subsection
3.1
as well as the bound stated in Theorem
1.2
in the case of infinite area surfaces. Finally, the typical spacing of eigenvalues is expected to be
\sim\lambda^{-1}
, so that the choice
\eta=\lambda^{-1}
should corresponds to estimates on eigenfunctions. The bound (
1.5
) then gives
\|P_{\lambda,\lambda^{-1}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\sim 1
, which corresponds to the conjecture of Iwaniec-Sarnak
[
34
]
- once again, up to subpolynomial factors.

A further argument in favor of the bound (
1.5
) has to do with the role played by geodesics. As far as estimating
\|P_{\lambda,\eta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}
goes, it seems that the most important geometric feature of the underlying Riemannian manifold is the presence of closed geodesics and their stability, more than the finiteness of the area. This is apparent in Euclidean geometry, as recalled in Subsection
1.2
: estimates for the torus or the cylinder differ from estimates from the whole space, and this can be related to the existence of closed geodesics in the former case, but not in the latter. Going back to hyperbolic geometry, we see that closed geodesics exist on quotients of the hyperbolic space, but that estimates are nevertheless the same for the hyperbolic space and its infinite area quotients. This can be explained by the instability of geodesics, and further supports the bound (
1.5
), which does not seem to be altered by the presence of closed geodesics.

1.6.
Organization of the paper and general ideas of the proof

We begin by recalling known harmonic analysis results on the hyperbolic plane and its quotients in Section
2
.

In the first part of the paper we consider the special case of the hyperbolic plane: we prove the desired upper bound on spectral projectors in Section
4
by adapting the interpolation method of Stein-Tomas
[
40
,
43
]
, and show that it is sharp in Section
3
. In Section
5
, we also prove new improved dispersive, Strichartz, and
L^{p}
smoothing estimates for functions with narrow support in frequency space.

The corresponding results are deduced in the case of convex cocompact surfaces with exponent of convergence
\delta\in[\hskip 1.0pt0,\frac{1}{2})
in Section
6
. In this case, the sum defining the periodized kernel of the various Fourier multipliers converges absolutely; this leads to
L^{\infty}
bounds, from which one can argue as in the case of the hyperbolic plane.

Section
7
focuses on the case
\delta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. In this case, the key estimate is due to Bourgain-Dyatlov
[
13
]
, which leads to a local smoothing estimate for the Schrödinger group. On the one hand, this smoothing estimate allows to bound the spectral projector when restricted to compact subsets of the surface. On the other hand, the improved Strichartz and
L^{p}
smoothing estimates allow to deal with the hyperbolic ends. This approach is reminiscent of Staffilani-Tataru
[
39
]
, who were the first to leverage local smoothing to obtain Strichartz estimates.

Appendix
A
is dedicated to the extension of the Sogge theorem to the case of a complete manifold, Appendix
B
to the proof of unboundedness of projectors in the case of the parabolic cylinder, and Appendix

\mathbb{H}

The aim of this section is to recall some basic formulas and facts related to generalizations of the Fourier transform to hyperbolic surfaces. We use

Before doing so, let us first fix the following normalization for the Fourier transform on the real line :

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\!f(x)\,e^{-ix\xi}\,dx\hskip 1.0pt.

With this normalization, the Fourier transform is an isometry from

2.1.
The spherical Fourier transform on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}

Consider a radial function
f
only depending on the distance
r
to the origin
(which is
i

The spherical Fourier transform and its inverse are given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}f(\lambda)=\widetilde{f}(\lambda)=2\hskip 0.5pt\pi\int_{0}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\!f(r)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\hskip 1.0pt\sinh r\,dr\hskip 1.0pt,\quad f(r)=\tfrac{1}{2\hskip 0.5pt\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\!\widetilde{f}(\lambda)\,\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\hskip 1.0pt,

where the spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}(r)

and the Harish-Chandra function
\mathbf{c}(\lambda)

are given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(r)=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\hskip 1.0ptr}\!\cos(\lambda\hskip 0.5pts)\,(\cosh r\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\cosh s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\hskip 1.0pt,\quad\mathbf{c}(\lambda)=\tfrac{\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pti\lambda)}\,.

Notice that

(2.1)

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{\lambda}(r)\sim\varphi_{0}(r)\sim(1\!+\hskip-1.0ptr)\,e^{-\frac{r}{2}}\quad\text{when}\quad\lambda\hskip 0.5ptr\hskip-1.0pt<\!1\hskip 0.5pt,\\
|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\sim\,\begin{cases}\,\lambda^{2}&\text{for $\lambda$ small, say $0\hskip-1.0pt<\hskip-1.0pt\lambda\hskip-1.0pt<\!1$\hskip 0.5pt,}\\
\,\lambda&\text{for $\lambda$ large, say $\lambda\hskip-1.0pt\geq\!1$\hskip 0.5pt.}\end{cases}\end{gathered}

In this context, Plancherel’s formula writes

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H})}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}|\widetilde{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\,|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda\hskip 1.0pt.

The spherical Fourier transform diagonalizes the Laplacian and allows to define its functional calculus. Setting

D=\sqrt{\Delta-\tfrac{1}{4}\,},

and given an even function
m
, there holds

m(D)f=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigl(m\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}f\bigr).

Finally, the operator
m(D)
can be realized through its convolution kernel

K_{\mathbb{H}}(r)=-\,\tfrac{1}{2\hskip 1.0pt\pi^{\hskip 0.5pt3/2}}\int_{\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\!\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\hskip 1.0pt\widehat{m}(s)\,(\cosh s\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\cosh r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,ds\hskip 1.0pt,

for which

[\hskip 0.5ptm(D)f\hskip 0.5pt](x)=[\hskip 0.5ptf\hskip-1.0pt*\hskip-1.0ptK_{\mathbb{H}}\hskip 0.5pt](x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}}K_{\mathbb{H}}(\operatorname{dist}(x,y))\,f(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\hskip 1.0pt.

The formula above holds true for radial or non-radial functions
f
.

2.2.
The Fourier transform on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}
in the upper-half plane model

In this model, the hyperbolic plane is represented by the complex upper-half plane
\{z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,y>0\}
,
which turns out to be more convenient for some computations. We follow here
[
9
, Ch. 4]

The generalized eigenfunctions are given by

E(\lambda,\xi,z)=\mathbf{C}(\lambda)\,\bigl(\tfrac{y}{(x\hskip 1.0pt-\hskip 1.0pt\xi)^{2}+\hskip 1.0pty^{2}}\bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{C}(\lambda)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi i\hskip 1.0pt\lambda\hskip 1.0pt\mathbf{c}(\lambda)}=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\hskip 1.0pti\lambda)}{\Gamma(1\hskip 0.5pt+\hskip 1.0pti\lambda)}\,.

Notice that

|\mathbf{C}(\lambda)|\sim\begin{cases}\,1&\text{for $\lambda$ small, say $0\hskip-1.0pt\leq\hskip-1.0pt\lambda\hskip-1.0pt<\!1$\hskip 0.5pt,}\\
\,\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{for $\lambda$ large, say $\lambda\hskip-1.0pt\geq\!1$\hskip 0.5pt.}\end{cases}

The spectral projector
\mathbf{P}_{\hskip-1.0pt\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}(D)
has integral kernel

(2.2)

K_{\lambda}(z,z^{\prime})=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\hskip 1.0pt\lambda^{2}\hskip 1.0pt\Bigl(\int_{-\infty}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}E(\lambda,\xi,z)\,\overline{E(\lambda,\xi,z^{\prime})}\,d\xi\Bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\hskip 1.0pt|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{-2}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(d(z,z^{\prime})\hskip-1.0pt)\hskip 1.0pt.

This leads to the following formulas for the Fourier transform, its inverse, and Plancherel’s identity:

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(\lambda,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{H}}f(z^{\prime})\,\overline{E(\lambda,\xi,z^{\prime})}\,dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle f(z)=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\hskip 0.5pt\infty}\widetilde{f}(\lambda,\xi)\,E(\lambda,\xi,z)\,d\xi\,\lambda^{2}\hskip 1.0ptd\lambda

\displaystyle\bigl\|f\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H})}^{2}=\tfrac{2}{\pi}\,\bigl\|\hskip 1.0pt\lambda\hskip 1.0pt\widetilde{f}\,\bigr\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda,\xi}}^{2}.

2.3.
The Fourier transform on hyperbolic surfaces

Appendix A
Sogge’s theorem on complete manifolds with bounded geometry

We show in this appendix how Sogge’s bound (
1.2
) on the operator norm of spectral projectors can be extended from compact manifolds to complete manifolds with bounded geometry, which means here

•

uniform bound on derivatives of any order of the metric,

•

injectivity radius bounded from below.

These conditions are satisfied for the quotients of the hyperbolic plane considered in Sections
6
and
7

Starting from a complete manifold
X
with bounded geometry, the strategy of the proof will be to reduce matters to the setting of a compact manifold, where the proof in the textbook by Sogge
[
38
]
applies. We restrict to the case of dimension
2
, but all arguments extend straightforwardly to higher dimensions.

The upper bound : reduction to a finite range operator.

We aim at proving the bound

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(p)},

for any fixed
\eta_{0}>0
and
\lambda>1
.
Firstly, note that it suffices to prove this bound for the operator

(A.1)

\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}=\chi(\sqrt{\Delta}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\lambda)+\chi(\sqrt{\Delta}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\lambda)\hskip 1.0pt,

where
\chi
is an even Schwartz function such that

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\chi}\subset[-\varepsilon_{0},-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}]\cup[\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},\varepsilon_{0}]

(where
5\hskip 1.0pt\varepsilon_{0}
is smaller than the injectivity radius of
X
)
and
\chi(0)=1
.
Indeed, there exists
0<\eta_{1}<1
such that
\mathbf{1}_{[-\eta_{1},\eta_{1}]}\leq 2\hskip 1.0pt|\chi|
,
hence

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,{\eta}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\leq\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,{\eta_{1}}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\quad\forall\,0<\eta\leq\eta_{1}\hskip 1.0pt.

If
\eta_{0}>\eta_{1}
, we split up the interval
[-\eta_{0},\eta_{0}]
into

N\sim\tfrac{1}{\eta_{1}}
disjoint subintervals
I_{j}
of length
\leq\eta_{1}
,
we estimate

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,\eta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{N}\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda+I_{j}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\,.

Secondly, the operator
\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}
can be written under the form

\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}=\sqrt{\hskip-0.5pt\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\widehat{\chi}(t)\hskip 0.5pt\cos\hskip 0.5pt(t\hskip 0.5pt\sqrt{\hskip-0.5pt\Delta}\hskip 1.0pt)\hskip 1.0pt\cos(t\lambda)\,dt\,.

Therefore, by finite propagation speed for the wave equation,
its kernel
K_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})
vanishes if
{d(x,x^{\prime})}>\varepsilon_{0}
.

Proof of the upper bound.

Consider smooth bump functions
\phi_{i}\hskip-1.0pt:\!X\!\to\hskip-1.0pt[0,1]

and
\psi_{i}\hskip-1.0pt:\!X\!\to\hskip-1.0pt[0,1]
such that

•

\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}^{2}=1
on
X
,

•

each
\phi_{i}
is supported in a ball
B(x_{i},\varepsilon_{0})
,

•

\psi_{i}=1
on
B(x_{i},2\varepsilon_{0})
and
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{i}\subset B(x_{i},3\varepsilon_{0})
,

•

the balls
B(x_{i},3\varepsilon_{0})
have uniformly bounded overlap,
hence
\sum_{i}\psi_{i}\lesssim 1
on
X
.

Notice that
\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\phi_{i}=\psi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\phi_{i}

by finite propagation speed.
Now that everything has been localized around
x_{i}
,
we can consider a compact manifold
K_{i}
agreeing with
X
on
B(x_{i},4\varepsilon_{0})

and deduce from Sogge’s result that

\|\hskip 1.0pt\psi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\hskip 1.0pt\phi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}

uniformly in
i\!\in\!I
.
By using successively the locality of
\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}
, the bounded overlap,
the boundedness of
\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}
,
the inclusion
\ell^{2}(I)\subset\ell^{p}(I)
, and once again the bounded overlap, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigl\|\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\hskip 1.0ptf\hskip 1.0pt\bigr\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\,\sum_{i\in I}\psi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\bigl(\phi_{i}^{2}f\bigr)\Bigr\|_{L^{p}}\sim\Bigl(\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{i\in I}\,\bigl\|\hskip 1.0pt\psi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\hskip 1.0pt\phi_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\bigl(\phi_{i}\hskip 1.0ptf\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\Bigl(\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{i\in I}\,\bigl\|\phi_{i}f\bigr\|_{L^{2}}^{p}\Bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt1/p}\leq\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\Bigl(\hskip 1.0pt\sum_{i\in I}\,\bigl\|\phi_{i}f\bigr\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\hskip 1.0pt\|f\|_{L^{2}}\hskip 1.0pt,

which is the desired upper bound.

Remark A.1
.

Notice that this upper bound holds true for

\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\!=\chi(\sqrt{\Delta}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\lambda)+\chi(\sqrt{\Delta}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\lambda)
,
where
\chi
is any Schwartz function.

\bigl\|\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\bigr\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}=\Bigl\|\hskip 1.0pt\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\hskip-6.0pt\sum_{n\in 2\mathbb{N}+1}\hskip-6.0pt\mathbf{1}_{[n-1,n+1)}(\sqrt{\Delta}\hskip 1.0pt)\hskip 1.0pt\Bigr\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\leq\sum_{n}\,|w_{\lambda,n}|\,\bigl\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{n,1}\bigr\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\hskip 1.0pt,

where

\bigl\|\mathscr{P}^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,1}\bigr\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{n}\hskip 1.0pt(1\!+\hskip-1.0pt|\lambda\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0ptn|)^{-\gamma(p)-2}\hskip 1.0ptn^{\gamma(p)}\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}\hskip 1.0pt.

The lower bound : reduction to a finite range operator.

We aim at proving that there exists a constant
{\eta}_{0}>0
such that,
for every
\lambda>1

\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{\lambda,{\eta_{0}}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{p}}\gtrsim\lambda^{\gamma(p)}.

Defining

\displaystyle\lambda^{\gamma(p)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\,\sum_{n}w_{{\lambda},n}^{2}\,\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{n,1}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\hskip 1.0pt\Bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt1/2}\leq\Bigl(\,\sum_{n}w_{\lambda,n}^{2}\,\|P^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}_{n,1}\|_{L^{p^{\hskip 0.5pt\prime}}\!\to L^{2}}^{2}\hskip 1.0pt\Bigr)^{\hskip-1.0pt1/2},

for

A perfect obstruction theory for 𝐒𝐔 ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathbf{SU}(2) -Higgs sheaves
Simon Schirren

Abstract




Abstract.

We present a new method for constructing virtual cycles for rank-2 Higgs sheaves
(E,\phi)
on a smooth projective surface
S
. Using this, we redefine the
\mathbf{SU}(2)
-perfect obstruction theory previously constructed by Tanaka-Thomas. The key step in our construction involves modifying the
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-localisation formula of Graber-Pandharipande by replacing the torus action with an involution
(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*})
.

0.2.
Spectral sheaves

Let
E
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbf{C}
and
\phi\in\mathrm{End}(E)
.

We make the
\mathbf{C}
-module
E
into a
\mathbf{C}[t]
-module by defining
t.e:=\phi(e)
, which is indeed a commutative action, as
\phi
commutes with itself and thus defines a sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}:=\mathbf{Spec}(\mathbf{C}[t])
.

We see that
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
is a torsion sheaf, whose scheme-theoretic support is given by the zeros of
m_{\phi}(t)\in\mathbf{C}[t]

\phi

Conversely, given a coherent torsion sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}
we set

for
\pi:\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}\rightarrow*

t\cdot\mathrm{id}
the tautological endomorphism acting on
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}
.

These two constructions are inverse to each other, so giving a torsion sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbf{A}^{1}
is equivalent to giving a pair
(E,\phi)
of vector space and endomorphism.

\mathcal{E}_{\phi}

which one could use as a definition
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}:=\mathrm{coker}(\pi^{*}\phi-t\cdot\mathrm{id})
.

\phi
is diagonalisable if and only if
m_{\phi}(t)
has distinct linear factors. In this case, it agrees with
\chi_{\phi}(t)
, the characteristic equation of
\phi
and
\mathcal{E}
is supported on the eigenvalues of
\phi
. For arbitrary
\phi
, the above
2
-step resolution defines a
divisor class

[

, Ch.2]

-step resolution.

\textrm{div}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi}):=\det(\pi^{*}\phi-t\cdot\mathrm{id})=\chi_{\phi}(t)
for
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
. One can think of

as the sheaf of (generalised) eigenspaces of

Higgs pairs

We do this now in families: fix a smooth projective surface

and a pair

where

is torsion free rank

0.4.
Moduli spaces

One is interested in describing
families
of sheaves
\mathcal{E}
after fixing rank and Chern classes, for which we need a
moduli space

\mathcal{N}
, i.e. a scheme whose closed points parametrize isomorphism classes
[\mathcal{E}]
of those sheaves (the ones of our interest are called stable sheaves, in particular we have
\mathbf{Aut}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbf{C}^{\times}
). These spaces
\mathcal{N}
turn out to have a dimension differing from a given
\mathit{expected\;dimension}
: namely, for a moduli space
\mathcal{N}
, we can associate to one of its closed points
[\mathcal{E}]
the spaces
\mathrm{def}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mathrm{ob}(\mathcal{E})
, the deformations and obstructions of
\mathcal{E}
in
\mathcal{N}
. These finite
\mathbf{C}
-vector spaces are given by the cohomology groups
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
for
i=1,2
. We call the difference

\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{def}(\mathcal{E})-\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathrm{ob}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{ext^{1}}-\mathrm{ext^{2}}

the
virtual
or
expected dimension

vd
of
\mathcal{N}
, provided the number is constant over all of
\mathcal{N}
.
The challenge lies now in defining the
virtual
fundamental class
[\mathcal{N}]^{vir}\in A_{vd}(\mathcal{N})
in the Chow group
A_{*}(\mathcal{N})
of the given virtual dimension. This has been introduced in
[
LT
]
and
[
BF
]
in the late 90’s.

In general for a moduli space
\mathcal{N}
parametrizing stable sheaves
\mathcal{E}
, there are bounds
2
2

2

See
[
HL
, pp.113-115]
for a precise statement.

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})\geq\dim_{[\mathcal{E}]}\mathcal{N}\geq\mathrm{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})

and we see
\mathrm{ext}^{2}=0
implies the smoothness of
\mathcal{N}
at
[\mathcal{E}]
. This happens e.g. for the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{L}
of stable vector bundles
E
and fixed determinant
\det(E)=L
over a smooth projective curve
C
. Here, deformations and obstructions are given by
\mathrm{ext}^{1}(E,E)_{0}
and
\mathrm{ext}^{2}(E,E)_{0}=0
; the suffix

denotes trace-free. In this case, a natural choice for the "virtual" fundamental class

would be the usual fundamental class

If
\mathcal{E}
is a stable sheaf on a smooth projective threefold

which only depends on the Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}
and on

\mathcal{N}
.
Furthermore, if
X

\mathrm{ext}^{1}-\mathrm{ext}^{2}=0.

This means if
[\mathcal{N}]^{vir}
exists and the moduli
\mathcal{N}

\int_{[\mathcal{N}]^{vir}}1\in\mathbf{Z}

to an integer. This is called a

or

0.6.
Vafa-Witten invariants

In this paper, we are interested in the moduli space
\mathcal{N}
of Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
or equivalently their spectral sheaves
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X=K_{S}
. To compute interesting invariants and by what we have discussed before, we replace the fundamental cycle
[\mathcal{N}]
by a virtual cycle
[\mathcal{N}]^{vir}
of some given expected dimension.

However,
\mathcal{N}
is non-compact, as it admits a
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-action by scaling the Higgs pairs
\lambda:(E,\phi)\mapsto(E,\lambda\phi)
or equivalently the fibres of
X\rightarrow S
and thus acts on
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
by pull-back. Thus, a virtual cycle on
\mathcal{N}
is a priori not interesting; the relevant virtual cycle arises after passing to the
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-fixed locus
\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}
. The "
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-localisation of virtual cycles", i.e. localising the virtual cotangent bundle
V^{\bullet}
on
\mathcal{N}
at the fixed locus
\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}
goes back to
[
GP
]
and allows us to compute invariants in the
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-equivariant setting via a (virtual) Bott residue formula

\int_{[\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}]^{vir}}\frac{1}{e(N^{vir})}.

Here,
e(N^{vir})
denotes the Euler or top Chern class of the
virtual
normal bundle of the obstruction theory: namely, if
V^{\bullet}
is the (
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
-equivariant) obstruction complex,
N^{vir}
is constructed by taking the restriction
V^{\bullet}|_{\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}}
to the fixed locus and taking the non-zero weight part.
The fixed locus
\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}
has two components, the "
instanton branch
"
\phi=0
and the "
monopol branch
"
\phi\neq 0
.

Taking kernels and co-kernels of the columns defines kernels and co-kernels of Higgs pairs, which form the abelian category

2.3.
Higgs bundles and their spectral sheaves

Instead of working with Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
, we consider their
spectral sheaves

\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X
, which are built as follows: Over a point
s\in S
, we attach the eigenspaces of
\phi_{s}
acting on
E_{s}
to their eigenvalues on the fibre
X_{s}\cong\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
.

Globally on
S
, we make

into a
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\oplus_{i}K_{S}^{-i}-
module via

E\otimes K_{S}^{-i}\xrightarrow{\phi^{i}}E.

This gives a torsion sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on

, preserving stability (definition recalled below) defining an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{Coh}_{c}(X)\cong\mathbf{Higgs}(S)

(see
[
TT
, Prop.2.2]
) between Higgs pairs on
S
and coherent sheaves on
X
of compact support, where the arrow from right to left is the spectral construction.

Conversely, starting with a compactly supported coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, its push-down
E:=\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}
is a torsion free, coherent sheaf and we get
\phi:=\pi_{*}(\tau\cdot\mathrm{id})
from the action of
\tau\cdot\mathrm{id}
on
X
for the tautological section
\tau\in\pi^{*}K_{S}
.

2.4.
Gieseker Stability

A Higgs pair
(E,\phi)
on
S
is
Gieseker stable
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
if

(2.4.1)

\frac{\chi(F(n))}{\mathrm{rank}(F)}<\frac{\chi(E(n))}{\mathrm{rank}(E)}\;\textrm{for}\;n\gg 0,

and all
\phi
-invariant proper non-zero subsheaves
F\subset E
.

For the stability of
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X
, we introduce the following notation:

Definition 2.4.2
.

For a spectral sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X
, we denote by
r(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})
the leading coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
, which agrees with the one of
E
: Indeed, as
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi}(n))=\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{O}(n)=E(n)
, we have
\chi(\mathcal{E}_{\phi}(n))=\chi(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}_{\phi}(n))=\chi(E(n))
and we can write

r(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})=\mathrm{rank}(E)\int_{S}h^{2}=\mathrm{rank}(E)\deg(S).

A Gieseker stable Higgs pair
(E,\phi)
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
is equivalent to a Gieseker stable spectral sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
with respect to the polarisation defined by
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
on
X
. This is the condition

(2.4.3)

\frac{\chi(\mathcal{F}(n))}{r(\mathcal{F})}<\frac{\chi(\mathcal{E}_{\phi}(n))}{r(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})}\;\textrm{for}\;n\gg 0,

for all proper non-zero subsheaves
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
. Here and for the rest of this discussion, we denote
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1):=\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
.

2.5.
Chern classes

The Chern classes for
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on

(E,\phi)
on

(E,\phi=0)

We will restrict entirely to the case

Then
\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})=i_{*}(\mathrm{ch}(E_{\phi})\cdot\mathrm{td}(T_{i}))

2.6.
Slope stability

Using the same notation as above, a Higgs pair
(E,\phi)
is slope-stable if

(2.6.1)

\mu(F)<\mu(E).

where

3.
The involution

3.0.
Summary

We will define an involution
\iota:\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
extending the classical map

(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*})

to all torsion free pairs Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
on
S
. This will be phrased as

\iota:\mathcal{E}_{\phi}\mapsto\sigma_{{\mathrm{tr}\phi}}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{\phi}\otimes\pi^{*}\det(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}_{\phi})^{-1}

for their corresponding spectral sheaves on
X
, where
\sigma_{{\mathrm{tr}\phi}}
translates the points in the fibres of
\pi:X\rightarrow S
by
\mathrm{tr}\phi
. We will define this as a functor of families of spectral sheaves, such that
\iota
above is its classifying map.

3.1.
The involution for vector bundles

Definition 3.1.1
.

Assuming
\mathrm{rank}(E),\deg(E)
to be coprime, let
(\mathsf{E},\Phi)
be a family parametrising Higgs bundles
(E,\phi)
on
S
. As each
E
is locally free, so is
\mathsf{E}
, thus
\mathsf{E^{*,*}}\cong\mathsf{E}
holds and

(\mathsf{E},\Phi)\mapsto(\mathsf{E}^{*},-\Phi^{*})

defines an involution.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Assuming
\mathcal{N}
consists of pairs with all
E
locally free (or shrink it such that it does) and that
(E^{*},-\phi^{*})
is also stable, then fixing the invariants
(2,c_{1},c_{2})
for
(E,\phi)
as in Sec.
2.5
, this defines a classifying map

\mathcal{N}(2,c_{1},c_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(2,-c_{1},c_{2})

with square equal to the identity, which is on closed points given by

[(E,\phi)]\mapsto[(E^{*},-\phi^{*})].

As in this case Gieseker = slope stable, it is enough to check that this map preserves slope stability: Ineed, let
(E,\phi)
be stable and let
E^{*}\rightarrow Q
be a Higgs quotient,
Q^{*}\subset E
and
\mu(Q)=-\mu(Q^{*})>-\mu(E)=\mu(E^{*})
, so
(E,-\phi^{*})
is indeed stable.

We will modify this involution in the next part and furhermore, as we are interested in the locus of
\mathcal{N}
where
\det(E)\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}
, it is sufficient to restrict entirely to

c_{1}(E)=0

for the rest of this discussion. In this case, the classifying map is a genuine involution on
\mathcal{N}(2,0,c_{2})
.
In order to generalise to torsion free
E
and hence to all of
\mathcal{N}
, we need some linear algebra:

3.2.
Skew maps

The reader only interested in the results of this rather technical subsection might jump directly to the final Rmk.
3.2.4
.

Let
\alpha:E\rightarrow E^{*}
be a map from a locally free rank
2
sheaf
E
to its dual. Then
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{*}
defines an involution on
\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*})
, i.e. splits

\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*})\cong\mathcal{S}ym(E^{*})\oplus\wedge^{2}E^{*}

into
\pm 1
eigenspaces: sections of the former are self-dual maps
\alpha=\alpha^{*}
and sections of the latter skew maps
\alpha^{*}=-\alpha
.

As
E
is rank
2
, a section
\alpha
of
\wedge^{2}E^{*}
defines an isomorphism whenever it is non-zero, which gives a canonical identification

E\otimes(\wedge^{2}E^{*})\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*}

by evaluation.

We explain sections of
\wedge^{2}E^{*}
in more detail: Fix an open
U\subset S
. A section
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}
of
\wedge^{2}E^{*}(U)
becomes a skew map
E(U)\rightarrow E^{*}(U)
via

[\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}](e):=\alpha_{1}(e)\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{2}(e)\alpha_{1}\in E^{*}(U)

for
e\in E(U)
.

We need the following fact relating trace and wedge product and refer the reader to
[
FH
, pp.111-112]
for a proof:

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
and
\phi:E\rightarrow E\otimes K_{S}
. Then
\sum_{i}e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\phi(e_{i})\wedge\dots\wedge e_{r}=\mathrm{tr}(\phi)e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{r}
for sections
e_{i}
of
E
.

Now let
\alpha:E(U)\rightarrow E^{*}(U)
be skew-map over
U
corresponding to a section
\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\in\wedge^{2}E^{*}(U)
.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

We have

\alpha\phi-(\alpha\phi)^{*}=\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\alpha

as elements of
E^{*}\otimes E^{*}\otimes K_{S}(U)
.

Proof.

As
\alpha
is skew, we only need to show
\alpha\phi-(-\phi^{*}\alpha)=\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\alpha
.

Indeed, for
e\in E(U)
and as
\alpha^{*}=-\alpha
, the LHS equals

\displaystyle(\alpha\phi+\phi^{*}\alpha)(e)

\displaystyle=((\phi^{*}\alpha_{1})(e)\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{2}(e)\phi^{*}\alpha_{1})+(\alpha_{1}(e)\phi^{*}\alpha_{2}-(\phi^{*}\alpha_{2})(e)\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle=[\phi^{*}\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\wedge\phi^{*}\alpha_{2}](e)

\displaystyle=[\mathrm{tr}(\phi^{*})\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}](e)=\mathrm{tr}(\phi)[\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}](e)=\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\alpha(e)

where we used Lem.
3.2.1
in the equality between second and third line.
∎

Lastly, we find:

Corollary 3.2.3
.

The following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes\bigwedge^{2}E^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes K_{S}\otimes\bigwedge^{2}E^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{*}\otimes K_{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\phi-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id})\otimes 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\phi^{*}}

commutes.

Proof.

Indeed, for
e\otimes\alpha
, going down the LHS side gives
-\phi^{*}\alpha(e)
. On the RHS, we get
\alpha(\phi-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id})(e)=\alpha\phi(e)-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\alpha(e)=-\phi^{*}\alpha(e)
by Lem.
3.2.2
.
∎

Remark 3.2.4
.

This implies that

(E^{*},-\phi^{*})\cong(E\otimes\wedge^{2}E^{*},(\phi-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id})\otimes 1)

are isomorphic Higgs bundles. Splitting

\mathcal{E}nd(E)\otimes K_{S}=(\mathcal{E}nd_{0}(E)\otimes K_{S})\oplus K_{S}\cdot\mathrm{id},

we can write
\phi=\phi_{0}\oplus\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id}
and see that
\phi-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id}=\phi_{0}\oplus-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id}
.

Remark 3.2.5
.

Using this, we may redefine the involution

(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*})

on Higgs bundles (again because locally frees are reflexive) as

(E,\phi)\mapsto(E\otimes\wedge^{2}E^{*},(\phi-\mathrm{tr}(\phi)\cdot\mathrm{id})\otimes 1)

under the isomorphism stated in Cor.
3.2.3
. This observation allows us now to generalise
\iota
to torsion free sheaves:

Lemma 3.5.2

𝐚

Abstract

In this paper, we prove a criterion for complexity in
g
-ary expansions of a rational fraction
a/b<1
with gcd
(a,b)=1
. We prove that for any purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
and all
j\geq 1
, each sequence of
j
digits occurs in the
g
-ary repetend of
a/b^{k}
with a relative frequency that approaches
1/g^{j}
with an increasing
k
. The absolute frequencies can be calculated by means of a simple transition matrix. Let
(a_{k})
be a sequence of positive integers relatively prime to
b
. We prove that each sequence of
j
digits occurs in the
g
-ary repetend of
a_{k}/b^{k}
with a relative frequency that approaches
1/g^{j}
with an increasing
k
, unless all prime factors of
b
divide the base
g\geq 2
.

Introduction

A
proper fraction
is a rational number
a/b\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
0<a/b<1
, where
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, and gcd
(a,b)=1
. Let
g\geq 2
be a positive integer. Any proper fraction
a/b
with a denominator
b
relatively prime to
g
has a unique
g
-ary expansion

a/b=x_{1}g^{-1}+x_{2}g^{-2}+x_{3}g^{-3}+\cdots,

where the digits
x_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
belong to the set
\{0,1,\ldots,g-1\}
. We write this expansion

0.\,x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots.

This
g
-ary expansion is purely periodic since gcd
(b,g)=1
. The smallest positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{i}=x_{i+n}
for all i
\in\mathbb{N}
is the
period
, and the word
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
is the
repetend
of the expansion.

Conversely, a
purely periodic

g
-ary expansion
0.\,x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots
represents a proper fraction
a/b
with a denominator
b
relatively prime to
g
, with the only exceptions
0=0.000\cdots
and
1=0.(g-1)(g-1)(g-1)\cdots
.

The period of a purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
is given by

\textrm{ord}_{g}(b):=\textrm{min}\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid g^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{b}\}.

Definition 1
.

(Lothaire
[
5
]
). A word
f
is called a
factor
of a word
x
if there exist words
u
,
v
such that
x=ufv
.

Definition 2
.

Let
j\geq 1
be a positive integer. The
enlarged repetend
of a purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
is the finite word

F_{g}(\frac{a}{b},j):=(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)})(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{j-1}).

For
j=1
, the second factor is the empty word.

Since the
g
-ary expansion is purely periodic, any factor (subword) of length
j
in the expansion is already a factor of
F_{g}(a/b,j)
.

Definition 3
.

A word of length
j\geq 1
is called a
j
-word
. The
j
-word at
i
is the word

x_{i-j+1}x_{i-j+2}\cdots x_{i},\quad\textrm{where }1\leq j\leq i.

The sequence of
j
-words at
i
in the
g
-ary expansion of the purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
is the sequence

\big(x_{i-j+1}x_{i-j+2}\cdots x_{i}\big)_{i=j}^{\infty}.

However, any of its
j
-words can already be found in the finite sequence

S_{g}(\frac{a}{b},j):=\big(x_{i-j+1}x_{i-j+2}\cdots x_{i}\big)_{i=j}^{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)+j-1}.

Each
j
-word of
S_{g}(a/b,j)
can be interpreted as a
base
g
integer
with
j
digits:

x_{i-j+1}x_{i-j+2}\cdots x_{i}=x_{i-j+1}g^{j-1}+x_{i-j+2}g^{j-2}+\cdots+x_{i}g^{0},

where
x_{i}
is the least significant digit and where the leading digits are allowed to be zeros.

It is convenient to use base 10 numbers when referring to
j
-words of
S_{g}(a/b,j)
. Each
j
-word receives a
list number

s
from
0
to
g^{j}-1
which is its value when read as a base
g
integer. The
j
-word at
i
receives the list number

s=\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}x_{i-m}g^{m}.

For instance, if
g=2
and
j=3
, the list number of the word
110
is
6
, and the list number of
011
is
3
.

We denote the set of list numbers by

J_{g}:=\{0,1,\ldots,g^{j}-1\}.

Throughout this paper we shall use the letter
s
to represent either
j
-words or their respective list numbers. When translating a list number back into a word, we take care of eventually leading zeros. For instance, if
g=2
and
j=4
, the list number
5
is the word
0101
.

The
absolute frequency

Definition 4
.

Let
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots
be the binary expansion of
x
. The

of

counts the number
P(x,m)
of different factors of length
m
in

.

The number

, so if

. On the other hand, if

.

In a former paper
[
4
]
, we studied the effect of successive divisions by
3
on the complexity of the binary expansion of
1/3^{k}
. We generalize these results for all reduced fractions of the form
a/b^{k}
in any integer base
g\geq 2
.

Following Waldschmidt
[
12
]
, a real number
\xi
is called
simply normal in base

g
if each digit occurs with (well-defined limiting relative) frequency
1/g
in its
g
-ary expansion. Furthermore, a real number
\xi
is called
normal in base

g
if it is simply normal in base
g^{j}
for all
j\geq 1
. Hence a real number
\xi
is normal in base
g
if and only if, for all
j\geq 1
, each sequence of
j
digits occurs with (limiting relative) frequency
1/g^{j}
in its
g
-ary expansion.

Our issue is far simpler. The fractions have an eventually periodic
g
-ary expansion. Nevertheless, we borrow the concept of normality for our purpose since it describes exactly the digits structure of an increasingly long repetend under successive divisions by
b
. With an increasing
k
, larger than an initial constant
c_{g}(b)
, which depends only on
b
and
g
, the
g
-ary period ord
{}_{g}(b^{k})
is strictly increasing unless all prime factors of
b
divide
g
.

We prove that for any purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
and all

, each sequence of
j
digits occurs in the
g
-ary repetend of
a/b^{k}
with a relative frequency that approaches

with an increasing
k
(Theorem

). This proof is given in Sections

and
4
.

Also, in Section
2
, we prove that the set of (distinct)

-words in

is completely determined by the corresponding set of
j
-words in
F_{g}(a/b^{k},j)
for all sufficiently large

(Proposition
1
). Next, in Section
3
, we prove a criterion for complexity in g-ary expansions of rational fractions (Theorem
1
). Further, in Section

, we show that the absolute frequencies can be calculated by means of a simple transition matrix (Proposition
2
and Example

), which guarantees that the oscillation of absolute frequencies of

is bounded for all

(Proposition

Lemma 1
.

Let
0.\,x_{1}x_{2}\cdots
be the
g
-ary expansion of a purely periodic proper fraction
a/b
. For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the quotient
q_{i}=\Big\lfloor ag^{i}/b\Big\rfloor

is the
g
-ary integer
q_{i}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{i}
.

Proof.

(a) We have
a/b=x_{1}g^{-1}+x_{2}g^{-2}+\cdots
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{ag^{i}}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}g^{i-1}+x_{2}g^{i-2}+x_{3}g^{i-3}+\cdots+x_{i}g^{i-i}+x_{i+1}3g^{i-{(i+1)}}+\cdots

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle x_{1}g^{i-1}+x_{2}g^{i-2}+\cdots+x_{i}g^{i-i}+(g-1)g^{-1}+(g-1)g^{-2}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}g^{i-1}+x_{2}g^{i-2}+\cdots+x_{i}g^{0}+1

imply that

q_{i}=\Big\lfloor\frac{ag^{i}}{b}\Big\rfloor.

Hence
q_{i}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{i}
.
\Box

Lemma 2
.

The sequence of
j
-words at
i
in the enlarged
g
-ary repetend
F_{g}(a/b,j)
of a purely periodic proper fraction is given by the following sequence of list numbers:

S_{g}(\frac{a}{b},j)=\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{ag^{i}}{b}\Big\rfloor\bmod{g^{j}}\Big)_{i=j}^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b)+j-1}.

Proof.
By Lemma
1
, the integer quotient
q_{i}=\lfloor ag^{i}/b\rfloor
is the
g
-ary integer
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{i}
. If
i\geq j
, then

(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{i})\bmod{g^{j}}=x_{i-j+1}x_{i-j+2}\cdots x_{i}.\\

\Box

Note that
S_{g}(a/b,1)
is the repetend of
a/b
.

For a fixed
b\not=1
relatively prime to
g
, the set of purely periodic proper fractions with denominator
b
splits in exactly
\varphi(b)/\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)
equivalence classes, where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function: any two of these fractions are
g
-equivalent if their numerators belong to the same coset of the cyclic subgroup
D\subset\big(\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z}\big)^{\times}
generated by
g
modulo
b
. Indeed, each numerator
a
relatively prime to
b
defines the coset

aD=\big\{ag^{i}\,\big|\,a\in\big(\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z}\big)^{\times},\,\,\,g^{i}\in D\quad\textrm{for all }i\in\{1,2,\ldots,\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)\}\big\}.

We define the
rotate left operator

\rho
by

\rho(s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\cdots s_{t}):=s_{2}s_{3}\cdots s_{t}s_{1}.

The following lemma shows that
g
-equivalent purely periodic proper fractions have up to successive left rotations the same
j
-words in the enlarged repetend.

Lemma 3
.

Let
a/b
be a purely periodic proper fraction and
j,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have

S_{g}\Big(\frac{(ag^{n})\bmod{b}}{b},j\Big)=\rho^{n}\Big(S_{g}(\frac{a}{b},j)\Big).

Proof.
For
n=1
,
we have
a/b=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots
. Hence
ag/b=x_{1}.\,x_{2}x_{3}\cdots
. Therefore,

\frac{(ag)\bmod{b}}{b}=0.\,x_{2}x_{3}\cdots.

By comparing the two sequences

\displaystyle S_{g}(a/b,j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{j},\,x_{2}x_{3}\cdots x_{j+1},\ldots,\,x_{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)}x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{j-1}),

\displaystyle S_{g}\Big(\big((ag)\bmod{b}\big)/b,j\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{2}x_{3}\cdots x_{j+1},\ldots,\,x_{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)}x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{j-1},\,x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{j}),

we get the statement for
n=1
. For any other
n\in\{2,3,\ldots,\textrm{ord}_{g}(b)\}
a similar argument holds by comparing the following expansions:

\displaystyle\big((ag^{n-1})\bmod{b}\big)/b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.\,x_{n}x_{n+1}\cdots,

\displaystyle\big((ag^{n})\bmod{b}\big)/b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.\,x_{n+1}x_{n+2}\cdots.

\Box

.

Let

Lemma 5
.

Let
a/b
be a purely periodic proper fraction and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The remainders of the sequence

R_{g}(\frac{a}{b},j):=\Big((ag^{i-j})\bmod{b}\Big)_{i=j}^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b)+j-1}

Lemma 6
.

Let
b>1
be an integer relatively prime to
g\geq 2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

h:=\frac{g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}-1}{b^{k}}.

Proof.
The integer

g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}\equiv 1\pmod{b^{k}}.

We consider two cases.

(a) If
b=d
, then

g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}-1=hb^{k}=nb^{k+1}\quad\textrm{for some }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus

g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}\equiv 1\pmod{b^{k+1}},

implying that ord
{}_{g}(b^{k})
is a multiple of ord
{}_{g}(b^{k+1})
. However, ord
{}_{g}(b^{k+1})
is a multiple of ord
{}_{g}(b^{k})
since

g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k+1})}\equiv 1\pmod{b^{k+1}}\quad\textrm{implies that}\quad g^{\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k+1})}\equiv 1\pmod{b^{k}}.

Hence ord
{}_{g}(b^{k+1})=\,\,
ord
{}_{g}(b^{k})
.

(b) If

g^{n\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}\equiv 1\pmod{b^{k+1}}

for some integer
n>1
, then we get
4
4

4

The symbol
{\bf O}\big(hb^{k+1}\big)
means that all the remaining terms are divisible by

.

g^{n\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{g}(b^{k})}=1+n(hb^{k})+{\bf O}\big(hb^{k+1}\big)

by raising both sides of

to the

must divide

. So we have

Hence

\Box

We now consider the new sequence

P^{\prime\prime}:=\bigg(\frac{a^{\prime}_{k}\bmod{\big((b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}\big)}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}}\bigg)_{k=1}^{\infty}:=\bigg(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}}\bigg)_{k=1}^{\infty}.

The first
dn=c_{g}{(b^{\prime})}
numerators of
P^{\prime}
remain unchanged. Let
k=c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t
(
t\in\mathbb{N})
. We claim that sequences

R_{g}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}_{k}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}},j\Big)=\big(r^{\prime}_{v}\big)_{v=0}^{\textrm{ord}_{g}((b^{\prime})^{k})-1}\quad\textrm{and}\quad R_{g}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}_{k}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}},j\Big)=\big(r^{\prime\prime}_{u}\big)_{u=0}^{\textrm{ord}_{g}((b^{\prime})^{k})-1},

except for left rotations, have the same remainders.

Indeed, the two sequences share the remainder
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}
. Of course,
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}=r^{\prime\prime}_{0}
. Furthermore we have

R_{g}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}}{(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}},j\Big)=R_{g}\Big(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}}{(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}},j\Big):=\big(r_{i}\big).

Note that this equality holds even if
a^{\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}>(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}
. We apply Lemma
9
and get the representation

a^{\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}=r^{\prime}_{0}=r_{i_{0}}+n_{0}(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}

for an appropriate
r_{i_{0}}\in\big(r_{i}\big)
and some
n_{0}\in\{0,1,\ldots,(b^{\prime})^{t}-1\}
.
Since
a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}\equiv a^{\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}\pmod{(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}}
, we have
r_{i_{0}}=a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}
. Therefore,

r^{\prime}_{0}=a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}+n_{0}(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}.

By Lemma
9
, there is a remainder
r^{\prime}_{v_{0}}\in\big(r^{\prime}_{v}\big)
such that

r^{\prime}_{v_{0}}=a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}+0\cdot(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}=a^{\prime\prime}_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})+t}.

Hence
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}
also belongs to the sequence
\big(r^{\prime}_{v}\big)
.

Since
\big(r^{\prime}_{v}\big)
and
\big(r^{\prime\prime}_{u}\big)
are left rotations of the same sequence, the corresponding sequences of
j
-words also are left rotations of the same sequence by Lemma
4
. It follows that the corresponding terms of
P^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime\prime}
, except for left rotations, have the same repetends.

Let
D_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}
be the cyclic subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
generated by
g
modulo
(b^{\prime})^{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}
. Furthermore, let
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{h},\ldots,z_{\ell}\}
be representatives of the

\ell=\frac{\varphi\big((b^{\prime})^{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b^{\prime})}\big)}{\textrm{ord}_{g}((b^{\prime})^{\textrm{ord}_{g}(b^{\prime})}\big)}

cosets of
D_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}
.

Starting at
k=c_{g}(b^{\prime})
, we replace each numerator
a^{\prime\prime}_{k}
of
P^{\prime\prime}
by the representative
z_{h}
of the corresponding coset
z_{h}D_{c_{g}(b^{\prime})}
and get the sequence

P^{\prime\prime\prime}:=\Big(\frac{y_{k}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}}\Big)_{k=c_{g}(b^{\prime})}^{\infty},

where
y_{k}\in\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\}
. The corresponding terms of
P
and
P^{\prime\prime\prime}
have left rotations of the same repetends.

The sequence
P^{\prime\prime\prime}
splits into finitely many subsequences
\big(z_{h}/(b^{\prime})^{k_{i}}\big)_{k_{i}}
of terms with equal numerator
z_{h}
. Any such subsequence is also a subsequence of

Z_{h}:=\Big(\frac{z_{h}}{(b^{\prime})^{k}}\Big)_{k=c_{g}(b^{\prime})}^{\infty},

and the statements of Section
4
hold for this sequence.

In particular, the oscillation of absolute frequencies of
j
-words in
F_{g}(z_{h}/(b^{\prime})^{k},j)
is bounded by
C_{g}(z_{h}/b^{\prime},j)
for all
k\geq c_{g}(b^{\prime})
(see Proposition
5
). Since there are only finitely many sequences
Z_{h}
,

c:=\textrm{max}\big\{C_{g}(z_{h}/b^{\prime},j)\,\big|\,z_{h}\in\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\}\big\}

is a bound for all
Z_{h}
. Hence the oscillation of absolute frequencies of
j
-words in the enlarged repetend of the proper fractions
a_{k}/b^{k}
is bounded too for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
\Box

Theorem 3
.

Let
b>1
be a positive integer and
\big(a_{k}\big)_{k=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of positive integers relatively prime to
b
. For all
j\geq 1
, each sequence of
j
digits occurs in the
g
-ary repetend of
a_{k}/b^{k}
with a relative frequency that approaches
1/g^{j}
for an increasing
k
, unless all prime factors of
b
divide the base
g\geq 2
.

Proof.
The statement holds for the enlarged repetend by Proposition
7
. It also holds for the repetend by the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
2
.

If all prime factors of
b
divide
g
, then the
g
-ary expansion of
a/b^{k}
is eventually constant and equal to
0
or
g-1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
\Box

6
A Helpful Sequence

Lemma 15
.

Let
a/b\in\mathbb{Q}
be a positive fraction in lowest terms:
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, and
\emph{gcd}(a,b)=1
.
Let
A:=\{0,1,2,\ldots,a-1\}
and
B:=\{0,1,2,\ldots,b-1\}
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

\Big\{\big\lfloor t\cdot\frac{a}{b}\big\rfloor\,\Big|\,t\in B\Big\}=A
if and only if
\quad 0<\frac{a}{b}<1
.

2.

Let
0<\frac{a}{b}<1
and
u:=b\bmod{a}
. In the sequence

\bigg(\Big\lfloor t\cdot\frac{a}{b}\Big\rfloor\bigg)_{t=0}^{b-1},\\

each element of
A
occurs either
\lceil b/a\rceil
or
\lfloor b/a\rfloor
times. There are exactly
u
elements of
A
occurring
\lceil b/a\rceil
times; each of the remaining
a-u
elements occurs
\lfloor b/a\rfloor
times.

Proof
. Lemma
15
is a simplified version of Proposition 23 in
[
4
]
.
\Box

Lemma 16
.

Let
z/m>0
be a rational fraction such that
\mathrm{gcd}(z,m)=d
.
Let
Z=\{0,1,\ldots,z-1\}
and
M=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

\Big\{\big\lfloor t\cdot\frac{z}{m}\big\rfloor\big|t\in M\Big\}=Z
if and only if
0<\frac{z}{m}\leq 1
;

2.

Let
0<\frac{z}{m}<1
be and
u:=\frac{m}{d}\bmod{\frac{z}{d}}
. In the sequence

S:=\bigg(\Big\lfloor t\cdot\frac{z}{m}\Big\rfloor\bigg)_{t=0}^{m-1},

each element of
Z
occurs either
\lceil m/z\rceil
or
\lfloor m/z\rfloor
times. There are exactly
ud
elements of
Z
occurring
\lceil m/z\rceil
times, and
0
is one of them; each of the remaining
z-ud
elements occurs
\lfloor m/z\rfloor
times.

Proof.
Since
z=da
,
m=db
and gcd
(a,b)=1
, we have

Z=\bigcup_{x=0}^{d-1}\{xa,xa+1,\ldots,xa+a-1\},\quad M=\bigcup_{x=0}^{d-1}\{xb,xb+1,\ldots,xb+b-1\}.

Each
t\in M
has a unique representation

t=xb+y,\quad\textrm{where}\quad 0\leq y\leq b-1\quad\textrm{and}\quad 0\leq x\leq d-1.

It follows that

\Big\lfloor t\cdot\frac{z}{m}\Big\rfloor=xa+\Big\lfloor y\cdot\frac{a}{b}\Big\rfloor.

Hence

S=(S^{\prime},a+S^{\prime},2a+S^{\prime},\ldots,(d-1)a+S^{\prime}),\quad\textrm{where}\quad S^{\prime}:=\bigg(\Big\lfloor y\cdot\frac{a}{b}\Big\rfloor\bigg)_{y=0}^{b-1}.

Of course,
a+S^{\prime}
means that we add
a
to each term of
S^{\prime}
.

We apply Lemma
15
to the sequence
S^{\prime}
. The set of integers in
S^{\prime}
is
A:=\{0,1,\ldots,a-1\}
if and only if
0<a/b<1
, and
u
elements of
A
occur
\lceil b/a\rceil
times and
a-u
elements
\lfloor b/a\rfloor
times in
S^{\prime}
. The number
0
occurs
\lceil b/a\rceil
times, because it is the only integer in the sequence
(ya/b)_{y=0}^{b-1}
. If
A
is the set of integers in
S^{\prime}
, with
0<a/b<1
, then
S
is a sequence of consecutive integers from
0
to
da-1
, wherein
ud
integers are repeated
\lceil m/z\rceil
times and
z-ud
elements
\lfloor m/z\rfloor
times. Therefore,
Z
is the set of integers in
S
, and this is also true for
z/m=1
since then
Z=M
.
\Box

Cohomology and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 17B38; Secondary 17B60,17A99 Thanks: Qiao was partially supported by NSFC grant 11971282.
Jia Zhao, Yu Qiao*

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras via the Yamaguti cohomology. Then we use this type of cohomology to characterize deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We show that if two linear or formal deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operator are equivalent, then their infinitesimals are in the same cohomology class in the first cohomology group. Moreover, an order n n deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator can be extended to an order n + 1 n+1 deformation if and only if the obstruction class in the second cohomology group is trivial.



1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the cohomology theory and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Lie-Yamaguti algebras are a generalization of Lie algebras and Lie triple systems, which can be traced back to Nomizu’s work on the invariant affine connections on homogeneous spaces in 1950’s (
[
23
]
) and Yamaguti’s work on general Lie triple systems and Lie triple algebras (
[
31
,
32
]
). Kinyon and Weinstein first called this object a Lie-Yamaguti algebra when studying Courant algebroids in the earlier 21st century
[
16
]
. Since then, this system is called a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, which has attracted much attention and is widely investigated recently. For instance, Benito and his collaborators deeply explored irreducible Lie-Yamaguti algebras and their relations with orthogonal Lie algebras
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. Deformations and extensions of Lie-Yamaguti algebras were examined in
[
19
,
20
,
34
,
35
]
. Sheng, the first author, and Zhou analyzed product structures and complex structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras by means of Nijenhuis operators in
[
25
]
. Takahashi studied modules over quandles using representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras in
[
27
]
.

Another two topics of the present paper are deformation theory and Rota-Baxter operators, which play important roles in both mathematics and mathematical physics. In mathematics, informally speaking, a deformation of an object is another object that shares the same structure of the original object after a perturbation. Motivated by the foundational work of Kodaira and Spencer
[
17
]
for complex analytic structures, the generalization in algebraic geometry of deformation theory was founded
[
15
]
. As an application in algebra, Gerstenhaber first studied the deformation theory on associative algebras
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. Then Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this idea and established the similar results on Lie algebras
[
21
,
22
]
. Deformations of other algebraic structures such as pre-Lie algebras have also been developed
[
7
]
. In general, deformation theory was set up for binary quadratic operads by Balavoine
[
1
]
.

Meanwhile, Baxter introduced the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras when studying fluctuation theory
[
2
]
. Then Kupershmidt introduced the notion of
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators (called relative Rota-Baxter operators in the present paper) on Lie algebras which is a weighted zero Rota-Baxter operator when he found that a relative Rota-Baxter operator is a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation in
[
18
]
. For more details about classical Yang-Baxter equations and Rota-Baxter operators, one can see
[
24
]
and
[
14
]
.

Since deformation theory and Rota-Baxter operators have important applications in mathematics and mathematical physics, Sheng and his collaborators developed cohomology theories and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a series of algebraic structures such as Lie algebras,
3
-Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras. All such cohomology theories obey the following rule: the infinitesimal of a formal deformation is characterized by the cohomology class in the first cohomology group, and the obstruction class is trivial if and only if an order
n
deformation can be extended to an order
n+1
deformation. See
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
for more details. Recently, Sheng and the first author introduced the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras and revealed the fact that a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra is the underlying algebraic structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators
[
26
]
.

By the virtue of Lie-Yamaguti algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators, it is natural to ask the following question: Does there exist an appropriate cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which can be used to classify certain types of deformations? We tackle this problem as follows.

The most important step is to construct a suitable cohomology theory for relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. It has been proved that in
[
26
]
if
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
, then there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
on
V
. The key role played in this step is to construct a representation of this Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
on
\mathfrak{g}
(viewed as the representation space), that is, we shall give the explicit formulas of linear maps
\varrho:V\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\varpi:\otimes^{2}V\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
D_{\varrho,\varpi}
, which are linked with the representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
and the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
, such that the triple
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho,\varpi)
becomes a representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
V
(see Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
).
Consequently, we obtain the corresponding Yamaguti cohomology of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho,\varpi)
.
However, note that the cochain complex of Yamaguti cohomology starts only from
1
-cochians,
not
from
0
-cochains.
The main
difficulty
is to choose
0
-cochains appropriately and build a proper coboundary map from the set of
0
-cochains to that of
1
-cochains.

Our strategy
is to define the set of
0
-cochains to be
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, then construct the coboundary map explicitly (see Proposition
3.5
).
In this way, we obtain a cochain complex (associated to
V
) starting from
0
-cochains,
which gives rise to the cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
on Lie-Yamaguti algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
(see Definition
3.6
). A Lie-Yamaguti algebra owns two algebraic operations, which makes its cochain complex much more complicated than others, while other algebras such as Lie algebras, pre-Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras or even
3
-Lie algebras owns only one structure map. As a result, the computation is technical in defining the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators.

The next step is to make use of the cohomology theory to investigate deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We consider three kinds of deformations: linear, formal, and higher order deformations. It turns out that our cohomology theory satisfies the rule that is mentioned above and works well (see Theorem
4.7
, Theorem
4.23
, and Theorem
4.29
).

As stated before, a Lie triple system is a spacial case of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, so the conclusions in the present paper can also be adapted to the Lie triple system context. See
[
8
]
for more details about cohomologies and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie triple systems. However, unlike other algebras such as Lie algebras or Leibniz algebras, there does not exist a suitable graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are just the Lie-Yamaguri algebra structure by now, thus we do not find a suitable
L_{\infty}
-algebra that controls the deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators. We will overcome this problem in the future and also expect new findings in this direction.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section 2, we recall some basic concepts including those of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and cohomologies.
In Section 3, the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras is constructed by using that of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.
Finally, we utilize our established cohomology theory to analyze three kinds of deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras: namely linear, formal, and higher order deformations in Section 4.

2.
Preliminaries: Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and cohomologies

In this paper, all vector spaces are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite-dimensional.

In this section, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theories.
The notion of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was first defined by Yamaguti in
[
31
,
32
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
16
]

A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w]+[z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

(4)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket.

In the sequel, we denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. Define a trilinear bracket

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

by

\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket:=[[x,y],z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then by a direct computation, we know that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

The following example is even more interesting.

Example 2.3
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold with an affine connection, and denote by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, set

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-T(x,y),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-R(x,y)z,

where
T
and
R
are torsion tensor and curvature tensor respectively. It turns out that the triple

(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. See
[
23
]
for more details.

The following two notions are standard.

Definition 2.4
.

[
25
,
27
]

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
are two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A
homomorphism
from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
that preserves the Lie-Yamaguti algebra structures, that is, for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle~\phi(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

If, moreover,
\phi
is a bijection, it is then called an
isomorphism
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
32
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space
V
equipped with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and a bilinear map
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(5)

\displaystyle\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

(8)

\displaystyle\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],

where the bilinear map
D_{\rho,\mu}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is given by

(10)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y):=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is obvious that
D_{\rho,\mu}
is skew-symmetric. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
(V;\rho,\mu)
, or we call
(\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
in the sequel.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\rho=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie tripe system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
:
(V;\mu)
. If
\mu=0
,
D_{\rho,\mu}=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
:
(V;\rho)
. Hence a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a natural generalization of that of a Lie algebra or of a Lie triple system.

By a direct computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Then the following equalities are satisfied:

(11)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}([x,y],z)+D_{\rho,\mu}([y,z],x)+D_{\rho,\mu}([z,x],y)=0;

(12)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+D_{\rho,\mu}(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),D_{\rho,\mu}(z,w)];

(13)

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)=\mu(x,w)\mu(z,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(z,x)-\mu(z,w)D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y).

Example 2.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We define linear maps
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{R}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
x\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{x}
and
(x,y)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
respectively, where
\mathrm{ad}_{x}z=[x,z]
and
\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket
for all
z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
forms a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, where
\mathfrak{L}:=D_{\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}=\mathfrak{R}_{y,x}-\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}+[\mathrm{ad}_{x},\mathrm{ad}_{y}]-\mathrm{ad}_{[x,y]},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

By (
1
), we have

(14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In this case,
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras can be characterized by the semidirect Lie-Yamaguti algebras. This fact is revealed via the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
34
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
V
a vector space. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be linear maps. Then
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
if and only if there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho,\mu},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu})
on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
which is defined by for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in V
,

(15)

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u,

(16)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)w+\mu(y,z)u-\mu(x,z)v,

where
D_{\rho,\mu}
is given by (
10
).
This Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho,\mu},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu})
is called the
semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra
, and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}V
.

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation, so we omit the details. Or one can see

for more details.

Let us recall the cohomology theory on Lie-Yamaguti algebras given in
[
32
]
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote the set of
p

C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)~(p\geqslant 1)

\displaystyle C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\triangleq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n},V)\times\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V),&\forall n\geqslant 1,\\
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V),&n=0.\end{cases}

In the sequel, we recall the coboundary map of
p
-cochains:

•

If

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(f,g)\mapsto(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)),

is given as follows:

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\rho(x_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})-\rho(y_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{n+1},y_{n+1}])\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X}_{k})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\mu(y_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})-\mu(x_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

.

if

With the notations above, for any

, if
p=2
) such that
(f,g)=\delta(h,s)
(resp.
(f,g)=\delta(t)
), then it is called a
p
-coboundary (
p\geqslant 2
). The set of
p
-cocycles and that of
p
-coboundaries is denoted by
Z^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
B^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
respectively. The resulting
p
-cohomology group is defined to be the factor space

H^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)=Z^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)/B^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V).

In particular, we have

H^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{f\in C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V):\delta(f)=0\}.

3.
Cohomologies of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we build the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Once a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with respect to a representation is given, we obtain a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on the representation space. Then we will construct a representation of the representation space (viewed as a Lie-Yamaguti algebra) on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra as a vector space. At the beginning, we recall some notions and conclusions in
[
26
]
about relationship between relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras and pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

[
26
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. A linear map
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
if
T
satisfies

(17)

\displaystyle~[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\Big),

(18)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Recall also that in
[
26
]
once a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is given, there exists a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
on
A
as follows:

\displaystyle[x,y]_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x*y-y*x,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}_{D}+\{x,y,z\}-\{y,x,z\},\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

We call the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
the
sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
26
]

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Define

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v,\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Then
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, which is sub-adjacent to the pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
defined by

(19)

\displaystyle u*v=\rho(Tu)v,\quad\{u,v,w\}=\mu(Tv,Tw)u,\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Thus
T
is a homomorphism from
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

So far, the representation space
V
has been endowed with a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure
([\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
. We will give a representation of
V
on
\mathfrak{g}
(viewed as a vectors space) in the sequel. Define two linear maps
\varrho:V\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\varpi:\otimes^{2}V\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

(20)

\displaystyle\varrho(u)x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle[Tu,x]+T\big(\rho(x)u\big),

(21)

\displaystyle\varpi(u,v)x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,Tu,Tv\right\rrbracket-T\big(D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tu)v-\mu(x,Tv)u\big),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v\in V.

The following proposition gives the explicit formula of
D_{\varrho,\varpi}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Then with the above notations, we have

(22)

\displaystyle D_{\varrho,\varpi}(u,v)x=\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,x\right\rrbracket-T\Big(\mu(Tv,x)u-\mu(Tu,x)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v\in V,~x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Since
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator, by a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle D_{\varrho,\varpi}(u,v)x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{rep}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\varpi(v,u)x-\varpi(u,v)x+[\varrho(u),\varrho(v)]x-\varrho([u,v]_{T})x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{repre1},\eqref{repre2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,Tv,Tu\right\rrbracket-T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tv)u-\mu(x,Tu)v\Big)-\left\llbracket x,Tu,Tv\right\rrbracket+T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tu)v-\mu(x,Tv)u\Big)

\displaystyle+[Tu,[Tv,x]]+[Tu,T(\rho(x)v)]+T(\rho([Tv,x])u)+T(\rho(T(\rho(x)v)u))

\displaystyle-[Tv,[Tu,x]]-[Tv,T(\rho(x)u)]-T(\rho([Tu,x])v)-T(\rho(T(\rho(x)u)v))

\displaystyle-[T(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u),x]-T(\rho(x)\rho(Tu)v)+T(\rho(x)\rho(Tv)u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Ooperator1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,Tv,Tu\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket x,Tu,Tv\right\rrbracket+[Tu,[Tv,x]]-[Tv,[Tu,x]]-[[Tu,Tv],x]

\displaystyle-T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tv)u-\mu(x,Tu)v\Big)+T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tu)v-\mu(x,Tv)u\Big)

\displaystyle+T(\rho(Tu)\rho(x)v-\rho(T(\rho(x)v)u)-T(\rho(Tv)\rho(x)u-\rho(T(\rho(x)u)v)

\displaystyle+T(\rho([Tv,x])u)+T(\rho(T(\rho(x)v)u))-T(\rho([Tu,x])v)-T(\rho(T(\rho(x)u)v))

\displaystyle-T(\rho(x)\rho(Tu)v)+T(\rho(x)\rho(Tv)u)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{LY1},\eqref{rep}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,x\right\rrbracket-T\Big(\mu(Tv,x)u-\mu(Tu,x)v\Big).

The conclusion thus follows.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
.
Then
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho,\varpi)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
, where
\varrho,~\varpi
and

are given by (
20
)-(

We may prove Theorem
3.4
by checking that
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho,\varpi)
satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.5
, but here we will prove it by another way. In order to do this, we should go back some notions in
[
25

. Recall that a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a linear map
N:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\displaystyle[Nx,Ny]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\big([Nx,y]+[x,Ny]-N[x,y]\big),

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket Nx,Ny,Nz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\Big(\left\llbracket Nx,Ny,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Nx,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ny,Nz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-N\left\llbracket Nx,y,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,Ny,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

We denote a pair of deformed brackets
([\cdot,\cdot]_{N},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{N})
by

(23)

\displaystyle[x,y]_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Nx,y]+[x,Ny]-N[x,y],

(24)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Nx,Ny,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Nx,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ny,Nz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-N\left\llbracket Nx,y,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,Ny,z\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,y,Nz\right\rrbracket+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{N})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and

is a Lie-Yamaguti homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{N},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{N})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
:

It is direct to see that if
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
, then

N_{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&T\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}V
. Then by (
23
) and (
24
), we deduce that there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
V\oplus\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
given by for all

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{N_{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[N_{T}(x+u),y+v]_{\rho,\mu}+[x+u,N_{T}(y+v)]_{\rho,\mu}-N_{T}[x+u,y+v]_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,y+v]_{\rho,\mu}+[x+u,Tv]_{\rho,\mu}-N_{T}([x,y]+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,y]+\rho(Tu)v+[x,Tv]-\rho(Tv)u-T(\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}+\varrho(u)y-\varrho(v)x,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{N_{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket N_{T}(x+u),N_{T}(y+v),z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket N_{T}(x+u),y+v,N_{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket x+u,N_{T}(y+v),N_{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle-N_{T}(\left\llbracket N_{T}(x+u),y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket x+u,N_{T}(y+v),z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu})+\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,N_{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket Tu,y+v,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket x+u,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle-N_{T}(\left\llbracket Tu,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket x+u,Tv,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}+\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,z\right\rrbracket+D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\left\llbracket Tu,y,Tw\right\rrbracket-\mu(Tu,Tw)v+\left\llbracket x,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket+\mu(Tv,Tw)u

\displaystyle-T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,y)w-\mu(Tu,z)v+D_{\rho,\mu}(x,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,z)u+\mu(y,Tw)u-\mu(x,Tw)v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T}+D_{\varrho,\varpi}(u,v)z+\varpi(v,w)x-\varpi(u,w)y,

which implies that
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho,\varpi)
is a representation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
. This finishes the proof.∎

Having endowed the vector space

([\cdot,\cdot]_{T},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})

\Big(\oplus_{p=1}^{\infty}C_{\rm LieY}^{p}(V,\mathfrak{g}),(\delta_{\rm I}^{T},\delta_{\rm II}^{T})\Big).

More precisely, if

is given by for any

\displaystyle\Big(\delta^{T}_{\rm I}(f,g)\Big)(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big([Tu_{n+1},g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},v_{n+1})]-[Tv_{n+1},g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},u_{n+1})]

\displaystyle\quad\quad-g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},\rho(Tu_{n+1})v_{n+1}-\rho(Tv_{n+1})u_{n+1})+T\big(\rho(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},v_{n+1}))u_{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-T\big(\rho(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},u_{n+1}))v_{n+1}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k+1}\Big(\left\llbracket Tu_{k},Tv_{k},f(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1})\right\rrbracket+T\big(\mu(Tv_{k},f(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1}))u_{k}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-T\big(\mu(Tu_{k},f(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1}))v_{k}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}f(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{k}\circ\mathcal{V}_{l},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1}),

\displaystyle\Big(\delta^{T}_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\left\llbracket g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},u_{n+1}),Tv_{n+1},Tw\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},v_{n+1}),Tu_{n+1},Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\quad\quad+T\big(D_{\rho,\mu}(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},u_{n+1}),Tv_{n+1})w-\mu(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},u_{n+1}),Tw)v_{n+1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+D_{\rho,\mu}(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},v_{n+1}),Tu_{n+1})w-\mu(g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n},v_{n+1}),Tw)u_{n+1}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\Big(\left\llbracket Tu_{k},Tv_{k},g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},w)\right\rrbracket+T\big(\mu(Tv_{k},g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},w))u_{k}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\mu(Tu_{k},g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},w))v_{k}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{k}\circ\mathcal{V}_{l},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},w)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathcal{V}_{k}},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{n+1},\left\llbracket u_{k},v_{k},w\right\rrbracket_{T}),

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

\displaystyle-T(D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})[u,v]_{T})+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},T[u,v]_{T}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Ooperator1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{LY3},\eqref{RLYe}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{2cochain}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle-\delta(\mathfrak{X})(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T})+D_{\varrho,\varpi}(u,v)\Big(\delta(\mathfrak{X})w\Big)+\varpi(v,w)\Big(\delta(\mathfrak{X})u\Big)-\varpi(u,w)\Big(\delta(\mathfrak{X})v\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{repre1},\eqref{repre2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,TD_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})w-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tw\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket TD_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})u-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tu\right\rrbracket,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket Tu,TD_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})v-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-TD(\mathfrak{X})\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},T\big(D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\big)\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle-T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}\big(TD_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})u-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tu\right\rrbracket,Tv\big)w-D_{\rho,\mu}\big(TD_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})v-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket,Tu\big)w\Big)

\displaystyle T\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(Tu,Tv)D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})w+\mu(Tv,Tw)D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})u-\mu(Tu,Tw)D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{X})v\Big)

\displaystyle 0.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
T

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

n

(26)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{n}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{g}):=\begin{cases}C^{n}_{\rm LieY}(V,\mathfrak{g}),&n\geqslant 1,\\

Define the coboundary map

(27)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}:=\begin{cases}\delta^{T}=(\delta^{T}_{\rm I},\delta^{T}_{\rm II}),&n\geqslant 1,\\
\delta,&n=0.\end{cases}

Thus we obtain a well-defined cochain complex

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{g}):=\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{B}^{n}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{g}),\quad n\geqslant 1.

4.
Deformatons of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we will use the cohomology theory constructed in the former section to characterize deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

4.1.
Linear deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we aim to linear deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and we show that the infinitesimals of two equivalent linear deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on Lie-Yamaguti algebra are in the same cohomology class of the first cohomology group.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. A
homomorphism
from
T^{\prime}
to
T
is a pair
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{V})
, where
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism and
\phi_{V}:V\to V
is a linear map satisfying

(29)

\displaystyle T\circ\phi_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ T^{\prime}

(30)

\displaystyle~\phi_{V}(\rho(x)v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{V}(v),

(31)

\displaystyle~\phi_{V}\mu(x,y)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))(\phi_{V}(v)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V.

In particular, if
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi_{V}
are invertible, then
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{V})
is called an
isomorphism
from
T^{\prime}
to
T
.

By a direct computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
, and
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{V})
a homomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
, then we have

(32)

\displaystyle\phi_{V}D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))(\phi_{V}(v)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V.

In the sequel, we would write
D
for
D_{\rho,\mu}
without ambiguity.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
and
(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{V})
a homomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
. Then
\phi_{V}
is a homomorphism from pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra from
(V,*_{T^{\prime}},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T^{\prime}})
to
(V,*_{T},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T})
.

Proof.

For all
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{V}(u*_{T^{\prime}}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{V}(\rho(T^{\prime}u)v)=\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}u)\phi_{V}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(T(\phi_{V}(u))\phi_{V}(v))=\phi_{V}(u)*_{T}\phi_{V}(v),

\displaystyle\phi_{V}(\{u,v,w\}_{T^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{V}(\mu(T^{\prime}v,T^{\prime}w)u)=\mu(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}v),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}w))(\phi_{V}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(T(\phi_{V}(v)),T(\phi_{V}(w)))(\phi_{V}(u))=\{\phi_{V}(u),\phi_{V}(v),\phi_{V}(w)\}_{T}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

The notion of linear deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators is given as follows.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
for all

\mathfrak{T}
generates a

of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T

It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{T}
generates a linear deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

if and only if

(33)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}u,Tv]+[Tu,\mathfrak{T}v]=T\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}v)u\big)+\mathfrak{T}\big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u\big),

(34)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v]=\mathfrak{T}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}v)u\big),

(36)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)v-\mu(Tu,Tw)v\Big)

\displaystyle+T\Big(D(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v)w+D(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)u

\displaystyle+\mu(\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,\mathfrak{T}w)v-\mu(\mathfrak{T}u,Tw)v\Big),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{I}v,\mathfrak{I}w)v-\mu(\mathfrak{I}u,\mathfrak{I}w)v\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{T}\Big(D(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v)w+D(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)u

\displaystyle+\mu(\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,\mathfrak{T}w)v-\mu(\mathfrak{T}u,Tw)v\Big),

(37)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w\right\rrbracket=\mathfrak{T}\Big(D(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}w)v\Big).

The following can be deduced easily.

(i)

(

\delta^{T}

(ii)

(

\mathfrak{T}

(V;\rho,\mu)

Let
(A,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and let

\omega_{1},~\omega_{2}:\otimes^{3}A\to A

Proposition 4.5
.

If
\mathfrak{T}
generates a linear deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

(40)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{T}}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\mathfrak{T}(u))v,

(41)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}^{1}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)u+\mu(\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)u,

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}_{T}+t\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}^{1}(u,v,w)+t^{2}\omega_{\mathfrak{T}}^{2}(u,v,w).

\displaystyle 0,

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle 0.

Theorem 4.7
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

(V;\rho,\mu)

(51)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},T(D(\mathfrak{X})v)-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=0,\quad\forall v\in V.

We denote by
\mathsf{Nij}(T)
the set of Nijenhuis elements with respect to
T
.

It is obvious that a trivial deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Nijenhuis element. Indeed, the converse is also true.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Then for any Nijenhuis element
\mathfrak{X}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
,
T_{t}:=T+t\mathfrak{T}
with
\mathfrak{T}:=\delta(\mathfrak{X})
is a trivial linear deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
.

One way to prove the above Theorem is to check that
\mathfrak{T}
satisfies Eqs. (
33
)-(
37
). Now, however, we decide to prove it another way. Let us give a lemma first, which follows from a direct computation.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Let
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra isomorphism and
\phi_{V}:V\to V
an isomorphism between vector spaces such that Eqs. (
30
)-(
31
) hold. Then
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ T\circ\phi_{V}:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

Proof of Theorem
4.9
:
For any Nijenhuis element
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathsf{Nij}(T)\subset\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, we define

(52)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}=\delta\mathfrak{X}.

Since
\mathfrak{X}
is a Nijenhuis element, for all
t
,
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}
satisfies

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})\circ T_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\circ({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X})),

\displaystyle~({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X}))\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})(x))({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X}))(v),

\displaystyle~({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X}))\mu(x,y)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})(x),{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})(y))({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X}))(v),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in V.

For
t
sufficiently small, we see that
{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra isomorphism and that
{{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X})
is an isomorphism between vector spaces. Thus, we have

T_{t}=({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})^{-1}\circ T\circ({{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X})).

By Lemma
4.10
, we see that
T_{t}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
for
t
sufficiently small. Thus
\mathfrak{T}=\delta\mathfrak{X}
satisfies Eqs. (
33
)-(
37
). Therefore,
T_{t}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator for all
t
, which implies that
\mathfrak{T}
generates a liner deformation of
T
. It is easy to see that this deformation is trivial.
∎

In the sequel, we are going to consider deformations of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras which is a special case of relative Rota-Baxter operators. Thus the conclusions of Rota-Baxter operators are direct corollaries of the former. We will also give some examples of Nijenhuis elements of Rota-Baxetr operators at the end of this section. Recall that a Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator with respect to the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
, i.e., a linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(53)

\displaystyle[Rx,Ry]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big([Rx,y]-[Ry,x]\Big),

(54)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Rx,Ry,Rz\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big(\left\llbracket Rx,Ry,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ry,Rz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Rx,y,Rz\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
R
be a Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then the induced pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is given by
x*_{R}y=[Rx,y],~\{x,y,z\}_{R}=\left\llbracket x,Ry,Rz\right\rrbracket
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, and its sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure is given by
[x,y]_{R}=[Rx,y]-[Ry,x],~\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{R}=\left\llbracket Rx,Ry,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ry,Rz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Rx,y,Rz\right\rrbracket
for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
R
be a Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then
\varrho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}),~\varpi:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
given by

(55)

\displaystyle\varrho(x)y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Rx,y]-R\Big([x,y]\Big),

(56)

\displaystyle\varpi(x,y)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket z,Rx,Ry\right\rrbracket-R\Big(\left\llbracket z,Rx,y\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket x,Ry,z\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

forms a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{R},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{R})
on itself, where

(57)

\displaystyle D_{\varrho,\varpi}(x,y)z=\left\llbracket Rx,Ry,z\right\rrbracket-R\Big(\left\llbracket x,Ry,z\right\rrbracket-\left\llbracket y,Rx,z\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 4.12
.

Let

Definition 4.13

Let

.

Let

Definition 4.15

At the end of this subsection, we will give two examples of Nijenhuis elements associated to Rota-Baxter operators.

Example 4.16

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

\{e_{1},e_{2}\}

Example 4.17

Let
\mathfrak{g}

4.2.
Formal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we study formal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
be a ring of power series of one variable
t
. For any linear vector space
V
,
V[[t]]
denotes the vector space of formal power series of
t
with the coefficients in
V
. If
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, then there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure over the ring
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
on
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]]
given by

(58)

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=s}\Big[x_{i},y_{j}\Big]t^{s},

(59)

\displaystyle~\small{\left\llbracket\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j},\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z_{k}t^{k}\right\rrbracket}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j+k=s}\left\llbracket x_{i},y_{j},z_{k}\right\rrbracket t^{s},\quad\forall x_{i},y_{j},z_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}.

For any representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, there is a nature representation of the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
on the
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-module
V[[t]]
given by

(60)

\displaystyle\rho\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}v_{k}t^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+k=s}\rho(x_{i})v_{k}t^{s},

(61)

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}v_{k}t^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j+k=s}\mu(x_{i},x_{j})v_{k}t^{s},\quad\forall x_{i},y_{j}\in\mathfrak{g},~v_{k}\in V.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Consider the power series

(62)

\displaystyle T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i},\quad\mathfrak{T}_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(V,\mathfrak{g}),

that is,
T_{t}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathfrak{g})[[t]]=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathfrak{g}[[t]])
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)

T_{t}
is given by (
62

\mathfrak{T}_{0}=T

also satisfies

(63)

\displaystyle[T_{t}u,T_{t}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}\Big(\rho(T_{t}u)v-\rho(T_{t}v)u\Big),

(64)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket T_{t}u,T_{t}v,T_{t}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(T_{t}u,T_{t}v)w+\mu(T_{t}v,T_{t}w)u-\mu(T_{t}u,T_{t}w)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

We say that
T_{t}
is a

of

Recall that a formal deformation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a pair of power series
f_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}t^{i}
and
g_{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{j}t^{j}

f_{0}=[\cdot,\cdot]
and

defines a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
(
[
19
]
). Based on the relationship between the relative Rota-Baxter operators and the pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.19
.

If
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
is a formal deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to
(V;\rho,\mu)
. Then
([\cdot,\cdot]_{T_{t}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T_{t}})

(65)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{i}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{i}v\Big)t^{i},

(66)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=k}\Big(D_{\rho,\mu}(\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{i}v,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)v\Big)t^{k},\quad u,v,w\in V,

.

If

Definition 4.21
.

Definition 4.22
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

(V;\rho,\mu)

Theorem 4.23
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If two formal deformations
\bar{T}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{T}_{i}}t^{i}
and

Let
(\phi_{t},\varphi_{t})
be the maps defined by (
69
), which makes two deformations
\bar{T}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{T}_{i}}t^{i}
and

equivalent. By (

), we have

\bar{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}v=\mathfrak{T}_{1}v+TD(\mathfrak{X})v-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket=\mathfrak{T}_{1}v+\delta(\mathfrak{X})(v),\quad\forall v\in V,

which implies that
\bar{\mathfrak{T}}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
are in the same cohomology classes.
∎

Definition 4.24
.

A relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
is
rigid
if all formal deformations of
T
are trivial.

Proposition 4.25
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If
\mathcal{Z}^{1}(V,\mathfrak{g})=\delta(\mathsf{Nij}(T))
, then
T
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be a formal deformation of
T
, then Proposition
4.20
gives
\mathfrak{T}_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
. By the assumption,
\mathfrak{T}_{1}=\delta(\mathfrak{X})
for some
\mathfrak{X}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
. Then setting
\phi_{t}={{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}},~\varphi_{t}={{\rm{Id}}}_{V}+tD(\mathfrak{X})
, we get a formal deformation

\bar{T}_{t}:=\phi_{t}^{-1}\circ T_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}.

Thus
\bar{T}_{t}
is equivalent to
T_{t}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({{\rm{Id}}}-\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}}t+(\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})^{2}t^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{i}(\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{X}})^{i}t^{i}+\cdots)(T_{t}(v+tD(\mathfrak{X})v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tv+(\mathfrak{T}_{1}v+T(D(\mathfrak{X})v)-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket)t+\bar{\mathfrak{T}}_{2}vt^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tv+\bar{\mathfrak{T}}_{2}(v)t^{2}+\cdots.

Repeating this procedure, we get that
T_{t}
is equivalent to
T
.
∎

4.3.
Higher order deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we will introduce a special cohomology class associated to an order
n
deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator, and show that a deformation of order
n
of a relative Rota-Baxter operators is extendable if and only if this cohomology class in the second cohomology group is trivial. Thus we call this cohomology class the obstruction class of a deformation of order
n
being extendable.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
with
\mathfrak{T}_{0}=T
,
\mathfrak{T}_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathfrak{g})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, defines a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module from
V[t]/(t^{n+1})
to the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
satisfying

(73)

\displaystyle[T_{t}u,T_{t}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}(\rho(T_{t})u-\rho(T_{t}v)u),

(74)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket T_{t}u,T_{t}v,T_{t}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}(D_{\rho,\mu}(T_{t}u,T_{t}v)w+\mu(T_{t}v,T_{t}w)u-\mu(T_{t}u,T_{t}w)v),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V,

we say that
T_{t}
is an
order
n
deformation
of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
.

Remark 4.27
.

The left hand side of Eqs. (
73
) and (
74
) hold in the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
and the right hand side of Eqs. (
73
) and (
74
) make sense since
T_{t}
is a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module map.

Definition 4.28
.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If there exists a
1
-cochain
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathfrak{g})
such that
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
T
, then we say that
T_{t}
is
extendable
.

The following theorem is the third key conclusion in this section.

Theorem 4.29
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
, and
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. Then
T_{t}
is extendable if and only if the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(V,\mathfrak{g})
is trivial, where
\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=(\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T},\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{2}(V,\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

(75)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 1}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2}]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1})v_{2}-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2})v_{1})\Big),

(76)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1,\atop n\geqslant i,j,k\geqslant 0}\Big(\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{i}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3}\right\rrbracket-\mathfrak{T}_{i}(D(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{2})v_{3}

\displaystyle+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3})v_{1}-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3})v_{2}\Big),\quad\forall v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{T_{t}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be the extension of
T_{t}
, then for all
u,v,w\in V
,

(77)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{T_{t}}\Big(\rho(\widetilde{T_{t}}u)v-\rho(\widetilde{T_{t}}v)u\Big),

(78)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v,\widetilde{T_{t}}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{T_{t}}\Big(D(\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v)w+\mu(\widetilde{T_{t}}v,\widetilde{T_{t}}w)u-\mu(\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}w)v\Big).

Expanding the Eq.(
77
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 0}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)\Big)=0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 1}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)\Big)+[\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv]+[Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v]

\displaystyle\quad-\big(T(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v)u)+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u)\big)=0,

i.e.,

(79)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T}+\delta_{\rm I}^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})=0.

Similarly, expanding the Eq.(
78
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

(80)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T}+\delta_{\rm II}^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})=0.

From (
79
) and (
80
), we get

\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=-\delta^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}).

Thus, the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]
is trivial.

Conversely, suppose that the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]
is trivial, then there exists
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
, such that
\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=-\delta^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}).
Set
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
. Then for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant n+1
,
\widetilde{T_{t}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=s}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=s}\Big(\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\right\rrbracket-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(D(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)v\big)\Big)=0.

which implies that
\widetilde{T_{t}}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
T
. Hence it is a extension of
T_{t}
.
∎

Definition 4.30
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
, and
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. Then the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(V,\mathfrak{g})
defined in Theorem
4.29
is called the
obstruction class
of
T_{t}
being extendable.

Corollary 4.31
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho,\mu)
. If
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(V,\mathfrak{g})=0
, then every
1
-cocycle in
\mathcal{Z}_{T}^{1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
is the infinitesimal of some formal deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
.

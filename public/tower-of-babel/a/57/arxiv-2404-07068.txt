Entanglement entropy in the ground state of non-interacting massless Dirac fermions in dimension one
Fabrizio Ferro, Paul Pfeiffer and Wolfgang Spitzer

Abstract
. We present a novel proof of a formula of Casini and Huerta for the entanglement entropy of the ground state of non-interacting massless Dirac fermions in dimension one localized to (a union of) intervals and generalize it to the case of Rényi entropies. At first, we prove that these entropies are well-defined for non-intersecting intervals. This is accomplished by an inequality of Alexander V. Sobolev. Then we compute this entropy using a trace formula for Wiener–Hopf operators by Harold Widom. For intersecting intervals, we discuss an extended entropy formula of Casini and Huerta and support this with a proof for polynomial test functions (instead of entropy).

1.
Introduction

We consider non-interacting, massless relativistic Dirac fermions on the real line. For simplicity, we may assume that these fermions are spinless. The single-particle Hamiltonian of this Fermi system is given by the momentum operator
-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x
, which acts self-adjointly on the Sobolev space,
\mathsf{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. The (pure) ground state of fermions is characterized by the (orthogonal) projection operator
P\coloneqq 1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)
on the Hilbert space
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
of square-integrable, complex-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, or equivalently,
P
is the spectral projection of
-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x
onto the positive (infinite) Fermi sea,
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\coloneqq\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}:\xi\geq 0\}
. Below in (
1.6
), this operator
P
will be denoted by
\mathrm{Op}(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}})
. It is related to the Hilbert transform
\mathrm{H}
on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(see
[
18
, p. 26]
), which is given by the convolution with
x\mapsto 1/(\pi x)
, that is,
(\mathrm{H}\varphi)(x)\coloneqq\pi^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,\varphi(y)/(x-y)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, interpreted as a principal-value integral. The simple relation between
P
and
\mathrm{H}
is

P=\frac{1}{2}\mathds{1}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{H}\,,

(1.1)

where
\mathds{1}
is the identity operator on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Therefore,
P
has the integral kernel

\displaystyle P(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\delta(x-y)-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{1}{x-y}\,,\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(1.2)

As a pure state,
P
has absolute entropy zero. Given some (bounded or possibly unbounded) Borel subset
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}
we reduce this ground state locally to
\Lambda
and define on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
the operator

P(\Lambda)\coloneqq 1_{\Lambda}{P}1_{\Lambda}\,,

(1.3)

where we use the same symbol,
1_{\Lambda}
, for the indicator function of
\Lambda
and for the multiplication operator by
1_{\Lambda}
in position space. To this end, let
X
be the space multiplication operator informally defined as
(X\varphi)(x)\coloneqq x\varphi(x)
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for suitable
\varphi\in\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then we identify
1_{\Lambda}
with
1_{\Lambda}(X)
. More generally, let
f\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be a bounded, measurable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
, then the bounded operator
f(X)
on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is defined, as usual, by

\big(f(X)\varphi\big)(x)\coloneqq f(x)\varphi(x)\,,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,\varphi\in\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\,.

(1.4)

The operator
P(\Lambda)
in (
1.3
) characterizes the ground state reduced locally to
\Lambda
in the sense that all its expectation values are determined by the inner products
\langle f,P(\Lambda)g\rangle
, for
f,g\in\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and the Wick rule or quasi-free property to compute higher order correlation functions in terms of a determinant. See, for example
[
11
, Chapter 4]
,
[
15
,
20
]
for details. For general
\Lambda
, the operator
P(\Lambda)
is not a projection and no longer corresponds to a pure state of fermions but it satisfies
0\leq P(\Lambda)\leq\mathds{1}
. Informally, we denote the absolute (von-Neumann) entropy of the ground state localized to
\Lambda
by
S(\Lambda)\coloneqq{\rm tr}\,h_{1}(P(\Lambda))
, see (
1.17
) for the definition of
h_{1}
. However, for general
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, the operator
h_{1}(P(\Lambda))
is positive but not trace class and thus
S(\Lambda)
is infinite. Therefore, we consider certain relative entropies or entropy differences. Let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be another Borel subset disjoint from
\Lambda
. Our goal is to understand a quantity which is informally the sum of the individual entropies
S(\Lambda)+S(\Lambda^{\prime})
minus the entropy
S(\Lambda\cup\Lambda^{\prime})
of the union. Below we denote this by
{\rm tr}\,\Delta(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};h_{1})
with the von-Neumann entropy function
h_{1}
defined in (
1.17
), where

\Delta(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)\coloneqq f\big(P(\Lambda)\big)+f\big(P(\Lambda^{\prime})\big)-f\big(P(\Lambda\cup\Lambda^{\prime})\big)\,.

(1.5)

It turns out that (under certain assumptions on
\Lambda
,
\Lambda^{\prime}
and
f
, see
Assumptions 1.1
)
\Delta(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)
is a trace-class operator. For
f=h_{1}
, it is a measure of entanglement of the ground state localized to
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
. We refer to this as the von-Neumann entanglement entropy and denote this by
\mathsf{EE}(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})
. Another frequently used and maybe more appropriate name for this entanglement entropy is mutual information. Casini and Huerta computed this entropy difference explicitly (for the case of a union of intervals and the von-Neumann entropy) and therefore we refer to this as the Casini–Huerta formula. Later, using the same integral representation for the von-Neumann entropy function, Longo and Xu (rigorously) proved this formula. More recently, using the Cauchy formula and the so-called replica-trick, Blanco et al.
[
2
]
have computed the integer Rényi entanglement entropies, that is,
{\rm tr}\,\Delta(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};h_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with the Rényi entropy function
h_{n}
defined in (
1.16
).

In our approach proving the trace-class property boils down to showing that
1_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Op}(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}})1_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
lies in the Schatten–von Neumann class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p>0
and employing an inequality by A.V. Sobolev that reduces the entropy difference to the
p
(quasi-)norm of
1_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Op}(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}})1_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
.

We are not aware of a (rigorous) generalization of the approach by Casini–Huerta (and the method of proof of Longo–Xu) to all Rényi entropies. We do mention our generalization to Rényi entropies with Rényi index
\alpha<1
, see
Subsection 4.1
. Another drawback of Casini–Huerta’s method is that it relies on the explicit knowledge of the resolvent of
P(\Lambda)
, which is usually not available explicitly and the main reason why there is no such simple formula in the ground state of massive Dirac fermions. See
[
21
]
for the latest developments on massive free Dirac fermions in one spatial dimension. At this point we also mention the mathematical progress on entanglement entropy for free Dirac fermions in higher spatial dimensions in the recent papers
[
4
,
9
]
.

The operator

\displaystyle W(a)\coloneqq{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}\mathrm{Op}(a){1}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}

, and

(2)

2.
Trace-class properties

We say that a compact operator
A
on a (separable) Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is in the Schatten–von Neumann class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for some
p>0
if its singular values,
s_{n}(A),n\in\mathbb{N}
, form a sequence in
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
. The singular values
s_{n}(A)
of
A
are the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{A^{*}A}
. We arrange them in decreasing order,
s_{n}(A)\geq s_{n+1}(A)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We equip
\mathcal{S}_{p}
with the (quasi-)norm

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\coloneqq\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(A)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\,,\quad A\in\mathcal{S}_{p}\,.

(2.1)

For
p\geq 1
, the space
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is then a normed space, while for
p\in(0,1)
it is a so-called quasi-normed space since this quasi-norm does not satisfy the usual triangle inequality but instead the so-called
p
-triangle inequality,
\|A_{1}+A_{2}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}\leq\|A_{1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}+\|A_{2}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}
. The vector space
\mathcal{S}_{p}
forms an ideal in the sense that if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
and
A\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
then
S_{1}AS_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
with (the Hölder inequality)
\|S_{1}AS_{2}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq\|S_{1}\|\|S_{2}\|\|A\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm. We frequently use that the adjoint operator
A^{*}
of
A
has the same
p
-quasi norm as
A
, that is,
\|A^{*}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}=\|A\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
for all
p>0
. The space
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is the ideal of trace-class operators and a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
. If an operator
A\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then we say that
A
is trace class. The ideal of compact operators may be written as
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
. As a general reference to the above we recommend
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.

For fixed operator
A
, the
p
(quasi-)norm
\|A\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
is increasing with decreasing
p
. So it is harder to prove an upper bound on
\|A\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
if we lower
p
.

Besides the projection
P=1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}(-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)
, it is convenient from a notational point of view to introduce the projections
Q_{1}\coloneqq 1_{I_{1}}(X)
,
Q_{2}\coloneqq 1_{I_{2}}(X)
and
Q\coloneqq Q_{1}+Q_{2}=1_{I}(X)
with
I=I_{1}\cup I_{2}
. Then, assuming
f(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)

\displaystyle=f\big(P(I_{1})\big)+f\big(P(I_{2})\big)-f\big(P(I)\big)=f(Q_{1}PQ_{1})+f(Q_{2}PQ_{2})-f(QPQ)

\displaystyle=f(Q_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2})-f(QPQ)\,,

(2.2)

since
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\emptyset
, or equivalently
Q_{1}Q_{2}=0
. This is a crucial identity. If we took the right-hand side, that is,
f(Q_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2})-f(QPQ)
, as our definition of
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
then we would not need to assume
f(0)=0
.

2.1.
Polynomial test functions

In the simplest (non-trivial) case we consider the linear function
f(t)=t
in
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
. Then,

\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t)=-T-T^{*}

with the operator
T\coloneqq Q_{1}PQ_{2}:\mathsf{L}^{2}(I_{2})\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(I_{1})
and its adjoint operator
T^{*}
, both extended trivially (by
0
) to act on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. According to (
1.2
),
T
has integral kernel

T(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{1}{x-y}\,,\quad x\in I_{1}\,,y\in I_{2}

(2.3)

since the intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
have a positive distance. For the same reason, this kernel is not singular (in fact, arbitrarily often differentiable) and the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of
T
is bounded, that is,

\big\|Q_{1}PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{I_{1}}\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2}}<\infty\,.

The operator
T
is not only in the Hilbert–Schmidt class
\mathcal{S}_{2}
but in any Schatten–von Neumann class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p>0
. Since we use this statement a number of times we formulate this in the following

LEMMA 2.1
.

The operator
T
with integral kernel defined in (
2.3
) and with the intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
satisfying
Assumptions 1.1
is in any Schatten–von Neumann class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p>0
. More generally, let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be bounded, measurable functions with supports in the closures
\widebar{I_{1}}
and
\widebar{I_{2}}
, respectively. Then, the operator
f_{1}(X)Pf_{2}(X)
is in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
with
\big\|f_{1}(X)Pf_{2}(X)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(I_{1})}\,\|f_{2}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(I_{2})}\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
for any
p>0
. See (
1.4
) for the definition of
f_{j}(X)
.

Proof.

Let us first get rid of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. We use
f_{j}(X)=f_{j}(X)Q_{j}=Q_{j}f_{j}(X)
, the Hölder inequality and the fact that the operator norm
\|f_{j}(X)\|
equals the
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}
-norm
\|f_{j}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(I_{j})}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\big\|f_{1}(X)Pf_{2}(X)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\big\|f_{1}(X)Q_{1}PQ_{2}f_{2}(X)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq\|f_{1}(X)\|\,\big\|Q_{1}PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\,\|f_{2}(X)\|

\displaystyle=\|f_{1}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(I_{1})}\,\|f_{2}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(I_{2})}\big\|Q_{1}PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\,,

(2.4)

which is the claimed inequality. We still have to show that
T\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
. To this end, we may slightly enlarge
I_{1}
to
\widetilde{I_{1}}
so that
\widetilde{I_{1}}
is bounded and
\widetilde{I_{1}}
still has a positive distance to
{I_{2}}
. Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\widetilde{I_{1}})
be a cutoff function such that
\varphi(x)=1
for
x\in I_{1}
. Then,
Q_{1}=Q_{1}\varphi(X)
and
T=Q_{1}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}
. The (standard) trick is to insert the operator
\mathds{1}_{1}=(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}
between
Q_{1}
and
\varphi(X)P
, where
\mathds{1}_{1}
is the identity operator on
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I_{1}})
and
A
is a differential operator on
\widetilde{I_{1}}
whose eigenvalues are known explicitly and the integer
\beta>0
is properly chosen. A natural candidate for this is the Laplacian
A\coloneqq-\mathrm{d}^{2}/\mathrm{d}x^{2}
on
\widetilde{I_{1}}
, say with Neumann boundary conditions. The inverse
(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-1}
is well-defined since
\mathds{1}_{1}+A
is a strictly positive operator and can be inverted on
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I_{1}})
. Thus, the (integer) power
(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}=\big((\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-1}\big)^{\beta}
is well-defined on
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I_{1}})
. This operator may be extended by 0 to define an operator on
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We avoid this by including the operator
\widetilde{Q_{1}}\coloneqq 1_{\widetilde{I_{1}}}(X)
to the right of
(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}
. Then, we write

Q_{1}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}=Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}=Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}\widetilde{Q_{1}}\cdot\widetilde{Q_{1}}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}\,.

The eigenvalues (equal to the singular values) of
A
are
(n\pi/|\widetilde{I_{1}}|)^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,

\big\|Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}\widetilde{Q_{1}}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}^{p}\leq 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\left(\frac{n\pi}{|\widetilde{I_{1}}|}\right)^{2}\right)^{-\beta p}<1+(|\widetilde{I_{1}}|/\pi)^{2\beta p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2\beta p}<\infty\,,

(2.5)

if
\beta>1/(2p)
. The operator
\widetilde{Q_{1}}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}
is bounded, in fact Hilbert–Schmidt. We remark that the operator
\varphi(X)
is required in order to satisfy the boundary conditions of
A
. We estimate its Hilbert–Schmidt norm,

\displaystyle\big\|\widetilde{Q_{1}}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\beta}{\beta\choose k}\big\|\widetilde{Q_{1}}A^{k}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\beta}{\beta\choose k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}{2k\choose\ell}\left\lVert\varphi^{(2k-\ell)}\right\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\widetilde{I_{1}})}\left(\int_{\widetilde{I_{1}}}\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left|\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial x^{\ell}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)(x-y)}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\beta}{\beta\choose k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k}{2k\choose\ell}\left\lVert\varphi^{(2k-\ell)}\right\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\widetilde{I_{1}})}\frac{\ell!}{2\pi}\left(\int_{\widetilde{I_{1}}}\mathrm{d}x\int_{I_{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left|\frac{1}{(x-y)^{1+\ell}}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\eqqcolon N_{\beta,\varphi}\,.

(2.6)

This
N_{\beta,\varphi}
is finite as long as
\widetilde{I_{1}}
and
I_{2}
are separated by a positive distance and
\widetilde{I_{1}}
is bounded, which they are. Altogether, the operator
T
is the product of an operator in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
and a bounded operator and therefore in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
with

\displaystyle\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}\widetilde{Q_{1}}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\,\big\|\widetilde{Q_{1}}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}\widetilde{Q_{1}}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\,\big\|\widetilde{Q_{1}}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{\beta}\varphi(X)PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|Q_{1}(\mathds{1}_{1}+A)^{-\beta}\widetilde{Q_{1}}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\,N_{\beta,\varphi}<\infty\,,

(2.7)

if
\beta>1/(2p)
. Here, we used the Hölder inequality and monotonicity of the norms.
∎

As a direct consequence of this lemma, the operator
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p>0
and

\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}\leq 2^{1/p}\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{p}}

for
p\leq 1
. A fancy version of our down-to-earth bounds are the bounds by Birman–Solomyak, see
[
1
]
.

Let us treat one more example explicitly, namely the quadratic polynomial
f(t)=t^{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t^{2})

\displaystyle=Q_{1}PQ_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2}PQ_{2}-(Q_{1}+Q_{2})P(Q_{1}+Q_{2})P(Q_{1}+Q_{2})

\displaystyle=-\big(T^{*}T+TT^{*}+Q_{1}PT+T^{*}PQ_{1}+TPQ_{2}+Q_{2}PT^{*}\big)\,.

Again, by the ideal properties, all operators in the sum are in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
and so is
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t^{2})
with the estimate
\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t^{2})\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq 6\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
and a similar bound for general
p\leq 1
.

This generalizes to arbitrary polynomials
f(t)=t^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N},m\geq 3
. If we expand the powers in
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
then it is a sum of
2^{m+1}-2
, terms which all include at least one factor of
T
or
T^{*}
. As a result we obtain for general monomials,

\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq(2^{m+1}-2)\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\,,

(2.8)

which extends to arbitrary polynomials
f
(with
f(0)=0
) by the triangle inequality. This is usually not a good bound and certainly not for our next step when we consider functions
f
satisfying
Assumptions 1.1
. Since the integral kernel is smooth we can calculate the trace of
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
by the integral over the diagonal of its integral kernel.

The same steps can be performed to prove that
D(I_{1},I_{2};f)
is trace class for any monomial
f
. Clearly,
D(I_{1},I_{2};t)=0
and

W\big(f(1_{I_{1}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x))\big)+W\big(f(1_{I_{2}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x))\big)-W\big(f(1_{I_{1}\cup I_{2}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x))\big)=0\,,

for any (bounded, measurable) function
f
with
f(0)=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle D(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})=\big(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}1_{I_{1}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}\big)^{m}+\big(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}1_{I_{2}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}\big)^{m}-\big(1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}1_{I_{1}\cup I_{2}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}\big)^{m}\,.

As above we introduce the operator
\tilde{T}\coloneqq 1_{I_{1}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x)1_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}(X)1_{I_{2}}(-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x):\mathsf{L}^{2}(I_{2})\to\mathsf{L}^{2}(I_{1})
, which acts in Fourier space. It has the integral kernel

\tilde{T}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{1}{\xi-\eta}\,,\quad\xi\in I_{1}\,,\eta\in I_{2}

and is in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p>0
. Expanding the powers of
D(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})
we always have
\tilde{T}
or its adjoint
\tilde{T}^{*}
as a factor and hence
D(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})
is in
\mathcal{S}_{p}
as well.

2.2.
General test functions satisfying
Assumptions 1.1

In the general case we use a theorem by Alexander V. Sobolev
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
. It applies in his notation to
n=2,q\leq 1
,
\sigma\in(0,1]
,
(2-\sigma)^{-1}<q
,
\sigma<\gamma
and self-adjoint operators
A,B
such that
A-B\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma q}
. Then, for
f\in\mathsf{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{x_{0}\})\cap\mathsf{C}({\mathbb{R}})
with support in
[x_{0}-R,x_{0}+R]
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
R>0
,

\|f(A)-f(B)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}\leq CR^{\gamma-\sigma}\|f\|_{x_{0},\gamma}\,\||A-B|^{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}\,,

(2.9)

where the constant
C
is independent of the operators
A,B
, of the function
f
and of the parameter
R
. We recall the norm
\|f\|_{x_{0},\gamma}
from (
1.19
),

\|f\|_{x_{0},\gamma}=\max_{0\leq k\leq 2}\sup\big\{|f^{(k)}(x)||x-x_{0}|^{-\gamma+k}:x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{x_{0}\}\big\}\,.

In what follows, we adhere to the convention of representing any constant present in the inequalities by the symbol
C
, even if its value changes from line to line. At this point, we want to remark that this norm is submultiplicative in the following way: Let
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathsf{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{x_{0}\})\cap\mathsf{C}({\mathbb{R}})
for some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, we have

\|g_{1}g_{2}\|_{x_{0},\gamma}\leq C\|g_{1}\|_{x_{0},\gamma}\|g_{2}\|_{x_{0},0}\,,

(2.10)

as can be seen with the product rule on
gh
and the reordering of factors,

\big|g_{1}^{(k_{1})}(x)g_{2}^{(k_{2})}(x)\big|\,|x-x_{0}|^{-\gamma+k_{1}+k_{2}}=\big|g_{1}^{(k_{1})}(x)\big|\,|x-x_{0}|^{-\gamma+k_{1}}\times\big|g_{2}^{(k_{2})}(x)\big|\,|x-x_{0}|^{-0+k_{2}}

for
0\leq k_{1}+k_{2}\leq k\in\{0,1,2\}
.

To extend this to the slightly more general functions in
Assumptions 1.1
with
|\mathcal{X}|>1
, we use the sum representation
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
, where
\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}<\infty
and the
q
-triangle inequality to deduce

\displaystyle\big\|f(A)-f(B)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big\|f_{i}(A)-f_{i}(B)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}^{q}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big\|f_{i}\big\|_{x_{i},\gamma}^{q}\,\big\||A-B|^{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}^{q}\,.

Applying convexity of
x\mapsto x^{1/q}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
(since
q\leq 1
) we get

\displaystyle\|f(A)-f(B)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{1/q-1}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\Big)\||A-B|^{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{q}}\,.

We choose
A\coloneqq Q_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2}
and
B\coloneqq QPQ
with
Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
(as above). We do not need this here but with this inequality we can even show that
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};h_{\alpha})\in\mathcal{S}_{q}
for any
q>1/2
by choosing
0<\sigma<2-1/q
and
\sigma<\alpha
.

We set
q\coloneqq 1
. Let us begin by simplifying the right-hand side of the last inequality, specifically, we note
A-B=-T-T^{*}
and thus

\||A-B|^{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=\|A-B\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}=\|-T-T^{*}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}\leq 2\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}<\infty\,.

Thus, we have shown

\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=\|f(A)-f(B)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq C\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\Big)\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}<\infty\,,

(2.11)

which means that
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
is trace class.

To compute the trace of
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
for general
f
, we will split
f
into a sum of a
\mathsf{C}^{2}
-function, which we will take care of in the following section and a function, which yields a small trace norm. Consider a smooth cutoff function
\zeta\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((-1,1))
with
\zeta(x)=1
for
x\in[-1/2,1/2]
and for
i=1,\dots,n,\delta\in(0,1)
, and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\zeta_{i,\delta}(x)\coloneqq\zeta\big((x-x_{i})/\delta\big)\,.

Thus,
\zeta_{i,\delta}\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}((x_{i}-\delta,x_{i}+\delta))
,
\zeta_{i,\delta}(t)=1
for
x_{i}-\delta/2\leq x\leq x_{i}+\delta/2
and
\|\zeta_{i,\delta}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\leq C\delta^{-k}
for
k=0,1,2
. In particular,
\|\zeta_{i,\delta}\|_{x_{i},0}\leq C
. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we define
f_{i,\delta}(x)\coloneqq f_{i}(x)\zeta_{i,\delta}(x)
and
f_{0,\delta}(x)\coloneqq f(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i,\delta}(x)
. We point out that
f_{0,\delta}\in\mathsf{C}^{2}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
as the functions
x\mapsto f_{i}(x)\big(1-\zeta_{i,\delta}(x)\big)
are
\mathsf{C}^{2}
-smooth. Due to the submultiplicativity (
2.10
), we conclude

\|f_{i,\delta}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\leq C\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\,\|\zeta_{i,\delta}\|_{x_{i},0}\leq C\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\,.

Thus, as
f_{i,\delta}
has its support inside
[x_{i}-\delta,x_{i}+\delta]
, we can conclude with (
2.9
)

\displaystyle\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f-f_{0,\delta})\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f_{i,\delta})\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}C\delta^{\gamma-\sigma}\|f_{i,\delta}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\,\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{\gamma-\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{x_{i},\gamma}\,\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}\,<\infty\,.

Because
\gamma>\sigma
we get that

{\rm tr}\,\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}{\rm tr}\,\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f_{0,\delta})\,.

(2.12)

Since we know the trace with smooth test functions such as
f_{0,\delta}
, we can perform the limit and obtain our main result.

3.
Computation of the trace for sufficiently smooth test functions

3.1.
Fourier representation

The actual computation of the trace of
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
is done by relating it to the Widom formula for the trace of a “smooth” version of
D(I_{1},I_{2};f)
. We use the standard Fourier representation

\displaystyle f(A)-f(B)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,t\hat{f}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}stA}(A-B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(1-s)B}

(3.1)

for bounded operators
A,B
and functions
f
such that the
\mathsf{L}^{1}
-norm of
t\hat{f}
,

\|t\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,|t\hat{f}(t)|

is finite. Here is a quick proof of this representation. We write

f(x)-f(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,\hat{f}(t)\Big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tx}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}ty}\Big)\,,\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

and use Duhamel’s formula,

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tA}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tB}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(sA+(1-s)B)}=\mathrm{i}t\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\,\Big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tsA}(A-B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t(1-s)B}\Big)\,.

Altogether, under the condition
\|t\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty
, we proved the representation (
3.1
).

If, in addition
A-B
is trace class, then
f(A)-f(B)
is also trace class and the estimate

\|f(A)-f(B)\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\|t\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|A-B\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

follows.

Before we continue we make the following

REMARK 3.1
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathsf{C}^{\beta+1}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{R}})
for
\beta\in\{1,2\}
. Then, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we get

\displaystyle\|t^{\beta}\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1+t^{2})^{\beta/2}\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|(1+t^{2})^{(\beta+1)/2}\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,\|1/\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1+t^{2})^{(\beta+1)/2}\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,C\leq C\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\beta+1}({\mathbb{R}})}\,.

(3.2)

The last Sobolev norm,
\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}^{\beta+1}({\mathbb{R}})}
, is finite, which implies
\|t^{\beta}\hat{f}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty
.

With this Fourier representation, we can show (again) that for
f\in\mathsf{C}^{2}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
, the operator
\Delta(I_{1},I_{2};f)
is trace class if we use
A=Q_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2}
and
B=QPQ
as above. This is a side remark but this representation will be used again shortly.

3.2.
Smooth version and the relation to Widom’s formula

Let us introduce smooth versions
Q_{1,\varepsilon}
and
Q_{2,\varepsilon}
of the projections
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
, respectively of the corresponding indicator functions. To this end, for any
0<\varepsilon<\operatorname{dist}(I_{1},I_{2})/4
, we define the intervals
I_{j,\varepsilon}\coloneqq(a_{j}+\varepsilon,b_{j}-\varepsilon)\subset I_{j}
for
j=1,2
. Let
\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}\in\mathsf{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be cutoff functions such that
\varphi_{j,\varepsilon}\equiv 0
on
I_{j}^{\complement}
,
\varphi_{j,\varepsilon}\equiv 1
on
I_{j,\varepsilon}
, and
0\leq\varphi_{j,\varepsilon}\leq 1
for
j=1,2
. Thus,
\varphi_{j,\varepsilon}
converges pointwise to
1_{I_{j}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We may write
Q_{1,\varepsilon}=\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}(X)
and
Q_{2,\varepsilon}=\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}(X)
to denote the multiplication operators by the functions
\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}
and
\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}
. We also set
Q_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq Q_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon}=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(X)
with the function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}+\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}
.

Let

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon},\quad B_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon}\,,

(3.3)

and
A=Q_{1}PQ_{1}+Q_{2}PQ_{2}
and
B=QPQ
as above.

The operator
f(A)-f(B)
is trace-class since
A-B=-Q_{1}PQ_{2}-Q_{2}PQ_{1}=-T-T^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
by
Lemma 2.1
. The same is true for the operator
f(A_{\varepsilon})-f(B_{\varepsilon})
since
A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}=-\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}(X)P\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}(X)-\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}(X)P\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}(X)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
by the general version of
Lemma 2.1
with
f_{1}=\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}
and
f_{2}=\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}
.

Using (
3.1
), we write

\displaystyle[f(A_{\varepsilon})-

\displaystyle f(B_{\varepsilon})-f(A)+f(B)]

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,t\hat{f}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\,\left(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}stA_{\varepsilon}}(A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-s)tB_{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}stA}(A-B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-s)tB}\right).

(3.5)

We want to show that this integral converges to
0
in trace norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
. As
\lVert t\hat{f}(t)\rVert_{\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}<\infty
, by dominated convergence, it suffices to show that, in trace norm, the integrand is uniformly bounded and converges pointwise to
0
. To this end, we will use the following

LEMMA 3.2
.

Let
S_{n},Z_{n}
be two sequences of uniformly (in operator norm) bounded operators on a Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})
and let
Z_{n}
converge to
0
strongly. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{R}
be a trace class operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then, the operator sequences
Z_{n}\mathcal{R}S_{n}
and
S_{n}\mathcal{R}Z_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and converge to
0
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

For the sake of completeness, we provide a quick proof of this well-known fact. See
[
10
, Theorem A.1]
for a slightly different proof.

Proof.

As
(Z_{n}\mathcal{R}S_{n})^{*}=S_{n}^{*}\mathcal{R}^{*}Z_{n}^{*}
, it suffices to show that the claim holds for
Z_{n}\mathcal{R}S_{n}
. We observe (recall,
\|Z\|
stands for the operator norm of
Z
)

\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}S_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|S_{n}\|\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z_{n}\|\,\|\mathcal{R}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,\|S_{n}\|\,.

Thus, the sequence is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and it suffices to show that
Z_{n}\mathcal{R}\to 0
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. For this, let
(\psi_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of the kernel of
\mathcal{R}
, which recovers the singular values of
\mathcal{R}
, meaning
\|\mathcal{R}\psi_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=s_{m}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
P_{m}
be the projection onto
\psi_{m}
. We shall now use the triangle inequality to obtain

\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=\Big\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}P_{m}\Big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}P_{m}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z_{n}\mathcal{R}\psi_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

As
n\to\infty
, the
m^{\rm{th}}
entry of this sum converges to
0
, as
Z_{n}
converges strongly to
0
. As the
m^{\rm{th}}
entry is always bounded by
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Z_{n}\|\,s_{m}(\mathcal{R})
, the sum converges to
0
by dominated convergence.
∎

Let us get back to the integrand in (
3.5
). Let
\tau\coloneqq st
and
\tau^{\prime}\coloneqq(1-s)t
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}(A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A}(A-B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}(A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}-A+B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A})(A-B)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A}(A-B)(\mathrm{e}^{i\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B})\,.

(3.8)

We shall first see that the second term (
3.7
) is of the form mentioned above. The operator
A-B
is trace class. We note that
A_{\varepsilon}
converges to
A
strongly as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This is because as a multiplication operator,
(\varphi_{\varepsilon}-1_{I})\psi
converges in
\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
to 0 for any
\psi\in\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Therefore, also
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}
tends to
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A}
strongly. Moreover, the sequence
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A}
is uniformly bounded by 2. Finally,
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}
is a unitary operator. Thus, we can apply
Lemma 3.2
for this summand. For the third term (
3.8
) the same arguments apply.

Finally, for the trace norm of the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.6
) we estimate

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau A_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle(A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}-A+B)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}B_{\varepsilon}}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\big\|A_{\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}-A+B\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}-Q_{1}PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\big\|Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}-Q_{2}PQ_{1}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle=2\big\|Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}-Q_{1}PQ_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big\|(Q_{1,\varepsilon}-Q_{1})Q_{1}PQ_{2}Q_{2,\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+2\big\|Q_{1}PQ_{2}(Q_{2,\varepsilon}-Q_{2})\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\,.

We used that
Q_{j,\varepsilon}=Q_{j,\varepsilon}Q_{j}
, as
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{j,\varepsilon})\subset I_{j}
. Here, the operator
\mathcal{R}=Q_{1}PQ_{2}=T
is trace class by
Lemma 2.1
. The uniform boundedness follows as
0\leq\varphi_{j,\varepsilon}\leq 1
and we already established the strong convergence of
Q_{j,\varepsilon}-Q_{j}
to
0
. Thus, the terms inside the trace norm converge to
0
in trace norm due to
Lemma 3.2
and thus, we have shown that the integrand in (
3.5
) converges pointwise to
0
in trace norm and is uniformly bounded in trace norm, which implies that the integral converges to
0
using dominated convergence.

For the next step we use the following

LEMMA 3.3
.

Suppose that
\hat{f}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
,
t\mapsto t^{2}\hat{f}(t)\in\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
f(0)=0
. Then, with the above definitions of
Q_{1,\varepsilon}
,
Q_{2,\varepsilon}
,
Q_{\varepsilon}=Q_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon}
, and
P
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,

\displaystyle\big[f(Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon})+f(Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon})-f(Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon})\big]

(3.9)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[f(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)+f(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)-f(PQ_{\varepsilon}^{2}P)\big]

(3.10)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[f(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)-Pf(Q_{1,\varepsilon}^{2})P\big]+{\rm tr}\,\big[f(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)-Pf(Q_{2,\varepsilon}^{2})P)\big]

(3.11)

\displaystyle-{\rm tr}\,\big[f(PQ_{\varepsilon}^{2}P)-Pf(Q_{\varepsilon}^{2})P)\big]\,.

(3.12)

REMARK 3.4
.

Below we will consider
f\in\mathsf{C}_{c}^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
. By (
3.2
) the above integrability conditions on
f
are then satisfied.

Proof.

We begin this proof with the polynomial test functions
f(t)=t^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Strictly speaking, no such function satisfies the integrability conditions stated in the Lemma. However, we may replace
f
by
f\varphi
, where
\varphi\in\mathsf{C}_{c}^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
with support in
[-1,2]
and the property
\varphi(t)=1
for
t\in[0,1]
. The function
f\varphi
satisfies all the asked integrability conditions. Since all the involved operators
A\in\{Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon},\ldots,PQ_{\varepsilon}^{2}P\}
have their spectra inside
[0,1]
, we have
f(A)=(f\varphi)(A)
for these operators.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us introduce the operators

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon})^{n}+(Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon})^{n}-(Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon})^{n}\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle F_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}+(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}-(PQ_{\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}\,.

(3.14)

Then these are the operators inside the traces in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). We need to show that
E_{n}
and
F_{n}
are trace class and have the same trace. Note for a start that
E_{1}=-Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}-Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}
is trace class by
Lemma 2.1
with
f_{1}=\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}
and
f_{2}=\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}
and vice versa. The operator
F_{1}=0
is trivially trace class and with the same (zero) trace as
E_{1}
(see the arguments below (
3.22
)).

For general
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following recursion relations

\displaystyle PQ_{\varepsilon}E_{n}Q_{\varepsilon}P

\displaystyle=F_{n+1}\,,

(3.15)

\displaystyle Q_{\varepsilon}PF_{n}PQ_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=E_{n+1}

(3.16)

\displaystyle+Q_{1,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}+Q_{1,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}

(3.17)

\displaystyle+Q_{2,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+Q_{2,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}+Q_{1,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}\,.

(3.19)

To prove these relations, we merely use
Q_{\varepsilon}Q_{j,\varepsilon}=Q_{j,\varepsilon}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
.

Let us first consider the remainder terms in (
3.17
–
3.19
). All of them contain the factor
T_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}=\varphi_{1,\varepsilon}(X)P\varphi_{2,\varepsilon}(X)
or the adjoints
T_{\varepsilon}^{*}
. These operators are trace class by
Lemma 2.1
. The remaining factors are all bounded (in operator norm) by 1. Therefore, by the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\big\|\eqref{remainder 1}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|Q_{1,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n-1}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}T_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\big\|T_{\varepsilon}Q_{2,\varepsilon}P(PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P)^{n-1}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big\|T_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\eqqcolon C/3\,.

The same bound applies to the other two remainder terms so that

\big\|\eqref{remainder 1}+\eqref{remainder 2}+\eqref{remainder 3}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq C\,,

(3.20)

independent of
n
. Together with the above recursion relations, this yields the bounds

\displaystyle\|E_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq Cn\,,\quad\|F_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq Cn

(3.21)

for some constant
C
as we shall show now. Equation (
3.15
) and the Hölder inequality gives the bound (i)
\|F_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\|E_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
. Equation (
3.16
) and the above estimate on the remainder terms show the bound (ii)
\|E_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+C
with the constant
C
in (
3.20
). We combine and iterate the inequalities (i) and (ii) and obtain

\|F_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\|F_{n-1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+C\leq\|F_{n-3}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+2C\leq\ldots\,.

We end up either at
F_{1}=0
or at
F_{2}=-PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}P-PQ_{2,\varepsilon}^{2}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}^{2}P
. By
Lemma 2.1
,

\big\|F_{2}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=2\big\|PQ_{1,\varepsilon}Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}Q_{2,\varepsilon}P\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq 2\big\|Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=2\big\|T_{\varepsilon}\big\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq C<\infty\,.

This proves the 1-norm estimate on
F_{n}
in (
3.21
). But since we also have the estimate
\|E_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+C
we have also proved the first bound in (
3.21
) on the 1-norms of
E_{n}
.

Now we come to the computation of the trace of
E_{n}
and of
F_{n}
. In the first step we use the cyclicity
{\rm tr}\,(AB)={\rm tr}\,(BA)
, provided that
AB
and
BA
are both trace class. Here,
A=PQ_{\varepsilon}
and
B=E_{n}Q_{\varepsilon}P
are, of course, bounded. The operators
AB
and
BA
are indeed trace class because
E_{n}
is trace class. Then, by (
3.15
)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,F_{n+1}={\rm tr}\,\big[PQ_{\varepsilon}E_{n}Q_{\varepsilon}P\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[E_{n}Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon}\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[(Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{1,\varepsilon}P(Q_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon})+(Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{2,\varepsilon}P(Q_{1,\varepsilon}+Q_{2,\varepsilon})-(Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon})^{n+1}\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[E_{n+1}+(Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}+(Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,E_{n+1}\,.

(3.22)

The last equality follows since the trace-class operators
(Q_{j,\varepsilon}PQ_{j,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{j,\varepsilon}PQ_{j^{\prime},\varepsilon}
with
j\not=j^{\prime}
have zero trace. There are various ways to see the latter. One is to use that the smooth integral kernel vanishes on the diagonal and apply Mercer’s theorem, see
[
3
]
. Another one uses an orthonormal basis
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with support either in
I_{1}
, in
I_{2}
, or in
(I_{1}\cup I_{2})^{\complement}
so that any
\langle\psi_{n},(Q_{j,\varepsilon}PQ_{j,\varepsilon})^{n}Q_{j,\varepsilon}PQ_{j^{\prime},\varepsilon}\psi_{n}\rangle=0
. A third method is to use cyclicity and permute
Q_{j,\varepsilon}
inside the trace to hit
Q_{j^{\prime},\varepsilon}
so that
Q_{j,\varepsilon}Q_{j^{\prime},\varepsilon}=0
appears as a factor.

In the general case with
\hat{f}\in\mathsf{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap\mathsf{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, we use the Fourier representation

f(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\hat{f}(x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}xA}

for the bounded operators
A
equal to
Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}
,
Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}
and
Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon}
and expand the exponentials. That leads to

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,

\displaystyle\big[f(Q_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon})+f(Q_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon})-f(Q_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon})\big]

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\hat{f}(x)\,{\rm tr}\,\big[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}xQ_{1,\varepsilon}PQ_{1,\varepsilon}}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}xQ_{2,\varepsilon}PQ_{2,\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}xQ_{\varepsilon}PQ_{\varepsilon}}-\mathds{1}\big]

(3.24)

4.1.
Integral representation of Rényi entropies
h_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\leq 1

As mentioned, the computation of Casini–Huerta and the proof of Longo–Xu rely on an integral representation of the von-Neumann entropy
h_{1}
and on the operator-concavity
2
2

2

A real-valued, measurable function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is called operator concave if for all bounded self-adjoint operators
A
and
B
on a Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and real numbers
t\in(0,1)
,
f(tA+(1-t)B)\geq tf(A)+(1-t)f(B)
. Positivity of operators is meant as the positivity of the inner product, that is,
A\geq 0
if
\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.
property of the function
h_{1}
; see
[
13
, Lemma 3.4]
for a proof of this property. This can be extended to the
\alpha
-Rényi entropies for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Here, we only provide a short sketch, while we refer to Ref.
[
8
]
for detailed proofs of our results.

To develop a suitable integral representation of the Rényi entropy function, we interpret
h_{\alpha}
as an analytic function defined on the complex domain
D\coloneqq\Pi_{+}\cup\Pi_{-}\cup(0,1)
, taking the principal branch of the logarithm
\ln(z)
and of the power function
z^{\alpha}
, where we denoted by
\Pi_{\pm}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\pm\mathrm{imag}({z)}>0\}
the open upper and lower complex half-planes with the real line removed.

We recall (see
[
7
]
) that any analytic self-map in the upper complex half-plane
m:\Pi_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
m(\Pi_{+})\subseteq\overline{\Pi}_{+}
is a Nevanlinna–Herglotz function (NH in short) and it may be uniquely represented by the sum of a linear and an integral term. Rearranging the argument of the logarithm in the complex Rényi entropy function, we rewrite it as

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\ln\left(z^{\alpha}\left(1+\left(\frac{1-z}{z}\right)^{\alpha}\right)\right)=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\ln({z})+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}L_{\alpha}(-z)\,,

(4.1)

where the function
L_{\alpha}:z\mapsto\ln\left(1+\left(\frac{1+z}{-z}\right)^{\alpha}\right)

is defined on the same complex domain as
h_{\alpha}
. The function
z\mapsto\frac{1}{-z}
,
the logarithm
\ln({z})
and, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the power function
z\mapsto z^{\alpha}
are NH,
and therefore the function
L_{\alpha}
is NH for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Combining the unique Nevanlinna–Herglotz representations of the logarithm and of

,

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{B({\alpha})}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda\,

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(\lambda)\Bigg(R_{z}(\lambda)-R_{z}(-\lambda)+\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\lambda}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\right)^{2}+1}-\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\lambda}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\lambda\right)^{2}+1}\Bigg)\,,

\displaystyle B(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+2^{\alpha}+2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\cos\Big(\frac{3}{4}\alpha\pi\Big)\right),\hskip 14.22636ptf_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{\left(\frac{2\lambda-1}{2\lambda+1}\right)^{\alpha}\sin({\alpha\pi})}{1+\left(\frac{2\lambda-1}{2\lambda+1}\right)^{\alpha}\cos({\alpha\pi})}\right)\,,

\displaystyle R_{z}:{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\lambda\mapsto R_{z}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{z-\frac{1}{2}+\lambda}\,.

\ln\Big[\frac{a_{2}+r-a_{1}}{a_{2}+r-b_{1}}\Big]=\frac{1}{r}|I_{1}|+O(r^{-2})\,.

and

as above, the Rényi entanglement entropy behaves to leading order in the separating distance

as
(1+\alpha)/(12\alpha)|I_{1}||I_{2}|/r^{2}
, if both intervals are bounded and as
(1+\alpha)/(12\alpha)|I_{1}|/r
if
I_{1}
is bounded and
I_{2}
is unbounded.

On the other hand, if the intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
get close to one another, the entanglememt entropy diverges logarithmically. That is, suppose that
I_{1}=(a_{1},b_{1})
and
I_{2}=(b_{1}+\varepsilon,b_{2})
with
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\frac{(b_{1}+\varepsilon-a_{1})(b_{2}-b_{1})}{\varepsilon(b_{2}-a_{1})}=O(1/\varepsilon)\,.

In the case that the closures of the intervals overlap at just a single point
b_{1}
(put
\varepsilon=0
above), then the crucial operator
1_{I_{1}}(X)P1_{I_{2}}
is not trace class and our further estimates are out of reach. It can be seen immediately that this operator is not Hilbert–Schmidt. In fact, it will not be in any Schatten–von Neumann class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any
p<\infty
.

4.3.
A generalization to intersecting domains

In the earlier paper
[
5
]
(without the explicit formula for the von Neumann entropy), Casini and Huerta introduced the trace of the following operator

F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)\coloneqq f(P(\Lambda))+f(P(\Lambda^{\prime}))-f(P(\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}))-f(P(\Lambda\cup\Lambda^{\prime}))

(4.6)

in the case of the von-Neumann entropy function
f=h_{1}
and for not necessarily disjoint Borel sets
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
. See also the recent review
[
20
]
. They showed various nice properties of
{\rm tr}\,F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};h_{1})
as a function of the sets
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
, in particular, its positivity as a consequence of strong subadditivity of quantum mechanical entropy. The latter property was (first) proved by Lieb and Ruskai in
[
14
]
. In general, the
\alpha
-Rényi entropy is not strongly subadditive and positivity of the
\alpha
-Rényi entanglement entropy is hence not clear. In any case, not much is known in general whether
F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)
is trace class.

Let us mention the following property. Suppose that
f
satisfies
f(0)=0
(as usual) and the symmetry
f(t)=f(1-t)
, like for the Rényi entropies. Then, the operator
f(P(\Lambda))
has the same eigenvalues as
f(P(\Lambda^{\complement}))
including multiplicities (except for the eigenvalue 0) and informally we have that
{\rm tr}\,F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)={\rm tr}\,F(\Lambda^{\complement},(\Lambda^{\prime})^{\complement};f)
.

This implies (informally) that in the case of (disjoint) sets
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
satisfying our
Assumptions 1.1
,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,\Delta(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\big[f(P(\Lambda^{\complement}))+f(P((\Lambda^{\prime})^{\complement}))-f(P(\Lambda^{\complement}\cap(\Lambda^{\prime})^{\complement}))\big]

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,F(\Lambda^{\complement},(\Lambda^{\prime})^{\complement};f)\,.

There is a trivial but useful expression that relates the operator
F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)
to the difference of two
\Delta(\cdot,\cdot;f)
operators with certain disjoint sets. More concretely, we have

LEMMA 4.2
.

For any Borel subsets
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
{\mathbb{R}}
we have

F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)=F(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime};f)-F(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime};f)=\Delta(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime};f)-\Delta(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime};f)\,.

(4.7)

Note that the sets in the arguments of
\Delta
are disjoint, but not necessarily their closures.

We find it convenient to change our notation and write in the remaining of this section instead of
1_{\Lambda}P1_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
simply
\Lambda P\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The first equality is (almost) tautological as

\displaystyle F(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime};f)-F(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime};f)

\displaystyle=f\big((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})P(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\big)+f(\Lambda^{\prime}P\Lambda^{\prime})-f\big(((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\cup\Lambda^{\prime})P((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\cup\Lambda^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle-f\big((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})P(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\big)-f\big((\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime})P(\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime})\big)+f\big(((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\cup(\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}))P((\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime})\cup(\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}))\big)

\displaystyle=f(\Lambda^{\prime}P\Lambda^{\prime})-f\big((\Lambda\cup\Lambda^{\prime})P(\Lambda\cup\Lambda^{\prime})\big)-f\big((\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime})P(\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime})\big)+f(\Lambda P\Lambda)

\displaystyle=F(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime};f)\,.

The second equality follows trivially from the definition.
∎

As in the main body of this paper, we assume that
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
are intervals and we use the letters
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

CONJECTURE 4.3
.

Suppose that the intersection of the closures of the (open) intervals

and
I_{2}
has positive (finite) Lebesgue measure. In addition, we assume that one of the non-empty sets

or

is bounded. Then, for

defined in (

{\rm tr}\,F(I_{1},I_{2};f)=\frac{U(0,1;f)}{2\pi^{2}}\ln\Big[\frac{|I_{1}||I_{2}|}{|I_{1}\cap I_{2}||I_{1}\cup I_{2}|}\Big]\,.

(4.8)

REMARKS 4.4
.

(1)

This formula is in agreement with formula (17) in
[
5
]
and with Theorem 5.1(1) in
[

]
for the von-Neumann entropy function

.

(2)

Under the given conditions, one of the intervals must be bounded. The right-hand side of (

) is, of course, well-defined if both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are bounded. Suppose that
I_{1}
is bounded and
I_{2}
is unbounded, then the argument of the logarithm is understood as
\frac{|I_{1}|}{|I_{1}\cap I_{2}|}

Proof for polynomial test functions
f(t)=t^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the first part we show that

is trace class. Note that we cannot use
Lemma 4.2
here since
\Delta(I_{1}\setminus I_{2},I_{2};t^{m})
and
\Delta(I_{1}\setminus I_{2},I_{1}\cap I_{2};t^{m})
are not trace class. We will use that relation to compute the trace of
F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})
though.

For a start, let us consider the linear test function
f(t)=t
. Then, by some simple calculations,

\displaystyle F(I_{1},I_{2};t)

\displaystyle=\Delta(I_{1}\setminus I_{2},I_{2};t)-\Delta(I_{1}\setminus I_{2},I_{1}\cap I_{2};t)

\displaystyle=-(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})-(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})P(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})=-T-T^{*}\,,

(4.9)

where the operator
T\coloneqq(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})
is defined as in
Subsection 2.1
with
I_{1}
replaced by
I_{1}\setminus I_{2}
and
I_{2}
replaced by
I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
. Note that there is a positive distance between the intervals
I_{1}\setminus I_{2}
and
I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
of
|I_{1}\cap I_{2}|
and therefore

is in the Schatten class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for any

.

Now we deal with the polynomial
f(t)=t^{m}
by induction over

. We claim that
F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})
is trace class for every integer
m\geq 1
. We have just seen the starting case
m=1
. Thus, we consider

\displaystyle F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m+1})

\displaystyle=(I_{1}PI_{1})^{m}P(I_{1}\cup I_{2}-I_{2}\setminus I_{1})+(I_{2}PI_{2})^{m}P(I_{1}\cup I_{2}-I_{1}\setminus I_{2})

\displaystyle-[(I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})]^{m}P(I_{1}\cup I_{2}-I_{1}\setminus I_{2}-I_{2}\setminus I_{1})-[(I_{1}\cup I_{2})P(I_{1}\cup I_{2})]^{m}P(I_{1}\cup I_{2})

\displaystyle=F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})P(I_{1}\cup I_{2})+\big[\big((I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big)^{m}-(I_{1}PI_{1})^{m}\big]P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})

\displaystyle+\big[\big((I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big)^{m}-(I_{2}PI_{2})^{m}\big]P(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})\,.

(4.10)

The first summand is in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
by the induction hypothesis. The remaining two summands are of the same form except for the interchange of the indices
1
and
2
. Thus, it suffices to show that one of them is trace class. To this end, we perform another induction over the statement that

\displaystyle G_{m^{\prime}}\coloneqq\Big[(I_{1}(I_{1}PI_{1})^{m^{\prime}}-(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big((I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big)^{m^{\prime}}\Big]P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}

(4.11)

for any
m^{\prime}\geq 0
. The factors
I_{1}\cap I_{2}
and
I_{1}
we smuggled in only change
G_{0}
, which is a more convenient induction start. We see that
G_{0}=(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. For the induction step, we consider

\displaystyle G_{m^{\prime}+1}

\displaystyle=\Big[I_{1}PI_{1}(I_{1}PI_{1})^{m^{\prime}}-(I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big((I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big)^{m^{\prime}}\Big]P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})

\displaystyle=I_{1}PG_{m^{\prime}}+(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big((I_{1}\cap I_{2})P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\big)^{m^{\prime}}P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\,.

(4.12)

Once more, the first term is trace class by the induction hypothesis and for the second summand, we perform yet another induction. The induction claim is that the operator

\displaystyle H_{m^{\prime\prime}}\coloneqq(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P\left[P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})P\right]^{m^{\prime\prime}}(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}

(4.13)

for any
m^{\prime\prime}\geq 1
. The second summand in (
4.12
) is
H_{m^{\prime}+1}
. For the case
m^{\prime\prime}=1
, we split the bounded interval
I_{1}\cap I_{2}
in the middle such that
\overline{I_{1}\cap I_{2}}=\overline{J_{1}}\cup\overline{J_{2}}
for certain (disjoint, bounded) intervals
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
. Secondly, we choose
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
such that
\operatorname{dist}(I_{1}\setminus I_{2},J_{1})=\operatorname{dist}(I_{2}\setminus I_{1},J_{2})=\lvert I_{1}\cap I_{2}\rvert/2
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert H_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}=\left\lVert(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(J_{1}+J_{2})P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\leq\left\lVert(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})PJ_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\left\lVert J_{2}P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}<\infty\,.

(4.14)

For the induction step, we introduce the intervals
J_{3},J_{4}
, which are the connected components of
(I_{1}\cup I_{2})^{\complement}
. One of them may be empty. Thus, we have
\mathds{1}=(I_{1}\cap I_{2})+(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})+(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})+J_{3}+J_{4}
. We note that
J_{3}
and
J_{4}
have positive distance from the finite interval
I_{1}\cap I_{2}
, as
I_{1}\setminus I_{2}
and
I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
have positive length. We can now conclude the induction step,

\displaystyle\left\lVert H_{m^{\prime\prime}+1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P\big(\mathds{1}-J_{3}-J_{4}-(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})-(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\big)P\left[P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})P\right]^{m^{\prime\prime}}(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert H_{m^{\prime\prime}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\left\lVert J_{3}P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\left\lVert J_{4}P(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})PH_{m^{\prime\prime}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}+\left\lVert(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})P(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}<\infty\,.

(4.15)

In order to compute the trace of
F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})
we resort to the strong continuity of (the indicator functions)

to
{I_{1}\setminus I_{2}}
as

. If both intervals are bounded, we may assume without loss of generality that
I_{1}=(0,a)
,
I_{2}=(1,b)
with

. Then
I_{1}\setminus I_{2}=(0,1)
and
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=(1,a)
. For
0<\varepsilon<1
, let
(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq(0,1-\varepsilon)
. Then, both

are trace class and we know how to compute this trace by

,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\rm tr}\,\big\{\Delta\big((I_{1}\setminus I_{2})_{\varepsilon},I_{2};t^{m}\big)-\Delta\big((I_{1}\setminus I_{2})_{\varepsilon},I_{1}\cap I_{2};t^{m}\big)\big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(0,1;t^{m})}{2\pi^{2}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big\{\ln\Big[\frac{b-1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon b}\Big]-\ln\Big[\frac{a-1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon a}\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(0,1;t^{m})}{2\pi^{2}}\ln\Big[\frac{a(b-1)}{(a-1)b}\Big]\,,

(4.16)

which is the claimed formula.

Suppose that
I_{1}
is bounded and
I_{2}
is unbounded. Then, we may assume that
I_{1}=(0,a)
,
I_{2}=(1,\infty)
with

. By using

we obtain,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,F(I_{1},I_{2};t^{m})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\rm tr}\,\big\{\Delta\big((I_{1}\setminus I_{2})_{\varepsilon},I_{2};t^{m}\big)-\Delta\big((I_{1}\setminus I_{2})_{\varepsilon},I_{1}\cap I_{2};t^{m}\big)\big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(0,1;t^{m})}{2\pi^{2}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big\{\ln\Big[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big]-\ln\Big[\frac{a-1}{\varepsilon a}\Big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(0,1;t^{m})}{2\pi^{2}}\ln\Big[\frac{a}{a-1}\Big]\,.

(4.17)

This finishes the proof of
Conjecture 4.3
for polynomial test functions.

∎

Adiabatic sweep theorem for three-dimensional dipolar Bose gases
Alexander Yu. Cherny

Abstract
The variational theorem for the scattering length in the presence of the dipole-dipole interaction is developed. The theorem is applied to the spinless dipolar Bose gas in three dimensions. We calculated analytically the long-range tails of the single-particle momentum distribution and static structure factor, and the pair distribution function at short distances. The momentum distribution is inversely proportional to q 4 q^{4} with the anisotropic prefactor. In the absence of the dipole-dipole interaction, Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem is reproduced as a particular case. For the homogeneous dilute Bose gas, all the relations are calculated analytically.



I
Introduction

Dipolar quantum gases are an important tool to study quantum many-body effects both theoretically and experimentally (see the reviews
Baranov 2008
;
Lahaye et al. 2009
;
Baranov et al. 2012
;
Pitaevskii and Stringari 2016
). The anisotropic long-range dipole-dipole interaction leads to a variety of their properties and provides a way of controlling them with external electromagnetic fields.

The physical properties of the dilute dipolar Bose gas with magnetic or electric moment are governed by two parameters:
the scattering length
a
and the absolute value of the dipole moment
d
. This property is called universality. The scattering length arises due to an additional short-range interaction (for details, see Sec.
II.1
below). It is convenient to introduce the effective dipole range
r_{\mathrm{dd}}=md^{2}/(3\hbar^{2})
, which has the dimension of length and determines the strength of the dipole-dipole interaction (see, e.g., Ch. 25 of Ref.
Pitaevskii and Stringari 2016
). The dimensionless parameter
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}=r_{\mathrm{dd}}/a
characterises the relative strength of the dipole-dipole interaction. Here
m
is the mass of interacting particles.

The dipolar Bose gas in three dimensions can be thermodynamically unstable due to the attractive part of the dipole-dipole interactions. To stabilize it, the additional short-range interaction should be repulsive (
a>0
) and sufficiently large (
a>r_{\mathrm{dd}}
). Otherwise, the system breaks down into tiny clusters called droplets (see the reviews
Ferrier-Barbut 2019
;
Luo et al. 2021
;
Böttcher et al. 2020
) or even collapses completely. Recently, the ground state energy and correlation functions were obtained for the homogeneous dilute dipolar Bose gas
Cherny 2019
when the stability conditions are satisfied.

In the absence of the long-range dipole-dipole interaction (
d=0
), the single-particle momentum distribution at high momenta is proportional to
1/q^{4}
, and the proportionality factor depends on the derivative of the ground-sate energy with respect to the scattering length (Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem
Tan 2008a
;
Tan 2008b
). The question arises how to extend Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem to the dipolar gases in three dimensions. This problem is a challenge for the theory of cold gases
Hofmann and Zwerger 2021
because of the long-rang nature of the dipole-dipole interaction, which makes it impossible to apply Tan’s theorem directly to these systems. In this paper, we suggest a solution of the problem by means of the variational theorem for the scattering length
Cherny and Shanenko 2000
;
Cherny 2021
;
Cherny 2022
, extended to the dipole-dipole interaction.

We found analytically the single-particle momentum distribution at high momenta, see Eq. (
21
). It is anisotropic and still proportional to the derivative of the ground-sate energy with respect to the scattering length. In addition, the short-range behaviour of the pair distribution function (
24
) was obtained explicitly, which allows us to calculate the dynamic structure factor (
27
) at large momenta. The equations (
21
), (
24
), and (
27
) are the main results of the paper.

We emphasize that the tail of single-particle momentum distribution is calculated only for universal systems. If the three-body Efimov states for the dipole-dipole interaction
Wang et al. 2011
give a significant contribution to the ground-state energy then an additional control parameter should appear by analogy with radially-symmetric short-range potentials
Werner 2008
;
Werner et al. 2009
;
*werner12
.

The paper is organized as follows. The next section regards the dipole-dipole interaction and its properties. In particular, the behaviour of the two-body scattering at small distances are considered, and the formula for the short-range scattering amplitude is derived. Besides, an analytical formula is found for the zero-energy scattering amplitude. In Sec.
III
, we obtain the variational theorem for the scattering length in the presence of the dipole-dipole interaction. In Sec.
IV
, the relations are found for the single-particle momentum distribution, pair distribution function, and static structure factor. Finally, the obtained results are summarized and some prospects are discussed in Sec.
V
.

II
The dipole-dipole interaction and its properties

II.1
The dipole-dipole interaction

A realistic interaction in the dipolar Bose gas can be presented as the sum

\displaystyle V(\bm{r})=V_{0}(r)+V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{r},{l_{0}})

(1)

of a short-range potential
V_{0}(r)
, typically decreasing at large distances as
1/r^{6}

Baranov et al. 2012
, and the long-range dipole tail

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{r},{l_{0}})=\begin{cases}-2d^{2}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})/{r^{3}},&\text{for }r\geqslant{l_{0}},\\
0,&\text{for }r<{l_{0}}.\end{cases}

(2)

Here
{l_{0}}
is a cutoff parameter of order of atomic size
1
1

1

Note that in our paper
Cherny 2019
, the notation
r_{0}
was used instead of
l_{0}
.
,
P_{2}(x)=(3x^{2}-1)/2
is the Legendre polynomial of the second order and
\hat{r}=\bm{r}/r
is the unit vectors along the direction of the relative coordinate
\bm{r}
. The dipoles are supposed to be parallel and aligned along the direction
\hat{d}
by a homogeneous external field. We denote the characteristic radius of the short-range interaction
V_{0}(r)
as
r_{0}
. If the short-range potential contains a repulsive core at distances more than
l_{0}
then one can put
l_{0}=0
in Eq. (
2
).

The Fourier transform of the long-range part of the interaction (
2
) in the limit
l_{0}\to 0
is given by

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{q})=\frac{8\pi d^{2}}{3}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q}),

(3)

which is independent of the absolute value of
\bm{q}
.

At low energies, the form of the short-range potential
V_{0}(r)
is not important, and its physical properties is controlled by the low-energy scattering amplitude, which is proportional to the scattering length
a
(universality). This enables us to introduce the pseudopotential, which implies, to put it simply, that the short-range part of a real two-body interaction (
1
) is replaced by
\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}\delta(\bm{r})
. Universality for cold dipolar gases assumes that two potentials of different shape are indistinguishable at the low-energy scale as long as they have the same scattering length and the effective dipole range. It follows that the ground-state energy depends only on the particle mass, scattering length, effective dipole range, and parameters of an external trap.

II.2
The wave function of two oriented dipoles at small distances

The scattering amplitude
f
for a short-range potential is defined
Landau and Lifshitz 1977
through the long-range asymptotics of the wavefunction

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{r})\simeq e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r}}+f\frac{e^{ipr}}{r},

(4)

which obeys the Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}+p^{2})\varphi=2m^{*}V(\bm{r})\varphi/\hbar^{2}.

(5)

Here
\bm{p}
is the wavevector of incident particles of mass
m^{*}
, which are scattered by the potential
V(\bm{r})
. In the case of two-body scattering with the relative momentum
\hbar\bm{p}
, we should put
m^{*}=m/2
.

The potential (
1
) is of long-range type due to the dipole part proportional to
P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})/{r^{3}}
. Nevertheless, the plane wave at large distances is not distorted, and the scattering part of the wavefunction remains proportional to
1/r
. Thus the asymptotics (
4
) remains intact. However, the long-range nature of the dipole-dipole interaction leads to a complicated behaviour of
f
. In particular, it differs
qualitatively
in the ranges
r\gg 1/p
and
r\ll 1/p
.

The scattering amplitude
f
is obtained from the asymptotics (
4
) in the regime
r\gg 1/p
. It depends on
all
the directions involved:
\hat{d}
,
\hat{r}
, and
\hat{p}
. This can be seen even in the Born approximation (see Appendix
A
).

On the other hand, at the distances
r\ll 1/p
, one can go the limit
p=0
in Eq. (
5
). Then the asymptotics of its solution in the range
r_{0}\ll r\ll 1/p
takes the form
Cherny 2019

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{r})\simeq 1-\frac{a-r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})}{r}.

(6)

The relation (
6
) can be checked directly with the identities
\nabla^{2}1/r=0
and
\nabla^{2}P_{2}(\cos\vartheta)/{r}=-6P_{2}(\cos\vartheta)/{r^{3}}
for arbitrary
r\not=0
. Here
\cos\vartheta=\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}
when the
z
-axis is parallel to the direction of the dipole moment. Thus the
tail of the dipole-dipole interaction in the Schrödinger equation cancels out due to the presence of the anisotropic part of the wavefunction
r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\cos\vartheta)/{r}
.

One can also call the quantity
f=-a+r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})
in Eq. (
6
) the “short-range” scattering amplitude. It is
independent
of the directions of incident particles
\hat{p}
. We show in the Born approximation (see Appendix
A
) that the small-momentum correction to

is proportional to
pr
with the prefactor depending on the direction
\hat{p}
. This agrees well with the above results.

II.3
The scattering amplitude and pseudopotential for the dipole-diploe interaction

To find the wavefunction in the range
r_{0}\ll r\ll 1/p
, it is sufficient to consider the two-body scattering at zero energy. This implies that
p=0
and
m^{*}=m/2
in the Schrödinger equation (
5

Separating the scattering part
\psi(\bm{r})
of the wave function
\varphi(\bm{r})=1+\psi(\bm{r})
and taking the Fourier transformation of the Schrödinger equation yield

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{q})=-\frac{U(\bm{q})}{2T({\bm{q}})}.

(7)

Here
T({\bm{q}})=\hbar^{2}q^{2}/(2m)
and
\psi(\bm{q})
are the free-particle dispersion and the Fourier transform of

, respectively, and

\displaystyle U(\bm{q})=\int{d}r^{3}\,e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}V(\bm{r})\varphi(\bm{r})

(8)

is the scattering amplitude "off the mass shell"
2
2

2

For a short-range potential, the scattering amplitude

in Eq. (
4

\int{d}r^{3}\,e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}V(\bm{r})\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})

\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})

5

For the wavefunction, the range
r_{0}\ll r\ll 1/p

in momentum space. Then, in Eq. (

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{r})=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{2l}(r)P_{2l}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}+\frac{8\pi d^{2}}{3}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}\left[1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}+3\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}\right],

(10)

where the scattering length is given by
Cherny 2019

\displaystyle a=\frac{m}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!{d}r\,r^{2}V_{0}(r)\varphi_{0}(r)-\frac{6r_{\mathrm{dd}}}{5}\int_{l_{0}}^{\infty}{d}r\frac{\varphi_{2}(r)}{r}.

(11)

The low-energy scattering amplitude (
II.3
) is independent of the absolute value of the wavevector but depends on its direction. For this reason, its value at

is undefined. The low-momentum amplitude (
II.3

6

7

Equation (
6

\varphi_{0}(r)\simeq 1-a/r
and
\varphi_{2}(r)\simeq r_{\mathrm{dd}}/r
explicitly determine the main asymptotic behaviour of the wave function when

. However, the Schrödinger equation relates any

\varphi_{2l-2}

\varphi_{2l+2}

Cherny 2019
). This implies that all even momenta implicitly contribute to the scattering length.

The inverse Fourier transformation of the low-energy scattering amplitude gives us the effective pseudopotential for the dipolar gas
Yi and You 2000

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{r})=\!\int{d}q^{3}\,e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}U_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})=\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}\delta(\bm{r})\!-\!\frac{2d^{2}}{r^{3}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r}).

II.4
The analytical expression for the zero-energy scattering amplitude

We define the scattering amplitude
a
through the asymptotics (
6
) of the Schrödinger equation (
5
) with
p=0
. As explained above, this asymptotics corresponds to the
short-range
behaviour of the solution of the Schrödinger equation with
p\not=0
in the range
r_{0}\ll r\ll 1/p
. On the other hand, the scattering length is usually defined in the literature

;
Ronen et al. 2006
as the spherically symmetric component of the
long-range
scattering amplitude [see Eq. (
4
)] in the zero-energy limit
p\to 0
. Let us show that these definitions are equivalent and obtain analytically the zero-energy scattering amplitude as a function of the incident
\hat{p}
and scattered

The scattering amplitude can be expanded in the spherical harmonics
Yi and You 2000

\displaystyle f(\hat{p},\hat{r},p)=4\pi\sum_{lm,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}t_{lm}^{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(p)Y_{lm}^{*}(\hat{p})Y_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\hat{r})

\displaystyle a=-\lim_{p\to 0}\langle f(\hat{p},\hat{r},p)\rangle_{\hat{p},\hat{r}},

(13)

where the brackets
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\hat{p},\hat{r}}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int d\hat{p}\,d\hat{r}\cdots
stand for the average over all directions of unit vectors
\hat{p}
and
\hat{r}
.

The scattering amplitude can be written down through the matrix element (see the discussion in Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle f(\hat{p},\hat{r},p)=

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}U_{p\hat{p}}(p\hat{r}),

(14)

\displaystyle U_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}y\,e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{y}}V(\bm{y})\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{y})

(15)

with
\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{y})
being the solution of the Schrödinger equation (
5
). Separating the plane wave

and substituting

into Eq. (

\displaystyle U_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}y\,e^{-i(\bm{q}-\bm{p})\cdot\bm{y}}V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{y})+\int d^{3}y\,e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{y}}V_{0}(y)\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle+\int d^{3}y\,e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{y}}V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{y})\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{y}),

(16)

where
\bm{p}=p\hat{p}
and
\bm{q}=p\hat{r}
.

The first term in Eq. (

) is calculated explicitly. In the limit
p\to 0
, it is given by the Fourier transform (

) of
\hat{r}-\hat{p}
. The second and third integrals converge absolutely for large

falls off faster than

\int d^{3}y\,V_{0}(y)\varphi(\bm{y})

is the solution of the the Schrödinger equation (

and

\int d\hat{y}P_{l}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{y})P_{l^{\prime}}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{y})=4\pi\delta_{ll^{\prime}}/(2l+1)

it is easy to verify that the sum of the the second and third terms amounts to

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to 0}f(\hat{p},\hat{r},p)=-a-2r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}\left(\frac{\hat{d}\cdot(\hat{r}-\hat{p})}{\sqrt{2(1-\hat{p}\cdot\hat{r})}}\right)

III
The variational theorem for the scattering length in the presence of the dipole-dipole interaction

The variational theorem for the scattering length was suggested in our previous publications
Cherny and Shanenko 2000
;
Cherny 2021
;
Cherny 2022
. Although it was obtained for short-range radially symmetric potentials, the theorem can easily be generalized to the dipole-dipole interaction, for which it takes a similar form. We apply the variational theorem for the total potential (
1
).

The main problem of the generalization lies in the long-range nature of the dipole-dipole interaction, which leads to the logarithmic divergence of the integral
\int d^{3}r{V}(\bm{r})\varphi(\bm{r})
, in which the wavefunction
\varphi(\bm{r})
obeys Eq. (
5
) at
p=0
and has the asymptotics (
6
). As a consequence, the limit of the short-range scattering amplitude (
II.3
) as
q\to 0
does not exist and
U(0)
is not defined. However, the above integral can be understood as the limit
\int d^{3}r\cdots=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0}^{R}dr\,r^{2}\int d\hat{r}\cdots
, where
\int d\hat{r}
denotes the integration over all directions of the radius vector. Then the zero-momentum scattering amplitude is well-defined and given by

\displaystyle U(0)=\int d^{3}r{V}(\bm{r})\varphi(\bm{r})=\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}.

(18)

This relation can be proved with the help of the Gauss-Ostrogradsky (divergence) theorem.

Following Ref.
Cherny 2022
, we consider the two-particle scattering problem with zero total energy and momentum for arbitrary single-particle dispersion
T(\bm{q})
. It is supposed to be quite close to the usual free-particle dispersion
T({q})=\hbar^{2}q^{2}/(2m)
(see Ref.
Cherny 2022
for details). The two-body Schrödinger equation takes the form

2T\left(-i\hbar\nabla\right)\varphi(\bm{r})+{V}(\bm{r})\varphi(\bm{r})=0.

Separating the scattering part of the wave function
\varphi(\bm{r})=1+\psi(\bm{r})
and following the same arguments as in the papers
Cherny 2021
;
Cherny 2022
, we obtain the zero-momentum scattering amplitude

{U}(0)=\int{d}^{3}r\left[\psi(\bm{r})2T\left(-i\hbar\nabla\right)\psi(\bm{r})+{V}(\bm{r})\varphi^{2}(\bm{r})\right]

and arrive at the variational theorem

\displaystyle\delta{U}(0)=\int\frac{{d}^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,2\delta T(\bm{q})\psi^{2}(\bm{q})+\int{d}^{3}r\,\delta{V}(\bm{r})\varphi^{2}(\bm{r}).

Here the Fourier transform of the scattering part of the wave function is given by Eq. (
7
). The last integral converges if it is understood as discussed above.

We are looking for the behaviour of the two-body wavefunction in the ranges
r_{0}\ll r\ll 1/p
and
p\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}
in real and momentum spaces, respectively. Then one can replace the scattering amplitude by its low-momentum limit (
II.3
) and the wavefunction by its asymptotics (
6
), which finally yields

\displaystyle\frac{\delta U(0)}{\delta T(\bm{q})}=

\displaystyle\,2\psi^{2}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{U^{2}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})}{2T^{2}(q)}=\frac{m^{2}U^{2}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})}{4\pi^{3}\hbar^{4}q^{4}},

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta U(0)}{\delta V(\bm{r})}=

\displaystyle\,\varphi^{2}(\bm{r})=\left(1-\frac{a-r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})}{r}\right)^{2}.

(20)

The second equality in Eq. (
19
) assumes that the variation is taken in the vicinity of the usual free-particle dispersion.

IV
Applications of the variational theorem

In general, the ground-state energy depends on the interparticle interactions, single-particle dispersion, and parameters of a trapping potential. Universality implies that it depends on the scattering length, dipole range, and frequency of the harmonic trap:
E=E(a,r_{\mathrm{dd}},\omega)
. On the other hand, the momentum single-particle distribution can be calculated by varying the energy with respect to the single-particle dispersion
Cherny 2021
:
\frac{\delta E}{\delta T({\bm{q}})}=\frac{N({\bm{q}})}{(2\pi)^{3}}
. For a universal system, the single-particle dispersion is involved through the scattering length, which yields
\frac{\delta E}{\delta T({\bm{q}})}=\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\frac{\delta a}{\delta T({\bm{q}})}
. Then the variational theorem allows us to obtain the momentum distribution at large momenta. Similar arguments can be used to calculate the spatial correlations.

IV.1
The long-range tail of the momentum distribution

Using the above arguments, we can find the long-range tail of the single-particle momentum distribution in a trapped dipolar Bose-gas with the relation
Cherny 2021

\displaystyle N({\bm{q}})=(2\pi)^{3}\frac{\delta a}{\delta T({\bm{q}})}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},\omega,N},

where
N
is the total number of particles. For technical convenience, the mass is kept constant during the variation, because in this case Eq. (
18
) yields
\delta U(0)=\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}\delta a
, and besides
r_{\mathrm{dd}}
and
\omega

19

\displaystyle N(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{8\pi ma^{2}}{\hbar^{2}q^{4}}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},\omega,N}h(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q}),

(21)

\displaystyle h(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q})=

\displaystyle\left[1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}+3\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}\right]^{2}.

(22)

The distribution is normalized to the total number of particles:
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int{d}^{3}q\,N(\bm{q})=N

Near the unitary regime

q_{y}=h\sin\theta\sin\phi
, and
q_{z}=h\cos\theta
. The dipoles are aligned along the
z
-axis. The parameter
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}
is given by
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}=r_{\mathrm{dd}}/a
and characterises the relative strength of the dipole-dipole interaction. The condition
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}<1
is needed for stability of the dipolar Bose gas in three dimensions. The anisotropy of the momentum distribution strongly increases when
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}
gets closer to one.

For quantum gases, the typical value of de Broglie wavelength
2\pi/p
should be of order of the mean distance between particles or higher. Then the relation (
21
) is valid in the range
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}
, where
\xi
determines the characteristic scale of the many-body effects, which is also of order of the mean distance.

In the particular case of the homogeneous gas, the distribution
N(\bm{q})
is proportional to the mean occupation numbers
n_{\bm{q}}
:
N(\bm{q})=n_{\bm{q}}V
, where
V
is the volume of the system. In Eq. (
21
), the partial derivative of the ground state with respect to the scattering length should be taken at constant volume.

The distribution over the absolute value of momentum is given by the average of Eq. (
21
) over the directions of
\hat{q}
, that is, over the solid angle with the volume element
d\hat{q}=\sin\vartheta d\varphi d\vartheta
:

\displaystyle N(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\hat{q}\,N(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi ma^{2}}{\hbar^{2}q^{4}}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},\omega,N}\left(1+\frac{4\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}^{2}}{5}\right).

(23)

In the absence of the dipole-dipole interaction,
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}=0
Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) are reduced to Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem

\displaystyle q^{4}N({q})=

\displaystyle\,{\cal I}=\frac{8\pi ma^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{\omega,N},

where
{\cal I}
is Tan’s constant for trapped gases
Tan 2008a
. In this case, the long-range momentum distribution becomes radially symmetric.

The equation (
23
) cannot be applied for calculation of the kinetic energy per particle, which is proportional to the integral of
q^{2}N(q)
and thus diverges at large momenta. The reason is that the momentum distributions (
21
) and (
23
) are valid in the range
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}
. In order to eliminate the divergence in the kinetic energy, we need to obtain the momentum distribution for
q\gtrsim 1/r_{0}
. Note that the same problem arises in the standard Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem
Tan 2008a
;
Tan 2008b
for the same reason. In the papers
Cherny 2021
;
Cherny 2022
by the author, the solution of this problem was suggested for a short-range potential. The same could be done for the dipole-dipole interaction. However, the range
q\gtrsim 1/r_{0}
in the momentum space is not yet accessible experimentally, and in this paper we restrict ourselves to the range
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}
.

IV.2
The shot-range behaviour of the pair distribution function

In this section, we find the short-range behaviour of the pair distribution function with the help of the variational theorem
Cherny 2021
. Here we restrict ourselves to the homogeneous case. The pair correlation function
g(\bm{r})
determines the density-density correlations, and it is proportional to the conditional probability to find a particle at point
\bm{r}
provided another particle is located at the origin
\bm{r}=0
.

The pair distribution function is obtained by means of variation of the ground-state with respect to the interaction potential (see details in Ref.
Cherny 2021
)

\displaystyle g(\bm{r})=\frac{2}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\frac{\delta E}{\delta V(\bm{r})}=\frac{2}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\frac{\delta a}{\delta V({\bm{r}})}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},V,N},

where
n=N/V
is the density of particles. The variation
\delta{V}(\bm{r})
is supposed to be localized within
r\ll\xi
, that is, it can be related to the short-range part of the interaction potential (
1
):
\delta{V}(\bm{r})=\delta{V}_{0}(\bm{r})
. We get in this manner

\displaystyle g(\bm{r})=\,

\displaystyle w\left[1-\frac{a-r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})}{r}\right]^{2},

(24)

\displaystyle w=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},V,N}\frac{m}{2\pi n^{2}\hbar^{2}}

(25)

when
r_{0}\ll r\ll\xi
.

Note that we cannot use Eq. (
24
) for calculating the full interaction energy
\frac{N^{2}}{2V}\int d^{3}r\,g(\bm{r})V(\bm{r})
, because the contribution of the long-range dipole-dipole forces is significant at the distances of order
\xi
and higher.

In the absence of the dipole-dipole interaction at
r_{\mathrm{dd}}
=0, we reproduce the results obtained by Cherny and Shanenko twenty years ago
Cherny and Shanenko 2000
;
Cherny and Shanenko 2001
.

IV.3
The static structure factor

For the homogeneous system, the static structure factor can be obtained (see Ref.
Pitaevskii and Stringari 2016
, Sec. 18.5) by taking the Fourier transformation of the pair distribution function (
24
)

\displaystyle S(\bm{q})=1+n\int{d}^{3}r[g(\bm{r})-1]e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(26)

In this way we get for
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle(\bm{q})=1-\frac{8\pi anw}{q^{2}}\left(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}+3\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}x^{2}\right)+\frac{\pi^{2}a^{2}nw}{q}

\displaystyle\times\left[2\!-\!\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}\!+\!\frac{11\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}^{2}}{16}\!+\!3\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}\left(1\!-\!\frac{5\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}}{8}\right)x^{2}\!+\!\frac{27}{16}\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}^{2}x^{4}\right],

(27)

where
q
is the absolute value of
\bm{q}
,
w
is given by Eq. (
25
), and we put by definition
x=\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q}=\cos\theta
.

Note that the
\delta
-function contribution, which appears formally in the right-hand side of Eq. (
26
), should be ignored. It arises at
w\not=1
because Eq. (
24
) does not describe correctly the long-range behaviour of the pair distribution function, which should tend to one when
r\gg\xi
.

IV.4
The weakly interacting spinless Bose gas at zero temperature

We apply the obtained relations to the homogeneous dilute spinless Bose gas at zero temperature. Its ground-state energy is given by
Cherny 2019

\displaystyle E=\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}a}{m}\frac{N^{2}}{V}\left[1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{na^{3}}{\cal Q}_{5}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}})\right],

(28)

where we use the notation

\displaystyle{\cal Q}_{l}(y)=(1-y)^{l/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{l}{2};\frac{3}{2};-\frac{3y}{1-y}\right)

with
{}_{2}F_{1}
being the hypergeometric function. Actually, Eq. (
28
) is the ground-state expansion with respect to the small parameter
na^{3}
, and the stability condition requires that
a>0
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}=r_{\mathrm{dd}}/a<1
. At
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}=0
, we arrive at the famous Lee-Huang-Yang result
Lee et al. 1957
, since
{\cal Q}_{5}(0)=1
.

The equation (
28
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial a}\right)_{r_{\mathrm{dd}},V,N}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}\frac{N^{2}}{V}\left[1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{na^{3}}{\cal P}_{5}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}})\right],

(29)

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{l}(y)=

\displaystyle\frac{(l+1){\cal Q}_{l}(y)-(1+2y)^{l/2}}{2(1-y)}.

Substituting Eq. (
29
) into the general relation (
21
), we obtain the average occupation numbers when
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}

\displaystyle n_{\bm{q}}=\frac{16\pi^{2}n^{2}a^{2}}{q^{4}}f(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q})\left[1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{na^{3}}{\cal P}_{5}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}})\right].

(30)

On the other hand, the Bogoliubov theory
Bogoliubov 1947
gives us for
\bm{q}\not=0

Cherny 2019

\displaystyle n_{\bm{q}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{T({q})+nU_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})}{\sqrt{T({q})^{2}+2nU_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})T({q})}}-1\right),

(31)

where
U_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})
is the pseudopotential (
II.3
) and
T({q})
is the usual free-particle dispersion. The long-range tail of the occupation numbers (
31
) is given by

\displaystyle n_{\bm{q}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{n^{2}U^{2}_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})}{T^{2}({q})}=\frac{16\pi^{2}n^{2}a^{2}}{q^{4}}f(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{q}).

(32)

One can see that Eqs. (
30
) and (
32
) coincide in the main order, but Eq. (
30
) is more precise and contains the correction of order of
\sqrt{na^{3}}
.

In the framework of the Bogoliubov theory, the dynamic structure factor takes the form

\displaystyle S(\bm{q})=\frac{T({q})}{\sqrt{T({q})^{2}+2nU_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})T({q})}},

which yields for
1/\xi\ll q\ll 1/r_{0}

\displaystyle S(\bm{q})=1-\frac{nU_{\mathrm{ps}}(\bm{q})}{T({q})}=1-\frac{8\pi an}{q^{2}}\left(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}+3\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}x^{2}\right).

(33)

The accuracy of Eq. (
27
), obtained with the variational theorem, is higher. First, it contains the additional term proportional to
1/q
, and second, the coefficient
w
in the first term includes the correction of order of
\sqrt{na^{3}}
:

\displaystyle w=1+\frac{128}{15\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{na^{3}}{\cal P}_{5}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{dd}}),

(34)

as it follows from Eqs. (
25
) and (
29
).

V
Conclusions

We extended the variational theorem for the scattering length
Cherny and Shanenko 2000
;
Cherny 2021
;
Cherny 2022
to the potential (
1
), which is the sum of a short-range and dipole-dipole interactions. The generalization is based on the short-range asymptotics (
6
) of the two-body wavefunction
Cherny 2019
.

As a byproduct, we obtain analytical expression (
17
) for the zero-energy scattering amplitude in the presence of the dipole-dipole interaction.

The variations of the ground-state energy with respect to the one-particle dispersion and the short-range part of the interaction enable us to obtain the single-particle momentum distribution (
21
) at large momenta and the pair distribution function (
24
) at small distances. For universal systems, these variations are realized through the scattering length, which allows us to apply the variational theorem (
19
) and (
20
).

The momentum distribution contains the axially symmetric prefactor with the axis being parallel to the direction of the dipoles, see Fig.
1
. Integrating out the angles, we arrived at the distribution over the absolute value of momentum (
23
). We get Tan’s adiabatic sweep theorem as a particular case of Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) when the dipole-dipole interaction is switched off.

The static structure factor (
27
) was found with the Fourier transformation of the pair distribution function.

We verified the obtained general relations for the dipolar Bose gas in the particular case of the weakly interacting Bose gas
Cherny 2019
, although the relations are applicable for arbitrary large values of the scattering length.

The relations (
21
), (
24
), and (
27
) can easily be generalized to finite temperatures. It suffices to replace the ground-state energy in the equations with the free energy (see the details in Ref.
Cherny 2021
). This is all the more important when the scattering length is large, because finite temperatures are needed
Chevy and Salomon 2016
for stability of the Bose gas in the large-gas-parameter regime
na^{3}\gg 1
. Such regime with a large scattering length together with high electric dipole moment could be realized in Rydberg atoms
Balewski and Pfau 2016
or polar bosonic molecules
Moses et al. 2017
, although in these systems the regime of Bose condensation is not reached yet experimentally.

Appendix A
The Born approximation for the dipole-dipole interaction

We consider the scattering of two spinless bosons in the center-of-mass system and therefore are looking for an even solution of the Schrödinger equation (
5
) with the potential (
2
) when
l_{0}\to 0
.

The wavefunction can be written in the form
\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})=\cos(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{r})+\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})
. In the Born approximation, the term
\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})
in the right-hand side of Eq. (
5
) is neglected. Taking the Fourier transformation of the Schrödinger equation yields

(-q^{2}+p^{2})\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=\frac{m}{\hbar^{2}}V_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})

where
\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})
is the is the Fourier transform of
\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})
, and

\displaystyle V_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=\frac{V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{p}+\bm{q})+V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{p}-\bm{q})}{2}

(35)

is the symmetrized Fourier transform of the dipole-dipole interaction (
3
).

After the inverse Fourier transformation we are left with

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})=-\frac{m}{(2\pi)^{3}\hbar^{2}}\int d^{3}q\,e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\frac{V_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})}{q^{2}-p^{2}-i0},

(36)

where the passage round the pole
q^{2}=p^{2}
removes the ambiguity of the result (see Ref.
Landau and Lifshitz 1977
, Sec. 130).

The long-range asymptotics for
r\gg 1/p
can be obtained from Eq. (
36
) in the standard way
Landau and Lifshitz 1977
:

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})\simeq-\frac{mV_{\hat{p}}(\hat{r})}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{e^{ipr}}{r}.

(37)

Here we use the scaling property of the Fourier transform of the dipole-dipole interaction (
35
):
V_{\bm{p}}(p\hat{r})=V_{\hat{p}}(\hat{r})
. The scattering amplitude depends on all the orientations: the dipoles
\hat{d}
, wavevector
\hat{p}
of the relative momentum, and radius vector
\hat{r}
.

To calculate the scattering part of the wavefunction (
36
) when
r\ll 1/p
, we represent it in the form

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})=

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{(2\pi)^{3}\hbar^{2}}\int d^{3}q\,e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\frac{V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{q})}{q^{2}}-\frac{m}{(2\pi)^{3}\hbar^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\int d^{3}q\,e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\left[\frac{V_{\hat{p}}(\bm{q}/p)}{q^{2}-p^{2}-i0}-\frac{V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{q})}{q^{2}}\right].

The first integral is easily calculated as
r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})/r
. By means of the scaling substitution
\bm{q}=p\bm{y}
in the second integral, we obtain in the limit
pr\to 0

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\mathrm{dd}}P_{2}(\hat{d}\cdot\hat{r})}{r}-p\,C,

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{(2\pi)^{3}\hbar^{2}}\int d^{3}y\left[\frac{V_{\hat{p}}(y\hat{y})}{y^{2}-1-i0}-\frac{V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{y})}{y^{2}}\right].

The last integral converges, because
V_{\hat{p}}(y\hat{y})-V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\hat{y})=O(1/y^{2})
at large
y
. Here the symbol
O(z)
denotes the terms of order of
z
or smaller. One can see that the prefactor
C
depends on the direction of the relative momentum
\hat{p}
, as expected.

The Born approximation for the dipole-dipole interaction was used in the literature many years ago
Cross and Gordon 1966
.

Note that beyond the Born approximation, Eq. (
36
) is still valid but the Fourier transform of the dipole-dipole interaction
V_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})
should be replaced by the matrix element
U_{\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}re^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}V_{\mathrm{dd}}(\bm{r})\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})
. The matrix element is the sum of the Born term and the integral containing the scattering piece. The latter converges absolutely for large
r
, since the integrand is proportional to
1/r^{4}
, and then no problem arises with the scattering piece. With the same considerations, we arrive at the asymptotics (
37
) proportional to
U_{\bm{p}}(p\hat{r})e^{ipr}/r
. This makes the scheme consistent: starting from the assumption that the asymptotics of the wavefunction
\varphi_{\bm{p}}(\bm{r})
is the sum of the plane wave and the spherical wave proportional to
e^{ipr}/r
, we derive this asymptotics from the Schrödinger equation in momentum representation.

Paley–Wiener Theorem for Probabilistic Frames
Dongwei Chen

Abstract
. This paper studies the Paley–Wiener theorem for probabilistic frames. The Paley–Wiener theorem claims that if a sequence is close to a basis in a Banach space, then this sequence is also a basis. Similar results are extended to frames in Hilbert spaces. In this work, we claim that if a probability measure is close to a probabilistic frame, this probability measure is also a probabilistic frame. Moreover, based on operator theory, we obtain the frame bounds for such probability measures that are close to a given probabilistic frame in the 2-Wasserstein metric, which alternatively shows the fact that the set of probabilistic frames is open in the 2-Wasserstein topology.

1.
Introduction

Proposed by Paley and Wiener, the Paley–Wiener theorem is a classical result about the stability of a basis in a Hilbert space
[
1
]
.
Later, Boas noticed that Paley and Wiener’s proof still held in a Banach space:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 1 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\{{\bf x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a basis for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
with norm
\|\cdot\|
. Suppose
\{{\bf y}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{X}
such that

\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}({\bf x}_{i}-{\bf y}_{i})\Big\|\leq\lambda\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}{\bf x}_{i}\Big\|

for some constant
0\leq\lambda<1
and all choices of scalars
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\ (n=1,2,3,\dots)
. Then
\{{\bf y}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a basis for the Banach space
\mathcal{X}
.

Since then, this theorem have been generalized to many topics including
entire functions of exponential type
[
3
]
, frames in Hilbert spaces
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and their extensions, such as Banach frames
[
7
]
, g–frames
[
8
]
, and operator represented frames
[
9
]
. As the extension of orthonormal basis, frames were firstly introduced by Duffin and Schaeffer in the context of nonharmonic analysis
[
10
]
and have been applied in many areas, for instance, the Kadison-Singer problem
[
11
]
, time-frequency analysis
[
12
]
, and wavelet analysis
[
13
]
.

Recall that a sequence
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
(frame bounds) such that for any
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}
,

A\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|{\bf f}\|^{2}.

Furthermore,
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is called a tight frame if
A=B
and Parseval if
A=B=1
.
Compared to a basis in
\mathcal{H}
, a frame permits linear dependence between elements and also allows each vector to be written as a linear combination of frame elements in a redundant way.
In signal process, there are two ways to reconstruct a signal by frames . One is through tight frames. If
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a tight frame with bound
A>0
, then for any
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}
,
{\bf f}=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf f}_{i}.

The other way uses dual frames. A sequence
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{H}
is called a
dual frame
of
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
if for any
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}
,

{\bf f}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf g}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf f}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf g}_{i}.

An example of dual frames for
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is the canonical dual frame
\{{\bf S}^{-1}{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
where
{\bf S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is called the
frame operator
, which is given by

{\bf S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\ \ \ {\bf S}({\bf f})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}_{i}\right\rangle{\bf f}_{i},\ \forall\ {\bf f}\in\mathcal{H}.

Indeed,
{\bf S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is bounded and invertible. See
[
14
]
for more details on frames.

As a particular case of the Paley-Wiener theorem for frames, it is well-known that if
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with bounds
A
and
B
, and
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}
is quadratically close to a sequence
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}
, that is to say, if

K:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\|{\bf f}_{i}-{\bf g}_{i}\|^{2}<A,

then
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}
is also a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{K})^{2}
and
(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{K})^{2}

[
5
]
. In this work, we get a similar result for probabilistic frames.

As proposed in
[
15
,
16
]
, a probability measure
\mu
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
probabilistic frame
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
(frame bounds) such that for any
\bf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

A\|\bf{x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle\bf{x},\bf{y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu(\bf{y})\leq B\|\bf{x}\|^{2}.

It has been shown that the set of probabilistic frames is open in the 2-Wasserstein metric
[
17
,
18
]
, meaning that if a probability measure is close enough to a probabilistic frame in the 2-Wasserstein metic, this measure is also a probabilistic frame.

As stated in Corollary
1.3
, one of our contributions in this work is that we also find the frame bounds for such probability measures, which alternatively shows the openness of probabilistic frames. The following proposition is the "quadratic close" version of probabilistic frames. The proof placed in the last section is mainly based on the invertibility of some operators.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any transport coupling
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, suppose

\lambda:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})<A,

then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

If
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is optimal, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose

W_{2}(\mu,\nu)<\sqrt{A},

then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
(\sqrt{A}-W_{2}(\mu,\nu))^{2}
and
M_{2}(\nu)
. Furthermore, the set of probabilistic frames is open in the 2-Wasserstein topology.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
in Proposition
1.2
be the optimal coupling with respect to
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
, and the openness is trivial.
∎

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce probabilistic frames, optimal transport, and the invertibility of linear operators on Banach spaces.
In Section
3
, we first generalize the Paley-Wiener theorem to probabilistic frames and then give a sufficient perturbation condition using probabilistic dual frames. In the end, we give the proof of Proposition
1.2
and Proposition
3.10
.

2.
Mathematical Preliminaries

2.1.
Probabilistic Frames and Optimal Transport

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite
2
-moment
M_{2}(\mu)
, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):M_{2}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}d\mu({\bf x})<+\infty\}.

Let
B_{r}({\bf x})
be the open ball centered at
{\bf x}
with radius
r>0
. The
support
of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by
supp(\mu)=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for any}\ r>0,\mu(B_{r}({\bf x}))>0\}.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a measurable map.
f_{\#}\mu
is called the
pushforward
of
\mu
by the map
f
, and
f_{\#}\mu(E):=(\mu\circ f^{-1})(E)=\mu\big(f^{-1}(E)\big)\ \text{for any Borel set}\ E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}.

If
f
is linear given by a matrix
{\bf A}
, then
{\bf A}_{\#}\mu
is used to denote
f_{\#}\mu
.

Note that taking
\mu_{f}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N}\delta_{{\bf y}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the frame definition is equivalent to

\frac{A}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu_{f}({\bf y})\leq\frac{B}{N}\|{\bf x}\|^{2},\ \text{for any}\ {\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which takes frames as discrete probabilistic measures. Inspired by this insight, M. Ehler and K. A. Okoudjou in
[
15
,
16
]
proposed the concept of probabilistic frame:

Definition 2.1
.

\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
probabilistic frame
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

A\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle|^{2}d\mu({\bf y})\leq B\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

\mu
is called a tight probabilistic frame if
A=B
and Parseval if
A=B=1
. Moreover,
\mu
is said to be a Bessel probability measure if only the upper bound holds.

Clearly if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mu
is Bessel with bound
M_{2}(\mu)
. Furthermore, we could define the
frame operator

{\bf S}_{\mu}
for the probabilistic frame
\mu
as the following matrix:

{\bf S}_{\mu}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf y}{\bf y}^{t}d\mu({\bf y}).

Then we could characterize probabilistic frames via frame operators.

Proposition 2.2

(Theorem 12.1 in
[
16
]
, Proposition 3.1 in
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(1)

\mu
is a probabilistic frame
\Leftrightarrow

{\bf S}_{\mu}>0
(positive definite)
\Leftrightarrow

\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
span\{supp(\mu)\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

\mu
is a tight probabilistic frame with bound
A>0

\Leftrightarrow

{\bf S}_{\mu}=A\ {\bf Id}
.

Since probabilistic frames are in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then one can use the 2-Wasserstein metric
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
to quantify the distance between two probabilistic frames
\mu
and
\nu
:
W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu):=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}{\text{inf}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right\|^{2}\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf y})
.
Here
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
is the set of transport couplings with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
, and is given by
\Gamma(\mu,\nu):=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}):{\pi_{{x}}}_{\#}\gamma=\mu,\ {\pi_{{y}}}_{\#}\gamma=\nu\}

where
\pi_{{x}}
,
\pi_{{y}}
are the projections: for any
({\bf x},{\bf y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\pi_{{x}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf x},\pi_{{y}}({\bf x},{\bf y})={\bf y}
. Another fact is that
{\bf S}_{\mu}
is symmetric and
{\bf S}_{\mu}>0
if
\mu
is a frame with bounds
A
and
B
. Let
\|{\bf S}_{\mu}\|_{2}
be the 2-matrix norm of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
which is the largest eigenvalue of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
. Since each eigenvalue of
{\bf S}_{\mu}
is between
A
and
B
, then

A\leq\|{\bf S}_{\mu}\|_{2}\leq B,\ 1/B\leq\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1/A\ \text{and}\ 1/\sqrt{B}\leq\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}\|_{2}\leq 1/\sqrt{A}.

One can also reconstruct a signal by tight probabilistic frames and
probabilistic dual frames
which are also known as transport duals
[
20
]
. Note that every transport dual is a probabilistic frame.

Definition 2.3
.

The set of
transport duals
of the probabilistic frame
\mu
is given by

D_{\mu}:=\Big\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\exists\ \gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)\ \text{s.t.}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\bf x}{\bf y}^{t}d\gamma(x,y)={\bf Id}\Big\}.

There are two special frames relate to
\mu
:
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}}_{\#}\mu
and
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu
. The former is called the canonical Parseval frame of
\mu
and the later the canonical dual frame of
\mu
(with bounds
\frac{1}{B}
and
\frac{1}{A}
). Note that
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu\in D_{\mu}
since
\gamma:={({\bf Id}\times{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})}_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu)
.
Then, we have the following two reconstructions: for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(2.1)

{\bf f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1/2}{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x});

(2.2)

{\bf f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x}).

For interested readers, we refer to
[
15
,
21
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
16
,
22
,
20
,
23
,

,

]
for more details on probabilistic frames, and
[

]
for Wasserstein distance and optimal transport.

2.2.
Invertibility of Linear Operators on Banach Spaces

This subsection introduces the invertibility of linear operators on Banach spaces. It is well-known that a bound linear operator
U
on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
is invertible and
\|U^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\|I-U\|}
if
\|I-U\|<1
,
where
I
is identity operator in
\mathcal{X}
. Casazza and Christensen generalized the above result to the following lemma and corollary.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 1 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
U:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
a linear operator on
\mathcal{X}
. If there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1)
such that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\|U{\bf x}-{\bf x}\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|{\bf x}\|+\lambda_{2}\|U{\bf x}\|.

Then
U
is bounded invertible, and for any
{\bf x}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|{\bf x}\|\leq\|U{\bf x}\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|{\bf x}\|,\ \frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\|{\bf x}\|\leq\|U^{-1}{\bf x}\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|{\bf x}\|.

Corollary 2.5

(Remark of Corollary 1 in
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are Banach spaces. Let
U:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a bounded linear operator,
\mathcal{X}_{0}
a dense subspace of
\mathcal{X}
, and
V:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
a linear mapping. If for any
{\bf x}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
,

\|U{\bf x}-V{\bf x}\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|U{\bf x}\|+\lambda_{2}\|V{\bf x}\|+\delta\|{\bf x}\|,

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\delta\in[0,1)
. Then
V
has a unique extension to a bounded linear operator (of the same norm) from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{Y}
, and the extension still satisfies the inequality.

3.
Probabilistic Frame Perturbations

Casazza and Christensen generalized the Paley–Wiener Theorem to study the stability of frames in Hilbert spaces as below.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 2 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a frame for a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
, and
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose there exist constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\delta\geq 0
such that
\text{max}\ (\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}},\lambda_{2})<1
and

\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}({\bf f}_{i}-{\bf g}_{i})\Big\|\leq\lambda_{1}\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}{\bf f}_{i}\Big\|+\lambda_{2}\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}{\bf g}_{i}\Big\|+\delta\Big[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|c_{i}|^{2}\Big]^{1/2}

for all scalars
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}(n=1,2,3,\dots)
. Then
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with bounds

A\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{2}\ \text{and}\ B\Big(1+\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{B}}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{2}.

Furthermore, one could use dual frames in the frame perturbations:

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem 2.1 in
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a frame for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
, and
\{{\bf h}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
a dual frame of
\{{\bf f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with upper frame bound
0<D<\infty
. Suppose
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
such that

\alpha:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|{\bf f}_{i}-{\bf g}_{i}\|^{2}<\infty,\ \beta:=\ \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|{\bf f}_{i}-{\bf g}_{i}\|\|{\bf h}_{i}\|<1.

Then
\{{\bf g}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a frame in
\mathcal{H}
with bounds
\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}}{D}
and
B(1+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{B}})
.

Inspired by Theorem
3.1
, we establish the Paley-Wiener type theorem for probabilistic frames in the following subsection. We use Casazza and Christensen’s criteria on the invertibility of linear operators.
Through out the paper, we denote
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with compact support.

3.1.
Paley-Wiener Theorem for Probabilistic Frames

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose there exist constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\delta\geq 0
such that
\text{max}\ (\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}},\lambda_{2})<1
and for all
w\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\begin{split}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf x}){\bf x}d\mu({\bf x})&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf y}){\bf y}d\nu({\bf y})\Big\|\\
&\leq\lambda_{1}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf x}){\bf x}d\mu({\bf x})\Big\|+\lambda_{2}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf y}){\bf y}d\nu({\bf y})\Big\|+\delta\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.\end{split}

Then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{A^{2}(1-(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}}))^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{2})^{2}M_{2}(\nu)}
and
M_{2}(\nu)
.

Proof.

Since
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\nu
is Bessel with bound
M_{2}(\nu)
. Now let us get the lower frame bound. Define
U:L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
T:L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the following

U(w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf x}){\bf x}d\mu({\bf x}),\ T(w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w({\bf y}){\bf y}d\nu({\bf y}).

Clearly
U
is bounded linear and
\|U\|^{2}\leq M_{2}(\mu)
. Furthermore,
T
is linear and well-defined (bounded). Since
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
, then by the theorem assumption and Corollary
2.5
, we know that
T
could be extended uniquely to a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
that is still denoted by
T
, and for any
w\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

(3.1)

\|U(w)-T(w)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|U(w)\|+\lambda_{2}\|T(w)\|+\delta\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Therefore, for any
w\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

\|T(w)\|\leq\|U(w)\|+\|U(w)-T(w)\|\leq\big((\lambda_{1}+1)\big\|U\|+\delta\big)\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\lambda_{2}\|T(w)\|.

Thus
T
is bounded and
\|T\|\leq\frac{(\lambda_{1}+1)\|U\|+\delta}{1-\lambda_{2}}<+\infty.

Define
U^{+}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)
by

(U^{+}{\bf x})(\cdot):=(U^{*}(UU^{*})^{-1}{\bf x})(\cdot)=(U^{*}({\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}))(\cdot)=\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x},\cdot\right\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu).

where
U^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
U
and
UU^{*}={\bf S}_{\mu}
. Then

\begin{split}\|U^{+}{\bf x}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\mu({\bf y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d({{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu)({\bf y})\leq\frac{1}{A}\|{\bf x}\|^{2},\end{split}

where the inequality is due to the frame property of
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu
with upper bound
\frac{1}{A}
. For any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, replacing
w
in Equation
3.1
with
U^{+}{\bf x}
leads to

\begin{split}\|{\bf x}-T(U^{+}{\bf x})\|\leq(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}})\|{\bf x}\|+\lambda_{2}\|T(U^{+}{\bf x})\|.\end{split}

Since
\text{max}(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}},\lambda_{2})<1
, then, by Lemma
2.4
,
TU^{+}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is invertible, and
\|(TU^{+})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}})}.

Then, any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
could be written as

{\bf x}=TU^{+}(TU^{+})^{-1}{\bf x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}(TU^{+})^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle{\bf y}d\nu({\bf y}).

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|{\bf x}\|^{4}&=\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf x}\right\rangle^{2}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}(TU^{+})^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle d\nu({\bf y})\Big|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}(TU^{+})^{-1}{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\\
&\leq\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\ \|(TU^{+})^{-1}\|^{2}\ \|{\bf x}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf y}\|^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\\
&\leq\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{A^{2}}\Big(\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}})}\Big)^{2}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y}),\end{split}

where the first inequality comes from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in
L^{2}(\nu)
, and
\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}
is the 2-matrix norm of
{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}
with
\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{A}
.
Thus, for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\frac{A^{2}(1-(\lambda_{1}+\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{A}}))^{2}}{(1+\lambda_{2})^{2}M_{2}(\nu)}\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\leq M_{2}(\nu)\ \|{\bf x}\|^{2}.\qed

The following lemma is inspired by the case
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0,\delta=\sqrt{R}
. However, we only need the definition of probabilistic frames for the proof.

Lemma 3.4

(Sweetie’s Lemma)
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose there exists a constant
R
where
0<R<A
, such that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\mu({\bf y})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf z})\Big|\leq R\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

Then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
A-R
and
B+R
.

Proof.

From the lemma condition, we know that for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\mu({\bf y})-R\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf z})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\mu({\bf y})+R\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.

Since
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
A
and
B
, then for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A-R)\|{\bf x}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf z})\leq(B+R)\|{\bf x}\|^{2}.\qed

Remark 3.5
.

Lemma
3.4
could be improved to any
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
. If for any
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}-\left\langle{\bf x},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\gamma({\bf y},{\bf z})\Big|\leq R\|{\bf x}\|^{2},

then
\nu
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
A-R
and
B+R
.

3.2.
Perturbations Including Probabilistic Dual Frames

Similar to Theorem
3.2
, we give a sufficient pertubation condition where the probabilistic dual frames are used in this subsection.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose
\mu
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nu
is a probabilistic dual frame of
\mu
with respect to
\gamma_{12}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
. Let
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma_{23}\in\Gamma(\nu,\eta)
. Then there exists
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
with marginals
\gamma_{12}
and
\gamma_{23}
, and if

\sigma:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|\|{\bf y}\|d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})<1,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{(1-\sigma)^{2}}{M_{2}(\nu)}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.
And if the upper frame bound for
\nu
is
D>0
, then the frame bounds for
\eta
are
\frac{(1-\sigma)^{2}}{D}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.

Proof.

By Gluing Lemma
[
26
, pp.59]
, there exists
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\pi_{xy}}_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\gamma_{12},\ {\pi_{yz}}_{\#}\tilde{\pi}=\gamma_{23}
where
\pi_{xy}
and
\pi_{yz}
are projections to
({\bf x},{\bf y})
and
({\bf y},{\bf z})
coordinates.
Since
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\eta
is Bessel with bound
M_{2}(\eta)
. Next define a linear operator
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

L({\bf f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle{\bf z}\ d\gamma_{23}({\bf y},{\bf z}),\ \text{for any}\ {\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
\nu
is the probabilistic dual frame of
\mu
with respect to
\gamma_{12}\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, then

{\bf f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\gamma_{12}({\bf x},{\bf y}),\ \text{for any}\ {\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}-L({\bf f})\|&=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle({\bf x}-{\bf z})\ d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z})\Big\|\leq\sigma\|{\bf f}\|<\|{\bf f}\|.\end{split}

Thus
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is invertible and
\|L^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}
. Note that for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\bf f}=LL^{-1}({\bf f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle{\bf z}\ d\gamma_{23}({\bf y},{\bf z}).

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}\|^{4}&=\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}\right\rangle^{2}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle\ d\gamma_{23}({\bf y},{\bf z})\Big|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z})\leq\frac{M_{2}(\nu)}{(1-\sigma)^{2}}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z}),\end{split}

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy Schwarz inequality in
L^{2}(\gamma_{23})
and
\gamma_{23}\in\Gamma(\nu,\eta)
. Thus, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\frac{(1-\sigma)^{2}}{M_{2}(\nu)}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\leq\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z}).

Therefore,
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{(1-\sigma)^{2}}{M_{2}(\nu)}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.
If the upper frame bound for the probabilistic dual frame
\nu
is
0<D<\infty
, then

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}\|^{4}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf y}\right\rangle^{2}d\nu({\bf y})\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z})\leq\frac{D\|{\bf f}\|^{2}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z}).\end{split}

In this case, the frame bounds for
\eta
are
\frac{(1-\sigma)^{2}}{D}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.
∎

We have the following corollary by using the canonical probabilistic dual frame.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
, and
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If

\hat{\sigma}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\|\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|d\mu({\bf x})d\eta({\bf z})<1

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
A(1-\hat{\sigma})^{2}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
.

Proof.

Let
\nu={{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu
, the dual frame of
\mu
with respect to
\gamma_{12}:={({\bf Id}\times{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1})}_{\#}\mu\in\Gamma(\mu,{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu)
, and
\gamma_{23}:={{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu\otimes\eta\in\Gamma({{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu,\eta)
. Then, by Gluing Lemma
[
26
, pp.59]
,
\tilde{\pi}:=\gamma_{12}\otimes\eta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus

\hat{\sigma}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\|d\mu({\bf x})d\eta({\bf z})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|\|{\bf y}\|d\tilde{\pi}({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf z}).

Since
\hat{\sigma}<1
and the upper frame bound of
{{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}}_{\#}\mu
is
\frac{1}{A}
,
then by Theorem
3.6
,
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
A(1-\hat{\sigma})^{2}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
.
∎

Remark 3.8

Since
\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\|\leq\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}\|{\bf x}\|\leq\frac{1}{A}\|{\bf x}\|

\hat{\epsilon}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|\ \|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|d\mu({\bf x})d\eta({\bf z})<A,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
A(1-\hat{\sigma})^{2}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
, since
\hat{\sigma}\leq\frac{\hat{\epsilon}}{A}<1
.

Indeed, Corollary
3.7
and Remark
3.8
could be generalized to any
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\eta)
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mu
be a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
, and
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\eta)
, suppose

\epsilon:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}\|\ \|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})<A,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{(A-\epsilon)^{2}}{B}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
,
and if

\chi:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\|\ \|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})<1,

then
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{A^{2}(1-\chi)^{2}}{B}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.

Proof.

Since
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\eta
is Bessel with bound
M_{2}(\eta)
. By the reconstruction of the canonical dual frame in Equation
2.2
,
{\bf f}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf x}\ d\mu({\bf x}),\ \text{for any}\ {\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Now define
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
L({\bf f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf z}\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z}).

Therefore,

\|{\bf f}-L({\bf f})\|=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle({\bf x}-{\bf z})\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})\Big\|\leq\epsilon\ \|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}\ \|{\bf f}\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{A}\|{\bf f}\|.

Thus,
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is invertible and
\|L^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\epsilon/A}
.
Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

{\bf f}=LL^{-1}({\bf f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle{\bf z}\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z}).

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|{\bf f}\|^{4}&=\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf f}\right\rangle^{2}=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})\Big|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}L^{-1}{\bf f},{\bf x}\right\rangle^{2}d\mu({\bf x})\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z})\\
&\leq B\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\ \|L^{-1}\|^{2}\ \|{\bf f}\|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z})\leq\frac{B\|{\bf f}\|^{2}}{A^{2}(1-\frac{\epsilon}{A})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z}),\end{split}

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
L_{2}(\gamma)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
, and the second inequality comes from the frame property of
\mu
with upper frame bound
B
.
Then, for any
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\frac{(A-\epsilon)^{2}}{B}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\leq\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf z}\right\rangle^{2}d\eta({\bf z})
.
Thus,
\eta
is a probabilistic frame with bounds
\frac{(A-\epsilon)^{2}}{B}
and
M_{2}(\eta)
.
Furthermore, if

\chi:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|\|{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\|d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})<1,

then

\|{\bf f}-L({\bf f})\|=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle{\bf f},{\bf S}_{\mu}^{-1}{\bf x}\right\rangle({\bf x}-{\bf z})\ d\gamma({\bf x},{\bf z})\Big\|\leq\chi\ \|{\bf f}\|.

Therefore,
L
is invertible and
\|L^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\chi}
.
Similar arguments show that
\eta
is a probabilistic frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounds
\frac{A^{2}(1-\chi)^{2}}{B}\ \text{and}\ M_{2}(\eta)
.
∎

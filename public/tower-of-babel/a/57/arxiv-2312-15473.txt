Abstract
. We prove that the boundary of an almost minimizer of the intrinsic perimeter in a plentiful group can be approximated by intrinsic Lipschitz graphs. Plentiful groups are Carnot groups of step 2 2 whose center of the Lie algebra is generated by any co-dimension one horizontal subspace. For example, H H -type groups not isomorphic to the first Heisenberg group are plentiful. Our results provide the first extension of the regularity theory of intrinsic minimal surfaces beyond the family of Heisenberg groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Framwork

A
Carnot group
is a Lie group whose Lie algebra admits a suitable stratification in which the first layer—the so-called
horizontal distribution
—generates all the other layers
[
3
,
31
]
.
Non-commutative Carnot groups, endowed with the
Carnot–Carathéodory distance
naturally induced by the horizontal distribution, are not Riemannian at any scale, hence providing an interesting and reach setup for Analysis.

The study of Geometric Measure Theory in Carnot groups started from the pioneering work
[
12
]
, and the regularity of sets that are local minimizers for the
horizontal perimeter
, i.e., the perimeter naturally induced by the horizontal distribution, is one of the most important open problems in the field.
All regularity results known so far are limited to the
Heisenberg groups

\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, and assume some additional strong
a priori
regularity and/or some restrictive geometric structure of the minimizer
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
30
,
26
]
.
On the other hand, there are examples of minimal surfaces in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
that are only Lipschitz continuous in the standard sense
[
27
,
28
]
.

The first step in the celebrated De Giorgi’s regularity theory for sets of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is based on a good approximation of the boundary of minimizing sets
[
15
,
19
]
, namely, the so-called
Lipschitz approximation
.
In the original strategy, the approximation is made by convolution and the estimates strongly rely on a

monotonicity formula
.
However, the validity of such a formula is an open problem in the sub-Riemannian setting
[
9
]
.
A more flexible approach has been proposed in
[
29
]
by means of Lipschitz graphs.
Although the boundary of sets with finite horizontal perimeter may be quite irregular from an Euclidean point of view
[
17
]
, the natural notion of
intrinsic Lipschitz graphs

[
11
,
14
]
turns out to be effective in the approximation within this framework
[
25
,
23
,
26
]
.

1.2.
Main result

In the present paper, we provide an extension of the approximation by means of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs in the Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
, achieved in
[
25
,
23
]
, in a more general class of Carnot groups of step
2
, that we call
plentiful groups
.

In a nutshell, plentiful groups are characterized by the property that any co-dimension
1
subspace of the first layer of their Lie algebra still generates the second layer (see
Section

3
).
The class of plentiful groups not only includes the important family of
H
-type groups

[
16
]
, but also other interesting examples (see
Example

3.4
below).

Our main result can be stated as follows, see
Sections

2
and
4
for the notation.
For an even more general result concerning
almost minimizers
, see
Theorem

5.2
.

Theorem 1.1

(Intrinsic Lipschitz approximation)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a plentiful group.
For any
L\in(0,1)
, there exist
\varepsilon,C>0
, depending on
L
only, with the following property.
If
\nu
is a horizontal direction and
E\subset\mathbb{G}
is a minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in the cylinder
C_{324}
with intrinsic cylindrical excess
{\mathbf{e}}(E,0,324,\nu)\leq\varepsilon
and
0\in\partial E
, then, letting

M=C_{1}\cap\partial E,\quad M_{0}=\left\{q\in M:\sup_{0<r<16}{\mathbf{e}}(E,q,r,\nu)\leq\varepsilon\right\},

there exists an intrinsic Lipschitz funciton
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{W}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{\mathbb{W}}|\varphi|\leq L,\quad\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi)\leq c_{\mathbb{G}}\,L,

M_{0}\subset M\cap\Gamma,\quad\Gamma=\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;D_{1}),

\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\bigtriangleup\Gamma)\leq C\,{\mathbf{e}}(E,0,324,\nu),

\int_{D_{1}}|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n-1}\leq C\,{\mathbf{e}}(E,0,324,\nu),

where
c_{\mathbb{G}}>0
is a structural constant independent of
L
.

Theorem

1.1
perfectly generalizes
[
25
]
*Th. 5.1 to plentiful groups, in fact providing a sub-optimal version of the Lipschitz approximation proved in
[
23
]
*Th. 3.1 in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
(also see
[
19
]
*Th. 23.7 for the corresponding result in the Euclidean setting).

In
Theorem

1.1
, differently from the corresponding result in
[
23
]
, the constants
\varepsilon
and
C
may depend on the chosen Lipschitz constant
L
.
This is due to the current lack of an analog of the deep
height estimate
proved in
[
24
]
for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, in plentiful groups.
However, we believe that the algebraic framework provided by plentiful groups is the correct setting where to possibly extend
Theorem

1.1
to its optimal version. The validity of the height estimate in the context of plentiful groups will be the object of future works.

2.1.
Carnot groups

A Carnot group
(\mathbb{G},\star)
is a connected, simply connected and nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of left-invariant vector fields has dimension
n
and admits a stratification of step
s\in\mathbb{N}
, that is,

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

where the vector spaces
V_{1},\dots,V_{s}\subset\mathfrak{g}
satisfy

V_{i}=[V_{1},V_{i-1}]\quad\text{for}\ i=1,\dots,s-1,\quad[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}.

We set
m_{i}=\dim(V_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
We fix an
adapted basis
of
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. a basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
such that

X_{h_{i-1}+1},\dots,X_{h_{i}}\ \text{is a basis of}\ V_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,s.

We endow the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the left-invariant Riemannian metric
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
that makes the basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
orthonormal.
Exploiting the exponential identification
p=\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}X_{i}\right)
, we can identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, endowed with the group law determined by the Campbell–Hausdorff formula.
In particular, the identity
e\in\mathbb{G}
corresponds to
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the
inversion map
becomes
\iota(p)=p^{-1}=-p
for any
p\in\mathbb{G}
. Moreover, it is not restrictive to assume that
X_{i}(0)=\mathrm{e}_{i}
for any
i=1,\dots,n
.
Therefore, by left-invariance, we get

X_{i}(p)=d\tau_{p}\mathrm{e}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\tau_{p}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
is the
left-translation
by
p\in\mathbb{G}
, i.e.
\tau_{p}(q)=p\star q
for any
q\in\mathbb{G}
.

For any
i=1,\dots,n
, the
degree

d(i)\in\left\{1,\dots,\kappa\right\}
of the basis vector field
X_{i}
is
d(i)=j
if and only if
X_{i}\in V_{j}
.
The group
dilations

(\delta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\geq 0}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
are hence given by

\delta_{\lambda}(p)=\delta_{\lambda}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=(\lambda p_{1},\dots,\lambda^{d(i)}p_{i},\dots,\lambda^{s}p_{n})\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{G}.

The Haar measure of the group
\mathbb{G}
coincides with the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathscr{L}^{n}
.
The homogeneity property
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\delta_{\lambda}(E))=\lambda^{Q}\mathscr{L}^{n}(E)
holds for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
, where
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}i\dim(V_{i})\in\mathbb{N}
is the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
.
For notational convenience, we use the shorthand
|E|=\mathscr{L}^{n}(E)
.

Following
[
13
]
*Th. 5.1, we fix the left-invariant and homogeneous distance
d_{\infty}(p,q)=d_{\infty}(q^{-1}\cdot p,0)
for
p,q\in\mathbb{G}
, where, identifying
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{s}}
as above and letting
\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
be the canonical projection for
i=1,\dots,s
,

(2.1)

d_{\infty}(p,0)=\max\left\{\epsilon_{i}|\pi_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}(p)|_{\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}}^{1/i}:i=1,\dots,s\right\}\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{G},

with constants
\epsilon_{1}=1
and
\epsilon_{i}\in(0,1)
for all
i=2,\dots,s
depending on the structure of
\mathbb{G}
.
We use the shorthand
\|p\|_{\infty}=d_{\infty}(p,0)
for
p\in\mathbb{G}
.
Consequently, for
p\in\mathbb{G}
and
r>0
, we define the open and closed balls

B_{r}(p)=\left\{q\in\mathbb{G}:d(q,p)<r\right\},\quad\bar{B}_{r}(p)=\left\{q\in\mathbb{G}:d(q,p)\leq r\right\},

with the shorthands
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
and
\bar{B}_{r}=\bar{B}_{r}(0)
.

2.2.
Sets of finite perimeter

A set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
is of
locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter
in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
if there exists a
\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
-valued Radon measure
\mu_{E}
on
\Omega
such that

\int_{E}{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\langle\phi,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}\rangle\quad\text{for all}\ \phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}).

Here and in the following,
{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{G}}\phi=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}X_{i}\phi_{i}
is the
horizontal divergence
of
\phi
.
If
|\mu_{E}|(\Omega)<+\infty
, then
E
has
finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter
in
\Omega
.
We also use the notation
P(E;A)=|\mu_{E}|(A)
for any Borel
A\subset\mathbb{G}
and the shorthand
P(E)=P(E;\mathbb{G})
.
If
E\subset\mathbb{G}
has (Euclidean) Lipschitz topological boundary
\partial E
, then

(2.2)

P(E;\Omega)=\int_{\partial E\cap\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\left\langle N_{E},X_{i}\right\rangle^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

where
N_{E}
is the standard (inner) unit normal to
\partial E
and
\mathscr{H}^{s}
is the standard
s
-Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
s\in[0,n]
.
By the Radon–Nykodim Theorem, there is a Borel function
\nu_{E}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
, called (
measure-theoretic
)
inner horizontal normal
of
E
in
\Omega
, such that
\mu_{E}=\nu_{E}|\mu_{E}|
with
|\nu_{E}|=1

\mu_{E}
-a.e. in
\Omega
.
The
reduced boundary
of
E
is the set
\partial^{*}E
of
p\in\mathbb{G}
such that

p\in\mathrm{supp}|\mu_{E}|\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{E}(p)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu_{E}(B_{r}(p))}{|\mu_{E}|(B_{r}(xp))}\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{1}}.

The (
measure-theoretic
)
boundary
of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
is

(2.3)

\partial E=\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:|E\cap B_{r}(p)|>0\ \text{and}\ |E^{c}\cap B_{r}(p)|>0\ \text{for all}\ r>0\right\}.

Up to modify a set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
of locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in an
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-negligible way, arguing
verbatim
as in
[
19
]
*Prop. 12.19, we can always assume that
\partial E
coincides with the topological boundary of
E
.

2.3.
Perimeter minimizers

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be a (non-empty) open set and let
E\subset\mathbb{G}
be a set with locally
finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\mathbb{G}
.
We say that the set
E
is a

(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\Omega
if there exist
\Lambda\in[0,+\infty)
and
r_{0}\in(0,+\infty]
such that

P(E;B_{r}(p))\leq P(F;B_{r}(p))+\Lambda\,|E\bigtriangleup F|

for any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{G}
,
p\in\Omega
and
r<r_{0}
such that

E\bigtriangleup F\Subset B_{r}(p)\Subset\Omega
.
If
\Lambda=0
and
r_{0}=\infty
, then
E
is a
locally

\mathbb{G}
-perimeter minimizer in
\Omega
, that is,

P(E;B_{r}(p))\leq P(F;B_{r}(p))

for any measurable set
F\subset\mathbb{G}
,
p\in\Omega
and
r>0
such that

E\bigtriangleup F\Subset B_{r}(p)\Subset\Omega
.

Remark 2.1

(Scaling of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers)
.

If the set
E
is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
, then the set
E_{p,r}=\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(\tau_{p^{-1}}(E))
is a
(\Lambda^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime})
-minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\Omega_{p,r}=\delta_{\frac{1}{r}}(\tau_{p^{-1}}(\Omega))
for every
p\in\mathbb{G}
and
r>0
, where

\Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda r
and
r_{0}^{\prime}=r_{0}/r
.
In particular, the product
\Lambda r_{0}
is invariant by dilation, and thus it is convenient to assume that
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1
, as we
shall always do in the following.

2.4.
Carnot groups of step 2

From now on, we work in a Carnot group
(\mathbb{G},\star)
of step
s=2
, so that
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
,
[V_{1},V_{1}]=V_{2}
,
[V_{1},V_{2}]=\left\{0\right\}
,
n=m_{1}+m_{2}
and
Q=m_{1}+2m_{2}
.
We fix an adapted orthonormal basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}},T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{2}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, so that
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}}
and
T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{2}}
are orthonormal bases of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively.
As well known (see
[
3
]
*Sec. 3.2 for instance), exploiting exponential coordinates associated to
X_{1},\dots,X_{m_{1}},T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{2}}
,

(2.4)

p\star q=(x,t)\star(\xi,\tau)=\left(x+\xi,t+\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle\right)

for
p,q\in\mathbb{G}
, with
p=(x,t)
,
q=(\xi,\tau)
,
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
,
t,\tau\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}
, where
{\mathsf{B}}=({\mathsf{B}}^{1},\dots,{\mathsf{B}}^{m_{2}})
is an
m_{2}
-tuple of linearly independent skew-symmetric
m_{1}\times m_{1}
matrices and

\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle=\left(\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}^{1}x,\xi\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}^{m_{2}}x,\xi\right\rangle\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}.

With this notation, we recognize that
\|p\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{|x|,\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{|t|}\right\}
and
\delta_{\lambda}(p)=\delta_{\lambda}(x,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)
for
\lambda\geq 0
and
p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}
.
Finally, we let
{\mathcal{C}}\in(0,+\infty)
be such that

(2.5)

\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle\right|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\,|x|\,|\xi|\quad\text{for all}\ x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}.

2.5.
Stratified changes of coordinates

Let
X_{1}^{\prime},\dots,X_{m_{1}}^{\prime}
be another orthonormal basis of
V_{1}
.
Given
p\in\mathbb{G}
, let
p=(x^{\prime},t)
be the exponential coordinates associated with the adapted basis
X_{1}^{\prime},\dots,X_{m_{1}}^{\prime},T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{2}}
.
Then
x^{\prime}=Mx
, for a suitable orthogonal
m_{1}\times m_{1}
matrix
M
.
Being
M
orthogonal,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is not affected by this change of coordinates. Moreover, in these new coordinates,

p\star q=(x^{\prime},t)\star(\xi^{\prime},\tau)=\left(x^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime},+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right\rangle\right),

where
{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}=({{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}^{1},\dots,{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}^{m_{2}})
and
{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}^{j}=M{\mathsf{B}}^{j}M^{T}
for any
j=1,\ldots,m_{2}
. Notice that

\sup_{x^{\prime}\neq 0}\frac{|{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}^{j}x^{\prime}|}{|x^{\prime}|}=\sup_{x^{\prime}\neq 0}\frac{|M{\mathsf{B}}^{j}M^{T}x^{\prime}|}{|x^{\prime}|}=\sup_{x^{\prime}\neq 0}\frac{|{\mathsf{B}}^{j}M^{T}x^{\prime}|}{|x^{\prime}|}=\sup_{x^{\prime}\neq 0}\frac{|{\mathsf{B}}^{j}M^{T}x^{\prime}|}{|M^{T}x^{\prime}|}=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{|{\mathsf{B}}^{j}x|}{|x|}

for any
j=1,\ldots,m_{2}
, which in turn implies that

(2.6)

\left|\left\langle{{\tilde{\mathsf{B}}}}x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\,|x^{\prime}|\,|\xi^{\prime}|,

with the same constant
{\mathcal{C}}
as in (
2.5
). We stress that, although the above change of coordinates induces an isometry of
\mathfrak{g}
, it may not be a group morphism (e.g., see
[
20
]
*Ex. 2.15).
In fact, a simple computation shows that
M
induces a group morphism if and only if
{\mathsf{B}}^{j}M=M{\mathsf{B}}^{j}
for every
j=1,\ldots,m_{2}
.

2.6.
Further properties of perimeter minimizers

In a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
of step
2
, locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter sets enjoy further regularity properties, see
[
13
]
*Sec. 3.
In particular, for any set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
with locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter,

P(E;A)=\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}(\partial^{*}E\cap A)\quad\text{for each Borel}\ A\subset\mathbb{G},

see
[
13
]
*Th. 3.10 and
[
21
]
*Th. 1.3 (as well as the discussion around
[
31
]
*Th. 5.18).
Here and in the rest of the paper, for any
E\subset\mathbb{G}
we let

\mathscr{S}^{s}_{\infty}(E)=\sup_{\delta>0}\mathscr{S}^{s}_{\infty,\delta}(E),

be the
spherical
s
-Hausdorff measure of
E
(relative to
d_{\infty}
in (
2.1
)), where, for any
\delta>0
,

\mathscr{S}^{s}_{\infty,\delta}(E)=\inf\left\{c_{\mathbb{G}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathrm{diam}_{d_{\infty}}B_{i})^{s}:E\subset\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i},\ B_{i}\ \text{$d_{\infty}$-ball with}\ \mathrm{diam}_{d_{\infty}}B_{i}<\delta\right\},

where

is a renormalizing constant that we do not need to specify here.
We can state the following results concerning the properties of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in Carnot groups of step

The proofs are straightforward adaptations of those for

-minimizers of the Euclidean perimeter in

Theorem 2.2

(Density estimates)
.

There exist
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that, if
E\subset\mathbb{G}
is a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in the open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
,
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1
,

,

, then

c_{1}\leq\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(p)|}{r^{Q}}\leq c_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{3}\leq\frac{\mu_{E}(B_{r}(p))}{r^{Q-1}}\leq c_{4}\quad\text{for}\ r\in(0,r_{0}).

In particular,
\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}\big((\partial E\setminus\partial^{*}E)\cap\Omega\big)=0
.

Proof.

The result follows by adapting the proof of
[

*Th. 21.11, invoking
[

Theorem 2.3

(Compactness)
.

If
(E_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in the open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
,
\Lambda r_{0}\leq 1
, then there exist a subsequence
(E_{j_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizer of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{G}
in
\Omega
such that

E_{j_{k}}\to E\quad\text{in}\ L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad|\mu_{E_{j_{k}}}|\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}|\mu_{E}|\quad\text{as}\ k\to+\infty.

Moreover,
(\partial E_{j_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\partial E
in the sense of Kuratowski, i.e.:

(i)

if
p_{j_{k}}\in\partial E_{j_{k}}\cap\Omega
and
p_{j_{k}}\to p\in\Omega
as

, then
p\in\partial E
;

(ii)

if
p\in\partial E\cap\Omega
, then there exist
p_{j_{k}}\in\partial E_{j_{k}}\cap\Omega
such that
p_{j_{k}}\to p
as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

The result follows by adapting the proof of
[
19
]
*Prop. 21.13 and Th. 21.14, exploiting the density estimates provided by
Theorem

2.2
.
Details are omitted.
∎

We underline that

and

contain the only properties concerning

-minimizers of the

2.7.
Complementary subgroups

As in
[
11
]
*Sec. 4, we consider two
complementary
subgroups
{\mathbb{W}}
and
{\mathbb{V}}
of
\mathbb{G}
, i.e., such that
{\mathbb{V}}\cap{\mathbb{W}}=\left\{0\right\}
and
\mathbb{G}={\mathbb{W}}\star{\mathbb{V}}
.
We also assume that
{\mathbb{V}}
is a
1
-dimensional and, consequently, horizontal subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
.
Precisely, we have

{\mathbb{V}}=\left\{\exp(sV):s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\quad\text{for some $V\in V_{1}$ with $|V_{1}|=1$.}

In the following, in the spirit of
Section

2.5
, we will often choose an orthonormal basis
X_{1},X_{2}\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
of
V_{1}
adapted to the decomposition
{\mathbb{W}}\star{\mathbb{V}}
, that is
X_{1}=V_{1}
and

{\mathbb{V}}=\exp(\mathrm{span}\left\{X_{1}\right\}),\quad{\mathbb{W}}=\exp(\mathrm{span}\left\{X_{2},\dots,X_{m_{1}},T_{1},\dots,T_{m_{2}}\right\}).

We naturally identify

{\mathbb{V}}\equiv\mathbb{R},\quad{\mathbb{W}}\equiv\left\{p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}:x_{1}=0\right\}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

In particular,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is identified with
(0,w)\in\mathbb{G}
.
Consequently, given
A\subset{\mathbb{W}}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, any function
\varphi\colon A\subset{\mathbb{W}}\to{\mathbb{V}}
can be identified with a function
\varphi\colon A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
.

2.8.
Height function and projections

For a given
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
with
|\nu|=1
, we let the group homomorphism

\text{\large h}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\text{\large h}(p)=\langle\nu,x\rangle\quad\text{for}\ p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G},

be the
height function
.
We let
\pi_{\mathbb{V}}\colon\mathbb{G}\to{\mathbb{V}}
,
\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)=\text{\large h}(p)\nu
for
p\in\mathbb{G}
, where, with a slight abuse of notation, we identify
\nu
with
(\nu,0)\in\mathbb{G}
, be the
projection on
{\mathbb{V}}
.
Moreover, we let
\pi_{\mathbb{W}}\colon\mathbb{G}\to{\mathbb{W}}
, uniquely given by the relation

(2.7)

p=\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\star\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\quad\text{for}\ p\in\mathbb{G},

be the
projection on
{\mathbb{W}}
.
Using the shorthands
x^{\parallel}=\text{\large h}(p)\nu
and
x^{\perp}=x-x^{\parallel}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
,
and exploiting (
2.4
) we easily get that, for
p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}
,

\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)=(x^{\parallel},0),\quad\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)=\left(x^{\perp},t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right),

owing to the fact that
\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}y,y\right\rangle=0
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
by skew-symmetry.
Let us also observe that,
for
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

w\star(s\mathrm{\nu})=\exp(s\nu)(w)\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{G},

where, again with an abuse of notation, we identify
\nu
with its associated left-invariant vector field.
Finally, by definition, we can estimate

(2.8)

\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\|_{\infty}=|\langle x,\nu\rangle|\leq|x|\leq\|p\|_{\infty}

and, consequently,

(2.9)

\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty}=\|p\star\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)^{-1}\|_{\infty}\leq\|p\|_{\infty}+\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)^{-1}\|_{\infty}=\|p\|_{\infty}+\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|p\|_{\infty}.

2.9.
Disks and cylinders

We let

D_{r}=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{W}}:\|w\|_{\infty}<r\right\}

be the
open disk centered at
0\in{\mathbb{W}}
of radius
r>0
, and we set
D_{r}(w)=w\star D_{r}
for any
w\in{\mathbb{W}}
.
Note that
\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(D_{r}(w))=\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(D_{1})\,r^{Q-1}
for all
r>0
and
w\in{\mathbb{W}}
.
We also let

C_{r}=D_{r}\star(-r,r)=\left\{w\star(s\mathrm{\nu}):w\in D_{r},\ s\in(-r,r)\right\}

be the
open cylinder with central section
D_{r}
and height
2r
, and we set
C_{r}(p)=p\star C_{r}
for any
p\in\mathbb{G}
.
We also let

A\star\mathbb{R}=\left\{w\star(s\mathrm{\nu}):w\in A,\ s\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

be the
open infinite cylinder with central section
A\subset{\mathbb{W}}
.
In virtue of (
2.7
), we have that

p\in C_{r}\iff\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\in D_{r},\ \text{\large h}(p)\in(-r,r)\iff\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty}<r,\ |\text{\large h}(p)|<r

Thanks to the inequalities (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), the left-invariant map
\|\cdot\|_{C}\colon\mathbb{G}\to[0,+\infty)
,

(2.10)

\|p\|_{C}=\max\left\{\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty},|\text{\large h}(p)|\right\}\quad\text{for}\ p\in\mathbb{G},

is a quasi-norm such that
C_{r}=\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:\|p\|_{C}<r\right\}
and

(2.11)

\|p\|_{C}\leq 2\|p\|_{\infty},\quad\|p\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|p\|_{C},\quad\text{for}\ p\in\mathbb{G}.

Consequently,
d_{C}\colon\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\to[0,+\infty)
,
d_{C}(p,q)=\|q^{-1}\star p\|_{C}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{G}
, is a left-invariant quasi-distance on
\mathbb{G}
and

(2.12)

B_{r/2}(p)\subset C_{r}(p)\subset B_{2r}(p)\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{G},\ r>0.

2.10.
Cylindrical excess

A concept which plays a key role in the regularity theory of
(\Lambda,r_{0})
-minimizers of the
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter is the
cylindrical excess
, see
[
19
]
*Ch. 22 for the Euclidean setting and
[
25
,
24
,
26
,
23
]
for the Heisenberg groups.

Definition 2.4

(Cylindrical excess)
.

The
cylindrical excess
of a locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter set
E\subset\mathbb{G}
at
p\in\partial E
, at scale
r>0
, and with respect to the horizontal direction
\nu
, is

\begin{split}{\mathbf{e}}(E,p,r,\nu)&=\frac{1}{2\,r^{Q-1}}\int_{C_{r}(p)}|\nu_{E}(p)-\nu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}(p)\\
&=\frac{1}{r^{Q-1}}\int_{C_{r}(p)\cap\partial^{*}E}\left(1-\left\langle\nu_{E}(p),\nu\right\rangle^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}(p).\end{split}

If no confusion arises, we set

{\mathbf{e}}(p,r)={\mathbf{e}}(E,p,r)={\mathbf{e}}(E,p,r,\nu)
and
{\mathbf{e}}(r)={\mathbf{e}}(0,r)
.

The basic properties of the cylindrical excess introduced in
Definition

2.4
can be plainly recovered from the corresponding ones known in the Euclidean setting, see
[
19
]
*Ch. 22, and the Heisenberg groups, see
[
25
]
*Sec. 3 and
[
24
]
*Sec. 3B.
We omit the statements.
The following result corresponds to
[
24
]
*Lem. 3.4 and Cor. 3.5, which were stated in the setting of the Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
(also see
[
19
]
*Lem. 22.11 for the Euclidean case).
The very same results hold for any Carnot group of step
2
, with identical proof.

Lemma 2.5

(Excess measure)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{G}
be a set with locally finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter with
0\in\partial E
.
If there exists
s_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup\left\{|\text{\large h}(p)|:p\in C_{1}\cap\partial E\right\}\leq s_{0},

\mathscr{L}^{n-1}\left(\left\{p\in E\cap C_{1}:\text{\large h}(p)>s_{0}\right\}\right)=0,

\mathscr{L}^{n-1}\left(\left\{p\in C_{1}\setminus E:\text{\large h}(p)<-s_{0}\right\}\right)=0,

then, for a.e.
s\in(-1,1)
and any
\phi\in C_{c}(D_{1})
, letting

M=C_{1}\cap\partial^{*}E,\quad M_{s}=M\cap\left\{\text{\large h}>s\right\},\quad E_{s}=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{W}}:w\star(s\mathrm{\nu})\in E\right\},

we have

\int_{E_{s}\cap D_{1}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n-1}=\int_{M_{s}}\phi\circ\pi_{\mathbb{W}}\,\left\langle\nu_{E},\nu\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}.

Consequently, for any Borel set
G\subset D_{1}
,

\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(G)=\int_{M\cap\pi_{\mathbb{W}}^{-1}(G)}\left\langle\nu_{E},\nu\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty},

(2.13)

\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(G)\leq\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}\left(M\cap\pi_{\mathbb{W}}^{-1}(G)\right).

Moreover, we have

0\leq\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}(M_{s})-\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(E_{s}\cap D_{1})\leq{\mathbf{e}}(E,0,1)\quad\text{for a.e.}\ s\in(-1,1),

\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}(M)-\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(D_{1})={\mathbf{e}}(E,0,1).

3.
Plentiful groups

Contrarily to what happens in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the fact that
{\mathbf{e}}(E,p,r)=0
for some
p\in\partial E
and
r>0
does not necessarily imply that
\partial E
is flat in a neighborhood of
p
.
This indeed happens in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, see the example in
[
22
]
*Th. 1.5 and the characterization provided by
[
25
]
*Prop. 3.7.
Nevertheless, this is not the case for any Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, as proved in
[
25
]
*Prop. 3.6.
Consequently, in order to avoid minimal surfaces with zero excess that are not flat, we need to restrict our attention to a special class of Carnot groups, defined as follows.

Definition 3.1

(Plentiful group)
.

We say that a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
of step
2
is
plentiful
if any
V\subset V_{1}
with
\dim V=m_{1}-1
satisfies
[V,V]=V_{2}
.

The property of being plentiful is well behaved with respect to Lie group isomorphisms.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{1}
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}
be two Carnot groups of step
2
.
If
\mathbb{G}_{1}
is plentiful and
\phi\colon\mathbb{G}_{1}\to\mathbb{G}_{2}
is a Lie groups isomorphism, then also
\mathbb{G}_{2}
is plentiful.

Proof.

Set
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
, with
V_{2}=[V_{1},V_{1}]
and
W_{2}=[W_{1},W_{1}]
.
Note that
\mathrm{d}\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is an isomorphism preserving the stratification of the corresponding algebras.
Hence, letting
W\subset W_{1}
be as in
Definition

3.1
for
\mathbb{G}_{2}
,
V=(d\phi)^{-1}(W)
is an
(m-1)
-dimensional vector subspace of
V_{1}
. Thus, since
\mathbb{G}_{1}
is plentiful, we get that

[W,W]=[d\phi(V),d\phi(V)]=d\phi([V,V])=d\phi(V_{2})=W_{2},

proving that also
\mathbb{G}_{2}
is plentiful.
∎

We observe that the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is not plentiful.
More generally, every
free
Carnot group of step
2
(see
[
3
]
*Sec. 3.3 for the precise definition) is not plentiful.
On the other hand, the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is plentiful for any
n\geq 2
.
More in general, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

An
H
-type group is plentiful if and only if it is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.

We recall that a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
of step
2
is of
H
-type
if, for any
Z\in V_{2}
, the map
J_{Z}\colon V_{1}\to V_{1}
given by

(3.1)

\langle J_{Z}(X),Y\rangle=\langle Z,[X,Y]\rangle\quad\text{for any}\ X,Y\in V_{1}

is orthogonal whenever
|Z|=1
.
Notice that
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is of
H
-type for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Let
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m_{2}}
be an orthonormal basis of
V_{2}
and let
X\in V_{1}
.
By
[
3
]
*Prop. 18.1.8, for any
X\in V_{1}
, it holds that
X,J_{T_{1}}(X),\dots,J_{T_{m_{2}}}(X)
is an orthonormal subfamily of
V_{1}
, hence yielding that
m_{1}\geq m_{2}+1
. Fix
V\subset V_{1}
as in
Definition

3.1
and let
v\in V_{1}\cap V^{\perp}
be such that
|v|=1
.
We now distinguish two cases.

Case 1
.
Let us assume that
m_{1}>m_{2}+1
.
In view of (
3.1
) and
[
3
]
*Prop. 18.1.8,
J_{T_{1}}(v),\dots,J_{T_{m_{2}}}(v)
is hence an orthonormal subfamily of
V
. Moreover, again owing to the fact that
m_{1}>m_{2}+1
, there exists
w\in V
which is orthogonal to
J_{T_{1}}(v),\dots,J_{T_{m_{2}}}(v)
and satisfies
|w|=1
. Again by
[
3
]
*Prop. 18.1.8, we get

\left\langle v,J_{T_{j}}(w)\right\rangle=-\left\langle w,J_{T_{j}}(v)\right\rangle=0

for any
j=1,\ldots,m_{2}
, which implies that
J_{T_{j}}(w)\in V
for any
j=1,\ldots,m_{2}
. Since
[w,J_{T_{j}}(w)]=T_{j}
for each
j=1,\dots,m_{2}
by (
3.1
), we conclude that
[V,V]=V_{2}
, as desired.

Case 2
.
Now assume that
m_{1}=m_{2}+1
.
We can assume that
m_{1}>2
, since otherwise
\mathbb{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
We recall that
\mathbb{G}
is of
H
-type if and only if, for any
X\in V_{1}
with
|X|=1
, the map
\ad_{X}=[X,\,\cdot\,]
is a surjective isometry from
\ker(\ad_{X})^{\perp}\cap V_{1}
to
V_{2}
, see
[
16
,
8
]
.
Since
m_{1}=m_{2}+1
, we infer that
\ker(\ad_{X})^{\perp}\cap V_{1}=X^{\perp}\cap V_{1}
. Let
X\in V
be such that
|X|=1
.
By the previous considerations,
\dim(\ad_{X}(V\cap X^{\perp}))=m_{2}-1
. Let
T\in V_{2}\cap\ad_{X}(V\cap X^{\perp})^{\perp}
be such that
|T|=1
. Since
[X,J_{T}(X)]=T
and
\ad_{X}
is injective, we infer that, up to a sign,
v=J_{T}(X)
. Since
m_{1}>2,
and hence
\dim(V)>1
, let
Y\in V
be such that
|Y|=1
and
\langle X,Y\rangle=0
. By
[
16
]
, we infer that

\left\langle J_{T}(Y),v\right\rangle=\left\langle J_{T}(Y),J_{T}(X)\right\rangle=-\left\langle Y,J_{T}^{2}(X)\right\rangle=\langle Y,X\rangle=0,

and so
J_{T}(Y)\in V
.
Since
[Y,J_{T}(Y)]=T
, we get
[V,V]=V_{2}
, concluding the proof.
∎

We point out that the class of plentiful groups is broader than that of
H
-type groups.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{7,5,2}
of dimension
7
, rank
5
and step
2
, with only non-trivial commutation relations given by

[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{3},X_{4}]=T_{1},\quad[X_{1},X_{5}]=[X_{2},X_{3}]=T_{2}

(for a construction, see
[
18
]
*(27B)).
Let
\mathbb{G}_{7,5,2}
be its associated Carnot group.
In view of
[
3
]
*Prop. 18.1.5,
\mathbb{G}_{7,5,2}
is not of
H
-type.

We claim that
\mathbb{G}_{7,5,2}
is plentiful.
To this aim, let us fix
V\subset V_{1}
as in
Definition

3.1
and let
v\in V_{1}\cap V^{\perp}
be such that
|v|=1
. We let
v=\sum_{j=1}^{5}a_{j}X_{j}
, where
a_{j}=\langle v,X_{j}\rangle
.
We now observe that
W_{j}=X_{j}-a_{j}v\in V
for
j=1,\dots,5
are such that

(3.2)

[W_{1},W_{4}]+[W_{2},W_{3}]=\alpha T_{2},\quad\text{with}\ \alpha=\left(a_{1}^{2}+\left(a_{4}^{2}-a_{4}a_{5}+a_{5}^{2}\right)\right)\geq 0,

and

(3.3)

\begin{split}[W_{1},W_{2}]&=\left(1-a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2}\right)T_{1}+\left(a_{1}a_{3}-a_{2}a_{5}\right)T_{2},\\[4.30554pt]
[W_{1},W_{5}]&=-a_{2}a_{5}\,T_{1}+\left(1-a_{1}^{2}-a_{5}^{2}\right)T_{2},\\[4.30554pt]
[W_{3},W_{4}]&=\left(1-a_{3}^{2}-a_{4}^{2}\right)T_{1}+a_{2}a_{4}\,T_{2}.\end{split}

We now distinguish two cases, depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha>0
in (
3.2
).

If
\alpha=0
, then
a_{1}=a_{4}=a_{5}=0
.
Due to (
3.3
), we get
[W_{1},W_{5}]=T_{2}
,
[W_{1},W_{2}]=a_{3}^{2}\,T_{1}
and
[W_{3},W_{4}]=a_{2}^{2}\,T_{1}
, proving the claim, since either
a_{2}\neq 0
or
a_{3}\neq 0
.

If
\alpha>0
instead, then
T_{2}\in[V,V]
by (
3.2
).
Therefore, by (
3.3
), we get
(1-a_{3}^{2}-a_{4}^{2})T_{1}\in V
and
(1-a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2})T_{1}\in V
.
If
a_{3}^{2}+a_{4}^{2}\neq 1
, then
T_{1}\in V
.
If
a_{3}^{2}+a_{4}^{2}=1
instead, then
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
and so
T_{1}\in V
, proving the claim.

Our interest for plentiful groups is encoded in the following result, which is a sort of localized version of
[
13
]
*Lem. 3.6.
This result is essential in the proof of
Theorem

3.6
, where we prove that plentiful groups do not admit non-flat surfaces with zero excess.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a plentiful Carnot group.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be a non-empty connected open set and let
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m_{1}}
be an orthonormal basis of
V_{1}
.
If
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{G})
is such that
Z_{1}f\geq 0
and
Z_{i}f=0
for
i=2,\dots,m_{1}
in
\Omega
, then the level sets of
f
in
\Omega
coincide with left translations of
\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:\left\langle p,Z_{1}(0)\right\rangle=0\right\}
.

Proof.

We can assume
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, since the general case can be recovered by approximation.
Clearly,
Z_{i}Z_{j}f=0
for all
i,j=2,\dots,m_{1}
and thus, since
\mathbb{G}
is plentiful,
Tf=0
in
\Omega
for any
T\in V_{2}
.
Since the left-invariant distribution
\mathscr{D}
generated by the vector fields
\mathfrak{g}\setminus\mathrm{span}\left\{Z_{1}\right\}
is involutive,
\mathbb{G}
is foliated by smooth
(n-1)
-dimensional manifolds tangent to
\mathscr{D}
which, in
\Omega
, coincide with the level sets of
f
.
Since
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m_{1}}
are orthonormal and left-invariant, each leaf of the foliation coincides with the leaf passing through
0\in\mathbb{G}
, that is,
\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:\left\langle p,Z_{1}(0)\right\rangle=0\right\}
, up to a left translation.
∎

The following crucial result extends
[
25
]
*Prop. 3.6 to plentiful groups.
We notice that
Theorem

3.6
below can be achieved as
[
25
]
*Prop. 3.6 by a straightforward adaptation of
[
25
]
*Lem. 3.5.
However, we prove
Theorem

3.6
via a different and plainer argument, somewhat reminiscent of the proof of
[
13
]
*Claim 3.7, by exploiting
Lemma

3.5
.

Theorem 3.6

(Locally constant normal)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a plentiful Carnot group. Let
E\subset\mathbb{G}
be a set with finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
B_{r}(p)
, for
p\in\partial E
and
r>0
.
If
\nu_{E}(q)=\nu
for
\mu_{E}
-a.e.
q\in B_{r}(p)
, then

E\cap B_{r}(p)=\left\{q\in B_{r}(p):\text{\large h}(q)>\text{\large h}(p)\right\}

up to
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-negligible sets.

Proof.

We can clearly assume that
p=0
up to a translation.
Take
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
and consider the left-invariant differential operator

L_{\zeta}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\zeta_{j}X_{j}

and the test horizontal vector field
\phi=\zeta\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{r};\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}})
for some arbitrary
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{r};\mathbb{R})
.
By assumption, we can compute

\displaystyle\int_{E}L_{\zeta}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=\int_{E}{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n}=-\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\phi,\nu_{E}\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}=-\int_{B_{r}}\psi\,\left\langle\zeta,\nu\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E},

yielding that
L_{\zeta}\mathbf{1}_{E}=0
if
\left\langle\zeta,\nu\right\rangle=0
and
L_{\zeta}\mathbf{1}_{E}\geq 0
if
\zeta=\nu
in
B_{r}
.
By
Lemma

3.5
,

E\cap B_{r}=\tau_{q}\left(\left\{\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{G}:\text{\large h}(\tilde{q})>0\right\}\right)\cap B_{r}\quad\text{for some}\ q\in\mathbb{G}.

To conclude, we just need to show that
\text{\large h}(q)=0
, as this yields

\tau_{q}\left(\left\{\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{G}:\text{\large h}(\tilde{q})>0\right\}\right)=\left\{\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{G}:\text{\large h}(\tilde{q})>0\right\}.

Indeed, if
\text{\large h}(q)>0
, then
B_{\rho}\cap\tau_{q}\left(\left\{\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{G}:\text{\large h}(\tilde{q})>0\right\}\right)=\emptyset
for some
\rho\in(0,r)
, yielding

|B_{\rho}\cap E|=\left|B_{\rho}\cap\tau_{q}\left(\left\{\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{G}:\text{\large h}(\tilde{q})>0\right\}\right)\right|=0,

against the assumption that
0\in\partial E
, recall (
2.3
).
The case
\text{\large h}(q)<0
can be similarly addressed by considering
E^{c}
in place of
E
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Intrinsic cones

The following definition rephrases

Definition 4.1

(Intrinsic cones)
.

The open
X_{1}
-cone
with vertex
0\in\mathbb{G}
and aperture
\alpha\in(0,+\infty]
is the set

C(0,\alpha)=\left\{p\in\mathbb{G}:\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty}<\alpha\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\|_{\infty}\right\}.

The corresponding
negative
and
positive cones
are

C^{\pm}(0,\alpha)=\left\{p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}:\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty}<\alpha\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\|_{\infty},\ x_{1}\gtrless 0\right\}.

Consequently, we let
C(p,\alpha)=p\star C(0,\alpha)
and
C^{\pm}(p,\alpha)=p\star C^{\pm}(0,\alpha)
for
p\in\mathbb{G}
.

Note that, given
p=(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}
and
\alpha\geq 0
,
\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p)\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha\|\pi_{\mathbb{V}}(p)\|_{\infty}
rewrites as

(4.1)

\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\,\right|^{1/2}\right\}\leq\alpha|x_{1}|.

The following result collects some elementary properties of cones in Carnot groups of step
2
, generalizing
[
25
]
*Lem. 4.5.
We briefly detail its proof for the ease of the reader.

Lemma 4.2

(Properties of cones)
.

The following hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{s<s_{0}}C^{+}(p\star s\mathrm{e}_{1},\alpha)=\mathbb{G}

for all
\alpha>0
,
p\in\mathbb{G}
and
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

\displaystyle C^{-}(0,\alpha)\subset\iota\left(C^{+}\left(0,\alpha+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\alpha{\mathcal{C}}}\right)\right)

for all
\alpha>0
;

(iii)

C^{\pm}(p,\beta)\subset C^{\pm}(0,\gamma)

for all

p\in C^{\pm}(0,\alpha)
, with
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and

\gamma=\max\left\{\alpha,\beta,\tfrac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\sqrt{\left(\alpha\beta+2\beta\right){\mathcal{C}}}\right\},

where
{\mathcal{C}}>0
is the constant in (
2.5
).

Proof.

We prove each statement separately.

Proof of
(i)
.
Assume
p=0
and note that, in virtue of (
2.6
) and (
4.1
), we can compute

\begin{split}C^{+}(s\mathrm{e}_{1},\alpha)&=s\mathrm{e}_{1}\star C^{+}(0,\alpha)\\
&=s\mathrm{e}_{1}\star\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}:\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\right\}<\alpha x_{1}\right\}\\
&=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}:\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}-2s\mathrm{e}_{1}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\right\}<\alpha(x_{1}-s)\right\}.\end{split}

Hence
(i)
for
p=0
follows from the fact that, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}
, there is
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}-2s\mathrm{e}_{1}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}<\alpha(x_{1}-s)\quad\text{for all}\ s<\sigma.

By left translation,
(i)
holds for any
p\in\mathbb{G}
.

Proof of
(ii)
.
For any
\beta>0
we have that

\iota\left(C^{+}(0,\beta)\right)=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{G}:\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\right\}<-\beta x_{1}\right\}.

Hence, if
(x,t)\in C^{-}(0,\alpha)
, then

\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left|x^{\perp}\right|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|<\alpha{\mathcal{C}}\left|x^{\parallel}\right|^{2}
and so

\begin{split}\epsilon_{2}\left|t+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\leq\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}+\epsilon_{2}\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}<-\left(\alpha+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\alpha{\mathcal{C}}}\right)x_{1},\end{split}

proving
(ii)
.

Proof of
(iii)
.
If
p=(x,t)\in C^{+}(0,\alpha)
, then

(4.2)

\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\right\}\leq\alpha x_{1}.

Moreover, if
q\in C^{+}(p,\beta)
, then
q=p*w
with
w=(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{G}
such that

(4.3)

\max\left\{\left|\xi^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|\tau-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}\xi^{\perp},\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\right\}\leq\beta\xi_{1}.

Now, since

q=(x,t)\star(\xi,\tau)=\left(x+\xi,t+\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle\right)
,
we can write

\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q)\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{\left|x^{\perp}+\xi^{\perp}\right|,\epsilon_{2}\left|t+\tau+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}(x^{\perp}+\xi^{\perp}),x^{\parallel}+\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle^{1/2}\right|\right\}.

Since
\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\parallel},\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle=0
, by (
2.6
) we easily see that

(4.4)

\begin{split}\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle-\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}\xi^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|&=\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},\xi^{\perp}\right\rangle+2\,\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\parallel},\xi^{\perp}\right\rangle\right|\\
&\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left(\left|x^{\perp}\right|\left|\xi^{\perp}\right|+2\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\left|\xi^{\perp}\right|\right)\\
&\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha\beta+2\beta\right)\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\left|\xi^{\parallel}\right|.\end{split}

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) yield that

\begin{split}\epsilon_{2}\big|t+\tau&+\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}(x^{\perp}+\xi^{\perp}),x^{\parallel}+\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle\big|^{1/2}\leq\epsilon_{2}\left|t-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\\
&\quad+\epsilon_{2}\left|\tau-\tfrac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}\xi^{\perp},\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}+\tfrac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\left|\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x,\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}x^{\perp},\xi^{\parallel}\right\rangle-\left\langle{\mathsf{B}}\xi^{\perp},x^{\parallel}\right\rangle\right|^{1/2}\\
&\leq\alpha x_{1}+\beta\xi_{1}+\tfrac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\sqrt{{\mathcal{C}}\left(\alpha\beta+2\beta\right)}\,x_{1}^{1/2}\xi_{1}^{1/2},\end{split}

immediately implying that
q\in C^{+}(0,\gamma)
.
The case of negative cones is similar.
∎

4.2.
Intrinsic Lipschitz graphs and functions

The following definition rephrases
[
25
]
*Def. 4.6 and
[

]
*Def. 11 and Prop. 3.3.

Definition 4.3

(Intrinsic Lipschitz graph and function)
.

The
intrinsic graph
of
\varphi\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
over the non-empty set
A\subset{\mathbb{W}}
is

\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;A)=\left\{\Phi(w):w\in A\right\}=\left\{w\star\varphi(w):w\in A\right\}\subset\mathbb{G},

where
\Phi\colon A\to\mathbb{G}
,
\Phi(w)=w\star\varphi(w)
for
w\in A
, is the
graph map
.
We say that
\varphi
is
intrinsic Lipschitz
on
A
with
intrinsic Lipschitz constant

L\in[0,+\infty)
, and we write
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(A)
and
L=\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi;A)
,
if, for
L>0
,

\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;A)\cap C(p,1/L)=\emptyset\quad\text{for all}\ p\in\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;A),

and
\varphi
constant on
A
for
L=0
.
Equivalently, for all
p,q\in\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;A)
, it holds that

|\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))-\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q))|\leq L\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q^{-1}\star p)\|_{\infty}.

We use the shorthand
\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi;{\mathbb{W}})
.

As established in
[
11
]
*Prop. 3.8, intrinsic Lipschitz functions are continuous—in fact,
\tfrac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous, since
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group of step
2
.

The following result, which generalizes
[
25
]
*Prop. 4.8, is a particular instance of
[
11
]
*Th. 4.1 and

[
32
]
*Th. 1.5.
The key point here is to provide an explicit bound on the intrinsic Lipschitz constant of the intrinsic Lipschitz extension.

Theorem 4.4

(Intrinsic Lipschitz extension)
.

There is
c=c(\epsilon_{2},{\mathcal{C}})>0
with the following property.
If
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(A)
for some
\emptyset\neq A\subset{\mathbb{W}}
, with
L=\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi;A)
, then there exists
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}({\mathbb{W}})
such that
\psi(w)=\varphi(w)
for all
w\in A
,
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{W}})}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}
and

\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\psi)\leq c\,\max\left\{L,L^{4}\right\}.

Here
{\mathcal{C}}>0
is the constant in (
2.5
).

Proof.

Assume
L>0
to avoid trivialities, let
\alpha=1/L
, and define the open set

E=\bigcup_{w\in A}C^{+}(\Phi(w),\alpha)\neq\emptyset.

Setting

\beta=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha+2}\,\frac{4}{4+{\mathcal{C}}\epsilon_{2}^{2}}
,
by
Lemma

4.2
(iii)
we get that, if
q\in E
, then
C^{+}(q,\beta)\subset E
.
By an elementary continuity argument, the latter inclusion also holds for any
q\in\partial E
, the topological boundary of
E
.
Consequently, if
p,q\in\partial E
, then
p\notin C^{+}(q,\beta)
.
As in the proof of
[
25
]
*Prop. 4.8, we thus get that
\psi\colon{\mathbb{W}}\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

\psi(w)=s_{w}\mathrm{e}_{1},\quad\text{where}\ s_{w}=\min\left\{\inf\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}:w\star s\mathrm{e}_{1}\in E\right\},\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}\right\}\ \text{for}\ w\in{\mathbb{W}},

is well defined and such that
\psi(w)=\varphi(w)
for all
w\in A
,
\mathrm{gr}(\psi;{\mathbb{W}})\subset\partial E
and
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{W}})}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(A)}
.
Finally, given
p,q\in\mathrm{gr}(\psi;{\mathbb{W}})
, arguing as in the proof of
[
25
]
*Prop. 4.8 and in virtue of
Lemma

4.2
(ii)
, we get that, if
p\notin C^{+}(q,\beta)
, then
q\notin C^{-}(p,\gamma)
, where
\gamma>0
is chosen such that
\beta=\gamma+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\gamma{\mathcal{C}}}
, that is,
\gamma=\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}^{2}{\mathcal{C}}+4\beta}-\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{{\mathcal{C}}}\right)^{2}
.
In particular,
\psi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}({\mathbb{W}})
with
\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\psi)=1/\gamma
, and a simple computation yields that
\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\psi)\leq c\max\left\{L,L^{4}\right\}
with
c=c(\epsilon_{2},{\mathcal{C}})
>0, concluding the proof.
∎

) and again by
Lemma

5.1
, we infer that

\sup\left\{|\text{\large h}(v)|:v\in C_{1}\cap\partial F\right\}\leq L.

In particular, choosing

v=\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}(q^{-1}\star p)\in C_{1}\cap\partial F
,
we get that

|\text{\large h}(q^{-1}\star p)|\leq L\,d_{C}(p,q).

Since
L<1
, the above inequality, combined with the definition in (
2.10
), yields that
d_{C}(p,q)=\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q^{-1}\star p)\|_{\infty}
, so that

(5.8)

|\text{\large h}(q^{-1}\star p)|\leq L\,\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q^{-1}\star p)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for all}\ p\in M,\ q\in M_{0}.

As a consequence, the projection
\pi_{\mathbb{W}}
is invertible on
M_{0}
and we can thus define a function
\varphi\colon\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
by letting
\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))=\text{\large h}(p)
for all
p\in M_{0}
.
Due to (
5.8
), we get that

|\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))-\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q))|\leq L\,\|\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(q^{-1}\star p)\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for all}\ p,q\in M_{0},

so that
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M_{0}))
with
\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi;\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M_{0}))\leq L<1
, in virtue of
Definition

4.3
.
Since
M_{0}\subset M
, from (
5.6
) we also get that

|\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|\leq L
for all
p\in M_{0}
.
By
Theorem

4.4
, we can find an extension of
\varphi
to the whole
{\mathbb{W}}
(for which we keep the same notation) such that
\mathrm{Lip}_{{\mathbb{W}}}(\varphi)\leq c(\epsilon_{2},{\mathcal{C}})\,L
and
|\varphi(w)|\leq L
for all
w\in{\mathbb{W}}
.
By construction, we also get that
M_{0}\subset M\cap\Gamma
, where
\Gamma=\mathrm{gr}(\varphi;D_{1})
.
This proves (
5.1
) and (
5.2
).

Step 2: covering argument
.
We now prove (
5.3
) via a covering argument.
By definition of
M_{0}
, for each
q\in M\setminus M_{0}
there exists
r_{q}\in(0,16)
such that

(5.9)

\int_{C_{r_{q}}(q)\cap\partial E}\frac{|\nu_{E}-\nu|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}>\varepsilon\,r_{q}^{Q-1}.

The family of balls

\left\{B_{2r_{q}}(q):q\in M\setminus M_{0}\right\}

is a covering of
M\setminus M_{0}
.
By Vitali’s Covering Lemma, there exist
q_{h}\in M\setminus M_{0}
, for
h\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the countable subfamily

\left\{B_{2r_{h}}(q_{h}):r_{h}={r_{q_{h}}},\ q_{h}\in M\setminus M_{0},\ h\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is disjoint, and the family
\left\{B_{10r_{h}}(q_{h}):h\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is still a covering of
M\setminus M_{0}
.
Therefore, by
Theorem

2.2
, we can estimate

(5.10)

\begin{split}\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\setminus M_{0})&\leq\sum_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}\big((M\setminus M_{0})\cap B_{10r_{h}}(q_{h})\big)\\
&\leq\sum_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\cap B_{10r_{h}}(q_{h}))\leq c\sum_{h\in\mathbb{N}}r_{h}^{Q-1},\end{split}

where
c>0
is a constant that does not dependent on
L
,
\Lambda
or
r_{0}
.
Now note that
B_{10r_{h}}(q_{h})\subset C_{324}
for all
h\in\mathbb{N}
, since, in virtue of (
2.11
), any
p\in B_{10r_{h}}(q_{h})
satisfies

\|p\|_{C}\leq 2\|p\|_{\infty}\leq 2d_{\infty}(p,q_{h})+2\|q_{h}\|_{\infty}<20r_{h}+4\|q_{h}\|_{C}<324.

Moreover, since
C_{r_{h}}(q_{h})\subset B_{2r_{h}}(q_{h})
by (
2.12
), also the cylinders
\left\{C_{r_{h}}(q_{h}):h\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
are disjoint and contained in
C_{324}
.
Therefore, by combining (
5.9
) with (
5.10
), we get that

\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\setminus M_{0})\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{C_{r_{h}}(q_{h})\cap\partial E}\frac{|\nu_{E}-\nu|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\,{\mathbf{e}}(324).

Consequently, since
M\setminus\Gamma\subset M\setminus M_{0}
, we conclude that

\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\setminus\Gamma)\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\,{\mathbf{e}}(k),

which is the first half of (
5.3
).
To prove the second half of (
5.3
), we observe that

{\mathbf{e}}(2)\leq\left(\frac{324}{2}\right)^{Q-1}{\mathbf{e}}(324)\leq\omega(L,\Lambda,r_{0}),

thanks to the properties of the excess and (
5.5
).
Hence, by (
2.13
) in
Lemma

2.5
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(\Gamma\setminus M)

\displaystyle=\int_{\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(\Gamma\setminus M)}\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+\|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{W}})}^{2}}\,\mathscr{L}^{n-1}(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(\Gamma\setminus M))

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+\|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{W}})}^{2}}\,\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}\big(M\cap\pi_{\mathbb{W}}^{-1}(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(\Gamma\setminus M))\big).

In virtue of
Theorem

4.7
, we can estimate

\sqrt{1+\|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{W}})}^{2}}\leq C_{L},

where
C_{L}>0
depends on
L
only.
Since

M\cap\pi_{\mathbb{W}}^{-1}(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(\Gamma\setminus M))\subset M\setminus\Gamma
,
we get that

\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(\Gamma\setminus M)\leq C_{L}\,\mathscr{S}_{\infty}^{Q-1}(M\setminus\Gamma)\leq\frac{C_{L}}{\varepsilon}\,{\mathbf{e}}(k),

completing the proof of (
5.3
).

Step 3: estimate on the
L^{2}
energy
.
Finally, we prove (
5.4
).
By
Theorem

4.8
and
[
1
]
*Cor. 2.6, for
\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}
-a.e.
p\in M\cap\Gamma
there exists
\sigma(p)\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
such that

\nu_{E}(p)=\sigma(p)\,\frac{\big(1,-\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))\big)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|^{2}}}.

Taking into account that, for
\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}
-a.e.
p\in M\cap\Gamma
,

\frac{|\nu_{E}(p)-\nu(p)|^{2}}{2}=1-\left\langle\nu_{E}(p),\nu(p)\right\rangle\geq\frac{1-\left\langle\nu_{E}(p),\nu(p)\right\rangle^{2}}{2},

we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{e}}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M\cap\Gamma}\frac{1-\left\langle\nu_{E}(p),\nu(p)\right\rangle^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}(p)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M\cap\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|^{2}}{1+|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M\cap\Gamma)}\frac{|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(w)|^{2}}{1+|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(w)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{Q-1}(w).

By
Theorem

4.7
and the scaling property of the excess, we get that

\int_{\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M\cap\Gamma)}|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{Q-1}\leq C_{L}\,{\mathbf{e}}(324),

where
C_{L}>0
depends on
L
only.
Moreover, by
Theorem

4.8
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(M\bigtriangleup\Gamma)}|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{Q-1}\leq\int_{M\bigtriangleup\Gamma}\frac{|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|^{2}}{1+|\nabla^{\varphi}\varphi(\pi_{\mathbb{W}}(p))|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{E}(p)\leq\mathscr{S}^{Q-1}_{\infty}(M\bigtriangleup\Gamma),

and (
5.4
) immediately follows from (
5.3
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Discrete stochastic maximal L p L^{p} -regularity and convergence of a spatial semidiscretization for a stochastic parabolic equation Thanks: This work was partially supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant 12301525 and Natural Science Foundation of Sichuan province under grant 2023NSFSC1324.
Binjie Li, Qin Zhou

Abstract
This study investigates the boundedness of the H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus for the discrete negative Laplace operator, subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The discrete negative Laplace operator is implemented using the finite element method, and we establish that its H ∞ H^{\infty} -calculus is uniformly bounded with respect to the spatial mesh size. Using this finding, we derive a discrete stochastic maximal L p L^{p} -regularity estimate for a spatial semidiscretization. Furthermore, we provide a nearly optimal pathwise uniform convergence estimate for this spatial semidiscretization under a wide range of spatial L q L^{q} -norms.

Suppose that

•

•

For any

\mathcal{R}
-boundedness.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be Banach spaces. A family
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
is
called
\mathcal{R}
-bounded if there exists
C>0
such that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, sequences
(B_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
,
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subseteq E_{1}
, and independent symmetric
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
,
the following inequality holds:

\mathbb{E}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}B_{n}x_{n}\Bigr\|_{E_{2}}^{2}\leqslant C\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}x_{n}\Bigr\|_{E_{1}}^{2}.

Here,
\mathbb{E}
denotes expectation over the probability space supporting
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
.
The smallest such constant
C
is termed the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness constant
of
\mathcal{A}
. For further foundational results, refer to
[
37
, Chapter 4]
.

H^{\infty}
-calculus.

A sectorial operator
A
on some Banach space
E
is said to have a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus if there exist an angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\varphi(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\varphi(z)(z-A)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(E)}\leqslant C\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\theta}}\left|\varphi(z)\right|

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
.
The space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\varphi:\Sigma_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}\mid\text{$\varphi$ is analytic, and there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\theta}}\left(\frac{1+\left|z\right|^{2}}{\left|z\right|}\right)^{\varepsilon}\left|\varphi(z)\right|<\infty\bigg\}.

Henceforth, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
, we denote the norm by

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}:=\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\theta}}|\varphi(z)|.

For an exhaustive treatment of
H^{\infty}
-calculus, the reader is referred to
[
20
, Chapter 10]
.

Definition of
A_{q}
.

For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by

the realization of the negative Laplacian

in

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The space

is denoted by

. For each

,

represents the domain of

, equipped with the norm

\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{q}}:=\|A_{q}^{\alpha}v\|_{X_{0}^{q}},\quad\forall v\in X_{\alpha}^{q}.

For each
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
X_{-\alpha}^{q^{\prime}}
denotes the dual space of
X_{\alpha}^{q}
. It is well-known that
A_{q}
uniquely extends to a bounded linear operator from
X_{\alpha}^{q}
to
X_{\alpha-1}^{q}
, satisfying

\|A_{q}v\|_{X_{\alpha-1}^{q}}=\|v\|_{X_{\alpha}^{q}},\quad\forall v\in X_{\alpha}^{q}.

The space
X_{1}^{q}
coincides with the standard Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
, equipped with equivalent norms (cf.
[
12
, Theorem 9.15]
). By
[
8
, Theorem 2]
and
[

, Theorem 16.5]
, for any
0<\alpha<1
,
X_{\alpha}^{q}
coincides with the complex interpolation space
[X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q}]_{\alpha}
, with equivalent norms. Furthermore, by
[
45
, Theorem 16.15]
,
X_{1/2}^{q}
corresponds to the standard Sobolev space

, ensuring the validity of the identity

\langle A_{q}u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{for all }u\in X_{1/2}^{q}\text{ and }v\in X_{1/2}^{q^{\prime}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
X_{-1/2}^{q}
and
X_{1/2}^{q^{\prime}}
.

Definition of
A_{h}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a conforming quasi-uniform tetrahedral mesh of
the domain
\mathcal{O}
, where
h:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}
denotes the maximum element diameter. We assume the triangulation satisfies the vertex compatibility condition: each vertex on

, where

denotes the polyhedral domain induced by

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)
denotes the space of all linear polynomials
on tetrahedron
K
. We consider the following quantities:

\displaystyle\varrho_{1}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\frac{h_{K}}{\rho_{K}},\quad\varrho_{2}:=\frac{\max_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}}{\min_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}},\quad\varrho:=\{\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}\},

where

Let

.
Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between

For any

It is evident that

is a symmetric and positive-definite operator on
X_{h}
,
where
X_{h}
is equipped with the
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
-norm.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, let

X_{\alpha,h}^{q}
denote the Banach space
X_{h}

\|v_{h}\|_{X_{\alpha,h}^{q}}:=\|A_{h}^{\alpha}v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in X_{h}.

For further properties of

Main Results

The main results of this paper are the following three theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

and
q\in(1,\infty)

(i)

For any

\lVert{\phi(A_{h})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X^{q}_{0,h})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

(3.1)

(ii)

For any

, it holds that

\lVert{A_{h}^{it}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}e^{\theta\lvert{t}\rvert}.

(3.2)

(iii)

The operator family

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded on
X_{0,h}^{q}
,
and its
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness constant is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

and
q\in[2,\infty)
.
For any

\biggl\lVert\int_{0}^{(\cdot)}e^{(s-(\cdot))A_{h}}f_{h}\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)\biggr\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|f_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{0,h}^{q}))}

(3.3)

holds, where

denotes the analytic semigroup generated by

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

,
q\in[2,\infty)
, and suppose that

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y(t)+A_{q}y(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=f(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),

Then, the following error estimate holds:

(3.5)

Furthermore, for any

\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lVert{(y-y_{h})(t)}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha}^{q}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}\leqslant C_{\alpha,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\big(1+\lvert{\ln h}\rvert^{1-1/p}\big)h^{1+2(\alpha-1/p)}\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q}))}.

Theorem

These properties have important implications for the numerical analysis of SPDEs:

•

Property (i) is essential for establishing discrete maximal

-regularity in both semi-discrete and fully discrete finite element approximations of SPDEs, playing a particularly critical role in fully discrete settings.

•

Property (ii) facilitates the application of

This equivalence is critical for analyzing SPDEs with low spatial regularity. For the linear finite-element method,
[

derived property (ii) under an acuteness condition on the spatial mesh.

•

Property (iii) enables the application of Weis’ fundamental result

-regularity estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{(\cdot)}e^{(s-(\cdot))A_{h}}f_{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}+\left\|A_{h}\int_{0}^{(\cdot)}e^{(s-(\cdot))A_{h}}f_{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}

\displaystyle C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|f_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})},

Remark 3.2
.

We briefly demonstrate a simple application of
Theorem

3.2
to the numerical analysis of nonlinear stochastic parabolic equations as follows.
Let
q\in[2,\infty)
, and consider a nonlinear mapping
F:X_{0}^{q}\to\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q})
that satisfies the following conditions for a positive constant
C_{F}
:

(i)

\lVert{F(u)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{F}\big(1+\lVert{u}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}\big)
for all

.

(ii)

\lVert{F(u)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,X_{1/2}^{q})}\leqslant C_{F}\big(1+\lVert{u}\rVert_{X_{1/2}^{q}}\big)
for all
u\in X_{1/2}^{q}
.

(iii)

\lVert{F(u)-F(v)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{F}\lVert{u-v}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}
for all
u,v\in X_{0}^{q}
.

We consider the following spatial semidiscretization:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)+A_{h}y_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=P_{h}F(y_{h}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),&0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y_{h}(0)=0,\end{cases}

where
T\in(0,\infty)
is a fixed terminal time.
Choose any
p\in(2,\infty)
. Using
[
40
, Lemma 2.1]
, the ideal property of
\gamma
-radonifying operators (
[
20
, Theorem 9.1.10]
),
Lemma

4.5
(i) with
\alpha=0
, and
Lemma

4.3
(i), we derive that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\lVert{y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;X_{0,h}^{q})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{(s-t)A_{h}}P_{h}F(y_{h}(s))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)\right\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,\mathcal{O}}\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{e^{(s-t)A_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}^{p}\lVert{P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{0,h}^{q})}^{p}\lVert{F(y_{h}(s))}\rVert_{\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\,\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{F(y_{h}(s))}\rVert_{\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s.

By condition (i), it follows that

\lVert{y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;X_{0,h}^{q})}^{p}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{y_{h}(s)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;X_{0,h}^{q})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Applying Gronwall’s inequality confirms the uniform boundedness of
y_{h}
in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);X_{0,h}^{q})
with respect to
h
. Condition (i), along with the stability property of
P_{h}
(see
Lemma

4.3
(i)), guarantees that
P_{h}F(y_{h})
is uniformly bounded in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,X_{0,h}^{q}))
with respect to
h
. By
Theorem

3.2
, we conclude that
y_{h}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);X_{1/2,h}^{q})
with respect to
h
.
Since
X_{1/2,h}^{q}
is continuously embedded in
X_{1/2}^{q}
,
condition (ii) ensures that
F(y_{h})
is uniformly bounded in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,X_{1/2}^{q}))
, and consequently
P_{h}F(y_{h})
is uniformly bounded in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,X_{1/2,h}^{q}))
. A second application of
Theorem

3.2
yields the uniform boundedness of
\lVert{y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);X_{1,h}^{q})}
,
providing essential stability for analyzing fully discrete approximations.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

(4.4)

,

(ii)

) in Lemma

For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, let
R_{h}:X_{1/2}^{q}\to X_{h}

\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall u\in X_{1/2}^{q},\,v_{h}\in X_{h}.

Lemma 4.6
.

The operator
R_{h}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For any
q\in(1,\infty)
,

\lVert{I-R_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q},X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

(4.5)

(ii)

For any
q\in[2,\infty)
,

\lVert{I-R_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1/2}^{q},X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h.

(4.6)

Proof.

We first recall that
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded convex domain with boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}

of class
C^{2,0}
, and that each vertex on
\partial\mathcal{O}_{h}
also
lies on
\partial\mathcal{O}
.
This implies that
\mathcal{O}_{h}
is a convex polyhedral domain satisfying

\max_{p_{1}\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{h}}\inf_{p_{2}\in\partial\mathcal{O}}|p_{1}-p_{2}|\leqslant C_{\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

(4.7)

Following the proof strategy of
[
4
, Theorem 8.5.3]
and employing
[
1
, Equation (A.10)]
to handle estimates over
\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h}
, we derive the stability estimate

\|R_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1/2}^{q},X_{1/2,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}},\quad\forall q\in(1,\infty).

This stability estimate routinely leads to the conclusion that

\|\nabla(v-R_{h}v)\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}_{h})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h\lVert{v}\rVert_{X_{1}^{q}},\quad\forall v\in X_{1}^{q},\quad\forall q\in(1,\infty).

(4.8)

Below, we provide an outline of the proof for
Eq.

4.5
, as the argument is standard and
Eq.

4.6
can be derived through a straightforward modification of this proof. For a fixed
q\in(3,\infty)
and any given
u\in X_{1}^{q}
and
g\in X_{0}^{q^{\prime}}
, let
u_{h}:=R_{h}u
and
w:=A_{q^{\prime}}^{-1}g
. It follows that

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(u-R_{h}u)g\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{h}}\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\cdot\nabla(w-w_{h})\,\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
w_{h}
denotes the Lagrange interpolation of
w
in
X_{h}
.
For
I_{1}
, applying integration by parts followed by Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})}\|\Delta w\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})}+\|u\|_{L^{2q/3}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h})}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2q/(2q-3)}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})}\|w\|_{X_{1}^{q^{\prime}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2q/3}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h})}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2q/(2q-3)}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right).

By the trace inequality (see, e.g.,
[
45
, Theorem 1.39]
), it follows that

\displaystyle I_{1}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})}+\|u\|_{L^{2q/3}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h})}\right)\|w\|_{X_{1}^{q^{\prime}}}.

By
Eq.

4.7
, the boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{O}
,
and Sobolev’s embedding theorem, an elementary calculation gives

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2q/3}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{h})}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}}.

Combining these estimates, we conclude

I_{1}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}}\|w\|_{X_{1}^{q^{\prime}}}.

For
I_{2}
, by applying
Eq.

4.8
and the standard interpolation error estimate for Lagrange interpolation, we obtain

I_{2}\leqslant\|\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla(w-w_{h})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}_{h};\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}}\|w\|_{X_{1}^{q^{\prime}}}.

Combining the bounds for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, we arrive at

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(u-R_{h}u)g\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}}\|w\|_{X_{1}^{q^{\prime}}}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|u\|_{X_{1}^{q}}\|g\|_{X_{0}^{q^{\prime}}},

which holds for all
u\in X_{1}^{q}
and
g\in X_{0}^{q^{\prime}}
.
This establishes
Eq.

4.5
for
q\in(3,\infty)
.
The equality
\|I-R_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q},X_{0}^{q})}=\|I-R_{h}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q^{\prime}},X_{0}^{q^{\prime}})}
for
q\in(1,3/2)
implies that
Eq.

4.5
also holds in this range. Interpolation then extends the result to
q\in[3/2,3]
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Then, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\theta}
, we have

\lVert{(z-A_{q})^{-1}-(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

Proof.

We begin by noting that

\displaystyle\lVert{(I-P_{h})(z-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{I-P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q},X_{0}^{q})}\lVert{(z-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2},

where the last inequality is justified by
Lemma

4.1
(i) and
Lemma

4.3
(ii).
It suffices, therefore, to prove that

\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-P_{h}(z-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

To this end, consider the following algebraic manipulation:

\displaystyle(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-P_{h}(z-A_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(z-A_{h})^{-1}(P_{h}(z-A_{q})-(z-A_{h})P_{h})(z-A_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(z-A_{h})^{-1}(A_{h}P_{h}-P_{h}A_{q})(z-A_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(z-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}(P_{h}A_{q}^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}P_{h})A_{q}(z-A_{q})^{-1}.

Subsequently,

\displaystyle\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-P_{h}(z-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}A_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\lVert{P_{h}A_{q}^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\lVert{A_{q}(z-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\leqslant}}{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{P_{h}A_{q}^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{=}}{}

\displaystyle C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{(P_{h}-R_{h})A_{q}^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{P_{h}-R_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q},X_{0}^{q})}\lVert{A_{q}^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(iii)}}}{{\leqslant}}{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2},

where step (i) follows from
Lemma

4.1
(i) and
inequality
Eq.

4.2
, step (ii) exploits the identity
R_{h}=A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}A_{q}
,
and step (iii) is a direct consequence of
Lemma

4.3
(ii) and

Lemma

4.6
(i),
combined with the fact
\lVert{A_{q}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q})}=1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, we have

\lVert{A_{h}^{-\alpha}P_{h}-A_{q}^{-\alpha}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\alpha}.

(4.9)

Proof.

The case of
\alpha=1
follows immediately from the identity

A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}-A_{q}^{-1}=(R_{h}-I)A_{q}^{-1}
combined with inequality
(
4.5
) and the equality

\lVert{A_{q}^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q})}=1
.
For
0<\alpha<1
, set
\theta=\pi/4
.
Using the Dunford integral representation

\displaystyle A_{h}^{-\alpha}P_{h}-A_{q}^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\lambda^{-\alpha}\bigl[(\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

we estimate the norm as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert{A_{h}^{-\alpha}P_{h}-A_{q}^{-\alpha}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\lVert{(re^{i\theta}-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(re^{i\theta}-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\lVert{(re^{-i\theta}-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(re^{-i\theta}-A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\,\mathrm{d}r.

For any
r\in(0,\infty)
, by Lemma
4.3
(i), inequality (
4.1
), and Lemma
4.1
(i), we have

\|(re^{\pm i\theta}-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(re^{\pm i\theta}-A_{q})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant\frac{C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}}{1+r}.

Additionally, by Lemma
4.7
, we obtain

\|(re^{\pm i\theta}-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(re^{\pm i\theta}-A_{q})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

Combining these estimates, we get

\|A_{h}^{-\alpha}P_{h}-A_{q}^{-\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}\min\{h^{2},r^{-1}\}\,\mathrm{d}r\leqslant C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\alpha}.

This completes the proof of (
4.9
) for
0<\alpha<1
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
q\in(1,\infty)
. Then,
for any
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha}^{q}}\leqslant C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha,h}^{q}}.

Proof.

We proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\lVert{A_{q}^{-\alpha}v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{A_{q}^{-\alpha}v_{h}-A_{h}^{-\alpha}v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}+\lVert{A_{h}^{-\alpha}v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(i)}}{\leqslant}C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\alpha}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0}^{q}}+\lVert{A_{h}^{-\alpha}v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(ii)}}{\leqslant}C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\alpha}v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha,h}^{q}},

where step (i) applies Lemma
4.8
,
and step (ii) utilizes Lemma
4.5
(ii).
This establishes the desired inequality, thereby concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
. Then, for any
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{1-1/p,h}^{q}}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}(1+\lvert{\ln h}\rvert^{1-1/p})\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}.

Proof.

Starting with the identity
v_{h}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}A_{h}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}t
,
we infer that

\displaystyle\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{1-1/p,h}^{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1+h^{2}}^{\infty}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{1+h^{2}}^{\infty}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}}\big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t+C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}},

by
Lemma

4.5
(i) and the
h
-independent continuous embedding

(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}\hookrightarrow X_{0,h}^{q}
(see
Remark

4.1
).
It remains therefore to prove that

\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\big(1+\lvert{\ln h}\rvert^{1-1/p}\big)\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}.

(4.10)

To this end, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{2-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{1-1/p}e^{-\frac{t}{2}A_{h}}}\big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\big\lVert{A_{h}e^{-\frac{t}{2}A_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}^{1-1/p}e^{-\frac{t}{2}A_{h}}}\big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}e^{-\frac{t}{2}A_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the last inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality.
For the first factor, splitting the integral into
(0,h^{2})
and
(h^{2},1+h^{2})
,
we apply
Lemma

4.5
to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\lVert{A_{h}^{1-1/p}e^{-tA_{h}/2}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\left[\int_{0}^{h^{2}}h^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{h^{2}}^{1+h^{2}}t^{-1}\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\big(1+|\ln h|^{1-1/p}\big).

By Proposition 6.2 in
[
32
]
, the second factor satisfies

\left(\int_{0}^{1+h^{2}}\big\lVert{A_{h}e^{-tA_{h}}v_{h}}\big\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}.

Combining these estimates establishes (
4.10
), completing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in(1,\infty)
.
For any
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, define
v:=A_{q}^{-1}A_{h}v_{h}
.
Applying inequality (
4.9
) with
\alpha=1
, we obtain

\|v-v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|A_{h}v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}}.

By the triangle inequality, the norm of
v_{h}
satisfies

\|v_{h}\|_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\leqslant\|v\|_{X_{0}^{q}}+C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}\|A_{h}v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}}.

Using the relation
\|v\|_{X_{1}^{q}}=\|A_{h}v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}}
and the continuous embedding
X_{1}^{q}\hookrightarrow X_{0}^{q}
, we deduce that

\|v_{h}\|_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|A_{h}v_{h}\|_{X_{0}^{q}}=C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|v_{h}\|_{X_{1,h}^{q}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in X_{h}.

Consequently, invoking
[
32
, Proposition 1.3]
, we establish the inequality

\|v_{h}\|_{X_{0,h}^{q}}\leqslant C_{\alpha,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|v_{h}\|_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{\alpha,p}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in X_{h}.

Remark 4.2
.

We observe that the operator

. By leveraging the spectral decomposition of

and invoking

]

present in

4.2
Proof of
Theorem

3.1

The proof proceeds in seven steps: assertions (i) and (ii) follow from
Steps 1–5 and Step 6 respectively, while assertion (iii) is established in Step 7.

Step 1.
We prove that

\lVert{\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}),

(4.11)

where the operator
\widetilde{A}_{q}
is defined as

\widetilde{A}_{q}:=(h^{2}+A_{q})(1+h^{2}A_{q})^{-1}.

(4.12)

The introduction of
\widetilde{A}_{q}
is motivated by
[
37
, Section 3.1]
.
Furthermore, according to Proposition 3.1.4 from
[
37
]
,

\widetilde{A}_{q}
is bounded, has a bounded a inverse, and is a sectorial operator.
For any
z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1+h^{2}A_{q})\left(z+h^{2}zA_{q}-h^{2}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-h^{2}z}(1+h^{2}A_{q})\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}\left(h^{-2}+A_{q}\right)\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}\left[h^{-2}+\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)\right]\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}\left[\frac{h^{-2}-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)\right]\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-h^{4}}{(1-h^{2}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}-\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}.

Choose any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
.
Invoking the integral identity

\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z,

and using Cauchy’s formula, which implies

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}\,\mathrm{d}z=-\phi(h^{-2}),

we deduce that

\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=\phi(h^{-2})+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-h^{4}}{(1-h^{2}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z.

(4.13)

Since
|\operatorname{Arg}((z-h^{2})/(1-h^{2}z))|>\theta
for all
z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
,
it is straightforward to verify that the integral in the above equation is indeed convergent
by part (i) of
Lemma

4.1
. Fix any
0<\theta^{\prime}<\theta
and define

\varphi(\lambda)=\phi\Big(\frac{{h^{2}}+\lambda}{1+{h^{2}}\lambda}\Big)-\phi\Big(\frac{1}{h^{2}}\Big),\quad\forall\lambda\in\overline{\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}.

Given that
\phi
is analytic in
\Sigma_{\theta}
and uniformly
bounded therein, an elementary computation confirms that
\varphi
is analytic
on
\overline{\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}
, and satisfies

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\omega\in[-\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}]}r|\varphi(re^{i\omega})|<\infty.

For the second term in the right-hand side of
Eq.

4.13
,
we perform the following analysis:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-{h^{4}}}{(1-{h^{2}}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-{h^{2}}}{1-{h^{2}}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-{h^{4}}}{(1-{h^{2}}z)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{z-{h^{2}}}{1-{h^{2}}z}-\lambda\right)^{-1}(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-{h^{4}}}{(1-{h^{2}}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-{h^{2}}}{1-{h^{2}}z}-\lambda\right)^{-1}(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-{h^{4}}}{(1-{h^{2}}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-{h^{2}}}{1-{h^{2}}z}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z\,(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\left[\frac{1}{z-\frac{h^{2}+\lambda}{1+h^{2}\lambda}}-\frac{1}{z-\frac{1}{h^{2}}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z\,(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where in (i) we use the fact that
(z-h^{2})/(1-h^{2}z)\not\in\overline{\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}
,
in (ii) we apply Fubini’s theorem to interchange the order of integration.
Since
(h^{2}+\lambda)/(1+h^{2}\lambda)
belongs to
\Sigma_{\theta}

for all
\lambda\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}
,
using Cauchy’s formula along with the definition of
\varphi
, we can further deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\frac{1-{h^{4}}}{(1-{h^{2}}z)^{2}}\left(\frac{z-{h^{2}}}{1-{h^{2}}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\left[\phi\Big(\frac{h^{2}+\lambda}{1+h^{2}\lambda}\Big)-\phi\Big(\frac{1}{h^{2}}\Big)\right]\,(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\varphi(\lambda)(\lambda-A_{q})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\varphi(A_{q}).

By combining the above result with the equality
Eq.

4.13
, we obtain

\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=\phi(h^{-2})+\varphi(A_{q}).

Using Corollary 3.1.11 from
[
37
]
, we further arrive at

\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=\phi(h^{-2})+\varphi(0)(1+A_{q})^{-1}+\Psi(A_{q}),

where the function
\Psi
is defined as:

\Psi(\lambda):=\varphi(\lambda)-\frac{\varphi(0)}{1+\lambda},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}.

This leads to the inequality

\lVert{\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant\lvert{\phi(h^{-2})}\rvert+\lvert{\varphi(0)}\rvert\lVert{(1+A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}+\lVert{\Psi(A_{q})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}.

The desired inequality
Eq.

4.11
then follows from
Lemma

4.1
,
alongside the observation that
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}})
and

\lvert{\phi(h^{-2})}\rvert+\lvert{\varphi(0)}\rvert+\lVert{\Psi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}})}\leqslant C_{\theta}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

Step 2.
For any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
,
we aim to establish the decomposition

\phi(A_{h})P_{h}-\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=I_{1}(\phi)+I_{2}(\phi)+I_{3}(\phi),

(4.14)

where the components
I_{j}(\phi)
, for
j=1,2,3
, are given respectively by

\displaystyle I_{1}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}\cos\theta},0)e^{i\theta}}\phi(z)\left[(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z,

(4.15)

\displaystyle I_{2}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(0,\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}\cos\theta})e^{-i\theta}}\phi(z)\left[(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z,

(4.16)

\displaystyle I_{3}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}+i(-\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta,\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta)}\phi(z)\left[(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z,

(4.17)

with
c^{*}
being a positive constant depending only on
q
,
\varrho
, and the
geometric characteristics of
\mathcal{O}
.
In light of the definition of
\widetilde{A}_{q}
in
Eq.

4.12

and the properties of
A_{q}
stated in part (i) of
Lemma

4.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\lVert{\widetilde{A}_{q}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant h^{2}\lVert{(1+h^{2}A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}+\lVert{A_{q}(1+h^{2}A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle=\lVert{(h^{-2}+A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}+h^{-2}\lVert{A_{q}(h^{-2}+A_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{q,\mathcal{O}}h^{-2}.

Further, using the well-known inverse estimate that

\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{1,h}^{q}}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{-2}\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert_{X_{0,h}^{q}}
for all
v_{h}\in X_{h}
, we infer

\lVert{A_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{-2}.

Consequently, there exists a positive constant
c^{*}
, solely determined
by
q
,
\varrho
, and the geometric characteristics of
\mathcal{O}
,
such that

c^{*}h^{-2}\geqslant 2\max\left\{\lVert{A_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})},\,\lVert{\widetilde{A}_{q}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\right\},

(4.18)

yielding the resolvent set inclusion

\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,\middle|\,\operatorname{Re}z\geqslant c^{*}h^{-2}\right\}\subseteq\rho(\widetilde{A}_{q})\cap\rho(A_{h}).

Given that

\mathbb{C}\setminus(0,\infty)\subseteq\rho(\widetilde{A}_{q})\cap\rho(A_{h}),

we can refine this inclusion to yield

\mathbb{C}\setminus(0,c^{*}h^{-2})\subseteq\rho(\widetilde{A}_{q})\cap\rho(A_{h}).

(4.19)

Therefore, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
, utilizing the identity

\displaystyle\phi(A_{h})P_{h}-\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\phi(z)\left[(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}z,

we can invoke Cauchy’s integral theorem to deform the contour of integration,
thereby achieving the desired decomposition
Eq.

4.14
.

Step 3.
We proceed to prove that

\lVert{I_{1}(\phi)+I_{2}(\phi)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}).

(4.20)

To this end, consider an arbitrary element
\phi
within
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
.
For any
z
on the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle(z-A_{q})^{-1}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A_{q}-\widetilde{A}_{q})(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}(z-A_{q})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}(A_{q}^{2}-1)\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}(z-A_{q})^{-1},

where the last step uses the definition of
\widetilde{A}_{q}
in
Eq.

4.12
.
Given that
\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}
lies exterior to
\Sigma_{\theta}
for
all
z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
, using the properties of
A_{q}
in part (i) of
Lemma

4.1
,
we confirm the uniform boundedness in
\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})
of the operators

A_{q}^{2}\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}(z-A_{q})^{-1}

and

\left(\frac{z-h^{2}}{1-h^{2}z}-A_{q}\right)^{-1}(z-A_{q})^{-1}

with respect to both

and
z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
.
This leads to the conclusion that, for any
z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}
,

\lVert{(z-A_{q})^{-1}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\frac{h^{2}}{|1-h^{2}z|},

which, combined with the inequality

\frac{1}{|1-h^{2}z|}\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-\cos\theta)(1+h^{4}|z|^{2})}},\quad\forall z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta},

yields

\lVert{(z-A_{q})^{-1}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2}.

Combining this result with the estimate from
Lemma

4.7
, we attain

\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant{}C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2},\quad\forall z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}.

Using this inequality, together with
Eqs.

4.15
and
4.16
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\lVert{I_{1}(\phi)+I_{2}(\phi)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}h^{-2}\max_{z\in\partial\Sigma_{\theta}}\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

This confirms the claim
Eq.

4.20
.

Step 4.
Let us prove that

\lVert{I_{3}(\phi)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta}).

(4.21)

For any
z
in the complex segment
c^{*}h^{-2}+i(-c^{*}h^{-2}\tan\theta,c^{*}h^{-2}\tan\theta)
,
we begin by bounding the norm as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}+\lVert{(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{q,\varrho}\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}+\lVert{(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:Ph}(i))}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{q,\varrho}}{|z|-\lVert{A_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}}+\frac{1}{|z|-\lVert{\widetilde{A}_{q}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2},

where the third and fourth inequalities follow from (
4.18
) and the fact that
|z|\geqslant c^{*}h^{-2}
.
Here, we recall that
c^{*}
is a positive constant that depends solely on
q
,
\varrho
, and the geometric characteristics of
\mathcal{O}
.
Consequently, given any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
,
from
Eq.

4.17
we deduce that

\displaystyle\lVert{I_{3}(\phi)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}h^{-2}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\max_{z\in\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}+i\left(-\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta,\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta\right)}\lVert{(z-A_{h})^{-1}P_{h}-(z-\widetilde{A}_{q})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

This completes the proof of (
4.21
).

Step 5.

For
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
,
we combine the estimates from
Eqs.

4.14
,
4.20

\|\phi(A_{h})P_{h}-\phi(\widetilde{A}_{q})\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})}.

In conjunction with the bound (

.

Step 6.

To prove assertion (ii), let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. For
\epsilon\in(0,1)

\Sigma_{\theta}
as

\phi_{\epsilon,t}(z):=z^{it}\frac{z^{\epsilon}}{(1+z)^{2\epsilon}},

where

uses the principal branch of the logarithm with arguments in
(-\pi,\pi)
. Define
\Gamma
as the concatenation of three line segments:

\Gamma:=\left[\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}\cos\theta},0\right]e^{i\theta}\cup\left[0,\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}\cos\theta}\right]e^{-i\theta}\cup\left[\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}-i\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta,\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}+i\frac{c^{*}}{h^{2}}\tan\theta\right],

with

from Step 2. By
Eq.

4.19
and Cauchy’s theorem, we deform the integration contour to obtain

A_{h}^{it}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}z^{it}(z-A_{h})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{\epsilon,t}(A_{h})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\phi_{\epsilon,t}(z)(z-A_{h})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z.

The uniform bound

\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1)}\lVert{\phi_{\epsilon,t}(\cdot)(\cdot-A_{h})^{-1}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma;\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q}))}<\infty,

coupled with pointwise convergence
\phi_{\epsilon,t}(z)(z-A_{h})^{-1}\to z^{it}(z-A_{h})^{-1}
on
\Gamma\setminus\{0\}
, allows the application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem to conclude

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\phi_{\epsilon,t}(A_{h})=A_{h}^{it}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q}).

Since
\phi_{\epsilon,t}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta})
, estimate (
3.1
) yields

\lVert{\phi_{\epsilon,t}(A_{h})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\theta}}\Big|z^{it}\frac{z^{\epsilon}}{(1+z)^{2\epsilon}}\Big|\leqslant C_{\theta,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}e^{\theta|t|}.

Thus, the desired inequality
Eq.

3.2
follows.
This completes the proof of assertion (ii).

Step 7.
Let us establish assertion (iii). Fix any
\theta^{\prime}\in(0,\theta)
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus{\overline{\Sigma}_{\theta}}
, define

\phi_{z}(\lambda):=\frac{z}{z-\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\phi_{z}(\cdot):\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}

is analytic for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}}
,
and that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}}\lvert{\phi_{z}(\lambda)}\rvert<\infty.

By assertion (i) of this theorem, we have

\lVert{\phi(A_{h})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X^{q}_{0,h})}\leqslant C_{\theta^{\prime},q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\phi}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}})},\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\theta^{\prime}}).

Furthermore, since
X_{0,h}^{q}
is a closed subspace of
X_{0}^{q}
,
from propositions 7.5.3 and 7.5.4 in
[
20
]

it follows that
X_{0,h}^{q}
inherits Pisier’s contraction property,
with the associated constants being independent of
h
.
Consequently, by Theorem 10.3.4 from
[
20
]
,
we deduce that the family

\bigl\{\phi_{z}(A_{h})\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta}}\bigr\}

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded on
X_{0,h}^{q}
, and its
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness
constant is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
.
This establishes the assertion (iii) of
Theorem

3.1
, thereby completing the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

\blacksquare

4.3
Proof of
Theorem

3.3

Step 1 (Estimate for
y_{h}-P_{h}y
).

From
Eq.

3.4
, almost surely,

y(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A_{q}y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)\quad\text{in }X_{-1/2}^{q}\text{ for all }t\in[0,\infty).

Applying
P_{h}
to both sides yields

\mathrm{d}P_{h}y(t)+P_{h}A_{q}y(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=P_{h}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad t\geqslant 0.

Subtracting this equation from
Eq.

1.1
, we obtain, almost surely,

\mathrm{d}e_{h}(t)+A_{h}e_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\big(P_{h}A_{q}y-A_{h}P_{h}y\big)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad t\geqslant 0,

where
e_{h}(t):=y_{h}(t)-P_{h}y(t)
for all
t\geqslant 0
.
By Theorem 3.2 in
[
11
]
, for any
\beta\in[0,1]
,
we obtain almost surely:

\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}e_{h}^{\prime}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}+\lVert{A_{h}A_{h}^{-\beta}e_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}(P_{h}A_{q}y-A_{h}P_{h}y)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}.

This implies the following result, almost surely, by
[
32
, Corollary 1.14]
:

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}e_{h}(t)}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}+\lVert{A_{h}^{1-\beta}e_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}(P_{h}A_{q}y-A_{h}P_{h}y)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}.

For the right-hand side, we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}(P_{h}A_{q}y-A_{h}P_{h}y)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lVert{A_{h}^{1-\beta}(R_{h}y-P_{h}y)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}\quad\text{(since $A_{h}^{-1}P_{h}A_{q}y=R_{h}y$)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle C_{\beta,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2(\beta-1)}\lVert{R_{h}y-P_{h}y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:etAh}(ii))}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\beta,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\beta-1}\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2}^{q})}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:Ph}(ii) and \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:Rh}(ii))}.

Thus, for any
\beta\in[0,1]
, we have almost surely that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}e_{h}(t)}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}+\lVert{A_{h}^{1-\beta}e_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\beta,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\beta-1}\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2}^{q})}.

It follows that, for any
\beta\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lVert{A_{h}^{-\beta}(y_{h}-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}+\lVert{A_{h}^{1-\beta}(y_{h}-P_{h}y)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0,h}^{q})}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle C_{\beta,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h^{2\beta-1}\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2}^{q})}.

Step 2 (Proof of Error Estimate
Eq.

3.5
).

The error estimate
Eq.

3.5
follows from a straightforward application of the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\lVert{y-y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert{y-P_{h}y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0}^{q})}+\lVert{y_{h}-P_{h}y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{0}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2}^{q})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}h\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};\gamma(H,X_{0}^{q}))}.

Here, the second inequality uses
Lemma

4.3
(ii) and
inequality
Eq.

4.22
with
\beta=1
, while the third inequality follows from
Lemma

4.2
. This completes the proof of
Eq.

3.5
.

Step 3 (Proof of Error Estimate
Eq.

3.6
).

To establish the error estimate
Eq.

3.6
for
\alpha\in[0,1/p]
, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{(y_{h}-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha}^{q}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\leqslant}}{}

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{(y_{h}-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha,h}^{q}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{A_{h}^{-(1-1/p+\alpha)}(y_{h}-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{X_{1-1/p,h}^{q}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leqslant}}{}

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}(1+|\ln h|^{1-1/p})\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{A_{h}^{-(1-1/p+\alpha)}(y_{h}-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{(X_{0,h}^{q},X_{1,h}^{q})_{1-1/p,p}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(iii)}}}{{\leqslant}}{}

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,p,q,\varrho,\mathcal{O}}(1+|\ln h|^{1-1/p})h^{1+2(\alpha-1/p)}\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+};X_{1/2}^{q})}.

Here, step (i) follows from Lemma
4.9
, step (ii) uses Lemma
4.10
, and step (iii) is derived from
Eq.

4.22
with
\beta=1-1/p+\alpha
.
Additionally, we have:

\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{(y-P_{h}y)(t)}\rVert_{X_{-\alpha}^{q}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}\leqslant C_{\alpha,p,q,\varrho}h^{1+2(\alpha-1/p)}\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\lVert{y(t)}\rVert_{(X_{0}^{q},X_{1}^{q})_{1/2-1/p,p}}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p},

where we apply
[
32
, Theorem 1.6]
along with the standard bounds:

\lVert{I-P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{q},X_{-\alpha}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\alpha,q,\varrho}h^{2\alpha},\quad\lVert{I-P_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X_{1}^{q},X_{-\alpha}^{q})}\leqslant C_{\alpha,q,\varrho}h^{2+2\alpha}.

Combining these results with the regularity of
y
as stated in
Lemma

4.2
, we establish the desired pathwise uniform convergence estimate
Eq.

3.6
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this research, we have established a critical property:
the boundedness of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus for the discrete negative Laplace operator
does not depend on the spatial mesh size. This foundational insight has enabled us to
deduce the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate for
finite element-based spatial semidiscretizations.
Furthermore, we have derived a nearly optimal pathwise uniform convergence
estimate within the context of general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.

The numerical analysis of nonlinear stochastic partial differential equations
within the context of general spatial
L^{q}
-norms is a theoretically significant
yet underexplored field. The theoretical advancements presented in this paper will
be useful for future numerical investigations in this area.

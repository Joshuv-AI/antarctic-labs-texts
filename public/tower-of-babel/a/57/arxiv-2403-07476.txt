1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the question of extending the applicability of the Chabauty–Coleman–Kim method for studying rational points on higher genus curves
X/\mathbb{Q}
. This method produces sets
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}\subset X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for each
n>0
, which should provide successive approximations to
X(\mathbb{Q})
:

X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\supset X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{1}\supset X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}\supset\ldots\supset X(\mathbb{Q}).

Kim has conjectured
[
BDCKW18
]
that for
n\gg 0
,
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{n}=X(\mathbb{Q})
. In the context we work in (that of genus two curves) it is often enough to produce a
finite
set, since then the Mordell–Weil sieve can rule out ‘fake rational points’.

When
n=1
, the problem of finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{1}
is well understood, as this is exactly the set produced by the Chabauty–Coleman method: a sufficient condition is that
r<g
, where
r
is the Mordell–Weil rank of the Jacobian of
X
and
g
is the genus of
X
. When
n=2
, it is known that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
is finite when
r<g+\rho(J)-1
, where
\rho(J):=\rk\mathrm{NS}(J)
is the rank of the Néron–Severi group of
J
(over
\mathbb{Q}
).

The obstruction to extending this result is the rank of the mysterious
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
H^{1}_{f}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}|\mathbb{Q}),\wedge^{2}T_{p}J)
, which is the Bloch–Kato Selmer group of the higher Chow group
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(\mathrm{Jac}(X),1)
, or of the Galois representation
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Jac}(X)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}(2))\simeq\wedge^{2}T_{p}J
. The Bloch–Kato conjectures
[
BK90
]
give a precise formula for this number, which implies that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
should be finite whenever
r<g^{2}+\rho(J)-1
. In this series of papers we introduce
2
-descent methods for proving bounds on
H^{1}_{f}(\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}|\mathbb{Q}),\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
. The first paper in this series
[
Dog23
]
applied these to provably determine the set of rational points on the curve
y^{2}-y=x^{5}-x
, answering a question of Bugeaud, Mignotte, Siksek, Stoll and Tengely
[
BMS+08
]
. In this paper we extend these methods to provide fairly robust criteria for finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
for a genus
2
curve
X
with a rational Weierstrass point. As an application we prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Of the 6,603 curves in the LMFDB with Mordell–Weil rank 2 and exactly one rational Weierstrass point, at least
3,323
satisfy
\#X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}<\infty
. Moreover, for each such
X
,
X^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
is finite whenever
X^{\prime}
is a quadratic twist of
X
with Jacobian of Mordell–Weil rank
\leq 2
.

By contrast, only 8 of the the 6,603 curves in question satisfy the condition
\rho(J)>1
usually associated with quadratic Chabauty.

1.1.
Relation to elliptic curve Chabauty

A number of authors including Bruin
[
Bru03
]
, Flynn and Wetherell
[
FW99
]
and recently Hast
[
Has22
]
have studied rational points on genus two curves using a method called elliptic curve Chabauty. Specifically, given a genus 2 curve
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
with a rational Weierstrass point one can find a finite set of covers
f_{\alpha}:Y_{\alpha}\to X
(all isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
) such that
X(\mathbb{Q})=\cup f_{\alpha}(Y_{\alpha})
and such that
\mathrm{Jac}(Y_{\alpha})
contains an isogeny factor isomorphic to the Weil restriction of an elliptic curve. This elliptic curve will be the Jacobian of the genus one curve defined by the quartic obtained by removing a root of a degree 5 polynomial defining
X
.

This method may also be thought of as a nonabelian version of the Chabauty–Coleman method, in the sense that the Tate module of the Jacobian of
Y_{\alpha}
is a subquotient of the étale fundamental group of
X_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
. In fact the analogy is stronger: as we explain in section
6.1
, the Galois cohomology of
\wedge^{2}J[2]
and
\mathrm{Jac}(Y_{\alpha})[2]
are closed related. Intriguingly, however, the Selmer conditions can be
different
, meaning that one can instances where the
2
-descent methods described in this paper can prove finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
, but
2
-descent methods for elliptic curve Chabauty are insufficient, and vice versa.

1.2.
Arithmetic statistics of Bloch–Kato Selmer groups

Although this paper only considers the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
for a hyperelliptic Jacobian
J
, it seems reasonable to believe that the general idea (bounding ranks of Bloch–Kato Selmer groups by ‘elementary’ descent methods) has a larger realm of applicability. In considering the merits of such an approach, one may compare with the case of Selmer groups of elliptic curves. Here two notable applications of elementary
2
-descent are explicit computations on a given elliptic curve, and proving results in arithmetic statistics. This paper, and the previous paper in this series, demonstrate the analogue of the first application for the Selmer group of a higher Chow group. It would be interesting to further explore the analogue of the second application, i.e. to explore the extent to which it possible to prove results about Bloch–Kato Selmer groups ‘on average’, or for a positive proportion of motives in a family.

The simplest (new) case would seem to be the Galois representation
T_{2}J(n)
, where
J
is a
g
-dimensional hyperelliptic Jacobian and
n
is a (nonzero) integer. Then the Bloch–Kato conjectures imply that the rank of
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q},T_{2}J(n))
should be
g
when
n>0
and
0
when
n<0
. In proposition
8
we explain a relation between this conjecture and certain results of Ho, Shankar and Varma
[
HSV18
]
.

2.
A review of
2
-descent for Bloch–Kato Selmer groups

We first (very briefly) recall the context of this paper. More details can be found in
[
Dog23
]
(although there are usually better references for what we describe below, this reference will usually prove convenient because this paper is a continuation of it). Let
X/\mathbb{Q}
be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve of genus
g
with Jacobian
J
. We have the following sufficient condition for finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
.

Lemma 1

(
[
Dog23
]
, Lemma 25)
.

The set
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
is finite whenever

\dim H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)<\frac{(3g-2)(g+1)}{2}-\rk J(\mathbb{Q}).

The goal of this paper is to find ways for bounding the dimension of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
. The dimension of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
is predicted by the following special case of the Bloch–Kato conjectures (see
[
Dog23
, §6]
for an explanation of the deduction).

Conjecture 1
.

[
[
BK90
]
]
Let
Z/\mathbb{Q}
be a smooth projective variety with good reduction outside
S
, suppose
2j\geq i
. Then

\dim H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},H^{j}(Z_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathbb{Q}_{p}(i)))=0.

The dimension of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}V_{2}J)
is (unconditionally) equal, via the short exact sequence

0\to\wedge^{2}T_{2}J\to\wedge^{2}V_{2}J\to\wedge^{2}V_{2}J/\wedge^{2}T_{2}J\to 0,

to the rank of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
.

In this paper, unlike
[
Dog23
]
, we do not attempt to use the crystalline condition. Instead, we will try to estimate the rank of the larger space
H^{1}_{f,\{2\}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
. By definition this is simply the subspace of cohomology classes which are unramified outside a finite set of primes and whose restriction to each prime away from
2
is torsion. We will sometimes denote this subspace simply by
H^{1}_{\{2\}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
.

We first explain how much information working with
H^{1}_{f,\{2\}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
. throws away. We have

\dim H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)\leq\dim H^{1}_{f,\{2\}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)+1.

In general, we would expect this inequality to be strict, as explained by the following lemma, which follows straightforwardly from standard facts about Galois cohomology and
p
-adic Hodge theory (see e.g.
[
Ber13
]
).

Lemma 2
.

We have

\dim H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)-\dim H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\wedge^{2}V_{p}J)=\binom{g}{2}+\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(1),\wedge^{2}V_{p}J).

Hence Conjecture
1
predicts that, replacing
H^{1}_{f}
with
H^{1}_{f,\{2\}}
, we should be able to prove finiteness of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})_{2}
whenever

\rk J(\mathbb{Q})<\binom{g+1}{2}.

For example, when
g=2
, we expect
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})_{2}
to be finite whenever the Mordell–Weil rank of the Jacobian is less than
4
, but we expect to be able to prove finiteness without using the crystalline condition whenever the rank is less than
3
.

The basic strategy for bounding the rank of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
, or
H^{1}_{f,\{2\}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
, is to try to describe their image in

. Recall that by the short exact sequence

0\to\wedge^{2}T_{2}J\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot 2}}{{\longrightarrow}}\wedge^{2}T_{2}J\to\wedge^{2}J[2]\to 0

0\to H^{1}(K,\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\to H^{1}(K,\wedge^{2}J[2])\to H^{2}(K,\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)[2]\to 0.

3.
Boundary maps for
J[4]

The main result in this paper is a method for computing appropriate ‘Selmer conditions’ on
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\wedge^{2}J[2])
at the prime 2. We have two approaches to describing the image of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
in
H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}},\wedge^{2}J[2])
. The first, which is needed in
[
Dog23
]
, is to construct classes using the nonabelian
(x-T)
map. Since it is easy to compute the rank of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
, if one can find enough crystalline classes one can verify that one has found a basis for the image of
H^{1}_{f}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}},\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)
in
H^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{2}},\wedge^{2}J[2])
by a dimension count.

The second approach, taken in this paper, is to construct liftability obstructions. Specifically, in the next section we try to compute the boundary map

H^{1}(K,\wedge^{2}J[2])\to H^{2}(K,\wedge^{2}J[2])

associated to the short exact sequence

0\to\wedge^{2}J[2]\to\wedge^{2}J[4]\to\wedge^{2}J[2]\to 0.

In this section, as a step towards this we describe the boundary map

\delta:H^{1}(K,J[2])\to H^{2}(K,J[2])

associated to the short exact sequence

0\to J[2]\to J[4]\to J[2]\to 0.

The split short exact sequence

0\to J[2]\to\mathrm{Ind}^{K_{f}}_{K}\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\to 0

gives isomorphisms

H^{1}(K,J[2])\simeq\mathrm{Ker}(K_{f}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})

and

H^{2}(K,J[2])\simeq\mathrm{Ker}(H^{2}(K_{f},\mathbb{F}_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(K,\mathbb{F}_{2})).

Our characteristation of
\delta
is in terms of these isomorphisms. We let
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
denote the two inclusions of
K_{f}
into
K_{f,2}
, letting
\alpha
and
\beta
denote the images of
x
under the corresponding maps

K[x]\to K_{f,2}.

Let
\mathrm{cores}^{(1)}
and
\mathrm{cores}^{(2)}
denote the corestriction maps corresponding to the inclusions of
K_{f}
into
K_{f,2}
via
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
respectively.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose that
X
descends to a subfield

associated to

by

z\mapsto\mathrm{cores}^{(2)}_{K_{f,2}|K_{f}}(i_{1}(z)\cup((\beta-\alpha)f^{\prime}(\beta)))

Remark 1
.

We expect that the formula for the boundary map should still hold without the condition on the existence of a rational non-Weierstrass point or the condition on the
2

Lemma 3
.

Let

Let

be a factorisation of

. Let
K_{i}:=K[x]/(f_{i})
, and let
\alpha_{i}
be the image of

in
K_{i}
. Finally, let

\langle,\rangle_{K_{i}}:K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\times K_{i}\times\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\simeq H^{2}(K_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})

be the Hilbert symbol map. Note that we have an isomorphism

H^{2}(K,J[2])\simeq\mathrm{Ker}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{2})

by Tate duality. Hence the boundary map may be viewed as a map

\mathrm{Ker}(\oplus_{i=1}^{m}K_{i}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}.

.

Suppose

3.1.
Strategy of proof of Proposition

We will reduce the proof of Proposition

We deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 19
.

The boundary map

5.1.
Local aspects at infinity: dimensions

Suppose

is a torsion-free abelian group with an indecomposable action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\langle c\rangle
. Then there are exactly three options for
M
. Either
M=\mathbb{Z}
with the trivial action,
M=\mathbb{Z}(1)
(i.e. rank 1, and
c
acts as
-1
) or
M=M_{c}:=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}]
. It is straightforward to verify the following computations.

Lemma 20
.

(1)

M_{c}\otimes\mathbb{Z}(1)\simeq M_{c}
,

(2)

M_{c}\otimes M_{c}\simeq M_{c}^{\oplus 2}
,

(3)

\wedge^{2}M_{c}\simeq\mathbb{Z}(1)
,

(4)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0.

(5)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2}(1))=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

(6)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}_{2}\otimes M_{c})=0.

(7)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{F}_{2}\otimes M_{c})=0
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
f
be an odd degree separable polynomial in
\mathbb{R}[x]
with exactly
r_{1}(f)
real roots. Let
J
denote the Jacobian of

T_{2}(\mathrm{Jac}(X))\simeq(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}(1))^{(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}\oplus M_{c}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{g-(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the corresponding claim for
H_{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
. By the classification of indecomposable
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-modules,
H_{1}(\mathbb{X}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{a}\oplus\mathbb{Z}(1)^{b}\oplus M_{c}^{d}
,where
a+b+2c=2g
. Furthermore we must have
a+d=g
, and by the Lemma we have

a+b+d=\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{R},J[2]).

On ther other hand we also have

\dim H^{0}(\mathbb{R},J[2])=r_{1}(f)+r_{2}(f)-1.

The lemma follows from rearranging these formulas.
∎

We arrive at the following Lemma, which gives a liftability obstruction when

has more than one real root.

Lemma 22
.

For
f
and
J
as above,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{1}(\Gal(\mathbb{C}|\mathbb{R}),\wedge^{2}T_{2}J)=\binom{(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}{2}+g,

and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}H^{1}(\Gal(\mathbb{C}|\mathbb{R}),\wedge^{2}J[2])=\binom{2(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}{2}+g.

Proof.

By Lemma
20
, we deduce

\wedge^{2}T_{2}J\simeq\mathbb{Z}(1)^{\binom{(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}{2}+g}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\binom{(r_{1}(f)-1)/2}{2}}\oplus M_{c}^{(r_{1}(f)-1+g)(5r_{1}(f)+g-1)/2},

and conclude from Lemma
20
.
∎

5.2.

6.1.
The relation with elliptic curve Chabauty

We now explain the relation with elliptic curve Chabauty, as discussed in the introduction. Let

be a genus
2

, and consider the curve

Note that the

rather than

are not the same as the condition of lifting to

-descent methods from this paper prove finiteness of

, but that there is a twist of the cover

(coming from a rational point of

On the other hand, a common obstruction to both methods is

and the elliptic curves above presumably in some cases here one would again see a difference between the Tate–Shafarevich classes which survive.

There are a number of possible ways to extend these results to prove finiteness of

The following more serious obstacles remain.

(1)

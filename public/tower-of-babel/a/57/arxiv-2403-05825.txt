A direct proof of well-definedness for the polymatroid Tutte polynomial
Xiaxia Guan Xian’an Jin School of Mathematical Sciences Xiamen University P. R. China Email:gxx0544@126.com;xajin@xmu.edu.cn

Abstract
For a polymatroid P P over [ n ] [n] , Bernardi, Kálmán and Postnikov [ Adv. Math. 402 (2022) 108355] introduced the polymatroid Tutte polynomial 𝒯 P \mathscr{T}_{P} relying on the order 1 < 2 < ⋯ < n 1<2<\cdots<n of [ n ] [n] , which generalizes the classical Tutte polynomial from matroids to polymatroids. They proved the independence of this order by the fact that 𝒯 P \mathscr{T}_{P} is equivalent to another polynomial that only depends on P P . In this paper, similar to the Tutte’s original proof of the well-definedness of the Tutte polynomial defined by the summation over all spanning trees using activities depending on the order of edges, we give a direct and elementary proof of the well-definedness of the polymatroid Tutte polynomial.

Introduction

The Tutte polynomial, introduced by Tutte

8

It generalizes many classical graph invariants, such as the chromatic polynomial, the flow polynomial etc. Furthermore, the Tutte polynomial and its evalutions play an important role in statistical physics, knot theory, and many other areas of mathematics and physics.

Crapo

[

[n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
,
2^{[n]}=\{I|I\subset[n]\}
, and let
\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2},\cdots,\textbf{e}_{n}
denote the canonical basis of

.

P\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}

P=P_{f}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
(i.e., on the ground set
[n]
) with the rank function

is given by

\left\{(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\leq f(I)\ \text{for any}\ I\subset[n]\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i\in[n]}a_{i}=f([n])\right\},

where

(2)

A vector
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called a

. It is easy to see that the set of bases (viewed as element of

) of any matroid is a polymatroid.

We now recall the definition of the polymatroid Tutte polynomial.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid over
[n]
.
For a basis
\textbf{a}\in P
, an index
i\in[n]
is
internally active
if
\textbf{a}-\textbf{e}_{i}+\textbf{e}_{j}\notin P
for any
j<i
. Let
\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})=\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})\subset[n]
denote the set of all
internally active indices with respect to
a
.

An index
i\in[n]
is
externally active
if
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\notin P
for any
j<i
. Let
\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})=\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})\subset[n]
denote the set of all externally active indices with respect to
a
.

The
polymatroid Tutte polynomial

\mathscr{T}_{P}(x,y)
is defined as

\mathscr{T}_{P}(x,y):=\sum_{\textbf{a}\in P}x^{oi(\textbf{a})}y^{oe(\textbf{a})}(x+y-1)^{ie(\textbf{a})},

where

oi(\textbf{a}):=|\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})\setminus\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})|,

oe(\textbf{a}):=|\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})\setminus\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})|,

ie(\textbf{a}):=|\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})\cap\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})|.

Bernardi et al.
[
1
]
showed that if
M\subset 2^{[n]}
is a matroid of rank
d
over
[n]
, and
P=P(M)\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
is its corresponding polymatroid, then

T_{M}(x,y)=\frac{(x+y-xy)^{n}}{x^{n-d}y^{d}}\mathscr{T}_{P}(\frac{x}{x+y-xy},\frac{y}{x+y-xy}).

Definition 1.3
.

For any
\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

d^{>}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}):=\sum_{i:a_{i}>b_{i}}(a_{i}-b_{i})\ \text{and}\ d^{<}(\textbf{a},\textbf{b}):=\sum_{i:b_{i}>a_{i}}(b_{i}-a_{i}).

Definition 1.4
.

For any polymatroid
P\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and any vector
\textbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define

d^{>}(P,\textbf{c}):=\min_{\textbf{a}\in P}d^{>}(\textbf{a},\textbf{c})\ \text{and}\ \ d^{<}(P,\textbf{c}):=\min_{\textbf{a}\in P}d^{<}(\textbf{a},\textbf{c}).

Moreover, define
the formal power series
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{P}(u,v)
in two variables
u
and
v
by

\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{P}(u,v):=\sum_{\textbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}wt_{P}(\textbf{c}),

where

wt_{P}(\textbf{c}):=u^{d^{>}(P,\textbf{c})}v^{d^{<}(P,\textbf{c})}.

Bernardi et al.
[
1
]
established a relation between
\mathscr{T}_{P}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{P}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
1
, Theorem 10.6]

For any polymatroid
P
, we have that

\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{P}(u,v)=\mathscr{T}_{P}(\frac{1}{1-u},\frac{1}{1-v}).

They also prove that the polymatroid Tutte polynomial is equivalent to the one introduced by Cameron and Fink
[
2
]
.
For a polymatroid
P
, although the definition of
\mathscr{T}_{P}
relies on the natural order

of the ground set
[n]
,
\widetilde{\mathscr{T}}_{P}
depends only on

implies that
\mathscr{T}_{P}
is an invariant of polymatroids

. Namely, for a permutation

w\in S_{n}
and a point
\textbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define

w(\textbf{a}):=(a_{w(1)},\cdots,a_{w(n)})
, and

w(P):=\{w(\textbf{a})|\textbf{a}\in P\},

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group acting on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by permutations of coordinates. Then
\mathscr{T}_{P}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, which can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
1
, Theorem 4.7]

Let
P
be a polymatroid on
[n]
. Then

\mathscr{T}_{P}(x,y)=\mathscr{T}_{w(P)}(x,y)\ \text{for any}\ w\in S_{n}.

In 1954, Tutte
[
8

introduced the Tutte polynomial of graphs as follows.

Definition 1.7

Let

be a spanning tree of

, an edge

in

\tau
.

An edge

denote the number of externally edges with respect

\tau

The
Tutte polynomial

of the graph

is the following sum
over all spanning trees

T_{G}(x,y)=\sum_{\tau\ \text{is a spanning tree of}\ G}x^{i_{G}(\tau)}y^{j_{G}(\tau)}.

In this paper, similar to the idea that Tutte

]
proved that his polynomial does not depend on the order of edges, our proof is direct and elementary for Theorem

Several lemmas

The main aim of this section is to provide several lemmas used in the next section.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid on
[n]
. For any basis
\textbf{a}\in P
and three distinct indices
i,j,k\in[n]
, if
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{i}\in P
and
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
, then
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
.

Proof.

Let
\textbf{b}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{i},

\textbf{c}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}
and
\textbf{d}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{j}.
Let
f
be the rank function of the polymatroid
P
. It is easy to see that

\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[n]}d_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[n]}a_{i^{\prime}}=f([n]).

We now divide two cases to prove that
\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I}d_{i^{\prime}}\leq f(I)
for any subset
I\subset[n]
.

(i)

If
i\notin I
, then
\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I}d_{i^{\prime}}\leq\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I}b_{i^{\prime}}\leq f(I).

(ii)

If
i\in I
, then
\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I}d_{i^{\prime}}\leq\sum\limits_{i^{\prime}\in I}c_{i^{\prime}}\leq f(I).

Hence, the vector
\textbf{d}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
by the definition of polymatroids.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In this section, we give a direct proof of Theorem
1.6
.

It is enough to prove that

\mathscr{T}_{P}(x,y)=\mathscr{T}_{w(P)}(x,y),

where

w(P):=\{(a_{1},\cdots,a_{h+1},a_{h},\cdots,a_{n})\mid(a_{1},\cdots,a_{h},a_{h+1},\cdots,a_{n})\in P\}.

Namely,

1<2<\cdots<h-1<h+1<h<\cdots<n\ \text{in}\ w(P).

Note that for any vector
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\textbf{a}\in P
if and only if
w(\textbf{a})\in w(P)
. Moreover, for two distinct indices
i,j\in[n]
,
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
if and only if
w(\textbf{a})+w(\textbf{e}_{i})-w(\textbf{e}_{j})\in w(P)
. Hence, for simplicity, we will state
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
if
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\in P
or
w(\textbf{a})+w(\textbf{e}_{i})-w(\textbf{e}_{j})\in w(P)
holds.

It is easy to see the following Facts 1, 2 and 3.

Fact 1.
For any
\textbf{a}\in P
and for any
i\in[n]\setminus\{h,h+1\}
,

(i)

i\in\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})
if and only if
i\in\mathrm{Int}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
;

(ii)

i\in\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
if and only if
i\in\mathrm{Ext}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
.

Fact 2.
For any
\textbf{a}\in P
,

(i)

if
h\notin\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})
, then
h\notin\mathrm{Int}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
;

(ii)

if
h\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
, then
h\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
.

Fact 3.
For any
\textbf{a}\in P
,

(i)

if
h+1\notin\mathrm{Int}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
, then
h+1\notin\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})
;

(ii)

if
h+1\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
, then
h+1\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
.

For any
\textbf{a}\in P
, let

\textbf{a}^{\prime}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{h}-\textbf{e}_{h+1}

and

\textbf{a}^{\prime\prime}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{h+1}-\textbf{e}_{h}.

Claim 1.
Assume that
\textbf{a}^{\prime}\in P
.

(i)

If
h\notin\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})
, then
h+1\notin\mathrm{Int}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
.

(ii)

If
h+1\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
, then
h\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
.

Proof of Claim 1.
(i) If
h\notin\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})
, then there is some
k<h
so that
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{h}\in P
. Since
\textbf{a}^{\prime}=\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{h}-\textbf{e}_{h+1}\in P
, by Lemma
2.1
,
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{k}-\textbf{e}_{h+1}\in P
. Hence,
h+1\notin\mathrm{Int}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
.

(ii) If
h+1\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{a}))
, then there is some
k^{\prime}<h
so that
\textbf{a}-\textbf{e}_{k^{\prime}}+\textbf{e}_{h+1}\in P
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\textbf{a}-\textbf{e}_{k^{\prime}}+\textbf{e}_{h}\in P
as
\textbf{a}^{\prime}\in P
. Therefore,
h\notin\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
.

Now we can prove Theorem
1.6
as follows.

For a basis
\textbf{a}\in P
, recall its associated subset
\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{a}}\subset P
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{a}}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in P\bigg|\ b_{i}=a_{i}\ \text{for any}\ i\in[n]\setminus\{h,h+1\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\textbf{b}^{i}:i=1,2,\cdots,l\},

where
\textbf{b}^{i+1}=\textbf{b}^{i}+\textbf{e}_{h}-\textbf{e}_{h+1}
for any
i=1,2,\cdots,l-1
.

1.

If
l=1
, then by Case 1, we have

\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{a}}}x^{oi_{P}(\textbf{b})}y^{oe_{P}(\textbf{b})}(x+y-1)^{ie_{P}(\textbf{b})}=\sum_{w(\textbf{b})\in\mathcal{F}_{w(\textbf{a})}}x^{oi_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}y^{oe_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}(x+y-1)^{ie_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}.

2.

If
l\geq 2
, then by Case 2, for any
i\in A=\{2,3,\cdots,l-1\}
(if
l=2
, then

),

x^{oi_{P}(\textbf{b}^{i})}y^{oe_{P}(\textbf{b}^{i})}(x+y-1)^{ie_{P}(\textbf{b}^{i})}=x^{oi_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}^{i}))}y^{oe_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}^{i}))}(x+y-1)^{ie_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}^{i}))}.

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\{\textbf{b}^{1},\textbf{b}^{l}\}}x^{oi_{P}(\textbf{b})}y^{oe_{P}(\textbf{b})}(x+y-1)^{ie_{P}(\textbf{b})}

\displaystyle\sum_{w(\textbf{b})\in\{w(\textbf{b}^{1}),w(\textbf{b}^{l})\}}x^{oi_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}y^{oe_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}(x+y-1)^{ie_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}.

\sum_{\textbf{b}\in\mathcal{F}_{\textbf{a}}}x^{oi_{P}(\textbf{b})}y^{oe_{P}(\textbf{b})}(x+y-1)^{ie_{P}(\textbf{b})}=\sum_{w(\textbf{b})\in\mathcal{F}_{w(\textbf{a})}}x^{oi_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}y^{oe_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}(x+y-1)^{ie_{w(P)}(w(\textbf{b}))}.

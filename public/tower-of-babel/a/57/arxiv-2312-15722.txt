Frequency-Domain Identification of Discrete-Time Systems using Sum-of-Rational Optimization footnoteinfo This work was partially supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation under NCCR Automation, grant agreement 51NF40_180545.
Mohamed Abdalmoaty, Jared Miller, Mingzhou Yin, Roy Smith

Abstract
We propose a computationally tractable method for the identification of stable canonical discrete-time rational transfer function models, using frequency domain data. The problem is formulated as a global non-convex optimization problem whose objective function is the sum of weighted squared residuals at each observed frequency datapoint. Stability is enforced using a polynomial matrix inequality constraint. The problem is solved globally by a moment-sum-of-squares hierarchy of semidefinite programs through a framework for sum-of-rational-functions optimization. Convergence of the moment-sum-of-squares program is guaranteed as the bound on the degree of the sum-of-squares polynomials approaches infinity. The performance of the proposed method is demonstrated using numerical simulation examples.

Introduction

Fitting parametric
LTI
(
LTI
) models to noisy frequency domain data is a classical problem in system identification
(
Pintelon and Schoukens 2012
;
Ljung 1999
)
. Many methods and algorithms based on stochastic as well as deterministic frameworks have been developed and used. In particular, the

(

) method, and

(

and
(

. They define estimators as solutions to optimization problems; however, except for a few very specific model structures (e.g., ARX models), their implementation is complicated by the fact that the resulting optimization problems are highly non-convex. Solutions of

The goal of this paper is to perform parametric identification of stable canonical rational
LTI
models through global optimization of a weighted 2-norm of the residuals in the frequency domain. For strictly proper discrete-time SISO system with a known fixed order
n

\displaystyle G(z;(a,b))=\frac{B(z^{-1})}{1+A(z^{-1})}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}z^{-k}}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}z^{-k}}

(1)

where

\mathcal{Z}
-transform.
The problem is formulated in the frequency domain using a sequence of

at frequencies
\omega_{f}
, forming the data set
\mathcal{D}=\{(\omega_{f},G_{f})\}_{f=1}^{N_{f}}

\displaystyle G_{f}=G_{\circ}(e^{j\omega_{f}})+\eta_{f}.

(2)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\lVert W\odot\eta(a,b)\rVert_{2}^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{f=1}^{N_{f}}\lvert W_{f}\left(G_{f}-G\left(e^{j\omega_{f}};(a,b)\right)\right)\rvert^{2}.

\displaystyle P^{*}=\inf_{(a,b)}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}(a,b).

\displaystyle p^{*}=\inf_{\mu,\nu_{\ell}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)}\textstyle

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\langle p_{\ell}(x),\nu_{\ell}(x)\rangle:

\displaystyle\langle 1,\mu\rangle=1

(8b)

\displaystyle\forall\ell\in\{1,\dots,L\}:\textrm{\eqref{eq:abscont} holds}.

(1+\bar{A}(e^{-j\omega f}))=0
. For every

, let

denote the multiplicity of the root

There are two possible circumstances going forward. The first is that

(removable singularity), which implies the existence of a complete pole-zero cancellation leaving

(including if

.

Considering that
\forall f\in\mathcal{F}:\ \nu_{f}=0
, the objective in (
22a
) would return

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{f^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{F}}^{N_{f}}\langle p_{f^{\prime}}(a,b),\nu^{\prime}_{f^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{f^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{F}}^{N_{f}}p_{f}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})/q_{f}(a^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}).

(24)

In summary, the measures

and
\nu_{f}
may be constructed for every

with an objective in (

) attaining the
\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{D}}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
(
\lvert\mathcal{F}\rvert=0
) or lower-bounding it (
\lvert\mathcal{F}\rvert>0
). This proves the theorem.

6
Conclusion

We proposed a method of estimating canonical rational transfer functions based on the global minimization of a frequency-response weighted residuals 2-norm objective. The original non-convex problem is converted to an equivalent linear program in measures by using a sum-of-rational formulation. Certifiable global optima can be obtained through moment-
SOS
-based truncations of the linear program in measures.

The extended version of this paper at
Abdalmoaty et al. 2023
includes further discussions about strong duality, identification of continuous-time systems, closed-loop identification, and identification of outlier-corrupted data.
Future work includes the addition of regularization terms in order to prevent overfitting the noise process, and a homogenized formulation to reduce the conservatism of Assumption
3
. Other work includes improvement of the
SDP
’s numerical conditioning, especially for cases where the poles are close to the boundary of the unit disk.

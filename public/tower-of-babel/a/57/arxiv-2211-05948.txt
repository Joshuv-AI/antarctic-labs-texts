Abstract
. The local structure of terminal Brauer classes on arithmetic surfaces were classified in [ CI21 ] generalising the classification on geometric surfaces carried out in [ CI05 ] . Part of the interest in these classifications is that it enables the minimal model program to be applied to the noncommutative setting of orders on surfaces. In this paper, we give étale local structure theorems for terminal orders on arithemtic surfaces, at least when the degree is a prime p > 5 p>5 . This generalises the structure theorem given in the geometric case. They can all be explicitly constructed as algebras of matrices over symbols. From this description one sees that such terminal orders all have global dimension two, thus generalising the fact that terminal (commutative) surfaces are smooth and hence homologically regular.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth point on a complex variety of dimension
d
, the étale local structure is
\operatorname{Spec}R
where
R=\mathbb{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
, the algebra of algebraic power series in
d
variables. This result elegantly captures, in an algebraic fashion, the idea that manifolds are all locally Euclidean. In
[
CI05
]
, a noncommutative analogue of this result was given for the case of orders on a complex surface. Here we extend the result to arbitrary surfaces, at least under some mild hypotheses.

More precisely, let
R
be an excellent noetherian normal two dimensional Hensel local domain with fraction field
K
. Let
\Lambda
be a maximal
R
-order in some central simple
K
-algebra. It is natural to associate the Brauer class
\beta_{\Lambda}\in\textup{Br}\,K,
to this algebra, so one can use the minimal model program for surfaces enriched by a Brauer class as developed in
[
CI05
]
and
[
CI21
]
. More generally, if
\Lambda
is a normal
R
-order (see Definition
2.1
) then we attach a localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{\mathfrak{p}})
(in Definition
2.2
) where the integers

measure how much
\Lambda
deviates from being maximal at the codimension one prime
\mathfrak{p}
.

When the residue field
\kappa
is algebraically closed, terminal localised Brauer classes
(\beta,g_{\mathfrak{p}})
, and the corresponding terminal orders were completely classified in
[
CI05
]
and shown to always have global dimension two. In
[
CI05
]
, it is shown that i)
R
is smooth, ii)
\beta
is zero or has ramification along a normal crossing divisor
C_{1}\cup C_{2}
, and iii)
\Lambda
is maximal except possibly along a curve of multiplicity one when
\beta=0,
or one of the
C_{i}
otherwise. Furthermore, a complete structure theorem was given for terminal normal orders in this case (see
[
CI05
, Section 2]
). If
\Lambda
is maximal, then it is isomorphic to a full matrix algebra over a symbol
\Delta=R\langle y,z\rangle/(y^{m}-u,z^{m}-v,zy-\zeta yz)
where
\zeta
is some primitive
m
-th root of unity and
u,v\in R
is a regular system of parameters. In general, one obtains a triangular modulo
z
matrix algebra over such symbols as defined in Definition
2.4
.

When
\kappa
is a finite field of characteristic prime to the order
m
of
\beta
, it turns out there are more possibilities for terminal localised Brauer classes. If
m
is a prime
>5
, the terminal localised Brauer classes were completely classified in
[
CI21
]
. The new possibilities are summed up in Definitions
3.1
and
5.1
, but briefly, when
R
is regular, there is the additional possibility that
\beta
is ramified on a single multiplicity one curve, and more interestingly,
R
can also be a type of Hirzebruch-Jung singuluarity, in which case
\beta\neq 0,
though it is unramified along codimension one primes of
R
. Our main theorem is the following result which generalises the aforementioned structure theory of terminal normal orders to this arithmetic situation. Again, symbols feature significantly but in the more general sense of tensor products of a
\mathbb{Z}/m
-extension of

\mu_{m}
-extension and graded components skew commute according to the natural pairing
\mathbb{Z}/m\times\mu_{m}\to\mu_{m}

).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be an excellent noetherian two-dimensional normal Hensel local domain. Let
\Lambda
be a terminal
R
-order whose degree is a prime, say
m>5
. If the residue field of
R
contains a primitive
m
-th root of unity and has trivial Brauer group, then
\Lambda
has global dimension two. In fact, all such
\Lambda
are explicitly constructed in Propositions
3.5
,
3.3
and Theorem
5.7
as various triangular modulo
z
matrix algebras over symbols.

To prove the theorem, we construct explicit examples of normal orders with all the possible terminal localised Brauer classes. In the case when
R
is regular, this is relatively straight forward. In the singular case, we need to show that our Hirzebruch-Jung singularity, defined to have a minimal resolution whose exceptional locus is a string of projective lines defined over the residue field of
R
, is actually a cyclic quotient singularity. We prove this in Section
4
, and give an explicit construction of the regular cyclic cover which is used in the construction of the corresponding terminal orders. The other step is to show that these explicitly constructed orders are sufficiently nice (in particular, have global dimension two) and that any normal order with the same ramification data has to be Morita equivalent to them. We follow the basic framework of
[
CI05
]
, Unfortunately, the use of the Cohen-Structure theorem in
[
CI05
]
is unavailable in this setting, so we give a streamlined method avoiding this tool in Section
2
.

2.
Uniqueness result for regular almost maximal orders

In this section, we review basic definitions of orders, their ramification theory and the relationship with the Brauer group. Since the notion of maximal orders is not stable under étale localisation, we review normal orders as introduced in
[
CI05
]
. The main result is a uniqueness type result for certain normal orders which have global dimension two and are maximal everywhere except possibly on a single irreducible divisor. This was proved in
[
CI05
]
over an algebraically closed field using some complicated argument in §2.3. We present a streamlined proof here using the classification of normal orders over discrete valuation rings found in the Appendix §
6
.

Let
R
be a noetherian normal domain and
K
its field of fractions. Given a central simple
K
-algebra
Q
, an
order

A
in
Q
is an
R
-subalgebra such that
A
is a finitely generated
R
-module such that
KA=Q
. Then in fact
K\otimes_{R}A\simeq Q
so we sometimes dispense with explicitly mentioning
Q
and say a finite
R
-algebra
A
is an
R
-order if it is a torsion-free
R
-module such that
K\otimes_{R}A
is a central simple
K
-algebra. We define the
degree
of
A
to be the
\deg A:=\deg K\otimes_{R}A=\sqrt{\dim_{K}K\otimes_{R}A}
.

One ought to think of
A
as a model of the noncommutative “field”
Q
in this case. The classical noncommutative analogue of the notion of normality, is that the order is
maximal
, that is, if
A^{\prime}
is another order in
Q
containing
A
, then
A=A^{\prime}
. Unfortunately, this notion is not stable under étale localisation. When
R
is two-dimensional, the following condition was introduced in
[
CI05
]
to remedy this defect, taking its cue from Serre’s criterion for normality in the commutative case.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a two-dimensional normal domain. An
R
-order
A
is said to be
normal
if the following two conditions hold.

(1)

A
is a reflexive
R
-module.

(2)

For every height one prime
\mathfrak{p}
, the localisation
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
normal
in the sense that its radical is principal as a left and right ideal.

The second condition is thoroughly analysed in the Appendix §
6
. Note that maximal orders are normal, and that normal orders are tame.

Let
R
be a two-dimensional normal domain and
A
be a normal
R
-order. Since
K\otimes_{R}A
is a central simple
K
-algebra, it determines a corresponding Brauer class
\beta_{A}\in\text{Br}\,K
. Given any codimension one prime
\mathfrak{p}\triangleleft R
, with corresponding residue field
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
, there is a ramification map

a_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\text{Br}\,K\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

As noted by Artin and Mumford
[
AM72
]
, this map can be interpreted in terms of orders as follows. First note that
a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{A})
, being an element of
H^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is given by a cyclic field extension
\kappa^{\prime}
of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
and a choice of generator
\sigma
for the Galois group
\text{Gal}(\kappa^{\prime}/\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))
. Let
J
be the radical of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
which is principal, and so is generated by an element, say
\pi
. Then
\kappa^{\prime}=Z(A_{\mathfrak{p}}/J)
and
\sigma
is the automorphism induced by conjugation by
\pi
. Note that
a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{A})=0
means that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Azumaya so the collection of non-zero
a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{A})
is called the ramification data of
A
. The above results are described in more detail in
[
AdJ
, §1]
,
[
Cha
]
,
[
Cha10
, §4]
,
[
GI21
, §1]
.

If now
B
is a normal order contained in the maximal order
A
above, then from the Appendix, we know that
Z(B_{\mathfrak{p}}/\text{rad}\,B_{\mathfrak{p}})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\kappa^{\prime}
for some
d
. Furthermore, conjugation by a generator
t
of
\text{rad}\,B_{\mathfrak{p}}
permutes the
d
factors cyclically, and conjugation by
t^{d}
reduces to
\sigma
. The ramification data of

will thus not only include the
a_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{A})
, but also the integers
g_{\mathfrak{p}}:=d
. Since
B
is generically Azumaya and thus maximal, we find on varying
\mathfrak{p}
that all but finitely many of the
g_{\mathfrak{p}}
will be one.

Definition 2.2
.

A
localised Brauer class on
R
is a pair
(\beta,g_{\mathfrak{p}})
consisting of a Brauer class
\beta\in\text{Br}\,K
and a function assigning to each codimension one prime
\mathfrak{p}\triangleleft R
a positive integer
g_{\mathfrak{p}}
which equals one for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}
. In particular, the localised Brauer class of the normal order
B
above is
(\beta_{A},g_{\mathfrak{p}})
in the notation of the previous paragraph.

We now give an instance where the localised Brauer class and
R
-rank of a normal
R
-order determines the isomorphism class of the order.

Assumption 2.3
.

Suppose now that
R
is a Hensel local two-dimensional normal domain. Let
\Delta
be a maximal
R
-order in a division ring and suppose that there exists a normal element
z\in\Delta
such that

(1)

The quotient
\Delta/z\Delta
is supported, as an
R
-module on a codimension one prime
\mathfrak{q}

(2)

The element

generates the radical of
\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}}

(3)

\Delta/z\Delta
is hereditary.

Under these assumptions, we construct the order

(2.1)

\Delta_{d}=\Delta_{d}(z):=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta&\Delta&\cdots&\Delta\\
z\Delta&\Delta&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z\Delta&\cdots&z\Delta&\Delta\end{pmatrix}\subseteq M_{d}(\Delta)

Definition 2.4
.

We will refer to the subalgebra
\Delta_{d}
in (
2.1
) above as a
triangular modulo
z
matrix algebra
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Under Assumption
2.3
, the order
\Delta_{d}
is normal and has global dimension two. Furthermore, its localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{\mathfrak{p}})
is given by

(1)

\beta
is the Brauer class of
\Delta
.

(2)

g_{\mathfrak{q}}=d
and all other

.

Proof.

Note (1) follows from the fact that
\Delta_{d}
is an order in
M_{n}(K\Delta)
. To check

is a reflexive

-module, note first that the

is reflexive being a maximal order. Also,

are reflexive as well.

We consider now local structure at a codimension one prime

, then from Assumption

(1), we know that

Finally, consider the normal element

t:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\vdots&&\ddots&1\\

By Assumption

is hereditary, so

itself must have global dimension two.
∎

In the light of this proposition, we might consider
\Delta_{d}
to be regular and almost maximal as in the title of this section.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.3
hold and let
\Delta_{d}
be the normal
R
-order of (
2.1
). Let
\Lambda
be any normal

-order with the same localised Brauer class as

. Then

is an integer and
\Lambda\simeq M_{n}(\Delta_{d})
so has global dimension two.

Proof.

Suppose
\deg\Delta_{d}|\deg\Lambda
and let
\Lambda^{\prime}:=M_{n}(\Delta_{d})
. Since the localised Brauer classes of
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}
coincide, as do their degrees, we may embed them both in a common central simple
K
-algebra

. By Corollary

, we know that
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{q}}\simeq\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{q}}
so by altering one of the embeddings, we may suppose that we actually have
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{q}}=\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Consider the
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})
-bimodule
B:=(\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime})^{**}\subset Q
, the reflexive hull of the
R
-module
\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}
. Now
\Lambda^{\prime}
has global dimension two by Proposition

and

is Cohen-Macaulay as an

-module so is projective as a

-module by
[

(the hypotheses are stated differently there but the proof applies in our case).

We first show an isomorphism of right

modules,
B_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\simeq\Lambda^{\prime}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Now

is Henselian so Krull-Schmidt holds for
\Lambda^{\prime}
-modules. The indecomposable projective

-modules are isomorphic to summands of
\Lambda^{\prime}
and there are exactly

isomorphism classes of these, say

, corresponding to the rows of
\Delta_{d}
. From Proposition

there exists an integer

such that
(P_{i})_{\mathfrak{q}}/(P_{i})_{\mathfrak{q}}(\text{rad}\,\Lambda^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{q}})\simeq S_{i}^{\oplus r}
where

is a simple

are all non-isomorphic. In particular, two finitely generated projective

-modules are isomorphic, if and only if their localisations at

are isomorphic. Now by our choice of embeddings,

so

as desired.

To complete the proof of the theorem when

, it suffices to show that the natural map
\Lambda\to\operatorname{End}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}B=\Lambda^{\prime}
is an isomorphism. Since both sides are reflexive, this can be checked on codimension one primes
\mathfrak{p}
. When
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}
, it is an isomorphism since
\Lambda
is maximal. When
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}
, it is an isomorphism since we recalibrated so

.

If

does not divide

, we apply the special case proved to

, which shows that at least

is Morita equivalent to
\Delta_{d}
. Hence
\Lambda\simeq\operatorname{End}_{\Delta_{d}}P
for some projective

-module. We can argue as before, looking locally at

to see that

for some

∎

3.
toral terminal orders, regular centre case

We have a series of concepts that are motivated by toric or toroidal geometry, but that does not satisfy either of these definitions. We call these
toral
.
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two-dimensional noetherian regular Hensel local domain with field of fractions
K
. We introduce the notion of a
toral terminal
localised Brauer class on
R
. These are all terminal.
Futhermore, in the special setting of
[
CI21
]
, i.e. when
R
is an arithmetic surface with finite residue field and the ramification data are all
p
-torsion for some prime

, this exactly agrees with terminal. Assuming that

has enough roots of unity and a trivial Brauer group, we then classify the normal

-orders associated to toral terminal localised Brauer classes and show they all are regular in the sense that they have global dimension two.

In the regular centre case, toral terminal localised Brauer classes fall into two types. The first, without secondary ramification is defined below.

Definition 3.1
.

A localised Brauer class
(\beta,g_{\mathfrak{p}})
on
R
is
toral terminal without secondary ramification
if there exists a regular system of parameters

such that the following holds

(1)

\beta
is unramified at every codimension one prime
\mathfrak{p}
except possibly
\mathfrak{p}=(u)
(that is,

) and,

(2)

All
g_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
except possibly
\mathfrak{p}=(v)
.

We will construct orders via symbols and so need to assume the existence of enough roots of unity.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose
\zeta\in R
is a primitive

-th root of unity. Given

we define the

-symbol

(a,b):=(a,b)^{R}_{\zeta}
to be the
R
-algebra

\Lambda=\frac{R\langle x,y\rangle}{(x^{n}-a,y^{n}-b,yw-\zeta xy)}.

.

Let
R
be a two-dimensional regular noetherian Hensel local domain and

be a toral terminal localised Brauer class with ramification as given in Definition
3.1
. Suppose that
\text{Br}\,R/\mathfrak{m}=0
and

possesses a primitive

-th root of unity

(1)

(2)

4.
Hirzebruch-Jung singularities as cyclic quotient singularities

Over the complex numbers the Hirzebruch-Jung singularities are well understood and all arise as cyclic quotient singularities. In this section, we show a similar result for two-dimensional normal singularities which are Hirzebruch-Jung in the sense that their minimal resolution is a string of projective lines defined over the residue field.

More precisely, suppose that
R
is a two-dimensional normal noetherian excellent commutative Hensel local domain with residue field
\kappa
. Suppose it is a
\kappa
-rational Hirzebruch-Jung
singularity in the sense that it has a rational minimal resolution
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}R
such that the exceptional locus is a string
E_{1},\ldots E_{r}
of exceptional curves isomorphic to the projective line over
\kappa

Appendix

The theory of normal and more generally hereditary orders over a complete discrete valuation ring is well-known and can be found in standard texts such as Reiner’s classic text
[
Rei75
]
. In this appendix, we extend some results to arbitrary discrete valuation rings
R
which are not necessarily complete.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of
R
and
K
be its field of fractions. Let
\Delta
be a maximal order in some
K
-central division ring
K\Delta
. Let
\Lambda\subseteq M_{n}(\Delta)
be an hereditary order in
M_{n}(K\Delta)
with say Jacobson radical
J
. Note that
\Delta/\textup{rad}\,\Delta
is central simple, say isomorphic to

where

is a division ring.

Proposition 6.1
.

Consider a right projective
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
\textup{End}\,P\simeq\Delta
. Then
P/PJ\simeq S^{\oplus r}
where
S
is a simple
\Lambda
-module and

is the integer such that
\Delta/\textup{rad}\,\Delta\simeq M_{r}(D)
for some division ring
D
. This result holds in particular for
P=\Delta^{n}
.

Proof.

Consider the natural ring homomorphism

\Delta=\textup{End}_{\Lambda}P\to\textup{End}_{\Lambda}P/PJ.

This map is surjective since
P
is projective. From the ideal theory of maximal orders
[
AG60
, Theorem 2.3]
, the only semisimple quotient of
\Delta
is
\Delta/\textup{rad}\,\Delta\simeq M_{r}(D)
so
P/PJ
must be the direct sum of
r
copies of a single simple.

Note finally that
\textup{End}_{\Lambda}\Delta^{n}=\textup{End}_{M_{n}(\Delta)}\Delta^{n}
since
\Lambda
is also an order in
M_{n}(K\Delta)
. The final statement follows thus from
\Delta=\textup{End}_{M_{n}(\Delta)}\Delta^{n}
which is a consequence of Morita theory.
∎

Definition 6.2
.

We say that
\Lambda
is

if its Jacobson radical

is free of rank 1 as a left and right module.

If
\Lambda
is normal, then it is easy to see that one can choose a

. We refer to this as the

on the Wedderburn components, which induces an analogous

). Suppose there are
d

. The following show that the

Proposition 6.3

The action of

permutes all the simples cyclically. In other words, by re-indexing if necessary, we may assume that
S_{i}^{\oplus r}\simeq Pt^{i-1}/Pt^{i}
where

.

Proof.

,
From Proposition

, we may assume
S_{1}
is the simple such that

. The composition factors of any finite length quotient of

. Let

For our structure theory, we will need the following order

As subsets of

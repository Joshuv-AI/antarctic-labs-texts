Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a global existence and blow-up of the positive solutions to the initial-boundary value problem of the nonlinear porous medium equation and the nonlinear pseudo-parabolic equation on the stratified Lie groups. Our proof is based on the concavity argument and the Poincaré inequality, established in [ 38 ] for stratified groups.

1.
Introduction

The main purpose of this paper is to study the global existence and blow-up of the positive solutions to the initial-boundary problem of the nonlinear porous medium equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\mathcal{L}_{p}(u^{m})=f(u),\,\,\,&x\in D,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,t)=0,\,\,\,&x\in\partial D,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\,\,\,&x\in\overline{D},\end{cases}

(1.1)

and the nonlinear pseudo-parabolic equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}-\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u_{t})-\mathcal{L}_{p}u=f(u),\,\,\,&x\in D,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,t)=0,\,\,\,&x\in\partial D,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,\,\,\,&x\in\overline{D},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
m\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
,
f
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}
,
f(0)=0
, and such that
f(u)>0
for
u>0
. Furthermore, we suppose that
u_{0}
is a non-negative and non-trivial function in
C^{1}(\overline{D})
with
u_{0}(x)=0
on the boundary
\partial D
for
p=2
and in
L^{\infty}(D)\cap\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
for
p>2
, respectively.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group. We say that an open set
D\subset\mathbb{G}
is an admissible domain if it is bounded and if its boundary
\partial D
is piecewise smooth and simple, that is, it has no self-intersections.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group. Let
D\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open set, then we define the functional spaces

S^{1,p}(D)=\{u:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R};u,|\nabla_{H}u|\in L^{p}(D)\}.

(1.3)

We consider the following functional

\mathcal{J}_{p}(u):=\left(\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus, the functional class
\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
can be defined as the completion of
C_{0}^{1}(D)
in the norm generated by
\mathcal{J}_{p}
, see e.g.
[
7
]
.

A Lie group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ)
is called a stratified (Lie) group
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a) For some integer numbers
N_{1}+N_{2}+...+N_{r}=n
,
the decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{r}}
is valid, and
for any
\lambda>0
the dilation

\delta_{\lambda}(x):=(\lambda x^{\prime},\lambda^{2}x^{(2)},\ldots,\lambda^{r}x^{(r)})

is an automorphism of
\mathbb{G}.
Here
x^{\prime}\equiv x^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}
and
x^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{k}}
for
k=2,\ldots,r.

(b) Let
N_{1}
be as in (a) and let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N_{1}}
be the left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
such that

X_{k}(0)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}|_{0}
for
k=1,\ldots,N_{1}.
Then the Hörmander rank condition must be satisfied, that is,

{\rm rank}({\rm Lie}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N_{1}}\})=n,

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then, we say that the triple
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\circ,\delta_{\lambda})
is a stratified (Lie) group.

Recall that the standard Lebesgue measure
dx
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the Haar measure for
\mathbb{G}
(see e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
39
]
).
The left-invariant vector field
X_{j}
has an explicit form:

X_{k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\prime}_{k}}+\sum_{l=2}^{r}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{l}}a_{k,m}^{(l)}(x^{\prime},...,x^{(l-1)})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{m}^{(l)}},

(1.4)

see e.g.
[
39
]
.
The following notations are used throughout this paper:

\nabla_{H}:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N_{1}})

for the horizontal gradient, and

\mathcal{L}_{p}f:=\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}f|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}f),\quad 1<p<\infty,

(1.5)

for the
p
-sub-Laplacian. When
p=2
, that is,
the second order differential operator

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}X_{k}^{2},

(1.6)

is called the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
.
The sub-Laplacian
\mathcal{L}
is a left-invariant homogeneous hypoelliptic differential operator and it is known that
\mathcal{L}
is elliptic if and only if the step of
\mathbb{G}
is equal to 1.

2.
Nonlinear porous medium equation

In this section, we prove the global solutions and blow-up phenomena of the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
).

2.1.
Blow-up solutions of the nonlinear porous medium equation

We start with the blow-up properly.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group with
N_{1}
being the dimension of the first stratum. Let
D\subset\mathbb{G}
be an admissible domain. Let
2\leq p<\infty
with
p\neq N_{1}
.

Assume that function
f
satisfies

\alpha F(u)\leq u^{m}f(u)+\beta u^{pm}+\alpha\gamma,\,\,\,u>0,

(2.1)

where

F(u)=\frac{pm}{m+1}\int_{0}^{u}s^{m-1}f(s)ds,\,\,\,m\geq 1,

for some

\gamma>0,\,\,0<\beta\leq\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-m-1)}{m+1}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\alpha>m+1,

where
R=\sup_{x\in D}|x^{\prime}|
and
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
with
x^{\prime}
being in the first stratum.
Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(D)\cap\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
satisfy the inequality

J_{0}:=-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}_{0}(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx>0.

(2.2)

Then any positive solution
u
of (
1.1
) blows up in finite time
T^{*},
i.e., there exists

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M}{\sigma\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx},

(2.3)

such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau=+\infty,

(2.4)

where
M>0
and
\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{pm\alpha}}{m+1}-1>0
.
In fact, in (
2.3
), we can take

M=\frac{(1+\sigma)(1+1/\sigma)(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx)^{2}}{\alpha(m+1)J_{0}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that condition on nonlinearity (
2.1
) includes the following cases:

1.

Philippin and Proytcheva
[
35
]
used the condition

(2+\epsilon)F(u)\leq uf(u),\,\,\,u>0,

(2.5)

where
\epsilon>0
. It is a special case of an abstract condition by Levine and Payne
[
31
]
.

2.

Bandle and Brunner
[
4
]
relaxed this condition as follows

(2+\epsilon)F(u)\leq uf(u)+\gamma,\,\,\,u>0,

(2.6)

where
\epsilon>0
and
\gamma>0
.

These cases were established on the bounded domains of the Euclidean space, and it is a new result on the stratified groups.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Assume that
u(x,t)
is a positive solution of (
1.1
). We use the concavity method for showing the blow-up phenomena. We introduce the functional

\displaystyle J(t):=-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx,

(2.7)

and by (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle J(0)=-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}_{0}(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx>0.

(2.8)

Moreover,
J(t)
can be written in the following form

J(t)=J(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dJ(\tau)}{d\tau}d\tau,

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dJ(\tau)}{d\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\frac{d}{d\tau}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,\tau)|^{p}dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\frac{d}{d\tau}(F(u(x,\tau))-\gamma)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{p}{m+1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,\tau)|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u^{m}\cdot\nabla_{H}(u^{m}(x,\tau))_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}F_{u}(u(x,\tau))u_{\tau}(x,\tau)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{m+1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[\mathcal{L}_{p}(u^{m})+f(u)](u^{m}(x,\tau))_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{pm}{m+1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau.

Define

E(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau+M,\,\,t\geq 0,

with
M>0
to be chosen later. Then the first derivative with respect
t
of
E(t)
gives

E^{\prime}(t)=\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,t)dx=(m+1)\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{m}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}(x,\tau)d\tau dx+\int_{D}u^{m+1}_{0}(x)dx.

By applying (
2.1
), Lemma
1.2
and
0<\beta\leq\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-m-1)}{m+1}
, we estimate the second derivative of
E(t)
as follows

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(m+1)\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=-(m+1)\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+(m+1)\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)f(u(x,t))dx

\displaystyle\geq-(m+1)\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+(m+1)\int_{D}\left[\alpha F(u(x,t))-\beta u^{pm}(x,t)-\alpha\gamma\right]dx

\displaystyle=\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+(\alpha-m-1)\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx-\beta(m+1)\int_{D}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\geq\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}(\alpha-m-1)-\beta(m+1)\right]\int_{D}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\geq\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha(m+1)J(t)

\displaystyle=\alpha(m+1)J(0)+p\alpha m\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau.

By employing the Hölder and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, we obtain the estimate for
[E^{\prime}(t)]^{2}
as follows

\displaystyle[E^{\prime}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}(u^{m+1}(x,\tau))_{\tau}d\tau dx\right)^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{m}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{(m+1)/2+(m-1)/2}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u^{m+1}d\tau\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u^{m-1}u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)d\tau\right)^{1/2}dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2},

for arbitrary
\delta>0
. So we have

\small[E^{\prime}(t)]^{2}\leq(m+1)^{2}(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}u_{\tau}^{2}dxd\tau\right)+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}dx\right)^{2}.

(2.10)

The previous estimates together with
\sigma=\delta=\frac{\sqrt{pm\alpha}}{m+1}-1>0
where positivity comes from
\alpha>m+1
, imply

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}(t)E(t)-(1+\sigma)[E^{\prime}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha M(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}_{0}|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0})-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+pm\alpha\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)u^{m-1}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle-(m+1)^{2}(1+\sigma)(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m+1}dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle-(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha M(m+1)J(0)-(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}.

By assumption
J(0)>0
, thus if we select

M=\frac{(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}(x)dx\right)^{2}}{\alpha(m+1)J(0)},

that gives

E^{\prime\prime}(t)E(t)-(1+\sigma)(E^{\prime}(t))^{2}\geq 0.

(2.11)

We can see that the above expression for
t\geq 0
implies

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{E^{\prime}(t)}{E^{\sigma+1}(t)}\right]\geq 0\Rightarrow\begin{cases}E^{\prime}(t)\geq\left[\frac{E^{\prime}(0)}{E^{\sigma+1}(0)}\right]E^{1+\sigma}(t),\\
E(0)=M.\end{cases}

Then for
\sigma=\frac{\sqrt{pm\alpha}}{m+1}-1>0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sigma}\left[E^{-\sigma}(t)-E^{-\sigma}(0)\right]\geq\frac{E^{\prime}(0)}{E^{\sigma+1}(0)}t,

and some rearrangements with
E(0)=M
give

E(t)\geq\left(\frac{1}{M^{\sigma}}-\frac{\sigma\int_{D}u^{m+1}_{0}(x)dx}{M^{\sigma+1}}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}.

Then the blow-up time
T^{*}
satisfies

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M}{\sigma\int_{D}u_{0}^{m+1}dx}.

That completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Global existence for the nonlinear porous medium equation

We now show that under some assumptions, if a positive solution to (
1.1
) exists, its norm is globally controlled.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group with
N_{1}
being the dimension of the first stratum. Let
D\subset\mathbb{G}
be an admissible domain. Let
2\leq p<\infty
with
p\neq N_{1}
.

Assume that

\alpha F(u)\geq u^{m}f(u)+\beta u^{pm}+\alpha\gamma,\,\,\,u>0,

(2.12)

where

F(u)=\frac{pm}{m+1}\int_{0}^{u}s^{m-1}f(s)ds,\,\,\,m\geq 1,

for some

\gamma\geq 0,\,\,\alpha\leq 0\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\beta\geq\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-m-1)}{m+1},

where
R=\sup_{x\in D}|x^{\prime}|
and
x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
with
x^{\prime}
being in the first stratum.

Assume also that
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(D)\cap\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
satisfies inequality

J_{0}:=\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}_{0}(x)|^{p}dx>0.

(2.13)

If
u
is a positive local solution of problem (
1.1
), then it is global and satisfies the following estimate:

\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,t)dx\leq\int_{D}u^{m+1}_{0}(x)dx.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
2.1
, the functional

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx

\displaystyle=J_{0}+\frac{pm}{m+1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau.

Let us define

\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{D}u^{m+1}(x,t)dx.

By applying (
2.12
), Lemma
1.2
and
\beta\geq\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-m-1)}{m+1}
, respectively, one finds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=(m+1)\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=(m+1)\left[\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t))+\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)f(u(x,t))dx\right]

\displaystyle=(m+1)\left[-\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}u^{m}(x,t)f(u(x,t))dx\right]

\displaystyle\leq(m+1)\left[-\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}\left[\alpha F(u(x,t))-\beta u^{pm}(x,t)-\alpha\gamma\right]dx\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle-(m+1-\alpha)\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx-\beta(m+1)\int_{D}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}(m+1-\alpha)+\beta(m+1)\right]\int_{D}u^{pm}(x,t)dx

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(m+1)\left[-\frac{1}{m+1}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u^{m}(x,t)|^{2}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha(m+1)J(t).

We can rewrite
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)
by using (
2.9
) and
\alpha\leq 0
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\leq\alpha(m+1)J(0)+p\alpha m\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u^{m-1}(x,\tau)u_{\tau}^{2}(x,\tau)dxd\tau\leq 0.

(2.14)

That gives

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0).

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

3.
Nonlinear pseudo-parabolic equation

In this section, we prove the global solutions and blow-up phenomena of the initial-boundary value problem (
1.2
).

3.1.
Blow-up phenomena for the pseudo-parabolic equation

We start with conditions ensuring the blow-up of solutions in finite time.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group with
N_{1}
being the dimension of the first stratum. Let
D\subset\mathbb{G}
be an admissible domain. Let
2\leq p<\infty
with
p\neq N_{1}
.

Assume that

\alpha F(u)\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{p}+\alpha\gamma,\,\,\,u>0,

(3.1)

where

F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds,

for some

\displaystyle\alpha>p\,\,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\,\,0<\beta\leq\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-p)}{p},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\gamma>0\,\,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\,\,R=\sup_{x\in D}|x^{\prime}|.

Assume also that
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(D)\cap\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
satisfies

\mathcal{F}_{0}:=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx>0.

(3.3)

Then any positive solution
u
of (
1.2
) blows up in finite time
T^{*},
i.e., there exists

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M}{\sigma\int_{D}u_{0}^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}dx},

(3.4)

such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau=+\infty,

(3.5)

where
\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1>0
and

M=\frac{(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\left(\int_{D}u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}dx\right)^{2}}{2\alpha\mathcal{F}_{0}}.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The proof is based on a concavity method. The main idea is to show that
[E^{-\sigma}_{p}(t)]^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
which means that
E^{-\sigma}_{p}(t)
is a concave function, for
E_{p}(t)
defined below.

Let us introduce some notations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t):=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(0):=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx,

with

F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds.

We know that

\mathcal{F}(t)=\mathcal{F}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\mathcal{F}(\tau)}{d\tau}d\tau,

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\mathcal{F}(\tau)}{d\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\frac{d}{d\tau}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\frac{d}{d\tau}(F(u)-\gamma)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}dxd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}F_{u}(u)u_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[\mathcal{L}_{p}u+f(u)]u_{\tau}dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u_{\tau}^{2}-u_{\tau}\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u_{\tau})dxd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}dxd\tau.

Let us define

\displaystyle E_{p}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau+M,\,\,t\geq 0,

with a positive constant
M>0
to be chosen later. Then

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{p}(t)=\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dx=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau+\int_{D}u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}dx.

(3.7)

Now we estimate
E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)
by using assumption (
3.1
) and integration by parts, that gives

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{D}uu_{t}dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{D}[u\mathcal{L}_{p}u+u\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u_{t})+uf(u)]dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{D}[|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{t}]dx+2\int_{D}uf(u)dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle\geq-2\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+2\int_{D}\left[\alpha F(u)-\beta u^{p}-\alpha\gamma\right]dx

\displaystyle=2\alpha\left[-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u)-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{2(\alpha-p)}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx-2\beta\int_{D}u^{p}dx.

Next we apply Lemma
1.2
, which gives

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha\left[-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u)-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle+2\left[\frac{|N_{1}-p|^{p}}{(pR)^{p}}\frac{(\alpha-p)}{p}-\beta\right]\int_{D}u^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha\left[-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u)-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle=2\alpha\mathcal{F}(t),

with
\mathcal{F}(t)
as in (
3.6
), then
E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)
can be rewritten in the following form

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)\geq 2\alpha\mathcal{F}(0)+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}]dxd\tau.

(3.8)

Also we have for arbitrary
\delta>0
, in view of (
3.7
),

\displaystyle[E^{\prime}_{p}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}[u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx\right)^{2}.

Then by taking
\sigma=\delta=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1>0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)E_{p}(t)-(1+\sigma)[E^{\prime}_{p}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha M\mathcal{F}(0)+2\alpha\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}]dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx]d\tau\right)

\displaystyle-(1+\sigma)(1+\delta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau\right)^{2}-(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}[u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha M\mathcal{F}(0)-(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}[u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\alpha\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}]dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx]d\tau\right)\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[uu_{\tau}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}]dxd\tau\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2\alpha M\mathcal{F}(0)-(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}dx\right)^{2}.

Note that in the last line we have used the following inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dxd\tau\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}]dxd\tau\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[uu_{\tau}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}]dxd\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left[\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u_{\tau}^{2}d\tau dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{2}\geq 0,

where making use of the Hölder inequality and Cauchy-Schawrz inequality we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}[uu_{\tau}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}]dxd\tau\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u_{\tau}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx+\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u_{\tau}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}u_{\tau}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}_{\tau}d\tau dx\right)+\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau dx\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\left[\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}d\tau dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2}_{\tau}d\tau dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}d\tau dx\right)\left(\int_{D}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}d\tau dx\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By assumption
\mathcal{F}(0)>0
, thus we can select

M=\frac{(1+\sigma)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left(\int_{D}u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}dx\right)^{2}}{2\alpha\mathcal{F}(0)},

that gives

E^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t)E_{p}(t)-(1+\sigma)[E^{\prime}_{p}(t)]^{2}\geq 0.

(3.9)

We can see that the above expression for
t\geq 0
implies

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{E^{\prime}_{p}(t)}{E^{\sigma+1}_{p}(t)}\right]\geq 0\Rightarrow\begin{cases}E^{\prime}_{p}(t)\geq\left[\frac{E^{\prime}_{p}(0)}{E^{\sigma+1}_{p}(0)}\right]E^{1+\sigma}_{p}(t),\\
E_{p}(0)=M.\end{cases}

Then for
\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-1>0
, we arrive at

E_{p}(t)\geq\left(\frac{1}{M^{\sigma}}-\frac{\sigma\int_{D}[u_{0}^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx}{M^{\sigma+1}}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{\sigma}}.

Then the blow-up time
T^{*}
satisfies

0<T^{*}\leq\frac{M}{\sigma\int_{D}[u_{0}^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Global solution for the pseudo-parabolic equation

We now show that positive solutions, when they exist for some nonlinearities, can be controlled.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a stratified group with
N_{1}
being the dimension of the first stratum. Let
D\subset\mathbb{G}
be an admissible domain. Let
2\leq p<\infty
with
p\neq N_{1}
.

Assume that function
f
satisfies

\alpha F(u)\geq uf(u)+\beta u^{p}+\alpha\gamma,\,\,\,u>0,

(3.10)

where

F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds,

for some

\beta\geq\frac{(p-\alpha)}{2}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\alpha\leq 0,\,\,\gamma\geq 0.

(3.11)

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(D)\cap\mathring{S}^{1,p}(D)
satisfy

\mathcal{F}_{0}:=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}(x)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u_{0}(x))-\gamma)dx>0.

(3.12)

If
u
is a positive local solution of problem (
1.2
), then it is global and satisfies the following estimate:

\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dx\leq\exp({-(p-\alpha)t})\int_{D}[u^{2}_{0}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u_{0}|^{p}]dx.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t):=\int_{D}[u^{2}+\frac{2}{p}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}]dx.

Now we estimate
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)
by using assumption (
3.10
), that gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{D}uu_{t}dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{D}[u\mathcal{L}_{p}u+u\nabla_{H}\cdot(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u_{t})+uf(u)]dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{D}[|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{H}u\cdot\nabla_{H}u_{t}]dx+2\int_{D}uf(u)dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{D}(|\nabla_{H}u|^{p})_{t}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha\left[-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u)-\gamma)dx\right]-\frac{2(p-\alpha)}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx-2\beta\int_{D}u^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha\left[-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u)-\gamma)dx\right]

\displaystyle-(p-\alpha)[E_{p}(t)-\int_{D}u^{2}dx]dx-2\beta\int_{D}u^{2}dx,

\displaystyle=2\alpha\mathcal{F}(t)-(p-\alpha)\mathcal{E}(t)+[p-\alpha-2\beta]\int_{D}u^{2}dx,

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(t)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{D}|\nabla_{H}u(x,t)|^{p}dx+\int_{D}(F(u(x,t))-\gamma)dx

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}dxd\tau.

Since
\beta\geq\frac{p-\alpha}{2}
we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)+(p-\alpha)\mathcal{E}(t)\leq 2\alpha\left[\mathcal{F}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla_{H}u|^{p-2}|\nabla_{H}u_{\tau}|^{2}dxd\tau\right]\leq 0.

This implies,

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\exp({-(p-\alpha)t})\mathcal{E}(0),

finishing the proof.
∎

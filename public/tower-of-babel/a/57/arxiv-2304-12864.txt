Stability analysis of SIR and SIRS models with non monotone incidence function and various mortality rates
Y. Mohamed, A. Ahmedou, M. S. B. Elemine Vall

Abstract
This study uses the Lyapunov method, the Poincaré-Bendixson theorem, and the Dulac criterion to analyze the stability of SIR and SIRS with non-monotone incidence and different mortality rates.



Key Words :
SIR epidemic model, Non monotone incidence rate, Global stability,
Direct Lyapunov method, Dulac’s criterion, Poincaré Bendixson theorem.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification :
34D08, 34D23, 03H05.

Introduction

Mathematical models became important tools for analyzing the spread and control of infectious diseases. The incidence function is an essential component to study the spread of infectious disease. In general the incidence is represented as a linear function of the infection class, by a principle of mass action:

g_{1}(I)S=\beta IS.

In their study of cholera epidemic, Capasso and Serio

g_{2}(I)S=\frac{\beta IS}{1+\alpha I}

(1.2)

where
\displaystyle\beta I
measure the infection force of the disease and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha I}

In

g(I)S=\frac{\beta I^{p}S}{1+\alpha I^{q}}

If the the function

\displaystyle g(.)

\displaystyle I
is small and decreasing when
\displaystyle I

We considered in this paper a nonlinear and non-monotone incidence function of the previous form with
\displaystyle p=1

\displaystyle q=2

g(I)S=\frac{\beta IS}{1+\alpha I^{2}}

The global stability is an interesting classic question in the mathematical epidemiology models. Korobeinikov and Wake in
[
12
]
used a Lyapunov function of Volterra type to show the global stability of SIS, SIR and SIRS models with bi-linear incidence. Adda and Bichara have improved and completed Korobeinikov and Wake results by considering some SIR and SIRS models with bi-linear incidence and differential mortality rates
[
11
]
.
By using the Poincaré-Bendixson theorem, the Dulac criteria, and the Lyapunov function method to establish the conditions for global stability, Vargas-De-Leon has studied the global stability of the SIS, SIR, and SIRS models with standard incidence in
[
20
,
21
]
. More specifically, the author has construct a Lyapunov function with two components, one of quadratic type and the second of Volterra type.

In this paper we propose two methods to show the global stability of SIR and SIRS models with nonmontone incidence rate and with various mortality rates.
Our approach involves reducing the system to a planar one and then applying the Dulac criterion to eliminate periodic curves. To draw a conclusion, we also utilize the Poincaré-Bendixson theorem. Furthermore, we construct a Lyapunov function consisting of a quadratic term and a Volterra type term.

The paper is structured as follows: In the second section, we formulate the model and we calculate the basic reproduction number
\displaystyle R_{0}
(It is defined as the number of new cases of infection caused by an infected individual in a population susceptible
[

[

\displaystyle R_{0}<1
and it is globally asymptotically stable. Finally when
\displaystyle R_{0}>1
we show that the endemic equilibrium point is locally asymptotically stable and the global stability of endemic steady state using the Poincaré-Bendixson theorem, Dulac’s criterion and Lyapunov’s method in sections 5 and 6. In the section 7 we deduce the same previous results for a SIRS model. Finally, we conclude in section 8.

Model presentation

In this section we describe an SIR model with various mortality rate and non monotone incidence function by assuming that the incubation period is negligible.
Let
\displaystyle N(t)
be total population which is divided into three classes:
susceptible individuals
\displaystyle S(t)
, infected individuals
\displaystyle I(t)
, and individuals that have recovered/remove from the system
\displaystyle R(t)
. Based on this consideration, the total population is
\displaystyle N(t)=S(t)+I(t)+R(t)
.

We assume that all new born became susceptible and that the births recompense the deaths, that is,

b=\mu_{1}S+\mu_{2}I+\mu_{3}R.

(2.1)

which means the size of the population
\displaystyle N(t)
remains constant.

We model the contact by the following non monotone function:

g(I)=\frac{\beta I}{1+\alpha I^{2}}

with
\displaystyle\alpha>0
, the terms
\displaystyle\beta I
and
\displaystyle 1+\alpha I^{2}
represent respectively the force of the incidence and the inhibition effect.

We have the following scheme:
\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mu_{1}
\displaystyle b
\displaystyle I
\displaystyle\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}
\displaystyle\mu_{2}
\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\gamma
\displaystyle\mu_{3}

Through the previous scheme, a system of nonlinear differential equations is obtained and presented below.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{\prime}=b-\mu_{1}S-\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}.\\
I^{\prime}=\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I\\
R^{\prime}=\gamma I-\mu_{3}R.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Thanks to (
2.1
) the previous system can be write

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{\prime}=-\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}+\mu_{2}I+\mu_{3}R.\\
I^{\prime}=\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I.\\
R^{\prime}=\gamma I-\mu_{3}R.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

Since
\displaystyle S(t)+I(t)+R(t)=N
=Constant, the system
\displaystyle(\ref{b})
is equivalent to the following planar system :

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{\prime}=-\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}+\mu_{2}I+\mu_{3}(N-S-I).\\
I^{\prime}=\frac{\beta SI}{(1+\alpha I^{2})}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I.\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

For the simplicity of notations, we put
\displaystyle S=\frac{S}{N}
,
\displaystyle I=\frac{I}{N}
,
\displaystyle\beta=N\beta
and
\displaystyle\alpha=N^{2}\alpha

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{\prime}=\mu_{3}+(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})I-\mu_{3}S-\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}.\\
I^{\prime}=\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I.\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

with
\displaystyle S\geq 0,I\geq 0
such that
\displaystyle S+I\leq 1.

The biological domain of the previous system is the following standard compact positively invariant simplex given by

D=\left\{(S,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:S+I\leq 1\right\}

3
Local stability of the free-diseases equilibrium

It is very easy to show that the system
\displaystyle(\ref{dd})
admits a single free-diseases equilibrium point given by
\displaystyle x_{DFE}=(S_{0},0)=(1,0).

Furthermore, the basic reproduction number of the system
\displaystyle(\ref{dd})
is obtained by Diekmann et al. in
[
7
]
and Driessche and Watmough in
[
8
]
as follows

R_{0}=\frac{1}{\mu_{2}+\gamma}\times\frac{\partial g}{\partial I}(1,0)=\frac{\beta}{\mu_{2}+\gamma}.

This enables us to state the following local stability result.

Theorem 1

The free-diseases equilibrium point
\displaystyle x_{DFE}
is locally asymptotically stable if we assume that
\displaystyle R_{0}<1
and unstable elsewhere.

Proof.

This proof based on the direct calculation of eigenvalues of the Jacobin matrix of system, that is, the matrix defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle J(S,I)=\begin{bmatrix}-\mu_{3}-\frac{\beta I}{1+\alpha I^{2}}&(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})-\frac{\beta S(1-\alpha I^{2})}{(1+\alpha I^{2})^{2}}\\
\frac{\beta I}{1+\alpha I^{2}}&\frac{\beta S(1-\alpha I^{2})}{(1+\alpha I^{2})^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)\\
\end{bmatrix}

at the free-diseases equilibrium point, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle J(1,0)=\begin{bmatrix}-\mu_{3}&\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}-\beta\\
0&\beta-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)\\
\end{bmatrix}

Therefore the eigenvalues of this matrix are
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=-\mu_{3}<0
and
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=(\mu_{2}+\gamma)(R_{0}-1)
.

Then, if
\displaystyle R_{0}\geq 1
the second eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}
remains positive, which means that the free-diseases equilibrium point
\displaystyle x_{DFE}
is unstable and if
\displaystyle R_{0}<1
, the second eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}<0
then
\displaystyle x_{DFE}
is asymptotically stable.

\displaystyle\square

5
Local stability of the endemic equilibrium point

We look at the existence of endemic equilibrium point. This endemic equilibrium state satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0=\mu_{3}+(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})I-\mu_{3}S^{\ast}-\frac{\beta S^{\ast}I^{\ast}}{1+\alpha(I^{\ast})^{2}}.\\
0=\frac{\beta S^{\ast}I^{\ast}}{1+\alpha(I^{\ast})^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I^{\ast}.\\
\end{array}\right.

By solving the previous nonlinear system, we will have

x^{*}=(S^{\ast},I^{\ast})=\left(\frac{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}}{R_{0}},\quad\frac{-(\mu_{3}+\gamma)R_{0}+\sqrt{(\mu_{3}+\gamma)^{2}R_{0}^{2}+4\alpha\mu_{3}^{2}(R_{0}-1)}}{2\alpha\mu_{3}}\right).

It is easy to show that if
\displaystyle R_{0}>1,
then
\displaystyle S^{*}>0,I^{*}>0
and
\displaystyle S^{*}+I^{*}\leq 1
. Now, we are in position to quote the following local stability theorem.

Theorem 3

If
\displaystyle R_{0}>1
. Then, the endemic equilibrium point
\displaystyle x^{*}
is locally asymptotically stable.

Proof.

This proof is quite similar to the one in theorem
1
. We start by giving the Jacobin matrix of system (
2.5
) at the endemic equilibrium, that is ,

\displaystyle\displaystyle J(S^{\ast},I^{\ast})=\begin{bmatrix}-\mu_{3}-\frac{\beta I^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}}&(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})-\frac{\beta S^{*}(1-\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})}{(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})^{2}}\\
\frac{\beta I^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}}&\frac{\beta S^{*}(1-\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})}{(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)\\
\end{bmatrix}

Since at the endemic equilibrium point, we have

\frac{\beta S^{*}I^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}}=(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I^{*}

which yields

\mu_{2}+\gamma=\frac{\beta S^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}}

and

\frac{\beta S^{*}-\alpha\beta S^{*}{I^{*}}^{2}}{\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)=\frac{\beta S^{*}-\alpha\beta S^{*}{I^{*}}^{2}}{\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{\beta S^{*}\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{-2\alpha\beta S^{*}{I^{*}}^{2}}{\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)^{2}},

and because

\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}=\frac{\beta S^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}}-\frac{\mu_{3}}{I^{*}}(1-S^{*})

The Jacobain matrix
\displaystyle J(x^{*})
, can be write

\displaystyle\displaystyle J(x^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}-\mu_{3}-X&Y-\frac{\mu_{3}}{I^{*}}(1-S^{*})\\
X&-Y\\
\end{bmatrix}

where
\displaystyle X=\frac{\beta I^{*}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}},

\displaystyle Y=\frac{\alpha\beta S^{*}{I^{*}}^{2}}{2\left(1+\alpha{I^{*}}^{2}\right)^{2}}

and in consequences, we have

Tr(J(x^{*}))=-\mu_{3}-X-Y<0\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\det J(x^{*})=\mu_{3}Y+\frac{\mu_{3}}{I^{*}}(1-S^{*})X\geq 0

Thus, the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle(J(x^{*}))
have a negative real part, which means the endemic equilibrium
\displaystyle x^{*}
is locally asymptotically stable.

\displaystyle\square

6
Global stability of endemic equilibrium point

In this section, we propose two methods to prove the global stability result state in the following theorem.

6.1
Dulac’s criteria

Theorem 4

Assume that
\displaystyle R_{0}>1.
Then, the endemic equilibrium point is globally asymptotically stable.

Proof.

Consider the following planar system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S^{\prime}=\mu_{3}+(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})I-\mu_{3}S-\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}=f_{1}(S,I).\\
I^{\prime}=\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I=f_{2}(S,I).\\
\end{array}\right.

We employ the Dulac’s criterion to achieve this proof by taking the following Dulac function
\displaystyle B(S,I)=\frac{1+\alpha I^{2}}{\beta SI}
, we obtain

\frac{\partial Bf_{1}}{\partial S}(S,I)+\frac{\partial Bf_{2}}{\partial I}(S,I)=-\frac{\mu_{2}(1+\alpha I^{2})}{\beta S^{2}}-\frac{2\alpha I(\mu_{2}+\gamma)}{\beta S}+\frac{\mu_{3}(1+\alpha I^{2})}{\beta S^{2}}(1-\frac{1}{I})<0

Thus, the system (
2.5
) does not have a limit cycle in
\displaystyle D
.

In view of theorem
3

\displaystyle x^{*}
is locally asymptotically stable and since
\displaystyle D
is positively invariant set, then Poincaré-Bendixson theorem shows that
\displaystyle x^{*}
is globally asymptotically stable.

\displaystyle\square

6.2
Lyapunov method

Now, we are in position to state another proof of theorem
1
based on Lyapunov functions.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle V
be the Lyapunov function defined by

V(S,I)=\frac{1}{2}(S-S^{\ast}+I-I^{\ast})^{2}+\frac{2\mu_{3}+\gamma}{\beta}(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})\left(I-I^{\ast}-I^{\ast}\ln\left(\frac{I}{I^{\ast}}\right)\right).

Then, the time derivative of
\displaystyle V
may be write as

\displaystyle\displaystyle V^{\prime}(S,I)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(I^{\prime}+S^{\prime})(S-S^{\ast}+I-I^{\ast})+\frac{2\mu_{3}+\gamma}{\beta}(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})I^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{I^{\ast}}{I}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mu_{3}-\mu_{3}S-(\mu_{3}+\gamma)I)(S-S^{\ast}+I-I^{\ast})+\frac{2\mu_{3}+\gamma}{\beta}(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})\left(\frac{\beta SI}{1+\alpha I^{2}}-(\mu_{2}+\gamma)I)\right)\left(1-\frac{I^{\ast}}{I}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mu_{3}(S^{\ast}-S)-(\mu_{3}+\gamma)(I^{\ast}-I))(S-S^{\ast}+I-I^{\ast})+(2\mu_{3}+\gamma)(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})\left(\frac{S}{1+\alpha I^{2}}-\frac{S^{\ast}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}}\right)\left(I-I^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\mu_{3}(S-S^{\ast})^{2}-(\mu_{3}+\gamma)(I-I^{\ast})^{2}-(2\mu 3+\gamma)(I-I^{\ast})(S-S^{\ast})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(2\mu_{3}+\gamma)(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})\left[\frac{S-S^{\ast}}{1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha S({I^{\ast}}^{2}-I^{2})}{(1+\alpha I^{2})(1+\alpha{I^{\ast}}^{2})}\right](I-I^{\ast})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\mu_{3}(S-S^{\ast})^{2}-(\mu_{3}+\gamma)(I-I^{\ast})^{2}-\frac{(2\mu_{3}+\gamma)\alpha S(I^{\ast}+I)}{1+\alpha I^{2}}(I-I^{\ast})^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

Therefore,
\displaystyle V^{\prime}=0
if and only if
\displaystyle S=S^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle I=I^{\ast}

\displaystyle\{(S,I)\in D:V^{\prime}=0\}

\displaystyle x^{\ast}
.

Then, according to LaSalle principle in

13

14

the positive equilibrium state
\displaystyle x^{\ast}
is globally asymptotically stable in

\displaystyle\square

Conclusion

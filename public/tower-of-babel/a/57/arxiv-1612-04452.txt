Abstract
. In this paper we study Johnson-Schechtman inequalities for noncommutative martingales. More precisely, disjointification inequalities of noncommutative martingale difference sequences are proved in an arbitrary symmetric operator space E ⁡ ( ℳ ) E(\mathcal{M}) of a finite von Neumann algebra ℳ \mathcal{M} without making any assumption on the Boyd indices of E E . We show that we can obtain Johnson-Schechtman inequalities for arbitrary martingale difference sequences and that, in contrast with the classical case of independent random variables or the noncommutative case of freely independent random variables, the inequalities are one-sided except when E = L 2 ​ ( 0 , 1 ) E=L_{2}(0,1) . As an application, we partly resolve a problem stated by Randrianantoanina and Wu in [ 48 ] . We also show that we can obtain sharp Φ \Phi -moment analogues for Orlicz functions satisfying p p -convexity and q q -concavity for 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 1\leq p\leq 2 , q = 2 q=2 and p = 2 p=2 , 2 < q < ∞ 2<q<\infty . This is new even for the classical case. We also extend and strengthen the noncommutative Burkholder-Gundy inequalities in symmetric spaces and in the Φ \Phi -moment case.

2.1.
Singular value function

Let

Recall that

\mu(t,x)=\inf\{s>0:\tau(e_{(s,\infty)}(|x|))\leq t\},\quad t>0.

The function

is decreasing and right-continuous

21

. For the case that

is the abelian von Neumann algebra

[

37
]

2.2.
Symmetric operator spaces

A quasi-Banach function space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
on the interval
(0,\alpha)
,
0<\alpha\leq\infty
is called symmetric if for any
g\in E
and for any measurable function
f
with
\mu(f)\leq\mu(g)
, we have
f\in E
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq\|g\|_{E}.

For a given symmetric quasi-Banach space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
, we define the corresponding noncommutative space on
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
by setting

E({\mathcal{M}},\tau):=\{x\in L_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\mu(x)\in E\}.

Endowed with the quasi-norm
\|x\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}:=\|\mu(x)\|_{E},
the space
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is called the noncommutative symmetric space associated with
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
corresponding to the function space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
. It is shown in
[
51
]
that the quasi-norm space
(E({\mathcal{M}}),\|\cdot\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})})
is complete if
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
is complete. For every semifinite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{N}}
, the space
Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})
is well defined due to
[
34
]
.

The following useful construction can be found in
[
28
]
. If
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach space on the interval
(0,1)
, then the space
Z_{E}^{2}
consists of all measurable functions
f\in L_{1}(0,\infty)+L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
for which

\|f\|_{Z_{E}^{2}}:=\|\mu(f)\chi_{(0,1)}\|_{E}+\|f\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}<\infty.

It is obvious that the functional
\|\cdot\|_{Z_{E}^{2}}
is a quasi-norm on
Z_{E}^{2}.

For
f\in L_{1}(0,\infty)+L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
and
t>0,
it is well known that

(2.1)

\frac{1}{4}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\mu(t,f)dt+(\int_{1}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(t,f)dt)^{1/2}\Big)\\
\leq\|f\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mu(t,f)dt+\Big(\int_{1}^{\infty}\mu^{2}(t,f)dt\Big)^{1/2}.

Thus it is easy to see that

\|f\|_{Z_{E}^{2}}\approx\|\mu(f)\chi_{(0,1)}\|_{E}+\|f\chi_{[1,\infty)}\|_{L_{2}}.

In what follows, our other unexplained terminologies concerning symmetric function spaces are standard; see for example
[
37
]
for definitions of Boyd indices,

-convexity, etc.

2.4.
Orlicz functions and Orlicz spaces

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an Orlicz function, that is,
\Phi
is an even convex function such that
\Phi(0)=0
and
\Phi(\infty)=\infty.
Given an Orlicz function
\Phi
and an operator
x\in L_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, we have by
[
21
, Corollary 2.8]
,

\tau(\Phi(|x|))=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\mu(t,x)\big)dt.

Obviously, if
\Phi(t)=t^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
then this reduces to the usual
p
-moment of
|x|.
By induction, it follows from
[
21

that for every sequence
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n}\subset L_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
and scalars
\lambda_{i}\in(0,1)
with
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq 1

(2.3)

\tau\Big(\Phi\Big(|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}|\Big)\Big)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}\tau\Big(\Phi(|x_{i}|)\Big).

We now recall the definition of Orlicz spaces. Given an Orlicz function
\Phi
, the Orlicz function space
L_{\Phi}(0,\alpha)
,
0<\alpha\leq\infty
is the set of all measurable functions
f
on
(0,\alpha)
such that

\|f\|_{L_{\Phi}}:=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0,\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{|f(t)|}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 1{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\Big\}}.

The Banach space
(L_{\Phi}(0,\alpha),\|\cdot\|_{L_{\Phi}})
has the Fatou property (see
[
35

) and hence is a fully symmetric Banach space in the sense of
[
19
]
. An Orlicz function is called to satisfy
\Delta_{2}
-condition if there exists a constant

such that
\Phi(at)\lesssim_{a}\Phi(t)
for all

Note that if
\Phi
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then

if and only if
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(|f|)<\infty.

If
\Phi
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then by (
2.3
) we have

(2.4)

\tau(\Phi(x+y))\lesssim_{\Phi}\tau(\Phi(x))+\tau(\Phi(y)),\quad x,y\in L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}}).

Given
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, an Orlicz function
\Phi
is said to be
p
-convex if the function

is convex, and
\Phi
is said to be

is concave. If
\Phi
is
p
-convex and
q
-concave for

then the associated Orlicz space

is
p
-convex and is
q
-concave
[
31

, and hence the Boyd indices of
L_{\Phi}
satisfy

(see
[
37
]
or
[

, Lemma 4.9]
), which also implies that the standard Matuzewska-Orlicz indices of the Orlicz function
\Phi
satisfy

[

, Theorem 4.2]
. It also follows from

that the

-condition is equivalent to

In this section we establish disjointification inequalities for noncommutative martingale difference sequences in symmetric operator spaces. We first state several lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and let
(\mathcal{N},\nu)
be a semifinite atomless one. Suppose that
E\in Int(L_{1}(0,1),L_{2}(0,1))
is a symmetric quasi-Banach space. If
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is a linear contraction, then

\|Tx\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim_{E}\|x\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})},\quad\forall x\in Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}}).

Proof.

To see this, fix
x=x^{*}\in Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})
and consider the spectral projection of
|x|
associated with the interval
(\mu(1,x),\infty)
,

p:=e_{(\mu(1,x),\infty)}(|x|).

Clearly,
\nu(p)\leq 1.
Since
{\mathcal{N}}
is atomless, we can fix a projection
q\in{\mathcal{N}}
such that
q\geq p
and
\nu(q)=1.
By assumptions,
T:L_{1}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{2}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
is a contraction. Since
E
is an interpolation space between
L_{1}(0,1)
and
L_{2}(0,1),

it follows from
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
that
T:E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)\to E({\mathcal{M}})
is a bounded map. Since
\mu(t,qxq)=0
for all
t
greater than trace of
q,
we have

\mu(qxq)=\mu(qxq)\chi_{(0,1)}\leq\mu(x)\chi_{(0,1)}.

On the other hand,
1-q\leq 1-p
and therefore by (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\|qx(1-q)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(1-q)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}=\nu(x(1-q)x)\leq\nu(x(1-p)x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x(1-p)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}=\int_{\{\mu(x)\leq\mu(1,x)\}}\mu^{2}(t,x)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mu(x)\chi_{(1,\infty)}\|_{2}^{2}+\mu^{2}(1,x)\lesssim\|\mu(x)\|^{2}_{L_{1}+L_{2}}.

Similarly,

\|(1-q)x\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}\lesssim\|\mu(x)\|^{2}_{L_{1}+L_{2}}.

Consequently, taking into account that
\|\cdot\|_{E}\leq\|\cdot\|_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\|Tx\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T(qxq)\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+\|T(qx(1-q))\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+\|T((1-q)x)\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T(qxq)\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+\|T(qx(1-q))\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}+\|T((1-q)x)\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{E}

\displaystyle\|qxq\|_{E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)}+\|qx(1-q)\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}+\|(1-q)x\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mu(x)\chi_{(0,1)}\|_{E}+\|\mu(x)\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}\lesssim\|x\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}.

Hence,

\|Tx\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\leq\|T(\Re(x))\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+\|T(\Im(x))\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim_{E}\|x\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})},\quad\forall x\in Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}}).

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
E\in Int(L_{1}(0,1),L_{2}(0,1))
be a symmetric quasi-Banach space and let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset E({\mathcal{M}})
be a sequence of martingale differences. We have

\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim_{E}\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{M}
is atomless. Indeed, otherwise, we consider algebra
\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,1)
and a sequence
\{x_{k}\otimes 1\}_{k\geq 0}.

Consider the operator

T:\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\to\sum_{k\geq 0}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(x_{k})\Big),\quad\forall\,(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset(L_{1}+L_{2})({\mathcal{M}}).

We claim that
T
is a bounded map from
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})
to
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}}).
In fact
\{\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(x_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
is an orthogonal sequence and, therefore,

\|T(\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k})\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\|\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

We also claim that
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is bounded. Indeed, we have

\|T(\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k})\|_{1}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\|\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(x_{k})\|_{1}\leq 2\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\|_{1}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain that
T:Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})\to E({\mathcal{M}})
and, moreover,

\|Tx\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim_{E}\|x\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})},\quad x\in Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty}).

In particular, if
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset E({\mathcal{M}})
is a sequence of martingale differences, then

\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}=\|T(\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k})\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}\lesssim_{E}\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and let
(\mathcal{N},\nu)
be a semifinite atomless one. Let
E\in Int(L_{2}(0,1),L_{\infty}(0,1))
be a symmetric quasi-Banach space. If
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})
and
T:L_{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})
is a linear contraction which maps self-adjoint operators to self-adjoint ones, then

\|Tx\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}\lesssim_{E}\|x\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})},\quad x\in E({\mathcal{M}}).

Proof.

To see this, fix
x=x^{*}\in E({\mathcal{M}})
and consider the spectral projection of
|Tx|
associated with the interval
(\mu(1,Tx),\infty)
,

p:=e_{(\mu(1,Tx),\infty)}(|Tx|).

It is clear that
\nu(p)\leq 1.
Fix a projection
q\in N
such that
q\geq p
and
\nu(q)=1.
Consider the operator

S:z\to q\cdot Tz\cdot q,\quad z\in(L_{2}+L_{\infty})({\mathcal{M}}).

Using the assumption on
T
, we immediately obtain that
S:L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{2}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)
and
S:L_{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})\to L_{\infty}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)
are also contractions. Since
E
is an interpolation space between
L_{2}(0,1)
and
L_{\infty}(0,1),

it follows from
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
that
S:E({\mathcal{M}})\to E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)
is a bounded map.
Noting that
\mu(q\cdot Tx\cdot q)=\mu(q\cdot Tx\cdot q)\chi_{(0,1)}
and

\|(1-q)\cdot Tx\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq\|(1-p)\cdot Tx\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq\mu(1,Tx),

we have

\displaystyle\|Tx\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|q\cdot Tx\cdot q\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}+\|q\cdot Tx\cdot(1-q)\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}+\|(1-q)\cdot Tx\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Sx\|_{E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)}+\|q\cdot Tx\cdot q\|_{2}+\|q\cdot Tx\cdot(1-q)\|_{(L_{2}\cap L_{\infty})({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|(1-q)\cdot Tx\|_{(L_{2}\cap L_{\infty})({\mathcal{N}})}\leq\|Sx\|_{E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)}+3\|Tx\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\|(1-q)\cdot Tx\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})}\lesssim_{E}\|Sx\|_{E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)}+3\|Tx\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}+2\mu(1,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|Sx\|_{E(q{\mathcal{N}}q)}+5\|Tx\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}.

Thus,

\|Tx\|_{Z_{E}^{2}({\mathcal{N}})}\lesssim_{E}\|x\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+5\|x\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}\leq 6\|x\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})},\,\quad x=x^{*}\in E({\mathcal{M}}).

By splitting
x
into its real part and imaginary parts, we conclude the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|y_{k}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|y_{k}|^{2})\otimes e_{11}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}u_{k-1}(y_{k})^{*}u_{k-1}(y_{k})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}u_{k-1}(y_{k})\otimes e_{k1}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}.

Let
2\leq r<\infty.
Consider the mapping

S:L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))\to L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))
by setting

Sy=T(y\cdot(1\otimes e_{11})),\quad y=\sum_{k,j}y_{kj}\otimes e_{kj}\in L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})),

where

T:\sum_{k\geq 0}y_{k1}\otimes e_{k1}\to\sum_{k\geq 0}u_{k}(y_{k1})\otimes e_{k1}.

We claim that
S
is bounded from
L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))
into
L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})).

Indeed, by the dual Doob inequality
[
29
, Theorem 0.1]
we have

\displaystyle\|Sy\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}u_{k-1}(y_{k1})\otimes e_{k1}\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|y_{k1}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{r}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|y_{k1}|^{2})\big\|^{1/2}_{L_{\frac{r}{2}}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}|y_{k1}|^{2}\big\|^{1/2}_{L_{\frac{r}{2}}({\mathcal{M}})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}|y_{k1}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{r}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}y_{k1}\otimes e_{k1}\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}\leq\|y\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}.

Hence,
S
maps boundedly from
E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))
into
E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})).
Setting
y=\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k1},
we conclude that

\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}u_{k-1}(x_{k})\otimes e_{k1}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}\lesssim_{E}\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k1}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}))}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}|x_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})},

which is our desired inequality and the proof is complete.
∎

As noted in the introduction, Theorem

However, we have the following substitute, given in Theorem

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\Phi
be a
2
-convex and
q
-concave Orlicz function such that

(5.7)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{q}}=0,\quad 2<q<\infty.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite infinite von Neumann algebra and
x\in L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}}).

Then there exists a measurable function
x_{\Phi}
on
(0,\infty)
such that

\|x\|_{L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}})}\approx_{q}\|x\otimes x_{\Phi}\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})({\mathcal{M}}\otimes L_{\infty}(0,\infty))}.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
satisfy the assumptions. By
[
2
, Lemma 6]
, the function
\phi
defined by the setting
\Phi(t)=t^{2}\phi(t^{q-2})
is quasi-concave. By
[
35
, Theorem II.1.1]
, there exists a concave increasing function
\phi_{0}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\phi_{0}\leq\phi\leq\phi_{0}.
It follows from (
5.7
) that
\phi_{0}(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\phi_{0}(t)}{t}=0.
By
[
11
, Lemma 5.4.3]
, we have

\phi_{0}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{t,\tau\}d(-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\tau)).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{t^{q},\tau t^{2}\}d(-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\tau))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau=s^{2-q}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{t^{q},s^{2-q}t^{2}\}d(\phi_{0}^{\prime}(s^{2-q}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{(ts)^{q},(ts)^{2}\}d\nu(s)\approx_{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}N_{q}(ts)d\nu(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\Phi_{0}(t),

where the positive measure
d\nu(s)=s^{-q}d(\phi_{0}^{\prime}(s^{2-q}))
and the Orlicz function
N_{q}
is defined by the setting

N_{q}(t)=\begin{cases}2t^{q},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1\\
qt^{2}-q+2,\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

The equivalence
\Phi(t)\approx_{q}\Phi_{0}(t)
for all
t>0
guarantees that
L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}})=L_{\Phi_{0}}({\mathcal{M}}).
Now let
\|x\|_{L_{\Phi_{0}}({\mathcal{M}})}=\lambda.
It follows from
\mu(x\otimes y)=\mu(\mu(x)\otimes\mu(y))
that

\displaystyle 1=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}\Big(\frac{\mu(t,x)}{\lambda}\Big)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}N_{q}\Big(\frac{\mu(t,x)s}{\lambda}\Big)d\nu(s)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tau\otimes\int)\Big(N_{q}\Big(\frac{x\otimes x_{\Phi}}{\lambda}\Big)\Big),

where
x_{\Phi}
is a measurable function on
(0,\infty)
whose distribution is
\nu.
Now the well-known property of Orlicz norms (see e.g.
[
45
, I,1.2,Proposition 11]
) implies that

\|x\|_{L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}})}\approx_{q}\|x\|_{L_{\Phi_{0}}({\mathcal{M}})}=\|x\otimes x_{\Phi}\|_{L_{N_{q}}({\mathcal{M}}\otimes L_{\infty}(0,\infty))}.

One can easily show that
(L_{2}+L_{q})(0,\infty)=L_{N_{q}}(0,\infty)
, and we conclude this proof.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite infinite von Neumann algebra. Suppose that for every sequence

of martingale differences,

the inequality

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|x_{k}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|x_{k}^{*}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})(\mathcal{M})}

holds. If

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{E}

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes e_{k}\Big\|_{E({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|x_{k}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}+\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|x_{k}^{*}|^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{E({\mathcal{M}})}

Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset E(\mathcal{M})
be an arbitrary sequence of martingale differences. Let
\Phi
be as in Lemma
5.8
. Take
x_{\Phi}
on
(0,\infty)
from Lemma
5.8

This sequence consists of martingale differences with respect to the filtration

By Lemma

we have

(5.8)

\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}})}\approx_{q}\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\otimes x_{\Phi}\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,\infty))}.

Applying the assumption to the sequence

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}x_{k}\|_{L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q}

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k\geq 0}(x_{k}\otimes x_{\Phi})\otimes e_{k}\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,\infty)\bar{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}(\mathcal{E}_{k-1}\otimes 1)(|x_{k}|^{2}\otimes x_{\Phi}^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{(L_{2}+L_{q})({\mathcal{M}}\bar{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,\infty))}

6.
Disjointification inequalities: the
\Phi
-moment case

In this section, we prove
\Phi
-moment version of
disjointification
inequalities for noncommutative martingale difference sequences. The main result is Theorem
1.6
, which is new even in the classical case.

We consider the Orlicz function

(6.1)

M(t)=\begin{cases}t^{2},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1\\
2t-1,\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
and
({\mathcal{N}},\sigma)
be finite von Neumann algebras. If
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
is a contraction, then

\tau(M(|Tx|))\leq 4\sigma(M(|x|)).

Proof.

Split
x=x_{1}+x_{2},
where
x_{1}=xe_{[0,1]}(|x|)
and
x_{2}=xe_{(1,\infty)}(|x|).
By
[
38
, Theorem 3.3.3]
, we have

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mu(Tx)=\mu(Tx_{1}+Tx_{2})}\prec\prec\mu(Tx_{1})+\mu(Tx_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu(2Tx_{1})+\mu(2Tx_{2})\Big).

and, therefore,

\tau(M(|Tx|))\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbb{E}}\Big(M\Big(\frac{1}{2}\big(\mu(2Tx_{1})+\mu(2Tx_{2})\big)\Big)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tau(M(2|Tx_{1}|))+\tau(M(2|Tx_{2}|))\Big).

It is easy to see
M(2t)\leq 4M(t),

M(t)\leq 2t
and
M(t)\leq t^{2}
for every
t>0,
and hence we get

\tau(M(|Tx|))\leq 2\tau(M(|Tx_{1}|))+2\tau(M(|Tx_{2}|))\leq 2\tau(|Tx_{1}|^{2})+4\tau(|Tx_{2}|).

Since
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
is a contraction, it follows that

\tau(M(|Tx|))\leq 2\sigma(|x_{1}|^{2})+4\sigma(|x_{2}|)\leq 4{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sigma}(M(|x_{1}|))+4{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sigma}(M(|x_{2}|))=4\sigma(M(|x|)).

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and let
(\mathcal{N},\nu)
be an atomless semifinite one. If
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is a linear contraction, then for
M
given by (
6.1
) we have

\tau(M(|Tx|))\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{1}M(\mu(s,x))ds+M(\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}})\Big).

Proof.

To see this, fix
x=x^{*}\in L_{0}({\mathcal{N}})
and consider the spectral projection

p=e_{(\mu(1,x),\infty)}(|x|).

Clearly, trace of
p
is less than or equal to
1.
Fix a projection
q\geq p
whose trace equals
1.
Clearly,
T:L_{1}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{2}(q{\mathcal{N}}q)\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
is a contraction.
Again by
[
38
, Theorem 3.3.3]
, we have

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mu(Tx)}\prec\prec\mu(T(pxp))+\mu(T((1-p)xp))+\mu(T(x(1-p))).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tau(M(|Tx|))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbb{E}}\Big(M\Big(\mu(T(pxp))+\mu(T((1-p)xp))+\mu(T(x(1-p)))\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau(M(T(3|pxp|)))+\tau(M(3|T((1-p)xp)|))+\tau(M(3|T(x(1-p))|)).

Applying Lemma
6.1
to the finite algebras
\mathcal{M}
and
q\mathcal{N}q
and noting that
M(3t)\leq 9M(t)
for every
t>0
, we obtain that

\tau(M(T(3|pxp|)))\leq 4\sigma(M(3|pxp|))\leq 36\sigma(M(|pxp|))\leq 36\int_{0}^{1}M(\mu(s,x))ds.

Define concave function
M_{0}
by setting
M_{0}(t)=M(t^{1/2}),\,t>0.
It follows from Jensen inequality and the assumption that

\tau(M(|Tz|))=\tau(M_{0}(|Tz|^{2}))\leq M_{0}(\tau(|Tz|^{2}))=M_{0}(\|Tz\|_{2}^{2})=M(\|Tz\|_{2})\leq M(\|z\|_{2}).

Therefore,

\tau(M(3|T((1-p)xp)|))+\tau(M(3|T(x(1-p))|))\leq 2M(3\|x(1-p)\|_{2})\\
=2M(3\|\mu(x)\chi_{(1,\infty)}\|_{2})\lesssim 2M(3\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}})\leq 18M(\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}).

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and let
(\mathcal{N},\nu)
be a semifinite atomless one. Let
\Phi
be a
2
-concave Orlicz function. If
T:L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})
and
T:L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\to L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})
is a linear contraction, then

\tau(\Phi(|Tx|))\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(\mu(s,x))ds+\Phi(\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}).

Proof.

Define the function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
by setting
\Phi(t)=t\phi(t),

t>0.
Since
\Phi
is convex and
2
-concave, it follows that
\phi
is quasi-concave; see
[
2
, Lemma 6]
. By
[
35
, Theorem II.1.1]
, there exists a concave increasing function
\phi_{0}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\phi_{0}\leq\phi\leq\phi_{0}.
Without loss of generality,
\phi_{0}(0)=0
and
\phi_{0}(t)=o(t),

t\to\infty.
By
[
11
, Lemma 5.4.3]
, we have

\phi_{0}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\{t,\lambda\}d(-\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\lambda)).

Arguing as in Lemma
5.8
, we obtain that

\Phi(t)\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}M(\frac{t}{\lambda})d\nu(\lambda)

for some (positive) measure
\nu
on
(0,\infty).

Hence,

\tau(\Phi(|x|))\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\Big(M(\frac{|x|}{\lambda})\Big)d\nu(\lambda).

Therefore, by Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\tau(\Phi(|Tx|))

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\Big(M(\frac{|Tx|}{\lambda})\Big)d\nu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}M(\mu(s,\frac{x}{\lambda}))ds+M(\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}})\Big)d\nu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}M\Big(\mu(s,\frac{x}{\lambda})\Big)d\nu(\lambda)ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}M\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}\Big)d\nu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(\mu(s,x))ds+\Phi(\|x\|_{L_{1}+L_{2}}),

which is as our desired inequality and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Phi
be a
2
-convex Orlicz function. We have

(\tau\otimes\int)\Big(\Phi\Big(|\sum_{k\geq 0}y_{k}\otimes r_{k}|\Big)\Big)\geq\tau(\Phi((\sum_{k\geq 0}|y_{k}|^{2})^{1/2})).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we have finitely many (let us say,
n
) summands in the inequality above. Let

\mathcal{R}_{n}
be the set of all maps from
\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
into
\{-1,1\},
that is

\mathcal{R}_{n}=\Big\{\epsilon:\{0,\cdots,n-1\}\to\{-1,1\}\Big\}.

For every
\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n},
we set

T_{\epsilon}:=|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\epsilon(k)y_{k}|^{2}\quad{\rm and}\quad A_{\epsilon}:=\big\{t\in(0,1):r_{k}(t)=\epsilon(k),0\leq k\leq n-1\big\}.

It is clear that the Lebesgue measure of
A_{\epsilon}
is
2^{-n}.

Set
\Phi_{0}(t)=\Phi(t^{1/2}),

t>0.
Since
\Phi
is
2
-convex, it follows that
\Phi_{0}
is convex. Hence, we have

(6.2)

(\tau\otimes\int)\Big(\Phi\Big(|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{k}\otimes r_{k}|\Big)\Big)=\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}(\tau\otimes\int_{A_{\epsilon}})\Big(\Phi\Big(|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{k}\otimes r_{k}|\Big)\Big)=2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\tau(\Phi_{0}(T_{\epsilon})).

Applying (
2.3
), we obtain

(6.3)

2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\tau(\Phi_{0}(T_{\epsilon}))\geq\tau(\Phi_{0}(2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}T_{\varepsilon})).

Note that for
0\leq l,m\leq n-1,

\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\epsilon(l)\epsilon(m)=\begin{cases}2^{n},\quad l=m\\
0,\quad l\neq m.\end{cases}

It is immediate that

2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}T_{\varepsilon}=2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\epsilon(k)y_{k}|^{2}=2^{-n}\sum_{l,m=0}^{n-1}y_{l}^{*}y_{m}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\epsilon(l)\epsilon(m)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|y_{k}|^{2}.

Consequently,

(6.4)

\tau(\Phi_{0}(2^{-n}\sum_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}T_{\varepsilon}))=\tau(\Phi_{0}(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|y_{k}|^{2}))=\tau(\Phi((\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|y_{k}|^{2})^{1/2})).

Combining (
6.2
), (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\Phi
be an arbitrary Orlicz function. If
0\leq z_{k}\in L_{\Phi}({\mathcal{M}}),

k\geq 0,
then we have

\tau(\Phi(\sum_{k\geq 0}z_{k}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbb{E}}(\Phi(\mu(Z)\chi_{(0,1)}))+\Phi(\|Z\|_{1})\Big),\quad Z=\sum_{k\geq 0}z_{k}\otimes e_{k}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose that the spectral projection
e_{t}({z_{k}})=0
for every
t>0.
Set

z_{1k}=z_{k}e_{(\mu(1,Z),\infty)}(z_{k}),\quad z_{2k}=z_{k}e_{[0,\mu(1,Z)]}(z_{k}).

On integrable reductions of two-dimensional Toda-type lattices.
I.T.Habibullin, A.U.Sakieva

Abstract
The article considers lattices of the two-dimensional Toda type, which can be interpreted as dressing chains for spatially two-dimensional generalizations of equations of the class of nonlinear Schrödinger equations. The well-known example of this kind of generalization is the Davey-Stewartson equation. It turns out that the finite-field reductions of these lattices, obtained by imposing cutoff boundary conditions of an appropriate type, are Darboux integrable, i.e., they have complete sets of characteristic integrals. An algorithm for constructing complete sets of characteristic integrals of finite field systems using Lax pairs and Miura-type transformations is discussed.



Keywords:
integrability in sense of Darboux, generating function of integrals, Lax pairs, Liouville type equations, Volterra chain

2
Complete sets of characteristic integrals

Let us consider a system of the hyperbolic type equations of the form

u_{i,xy}=F_{i}(u_{1},\dots u_{N},u_{1,x},\dots u_{N,x},u_{1,y},\dots u_{N,y})\hskip 11.74988pti=1,2,\dots N,

(2.1)

where
F_{i}
are analytic functions defined in some region of the space
\textbf{С}^{3N}
.

We recall some basic definitions (see, for example,
[
14
]
). Smooth function of the form

I=I(u_{1},\dots u_{N},u_{1,x},\dots u_{N,x},u_{1,xx},\dots u_{N,xx},\dots,u_{1,r},\dots u_{N,r}),

where the notation is accepted
u_{j,s}:=\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial x^{s}}u_{j}
, is called a
y
-integral of the order
r
of the system (
2.1
), if the condition is satisfied
D_{y}I=0
, where the derivative with respect to the variable
y
is evaluated by means of the system (
2.1
). In other words to look for the
y
-integral we have a relation

D_{y}I=\sum_{i}(u_{i,y}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}+F_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i,x}}+D_{x}(F_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i,xx}}+\cdots)I=YI=0.

A set of
y
-integrals
\left\{I_{i}\right\}^{i=N}_{i=1}
having the orders
r_{i}
for the system of equations (
2.1
) constitutes a complete set of independent
y
-integrals, if the condition holds

\det\left(\frac{\partial I_{j}}{\partial u_{i,m}}\right)=\begin{vmatrix}\frac{\partial I_{1}}{\partial u_{1,r_{1}}}&\frac{\partial I_{1}}{\partial u_{2,r_{1}}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial I_{1}}{\partial u_{N,r_{1}}}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\frac{\partial I_{N}}{\partial u_{1,r_{N}}}&\frac{\partial I_{N}}{\partial u_{2,r_{N}}}&\ldots&\frac{\partial I_{N}}{\partial u_{N,r_{N}}}\end{vmatrix}\neq 0.

(2.2)

Let’s formulate an effective algebraic criterion (see
[
14
]
) for the existence of a complete set of integrals of the system (
2.1
).

Theorem 1
.
System of equations (
2.1
)
admits a complete set of independent
y
-integrals if and only if the characteristic Lie algebra over the ring of locally analytic functions of the variables
\bar{u}_{y},\bar{u},\bar{u}_{x},\bar{u}_{xx},\dots
, where
\bar{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})
, generated by the characteristic operators

X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j,y}},\,j=1,\dots N;\hskip 23.49976ptY=\sum_{i}u_{i,y}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}+F_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i,x}}+D_{x}(F_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i,xx}}+\cdots

is of finite dimension.

Integrals and characteristic algebra in the direction
x
are defined similarly.
As an illustrative example, we consider the well-known Liouville equation

u_{xy}=e^{u}.

(2.3)

Differentiating it with respect to
x
and using the equation (
2.3
) once again, we find
u_{xxy}=u_{x}u_{xy}
. Next, we integrate it over
y
and arrive at the relation

u_{xx}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}=h(x),

(2.4)

where
h(x)
is an arbitrary function. Similarly we have

u_{yy}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{y}=g(y).

(2.5)

Now it’s clear that the functions
I=u_{xx}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{x}
and
J=u_{yy}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}_{y}
are, respectively,
y-
and
x-
integrals. Due to the presence of the complete sets of integrals, the variables in the Liouville equation are separated in such a way that the problem of integrating the equation is reduced to finding a joint solution of two ordinary differential equations. From the relations (
2.4
), (
2.5
) it is easy to derive the formula for the general solution of the equation (
2.3
), found by Liouville:

u\left(x,y\right)=\ln\frac{2p^{\prime}(x)q^{\prime}(y)}{\left(p(x)+q(y)\right)^{2}},

where
p=p(x)
,
q=q(y)
are arbitrary functions, and the prime over the letter denotes the derivative.

Remark.
This example clearly indicates that the presence of Darboux-integrable reductions can be successfully used to construct explicit particular solutions of the original three-dimensional Toda-type chain by first finding a solution to the reduced system and then extending it to all values of the discrete variable (see
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
).

3
On the algorithm for constructing characteristic integrals by using the Lax pair.

In mathematical physics, the two-dimensional Toda lattice equation (
1.1
) is often used in the following form

u_{n,xy}=e^{u_{n+1}-{u_{n}}}-e^{u_{n}-u_{n-1}},

(3.1)

where the dependent variables are related as follows
h_{j}=\exp{(u_{j}-u_{j-1})}
. The lattice (
3.1
) is an integrable equation in the sense that it admits the Lax representation, i.e. expresses the necessary and sufficient condition for the compatibility of an overdetermined system of linear equations of the form
[
31
]

\psi_{n,x}=u_{n,x}\psi_{n}+\psi_{n+1},\hskip 11.74988pt\psi_{n,y}=-e^{u_{n}-u_{n-1}}\psi_{n-1}.

(3.2)

The Toda lattice (
3.1
) admits several types of finite field reductions that are Darboux integrable systems. Here we will focus on the reductions corresponding to the Cartan matrices of simple Lie algebras of the series A:

\displaystyle u_{1,xy}=e^{u_{2}-u_{1}},

\displaystyle u_{2,xy}=e^{u_{3}-u_{2}}-e^{u_{2}-u_{1}},

\displaystyle.........................................

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{N-1,xy}=e^{u_{N}-u_{N-1}}-e^{u_{N-1}-u_{N-2}},

\displaystyle u_{N,xy}=-e^{u_{N}-u_{N-1}}.

These reductions are obtained from (
3.1
) by imposing the termination conditions
u_{0}=\infty
and
u_{N+1}=-\infty
.

It is well known that the system (
3.3
) admits complete sets of characteristic integrals in both characteristic directions
x
and
y
(see
[
22
]
). In the mentioned work S.V. Smirnov found the generating functions of characteristic integrals of generalized Toda lattices corresponding to simple Lie algebras of the series
A
,
B
and
C
, using the properties of a sequence of the Laplace transformations of a linear partial differential equation of hyperbolic type and thereby proved the Darboux integrability of these systems. Below, in this section, we slightly modify the scheme suggested in
[
22
]
, focusing on the Lax pair in order to then apply it to a more general situation that does not have a direct connection with iterations of the Laplace transformation.

The Lax pair for the system (
3.3
) is easily derived from the system (
3.2
). The first Lax equation has the form of a system of ODEs in
x

\displaystyle\psi_{1,x}=u_{1,x}\psi_{1}+\psi_{2},

\displaystyle\psi_{2,x}=u_{2,x}\psi_{2}+\psi_{3},

\displaystyle.........................................

(3.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{N-1,x}=u_{N-1,x}\psi_{N-1}+\psi_{N},

\displaystyle\psi_{N,x}=u_{N,x}\psi_{N}.

The second Lax equation is defined similarly

\displaystyle\psi_{1,y}=0,

\displaystyle\psi_{2,y}=-e^{u_{2}-u_{1}}\psi_{1},

\displaystyle.........................................

(3.5)

\displaystyle\psi_{N-1,y}=-e^{u_{N-1}-u_{N-2}}\psi_{N-2},

\displaystyle\psi_{N,y}=-e^{u_{N}-u_{N-1}}\psi_{N-1}.

The system of equations (
3.4
) obviously implies the relation

\psi_{N}=(D_{x}-u_{N-1,x})(D_{x}-u_{N-2,x})\dots(D_{x}-u_{2,x})(D_{x}-u_{1,x})\psi_{1}.

(3.6)

Hence due to the fact that
(D_{x}-u_{N,x})\psi_{N}=0
the function
\psi_{1}
is a joint solution of two ordinary differential equations

B\psi_{1}=0,\hskip 11.74988pt\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\psi_{1}=0,

(3.7)

where

B=(D_{x}-u_{N,x})(D_{x}-u_{N-1,x})\dots(D_{x}-u_{2,x})(D_{x}-u_{1,x}).

(3.8)

Let’s open the brackets and rewrite operator
B
as a polynomial in
D_{x}

B=D_{x}^{N}+\alpha_{1}D_{x}^{N-1}+\dots+\alpha_{N}.

(3.9)

We apply the operator
D_{y}
to both sides of the first equation in (
3.7
) and by virtue of the conditions
\forall j\geq 0\hskip 11.74988pt\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{j+1}}{{\partial x^{j}}{\partial y}}\psi_{1}=0}
we obtain the following relation

D_{y}(\alpha_{1})D_{x}^{N-1}(\psi_{1})+D_{y}(\alpha_{2})D_{x}^{N-2}(\psi_{1})+\dots+D_{y}(\alpha_{N})\psi_{1}=0.

(3.10)

Note that the functions
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots\psi_{N})
correspond to an arbitrary solution of the system of equations (
3.4
) with fixed coefficients and that’s why they can be considered as independent variables. On the other hand side, a set of the variables

(\psi_{1}
,
D_{x}^{N-1}(\psi_{1})
,…,
D_{x}^{N-1}(\psi_{1}))
is related with the above mentioned set of variables
(\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2}
,…,
\psi_{N})
by some non-degenerate linear transformation following from the system (
3.4
). For example,
\psi_{1,x}=u_{1,x}\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{1xx}=(u_{1xx}+u_{1x}^{2})\psi_{1}+(u_{1x}+u_{2x})\psi_{2}+\psi_{3}
, etc. Thus it is also a linearly independent set. Consequently, from the equality (

) we have

for any

. It shows that the functions

are

-integrals of the nonlinear system (

). In

it was proven that the functions

form a complete set of independent

-integrals of the system (

). Due to the invariance of the system under the involution

\displaystyle u_{1,xy}=u_{1,x}u_{1,y}\left(\frac{1}{u_{1}-c_{0}}-\frac{1}{u_{2}-u_{1}}\right),

(4.2)

It was proved in

]

To begin with, we note that the first-order integral can be easily derived from elementary considerations. Indeed, let us rewrite the system in the form:

\displaystyle\frac{u_{1,xy}}{u_{1,x}}=u_{1,y}\left(\frac{1}{u_{1}-c_{0}}-\frac{1}{u_{2}-u_{1}}\right),

D_{y}\log(u_{1x}u_{2x}\dots u_{mx})=D_{y}\log(g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{m+1}).

It obviously follows from this fact that the function

J=u_{1x}u_{2x}\dots u_{mx}(g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{m+1})

, …

,

r_{2}=\left(\log\frac{v_{1}-\epsilon}{A_{1}}\right)_{x}-B_{2}=\frac{v_{1,x}}{v_{1}-\epsilon}-\frac{v_{1,xx}+2v_{1}v_{1,x}}{v_{1,x}+v^{2}_{1}-1}+\frac{v_{2,x}+v_{2}\delta-1}{\delta-v_{2}}+\frac{v_{2,x}-v_{1,x}}{v_{2}-v_{1}}.

(5.26)

The integral
\beta_{1}=I
is easily evaluated:

I=r_{1}+r_{2}=\frac{2v_{1,x}}{v_{1}-\epsilon}-\frac{v_{1,xx}+2v_{1}v_{1,x}}{v_{1,x}+v^{2}_{1}-1}+\frac{v_{2,x}+v_{1}v_{2}-1}{v_{2}-v_{1}}+\frac{v_{2,x}+v_{2}\delta-1}{\delta-v_{2}}.

Due to the Lemma 1 we have the integral
\beta_{2}=J
(evidently it is non-autonomous for
\epsilon\neq-\delta
):

J=\frac{\left(v_{1,x}+v^{2}_{1}-1\right)\left(v_{2,x}+v^{2}_{2}-1\right)}{\left(v_{1}-\epsilon\right)\left(v_{2}-v_{1}\right)\left(v_{2}-\delta\right)}e^{-\left(\epsilon+\delta\right)y}.

(5.27)

These two functions
I
and
J
constitute the complete set of the
y
-integrals for the system (
5.23
).

Division properties of commuting polynomials
Kimiko Hasegawa and Rin Sugiyama

Abstract
. Polynomials commute under composition are referred to as commuting polynomials . In this paper, we study division properties for commuting polynomials with rational (and integer) coefficients. As a consequence, we show an algebraic particularity of the commuting polynomials coming from weighted sums for cycle graphs with pendant edges ( [ 2 ] ).

1.
Introduction

Polynomials commute under composition are referred to as
commuting polynomials
.
A set of pairwise commuting polynomials containing one polynomial of each
positive degree is called a
chain
(Definition
1.1
).
The most easy and important example is the monic monomials
\{x^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Another important example is given by the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(Definition
2.1
).

Conjugation by a linear polynomial defines an equivalence relation called
similarity
.
The similarity plays a key role to study commuting polynomials and chains.
In 1950s, Block and Theilman
[
1
]
, Jacobstahl
[
4
]
independently proved the classification theorem for chains (Theorem
1.4
), which states that up to similarity there are only two chains; one is the monic monomials.
The other is the Chebyshev polynomials.

Recently, Fujita-Hasegawa-Inaba-Kondo
[
2
]
gave two formulas for the weighted sums for cycle graphs with pendant edges, which use the following polynomials

(1.1)

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)=2T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}+1\right)-2,\qquad\tilde{F}_{n}(x)=(x+1)^{n}-1.

By direct computations, we easily see that the two sets
\{F_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\tilde{F}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are also chains and similar to
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
respectively.
Furthermore, the factorization of
F_{n}(x)
in
[
2
]
for some small
n
suggests a general form of factorization.

Our motivation comes from particular algebraic properties of
F_{n}(x)
and
\tilde{F}_{n}(x)
, and we will show its particularity in all chains.

We now give a more precise description of our work.

1.1.
Commuting polynomials

We recall several notions from
[
8
]
(
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
).
Let
A
be an integral domain containing
\mathbb{Z}
and
K
be the quotient field of
A
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
chain
in
A[x]
is a set of polynomials
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset A[x]
which satisfies deg
(f_{n}(x))=n
and any two polynomials of the set commute with each other.

Remark 1.2
.

A chain of commuting polynomials is called an entire set of commutative polynomials in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
f(x),g(x)\in K[x]
.
Then
f(x)
is
similar
to
g(x)
if there exists
\lambda(x)=ax+b\in K[x]\ (a\neq 0)
such that
g(x)=\lambda^{-1}(f(\lambda(x)))
.

Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{g_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be chains in
K[x]
.
Then
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
similar
to
\{g_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if and only if there exists
\lambda(x)=ax+b\in K[x]\ (a\neq 0)
such that
g_{n}(x)=\lambda^{-1}(f_{n}(\lambda(x)))
for any
n
.

The following is the classification theorem for chains.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a chain in
K[x]
.
Then
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is similar to only one of the two
\{x^{n}\}
or
\{T_{n}(x)\}
.

From Theorem
1.4
, we use the following terminology in this paper.

Definition 1.5
.

A chain is said to be of
monomial type
(resp.
Chebyshev type
) if it is similar to
\{x^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(resp.
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
).

By the definition of similarity, a chain
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Chebyshev (resp. monomial) type in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
is presented as follows; let
\lambda(x)=ax+b\ (a,b\in\mathbb{Q},a\neq 0)
,

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}(T_{n}(\lambda(x)))=\frac{1}{a}(T_{n}(ax+b)-b)

\displaystyle\Bigl(\text{resp. }f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}(\lambda(x)^{n})=\frac{1}{a}((ax+b)^{n}-b)\Bigr)

1.2.
Results

We discuss the following conditions for chains of Chebyshev (resp. monomial) type in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
separately.

Definition 1.6
.

We define conditions (i)–(iv) for a chain
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
as follows;

Condition (i):

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}(x)
is a monic polynomial

Condition (ii):

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]

Condition (iii):

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N},

m\mid n\Leftrightarrow f_{m}(x)\mid f_{n}(x)
in
\mathbb{Q}[x]

Condition (iv):

\gcd(f_{m}(x),f_{n}(x))=f_{\gcd(m,n)}(x)

We interpret Conditions (i)–(iii) in terms of
\lambda(x)=ax+b
, i.e., conditions on
a
and
b
.
In particular, for
Condition (iii):
, we first consider the Euclidean division of
f_{n}(x)
by
f_{m}(x)
and give a description of the quotient and the remainder (Proposition
2.6
and Proposition
3.2
).
Using the description of the remainder, we discuss the condition on
a,b
corresponding to
Condition (iii):
(Corollary
2.8
and Corollary
3.3
).
For
Condition (iv):
, we first give the factorization in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
(Theorem
2.13
) and using it we discuss a property on the greatest common divisor (Corollary
2.14
and Corollary
3.4
).

As a consequence of results in this paper, we have the following corollary which shows an algebraic particularity of the two chains
\{F_{n}(x)\}
and
\{\tilde{F}_{n}(x)\}
of (
1.1
) in all chains.

Corollary 1.7

(Corollary
4.1
)
.

Suppose a chain
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies all of
Condition (i):
,
Condition (ii):
and
Condition (iii):
.
Then we have

f_{n}(x)=F_{n}(x)=2T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}+1\right)-2\quad\text{or}\quad f_{n}(x)=\tilde{F}_{n}(x)=(x+1)^{n}-1.

Additionally, in this case
Condition (iv):
is also satisfied.

Remark 1.8
.

For any sequence of polynomials, we can ask wether Conditions (i)–(iv) hold or not.
For example, Webb and Parberry
[
6
]
proved that Fibonacci polynomials
\{U_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy Conditions (ii)–(iv).
Condition (i) holds except
U_{0}=0,U_{1}=1
.
But
\{U_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not chain.
It is natural to ask how much Condition (i)–(iv) characterize a sequence
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of polynomials with
\deg f_{n}(x)=n
but not a chain.

2.
Chains of Chebyshev type

We first recall the definition and some properties of Chebyshev polynomials from
[

.
From §2.2, we discuss conditions in Definition

2.1.
Chebyshev polynomials

Definition 2.1
.

The
Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind

T_{n}(x)
is defined by the following recurrence relation;

(2.1)

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)=1,\quad T_{1}(x)=x,\quad T_{n}(x)=2xT_{n-1}(x)-T_{n-2}(x)\ (n\geq 2).

The
Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind

U_{n}(x)
is defined by the following recurrence relation;

\displaystyle U_{0}(x)=1,\quad U_{1}(x)=2x,\quad U_{n}(x)=2xU_{n-1}(x)-U_{n-2}(x)\ (n\geq 2).

Remark 2.2
.

The Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
and
U_{n}(x)
also may be defined as follows;

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=\cos(n\arccos x),\qquad U_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}T^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=\frac{\sin((n+1)\arccos x)}{\sin(\arccos x)}

Proposition 2.3

(
[
5
, p.6, Property 2]
)
.

For any
m,n\,(m\leq n)
, the quotient
q_{m,n}(x)
and the remainder
r_{m,n}(x)
of the Euclidean division of
T_{n}(x)
by
T_{m}(x)
are given by

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(x)=2\sum_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{i}T_{n-(2i-1)m}(x),\quad r_{m,n}(x)=(-1)^{l}T_{|n-2lm|}(x),

if there is an integer
l\geq 1
satisfying
(2l-1)m<n\leq 2lm
, otherwise

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(x)=2\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(-1)^{i}T_{n-(2i-1)m}(x)+(-1)^{l-1},\quad r_{m,n}(x)=0,

where
l
satisfies
n=(2l-1)m
.

2.2.
Monic and integer coefficient conditions

We discuss conditions in Definition
1.6
for a chain
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Chebyshev type in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
;

f_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{a}(T_{n}(ax+b)-b)\qquad(a,b\in\mathbb{Q},a\neq 0).

We prepare a lemma about the recurrence relation for
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a chain of Chebyshev type in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
.
Then
f_{n}(x)
has the following recurrence relation for
n\geq 3

f_{n}(x)=2(ax+b)\cdot f_{n-1}(x)-f_{n-2}(x)+2bx+\frac{2b(b-1)}{a}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}T_{n}(ax+b)-\frac{b}{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}(2(ax+b)T_{n-1}(ax+b)-T_{n-2}(ax+b))-\frac{b}{a}

\displaystyle=2(ax+b)f_{n-1}(x)+\frac{2b(ax+b)}{a}-f_{n-2}(x)-\frac{2b}{2}

\displaystyle=2(ax+b)\cdot f_{n-1}(x)-f_{n-2}(x)+2bx+\frac{2b(b-1)}{a}.

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a chain of Chebyshev type in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
, that is,
f_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{a}(T_{n}(ax+b)-b)
.
Then we have

(1)

Condition (i):
holds if and only if
a=\frac{1}{2}
.

(2)

Condition (ii):
holds if and only if
a\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\ \text{and}\ b-1\in a\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(1)
It is clear because the leading coefficient of
T_{n}(x)
is
2^{n-1}
.

(2)
Since
f_{2}(x)=2ax^{2}+4bx+\frac{(2b+1)(b-1)}{a}
and
f_{3}(x)=4a^{2}x^{3}+12abx^{2}+3(4b^{2}-1)x+\frac{4(b^{3}-b)}{a}
, by an elementary computation, we have

\displaystyle f_{2}(x),f_{3}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\ \iff\ a\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\ \text{and}\ b-1\in a\mathbb{Z}.

In this case, we also have

2(ax+b),\ 2bx+\frac{2b(b-1)}{a}\in\mathbb{Z}[x].

From the recurrence relation (Lemma
2.4
)

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=2(ax+b)\cdot f_{n-1}(x)-f_{n-2}(x)+2bx+\frac{2b(b-1)}{a},

the desired statement is obtained by induction on
n
.
∎

2.3.

Proposition 2.6
.

For

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{a}(b(c_{k}-1)-c_{k-1})&\text{if}\ \ n=km\\

\displaystyle d_{m,n}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2b\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}-c_{k}&\text{if}\ \ n=km\\
\displaystyle 2b\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}&\text{if}\ \ m\nmid n\end{cases}

\displaystyle R_{m,n}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}b(c_{k}-1)-c_{k-1}&\text{if}\ \ n=km\\
c_{k+1}g_{n-km}(x)-c_{k}g_{(k+1)m-n}(x)+b(c_{k+1}-c_{k}-1)&\text{if}\ \ m\nmid n.\end{cases}

The statement for

We assume that

2.4.
Greatest common divisor and Factorization

From Proposition
2.5
, a chain of monic polynomials of the Chebyshev type in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
is given by

\left\{2T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}+b\right)-2b\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\quad\left(b\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\right).

We first describe all roots of
T_{n}(\frac{x}{2})-b
and give the factorization of
2T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}+b\right)-2b
for
b=0,\pm 1,\pm\frac{1}{2}
.
Using these, we show a property for the greatest common divisor in case of Chebyshev type.

Lemma 2.10
.

For any
n>0
, we have

T_{n}\left(\dfrac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)=\dfrac{t^{n}+t^{-n}}{2}.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
n
.
For
n=1,2
, we have

T_{1}\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)=\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2},\quad T_{2}\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)=2\cdot\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)^{2}-1=\frac{t^{2}+t^{-2}}{2}.

For
n\geq 3
, by the recurrence relation of
T_{n}(x)
and the induction hypothesis, we obtain that

\displaystyle T_{n}\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)T_{n-1}\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)-T_{n-2}\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(t+t^{-1})\cdot\frac{t^{n-1}+t^{-(n-1)}}{2}-\frac{t^{n-2}+t^{-(n-2)}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{n}+t^{-n}}{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

For

, the roots of
T_{n}(\frac{x}{2})-b
are given as follows;

(1)

in case

Here

In particular, for

\pm 2

(2)

(3)

in case

Proof.

We first prove (1).
Put

.
Then we have

T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)-b=T_{n}(\cos t)-b=\cos(nt)-\cos\theta_{b}=0.

Solving the last equation about

t=\pm\frac{\theta_{b}+2i\pi}{n}\quad(i=0,\dots,n-1)

and thus the roots are given by

Furthermore, since
\theta_{1}=0
and

, the roots for

and

On the other hand, we have

\frac{d}{dx}\left(T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)-b\right)=\frac{n}{2}U_{n-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

Since it is know that the roots of

are given by

\cos\frac{i\pi}{n}\quad(i=1,\dots,n-1),

we obtain that for

except

when

\frac{d}{dx}\left(T_{n}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)-1\right)\Big|_{x=2\cos(\frac{2i\pi}{n})}=\frac{n}{2}U_{n-1}\left(\cos\frac{2i\pi}{n}\right)=0

and for

Hence in cases
b=\pm 1
the roots not equal to

are multiple roots, and the proof for (1) completes.

We next put
x=t+t^{-1}

2.10

Solving the last equation about

t=\begin{cases}\sqrt[n]{b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-1}}\zeta_{n}^{i}&(b>1)\\
\sqrt[n]{|b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-1}|}\zeta_{2n}^{2i+1}&(b<-1)\end{cases}\quad(i=0,\dots,n-1)

Theorem 2.13

(5)

Condition (i):

(2)

3.2.

Proposition 3.2
.

For

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(x)=a\sum_{i=1}^{k}b^{i-1}f_{n-im}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}b^{i},\quad r_{m,n}(x)=b^{k}f_{n-km}(x)+\frac{b^{k+1}-b}{a},

of the Euclidean division of

by
g_{m}(x)
.

3.3.

Corollary 3.4

For any positive integers

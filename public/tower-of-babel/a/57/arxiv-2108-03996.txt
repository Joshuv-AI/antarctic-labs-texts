Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the first and third cohomology groups on Leibniz triple systems, which can be applied to extension theory and 1 1 -parameter formal deformation theory. Specifically, we investigate the central extension theory for Leibniz triple systems and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalent classes of central extensions of Leibniz triple systems and the third cohomology group. We study the T ∗ T^{*} -extension of a Leibniz triple system and we determined that every even-dimensional quadratic Leibniz triple system ( 𝔏 , B ) (\mathfrak{L},B) is isomorphic to a T ∗ T^{*} -extension of a Leibniz triple system under a suitable condition. We also give a necessary and sufficient condition for a quadratic Leibniz triple system to admit a symplectic form. At last, we develop the 1 1 -parameter formal deformation theory of Leibniz triple systems and prove that it is governed by the cohomology groups.

2.
Cohomology of Leibniz triple systems

In this section, we first recall some basic definitions and give the dual modules of Leibniz triple systems. Then we will give the first and third cohomology group of Leibniz triple systems.

Definition 2.1
.

[
4
]

A Leibniz triple system is a vector space
\mathfrak{L}
endowed with a trilinear operation
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{L}
satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}-\{\{a,b,d\},c,e\}-\{\{a,b,e\},c,d\}+\{\{a,b,e\},d,c\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\{a,\{b,c,d\},e\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}-\{\{a,c,b\},d,e\}-\{\{a,d,b\},c,e\}+\{\{a,d,c\},b,e\},

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathfrak{L}
.

Note that a Leibniz triple system can be given by a Lie triple system with the same ternary product. A Leibniz algebra
L
with product
[\cdot,\cdot]
becomes a Leibniz triple system when
\{x,y,z\}:=[[x,y],z],
for all
x,y,z\in L.
More examples refer to
[
4
]
. Denote by End
(\mathfrak{L})
the set consisting of all linear maps on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
1
]

Let
I
be a subspace of a Leibniz triple system
\mathfrak{L}.
Then
I
is called a subsystem of
\mathfrak{L},
if
\{I,I,I\}\subset I;

I
is called an ideal of
\mathfrak{L},
if
\{I,\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}\}+\{\mathfrak{L},I,\mathfrak{L}\}+\{\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L},I\}\subset I.
Moreover, if
\{I,I,\mathfrak{L}\}=\{I,\mathfrak{L},I\}=\{\mathfrak{L},I,I\}=0,
then

is called an abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{L}.

Definition 2.3
.

[

Let

-module, if

(2)

\{a,b,c\}\in V

4.1.

In this subsection, we will introduce the quadratic Leibniz triple system

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Leibniz triple system. A bilinear form

\mathfrak{L}
is said to be symmetric if

x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
.

\mathfrak{L}^{\bot}=0,
where
\mathfrak{L}^{\bot}=\{x\in\mathfrak{L}~|~B(x,y)=B(y,x)=0,\forall y\in\mathfrak{L}\}.

For any

denote by
R_{(x,y)},

M_{(x,y)}
and
L_{(x,y)}
the right, middle and left multiplication operator on
\mathfrak{L},
respectively, i.e.,
R_{(x,y)},~M_{(x,y)},~L_{(x,y)}:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow

(\mathfrak{L})
are defined as
R_{(x,y)}z:=\{z,x,y\},

M_{(x,y)}z:=\{x,z,y\}
and
L_{(x,y)}z:=\{x,y,z\},
which can be used to characterize the invariance of

Definition 4.2
.

A symmetric bilinear form
B
on a Leibniz triple system
\mathfrak{L}
is said to be right-invariant
(
resp. left-invariant or middle-invariant
)
if

\displaystyle B(R_{(a,b)}x,y)

\displaystyle=B(x,R_{(b,a)}y)

\displaystyle\Big({\rm resp}.~B(L_{(a,b)}x,y)=B(x,L_{(b,a)}y),~

\displaystyle{\rm or}~B(M_{(a,b)}x,y)=B(x,M_{(b,a)}y)\Big),

for all

.

Remark 4.3
.

If

is symmetric then any two invariance of

lead to the third invariance. Here, we only check that the middle-invariant can be deduced by the right-invariant and left-invariant as an example, which follows from for all

\displaystyle B(M_{(a,b)}x,y)=

\displaystyle B(L_{(a,x)}b,y)=B(b,L_{(x,a)}y)=B(b,R_{(a,y)}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(R_{(y,a)}b,x)=B(M_{(b,a)}y,x)=B(x,M_{(b,a)}y).

4.3.
Symplectic form of a Leibniz triple system

In this subsection, we will introduce the symplectic form of a Leibniz triple system, and we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a quadratic Leibniz triple system to admit a symplectic form.

Definition 4.17
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Leibniz triple system. A symplectic form is a non-degenerate skewsymmetric bilinear form
\omega
satisfying

(4.6)

\displaystyle\omega(u,\{x,y,z\})+\omega(x,\{u,z,y\})+\omega(y,\{z,u,x\})+\omega(z,\{y,x,u\})=0,

for all
x,y,z,u\in\mathfrak{L}.
A Leibniz triple system endowed with a symplectic form is called symplectic Leibniz triple system, denoted by
(\mathfrak{L},\omega).

Definition 4.18
.

A symmetric
(
resp. skewsymmetric
)
linear map
f
on
\mathfrak{L}
with respect to
B
satisfying
B(f(x),y)=B(x,f(y))

({\rm resp}.~B(f(x),y)=-B(x,f(y))),
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}.

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},B)
be a quadratic Leibniz triple system. There is a symplectic form
\omega
on
\mathfrak{L}
if and only if there is an invertible derivation of
\mathfrak{L}
which is skewsymmetric with respect to

Proof.

For the sufficiency, let

be an invertible and skewsymmetric derivation on
\mathfrak{L}
with respect to
B.
Define
\omega(x,y)=B(D(x),y).
In the following we prove that
\omega
is skewsymmetric, non-degenerate and satisfies Eq. (
4.6
). Since

\omega(x,y)=B(D(x),y)=-B(x,D(y))=-B(D(y),x)=-\omega(y,x),

and
D
is invertible,
B
is non-degenerate, we have
\omega
is skewsymmetric and non-degenerate. Furthermore, by the skewsymmetric of
D
with respect to
B
and the invariance of

\displaystyle\omega(u,\{x,y,z\})=

\displaystyle B(D(u),\{x,y,z\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(u,D(\{x,y,z\}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(u,\{D(x),y,z\})-B(u,\{x,D(y),z\})-B(u,\{x,y,D(z)\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(\{u,z,y\},D(x))-B(\{z,u,x\},D(y))-B(\{y,x,u\},D(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-B(D(x),\{u,z,y\})-B(D(y),\{z,u,x\})-B(D(z),\{y,x,u\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega(x,\{u,z,y\})-\omega(y,\{z,u,x\})-\omega(z,\{y,x,u\}),

\displaystyle\omega(u,\{x,y,z\})+\omega(x,\{u,z,y\})+\omega(y,\{z,u,x\})+\omega(z,\{y,x,u\})=0,

For the necessity, if

Define

by

and
B,

\omega

\displaystyle B(D(u),\{x,y,z\})+B(D(x),\{u,z,y\})+B(D(y),\{z,u,x\})+B(D(z),\{y,x,u\})=0.

Since

For the invertible of

(5.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\{d_{1}(u,x,y),z,v\})-\{d_{1}(u,y,x),z,v\}-\{d_{1}(u,z,x),y,v\}+\{d_{1}(u,z,y),x,v\}\\
&-\{u,d_{1}(x,y,z),v\}+d_{1}(\{u,x,y\},z,v)-d_{1}(\{u,y,x\},z,v)-d_{1}(\{u,z,x\},y,v)\\
&+d_{1}(\{u,z,y\},x,v)-d_{1}(u,\{x,y,z\},v)=0.\end{aligned}

So
d_{1}
is a
3
-cocycle with respect to adjoint module
\mathfrak{L}.
Similarly, one can check that
d_{n}
is a
3
-cocycle.
∎

Now, we characterize the
1
-parameter formal deformation through cohomology of
\mathfrak{L}.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Leibniz triple system. Suppose that
d_{t}(x,y,z)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}(x,y,z)t^{i}
and
d_{t}^{\prime}(x,y,z)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y,z)t^{i}
are two
1
-parameter formal deformations of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}).
They are called equivalent, denote by
d_{t}\sim d_{t}^{\prime},
if there is a formal isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}[[t]]
-modules

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}(x)t^{i}:(\mathfrak{L}[[t]],d_{t})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{L}[[t]],d_{t}^{\prime}),

where each
\phi_{i}:\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear map
(
extended to be
\mathbb{F}[[t]]
-linear
)
and
\phi_{0}=
id
{}_{\mathfrak{L}},
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi_{t}d_{t}(x,y,z)=d_{t}^{\prime}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)).

If
d_{1}=d_{2}=...=0,

d_{t}=d_{0}
is said to be the null deformation. A
1
-parameter formal deformation
d_{t}
is called trivial if
d_{t}\sim d_{0}.
A Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is called analytically rigid, if every
1
-parameter formal deformation
d_{t}
is trivial.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
d_{t}(x,y,z)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}(x,y,z)t^{i}
and
d_{t}^{\prime}(x,y,z)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y,z)t^{i}
be equivalent
1
-parameter formal deformations of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}).
Then
d_{1}
and
d_{1}^{\prime}
belong to the same cohomology class in
H^{3}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}).

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi_{t}(x)=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}(x)t^{i}
is the formal
\mathbb{F}[[t]]
-module isomorphism such that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}\Big(\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}d_{j}(x,y,z)\Big)t^{i+j}=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}\Big(\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\phi_{k}(x)t^{k},\sum\limits_{l\geq 0}\phi_{l}(y)t^{l},\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}\phi_{m}(z)t^{m}\Big)t^{i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}(d_{j}(x,y,z))t^{i+j}=\sum\limits_{i+k+l+m=n}d_{i}^{\prime}(\phi_{k}(x),\phi_{l}(y),\phi_{m}(z))t^{i+k+l+m}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=1}\phi_{i}(d_{j}(x,y,z))=\sum\limits_{i+k+l+m=1}d_{i}^{\prime}(\phi_{k}(x),\phi_{l}(y),\phi_{m}(z)),

that is,

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,y,z)+\phi_{1}(\{x,y,z\})=\{\phi_{1}(x),y,z\}+\{x,\phi_{1}(y),z\}+\{x,y,\phi_{1}(z)\}+d_{1}^{\prime}(x,y,z),

we have

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,y,z)-d_{1}^{\prime}(x,y,z)=\{\phi_{1}(x),y,z\}+\{x,\phi_{1}(y),z\}+\{x,y,\phi_{1}(z)\}-\phi_{1}(\{x,y,z\}).

Then
d_{1}-d_{1}^{\prime}\in B^{3}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}).

∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Leibniz triple system such that
H^{3}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L})=0.
Then
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is analytically rigid.

Proof.

Let
d_{t}
be a
1
-parameter formal deformation of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}).
Suppose that
d_{t}=d_{0}+\sum\limits_{i\geq n}d_{i}t^{i},
and by Proposition
5.3

d_{n}\in Z^{3}(\mathfrak{L},V)=B^{3}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}).

It follows that there exists
f_{n}\in C^{1}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}),
such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle d_{n}(x,y,z)=\{f_{n}(x),y,z\}+\{x,f_{n}(y),z\}+\{x,y,f_{n}(z)\}-f_{n}(\{x,y,z\}).

Let
\phi_{t}=
id
{}_{\mathfrak{L}}-f_{n}t^{n}
and
d_{t}^{\prime}(x,y,z)=\phi_{t}^{-1}d_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)).
It is straightforward to prove that
d_{t}^{\prime}
is a
1
-parameter formal deformation of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
and
d_{t}\sim d_{t}^{\prime}.
Suppose that
d_{t}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}t^{i},
we express the coefficient in front of the
d_{t}^{\prime}
in terms of
d_{i}^{\prime}.
Then

\displaystyle({\rm id}_{\mathfrak{L}}-f_{n}t^{n})\Big(\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y,z)t^{i}\Big)=\Big(d_{0}+\sum\limits_{i\geq n}d_{i}t^{i}\Big)(x-f_{n}(x)t^{n},y-f_{n}(y)t^{n},z-f_{n}(z)t^{n}),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y,z)t^{i}-\sum\limits_{i\geq 0}f_{n}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y,z)t^{i+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}-(\{f_{n}(x),y,z\}+\{x,f_{n}(y),z\}+\{x,y,f_{n}(z)\})t^{n}+(\{f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y),z\}

\displaystyle+\{x,f_{n}(y),f_{n}(z)\}+\{f_{n}(x),y,f_{n}(z)\})t^{2n}-\{f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y),f_{n}(z)\}t^{3n}+\sum\limits_{i\geq n}d_{i}(x,y,z)t^{i}

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i\geq n}(d_{i}(f_{n}(x),y,z)+d_{i}(x,f_{n}(y),z)+d_{i}(x,y,f_{n}(z)))t^{i+n}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i\geq n}(d_{i}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y),z)+d_{i}(x,f_{n}(y),f_{n}(z))+d_{i}(f_{n}(x),y,f_{n}(z)))t^{i+2n}

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i\geq n}(d_{i}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y),f_{n}(z))t^{i+3n}.

Then we have
d_{1}^{\prime}=...=d_{n-1}^{\prime}=0
and

\displaystyle d_{n}^{\prime}(x,y,z)-f_{n}(\{x,y,z\})=-(\{f_{n}(x),y,z\}+\{x,f_{n}(y),z\}+\{x,y,f_{n}(z)\})+d_{n}(x,y,z).

By Eq. (
5.9
),
d_{n}^{\prime}=0
and
d_{t}^{\prime}=d_{0}+\sum\limits_{i\geq n+1}d_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}.
Repeat this procedure, one observe that there is no nontrivial deformation of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}),
i.e.,
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is analytically rigid.
∎

At last, we define two
5
-cochains Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(i=1,2)
by

\displaystyle{\rm Ob}_{n+1}^{1}(u,v,x,y,z)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n+1\atop i,j>0}\Big(d_{i}(d_{j}(u,v,x),y,z)-d_{i}(d_{j}(u,v,y),x,z)

\displaystyle-d_{i}(d_{j}(u,v,z),x,y)+d_{i}(d_{j}(u,v,z),y,x)-d_{i}(u,v,d_{j}(x,y,z))\Big),

\displaystyle{\rm Ob}_{n+1}^{2}(u,v,x,y,z)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n+1\atop i,j>0}\Big(d_{i}(d_{j}(u,v,x),y,z)-d_{i}(d_{j}(u,x,v),y,z)

\displaystyle-d_{i}(d_{j}(u,y,v),x,z)+d_{i}(d_{j}(u,y,x),v,z)-d_{i}(u,d_{j}(v,x,y),z)\Big).

Definition 5.7
.

The
5
-cochains Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(\mathfrak{L})\in C^{5}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L})(i=1,2)
are called
(n+1)
-th obstruction cochain for extending a given deformation of order
n
(i.e.
d_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}d^{i}t^{i}
) to a deformation of
\mathfrak{L}
of order
(n+1).

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
d_{t}
be a deformation of
\mathfrak{L}
of order
n.
Then
d_{t}
extends to a deformation of order
n+1,

d_{n+1}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n+1}d^{i}t^{i}
if and only if

Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(\mathfrak{L})=\delta^{3}_{i}d_{n+1}(i=1,2).

Proof.

For the necessity, it is obvious by the definition of Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(\mathfrak{L})
and Eqs. (
5.5
) and (
5.6
).

For the sufficiency, suppose Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(\mathfrak{L})=\delta^{3}_{i}d_{n+1}(i=1,2)
, for some
3
-cochain
d_{n+1}\in C^{3}(\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}),
that is Ob
{}_{n+1}^{i}(\mathfrak{L})
is a coboundary and
\tilde{d_{t}}=d_{t}+d_{n+1}t^{n+1}.
Observe that
\tilde{d_{t}}
satisfies Eqs. (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) for
0\leq r\leq n+1
. So
\tilde{d_{t}}
is an extension of
d_{t}
and is of order
n+1.

∎

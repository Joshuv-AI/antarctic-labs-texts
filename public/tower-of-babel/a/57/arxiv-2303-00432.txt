Realization of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Naqi Fan, Lijun Zhang

Abstract
Dynamic evolution behaviors of dimension-varying control systems often appear in the genetic regulatory network and the vehicle clutch system etc. An interesting and significant study on dimension-varying control systems is how to realize the dimension-varying (the transient dynamics) process smoothly between the different dimensional subsystems. The quotient space approach is considered as an effective tool to model the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems. This paper investigates the realization problem of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems. By revealing the structure of the controllable subspace for the linear system on quotient space, we propose the condition for the realization of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems, based on which a new scheme for modeling the transient dynamics is given. Moreover, the proposed scheme justifies the existing result for the strategy for modelling the transient dynamics. A numeric example is given to illustrate our theoretical results.

Introduction

Dimension-varying control systems, also known as cross-dimensional control systems, are used for modeling complex systems with state spaces of different dimensions.
In practice, many dynamic behaviours can be modeled by a dimension-varying control system.
For example, in the internet or some other service-based networks, some users may join or withdraw every now and then

During the dimension-varying process, the dimension-varying control system evolves from one model to another model of different dimensions.

In the past, researchers did some work on dimension-varying control systems, where they usually treated dimension-varying control systems as switching systems and investigated the stability and control design problem based on the switching system theory

The processes of docking, undocking, departure and joining in spacecrafts formation take even longer
[

,

4

. Therefore, it is significant to study the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems. Modeling and control design for the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems can improve the performance of mechanical or other systems.

However, for decades, mathematically few researches on the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems appear due to the lack of proper theoretical tool. Fortunately, Prof. Cheng
[
11

in 2019 developed the theory of dimension-varying control systems, where he investigated the underlying mathematics of dimension-varying control systems and deliver a masterful exposition of dimension-varying control systems to model and analyse the dynamics of dimension-varying process of linear systems. Typically, based on quotient space theory, Cheng in

proposed a projecting system of dimension-varying control systems on a developed quotient space which consists of the equivalent class of the state of different dimensional systems. In consequence, the study on the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems is treated as the investigation on the corresponding projecting systems on quotient space.

Furthermore, we present a condition for the realization of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems based on the controllability of the projecting system.
In particular, based on the proposed condition, a new scheme for modeling the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems is developed, based on which the realization of the transient dynamics of vehicle clutch systems in

2.2
Problem Description

Note that a system on quotient space is a set of equivalent systems of different dimensions, hence the systems of different dimensions can be “lifted" up to a space of the identical dimension.
This makes it possible to model the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems on quotient space.
Therefore, by Definition
8
, the projection of system (
1
) on quotient space can be described as follows.

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{1}:\dot{\bar{x}}(t)=\hat{A}_{1}\vec{\circ}\bar{x}(t)+\bar{B}_{1}u_{1}(t),\quad\bar{x}\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{p},

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}_{2}:\dot{\bar{y}}(t)=\hat{A}_{2}\vec{\circ}\bar{y}(t)+\bar{B}_{2}u_{2}(t),\quad\bar{y}\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{q},

where
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{p}=\{\bar{x}|x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\}
and
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{q}=\{\bar{y}|y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\}
. For simplify, hereafter we use the notation
\Omega
to represent

or
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
.
Based on system (
6
), we can convert the transient dynamics from
\Sigma_{1}
to
\Sigma_{2}
of system (
1
) into that of
\bar{\Sigma}_{1}
to
\bar{\Sigma}_{2}
on quotient space during the transient period

.

Definition 9
.

[
10
]

For system (
1
), the transient dynamics is said to be properly realized from a given starting state

to

Here several intriguing and challenging problems arise:

1.

Whether the realization problem of system (

2.

What conditions need to be satisfied for system (

3.

What conditions should be satisfied to model the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems?

Now, we will discuss each of these problems in details.

Some Analyses for Linear Systems on Quotient Space

3.1
The controllable subspace of linear system on
\Omega

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{span}\left\{B,AB,\cdots,A^{r-1}B\right\}
be the controllable subspace of system (
4
), the controllable subspace of the projecting system (
5
),
denoted by

, is

\overline{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{span}\{\bar{B},\overline{AB},\overline{A^{2}B},\cdots,\overline{A^{r-1}B}\}

Proof.

It is easy to calculate that the trajectory of the projecting system (
5
) with the initial

is

\bar{x}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\hat{A}t}\vec{\circ}\bar{x}(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\hat{A}(t-\tau)}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}u(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(7)

Let
\mathbf{0}:=\{[\underbrace{0,0,\cdots,0}_{n}]^{T}\mid n=1,2,\cdots\}
.
Suppose
\bar{x}(t_{0})
is driven to the origin
\bar{x}(t)=\mathbf{0}
, then (
7
) is further written as

\bar{x}(t_{0})=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\hat{A}(t_{0}-\tau)}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}u(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(8)

It can be obtained from Definition
3
that the rank of
\hat{A}
is equal to that of

. Using Cayley¨CHamilton theorem
[

]
, we have the following Taylor expansion,

\mathrm{e}^{\hat{A}(t_{0}-\tau)}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{j}(t_{0}-\tau)\hat{A}^{j},

where
\hat{A}^{j}=\hat{A}^{0}\circ\hat{A}^{1}\circ\cdots\hat{A}^{j}
.

Let
\gamma_{j}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\lambda_{j}(t_{0}-\tau)u(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau
, (
8
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\bar{x}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\hat{A}^{j}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}\gamma_{j}

(9)

\displaystyle=-[\begin{array}[]{llll}\bar{B}&\hat{A}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}&\cdots&\hat{A}^{r-1}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}\end{array}][\begin{array}[]{cccc}\gamma_{0}&\gamma_{1}&\cdots&\gamma_{r-1}\end{array}]^{T}

It follows from Definition

.
Together with Definition

, we have
\hat{A}^{j}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}=\widehat{A^{j}}\vec{\circ}\bar{B}=\overline{A^{j}\vec{\circ}B}

Furthermore, note that the column number of

\overline{A^{j}\vec{\circ}B}=\overline{A^{j}B}

Thus, (

\displaystyle\bar{x}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=-[\begin{array}[]{llll}\bar{B}&\overline{A\vec{\circ}B}&\cdots&\overline{{A}^{r-1}\vec{\circ}B}\end{array}][\begin{array}[]{cccc}\gamma_{0}&\gamma_{1}&\cdots&\gamma_{r-1}\end{array}]^{T}

\displaystyle=-[\begin{array}[]{llll}\bar{B}&\overline{AB}&\cdots&\overline{{A}^{r-1}B}\end{array}][\begin{array}[]{cccc}\gamma_{0}&\gamma_{1}&\cdots&\gamma_{r-1}\end{array}]^{T}

Note that
\Omega
is a vector space (recall Definition
2
), the controllable subspace of system (
5
) can be expressed as

\overline{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{span}\{\bar{B},\overline{AB},\overline{A^{2}B},\cdots,\overline{A^{r-1}B}\}

∎

In contrast with

are equivalence classes of elements of

Lemma

This means that the study on the transient dynamics of system (

That is, for dimension-varying control systems, instead of studying the realization of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems, we may study the corresponding problem on quotient space. Definition 10

Lemma 2

Since

\displaystyle\xi_{\pi}=

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}\overrightarrow{\ltimes}\xi=T_{\Lambda}\overrightarrow{\ltimes}(y\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T_{\Lambda}\otimes I_{\alpha})(y\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}y\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle y_{\pi}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}.

Hence we have

In this section, a condition is presented for the realization of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems.
To formulate our main result precisely, we shall need some more notations and terminology.

Let

Denote

Definition 11

.
That is, the missing elements of

5
Condition for Modeling the Transient Dynamics of Dimension-Varying Systems

A necessary condition for modeling the transient dynamics is proposed in this section, based on which a new scheme for modeling the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems is developed.
Accordingly, a numerical example is given to illustrate the effectiveness of our proposed the theoretical results.

Based on Definition
9
, the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control system (
6
) on quotient space
\Omega
is modeled as the following unified form:

\dot{\bar{z}}=\bar{F}(\bar{z},u_{1},u_{2}),\bar{z}\in\Omega.

(22)

(

) is called

. Our purpose is to realize the transient process of (

) with the help of the transient dynamics model (

) on
\Omega
.
Based on Theorem
1
, a necessary condition for modeling the transient dynamics can be obtained naturally.

Corollary 2
.

With the same notations as Theorem
1
. Let
\bar{\mathcal{C}_{z}}
be the the controllable subspace of (
22
). Then a necessary condition for modeling the transient dynamics of system (
6
) is as follows:

\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{1}+\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\subset\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{z}.

(23)

Next, we will demonstrate that the modelling strategy of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems proposed by Prof. Cheng
[
11
]
satisfies the condition of Corollary
2
.

Let

be the least common multiple of

and

. Then
(
6
) can be “lifted" to

as

\begin{gathered}\Sigma_{z}^{1}:\dot{z}(t)=\mathbf{A_{1}}z(t)+\mathbf{B_{1}}u_{1}(t),\quad z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\Sigma_{z}^{2}:\dot{z}(t)=\mathbf{A_{2}}z(t)+\mathbf{B_{2}}u_{2}(t),\quad z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{gathered}

(24)

where

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{A_{1}}=A_{1}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{n/p},\mathbf{B_{1}}=B_{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{n/p},\\

Cheng
[

modelled the transient dynamics of system (

(26)

Proposition 1
.

With the same notations as Theorem

. Let
\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{z}
be the controllable subspace of the transient dynamics (

\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{1}+\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\subset\bar{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{z}.

The solution of (

\bar{z}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{*}(t-t_{0})}\vec{\circ}\bar{z}_{0}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{*}(t-\tau)}\vec{\circ}\bar{\mathbf{B}}_{1}^{*}u_{1}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{*}(t-\tau)}\vec{\circ}\bar{\mathbf{B}}_{2}^{*}u_{2}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\{[\begin{array}[]{llll}\bar{\mathbf{B}}^{*}_{1}&\overline{\mathbf{A}^{*}\mathbf{B}^{*}_{1}}&\cdots&\overline{(\mathbf{A}^{*})^{n-1}\mathbf{B}^{*}_{1}}\end{array}]\}+\operatorname{span}\{[\begin{array}[]{llll}\bar{\mathbf{B}}^{*}_{2}&\overline{\mathbf{A}^{*}\mathbf{B}^{*}_{2}}&\cdots&\overline{(\mathbf{A}^{*})^{n-1}\mathbf{B}^{*}_{2}}\end{array}]\},

6
Conclusion

The realization problem of the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems has been investigated in this work.
By analyzing the controllable subspaces of linear systems on quotient space, a condition for the realization of the transient process for dimension-varying control systems has been given, based on which a new strategy for modelling the transient dynamics of dimension-varying control systems on quotient space is presented. As a result, theoretically we prove that the dynamic evolution between systems of different dimensions can be implemented by designing the control of the transient dynamics model on quotient space. The correctness of theoretical results is verified by a numerical example.

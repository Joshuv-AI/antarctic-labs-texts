Stabilization by Multiplicative Itô Noise for Chafee-Infante Equation in Perforated Domains
Hong Hai Ly, Bao Quoc Tang

Abstract
The stabilization by noise for parabolic equations in perforated domains, i.e. domains with small holes, is investigated. We show that when the holes are small enough, one can stabilize the unstable equations using suitable multiplicative Itô noise. The results are quantitative, in the sense that we can explicitly estimate the size of the holes and diffusion coefficients for which stabilization by noise takes place. This is done by using the asymptotic behaviour of the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian as the hole shrinks to a point.



Keywords—
Stabilization by noise, Perforated domains, Asymptotic behaviour of eigenvalues

1
Introduction and main result

Consider a bounded domain
\mathcal{O}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\{2,3\}
, with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. Within this domain, there is a family of small pairwise disjoint open holes
\{E^{i}_{\varepsilon}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 1
, which have size of order
\varepsilon>0
, i.e. for each
E^{i}_{\varepsilon}
, there is a ball
\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
with radius
\varepsilon
such that
E^{i}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
. We assume that the holes are properly inside the domain, i.e.
\overline{E_{\varepsilon}^{i}}\subset\mathcal{O}
, and the boundary
\partial E^{i}_{\varepsilon}
is also Lipschitz for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. The open perforated domain
\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}\backslash\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\overline{E^{i}_{\varepsilon}}
is obtained by removing these holes from
\mathcal{O}
. We study in this work a stochastic Chafee-Infante equation defined on this domain, described by the following

\begin{cases}du+(-\Delta u+u^{3}-\beta u)dt=h(t,u)dW_{t},&x\in\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon},\;t>0,\\
u(x,t)=0,&x\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon},\;t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\beta>0
and
W_{t}
is a standard real-valued scalar Wiener process defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
with natural filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
dW_{t}
denotes the Itô differential and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
is given initial data. It holds obviously
\partial\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}=\cup_{j=1}^{\ell}\partial E_{\varepsilon}^{i}\cup\partial\mathcal{O}
.

The diffusion coefficient
h:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to satisfy following conditions:

(
H_{1}
)

for all
t\geq 0
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
h(t,0)=0
,

(
H_{2}
)

for all
t\geq 0
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
|h(t,u)|^{2}\leq\gamma(t)|u|^{2}
, where
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds\leq\gamma_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ with }\gamma_{0}\text{ is a non-negative constant.}

(
H_{3}
)

for
t\geq 0
,
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
|h(t,u)u|^{2}\geq\rho(t)|u|^{4}
, where
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function such that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)ds\geq\rho_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ with }\rho_{0}\text{ is a non-negative constant.}

The assumption
(

H
1

)
is to ensure that
u\equiv 0
is a trivial steady state solution to (
1
). Conditions
(

H
2

)
and
(

H
3

)
are Lipschitz continuity at zero and dissipative properties.
Typical examples of the diffusion coefficient
h(t,u)
satisfying conditions
(

H
1

)
–
(

H
3

)
include

•

the linear function
h(t,u)=\alpha u
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, for which
\gamma(t)=\rho(t)=\gamma_{0}=\rho_{0}=\alpha^{2}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

•

or the nonlinear function
h(t,u)=\dfrac{(2+t^{2})(1+u^{2})}{(1+t^{2})(2+u^{2})}u
for which
\gamma(t)
and
\rho(t)
can be chosen as

\gamma(t)=16\left(\frac{2+t^{2}}{1+t^{2}}\right)^{2},\hskip 9.24994pt\rho(t)=\left(\frac{2+t^{2}}{2(1+t^{2})}\right)^{2},

and consequently one can choose
\gamma_{0}=64
and
\rho_{0}=1
.

Note that when
h(t,u)\equiv 0
, (
1
) becomes the classical deterministic Chafee-Infante equation, for which the stability of the zero steady state is determined by the value of
\beta>0
and the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition.

To put our work into context, let us briefly review the literature on stabilization by noise. The stabilization using multiplicative noise has been investigated since the eighties firstly for finite dimensional systems, see e.g.
[
GS82
,
ACW83
,
Arn90
]
. For the linear finite dimensional system
\dot{x}=Ax
, this phenomenon is completely characterized in
[
ACW83
]
where it was proved to be stabilizable by linear multiplicative noise if and only
\text{Trace}(A)<0
. The case of nonlinear systems and nonlinear noise, still in finite dimensional setting, is more complicated as it was shown in
[
AMR08
]
that random noise can also destabilize stable systems. The first work dealing with infinite dimensional system is
[
Kwi99
]
, therein the author showed that the multiplicative noise shifts the spectrum of Laplacian to the right, thus stabilizing the system. The stabilization by noise for partial differential equations has been then studied extensively, see e.g.
[
CLM01
,
Kwi02
,
CGAR03
,
CR04
,
CCLR07
]
. All these works showed that the is an infinite range of noise intensity that stabilizes the deterministic system. Notably, a recent work
[
FSTT19
]
showed that when the noise is acting solely on the boundary, one might stabilize the deterministic system only with a finite range of noise intensities. It is worthwhile to emphasize that, up to our knowledge, all the existing works considered solid domains, i.e. without holes.
On the other hand, the stabilization by
deterministic
control in perforated domains has been also investigated by many authors since it appears frequently in applications, see e.g.
[
Zhi92
,
CDZ91
,
CDZ94
,
BCCW11
,
BS22
]
. Motivated by these developments, we investigate in this paper, for the first time, the stabilizing effect of multiplicative noise in perforated domains for the Chafee-Infante equation (
1
).

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under
(

H
2

)
–
(

H
3

)
, there exists a unique global strong solution to the stochastic equation (
1
).

If
(

H
1

)
is fulfilled and

\rho_{0}-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}>-\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+\beta,

where
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})
is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet-Laplacian on
\mathcal{O}
,
then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\varepsilon_{0}\sim\begin{cases}e^{-\left(\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O})+\rho_{0}-(\beta+{\gamma_{0}}/{2})\right)^{-1}}&\text{ for }n=2,\\
\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O})+\rho_{0}-(\beta+{\gamma_{0}}/{2})&\text{ for }n=3,\end{cases}

(2)

and for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the zero steady state solution to (
1
) is globally exponentially stable almost surely, i.e.

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq Ce^{-\delta t}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

where the positive constants
C,\delta

are independent of
\varepsilon
. Here
A\sim B
means that
C_{0}A\leq B\leq C_{1}A
for positive constants
C_{0},C_{1}
independent of
A
and
B
.

Remark 1.2
.

We remark that the stabilization shown in Theorem
1.1
is due to the multiplicative Itô noise, which, after applying the Itô formula, produces an “artifical” stabilizing term (see the proof in Section
3
. If the noise is considered in the sense of Stratonovich, such a term does not appear and the stabilizing effect is more intriguing. To obtain the stabilization by Stratonovich noise, following
[
ACW83
,
CR04
,
CCLR07
]
, we will need to considered
a collection of noisy terms
of the form
\sum_{i}B_{i}u\circ dW_{t}^{i}
for suitable operators
B_{i}
. This interesting case is left for future research.

It is emphasised that
\varepsilon_{0}
defined in (
2
) depends explicitly and only on the parameters
\beta,\rho_{0},\gamma_{0}
and the domain
\mathcal{O}
through the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})
. It is known that when
\beta>\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})
, the zero steady state to the deterministic equation (
1
), i.e.
h\equiv 0
, is unstable (see e.g.
[
FSTT19
, Theorem 2.2]
for the case of Robin boundary conditions, whose arguments can be also applied to Dirichlet boundary conditions). By choosing the diffusion coefficient
h(t,u)
such that (
2
) holds, for instance,
h(t,u)=\alpha u
with
\alpha^{2}>2(-\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+\beta)
, then the zero solution is (exponentially) stabilized provided that the size
\varepsilon
of the holes do not exceed
\varepsilon_{0}
given in (
2
). Our main idea is to use the asymptotic behavior of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
of the Dirichlet-Laplacian in
\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which will be proved in Section
2
. We then utilize these results in Section
3
to prove our main theorem.

2
Asymptotic behavior of the first eigenvalue

On the perforated domain
\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}
, we investigate the eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\varphi(x)=\lambda(\varepsilon)\varphi(x),&x\in\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon},\\
\varphi(x)=0,&x\in\partial\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

(3)

Here,
\varphi(x)
represents the eigenfunction, and
\lambda(\varepsilon)
is the corresponding eigenvalue.

Let
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
denote the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}
under the Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}
, and let
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})
be the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\mathcal{O}
under the Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\mathcal{O}
. It is classical that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\;\text{ and }\;\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}^{2},

(4)

for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
and all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
.

The asymptotic behavior of
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
has been extensively investigated in the literature, see e.g.
[
Oza81
,
Oza82
,
MNP85
,
ALM22
]
.In the early works, the holes are often considered to be balls, which makes the analysis easier. In order to allow various shapes for the holes, later works utilize the concept of
capacity potential
, which measures the ability of the set
A
to hold electric charge or conduct heat, see e.g.
[
Cho54
]
.
The capacity potential of a set
A\subset\mathcal{O}
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\textup{cap}(A):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx:\,u\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O}),\,u\geq 1\text{ on }A\right\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the capacity potential
\textup{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})
is estimated as

\displaystyle\textup{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{2\pi}{-\log\varepsilon}+o(\log^{-1}(\varepsilon))&\text{if }n=2,\\
4\pi\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)&\text{if }n=3.\end{cases}

Proof.

According to
[
Flu95
]
, the capacity
\textup{cap}(\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon})
is given by:

\displaystyle\textup{cap}(\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2\pi}{-\log\varepsilon}+o(\log^{-1}(\varepsilon))&\text{if }n=2,\\
4\pi\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)&\text{if }n=3.\end{cases}

The monotonicity property of capacity
[
Cho54
, Chapter II]
) states that the capacity of a smaller set is less than or equal to the capacity of any larger set containing it. Consequently, since
E^{i}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\text{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})\leq\text{cap}(\mathbb{B}^{i}_{\varepsilon})
, which gives the desired bound and finishes the proof of this Lemma.
∎

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, each hole
E_{\varepsilon}^{i}
is assumed to shrink to a fixed point
x_{i}\in E_{\varepsilon}^{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. Now, we present the following theorem that establishes a relationship between the first eigenvalues of the perforated and original domains.

Theorem 2.2
.

The first eigenvalue of problem (
3
) has the following asymptotics as
\varepsilon
tends to zero

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}_{1}(O_{\varepsilon})=\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{1}^{2}(x_{i})\,\textup{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})+o(\textup{cap}(\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}E^{i}_{\varepsilon})),\hskip 9.24994pt\ell\in\mathbb{N},

(5)

where
\varphi_{1}
represents the first eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O})
. Consequently

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+O((\log\varepsilon)^{-1})&\text{ for }n=2,\\
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+O(\varepsilon)&\text{ for }n=3.\\
\end{cases}

(6)

Proof.

By applying
[
Flu95
, Theorem 6]
and using sub-additivity of the capacity and its asymptotic additivity for small disjoint sets, we obtain the asymptotic expansion for each
simple
eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
as follows:

\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})=\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{O})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{k}^{2}(x_{i})\textup{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})+o(\textup{cap}(\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}E^{i}_{\varepsilon}))\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

where
\varphi_{k}
are
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
-orthonormal eigenfunctions associated with
\lambda_{k}(\mathcal{O})
. It is classical that the first eigenvalue is simple, and therefore we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}_{1}(O_{\varepsilon})=\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O})+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{1}^{2}(x_{i})\textup{cap}(E^{i}_{\varepsilon})+o(\textup{cap}(\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}E^{i}_{\varepsilon})),

which is the desired asymptotic expansion (
5
).

To prove (
6
), it is sufficient to show that
\varphi_{1}
is in
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
. Since the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
is only Lipschitz continuous, using maximal regularity of elliptic equation for
-\Delta\varphi_{1}=\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})\varphi_{1}
only gives
\varphi_{1}\in H^{3/2}(\mathcal{O})
which is not enough to obtain
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
-bound in the case
n=3
. Therefore, we use the results in

[
Dav89
]
(see Theorem 2.1.4 and Example 2.1.8 therein), where it was shown that
\varphi_{k}
are in
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})
for all
k\geq 1
. The asymptotic estimate (
6
) the follows directly from (
5
), Lemma
2.1
and the boundedness of
\varphi_{1}
.
∎

3
Stabilization by noise

We start with the well-posedness for the stochastic equation (
1
).

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Par80
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Under assumptions
(

H
2

)
–
(

H
3

)
, the equation (
1
) has a unique global strong solution in the sense

(i)

u\in L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}))
, and

(ii)

for
f(u)=u^{3}-\beta u
,

u(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(-\Delta u(s)+f(u(s)))ds=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}h(s,u(s)dW_{s},\hskip 9.24994pt\;\text{a.e.}\;t\in(0,T),\;\text{a.s.}\;\omega\in\Omega.

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
0\neq u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})
and let
u(t)
be the corresponding solution to problem (
1
) with initial
u_{0}
obtained in Theorem
3.1
. Applying Itô’s formula (see e.g.
[
GM10
, Theorem 4.3]
or, more general
[
GM10
, Theorem 6.10]
), for
\psi(u):=\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}
, and using

\psi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{2\langle u,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}},\hskip 9.24994pt\psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\xi=\frac{2\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}-\frac{4\langle u,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\langle u,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}},\hskip 9.24994ptu,\xi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}).

we get

\displaystyle\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}=

\displaystyle\log\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2}{\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}\times

(7)

\displaystyle\times\left(\|\nabla u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}}f(u(s))u(s)dx-\frac{1}{2}\|h(s,u(s))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\right)ds

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}dW_{s}.

Since
f(u)=u^{3}-\beta u
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}}f(u(s))u(s)dx\geq-\beta\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}.

(8)

Moreover, thanks to the Poincaré inequality in (
4
)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\geq\lambda^{D}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}.

(9)

Using assumption
(

H
2

)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|h(s,u(s))\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds.

(10)

Let
\delta>0
be arbitrary.
To deal with the stochastic integral, we utilize the exponential martingale inequality (see, e.g.
[
Liu97
, Lemma 1.1]
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\omega:\sup_{0\leq t\leq w}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}dW_{s}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}ds\right]>\frac{2\log k}{\alpha}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}

where
0<\alpha<1
,
w=k\delta
and
k=1,2,\dots
. We then apply the well-known Borel-Cantelli lemma to obtain that there exists an integer
k_{0}(\delta,\omega)>0
for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}dW_{s}\leq\frac{2\log k}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}ds

(11)

for all
0\leq t\leq k\delta
,
k\geq k_{0}(\delta,\omega)
. Combining (
7
)–(
11
), we see that there exists a positive random integer
k_{1}(\delta)
such that almost surely

\displaystyle\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq\log\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle+\frac{4\log k}{\alpha}+(2\beta-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}))t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds-(2-\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\langle u(s),h(s,u(s))\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}{\|u(s)\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}}ds,

for all
(k-1)\delta\leq t\leq k\delta
,
k\leq k_{1}(\delta)
, which for the preceding
\delta>0
together with
(

H
3

)
immediately implies that

\displaystyle\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq\log\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}+\frac{4\log k}{\alpha}+(2\beta-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}))t+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds-(2-\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)ds.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{1}{t}\log\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}+\frac{1}{t}\frac{4\log k}{\alpha}+(2\beta-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}))-(2-\alpha)\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)ds+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)ds.

Taking the limsup of both sides as

, using
\limsup(-y_{n})=-\liminf y_{n}
, and the assumptions
(

H
2

)
and
(

H
3

)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq 2\beta-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})-(2-\alpha)\rho_{0}+\gamma_{0}.

Passing to the limit

yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq 2\beta+\gamma_{0}-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})-2\rho_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ a.s.}

Due to Theorem
2.2
, we have the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+O((\log\varepsilon)^{-1})&\text{ for }n=2,\\
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+O(\varepsilon)&\text{ for }n=3.\\
\end{cases}

Therefore, for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
with
\varepsilon_{0}
is in (
2
), we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon})}\leq 2\beta+\gamma_{0}-2\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})-2\rho_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ a.s.}

It follows that for
\lambda_{1}^{D}(\mathcal{O})+\rho_{0}>\beta+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{0}
, the zero solution becomes exponentially stable.
∎

Introduction

In his inspiring article
[
12
]
D. Ornstein showed that if
Q(D),P_{1}(D),\ldots P_{m}(D)
are homogeneous differential operators of the same order and
Q\notin\operatorname{span}\{P_{j}\}
then, for any
C>0
, the inequality

\left\|{Q(D)f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|{P_{j}(D)f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

(1)

does not hold.
In particular, for any
C>0
and any multi-indices
\beta,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
with
|\beta|=|\alpha_{1}|=\ldots=|\alpha_{m}|
,
\beta\notin\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}

the inequality

\left\|{D^{\beta}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|{D^{\alpha_{j}}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

(2)

does not hold (in this paper
L_{1}(\TT^{d})
is considered with respect to the normalized Haar measure).

In this paper we deal with the quantitative version of this theorem. We are interested in the constant of Bernstein type i.e. what is the growth of the best constant C in (
1
) when the inequality is restricted to the polynomials of degree at most
n
. To the best of our knowledge no result of such type is known.

Our main idea is to use the properties of finite Riesz products
[
14
]
.
In fact we are constructing explicitly the trigonometric polynomials for which our bounds hold. For different (but qualitative) proofs of Ornstein non-inequality in the isotropic case check for example
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
.

Focusing for a moment on the simplest case of our results we get the following:

Corollary 1
.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P_{N}
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
of degree
N
, which satisfies

\left\|{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}P_{N}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\left\|{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}P_{N}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant 1

but

\left\|{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}P_{N}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}>C\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}N.

We do not know if the bound from Corollary
1
is sharp. In fact one can establish in a rather standard way that the mixed derivative from Corollary 1 could not have norm greater than
c\ln n
. Indeed the (linear and invariant) operator
T
which retrieves the mixed derivative from the pure ones is of a weak type
(1,1)
(see
[
5
]
). Hence, by the Nikolskii type inequality for Lorentz spaces (see
[
15
, Theorem 3]
and
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
), for a trigonometric polynomial
f
of degree
N

\begin{split}\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}&=\|Tf\|_{L_{1,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leqslant C\ln(1+N)\|Tf\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&\leqslant C\ln(1+N)\left(\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}+\|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\right).\end{split}

The same comment concerns all the results obtained in this paper. All bounds from below presented here are of the form
(\ln N)^{\phi}
for some
\phi<1
, while the common upper bound is
\ln N
. Nevertheless we conjecture that the optimal exponent
\phi
should be equal to one (see Remark
8
).

This paper contains final results of the study we began in
[
8
]
. It seems it is the first use in the literature of trigonometric polynomials in the context of Ornstein non-inequalities.

Results

In this paper we consider more general, anisotropic case of the inequality (
2
) i.e. such that there exists
\Lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
\langle\alpha_{1}\;,\;\Lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{2}\;,\;\Lambda\rangle=\cdots=\langle\beta\;,\;\Lambda\rangle
. This case was already considered in the literature (see
[
6
]
), with an additional assumption on the parity of derivatives
|\alpha_{j}|\equiv_{2}|\beta|
. (We mention that the results obtained there were only of the qualitative nature.) In our present approach we remove this ”parity assumption”. However we still need other geometric conditions.

The paper contains two results which are proved in quite similar way. Each of them provides a geometric criterion for a set of symbols of partial derivatives which yields quantitative estimates of Ornstein type.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta
are multi-indices in
\left(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\right)^{d}
and there exists a pair of vectors
\Gamma,\;\Lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
for which the following conditions are satisfied

\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle=\langle\beta,\Lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{2},\Lambda\rangle=\ldots=\langle\alpha_{m},\Lambda\rangle,

and

\langle\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle>\langle\beta,\Gamma\rangle>\langle\alpha_{2},\Gamma\rangle\geqslant\ldots\geqslant\langle\alpha_{m},\Gamma\rangle,

(3)

Let
K_{N}
be the smallest constant such that for every trigonometric polynomial
f
of degree at most
N
the following estimate hold

\left\|{D^{\beta}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant K_{N}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|{D^{\alpha_{j}}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(4)

Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

K_{N}>C(\ln N)^{\phi},

where
\phi=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\langle\alpha_{1}-\beta,\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\Gamma\rangle}\right)
.

Remark 3
.

The inequalities (
3
) could be satisfied for different vectors
\Gamma
and then for different permutations of the set
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}
. For any fixed set of multi-indices the choice of the optimal vector
\Gamma
is a simple optimization problem. In dimension 2 for fixed
\alpha_{1}
the value of
\phi
doesn’t depend on choice of
\Gamma
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta
are multi-indices in
\left(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\right)^{d}
and there exists a vector
\Lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
†
†

In this paper we put
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
for which the following condition is satisfied

\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle=\langle\beta,\Lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{2},\Lambda\rangle=\ldots=\langle\alpha_{m},\Lambda\rangle.

Suppose moreover that there exists
\beps\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that

\langle\beta,\beps\rangle\not\equiv\langle\alpha_{1},\beps\rangle\;\mbox{mod }2\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle\alpha_{j},\beps\rangle\equiv\langle\alpha_{1},\beps\rangle\;\mbox{mod }2

(5)

for
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
Let
K_{N}
be the smallest constant such that, for every trigonometric polynomial
f
of degree at most
N
, the following estimate hold

\left\|{D^{\beta}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant K_{N}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|{D^{\alpha_{j}}f}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

(6)

Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

K_{N}>C(\ln N)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 5
.

The case
\beps=(1,1,\ldots,1)
corresponds to the anisotropic Sobolev space which contains an invariant, complemented, infinite dimensional subspace isomorphic to a Hilbert space (see
[
13
]
for details).

Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
and
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d})
. We introduce an auxiliary sequence
(b_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
depending on the parity of our multi-indices. If
|\alpha_{1}|-|\beta|
is even we put
b_{k}:=(2+(-1)^{k})
, if not we put
b_{k}:=1
. For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define a sequence of vectors
(a_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
by the formula
a_{k}=(a_{k}(1),\ldots,a_{k}(d))
, where

a_{k}(j):=3^{\lambda_{j}2kn}b_{k}^{\gamma_{j}}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor

(7)

and

\theta:=\frac{1}{\langle\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\Gamma\rangle}

(8)

Since
\gamma_{j}
and
\lambda_{j}
are positive integers we know that, for any
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\},

a_{k}(j)>3^{2(n-1)}a_{k-1}(j).

(9)

and

\|a_{k}\|_{2}>3^{2(n-1)}\|a_{k-1}\|_{2}.

(10)

We define a modified Riesz product based on this sequence

R_{n}(x)=-1+\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\cos\langle x,a_{k}\rangle\right),

(11)

and the family of sets

A_{k}=\{q:q=a_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\xi_{j}a_{j},\;\xi_{j}\in\{-1,0,1\}\mbox{ for }j\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\;\}.

From (
10
) by standard calculations we know that every point in
A_{k}
has a unique representation as
a_{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\xi_{j}a_{j}
. From (
9
) there exists a constant
2\geqslant\tau>1
independent of
k
and
j
such that

\frac{1}{\tau}a_{k}(j)\leqslant|q(j)|\leqslant\tau a_{k}(j),

(12)

for all
q\in A_{k}
.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we denote
n^{\mu}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}^{\mu(j)}
. For
q\in A_{k}
of the form
q=a_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\xi_{j}a_{j}
we set
r(q)=\#\{j:\xi_{j}\neq 0\}+1
and
r(-q)=r(q)
. Let
W_{n}(x)
be a polynomial given by the formula

W_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{q\in A_{k}\cup-A_{k}}\frac{i^{-|\alpha_{1}|}}{q^{\alpha_{1}}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}.

(13)

Note that

D^{\alpha_{1}}W_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{q\in A_{k}\cup-A_{k}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}=R_{n}(x).

Moreover, for
\mu\in\{\beta,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}

D^{\mu}W_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{q\in A_{k}\cup-A_{k}}i^{|\mu|-|\alpha|}\frac{q^{\mu}}{q^{\alpha_{1}}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}e^{i\langle q,x\rangle},

which could be represented as

D^{\mu}W_{n}(x)=B_{\mu,n}(x)+G_{\mu,n}(x),

(14)

where

B_{\mu,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{q\in A_{k}}\frac{i^{|\mu|-|\alpha_{1}|}}{2^{r(q)}}\left(\left(\frac{q^{\mu}}{q^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{a_{k}^{\mu}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\right)e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}+\left(\frac{(-q)^{\mu}}{(-q)^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{(-a_{k})^{\mu}}{(-a_{k})^{\alpha_{1}}}\right)e^{i\langle-q,x\rangle}\right)

(15)

and

G_{\mu,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}i^{|\mu|-|\alpha_{1}|}\frac{a_{k}^{\mu}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\sum_{q\in A_{k}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}\left(e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}+(-1)^{|\mu|-|\alpha_{1}|}e^{i\langle-q,x\rangle}\right).

(16)

First we estimate the
L_{1}
-norm of
B_{\mu,n}
. Let
v=(\frac{q(1)}{a_{k}(1)},\ldots,\frac{q(d)}{a_{k}(d)})
for
q\in A_{k}
. From (
9
)

\|v-\mathbf{1}\|_{2}\leqslant C(d)3^{-2n},

(17)

where
\mathbf{1}:=(1,\dots,1)
.
Observe that for
q\in A_{k}
by (
12
), (
17
) and by Lipschitz continuity of functions
x^{\alpha_{1}}
,
x^{\mu}
on the cube
[\frac{1}{\tau},\tau]^{d}
we get

\begin{split}\big|q^{\mu}a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}-a_{k}^{\mu}q^{\alpha_{1}}\big|&\leqslant\big|q^{\mu}\left(a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}-q^{\alpha_{1}}\right)\big|+\big|q^{\alpha_{1}}\left(q^{\mu}-a_{k}^{\mu}\right)\big|\\
&\leqslant C\left(|q^{\alpha_{1}}||a_{k}^{\mu}|\big|1-v^{\alpha_{1}}\big|+|q^{\mu}||a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}|\big|1-v^{\mu}\big|\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{oszacowaniewspol}}}{{\leqslant}}C|a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}||a_{k}^{\mu}|\left(\big|1-v^{\alpha_{1}}\big|+\big|1-v^{\mu}\big|\right)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lip.}}{{\leqslant}}C\|\mathbf{1}-v\|_{2}|a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}||a_{k}^{\mu}|\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{raznizej}}}{{\leqslant}}C3^{-2n}|a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}||a_{k}^{\mu}|.\end{split}

(18)

We calculate
a_{k}^{\mu}
and
a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}:

a_{k}^{\mu}=3^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mu(j)\lambda_{j}2kn}b_{k}^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mu(j)\gamma_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{i}}\rfloor^{\mu(j)}=3^{\langle\mu,\Lambda\rangle 2kn}b_{k}^{\langle\mu,\Gamma\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor^{\mu(j)},

(19)

and similarly (replacing
\mu
by
\alpha_{1}
),

a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}=3^{\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle 2kn}b_{k}^{\langle\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor^{\alpha_{1}(j)}.

(20)

Since we only use a finite number of exponents, there is a constant
C>1
such that for any
\nu\in\{\beta,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\},

\frac{1}{C}n^{\theta\cdot\langle\nu,\Gamma\rangle}\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor^{\nu(j)}\leqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\nu,\Gamma\rangle}.

(21)

For every
\mu
as above we have
\langle\mu,\Lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle
. By (
12
), (
18
) and (
19
), (
20
), (
21
) we get

\big|\left(\frac{q^{\mu}}{q^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{a_{k}^{\mu}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\right)\big|=\big|\frac{q^{\mu}a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}-a_{k}^{\mu}q^{\alpha_{1}}}{q^{\alpha_{1}}a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\big|\leqslant C3^{-2n}\frac{|a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}||a_{k}^{\mu}|}{|a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}|^{2}}\leqslant C3^{-2n}n^{\langle\mu-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\leqslant C3^{-n}.

Plugging the above estimates for
\mu\in\{\beta,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}
into the formula for
B_{\mu,n}
we get

\left\|{B_{\mu,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{q\in A_{k}}2C3^{-n}\leqslant 2C3^{-n}3^{n}=2C.

We pass to the estimates of the
L_{1}
norm of
G_{\alpha_{j},n}
for
j\geqslant 2
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
we define

\psi_{k}(x)=\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}\left(1+\cos\langle x,a_{l}\rangle\right),

Simple algebraic manipulation gives us

G_{\alpha_{j},n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}i^{|\mu|-|\alpha_{1}|}\frac{a_{k}^{\alpha_{j}}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{i\langle a_{k},x\rangle}+(-1)^{|\alpha_{j}|-|\alpha_{1}|}e^{i\langle-a_{k},x\rangle}\right)\psi_{k}(x).

Since
\langle\alpha_{j},\Lambda\rangle=\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle
, by (
19
) and (
20
) we get

\bigg|\frac{a_{k}^{\alpha_{j}}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\bigg|\leqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}.

Therefore

\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|{\psi_{k}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

As Riesz products
\psi_{l}
’s have
L_{1}
norms equal to 1. Hence

\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle+1}.

By (
3
) and (
8
), for any
j\in\{2,\ldots,m\},

\theta\cdot\langle\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle=\frac{\langle\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\Gamma\rangle}\leqslant-1.

Hence there exists
C>0
such that

\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C,

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
j\in\{2,\cdots,m\}
.
Therefore for
j\in\{2,\cdots,m\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\left\|{D^{\alpha_{j}}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant\left\|{B_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C.

Since
D^{\alpha_{1}}W
is a modified Riesz product,

\left\|{D^{\alpha_{1}}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant 2.

Summing the above inequalities we get

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\|{D^{\alpha_{j}}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C.

(22)

Now we estimate
\left\|{D^{\beta}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
from below. Since the norm of
B_{\beta,n}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
n
, it is enough to show that the norm of
G_{\beta,n}
is large.

Remark 6
.

In
[
11
, Remark on p. 563]
Y. Meyer observes that the condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(j)}{a_{k+1}(j)}<+\infty
yields

\begin{split}\bigg\|\sum_{\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}}b\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k}(j)\right)\operatorname{exp}&\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k}(j)t\right)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\\
&\simeq\bigg\|\sum_{\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}}b\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k}(j)\right)\operatorname{exp}\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}t_{k}\right)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\end{split}

The constant in the above isomorphism depends only on the value of
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(j)}{a_{k+1}(j)}
. For elementary proofs of this fact see note
[
7
]
or
[
2
, Proposition 4]
.
By a simple tensoring argument

\begin{split}\bigg\|\sum_{\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}}b\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k}\right)\operatorname{exp}&\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\xi_{k}a_{k},t\rangle\right)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\\
&\simeq\bigg\|\sum_{\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}}b\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k}\right)\operatorname{exp}\left(i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\xi_{k},t_{k}\rangle\right)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{{nd}})}\end{split}

In our case there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
n
such that finite sequence
(a_{k}(j))_{k=1}^{n}
define by (
7
) satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}(j)}{a_{k+1}(j)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{nawspol}}}{{<}}n3^{-2n}<C<+\infty

for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Hence when calculating
L_{1}
norm we can treat exponents with different
a_{k}
as independent random variables.

We consider two cases separately.

Case I:
|\alpha_{1}|-|\beta|
is even.

In this case

|G_{\beta,n}(x)|=\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\cos(\langle a_{k},x\rangle)\psi_{k}(x)\bigg|=\bigg|\frac{a_{1}^{\beta}}{a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\psi_{1}+\frac{a_{n}^{\beta}}{a_{n}^{\alpha_{1}}}\psi_{n+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{a_{k+1}^{\beta}}{a_{k+1}^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\right)\psi_{k+1}\bigg|.

Since
\psi_{l}
are Riesz products, by (
10
) and by the inequality of Latała (Theorem 1
[
10
]
, see also
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
).

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant\bigg|\frac{a_{1}^{\beta}}{a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{a_{n}^{\beta}}{a_{n}^{\alpha_{1}}}\bigg|+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigg|\frac{a_{k+1}^{\beta}}{a_{k+1}^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\bigg|.

From the definition of
a_{k}
(and the fact that
\langle\alpha_{1},\Lambda\rangle=\langle\beta,\Lambda\rangle
) we get

\bigg|\frac{a_{k+1}^{\beta}}{a_{k+1}^{\alpha_{1}}}-\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\bigg|\geqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}|b_{k}^{\langle\beta,\Gamma\rangle}-b^{\langle\beta,\Gamma\rangle}_{k+1}|\geqslant Cn^{\theta\cdot\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}|3^{\langle\beta,\Gamma\rangle}-1|.

From the definition of
\theta
we get

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant Cn^{1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}

and consequently

\left\|{D^{\beta}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant Cn^{1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}.

There exists
C>0
independent of
n
such that
|a_{k}|\leqslant 3^{Cn^{2}}
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
. Therefore
\operatorname{deg}W_{n}(x)\leqslant 3^{Cn^{2}}
and
\ln(\operatorname{deg}W_{n}(x))\leqslant Cn^{2}
. Hence for large enough
n
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|{D^{\beta}W_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant C(\ln\operatorname{deg}W_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\right)}.

From (
22
) and (
4
) we get

K\geqslant C(\ln\operatorname{deg}W_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\right)}.

Case II:
|\alpha_{1}|-|\beta|
is odd.

In this case

|G_{\beta,n}(x)|=\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}\sin(\langle a_{k},x\rangle)\psi_{k}(x)\bigg|.

By the definition of the sequence
a_{k}

\left|\frac{a_{k}^{\beta}}{a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}}-n^{-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}\right|=\left|\frac{\prod_{s=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor^{\beta(s)}}{\prod_{s=1}^{d}\lfloor n^{\theta\gamma_{j}}\rfloor^{\alpha_{1}(s)}}-n^{-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}\right|\leqslant\frac{C}{n^{\theta}}\cdot n^{-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}.

Therefore

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant n^{-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}\left\|{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sin(\langle a_{k},x\rangle)\psi_{k}(x)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}-\frac{C}{n^{\theta}}\cdot n^{1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}.

However

\begin{split}\left\|{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sin(\langle a_{k},x\rangle)\psi_{k}(x)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}&=\left\|{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\cos(\langle a_{k},x\rangle)-e^{i\langle a_{k},x\rangle}\right)\psi_{k}(x)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\\
&\geqslant\left\|{\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{i\langle a_{k},x\rangle}\psi_{k}(x)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}-\left\|{\psi_{n+1}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\\
&\geqslant\left\|{\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{i\langle a_{k},x\rangle}\psi_{k}(x)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}-1.\end{split}

The sequence
a_{k}
satisfies the assumptions of Meyer’s theorem (see Remark
6
). Because of that we can use Lemma 2 from
[
16
]
, which gives

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\langle a_{k},x\rangle}\psi_{k}(x)\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant Cn.

Thus

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant\left(C_{1}-\frac{C}{n^{\theta}}\right)n^{1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}.

Therefore for large enough
n
we get

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant Cn^{1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}}

and similarly as is in Case I we obtain

K\geqslant C(\ln\operatorname{deg}W_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\langle\beta-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1},\Gamma\rangle}\right)}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4

Proof.

We prove Theorem
4
in an analogous way. First we define the sequence
a_{k}
by the formula

a_{k}(j)=3^{\lambda_{j}2kn}(-1)^{\beps_{j}k}.

Once again we use the modified Riesz products (
11
) and the corresponding polynomials
W_{n}
(
13
). As in the proof of Theorem
2
we have

D^{\mu}W_{n}(x)=B_{\mu,n}(x)+G_{\mu,n}(x)

for any
\mu\in\{\beta,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}
and
B_{\mu,n}(x)
,
G_{\mu,n}(x)
defined as in (
15
) and (
16
). Since the sequence
a_{k}
has super-exponential growth

\frac{|a_{k}(j)|}{|a_{k+1}(j)|}\leqslant 3^{-2n},

we obtain the bounds on
B_{\mu,n}(x)

\left\|{B_{\mu,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant C

for any
\mu\in\{\beta,\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}
.
Note that by (
5
)

a_{k}^{\alpha_{j}}=3^{\langle\Lambda,\;\alpha_{j}\rangle}(-1)^{k\langle\beps,\;\alpha_{j}\rangle}=3^{\langle\Lambda\;,\;\alpha_{1}\rangle}(-1)^{k\langle\beps,\;\alpha_{1}\rangle}=a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}.

Hence by (
16
),

G_{\alpha_{j},n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}i^{|\alpha_{j}|-|\alpha_{1}|}\sum_{q\in A_{k}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}\left(e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}+(-1)^{|\alpha_{j}|-|\alpha_{1}|}e^{i\langle-q,x\rangle}\right).

Thus for
|\alpha_{1}|\equiv|\alpha_{j}|\mod 2
we get

\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left\|{-1+\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\cos\langle x,a_{k}\rangle\right)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left\|{R_{n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant 2

and for
|\alpha_{1}|\equiv|\alpha_{j}|+1\mod 2

\left\|{G_{\alpha_{j},n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left\|{-1+\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\sin\langle x,a_{k}\rangle\right)}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leqslant 2.

The only thing left to do is the estimate on the norm of
G_{\beta,n}
from below. By (
5
) we get

a_{k}^{\beta}=3^{\langle\Lambda,\;\beta\rangle}(-1)^{k\langle\beps,\;\beta\rangle}=3^{\langle\Lambda,\;\alpha_{1}\rangle}(-1)^{k(\langle\beps,\;\alpha_{1}\rangle+1)}=(-1)^{k}a_{k}^{\alpha_{1}}.

Therefore

G_{\beta,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}i^{|\beta|-|\alpha_{1}|}\sum_{q\in A_{k}}\frac{1}{2^{r(q)}}\left(e^{i\langle q,x\rangle}+(-1)^{|\beta|-|\alpha_{1}|}e^{i\langle-q,x\rangle}\right).

Let
g

g_{k}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(1+\cos\langle x,a_{k}\rangle\right),&\;\mbox{ for }|\beta|\equiv|\alpha_{1}|\mod 2,\\

Then

|G_{\beta,n}(x)|=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\left(g_{k+1}(x)-g_{k}(x)\right)\right|=\left|(-1)^{n-1}g_{n}(x)+g_{0}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}2(-1)^{k-1}g_{k}(x)\right|.

Applying Latała’s inequality (Theorem 1 in

]
) we obtain

\left\|{G_{\beta,n}}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\geqslant Cn.

(23)

As in previous section we get

K\geqslant C(\ln\operatorname{deg}W_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Remark 7
.

Actually to obtain estimate (
23
) we could use a weaker (random) form of Latała’s inequality (see Lemma 1 in
[
16
]
). However, to do this one needs to adjust the construction to the randomness of choice of signs, which significantly complicates the redaction.

Remark 8
.

The careful study of the above proofs shows that the source of sub-logarithmic growth of constant lies in super-exponential growth of the sequence
a_{k}
(see (
9
)). There are two reasons for significant growth of this sequence. In order to use the Latała’s inequality just geometrical growth would be enough (see
[
2
]
). However, we use a Riesz product as one of the derivatives involved in the proof. To recover the Riesz product structure for the remaining derivatives - which we need to use in the inequality of Latała - we perturbed the actual functions. Our method to control the arising error terms requires super-exponential growth of the sequence
a_{k}
. It seems that any improvement of this method would require a more delicate study of the aforementioned error terms.

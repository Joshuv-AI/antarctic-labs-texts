Positive Intermediate Ricci Curvature on Fibre Bundles
Philipp REISER a and David J. WRAITH b

Abstract
We prove a canonical variation-type result for submersion metrics with positive intermediate Ricci curvatures. This can then be used in conjunction with surgery techniques to establish the existence of metrics with positive intermediate Ricci curvatures on a wide range of examples which had previously only been known to admit positive Ricci curvature, such as highly connected manifolds and exotic spheres. Further, we extend results of the second author on the moduli space of metrics with positive Ricci curvature to positive intermediate Ricci curvatures.

Introduction

A basic problem in Riemannian geometry is to understand the class of manifolds which satisfy a given curvature condition, with positive curvature conditions having always played an important role. A fundamental question in this regard is as the following: given a fibre bundle in which both base and fibre (which we will assume to be closed manifolds) admit Riemannian metrics which satisfy a particular curvature condition, when does the total space admit such a metric? If the curvature condition is positive scalar or positive Ricci curvature, (and assuming the fibre metric is invariant under the action of a Lie structure group), the answer turns out to be “always”. The construction which establishes the existence of such total space metrics is called the
“canonical variation”
. One begins with a submersion metric on the total space with totally geodesic fibres. This is determined by the given base and fibre metrics, together with a choice of horizontal distribution. One then computes the effect on curvature of shrinking the fibre metrics by a uniform scaling factor. This computation, which rests on the O’Neill formulas for Riemannian submersions, shows that provided the scaling factor is small enough, the total space metric will satisfy the desired curvature condition, see, e.g.,
[
2
, Proposition 9.70]
.

It is clear that one can obtain a wealth of new examples in this way. Indeed, it is hard to overstate the importance of this construction in Riemannian geometry. For example, the first examples of Ricci positive metrics on exotic spheres were established by this method (see
[
23
,
24
]
), and Stolz’s celebrated classification result for simply-connected manifolds of positive scalar curvature depends on it in a crucial way
[
30
]
. Moreover, the canonical variation can often be combined with other techniques to produce powerful construction tools (see, for example,
[
36
]
and
[
25
]
, as well as examples later in this paper).

Given the centrality of the canonical variation in positive scalar and positive Ricci curvature geometry, it makes sense to ask for which other positive curvature conditions such a result will hold. Note that in the context of positive sectional curvature the analogous statement is not true: the situation here is much more complicated.

The focus of this paper is on positive
intermediate Ricci curvatures
. These constitute a natural family of positive curvature conditions which lie in-between positive Ricci and positive sectional curvature. We begin by recalling the definition of these curvatures.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a point
p
in a Riemannian manifold
M
, and a collection
v_{0},\dots,v_{k}
of orthonormal vectors in
T_{p}M
, the
k^{\rm th}
-intermediate Ricci curvature at
p
corresponding to this choice of vectors is defined to be
\sum_{i=1}^{k}K(v_{0},v_{i})
, where
K
denotes the sectional curvature.

Notice that for an
n
-dimensional manifold, these curvatures interpolate between the Ricci curvature and the sectional curvature as
k
ranges from
n-1
down to 1. We will denote the
k^{\rm th}
-intermediate Ricci curvature by
{\rm Ric}_{k}
.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold, and suppose that
(M,g)\xrightarrow{\pi}(B^{q},\check{g})
is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibres. Suppose that
(B,\check{g})
has
{\rm Ric}_{k_{1}}>0
, and that the fibres
\bigl(F_{b}^{p},\hat{g}_{b}\bigr)=\bigl(\pi^{-1}(b),g|_{\pi^{-1}(b)}\bigr)
for
b\in B

(
which are necessarily isometric
)
have
{\rm Ric}_{k_{2}}>0
. Then there exists
\tau=\tau(M,g,\pi)>0
such that for all positive
t<\tau
, scaling
g
in fibre directions by
t
yields a metric on
M
with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
for every
k\geq\max\{k_{1}+p,k_{2}+q\}
.

Although intermediate curvatures have been studied to some extent for several decades, recent years have seen a significant increase in interest. This perhaps reflects a realisation of their potential to provide a more full view of Riemannian curvature, and to cast the classical measures of curvature in a broader context which could ultimately shed more light on classical problems, such as the geometry of submanifolds (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
14
,
15
]
), symmetries (see, e.g.,
[
21
,
22
]
), synthetic notions of classical curvature conditions (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
) and topological implications (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
16
,
28
,
34
,
38
]
). Further, examples of manifolds with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
were constructed in the presence of symmetries in
[
1
,
8
]
, and by using surgery techniques in
[
26
]
. Nevertheless, it remains a difficult problem to construct metrics of
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
and examples are still rare, as one of the main difficulties lies in detecting when a metric satisfies such a condition. We will say a little more about our approach to this problem at the end of this section.

Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic fibres, as demanded by the hypotheses of Theorem
A
, are easy to construct. By a result of Vilms
[
32
]
(see also
[
2
, Theorem 9.59]
),
fibre bundles can be equipped with such structures under mild conditions. This immediately yields the following.

Corollary B
.

Let
M\xrightarrow{\pi}B^{q}
be a fibre bundle with fibre
F^{p}
and structure group
G
so that base and fibre are compact. Suppose that
B
admits a metric
\check{g}
with
{\rm Ric}_{k_{1}}>0
and
F
admits a
G
-invariant metric
\hat{g}
with
{\rm Ric}_{k_{2}}>0
. Then
M
admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
for every
k\geq\max\{k_{1}+p,k_{2}+q\}
.

Corollary
B
is a direct generalization of a result of Gromoll and Walschap for positive Ricci curvature
[
11
, Theorem 2.7.3]
, see also
[
4
,
23
,
24
]
.

We observe that Corollary
B
agrees precisely with the situation for Riemannian products, (see
[
8
, Proposition 2.2]
). Thus
twisting the fibres has no impact on the existence of positive intermediate Ricci curvatures
in this range.

In
[
26
]
, the authors considered Gromov’s bound on Betti numbers for manifolds with sectional curvature bounded below
[
12
]
. This was shown to fail for positive Ricci curvature by Sha and Yang
[
27
]
, and in
[
26
]
it was established that this failure continues for positive
k^{\rm th}
intermediate Ricci curvatures for
k
down to roughly the middle dimension. This result was proved by establishing a surgery theorem for positive intermediate Ricci curvatures. Taking surgery insights from
[
26
]
and combining them with Corollary
B
leads to the following.

Theorem C
.

Let
W
be the manifold obtained by plumbing linear disc bundles over spheres according to a simply-connected graph. For a fixed bundle in this graph denote the base dimension by
q+1
and the fibre dimension by
p+1
and suppose that
p,q\geq 2
. Then
\partial W
admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
for all
k\geq\max\{p,q\}+2
.

In short, Corollary
B
allows the basic
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
surgery result in
[
26
]
to be enhanced by allowing surgery to be performed using different trivialisations of the normal bundle, which results in Theorem
C
. This is analogous to the improvement of the basic Ricci positive surgery theorem in
[
27
]
achieved by the second author in
[
36
]
, and further enhanced by the first author in
[
25
]
. (For a detailed discussion of surgery with different trivialisations in the context of positive Ricci curvature, see, for example,
[
37
, Section 3]
.)

In
[
26
]
, the authors showed the existence of
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
metrics on the connected sums
\sharp_{r}S^{n}\times S^{m}
for
n,m\geq 2
with
n\neq m
, whenever
k\geq\max\{n,m\}+1
, and also on
\sharp_{r}S^{n}\times S^{n}
for
n\geq 3
when
k\geq n+2
. Using Theorem
C
, we can now greatly extend this collection of examples with the following, which generalize the positive Ricci curvature existence results of the second author
[
35
]
in the case of
(1)
and
(3)
below, and those of D. Crowley and the second author
[
5
]
in the case of
(2)
.

Theorem D
.

We have the following examples of manifolds with a metric of
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
:

(1)

Every homotopy sphere
\Sigma^{2m-1}
that bounds a parallelisable manifold admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
for
k\geq m+1
.

(2)

Let
M^{4m-1}
,
m\geq 2
, be a
(2m-2)
-connected manifold that is
(2m-1)
-parallelisable if
m\equiv 1\mod 4
. Then there is a homotopy sphere
\Sigma
so that
M\#\Sigma
admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
for
k\geq 2m+1
.

(3)

The unique exotic
8
-sphere admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{6}>0
, and the unique exotic
16
-sphere admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{10}>0
.

For
m=2,3
item
(2)
can be refined: In fact, every 2-connected 7-manifold admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{5}>0
and every 4-connected 11-manifold admits a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{7}>0
, cf.
[
5
, Corollary 5.4]
.

The examples in item
(3)
are exotic spheres which can be obtained by plumbing and do not bound a parallelisable manifold. Note that while we only listed the most well-known examples of this type, there exist further examples which provide additional applications of Theorem
C
.

For more than a decade, one of the major trends in Riemannian geometry has been the study of spaces and moduli spaces of Riemannian metrics satisfying various forms of positive curvature condition, see, e.g.,
[
31
]
and the references therein. In this direction, we consider moduli spaces of positive intermediate Ricci curvature metrics, and propose the following result.

Theorem E
.

Let
M
be any of the manifolds appearing in
(1)
of Theorem
D
with
m
even, or appearing in
(2)
in the following situations:
m
is odd, or
m
is even and the Pontryagin class
p_{\frac{m}{2}}(M)\in H^{2m}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is a torsion element. Then for any
k
for which Theorem
D
guarantees us a metric with
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
, the moduli space of
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
metrics on
M

(
in
(1))
, resp.
M\#\Sigma

(
in
(2))
, has infinitely many path-components.

In
[
20
]
, Kreck and Stolz showed that the positive sectional curvature (i.e.,
{\rm Ric}_{1}>0
) moduli space of certain
7
-dimensional manifolds has at least two path-components. Moreover in
[
7
]
, for each dimension
4n-1\geq 7
, infinite families of closed manifolds are constructed where the positive Ricci curvature (i.e.,
{\rm Ric}_{4n-2}>0
) moduli space has infinitely many path-components, and in
[
8
]
it is shown that these results in fact hold for
{\rm Ric}_{4n-3}>0
. Besides these results, to the best of our knowledge, Theorem
E
is the first result on moduli spaces for curvature conditions stricter than positive Ricci curvature.

To prove Theorem
E
, one needs to use the surgery techniques behind Theorem
C
together with ideas from
[
37
]
. However establishing this result turns out to require very delicate arguments, and its proof occupies a significant part of the sequel.

It was established independently in
[
6
,
10
]
that the moduli space of metrics of positive Ricci curvature on every total space
E^{7}
of a linear
S^{3}
-bundle over
S^{4}
such that
H^{4}(E)
is torsion has infinitely many path-components, and this result has been extended in
[
33
]
to all total spaces
E^{15}
of linear
S^{7}
-bundles over
S^{8}
such that
H^{8}(E)
is torsion. Since the metrics considered in
[
33
]
are submersion metrics as in Corollary
B
(or, more precisely, as in Corollary
3.1
below), we directly obtain that these results in fact hold for
{\rm Ric}_{5}>0
and
{\rm Ric}_{9}>0
, respectively. We note that this fact also follows from Theorem
E
since all these spaces are contained in
(2)
of Theorem
D
, and it follows from the proof of Theorem
D
that for these spaces the corresponding homotopy sphere appearing in Theorem
D
can be chosen to be a standard sphere.

In order to establish Theorem
A
and Corollary
B
, we study the following operator. For
X\in T_{p}M
, define

\displaystyle R_{X}\colon\ T_{p}M

\displaystyle\to T_{p}M,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\mapsto R(X,Y)X

(Note that, depending on the chosen sign convention for the curvature tensor, there exist different conventions to define this operator. See Section
2
for our choice of sign convention.)
It is easy to see that this operator is symmetric, so there exists an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Given orthonormal unit vectors
X
,
Y
, if
Y
is an eigenvector of
R_{X}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, then

\lambda=\langle R_{X}(Y),Y\rangle=K(X,Y).

Thus we can obtain
k^{\rm th}
intermediate Ricci curvatures by summing eigenvalues of
R_{X}
. More precisely, we have the following.

Lemma 1.2
.

A Riemannian manifold
M
has
{\rm Ric}_{k}>0
if and only if for every unit vector
X\in TM
, the sum of any
(k+1)
-eigenvalues of
R_{X}
is positive.

Note that this is essentially
[
26
, Lemma 2.6]
, and we refer the reader there for more details. (Of course
X
is an eigenvector of
R_{X}
with eigenvalue 0, and therefore
\langle R_{X}(X),X\rangle=K(X,X)=0
, hence the need to sum
k+1
eigenvectors to obtain a positive sum.) Theorem
A
is then obtained by a careful analysis of the eigenvectors of
R_{X}
.

This paper is laid out as follows: in Section
2
, we examine the canonical variation in the context of intermediate Ricci curvatures, and in Section
3
, we establish all the main theorems.

2
Curvature formulae

In this section, we present some formulas for the curvature tensor of a Riemannian submersion. We are interested in the case where the fibres are totally geodesic, and the effect the canonical variation has on the curvature tensor in this case. Recall that the canonical variation involves scaling the fibre metrics by a uniform constant, leaving the base metric and the horizontal distribution untouched. The case of particular interest is when the fibre scaling factor approaches zero. Note that the canonical variation preserves the property of being totally geodesic.

The starting point for the curvature formulas we will require is the O’Neill formulas for Riemannian submersions, as presented, for example, in
[
2
]
. As this book is the definitive reference for the curvature of Riemannian submersions and the canonical variation, we will adopt the conventions used there. In particular, note that for the canonical variation, we will scale in fibre directions by a constant
t>0

(
as opposed to the more usual
t^{2}
)
. We also use the following curvature tensor convention:

\langle R(V,W)X,Y\rangle=\bigl\langle\nabla_{W}\nabla_{V}X-\nabla_{V}\nabla_{W}X+\nabla_{[V,W]}X,Y\bigr\rangle,

where
\langle\,,\,\rangle
denotes the Riemannian metric.

The formulas below are due to O’Neill and Gray. They appear in
[
2
]
as Theorem 9.28. In stating these, we have specialized to the case of totally geodesic fibres, i.e., we have assumed that the
T
-tensor is identically zero. Recall that the
A
-tensor
of a Riemannian submersion is a
(2,1)
-tensor field which vanishes if and only if the horizontal distribution is integrable. We refer to
[
2
, Section 9.C]
for its definition and basic properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibres, let
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
Z^{\prime}
denote arbitrary horizontal vectors, and
U
,
V
,
W
,
W^{\prime}
arbitrary vertical vectors, all over the same base point. Then

\displaystyle\bigl\langle R(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle R(U,V)W,X\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle R(X,U)Y,V\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle+\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle R(U,V)X,Y\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle+\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle R(X,Y)Z,U\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{Z}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle,

\displaystyle\bigl\langle R(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle-2\bigl\langle A_{X}Y,A_{Z}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle+\bigl\langle A_{Y}Z,A_{X}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle A_{X}Z,A_{Y}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle.

Here,
\hat{R}
is the curvature tensor of the fibre metric, and
\check{R}
that of the base metric.

The next lemma is an adaptation of
[
2
, Lemma 9.69]
, where we only state the effect of the canonical variation on formulas which appear in Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a Riemannian submersion with fibre metric
\hat{g}
, we scale in fibre directions by
t>0
, that is, we take the new fibre metric to be
t\hat{g}
, but leave the base metric and horizontal distribution unchanged. Denoting quantities for the rescaled metric with a superscript
t
, and using the same vector notation as in Lemma
2.1
, the following formulas hold:

\displaystyle A^{t}_{X}Y=A_{X}Y,

\displaystyle A^{t}_{X}U=tA_{X}U,

\displaystyle[\langle(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z,U\rangle]^{t}=t\langle(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z,U\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle]^{t}=t\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle+\bigl(t-t^{2}\bigr)\bigl(\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle\bigr).

It can be deduced from the Koszul formula, for example, that scaling a metric
g
by a constant factor, i.e., replacing
g
by
t\cdot g
for
t>0
, leaves the Levi-Civita connection unchanged. Therefore given vectors
U
,
V
,
W
, we see immediately that
R(U,V)W
is unaffected by scaling the metric as well. Hence, in the case of arbitrary vertical vectors
U
,
V
,
W
,
W^{\prime}
, we then have

\bigl[\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=t\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle.

Further, since the metric is unchanged in horizontal directions, for horizontal vectors
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
Z^{\prime}
, we have

\bigl[\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle.

Combining these observations with Lemma
2.2
, a computation yields the following formulas, which show how the expressions Lemma
2.1
transform under the canonical variation.

Corollary 2.3
.

Under the same assumptions and with the same notation as the previous two lemmas, the following formulas hold:

\displaystyle\bigl[\bigl\langle R(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=t\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U,V)W,X\rangle]^{t}=0,

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,U)Y,V\rangle]^{t}=t\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle+\bigl(t-t^{2}\bigr)(\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle)

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(X,U)Y,V \rangle]^t =}{}+t^{2}\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(X,U)Y,V \rangle]^t }{}=t\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle+t\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle+\bigl(t^{2}-t\bigr)\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U,V)X,Y\rangle]^{t}=t\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle-t\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle+t^{2}\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-t^{2}\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(U,V)X,Y \rangle]^t =}{}+\bigl(t-t^{2}\bigr)(\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(U,V)X,Y \rangle]^t =}{}-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle+\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle)

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(U,V)X,Y \rangle]^t }{}=t\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle-t\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(U,V)X,Y \rangle]^t =}{}+\bigl(2t-t^{2}\bigr)(\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle),

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,Y)Z,U\rangle]^{t}=t\langle(\nabla_{Z}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle,

\displaystyle\bigl[\bigl\langle R(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!-2t\bigl\langle A_{X}Y,A_{Z}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!+t\bigl\langle A_{Y}Z,A_{X}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!-t\bigl\langle A_{X}Z,A_{Y}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle.

Let us fix the following notation. Let
g
denote the original (unscaled) Riemannian submersion metric, and denote by
g_{t}
the canonical variation metric corresponding to the vertical scaling by
t
. We now wish to specialize to the case where all vectors are unit vectors for
g_{t}
. Suppose that
U
,
V
,
W
,
W^{\prime}
are all unit vertical vectors for
g
(i.e., the case
t=1
), then the corresponding unit vectors for
g_{t}
are
U/\sqrt{t}
,
V/\sqrt{t}
,
W/\sqrt{t}
,
W^{\prime}/\sqrt{t}
. From Corollary
2.3
, we immediately obtain the expressions below.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
X
,
Y
,
Z
,
Z^{\prime}
are unit horizontal vectors for
g_{t}
. If
U
,
V
,
W
,
W^{\prime}
are all unit vertical vectors for
g
, and
U_{t}
,
V_{t}
,
W_{t}
,
W^{\prime}_{t}
the corresponding unit vectors for
g_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\bigl[\bigl\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})W_{t},W^{\prime}_{t}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=\frac{1}{t}\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)W,W^{\prime}\bigr\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})W_{t},X\rangle]^{t}=0,

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,U_{t})Y,V_{t}\rangle]^{t}=\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle+\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle+(t-1)\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})X,Y\rangle]^{t}=\langle(\nabla_{U}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\langle R(U_t,V_t)X,Y \rangle]^t =}{}+(2-t)(\langle A_{X}U,A_{Y}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}V,A_{Y}U\rangle),

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,Y)Z,U_{t}\rangle]^{t}=\sqrt{t}\langle(\nabla_{Z}A)_{X}Y,U\rangle,

\displaystyle\bigl[\bigl\langle R(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\bigr]^{t}=\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)Z,Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!-2t\bigl\langle A_{X}Y,A_{Z}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!+t\bigl\langle A_{Y}Z,A_{X}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle\!-t\bigl\langle A_{X}Z,A_{Y}Z^{\prime}\bigr\rangle.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\Psi
be a unit tangent vector with respect to
g_{t}
, i.e., we can write
\Psi=\lambda U_{t}+\mu X
with
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
so that
\lambda^{2}+\mu^{2}=1
. The map
R_{\Psi}^{t}
, according to the splitting into vertical and horizontal parts, is given by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{t}\hat{R}_{U}+O(t)&O(|\lambda|)+O\bigl(\sqrt{t}\bigr)\\
O(|\lambda|)+O\bigl(\sqrt{t}\bigr)&\mu^{2}\check{R}_{X}+O(|\lambda|)+O(t)\end{pmatrix}

as
t,\lambda\to 0
.

Here for a linear map
T_{\varepsilon}
that depends on a parameter
\varepsilon
we say that
T_{\varepsilon}=O(f(\varepsilon))
for a real function
f
, if the operator norm
\lVert T_{\varepsilon}\rVert
satisfies
\lVert T_{\varepsilon}\rVert=O(f(\varepsilon))
in the usual sense.

Proof.

We first consider the first entry of the matrix. Here, we have

\displaystyle[\langle R(\Psi,V_{t})\Psi,W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\lambda^{2}[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})U_{t},W_{t}\rangle]^{t}+\mu^{2}[\langle R(X,V_{t})X,W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\lambda\mu\bigl([\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})X,W_{t}\rangle]^{t}+[\langle R(X,V_{t})U_{t},W_{t}\rangle]^{t}\bigr).

By Corollary
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})U_{t},W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\frac{1}{t}\bigl\langle\hat{R}(U,V)U,W\bigr\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,V_{t})X,W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}X,W\rangle+t\langle A_{X}V,A_{X}W\rangle,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})X,W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=0,

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,V_{t})U_{t},W_{t}\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=0.

It can be checked that
\nabla_{V}A
enjoys the same antisymmetry property with respect to pairs of horizontal vectors as the
A
-tensor itself. Thus
\langle(\nabla_{V}A)_{X}X,W\rangle=0
. Since the
A
-tensor is bounded (as is any operator on a finite-dimensional vector space) and since
|\mu|\leq 1
, the first entry of the matrix follows.

Similarly, for the second entry we have

\displaystyle[\langle R(\Psi,V_{t})\Psi,Y\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\lambda^{2}[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})U_{t},Y\rangle]^{t}+\mu^{2}[\langle R(X,V_{t})X,Y\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\lambda\mu\bigl([\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})X,Y\rangle]^{t}+[\langle R(X,V_{t})U_{t},Y\rangle]^{t}\bigr).

Again, by Corollary
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})U_{t},Y\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=0,

\displaystyle[\langle R(X,V_{t})X,Y\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\sqrt{t}\langle(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}Y,V\rangle=O\bigl(\sqrt{t}\bigr),

and both
[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})X,Y\rangle]^{t}
and
[\langle R(X,V_{t})U_{t},Y\rangle]^{t}
are bounded as
t\to 0
. Hence, we obtain, again by using
|\mu|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle[\langle R(U_{t},V_{t})U_{t},Y\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=O\bigl(\sqrt{t}\bigr)+O(|\lambda|).

By the symmetry properties of
R_{\Psi}
, we obtain the same conclusion for the third entry.

Finally, for the fourth entry, we have

\displaystyle[\langle R(\Psi,Y)\Psi,Z\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=\lambda^{2}[\langle R(U_{t},Y)U_{t},Z\rangle]^{t}+\mu^{2}[\langle R(X,Y)X,Z\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\lambda\mu\bigl([\langle R(U_{t},Y)X,Z\rangle]^{t}+[\langle R(X,Y)U_{t},Z\rangle]^{t}\bigr)

By Corollary
2.4
, the terms
[\langle R(U_{t},Y)U_{t},Z\rangle]^{t}
,
[\langle R(U_{t},Y)X,Z\rangle]^{t}
, and
[\langle R(X,Y)U_{t},Z\rangle]^{t}
are bounded as
t\to 0
and

[\langle R(X,Y)X,Z\rangle]^{t}=\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)X,Z\bigr\rangle+O(t).

Hence, using
|\mu|,|\lambda|\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle[\langle R(\Psi,Y)\Psi,Z\rangle]^{t}

\displaystyle{}=O(|\lambda|)+\mu^{2}\bigl(\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)X,Z\bigr\rangle+O(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle{}=\mu^{2}\bigl(\bigl\langle\check{R}(X,Y)X,Z\bigr\rangle\bigr)+O(|\lambda|)+O(t).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be smooth functions such that
f_{1}(T)=f_{2}(T)
and
f_{1}^{\prime}(T)=f_{2}^{\prime}(T)

, then

\frac{f_{1}^{\prime}(t)}{f_{1}(t)}>\frac{f_{2}^{\prime}(t)}{f_{2}(t)}

for all
t>T
.

Proof.

The claim holds if and only if

f_{1}^{\prime}(t)f_{2}(t)-f_{2}^{\prime}(t)f_{1}(t)>0

for all
t>T
. Further, by the hypothesis, we have equality for
t=T
. Differentiation yields

\bigl(f_{1}^{\prime}f_{2}-f_{2}^{\prime}f_{1}\bigr)^{\prime}=f_{1}^{\prime\prime}f_{2}-f_{2}^{\prime\prime}f_{1},

which is positive on
(T,\infty)
.
∎

Assuming the curvature expressions (

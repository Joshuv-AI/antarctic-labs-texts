Kinetic theory of dilute granular gases having an inverse power law potential
Satoshi Takada

Abstract
The kinetic theory of dilute granular gases having an inverse power law repulsive potential is studied. We derive the time evolution of the temperature and the transport coefficients from the Boltzmann equation. We also investigate the linear stability analysis of the hydrodynamics, and study the softness dependence of the thresholds for the shear and heat modes against the restitution coefficient.



I
introduction

To understand the behavior of granular materials is important in both science and industry.
One of the most successful treatments of granular materials is the kinetic theory of dilute inelastic gases.
Among them, the kinetic theory of inelastic hard-core gases is well studied
Savage1981
;
Jenkins1983
;
Lun1984
;
Jenkins1985
;
Brey1998
;
Garzo1999
;
Huthmann2000
;
Brilliantov
.
When the homogeneous cooling state is realized, the decrease of the temperature due to inelastic collisions is proportional to the inverse square of the time in the later stage, which is known as the Haff’s law
Haff1983
.
Brey
et al.

Brey1998
have derived the transport coefficients for dilute inelastic hard-core gases and obtained the hydrodynamics, and later, Garzó and Dufty
Garzo1999
have derived those for moderately dense gases.
There are some studies considering hard-core gases with a velocity dependent
Poeschel2003
;
Takada2017
;
Takada2022
or a stochastic
Serero2015
restitution coefficient.
For the former system, the time evolution of the temperature was found to deviate from the Haff’s law
Poeschel2003
and the transport coefficients were also derived
Takada2017
;
Takada2022
.

One of the extensions of the kinetic theory to other potentials is that of repulsive hard-core with the square-well.
The transport coefficients for elastic system were derived almost seventy years ago
Holleran1951
;
Longuet-Higgins1958
;
Davis1961
;
Davis1965
;
Altenberger1975
;
Hirschfelder
, which is known to reproduce the temperature dependence of the shear viscosity of molecules such as nitrogen
Hirschfelder
.
Recently, this theory was extended to inelastic systems
Takada2015
.
In Ref.
Takada2015
, the kinetic theory was found to be invalid for the low temperature regime even if the inelasticity is sufficiently small.
In the low temperature regime, there appear some pairs of particles trapped into the attractive well, and the assumption of two body collisions may be violated.

Another extension is the kinetic theory for soft core particles.
Especially, there are a lot of papers studying elastic and inelastic Maxwell molecules
Bobylev2000
;
Ben-Naim2000
;
Ernst2002
;
Santos2003
;
Garzo2005
;
Khalil2014
;
Chapman
;
Hirschfelder
, where the intermolecular potential is given by inverse fourth power potential.
For Maxwell molecules, the collision frequency is known to be independent of the temperature, and in this case, the treatment of the Boltzmann equation becomes simpler.
Santos
Santos2003
has derived the transport coefficients for inelastic system and hydrodynamics up to Navier-Stokes order.
Later, Khalil
et al.

Khalil2014
have considered the generalized Maxwell model, where the temperature dependence of the collision frequency is generalized, and derived hydrodynamics of this model up to Burnett order.

In this paper, we try to construct the kinetic theory of dilute granular gases having an inverse power law repulsive potential and investigate the effect of softness on the potential.
For example, the intermolecular potentials of methane and hydrogen are approximately described by inverse sixth and inverse twelfth potentials, respectively, and the kinetic theory well reproduces the temperature dependence of the transport coefficients
Chapman
.
We also note that because the scattering process for this potential is analytically or numerically solvable depending on the exponent of the potential
Whittaker
;
GoldsteinMechanics
, we can expect to obtain simple expressions of the transport coefficients.
We also try to investigate the linear stability of the hydrodynamic equations and derive the restitution coefficient dependence of the thresholds of the wave number.

The organization of this paper is as follows:
In the next Section, we explain our model and study the homogeneous cooling state and derive the transport coefficients from the Boltzmann equation.
We also derive the hydrodynamics up to Navier-Stokes order and study the linear stability analysis.
In Sects.
III
and
IV
, we discuss and summarize our results.
In Appendix
A
, we briefly explain the scattering process for particles having an inverse power law potential.
In Appendix
B
, we define the Basic integral to make our calculations simpler.

II
Boltzmann equation

In this section, we study the homogeneous cooling state and derive the transport coefficients from the Boltzmann equation.
We also investigate the linear stability of this homogeneous state.
Let us consider the particles whose mass is
m
and whose interaction is given by the inverse
\alpha
law potential as

\displaystyle U(r)=\varepsilon\left(\frac{d}{r}\right)^{\alpha},

(1)

where
\varepsilon
represent the magnitude of the repulsive potential and
d
is the effective diameter of the particle.
For
\alpha\to\infty
, this potential reduces to the hard-core potential.
Let us consider the distribution function
f(\bm{r},\bm{v},t)
satisfying the Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\bm{v}_{1}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\right)f(\bm{r},\bm{v}_{1},t),=I(f,f)

(2)

with the collision integral

\displaystyle I(f,f)

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{v}_{2}\int d\hat{\bm{k}}\sigma(\chi,\bm{v}_{12})v_{12}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\left[\chi_{e}f(\bm{r},\bm{v}_{1}^{\prime\prime},t)f(\bm{r},\bm{v}_{2}^{\prime\prime},t)-f(\bm{r},\bm{v}_{1},t)f(\bm{r},\bm{v}_{2},t)\right],

(3)

where the relationship between the pre-collisional velocities
\bm{v}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\bm{v}_{2}^{\prime\prime}
and the post-collisional velocities
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bm{v}_{1}=\bm{v}_{1}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1+e}{2}(\bm{v}_{12}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\hat{\bm{k}})\hat{\bm{k}},\\
\displaystyle\bm{v}_{2}=\bm{v}_{2}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1+e}{2}(\bm{v}_{12}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\hat{\bm{k}})\hat{\bm{k}},\end{cases}

(4)

with
\bm{v}_{12}=\bm{v}_{1}-\bm{v}_{2}
,
\chi
is the deflection angle,
\sigma(\chi,\bm{v}_{12})
is the scattering cross section,
\chi_{e}
is related to the Jacobian of the transformation between
(\bm{v}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\bm{v}_{2}^{\prime\prime})
and
(\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2})
, and
e
is the restitution coefficient independent of the relative velocity.
It is noted that the relationship between pre-collisional and post-collisional velocities is more complicated for real situations, for example, acceleration or deceleration should be considered as discussed in Ref.
Takada2015
.
This means that the relationship (
4
) is a toy model.
In this paper, however, we focus on this collision rule even in this system.
This is because we can expect to obtain various features different from that of hard-core gases, and this can be regarded as a simple model of the approximation as discussed later.
Indeed, the inelastic Maxwell models (
\alpha=4
) are well studied.
We also note that the impact parameter and the velocity dependence of the scattering angle are discussed in Appendix
A
.

II.1
Homogeneous cooling state

We expand the velocity distribution function in terms of the Sonine polynomials as

f^{(0)}(\bm{v},t)=f_{\rm M}(\bm{V})\left[1+a_{2}S_{2}\left(\frac{mV^{2}}{2T}\right)\right],

(5)

where
V=|\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}|
is the local fluctuation of the velocity with the average velocity
\bar{\bm{v}}
,
f_{\rm M}(V)=n(m/2\pi T)^{3/2}\exp(-mV^{2}/2T)
is the Maxwell distribution function and
S_{2}(x)\equiv x^{2}/2-5x/2+15/8
is the Sonine polynomials
Brilliantov
;
Brey1998
;
Garzo1999
;
Takada2015
.
Let us define the
n
-th moment
\mu_{n}
as

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{1}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{2}\int\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{k}}\tilde{\sigma}(\chi,c_{12})c_{12}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\tilde{f}^{(0)}(\bm{c}_{1})\tilde{f}^{(0)}(\bm{c}_{2})\Delta[c_{1}^{n}+c_{2}^{n}],

(6)

where
\bm{c}_{i}\equiv\bm{v}_{i}/v_{\rm T}
with the thermal velocity
v_{\rm T}=\sqrt{2T/m}
,
\chi
is the deflection angle,
\tilde{f}(\bm{c}_{i})=(v_{\rm T}(t)^{3}/n)f(\bm{v}_{i})
with the number density
n

\Delta[\psi(\bm{c})]\equiv\psi(\bm{c}^{\prime})-\psi(\bm{c})
is the difference between pre- and post-collisional velocities.
With the aid of the Basic integral shown in Appendix
B
, we can obtain the second and fourth moments as

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{T}\right)^{2/\alpha}\Gamma\left(3-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)(S_{1}+a_{2}S_{2}),

(7a)

\displaystyle\mu_{4}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{T}\right)^{2/\alpha}\Gamma\left(3-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)(T_{1}+a_{2}T_{2}),

(7b)

where the explicit forms of
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
T_{1}
and

are given by Eqs. (
45
) and (
47

A_{\alpha,j}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tilde{b}\hskip 1.99997pt\tilde{b}\cos^{j}\theta_{\alpha}

(8)

(the detailed derivation is shown in Appendix
B
).
These two moments are related to each other as
(4/3)\mu_{2}\langle c^{4}\rangle=\mu_{4}
with
\langle c^{4}\rangle=(15/4)(1+a_{2})

Brilliantov
.
From this relationship with Eqs. (
7
), we can evaluate the coefficient
a_{2}
as

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{1}-5S_{1}}{5S_{1}+5S_{2}-T_{2}},

(9)

where the explicit form is not shown here because of its complicated form.
It is noted that because the quantities
A_{\alpha,2}
and
A_{\alpha,4}
are both pure numbers, we can numerically evaluate them as calculated in the previous studies
Chapman1922
;
Chapman
;
Resibois
.
It should be noted that for
\alpha\to\infty
,
A_{\alpha,2}
and
A_{\alpha,4}
reduce to
1/4
and
1/6
, respectively, and then
a_{2}
reduces to that for hard-core gases
a_{2}^{(\rm HC)}=16(1-e)(1-2e^{2})/[81-17+30e^{2}(1-e)]

.
Figure
1
shows the restitution coefficient dependence of

.
The homogeneous cooling state of this model deviates from the Maxwell distribution function more than that of inelastic hard-core model for small
e
, and the deviation becomes smaller and smaller for large
\alpha
.
We also note that
a_{2}
for
\alpha=6
and
14
becomes negative for small inelasticity.
This behavior is qualitatively similar to the case for hard-core gases because
a_{2}^{(\rm HC)}
is negative for
e>1/\sqrt{2}
.
On the other hand,
a_{2}
for
\alpha=4

.

Let us derive the time evolution of the temperature for homogeneous cooling state.
From the second moment of the Boltzmann equation, the time evolution is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\zeta T,

(10)

with
\zeta=(2/3)nd^{2}\sqrt{2T/m}\mu_{2}
.
For
\alpha\neq 4
, Eq. (
10
) can be solved analytically with the initial condition
T=T_{0}
at
t=0
as

T(t)=\frac{T_{0}}{\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{t}{\tau_{\alpha}}\right)^{2\alpha/(\alpha-4)}},

(11)

where the characteristic time
\tau_{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{4(\alpha-4)}{3\alpha}nd^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T_{0}}{m}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{T_{0}}\right)^{2/\alpha}A_{\alpha,2}\Gamma\left(3-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\left(1+\frac{3\alpha^{2}-16\alpha+16}{16\alpha^{2}}a_{2}\right)(1-e^{2}).

(12)

For
\alpha=4
, the time evolution is given by

T(t)=T_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_{4}}\right),

(13)

with

\tau_{4}^{-1}=2\pi nd^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{m}}A_{4,2}(1-e^{2}),

(14)

whose dependence on the time is consistent with the previous study
Ernst2002
.
It should be noted that
\tau_{4}
is independent of
a_{2}
because
S_{2}
becomes zero for
\alpha=4
.
For
\alpha>4
, the exponent of the denominator in Eq. (
11
),
2\alpha/(\alpha-4)
, is larger than
2
for hard-core gases, which means that the decrease of the temperature for this system is faster than that for inelastic hard-core gases in the later stage of the evolution.

II.2
Transport coefficients

New, let us derive the transport coefficients from the Boltzmann equation.
We can obtain a set of first-order hydrodynamic equations from the Boltzmann equation (
2
) as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot(n\bm{u})=0,

(15a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{u}}{\partial t}+\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}+\frac{1}{nm}\bm{\nabla}\cdot P=0,

(15b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}+\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\nabla}T+\frac{2}{3n}(P:\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{q})+\zeta T=0.

(15c)

Here,
n=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{v}f(\bm{v},t)
,
\bm{u}=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{v}\bm{v}f(\bm{v},t)
,
T=(1/3)\int\mathrm{d}\bm{v}mv^{2}f(\bm{v},t)
,
P
, and
\bm{q}
are the density field, the flow velocity field, the granular temperature field, the pressure tensor, and the heat flux respectively.
To close the equations, we put the constitutive relations as

\displaystyle P_{ij}

\displaystyle=p\delta_{ij}-\eta\left(\nabla_{i}u_{j}+\nabla_{j}u_{i}-\frac{2}{3}\delta_{ij}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u}\right),

(16a)

\displaystyle\bm{q}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\bm{\nabla}T-\mu\bm{\nabla}n,

(16b)

respectively, where
p=nT
is the static pressure,
\eta
is the shear viscosity,
\kappa
is the heat conductivity, and
\mu
is the coefficient of the heat flux proportional to the density gradient.
To determine the microscopic expressions of the transport coefficients, let us expand the distribution function as

f=f^{(0)}+\epsilon f^{(1)}+\cdots,

(17)

where
f^{(0)}
is the homogeneous solution (
5
), and
\epsilon
is the small parameter relating to the gradients of the flow fields.
We also expand the time derivative as
\partial/\partial t=\partial^{(0)}/\partial t+\epsilon\partial^{(1)}/\partial t+\cdots
.
Using the same procedure as the ordinary Chapman-Enskog method
Brey1998
;
Garzo1999
;
Takada2015
,
we can write the differential equation of the shear viscosity
\eta

Brilliantov
;
Takada2015
as

-\frac{2}{3}T\mu_{2}\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial T}-\frac{2}{5}\Omega_{\eta}^{e}\eta=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{mT}{2}},

(18)

where the quantity
\Omega_{\eta}^{e}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Omega_{\eta}^{e}

\displaystyle\equiv\int\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{1}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{c}_{2}\int\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{k}}\tilde{\sigma}(\chi,c_{12})c_{12}\phi(c_{1})\phi(c_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\times\left[1+a_{2}S_{2}(c_{1}^{2})\right]\tilde{D}_{ij}(\bm{c}_{2})\Delta\left[\tilde{D}_{ij}(\bm{c}_{1})+\tilde{D}_{ij}(\bm{c}_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{T}\right)^{2/\alpha}\Gamma\left(4-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right)\left(\omega_{\eta,1}+a_{2}\omega_{\eta,2}\right),

(19)

where
\omega_{\eta,1}
and
\omega_{\eta,2}
are given by Eqs. (
50
), respectively (the detailed derivation is shown in Appendix
B
).
The left hand side of Eq. (
18
) scales as
\eta T^{-2/\alpha}
while the right hand side scales as
T^{1/2}
, which means that the shear viscosity should scale as
\eta\sim T^{(\alpha+4)/(2\alpha)}
.
Then,
T(\partial\eta/\partial T)=(\alpha+4)/(2\alpha)\eta
is satisfied.

(21)

under the linear approximation with respect to
a_{2}
.
It is noted that

II.3
Linear stability analysis

In this subsection, let us perform linear stability analysis for homogeneous cooling state of this system.
Using the constitutive relations (
16
), we can rewrite a set of hydrodynamic equations (
15
) as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot(n\bm{u})=0,

(27a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{u}}{\partial t}+\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\bm{u}=-\frac{1}{nm}\bm{\nabla}p+\frac{\eta}{nm}\left[\nabla^{2}\bm{u}+\frac{1}{3}\bm{\nabla}(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u})\right],

(27b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}+\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\nabla}T

\displaystyle=-\zeta T+\frac{2}{3n}\left(\kappa\nabla^{2}T+\mu\nabla^{2}n\right)-\frac{2}{3n}p(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u})

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{2}{3n}\eta\left[(\nabla_{i}u_{j})(\nabla_{j}u_{i})+(\nabla_{j}u_{i})(\nabla_{i}u_{j})-\frac{2}{3}(\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{u})^{2}\right].

(27c)

We linearize these equations around the homogeneous cooling state, and only consider the linear term with respect to the density
\rho\equiv\delta n/n_{\rm H}
,
\bm{w}\equiv\bm{u}/v_{\rm T}
, and
\theta\equiv\delta T/T_{\rm H}
, where
\delta n
and
\delta T
are the fluctuations around the homogeneous state of the density and the temperature, respectively, and
n_{\rm H}
and
T_{\rm H}
are the density and the temperature of homogeneous system, respectively.
We also define dimensionless time and space variables
\tau
and
\hat{\bm{r}}
as
\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\nu_{\rm H}(t^{\prime})
and
\hat{\bm{r}}=(2\nu_{\rm H}(t)/v_{\rm T}(t))\bm{r}

III
Discussion

Let us compare our results for
\alpha=4
with those reported in the previous studies.
In Ref.
Khalil2014
, the generalized inelastic Maxwell model was studied, where the temperature dependence of the collision frequency is
T^{\gamma}
.
They obtained the explicit forms of the transport coefficients for this system, and for example, the temperature dependence of the shear viscosity is given by
T^{1-\gamma}
.
For inelastic Maxwell model, the collision frequency is independent of the temperature, which means
\gamma=0
.
Putting
\gamma=0
into the shear viscosity derived in Ref.
Khalil2014
, its temperature dependence becomes linear, which is consistent with our result (
20
) for
\alpha=4
.
It is also noted that if we choose
\gamma=(\alpha-4)/2\alpha
in Ref.
Khalil2014
, the temperature dependence of the transport coefficients is qualitatively consistent with that of our results for arbitrary
\alpha
, while the quantitative agreement is poor because of the ignorance of the scattering processes.

Figure 7:
Velocity dependence of the restitution coefficient for the inverse power law potential with the damping term (
34
) for
\zeta_{\rm vis}=\sqrt{d^{2}/m\varepsilon}
(red solid line) and
2\sqrt{d^{2}/m\varepsilon}
(blue dashed line), where
v^{*}\equiv v\sqrt{m/\varepsilon}
.

For
\alpha<4
, the time evolution of the temperature changes drastically.
In this case, the characteristic time
\tau_{\alpha}
and the exponent of the denominator in Eq. (
11
) becomes negative,
which means that our theory is only valid for
t\leq-\tau_{\alpha}(>0)
because the temperature becomes zero at
t=-\tau_{\alpha}
and thereafter all particles stop.
This singular behavior comes from the choice of the model of inelasticity.
As mentioned in Sec.
II
, our model is oversimplified model, because we ignore the velocity and the angle dependence of the restitution coefficient.
In actual systems, the energy dissipation is characterized by core collisions or deformations of the surface
Dufty2008
.
For example, this can be modeled by the following force acting on the
i
-th particle:

\bm{F}_{i}=-\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{\nabla}_{i}U(|\bm{r}_{ij}|)-\zeta_{\rm vis}\sum_{j\neq i}\Theta(d-|\bm{r}_{ij}|)(\bm{v}_{ij}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}_{ij})\hat{\bm{r}}_{ij},

(34)

instead of the constant restitution coefficient (
4
), where
\Theta(r)
is a step function,
\bm{r}_{ij}
and
\bm{v}_{ij}
is the relative position and velocity between particles, respectively,
\hat{\bm{r}}_{ij}=\bm{r}_{ij}/|\bm{r}_{ij}|
, and
\zeta_{\rm vis}
is the dissipation rate.
Figure
7
shows the initial velocity dependence of the restitution coefficient when two particles collide head-on.
In this case, the energy dissipation happens only when the particle overcomes the repulsive force
\sim r^{-(\alpha+1)}
of the other particle and there is a threshold for the speed of the particles.
This means that when the temperature is sufficiently small, almost all particles cannot approach because of the strong repulsive forces and does not lose energy.
Thus, the oversimplification of the inelasticity results in the singular behavior of the temperature evolution.
As shown in Fig.
7
, however, our simple model (
4
) can be regarded as a good approximation when the average velocity of the particles are sufficiently high.
Quantitatively, we need the modification of the treatment, but this is a future work.

IV
Summary

In this paper, we have derived the transport coefficients of granular gases having an inverse power law potential.
We have investigated the homogeneous cooling state and found that the decrease of the temperature is proportional to
t^{-2\alpha/(\alpha-4)}
for
\alpha\neq 4
in the long time limit and exponential to the time for
\alpha=4
.
We have also derived the transport coefficients using the Chapman-Enskog theory and obtained the explicit temperature dependence of them.
The linear stability analysis has shown the divergence of the threshold for the heat mode for small restitution coefficient.
We have also found that the behaviors of the thresholds for the shear and heat modes are opposite with respect to the softness of the potential.

Appendix A
Scattering processes

In this Appendix, we briefly summarize the scattering processes and show its explicit form for some exponents.
Let us consider a scattering process in which two particles approach from far away with relative velocity
\bm{v}
and leave with the relative velocity
\bm{v}^{\prime}
after the scattering in the frame that the target is stationary.
The incident angle
\theta
between
\bm{v}
and the normal unit vector
\hat{\bm{k}}
at the closest distance
r=r_{\rm min}
between colliding particles is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=b\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\displaystyle\sqrt{1-b^{2}u^{2}-\frac{4}{mv^{2}}U(1/u)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\displaystyle\sqrt{1-x^{2}-\left(\frac{x}{\tilde{b}}\right)^{\alpha}}},

(35)

where
\tilde{b}=(mv^{2}/4\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}b/d
and
x_{0}=bu_{0}
with
u_{0}=1/r_{\rm min}
which is the smaller one of the positive solutions of the inner of the denominator in Eq. (
35
).
It should be noted that
\theta_{\alpha}
is only a function of
\tilde{b}
and we can evaluate Eq. (
35
) for arbitrary exponent
\alpha
.
We also note that Eq. (
35
) is analytically solvable for some
\alpha
.
For
\alpha=4
, Eq. (
35
) reduces to

\theta_{4}=\frac{\tilde{b}}{(\tilde{b}^{4}+4)^{1/4}}K\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\tilde{b}^{2}}{2\sqrt{\tilde{b}^{4}+4}}\right),

(36)

with the complete elliptic integral of the first kind
K(k)

Abramowitz
.
For
\alpha=6
, Eq. (
35
) can be rewritten in terms of
y=1/x^{2}-1/3
as

\displaystyle\theta_{6}

\displaystyle=\int_{1/x_{0}^{2}-1/3}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\displaystyle\sqrt{4y^{3}-\frac{4}{3}y-\frac{8}{27}-\frac{4}{\tilde{b}^{6}}}}

\displaystyle\equiv\wp^{-1}(e_{2};g_{2},g_{3})=\omega_{2},

(37)

where
\wp^{-1}(z;g_{2},g_{3})
is the inverse Weierstrass’
\wp

Abramowitz
with the two invariants

and
g_{3}=8/27+4/\tilde{b}^{6}

e_{2}\equiv 1/x_{0}^{2}-1/3
, and
\omega_{2}
is the half period relating to
e_{2}
.
For
n=14

35
) because this integral cannot be solved analytically.

Dijet Acoplanarity in CUJET3 as a Probe of the Nonperturbative Color Structure of QCD Perfect Fluids
M. Gyulassy, P.M. Jacobs, J. Liao, S. Shi, X.N. Wang, F. Yuan

Abstract
Using the CUJET3=DGLV+VISHNU jet-medium interaction framework, we show that dijet azimuthal acoplanarity in high energy A + A A+A collisions is sensitive to possible non-perturbative enhancement of the jet transport coefficient, q ^ ​ ( T , E ) \hat{q}(T,E) , in the QCD crossover temperature T ∼ 150 − 300 T\sim 150-300 MeV range. With jet-medium couplings constrained by global RHIC& LHC χ 2 \chi^{2} fits to nuclear modification data on R A ​ A ​ ( p T > 20 ) ​ GeV R_{AA}(p_{T}>20)\;{\rm GeV} , we compare predictions of the medium induced dijet transverse momentum squared, Q s 2 ∼ ⟨ q ^ ​ L ⟩ ∼ Δ ​ ϕ 2 ​ E 2 Q_{s}^{2}\sim\langle\hat{q}L\rangle\sim\Delta\phi^{2}E^{2} , in two models of the temperature, T T , and jet energy E E dependence of the jet medium transport coefficient, q ^ ​ ( T , E ) \hat{q}(T,E) . In one model, wQGP, the chromo degrees of freedom (dof) are approximated by a perturbative dielectric gas of quark and gluons dof. In the second model, sQGMP, we consider a nonperturbative partially confined semi-Quark-Gluon-Monopole-Plasma with emergent color magnetic dof constrained by lattice QCD data. Unlike the slow variation of the scaled jet transport coefficient, q ^ w ​ Q ​ G ​ P / T 3 \hat{q}_{wQGP}/T^{3} , the sQGMP model q ^ s ​ Q ​ G ​ M ​ P / T 3 \hat{q}_{sQGMP}/T^{3} features a sharp maximum in the QCD confinement crossover T T range. We show that the dijet path averaged medium induced azimuthal acoplanarity, Δ ​ ϕ 2 \Delta\phi^{2} , in sQGMP is robustly ∼ 2 \sim 2 times larger than in perturbative wQGP. even though the radiative energy loss in both models is very similar as needed to fit the same R A ​ A R_{AA} data. Future A+A dijet acoplanarity measurements constrained together with single jet R A ​ A R_{AA} and v n v_{n} measurements therefore appears to be a promising strategy to search for possible signatures of critical opalescence like phenomena in the QCD confinement temperature range.

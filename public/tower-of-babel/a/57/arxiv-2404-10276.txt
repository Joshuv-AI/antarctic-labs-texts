CMC hypersurface with finite index in hyperbolic space ℍ 4 \mathbb{H}^{4}
Han Hong

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that there are no simply-connected complete noncompact constant mean curvature hypersurfaces with the mean curvature H > 1 H>1 and finite index satisfying subexponential end growth in hyperbolic space ℍ 4 \mathbb{H}^{4} . A more general nonexistence result can be proved in a 4 4 -dimensional Riemannian manifold with certain curvature conditions. We also show that 4 4 -manifold with Ric > 1 \operatorname{Ric}>1 does not contain any simply-connected complete noncompact minimal stable hypersurface with subexponential end growth. The proof relies on the harmonic function theory developed by Li-Tam-Wang and the μ \mu -bubble initially introduced by Gromov and further developed by Chodosh-Li-Stryker in the context of stable minimal hypersurfaces.

Introduction

The well-known Bernstein problem has been completely settled due to works of W. Fleming
[
28
]
, De Giorgi
[

]
, F. Almgren
[

]
, J. Simons

, and E. Bombieri, De Giorgi, and E. Giusti
[

. It states that a complete minimal graph over

for

must be hyperplane. As a natural generalization, it was proved by D. Fischer-Colbrie and R. Schoen
[
26
]
, M. do Carmo and C. Peng
[
22
]
and A. Pogorelov
[

independently that the only two-sided stable complete minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are planes. R. Schoen, L. Simon and S. Yau
[
38

proved the higher dimensional stable Bernstein theorem for
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for

under the Euclidean volume growth condition. Note that recently C. Bellettini generalized it to
\mathbb{R}^{7}
in
[

]
.

We recall that a two-sided minimal immersion
M^{n}
into a Riemannian manifold
X^{n+1}
is stable if for any compactly supported function
\varphi
on
M
, we have

\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq\int_{M}(|A_{M}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\nu,\nu))\varphi^{2}

or the Jacobi operator
J=\Delta_{M}+|A_{M}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{N}(\nu,\nu)
is nonnegative. We say that the hypersurface
M
has finite index if
\sup_{i}\operatorname{Ind}(\Omega_{i})<\infty
where
\{\Omega_{i}\}
are compact subsets of
M
exhausting
M
.
M
is stable if
\operatorname{Ind}(\Omega_{i})=0
for any
i
.

In the last few years, there were some exciting progress toward the stable Bernstein problem in Euclidean space. O. Chodosh and C. Li
[
14
]
eliminated the volume growth condition in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
by using level set method, thus proving the 4-dimensional stable Bernstein theorem. G. Catino, P. Mastrolia and A. Roncoroni
[

]
provided a different proof by using the conformal method and the volume comparison under the nonnegative 2-Bakry-Emery-Ricci curvature. Later, O. Chodosh and C. Li
[

]
discovered a new method in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
based on the theory of the
\mu
-bubble due to M. Gromov
[
30
]
. This idea was also successively applied to
\mathbb{R}^{5}
[
11
]
and
\mathbb{R}^{6}
[
35
]
. Thus the stable Bernstein problem remains open in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
.

The stability concept also makes sense for constant mean curvature (CMC) hypersurfaces. Let
M^{n}\subset X^{n+1}
be a CMC hypersurface, we say that it is weakly stable if

\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq\int_{M}(|A_{M}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\nu,\nu))\varphi^{2}

for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
satisfying

\int_{M}\varphi=0.

This stems from the second derivative of area functional under the compactly supported variation that preserves the enclosed volume. That
M
has finite index can defined same as the minimal case above.

H. Alencar and M. do Carmo
[
1
]
proved that a complete CMC hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with finite index and with polynomial volume growth must be minimal. Since entire CMC graphs are stable and of polynomial volume growth, their result generalized S. Chern’s theorem
[
10
]
. The polynomial growth was relaxed to sub-exponential growth later by M. do Carmo and D. Zhou
[
23
]
. Thus M. do Carmo posed the question
[
24
]
: Is a complete noncompact weakly stable (or generally finite index) constant mean curvature hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, where
n\geq 3
, necessarily minimal ? This question had already been answered positively for
n=2
(see
[
18
]
). Later, the answer was proved to be positive for
n=3,4
(by M. Elbert, B. Nelli and H. Rosenberg
[
25
]
, as well as independently by X. Cheng
[
9
]
, both using a generalized Bonnet-Meyer’s argument). Assuming the volume entropy of
M
is zero, S. Ilias, B. Nelli and M. Soret
[
31
]
provided a positive answer to M. do Carmo’s question in all dimensions. Thus, M. do Carmo’s question remains open in higher dimensions.

The hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature one in hyperbolic space behave akin to minimal hypersurfaces in Euclidean space (see
[
7
]
). Thus, regarding do Carmo’s question, it is natural to ask: Does a complete noncompact constant mean curvature hypersurface
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 2
, with mean curvature
H\geq 1
and finite index necessarily have mean curvature
1
?

In this paper, we answer the question positively in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{4}
under an extra condition. Specifically, we prove

Theorem 1.1
.

There is no simply-connected complete noncompact constant mean curvature hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
satisfying subexpoenntial end growth and

H_{M}>1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \operatorname{Ind}(M)<\infty.

This is equivalent to that simply-connected complete CMC hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{4}

satisfying three
conditions in the theorem are compact. The subexponential end growth is a condition that describes the growth of the number of components outside compact subsets of the hypersurface (see Def
3.3
). The subexponential end growth can be replaced by a stronger assumption that
M
has finitely many ends. The hypothesis of the mean curvature in the theorem is sharp since the horosphere is a hypersurface of constant mean curvature one and is stable, naturally of finite index. It is worth mentioning that there exist rotational spherical catenoids with finite total curvature and finite index in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
with
0\leq H<1
and they are complete noncompact
[
21
,
5
]
. In fact, we prove a more general nonexistence theorem in 4-dimensional Riemannian manifolds (see Theorem
5.1
).

Previous to our result, this was proved by A. Da Silveira
[
18
]
in dimension
n+1=3
without any extra condition. For higher dimensions, there are only partial results. X. Cheng
[
9
]
proved that any complete finite index hypersurface in hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{4}
and
\mathbb{H}^{5}
with constant mean curvature
H
satisfying
H^{2}>\frac{10}{9}
and
H^{2}>\frac{7}{4}
, respectively, must be compact. These two numbers were improved to
\frac{64}{63}
in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
and
\frac{175}{148}
in
\mathbb{H}^{5}
by Q. Deng
[
20
]
. As mentioned before, the dimension restriction and strange mean curvature assumption come from the Bonnet-Meyer’s type method and appear a lot in the study of the structure of CMC hypersurfaces. In fact, CMC hypersurface
M
in general ambient Riemannian manifold with following assumption (see
[
20
,
8
]
)

\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-biRic}\geq-\frac{4k^{2}-n+1}{4((k-1)n+1)}n^{2}H_{M}^{2}

for
k\in(\frac{n-1}{n},\frac{4}{n-1})
and
n=3,4,5
usually owns compactness or connectedness at infinity. This method seems to prevent the generalization to higher dimensions and the optimal mean curvature. On the other hand, in higher dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, S. Ilias, B. Nelli and M. Soret
[
31
]
solved this question for all

but assuming zero volume entropy of

Our idea of proof is different and uses different assumptions from zero volume entropy assumption for
\mathbb{H}^{4}
. The proof relies on the harmonic function theory on manifolds and the volume growth estimate based on the warped
\mu
-bubble technique. We outline the proof as follows.

•

M
is of finite index, thus we consider a component of the complement of a large compact subset (a metric ball of large radius) of
M
. It produces a stable manifold
E
with compact boundary. This implies that
E
has positive first Laplacian spectrum.

•

E
is nonparabolic manifold with compact boundary.
We apply the volume growth of nonparabolic end by P. Li and J. Wang. The assumption of subexponential end growth ensures that the growth rate of the number of connected components in an annulus
(B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R})\cap E
is less than exponential growth.

•

The warped
\mu
-bubble technique can be used to obtain an almost linear volume growth on stable single-end CMC hypersurface
M
with
H_{M}>1
in hyperbolic space, or even more general manifolds with the following
k
-weighted scalar curvature condition (Theorem
4.4
):

\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+(6k+6)H_{M}^{2}\geq 2\epsilon_{0}

for a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
and
k\in[1/2,2]
.
This part restricts our dimension to
n=4
. The result follows from the contradiction on volume growth of ends (Theorem
5.1
).

The volume growth of the end is also studied in
[
12
]
under nonnegative Ricci curvature condition.

One interesting consequence of Theorem
5.1
is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X^{4}
be a Riemannian manifold with
\operatorname{Ric}_{X}\geq 1
. There is no simply-connected complete noncompact minimal hypersurface
M^{3}
with finite index (including stable case) and subexponential end growth.

Again we can replace the subexponential end growth condition by finite ends. This theorem can be thought as a noncompact analogue of the well-known result: stable closed minimal hypersurface does not exist in a space with positive Ricci curvature.

Remark 1.3
.

The subexponential growth condition is important. The following example is provided by Chodosh-Li-Stryker
[
13
, Appendix A.2]
. Consider
M_{0}=(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\#(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
with a scalar flat metric
h_{0}.
The universal cover
\tilde{M}
of
M_{0}
has a positive lower bound
\epsilon
for its essential spectrum. Consider
X=M_{0}\times(-\delta,\delta)
with metric
h_{t}=h_{0}+t^{2}(\operatorname{Ric}_{h_{0}}-2\epsilon h_{0})+dt^{2}
for sufficiently small
\delta
and
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Then
\operatorname{Ric}_{X}\geq\epsilon
and
\tilde{M}
is a complete noncompact stable minimal (in fact, totally geodesic) hypersurface in
X
. However, it seems that the end growth of
\tilde{M}
is not subexponential.

Remark 1.4
.

The manifold
X
in last remark is not complete. Chodosh-Li-Strker raised a question that whether completeness of the ambient manifold is important in proving nonexistence of complete noncompact stable minimal hypersurface in space with positive Ricci curvature. We provide a different assumption for this question instead of completeness.

2.
Preliminary

Parabolicity and nonparabolicity are important concepts regarding complete noncompact manifolds. It has been thoroughly
studied by using harmonic function theory in
[
33
]
. We recall some basics for convenience of readers.

Definition 2.1
.

A manifold
M
is parabolic if it does not admit a symmetric, positive Green’s function. Otherwise it is nonparabolic.

The notion of parabolicity can be localized at an end of the manifold. Let
\Omega
be a compact subset of
M
, an end
E
with respect to
\Omega
is simply an unbounded component of
M\setminus\Omega
. An end
E
is parabolic if it does not admit a positive harmonic function
f
satisfying

f|_{\partial E}=1\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ f|_{E^{\circ}}<1.

Otherwise, it is nonparabolic.
A complete noncompact
M
is nonparabolic if and only if
M
has a nonparabolic end. It is also possible for a nonparabolic manifold to have many parabolic ends. The compact set
\Omega
is usually set to be a geodesic ball with large radius
R
and centered at point
p
. Hereafter, without ambiguity, we simply write
B_{R}
instead of
B_{R}(p).

Let
E_{0}
be one component of
M\setminus B_{R_{0}}
and
L
be a positive number. One then considers the sequence of harmonic functions defined on
E_{k}=E_{0}\cap B_{kL+R_{0}}
satisfying

\begin{cases}f_{k}=0&\text{on}\ E_{0}\cap\partial B_{kL+R_{0}}\\
f_{k}=1&\text{on}\ \partial E_{0}\end{cases}

Notice that
E_{0}=\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{k}
. We also often use
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to denote the end
E_{0}
. An end
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with respect to
B_{R_{0}}
is nonparabolic if and only if
f_{k}
converges uniformly and locally to a nonconstant function
f
defined on
E_{0}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
f\equiv 1
when it is parabolic.

We first prove

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be a complete CMC hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with finite index and the scalar (averaged) mean curvature satisfies
H_{M}>1
. Then
M
has no parabolic ends.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is noncompact, otherwise we are done. Since
M
has finite index, it is well-known that outside a compact subset
\Omega
of
M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M\setminus\Omega}(|\mathring{A}|^{2}+nH_{M}^{2}-n)\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq n(H_{M}^{2}-1)\int_{M\setminus\Omega}\varphi^{2}

for
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M\setminus\Omega)
. Choose
p\in M
and
R_{0}>0
such that
B_{p}(R_{0})
contains
\Omega
. Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an end with respect to
B_{p}(R_{0})
.

Fix
k_{0}>1
. Let
\varphi
be a cut-off function such that

\varphi=1\ \text{on}\ E_{k_{0}+\ell}\setminus E_{k_{0}},\ \ \varphi=0\ \text{on}\ \partial E_{0},\ \text{and}\ |\nabla\varphi|\leq C

where
C
is independent of
\ell
. Plug
\varphi f_{k}
into above inequality, since
f_{k}
is harmonic, we obtain by integration of by parts

\displaystyle(nH_{M}^{2}-n)\int_{E_{0}}(\varphi f_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{0}}|\nabla(\varphi f_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{0}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}f_{k}^{2}.

If
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is parabolic, then
f_{k}\rightarrow 1
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Above inequality turns

(nH_{M}^{2}-n)|E_{k_{0}+\ell}\setminus E_{k_{0}}|\leq C|E_{k_{0}}|

for any
\ell>0
. This implies that volume of the end
\{E_{k}\}
is finite.

On the other hand, the volume of each end (parabolic or nonparabolic) of a CMC hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry is infinite. Hyperbolic space has bounded geometry. Thus all ends of
M
are nonparabolic if there exist ends, completing the proof.
∎

As we can see from the proof, a more general nonexistence theorem holds in Riemannian manifolds with bounded geometry.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a complete CMC hypersurface in
(X,g)
with finite index and the scalar mean curvature satisfies
H_{M}>1
. Assume that
nH_{M}^{2}+\inf_{M}\operatorname{Ric}_{X}>0
and
X
has bounded geometry. Then
M
has no parabolic ends.

Note that a manifold being nonparabolic is weaker than saying that it has no parabolic ends.

3.
Parabolicity and nonparabolicity of manifolds

In this section, we present the volume growth of the nonparabolic ends of a manifold.

We first recall some classical results by P. Li and J. Wang. Let
E_{0}
be an end of the complete manifold
M
. Let
\lambda_{1}(E_{0})
denote the infimum of the spectrum of the Laplacian on
E
with Dirichlet boundary condition, it can be characterized by

\lambda_{1}(E_{0})=\inf_{f\in W^{1,2}_{c}(E_{0})}\frac{\int_{E_{0}}|\nabla f|^{2}}{\int_{E_{0}}f^{2}}

where the infimum is taken over all compactly supported functions in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{c}(E_{0})
. Here the compact support allows test functions to be nonzero on
\partial E_{0}
. We can take any compact exhaustion
\Omega_{i}
of
E_{0}
, such that
\lambda_{1}(E_{0})=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{i}).

Theorem 3.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
E_{0}
be an end of complete manifold with
\lambda_{1}:=\lambda_{1}(E_{0})>0
. If
E_{0}
is nonparabolic end, then
E_{0}
must have exponential volume growth given by

|(B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R})\cap E_{0}|\geq Ce^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}R}

for large
R
and some constant
C
depending on the end.

In fact, by following the proof, we can see that the eventual estimate is

|(B_{R+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}}\setminus B_{R})\cap E_{0}|\geq C\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}R}

where
C
is a universal constant depending on
E_{0}.

We write this result in our terminology as follows. Let
L
be a fixed positive number which will be determined in later sections.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
E_{0}
is a nonparabolic end and
\lambda_{1}:=\lambda_{1}(E_{0})>0
, denote
\ell=\left\lfloor L\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\right\rfloor
, then

|(B_{(k+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}})\cap E_{0}|\geq C(L,\lambda_{1})e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}(kL+R_{0})}

where
C(L,\lambda_{1})
is a constant depending on
L,\lambda_{1}
and the end
E_{0}.

Proof.

We divide
(B_{(k+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}})\cap E_{0}
into
\ell
slices and estimate

\displaystyle|(B_{(k+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}})\cap E_{0}|

\displaystyle\geq|(B_{(kL+R_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}})\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}})\cap E_{0}|

\displaystyle+\cdots

\displaystyle+|(B_{(kL+R_{0}+\ell\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}})\setminus B_{(kL+R_{0}+(\ell-1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}})\cap E_{0}|

Estimating each slice by using Theorem
3.1
and summing them all proves the Lemma.
∎

3.1.
Subexponential end growth

Again note that it is possible (when
M
has infinitely many ends) that as
k
increases, the number of components of
M\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}}
strictly increases. Thus we define the growth of ends in terms of
k
.

Definition 3.3
.

For fixed
R_{0}>0
and
L>0
, denote
N_{M,k}
to be

N_{M,k}:=\text{the number of connected components of}\ M\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}}.

We say the
M
has subexponential end growth if

\limsup_{k\rightarrow}\frac{\ln N_{M,k}}{k}=0,

equivalently,

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N_{M,k}}{e^{Ck}}=0

for any constant
C>0.

This definition is independent of center point of the balls, so we ignore it. We localize the growth at one end. If
M
has subexponential end growth, then there exists one component
E
of
M\setminus B_{R_{0}}
such that the number of connected components of
E\setminus B_{kL+R_{0}}
has subexponential growth.

4.
\mu
-bubble, diameter estimate and volume bound

In this section, we observe that the theory of the warped
\mu
-bubble used in
[
13
]
can yield volume bound for ends of CMC hypersurface in Riemannian manifold with possibly negative curvature.

Let
M^{n}
be a hypersurface immersed in
X^{n+1}
with the unit normal vector field
\bar{\nu}
and
\Sigma^{n-1}
be a hypersurface in
M^{n}
the unit normal vector field
\nu
. Let
R_{X},R_{\Sigma}
be the scalar curvatures of
X,\Sigma
respectively. Let
\operatorname{Ric}_{X},\operatorname{Ric}_{M}
denote the Ricci curvature of
X,M
respectively. Let
A_{M}
and
A_{\Sigma}
denote the second fundamental form of
M
in
X
and
\Sigma
in
M
respectively. Suppose
H_{M}
and
H_{\Sigma}
are the scalar mean curvature of
M
in
X
and
\Sigma
in
M
respectively, i.e., the average of the trace of
A_{M}
and
A_{\Sigma}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given an
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(X^{n+1},g)
, where
n\geq 2
, and one unit tangent vector
V\in T_{p}X
at
p
, the
k
-weighted scalar curvature in the direction
V
at
p
is defined by

\operatorname{k-R_{X}}(V):=\operatorname{Ric}(e_{1},e_{1})+\cdots+\operatorname{Ric}(e_{n},e_{n})+(2k-1)\operatorname{Ric}(V,V)

where
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n},V\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}M.

When
k=1
,
\operatorname{k-R_{X}}
is just the Scalar curvature of
X.
It can also be written as
\operatorname{k-R_{X}}(V)=R_{X}+2(k-1)\operatorname{Ric}(V,V).

Proposition 4.2
.

Under above setting, there hold

R_{X}-R_{\Sigma}+H_{M}^{2}+H_{\Sigma}^{2}=2\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+|A_{M}|^{2}

and for any
k\geq\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle k\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+k|A_{M}|^{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{1}{2}(n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+\frac{n(2k+n-1)}{2}H_{M}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}.

Proof.

It follows from the definition directly. Choose a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n+1}\}
at a point
p
in
X
such that
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
is a local orthonormal frame for
\Sigma
and
\nu=e_{n},\bar{\nu}=e_{n+1}
. Let
h^{M}_{ij}
and
h^{\Sigma}_{ij}
be the scalar second fundamental form of
A_{M}
and
A_{\Sigma}
respectively. Let
R^{X},R^{M},R^{\Sigma}
be the curvature tensor of
X,M,\Sigma
respectively. Thus by the Gauss equation repeatedly,

\displaystyle R_{X}-2Ric_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}R^{X}_{ijij}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(R^{M}_{ijij}-h^{M}_{ii}h^{M}_{jj}+(h^{M}_{ij})^{2})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}R^{M}_{ijij}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}R^{M}_{inin}+|A_{M}|^{2}-n^{2}H_{M}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-1}R^{M}_{ijij}+2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A_{M}|^{2}-n^{2}H_{M}^{2}

\displaystyle=R_{\Sigma}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-(n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}+2\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A_{M}|^{2}-n^{2}H_{M}^{2}.

Rearranging terms completing the first equation. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(R_{X}-R_{\Sigma}+n^{2}H_{M}^{2}+(n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-|A_{M}|^{2})

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(R_{X}-2\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}((n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}+n^{2}H_{M}^{2}-|A_{M}|^{2}-R_{\Sigma})

Then by definition,

\displaystyle k\operatorname{Ric}_{X}

\displaystyle(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+k|A_{M}|^{2}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{1}{2}R_{X}+(k-1)\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu}))+(k-\frac{1}{2})|A_{M}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}n^{2}H_{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+\left(n(k-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}n^{2}\right)H_{M}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)^{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}.

This completes the proof of the second equation.
∎

4.1.
Closed
\mu
-bubble

We first prove a diameter bound for the warped
\mu
-bubble in ends of CMC hypersurface with finite index. This uses the theory of
\mu
-bubble introduced by M. Gromov and applied to stable minimal hypersurfaces in 4-manifolds by O. Chodosh, C. Li and D. Stryker
[
13
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X^{4},g)
be a complete 4-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
M^{3}\subset X^{4}
be a stable two-sided CMC hypersurface with the compact boundary component
\partial M
and of the mean curvature
H_{M}
. Let

\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+(3k+3)H_{M}^{2}\geq 2\epsilon_{0}

for a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
and some
k\in[1/2,2]
. Suppose that there exists
p\in M
such that
d_{M}(p,\partial M)\geq L/2
for a positive constant
L>8\pi/\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}
. Then there exists a relatively open set
\tilde{\Omega}\subset B^{M}_{L/2}(\partial M)
and a smooth surface
\Sigma
(possibly disconnected) such that
\Sigma\cup\partial M=\partial\tilde{\Omega}
and the diameter of each component of
\Sigma
is at most
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}
.

Proof.

This is from the technique of
\mu-
bubble that has been studied and applied in many settings. Since
M
is (strongly) stable, we have that for any compactly supported function
\varphi
on
M
,

\int_{M}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-(\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+|A_{M}|^{2})\varphi^{2}\geq 0.

It follows that there exists a positive function
u\in C^{\infty}(M\setminus\partial M)
such that

(4.1)

\Delta_{M}u+(\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+|A_{M}|^{2})u\leq 0.

When
M
is compact, the above conclusion is trivial. Otherwise, it can be obtained by following D. Fischer-Colbrie and R. Schoen’s paper
[
26
]
.
The rest of the proof uses the second variation of the warped
\mu
-bubble and diameter estimate (see
[
13
]
).

Let
\rho_{0}
be a smoothing of the distance function
d(x,\partial M)
such that
|\nabla\rho_{0}|\leq 2
. For a fixed small
\epsilon>0
, define

h(x)=-\frac{4\pi}{L/2-2\epsilon}\tan(\rho)

where

\rho=\frac{(\rho_{0}-\epsilon)\pi}{L/2-2\epsilon}-\frac{\pi}{2}.

We use functions
u
and
h
to construct
\mu
-bubble
\Sigma
(possibly disconnected) and the domain
\tilde{\Omega}
enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\partial M
. Let
\Omega_{0}=\{x\in M:\epsilon<\rho_{0}<\frac{L}{4}\}
and
\Omega_{1}=\{x\in M:\epsilon<\rho_{0}\leq\frac{L}{2}-\epsilon\}
. For any
k\in[1/2,2]
, consider

\mathscr{A}(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u^{k}-\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})u^{k}h

for Caccioppoli sets
\Omega
such that
\Omega\Delta\Omega_{0}
is compactly supported in
\Omega_{1}^{\circ}.
There exists a smooth minimizer, still denoted by
\Omega
, of
\mathscr{A}(\cdot)
since
u^{k}
is positive. For simplicity, let
\Sigma
be one of the component of
\partial\Omega
contained in
M^{\circ}.

The critical surface
\Sigma
satisfies

3H_{\Sigma}=-k\frac{D_{\nu}u}{u}+h.

And the nonnegative second derivative of
\mathscr{A}(\Omega)
at the critical point yields that for any function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}u^{k}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi|^{2}-ku^{k-1}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u\geq\int_{\Sigma}-ku^{k-1}\varphi^{2}\Delta_{M}u+(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu))u^{k}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}u^{k}D_{\nu}h+k\varphi^{2}u^{k-1}hD_{\nu}u-k(k-1)u^{k-2}\varphi^{2}(D_{\nu}u)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}(-k\frac{\Delta_{M}u}{u}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{9}{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2})u^{k}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}(h^{2}+2D_{\nu}u)u^{k}\varphi^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}k^{2}-k(k-1))u^{k-2}\varphi^{2}(D_{\nu}u)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}(-k\frac{\Delta_{M}u}{u}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{9}{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2})u^{k}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}(h^{2}+2D_{\nu}h)u^{k}\varphi^{2}.

Let
\varphi=u^{-k/2}\phi
, plugging it in the left-hand side of the above equation, by divergence theorem and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}u^{k}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\varphi|^{2}-ku^{k-1}\varphi^{2}\Delta_{\Sigma}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma}(1+\frac{k}{2\varepsilon})|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}+(k(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-1)+\frac{k^{2}}{4})u^{-2}\phi^{2}|\nabla^{\Sigma}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{4-k}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}

by choosing
\varepsilon=2-\frac{k}{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{4}{4-k}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}(-k\frac{\Delta_{M}u}{u}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{9}{2}H_{\Sigma}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2})\phi^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{0}+h^{2}+2D_{\nu}h)\phi^{2}.

The last term is nonnegative if
L
satisfies

\epsilon_{0}+h^{2}+2D_{\nu}h\geq\epsilon_{0}-\left(\frac{4\pi}{L/2-2\epsilon}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

that is

L\geq 2(\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}+2\epsilon).

Using equation (
4.1
) and Proposition
4.2
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-\frac{4-k}{4}(|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{9H_{\Sigma}^{2}}{2}+k\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\bar{\nu},\bar{\nu})+k|A_{M}|^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2})\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-\frac{4-k}{4}(\frac{1}{2}\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+(3k+3)H_{M}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}-\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2})\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-\frac{4-k}{4}(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}-\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2})\phi^{2}

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.
Thus there exists a positive function
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

\Delta v+\frac{4-k}{4}(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}-K_{\Sigma})v\leq 0.

We can not directly apply the diameter estimate in
[
16
, Lemma 16]
due to the coefficient in front of
K_{\Sigma}.
However, we can still follow the idea of
[
16
, Lemma 16]
to show

\operatorname{diam}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}.

In fact, assume that there exists
p,q\in M
with
d(p,q)>\frac{2\sqrt{2}\pi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}
. Following
[
16
, Lemma 16]
, there is
h>0
such that
\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+D^{\Sigma}_{\nu}h>0
. Let
b=\frac{4}{4-k}
. Consider

\mathscr{A}(K)=\int_{\gamma}v^{b}-\int_{K}(\chi_{K}-\chi_{K_{0}})hv^{b}

where
K_{0}
is the reference Caccioppoli set
B_{\epsilon}(p)\subset K_{0}\subset\Sigma\setminus B_{\epsilon}(q).

The minimizing closed
\mu
-curve
\gamma
exists in
\Sigma
. By conducting similar calculation as in (
4.2
) to
\gamma
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}v^{b}|\nabla^{\gamma}\varphi|-bv^{b-1}\varphi^{2}\Delta_{\gamma}v

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\gamma}(-b\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma}v}{v}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\gamma}^{2}+K_{\Sigma})v^{b}\varphi^{2}+\frac{b}{2}(2-b)u^{b-2}\varphi^{2}(D_{\nu}v)^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+D_{\nu}h)v^{b}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\gamma}(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+D_{\nu}h)v^{b}\varphi^{2}+\int_{\gamma}\frac{b}{2}(2-b)u^{b-2}\varphi^{2}(D_{\nu}v)^{2}.

Plugging
\varphi=v^{\frac{2}{k-4}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\frac{4(k-3)}{(4-k)^{2}}v^{-2}|\nabla^{\gamma}v|^{2}\geq\int_{\gamma}(\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}h^{2}+D_{\nu}h)+\int_{\gamma}\frac{8-4k}{(4-k)^{2}}v^{-2}(D^{\Sigma}_{\nu}v)^{2}

leading to a contradiction for any
k\in[1/2,2]
.

Let
\tilde{\Omega}=\Omega\cup\{\rho_{0}\leq\epsilon\}.
We complete the proof.

∎

4.2.
Volume bound for slices in an end

In this subsection, we apply the diameter of
\mu
-bubble to estimate the volume of the slice in an end.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X^{4},g)
be a complete 4-dimensional Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry and let
M
be a noncompact simply-connected CMC hypersurface in
X
with finite index. Suppose

\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+(6k+6)H_{M}^{2}\geq 2\epsilon_{0}

for a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
and some
k\in[1/2,2]
. Let
R_{0}
be large such that
M\setminus B_{R_{0}}(p)
is stable where
p
is a fixed point on
M
and
E_{0}
be an end of
M
with respect to
B_{R_{0}}(p)
. Let
\{F_{ij}\}_{j=1}^{i_{j}}
be the collection of finite components of
E_{0}\cap(B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}(p)\setminus B_{iL+R_{0}}(p))
where
L>0
is taken from Theorem
4.3
and
i_{j}
is the number of components. Then there is a universal constant
C>0
such that

|F_{ij}|\leq C

for any
i
and any
j\in{1,\cdots,i_{j}}
.

Proof.

First note that
E_{0}
is stable. We claim that for any compact stable compact subset
\Omega\subset E_{0}
immersed in
X^{4}
, it satisfies

\sup_{x\in\Omega}|A_{\Omega}|(x)\min\{1,d_{\Omega}(x,\partial\Omega)\}\leq C

for a constant
C=C(X,g)
. When
M
is complete (
\partial\Omega=\emptyset
), this has been shown by H. Rosenberg, R. Souam and E. Toubiana
[
37
]
in dimension
2
. If
M
is
3
-dimensional minimal immersion, this was proven by O. Chodosh, C. Li and D. Stryker
[
13
]
. The proof of the claim follows in a similar manner as in
[
13
]
by using the standard point-picking argument. If the claim is not true, there exists a sequence of CMC immersions
\Omega_{i}
and a sequence of points
p_{i}\in\Omega_{i}\setminus\partial\Omega_{i}
such that
|A_{\Omega_{i}}|(p_{i})\min\{1,d_{\Omega_{i}}(p_{i},\partial\Omega_{i})\}\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. This means that
|A_{\Omega_{i}}|(p_{i})\rightarrow\infty
. The next step is to dilate the metric by
|A_{\Omega_{i}}|(p_{i})
and study the convergence of
\Omega_{i}
. Note that
|A|
at
p_{i}
under the new metric is one. The rest of proof follows as in
[
13
]
. The only difference in our case is that we are dealing with CMC hypersurface with nonzero mean curvature. However, notice that the dilations by
|A_{\Omega_{i}}|
of CMC sequence will be complete minimal hypersurface. Hence, again we can use stable Bernstein theorem for minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
to obtain contradiction in the point-picking argument. Thus we have proven that
|A|
of
E_{0}
is uniformly bounded by the constant that only depends on
X.

First let us remark that the set
\{F_{ij}\}
for any fixed
i
may contain two types of connected components. The first type consists of connected components that have boundary components belonging to both
\partial B_{iL+R_{0}}
and
\partial B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}
. The second type consists of connected components that only have boundary components belonging to
\partial B_{iL+R_{0}}
. The result of volume estimate holds for both types as follows.

For any fixed
i
and
j
, we apply Theorem
4.3
to
F_{ij}
of first type. Note that
\partial F_{ij}=(\partial F_{ij}\cap\partial B_{iL+R_{0}})\cup(\partial F_{ij}\cap\partial B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}).
We assume
(\partial F_{ij}\cap\partial B_{iL+R_{0}})=\partial M
in Theorem
4.3
. We denote the corresponding warped
\mu
-bubble surfaces by
\Sigma_{ij}
. Note that
\Sigma_{ij}
may not be connected. Since the hypersurface
M
is simply-connected, then each
F_{ij}
is simply connected and the union of
F_{mj}
over all
m<i
and
1\leq j\leq m_{j}
is simply-connected. We claim that each
F_{ij}
has uniformly bounded diameter.

For the component
F_{ij}
, there must exist only one component in
\{F_{(i-1)j}\}
that shares a common boundary portion as
F_{ij}.
Denote the overlapped boundary by
\partial_{1}F_{ij}=(\partial F_{ij}\cap\partial B_{iL+R_{0}})
. The uniqueness is guaranteed by simply-connectedness. Denote this component by
F^{*}_{(i-1)j}
and denote its corresponding
\mu
-bubble by
\Sigma^{*}_{(i-1)j}
. Again
\Sigma^{*}_{(i-1)_{j}}
may not be connected. It is not hard to see that there must exist a component, denoted by
\Sigma^{**}_{(i-1)j}
, in
\Sigma^{*}_{(i-1)j}
which separates
\partial_{1}F_{ij}
and
\partial F^{*}_{(i-1)j}\cap\partial B_{(i-1)L+R_{0}}.
For any two points
x,y
in
F_{ij}
, we connect them to the center
p
by minimizing geodesics
\tau
and
\gamma
, respectively. By the separating property of
\Sigma^{**}_{(i-1)j}
,
\tau
and
\gamma
both intersect
\Sigma^{**}_{(i-1)j}
at
x^{*}
and
y^{*}
, respectively. Then lengths of curves
\tau_{xx^{*}}
and
\gamma_{yy^{*}}
are bounded by
2L
. Here
\tau_{xx^{*}}
denotes the portion of
\tau
connecting
x
and
x^{*}.

By triangle inequality and diameter estimate in Theorem
4.3
, we know that diameter of
F_{ij}
satisfies

d_{F_{ij}}(x,y)\leq 4L+\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}.

If
F_{ij}
is the second type, by following the same steps we obtain above diameter bound. The curvature estimate above implies that
F_{k}
has uniform bounded second fundamental form. On the other hand, the Guass equation yields

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(e_{1},e_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}R_{X}(e_{1},e_{i},e_{1},e_{i})+nh_{11}H-\sum_{i=1}^{3}h_{1i}^{2}.

Since
X
has bounded geometry, the curvature of
X
is uniformly bounded. Thus the Ricci curvature of
M
is uniformly bounded from below. Hence, by volume
comparison theorem and bounded diameter of
F_{ij}
, the volume of
F_{ij}
has a uniform upper bound.

∎

5.
Proof of main results

In this section we prove the main theorem. In fact, we prove a more general theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

A simply-connected complete constant mean curvature hypersurface
M
in
(X^{4},g)
with the bounded geometry satisfying subexponential end growth and

H_{M}^{2}+\inf_{M}\operatorname{Ric}_{X}\geq\delta

for positive constant
\delta
and

\operatorname{Ind}<\infty

must be compact.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
4.4
. Indeed, on the contrary, we assume that there exists a simply-connected complete noncompact hypersurface
M^{3}
in
X^{4}
satisfying subexponential end growth, finite index condition and the curvature condition in the theorem. By Lemma
2.3
, all ends of
M
are nonparabolic. This is ensured solely by the curvature condition and finite index. Notice that the number of nonparabolic ends can be infinite.

It is well-known that for finite index CMC hypersurface, there exists a comapact subset such that the hypersurface is strongly stable outside (see
[
27
]
). Take a nonparabolic end
E
with respect to the compact subset such that
E
is stable. Since
M
satisfies subexponential end growth condition and is simply-connected, we can assume that
E
satisfies

\text{the number of connected components of}\ E\cap(B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{iL+R_{0}})=o(e^{Ci})

for any
C>0.

Note that
3H_{M}^{2}+\inf_{M}\operatorname{Ric}\geq\delta
implies the curvature assumption in Lemma
4.4
for some
k\in[1/2,2]
. In fact, it implies that
12H_{M}^{2}+\inf_{M}R_{X}\geq 4\delta
and by the definition of
\operatorname{k-R_{X}}
, we have that

\inf_{M}\operatorname{k-R_{X}}+(6k+6)H_{M}^{2}\geq 2(k+1)\delta.

Thus we can apply Lemma
4.4
to
E
. Then every component in
E\cap(B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{iL+R_{0}})
has uniformly bounded volume. Thus

|E\cap(B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{iL+R_{0}})|=o(e^{Ci})

for any
C>0.

However, since
E
is stable, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E}|\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}(|A_{M}|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\nu,\nu))\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Sigma}(3H_{M}^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}_{X}(\nu,\nu))\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\delta\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}

which says that
\lambda_{1}(E)>0.

According to Lemma
3.2
,
E
satisfies exponential volume growth:

|E\cap(B_{(i+1)L+R_{0}}\setminus B_{iL+R_{0}})|\geq C\cdot e^{Ci}

for a constant
C=C(\delta)
depending on
\delta.

This leads to a contradiction.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

In general, whether finite index of CMC hypersurface directly implies finite ends and first Betti number is unknown. For minimal hypersurfaces in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, this is true and proved by O. Chodosh, D. Maximo
[
17
]
and C. Li
[
32
]
, respectively.

6.
A question in hyperbolic space

In this section we raise a question that is related to the topic of this paper. The question is to
classify complete noncompact weakly stable constant mean curvature hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{4}
with the mean curvature
H\geq 1
.

In the following we provide some motivations.
Although the weak stability (see the introduction) is usually defined for nonzero constant mean curvature hypersurface while stability is defined for minimal hypersurface, it still makes sense to define weakly stable minimal hypersurface. We turn our discussion back to Euclidean space first. The generalized stable Bernstein problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
can be stated as: Classify complete noncompact weakly stable two-sided constant mean curvature hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for
3\leq n+1\leq 7
. This can be divided into two parts.

(1)

do Carmo’s question: Complete weakly stable (or more generally finite index) constant mean curvature hypersurface with nonzero mean curvature must be compact ? As mentioned in the introduction, this was proved for
n+1=3,4
.

(2)

Complete noncompact weakly stable two-sided minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for
3\leq n+1\leq 7
must be hyperplane ?

In fact, we have the following result that follows from O. Chodosh and C. Li
[
14
,
15
]
, G. Catino, P. Mastrolia and A. Roncoroni
[
29
]
, O. Chodosh, C. Li, P. Minter and D. Stryker
[
11
]
as well as L. Mazet
[
35
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a weakly stable complete noncompact CMC hypersurface (two-sided if it is minimal) in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
for
n=3,4,5
. Then
M
must be a hyperplane. In particular, complete weakly stable CMC hypersurface with nonzero mean curvature in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
must be compact.

Proof.

We show that the curvature estimate

|A_{M}|(x)d_{M}(x,\partial M)\leq C

holds for weakly stable compact CMC hypersurface
M
. Otherwise, we use the standard point-picking argument to get a limiting hypersurface
M_{\infty}
which is a complete noncompact, two-sided, weakly stable minimal immersion into
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|A_{M_{\infty}}|(0)=1
and the bounded second fundamental form. It is known that the area of
M_{\infty}
is infinity and by the Gauss equation,

\operatorname{Ric}_{M_{\infty}}(e_{1},e_{1})=-h_{11}H+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}h^{2}_{1i},

so the Ricci curvature of
M_{\infty}
has a lower bound. By the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem, we have that
|B_{R+1}|\leq C|B_{R}|
. Thus according to L. Barbosa and P. Berard
[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
, the weak index and strong index of
M_{\infty}
coincide, so
M_{\infty}
must be stable. Hence, by stable Bernstein theorem mentioned above,
M_{\infty}
is a hyperplane, contradicting with the second fundamental form at origin.

The curvature estimate by letting
\partial M
diverge to infinity implies that
M
must be hyperplane.
∎

The question raised in the hyperbolic space at the beginning of this section can be analyzed in a similar manner. We partially answer this question in the current paper for
H>1
.

Abstract
Under a general categorical procedure for the extension of dual equivalences as presented in this paper’s predecessor, a new algebraically defined category is established that is dually equivalent to the category 𝐋𝐊𝐇𝐚𝐮𝐬 \bf LKHaus of locally compact Hausdorff spaces and continuous maps, with the dual equivalence extending a Stone-type duality for the category of extremally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff spaces and continuous maps. The new category is then shown to be isomorphic to the category 𝐂𝐋𝐂𝐀 \bf CLCA of complete local contact algebras and suitable morphisms. Thereby, a new proof is presented for the equivalence 𝐋𝐊𝐇𝐚𝐮𝐬 ≃ 𝐂𝐋𝐂𝐀 op {\bf LKHaus}\simeq{\bf CLCA}^{\rm op} that was obtained by the first author more than a decade ago. Unlike the morphisms of 𝐂𝐋𝐂𝐀 \bf CLCA , the morphisms of the new category and their composition law are very natural and easy to handle.

Facts 2.1
.

(The Stone duality)
.
The functor
{\sf CO}:{\bf Top}\longrightarrow{\bf Boo}^{{\rm op}}

assigns to every topological space
X

its Boolean algebra
(\mbox{{\rm CO}}(X),\subseteq)
of closed and open subsets and operates on morphisms by taking inverse images of sets.
Its restriction to Stone spaces (= zero-dimensional compact Hausdorff spaces) is part of
the Stone duality
[
31
]
,

{\sf Ult}:{\bf Boo}^{{\rm op}}\longrightarrow{\bf Stone}\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ {\sf CO}:{\bf Stone}\longrightarrow{\bf Boo}^{{\rm op}}.

The other part,
\sf Ult
,
assigns to a Boolean algebra
A
the
space
{\sf Ult}(A)

of ultrafilters in
A
and operates on morphisms by taking inverse images of ultrafilters. The
topology of
{\sf Ult}(A)

takes
the family
\{\varepsilon_{A}(a)\ |\ a\in A\}
as a base for closed sets, where

\varepsilon_{A}(a)\overset{\mathrm{df}}{=}\{{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mbox{{\rm Ult}}(A)\ |\ a\in{\mathfrak{u}}\},

for every
a\in A
. (Note that the family
\{\varepsilon_{A}(a)\ |\ a\in A\}
is also an open base for
the topology of
\mbox{{\sf Ult}}(A)
.)
The
Stone map

\varepsilon_{A}:A\longrightarrow{\sf CO}({\sf Ult}(A))

is a
{\bf Boo}
-isomorphism and, in our setting, serves as
the counit of the Stone dual equivalence at

A
.
The adjunction unit
at a Stone space
X
is the
homeomorphism

\eta_{X}:X\longrightarrow{\sf Ult}({\sf CO}(X)),\quad x\longmapsto{\mathfrak{u}}_{x}\overset{\mathrm{df}}{=}\{U\in{\sf CO}(X)\,|\,x\in U\}.

It is well-known
(
[
19
,
20
]
)
that, under the Stone duality,
complete
Boolean algebras correspond to compact Hausdorff spaces that are
extremally disconnected
(so that the closure of any open set is still open). Hence, in a self-explanatory notation, one has the dual equivalence

\textstyle{{\bf CBoo}^{\rm op}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sf Ult\;\;\;}
\textstyle{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}
\textstyle{{\bf EdKH}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces.}
\scriptstyle{\sf CO\;\;\;}

Facts 2.2
.

A pair
(A,{\,\smallfrown\,})
, where
A
is a Boolean algebra and
{\,\smallfrown\,}
is a relation on it, is called a
contact algebra
(
[
14
]
) if it satisfies the following conditions:

{\rm C}1
.

a\smallfrown a
whenever
a>0
;

{\rm C}2
.

a\smallfrown b
implies
a>0
and
b>0
;

{\rm C}3
.

a\smallfrown b
implies
b\smallfrown a
;

{\rm C}4
.

a\smallfrown(b\vee c)
if, and only if, ​
a\smallfrown b
or
a\smallfrown c
.

With the
non-tangential inclusion
relation
\ll
(denoted sometimes by
\ll_{\,\smallfrown\,}
as well) on
A
defined by

(a\ll b\iff a\not\smallfrown b^{*}),

these conditions may equivalently be stated as

I 1.

a\ll b\text{ implies }a\leq b
;

I 2.

a\leq b\ll c\leq d
implies
a\ll d
;

I 3.

0\ll 0
, and
a\ll b
implies
b^{*}\ll a^{*}
;

I 4.

a\ll c
and
b\ll c
implies
a\vee b\ll c
.

In terms of
\ll
, the contact relation
\smallfrown
takes the form
(a\smallfrown b\iff a\not\ll b^{*})
.

A contact algebra
A
is said to be a
normal contact algebra
(see
[
34
,
17
]
, where, however, different names have been used) if it also satisfies the condition

I5. if
a\ll c
and
c>0
, then
a\ll b\ll c
for some
b>0
.

The typical examples of (normal) contact algebras can be obtained as follows.

A set
F
in a topological space
X
is
regular closed
(or a
closed domain

[
16
]
) if it is the closure of its interior in
X

F={\rm cl(int}(F))
. The collection
{{\rm RC}}(X)
of all regular closed sets in

becomes a Boolean algebra, with the Boolean operations
\vee,\wedge,\,^{*},0,1
given by

\displaystyle F\vee G

\displaystyle=F\cup G,

\displaystyle F\wedge G

\displaystyle=\mbox{{\rm cl}}(\mbox{{\rm int}}(F\cap G)),

\displaystyle F^{*}

\displaystyle=\mbox{{\rm cl}}(X\setminus F),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\emptyset,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=X.

The Boolean algebra
{\sf RC}(X)
is actually complete, with the infinite joins and meets given by

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i\in I}F_{i}

\displaystyle=\mbox{{\rm cl}}(\bigcup_{i\in I}F_{i})\ =\mbox{{\rm cl}}(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mbox{{\rm int}}(F_{i}))=\mbox{{\rm cl}}(\mbox{{\rm int}}(\bigcup_{i\in I}F_{i}))\quad\text{and}\quad\bigwedge_{i\in I}F_{i}

\displaystyle=\mbox{{\rm cl}}(\mbox{{\rm int}}(\bigcap_{i\in I}F_{i})).

With the

In

reads as

F\ll_{X}G\iff F\subseteq\mbox{{\rm int}}_{X}(G).

2.4
.

A
local contact algebra
is a contact algebra

, provided with a (not necessarily proper) ideal

of

(BC1) if

(BC3) for every element

The local contact algebra is

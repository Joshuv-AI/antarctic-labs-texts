Characteristic kernels on Hilbert spaces, Banach spaces, and on sets of measures
Johanna Ziegel, David Ginsbourger, Lutz Dümbgen

Abstract
We present new classes of positive definite kernels on non-standard spaces that are integrally strictly positive definite or characteristic. In particular, we discuss radial kernels on separable Hilbert spaces, and introduce broad classes of kernels on Banach spaces and on metric spaces of strong negative type. The general results are used to give explicit classes of kernels on separable L p L^{p} spaces and on sets of measures.



1
Introduction

Symmetric, positive definite functions, so-called
kernels
, are of great importance in many different areas.
In spatial statistics, kernels are omnipresent as they appear as covariance functions of second order random fields
(
Stein 1999
)
.
In machine learning, the “kernel trick” allows to generalize various methods from classical multivariate statistics pertaining to classification, dimensionality reduction, and regression by working in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) instead of the original input domain; see
Hofmann et al. 2008
,
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
and references therein.
A related family of kernel methods relies on embeddings of probability measures into an RKHS, so-called kernel mean embeddings (KMEs): see
Berlinet and Thomas-Agnan 2004
,
Sriperumbudur et al. 2010
. Considering the RKHS-norm between the embedding of two measures yields a pseudometric on probability measures which is known as the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD). Its applications are manifold ranging from kernel two-sample tests
(
Gretton et al. 2012
)
over independence test
(
Gretton et al. 2005
)
to learning on distributional data
(
Sutherland 2016
,
Szabó et al. 2016
)
.
In statistical forecasting, kernel scores are important proper scoring rules that allow to compare the skill of competing probabilistic predictions
(
Dawid 2007
,
Gneiting and Raftery 2007
)
with the most prominent example being the continuous ranked probability score
(
Matheson and Winkler 1976
)
. The aim of this paper is to provide new classes of kernels on non-standard input spaces that are characteristic or even integrally strictly positive definite.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space and
k
a measurable kernel on
X
. The kernel score
S_{k}
associated with
k
is the map

S_{k}(P,x)=-\int_{X}k(\omega,x)\,\mathrm{d}P(\omega)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\int_{X}k(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}P(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}P(\omega^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}k(x,x),

(1)

for
P\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)
,
x\in X
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)
is a suitable set of probability measures on
X
such that all integrals are finite; see Section
2
. In contrast to
Dawid 2007
, the last term in (
1
) is missing in the definition of kernel scores by
Gneiting and Raftery 2007
. We include it, since it ensures that the kernel score
S_{k}
is non-negative.
By
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
,
S_{k}
is a proper scoring rule with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)
, that is, for all
P,Q\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)
,
S_{k}(P,P)\leq S_{k}(Q,P)
, where
S_{k}(Q,P)=\int_{X}S_{k}(Q,x)\,\mathrm{d}P(x)
. The MMD
\gamma_{k}
associated to
k
is given by

\gamma_{k}(P,Q)=\sqrt{2(S_{k}(Q,P)-S_{k}(P,P))},\quad P,Q\in\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X),

(2)

see
Gretton et al. 2012
. Each proper scoring rule
S
gives rise to a divergence defined by
d(P,Q)=S(Q,P)-S(P,P)

(
Gneiting and Raftery 2007
)
. Equation (
2
) shows that the divergences of kernel scores are squared MMDs, and vice versa.

Some MMDs have also been studied in statistics under the name of energy distances
(
Székely and Rizzo 2004
,
Székely and Rizzo 2005
,
Baringhaus and Franz 2004
)
, and they have been applied to various multivariate testing problems. Building on energy distances,
Székely et al. 2007
,
Székely and Rizzo 2009
have proposed distance covariances, which have been studied further in probability theory
(
Lyons 2013
)
and statistics
(
Matsui et al. 2017
,
Betken and Dehling 2021
)
. It should be noted that until the work of
Sejdinovic et al. 2013
, it was not clear that energy distances are in fact a special case of MMDs, see also the discussion before Theorem
4.2
.

Generally, MMDs are pseudometrics on probability measures, that is, they are symmetric and non-negative and they satisfy the triangle inequality; see Section
2
where it is explained how MMDs are even seminorms on some suitable vector space of signed measures. It may happen, however, that the MMD between two different probability measures is zero. Kernels for which the MMD is a metric (or a norm) are called
characteristic
. A closely related notion is that of
universality
of a kernel; see Section
2
for details.

Characteristic and universal kernels are of particular interest in applications for various reasons: A characteristic kernel gives rise to a
strictly
proper kernel score, that is,
S_{k}(P,P)=S_{k}(Q,P)
implies
P=Q
, which encourages truthful reports of forecasters
(
Gneiting and Raftery 2007
)
. Equivalently, it yields MMDs that are metrics and not only pseudometrics, which is important for example for their application in two-sample tests. Universality of kernels is of fundamental importance for the statistical analysis of learning methods such as support vector machines. It allows to establish asymptotic consistency results
(
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
, Chapter 6 and 7)
.

Constructing positive definite kernels on arbitrary sets is a non-trivial task. However, if it can be accomplished, it opens the door to a plethora of methods including the ones mentioned above. By now, the literature is fairly rich concerning positive and strictly positive definite kernels on non-standard input spaces.
If these spaces are locally compact Hausdorff spaces, even characteristic and universal kernels are well-understood
(
Steinwart 2001
,
Sriperumbudur et al. 2010
,
Simon-Gabriel and Schölkopf 2018
)
. Of particular interest to this paper are the results of
Christmann and Steinwart 2010
. They provide universal kernels on general compact metric spaces. In particular, they provide kernels on the space of probability measures over a compact metric space. These kernels have found application in kernel-ridge regression with distribution-valued covariates
(
Szabó et al. 2016
)
, see also
Muandet et al. 2012
.

If the input space is not locally compact, then the question of kernels being characteristic or universal is wide open.
Just recently,
Hayati et al. 2020
have shown the existence of a continuous characteristic kernel on an infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space.
Wynne and Duncan 2022
define a class called Squared Exponential
T
(SE-T) kernels on a separable Hilbert space, and establish that for a nuclear, self-adjoint, and non-negative operator
T
, the resulting SE-T kernel is characteristic if only if
T
is injective
(
Wynne and Duncan 2022
, Theorem 3)
. Building on this result, they can also construct characteristic kernels on some Polish spaces.

The results that we present in this paper go further in several respects. On a separable Hilbert space
H
, we show in Section
3
that the Gaussian kernel is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to all finite signed measures on
H
, and extend this result to the whole class
\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
of strictly positive definite radial kernels on
H
. In Section
4.1
, we introduce constructions of integrally strictly positive definite kernels on Banach spaces and on metric spaces of strong negative type. The first one builds on characteristic functions of Gaussian measures. The second one arises from chaining functions of
\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
with the metric. Specific results for the case of separable
L^{p}
spaces are presented in Section
4.2
, yielding two different classes of integrally strictly positive definite kernels using the first construction for
1<p<\infty
, and the second construction for
1<p\leq 2
. Finally, in Section
5
, we present results on integrally strictly positive definite kernels on sets of measures over locally compact Hausdorff spaces equipped with the topology of weak convergence. The considered classes of kernels rely on KMEs into RKHSs as well as into an
L^{2}
space. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary background and notation. While this section is quite technical, we hope that it makes the overall exposition close to self-contained. The paper concludes with a short discussion in Section
6
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Kernel embeddings

We follow the notation used in
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
.
For a measurable space
(X,\mathcal{A})
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}(X)
the space of all finite signed measures on
X
and equip it with the total variation norm
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
. The set of probability measures is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
, and the set of finite non-negative measures by
\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
. We define
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}\;|\;\mu(X)=0\}
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mu)
the space of all measurable functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
that are
\mu
-integrable. Let us recall the notion of Pettis integrals. Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
f:X\to H
a map. Then,
f
is weakly measurable if
\langle w,f\rangle_{H}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable for all
w\in H
, and
f
is weakly integrable with respect to
\mu
if
\langle w,f\rangle_{H}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mu)
for all
w\in H
. If
f
is weakly integrable, then there exists a unique
i_{\mu}(f)\in H
such that for all
w\in H
, we have

\langle w,i_{\mu}(f)\rangle_{H}=\int_{X}\langle w,f\rangle_{H}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(3)

The element
i_{\mu}(f)
is called the Pettis integral of
f
with respect to
\mu
and we use the more intuitive notation
\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu:=i_{\mu}(f)

(
Diestel and Uhl 1977
, Chapter II.3)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space of real-valued functions on
X
. A function
k:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is a reproducing kernel of
H
if
k(\cdot,x)\in H
for all
x\in X
, and
\langle f,k(\cdot,x)\rangle_{H}=f(x)
for all
f\in H
,
x\in X
. The Moore-Aronszajn theorem states that for every kernel
k
, that is, for every symmetric, positive definite function
k:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
, there is an associated RKHS
H_{k}
of real-valued functions on
X
with reproducing kernel
k

(
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
, Chapter 4)
. The canonical feature map is
\Phi_{k}:X\to H_{k},x\mapsto k(\cdot,x)
. The RKHS
H_{k}
consists of measurable functions on
X
if
k
is measurable
(
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
, Lemma 4.24)
. Therefore, since
\langle f,\Phi_{k}\rangle_{H_{k}}=f
for all
f\in H_{k}
,
\Phi_{k}
is weakly measurable if
k
is measurable. The canonical feature map
\Phi_{k}
is weakly integrable with respect to
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mu)
for all
f\in H_{k}
. By
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
, a sufficient condition for this is

\int_{X}\sqrt{k(x,x)}\,\mathrm{d}|\mu|(x)<\infty.

We define

\mathcal{M}^{k}(X):=\big\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;|\;H_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mu)\big\},

and
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)=\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
. If
k
is bounded, then
\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X)
. It is also necessary for
\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X)
that
k
is bounded, which can be seen by combining
Sriperumbudur et al. 2010
and
Steinwart and Christmann 2008
as remarked by
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
. The vector space
\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)
is the largest set on which the kernel mean embedding

\Phi_{k}(\mu):=\int_{X}\Phi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int k(\cdot,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

can be defined. The kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k}\colon\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)\to H_{k}
is linear, so

\|\mu\|_{H_{k}}:=\|\Phi_{k}(\mu)\|_{H_{k}}

(4)

defines a (new) semi-norm on
\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)
. This semi-norm is a norm, if and only if the kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k}
is injective. If the kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k}
restricted to
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{k}(X)
is injective, then the kernel
k
is called
characteristic
. Using (
3
), we see that for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)
, it holds that

\langle\Phi_{k}(\mu_{1}),\Phi_{k}(\mu_{2})\rangle_{H_{k}}=\int_{X}\int_{X}k(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(x).

Therefore,
\Phi_{k}
is injective if and only if

\|\mu\|_{H_{k}}^{2}=\int_{X}\int_{X}k(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})>0

(5)

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)\backslash\{0\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a measurable kernel on
X
and
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{k}(X)
. Then,
k
is called
strictly integrally positive definite
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
if
\|\mu\|_{H_{k}}^{2}>0
for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\backslash\{0\}
.

Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
show that a bounded measurable kernel
k
on
X
is characteristic if and only if it is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
; see also
Sriperumbudur et al. 2010
. The following lemma, generalizes
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
to arbitrary convex mixtures of kernels which will be useful in subsequent arguments.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space,
\nu
a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
with
\nu\not=0
, and
k_{\theta}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
a family of uniformly bounded kernels on
X
with RKHSs
H_{\theta}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. We assume that
(x,y,\theta)\mapsto k_{\theta}(x,y)
is
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
-measurable. Let
H
be the RKHS of the kernel
k=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\theta)
. Then, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
, we have

\|\mu\|_{H}^{2}=\int\|\mu\|_{H_{\theta}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\theta).

If there is a Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
\nu(A)>0
such that
k_{\theta}
is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(X)
for all
\theta\in A
, then
k
is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}
. In particular, if all
k_{\theta}
have injective kernel mean embeddings, then so does
k
. If all
k_{\theta}
are characteristic, then so is
k
.

Proof.

By the definition of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
at (
4
) and Fubini’s theorem, we have that for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
,

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{X}k(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\theta}(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{X}\int_{X}k_{\theta}(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\theta)=\int\|\mu\|_{H_{\theta}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\theta).

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
with
\mu\not=0
. If
\|\mu\|_{H_{\theta}}>0
for all
\theta
in a set
A
of strictly positive
\nu
-measure, then it follows that
\|\mu\|_{H}>0
by the formula we have just shown.
∎

2.2
Further definitions and notation

Let
(X,\tau)
be a Hausdorff space and
\mathcal{B}(X)
its Borel-
\sigma
-algebra. A Borel measure
\nu
on
X
is a non-negative measure on
\mathcal{B}(X)
such that
\nu(K)<\infty
for all compact sets
K\subseteq X
. A Borel measure
\nu
is called a Radon measure if it is (inner and outer) regular. Let
\mathcal{M}^{*}(X)
denote the space of all finite signed Radon measures on
X
with the total variation norm. By Ulam’s theorem
(
Bauer 2001
, Lemma 26.2)
, if
X
is a Polish space, then every finite Borel measure is a finite Radon measure.

For a locally compact Hausdorff space
(X,\tau)
, let
C_{0}(X)
be the space of continuous functions that vanish at infinity with the supremum norm. By Riesz’s representation theorem
(
Rudin 1970
, Theorem 6.19)
, the topological dual
C_{0}(X)^{\prime}
of
C_{0}(X)
is equal to
\mathcal{M}^{*}(X)
. A continuous kernel
k
on
X
is called
universal
if
H_{k}\subseteq C_{0}(X)
and
H_{k}
is dense in
C_{0}(X)
. A bounded continuous kernel
k
on
X
is called a
C_{0}(X)
-kernel if
H_{k}\subseteq C_{0}(X)
.
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
show that a
C_{0}(X)
-kernel is universal if and only if it is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}^{*}(X)
. If such a kernel exists, then
\mathcal{M}^{*}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X)
by
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
.

A Radon measure
\mathcal{W}
on a Banach space
B
is called a centered Radon Gaussian measure if the image measure
x^{\prime}\#\mathcal{W}
on
\mathbb{R}
is centered Gaussian for all
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, where
B^{\prime}
denotes the topological dual of
B
; see
Bogachev 1998
for a comprehensive introduction to Gaussian measures on infinite dimensional spaces. For two Banach spaces
B
and
C
, we denote the set of continuous linear operators from
B
to
C
by
L(B,C)
. For
x\in B
and
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, we write
\langle x^{\prime},x\rangle=x^{\prime}(x)
for the duality relation. We sometimes identify elements of
B
with elements of
B^{\prime\prime}
via the canonical embedding.

For a normed vector space
(V,\|\cdot\|)
, a symmetric function
k
on
V\times V
is called radial if it is of the form
k(x,y)=\varphi(\|x-y\|^{2})
,
x,y\in V
for some function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
.

3
Radial kernels on Hilbert spaces

In this section, we characterize continuous integrally strictly positive definite radial kernels on separable Hilbert spaces

Continuous (strictly) positive definite radial functions on Hilbert spaces have been characterized by
Schoenberg 1938
and
Bachoc et al. 2020
. The latter reference extends Schoenberg’s results for separable Hilbert spaces to general Hilbert spaces. For the continuous radial function
k
to be positive definite, that is, a kernel, the function
\varphi
has to be of the form

\varphi(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-xt}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x),\quad t\geq 0,

(6)

where
\nu
is a finite Borel measure on
[0,\infty)
, that is,
\varphi
is the Laplace transform of
\nu
. We denote the class of all Laplace transforms of finite Borel measures on
[0,\infty)
by
\Phi_{\infty}
. If
\operatorname{supp}\nu\not=\{0\}
and
\nu\not=0
, then
\varphi
at (
6
) induces a strictly positive definite function on
H
. We denote the class of all such functions with
\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. The method of proof of
Bachoc et al. 2020
does not use that the Hilbert space is complete. In fact, their argument works for any vector space
V
with a scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and functions of the form
\varphi(\langle x-y,x-y\rangle)
,
x,y\in X
. We make use of this fact in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Christmann and Steinwart 2010
show that the Gaussian kernel
\exp(-\|x-y\|_{H}^{2}/2)
,
x,y\in H
, is universal on every compact subset
K
of a separable Hilbert space
H
, that is it is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(K)
. They suggest that their result can be generalized to hold for arbitrary
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
and this is indeed the case by Lemma
2.1
. In summary, every continuous strictly positive definite radial kernel is universal on every compact subset of
H
.
We show that the restriction to compact subsets of
H
is not necessary.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space. Then the Gaussian kernel on
H
is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(H)
.

Hayati et al. 2020
have previously shown that the kernel mean embedding of the Gaussian kernel on
H
is injective restricted to the family of Gaussian measures on
H
.
Wynne and Duncan 2022
show that the Gaussian kernel is characteristic on
H
. What distinguishes Theorem
3.1
from the latter result is the broader class of measures on which the associated kernel mean embedding is injective (integrally strictly positive definite versus characteristic) and the shorter proof provided for our more general result. Additionally, Lemma
2.1
yields the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then the kernel
k
on
H
defined by

k(x,y):=\varphi\big(\|x-y\|^{2}\big),\quad x,y\in H,

is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(H)
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
relies on the following lemma, that generalizes a standard result in probability theory.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(H)
. Define

m_{j}(y)=\int\exp(\langle x,y\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)\in[0,\infty]

for
j=1,2
and arbitrary
x\in H
. Suppose that for some
\varepsilon>0
,
m_{1}(y)=m_{2}(y)<\infty
whenever
\|y\|\leq\varepsilon
. Then
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

Proof.

Note that
m_{j}(0)=\mu_{j}(H)
, so
\mu_{1}(H)=\mu_{2}(H)
, and we may assume without loss of generality that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(H)
. If
\dim(H)=d<\infty
, we may identify
H
with
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, equipped with its standard inner product. In that case, the result is well-known from probability theory.

Let
\dim(H)=\infty
. Separability of
H
implies the existence of a complete orthonormal system
(\phi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
H
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Pi_{n}
be the orthogonal projection from
H
onto the
n
-dimensional linear subspace
H_{n}:=\operatorname{span}(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})
. If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are random variables with distributions
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, respectively, then

m_{j}(y)=\mathbb{E}\exp(\langle X_{j},y\rangle)

for
j=1,2
and
y\in H
. Specifically, if
y\in H_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

m_{j}(y)=\mathbb{E}\exp(\langle\Pi_{n}X_{j},y\rangle).

Consequently, if
m_{1}(y)=m_{2}(y)<\infty
whenever
\|y\|\leq\varepsilon
, then
\Pi_{n}X_{1}
and
\Pi_{n}X_{2}
have the same distribution for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. But this implies that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
, because
\Pi_{n}X_{j}\to X_{j}
almost surely as
n\to\infty
for
j=1,2
, and hence, for any bounded continuous function
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
, by dominated convergence,

\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f(\Pi_{n}X_{1})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}f(\Pi_{n}X_{2})=\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(x).

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\mu=\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\in\mathcal{M}(H)
with
\mu_{+},\mu_{-}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(H)
. Then, if
k(x,y)=\exp(-\|x-y\|_{H}^{2}/2)
is the Gaussian kernel and
y\in H
,

\Phi_{k}(\mu)(y)=\exp\Big(-\frac{\|y\|_{H}^{2}}{2}\Big)\Bigl(\int\exp(\langle x,y\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{+}(x)-\int\exp(\langle x,y\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{-}(x)\Bigr)

with the finite measures
\tilde{\mu}_{+},\tilde{\mu}_{-}
given by
\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{j}(x):=\exp(-\|x\|_{2}^{2}/2)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
. Thus,
\Phi_{k}(\mu)=0
is equivalent to

\int\exp(\langle x,y\rangle_{H})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{+}(x)=\int\exp(\langle x,y\rangle_{H})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{-}(x),\quad\text{for all $y\in H$.}

By definition of the measures
\tilde{\mu}_{\pm}
, the latter integrals are finite. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\tilde{\mu}_{+}=\tilde{\mu}_{-}
. But
\mu_{\pm}
may be represented as
\mu_{\pm}(dx)=\exp(\|x\|_{H}^{2}/2)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{\pm}(x)
, so
\mu_{+}=\mu_{-}
, that is,
\mu=0
.
∎

While we focus on radial kernels in this section, our results carry over to kernels obtained by composing arguments via suitable classes of maps, in the spirit of the SE-T kernels of
Wynne and Duncan 2022
. In particular, Proposition
5.2
shows that
Wynne and Duncan 2022
holds when replacing the squared exponential kernel with any radial kernel in
\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
, thus straightforwardly proving
Wynne and Duncan 2022
.

4
Characteristic kernels on Banach spaces

4.1
General results

Berg et al. 1984
discuss the question whether the Gaussian kernel, that is,
t\mapsto\exp(-t^{2})
leads to a radial kernel on a Banach space
B
. The answer is negative in the following sense
(
Berg et al. 1984
, Lemma 5.2.2.2)
: If
k(x,y)=\exp(-\lambda\|x-y\|^{2})
is positive definite on
B
, then
B
is already a Hilbert space. Therefore, there is no hope for a generalization of Theorem
3.1
to Banach spaces.

On the Banach space
C([0,1])
of continuous functions on
[0,1]
with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, the situation is even more drastic in the sense that
\varphi(\|f-g\|_{\infty})
,
f,g\in C([0,1])
is not positive definite for any
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}

(
Berg et al. 1984
, Theorem 5.3.2.3)
. An intermediate situation is encountered for
L^{p}
-spaces, where the Gaussian kernel does not lead to a radial kernel for
p\not=2
but the Laplace kernel, that is,
\exp(-\|f-g\|_{L^{p}})
,
f,g\in L^{p}
does; compare Corollary
4.4
.

In this section, we provide two construction principles for integrally strictly positive definite or characteristic kernels on Banach spaces. The first one (Theorem
4.1
) yields kernels that are similar to Gaussian kernels but with respect to a different norm, whereas the second one yields variants of Laplace kernels (Theorem
4.2
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
B
be a Banach space and
A\in L(B^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime})
an operator that induces a non-degenerate centered Radon Gaussian measure on
B^{\prime}
with covariance
A
, and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then,

k(x,y):=\varphi\Big(\langle A(x-y),x-y\rangle\Big),\quad x,y\in B\subseteq B^{\prime\prime},

(7)

is a kernel on
B
that is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}^{*}(B)
.

Proof.

Showing that
k
is strictly positive definite on
B
works with the same arguments as
Bachoc et al. 2020
.
By definition, the centered Gaussian measure
\mathcal{W}
on
B^{\prime}
with covariance operator
A
has characteristic function

\int_{B^{\prime}}\exp(i\langle x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime}\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{W}(x^{\prime})=\exp\Big(-\frac{\langle Ax^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\rangle}{2}\Big),\quad x^{\prime\prime}\in B^{\prime\prime}.

(8)

Since
\mathcal{W}
is assumed to be non-degenerate, it has full support, that is, it assigns positive probability to every non-empty open subset of
B^{\prime}
.
Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{*}(B)
. Then, using (
8
) and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{H_{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\exp\Big(-\frac{\langle A(x-y),x-y\rangle}{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}\int_{B}\int_{B}\exp(i\langle x^{\prime},x-y\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{W}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}\bigg|\int_{B}\exp(i\langle x^{\prime},x\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{W}(x^{\prime}).

Therefore, if
\|\mu\|_{H_{k}}^{2}=0
, then
\hat{\mu}(x^{\prime}):=\int_{B}\exp(i\langle x^{\prime},x\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0
for
\mathcal{W}
-almost all
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
. The map
\hat{\mu}
is continuous with respect to weak convergence on
B^{\prime}
, and therefore also with respect to the operator norm convergence on
B^{\prime}
. This implies that
\hat{\mu}(x^{\prime})=0
for all
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
. Since
\hat{\mu}(0)=\mu^{+}(B)-\mu^{-}(B)=0
, we see that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(B)
, and it is no loss of generality to assume that
\mu^{+},\mu^{-}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(B)
. We obtain that the characteristic functions (or Fourier transforms) of
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
are equal, and therefore,
\mu=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}=0

(
Bogachev 1998
, Proposition A.3.18)
. Using the covariance operator
\alpha A
for any
\alpha>0
instead of
A
, it follows that the kernel at (
7
) with
\varphi(t)=\exp(-\alpha t^{2})
is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}^{*}(B)
. The claim now follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

The kernels defined at (
7
) in Theorem
4.1
are not radial with respect to the norm of the Banach space
B
. However, they are radial with respect to a different norm on
B
given by
\|x-y\|_{A}=\sqrt{\langle A(x-y),x-y\rangle}
,
x,y\in B
, with respect to which
B
is generally not complete.

Recently,
Pan et al. 2018
have suggested the ball divergence, which is a divergence between probability measures on separable Banach spaces. If one of the measures has full support, then the divergence can only be zero if the two measures are identical
(
Pan et al. 2018
, Theorem 2)
. Their divergence cannot be the squared MMDs induced by a kernel on the Banach space since its square root does not satisfy the triangle inequality. However, it is closely related to an MMD
(
Pan et al. 2018
, Remarks 2.2 and 2.4)
.

Characterizing operators
A\in L(B^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime})
that can be obtained as covariances of Gaussian measures on
B^{\prime}
is generally difficult. But there are some notable exceptions such as separable
L^{p}
-spaces which we treat in Section
4.2
. If
B=H
is a separable Hilbert space, then the operators that induce non-degenerate centered Gaussian measures are the nuclear, self-adjoint strictly positive operators
(
Baxendale 1976
, Corollary 2.3)
. However, Corollary
3.2
is stronger than Theorem
4.1
in this case. This can be seen as follows. Let
T:H\to H
be injective and measurable,
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(H)
. Then, denoting the image measure of
\mu
under
T
by
T\#\mu
,

0=\int\varphi\big(\|T(x-y)\|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\int\varphi\big(\|x-y\|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}T\#\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}T\#\mu(y),

implies that
T\#\mu=0
by Corollary
3.2
. Since
T
is injective and measurable, it is bi-measurable by Purves’ theorem
(
Purves 1966
)
, that is, we have
T(M)\in\mathcal{B}(H)
and
T^{-1}(T(M))=M
. This implies that
\mu=0
. Taking
T
to be a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, we obtain the result of Theorem
4.1
for separable Hilbert spaces.

On some Banach space radial characteristic kernels exist. We show a more general result. Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space. It is said to be of
negative type
if
\rho
is negative definite in the sense of
(
Berg et al. 1984
, Definition 3.1.1)
, that is, if for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=0
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in X
, it holds that
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}c_{j}\rho(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 0
. A metric
\rho
of negative type induces a positive definite kernel
k
on
X
by defining
k(x,y)=\rho(x,z_{0})+\rho(y,z_{0})-\rho(x,y)
for some fixed
z_{0}\in X

(
Berg et al. 1984
, Lemma 3.2.1)
. The relation between the metric
\rho
and the induced so-called distance kernel
k
has been studied in detail by
Sejdinovic et al. 2013
. In particular, they show that (under suitable integrability assumptions) the energy distance with respect to
\rho
is the same as the squared MMD with respect to
k

(
Sejdinovic et al. 2013
, Theorem 22)
.
Define

\mathcal{M}_{\rho}(X)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)\;|\;\exists z_{0}\in X\;\text{such that}\;\int\rho(x,z_{0})\,\mathrm{d}|\mu|(x)<\infty\}.

Following
Lyons 2013
, the metric space is of
strong negative type
if for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}_{\rho}(X)
, it holds that

\int\int\rho(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0\quad\text{implies that}\quad\mu=0.

The notion of a metric of strong negative type
(
Lyons 2013
, Section 3)
is the same as requiring the associated distance kernel
k
to be characteristic (restricted to probability measures
\mu
that satisfy
\int_{X}k(x,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space of strong negative type and
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then,

k(x,y):=\varphi(\rho(x,y)),\quad x,y\in X,

is a kernel on
X
that is characteristic, that is, strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to show that the Laplace kernel
\exp(-\rho(x,y))
,
x,y\in X
, is strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
. Let
z_{0}\in X
and define
k(x,y)=\rho(x,z_{0})+\rho(y,z_{0})-\rho(x,y)
,
x,y\in X
. Since
\rho
is a metric,
k
is non-negative. By
Berg et al. 1984
,
k
is a kernel on
X
, and for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
k^{\ell}
is also a kernel.
For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int\int\exp(-\rho(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int\int\exp(k(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\int\int k(x,y)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(y),

using monotone convergence, where the measure
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}(X)
is given by
\tilde{\mu}=\tilde{\mu}_{+}-\tilde{\mu}_{-}
with
\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{\pm}(x)=\exp(-\rho(x,z_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\pm}(x)
and
\mu_{\pm}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
are such that
\mu=\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}
. Note also that
\int\sqrt{\exp(k(x,x)}\,\mathrm{d}|\tilde{\mu}|(x)=|\tilde{\mu}|(X)<\infty
.
Since all summands on the right hand side are non-negative,
\int\int\exp(-\rho(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0
implies that
\tilde{\mu}(X)=0
, and

0=\int\int k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(y)=-\int\rho(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}(y).

Since
(X,\rho)
is of strong negative type, we find that
\tilde{\mu}=0
which allows to conclude that
\mu=0
.
∎

4.2
Kernels on
L^{p}
-spaces

We consider now the special case of separable
L^{p}
-spaces,
1<p<\infty
. In this case, we can derive explicit expressions for integrally strictly positive definite kernels using Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
. For the first construction, the crucial ingredient is that characterizations of covariance operators on the dual are available in this case.

The kernels presented in this section may find application in the construction of new kernel two-sample tests for functional data, see
Wynne and Duncan 2022
for exisiting work in this direction. They can also be used to construct kernel scores for probabilistic predictions of outcomes taking functional values such as precipitation fields over spatial domains.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space and and
\lambda
a finite or
\sigma
-finite measure on
(X,\mathcal{A})
such that
L^{p}(\lambda)
is separable. Let
1<q<\infty
such that
1/p+1/q=1
. Then, also
L^{q}(\lambda)
is separable. If
A\in L(L^{p}(\lambda),L^{q}(\lambda))
is the covariance of a centered Gaussian measure on
L^{q}(\lambda)
, then by
Bogachev 1998
, there exists a measurable kernel
k_{1}
on
X
with

\int_{X}k_{1}(x,x)^{\frac{q}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)<\infty

(9)

and

(Af)(x)=\int_{X}k_{1}(x,y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(y),\quad f\in L^{p}(\lambda),\;x\in X.

(10)

Conversely, any measurable kernel
k_{1}
on
X
that satisfies (
9
) induces a covariance of some centered Gaussian measure via (
10
).
This leads to the following corollary to Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
L^{p}(\lambda)
is separable and that
k_{1}
is a measurable kernel on
X
that satisfies (
9
), and

\int_{X}\int_{X}k_{1}(x,y)g(x)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)>0\quad\text{for all $g\in L^{p}(\lambda)\backslash\{0\}$.}

(11)

Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then,

k_{2}(f,g):=\varphi\Big(\int_{X}\int_{X}k_{1}(x,y)(f(x)-g(x))(f(y)-g(y))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)\Big),\quad f,g\in L^{p}(\lambda),

is a kernel on
L^{p}(\lambda)
that is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(L^{p}(\lambda))
.

Proof.

We need to show that
k_{1}
induces a
nondegenerate
centered Gaussian measure on
L^{q}(\lambda)
. A centered Gaussian measure
\tau
on
L^{q}(\lambda)
is nondegenerate if and only if its Cameron-Martin
H(\tau)
space is dense in
L^{q}(\lambda)
. The space
H(\tau)
contains the image of the operator
A
defined at (
10
)
(
Bogachev 1998
, Theorem 3.2.3)
. By
Werner 2002
, the image of
A
is dense in
L^{q}(\lambda)
if and only if the null space of the adjoint operator
A^{\prime}=A\in L(L^{p}(\lambda),L^{q}(\lambda))
is trivial. This in turn is equivalent to the following condition for any
g\in L^{p}(\lambda)
:

\int_{X}\int_{X}k_{1}(x,y)g(x)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda(x)=0\quad\text{for all $f\in L^{p}(\lambda)$}\quad\implies\quad g=0.

This condition is implied by (
11
).
∎

The condition at (
11
) is generally different to the kernel
k_{1}
being integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(X)
. This is easily seen by choosing
\lambda
to be a measure with finite support.
Sriperumbudur et al. 2010
consider the definition of an integrally strictly positive definite kernel a generalization of the condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k_{1}(x,y)g(x)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y>0
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by
Stewart 1976
. The cases
p\in\{1,\infty\}
are not included in Corollary
4.3
since
L^{p}
is not reflexive in this case.

Interestingly, Corollary
4.3
also comes as an echo to
Wynne and Duncan 2022
and its generalization given by Proposition
5.2
, by taking for
\mathcal{Y}
the RKHS
H_{1}
associated with
k_{1}
and for
T
the operator
A
defined by (
10
) seen as a mappping from
L^{p}(\lambda)
to
H_{1}
. The condition at (
11
) then ensures that the latter operator is injective.

The norm of separable
L^{p}
spaces for
1\leq p\leq 2
is of negative type and even of strong negative type for
1<p\leq 2

(
Linde 1986
,
Lyons 2013
)
. Therefore, we obtain the following corollary to Theorem
4.2
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose that
L^{p}(\lambda)
is separable and
1<p\leq 2
. Let
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then,

k(x,y):=\varphi(\|f-g\|_{L^{p}}),\quad f,g\in L^{p}(\lambda),

is a kernel on
L^{p}(\lambda)
that is characteristic, that is, strictly integrally positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{0}(L^{p}(\lambda))
.

5
Kernels on measures

In this section, we use Corollary
3.2
to construct integrally strictly positive definite kernels on spaces of measures over locally compact Hausdorff spaces. Given a locally compact Hausdorff space
(X,\tau)
and a characteristic kernel
k_{1}
on
X
, we can injectively embed
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
in the Hilbert space
H_{k_{1}}
via the kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k_{1}}
. Now, we can apply Corollary
3.2
to the image of
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
under
\Phi_{k_{1}}
. The essential idea of this construction has appeared previously in the literature, see for example
Christmann and Steinwart 2010
;
Buathong et al. 2020
;
Hamid et al. 2021
. To the best of our knowledge,
Christmann and Steinwart 2010
are the only ones to consider universality of the resulting kernels, and not only (strict) positive definiteness. In contrast to their result, the following theorem does not require compactness of
X
. Below, we discuss the relation of Theorem
5.1
in the special case of compact
X
to the result of
Christmann and Steinwart 2010
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, and let
k_{1}
a bounded continuous kernel on
X
that is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(X)
and with separable RKHS
H_{k_{1}}
. Then, for any
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
, the kernel

k_{2}(\mu,\nu):=\varphi\Big(\int_{X}\int_{X}k_{1}(x,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)(x)\,\mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)(x^{\prime})\Big),\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(X),

is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(X))
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
is equipped with the topology of weak convergence of probability measures.

If
X
is additionally Polish, then
k_{2}
is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{+}(X))
, where
\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
is equipped with the topology of weak convergence of measures
(
Bauer 2001
, Definition 30.7)
.

Proof.

The kernel
k_{2}
is the composition of
\tilde{k}_{2}
with the kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k_{1}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)\to H_{k_{1}}
, where

\tilde{k}_{2}(f,f^{\prime}):=\varphi\big(\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{H_{k_{1}}}\big),\quad f,f^{\prime}\in H_{k_{1}}.

Since
H_{k_{1}}
is assumed to be separable, Corollary
3.2
shows that
\tilde{k}_{2}
is a kernel on
H_{k_{1}}
that is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(H_{k_{1}})
. This immediately implies that
k_{2}
is a kernel on
\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
.

Let
\Xi\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{+}(X))
. By
Simon-Gabriel and Schölkopf 2018
, the kernel mean embedding
\Phi_{k_{1}}:\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)\to H_{k_{1}}
is continuous with respect to weak convergence and thus measurable. Therefore, we can consider the image measure
\Phi_{k_{1}}\#\Xi\in\mathcal{M}(H_{k_{1}})
. We obtain that

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)}\varphi\big(\|\Phi_{k_{1}}(\mu)-\Phi_{k_{1}}(\mu^{\prime})\|^{2}_{H_{k_{1}}})\,\mathrm{d}\Xi(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\Xi(\mu^{\prime})\\
=\int_{H_{k_{1}}}\int_{H_{k_{1}}}\varphi\big(\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{H_{k_{1}}})\,\mathrm{d}(\Phi_{k_{1}}\#\Xi)(f)\,\mathrm{d}(\Phi_{k_{1}}\#\Xi)(f^{\prime}).

If the above expression vanishes, then we obtain that
\Phi_{k_{1}}\#\Xi=0
since
\tilde{k}_{2}
is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(H_{k_{1}})
.

By
Simon-Gabriel et al. 2020
,
\|\cdot\|_{H_{k_{1}}}
defined at (
5
) metrizes weak convergence in
\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
, and hence,

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(X))=\{\Phi_{k_{1}}^{-1}(B)\;|\;B\in\mathcal{B}(H_{k_{1}})\}
. Therefore,
\Xi=0
, which yields the first part of the theorem.

Suppose now that
X
is additionally Polish. Then,
\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
is Polish. Therefore, using that
\Phi_{k_{1}}
is measurable and injective, for any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
, also
\Phi_{k_{1}}(B)\subseteq H_{k_{1}}
is a Borel set by Purves’ theorem
(
Purves 1966
)
. This implies that
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}_{+}(X))=\{\Phi_{k_{1}}^{-1}(B)\;|\;B\in\mathcal{B}(H_{k_{1}})\}
. Therefore,
\Xi=0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

The proof of the second part of Theorem
5.1
actually shows the following, more general statement by setting
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{M}_{+}(X)
,
H=H_{k_{1}}
and
T=\Phi_{k_{1}}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Polish space,
H
a separable Hilbert space,
T
a measurable and injective mapping from
\mathcal{X}
to
H
, and
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
.
Then, the kernel
k
on
\mathcal{X}
defined by

k(x,x^{\prime}):=\varphi\big(\|T(x)-T(x^{\prime})\|^{2}\big),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},

is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
.

If there exists a finite Borel measure
\nu
with full support on a Hausdorff space
(X,\tau)
, then
Steinwart and Scovel 2012
shows that any bounded and separately continuous kernel
k
has a separable RKHS
H_{k}
. If the Hausdorff space in Theorem
5.1
is compact, then it is sufficient to require
k_{1}
to be bounded, continuous and characteristic. In the proof one can then employ
Simon-Gabriel et al. 2020
. For the second part it suffices to note that by
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
,
(X,\tau)
is necessarily Polish.

Christmann and Steinwart 2010
provide a second approach to constructing universal kernels on probability measures over compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
via comparing Fourier transforms (or characteristic functions). Our results allow for a similar construction on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a finite measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
\hat{\mu}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp(i\langle x,s\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote its Fourier transform. For a complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
\bar{z}
its complex conjugate.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with full support, and
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
. Then,

k(\mu,\nu):=\varphi\big(\|\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big),\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

is an integrally strictly positive definite kernel with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the topology of weak convergence of probability measures.

Proof.

The kernel

\tilde{k}(f,g):=\varphi\big(\|f-g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big),\quad f,g\in L^{2}(\lambda)

(12)

is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(L^{2}(\lambda))
by Corollary
3.2
. The map
\Phi
from
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{2}(\lambda)
that maps each probability measure
\mu
to its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
is injective and continuous by standard properties of Fourier transforms. It is important here that
\lambda
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore,
L^{2}
-convergence of
(\hat{\mu}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
implies weak convergence of
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, two measures in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
coincide if and only if their image measures under
\Phi
coincide.
∎

A further approach to constructing (strictly) positive definite kernels on absolutely continuous probability distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite second moment is given in
Bachoc et al. 2020
.
They compose a positive definite kernel on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the inverse of the optimal transport maps of the considered probability measures to a reference distribution. The reference distribution is usually chosen as a Wasserstein barycenter. Investigating whether the resulting kernels are integrally strictly positive definite is not straightforward. However, Proposition
5.2
can be leveraged to establish the integrally strictly positive definiteness of a class of covariance kernels on Wasserstein space building upon
(
Mérigot et al. 2020
)
as developed in the next example.

Example 5.1

(Some integrally strictly positive definite kernels on Wasserstein space)
.

Let
K,K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact and convex with unit Lebesgue measure. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
,
Mérigot et al. 2020
denote by
T_{\mu}
the optimal transport map between the Lebesgue measure on
K^{\prime}
and
\mu
, and they call the map
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)\mapsto T_{\mu}\in L^{2}(K^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{d})
the
Monge embedding
.
Mérigot et al. 2020
show that there exists a constant
C
(depending on
d
,
K
and
K^{\prime}
) such that for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
, the following holds:

W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\leq\tilde{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\leq CW_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{\frac{2}{15}},

where
W_{2}
is the
2
-Wasserstein distance on
\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
and the distance
\tilde{W}_{2}
on
\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
is defined by
\tilde{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\|T_{\mu}-T_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}
. This implies in particular that the Monge embedding is injective and continuous on
\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
endowed with the
2
-Wasserstein metric, which is a complete separable metric space
(
Panaretos and Zemel 2020
, Chapter 2)
. Applying Proposition
5.2
, we obtain that for any
\varphi\in\Phi_{\infty}^{+}
, the kernel defined by

k(\mu,\nu):=\varphi\big(\tilde{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\big),\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(K),

is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}_{1}(K))
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}(K)
is equipped with the
2
-Wasserstein topology, which coincides with the topology of weak convergence.

Kernels over measures have been used in the literature to learn kernels of Gaussian processes. When the focus is on radial or translation invariant kernels on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then kernels can be chosen via their spectral measure given by Bochner’s theorem
(
Wendland 2005
, Theorem 6.6)
which has been advocated for example by
Kom Samo and Roberts 2015
,
Benton et al. 2019
,
Hamid et al. 2021
.

Example 5.2

(Translation invariant kernels)
.

Bochner’s theorem states that a continuous positive definite function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with values in
\mathbb{C}
) that depends only on the difference
x-y
of the arguments
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and is one if
x=y
is necessarily of the form
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(x-y)
with
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
being the Fourier transform of some probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The arguments in the proof of Proposition
5.3
show that the set
\mathcal{F}_{d}
of all such
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is a closed subset of
L^{2}(\lambda)
for any probability measure
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with full support. Therefore, the kernel
\tilde{k}
given at (
12
) restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{d}
is integrally strictly positive definite with respect to
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{d})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{d}
is equipped with
L^{2}(\lambda)
-norm. Convergence in
L^{2}(\lambda)
is equivalent to compact convergence on
\mathcal{F}_{d}
, and this is in turn equivalent to pointwise convergence. Therefore, the
\sigma
-algebras generated by these three topologies on
\mathcal{F}_{d}
coincide, so we could equivalently consider
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{d})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{d}
is equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence.

Example 5.3

(Radial kernels)
.

It is also possible to construct integrally strictly positive definite kernels on
\Phi_{\infty}
, that is, on radial positive definite kernels. The functions in
\Phi_{\infty}
are all Laplace transforms of measures on
[0,\infty)
. Proceeding analogously to Example
5.2
, one can see
\Phi_{\infty}
as a closed subset of
L^{2}(\lambda)
for some probability measure
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
with full support, and use (
5.3
) to define integrally strictly positive definite kernels on
\Phi_{\infty}
.

6
Discussion

Kernels have been found to be fruitful in a variety of domains, and the presented results on kernels on non-standard spaces are likely to be of interest to extend the applicability of kernel methods.
In particular, we enrich the collection of kernels on Hilbert and Banach spaces with classes enjoying proven integrally strictly positive definiteness, which may be relevant in functional two-sample testing
(
Gretton et al. 2012
)
and further endeavors in functional data analysis.
Kernels on measures are of special interest for machine learning with set- and distributional data
(
Muandet et al. 2012
,
Sutherland 2016
,
Szabó et al. 2016
,
Buathong et al. 2020
)
, as well as in the evaluation of probabilistic forecasts
(
Steinwart and Ziegel 2021
)
with kernel scores associated with characteristic kernels providing a versatile source of strictly proper scoring rules. To give a concrete example of a new avenue of research, scoring rules for point processes are just in their infancy
(
Heinrich et al. 2019
,
Brehmer et al. 2021
)
, and results established in the present paper pave the way to novel classes of strictly proper (kernel) scoring rules on such objects.

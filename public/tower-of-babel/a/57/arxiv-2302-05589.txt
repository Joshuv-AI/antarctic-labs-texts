Abstract

The Lagrangian formalism for variational problem for second-order
delay ordinary differential equations (DODEs) is developed. The
Noether-type operator identities and theorems for DODEs of second
order are presented. Algebraic construction of integrals for
DODEs based on symmetries are demonstrated by examples.

Introduction

Lie group analysis is efficient tool for studying ordinary and
partial differential equations since its introduction in the
classical work of Sophus Lie in
[
1
,
2
]
.
A symmetry transformation of a differential equation maps a solution
into a solution. This property is used to obtain new solutions from known
ones, and to classify equations into equivalence classes. It can also
be used to obtain exact analytic solutions that are invariant under
some subgroup of the symmetry group (‘group invariant solutions’).
Applications of Lie groups to differential equations is the topic of
many books and
articles
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Since the fundamental work of
E.Noether
[
9
]
the symmetry group becomes a starting point
to obtain first integrals and conservation laws for differential
equations, which possess Lagrangian or Hamiltonian formulation.
Recently, the connections between a symmetry group and conservation laws
for differential equations that do not have a variational formulation (and
hence they have neither a Lagrangian nor a Hamiltonian)
were developed in
[
5
,
10
]
.

More recently applications of Lie groups of transformations have
been extended to finite-difference, differential-difference,
discrete equations and integral equations
[
11
,

,

,

,

,

]
.

This article is part of a study that aims to extend group analysis
application to another type of equations: delay differential
equations. In the recent
article
[
30
]
there
was presented the Lie group classification of first-order delay
ordinary differential equations. Linear first-order delay ordinary
differential equations were considered in
article
[
31
]
. Lie
group classification of delay second-order ordinary differential
equations was studied in

[

]
. Exact solutions
for delay PDEs were constructed in
[
33

.

The purpose of this article is to construct Lagrangian formalism and
the Noether-type theorems for second-order delay ordinary
differential equations.

As shown below, the variational problem leads to an
ODE with a delay of second-order (in the sense of the order
of a differential equation) with two delays. Thus, we have to set
an initial-value problem for delay ODEs with
two
delays.

We consider the formulation of the initial-value problem for a
second-order ordinary delay differential equation with two constant
delays
\tau

\ddot{u}=F(\ddot{u}^{--},\ddot{u}^{-},\dot{u}^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{--},u^{-},u,u^{--},t,t^{-},t^{--}),\qquad x\in I,

(1.
1)

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is some finite or semifinite
interval,
t^{-}=t-\tau,\quad t^{--}=t-2\tau
. Functions
u^{-}=u(t-\tau),u^{--}=u(t-2\tau)
, while function
F
satisfies the condition

\left({\partial F\over\partial{u}^{--}}\right)^{2}+\left({\partial F\over\partial\dot{u}^{--}}\right)^{2}+\left({\partial F\over\partial\ddot{u}^{--}}\right)^{2}{\not\equiv}0.

(1.
2)

The variable
t
varies continuously over the entire interval, where
Eq. (
1.
1
) is defined.

In order to solve DODEs on some interval
I
one must add initial
conditions to the DODE (
1.
1
). Contrary to the case of
ordinary differential equations, the initial condition must be given
by a smooth function
\varphi(t)
and it’s derivative
\dot{\varphi}(t)
on an initial interval
I_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}
, e.g.

u(t)=\varphi(t),\qquad\dot{u}(t)=\dot{\varphi}(t),\qquad t\in[t_{-2},t_{0}],\quad t_{0}-t_{-2}=2\tau,\quad t_{0}-t_{-1}=\tau.

(1.
3)

For a constant
\tau
this leads to the
method of
steps

[
36
]
for solving the DODE either
analytically or numerically. Hence, for
t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{1}=t_{0}+\tau
we replace

u^{--}=u(t^{--})=\varphi(t-2\tau),\quad\dot{u}^{--}=u(t-2\tau)=\dot{\varphi}(t-2\tau),\quad\ddot{u}^{--}=\ddot{u}(t-2\tau)=\ddot{\varphi}(t-2\tau),

u^{-}=u(t^{-})=\varphi(t-\tau),\quad\dot{u}^{-}=u(t-\tau)=\dot{\varphi}(t-\tau),\quad\ddot{u}^{-}=\ddot{u}(t-\tau)=\ddot{\varphi}(t-\tau),

and this reduces DODE (
1.
1
) to the ODE

\ddot{u}=F(\ddot{\varphi}(t-2\tau),\dot{\varphi}(t-2\tau),{\varphi}(t-2\tau),\ddot{\varphi}(t-\tau),\dot{\varphi}(t-\tau),\varphi(t-\tau),u,\dot{u},t,t^{-},t^{--}),

(1.
4)

which is solved with the initial conditions

u(t_{0})=\varphi(t_{0}),\quad\dot{u}(t_{0})=\dot{\varphi}(t_{0}).

On the second step we consider the same procedure: we solve the
DODE (
1.
1
) where
u(u-\tau),u(u-2\tau)
, it’s
derivatives and the initial conditions are known from the first step.
Thus, one continues at each step solving the ODE with data from the previous step.
This
procedure provides a solution that is in general continuous at the
points
t_{n}=t_{0}+n\tau
.

The method of steps for solving the initial value
problem introduces a natural sequence of intervals

[t_{n},t_{n+1}],\qquad n=-1,0,1,2,...

(1.
5)

We should indicate that peculiarity of the
initial value problem for DODEs with
two
delays is that the
initial conditions should be given on a double length interval

[t_{-2},t_{0}]
.

This paper is devoted to analytic technique of integration of a
second-order DODE, based on its symmetry and Lagrangian formalism. The Noether-type theorems for invariant delay ordinary
differential equations of a second-order are presented. The
integration technique based on a symmetry of an DODE is demonstrated by
examples.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe how to
apply symmetry generators to delay ODEs. The analog of the first
Noether’s theorem of the invariance condition for delay functional
is proved in Section 3. In Section 4 we show that the stationary
value of delay functional along a given group orbit at an arbitrary
point
t,u
is achieved on so called local extremal equation,
special case of which is an equation named the Elsgolts equation.
The invariance of the Elsgolts equation considered in Section 5.
In section 6 we introduce the first integrals for DODE, which are sufficiently
different from those for the ODE. The main operator identities, which
allow us to formulate a Noether-type theorem for the conservation law and
symmetries are given in section 7. In section 8 we demonstrate
application of the developed methodology by examples. Short observation
of the obtained results are considered in the Conclusion.

2
Point symmetries of delay ODEs

We consider a second-order delay ODE (
1.
1
) and its point
symmetries defined by a generator in the standard form:

X={\xi(t,u)}{\partial{}\over\partial t}+{\eta(t,u)}{\partial{}\over\partial u}+...,

(2.
1)

which we prolong for
\dot{u},\ddot{u}
using standard
formulas, and for the shifted values
(t^{-},u^{-},\dot{u}^{-}),(t^{--},u^{--},\dot{u}^{--})
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{X}={\xi(t,u)}{\partial{}\over\partial t}+{\eta(t,u)}{\partial{}\over\partial u}+{\xi^{-}(t^{-},u^{-})}{\partial{}\over\partial t^{-}}+{\eta^{-}(t^{-},u^{-})}{\partial{}\over\partial u^{-}}\\[8.61108pt]
+(D(\eta)-\dot{u}D(\xi))\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}}+(D^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}D^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}+....\\[8.61108pt]
+({\xi(t,u)-\xi^{-}(t^{-},u^{-})}){\partial{}\over\partial\tau}+...,\end{array}

(2.
2)

where
\tau=t-t^{-}=t^{-}-t^{--}=const
, and

D=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial t}+\dot{u}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u}+...,\qquad D^{-}=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial t^{-}}+\dot{u}^{-}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u^{-}}+....

are the operators of the total derivative at points
t
and
t^{-}
,
respectively.

We also use the operators

D^{+}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}+\dot{u}^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{+}}+\ddot{u}^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{u}^{+}}+...,

and the following sum of operators

\bar{D}=D^{-}+D+D^{+}.

We split the operator of the total derivative into three
different operators for preserving their point character.
In this case they can be multiplied (from the left) by any functions
\xi(t,u)
,

\xi^{-}(t^{-},u^{-})
and
\xi^{+}(t^{+},u^{+})
, respectively, without loss
the nature of its tangent conditions (for belonging to a higher order
group or Lie-Bäcklund group, see
[
37
]
).

We also use a pair of shift operators to the right and left hand-sides,
respectively:

S_{+}(f(t,u))=f(t+\tau,u^{+})=f^{+},\qquad S^{-}(f(t,u))=f(t-\tau,u^{-})=f^{-}.

(2.
3)

The shift operators can be expressed using the operators of the
total derivative
D
:

S_{+}={\sum_{s\geq 0}{\frac{\tau^{s}}{s!}}D^{s}},\quad S^{-}={\sum_{s\geq 0}{\frac{(-\tau)^{s}}{s!}}(D^{-})^{s}}.

Here and below, it is assumed that the delay parameter
\tau=const
. To keep
regular intervals invariant with respect to generator
(
2.
1
) we should consider those of them, which satisfy the
condition (see
[
17
]
):

(S_{+}-1)(S_{-}-1)[\xi(t,u)]=0,

(2.
4)

or

\xi^{+}-2\xi+\xi^{-}=0.

(2.
5)

Thus, the criterion of the invariance of equation (
1.
1
)
reads:

\tilde{X}(\ddot{u}-F(\ddot{u}^{-},\dot{u}^{-},\dot{u},u,u^{-},t,...))_{|_{(\ref{DODE1})}}=0,

(2.
6)

provided by condition (
2.
5
).

3
The invariance of delay Lagrangians

Let a one-parameter group of point transformations be:

t_{*}=f(t,u,a),\quad u_{*}=g(t,u,a),\quad{t_{*}}^{-}=f^{-}(t^{-},u^{-},a),\quad{u_{*}}^{-}=g^{-}(t^{-},u^{-},a),

(3.
1)

which are prolonged for derivatives with the help of standard
formulas of Lie group analysis

[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. We need
also a group action on the differential:

dt_{*}=Df(t,u,a)da.

(3.
2)

Considering delay functional

L=\int_{a}^{b}{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})\,dt,

(3.
3)

we chose any inner interval
a+\tau<t_{1}<t_{2}<b
for the anaysis:

L=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})\,dt.

(3.
4)

The functional is referred as invariant, if

L=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})\,dt=\int_{t^{*}_{1}}^{t^{*}_{2}}{\cal L}(t_{*},u_{*},{u_{*}}^{-},{\dot{u}}_{*},{\dot{u}_{*}}^{-})\,dt^{*}.

(3.
5)

The transformed variables and the interval of integration are represented by
means of the initial values and the group parameter

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})\,dt=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\cal L}(f,g,g^{-},\dot{g},\dot{g}^{-},)\,J(\frac{f}{t})dt,

(3.
6)

where
J(\frac{f}{t})
is the Jacobian of the transformation
(
3.
1
).

As the interval is arbitrary one can omit the integration:

{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})dt={\cal L}(f,g,g^{-},\dot{g},\dot{g}^{-},)J(\frac{f}{t})dt.

(3.
7)

Hence, the variational integral is invariant if and only if the
elementary action is invariant with respect to (
3.
7
).

Differentiating (
3.
7
) with respect to a group parameter
a
,
and setting
a=0
, we obtain a criterion for a delay functional to be
invariant.

Theorem 3.1
.

(First Noether’s theorem for delay ODEs)

Functional (
3.
3
) is invariant if and only if

\xi\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t}+\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial{t^{-}}}+\eta\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u}+\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial{u^{-}}}+

(3.
8)

+(D(\eta)-\dot{u}D(\xi))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}}+(D^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}D^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}+{\cal L}D(\xi)=0.

We should note that, in contrast to the classical case of ODEs, there are

two
group orbits passing through the elementary action
{\cal L}(t,u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})dt
. When
\tau\rightarrow 0
the criterion (
3.
8
) becomes the classical first Noether’s theorem.

Remark 1
.

Paper
[
38
]
(see also
[
39
]
) contains wrong formula for the invariance of a
delay Lagrangian. Therefore, the subsequent formulas and
formulations are also incorrect.

4
The Elsgolts extremal equation and local extremal equation

Consider the delay functional (
3.
3
)

L=\int_{a}^{b}{\cal L}(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})\,dt

(4.
9)

and apply the variational operator along a fixed curve (for
considerations of directional variation see

[
40
]
).

The variation of all variables is applied
only
along the
group orbit at the internal point
(t,u),a<t<b
:

\delta t=\xi\delta a,\quad\delta u=\eta\delta a,\quad\delta\dot{u}=({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\delta a,\quad\delta(dt)={D}(\xi)dt\delta a,

where
\delta a
is the variation of the group parameter
a
.

Variation of the functional only along a group orbit yields:

\begin{array}[]{c}\delta L=\int_{a}^{b}\left[\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t}\delta t+\frac{\partial{\cal L}^{+}}{\partial{t}}\delta t+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u}\delta u+\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}\delta u+\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}}\delta\dot{u}+\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}\delta\dot{u}\right)dt+{\cal L}\delta(dt)\right]=\\[8.61108pt]
=\int_{a}^{b}\left(\xi\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial{\cal L}^{+}}{\partial{t}}\right)+\eta\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u}+\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}\right)+({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}}+\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}\right)+{\cal L}{D}(\xi)\right)dt\delta a.\end{array}

(4.
10)

Integration by parts gives (here boundary values are not varied):

\begin{array}[]{c}\delta L=\int_{a}^{b}\xi\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial t}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial t}+D\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+D\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-D({\cal L})\right)+\\[8.61108pt]
+\eta\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial u}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial u}-D\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-D\left({\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial{\dot{u}}}\right)\right)dt\delta a.\end{array}

(4.
11)

The integral is equal to zero for arbitrary interval of integration if
the integrand is zero. Hence, we arrive at
the
local extremal equation
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\xi\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial t}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial t}+D\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+D\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-D({\cal L})\right)\\[2.0pt]
+\eta\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial u}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial u}-D\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-D\left({\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial{\dot{u}}}\right)\right)=0,\end{array}

(4.
12)

which
explicitly depends
on
\xi,\eta
, i.e. on a given
group.

Notice, that a local extremal equation contains
\cal L
and
\cal L^{+}
, and therefore, the equation contains
two delays
. The
operator
{D}
is applied at the point
(t,u)
, and the
directional variation only occurs along the group orbit passing through
the point
(t,u)
.

”Vertical variation”, i.e. variation by
u
when
\xi=0
, yields
the following extremal delay equation

\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u}-{D}(\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}})+\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}-{D}(\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}})=\frac{\delta}{\delta u}({\cal L}+{\cal L^{+}})=0,

(4.
13)

This
equation is known since Elsgolts
[
36
]
(see also

[
45
]
,
[
46
]
).
We will call it
the Elsgolts equation
.

We call
the Elsgolts variational
derivative
the following operator

{\frac{\delta}{\delta u}}_{(E)}=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u}-{D}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}}+S_{+}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u^{-}}-{D}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}\right),

(4.
14)

which one can apply to a delay Lagrangian
{\cal L}
. Notice, that the
shift operator
S_{+}
acts on
all
arguments.

The Elsgolts equation operates with
{\cal L}
and
{\cal L}^{+}
,
while the criterion of invariance of a Lagrangian only involves

\cal L
. We should stress that in contrast to a case of ODEs and
PDEs, the Elsgolts equation is generally not an
equation, on a solution of which the Lagrangian achieves its
extremal value.

We also separate out ”horizontal” variation

{\frac{\delta}{\delta t}}({\cal L}+{\cal L^{+}})={\partial{\cal L}\over\partial t}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial t}+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-{D}({\cal L})=0,

(4.
15)

which yields a variational equation for the case
\eta
=0.

The horizontal variational operator is:

{\delta\over\delta t}={\partial\over\partial t}+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+S_{+}\left({\partial\over\partial t^{-}}+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial\over\partial\dot{u}^{-}}\right)\right)-{D}.

(4.
16)

Now one can rewrite the local extremal equation as

\xi{\delta L\over\delta t}+\eta{\delta L\over\delta u}_{(E)}=0.

(4.
17)

The local extremal equation gives a necessary condition for a
Lagrangian to achieve extremal value on the direction of a given
group. The invariance of a Lagrangian is not needed (for directional
variation see
[
40
]
). Connection of the
invariance of a Lagrangian with local extremal equation is the subject of the next section.

5
Invariance of the Elsgolts equation

Consider the generator

X=\xi(t,u)\partial_{t}+\eta(t,u)\partial_{u}

preserving constant delay.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let the coefficient
\xi(t)
of the generator

X=\xi(t)\partial_{t}+\eta(t,u)\partial_{u}

satisfy condition (
2.
5
) and

{\dot{\xi}_{t}}={\dot{\xi^{+}}_{t^{+}}}
. The following identity holds:

\frac{\delta}{\delta u}_{(E)}\left(X{\cal L}+{\cal L}{D}(\xi)\right)=X\left(\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}\right)+(\eta_{u}+{D}(\xi)-\dot{u}\xi_{u})\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}.

(5.
18)

The proof can be obtained by direct calculations.

In more details relation (
5.
18
) can be rewritten as

\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u}-\bar{D}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}}\right)\left(X{\cal L}+{\cal L}\bar{D}(\xi)+X{\cal L}^{+}+{\cal L}^{+}\bar{D}(\xi^{+})\right)\\[8.61108pt]
=X\left(\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u}-\bar{D}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}}\right)\left({\cal L}+{\cal L}^{+}\right)\right)+\left(\left(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial u}-\bar{D}\frac{\partial{}}{\partial\dot{u}}\right)\left({\cal L}+{\cal L}^{+}\right)\right)({\eta_{u}}+\bar{D}(\xi)+\dot{u}\xi_{u}).\end{array}

(5.
19)

From identity (
5.
18
) one can establish the relation between
the invariance of a Lagrangian and the invariance of the corresponding
Elsgolts equation.

Considering Eq.(
5.
18
) on a solution of the Elsgolts equation
one has

{\frac{\delta}{\delta u}}_{(E)}\left(X{\cal L}+{\cal L}{D}(\xi)\right)|_{\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}=0}=X\left(\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}\right)|_{\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}=0}=0.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let the generator
X
satisfy property
(
2.
5
) and
{\dot{\xi}_{t}}={\dot{\xi^{+}}_{t^{+}}}
. Then the
Elsgolts equation is invariant if and only if the following
condition holds:

\frac{\delta}{\delta u}_{(E)}\left(X{\cal L}+{\cal L}{D}(\xi)\right)|_{\frac{\delta{\cal L}}{\delta u}_{(E)}=0}=0

(5.
20)

for any solution of the Elsgolts equation.

Note that Theorem
5.2
well matches with the analogous theorem for
ODE
[
41
]
.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the Lie algebra
L_{4.13}

[
43
]

of the generators

X_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u},\,\,\,X_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\,\,\,X_{3}=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\,\,\,X_{4}=u\frac{\partial}{\partial u},

(5.
21)

which preserves the uniformity of delay parameter
\tau
.

The complete set
of invariants of this Lie algebra consists of the invariants

I_{1}=\frac{u-u^{-}}{\dot{u}(t-t^{-})},\,\,\,I_{2}=\frac{\dot{u}^{-}}{\dot{u}},\,\,\,\,I_{3}=(t-t^{-})\frac{\ddot{u}}{\dot{u}}.

As all generators (
5.
21
) satisfy property
(
5.
20
), the Elsgolts equation
with the Lagrangian
{\cal L}={\cal L}(I_{1},I_{2})
admits the generators (
5.
21
).
Meanwhile the Lagrangian
{\cal L}={\cal L}(I_{1},I_{2})
does not
admit the scaling generator
X_{3}=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
.

6
First integrals for DODEs

The standard form of a solution (integral) of an ODE

I(t,u)=A,

(6.
22)

can not satisfy any initial data which are given on an initial
interval for DODEs. Hence, we have to introduce another form of an
integral.

DODEs (
1.
1
) can contain the dependent variable and its
derivatives at three points
t^{+}
,
t
and
t^{-}
. It is possible
to consider two types of conserved quantities: differential first
integrals and difference first integrals.

Definition 6.1
.

Quantity

I(t^{+},t,t^{-},u^{+},u,u^{-},\dot{u}^{+},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-})=A_{0}

(6.
23)

is called
a differential first integral
of DODE (
1.
1
)
if it holds constant on the solutions of the DODE.

Consideration of differential first integrals needs differentiation
and requires to chose constant delays. We have to define
differentiation of functions, which depend on the dependent variables for
several values of the independent variable. Differentiating
I
at
some point, for example, at the point
t
, we get

\bar{D}(I)=(I_{t^{+}}+I_{u^{+}}\dot{u}^{+}+I_{\dot{u}^{+}}\ddot{u}^{+}){dt^{+}\over dt}+(I_{t}+I_{u}\dot{u}+I_{\dot{u}}\ddot{u})+(I_{t^{-}}+I_{u^{-}}\dot{u}^{-}+I_{\dot{u}^{-}}\ddot{u}^{-}){dt^{-}\over dt},

where

\bar{D}={\partial\over\partial t}+\dot{u}{\partial\over\partial u}+\ddot{u}{\partial\over\partial\dot{u}}+\cdots+{\partial\over\partial t^{-}}+\dot{u}^{-}{\partial\over\partial u^{-}}+\ddot{u}^{-}{\partial\over\partial\dot{u}^{-}}+\cdots\\
+{\partial\over\partial t^{+}}+\dot{u}^{+}{\partial\over\partial u^{+}}+\ddot{u}^{+}{\partial\over\partial\dot{u}^{+}}+\cdots

(6.
24)

Generally, the delay can depend on the independent variable
t
as
well as on the solution
u(t)
of the DODE. Then, the values
t^{+}

and
t^{-}
have the same dependence. It makes sense to restrict the
considered delays by the conditions

{dt^{+}\over dt}=1,\qquad{dt^{-}\over dt}=1

(6.
25)

for all considered
t
. In this case the differentiation does not
depend on a chosen point, and we obtain that the differential
first integrals should satisfy the equation

\bar{D}(I)=I_{t^{+}}+I_{t}+I_{t^{-}}+I_{u^{+}}\dot{u}^{+}+I_{u}\dot{u}+I_{u^{-}}\dot{u}^{-}+I_{\dot{u}^{+}}\ddot{u}^{+}+I_{\dot{u}}\ddot{u}+I_{\dot{u}^{-}}\ddot{u}^{-}=0,

(6.
26)

which should hold on the solutions of the considered DODE.

Conditions (
6.
25
) imply that the delays
should be solution independent. Moreover, integrating these
conditions, we obtain the delay equations that justifies the
choice of the constant delays for Eq. (
1.
1
). If we tried to
consider nonconstant delays, we would have difficulties with
defining differentiation and differential first integrals.

Definition 6.2
.

A function

J(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-},\ddot{u},\ddot{u}^{-})

(6.
27)

is called
a difference first integral
of DODE (
1.
1
) if
it satisfies the equation

(S_{+}-1)J=0

(6.
28)

on solutions of the DODE.

We illustrate the definitions of the first integrals on a simple
example.

Example 6.1
.

DODE

\ddot{u}^{+}=\ddot{u}^{-}

has differential first integral

I=\dot{u}^{+}-\dot{u}^{-},

and difference first integral

J=\ddot{u}-\ddot{u}^{-}.

Differential first integrals are constant on the solutions of
DODEs. In this case, the difference first integral should not be
constant on DODE solutions, they can be periodic
functions with period
\tau
, where
\tau
is the delay parameter.

7
The Noether-type identity for delay Lagrangians

In this Section we introduce the main operator identity, which relates
together the invariance of a Lagrangian and first integral of local extremal equation.

We consider a Lagrangian function

{\cal L}={\cal L}(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-}),

(7.
29)

which depends on
t,t^{-}
, and we associate its invariance with
first integral.

Lemma 7.1
.

The following identity holds (
\tau
=const):

\begin{array}[]{c}\xi\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t}+\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t^{-}}+\eta\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u}+\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial{u^{-}}}+({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}}+\\[8.61108pt]
+(D^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}D^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}+{\cal L}{D}(\xi)\equiv\\[8.61108pt]
\equiv\eta\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial u}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial u}-{D}\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-{D}\left({\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial{\dot{u}}}\right)\right)+\\[8.61108pt]
+\xi\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial t}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial t}+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-{D}({\cal L})\right)+\\[8.61108pt]
+\bar{D}\left[\xi{\cal L}+(\eta-\dot{u}\xi)\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)\right]+\\[8.61108pt]
+(1-S_{+})\left(\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t^{-}}+\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u^{-}}+({D}^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}{D}^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}\right)=0.\end{array}

(7.
30)

Identity (
7.
30
) allows one to specify
different DODEs on the right-hand side and formulate various appropriate
theorems (a similar idea was implemented for ODEs in

[
42
]
).

Theorem 7.2
.

(
Delay version of Noether’s theorem for
a local extremal equation
)

Let a functional
{\cal L}
be invariant with respect to a
one-parameter group of transformations. Then the invariance of the Lagrangian gives the integral

\bar{D}\left[\xi{\cal L}+(\eta-\dot{u}\xi)\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)\right]=0

(7.
31)

for each solution of a local extremal equation (
4.
17
),
provided by the difference relation

\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t^{-}}+\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u^{-}}+({D}^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}{D}^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}=

(7.
32)

=\xi\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial t}+\eta\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}+({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}.

The difference integral (or periodic condition) relates
corresponding values at two points and is not a constant for each
point of the considered interval.

Remark 2
.

It should be noted that the local extremal equation (
4.
17
)
depends on second-order derivatives, and usually contains
two
delays.

Consequences of Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
7.2

The
7.2
theorem has an obvious generalization for the following
cases:

1. If an action of a symmetry operator on an elementary Lagrangian
action
{\cal L}dt
gives
\bar{D}(A(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-}))
, then one can add the corresponding function
(-A)
into the
expression of local extremal equation. This is a generalization
similar to that for ODE
[
44
]
.

2. If an action of a symmetry operator on an elementary Lagrangian
action
{\cal L}dt
gives
(S_{+}-1)(B(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-}))
, then one can add the corresponding function
(-B)
into the
expression of difference integral (periodic condition).

Both cases can exist in one action of a symmetry operator on an
elementary Lagrangian action
{\cal L}dt
.

3. It may happens that expression (
7.
32
) is equivalent to
some divergent term
\bar{D}(C(t,t^{-},u,u^{-},\dot{u},\dot{u}^{-}))
:

\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t^{-}}+\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u^{-}}+({D}^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}{D}^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}-

(7.
33)

-\xi\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial t}-\eta\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}-({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}=\bar{D}(C),

then
we arrive to the case 1 (see examples of the linear oscillator
below).

Below we consider two special cases of Noether’s theorem for two
special cases of admitted group, which are in the examples.

Corollary
.

(
First special version of Noether’s theorem for
delay ODEs
)

Let a functional
{\cal L}
be invariant with respect to a
one-parameter group of transformations with
\xi=0
. Then the
invariance of the Lagrangian gives the integral

\bar{D}\left[\eta\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)\right]=0

(7.
34)

for each solution of the Elsgolts equation

{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial u}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial u}-\bar{D}\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-\bar{D}\left({\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial{\dot{u}}}\right)=0,

(7.
35)

under the following periodic condition (difference integral)

\eta^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial u^{-}}+({D}^{-}(\eta^{-})-\dot{u}^{-}{D}^{-}(\xi^{-}))\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}=

(7.
36)

=\eta\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial u}+({D}(\eta)-\dot{u}{D}(\xi))\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}.

Corollary
.

(
Second special version of Noether’s theorem for
delay ODEs
)

Let a functional
{\cal L}
be invariant with respect to a
one-parameter group of transformations with
\eta=0
. Then the
invariance of Lagrangian yields the integral

\bar{D}\left[\xi{\cal L}+\dot{u}\xi\left({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)\right]=0

(7.
37)

for each solution of the following equation

{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial t}+{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial t}+\bar{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)+\bar{D}\left(\dot{u}{\partial{\cal L}^{+}\over\partial\dot{u}}\right)-\bar{D}({\cal L})=0,

(7.
38)

under the following periodic condition (difference integral)

\xi^{-}\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial t^{-}}-\dot{u}^{-}{D}^{-}(\xi^{-})\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial\dot{u}^{-}}=\xi\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial t}-\dot{u}{D}(\xi)\frac{\partial{\cal L^{+}}}{\partial\dot{u}}.

(7.
39)

Remark 3
.

It should be noted that the Theorems formulated above
yield restriction (periodic conditions) on the set of solutions of
the corresponding equations (
7.
35
) and (
7.
38
).

Remark 4
.

It may happens that expression (
7.
36
) or (
7.

equivalent to some divergent term
\bar{D}(C)
, then we add the
term
-\bar{D}(C)
to integrals (
7.

), (
7.

). In these

Conclusion

Lagrangian formalism for variational problem of second-order
delay differential equations (DODEs) was considered in the present
paper. The invariance condition for delay functional was established
and the delay analogue of first Noether’s theorem was proved. It was
shown that the variation of a functional along the only group orbit
gives some equation (named a local extremal equation), which
explicitly depends on a group. For ‘vertical variation’ a local extremal equation degenerates to the Elsgolts equation named after mathematician, who was the first to
derive it. For ‘horizontal’ variation one gets some other DODEs. The
necessary and sufficient condition for the Elsgolts equation to be
invariant is established. The local extremal equation and the
Elsgolts equation have in general
two
delay parameters and it
is far from common consideration of DODEs in the literature. The
appropriate initial value problem for a DODE with two delays was
formulated. The generalization of the term a ‘first integral’ for
DODEs has been given.

The basic Noether-type operator identity and delay analogues of the
Noether theorem for invariant of second-order delay ordinary differential
equations are presented. The integration
technique of DODEs based on symmetry is demonstrated by examples.

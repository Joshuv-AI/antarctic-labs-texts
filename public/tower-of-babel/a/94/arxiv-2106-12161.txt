Abstract
: A matrix-based framework for Bayesian games is presented, using semi-tensor product of matrices. Static Bayesian games are considered first. Matrix expression of Bayesian games is proposed. Three kinds of conversions, which convert Bayesian games to complete information games are investigated, certain properties are obtained, including two kinds of Bayesian-Nash equilibriums. Finally, dynamic Bayesian games are considered. Markoven dynamic equations are obtained for some strategy updating rules.

Keywords
:
static bayesian game, conversion, evolutionary bayesian game, semi-tensor product (STP) of matrices.

Not only because they were born in almost same time period, but also they have similar purpose: to “manipulate” objects according to players’ purpose. Nowadays, more people try to use techniques in game theory to control problems, which are sometimes called game-based control, or game theoretic control

Bayesian game is also called the incomplete information game. Comparing with complete information game, which is also called normal noncooperative game, Bayesian has more practical applications, because games with incomplete information, or uncertainty, are widely existing in real world, for instance
[

]
gives some interesting examples such as Cournot competition under asymmetric information, first-price, sealed-bid auction, etc. Its various applications are investigated
[

,

,

.

Recently, semi-tensor product (STP) of matrices has been used to study some problems in game theory. For instance, there are STP-based investigations for the evolution of dynamic games

, potential games
[

,

]
, vector space structure of finite games
[

, and Boolean games

, just to mention a few.

Potential game was firstly proposed by
[

]
, and then systematically developed in

The purpose of this paper is to provide a framework for Bayesian game. Roughly speaking, the major technique to deal with Bayesian game is to convert it into a normal game, which is also called complete information game. This method was firstly proposed by Harsanyi
[

,
24

[

The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 consists of some necessary preliminaries, including STP, normal noncooperative game, and potential game. Section 3 considers the matrix expression of static Bayesian game. In Section 4, two types fo Bayesian-Nash equilibriums are proposed. In Section 5, three kinds of conversions from Bayesian game to normal game. Section 6 considers two kinds of Bayesian potential games. Their verifications and some properties are presented. In Section 7, the dynamic Bayasian games are investigated. Particular interest has been put on some stratify updating rules, which lead to Markovin dynamic equations. Section 8 is a brief conclusion.

Preliminaries

2.1

STP of matrices

This subsection briefly reviews STP of matrices.

Definition 2.1
.

[
5
,
6
]
:
Let
M\in{\cal M}_{m\times n}
,
N\in{\cal M}_{p\times q}
, and
t=\lcm\{n,p\}
be the least common multiple of
n
and
p
.
The semi-tensor product (STP) of
M
and
N
, denoted by
M\ltimes N
, is defined as

\displaystyle\left(M\otimes I_{t/n}\right)\left(N\otimes I_{t/p}\right)\in{\cal M}_{mt/n\times qt/p},

(1)

where
\otimes
is the Kronecker product.

Note that when
n=p
,
M\ltimes N=MN
. That is, the semi-tensor product is a generalization of conventional matrix product. Moreover, it keeps all the properties of conventional matrix product available
[
6
]
. Hence we can omit the symbol
\ltimes
. Throughout this paper the matrix product is assumed to be STP, and the symbol
\ltimes
is mostly omitted.

The following are some basic properties:

Proposition 2.2
.

1.

(Associative Law)

\displaystyle(F\ltimes G)\ltimes H=F\ltimes(G\ltimes H).

(2)

2.

(Distributive Law)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}F\ltimes(aG\pm bH)=aF\ltimes G\pm bF\ltimes H,\\
(aF\pm bG)\ltimes H=aF\ltimes H\pm bG\ltimes H,\quad a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}.\end{cases}

(3)

Proposition 2.3
.

1.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be two columns. Then

\displaystyle X\ltimes Y=X\otimes Y.

(4)

2.

Let
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
,
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be two rows. Then

\displaystyle\omega\ltimes\sigma=\sigma\otimes\omega.

(5)

The following proposition shows that the STP satisfies the block-multiplication law.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

A=\begin{bmatrix}A^{11}&A^{12}&\cdots&A^{1q}\\
A^{21}&A^{22}&\cdots&A^{2q}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
A^{p1}&A^{p2}&\cdots&A^{pq}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}B^{11}&B^{12}&\cdots&B^{1r}\\
B^{21}&B^{22}&\cdots&B^{2r}\\
\vdots&~&~&~\\
B^{q1}&B^{q2}&\cdots&B^{qr}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where

\begin{array}[]{l}n_{c}(A^{ij})=n_{c}(A^{ik}),\quad 1\leq j,k\leq q,\;\forall i,\\
n_{r}(B^{ik})=n_{r}(B^{jk}),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq q,\;\forall k.\end{array}

(
n_{c}(A)
(
n_{r}(A)
) is the column (row) of
A
.
Then

A\ltimes B=\left(C^{i,j}\right),

where

C^{i,j}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{k=1}^{q}A^{i,k}\ltimes B^{k,j},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,p;\;j=1,2,\cdots,r.

About the transpose, we have

Proposition 2.5
.

\displaystyle(A\ltimes B)^{\mathrm{T}}=B^{\mathrm{T}}\ltimes A^{\mathrm{T}}.

(6)

About the inverse, we have

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume
A
and
B
are invertible, then

\displaystyle(A\ltimes B)^{-1}=B^{-1}\ltimes A^{-1}.

(7)

The following property is for STP only.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a column and
M
a matrix. Then

\displaystyle X\ltimes M=\left(I_{m}\otimes M\right)X.

(8)

Let
m\geq 1
,
n\geq 1
, and
1\leq k\leq mn
. Define

\displaystyle j_{k}:=k(mod~n);\quad i_{k}=(k-j)/n+1.

(9)

Definition 2.8
.

[
5
]
A matrix
W_{[m,n]}\in{\cal M}_{mn\times mn}
, defined by

\displaystyle\Col_{k}\left(W_{[m,n]}\right):=\delta_{mn}^{(j_{k}-1)m+i},\quad k=1,\cdots,mn,

(10)

is called the
(m,n)
-th dimensional swap matrix.

The basic function of the swap matrix is to “swap” two vectors. That is,

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be two columns. Then

\displaystyle W_{[m,n]}\ltimes X\ltimes Y=Y\ltimes X.

(11)

Define a matrix
PR_{n}\in{\cal L}_{n^{2}\times n}
, called the order reducing matrix, as

\displaystyle\Col_{i}(PR_{n})=\delta_{n^{2}}^{(i-1)n+i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(12)

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{M}_{p\times n}
and
B\in\mathcal{M}_{q\times n}
. Then the Khatri-Rao Product of
A
and
B
is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}A*B=[\Col_{1}(A)\ltimes\Col_{1}(B),\cdots,\Col_{n}(A)\ltimes\Col_{n}(B)]\in\mathcal{M}_{pq\times n}.\end{array}

2.2

Normal Non-cooperative Game

Definition 2.11
.

A (finite non-cooperative) normal game is described as a triple
G=(N,S,C)
, where

(i)

N=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
is the set of players;

(ii)

S=\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}
is called the profile, with

\displaystyle S_{i}=\{s^{i}_{1},s^{i}_{2},\cdots,s^{i}_{k_{i}}\},\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

(14)

the set of strategies of player
i
, and
S_{-i}:=\prod_{j\neq i}^{n}S_{j}
;

(iii)

C=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
c_{i}:S\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

\displaystyle c_{i}:=c_{i}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}),\quad s_{j}\in S_{j},\;j=1,\cdots,n,\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

(15)

is called the payoff function of player
i
.

Denote the structure vector of
c_{i}
by
V_{i}^{c}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
. Assume
|N|=n
and
k_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
are fixed, we denote the set of such games by

{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
. Note that if
G\in{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
, then
G
is uniquely determined by
\{V_{i}^{c}\;\big|\;i=1,\cdots,n\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{n\kappa},

(16)

where
\kappa=\prod_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
.

Definition 2.12
.

G\in{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
is expressed as
V_{G}=(V_{1}^{c},\cdots,V_{n}^{c})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\kappa}
, where
V_{i}^{c}
is the structure vector of
c_{i}
of
G
,
i=1,\cdots,n
.

Remark 2.13
.

The decision-making (or strategy-selection of each player
j
) is based on the difference of
c_{j}
over two different strategy profiles

c_{j}(s)-c_{j}(s^{\prime}),\quad j=1,\cdots,n;\;s,s^{\prime}\in S.

For instance, the following concepts are depending on such differences:

(i)

A strategy profile
s^{*}=(s_{1}^{*},\cdots,s_{n}^{*})
is a Nash equilibrium if

\displaystyle c_{i}(s_{i}^{*},s_{-i}^{*})\geq c_{i}(s_{i},s_{-i}^{*}),\quad\forall s_{i}\in S_{i},~s_{-i}^{*}\in S_{-i},i=1,\cdots,n.

(17)

It is easy to verify that Nash equilibrium depends on
\{c_{j}(s)-c_{j}(s^{\prime})\;\big|\;j=1,\cdots,n\}
only.

(ii)

In the definition of potential games
[
30
]
it is assumed that there is a pseudo-mixed-logical function
P:\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\cal D}_{k_{i}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that

c_{i}(x_{i},s_{-i})-c_{i}(y_{i},s_{-i})=P(x_{i},s_{-i})-P(y_{i},s_{-i}),\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

It is clear that whether a game is potential depends on
\{c_{j}(s)-c_{j}(s^{\prime})\;\big|\;j=1,\cdots,n\}
only.

(iii)

Consider an evolutionary game, two of the most commonly used strategy updating rules (SURs) are the better and best response dynamics
[
11
]
.

•

Better response SUR: updating its strategy to a strategy in
\{s_{i}\;\big|\;c_{i}(s_{i},s_{-i}(t))>c_{i}(s(t))\}
, chosen uniformly at random;

•

Best response SUR: updating its strategy to a strategy in
\argmax_{s_{i}}c_{i}(s_{i},s_{-i}(t))
, chosen uniformly at random;

One sees easily that they depend on
\{c_{j}(s)-c_{j}(s^{\prime})\;\big|\;j=1,\cdots,n\}
only.

The following proposition is obvious.

Proposition 2.14
.

Two games
G
and
G^{\prime}
are equivalent (for decision making) if there exists a row vector
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
such that

\displaystyle V^{c}_{i}+v=V^{c^{\prime}}_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(18)

From Proposition
2.14
one sees easy that a game (precisely, an equivalent class of games) can be expressed as

\displaystyle W_{G}:=[V^{c}_{2}-V^{c}_{1},\;V^{c}_{3}-V^{c}_{1},\;\cdots,\;V^{c}_{n}-V^{c}_{1}].

(19)

We call (
19
) the equivalent vector form of
G
.

Using equivalent vector form of
G
, one sees that
G_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
is an
(n-1)\kappa
dimensional vector space. That is,

\displaystyle G_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}\sim{\mathbb{R}}^{(n-1)\kappa}.

(20)

2.3

Potential Game

Definition 2.15
.

[
30
]
Consider a finite game
G\in{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
.
If there is a function
P:S\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, such that for every
i\in N
and for every
s_{-i}\in S_{-i}

\displaystyle c_{i}(x,s_{-i})-c_{i}(y,s_{-i})=P(x,s_{-i})-P(y,s_{-i}),\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

(21)

then
G
is called an exact potential game.

The followings are some fundamental properties of potential.

Theorem 2.16
.

[
30
]
If
G
is a potential game, then the potential function
P
is unique up to a constant number. Precisely, if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are two potential functions, then
P_{1}-P_{2}=c_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 2.17
.

[
30
]
Let
P
be a potential function for
G
. Then
s\in S
is an equilibrium point of
G
, if and only if

\displaystyle P(s)\geq P(s_{-i},x),\quad\forall x\in S_{i},\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(22)

Particularly, if
P
admits a maximal value in
S
, then
G
has at least one pure Nash equilibrium.

Corollary 2.18
.

Every finite potential game possesses a pure Nash equilibrium.

The following argument is a generalization of potential equation
[
7
]
.

Assume
G\in{\cal G}_{[n;k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
is potential, define

\displaystyle d_{i}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}):=c_{i}(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})-P(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}),\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(23)

Then it is easy to verify that
d_{i}
is independent of
s_{i}
, denoted by
d_{i}=d_{i}(s_{1},\cdots,\hat{s}_{i},\cdots,s_{n})
.
In vector form (
23
) becomes

\displaystyle V^{d}_{i}\ltimes_{j\neq i}s_{j}=V^{c}_{i}s-V^{P}s,\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(24)

where
s=\ltimes_{j=1}^{n}s_{j}
.

Define

E_{i}:=I_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes{\bf 1}^{T}_{k_{i}}\otimes I_{\beta_{i}},\quad i=1,\cdots,n,

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{1}=1,&\alpha_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},\;j\geq 2\\
\beta_{n}=1,&\beta_{i}=\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}k_{j},\;j\leq n-1.\end{array}

Then (
24
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle V^{d}_{i}E_{i}=V^{c}_{i}-V^{p},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(25)

Solving the first equation to get

V^{P}=V^{c}_{1}-V^{d}_{1}E_{1},

and plugging it into the other equations of (
25
) yield

\displaystyle V^{d}_{i}E_{i}=V^{d}_{1}E_{1}=V^{c}_{i}-V^{c}_{1},\quad i=2,3,\cdots,n.

(26)

Denote

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=(V^{d}_{i})^{T},\quad i=1,\cdots,n;\quad b:=W_{G}^{T}.

(27)

Then (
26
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Psi\xi=b,

(28)

where
\xi=(\xi_{1}^{T},\cdots,\xi_{n}^{T})^{T}
and

\displaystyle\Psi=\begin{bmatrix}-E_{1}^{T}&E_{2}^{T}&0&\cdots&0\\
-E_{1}^{T}&0&E_{3}^{T}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&~&~&~&~\\
-E_{1}^{T}&0&0&\cdots&E_{n}^{T}.\end{bmatrix}

(29)

Summarizing the above argument, we have

Theorem 2.19
.

[
9
]
Let
G\in{\cal G}_{[n,k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]}
.
G
is a potential game, if and only if (
28
) has solution.
Moreover, when a solution exists, then

\displaystyle V^{P}=V^{c}_{1}-\xi_{1}^{T}E_{1}.

(30)

Static Bayesian Game

Definition 3.1
.

[
18
]
A (finite) static Bayesian game (BG)
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)

consists of

(i)

Player set:

\displaystyle N=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}.

(31)

(ii)

Type set:

\displaystyle T=T_{1}\times T_{2}\times\cdots\times T_{n},

(32)

where
T_{i}
is the type set of player
i
with

\displaystyle T_{i}=\{t_{i}^{1},t_{i}^{2},\cdots,t_{i}^{\tau_{i}}\},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(33)

(iii)

Profile and actions:

\displaystyle A=A_{1}\times A_{2}\times\cdots\times A_{n},

(34)

where
A_{i}
is the action set of player
i
with

\displaystyle A_{i}=\left\{a_{i}^{1},a_{i}^{2},\cdots,a_{i}^{r_{i}}\right\},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n,

(35)

and
A_{i}(t_{i}^{j})
is the admissible actions under type
t_{i}^{j}
with

\displaystyle A_{i}(t_{i}^{j})\subset A_{i},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,\tau_{i},\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(36)

(iv)

Payoff functions:

\displaystyle c_{i}:A_{1}(t_{1}^{j_{1}})\times A_{2}(t_{2}^{j_{2}})\times\cdots\times A_{n}(t_{n}^{j_{n}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(37)

(v)

Beliefs:

\displaystyle p_{i}\left(t_{-i}\;|\;t_{i}\right)=\dfrac{Pr(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})}{Pr(t_{i})}=\dfrac{Pr(t_{i},t_{-i})}{\mathop{\sum}\limits_{t^{\prime}_{-i}}Pr(t_{i},t^{\prime}_{-i})},

(38)

where
t_{-i}\in T_{-i}:=\prod_{j\neq i}T_{j}
.
Pr(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})
, called the common prior, is a common knowledge, and
t_{i}
is a private knowledge for player
i
.

Identify
i\sim\delta_{l}^{i}\in D_{l}
, then
\delta_{l}^{i}
is called the vector representation of integer
i.
By virtue of vector representation of actions and types, then the action set
A_{i}
and type set
T_{i}
can be expressed as

A_{i}=\left\{\delta_{r_{i}}^{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq r_{i}\right\};T_{i}=\left\{\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{j}\;|\;1\leq j\leq\tau_{i}\right\},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

Denote by
r=\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}
,
\tau=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
,
R_{-\infty}=R\cup\{-\infty\}
, and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{c}_{i}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})=\begin{cases}c_{i}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}),\quad a_{j}\in A_{j}(t_{j}),\\
-\infty,\quad\mbox{otherwise},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.\end{cases}\end{array}

Then we have

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{i}:\Delta_{r\tau}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{-\infty},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

(40)

Using (
3
), it is obvious that for each
i
there exists a unique row vector
V_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{-\infty}^{r\tau}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{i}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})=V_{i}^{\bar{c}}ta,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(41)

where
a=\ltimes_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
,
t=\ltimes_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}
. Let
V_{G}:=[V_{1}^{\bar{c}},\cdots,V_{n}^{\bar{c}}]
, which is called the structure vector of Bayesian game
G
.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

Here we assume the purpose of each player is to maximize his payment. So using
\bar{c}_{i}
to optimize payment is equivalent to

. If the purpose of each player is to minimize his cost, then in (
3
)

.

(ii)

In fact, (
41

, where
a=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\in\Delta_{r}

.

Bayesian-Nash Equilibrium

This section proposes two kinds of types first.

Definition 4.1
.

There are two kinds of types:

(i)

Types of Nature (TN):

The types are determined by pre-assigned distribution
Pr(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})
, which is a common knowledge.
Each player
i
knows the type
t_{i}
assigned to him. The type is assigned by Nature.

(ii)

Types of Human (TH):

Player
i
has the right to choose
t_{i}
for entering the game. Then
t_{i}
becomes part of strategy for player
i
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.

The expected payoff functions under different kinds of types are defined as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

(i)

TN:

The expected value of player
i
, using actions
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}E^{N}_{i}(a)&:=&E_{i}(a(T))\\
&=&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{t\in T}Pr(t)\bar{c}_{i}(a,t),\\
&=&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{t\in T}Pr(t)V^{\bar{c}}_{i}(a,t),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

(ii)

TH:

The expected value of player
i
, using actions
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
and type
t_{i}^{j}
is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}E^{H}_{i}(a,t_{i}^{j})&:=&E_{i}(a(T)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{j})\\
&=&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{t_{-i}\in T_{-i}}p_{i}(t_{-i}|t_{i}^{j})\bar{c}_{i}(a,t^{j}_{i},t_{-i}),\\
&=&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{t_{-i}\in T_{-i}}p_{i}(t_{-i}|t_{i}^{j})V^{\bar{c}}_{i}ta,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

Proof:

It is easy to verify the results by virtue of (
41
)

\Box

Definition 4.3
.

Consider a (finite) static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.

(i)

TN:

A profile
\left(a_{1}^{*},a_{2}^{*},\cdots,a_{n}^{*}\right)
is said to be a pure Bayesian-Nash equilibrium, if for each
i
and any
t_{i}^{j}\in T_{i}
the following inequalities hold.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}E^{N}_{i}(a^{*}(t)|t_{i})\geq E^{N}_{i}\left(a_{1}^{*}(t_{1}),\cdots,a_{i-1}^{*}(t_{i-1}),a_{i},a_{i+1}^{*}(t_{i+1}),\cdots,a_{n}^{*}(t_{n})|t_{i}\right),\quad\forall t_{i}\in T_{i};\;i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

(ii)

TH:

A profile
\left(a_{1}^{*}(t_{1}^{*}),a_{2}^{*}(t_{2}^{*}),\cdots,a_{n}^{*}(t_{n}^{*})\right)
is said to be a pure Bayesian-Nash equilibrium, if for each
i
and any
t\in T
the following inequalities hold.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}E^{H}_{i}\left(a_{1}^{*}(t_{1}^{*}),a_{2}^{*}(t_{2}^{*}),\cdots,a_{n}^{*}(t_{n}^{*})\right)\geq E^{H}_{i}\left(a_{1}^{*}(t_{1}^{*}),\cdots,a_{i-1}^{*}(t^{*}_{i-1}),\right.\\
~~\left.a_{i}(t_{i}),a_{i+1}^{*}(t_{i+1}^{*}),\cdots,a_{n}^{*}(t_{n}^{*})\right),\forall t_{i}\in T_{i},\;i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

Definition 4.4
.

Consider a (finite) static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.
The belief vector of player
i
for
t_{i}^{j}
is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllccc}p_{i}(t_{i}^{j}):&=&Pr(t_{-i}\in T_{-i}|t_{i}=t_{i}^{j})\\

&&\\
~~~~~p_{i}&=&[p_{i}(t_{i}^{1}),p_{i}(t_{i}^{2}),\cdots,p_{i}(t_{i}^{\tau_{i}})],~~~~i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

Conversions in Bayesian Games

The key idea to deal with Bayesian game is to convert it into a normal game, which is also called complete information game. This method was firstly proposed by Harsanyi
[
22
,
24
,
25
]
, which is called Harsanyi conversion.
Another commonly used transformation is Selten conversion
[
39
]
. Besides Harsanyi conversion and Selten conversion, we provide a new kind of conversion, called Action-Type conversion.

Definition 5.1
.

Consider a finite Bayesian game
G
with prior probability distribution
Pr(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})
. Three conversions are defined as follows:

(i)

Harsanyi Conversion (H-Conversion):
Define

\displaystyle c_{i}^{H}(a):=Ec_{i}(a),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(58)

(ii)

Selten Conversion (S-Conversion): Player
i
knows his type
t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i}
.
Define

\displaystyle c_{i}^{S}(a):=E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(59)

(iii)

Action-Type Conversion (AT-Conversion): Player
i
is able to choose
t_{i}
.
Define

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}c_{i}^{AC}(a,t_{i}):=\left[E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{1}),E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{2}),\cdots,E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{\tau_{i}})\right],\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

The corresponding complete information games are called Harsanyi Bayesian game, Selten Bayesian game, and Action-Type Bayesian game of Bayesian game
G
, respectively.

Theorem 5.2
.

The structure vectors of three conversions are as follows:

(i)

H-Conversion: Denote by

p=\left[Pr(t_{1}^{1},\cdots,t_{n}^{1}),Pr(t_{1}^{1},\cdots,t_{n}^{2}),\cdots,Pr(t_{1}^{\tau_{1}},\cdots,t_{n}^{\tau_{n}})\right]^{T},

then

\displaystyle V_{i}^{H}=V^{\bar{c}}_{i}p,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n,

(61)

where
V_{i}^{H}
is the structure vector of
c_{i}^{H}(a).

(ii)

S-Conversion: For a given
\bar{t}=(\bar{t}_{1},\cdots,\bar{t}_{n})

\displaystyle V_{i}^{S}=V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{\bar{t}_{i}}p_{i}(\bar{t}_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(62)

where
V_{i}^{S}
is the structure vector of
c_{i}^{S}(a).

(iii)

AT-Conversion:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}V_{i}^{AT}&=&V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\left[I_{\tau_{i}}*p_{i}\right],\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,n.\end{array}

where
*
is the Khatra-Rao product of matrices, and
V_{i}^{AT}
is the structure vector of
c_{i}^{AT}(a,t_{i}).

Proof:

(i)

According to Harsanyi Conversion,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllccc}c_{i}^{H}(a)&=&\sum_{t\in T}Pr(t){\bar{c}}_{i}(a,t)\\
&&\\
&=&\sum_{t\in T}Pr(t)V^{\bar{c}}_{i}ta\\
&&\\
&=&V^{\bar{c}}_{i}pa:=V_{i}^{H}a.\end{array}

(ii)

According to Selten Conversion, player
i
knows his type
t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllccc}c_{i}^{S}(a)&=&E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i})\\
&&\\
&=&\sum_{t_{-i}\in T_{-i}}p(t_{-i}|\bar{t}_{i}){\bar{c}}_{i}(a,\bar{t}_{i},t_{-i})\\
&&\\
&=&\sum_{t_{-i}\in T_{-i}}p(t_{-i}|\bar{t}_{i})V^{\bar{c}}_{i}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\bar{t}_{i}t_{-i}a\\
&&\\
&=&V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{\bar{t}_{i}}p_{i}(\bar{t}_{i})a\\
&&\\
&=&V_{i}^{S}a.\end{array}

(iii)

According to Action-Type Conversion,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllccc}c_{i}^{AT}(a,t_{i})&=&\left[E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{1}),E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{2}),\cdots,E(c_{i}(a)|t_{i}=t_{i}^{\tau_{i}})\right]\\
&&\\
&=&\left[V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{t_{i}^{1}}p_{i}(t_{i}^{1})a,~\cdots,~V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{t_{i}^{\tau_{i}}}p_{i}(t_{i}^{\tau_{i}})a\right]\\
&&\\
&=&V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\left[\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{t_{i}^{1}}p_{i}(t_{i}^{1}),~\cdots,~\delta_{\tau_{i}}^{t_{i}^{\tau_{i}}}p_{i}(t_{i}^{\tau_{i}})\right]t_{i}a\\
&&\\
&=&V_{i}^{\bar{c}}W_{[\tau_{i},\prod_{k=1}^{i-1}\tau_{k}]}\left[I_{\tau_{i}}*p_{i}\right]t_{i}a\\
&&\\
&=&V_{i}^{AT}t_{i}a.\end{array}

\Box

Definition 5.3
.

Consider a (finite) static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.

(i)

Harsanyi Bayesian game:

a^{*}\in A
is called a Bayesian-Nash equilibrium for Harsanyi BG (H-BN-E), if
a^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle c_{i}^{H}(a_{i}^{*},a_{-i}^{*})\geq c_{i}^{H}(a_{i},a_{-i}^{*}),\quad\forall a_{i}\in A_{i},\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(87)

(ii)

Selten Bayesian game:

a^{*}\in A
is called a Bayesian-Nash equilibrium for Selten BG (S-BN-E) with respect to pre-assigned
\bar{t}
, if
a^{*}
satisfies

\displaystyle c_{i}^{S}(a_{i}^{*},a_{-i}^{*})\geq c_{i}^{S}(a_{i},a_{-i}^{*}),\quad\forall a_{i}\in A_{i},\;i=1,\cdots,n.

(88)

(iii)

Action-Type Bayesian game:

(t^{*},a^{*})\in T\times A
is called a Bayesian-Nash equilibrium for Action-Type BG (AT-BN-E), if
(t^{*},a^{*})
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i}^{*},t_{i}^{*},a_{-i}^{*})\geq c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i},t_{i},a_{-i}^{*}),\quad\forall t_{i}\in T_{i},\forall a_{i}\in A_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.\end{array}

Theorem 5.4
.

Let O-BN-E be the original B-N equilibrium from Definition
4.3
. Compered with original BN, we have the following results:

(i)

a^{*}~\mbox{is an O-BN-E}\begin{array}[]{c}\Rightarrow\\

(ii)

a^{*}~\mbox{is an O-BN-E}\begin{array}[]{c}\Rightarrow\\

(iii)

a^{*}~\mbox{is an O-BN-E}\begin{array}[]{c}\not\Rightarrow\\
\Leftarrow\end{array}(t^{*},a^{*})~\mbox{is an AT-BN-E}.

Bayesian Potential Game

Definition 6.1
.

[
26
]
Consider a (finite) static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.
It is called a TN weighted potential game, if there exists a function
F:T\times A\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
t\in T

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i},t)-c_{i}(a_{i},a_{-i},t)=w_{i}\left(F(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i},t)-F(a_{i},a_{-i},t)\right),\\
~~~~~a^{\prime}_{i},a_{i}\in A_{i},\;a_{-i}\in A_{-i},\;t\in T,\end{array}

where
w_{i}>0
. When
w_{i}=1
, it is a TN potential game,
\forall i
.

Definition 6.2
.

Consider a (finite) static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
. It is called a TH weighted potential game, if there exists a function
F:T\times A\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
t_{i},t_{i}^{\prime}\in T_{i},~a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}\in A_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i},t_{i}^{\prime},t_{-i})-c_{i}(a_{i},a_{-i},t_{i},t_{-i})=w_{i}\left(F(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i},t_{i}^{\prime},t_{-i})-F(a_{i},a_{-i},t_{i},t_{-i})\right),\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\forall\;a_{-i}\in A_{-i},t_{-i}\in T_{-i}.\end{array}

where
w_{i}>0
. When
w_{i}=1
, it is a TH potential game,
\forall i
.

The following proposition comes from definition immediately.

Proposition 6.3
.

\mbox{TH potential game}~\Rightarrow\mbox{TN potential game}.

Remark 6.4
.

[
35
]
It is obvious that a Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
is TN-potential, if and only if, under each type it is potential. Hence, we can use potential equation [6] to check whether a game is potential and to construct potential function. Please refer to Theorem
2.19
.

Definition 6.6
.

Consider a finite static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.

(i)

It is called a Harsanyi weighted potential game, if there exists a function
Q^{H}:A\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}\in A_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}^{H}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i})-c_{i}^{H}(a_{i},a_{-i})=w_{i}\left(Q^{H}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i})-Q^{H}(a_{i},a_{-i})\right),~~~\forall\;a_{-i}\in A_{-i}.\end{array}

where
w_{i}>0
. When
w_{i}=1
,
\forall i
, it is a Harsanyi potential game.

(ii)

It is called a Selten weighted potential game for the pre-assigned
t\in T
, if there exists a function
Q^{S}:A\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}\in A_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}^{S}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i})-c_{i}^{S}(a_{i},a_{-i})=w_{i}\left(Q^{S}(a_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i})-Q^{S}(a_{i},a_{-i})\right),~~~\forall\;a_{-i}\in A_{-i}.\end{array}

where
w_{i}>0
. When
w_{i}=1
,
\forall i
, it is a Selten potential game.

(iii)

It is called a Action-Type weighted potential game, if there exists a function
Q^{AT}:T\times A\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}\in A_{i}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i}^{\prime},t_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i})-c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i},t_{i},a_{-i})=w_{i}\left(Q^{AT}(a_{i}^{\prime},t_{i}^{\prime},a_{-i},t_{-i})-Q^{AT}(a_{i},t_{i},a_{-i},t_{-i})\right),~\forall\;a_{-i}\in A_{-i}.\end{array}

where
w_{i}>0
. When
w_{i}=1
,
\forall i
, it is a Action-Type potential game.

The following proposition is obvious.

Proposition 6.7
.

Consider a finite static Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
.

(i)

If
G
is a Harsanyi potential game, then
G
has at least one H-BN-E (the potential maximizer).

(ii)

If
G
is a Selten potential game, then
G
has at least one S-BN-E (the potential maximizer).

(iii)

If
G
is a Action-Type potential game, then
G
has at least one AT-BN-E (the potential maximizer).

Proposition 6.8
.

Consider a finite Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
. If
G
is a TN or TH potential game with potential function
F(a,t)
, then the corresponding Harsanyi Bayesian game is a Harsanyi potential game with potential function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllcc}Q^{H}(a)&=&\sum_{t\in T}Pr(t)F(a,t)\\

\end{array}

where
V_{F}^{c}
is the structure vector of
F(a,t).

Dynamic Bayesian Games

7.1

Dynamics of Selten Bayesian Games

For a repeated Bayesian game, its dynamics depends on two facts: (1) the strategy updating rule (SUR); (2) the conversion. Cosider a Bayesian game, a fundamental assumption in this section is, for a given conversion, the player updates his action or type according to his SUR to optimize the corresponding conversion payoff.

Definition 7.1
.

(i)

The dynamics is called asynchronous if at each time there is only one player who is allowed to update his action or type.

(ii)

The dynamics is called synchronous if at each time all players can update actions or types synchronously.

Consider a Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
. Assume the conversion is S-Conversion. As the dynamics depends on the information acquired, we assume player
i
knows his type
\bar{t}_{i}\in T_{i}
and other players’ actions
a_{-i}(k)
at time
k+1
. We introduce the following asynchronous dynamics for Selten Bayesian games.

(i)

If the SUR is asynchronous myopic best response adjustment (MBRA), then at time
k
there is only one player (say player
i
) to update his action as follows

\displaystyle a_{i}(k+1)=\argmax_{a_{i}\in A_{i}}E(c_{i}(a_{i},a_{-i}(k))|t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i}),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(117)

where
\bar{t}=(\bar{t}_{1},\bar{t}_{2},\cdots,\bar{t}_{n})
the pre-assigned type profile.

(ii)

If the SUR is asynchronous logit response (LR), then at time
k
there is only one player (say player
i
) to select
a_{i}
according to the following probability

\displaystyle Pr(a_{i}(k+1)=a_{i}|a(k))=\dfrac{e^{\frac{1}{T}E(c_{i}(a_{i},a_{-i}(k))|t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i})}}{\sum_{a^{\prime}_{i}\in A_{i}}e^{\frac{1}{T}E(c_{i}(a^{\prime}_{i},a_{-i}(k))|t_{i}=\bar{t}_{i})}},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(118)

We give an example to show how to get the dynamic equation for repeated Bayesian game under S-Conversion.

Theorem 7.4
.

Consider a Selten Bayesian potential game

. Suppose each player updates its action according to asynchronous LR. Then

(i)

to
a^{\prime}
under the dynamics of asynchronous LR.

is an invariant distribution, we only need to prove that the following detailed balance condition is satisfied

\mu_{T}(a)P(a;a^{\prime})=\mu_{T}(a^{\prime})P(a^{\prime};a),~~\forall a,a^{\prime}\in A.

If
a=a^{\prime}

a^{\prime}

Dynamics of Action-Type Bayesian Games

Consider a Bayesian game
G=(N,T,A,c,Pr)
. Assume the conversion is AT-Conversion. Unlike Selten Bayesian game, the SUR of AT Bayesian game consists of action updating rule (AUR) and type updating rule (TUR). If the AUR of player
i
is
f_{i}
, and the TUR of player
i
is
g_{i}
, then the SUR is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{cases}a_{i}(k+1)=f_{i}(a_{1}(k),\cdots,a_{n}(k),t(k)),\\
t_{i}(k+1)=g_{i}(a_{1}(k),\cdots,a_{n}(k),t(k)).\end{cases}\end{array}

The player can update its action and type, concurrently or separately. Assume in the decision-making process player
i
knows his type
t_{i}(k)
and other player’s actions
a_{-i}(k)
at time
k+1
, but he doesn’t know other player’s type
t_{-i}(k)
.

Firstly, we design different asynchronous MBARs for Action-Type Bayesian games, which are shown as follows:

(i)

The SUR is called asynchronous concurrent MBRA (C-MBRA), if at time
k
the updating player
i
chooses his action and type concurrently, which is shown as follows

\displaystyle a_{i}(k+1)\ltimes t_{i}(k+1)=\argmax_{(a_{i},t_{i})\in A_{i}\times T_{i}}c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i},t_{i},a_{-i}(k)),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(121)

(ii)

The SUR is called asynchronous separate MBRA (S-MBRA), if at time
k
the updating player
i
either chooses his action as follows

\displaystyle a_{i}(k+1)=\argmax_{a_{i}\in A_{i}}c_{i}^{AT}(a_{i},t_{i}(k),a_{-i}(k)),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(122)

or selects type as follows

\displaystyle t_{i}(k+1)=\argmax_{t_{i}\in T_{i}}c_{i}^{AT}(t_{i},a_{i}(k),a_{-i}(k)),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(123)

Theorem 7.6
.

G=(N,A,T,c,Pr)
. If each player updates its action according to asynchronous C-MBAR or S-MBAR, then the dynamics converges to AT-BN-E.

Proof:

Suppose the action-type profile at time
k
is
(a(k),t(k))
. Suppose the updating player is
i
at time
k
using asynchronous C-MBAR, then

\begin{array}[]{cccl}&&Q^{AT}(a(k+1),t(k+1))-Q^{AT}(a(k),t(k))\\
&=&c^{AT}_{i}(a(k+1),t_{i}(k+1))-c^{AT}_{i}(a(k),t_{i}(k))\\
&=&c^{AT}_{i}(a_{i}(k+1),a_{-i}(k),t_{i}(k+1))-c^{AT}_{i}(a_{i}(k),a_{-i}(k),t_{i}(k))\geq 0,\\
\end{array}

where

Since the action profile is finite, after finite steps, the asynchronous MBAR will converge to the potential maximizer, which is an AT-BN-E of

Conclusion

The main purpose of this paper is to provide a fundamental framework for finite Bayesian games, using STP expression of finite games.

Three conversions from incomplete information to complete information are discussed, which are Harsanyi, Selten, and AT conversions respectively. Formulas are obtained for three conventions respectively.
In addition, Bayesian potential game is also investigated.
Finally, the evolutive equations for dynamic Bayesian games are also provided according to SURs and conversions.

There are many problems remain for further investigation. For instance, the following are some challenging topics:

(i)

A generalization: State-based game. (As time-varying types.)

(ii)

Deal with all history based knowledge SUR. (Truncation)

(iii)

Learning-based optimization.

(iv)

Calculating Bayesian Nash equilibrium.

(v)

Dynamic Bayesian Nash equilibrium.

(vi)

Application of Potential Bayesian game.

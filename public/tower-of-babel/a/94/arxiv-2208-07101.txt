Liouville Theorem on Ricci shrinkers with constant scalar curvature and its application
Weixiong Mai, Jianyu Ou

Abstract
. In this paper we consider harmonic functions on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. A Liouville theorem is proved without using gradient estimate : any bounded harmonic function is constant on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. As an application, we show that the space of harmonic functions with polynomial growth has finite dimension.

1.
Introduction

The classical Liouville theorem which is named after Joseph Liouville states that any bounded holomorphic function on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
must be constant, which also holds for harmonic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It has a huge impact crossing many fields such as complex analysis, partial differential equations, geometry and so on.
In 1975, S. T. Yau
[
35
]
proved that any bounded harmonic function must be constant on an
n
-dimension manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, and later, he and S. Y. Cheng
[
4
]
showed a gradient estimate on the manifold with Ricci curvature lower bounded to give an effective version of the Liouville theorem, where the gradient estimate method plays a crucial role in the study of harmonic function on complete manifolds. Since Yau’s seminal paper, the Liouville type property and the gradient method have been generalized to many partial differential equations on manifolds (see e.g.
[
15
,
8
]
and the references therein for more information).

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)
the space of harmonic functions with polynomial growth at most
d
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)=\{u:u(x)\text{ is harmonic on }M^{n};\sup_{B_{p}(r)}|u(x)|\leq C(1+r)^{d}\text{ for some }C>0\},

where
B_{p}(r)
is the geodesic ball with radius
r
centered at
p.

In
[
36
, 1987]
, Yau gave a conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, then
\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{d}(M))<\infty
for any
d>0.

This conjecture can be regard as a more general Liouville property.
The conjecture was firstly proved by P. Li and L.-F. Tam for the case
d=1
in
[
16
]
, and for the
2
-dimensional case (
n=2
) in
[
17
]
.
T. H. Colding and W. P. Minicozzi (
[
6
,
7
]
) completely proved the conjecture in 1997. In
[
6
]
, they proved the result of finite dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)
under weaker assumptions:
M
is a complete manifold satisfying a volume doubling bound and a scale-invariant Poincare inequality. One can see more in
[
8
]
. In fact, the study of
\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)
has been further developed, see e.g.
[
34
,
10
,
14
]
for the existence of non-constant functions in
\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)
, and see
[
19
,
25
]
for the study of Yau’s conjecture in complete Kähler manifolds.

In this paper, we consider a Liouville theorem and the space of harmonic function with polynomial growth on complete gradient Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature. In fact, the study of Liouville type theorem and
Conjecture
1.1
in gradient shrinking Ricci solitons have been also considered by some researchers, recently. To state the results, we introduce some notations.

A complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton if there exists a smooth function
f
on
M
satisfying the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla\nabla f={\frac{1}{2}}g,

where
\mathrm{Ric}
is the Ricci tensor and
\nabla\nabla f
is the Hessian of the function
f
.
The function
f
is called a potential function of the gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. Ricci soliton can be seen as natural extensions of Einstein metrics. Obviously, if
f
is a constant function, the gradient Ricci soliton is simply an Einstein manifold. On the other hand, gradient Ricci solitons are also self-similar solutions to Hamilton’s Ricci flow and play an important role in the study of formation of singularities in the Ricci flow. We refer to
[
1
]
for a nice survey on the subject.

For shrinking Ricci solitons, many people studied the
f
-harmonic functions, that is the function
u
such that
\triangle u-<\nabla u,\nabla f>=0
. H. Ge and S. Zhang
[
12
]
showed that any positive
f
-harmonic function on shrinkers is constant. However, for the standard harmonic function on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons, we do not know whether any bounded harmonic function is constant. In
[
20
]
O. Munteanu and N. Sesum proved the harmonic function
u
with
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv<\infty
on gradient shrinking Kahler-Ricci soliton is constant.

As an analogue of Yau’s conjecture (
Conjecture
1.1
), an interesting question is following:

Question 1.1
.

Let
M
be a complete shrinking Ricci soliton, then
\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{d}(M))<\infty
for any
d>0.

For this question, the following results are known. O. Munteanu and J. Wang
[
23
]
showed that the space of holomorphic functions with polynomial growth on Kahler-Ricci shrinkers has finite dimension. Recently, J.-Y. Wu and P. Wu
[
33
]
proved that the space of harmonic functions with polynomial growth on complete non-compact shrinkers with
R(x)d^{2}(x,o)\leq c_{0}
has finite dimension, where
d(x,o)
is the distance function from point
x\in M
to a fixed poiont
o\in M
.

In this paper we will prove a Liouville theorem and affirmatively answer
Question
1.1
on a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature. Our main results are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a complete non-compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature. Any bounded harmonic function on
M
is constant.

Since Kähler-Ricci solitons are also Ricci solitons, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

All bounded harmonic (holomophic) functions on complete gradient shrinking Kähler-Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature are constant.

Consequently, using Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a uniform doubling property of harmonic functions with polynomial growth. By the argument in
[
23
]
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a complete non-compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature, then
\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{d}(M))<\infty
for any
d>0.

Recently, Colding and Minicozzi
[
9
]
considered the solution of the heat equation which is often called caloric functions. We say
u\in\mathcal{P}_{d}(M)
if
u
is an ancient caloric function and for some
p\in M
and a constant
C_{n}
such that

\sup_{B_{p}(r)\times[-r^{2},0]}|u|\leq C_{u}(1+r)^{d},\ \forall r>0.

They proved that if a complete manifold
M
has polynomial volume growth and
k
is a nonnegative integer, then

\dim\mathcal{P}_{2k}(M)\leq\sum^{k}_{i=0}\dim\mathcal{H}_{2i}(M).

By Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a complete non-compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature, then
\text{dim}(\mathcal{P}_{d}(M))<\infty
for any
d>0.

Remark 1.1
.

In many studies of Yau’s conjecture (
Conjecture
1.1
), the method of frequency function plays a crucial role (see
[
6
,
7
,
34
]
). In this paper our argument, including the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, is also based on the method of frequency function. However, instead of using the frequency function given in the above references, our frequency function is from
[
37
]
(see also
[
26
]
), which has its own advantage in computation.
It should be also noted that J. Ou in
[
26
]
proved a monotonicity result for the used frequency function on gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature, which plays a role in our proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, a lot of attention has been paid to the study of gradient Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature in recent years.

Peterson and Wylie
[
29
]
defined a gradient Ricci soliton
(M,g,f)
to be rigid if it is a flat bundle
N\times_{\Gamma}\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
N
is an Einstein manifold,
\Gamma
acts freely on
N
and by orthogonal transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
f=\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. They
[
29
]
proved that a gradient Ricci soliton is rigid if and only if it has constant scalar curvature and is radially flat, i.e.
Rm(\nabla f,\cdot,\nabla f,\cdot)=0
.

H.-D. Cao proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2

(H.-D. Cao)
.

Gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature are rigid.

Fernández-López and García-Río
[
11
]
investigated gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature using isoparametric theory, they proved that the constant scalar curvature has to be
0,\ 2\lambda,\ ...,\ (n-1)\lambda
, or
n\lambda
. They
[
11
]
classified four-dimensional and six-dimensional gradient Kähler-Ricci solitons of constant scalar curvature. J.-Y. Wu, P. Wu and Wylie
[
32
]
classified four-dimensional gradient Kähler-Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature, independently. Finally, X. Cheng and D. Zhou
[
5
]
finished the classification of 4-d gradient Ricci shrinking solitons with constant scalar curvature.

Whether
Conjecture
1.2
for high dimension holds or not is still unknown.
In some sense Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
provide some positive information on this conjecture since our results are expected if the conjecture has an affirmative answer.

The paper is organized as follows. We recall some properties of gradient shrinking Ricci solitons in Section 2. We will prove Theorem
1.1
in Section 3. Theorem
1.2
will be proved in Section 4.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall some basic results for gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Hamilton
[
13
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g_{ij},f)
be a complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. Then, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}R=2R_{ij}\nabla_{j}f

and

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}-f=C_{0}

for some constant
C_{0}
.

Lemma 2.2

(B.-L. Chen
[
3
]
, Pigola-Rimoldi-Setti
[
28
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. Then the scalar curvature is nonnegative. Moreover, the scalar curvature is positive unless
(M^{n},g,f)
is the Gaussian soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^{2})
.

In constant scalar curvature case,
[
11
]
, Fernández-López and García-Río gave the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1

(Fernández-López& García-Río
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature. Then the scalar curvature
R\in\{0,{1\over 2},1,...,{n\over 2}\}
. Moreover, no complete gradient shrinking Ricci soliton may exist with
R={1\over 2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
R={n\over 2}
, we obtain
M
is n-dimension Einstein manifold. In fact when
(M,g,f)
is a complete non-compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
R
, then
R\in\{0,1,{3\over 2},2,...,{{n-1}\over 2}\}
.

H.-D. Cao and Zhou
[
2
]
proved that the potential function of gradient shrinking Ricci solitons grows quadratically,

Lemma 2.3

(Cao-Zhou
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,f)
be a complete non-compact gradient shrinking Ricci soliton. Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda}{2}(r(x)-c_{1})^{2}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}(r(x)+c_{2})^{2}\end{split}

where
r(x)=d(x_{0},x)
is the distance function from some fixed point
x_{0}\in M
.

Note that if we normalize
f
to
f_{0}
by adding the constant
C_{0}
, then we have

(1)

\displaystyle{}R+|\nabla f_{0}|^{2}=f_{0}

Moreover, if the scalar curvature
R
is a constant and we set the new
f=f_{0}-R
, we obtain

(2)

\displaystyle{}2R_{ij}\nabla_{j}f=\nabla_{i}R=0

and

(3)

\displaystyle{}|\nabla f|^{2}=f.

Here
f
is also a potential function of
M
.
Let
\rho=2\sqrt{f}
, then
|\nabla\rho|\equiv 1
. In the rest of the paper,
f
is the one satisfying (
3
) and (
2
).

Let
(M,g,f)
be a shrinking Ricci soliton. Set
D_{t}:=\{x\in M|f(x)\leq t\}
.
In
[
5
]
, Cheng and Zhou have already obtained the volume growth of
D_{t}
. Here we prove it again:

Theorem 2.1

(Cheng-Zhou
[
5
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete shrinking Ricci soliton with constant scalar curvature
R
as above.
\Omega(r):=\{x\in M|\rho(x)\leq r\}.
Then
Vol(\Omega(r))=cr^{n-2R}
for some constant
c
.

Proof:

By the method in
[
2
]
:

Let
V(r)=\int_{\Omega(r)}dV
. By co-area formula,
V(r)=\int^{r}_{0}\int_{\partial\Omega(s)}{1\over|\nabla\rho|}dAds
. That means

V^{\prime}(r)=\int_{\partial\Omega(r)}{1\over|\nabla\rho|}dA=\int_{\partial\Omega(r)}dA.

First we have

\displaystyle(n-2R)V(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nV(r)-2\int_{\Omega(r)}RdV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega(r)}\triangle fdV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\partial\Omega(r)}\nabla f\cdot{\nabla\rho\over|\nabla\rho|}dA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\partial\Omega(r)}|\nabla f|dA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rV^{\prime}(r).

That means
{V(r)\over V^{\prime}(r)}={n-2R\over r}
, i.e.
\log V(r)=(n-2R)\log r+\log(V(1)).
So
V(r)=cr^{n-2R}
, where
c=V(1)
is a constant.

\square

Remark 2.2
.

By Theorem
2.1
, we obtain
Vol(D_{t})=ct^{{n\over 2}-R}
for some positive constant
c
. The area of
\partial D_{t}
is
Area(D_{t})=c({n-2R})t^{{n\over 2}-R-{1\over 2}}
.

Now let us introduce some notations.
For a harmonic function
u
, we define

H(t)=\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}(t-f)^{\alpha}dv,

and

J(t)=\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}(t-f)^{\alpha+1}

with
\alpha\geq 0.
In particular, we denote by

h(t)=\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv

for
H(t)
with
\alpha=0.

Then we define the frequency function as

N(t)=\frac{J(t)}{H(t)}.

When
\alpha\geq 2,
a direct computation yields

(4)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}}{t}H(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\int_{D_{t}}R(t-f)^{\alpha-1}u^{2}dv+\frac{2}{t(\alpha+1)}J(t)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{D_{t}}R(t-f)^{\alpha}u^{2}dv

and one can show that

H(t)\leq t^{\alpha}h(t)

and for any
0<t<s
,

h(t)\leq{H(s)\over{(s-t)^{\alpha}}}.

Moreover, in
[
26
]
it is also shown that
t^{\sqrt{n}-1}N(t)
with
\alpha\geq 2
is nondescreasing for gradient shrinking Ricci solitons with constant scalar curvature, which plays a role in the present paper.
We refer to
[
26
]
for general discussions on
N(t).

In this paper we will focus on the case of constant scalar curvature
R
. As mentioned previously, we choose the potential function
f
with
|\nabla f|^{2}=f
.
By (
4
), we have

(5)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(t)={\alpha+{n\over 2}-R\over t}H(t)+{2\over{(\alpha+1)t}}J(t),

and

(6)

\displaystyle{}{d\over{dt}}(\ln H(t)))={{\alpha+{n\over 2}-R}\over t}+{2\over(\alpha+1)t}N(t).

Proposition 2.2
.

If
N(t)\geq m>0
, then
{d\over dt}(t^{-{2m\over{\alpha+1}}}H(t)){>}0
.

Proof:

By (
6
), we see that
{d\over{dt}}(\ln H(t)))>{2\over(\alpha+1)t}N(t)\geq{2m\over(\alpha+1)t}
. Thus we finish the proof.

\square

A harmonic function
u
is said to be with polynomial growth of order
2d>0
if

(7)

\sup_{x\in D_{t}}|u(x)|\leq C(t+1)^{d}.

Note that the above definition coincides with the standard one (cf.
H_{2d}(M)
) by using Cao-Zhou’s result (see Lemma
2.3
).

3.
Liouville theorem on Ricci shrinkers with constant scalar curvature

By the definition of
N(t)
with
\alpha=0,
one has

(8)

\displaystyle N(t)=\frac{J(t)}{h(t)}=\frac{\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}(t-f)dv}{\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\alpha=0,
and
u
is a non-constant harmonic function satisfying
3\delta>u>\delta,
where
\delta
is a positive constant, there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}N(t)=0

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t)=0.

Proof:

For
\alpha=0,
by a direct computation we have

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N(t)&=\frac{\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}(t-f)dv}{\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\int_{D_{t}}\langle\nabla u^{2},\nabla f\rangle dv}{\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{-\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}\triangle fdv+\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma}{\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{t\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\sigma}{\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{th^{\prime}(t)}{h(t)}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\ln h(t))^{\prime}}{(\ln t)^{\prime}}.\end{split}

We first consider
\lim_{t\to 0}N(t)=0,
it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\ln h(t))^{\prime}}{(\ln t)^{\prime}}=\frac{n}{2}-R.

By LHoptial’s rule, we have

(10)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\ln h(t)}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{(\ln h(t))^{\prime}}{(\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R})^{\prime}},

Note that we also have

\displaystyle\delta^{2}ct^{\frac{n}{2}-R}\leq\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv\leq 9\delta^{2}ct^{\frac{n}{2}-R},

which gives

\displaystyle\ln(\delta^{2}c)+\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}\leq\ln\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv\leq\ln(9\delta^{2}c)+\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}.

For small
t
we just assume
\ln\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv<0.
Therefore,

\displaystyle-\ln(9\delta^{2}c)+(-\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R})\leq-\ln\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv\leq-\ln(\delta^{2}c)+(-\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}),

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{-\ln(9\delta^{2}c)}{(-\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R})}+1\leq\frac{\ln\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}\leq\frac{-\ln(\delta^{2}c)}{(-\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R})}+1.

This leads to
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\ln h(t)}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}=1.
Combining with Lemma
3.1
and (
10
), we have
\lim_{t\to 0}N(t)=0.

Similarly, for large
t,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ln(\delta^{2}c)}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}+1\leq\frac{\ln\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}\leq\frac{\ln(9\delta^{2}c)}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}+1,

which implies
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln h(t)}{\ln t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}=1,
and consequently,
\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t)=0.

\square

Now, we can prove the Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
U
be a bounded harmonic function, i.e.,

\displaystyle|U(x)|<{B}<\infty,

where
B
is a positive constant. Let
u(x)=\frac{\delta U(x)}{B}+\delta+\delta,
which means
3\delta>u>\delta,
where
\delta
is a (small) positive constant. Then we have
u
is a constant, and consequently,
U
is a constant.

Proof:

By Proposition
2.1
, we know in the complete noncompact case the scalar curvature must be chosen by
\{0,1,{3\over 2},...,{n-1\over 2}\}
. We consider the following two cases:

Case I:

\frac{n-2}{2}\leq R{\leq\frac{n-1}{2}}

There are two different proofs for
Case I
. The first one is similar to that given in
[
27
, Theorem 11, page 8]
.
First we suppose that
u
is not a constant.
Let
K(t)=\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv,
and then
K^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\partial D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla f|^{-1}d\sigma.

Using integration by parts and Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle K(t)=

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{t}}u\triangle udv+\int_{\partial D_{t}}u\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_{t}}u\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla f|^{-1}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which gives

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{K(t)}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{K^{\prime}(t)}{K^{2}(t)}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}dv}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9\delta^{2}c({n-2R})t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}},

where we have used
\int_{\partial D_{t}}d\sigma=c(n-2R)t^{\frac{n}{2}-R-\frac{1}{2}}
and
3\delta>u>\delta.

Integrating from
t_{1}
to
t_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K(t_{1})}>\frac{1}{K(t_{1})}-\frac{1}{K(t_{2})}\geq\frac{1}{9c\delta^{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}dt.

Since
\frac{n-2}{2}\leq R\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, we have
\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{n}{2}-R\leq 1,
and consequently,
\frac{1}{t}\leq\frac{1}{t^{\frac{n}{2}-R}}\leq\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Therefore, by letting
t_{2}\to\infty,
the right-hand side of the above inequality tends to infinity, which implies that
K(t_{1})=0.
We can conclude that
u
is a constant, which gives a contradiction.

Alternatively, we can also prove
Case I
by Lemma
3.1
immediately. By the definition of
N
with
\alpha=0
(see (
8
)), we have

\displaystyle\frac{t}{2}\int_{D_{\frac{t}{2}}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv\leq\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv=J(t)=N(t)h(t)\leq 9\delta^{2}Vol(D_{1})N(t)t^{\frac{n}{2}-R},

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\frac{t}{2}}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv\leq CN(t)t^{\frac{n}{2}-R-1}.

Since
-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{n}{2}-R-1\leq 0
, and
\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t)=0
by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv=0

by letting
t\to\infty.

Hence,
u
is a constant.

Case II:

0\leq R\leq{{n-3}\over 2}

Assume that
u
is not a constant.
By (
9
), for such
u
we also have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R}{t}h(t)+\frac{2}{t}J(t),

which gives

\displaystyle J(t)=\frac{1}{2}(th^{\prime}(t)-(\frac{n}{2}-R)h(t)).

Then

\displaystyle 2t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}J(t)=t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h^{\prime}(t)-(\frac{n}{2}-R)t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}h(t)=(t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(t))^{\prime}.

Since
t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(t)
is monotone increasing and
3\delta>u>\delta,
we have

\displaystyle c\delta^{2}\leq t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}dv\leq 9c\delta^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle c\delta^{2}\leq\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(t)\leq 9c\delta^{2}

and

\displaystyle c\delta^{2}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(t)\leq 9c\delta^{2}

Note that

\displaystyle t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(t)-\epsilon^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(\epsilon)=\int_{\epsilon}^{t}(s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}h(s))^{\prime}ds=2\int_{\epsilon}^{t}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}J(s)ds.

By letting
\epsilon\to 0
and
t\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle 0<C_{1}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}J(s)ds\leq C_{2}<\infty.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}J(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{D_{s}}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}(s-f(x))dvds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\chi_{\{x:f(x)<s\}}(x)(s-f(x))dvds

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}\chi_{\{s:s>f(x)\}}(s)(s-f(x))dsdv

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\int_{f}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}(s-f(x))dsdv

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\left(\int_{f}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}ds-f(x)\int_{f}^{\infty}s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)}ds\right)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\left(\frac{s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}}{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}\big|_{f}^{\infty}-f(x)\frac{s^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}}{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}\big|_{f}^{\infty}\right)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\left(\frac{f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R-1)}}{\frac{n}{2}-R-1}-\frac{f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R-1)}}{\frac{n}{2}-R}\right)dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\frac{n}{2}-R)(\frac{n}{2}-R-1)}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R-1)}dv,

which is bounded from above and below.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv.

By integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv=

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{t}}u\triangle uf^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv+(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)\int_{D_{t}}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla f\rangle f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}u\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma.

By integration by parts again, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{t}}\langle\nabla u^{2},\nabla f\rangle f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}(\triangle f)f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}dv+(\frac{n}{2}-R)\int_{D_{t}}u^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)-1}dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}u^{2}\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}u^{2}\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma,

where we have used
\triangle f=\frac{n}{2}-R
and
f=|\nabla f|^{2}.

By a combination of the above equalities, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)}\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma+\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}u\langle\nabla u,\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}\rangle d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}}u^{2}d\sigma+t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv.

That is

\displaystyle\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv=(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)\int_{\partial D_{t}}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}}u^{2}d\sigma+t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv.

Note that

\displaystyle J(t)=N(t)h(t),

which implies that

\displaystyle t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}\int_{D_{\frac{t}{2}}}|\nabla u|^{2}dv\leq C_{3}N(t),

where the right-hand side tends to
0
as
t\to\infty
or
t\to 0
given by Lemma
3.1
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv=(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}}d\sigma,

and

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{D_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}f^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+1}dv=(\frac{n}{2}-R+1)\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\partial D_{t}}u^{2}t^{-(\frac{n}{2}-R)+\frac{1}{2}}d\sigma\geq C_{4}>0,

which gives a contradiction.
Hence,
u
is a constant.

\square

4.
Application : dimension estimate

To prove the Theorem
1.2
, we need to show that the following doubling property for harmonic function with polynomial growth.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
d>0,
if
u
is harmonic with polynomial growth with order
2d,
i.e.,

\sup_{x\in D_{t}}|u(x)|\leq C(t+1)^{d},

then for any
1<t<T<\infty,
there exists a large
\lambda
such that

N(t)\leq{(\alpha+1)\lambda^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}),

with
\alpha\geq 2
,
\epsilon>0.

Moreover,

\frac{H(t_{2})}{H(t_{1})}\leq\frac{t_{2}^{L}}{t_{1}^{L}},

or equivalently,
H(2t)\leq 2^{L}H(t)
for
1<t<T,

where
L=\alpha+\frac{n}{2}-R+2\lambda^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}).

Proof:

We denote
N_{u}(t)=N(t)
. If the conclusion does not hold, then for any large
\lambda_{i}
, there exist
u_{i}
and
T>t_{i}>1
such that
N_{u_{i}}(t_{i})=N_{i}(t_{i})>(\alpha+1)\lambda_{i}^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})
.
Note that when
\alpha\geq 2,

t^{\sqrt{n}-1}N(t)
is nondescreasing (see
[
26
]
). Then we have

(11)

\displaystyle t^{\sqrt{n}-1}N_{i}(t)\geq t_{0}^{\sqrt{n}-1}N(t_{0})>(\alpha+1)\lambda_{i}^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}),

which holds for all
t>t_{i}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle N_{i}(t)>(\alpha+1)(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}),

for
\lambda_{i}T>t>T
.
Then applying Proposition
2.2
with
m=(\alpha+1)(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}),
we have

t^{-2(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})}H(t)
is nondescreasing for
\lambda_{i}T>t>T
.
Consequently, we have

t^{-2(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})}H_{i}(t)\geq T^{-2(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})}H_{i}(T)

for
T<t<\lambda_{i}T.

Then, by the volume of
D_{t}
for the constant scalar curvature case (i.e.,
Vol(D_{t})=ct^{\frac{n}{2}-R}
), there holds

\displaystyle ct^{\alpha+\frac{n}{2}-R}\sup_{D_{t}}u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}Vol(D_{t})\sup_{D_{t}}u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{D_{t}}u_{i}^{2}\int_{D_{t}}(t-f)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle H_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle T^{-2(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})}H_{i}(T)t^{2d+2\epsilon+{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle T^{-2(d+\epsilon+\frac{n-R+\alpha}{2})}H_{i}(T)t^{2d+2\epsilon+\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha},

which implies

\displaystyle c\sup_{D_{t}}u_{i}^{2}\geq T^{-2d-2\epsilon-\frac{n}{2}+R-\alpha}H_{i}(T)t^{2d+2\epsilon},

for
\lambda_{i}T>t>T
.

Then we have

\displaystyle cC^{2}t^{2d}

\displaystyle>T^{-2d-2\epsilon-\frac{n}{2}+R-\alpha}t^{2d+2\epsilon}H_{i}(T)

for
\lambda_{i}T>t>T.

Therefore

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}((\lambda_{i}-1)T)^{-2\epsilon}>\widetilde{C}t^{-2\epsilon}>H_{i}(T),

for
\lambda_{i}T>t>(\lambda_{i}-1)T.

Since
\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=\infty,
we have
\lim_{i\to\infty}H_{i}(T)=0,
which implies
\lim_{i\to\infty}u_{i}=0.
Thus, for sufficiently large
i,
we have that
u_{i}
is uniformly bounded. By Theorem
1.1
, we have
u_{i}
is a constant, and then
N_{i}(t)\equiv 0,
which contradicts to
N_{i}(t_{i})>(\alpha+1)\lambda_{i}^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}).

Therefore, there exists a
\lambda
such that

(12)

\displaystyle N(t)\leq{(\alpha+1)\lambda^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2})

for
1<t<T.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle(\ln H(t))^{\prime}\leq\frac{L}{t},\quad 1<t<T,

with
L=\alpha+\frac{n}{2}-R+2\lambda^{\sqrt{n}-1}T^{\sqrt{n}-1}(d+\epsilon+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-R+\alpha}{2}).
Then

\ln\frac{H(t_{2})}{H(t_{1})}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(\ln H(t))^{\prime}dt\leq\ln\frac{t_{2}^{L}}{t_{1}^{L}},

which means
\frac{H(t_{2})}{H(t_{1})}\leq\frac{t_{2}^{L}}{t_{1}^{L}}
.

\square

1.2
is given as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

By Proposition
4.1
the harmonic function
u
with polynomial growth has the doubling property:

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{D(5t)}|u|^{2}dv\leq C(L)\int_{D(t)}|u|^{2}dv,

for
1<t<5t<T
.

Using the same argument in
[
23
]
, we can have the conclusion. For completeness, we include a proof.

By
[
2
]
(see also Lemma
2.3
),
t
can be chosen such that

D(t)\subset B_{p}(3r)

and

B_{p}(4r)\subset D(5t),

where

is a fixed point in
M
and

(14)

\displaystyle{}\int_{B_{p}(4r_{0})}|u|^{2}dv\leq C_{0}(L)\int_{B_{p}(3r_{0})}|u|^{2}dv,

for some
r_{0}
depending only on

.

Note that (
14
) holds for all harmonic function with polynomial growth on

with some

.

Then, by a result of Li
[

\displaystyle{}\int_{B_{p}(3r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2}dv\leq{nV_{p}(3r_{0})\over{\dim{\mathcal{H}_{d}(M)}}}\sup_{B_{p}(3r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2},

where

On the other hand, the Sobolev constant of

can be controlled by a constant depending only on

and

(
[

]
). Then using the Moser iteration for the subharmonic function

, there holds

(16)

\displaystyle{}\sup_{B_{p}(3r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2}dv\leq{C_{1}(n,d)\over{V_{p}(4r_{0})}}\int_{B_{p}(4r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2}dv.

By (
14
), (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{p}(4r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}(L)\int_{B_{p}(3r_{0})}|u_{0}|^{2}dv

.

Maximal and ( m , ϵ ) (m,\epsilon) -Kakeya bounds over ℤ / N ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}} for general N N
Manik Dhar

Abstract
We derive Maximal Kakeya estimates for functions over ℤ / N ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}} proving the Maximal Kakeya conjecture for ℤ / N ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}} for general N N as stated by Hickman and Wright [ HW18 ] . The proof involves using polynomial method and linear algebra techniques from [ Dha21 , Ars21a , DD21 ] and generalizing a probabilistic method argument from [ DD22 ] . As another application we give lower bounds for the size of ( m , ϵ ) (m,\epsilon) -Kakeya sets over ℤ / N ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}} . Using these ideas we also give a new, simpler, and direct proof for Maximal Kakeya bounds over finite fields (which were first proven in [ EOT10 ] ) with almost sharp constants.

Introduction

Throughout this paper
[r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

We first begin with describing a special case of our results for sets as opposed to functions in general. Given a set
S\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
we can determine the largest size of intersection a line in a given direction can have with
S
. We want to lower bound the size of
S
by using this data for every direction. We first define the set of possible directions a line can take in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
.

Definition 1.1

(Projective space
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
)
.

For
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are distinct primes. The Projective space
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
consists of vectors
u\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
upto unit (
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
) multiples of each other such that
u

(
mod
p_{i}^{k_{i}})
has at least one unit co-ordinate for every
i\in[r]
.

For each direction in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
we pick a representative in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. This allows us to treat
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
as a subset of
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. We note then a line
L
in direction
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
is going to be a set of the form
\{a+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. We can now state our set size lower bound.

Theorem 1.2

(Set size lower bounds from maximal intersection sizes over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

Say we are given
S\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
where
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
. We then have the following lower bound,

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle\geq C_{N,n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\sup\limits_{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|\{a+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cap S|^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=C_{N,n}|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|^{-1}\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}\sup\limits_{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|\{a+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cap S|^{n},

where

C_{N,n}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k_{j}\log(p_{j})+1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}.

Note,
\sup_{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|\{a+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cap S|
is the maximal intersection a line in direction
u
can have with
S
. The above bound gives us new bounds for the Kakeya problem over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
and generalizes them. To see that let us first define Kakeya sets.

Definition 1.3

(
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya sets)
.

Let
N,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. A subset
S\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
is said to be
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya when for at least an
\epsilon

u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
there exists a line
L_{u}=\{a+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
in direction
u
such that
|L_{u}\cap S|\geq m
.

We note
(N,1)
-Kakeya sets are often just referred to as Kakeya sets. The following is an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.4

(
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
for general
N
)
.

Say we are given an
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya set
S\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
where
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
and
p_{1}>\ldots>p_{r}
. We then have the following lower bound.

|S|\geq C_{N,n}\epsilon m^{n},

where

C_{N,n}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{k_{j}\log(p_{j})+1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}.

To our knowledge, Corollary

-Kakeya set bounds over

N

The general case of our results are Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
for general
N
which gives norm lower bounds for functions
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
using the data of the largest ‘intersection’ a line in a direction can have with
f
. The size of ‘intersections’ of a line
L
and
f
is
\sum_{x\in L}f(x)
. This information about ‘intersections’ is encapsulated using the following definition.

Definition 1.5

(Maximal function)
.

Given
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
we define the maximal function
f^{*}:{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
as follows,

f^{*}(u)=\sup\limits_{a\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}\left(\sum\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}}f(a+tu)\right).

The most general result we prove will lower bound the norm of
f
by the norm of
f^{*}
. Before describing the statement in its full generality we first state it for
N=p^{k}
.

Theorem 1.6

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

Let
n>0
be an integer,
p
prime and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any function
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
we have the following bound,

\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq C_{p^{k},n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|f^{*}(u)|^{n}]=\frac{C_{p^{k},n}}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right),

where

C_{p^{k},n}=\frac{1}{(2(\lceil\log_{p}(\max_{u}f^{*}(u))+\log_{p}(n)\rceil))^{n}}.

When
p>n
we can improve
C_{p^{k},n}
to,

C_{p^{k},n}=(\lceil\log_{p}(\max_{u}f^{*}(u))\rceil+1)^{-n}(1+n/p)^{-n}.

We note
\log_{p}(\max_{u}f^{*}(u))
is bounded above by
k+\log_{p}(\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}})
which means that as long as the terms are not too big we can take the above inequality as a comparison between the
\ell^{n}
norms of
f
and
f^{*}
.

Bounds of these types were first proven for finite fields in
[
EOT10
]
. Our results will prove these bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
for general
N
.

To state the general case of composite
N
we first need some simple facts which follow from the Chinese remainder theorem.

Fact 1.7

(Geometry of
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

Let
p,N_{0},n,k\in{\mathbb{N}},R={\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}},R_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}/N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}
with
p
prime and co-prime to
N_{0}
. Using the Chinese remainder theorem we know that any co-ordinate in
R^{n}
can be uniquely represented by a tuple in
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\times R_{0}^{n}
. Also any direction in the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}R^{n-1}
can again be uniquely represented by a tuple in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{P}}R_{0}^{n-1}
. Finally, any line
L
with direction
b=(b_{p},b_{0})\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{P}}R_{0}^{n-1}
in
R^{n}
is equivalent to the product of a line
L_{p}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
in direction
b_{p}
and a line
L_{0}\subset R_{0}^{n}
in direction
b_{0}
.

We will need to define a new quantity on which our bounds will depend.

Definition 1.8

(
p
-Maximal weight)
.

For
p
and
N
coprime and
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
we define the
p
-maximal weight
\textsf{mweight}(f,p)
as follows:

Let
L(u)=\{a_{u}+tu|t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
be a line such that
\sum_{x\in L(u)}f(x)=f^{*}(u)
. Using Fact
1.7
we note that the line
L(u)
can be written as a product of lines
L_{p}(u)\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
and
L_{1}(u)\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. We define,

\textsf{mweight}(f,p)=\sup_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1},z\in L_{1}(u)}\sum\limits_{x\in L_{p}(u)}f((x,z)).

Note,
\textsf{mweight}(f,p)
for
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
is simply
\max_{u}f^{*}(u)
.

We can finally state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.9

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
for general
N
)
.

Let
n>0
be an integer and
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
with
p_{i}
primes and
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any function
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
we have the following bound,

\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq C_{N,n}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|f^{*}(u)|^{n}]=\frac{C_{N,n}}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right),

where

\displaystyle C_{N,n}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2(\log(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+1)\lceil\log_{p_{1}}(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+\log_{p_{1}}(n)\rceil}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2(k_{r}+\lceil\log_{p_{r}}(n)\rceil)}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}\log(p_{i})+1)(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}

.

Similarly to the comment after Theorem
1.6
we note that
\log(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))
is bounded above by
k_{1}\log(p_{1})+\log(\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}})
. As before as long as the terms are not too big we can take the above inequality as a comparison between the
\ell^{n}
norms of
f
and
f^{*}
.

The overall argument will be via induction. The base case of Theorem
1.9
is Theorem
1.6
(in fact the constants are slightly better for the
r=1
case). We also note if we apply Theorem
1.9
for
f
equaling the indicator function
{\mathbf{1}}_{S}:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
of a set
S
we will get Theorem
1.2
(it is easy to see that
\textsf{mweight}({\mathbf{1}}_{S},p_{1})\leq p_{1}
). Recall,
{\mathbf{1}}_{S}(x)=1
if
x\in S
and
0
otherwise.

The main ideas in the proof involve using and extending the probabilistic and the polynomial method tools from the papers

,

-Kakeya bounds were not known for non prime power

[

[

Theorem 1.10

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over finite fields)
.

Let
n>0
be an integer,
q
a prime power. For any function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we have the following bound,

\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\underset{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}}{{\mathbb{E}}}[|f^{*}(u)|^{n}]=\frac{1}{2^{n}}|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right).

The first bounds of this type were proven in
[
EOT10
]
where it was also proven for the more general case of varities by reducing to the case of the case of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{q}

{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{q}
as that is where our new argument is being used.

The sharpness of the above theorem is evident if we take the indicator function of Kakeya sets which then gives us the lower bounds from

Preliminaries

2.1
Multiplicities and Hasse derivative

We first review the definitions of multiplicities and Hasse derivatives that will be needed in the proof (see
[
DKSS13
]
for a more detailed discussion). We will allow the definitions to be over an arbitrary field
{\mathbb{F}}
since we will need to apply them both for
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
(for Theorem
1.10
) and also for
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
(for Theorem
1.9
and Theorem
1.6
).

Definition 2.1

(Hasse Derivatives)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field. Given a polynomial
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and an
\mathbf{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
the
\mathbf{i}
th Hasse derivative of
Q
is the polynomial
Q^{(\mathbf{i})}
in the expansion

Q(x+z)=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}}Q^{(\mathbf{j})}(x)z^{\mathbf{j}}

where
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})
,
z=(z_{1},...,z_{n})
and
z^{\mathbf{j}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}^{j_{k}}
.

Hasse derivatives satisfy the following useful property (see
[
DKSS13
]
for a proof). We will only need this property to show that, if
Q^{(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j})}
vanishes at a point then so does
(Q^{(\mathbf{i})})^{(\mathbf{j})}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a polynomial
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we have

(Q^{(\mathbf{i})})^{(\mathbf{j})}=Q^{(\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j})}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\binom{i_{k}+j_{k}}{i_{k}}

We make precise what it means for a polynomial to vanish on a point
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
with multiplicity. First we recall for a point
\mathbf{j}
in the non-negative lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, its weight is defined as
\text{wt}(\mathbf{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}j_{i}
.

Definition 2.3

(Multiplicity)
.

For a polynomial
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and a point
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
we say
Q
vanishes on
a
with multiplicity
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, if
m
is the largest integer such that all Hasse derivatives of
Q
of weight strictly less than
m
vanish on
a
. We use
\textsf{mult}(Q,a)
to refer to the multiplicity of
Q
at
a
.

Note that the number of Hasse derivatives over
{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with weight strictly less than
m
is
\binom{n+m-1}{n}
. Hence, requiring that a polynomial vanishes to order
m
at a single point
a
enforces the same number of homogeneous linear equations on the coefficients of the polynomial. We will use the following simple property concerning multiplicities of composition of polynomials (see
[
DKSS13
]
for a proof).

Lemma 2.4
.

Given a polynomial
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and a tuple
G=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
of polynomials in
{\mathbb{F}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
we have,

\textsf{mult}(Q\circ G,a)\geq\textsf{mult}(Q,G(a)).

Finally, we need a lemma which given a
\epsilon
fraction of directions
B\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}
we can find a large set of monomials of a fixed degree (not depending on
\epsilon
) such that any linear combination of these monomials doesn’t vanish on the entirety of
B
with high multiplicity.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
B\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}
with
|S|\geq\epsilon|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|,\epsilon\in[0,1]
then for any
d<rq
there exists a set
P_{B}(d,r),|P_{B}(d,r)|=\epsilon\binom{d+n}{n}
of monomials of degree exactly
d
such that no non-zero linear combination of monomials in
P_{B}(d,r)
vanishes with multiplicity at least
r
over all points in
B
.

This is analogous to Corollary 6.12 in
[
DD22
]
. The proof is nearly identical and uses a probabilistic method argument with the Schwartz-Zippel lemma
[
Sch79
,
Zip79
]
. For completeness, we give a proof in the appendix.

Rank of matrices with polynomial entries

We will be needing a number of preliminaries from

]

Definition 2.6

(Rank of matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle
)
.

Given a field
{\mathbb{F}}
and a matrix
M
with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle

We now need some simple properties related to the crank of tensor products. To that end we first define the tensor/Kronecker product of matrices.

Definition 2.10

(Kronecker Product of two matrices)
.

Given a commutative ring

We will need the following simple property of Kronecker products which follows from the corresponding property of the tensor product of linear maps.

Fact 2.11

.

Given matrices

(A_{1}\otimes A_{2})\cdot(B_{1}\otimes B_{2})=(A_{1}\cdot B_{1})\otimes(A_{2}\cdot B_{2}).

We also need a simple fact about tensor product of a set of linearly independent rows with arbitrary vectors.

Fact 2.12

(Linear independence of Kronecker products)
.

Let

. Given linearly independent vectors

then the set of vectors

3
Warm Up: Maximal Kakeya bounds over finite fields

In this section we prove Theorem
1.10
. We restate it here.

Theorem
1.10

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over finite fields)
.

Let
n>0
be an integer,
q
a prime power. For any function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we have the following bound,

\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq\frac{1}{(2-1/q)^{n}}|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right).

By a limiting and scaling argument we note that it suffices to prove the statement above only for natural number valued functions.

We first give a brief sketch for a weaker bound for the setting where
f
is the indicator function of a set to give intuition. The proof will be a direction generalization of the proof of the Kakeya set problem via the polynomial method. First let us describe the proof in
[
Dvi09
]
for the Kakeya set problem. That paper corresponds to the setting where the function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
is the indicator function of a set
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
such that
f^{*}
is always
q
. That is
S
contains a line in every direction.

Dvir’s proof works by assuming the set has size smaller than
\binom{q-1+n}{n}\geq q^{n}/n!
which is the number of degree at most
q-1
monomials in
n
variables. This means we can find a non-zero linear combination of these monomials to construct a polynomial
Q
of degree at most
q-1
which vanishes on the entirety of
S
. As
S
contains a line in each direction
Q
can be restricted on these lines and will vanish on
q
points while having degree at most
q-1
. By elementary algebra we can observe that the highest degree part of
Q
vanishes on every direction. This leads to a contradiction as a homogenous polynomial of degree at most
q-1
can not vanish over every direction by the Schwartz-Zipple lemma
[
Sch79
,
Zip79
]
.

We modify this proof using Lemma
2.5
to find a more suitable polynomial. We now start out with an arbitrary set
S
and let
f
be its indicator function. Clearly,
f^{*}\leq q
. Let

B_{\geq k}=\{u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|f^{*}(u)\geq k\},

in other words it is the set of directions for which there exists a line in that direction with intersection at least
k
with
S
. We also let
\epsilon_{\geq k}=|B_{\geq k}|/|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|
.

We will prove that,

|S|\geq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{q-1}\epsilon_{\geq k+1}\binom{k+n-1}{n-1}.

(2)

Rearranging the RHS will show that the above inequality gives us the desired bounds but with worse constants. For each
0\leq k\leq q-1
we can find a set of monomials
P(k)
of degree
k
such that any of their linear combinations do not vanish over
B_{\geq k+1}
.

If we say (
2
) does not hold then we can find a non-zero polynomial
Q
formed by taking a linear combination of monomials from
\bigcup_{k=0}^{q-1}P(k)
which vanishes on
S
. Say
Q
is of degree
d
. Then consider the set of directions
B_{\geq d+1}
. For each direction in
B_{\geq d+1}
there exists a line in that direction such that
Q
vanishes on
d+1
of its points. As
Q
is of degree
d
again by elementary algebra we can say that the degree
d
part of
Q
then must vanish on the direction vector. But the degree
d
part of
Q
is a linear combination of monomials from
P(d)
and hence can’t vanish on the entirety of
B_{\geq d+1}
leading to a contradiction.

The general statement is proven by letting derivatives also vanish and a more careful analysis to get better constants.

Proof.

Let
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
and we define
B_{\geq k}=\{u||f^{*}(u)|\geq k,u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}\}
and
B_{k}=\{u||f^{*}(u)|=k,u\in{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}\}
. In other words,
B_{\geq k}
is the set of directions where the maximal function is at least
k
(similarly for
B_{k}
). We also let
w=\max_{u}f^{*}(u)
. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
0\leq k\leq w
, let

\epsilon_{\geq k}=|B_{\geq k}|/|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|,

\epsilon_{k}=|B_{k}|/|{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}|

r_{k}=\left\lceil\frac{mk+1}{2q-1}-1\right\rceil,

and

d_{k}=r_{k}q-1.

By definition,
\epsilon_{\geq k}=\sum\limits_{i=k}^{w}\epsilon_{k}
. We also note that if
m\geq 2q
then
0=r_{0}<r_{1}<r_{2}<\ldots<r_{w}
. The same also holds for
-1=d_{0}<d_{1}<\ldots<d_{w}
.

We will prove the following inequality for all
m\geq 2q
,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{w}\epsilon_{\geq i}\sum\limits_{j=d_{i-1}+1}^{d_{i}}\binom{j+n-1}{n-1}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}}\binom{mf(x)+n-1}{n}.

(3)

We claim (

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{w}\epsilon_{\geq i}\sum\limits_{j=d_{i-1}+1}^{d_{i}}\binom{j+n-1}{n-1}

is

We now just have to prove (

, using Lemma

we pick a set of monomials

of degree

with multiplicity at least

.

) doesn’t hold, then we can find a non-zero polynomial

. This is because vanishing with multiplicity at least

many linear constraints and we have

many coefficients.

Claim 3.1
.

For all

.

. By Lemma

Base case: Maximal Kakeya bounds over

The proof here involves replicating the arguments in the proof of Theorem

Theorem
1.6

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

Let

p

When

to,

C_{p^{k},n}=(\lceil\log_{p}(\max_{u}f^{*}(u))\rceil+1)^{-n}(1+n/p)^{-n}.

Proof.

Let

and we define
B_{\geq v}=\{u||f^{*}(u)|\geq v,u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}\}
and
B_{v}=\{u||f^{*}(u)|=v,u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}\}
. In other words,
B_{\geq v}
is the set of directions where the maximal function is at least
v
(similarly for
B_{v}
). We also let
w=\max_{u}f^{*}(u)
. For
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
0\leq v\leq w
, let

\epsilon_{\geq v}=|B_{\geq v}|/|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|,

\epsilon_{v}=|B_{v}|/|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|

r_{v}=mv.

By definition,
\epsilon_{\geq v}=\sum\limits_{i=v}^{w}\epsilon_{v}
.

We prove the following for all

.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{w}\epsilon_{\geq i}\sum\limits_{j=r_{i-1}+1}^{r_{i}}\left(\left\lceil\binom{j\lceil\log_{p}(j)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\binom{(j-1)\lceil\log_{p}(j-1)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}\binom{mf(x)+n-1}{n}.

(5)

We claim (
4
) proves the Theorem. Let us prove that first. The LHS of (
4
) can be re-arranged to give,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{w}\epsilon_{\geq i}\sum\limits_{j=r_{i-1}+1}^{r_{i}}\left(\left\lceil\binom{j\lceil\log_{p}(j)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\binom{(j-1)\lceil\log_{p}(j-1)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{w}\sum_{v=i}^{w}\sum\limits_{j=r_{i-1}+1}^{r_{i}}\epsilon_{v}\left(\left\lceil\binom{j\lceil\log_{p}(j)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\binom{(j-1)\lceil\log_{p}(j-1)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{w}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{r_{v}}\epsilon_{v}\left(\left\lceil\binom{j\lceil\log_{p}(j)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lceil\binom{(j-1)\lceil\log_{p}(j-1)\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{w}\epsilon_{v}\left\lceil\binom{r_{v}\lceil\log_{p}(r_{v})\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil.

(6)

If we set
m=n
then using (
4
) and (
4
) we have,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{v=1}^{w}\epsilon_{v}\frac{v^{n}}{\lceil\log_{p}(v)+\log_{p}(n)\rceil^{n}}\leq n^{-n}\sum_{v=1}^{w}\epsilon_{v}\left\lceil\binom{r_{v}\lceil\log_{p}(r_{v})\rceil^{-1}+n}{n}\right\rceil\leq\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}\frac{1}{n!}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(f(x)+i/n)^{n}.

As

If we set

Now it remains to prove (
4
) for all

.

(7)

Recall,

Consider the function

Induction Step: Maximal Kakeya bounds over

We first give an outline of the proof in a special case with worse constants to highlight the overall strategy. The goal is to extend the proof from

using the probabilistic method and Corollary

Let

.

’th root of unity in

. In other words,

the dimension of the space spanned by the rows in

for

,

. Let us call this set of rows

.

2.24
will give us a number of linearly independent vectors

Theorem
1.9

(Maximal Kakeya bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

Let

. For any function

\sum\limits_{x\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}}|f(x)|^{n}\geq\frac{C_{N,n}}{|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|}\left(\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}}|f^{*}(u)|^{n}\right),

where

\displaystyle C_{N,n}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2(\log(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+1)\lceil\log_{p_{1}}(\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{1}))+\log_{p_{1}}(n)\rceil}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2(k_{r}+\lceil\log_{p_{r}}(n)\rceil)}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2(k_{i}\log(p_{i})+1)(k_{i}+\lceil\log_{p_{i}}(n)\rceil)}\right)^{n}

.

Proof.

The
r=1
case is Theorem
1.6
. Let the theorem be true for some
r
. We will now prove it for
N=p_{0}^{k_{0}}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}=p_{0}^{k_{0}}N_{1}
. Let
f:({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
and
w=\textsf{mweight}(f,p_{0})
. We will use Fact
1.7
to decompose objects into a
{\mathbb{Z}}/p_{0}^{k_{0}}{\mathbb{Z}}
component and
{\mathbb{Z}}/N_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}
component.

Let
L(u)
be the line in direction

Proof of Lemma

The proof here only involves a minor adaptation of the ideas in
[

]
.

First, we state the multiplicity version of the Schwartz-Zippel bound
[
Sch79
,
Zip79
]

(see
[
DKSS13
]
for a proof). We denote by
{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},..,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
the space of polynomials of total degree at most
d
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Lemma A.1

(Schwartz-Zippel with multiplicities)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field,
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and let
f\in{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},..,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
be a non-zero polynomial. Then, for any finite subset
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}
,

\sum\limits_{a\in U^{n}}\textsf{mult}(f,a)\leq d|U|^{n-1}.

A.1
The EVAL matrix, its submatrices and their ranks

Our main object of interest is the matrix encoding the evaluation of a subset of monomials (with their derivatives) on a subset of points.

Definition A.2

(
\textsf{EVAL}^{m}(S,W)
matrix)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field, and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Given a set
S\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and a set of monomials
W\subset{\mathbb{F}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we let
\textsf{EVAL}^{m}(S,W)
denote an
|S|\binom{m-1+n}{n}\times|W|
matrix whose columns are indexed by
W
and rows are indexed by tuples
(x,\mathbf{j})\in S\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
s.t.
\text{wt}(\mathbf{j})<m
.
The
((x,\mathbf{j}),f)
th entry of this matrix is,

f^{(\mathbf{j})}(x).

In other words, the
(x,\mathbf{j})
th row of the matrix consists of the evaluation of the
\mathbf{j}
’th Hasse derivative of all
f\in W
at
x
. Equivalently, the
f
’th column of the matrix consists of the evaluations of weight strictly less than
m
Hasse derivatives of
f
at all points in
S
.

Let
W_{d,n}
denote the set of monomials in
n
-variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
of degree at exactly
d
. Our first lemma shows that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rank of
\textsf{EVAL}^{m}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1},W_{d,n})
is maximal whenever
d
is not too large. This is essentially the Schwartz-Zippel lemma since it means that a polynomial of bounded degree could be recovered from its evaluations (up to high enough order) on a product set and evaluations of homogenous polynomials over
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}
gives us evaluations over the entirety of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
.

Lemma A.3

(Rank of
\textsf{EVAL}^{m}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1},W_{d,n})
)
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
then for all
d<mq
we have,

\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\textsf{EVAL}^{m}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1},W_{d,n})=|W_{d,n}|=\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}.

Proof.

Let
v_{m}
be the column of
\textsf{EVAL}^{m}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1},W_{d,n})
corresponding to the monomial
m\in W_{d,n}
. The linear combination
\alpha_{m}v_{m}
is precisely the evaluation of Hasse derivative of weight strictly less than
m
of the homogenous polynomial
\alpha_{m}m
over
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}
. If such a linear combination is zero then we get a homogenous linear polynomial of degree
d<mq
which vanishes on the entirety of
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n-1}
with multiplicity at least
m
. As the polynomial is homogenous the same holds over the entirety of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. This immediately contradicts Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Our final lemma, which is the heart of this section, shows that if we have a set
S

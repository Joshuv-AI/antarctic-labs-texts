Superintegrability on the 3-dimensional spaces with curvature. Oscillator-related and Kepler-related systems on the Sphere S 3 S^{3} and on the Hyperbolic space H 3 H^{3}
José F. Cariñena, Manuel F. Rañada, Mariano Santander, [2pt] Departamento de Física Teórica and IUMA, Facultad de CienciasUniversidad de Zaragoza, 50009 Zaragoza, Spain, [2pt] Departamento de Física Teórica and IMUVa, Facultad de CienciasUniversidad de Valladolid, 47011 Valladolid, Spain

Abstract
The superintegrability of several Hamiltonian systems defined on three-dimensional configuration spaces of constant curvature is studied. We first analyze the properties of the Killing vector fields, Noether symmetries and Noether momenta. Then we study the superintegrability of the Harmonic Oscillator, the Smorodinsky-Winternitz (S-W) system and the Harmonic Oscillator with ratio of frequencies 1:1:2 and additional nonlinear terms on the 3-dimensional sphere S 3 S^{3} ( OPEN κ > 0 ) \kappa>0) and on the hyperbolic space H 3 H^{3} ( κ < 0 \kappa<0 ). In the second part we present a study first of the Kepler problem and then of the Kepler problem with additional nonlinear terms in these two curved spaces, S 3 S^{3} ( OPEN κ > 0 ) \kappa>0) and H 3 H^{3} ( κ < 0 \kappa<0 ). We prove their superintegrability and we obtain, in all the cases, the maximal number of functionally independent integrals of motion. All the mathematical expressions are presented using the curvature κ \kappa as a parameter, in such a way that particularizing for κ > 0 \kappa>0 , κ = 0 \kappa=0 , or κ < 0 \kappa<0 , the corresponding properties are obtained for the system on the sphere S 3 S^{3} , the Euclidean space l ​ E 3 {\rm l\kern-1.19995ptE}^{3} , or the hyperbolic space H 3 H^{3} , respectively.

Introduction

A Hamiltonian system is superintegrable if it is integrable in the Liouville sense and it admits more globally defined constants of motion than degrees of freedom. If the system has three degrees of freedom and it admits five functionally independent integrals of motion then the system is maximally superintegrable. At the classical level superintegrability means that all bounded trajectories are closed while at the quantum level this property is related to the degeneracy of the energy levels.
The two best known examples of these systems are the harmonic oscillator and the Kepler-Coulomb problem. In fact, probably the first and oldest study on this matter was the theorem of Bertrand

2.1

We recall that a vector field

A three-dimensional Riemann manifold with constant curvature admits six linearly independent Killing vector fields, that generate six different one-parameter groups of continuous isometries of the manifold, and that can be grouped in two sets of three vector fields.

, that is a parabolic potential without singularities, appears in this formalism as making a separation between two different situations (see Fig. 1).
In the spherical case the potential has an infinite potential barrier on the geodesic circle

The hyperbolic potential

\kappa<0

\lim{}_{r\to\infty}V_{\kappa}=1/|\kappa|

A quantization of this system was studied in
[

\kappa

We also mention that this oscillator was also studied in

First, in both cases, spherical (
\kappa>0
) and hyperbolic (
\kappa<0
) spaces, the Hamiltonian system has spherical symmetry and therefore the three components
(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
of the angular momentum are time preserved in time evolution.

Now let us consider the following three complex functions

M_{1\kappa}=P_{1}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,\bigr(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)\bigr)(\sin\theta\cos\phi)\,,{\quad}M_{2\kappa}=P_{2}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,\bigr(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)\bigr)(\sin\theta\sin\phi)\,,

M_{3\kappa}=P_{3}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,\bigr(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)\bigr)(\cos\theta)\,,

where

,

Then we have

\bigl\{M_{1\kappa}\,,H(\kappa)\bigr\}={\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\,\alpha\,M_{1\kappa}\,,{\quad}\bigl\{M_{2\kappa}\,,H(\kappa)\bigr\}={\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\,\alpha\,M_{2\kappa}\,,{\quad}\bigl\{M_{3\kappa}\,,H(\kappa)\bigr\}={\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\,\alpha\,M_{3\kappa}\,,

where
\lambda_{\kappa}
denotes
\lambda_{\kappa}=1/\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)

Therefore the moduli
|\,M_{j\kappa}\,|
of the three functions
M_{j\kappa}
,

, satisfy

{d\over dt}\,|\,M_{1\kappa}\,|^{2}=\Bigl({d\over dt}\,M_{1\kappa}\Bigr)M_{1\kappa}^{*}+\,M_{1\kappa}\Bigl({d\over dt}M_{1\kappa}^{*}\Bigr)=({\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha M_{1\kappa})M_{1\kappa}^{*}+M_{1\kappa}(-{\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha\,M_{1\kappa}^{*})=0\,,

{d\over dt}\,|\,M_{2\kappa}\,|^{2}=\Bigl({d\over dt}\,M_{2\kappa}\Bigr)M_{2\kappa}^{*}+\,M_{2\kappa}\Bigl({d\over dt}M_{2\kappa}^{*}\Bigr)=({\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha M_{2\kappa})M_{2\kappa}^{*}+M_{2\kappa}(-{\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha\,M_{2\kappa}^{*})=0\,,

{d\over dt}\,|\,M_{3\kappa}\,|^{2}=\Bigl({d\over dt}\,M_{3\kappa}\Bigr)M_{3}^{*}+\,M_{3\kappa}\Bigl({d\over dt}M_{3\kappa}^{*}\Bigr)=({\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha M_{3\kappa})M_{3\kappa}^{*}+M_{3\kappa}(-{\rm i\,}\lambda_{\kappa}\alpha\,M_{3\kappa}^{*})=0\,,

Therefore the following three
\kappa
-dependent functions

\displaystyle K_{11\kappa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\,\bigr(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\bigr)(\sin\theta\,\cos\phi)^{2}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle K_{22\kappa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{2}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\,\bigr(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\bigr)(\sin\theta\,\sin\phi)^{2}\,,

M_{1\kappa}M_{2\kappa}^{*}=K_{12\kappa}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,J_{3}\,,{\quad}M_{2\kappa}M_{3\kappa}^{*}=K_{23\kappa}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,J_{1}\,,{\quad}M_{3\kappa}M_{1\kappa}^{*}=K_{31\kappa}+{\rm i\,}\alpha\,J_{2}\,,

\displaystyle K_{12\kappa}

\bigl\{c_{1}K_{11\kappa}+c_{2}J_{1}\,,\,K_{22\kappa}+K_{33\kappa}+\kappa\,(J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\bigr\}=0\,,

\bigl\{c_{1}K_{22\kappa}+c_{2}J_{2}\,,\,K_{11\kappa}+K_{33\kappa}+\kappa\,(J_{1}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\bigr\}=0\,,

Another prototypical example of integrable system is the Kepler problem and therefore we fix in this section our attention on such a problem in the three-dimensional spaces considered in
the preceding sections.

4.1
Kepler Hamiltonian

The following curvature-dependent function is the spherical, Euclidean, or hyperbolic, Kepler Hamiltonian with curvature
\kappa

H_{K}(\kappa)={1\over 2}\,\Bigl(p_{r}^{2}+{1\over\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)}\Bigl(p_{\theta}^{2}+{p_{\phi}^{2}\over\sin^{2}\theta}\Bigr)\Bigr)+{k\over\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)}\,,

(38)

i.e., the potentials of the Kepler problem on the unit sphere (
\kappa=1
), on the Euclidean space, or on the unit Lobachevsky space (
\kappa=-1
), arise as the following three particular cases

V_{1}={k\over\tan(r)}\,,{\qquad}V_{0}={k\over r}\,,{\qquad}V_{-1}={k\over\tanh(r)}.

The situation is rather similar to the one obtained in Section
3.1
for the harmonic oscillator.
Also in this case the Euclidean function
V_{0}=k/r
appears in this formalism as making a separation between two different behaviours (see Fig. 2).

This potential is central (for all the values of
\kappa
) so the three components
(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
of the angular momentum are integrals of motion, namely

\bigl\{J_{1}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,,{\quad}\bigl\{J_{2}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,,{\quad}\bigl\{J_{3}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,.

and therefore this curvature-dependent system, as any other central potential, is Liouville integrable. Moreover, as in the Euclidean case, there exists an additional set of integrals of motion, because
the following three functions

\displaystyle K_{RL1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{2}J_{3}-P_{3}J_{2})+k\,(\sin\theta\cos\phi)\,,

(39)

\displaystyle K_{RL2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{3}J_{1}-P_{1}J_{3})+k\,(\sin\theta\sin\phi)\,,

(40)

\displaystyle K_{RL3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{1}J_{2}-P_{2}J_{1})+k\,(\cos\theta)\,,

(41)

that are functionally independent

dK_{RL1}\,\wedge\,dK_{RL2}\,\wedge\,dK_{RL3}\neq 0\,,

are integrals of motion

\bigl\{K_{RL1}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,,{\quad}\bigl\{K_{RL2}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,,{\quad}\bigl\{K_{RL3}\,,H_{K}(\kappa)\bigr\}=0\,.

They must be considered as the curved version of the standard Runge-Lenz vector.
Their Poisson brackets are given by

\bigl\{K_{RL1}\,,K_{RL2}\bigr\}=-2J_{3}\bigl(H_{K}(\kappa)-\kappa\,(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\bigr)\,,

\bigl\{K_{RL2}\,,K_{RL3}\bigr\}=-2J_{1}\bigl(H_{K}(\kappa)-\kappa\,(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\bigr)\,,

\bigl\{K_{RL3}\,,K_{RL1}\bigr\}=-2J_{2}\bigl(H_{K}(\kappa)-\kappa\,(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\bigr)\,,

and the Poisson brackets of each one with the angular momenta are

\bigl\{J_{1}\,,c_{1}K_{RL1}+c_{2}K_{RL2}+c_{3}K_{RL3}\bigr\}=c_{2}K_{RL3}-c_{3}K_{RL2}\,,

\bigl\{J_{2}\,,c_{1}K_{RL1}+c_{2}K_{RL2}+c_{3}K_{RL3}\bigr\}=c_{3}K_{RL1}-c_{1}K_{RL3}\,,

\bigl\{J_{3}\,,c_{1}K_{RL1}+c_{2}K_{RL2}+c_{3}K_{RL3}\bigr\}=c_{1}K_{RL2}-c_{2}K_{RL1}\,,

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
, and
c_{3}
are arbitrary constants.

The preceding results are summarised in the following proposition:

Proposition 4

The
\kappa
-dependent Kepler Hamiltonian defined in the 3-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
(
\kappa>0)
and Hyperbolic space
H^{3}
(
\kappa<0
)

H_{K}(\kappa)={1\over 2}\,\Bigl(p_{r}^{2}+{1\over\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)}\Bigl(p_{\theta}^{2}+{p_{\phi}^{2}\over\sin^{2}\theta}\Bigr)\Bigr)+{k\over\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)}

It is spherically symmetric, with the three components (

5
Final comments

We have studied the superintegrability of Hamiltonian systems defined on three-dimensional configuration spaces of constant curvature.
As observed in the Introduction the two more important superintegrable systems are just the harmonic oscillator and the Kepler problem and because of this we have focused our study on these two systems (usually known as Bertrand potentials) as well as to some other related systems obtained from them by addition of nonlinear terms.

There are certain important points that are fundamental for the approach presented in this article.
We mention two. First, we have presented a curvature-dependent formalism (all the functions depend of
\kappa
as a parameter) but, given a superintegrable Euclidean system, then many different
\kappa
-dependent potentials can be constructed with the same flat limit; the important point is that if we require that the superintegrability must be preserved then this condition singles out a very particular system among all the possible curved version of the Euclidean system.
Second, the curvature-dependent formalism we have presented permit us the study of the Hamiltonian system at the same time in both curved manifolds; that is, spherical (curvature
\kappa
positive) and hyperbolic (curvature
\kappa
negative).
This is also a very important point since these two spaces are geometrically rather different but, in spite of this, first the Hamiltonian function
H(\kappa)
and then all the integrals of motion
K_{j\kappa}
can be expressed in an unique form valid for the two spaces.

We have proved the quadratic superintegrability (and we have obtained all the integrals of motion) of three oscillators; the isotropic harmonic, the Smorodinsky-Winternitz (S-W) system, and the 2:1:1 oscillator with nonlinear terms

Appendix I. Properties of the matrix
[K_{ij\kappa}]

The symmetric matrix
\left[K_{ij\kappa}\right]
of the
\kappa
-depending integrals of motion,

\bigl\{K_{ij\kappa}\,,H(\kappa)\bigr\}=0
, obtained in the section (
3.1
) represents a generalization of the Fradkin tensor
[
3
]
for the dynamics of the curvature-dependent Hamiltonian
H(\kappa)
. Now we present its more important algebraic properties

(i)

The trace of the matrix
[K_{ij\kappa}]
, that in the Euclidean case is just the Hamiltonian, is now the Hamiltonian plus a curvature-dependent term (related with the angular momentum) that vanish in the Euclidean limit

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits[K_{ij\kappa}]=K_{11\kappa}+K_{22\kappa}+K_{33\kappa}\,,{\quad}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits[K_{ij\kappa}]+\kappa\,(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})=2H(\kappa)\,.

(ii)

The matrix
[K_{ij\kappa}]
is singular, that is,

\det[K_{ij\kappa}]=0
.
In fact, the six matrix elements
K_{ij\kappa}=K_{ji\kappa}
,
i,j=1,2,3
, are six different integrals of motion for the Hamiltonian
H(\kappa)
and, as only five of them can be independent, the equation
\det[K_{ij}]=0
states an algebraic relation between them.

(iii)

The action of
\left[K_{ij}\right]
on the angular momentum is given by

\left[\matrix{K_{11\kappa}&K_{12\kappa}&K_{13\kappa}\cr K_{21\kappa}&K_{22\kappa}&K_{23\kappa}\cr K_{31\kappa}&K_{32\kappa}&K_{33\kappa}\cr}\right]\left[\matrix{J_{1}\cr J_{2}\cr J_{3}}\right]=0,

that can be rewritten as the following three equations

K_{11\kappa}J_{1}+K_{12\kappa}J_{2}+K_{13\kappa}J_{3}=0\,,

K_{21\kappa}J_{1}+K_{22\kappa}J_{2}+K_{23\kappa}J_{3}=0\,,

K_{31\kappa}J_{1}+K_{32\kappa}J_{2}+K_{33\kappa}J_{3}=0\,.

The contraction of
[K_{ij\kappa}]
with the angular momentum gives zero.

(iv)

The following relations between the components of the matrix are true:

x_{\kappa}^{2}K_{22\kappa}-2x_{\kappa}y_{\kappa}K_{12\kappa}+y_{\kappa}^{2}K_{11\kappa}=(\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r))^{2}J_{3}^{2}\,,{\qquad}y_{\kappa}^{2}K_{33\kappa}-2y_{\kappa}z_{\kappa}K_{23\kappa}+z_{\kappa}^{2}K_{22\kappa}=(\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r))^{2}J_{1}^{2}\,,

z_{\kappa}^{2}K_{11\kappa}-2z_{\kappa}x_{\kappa}K_{31\kappa}+x_{\kappa}^{2}K_{33\kappa}=(\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r))^{2}J_{2}^{2},

where we recall that
\lim{}_{\kappa\to 0}\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)=1
.

(v)

The following relations between the components of the matrix are true:

K_{11\kappa}K_{22\kappa}-K_{12\kappa}^{2}=\alpha^{2}J_{3}^{2}\,,{\quad}K_{22\kappa}K_{33\kappa}-K_{23\kappa}^{2}=\alpha^{2}J_{1}^{2}\,,{\quad}K_{33\kappa}K_{11\kappa}-K_{31\kappa}^{2}=\alpha^{2}J_{2}^{2}\,.

(vi)

The following three algebraic properties are true

\displaystyle K_{ij\kappa}x_{i\kappa}x_{j\kappa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bigl(x_{\kappa}^{2}+y_{\kappa}^{2}+z_{\kappa}^{2}\bigr)H(\kappa)-(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle K_{ij\kappa}x_{i\kappa}P_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{r}\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r))\Bigl(2H(\kappa)-\kappa(J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+J_{3}^{2})\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle K_{ij\kappa}P_{i}P_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P_{1}^{2}+P_{2}^{2}+P_{3}^{2})^{2}+\alpha^{2}(\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r))^{2}\,p_{r}^{2}\,,

where we have made use of the following equality

x_{\kappa}P_{1}+y_{\kappa}P_{2}+z_{\kappa}P_{3}=p_{r}\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)\,.

7
Appendix II. Two other alternative approaches

We have studied the dynamics on the curvature constant spaces
S^{3}
and
H^{3}
by making use of the curvature-dependent trigonometric and hyperbolic functions
\mathop{\rm C}\nolimits_{\kappa}(x)
and
\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}(x)
.
In this way the expression of the differential element of distance
ds^{2}(\kappa)
and the kinetic function
T_{g}(\kappa)

take the following form when written in geodesic polar coordinates
(r,\theta,\phi)

ds^{2}(\kappa)=dr^{2}+\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\,d\theta^{2}+\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\,\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\,,

and

L=T_{g}(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\left(v_{r}^{2}+\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\,v_{\theta}^{2}+\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}^{2}(r)\sin^{2}\theta\,v_{\phi}^{2}\right)\,.

Next we present two other different approaches that can be obtained from this one by making use of a change of the geodesic distance
r
but preserving the angular coordinates.

1.

Let us consider the
\kappa
-dependent change
(r,\theta,\phi)\to(\rho,\theta,\phi)
given by

\rho=\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)
.
Then, when written in these new coordinates, the
\kappa
-dependent metric and kinetic term become

ds_{\kappa}^{2}={dr^{2}\over 1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\,,

and

L=T_{g}(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{\rho}^{2}\over 1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}+\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)\,.

In this case, the Lagrangians of the harmonic oscillator and Kepler problem take the form

L(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{\rho}^{2}\over 1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}+\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)-{\alpha^{2}\over 2}\left({\rho^{2}\over 1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}\right)\,,

and

L(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{\rho}^{2}\over 1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}+\rho^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}+\rho^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)-k\left({\sqrt{1-\kappa\,\rho^{2}}\over\rho}\right)\,.

This formalism was used for example in
[
54
,
55
,
56
,
57
]

2.

Let us now consider a new
\kappa
-dependent change
(r,\theta,\phi)\to(R,\theta,\phi)
given by

R=\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)
.
Then, when written in these new coordinates, the
\kappa
-dependent metric and kinetic term become

ds_{\kappa}^{2}={d\rho^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})^{2}}+{R^{2}\,d\theta^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}+{\rho^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}\,,

and

L=T_{g}(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{R}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})^{2}}+{R^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}+{R^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)

In this case, the Lagrangians of the harmonic oscillator and Kepler problem take the form

L(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{R}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})^{2}}+{R^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}+{R^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)-{\alpha^{2}\over 2}R^{2}\,,

and

L(\kappa)=({1\over 2})\,\Bigl({v_{R}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})^{2}}+{R^{2}\,v_{\theta}^{2}\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}+{R^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\over(1+\kappa\,R^{2})}\,v_{\phi}^{2}\Bigr)-{k\over R}\,.

This approach is the one studied by Higgs in Ref.

[
72
]
(the study of Higgs was originally limited to a
spherical geometry but the idea can be extended to the
hyperbolic space).

We note that both radial variables,
\rho
and
R
, are well defined.
In the hyperbolic
\kappa<0
case the two functions
\mathop{\rm S}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)
and
\mathop{\rm T}\nolimits_{\kappa}(r)
are
positive for
r>0
and concerning the spherical
\kappa>0
case this
property is also true because then
r
is restricted to a bounded
interval.

The situation can be summarised as follows.
We have obtained three alternative ways of describing the Lagrangian/Hamiltonian systems on spaces of constant curvature: the original trigonometric/hyperbolic formalism
and the two other approaches obtained from it.
Of course, each one of these three different approaches has its own
characteristics and advantages.

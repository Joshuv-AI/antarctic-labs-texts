Non-invertible quasihomogeneous singularities and their Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds
Anton Rarovskii

Abstract
. According to the classification of quasihomogeneous singularities, any polynomial f f defining such singularity has a decomposition f = f κ + f a ​ d ​ d f=f_{\kappa}+f_{add} . The polynomial f κ f_{\kappa} is of the certain form while f a ​ d ​ d f_{add} is only restricted by the condition that the singularity of f f should be isolated. The polynomial f a ​ d ​ d f_{add} is zero if and only if f f is invertible, and in the non-invertible case f a ​ d ​ d f_{add} is arbitrarily complicated. In this paper we investigate all possible polynomials f a ​ d ​ d f_{add} for a given non-invertible f f . For a given f κ f_{\kappa} we introduce a specific small collection of monomials that build up f a ​ d ​ d f_{add} such that the polynomial f = f κ + f a ​ d ​ d f=f_{\kappa}+f_{add} defines an isolated quasihomogeneous singularity. If ( f , ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ ) (f,{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}) is Landau-Ginzburg orbifold with such non-invertible polynomial f f , we provide the quasihomogeneous polynomial f ¯ \bar{f} such that the orbifold equivalence ( f , ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ ) ∼ ( f ¯ , { id } ) (f,{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\sim(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\}) holds. We also give an explicit isomorphism between the corresponding Frobenius algebras.

Non-invertible polynomials

In this paper we will consider another class of

and corresponded Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds

Suppose now that we start from the quasihomogeneous

Orbifold equivalence

The second part of this paper is devoted to orbifold equivalence
(f,G)\sim(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})
between Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds. It can be useful for the investigation of Landau-Ginzburg orbifolds with non-trivial group
G
. Orbifold equivalence could be roughly understood as the equivalence
\mathrm{MF}(\bar{f})\cong\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f)
of categories of
G
-equivariant matrix factorizations. In this case there is an isomorphism between Hochschild cohomology of the category
\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f)
and an algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(\bar{f})
.

In our work we use the theorem introduced in
[
BP
]
,
[
Io
]
(Theorem
25
in text), which provides the method to construct orbifold equivalence
(f,G)\sim(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})
by crepant resolution of
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G
. In particular, we obtain the orbifold equivalence between pairs with polynomials that are non-invertible. We use the explicit form of polynomials with a “loop with branches” graph to construct a new polynomial corresponding to the same graph but with one additional edge (Theorem
26
in text). We also use Shklyarov’s
[
Sh
]
techniques to calculate structure constants of
{\mathsf{HH}}^{*}(\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f))
. It helps us to find an explicit isomorphism between their Frobenius algebras (Proposition
34
in text). These results should help to research Frobenius structures and mirror symmetry for
(f,G)
via the classical singularity structures for
\bar{f}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Consider
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}]
a ring of polynomials with complex coefficients. We call a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]

non-degenerate
if
f
defines an isolated singularity at the origin, i.e. the system of equations
\{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}=\dots=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{N}}\ =0\}
has the unique solution at
0
. Moreover, throughout this paper we assume that
f
does not contain the summands of the type
x_{i}x_{j}
.

Definition 1
.

The polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
is called
quasihomogeneous
with a set of weights

(v_{1},...,v_{N},d)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{N+1}
if the equality

\displaystyle f(\lambda^{v_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{v_{2}}x_{2},...,\lambda^{v_{N}}x_{N})=\lambda^{d}f(x_{1},x_{2},...x_{N})

is true for any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Remark 2
.

In Sections 6 and 7 and we use the reduced system of weights
(q_{1},q_{2},...q_{N})
, which can be obtained as
(q_{1},q_{2},...q_{N}):=(v_{1}/d,\dots,v_{N}/d)
. Moreover,
q_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
HK
]
, Theorem 3.7).

Now let
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
be a non-degenerate quasihomogeneous polynomial.

Definition 3
.

Jacobian algebra
of polynomial
f
is the quotient ring of the ring of polynomials
{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
on the ideal generated by partial derivatives of
f
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Jac}(f):=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}]/_{(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}},\dots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{N}})}

The dimension of a Jacobian algebra is called
Milnor number
and is denoted by
\mu_{f}
, and we have
\mu_{f}<\infty
if and only if
f
is non-degenerate (see
[
AGV
]
).

Definition 4
.

A non-degenerate quasihomogeneous polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
is called
invertible
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

The number of variables is the same as the number of monomials in
f
:

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{E_{ij}}

For some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and integer non-negative

E_{ij}
for
i,j=1,\dots,N
.

•

The matrix
(E_{ij})
is invertible over
\mathbb{Q}
.

If non-degenerate quasihomogeneous
f
does not satisfy the conditions above, the corresponding polynomial is called non-invertible, and in this work we are interested in this case.

Definition 5
.

The group of
maximal diagonal symmetries
of
f
is the group:

\displaystyle G_{f}=\{(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{N})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}\>|\>f(g_{1}x_{1},g_{2}x_{2},\dots,g_{N}x_{N})=

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\}

Any subgroup
G\subseteq G_{f}
is called a group of
diagonal symmetries
(or just a group of
symmetries
). With each element
g\in G
we can associate an algebra
\mathrm{Jac}(f^{g})
of the polynomial
f^{g}=f|_{Fix(g)}
where
Fix(g)=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\>|\>g\cdot x=x\}
. In addition, if
Fix(g)=\{0\}
, we put
f^{g}:=1
.

Proposition 6

(
[
ET1
]
, Prop. 5)
.

If the polynomial
f
is non-degenerate, then for every
g\in G
with non-trivial fixed locus, the polynomial
f^{g}
is also non-degenerate.

4.
Non-invertible quasihomogeneous singularities

Now we start with a map
\kappa
and the systems of weights
(v_{1},...,v_{N},d)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{N+1})_{d}
, and construct by them quasihomogeneous polynomial
f_{\kappa}
(may be degenerate) and consider
R=supp(f_{\kappa})
. In this section our aim is to obtain the explicit non-degenerate polynomial
f
such that
f=f_{\kappa}+f_{add}
starting from the fixed
f_{\kappa}

Definition 14
.

A (nonempty) subset of indexes
J\subset I=\{1,\dots,N\}
is called
failing set
for a given
R
, if it does not satisfy the condition
(C1)
(or any other equivalent condition), for instance:

(NC1):

R\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J}=\varnothing

and
\forall\ K\subset I\backslash J\text{ such that }|K|=|J|

\text{ it is true that }\exists\ k\in K\ R_{k}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J}=\varnothing

It means also that
R
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
7
if there is no failing set for this
R
. Note that if there are failing sets for
R
then
f_{\kappa}
is degenerate by Theorem
8
. We want to describe the connection between failing sets of such
R
and the map
\kappa
.

Remark 15
.

Note that for a given graph
\Gamma_{\kappa}
the set of powers
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
defines the system of weights
(v_{1},...,v_{N},d)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{N})_{d}
. We will use it in what follows, since sometimes it is easier to work with powers.

Proposition 16
.

Let
J
be a failing set for
R=supp(f_{\kappa})
. Then
\forall j\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
,
\{j,\kappa(j)\}\nsubseteq J
. Moreover, the statement is also true for fixed points of
\kappa
:
\kappa(j)=j
.

Proof.

In this case
a_{j}e_{j}+e_{\kappa(j)}\in R\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J}\neq\varnothing
and by
(C1a)

J
is not failing.
∎

Consider the example and find failing sets for some degenerate quasihomogeneous polynomial
f_{\kappa}
.

Definition 18
.

Let

Theorem 19
.

Let

be an admissible collection of

and assume there is a set of multipowers

Lemma 21

Let

be admissible for

. Then for the set

(4.1)

\displaystyle R^{\prime}:=R\cup{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J_{1}}\cup{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J_{2}}\cup\dots\cup{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{J_{l}}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{N})_{d}

for

can not be satisfied for

(otherwise

for

\exists

Proof.

. Due to the condition

6.
Crepant resolutions and orbifold equivalence

In this Section we are going to consider Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
(f,G)
, where quasihomogeneous polynomial
f
corresponds to loop with branches and 1 isolated vertex and the group
G
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We describe a new polynomial
\bar{f}
which is obtained from
f
using the gluing a point operation and introduce the orbifold equivalence
(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})\sim(f,G)
.

6.1.
Background

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a holomorphic
G
-invariant function. Thus it could be defined on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G
. Let
\tau\colon\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G
be a crepant resolution of the
singularity. The map
f
then lifts to a function on
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G}
by taking composition with
\tau
and we obtain a function
\hat{f}\colon\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G}
be covered by some charts
U_{1},\dots,U_{s}
all isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
. We denote
\hat{f}_{i}
as a restriction of
\hat{f}
on the each chart

\hat{f}_{i}=\hat{f}|_{U_{i}}\colon U_{i}\to{\mathbb{C}}

If we consider a non-degenerate quasihomogeneous polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[\textbf{x}]
and a group of symmetries
G
, we can associate with the pair
(f,G)
the category
\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f)
(we refer to
[
Orl1
]
,
[
Orl2
]
,
[
Io
]
for details). In the category language we formulate the definition of orbifold equivalence (see
[
Io
]
) and have the following theorem:

Theorem 25
.

(
[
BP
]
,
[
Io
]
)
Let
f
and
\hat{f}
both have isolated singularities at the unique points
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G
and
w\in\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}/G}
respectively. Then the pairs
(f,G)
and
(\hat{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})
are orbifold equivalent. Namely there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{MF}(\hat{f})\cong\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f)

Moreover, suppose that
\hat{f}
has a singularity at the origin and there is an affine chart
U_{i}
such that
\hat{f}_{i}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\cong U_{i}\to{\mathbb{C}}
has a singularity at
0
and
\hat{f}_{j}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\cong U_{j}\to{\mathbb{C}}
does not have any singularities
\forall j\neq i
. Then:

\mathrm{MF}(\hat{f})\cong\mathrm{MF}(\hat{f}_{i})

It means that for orbifold equivalence between
(f,G)
and
(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})
it is sufficient to find the suitable affine chart such that
\bar{f}=\hat{f}_{m}
and
\hat{f}_{m}
is the unique non-degenerate polynomial in the collection
\{\hat{f}_{i}\}
.

6.2.
Orbifold equivalence for the loop with branches

Now we start with a polynomial
f_{\kappa_{0}}
with one connected component, and assume that there is an index
t\in T_{\kappa_{0}}
(i.e. the set of leaves) such that the corresponding power is even, i.e. there is a monomial
x_{t}^{2a_{t}}x_{\kappa(t)}
. Without loss of generality we put
t=1
and
\kappa(1)=2
.

By Theorem
22
we obtain
f_{0}=f_{\kappa_{0}}+f_{add}
, where
f_{add}
is given by the admissible collection for
R=supp(f_{\kappa_{0}})
. Then we consider the polynomial
f_{\kappa}=f_{\kappa_{0}}+x_{N+1}^{2}
with the set of powers
(2a_{1},\dots,a_{N},2)
which is quasihomogeneous with the reduced set of weights
(q_{1},\dots,q_{N},1/2)
. Note that the corresponding graph
\Gamma_{\kappa}
is a disjoint union of
\Gamma_{\kappa_{0}}
and 1 isolated vertex. Since
x_{N+1}^{2}
is invertible, the admissible collection
A_{R}
for
R=supp(f_{\kappa})
coincides with the admissible collection for
R=supp(f_{\kappa_{0}})
. It follows that
f=f_{\kappa}+f_{add}
is non-degenerate by Theorem

(with the same
f_{add}
as for
f_{0}
).

Our object of research is Landau-Ginzburg orbifold (
f,G)
with the symmetry group
G\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\langle g\rangle
acting as follows:

g\cdot x_{m}=-x_{m}\;\text{if}\;m=1;N+1

g\cdot x_{m}=x_{m}\;\text{else}

It means that
g
acts non-trivially only on isolated vertex and on the leaf with even power.

For the group
G
described above we have
{\mathbb{C}}^{N+1}/G\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{N-1}\times\{w^{2}=uv\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{N+1}
by identifying
\{u=x_{1}^{2},v=x_{N+1}^{2},w=x_{1}x_{N+1}\}
or
\{v=x_{1}^{2},u=x_{N+1}^{2},w=x_{1}x_{N+1}\}
which gives us

and

covering
{\mathbb{C}}^{N+1}/G
, where for

(x_{2},\dots,x_{N},y,z)\longrightarrow(x_{2},\dots,x_{N},y,yz^{2})

and for

:

(x_{2},\dots,x_{N},y,z)\longrightarrow(x_{2},\dots,x_{N},y^{2}z,z)

Now we are ready to formulate the following theorem:

Theorem 26
.

Let Landau-Ginzburg orbifold
(f,G)
be as above. Then there is a non-degenerate quasihomogeneous polynomial
\bar{f}
with the following properties:

(\rm{i})

\bar{f}
has a reduced system of weights
(2q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{N},1/2-q_{1})

(\rm{ii})

\bar{f}=f_{\bar{\kappa}}+\bar{f}_{add}
with the graph
\Gamma_{\bar{\kappa}}=\circ_{\Gamma_{\kappa}}(N+1,1)
and
\bar{f}_{add}=f_{add}(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})
where we put
t_{1}^{2}=x_{1}
and
t_{m}=x_{m}
for

;

such that
(f,G)
and
(\bar{f},\{\mathrm{id}\})
are orbifold equivalent. In particular there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{MF}(\bar{f})\cong\mathrm{MF}_{G}(f).

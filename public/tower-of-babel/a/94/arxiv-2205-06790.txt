Schur-Sato theory for quasi-elliptic rings
Alexander Zheglov

Abstract
The notion of quasi-elliptic rings appeared as a result of an attempt to classify a wide class of commutative rings of operators found in the theory of integrable systems, such as rings of commuting differential, difference, differential-difference, etc. operators. They are contained in a certain non-commutative ”universal” ring - a purely algebraic analogue of the ring of pseudodifferential operators on a manifold, and admit (under certain mild restrictions) a convenient algebraic-geometric description. An important algebraic part of this description is the Schur-Sato theory - a generalisation of the well known theory for ordinary differential operators. Some parts of this theory were developed earlier in a series of papers, mostly for dimension two. In this paper we present this theory in arbitrary dimension. We apply this theory to prove two classification theorems of quasi-elliptic rings in terms of certain pairs of subspaces (Schur pairs). They are necessary for the algebraic-geometric description of quasi-elliptic rings mentioned above. The theory is effective and has several other applications, among them is a new proof of the Abhyankar inversion formula.

\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
-module and right
\Pi_{n}
-module structure on
\hat{\Pi}_{n}
:

(P\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym},A\in\hat{\Pi}_{n},Q\in\Pi_{n})\rightarrow P\circ A\circ Q\in\hat{\Pi}_{n}
.

Besides, the properties 1-3 from remark
2.2
hold also for the module
\hat{\Pi}_{n}
.

Proof.

Any
A\in\hat{\Pi}_{n}
can be uniquely represented as a sum
A=A_{+}+A_{-}
, where
A_{+}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
and
A_{-}=\sum_{i\geq 0}A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i}
. We define a left
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
-module structure by the rule

P\circ A:=P\cdot A_{+}+P\circ A_{-}
, where

P\circ A_{-}=\sum_{i\geq 0}(P\cdot A_{i})\partial_{n}^{-i},

(3.4)

and this sum is well defined by the following reasons. By the properties of the function
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
we have
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(A_{i})\leq\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(A)+i
for any
i\geq 0
, and therefore
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P\cdot A_{i})\leq\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)+\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(A_{i})+i
(in particular,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P\circ A_{-})<\infty
is this sum is well defined). Let
P\cdot A_{i}=\sum_{j\geq 0}B_{i,j}\partial_{n}^{j}
, then

B_{i,j}=\sum_{l\geq 0}C_{j+l}^{l}P_{j+l}\partial_{n}^{l}(A_{i}),

where
P=\sum_{l\geq 0}P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l}
, and

(P\cdot A_{i})\partial_{n}^{-i}=\sum_{k=-i}^{\infty}C_{i,k}\partial_{n}^{k},

where
C_{i,k}=B_{i,k+i}
. Then the sum (
3.4
) is well defined if the sums
\sum_{i\geq 0}C_{i,k}
are well defined for any
k
. Note that
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(C_{i,k})\leq\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)+\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(A)-k
, and therefore we need to look at the slice decompositions of
C_{i,k}
. As

C_{i,k}=B_{i,k+i}=\sum_{l\geq 0}C_{k+i+l}^{l}P_{k+i+l}\partial_{n}^{l}(A_{i}),

and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P_{l})\leq\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)-l
, in the case when
\alpha:=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)-k-i<0
the slice decomposition is

C_{i,k}=\sum_{|\underline{p}|\geq|\alpha|}\frac{x^{\underline{p}}}{\underline{p}!}(C_{i,k})_{(\underline{p})}.

Thus, the sum
\sum_{i\geq 0}C_{i,k}
is well defined, as there are only finitely many terms of the form
\frac{x^{\underline{p}}}{\underline{p}!}(C_{i,k})_{(\underline{p})}
for any given
|\underline{p}|
in the slice decompositions.

By similar arguments,
P\circ A=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\circ A
(as there are only finitely many terms of the form
\frac{x^{\underline{p}}}{\underline{p}!}((P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\circ A)_{(\underline{p})}
for any given
|\underline{p}|
in the whole sum), and

(P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\circ A=\sum_{i\in{\mbox{\sdbl Z}}}(P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\cdot(A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i})

(the product is taken in the ring
\Pi_{n}
), since there are only finitely many summands of given
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
-order in this sum. Therefore,

P\circ A=\sum_{l\geq 0}\sum_{i\in{\mbox{\sdbl Z}}}(P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\cdot(A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i}),

in particular, all axioms of the left module are satisfied.

Analogously, we define a right
\Pi_{n}
-module structure by the rule
A\circ Q:=A\circ Q_{+}+A\circ Q_{-}
, where
A\circ Q_{+}=A_{+}\cdot Q_{+}+A_{-}\cdot Q_{+}
(the first product is taken in
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
, and the second in
\Pi_{n}
), and
A\circ Q_{-}=A_{+}\circ Q_{-}+A_{-}\cdot Q_{+}
. By the same arguments as above

A\circ Q=\sum_{i\in{\mbox{\sdbl Z}}}\sum_{j\geq m}(A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i})\cdot(Q_{j}\partial_{n}^{-j}).

Therefore, as
\Pi_{n}
is an associative ring,

(P\circ A)\circ Q=\sum_{j\geq m}\sum_{l\geq 0}\sum_{i\in{\mbox{\sdbl Z}}}((P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\cdot(A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i}))\cdot(Q_{j}\partial_{n}^{-j})=\\
\sum_{l\geq 0}\sum_{i\in{\mbox{\sdbl Z}}}\sum_{j\geq m}(P_{l}\partial_{n}^{l})\cdot((A_{i}\partial_{n}^{-i})\cdot(Q_{j}\partial_{n}^{-j}))=P\circ(A\circ Q)

and
A
is a bimodule.

The properties 1-3 are now obvious.
∎

4.2
\Gamma
-order and quasi elliptic rings

In this subsection we describe commutative subrings in
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
mentioned in Introduction – the quasi-elliptic rings – that admit effective description in terms of algebro-geometric spectral data.

First we introduce the notion of
\Gamma
-order. This order is defined on
some elements
of the ring
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
or of the ring
\hat{E}_{n}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let’s denote by
\hat{D}_{n}^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}}
the subring in
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
consisting of operators
not depending
on
\partial_{i_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{i_{q}}
. The
\Gamma
-order is defined recursively.

We say that a nonzero operator
P\in\hat{D}_{n}^{2,3,\ldots n}
has
\Gamma
-order
k_{1}
if
P=\sum_{s=0}^{k_{1}}p_{s}\partial_{1}^{s}
, where
0\neq p_{k_{1}}\in\hat{R}
.

We say that a nonzero operator
P\in\hat{D}_{n}^{i+1,i+2,\ldots,n}
has
\Gamma
-order
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i})
if
P=\sum_{s=0}^{k_{i}}p_{s}\partial_{i}^{s}
, where
p_{s}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{i,i+1,\ldots,n}
, and the
\Gamma
-order of
p_{k_{i}}
is
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1})
.

We say that a nonzero operator
P\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}

has
\Gamma
-order

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P)=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})

if
P=\sum_{s=0}^{k_{n}}p_{s}\partial_{n}^{s}
, where
p_{s}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
,
and the
\Gamma
-order of
p_{k_{n}}
is
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})
.

In this situation we say that the operator
P
is
monic
if the highest coefficient (defined recursively in analogous way)
p_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}
is
1
.

The same definition works for the rings
\hat{E}_{n},\hat{\Pi}_{n}
with minimal modification at the last step:
P\in\hat{\Pi}_{n}

has
\Gamma
-order

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P)=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})

if
P=\sum_{s=-\infty}^{k_{n}}p_{s}\partial_{n}^{s}
, where
p_{s}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
,
and the
\Gamma
-order of
p_{k_{n}}
is
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})
.

The
\Gamma
-orders are ordered with respect to the anti-lexicographical order on
{\mbox{\dbl Z}}^{n}
, i.e.
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})<(k_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,k_{n}^{\prime})
iff
k_{n}<k_{n}^{\prime}
or
k_{n}=k_{n}^{\prime}
and
k_{n-1}<k_{n-1}^{\prime}
, or
k_{n}=k_{n}^{\prime}
,
k_{n-1}=k_{n-1}^{\prime}
and
k_{n-2}<k_{n-2}^{\prime}
etc.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that an operator
P\in\hat{E}_{n}

satisfies condition
A_{1}
if
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)\leq|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P)|
.

It is easy to see that any
monic
operator
P
satisfies condition
A_{1}
iff
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P)|
.

The
\Gamma
-order, like the function
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
, has similar properties:

Lemma 4.1
.

(cf.
[
61
, Lemma 2.7]
)

Assume
P_{1},P_{2}\in\hat{E}_{n}
satisfy the condition
A_{1}
. Then
P_{1}P_{2}
satisfies the condition
A_{1}

and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{1}P_{2})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{1})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{2})
.

Proof.

The first property follows from the second and from the properties of function
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
. The second property follows by induction on
n
because of the properties of
HT_{n}
. For
n=1
this is true, because
\hat{R}_{y}
is a domain.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If the operator
S=1-S_{-}
, where
S_{-}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}[[\partial_{n}^{-1}]]\partial_{n}^{-1}
, satisfies the condition
A_{1}
(or equivalently
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S)|=0
), then the operator
S^{-1}=1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(S_{-})^{q}
also satisfies it (and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{-1})=0
,
|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S^{-1})|=0
).

Proof.

By the properties of
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
we have
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{-})\leq 0
. So, the corollary will be proved if we show the series
1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(S_{-})^{q}
is well defined. By the properties of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
we have
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{-})^{k}\leq-k
. So, the series is well defined and determines the inverse operator. Clearly, it satisfies

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S^{-1})=(0,\ldots,0)
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

The subring
B\subset\hat{D}_{n}\subset\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
of commuting operators is called
1-quasi elliptic
(or just quasi-elliptic for brevity) if there are
n
operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
such that

1.

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{i})=(0,\ldots 0,1,0\ldots 0,l_{i})
for
1\leq i<n
,
where
1
stands at the
i
-th place and
l_{i}\in{\mbox{\dbl Z}}_{+}
;

2.

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{n})=(0,\ldots,0,l_{n}),
where

;

3.

For
1\leq i\leq n

\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P_{i})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{i})|
;

4.

P_{i}
are monic.

In general, we call operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}

if they satisfy the conditions 1-3 above, and we call them monic formally quasi elliptic, if they satisfy the conditions 1-4 above.

Remark 4.1
.

According to definition, the quasi-elliptic rings are contained in
\hat{D}_{n}
. However we’ll consider them in
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
because of a weak equivalence relation used in one of classification theorems
8.2
below. Besides, if we consider a quasi-elliptic ring of rank one (see definition

will be still commutative quasi-elliptic ring contained in

. The proof of this result appears to be quite non-trivial and requires not only the technique developed in this paper, but several extra results we’ll present in a subsequent paper
[
64
]
.

Example 4.1
.

Immediately from definition
4.4
it follows that 1-quasi-elliptic subalgebras in
\hat{D}_{1}
are exactly

subalgebras of ordinary differential operators.

Recall that an ordinary differential operator

of positive order

is called
(formally) elliptic
if

. A ring

containing an elliptic element is called

.

There is a well known extensive classification theory of such rings, see Introduction.

5.1
Roots of formally quasi-elliptic operators

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
be monic formally quasi-elliptic operators with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{i})=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,l_{i})
,

, where

stands at the
i
-th place, and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{n})=(0,\ldots,0,l_{n})

1.

There exist unique monic commuting operators
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(L_{i})=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
(
1
is on the
i
-th place) such that
L_{n}^{l_{n}}=P_{n}
,
L_{i}L_{n}^{l_{i}}=P_{i}
,
i<n
.

2.

If
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
satisfy the condition
A_{1}
then
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
satisfy the condition
A_{1}
. In particular, they are monic formally quasi-elliptic as well.

3.

If
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in D_{n}
then
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in E_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is an easy generalisation of analogous claim in
[
61
, Lemma 2.9]
.

1
. We can find each coefficient of the operator
L_{n}=\partial_{n}+u_{0}+u_{-1}\partial_{n}^{-1}+\ldots
, where

step by step, by solving the system of equations, which can be obtained by comparing the coefficients of
P_{n}
and
L_{n}^{l_{n}}
: if
P_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{l_{n}}p_{i}\partial_{n}^{i}
with
p_{l_{n}}=1
, then

l_{n}u_{0}=p_{l_{n}-1},\mbox{\quad}l_{n}u_{-i}+F(u_{0},\ldots,u_{-i+1})=p_{l_{n}-1-i},

(5.1)

where
F
is a polynomial in
u_{0},\ldots,u_{-i+1}
and their derivatives.
Clearly, this system is uniquely solvable. So, the operator
L_{n}
is uniquely defined. Note that
L_{n}
is invertible in
\hat{E}_{n}
and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(L_{n}^{-1})=(0,\ldots,0,-1)
. Therefore, the operators
L_{i}=P_{i}L_{n}^{-l_{i}}
for

are also uniquely defined.

The same arguments show that item
3
is true.

The proof of item
2
is by induction. If
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
satisfy the condition
A_{1}
, then from equations (
5.1
) we get
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(u_{0})\leq 1
and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(u_{-i})\leq 1+i
, because

is the coefficient at
\partial_{n}^{-i}
of the expression

whose order is

by induction (and clearly

\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(L_{n})=1
(since

and therefore

.

Corollary 5.1

Let

They are normalised (almost normalised) iff the corresponding operators
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
are normalised (almost normalised).

Proof.

This claim follows directly from the proof of lemma

Lemma 5.2
.

For any monic formally quasi-elliptic operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{i})=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,l_{i})
,
i<n
, where
1
stands at the
i
-th place, and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(P_{n})=(0,\ldots,0,l_{n})
, we have

1.

There exists an invertible function
f\in\hat{R}_{y}
such that the operators

f^{-1}P_{1}f,\ldots f^{-1}P_{n}f
are almost normalized.

2.

If
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are almost normalised, then there exists an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
of the form
S=\exp(\int pdx_{n})
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
, where
\partial_{i}(p)=0
for
i<n
, such that the operators
S^{-1}P_{1}S,\ldots,S^{-1}P_{n}S
are normalized.

Combining with previous item, for any monic formally quasi-elliptic operators

P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}
there exists an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
such that the operators
S^{-1}P_{1}S,\ldots,S^{-1}P_{n}S
are normalized.

3.

If
S_{1}
is another operator with such a property, then all coefficients of the operator
S^{-1}S_{1}
are from
K_{y}
. In particular, if
K_{y}=K
, then
S^{-1}S_{1}\in K
.

Proof.

The proof is an easy generalisation of analogous claim in
[
61
, Lemma 2.10]
.

1
) Let’s show that there exists an invertible function
f\in\hat{R}_{y}
such that

f^{-1}P_{n}f=\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{n}-1}p^{\prime}_{n,s}\partial_{n}^{s},\mbox{\quad}f^{-1}P_{i}f=\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{i}-1}p^{\prime}_{i,s}\partial_{n}^{s}.

(5.2)

Let
P_{i}=\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{i}}p_{i,s}\partial_{n}^{s}
and
p_{i,l_{i}}=\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}+g_{i}
,
g_{i}\in\hat{R}_{y}
. Since the operators
P_{i}
commute,
g_{i}
don’t depend on
x_{n}
for any
i<n
.

Easy direct computations show that for any invertible function
f\in\hat{R}_{y}
we have

f^{-1}P_{n}f=\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{n}-1}p^{\prime}_{n,s}\partial_{n}^{s},\mbox{\quad}f^{-1}P_{i}f=(\partial_{i}+f^{-1}\partial_{i}(f)+g_{i})\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{i}-1}p^{\prime}_{i,s}\partial_{n}^{s},\quad i<n

with some coefficients
p^{\prime}_{j,s}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
. Now we can construct the needed function by induction.
First, we can find a function
f_{1}
in the form
f_{1}=\exp(-\int g_{1}dx_{1})
. After conjugating all operators by
f_{1}
we get new operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
, where
P_{1}
has the from from equation (
5.2
). Since the operators
P_{i}
commute, the new functions
g_{i}
,
i>1
do not depend on
x_{n},x_{1}
(as easy direct calculations with the highest terms show). Then we can find a function
f_{2}
in the form
f_{2}=\exp(-\int g_{2}dx_{2})
. Since it does not depend on
x_{n},x_{1}
too, after conjugating all operators by
f_{2}
we get new operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
, where
P_{1},P_{2}
has the from from equation (
5.2
). Continuing this procedure, we can find the needed function as the product
f:=f_{1}\ldots f_{n-1}
.

2
) By item
1
, conjugating by an appropriate function
f
we can reduce the claim to the operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
that look like the right hand side in (
5.2
) (obviously,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(f)=0
).

Then direct calculations show that for any
i<n

0=[P_{i},P_{n}]=\partial_{i}(p_{n,l_{n}-1})\partial_{n}^{l_{n}+l_{i}-1}+A,

where
A
consists of monomials with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
-order less that
l_{n}+l_{i}-1
, that is the expression
p_{n,l_{n}-1}
don’t depend on
x_{i}
,
i<n
. Therefore, we may look for an operator
S
such that
\partial_{i}(S)=0
,
i<n
. Direct computations (note that such an
S
commutes with

p_{n,l_{n}-1}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
) show that

S^{-1}P_{n}S=\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}+(p_{n,l_{n}-1}+l_{n}S^{-1}\partial_{n}(S))\partial_{n}^{l_{n}-1}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{n}-2}p^{\prime}_{n,s}\partial_{n}^{s},\mbox{\quad}S^{-1}P_{i}S=\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}+\sum_{s=-\infty}^{l_{i}-1}p^{\prime}_{i,s}\partial_{n}^{s}.

(5.3)

Hence, we can find a needed operator in the form
S=\exp(-\int p_{n,l_{n}-1}/l_{n}dx_{n})
, where we choose the integral in such a way that
(-\int p_{n,l_{n}-1}/l_{n}dx_{n})|_{\underline{x}=\underline{0}}=0
. Since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P_{n})=l_{n}
, we have
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(p_{n,l_{n}-1})\leq 1
and therefore
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(-\int p_{n,l_{n}-1}/l_{n}dx_{n})\leq 0
.
Since
p_{n,l_{n}-1}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
and the coefficients of the expression
(-\int p_{n,l_{n}-1}/l_{n}dx_{n})
don’t depend on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}
, the exponent is well defined and its order is zero. clearly,
S
is invertible. Since the conjugation by
f
and
S
preserves the
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}
-orders and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
-orders of operators
P_{i}
, the normalised operators are also formally quasi-elliptic.

3
) Let
S^{\prime}:=S^{-1}S_{1}
. Then conjugation by
S^{\prime}
preserves the normalised form of operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Then from (
5.3
) it follows that
\partial_{n}(S^{\prime})=0
. Clearly, we must also have
(S^{\prime})^{-1}\partial_{i}S^{\prime}=\partial_{i}
, where from
\partial_{i}(S^{\prime})=0
for all
i\leq n
. So, the coefficients of the operator
S^{\prime}
are from
K_{y}
. If
K_{y}=K
, then, since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{\prime})=0
, it follows
S^{\prime}\in K
.
∎

5.3
Higher-dimensional analogue of the Schur theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}
be monic almost normalized formally quasi-elliptic operators with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(L_{i})=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
, where
1
stands at the
i
-th place,
1\leq i\leq n
:

L_{n}=\partial_{n}+v_{n,0}+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}v_{n,q}\partial_{n}^{-q}\quad L_{i}=\partial_{i}+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}v_{i,q}\partial_{n}^{-q},\quad i<n.

Then

1.

There exists a monic invertible operator
S=1+S^{-}
, where
S^{-}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}[[\partial_{n}^{-1}]]\partial_{n}^{-1}
, with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S)=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S^{-1})=(0,0)
such that
S^{-1}\partial_{i}S=L_{i}
,
1\leq i<n
,
S^{-1}L_{n,0}S=L_{n}
, where
L_{n,0}=\partial_{n}+v_{n,0}
.

Moreover,
S
satisfies the condition
A_{1}
, i.e. in particular
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S)|=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{-1})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S^{-1})|=0

2.

If
S_{1}
is another operator with such a property, then

SS_{1}^{-1}\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((L_{n,0}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}

and satisfies
A_{1}
. In particular, if
K_{y}=K
or
SS_{1}^{-1}\in E_{n}
, then
SS_{1}^{-1}\in K_{y}[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((L_{n,0}^{-1}))
.

3.

If
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in E_{n}
, then
S\in E_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is an easy generalisation of analogous claim in
[
61
, Lemma 2.11]
, cf. also analogous theorem in
[
43
]
.

1
) It suffices to prove the following fact: if

L_{n}=\partial_{n}+v_{n,0}+\sum_{q=k\geq 1}^{\infty}v_{n,q}\partial_{n}^{-q}\quad L_{i}=\partial_{i}+\sum_{q=k\geq 1}^{\infty}v_{i,q}\partial_{n}^{-q},\quad 1\leq i,j<n,\mbox{\quad}[L_{i},L_{j}]=0,

then there exists an operator
S_{k}=1+s_{k}\partial_{n}^{-k}
with
s_{k}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
such that

S_{k}^{-1}L_{i}S_{k}=\partial_{i}+\sum_{q=k+1}^{\infty}v^{\prime}_{i,q}\partial_{n}^{-q},\quad\quad 1\leq i<n,\quad S_{k}^{-1}L_{n}S_{k}=\partial_{n}+v_{n,0}+\sum_{q=k+1}^{\infty}v^{\prime}_{n,q}\partial_{n}^{-q}.

Indeed, if this fact is proved, then
S^{-1}=\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}S_{q}
, where
S_{1}
is taken for given
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
,
S_{2}
is taken for
S_{1}^{-1}L_{i}S_{1}
, and so on. It is not difficult to see that the sequence
\{\prod_{q=1}^{m}S_{q}\}
is convergent in
\hat{E}_{n}
(with respect to the topology given by pseudo-valuation
-\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
), i.e. the infinite product is well defined.

To prove the fact let’s note first that, since
[L_{i},L_{j}]=0
, it follows
\partial_{i}(v_{j,k})-\partial_{j}(v_{i,k})=0
for
i,j\neq n
and
\partial_{n}(v_{j,k})-\partial_{j}(v_{n,k})+[v_{n,0},v_{j,k}]=0
,
\partial_{j}(v_{n,0})=0
for
j\neq n
. Besides,

S_{k}^{-1}\partial_{i}S_{k}=\partial_{i}+S_{k}^{-1}\partial_{i}(S_{k})=\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}(s_{k})\partial_{n}^{-k}+\ldots,\quad i<n

S_{k}^{-1}L_{n,0}S_{k}=\partial_{n}+S_{k}^{-1}\partial_{n}(S_{k})+S_{k}^{-1}v_{n,0}S_{k}=\partial_{n}+v_{n,0}+(\partial_{n}(s_{k})+[v_{n,0},s_{k}])\partial_{n}^{-k}+\ldots,

whence
s_{k}
can be found from the following system:

\partial_{i}(s_{k})=-v_{i,k}\mbox{\quad}\partial_{n}(s_{k})+[v_{n,0},s_{k}]=-v_{n,k},\quad 1\leq i<n.

(5.4)

This system is solvable, because it is compatible and all coefficients of
v_{i,k}
belong to
\hat{R}_{y}
(so that we can always integrate them).

Since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(L_{i})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(L_{i})|=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(v_{i,k})\leq 1+k
and therefore
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(s_{k})\leq k
. Hence
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}S_{k}=0
(since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(s_{k}\partial_{n}^{-k})\leq 0
and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(1)=0
). Therefore,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}S_{q})=0
. Clearly, the operator
(\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}S_{q})
is invertible, because it is of the form
1+S^{-}
, where
S^{-}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}[[\partial_{n}^{-1}]]\partial_{n}^{-1}
. By the same reason its
\Gamma
-order is
(0,\ldots,0)
, i.e. the operator
S:=(\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}S_{q})^{-1}
satisfies the condition
A_{1}
. The rest of the claim follows from corollary
4.1
.

2
) Clearly,
[SS_{1}^{-1},\partial_{i}]=0
for any
1\leq i<n
and
[SS_{1}^{-1},L_{n,0}]=0
. Note that any series in
\hat{E}_{n}
can be written as a series in
L_{n,0}^{-1}
with coefficients in
\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
and vice versa (note that
L_{n,0}^{-1}=\partial_{n}^{-1}(1+v_{n,0}\partial_{n}^{-1})^{-1}
and
\partial_{n}^{-1}=L_{n,0}^{-1}(1-v_{n,0}L_{n,0}^{-1})^{-1}
). Therefore, the operator
SS_{1}^{-1}
written as a series in
L_{n,0}^{-1}
has constant coefficients, for all these coefficients commute with
\partial_{i}
,
i<n
and with
v_{n,0}
(as they don’t depend on
\partial_{n}
and on
x_{i}
with
i<n
) and therefore must commute also with
\partial_{n}
, i.e. it has coefficients from
K_{y}
.

By lemma
4.1
and corollary
4.1
it satisfies the condition
A_{1}
. So, if
K_{y}=K
, then
SS_{1}^{-1}\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))
.

3
) The proof is the same as in item
1
). Note that in this case
L_{n,0},L_{n,0}^{-1}\in E_{n}
.
∎

An immediate corollary of all these technical statements is

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{E}_{n}
be monic formally quasi-elliptic operators.

The set of commuting with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
operators in
\hat{E}_{n}
is a commutative ring, which can be embedded into the ring
K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))
.

Analogously, the set of commuting with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
operators in
\Pi_{n}
is a commutative ring, which can be embedded into the ring
K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
.

If
K_{y}=K
, then it can be embedded into the ring
K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))

(
K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
respectively).

Proof.

By lemma
5.2
there exists an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
such that the operators
P_{i}^{\prime}:=S^{-1}P_{i}S
are normalised. By lemma
5.1
and corollary
5.1
the corresponding operators
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
are normalised as well. By theorem
5.1
there is an invertible operator
S_{1}
such that
S_{1}L_{i}S_{1}^{-1}=\partial_{i}
. In particular,
S_{1}P_{n}^{\prime}S_{1}^{-1}=\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}
and
S_{1}P_{i}^{\prime}S_{1}^{-1}=\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}
for
i<n
.

If
[Q,P_{i}]=0
, then
[S_{1}S^{-1}QSS_{1}^{-1},\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}]=0
and
[S_{1}S^{-1}QSS_{1}^{-1},\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}]=0
for
i<n
. Put
Q^{\prime}:=S_{1}S^{-1}QSS_{1}^{-1}
, and assume
Q^{\prime}=\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}q_{j}\partial_{n}^{-j}
with
q_{j}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
. Assume that
q_{l}
is the first coefficient not belonging to the ring
K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]
. Then

0=[Q^{\prime},\partial_{n}^{l_{n}}]=-l_{i}\partial_{n}(q_{l})\partial_{n}^{l_{i}-1-l}+\mbox{terms of lower $\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}$-order,}

where from
\partial_{n}(q_{l})=0
. Analogously,

0=[Q^{\prime},\partial_{i}\partial_{n}^{l_{i}}]=-\partial_{i}(q_{l})\partial_{n}^{l_{i}-l}+\mbox{terms of lower $\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}$-order,}

where from
\partial_{i}(q_{l})=0
for all
i<n
. Hence,
q_{l}
has constant coefficients,
q_{l}\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]
– a contradiction. If
K_{y}=K
, then, since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(q_{l})<\infty
, we must have
q_{l}\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]
.

So,
Q^{\prime}\in K_{y}[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))
and therefore the set of commuting with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
operators in
\hat{E}_{n}
form a commutative subring, which can be embedded into the ring
K_{y}[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))
.

If
Q\in\Pi_{n}
, then
Q^{\prime}\in\Pi_{n}
too, since
S,S_{1}
are of finite order. So,

Q^{\prime}\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The embedding from corollary is made with the help of conjugation by an operator
S
. We will call it a
Schur operator
. Note that it has an invertible symbol, thus we get
\sigma(S^{-1}QS)=\sigma(S)^{-1}\sigma(Q)\sigma(S)\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
for any operator
Q
commuting with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Denote by
B
the ring of operators commuting with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Then we get an embedding of the ring
\mathop{\rm gr}(B)
into the ring of pseudo-differential operators with constant coefficients, where
\mathop{\rm gr}(B)
denotes the associated graded ring with respect to the filtration defined by the function
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
.

Another corollary is the result on ”purity” of rings of differential operators.

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume
K_{y}=K
. Let
B\subset D_{n}\subset\hat{D}_{n}
be a quasi-elliptic ring of commuting partial differential operators. Then the centraliser
C(B)\subset\hat{D}_{n}
is a ring of partial differential operators, i.e.
C(B)\subset D_{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is an easy generalisation of
[
61
, Prop.3.1]
.

By lemma
5.2
there exists an invertible function
f
such that the operators
P_{i}^{\prime}:=f^{-1}P_{i}f
are almost normalised, where
P_{i}
are quasi-elliptic operators from
B
. By lemma
5.1
and corollary
5.1
the corresponding operators
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\in E_{n}
are almost normalised as well.

Then the same arguments as in corollary
5.2
combined with theorem
5.1
, item 3 show that there is an operator
S\in E_{n}
such that
SBS^{-1}\subset K_{y}[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((L_{n,0}^{-1}))
. Namely, If
[Q,P_{i}^{\prime}]=0
, then
[SQS^{-1},L_{n,0}^{l_{n}}]=0
and
[SQS^{-1},\partial_{i}L_{n,0}^{l_{i}}]=0
for
i<n
. Put
Q^{\prime}:=SQS^{-1}
, and assume
Q^{\prime}=\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}q_{j}L_{n,0}^{-j}
with
q_{j}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
. Assume that
q_{l}
is the first coefficient not belonging to the ring
K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]
. Then (note that
[q_{l},L_{n,0}]\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
)

0=[Q^{\prime},L_{n,0}^{l_{n}}]=-l_{i}[q_{l},L_{n,0}]\partial_{n}^{l_{i}-1-l}+\mbox{terms of lower $\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}$-order,}

where from
[q_{l},L_{n,0}]=0
. Analogously,

0=[Q^{\prime},\partial_{i}L_{n,0}^{l_{i}}]=-\partial_{i}(q_{l})\partial_{n}^{l_{i}-l}+\mbox{terms of lower $\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}$-order,}

where from
\partial_{i}(q_{l})=0
for all
i<n
. Hence,
q_{l}
has constant coefficients. Since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(q_{l})<\infty
, we must have
q_{l}\in K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]
– a contradiction.

Then we have also

SC(B)S^{-1}\subset K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]((L_{n,0}^{-1}))\subset E_{n}.

Therefore,
C(B)\subset\hat{D}_{n}\cap E_{n}=D_{n}
.
∎

5.4
Admissible operators

Yet another corollary is the description of the so called admissible operators. They appear naturally as compositions of invertible operators
S,S^{-1}
from lemma
5.2
and theorem
5.1
for different choices of formally quasi-elliptic operators
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. We will need this description to prove the classification theorems in section
8
.
In this section we give only preparatory result, the final result will be given in proposition
8.1
below.

Definition 5.2
.

(cf.
[
61
, Def.3.3]
)

An operator
T\in\Pi_{n}
is said to be 1-admissible if it is an invertible operator,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T)=0
, such that

T^{-1}\partial_{i}T\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. The set of all admissible operators in
\hat{E}_{n}
is denoted by
\mathop{\rm Adm}_{1}(\hat{E}_{n})
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
T\in\Pi_{n}
be a 1-admissible operator.

Then it holds

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1}qT)=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q),\quad\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(T^{-1}qT)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(q)\quad\forall q\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}.

In particular, the operators
T^{-1}\partial_{i}T
satisfy the condition
A_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
q^{\prime}=T^{-1}qT
. Assume, say,
s=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q^{\prime})\neq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q)
. Since
Tq^{\prime}=qT
and
{HT}_{n}(q^{\prime})
, being an operator with constant coefficients, is not a right zero divisor, we have, by section
3
,

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(Tq^{\prime})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T)+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q^{\prime})\leq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q)+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T),

(5.5)

i.e.
s=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q^{\prime})\leq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q)
. If we take an invertible
q
in
\hat{E}_{n}
(say
q=\partial_{n}
),
then
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1}q^{l}T)=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}((T^{-1}qT)^{l})=ls
for any
l\in{\mbox{\dbl Z}}
, because
T^{-1}qT
has constant coefficients. On the other hand, we have by section
3

sl=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1}q^{l}T)\leq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q^{l})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T)=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1})+l\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q),

which is impossible if
l\ll 0
and
s\neq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q)\neq 0
, since
T^{-1}\in\hat{E}_{n}
has finite
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
-order.
If
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q)=0
and
s<0
, then by the same arguments,

0=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(q^{l})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T(q^{\prime})^{l}T^{-1})\leq ls+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1}),

which is impossible if
l\gg 0
. Since any

) and an element of

Now let’s show that

-order, since

). Since

, where l.o.t. mean terms with

-order less than one. Note that

(it is easy to see directly by comparing the coefficients of operators

.

If
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(q_{i}^{\prime})\leq 0
, then

Clearly, then we should have

Definition 6.6

.

•

.

.

F\in\mathsf{Gr}_{0}(V_{n})

, then any unit

). Therefore,

for any

Lemma 6.4
.

,

Example 6.2
.

As an application of our general Sato theory we can give a short proof (though not effective) of the generalized Birkhoff decomposition theorem from

Recall the notation from
[
39
]

\nu(t_{n}):=n
,
\nu(x)=1

Set

,

6.3
Schur pairs, two constructions

The Schur theory for
\hat{D}_{n}
leads to a notion of Schur pair associated to a commutative ring of operators. In this section we describe this notion for quasi-elliptic subrings from
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
. A review of this notion for subrings from
D_{1}
see in
[
37
]
and in
[
48
, §5]
for generalisations in the case of relative situation. The Sato theory is used to establish one-to-one correspondence between Schur pairs and quasi-elliptic rings.

Definition 6.7
.

We say that a pair of subspaces
(A,W)
, where
A,W\subset V_{n}
and
A
is a
K
-algebra with unity such that
W\cdot A\subset W
, is a
1-Schur pair in
V_{n}
if
A
and
W
are 1-spaces (see definition
6.2
) and
W\in\mathsf{Gr}_{\mu}(V_{n})
.

We say that 1-Schur pair is a quasi elliptic 1-Schur pair if
A
is a quasi-elliptic ring.

We say that two 1-Schur pairs
(A,W)
and
(A^{\prime},W^{\prime})
are equivalent if
A^{\prime}=T^{-1}AT
and
W^{\prime}=W\diamond T:=\pi(W\circ T)
, where
T
is a 1-admissible operator, cf. definition
5.2
, and we consider
W
as a submodule of the bimodule
\hat{\Pi}_{n}
, cf. lemma
3.1
and definition
6.1
.

The Schur pairs appear naturally from quasi-elliptic subrings from
\hat{D}_{n}
due to the following construction.

Construction 1.
Given a quasi-elliptic ring
B\subset\hat{D}_{n}
we choose a set of monic formally quasi-elliptic elements
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Then by lemma
5.1
there exist monic formally quasi-elliptic operators
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(L_{i})=1
for all
i
. Then by lemma
5.2
and corollary
5.1
there exists an operator
S_{0}
such that
S_{0}^{-1}L_{i}S_{0}
and
S_{0}^{-1}P_{i}S_{0}
are normalized (of the same order), and by theorem
5.1
there exists a monic invertible operator
S
satisfying the condition
A_{1}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S)|=0
such that
SS_{0}^{-1}L_{i}S_{0}S^{-1}=\partial_{i}\in V_{n}
.

The spaces
A
and
W
are obtained as follows:

A:=SS_{0}^{-1}BS_{0}S^{-1}\subset V_{n}\cap\Pi_{n}\quad W=F\diamond(S_{0}S^{-1})=F\diamond S^{-1}\subset V_{n}\cap\Pi_{n}

(the last equality follows from the special form of
S_{0}
, so that
F\diamond S_{0}=F
). Note that
\mathop{\rm Supp}(W)=F
,
W\in\mathsf{Gr}_{0}(V_{n})
, and
{{\rm dim\,}}_{K}(\mathop{\rm Supp}(W_{k}))=H_{F}(k)
, because
LT(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond S^{-1})=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}
for any
\underline{j}\in{\mbox{\dbl N}}_{0}^{n}
.

By the same facts from the Schur theory it follows that
A
does not depend on the choice of
S_{0},S
(but
W
depends: it changes by a multiplication with a 1-admissible operator with constant coefficients), but may depend on the choice of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. Choosing other elements
P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{n}^{\prime}
we obtain another subring
A^{\prime}\subset V_{n}
, which differs from
A
by conjugation with a 1-admissible operator. This explains the definition of equivalence relation between 1-Schur pairs. Note that in both cases 1-Schur pairs
(A,W)
and
(A^{\prime},W^{\prime})
are equivalent.

Construction 2.
Vice versa, given a 1-Schur pair
(A,W)
with
\mathop{\rm Supp}(W)=F
, we can apply theorem
6.1
, item 3 and find an invertible Sato operator
S
. Then
B:=SAS^{-1}\in\hat{E}_{n}\cap\Pi_{n}
consists of operators
P
such that
F\diamond P\subseteq F
, hence
B\subset\hat{D}_{n}
by theorem
6.1
, item 5. If
(A,W)
is a quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pair, then
since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S)|=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{-1})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S^{-1})|=0
and formal quasi-elliptic operators from
A
satisfy condition
A_{1}
(by definition), it follows from lemma
4.1
and corollary
4.1
that
B
is also quasi-elliptic.

For a generic Schur pair, the following theorem is an improvement of
[
9
, Th. 5.18]

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(A,W)
be a 1-Schur pair and
S
be a Sato operator of
W
. Then the following statements are true.

1.

For any element
f\in A
, there exists a uniquely determined operator
\mathtt{L}_{S}(f)\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}

S\circ f=\mathtt{L}_{S}(f)\circ S.

Moreover,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}\bigl(\mathtt{L}_{S}(f)\bigr)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(f)

2.

Next, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in A

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K

\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})=\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1})\cdot\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{2})\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad\mathtt{L}_{S}(\lambda_{1}f_{1}+\lambda_{2}f_{2})=\lambda_{1}\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1})+\lambda_{2}\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{2})

In other words, the map
A\rightarrow\hat{D}_{n}^{sym},f\mapsto\mathtt{L}_{S}(f)
is a homomorphism of
K
–algebras, which is moreover

Proof.

For any multiindex
\underline{k}\in{\mbox{\dbl N}}_{0}^{n}
we define
w_{\underline{k}}:=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{k}}\diamond S\cdot f\in W
. Since

, there exist (uniquely defined) elements

.

Put

, item 1,2 there exists an operator

such that

S\circ f=\mathtt{L}_{S}(f)\circ S.

The uniqueness of

follows from the regularity of

.

(2) Let

S\circ(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})=\bigl(\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1})\circ S\bigr)\circ f_{2}=\bigl(\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1})\cdot\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{2})\bigr)\circ S.

Since the operator

is uniquely determined by

\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1})\cdot\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{2})=\mathtt{L}_{S}(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})

A quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pair is called

Remark 6.1
.

1) Note that construction 1 produces almost normalised Schur pairs in the following sense: if we multiply

6.4

(0,\ldots,0,1,p-1)<(0,\ldots,0,p)

2.

For a given

where

and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(c_{i,j})\leq\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(Q)-j
for all

(so that

3.

In particular, if

If

Proof.

Definition 7.1
.

7.2
Schur theory,
n=1

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
P\in\hat{D}_{1}^{sym}
be a regular operator with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\bar{P})=k>0
and such that
\bar{P}
is regular.

Then there exists an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{1}^{sym}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
such that

P=S^{-1}\partial_{1}^{k}S
.

Proof.

We’ll find the operator
S
recursively, looking for the partial slice decompositions
S_{[q]}
,

S=\sum_{q\geq 0}S_{[q]}
.

For any
p\geq 0
we need to solve the system

(\partial_{1}^{k}S)_{[p]}=(SP)_{[p]}.

Note that
(\partial_{1}^{k}S)_{[p]}=\partial_{1}^{k}(S_{[p+k]})+\mbox{expression depending on $S_{[q]}$, $q<p+k$}
,
and
(SP)_{[p]}=((S_{[0]}+\ldots+S_{[p]})P)_{[p]}
. Put
S_{[0]}:=1
,
S_{[p]}:=0
for
1\leq p\leq k-1
. Then for
p=0
we have

(\partial_{1}^{k}S)_{[0]}=\partial_{1}^{k}(S_{[k]})+\partial_{1}^{k}=P_{[0]},

and therefore the slice
S_{(k)}
is uniquely determined; besides,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{[k]})\leq 0
. By the same reason all partial slice decompositions
S_{[p]}
,
p>k
will be uniquely determined by this system and by induction
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{[p]})\leq 0
as well. Since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{[0]})=0
, we get
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
too. Note that
(\partial_{1}^{k}\bar{S})_{[p]}=(\bar{S}\bar{P})_{[p]}
automatically.

By corollary
6.1

S
is invertible iff
S
,
\bar{S}
are regular. First note that
S
,
\bar{S}
are
k
-regular:
for any
0\leq i\leq k
we have

\partial_{1}^{i}\diamond S=\partial_{1}^{i}\diamond(S_{[0]}+\ldots+S_{[k]})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{1}^{i}\quad\mbox{if $i<k$}\\
P_{[0]}\quad\mbox{if $i=k$}\end{array}\right.,\quad

\partial_{1}^{i}\diamond\bar{S}=\partial_{1}^{i}\diamond(\bar{S}_{[0]}+\ldots+\bar{S}_{[k]})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{1}^{i}\quad\mbox{if $i<k$}\\
\bar{P}_{[0]}\quad\mbox{if $i=k$}\end{array}\right.

Since
P
,
\bar{P}
are regular, we have
\sigma(P)_{[0]}\neq 0
,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\sigma(P)_{[0]})=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P_{[0]})=k
and analogously
\overline{\sigma(P)}_{[0]}\neq 0
,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\overline{\sigma(P)}_{[0]})=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\bar{P}_{[0]})=k
, therefore the elements

1,\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{1}^{k-1},\overline{\sigma(P_{[0]})}
are linearly independent.

Now we can prove that
S
is
p
-regular for
p>k
by induction. For any
p>k
we have

\partial_{1}^{p}\diamond S=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(\partial_{1}^{k}S)=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(SP)=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(S_{[0]}+\ldots+S_{[p-k]})P,

\partial_{1}^{p}\diamond\bar{S}=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(\partial_{1}^{k}\bar{S})=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(\bar{S}\bar{P})=\partial_{1}^{p-k}\diamond(\bar{S}_{[0]}+\ldots+\bar{S}_{[p-k]})\bar{P}.

Since
P
,
\bar{P}
are regular and
(S_{[0]}+\ldots+S_{[p-k]})
,
(\bar{S}_{[0]}+\ldots+\bar{S}_{[p-k]})
are
(p-k)
-regular by induction, the symbols of the last elements in the equalities are not equal to zero (cf. lemma
6.1
), i.e.
S
,
\bar{S}
are
p
-regular and therefore they are regular by induction.

∎

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
be commuting operators with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(P_{i})=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\bar{P}_{i})=k
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Assume that the spectral module
F
of the ring
K[\sigma(\bar{P}_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(\bar{P}_{n})]
is finitely generated and Cohen-Macaulay.

Then there exists an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
such that

S^{-1}\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S=P_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.

Remark 7.1
.

The condition on the spectral module is satisfied for many examples arising in quantum integrable systems, e.g. for rings of commuting partial differential operators, cf.
[
32
, Th. 4]
,
[
62
, Cor. 1]
,
[
61
, Th. 4.1]
. Further examples will appear in a subsequent paper.

Proof.

The proof is similar to proposition
7.3
.

Construction.
Namely, for any
p\geq 0
we need to solve the system

(\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}S)_{[p]}=(SP_{n})_{[p]},\quad,\ldots,\quad(\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}S)_{[p]}=(SP_{1})_{[p]}

(7.7)

We have again

(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{[p]}=\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}(S_{[p+k_{n}]})+\mbox{expression depending on $S_{[q]}$, $q<p+k_{i}$},

and
(SP_{i})_{[p]}=((S_{[0]}+\ldots+S_{[p]})P_{i})_{[p]}
. In particular, if this system is compatible, the expressions
\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}(S_{[p+k_{i}]})
are uniquely determined via expressions depending on
S_{[q]}
,
q<p+k_{i}
and
P_{i}
.

Put
S_{[0]}:=1
,
S_{(\underline{j})}:=0
for
\underline{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\neq\underline{0}
with
j_{i}<k_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then for
p=0
we have

(\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}S)_{[0]}=\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}(S_{[k_{n}]})+\partial_{n}^{k_{n}}=P_{n,[0]},\quad,\ldots,\quad(\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}S)_{[0]}=\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}(S_{[k_{1}]})+\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}=P_{1,[0]}

From this system the slices
S_{(\underline{j})}
with
\underline{j}=(0,\ldots,0,k_{i},0,\ldots,0)
,
i=1,\ldots,n
are uniquely determined.

Note that we have defined the partial slice decompositions
S_{[q]}
with
q\leq k_{1}
. We will define slices
S_{(\underline{j})}
with
q:=|\underline{j}|>k_{1}
by induction on
q
. Assume we have defined all slices
S_{(\underline{j})}
with
|\underline{j}|<q
. We first determine the slices
S_{(\underline{j})}
with
j_{1}\geq k_{1}
from the equation

(\partial_{1}^{k_{1}}S)_{[q-k_{1}]}=(SP_{1})_{[q-k_{1}]}

of the system (
7.7
). We claim that other equations of this system remain compatible, i.e. for all
\underline{j}
with
j_{1}\geq k_{1}
as above we have

(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{(\underline{j})}=(SP_{i})_{(\underline{j})},\quad i=2,\ldots,n.

Indeed, we have on the one side

(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{(\underline{j})}=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{[0]}=(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}SP_{1})_{[0]}=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{[0]}\diamond P_{1},

and on the other side

(SP_{i})_{(\underline{j})}=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond(SP_{i})=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}SP_{i})_{[0]}=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}SP_{1}P_{i})_{[0]}=\\
(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}SP_{i}P_{1})_{[0]}=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}SP_{i})_{[0]}\diamond P_{1}.

By induction, we have

(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{[0]}=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}\diamond(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)=(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{(j_{1}-k_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n})}=\\
(SP_{i})_{(j_{1}-k_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{n})}=(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}-(k_{1},0,\ldots,0)}SP_{i})_{[0]},

thus
(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{(\underline{j})}=(SP_{i})_{(\underline{j})}
.

Therefore, continuing this line of reasoning, we can determine recursively the slices
S_{(\underline{j})}
with
j_{i}\geq k_{i}
,
j_{l}<k_{l}
for
l<i
from the equation

(\partial_{i}^{k_{i}}S)_{[q-k_{i}]}=(SP_{i})_{[q-k_{i}]}

for each
i=2,\ldots,n
.

At the end of the induction procedure we’ll get the operator
S
. Note that
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=0
by construction.
Automatically we get also the analogue of the equation (
7.7
) for
\bar{S}
,
\bar{P}
.

Regularity.
By corollary
6.1

S
is invertible iff
\bar{S}
is regular. Note that for any
\underline{j}=(l_{1}+q_{1}k_{1},l_{2}+q_{2}k_{2},\ldots,l_{n}+q_{n}k_{n})
, where
q_{i}\in{\mbox{\dbl Z}}_{+}
,
l_{i}<k_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond\sigma(S)=\sigma(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond\bar{S})=\sigma(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{l}}\diamond\bar{S}\bar{P}_{1}^{q_{1}}\ldots\bar{P}_{n}^{q_{n}})=\sigma(\underline{\partial}^{\underline{l}}\diamond\bar{P}_{1}^{q_{1}}\ldots\bar{P}_{n}^{q_{n}})=\underline{\partial}^{\underline{l}}\diamond\sigma(\bar{P}_{1}^{q_{1}}\ldots\bar{P}_{n}^{q_{n}}).

Now note that the elements
\underline{\partial}^{\underline{l}}
belong to the set of generators of the free

K[\sigma(\bar{P}_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(\bar{P}_{n})]
-module
F
(
F
is free since it is Cohen-Macaulay, see
[
7
]
). Indeed, for any fixed system of generators
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
the
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
-order of any linear combination
\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{m}e_{m}
, where
\alpha_{i}
are polynomials without zero terms, is greater than
k
. Thus, each monomial
\underline{\partial}^{\underline{l}}
must be a linear combination of
e_{i}
’s with
constant
coefficients. Thus, making a linear change, we can replace some of generators by these monomials. On the other hand, all such monomials are obviously linear independent over
K
.

Therefore, all elements
\underline{\partial}^{\underline{j}}\diamond\sigma(\bar{S})
are linearly independent, and
\bar{S}
is regular.

∎

Corollary 7.1
.

Any invertible linear change of variables
\partial_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}\partial_{j}
,
c_{i,j}\in K
,
i=1,\ldots,n
can be obtained by conjugation with an operator
S\in\hat{U}_{n}^{sym}
. More precisely,

S=c_{0}\exp(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(c_{i,j}-\delta_{i,j})x_{j})*\partial_{i}))\in\hat{D}_{n}^{sym},

(7.8)

where
c_{0},c_{i,j}\in K
,
c_{0}\neq 0
, and
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta. In particular,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{-1})=0
, and

S^{-1}\partial_{i}S=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}\partial_{j},\mbox{\hskip 10.22217pt}x_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}^{\prime}x_{j},

where
(c_{i,j}^{\prime})
denotes the inverse matrix of
(c_{i,j})
.

Proof.

The proof immediately follows from theorem
7.1
. If
(c_{i,j}^{\prime})
denotes the inverse matrix of
(c_{i,j})
, then it sends
x_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_{i,j}^{\prime}x_{j}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

By theorem
2.1
, item 4) the operator
S
gives the automorphism
x_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_{i,j}x_{j}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1

of the ring
\hat{R}
. In particular, it is invertible with the inverse

S^{-1}=\exp(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(c_{i,j}^{\prime}-\delta_{i,j})x_{j})*\partial_{i}))
. Note that
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S^{-1})=0
. Direct calculations show that

S^{-1}\partial_{i}S=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i,j}\partial_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
∎

8
Classification theorems

Now we are ready to prove a first classification theorem - a classification of certain equivalent classes of quasi-elliptic rings in terms of Schur pairs.

Definition 8.1
.

(cf.
[
61
, Def.3.4]
)

The commutative quasi-elliptic rings
B_{1}
,
B_{2}\subset\hat{D}_{n}
are said to be
equivalent in
\hat{D}_{n}
if there is an invertible operator
S\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
such that
B_{1}=SB_{2}S^{-1}
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Any 1-admissible operator
T
can be written in the form

T=S_{2}S_{1},

where
S_{2}\in\hat{E}_{n}
is monic, satisfies
A_{1}
,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{2})=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{2}^{-1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{2}^{-1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{2})=0
,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S_{2})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S_{2}^{-1})=(0,\ldots,0)
, and
S_{1}\in\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
is invertible and
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{1})=0
.

Proof.

Put
q_{i}^{\prime}=T^{-1}\partial_{i}T
. From lemma
5.3
it follows that for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
we have
q_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{i;j,k}\partial_{j}^{k}+l.o.t.
, where l.o.t. mean terms with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
-order less than zero and
c_{i;j,k}\in K_{y}
, and
q_{n}^{\prime}=\partial_{n}+l.o.t.
, where l.o.t. mean terms with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}
-order less than one. Moreover,
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{i;j,k}\partial_{j}^{k})=1
.

Note that
{HT}_{n}(T\cdot{HT}_{n}(q_{i}^{\prime})\cdot T^{-1})=\partial_{i}
, i.e. in particular the map

\partial_{i}\mapsto\partial_{i}^{\prime}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{i;j,k}\partial_{j}^{k},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1

is invertible. Since
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(\partial_{i}^{\prime})=1
, and
{HT}_{n}(T\partial_{i}^{\prime}T^{-1})=\partial_{i}\notin\mathfrak{m}
, we must have
\overline{\partial_{i}^{\prime}}\neq 0
, and the map

\partial_{i}\mapsto\overline{\partial_{i}^{\prime}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}c_{i;j}\partial_{j},\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1\quad c_{i;j}\in K

is invertible too.
In particular, the spectral module
F_{n-1}=K[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]
of the ring
K[\overline{\partial_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,\overline{\partial_{n-1}^{\prime}}]
is Cohen-Macaulay. Then by theorem
7.1
there exists an invertible operator
S_{0}\in\hat{D}_{n-1}^{sym}\subset\hat{D}_{n}^{n}
with
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{0})=0
such that
S_{0}\partial_{i}^{\prime}S_{0}^{-1}=\partial_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Put
T_{1}=TS_{0}
. Then the operators
T_{1}^{-1}\partial_{i}T_{1}
are monic formally quasi-elliptic. Then by lemma
5.2
there exists an operator

S_{1}=c_{0}\exp(\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}x_{i})\exp(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{i,n}x_{n}\partial_{i})
(see the proof of lemma
5.2
) such that the operators
S_{1}^{-1}T_{1}^{-1}\partial_{i}T_{1}S_{1}
are normalized.
Note that again
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{1})=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{1}^{-1})=0
.
And by theorem
5.1
there exists an operator
S_{2}\in\hat{E}_{n}
such that
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{2})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S_{2})|=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(S_{2}^{-1})=|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(S_{2}^{-1})|=0
,
S_{2},S_{2}^{-1}
satisfies
A_{1}
and
S_{2}^{-1}S_{1}^{-1}T_{1}^{-1}\partial_{i}T_{1}S_{1}S_{2}=\partial_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Therefore,
T^{\prime}:=T_{1}S_{1}S_{2}\in K_{y}[[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}]]((\partial_{n}^{-1}))\cap\Pi_{n}
. Since all operators in this product are of order zero and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T_{1}S_{1}S_{2})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T)+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{0})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{1})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(S_{2})=0
, it follows that
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(T^{\prime})=0
, where from
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}((T^{\prime})^{-1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}((T^{\prime})^{-1})=0
. Thus,
T=T^{\prime}S_{2}^{-1}S_{1}^{-1}S_{0}^{-1}
and therefore
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(T)=\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(T^{-1})=0
,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T)=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{n}(T^{-1})=0
. The rest of the proof is straightforward.
∎

Theorem 8.1
.

(cf.
[
61
, Th.3.2]
)

There is a one to one correspondence between the classes of equivalent quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pairs
(A,W)
in
V_{n}
of rank
r
with
\mathop{\rm Supp}(W)=F
and the classes of equivalent in
\hat{D}_{n}
quasi-elliptic rings
B\subset\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
of rank
r
.

Remark 8.1
.

In
[
61
, Th.3.2]
(see also
[
32
, Sec. 2]
) a correspondence between slightly narrower classes was established: namely, the 1-admissible operators were assumed to satisfy an extra condition. In fact, the extended equivalence classes are not much more, as it can be seen from the proof.

Proof.

In one way we use the construction 1 from section
6.3
: given a 1-quasi elliptic ring
B
we construct a pair
(A,W)
with the required properties.
If we choose any equivalent 1-quasi elliptic ring
B^{\prime}
, then obviously the pair constructed by
B^{\prime}
will be equivalent to
(A,W)
.

In another way we need to use the analogue of the Sato theory for subspaces in
V_{n}
from section
6
. Namely, given a quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pair
(A,W)
, we construct the corresponding ring
B
by construction 2 from section
6.3
with the help of a Sato operator
S
.

If
(A^{\prime},W^{\prime})
is an equivalent 1-Schur pair, then
A^{\prime}=T^{-1}AT
,
W^{\prime}=WT
for some 1-admissible operator
T
, which can be written (see proposition
8.1
) in the form
T=S_{2}S_{1}
. Then it is easy to see that

W^{\prime}=W\diamond(S_{2}S_{1})=(F\diamond S_{1})\diamond(S_{1}^{-1}SS_{2}S_{1})=F\diamond(S_{1}^{-1}SS_{2}S_{1}),

i.e. the corresponding Sato operator for the space
W^{\prime}
from theorem
6.1
is

S^{\prime}=S_{1}^{-1}SS_{2}S_{1}
. So, the corresponding ring
B^{\prime}=S^{\prime}A^{\prime}(S^{\prime})^{-1}=S_{1}^{-1}BS_{1}
, i.e. it is equivalent to
B
.

At last, note that the composition of two constructions
B\rightsquigarrow(A,W)
,
(A,W)\rightsquigarrow B
leads to equivalent objects.
∎

The theorem above can be improved for quasi-elliptic rings of rank one: as we have noted in remark
6.2
the set of admissible linear changes is not empty for such rings.

Definition 8.2
.

Two quasi-elliptic rings
B_{1},B_{2}\subset\hat{D}_{n}
are said to be
weakly equivalent
if there exits a unit
U\in\hat{U}_{n}^{sym}
such that
B_{1}=UB_{2}U^{-1}
.

Two quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pairs
(A,W)
and
(A^{\prime},W^{\prime})
are said to be
weakly equivalent
if
A^{\prime}=\varphi_{m}\circ Ad(T_{m})\circ\ldots\varphi_{1}\circ Ad(T_{1})(A)
and
W^{\prime}=\varphi_{m}\circ Ad(T_{m})\circ\ldots\varphi_{1}\circ Ad(T_{1})(W)
for some
m\in{\mbox{\dbl N}}
, where
T_{i}
are 1-admissible operators and
\varphi_{i}
are admissible linear changes of variables
4
4

4

Of course,
\varphi_{i}
is an admissible change for the pair
(Ad(T_{i-1})\circ\ldots\varphi_{1}\circ Ad(T_{1})(A),Ad(T_{i-1})\circ\ldots\varphi_{1}\circ Ad(T_{1})(A)(W))
,
Ad(T)(A):=T^{-1}AT
,
Ad(T)(W):=W\diamond T
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
K
be a complete field. Assume
K_{y}=K
.

There is a one to one correspondence between the classes of weakly equivalent quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pairs
(A,W)
in
V_{n}
of rank one with
\mathop{\rm Supp}(W)=F
and the classes of weakly equivalent quasi-elliptic rings of commuting operators
B\subset\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
of rank one.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of theorem
8.1
. Given two weakly equivalent rings
B_{1},B_{2}
we need to show that the corresponding quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pairs are weakly equivalent.

Assume
B_{1}=UB_{2}U^{-1}
, denote by
(A_{i},W_{i})
normalized 1-Schur pairs of rings
B_{i}
constructed by the construction 1 of section
6.3
.

First, taking appropriate powers of formally quasi-elliptic elements in
A_{1}
and making an appropriate linear change of variables
\varphi^{\prime}
from lemma
6.6
we can replace the 1-Schur pair
(A_{1},W_{1})
with the quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pair
(A_{1}^{\prime},W_{1}^{\prime}):=(\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}),\varphi^{\prime}(W_{1}))
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1})
contains formally quasi-elliptic operators of the same
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
-order (we can find an admissible such
\varphi^{\prime}
by remark
6.2
since the Schur pairs are of rank one).
By proposition
6.1
the quasi-elliptic ring
B_{1}^{\prime}
corresponding to this Schur pair by construction 2 from section
6.3
is weakly equivalent to
B_{1}
. i.e.
B_{1}^{\prime}
is weakly equivalent to
B_{2}
. Obviously, it suffices to prove that
(A_{1}^{\prime},W_{1}^{\prime})
is weakly equivalent to
(A_{2},W_{2})
, and we’ll assume below that
(A_{1},W_{1})
satisfies the properties of the pair
(A_{1}^{\prime},W_{1}^{\prime})
.

Let
B_{i}=S_{i}A_{i}S_{i}^{-1}
,
W_{i}=F\diamond S_{i}
. Denote by

\varphi_{i}:W_{i}\rightarrow F
,
w_{i}\mapsto w_{i}\diamond S_{i}^{-1}
the isomorphisms of
A_{i}
-modules
W_{i}
with
B_{i}
-modules
F
(
a_{i}\mapsto S_{i}a_{i}S_{i}^{-1}
). Then
\varphi_{i}^{-1}
are given as
\varphi_{i}:F\rightarrow W_{i}
,
w\mapsto w\diamond S_{i}
. At last, denote by
\psi:F\rightarrow F
,
w\mapsto w\diamond U^{-1}
the isomorphism of
B_{i}
-modules
F
(
b_{1}\mapsto Ub_{1}U^{-1}
). Then the composition
\tilde{\varphi}:=\varphi_{2}^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\varphi_{1}
determines an isomorphism of
A_{i}
-modules
W_{i}
. By construction, it preserves the
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
-order filtration on
W_{i}
and on
A_{i}
. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A_{1}
be formally quasi-elliptic elements of the same
\mathop{\rm\bf ord}
-order, with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(a_{1})=(0,\ldots,0,p)
,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(a_{i})=(\underline{0})-(i-1)
. Let
\tilde{a}_{1}
be the monic
p
-th root of
a_{1}
:
\tilde{a}_{1}^{p}=a_{1}
.
Applying lemma
6.6
and remark
6.2
, we can find an admissible linear change of variables
\varphi
such that
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\varphi(\tilde{\varphi}(a_{i})))=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(a_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Applying theorem
5.1
and lemma
5.2
, we can find a 1-admissible operator
T
such that
T\varphi(\tilde{\varphi}(a_{i}))T^{-1}=a_{i}
,
i=2,\ldots,n
and
T\varphi(\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{a}_{1}))T^{-1}=\tilde{a}_{1}
. Then
T\varphi(\tilde{\varphi}(A_{1}))T^{-1}=A_{1}
. For, arguing as in lemma
6.6
, for any
a\in A_{1}
we have
a\tilde{a}_{1}^{-\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(a)}\in K[[a_{2}\tilde{a}_{1}^{-\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(a_{2})},\ldots,a_{n}\tilde{a}_{1}^{-\mathop{\rm\bf ord}(a_{n})},\tilde{a}_{1}^{-1}]]
. Thus,
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi^{-1}\circ Ad(T)
, i.e.
\varphi(A_{2})=T^{-1}A_{1}T
and
(A_{2},W_{2})
is weakly equivalent to
(A_{1},W_{1})
.

Conversely, two weakly equivalent quasi-elliptic 1-Schur pairs of rank one give, via the construction 2 and proposition
6.1
, two weakly equivalent quasi-elliptic rings in
\hat{D}_{n}^{sym}
.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

If
K_{y}\neq K
, the theorem holds if an additional assumption is added: namely, in each equivalence class of Schur pairs corresponding to quasi-elliptic rings via construction 1, there should exist a representative pair satisfying the extra condition of remark
6.2
, item 1).

Example 8.1
.

Let’s illustrate theorem
8.2
as well as some other theorems of these paper on the explicit example calculated in
[
9
, Sec. 6]
.

Recall that there was a family of 1-Schur pairs
(A,W_{\beta})
defined, where
A,W_{\beta}\in{\mbox{\dbl C}}[z_{1},z_{2}]
,
A={\mbox{\dbl C}}[z_{1}^{2},z_{1}^{3},z_{2}^{2},z_{2}^{3}]
, where
z_{i}=\partial_{i}
, and

W_{\beta}={\mbox{\dbl C}}\cdot w+(\xi_{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}z_{2}+\beta z_{1})z_{1}^{2}{\mbox{\dbl C}}[z_{1}]+(\xi_{1}+\xi_{1}^{2}z_{1}+\beta z_{2})z_{2}^{2}{\mbox{\dbl C}}[z_{2}]+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}{\mbox{\dbl C}}[z_{1},z_{2}],

where

w=1+\xi_{1}z_{1}+\xi_{2}z_{2}+(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}-\beta)\cdot\left(z_{1}z_{2}+\left(\dfrac{z_{1}^{2}}{\xi_{2}^{2}}+\dfrac{z_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{1}^{2}}\right)\right).

In
[
9
, Th. 6.5]
corresponding Sato operators
S_{\beta}
were found,
S_{\beta}=S_{0}+\beta T
, where

S_{0}:=\partial_{1}\partial_{2}+\frac{1}{\xi_{2}-x_{2}}\partial_{1}+\frac{1}{\xi_{1}-x_{1}}\partial_{2}+\frac{1}{(\xi_{1}-x_{1})(\xi_{2}-x_{2})}

and

\begin{split}T=&\frac{1}{(\xi_{1}-x_{1})(\xi_{2}-x_{2})}\left(\frac{1}{\xi_{2}}\left(\delta_{2}\partial_{1}+(\xi_{1}-x_{1})\delta_{2}\partial_{1}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\xi_{1}}\left(\delta_{1}\partial_{2}+(\xi_{2}-x_{2})\delta_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}\right)\right)+\\
&\frac{1}{(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}-\beta)(\xi_{1}-x_{1})(\xi_{2}-x_{2})}\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\left(1+\beta\left(\frac{\partial_{1}}{\xi_{2}}+\frac{\partial_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\right)\right),\end{split}

It is not difficult to see that
A
is not quasi-elliptic, but for a generic linear change of variables (obviously, all linear changes are admissible in our case) it becomes quasi-elliptic. Besides, for a generic linear change
\varphi
we have
\mathop{\rm Supp}(\varphi(W_{\beta})z_{2}^{-2})=F
. By definition, all such quasi-elliptic Schur pairs
(\varphi(A),\varphi(W_{\beta})z_{2}^{-2})
are weakly equivalent. By theorem
6.1
, item 3 there exists another Sato operator
S_{n}
(invertible in
\hat{E}_{n}
) for the space
\varphi(W_{\beta})z_{2}^{-2}
. By theorem
6.1
, item 4 we have
\varphi(S_{\beta})\partial_{2}^{-2}=U\circ S_{n}
for some unity
U\in\hat{U}_{n}^{sym}
.

By theorem
7.1
the linear change
\varphi
can be defined by conjugation with a Schur operator
S_{\varphi}\in\hat{D}_{2}^{sym}
of order zero. Then the corresponding rings of commuting operators are just conjugated rings
S_{\varphi}\mathtt{L}(A)S_{\varphi}^{-1}
. By theorem
8.2
they are weakly equivalent as well.

Besides, such pairs
(\varphi(A),\varphi(W_{\beta})z_{2}^{-2})
fit definition of a Schur pair (and even a pre-Schur pair of rank one) from papers
[
61
]
,
[
62
, Def. 14]
, and therefore determine an algebraic-geometric pre-spectral data, see e.g.
[
62
, Th. 4]
(in these constructions the Sato operator
S_{n}
above was essentially used). By
[
62
, Th. 3]
the last data can be extended to a (reduced) geometric datum
(X,C,p,{{\cal F}}_{\beta})
of rank one, where
X
is a projective surface (a natural compactification of
{{\rm Spec\,}}(A)
),
C
is an integral ample Cartier divisor (by
[
62
, Th. 2]
),
{{\cal F}}_{\beta}={{\rm Proj\,}}(\widetilde{W_{\beta}})
is a torsion free spectral sheaf with fixed Hilbert polynomial (see
[
9
, Sec. 6]
or
[
62
, Def. 13, 14]
), and
p
is a regular point on
C
.

From
[
9
, Sec. 6]
we know that the normalisation of the surface
{{\rm Spec\,}}(A)
is just the affine plane
{\mbox{\dbl A}}^{2}
. By
[
31
, Th.2.1.]
we have
C^{2}=1
and
C
is a rational curve, and therefore by
[
32
, Th. 3.2]
the normalisation of
X
is isomorphic to the projective plane
{\mbox{\dbl P}}^{2}

W_{\beta}
and from definition of the spectral sheaf

it is not difficult to see that
{{\cal F}}_{\beta}|_{C}\simeq n_{*}({{\cal O}}_{{\mbox{\sdbl P}}^{1}})
, where
n:{\mbox{\dbl P}}^{1}\rightarrow C
is the normalisation morphism, iff
\beta=0
. In this case the operator
S_{0}
is just a differential operator and
\varphi(S_{\beta})\partial_{2}^{-2}=cS_{n}
,

[

this confirms the conjecture 7.11 about characterisation of commuting PDOs between all quasi-elliptic rings from

It is not difficult to see that the corresponding to such a geometric data Schur pairs from

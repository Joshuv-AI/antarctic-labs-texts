Regularity theory for fully nonlinear equations of porous medium-type Thanks: Hyungsung Yun has been supported by the KIAS Individual Grant (No. MG097801) at Korea Institute for Advanced Study.
Hyungsung Yun

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the regularity results for nonnegative viscosity solutions to fully nonlinear equations of porous medium-type in bounded domains with the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, to be precise, we prove the global C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -estimates of viscosity solutions. In many PDE problems, the C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -estimates have been obtained through Schauder-type estimates. However, the Schauder-type estimates are not applicable to the porous medium-type equations. We provide techniques for handling porous medium-type equations so that the global C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} -estimates can be established.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem for fully nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations of porous medium-type

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)&&\text{in }\Omega\times(-T,0]\\
u&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega\times\{t=-T\}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[-T,0),\end{aligned}\right.

where
0<\gamma<1
,
u_{0}>0
in
\Omega
, and
\Omega
is a bounded domain. Here the fully nonlinear operator
F
is uniformly parabolic with certain structural conditions (the hypotheses on
F
will be precisely stated in
Section

2
).

We present some previous literature that provides motivation for the problem (
1.1
). Using hodograph transform and change of variables that flattens the boundary, the equation in (
1.1
) is converted to a degenerate equation of the form

(1.2)

v_{t}=x_{n}^{\gamma}\widetilde{F}(D^{2}v,Dv,x,t)\quad\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\coloneqq B_{1}^{+}\times(-1,0].

Since the
Dv
dependence of
\widetilde{F}
can be treated as an
(x,t)
-variable thanks to the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of
v
, the global
C^{1,\alpha}
and
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem for (
1.2
) can be obtained from the results of Lee and Yun
[
15
]
. Let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
) and let
v
be a solution to (
1.2
). Then, the higher regularity of
u
can be described by the
C_{s}^{k,\alpha}
-regularity of
v
for the singular metric

s[(x,t),(y,\tau)]\coloneqq\frac{|x-y|}{x_{n}^{\gamma/2}+y_{n}^{\gamma/2}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\gamma/2}}+\sqrt{|t-\tau|},

where
C_{s}^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is the
s
-Hölder space with respect to metric
s
; we refer to
[
6
,
10
]
and see also
[
12
,
13
]
. Although we can obtain the
C_{s}^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of
u
in this way, the
C_{s}^{2,\alpha}
-regularity still does not deduce the regularity of
u
in the classical sense. Nevertheless, results in
[
15
]
naturally lead to the following question:

Question.
Are solutions to the problem (
1.1
) classical up to the boundary?

There is an answer to this question when
F(D^{2}u,x,t)=\Delta u
. Consider the Cauchy–Dirichlet problem for the porous medium equation of nondivergence form

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=u^{\gamma}\Delta u&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega\times\{t=0\}\\
u&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty).\end{aligned}\right.

For the problem (
1.3
), the above question was proved by Jin, Ros-Oton, and Xiong
[
9
]
, to be precise, they show that the optimal regularity of weak solutions
u
to the problem (
1.3
) with a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
is

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(\cdot,t)&\in C^{2,1-\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})&&\text{for all }t>T^{*}\\
u(x,\cdot)&\in C^{\infty}(T^{*},\infty)&&\text{uniformly in }x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{aligned}\right.

where the time
T^{*}>0
satisfying

(1.4)

u(\cdot,t)\in\textnormal{Lip}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\text{for all }t>T^{*}.

Their results
[
9
]
lead us to expect that the above question would also be positive, but there are technical differences between the two problems. Since the equation in (
1.3
) can be transformed into a divergence form, variational techniques are applicable to (
1.3
), to be precise, by setting
u=(1-\gamma)^{-1/\gamma}v^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}
, the problem (
1.3
) can be transformed into the porous medium equation of divergence form

\left\{\begin{aligned} v_{t}&=\Delta v^{m}&&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
v&=v_{0}&&\text{in }\Omega\times\{t=0\}\\
v&=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty),\end{aligned}\right.

where
m\coloneqq 1/(1-\gamma)
and
u_{0}\coloneqq(1-\gamma)^{-1/\gamma}v_{0}^{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}
. However, their techniques for weak solutions are not suitable for the problem (
1.1
) of the nondivergence form, thus to answer the question, we need to study the regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
).

The goal of this paper is to answer the above question.

1.2.
Fully nonlinear degenerate parabolic equation

Let us summarize the contributions of the preceding literature which deals with fully nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations of the form

(1.8)

u_{t}=\sigma(Du,u,x,t)F(D^{2}u,x,t)+f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\times\overline{\Omega_{T}}\to\mathbb{R}
is nonnegative function. Note that (
1.8
) becomes a degenerate parabolic equation when
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)
vanishes, and those points
(x,t)
can be interior or boundary points.

When the degeneracy of (
1.8
) is given as
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)=x_{n}^{\gamma}
and part of the boundary of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is contained in
\{x_{n}=0\}
, that is, (
1.8
) is expressed as

(1.9)

u_{t}=x_{n}^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)+f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
and
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.9
) was developed in
[
15
]
. Since the degeneracy of (
1.9
) only occurs at the flat boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
, it becomes a uniformly parabolic equation in
K_{T}
for any
K\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega
, so it is important to study the regularity of viscosity solutions at the boundary points. The results in
[
15
]
are closely related to (
1.1
) and will actually be used to obtain regularity results in this article. In particular, the regularity result for (
1.9
) can be used to prove the regularity of solutions to the porous medium equation in the case of
F(D^{2}u,x,t)=\Delta u
; we refer to
[
6
,
10
]
.

The next important case is
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)=|Du|^{\gamma}
, and the nonlinear operator
F
depends only on matrix variable, that is, (
1.8
) is expressed as

(1.10)

u_{t}=|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)+f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T}.

Unlike (
1.9
), which was covered in
[
15
]
, (
1.10
) can be degenerate at interior points and the set of degenerate points is not specified, which is one of the difficult factors to prove regularity results. The interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to (
1.10
) under the assumption that
F
is concave or convex with
F\in C^{1,\kappa}(\mathcal{S}^{n})
was developed in
[
14
]
. Even if we only assume the uniform ellipticity condition on
F
, the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions can be inferred, but it is still an open problem, and the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to

u_{t}=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}F(D^{2}u)+f\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T}

has not been developed, even for non-degeneracy
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma/2}
.

On the other hand, the elliptic analogue of (
1.10
) has been relatively widely studied in the last decade. To be precise, the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)=f\quad\text{in }B_{1}

was developed in
[
8
]
.

The case of
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)=u
is directly related to the porous medium equation. It is quite a delicate issue to discuss the well-posedness of viscosity solutions to these types of equations since it is difficult to obtain the comparison principle, but there are well-known results when
F
is a smooth linear operator. To be precise, the well-posedness of viscosity solutions to

u_{t}=u\Delta u+|Du|^{2}\quad\quad\text{in }Q_{1}

was developed in
[
3
,
7
]
. When
F
is a measurable linear operator, we can expect Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions, as in the case of uniformly parabolic equations. For symmetric matrices
A=(a^{ij}(x))
,
B=(b^{ij}(x))
that satisfy the uniformly ellipticity condition, the Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions to

(1.11)

u_{t}=ua^{ij}D_{ij}u+b^{ij}D_{i}uD_{j}u\quad\text{in }Q_{1}

was developed in
[
4
]
. They developed the modified Krylov-Safonov theory to be applicable to degenerate equations (
1.11
) and derived their results.

In this article, we focus on the case of
\sigma(Du,u,x,t)=u^{\gamma}
, and although studies on the model equation
u_{t}=u^{\gamma}\Delta u
can be found in many literatures, studies on the fully nonlinear equations
u_{t}=u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)
are still in initial stages.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

Given initial data
u_{0}
that is positive in
\Omega
, the viscosity solution
u
of (
1.1
) is also positive in
\Omega_{T}
, and hence the equation in (
1.1
) degenerates near
\partial_{s}\Omega_{T}
, so the interior regularity for (
1.1
) follows the results for uniformly parabolic equations. For this reason,
Theorem

1.1
is proven by obtaining the boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity and extending it to the global regularity.

In many PDE problems, the
C^{1,\alpha}
and
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularities have been proven through Schauder-type estimates. However, for the equation in (
1.1
), these Schauder-type estimates are not applicable due to
the structure of the product of
u^{\gamma}
and
F
. In this paper, we provide a technique for handling this product structure so that Schauder-type estimates can be applied.

It is an important observation that the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity can be obtained for functions in solution classes, and it can be shown that the function
u
that satisfies problem (
1.7
) belongs to a wider solution class from the boundary behavior of
u
. By the compactness argument, the boundary
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of functions in the wider solution class can be obtained from the regularity result of the solution class established in
[
15
]
. On the other hand, the boundary regularity of functions in a solution class is at most
C^{1,\alpha}
, so the boundary
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity cannot be obtained with these techniques. To solve this issue, we need additional information about the boundary behavior of
u
to enable Schauder-type estimates. We converted the structure of the product
u^{\gamma}
and
F
into Hölder continuity of nonlinear operator with degeneracy
[\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)]^{\gamma}
using the following property:

\frac{u}{\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)}\in C^{\alpha_{0}}(\overline{\Omega_{T}\cap Q_{\rho}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{1}\leq\frac{u}{\textnormal{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\quad\text{on }\in\overline{\Omega_{T}},

for some constants
C_{1}>0
,
C_{2}>0
, and
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
. This property allows us to apply the Schauder-type estimates and obtain global regularity.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we summarize some basic notations and gather definitions and known regularity results used throughout the paper.

For a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and

, we denote the cylinder and upper cylinder as

Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})\coloneqq B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{r}^{+}(x_{0},t_{0})\coloneqq Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})\cap\{x_{n}>0\}.

where
B_{r}(x_{0})
is an open ball with center
x_{0}
and radius

. For convenience, we denote

Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{r}^{+}=Q_{r}^{+}(0,0).

For general domains, we denote
\Omega_{T}\coloneqq\Omega\times(-T,0]
for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a time

. We define the bottom, corner, side, and parabolic boundary as

\displaystyle\partial_{b}\Omega_{T}\coloneqq\Omega\times\{t=-T\},\quad\partial_{c}\Omega_{T}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\times\{t=-T\},\quad\partial_{s}\Omega_{T}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\times(-T,0),

and

\partial_{p}\Omega_{T}\coloneqq\partial_{b}\Omega_{T}\cap\partial_{c}\Omega_{T}\cap\partial_{s}\Omega_{T}.

2.1.
Hölder continuity

Defines the Hölder continuity of functions used in the description of the theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded set and
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. We say that
f
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in A
(denoted by
f\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,

, and a polynomial
P_{f}(x,t)
with
\deg P_{f}\leq k
such that

(2.1)

|f(x,t)-P_{f}(x,t)|\leq C(|x-x_{0}|+\sqrt{|t-t_{0}|})^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in A\cap Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}).

We define

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0\mid\eqref{cka_f}\text{ holds with }P_{f}(x,t)\text{ and }C\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[f]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0},t_{0})}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}|D^{i}P_{f}(x_{0},t_{0})|.

We next describe the smoothness of
\partial\Omega
in the pointwise sense. We note that the following deﬁnition, which was introduced in
[
17
,
18
]
, does not depend on the graph representation of
\partial\Omega
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. We say that
\partial\Omega
is
C^{k,\alpha}
at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
(denoted by
\partial\Omega\in C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0})
), if there exist constant
C>0
,
r>0
, a new coordinate system
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
, and a polynomial
P_{\partial\Omega}(x^{\prime})
with
\deg P_{\partial\Omega}\leq k
,
P_{\partial\Omega}(0^{\prime})=0
, and
DP_{\partial\Omega}(0^{\prime})=0
such that
x_{0}=0
in this coordinate system,

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\mid x_{n}>P_{\partial\Omega}(x^{\prime})+C|x^{\prime}|^{k+\alpha}\}\subset B_{r}\cap\Omega,

\displaystyle B_{\rho}\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\mid x_{n}<P_{\partial\Omega}(x^{\prime})-C|x^{\prime}|^{k+\alpha}\}\subset B_{r}\cap\Omega^{c}.

We define

\displaystyle[\partial\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\{C>0\mid\eqref{cka_dom}\text{ holds with }P_{\partial\Omega}(x^{\prime},t)\text{ and }C\},

\displaystyle\|\partial\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[\partial\Omega]_{C^{k,\alpha}(x_{0})}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}|D^{i}P_{\partial\Omega}(0^{\prime})|.

Moreover, if
\partial\Omega\in C^{k,\alpha}(a)
for all
a\in\partial\Omega
, we define

\|\partial\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\coloneqq\sup_{a\in\partial\Omega}\|\partial\Omega\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(a)}.

2.2.
Viscosity solutions

We now introduce the concept of viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear parabolic equations:

(2.3)

u_{t}=u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t),

where
0<\gamma<1
and we assume that the fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and satisfies the following condition:

M\leq N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(M,x,t)\leq F(N,x,t)\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}.

Definition 2.3

(Test functions)
.

Let
u
be a continuous function in
\Omega_{T}
. The function
\varphi:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
test function
if it is
C^{1}
with respect to
t
and
C^{2}
with respect to
x
.

(i)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from above at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

0<u\leq\varphi\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

(ii)

We say that the test function
\varphi
touches
u
from below at
(x,t)
if there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
(x,t)
such that

u\geq\varphi>0\quad\mbox{in }U\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u(x,t)=\varphi(x,t).

We now give the definitions of viscosity solutions of (
2.3
). These definitions are similar to the definitions in
[
3
,
7
]
for linear equations and
[
4
]
for nonlinear equations.

Definition 2.4

(Viscosity solutions)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be a fully nonlinear parabolic operator.

(i)

Let
u:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative upper semicontinuous function. We say
u
is a
viscosity subsoution
of (
2.3
) in
\Omega_{T}
provided the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from above at
(x,t)
, then

\varphi_{t}(x,t)\leq\varphi(x,t)^{\gamma}F(D^{2}\varphi(x,t),x,t).

(ii)

Let
u:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative lower semicontinuous function. We say
u
is a
viscosity supersoution
of (
2.3
) in
\Omega_{T}
provided the following condition holds: if for any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
with
u(x,y)>0
and any test function
\varphi
touching
u
from below at
(x,t)

2.3.

We introduce the Pucci’s extremal operators, which are important operators in the study of fully nonlinear equations.

Definition 2.5

(Pucci’s extremal operators)

Given ellipticity constants

(F1)

(F3)

Let

3.
Comparison with concave solutions

In this section, we discuss a conditional comparison principle for (
2.3
). In general, the comparison principle for fully nonlinear equations can be obtained under appropriate structure conditions.

We say the fully nonlinear operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is
proper
provided that for each
(M,p,x,t)\in\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega_{T}
,

F(M,p,s,x,t)\leq F(M,p,r,x,t)\quad\text{whenever }r\leq s.

Theorem 8.2 in
[
5
]
provides a comparison principle for many kinds of proper
F
, but does not provide a comparison principle for (
2.3
) because the fully nonlinear operator in (
2.3
) is not proper. However, as a special case, a conditional comparison principle can be obtained if one of the two viscosity solutions is concave in
x
. To demonstrate a conditional comparison principle of (
2.3
), we introduce semijets and theorems suggested in
[
5
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Parabolic semijets)
.

Let
u
be a function defined in
\Omega_{T}
and let
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
.

(i)

A
parabolic superjet

\mathscr{P}^{2,+}u(x,t)
consists of
(a,p,M)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}
which satisfy

\displaystyle u(y,s)

\displaystyle\leq u(x,t)+a(s-t)+p\cdot(y-x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{T}M(y-x)+o(|s-t|+|z-x|^{2})\quad\text{as $(y,s)\to(x,t)$}.

Similarly, we can define a
parabolic subjet

\mathscr{P}^{2,-}u(x,t)
. It immediately follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{2,-}u(x,t)=-\mathscr{P}^{2,+}(-u)(x,t).

(ii)

A
limiting superjet

\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,+}u(x,t)
consists of
(a,p,M)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}
with the following statement holds: there exists a sequence
\{(x_{n},t_{n},a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
(a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\in\mathscr{P}^{2,+}u(x_{n},t_{n})
and

\text{$(x_{n},t_{n},u(x_{n},t_{n}),a_{n},p_{n},M_{n})\to(x,t,u(x,t),a,p,M)$}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We define a
parabolic subjet

\overline{\mathscr{P}}^{2,-}u(x,t)
in a similar way.

(Jensen–Ishii’s Lemma,

Let

Lemma 3.4

(Comparison with concave supersolution)

Suppose that

Proof.

We first observe that for a matrix

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{t}

\displaystyle\geq(\tilde{v}-\varphi)^{\gamma}F(D^{2}\tilde{v}+\delta A,x,t)+\delta/T^{2}

as

Note also that

satisfies

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle\leq u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)

\displaystyle\leq u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u+\delta A,x,t)+\delta/T^{2}\quad\text{in the viscosity sense in }\Omega_{T}.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that

for some

For convenience, subindex

If

and

\displaystyle 0<m=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\left(u(x,-T)-\tilde{v}(y,-T)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|x-y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=u(\overline{x},-T)-\tilde{v}(\overline{x},-T)<0

which is not possible. Similarly, we can see that

(x_{\varepsilon},y_{\varepsilon},t_{\varepsilon})
, there exists

and

,

such that for all

where

,

Then it immediately follows that

and

\|\partial\widetilde{\Omega}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0)}\leq r^{\alpha}\|\partial\Omega\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0)}\leq\delta.

From (

,

,

, and

\displaystyle|g(y,s)|

\displaystyle\leq r^{-2-\alpha}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}r^{2+\alpha}(|y|+\sqrt{|s|})^{2+\alpha}+\sqrt{n}K\|\partial\Omega\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(0)}|x||x^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta(|y|+\sqrt{|s|})^{2+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }(y,s)\in\partial_{s}\widetilde{\Omega}_{\widetilde{T}}\cap Q_{1}.

By

for
v

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|v-\widetilde{P}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{\widetilde{T}}\cap Q_{\eta})}\leq\eta^{2+\alpha},

In the regularity theory of nonlinear PDE problems, the Schauder-type estimates is mainly used to establish
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions. However, due to the structure of the product
u^{\gamma}
and

in (

). Even if we consider a wider solution class, such as when obtaining the

-estimates, this difficulty cannot be resolved because the boundary regularity of functions in the solution class is at most

. To solve this, we try to convert the structure of the product

and
F
into a Hölder continuity of fully nonlinear operator. To be precise, we convert the equation in (

) as follows:

u_{t}=u^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t)=[\textnormal{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)]^{\gamma}\widetilde{F}(D^{2}u,x,t)\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T},

where

h(x,t)\coloneqq\frac{u(x,t)}{\textnormal{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{F}(D^{2}u,x,t)\coloneqq h^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x,t).

According to
Lemma

4.1
, we have
C_{1}<h<C_{2}
in
\overline{\Omega_{T}}
, so
\widetilde{F}
satisfies
(F1)
. Therefore if we have

, Schauder-type estimates can be applied to the equation in (

Single-Molecule Magnet Mn 12 on GaAs-supported Graphene: Gate Field Effects from First Principles
Shuanglong Liu, 1,2,3 Maher Yazback, 1,2,3 James N. Fry, 1 Xiao-Guang Zhang, 1,2,3 Hai-Ping Cheng 1,2,3

Abstract
We study gate field effects on the heterostructure Mn 12 ​ O 12 ​ ( COOH ) 16 ​ ( H 2 ​ O ) 4 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{(}\text{COOH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{16}}}\text{(}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} | | graphene | | GaAs via first-principles calculations. We find that under moderate doping levels electrons can be added to but not taken from the single-molecule magnet Mn 12 ​ O 12 ​ ( COOH ) 16 ​ ( H 2 ​ O ) 4 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{(}\text{COOH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{16}}}\text{(}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} ( Mn 12 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}} ). The magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) of Mn 12 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}} decreases as the electron doping level increases, due to electron transfer from graphene to Mn 12 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}} and change in the band alignment between Mn 12 \text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}} and graphene. At an electron doping level of − 5.00 × 10 13 cm − 2 -5.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2} , the MAE decreases by about 18% compared with zero doping. The band alignment between graphene and GaAs is more sensitive to electron doping than to hole doping, since the valence band of GaAs is close to the Fermi level. The GaAs substrate induces a small band gap in the supported graphene under zero gate field and a nearly strain-free configuration. Finally, we propose a vertical tunnel junction for probing the gate dependence of MAE via electron transport measurements.

II
Method

All calculations are based on density functional theory (DFT)
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
as implemented in the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)
Kresse 1995
;
Kresse and Joubert 1999
and the SIESTA package
Soler et al. 2002
.
We use VASP to relax atomic structures with no applied
electric field and SIESTA to calculate electronic structures in the
presence of a gate electric field.
The atomic structure is kept at the relaxed configuration with no electric field.

In VASP calculations, we apply an energy cutoff of
500\,\textrm{eV}
for plane waves and projector augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials
Blochl 1994
.
We adopt the exchange correlation energy functional proposed by Klimes
et al.
, optB86b
Klimes et al. 2011
, to include the van
der Waals interaction between graphene and
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
(or GaAs).
Given the large supercell size (
a=b\approx 3.2\,\textrm{nm}
,
c=5.2\,\textrm{nm}
),
only the
\Gamma
point is sampled in reciprocal space.
The energy tolerance for electronic self-consistency and the force
tolerance for ionic relaxation are set to
1\times 10^{-6}\,\textrm{eV}

and
0.02\,\textrm{eV}/\textrm{\AA}
respectively.
We use Gaussian smearing with a smearing parameter of
0.2\,\textrm{eV}

to facilitate the electronic self-consistent procedure.
In order to eliminate interaction between periodic images in the
perpendicular direction (with respect to the GaAs slab), a vacuum layer of
at least
18\,\textrm{\AA}
is added and electric dipole corrections
(for both energy and force) are enabled.
On-site Coulomb interaction (
U
) within the DFT+
U
method and
spin-orbit interactions are not considered for ionic relaxations.

In SIESTA calculations, we apply double-
\zeta
polarized (DZP) basis functions
Soler et al. 2002

for Mn and O atoms and single-
\zeta
polarized (SZP) basis functions for C, H, Ga, and As atoms.
Such a mixed basis set allows us to describe the magnetic properties (due to Mn atoms) accurately with less computational load.
A SZP basis set is often not sufficiently accurate for structural relaxation, but it usually produces reasonably good electronic structure for a fixed atomic structure.
The basis functions are optimized for
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
, graphene, and the GaAs slab separately.
Detailed specifications of the basis functions are presented in Appendix
A
.
Since the optB86b functional is not available in SIESTA and the atomic structure is fixed, we use the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange correlation energy functional
Perdew et al. 1996
instead.
We apply norm-conserving pseudopotentials as generated by the
Troullier-Martins scheme
Troullier and Martins 1991
and a mesh cutoff
of
200\,\textrm{Ry}
for real space sampling.
To accurately determine the Fermi energy, we adopt a
6\times 6

k
-grid
for sampling reciprocal space
Pack and Monkhorst 1977
and the

4^{\textrm{th}}
order Methfessel-Paxton smearing method
Methfessel and Paxton 1989
with a
smearing temperature of
200\,\textrm{K}
.
Results of convergence tests with respect to the smearing temperature and with respect to the
k
-grid are given in Tables
B1
and
B2
respectively (see Appendix
B
).
The effects of a single back gate are modeled via the effective screening medium (ESM) method
Otani and Sugino 2006
.
In the single gate configuration, the boundary condition for the back-gate (vacuum) side is constant electrostatic potential (vanishing first derivative of the potential).
Such a non-periodic boundary condition for the electrostatic potential is imposed
by the corresponding Green’s function in the ESM method.
In our simulations, the back-gate (vacuum) boundary is
15\,\textrm{\AA}
below (above) the bottom (the top) of the system under study.
In order to improve the numerical results for band alignment within the
heterostructure
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
, we treat the semicore Ga
d
electrons as valence
electrons and adopt the DFT+1/2 approach for GaAs
Ferreira et al. 2008
;
Yuan et al. 2018
.
A DFT+1/2 cutoff radius of
3.8\,\textrm{Bohr}
is applied to limit the range of
self-energy potential for As
4p
orbitals
Ribeiro et al. 2011
.
The DFT+1/2 band gap of bulk GaAs is calculated to be
1.526\,\textrm{eV}
,
in good agreement with the experimental value of

1.52\,\textrm{eV}
at low temperatures
Blakemore 1982
,
as opposed to
0.236\,\textrm{eV}
without this approach.
For the same purpose of improving the band alignment, we apply the DFT+
U

method proposed by Dudarev
et al.

Dudarev et al. 1998
and set the on-site
Coulomb interaction
U
to
4\,\textrm{eV}
for Mn atoms.
This value for
U
yields good agreement of the density of states of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
compared with X-ray photoemission spectra (XPS) and X-ray emission spectra (XES)
measurements
Boukhvalov et al. 2007
.

We also use the SIESTA package to calculate the MAE of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
on graphene
without the GaAs substrate (to be justified later).
Spin-orbit interactions are included via the pseudopotentials
Bachelet and Schlüter 1982
;
Kleinman 1980
and evaluated in an on-site approximation
Fernández-Seivane et al. 2006
.
At high electron doping levels, the self-consistent calculation fails
to converge with the Methfessel-Paxton smearing method.
This convergence problem is solved by using the Fermi-Dirac smearing method.
To improve the numerical accuracy, we apply a
24\times 24

k
-grid
together with a smearing parameter of
0.041\,\textrm{eV}
and an energy tolerance
of
1\times 10^{-6}\,\textrm{eV}
for electronic self-consistency.
We set the spin-orbit coupling strength parameter to be 1.34, such that the calculated MAE (
5.2\,\textrm{meV}
, or
61\,\textrm{K}
) of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{(}\text{COOCH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{16}}}\text{(}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

close to the experimental value
Sessoli et al. 1993b

The DFT+
U
method is not applied for calculating the MAE.

III.2
Electronic structure

Now, we turn to the electronic structure of the
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
heterostructure
under both zero and finite gate electric fields.
Without spin-orbit coupling, isolated graphene is a semi-metal, without an energy gap at the Fermi level.
When graphene is supported on the GaAs(111) surface, calculation using SIESTA shows an energy gap of
2.2\,\textrm{meV}
at the Dirac point.
There are two possible factors that can induce such an energy gap in graphene,
1) a structural distortion in graphene itself, and
2) the non-uniform potential due to the GaAs substrate.
To identify which factor is responsible for the gap, we remove the GaAs substrate and compute the band structure for isolated graphene but with the same structural distortion.
The resulting band structure is gapless to within our numerical precision
(
\sim 0.1\,\textrm{meV}
).
From this we conclude that the
2.2\,\textrm{meV}
energy gap is due to the non-uniform potential of the GaAs substrate.
The band gap of graphene does not change after adsorption of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.

A negative (positive) carrier density means electron (hole) doping.
All energies are measured relative to the Fermi energy, which is set at zero.
Overall, all these three typical energies decrease with
electron doping and increase with hole doping level.

E_{\textrm{Dirac}}
in particular is more sensitive to electron doping than hole doping.
For example,
E_{\textrm{Dirac}}
is
-0.195\,\textrm{eV}
at a charge density of
\rho=-0.50\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
, but only
+0.008\,\textrm{eV}
at
+0.50\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
.
The asymmetric response of
E_{\textrm{Dirac}}
to charge doping can be
understood from two aspects.
First, electrons are mainly added to (taken from) graphene before the
HOMO of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
(the VBM of GaAs) is brought to the Fermi level, whence
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
(GaAs) becomes charged (doped).
However, the VBM of GaAs is much closer to the Fermi level than the HOMO of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
Second, GaAs has a much higher density of states than graphene.
Therefore, the same amount of charge causes a smaller shift in the
energy bands when GaAs is doped than when graphene is doped.
At
\rho=-1.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
, the LUMO orbital of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
is
only a few meV above the Fermi level.
According to our calculations, the LUMO of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
becomes partially occupied,
and thus
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
is negatively charged.
It is noteworthy that the Dirac point of graphene becomes lower in energy
than the VBM of GaAs at a point between

\rho=0.50\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}

and
\rho=1.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
.
We also simulate gate field effects on the band alignment of
\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}

without
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
By comparing with
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
, we find that the influence of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
on both

E_{\textrm{Dirac}}
and
E_{\textrm{VBM}}
is smaller than

1\,\textrm{meV}
within
the charge density range of
[-0.50:1.00]\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
.
At
\rho=-1.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}^{-2}
,
E_{\textrm{Dirac}}

(
E_{\textrm{VBM}}
) increases by 3.3 (2.0) meV upon the adsorption of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.

III.3
Magnetic anisotropy

Next, we examine how electrostatic doping affects the magnetic anisotropy energy of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
We have shown in Section
III.2
that electrons can be added to but cannot be taken from
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
at moderate doping levels.
Therefore, we consider the effects of electron doping on MAE in particular.
When the charge doping density is higher in magnitude than
-1.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2}
, there are only graphene and
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
states near the Fermi level.
According to second order perturbation theory
Wang et al. 1993
, the MAE is dominated by pairs of occupied and unoccupied states around the Fermi level.
Therefore, it should be a good approximation to calculate MAE without GaAs.
Based on the heterostructure
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
without GaAs, we calculate the MAE as

\mathrm{MAE}=E_{\perp}-E_{\parallel}
, where
E_{\perp}
(
E_{\parallel}
) is the DFT total energy for the spin of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
perpendicular (parallel) to the magnetic easy axis.
The calculated MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
versus the charge doping density is shown in Fig.
7
.
As seen from the figure, the MAE decreases as the electron doping level increases.
The MAE at
\rho=-5.0\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2}
decreases by about 18% compared with the value at zero doping.
It is noteworthy that the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
at zero doping is 1% smaller than the MAE of an isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}

Li et al. 2014
.

Figure 7:

Magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) versus charge doping density for
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
.

We can relate the decrease in MAE in terms of electron
transfer from graphene to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
Fig.
8
shows the number of electrons transferred
from graphene to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
versus the charge doping density.
The number of electrons added to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
increases with the electron doping
density, and about 0.18 electrons are added to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
at

\rho=-5.0\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2}
.
Along with the electron transfer, the magnetic moment of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
,
in units of Bohr magneton, increases by nearly the same amount as the number of electrons transferred.
The magnetic moment of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
increases rather than decreases since the spin polarization of the LUMO orbital, which receives the added electrons, is parallel to the total spin of the whole
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule.
Previously, Park and Pederson considered potassium addition to introduce extra electrons on
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}

Park and Pederson 2004
.
They found that the MAE of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
decreases by 15%, 37%, and 56% with 1, 2, and 4 extra electrons respectively.
As a numerical experiment, we add extra electrons to a single isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule which has the same atomic positions as the
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule of the
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
heterostructure.
The MAE of the isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule decreases as the number of added electrons increases from zero to one.
The MAE of isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
decreases by 15% with one additional electron, coincident with Park and Pederson’s findings.
This confirms that electron transfer from graphene to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
is responsible for the decrease in the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
under electron doping.

However, with 0.18 additional electrons, the MAE of isolated

\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
decreases by 2%, which is much smaller than the decrease
of 18% in the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
.
This can be understood within second order perturbation theory,
where pairs of occupied
and unoccupied states can be classified into three types,

\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
-
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
, graphene-graphene, and
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
-graphene.
The 2% decrease in the MAE of the isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
can be
understood as a decrease in the contribution from the
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
-
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
pairs.
Since contributions to the MAE from graphene-graphene pairs are
negligibly small (as seen from isolated graphene),
we owe the extra reduction in the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
to a decrease in the contribution from the
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
-graphene pairs.
Such an argument is supported by the observation that electron
doping dramatically changes the band alignment between
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and
graphene and thus the pairs of occupied graphene (
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
) states
and unoccupied
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
(graphene) states around the Fermi level.
Note that the change in the band alignment between
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and graphene
is mainly caused by electrostatic doping rather than by electron
transfer between
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and graphene.
Thus, the change in the band alignment between
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and
graphene is another factor that is responsible for the decrease
in the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
under electron doping.

In order to investigate the possible role of a local electric field between

\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and graphene, we take the isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule again
and move it close to (
2.46\,\textrm{\AA}{}
away from) the plane where the
boundary condition of constant electrostatic potential is enforced.
In this way, we retain a strong local electric field below
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}

when the
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule is charged.
Otherwise, the local electric field is not as strong in our earlier
numerical experiment using the isolated
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
molecule.
It turns out that the decrease in MAE is still 2% with 0.18
additional electrons even though a strong local electric field is present below
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
Therefore, we exclude the local electric field between
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
and
graphene and the associated electric field across
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
as a separate
reason for the decrease in the MAE of
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
under electron doping.
From perturbation theory
Brereton et al. 1981
, magnetic anisotropy of molecules is closely related to a molecular quadrupole moment, which should change with gate electric field.
Since we determine molecular orbitals and thus electron density self-consistently, the change in molecular quadrupole moment and its influence on MAE have been captured implicitly by our calculations.
However, the effect of electric field on MAE via spin-orbit coupling amplitude is still unknown.

\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
on an
energy scale of
1\,\textrm{meV}
relative to the ground state energy of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
Such spin states are detectable via transport measurements in single-molecule
break junctions based on previous studies
Heersche et al. 2006
;
Jo et al. 2006
;
Zyazin et al. 2010
.

The mechanism for detecting spin excitations in a tunneling current is similar to the approach for magnetic tunnel junctions
Zhang et al. 1997
;
Du et al. 2010
.
The spin excitations can also be probed in the vertical tunnel
junction shown in Fig.
9
a.
As established in Section
III.3
, the MAE of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
can be reduced by electron doping.
Consequently, the energy spacings between spin excitations are reduced, as illustrated in Fig.
9
c.
This is likely the case even though
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
is fractionally charged and
the spin projection
m_{\textrm{s}}
is no longer a good quantum index.
At finite bias voltages, the Fermi level of the bottom graphene layer

E_{\textrm{F}}
is different from that of the top graphene
E_{\textrm{F}}^{\prime}
.
We call the energy range between
E_{\textrm{F}}
and
E_{\textrm{F}}^{\prime}
the bias window.
Whenever a spin excitation enters the bias window
(see an illustration in Fig.
9
d), an additional transmission path through the excitation
of the spin state is opened, leading to a sudden increase in the tunneling current
I
.
The record of multiple spin excitations allows determination of the energy spacings between these spin excitations, and thus the MAE of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
.
As gate voltage varies, the gate-dependence of MAE can then be probed.

In general, one can expect sensitive change of the tunneling current due to the spin state of the SMM in the junction. Therefore tunneling measurements can be used as a more general probe of the spin state of the SMM than just the MAE.
We comment that the usual DFT plus non-equilibrium Green’s function (DFT
+
NEGF) approach
Taylor et al. 2001
cannot capture the transport signals due to spin excitations.
See Appendix
C
for DFT
+
NEGF results at zero bias voltage.

IV
Conclusion and discussion

In conclusion, we have simulated gate field effects on the heterostructures

{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
,
\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
, and
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
.
In the
{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}|\textrm{GaAs}
heterostructure,
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
can gain but not lose electrons
at moderate doping levels.
The MAE of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
adsorbed on graphene decreases by about 18% at an electron
doping level of
-5.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2}
.
Such a decrease in the MAE is due to electron transfer from graphene
to
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
as well as the change in the band alignment between Mn12 and graphene.
The band alignment between graphene and GaAs is more sensitive to
electron doping than to hole doping.
At an electron doping level of
-1.00\times 10^{13}\,\textrm{cm}{}^{-2}
,
the Dirac point of graphene is lifted by about
0.24\,\textrm{eV}
relative to
the valence band maximum of GaAs.
Compared with electrostatic doping, the adsorption of
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
does not
have much effect on the band alignment between graphene and GaAs.
The GaAs substrate induces a band gap of about
2.2\,\textrm{meV}
in graphene due to
the interaction between GaAs and graphene.
It remains to study gate field effects on similar heterostructures with
other ligands around the
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{(}\text{COOR}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{16}}}\text{(}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
molecule.

Appendix C
Electron Transmission

We calculated electron transmission for a
\textrm{Gr}|{}\mathrm{Mn}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{12}}}|\textrm{Gr}
junction under zero bias by Caroli’s formula
Caroli et al. 1971
.
The Green’s functions in Caroli’s formula are based on the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian with spin-orbit coupling.
Due to the huge system size (1148 atoms), we included only the
\Gamma
point for both SCF and transport calculations.
Fig.
C1
shows the electron transmission as a function of energy above the Fermi level (set at zero).
There are four peaks, corresponding to the LUMO (at
0.2\,\textrm{eV}
), LUMO+1, LUMO+2, and LUMO+3 orbitals respectively.
There are no peaks due to spin excitations, which are a few meV above the Fermi level in Fig.
C1
.
It requires theoretical development beyond the DFT+NEGF method to obtain electron transport signals due to spin excitations.
It is noteworthy that both absolute and relative peak positions are tunable by electron doping as well as by spin alignment.

. The black (red) line is for the spin aligned in the easy (a hard) direction. The Fermi level is set to zero.

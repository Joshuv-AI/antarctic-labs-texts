Two- and three-gluon glueballs of C = + C=+
Hua-Xing Chen 1, Wei Chen 2, Shi-Lin Zhu 3

Abstract
We study two- and three-gluon glueballs of C = + C=+ using the method of QCD sum rules. We systematically construct their interpolating currents, and find that all the spin-1 currents of C = + C=+ vanish. This suggests that the “ground-state” spin-1 glueballs of C = + C=+ do not exist within the relativistic framework. We calculate masses of the two-gluon glueballs with J P ​ C = 0 ± ⁣ + / 2 ± ⁣ + J^{PC}=0^{\pm+}/2^{\pm+} and the three-gluon glueballs with J P ​ C = 0 ± ⁣ + / 2 ± ⁣ + J^{PC}=0^{\pm+}/2^{\pm+} . We propose to search for the J P ​ C = 0 − ⁣ + / 2 − ⁣ ± / 3 ± ⁣ − J^{PC}=0^{-+}/2^{-\pm}/3^{\pm-} three-gluon glueballs in their three-meson decay channels in future BESIII, GlueX, LHC, and PANDA experiments.

II.1
Couplings of tensor currents

In the present study we shall use some special tensor currents to study glueballs with non-zero spins
J\neq 0
. These currents have
2\times J
Lorentz indices with certain symmetries, and they couple to both positive- and negative-parity glueballs. In this subsection we briefly explain how we deal with them.

We assume
J_{\alpha\beta}
to be a tensor current with two antisymmetric Lorentz indices
\mu
and
\nu
. Taking the current
J_{\alpha\beta}=\bar{c}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}c
as an example, it can be separated into (
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2,3
and
i,j=1,2,3
):

J_{\alpha\beta}=\bar{c}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}c\rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{c}\sigma_{ij}c\,,\,{P=+}\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\bar{c}\sigma_{0i}c\,,\,{P=-}\,.\end{array}\right.

(1)

Accordingly, it couples to both positive- and negative-parity charmonia through

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\alpha\beta}|h_{c}(\epsilon,p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if^{T}_{h_{c}}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\alpha\beta}|J/\psi(\epsilon,p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if^{T}_{J/\psi}(p_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta}-p_{\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha})\,,

(3)

where
f^{T}_{h_{c}}
and
f^{T}_{J/\psi}
are relevant decay constants. Given the Lorentz structures of
J/\psi
and
h_{c}
to be totally different, they can be clearly separated from each other. For example, we can isolate
h_{c}
at the hadron level by investigating the two-point correlation function containing

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{\alpha\beta}|h_{c}\rangle\langle h_{c}|J_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f^{T}_{h_{c}}\right)^{2}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}p^{\nu}\epsilon_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\epsilon^{*\mu^{\prime}}p^{\nu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(f^{T}_{h_{c}}\right)^{2}~p^{2}~\left(g_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}g_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}-g_{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}g_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}}\right)+\cdots\,,

since the correlation function of
J/\psi
dose not contain the above coefficient. It is not so easy to isolate
J/\psi
from
J_{\alpha\beta}
at the hadron level. Instead, we can investigate its partner current

\tilde{J}_{\alpha\beta}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\times J^{\gamma\delta}\,,

(5)

which couples to
J/\psi
and
h_{c}
just in the opposite ways:

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{\alpha\beta}|J/\psi(\epsilon,p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\tilde{f}^{T}_{J/\psi}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{\alpha\beta}|h_{c}(\epsilon,p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\tilde{f}^{T}_{h_{c}}(p_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta}-p_{\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha})\,.

(7)

Accordingly, we can use the two currents
J_{\alpha\beta}
and
\tilde{J}_{\alpha\beta}
to study and well separate
J/\psi
and
h_{c}
.

We apply the above process to generally investigate the current
J^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{N},\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{N}}
, which has
2N=2J
Lorentz indices with certain symmetries,
e.g.
, the spin-2 current
J^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
has four Lorentz indices, satisfying

J^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}=-J^{\beta_{1}\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}}=-J^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{2},\beta_{1}\alpha_{2}}=J^{\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1},\beta_{2}\beta_{1}}\,.

(8)

Its coupling can be written as:

\langle 0|J^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{N},\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{N}}|X\rangle=if_{X}\mathcal{S}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{N}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\,,

(9)

where
X
is the corresponding state having the same parity as
J^{i_{1}\cdots i_{N},j_{1}\cdots j_{N}}
(
i_{1}\cdots j_{N}=1,2,3
);
\mathcal{S}
denotes symmetrization and subtracting trace terms in the two sets
\{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{N}\}
and
\{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{N}\}
simultaneously, with

[\cdots]^{N}=\epsilon^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}p_{\nu_{1}}\cdots\epsilon^{\alpha_{N}\beta_{N}\mu_{N}\nu_{N}}p_{\nu_{N}}\,.

(10)

Note that the current
J^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{N},\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{N}}
can also couple to the other state
X^{\prime}
having the parity opposite to
X
, but this state
X^{\prime}
can not be easily isolated at the hadron level, so we do not consider it in the present study.

II.2
Two-gluon glueball currents

In this subsection we use the gluon field strength tensor
G^{a}_{\mu\nu}
to construct two-gluon glueball currents, with
a
the color index and
\mu,\nu
the Lorentz indices. We also need
\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}=G^{a,\rho\sigma}\times\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}/2
to denote the dual gluon field strength tensor, and
f^{abc}
to denote the totally antisymmetric
SU(3)_{C}
structure constant. In the present study we only consider local glueball currents without explicit derivatives, although
G^{a}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}
contain covariant derivatives inside themselves.

In Ref.
Jaffe:1985qp
the authors use the chromoelectric and chromomagnetic fields (
i,j=1,2,3
),

E_{i}=G_{i0}~~~~{\rm and}~~~~B_{i}=-{1\over 2}\epsilon_{ijk}G^{jk}\,,

(11)

to write down all the non-relativistic low-dimension two-gluon glueball operators:

\displaystyle 0^{++}~~~~~\vec{E}_{a}^{2}\pm\vec{B}_{a}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle 0^{-+}~~~~~\vec{E}_{a}\cdot\vec{B}_{a}\,,

\displaystyle 1^{-+}~~~~~\vec{E}_{a}\times\vec{B}_{a}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle 2^{++}~~~~~\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[E_{a}^{i}E_{a}^{j}\pm B_{a}^{i}B_{a}^{j}]\,,

\displaystyle 2^{-+}~~~~~\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[E_{a}^{i}B_{a}^{j}-B_{a}^{i}E_{a}^{j}]\,,

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denotes symmetrization and subtracting trace terms in the set
\{ij\}
.

We construct their corresponding relativistic currents in order to perform QCD sum rule analyses:

\displaystyle J_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{s}^{2}G_{a}^{\alpha\mu}\tilde{G}^{a,\beta}_{\mu}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[g_{s}^{2}G_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}G^{a,\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}]\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}[g_{s}^{2}G_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tilde{G}^{a,\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}]\,.

(17)

We shall explicitly prove in Appendix
A
that the third current
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
vanishes, suggesting that the “ground-state” two-gluon glueball of
J^{PC}=1^{-+}
does not exist within the relativistic framework.

The former two currents
J_{0}
of
J^{PC}=0^{++}
and
\tilde{J}_{0}
of
J^{PC}=0^{-+}
couple to the
J^{PC}=0^{++}
and
0^{-+}
two-gluon glueballs
|{\rm GG};{J^{PC}}\rangle
, respectively:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{0}|{\rm GG};{0^{++}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0^{++}}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{0}|{\rm GG};{0^{-+}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0^{-+}}\,,

(19)

where
f_{0^{++}}
and
f_{0^{-+}}
are decay constants. Besides, the current
J_{0}
has a partner,

J_{0}^{\prime}=g_{s}^{2}\tilde{G}_{a}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(20)

whose sum rule result is the same as that of
J_{0}
.

The latter two currents
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
and
\tilde{J}_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
couple to the
J^{PC}=2^{++}
and
2^{-+}
glueballs through:

\displaystyle\langle 0|J_{2}^{\cdots}|{\rm GG};{2^{++}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{2^{++}}\mathcal{S}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{2}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{2}^{\cdots}|{\rm GG};{2^{-+}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle if_{2^{-+}}\mathcal{S}[\epsilon^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}p_{\nu_{i}}]^{2}\epsilon_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\,.

(22)

The current
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
also has a partner,

J_{2}^{\prime\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}=\mathcal{S}[g_{s}^{2}\tilde{G}_{a}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tilde{G}^{a,\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}]\,.

(23)

whose sum rule result is the same as that of
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
.

III
QCD sum rule analyses

In this section we use the two-gluon glueball currents
J_{0}
,
\tilde{J}_{0}
,
J_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
, and
\tilde{J}_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
as well as the three-gluon glueball currents
\eta_{0}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
,
\eta_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}
, and
\tilde{\eta}_{2}^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}

Chen:2021cjr
for detailed discussions.

We take the current
\tilde{J}_{0}
defined in Eq. (
14

\Pi(q^{2})\equiv i\int d^{4}xe^{iqx}\langle 0|{\bf T}[\tilde{J}_{0}(x)\tilde{J}_{0}^{\dagger}(0)]|0\rangle\,,

(35)

separately at hadron and quark-gluon levels.

At the hadron level we express Eq. (
35

\Pi(q^{2})=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\rho(s)}{s-q^{2}-i\varepsilon}ds\,,

(36)

with
\rho(s)={\rm Im}\Pi(s)/\pi
the spectral density. It is parameterized using one
pole dominance for the ground state
X
as well as the continuum contribution,

\displaystyle\rho(s)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\delta(s-M^{2}_{n})\langle 0|\tilde{J}_{0}|n\rangle\langle n|\tilde{J}_{0}^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{2}_{X}\delta(s-M^{2}_{X})+\rm{continuum}\,.

At the quark-gluon level we insert Eq. (
14

35

36

\Pi(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})\equiv f^{2}_{X}e^{-M_{X}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}=\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho(s)ds\,.

(38)

X

M^{2}_{X}(s_{0},M_{B})=\frac{\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}s\rho(s)ds}{\int^{s_{0}}_{0}e^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}\rho(s)ds}\,.

V
Summary and Discussions

In this paper we study two- and three-gluon glueballs of
C=+
using the method of QCD sum rules, including

•

the two-gluon glueballs with the quantum numbers
J^{PC}=0^{\pm+}
,
1^{-+}
, and
2^{\pm+}
;

•

the three-gluon glueballs with the quantum numbers
J^{PC}=0^{\pm+}
,
1^{\pm+}
, and
2^{\pm+}
.

We systematically construct their interpolating currents, and find that all the spin-1 currents of
C=+
vanish, suggesting that the “ground-state” spin-1 glueballs of
C=+
do not exist within the relativistic framework. This behavior is consistent with Lattice QCD calculations
Chen:2005mg
;
Mathieu:2008me
;
Meyer:2004gx
;
Gregory:2012hu
.

We use spin-0 and spin-2 glueball currents to perform QCD sum rule analyses, and calculate masses of their corresponding spin-0 and spin-2 glueballs. All these spin-2 currents have four Lorentz indices with certain symmetries, so that they couple to both positive- and negative-parity glueballs, which need to be further separated at the hadron level. We refer to Ref.
Chen:2021cjr
for detailed discussions.

To end this paper, we briefly discuss possible decay patterns of two- and three-gluon glueballs. The two-gluon glueballs can decay after exciting two quark-antiquark pairs, and recombine into two mesons. However, it is rather difficult to differentiate them from standard

Appendix A
Spin-1 currents of
C=+

In this appendix we prove that the three spin-1 currents
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
,
\eta_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
, and
\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
all vanish. Their definitions are given in Eqs. (
15
), (
27
), and (
28
), respectively. For simplicity, we shall not differentiate the superscript and subscript in the following calculations.

Firstly, we investigate the current
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
. Due to the Lorentz invariant, we simply assume
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1\cdots 3
; besides, we need the Lorentz indices
\mu=0/i
,
\rho=0/k
, and
\sigma=0/l
, with
i/k/l=1\cdots 3
. We obtain:

\displaystyle 2J_{1}^{0\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G_{a}^{0\mu}\tilde{G}_{a}^{\beta\mu}-\{0\leftrightarrow\beta\}\,,

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{a}^{0\mu}G_{a}^{\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{\beta\mu\rho\sigma}-G_{a}^{\beta\mu}G_{a}^{\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{0\mu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{a}^{0i}G_{a}^{k0}\epsilon^{\beta ik0}+G_{a}^{0i}G_{a}^{0l}\epsilon^{\beta i0l}-G_{a}^{\beta i}G_{a}^{kl}\epsilon^{0ikl}\,.

After interchanging
i\leftrightarrow k
, the first term turns out to be zero:

G_{a}^{0i}G_{a}^{k0}\epsilon^{\beta ik0}=G_{a}^{0k}G_{a}^{i0}\epsilon^{\beta ki0}=G_{a}^{0i}G_{a}^{k0}\epsilon^{\beta ki0}\rightarrow 0\,.

(72)

So does the second term. The third term is non-zero when
\beta=k
or
\beta=l
. However, for the case
\beta=k
, we can interchange
i\leftrightarrow l
and obtain (not sum over
\beta
here):

G_{a}^{\beta i}G_{a}^{\beta l}\epsilon^{0i\beta l}=G_{a}^{\beta l}G_{a}^{\beta i}\epsilon^{0l\beta i}=G_{a}^{\beta i}G_{a}^{\beta l}\epsilon^{0l\beta i}\rightarrow 0\,.

(73)

So does the case
\beta=l
. Therefore, the third term is also zero, and the current
J_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
vanishes.

Secondly, we investigate the current
\eta_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
:

\displaystyle 2\eta_{1}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f_{abc}\tilde{G}_{a}^{\mu\nu}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{c}^{\alpha\beta}

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G_{a}^{\rho\sigma}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{c}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G_{a}^{\mu\nu}G_{b}^{\rho\sigma}G_{c}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{abc}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{a}^{\rho\sigma}G_{c}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle 0\,.

In the above expressions, we have consequently interchanged
\mu\nu\leftrightarrow\rho\sigma
and
a\leftrightarrow b
. Similarly, we can prove the current
\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{\alpha\beta}
to be zero.

One can construct more spin-1 three-gluon glueball currents of
C=+
, such as:

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{\prime\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}G_{a}^{\alpha\mu}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{c}^{\nu\beta}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{\prime\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}G_{a}^{\alpha\mu}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}_{c}^{\nu\beta}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}\,.

(76)

It is straightforward to prove the former current
\eta_{1}^{\prime\alpha\beta}
to be zero:

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{\prime\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}G_{a}^{\alpha\mu}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{c}^{\nu\beta}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}G_{a}^{\alpha\nu}G_{b}^{\nu\mu}G_{c}^{\mu\beta}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{abc}G_{a}^{\beta\nu}G_{b}^{\nu\mu}G_{c}^{\mu\alpha}+\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{abc}G_{c}^{\beta\nu}G_{b}^{\nu\mu}G_{a}^{\mu\alpha}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f_{abc}G_{c}^{\nu\beta}G_{b}^{\mu\nu}G_{a}^{\alpha\mu}-\{\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle 0\,.

It is a bit tricky but one can still prove the latter current
\tilde{\eta}_{1}^{\prime\alpha\beta}
to be zero, after explicitly writing out all its Lorentz indices. We have done this using the software
Mathematica

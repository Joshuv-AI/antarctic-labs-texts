Inequalities between Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues of the magnetic Laplacian
Vladimir Lotoreichik

Abstract
. We consider the magnetic Laplacian with the homogeneous magnetic field in two and three dimensions. We prove that the ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -th magnetic Neumann eigenvalue of a bounded convex planar domain is not larger than its k k -th magnetic Dirichlet eigenvalue. In three dimensions, we restrict our attention to convex domains, which are invariant under rotation by an angle of π \pi around an axis parallel to the magnetic field. For such domains, we prove that the ( k + 2 ) (k+2) -th magnetic Neumann eigenvalue is not larger than the k k -th magnetic Dirichlet eigenvalue provided that this Dirichlet eigenvalue is simple. The proofs rely on a modification of the strategy due to Levine and Weinberger.

35P15, 81Q10

1.
Introduction

The aim of the present paper is to obtain inequalities between Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues of the magnetic Laplacian on a bounded domain in the spirit of the Levine-Weinberger and Friedlander-Filonov inequalities for the usual (non-magnetic) Laplacian. The Levine-Weinberger inequality
[
LW86
]
states that on a
d
-dimensional (
d\geq 2
) bounded convex domain the
(k+d)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian does not exceed its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue. Without the convexity assumption, it was proved by Friedlander
[
F91
]
that the
(k+1)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian does not exceed its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue. This inequality was later shown by Filonov
[
F05
]
to be strict and under weaker regularity assumptions on the domain. A discussion of these results can be found in the recent monograph
[
LMP
]
.

Our analysis is motivated by the question to what extent these eigenvalue inequalities survive the presence of the
homogeneous magnetic field. We focus on physically motivated two- and three-dimensional settings.
Inequalities between Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues of the magnetic Laplacian are already considered by Frank and Laptev in
[
FL10
]
. The present paper is partially inspired by their considerations and contains improvements of their results under additional assumptions.

First, it is shown in
[
FL10
]
in two dimensions that,
below any Landau level,
the number of magnetic Neumann eigenvalues exceeds at least by one the number of magnetic Dirichlet eigenvalues.
This result suggests that an inequality between individual eigenvalues with an index shift can hold. In the present paper, we prove
that on any bounded planar convex domain the
(k+1)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the magnetic Laplacian does not exceed its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue.
It is also proved in
[
FL10
]

that in three dimensions the
(k+1)
-th
magnetic Neumann eigenvalue is smaller than the
k
-th magnetic Dirichlet eigenvalue. A natural question is whether one can improve the index shift in this inequality.
We restrict our attention to convex domains, which coincide with itself upon rotation by an angle of
\pi
around an axis parallel to the vector of the magnetic field. For such symmetric domains, we prove that the

(k+2)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the magnetic Laplacian does not exceed its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue provided that the latter Dirichlet eigenvalue is simple.

Recently, Rohleder
[
R23
]
established, using a new variational principle, that in two dimensions the Levine-Weinberger inequality is valid for all sufficiently smooth bounded simply-connected domains (without the convexity assumption). The validity of the Levine-Weinberger inequality for general bounded domains in all space dimensions is a prominent open problem
[
LMP
, Conjecture 3.2.41]
. In this perspective, one may ask whether the inequalities obtained in the present paper are also valid without the convexity assumption.

1.1.
Main results

Let us describe our results in more detail. To this aim we need first to define the operators we consider.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d\in\{2,3\}
be a bounded Lipschitz
domain. Note that any bounded convex domain has Lipschitz boundary (see
[
Gr
, Corollary 1.2.2.3]
). We choose the vector potential
{\bf A}\colon\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of the homogeneous magnetic field of intensity
b>0
in the Landau gauge

\displaystyle{\bf A}(x)=\begin{cases}(0,bx_{1})^{\top},&d=2,\\
(0,bx_{1},0)^{\top},&d=3,\end{cases}

where we use the convention
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
for
d=2
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
for
d=3
.
In two dimensions, the respective magnetic field is given by
\mathrm{curl}\,{\bf A}=\partial_{1}A_{2}-\partial_{2}A_{1}=b
and it points in the direction orthogonal to the plane. The particular choice of the gauge of the homogeneous magnetic field
in two dimensions is not
restrictive for further considerations, because our results concern convex domains, for which there is gauge invariance of the spectra for the magnetic Laplacians;
cf.

[
FH
, Appendix D]
.
In three dimensions,
we get after a simple computation
that the magnetic field is given by
\mathrm{curl}\,{\bf A}=(0,0,b)^{\top}
and thus points along the
x_{3}
-axis.
The direction of the vector of the magnetic field in three dimensions is essential in our analysis.

Let
H^{1}(\Omega)
be the standard
L^{2}
-based first-order Sobolev space and let
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Consider the following closed, densely defined, non-negative quadratic forms

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}[u]

\displaystyle:=\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{d})},\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}:=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega}[u]

\displaystyle:=\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{d})},\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega}:=H^{1}(\Omega),

in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega)
;
cf.

[
FH
, Section 1.2]
.
Let
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega}
be the self-adjoint operators in
L^{2}(\Omega)
associated with the forms
\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega}
, respectively. Compactness of the embedding of
H^{1}(\Omega)
into
L^{2}(\Omega)
yields that the spectra of both
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
are purely discrete.
Denote by
\{\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}

and
\{\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
the eigenvalues
of, respectively,
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
, enumerated in the non-decreasing order and repeated with multiplicities taken into account.

It was proved in two dimensions by Frank and Laptev in
[
FL10
, Theorem 3.1 (1)]
that

\#\big\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\leq b(2q+1)\big\}+1\leq\#\big\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)<b(2q+1)\big\},\qquad\text{for all}\,\,q\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Namely, for any
q\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, if the operator
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
has
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
eigenvalues less or equal to
b(2q+1)
, then the operator
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
has at least
j+1
eigenvalues less than
b(2q+1)
. As we have already mentioned, this result suggests that an inequality with an index shift between the individual eigenvalues of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
may hold. This inequality under an additional convexity assumption is the first main result of the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a bounded, convex domain. Then, for any
b>0
,

\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega),\qquad\text{for all}\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In the same paper, Frank and Laptev
established in
[
FL10
, Theorem 3.1 (2)]
that, in three dimensions the inequality
\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega)<\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
holds for any
b>0
and all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
In our second main result, we prove a (non-strict) eigenvalue inequality with a higher index shift under the convexity assumption in the class of domains invariant under rotation by angle of
\pi
around the
x_{3}
-axis (the axis along which the magnetic field is oriented). In order to formulate this result we introduce the mapping

(1.2)

{\mathsf{J}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\qquad{\mathsf{J}}x=(-x_{1},-x_{2},x_{3})^{\top}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be a bounded, convex domain such that
{\mathsf{J}}(\Omega)=\Omega
. Let
b>0
be fixed. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
is a simple eigenvalue of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
. Then it holds that

\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega).

Theorem
1.2
applies to all domains rotationally invariant around the
x_{3}
-axis including the ball or properly oriented ellipsoid with two semi-axes of equal length. It also applies to some symmetric polyhedral domains. For example, it can be applied to the octahedron oriented so that one of its diagonals lies on the
x_{3}
-axis.

The Levine-Weinberger inequality is sharp in the sense that, in general, the index
k+d
can not be replaced by
k+d+1
. Indeed, as is well-known, for the disk the fourth Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian is larger than its first Dirichlet eigenvalue;
cf.

[
R23
, Example 4.6]
. We provide a numerical evidence in Section
5
below, that in the magnetic case the third Neumann eigenvalue of the disk is not larger than its first Dirichlet eigenvalue. Thus, an example showing sharpness of Theorem
1.1
(if it exists) should either be not the disk or should be based on comparison of higher eigenvalues for the disk.
In view of this observation, it remains an open question whether the index
k+1
in Theorem
1.1
can be replaced by
k+2
. The assumption of simplicity of the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue in Theorem
1.2
is technical and we expect that it can be removed. However, the symmetry of the domain is essential for the argument and it is not
a priori
clear whether the inequality in Theorem
1.2
holds for general convex domains.

Both theorems are first proved for convex polyhedral domains.
The results for general convex domains are deduced via approximation.
The eigenvalue inequalities for polyhedral domains are proved via the min-max principle, where
as the trial subspace we select the span of the first
k
magnetic Dirichlet eigenfunctions and of all but one first-order partial derivatives of the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenfunction.
In the original proof
[
LW86
]
of the Levine-Weinberger inequality for the Laplacian it was possible to use all first-order partial derivatives of the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenfunction in the construction of the trial subspace.
In the magnetic case, we need to exclude the partial derivative with respect to the variable
x_{1}
, on which the vector potential
{\bf A}
depends. Similar idea was used in
[
LR17
]
for the Laplacian with mixed boundary conditions and
in
[
R21
]
for the Schrödinger operator on a bounded domain with (electric) potential dependent on the number of directions strictly less than the space dimension. The main novel difficulties of the magnetic problem is that the perturbation is non-additive and that the eigenfunctions are genuinely complex-valued. The fact that the eigenfunctions of the magnetic Dirichlet Laplacian are complex-valued leads to additional difficulties. It is the main reason for the symmetry assumption imposed in the formulation of Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Overview of the literature

The results of Levine, Weinberger, Friedlander, Filonov, and Rohleder mentioned in the beginning of the introduction were preceded by eigenvalue inequalities in more special settings or under additional assumptions.
Pólya proved
in
[
P52
]
that the second Neumann eigenvalue
of the Laplacian on a bounded smooth planar domain
is smaller than its first Dirichlet eigenvalue. For convex, two-dimensional domains with
C^{2}
-boundary Payne proved in
[
P55
]
that the
(k+2)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian is smaller than its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue, the inequality which was later generalized by Levine and Weinberger to higher dimensions. The Friedlander-Filonov inequality was conjectured by Payne in
[
P91
]
and preceded by the paper of Aviles
[
A86
]
, in which this inequality was proved under an additional assumption on the mean curvature of the boundary.

Similar type inequalities are also obtained for other types of differential operators. Besides the already mentioned papers:
[
FL10
]
on the sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg group and the magnetic Laplacian,
[
LR17
]
on the Laplacian with mixed boundary conditions,
[
R21
]
on the Schrödinger operator, we also refer in this context to recent results
[
DT24
]
on the Stokes operator
and to
[
L24
,
Pr19
]
on the biharmonic and more general polyharmonic operators. The literature on this subject is quite extensive and it is difficult to mention all related results.

Recently, geometric bounds
[
CLPS23
,
EKP16
,
KL23
,
LS15
]
and isoperimetric inequalities
[
CLPS24
,
FFG+23
]
for the eigenvalues of the magnetic Neumann Laplacian attracted considerable attention.
The eigenvalues inequalities obtained in the present paper can also be viewed as estimates of magnetic Neumann eigenvalues but
in terms of magnetic Dirichlet eigenvalues.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we collect properties of the magnetic Laplacian on a bounded domain and recall a formula for integration by parts on polyhedral domains. Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
3
.
In turn, a proof of Theorem
1.2
is provided in Section
4
. Section
5
contains
a numerical analysis of the case of the disk, which is motivated
by the intention to check sharpness of the index shift in Theorem
1.1
. The paper is complemented by Appendix
A
, in which
we show lower semicontinuity of magnetic Neumann eigenvalues on varying convex domains satisfying an inclusion property. This auxiliary statement is used in extending our results from polyhedral domains to general convex domains.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Magnetic Laplacian on a bounded domain

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded Lipschitz domain. We denote by
\partial\Omega
the boundary of
\Omega
and by
\nu
the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. This normal vector is well defined almost everywhere on
\partial\Omega
thanks to Rademacher’s theorem.
By the magnetic Laplacian we understand the following
second-order differential expression

-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}=-\Delta+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}.

It is a special case of the most general second-order differential expression considered in
[
McL
, Chapter 4]
(see also
[
BR12
,
BR20
]
).

The magnetic Dirichlet Laplacian
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
associated with the quadratic form
\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
in (
1.1
) can be characterised as (see
[
FH
, Section 1.2]
)

(2.1)

{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}u=-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}u,\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}=\big\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\big\}.

It follows directly from
[
Gr
, Theorem 3.2.1.2]
that

(2.2)

\Omega\,\,\text{is convex}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Recall that for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
its trace on the boundary
u|_{\partial\Omega}
is well defined as a function in
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and for
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with

\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
its trace of the normal derivative
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}
is well defined as a distribution in
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
; see
[
BGM22
]
for details.

The magnetic Neumann Laplacian
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
associated with the quadratic form
\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}
in (
1.1
) can be characterised as (see
[
FH
, Section 1.2]
and
[
BR20
, Section 5]
)

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}u

\displaystyle=-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}u,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\big\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\colon\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\nu\cdot(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u=0~~\text{on}~\partial\Omega\big\},

where
\nu\cdot(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u
on the boundary
\partial\Omega

should be identified with
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot{\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega}u|_{\partial\Omega}
being, by the above discussion, well defined
as a distribution
in
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Recall also that for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and any
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\Delta v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we have by
[
BGM22
, Eq. (5.112)]
the following formula for integration by parts

(2.4)

((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})v)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{d})}\!=\!(u,-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\!+\!\langle u|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}v|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot{\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega}v|_{\partial\Omega}\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1/2,-1/2}
stands for the duality product between
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, which is compatible with the inner product in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
(linear in the first entry).

The eigenvalues of the magnetic Laplacians

{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}

can be characterised by the min-max principle (see,
e.g.
,
[
FLW23
, Theorem 1.28]
)

(2.5a)

\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\dim{\mathcal{L}}=k\end{smallmatrix}}\max_{u\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{d})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

(2.5b)

\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\dim{\mathcal{L}}=k\end{smallmatrix}}\max_{u\in{\mathcal{L}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{d})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

where the minima are taken with respect to
k
-dimensional linear subspaces
{\mathcal{L}}
of respective Sobolev spaces and the maxima are taken with respect to all non-zero functions in the subspace
{\mathcal{L}}
. The minima are attained on the spans of
k
orthonormal eigenfunctions of respective operators corresponding to their first
k
eigenvalues while the maximum is subsequently attained on the eigenfunctions corresponding to the
k
-th eigenvalue.
As a direct consequence of the above variational characterisation we get that for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a subspace
{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\dim{\mathcal{L}}=k
such that

(2.6)

\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}[u]\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\text{for all}\,\,u\in{\mathcal{L}}.

The next lemma is a special case of
[
BR12
, Proposition 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded connected Lipschitz domain.
Let an open subset
\omega\subset\partial\Omega
be non-empty.
Let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}u=\lambda u
with some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}

and that
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\omega}=0
. Then there holds
u\equiv 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

Under the assumption
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
the Neumann trace
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}
,
is more regular and belongs to
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
(see
[
BGM22
, Eq. (1.18)]
). Thus, its restriction
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\omega}
to
\omega\subset\partial\Omega
is naturally defined.

2.2.
Integration by parts in convex polygons and polyhedra

In this subsection we recall a formula for integration by parts on polyhedral domains, which is derived in
[
LR17
, Appendix A]
in all space dimensions. Its two-dimensional version
can be found in the classical monograph by Grisvard (see
[
Gr
, Lemma 4.3.1.2]
). Before we proceed, we recall the definition of polyhedral domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d=2,3
. The definition can be extended to dimensions
d>3
, but it is not needed for our purposes.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded, connected Lipschitz domain.

(i)

For
d=2
,
\Omega
is a polyhedral (or polygonal) domain if its boundary
\partial\Omega
is the union of finitely many line segments.

(ii)

For
d=3
,
\Omega
is a polyhedral domain if for each plane
H\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
the intersection
H\cap\Omega
is either a polygon in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

(where we identify
H
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
) or empty.

We remark that any polyhedral domain is Lipschitz.
Now we are ready to state a formula for integration by parts on convex polyhedral domains. In the below proposition and in the following, we will occasionally use the abbreviation for the partial derivatives
\partial_{ij}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
. We will also use the abbreviation
\partial_{i}^{2}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
LR17
, Lemma A.1]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded convex polyhedral domain and let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be real-valued. Then

\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{km}u)(\partial_{kj}u){\,\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{mj}u)(\partial_{kk}u){\,\mathrm{d}}x

for all
j,k,m\in\{1,2,3\}
if
d=3
or for all
j,k,m\in\{1,2\}
if
d=2
.

A direct consequence of the above proposition will be useful in the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded convex polyhedral domain and let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then

{\rm Re}\,\left\{\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{km}u)(\overline{\partial_{kj}u}){\,\mathrm{d}}x\right\}={\rm Re}\,\left\{\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{mj}u)(\overline{\partial_{kk}u}){\,\mathrm{d}}x\right\}

for all
j,k,m\in\{1,2,3\}
if
d=3
or for all
j,k,m\in\{1,2\}
if
d=2
.

Proof.

Clearly, we have
{\rm Re}\,u,{\rm Im}\,u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. By Proposition
2.4
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{km}({\rm Re}\,u)\partial_{kj}({\rm Re}\,u){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{mj}({\rm Re}\,u)\partial_{kk}({\rm Re}\,u){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{km}({\rm Im}\,u)\partial_{kj}({\rm Im}\,u){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{mj}({\rm Im}\,u)\partial_{kk}({\rm Im}\,u){\,\mathrm{d}}x,

from which the desired identity immediately follows.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We will prove the theorem first for convex polygons.
The eigenvalue inequality for general convex domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
will be then derived from the eigenvalue inequality for convex polygons via approximation.
Assume until the last step of the proof that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a convex polygon.

Step 1: construction of a trial subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

For the sake of brevity we use the notation
\lambda=\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
. By the consequence (
2.6
) of the min-max principle
for the magnetic Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.5a
) there exists a subspace

{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\dim{\mathcal{L}}=k
such that

(3.1)

\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}[u]\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\text{for all}\,\,u\in{\mathcal{L}}.

Let
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding
to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. By (
2.2
) we infer that
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and, in particular, we get as a consequence that
\partial_{2}v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let us define the subspace
{\mathcal{M}}
of
H^{1}(\Omega)
by

(3.2)

{\mathcal{M}}:={\mathcal{L}}+{\rm span}\,\{\partial_{2}v\}.

Our next aim is to verify that
\dim{\mathcal{M}}=k+1
. To this aim it suffices to show that
\partial_{2}v\notin H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We will prove this statement by contradiction.
Suppose for the moment that
\partial_{2}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let us choose the side
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
of the polygon
\Omega
such that the (outer) unit normal vector
\nu_{\Gamma}=(\nu_{\Gamma,1},\nu_{\Gamma,2})^{\top}
to this side is not parallel to the
x_{1}
-axis. It is clearly possible to do, because in any polygon there are sides not parallel to each other. Let us also denote by
\tau_{\Gamma}=(-\nu_{\Gamma,2},\nu_{\Gamma,1})^{\top}
a unit tangential vector to
\Gamma
. It follows from
v|_{\partial\Omega}=0
that the tangential derivative of
v
on
\Gamma
vanishes

\tau_{\Gamma}\cdot(\nabla v)|_{\Gamma}=-\nu_{\Gamma,2}\partial_{1}v|_{\Gamma}+\nu_{\Gamma,1}\partial_{2}v|_{\Gamma}=-\nu_{\Gamma,2}\partial_{1}v|_{\Gamma}=0.

As a consequence we get since
\nu_{\Gamma,2}\neq 0
that
\partial_{1}v|_{\Gamma}=0
. Altogether, the function
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies the differential equation

-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v=\lambda v
in
\Omega
and its Neumann trace on
\Gamma
vanishes
\partial_{\nu}v|_{\Gamma}=\nu_{\Gamma,1}\partial_{1}v|_{\Gamma}+\nu_{\Gamma,2}\partial_{2}v|_{\Gamma}=0
. Hence, we get by Lemma
2.1
that
v
is identically zero in
\Omega
leading to a contradiction.

Step 2: application of the min-max principle.

Any
w\in{\mathcal{M}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
can be represented as

(3.3)

w=u+c\partial_{2}v

with some
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
u\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Substituting
w
into the quadratic form for the magnetic Neumann Laplacian on the convex polygon
\Omega
we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]

\displaystyle=\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}

\displaystyle+2{\rm Re}\,\left\{\left((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c\partial_{2}v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\right\}+\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}.

We will analyse the three terms appearing on the right-hand side in the above equation separately.
We derive from (
3.1
)

(3.5)

\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Note that

-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}\partial_{2}v=-\Delta\partial_{2}v+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}v\\
=-\partial_{2}(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v=\lambda\partial_{2}v\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where we first evaluated
\Delta\partial_{2}v
in the distributional sense and used that
v
is an eigenfunction
of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. In particular, we also get as a consequence

\Delta\partial_{2}v=2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}v-\lambda\partial_{2}v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

By (
2.4
) we can perform the integration by parts

(3.6)

\left((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c\partial_{2}v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}=-\left(u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}c\partial_{2}v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda(u,c\partial_{2}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where the boundary terms vanished since
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Next, we decompose
v=v_{1}+{\mathsf{i}}v_{2}
with real-valued

v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\partial_{2}v_{1}+{\bf A}\partial_{2}v_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}+\|\nabla\partial_{2}v_{2}-{\bf A}\partial_{2}v_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}+bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}+|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}-bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}+|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}\right){\,\mathrm{d}}x.

Since
\Omega
is a convex polygon and
v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
are real-valued, we can apply the identity in Proposition
2.4

to get

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{j}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}^{2}v_{j}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{j}{\,\mathrm{d}}x,\qquad j=1,2.

Recall that
v
is an eigenfunction of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Thus,
v
satisfies the partial differential equation

(3.9)

-\Delta v+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}v+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v=\lambda v.

Using the above decomposition
v=v_{1}+{\mathsf{i}}v_{2}
with real-valued
v_{1},v_{2}
we derive from (
3.9
)

(3.10)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{1}-2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v_{1}=\lambda v_{1},\\
-\Delta v_{2}+2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v_{2}=\lambda v_{2}.\end{cases}

From the above two equations we can easily express

\partial_{1}^{2}v_{1}
and
\partial_{1}^{2}v_{2}
as follows

(3.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{2}v_{1}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}-2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v_{1}-\lambda v_{1},

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{2}v_{2}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}+2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v_{2}-\lambda v_{2}.

Combining the identity (
3.8
) for
j=1
with the first equation in (
3.11
) we obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}|^{2}-2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}-\lambda v_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}|^{2}-2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}-b^{2}x_{1}^{2}|\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}+\lambda|\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}){\,\mathrm{d}}x,

where we performed twice the integration by parts in the second step.
Analogously we find from the identity (
3.8
) for
j=2
combined with the second equation in (
3.11
)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big(-|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}|^{2}+2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}+bx_{1}^{2}v_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}-\lambda v_{2}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big(-|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}|^{2}+2bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}-b^{2}x_{1}^{2}|\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}+\lambda|\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x.

It follows from (
3.12
) and (
3.13
)
that

\int_{\Omega}\big(|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}+|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x=\lambda\|\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{1}+bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{2}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{2}^{2}v_{2}-bx_{1}\partial_{2}v_{1}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x.

Substituting the last identity into (
3.7
) we get

(3.14)

\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{2})}=\lambda\|\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, we get
from (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+2\lambda{\rm Re}\,\big\{(u,c\partial_{2}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}+|c|^{2}\lambda\|\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\|u+c\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We have shown that for any
w\in{\mathcal{M}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
it holds that
\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]\leq\lambda\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Since
{\mathcal{M}}
is a
(k+1
)-dimensional linear subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
, we get by the min-max principle (
2.5b
) that
\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\lambda=\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
. Thus, the inequality in the theorem is proved for convex polygons.

Step 3: general convex domains.

For any bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
one can construct by
[
Br08
]
a sequence of convex polygons
\{\Omega_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that the inclusion
\Omega\subset\Omega_{n}
holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and that
|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|\rightarrow 0
. Let us fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The min-max principle for the magnetic Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.5a
) and the inclusion
\Omega\subset\Omega_{n}
immediately imply that

(3.15)

\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n}),\qquad\text{for all}\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

It follows from Proposition
A.2
in Appendix
A
that

(3.16)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\geq\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega).

Combining (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) with the eigenvalue inequality in the theorem already shown for convex polygons, we get

\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k+1}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega),

by which the inequality in the theorem is proved also for general convex domains.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof of this theorem follows similar strategy as the proof of Theorem
1.1
. However, there will also be some new ideas related to the symmetry assumption on the domain. We will prove the theorem first for convex polyhedral domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
satisfying the symmetry condition.
The eigenvalue inequality for general convex domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
will be then derived via approximation.
Assume until the last step of the proof that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a convex polyhedral domain such that
{\mathsf{J}}(\Omega)=\Omega
, where the mapping
{\mathsf{J}}
is defined as in (
1.2
).

Step 1: Symmetry of the eigenfunction.

For the sake of brevity we use the notation
\lambda=\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
for the
k
-th eigenvalue of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
. Recall that this eigenvalue is assumed to be simple.
Let
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding
to the eigenvalue
\lambda
.
Let us consider the function
\hat{v}:=v\circ{\mathsf{J}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We get that

\displaystyle-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}\hat{v}

\displaystyle=-\Delta(v\circ{\mathsf{J}})+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}(v\circ{\mathsf{J}})+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}(v\circ{\mathsf{J}})

\displaystyle=-(\Delta v)\circ{\mathsf{J}}-2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}(\partial_{2}v)\circ{\mathsf{J}}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}(v\circ{\mathsf{J}})

\displaystyle=\big(-\Delta v+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}v+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v\big)\circ{\mathsf{J}}=0.

Hence,
\hat{v}
is also an eigenfunction of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. By simplicity of this eigenvalue we conclude that
\hat{v}=zv
with some
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
|z|=1
.

Step 2: construction of a trial subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

By the min-max principle
for the magnetic Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.5a
) there exists a subspace

{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\dim{\mathcal{L}}=k
such that

(4.1)

\mathfrak{h}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\Omega}[u]\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\qquad\text{for all}\,\,u\in{\mathcal{L}}.

For the eigenfunction
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

of the magnetic Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
, we get
\partial_{2}v,\partial_{3}v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Let us define the subspace
{\mathcal{M}}
of
H^{1}(\Omega)
by

(4.2)

{\mathcal{M}}:={\mathcal{L}}+{\rm span}\,\{\partial_{2}v,\partial_{3}v\}.

Our next aim is to verify that
\dim{\mathcal{M}}=k+2
.
In three dimensions this argument is slightly more involved than in two dimensions. Clearly, we have
\dim{\mathcal{M}}\leq k+2
. Suppose for the moment that
\dim{\mathcal{M}}<k+2
. Hence, there would exist
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
(not both equal to zero) such that
c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v\in{\mathcal{L}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Without loss of generality we can assume that real parts not of both
c_{1},c_{2}
are equal to zero.
Let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be a face of the polyhedral domain
\Omega
and let
\tau_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
\tau_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be linearly independent unit tangential vectors to
\Gamma
. It is always possible to choose the face
\Gamma
so that

(4.3)

{\rm span}\,\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},(0,c_{1},c_{2})^{\top}\}={\mathbb{C}}^{3},

as
otherwise, the non-trivial vector
(0,{\rm Re}\,c_{1},{\rm Re}\,c_{2})^{\top}
would be tangential to every face of the polyhedral domain
\Omega
, which is not possible for clear geometric reasons. Let us assume that the face
\Gamma
is chosen so that (
4.3
) holds. It would follow from
v,c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

that

\tau_{1}\cdot(\nabla v)|_{\Gamma}=\tau_{2}\cdot(\nabla v)|_{\Gamma}=0,\qquad(0,c_{1},c_{2})^{\top}\cdot(\nabla v)|_{\Gamma}=0.

Hence, we would get using (
4.3
) that
(\nabla v)|_{\Gamma}=0
and, in particular, we would have
\partial_{\nu}v|_{\Gamma}=0
.
Thus, the function
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
would satisfy the differential equation

-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v=\lambda v
in
\Omega
and
\partial_{\nu}v|_{\Gamma}=0
. By Lemma
2.1
we would get that
v
is identically zero in
\Omega
leading to a contradiction.

Step 3: application of the min-max principle.

Any
w\in{\mathcal{M}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
can be represented as

(4.4)

w=u+c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v

with some
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
u\in{\mathcal{L}}
. Substituting
w
into the quadratic form for the magnetic Neumann Laplacian on the domain
\Omega
we get

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]

\displaystyle=\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}+2{\rm Re}\,\left\{\left((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}\right\}

\displaystyle+\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}.

We will analyse the three terms appearing on the right hand side in the above equation separately.
We derive from (
4.1
)

(4.6)

\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Note that

(4.7)

\displaystyle-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)

\displaystyle=-\Delta(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)+2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)

\displaystyle=-(c_{1}\partial_{2}+c_{2}\partial_{3})(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v=\lambda(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\in L^{2}(\Omega).

where the
\Delta\partial_{j}v
,
j=2,3
, should be understood first in the distributional sense. As a direct consequence of the last computation we infer that
\Delta\partial_{j}v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for
j=2,3
.
By (
2.4
) we can perform the integration by parts

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}

\displaystyle=-\left(u,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda(u,c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where the boundary terms vanished since
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The analysis of the last term on the right hand side in (
4.5
) is the most involved. We get

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}

\displaystyle=|c_{1}|^{2}\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}+2{\rm Re}\,\big\{((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c_{1}\partial_{2}v,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c_{2}\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}\big\}

\displaystyle+|c_{2}|^{2}\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{3}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}.

For any face
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
of the polyhedral domain
\Omega
we derive from
v|_{\Gamma}=0
that

\nabla v|_{\Gamma}=\big(\partial_{\nu}v|_{\Gamma}\big)\nu
.
Hence, we get that
\partial_{i}v|_{\Gamma}=\nu_{i}\partial_{\nu}v|_{\Gamma}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})^{\top}
.
In particular, we get for any
i\in\{2,3\}
using integration by parts

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\,\left({\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Omega}x_{1}\partial_{2}v\overline{\partial_{ii}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\right)

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Re}\,\left({\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Omega}x_{1}\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\right)+2{\rm Re}\,\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathsf{i}}x_{1}\nu_{i}(\partial_{2}v|_{\partial\Omega})\overline{(\partial_{i}v|_{\partial\Omega})}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Re}\,\left({\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Omega}x_{1}\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\right)+{\rm Re}\,\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}{\mathsf{i}}x_{1}\nu_{i}^{2}\nu_{2}\big|\partial_{\nu}v|_{\partial\Omega}\big|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=-2{\rm Re}\,\left({\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Omega}x_{1}\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\right)

(4.10)

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\left({\mathsf{i}}\int_{\Omega}x_{1}\big(\partial_{i}v\overline{\partial_{2i}v}-\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x\right).

Using that
v
is an eigenfunction of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
and
performing the integration by parts with the help of (
4.10
) and Corollary
2.5
we get for
i\in\{2,3\}

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{i}v|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=-\lambda\int_{\Omega}v\overline{\partial_{ii}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v\overline{\partial_{ii}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\int_{\Omega}(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}v\overline{\partial_{ii}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\int_{\Omega}\big(\partial_{11}v+\partial_{22}v+\partial_{33}v-2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}v-b^{2}x_{1}^{2}v\big)\overline{\partial_{ii}v}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\int_{\Omega}\big(\partial_{1i}v\overline{\partial_{1i}v}+\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{2i}v}+\partial_{3i}v\overline{\partial_{3i}v}-2{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2}v\overline{\partial_{ii}v}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}\partial_{i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\int_{\Omega}\big(|\partial_{1i}v|^{2}+|\partial_{2i}v|^{2}\!+\!|\partial_{3i}v|^{2}-{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{i}v\overline{\partial_{2i}v}\!+\!{\mathsf{i}}bx_{1}\partial_{2i}v\overline{\partial_{i}v}+b^{2}x_{1}^{2}|\partial_{i}v|^{2}\big){\,\mathrm{d}}x

(4.11)

\displaystyle={\rm Re}\,\big((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{i}v,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{i}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}=\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{i}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}.

Moreover, we find via the integration by parts formula (
2.4
)
and using identity (
4.7
)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\big((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\big(\partial_{2}v,-(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})^{2}\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\langle\partial_{2}v|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{3}v)|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot({\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega})\partial_{3}v|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\big(\partial_{2}v,\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\big\langle\partial_{2}v|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{3}v)|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot({\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega})\partial_{3}v|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, our aim is to show that under the imposed symmetry assumption the duality product on the right hand side vanishes. For the function
\hat{v}=v\circ{\mathsf{J}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
constructed in Step 1 we get

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{2}v|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{3}v)|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot({\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega})\partial_{3}v|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\big\langle\partial_{2}\hat{v}|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{3}\hat{v})|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot({\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega})\partial_{3}\hat{v}|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}\hat{v},(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{3}\hat{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}-\lambda(\partial_{2}\hat{v},\partial_{3}\hat{v})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\big(((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v)\circ{\mathsf{J}},((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{3}v)\circ{\mathsf{J}}\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}+\lambda\big((\partial_{2}v)\circ{\mathsf{J}},(\partial_{3}v)\circ{\mathsf{J}}\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\big(((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{2}v,((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}+\lambda\big(\partial_{2}v,\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\big\langle\partial_{2}v|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{3}v)|_{\partial\Omega}-{\mathsf{i}}\nu\cdot({\bf A}|_{\partial\Omega})\partial_{3}v|_{\partial\Omega}\big\rangle_{\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}}=0,

where in the first step we used
\hat{v}=zv
with some
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
|z|=1
, in the second step we applied the formula (
4.12
), in the third step we used that
\hat{v}=v\circ{\mathsf{J}}
, in the fourth
step we implicitly used the change of variables
x^{\prime}={\mathsf{J}}x
and used the symmetry of
\Omega
, in the fifth step we again applied the formula (
4.12
) in the opposite direction. Finally, the whole expression vanished, since we have shown that the duality product is equal to minus itself.
Hence, we end up with
the identity

(4.13)

{\rm Re}\,\big\{\big((\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c_{1}\partial_{2}v,(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})c_{2}\partial_{3}v\big)_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}\big\}=\lambda{\rm Re}\,(c_{1}\partial_{2}v,c_{2}\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

where we implicitly used that
(\partial_{2}v,\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-(v,\partial_{23}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=-(\partial_{23}v,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is a real number.
Substituting (
4.11
) and (
4.13
) into (
4.9
) we arrive at

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})(c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{C}}^{3})}

\displaystyle=\lambda|c_{1}|^{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+2\lambda{\rm Re}\,\big\{(c_{1}\partial_{2}v,c_{2}\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}+\lambda|c_{2}|^{2}\|\partial_{3}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\|c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, we get
from (
4.6
), (
4.8
) and (
4.14
) that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+2\lambda{\rm Re}\,\big\{(u,c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}+\lambda\|c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\|u+c_{1}\partial_{2}v+c_{2}\partial_{3}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lambda\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We have shown that for any
w\in{\mathcal{M}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
it holds that
\mathfrak{h}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega}[w]\leq\lambda\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Since
{\mathcal{M}}
is a
(k+2)
-dimensional linear subspace of
H^{1}(\Omega)
, we get by the min-max principle that
\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\lambda=\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)
. Thus, the inequality in the theorem is proved for convex polyhedral domains invariant under the mapping
{\mathsf{J}}
.

Step 4: general convex domains invariant under the mapping
{\mathsf{J}}
.

For any bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
one can construct by
[
Br08
]
a sequence of convex polyhedral
domains
\{\Omega_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that the inclusion
\Omega\subset\Omega_{n}
holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and that
|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|\rightarrow 0
. If the domain
\Omega
satisfies, in addition,
\Omega={\mathsf{J}}(\Omega)
, we can consider the sequence of convex polyhedral domains
\widehat{\Omega}_{n}:=\Omega_{n}\cap{\mathsf{J}}(\Omega_{n})
, which clearly satisfies both conditions
\Omega\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
|\widehat{\Omega}_{n}\setminus\Omega|\rightarrow 0
and, in addition, we have
\widehat{\Omega}_{n}={\mathsf{J}}(\widehat{\Omega}_{n})
.

Let us fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The min-max principle for the magnetic Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
2.5a
) and the inclusion
\Omega\subset\widehat{\Omega}_{n}
immediately imply that

(4.15)

\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\widehat{\Omega}_{n}),\qquad\text{for all}\,\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

It follows from Proposition
A.2
in Appendix
A
that

(4.16)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\widehat{\Omega}_{n})\geq\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\Omega).

Combining (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) with the eigenvalue inequality in the theorem already shown for convex polygons, we get

\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k+2}^{b}(\widehat{\Omega}_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}^{b}(\widehat{\Omega}_{n})\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega),

by which the inequality in the theorem is proved also for general convex domains invariant under the mapping
{\mathsf{J}}
.

5.
The case of the disk

In this section, we will consider the magnetic Laplacian on the disk. Recall that the Levine-Weinberger inequality in two dimensions reads as follows: the
(k+2)
-th Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian on a planar convex domain is not larger than its
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue. The index
k+2
can not be replaced by
k+3
, because the fourth Neumann eigenvalue of the Laplacian on the disk is larger than its first Dirichlet eigenvalue. Thus, it is natural to test the magnetic eigenvalue inequality in Theorem
1.1
also on the disk.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the disk of unit radius centred at the origin. In view of the scaling properties
\lambda_{k}^{b}(t{\mathcal{B}})=t^{-2}\lambda_{k}^{bt^{2}}({\mathcal{B}})

and
\mu_{k}^{b}(t{\mathcal{B}})=t^{-2}\mu_{k}^{bt^{2}}({\mathcal{B}})

valid for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
t>0
(see
[
CLPS23
, p. 456]
), it suffices to test the eigenvalue inequality on the unit disk.
The eigenvalues of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\mathcal{B}}
are continuous functions in
b
(cf.
[
KL22
, Appendix B]
for the Neumann case, the Dirichlet case is analogous).
Hence, we know that
\mu_{4}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})>\lambda_{1}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})

for all sufficiently small
b>0
, because the same inequality holds for the usual Laplacian, which corresponds to
b=0
.
Thus, the index
k+1
in Theorem
1.1
can not be, in general, replaced by
k+3
. However, this observation does not exclude the possibility that the index
k+1
can be replaced by
k+2
.

It follows from
[
FFG+23
, Proposition 2.1]
that

\mu_{3}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})<b
if and only if
b>4
,
because the flux
\Phi:=\frac{b|{\mathcal{B}}|}{2\pi}=\frac{b}{2}>2
for
b>4
. On the other hand, according to
[
FH
, Lemma 1.4.1]
we have
\lambda_{1}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})\geq b
for all
b>0
. Hence, we get that
\mu_{3}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})<\lambda_{1}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
for all
b>4
.
In the remaining part of this appendix, we will analyse the case
b\in(0,4]
numerically. The numerics is rather simple and reduces to solving explicit transcendental equations in terms of generalized Laguerre functions.

The magnetic Neumann Laplacian
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{{\mathcal{B}}}
can be decomposed by
e.g.

[
KL22
, Section 3]
up to unitary equivalence as

(5.1)

{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{{\mathcal{B}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathsf{T}}_{b,n},

where the self-adjoint fiber operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{b,n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, acts in the Hilbert space
L^{2}((0,1);r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)
and is associated with the quadratic form

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{t}}_{b,n}[f]

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}r{\,\mathrm{d}}r+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{n}{r}-\frac{br}{2}\right)^{2}|f(r)|^{2}r{\,\mathrm{d}}r,

\displaystyle\qquad\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{b,n}

\displaystyle:=\big\{f\colon f,f^{\prime},nfr^{-1}\in L^{2}((0,1);r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)\big\}.

Let us denote by
\mu_{n,1}=\mu_{n,1}(b)
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the lowest eigenvalue of the fiber operator
{\mathsf{T}}_{b,n}
.
In view of the decomposition (
5.1
) for any
\lambda>0

(5.3)

\#\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon\mu_{k}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})\leq\lambda\}\geq\#\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon\mu_{n,1}(b)\leq\lambda\}.

Let us denote by
L^{a}_{\gamma}
the generalized Laguerre function (see,
e.g.
,
[
AS
, §22]
for precise definitions and properties).
By the analysis in
[
CLPS23
, Appendix B]
,

\mu_{n,1}
is the smallest positive solution of the equation

(5.4)

F_{n,b}(\mu):=\frac{{\,\mathrm{d}}}{{\,\mathrm{d}}r}\left(e^{-\frac{br^{2}}{4}}r^{n}L^{n}_{\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\mu}{b}-1\big)}\left(\frac{r^{2}b}{2}\right)\right)\bigg|_{r=1}=0,

and the associated eigenfunction is given by

v_{n}(r)=e^{-\frac{br^{2}}{4}}r^{n}L^{n}_{\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\mu_{n,1}}{b}-1\big)}\left(\frac{r^{2}b}{2}\right).

For the magnetic Dirichlet Laplacian on the disk
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm D}}^{\mathcal{B}}
we know according to
[
E96
, Proposition 2.1]
that
\lambda_{1}^{b}({\mathcal{B}})
is equal to the lowest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0,1}=\lambda_{0,1}(b)
of the one-dimensional self-adjoint fiber operator in
L^{2}((0,1);r{\,\mathrm{d}}r)

associated with the quadratic form

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{t}}_{b}[f]

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}r{\,\mathrm{d}}r+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{b^{2}r^{3}}{4}|f(r)|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathfrak{t}}_{b}

\displaystyle:=\big\{f\colon f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,1);r{\,\mathrm{d}}r),\,f(1)=0\big\}.

This is a direct consequence of the fact that, in the symmetric gauge of the homogeneous magnetic field, the ground state of the magnetic Laplacian on the disk is a radial function.
Mimicking the analysis in
[
CLPS23
, Appendix B]
we get that

\lambda_{0,1}
is the smallest positive solution of the equation

(5.5)

G_{b}(\lambda):=L^{0}_{\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\lambda}{b}-1\big)}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)=0.

\displaystyle=L^{0}_{1}(1)=0,

.

Appendix A
Lower semicontinuity of magnetic Neumann eigenvalues

In this appendix, we establish a lower semi-continuity result for the magnetic Neumann eigenvalues on varying convex domains satisfying an inclusion property. Although, we expect that under our assumptions the magnetic Neumann eigenvalues are in fact continuous with respect to the variation of the domain, lower semi-continuity turns out to be sufficient for our purposes. General stability results for the eigenvalues of
elliptic operators with Neumann-type boundary conditions under variations of the domain can be found
e.g.
in
[
BD02
,
BL07
]
, see also the references therein. We expect that the abstract results in
[
BL07
]
can be adjusted to the magnetic Neumann Laplacian, but we prefer to perform a more explicit analysis.

In the proof of a lower semi-continuity result the following lemma is useful.

Lemma A.1

(
[
MKh
, Lemma 4.9, Remark 4.2]
)
.

Let
U,V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, be bounded convex domains such that
U\subset V
. Then for any
u\in H^{1}(V)
the following inequality holds

\int_{V\setminus U}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\leq\frac{2|V\setminus U|}{|V|}\int_{V}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{C(d)({\rm diam}\,(V))^{d+1}|V\setminus U|^{1/d}}{|V|}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x,

where
{\rm diam}\,(V)>0
is the diameter of
V
and the constant
C(d)>0
depends only on the dimension.

Now, we are ready to formulate and prove the key result of this appendix.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
d=2,3
be a bounded convex domain. Let
\{\Omega_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of bounded convex domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\Omega_{n}\supset\Omega
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and that
|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|\rightarrow 0
. Then, for all
b>0
and any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\geq\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega).

Proof.

For the convenience of the reader, we split the argument into two steps. In the first step, we obtain estimates of the eigenfunctions of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega_{n}}
inside
\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega
. Then, in the second step we apply the min-max principle to
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega}
using
as trial functions the restrictions to
\Omega
of the eigenfunctions of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,{\rm N}}^{\Omega_{n}}
. This strategy is reminiscent of the one used by Levine and Weinberger in
[
LW86
, Lemma 4.6]
.

Step 1: estimates of the eigenfunctions in
\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega
.

Let us fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
u_{1,n},u_{2,n},\dots,u_{k,n}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{n})
be the orthonormal eigenfunctions of
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega_{n}}
corresponding to the
lowest
k
eigenvalues:
\mu_{1}^{b}(\Omega_{n}),\mu_{2}^{b}(\Omega_{n}),\dots,\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})
.
Note that the geometric assumptions on
\Omega_{n}
and
\Omega
yield

\rho:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm diam}\,(\Omega_{n})<\infty\qquad\alpha:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|<\infty.

Combining Lemma
A.1
and the diamagnetic inequality
[
LL
, Theorem 7.21]
we get for any
l\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega}|u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|}{|\Omega_{n}|}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{C(d)({\rm diam}\,(\Omega_{n}))^{d+1}|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|^{1/d}}{|\Omega_{n}|}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|\nabla|u_{l,n}||^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|}{|\Omega|}+\frac{C(d)\rho^{d+1}|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|^{1/d}}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left(2|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|+C(d)\rho^{d+1}|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|^{1/d}\mu_{l}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\right).

Applying the min-max principles (
2.5
) to
the operators
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm N}^{\Omega_{n}}
,
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega_{n}}
, and
{\mathsf{H}}_{b,\rm D}^{\Omega}
we get for
l\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
the following chain of inequalities

\mu_{l}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\leq\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\leq\lambda_{k}^{b}(\Omega),

where for the last inequality we employed
\Omega\subset\Omega_{n}
. Thus, there exists a constant
c>0
, which depends only on
\Omega
,
\alpha
,
\rho
,
b
, and
k
such that

\int_{\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega}|u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\leq c|\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega|^{1/d}

for all
l\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.
In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
a sufficiently large
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for
any
l\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
and all
n\geq N

(A.1)

\int_{\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega}|u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\leq\varepsilon.

Step 2: application of the min-max principle.
Consider the subspace

{\mathcal{L}}_{k,n}:={\rm span}\,\{u_{1,n}|_{\Omega},u_{2,n}|_{\Omega},\dots,u_{k,n}|_{\Omega}\}\subset H^{1}(\Omega).

For any
v=\sum_{l=1}^{k}c_{l}u_{l,n}|_{\Omega}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k,n}

with arbitrary
c_{l}\in{\mathbb{C}}
(
l=1,2,\dots,k
) we get

(A.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\int_{\Omega}u_{i,n}\overline{u_{j,n}}{\,\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{l=1}^{k}|c_{l}|^{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{l,n}|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x-\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{smallmatrix}}^{k}|c_{i}||c_{j}|\left|\int_{\Omega_{n}\setminus\Omega}u_{i,n}\overline{u_{j,n}}{\,\mathrm{d}}x\right|\geq\sum_{l=1}^{k}|c_{l}|^{2}(1-k\varepsilon).

Hence, for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
we get that
v\neq 0
provided that not all of the coefficients
\{c_{l}\}_{l=1}^{k}
are equal to zero. Hence, it holds that
\dim{\mathcal{L}}_{k,n}=k
for all sufficiently large
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

By (
2.5b
) we conclude that
there exist complex coefficients
\{c_{l}^{\prime}\}_{l=1}^{k}
such that for
v=\sum_{l=1}^{k}c_{l}^{\prime}u_{l,n}|_{\Omega}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

one has

\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})v|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x}.

Let us define the function
u=\sum_{l=1}^{k}c_{l}^{\prime}u_{l,n}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{n})
.
Choosing
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the estimate in (
A.1
) holds with
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{k}
for all
n\geq N
and applying the lower bound in (
A.2
) we end up with

\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{1-k\varepsilon}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}}|(\nabla-{\mathsf{i}}{\bf A})u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u|^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}}x}\leq\frac{\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})}{1-k\varepsilon}.

From this inequality we get

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega_{n})\geq\mu_{k}^{b}(\Omega)(1-k\varepsilon).

The claim follows from the fact that we can take
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small.
∎

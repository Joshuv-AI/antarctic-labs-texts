Twist Automorphisms and Poisson Structures
Yoshiyuki KIMURA a, Fan QIN b and Qiaoling WEI c

Abstract
We introduce (quantum) twist automorphisms for upper cluster algebras and cluster Poisson algebras with coefficients. Our constructions generalize the twist automorphisms for quantum unipotent cells. We study their existence and their compatibility with Poisson structures and quantization. The twist automorphisms always permute well-behaved bases for cluster algebras. We explicitly construct (quantum) twist automorphisms of Donaldson–Thomas type and for principal coefficients.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{@{}ccc}M^{\circ}(t_{0})&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t_{0}}^{M}}&M^{\circ}(t)\\
\uparrow p^{*}&&\uparrow p^{*}\\

More precisely,

is represented by the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&0\\
0&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)B(t_{0})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}B_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&-{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}\\
C&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and

Equivalently,

twist automorphisms are determined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t_{\Delta}}^{A}(A_{i}(t_{\Delta}))

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}A_{i}(t_{\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}_{L}\Delta}),\qquad\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t_{\Delta}}^{X}(X_{i}(t_{\Delta}))=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}X_{i}(t_{\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}_{L}\Delta}).

They give rise to automorphisms on
\mathcal{U}^{A}(t_{\Delta})
and
\mathcal{U}^{X}(t_{\Delta})

associated to the Dehn twist
\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}_{L}
.

7.3
Once-punctured digon

Example 7.2
.

The following cluster Poisson algebra arises from
{\rm PGL}_{3}
-local
systems on a once-punctured digon
[
49
]
.

Take
I=\{1,2,3,4\}
, with
I_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}=\{2,4\}
and
I_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}=\{1,3\}
.
Define the initial seed
t
such that its
B
-matrix is

B=\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&-1&\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}1\\
\hphantom{-}1&\hphantom{-}0&-1&\hphantom{-}0\\
\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}1&\hphantom{-}0&-1\\
-1&\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}1&\hphantom{-}0\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
B_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}=0
. So we cannot endow a compatible Poisson
structure
\lambda
for
{\mathcal{LP}}^{A}(t)
. We have
W=-B
.

Let
t^{\prime}
denote the seed
\boldsymbol{\mu}t
where
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mu_{2}\mu_{4}
.
Its
B
-matrix is
B^{\prime}=-B
. We have
W^{\prime}=-W
. We can check the following
mutation rule:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}X_{2}^{\prime}=X_{2}^{-1},\qquad\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}X_{4}^{\prime}=X_{4}^{-1},\qquad\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}X_{1}^{\prime}=X_{1}\cdot X_{2}\cdot(1+X_{2})^{-1}\cdot(1+X_{4}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}X_{3}^{\prime}=X_{3}\cdot X_{4}\cdot(1+X_{2})\cdot(1+X_{4})^{-1}.

Then
t^{\prime}=t[1]
with
\sigma={\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}
,
W^{\prime}=-W
.

Let us construct Poisson automorphisms on
\mathcal{U}^{X}(t)
. Note that
we have
W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}^{\rm T}\\
W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with row and column indices
(2,4,1,3)
, where
W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with row indices
\{1,3\}
and column indices
\{2,4\}
. We want
to construct a linear isomorphism
\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t}^{N}:=\colon N(t)\rightarrow N(t^{\prime})

represented by an
\mathbb{Z}
-valued invertible matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}\\
0&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}\\
0&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}\end{pmatrix}^{\rm T}W^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}\\
0&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=W.

The desired equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle-\begin{pmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}\\
0&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}\end{pmatrix}^{\rm T}\begin{pmatrix}0&-W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}^{\rm T}\\
W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\operatorname{\mathsf{Id}}}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}\\
0&V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&-W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}^{\rm T}\\
W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

By computing its block submatrices, we reduce the equation to the
equations

\displaystyle-V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}^{\rm T}W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}

\displaystyle=W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}},\qquad V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}^{\rm T}W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}^{\rm T}V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}-V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}^{\rm T}W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}=0.

For the first equation (for
\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t}^{N}
to be a variation map),
the solution takes the form
V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}=\smash{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda-1&\mu\\
\lambda&\mu-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
for any
\lambda
,
\mu
. Since we are looking for a bijection
\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t}^{N}

between lattices, we must have
\lambda+\mu=0,2
.

The second equation (for
\operatorname{\mathsf{var}}_{t}^{N}
to further be Poisson) is
equivalent to

-V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}^{\rm T}W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}^{\rm T}+W_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{f}},\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}}}}}V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}=0

by
the first equation. Then its solution takes the form
V_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\operatorname{\mathsf{uf}},\operatorname{\mathsf{f}}}}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\alpha&\beta\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for any
\alpha
,
\beta
.

The resulting twist automorphism on the fraction field takes the following
form:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}X_{2}=X_{2}^{-1},\qquad\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}X_{4}=X_{4}^{-1},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}X_{1}=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}((X^{\prime})^{(\lambda-1,\alpha,\lambda,\alpha)})=X^{(\lambda-1,\lambda-1-\alpha,\lambda,\lambda-\alpha)}(1+X_{2})(1+X_{4})^{-1},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}X_{3}=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{*}((X^{\prime})^{(\mu,\beta,\mu-1,\beta)})=X^{(\mu,\mu-\beta,\mu-1,\mu-1-\beta)}(1+X_{2})^{-1}(1+X_{4}).

Note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}(X_{1}X_{3})=(X_{1}X_{3})^{\lambda+\mu-1}(X_{2}X_{4})^{\lambda+\mu-1-\alpha-\beta}
.

Let us consider elements
E=X_{1}\cdot(1+X_{4})
,
F=X_{3}\cdot(1+X_{2})
,

K=X_{1}\cdot X_{3}\cdot X_{4}
and
K^{\prime}=X_{1}\cdot X_{2}\cdot X_{3}

(the Chevalley generators, see
[
49
]
). It is
straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}\big)^{*}E=X_{1}^{\prime}\cdot(1+X_{2}^{\prime}),\qquad\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}\big)^{*}F=X_{3}^{\prime}\cdot(1+X_{2}^{\prime}),\qquad\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}\big)^{*}K=X_{1}^{\prime}X_{2}^{\prime}X_{3}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\big(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-1}\big)^{*}K^{\prime}=X_{1}^{\prime}X_{3}^{\prime}X_{4}^{\prime},

so they are elements in the
cluster Poisson algebra
\mathcal{U}^{X}
.

We check that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}E=X^{(\lambda-1,\lambda-1-\alpha,\lambda,\lambda-\alpha)}(1+X_{2})(1+X_{4})^{-1}\big(1+X_{4}^{-1}\big)=(X_{1}X_{3})^{\lambda-1}(X_{2}X_{4})^{\lambda-1-\alpha}F,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}F=X^{(\mu,\mu-\beta,\mu-1,\mu-1-\beta)}(1+X_{4})(1+X_{2})^{-1}\big(1+X_{2}^{-1}\big)=(X_{1}X_{3})^{\mu-1}(X_{2}X_{4})^{\mu-1-\beta}E,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}K=(X_{1}X_{3})^{\lambda+\mu-1}(X_{2}X_{4})^{\lambda+\mu-1-\alpha-\beta}X_{4}^{-1},

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}K^{\prime}=(X_{1}X_{3})^{\lambda+\mu-1}(X_{2}X_{4})^{\lambda+\mu-1-\alpha\beta}X_{2}^{-1}.

For the special case
\lambda=\mu=1
,
\alpha=\beta=0
, we recover
the following automorphism in (Drinfeld double) quantum groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}E

\displaystyle=F,\qquad\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}F=E,\qquad\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}K=K^{\prime},\qquad\operatorname{\mathsf{tw}}K^{\prime}=K.

Eternal vertex cover number of maximal outerplanar graphs
Jasine Babu, K. Murali Krishnan, Veena Prabhakaran, Nandini J. Warrier

Abstract
Eternal vertex cover problem is a variant of the classical vertex cover problem modeled as a two player attacker-defender game. Computing eternal vertex cover number of graphs is known to be NP-hard in general and the complexity status of the problem for bipartite graphs is open. There is a quadratic complexity algorithm known for this problem for chordal graphs. Maximal outerplanar graphs forms a subclass of chordal graphs, for which no algorithm of sub-quadratic time complexity is known. In this paper, we obtain a recursive algorithm of linear time for computing eternal vertex cover number of maximal outerplanar graphs.



1
Introduction

A set
S
of vertices in a graph
G
forms a vertex cover of
G
if
every edge in
G
has at least one end point in
S
. The size of a minimum vertex cover in
G
, called the vertex cover number of
G
, will be denoted by
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
. The eternal vertex cover problem of graphs is motivated by the following dynamic network security / fault-tolerance model. A network can be modeled by a graph
G(V,E)
, where the nodes of the network are represented by the vertices of
G
and the links by the edges of
G
. The problem is to deploy a minimum set of guards at the nodes, so that if there is an attack (or fault) on a single link at any time, a guard is available at the end of the link, who can move across the link to defend (or repair) the attack (or fault). Simultaneously, the remaining guards need to reconfigure themselves, possibly by repositioning themselves to one of their adjacent nodes, so that any attack (or fault) on a single edge at any future instant of time can also be protected in the same manner. Thus, the model requires guaranteeing protection against single link attacks/failures ad-infinitum. It is immediate that lowest number of guards needed to achieve this goal in a graph
G
is at least
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
. If
k
guards are sufficient to achieve the goal, then we say that
G
is
k
-defendable
.

Formally, guards are initially placed on a vertex cover
C_{0}
of
G
, forming an initial configuration. Suppose at instant
i
, guards are placed in a configuration
C_{i}
and an attack occurs on an arbitrary edge
uv\in E(G)
, then a guard at either
u
or
v
(or both) must move across the edge. Other guards may or not move across one of their neighboring edges simultaneously. At the end of these movements, the next configuration
C_{i+1}
is reached. To ensure that all edges remain guarded at the instant
i+1
, we require
C_{i+1}
to be a vertex cover of
G
. The minimum number of guards necessary for a graph
G
to be protected against any infinite sequence of single edge attacks is called the eternal vertex cover number of
G
, denoted by
\operatorname{evc}(G)
. The eternal vertex cover problem has two models. The first one allows only at most one guard on a vertex in any configuration, while in the second model this constraint is absent. The results in this paper work in both the models.

Computing
\operatorname{evc}(G)
for an arbitrary graph
G
is NP-hard, though in PSPACE
[
1
]
, and is fixed parameter tractable with
\operatorname{evc}(G)
as parameter
[
1
]
. The problem appears significantly harder than
\operatorname{mvc}
computation. For instance,
\operatorname{mvc}
computation is polynomial time for bipartite graphs, but the complexity status of the problem is open for bipartite graphs
[
1
]
.
Consequent to the hardness results, work on solving the problem on various graph classes have been attempted in the literature. It is known that the problem is NP-complete even for biconnected internally triangulated planar graphs
[
2
]
. Polynomial time algorithms for computing
\operatorname{evc}(G)
were known exactly only for very elementary graph classes such as an
O(n)
algorithm for trees
[
3
]
, a polynomial time algorithm for a tree-like graph class
[
4
]
and a linear time algorithm for cactus graphs
[
5
]
.
A recent structure theorem developed in
[
6
]
has resulted in a quadratic time algorithm for chordal graphs. Similar graph protection problems defined on parameters like dominating sets
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
and independent sets
[
13
,
14
]
are also studied in literature.

An outerplanar graph is a planar graph that admits a planar embedding with all its vertices lying on the exterior face and it is maximal outerplanar if addition of any more edges between existing vertices will make the graph not outerplanar. Since maximal outerplanar graphs are chordal, it follows from
[
6
]
that its
\operatorname{evc}
number can be computed in quadratic time.
We improve the complexity and show that the
\operatorname{evc}
number of maximal outerplanar graphs can be computed in linear time.

Our algorithm takes a maximal outerplanar graph
G
on at least three vertices and an edge
uv
on the outer face of
G
and recursively computes
\operatorname{evc}(G)
,
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
along with some related parameters. If both
u
and
v
are of degree greater than two, then the recursion is applied on the two induced subgraphs
G_{u}
and
G_{v}
of
G
as shown in Fig.
1
. Otherwise, the recursion works on the graph obtained by deleting the degree two end point of the edge
uv
from
G
. Since
\operatorname{evc}(G)
happens to be not merely a function of the eternal vertex cover number and vertex cover number of these associated subgraphs, the algorithm has to recursively compute this larger set of parameters. The linear time complexity of the algorithm is derived from Theorems
3.1
to
5.2
, which assert that
\operatorname{evc}(G)
can be determined in constant time from this larger set of parameters of the associated subgraphs. Apart from the algorithmic result, these theorems serve to demonstrate the structural connection between the
MVC
problem and the
EVC
problem for maximal outerplanar graphs.

2
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a graph with
S\subseteq V
. The parameter
\operatorname{evc}_{S}(G)
denotes the minimum number
k

G
is
k
-defendable with all vertices of

S

\operatorname{mvc}_{S}(G)

the size of the smallest cardinality vertex cover of

containing all vertices of

. When

, we shorten the notation
\operatorname{evc}_{\{v_{1}\cdots v_{i}\}}(G)

and

Proposition
1
is an easy adaptation of a result from known literature.

Proposition 1

[
2
]

Let
G(V,E)
be a maximal outerplanar graph with at least two vertices and
S\subseteq V
.
If for every vertex
v\in V\setminus S
,

\operatorname{mvc}_{{}_{S\cup\{v\}}}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{{}_{S}}(G)
, then
\operatorname{evc}_{{}_{S}}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{{}_{S}}(G)
. Otherwise,

\operatorname{evc}_{{}_{S}}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{{}_{S}}(G)+1
. Hence,
\operatorname{evc}_{{}_{S}}(G)=\max_{v\in V(G)}\operatorname{mvc}_{{}_{S\cup\{v\}}}(G)
.

Proposition 2

Let
G
be a maximal outerplanar graph with an edge
uv
. Then,

\operatorname{evc}_{v}(G)\leq\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)\leq\operatorname{evc}(G)+1
.

Proof

Let
\operatorname{evc}(G)=k
. The first part of the inequality is
obvious. So, we prove the second part. By
Proposition
1
, for
each vertex
x\in V(G)
,
\operatorname{mvc}_{x}(G)\leq k
.
Since
uv\in E(G)
, this implies that
\operatorname{mvc}_{xu}(G)\leq k
or

\operatorname{mvc}_{xv}(G)\leq k
. Therefore, for any vertex

x\in V(G)
, we have
\operatorname{mvc}_{xuv}(G)\leq k+1
. Hence by
Proposition
1
,
\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)\leq k+1=\operatorname{evc}(G)+1
.∎

. Consider the edge

(2)

3
Computation of the mvc parameters

Throughout this section, we assume that
G
is a maximal outerplanar graph of at least four vertices,
uv
is an edge on the outer face of
G
and
w=\Delta(uv)
. The results in this section show that the mvc parameters of
G
with respect to the edge
uv
- viz.,
\mathscr{M}(G,uv)
, can be computed in constant time if the mvc parameters of
G_{u}
with respect to
uw
- viz.,
\mathscr{M}(G_{u},uw)
and the mvc parameters of
G_{v}
with respect to
vw
- viz.,
\mathscr{M}(G_{v},vw)
are given.

The following observation is easy to see.

Observation 2

If
deg_{G}(u)=2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
, then,
(1)
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})
(2)
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})+1
, (3)
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)
and (4)
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v})+1
.

The following theorem is a consequence of Observation
2
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
G
be a maximal outerplanar graph and
uv
be an edge on the outer face of
G
such that
deg_{G}(u)=2
.
Let
w=\Delta(uv)
.

1.

Given
\mathscr{M}(G_{v},vw)
, it is possible to compute
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
in constant time.

2.

Given
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
and
\mathscr{M}(G_{v},vw)
, it is possible to compute the remaining mvc parameters of
G
with respect to
uv
in constant time.

Now, we look at the computation of
\mathscr{M}(G,uv)
when
deg_{G}(u)>2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
.

The following observation is easy to obtain.

Observation 3

If
deg_{G}(u)>2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
, then

\operatorname{mvc}(G)\in\{\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})\}
.

The next lemma gives a method to compute
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
using
\mathscr{M}(G_{u},uw)
and
\mathscr{M}(G_{v},vw)
.

Lemma 1

If
deg_{G}(u)>2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
, then

\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})\}
.

Proof

It can be easily seen that

\operatorname{mvc}(G)\leq\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})\}
. By Observation
3
, we have the following two cases to consider.

If
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1
, then both
G_{u}
and
G_{v}
have a minimum vertex cover with
w
. Further, either
\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})
or
\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})
. Hence, it is clear that

\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})-1\}
. From this, the result follows.

If
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})
, then by the upper bound shown in the beginning of this proof,
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})=\operatorname{mvc}(G)\leq\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})+mvc_{vw}(G_{v})-1,\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})-1\}
. From this, the result follows.∎

The next lemma shows that given
\operatorname{mvc}(G)
,
\mathscr{M}(G_{u},uw)
and
\mathscr{M}(G_{v},vw)
, the values of
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)
,
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)
and
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)
are computable in constant time.

Lemma 2

Let
deg_{G}(u)>2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
. If
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1
, then

\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1
,

\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1
and

\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1
.

Proof

Let
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})=k_{u}
and
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})=k_{v}
.
Since
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=k_{u}+k_{v}-1
, both
G_{u}
and
G_{v}
have a minimum vertex cover with
w
. Let
k=\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)
. Since
\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})=k_{v}
, we can observe that
G
has a vertex cover
C
of size
k
such that
u,w\in C
. Now, it can be easily seen that
C
is the union of a vertex cover of
G_{u}
containing
both
u
and
w
and a minimum vertex cover of
G_{v}
with
w
. Therefore,

\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})-1
. Similarly, it is easy to see that
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1
and

\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}_{uw}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})-1
.
∎

Lemma 3

Let
deg_{G}(u)>2
and
deg_{G}(v)>2
. If
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})
, then

\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
,

\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
and

\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
.

Proof

Let
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})=k_{u}
and
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})=k_{v}
.

First we prove that
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
. It can be easily seen that

\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)\leq\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)\\
+1\}
.
Now, we need to show that

\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)\geq\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v}),\\
\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
.
We know that
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)\in\{\operatorname{mvc}(G),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
. If
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1
, then the proof is immediate, because
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)
is equal to
one of the three quantities listed and so it is greater than or equal to their minimum.
Otherwise, we have
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})
. In this case, it is enough to show that among the three parameters,
the first or the second will be equal to
\operatorname{mvc}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})
. For contradiction, suppose this was not the case. Then,
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})=k_{u}+1
and
\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})=k_{v}+1
.
Consider any minimum vertex cover
S
of
G
such that
u\in S
. If
w\in S
, then
|S|\geq\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{v})-1\geq k_{u}+k_{v}+1
, a contradiction.
If
w\notin S
, again
|S|\geq\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})\geq k_{u}+k_{v}+1
, a contradiction. Hence, we are done.
The proof for
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G)
is symmetric to the previous case.

Now, we show that
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
. It is easy to see that

\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)\leq\min\{\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v}),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
.
We know that
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)\in\{\operatorname{mvc}(G),\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1\}
.
Suppose
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)=k_{u}+k_{v}
. In this case, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})=k_{u}
and
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v})=k_{v}
. For contradiction,
assume
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})=k_{u}+1
. Then,
\operatorname{mvc}_{w}(G_{u})=k_{u}
. Since,
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=k_{u}+k_{v}
,
\operatorname{mvc}_{vw}(G_{v})=k_{v}
. Then,
\operatorname{mvc}(G)=k_{u}+k_{v}-1
,
which is a contradiction. Hence,
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})\leq k_{u}
. Similarly,
\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v})\leq k_{v}
as required.
Now, suppose
\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1=k_{u}+k_{v}+1
. In this case, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G_{u})+\operatorname{mvc}_{v}(G_{v})\geq\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G)+1
,
which is straightforward to obtain. ∎

4
Bounds on the evc parameters

In this section, we will derive some bounds on the evc parameters of a maximal outerplanar graph. These bounds will be used in the next section for the recursive computation of the evc parameters. Throughout this section, we consider
G
to be a maximal outerplanar graph on at least four vertices,
uv
an edge on its outer face and
w=\Delta(uv)
.

First, we derive some bounds for
\mathscr{E}(G,uv)
when

.

Lemma 4

If
u
is a degree-2 vertex in
G
, then:

1.

\operatorname{evc}(G)\leq\operatorname{evc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1

2.

\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{evc}_{v}(G_{v})+1

3.

\operatorname{evc}(G)\geq max\{\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})+1,\operatorname{evc}_{vw}(G_{v})\}

4.

\operatorname{evc}_{v}(G)=\text{max}\{\operatorname{mvc}_{uv}(G),\operatorname{evc}(G)\}

Proof

Let
\operatorname{evc}(G)=k
.

1.

First inequality is straightforward. So, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{evc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1
. From
Proposition
1
,
\max_{x\in V(G_{v})}\operatorname{mvc}_{x}(G_{v})=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})
. Hence,
\max_{x\in V(G_{v})}\operatorname{mvc}_{xu}(G)=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1
. Hence, by
Proposition
1
,
we get
\operatorname{evc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1
.

2.

Similar to the previous case, by Proposition
1
,
\max_{x\in V(G_{v})}\operatorname{mvc}_{x}(G_{v})=\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})
. Hence,
\max_{x\in V(G_{v})}\operatorname{mvc}_{xuv}(G)\leq\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1
. Hence, by
Proposition
1
,
we get
\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)\leq\operatorname{evc}(G_{v})+1
. By Proposition
2
and Part 1 of this lemma,
\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)\geq\operatorname{evc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{evc}_{v}(G_{v})+1
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{evc}_{uv}(G)=\operatorname{evc}_{v}(G_{v})+1
.

3.

By Observation
2
, we get
\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)=\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})+1
. By Proposition
1
, since
\operatorname{evc}(G)\geq\operatorname{mvc}_{u}(G)
,
we have
\operatorname{evc}(G)\geq\operatorname{mvc}(G_{v})+1
.

By Proposition
1
, for any vertex
x\in V(G)
,
\operatorname{mvc}_{x}(G)\leq k
. Further, any vertex cover of
G
contain at least two vertices among
u
,
v
and
w
. Let
x
be an arbitrary vertex in
G_{v}
and
C
be a vertex cover of
G
of size
k
containing
x
. If
C
contains both
v
and
w
, the vertex cover obtained by restricting
C
to

(in the appendix) gives a linear time refinement of Algorithm
1
. The details of the correspondence between the two algorithms are given in the appendix.

7
Conclusion

This paper presents a linear time algorithm for computing the eternal vertex cover number of maximal outerplanar graphs,
lowering the best known upper bound to the complexity of the problem from quadratic
[
6
]
time to linear. The techniques
presented in the paper make crucial use of the planarity of the underlying graph to yield a divide and conquer algorithm for
computing the
\operatorname{evc}
number of a maximal outerplanar graph. Attempts to generalize
the techniques to maximal planar graphs may not be successful due to the known NP hardness result on the evc computation of biconnected internally triangulated planar graphs
[
2
]
. However,
the complexity status of the problem of computing the
\operatorname{evc}
number of outerplanar graphs is open and may be attempted using the techniques developed in this work.

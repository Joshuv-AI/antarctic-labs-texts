On the Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system: Ground state & least energy nodal solutions with nonsmooth nonlinearity
Anouar Bahrouni, Hlel Missaoui

Abstract
In this paper, the following Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system is studied { − △ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u + q 2 ​ ϕ ​ u = f ⁡ ( x , u ) , in ​ ℝ 3 , − △ ​ ϕ + a 2 ​ △ 2 ​ ϕ = 4 ​ π ​ u 2 , in ​ ℝ 3 , \left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\triangle u+V(x)u+q^{2}\phi u&=f(x,u),&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\ &&\\ -\triangle\phi+a^{2}\triangle^{2}\phi&=4\pi u^{2},&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right. where a > 0 a>0 and q ≠ 0 q\neq 0 . Under suitable assumptions on f f and V V , by using minimizations arguments and generalized subdifferential, the existence of a ground state with a fixed sign and a least energy nodal solutions for this system are obtained. Moreover, we prove that the energy of the nodal solution is twice as large as that of the ground state solution.



Keywords:
Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky System, Ground state solution, Nodal solutions, Nehari method.

Introduction

This paper was motivated by some works that have appeared in recent years concerning the nonlinear Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system of the
type

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\triangle u+V(x)u+q^{2}\phi u&=f(x,u),&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&&\\
-\triangle\phi+a^{2}\triangle^{2}\phi&=4\pi u^{2},&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\end{array}\right.

(
\mathcal{BPS}
)

where
u,\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
is the Bopp-Podolsky parameter,
q\neq 0
,
V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the potential function
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a carathéodory function.

To the best of our knowledge, this new kind of elliptic system (

⁢
B
P
S

) was introduced recently for the first time
in
[
2
]
by P. d’Avenia and G. Siciliano, although the problem was known among physicists. The system (

⁢
B
P
S

) was named Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system because it appears when we couple a Schrödinger field
\psi(x,t)
with
its electromagnetic field in the Bopp-Podolsky electromagnetic theory. The
Bopp-Podolsky theory, see
[
5
,
22
]
, is a second-order gauge theory for the electromagnetic field. It was introduced to solve the so-called ”infinity problem” that appears in the classical Maxwell theory. In fact, by the well-known Poisson equation (or Gauss law), the electrostatic potential
\phi
for a given charge distribution whose density is
\rho
satisfies the equation

-\triangle\phi=\rho,\ \ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.1)

If
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
, (
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
), then
\mathcal{G}(x-x_{0})
, with
\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}(x):=\frac{1}{|x|}}
, is the fundamental solution
of (
1.1
) and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}_{M}}(\mathcal{G}):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{G}|^{2}dx=+\infty

its electrostatic energy. Thus, in the Bopp-Podolsky theory, the equation (
1.1
) is replaced by

-\triangle\phi+a^{2}\triangle^{2}\phi=\rho,\ \ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Therefore, In this case, if
\rho=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}}
, (
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
), we are able to know explicitly the solution of the equation (
1.2
)
and to see that its energy is finite or not. Fortunately, in
[
2
]
P. d’Avenia and G. Siciliano proved that

\mathcal{K}(x-x_{0})
with,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}(x):=\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x|}{a}}}{|x|}}
, is the fundamental solution of the equation

-\triangle\phi+a^{2}\triangle^{2}\phi=4\pi\delta_{x_{0}},\ \ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

The solution of the previous equation has no singularity in
x_{0}
since it satisfies

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\mathcal{K}(x-x_{0})=\frac{1}{a},

and its energy is

\mathcal{E}_{BP}(\mathcal{K}):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\mathcal{K}|^{2}dx+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\triangle\mathcal{K}|^{2}dx<+\infty.

For more details about this subject see
[
2
, Section 2]
.

An important fact involving system (

⁢
B
P
S

) is that this class of system can be transformed into a
Schrödinger equation with a nonlocal term (see
[
2
]
), which allows us to use variational
methods. Effectively, in
[
2

, P. d’Avenia and G. Siciliano, by the Lax-Milgram Theorem, proved that for a given
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

-\triangle\phi_{u}+a^{2}\triangle^{2}\phi_{u}=4\pi u^{2},\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},

\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{D}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\psi\nabla\varphi+a^{2}\triangle\psi\triangle\varphi dx.

Otherwise,

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \triangle\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\},

with

\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\left\{\phi\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \nabla\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\}.

The space
\mathcal{D}
is an Hilbert space continuously embedded into
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Moreover, they proved that the unique solution
\displaystyle{\phi_{u}:=\mathcal{K}*u^{2}=\frac{1-e^{-\frac{|x|}{a}}}{|x|}*u^{2}}

verifies the following properties

For any

(4)

for every

\mathcal{P}
)

The paper
[
2

seems to be the first paper that Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system is studied in the mathematical

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\triangle u+wu+q^{2}\phi u&=|u|^{p-2}u,&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\\

Again the solutions converge to the solution of the limit case.
Moreover, by using a Pohozaev-type identity, they proved that the

.
However, the authors do not cover critical cases. In
[
7

, Chen
and Tang studied the existence of solutions of the critical system
(

⁢
B
P
S

). By using some new analytic techniques and new

Nehari type in constant potential case. In all those papers, the

related to nodal ( or sign-changing ) solutions, we found a few
papers, see
[
15
,
24
,
26
]
. In
[
15
]
, the authors proved the
existence of least-energy sign-changing solutions for the critical
Schrödinger-Bopp-Podolsky system. In
[

, by using a

⁢

) with superlinear nonlinearity. Moreover, the asymptotic

[
26

, Q. Zhang used some stronger conditions on the

Motivated by the above papers, in this work we are interested in finding ground state and least-energy nodal solutions for the problem (

⁢
B
P
S

), or rather for the

P

. More precisely, we assume the following assumption on the potential function
V(\cdot)
:

(V_{0})

V(x)\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}_{+})
is coercive.

For what concerns the nonlinearity reaction term

is a measurable, locally Lipschitz in the second variable

(f_{1})

\displaystyle{\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(x,t)}{t}=0}

(f_{2})

\displaystyle{\lim\limits_{|t|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{5}}=0}

\displaystyle{F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)ds}

, a.a.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

subdifferential” of

Once that we will apply variational
methods on the problem (

P

\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx}

There exists

x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and all

Therefore, our assumption

Next, we state our main results for problem (

Suppose that the hypotheses

(f_{1})-(f_{4})

As a consequence of Theorems

In this Section, we give the variational setting and the corresponding energy functional associated with problem (

First, denote by

Under the assumption

H_{V}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)u^{2}dx<+\infty\right\},

\|u\|:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)u^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \text{for all}\ u\in H_{V}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Theorem 2.1

(see

4

.

Under the assumption

For each

Lemma 2.2

Assume that assumptions

(1)

\displaystyle{\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,u_{n})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,u)dx}.

(2)

\displaystyle{\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,u_{n}^{\pm})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,u^{\pm})dx}.

(3)

Next, we would like to mention that, from assumptions
(f_{1})-(f_{2})
and Lemma

, the existence of solutions for problem (

the corresponding energy functional to problem (

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)u^{2}dx\right)+\frac{q^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi_{u}u^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,u)dx

Since we are looking for least energy nodal solutions (or sign-changing solutions), our second goal is to prove the

A main tool used in the present paper is the subdifferential theory of Clark
[
8
,
9
]
for locally Lipschitz functionals. Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space,
\mathbb{X}^{*}
its topological dual, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}
denote the duality brackets for the pair

Definition 2.3
.

Let the functional
\Psi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\Psi
is locally Lipschitz if, for every
x\in\mathbb{X}
, there exists an open neighborhood

of

|\Psi(u)-\Psi(v)|\leq k_{x}\|u-v\|_{\mathbb{X}}\ \ \text{for all}\ u,v\in U(x).

Definition 2.4
.

Given a locally Lipschitz function
\Psi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the ”generalized directional derivative” of
\Psi
at
u\in\mathbb{X}
in the direction
v\in\mathbb{X}
, denoted by
\tilde{\Psi}(u;v)
, is defined by

\tilde{\Psi}(u;v)=\mathop{\limsup_{x\rightarrow u}}_{t\searrow 0}\frac{\Psi(x+tv)-\Psi(x)}{t}.

Definition 2.5
.

The ”generalized subdifferential” of
\Psi
at
u\in\mathbb{X}
is the set
\partial\Psi(u)\subseteq\mathbb{X}^{*}
given by

\partial\Psi(u):=\left\{\Psi^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\langle\Psi^{*},v\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\tilde{\Psi}(u;v),\ \ \text{for all}\ v\in\mathbb{X}\right\}.

The Hahn-Banach theorem implies that
\partial\Psi(u)\neq\emptyset
for all
u\in\mathbb{X}
, it is convex and
w^{*}
-compact ( in weak topology sense ). If
\Psi
is also convex, then it coincides with the subdifferential in the sense of convex functionals, see
[
11
]
. If
\Psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{R})
, then
\partial\Psi(u)=\left\{\Psi^{{}^{\prime}}(u)\right\}
. Note that the generalized subdifferential has a remarkable calculus, similar to that in the classical derivative, see
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
.

3
Ground state solution

In this section, we prove the existence of a weak solution of (

P

) which minimizes
J_{\left|{}_{\mathcal{N}}\right.}

a ”ground state solution”.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the assumptions
(f_{1})-(f_{4})
and
(V_{0})
hold. Then, for each
u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}

t_{u}>0
such that

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}
, we define the fibering map
h_{u}:(0,+\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

h_{u}(t):=J(tu),\ \ \text{for all}\ t>0.

(3.1)

From Lemma
1.1
, for all
t\in(0,+\infty)

\displaystyle h_{u}(t)

Lemma 4.3
.

(V_{0})

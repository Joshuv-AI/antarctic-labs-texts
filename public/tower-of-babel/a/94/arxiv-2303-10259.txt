Equivariant orientation of vector bundles over disconnected base spaces
Prasit Bhattacharya, Foling Zou

Abstract
. In this paper, we view the equivariant orientation theory of equiva riant vector bundles from the lenses of equivariant Picard spectra. This viewpoint allows us to identify, for a finite group G \mathrm{G} , a precise condition under which an R \mathrm{R} -orientation of a G \mathrm{G} -equivariant vector bundle is encoded by a Thom class. Consequently, we are able to construct a generalization of the first Stiefel–Whitney class of a “homogeneous” G \mathrm{G} -equivariant bundle with respect to an 𝔼 ∞ G \mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{G}} -ring spectrum R \mathrm{R} . As an application, we show that the 2 2 -fold direct sum of any homogeneous bundle is H ​ 𝒜 ¯ G \mathrm{H}\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}} -orientable, where 𝒜 ¯ G \underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}} is the Burnside Mackey functor. We notice that H ​ 𝒜 ¯ G \mathrm{H}\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}} -orientability is equivalent to H ​ ℤ ¯ \mathrm{H}\underline{\mathbb{Z}} -orientability when the order of G \mathrm{G} is odd. When the order of G \mathrm{G} is even, we show that a G \mathrm{G} -equivariant analog of the tautological line bundle over ℝ ​ ℙ ∞ \mathbb{RP}^{\infty} is H ​ ℤ ¯ \mathrm{H}\underline{\mathbb{Z}} -orientable but not H ​ 𝒜 ¯ G \mathrm{H}\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}} -orientable.

. Introduction

In differential topology, an orientation of a vector bundle is an
orientation of each fiber which is compatible with the topology of the base space.
In homotopy theory, an orientation is expressed as a Thom class in the ordinary cohomology with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients of its Thom space. The concept of Thom class
extends to extraordinary cohomology theories:
For any ring spectrum
\mathrm{R}

\mathrm{R}
-Thom class of a vector bundle
\upxi

in the
\mathrm{R}
-cohomology of its Thom space

{\sf u}_{\upxi}\in[\mathrm{Th}(\upxi),\Sigma^{\dim\upxi}\mathrm{R}]=\mathrm{R}^{\dim\upxi}(\mathrm{Th}(\upxi))

such that its restriction to the Thom space over any point is a unit in the homotopy groups of
\mathrm{R}
. This definition led to many interesting ideas and results, which are now foundational in homotopy theory and its application to geometry.

Equivariant orientation theory was systematically developed in the eighties and nineties, geometrically in
[

One of the challenges of these developments was that many important results,

\mathsection

were best understood for
\mathrm{G}
-connected spaces. This is because the equivariant dimension of a vector bundle is not well defined unless the base space is

In this paper, we propose a different approach to equivariant orientation theory, where the existence of an orientation of an equivariant bundle is determined through a two-step obstruction theory. As a consequence, we are able to make explicit calculations to check whether a given equivariant bundle admits an orientation or not, even when its base space is equivariantly disconnected.

Appendix A
Units of Burnside Tambara functors

In anticipation of future applications of
1.22
, we record the Mackey functor structure of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}
for a few familiar abelian
2
-groups.
The starting point of these calculations is a fundamental result due to Matsuda
[
Mat82
]
which identifies
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}(\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}):=\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\times}
, where
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{H}}
is the Burnside ring of the group
\mathrm{H}
.

Theorem A.1

(Matsuda)
.

For a finite group
\mathrm{G}
, the units
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{m+1}
, where
m
is the cardinality of the set

\mathcal{I}=\{\mathrm{H}:\text{$\mathrm{H}\subset\mathrm{G}$ has index $2$}\}/(\text{conjugation}).

The elements
[\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}]-1
for
\mathrm{H}\in\mathcal{I}
, along with
-1
, form a basis for
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}
.

While the restriction maps of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}
is straightforward to determine, the transfer maps are deduced from the formula of norm map
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{G}}:\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H})\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{G})
given by

(A.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{X})=\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{G},\mathrm{X}),}

where
\mathrm{X}
is a finite
\mathrm{H}
-set (see
[
Maz13
, Example 1.4.6]
as well as
[
Nak14
, Appendix A]
). The Mackey functor structure of
\underline{\cal{A}}_{\mathrm{G}}^{\times}
can be complicated depending on the structure of
\mathrm{G}
and
has been the subject of research for several decades
[
Mat82
,
MM83
,
Yos90
,
Yn05
,
Bou07
,
Bar16
,
Bou21
,
VE23
]
.

When
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}
, then the subgroups of
\mathrm{G}
are cyclic of order
2^{k}
, in fact, there is exactly one for each
0\leq k\leq n
. We will denote this subgroup by
\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}
. From
A.1
, we get

\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}}^{\times}(\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\{{\sf a}_{k},{\sf b}_{k}\},

where
{\sf a}_{k}=-1
and
{\sf b}_{k}=[\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-1}}]-1
.

Proposition A.3
.

The restriction and the transfer map in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}}^{\times}(\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}}^{\times}(\mathrm{C}_{2^{n}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-1}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf res}_{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf tr}_{k}}

satisfies

{\sf tr}_{k}({\sf a}_{k-1})={\sf tr}_{k}({\sf b}_{k-1})={\sf b}_{k},\ {\sf res}_{k}({\sf a}_{k})={\sf a}_{k-1},\ {\sf res}_{k}({\sf b}_{k})=0.

Proof
.

The map
{\sf res}_{k}
is immediate from restricting the actions. The transfer is obtained by calculating the norm in the Burnside Tambara functor
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{G}
using the formula (
A.2
).
When
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-i}}
and
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}
in (
A.2
), we get

\begin{array}[]{clll}\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-i}}}^{\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}}(l)&=&l+\frac{l^{2}-l}{2}[\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-1}}]+\frac{l^{4}-l^{2}}{4}[\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-2}}]+\dots\\
&&\hskip 150.0pt\cdots+\frac{l^{2^{k-i}}-l^{2^{k-i-1}}}{2^{i}}[\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}/\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-i}}].\end{array}

By setting
i=1
and
l=-1
in the above equation, we get
{\sf tr}_{k}({\sf a}_{k-1})={\sf b}_{k}
, and by setting
i=k
and
l=-1
, we get
{\sf tr}_{e}^{\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}}({\sf a}_{0})={\sf b}_{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq n
.
Thus,

\displaystyle{\sf tr}_{k}({\sf b}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf tr}_{k}({\sf tr}_{e}^{\mathrm{C}_{2^{k-1}}}({\sf a}_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf tr}_{e}^{\mathrm{C}_{2^{k}}}({\sf a}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf b}_{k}

as desired.
∎

Abstract
. In this work, we establish a connection between the cumulative residual entropy and the Gini mean difference (GMD). Some relationships between the extropy and the GMD, and the truncated GMD and dynamic versions of the cumulative extropy are also established. We then show that several entropy and extropy measures discussed here can be brought into the framework of probability weighted moments, which would enable us to find estimators of these measures. Keywords: Cumulative entropy; Cumulative residual entropy; Extropy; Gini mean difference; Probability weighted moments; Weighted cumulative residual entropy.



1.
Introduction

The Gini mean difference (GMD) is a prominent measure that is used extensively in economics.
Let
X
be a non-negative random variable with absolutely continuous distribution function
F
and finite mean
\mu
, and
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be two independent random variables from
F
. Then, the GMD is defined as

GMD=E|X_{1}-X_{2}|.

For more details on GMD and other measures derived from it, interested readers may refer to Yitzhaki and Schechtman (2013).

The truncated version of the GMD has also been discussed for studying inequality prevailing in the poor and affluent groups, while the left truncated version has found application in reliability analysis. For more details, see Nair et al. (2012) and Behdani et al. (2020). Recently, Nair and Vineshkumar (2022) discussed some relationships between the cumulative entropy and income gap ratio, Lorenz curve, Gini index, Bonferroni curve and Zenga Curve.

In many practical situations, measuring the uncertainty associated with a random variable is quite important, and many measures have been introduced for this purpose. The seminal work on information theory started with the concept of Shannon entropy or differential entropy introduced by Shannon (1948). Since then, different measures of entropy have been discussed, each one being suitable for some specific situations. A few of the widely used measures of entropy are cumulative residual entropy (Rao et al., 2004), cumulative entropy (Di Crescenzo and Longobardi, 2009), the corresponding weighed measures by Mirali et al. (2016) and Mirali and Baratpour (2017), and a general measure of cumulative residual entropy (Sudheesh et al., 2022).

One important generalization of Shannon entropy is due to Tsallis (1988), known as generalized Tsallis entropy of order
\alpha
.
Many extensions and modifications are available for it as well. For example, Rajesh and Sunoj (2019) proposed cumulative residual Tsallis entropy of order
\alpha
, while Chakraborty and Pradhan (2021a) defined weighted cumulative residual Tsallis entropy (WCRTE) of order
\alpha
and its dynamic version.
Calì et al. (2017) introduced cumulative Tsallis past entropy of order
\alpha
. Chakraborty and Pradhan (2021b) introduced weighted cumulative Tsallis entropy (WCTE) of order
\alpha
, and also studied its dynamic version.

An alternative measure of uncertainty, called extropy, was introduced by Lad et al. (2015) as a complementary dual of entropy.
Jahanshahi et al. (2020) and Tahmasebi and Toomaj (2022) studied cumulative residual extropy and negative cumulative extropy, while Balakrishnan et al. (2022) and Chakraborty and Pradhan (2022) discussed different weighted versions of extropy. Sudheesh and Sreedevi (2022) established some relationships between different extropy measures and reliability concepts, and Sudheesh et al. (2022) established some relationships between entropy and extropy measures.

The probability weighted moments (PWM) generalize the concept of moments of a probability distribution, and they have been used effectively for estimating the parameters of different distributions. The PWM was introduced by Greenwood et al. (1979). Here, we show that may extropy measures mentioned above can be expressed in terms of PWM.

The rest of this article is organised as follows. In Section 2, using a generalized cumulative residual entropy, we derive connections between some entropy measures and GMD. In Section 3, we establish some relationships between GMD and extropy measures. In Section 4, we show that several extropy measures can be brought into the framework of PWM. Finally, we make some concluding remarks in Section 5.

2.
Connection between entropy and GMD

Let
X
be a non-negative random variable with absolute continuous distribution function
F
and let
\bar{F}(x)=P(X>x)
denote the survival function. Let us further assume that the mean
\mu=E(X)<\infty
.

2.1.
Cumulative residual entropy and GMD

We now consider the generalized cumulative residual entropy measure introduced recently by Sudheesh et al. (2022).

Definition 1
.

Let
X
be a non-negative random variable with absolute continuous distribution function
F
. Further, let
\phi(.)
be a function of
X
and
w(.)
be a weight function. Then, the generalized cumulative residual entropy is defined as

(1)

\mathcal{GE}(X)=\int w(u)E\Big[\phi(X)-\phi(u)|X>u\Big]dF(u).

Sudheesh et al. (2022) then showed that several measures of entropy available in the literature can be deduced from (
1
) with different choices of
w(\cdot)
and
\phi(\cdot)
.
We now show that GMD is a special case of
\mathcal{GE}(X)
.

Theorem 1
.

For the choice of
w(u)=\bar{F}(u)
and
\phi(x)=2x
,
\mathcal{GE}(X)=GMD
.

Proof:

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be independent and identical random variables having the distribution function
F
. With
GMD=E|X_{1}-X_{2}|
, and because
|X_{1}-X_{2}|=\max(X_{1},X_{2})-\min(X_{1},X_{2})
, the GMD can alternatively be expressed as

(2)

GMD=E\left(\max(X_{1},X_{2})-\min(X_{1},X_{2})\right).

Now, for the choice of
w(u)=\bar{F}(u)
and
\phi(x)=2x
, the expression in (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{GE}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}(u)E(X-u|X>u)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}(u)\left(\frac{1}{\bar{F}(u)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(y-u)dF(y)\right)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{u}^{\infty}ydF(y)dF(u)-2\int_{0}^{\infty}u\int_{u}^{\infty}dF(y)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y\cdot 2F(y)dF(y)-\int_{0}^{\infty}y\cdot 2\bar{F}(y)dF(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\max(X_{1},X_{2}))-E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})),

which is precisely the GMD.

Instead, if we choose
w(u)=\bar{F}(u)
and
\phi(x)=2x^{2}
, (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{GE}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{u}^{\infty}y^{2}dF(y)dF(u)-2\int_{0}^{\infty}u\int_{u}^{\infty}dF(y)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{2}\cdot 2F(y)dF(y)-\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{2}\cdot 2\bar{F}(y)dF(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\max(X_{1},X_{2})^{2})-E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})^{2}).

In general, for the choices of
w(u)=\bar{F}(u)
and
\phi(x)=2x^{v}
for some positive integer
v
, we similarly have

\displaystyle\mathcal{GE}(X)=E\left(\max(X_{1},X_{2})^{v}-\min(X_{1},X_{2})^{v}\right).

Also, for
w(x)=\bar{F}^{k-1}(u)
,
k=2,3,\ldots
and
\phi(x)=kx^{v}
, we get

\mathcal{GE}(X)=E\left(\max(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})^{v}-\min(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})^{v}\right).

In the particular case of
v=1
, we similarly have

\displaystyle\mathcal{GE}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\max(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})-\min(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\max(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))-E(X_{1})+E(X_{1})-(E(\min(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle EG_{k}(-X)-EG_{k}(X),

where
EG_{k}(X)
and
EG_{k}(-X)
are the risk-premium and the gain-premium of
X
of
order
k
, respectively. These two quantities have found key applications in bid and ask prices in finance; see Agouram and Lakhnati (2016).
The above expression can alternatively be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{GE}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\max(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))-E(X_{1})+E(X_{1})-(E(\min(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kCov(X,F^{k-1}(X))-kCov(X,\bar{F}^{k-1}(X)).

In particular, when
k=2
, we deduce

\mathcal{GE}(X)=4Cov(X,F(X)),

which is yet another representation of GMD.

2.2.
Generalized cumulative entropy and GMD

We now consider the generalized cumulative entropy and discuss its relationship with GMD.

Definition 2

(Sudheesh et al., 2022)
.

Let
X
be a non-negative random variable with absolute continuous distribution function
F
. Further, let
\phi(\cdot)
be a function of
X
and
w(\cdot)
be a weight function. Then, the generalized cumulative entropy is defined as

(3)

\mathcal{GCE}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}w(u)E\Big[\phi(u)-\phi(X)|X\leq u\Big]dF(u).

In particular, for the choice of
w(u)=1
and
\phi(x)=x
, (
3
) reduces to
\mathcal{CE}(X)
(Di Crescenzo and Longobardi, 2009).

Theorem 2
.

For the choice of
w(u)=F(u)
and
\phi(x)=x
,
\mathcal{GCE}(X)
reduces to the GMD.

Proof:
Consider

\displaystyle\vskip-14.45377pt\mathcal{GE}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}{F}(u)E(u-X|X<u)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}{F}(u)\left(\frac{1}{{F}(u)}\int_{0}^{u}(u-y)dF(y)\right)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{u}udF(y)dF(u)-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{u}ydF(y)dF(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(\max(X_{1},X_{2})\right)-\int_{0}^{\infty}y\cdot 2\bar{F}(y)dF(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(\max(X_{1},X_{2})-\min(X_{1},X_{2})\right).

Further, for the choice of
w(u)=F^{k}(u)
and
\phi(x)=(k+1)x
, we have

\mathcal{GCE}(X)=E\left(\max(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1})-\min(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1})\right).

We now show that GMD is a special case of the cumulative residual Tsallis entropy of order
\alpha
defined by Rajesh and Sunoj (2019) as

(4)

CRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\bar{F}(x)-\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x))dx,\,\alpha>0,\,\alpha\neq 1.

For
\alpha=2
, the above expression becomes

(5)

\vskip-7.22743ptCRT_{2}(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\bar{F}(x)-\bar{F}^{2}(x))dx.

For a non-negative random variable
X
, we have
\mu=E(X)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}(x)dx
. Noting that
\bar{F}^{2}(x)
is the survival function of
\min(X_{1},X_{2})
, we simply obtain

(6)

\vskip-7.22743ptCRT_{2}(X)=E(X_{1})-E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})).

Now, because
|X_{1}-X_{2}|=X_{1}+X_{2}-2\min(X_{1},X_{2})
, we obtain
GMD=2CRT_{2}(X)
.

3.
Connection between extropy and GMD

Tahmasebi and Toomaj (2022) discussed a relationship between GMD and cumulative extropy. Here using the generalized entropy measures introduced by Sudheesh et al. (2022), we establish some relationships between different dynamic extropy measures and the right and left truncated GMD.

First, we express the GMD in terms of cumulative residual extropy and residual extropy. For a non-negative random variable
X
, Jahanshahi et al. (2020) defined the cumulative residual extropy as

(7)

\mathcal{CRJ}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}^{2}(x)dx,

while the cumulative extropy is defined as (Tahmasebi and Toomaj, 2020)

(8)

\mathcal{CJ}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-{F}^{2}(x))dx.

Using the survival functions of
\max(X_{1},X_{2})
and
\min(X_{1},X_{2})
, we easily find that

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{CRJ}(X)-2\mathcal{CJ}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-{F}^{2}(x))dx-\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(\max(X_{1},X_{2})-\min(X_{1},X_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle GMD.

Sudheesh and Sreedevi (2022) discussed the non-parametric estimation of
\mathcal{CRJ}(X)
and
\mathcal{CJ}(X)
for right censored data, and so using the above relationship, we can readily obtain an estimator of the GMD based on right censored data.

Sathar and Nair (2021) defined dynamic survival extropy as

(9)

J_{t}(X)=-\frac{1}{2\bar{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\bar{F}^{2}(x)dx,

while Kundu (2021) introduced dynamic cumulative extropy as

(10)

H_{t}(X)=-\frac{1}{2{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}{F}^{2}(x)dx.

Sudheesh and Sreedevi (2022) proposed simple alternative expressions for different extropy measures, and then used them to establish some relationships between different dynamic and weighted extropy measures and reliability concepts.

The left truncated GMD is given by

GMD_{L}=\frac{1}{\bar{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}(\bar{F}(t)-2\bar{F}(x))xdF(x)

while the right truncated GMD is defined as

GMD_{R}=\frac{1}{{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}(2{F}(x)-{F}(t))xdF(x).

Theorem 3
.

Let
m(x)=E(X-x|X>x)
and
r(x)=E(x-X|X\leq x)
. Then, the relationships between the GMD and the dynamic survival extropy and/or the dynamic cumulative extropy are

(i)

(11)

GMD_{L}=m(x)+2J_{t}(X),

(ii)

(12)

GMD_{R}=-2H_{t}(x)-r(x).

Proof:
Consider the left truncated GMD given by

(13)

\displaystyle GMD_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}(\bar{F}(t)-2\bar{F}(x))xdF(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\bar{F}(t)-t+t-2\bar{F}(x)\right)xdF(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}(x-t)dF(x)-\frac{1}{\bar{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}(x-t)2\bar{F}(x)dF(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(x)-E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})-t|\min(X_{1},X_{2})>t).

Sudheesh and Sreedevi (2022) expressed
J_{t}(X)
in (
9
) as

(14)

J_{t}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E\left(\min(X_{1},X_{2})-t|\min(X_{1},X_{2})>t\right).

Substituting (
14
) in (
13
), we obtain the relationship in (
11
).

Next, consider the right truncated GMD given by

(15)

\displaystyle GMD_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{F}^{2}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}(2{F}(x)-{F}(t))xdF(x).

After some algebraic manipulation, we obtain from (
15
) that

(16)

\displaystyle GMD_{R}=E\left(t-\min(X_{1},X_{2})|\min(X_{1},X_{2})\leq t\right)-r(x).

Sudheesh and Sreedevi (2022) expressed
H_{t}(X)
as

(17)

H_{t}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E\left(t-\max(X_{1},X_{2})|\max(X_{1},X_{2})\leq t\right).

Substituting (
17
) in (
16
) we obtain the relationship in (
12
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be two independent and identical random variables having distribution function
F
, and
Z=\min(X_{1},X_{2})
be the lifetime of a series system with the two components. Then, the generalized residual entropy associated with
Z
is the weighted average of the difference between the left truncated GMD and the mean residual life.

Proof:
Using (
3
), we define the generalized residual entropy associated with
Z
as

(18)

\mathcal{GE}(Z)=\int 2w(u)E\Big[\phi(Z)-\phi(u)|Z>u\Big]\bar{F}(u)dF(u),

where
\phi(.)
is a function of
Z
and
w(.)
is a weight function.
Now, for the choice of
w(u)=-\frac{1}{2}
and
\phi(z)=z
, from (
18
), we obtain

\mathcal{GE}(Z)=\int(GMD_{L}-m(u))\bar{F}(u)dF(u).

Theorem 5
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be two independent and identical random variables having distribution function
F
, and
Z=\max(X_{1},X_{2})
be the lifetime of a parallel system with the two components. Then, the generalized cumulative entropy associated with
Z
is the weighted average of the sum of the right truncated GMD and the mean past life.

Proof:

The generalized cumulative entropy associated with
Z
is defined as

(19)

\mathcal{GCE}(Z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}2w(u)E\Big[\phi(u)-\phi(X)|X\leq u\Big]F(u)dF(u).

Again, for the choice of
w(u)=\frac{1}{2}
and
\phi(z)=z
, from (
19
), we obtain

\mathcal{GCE}(Z)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(GMD_{R}+r(x))F(u)dF(u).

So, the generalized cumulative entropy associated with
Z
is the weighted average of sum of the right truncated GMD and the mean past life.

4.
Connections with probability weighted moments

We now show that several measures discussed in the preceding sections can be represented in terms of PWM.

The PWM of a random variable
X
with distribution function
F
is defined as (Greenwood et al., 1979)

(20)

\mathcal{M}_{p,r,s}=\mathbb{E}\left\{X^{p}F^{r}(X)(1-F(X))^{s}\right\},

where
p
,
r
and
s
are any real numbers, for which the involved expectation exists.

First, we consider the GMD and related measures. From the proof of Theorem 1, we can express GMD as

GMD=\int_{0}^{\infty}2yF(y)dF(y)-\int_{0}^{\infty}2y\bar{F}(y)dF(y),

and so we have

GMD=2E(XF(X))-2E(X\bar{F}(X)),

which, when compared with (
20
), yields

GMD=2\mathcal{M}_{1,1,0}-2\mathcal{M}_{1,0,1}.

Several income inequality measures are derived from the GMD by choosing different
weights in the expectation and one among them is the S-Gini family of indices (Yitzhaki and Schechtman, 2013). The absolute S-Gini index is defined as

S_{v}=-Cov(X,\bar{F}^{v-1}(X)),\quad v>0\,\text{ and }\,v\neq 1,

which can be rewritten as

S_{v}=-E(X\bar{F}^{v-1}(X))+\frac{1}{v}E(X).

Hence, the absolute S-Gini index can be represented as

S_{v}=\frac{1}{v}\mathcal{M}_{1,0,0}-\mathcal{M}_{1,0,v-1}.

Next, we consider different extropy measures. The cumulative extropy and the weighted cumulative extropy can be expressed as (Sudheesh and Sreedevi, 2022)

\mathcal{CRJ}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E(\max(X_{1},X_{2})),

\mathcal{CRJW}(X)=-\frac{1}{4}E(\max(X_{1},X_{2})^{2}).

Observing that
2F(x)f(x)
is the density function of
\max(X_{1},X_{2})
, the extropy measures given above can be rewritten as

\mathcal{CRJ}(X)=-E(XF(X)),

\mathcal{CRJW}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E(X^{2}F(X)),

so that

\mathcal{CRJ}(X)=-\mathcal{M}_{1,1,0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{CRJW}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}_{2,1,0}.

The cumulative residual entropy and the weighted cumulative residual entropy can be expressed as (Sudheesh and Sreedevi, 2022)

\mathcal{CE}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})),

\mathcal{WCE}(X)=-\frac{1}{4}E(\min(X_{1},X_{2})^{2}).

Here again, we observe that
2\bar{F}(x)f(x)
is the density function of
\min(X_{1},X_{2})
, and so

\mathcal{CE}(X)=-E(X\bar{F}(X))

\mathcal{WCE}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}E(X^{2}\bar{F}(X))),

from which we readily obtain

\mathcal{CE}(X)=-\mathcal{M}_{1,0,1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{WCE}(X)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}_{2,0,1}.

Next, we show that the cumulative (residual) Tsallis entropy of order
\alpha
can be written in terms of PWM. Let us assume that
\alpha
is an integer. Recall that

(21)

CRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\bar{F}(x)-\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x))dx.

We can show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x\alpha\bar{F}^{\alpha-1}(x)dF(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x)dx,

and so we can write from (
21
) that

CRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}E(X)-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}E(X\bar{F}^{\alpha}(X)),

which yields

CRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\mathcal{M}_{1,0,0}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\mathcal{M}_{1,0,(\alpha-1)}.

For a non-negative continuous random variable
X
, the weighted cumulative residual Tsallis entropy (WCRTE) of order
\alpha
is defined as (Chakraborty and Pradhan, 2021a)

WCRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}x(\bar{F}(x)-\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x))dx.

After some simple algebra, we can show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}\alpha\bar{F}^{\alpha-1}(x)dF(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}2x\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x)dx,

so that

WCRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{2(\alpha-1)}E(X^{2})-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}E(X^{2}\bar{F}^{\alpha}(X))

from which we readily obtain

WCRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{2(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{2,0,0}-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{2,0,(\alpha-1)}.

Calì et al. (2017) introduced the cumulative Tsallis past entropy as

CT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}({F}(x)-F^{\alpha}(x))dx,\,\alpha>0,\alpha\neq 1,

while Chakraborty and Pradhan (2021b) defined the weighted cumulative Tsallis entropy (WCTE) of order
\alpha
as

(22)

WCT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}x(F(x)-F^{\alpha}(x))dx,\,\alpha>0,\alpha\neq 1.

As in the case of CRTE and WCRTE, we obtain

CT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{1,0,0}-\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{1,(\alpha-1),0},

WCRT_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{2(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{2,0,0}-\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\mathcal{M}_{2,(\alpha-1),0}.

Sharma and Taneja (1975) and Mittal (1975) independently introduced the STM entropy as

(23)

S_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}(f^{\alpha}(x)-f^{\beta}(x))dx.

Some entropy measures discussed in the literature can be derived from (
23
) by taking different values of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Sudheesh et al. (2022) introduced a cumulative residual STM entropy as

SR_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x)-\bar{F}^{\beta}(x))dx,

and the cumulative past STM entropy as

SP_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}(F^{\alpha}(x)-F^{\beta}(x))dx.

They also introduced weighted versions of these measures as

SRW_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}x(\bar{F}^{\alpha}(x)-\bar{F}^{\beta}(x))dx

and

SPW_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}x(F^{\alpha}(x)-F^{\beta}(x))dx.

We now express these measures in terms of PWM as follows, presented without proofs, for the sake of conciseness:

SR_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}_{1,0,(\alpha-1)}-\mathcal{M}_{1,0,(\beta-1)}),

SP_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}(\mathcal{M}_{1,(\alpha-1),0}-\mathcal{M}_{1,(\beta-1),0}),

SRW_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{2(\beta-\alpha)}(\mathcal{M}_{2,0,(\alpha-1)}-\mathcal{M}_{2,0,(\beta-1)}),

SPW_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{2(\beta-\alpha)}(\mathcal{M}_{2,(\alpha-1),0}-\mathcal{M}_{2,(\beta-1),0}).

These representations in terms of PWM would enable us to develop inferential methods for all theses measures using the known results on PWM.

5.
Concluding remarks

The GMD is a well-known measure of dispersion that is used extensively in economics. We have established here some relationships between the information measures and the GMD. We have shown that the GMD is a special case of the generalized cumulative residual entropy proposed recently by Sudheesh et al. (2022). We have also established some relationships between the GMD and extropy measures. We have further presented some relationships between the dynamic versions of the cumulative extropy and the truncated GMD.

The probability weighted moments (PWM) generalize the concept of moments of a probability distribution. The estimates derived using probability weighted moments are often superior than the standard moment-based estimates. We have shown here that many of the information measures can be expressed in terms of PWM, which would enable us to develop inferential methods for these measures using the well-known results on PWM.

Refined regularity of the blow-up set linked to refined asymptotic behavior for the semilinear heat equation Thanks: H. Zaag is supported by the ERC Advanced Grant no. 291214, BLOWDISOL and by the ANR project ANAÉ ref. ANR-13-BS01-0010-03. —————– August 10, 2026


Abstract
. We consider u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) , a solution of ∂ t u = Δ ​ u + | u | p − 1 ​ u \partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u which blows up at some time T > 0 T>0 , where u : ℝ N × [ 0 , T ) → ℝ u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R} , p > 1 p>1 and ( N − 2 ) ​ p < N + 2 (N-2)p<N+2 . Define S ⊂ ℝ N S\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} to be the blow-up set of u u , that is the set of all blow-up points. Under suitable nondegeneracy conditions, we show that if S S contains a ( N − ℓ ) (N-\ell) -dimensional continuum for some ℓ ∈ { 1 , … , N − 1 } \ell\in\{1,\dots,N-1\} , then S S is in fact a 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} manifold. The crucial step is to derive a refined asymptotic behavior of u u near blow-up. In order to obtain such a refined behavior, we have to abandon the explicit profile function as a first order approximation and take a non-explicit function as a first order description of the singular behavior. This way we escape logarithmic scales of the variable ( T − t ) (T-t) and reach significant small terms in the polynomial order ( T − t ) μ (T-t)^{\mu} for some μ > 0 \mu>0 . The refined asymptotic behavior yields geometric constraints of the blow-up set, leading to more regularity on S S .

1.
Introduction.

We are interested in the following semilinear heat equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}u&=&\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u(t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
p>1
or
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
.

It is well known that for each initial data
u_{0}
the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for some
0<T\leq+\infty
, and that either
T=+\infty
or

T<+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=+\infty.

In the latter case we say that the solution blows up in finite time, and
T
is called the blow-up time. In such a blow-up case, a point
\hat{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called a blow-up point if
u(x,t)
is not locally bounded in some neighborhood of
(\hat{a},T)
, this means that there exists
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(\hat{a},T)
such that
|u(x_{n},t_{n})|\to+\infty
when
n\to+\infty
. We denote by
S
the blow-up set, that is the set of all blow-up points of
u
.

Given
\hat{a}\in S
, we know from Velázquez
[
Vel92
]
(see also Filippas and Kohn
[
FK92
]
, Filippas and Liu
[
FL93
]
, Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV93
]
, Merle and Zaag
[
MZ00
]
) that up to replacing
u
by
-u
, one of
following two cases occurs:

- Case 1 (non degenerate rate of blow-up): For all
K_{0}>0
, there is an orthonormal
(N\times N)
matrix
Q_{\hat{a}}
and
\ell_{\hat{a}}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that

\sup_{|\xi|\leq K_{0}}\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\hat{a}+Q_{\hat{a}}\xi\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|},t)-f_{\ell_{\hat{a}}}(\xi)\right|\to 0\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\to T,

(1.2)

where

f_{\ell_{\hat{a}}}(\xi)=\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{4p}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_{\hat{a}}}\xi_{i}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.3)

- Case 2 (degenerate rate of blow-up): For all
K_{0}\geq 0
, there exists an even integer
m\geq 4
such that

\sup_{|\xi|\leq K_{0}}\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\hat{a}+\xi(T-t)^{\frac{1}{m}},t)-\left(p-1+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right|\to 0\;\;\text{as}\;\;t\to T,

(1.4)

where
\xi^{\alpha}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
,
|\alpha|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}
if
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
and
\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\geq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

According to Velázquez
[
Vel92
]
, if case 1 occurs with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
or case 2 occurs with
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}>0
for all
\xi\neq 0
, then
\hat{a}
is an isolated blow-up point. Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV92b
]
and
[
HV92a
]
proved that the profile (
1.3
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
is generic in the case
N=1
, and they announced the same for
N\geq 2
, but they never published it. Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
BK94
]
, Merle and Zaag
[
MZ97
]
show the existence of initial data for (
1.1
) such that the corresponding solutions blow up in finite time
T
at only one blow-up point
\hat{a}
and verify the behavior (
1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
. The method of
[
MZ97
]
also gives the stability of the profile (
1.3
) (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
) with respect to perturbations in the initial data (see also Fermanian, Merle and Zaag
[
FMZ00
]
,
[
FZ00
]
for other proofs of the stability). In
[
EZ11
]
and
[
NZ16a
]
, the authors prove the stability of the profile (
1.3
) (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
) with respect to perturbations in the initial data and also in the nonlinearity, in some class allowing lower order terms in the solution and also in the gradient. All the other asymptotic behaviors are suspected to be unstable.

When

\ell_{\hat{a}}\leq N-1

in (
1.2
), we do not know whether
\hat{a}
is isolated or not, or whether
S
is continuous near
\hat{a}
. In this paper, we assume that
\hat{a}
is a non-isolated blow-up point and that
S
is continuous locally near
\hat{a}
, in a sense that we will precisely define later. Our main concern is the regularity of
S
near
\hat{a}
. The first relevant result is due to Velázquez
[
Vel93
]
where the author showed that the Hausdorff measure of
S
is less or equal to
N-1
. No further results on the description of
S
were known until the contributions of Zaag
[
Zaa02a
]
,
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
(see also
[
Zaa02c
]
for a summarized note). In
[
Zaa02a
]
, the author proves that if
S
is locally continuous, then
S
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
manifold. He also obtains the first singularity description near
\hat{a}
. More precisely, he shows that (see Theorems 3 and 4 in
[
Zaa02a
]
) for some
t_{0}<T
and
\delta>0
, for all
K_{0}>0
,
t\in[t_{0},T)
and
x\in B(\hat{a},2\delta)
such that
d(x,S)\leq K_{0}\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}
,

\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t)-f_{1}\left(\frac{d(x,S)}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}\right)\right|\leq C(K_{0})\frac{\log|\log(T-t)|}{|\log(T-t)|},

(1.5)

where
f_{1}
is defined in (
1.3
) (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
). Moreover, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus S
,
u(x,t)\to u^{*}(x)
as
t\to T
with

u^{*}(x)\sim U(d(x,S))=\left(\frac{8p}{(p-1)^{2}}\frac{|\log d(x,S)|}{d^{2}(x,S)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\quad\text{as}\;\;d(x,S)\to 0\;\;\text{and}\;\;x\in B(\hat{a},2\delta).

(1.6)

If

\ell_{\hat{a}}=1,

Zaag in
[
Zaa02b
]
further refines the asymptotic behavior (
1.5
) and gets to error terms of order
(T-t)^{\mu}
for some
\mu>0
. This way, he obtains more regularity on the blow-up set
S
. The key idea is to replace the explicit profile
f_{1}
in (
1.5
) by a non-explicit function, say
\tilde{u}(x_{1},t)
, then to go beyond all logarithmic scales through scaling and matching. In fact, for
\tilde{u}(x_{1},t)
, Zaag takes a symmetric, one dimensional solution of (
1.1
) that blows up at the same time
T
only at the origin, and behaves like (
1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
. More precisely, he abandons the explicit profile function
f_{1}
in (
1.5
) and chooses a non explicit function
\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(d(x,S),t)
as a first order description of the singular behavior, where
\tilde{u}_{\sigma}
is defined by

\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x_{1},t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma}{p-1}}\tilde{u}\left(e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}x_{1},T-e^{-\sigma}(T-t)\right).

(1.7)

He shows that for each blow-up point
a
near
\hat{a}
, there is an optimal scaling parameter
\sigma=\sigma(a)
so that the difference
(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}_{\sigma(a)}(d(x,S),t)\right)
along the normal direction to
S
at
a
is minimum. Hence, if the function
\tilde{u}_{\sigma(a)}(d(x,S),t)
is chosen as a first order description for
u(x,t)
near
(a,T)
, we escape logarithmic scales. More precisely, for all
t\in[t_{0},T)
and
x\in B(\hat{a},2\delta)
such that
d(x,S)\leq K_{0}\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}
,

(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left|u(x,t)-\tilde{u}_{\sigma(a)}(d(x,S),t)\right|\leq C(T-t)^{\mu},

(1.8)

for some
\mu>0
. Note that any other value of
\sigma\neq\sigma(a)
in (
1.8
) gives an error of logarithmic order of the variable
(T-t)
(the same as in (
1.5
)). Exploiting estimate (
1.8
) yields geometric constraints on
S
which imply the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\frac{1}{2}-\eta}
-regularity of
S
for all
\eta>0
. A further refinement of (
1.8
) given in
[
Zaa06
]
yields better estimates in the expansion of
u(x,t)
near
(a,T)
. Moreover, some following terms in the expansion of
u(x,t)
near
(a,T)
contain geometrical descriptions of
S
, resulting in more regularity of
S
, namely the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-regularity.

In this work, we want to know whether the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-regularity near
\hat{a}
proven in
[
Zaa06
]
for
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
would hold in the case where
u
behaves like (
1.2
) near
(\hat{a},T)
with

\ell_{\hat{a}}\in\{2,\dots,N-1\}.

(1.9)

Since the author in
[
Zaa02a
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
obtains the result only when
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
, this corresponds to
(N-1)
-dimensional blow-up set (the codimension of the blow-up set is one, according to
[
Zaa02a
]
). In our opinion, in those papers the major obstacle towards the case (
1.9
) lays in the fact that the author could not refine the asymptotic behavior (
1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}\in\{2,\dots,N-1\}
to go beyond all logarithmic scales and get a smaller error term in polynomial orders of the variable
(T-t)
. It happens that a similar difficulty was already encountered by Fermanian and Zaag in
[
FZ00
]
, when they wanted to find a sharp
profile in the case (
1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N
, which corresponds to an isolated blow-up point, as we have pointed out right after estimate (
1.4
). Such a sharp profile could be obtained in
[
FZ00
]
only when
N=1
(which corresponds also to
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
): no surprise it was
\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(x_{1},t)
, the dilated version of
\tilde{u}(x_{1},t)
, the one-dimensional blow-up solution mentioned between estimates (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). As a matter of fact, the use of
\tilde{u}(x_{1},t)
was first used in
[
FZ00
]
for the isolated blow-up point in one space dimension (
N=1
and
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
), then later in higher dimensions with a
(N-1)
dimensional blow-up surface (
N\geq 2
and still
\ell_{\hat{a}}=1
) in
[
Zaa02b
]
.

The interest of
\tilde{u}(x_{1},t)
is that it provides a one-parameter family of blow-up solutions, thanks to the scaling parameter in (
1.7
), which enables to get the sharp profile by suitably choosing the parameter.

Handling the case
\ell_{\hat{a}}\geq 2
remained open, both for the case of an isolated point (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N\geq 2
) and a non-isolated blow-up point (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=2,\cdots,N-1
). From the refinement of the expansion around the explicit profile in
f_{\ell_{\hat{a}}}
in (
1.2
), it appeared than one needs a
\frac{\ell_{\hat{a}}(\ell_{\hat{a}}+1)}{2}
-parameter family of blow-up solutions obeying (
1.2
).

Such a family was constructed by Nguyen and Zaag in
[
NZ16b
]
, and successfully used to derive a sharp profile in the case of an isolated blow-up point (
\ell_{\hat{a}}=N\geq 2
), by fine-tuning the
\frac{\ell_{\hat{a}}(\ell_{\hat{a}}+1)}{2}=\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
parameters.

In this paper, we aim at using that family to handle the case of a non-isolated blow-up point (
N\geq 2
and
\ell_{\hat{a}}=2,\cdots,N-1
), in order to generalize the results of Zaag in
[
Zaa02a
]
,
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
, proving in particular the

regularity of the blow-up set, under the mere hypothesis that it is continuous.

The main result in this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
\mathcal{C}^{2}
regularity of the blow-up set assuming
\mathcal{C}^{1}
regularity)
.

Take
N\geq 2
and
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
. Consider
u
a solution of (
1.1
) that blows up in finite time
T
on a set
S
and take
\hat{a}\in S
where
u
behaves locally as stated in (
1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=\ell
. If
S
is locally a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
manifold of dimension
N-\ell
, then it is locally
\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
was already proved by Zaag
[
Zaa06
]
only when
\ell=1
. Thus, the novelty of our contribution lays in the case
\ell\in\{2,\dots,N-1\}
and
N\geq 3
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, Zaag
[
Zaa02a
]
already proved that
S
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
manifold near
\hat{a}
, assuming that
S
is continuous. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
can be restated under a weaker assumption. Before stating this stronger version, let us first clearly describe our hypotheses and introduce some terminologies borrowed from
[
Zaa02a
]
(see also
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
). According to Velázquez
[
Vel92
]
(see Theorem 2, page 1571), we know that for all
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta(\epsilon)>0
such that

S\cap B(\hat{a},2\delta)\subset\Omega_{\hat{a},\epsilon}\equiv\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\left|P_{\hat{a}}(x-\hat{a})\right|\geq(1-\epsilon)|x-\hat{a}|\right\},

where
P_{\hat{a}}
is the orthogonal projection over
\pi_{\hat{a}}
, where

\pi_{\hat{a}}=\hat{a}+\text{span}\{Q_{\hat{a}}^{T}e_{\ell_{\hat{a}}+1},\cdots,Q_{\hat{a}}^{T}e_{N}\}

is the so-called ”weak” tangent plane to
S
at
\hat{a}
. Roughly speaking,
\Omega_{\hat{a},\epsilon}
is a cone with vertex
\hat{a}
and shrinks to
\pi_{\hat{a}}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. In some ”weak” sense,
S
is
(N-\ell_{\hat{a}})
-dimensional. In fact, here comes our second hypothesis: we assume there is
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}((-1,1)^{N-\ell_{\hat{a}}},\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\Gamma(0)=\hat{a}
and
Im\,\Gamma\subset S
, where
Im\,\Gamma
is at least
(N-\ell_{\hat{a}})
-dimensional, in the sense that

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\text{$\forall b\in Im\,\Gamma$, there are $(N-\ell_{\hat{a}})$ independent vectors $v_{1},\dots,v_{N-\ell_{\hat{a}}}$ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and}&\\
&\text{$\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{N-\ell_{\hat{a}}}$ functions in $\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,1],S)$ such that $\Gamma_{i}(0)=b$ and $\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}(0)=v_{i}$.}&\end{array}

(1.10)

The hypothesis (
1.10
) means that
b
is actually non-isolated in
(N-\ell_{\hat{a}})
independent directions. We assume in addition that
\hat{a}
is not an endpoint in
Im\,\Gamma
in the sense that

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\text{$\forall\epsilon>0$, the projection of $\Gamma((-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{N-\ell_{\hat{a}}})$ on the "weak" tangent plane $\pi_{\hat{a}}$}&\\
&\text{at $\hat{a}$ contains an open ball centered at $\hat{a}$.}&\\
\end{array}

(1.11)

This is the stronger version of our result:

Theorem
1.1
’.

Take
N\geq 2
and
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
. Consider
u
a solution of (

) that blows up in finite time

behaves locally as stated in (

) with

. Consider
\Gamma\in\mathcal{C}((-1,1)^{N-\ell},\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

is at least
(N-\ell)
-dimensional (in the sense (

)). If
\hat{a}

\delta>0
,

.

Let us now briefly give the main ideas of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The proof is based on techniques developed by Zaag in
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
for the case when the solution of equation (
1.1
) behaves like (
1.2
) with
\ell=1
. As in
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
, the proof relies on two arguments:

-

The derivation of a sharp blow-up profile of
u(x,t)
near the singularity, in the sense that the difference between the solution
u(x,t)
and this sharp profile goes beyond all logarithmic scales of the variables
(T-t)
. This is possible thanks to the recent result in
[
NZ16b
]
.

-

The derivation of a refined asymptotic profile of
u(x,t)
near the singularity linked to geometric constraints on the blow-up set. In fact, we derive an asymptotic profile for
u(x,t)
in every ball
B(a,K_{0}\sqrt{T-t})
for some
K_{0}>0
and
a
a blow-up point close to
\hat{a}
. Moreover, this profile is continuous in
a
and the speed of convergence of
u
to each one in the ball
B(a,K_{0}\sqrt{T-t})
is uniform with respect to
a
. If
a
and
b
are in
S
and
0<|a-b|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{T-t}
, then the balls
B(a,K_{0}\sqrt{T-t})
and
B(b,K_{0}\sqrt{T-t})
intersect each other, leading to different profiles for
u(x,t)
in the intersection. However, these profiles have to coincide, up to the error terms. This makes a geometric constraint which gives more regularity for the blow-up set near
\hat{a}
.

Let us explain the difficulty raised in
[
Zaa02b
]
and
[
Zaa06
]
for the case
\ell\geq 2
. Consider
a\in S\cap B(\hat{a},2\delta)
for some
\delta>0
and introduce the following self-similar variables:

W_{a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad y=\frac{x-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t).

(1.12)

Then, we see from (
1.1
) that for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\log T,+\infty)
,

\frac{\partial W_{a}}{\partial s}=\Delta W_{a}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla W_{a}-\frac{W_{a}}{p-1}+|W_{a}|^{p-1}W_{a}.

(1.13)

Under the hypotheses stated in Theorem
1.1
, Zaag
[
Zaa02a
]
proved in Proposition 3.1, page 513 and in Section 6.1, pages 530-533 that for all
a\in S_{\delta}\equiv S\cap B(\hat{a},2\delta)
for some
\delta>0
and
s\geq-\log T
, there exists an
(N\times N)
orthogonal matrix
Q_{a}
such that

\left\|W_{a}(Q_{a}y,s)-\left\{\kappa+\frac{\kappa}{2ps}\left(\ell-\frac{|\bar{y}|^{2}}{2}\right)\right\}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\leq C\frac{\log s}{s^{2}},

(1.14)

where
\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
,
\bar{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{\ell_{a}})
,
Q_{a}
is continuous in terms of
a
such that
\{Q_{a}^{T}e_{j}|j=\ell+1,\dots,N\}
spans the tangent plane
\pi_{a}
to
S
at
a
and
Q_{a}^{T}e_{i},i=1,\cdots,\ell
are the normal directions to
S
at
a
,

is the weighted

space associated with the weight
\rho=\dfrac{1}{(4\pi)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}
. Note that the estimate (
1.14
) implies (
1.5
) (see Appendix C in
[

]
).

When
\ell=1
, in order to refine estimate (

), the author in
[

]
subtracts from

a 1-dimensional solution with the same profile. Let us do the same when

, and explain how the author succeeds in handing the case
\ell=1
and gets stuck when
\ell\geq 2
. To this end, we consider
\hat{u}(\bar{x},t)
with

a radially symmetric solution of (

) in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
which blows up at time

1.2
) with
\ell_{\hat{a}}=\ell
(see Appendix A.1 in

]

2.
Setting of the problem and strategy of the proof of the

-regularity of the blow-up set.

In this section we give the main steps of the proof of Theorems

and
1.3
. All long and technical proofs will be left to the next section. We proceed in 3 parts corresponding to 3 separate subsections. For the reader’s convenience, we briefly describe these parts as follows:

•

•

Part 2: Through the introduction of a local chart, we give a geometrical constraint on the expansion of the solution linked to the asymptotic behavior (see Proposition

and

In what follows,

1.13
) and behave like (
2.4
). We see from (
1.13
) and (
2.4
) that for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\log T,+\infty)
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{s}g=\mathscr{L}g+\alpha g,\\
\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\leq C\frac{\log s}{s^{2}},\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

where

\mathscr{L}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla+1,

and

\alpha(y,s)=\frac{|W_{1}|^{p-1}W_{1}-|W_{2}|^{p-1}W_{2}}{W_{1}-W_{2}}-\frac{p}{p-1}\quad\text{if}\;\;W_{1}\neq W_{2},

in particular,

\alpha(y,s)=p|W_{0}(y,s)|^{p-1}-\frac{p}{p-1},\quad\text{for some}\;\;W_{0}(y,s)\in(W_{1}(y,s),W_{2}(y,s)).

(3.3)

The operator
\mathscr{L}
is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{L})\subset L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Its spectrum consists of eigenvalues

spec(\mathscr{L})=\left\{\lambda_{n}=1-\frac{n}{2},\;n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The eigenfunctions corresponding to
1-\frac{n}{2}
are

h_{\beta}(y)=h_{\beta_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots h_{\beta_{N}}(y_{N}),\quad\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{N}=|\beta|=n,

(3.4)

where

h_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{[m/2]}\frac{m!}{i!(m-2i)!}(-1)^{i}\xi^{m-2i}\quad\text{for}\;\;m\in\mathbb{N},

satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{m}(\xi)h_{n}(\xi)\rho(\xi)d\xi=2^{m}m!\delta_{m,n}.

The component of
g
on
h_{\beta}
is given by

g_{\beta}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}k_{\beta}(y)g(y,s)\rho(y)dy,\quad\text{where}\quad k_{\beta}(y)=\frac{h_{\beta}(y)}{\|h_{\beta}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}}.

If we denote by
P_{n}
the orthogonal projector of
L^{2}_{\rho}
over the eigenspace of
\mathscr{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1-\frac{n}{2}
, then

P_{n}g(y,s)=\sum_{|\beta|=n}g_{\beta}(s)h_{\beta}(y).

Since the eigeinfunctions of
\mathscr{L}
span the whole space
L^{2}_{\rho}
, we can write

g(y,s)=\sum_{n\in N}P_{n}g(y,s)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}g_{\beta}(s)h_{\beta}(y)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N},|\beta|\leq k}g_{\beta}(s)h_{\beta}(y)+R_{k+1}g(y,s),

where
R_{k}g=\sum\limits_{n\geq k}P_{n}g
. We also denote

I(s)^{2}=\|g(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}l_{n}^{2}(s)=\sum_{n\leq k}l_{n}^{2}(s)+r_{k+1}^{2}(s),

(3.5)

where

l_{n}(s)=\|P_{n}g(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}},\quad r_{k}(s)=\|R_{k}g(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}}.

(3.6)

As for
\alpha
, we have the following estimates:

Lemma 3.1

(Estimates on
\alpha
)
.

For all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
s\geq-\log T
, we have

\alpha(y,s)\leq\frac{C}{s},\quad|\alpha(y,s)|\leq\frac{C}{s}(1+|y|^{2}),

and

\left|\alpha(y,s)+\frac{1}{4s}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}h_{2}(y_{i})\right|\leq\frac{C}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}(1+|y|^{3}).

(3.7)

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
FZ00
]
where the case
\ell=N
was treated.
∎

In the following lemma, we project equation (
3.2
) on the different modes to get estimates for
I(s)
,
l_{n}(s)
and
r_{n}(s)
. More precisely, we claim the following:

Lemma 3.2

(Evolution of
I(s)
,
l_{n}(s)
and
r_{n}(s)
)
.

There exist
s_{3}\geq-\log T
and
s_{*}>0
such that for all
s\geq s_{3}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, we have

i)

\left|l^{\prime}_{n}(s)+\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)l_{n}(s)\right|\leq C(n)\dfrac{I(s)}{s}
.

ii)

I^{\prime}(s)\leq\left(1-\frac{n+1}{2}+\frac{C_{0}}{s}\right)I(s)+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2}(n+1-k)l_{k}(s)
.

iii)

\left|g^{\prime}_{\beta}(s)+\left(-1+\frac{|\beta|}{2}+\frac{1}{s}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\right)g_{\beta}(s)\right|\leq C(\beta)\left(\dfrac{1}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}I(s)+\dfrac{1}{s}\left(l_{|\beta|-2}(s)+l_{|\beta|+2}\right)\right)
.

iv)

r^{\prime}_{n}(s)\leq\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)r_{n}(s)+\frac{C}{s}I(s-s_{*})
.

Proof.

For
(i)
and
(ii)
, see Lemma 2.7, page 1197 in
[
FZ00
]
. For
(iii)
, see Appendix B.1, page 545 in
[
Zaa02b
]
for a similar calculations. For
(iv)
, see page 523 in
[
Zaa06
]
, where the calculation is mainly based on the following regularizing property of equation (
3.2
) by Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV93
]
(control of the
L^{4}_{\rho}
-norm by the
L^{2}_{\rho}
-norm up to some delay in time, see Lemma 2.3 in
[
HV93
]
):

\left(\int g^{4}(y,s)\rho dy\right)^{1/4}\leq C\left(\int g^{2}(y,s-s_{*})\rho dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{for some}\;\;s_{*}>0.

This ends the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

In the next step, we use Lemma
3.2
to show that either the null mode or a negative mode of
\mathscr{L}
will dominate as
s\to+\infty
. In particular, we have the following:

Proposition 3.3

(Dominance of a mode and its description)
.

We have

i)\;
Either for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
l_{n}(s)=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{I(s)}{s}\right)
and there exist
\sigma_{n}
,
C_{n}>0
and
C_{n}^{\prime}>0
such that

\forall s\geq\sigma_{n},\quad I(s)\leq C_{n}s^{C^{\prime}_{n}}exp\left((1-n/2)s\right).

ii)
Or there is
n_{0}\geq 2
such that

I(s)\sim l_{n_{0}}(s)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\forall n\neq n_{0},\;\;l_{n}(s)=\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{I(s)}{s}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty.

(3.8)

Moreover,

\bullet

If
n_{0}=2
, namely
I(s)\sim l_{2}(s)
, then

\forall|\beta|=2,\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|g_{\beta}(s)|\leq C\frac{\log s}{s^{5/2}}&\text{if}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\neq 2,\\
\left|g_{\beta}(s)-\frac{c_{\beta}}{s^{2}}\right|\leq C\frac{\log s}{s^{5/2}}&\text{if}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}=2.\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

\bullet

If
n_{0}=3
, namely
I(s)\sim l_{3}(s)
, then

I(s)\leq C_{0}e^{-\frac{s}{2}}s^{C_{0}}\quad\text{for some}\;\;C_{0}>0.

(3.10)

Proof.

See Proposition 2.6, page 1196 in
[
FZ00
]
for the existence of a dominating component, where the proof relies on
(i)
and
(ii)
of Lemma
3.2
. If case
(ii)
occurs with
n_{0}=2
, we write from
(iii)
of Lemma
3.2
: for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
with
|\beta|=2
,

\left|g^{\prime}_{\beta}(s)+\frac{g_{\beta}}{s}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\right|\leq C(\beta)\left(\dfrac{I(s)}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\dfrac{l_{0}(s)+l_{4}(s)}{s}\right)\leq C(\beta)\dfrac{I(s)}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C(\beta)\frac{\log s}{s^{7/2}},

where we used (
3.8
) and (
3.2
) from which
l_{0}(s)+l_{4}(s)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{I(s)}{s}\right)
and
I(s)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\right)
. Since
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}
is only equal to
0,1
or
2
if
|\beta|=2
, estimate (
3.9
) follows after integration. Estimate (
3.10
) immediately follows from
(i)
of Lemma
3.2
. This ends the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Let us now derive Proposition
2.1
from Proposition
3.3
. Indeed, we see from Proposition
3.3
that if case
i)
occurs, we already have exponential decay for

. If case
(ii)
occurs with

, we write from part
(i)
of Lemma

,

\left|l^{\prime}_{n_{0}}(s)+\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}-1\right)l_{n_{0}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{s}l_{n_{0}}.

Since

in a neighbourhood of infinity, this gives

l_{n_{0}}(s)\leq C_{0}s^{C_{0}}e^{\left(1-\frac{n_{0}}{2}\right)s}\leq C_{0}s^{C_{0}}e^{-\frac{s}{2}},

which yields (
2.8
). If case
(ii)
occurs with
n_{0}=2
, by definition of
P_{2}
, we derive from (
3.9
) that there is a symmetric, real
(\ell\times\ell)
-matrix
\mathcal{B}
such that

P_{2}g(y,s)=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{y}^{T}\mathcal{B}\bar{y}-tr(\mathcal{B})\right)+o\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right),

which is (
2.7
). This concludes the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
\square

3.2.
\mathcal{C}^{1,\frac{1}{2}-\eta}
-regularity of the blow-up set.

We give the proof of Proposition
2.8
in this section. The proof uses the argument given in
[
Zaa02b
]
treated for the case
\ell=1
. Here we shall exploit the refined estimate (
2.12
) to obtain a geometric constraint on the blow-up set. Without loss of generality, we assume
\hat{a}=0
and
Q_{\hat{a}}=Id
. Under the hypotheses of Proposition
2.8
, we know that
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}^{1}((-\delta_{1},\delta_{1})^{N-\ell},\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
with
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
. If we introduce

\Gamma(\tilde{x})=(\gamma_{1}(\tilde{x}),\cdots,\gamma_{\ell}(\tilde{x}),\tilde{x}),\quad\tilde{x}=(x_{\ell+1},\cdots,x_{N}),

then

\text{Im}\,\Gamma\cap B(0,2\delta)=\text{graph}(\gamma)\cap B(0,2\delta)=S_{\delta}.

Consider
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{h}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N-\ell}
such that
\tilde{x}
as well as
\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}
are in
B(0,\delta_{1})
and
\Gamma(\tilde{x})
as well as
\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})
are in
S_{\delta}
. For all
t\in[T-e^{-s_{0}-1},T)
such that
|\Gamma(\tilde{x})-\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})|\leq\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}
, we use (
2.12
) with
x=a=\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})
, then with
x=\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})
and
a=\Gamma(\tilde{x})
) to find that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),t)-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,s)\right|&\leq Ce^{-\frac{s}{2}}s^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}},\\
\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),t)-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),s},s)\right|&\leq Ce^{-\frac{s}{2}}s^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}},\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

where
\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),s}
is defined as

\bar{y}_{a_{1},a_{2},s}=e^{\frac{s}{2}}\big((a_{1}-a_{2})\cdot Q_{a_{1}}e_{1},\cdots,(a_{1}-a_{2})\cdot Q_{a_{1}}e_{\ell}\big).

(3.12)

Since
\Gamma
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, we have

|\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\Gamma(\tilde{x})|\leq C|\tilde{h}|.

Let us fix
t=\tilde{t}(\tilde{x},\tilde{h})
such that

|\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\Gamma(\tilde{x})|=\sqrt{(T-\tilde{t})|\log(T-\tilde{t})|},

(3.13)

and take
\tilde{h}\in B_{N-\ell}(0,h_{1}(s_{0}))
for some
h_{1}(s_{0})>0
, then we have
\tilde{t}\geq T-e^{-s_{0}-1}
. Hence, if
\tilde{s}=-\log(T-\tilde{t})
, we have by (
3.11
),

\left|w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\tilde{s}},\tilde{s})\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}}.

(3.14)

Similarly, by changing the roles of
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}
, we get

\left|w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\tilde{s}},\tilde{s})\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}},

(3.15)

where
\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\tilde{s}}
is defined as in (
3.12
).

From a Taylor expansion for
w_{\mathcal{B}}(\bar{y},\tilde{s})
near
\bar{y}=0
, we write

w_{\mathcal{B}}(\bar{y},\tilde{s})=w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})+\bar{y}\cdot\nabla w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})+\frac{1}{2}\bar{y}^{T}\nabla^{2}w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})\bar{y}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\bar{y}|^{3}|\nabla^{3}w_{\mathcal{B}}(z,\tilde{s})|\right),

(3.16)

for some
z
between
0
and
\bar{y}
.

Since (
2.3
) and (
2.9
) also hold in
\mathcal{C}_{loc}^{k}
by parabolic regularity, we deduce that

|\nabla w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log\tilde{s}}{\tilde{s}^{2}}\right),\quad\nabla^{2}w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})=-\frac{\kappa}{4p\tilde{s}}I_{\ell\times\ell}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log\tilde{s}}{\tilde{s}^{2}}\right).

From
[
MZ98
]
(see Theorem 1), we know that
\|\nabla^{3}w_{\mathcal{B}}(\tilde{s})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}}}
. Substituting all these above estimates into (
3.16
) yields

w_{\mathcal{B}}(\bar{y},\tilde{s})\leq w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})-\frac{\kappa}{8p\tilde{s}}|\bar{y}|^{2}+\frac{C_{3}|\bar{y}|^{3}}{6\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{C\log\tilde{s}}{\tilde{s}^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\forall|\bar{y}|\leq\frac{3\kappa}{8C_{3}p}\sqrt{\tilde{s}},\quad w_{\mathcal{B}}(\bar{y},\tilde{s})\leq w_{\mathcal{B}}(0,\tilde{s})-\frac{\kappa}{16p\tilde{s}}|\bar{y}|^{2}.

(3.17)

We claim from (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) the following:

\left|w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,\tilde{s})\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}},

(3.18)

Indeed, if
w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,\tilde{s})\geq 0
, then we have by (
3.17
) and (
3.15
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,\tilde{s})

\displaystyle\leq w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\tilde{s}},\tilde{s})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}}.

If
w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}))}(0,\tilde{s})\leq 0
, then we do as above and use (
3.14
) instead of (
3.15
) to obtain (
3.18
).

From (
3.18
), (
3.14
) and (
3.17
), we get

\frac{\kappa}{16p\tilde{s}}|\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\tilde{s}}|^{2}\leq w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(0,\tilde{s})-w_{\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(\tilde{x}))}(\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\tilde{s}},\tilde{s})\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{3}{2}+C_{0}}.

Hence, we obtain

|\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\tilde{s}}|^{2}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}\tilde{s}^{\frac{5}{2}+C_{0}}.

(3.19)

From the definition (
3.12
), we have

|\bar{y}_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h}),\tilde{s}}|=e^{\frac{\tilde{s}}{2}}d(\Gamma(\tilde{x}),\pi_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})}),

(3.20)

where we recall
\pi_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})}
is the tangent plan of
S
at
\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})
. In the other hand, we claim that

d(\Gamma(\tilde{x}),T_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})})\geq\frac{|\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})-\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|^{2}}},

(3.21)

where
S_{i}
is the surface of equation
x_{i}=\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})
,
T_{i,\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})}
is the tangent plan of
S_{i}
at
\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})
. Indeed, we note that

d(\Gamma(\tilde{x}),T_{i,\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})})=\frac{|\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})-\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|^{2}}},

and

, hence, (
3.21
) follows from
d(\Gamma(\tilde{x}),T_{\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})})\geq d(\Gamma(\tilde{x}),T_{i,\Gamma(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})})
.

Combining (
3.19
), (
3.20
), (
3.21
) together with the relation
\tilde{s}=-\log(T-\tilde{t})
yields

|\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})-\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|^{2}\leq C(T-\tilde{t})^{\frac{3}{2}}|\log(T-\tilde{t})|^{\frac{5}{2}+C_{0}}.

If we denote

, then we have by the relation (

),

|\log(T-\tilde{t})|\sim 2|\log A|,\quad T-\tilde{t}\sim\frac{A^{2}}{2|\log A|}\quad\text{as}\quad A\to 0.

Hence,

|\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x}+\tilde{h})-\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})-\tilde{h}\cdot\nabla\gamma_{i}(\tilde{x})|^{2}\leq CA^{3}|\log A|^{1+C_{0}}\leq C|\tilde{h}|^{3}|\log|\tilde{h}||^{1+C_{0}},

which yields (
2.25
). This concludes the proof of Proposition
2.8
.
\square

3.3.
A geometric constraint linking the blow-up behavior of the solution to the regularity of the blow-up set.

This section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
2.7
. The proof follows ideas given in
[
Zaa06
]
. Recall from the hypothesis that
\gamma_{a}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha^{*}}((-\epsilon_{a},\epsilon_{a})^{N-\ell},\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
for some
\alpha^{*}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\epsilon_{a}>0
, and that
\gamma_{a,i}(0)=\nabla\gamma_{a,i}(0)=0
, we have for all
|\tilde{\xi}|<\epsilon_{a}
,

|\gamma_{a,i}(\tilde{\xi})|\leq C|\tilde{\xi}|^{1+\alpha^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\gamma_{a,i}(\tilde{\xi})|\leq C|\tilde{\xi}|^{\alpha^{*}}.

(3.22)

In what follows,
k\in\{\ell+1,\cdots,N\}
is fixed, and we use indexes

and
m
for the range
1,\cdots,\ell
, index

for the range
\ell+1,\cdots,N
.

We now use (
3.22
) to approximate all the terms appearing in (
2.22
).

(a)
Term
\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\eta_{i}(b)
. From the local coordinates (
2.23
), we have

\eta_{i}(b)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\gamma_{a,i}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})|^{2}}}\left(\eta_{i}(a)-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{N}\frac{\partial\gamma_{a,i}}{\partial\xi_{j}}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\tau_{j}(a)\right).

Using (
3.22
) and the fact that
\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\eta_{i}(a)=0
and
\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\tau_{j}(a)=\delta_{k,j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\eta_{i}(b)+\frac{\partial\gamma_{a,i}}{\partial\xi_{k}}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\gamma_{a,i}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})|^{2}}}\right)\frac{\partial\gamma_{a,i}}{\partial\xi_{k}}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{a,i}}{\partial\xi_{k}}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\right||\nabla\gamma_{a,i}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\partial\gamma_{a,i}}{\partial\xi_{k}}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\right|e^{-\alpha^{*}s}.

(3.23)

(b)
Term
\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\tau_{j}(b)
. From (
2.23
) and (
3.22
), we have

|b-a|\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\gamma_{a,i}(e^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{y})\right|+e^{-\frac{s}{2}}|\tilde{y}|\leq Ce^{-\frac{s}{2}}.

Since
\eta_{i}
and
\tau_{j}
are
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha^{*}}
, it holds that

|\eta_{i}(a)-\eta_{i}(b)|+|\tau_{j}(a)-\tau_{j}(b)|\leq C|a-b|^{\alpha^{*}}\leq Ce^{-\alpha^{*}\frac{s}{2}}.

This follows that

\begin{array}[]{c}|\eta_{i}(a)\cdot\eta_{m}(b)-\delta_{i,m}|+|\tau_{k}(a)\cdot\tau_{j}(b)-\delta_{k,j}|\leq Ce^{-\alpha^{*}\frac{s}{2}},\\
|\eta_{i}(a)\cdot\tau_{j}(b)|+|\eta_{i}(b)\cdot\tau_{j}(a)|\leq Ce^{-\alpha^{*}\frac{s}{2}}.\end{array}

(3.24)

(c)
The point
Y(a,y,s)
. Using (
2.18
), (
2.21
) and (
2.23
), we write

\displaystyle Y_{m}

(3.25)

(d)

3.4.
Further refined asymptotic behavior.

We prove Proposition

Lemma 3.4

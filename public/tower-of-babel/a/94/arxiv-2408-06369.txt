Calculation of the Relaxation Modulus in the Andrade Model by Using the Laplace Transform
Juan Luis González-Santander 1, , Giorgio Spada 2 , Francesco Mainardi 3 and Alexander Apelblat 4

Abstract
In the framework of the theory of linear viscoelasticity, we derive an analytical expression of the relaxation modulus in the Andrade model G α ​ ( t ) G_{\alpha}\left(t\right) for the case of rational parameter α = m / n ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha=m/n\in(0,1) in terms of Mittag–Leffler functions from its Laplace transform G ~ α ​ ( s ) \tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right) . It turns out that the expression obtained can be rewritten in terms of Rabotnov functions. Moreover, for the original parameter α = 1 / 3 \alpha=1/3 in the Andrade model, we obtain an expression in terms of Miller-Ross functions. The asymptotic behaviours of G α ​ ( t ) G_{\alpha}\left(t\right) for t → 0 + t\rightarrow 0^{+} and t → + ∞ t\rightarrow+\infty are also derived applying the Tauberian theorem. The analytical results obtained have been numerically checked by solving the Volterra integral equation satisfied by G α ​ ( t ) G_{\alpha}\left(t\right) by using a successive approximation approach, as well as computing the inverse Laplace transform of G ~ α ​ ( s ) \tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right) by using Talbot’s method.



1
The Andrade Model in Linear Viscoelasticity

In the framework of linear viscoelasticity theory, a “transient” phase of
deformation occurs right after the elastic response in creep phenomena and
is marked by a strain rate that changes over time
[
1
]
. Among
the rheological laws that exhibit a transient phase, the Andrade model has
been effectively used to describe the behavior of various materials. This
model was initially introduced by Andrade in 1910 to describe the elongation
of metallic wires under constant tensile stress
[
2
]
. Its main
feature is a transient that exhibits a fractional power function time
dependence
\sim
t^{\alpha}
. In their empirical stress–strain relationship,
Andrade proposed the exponent
\alpha=1/3

[
3
]
.
Nevertheless, later laboratory investigations have shown that values within
the range of
0<\alpha<1
are indeed possible for certain
materials
[
4
]
. It is worth noting that during the last dozen years, there has been an increasing interest in the Andrade model
[
5
,
6
]
. Using the modern formulation of the Andrade
model
[
7
]
, the creep compliance
J_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
(i.e., the
strain per unit stress) is given by

J_{\alpha}\left(t\right)=J_{U}+\beta t^{\alpha}+\frac{t}{\eta},\quad t\geq 0,

(1)

where
J_{U}
is the unrelaxed compliance,
\eta
is the steady state
Newtonian viscosity,
\beta
is the magnitude of the inelastic
contribution, and
\alpha
represents the frequency of the compliance. The
number of free parameters that appear in (
1
) can be
reduced by adopting a useful parametrization given in
[
8
]
, whose
validity is discussed in
[
4
]
. Essentially, this parametrization
performs the following change of variables:

J_{U}=\frac{1}{\mu},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{\mu\,\tau^{\alpha}},\quad\eta=\mu\,\tau,

(2)

thus, we obtain

J_{\alpha}\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{\mu}\left[1+\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha}+\frac{t}{\tau}\right],\quad t\geq 0,

(3)

where the parameters
\mu,\tau>0
have a clear and important physical
meaning. The aim of this paper is to analytically calculate in closed form
the relaxation modulus
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
(i.e., the stress per
unit strain) from the creep compliance
J_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
given
in (
3
) by using the inverse Laplace transform.

It is worth noting that the computation of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)

from
J_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
is also possible by numerically solving
the following Volterra integral equation of the second kind (
[
9
]

[Eqn. 2.87]
):

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)=\mu\left[1-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dJ_{\alpha}\left(t^{\prime}\right)}{dt^{\prime}}G_{\alpha}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)dt^{\prime}\right],

(4)

just as has been done for other models such as that of Jeffrey-Lomnitz
rheological law
[
10
]
. Another numerical approach is to
obtain the relaxation modulus in the Laplace domain

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\mathcal{L}\left[G_{\alpha}\left(t\right);s\right]
,
and then numerically evaluate the inverse Laplace
transform to obtain
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
. However, an analytical
solution is more desirable since it shows the role and weight of the model
parameters explicitly. In addition, analytical approaches are generally more
computationally efficient. In the present approach, we just analytically
calculate the inverse Laplace transform of
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
. This result has been applied by some of the authors in

[
11
]
, and here we provide the mathematical details of the calculation.

Next, we derive
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
in the Andrade model.
For this purpose, apply the Laplace transform to (
3
) in order
to obtain

\mathcal{L}\left[J_{\alpha}\left(t\right);s\right]=\tilde{J}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{\mu\,s^{2}}\left[s+\Gamma\left(1+\alpha\right)\tau^{-\alpha}s^{1-\alpha}+\frac{1}{\tau}\right].

(5)

However, since the following relation is satisfied in any linear
viscoelasticity
rheology (
[
9
]
[Eqn. 2.8])

\tilde{J}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{s^{2}},

(6)

we conclude

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{\mu\,\tau}{s\,\tau+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}+1}.

(7)

As mentioned above, the range of values that are interesting for
\alpha

are between
0
and
1
, so we restrict our study to
\alpha\in(0,1)
. It
is worth noting that (
7
) can also be obtained rewriting the
Volterra integral Equation (
4
) in terms of the Laplace
convolution
product
[
12
]
, i.e.,

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)=\mu\left(1-G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\ast\frac{dJ_{\alpha}\left(t\right)}{dt}\right),

(8)

thus taking the Laplace transform in (
8
) and
solving for
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
, we arrive at (
7
).

The manuscript is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we perform the calculation of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
for
\alpha=1/3
(as first suggested by Andrade) in terms of Miller-Ross functions.
Section
3
generalizes this result for
rational
\alpha
in terms of a finite sum of Mittag–Leffler functions,
which in turn can be expressed as a linear combination of Rabotnov
functions. It is worth highlighting that although in principle
\alpha
can be a real number, the value it actually acquires in rheological models is a positive fractional number less than unity. Section
4
calculates the asymptotic
behaviour of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
for
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
t\rightarrow+\infty
by using the Tauberian theorem. Section
5
shows some numerical verifications on the expressions of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
derived in the previous sections, as well as on
their asymptotic behaviors. Finally, we collect our conclusions in Section
6
.

Laplace Inversion in Terms of Miller-Ross Functions for
\alpha=1/3

Consider in (
7
) the following change of variables:

b=\frac{1}{\tau},\quad c=\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}{\tau^{\alpha}},

(9)

to rewrite
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
as

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{\mu}{b+s+c\,s^{1-\alpha}}.

(10)

In order calculate the inverse Laplace transform of
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
for the case
\alpha=1/3
, i.e.,

\tilde{G}_{1/3}\left(s\right)=\frac{\mu}{b+s+c\,s^{2/3}},

(11)

apply the identity

\left(x+y\right)\left(x^{2}-xy+y^{2}\right)=x^{3}+y^{3},

(12)

taking
x=b+s
, and
y=c\,s^{2/3}

\frac{1}{\mu}\tilde{G}_{1/3}\left(s\right)=b^{2}\underset{\tilde{R}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{\frac{1}{p\left(s\right)}}}+2b\underset{\tilde{Q}\left(s\right)=s\,\tilde{R}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{\,s\frac{1}{p\left(s\right)}}}+\underset{s\,\tilde{Q}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{\frac{s^{2}}{p\left(s\right)}}}-bc\,\underset{\tilde{U}_{2/3}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{\frac{s^{2/3}}{p\left(s\right)}}}-c\,\underset{s\,\tilde{U}_{2/3}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{s\frac{s^{2/3}}{p\left(s\right)}}}+c^{2}\underset{\tilde{U}_{4/3}\left(s\right)}{\underbrace{\frac{s^{4/3}}{p\left(s\right)}}},

(13)

where

p\left(s\right)=s^{3}+\left(3b+c^{3}\right)s^{2}+3b^{2}s+b^{3}=\prod_{k=1}^{3}\left(s-s_{k}\right),

(14)

and
s_{k}
are the roots of the cubic equation
p\left(s\right)=0
, i.e.,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle s_{1}=-\frac{1}{3}\left(M-N-L\right),\\
\displaystyle s_{2}=-\frac{1}{3}\left(M+e^{i\,\pi/3}N+e^{-i\,\pi/3}L\right),\\
\displaystyle s_{3}=-\frac{1}{3}\left(M+e^{-i\,\pi/3}N+e^{i\,\pi/3}L\right),\end{array}

(15)

being

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle M=3b+c^{3},\\
\displaystyle L=\sqrt[3]{\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{3b^{3}c^{6}\left(27b+4c^{3}\right)}-\frac{27}{2}b^{2}c^{3}-9\,bc^{6}-c^{9}},\\
\displaystyle N=\frac{\left(6b+c^{3}\right)c^{3}}{L}.\end{array}

(16)

First, rewrite
\tilde{R}\left(s\right)
as

\tilde{R}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{p\left(s\right)}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{s-s_{k}},\quad\alpha_{k}=\prod_{j\neq k}^{3}\frac{1}{s_{k}-s_{j}}.

(17)

Note that a simple algebraic calculation shows that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{3}\alpha_{k}=0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{3}\alpha_{k}\,s_{k}=0.\end{array}

(18)

Now, define the following functions

R\left(t\right)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{R}\left(s\right);t\right],

(19)

thus,

Q\left(t\right)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{Q}\left(s\right);t\right]=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[s\,\tilde{R}\left(s\right);t\right]=R^{\prime}\left(t\right)+R\left(0\right)\,\delta\left(t\right),

(20)

and

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[s\,\tilde{Q}\left(s\right);t\right]=Q^{\prime}\left(t\right)+Q\left(0\right)\delta\left(t\right)=R^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)+R^{\prime}\left(0\right)\,\delta^{\prime}\left(t\right).

(21)

Furthermore,

U_{\nu}\left(t\right)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{U}_{\nu}\left(s\right);t\right]=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{s^{\nu}}{p\left(s\right)};t\right],

(22)

thus

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[s\,\tilde{U}_{\nu}\left(s\right);t\right]=U_{\nu}^{\prime}\left(t\right)+U_{\nu}\left(0\right)\,\delta\left(t\right).

(23)

Therefore, the inverse Laplace transform of
\tilde{G}_{1/3}\left(s\right)

is given by

\displaystyle G_{1/3}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{G}_{1/3}\left(s\right);t\right]

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\left\{b^{2}\,R\left(t\right)+2b\,\left[R^{\prime}\left(t\right)+R\left(0\right)\,\delta\left(t\right)\right]+R^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)+R^{\prime}\left(0\right)\,\delta^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.bc\,U_{2/3}\left(t\right)-c\left[U_{2/3}^{\prime}\left(t\right)+U_{2/3}\left(0\right)\,\delta\left(t\right)\right]+c^{2}\,U_{4/3}\left(t\right)\right\}.

3
Laplace Inversion in Terms of Rabotnov Functions

Consider (
7
) for the case
\alpha=\frac{m}{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
,

0<m<n,\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N},

(38)

and perform the change of variables
r=\left(s\tau\right)^{1/n}
,

\tilde{G}_{m/n}\left(r\right)=\frac{\mu\,\tau}{\underset{p_{n,m}\left(r\right)}{\underbrace{r^{n}+\Gamma\left(1+\frac{m}{n}\right)r^{n-m}+1}}},

(39)

where
p_{n,m}\left(r\right)
is a polynomial of
n
-th order. If
p_{n,m}\left(r\right)
has non-repeated roots
r_{k}
,
\left(k=1,\ldots,n\right)
, then, according to (
[
15
]
[Eqn. 17:13:10]), we have

\frac{1}{p_{n,m}\left(r\right)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)\left(r-r_{k}\right)},

(40)

thus

\tilde{G}_{m/n}\left(r\right)=\mu\,\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)\left(r-r_{k}\right)},

(41)

and

\tilde{G}_{m/n}\left(s\right)=\mu\,\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)\left(s^{1/n}\tau^{1/n}-r_{k}\right)}.

(42)

Define,

\displaystyle f_{m/n}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{G}_{m/n}\left(s/\tau\right);t\right]

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s^{1/n}-r_{k}}\right],

and apply (
[
15
]
[Eqn. 45:14:4])

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{s^{\mu-\nu}}{s^{\mu}-a};t\right]=t^{\nu-1}\,\mathrm{E}_{\mu,\nu}\left(a\,t^{\mu}\right),

(44)

where
\mathrm{E}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(z\right)
denotes the
two-parameter Mittag–Leffler function (
[
15
]
[Eqn. 45:14:2]),

\mathrm{E}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma\left(\beta+\alpha\,k\right)}.

(45)

Therefore, for
\mu=\nu=\frac{1}{n}
, we have

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s^{1/n}-a};t\right]=t^{1/n-1}\mathrm{E}_{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}}\left(a\,t^{1/n}\right).

(46)

Insert (
46
) in (
43
) to arrive at

f_{m/n}\left(t\right)=\mu\,\tau\,t^{1/n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{E}_{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}}\left(r_{k}\,t^{1/n}\right)}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)}.

(47)

Finally, apply the property (
[
13
]
[Eqn. 1.1.1(3)]),

\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{F}\left(s\right);t\right]=F\left(t\right)\ \leftrightarrow\ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{F}\left(a\,s\right);t\right]=\frac{1}{a}F\left(\frac{t}{a}\right),

(48)

to obtain

G_{m/n}\left(t\right)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{G}_{m/n}\left(s\right);t\right]=\frac{1}{\tau}\,f_{m/n}\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right),

(49)

i.e.,

G_{m/n}\left(t\right)=\mu\,\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{1/n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{E}_{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}}\left(r_{k}\,\left(t/\tau\right)^{1/n}\right)}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)},\quad t\geq 0,

(50)

where remember that
r_{k}
are the
n
non-repeated roots of the
polynomial:

p_{n,m}\left(r\right)=r^{n}+\Gamma\left(1+\frac{m}{n}\right)r^{n-m}+1.

(51)

Note that the solution is a linear combination of Rabotnov functions (
[
9
]
[Eqn. E.46]):

\mathrm{R}_{\nu}\left(\mu,t\right)=t^{\nu}\,\mathrm{E}_{\nu+1,\nu+1}\left(\mu\,t^{\nu+1}\right),

(52)

thereby,

G_{m/n}\left(t\right)=\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{R}_{1/n-1}\left(r_{k},t/\tau\right)}{p_{n,m}^{\prime}\left(r_{k}\right)}.

(53)

For the particular case
\alpha=m/n=1/3
, we have

G_{1/3}^{\mathrm{R}}\left(t\right)=\mu\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{\mathrm{R}_{-2/3}\left(r_{k},t/\tau\right)}{2\,\Gamma\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)r_{k}+3\,r_{k}^{2}},\quad t\geq 0,

(54)

where the superscript
\mathrm{R}
takes into account that
G_{1/3}\left(t\right)
is given in terms of Rabotnov functions, and
r_{k}
are the
three distinct roots of the cubic equation,

r^{3}+\Gamma\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)r^{2}+1=0,

(55)

i.e.,

\begin{array}[]{l}r_{1}\approx-1.40184,\\
r_{2}\approx 0.254432-0.805364\,i,\\
r_{3}\approx 0.254432+0.805364\,i.\end{array}

(56)

4
Asymptotic Behaviour via Tauberian Theorem

Next, we will obtain the asymptotic behaviour of the relaxation modulus
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and as
t\rightarrow+\infty
from its Laplace transform
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)

by using the following version of the Tauberian theorem, (for other version of the Tauberian theorem, see
[
16
]
).

Theorem 1

Consider that the Laplace transform of a function
f\left(t\right)
is
given by
\tilde{f}\left(s\right)=\mathcal{L}\left[f\left(t\right);s\right]
. The asymptotic behaviour of
\tilde{f}\left(s\right)
as
s\rightarrow+\infty
is given by

\tilde{f}\left(s\right)\approx\mathcal{L}\left[g\left(t\right);s\right],\quad s\rightarrow+\infty,

(57)

where
g\left(t\right)
is the asymptotic behaviour of
f\left(t\right)

as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Furthermore, the asymptotic behaviour of
\tilde{f}\left(s\right)
as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
is given by

\tilde{f}\left(s\right)\approx\mathcal{L}\left[h\left(t\right);s\right],\quad s\rightarrow 0^{+},

(58)

where
h\left(t\right)
is the asymptotic behaviour of
f\left(t\right)

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof.
On the one hand, consider that the asymptotic behaviour of
f\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
is
given by

f\left(t\right)\approx g\left(t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\,t^{b_{k}},\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+},

(59)

with
N=0,1,2,\ldots
, and
0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{N}
. Apply the
Laplace transform to (
59
) in order to obtain

\tilde{f}\left(s\right)=\mathcal{L}\left[f\left(t\right);s\right]\approx\mathcal{L}\left[g\left(t\right);s\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\,\mathcal{L}\left[t^{b_{k}};s\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{a_{k}\,\Gamma\left(b_{k}+1\right)}{s^{b_{k}+1}}.

Therefore, we obtain the asymptotic behaviour of
\tilde{f}\left(s\right)

as
s\rightarrow+\infty
,

\tilde{f}\left(s\right)\approx\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{a_{k}\,\Gamma\left(b_{k}+1\right)}{s^{b_{k}+1}},\quad s\rightarrow+\infty,

as we wanted to prove.

On the other hand, consider that the asymptotic behaviour of
\tilde{f}\left(s\right)
as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
is given by

\tilde{f}\left(s\right)\approx\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\,s^{d_{k}},\quad s\rightarrow 0^{+},

(60)

with
N=0,1,2,\ldots
, and
0\leq d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{N}
. Apply the
inverse Laplace transformto (
60
) in order to obtain

f\left(t\right)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\tilde{f}\left(s\right);t\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[s^{d_{k}};t\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{c_{k}}{\Gamma\left(-d_{k}\right)\,t^{d_{k}+1}}\,,

Therefore, we obtain the asymptotic behaviour of
f\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
,

f\left(t\right)\approx h\left(t\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{c_{k}}{\Gamma\left(-d_{k}\right)\,t^{d_{k}+1}},\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,

as we wanted to prove.

4.1
Asymptotic Behaviour for
t\rightarrow+\infty

We know that the Laplace transform of the relaxation modulus in the Andrademodel is (
7
)

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{\mu\,\tau}{s\,\tau+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}+1},\quad\alpha\in\left(0,1\right).

(61)

Since

\frac{1}{1-x}=1+x+x^{2}+\cdots,\quad\left|x\right|<1,

(62)

we have that

\frac{1}{1+x}\approx 1-x,\quad x\rightarrow 0^{+},

(63)

thus, taking
x=s\,\tau+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}
, we get

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)\approx\mu\,\tau\left[1-s\,\tau-\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}\right],\quad s\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(64)

Since
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
,

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)\approx\mu\,\tau\left[1-\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\,\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}\right],\quad s\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(65)

According to (
58
), the asymptotic behaviour of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
is calculated as

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mu\,\tau\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[1-\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\,\left(s\,\tau\right)^{1-\alpha};t\right]

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,\tau\,\delta\left(t\right)-\mu\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha-1\right)}\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-2}.

Applying the factorial property of the Gamma function,
\Gamma\left(z+1\right)=z\,\Gamma\left(z\right)
, and taking into account that
\delta\left(t\right)=0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, we conclude that

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\,\alpha\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-2},\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

(67)

Taking more terms in the expansion of
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)

as
s\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we can calculate more terms of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
by using (
58
).
Thereby, we obtain

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\,\alpha\left[\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha-2}-2\,\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(1+\alpha\right)}{\Gamma\left(2\alpha-1\right)}\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{2\alpha-3}+\cdots\right],\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

(68)

For the particular case
\alpha=\frac{1}{3}
in (
67
), we
have

G_{1/3}\left(t\right)\approx\frac{2}{9}\,\mu\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{-5/3},\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

(69)

As a consistency test, we can obtain (
69
) from the the
expression given in (
50
) for
G_{m/n}\left(t\right)

with
m=1
and
n=3
, and the asymptotic formula (
[
17
]
[Eqn. 18.1(22)]),

\mathrm{E}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(z\right)\approx-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{z^{-k}}{\Gamma\left(\beta-\alpha k\right)}+O\left(\left|z\right|^{-N}\right),\quad z\rightarrow\infty,\quad\left|\arg\left(-z\right)\right|<\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\pi.

(70)

4.2
Asymptotic Behaviour for
t\rightarrow 0^{+}

Rewrite the Laplace transform of the relaxation modulus (
61
), as
follows:

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)=\frac{\mu}{s}\left[\frac{1}{1+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-\alpha}+\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-1}}\right].

(71)

Note that, for
a\in\left(0,1\right)
(thus
1-a\in\left(0,1\right)
),
and
A\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1+A\,y^{-a}+y^{-1}}{1+A\,y^{-a}}=\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{y+A\,y^{1-a}+1}{y+A\,y\,^{1-a}}=1,

(72)

thus, taking
A=\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)
,
a=\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
, and
y=s\tau\rightarrow+\infty
, (i.e.,
s\rightarrow+\infty
, since
\tau>0
), we get

1+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-\alpha}+\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-1}\approx 1+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-\alpha},\quad s\rightarrow+\infty.

(73)

Apply (
73
) to (
71
), in order to obtain

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)\approx\frac{\mu}{s}\left[\frac{1}{1+\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(s\,\tau\right)^{-\alpha}}\right],\quad s\rightarrow+\infty.

(74)

Now, perform the change of variables
x=1/z
in (
63
),

\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{z}}\approx 1-\frac{1}{z},\quad z\rightarrow+\infty,

(75)

and take
z=\frac{\left(s\,\tau\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}\rightarrow+\infty
(i.e.,
s\rightarrow+\infty
), to arrive at

\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)\approx\mu\left[\frac{1}{s}-\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}{\tau^{\alpha}s\,^{\alpha+1}}\right],\quad s\rightarrow+\infty.

(76)

According to (
57
), the asymptotic behaviour of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
is calculated as

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s}-\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}{\tau^{\alpha}s\,^{\alpha+1}};t\right],

(77)

i.e.,

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\left[1-\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha}\right],\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(78)

Again, taking more terms in the expansion of
\tilde{G}_{\alpha}\left(s\right)
as
s\rightarrow+\infty
, we can calculate more terms of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
by using (
57
). Thereby, we obtain

G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\left[1-\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{\alpha}+\,\frac{\Gamma^{2}\left(1+\alpha\right)}{\Gamma\left(2\alpha-1\right)}\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{2\alpha}+\cdots\right],\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(79)

Note that the particular case
\alpha=\frac{1}{3}
in (
78
)
yields

G_{1/3}\left(t\right)\approx\mu\left[1-\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{1/3}\right],\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(80)

As a consistency test, we can obtain (
80
) from the the
expression given in (
50

G_{m/n}\left(t\right)

with
m=1
and

thus,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)=\mu.

(82)

According to (

G_{\alpha}\left(x\right)\approx\mu

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)

5.1
Volterra Integral Equation

The quadrature formulas to numerically solve the Volterra integral equation

[
18
]
that satisfies
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
, i.e., Equation
(
81
), fail because from (
80
), recalling that
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
and
\mu,\tau>0
, we have that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{dG_{\alpha}\left(t\right)}{dt}=-\infty.

(85)

However, we can apply a successive approximation method in order to
numerically compute
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
(
[
19
]
[Sect. 2.1]).
This method states that if we have the Volterra integral equation of the
second kind

u\left(t\right)=f\left(t\right)+\int_{0}^{t}K\left(x,t\right)\,u\left(x\right)\,dx,

(86)

we take as zeroth approximation

u^{\left(0\right)}\left(t\right)=f\left(t\right),

(87)

and for the successive approximations
j=1,2,\ldots

u^{\left(j\right)}\left(t\right)=f\left(t\right)+\int_{0}^{t}K\left(x,t\right)\,u^{\left(j-1\right)}\left(x\right)\,dx.

(88)

Figure

in (

)), for
\mu=\tau=1
and
\alpha=1/3
. It is apparent
that as the order of approximation increases, we get a better approximation

G_{1/3}\left(t\right)
obtained in (

) or (

compared to the
analytical solution

).

6
Conclusions

Considering the Andrade model in linear viscoelasticity, we have derived for
the first time an analytical expression for the relaxation modulus in the
time domain
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
considering a rational parameter
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
in terms of Mittag–Leffler functions (or
equivalently, as a linear combination of Rabotnov functions). For the
original parameter
\alpha=1/3
of the Andrade model, we have derived a
particular expression for
G_{1/3}\left(t\right)
in terms of Miller-Ross
functions. It turns out that this last expression is numerically more
efficient (approximately twice faster) than the equivalent one in terms of
Rabotnov functions.

Furthermore, we have obtained the asymptotic behaviour of
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
for
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
t\rightarrow+\infty
using the
Tauberian theorem. We have derived the same expression for the asymptotic
behaviour as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
by using the Volterra integral equation of
the second kind that
G_{\alpha}\left(t\right)
satisfies.

Finally, numerical computations for particular values of the parameters have
been performed in order to verify the analytical solutions obtained. For
this purpose, we have used Talbot’s method for the numerical computation of
the inverse Laplace transform, and the method of successive approximations
for the numerical evaluation of the Volterra integral equation of the second
kind.

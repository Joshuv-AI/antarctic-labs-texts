Convergence analysis of the adaptive stochastic collocation finite element method Thanks: Acknowledgements. The work of the first author was supported by the EPSRC grant EP/W010925/1.
Alex Bespalov, Andrey Savinov

Abstract
. This paper is focused on the convergence analysis of an adaptive stochastic collocation algorithm for the stationary diffusion equation with parametric coefficient. The algorithm employs sparse grid collocation in the parameter domain alongside finite element approximations in the spatial domain, and adaptivity is driven by recently proposed parametric and spatial a posteriori error indicators. We prove that for a general diffusion coefficient with finite-dimensional parametrization, the algorithm drives the underlying error estimates to zero. Thus, our analysis covers problems with affine and nonaffine parametric coefficient dependence.

1.
Introduction

Sparse grid stochastic collocation is an established and well-studied computational method
for solving high-dimensional parametric partial differential equations (PDEs)
that are ubiquitous in uncertainty quantification models.
The sparsity of the underlying set of collocation points is critical for this task even for moderately high-dimensional problems,
as basic tensor-product grids of collocation points yield approximations suffering from the curse of dimensionality;
see, for example,

[
BNT07
,
NTW08b
,
NTW08a
,
Bie11
,
BTNT12
,
NTT16
,
EST18
,
DuNSZ23
]
.
Furthermore, in a typical setting of a high-dimensional parametric PDE, the solution is anisotropic in the parameter domain,
calling for adaptive enrichment of sparse grid collocation points.

Adaptively generated sparse grids trace back to the work of Gerstner and Griebel
[
GG03
]
on high-dimensional
quadrature
.
Their ideas have found successful applications to collocation methods for parametric PDEs,
see, for example,
[
CCS14
,
NTTT16
]
,
where parametric adaptivity is driven by heuristic error indicators that require solving additional PDEs.
An alternative approach, proposed in
[
GN18
]
, is based on a posteriori error estimation.
Here, a reliable residual-based a posteriori error estimator is derived to control two distinct sources of discretization error
arising from parametric (sparse grid collocation) and spatial (finite element) components of approximations.
Crucially, this error estimator avoids the solution of additional PDEs;
it is also localizable and, thus, can be readily used in an adaptive algorithm.
In particular, the parametric component of the error estimator is used in
[
GN18
]
to design an algorithm
that generates adaptive sparse grid (semidiscrete) approximations.
The convergence analysis of a modified version of the adaptive algorithm in
[
GN18
]
is performed in
[
EEST22
]
.
In
[
FS21
]
, the authors extended the adaptive algorithm proposed in
[
GN18
]
to include spatial (finite element) adaptivity
and proved convergence of the resulting ‘fully adaptive’ algorithm.

It is important to note that the a posteriori error estimation framework developed in
[
GN18
]

and, hence, the adaptive algorithms in
[
GN18
,
EEST22
,
FS21
]
, are inherently restricted to parametric PDEs whose inputs
have
affine
dependence on parameters; cf.
[
GN18
, section 4]
.
In our recent work
[
BSX22
]
, we proposed a novel a posteriori error estimation strategy
for finite element-based sparse grid stochastic collocation approximations.
This error estimation strategy is applicable
to general elliptic parametric PDEs
with either affine or nonaffine parametric dependence of inputs.
It is similar in spirit to the hierarchical error estimation framework proposed in the context of stochastic Galerkin finite element methods,
see
[
BPS14
,
BS16
,
BPRR19a
,
BPRR19b
,
BX20
]
.

In this contribution, we bridge a gap in the existing theory of adaptive algorithms for stochastic collocation finite element methods (SC-FEMs)
by extending the convergence analysis in
[
EEST22
,
FS21
]
to a broader class of parametric elliptic PDEs that covers
problems with
nonaffine
parametric coefficients.
Specifically, we study convergence of an adaptive algorithm guided by reliable a posteriori error estimates
and the associated error indicators proposed in
[
BSX22
]
.
We note that the adaptive algorithm considered in this work is slightly different from the one proposed in
[
BSX22
]
;
the difference lies in how the algorithm performs parametric marking and enrichment (see Remark
8
).
Our main result in Theorem
15
shows that the modified adaptive algorithm generates
SC-FEM approximations, with the corresponding sequence of error estimates converging to zero.
A key ingredient of our analysis is a certain summability property of Taylor coefficients for semidiscrete (finite element) approximations.
We prove that this summability property holds in the case of affine-parametric coefficients
satisfying the uniform ellipticity assumption (see Lemma
1
).
Furthermore, for PDEs with general parametric coefficients, we show that the assumptions guaranteeing the analyticity of the exact solution
in the parameter domain also ensure the required summability property of Taylor coefficients for semidiscrete approximations
(see Lemma
2
and Remark
3
).

The outline of the paper is as follows.
After introducing the parametric model problem in section
2
,
we set up its stochastic collocation discretization in section
3
.
Section
4
focuses on the summability property of Taylor coefficients for semidiscrete (finite element) approximations.
In section
5
, we recall the main components of the a posteriori error estimation strategy
developed in
[
BSX22
]
and present the adaptive SC-FEM algorithm.
Sections
6
and
7
focus on proving convergence of parametric and spatial
error estimates for SC-FEM approximations generated by the adaptive algorithm.
In section
8
, we formulate and prove the main result of this work.
The results of numerical experiments are presented and discussed in section
9
.

2.
Parametric model problem

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\;(d=2,3)
be a bounded Lipschitz domain with
polytopal
boundary
\partial D
;
we will refer to
D
as the
spatial
domain.
Let us also introduce the
parameter
domain

\Gamma:=\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\ldots\times\Gamma_{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
where
M\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\Gamma_{m}
(
m=1,\ldots,M
) is a bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\pi(\mathbf{y}):=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\pi_{m}(y_{m})
be a probability measure on
(\Gamma,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma))
;
here,

\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\Gamma
, and

\pi_{m}
denotes a probability measure on
(\Gamma_{m},\mathcal{B}(\Gamma_{m}))
for
m=1,\ldots,M
.

We consider the following parametric elliptic problem:
find
u:\overline{D}\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})\nabla u(\cdot,\mathbf{y}))

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in $D$},

\displaystyle u(\cdot,\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on $\partial D$}

\pi
-almost everywhere on
\Gamma
(i.e., almost surely).
Here, the forcing term
f\in L^{2}(D)
is deterministic and
the coefficient
a
is a random field on
(\Gamma,\mathcal{B}(\Gamma),\pi)
over
L^{\infty}(D)
.
We assume that the coefficient
a
is positive and bounded, i.e.,

(2)

0<a_{\min}\leq\operatorname*{ess\;inf}_{x\in D}a(x,\mathbf{y})\leq\operatorname*{ess\;sup}_{x\in D}a(x,\mathbf{y})\leq a_{\max}<\infty\quad\text{$\pi$-a.e. on $\Gamma$}

with some positive constants
a_{\min}
,
a_{\max}
.
This assumption implies the norm equivalence for the Sobolev space on the spatial domain:
for any
v\in\mathbb{X}:=H^{1}_{0}(D)
there holds

(3)

a_{\min}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq\|a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq a_{\max}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\quad\text{$\pi$-a.e. on $\Gamma$}.

For the purpose of finding the numerical solution to the parametric problem (
1
),
we write it in the following weak form:
given
f\in L^{2}(D)
, find
u:\Gamma\to\mathbb{X}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{D}a(x,\mathbf{y})\nabla u(x,\mathbf{y})\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{D}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{X},\ \text{$\pi$-a.e. on $\Gamma$}.

The above assumptions on the problem data
ensure
the existence and uniqueness of the
solution
u

in the Bochner space
\mathbb{V}:=L_{\pi}^{p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{X})
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
;
see
[
BNT07
, Lemma 1.1]
for details.

3.
Stochastic collocation finite element method

For the numerical solution of problem (
1
) we apply
the stochastic collocation finite element method.
Let us recall the main ideas, including the construction of the underlying approximation spaces.

We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
a mesh on the spatial domain
D

(i.e., a conforming
partition of
D
into compact nondegenerate simplices
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
),
and let
\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}
denote the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
.

Here and throughout the paper, we use
\bullet
as a placeholder for the iteration counter;
see, e.g.,
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
in Algorithm
6
.

For mesh refinement, we employ newest vertex bisection (NVB); see, e.g.,
[
Ste08
,
KPP13
]
.
We assume that any mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
employed for the spatial discretization
is obtained by (uniform or local) refinement of a given (coarse) initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
For the numerical solution of (
4
),
we employ the space
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
of continuous piecewise linear functions,

\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}:=\mathcal{S}^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}):=\{v\in\mathbb{X}:v|_{T}\text{ is affine for all }T\in\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}\}\subset\mathbb{X}=H^{1}_{0}(D).

In particular,
\mathbb{X}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{0}).

The standard basis of
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
is given by

\{\varphi_{\bullet,\xi}:\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}\setminus\partial D\}
, where

\varphi_{\bullet,\xi}
denotes the hat function associated with the vertex
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet}
be the mesh obtained by uniform NVB refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
, i.e.,

\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet}
is the coarsest mesh obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
such that:
(i) for
d=2
, all elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
are refined by three bisections (see, e.g.,
[
BPRR19a
, Figure 1]
);
(ii) for
d=3
,
all elements are refined as described in
[
EGP20
, section 2.1]
and illustrated in
[
EGP20
, Figures 2 and 3]
.
Then,
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\bullet}
denotes the set of vertices of
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet}
,
and
\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}^{+}:=(\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\bullet}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\bullet})\setminus\partial D

is the set of new interior vertices created by this refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
.
The finite element space associated with
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet}
is denoted as

\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}:=\mathcal{S}^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet})
,
and

\{\widehat{\varphi}_{\bullet,\xi}:\xi\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\bullet}\setminus\partial D\}
is the corresponding basis of hat functions.

Let
\mathbf{z}
be a fixed point in
\Gamma
.
We denote by
u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
the Galerkin finite element approximation satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{D}a(x,\mathbf{z})\nabla u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(x)\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{D}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}.

Hence, given a finite set
\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
of collocation points in
\Gamma
,
the SC-FEM approximation of the solution
u
to parametric problem (
1
) is given by

(6)

u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}(x,\mathbf{y}):=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}}u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(x)L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y}),

where
\{L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}\}
is a set of

multivariate
Lagrange basis functions constructed for the set of collocation points
\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
and satisfying

L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}
,
\forall\mathbf{z},\,\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
.

Note that the SC-FEM solution considered in this work
follows the so-called
single-level
construction that employs the same finite element space
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}

for all collocation points
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
(cf.
[
BNT07
,
NTW08b
,
GN18
,
BSX22
]
).
This is in contrast to the
multilevel
SC-FEM approximations that allow

\mathbb{X}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\not=\mathbb{X}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}
for
\mathbf{z}\not=\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
; see, e.g.,
[
LSS20
,
FS21
,
BS23
]
.

In the context of the numerical solution of high-dimensional parametric problems,
the state-of-the-art stochastic collocation methods employ the nodes of
sparse grids
as collocation points.
We briefly describe the construction of sparse grids in the next section.

3.1.
Sparse grid interpolation

Since any finite interval in
\mathbb{R}
can be mapped to
[-1,1]
via appropriate linear transformation,
we can assume without loss of generality that
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{2}=\ldots=\Gamma_{M}=[-1,1]
.
The construction of a sparse grid
\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}\subset\Gamma=[-1,1]^{M}
hinges on three ingredients:

•

a family of
nested sets
of 1D nodes on
[-1,1]

(in this work, we will consider the nested sets of Leja points and Clenshaw–Curtis (CC) quadrature points);

•

a strictly increasing function
\kappa:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying
\kappa(0)=0
,
\kappa(1)=1

(e.g.,

\kappa(i)=i
for Leja points and

\kappa(i)=2^{i-1}+1
,
i>1
for CC
nodes with the doubling rule).

•

a monotone finite set
\Lambda_{\bullet}\,{\subset}\,\mathbb{N}^{M}
of multi-indices;
specifically,

\Lambda_{\bullet}\,{=}\,\{\boldsymbol{\nu}\,{=}\,[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{M}]:\nu_{m}\in\mathbb{N},\,m=1,\ldots,M\}
is such
that
\#\Lambda_{\bullet}<\infty
and

\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}\Longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{m}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}\quad\forall\,m=1,\ldots,M\text{ such that }\nu_{m}>1,

where
(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{m})_{i}=\delta_{mi}
for all
i=1,\ldots,M
.
Note that the monotonicity property of
\Lambda_{\bullet}
implies that
\boldsymbol{1}=[1,1,\dots,1]\in\Lambda_{\bullet}
;

Now, for each
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}
, the set of nodes along the
m
-th coordinate axis in
\mathbb{R}^{M}

is given by the set
\mathcal{Y}_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}
such that
\#\mathcal{Y}_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}=\kappa(\nu_{m})
,
and we define

\mathcal{Y}^{\,(\boldsymbol{\nu})}:=\mathcal{Y}_{1}^{\kappa(\nu_{1})}\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}^{\kappa(\nu_{2})}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{Y}_{M}^{\kappa(\nu_{M})}.

For a given index set
\Lambda_{\bullet}
, the sparse grid
\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}

of collocation points on
\Gamma
is defined as

\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}=\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}:=\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}}\mathcal{Y}^{\,(\boldsymbol{\nu})}.

Let
I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}:C^{0}([-1,1];\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{P}_{\kappa(\nu_{m})-1}([-1,1];\mathbb{X})
be
the standard Lagrange interpolation operator associated with the set of 1D nodes
\mathcal{Y}_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}\subset[-1,1]
.
Here,
\mathbb{P}_{q}
is the set of univariate polynomials of degree at most
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Setting
I_{m}^{0}=0
for all
m=1,\ldots,M
, we define 1D detail operators

\Delta_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}:=I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}-I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m}-1)}.

Now, the sparse grid collocation operator associated with the sparse grid
\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}
is defined as

(7)

S_{\bullet}=S_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}:=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})},

where
\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}):=[\kappa(\nu_{1}),\ldots,\kappa(\nu_{M})]
and

\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}:=\bigotimes_{m=1}^{M}\Delta_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}
is called the hierarchical surplus operator.

The nestedness of 1D node sets and the monotonicity of the index set
\Lambda_{\bullet}

ensure the interpolation property for the operator
S_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}
(cf.
[
BNTT11
]
), i.e.,

(8)

S_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}:C^{0}(\Gamma;\mathbb{X})\to\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{X})\text{\; is such that \;}S_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}v(\mathbf{z})=v(\mathbf{z})\ \;\forall\,\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}},

where
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}}:=\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}}\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-\boldsymbol{1}}
with
\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})-\boldsymbol{1}}:=\bigotimes_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{P}_{\kappa(\nu_{m})-1}
.
Therefore, the SC-FEM solution defined by (
6
) can be written as

(9)

u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}(x,\mathbf{y})=[S_{\bullet}U_{\bullet}](x,\mathbf{y})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}}u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(x)L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y})

with a function
U_{\bullet}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
satisfying
U_{\bullet}(\mathbf{z})=u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}
for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
.

Let
\mathbb{W}\in\big\{\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{\bullet},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}\big\}
.
We introduce a semidiscrete approximation
w:\Gamma\to\mathbb{W}
such that
w(\cdot,\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{W}
satisfies

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{D}a(x,\mathbf{y})\nabla w(x,\mathbf{y})\cdot\nabla v(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{D}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{W},\ \text{$\pi$-a.e. in $\Gamma$}

(for each
\mathbb{W}\in\big\{\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{\bullet},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}\big\}
, the corresponding
semidiscrete approximation satisfying (
10
) will be denoted by

u_{0}^{\rm semi},\,u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}
and
\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}
, respectively).
We assume that the following two representations of
w
hold (cf.
[
GN18
, eq. (12)]
):

(11)

w(x,\mathbf{y}):=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}w_{\mathbf{i}}(x)P_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{y})=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left[\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}w\right]}(x,\mathbf{y})\quad\text{$\pi$-a.e. on $\Gamma$},

where
P_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{y})=\prod_{m=1}^{M}y_{m}^{i_{m}}
and

(12)

w_{\mathbf{i}}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{i}!}\frac{\partial^{\mathbf{i}}w(x,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{i}}}\Big|_{\mathbf{y}=\boldsymbol{0}},\quad\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0}

are the Taylor coefficients.
The following summability property of the Taylor coefficients
w_{\mathbf{i}}(x)
will play a key role in our analysis:
there exists
\boldsymbol{\rho}=[\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}]>\boldsymbol{1}

such that

(13)

\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathbf{i}}\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}\right)_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M})\quad\text{and}\quad\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2\mathbf{i}}\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}\leq C<\infty,

where
C
is independent of the underlying finite element space.
Hereafter, for two vectors
\mathbf{a}=[a_{1},\ldots,a_{M}]\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
\mathbf{b}=[b_{1},\ldots,b_{M}]\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we use the notation

\mathbf{a}^{\mathbf{b}}:=\prod_{m=1}^{M}a_{m}^{b_{m}}
and
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}:=\prod_{m=1}^{M}a_{m}b_{m}
;
we also write
\mathbf{a}>\mathbf{b}
iff
a_{m}>b_{m}
for all
m=1,\dots,M
;
furthermore, for
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
, we denote by
\mathbf{i}!=\prod_{m=1}^{M}i_{m}!
the multivariable factorial.

In the next section, we establish the conditions on the problem data
(specifically, on the coefficient
a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})
in (
1
)) that guarantee the summability property (
13
).

4.
The summability property of Taylor coefficients

We start with the case of the diffusion coefficient
a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})
having affine dependence on the parameters.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that the diffusion coefficient has affine representation, i.e.,

a(x,\mathbf{y})=a_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}a_{m}(x)y_{m}\quad\text{for all $x\in D$ and $\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma=[-1;1]^{M}$}.

If the expansion coefficients
a_{m}\in L^{\infty}(D)
,
m=0,1,\ldots,M
,
satisfy the uniform ellipticity assumption, i.e.,

\exists\,r>0\text{ such that }\ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}|a_{m}(x)|\leq a_{0}(x)-r\quad\forall x\in D,

then inequalities (
2
) hold and the Taylor coefficients
w_{\mathbf{i}}(x)
given by (
12
)
satisfy the summability property (
13
).

Proof.

It is easy to see that the uniform ellipticity assumption implies (
2
);
in particular, there holds

r\leq a_{0}(x)-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}|a_{m}(x)|\leq|a(x,\mathbf{y})|\leq a_{0}(x)+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}|a_{m}(x)|\leq 2a_{0}(x)-r

and therefore,

r\leq a_{\min}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{\max}\leq 2\operatorname*{ess\;\sup}_{x\in D}a_{0}(x)-r.

Now, in order to prove the summability property (
13
)
in the current setting of the affine representation satisfying the uniform ellipticity assumption,
let us define

\alpha:=1-\frac{a_{\min}}{\|a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}}\in(0,1).

Then for any
\boldsymbol{\rho}=[\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}]
with
1<\rho_{m}<\alpha^{-1}
(
m=1,\dots,M
), there holds

\displaystyle\delta:=\bigg\|\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}\rho_{m}|a_{m}|}{a_{0}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}

\displaystyle<\alpha^{-1}\,\bigg\|\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{M}|a_{m}|}{a_{0}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}=\alpha^{-1}\,\bigg\|1-\frac{a_{0}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}|a_{m}|}{a_{0}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\,\bigg(1-\frac{a_{\min}}{\|a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}}\bigg)=1.

This shows that the weighted uniform ellipticity assumption from
[
BCM17
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]

is satisfied (cf.
[
BCM17
, eq. (2.20)]
).
Repeating the arguments in the proof of
[
BCM17
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]
for the Taylor coefficients

w_{\mathbf{i}}
of the semidiscrete approximation
w
(rather than for the Taylor coefficients of the exact solution
u
) proves that

\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathbf{i}}\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}\right)_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0})
and

\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0}}\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathbf{i}}\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{(2-\delta)\|a_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(2-2\delta)\big(\!\operatorname*{ess\;inf}_{x\in D}a_{0}(x)\big)^{3}}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(D)}=:C<\infty,

where
C
is independent of the underlying finite element mesh; cf.
[
BCM17
, eq. (2.22)]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Next, inspired by the analysis in
[
BNT07
]

for a general diffusion coefficient
a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})
,
we identify the assumptions on
a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})
that ensure the summability property (
13
).

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that inequalities (
2
) hold for the diffusion coefficient
a(x,\mathbf{y})

and assume that for every
\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma
, the derivatives of
a(x,\mathbf{y})

with respect to parameters satisfy the following inequalities:

(14)

\left\lVert a^{-1}(\cdot,\mathbf{y})\,\frac{\partial^{\mathbf{k}}a(\cdot,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{k}}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq(2\boldsymbol{\delta})^{-\mathbf{k}}\,\mathbf{k}!\quad\forall\,\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}

with some vector
\boldsymbol{\delta}=[\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{M}]>\boldsymbol{1}.

Then the Taylor coefficients
w_{\mathbf{i}}(x)
given by (
12
)
satisfy the summability property (
13
).

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbb{W}\in\big\{\mathbb{X}_{0},\mathbb{X}_{\bullet},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}\big\}
.
Taking into account the assumptions (
14
) on the diffusion coefficient,
we can repeat the proof of
[
BNT07
, Lemma 3.2]
for
the semidiscrete approximation
w(\cdot,\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{W}
satisfying (
10
)
to make the following two conclusions:

•

the semidiscrete approximation
w
as a function of
\mathbf{y}
admits an analytic extension in the region

\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\left\{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\;\text{dist}(\zeta_{m},\Gamma_{m})\leq\sigma_{m},\;m=1,\dots,M\right\}

for some
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M}]
such that
\boldsymbol{1}<\boldsymbol{\sigma}<\boldsymbol{\delta}
;

•

there holds

\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\|w(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\zeta})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq C_{\rm reg}

with a positive constant
C_{\rm reg}
that depends on the problem data and is independent of the discretization in the spatial domain
(in fact,
C_{\rm reg}
is exactly the same as given in the proof of Lemma 3.2 in
[
BNT07
]
).

Hence, using Cauchy’s integral formula in each
\mathbf{y}
-coordinate, we obtain
for any
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
and for all
\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma
:

\frac{\partial^{\mathbf{i}}w(x,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{i}}}=\frac{\mathbf{i}!}{(2\pi i)^{M}}\int_{\partial B_{\sigma_{M}}(y_{M})}\dots\int_{\partial B_{\sigma_{1}}(y_{1})}\frac{w(x,\boldsymbol{\zeta})}{(\zeta_{1}-y_{1})^{i_{1}+1}\dots(\zeta_{M}-y_{M})^{i_{M}+1}}\;\mathrm{d}\zeta_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\zeta_{M},

where
\partial B_{\sigma_{m}}(y_{m})\subset\mathbb{C}
for each
m=1,\dots,M

denotes the circle of radius
\sigma_{m}
centered at
y_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}
.
Thus, we can estimate the
\mathbb{X}
-norm of the Taylor coefficient of
w
as follows:

\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}=\bigg\|\frac{1}{\mathbf{i}!}\frac{\partial^{\mathbf{i}}w(x,\mathbf{y})}{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{i}}}\Big|_{\mathbf{y}=\boldsymbol{0}}\bigg\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-\mathbf{i}}\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\|w(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\zeta})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq C_{\rm reg}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-\mathbf{i}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}.

Therefore, there exists a vector
\boldsymbol{\rho}=[\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{M}]
such that

\delta_{m}>\sigma_{m}>\rho_{m}>1
for all
m=1,\dots,M
and

\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathbf{i}}\big\|w_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{\mathbb{X}}\right)_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{M}_{0})
.
This proves (
13
) as required.
∎

Remark 3

)

) holds true for every

the exact solution

admits an analytic extension into a region in

due to

5.
Error estimates, error indicators and adaptive algorithm

In this section, we briefly recall the a posteriori error estimation strategy for SC-FEM approximations developed in
[
BSX22
]

as well as the associated error indicators that steer adaptive refinement;
we refer to
[
BSX22
, section 4]
for full details.

We denote by
\|\cdot\|
the norm in the Bochner space
\mathbb{V}=L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{X})
for a fixed
1\leq p\leq\infty

and we define
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{X}}:=\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}(D)}
.
We set
p=2
when computing the norms in
\mathbb{V}=L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma,\mathbb{X})
in practice.
The error estimation strategy developed in
[
BSX22
]
employs a hierarchical construction
(see, e.g.,
[
AO00
, Chapter 5]
).
This construction relies on

an
enhanced
SC-FEM approximation, denoted by
\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}
, and allows one to independently estimate
the spatial and parametric contributions to the overall discretisation error
u-u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}
.
For the specific construction of
\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}
, we follow
[
BSX22
, Remark 1]
:

\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}:=S_{\bullet}\widehat{U}_{\bullet}+\Big(\widehat{S}_{\bullet}\widetilde{U}_{\bullet,0}-S_{\bullet}U_{\bullet,0}\Big),

where

\widehat{S}_{\bullet}=S_{\widehat{\Lambda}_{\bullet}}:=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\widehat{\Lambda}_{\bullet}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})},

\text{$\widehat{U}_{\bullet}:\Gamma\to\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}$ satisfies $\widehat{U}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{z})=\widehat{u}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}$ for all $\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}$},

\text{$\widetilde{U}_{\bullet,0}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{X}_{0}$ is such that
$\widetilde{U}_{\bullet,0}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=u_{0\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}$ for all $\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{\bullet}$},

\text{$U_{\bullet,0}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{X}_{0}$ is such that $U_{\bullet,0}(\mathbf{z})=u_{0\mathbf{z}}$ for all $\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}$}.

Here,
\widehat{u}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\,{\in}\,\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}
denotes the enhanced Galerkin solution satisfying (
5
)
for all
v\,{\in}\,\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}
,
the functions
u_{0\mathbf{z}^{\prime}},\,u_{0\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbb{X}_{0}
solve (
5
) with
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
replaced by
\mathbb{X}_{0}
, and

\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{\bullet}
is the set of collocation points generated by the enriched index set
\widehat{\Lambda}_{\bullet}

that is obtained from the current index set
\Lambda_{\bullet}
by adding its reduced margin

(15)

{\rm R}_{\bullet}={\rm R}({\Lambda_{\bullet}}):=\{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}\backslash\Lambda_{\bullet}:\boldsymbol{\nu}{-}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{m}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}\text{ for all }m=1,\dots,M\text{ such that }\nu_{m}\,{>}\,1\}.

Under the assumption that
\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}
reduces the discretization error,
i.e.,

(16)

\|u-\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}\|\leq q_{\rm sat}\,\|u-u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}\|

with a constant
q_{\rm sat}\in(0,1)
independent of discretization parameters,
the error estimate in the SC-FEM approximation
u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}

is given by the sum of spatial and parametric contributions
(see equations (22), (23) in
[
BSX22
]
):

(17)

\|u-u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}\|\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq}\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big(1-q_{\rm sat}\big)^{-1}\|\widehat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm\,SC}-u_{\bullet}^{\rm SC}\|}\leq\big(1-q_{\rm sat}\big)^{-1}\big(\mu_{\bullet}+\tau_{\bullet}\big).

Here,
\mu_{\bullet}
and
\tau_{\bullet}
are, respectively, the spatial and parametric error estimates defined as follows
(cf.
[
BSX22
, eq. (24)]
and
[
BSX22
, §4.2 and Remarks 1 and 4]
, respectively):

(18)

\displaystyle\mu_{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\|S_{\bullet}(\widehat{U}_{\bullet}-U_{\bullet})\|=\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}}(\widehat{u}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}-u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}})\,L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\bigg\|

and

(19)

\tau_{\bullet}:=\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\bullet}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}}u_{0\mathbf{z}}\widehat{L}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\bigg\|,

where

\{\widehat{L}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}\}
is a set of
multivariate Lagrange basis functions constructed for the set of collocation points
\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}
and satisfying

\widehat{L}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}
for any
\mathbf{z},\,\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}
.

Remark 4
.

While the saturation assumption can be empirically justified when numerical approximations exhibit some asymptotic behavior,
the rigorous proofs exist either in the context of deterministic problems with constant coefficients
(see, e.g.,
[
DN02
,
CGG16
]
) or in the context of stochastic Galerkin FEM for parametric PDEs (see
[
BEEV24
]
).
When required for generic finite element approximations, the saturation assumption may fail
(see
[
BEK96
]
for a counterexample in the deterministic setting).
However, while our main result in the present paper (Theorem
15
) is proved independently of the saturation assumption (
16
),
for the convergence result in Corollary
16
the saturation assumption is required only for a sequence of
SC-FEM approximations living in nested discrete subspaces
\big(\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{X}_{\ell})\big)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

generated by the adaptive algorithm.

Let us now turn to the associated
spatial and parametric
error indicators (see
[
BSX22
, §4.1 and §4.2]
).
For each collocation point
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
, one can first compute local
(spatial)
two-level error indicators
associated with
new interior vertices created by uniform refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}

(recall that the same mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
is assigned to each collocation point):

(20)

\mu_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\xi):=\frac{\big|(f,\widehat{\varphi}_{\bullet,\xi})_{L^{2}(D)}-(a(\cdot,\mathbf{z})\nabla u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}},\nabla\widehat{\varphi}_{\bullet,\xi})_{L^{2}(D)}\big|}{\|\widehat{\varphi}_{\bullet,\xi}\|_{\mathbb{X}}}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}^{+}.

These indicators are then combined to produce the
spatial
error indicator for each
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}
:

(21)

\mu^{2}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}:=\sum\limits_{\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}^{+}}\mu^{2}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}(\xi).

Local mesh refinement is effected by using Dörfler marking on local error indicators (
20
)
to find a set of marked vertices
\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\bullet}^{+}
.
Then the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\circ}:=\refine(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet},\mathcal{M}_{\bullet})
is the refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}
such that

\mathcal{M}_{\bullet}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\circ}
, i.e., all marked vertices of
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{\bullet}
are vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{\circ}
.

In order to introduce parametric error indicators, we exploit a

useful property of the reduced margin that for a monotone
\Lambda_{\bullet}
and for any subset of marked indices

\text{M}_{\bullet}\subseteq{\rm R}_{\bullet}
, the index set
\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup\text{M}_{\bullet}
is also monotone.
Therefore, for each index
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\bullet}
, a natural parametric error indicator is given by
the norm of the hierarchical surplus associated with the parametric enhancement
as a result of adding
\boldsymbol{\nu}
to
\Lambda_{\bullet}
:

(22)

\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}=\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]:=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}}u_{0\mathbf{z}}\widehat{L}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\bigg\|=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|,

where
u_{0}^{\rm semi}
is the semidiscrete approximation satisfying (
10
) with

\mathbb{W}
replaced by
\mathbb{X}_{0}
.

In addition to
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]
, we introduce two other parametric indicators for each
\boldsymbol{\nu}\,{\in}\,{\rm R}_{\bullet}
:

\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}]
and
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}]
.
Here,
u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}
(resp.,
\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}
) is the semidiscrete approximation satisfying (
10
) with

\mathbb{W}
replaced by
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}
(resp.,
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\bullet}
).
Specifically, for
w\in\left\{u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi},\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}\right\}
, we define

(23)

\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[w]:=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}}w_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\widehat{L}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\bigg\|=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\bullet}\cup{\rm R}_{\bullet}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}w\bigg\|,

where

w_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}=\begin{cases}u_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}&\text{if $w=u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}$},\\
\widehat{u}_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}&\text{if $w=\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}$}.\end{cases}

Remark 5
.

We emphasize that
in order to steer the adaptive refinement in Algorithm
6
below,
we only use the parametric error indicators
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}=\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]

that are associated with approximations on the coarsest mesh and hence cheap to compute.
The two other parametric indicators,
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}]
and
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}[\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}]
,
are significantly more expensive to compute.
While these indicators are not part of the adaptive algorithm, they arise as theoretical tools in our analysis
(see Theorem
13
and its application in the proof of the main result in Theorem
15
).

We refer to
[
BS23
, section 3]
for a discussion of computational costs
associated with computing the error estimates
\mu_{\bullet}
and
\tau_{\bullet}
.
The key point is that in practice, the computation of these error estimates is only required to give
a reliable criterion for termination of the adaptive process and, therefore, can be done periodically.
On the other hand, the error indicators
\mu_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}
and
\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}
are cheaper to compute
and the following inequalities hold (see equations (31)–(34) and Remark 3 in
[
BSX22
]
):

(24)

\mu_{\bullet}\lesssim\sum\limits_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\bullet}}\mu_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\,\|L_{\bullet\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\bullet}\leq\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\bullet}}\tau_{\bullet\boldsymbol{\nu}}.

This motivates the use of the
error indicators in the marking strategy within the adaptive algorithm.

6.
Convergence of parametric error estimates

The goal of this section is to show that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\tau_{\ell_{k}}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}=0

along the subsequence
\left(\ell_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of iterations where parametric enrichments occur in Algorithm
6
.
We follow the idea that was used in
[
EEST22
]
in order to prove convergence
of the adaptive algorithm proposed in
[
GG03
]
.
We start by collecting some auxiliary results.
The following lemma establishes a useful property of hierarchical surplus operators.

Lemma 9

(
[
FS21
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}
be two multi-indices such that
\nu_{m}<\mu_{m}
for some
m\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
.
Then

\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}v(\mathbf{y})\equiv 0

for any
v\in C^{0}(\Gamma;\mathbb{X})
.

Next, we formulate the following abstract result for
l^{p}
-sequences.
This result was originally proved for
p=2
in
[
BPRR19a
, Lemma 15]
.
However, the proof is easy to generalize to the case of arbitrary
p\in[1,\infty)
; cf.
[
EEST22
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 10

(
[
BPRR19a
, Lemma 15]
)
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
(x_{n}^{(\ell)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and assume that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
and

\|x_{n}-x_{n}^{(\ell)}\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.
In addition,
let
g:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a continuous function with
g(0)=0
and
assume that there exists a sequence
(\mathcal{P}_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of nested subsets of
\mathbb{N}

(i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\ell+1}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) satisfying the following property:

(27)

x_{m}^{(\ell)}\leq g\Bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell+1}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\ell}}(x_{n}^{(\ell)})^{p}\Bigg)\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\ell+1}.

Then

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\ell}}x_{n}^{p}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.

Now, let
\Lambda_{\infty}:=\cup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\Lambda_{\ell}
and
{\rm R}_{\infty}:={\rm R}(\Lambda_{\infty})
.
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
, let us consider the following sequence:

(28)

\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}:=\big(\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\quad\text{with \ }\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}=\begin{cases}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}},&\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell},\\
0,&\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}),\end{cases}

where
\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}
are defined according to (
22
)
for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}
as well as for
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}
.

Lemma 11
.

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the sequence
\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}
is a subsequence of
\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and consider a multi-index

\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\big(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}\big)\subset\big(\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty}\big)
.
Using the definition of the reduced margin in (
15
) and applying Lemma
9
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:param:indicator}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}}+\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in(\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty})\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell})}\bigg)\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:param:indicator}}}{{=}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}.

This proves the statement of the lemma.
∎

Now, we are ready to establish convergence of parametric error estimates
along the subsequence of iterations for which parametric enrichment takes place.

Theorem 12
.

Suppose that the Taylor coefficients
[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}
,
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
, defined by (
12
)
with
w=u_{0}^{\rm semi}
satisfy the summability property (
13
).
Let
(\ell_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
denote the subsequence of iterations
where parametric enrichment occurs in Algorithm
6

and assume that
\ell_{k}\,{\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}}\,\infty
.
Then

the subsequences

\bigg(\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}\bigg)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\big(\tau_{\ell_{k}}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

converge to zero.

Proof.

We omit the subscript
k
to simplify notation in the proof and assume that
\ell=\ell_{k}{\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}}\,\infty
.
Using the sequences introduced in (
28
) and the triangle inequality, we estimate

(29)

\tau_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:err:indicators}}}{{\leq}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}|\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}-\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}|\leq\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\big\|\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}-\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\big\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})}.

We will complete the proof by showing that each term on the right-hand side of (
29
) converges to zero as
\ell\to\infty
.
We will do this in three steps.

Step 1.

First, we show that
\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
For any
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:param:indicator}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:param:indicator}}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}}+\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell})}\bigg)\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{0}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:poly:representation}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}P_{\mathbf{i}}\bigg\|=\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}\,\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\bigg\|,

where we used Lemma
9
in the second equality.
Hence, applying the triangle inequality and then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

(30)

\displaystyle\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\bigg(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2\mathbf{i}}\big\|[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\bigg(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\mathbf{i}}\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{L_{\pi}^{p}(\Gamma)}\bigg)^{1/2}.

The summability property (
13
) for the Taylor coefficients
[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}
implies

(31)

\bigg(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2\mathbf{i}}\big\|[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg)^{1/2}=:C_{1}<\infty.

Now, let us consider the second factor on the right-hand side of (
30
).
Firstly, introducing the Lebesgue constant
\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})
of the hierarchical surplus operator
\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}

(with respect to the
L_{\pi}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
-norm) and using the fact that
\Gamma=[-1,1]^{M}
, we estimate

(32)

\displaystyle\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{L_{\pi}^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{L_{\pi}^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\big\|P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|_{L_{\pi}^{\infty}(\Gamma)}=\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\cdot\max\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma}|\mathbf{y}^{\mathbf{i}}|=\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu}).

Secondly, since
I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}g=g
for any (univariate) polynomial
g

of degree
\leq\kappa(\nu_{m})-1
, we find that

\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{y})=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\Delta_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}P_{i_{m}}(y_{m})=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\Big(I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m})}-I_{m}^{\kappa(\nu_{m}-1)}\Big)P_{i_{m}}(y_{m})\equiv 0,

provided that
i_{m}\leq\max\,\{0,\kappa(\nu_{m}-1)-1\}
for at least one
m\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
.
Consequently,
\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{y})\not\equiv 0
for

\mathbf{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1})\geq\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}
.
Thus, the second sum on the right-hand side of (
30
) can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\mathbf{i}}\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{L_{\pi}^{p}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:L^p-norm:estimate}}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\mathbf{i}}\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}P_{\mathbf{i}}\big\|^{2}_{L_{\pi}^{p}(\Gamma)}

(33)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:L^p-norm:estimate}}}{{\leq}}\mathcal{L}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\mathbf{i}}=\mathcal{L}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,C^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\boldsymbol{\nu}},

where at the last step we used a finite product of geometric series to calculate the infinite sum over multi-indices as follows:

\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\mathbf{i}}=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\sum\limits_{i_{m}\geq\nu_{m}-1}\rho_{m}^{-2i_{m}}=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\rho_{m}^{-2(\nu_{m}-1)}}{1-\rho_{m}^{-2}}=C^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-2\boldsymbol{\nu}}\ \ \text{with $C^{2}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}:=\prod_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\rho_{m}^{2}}{1-\rho_{m}^{-2}}$}.

Now, combining (
30
), (
31
), and (
6
), we obtain

(34)

\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq C_{1}\,C_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}\lesssim\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}.

The Lebesgue constant of the hierarchical surplus operator can be estimated as follows:

(35)

\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\lesssim\begin{cases}\prod_{m=1}^{M}\nu_{m},&\text{for CC points,}\\[4.0pt]
\prod_{m=1}^{M}\nu^{2}_{m}\log{\nu_{m}},&\text{for Leja points};\end{cases}

for CC points, we refer to
[
FS21
, Section 2.1]
and
for Leja points the bound follows from
[
Chk13
, Theorem 3.1]
using the idea in
[
FS21
, Section 2.1]
.
Hence,
\left(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})

due to the integral convergence test for series.
Therefore, recalling the definition of
\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}
in (
28
),
we conclude from (
34
) that
\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Due to Lemma
11
, each element of
\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}
can be bounded in the same way, i.e.,

\tau_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\lesssim\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}
for all
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty}
.
Since
\left(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
, recalling the definition of
\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}
in (
28
), we conclude that
\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
.

Step 2.

Next, we prove that

\big\|\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}-\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\big\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.
We again apply Lemma
11
to derive

\displaystyle\big\|\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}-\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\big\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty})\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell})}\tau_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}=\sum\limits_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}\;\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{n}\cup{\rm R}_{n})\setminus(\Lambda_{n-1}\cup{\rm R}_{n-1})}\tau_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}\;\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{n}\cup{\rm R}_{n})\setminus(\Lambda_{n-1}\cup{\rm R}_{n-1})}\tau_{n\boldsymbol{\nu}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:tau:est:final}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum\limits_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}\;\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{n}\cup{\rm R}_{n})\setminus(\Lambda_{n-1}\cup{\rm R}_{n-1})}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty})\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell})}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}.

Since
\left(\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
and

\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty}=\cup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell}
,
we conclude that

(36)

\lim\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\big\|\widehat{\tau}_{\infty}-\widehat{\tau}_{\ell}\big\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})}=\lim\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in(\Lambda_{\infty}\cup{\rm R}_{\infty})\setminus(\Lambda_{\ell}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell})}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\rho}^{-\boldsymbol{\nu}}=0.

Step 3.

In this step, we apply Lemma
10
with
p=1
to prove that

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.
In fact, all hypotheses of Lemma
10
are
satisfied in the present setting:

\bullet

\mathbb{N}^{M}
is a countable set that can be identified (via a one-to-one map) with
\mathbb{N}
;

\bullet

\left(\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N}^{M})
is identified with a sequence

\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in l^{1}(\mathbb{N})
;

\bullet

\left(\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}

is identified with a sequence

\big(x^{(\ell)}_{n}\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
;

\bullet

after this identification, we conclude that

\lim\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\big\|x_{n}-x_{n}^{(\ell)}\big\|_{l^{1}(\mathbb{N})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:limit:sequence}}}{{=}}0
;

\bullet

\Lambda_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{N}^{M}
is
identified with a set
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{N}

for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
;

\bullet

the set of newly added indices

\mathcal{P}_{\ell+1}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{\ell}

\bullet

with this identification, one has
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\ell+1}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
Dörfler marking (
26a
) with
0<\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq 1
implies inequality (
27
)
with
g(s):=\frac{1-\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}}s

\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}\bigg(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}\setminus\left(\Upsilon_{\ell}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell}\}\right)}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Upsilon_{\ell}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell}\}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\bigg)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:dorfler}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Upsilon_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Upsilon_{\ell}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell}\}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}

and therefore

\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}\setminus\left(\Upsilon_{\ell}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell}\}\right)}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\frac{1-\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}}{\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Upsilon_{\ell}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell}\}}\widehat{\tau}_{\ell\boldsymbol{\mu}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}\setminus\Lambda_{\ell+1}.

Hence, applying Lemma
10
, we prove that

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}\setminus\Lambda_{\ell}}\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0
.
Therefore, recalling that
{\rm R}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{N}^{M}\setminus\Lambda_{\ell}
, we conclude that

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\widehat{\tau}_{\infty\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0
.

Now, the proof is completed by applying the results of Steps 2 and 3 to the right-hand side of (
29
) and
recalling that
\ell=\ell_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\infty
.
∎

Next, we prove convergence of

two subsequences associated with alternative parametric error indicators given by
(
23
) with

w\in\left\{u_{\bullet}^{\rm semi},\hat{u}_{\bullet}^{\rm semi}\right\}
.

Theorem 13
.

Suppose that the diffusion coefficient
a(x,\mathbf{y})
satisfies the assumptions of
either Lemma
1
or Lemma
2
.
Let
(\ell_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
denote the subsequence of iterations
where parametric enrichment occurs in Algorithm
6

and assume that
\ell_{k}\,{\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}}\,\infty
.
Then

the subsequences

\bigg(\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]\bigg)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg(\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[\hat{u}_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]\bigg)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

converge to zero.

Proof.

We will prove the convergence result for
\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]
,
while the proof for
\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[\hat{u}_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]
is exactly the same.
We will use the analyticity of the semidiscrete approximation
u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{X}_{\ell_{k}}

(this property follows from
[
BNT07
, Lemma 3.2]
;
see also the proof of Lemma
2
and Remark
3
).
Specifically,
the assumptions on the diffusion coefficient in
either Lemma
1
or Lemma
2
guarantee that
u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}(\cdot,\mathbf{y})

admits an analytic extension in the region

\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\left\{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\;\text{dist}(\zeta_{m},\Gamma_{m})\leq\sigma_{m},\;m=1,\dots,M\right\}

for some
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=[\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M}]
;
furthermore,

\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\|u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\zeta})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq C_{\rm reg}

with a positive constant
C_{\rm reg}
that depends on the problem data and is independent of the discretization in the spatial domain.
Hence, applying Lemma
9
and then
[
FS21
, Lemma 2.2]
,
we obtain for any
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}
:

\displaystyle\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:param:indicator1}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell_{k}}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|=\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}^{M}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:poly:representation}}}{{=}}\big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}\big\|

(37)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,e^{-\beta\|\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1})\|_{1}}\,\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Sigma(\Gamma,\boldsymbol{\sigma})}\|u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\zeta})\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq C_{\rm reg}\,\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,e^{-\beta\|\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1})\|_{1}},

where
\beta:=\min\limits_{m=1,\dots,M}\beta_{m}>0
with

\beta_{m}:=\log{\left(\frac{2\sigma_{m}}{|\Gamma_{m}|}+\sqrt{1+\frac{4\sigma_{m}^{2}}{|\Gamma_{m}|^{2}}}\right)}
,

\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})
is the Lebesgue constant,
and the hidden constant is independent of
u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}
and the discretization in the spatial domain.

Note that for any
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}
, the Lebesgue constant
\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})
can be bounded as follows:

\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lebesgue}}}{{\lesssim}}\begin{cases}\prod_{m=1}^{M}\nu_{m}\leq\left(\frac{\|\boldsymbol{\nu}\|_{1}}{M}\right)^{M}\leq\left(\frac{M+k+1}{M}\right)^{M}\lesssim(k+1)^{M}&\text{for CC points,}\\[4.0pt]
\prod_{m=1}^{M}\nu^{2}_{m}\log{\nu_{m}}\leq\prod_{m=1}^{M}\nu^{3}_{m}\lesssim(k+1)^{3M}&\text{for Leja points}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, the definition of the reduced margin implies that

\#{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}\leq(k+1)^{M}.

Therefore, using (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\,e^{-\beta\|\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1})\|_{1}}

(38)

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}(k+1)^{2M}e^{-\beta\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1})\|_{1}}&\text{for CC points,}\\[4.0pt]
(k+1)^{4M}e^{-\beta\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}}&\text{for Leja points}.\end{cases}

The parametric enrichment by adding any multi-index

\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell_{k}}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}\setminus\Upsilon_{\ell_{k}}
satisfying (
26b
)
ensures that
\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}
increases with parametric enrichments.
Let us prove this fact by estimating
\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}
in terms of the parametric enrichment counter
k
.
We consider the case of Leja points, and the arguments below apply immediately to CC points, since
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
is a bijection.
Let
b_{k}:=\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Note that for a given
b\in\mathbb{N}
there are
\binom{b+M-1}{b}
different multi-indices
\boldsymbol{\nu}\geq\boldsymbol{1}
such that

\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}=b
.
Therefore, by marking a multi-index
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell_{k}}
satisfying (
26b
) and adding it to the index set
\Lambda_{\ell_{k}}
,
it is guaranteed that any current value of
b_{k}
will increase after at most
\binom{b_{k}+M-1}{b_{k}}
parametric enrichments.
Consequently, the number of parametric enrichments required to reach a given value of
b_{k}
can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle k+1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}}\binom{n+M-1}{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}}\frac{(n+M-1)!}{n!(M-1)!}=\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}}\prod_{m=1}^{M-1}\frac{n+m}{m}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}}\prod_{m=1}^{M-1}\left(1+\frac{n}{m}\right)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}}(1+n)^{M-1}<\sum_{n=1}^{b_{k}+1}n^{M-1}\lesssim(b_{k}+1)^{M}.

Thus,
\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\|\boldsymbol{\nu}-\boldsymbol{1}\|_{1}=b_{k}\gtrsim\sqrt[M]{k+1}
.
Substituting this estimate into (
6
), we deduce that

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell_{k}}}\tau_{\ell_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}[u_{\ell_{k}}^{\rm semi}]\lesssim(k+1)^{4M}e^{-\beta\sqrt[M]{k+1}}\xrightarrow{k\xrightarrow{}\infty}0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

\mu_{\ell_{k}\mathbf{z}}

[

[

[

[
MSV08

[
MSV08

Thus, repeating the arguments in the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
MSV08
]
,
we establish the following result.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\mathbf{z}\in\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}

Let

.

It is important to note that the global reliability of the estimator (see (

) and
[
MSV08
, eq. (2.9a)]
)
is not needed in the proof of the above result.
The reliability is only used in
[
MSV08

to prove convergence of the true finite element error.
Likewise, we will use the reliability of our error estimate to establish the convergence
of adaptively generated SC-FEM approximations to the true solution of problem (

.

8.
Convergence of the adaptive algorithm

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this work.

Theorem 15
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(D)
and let the diffusion coefficient
a(x,\mathbf{y})
satisfy
the hypotheses of either Lemma
1
or Lemma
2
.
Then for any choice of marking parameters
\theta_{\mathbb{X}}
,
\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}
and
\vartheta
,
Algorithm
6
generates a convergent sequence of error estimates, specifically,

\mu_{\ell}+\tau_{\ell}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
.

Proof.

The assumption on
a(x,\mathbf{y})

implies that the Taylor coefficients
[u_{0}^{\rm semi}]_{\mathbf{i}}
,
\mathbf{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M}
, defined by (
12
)
with
w=u_{0}^{\rm semi}
satisfy the summability property (
13
)
(see Lemmas
1
and
2
).
This, in particular, enables the application of Theorem
12
in the proof below,
where we consider three possible refinement scenarios that may occur when running Algorithm
6
.

Scenario 1.

In the first scenario, the
spatial
refinement occurs finitely many times, i.e.,
\exists\,\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|L_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}<\vartheta\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}

for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
.
In this case, applying
Theorem
12
, we obtain

(39)

\tau_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:err:indicators}}}{{\lesssim}}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|L_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}<\vartheta\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.

Scenario 2.

In the second scenario, the
parametric
refinement occurs finitely many times, i.e.,

\exists\,\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\vartheta^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|L_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}

for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
.
In this case, starting with iteration
\ell=\ell_{0}
, the algorithm performs only spatial refinements.
Therefore, the set of collocation points
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}
(and, consequently, the associated set of Lagrange polynomials)
stays the same for all iterations
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
.
Thus, applying Theorem
14
,
we conclude that

\tau_{\ell}\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}\leq\vartheta^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|L_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.

Scenario 3.

Finally, both types of refinement may occur infinitely often.
In this case,
we split the sequences
\left(\mu_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\left(\tau_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
into disjoint subsequences
as follows:

\left(\mu_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}=\big(\mu_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\cup\big(\mu_{\ell^{(b)}_{k}}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\tau_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}=\big(\tau_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\cup\big(\tau_{\ell^{(b)}_{k}}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}};

here, the subsequences indexed by
(a)
(resp., by
(b)
) correspond to iterations
where only the spatial (resp., parametric) refinement occurs (see Figure
1
,
where we denote by

\bar{\mu}_{\ell}:=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\mu_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|L_{\ell\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}

(resp.,
\bar{\tau}_{\ell}:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell}}\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}
)

the weighted sum of spatial (resp., parametric) error indicators

at the
\ell
-th iteration).

Step 3.1.

Firstly, if for several consecutive

\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}=\ell^{(a)}_{k}+1
,
it means that a number of spatial refinements occur sequentially;
e.g., in Figure

\ell^{(a)}_{k}
with

.
Thus, the set of collocation points
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+i}}
and the associated set of Lagrange polynomials remain the same for

i=0,1,\dots,n
.
Due to Theorem
14
and the nature of the scenario we are considering,
the
weighted
sum of spatial indicators,

\bar{\mu}_{\ell_{k}^{(a)}}=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}}\mu_{{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}\mathbf{z}}\,\|L_{{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}\mathbf{z}}\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}
,
will eventually fall below the following threshold as

(41)

\vartheta\!\!\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}}}\tau_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}\boldsymbol{\nu}}\,{=}\,\vartheta\!\!\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}+1}}\tau_{(\ell^{(a)}_{k}+1)\boldsymbol{\nu}}\,{=}\,\ldots\,{=}\,\vartheta\!\!\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}+n}}\tau_{(\ell^{(a)}_{k}+n)\boldsymbol{\nu}}\,{=}\,\vartheta\!\!\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\tau_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xrightarrow{q\to\infty}0

(here, the equality is ensured by the fact that
\tau_{\ell\boldsymbol{\nu}}
are independent of mesh refinements,
since they are calculated using the coarsest-mesh Galerkin approximations; cf. (
22
)).
This triggers the change of the refinement type from spatial to parametric, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k}+n}}\mu_{({\ell^{(a)}_{k}+n})\mathbf{z}}\,\big\|L_{({\ell^{(a)}_{k}+n})\mathbf{z}}\big\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\mu_{{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\mathbf{z}}\,\big\|L_{{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\mathbf{z}}\big\|_{L^{p}_{\pi}(\Gamma)}<\vartheta\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\tau_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}\boldsymbol{\nu}}\xrightarrow{q\to\infty}0.

Step 3.2.

Next, let us consider the case when
\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}>\ell^{(a)}_{k}+1
,
i.e., at least one parametric enrichment occurs between two spatial refinements
(for example, see iterations
\ell_{7}^{(a)}=7
,
\ell_{8}^{(a)}=9
and
\ell_{0}^{(b)}=8
in Figure
1

We will show that in this case the spatial error estimate
\mu_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}
is bounded
by a quantity that converges to zero as
k\to\infty
.
Using the definition of spatial error estimates in (
18
) and the marking criterion (
26
)
for parametric enrichment, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|S_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}\Big(\widehat{U}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}-U_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}\Big)\Big\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:S:def}}}{{=}}\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\cup\Upsilon_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\big(\hat{u}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}-u_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}\big)\bigg\|

(42)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\big(\hat{u}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}-u_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}\big)\bigg\|+\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\big(\hat{u}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}-u_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}\big)\Big\|,

where
u_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}
and
\hat{u}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}
are semidiscrete solutions satisfying (
10
)
with
\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{X}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}
and
\mathbb{W}=\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}
, respectively.
Note that
u_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}^{\rm semi}=u_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}
, as the finite element mesh does not change
during the parametric enrichment step.
Therefore, the first term on the right-hand side of (
8
) is an element of the sequence

(\mu_{\ell^{(b)}_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, for which we have already proved convergence; cf. (
40
).
Thus,

(43)

\bigg\|\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\Lambda_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\big(\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}-u_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\big)\bigg\|=\mu_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\xrightarrow{q\to\infty}0.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
8
) can be estimated using the triangle inequality:

(44)

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\big(\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}-u_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\big)\Big\|\leq\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\Big\|+\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}u_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\Big\|.

For the first sum on the right-hand side of (
44
),
using Lemma
9
and the definition in (
23
)
with
w=\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}
, we find that

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\Big\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:poly:representation}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\bigg\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\Lambda_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}\cup{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\bigg\|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:param:indicator1}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\tau_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}\boldsymbol{\nu}}\big[\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\big].

13
, we conclude that

(45)

\sum\limits_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\in{\rm R}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}}\Big\|\Delta^{\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\boldsymbol{\nu})}\hat{u}_{\ell^{(b)}_{q}}^{\rm semi}\Big\|\xrightarrow{q\to\infty}0.

The same arguments apply to the second sum on the right-hand side of (
44
).

)–(

)

\mu_{\ell^{(a)}_{k+1}}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

.

\mu_{\ell}+\tau_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\ell\to\infty}0.

For each refinement scenario, we have established convergence of spatial and parametric error estimates to zero.

and

Corollary 16
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(D)
and let the diffusion coefficient
a(x,\mathbf{y})
satisfy
the hypotheses of either Lemma
1
or Lemma
2
.
Let
\big(u_{\ell}^{\rm SC}\big)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

6
and
denote by
\big(\widehat{u}_{\ell}^{\rm SC}\big)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

(as described in section
5
).
Suppose that the saturation assumption
(
16
)

.

This tolerance was reached after 31 iterations (including 5 parametric refinement steps) for Leja points and
after 31 iterations (including 3 parametric enrichments) for CC points.
We record the evolution of

the weighted sums of
error indicators
as well as the evolution of the corresponding error estimates;
see Figures

and

.
From these plots we can see that both the
weighted sums of

to that observed in Test Case I.
Additionally, for both test cases, we observe that the execution of the algorithm with Leja collocation points
requires more parametric enrichments than that with CC points to reach the same tolerance

(6 vs. 4 enrichments for test case I and 5 vs. 3 enrichments for test case II).
As a consequence, the overall computational time is increased when using Leja points compared to using CC points
(8216 seconds vs. 7757 seconds for test case I and 5427 seconds vs. 3936 seconds for test case II).

This is explained by the fact that every new multi-index generates more collocation points of the CC type than those of the Leja type.
For instance, in 1D, each index
i\in\mathbb{N}
generates one new Leja collocation point and
2^{i-1}
new CC points.

10.
Concluding remarks

Adaptive algorithms provide effective solution strategies for high-dimensional parametric PDE problems.
They generate accurate and fast-converging approximations that resolve local spatial features and
adapt to the parametric anisotropy of the PDE solution.
While many adaptive algorithms have been designed and implemented in this context,
the mathematical analysis of these solution strategies is much less developed.
In this paper, for a model parametric PDE problem, we have performed the convergence analysis of an adaptive SC-FEM algorithm
driven by hierarchical a posteriori error indicators proposed in
[
BSX22
]
.
Our main result in Theorem
15
provides a theoretical guarantee that, for any given positive tolerance,
the proposed adaptive algorithm terminates after a finite number of iterations.
Our theoretical results are valid for spatial domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
for affine or non-affine finite-dimensional parametrization of PDE inputs.

In this work, we have employed the
single-level
(rather than
multilevel
) construction of SC-FEM approximations
that assigns the same finite element space to all collocations points.
This choice is primarily motivated by the results of numerical experiments in
[
BSX22
,
BS23
]
.
These results have indicated that the single-level version is likely
to be more efficient when the same adaptively refined finite element mesh can adequately resolve solution features
for a range of individually sampled problems, which is often the case for the model parametric problem (
1
).
While the extension of our convergence analysis to the adaptive multilevel SC-FEM algorithm proposed in
[
BS23
]

is of interest from the theoretical point of view,
this extension is nontrivial due to a different marking strategy (see
[
BS23
, Algorithm 2]
)
and the need to incorporate an additional adaptive strategy for defining suitable meshes for newly added collocation points (see
[
BS23
, Algorithm 3]
).

Other possible extensions of this work include: (i) the parametrization of PDE inputs in terms of
countably infinite number of random parameters (i.e.,
M=\infty
) and the associated algorithmic aspects of dimension adaptivity
(see, e.g.,
[
GN18
, section 7]
); and
(ii) the important yet challenging case of unbounded random parameters
that appear, e.g., in parametric representations of log-normal random fields.
The progress in these directions hinges on finding reliable a posteriori error estimates and appropriate error indicators and
will require a nontrivial extension of the analysis and algorithmic developments in
[
BSX22
]
.

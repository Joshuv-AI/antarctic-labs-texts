1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a number field and
{\mathbb{A}}
be the ring of adeles of
k
. It is well known that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a locally
compact abelian group and the diagonal embedding of
k
into
{\mathbb{A}}
is a lattice, i.e. the image, which is
still denoted by
k
, is discrete and the quotient
k\backslash{\mathbb{A}}
is compact. The classical theory of harmonic
analysis on the quotient
k\backslash{\mathbb{A}}
leads to a great impact, via the famous 1950 Princeton Thesis of
J. Tate (
[
T50
]
), to the modern development of Number Theory, especially to the theory of automorphic

L
-functions.

In Tate’s thesis, the classical Fourier transform and the associated Poisson summation formula are responsible
for the meromorphic continuation and global functional equation of the Hecke
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
attached
to automorphic characters
\chi
of
k^{\times}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
.

In their pioneering work in 1972, R. Godement and H. Jacquet extended the work of Tate on
L(s,\chi)
to the standard automorphic
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
attached to any irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
(
[
GJ72
]
). In their work, the Fourier transform and the associated Poisson summation
formula for
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
are responsible for the meromorphic continuation and global functional equation of
L(s,\pi)
. Here
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}
denotes the space of all
n\times n
matrices.

In 2000, A. Braverman and D. Kazhdan (
[
BK00
]
) proposed that there should exist a generalized
Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\rho,\psi}
on
G({\mathbb{A}})
for any reductive group
G
defined over
k
and any
finite dimensional complex representation
\rho
of the
L
-group
{}^{L}G
; and if the associated Poisson
summation formula could be established, then there is a hope to prove the Langlands conjecture on
meromorphic continuation and global functional equation for automorphic
L
-function
L(s,\pi,\rho)
attached to the pair
(\pi,\rho)
, where
\pi
is any irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of

G({\mathbb{A}})
.
In his 2020 paper (
[
N20
]
), B. C. Ngô suggests that such generalized Fourier transforms could
be put in a framework that generalizes the classical Hankel transform for harmonic analysis on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
and
might be more useful in the trace formula approach to establish the Langlands conjecture of
functoriality in general.

In this paper, we explore a possibility that utilizes harmonic analysis on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
to understand Langlands
automorphic
L
-functions in general, as a vast generalization of the classical work of Tate (
[
T50
]
).
For a
k
-split reductive group
G
, let
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
be its complex dual group and

\rho
be an
n
-dimensional complex representation of
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
.
We introduce, for any irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation

{\sigma}
of
G({\mathbb{A}})
, the space

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
of
({\sigma},\rho)
-Schwartz functions on
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
and the

({\sigma},\rho)
-Fourier operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\sigma},\rho,\psi}
that takes
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
to

{\mathcal{S}}_{\widetilde{{\sigma}},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, where
\widetilde{{\sigma}}
is the contragredient of
{\sigma}
.
Then we show (Theorem
6.2
) that the
({\sigma},\rho)
-theta functions

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Theta_{{\sigma},\rho}(x,\phi):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha}x)

converges absolutely for all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. Our conjecture for the corresponding Poisson
summation formula is stated as follows.

Conjecture 1.1

(
(\sigma,\rho)
-Poisson Summation Formula)
.

Let
\rho\colon G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be any finite dimensional representation of the complex
dual group
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
.
For any given
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(G)
, there exist nontrivial
k^{\times}
-invariant linear functionals

{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma,\rho}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\sigma},\rho}
on
{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and

{\mathcal{S}}_{\widetilde{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, respectively, such that the

(\sigma,\rho)
-Poisson Summation Formula:

{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma,\rho}(\phi)={\mathcal{E}}_{\widetilde{\sigma},\rho}({\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma,\rho,\psi}(\phi))

holds for
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma,\rho,\psi}
are defined in Section
6.2
.

It is expected that such Poisson summation formulae on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
should be
responsible for the Langlands conjecture on the global functional equation of automorphic
L
-functions
associated the pairs
({\sigma},\rho)
. Variants of Conjecture
1.1
will be discussed in
Section
7.3
and see
Conjecture
7.4
for details.

In this paper we prove Conjecture
1.1
when
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
and
\rho

is the standard representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
(Theorem
4.7
), which we call a
\pi
-Poisson
summation formula on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
for any irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
.
A variant of Theorem
4.7
is established in Theorem
7.3
when

\pi
is an irreducible square-integrable automorphic representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
.
It is important to
point out that by Theorem
4.7
, Conjecture
1.1
holds for
(G,\rho)
and an
irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
{\sigma}
of
G({\mathbb{A}})
if the global Langlands functoriality is known for the pair
(G,\rho)
and the functorial transfer to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
of

is cuspidal.
Note that
in Theorem

, the

-invaraint linear functional

\pi
-theta function

As explained in Corollary

.
The desire is to
prove Conjecture
1.1
without using the global Langlands functoriality. It is expected that
Conjecture
1.1
can be proved directly for a split classical group
G
and the standard
representation
\rho
of the complex dual group

, by using the doubling method of I. Piatetski-Shapiro and S. Rallis in
[
GPSR87
]
) and the recent work of L. Zhang and the authors in
[
JLZ20
]
and of

]
.

To formulate and prove the
\pi
-Poisson summation formula on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
for any irreducible
cuspidal automorphic representation

of

), we consider the determinant morphism:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\det\colon{\mathrm{M}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{a};\quad{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m},

where
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}(k)=k
and
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k)={\mathrm{GL}}_{1}(k)=k^{\times}
, and descend the local theory of Godement-Jacquet in
[
GJ72
]
from
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
. With the fully developed local theory for

{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}

of

2.
Godement-Jacquet Theory and Reformulation

2.1.
Mellin transforms

We recall the local theory of Mellin transforms from the book of Igusa (
[
Ig78
, Chapter I]
) and state them in a slightly more general situation in order to treat the case
that meromorphic functions may have poles that are not real numbers. Since the proofs are almost the same, we omit the details.

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero. This means that it is either the complex field
{\mathbb{C}}
, the real field
{\mathbb{R}}
, or a finite extension of the
p
-adic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for some prime
p
.

When
F
is non-Archimedean, let
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
be the ring of integers with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
and fix a uniformizer
\varpi_{F}
of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
. Let
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{F}=\kappa_{F}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Fix the norm
|x|_{F}=q^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{F}(x)}
where
{\mathrm{ord}}_{F}:F\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is the valuation on
F
such that
{\mathrm{ord}}_{F}(\varpi_{F})=1
. Fix the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x
on
F
so that
{\mathrm{vol}}(\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x,{\mathfrak{o}}_{F})=1
. Let
\psi=\psi_{F}
be an additive character of
F
which is trivial on
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
but non-trivial on
\varpi^{-1}_{F}\cdot{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
. In particular the standard Fourier transform defined via
\psi_{F}
is self-dual w.r.t
\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x
. Similarly, fix a multiplicative Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x
on
F^{\times}
, which is normalized so that
{\mathrm{vol}}(\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x,{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{F})=1
. In particular
\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\frac{1}{{\zeta}_{F}(1)}\cdot\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x}{|x|_{F}}
, where
{\zeta}_{F}(s)
is the local Dedekind zeta factor attached to
F
.

When
F
is Archimedean, fix the following norm on
F
,

|z|_{F}=\bigg\{\begin{matrix}\text{absolute value of $z$},&F={\mathbb{R}}\\
z\overline{z},&F={\mathbb{C}}\end{matrix}.

Take the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x
on
F
that is the usual Lebesgue measure on
F
, and set

\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\bigg\{\begin{matrix}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x}{2|x|_{F}},&F={\mathbb{R}},\\
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x}{2\pi|x|_{F}},&F={\mathbb{C}}\end{matrix}

the multiplicative Haar measures on
F^{\times}
.
The additive character
\psi=\psi_{F}
of
F
is chosen as follows

\psi_{F}(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(2\pi ix),&F={\mathbb{R}},\\
\exp(2\pi i(x+\overline{x})),&F={\mathbb{C}}.\end{cases}

For convenience, set the following norm on
F
,

|\cdot|=\begin{cases}|\cdot|_{F},&F\neq{\mathbb{C}},\\
|\cdot|_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}},&F={\mathbb{C}}.\end{cases}

We denote by
{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
the set of all quasi-characters of
F^{\times}
. Define the topological group
\Omega_{F}
to be
\{\pm 1\}
if
F={\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}_{1}
if
F={\mathbb{C}}
, and the unit group

{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
if
F
is non-archimedean. It is clear that any
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
can be written as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\chi(x)=\chi_{u}(x)=\chi_{u,{\omega}}(x)=|x|_{F}^{u}\omega(\mathrm{ac}(x)),

for any
x\in F^{\times}
, with
u\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\omega}\in{\Omega}_{F}^{\wedge}
, the Pontryagin dual of

2.3.
Reformulation of Godement-Jacquet theory

The local theory of Godement-Jacquet can be reformulated within harmonic analysis and
L^{2}
-theory.

For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
, we define

(2.10)

\displaystyle\xi_{f}(g):=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)

for
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
. Then we define the Schwartz space on

to be

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)):=\{\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))\mid\ |\det g|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot\xi(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))\}.

Proposition 2.5
.

The Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
is a subspace of
L^{2}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F),\,\mathrm{d}g)
, which is the space of
square-integrable functions on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
.

Proof.

For
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
, write
\xi(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)
for some

f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
. We deduce the square-integrability of
\xi
by the following computation:

\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi(g)\overline{\xi(g)}\,\mathrm{d}g=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}f(g)\overline{f(g)}\,\mathrm{d}^{+}g=\int_{{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)}f(g)\overline{f(g)}\,\mathrm{d}^{+}g<\infty.

∎

Define the distribution kernel in the local theory of Godement-Jacquet to be

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(g):=\psi({\mathrm{tr}}g)\cdot|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}

where
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character of
F
.
We compute the convolution
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}
for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))

with
\xi(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)
for some

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(h)\xi(g^{-1}h)\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}h)\cdot|\det h|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot|\det g^{-1}h|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g^{-1}h)\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g).

Since

belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))

{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
.
We define the Fourier operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}
in the Godement-Jacquet theory to be

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi)(g):=\left(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}\right)(g)

for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
.

,
define

\pi_{\chi_{s}}:=\pi\otimes\chi_{s}=\pi\otimes\chi(\det)|\det|^{s}_{F}.

Then the following Laurent series

f_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\pi_{\chi_{s}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\pi_{\chi_{s}})

is convergent for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
sufficiently large, with a meromorphic continuation to
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and

f_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\pi_{\chi_{s}})={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s}}},\psi)={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\chi^{-1},\psi).

Proof.

For Part (1), we have to show that the invariant distribution
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(g)
is essentially compact
on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
. By a simple reduction, it suffices to show that, for any open compact subgroup
{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
, we have

\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)).

Since
{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(g)={\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(g^{-1})={\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\vee}(g)
,
the convolution
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}=\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\vee}
can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\vee}(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(h){\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(g^{-1}h)\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(gh){\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}gh)|\det gh|^{\frac{n}{2}}{\bf 1}_{m}(gh){\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

By definition,
{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\neq 0
if and only if
|\det h|_{F}=1
, and
{\bf 1}_{m}(gh)\neq 0
if and only if

|\det g|_{F}=q_{F}^{-m}
, i.e.
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{m}
. This implies that
{\bf 1}_{m}(gh)={\bf 1}_{m}(g)
.
The last integral can be written as

q^{-\frac{mn}{2}}_{F}\mathbf{1}_{m}(g)\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gh))\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h

which can be written as

\displaystyle q_{F}^{-\frac{mn}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{m}(g)\int_{{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gh))\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\,\mathrm{d}^{+}h=q_{F}^{-\frac{mn}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{m}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}({\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}})(g).

Hence we obtain that

\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{m}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}({\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}})(g).

Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}({\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}})(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
and
|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{m}(g)
is smooth
on
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
, we obtain that the convolution

\Phi_{m,\psi}*\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(g)
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and
the invariant distribution
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(g)
is essentially compact on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
.

For Part (2), recall from
[
Ber84
]
that the regular function
f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}
attached to
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}

is defined as follows. For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
and
v\in\pi
, there exists an open compact subgroup

{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
, such that
v\in\pi^{\mathcal{K}}
, the subspace of
{\mathcal{K}}
-fixed vectors in
\pi
.
We may define an action of
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}
on
\pi
via

(2.16)

\displaystyle\pi(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m})(v):=\pi(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\mathfrak{c}}_{\mathcal{K}})(v),

where
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\mathcal{K}}:={\mathrm{vol}}({\mathcal{K}})^{-1}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the normalized characteristic function of
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Since
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*{\mathfrak{c}}_{\mathcal{K}}
lies in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
, the right-hand side is well-defined, so is the left-hand side. It is clear that the action defined in (
2.16
) does not depend on the choice of
such an open compact subgroup
{\mathcal{K}}
.
By Schur’s lemma, there exists a constant
f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\pi)
, depending on
\pi
, such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\pi(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m})=f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\pi)\cdot{\mathrm{Id}}_{\pi}.

For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define that for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
,

(2.18)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\xi)(g):=(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee})(g)=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(h)\xi(g^{-1}h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

In order to include the quasi-characters
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
in the gamma function, we write

(2.19)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi[\chi]}(g):=\varphi_{\pi}[\chi](g):=\varphi_{\pi}(g)\chi(\det g)=(\chi(g)\pi(g)v,\widetilde{v}),

with
v\in V_{\pi}
and
\widetilde{v}\in V_{\widetilde{\pi}}
,
which is a matrix coefficient of
\pi
twisted by
\chi
. We may denote the space of such twisted matrix coefficients of
\pi
by
{\mathcal{C}}(\pi[\chi])
. It is clear that we have

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi[\chi]})={\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},\chi).

For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varphi_{\pi[\chi]}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi[\chi])
, and
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
, consider the zeta function of Godement-Jacquet with
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\xi)
as defined in (
2.18
)

(2.20)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\xi),\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]})={\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee},\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]}).

By Part (1) as proved above, we obtain that
\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
for
any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
. Hence the integral in (
2.20
) is absolutely
convergent for any
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
when
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
.

We write the right-hand side of (
2.20
) as

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g^{-1})\chi^{-1}(\det g)|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\,\mathrm{d}g,

which is equal to

(2.22)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}(g)(v,\widetilde{\pi}(g)\widetilde{v})\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\det g)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}g=(v,\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}(g)\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(g)\widetilde{v}\,\mathrm{d}g).

It is clear that

\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}(g)\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(g)\widetilde{v}\,\mathrm{d}g=\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v}=\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m})(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v}).

Since
\xi^{\vee}
belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
, the vector

\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v}
belongs to the space of

\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}
.
By definition, we have

\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m})=f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}})\cdot{\mathrm{I}}_{\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}}.

Hence we can write the right-hand side of (
2.22
) as

(2.23)

\displaystyle(v,\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}*\xi^{\vee}(g)\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(g)\widetilde{v}\,\mathrm{d}g)=f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}})\cdot(v,\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v}).

Next we compute the twisted coefficient
(v,\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v})
on the
right-hand side of (
2.23
) as follows:

\displaystyle(v,\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(\xi^{\vee})\widetilde{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi^{\vee}(h)(v,\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}(h)\widetilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi(h^{-1})(\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}(h^{-1})v,\widetilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi(h)(\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}(h)v,\widetilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi(h)\varphi_{\pi[\chi]}(h)|\det h|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi[\chi]}).

Hence we obtain the following functional equation

(2.24)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\xi),\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]})=f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}})\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi[\chi]})

for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
,
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
and
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
.

From Theorem
2.4
, when
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
is sufficiently small, the zeta integral

{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi),\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]})
converges absolutely for
any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
, any
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
and any unitary character

\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
. We write it as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi),\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\xi),\varphi^{\vee}_{\pi[\chi]})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi[\chi]})\cdot\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}}).

By comparing with the right-hand side of the functional equation in Theorem
2.4
, we obtain that whenever
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
is sufficiently small,

(2.25)

\displaystyle f_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}})=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{{\mathrm{GJ}},m}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s-\frac{1}{2}}}})={\gamma}(s,\pi\otimes\chi,\psi)={\gamma}(s,\pi_{\chi},\psi)

By changing
s\to s+\frac{1}{2}
, we get

f_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s}}})={\gamma}(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi_{\chi},\psi)={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi_{\chi_{s}},\psi).

By taking the contragredient of
\pi_{\chi_{s}}
, we obtain that

f_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\pi_{\chi_{s}})={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\widetilde{\pi_{\chi_{s}}},\psi)={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2}-s,\widetilde{\pi}\times\chi^{-1},\psi).

This finishes the proof of Part (2).
∎

3.
\pi
-Schwartz Functions and Fourier Operators

3.1.
Two spaces associated to
\pi

For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, we are going to define two spaces associated to
\pi
:

{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
.

The space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}={\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
consists of
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued meromorphic functions
{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi)
on
{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
that
satisfy the following conditions.

(1)

{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})
is a holomorphic multiple of the standard local
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\omega)
with
\chi_{s,{\omega}}(x)=|x|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\mathrm{ac}(x))
.

(2)

If
F
is non-Archimedean,
{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})
is nonzero for finitely many
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
, and for each
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
,

{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})\in L(s,\pi\times\omega)\cdot{\mathbb{C}}[q^{s},q^{-s}]
.

(3)

If
F
is Archimedean, for any polynomial
P(\chi_{s,{\omega}})=P_{\omega}(s)
, if
P(\chi_{s,{\omega}})L(s,\pi\times\omega)
is holomorphic in any vertical strip
S_{a,b}
as in (
2.3
),
with small neighborhoods at the possible poles of the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times{\omega})
removed,
then for any
{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}
, the product
P(\chi_{s,{\omega}}){\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})
is bounded in the same strip
S_{a,b}
, with small neighborhoods at the possible poles of
the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times{\omega})
removed.

From Part (3), we define a semi-norm to be

\mu_{a,b:P}({\mathfrak{z}}):=\sup_{a\leq{\mathrm{Re}}(s)\leq b}|P(\chi_{s,{\omega}})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})|.

Then the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}
is complete under the topology that is defined by the family of semi-norms
\mu_{a,b:P}
for all possible choice of data
a,b;P
as in Part (3) (
[
J09
, Section 4]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}
is a subspace of
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
as defined in Definition
2.2
.

Proof.

When
F
is non-Archimedean, the statement is a consequence of Theorem
2.4
. We would like to focus on the case when
F
is Archimedean.
In this case, it suffices to estimate the boundedness condition. To do so, recall the following classical Stirling formula (see
[
J09
, p.81]
for instance)

(3.1)

{\Gamma}(x+iy)\sim(2\pi)^{1/2}|y|^{x-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}

for
x
fixed and
|y|\to\infty
.

Consider the Archimedean local
L
-functions
L(s,\pi\times{\omega})=L(s,\pi\times\mathrm{ac}(\cdot)^{p})
,
which can be explicitly expressed in terms of classical
{\Gamma}
-functions with the local Langlands parameter of
\pi
. For instance, from

[
GJ72
, Section 8]
, there exists a finite family of pairs
\{(l_{i},u_{i})\}_{i=1}^{t}
with

u_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad l_{i}\in\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\{0,1\},&F={\mathbb{R}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}},&F={\mathbb{C}}\end{cases}

such that in the fixed bounded vertical strip

S_{a,b}=\{s\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid a\leq{\mathrm{Re}}(s)\leq b\},

up to a bounded factor in
S_{a,b}
, we have

L(s,\pi\times\mathrm{ac}(\cdot)^{p})\sim\begin{cases}\prod_{i=1}^{t}{\Gamma}(\frac{s+u_{i}+l_{i}+p}{2}),&F={\mathbb{R}}\\
\prod_{i=1}^{t}{\Gamma}(s+u_{i}+\frac{|l_{i}+p|}{2}),&F={\mathbb{C}}\end{cases}

with
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\{0,1\}
if
F={\mathbb{R}}
; and
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
if
F={\mathbb{C}}
.
Here
l_{i}+p
is understood to be zero if both
l_{i}
and
p
are equal to
1
when
F={\mathbb{R}}
.

It follows from the classical Stirling formula in (
3.1
), in particular the exponential decay of
{\Gamma}(x+iy)
along the imaginary axis, that for any polynomial

P_{\omega}(s)=P(s)\in{\mathbb{C}}[s]
when
F={\mathbb{R}}
, and

P_{\omega}(s)=P(s,p)\in{\mathbb{C}}[s,p]
, the product
P(s,p)L(s,\pi\times\mathrm{ac}(\cdot)^{p})

is bounded in vertical strip
S_{a,b}
with small neighborhoods at the possible poles removed. Hence from the definition of the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
, for any
{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
,
the product
P(s,p){\mathfrak{z}}(\chi)
, with
\chi(x)=|x|_{F}^{s}\mathrm{ac}(x)^{p}
is bounded in vertical strip
S_{a,b}
with small neighborhoods at the possible poles of the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\mathrm{ac}(\cdot)^{p})
removed.
Therefore we obtain that the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}={\mathcal{L}}_{\pi}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
is contained in the space
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
, as defined in Definition
2.2
.
∎

For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, we define (Definition
3.3
) the
\pi
-Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
attached to
\pi
, by using the theory of local zeta integrals of Godement-Jacquet,
and prove that

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})={\mathcal{M}}^{-1}({\mathcal{L}}_{\pi})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F^{\times})

by Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.3
.

Consider the following determinant map:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\det={\det}_{F}\colon{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\to F^{\times}.

It is clear that the kernel
\ker(\det)={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
. For each
x\in F^{\times}
,
the fiber of the determinant map
\det
is

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}:=\{g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mid\det g=x\}.

It is clear that each fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
is an
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
-torsor. Hence one has the
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
-invariant measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g
that is induced from the (normalized) Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g

on
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
.

For
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
as defined in (
2.11
),
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, and
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
,
the local zeta integral of Godement and Jacquet, as normalized in (

), can be written as

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},\chi)=\int_{F^{\times}}\left(\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}\xi(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g\right)\chi(x)|x|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x.

By Part (1) of Theorem
2.4
, the local zeta integral converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
large. Hence the inner integral of (
3.5
)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x):=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}\xi(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g=|x|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}f(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g,

if
\xi(g)=|\det g|^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)
for some

,
is absolutely convergent for almost all
x\in F^{\times}
and defines the fiber integration along the fibration in (
3.3
).
Moreover, we have

Proposition 3.2
.

For
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, the fiber integration in (
3.6
) that defines the function
\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x)
is absolutely convergent for all
x\in F^{\times}
, and
the function
\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x)
is smooth over
F^{\times}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show the proposition for the integral

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}f(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g

with any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
and
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
. In this case,
the product

is smooth on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
.
Since the fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
for any

is closed in
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
and in
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
, the restriction of

to the fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
is a Schwartz function on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}

(
[
BerZ76
]
for
F
non-Archimedean, and
[
AG08
, Theorem 4.6.1]
for
F
Archimedean).

When
F
is non-Archimedean, any
\varphi_{\pi}(g)\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
is locally constant (smooth)
on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
and hence is smooth on the fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
. This implies that the restriction of
f\cdot\varphi_{\pi}
is locally constant and compactly supported on the fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
.
Hence the integral in (
3.7
) is absolutely convergent for all

, and defines a

over
F^{\times}
.

When

is Archimedean, since

is a Casselman-Wallach representation of

, the matrix coefficient

has at most of polynomial growth on

(

(
[

Thus the integral in (

.
Now we write the integral in (

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}f(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g=\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)}f(t_{1}(x)g)\varphi_{\pi}(t_{1}(x)g)\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g

is the Haar measure of

runs in any

. Hence the integral in (

over

.

This finishes the proof.
∎

For any

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))^{\circ}}f(X)\varphi_{\pi}(X)\overline{f(Y)}\overline{\varphi_{\pi}(Y)}|\det X|_{F}^{{\alpha}_{0}-n}\,\mathrm{d}^{+}(X,Y)^{\circ}

Proposition 3.7
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the
\pi
-Schwartz space

{\mathcal{F}}(F^{\times})

Proof.

Note first that the
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}

3.4

By Theorem
2.3
, for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(\phi-\phi_{0})(\chi)=0

holds identically for any quasi-character
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
.
It remains to show that
\phi-\phi_{0}=0
holds identically.
By smoothness of
\phi
and
\phi_{0}

\phi
and
\phi_{0}
are square-integrable on
F^{\times}
.

For
\phi_{0}\in{\mathcal{F}}(F^{\times})
, there exists

F^{\times}
for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>s_{0}
,
via the asymptotic formula appearing in the definition of

.

For any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})

4.5
, we have

\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(x^{-1},{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi))=\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(1,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi^{x}))
. Since

\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})

is arbitrary, it is enough to show that

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi):=\sum_{\alpha\in k^{\times}}\phi(\alpha)

converges absolutely and the following identity

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(1,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi))

holds.

In order to prove that the summation
\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)
is absolutely convergent,
we write
\phi=\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
with

\xi(g)=|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}))
for
some
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}))
and

\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
. From (
4.12
) we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi}(g)=\int_{A_{n}({\mathbb{R}})^{+}{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})}\beta_{1}(hg)\beta_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\beta_{1}(hg)\beta_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h

for some
\beta_{1}\in V_{\pi}
and
\beta_{2}\in V_{\widetilde{\pi}}
, where
V_{\pi}
is the cuspidal
automorphic realization of
\pi
in
L^{2}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}),\omega)
and so is
V_{\widetilde{\pi}}
.

First, we have that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}({\alpha})=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\alpha}}\xi(g)\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}{\beta}_{1}(hg){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}_{\alpha}g.

From changing variable
g\to h^{-1}g
, we have that
\det g={\alpha}\cdot\det h
and (
4.15
) becomes

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}\cdot\det h}}\xi(h^{-1}g){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{{\alpha}\cdot\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

For
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}\cdot\det h}
, we change

g
to
t_{1}({\alpha})\cdot y
with
\det y=\det h
, where
t_{1}({\alpha})={\rm diag}({\alpha},{\mathrm{I}}_{n-1})\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)
. Then (
4.16
) can be written as

(4.17)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\xi(h^{-1}t_{1}({\alpha})g){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h,

since
{\beta}_{1}
is automorhic. For any
h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
, we have
|\det h|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
.
Hence we must have that
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}\subset{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
. It is clear that

{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}
is an
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
-torsor and the measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g
is
left
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)
-invariant.
Hence (
4.17
) can be written as

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\sum_{\epsilon\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)}\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\xi(h^{-1}t_{1}({\alpha})\epsilon g){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

Since any element
{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)
can be written as a product of
t_{1}({\alpha})
and
\epsilon
in a unique way,
we obtain that (
4.18
) is equal to

(4.19)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\left(\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}\xi(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)\right){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

Since
\xi(g)=|\det g|_{\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}))
for
some
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}))
, and
h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
and
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}
, we must have
that

\xi(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)=|\det(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)|_{\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)=f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g).

Hence we obtain that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}\xi(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)=\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g).

By
[
GJ72
, Lemma 11.7]
, for any

, the summation

\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)

is of moderate growth in
g,h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
as an automorphic function on

{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})\times{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
, and so is the summation

\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}\xi(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)

as an automorphic function in
g,h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
. Since both
\beta_{1}(g)
and
\beta_{2}(h)

are cuspidal, we obtain that the integral in (
4.19
) converges absolutely, and so does the

\pi
-theta function
\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)
at
x=1
.

Now we continue with the integral in (
4.19
) to prove the identity

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(1,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi)).

Recall from
[
GJ72
, Section 11]
and also
[
Luo
, Theorem 4.0.1]
the classical Poisson summation formula:

(4.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k)}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)=\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k)}|\det gh^{-1}|^{-n}_{\mathbb{A}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}{\gamma}h)

holds for any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}))
and
h,g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
. When
g,h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
, it can be
re-written according to the rank of
{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k)
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}{\gamma}h)

(4.22)

\displaystyle+\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k),{\mathrm{rank}}({\gamma})<n}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}{\gamma}h)-\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k),{\mathrm{rank}}({\gamma})<n}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g).

We denote the boundary terms by

(4.23)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{B}}_{f}(h,g):=\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k),{\mathrm{rank}}({\gamma})<n}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}{\gamma}h)-\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(k),{\mathrm{rank}}({\gamma})<n}f(h^{-1}{\gamma}g).

Then (
4.19
) can be written as a sum of the following two terms:

(4.24)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\left(\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}{\gamma}h)\right){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h,

and

(4.25)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}{\mathrm{B}}_{f}(h,g){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

From the proof of
[
GJ72
, Lemma 12.13]
and
[
Luo
, Lemma 4.1.4]
, we must have that
the term in (
4.25
) is zero, because of the cuspidality of both
\beta_{1}(g)
and
\beta_{2}(h)
. Hence we obtain that

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}})

is equal to the term in (
4.24
).

Now we write (
4.24
) as

(4.26)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\left(\sum_{{\gamma}\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(({\gamma}g)^{-1}h)\right){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

By writing back that
{\gamma}=t_{1}({\alpha})\cdot\epsilon
with
{\alpha}\in k^{\times}
and
\epsilon\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(k)
, we obtain that
(
4.26
) is equal to

(4.27)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{\det h}}\left(\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)((t_{1}({\alpha})g)^{-1}h)\right){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

By changing
t_{1}({\alpha})g
to
g
, we write (
4.27
) as

(4.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}\cdot\det h}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1}h){\beta}_{1}(g){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{{\alpha}\cdot\det h}g\,\mathrm{d}h.

After changing variable
g\to hg
, (
4.28
) can be written as

(4.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1})\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k)\backslash{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}}{\beta}_{1}(hg){\beta}_{2}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}_{{\alpha}}g,

which is equal to

(4.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g^{-1})\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{\alpha}g.

Finally, by changing
g
to
g^{-1}
, we obtain that (
4.19
) is equal to

(4.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})_{{\alpha}}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}_{\alpha}g

By Proposition
2.6
, when
\det g={\alpha}\in k^{\times}
, we have

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(g)={\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi)(g)

for
\xi(g)=|\det g|^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)
. Hence the summation in (
4.31
) is equal to

\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}})(1)=\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(1,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}})).

This proves the
\pi
-Poisson summation formula

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\Theta_{\widetilde{\pi}}(1,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi))

for all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. We are done.
∎

The proof of the
\pi
-Poisson summation formula in Theorem
4.7
uses the Poisson summation formula associated to the classical Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}
over the affine space
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
, without
using the global functional equation for the global zeta integrals
{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)
in Theorem
4.6
.
Hence we are able to obtain the global functional equation for the global zeta integrals

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)
as in Theorem
4.6
by using the
\pi
-Poisson summation formula
in Theorem
4.7
. Of course, this is essentially the same proof as the one
that uses the global functional equation of Godement-Jacquet zeta functions in Theorem
4.2
.
However, it seems still meaningful to point out the contribution of the
\pi
-Poisson summation formulae on

{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
in the theory of the global functional equation for the standard automorphic
L
-function

L(s,\pi\times\chi)
for any automorphic characters
\chi
of
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
and any
irreducible cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
, as an extension in a
different prospective of the Tate’s thesis to the study of higher degree automorphic
L
-functions.

5.
Absolute Convergence of Generalized Theta Functions

We explore in this section the possibilities to establish the
\pi
-Poisson summation formulae
(Theorem
4.7
) when the automorphic representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
may not be cuspidal.

5.1.
Absolute convergence of
\pi
-theta functions

Recall that we denote by
\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
the set of all equivalence classes of irreducible admissible representations of

{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
.
For any
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
, we have, as in (
4.1
),

{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})=\otimes_{\nu\in|k|}{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times}).

For
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, we are going to show that the
\pi
-theta function

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha}x)

converges absolutely under an assumption (Assumption
5.1
)
on the unramified local components
\pi_{\nu}
of
\pi
.

For any
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
, let
S_{\pi}
be a finite subset of local places of
k
containing
|k|_{\infty}
such that for any finite local place
\nu\not\in S_{\pi}
, the local component
\pi_{\nu}

is unramified. For any
\pi_{\nu}
with
\nu\not\in S_{\pi}
, via the Satake isomorphism, one has the
Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy class
c(\pi_{\nu})
in
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
associated to
\pi_{\nu}
. We write

(5.2)

\displaystyle c(\pi_{\nu}):={\rm diag}(q_{\nu}^{s_{1}(\pi_{\nu})},\cdots,q_{\nu}^{s_{n}(\pi_{\nu})})\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

up to the adjoint action of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, with
s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})\in{\mathbb{C}}
for
j=1,2,\cdots,n
,
where
q_{\nu}
is the cardinality of the residue field
\kappa_{\nu}={\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{\nu}
.
The following is the assumption we take on the unramified local components
\pi_{\nu}
of
\pi
.

Assumption 5.1
.

Let
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
be an irreducible admissible representation of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
.
There exists a positive real number
\kappa_{\pi}
, which depends only on
\pi
, such that

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\{{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}<\kappa_{\pi}

for every
\nu\not\in S_{\pi}
.

Then we need to prove some technical local results.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
with Assumption
5.1
,
there exists a positive real number
s_{\pi}\geq\kappa_{\pi}
such that
for any real number
a_{0}>s_{\pi}
, the following limit

\lim_{|x|_{\nu}\to 0}\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|_{\nu}^{a_{0}}=0

holds for any
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
and any local place
\nu\in|k|
.
In particular,
\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|_{\nu}^{a_{0}}
extends to a continuous function with compact support on
k_{\nu}
.

Proof.

When
\nu\in|k|_{\infty}
, the asymptotic of
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
near
x=0

is characterized in Definition
2.1
. In particular, following the notation in Definition
2.1
, the fixed sequence
\{{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
has strictly increasing real part
\{{\mathrm{Re}}({\lambda}_{k})\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Hence for any positive real number
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the inequality

s_{0}+{\mathrm{Re}}({\lambda}_{0})>0,

the limit

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|_{\nu}\to 0}\phi_{\nu}(x)\cdot|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}=0

holds as the limit formula in Definition
2.1
is term-wise differentiable and uniform (even after term-wise differentiation). Hence the function
\phi_{\nu}(x)\cdot|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}
is continuous with
compact support on
k_{\nu}
for any positive real number
s_{0}
satisfying
s_{0}+{\mathrm{Re}}({\lambda}_{0})>0
.
Since the set
|k|_{\infty}
is finite, it is possible to choose a sufficiently positive
s_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such
that the prescribed property holds for all functions
\phi_{\nu}(x)\cdot|x|^{a_{0}}_{\nu}
with

\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
at all
\nu\in|k|_{\infty}
, as long as
a_{0}\geq s_{\infty}
.

It remains to treat the case when
\nu\in|k|_{f}
, the finite local places of
k
. We consider the local
zeta integrals
{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})
for any
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k^{\times}_{\nu})
, and any unitary character
{\omega}_{\nu}\in{\Omega}_{\nu}^{\wedge}
. By Theorem
3.4
, it converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
sufficiently positive and admits a meromorphic continuation to
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
For each
\nu\in|k|_{f}
, we take
c_{\pi_{\nu}}
to be a sufficiently positive real number, such that

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})
converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>c_{\pi_{\nu}}
.
If
\nu\not\in S_{\pi}
, then
\pi_{\nu}
is unramified. In this case, the zeta integral

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})
converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>\kappa_{\pi}
, where the positive real number
\kappa_{\pi}
depends on
\pi
only, according to Assumption
5.1
. Hence if we
take a positive real number
c_{\pi}
with

(5.4)

\displaystyle c_{\pi}:=\max\{\kappa_{\pi},c_{\pi_{\nu}}\mid\nu\in S_{\pi}\cap|k|_{f}\},

then for any
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k^{\times}_{\nu})
, and any unitary character

{\omega}_{\nu}\in{\Omega}_{\nu}^{\wedge}
, the local zeta integrals
{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})

converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>c_{\pi}
at all finite local places
\nu\in|k|_{f}
.

By the Mellin inversion formula as displayed in (
2.5
), we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\phi_{\nu}(x)\cdot|x|_{\nu}^{d}=\sum_{{\omega}_{\nu}\in{\Omega}_{\nu}^{\wedge}}\left({\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}\right)\right){\omega}_{\nu}(\mathrm{ac}(x))^{-1},

where
z=q_{\nu}^{-s}
and
d>c_{\pi}
. Since the summation on the right-hand side is finite, it suffices to show that the following limit formula

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|_{\nu}\to 0}{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}\right)=0

holds for each
{\omega}_{\nu}\in{\Omega}_{\nu}^{\wedge}
.

It is clear that
{\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})
is holomorphic for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)>-(d-c_{\pi})
.
By Theorem
3.4
, we have

{\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})=p_{\nu}(s)\cdot L(s+d,\pi_{\nu}\times{\omega}_{\nu}),

where
p_{\nu}(s)\in{\mathbb{C}}[q_{\nu}^{s},q_{\nu}^{-s}]
, depending on
\phi_{\nu}
.
By the supercuspidal support of

\pi_{\nu}\otimes{\omega}_{\nu}
, we obtain that the representation
\pi_{\nu}\otimes{\omega}_{\nu}
can be
embedded, as an irreducible subrepresentation, into the following induced representation

\pi_{\nu}\otimes{\omega}_{\nu}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{\nu}:={\mathrm{Ind}}^{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(k_{\nu})}_{P(k_{\nu})}\tau_{\nu,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\tau_{\nu,t_{\nu}}

where
\tau_{\nu,j}
is an irreducible supercuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{G}}_{a_{\nu,j}}(k_{\nu})
with

n=a_{\nu,1}+\cdots+a_{\nu,t_{\nu}}
(c.f.
[
J71
]
).
By
[
GJ72
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have

L(s,\Pi_{\nu})=L(s,\tau_{\nu,1})\cdots L(s,\tau_{\nu,t_{\nu}}).

By
[
GJ72
, Corollary 3.6]
, we have

\frac{L(s,\pi_{\nu}\times{\omega}_{\nu})}{L(s,\Pi_{\nu})}

is a polynomial in
q_{\nu}^{-s}
. Hence we obtain that for the given

\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
, there exists a polynomial
{\mathcal{P}}_{v}(s)
in
q_{\nu}^{s}
and
q_{\nu}^{-s}
, depending on
\pi_{\nu}\otimes{\omega}_{\nu}
and
\phi_{\nu}
,
such that

(5.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})={\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(s)L(s+d,\Pi_{\nu}).

By applying
[
GJ72
, Proposition 5.11]
to the local
L
-functions
L(s,\tau_{\nu,j})
, we obtain that

L(s,\tau_{\nu,j})=1
when
\tau_{\nu,j}
is either supercuspidal (
a_{\nu,j}\geq 2
) or
a ramified character (
a_{\nu,j}=1
). Hence there exists an integer
1\leq r_{\nu}\leq t_{\nu}\leq n
, such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})={\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(s)\prod_{j=1}^{r_{\nu}}\frac{1}{1-q_{\nu}^{-s-d+s_{\nu,j}}}=\prod_{j=1}^{r_{\nu}}\left(\sum_{\ell_{j}=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-(s+d-s_{\nu,j})\ell_{j}}\right)

for some
s_{\nu,j}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, with
j=1,2,\cdots,r_{\nu}
.

Now we are ready to discuss the limit in (
5.6
). For
z=q_{\nu}^{-s}
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}={\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{r_{\nu}}\left(\sum_{\ell_{j}=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-\ell_{j}(d-s_{\nu,j})}\cdot z^{\ell_{j}}\right)}{z^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+1}},

where
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)
is a polynomial function in
z,z^{-1}
.
By taking the residue at
z=0
, we obtain that

(5.10)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+d,\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}\right)={\mathfrak{C}}_{0}(x)

where
{\mathfrak{C}}_{0}(x)
is the coefficient of the constant term of

(5.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{r_{\nu}}\left(\sum_{\ell_{j}=0}^{\infty}q_{\nu}^{-\ell_{j}(d-s_{\nu,j})}\cdot z^{\ell_{j}}\right)}{z^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)}}.

Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)
is a polynomial function in
z,z^{-1}
with degree depending on
\pi
,
without loss of generality, we may assume that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)\equiv 1
when we compute
{\mathfrak{C}}_{0}(x)
.
In this case, the constant term of (
5.11
) with
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(z)\equiv 1
is equal to

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{r_{\nu}}={\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\\
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r_{\nu}}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q_{\nu}^{-\ell_{1}(d-s_{\nu,j})-\cdots-\ell_{t_{0}}(d-s_{\nu,j})}.

When
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
,
\pi_{\nu}
is unramified,

{\rm diag}(q_{\nu}^{s_{\nu,1}},\cdots,q_{\nu}^{s_{\nu,n}})=c(\pi_{\nu})

is the Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy class associated to
\pi_{\nu}
in
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with

s_{\nu,j}=s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})
for
j=1,2,\cdots,n
.
By Assumption
5.1
and the definition of the positive real number
c_{\pi}
as in (

), we take
d_{0}=0
and have

(5.13)

\displaystyle d-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})>c_{\pi}-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})\geq 0

for all
j=1,2,\cdots,n
. For the remaining finite local places
\nu
, we may choose a positive
real number
d_{0}
such that

(5.14)

\displaystyle d+d_{0}-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})>c_{\pi}+d_{0}-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})\geq 0

for all
j=1,2,\cdots,r_{\nu}
and all
\nu\in S_{\pi}\cap|k|_{f}
.
Hence with the choice of

, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+d+d_{0},\phi_{\nu},{\omega}_{\nu})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{r_{\nu}}={\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\\
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r_{\nu}}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q_{\nu}^{-\sum_{j=1}^{r_{\nu}}\ell_{j}(d+d_{0}-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{r_{\nu}}={\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\\
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r_{\nu}}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q_{\nu}^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)(d+d_{0}-\max_{j}\{{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})\})}

(5.15)

\displaystyle=\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+r_{\nu}-1}{r_{\nu}-1}\cdot q_{\nu}^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)(d+d_{0}-\max_{j}\{{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})\})}.

\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+r_{\nu}-1}{r_{\nu}-1}

\lim_{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\to+\infty}\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+r_{\nu}-1}{r_{\nu}-1}\cdot q_{\nu}^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)(d+d_{0}-\max_{j}\{{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{\nu,j})\})}=0.

Therefore, by taking a positive real number
s_{\pi}=\max\{s_{\infty},c_{\pi}+d_{0}\}
,
we obtain that for any
a_{0}>s_{\pi}
, the function
\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|_{\nu}^{a_{0}}
is continuous over

and
has the limit

\lim_{|x|_{\nu}\to 0}\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|_{\nu}^{a_{0}}=0,

for any
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
and at any local place

.
We are done.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
satisfy Assumption
5.1
.
For any
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
, the basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})

is supported on
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}-\{0\}
with

{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}({\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{\nu})=1.

Moreover, there exists a positive real number
b_{\pi}\geq s_{\pi}
, which is independent of
\nu
, such that for any
b_{0}>b_{\pi}
,

\left|{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)\cdot|x|_{\nu}^{b_{0}}\right|\leq 1

holds for all
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
.

Proof.

We continue with the proof of Lemma
5.2
for the non-Archimedean case, and specialize it
to the unramified situation.
Note that the basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
is the Mellin inversion of the local unramified
L
-factor

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}})=L(s,\pi_{\nu}),

whose Mellin inversion can be calculated by (
5.5
) after setting
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}(s)=1
.
In other words, taking the constant
s_{\pi}
as in Lemma
5.2
, we have, for any
a_{0}>s_{\pi}
,

{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)\cdot|x|^{a_{0}}={\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+a_{0},{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}\right).

As in (
5.9
), we write

(5.16)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s+a_{0},{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}})=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-q_{\nu}^{-s-a_{0}+s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})})}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{\ell_{j}\geq 0}q_{\nu}^{\ell_{j}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})-a_{0})}z^{\ell_{j}}\right).

where we write
z=q_{\nu}^{-s}
and
c_{j}(\pi_{\nu})=q_{\nu}^{s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})}
.
From the Laurent expansion on the right-hand side, we obtain that the function

{\mathcal{Z}}(s+a_{0},{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}})|x|^{-s}_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{s}

is holomorphic in
z=q_{\nu}^{-s}
whenever
x\notin{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}
. By taking the residue at
z=0
, we
obtain that

{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)\cdot|x|^{a_{0}}=0\quad\text{for}\quad x\notin{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}.

Hence the basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)
has support included in
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}
.
Similarly, when
x\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{\nu}
,
|x|_{\nu}=1
and

{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\left({\mathcal{Z}}(s+a_{0},{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}})q_{\nu}^{s}\right)=1

because of the constant term on the right-hand side of (
5.16
).
Therefore, we obtain

{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}({\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{\nu})=1.

Finally, whenever
x\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, we apply (
5.1
) to the
unramified case, and obtain that

\left|{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)\cdot|x|^{b}\right|\leq\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}\cdot q_{\nu}^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}},

as long as
b>s_{\pi}
.
By Assumption
5.1
, we have

{\min}_{1\leq j\leq n}\{b-s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})\}>{\min}_{1\leq j\leq n}\{\kappa_{\pi}-s_{j}(\pi_{\nu})\}>0.

Therefore whenever
{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
,

\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}\cdot q_{\nu}^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}}\leq\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}\cdot 2^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}}

since
q_{\nu}\geq 2
for any
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
. It turns out that we only need to find a positive integer

b_{\pi}\geq s_{\pi}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that for any
b>b_{\pi}
, the following

\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}\cdot 2^{-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}}\leq 1

holds for any
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
and
{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
. Equivalently, after applying the function
\log_{2}
on both sides, the above inequality becomes

\log_{2}\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}-{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\cdot\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}\leq 0.

Hence it suffices to show the existence of
b_{\pi}\in{\mathbb{R}}
so that

\displaystyle\min_{j}\{b-{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}

\displaystyle=b-\max_{j}\{{\mathrm{Re}}(s_{j}(\pi_{\nu}))\}

\displaystyle>b_{\pi}-\kappa_{\pi}\geq\frac{\log_{2}\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}}{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)}

for any
{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
, i.e.

(5.17)

b_{\pi}\geq\kappa_{\pi}+\frac{\log_{2}\binom{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)+n-1}{n-1}}{{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)}

for any
{\mathrm{ord}}_{\nu}(x)\geq 1
. As a function of
t\geq 1
,

\log_{2}\binom{t+n-1}{n-1}=\log_{2}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(t+k)}{(n-1)!}\geq\log_{2}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1+k)}{(n-1)!}\geq\log_{2}(n)\geq 0,

Thus we obtain that

\frac{\log_{2}\binom{t+n-1}{n-1}}{t}\geq 0

for any
t\geq 1
. On the other hand, by L’Hôspital’s rule, one must have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log_{2}\binom{t+n-1}{n-1}}{t}=0.

It follows that as a continuous function in
t\geq 1
, there exists a constant
c_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\frac{\log_{2}\binom{t+n-1}{n-1}}{t}<c_{0}

for any
t\geq 1
. It is clear now that the inequality in (
5.17
) holds for any

b_{\pi}\geq\kappa_{\pi}+c_{0}.

Therefore it suffices to take
b_{\pi}=\max\{s_{\pi},\kappa_{\pi}+c_{0}\}
. We are done.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Absolute Convergence of
\pi
-Theta Functions)
.

Fix any
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
with Assumption
5.1
.
Then for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, the
\pi
-theta function

\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha}x)

is absolutely convergent for any
x\in{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
.

Proof.

For any
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
, let
S_{\pi}
be a finite subset of local places of
k
containing
|k|_{\infty}
and for any finite local place
\nu\not\in S_{\pi}
, the local component

\pi_{\nu}
is unramified.
We may assume that
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
is a pure restricted tensor of the form

\phi=\left(\otimes_{\nu\notin S_{\pi}}{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}\right)\otimes\left(\otimes_{\nu\in S_{\pi}}\phi_{\nu}\right)

with
\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
for all
\nu\in S_{\pi}
.

Fix a positive real number
s_{0}>b_{\pi}\geq s_{\pi}\geq\kappa_{\pi}
where the constants
\kappa_{\pi}
,
s_{\pi}
, and
b_{\pi}
are as given in Assumption
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
, and Lemma
5.3
, respectively.
By Lemma
5.3
, for any
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
, we have the function

{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}
is continuous on
k_{\nu}
and supported on
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}
. Moreover,
we have

\big|{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(x)|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}\big|\leq 1

for every
\nu\notin S_{\pi}
. Similarly, for any finite
\nu\in S_{\pi}
, the function

\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}

is continuous on
k_{\nu}
with compact support. We may assume that the support of

\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}
is contained in a fractional ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\nu}^{m_{\nu}}
for some integer
m_{\nu}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For any
{\alpha}\in k^{\times}
, the Artin product formula shows that
|{\alpha}|_{\mathbb{A}}=1
(
[
W73
]
).
Hence we obtain
that

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha})=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha})\cdot|{\alpha}|_{{\mathbb{A}}}^{s_{0}}.

Write
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\phi}:=\prod_{\nu\notin S_{\pi}}{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\phi}:=\prod_{\nu\in S_{\pi}\cap|k|_{f}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{m_{\nu}}
. Then by the weak approximation theorem (
[
W73
]
), the product

{\mathfrak{o}}_{\phi}\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}_{\phi}={\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)
is a fractional ideal of
{\mathfrak{o}}={\mathfrak{o}}_{k}
, the ring
of integers in
k
.
From the support of the functions
\phi_{\nu}\cdot|\cdot|^{s_{0}}
for all
\nu\in|k|_{f}
, we write

\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi)=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)}\left(\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\cdot|{\alpha}|_{\nu}^{s_{0}}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{f}}\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\cdot|{\alpha}|_{\nu}^{s_{0}}\right),

which, up to a positive constant, is essentially bounded by

\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)}\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\left|\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\right|\cdot|{\alpha}|_{\nu}^{s_{0}}.

Hence it suffices to show that the following summation

(5.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)}\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\left|\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\right|\cdot|{\alpha}|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}

is absolutely convergent.

To do so, we consider the following compact set

{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1):=\{({\alpha}_{\nu})\in\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}k_{\nu}\mid\ |{\alpha}_{\nu}|_{\nu}\leq 1,\forall\nu\in|k|_{\infty}\}.

We write (
5.18
) as

(5.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1)}\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\left|\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\right|\cdot|{\alpha}|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}+\sum_{{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)\smallsetminus\left({\mathfrak{m}}(\phi)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1)\right)}\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\left|\phi_{\nu}({\alpha})\right|\cdot|{\alpha}|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}

Since the fractional ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\phi}
is a lattice in the affine space
\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}k_{\nu}
,
its intersection with
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1)
is a finite set. By Lemma
5.2
, the function

\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\phi_{\nu}(x)|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}
is continuous over

\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}k_{\nu}
, and hence is bounded over
{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1)
. Thus, the first
summation in (
5.19
) is bounded.
For the second summation in (
5.19
), the function

\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}\phi_{\nu}(x)\cdot|x|^{s_{0}}_{\nu}
is of Schwartz type over

(\prod_{\nu\in|k|_{\infty}}k_{\nu})\smallsetminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}(1)
. Hence the second summation in (
5.19
)
is bounded by the same proof for the absolute convergence of the classical Poisson summation formula
(
[
W65
]
and also
[
Ig78
, Chapter 4]
).
This proves the absolute convergence of
\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)
for any

\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
.

For any
x\in{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
, we have
\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)=\Theta_{\pi}(1,\phi^{x})
with
\phi^{x}(y)=\phi(yx)
.
Hence
\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)
converges absolutely for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. We are done.
∎

Proof.

A cuspidal datum

{\mathrm{G}}_{n}

\nu

irreducible admissible representations of
G({\mathbb{A}})
, by means of the existence of
the local Langlands reciprocity map as in the local Langlands conjecture for
G
.

6.1.
On the local Langlands conjecture

We briefly review the local Langlands conjecture for
G
over any local field
F=k_{\nu}
for any local place
\nu\in|k|
.

For any Archimedean local fields, the local Langlands conjecture for
G
is a theorem of
Langlands, which follows from the Langlands classification theory (
[
L89
]
). At any non-Archimedean local places, for unramified representations, their local Langlands parameters are uniquely determined by the Satake isomorphism (
[
S63
]
) and also
[
C79
]
). In the following we review the local Langlands
conjecture for
F
-split reductive group
G
over non-Archimedean local field
F
of characteristic zero.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
be the Weil group attached to
F
. The set of local Langlands parameters is denoted by

\Phi_{F}(G)
, which continuous, Frobenius semisimple homomorphisms

(6.1)

\displaystyle\varsigma\colon{\mathcal{W}}_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow G^{\vee},

up to conjugation by
G^{\vee}
. The local Langlands conjecture asserts that there exists a reciprocity map

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}\colon\mathrm{Rep}(G(F))\longrightarrow\Phi_{F}(G),

where
\mathrm{Rep}(G(F))
is the set of equivalence classes of smooth representations of

G(F)
of finite length.
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
is expected to be surjective with finite fibers, and to satisfy
a series of compatibility conditions. One of the key issues is to formulate and prove the uniqueness of such local Langlands reciprocity map.

When
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, it is a theorem of Harris-Taylor (
[
HT01
]
), of G. Henniart (
[
H00
]
) and of
P. Scholze (
[
Sc13
]
) that the local Langlands reciprocity map exists and is unique with compatibility of
local factors, plus other conditions. Note that in this case, the uniqueness of such a local Langlands
reciprocity map is proved by Henniart using the special case of the local converse theorem (
[
H93
]
).
However, such a uniqueness is not known in general. When
G
is an
F
-quasisplit classical group,
then such a local Langlands reciprocity map exists due to the endoscopic classification of J. Arthur
(
[
Ar13
]
).

In their recent work (
[
FS21
]
), L. Fargues and P. Scholze use the geometrization method to understand the local Langlands conjecture. In particular, they establish a local Langlands reciprocity
map for any
F
-split reductive groups considered in this paper. More precise, Theorem I.9.6 of

[
FS21
]
asserts that for any
F
-split reductive group
G
, there exists a local Langlands reciprocity
map
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
from
\mathrm{Rep}(G(F))
to
\Phi_{F}(G)
, satisfying nine compatibility conditions. In particular
when
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze coincides with the unique one for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.
When
G
is an
F
-quasisplit classical group, the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze coincides with
the one by Arthur. Although it is still not known (as far as the authors know) if the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze is unique, it is the most promising one towards the local Langlands conjecture in
great generality.

From now on, we are going to take the following assumption.

Assumption 6.1
.

Over any non-Archimedean local field
F
of characteristic zero, for any
F
-split reductive group
G
, the reciprocity map
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
exists for the local Langlands conjecture for
G
.

We may simply take the reciprocity map
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
as defined in
[
FS21
, Theorem I.9.6]
for the local Langlands conjecture. In fact, the relevant discussions in the rest of this paper make no essential difference
on which reciprocity map
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
we are going to take. Of course, the difference may occur if one discuss
the definition of local
L
-functions
L(s,\sigma,\rho)
or
{\gamma}
-functions
{\gamma}(s,\sigma,\rho,\psi)
.
but we are not going to discuss those objects in the rest of this paper.

6.2.
Absolute convergence of
({\sigma},\rho)
-theta functions

Let
G
be a
k
-split reductive group. Take
\rho\colon G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
to be any
finite dimensional representation of the complex dual group
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
.
For any unitary
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{A}}(G)
, we write
\sigma=\otimes_{\nu}\sigma_{\nu}
with

\sigma_{\nu}\in\Pi_{k_{\nu}}(G)
, the set of equivalence classes of irreducible admissible representations of

G(k_{\nu})
. Note that at any Archimedean local place
\nu
of
k
, the local representation
\sigma_{\nu}
is
assumed to be of Casselman-Wallach type.

By Assumption
6.1
, for any local place
\nu\in|k|
, there exists a local
L
-parameter

\varsigma_{\nu}=\varsigma_{\nu}(\sigma_{\nu})
such that the composition
\rho\circ\varsigma_{\nu}
is a local

L
-parameter for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
. By the local Langlands conjecture for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
(
[
L89
]
,
[
H00
]
,
[
HT01
]
, and
[
Sc13
]
), there exists a unique irreducible admissible representation

(6.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{\nu}=\pi_{\nu}(\sigma,\rho,{\mathfrak{R}}_{k_{\nu},G})

belongs to
\Pi_{F}(n)
, which we may simply denote, if there is no confusion, by

(6.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{\nu}=\pi_{\nu}(\sigma_{\nu},\rho).

Define the
(\sigma_{\nu},\rho)
-Schwartz space
on
k_{\nu}^{\times}
to be

(6.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho}(k_{\nu}^{\times}):={\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times}).

At unramified local places, the
(\sigma_{\nu},\rho)
-basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho}
is taken to be
the
\pi_{\nu}
-basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\pi_{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi_{\nu}}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
. Then we can define the
(\sigma,\rho)
-Schwartz space
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
to be the restricted tensor product:

(6.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times}):=\otimes_{\nu}{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho}(k_{\nu}^{\times})

with respect to the basic function
{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho}
at almost all finite local places. Note that
the definition of the
(\sigma,\rho)
-Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
may rely on
the assumption of the local Langlands reciprocity map (Assumption
6.1
) at the ramified finite local
places of
\sigma
, when
G
is a general
k
-split reductive group.

Let
\psi=\otimes_{\nu}\psi_{\nu}
be a non-trivial additive character of
{\mathbb{A}}
with
\psi(a)=1
for any
a\in k
.
Define the
(\sigma_{\nu},\rho)
-Fourier operator

{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho,\psi_{\nu}}
on
k_{\nu}^{\times}
to be

(6.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho,\psi_{\nu}}:={\mathcal{F}}_{\pi_{\nu},\psi_{\nu}},

which is a linear transformation from the
(\sigma_{\nu},\rho)
-Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
to the
(\widetilde{\sigma_{\nu}},\rho)
-Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\widetilde{\sigma_{\nu}},\rho}(k_{\nu}^{\times})
. Then we define the
(\sigma,\rho)
-Fourier operator

(6.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma,\rho,\psi}:=\otimes_{\nu}{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma_{\nu},\rho,\psi_{\nu}},

which is a linear transformation from the
(\sigma,\rho)
-Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\sigma,\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
to the
(\widetilde{\sigma},\rho)
-Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\widetilde{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. Again, the definition of the
(\sigma,\rho)
-Fourier operator

{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma,\rho,\psi}
may rely on
the assumption of the local Langlands reciprocity map (Assumption
6.1
) at the ramified finite local
places of
\sigma
, when
G
is a general
k
-split reductive group.

Theorem 6.2

(Absolute Convergence of
({\sigma},\rho)
-Theta Functions)
.

Let
\rho\colon G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be any finite dimensional representation of the complex
dual group
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
. Take Assumption
6.1
for
G
.
Then for any given unitary
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{A}}(G)
, the
({\sigma},\rho)
-theta function

\Theta_{{\sigma},\rho}(x,\phi):=\sum_{{\alpha}\in k^{\times}}\phi({\alpha}x)

converges absolutely for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
x\in{\mathbb{A}}^{\times}
.

Proof.

As discussed above, under Assumption
6.1
for
G
, for any

{\sigma}=\otimes_{\nu}{\sigma}_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{A}}(G)
, we obtain
\pi_{\nu}=\pi_{\nu}({\sigma}_{\nu},\rho)
of

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(k_{\nu})
for all
\nu\in|k|
. Note that at
\nu\in|k|_{\infty}
,
\pi_{\nu}
is taken to be of Casselman-Wallach type. Hence
\pi:=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}
is an irreducible admissible representation of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
and belongs to
\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
. From (
6.5
) and (
6.6
), we have that

\Theta_{{\sigma},\rho}(x,\phi)=\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})={\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. By Theorem
5.4
, it is
sufficient to verify Assumption
5.1
for this
\pi
.

Since
{\sigma}=\otimes_{\nu}{\sigma}_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(G)
is unitary, for any
\nu\in|k|
,
{\sigma}_{\nu}
is an irreducible
admissible unitary representation of
G(k_{\nu})
, and is unramified for almost all
\nu\in|k|
.
Since
G
is
k
-split, we can fix a Borel pair
(B,T)
of
G
defined over
k
, with a fixed maximal

k
-split torus
T
of
G
. Let
\varrho
be the half-sum of positive roots with respect to the given pair

(B,T)
and let
{\delta}_{B}
be the modular character of
B(k_{\nu})
. Then for any coweight

\omega^{\vee}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathbb{G}}_{m},T)
,

{\delta}_{B}(\omega^{\vee}(\varpi_{\nu}))^{1/2}=q_{\nu}^{\langle\varrho,\omega^{\vee}\rangle}

where
\varpi_{\nu}
is a fixed uniformizer in
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\nu}
and
\omega^{\vee}
is viewed as a cocharacter from
k^{\times}_{\nu}
to
T(k_{\nu})
.

Let
S
be a finite subset of
|k|
containing
|k|_{\infty}
, such that for any
\nu\notin S
, both

{\sigma}_{\nu}
and
\pi_{\nu}
are unramified. For any
\nu\notin S
,
{\sigma}_{\nu}
is unitary and unramified. Then the zonal spherical function attached to
{\sigma}_{\nu}
, which is the normalized matrix coefficient of
{\sigma}_{\nu}
attached to spherical vectors in
{\sigma}_{\nu}
, is bounded by
1
(see
[
C79
, p.151, (40)]
for instance). Now let

c({\sigma}_{\nu})=(q_{\nu}^{s_{1}({\sigma}_{\nu})},...,q_{\nu}^{s_{r}({\sigma}_{\nu})})

be the Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy class of
{\sigma}_{\nu}
inside
T^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})\simeq({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{r}
, where
r
is the
k
-rational rank of
G
. Then by
[
Mac71
, Theorem 4.7.1]
,

\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\{|s_{j}({\sigma}_{\nu})|\}\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\{|\langle\varrho,\omega^{\vee}_{j}\rangle|\}

where
\{\omega^{\vee}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
is a fixed set of fundamental coweights.
Note that the result of
[
Mac71
]
assumes
G
to be simply-connected. But if we go over the proof
of
[
Mac71
, Theorem 4.7.1]
, the only result used is the explicit formula for zonal spherical functions
when the Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy class
c({\sigma}_{\nu})
of
{\sigma}_{\nu}
is non-singular.
Hence it suffices to apply the general formula appearing in
[
Cas80
, Theorem 4.2]
to the proof
of
[
Mac71
, Theorem 4.7.1]
.
Therefore
\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\{|s_{j}({\sigma}_{\nu})|\}
has an upper bound which is independent of the local places
\nu
.

At unramified local places,
we obtain the Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy class
c(\pi_{\nu})
of
\pi_{\nu}
to be

c(\pi_{\nu})=\rho(c({\sigma}_{\nu})).

for all
\nu\notin S
. It is clear that for this
\pi=\otimes_{\nu}\pi_{\nu}\in\Pi_{\mathbb{A}}(n)
, the family of
the Frobenius-Hecke conjugacy classes

\{c(\pi_{\nu})\mid\forall\nu\notin S\}

associated to the irreducible admissible representation
\pi
satisfies Assumption
5.1
. We are done.
∎

Note that the definition of the
(\sigma,\rho)
-theta function
\Theta_{\sigma,\rho}(x,\phi)
may depend
on the existence of the local Langlands reciprocity map
{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
for general
G
(Assumption
6.1
), However, the absolute convergence of
\Theta_{\sigma,\rho}(x,\phi)
in Theorem
6.2
only depends on the unramified data, and hence is independent of Assumption
6.1
.
As a consequence of Theorem
4.7
, we have

Corollary 6.3
.

Assume that the global Langlands functoriality is valid for
(G,\rho)
.
For
{\sigma}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(G)
, if its functorial transfer
\pi
is cuspidal on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
, then
Conjecture
1.1
holds with
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\sigma},\rho}(\phi)=\Theta_{{\sigma},\rho}(1,\phi)
for
any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
.

7.
Variants of Conjecture
1.1

In Theorem
4.7
, we have established a
\pi
-Poisson summation formula
(Conjecture
1.1
) for any
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cusp}}({\mathrm{G}}_{n})
and
\rho=\rm std
. In this section, we explore the possibilities
when
\pi
is not cuspidal.

7.1.
Certain special Schwartz functions

As before, we take
F
to be any local field of characteristic zero. For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, recall from
Definition
3.3
the space of
\pi
-Schwartz functions:

{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})={\mathrm{Span}}\{\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F^{\times})\mid\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)),\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)\},

where the
\pi
-Schwartz function
\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}
associated to a pair
(\xi,\varphi_{\pi})
is defined in (
3.6
). We introduce here a subspace of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
:

(7.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}^{\circ}(F^{\times}):={\mathrm{Span}}\{\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}\mid\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)),\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)\}.

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
F
be any local field of characteristic zero. For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the subspace

{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}^{\circ}(F^{\times})
of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
as defined in (
7.1
) is equal to the space

{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
of all smooth, compactly supported functions on
F^{\times}
.

First of all, via the determinant morphism
\det\colon{\mathrm{G}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, it is not hard to verify that the
fibre integration

\xi\mapsto\int_{\det g=x}\xi(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g

yields a surjective homomorphism from
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
to
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
.
For any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, the product
\xi(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)
belongs
to
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
. With the fibre integration through
\det
, the function
\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x)

belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
. Hence we obtain that

(7.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}^{\circ}(F^{\times})\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})

for any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
.
To prove the converse of (
7.2
), we are going to use different arguments for the non-Archimedean
case and the Archimedean case.

We first consider the
non-Archimedean case.

For any quasi-character
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
, it can be written in a unique way as
\chi(x)=|x|_{F}^{s}\cdot{\omega}(x)
with
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
. We may write
\chi=\chi_{s,{\omega}}
and

{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})={\mathbb{C}}\times{\Omega}^{\wedge}
. Furthermore, we write the space
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times}))
defined
in Definition
2.2
as

{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathbb{C}}\times{\Omega}^{\wedge})
. We denote by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
the subspace of
{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathbb{C}}\times{\Omega}^{\wedge})

consisting of functions
{\mathfrak{z}}(\chi_{s,{\omega}})={\mathfrak{z}}(s,{\omega})\in{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathbb{C}}\times{\Omega}^{\wedge})
with the following two properties:

(1)

{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})
is nonzero at finitely many
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
;

(2)

For any
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
,
{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})\in{\mathbb{C}}[q^{s},q^{-s}]
.

By Theorem
2.3
, the subspace
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
is in one to one correspondence with

{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(F^{\times})
via the Mellin transform and its inversion. Denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}
the
subspace of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
that is in ono to one correspondence with the subspace
{\mathcal{S}}^{\circ}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
.
From the discussion right after
[
Luo
, Theorem 3.1.1]
, for any given
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
,
the following subspace

{\mathcal{I}}^{\circ}_{\pi,{\omega}}:=\{{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},{\omega})\mid\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)),\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)\}

of the fractional ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{\pi,{\omega}}
as in Theorem
2.4

is equal to
{\mathbb{C}}[q^{s},q^{-s}]
. For the fixed
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
, the space
{\mathcal{I}}^{\circ}_{\pi,{\omega}}

consists of the restriction of functions in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
to the slice
{\mathbb{C}}\times\{{\omega}\}
, according to the definition of the space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
.
In other words, for any fixed
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
and
{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
,
there exists finitely many
\xi^{j}_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and

\varphi^{j}_{\pi,{\omega}}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, such that

{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})=\sum_{j}{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi^{j}_{\omega},\varphi^{j}_{\pi,{\omega}},{\omega})=\sum_{j}{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\xi^{j}_{\omega},\varphi^{j}_{\pi,{\omega}}},{\omega})

for any
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence with any fixed
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
, for any
{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
,
there exists
{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}({s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}
such that

(7.3)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})={\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}({s,{\omega}})

as functions in
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Define, for each
{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
, a function
{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})
with property:

{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})=\begin{cases}1,&{\rm if}\ {\omega}={\omega}_{0};\\
0,&{\rm if}\ {\omega}\neq{\omega}_{0}.\end{cases}

By definition, the function
{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
for each
{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
.
Hence from (
7.3
), we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})=\sum_{{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})=\sum_{{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}({s,{\omega}_{0}}),

for any
{\mathfrak{z}}({s,{\omega}})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
. Note here that the summations only takes finitely many

{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
. Hence to prove the converse of (
7.2
), it is enough to show that

(7.5)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{z}}_{{\omega}_{0}}(s,{\omega})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}({s,{\omega}_{0}})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}

for every
{\omega}_{0}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
. It is clear that (
7.5
) is an easy consequence of the following proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

The space
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
is an associative algebra without identity, and
the space
{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}
is an
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
-module under multiplication.

Proof.

From the definition of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
, it is clear that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
is an associative algebra under the multiplication
and has no identity.

To prove that
{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}
is an
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
-module, we take
{\mathfrak{z}}(s,{\omega})\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{cpt}}
and write
\phi
as the Mellin inversion of
{\mathfrak{z}}(s,{\omega})
. Via the determinant morphism
\det:{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\to F^{\times}
, we write

\phi(x)=\int_{\det g=x}f(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

for some
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
. For any
\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
,
we write
{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}(s,{\omega})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}
to be the Mellin transform of the function

\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\circ}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
. It is enough to show that

(7.6)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{z}}(s,{\omega})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}(s,{\omega})\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}_{\pi}.

It is clear that

(7.7)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{z}}(s,{\omega})\cdot{\mathfrak{z}}^{\circ}(s,{\omega})={\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi*\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}},{\omega}).

Now we compute the right-hand side of (
7.7
) with a fixed
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi*\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}},{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in F^{\times}}{\omega}(x)|x|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x\int_{y\in F^{\times}}\phi(y)\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(y^{-1}x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y

(7.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{F^{\times}}{\omega}(x)|x|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x\int_{F^{\times}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y\int_{\det g=y}f(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{y}g

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\int_{\det h=y^{-1}x}\xi(h)\varphi_{\pi}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{y^{-1}x}h.

After changing variable
g\to gh^{-1}
, the right-hand side of (
7.1
) is equal to

(7.9)

\displaystyle\int_{F^{\times}}{\omega}(x)|x|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x\int_{F^{\times}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y\int_{\det g=x}f(gh^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g\int_{\det h=y^{-1}x}\xi(h)\varphi_{\pi}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{y^{-1}x}h.

In (
7.9
), the integration in
y\in F^{\times}
yields the following identity:

(7.10)

\displaystyle\int_{y\in F^{\times}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y\int_{\det h=y^{-1}x}f(gh^{-1})\xi(h)\varphi_{\pi}(h)\,\mathrm{d}_{y^{-1}x}h=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}f(gh^{-1})\xi(h)\varphi_{\pi}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

. Then the following embedding

(8.7)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi}\colon\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})\mapsto\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi)\in L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})

with respect to the sobolev norms defined in (
8.3
) and (
8.6
), respectively.

Denote
by
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
the completion of the image
\Theta_{\pi}({\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times}))
in
L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
.
Since

{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(y)(\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(x)=\Theta_{\pi}(x,{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(y)\phi)

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
, with
x,y\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
, the closed subspace
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
is also
a
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
-module. Define the quotient space

(8.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta}:=L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})/\overline{\Theta_{\pi}},

which is also a
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
-module. The associated representation is denoted by
{\mathfrak{r}}_{\pi,\delta}
.
It is clear that the restriction of the
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
-module to
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}
is unitary
and has the following decomposition

(8.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta}|_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}}=\oplus_{\chi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}}}\ {\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}.

By the fixed (non-canonical) decomposition in (
8.2
), each eigenspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
is a
module of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
. The associated representation is denoted by
{\mathfrak{r}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
.
Note that
{\mathfrak{r}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
is also a representation of
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}={\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
on

{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
.
The action of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
generates a flow
with the infinitesimal generator

(8.10)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}(\theta):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left({\mathfrak{r}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}(\exp(\epsilon)-1)\right)\theta

for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
. As in
[
Cn99
]
, one should take the pair

(8.11)

\displaystyle({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi})

to be a candidate of the Pólya-Hilbert space. We call it a
Pólya-Hilbert-Connes pair
.

For any
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}}
, by the fixed non-canonical decomposition

{\mathcal{C}}_{k}={\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
as in (
8.2
), the character
\chi
has a unique extension to

{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
by defining that it is trivial on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
. We may still denote the extended character by

\chi
.

Theorem 8.2

(Critical Zeros of
L(s,\pi\times\chi)
)
.

Given any
\pi\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(n)
and a character
\chi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}}
, take
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
as in (
8.10
) with
\delta>1
.

(1)

The spectrum
{\mathrm{Sp}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi})
is discrete and is contained in
i\cdot{\mathbb{R}}
with
i=\sqrt{-1}
.

(2)

\mu\in{\mathrm{Sp}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi})
if and only if
L(\frac{1}{2}+\mu,\pi\times\chi)=0
.

(3)

The multiplicity
m_{{\mathrm{Sp}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi})}(\mu)
is equal to the largest integer
m<\frac{1+\delta}{2}
with
m\leq m_{L(s,\pi\times\chi)}(\frac{1}{2}+\mu)
, the multiplicity of
\frac{1}{2}+\mu

as a zero of the automorphic
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\chi)
.

Note Theorem
8.2
can be viewed as a reformulation of
[
S01
, Theorem 2]
in the adelic framework of
[
Cn99
]
and is an extension of
[
Cn99
, Theorem III.1]
from the Hecke
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)

to the standard automorphic
L
-functions
L(s,\pi\times\chi)
. See also
[
D01
]
for relevant discussion.

8.2.
Proof of Theorem
8.2

We are going to prove Theorem
8.2
by using an argument that combines the approach of
[
Cn99
]

and that of
[
S01
]
.

Consider the following pairing

(8.12)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})\ \times\ L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})&\rightarrow&{\mathbb{C}}\\
(\theta,\eta)&\mapsto&<\theta,\eta>.\end{matrix}

where the pairing is defined by the following integral

<\theta,\eta>:=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}\theta(x)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x.

For any
y\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
, we have

<{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(y)\theta,\eta>=<\theta,{\mathfrak{r}}_{-\delta}(y^{-1})\eta>

for any
\theta\in L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and
\eta\in L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
.

Consider a function
\eta\in L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
as a distribution on the eigenspace

{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
. Then we must have

(8.13)

\displaystyle<\theta,\eta>=0

for any
\theta\in\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
, and for any
t\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}
,

{\mathfrak{r}}_{-\delta}(t)\eta=\chi^{-1}(t)\eta

as a distribution on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\pi,\delta,\chi}
. Hence we may write,
for
x=ta\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}={\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
, the fixed non-canonical decomposition,

(8.14)

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\chi^{-1}(t)\beta(a)

where
\beta(a)
is a measurable function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
with

\|\beta\|_{\delta}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}}|\beta(a)|^{2}(1+(\log|a|)^{2})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}a<\infty.

The orthogonality in (
8.13
) can be written as

(8.15)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}\Theta_{\pi}(x,\phi)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=0

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. As in
[
S01
]
, we prove the following lemma,
which is a reformulation of Lemma 1 of
[
S01
]
.

Lemma 8.3
.

The subspace of
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
generated by functions of type:

(b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(t)=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}b(x)\Theta_{\pi}(x^{-1}t,\phi)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x

with all
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
is dense in
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
.

Proof.

The proof is a reformulation of the proof of
[
S01
, Lemma 1]
. For any

\theta\in\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
, we have

(b*\theta)(t)=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}b(x)\theta(x^{-1}t)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}b(x)\theta^{\vee}(t^{-1}x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x={\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(b)(\theta^{\vee})(t^{-1})

for any
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
. since
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
is a closed subspace of

L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and is a
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
-module, it is clear that
b*\theta
belongs to

\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
.
In particular, we have that
b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi)
belongs to
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
for
all
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
.

Next, by
[
Cn99
, Lemma 5]
, there exists a sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}
with
f_{n}
belonging
to the space
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
of the Bruhat-Schwartz functions on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
, such that

{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(f_{n})
tends strongly to one in
L^{2}_{\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and the norm of
{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(f_{n})

are bounded. Now following the same argument as in the proof of
[
S01
, Lemma 1]
, we obtain that there exists a sequence of functions
b_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
with property that

(1)

{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(b_{n})
converges strongly to one;

(2)

the norm of
{\mathfrak{r}}_{\delta}(b_{n})
is bounded;

(3)

b_{n}*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi)
converges to
\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi)
for any

\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
.

Therefore the linear span of
b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi)
with
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and

\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
is dense in
\overline{\Theta_{\pi}}
. We are done.
∎

By Lemma
8.3
, it is enough to consider the orthogonality

(8.16)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}(b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(x)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=0

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
.

Lemma 8.4
.

For any
\eta\in L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
,
the integral

\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}(b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(x)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x

is zero for any
b\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
if and only if

L(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\pi\times\chi)\cdot{\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})

is zero as a function in
\chi_{i\mu}
, where
\chi_{i\mu}
is any unitary character of
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
that
can be written as
\chi_{i\mu}(x)=\chi(t)a^{i\mu}
for
x=ta\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k}={\mathcal{C}}_{k}^{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}_{+}
, the fixed non-canonical decomposition.

Proof.

We are going to apply the Parseval formula for the Fourier transform from
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
to its unitary dual
\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}
to (
8.16
). Since
\chi_{i\mu}(x)=\chi(t)a^{i\mu}
, the Fourier transform
for
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
is

{\mathcal{M}}(\theta)(\chi_{i\mu})=\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}\theta(x)\chi_{i\mu}^{-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x.

By applying the Parseval formula to the integral

\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}(b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(x)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x,

we obtain that (
8.16
)
is equivalent to

(8.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}}{\mathcal{M}}(b)(\chi_{i\mu}){\mathcal{M}}(\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(\chi_{i\mu}){\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})\,\mathrm{d}\chi_{i\mu}=0

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
. It is easy to verify from definition
that

{\mathcal{M}}(\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(\chi_{i\mu})={\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi,\chi),

where the right-hand side is the global (
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
) zeta integral as defined in (
4.4
). From Corollary
4.4
and
[
GJ72
, Proposition 13.9]
, the global zeta integral
{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi,\chi)

is a bounded function in
\mu
. Hence the product

{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi,\eta}(\chi_{i\mu}):={\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi,\chi)\cdot{\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})

is a tempered distribution on
\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}
. Hence (
8.17
) is the same as

(8.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}}{\mathcal{M}}(b)(\chi_{i\mu}){\mathcal{T}}_{\phi,\eta}(\chi_{i\mu})\,\mathrm{d}\chi_{i\mu}=0

for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
.
Denote by
\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}_{\phi,\eta}(x)
the (inverse) Fourier transform of

{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi,\eta}(\chi_{i\mu})
. By using the Parseval formula for the (inverse) Fourier transform, we obtain
that (
8.18
) is equivalent to

(8.19)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}b(x)\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}_{\phi,\eta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x=0

for all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and
b(x)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
. Hence we must have that
(
8.19
) holds if and only if

\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}_{\phi,\eta}(x)=0
as distribution on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
,
which is equivalent to that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi,\eta}(\chi_{i\mu})=0
as distribution on
\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}
. In other words, we obtain that for any
\eta\in L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
,
the integral

\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{k}}(b*\Theta_{\pi}(\cdot,\phi))(x)\eta(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x

is zero for any
b\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{C}}_{k})
and any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
if and only if

(8.20)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi,\chi)\cdot{\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})=0

for all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
. By Corollary
4.4
and
[
GJ72
, Theorem 13.8]
, there exist
a finite many
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
such that

{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi_{1},\chi)+\cdots+{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\phi_{\ell},\chi)=L(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\pi\times\chi).

Thus we obtain that (
8.20
) implies

(8.21)

\displaystyle L(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\pi\times\chi)\cdot{\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})=0

as a function in
\chi_{i\mu}
.

To prove the converse, we consider factorizable data:
\phi=\otimes_{\nu}\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})

and
\chi=\otimes_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}
. The global zeta integral factorizes into an Euler product

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)=\prod_{\nu}{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},\chi_{\nu})

By Theorem
3.4
, we obtain that

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)=L(s,\pi\times\chi)\cdot\prod_{\nu\in S}\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},\chi_{\nu})}{L(s,\pi_{\nu}\times\chi_{\nu})}

where
S
is the finite set of local places, including all Archimedean local places of
k
, such that
for any
\nu\not\in S
, the data
\pi_{\nu}
and
\chi_{\nu}
are unramified, and the quotient

\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi_{\nu},\chi_{\nu})}{L(s,\pi_{\nu}\times\chi_{\nu})}

is holomorphic in
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence if
\eta\in L^{2}_{-\delta}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
satisfies

L(\frac{1}{2}+i\mu,\pi\times\chi)\cdot{\mathcal{M}}(\eta)(\chi_{i\mu})=0

as a function in
\chi_{i\mu}
, i.e. (
8.21
) holds,
then (
8.20
) holds for factorizable data:
\phi=\otimes_{\nu}\phi_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})

and
\chi=\otimes_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}
. Hence it holds for all
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and all
\chi
.
We are done.
∎

8.2

[
S01
, Page 178]
, which follows from the same argument of Connes (pp. 86–87, in the proof
of Theorem III.1 of
[
Cn99
]
). We omit the details.

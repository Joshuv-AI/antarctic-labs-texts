New form of kernel in equation for Nakanishi function
V.A. Karmanov

Abstract
The Bethe-Salpeter amplitude Φ ⁡ ( k , p ) \Phi(k,p) is expressed, by means of the Nakanishi integral representation, via a smooth function g ⁡ ( γ , z ) g(\gamma,z) . This function satisfies a canonical equation g = N ​ g g=Ng . However, calculations of the kernel N N in this equation, presented previously, were restricted to one-boson exchange and, depending on method, dealt with complex multivalued functions. Although these difficulties are surmountable, but in practice, they complicate finding the unambiguous result. In the present work, an unambiguous expression for the kernel N N in terms of real functions is derived. For the one-boson scalar exchange, the explicit formula for N N is found. With this equation and kernel, the binding energies, calculated previously, are reproduced. Their finding, as well as calculation of the Bethe-Salpeter amplitude in the Minkowski space, become not more difficult than in the Euclidean one. The method can be generalized to any kernel given by irreducible Feynman graph. This generalization is illustrated by example of the cross-ladder kernel.

Introduction

One of the efficient methods of solving the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation
[
1
]

\displaystyle\Phi(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i^{2}}{\left[(\frac{p}{2}+k)^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon\right]\left[(\frac{p}{2}-k)^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon\right]}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}iK(k,k^{\prime},p)\Phi(k^{\prime},p),

on using the Nakanishi representation
[
2
]
for the BS amplitude:

\displaystyle\Phi(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int_{-1}^{1}dz^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\gamma^{\prime}

(2)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{g(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})}{\left[\gamma^{\prime}+m^{2}-\frac{1}{4}M^{2}-k^{2}-p\cdot k\;z^{\prime}-i\epsilon\right]^{3}}.

(here

and finding the function

2

1

g(\gamma,z)
satisfies an equation which is relatively easy solvable and its solution
is smooth. Having found it numerically, one can restore by Eq. (
2

Then,
after substituting

For the massless ladder kernel (Wick-Cutkosky model

]

For massive ladder exchange kernel:

(3)

(

)

[

. Subsequent researches

,

,

,

In Ref.
[
8
]
, by substituting

in the form (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\gamma^{\prime}\int_{-1}^{1}dz^{\prime}\;V(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})g(\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime}),

The kernel

The equation in the form (
I

This resulted in instability of the numerical procedure which though was overcome in Ref.
[
8

Further efforts in this field were then directed to derivation of equation in the ”normal” form:

[
10
]
by numerical solution of Eq. (

I
) found in
[
8
]
and with the Euclidean space solutions. Besides, the stability of results was much better.

Then in Ref.
[
11
]
it was noticed that the integral in l.h.-side of Eq. (

) had a form of the Stieltjes transform which could be inverted analytically.
Inverting it, an equation, again in the form (

of the equation (

\displaystyle N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi+\epsilon}^{\pi-\epsilon}\;d\phi\;e^{i\phi}

\displaystyle\phantom{\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}}\times V\Bigl(\gamma e^{i\phi}-z^{2}m^{2}-(1-z^{2})\kappa^{2},z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime}\Bigr)

The equations in the form (

[

[

However, these kernels were obtained in rather different forms, so it was uneasy to compare them analytically. Their equivalence was demonstrated in
[

For the first glance, the way from the kernel

1

However, as it was noticed and demonstrated in examples already in

[

I

Though this ambiguity can be eliminated, this delicate problem requires some extra efforts and very attentive and careful definitions of branches of the multivalued complex functions.
This complicates the direct practical applications of the approach
[
11

to solving the BS equation via finding the function

from Eq. (

The aim of the present work is to represent the kernel
N
in Eq. (
5

We emphasize that we are only talking about a new, more convenient

of one and the same kernel.
The kernel

[
10
,
11
]
. However, its form is completely unambiguous, restricted to the real functions only, does not require delicate choosing branches of multivalued functions and therefore it makes direct numerical solving Eq. (

The advantages of this new representation of the kernel
N
in the equation (
5

Further content of the present paper is the following. In Sec.

[

,

]
. This serves as another test of our kernel and shows that the equation (

In Sec.
V

26

F(v)
depends on the sign of

D_{1}=B_{1}^{2}-4A_{1}C_{1}.

(28)

Namely, calculating the integral (
26
), we find:

F(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F^{-}(v),&\mbox{if $D_{1}<0$}\\
F^{+}(v),&\mbox{if $D_{1}>0$}\end{array}\right.,

(29)

where

\displaystyle F^{-}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha m^{2}}{2\pi}\left[\frac{B_{1}C_{1}+(B_{1}^{2}-2A_{1}C_{1})v}{A_{1}|D_{1}|[C_{1}+v(B_{1}+vA_{1})]}\right.

(30)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{4C_{1}}{|D_{1}|^{3/2}}\arctan\frac{B_{1}+2vA_{1}}{\sqrt{|D_{1}|}}\right]

and

\displaystyle F^{+}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha m^{2}}{2\pi}\left[\frac{B_{1}C_{1}+(B1^{2}-2A_{1}C_{1})v}{A_{1}D_{1}[C_{1}+v(B_{1}+vA_{1})]}\right.

(31)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{2C_{1}}{D_{1}^{3/2}}\log\left(1-\frac{2\sqrt{D_{1}}}{B_{1}+\sqrt{D_{1}}+2A_{1}v}\right)\right].

The equation (
25
) together with Eqs. (
29
-
31
) determine the regular part contribution
N_{reg}
.

Singular part contribution

The singular part contribution results from the integral (
12
) where
b(v)=a(v)
for some value of
v
. To extract it from (
12
), we calculate this integral analytically and then take the limit

b(v)\to a(v)
. Calculating the integral via primitive, we find:

\displaystyle n(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2c(v)(\pi-\epsilon)}{b^{2}(v)}+\frac{c(v)Arg\Bigl[b+a(v)\exp[i(\epsilon-\pi)]\Bigr]}{b^{2}(v)}-\frac{c(v)Arg\Big[(b(v)+a(v)\exp[i(-\epsilon+\pi)]\Bigr]}{b^{2}(v)}

(32)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{2a(v)c(v)\sin\epsilon}{b(v)[(b(v)-a(v)\cos\epsilon)^{2}+a^{2}(v)\sin^{2}\epsilon]}.

At
\epsilon\to 0
and
b(v)\to a(v)
, the last term in (
32
) only is singular and dominates. We keep it, take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
and make the replacement
a(v)\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}
.
In this way we find:

n_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)=-\frac{2c(v)\epsilon^{\prime}}{b(v)[(b(v)-a(v))^{2}+{\epsilon^{\prime}}^{2}]}.

Using the formula:

\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\epsilon}{(x^{2}+\epsilon^{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon\to 0}}{{=}}\delta(x),

we obtain

n_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)=-\frac{2\pi c(v)}{a(v)}\delta[b(v)-a(v)].

(33)

The difference
b(v)-a(v)
(a second order polynomial in
v
) is represented in the form of product, Eq. (
19
).
If roots
v_{\mp}
of the equation
b(v)-a(v)=0
vs.
v
are real and both are in the interval
0<v<1
, then the integral over
v
(the second term in (
15
)) is reduced to the sum over roots:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}n_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{-})

(34)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{+})

where we denoted

N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)=-\frac{2\pi c(v)}{A_{0}|v_{+}-v_{-}|\,a(v)},

(35)

a(v),\,c(v)
are defined in (
II.2
),
A_{0}
is defined in (
20
).
If only one root
v_{-}
is in the interval
0<v<1
, then instead of the sum (
34
) we should take the contribution of this root only:

\int_{0}^{1}n_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)dv=N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{-}).

(36)

As explained above, two real roots
v_{\mp}
have the same sign. Therefore the situation when one root
v_{+}
is in the interval
0<v<1
, whereas the root
v_{-}
is outside (and, hence,
v_{-}<0
) is impossible.

If both roots are outside of the interval
0<v<1
, the singular part does not contribute. This analysis is similar to one given in the points

1
-
4
above Eq. (
25
).

In this way, the equations
(
34
-
36
)
determine the singular part contribution
N_{sing}
.

III
Summary

In this section, summarizing the above results, we give the recipe of calculating the ladder massive exchange kernel

N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
in the equation (
5
). It takes a few following steps. Depending on the relation between
z,z^{\prime}
(
z<z^{\prime}
or
z>z^{\prime}
), the kernel

7

25

, eq. (
35
). The function

is constructed via another function
F(v)
, eq. (
29

N_{reg}
and

to
\tilde{N}
depend on the sign of
D_{0}
, Eq. (
24
), determining the existence of the real roots

v_{\mp}
of the equation
b(v)-a(v)=0
. If
D_{0}>0
and the real roots exist, the result depends on their positions relative to the interval
0\leq v\leq 1
. For all these cases the kernel
N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
in eq. (
5
) is defined below. The construction is a little bit lengthy, but it is coherent and absolutely unambiguous.

We do not stop at the contruction of the kernel
N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
, but we reduce below the equation (
5
) to the equation (
52
) defined in the half-interval
0\leq z\leq 1
. The kernel

in this reduced equation will be also expressed through
\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
.

We consider the following cases.

1.

D_{0}<0
. According to eq. (
23
), the roots
v_{\mp}
are complex.
Therefore, the singular contribution
N_{sing}
is absent. Besides,
b(v)-a(v)>0

Therefore

\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})=N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{1}=0,v_{2}=1)

with
N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{1},v_{2})
defined in (
25
).

2.

D_{0}>0
.
The roots
v_{\mp}
given by eq. (
23
) are real. The form of the kernel
\tilde{N}
is different in three following sub-cases, depending on the positions of the roots.

(a) The roots are out of the integration domain
0\leq v\leq 1
over
v
: either
v_{-}<v_{+}<0
, or

1<v_{-}<v_{+}
.
Then the kernel
\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
is still defined by Eq. (
37
).

(b) Both roots are in the integration interval:

0<v_{-}<v_{+}<1
. Then the kernel
\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{1}=0,v_{2}=v_{-})+N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{1}=v_{+},v_{2}=1)

(38)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{-})+N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{+}).

with
N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{1},v_{2})
defined in (
25
) and
N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v)
defined in (
35
).

(c) One root is in the integration interval
0\leq v\leq 1
, whereas the second one is at
v>1
. That is:

0<v_{-}<1<v_{+}
.
Then the kernel
\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
takes the form

\displaystyle\tilde{N}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{reg}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};0,v_{-})

(39)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle N_{sing}(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime};v_{-}).

We remind that the roots cannot have opposite signs (see remark below eq. (
24
)), therefore the case
v_{-}<0<v_{+}

The kernel
N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
is constructed via

7

20

22

This completes the calculations. Just this kernel

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{\small if $\gamma^{\prime}<\frac{1-z^{\prime}}{1-z}\gamma$}\\

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha m^{2}}{2\pi\gamma^{\prime}}\;\frac{\theta[\gamma^{\prime}(1-z)-\gamma(1-z^{\prime})]}{\gamma^{\prime}+m^{2}{z^{\prime}}^{2}+\kappa^{2}(1-{z^{\prime}}^{2})}

VII
Conclusion

We have found the new form of the kernel
N(\gamma,z,\gamma^{\prime},z^{\prime})
of the canonical equation (
5
):
g=Ng
, for the Nakanishi function
g
for the ladder kernel. This form differs from two forms found previously in Refs.
[
11
,
10
]
, though all three kernels are equivalent to each other.
The kernel in the form found in the present paper is expressed via the real functions, does not contain any ambiguities and the method of its calculation can be applied to any kernel given by irreducible Feynman graph. We outlined this generalization for the cross-ladder BS kernel. For the ladder, the integration is fulfilled analytically and the result for
N
is given by Eq. (
40
) in terms of
\tilde{N}
determined in Sec.
III
.
The solutions
g(\gamma,z)
of Eq. (
5
), defined in the interval
-1\leq z\leq 1
, are either symmetric relative to
z\to-z
, or antisymmetric.
For both solutions the equation (
5
) is reduced to Eq. (
52
), defined on the half-interval
0\leq z\leq 1
. For symmetric solution, the kernel is defined in Eq. (
51
). For antisymmetric one, the kernel is constructed as explained in Sec.
V
.
For the cross-ladder, the kernel is given by Eq. (
56
).

The canonical equation (
5
), in contrast to Eq. (
I
), does not contain the integral in l.h.-side and therefore it is solved easier, its solution is stable relative to the numerical procedure. The equation (
5
) is an equation in two variables, with smooth easy calculated kernel, and therefore finding its solution is not more difficult than for the Euclidean BS equation. However, it provides, via Nakanishi function, the BS amplitude in Minkowski space. Therefore, applications of these methods to solving the BS equation can give considerable advantages
[
12
,
13
]
.

Convolution semigroups on Rieffel deformations of locally compact quantum groups
Adam Skalski, Ami Viselter

Abstract
. Consider a locally compact quantum group 𝔾 \mathbb{G} with a closed classical abelian subgroup Γ \Gamma equipped with a 2 2 -cocycle Ψ : Γ ^ × Γ ^ → ℂ \Psi:\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C} . We study in detail the associated Rieffel deformation 𝔾 Ψ \mathbb{G}^{\Psi} and establish a canonical correspondence between Γ \Gamma -invariant convolution semigroups of states on 𝔾 \mathbb{G} and on 𝔾 Ψ \mathbb{G}^{\Psi} .

1.
Introduction and general preliminaries

The study of the Rieffel deformation in the sense of
[
Rie
]

in the context of compact quantum groups goes back at least to the
article
[
Wan
]
. At the same time an analogous deformation
was also studied for Kac algebras in the sense of Enock and Schwartz
in
[
EnV
]
, where it was
noted that the Rieffel deformation, at least formally, can be dually
realised as a special case of the Hopf algebraic cocycle twist. This
perspective was later investigated in depth in the context of general
locally compact quantum groups in
[
Kas
]

(available as a preprint since June ’06), which first recognised the
connection between the Rieffel deformation of (quantum) groups and
Landstad’s theory for crossed products
[
Land
]
.
A similar approach was adopted in
[
FiV
]

(available as a preprint since January ’08). It is worth noting that

[
Kas
]
focuses on the C
∗
-algebraic
picture and takes as ingredients of the construction a locally compact
group
G
equipped with a closed abelian subgroup
\Gamma
and a
continuous
2
-cocycle
\Psi
on
\hat{\Gamma}
, satisfying mild
additional assumptions, whereas
[
FiV
]

is concerned with the von Neumann algebraic formulation and its initial
data is a general locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
with a closed
abelian subgroup
\Gamma
and a bicharacter
\Psi
on
\hat{\Gamma}
,
again with some additional technical conditions satisfied. In each
case the authors produce a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}

in the sense of
[
KV1
]
. The need for
the extra assumptions was related to the fact that the cocycle twisting
of locally compact quantum groups was at the time not yet fully understood.
This changed with the groundbreaking work
[
DeC3
]
,
where it was shown that every cocycle twist of a locally compact quantum
group yields another locally compact quantum group.

In this paper we address the question of constructing quantum convolution
semigroups of states on Rieffel deformations. Quantum convolution
semigroups of states on locally compact quantum groups correspond
(in the probabilistic sense) to quantum Lévy processes, and we
refer to
[
SkV1
,
SkV2
]

for an introduction to the concept and its various incarnations and
applications. It should be noted that cocycle twisting was exploited
as a means of constructing quantum convolution semigroups already
in
[
SkV1
, Subsection 5.2]
,
but there – in comparison to this work – we were considering directly
the objects associated to the cocycle twist, and not to its dual.
This means in particular that the examples in
[
SkV1
, Subsection 5.2]

should be conceptually rather viewed as deformations of semigroups
of Herz–Schur multipliers, and not of classical convolution semigroups
of measures.

To achieve our aim, we formally put the Rieffel deformation of
[
Kas
,
FiV
]

under the umbrella of cocycle twisting in full generality, exploiting
De Commer’s results from
[
DeC3
]
.
We choose to work with the following setup, later called the
Standing
Hypothesis
: we start with a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}

equipped with a closed abelian subgroup
\Gamma
and a continuous

2
-cocycle
\Psi
on
\hat{\Gamma}
(with no extra assumptions).
This context has one clear advantage: the deformation procedure becomes
a fully symmetric operation, namely the new locally compact quantum
group
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}
again admits
\Gamma
as a subgroup, and
\mathbb{G}
is
itself a Rieffel deformation of
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}
.

Thus we can formulate the main result of our paper is the following
theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a locally compact quantum group with a closed
classical abelian subgroup
\Gamma
. Let
\Psi:\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C}

be a
2
-cocycle on
\hat{\Gamma}
. Then there is a canonical one-to-one
correspondence between
w^{*}
-continuous convolution semigroups
of states invariant under the adjoint action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{G}

and on its Rieffel deformation
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is in addition co-amenable, we can show that the correspondence
above preserves the
symmetry
(i.e. unitary antipode invariance)
of the relevant convolution semigroups.

As mentioned before the formulation of the theorem, the main motivation
behind developing such results comes from the desire to use a classical
locally compact group
G
with a convolution semigroup of probability
measures to produce a genuinely quantum stochastic evolution on the
deformed locally compact
quantum
group
G^{\Psi}
.
And indeed, we obtain as a very special case of the above result the
following corollary.

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group with a closed abelian
subgroup
\Gamma
and let
\Psi:\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C}

be a
2
-cocycle on
\hat{\Gamma}
. Every (symmetric)
w^{*}
-continuous
convolution semigroup of probability measures
{(\mu_{t})}_{t\geq 0}

on
G
that is invariant under the adjoint action of
\Gamma
(so
for example is supported on
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
, the centraliser of
\Gamma

inside
G
) determines in a canonical way a (symmetric)
w^{*}
-continuous
convolution semigroup
{(\mu_{t}^{\Psi})}_{t\geq 0}
of states on
the Rieffel deformation
G^{\Psi}
.

We also observe that given the assumption of the above corollary one
also has a natural realisation of
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
as a closed subgroup
of
G^{\Psi}
. This means that if we start from a convolution
semigroup of measures supported on
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
there is an alternative,
easier way of producing the semigroup
{(\mu_{t}^{\Psi})}_{t\geq 0}
.
We would like to stress however that even in this case we obtain a
non-trivial quantum stochastic evolution on the deformed quantum group.

The detailed plan of the paper is as follows. After preparing basic
notation and terminology and making a few remarks on non-degeneracy
in the last part of the introduction, in Section
2

we present the locally compact quantum group preliminaries and describe
the cocycle twisting in this analytic context, focusing on cocycles
coming from classical abelian subgroups and presenting in this case
an explicit formula for the multiplicative unitary of the twist. Section
3
,
which is potentially of independent value, formalises the dual relationship
between the C
∗
-algebraic Rieffel deformation and the cocycle twist,
all in the framework of Standing Hypothesis
2.2
. We
largely follow the approach of
[
Kas
]
,
but the greater generality forces us to improve several arguments.
Here we also study a few quantum group aspects of the Rieffel deformation,
and show that the setup with which we choose to work allows us to
view the Rieffel deformation as an ‘invertible’ transformation.
Indeed, a simple computation shows that the original Hopf–von Neumann
algebraic object is itself a cocycle twist of the twisted one, and
we discuss the corresponding fact on the level of C
∗
-algebraic
Rieffel deformations. Finally in Section
4
we
introduce convolution semigroups of states, provide a general construction
of Rieffel deformations of well-behaved completely positive maps,
and apply it together with the results of Section
3

to obtain the main theorems of the paper. In particular Theorem
A

above follows from Theorem
4.15
, and Corollary
B

follows from Theorems
4.15
and
4.18
.
Here we also discuss in Example
4.21
a concrete application
of Corollary
B
. Each subsection is based on its own individual
assumptions stated clearly in its beginning.

Lastly, we remark that the ultimate generalisation of the results
of this paper would concern the Rieffel deformation with respect to
an
arbitrary

2
-cocycle
\Omega
on
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, not necessarily
‘living on’ the dual of an abelian subgroup. This would involve
considering the dual
\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}:=\widehat{\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}}

of the cocycle twist
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
and proving that either

\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G}^{\Omega})
or
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}^{\Omega})
can
be seen as a crossed product-based deformation of
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
or
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
,
respectively, in a way that allows writing a convenient formula for
the co-multiplication of
\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}
based on that of

\mathbb{G}
(analogously to Theorems
3.2
and

3.3
). The starting point of such a
research would probably be
[
NeT
]
,
which has already found a presentation of
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G}^{\Omega})

in that spirit. The specific construction presented in our paper relies
heavily on the
2
-cocycle coming from an abelian subgroup, and probably
cannot be pushed further.

1.1.
Notation and conventions

The unit of a unital C
∗
-algebra is denoted by
\mathds{1}
. For a C
∗
-algebra

A
we write
\mathrm{M}(A)
for the multiplier algebra of
A
and denote
the unitary group of
\mathrm{M}(A)
by
\mathcal{U}(A)
. Further, given

u\in\mathcal{U}(A)
we write
\operatorname{Ad}(u)
for the inner automorphism
of
A
given by
x\mapsto uxu^{*}
. For C
∗
-algebras
A,B
, a
non-degenerate
*
-homomorphism from
A
to
\mathrm{M}(B)
is called a

morphism
from
A
to
B
and the space of these is denoted
by
\operatorname{Mor}(A,B)
. More generally, every
strict
, and in particular
every
non-degenerate
, completely positive map from
A
to

\mathrm{M}(B)

[
Lanc
, p. 49]
has a (unique) extension to a completely
positive map from
\mathrm{M}(A)
to
\mathrm{M}(B)
that is strictly continuous
on the unit ball
[
Lanc
, Corollary 5.7]
, and we usually do not
distinguish between these two maps.

Let
\Gamma
be a locally compact group. All
L^{p}
-spaces of
\Gamma

are with respect to the left Haar measure. We write
\lambda^{\Gamma}=\left(\lambda_{\gamma}^{\Gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

for the left regular representation of
\Gamma
on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
,
namely
(\lambda_{\gamma}^{\Gamma}f)(\gamma^{\prime})=f(\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime})

for
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. By a (unitary)
representation of
\Gamma
inside a C
∗
-algebra
A
we mean a
strictly continuous homomorphism
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

from
\Gamma
into the unitary group
\mathcal{U}(A)
. To every
such
\lambda
there is an associated morphism from the full group
C
∗
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Gamma)
to
A
mapping the representative
of
f\in\mathrm{C}_{c}(\Gamma)
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Gamma)
to
\int_{\Gamma}f(\gamma)\lambda_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma

(strict integration). Slightly abusing the notation we will also denote
the latter morphism by
\lambda
. By an action of
\Gamma
on a
C
∗
-algebra
A
we mean a continuous homomorphism
\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}(A)
,
where
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
is endowed with the point–norm topology as customary.

A (measurable)
2
-cocycle
on
\Gamma
is a measurable function

\Psi
from
\Gamma\times\Gamma
to the complex unit circle
\mathbb{T}

satisfying the
2
-cocycle condition

(1.1)

\Psi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\Psi(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})=\Psi(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\Psi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3})\qquad(\forall_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\in\Gamma}).

We mostly require
\Psi
to be continuous, as well as normalised
in the sense that
\Psi(e,\cdot)\equiv 1\equiv\Psi(\cdot,e)
. Under
the normalisation condition one has

(1.2)

\Psi(\gamma_{1}^{-1},\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=\overline{\Psi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}\Psi(\gamma_{1}^{-1},\gamma_{1})\qquad(\forall_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma}).

If
\Psi^{\prime}
is another
2
-cocycle on
\Gamma
, then the function

\Psi\boxtimes\Psi^{\prime}:\Gamma^{2}\times\Gamma^{2}\to\mathbb{T}
given
by

(1.3)

(\Psi\boxtimes\Psi^{\prime})\big((\gamma_{1},\gamma_{1}^{\prime}),(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}^{\prime})\big):=\Psi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\Psi^{\prime}(\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime}),\qquad(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{1}^{\prime}),(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}^{\prime})\in\Gamma^{2},

is a
2
-cocycle on
\Gamma^{2}
.

Assume from now on that
\Gamma
is abelian. We follow the convention
of
[
Ped
,
Kas
]
by
using the
inverse
Fourier transform on
\Gamma
, that is,
letting
\hat{f}(\hat{\gamma}):=\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle\hat{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle f(\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}\gamma

for
f\in\mathrm{C}_{c}(\Gamma)
and
\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}
(so
\left\langle\hat{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle

instead of
\overline{\left\langle\hat{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle}
).
The map that sends the representative of
f\in\mathrm{C}_{c}(\Gamma)
in
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma)

to
\hat{f}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})
extends to a canonical C
∗
-algebra
isomorphism
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})
; the canonical
strict extension of its inverse, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, sends

\gamma
treated as a function on
\hat{\Gamma}
that belongs to

\mathrm{C}_{b}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma}))
to
\lambda_{\gamma}^{\Gamma}

in
\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma))
. The isomorphism
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma)

is a restriction of the spatial isomorphism
L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\operatorname{VN}(\Gamma)

obtained by conjugation with the
L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\hat{\Gamma})

Fourier transform.

If
\lambda
is a representation of (the abelian)
\Gamma
inside
a C
∗
-algebra
A
, we treat the associated map
\lambda\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Gamma),A)

also as a map
\lambda\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma}),A)
using the above
isomorphism
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma)
. In other words,

\lambda(\hat{f})=\int_{\Gamma}f(\gamma)\lambda_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma

for all
f\in\mathrm{C}_{c}(\Gamma)
.

1.2.
A few remarks on non-degeneracy of inclusions and completely positive
maps

Non-degeneracy of
*
-homomorphisms and, more generally, of completely
positive maps plays an important role in this paper. We finish the
introductory section with a few remarks on this subject. Recall that
if
A,B
are C
∗
-algebras and
S:A\to\mathrm{M}(B)
is a completely positive
map, then
S
is said to be
non-degenerate
if for some/every
approximate identity
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
of
A

the net
\left(Se_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
converges strictly
to
\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(B)}
in
\mathrm{M}(B)
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
A,B
be C
∗
-algebras
and
S:A\to\mathrm{M}(B)
be a contractive completely positive map. If there
exists a contractive Banach algebraic approximate identity
\left(a_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}

of
A
such that
\left(Sa_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
converges
strictly to
\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(B)}
in
\mathrm{M}(B)
, then
S
is non-degenerate.

Proof.

We should show that
\left(a_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
can be
replaced by a C
∗
-algebraic approximate identity.

Since
\left(Sa_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
converges strictly
to
\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(B)}
, for every state
\omega
of
B
we have
\omega(Sa_{i})\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}1

by a standard argument involving Cohen’s factorisation theorem, hence
also
\omega(S\left|a_{i}\right|)\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}1

by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as
\left(a_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}

is contractive and
\omega\circ S
is also a state for
S
is contractive.

We may and will assume that
\left\|a_{i}\right\|<1
for
each
i\in\EuScript{I}
. This allows us to replace
\left(\left|a_{i}\right|\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}

by an increasing net as follows. Consider the order-preserving isomorphism

\Phi:\left\{a\in A_{+}:\left\|a\right\|<1\right\}\to A_{+}

given by
a\mapsto a(\mathds{1}-a)^{-1}
. Let
\EuScript{J}
be the directed
set of all finite subsets of
\EuScript{I}
ordered by inclusion.
Since
{\big(e_{j}^{\prime}:=\sum_{i\in j}\Phi\left(\left|a_{i}\right|\right)\big)}_{j\in\EuScript{J}}

is an increasing net in
A_{+}
satisfying
\Phi\left(\left|a_{i}\right|\right)\leq e_{j}^{\prime}

for all
i\in j\in\EuScript{J}
, the family
{\big(e_{j}:=\Phi^{-1}(e_{j}^{\prime})\big)}_{j\in\EuScript{J}}

is an increasing net in
\left\{a\in A_{+}:\left\|a\right\|<1\right\}

satisfying
\left|a_{i}\right|\leq e_{j}
for all
i\in j\in\EuScript{J}
.

Consequently, for every state
\omega
of
B
, from the fact that

\omega(S\left|a_{i}\right|)\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}1
we
infer that
\omega(Se_{j})\xrightarrow[j\in\EuScript{J}]{}1
because

\omega\circ S
is a state. Thus, by
[
KV1
, Lemma A.4]
,
the increasing net
\left(Se_{j}\right)_{j\in\EuScript{J}}
converges
strictly to
\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(B)}
. Finally, repeating the last argument
with
S
being the identity map on
A
shows that
\left(e_{j}\right)_{j\in\EuScript{J}}

is an approximate identity for
A
.
∎

The following is a general observation, or rather warning, that is
pertinent to the proofs of Theorem
3.3

and Proposition
4.5
.

Observation 1.2
.

Let
A,B
be C
∗
-algebras such that

A
is a non-degenerate C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathrm{M}(B)
, i.e. the inclusion
map is non-degenerate. We can, and throughout the paper tacitly will,
view
\mathrm{M}(A)
as a C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathrm{M}(B)
, namely the idealiser
of
A
inside
\mathrm{M}(B)

[
Lanc
, Proposition 2.3]
. Assume now
that
A,B
are non-unital. Then the closed unit ball of
\mathrm{M}(A)

has two (non-trivial) strict topologies, namely the one coming from

\mathrm{M}(A)
and the one coming from
\mathrm{M}(B)
. The former is finer than
the latter by one of the equivalent conditions of non-degeneracy
[
Lanc
, Proposition 2.5]
.
However, these topologies
do not
coincide in general when
A\nsubseteq B
.
Below we give two examples of this.

The first example, which is in the commutative case, was kindly communicated
to us by Mariusz Tobolski and one of the referees. Take
A:=\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})

and
B:=\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{0\right\})
. Then every bounded
sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

f_{n}(x)=1
for each
x\in\left[-n,-\frac{1}{n}\right]\cup\left[\frac{1}{n},n\right]

and
f_{n}(0)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
converges to
\mathds{1}
in the strict
topology of
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{0\right\})=\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{0\right\}))

but not in the strict topology of
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
. The topological
reason behind this is that the (embedding) map
\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{0\right\}\to\mathbb{R}

is
not compact covering
, i.e., there exists a compact subset
of the co-domain (here,
\left\{0\right\}
) that is not contained
in the image of any compact subset of the domain.

The second example was kindly communicated to us by Jacek Krajczok.
Take
A:=\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
B:=\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rtimes\mathbb{R}
, where the crossed product
is with respect to the (say, left) translation action. By the Stone–von
Neumann theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Wil
, Theorem 4.24]
)
there is a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rtimes\mathbb{R}\cong K(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
.
Now, every bounded sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f_{n}(0)=1
and
\operatorname{supp}f_{n}\subseteq\left[-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n}\right]

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
does not converge to
0
in the strict topology
of
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})=\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
, but converges to
0
in the strict topology
of
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))=\mathrm{M}(K(L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))
.

Observation 1.3
.

Let
B,D
be C
∗
-algebras. Then
we can and shall view
\mathrm{M}(B)\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathrm{M}(D)
as embedded (unitally)
inside
\mathrm{M}(B\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}D)
. Let also
A,C
be C
∗
-algebras such
that
A\subseteq\mathrm{M}(B)
and
C\subseteq\mathrm{M}(D)
non-degenerately. Then
by virtue of the embedding of
A\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}C
inside
\mathrm{M}(B)\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathrm{M}(D)

we also view
A\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}C
as embedded non-degenerately inside

\mathrm{M}(B\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}D)

[
Lanc
, pp. 37–38]
.

For
\omega\in B^{*}
we have the slice map
{\it\omega\otimes\mathrm{id}_{D}:\mathrm{M}(B\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}D)\to\mathrm{M}(D)}
,
and considering the restriction
\omega|_{A}\in A^{*}
(where, of
course, we are restricting to
A
the strict extension of
\omega

to
\mathrm{M}(B)
) we also have the slice map
(\omega|_{A})\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C}:\mathrm{M}(A\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}C)\to\mathrm{M}(C)
.
Each of these maps is continuous on the closed unit ball in the respective
strict topologies
[
Lanc
, p. 79]
. The assertion of this observation
is that, under the embedding
\mathrm{M}(A\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}C)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{M}(B\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}D)
,
the second slice map is the restriction of the first one. We leave
the details to the reader.

2.
Locally compact quantum groups and cocycle twisting

In this section we introduce locally compact quantum group preliminaries
and discuss the cocycle twisting in this context, focusing on cocycles
coming from closed abelian subgroups.

2.1.
Locally compact quantum groups

A
locally compact quantum group
in its von Neumann algebraic
form as introduced by Kustermans and Vaes is a pair
\mathbb{G}=\left(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\right)

such that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is a von Neumann algebra and
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

is a co-multiplication, i.e. a normal faithful unital
*
-homomorphism
that is co-associative:
(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{G}})\circ\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
,
such that there exist left and right Haar weights for
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
.
The Haar weights are unique up to scaling. We refer to
[
Kus
,
KV1
,
KV2
,
VD
]

for the full details.

When the context is clear, we drop the
\mathbb{G}
in symbols associated
to
\mathbb{G}
and adorn with hats symbols associated to the dual locally
compact quantum group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, e.g.
\Delta:=\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
and

\hat{\Delta}:=\Delta_{\hat{\mathbb{G}}}
.

In addition to the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, two C
∗
-algebras
providing different perspectives on
\mathbb{G}
are the reduced C
∗
-algebra

\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and the universal C
∗
-algebra
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})
. Each carries
its own version of the co-multiplication.

We work with the left Haar weight
\varphi
and left regular representation

W
, which implements
\Delta
by
\Delta(x)=W^{*}(\mathds{1}\otimes x)W

for all
x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. Identifying the GNS Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\varphi)

and
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi})
as customary and denoting
the common Hilbert space by
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, we view
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and

L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
as acting on it. The GNS map
\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})

of
\varphi
is denoted by
\Lambda
. We write
\nabla,J
for the
modular operator and modular conjugation of
\varphi
(on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
)
and
\left(\sigma_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
for the modular automorphism
group of
\varphi
. We will occasionally need the commutant quantum
group of
\mathbb{G}
, denoted
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
(so that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})^{\prime}

inside
B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
).

We put
L^{1}(\mathbb{G}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{*}
and note the density condition

(2.1)

\operatorname{\overline{span}}^{\sigma\text{-weak}}\left\{(\mathrm{id}\otimes\omega)\left(\Delta(x)\right):\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\right\}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

The antipode, the unitary antipode, and the scaling group of
\mathbb{G}

will be denoted

\mathtt{S}

,

\mathtt{R}

, and
\left(\tau_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,
respectively. We shall use, sometimes tacitly, the formula
\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}(x)=\hat{J}x^{*}\hat{J}

for
x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, and also require the formulas

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}\otimes\hat{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}})(W)=W,\text{ i.e. }(\operatorname{Ad}(\hat{J}\otimes J))(W)=W^{*},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}=\sigma\circ(\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}\otimes\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}})\circ\Delta,

where
\sigma
is the algebra-level flip operator, and

(2.4)

\left(\tau_{t}\otimes\sigma_{t}\right)\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\sigma_{t}\qquad(\forall_{t\in\mathbb{R}}).

We end this subsection with two notations. First, define
\mathcal{I}

as the subspace of
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
consisting of all
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})

such that the conjugate-linear map
\Lambda(x)\mapsto\omega(x^{*})
,

x\in\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}
, is bounded; equivalently, such that
there exists (uniquely)
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
with
\omega(x^{*})=\left\langle\xi,\Lambda(x)\right\rangle

for all
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}
. Second, define
L_{\sharp}^{1}(\mathbb{G})

to be the subspace of
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
consisting of all
\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})

such that some (unique) element of
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, denoted by
\omega^{\sharp}
,
satisfies
\omega^{\sharp}(x)=\overline{\omega(\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{S}$}}(x)^{*})}
for
all
x\in D(\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{S}$}})
.

2.2.
Cocycle twisting of locally compact quantum groups

We take the general approach to the Rieffel deformations of locally
compact quantum groups based on the work of De Commer on Galois objects
and cocycle twisting
[
DeC3
,
DeC1
]
,
which is introduced in this subsection. Let
\mathbb{G}
be a locally compact
quantum group and
\Omega
be a (measurable, unitary)
2
-cocycle

on the dual
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, that is, a unitary in
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

such that

(2.5)

(\Omega\otimes\mathds{1})\cdot(\hat{\Delta}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\Omega)=(\mathds{1}\otimes\Omega)\cdot(\mathrm{id}\otimes\hat{\Delta})(\Omega).

The map
\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}:L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

given by

\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}:=(\operatorname{Ad}\Omega)\circ\hat{\Delta}

is a co-multiplication on
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
by (
2.5
).
It is a deep result that
\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}
admits left and
right invariant weights, namely that
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}:=(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega})

is a (von Neumann algebraic) locally compact quantum group
[
DeC3
, Theorem 6.2]
,
called the
cocycle twisting
of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
by
\Omega
. Note
that
\Omega^{*}
may be viewed as a (measurable, unitary)
2
-cocycle
on
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
and we naturally have that
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
is
the cocycle twisting of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
by
\Omega^{*}
.

The analysis of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
, and in particular the formula
for its left regular representation, relies on the following construction
of
[
DeC3
, p. 97]
based on

[
VV
]
, see also
[
NeT
, p. 464]
.
Consider the trivial left action of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
on
\mathbb{C}
, which is
just the unital homomorphism
\mathbb{C}\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
.
Together with
\Omega
it forms a cocycle action, and the associated
cocycle (or twisted) crossed

product is the von Neumann algebra

\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C}=\overline{\big\{(\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}):\omega\in L^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\big\}}^{\sigma\text{-weak}}\subseteq B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})).

The dual of this trivial action is the right action
\alpha:\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C}\to(\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

of
\mathbb{G}
on
\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C}
determined by
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\alpha)(\hat{W}\Omega^{*})=\hat{W}_{13}{(\hat{W}\Omega^{*})}_{12}

(here the dual action is in the sense of
[
VV
, Proposition 1.4]
,
and in particular it is a right action; note however that
[
VV
]

uses in this context the equivalent concept of a left action of the
opposite locally compact quantum group). Denoting by
\theta
the
(unique) state on
\mathbb{C}
, its dual weight in the sense of
[
VV
, Definition 1.13]

is the n.s.f. weight
\tilde{\theta}
on
\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C}

given by
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\circ\alpha
; the image of this operator-valued
weight is
\mathbb{C}\mathds{1}
, which we identify with
\mathbb{C}
. With respect to
the natural representation of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}\prescript{}{\Omega}{\ltimes}\mathbb{C}

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
this dual weight
\tilde{\theta}
has a GNS construction
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
given by

(2.6)

\tilde{\Lambda}((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}))=\Lambda((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}))\qquad(\forall_{\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}}).

Denote by
\tilde{\nabla},\tilde{J}
the modular operator and modular
conjugation of
\tilde{\theta}
pertaining to this GNS construction.
The canonical unitary implementation of the cocycle action formed
by the trivial action of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
on
\mathbb{C}
and
\Omega
is precisely

(2.7)

X:=\tilde{J}J\in\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),

which actually belongs to
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
and is invariant under
the dual unitary antipode, i.e.
JXJ=X^{*}

[
VV
, Definition 1.18 and Proposition 1.19]

(that
X\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
is also proved in
[
DeC3
, p. 99, l. 3,4]
).

The left regular representation of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
, when acting
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, is

(2.8)

\hat{W}_{\Omega}=(\tilde{J}\otimes\hat{J})\Omega\hat{W}^{*}(J\otimes\hat{J})\Omega^{*}

[
DeC3
, Proposition 6.5]
.
Employing the unitary
X
above, we obtain

(2.9)

\begin{split}\hat{W}_{\Omega}&=(JX^{*}\otimes\hat{J})\Omega\hat{W}^{*}(J\otimes\hat{J})\Omega^{*}\\
&=(J\otimes\hat{J})(X^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Omega(J\otimes\hat{J})(J\otimes\hat{J})\hat{W}^{*}(J\otimes\hat{J})\Omega^{*}\\
&=(J\otimes\hat{J})(X^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Omega(J\otimes\hat{J})\hat{W}\Omega^{*}.\end{split}

The von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega})

is equipped with the left Haar weights
\hat{\varphi},\hat{\varphi}_{\Omega}

of
\hat{\mathbb{G}},\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
, respectively. The modular conjugation

\hat{J}_{\Omega}
of
\hat{\varphi}_{\Omega}
, when viewed canonically
as acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})=L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi})
,
equals the modular conjugation
\hat{J}
of
\hat{\varphi}
, because
the canonical unitary operator that intertwines the GNS representations
of
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
on
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi})

and
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi}_{\Omega})
is the same
unitary that intertwines
\hat{J}
and
\hat{J}_{\Omega}
(by a
general fact on von Neumann algebras
[
Tak
, Lemma IX.1.5 and its proof]
).
Note that this unitary was used in the proof of
[
DeC3
, Proposition 6.5]

to have the right leg of
\hat{W}_{\Omega}
in (
2.8
)
act on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})=L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi})
(in addition
to the left leg). See also
[
NeT
, text succeeding eq. (2.4)]
.

In this paper we are interested in the dual locally compact quantum
group
\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}:=\widehat{\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}}
, whose
L^{\infty}
-algebra
we also view as acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})=L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\varphi})

canonically. By the previous paragraph and a general formula stated
earlier, the unitary antipode
\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}}
is implemented
by
\hat{J}
:

(2.10)

\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}}(x)=\hat{J}x^{*}\hat{J}\qquad(\forall_{x\in\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}}).

Under certain assumptions,
\mathbb{G}^{\Omega}
can be seen as the Rieffel
deformation of
\mathbb{G}
with respect to
\Omega
. This is explained
in the next section.

We finish this subsection with a few more facts. The operators
\big(v_{t}:=\tilde{\nabla}^{it}\nabla^{-it}\big)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

belong to
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

[
DeC3
, p. 99, l. 2,3]

and form a
1
-cocycle for
\hat{\tau}
, i.e.
v_{s+t}=v_{s}\hat{\tau}_{s}(v_{t})

for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}

[
DeC3
, Proposition 6.3, (i)]
.
Then the scaling group
\hat{\tau}_{\Omega}=\left(\hat{\tau}_{\Omega,t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
equals
\left(\operatorname{Ad}(v_{t})\circ\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
Indeed, this is spelled out in
[
DeC1
, l. 19,20 in proof of Proposition 9.1.6]
.
Since the last reference is unpublished, we note the following alternative
simple proof. Consider the Connes cocycle derivative
\left(u_{t}:=\left[D\hat{\varphi}_{\Omega}:D\hat{\varphi}\right]_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
. Using the fact that the modular automorphism
group
\hat{\sigma}_{\Omega}=\left(\hat{\sigma}_{\Omega,t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

of
\hat{\varphi}_{\Omega}
equals
\left(\operatorname{Ad}(u_{t})\circ\hat{\sigma}_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

as well as the formula

\hat{\Delta}\left(u_{t}\right)\cdot\left(\hat{\tau}_{t}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{t}\right)\left(\Omega^{*}\right)=\Omega^{*}\cdot(v_{t}\otimes u_{t})\qquad(\forall_{t\in\mathbb{R}})

(see
[
DeC3
, proof of Proposition 6.3]
)
and the analogue of (
2.4
) for
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
,
one calculates that

\left(\left(\operatorname{Ad}(v_{t})\circ\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{\Omega,t}\right)\circ\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}=\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}\circ\hat{\sigma}_{\Omega,t}\qquad(\forall_{t\in\mathbb{R}}).

This implies that
\hat{\tau}_{\Omega}=\left(\operatorname{Ad}(v_{t})\circ\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

because of the analogues of (
2.1
) and (
2.4
)
for
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\left(\hat{\tau}_{t}\otimes\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)\left(\Omega\right)=\Omega

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then the positive selfadjoint operators
\tilde{\nabla},\nabla

strongly commute.

Proof.

Recall the operators
v_{t}
introduced in the beginning of the last
paragraph. From
[
DeC3
, Proposition 6.3, (ii)]

and the invariance assumption we get that

\Omega^{*}\left(v_{t}\otimes v_{t}\right)=\hat{\Delta}\left(v_{t}\right)\left(\hat{\tau}_{t}\otimes\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)\left(\Omega^{*}\right)=\hat{\Delta}\left(v_{t}\right)\Omega^{*},

that is,
\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}\left(v_{t}\right)=v_{t}\otimes v_{t}
,
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. So each
v_{t}
is a one-dimensional representation
of
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
, and is thus invariant under the scaling group

\hat{\tau}_{\Omega}
. Using the facts that
\hat{\tau}_{\Omega}=\left(\operatorname{Ad}(v_{t})\circ\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

and that
\left(v_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
1
-cocycle for
\hat{\tau}
,
we obtain

v_{t}=\hat{\tau}_{\Omega,s}(v_{t})=v_{s}\hat{\tau}_{s}\left(v_{t}\right)v_{s}^{*}=v_{s+t}v_{s}^{*}\qquad(\forall_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}}),

namely
\left(v_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a one-parameter unitary group.
By the definition of
\left(v_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and a classical
result
[
StZ
, Exercise E.9.24]
, this
means that the positive selfadjoint operators
\tilde{\nabla},\nabla

strongly commute.
∎

2.3.
Cocycle twisting via locally compact abelian subgroups

We lay out the hypothesis of this subsection and of paper’s main results.
Note that for a locally compact group, the two notions of being a
closed quantum subgroup of a locally compact quantum group, due to
Vaes and to Woronowicz
[
DKSS
]
,
coincide by
[
Daw3
, Corollary 5.7]
.

Standing Hypothesis 2.2
.

Let a locally compact abelian group
\Gamma
be
a closed quantum subgroup of a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
.
Let
\Psi
be a normalised continuous
2
-cocycle on
\hat{\Gamma}
.

The (Vaes) closed quantum subgroup assumption means that there exists
an injective normal
*
-homomorphism
L:L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

intertwining the co-multiplications (and thus also automatically the
unitary antipodes, scaling groups, etc.). Viewing
\Psi
as an element
of
\mathcal{U}(L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma}))
,
the operator
\Omega:=(L\otimes L)(\Psi)
is a
2
-cocycle on the
locally compact quantum group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, and we can form the locally
compact quantum groups
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Psi}:=\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
and
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}:=\widehat{\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Psi}}

as in the previous subsection.

Define
R:L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime})
by
R:=\operatorname{Ad}(\hat{J}J)\circ L
.
Since
L
intertwines the co-multiplications,

(2.11)

\hat{W}^{*}(\mathds{1}\otimes L(g))\hat{W}=(L\otimes L)(\Delta_{\hat{\Gamma}}(g))\text{ and }\hat{W}(\mathds{1}\otimes R(g))\hat{W}^{*}=(L\otimes R)(\Delta_{\hat{\Gamma}}(g))\qquad(\forall_{g\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})})

(the second formula follows from (
2.2
), (
2.3
),
and
\hat{\Gamma}
being abelian; the last fact is used to deduce
that the formula above, first checked for
g
being a character,
is valid for arbitrary
g
). Write
\left(L_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

and
\left(R_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
for the representation
of
\Gamma
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
associated with the morphisms
L|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma}),\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}))

and
R|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma}),\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime}))
.
Equivalently, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

L_{\gamma}:=L(\gamma)\text{ and }R_{\gamma}:=\hat{J}L_{\gamma}\hat{J},

where as usual we treat
\gamma
as a function in
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\hat{\Gamma})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})
.
Since
\gamma
is group-like in
L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})
, so is
L_{\gamma}

in
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}(L_{\gamma})=L_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma},\text{ equivalently: }\hat{W}^{*}(\mathds{1}\otimes L_{\gamma})\hat{W}=L_{\gamma}\otimes L_{\gamma},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\text{and thus }\hat{W}(\mathds{1}\otimes R_{\gamma})\hat{W}^{*}=L_{\gamma}\otimes R_{\gamma}.

(The map
\Gamma\ni\gamma\mapsto L_{\gamma}
, as an element of
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
,
is the bicharacter associated with
\Gamma
being a closed quantum
subgroup of
\mathbb{G}

[
MRW1
,
DKSS
]

up to left/right and Fourier transform conventions).

Before we continue, we give a concrete formula for the unitary
X

of (
2.7
) in the current setting.

Proposition 2.3
.

The unitary
X=\tilde{J}J\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

equals
L(u)
where
u\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})
is given by

(2.14)

u(\hat{\gamma}):=\Psi(-\hat{\gamma},\hat{\gamma}),\qquad\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}.

Proof.

Let
U:=L(u)
. We claim that for every
\omega\in L_{\sharp}^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})

we have

((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}))^{*}=((U\omega^{\sharp})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}).

Assuming that the claim is proved, denote by
T
and
\tilde{T}

the closures of the conjugate-linear operators
\Lambda(x)\mapsto\Lambda(x^{*})
,

x\in\mathcal{N_{\varphi}\cap N_{\varphi}^{*}}
, and
\tilde{\Lambda}(y)\mapsto\tilde{\Lambda}(y^{*})
,

y\in\mathcal{N_{\tilde{\theta}}\cap N_{\tilde{\theta}}^{*}}
(using
the notation of the previous section), both on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{\sharp}:=\{\omega\in L_{\sharp}^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})\cap\hat{\mathcal{I}}:\omega^{\sharp}\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}\}
.
If
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{\sharp}
, then by the von Neumann
algebraic and dual analogues of
[
KV1
, Result 8.6 and Proposition 8.13]

also
U\omega^{\sharp}\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}
and
\Lambda\big(((U\omega^{\sharp})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})\big)=U\Lambda((\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}))

as
U\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
, and we get from (
2.6
)
and the claim above that
((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}))^{*}\in\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{\theta}}

and

\tilde{T}\tilde{\Lambda}((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}))=\tilde{\Lambda}\big(((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}))^{*}\big)=\tilde{\Lambda}\big(((U\omega^{\sharp})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*})\big),

so that

\begin{split}\tilde{T}\Lambda((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}))&=\Lambda\big(((U\omega^{\sharp})\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})\big)=U\Lambda((\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}))\\
&=U\Lambda\big(((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}))^{*}\big)=UT\Lambda((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})).\end{split}

The set
\{\Lambda((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})):\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{\sharp}\}

is a core for
T
by
[
KV2
, Lemma 2.7]
,
hence
UT\subseteq\tilde{T}
, i.e.
UJ\nabla^{1/2}\subseteq\tilde{J}\tilde{\nabla}^{1/2}
.
Since
\Omega:=(L\otimes L)(\Psi)
and the (quantum) group
\hat{\Gamma}

has trivial scaling group,
\Omega
is invariant under
\left(\hat{\tau}_{t}\otimes\hat{\tau}_{t}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, the positive selfadjoint
operators
\tilde{\nabla},\nabla
strongly commute, and hence they
have a common core, and in particular a core for
\tilde{\nabla}^{1/2}

contained in the domain of
\nabla^{1/2}
. By the closedness of
UJ\nabla^{1/2}

this entails the equality
UJ\nabla^{1/2}=\tilde{J}\tilde{\nabla}^{1/2}
.
The uniqueness of the polar decomposition yields that (
\tilde{\nabla}=\nabla

and)
UJ=\tilde{J}
, as desired.

We finish by proving the claim. Let
\omega\in L_{\sharp}^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
.
For every
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
we have

\begin{split}\big((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}(L_{\gamma_{1}}\otimes L_{\gamma_{2}})^{*})\big)^{*}&=L_{\gamma_{2}}\cdot\big((L_{-\gamma_{1}}\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})\big)^{*}\\
&\overset{(\star)}{=}L_{\gamma_{2}}\cdot(L_{-\gamma_{1}}\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W})\\
&\overset{(\star\star)}{=}(\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}(L_{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}\otimes L_{\gamma_{2}}))\end{split}

(in
(\star)
we used that
(\hat{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{S}$}}}(L_{-\gamma_{1}}))^{*}=L_{\gamma_{1}}^{*}=L_{-\gamma_{1}}

and thus
L_{-\gamma_{1}}\omega\in L_{\sharp}^{1}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
and
{(L_{-\gamma_{1}}\omega)}^{\sharp}=L_{-\gamma_{1}}\omega^{\sharp}
,
and in
(\star\star)
we used (
2.12
)), that
is,

(2.15)

\big((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\cdot(L\otimes L)(\gamma_{1}\otimes\gamma_{2})^{*})\big)^{*}=(\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})\big(\hat{W}\cdot(L\otimes L)((\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})\otimes\gamma_{2})\big).

Write
\Theta
for the automorphism of
L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma})

that maps
F\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma})
to
(\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2})\mapsto F(-\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\hat{\gamma}_{2})
.
For every
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
we have
\Theta(\gamma_{1}\otimes\gamma_{2})=(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})\otimes\gamma_{2}
.
By linearity and continuity we infer from (
2.15
)
that

\big((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\cdot(L\otimes L)(F)^{*})\big)^{*}=(\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})\big(\hat{W}\cdot(L\otimes L)(\Theta(F))\big)\qquad(\forall_{F\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma})}).

Taking in particular
F:=\Psi
and applying the equality (
1.2
)
which in the language of this proposition reads
\Theta(\Psi)=\overline{\Psi}\cdot(u\otimes\mathds{1})
,
we obtain

\big((\omega\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{W}\Omega^{*})\big)^{*}=(\omega^{\sharp}\otimes\mathrm{id})\big(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}(U\otimes\mathds{1})\big)=((U\omega^{\sharp})\otimes\mathrm{id})\big(\hat{W}\Omega^{*}\big),

as claimed.
∎

Proposition
2.3
yields a more transparent formula
for the left regular representation
\hat{W}_{\Psi}:=\hat{W}_{\Omega}

(acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
) of the locally compact quantum
group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Psi}:=\hat{\mathbb{G}}_{\Omega}
. Define functions
\Upsilon,\mathring{\Psi}:\hat{\Gamma}\times\hat{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{T}

by

(2.16)

\begin{aligned} \Upsilon(\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2})&:=\overline{\Psi(-\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\hat{\gamma}_{2})},\\
\mathring{\Psi}(\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2})&:=\Psi(\hat{\gamma}_{1},-\hat{\gamma}_{1}-\hat{\gamma}_{2}),\end{aligned}\quad\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2}\in\hat{\Gamma}.

By (
1.2
) we have
\Upsilon=(u^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Psi

with
u\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Gamma})
defined in (
2.14
), hence

(\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\hat{\Gamma}}\otimes\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\hat{\Gamma}})((u^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Psi)^{*}=\mathring{\Psi}
.
As a result,

\begin{split}(J\otimes\hat{J})(L(u)^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Omega(J\otimes\hat{J})&=(\mathds{1}\otimes\hat{J}J)(J\otimes J)(L(u)^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Omega(J\otimes J)(\mathds{1}\otimes J\hat{J})\\
&=(\mathds{1}\otimes\hat{J}J)\cdot(L\otimes L)\big((\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\hat{\Gamma}}\otimes\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\hat{\Gamma}})((u^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Psi)\big)^{*}\cdot(\mathds{1}\otimes J\hat{J})\\
&=(\mathds{1}\otimes\hat{J}J)\cdot(L\otimes L)(\mathring{\Psi})\cdot(\mathds{1}\otimes J\hat{J})=(L\otimes R)(\mathring{\Psi}).\end{split}

Combining (
2.9
) with Proposition
2.3

we get
\hat{W}_{\Psi}=(J\otimes\hat{J})(L(u)^{*}\otimes\mathds{1})\Omega(J\otimes\hat{J})\hat{W}\Omega^{*}
.
Thus ultimately

(2.17)

\hat{W}_{\Psi}=(L\otimes R)(\mathring{\Psi})\cdot\hat{W}\cdot(L\otimes L)(\overline{\Psi}).

3.
The Rieffel deformation

This section is devoted to the study of C
∗
-algebraic Rieffel deformations
in the context of locally compact quantum groups.

3.1.
The Rieffel deformation of C
∗
- and von Neumann algebras

In this subsection we follow
[
Kas
, Sections 2,3]
,
which is based on
[
Land
]
, see also
[
Ped
, Section 7.8]
.
Let
\Gamma\overset{\rho}{\curvearrowright}A
be an action of a locally
compact abelian group
\Gamma
on a C
∗
-algebra
A
and let
\Psi

be a continuous
2
-cocycle on the dual
\hat{\Gamma}
. We construct
a deformed C
∗
-subalgebra
A^{\Psi}
of the multiplier algebra
of the crossed product
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
as follows.

Write
\iota:A\to\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
for the canonical embedding,

\lambda=\left(\lambda_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
for the
canonical representation of
\Gamma
inside
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
,
and
\hat{\rho}
for the dual action
\hat{\Gamma}\curvearrowright A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma

characterised by
\hat{\rho}
fixing
A
pointwise and satisfying

(3.1)

\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}}(\lambda_{\gamma})=\left\langle\hat{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle\lambda_{\gamma}\qquad(\forall_{\gamma\in\Gamma,\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}});

this again follows the convention of
[
Ped
,
Kas
]

in contrast to the common convention
[
Tak
,
FiV
]

of having
\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}}(\lambda_{\gamma})=\overline{\left\langle\hat{\gamma},\gamma\right\rangle}\lambda_{\gamma}
.
The map in
\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma),A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)

associated with
\lambda
(also denoted by
\lambda
) is injective.
From (
3.1
) we get

(3.2)

\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}}(\lambda(g))=\lambda(g(\cdot+\hat{\gamma}))\qquad(\forall_{g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma}),\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}}).

For each
\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}
we define a unitary element

\Psi_{\hat{\gamma}}\in\mathrm{C}_{b}(\hat{\Gamma})\cong\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}^{*}(\Gamma))

by
\Psi_{\hat{\gamma}}(\cdot)=\Psi(\cdot,\hat{\gamma})
. Then we
obtain a strictly continuous family of unitaries
\left(U_{\hat{\gamma}}:=\lambda(\Psi_{\hat{\gamma}})\right)_{\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}}

in
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
, which by (
3.2
)
and (
1.1
) satisfies

U_{\hat{\gamma}_{1}+\hat{\gamma}_{2}}=\overline{\Psi(\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2})}U_{\hat{\gamma}_{1}}\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}_{1}}(U_{\hat{\gamma}_{2}})\qquad(\forall_{\hat{\gamma}_{1},\hat{\gamma}_{2}\in\hat{\Gamma}})

(and the scalars belong to
\mathbb{T}
). So
\left(U_{\hat{\gamma}}\right)_{\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}}

forms a weak
1
-cocycle, in the sense of the C
∗
-algebraic analogue
of
[
Pop
, p. 281]
, for the dual action
\hat{\Gamma}\overset{\hat{\rho}}{\curvearrowright}A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
.
Since
\left(U_{\hat{\gamma}}\right)_{\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}}

is commuting,
{(U_{\hat{\gamma}}^{*})}_{\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}}

is also such a weak
1
-cocycle. Hence, the formula

(3.3)

\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}}^{\Psi}:=\operatorname{Ad}(U_{\hat{\gamma}}^{*})\circ\hat{\rho}_{\hat{\gamma}}\qquad(\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma})

defines a new (‘
deformed dual
’) action
\hat{\Gamma}\overset{\hat{\rho}^{\Psi}}{\curvearrowright}A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
,
which acts on
\left(\lambda_{\gamma}\right)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
like

\hat{\rho}
does in (
3.1
).

The
Landstad algebra

A^{\Psi}
is defined as the set of all
these
x\in\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
for which:

(L1):

x
is a fixed point of the action
\hat{\rho}^{\Psi}
;

(L2):

the map
\Gamma\ni\gamma\mapsto\lambda_{\gamma}x\lambda_{\gamma}^{*}

is norm continuous;

(L3):

and for all
f,g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})
we have
\lambda(f)x\lambda(g)\in A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
.

Then
A^{\Psi}
is a C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
,
and by
Landstad’s theorem
(
[
Land
]
,

[
Ped
, Theorem 7.8.8]
) the formula

(3.4)

\rho_{\gamma}^{\Psi}(x):=\lambda_{\gamma}x\lambda_{\gamma}^{*}\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma,x\in A^{\Psi})

defines an (‘deformed’) action
\Gamma\overset{\rho^{\Psi}}{\curvearrowright}A^{\Psi}

and there is an isomorphism
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma\cong A^{\Psi}\rtimes_{\rho^{\Psi}}\Gamma
.
This isomorphism is natural: after extending to the multiplier algebras,
it maps
A^{\Psi}\subseteq\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
given by (L1)–(L3)
above to its canonical copy in
\mathrm{M}(A^{\Psi}\rtimes_{\rho^{\Psi}}\Gamma)

and the canonical copy of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)

to the corresponding canonical copy in
\mathrm{M}(A^{\Psi}\rtimes_{\rho^{\Psi}}\Gamma)
.
In particular, the inclusion
A^{\Psi}\subseteq\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)

is non-degenerate.

A very similar deformation procedure is available in the von Neumann
algebraic setting: in this case, the Landstad algebra is just the
fixed-point algebra under the deformed dual action.

Remark 3.1
.

Landstad’s work
[
Land
]
covers actions
of non-abelian locally compact groups. It was extended to actions
of locally compact quantum groups on von Neumann algebras
[
VV
, Proposition 1.22]

and on C
∗
-algebras
[
Vae
, Theorem 6.7]
.

3.2.

of
[

∗
-algebraic setting in some stages of our work.

4.
Convolution semigroups on Rieffel deformations

This final section applies the results established earlier in the
context of convolution semigroups of states on locally compact quantum
groups, leading to the main results of the paper.

4.1.
Convolution semigroup basics

In this general subsection we introduce convolutions, convolution
operators and convolution semigroups and make several observations
on them.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a locally compact quantum group. The
convolution

of
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*}
is
\mu\star\nu:=(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*}
.
The pair
\left(\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*},\star\right)
is a (completely contractive
Banach) algebra with the co-unit
\epsilon
as its unit. Using tacitly
the natural embeddings of Banach spaces
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*}

given by restriction and by composing with the quotient map, respectively,
each ‘smaller’ algebra is an ideal in each ‘larger’ algebra.

For
\mu\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*}
the operators
\omega\mapsto\omega\star\mu

and
\omega\mapsto\mu\star\omega
on
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
are completely
bounded, and we denote by
\mathscr{L}_{\mu},\mathscr{R}_{\mu}\in B(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}))
,
respectively, their (completely bounded, normal) adjoints (we follow
the left/right convention of
[
Run
,
SkV1
]

extending the
L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
-bimodule actions on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

rather than the opposite convention of, e.g.,
[
JNR
,
SaS1
]
).
The linear maps
\mathscr{L}_{\mu},\mathscr{R}_{\mu}
on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

reduce to maps on
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})

[
RuV
, Lemma 1.3]
,
and for convenience, in this paper we will ordinarily write
\mathscr{L}_{\mu},\mathscr{R}_{\mu}

for the maps on
\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
. This should not lead to any confusion.
We call
\mathscr{L}_{\mu}
and
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}
,
\mu\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})^{*}
,
the left and right
convolution operators
on
\mathbb{G}
, respectively.

The operators
\mathscr{L}_{\mu},\mathscr{R}_{\mu}
admit more concrete
formulas. For instance, if
\mathds{W}\in\mathrm{M}(\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}K(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))

is the ‘left half-universal’ left regular representation and

\Delta^{\text{u-r}}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G}),\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G}))

is the ‘left half-universal’ co-multiplication given by
\Delta^{\text{u-r}}(x):=\mathds{W}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})}\otimes x)\mathds{W}
,

[
Kus

,
then
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta^{\text{u-r}}
.
Remark that this formula extends to the original convolution operator
on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
: for every
x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
we have
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}x=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})\left(\mathds{W}^{*}(\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})}\otimes x)\mathds{W}\right)
,
where the right-hand side is
a priori
in
\mathrm{M}(K(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})))
;
see
[
SaS2
, Theorem 6]
(based
on facts from
[
Daw2
,
JNR
]

gathered earlier in
[
DFSW
]
).

Definition 4.1

(
[
LS
,
SkV1
]
)
.

A

of states on
\mathbb{G}
(or of
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})

(2)
. The map

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

4.2.
Completely positive maps on Rieffel deformations

We state in this subsection a general result, Proposition
4.5
,
on the functoriality of the Rieffel deformation involving non-degenerate
completely positive maps. For
*
-homomorphisms this was shown in

[
Kas
, Subsection 3.2]
. See
also
[
MRW2
]
. We
use the setting and notation of Subsection
3.1
,
assuming that we are given an action
\Gamma\overset{\rho}{\curvearrowright}A

of a locally compact abelian group
\Gamma
on a C
∗
-algebra
A

and a continuous
2
-cocycle
\Psi
on
\hat{\Gamma}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
S:A\to A
is a non-degenerate
completely positive map commuting with the action
\Gamma\overset{\rho}{\curvearrowright}A

in the obvious sense:
\rho_{\gamma}\circ S=S\circ\rho_{\gamma}

for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then the canonical completely positive
‘extension’
\tilde{S}
of
S
to the crossed product
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma

is non-degenerate and preserves the algebra
A^{\Psi}
, and the respective
restriction
S^{\Psi}:A^{\Psi}\to A^{\Psi}
is a completely positive
map of norm
1
. The construction respects composition: if
T:A\to A

is another map satisfying the assumptions above then we have
(S\circ T)^{\Psi}=S^{\Psi}\circ T^{\Psi}
.

Proof.

Note first that whenever
S
is completely positive and equivariant
with respect to the
\Gamma
-action, it is well-known that it ‘extends’
to a completely positive map on the crossed product
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
,
which we denote by
\tilde{S}
, and which is determined by the formula

(4.2)

(\tilde{S}(f))(\gamma)=S(f(\gamma))\qquad(\forall_{f\in\mathrm{C}_{c}(\Gamma,A),\gamma\in\Gamma})

(this follows for example from
[
BEW
, Theorem 4.9 and Corollary 4.18]
).
Note that the proofs in
[
BEW
]

show that the norm of
\tilde{S}
equals to that of
S
, essentially
as one can always work in the trivial unitisation of
A
and suitably
rescale to work with the unital case.

We assume henceforth that the completely positive map
S:A\to A

is non-degenerate and show that the completely positive map
\tilde{S}:A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma\to A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma

is also non-degenerate.

Consider the following canonical
contractive Banach algebraic approximate identity of
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma

[
EKQR
, footnote to Remark A.8 (2)]
.
Let
\left(a_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
and
\left(z_{j}\right)_{j\in\EuScript{J}}

be approximate identities of
A
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}(\Gamma)
, respectively.
Then
\left(\iota(a_{i})\lambda(z_{j})\right)_{(i,j)\in\EuScript{I}\times\EuScript{J}}

is a contractive Banach algebraic approximate identity of
A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma
.
Since
S
is non-degenerate,
Sa_{i}\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(A)}

strictly in
\mathrm{M}(A)
. As a result,
\tilde{S}(\iota(a_{i})\lambda(z_{j}))=\iota(Sa_{i})\lambda(z_{j})\xrightarrow[(i,j)\in\EuScript{I}\times\EuScript{J}]{}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)}

strictly in
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
. Hence,
\tilde{S}
is non-degenerate
by Lemma
1.1
.

Note that as
U_{\hat{\gamma}}=\lambda(\Psi_{\hat{\gamma}})

for each
\hat{\gamma}\in\hat{\Gamma}
, we have
\tilde{S}(U_{\hat{\gamma}})=U_{\hat{\gamma}}

by the defining formula (
4.2
), so that
U_{\hat{\gamma}}

is in the multiplicative domain of
\tilde{S}
. On the other hand
(
4.2
) also shows that
\tilde{S}
commutes with the
dual action
\hat{\rho}
. Thus
\tilde{S}
commutes with the ‘deformed
dual’ action
\hat{\rho}^{\Psi}
, and so it preserves its fixed-point
space. The multiplicative domain argument as in the first line of
this paragraph shows that

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(\lambda(f)x\lambda(g))=\lambda(f)\tilde{S}(x)\lambda(g)\qquad(\forall_{x\in\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma),f,g\in\mathrm{C}_{0}(\hat{\Gamma})}),

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(\lambda_{\gamma}x\lambda_{\gamma}^{*})=\lambda_{\gamma}\tilde{S}(x)\lambda_{\gamma}^{*}\qquad(\forall_{x\in\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma),\gamma\in\Gamma}).

This shows that
\tilde{S}
preserves
A^{\Psi}
. Set
S^{\Psi}:=\tilde{S}|_{A^{\Psi}}:A^{\Psi}\to A^{\Psi}
.
Since
\tilde{S}
is contractive, so is
S^{\Psi}
. Moreover, if

\left(x_{i}\right)_{i\in\EuScript{I}}
is an approximate identity
for
A^{\Psi}
, then since
A^{\Psi}
is a non-degenerate subalgebra
of
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
, we have
x_{i}\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)}

strictly in
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
, and the non-degeneracy of

\tilde{S}
implies that
S^{\Psi}x_{i}=\tilde{S}x_{i}\xrightarrow[i\in\EuScript{I}]{}\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)}

strictly in
\mathrm{M}(A\rtimes_{\rho}\Gamma)
. Hence we must have
\left\|S^{\Psi}\right\|\geq 1
.
All in all,
\left\|S^{\Psi}\right\|=1
.

The last assertion about respecting composition is obvious.
∎

4.3.
Deforming convolution semigroups

We are ready to present the main results of this paper. The setting
is that of Standing Hypothesis
2.2
, i.e. we assume
that
\mathbb{G}
is a locally compact quantum group,
\Gamma
is a locally
compact abelian group that is a closed quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
,
and
\Psi
is a normalised continuous
2
-cocycle on
\hat{\Gamma}
.
The notation we use is that of Subsections
3.2

and
3.3
.

By (
2.12
) and (
2.13
)
the (commuting) left and right translation actions
\Gamma\overset{\rho^{L},\rho^{R}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})

given by

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\Delta\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{L}=(\rho_{\gamma}^{L}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta\text{ and }\Delta\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{R}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{R})\circ\Delta,\\
(\rho_{\gamma}^{R}\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{L})\circ\Delta=\Delta\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\qquad(\forall_{\gamma\in\Gamma})

(the first two identities were already noted in (
3.7
),
and they also follow from the fact that
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}
are the
left and right, respectively, quantum homomorphisms associated with

\Gamma\leq\mathbb{G}
).

Let
\pi_{\mathrm{u}}:\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
be the canonical surjection.
By (
4.3
) and
[
Kus
, Proposition 7.1]

the actions
\Gamma\overset{\rho^{L},\rho^{R}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})

lift uniquely to (commuting) actions
\Gamma\overset{\rho^{L,\mathrm{u}},\rho^{R,\mathrm{u}}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})

such that

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\rho_{\gamma}^{L}\circ\pi_{\mathrm{u}}=\pi_{\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{L,\mathrm{u}}\text{ and }\rho_{\gamma}^{R}\circ\pi_{\mathrm{u}}=\pi_{\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{R,\mathrm{u}},\\
\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{L,\mathrm{u}}=(\rho_{\gamma}^{L,\mathrm{u}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\text{ and }\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{R,\mathrm{u}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{R,\mathrm{u}})\circ\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\qquad(\forall_{\gamma\in\Gamma}),

and these actions satisfy

(4.7)

(\rho_{\gamma}^{R,\mathrm{u}}\otimes\rho_{\gamma}^{L,\mathrm{u}})\circ\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}=\Delta^{\mathrm{u}}\qquad(\forall_{\gamma\in\Gamma});

we leave filling the details to the reader. The resulting left-right
translation action
\Gamma^{2}\overset{\rho^{\mathrm{u}}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})

is given by
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\mapsto\rho_{\gamma_{1}}^{L,\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma_{2}}^{R,\mathrm{u}}
,
and its ‘diagonal’ is called the
adjoint action

\Gamma\overset{\mathrm{ad}^{\mathrm{u}}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})
;
namely,
\mathrm{ad}_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{u}}:=\rho_{\gamma}^{L,\mathrm{u}}\circ\rho_{\gamma}^{R,\mathrm{u}}

for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Our intention is to deform convolution semigroups on
\mathbb{G}
, so the
next proposition plays a key role. Recall from Theorem
3.2

that the natural representation
\pi^{\mathrm{can}}
of

the morphism
\Delta^{\Psi}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}},\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}})

.

Let

(1)

\Gamma\overset{\mathrm{ad}^{\mathrm{u}}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{u}}(\mathbb{G})

(2)

If the equivalent conditions above

a.

Applying the construction of Proposition

\mu^{\Psi}

Proof.

(1)
. The map

\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}

we obtain
\mathscr{R}_{\mu}^{\Psi}\circ\tilde{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}}=\tilde{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}}\circ\mathscr{L}_{\mu\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{G}}^{\Psi}
.
Consequently, using the fact that
\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}
is invariant
under
\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}
, we get

\begin{split}\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\pi^{\Psi}\circ\mathscr{R}_{\mu}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}&=\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\pi^{\Psi}\circ\mathscr{R}_{\mu}^{\Psi}\circ\tilde{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}\\
&=\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\pi^{\Psi}\circ\tilde{\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}}|_{\mathrm{C}_{0}(\mathbb{G})^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}}}\circ\mathscr{L}_{\mu\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{G}}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}\\
&=\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\pi^{\Psi}\circ\mathscr{L}_{\mu\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{G}}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}\\
&=\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}\circ\pi^{\Psi}\circ\mathscr{L}_{\mu\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}}}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of (
4.11
).
Now (
4.12
) follows easily if
\mu

is symmetric, that is,
\mu=\mu\circ\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}}
.
∎

The next theorem, which is the second main result of this paper, follows
as a direct corollary of Proposition
4.17
.
We stress again that the co-amenability assumption might be unnecessary.

Theorem 4.18
.

Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is co-amenable. Under the assumptions
of Theorem
4.15
, if each state in the convolution
semigroup
{(\mu_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{G}
is symmetric (i.e.,

\mathtt{R}

-invariant),
then each state in the induced convolution semigroup
{(\mu_{t}^{\Psi})}_{t\geq 0}

on
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}
is symmetric (i.e.,
\text{\scalebox{1.15}{$\mathtt{R}$}}_{\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}}
-invariant).

4.4.
The commutative case

In this subsection we focus on the particular case that
\mathbb{G}=G
is
a locally compact
group
; as before,
\Gamma
is an abelian
closed subgroup of
G
and
\Psi
is a normalised continuous
2
-cocycle
on
\hat{\Gamma}
. We write
G^{\Psi}
for
\mathbb{G}^{\Psi}
and
begin with a few observations.

Observation 4.19
.

In order for the locally compact quantum
group
G^{\Psi}
to be of interest it must not be commutative,
equivalently, its dual
\widehat{G^{\Psi}}=\hat{G}_{\Psi}

should not be co-commutative. This means that, with
\Omega:=(L\otimes L)(\Psi)\in\operatorname{VN}(G)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{VN}(G)

as usual, we should not have
\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}=\sigma\circ\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}
,
where
\sigma
is the flip operator and
\hat{\Delta}_{\Omega}=(\operatorname{Ad}\Omega)\circ\hat{\Delta}

as in Subsection
2.2
. Equivalently, the
unitary
\Omega\sigma(\Omega)^{*}\in\operatorname{VN}(G)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{VN}(G)
should
not belong to the commutant of
\left\{\lambda_{g}^{G}\otimes\lambda_{g}^{G}\in\operatorname{VN}(G)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{VN}(G):g\in G\right\}
.

Observation 4.20
.

Denote again by
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
the centraliser of
\Gamma
inside

G
. A computation similar to (
3.13
) shows that
the canonical embedding
L_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}
of
L^{\infty}(\widehat{Z_{G}(\Gamma)})=\operatorname{VN}(Z_{G}(\Gamma))

inside
L^{\infty}(\hat{G})=\operatorname{VN}(G)
turns
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
into a closed
quantum subgroup of
G^{\Psi}
. Furthermore, one can repeat
the proof of Proposition
3.5
to show that the
restriction map
\Pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}:\mathrm{C}_{0}(G)\to\mathrm{C}_{0}(Z_{G}(\Gamma))

induces a morphism
\Pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}^{\Psi}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(G)^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}},\mathrm{C}_{0}(Z_{G}(\Gamma))^{\overline{\Psi}\boxtimes\dot{\Psi}})

and the morphism
\pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}^{\Psi}\circ\Pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathrm{C}_{0}(G^{\Psi}),\mathrm{C}_{0}(Z_{G}(\Gamma)^{\Psi}=Z_{G}(\Gamma)))

is the strong quantum homomorphism associated to
Z_{G}(\Gamma)

being a closed quantum subgroup of
G^{\Psi}
, where the
equality
Z_{G}(\Gamma)^{\Psi}=Z_{G}(\Gamma)
follows from
Proposition
3.4
. In addition, the
co-unit
\epsilon_{G^{\Psi}}
of
G^{\Psi}
equals

\epsilon_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}\circ\pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}^{\Psi}\circ\Pi_{Z_{G}(\Gamma)}^{\Psi}\circ{(\pi^{\Psi})}^{-1}
.

Consequently, repeating the proof of Corollary
4.13

shows that for every probability measure
\mu
on
G
that is supported
by
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
, the ‘deformed’ state
\mu^{\Psi}
of
\mathrm{C}_{0}(G^{\Psi})

given by Proposition
4.10

(2)

is also ‘supported by
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
’, i.e., it factors through
the strong quantum homomorphism associated to
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
being
a closed quantum subgroup of
G^{\Psi}
. This strengthens
Remark
4.12

(1)
,
and means that when seeking in the commutative case examples of the
results of the previous subsection in which the convolution semigroup
of measures cannot be ‘transported directly’ to the Rieffel deformation,
we should avoid measures supported by the centraliser
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
.

Remark that if
\Gamma
is compact, then as a particular case of
Remark
4.14
we can take an arbitrary probability
measure
\nu
on
G
and integrate
\Gamma\ni\gamma\mapsto\nu\circ\mathrm{ad}_{\gamma}
,
where
\Gamma\overset{\mathrm{ad}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}(G)
is the
adjoint action, to get an
\mathrm{ad}
-invariant probability measure

\mu
on
G
, which can be later used to generate a convolution
semigroup of measures on
G
associated with the relevant compound
Poisson process and satisfying the assumptions of Corollary
B
.
In addition, if
\nu
has full support, then so has
\mu
. If
\Gamma

contains the centre
Z=Z(G)
of
G
, we can replace the condition
that
\Gamma
is compact by the slightly more general condition that
the quotient
\Gamma/Z
is compact, in which case the fact that
\Gamma\overset{\mathrm{ad}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}(G)

factors through the quotient map
\Gamma\to\Gamma/Z
allows us to
consider the induced map
\Gamma/Z\ni\left[\gamma\right]\mapsto\nu\circ\mathrm{ad}_{\gamma}

and integrate it (with respect to the Haar measure on
\Gamma/Z
)
to obtain an
\mathrm{ad}
-invariant probability measure on
G
.

Example 4.21
.

Take
G:=\mathrm{\tilde{E}}(2)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w&z\\
0&w^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right):z\in\mathbb{C},w\in\mathbb{T}\right\}
as a topological subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\Gamma:=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w&0\\
0&w^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right):w\in\mathbb{T}\right\}\cong\mathbb{T}.
Then
Z_{G}(\Gamma)=\Gamma
. As explained in the previous paragraph,
averaging allows constructing probability measures on
G
that are
invariant under the adjoint action
\Gamma\overset{\mathrm{ad}}{\curvearrowright}\mathrm{C}_{0}(G)

and have full support and so, in particular, are not supported by

Z_{G}(\Gamma)
.

Fixing
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
,
w_{1}\neq\pm w_{2}
, consider
the bicharacter
\Psi:=\Psi_{w_{1},w_{2}}
on
\hat{\mathbb{T}}\cong\mathbb{Z}

given by
\Psi_{w_{1},w_{2}}(n,m):=w_{1}^{n}w_{2}^{m}
,
(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
and form the Rieffel deformation locally compact quantum group
G^{\Psi}
.
To prove that
G^{\Psi}
is not commutative we should show
that
\Omega\sigma(\Omega)^{*}
does not belong to the commutant

\left\{\lambda_{g}^{G}\otimes\lambda_{g}^{G}\in\operatorname{VN}(G)\mathbin{\overline{\otimes}}\operatorname{VN}(G):g\in G\right\}^{\prime}
,
see Observation
4.19
. But
\Omega=\lambda_{j(w_{1})}^{G}\otimes\lambda_{j(w_{2})}^{G}

where
j(w):=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w&0\\
0&w^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for
w\in\mathbb{T}
, and so
\Omega\sigma(\Omega)^{*}=\lambda_{j(w_{1}w_{2}^{-1})}^{G}\otimes\lambda_{j(w_{2}w_{1}^{-1})}^{G}
,
which is indeed not in the above commutant because
w_{1}w_{2}^{-1}\notin\left\{1,-1\right\}
.

At the moment we do not have an example of a locally compact group

G
with a non-compact (or more generally, non-compact modulo the
centre of
G
) abelian subgroup
\Gamma
admitting a probability
measure on
G
which is invariant under the adjoint action of
\Gamma

and not supported by the centraliser
Z_{G}(\Gamma)
. The question
of whether or not such an example exists seems to be related to deep
topological group considerations. Uri Bader has informed us that results
of
[
BDL
]
imply that when
G
is an algebraic
group and
\Gamma
satisfies mild non-compactness assumptions, such
an invariant measure cannot exist.

Finding non-trivial examples where
\mathbb{G}
is a ‘genuine’ locally
compact quantum group is quite tricky and requires further work; the
main motivation behind this paper is to look for ways of producing
such examples. One can always take a single state, average it and
then construct the associated compound Poisson process as described
above for classical
\mathbb{G}
, but this is rather straightforward and
less interesting from the analytic point of view.

Asymptotics of rational representations for algebraic groups
Lander Guerrero Sánchez, Henrique Souza

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behaviour of the cohomology of subgroups Γ \Gamma of an algebraic group 𝖦 \mathsf{G} with coefficients in the various irreducible rational representations of 𝖦 \mathsf{G} and raise a conjecture about it. Namely, we expect that the dimensions of these cohomology groups approximate the ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Betti numbers of Γ \Gamma with a controlled error term. We provide positive answers when 𝖦 \mathsf{G} is a product of copies of 𝖲𝖫 2 \SL_{2} . As an application, we obtain new proofs of J. Lott’s and W. Lück’s computation of the ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Betti numbers of hyperbolic 3 3 -manifolds and W. Fu’s upper bound on the growth of cusp forms for non totally real fields, which is sharp in the imaginary quadratic case.



1.
Introduction

Discrete subgroups of semi-simple Lie groups and their cohomology are ubiquitous in modern mathematics. In the context of geometry and topology, they appear as fundamental groups of locally symmetric Riemannian manifolds and their cohomology groups have a natural interpretation in terms of differential forms. In the context of number theory, arithmetic lattices encode critical information about number fields and their cohomology is related to the theory of automorphic forms. At this point, it goes without saying that there is an extensive and deep literature on this broad topic, and just to name a few major works we cite the classical books of M. S. Raghunathan
[
Rag72
]
and A. Borel and N. Wallach
[
BW00
]

2.
Lie modules and Sylvester functions

The objective of this section is to define the concepts necessary to state a stronger version of Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
: the Sylvester rank and dimension functions associated to a finite dimensional representation of a group. We postpone to Section
3
the proof that the conjectures stated here are indeed stronger than their respective counterparts in the introduction.

A Sylvester module rank function on a ring
R
is a function defined on the class of all finitely presented
R
-modules taking values on the non-negative reals that satisfies a series of conditions analogous to that of the usual dimension of vector spaces over a field (cf. Definition
2.1
below). Analogously, a Sylvester matrix rank function assigns a non-negative real number to each matrix over
R
in a fashion similar to the usual rank of matrices over fields (cf. Definition
2.2
). The viewpoint of Sylvester functions not only allows us to explain the asymptotic behaviour of cohomology in a more uniform fashion, but also permit us to relax the hypothesis of
FP_{\infty}
and focus instead on arbitrary finitely generated subgroups - again, not necessarily discrete.

For this section, fix
\mathsf{G}
a semi-simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
and
\Gamma
a finitely generated subgroup of
\mathsf{G}
. Henceforth, we assume
\mathsf{G}
to be simply-connected (this will soon be made clear not toimply any loss of generality, see Remark
2.6
). We simultaneously see
\mathsf{G}
as an algebraic group, a linear group over
\mathbb{C}
and an affine group scheme defined over
\mathbb{Z}
by the associated Chevalley group, with context or an explicit reference making it clear which structure we are considering.

Since
\mathsf{G}
is linear over
\mathbb{C}
, by looking at the coefficients of the generators of
\varphi(\Gamma)
one can find a subring
S_{0}
of
\mathbb{C}
with field of fractions
L_{0}\leq\mathbb{C}
such that
\Gamma
is a subgroup of the group
\mathsf{G}(S_{0})
of
S_{0}
-points of
\mathsf{G}
, where the notion of
S_{0}
-points in the linear and algebraic sense coincide by the simply-connected hypothesis
[
Ste16
, Cor. 3 of Thm. 7]
.

Observe that every rational representation of
\mathsf{G}
over
\mathbb{C}
is also defined over the prime field, i.e. it is the extension of scalars of a rational representation
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Q})\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
- cf.
[
Ste16
, Cor. 1 to Thm. 7, Thm. 39(e) and the remark thereafter]
. Fix a maximal torus
\mathsf{T}
in
\mathsf{G}
and a set of positive roots for its root system
R
. The irreducible rational representations of
\mathsf{G}
are parameterized by the dominant integral elements
\bm{\lambda}
in the character group
\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})
by taking the highest weight appearing in the weight decomposition. Moreover,
\bm{\lambda}
can be further parameterized by
n
non-negative integers
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
once an order on the roots is fixed. If
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
is the irreducible
\mathsf{G}
-module associated to
\bm{\lambda}
, one can find a
rational form
(ie. a
\mathbb{Q}
-submodule)
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}}
in
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
that is equivariant under
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Q})
and such that
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
.

The uniqueness of the Chevalley group associated to a given weight lattice
[
Ste16
, Cor. 5 to Thm. 4’]
shows that the
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Q})
-isomorphism class of
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}}
does not depend on the chosen rational form. Hence, for any subfield
L
of
\mathbb{C}
we denote by
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}
the
\mathsf{G}(L)
-module obtained by the extension of scalars of
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}}
, which is well defined up to a rational isomorphism. In particular, we have that
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
.

For each field
L
containing
L_{0}
and each finitely generated right
L[\Gamma]
-module
M
, define

\dim^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}M=\frac{\dim_{L}M\otimes_{L[\Gamma]}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}{\dim_{L}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}\,.

If
L=\mathbb{C}
, we denote this function simply by
\dim^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a unital ring
R
, a function
\dim
on finitely presented
R
-modules valued on the non-negative reals is called a
Sylvester module dimension function
if it satisfies the following properties:

(SMod1)

\dim 0=0
and
\dim R=1
;

(SMod2)

\dim M_{1}\oplus M_{2}=\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{2}
; and

(SMod3)

if
M_{1}\to M_{2}\to M_{3}\to 0
is exact, then

\dim M_{3}\leq\dim M_{2}\leq\dim M_{1}+\dim M_{3}\,.

The function
\dim^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
satisfies (SMod1)-(SMod3), and is thus a Sylvester module dimension function on
L[\Gamma]
.

We define the associated matrix rank function analogously. If
A
is an
r\times s
matrix over
L[\Gamma]
and
M=L[\Gamma]^{r}/AL[\Gamma]^{s}
, then

\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}A=r-\dim_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}^{\Gamma}M\,.

(1)

Once more, if
L=\mathbb{C}
we denote this rank function simply by
\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\rk
on matrices over
R
valued on the non-negative real numbers is called a
Sylvester matrix rank function
if it satisfies the following properties:

(SMat1)

\rk A=0
if
A
is any zero matrix, and
\rk(1)=1
;

(SMat2)

\rk AB\leq\min\{\rk A\,,\,\rk B\}
for any pair of matrices
A
and
B
that can be multiplied;

(SMat3)

\rk\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}=\rk A+\rk B
for any matrices
A
and
B
; and

(SMat4)

\rk\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\geq\rk A+\rk B
for any matrices
A
,
B
and
C
of appropriate sizes.

The function
\rk=\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
satisfies (SMat1)-(SMat4) and is thus a Sylvester matrix rank function on
L[\Gamma]
.

There is a general relation of the form given by equation (
1
) that gives a bijection between the set of Sylvester matrix rank functions and Sylvester module dimension functions over any ring (
[
Jai19b
, Sec. 5]
). Other examples of Sylvester matrix rank functions on
L[\Gamma]
are the von Neumann rank
\rk_{\Gamma}
, the twisted von Neumann rank
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}
of Appendix
A
where
\chi\colon Z\leq_{\text{finite}}Z(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is any central character and the von Neumann ranks

\rk_{\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}}^{L}A=\frac{\rk_{L}A_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}{|\Gamma:\Gamma^{\prime}|}

induced by the finite index normal subgroups
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
, where
A_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
denotes the image of
A
under the natural map
L[\Gamma]\to L[\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}]
.

More generally, if
\varphi\colon R\to R^{\prime}
is a homomorphism of rings and
\rk
is a Sylvester matrix rank function on
R^{\prime}
, then the pullback along
\varphi
defines a Sylvester matrix rank function on
R
by
\varphi^{\#}\rk(A)=\rk(\varphi(A))
, whose associated Sylvester module dimension function
\varphi^{\#}\dim
is given by
\varphi^{\#}\dim M=\dim M\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}
. The functions rank functions
\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
,
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}
and
\rk_{\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}}^{L}
can be seen as the pullbacks of the rank functions defined on
\End_{L}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L})
,
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
and
L[\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}]
respectively.

If
N
is a left
L[\Gamma]
-module, one also defines

\dim_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}^{\Gamma}N=\frac{\dim_{L}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}\otimes_{L[\Gamma]}N}{\dim_{L}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}

and a direct computation using duals shows that it induces the same matrix rank function.

Remark 2.3
.

For a matrix
A\in\Mat(L[\Gamma])
defined over a field
L\geq L_{0}
and any field extension
L\leq K
, one also has that
A\in\Mat(K[\Gamma])
. Observe that the rank is independent of the choice of field, i.e.

\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{K}}^{\Gamma}A=\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}^{\Gamma}A\,.

This is because

\dim^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{K}}K\otimes_{L}M=\dim^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}M\,.

In particular,
\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}^{\Gamma}
is induced by the composition
L[\Gamma]\to\End_{L}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L})\to\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
.

Let us now state a stronger formulation of Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
in terms of Sylvester matrix rank functions. We recall our running hypothesis that
\mathsf{G}
is a semi-simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
and
\Gamma
is a finitely generated subgroup of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{C})
.

Given a central character
\chi\colon\mathsf{Z}(\mathsf{G})\to\chi^{\times}
, denote by
\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})_{\chi}
be the subset of the dominant integral elements
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})
such that
\mathsf{Z}(\mathsf{G})
acts on
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
through
\chi
. We believe that the general qualitative behaviour of the rank functions is described as follows:

Conjecture 2.4
.

As rank functions on
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, we have that

\lim_{\min\lambda_{i}\to\infty}\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}=\rk^{\chi}_{\Gamma}\,,

where the limit is taken over
\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})_{\chi}
and
\rk^{\chi}_{\Gamma}
is the twisted von Neumann rank of
\Gamma
by the central character
\chi
restricted to
\Gamma
.

As for the quantitative approach, we expect the following:

Conjecture 2.5
.

For any
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})_{\chi}
and any matrix
A
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, we have that

\left|\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}(A)-\rk^{\chi}_{\Gamma}(A)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)\,.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be a semi-simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathsf{G}
be the simply connected algebraic group with the same root system of
\mathsf{H}
. Then, if Conjecture
2.4
holds for any finitely generated subgroup of
\mathsf{G}
, it also does for
\mathsf{H}
. Indeed, any matrix over
\mathsf{H}
can be lifted to a matrix over
\mathsf{G}
, and one readily checks that both the ranks induced by a representation
\mathsf{W}
and the twisted von Neumann ranks over
\mathsf{G}
and over
\mathsf{H}
coincide for this pair of matrices. The same is true for Conjecture
2.5
: if it holds (virtually) for all finitely generated subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
, it also holds (virtually) for all finitely generated subgroups of
\mathsf{H}
. Hence, it suffices to prove these conjectures for the unique simply connected semi-simple algebraic group of any given root system.

3.
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers

Associated to any abstract group
\Gamma
, we have a sequence of numerical invariants
b_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
called the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers
of
\Gamma
. They are defined as the von Neumann dimension of the
\ell^{2}
-homology groups
\HH_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathcal{N}(\Gamma))
, where
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the action of
\Gamma
on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)

[
Lü02
, Sec. 6.5]
. If
\Gamma
is of type
FP_{n+1}
over
\mathbb{C}
, the
n
first
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
-Betti numbers of
\Gamma
can also be recovered from the complex
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]}\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
, where
F_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{C}
is a free resolution of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
with
F_{i}
of finite rank for
0\leq i\leq n+1
, by
[
Lü02
, Lem. 6.53]
. By taking instead the twisted von Neumann dimension
\dim^{\chi}_{\Gamma}
of
\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi})
as in Appendix
A
, one obtains the
i
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of
\Gamma
twisted by a central character
\chi
, and these all coincide with the usual
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers if
\Gamma
is torsion-free.

When
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
is the fundamental group of an orientable Riemannian manifold,
b_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
is related to the trace of the heat kernel of
M

[
Lü02
, Sec. 1.3.2]
. These numbers vanish if
\Gamma
is locally infinite amenable
[
Lü02
, Thm. 7.2]
, and the vanishing of
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
is related to fibering in virtually residually finite rationally solvable (RFRS) groups
[
Kie20
]
, a class that includes all virtually compact special groups and hence the fundamental groups of finite volume hyperbolic
3
-manifolds.

The main objective of this section is to show how one can compute
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers using the von Neumann rank for matrices, and hence to prove that the conjectures of Section
2
on Sylvester matrix rank functions imply their respective cohomological counterparts of the introduction.

A consequence of the (twisted) Lück’s approximation theorem is that, for any finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
\mathsf{W}
and any residual chain
\{\Gamma_{i}\}
of finite index normal subgroups of
\Gamma
, the
\ell^{2}
-Betti number
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
can be recovered as the homological gradient

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma_{i},\mathsf{W})}{|\Gamma:\Gamma_{i}|}\,,

as long as
\Gamma
is of type
FP_{i+1}
over
\mathbb{C}

[
Lü02
, Thm. 13.49]
(see also
[
BJ24
]
). If
\Gamma
is a subgroup of some
\mathsf{G}
satisfying Conjecture
2.4
, then the
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-modules
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
provide another way to approximate the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
\Gamma\leq\mathsf{G}
is a subgroup of type
FP_{n+1}
over
\mathbb{C}
that satisfies Conjecture
2.4
. If
\Gamma
intersects
\mathsf{Z}(\mathsf{G})
trivially, then for any sequence
\{\mathsf{W}_{k}\}
of irreducible representations of
\mathsf{G}
such that
\min\{\lambda_{1}(k),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(k)\}\to\infty
we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{k})}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}_{k}}=\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)

whenever
0\leq i\leq n
. In particular,
\Gamma
also satisfies Conjecture
1.1
.

Proof.

Let
F_{\bullet}\to\mathbb{C}
be a free resolution of
\mathbb{C}
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
such that
F_{i}
is of finite rank for every
0\leq i\leq k+1
. For every
1\leq i\leq k
, there exists a
s\times r
matrix
A
and a
r\times t
matrix
B
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
such that, after choosing bases for
F_{i-1}
,
F_{i}
and
F_{i+1}
, the maps
\psi
and
\varphi
in

\cdots\to F_{i+1}\overset{\psi}{\to}F_{i}\overset{\varphi}{\to}F_{i-1}\to\cdots

are given by right multiplication with
A
and
B
, respectively.

After tensoring with
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, we get a complex of the form

\cdots\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma)^{s}\overset{\widetilde{\psi}}{\to}\ell^{2}(\Gamma)^{r}\overset{\widetilde{\varphi}}{\to}\ell^{2}(\Gamma)^{t}\to\cdots\,.

The kernel of
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a closed
\Gamma
-invariant subspace of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
which contains the image of
\widetilde{\psi}
. The
i
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of
\Gamma
is then defined as

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=\dim_{\Gamma}\ker(\widetilde{\varphi})/\overline{\Image(\widetilde{\psi})}\,.=\dim\ker(\widetilde{\varphi})-\dim\overline{\Image(\widetilde{\psi})}\,,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\dim_{\Gamma}
is exact under short exact sequences
[
Lü02
, Thm. 6.7(4b)]
.

Using that
\dim_{\Gamma}\overline{\Image(\widetilde{\psi})}=\rk_{\Gamma}A
and
\dim_{\Gamma}\ker(\widetilde{\varphi})=r-\rk_{\Gamma}B
, we obtain that

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=r-\rk_{\Gamma}A-\rk_{\Gamma}B\,.

On the other hand, tensoring with
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
gives us the complex

\cdots\to\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{s}\overset{\widehat{\psi}}{\to}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{r}\overset{\widehat{\varphi}}{\to}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{t}\to\cdots\,,

and
\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})=\Tor_{i}^{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]}(\mathbb{C},\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
is isomorphic to
\ker(\widehat{\varphi})/\Image(\widehat{\psi})
. From this, we get that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker(\widehat{\varphi})/\Image(\widehat{\psi})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker(\widehat{\varphi})-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Image(\widehat{\psi})\,.

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Image(\widehat{\psi})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}\cdot\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}A
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ker(\widehat{\varphi})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}\cdot(r-\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}B)
, we conclude that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}\cdot(r-\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}A-\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}B)\,.

The result now follows from the validity of Conjecture
2.4
and Remark
A.5
. The statement about cohomology follows by considering duals over
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume now that
\Gamma\leq\mathsf{G}
is a subgroup of type
FP_{n+1}
over
\mathbb{C}
that satisfies Conjecture
2.5
. If
\Gamma
intersects
\mathsf{Z}(\mathsf{G})
trivially, then for any representation
\mathsf{W}
of
\mathsf{G}
with highest weight
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
we have that

\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}}-b_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)

for any
0\leq i\leq n
. In particular,
\Gamma
also satisfies Conjecture
1.2
.

Proof.

During the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we show that, under these hypotheses, we have an upper bound

\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}-\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)\right|\leq\left|\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}(A)-\rk_{\Gamma}(A)\right|+\left|\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}(B)-\rk_{\Gamma}(B)\right|

where
A
and
B
are matrices over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
. So, once again, the result follows from the validity of Conjecture
2.5
, Remark
A.5
and duality for passing to cohomology.
∎

It is important to note that in many cases, satisfying Conjecture
1.2
only virtually is enough to obtain bounds on cohomology. We do it in the following proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
\Gamma\leq\mathsf{G}
is a torsion-free subgroup of type
FP_{n+1}
that virtually satisfies Conjecture
2.5
. Then, if
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0
for some
0\leq i\leq n
, we have that

\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH^{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{W}}=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)\,.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
that satisfies Conjecture
2.5
. Let
\alpha\colon\HH_{i}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathsf{W})\to\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})
and
\beta\colon\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})\to\HH_{i}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathsf{W})
be the corestriction and transfer maps in homology, respectively. Since
\alpha\circ\beta
equals multiplication by the index
|\Gamma\colon\Gamma^{\prime}|
in
\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})
, it is an isomorphism and therefore
\beta
must be injective, see
[
Bro82
, Prop. 9.5(ii)]
. Consequently,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W})\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathsf{W})

and, since
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma^{\prime})=|\Gamma\colon\Gamma^{\prime}|\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0
, the claim follows.
∎

4.
p
-adic analytic groups

Our main tool for attacking Conjecture
2.5
is translating it into a statement about
p
-adic universal enveloping algebras. This is done in Section
5
by exploiting the rational structure of
\mathsf{G}
and embedding the finitely generated subfield
S_{0}\leq\mathbb{C}
into a
p
-adic field. By taking the closure with respect to the
p
-adic topology, we then shift the focus to asymptotic properties of
p
-adic analytic groups. Hence, this section is devoted to collecting the definitions, properties and theorems about
p
-adic analytic groups that will be needed for the rest of the paper.

Let
G
be a profinite group and
p
be and odd prime number. The completed group algebra of
G
, also referred to as the
Iwasawa algebra
in the literature, is

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U]\,,

where the inverse limit is taken over all the open normal subgroups
U
of
G
.
For any pair of right and left profinite
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-modules
M
and
N
, we let

M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}N\quad\text{and}\quad M\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}N

be the usual and the completed tensor product over the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, respectively (see
[
RZ10
, Sec. 5.5]
). Both have the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module, with the latter being the completion of the former with respect to the topology defined by the finite quotients of
M
and
N
. We note that, if either
M
or
N
is finitely generated, then
M\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}N\simeq M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}N
by
[
Bru66
, Lem. 2.1(i)]
.

A profinite group
G
is called
p
-adic analytic
if it is a Lie group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, see
[
DDSMS99
, Interlude A]
for the many equivalent definitions. We say that
G
has dimension
d
if it has dimension
d
as an analytic manifold; the integer
d
can also be recovered as the dimension over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of its Lie algebra and as the minimal number of topological generators for any open uniform subgroup
U
of
G
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Opposite to what the notation might suggest, the algebra
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is
not
in general isomorphic to the inverse limit
\varprojlim\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G/U]
. The completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is known to be an Ore domain if
G
is a torsion-free
p
-adic analytic profinite (and hence pro-
p
) group (see
[
AB06
, Sec. 4]
). Let
\mathcal{Q}_{G}
be the associated division ring of fractions. For any left
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
M
and any right
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
N
, we define

\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M=\dim_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}}\mathcal{Q}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}N=\dim_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}}N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\mathcal{Q}_{G}\,.

This is a Sylvester module dimension function on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
whose associated matrix rank function is
\rk_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}=\rk_{\mathcal{Q}_{G}}
. Observe that
\mathcal{Q}_{G}
also contains
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, and therefore induces a matrix rank function
\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
with associated dimension function
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]}
.

For any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
M
, we define
\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M
as
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}M
. The following result is an analogue of the Lück approximation theorem for analytic pro-
p
groups.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
Har79
, Thm. 1.10]
, cf.
[
BLLS14
, Thm. 2.1]
)
.

Let
G\leq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a torsion-free
p
-adic analytic group of dimension
d
. Let
U_{i}
be the intersection of
G
with the
i
-th congruence subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. If
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module, then

\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M)=\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}(M)\cdot|G:U_{i}|+O(|G:U_{i}|^{1-1/d})\,.

In particular, we have that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M)}{|G:U_{i}|}=\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}M\,.

From Harris’ result we obtain the analogue statement for rank functions.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G
be
p
-adic analytic torsion-free and
A
be a matrix over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
. Then,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{G/U_{i}}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A_{i}=\rk_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]}A\,,

where
A_{i}
denotes the image of
A
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G/U_{i}]
.

There is a correspondence between uniform pro-
p
groups and uniform
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras, originally described by Lazard
[
Laz65
]
, given by
\exp
and
\log
functors. Let
G
be a uniform pro-
p
group, and consider the formal power series

\exp(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{i}}{i!}\quad\text{and}\quad\log(X+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{X^{i}}{i}.

Inside the completed group algebra
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, the series
\log(g)
converges for all
g\in G
, and
\log(G)
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra with the bracket given by the commutator of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
. Furthermore, we have that
\exp(\log(G))=G
. More generally, if
G
is a subgroup of the group of units of a Banach
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra
A
, the topology on
G
is induced by the norm of
A
and all the elements
g-1
have norm strictly less than
1
, we can identify
\log(G)\subseteq A
with
\log(G)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras
[
DDSMS99
, Cor. 7.14]
. We call such an algebra
A
an
associative Banach envelope
of the uniform pro-
p
group
G
.

This correspondence allows us to describe algebraically the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
G
as a
p
-adic analytic group, which is nothing more than
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{log}(G)
. The Lie algebra
\operatorname{log}(G)
is
powerful
in the sense that
[\operatorname{log}(G),\operatorname{log}(G)]\subseteq p\operatorname{log}(G)
. If
G
has dimension
d
, then
\operatorname{log}(G)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
d
. Taking any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
\{h_{1},\cdots,h_{d}\}
of
\operatorname{log}(G)
, the Poincaré-Birkoff-Witt theorem tells us that the monomials
h_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{a_{d}}
form a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-basis of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Define a norm on the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
U(\mathfrak{g})
by setting

\left\|\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}\alpha_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}h_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{a_{d}}\right\|=\max\{|\alpha_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}|\}\,.

Since
\log(G)
is a powerful Lie algebra, this norm is submultiplicative. By completing
U(\mathfrak{g})
we obtain a Banach
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
called the
completed universal enveloping algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
, which can be described as

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}=\left\{\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}\alpha_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}h_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{a_{d}}:|\alpha_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}|\to 0\text{ as }(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\to\infty\right\}\,.

The completed universal enveloping algebra is an
almost commutative affinoid algebra
in the sense of
[
AW13
, Sec. 3]
. We collect some properties of the completed group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
and the associated completed universal enveloping algebra in the proposition that follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
,
\log(G)
and
\mathfrak{g}
be as before. Take an associative Banach envelope
A
for
G
with logarithm map
\log\colon G\to A
. Let
\iota\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
be the inclusion map from
\mathfrak{g}
into its completed universal enveloping algebra.

(a)

The ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is a local ring and the augmentation ideal
I
is the unique maximal two-sided ideal.

(b)

The set

T=\bigcup_{a\geq 0}p^{a}+I^{a+1}

is an Ore set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]
.

(c)

The composition

G\overset{\log}{\to}\mathfrak{g}\overset{\iota}{\to}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}\overset{\exp}{\to}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}

induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]T^{-1}\simeq\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
. In particular, we have a flat and epic embedding
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]\to\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
.

Proof.

(a) is generally true for completed group algebras of pro-
p
groups over DVRs with residue field of characteristic
p
, see
[
Bru66
, Sec. 4]
. (b) follows from
[
AW13
, Lem. 2.4]
. (c) is shown in
[
AW13
, Thm. 10.4]
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let us observe how this objects appear in the context of Conjecture
2.4
. The group
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-points of
\mathsf{G}
is a
p
-adic analytic group and, if
\mathfrak{h}
is the Chevalley
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra of
\mathsf{G}
, it is related to the analytic Lie algebra of
G
by an extension of scalars:
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{h}
.

Consider the congruence subgroups
U_{i}=\ker(\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{i}\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
. By
[
HKN11
, Lem. 2.2.2]
and the remark thereafter, the subgroups
U_{i}
are all uniform pro-
p
groups for
i\geq 1
. In fact,
U_{1}
can be identified with J.-P. Serre’s standard formal group associated to the group law defined by
\mathsf{G}
, and the
U_{i}
are given by the lower
p
-central series of
U_{1}
. Observe that
\mathfrak{g}
is the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra of all the
U_{i}
, since they are open in
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. To each
U_{i}
, we can associate Lazard’s
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra
\log(U_{i})
, and we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras
\log(U_{i})\simeq p^{i}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{h}
by observing that
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is linear over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and thus
U_{i}\to\mathsf{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is an associative Banach envelope.

If
M
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module that is finite dimensional over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, then the action of
U(\mathfrak{g})
continuously extends to an action of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
.
Consider now the modules
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of Section
2
. This action also defines a module
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
by restriction. On the other hand, we have an action of the abstract group algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[U_{i}]
on
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
which extends to an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
– by the completeness of
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and the description of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
as a ring of non-commutative power series
[
DDSMS99
, Thm. 7.20]
. Hence,
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
also has the structure of a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
-module.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have that
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\simeq\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
as
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
-modules.

Proof.

After identifying the underlying sets of
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
and
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, the isomorphism is an immediate consequence of the Lie group/Lie algebra correspondence, which provides the outer square of a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\log}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exp}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exp}

whose lower triangle commutes by continuity.
∎

Now that there is no ambiguity about the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{i}]\!]
-module structure of
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, we conclude this section by remarking that, for every closed subgroup
H
of
U_{i}
and finitely generated
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![H]\!]
-module
M
, we have the Sylvester module dimension functions

\dim^{H}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}M=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}M\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![H]\!]}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

with associated Sylvester matrix rank functions
\rk^{H}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
.

5.
Going from
\mathbb{C}
to
p
-adic fields

We keep the notation from Section
2
:
\mathsf{G}
is a semi-simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, each dominant integral element
\bm{\lambda}
in the character group
\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{T})
of a maximal torus
\mathsf{T}
yields an irreducible rational representation
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\Gamma
is a finitely generated subgroup of the group
\mathsf{G}(S_{0})
of
S_{0}
-points of
\mathsf{G}
, where
S_{0}
is a finitely generated subring of
\mathbb{C}
with field of fractions
L_{0}
.

The first step in translating Conjecture
2.4
to a
p
-adic analytic setting is the following result. By Cassels’ embedding theorem
[
Cas76
]
, for infinitely many primes
p
there is an embedding of

into
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. From this, we obtain the following result:

Proposition 5.1
.

For infinitely many primes

\mathsf{G}(S_{0})
embeds in
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

We call a prime

of the ring
S
. If

\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}}A=\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}A=\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}A

p
of

.

We now proceed to obtain the finite index subgroup

for which we are going to try and prove Conjecture

. We recall the congruence subgroups

of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

Lemma 5.2
.

For any enveloping prime
p
of
S
, there exists a finite index normal subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
such that its
p
-adic closure
\overline{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is a uniform pro-

Proof.

The
p

\overline{\Gamma}
in

p

\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma\cap V
.
∎

Let

, where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is the subgroup from Lemma

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A
be a matrix over
S[\Gamma^{\prime}]
, where
S_{0}\leq S
is a finitely generated subring of
\mathbb{C}
. If
p
is an enveloping prime of

, we have that

\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}A=\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]}A=\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]}A\,.

Proof.

Let
L
be the field of fractions of

, and take
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}\cap U_{i}
. Then, the
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
constitute a chain of normal subgroups of

of finite index with trivial intersection, and moreover

\displaystyle\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}A

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{C}}A_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}}^{L}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{G/(G\cap U_{i})}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}A_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{G/(G\cap U_{i})}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A_{i}\,,

denotes the image of

∎

It now only remains to reinterpret

.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every matrix

over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]
, we have that

\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}A=\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{G}A=\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{U_{1}}A\,.

Proof.

Let
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma]^{\prime})
,

be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]
-module
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{n}/A\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{m}
and
\widehat{M}
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
-module
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{n}/A\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{m}
. It is immediately checked that
\widehat{M}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]
. Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{G}\widehat{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\widehat{M}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!])\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![G]\!]}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}M\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\Gamma^{\prime}]}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\dim^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}M.

The first equality of matrix ranks then follows, and the second equality is proven analogously.
∎

We are now able to state and prove the connection between Conjectures
2.4
and
2.5
and the
p
-adic analytic setting.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a simply-connected and semi-simple algebraic group. Assume that, for any increasing sequence of weights
\{\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}(k)\}
such that
\min\{\lambda_{i}(k)\}
grows to infinity, we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{U_{1}}=\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]}

(2)

as rank functions on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
for all but finitely many primes
p
. Then, Conjecture
2.4
holds for all finitely generated subgroups
\Gamma
of
\mathsf{G}
.

Proof.

We will apply Corollary
B.5
. Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated and Zariski-dense subgroup of
\mathsf{G}
with
\Gamma\leq\mathsf{G}(S)
where
S
is a finitely generated subring of
\mathbb{C}
with field of fractions
L
. Proposition
5.1
gives us infinitely many enveloping primes
p
for
S
such that
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Then, by Lemma
5.2
we obtain a finite index normal subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
such that
\overline{\Gamma^{\prime}}=G
is an uniform subgroup of
U_{1}
. By Proposition
5.3
and Lemma
5.4
, condition (
2
) implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rk_{\mathsf{W}^{L}_{\bm{\lambda}_{k}}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}

on
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]
. Hence, it suffices to choose an enveloping prime
p
for which (
2
) holds.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

To apply Theorem
5.5
it suffices to show that, for every non-zero
x
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
and any sequence
\{\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}(k)\}
of dominant integral elements such that
\min\lambda_{i}(k)
goes to infinity, we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rk^{U_{1}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(x)=1\,.

Indeed, fixing a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on
\mathbb{N}
, the condition above implies that the natural map

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]\to\prod_{\omega}\End_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})

extends to a homomorphism from
\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1}}
. Hence, since both
\lim_{\omega}\rk^{U_{1}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
and
\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]}
are Sylvester matrix rank functions defined on the division ring
\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1}}
, they must coincide for all matrices over
\mathcal{Q}_{U_{1}}
. In particular, they coincide over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
.

The connection between the quantitative statement of Conjecture
2.5
and the
p
-adic seting requires passing down to the finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
whose
p
-adic closure is contained in the first congruence subgroup. However, for
\Gamma^{\prime}
there results are straightforward. Indeed, if

\left|\rk^{U_{1}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(A)-\rk_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]}(A)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)

(3)

for any matrix
A
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
, then it follows directly from Proposition
5.3
, Lemma
5.4
and Remark
A.5
that
\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfies Conjecture
2.5
.

However, once more it is enough to check the validity of (
3
) only for non-zero elements
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
. This follows from a lemma inspired by
[
Fu24
, Sec. 6]
:

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
R
be an Ore domain with Ore division ring of fractions
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\rk_{R}
be the Sylvester matrix rank function on
R
induced from
\mathcal{Q}
and let
\rk
be any other Sylvester matrix rank function on
R
. For any matrix
A\in\Mat(R)
, define
\Delta(A)=|\rk_{R}A-\rk A|
. Then, there are elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in R
(depending on
A
) such that

\Delta(A)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(x_{i})\,.

Proof.

Let
\dim_{R}
and
\dim
be the dimension functions associated to
\rk_{R}
and
\rk
, respectively. We can also describe
\Delta(A)
for
A\in\Mat_{n\times m}(R)
using
\dim_{R}
and
\dim
as follows:

\Delta(A)=\left|\dim_{R}R^{m}/R^{n}A-\dim R^{m}/R^{n}A\right|\,.

Hence, it suffices to prove that, for every finitely presented
R
-module
M
, there are elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in R
such that

\left|\dim_{R}M-\dim M\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(x_{i})\,.

Since
M
is finitely generated, we can always find
R
-linearly independent elements
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in M
such that the quotient
M/(\sum Rm_{i})
is torsion, and so
\mathcal{Q}\otimes_{R}M/(\sum Rm_{i})=0
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{Q}\otimes_{R}M
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
, it suffices to take a
\mathcal{Q}
-basis of this space and multiply each element by a suitable non-zero element of
R
so that they lie in the image of
M\to\mathcal{Q}\otimes_{R}M
. Consider then three short exact sequences:

\displaystyle 0\to R^{k}\to M\to Q\to 0\,,

(4)

\displaystyle 0\to N\to R^{l}\to M\to 0\,,

(5)

\displaystyle 0\to R^{j}\to N\to P\to 0\,,

(6)

where
N
is finitely generated and
P
and
Q
are torsion. Observe that we have that
k+j=l
since
\dim_{R}
is additive under short exact sequences.

Note that, since
\mathcal{Q}
is flat over
R
, submodules and quotients of torsion modules are again torsion. Since both
Q
and
P
are finitely generated, we can find filtrations

Q=Q_{0}>Q_{1}>\cdots>Q_{e}=0\,,

P=P_{0}>P_{1}>\cdots>P_{f}=0,

such that all of the quotients
Q_{i-1}/Q_{i}
and
P_{i-1}/P_{i}
is cyclic and torsion. If
y_{1},\ldots,y_{e}\in R
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{f}\in R
are non-zero elements that annihilate the quotients
Q_{i}/Q_{i+1}
and
P_{i}/P_{i+1}
, respectively, we obtain bounds

\dim Q\leq\sum_{i=1}^{e}\dim R/Ry_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\Delta(y_{i})\text{ and }\dim P\leq\sum_{i=1}^{f}\dim R/Rz_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\Delta(z_{i})\,.

Let
n=e+f
and
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{e},z_{1},\ldots,z_{f}\}
. Taking
\dim
with respect to (
4
) and (
6
), respectively, we get that

\dim M\leq k+\dim Q\leq\dim_{R}M+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(x_{i})\,,

(7)

\dim N\leq j+\dim P\leq\dim_{R}N+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(x_{i})\,.

(8)

Now, we take
\dim
with respect to (
5
) to obtain that

l=\dim_{R}M+\dim_{R}N\leq\dim N+\dim M\,,

which we reorganize as

\dim_{R}N-\dim N\leq\dim M-\dim_{R}M\,.

(9)

Now, plugging inequality (
8
) inside inequality (
9
) and combining with (
7
), we obtain that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\Delta(x_{i})\leq\dim M-\dim_{R}M\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta(x_{i})\,,

which is the desired bound.
∎

Combining this observation with Theorem
5.5
, we have managed to prove the following:

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a simply-connected and semi-simple algebraic group. Assume that for all but finitely many primes
p
and every non-zero element

, we have that

\left|\rk^{U_{1}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(x)-1\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)\,.

Then, for any finitely generated subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathsf{G}
, Conjecture
2.4
holds and Conjecture
2.5
virtually holds.

The case of
\mathsf{SL}_{2}

In the last section, we have shown in Corollary
5.8
how Conjectures
2.4
and
2.5
for
\mathsf{G}
can be (at least partially) obtained from a corresponding result on completed group algebras of the first congruence subgroup of
\mathsf{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

\mathsf{P}
)

Suppose now that all the irreducible factors of the root system

of
\mathsf{G}
are of type

, which is to say that
\mathsf{G}
is a quotient of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{SL}_{2}
by a finite central subgroup. Hence, to establish Conjecture
2.4
in this case it suffices to do so for

by the observations in Remark

.

Consider the first congruence subgroup

U_{1}=\ker\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\right)

of
\mathsf{G}
.
By Corollary
5.8
, we only need to show that

\left|\rk^{U_{1}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(x)-1\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{\min\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}}\right)

for almost all primes

. This is implied by a stronger theorem of W. Fu
[
Fu24
]
, whose argument we adapt below in the language of this paper for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
\Gamma
is any finitely generated subgroup of
\mathsf{G}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{SL}_{2}
, then Conjecture
2.4
holds and Conjecture
2.5
virtually holds.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\mathfrak{g}
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra of
U_{1}
. By Proposition
4.3
, we have an embedding
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]\subseteq\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
such that the
U_{1}
-module structure of
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
coincides with the one induced through this embedding (Lemma
4.5
).

For
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
, let
N
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
-module
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]/x\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]
and
M
be the
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
-module
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}/x\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
. It is immediately checked that
M\simeq N\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\![U_{1}]\!]}\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
as right
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
-modules.

In the language of
[
Fu24
]
,

is a
generic element
of
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}
(cf.
[
Fu24
, Thm. 5.1]
,
[
AW14
, Thm. 4.6 and Thm. 5.4]
). Hence,
[
Fu24
, Thm 3.5]
tells us that there exists a polynomial
f_{M}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of total degree

and of individual degree at most

in each variable such that

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\Hom_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}}(M,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})\leq f_{M}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

for every

Observe that, since the modules
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
are self-dual, i.e.

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\Hom_{\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}}(M,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})

\displaystyle 1-\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{U_{1}}(x)

5.8

7.
3-manifolds

Let
M
be an orientable hyperbolic
3
-manifold, i.e. a
3
-manifold admitting a complete Riemannian metric of constant negative curvature. We assume that its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)
is finitely generated.

Marden’s tameness theorem, proven independently by I. Agol in
[
Ago04
]
and D. Calegari and D. Gabai in
[
CG06
]
, says that
M
is homeomorphic to the interior of a compact
3
-manifold
\overline{M}
. In particular,
\pi_{1}(M)
is of type
F
over
\mathbb{C}
, and
M
can be decomposed into a compact core
C
that is homotopy equivalent to
M
(in fact,
C
is homeomorphic to
\overline{M}
) and a union of finitely many ends
E_{i}
, each one homeomorphic to a product
S_{i}\times(0,\infty)
, where
S_{i}
is a closed surface (see
[
Can08
, Sec. 5]
). Moreover, if
M
is of finite volume, then all the
S_{i}
are tori, which follows from the Thick-Thin decomposition of
M

[
Mar23
, Cor. 4.2.18]
. Observe that by the Poincaré duality of manifolds with boundary, if each
S_{i}
has genus
g_{i}
, then the Euler characteristic of
M
is
\chi(M)=\sum(1-g_{i})
.

We can identify
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
with a torsion-free subgroup of the group
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic
3
-space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. This group, in turn, is isomorphic to
\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\{\pm\operatorname{Id}\}
, which we know satisfies Conjecture
1.1
by Theorem
1.3
. Identifying
\bm{\lambda}=\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
, we note that the representations
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}=\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2}
of
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
that induce representations of
\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
are precisely those for which
-\operatorname{Id}
acts trivially, i.e. the representations where
\lambda
is even.

In
[
MFP12
]
, J. Porti and P. Menal-Ferrer calculated the dimensions of the homology groups
\HH_{i}(M,\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2})=\HH_{i}(\Gamma,\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proposition 7.1

(cf.
[
MFP12
, Cor. 3.6]
)
.

Let
M
a hyperbolic
3
-manifold with finitely generated fundamental group and whose ends contain exactly
k
toroidal cusps. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
even, we have that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{i}(M,\Sym^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i\in\{0,3\}\,,\\
k,&\text{if }i=2,\\
k-(\lambda+1)\chi(M),&\text{if }i=1\,.\end{cases}

Remark 7.2
.

There are lifts of
\Gamma
to subgroups of
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and, in
[
MFP12
]
, Porti and Menal-Ferrer obtained formulas for the dimension of
\HH_{i}(M,\Sym^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
for all
\lambda
, which for odd
\lambda
depends on how the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
subgroups attached to the toroidal cusps lift to
\mathsf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Moreover, in the case where
M
is closed, the vanishing of the cohomology groups
\HH_{i}(M,\Sym^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
follows from a theorem by M. Raghunathan
[
Rag65
]
.

The previous result together with Proposition
3.1
yields the following.

Corollary 7.3
.

For
M
as in Proposition
7.1
, we have that

b_{i}^{(2)}(M)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i\neq 1\,,\\
-\chi(M),&\text{if }i=1\,.\end{cases}

In
[
LL95
]
, J. Lott and W. Lück gave a general formula for the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of every compact
3
-manifold
M
. Using Marden’s tameness theorem to reduce the finitely generated to the compact case, one recovers the formula in Corollary
7.3
. By repeatedly applying the
\ell^{2}
-Mayer-Vietoris sequence, the Prime decomposition theorem reduces the general case to that of an irreducible
M
with infinite
\pi_{1}(M)
and the Loop theorem reduces to the case where the boundary of
M
is incompressible. Furthermore, by doubling
M
we may assume that it is closed. Then, the Geometrization theorem reduces the analysis even further to the cases where
M
is either Seifert fibered or hyperbolic.

Both cases are where the core of the calculation lies. That the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of Seifert fibered
3
-manifolds vanish can be seen, for instance, as a consequence of the existence of a virtual
S^{1}
-action
[
Lü02
, Thm. 1.40]
or of the virtual algebraic fibering of its fundamental group
[
Lü02
, Thm. 7.2(5)]
. The vanishing of
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers for closed hyperbolic
3
-manifolds is implied by a theorem of J. Dodziuk
[
Dod79
]
, and thus the trickiest part of Lott and Lück’s argument is the deduction of the finite volume hyperbolic case from the closed one (for more details, see
[
Lü02
, Sec. 4.2]
). This case is covered by Corollary
7.3
.

8.
Cusp forms for
\SL_{2}

The quantitative statement of Conjecture
1.2
has applications in the theory of automorphic forms for
\SL_{2}
. We will give here a brief account of the definitions of the theory of cusp forms necessary for stating our results, and we direct the reader to
[
Hid93
,
BC79
]
for details and further references. We mainly follow the exposition given in
[
Hid93
, Sec. 1]

Let
F
be a number field with
r_{1}+2r_{2}=(F\colon\mathbb{Q})
real and complex embeddings respectively, and let
I_{\mathbb{R}}=\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{r_{1}}\}
and
I_{\mathbb{C}}=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r_{2}}\}
be complete representative sets of real and complex embeddings, respectively, corresponding to the archimedean places of
K
. We define
I_{\infty}=I_{\mathbb{R}}\cup I_{\mathbb{C}}
and
I=I_{\infty}\cup\overline{I_{\mathbb{C}}}
. Consider the group

G=\SL_{2}(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})=\prod_{r_{1}}\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{r_{2}}\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\,.

Letting
\mathcal{O}_{F}
denote the ring of integers of
F
, we fix a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
that will be the
level
of our automorphic forms. Then, we get an embedding
\Gamma\to G
by taking the product of all maps induced by the
\tau_{i}
and the
\sigma_{j}
.

By quotienting out on the right the product
\prod_{r_{1}}\mathsf{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{r_{2}}\mathsf{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the standard maximal compact subgroups of
G
, we obtain the locally symmetric space

\mathbb{H}=\prod_{r_{1}}\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\prod_{r_{2}}\mathbb{H}^{3}

associated to
G
on which
\Gamma
acts. The quotient
M=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
is a
(2r_{1}+3r_{2})
-dimensional hyperbolic orbifold of finite volume, and it is a manifold if
\Gamma
is torsion-free. In this case, the Borel-Serre compactification attaches a boundary
\partial M
to the manifold
M=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
, and the resulting compact manifold has the same fundamental group
\Gamma
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{G}=\prod_{r_{1}}\fsl_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{r_{2}}\fsl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
the real Lie algebra of
G
and by
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{C}}=\prod_{r_{1}+2r_{2}}\fsl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
its complexification. The center of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a polynomial algebra in
r_{1}+2r_{2}
-generators called the
Casimir operators
. For each embedding
\delta\in I
, we obtain one Casimir operator
\Delta_{\delta}
given by the image under
\delta
of
\frac{1}{4}(EF+FE+H^{2}/2)
, where

E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

form the standard basis of
\fsl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Let
\bm{k}=(k_{\delta})_{\delta\in I_{\infty}}
be a
(r_{1}+r_{2})
-tuple of non-negative integers indexed by the archimedean places of
F
. Let
V_{\bm{k}}=\bigotimes_{\delta\in I_{\infty}}V_{\delta}
be the complex vector space given by the tensor product of
V_{\delta}=\mathbb{C}
for all the real embeddings
\delta
and
V_{\delta}=\operatorname{Sym}^{2k_{\delta}-2}\mathbb{C}
for all the complex embeddings
\delta
. It naturally has the structure of a right module over
\prod_{r_{1}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\prod_{r_{2}}\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
J
be a subset of
I_{\mathbb{R}}
. A
cusp form
of level
\Gamma
, type
J
and weight
\bm{k}
is a smooth function
f\colon\Gamma\backslash G\to V_{\bm{k}}
that satisfies the following properties:

(M1)

With the convention that
k_{\overline{\sigma}}=k_{\sigma}
, we have that
f
is an eigenfunction for all the Casimir operators
\Delta_{\delta}
with eigenvalue

\Delta_{\delta}f=\left(\frac{(k_{\delta}-2)^{2}}{2}+k_{\delta}-2\right)f\,.

(M2)

If we write
h\in\prod_{r_{1}}\mathsf{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{r_{2}}\mathsf{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
as

h=\left(\left(\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi\theta_{\tau})&\sin(2\pi\theta_{\tau})\\
-\sin(2\pi\theta_{\tau})&\cos(2\pi\theta_{\tau})\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\tau\in I_{\mathbb{R}}},(h_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in I_{\mathbb{C}}}\right)\,,

then

f(xh)=f(x)\left((e^{k_{\tau}\theta_{\tau}})_{\tau\in J},(e^{-k_{\tau}\theta_{\tau}})_{\tau\not\in J},(h_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in I_{\mathbb{C}}}\right)\,.

(M3)

For all
x\in G
, we have that

\int_{(\Gamma\cap U)\backslash U}f(ux)\,du=0\,,

where
U
is the product of all upper unitriangular subgroups of
G
and
du
is the Lebesgue measure on
U\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}
.

Remark 8.2
.

The usual notion of a cusp form is that of a scalar valued function
f^{0}\colon\Gamma\backslash G\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfies various finiteness and growth conditions
[
BC79
, p. 190]
. Such functions have a finite-dimensional span under the action of
\prod_{r_{1}}\mathsf{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{r_{2}}\mathsf{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on the right, and fixing a basis of this span gives us a vector valued cusp form according to our definition. Conversely, composing any vector valued cusp form with a linear functional gives us a scalar valued cusp form, see
[
Hid93
, Sec. 1.4]
.

We denote the space of all cusp forms of level
\Gamma
, type
J
and weight
\bm{k}
by
S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)
. If
J=I_{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote this space simply by
S_{\bm{k}}(\Gamma)
.
The current literature shows great interest in the asymptotic behaviour of the dimensions
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)
, either for a fixed weight
\bm{k}
and a residual chain of levels
\Gamma_{i}
(
[
Sav89
,
SX91
,
CE09
,
FGT10
]
), or for a fixed level
\Gamma
and increasing sequence of weights
\{\bm{k}_{i}\}
(
[
Shi63
,
FGT10
,
Mar12
,
Fu24
]
).

In this work, we are concerned with the latter case of growing weights for a fixed level. When
F
is totally real, the precise growth rate of
S_{\bm{k}}(\Gamma)
was obtained by H. Shimizu in
[
Shi63
]
as

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S_{\bm{k}}(\Gamma)\sim\prod_{\tau\in I}k_{\tau}\,.

When
F
is not totally real, the question of what the precise growth rate of
S_{\bm{k}}(\Gamma)
should be is very much open. Currently, the best general upper bound is the one given in
[
Fu24
]
:

Theorem 8.3

(
[
Fu24
, Thm 1.2]
)
.

If
F
is not totally real, then

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S_{\bm{k}}(\Gamma)=O\left(\frac{\prod_{\tau\in I_{\mathbb{R}}}k_{\tau}\times\prod_{\sigma\in I_{\mathbb{C}}}k_{\sigma}^{2}}{\min\{k_{\delta}\colon\delta\in I_{\infty}\}}\right)\,.

If
F
is imaginary quadratic, i.e. when
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup of a Bianchi group, this result combined with the lower bound from
[
FGT10
]
gives the precise growth rate.

Corollary 8.4

(
[
Fu24
, Cor 1.3]
)
.

If
F
is imaginary quadratic, then
\bm{k}=(k)
and

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S_{k}(\Gamma)\sim k\,.

Assume that
\Gamma
is torsion-free and that
k_{\delta}\geq 3
for each real or complex embedding
\delta
, and let
\lambda_{\delta}=k_{\delta}-2
with the convention that
k_{\delta}=k_{\overline{\delta}}
. If we set
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{\delta})
, then
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
becomes a
\Gamma
-module through the embedding
\Gamma\to\prod_{r_{1}+2r_{2}}\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
given by the product of all the embeddings
\Gamma\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
induced by the
\delta\in I
.

It is well known that the spaces
S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)
can be computed through the group cohomology of
\Gamma
, a result that goes by the names of the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism, the Matsushima-Murakami formula and, in the present formulation, is due to G. Harder. There is a natural procedure to attach to each cusp form
f\in S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)
a
\Gamma
-invariant
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
-valued closed differential
(r_{1}+2r_{2})
-form
\omega(f)
on
\mathbb{H}
, thus inducing a map
\omega\colon\bigoplus_{J\subseteq\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{r_{1}}\}}S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)\to\HH^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
. The reader may consult
[
Hid94
, Sec. 2.4]
for the details.

Let
\HH_{\text{cusp}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
denote the
cuspidal cohomology
of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
, i.e. the image of the induced map
\omega
. The precise statement of the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism that we need is as follows:

Theorem 8.5

(
[
Hid93
, Thm. 1.1]
,
[
Hid94
, Cor.2.2]
)
.

The map
\omega
yields an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{J\subseteq\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{r_{1}}\}}S_{\bm{k},J}(\Gamma)\simeq\HH_{\operatorname{cusp}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})\simeq\HH_{\operatorname{sq}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})\,,

where
\HH_{\operatorname{sq}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
denotes the subspace of ordinary cohomology represented by square-integrable forms.

Remark 8.6
.

We will also use that
\HH_{\text{cusp}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
is contained in the subspace of compactly supported cohomology
\HH_{\operatorname{c}}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
, which follows from
[
Bor81
, Cor. 5.5]
.

The proof of Theorem
8.3
applies
[
Fu24
, Thm. 3.5]
, which is the core result used in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, to the completed homology of
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
as constructed by M. Emerton in
[
Eme06
]
and combines this with a spectral sequence in the profinite homology of
U_{1}
to obtain the desired upper bound for
\HH_{*}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
. We propose a different proof of this result that avoids the use of completed cohomology entirely and depends solely on the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism from Theorem
8.5
and the results of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
8.3
.

Since we have that
\lambda_{\delta}=k_{\delta}-2
, for
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r_{1}+2r_{2}})
it suffices to show that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})=O\left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}\lambda_{i}}{\min\lambda_{i}}\right)\,.

Due to the fact that
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a compact manifold by the Borel-Serre construction, we conclude that
\Gamma
is of type
FP_{\infty}
over
\mathbb{C}
.

In order to apply Proposition
3.3
, we must first show that
\beta_{r_{1}+r_{2}}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=0
. If
r_{1}=0
and
r_{2}=1
, i.e. if
F
is imaginary quadratic, then
M
is a hyperbolic
3
-manifold of finite volume and we would be done by invoking Corollary
7.3
. However, in the general non-totally real case, we need something more.

By the proportionality principle of
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers (
[
Löh20
, Cor. 4.2.11]
,
[
Lü02
, Thm. 3.183, Cor. 7.37]
), the
(r_{1}+r_{2})
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of
\Gamma
and the
(r_{1}+r_{2})
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of
\mathbb{H}
are proportional to each other. Since the latter is a symmetric space of non-compact type and dimension
2r_{1}+3r_{2}
, its
(r_{1}+r_{2})
-th
\ell^{2}
-Betti number vanishes assuming that
r_{2}>0
(see
[
Lü02
, Thm. 5.12]
). The result now follows from Proposition
3.3
and the duality between the compactly supported cohomology group
\HH^{r_{1}+2r_{2}}_{\operatorname{c}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
and the homology group
\HH_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})
.
∎

Appendix A
Twisted von Neumann ranks

If
\Gamma
is a countable group, let
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
be the complex Hilbert space on an orthonormal basis given by the elements of
\Gamma
. Hence,
\Gamma
acts on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
on both sides as unitary operators. Any
r\times s
matrix
A
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
induces a bounded linear operator
\phi^{A}_{\Gamma}\colon\ell^{2}(\Gamma)^{s}\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma)^{r}
between Hilbert spaces by left multiplication. The
von Neumann rank
of
A
is defined as

\rk_{\Gamma}A=\dim_{\Gamma}\overline{\Image\phi^{A}_{\Gamma}}=s-\dim_{\Gamma}\Ker\phi^{A}_{\Gamma}\,,

where
\dim_{\Gamma}
is the
von Neumann dimension
of
\Gamma
(see
[
Lü02
, Def. 1.10]
). If
\Gamma
is not countable, then for every matrix
A
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
we define
\rk_{\Gamma}A
to be
\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}A
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is any finitely generated subgroup of
\Gamma
containing the elements that appear in the entries of
A
(this rank does not depend on
\Gamma^{\prime}
, see
[
Lü02
, Thm. 6.29]
).

We recall that if
\Gamma
is finite, then
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, a Hilbert
\Gamma
-module
V
is simply a
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module and we have that

\dim_{\Gamma}V=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V}{|\Gamma|}\quad\text{and}\quad\rk_{\Gamma}A=\frac{\rk_{\mathbb{C}}A}{|\Gamma|}\,.

The following result is a particular case of the
sofic Lück approximation theorem
.

Theorem A.1

(
[
Lü94
,
Jai19a
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}\geq\Gamma_{2}\geq\cdots
be a chain of normal subgroups of
\Gamma
of finite index with
\bigcap\Gamma_{i}=\{1\}
. For each matrix
A
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, let
A_{i}
denote its image in
\Mat(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma/\Gamma_{i}])
. We have that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\rk_{\Gamma/\Gamma_{i}}A_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\rk_{\mathbb{C}}A_{i}}{|\Gamma:\Gamma_{i}|}=\rk_{\Gamma}A\,.

There is a natural generalization of the von Neumann rank of finite groups to arbitrary fields. Suppose
Q
is a finite group. Then, for any field
L
and any matrix
A\in\Mat(L[Q])
, we define

\rk_{Q}^{L}A=\frac{\rk_{L}A}{|Q|}\,.

Observe that
\rk_{Q}^{\mathbb{C}}
is simply the von Neumann rank
\rk_{Q}
. For any field extension
L\leq K
, we have that
\rk_{Q}^{L}A=\rk_{Q}^{K}A
for every matrix
A\in\Mat(L[Q])
. If
R
is an integral domain with field of fractions
K
and
A\in\Mat(R[Q])
, we define
\rk_{Q}^{R}A=\rk_{Q}^{K}A
.

Let
Z
be a finite central subgroup of
\Gamma
. For any character
\chi\colon Z\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we consider the trace
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
on
\Gamma
given by

\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(g)=\begin{cases}\chi(g),&\text{if }g\in Z\,,\\
0,&\text{if }g\not\in Z\,.\end{cases}

Linearly extending the formula to
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
turns it into a tracial
*
-algebra, to which we will associate a Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
and a cyclic representation
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\to\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})
into its algebra of bounded operators. Recall that a trace
\operatorname{tr}
is
faithful
when
\operatorname{tr}(x^{*}x)=0
if and only if
x=0
. The set

N=\ker(\operatorname{tr})=\{x\in\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\colon\operatorname{tr}(x^{*}x)=0\}

is a two-sided ideal of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
and
\operatorname{tr}
always induces a faithful trace on
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]/N
. We will make use of the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal (or GNS for short) construction, given by the following proposition.

Proposition A.2

(
[
NS06
, cf. Prop. 7.2 and 7.6]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
*
-algebra with a faithful trace
\operatorname{tr}
. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is generated by unitary elements, i.e. elements
x\in\mathcal{A}
such that
x^{*}x=xx^{*}=1
. Then, up to unitary equivalence, there exist a unique Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
with a cyclic generating vector
e\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\langle\rho(x)(e),e\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(x)

for every
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

In the previous proposition, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is given by the Hilbert completion of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the inner product

\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(y^{*}x)\,,

the cyclic generating vector
e
is the group identity element
1
, and the representations are the extension of either the right or left action of
\mathcal{A}
on itself (observe that the liftings of both actions to
\mathcal{H}
commute). Before continuing with this construction, we list some properties of the traces
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\Gamma
,
Z
,
\chi
and
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}
be as before.

(1)

The kernel
N
of the trace
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}
is the two-sided ideal of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
generated by the central elements
\{z-\chi(z):z\in Z\}
.

(2)

Let
T
be a transversal for
\Gamma/Z
. Then, the image
T+N
of
T
in the quotient

\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}=\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]/N

is a basis consisting of unitary elements.

Proof.

Fix a transversal
T
for
\Gamma/Z
. It is clear that the image of
T
is a basis for

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]/\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\{z-\chi(z):z\in Z\}\,.

We want to prove that
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
. Observe that
N
contains every element of the form
z-\chi(z)
with
z\in Z
, and the surjective map
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
factors through
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
x=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z\in Z}a_{tz}tz
be an element in
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
and
\overline{x}=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z\in Z}a_{tz}t\chi(z)
be its image in
\mathcal{A}
. Since
T
is a basis of
\mathcal{A}
, we get that
\overline{x}
is zero if and only if

\sum_{t\in T}\left\|\sum_{z\in Z}a_{tz}\chi(z)\right\|^{2}=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z,w\in Z}\overline{a_{tw}}a_{tz}\chi(w^{-1}z)=0\,.

Writing
x^{*}=\sum_{s\in T}\sum_{w\in Z}\overline{a_{sw}}s^{-1}w^{-1}
, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(x^{*}x)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}\left(\sum_{t,s\in T}\sum_{z,w\in S}\overline{a_{sw}}a_{tz}s^{-1}tw^{-1}z\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}\left(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z,w\in Z}\overline{a_{tw}}a_{tz}w^{-1}z\right)\text{, since }\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(s^{-1}tw^{-1}z)\neq 0\text{ iff }s=t\,,

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z,w\in Z}\overline{a_{tw}}a_{tz}\chi(w^{-1}z)\,.

Hence,
\overline{x}=0
if and only if
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(x^{*}x)=0
, showing that the surjection
\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
is an isomorphism, and thus proving (1). For (2), it suffices to observe that the elements
t+N
for
t\in T
are unitary, which is immediate since
(t+N)^{*}=t^{-1}+N
.
∎

Hence, by Proposition
A.2
we can associate to
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
two representations into the bounded operators of a Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
, which is unique up to unitary equivalence. Let
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
be the weak closure of the left representation of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})
. This is a von Neumann algebra, i.e. a weak closed subset of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We call
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
the
twisted
\ell^{2}
-completion
of
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
and
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
the
twisted group von Neumann algebra
.

Observe that the trace
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}
extends to
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
by setting

\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(A)=\langle A(1),1\rangle\,

for every operator
A\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
. Any closed left
\Gamma
-invariant subspace
M\leq\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
comes with an orthogonal projection
P_{M}\colon\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}\to M
, which is the unique element
P_{M}\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
such that
P_{M}^{2}=P_{M}=P_{M}^{*}
and
\Image(P_{M})=M
. We can thus define the
twisted von Neumann dimension
of
M
to be

\dim_{\Gamma}^{\chi}M=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(P_{M})\,.

For a fixed
A\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
, we define the
twisted von Neumann rank
of
A
to be

\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}A=\dim_{\Gamma}^{\chi}\overline{\Image(A)}=1-\dim_{\Gamma}^{\chi}\ker(A)\,.

Note that the twisted von Neumann dimension extends naturally to closed subspaces
M
of
(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})^{n}
by setting

\dim_{\Gamma}^{\chi}M=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(P_{M})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle P_{M}(e_{i}),e_{i}\rangle,

where
P_{M}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi})
is the unique element such that
P_{M}^{2}=P_{M}=P_{M}^{*}
and
\Image(P_{M})=M
, and each
e_{i}
is the element of
(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})^{n}
containing
1
in the
i
-th coordinate and zero otherwise. Hence, the twisted von Neumann rank is also defined for bounded operators
(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})^{n}\to(\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi})^{m}
for all
n\,,m>0
.

If
\Gamma
is not countable, we can still define the twisted von Neumann rank
\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma}^{\chi}A
for matrices
A\in\Mat(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])
. Indeed, let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be any countable subgroup of
\Gamma
such that
A\in\Mat(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}])
,
Z^{\prime}=Z\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\prime}
be the restriction of
\chi
to
Z^{\prime}
. Then, we define
\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma}^{\chi}A
to be
\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}A
. The following lemma implies that
\operatorname{rk}_{\Gamma}^{\chi}A
is indeed well defined and does not depend on
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
,
Z^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\prime}
be as above. Fix a subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\leq\Gamma^{\prime}
and let
Z^{\prime\prime}=Z^{\prime}\cap\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
and
\chi^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
\chi^{\prime}
to
Z^{\prime\prime}
. The inclusion
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
induces a functor
I
from the category of closed
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}
-submodules of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}^{n}
to the category of closed
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
-submodules of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{n}
, for any
n
. Moreover, we have that

\dim_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime\prime}}M=\dim_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}I(M)\,.

Proof.

First, observe that the Hilbert space completion of the natural pre-Hilbert structure on

\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}}\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}\simeq_{\mathbb{C}}\bigoplus_{\Gamma^{\prime}/(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}Z^{\prime})}\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}

is
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
-isometrically isomorphic to
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
. Indeed, if
T^{\prime\prime}
is a transversal for
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}/Z^{\prime\prime}
and
X
is a transversal for
\Gamma^{\prime}/(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}Z^{\prime})
, then
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime\prime}X
is a transversal for
\Gamma^{\prime}/Z^{\prime}
and the map
\bigoplus_{\Gamma^{\prime}/(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}Z^{\prime})}\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}\to\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
given by
(\alpha_{x})_{x\in X}\mapsto\sum_{x\in X}\alpha_{x}x
is injective,
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
-equivariant and has dense image. Hence, if
M
is any closed
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}
-submodule of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}^{n}
, the Hilbert completion
I(M)
of
M\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime\prime}]}\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]
is a closed
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
-submodule of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{n}
.

In particular,
I
also induces a homomorphism of
*
-algebras
I\colon\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}\to\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
. We will now show that, for any
A\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}
, we have that

\operatorname{tr}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}(A)=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi^{\prime}}(I(A))\,.

By continuity and additivity, it suffices to verify this for
A=g\in\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
, for which this follows immediately from the definition of the action of
I(g)
on
\bigoplus_{\Gamma^{\prime}/(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}Z^{\prime})}\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
and hence on its Hilbert completion.

Now, for
M\leq\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}^{n}
we have that
\dim_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime\prime}}M=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}(P_{M})
, where
P_{M}\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})_{\chi^{\prime\prime}})
is as before. Since
I(P_{M})
satisfies that
I(P_{M})^{2}=I(P_{M})=I(P_{M})^{*}
and
\Image(I(P_{M}))=I(M)
, we must have that

\dim_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}I(M)=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi^{\prime}}I(P_{M})=\operatorname{tr}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}P_{M}=\dim_{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime\prime}}M\,.\qed

Remark A.5
.

If
Z=\{1\}
, then
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}
is simply the characteristic function
\operatorname{tr}_{\chi}(g)=\delta_{1,g}
and
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}\simeq\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
. Hence, the twisted von Neumann rank
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}
coincides with the classical von Neumann rank
\rk_{\Gamma}
. Even if
Z\neq\{1\}
, if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
that intersects
Z
trivially and
A
is a matrix over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]
, we have that
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}A=\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}A
by Lemma
A.4
. In particular, this is the case if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is torsion-free.

When
\Gamma
has torsion central elements, then distinct central characters lead to distinct rank functions. This is why the hypothesis on the central characters is essential to the statement of Conjecture
1.1
. As an example, consider
\Gamma
the cyclic group of order
2
inside
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
generated by
g=-\operatorname{Id}
and the element
g-1
inside
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
. Then, for the trivial character
\bm{1}(g)=1
, we have that
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\bm{1}}(g-1)=0
, while for the non-trivial character
\bm{-1}(g)=-1
we have that
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\bm{-1}}(g-1)=1
. This reflects on the cohomology of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2}
by the appearance of two clearly distinct converging subsequences

\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\HH^{0}(\Gamma,\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2})}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\lambda\text{ is even}\,,\\
0,&\text{if }\lambda\text{ is odd}\,,\end{cases}

where the parity of
\lambda
determines whether
\Gamma
acts on
\Sym^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{2}
through
\bm{1}
or
\bm{-1}
.

We recall that a von Neumann algebra whose center is exactly
\mathbb{C}
is called a
factor
. It is a well known fact that the group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is a factor if and only if
\Gamma
is an ICC group, i.e. every non-trivial conjugacy class is infinite. In particular,
\Gamma/Z
is ICC if and only if
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{1}
is a factor. We show that one direction still holds for all the twisted group von Neumann algebras.

Lemma A.6
.

Fix
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(Z)
. If
\Gamma/Z
is ICC then
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
is a factor.

Proof.

After modding out
\Gamma
by the kernel of
\chi
, we may assume that
\chi
is injective. We will construct a special transversal for
\Gamma/Z
. First, let
T_{0}
be a set of representatives in
\Gamma
for the conjugacy classes of
\Gamma/Z
. For any
t\in T_{0}
, let
S_{t}
be a set of left coset representatives of the stabilizer

\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(tZ)=\{h\in\Gamma:hth^{-1}=tz\text{ for some }z\in Z\}

of the coset
tZ
under the conjugation action of
\Gamma
. We assume that
1\in S_{t}
for every
t\in T_{0}
. Observe that the centralizer
C_{\Gamma}(t)
of
t
in
\Gamma
is contained in
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(tZ)
. Then, the set

T=\{sts^{-1}:t\in T_{0}\,,s\in S_{t}\}

is a transversal for
\Gamma/Z
containing
1
.

Fix an element
A\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
. When we restrict
A
to
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
, it acts as left multiplication by
x=A(1)\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
. Hence, it suffices to show that the only central elements in
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
are the constant multiplies of the identity.

Write a fixed element
x\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
uniquely as

x=\sum_{t\in T_{0}}\sum_{s\in S_{t}}a_{sts^{-1}}sts^{-1}

with
a_{sts^{-1}}\in\mathbb{C}
. If
h
is any element of
\Gamma
, then for every element
t\in T_{0}
and
s\in S_{t}
there exists unique elements
r=r(h,t,s)\in S_{t}
and
u=u(h,t,s)\in Z
such that
hsts^{-1}h^{-1}=rtr^{-1}u
. If for some
h,t,s
the element
u
is non-trivial, then
r^{-1}hs
is an element of
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(tZ)
that does not belong to
C_{\Gamma}(t)
. We will show that if
x
is central then
a_{sts^{-1}}=0
for
t\neq 1
arbitrary. Fixing such
t
, there are two cases to consider:

Case 1:
There exists an element
h\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(tZ)\smallsetminus C_{\Gamma}(t)
.

In this case, we have that
hth^{-1}=tz
for some non-trivial
z\in Z
. This also implies that for any
\widetilde{s}\in S_{t}
the element
\widetilde{s}h\widetilde{s}^{-1}
belongs to
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}Z)\smallsetminus C_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1})
. Moreover, if for some
s\in S_{t}
we have that

\widetilde{s}h\widetilde{s}^{-1}sts^{-1}\widetilde{s}h^{-1}\widetilde{s}^{-1}=\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}z^{\prime}

for some
z^{\prime}\in Z
, then
hs^{-1}\widetilde{s}\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(tZ)
, from which we obtain that
s=\widetilde{s}
.
We now compute

\displaystyle\widetilde{s}h\widetilde{s}^{-1}x\widetilde{s}h^{-1}\widetilde{s}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S_{t}}a_{sts^{-1}}\widetilde{s}h\widetilde{s}^{-1}sts^{-1}\widetilde{s}h^{-1}\widetilde{s}^{-1}+\sum_{t^{\prime}\neq t\in T_{0}}\cdots

\displaystyle=a_{\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}}\chi(z)\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}+\sum_{s\neq\widetilde{s}\in S_{t}}a_{sts^{-1}}\widetilde{r}t\widetilde{r}^{-1}\widetilde{u}+\sum_{t^{\prime}\neq t\in T_{0}}\cdots.

Thus, if
\widetilde{s}h\widetilde{s}^{-1}x\widetilde{s}h^{-1}\widetilde{s}^{-1}=x
, we have that
a_{\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}}\chi(z)=a_{\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}}
, which is only possible if
a_{\widetilde{s}t\widetilde{s}^{-1}}=0
. Since the choice of
\widetilde{s}
was arbitrary, we are done.

Case 2:
For every
h\in\Gamma
and every
s\in S_{t}
there is a unique
r=r(h,s)\in S_{t}
such that
hsts^{-1}h=rtr^{-1}
.

In particular, for
h
fixed, the map
s\mapsto r(h,s)
is a bijection in
S_{t}
. Take any
h\in\Gamma
and consider the element

\displaystyle hxh^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S_{t}}a_{sts^{-1}}hsts^{-1}h^{-1}+\sum_{t^{\prime}\neq t\in T_{0}}\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S_{t}}a_{h^{-1}sts^{-1}h}sts^{-1}+\sum_{t^{\prime}\neq t\in T_{0}}\cdots.

If
hxh^{-1}=x
, then by our choice of
T_{0}
, for every
s\in S_{t}
we must have that
a_{sts^{-1}}=a_{h^{-1}sts^{-1}h}
. If
x
is central, since the conjugacy class of
sts^{-1}
is infinite in
\Gamma
for
t\neq 1
, we must have infinitely many repeated coefficients
a_{h^{-1}sts^{-1}h}
appearing in the expansion of
x
, which is only possible if
a_{sts^{-1}}=0
for all
s
, due to the fact that the sum

\sum_{t\in T_{0}}\sum_{s\in S_{t}}|a_{sts^{-1}}|^{2}

is finite by definition.
∎

Remark A.7
.

The “only if” direction of the previous lemma is false in the twisted setting. For example, if

\Gamma=Q_{8}=\langle e,i,j,k\mid e^{2}=1\,,\,i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=ijk=e\rangle

\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}

\mathbb{C}
-algebra. The finite groups
\Gamma
for which there exists a central character

. The authors are currently unaware of an infinite group
\Gamma
containing a finite central subgroup

and a central character

such that
\Gamma/Z
is not ICC but
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}

We recall that a subset
S
of a ring

satisfies the

if it satisfies:

(1)

for all
r\in R
and
s\in S
, there exists
r^{\prime}\in R
and
s^{\prime}\in S
such that
rs^{\prime}=sr^{\prime}
; and

(2)

for all

and
s\in S
such that

, there exists
s^{\prime}\in S

.

If

called the

of

sending the elements of

In the case of the twisted von Neumann algebra

Appendix B
Crossed products and
*
-regular closures

We recall that a ring
R^{\prime}
is called a
crossed product
of a ring
R
and a group
G
if
R
can be identified with a subring of
R^{\prime}
and there is an injective function
\mu\colon G\to(R^{\prime})^{\times}
satisfying the following properties:

(CrPr1)

R^{\prime}
is a free left
R
-module on the set
\mu(G)
;

(CrPr2)

for all
g\in G
,
R
is closed under conjugation by
\mu(g)
; and

(CrPr3)

for every pair of elements
g
and
g^{\prime}
in
G
, the element

\tau(g,g^{\prime})=\mu(g)\mu(g^{\prime})\mu(gg^{\prime})^{-1}

belongs to
R^{\times}
.

In particular,
\mu
induces a function
\overline{\mu}\colon G\to\Aut(R)
that satisfies the following:

(CrPr1’)

for all
g
,
g^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime\prime}
in
G
, we have that

\tau(g,g^{\prime})\tau(gg^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})=\overline{\mu}(g)(\tau(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}))\tau(g,g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime})

in
R^{\times}
; and

(CrPr2’)

for all
g
and
g^{\prime}
in
G
and
r\in R
, we have that

\tau(g,g^{\prime})\overline{\mu}(gg^{\prime})(r)\tau(g,g^{\prime})^{-1}=\overline{\mu}(g)(\overline{\mu}(g^{\prime})(r))

in
R
.

Conversely, given
\overline{\mu}\colon G\to\Aut(R)
and
\tau\colon G\times G\to R^{\times}
satisfying (CrPr1’) and (CrPR2’), we can construct a ring
R*G
with underlying set the free
R
-module with basis
G
and multiplication obtained by linearly extending

(rg)\cdot(r^{\prime}g^{\prime})=\big(r\mu(g)(r^{\prime})\tau(g,g^{\prime})\big)gg^{\prime}\,

for
g,g^{\prime}\in G
and
r,r^{\prime}\in R
. If
R^{\prime}
is a crossed product of
R
and
G
, then there is an isomorphism of rings
R^{\prime}\simeq R*G
that restricts to the identity map on
R
, where
R*G
is constructed using the induced maps from
R^{\prime}
. If
\psi\colon R^{\prime}\to\bigoplus_{g\in G}R\mu(g)
is an
R
-module isomorphism, we say that
(\psi,\mu,\tau)
is an
(internal) twisting data
. A pair
(\overline{\mu},\tau)
is an
(external) twisting data
. The considerations above show that there exists a bijection between internal and external twisting data, and any twisting data determines a crossed product up to an
R
-ring isomorphism.

The crossed product satisfies a universal property: if
(\overline{\mu},\tau)
is a twisting data,
\varphi\colon R\to R^{\prime}
is any ring homomorphism and
\nu\colon G\to(R^{\prime})^{\times}
is any function such that (1)
\nu(g)\varphi(r)\nu(g)^{-1}=\varphi(\overline{\mu}(g)r)
and (2)
\varphi(\tau(g,g^{\prime}))=\nu(g)\nu(g^{\prime})\nu(gg^{\prime})^{-1}
, then there exists a unique ring homomorphism
\theta\colon R*G\to R^{\prime}
extending both
\varphi
and
\nu
.

Suppose now
R
is contained in a larger ring
R^{\prime}
and
(\overline{\mu},\tau)
is a twisting data on
R
. If there exists a function
\overline{\mu^{\prime}}\colon G\to\Aut(R^{\prime})
such that (a) for every
g\in G
and
r\in R
we have that
\mu^{\prime}(g)(r)=\mu(g)(r)
and (b) the pair
(\overline{\mu^{\prime}},\tau)
satisfies (CrPr2’) on
R^{\prime}
, then this pair also satisfies (CrPr1’) and we get an injective map
R*G\to R^{\prime}*G
extending the inclusion
R\to R^{\prime}
and the identity
G\to G
, where the crossed products are built using their respective data. In this case, we say that the crossed product structure on
R

extends
to a crossed product structure on
R^{\prime}
.

We will now explore how the rings
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
,
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
and
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
decompose as crossed products over finite quotients of
\Gamma
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a finite index normal subgroup of
\Gamma
and, if
\chi\colon Z=Z(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a central character, we denote by
Z^{\prime}=Z\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\prime}\colon Z^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
the induced character. By taking
\Gamma_{\chi}=\Gamma/Z
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}=\Gamma^{\prime}/Z^{\prime}
, observe that we have an embedding
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}\leq\Gamma_{\chi}
, and the quotient
\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma/(\Gamma^{\prime}Z)
.

Lemma B.1
.

The following are true:

(1)

The conjugation action of
\Gamma
on
\Gamma^{\prime}
induces a twisting data
\overline{\mu}\colon\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}\to\Aut(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}})
and
\tau\colon(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})^{2}\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\times}
, and
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}\simeq\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})
.

(2)

The crossed product structure on
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
extends to a crossed product structure on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
.

(3)

The subring of
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
generated by
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
and
\Gamma
is isomorphic to the crossed product
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma_{\chi^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

(1) Fix a set of representatives
T^{\prime}
for
\Gamma^{\prime}/Z^{\prime}
and
X
for
\Gamma/(\Gamma^{\prime}Z)
, so that
T=T^{\prime}X
is a transversal for
\Gamma/Z
and the projection
\Gamma\to\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}
is bijective when restricted to
X
. For each

be its representative in

. For any pair

and
g^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma_{\chi^{\prime}}

\Gamma^{\prime}/Z^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
(\Gamma^{\prime}Z)/Z

and

.

\overline{\mu}\in\Aut(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}})
be the conjugation by

\tau(g,g^{\prime})=\chi(z)h
where

and

are defined as above. A direct computation shows that

satisfies (CrPr1’) and (CrPr2’) and thus we can construct the crossed product
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})

\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi^{\prime}}
and the function
\nu\colon\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}^{\times}
that sends

induce, by the universal property of the crossed product, a ring homomorphism
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})\to\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi}
which is readily seen to be bijective being an isomorphism of left
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
-modules by Lemma
A.3
(2).

(2) Since the image of
x_{g}
in both
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
and
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
consists of units that normalize
\Gamma^{\prime}
, one only needs to show that

also satisfies (CrPr2’) on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
respectively. For
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
it suffices to show that the actions of both sides of (CrPr2’), seen as operators of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
, coincide on
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
. In fact, it suffices to show that they send the identity element to the same element, which follows from any

acting on
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
as multiplication by some element in
\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
. For
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
, observe that both sides of (CrPr2’) define automorphisms of
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
that coincide on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
, so they must be the same.

(3) This subring coincides with the image of the induced map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
, so it suffices to prove that this map is injective, that is, that the set
X
is linearly independent over
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
as operators on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
. This is immediate from the identification of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
with the Hilbert space
\bigoplus_{x\in X}\ell^{2}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}x
.
∎

We recall that in a
*
-regular ring
R^{\prime}
, for every element
r\in R^{\prime}
there are unique projections
e
and
f
such that
R^{\prime}e=R^{\prime}r
and
fR^{\prime}=rR^{\prime}

[
Jai19a
, Prop. 3.2(3)]
. Moreover, there exists a unique element
r^{[-1]}
, called the relative inverse of
r
, such that
r^{[-1]}r=e
and
rr^{[-1]}=f

[
Jai19a
, Prop. 3.2(4)]
.

If
R
is a subring of a
*
-regular ring
R^{\prime}
, then there exists a unique minimal
*
-regular subring
\mathcal{R}(R,R^{\prime})
of
R^{\prime}
containing
R
, called the
*
-regular closure
of
R
in
R^{\prime}

[
Jai19a
, Sec. 3.4]
. This ring is obtained inductively by setting
\mathcal{R}_{0}(R,R^{\prime})=R
and taking
\mathcal{R}_{i+1}(R,R^{\prime})
to be the subring generated by
\mathcal{R}_{i}(R,R^{\prime})
and the relative inverses of all the elements
rr^{*}
for
r\in\mathcal{R}_{i}(R,R^{\prime})
, see
[
AG17
, Prop. 6.2]
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
L
be a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
containing
L_{0}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a finite index normal subgroup of
\Gamma
. Then, we also get a decomposition
L[\Gamma]_{\chi}\simeq L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})
, the crossed product structure on
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
extends to

\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{L}=\mathcal{R}(L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}},\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})

and the subring of
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}
generated by
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{L}
and
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{L}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

The decomposition for
L[\Gamma]_{\chi}
is proven in the same way as for
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]_{\chi^{\prime}}
in Lemma
B.1
(1).

Assume now that we have already extended the twisting data for
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}}
to
\mathcal{R}_{i}(L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}},\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})
. To show that it extends to
\mathcal{R}_{i+1}(L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}},\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})
, all we must show is that this ring is invariant under conjugation by
x\in X
, where
X
is the chosen transversal of
\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}
for the twisting data, since it will inherit (CrPr2’) from the crossed product decomposition of
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
.

Hence, let
r\in\mathcal{R}_{i}(L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}},\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})
be any self-adjoint element and
e,f\in\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
be projections such that
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}r=\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}e
and
r\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}=f\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
. Then, for any
x\in X
, we have that
xex^{-1}
and
xfx^{-1}
must be the unique projections such that

\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}(xrx^{-1})=x(\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}r)x^{-1}=x(\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}e)x^{-1}=\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}(xex^{-1})\,,

(xrx^{-1})\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}=x(r\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})x^{-1}=x(f\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})x^{-1}=(xfx^{-1})\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}\,.

This shows that
xr^{[-1]}x^{-1}
must be the relative inverse of
xrx^{-1}
. Hence, we have that

xr^{[-1]}x^{-1}=(xrx^{-1})^{[-1]}\in\mathcal{R}_{i+1}(L[\Gamma^{\prime}]_{\chi^{\prime}},\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}})

as desired.

From the universal property of crossed products, we get a ring homomorphism
\theta\colon\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{L}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma_{\chi^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
which is the restriction of the injective map
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
, proving the last claim.
∎

Remark B.3
.

Observe that if
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
is a factor, then by Lemma
A.8
(e) the center of
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}
is contained in the center of
\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)_{\chi}
, and is therefore a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
.

We are now able to describe how we are going to make use of Lemma
A.6
. Conjecture
2.4
states that for any finitely generated subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathsf{G}
, for every sequence
\{\mathsf{W}_{k}\}
of representations of
\mathsf{G}
with the same central character
\chi\colon\mathsf{Z}(\mathsf{G})\cap\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that their highest weight parameters
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
(
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}(k)
) grow to infinity, and for every finitely generated subfield
L
of
\mathbb{C}
containing
L_{0}
, we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rk_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}^{\Gamma}=\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}

as rank functions. Indeed, for each matrix
A
over
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
, it suffices to take
L
the subfield generated by the coefficients of those entries, as we have that
\rk^{\mathsf{G}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{C}}}A=\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}A
. We may further assume that
\Gamma
is Zariski-dense, so that
Z
coincides with the center of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma/Z
is ICC.

Suppose now that we can find a normal subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
of finite index such that, if
\chi^{\prime}
denotes the restriction of
\chi
to
Z\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
, we have the convergence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rk^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}=\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}

of Sylvester matrix rank functions on
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]
. We will show that this already implies the desired convergence for
\Gamma
over
L
.

Theorem B.4
.

Let
\Gamma
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
,
\chi
,
\chi^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}
be as above. If
\rk^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
converges to
\rk_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\chi^{\prime}}
, then
\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
converges to
\rk_{\Gamma}^{\chi}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
, and consider the ultraproduct of tracial
*
-regular rings

\mathcal{D}=\prod_{\omega}\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}})

as described in
[
Jai19a
, Sec. 6.7]
. The representations of
\Gamma^{\prime}
induce a homomorphism
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{D}
whose
*
-regular closure we denote by
\mathcal{S}
. The fact that

\rk^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{\chi^{\prime}}=\lim_{\omega}\rk^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}

as rank functions over
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]
implies by
[
Jai19a
, Thm. 6.3]
that we have a rank preserving isomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})\to\mathcal{S}
that commutes with the maps
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})
and
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{S}
.

The representations of
\Gamma
also induce a homomorphism
L[\Gamma]\to\mathcal{D}
that clearly extends the map
L[\Gamma^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{D}
. By the universal property of the crossed product, we obtain a ring homomorphism
\theta\colon\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}\simeq\mathcal{R}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}^{L}*(\Gamma_{\chi}/\Gamma^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{D}
that extends
\varphi
. Since
\Gamma
is Zariski-dense,
\Gamma/Z
is ICC and thus, by Lemma
A.6
and Remark
B.3
, the center of
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
.

It was shown in
[
Goo79
, Thm. 19.13, 19.14]
(cf.
[
Jai19a
, Prop. 5.7]
) that
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}
admits at most one Sylvester matrix rank function. Since both
\rk^{\Gamma}_{\chi}
and
\lim_{\omega}\rk^{\Gamma}_{\mathsf{W}_{\bm{\lambda}}^{L}}
define Sylvester matrix rank functions on
\mathcal{R}(\Gamma)_{\chi}^{L}
, they must also coincide. The result for general limits then follows, as the choice of
\omega
was arbitrary.
∎

Corollary B.5
.

Conjecture

Compact almost automorphic dynamics of non-autonomous differential equations with exponential dichotomy and applications to biological models with delay
Alan Chávez, Nelson Aragonés, Manuel Pinto, Ulices Zavaleta

Abstract
In the present work, we prove that, if A ⁡ ( ⋅ ) A(\cdot) is a compact almost automorphic matrix and the system x’(t) = A(t)x(t) , possesses an exponential dichotomy with Green function G ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) G(\cdot,\cdot) , then its associated system y’(t) = B(t)y(t) , where B ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ∈ H ⁡ ( A ) B(\cdot)\in H(A) (the hull of A ⁡ ( ⋅ ) A(\cdot) ) also possesses an exponential dichotomy. Moreover, the Green function G ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) G(\cdot,\cdot) is compact Bi-almost automorphic in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , this implies that G ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) G(\cdot,\cdot) is Δ 2 \Delta_{2} - like uniformly continuous, where Δ 2 \Delta_{2} is the principal diagonal of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , an important ingredient in the proof of invariance of the compact almost automorphic function space under convolution product with kernel G ⁡ ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) G(\cdot,\cdot) . Finally, we study the existence of a positive compact almost automorphic solution of non-autonomous differential equations of biological interest having non-linear harvesting terms and mixed delays.

1
Introduction

Let us consider the non-autonomous differential equation

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)+f(t)\,,

(1)

and suppose that the associate system

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t)x(t)

(2)

possesses an exponential dichotomy, then the unique bounded solution to equation (
1
) is

x(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t,s)f(s)ds\,,

(3)

where
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Green function associated to (
2
). If
A(\cdot)
is an almost
automorphic matrix and
f
is an almost automorphic function, then the solution
x
is almost automorphic if
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is
Bi-almost automorphic. To prove that
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is Bi-almost automorphic is not trivial. For example, in infinite dimension, in order to do that, it is important to add as hypothesis that the resolvent operator also be almost automorphic, moreover it is necessary a delicate analysis of the behaviour of the Yosida approximations of the evolution family
A(\cdot)
, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
14
]
. In contrasts, in the finite dimensional case, A. Coronel and co-authors have reported in
[
9
]
the notion of integrable Bi-almost automorphy and proved that
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is integrable Bi-almost automorphy if a projection matrix
P
commutes with the fundamental matrix solution of (
2
).

The Bi-almost periodicity/automorphy notion of a continuous function of two variables, goes back at least to the work of T. J. Xiao et. al.
[
19
]
. This notion is very useful in the study of almost periodic/automorphic solutions of non-autonomous differential equations (see the works
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
16
]
and references cited therein) and also of integral equations with kernel
\Gamma(t,s)
depending on two variables, see for instance
[
7
]
.

The main purpose of the present work is to prove, in finite dimension, that if
A(\cdot)
is a compact almost automorphic matrix, and
the system (
2
) possesses an
(\alpha,K,P)
-exponential dichotomy (Definition
2.3
), then the system

y^{\prime}(t)=B(t)y(t)\,,

(4)

possesses an
(\alpha,K,P_{0})
-exponential dichotomy, where
B(\cdot)\in H(A)
and
H(A)
stands for the hull of
A(\cdot)
(see comments after Definition
2.1
), this is the analogous result to the case of almost periodic systems, see
(
12
, Theorem 7.6)
. We also prove, without the techniques and the machinary of the infinite dimensional case, that the associated Green function
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is compact Bi-almost automorphic (Theorem
3.1
in section 3).
Notice that, since
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is discontinuous in the principal diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, thus continuous almost everywhere, then it is measurable and therefore
G(\cdot,\cdot)
does not fall into the notion of Bi-almost periodicity/automorphy introduced in
[
19
]
which is given for continuous functions. This motivates the introduction, in this work, of the notion of (compact) Bi-almost automorphy for measurable functions. Also, we are able to prove that, although
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is not uniformly continuous (Proposition
2.1
), it is
\Delta_{2}
-like uniformly continuous
(Lemma
3.3
), where
\Delta_{2}:=\{(t,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the principal diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Also we study the existence of a positive compact almost automorphic solution of the following biological model with non-linear harvesting terms and mixed delays

\dot{u}(t)=-\alpha(t)u(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}(t)f_{i}(\lambda_{i}(t)u(t-\tau_{i}(t))+b(t)H(u(t-\sigma(t)))\,.

(5)

The present work is organized as follows: in section
2
, we introduce the notion of compact Bi-almost automorphy for measurable functions and revisit some preliminary facts on compact almost automorphic functions. In section
3
we prove the main result of the present work, thus Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
. In section
4
, we use the
\Delta_{2}
-like uniform continuity of
G(\cdot,\cdot)
to show invariance of the almost automorphic function space under convolutions whose kernel is the Green function
G(\cdot,\cdot)
. Finally, in section
5
we prove the existence of a unique positive compact almost automorphic solution of the delay differential equation (
5
).

2
Preliminaries

In the present work,
\mathbb{X}
is a real or complex Banach spaces with norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{X}}

,
||\cdot||
denotes the norm of matrices,
||\cdot||_{\infty}
denotes the supremum norm and
|\cdot|
is the absolute value.

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
, is compact almost
automorphic if, for every sequence
\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}
, there
exists a subsequence
\left\{s_{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}
and a function

\tilde{f}:{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
such that the following limits hold

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{C}}||f(t+s_{n})-\tilde{f}(t)||_{\mathbb{X}}=0,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{C}}||\tilde{f}(t-s_{n})-f(t)||_{\mathbb{X}}=0\,.

(6)

where
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}
, is compact.

If in (
6
) the limits are pointwise in
\mathbb{R}
,
f
is called almost automorphic.
We denote by
\mathcal{K}AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})
the apace of compact almost automorphic functions and by
AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})
the space of almost automorphic functions.
AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})
and
\mathcal{K}AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})
are Banach spaces under the supremum norm and
\mathcal{K}AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})\subset AA({\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{X})
holds, see
[
6
]
.

For an almost automorphic function
f
, its Hull, denoted by
H(f)
, is the set of all functions
\tilde{f}
that satisfy Definition
2.1
.

The following is a useful characterization of compact almost automorphic functions on the real line. For the multidimensional euclidean space, see
[
6
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

A continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
is compact almost automorphic if and only if it is almost automorphic and uniformly continuous.

In the following proposition, we summarize some properties of compact almost automorphic function, for further details see for instance
[
5
,
6
]
and the books
[
10
,
15
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, then

1.

If
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach algebra with norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{X}}
, addition
+_{\mathbb{X}}
and multiplication
\times_{\mathbb{X}}
, then
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
is also a Banach algebra with norm
||\cdot||_{\infty}
and the operations: if
f,g\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
, then

(f+g)(t):=f(t)+_{\mathbb{X}}g(t)\,,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(f\cdot g)(t):=f(t)\times_{\mathbb{X}}g(t)\,,\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

2.

f
is bounded and, if
\tilde{f}
is the limit function in definition
2.1
, then

||f||_{\infty}=||\tilde{f}||_{\infty}\,.

3.

If
F:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
be a continuous function, then
F\circ f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{Y}
is compact almost automorphic.

4.

The range of
f
is relatively compact.

5.

If
a\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
, then
F(t)=f(t-a(t))
is compact almost automorphic from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{X}
.

Next, we provide the definition of Bi-almost automorphy for measurable functions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
be a measurable function,
f
is compact Bi-almost automorphic if, for every sequence
\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
there exist a subsequence
\left\{s_{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}
and a function
\tilde{f}:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
such that, if
\mathbb{K}\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is compact, the following limits hold

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{(t,s)\in\mathbb{K}}||f(t+s_{n},s+s_{n})-\tilde{f}(t,s)||_{\mathbb{X}}=0\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{(t,s)\in\mathbb{K}}||\tilde{f}(t-s_{n},s-s_{n})-f(t,s)||_{\mathbb{X}}=0\,.

When the limits are pointwise in
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
f
is called Bi-almost automorphic.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Phi(\cdot)
be a fundamental matrix of system (
2
), then (
2
) has an exponential dichotomy with parameters
(\alpha,K,P)
, if there exist positive constants
\alpha,K
and
a projection
P
(
P^{2}=P
) such that
||G(t,s)||\leq Ke^{-\alpha\left|t-s\right|}
,
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Where
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Green function defined by:

G(t,s):=\begin{cases}\Phi(t)P\Phi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t\geq s$\,, }\\
-\Phi(t)(I-P)\Phi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t<s$}\,.\end{cases}

In this case, we say that system (
2
) has an
(\alpha,K,P)
-exponential dichotomy.

Definition 2.4
.

[
7
]

We say that a measurable function
C:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
is
\lambda
-bounded if, there exists a positive function
\lambda:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that, for every
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

||C(t+\tau,s+\tau)||_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\lambda(t,s)\,.

Example 2.1
.

If
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Green function defined in Definition
2.3
; then,
G(\cdot,\cdot)
is measurable and
\lambda
-bounded, for
\lambda(t,s)=Ke^{-\alpha|t-s|}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let us suppose that the Bi-almost automorphic function
C:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
is
\lambda
-bounded.
Then, its limit function
\tilde{C}:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{X}
(see definition
2.4
) satisfies:

||\tilde{C}(t+\tau,s+\tau)||_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\lambda(t,s)\,\,,\,\,\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Proof.

Let
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Since
C
is Bi-almost automorphic, there exist a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
and a function
\tilde{C}
such that the following pointwise limits hold

\tilde{C}(t,s):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}C(t+s_{n},s+s_{n})\,\,,\,\,\,C(t,s)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{C}(t-s_{n},s-s_{n})\,.

On the other hand, we have

||\tilde{C}(t+\tau,s+\tau)||_{\mathbb{X}}\leq||\tilde{C}(t+\tau,s+\tau)-C(t+\tau+s_{n},s+\tau+s_{n})||_{\mathbb{X}}+\lambda(t,s)\,.

Now, taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
in the last inequality we obtain the result.
∎

This Lemma improves
(
7
, Lemma 2.9)
, where it was proved that
\tilde{C}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the inequality
||\tilde{C}(t,s)||_{\mathbb{X}}\leq\lambda(t,s)\,\,,\,\,(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. This Lemma will be invoked in section
4
. Further details on almost automorphic functions can be found in
[
10
,
15
]
and on exponential dichotomy in
[
8
,
13
]
.

3
Bi-compact almost automorphy of the Green function
G(\cdot,\cdot)

Our main result in this section is the following Theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that system (
2
) has an
(\alpha,K,P)
-exponential dichotomy with fundamental matrix
\Phi(t)
,

\Phi(0)=I
and
A(\cdot)
is compact almost automorphic, that is:
Given and arbitrary sequence
\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
there exists a
subsequence
\left\{s_{n}\right\}\subset\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}
such that the following
limits hold uniformly on compacts sets of the real line

\lim_{n\to+\infty}A(t+s_{n})=:B(t),\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}B(t-s_{n})=A(t)\,.

(7)

Then, there exists a projection matrix
P_{0}
, such that the system

y^{\prime}(t)=B(t)y(t)\,,

(8)

has an
(\alpha,K,P_{0})
-exponential dichotomy. Furthermore, the associated Green function of system (
2
) is compact Bi-almost automorphic.

Before to prove Theorem
3.1
, we stablish the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Under hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
, there exists a subsequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}\}
such that, the following limits hold

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{C}}||\Phi_{n}(t)-\Psi(t)||=0,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in\mathcal{C}}||\Phi_{n}^{-1}(t)-\Psi^{-1}(t)||=0\,,

where
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}
is compact,
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi(t+\zeta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})
and
\Psi
is a fundamental matrix of (
8
).

Proof.

(
Proof of Lemma
3.2
)

It is sufficient to consider the compact interval
\mathcal{C}=[-a,a]
, with
a>0
.

From hypothesis, there exists a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
such that the limits in (
7
) hold. For this subsequence, let us consider the sequence
\Phi_{n}^{*}(t)=\Phi(t+s_{n})\Phi^{-1}(s_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
; and notice that for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\Phi_{n}^{*}(0)=I
and
\Phi_{n}^{*}(\cdot)
is a fundamental matrix of the system:

x^{\prime}(t)=A(t+s_{n})x(t)\,.

Then, by direct integration it follows that

\Phi_{n}^{*}(t)=I+\int_{0}^{t}A(u+s_{n})\Phi_{n}^{*}(u)du.

Let
M>0
be the supremum of
A(\cdot)
; then, by the Gronwall-Bellman Lemma we have

\left\|\Phi_{n}^{*}(t)\right\|\leq\left\|I\right\|e^{Mt}\leq\left\|I\right\|e^{Ma}\,.

That is,
\Phi_{n}^{*}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{C}
. Now, since

\left(\Phi_{n}^{*}(t)\right)^{\prime}=A(t+s_{n})\Phi_{n}^{*}(t),\quad\text{for $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and $t\in\mathcal{C}$}\,,

(9)

then the sequence of derivatives
\left(\Phi_{n}^{*}(\cdot)\right)^{\prime}
is uniformly bounded over
\mathcal{C}
.
It follows from Arzela-Ascoli Theorem that

\left\{\Phi_{n}^{*}\right\}
has a subsequence
\left\{\Phi_{n}\right\}
(i.e. there
exists an associated subsequence
\left\{\zeta_{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{s_{n}\right\}
) such that
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi(t+\zeta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})
and is uniformly convergent on
\mathcal{C}
.
That is, there exists
\Psi
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi_{n}=\Psi,\;\text{uniformly on $\mathcal{C}$.}

(10)

On the other hand, since the subsequence
\{\Phi_{n}\}
satisfies (
9
), then

\Phi^{\prime}_{n}(t)=A(t+s_{n})\Phi_{n}(t),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;t\in\mathcal{C}.

Therefore,
\Phi^{\prime}_{n}
converges uniformly over
\mathcal{C}
to
B(t)\Psi(t)
. Consequently,
\Psi(\cdot)
is differentiable and moreover

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi^{\prime}_{n}(t)=\Psi^{\prime}(t)\;\text{over $\mathcal{C}$ .}

Since
\Psi^{\prime}(t)=B(t)\Psi(t)
and
\Psi(0)=I
, then
\Psi
is a fundamental matrix of (
8
). That is,
\Psi(\cdot)
is non singular, and hence
\Psi^{-1}
exists on
\mathcal{C}
.

Now we prove that the following limit holds uniformly on
\mathcal{C}

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi^{-1}_{n}(t)=\Psi^{-1}(t)\,.

Firstly, notice that
\Phi^{-1}_{n}

satisfies the equation

x^{\prime}(t)=-x(t)A(t+\zeta_{n})\,;

and, by direct integration we have

\Phi^{-1}_{n}(t)=I-\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{-1}_{n}(u)A(u+\zeta_{n})du.

Similarly,

\Psi^{-1}(t)=I-\int_{0}^{t}\Psi^{-1}(u)B(u)du\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{n}^{-1}(t)-\Psi^{-1}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Psi^{-1}(u)B(u)-\Phi^{-1}_{n}(u)A(u+\zeta_{n})\right\|du

(11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Psi^{-1}(u)-\Phi^{-1}_{n}(u)\right\|du\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|A(u+\zeta_{n})-B(u)\right\|du\left\|\Psi^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}.

Now, since the limits in (
7
) are uniform in
\mathcal{C}
; given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq N_{0}
:

\sup_{t\in\mathcal{C}}\left\|A(t+\zeta_{n})-B(t)\right\|<\epsilon\,.

Therefore, from inequality (
11
) we have, for
n\geq N_{0}

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{n}^{-1}(t)-\Psi^{-1}(t)\right\|\leq\epsilon a\left\|\Psi^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Psi^{-1}(u)-\Phi^{-1}_{n}(u)\right\|du\,\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}\,;

and, from the Gronwall-Bellman Lemma again, we conclude

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{n}^{-1}(t)-\Psi^{-1}(t)\right\|\leq\epsilon a\left\|\Psi^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}e^{\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}a}\,.

∎

Next, we prove the main result of this section.

Proof.

(
Proof of Theorem
3.1
)

Let
\Phi
be a fundamental matrix of
(
2
) and

G(t,s):=\begin{cases}\Phi(t)P\Phi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t\geq s$ ,}\\
-\Phi(t)(I-P)\Phi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t<s$}.\end{cases}

(12)

its associated Green function such that
||G(t,s)||\leq Ke^{-\alpha\left|t-s\right|}
,
t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

The proof will be divided into three steps.

Step 1. The system (
8
) has an
(\alpha,K,P_{0})
-exponential dichotomy
.

Because of Lemma
3.2
, there exists a subsequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}\}
such that the following limits are uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
:

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi_{n}(t)=\Psi(t)\,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi_{n}^{-1}(t)=\Psi^{-1}(t)\,,

(13)

where
\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi(t+\zeta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})
and
\Psi
is a fundamental matrix of (
8
).

Note that
\left\|\Phi(\zeta_{n})P\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})\right\|\leq K
and
\left\|\Phi(\zeta_{n})Q\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})\right\|\leq K
, where
Q=I-P
. Thus, the sequences of matrices
\{\Phi(\zeta_{n})P\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})\}
and
\{\Phi(\zeta_{n})Q\Phi^{-1}(\zeta_{n})\}
are bounded; therefore,
there exists a subsequence

\left\{\eta_{n}\right\}\subset\left\{\zeta_{n}\right\}
such that:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi(\eta_{n})P\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})=:P_{0}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Phi(\eta_{n})Q\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})=:Q_{0}\,.

Notice that
P_{0}^{2}=P_{0}
and
P_{0}+Q_{0}=I
. Now, taking
n\to+\infty
in the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Phi(t+\eta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\right)\Phi(\eta_{n})P\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\left(\Phi(s+\eta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq Ke^{-\alpha(t-s)},\quad t\geq s

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Phi(t+\eta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\right)\Phi(\eta_{n})Q\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\left(\Phi(s+\eta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq Ke^{-\alpha(s-t)},\quad t<s\,,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi(t)P_{0}\Psi^{-1}(s)\right\|\leq Ke^{-\alpha(t-s)},\;t\geq s\,,

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi(t)Q_{0}\Psi^{-1}(s)\right\|\leq Ke^{-\alpha(s-t)},\;t<s\,.

That is, there exists a projection matrix
P_{0}
, such that the homogeneous system (
8
) has an
(\alpha,K,P_{0})
-exponential dichotomy with Green function:

\tilde{G}(t,s):=\begin{cases}\Psi(t)P_{0}\Psi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t\geq s$\, ,}\\
-\Psi(t)Q_{0}\Psi^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t<s$}\,.\end{cases}

(14)

Step 2. The Green function
G(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in (
12
) is Bi-almost automorphic
.

Let
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then, from hypothesis and
Step 1
, there exists a subsequence
\{\eta_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
such that the following pointwise limit holds

\lim_{n\to+\infty}G(t+\eta_{n},s+\eta_{n})=\tilde{G}(t,s)\,;

where,

{G}(t+\eta_{n},s+\eta_{n}):=\begin{cases}\Phi_{n}(t)P_{n}\Phi_{n}^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t\geq s$}\\
-\Phi_{n}(t)Q_{n}\Phi_{n}^{-1}(s),\quad&\text{$t<s$}\,,\end{cases}

(15)

\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi(t+\eta_{n})\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})
,
P_{n}:=\Phi(\eta_{n})P\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n}),\,\,Q_{n}:=\Phi(\eta_{n})Q\Phi^{-1}(\eta_{n})

and
\tilde{G}(\cdot,\cdot)
was defined in (
14
).

We claim that, there exists a subsequence
\left\{\xi_{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\eta_{n}\right\}
such
that the following limit is attained

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{G}(t-\xi_{n},s-\xi_{n})=G(t,s)\,.

In fact, since
\Psi(\cdot)
, with
\Psi(0)=I
, is a fundamental matrix of (
8
), then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Psi_{n}(t):=\Psi(t-\eta_{n})\Psi^{-1}(-\eta_{n})
is a fundamental matrix of the system

z^{\prime}(t)=B(t-\eta_{n})z\,.

Arguing as in the
Step 1
, we can find a subsequence
\{\xi_{n}\}\subset\{\eta_{n}\}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Psi(-\xi_{n})P_{0}\Psi^{-1}(-\xi_{n})=:\tilde{P}_{1},

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Psi(-\xi_{n})Q_{0}\Psi^{-1}(-\xi_{n})=:\tilde{Q}_{1}\,.

Notice that
\tilde{P}_{1}^{2}=\tilde{P}_{1}
,

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume
A1
-
A6
hold. If

\overline{b}L_{H}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\beta_{i}}\,\overline{\lambda_{i}}\,\ell_{f_{i}}<\underline{\alpha}

holds, then equation (
26
) has a unique compact almost automorphic solution in the set

\Omega=\left\{u\in KAA({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{+})\,:\,\gamma_{1}\leq u(t)\leq\gamma_{2},\,\,\,\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}\,.

Proof.

Let us define the operator

({\mathfrak{M}}u)(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\alpha(\xi)d\xi}(Nu)(s)ds\,,

(28)

where,

N:C({\mathbb{R}})\;{\longrightarrow}\;C({\mathbb{R}})
is given by

N(u)(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}(s)f_{i}(\lambda_{i}(s)u(s-\tau_{i}(s)))+b(s)H(u(s-\sigma(s)))\,.

(29)

Now we prove that
\Omega
is closed,
{\mathfrak{M}}\left(\Omega\right)\subset\Omega
and
{\mathfrak{M}}
is a contraction; after that, the conclusion will follow from the Banach fixed point Theorem.

•

\Omega

is closed
. In fact, let the sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset\Omega
such that
u_{n}\to u
uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
. Then, given
\epsilon>0,
there exist
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

||u_{n}-u||_{\infty}<\epsilon\,,\,\,\forall n\geq N_{0}\,.

Therefore, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\forall n\geq N_{0}
, we have

|u(t)|\leq||u_{n}-u||_{\infty}+|u_{n}(t)|<\epsilon+\gamma_{2}\,,

\gamma_{1}-\epsilon<u_{n}(t)-\epsilon<u(t)\,;

which means that
u\in\Omega
.

•

{\mathfrak{M}}\left(\Omega\right)\subset\Omega
. If
u
is compact almost automorphic, then from the properties in Proposition
2.2
, we have that
Nu
is compact almost automorphic and from Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.2
, we have that
{\mathfrak{M}}(u)
is compact almost automorphic too. Moreover, if
u\in\Omega
, we have:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underline{\beta_{i}}\,f_{i}(\overline{\lambda}{\gamma_{2}})+\underline{b}\,\underline{H}\leq\left(Nu\right)(s)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\beta_{i}}\overline{f_{i}}+\overline{b}\,\overline{H}\,;

therefore,

\gamma_{1}<\dfrac{1}{\overline{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underline{\beta_{i}}\,f_{i}(\overline{\lambda}{\gamma_{2}})+\underline{b}\,\underline{H}\right)\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\alpha(\xi)d\xi}\left(Nu\right)(s)ds\leq\dfrac{1}{\underline{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\beta_{i}}\overline{f_{i}}+\overline{b}\,\overline{H}\right)<\gamma_{2}\,.

•

{\mathfrak{M}}

is a contraction
. This follows from the inequality

||{\mathfrak{M}}(u)-{\mathfrak{M}}(v)||_{\infty}\leq\kappa||u-v||_{\infty}\,,\,\,\forall u,v\in\Omega\,;

where,

\kappa:=\dfrac{1}{\underline{\alpha}}\left(\overline{b}L_{H}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\beta_{i}}\,\overline{\lambda_{i}}\,\ell_{f_{i}}\right)<1\,.

Ancient Ricci flows with asymptotic solitons
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
We study ancient Ricci flows which admit asymptotic solitons in the sense of Perelman [ Per02 , Proposition 11.2] . We prove that the asymptotic solitons must coincide with Bamler’s tangent flows at infinity [ Bam20c ] . Furthermore, we show that Perelman’s ν \nu -functional is uniformly bounded on such ancient solutions; this fact leads to logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and Sobolev inequalities. Lastly, as an important tool for the proofs of the above results, we also show that, for a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature, the bound of the Nash entropy depends only on the local geometry around an H n H_{n} -center of its base point.

Introduction

Through Hamilton’s program
[
Ham93b
]
, the study of ancient solutions emerged into the field. Perelman’s seminal work
[
Per02
]
manifests not only that the understanding of ancient solutions is crucial to the analysis of the singularity formation, but also that ancient solutions are interesting in their own right. The ancient solution can be approached from different directions: one is the classification (c.f.
[
DHS12
]
,
[
Bre20
]
,
[
Li20
]
, etc.), which, in the higher-dimensional non-Kähler case, is impossible without any strong curvature assumption; another is the construction of examples (
[
BKN12
]
,
[
Lau13
]
,
[
LW16
]
, etc.), whose immense quantity with their diverse behaviors discloses the difficulty of the whole field; a third approach is the least specific—to study the geometric and analytic properties of ancient solutions, among which Perelman’s monotonicity formulas are very attracting ones. Works in this category include
[
Y09
]
,
[
N10
]
,
[
CZ11
]
,
[
Zh21
]
, to list but a few.

Bamler’s recent ground-breaking works
[
Bam20a
]
—
[
Bam20c
]
have shed more light on the field of Ricci flow. The estimates therein within the smooth category can be regarded as some good improvements of Perelman’s entropy techniques, whereas the weak version of the Ricci flow (called the
metric flow
) provides an intriguing object for the future study. While the latter is fascinating and promising, it is yet beyond the scope of the present article, and we shall focus on the former, just as the last two authors have done in
[
MZ21
]
.

As is well-known now, for ancient solutions under certain curvature conditions, Perelman’s monotonicity formulas
[
Per02
]
imply the existence of an asymptotic shrinker (c.f.
[
Per02
, Proposition 11.2]
and
[
N10
]
). On the other hand, Bamler
[
Bam20c
]
has proved that an ancient solution has a tangent flow at infinity, and that, if we further assume that the Nash entropy is uniformly bounded and that the underlying manifold is closed, then the tangent flow is a metric soliton with a singular set of co-dimension 4 (see
[
Bam20c
, Section 1.8]
; c.f.
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 7.8]
). A natural question arises that how Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker and Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity are related. Bamler himself remarks that, should the ancient solution be a
\kappa
-solution, they two would then coincide. But what about other cases? What is the weakest possible condition for this to be true? These are the questions that motivated the current study. In fact, one of our results is that the asymptotic shrinker and the tangent flow at infinity coincide if the former exists. We do not assume any other curvature condition for the ancient solution except for its time-wise boundedness.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow. Throughout this article, we shall make a technical assumption that
g(t)
has bounded curvature within each compact time interval, namely,

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\big|{\Rm}_{g_{t}}\big|<\infty\quad\text{ for all }\quad-\infty<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq 0.

(1.1)

Note that the curvature bound may depend on the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, and is not uniform on
M\times(-\infty,0]
. This assumption is only for the validity of the classical formulas (especially the integration by parts at infinity) concerning conjugate heat kernels and entropies. In particular, we do not assume the ancient solution in question to be a
\kappa
-solution or to be Type I.

Let
p_{0}\in M
be a fixed point. Then, for a sequence
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
\tau_{i}\nearrow\infty

\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
(p_{i},-\tau_{i})
are
\ell

(p_{0},0)

\ell_{p_{0},0}(p_{i},\tau_{i})\leq\frac{n}{2}
; see section 2.3 for more details of the definitions.
For most of the time in this article, we make the following assumption for the ancient solutions which we consider.

Assumption B:
For the fixed point
(p_{0},0)
and the sequences
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
as described above, there exists a smooth and complete Ricci flow
\big(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),p_{\infty}\big)_{t\in[-2,-1]}
, such that

\displaystyle\big(M,g_{i}(t),p_{i}\big)_{t\in[-2,-1]}\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{}}\big(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),p_{\infty}\big)_{t\in[-2,-1]}

(1.2)

in the smooth Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton sense, where the Ricci flow
g_{i}(t)
is obtained by the Type I scaling

\displaystyle g_{i}(t):=\tau_{i}^{-1}g(\tau_{i}t).

(1.3)

The statement of Assumption B is involved with a base point
(p_{0},0)
, a sequence of positive scales
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, and the choices of
\ell
-centers
(p_{i},-\tau_{i})
. Hence, if necessary, we shall refer to an ancient solution as “satisfying Assumption B with respect to
\big(p_{0},0,\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\big)
”. For the limit Ricci flow
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
in (

), we do not assume any shrinker structure, neither do we make any geometric assumption except for its completeness. However, it will soon be clear that, because of
[

]

We are now ready to state the first of our main theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Under Assumption B, the following hold (see section 2 for all the definitions involved).

(1)

\big(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),p_{\infty}\big)_{t\in[-2,-1]}
admits a shrinker structure, which makes it an asymptotic shrinker in the sense of Perelman (
[
Per02
, Proposition 11.2]
).

(2)

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)=\mu_{\infty},

where
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}
is the Nash entropy based at
(p_{0},0)
and
\mu_{\infty}
is the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker. In particular,
\mu_{\infty}
is the infimum of
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)
,
\tau>0
.

(3)

Any
\mathbb{F}
-limit of the sequence
\left\{\left((M,g_{i}(t))_{t\in[-2,-1]},(\nu_{t}^{i})_{t\in[-2,-1]}\right)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, where
\nu^{i}_{t}:=\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,\tau_{i}t}

is of the form

\left((M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))_{t\in[-2,-1]},(\nu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in[-2,-1)}\right),

up to isometry, where
(\nu_{t}^{\infty})_{t\in[-2,-1)}
is a conjugate heat flow made of a shrinker potential function.

The proof of the above theorem consists of several elements. First of all, the smooth convergence (
1.2
) in Assumption B implies locally uniform geometry bounds for

around points

, which, after scaling back, implies that

is locally uniformly Type I (see Definition

) along

[

]
, the limit
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
admits a shrinker structure, and it is an asymptotic shrinker in the sense of Perelman. Then, applying Theorem
1.7
to the the
\ell
-centers
(p_{i},-\tau_{i})
, we obtain a uniform lower bound for the Nash entropy. Thirdly, the Nash entropy bound provides a Gaussian upper bound for the conjugate heat kernel
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 7.2]
, which is applied to show Theorem
1.1
(2). Finally, the Gaussian upper bound of the conjugate heat kernel also implies that an
H_{n}
-center is always not far from an
\ell
-center; this is the main idea in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(3).

The last two authors
[
MZ21
]
have observed that, on an ancient solution with bounded curvature within each compact time interval, the limit of the Nash entropy
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(\tau)
and the limit of Perelman’s entropy
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{x,t}(\tau)
are independent of the base point
(x,t)
. What is new in the corollary below is that the limit of the reduced volume
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{V}_{x,t}(\tau)
is also independent of the base point
(x,t)
. This fact follows not from
[
MZ21
]
, but from an observation that in the statement of Assumption B, the base point
(p_{0},0)
is not so important as the sequence of positive scales
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution satisfying (
1.1
) and Assumption B with respect to
\big(p_{0},0,\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty},\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\big)
. Then the following holds.

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}}(\tau)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}}(\tau)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\log\mathcal{V}_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}}(\tau)=\mu_{\infty},

where
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{V}
stand for Perelman’s entropy and reduced volume, respectively,
(p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0})
is an arbitrary point in
M
, (in particular,
(p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0})
is not necessarily
(p_{0},0)
), and
\mu_{\infty}
is the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
(see section 2.2 and section 2.3 for the definitions).

As a complement of Theorem
1.1
, we also consider its reciprocal problem: is a tangent flow of an ancient solution also Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker? This, of course, is not true if the tangent flow is not smooth. However, if the tangent flow is smooth, then this problem can be easily answered with Bamler’s results in
[
Bam20c
]
, which are currently only proved to be true for a sequence of Ricci flows on closed manifolds and with bounded entropies. Hereby we would like to clarify that the following theorem is true
only if
the results in
[
Bam20c
]
(especially Theorem 1.6 therein) are valid for noncompact Ricci flows with bounded geometry within each compact time interval (as it is conventional, by
bounded geometry
we always mean that the sectional curvature is bounded from above and from below, and that the volumes of unit balls are uniformly bounded away from
0
). This generalization is not among the goals of the present article. However, its validity, with relatively strong assurance, is not only our speculation, but expectation.

Theorem 1.3

(The reciprocal of Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution with bounded geometry within each compact time interval and with bounded Nash entropy, that is, there is a point
p_{0}\in M
such that
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)\geq-Y
for all
\tau>0
, where
Y
is a constant. If a tangent flow of
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
at infinity (c.f. Definition
2.17
) is smooth, then it is also an asymptotic shrinker in the sense of Perelman.

Remark:
Of course, if we assume the underlying manifold
M^{n}
(not necessarily the underlying manifold of the tangent flow) to be closed, then the proof of the above theorem can easily go through, since the techniques of
[
Bam20c
]
do apply in this case.

Under Assumption B, we also consider Perelman’s
\nu
-functional (see section 2.2 for the definition) on the ancient solution. We prove that on such an ancient solution, the
\nu
-functional is uniformly bounded everywhere, and a lower bound (indeed, the infimum) is the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow satisfying (
1.1
) and Assumption B. Then we have

\displaystyle\inf_{t\leq 0}\nu(g(t))=\mu_{\infty}>-\infty,

(1.4)

where
\mu_{\infty}
is the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker in Theorem
1.1
(1). In particular, this result is true for all noncollapsed Type I ancient solutions and all
\kappa
-solutions.

Once the
\nu
-functional is known to be bounded, the following logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and Sobolev inequalities are simply consequences of straightforward computations (c.f.
[
Zhq07
,
LW20
]
).

Corollary 1.5

(The logarithmic Sobolev and Sobolev inequalities)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow satisfying (
1.1
) and Assumption B. Then, for any
t\in(-\infty,0]
, the following are true.

(1)

Logarithmic Sobolev inequality: for any compactly supported locally Lipschitz function
u
and positive scale
\tau>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}dg_{t}-\left(\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}\right)\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}+\left(\mu_{\infty}+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)\right)\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\int_{M}(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru)dg_{t}

.

(2)

Sobolev inequality: for any compactly supported locally Lipschitz function

, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dg_{t}\right)\leq C(n)e^{-\frac{2\mu_{\infty}}{n}}\int_{M}(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru)dg_{t}.

Here
\mu_{\infty}
is the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker in Theorem
1.1
(1). In particular, this result is true for all noncollapsed Type I ancient solutions and all
\kappa
-solutions.

Obviously, we may also use Theorem

to obtain a finer version of the main results in

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution satisfying (

(1)

Then,

\inf_{t\leq 0}\nu(g(t))\geq-\beta,

where

The next two results imply that the Nash entropy depends only on the local geometry around the
\ell
-centers or the

-centers, and does not depend on the geometry near the base point. These theorems are particularly useful in the long-time analysis of the Nash entropy.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each time interval compact in

. Let

,

,

, and assume that

. Let

be an

-center of

\displaystyle|{\Ric}|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{t-s}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{s}(z,\sqrt{t-s})\times[s-(t-s),s],

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s)\geq-\beta,

. Consequently, under the same assumption of the theorem, we have

\displaystyle K(x,t\,|\,y,s)\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{{\rm dist}_{s}^{2}(z,y)}{C(t-s)}\right)\quad\text{ for all }\quad y\in M,

where

depends only on
n
,
\alpha
, and

, and

is the conjugate heat kernel (see section 2.1 for the definition.)

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each time interval compact in
I
. Let
s
,
t\in I
,
s<t
, and assume that

. Let

and let
(z,s)
be an

-center of

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{s}}\big(B_{s}(z,\sqrt{t-s})\big)\geq\alpha(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Then all the conclusions in Theorem

.

Remarks:

1.

In Theorem
1.7
, the assumptions can be replaced by

\displaystyle|{\Ric}|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\varepsilon^{2}(t-s)}\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{s}(z,\varepsilon\sqrt{t-s})\times[s-\varepsilon^{2}(t-s),s],

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{s}}\big(B_{s}(z,\varepsilon\sqrt{t-s})\big)\geq\alpha\varepsilon^{n}(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle s-\varepsilon^{2}(t-s)\in I;

in Theorem
1.8
, the assumptions can be replaced by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{s}}\big(B_{s}(z,\varepsilon\sqrt{t-s})\big)\geq\alpha\varepsilon^{n}(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle s-\varepsilon^{2}(t-s)\in I,

where
\varepsilon
is any positive constant. If so, then the constants in the conclusions also depend on
\varepsilon
.

2.

Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
do not imply each other. The reason is because unless the Nash entropy is known to be bounded, one does not know whether an
\ell
-center is close to an
H_{n}
-center. In fact, the statement of Theorem
1.8
is much stronger than Theorem
1.7
. This is because of Bamler’s good gradient estimates for heat kernels.

3.

Comparing Theorem
1.8
with
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 6.2]
, we have that, if
(z,t-r^{2})
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(x,t)
, then
r^{-n}\Vol_{g_{t-r^{2}}}\left(B_{t-r^{2}}(z,\sqrt{2H_{n}}r)\right)
and

mutually bound each other.

Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
show an interesting behavior of the Nash entropy: it is the geometry around the
H_{n}
-centers or the
\ell
-centers that determines (a lower bound of) the Nash entropy. This is somewhat in contrast to our first impression, since the Nash entropy is defined as a global integral. But the
H_{n}
-concentration property provides a likely explanation: as we know, the

-centers are points around which the conjugate heat flow (regarded as a probability measure) accumulates its measure (

), and hence the region far away from an

-center should make little contribution to the Nash entropy. Furthermore, this “locality” behavior is also in time. In other words,

is bounded so long as the geometry near
(z,s)
, an

-center of

, is bounded, regardless of what is happening to the Ricci flow on
M\times[s,t]
. We hope these results to be useful in future studies.

Preliminaries

2.1
Conjugate heat kernel

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow.
We denote by

K(x,t\,|\,y,s)

the unique minimal fundamental solution to the heat equation coupled with
g(t)
, that is,

\displaystyle\Box_{x,t}K(x,t\,|\,y,s)=0,

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow s+}K(x,t\,|\,y,s)=\delta_{y}(x),

\displaystyle\Box^{*}_{y,s}K(x,t\,|\,y,s)=0,

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow t-}K(x,t\,|\,y,s)=\delta_{x}(y),

where

\Box_{x,t}:=\partial_{t}-\Delta_{g_{t},x},\quad\Box^{*}_{y,s}:=-\partial_{s}-\Delta_{g_{s},y}+R(y,s).

Throughout this paper, the laplacians and the covariant derivatives are all time-dependent, computed using the evolving metric of the Ricci flow. We shall suppress the subindices in the notations such as
\Delta_{g_{t},x}
when the metric and the variables are understood. It is well known that, whenever the integration by parts at infinity is valid, the conjugate heat equation preserves the integral. Hence, at least for Ricci flows with bounded curvature within each compact time interval, the measure
\nu_{x,t\,|\,s}
defined as

\displaystyle\nu_{x,t\,|\,s}(A):=\int_{A}K(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)dg_{s},\quad A\subset M

(2.1)

is always a probability measure, where
x\in M
,
s,t\in I
, and
s\leq t.
In this article, both the fundamental solution
K(x,t\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)
and the evolving probability measure
(\nu_{x,t\,|\,s})_{s\in I\cap(-\infty,t]}
will be referred to as the
conjugate heat kernel
, whenever there is no ambiguity. One can also use other positive solutions to the conjugate heat equation instead of a fundamental solution to construct time-dependent probability measures as (
2.1
), and such evolving probability measures are usually referred to as
conjugate heat flows
. For the conjugate heat kernel, there are the following logarithmic Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities proved in
[
HN14
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Hein-Naber’s logrithmic Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities
[
HN14
]
)
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in[-T,0]}
is a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature. Let
x_{0}\in M
be a fixed point and
\nu_{s}:=\nu_{x_{0},0\,|\,s}
for
s\in[-T,0).
Then, for any function
u\geq 0,\sqrt{u}\in C_{0}^{0,1}(M)
, we have

\int_{M}u\log u\,d\nu_{s}-\left(\int_{M}u\,d\nu_{s}\right)\log\left(\int_{M}u\,d\nu_{s}\right)\leq|s|\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}d\nu_{s}.

(2.2)

For any
u\in C_{0}^{0,1}(M),
we have

\int_{M}u^{2}d\nu_{s}-\left(\int_{M}ud\nu_{s}\right)^{2}\leq 2|s|\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}d\nu_{s}.

(2.3)

In the original statement of the above results, Hein-Naber
[
HN14
]
assumed bounded geometry for the Ricci flow. However, given all the estimates for the conjugate heat kernel (c.f.
[
CCGGIIKLLN10
, Corollary 26.26, Theorem 26.31]
,
[
EKNT08
, Theorem 10]
, and
[
Zhq06
,
BCP10
]
), it is not difficult to weaken the assumption to bounded curvature alone. We shall include the proof in Appendix A.

Hein-Naber also proved the following Gaussian concentration theorem using Davies’ technique and their logarithmic Sobolev inequalities.

Proposition 2.2

(Hein-Naber’s Gaussian concentration
[
HN14
]
)
.

Under the same assumption as the above proposition, we have

\nu_{s}(A)\nu_{s}(B)\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{{\rm dist}^{2}_{s}(A,B)}{8|s|}\right\},

(2.4)

for any measurable subsets
A
,
B\subset M
. Here
{\rm dist}_{s}
means the classical distance between two sets computed using the metric
g(s)
, not the Hausdorff distance.

2.2
Perelman’s entropy functionals

Let us recall Perelman’s definition of the
\mathcal{W}
-functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau):=\int_{M}\Big(\tau\big(|\nabla f|^{2}+R\big)+f-n\Big)(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg,

(2.5)

where
(M^{n},g)
is a Riemannian manifold,
f
is a smooth function, and
\tau>0
is a positive scale. If we let
u:=(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}
, then we may rewrite

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g,u,\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\tau\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}+R\right)-\log u-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n\right)u\,dg.

(2.6)

In fact, this form of the
\mathcal{W}
functional is what we will apply in the discussion of section 9. Note that for any
c>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(cg,f,c\tau)=\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau),\quad\overline{\mathcal{W}}(cg,c^{-\frac{n}{2}}u,c\tau)=\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g,u,\tau).

Perelman’s
\mu
-functional and
\nu
-functional are defined as follows.

\displaystyle\mu(g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g,u,\tau)\ \bigg|\ \sqrt{u}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M),\ u\geq 0,\ \text{ and }\int_{M}udg=1\right\},

\displaystyle\nu(g)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{\tau>0}\mu(g,\tau).

Here for the
\mu
functional we are adopting the definition in
[
CCGGIIKLLN07
]
; see the formula above Lemma 6.28 therein. It is well understood that
\mu(g,\tau)
is the logarithmic Sobolev constant at scale
\tau
, and that
\nu
is the Sobolev constant.

Let us fix a point
(p_{0},t_{0})
in the space-time of a Ricci flow
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
, and denote the conjugate heat kernel based at
(p_{0},t_{0})
as

\displaystyle K(p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,x,t):=(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f(x,t)},\quad t\in I\cap(-\infty,t_{0}).

Then,
Perelman’s entropy
and the
Nash entropy
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\big(g(t_{0}-\tau),f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau),\tau\big)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big(\tau\left(|\nabla f|^{2}+R\right)+f-n\Big)(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\,d\nu_{p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t_{0}-\tau},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\,d\nu_{p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t_{0}-\tau}-\frac{n}{2},

(2.8)

for all
\tau>0
and
t_{0}-\tau\in I
. The point
(p_{0},t_{0})
is called the
base point
. It is well known from Perelman
[
Per02
]
that both Perelman’s entropy and the Nash entropy are increasing in time (and hence decreasing in
\tau
). Although the monotonocity of
\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}
or
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}
requires the integration by parts at infinity, yet this is valid at least for Ricci flows with bounded curvature.

2.3
Perelman’s reduced distance

We briefly review Perelman’s reduced distance and reduced volume. Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in[-T,0]}
be a Ricci flow with bounded curvature. Let
(p_{0},t_{0})\in M\times(-T,0]
be a fixed point in space-time. Then, Perelman’s
reduced distance
is defined as

\displaystyle\ell_{p_{0},t_{0}}(x,\tau):=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\tau}}\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\tau}\sqrt{s}\left(|\dot{\gamma}(s)|_{g(t_{0}-s)}^{2}+R(\gamma(s),t_{0}-s)\right)ds,

(2.9)

where
x\in M
,
\tau\in(0,T-|t_{0}|]
, and the infimum is taken over all piecewise smooth curves
\gamma:[0,\tau]\rightarrow M
satisfying
\gamma(0)=p_{0}
and
\gamma(\tau)=x
. The minimizer of (
2.9
) is usually called a minimal
\mathcal{L}
-geodesic from
(p_{0},t_{0})
to
(x,t_{0}-\tau)
.
(p_{0},t_{0})
is called the base point of
\ell
, and whenever the base point is understood, we shall suppress the subindex in the notaion
\ell_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\cdot,\cdot)
. The
reduced volume based at
(p_{0},t_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau):=\int_{M}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\cdot,\tau)}dg_{t_{0}-\tau}.

(2.10)

Perelman’s reduced distance and reduced volume satisfy many nice equations and inequalities. Among them the most important one is the monotonicity of the reduced volume.

Proposition 2.3
.

Perelman’s reduced volume
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
is an increasing function in time (and hence a decreasing function in
\tau
).

The underlying reason for the monotonicity of the reduced volume is the fact that its integrand is a “sub”-conjugate heat kernel.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\ell_{p,0}(x,\tau)
be the reduced distance based at
(p,0)
. Then,
\displaystyle u(x,t):=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{p,0}(x,|t|)}
is a subsolution to the conjugate heat equation
-\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+Ru=0
which also converges to the Dirac delta measure based at
p
as
\tau\rightarrow 0+
. Precisely, this means

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\tau}-\Delta_{g_{-\tau}}\ell+\left|\,\nabla_{g_{-\tau}}\ell\,\right|_{g_{-\tau}}^{2}-R_{g_{-\tau}}+\frac{n}{2\tau}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0+}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{p,0}(\cdot,\tau)}=\delta_{p}.

Both of the formulas above are understood in the sense of distribution. In consequence, we have

\displaystyle(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{p,0}(x,|t|)}\leq K(p,0\,|\,x,t)\quad\text{ for all }(x,t)\in M\times[-T,0),

(2.11)

where
K(p,0\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)
is the conjugate heat kernel based at
(p,0)
.

By an elementary application of the maximum principle, Perelman
[
Per02
]
proved that
\ell(\cdot,\tau)
always attains its minimum. This minimum point should be viewed as the “center” of the reduced distance.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\ell_{p,0}
be the reduced distance based at
(p,0)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\min_{M}\ell(\cdot,\tau)\leq\frac{n}{2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau\in(0,T].

(2.12)

The point(s) where the minimum in formula (
2.12
) is attained plays an important role in our arguments. In most of the cases, it turns out that such a minimum point is not far from Bamler’s
H_{n}
-centers. Hence, we would like to assign a special term to these points.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow, and let
(x,t)\in M\times I
be a point in space time. Let
s\in I\cap(-\infty,t)
. Then,
(z,s)
is called an
\ell
-center
of
(x,t)
if

\displaystyle\ell_{x,t}(z,t-s)\leq\frac{n}{2}.

Remark:
Similar to the case of the
H_{n}
-center, the
\ell
-center is not necessarily unique at a fixed time
s
for a fixed base point
(x,t)
. Furthermore, in practice (especially when considering the base points for the blow-down sequence from which we obtain an asymptotic shrinker), a sequence of space-time points along which
\ell
is uniformly bounded serves equally well as a sequence of
\ell
-centers; see Definition
2.8
.

2.4
Shrinking gradient Ricci soliton and its entropy

A shrinking gradient Ricci soliton (or Ricci shrinker for short) is a tuple
(M^{n},g,f)
, where
(M^{n},g)
is a smooth Riemannian manifold, and
f
is a smooth function on
M
called a
shrinker potential
, satisfying

\Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\tfrac{1}{2}g.

Apparently, the shrinker potential is not unique, for one may always add a constant to it. If we normalize
f
such that

(4\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{M}e^{-f}dg=1,

then

\mu:=f-(|\nabla f|^{2}+R)

is a constant called the
shrinker entropy
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
be the 1-parameter group of self-diffeomorphisms generated by
\nabla f
with
\Phi_{0}:M\rightarrow M
being the identity map.
Define
\phi_{t}:=\Phi_{-\log|t|}
,

g(t):=|t|\phi^{*}_{t}g
, and
f_{t}=f\circ\phi_{t}
. Then,
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0)}
is an ancient solution to the Ricci flow, and
(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{t}}
is a solution to the conjugate heat equation. The evolving tuple
(M,g(t),f_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}
is called the
canonical form
of the Ricci shrinker. The canonical form satisfies

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{t}=\frac{1}{2|t|}g(t),

(2.13)

\displaystyle-|t|\big(|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}_{g_{t}}+R_{g_{t}}\big)+f_{t}\equiv\mu,

where
\mu
is the shrinker entropy. It turns out that the entropy of a fixed shrinker is unique (even if the normalized potential is not necessarily unique). In fact, Li-Wang
[
LW20
]
proved the following strong statement.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
LW20
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker, where
f
is normalized so that
\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}dg=1
. Let
\mu
be the shrinker entropy. Then we have

\displaystyle\mu=\mathcal{W}(g,f,1)=\mu(g,1)=\nu(g).

Furthermore, if we let
(M,g(t),f_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0)}
be the canonical form, then we also have

\displaystyle\mu=\mathcal{W}(g(t),f_{t},|t|)=\mu(g(t),|t|)=\nu(g(t))\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in(-\infty,0).

2.5
Locally uniformly Type I ancient solution

Cheng-Zhang
[
CZ20
]
studied a geometric condition for ancient Ricci flows, called the locally uniformly Type I condition. Precisely, it is defined as follows.

Definition 2.8

(Locally uniformly Type I ancient solutions)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow. Fix
p_{0}\in M
and let
\{(p_{i},-\tau_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset M\times(-\infty,0)
be a sequence of space-time points with
\tau_{i}\nearrow\infty
. Then,
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
is called
locally uniformly Type I
along the sequence
\{(p_{i},-\tau_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, if the following hold.

(1)

\ell_{p_{0},0}
is bounded along
\{(p_{i},-\tau_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, that is,

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}\ell(p_{i},\tau_{i})<\infty.

(2)

Around the space-time points
(p_{i},-\tau_{i})
, the curvature has a locally uniformly Type I bound. More precisely, there exists a positive function
C:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
with the following property: for all
A>0
, there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
A
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{-\tau_{i}}(p_{i},r\sqrt{\tau_{i}})\times[-2\tau_{i},-\tau_{i}]}|{\Rm}|\leq\frac{C(r)}{\tau_{i}}\quad\text{for all}\quad i\geq i_{0}\quad\text{and for all}\quad r\leq A.

(3)

There is a time-wise Ricci curvature lower bound for
g(t)
. In other words, there exists a continuous positive function
K:(-\infty,0]\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{t}}\geq-K(t)g(t)\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in(-\infty,0].

(4)

g(t)
is noncollapsed along
(p_{i},-\tau_{i})
. In other words,

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\Big((\tau_{i})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\text{inj}\big(g(-\tau_{i}),p_{i}\big)\Big)>0,

where
\text{inj}(g,x)
stands for the injectivity radius of the Riemannian metric
g
at
x
.

With the locally uniformly Type I condition, Cheng-Zhang
[
CZ20
]
obtained locally uniformly
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates for the reduced distance
\ell
, and consequently proved the existence of an asymptotic shrinker. We shall include these results below.

Proposition 2.9

(Proposition 5.1 in
[
CZ20
]
)
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be a locally uniformly Type I ancient solution as described in Definition
2.8
. Let
\ell_{i}(\cdot,\tau):=\ell_{p_{0},0}(\cdot,\tau_{i}\tau)
and
g_{i}(t):=\tau_{i}^{-1}g(\tau_{i}t)
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, there is a positive function
C(\cdot,\varepsilon):(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
with the following property: for any
r>0
, it holds that
0\leq\ell_{i}(x,|t|)\leq C(r,\varepsilon)
for all
(x,t)\in B_{g_{i,-1}}(p_{i},r)\times[-2,-1-\varepsilon]
whenever
i
is large enough.

Proposition 2.10

(Asymptotic Shrinker
[
CZ20
]
)
.

Let
g_{i}(t)
and
\ell_{i}
be as described in the above proposition, then, after possibly passing to a subsequence, the sequence of tuples
\displaystyle\Big\{\big(M,g_{i}(t),\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)\big)_{t\in[-2,-1]}\Big\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converges to (the canonical form of) a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
\big(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),\ell_{\infty}(\cdot,|t|)\big)_{t\in(-2,-1)}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{\infty}}(t)+\nabla^{2}\ell_{\infty}(\cdot,|t|)=\frac{1}{2|t|}g_{\infty}(t).

The convergence of the Ricci flows is in the pointed smooth Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton sense, and
\ell_{i}\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}
in the
C^{0,\alpha}_{\operatorname{loc}}
sense or in the weak
*W^{1,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}
sense on
M_{\infty}\times(1,2)
, where
\ell_{i}
should be understood to be pulled back by the defining diffeomorphisms of the Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton convergence, and
\alpha
is any number in
(0,1)
.

Corollary 2.11

(Noncollapsing
[
CZ20
]
)
.

A locally uniformly Type I ancient solution is (weakly)
\kappa
-noncollapsed on all scales, where
\kappa>0
depends only on the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker.

2.6
Metric flow and

H_{n}
-center

In this subsection we briefly review the notion of the metric flow introduced by Bamler
[
Bam20b
]
. Since here (and in the next subsection) we are not attempting to be comprehensive about all the nuances, the reader should always resort to
[
Bam20b
]
for more details. We shall use the notation
\mathcal{P}(X)
to represent the space of probability measures on a metric space
X.
The metric flow is introduced as a natural generalization of the Ricci flow space-time. A metric flow over
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a tuple

\big(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{t},({\rm dist}_{t})_{t\in I},(\nu_{x\,|\,s})_{x\in\mathcal{X},s\in I,s\leq\mathfrak{t}(x)}\big),

where
\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{X}\to I
is the time function and
\mathcal{X}_{t}:=\mathfrak{t}^{-1}(t)
is called the time slice at
t
,
{\rm dist}_{t}
is a metric on
\mathcal{X}_{t}
,
\nu_{x\,|\,s}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
is a family of probability measures called the
conjugate heat kernel
based at
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and it satisfies the usual reproduction formula: for any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}
in
I
and for any
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t_{3}}
, we have

\nu_{x\,|\,t_{1}}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t_{2}}}\nu_{\cdot\,|\,t_{1}}\,d\nu_{x\,|\,t_{2}}.

The sharp gradient estimate of Bamler
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 4.1]
is also axiomized into the definition of the metric flow. More details could be found in
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2]
. The last two authors generalized
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 4.1]
to noncompact Ricci flows with bounded curvature within each compact time interval (c.f.
[
MZ21
]
). Hence, the following observation is straightforward.

Theorem 2.12

(Bamler)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow with bounded curvature within each time interval compact in
I
.
Then it induces a canonical metric flow in the sense of
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2]
.

Proof.

By
[
CCGGIIKLLN10
, Lemma 26.16]
, the reproduction formula holds for the minimal heat kernel
K(x,t\,|\,y,s)
coupled with the flow
(M,g(t))
.
So
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2 (7)]
is satisfied if we choose
d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s}:=K(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{s}.

As mentioned in the above paragraph,
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2 (1)-(6)]
are satisfied.
∎

We shall then introduce some definitions and results for the metric flow. In particular, they can be applied to smooth Ricci flows satisfying the condition of the above theorem. Before we proceed to define the notion of
H
-concentration, let us recall the
variance
of two probability measures. Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, where
X
is a metric space, then their variance is defined as

{\rm Var}(\mu,\nu):=\int_{X\times X}{\rm dist}^{2}(y_{1},y_{2})\,d\mu(y_{1})d\nu(y_{2}),

where
{\rm dist}
is the metric on
X
. If
\mu
is the same as
\nu
, then we also denote
{\rm Var}(\mu):={\rm Var}(\mu,\mu)
.
H
-concentration is defined as follows.

Definition 2.13

(Definition 3.30 in
[
Bam20b
]
)
.

A metric flow
\mathcal{X}
over
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is said to be
H
-concentrated, where
H
is a positive number, if for any
s,t\in I
,
s\leq t
, and
x_{1},x_{2}

\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, we have,

\text{Var}(\nu_{x_{1}\,|\,s},\nu_{x_{2}\,|\,s})\leq\text{dist}_{t}^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+H(t-s).

(2.14)

Combining (
2.14
) with the reproduction formula, we have that, on an
H
-concentrated metric flow
\mathcal{X}
, for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{t},

{\rm Var}(\nu_{x_{1}\,|\,s},\nu_{x_{2}\,|\,s})+Hs

is non-decreasing in
s\in I\cap(-\infty,t]
, and is bounded from above by
{\rm dist}_{t}^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+Ht
(c.f.
[
Bam20b
, Proposition 3.34]
). This fact guarantees the existence of
H
-centers (c.f.
[
Bam20b
, Proposition 3.36]
), which are defined as follows.

Definition 2.14

(Definition 3.35 in
[
Bam20b
]
)
.

Let
s,t\in I
and
s\leq t
. A point
z

\in\mathcal{X}_{s}
is called an
H
-center of
x

\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
if

\text{Var}(\delta_{z},\nu_{x\,|\,s})\leq H(t-s).

The
H
-center adopted its name partially because the conjugate heat kernel accumulates its measure around it. Precisely, we have (
[
Bam20a
, Proposition 3.13]
and
[
Bam20b
, Lemma 3.37]
):

Proposition 2.15

(Bamler)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
H
-concentrated metric flow over
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
be a fixed point, and let
z\in\mathcal{X}_{s}
be an
H
-center of
x
, where
s<t
and
s,t\in I
. Then, for any
A>1
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{x\,|\,s}\big(B_{s}(z,\sqrt{AH(t-s)})\big)\geq 1-\frac{1}{A}.

Remark:
Bamler
[
Bam20a
, Proposition 3.2]
proved that if
\mathcal{X}=M^{n}\times I
is a Ricci flow space-time on a closed manifold
M^{n}
over an interval
I
, then
\mathcal{X}
must be
H_{n}
-concentrated, where

H_{n}:=\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}}{2}+4.

The same argument also works when the Ricci flow is complete and has bounded curvature within compact time intervals.
This is a fact which shall be used throughout this article.
In general, given
x

\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, and
s\leq t
,
H
-centers of
x
may not be unique in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
.

2.7
Metric flow pair and
\mathbb{F}
-convergence

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
is a metric flow over
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
A family of probability measures
\mu_{s}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{s})
, where
s\in I^{\prime}\subset I
, is called a
conjugate heat flow
if it satisfies the reproduction formula: for any
s,t\in I^{\prime},s\leq t,
we have

\mu_{s}=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}\nu_{x\,|\,s}\,d\mu_{t}(x).

A metric flow pair is then defined to be a metric flow coupled with a conjugate heat flow.

Definition 2.16

(Definition 5.1 in
[
Bam20b
]
)
.

A pair
\left(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}\right)
is called a
metric flow pair
over
I\subset\mathbb{R}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

I^{\prime}\subset I
, and
|I\setminus I^{\prime}|=0
, where
|\,\cdot\,|
is the Lebesgue measure;

•

\mathcal{X}
is a metric flow over
I^{\prime}
;

•

(\mu_{t})_{t\in I^{\prime}}
is a conjugate heat flow on
\mathcal{X}
with
\spt\mu_{t}=\mathcal{X}_{t}
for all
t\in I^{\prime}
.

The definition of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence requires the notions of coupling and
1
-Wasserstein distance between probability measures. Let
X,Y
be metric spaces.
For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(Y),
we denote by
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
the space of
couplings
between
\mu
and
\nu
, namely, the set of all the probability measures
q\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y)
satisfying

q(A\times Y)=\mu(A),\quad q(X\times B)=\nu(B),

for any measurable subsets
A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
. The
1
-Wasserstein distance between
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
is defined to be

{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{q\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}{\rm dist}(x,y)\,dq(x,y).

By the Kantorovich-Rubinstein Theorem, this definition is equivalent to

{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{f}\left(\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu\right),

where the supremum is taken over all bounded
1
-Lipschitz functions
f
on
X.

It is to be noted that for any metric flow
\mathcal{X}
, the
1
-Wassernstein distance between two conjugate heat flows satisfies a monotonicity property (
[
Bam20b
, Proposition 3.24(2)]
), namely, for any conjugate heat flows
(\mu^{1}_{s})_{s\in I^{\prime}}
and
(\mu^{2}_{s})_{s\in I^{\prime\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{\mathcal{X}_{s}}(\mu^{1}_{2},\mu^{2}_{s})\quad\text{ is non-decreasing in }\quad s\in I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime}.

(2.15)

Consequently, for any
x_{1}
and
x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{\mathcal{X}_{s}}(\nu_{x_{1}\,|\,s},\nu_{x_{2}\,|\,s})\leq{\rm dist}_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})\quad\text{ for all }\quad s<t.

(2.16)

In fact, this monotonicity property is
a consequence of the prescribed gradient estimate in the definition of the metric flow (
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2(6)]
).

We now introduce the definition of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence within a correspondence, because this is the only version we shall use, and because it is essentially equivalent to the definition of
\mathbb{F}
-convergence itself. See more details in
[
Bam20b
, Section 6]
. Let
\displaystyle\big\{(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu_{t}^{i})_{t\in I^{\prime,i}})\big\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}
be a sequence of metric flow pairs over a finite interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. A correspondence
\mathfrak{C}
is a collection of complete and separable metric spaces
(Z_{t},{\rm dist}^{Z_{t}})_{t\in I}
together with isometric embeddings

3
Conjugate heat kernel estimates on noncompact manifolds

In this section, we shall verify that

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The proof is not essentially different from
[

[

In fact, by elementary computations, we have that

\Box v=|\nabla u|^{2}-2(t-\tfrac{1}{2})|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-2|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 0.

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 4\Big(u^{2}\log J-u^{2}\log u\Big)\leq C(J),

where

For the rest of the proof, the reader may follow

]

∎

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad\quad n+2(t-s^{\prime})\cdot\frac{C}{t-s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(f(\cdot,s^{\prime})-\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})}\left(f(\cdot,s^{\prime})-\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})-\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})}\bigg(f^{2}(\cdot,s^{\prime})-2f\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)+\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\bigg)d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})}\left(-f^{2}(\cdot,s^{\prime})+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\right)d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}\big(B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})\big)-\nu_{x,t\,|\,s^{\prime}}\big(B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})\big)\sup_{B_{s}(z,\tfrac{r}{3})}f^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\left(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})+\tfrac{n}{2}\right)^{2}-C.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s)\geq\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(t-s^{\prime})\geq-\beta.

This finishes the proof of the theorem.

5

6

\displaystyle u^{A}:=\Phi\circ f^{A}.

Then we have

\displaystyle u^{A}\in[\Phi(-A),\Phi(A)]\subset(0,1)\quad\text{ everywhere on }\quad M_{\infty},

(6.4)

\displaystyle\big|\,u-u^{A}\,\big|\leq 1-\Phi(A)=\Phi(-A)\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{g_{\infty,s}}(z_{\infty},A^{2}),

(6.5)

\displaystyle u^{A}\equiv 1/2\quad\text{ on }\quad M_{\infty}\setminus B_{g_{\infty,s}}(z_{\infty},2A^{2}),

(6.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f^{A}\leq\tfrac{2}{A^{2}}\sup\left|A\zeta\left(\tfrac{f}{A}\right)\right|+\sup|\eta|\cdot A\operatorname{Lip}\zeta\cdot\operatorname{Lip}\tfrac{f}{A}\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\tfrac{2}{A}.

(6.7)

Let us fix two points
x,y\in M_{\infty}
. By (
6.4
), (
6.5
), and the bounded convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{A\rightarrow\infty}u^{A}_{t}(x),\ \lim_{A\rightarrow\infty}u^{A}_{t}(y)=u_{t}(x),\ u_{t}(y),

(6.8)

where

u_{t}^{A}(\cdot):=\int_{M_{\infty}}u^{A}\,d\nu^{\infty}_{\cdot,t\,|\,s},\quad u_{t}(\cdot):=\int_{M_{\infty}}u\,d\nu^{\infty}_{\cdot,t\,|\,s}.

In view of (
6.6
), for all
i
large enough we may define

\displaystyle f_{i}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{A}\circ\Psi_{i}^{-1}\quad\text{ on }\quad V_{i}=\Psi_{i}(U_{i})

\displaystyle f_{i}^{A}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{ on }\quad M_{i}\setminus V_{i}.

\displaystyle u_{i}^{A}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Phi\circ f_{i}^{A}.

Then, by the smooth convergence (
6.3
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}f_{i}^{A}\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{2}{A}+\varepsilon_{i},

and consequently (c.f.
[
MZ21
, Theorem 3.1]
),

\displaystyle\Big|\,\Phi^{-1}\circ u^{A}_{i,t}(\Psi_{i}(x))-\Phi^{-1}\circ u^{A}_{i,t}(\Psi_{i}(y))\,\Big|

(6.9)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\left(\left(T^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\tfrac{2}{A}+\varepsilon_{i}\right)^{2}+t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm dist}_{g_{i,t}}\big(\Psi_{i}(x),\Psi_{i}(y)\big),

for all
i
large enough, where
u_{i,t}^{A}:=\int_{M_{i}}u_{i}^{A}d\nu^{i}_{\cdot,t\,|\,s}
. In view of (
6.4
) and Proposition
2.15
, the integral of
\int_{M_{i}}u_{i}^{A}d\nu^{i}_{\cdot,t\,|\,s}
is uniformly negligible outside a large disk. Then, by the locally smoothly convergence of the conjugate heat kernels, we have (c.f. the proof of (
B.9
))

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}u^{A}_{i,t}(\Psi_{i}(x)),\ \lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}u^{A}_{i,t}(\Psi_{i}(y))=u_{t}^{A}(x),\ u_{t}^{A}(y).

Hence, (
6.9
) implies that

\displaystyle\big|\Phi^{-1}\circ u_{t}^{A}(x)-\Phi^{-1}\circ u_{t}^{A}(y)\big|\leq\left(\left(T^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\tfrac{2}{A}\right)^{2}+t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm dist}_{g_{\infty,t}}(x,y).

Therefore, by (
6.8
) and taking
A\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\big|\Phi^{-1}\circ u_{t}(x)-\Phi^{-1}\circ u_{t}(y)\big|\leq\left(T+t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm dist}_{g_{\infty,t}}(x,y).

Since
x,y\in M_{\infty}
are arbitrary, we have verified
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.2(6)]
.

Finally, it is also easy to verify the
H_{n}
-concentration property for
\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
, since it is but a consequence of Fatou’s lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

There is a positive solution to the conjugate heat equation
v:M_{\infty}\times[a,b)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
coupled with
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
, satisfying

locally smoothly on

Proof.

Arguing in the same way as the proof of
[
Lu12
, Theorem 2.1]
, we can find a nonnegative solution
v:M_{\infty}\times[a,b)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the conjugate heat equation, such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{*}K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)\to v

(6.10)

locally smoothly on
M_{\infty}\times[a,b)
.

In this case, Theorem
B.1
does not imply that
\int v_{s}\,dg_{\infty,s}=1
as it did in the proof of formula (
B.5
). This is because the base point
(p_{i},0)
of the conjugate heat kernel
K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)
is not in the region of the Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton convergence. We first of all observe from (
6.3
) that, there is a positive function
C:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
with the following property: for any
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Rm_{g_{i}}\right|\leq C(r)\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{g_{i,b}}(z_{i},r)\times[a,b]

(6.11)

whenever
i
is large enough. Since we also have

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\Vol_{g_{i,b}}\big(B_{g_{i,b}}(z_{i},1)\big)=\Vol_{g_{\infty,b}}\big(B_{g_{\infty,b}}(z_{\infty},1)\big)>0,

we may apply Theorem
1.8
and the remark below it to the
H_{n}
-center
(z_{i},b)
of
(p_{i},0)
, and consequently, we can find a positive number
Y
independent of
i
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{i}_{p_{i},0}(|b|)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

where
\mathcal{N}^{i}
is the Nash entropy of the Ricci flow
(M_{i},g_{i}(t))
. The argument in Proposition
5.6
then implies that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{i,b}}(z_{i},p^{\prime}_{i})\leq C\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

(6.12)

where
(p^{\prime}_{i},b)
is an
\ell
-center of
(p_{i},0)
, and
C
is a constant depending only on
Y
.

Now we will use (
2.11
) to obtain a uniform lower bound for
K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,p^{\prime}_{i},b-\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is an arbitrarily fixed number in
(0,b-a]
. Let us fix such an
\varepsilon
. (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) imply

\sup_{t\in[a,b]}\left|R_{g_{i}}(p^{\prime}_{i},t)\right|\leq C\quad\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N},

where
C
is a constant independent of
i
.
We may concatenate a minimal
\mathcal{L}
-geodesic from
(p_{i},0)
to
(p^{\prime}_{i},b)
and
the static curve from
(p^{\prime}_{i},b)

to
(p^{\prime}_{i},b-\varepsilon)
as a test curve in (
2.9
). This yields

\displaystyle\ell_{p_{i},0}(p^{\prime}_{i},|b|+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|b|+\varepsilon}}\left(2\sqrt{|b|}\ell_{p_{i},0}(p^{\prime}_{i},|b|)+\int_{|b|}^{|b|+\varepsilon}\sqrt{\tau}R_{g_{i}}(p^{\prime}_{i},-\tau)\,d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|b|+\varepsilon}}\left(2\sqrt{|b|}\cdot\frac{n}{2}+C(|b|+\varepsilon)^{3/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, by (
2.11
), we have

K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,p^{\prime}_{i},b-\varepsilon)\geq\frac{1}{4\pi(|b|+\varepsilon)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\ell_{p_{i},0}(p^{\prime}_{i},|b|+\varepsilon)}\geq c(\varepsilon)>0\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

By (
6.12
) again, we may find a point
p^{\prime}_{\infty}\in M_{\infty}
, such that
\Psi_{i}^{-1}(p^{\prime}_{i})\rightarrow p^{\prime}_{\infty}
after possibly passing to a subsequence. Consequently

v(p^{\prime}_{\infty},b-\varepsilon)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,p^{\prime}_{i},b-\varepsilon)\geq c(\varepsilon)>0.

Since
\varepsilon\in(0,b-a]
is arbitrary, it then follows from the strong maximum principle that

everywhere on
M_{\infty}\times[a,b)
.

Once we know that

is positive, by exactly the same argument as in the proof of (
B.5
), we have

\int_{M_{\infty}}v_{s}\,dg_{\infty,s}=1,

for any
s\in[a,b).

Indeed, here one may use the fact that

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{g_{i,s}}(z_{i},1)}K_{i}(p_{i},0\,|\,\cdot,s)dg_{i,s}=\int_{B_{g_{\infty,s}}(z_{\infty},1)}v(\cdot,s)dg_{\infty,s}>0

to show that
(z_{i},s)
is not far from an
H_{n}
-center of
(p_{i},0)
, for any
s\in[a,b)
, and the rest of the argument is the same as the proof of (
B.5
).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in I}
be a complete Ricci flow over a compact interval
I
and
o\in M
.
Then for any
A>0,\Omega:=\cup_{t\in I}\bar{B}_{t}(o,A)
is compact.

Proof.

Let
x_{j}\in\bar{B}_{s_{j}}(o,A)\subset\Omega
be an arbitrary sequence. Assume that
s_{j}\to\bar{s}\in I.
We shall prove that
\{x_{j}\}
has a convergent subsequence.
Suppose that

\sup_{B_{\bar{s}}(o,10A)\times I}|{\Ric}|\leq\Lambda.

We claim that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1),
there is
\bar{j},
such that if
j\geq\bar{j},

x_{j}\in B_{\bar{s}}(o,(1+\epsilon)A)
.
Suppose not. Then by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that there are
g(s_{j})
-minimal geodesics
\gamma_{j}:[0,\sigma_{j}]\to M
with

\gamma_{j}(0)=o,\gamma_{j}(\sigma_{j})=x_{j},\sigma_{j}<A
but there is a first time
\lambda_{j}<\sigma_{j}
such that
\gamma_{j}(\lambda_{j})\in\partial B_{\bar{s}}(o,(1+\epsilon/2)A).

Pick
\delta>0
such that

When
|s_{j}-\bar{s}|<\delta
, we have

\displaystyle A\geq L_{s_{j}}(\gamma_{j}|_{[0,\lambda_{j}]})

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\Lambda|s_{j}-\bar{s}|}L_{\bar{s}}(\gamma_{j}|_{[0,\lambda_{j}]})\geq(1-\epsilon/4){\rm dist}_{\bar{s}}(o,\gamma_{j}(\lambda_{j}))

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon/4)(1+\epsilon/2)A>(1+\epsilon/8)A,

which is a contradiction. Hence, after passing to a subsequence, we have
x_{j}\to\bar{x}
for some
\bar{x}\in\bar{B}_{\bar{s}}(o,A).

∎

8

In this section, we prove Corollary

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}}(\tau)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)\geq-Y>-\infty.

It then follows from Proposition
5.6
that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{i,-1}}(p_{i},z_{i}),\ {\rm dist}_{g^{\prime}_{i,-1}}(p^{\prime}_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})\leq C,

where

is independent of
i
. Hence, to prove (
8.1
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{i,-1}}(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i}),\ {\rm dist}_{g^{\prime}_{i,-1}}(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})\leq C,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})\leq C\sqrt{\tau_{i}},

(8.2)

for some constant
C
independent of
i
.

Let us prove (
8.2
) by contradiction. Assume that there are positive numbers
A_{i}\nearrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i},z_{i}^{\prime})\geq 10\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}}.

Then, whenever
i
is large enough, we may find a nonnegative
1
-Lipschitz function
\varphi_{i}
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\varphi_{i}
is compactly supported on
B_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i},2\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}})
,

(2)

\varphi_{i}\geq\sqrt{A_{i}H_{i}\tau_{i}}
on
B_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i},\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}})
,

(3)

0\leq\varphi_{i}\leq 2\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}}
everywhere,

(4)

the support of
\varphi_{i}
is disjoint from
B_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z^{\prime}_{i},\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau^{\prime}_{i}})
.

Using
\varphi_{i}
as a test function, we may compute

\displaystyle\quad\quad{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,-\tau_{i}},\nu_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}\,|\,-\tau_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\varphi_{i}d\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,-\tau_{i}}-\int_{M}\varphi_{i}d\nu_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}\,|\,-\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i},\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}})}\varphi_{i}d\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,-\tau_{i}}-\int_{M\setminus B_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z^{\prime}_{i},\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau^{\prime}_{i}})}\varphi_{i}d\nu_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}\,|\,-\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{A_{i}}\right)\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}}-\frac{1}{A_{i}}2\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{3}{A_{i}}\right)\sqrt{A_{i}H_{n}\tau_{i}}\rightarrow\infty,

as
i\rightarrow\infty
; here we have applied Proposition
2.15
. On the other hand, by (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle d_{W_{1}}^{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,-\tau_{i}},\nu_{p^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0}\,|\,-\tau_{i}})\leq d_{W_{1}}^{g_{t^{\prime}_{0}}}(\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,t^{\prime}_{0}},\delta_{p^{\prime}_{0}})<\infty.

This is a contradiction, and (
8.2
) follows; we have finished the proof of the theorem.
∎

. As before, we still consider an ancient solution

Lemma 8.2

\displaystyle V_{\infty}:=\int_{M_{\infty}}(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{\infty}(\cdot,|t|)}dg_{\infty,t}\equiv\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{V}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\infty}}(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{\infty}(\cdot,|t|)}dg_{\infty,t}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}dg_{i,t},

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B_{g_{i,t}}(z_{i},\sqrt{AH_{n}|t|})}(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}dg_{i,t}\leq\int_{M\setminus B_{g_{i,t}}(z_{i},\sqrt{AH_{n}|t|})}u_{i}(\cdot,t)dg_{i,t}\leq\frac{1}{A},

∎

Proof of Corollary

By

) and Assumption B with respect to

Let us fix an arbitrary

Proposition 9.4

For any

\displaystyle u(x,t)\geq c(r,\varepsilon)\tau_{i}^{-\frac{n}{2}}\quad\text{ for all }\quad(x,t)\in B_{-\tau_{i}}(p_{i},r\sqrt{\tau_{i}})\times[-2\tau_{i},-(1+\varepsilon)\tau_{i}].

\displaystyle{\rm dist}^{g_{-\tau_{i}}}_{W_{1}}(\nu_{y,t_{0}\,|\,-\tau_{i}},\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,-\tau_{i}})

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{t_{0}}(x_{0},y)+{\rm dist}^{g_{t_{0}}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{x_{0}},\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,t_{0}})\leq C,

and

) also implies the existence of a positive function

(9.6)

Next, we prove a Gaussian upper bound for

Proposition 9.5

According to the scaling property of the

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g(\tau_{i}t),u(\cdot,\tau_{i}t),T-\tau_{i}t)=\overline{\mathcal{W}}\left(\tau_{i}^{-1}g(\tau_{i}t),\tau_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}}u(\cdot,\tau_{i}t),\tfrac{T}{\tau_{i}}-t\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}{\rm dist}_{g_{i,t}}^{2}(p_{i},x)-C\quad\text{ for all }\quad(x,t)\in M\times[-2,-1],

\displaystyle f_{\infty}(x,t)

.

For all

Proposition 9.10

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{0}-}\int_{M}u(\cdot,t)\log u(\cdot,t)\,dg_{t}=\int_{M}u_{0}\log u_{0}\,dg_{t_{0}}.

10
Synthesis with the classical cases

In this section, we consider some classical cases in which Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker exists. We shall see that, in all these cases, Assumption B is satisfied. Hence, Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker is identical to Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity. We consider an ancient solution
(M,g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
with bounded curvature within each compact time interval, satisfying
either one
of the following conditions:

(1)

g(t)
satisfies a Type I curvature bound, that is, there is a constant
C
such that

|\Rm_{g(t)}|\leq\frac{C}{|t|}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in(-\infty,0).

(2)

g(t)
satisfies Hamilton’s trace Harnack, that is,

\frac{\partial R}{\partial t}+2\langle X,\nabla R\rangle+2\Ric(X,X)\geq 0\quad\text{ for all vector field }X,

and there is a constant
C
such that

|\Rm|\leq CR\quad\text{ everywhere on }\quad M\times(-\infty,0].

Except for the assumption above, we would also like to impose a
\kappa
-noncollapsing assumption. This is actually equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau>0,

where
(p_{0},t_{0})
is an arbitrarily fixed point on
M\times(-\infty,0]
.

Theorem 10.1
.

Under the assumptions of this section, Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker is identical to Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity.

Proof.

Let us fixed a point
p_{0}\in M
and a sequence
\tau_{i}\nearrow\infty
. Let
\ell
be the reduced distance based at
(p_{0},0)
and let
\{(p_{i},-\tau_{i})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
\ell
-centers of
(p_{0},0)
. We will still use
\ell_{i}(\cdot,\tau):=\ell_{i}(\cdot,\tau_{i}\tau)
and
g_{i}(t):=\tau_{i}^{-1}g(\tau_{i}t)
to denote the scaled reduced distances and the scaled Ricci flows. Note that
\ell_{i}
is the reduced distance based at
(p_{0},0)
with respect to the flow
g_{i}
. Recall that by
[
Per02
]
and
[
N10
]
, the following formulas hold everywhere on
M\times(-\infty,0)
in the barrier sense or in the sense of distribution

\displaystyle\big|\nabla\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)\big|^{2}+R_{i}(\cdot,t)\leq\frac{C\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}{|t|},

(10.1)

\displaystyle-2\frac{\partial\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}{\partial t}+\big|\nabla\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)\big|^{2}-R_{i}(\cdot,t)+\frac{\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}{|t|}=0.

(10.2)

Combining (
10.1
) and (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}{\partial t}\right|\leq\frac{C\ell_{i}(\cdot,|t|)}{|t|}.

Since

, there is a constant

\displaystyle\int_{-\delta_{i}^{-1}}^{-\delta_{i}}\int_{M}|t|\left|\,\Ric_{i}+\nabla^{2}f_{i}-\frac{1}{2|t|}g_{i}\,\right|^{2}d\nu^{i}_{t}dt<\delta_{i},

(11.4)

where

d\nu^{i}_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{i}}dg_{i,t}.

Since, by
[
Bam20b
, Theorem 9.31]
, the conjugate heat kernel and the Ricci flow converge smoothly along the sequence, we may take a limit for (
11.4
) and apply Fatou’s lemma to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\delta^{-1}}^{-\delta}\int_{M}|t|\left|\Ric_{\infty}+\nabla^{2}f_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2|t|}g_{\infty}\right|^{2}d\nu^{\infty}_{t}dt=0,\quad\text{ for any }\quad\delta\in(0,1),

where

d\nu^{\infty}_{t}:=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{\infty}}dg_{\infty,t}.

This finishes the proof Theorem
1.3
.

Appendix A

We prove Proposition

Let

, where we have denoted

.

(

,

,

,

Proof.

First, we prove the lower bound.

, Theorem 26.31]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\min\left\{\frac{1}{\Vol_{\bar{g}}B_{\bar{g}}\left(x,\sqrt{1+t}\right)},\frac{1}{\Vol_{\bar{g}}B_{\bar{g}}\left(y,\sqrt{1+t}\right)}\right\}\exp\left(-\frac{{\rm dist}_{\bar{g}}^{2}(x,y)}{c(1+t)}\right)

Let
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
be the standard decreasing smooth cutoff function such that
\eta|_{[0,1]}=1
,
\eta|_{[2,\infty)}=0
, and

Let us define a time-dependent cutoff function

for all

By the smoothness and completeness of the Ricci flow, we can find a positive number

, such that

\Ric\leq(n-1)\rho_{0}^{-2},\quad\text{ on }\quad\bigcup_{t\in[-1,0]}B_{g_{t}}(o,\rho_{0})\times\{t\}.

According to

\Box{\rm dist}_{t}(o,\cdot)\geq-\tfrac{5}{3}(n-1)\rho_{0}^{-1}\quad\text{ on }\quad M\setminus B_{t}(o,\rho_{0})

Let

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int U_{t}\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{t}=\int\Box(U_{t}\phi^{A})\,d\nu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int\left(-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\phi^{A}-2\langle\nabla U_{t},\nabla\phi^{A}\rangle+U_{t}\Box\phi^{A}\right)d\nu_{t},

where we have used the fact
\Box(u\log u)=-\tfrac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}

-1
to

\displaystyle\int U_{-1}\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{-1}-U_{0}(o)

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{0}\int\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{t}dt+2\int_{-1}^{0}\int\langle\nabla U_{t},\nabla\phi^{A}\rangle\,d\nu_{t}dt-\int_{-1}^{0}\int U_{t}\Box\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{t}dt

We shall estimate

Note that here we have only one-side bound for

, yet it is sufficient for our purpose. Now we consider

By the gradient estimate of heat equation

Zhq07
,
BCP10
]
(c.f.

) and the Shi-type estimates, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1+t}\log\frac{D_{0}}{u}\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times(-1,0],

\displaystyle|\nabla u|\leq\Lambda_{1},\quad|\nabla^{2}u|\leq\Lambda_{2}\quad\text{ on }\quad M\times[-1,0],

Thus, by (

) we have, for all

\displaystyle\int\langle\nabla U_{t},\nabla\phi^{A}\rangle\,d\nu_{t}

Letting

Integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\int\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}(y,s)\phi^{A}(y,s)\,d\nu_{x,t|s}(y)=\int\Box\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\phi^{A}\right)\,d\nu_{x,t|s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int\Box\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{x,t|s}-2\int\nabla\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\cdot\nabla\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{x,t|s}+\int\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\Box\phi^{A}\,d\nu_{x,t|s}.

Appendix B
Convergence of heat kernels.

Let
\{(M_{i},g_{i}(t),o_{i})_{t\in(-T_{i},0]}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complete Ricci flows with base point, and assume that each Ricci flow therein has bounded curvature within every compact time interval. Assume moreover that this sequence converges to
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),o)_{t\in(-T_{\infty},0]}
in the pointed smooth Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton sense, where
T_{\infty}=\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}T_{i}\in(0,\infty]
. Note that we do not make any assumption on the limit
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),o)_{t\in(-T_{\infty},0]}
.

By the definition of smooth convergence, we may find an increasing sequence of pre-compact open sets
U_{i}\subset M_{\infty}
with
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}=M_{\infty}
, a sequence of diffeomorphisms
\Psi_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}\subset M_{i}
, and a sequence of positive numbers
\varepsilon_{i}\searrow 0
, such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}(o)=o_{i},

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{i}^{*}g_{i}-g_{\infty}\|_{C^{[\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}]}(U_{i}\times[-T_{\infty}+\varepsilon_{i},0])}<\varepsilon_{i},

where we let
-T_{\infty}+\varepsilon_{i}=-\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}
in the case
T_{\infty}=\infty
.

Let
K_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s)
be the heat kernel coupled with
(M_{i},g_{i}(t))
as introduces in section 2.1. For any
x,y\in U_{i}
and for any
-T_{\infty}+\varepsilon_{i}\leq s<t\leq 0
, we shall define

\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s):=(\Psi_{i}^{*}K_{i})(x,t\,|\,y,s)=K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\Psi_{i}(y),s).

As a slight generalization of
[
Lu12
]
, we shall prove the following.

.

There is a positive heat kernel
K_{\infty}
coupled with
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
, such that, after passing to a subsequence, we have

\bar{K}_{i}\to K_{\infty},

C_{c}^{\infty}
-topology, and the convergence is uniform on any compact subset of

\mathcal{M}:=\big\{\,(x,t,y,s)\,\big|\,x,y\in M_{\infty},s,t\in(-T_{\infty},0],s<t\,\big\}.

For any
A\gg 1,
we define

\mathcal{M}_{A}:=\left\{(x,t,y,s)\in\mathcal{M}\,\Big|\,x,y\in B_{g_{\infty,0}}(o,A),-T_{\infty}+\tfrac{1}{A^{2}}\leq s\leq t-\tfrac{1}{A^{2}}<t\leq 0\right\},

.

(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}

, we have

|\nabla^{k}\Rm_{g_{\infty}}|\leq\Lambda_{k},\quad\text{ on }\quad B_{g_{\infty,0}}(o,10A)\times[-T_{\infty}+\tfrac{1}{A^{2}},0],

\Lambda_{k}=\Lambda_{k}(A)
.
Let us define

\bar{g}:=g_{\infty}(0),\quad\bar{g}_{i}(t):=\Psi_{i}^{*}g_{i}(t),

where we will use
\bar{g}
as the background metric on
M_{\infty}
. By the smooth convergence of the Ricci flows, we may find a large integer
\bar{i}
depending on
A
, such that whenver

, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\Rm_{\bar{g}_{i}}|\leq 2\Lambda_{k},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\quad B_{\bar{g}}(o,10A)\times[-T_{\infty}+\tfrac{1}{A^{2}},0],

(B.1)

\displaystyle C_{0}^{-1}\bar{g}\leq\bar{g}_{i}\leq C_{0}\bar{g},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\quad B_{\bar{g}}(o,10A)\times[-T_{\infty}+\tfrac{1}{A^{2}},0],

(B.2)

where

depends only on
\Lambda_{0}
,

, and

.
Let

, then for any

\displaystyle{\rm Vol}_{\bar{g}}\left[B_{\bar{g}}(x,r_{0})\right]\geq c_{1}{\rm Vol}_{\bar{g}}\left[B_{\bar{g}}(o,r_{0})\right]\geq c_{0}>0,

(B.3)

where

depends on

\Lambda_{0}
,

Let us use

to denote the backward and forward parabolic disk. Then, fixing any
(x_{*},t_{*},y_{*},s_{*})\in\mathcal{M}_{A}
, by the mean value inequality
[
CCGGIIKLLN10
, Theorem 25.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},r_{0})\times P^{+}(y_{*},s_{*},r_{0})}\bar{K}_{i}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup_{(x,t)\in P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},r_{0})}C\int_{P^{+}(y_{*},s_{*},2r_{0})}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)\,d\bar{g}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sup_{(x,t)\in P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},r_{0})}C\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{*}+4r_{0}^{2}}\int_{\Psi_{i}(B_{\bar{g}}(y_{*},2r_{0}))}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,y,s)\,dg_{i,s}(y)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,t)\in P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},r_{0})}C\int_{s_{*}}^{s_{*}+4r_{0}^{2}}\int_{M_{i}}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,y,s)\,dg_{i,s}(y)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C,

where
C
depends on
c_{0}
in (
B.3
),
\Lambda_{0}
,
A
, and
C_{0}
in (
B.2
). In particular,
C
is independent of
i\geq\bar{i}
and the point
(x_{*},t_{*},y_{*},s_{*})
. Here we have used the fact that
\int_{M_{i}}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,y,s)\,dg_{i,s}(y)\equiv 1
.

Next, fixing an arbitrary
(y,s)\in P^{+}(y_{*},s_{*},r_{0})
, we have, by the standard gradient estimate of the heat equation,

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,t)\in P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{0})}\big\|\,\partial^{k}_{t}\nabla^{l}_{x}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\,\big\|_{\bar{g}}\leq C_{k,l},

for all
k,l\geq 0
. Here the constant
C_{k,l}
depends on
C_{0}
in (
B.2
),
C
in (
B.4
),
\Lambda_{k+l}
, and
A
. In particular,
C_{k,l}
is independent of
i\geq\bar{i}
and the choice of the point
(y,s)\in P^{+}(y_{*},s_{*},r_{0})
. By using this estimate and the standard gradient estimate of the conjugate heat equation

\displaystyle\Box^{*}_{\bar{g}_{i,s},y,s}\Big(\partial^{k}_{t}\nabla^{l}_{x}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\Big)=0,

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,t,y,s)\in P^{-}(x_{*},t_{*},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{0})\times P^{+}(y_{*},s_{*},\tfrac{1}{2}r_{0})}\big\|\,\partial^{m}_{s}\nabla^{n}_{y}\partial^{k}_{t}\nabla^{l}_{x}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\,\big\|_{\bar{g}}\leq C_{k,l,m,n},

where
C_{k,l,m,n}
depends on
C_{0}
in (
B.2
),
C_{k,l}
,
A
, and
\Lambda_{m+n}
. In particular,
C_{k,l,m,n}
is independent of
i\geq\bar{i}
and the choice of the point
(x_{*},t_{*},y_{*},s_{*})\in\mathcal{M}_{A}
. Therefore, we have

\sup_{(x,t,y,s)\in\mathcal{M}_{A}}\big\|\,\partial^{m}_{s}\nabla^{n}_{y}\partial^{k}_{t}\nabla^{l}_{x}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\,\big\|_{\bar{g}}\leq C_{k,l,m,n},

for all
i\geq\bar{i}
and for all
k,l,m,n\geq 0
.

Finally, according to the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, by passing to a subsequence, we can find a smooth function
K_{\infty}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, such that

\bar{K}_{i}\to K_{\infty}

smoothly on compact subsets of
\mathcal{M}
. As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\Box_{g_{\infty,t},x,t}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\equiv 0,\quad\Box_{g_{\infty,s},y,s}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,y,s)\equiv 0\quad\text{ on }\quad\mathcal{M}.

The convergence of
\bar{K}_{i}
also implies that, fixing any
(x,t)
or
(y,s)\in M_{\infty}\times(-T_{\infty},0]
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)\rightarrow K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ locally smoothly on }\quad M_{\infty}\times(-T_{\infty},t),

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{i}(\cdot,\cdot\,|\,y,s)\rightarrow K_{\infty}(\cdot,\cdot\,|\,y,s)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ locally smoothly on }\quad M_{\infty}\times(s,0].

One may then easily apply the same arguments in
[
Lu12
, Section 2.5]
to show that

\lim_{s\to t^{-}}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)=\delta_{x},\quad\lim_{t\to s^{+}}K_{\infty}(\cdot,t\,|\,y,s)=\delta_{y},

in the sense of distributions.
The strong maximum principle then implies that
K_{\infty}>0
everywhere on
\mathcal{M}.

∎

Theorem B.2
.

For any
(x,t)\in M_{\infty}\times(-T_{\infty},0]
, we have
K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)dg_{\infty,s}\in\mathcal{P}(M_{\infty})
for all
s\in(-\infty,t]
. Furthermore,
K_{\infty}
satisfies the reproduction formula, that is,

\displaystyle\int_{S}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s^{\prime})dg_{\infty,s^{\prime}}=\int_{M_{\infty}}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,z,s)\left(\int_{S}K_{\infty}(z,s\,|\,y,s^{\prime})dg_{\infty,s^{\prime}}(y)\right)dg_{\infty,s}(z).

for all
x\in M
,
-T_{\infty}<s^{\prime}<s<t\leq 0
, and for all measurable set
S\subset M_{\infty}

Proof.

We will still use the same notation as in the proof of the former Theorem. Let us fix
(x,t)\in M_{\infty}\times(-T_{\infty},0]
and
s\in(-T_{\infty},0)
. We will prove

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\infty}}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}=1.

(B.5)

By Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\infty}}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{U_{i}}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,d\bar{g}_{i,s}

(B.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Psi_{i}(U_{i})}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{i,s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M_{i}}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{i,s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

To show the other direction of the inequality, we shall apply Proposition
2.15
. By the locally smooth convergence of
\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{g_{i,s}}(\Psi_{i}(x),1)}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{i,s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i,s}}(x,1)}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,d\bar{g}_{i,s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g_{\infty,s}}(x,1)}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

Hence, there is a positive constant
c_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g_{i,s}}(\Psi_{i}(x),1)}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{i,s}\geq c_{0}\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, by Proposition
2.15
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{i,s}}(z_{i},\Psi_{i}(x))\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{c_{0}}H(t-s)}+1\leq C\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

(B.7)

where
(z_{i},s)
is an
H_{n}
-center of
(\Psi_{i}(x),t)
with respect to the Ricci flow
(M_{i},g_{i})
.

In view of
(\ref{theHncenterdoesnotgoaway})
, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume
z^{\prime}_{i}:=\Psi_{i}^{-1}(z_{i})\rightarrow z^{\prime}_{\infty}\in M_{\infty}
. Hence, fixing any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g_{\infty,s}}\left(z^{\prime}_{\infty},\sqrt{\delta^{-1}H_{n}(t-s)}\right)}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}

(B.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i,s}}\left(z^{\prime}_{i},\sqrt{\delta^{-1}H_{n}(t-s)}\right)}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,d\bar{g}_{i,s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{g_{i,s}}\left(z^{\prime}_{i},\sqrt{\delta^{-1}H_{n}(t-s)}\right)}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{i,s}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ 1-\delta.

Combining (
B.6
) and (
B.8
), we have proved (
B.5
).

Next, we prove the reproduction formula. Let us fix
x\in M_{\infty}
,
-T_{\infty}<s^{\prime}<s<t\leq 0
, and measurable set
S\subset M_{\infty}
. Let us first of all consider the case when
S
is bounded. Then we have the functions

\displaystyle u_{i}:z\rightarrow\int_{S}\bar{K}_{i}(z,s\,|\,y,s^{\prime})\,d\bar{g}_{i,s^{\prime}}(y)

converge to the function

\displaystyle u_{\infty}:z\rightarrow\int_{S}K_{\infty}(z,s\,|\,y,s^{\prime})\,dg_{\infty,s^{\prime}}(y)

pointwise and with the uniform upper bound
1
.

Finally, in view (
B.8
), fixing any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have, by the bounded convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\infty}}u_{\infty}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}

(B.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g_{\infty,s}}\left(z^{\prime}_{\infty},\sqrt{\delta^{-1}H_{n}(t-s)}\right)}u_{\infty}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{\infty,s}+\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{\bar{g}_{i,s}}\left(z^{\prime}_{i},\sqrt{\delta^{-1}H_{n}(t-s)}\right)}u_{i}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,d\bar{g}_{i,s}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M_{i}}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t\,|\,\cdot,s)\left(\int_{\Psi_{i}(S)}K_{i}(\cdot,s\,|\,y,s^{\prime})\,dg_{i,s^{\prime}}(y)\right)\,dg_{i,s}+\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Psi_{i}(S)}K_{i}(\Psi_{i}(x),t|\cdot,s^{\prime})\,dg_{i,s^{\prime}}+\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S}\bar{K}_{i}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s^{\prime})\,d\bar{g}_{i,s^{\prime}}+\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}K_{\infty}(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s^{\prime})\,dg_{\infty,s^{\prime}}+\delta,

where we have applied the reproduction property of
K_{i}
, which is but standard for Ricci flows with bounded curvature
[
CCGGIIKLLN10
, Lemmas 26.8 and 26.16]
. Taking
\delta\rightarrow 0
, the case when
S
is bounded is done. If
S
is unbounded, then one can consider
S_{i}:=S\cap B_{g_{\infty,s^{\prime}}}(x,i)
and take
i\rightarrow\infty
, then the conclusion follows from the bounded convergence theorem.

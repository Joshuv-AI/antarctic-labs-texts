A lower bound on the top betti number of the Milnor fiber
David B. Massey

Abstract
. We derive a lower bound for the top possibly-non-zero betti number of the Milnor fiber of an analytic function in terms of the zero-dimensional Lê number and the internal monodromy of the vanishing cycles restricted to the complex link of the critical locus of the function.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32S25, 32S15, 32S55

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
. In order to have a convenient point to discuss, we assume that
{\mathbf{0}}\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Let
f:({\mathcal{U}},{\mathbf{0}})\rightarrow({\mathbb{C}},0)
be a nowhere-locally-constant complex analytic function. We let
\Sigma f
denote the critical locus of
f
and note that, near
{\mathbf{0}}
, this is contained in
V(f)
.

For
x\in V(f)
, we denote the Milnor fiber of
f
at
x
by
F_{f,x}
. We will use a field
\mathfrak{K}
as our base ring for cohomology (and for perverse sheaves). The “top” betti number
\tilde{b}_{n}
in the title refers to the dimension of
\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};\mathfrak{K})
, since
n
is the highest degree for which the cohomology may be non-zero (note that this is a non-traditional use of the term “betti number” unless
\mathfrak{K}
has characteristic zero).

We will use the derived category and perverse sheaves; references are
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
10
]
. We need to use field coefficients since our proof uses a perverse sheaf version of the fact the dimension of the cokernel of a linear transformation between finite-dimensional vector spaces is equal to the dimension of the kernel of the dual transformation.

Let
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
denote the shifted vanishing cycles
\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
along
f
. Thus
\tilde{b}_{n}=\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{\mathbf{0}}
. Let
L
be the restriction to
V(f)
of a generic linear form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
; we need for
L
to be generic enough so that
{\mathbf{0}}
is an isolated point in the support of
\phi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
. Then the stalk cohomology at the origin is zero outside of degree zero; we define
\lambda^{0}:=\lambda^{0}_{f}({\mathbf{0}})=\dim H^{0}(\phi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{\mathbf{0}}
.

If
L
is sufficiently generic,
\lambda^{0}
is the
0
-th Lê number, as defined algebraically in
[
6
]
as the intersection number

\lambda^{0}=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,L}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial L}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}},

where
\Gamma^{1}_{f,L}
is the relative polar curve of Hamm, Lê, and Teissier from
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
. Note that, while
\tilde{b}_{n}
can change with the base field,
\lambda^{0}
is independent of the field
\mathfrak{K}
.

In Theorem
3.1
, we give a lower bound - in terms of
\lambda^{0}
and the internal monodromy - for
\tilde{b}_{n}
, for an
f
where the critical locus has arbitrary dimension.
T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
is the
L
-monodromy automorphism on
\psi_{L}[-1]\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
(letting the value of
L
, not
f
, go around a small circle). We denote the image of
\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
(in the abelian category of perverse sheaves) by
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
; note that, as we shall discuss later,
{\mathbf{0}}
is an isolated point in the support of this image.

The first version of our main theorem is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Regardless of the dimension of
\Sigma f
,

\frac{\lambda^{0}-\dim\big(H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}\big)}{2}\ \leq\ \tilde{b}_{n}\ \leq\ \lambda^{0}-\dim\big(H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}\big).

In particular, if
T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}=\operatorname{id}
, then
\lambda^{0}/2\leq\tilde{b}_{n}\leq\lambda^{0}
.

In Section 2, we prove several lemmas that we need. In Section 3, we prove the main theorem, Theorem
3.1
, and give an example of the well-studied case where
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=1
. In Section 4, we analyze the case where
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f>1
more carefully and present Theorem
4.2
, the second version of our main theorem, in which we make the calculation of
H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}
more manageable.

2.
Lemmas

We continue with our base ring being the field
\mathfrak{K}
. Let
X
be a complex analytic space. The category
\operatorname{Perv}(X)
of perverse sheaves on
X
is abelian.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x\in X
and suppose that
x
is an isolated point in the support of
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\in\operatorname{Perv}(X)
, which implies that the stalk cohomology
H^{k}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}
is zero if
k\neq 0
.

Suppose we have maps of perverse sheaves
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
and
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
. Let
\alpha_{x}^{k}
and
\beta_{x}^{k}
denote the corresponding maps on stalk cohomology:

\alpha^{k}_{x}:H^{k}(\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})_{x}\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\beta^{k}_{x}:H^{k}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathbf{B}^{\bullet})_{x}.

Then,

(1)

x
is an isolated point in the supports of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha
,
{\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha
,
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\beta
,
\ker\beta
, and
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha)
;

(2)

H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)_{x}\cong\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\alpha^{0}_{x})
and
H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)_{x}\cong{\operatorname{coker}\,}(\alpha^{0}_{x})
; and

(3)

H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\beta)_{x}\cong\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta^{0}_{x})
and
H^{0}(\ker\beta)_{x}\cong\ker(\beta^{0}_{x})
.

Proof.

We will prove the statements regarding
\alpha
. The proofs of the statements regarding
\beta
are completely analogous; we leave them as an exercise. That
x
is isolated in
\operatorname{supp}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha)
is immediate from
x
being isolated in
\operatorname{supp}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha
(or in
\operatorname{supp}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha)
).

We have the canonical exact sequence in
\operatorname{Perv}(X)
:

0\rightarrow\ker\alpha\rightarrow\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha,

which we can split into two short exact sequences

(1)

0\rightarrow\ker\alpha\rightarrow\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\sigma}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha\rightarrow 0\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt0\rightarrow\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha\rightarrow 0,

where
\alpha=\tau\circ\sigma
. From the second short exact sequence above, if we restrict to
{\mathcal{U}}-\{x\}
where
{\mathcal{U}}
is a small open neighborhood of
x
, we obtain
0
in the middle; this shows that
x
is an isolated point in the supports of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha
and
{\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha
. From this, we immediately conclude that we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)_{x}\xrightarrow{\tau^{0}_{x}}H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}\rightarrow H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)_{x}\rightarrow 0.

Now the first short exact sequence in (
1
) gives us the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{0}(\ker\alpha)_{x}\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbf{A}^{\bullet})_{x}\xrightarrow{\sigma^{0}_{x}}H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)_{x}\rightarrow 0.

Combining these two last short exact sequences and using that
\alpha^{0}_{x}=\tau^{0}_{x}\circ\sigma^{0}_{x}
, we conclude the isomorphisms in Item 2.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

The reader should appreciate that there really was something to prove in Lemma
2.1
; some things which may seem “obvious” are, in fact, not true. Suppose we have a map
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\gamma}\mathbf{B}^{\bullet}
between perverse sheaves on
X
such that a point
x\in X
is isolated in the support of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\gamma
. Then, in general,
H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\gamma)_{x}\not\cong\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\gamma^{0}_{x})
. See Example 3.3 in
[
8
]
.

Below, we use the notation from Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
and
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
be perverse sheaves on
X
. Let
x\in X
and suppose that
x
is an isolated point in the support of
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
. Suppose that we have maps
\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathbf{A}^{\bullet}
such that
H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)_{x}\cong H^{0}(\ker\beta)_{x}
.

Then,

(1)

\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}(\alpha^{0}_{x}))=\dim H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)_{x},

(2)

x
is an isolated point in the support of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha)
, and

(3)

\frac{\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))_{x}}{2}\leq\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}(\alpha^{0}_{x}))\leq\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))_{x}.

Proof.

Items 1 and 2 follow from Lemma
2.1
.

In the remainder of this proof, to eliminate cumbersome notation, when
x
is an isolated point in the support of some perverse sheaf
\mathbf{F}^{\bullet}
, we shall write simply
\dim\mathbf{F}^{\bullet}
in place of
\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{F}^{\bullet})_{x}
.

The proof is really just an exercise in linear algebra.

The second inequality is the most simple.

\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))\leq\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\beta)=\dim\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}-\dim(\ker\beta)=\dim\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}-\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha).

For the first inequality, we have:

\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha)=\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta_{|_{\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha}})\cong\frac{\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha}{\ker\beta\cap\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha}.

\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))=\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)-\dim(\ker\beta\cap\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)\geq\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)-\dim(\ker\beta).

So, using that
\dim{\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha\cong\dim\ker\beta
,

\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))\geq\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)-\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha).

-\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))\leq-\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)+\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha).

\dim\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}-\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))\leq\dim\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}-\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\alpha)+\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)=2\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha).

\dim({\operatorname{coker}\,}\alpha)\geq\frac{\dim\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}-\dim(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))}{2}.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

Recall our warning from Remark
2.2
. It could easily be that
H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta\alpha))_{x}
is
not
isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits(\beta^{0}_{x}\circ\alpha^{0}_{x})
.

Before we give our final lemma of this section, we will need to have a long discussion, which will include the proof.

Since our base ring is a field, Verdier dualizing takes perverse sheaves to perverse sheaves and so takes exact sequences of perverse sheaves to exact sequences of perverse sheaves.

Let
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
be a perverse sheaf on
X
and let
g:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex analytic function such that some point
x\in X
is an isolated point in
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
. Then the stalk cohomology
H^{*}(\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}
is zero except, possibly, in degree zero.

Let
m:V(g)\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion. Consider the two standard natural distinguished triangles

(2)

\rightarrow m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\rightarrow\psi_{g}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{can}^{g}}\phi_{g}[-1]\xrightarrow{[1]}

and

\rightarrow\phi_{g}[-1]\xrightarrow{\mathbf{var}^{g}}\psi_{g}[-1]\rightarrow m_{!}m^{!}[1]\xrightarrow{[1]}.

Applying these to
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
and taking perverse cohomology, we have exact sequences of perverse sheaves:

0\rightarrow{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{0}(m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow\psi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}}\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{1}(m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{-1}(m_{!}m^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}}\psi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{0}(m_{!}m^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\rightarrow 0.

Thus,
{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\cong{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{1}(m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})
and
\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\cong{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{-1}(m_{!}m^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})
.

Note that
x
is an isolated point in the supports of both of these perverse sheaves; so, at
x
, they are effectively just finite-dimensional vector spaces in degree
0
.

The composition
\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
is equal to
\operatorname{id}-T^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
, where
T^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
is the monodromy automorphism on
\psi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
.

Now suppose that
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
be a self-dual perverse sheaf on
X
; that is, suppose there is an isomorphism
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow[\cong]{\omega}{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is the Verdier dualizing functor. As we are using field coefficents, dualizing commutes with perverse cohomology with a negation in the degree. Thus,

{\mathcal{D}}\big({\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)\cong{\mathcal{D}}{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{1}(m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{-1}({\mathcal{D}}m_{*}m^{*}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong

{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{-1}(m_{!}m^{!}[1]{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong{{\phantom{|}}{}^{\mu}\hskip-1.8063ptH}^{-1}(m_{!}m^{!}[1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})\cong\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}.

Note that taking stalk cohomology in the distinguished triangle (
2
) tells us that

(3)

{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{(\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{x}\}\cong H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}.

From this discussion, and Lemma
2.1
, it follows immediately that we have:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
be a perverse sheaf on
X
. Suppose that
g:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex analytic function such that a point
x\in X
is an isolated point in
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
. Then,

(1)

x
is an isolated point in
\operatorname{supp}{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
, in
\operatorname{supp}\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
, in
\operatorname{supp}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
and in
\operatorname{supp}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
;

(2)

the stalk cohomologies at
x
of
\phi_{g}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
,
{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
,
\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
, and
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
are all concentrated in degree 0; and

(3)

if
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is a self-dual perverse sheaf, we have

\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{x}=\dim{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{(\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{x}\}=\dim H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{x}=

\dim H^{0}(\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{x}=\dim\ker\{(\mathbf{var}^{g}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{x}\}.

3.
The Main Theorem

We return to the setting of the introduction. Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
, let
{\mathbf{0}}\in{\mathcal{U}}
, and let
f:({\mathcal{U}},{\mathbf{0}})\rightarrow({\mathbb{C}},0)
be a nowhere-locally-constant complex analytic function.

Let
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
denote the Milnor fiber of
f
at
{\mathbf{0}}
. Let
\tilde{b}_{n}:=\dim\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};\mathfrak{K})
.

Let
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}:=\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
. As is well-known,
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\cong{\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
(see, for instance,
[
7
]
and use that
{\mathcal{D}}{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]\cong{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
). As in the introduction, let
L
be the restriction to
V(f)
of a generic linear form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
; we need for
L
to be generic enough so that
{\mathbf{0}}
is an isolated point in the support of
\phi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
(in
[
6
]
, the condition is that
L
is
prepolar
). Let
\lambda^{0}:=\dim H^{0}(\phi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{\mathbf{0}}
. This
L
will take the place of
g
in Lemma
2.5
.

Referring to the previous section, we have the maps

\psi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}}\phi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{var}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}}\psi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}.

The composition
\mathbf{var}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\circ\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
is equal to
\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
, where
T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
is the monodromy automorphism on
\psi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
(letting the value of
L
, not
f
, go around a small circle).

Now, given the lemmas of the previous section, the proof of the main theorem will be quite short.

Theorem 3.1
.

(
Main Theorem, version 1)
Regardless of the dimension of
\Sigma f
,
{\mathbf{0}}
is isolated in the support of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
and

\frac{\lambda^{0}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}}{2}\ \leq\ \tilde{b}_{n}\ \leq\ \lambda^{0}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}.

In particular, if
T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}=\operatorname{id}
, then
\lambda^{0}/2\leq\tilde{b}_{n}\leq\lambda^{0}
.

Proof.

(
3
) tells us that

\tilde{b}_{n}=\dim H^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{\bullet})_{\mathbf{0}}=\dim{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{(\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\}.

Lemma
2.5
tells us that
{\mathbf{0}}
is isolated in the support of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
and that

\dim H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}=\dim H^{0}(\ker\{\mathbf{var}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}.

Thus, the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied, with
\alpha=\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
and
\beta=\mathbf{var}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
. and we conclude that

\tilde{b}_{n}=\dim{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{(\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\}=\dim H^{0}({\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\mathbf{can}^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}

and

\frac{\lambda^{0}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}}{2}\ \leq\ \tilde{b}_{n}\ \leq\ \lambda^{0}-\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

One may wish to use a finite field as the base ring in order to have a statement which involves torsion in the integral cohomology in the Milnor fiber. In addition, in theory,

\dim H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}

could change when we change base fields.

As we mentioned in the introduction, if
L
is sufficiently generic,
\lambda^{0}
is the
0
-th Lê number, as defined algebraically in
[
6
]
as the intersection number

\lambda^{0}=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,L}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial L}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}},

and thus is independent of the base field.

Example 3.3
.

Suppose that
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=1
. In this case, we can relate Theorem
3.1
to a good deal of previous work in the area.

Shrinking
{\mathcal{U}}
if necessary, we assume that each component
C
of
\Sigma f
is contained in
V(f)
, that
C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
is a Whitney stratum of
V(f)
, and that
C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
is homeomorphic to a punctured disk. If one takes a generic transverse hyperplane slice
H
at a point
p\in C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
, then the isomorphism-type of the degree
(n-1)
cohomology of the Milnor fiber
F_{f_{|_{H}},p}
is independent of
p\in C-{{\mathbf{0}}}
and is
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\mu^{\circ}_{C}}
, where
\mu^{\circ}_{C}
is called the generic transverse Milnor number on
C
.

In addition, since each
C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
is homeomorphic to a punctured disk, the fundamental group of
C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
. And so, corresponding to either one of the two generators of
\pi_{1}(C-\{{\mathbf{0}}\})
, for each
C
, we obtain an
internal (a.k.a. vertical) vanishing cycle monodromy isomorphism

h_{C}:{\mathfrak{K}}^{\mu^{\circ}_{C}}\xrightarrow[\cong]{}{\mathfrak{K}}^{\mu^{\circ}_{C}}

by traveling around the generator. This should
not
be confused with the Milnor monodromy, where the value of
f
travels around a circle.

It is a well-known result of Siersma
[
12
]
that there is an injection

(4)

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};{\mathfrak{K}})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{C}\ker\{\operatorname{id}-h_{C}\}.

Thus, in a sense, the most problematic case for obtaining a “nice” upper-bound on the (reduced) degree
(n-1)
betti number of
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
is the case where all of the
h_{C}
are the identity.

Now, for a generic linear form
L
, there are the algebraically defined Lê numbers of
f
at
{\mathbf{0}}
:

\lambda^{0}:=\lambda^{0}_{f,L}({\mathbf{0}})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\lambda^{1}:=\lambda^{1}_{f,L}({\mathbf{0}});

see
[
6
]
. The 1-dimensional Lê number is easy to describe:

\lambda^{1}=\sum_{C}(\operatorname{mult}C)\mu^{\circ}_{C}=\dim_{\mathbf{0}}H^{0}(\psi_{L}[-1]\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1])_{\mathbf{0}},

where we do not distinguish in the notation between
L
and its restriction to
V(f)
.

The definition of the 0-dimensional Lê number is the intersection number

\lambda^{0}=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,L}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial L}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}=\dim_{\mathbf{0}}H^{0}(\phi_{L}[-1]\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1])_{\mathbf{0}}.

Let us denote the betti numbers of the Milnor fiber by

\tilde{b}_{n-1}:=\dim\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};\mathfrak{K})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\tilde{b}_{n}:=\dim\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};\mathfrak{K}).

As part of the general theory of Lê cycles and numbers, we have

\tilde{b}_{n-1}\leq\lambda^{1},\hskip 14.45377pt\tilde{b}_{n}\leq\lambda^{0},\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\tilde{b}_{n}-\tilde{b}_{n-1}=\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{1}.

Because of the equality above, bounds on
\tilde{b}_{n-1}
yield bounds on
\tilde{b}_{n}
(and vice versa). Note that (
4
) tells us that the only way that we could possibly have
\tilde{b}_{n-1}=\lambda^{1}
is for each component
C
of
\Sigma f
to be smooth at the origin and for the internal monodromy to be trivial.

So, suppose all components
C
of
\Sigma f
are smooth at the origin and that, for all
C
, the internal monodromy is trivial, i.e.,
h_{C}=\operatorname{id}
for all
C
. Then is it possible that
\tilde{b}_{n-1}=\lambda^{1}
or, equivalently, that
\tilde{b}_{n}=\lambda^{0}
?
No
, unless
\lambda^{0}=0
; see
[
5
]
in the general case and
[
11
]
in the case where
\lambda^{1}=1
.

How does any of this relate to Theorem
3.1
?

With
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}:=\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
and
T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}
being the
L
-monodromy automorphism on
\psi_{L}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
, it is an easy exercise to show that

\bigoplus_{C}\ker\{\operatorname{id}-h_{C}\}\ \cong\ \ker\{(\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\}\cong H^{0}\big(\ker\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

Therefore, the injection (
4
) tells us that

\tilde{b}_{n-1}\leq\dim H^{0}\big(\ker\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}

or, equivalently, that

\tilde{b}_{n-1}\leq\lambda^{1}-\dim H^{0}\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

Since
\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{1}=\tilde{b}_{n}-\tilde{b}_{n-1}
, the inequalities above are equivalent to

\tilde{b}_{n}\leq\lambda^{0}-\dim H^{0}\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}};

this is the upper bound in Theorem
3.1
.

However, as we wrote in the introduction, the lower bound in Theorem
3.1
really seems to be new.

4.
The case of higher-dimensional critical loci

Throughout this section, we continue with all of our previous notation; in particular,
\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
is the perverse sheaf
\phi_{f}[-1]{\mathfrak{K}}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}[n+1]
.

In the previous section, we saw that the upper bound in Theorem
3.1
is equivalent to a well-known upper bound in the case where
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=1
. However, in this section, we assume
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f>1
.

With this assumption, both bounds on the top betti number in Theorem
3.1
are new. However, it is hard to work with
\dim H^{0}\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{L}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}
since it need not equal the dimension of the image of the map on stalk cohomology. In this section, we will “fix” this problem.

We will assume that, after a generic linear change of coordinates (generic with respect to
f
at the origin), that we have prepolar coordinates
\mathbf{z}:=(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
for
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
; prepolar coordinates are defined and used throughout
[
6
]
. In the remainder of this section, we shall not distinguish in the notation between the coordinate functions
z_{k}
and their restrictions to subspaces. The function
z_{0}
will be used in place of the linear form
L
from the previous sections.

As the coordinates
\mathbf{z}
are prepolar, the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin exist and are defined algebraically in
[
6
]
. As we wrote earlier, the definition of the 0-dimensional Lê number is the intersection number

\lambda^{0}=\lambda^{0}_{f,\mathbf{z}}({\mathbf{0}}):=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,z_{0}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}.

However, the easy characterization of
\lambda^{1}=\lambda^{1}_{f,\mathbf{z}}({\mathbf{0}})
that we gave in Example
3.3
does not apply in the case where
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f>1
. We shall not give the definition here, but will show how it is calculated in the midst of Example
4.3
below.

What is important here is that, for prepolar coordinates, the algebraic definition agrees with the calculation via iterated nearby and vanishing cycles. That is, Theorem 10.8 of
[
6
]
or Theorem 7.6.9 of
[
9
]
give us that
{\mathbf{0}}
is an isolated point in the support of each of the perverse sheaves
\phi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
and
\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}
, and

\lambda^{0}_{f,\mathbf{z}}({\mathbf{0}})=\dim H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}

and

\lambda^{1}_{f,\mathbf{z}}({\mathbf{0}})=\dim H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

Now, we want to describe
H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}
as the image of a map on stalk cohomology. We prove:

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the isomorphism

\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}):\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}

and its induced map on stalk cohomology

\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}:H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}\rightarrow H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

There is an isomorphism

H^{0}(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})_{\mathbf{0}}\ \cong\ \mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}.

Proof.

First, note that, since
{\mathbf{0}}
is isolated in the support of
\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}
(as we saw in the previous section),

H^{0}\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)_{\mathbf{0}}\cong H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})\big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

So we need to show

(5)

H^{0}\big(\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})\big)_{\mathbf{0}}\ \cong\ \mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}.

The argument is entirely similar to that in Lemma
2.1
. Consider the exact sequence of perverse sheaves

0\rightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\rightarrow\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}}\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\rightarrow 0.

Split this into two short exact sequences

0\rightarrow\ker\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\rightarrow\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\xrightarrow{\beta}\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\rightarrow 0,

where
\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}=\beta\alpha
.

Now apply the functor
\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]
to both of these short exact sequences. We obtain new short exact sequences:

0\rightarrow\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\big(\ker\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)\rightarrow\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\alpha)}\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\})\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\big(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)\xrightarrow{\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\beta)}\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\psi_{z_{0}}[-1]\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\phi_{z_{1}}[-1]\big({\operatorname{coker}\,}\{\operatorname{id}-T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}}\}\big)\rightarrow 0,

where
\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})=\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\beta)\circ\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](\alpha)
and
{\mathbf{0}}
is an isolated point in the support of all of the perverse sheaves appearing. Thus, these two short exact sequences yield two short exact sequences on the degree
0
stalk cohomologies at
{\mathbf{0}}
. From this, one easily concludes that (
5
) holds, and we are finished.
∎

Combining Proposition
4.1
with Theorem
3.1
, we have:

Theorem 4.2
.

(
Main Theorem, version 2)

The following inequalities hold:

\frac{\lambda^{0}-\dim\left(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\right)}{2}\ \leq\ \tilde{b}_{n}\ \leq\ \lambda^{0}-\dim\left(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\right),

or, equivalently,

\lambda^{0}-2\tilde{b}_{n}\ \leq\ \dim\left(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\right)\ \leq\ \lambda^{0}-\tilde{b}_{n}.

In particular,
\tilde{b}_{n}=0
if and only if
\lambda^{0}=\dim\left(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{z_{1}}[-1](T^{z_{0}}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\right)
.

Example 4.3
.

Consider
f:{\mathbb{C}}^{4}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
given by
f(u,v,x,y):=y^{2}+x^{5}+ux^{4}+v^{2}x^{2}
. We use the coordinate system
(u,v,x,y)
(in that order). We will calculate the Lê cycles
\Lambda^{k}
and Lê numbers
\lambda^{k}
at
{\mathbf{0}}
; along the way, our calculations will show that these coordinates are prepolar. As part of the process, we must calculate the relative polar cycles
\Gamma^{k}
. We will suppress references to
f
and the coordinate system for the Lê cycles/numbers and polar cycles.

We have, as sets,

\Sigma f=V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial u},\frac{\partial f}{\partial v},\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)=V(x^{4},2vx^{2},5x^{4}+4ux^{3}+2xv^{2},2y)=V(x,y).

We proceed as in
[
6
]
to find that, as cycles:

\Gamma^{3}=V(2y)=V(y).

\Gamma^{3}\cdot V(5x^{4}+4ux^{3}+2xv^{2})=V(y)\cdot\big[V(x)+V(5x^{3}+4ux^{2}+2v^{2})\big]=

V(5x^{3}+4ux^{2}+2v^{2},y)+V(x,y)=\Gamma^{2}+\Lambda^{2}\ \ \textnormal{(respectively)}.

\Gamma^{2}\cdot V(2vx^{2})=V(5x^{3}+4ux^{2}+2v^{2},y)\cdot\big[V(v)+2V(x)\big]=

V(5x^{3}+4ux^{2},v,y)+2V(5x^{3}+4ux^{2}+2v^{2},x,y)=V(5x+4u,v,y)+2V(x,y,v)+4V(x,y,v)=

V(5x+4u,v,y)+6V(x,y,v)=\Gamma^{1}+\Lambda^{1}.

\Gamma^{1}\cdot V(x^{4})=V(5x+4u,v,y)\cdot 4V(x)=4[{\mathbf{0}}]=\Lambda^{0}.

Thus,
\lambda^{2}=1
,
\lambda^{1}=6
, and
\lambda^{0}=4
. This means there is a chain complex

0\rightarrow{\mathfrak{K}}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{K}}^{6}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{K}}^{4}\rightarrow 0,

where the cohomology in the
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\lambda^{k}}
position is isomorphic to
\widetilde{H}^{3-k}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};{\mathfrak{K}})
.

But for this
f
, we can calculate the cohomology precisely. Let
g=x^{5}+ux^{4}+v^{2}x^{2}
. Then
f
is referred to as the suspension of
g
, for the Milnor fiber of
f
at
{\mathbf{0}}
has the homotopy-type of the suspension of the Milnor fiber of
g
at
{\mathbf{0}}
and, hence, the reduced cohomology of
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
is obtained from the reduced cohomology of
g
, shifted up one degree.

Since
g
is weighted-homogeneous,
F_{g,{\mathbf{0}}}
is diffeomorphic to
g^{-1}(1)
. But we can solve for
u
in the equation
x^{5}+ux^{4}+v^{2}x^{2}=1
to obtain

u=\frac{1-x^{5}-v^{2}x^{2}}{x^{4}}.

Therefore
F_{g,{\mathbf{0}}}
is homotopy-equivalent to
S^{1}
, and so the reduced cohomology of
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
is zero outside of degree 2 and
\widetilde{H}^{2}(F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}};\mathfrak{K})\cong\mathfrak{K}
. In particular
\tilde{b}_{3}=0
, and thus Theorem
4.2
tells us that

4=\lambda^{0}=\dim\left(\mathop{\rm im}\nolimits\left\{\big(\operatorname{id}-\phi_{v}[-1](T^{u}_{\mathbf{P}^{\bullet}})\big)^{0}_{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\right).

Remark 4.4
.

In Example
4.3
, we used Theorem
4.2
“backwards” from the way one might expect. Rather than using data about the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles near, but not at, the origin to gain information about the Milnor fiber at the origin, we used information at the origin to tell us something about the structure of the vanishing cycles nearby.

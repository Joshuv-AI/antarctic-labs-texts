A refinement of the Šidák-Khatri inequality and a strong Gaussian correlation conjecture
Rotem Assouline, Arnon Chor, Shay Sadovsky

Abstract
We prove two special cases of a strengthened Gaussian correlation conjecture, due to Tehranchi, and show that if the conjecture holds asymptotically, it holds for any dimension. Additionally, we use these special cases to prove a refined version of the Šidák-Khatri inequality.



1
Introduction

Over 50 years ago, Šidák
[
16
,
17
]
and independently, Khatri
[
10
]
, proved the following inequality for
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
symmetric Gaussian random variables:

\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1).

Our first theorem is a refinement of this result.

Theorem 1

(Refined Šidák-Khatri inequality)
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be zero-mean jointly Gaussian real random variables, and let
a_{1}\in(0,\infty]
. Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1+a_{1})\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])\\
&\quad\geq\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1)\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1+a_{1},|X_{2}|\leq 1,\dots,|X_{n}|\leq 1).\end{split}

(1)

In particular, we find that

\dfrac{\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1)}\geq\sup_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1+a_{i}\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1+a_{i})}.

(2)

Clearly, when
a_{i}=\infty
for all
i\in[n]
, the inequality becomes the Šidák-Kahtri inequality. In addition, renormalizing, we find that

\dfrac{\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq c_{i}\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq c_{i})}

is increasing each of the
c_{i}
’s (see Remark
10
).

The Gaussian Correlation inequality, which is a generalization of the Šidák-Khatri inequality, was proved by Royen in
[
14
]
and states the following:

\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])\geq\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[k])\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n]\setminus[k]).

(3)

In attempt to generalize Theorem
1
, we present the following conjecture, which is equivalent to a conjecture of Teheranchi
[
19
]
(stated below).

Conjecture 2
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be zero-mean jointly Gaussian real random variables, and let
s_{1},\dots,s_{n},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in[0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}+t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n])

\displaystyle\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq\min\{s_{i},t_{i}\}\ \forall i\in[n])

(4)

\displaystyle\geq\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}\ \forall i\in[n])

\displaystyle\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]).

Our proof relies on the geometric interpretation of the inequality with respect to the usual Gaussian probability measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted henceforth as
\gamma_{d}
. The classical Šidák-Khatri inequality can be interperted as the inequality
\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)
when
K
is an origin-symmetric convex set and
T
is a symmetric slab. In this geometric setting, this is due to Giannopoulos
[
6
]
and Szarek and Werner
[
18
]
.

Theorem
1
is restated and proved in the geometric setting as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an origin-symmetric convex body and
T=\{x:\ |\langle x,u\rangle|\leq 1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a symmetric slab with
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,

\gamma_{d}({\rm conv}\{K\cup T\})\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T).

(5)

Equation (
5
) does not hold for all origin symmetric and convex
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as will be seen in Example
14
. However, the following weaker inequality, which is a strengthening of Royen’s Gaussian correlation inequality, was conjectured to be true by Tehranchi
[
19
]
.

Conjecture 4
.

Let
d>0
and let
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be origin-symmetric convex sets, then

\gamma_{d}(K+T)\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)

(6)

where
\gamma
is the usual Gaussian probability measure.

In
[
19
]
, Tehranchi showed that Conjecture
4
holds up to some constant, improving on a result by Schechetman, Schlumprecht, and Zinn
[
15
]
(these results and other related works are elaborated on in the Background section).

The proof of the equivalence of Conjectures
4
and
2
is a classical argument, which we present in Proposition
7
in the Background section. Using a similar argument we show that Theorem
3
implies Theorem
1
.

We also present two partial results in the direction of the conjecture. First, we prove it for unconditional convex sets.

Theorem 5
.

Let
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be unconditional convex sets, then (
6
) holds.

Second, we show that an asymptotic version of Conjecture
4
would imply the conjecture itself.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that there is a sequence of positive numbers
(c_{N})
with
\lim_{N\to\infty}c_{N}^{1/N}=1
, such that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
K,T
origin-symmetric convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\gamma_{N}(K+T)\gamma_{N}(K\cap T)\geq c_{N}\gamma_{N}(K)\gamma_{N}(T).

Then, Conjecture
4
holds for all
d>0
and all
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2
Background

In this note, we switch between a probabilistic setting of
n
random variables and a geometric setting of functions and sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We will use some capital letters (e.g.
X,Y,X_{i},Y_{j}
) to denote random vectors and some to denote convex sets (e.g.
K,L,T
). The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
will be the standard product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The Gaussian measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted by
\gamma_{d}
, is the probability measure with density proportional to
e^{-\|x\|^{2}/2}
.
Finally, the Minkowski sum of two sets is the set

K+T:=\{x+y:x\in K,\ y\in T\}

and
K
will be called an origin-symmetric (or centrally symmetric) convex set if
K=-K
. The convex hull of a set
S
is the smallest convex set
K
such that
S\subseteq K
, and is denoted by
{\rm conv}\{S\}
. The support function of a convex set
K
is
h_{K}(x)=\sup_{y\in K}\langle x,y\rangle
. We denote
[n]=\{1,...,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The study of Gaussian correlation inequalities started with the works of Šidák
[
16
,
17
]
and Khatri
[
10
]
, who independently proved the case of correlation between one random Gaussian and
n-1
others (or, in the geometric formulation, between a convex body and a slab). It was conjectured by Das Gupta, Eaton, Olkin, Perlman, Savage, and Sobel in
[
7
]
, and later proved by Royen in
[
14
]
, that for
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
centrally symmetric,

\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T).

(7)

This inequality is known as the Gaussian Correlation Inequality.

The history of this problem is as follows. Pitt
[
12
]
published a proof of inequality (
7
) in two dimensions, and introduced the decoupling approach to the problem. However, at the time Pitt’s results could not be translated to higher dimension, and so proofs of special cases were given: in
[
15
]
Schechtman, Schlumprecht, and Zinn proved the inequality for two ellipsoids, for a body and a ball, and in some other cases. In
[
8
]
, Hargé proved the inequality for a body and an ellipsoid, and in
[
3
]
, Cordero-Erasquin used the Cafarelli contraction theorem to give another proof of this case.

Finally, Royen proved the Gaussian correlation conjecture in
[
14
]
, using Pitt’s decoupling approach together with some fine analysis. He also proved that the only equality cases in (
7
) are for ‘orthogonal’ sets, i.e. for
K=K_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},T=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times T_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}
. Royen’s proof is actually applicable to to a large family of distributions, for a simplified reading of his proof see
[
11
]
.

Since Royen’s proof, there has been some work on expanding and refining the conjecture. Eskenazis, Nayar and Tkocz
[
5
]
have extended the Gaussian correlation inequality to Gaussian mixtures, thus also proving a spherical correlation inequality (with respect to convex sets on the sphere).

The version of Conjecture
4
presented in this note was proposed by Tehranchi in
[
19
]
,
who also strengthened a result of Schechtman, Schlumprecht, and Zinn
[
15
]
, and showed that

\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)\leq(1-s)^{-d/2}\gamma_{d}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2(1-s)}{1+t}}(K\cap T)\right)\gamma_{d}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-s}{2(1-t)}}(K+T)\right)

for all
\sqrt{s}\leq t<1
.
Conjecture
4
is also motivated by the fact the a similar inequality holds for the Lebesgue measure, namely for
K,T
origin-symmetric,

{\rm{Vol}}(K+T){\rm{Vol}}(K\cap T)\geq{\rm{Vol}}(K){\rm{Vol}}(T),

as shown by Rogers and Shephard in
[
13
]
(for more on this see
[
2
, Section 1.5]
).

A final motivation for this work is the quantitative version of the Gaussian Correlation Inequality. In
[
4
, Theorem 31]
, De, Nadimpalli and Servedio defined a function
c(K,T)\geq 0
and determined that

\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)-\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)\geq c(K,T).

Conjecture
4
is another example of a quantitative bound, as it can be rewritten as

\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)-\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)\geq\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{d}(K+T)}-1\right)\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T).

The probabilistic version of the strong Gaussian Correlation conjecture, Conjecture
2
, is new, as far as we know. However, it is equivalent to
4
:

Proposition 7
.

Conjecture
4
holds if and only if Conjecture
2
holds.

Proof.

Assume that Conjecture
4
holds, and denote by
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
the centered Gaussian vector whose indices are the Gaussian variables of Conjecture
2
. The Gaussian vector
X
can be expressed as a linear image of a standard Gaussian
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\in[n]
, i.e. there exist
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
X_{i}=\langle Y,u_{i}\rangle
. Construct the following two convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

K=\{y:|\langle y,u_{i}\rangle|\leq s_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\},\ \ T=\{y:|\langle y,u_{i}\rangle|\leq t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\}.

(8)

Note that
K+T\subseteq\{y:|\langle y,u_{i}\rangle|\leq s_{i}+t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\}
, so applying (
6
), we get that

\displaystyle\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}+t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)\cdot\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq\min\{s_{i},t_{i}\}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\gamma_{d}(K+T)\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T)

\displaystyle\quad=\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)\cdot\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right).

In the other direction, assume Conjecture
2
holds. Let
K
and
T
be origin-symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\in[n]
. Assume that
K,T
are polytopes; the inequality for general origin-symmetric convex bodies follows by a standard approximation argument.

Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote the set of outer unit normals of facets of
K+T
. Note that any outer unit normal of a facet of
K
,
T
, or
K\cap T
is already in this set. Taking
Y
a
d
-dimensional standard Gaussian, we construct
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
jointly Gaussian random variables by taking
X_{i}=\langle Y,u_{i}\rangle
. Then applying (
4
) with
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
s_{i}=h_{K}(u_{i})
, and
t_{i}=h_{T}(u_{i})
, we recall that
h_{K+T}=h_{K}+h_{T}
and
h_{K\cap T}=\min\{h_{K},h_{T}\}
to get the following:

\displaystyle\gamma_{d}(K+T)\cdot\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)

\displaystyle\quad=\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}+t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)\cdot\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq\min\{s_{i},t_{i}\}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq s_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)\cdot\Pr\left(|X_{i}|\leq t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\right)=\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T).

∎

Remark 8
.

This proof is quite standard, and a similar variant of it has been used to show the equivalence of Equations (
3
) and (
7
).

3.1
The refined Šidák-Khatri inquality

To prove Theorem
1
we will use a lemma similar to Giannopoulos’s proof
[
6
]
of the Šidák-Khatri inquality:

Lemma 9
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
d=d_{1}+d_{2}
. Let
K
be an origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
and let
T=T_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
be a product body, where
T_{1}
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
. Denote by
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, the orthogonal projections.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
define the function
f(s)=\gamma_{d_{2}}(K\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(s))
, and assume that for any
z>0
,
\left\{s\in\pi_{1}(K)\ \middle|\ f(s)\geq z\right\}
and
T_{1}
satisfy (
5
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
.

Then
K
and
T
also satisfy
(
5
)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We leave the proof of this lemma for later, and show that it immediately gives a proof of Theorem
3
:

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Assume without loss of generality that
u\in{\rm sp}\{e_{1}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
e_{1}
is the first standard basis vector. In the notation of Lemma
9
, take
d_{1}=1,d_{2}=d-1
, then
T_{1}
is a symmetric interval
[-a,a]
(with
a=1/\|u\|
) and for any
z>0
,
\left\{s\in\pi_{1}(K)\ \middle|\ f(s)\geq z\right\}
is also some symmetric interval,
[-b(z),b(z)]
, by log-concavity of
f
. These intervals clearly satisfy (
5
), since for
d=1
, Theorem
3
holds trivially.
∎

Using Theorem
3
we prove the refined Šidák-Khatri inequality.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Denote
X=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. The Gaussian vector
X
can be expressed as a linear image of a standard Gaussian
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\in[n]
, i.e. there exist
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
X_{i}=\langle Y,u_{i}\rangle
.
Taking
s_{1}=1+a_{1},s_{2}=1,\dots,s_{n}=1
and
t_{1}=1,t_{2}=\infty,\dots,t_{n}=\infty
, we construct the two bodies

K=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\langle y,u_{i}\rangle|\leq s_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\},\ \ T=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\langle y,u_{i}\rangle|\leq t_{i}\ \forall i\in[n]\},

and note that
T
is a symmetric slab in direction
u_{1}
. Applying Theorem
3
, and using the fact that
{\rm conv}(K\cup T)\subseteq\{y:|\langle y,u_{1}\rangle|\leq 1+a_{1}\}
, we find that

\displaystyle\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1+a_{1})\cdot\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])

\displaystyle\quad=\gamma_{d}({\rm conv}(K\cup T))\cdot\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\gamma_{d}(K)\cdot\gamma_{d}(T)

\displaystyle\quad=\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1)\cdot\Pr(|X_{1}|\leq 1+a_{1},\dots,|X_{n}|\leq 1).

This proves (
1
), and since the choice of the first coordinate
X_{1}
was arbitrary, we may apply the inequality inductively on other coordinates and find that for all
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}>0
,

\dfrac{\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1)}\geq\dfrac{\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1+a_{i}\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|X_{i}|\leq 1+a_{i})}.

Since the inequality holds for all
\{a_{i}\}
, we prove (
2
).
∎

Remark 10
.

Our refined Šidák inequality may be reinterpreted for
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}\sim N(0,1)
standard Gaussians (Taking
Y_{i}=\frac{X_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}
) as

\dfrac{\Pr(|Y_{1}|\leq c_{1},\dots,|Y_{n}|\leq c_{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|Y_{i}|\leq c_{i})}\geq\sup_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in(0,\infty)}\dfrac{\Pr(|Y_{1}|\leq c_{1}+a_{1},\dots,|Y_{n}|\leq c_{n}+a_{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|Y_{i}|\leq c_{i}+a_{i})},

for any choice of
c_{i}>0
for all
i
.
In Šidák’s original paper, he uses the same inequality with 1 on the left hand side to obtain a confidence rectangle for
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
. In particular, for confidence level of
(1-\alpha)
one takes
c_{i}=c
such that
\Phi(c)=\frac{1}{2}(1+(1-\alpha)^{1/k})
.

In the refined inequality, if one may find any
a>0
such that

A=\frac{\Pr(|Y_{i}|\leq c+a\ \forall i\in[n])}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Pr(|Y_{i}|\leq c+a)}>1

then the same choice of
c_{i}=c
would improve the confidence level to
A(1-\alpha)
.

Finally, let us return to the proof of the lemma from the beginning of this subsection.

Proof of Lemma
9
.

Note that
{\rm conv}\{K\cup T\}=\pi_{1}({\rm conv}\{K\cup T\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
. Then by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\gamma_{d}({\rm conv}\{K\cup T\})\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{d_{1}}(\pi_{1}({\rm conv}\{K\cup T\}))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}f(s)\mathbbm{1}_{T_{1}}(s)\mathop{}\!d\gamma_{d_{1}}(s)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{d_{1}}({\rm conv}\{\pi_{1}(K)\cup T_{1}\})\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{d_{1}}(\left\{s\in T_{1}\ \middle|\ f(s)\geq z\right\})\mathop{}\!dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{d_{1}}(\{f\geq z\}\cap T_{1})\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}({\rm conv}\{\pi_{1}(K)\cup T_{1}\})\mathop{}\!dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{d_{1}}(\{f\geq z\}\cap T_{1})\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}({\rm conv}\{\{f\geq z\}\cup T_{1}\})\mathop{}\!dz,

where the last inequality holds since for any
z>0
,
\{f\geq z\}\subseteq\pi_{1}(K)
by definition. By the assumption, for any
z>0
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{d_{1}}(\{f\geq z\}\cap T_{1})\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}({\rm conv}\{\{f\geq z\}\cup T_{1}\})\geq\gamma_{d_{1}}(\{f\geq z\})\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}(T_{1}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\gamma_{d}({\rm conv}\{K\cup T\})\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma_{d_{1}}(\{f\geq z\})\mathop{}\!dz\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}(T_{1})=\gamma_{d}(K)\cdot\gamma_{d_{1}}(T_{1}).

Noting that
\gamma_{d}(T)=\gamma_{d_{1}}(T_{1})
, the proof is completed.
∎

T_{1}

5
) in

3.2
Stronger Gaussian correlation inequality

We begin with a simple proof of Theorem
5
, which shows that (
6
) holds for unconditional convex sets. Recall that a convex set is called unconditional if it is symmetric with respect to all coordinate hyperplanes. Following
[
15
]
, we use the Karlin-Rinott Theorem. Denote by
\preccurlyeq
the product partial order on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.
x\preccurlyeq y
if and only if
\forall i:x_{i}\leq y_{i}
. This is a lattice; denote by
\lor,\land
join and meet operations (i.e. coordinate-wise maximum and minimum).

Theorem 12

(
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a product measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
f_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 4
,
be nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

f_{1}(x)f_{2}(y)\leq f_{3}(x\lor y)f_{4}(x\land y).

Then,

\int f_{1}d\mu\int f_{2}d\mu\leq\int f_{3}d\mu\int f_{4}d\mu.

We mention that this theorem is a continuous version of the celebrated four-function theorem
[
1
]
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Let
Q=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ 0\preccurlyeq x\}
be the positive orthant. Note that by uncoditionality of
K
, we have that if
0\preccurlyeq x\preccurlyeq y\in K
, then also
x\in K
, and similarly for
T
.

By unconditionality of
K
and
T
, if
x\in K\cap Q
and
y\in T\cap Q
, then since
x\land y\preccurlyeq x,y
we have
x\land y\in K\cap T\cap Q
. By the positivity of elements in
Q
we have
x\lor y\preccurlyeq x+y
which implies that
x\lor y\in(K+T)\cap Q
.
It follows that
\gamma_{d}
and the functions

f_{1}=1_{K\cap Q},\ f_{2}=1_{T\cap Q},\ f_{3}=1_{(K+T)\cap Q},\ f_{4}=1_{K\cap T\cap Q}

satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
12
, and we find that

\gamma_{d}(K\cap Q)\gamma_{d}(T\cap Q)\leq\gamma_{d}(K\cap T\cap Q)\gamma_{d}((K+T)\cap Q).

Since
\gamma_{d}(K)=2^{n}\gamma_{d}(K\cap Q)
, and similarly for
T,K\cap T
and
K+T
, the theorem follows.
∎

Remark 13
.

Theorem
5
holds for any symmetric product measure, and the proof is verbatim.

Next, we see that unlike in Theorem
3
, in the unconditional case the Minkowski sum in Conjecture
4
cannot be replaced with a smaller set such as the convex hull of
K,T
.

Example 14
.

Let
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
N\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
K=[-1/N,1/N]\times[-N,N]
, and
T=[-N,N]\times[-1/N,1/N]
. Denoting
L={\rm conv}\{\pm e_{1},\pm e_{2}\}
where
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(K)\gamma_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}([-1/N,1/N])^{2}\gamma_{1}([-N,N])^{2},

while

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(K\cap T)\gamma_{2}({\rm conv}(K\cup T))

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}([-1/N,1/N])^{2}\gamma_{2}({\rm conv}(K\cup T))

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}([-1/N,1/N])^{2}\gamma_{2}((N+1/N)L)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}([-1/N,1/N])^{2}\gamma_{1}\left(\left[-\frac{(N+1/N)}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{(N+1/N)}{\sqrt{2}}\right]\right)^{2}

where the inequality is due to the inclusion
K,T\subseteq(N+1/N)L
, and the volume of the square
(N+1/N)L
can be computed by the rotation invariance of
\gamma_{2}
. The inequality

\gamma_{2}(K)\gamma_{2}(T)\leq\gamma_{2}(K\cup T)\gamma_{2}({\rm conv}(K\cup T))

would hence imply that

\gamma_{1}([-N,N])\leq\gamma_{1}\left(\left[-\frac{(N+1/N)}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{(N+1/N)}{\sqrt{2}}\right]\right)

which is false for
N\geq 3
.

We end this subsection with the proof of Theorem
6
, which is a standard tensorization argument.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Let
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be origin-symmetric sets.
Denote by
K^{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}K\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{Nd}
, and similarly
T^{N}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}T\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{Nd}
. Then, by the assumption, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N}+T^{N})\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N}\cap T^{N})\geq c_{Nd}\cdot\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N})\gamma_{Nd}(T^{N}).

Note that
K^{N}+T^{N}=(K+T)^{N}
, and
K^{N}\cap T^{N}=(K\cap T)^{N}
. Since
\gamma_{Nd}
is a product measure,
\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N})=(\gamma_{d}(K))^{N}
,
\gamma_{Nd}(T^{N})=(\gamma_{d}(T))^{N}
,
\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N}+T^{N})=(\gamma_{d}(K+T))^{N}
and
\gamma_{Nd}(K^{N}\cap T^{N})=(\gamma_{d}(K\cap T))^{N}
. Plugging these into the inequality above and taking
N
-th root gives

\gamma_{d}(K+T)\gamma_{d}(K\cap T)\geq c_{Nd}^{1/N}\gamma_{d}(K)\gamma_{d}(T).

As
d
is constant we may apply our assumption on
c_{N}
and take
N\to\infty
in the inequality above to conclude the proof.
∎

Hölder’s inequality and its reverse – a probabilistic point of view
Lorenz Frühwirth, Joscha Prochno

Abstract
In this article we take a probabilistic look at Hölder’s inequality, considering the ratio of terms in the classical Hölder inequality for random vectors in ℝ n \R^{n} . We proof a central limit theorem for this ratio, which then allows us to reverse the inequality up to a multiplicative constant with high probability. The models of randomness include the uniform distribution on ℓ p n \ell_{p}^{n} balls and spheres. We also provide a Berry-Esseen type result and prove a large and a moderate deviation principle for the suitably normalized Hölder ratio.



1
Introduction & Main results

There are a number of classical inequalities frequently used throughout mathematics. A natural question is then to characterize the equality cases or to determine to what degree a reverse inequality may hold. The latter shall be the main focus here and we begin by motivating and illustrating the approach in the case of the classical arithmetic-geometric mean inequality (AGM inequality), which has attracted attention in the past decade. Let us recall that the AGM inequality states that for any finite number of non-negative real values, the geometric mean is less than or equal to the arithmetic mean. More precisely, for all
n\in\N
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\geq 0
it holds that

\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\Big)^{1/n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}

and equality holds if and only if
x_{1}=\dots=x_{n}
. Setting
y_{i}:=\sqrt{x_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, we obtain

\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\Big)^{1/n}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

For a point
y
in the Euclidean unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}:=\{x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\R^{n}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2}=1\}
, this leads to the estimate

\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i}|\Big)^{1/n}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

It is natural to ask whether this inequality can be reversed for a “typical” point in
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}
and in
[
6
, Proposition 1]
, Gluskin and Milman showed that for any
t\in\R
,

\sigma^{(n)}_{2}\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}\ :\ \Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|\Big)^{1/n}\geq t\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big\}\Big)\geq 1-(1.6\sqrt{t})^{n},

where
\sigma^{(n)}_{2}
denotes the unique rotationally invariant probability surface measure (the Haar measure) on
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}
.
For large dimensions
n\in\N
this means that with high probability, we can reverse the AGM inequality up to a constant. The problem was then revisited by Aldaz in
[
1
, Theorem 2.8]
and he showed that for all
\epsilon>0,k>0
there exists an
N:=N(k,\epsilon)\in\N
such that for every
n\geq N

\sigma^{(n)}_{2}\Bigg(\Bigg\{x\in\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{(1-\epsilon)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma+\log 2)}}{\sqrt{n}}<\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|\Big)^{1/n}<\frac{(1+\epsilon)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma+\log 2)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigg\}\Bigg)\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{k}},

where
\gamma=0,5772\dots
is Euler’s constant. The previous works motivated Kabluchko, Prochno, and Vysotsky
[
8
]
to study the asymptotic behavior of the
p
-generalized AGM inequality, which states that for
p\in(0,\infty)
,
n\in\N
, and
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\R^{n}
,

\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|\Big)^{1/n}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

The authors then analyzed the quantity

\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\frac{\big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|\big)^{1/n}}{||x||_{p}},

where
||x||_{p}:=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}
,
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\R^{n}
. Similar as in the case of the classical AGM inequality above it is now natural to consider points
x\in\R^{n}
that are uniformly distributed on the
\ell_{p}^{n}
unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p}
or the
\ell_{p}^{n}
unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p}
respectively, where

\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p}:=\big\{x\in\R^{n}\ :\ ||x||_{p}\leq 1\big\}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p}:=\big\{x\in\R^{n}\ :\ ||x||_{p}=1\big\}.

In
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
, for a constant
m_{p}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
p
, it is shown that

\sqrt{n}\big(e^{-m_{p}}\mathcal{R}_{n}-1\big),\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N

converges to a centered normal distribution with known variance and in
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
a large deviation principle for the sequence
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\in\N}
is proven (see Section
2.2
for the definition of an LDP).

The work
[
8
]
of Kabluchko, Prochno, and Vysotsky was then recently complemented by Thäle in
[
13
]
, who obtained a Berry-Esseen type bound and a moderate deviation principle for a wider class of distributions on the
\ell_{p}^{n}
balls (see
[
3
]
). In the subsequent paper
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, Kaufmann and Thäle were able to identify the sharp asymptotics of
(\mathcal{R}_{n})_{n\in\N}
.

Another classical inequality which is used throughout mathematics and applied in numerous situations is Hölder’s inequality. While, as outlined above, the AGM inequality is by now well understood from a probabilistic point of view, here we shall focus on Hölder’s inequality and take a probabilistic approach in the same spirit. We recall that for
n\in\N
and
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, Hölder’s inequality states that for all points
x,y\in\R^{n}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|\leq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}.

(1)

The random quantity to be analyzed is therefore the ratio

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N,

(2)

where we assume that
X^{(n)}
and
Y^{(n)}
are independent random points in
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p}
and
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q}
, respectively. In fact, we focus here on the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, i.e., we consider the cases where
X^{(n)}\sim\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p})
and
Y^{(n)}\sim\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q})
or
X^{(n)}\sim\U(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p})
and
Y^{(n)}\sim\U(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{q})
. The uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p}
is given by the normalized Lebesgue measure, whereas there are two meaningful uniform distributions on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p}
, namely the surface measure denoted by
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
and the cone probability measure
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
(see Subsection
2.2
for precise definitions).

1.1
Main results – Limit theorems for the Hölder ratio

Let us now present our main results. For the sake of brevity, we first introduce the following general assumption on our random quantities.

Assumption A
.

Let
X^{(n)},Y^{(n)}
be independent random vectors in
\R^{n}
and let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. We assume either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim

\U(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}
, where
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
and
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
denote the cone probability measure and the surface probability measure on
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, respectively.

The following quantities appear in the formulation of Theorems
A
and
D
. Let
\Gamma
denote the Gamma-function and set

\mathbf{C_{p,q}}=\left(\begin{matrix}p^{2/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}q^{2/q}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}-m_{p,q}^{2}&m_{p,q}&m_{p,q}\\
m_{p,q}&p&0\\
m_{p,q}&0&q\end{matrix}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptd_{p,q}:=\left(1,-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p},-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}\right),

(3)

where
m_{p,q}:=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}
.

1.1.1
The CLT and Berry-Esseen bounds for
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}

We start with the central limit theorem and a Berry-Esseen type result for the Hölder ratio
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}
(see (
2
)). As a consequence we shall see that Hölder’s inequality may be reversed up to a specific multiplicative constant only depending on
p
and
q
with high probability.

Theorem A

(Central limit theorem)
.

Let
X^{(n)},Y^{(n)}
be random vectors satisfying Assumption
A
and let
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
be given as in (
2
), i.e.,

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

Then, we have

\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z,

(4)

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{p,q}^{2})
,
\sigma_{p,q}^{2}:=\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle\in(0,\infty)
with
\mathbf{C_{p,q}}
and
d_{p,q}
as in (
3
).

Remark 1.1
.

As a consequence of Theorem
A
, for any
t\in\R
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}^{(n)}Y_{i}^{(n)}|\geq\Big(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}+m_{p,q}\Big)||X^{(n)}||_{p}||Y^{(n)}||_{q}\Big]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{p,q}}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{p,q}^{2}}}dx.

In particular, for
t=0
, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}^{(n)}Y_{i}^{(n)}|\geq m_{p,q}||X^{(n)}||_{p}||Y^{(n)}||_{q}\Big]=\frac{1}{2}.

This means, with a probability tending to
1/2
, we can reverse Hölder’s inequality up to the explicit constant
m_{p,q}=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)}
.

We are also able to provide a quantitative version of Theorem
A
, i.e., a Berry-Esseen type result. For real-valued random variables
X
and
Y
on a common probability space, we define the Kolmogorov-distance

d_{Kol}(X,Y):=\sup_{t\in\R}\Big|\mathbb{P}\left[X\leq t\right]-\mathbb{P}\left[Y\leq t\right]\Big|.

(5)

Theorem B

(Berry-Esseen bound)
.

Let
X^{(n)},Y^{(n)}
be random vectors satisfying Assumption
A
and let
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
be given as in (
2
). Then there exists a constant
C_{p,q}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
p
and
q
, such that

d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\big),Z\right)\leq C_{p,q}\frac{\log(n)}{\sqrt{n}},

where
m_{p,q}=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}
,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{p,q}^{2})
and
\sigma_{p,q}^{2}=\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle
is the same quantity as in Theorem
A
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
B
gives a similar asymptotic bound of the distance to a normal distribution as the classical theorem of Berry-Esseen. The
„{}\log(n)“
on the right-hand side seems to be owed to our method of proof and we conjecture that this factor is not necessary.

Remark 1.3
.

Although Theorem
B
implies Theorem
A
, we provide a direct and more self-contained proof of Theorem
A
, which also contains estimates which we use in the proof of Theorem
D
.

1.1.2
Moderate and large deviations for
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}

Two other classical types of limit theorems in probability theory are moderate and large deviations, which typically occur between the normal fluctuations scale and the larger one of a law of large numbers. Here the probabilistic behavior is indeed different and universality is replaced by a tail sensitivity, which enters rate and/or speed in a subtle way. For the definitions we refer to Section
2
below.

Theorem C

(Large deviation principle)
.

Let
X^{(n)},Y^{(n)}
be random vectors satisfying Assumption
A
and let
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
be given as in (
2
). Then,
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
satisfies a large deviation principle in
\R
at speed
n
and with good rate function
\mathbb{I}:\R\rightarrow[0,\infty]
defined as

\mathbb{I}(x):=\begin{cases}\inf\Big\{\Lambda^{*}(u,v,w)\ :\ x=\frac{u}{v^{1/p}w^{1/q}}\Big\},&:x>0\\
+\infty&:x\leq 0.\end{cases}

(6)

The function
\Lambda^{*}:\R^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is given by

\Lambda^{*}(u,v,w):=\sup_{(r,s,t)\in\R^{3}}\big[su+vt+wr-\Lambda(r,s,t)\big],\hskip 10.00002pt(u,v,w)\in\R^{3},

where

\Lambda(r,s,t):=\log\int_{\R^{2}}c_{p,q}\exp\Big(r|xy|+s|x|^{p}+t|y|^{q}-\frac{|x|^{p}}{p}-\frac{|y|^{q}}{q}\Big)dx\,dy,\hskip 10.00002pt(r,s,t)\in\R^{3}

with
c_{p,q}:=\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{p})}\frac{1}{2q^{1/q}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{q})}
.

The next result concerns the moderate deviation principle for the Hölder ratio and complements the central limit theorem and the large deviation principle already presented.

Theorem D

(Moderate deviation principle)
.

Let
X^{(n)},Y^{(n)}
be random vectors satisfying Assumption
A
and let
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
be given as in (
2
). Further, assume that
(b_{n})_{n\in\N}\in\R^{\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}=\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=0.

Then
\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\big)\right)_{n\in\N}
satisfies a moderate deviation principle in
\R
at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
and with a good rate function
\I:\R\rightarrow[0,\infty]
given by
\I(t):=\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{p,q}^{2}}
, where
\sigma_{p,q}^{2}=\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle\in(0,\infty)
with
\mathbf{C_{p,q}}
and
d_{p,q}
as in (
3
), while
m_{p,q}=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}
.

Notation and Preliminaries

We shall now briefly introduce the notation used throughout the text together with some background material on large deviations and some further results used in the proofs.

2.1
Notation

For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
d\in\N
and
x\in\R^{d}
,

||x||_{p}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

denotes the
p
-norm in
\R^{d}
. We recall the definitions of the
\ell_{p}^{n}
unit ball and the
\ell_{p}^{n}
unit sphere, i.e.,

\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}:=\left\{x\in\R^{n}\ :\ ||x||_{p}\leq 1\right\}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}:=\left\{x\in\R^{n}\ :\ ||x||_{p}=1\right\}.

Moreover, for
x,y\in\R^{d}
,
\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
is the standard scalar product on
\R^{d}
.

\mathscr{B}(\R^{d})
denotes the Borel-sigma algebra on
\R^{d}
.
For
p\geq 1
,
\gamma_{p}
denotes the
p
-generalized Gaussian distribution with Lebesgue-density

\frac{d\gamma_{p}}{dx}(x):=\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)}e^{-|x|^{p}/p},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\R.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
the normal distribution with mean
\mu\in\R
and variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
. For two distributions
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
on
\mathscr{B}(\R^{d})
, we denote by
\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2}
the product measure of
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
. Given a sequence of real-valued random variables
(X_{n})_{n\in\N}
and another real-valued random variable
X
, we denote by
X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
convergence in distribution. We shall also write iid for independent and identically distributed.

2.2
Basics from large deviation theory and probability

Let
d\in\N
and
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a sequence of
\R^{d}
-valued random variables and let
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a sequence of real numbers tending to infinity. We say that
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
satisfies a large deviation principle (LDP) in
\R^{d}
at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
if and only if there exists a good rate function (GRF)
\I:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, i.e.,
\I
has compact level sets, such that

-\inf_{x\in A^{\circ}}\I(x)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\prb[\xi_{n}\in A^{\circ}]\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\prb[\xi_{n}\in\overline{A}]\leq-\inf_{x\in\overline{A}}\I(x)

(7)

for all
A\in\mathscr{B}(\R^{d})
.

There are different ways to show that a certain sequence of random variables satisfies an LDP, one of the most commonly used is the so called contraction principle (see, e.g., Theorem 4.2.1 in
[
4
]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Contraction principle)
.

Let
d,n\in\N
and
f:\R^{d}\rightarrow\R^{n}
be a continuous function. Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a sequence of random variables that satisfies an LDP in
\R^{d}
at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
with GRF
\mathbb{I}:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Then, the sequence
(f(\xi_{n}))_{n\in\N}
satisfies an LDP in
\R^{n}
at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
with the GRF
\mathbb{I}^{\prime}:\R^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\mathbb{I}^{\prime}(y):=\inf_{x\in f^{-1}(\{y\})}\mathbb{I}(x).

We shall also use Cramér’s large deviation theorem for
\R^{d}
-valued random variables (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Corollary 6.1.6]
).

Proposition 2.2

(Cramér’s theorem)
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in\N}
be a sequence of iid
\R^{d}
-valued random variables such that
0\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\circ}
, where

\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}:=\big\{t\in\R^{d}\ :\ \Lambda(t)=\log\E[e^{\langle t,X_{1}\rangle}]<\infty\big\}.

Then, the sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
with

\xi_{n}:=\frac{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}}{n},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N

satisfies an LDP in
\R^{d}
at speed
n
with GRF
\Lambda^{*}:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\Lambda^{*}(x):=\sup_{t\in\R^{d}}\big[\langle x,t\rangle-\Lambda(t)\big].

Two sequences of
\R^{d}
-valued random variables
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\N}
are said to be exponentially equivalent at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
, if for all
\epsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mathbb{P}\big[\big||\xi_{n}-\eta_{n}\big||_{2}>\epsilon\big]=-\infty.

(8)

The following result can be found, e.g., in
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.13]
, and states that if a sequence of random vectors satisfies an LDP and is exponentially equivalent to another sequence of random vectors, then both satisfy the same LDP.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\N}
be two random
\R^{d}
-valued sequences. Assume that
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
satisfies an LDP at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
with GRF
\I:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Moreover, let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\N}
be exponentially equivalent at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
. Then,
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\N}
satisfies an LDP at speed
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
with the same GRF
\I:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a sequence of
\R^{d}
-valued random variables. We say that the sequence of random variables satisfies a moderate deviation principle (MDP) if and only if
\big(\frac{\xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}b_{n}}\big)_{n\in\N}
satisfies an LDP at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
and with some GRF
\mathbb{I}:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
for some positive sequence
(b_{n})_{n\in\N}
satisfying
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}b_{n}=\infty
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=0
.
The scaling by
\sqrt{n}b_{n}
is typically faster than the scaling in a central limit theorem but slower than the scaling in a law of large numbers. This property of an MDP is nicely illustrated in the following Cramér-type theorem (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 3.7.1]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
d\in\N
and
(X_{i})_{i\in\N}
be a sequence of iid
\R^{d}
-valued random variables such that

\Lambda(t)=\log\E[e^{\langle t,X_{1}\rangle}]<\infty,

for all
t
in some ball around the origin,
\E[X_{1}]=0
and
\mathbf{C}
, the covariance matrix of
X_{1}
, is invertible. Let
(b_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a sequence of real numbers with

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}b_{n}=\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=0.

Then, the sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
with
\xi_{n}:=\frac{1}{b_{n}\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
satisfies an LDP in
\R
at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
with GRF
\I:\R^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\I(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\mathbf{C}^{-1}x\rangle,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\R^{d}.

The following result is taken from
[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3}
be three random variables, let
Z
be a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
and let
\epsilon>0
. Then,

\sup_{t\in\R}\big|\mathbb{P}[Y_{1}+Y_{2}+Y_{3}\geq t]-\mathbb{P}[Z\geq t]\big|\leq\sup_{t\in\R}\big|\mathbb{P}[Y_{1}\geq t]-\mathbb{P}[Z\geq t]\big|+\mathbb{P}\left[|Y_{2}|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[|Y_{3}|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]+\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}.

There are two meaningful uniform distributions on the
\ell_{p}^{n}
unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, namely the cone probability measure
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
and the surface probability measure
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
. In the following, we will briefly discuss their theoretical foundation as well as the relation between those two distributions. We can equip
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p}
with the trace Borel-sigma algebra on
\R^{n}
which we denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})
. For
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})
, the cone probability measure
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
is then defined as

\mu^{(n)}_{p}(A):=\frac{\lambda^{(n)}([0,1]A)}{\lambda^{(n)}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})},

where
\lambda^{(n)}
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathscr{B}(\R^{n})
and
[0,1]A:=\{x\in\R^{n}\ :\ x=ra,\ r\in[0,1],\ a\in A\}
. By a result of Schechtman and Zinn
[
12
]
and Rachev and Rüschendorf
[
11
]
, we know that for
X_{p}^{(n)}\sim\U\left(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}\right)
and
Y_{p}^{(n)}\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{p}^{(n)}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}U^{1/n}\frac{\zeta^{(n)}}{||\zeta^{(n)}||_{p}}\\
Y_{p}^{(n)}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\zeta^{(n)}}{||\zeta^{(n)}||_{p}},\end{split}

(9)

where
U\sim\U([0,1])
and
\zeta^{(n)}:=(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})
are independent and
(\zeta_{i})_{i\in\N}
is an iid sequence distributed with respect to the
p
-generalized Gaussian distribution
\gamma_{p}
; we recall the corresponding Lebesgue-density

\frac{d\gamma_{p}}{dx}(x)=\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{p})}e^{-|x|^{p}/p},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\R.

(10)

Let
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
be the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff probability measure or, equivalently, the
(n-1)
-dimensional normalized Riemannian volume measure on
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
. We have the following relation between
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
and
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
(see
[
10
, Lemma 2]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
n\in\N
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
,

\frac{d\sigma^{(n)}_{p}}{d\mu^{(n)}_{p}}(x)=C_{n,p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2p-2}\Big)^{1/2},

where

C_{n,p}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2p-2}\mu^{(n)}_{p}(dx)\Big)^{-1/2}.

If
p=1,2
, it is clear that
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}=\mu^{(n)}_{p}
. We remark that in case of
p=\infty
, we know that (see
[
10
]
)
\sigma^{(n)}_{\infty}=\mu^{(n)}_{\infty}
. In contrast, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
with
p\neq 2
, we have that
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\neq\mu^{(n)}_{p}
. Nevertheless, for large
n\in\N
, one can prove that
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}
and
\mu^{(n)}_{p}
are close in the total variation distance (see
[
10
, Theorem 2]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

For all
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

||\mu^{(n)}_{p}-\sigma^{(n)}_{p}||_{TV}:=\sup_{A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})}|\mu^{(n)}_{p}(A)-\sigma^{(n)}_{p}(A)|\leq\frac{c_{p}}{\sqrt{n}},

where
c_{p}\in(0,\infty)
only depends on
p
.

Proofs of the main results

Before we continue with some technical results and the proofs of the main theorems, let us recall here that all theorems stated in Section
1.1
assume Assumption
A
.

We begin with a technical Lemma giving a useful representation of the Hölder ratio
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}
,
n\in\N
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and assume that either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. Then, we have

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}},

(11)

where
\big((\zeta_{i},\eta_{i})\big)_{i\in\N}
is an iid sequence with
(\zeta_{1},\eta_{1})\sim\gamma_{p}\otimes\gamma_{q}
.

Proof.

First, assume that
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q})
. Then, using the Schechtmann-Zinn representation in (
9
), we have
X^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}U^{1/n}\frac{\zeta^{(n)}}{||\zeta^{(n)}||_{p}}
, where
U
and
\zeta^{(n)}=(\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{n})
are independent with
U\sim\U([0,1])
and iid
\zeta_{i}\sim\gamma_{p}
for
i\in\N
. The random variable
Y^{(n)}
has a similar form, i.e.,
Y^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}V^{1/n}\frac{\eta^{(n)}}{||\eta^{(n)}||_{q}}
, where
V
and
\eta^{(n)}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n})
are independent with
V\sim\U([0,1])
and iid
\eta_{i}\sim\gamma_{q}
for
i\in\N
. Using this leads to

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}.

Now assume that
X^{(n)}
and
Y^{(n)}
are independent and distributed with respect to the cone measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{p}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{q}
respectively, i.e.,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu_{p}^{(n)}\otimes\mu_{q}^{(n)}
. Then, again by (
9
), we have that
X^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\zeta^{(n)}}{||\zeta^{(n)}||_{p}}
and
Y^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\eta^{(n)}}{||\eta^{(n)}||_{q}}
with
\zeta^{(n)}=(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{n})
and
\eta^{(n)}=(\eta_{1},...,\eta_{n})
, where
\big((\zeta_{i},\eta_{i})\big)_{i\in\N}
is an iid sequence with
(\zeta_{1},\eta_{1})\sim\gamma_{p}\otimes\gamma_{q}
. Hence, we receive the same representation in distribution for
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}
, i.e., we have

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

∎

3.1
Proof of Theorem
A

We start with an auxiliary Lemma that is used in the proof of the central limit theorem stated as Theorem

and also later in the proof of Theorem

(see Section

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X^{(n)}
and
Y^{(n)}

(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n-1})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n-1})

(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Y^{(n)}_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}.

Then, we can write

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=m_{p,q}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(3)}+R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big),

(12)

where
m_{p,q}=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}

,
S_{n}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1)
and

,
i\in\N
. The function

has the property that there is an

|R(x,y,z)|\leq M||(x,y,z)||^{2}_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }\hskip 10.00002pt||(x,y,z)||_{2}\rightarrow 0.

(13)

Proof.

(of Lemma
3.2
)

Consider the function

F(x,y,z):=\frac{x+m_{p,q}}{(1+y)^{1/p}(1+z)^{1/q}},

where

is the constant from Lemma

. Clearly,
F
is twice continuously differentiable in

which contains an open neighborhood of
(0,0,0)
. So, the Taylor expansion of first order exists locally around
(0,0,0)
and, for

F(x,y,z)=m_{p,q}+x-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}y-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}z+R(x,y,z),

(14)

where there exists

.
Using the representation of

from Lemma

, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q}\big)+m_{p,q}}{\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1\big)+1\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1\big)+1\Big)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(1)}+m_{p,q}}{\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(2)}+1\big)^{1/p}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(3)}+1\big)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle=F\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

where we have used that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and the quantities

are given as in Lemma

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=m_{p,q}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q\sqrt{n}}S_{n}^{(3)}+R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big),

as claimed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

First, we assume that either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n-1})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n-1})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. Then, we can use the Taylor expansion in (
12
) from Lemma
3.2
, where we get

\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)}+\sqrt{n}R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big),

(15)

where
S_{n}^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q})
,
S_{n}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1)
and
S_{n}^{(3)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1)
with iid
(\zeta_{i},\eta_{i})\sim\gamma_{p}\otimes\gamma_{q}
,
i\in\N
. We show that

\sqrt{n}R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(16)

To that end, we recall (
13
) from Lemma
3.2
. There, we showed that for sufficiently small
\delta>0
and
(x,y,z)\in\R^{3}
with
||(x,y,z)||_{2}<\delta

|R(x,y,z)|\leq M||(x,y,z)||_{2}^{2}
for some constant
M\in(0,\infty)
. This gives the following estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)}{\Big|\Big|\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}}>M\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\Big|\Big|\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}>\delta^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big[\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)^{2}>\delta^{2}\Big]\longrightarrow 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ as }\hskip 10.00002ptn\rightarrow\infty.

The latter holds due to Slutsky’s theorem and the fact that
\frac{S_{n}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
,
i=1,2,3
as

by the strong law of large numbers (note that
\mathbb{E}[|\zeta_{1}||\eta_{1}|]=m_{p,q}
and
\mathbb{E}[|\zeta_{1}|^{p}]=\mathbb{E}[|\eta_{1}|^{q}]=1
). Further, we have for
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)>\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}\Big|\Big|\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)}{\Big|\Big|\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}}>M\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(1)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(2)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(3)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\Big]+\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)}{\Big|\Big|\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}}>M\right].

S_{n}^{(i)}
converges to a normal distribution as
n\rightarrow\infty
for

\frac{(S_{n}^{(i)})^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

n\rightarrow\infty
and hence, again employing Slutsky’s theorem, we get

\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(1)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(2)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\big(S_{n}^{(3)}\big)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\Big]\longrightarrow 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as}\hskip 10.00002ptn\rightarrow\infty.

This completes the argument and shows the claim in (
16
).
Now, let us consider the sequence

S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}\left(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1\right)-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}\left(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1\right)\right),\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

\displaystyle\sigma_{p,q}^{2}:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}\left[|\zeta_{1}\eta_{1}|-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}|\zeta_{1}|^{p}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}\left[\left\langle d_{p,q},\left(|\zeta_{1}\eta_{1}|,|\zeta_{1}|^{p},|\eta_{1}|^{q}\right)\right\rangle\right]

Where

is the covariance matrix of the vector

(19)

.

D_{n}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|}{||x||_{p}||y||_{q}}\in A\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}),B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})}\left|\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}\big[A\times B\big]-\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}\big[A\times B\big]\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}),B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})}\left|\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\big[A\big]\mu^{(n)}_{q}\big[B\big]-\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}\big[A\times B\big]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})}\left|\sigma^{(n)}_{q}\big[B\big]-\mu_{q}^{(n)}\big[B\big]\right|+\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})}\left|\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\big[A\big]-\mu_{p}^{(n)}\big[A\big]\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

The latter follows from Proposition
2.7
. Further, we used that
D_{n}=D_{n}^{1}\times D_{n}^{2}
, where
D_{n}^{1}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})
and
D_{n}^{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})
(since
D_{n}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})=\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})\times\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})
, where the latter holds by, e.g.
[
4
, Theorem D.4]
).
∎

3.2
Proof of Theorem
B

We now present the proof of the Berry-Esseen bound.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

First, we assume that either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. Then, we recall identity (
12
) from Lemma
3.2
, i.e.,

\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)}+\sqrt{n}R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big),

where
S_{n}^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q})
,
S_{n}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1)
and
S_{n}^{(3)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1)
with iid
(\zeta_{i},\eta_{i})\sim\gamma_{p}\otimes\gamma_{q}
,
i\in\N
. We can apply Proposition
2.5
with
Y_{1}:=S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)}
,
Y_{2}:=\sqrt{n}R\Big(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)
and
Y_{3}:=0
, which yields, for
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{p,q}^{2})
and
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),Z\right)&\leq d_{Kol}\left(S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)},Z\right)\\
&+\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}\left|R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]+\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{p,q}^{2}}}.\end{split}

(20)

By the definition of
(S_{n}^{(i)})_{n\in\N}
for
i=1,2,3
, we have

S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q}+\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}\left(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1\right)+\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}\left(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1\right)\right),

which is a sum of iid centered random variables with finite third moments. Hence, the classical Berry-Esseen theorem (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Chapter XVI.5, Theorem 1]
) gives us a constant
C_{1}\in(0,\infty)
such that

d_{Kol}\left(S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}S_{n}^{(3)},Z\right)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{n}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

(21)

Now, we establish an upper bound of the same order for

\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}\left|R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right],\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

We recall the local behavior of the function
R
around zero given in Lemma
3.2
. We have that there exist constants
M,\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
|R(x,y,z)|\leq M||(x,y,z)||_{2}^{2}
for all
(x,y,z)\in\R^{3}
with
||(x,y,z)||_{2}\leq\delta
. This gives us the following estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}\left|R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]&\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}>\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{2\sqrt{n}M}}\right]\\
&+\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}>\delta\right].\end{split}

(22)

For a random vector
Y=(Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3})\in\R^{3}
and for every
\overline{\delta}\in(0,\infty)
, we have the following upper bound

\mathbb{P}\left[||Y||_{2}>\overline{\delta}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[|Y_{1}|>\frac{\overline{\delta}}{\sqrt{3}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[|Y_{2}|>\frac{\overline{\delta}}{\sqrt{3}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[|Y_{3}|>\frac{\overline{\delta}}{\sqrt{3}}\right].

Applying this to the right-hand side in Equation (
22
) leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{n}\left|R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right]\leq P_{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+P_{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+P_{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

where
P_{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right):=\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{S_{n}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|>\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{6\sqrt{n}M}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{S_{n}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|>\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{3}}\right]
,
i=1,2,3
. To bound these quantities, we use
[
7
, Lemma 2.9]
with
\epsilon=\epsilon_{n}=\tilde{C}_{p,q}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\beta_{n}=\overline{C}_{p,q}n
, where
\tilde{C}_{p,q},\overline{C}_{p,q}\in(0,\infty)
are suitably chosen constants only depending on
p
and
q
. As shown in
[
7
, Section 5.3]
, there exist constants
C_{i,p,q}\in(0,\infty)
,
i=1,2,3
, such that

P_{n}\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\leq\frac{C_{i,p,q}}{\sqrt{n}},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

For the quantity in (
20
), by combining the previous estimate and (
21
), we get

d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),Z\right)\leq\frac{\hat{C}_{p,q}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}},

with
\hat{C}_{p,q}:=C_{1}+C_{1,p,q}+C_{2,p,q}+C_{3,p,q}
. Since
\epsilon_{n}=\tilde{C}_{p,q}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}
, we can find a constant
C_{p,q}\in(0,\infty)
such that

d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),Z\right)\leq C_{p,q}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N,

(23)

as claimed.

Now we consider the case when
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}
and recall that
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X^{(n)}_{i}Y^{(n)}_{i}|}{||X^{(n)}||_{p}||Y^{(n)}||_{q}}
. Further, let
(\tilde{X}^{(n)},\tilde{Y}^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
and define
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{p,q}^{(n)}:=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\tilde{X}^{(n)}_{i}\tilde{Y}^{(n)}_{i}|}{||\tilde{X}^{(n)}||_{p}||\tilde{Y}^{(n)}||_{q}}
. We want to show that there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),\sqrt{n}\big(\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big)\right)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

For a fixed
t\in\R
, we recall that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}\geq t]=\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n,t})
as well as
\mathbb{P}[\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}^{(n)}\geq t]=\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n,t})

with the set
D_{n,t}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|}{||x||_{p}||y||_{q}}\geq t\Big\}
. Thus, for all
n\in\N
, we have

\displaystyle d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),\sqrt{n}\big(\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle=d_{Kol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)},\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}^{(n)}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in\R}\big|\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n,t})-\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n,t})\big|

\displaystyle\leq||\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}-\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}||_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

(25)

Equation (
24
) follows immediately from the definition of the Kolmogorov distance. The estimate in (
25
) follows from Proposition
2.7
, since, for some
A=A_{1}\times A_{2}
with
A_{1}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})
and
A_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sigma_{p}^{(n)}\otimes\sigma_{q}^{(n)}(A)-\mu_{p}^{(n)}\otimes\mu_{q}^{(n)}(A)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sigma_{p}^{(n)}(A_{1})-\mu_{p}^{(n)}(A_{1})\right|+\left|\sigma_{q}^{(n)}(A_{2})-\mu_{q}^{(n)}(A_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\sigma_{p}^{(n)}-\mu_{p}^{(n)}\right|\right|_{TV}+\left|\left|\sigma_{q}^{(n)}-\mu_{q}^{(n)}\right|\right|_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

By maximizing over all such
A
, we get
||\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}-\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}||_{TV}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}
.
Now, for a
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{p,q}^{2})
, we get the following estimate

\displaystyle d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big),Z\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\big(\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}-m_{p,q}\Big),\sqrt{n}\big(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\Big)\right)+d_{Kol}\left(\sqrt{n}\Big(\mathscr{\tilde{R}}_{p,q}-m_{p,q}\Big),Z\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}+C_{p,q}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C+C_{p,q}\right)\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}},

where we used the bound established in (
23
) and the first part of this proof.
∎

3.3
Proof of Theorem
C

In the proofs of our main results, we frequently use the probabilistic representation of random variables distributed according to the cone measure
\mu_{p}^{(n)}
(see Equation (
9
)). For the surface measure things are more delicate as we do not have such a representation. In order to establish large deviation and moderate deviation results for the surface measure
\sigma_{p}^{(n)}
, we will need the following exponential equivalence of the cone measure
\mu_{p}^{(n)}
and the surface measure
\sigma_{p}^{(n)}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\R)
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and define

D_{n}:=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|}{||x||_{p}||y||_{q}}\in A\Big\}.

Then, it holds that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})-\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})\Big|=0,

where
(s_{n})_{n\in\N}
is a positive sequence with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log n}{s_{n}}=0
.

Proof.

Recall the Lebesgue-density
\frac{d\sigma^{(n)}_{p}}{d\mu^{(n)}_{p}}(x)=C_{n,p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2p-2}\Big)^{1/2}=:h_{n,p}(x)
, where
C_{n,p}\in(0,\infty)
denotes the normalizing constant. By Lemma 2.2 in
[
8
]
, there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, we have
n^{-C}\leq h_{n,p}(x)\leq n^{C}
. Further, we can write
D_{n}=D_{n}^{1}\times D_{n}^{2}
, where
D_{n}^{1}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})
and
D_{n}^{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})
(note that
D_{n}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})=\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1})\times\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})
, where the latter holds, e.g., by Theorem D.4 in
[
4
]
). We hence get the following estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})-\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})\Big|\leq&\Big|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}(D^{1}_{n})-\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}(D^{1}_{n})\Big|\\
&+\Big|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D^{2}_{n})-\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D^{2}_{n})\Big|.\end{split}

(26)

We consider the first expression on the right-hand side in (
26
), where we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}(D^{1}_{n})-\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}(D^{1}_{n})\Big|=\left|\frac{1}{s_{n}}\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{p}^{(n)}(D_{n}^{1})}{\mu_{p}^{(n)}(D_{n}^{1})}\right)\right|\leq C\frac{\log\left(n\right)}{s_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Here, we used that
n^{-C}\mu_{p}^{(n)}(D_{n}^{1})\leq\sigma_{p}^{(n)}(D_{n}^{1})\leq n^{C}\mu_{p}^{(n)}(D_{n}^{1})
. The second term in (
26
) can be treated analogously.
∎

We will now present the proof of the large deviation principle.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

First, assume that either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{p})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{q})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. We use the probabilistic representation of
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
and Cramér’s theorem (see Proposition
2.2
) together with the contraction principle (see Lemma
2.1
). By Lemma
3.1
, we have that

\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N,

where
(\zeta_{i})_{i\in\N}
is an iid sequence of
p
-generalized Gaussian distributed random variables and
(\eta_{i})_{i\in\N}
is an iid sequence of
q
-generalized Gaussian distributed random variables and both are independent. We consider the sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
with

\xi_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|,|\zeta_{i}|^{p},|\eta_{i}|^{q}\big)\in\R^{3},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N.

(27)

The summands on the right-hand side in (
27
) are iid and thus, we want to apply Cramér’s theorem (see Proposition
2.2
) in order to establish an LDP for
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
. We do this by showing that the cumulant generating function
\Lambda:\R^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
with

\Lambda(r,s,t):=\log\E\big[e^{r|\zeta_{1}\eta_{1}|+s|\zeta_{1}|^{p}+t|\eta_{1}|^{q}}\big],

(28)

is finite in some ball around
0\in\R^{3}
.
Using the density in (
10
), we can write (
28
) as

\Lambda(r,s,t)=\log\int_{\R^{2}}e^{r|xy|+s|x|^{p}+t|y|^{q}-\frac{1}{p}|x|^{p}-\frac{1}{q}|y|^{q}}c_{p,q}dx\,dy,

where
c_{p,q}=\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{p})}\frac{1}{2q^{1/q}\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{q})}
. Then,
\Lambda(r,s,t)
is finite if and only if

c_{p,q}\int_{\R}\int_{\R}e^{r|xy|+\big(s-\frac{1}{p}\big)|x|^{p}+\big(t-\frac{1}{q}\big)|y|^{q}}dx\,dy<\infty.

Let us fix a point
(r,s,t)\in\R^{3}
with
|r|<\epsilon
,
|s|<\epsilon
and
|t|<\epsilon
, where we choose
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2(1+\max(q,p))}
. Then, using that
|xy|\leq\frac{1}{p}|x|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}|y|^{q}
for all
x,y\in\R
, we get the following estimate

\displaystyle c_{p,q}\int_{\R}\int_{\R}e^{r|xy|+\big(s-\frac{1}{p}\big)|x|^{p}+\big(t-\frac{1}{q}\big)|y|^{q}}dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{p,q}\int_{\R}\int_{\R}e^{\Big(s-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{r}{p}\Big)|x|^{p}}e^{\Big(t-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{r}{q}\Big)|y|^{q}}dx\,dy<\infty.

The integral on the right-hand side is finite, since
\Big(s-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{r}{p}\Big)<0
and
\Big(t-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{r}{q}\Big)<0
by our choice of
\epsilon
.
So, we are able to find an open neighborhood
U\subseteq\R^{3}
around zero such that
\Lambda(r,s,t)<\infty
for all
(r,s,t)\in U
.
Now we can apply Cramér’s theorem to the sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
defined in (
27
). It follows that
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\N}
satisfies an LDP in
\R^{3}
at speed
n
with GRF
\Lambda^{*}:\R^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\Lambda^{*}(u,v,w):=\sup_{(r,s,t)\in\R^{3}}\big[ru+tv+sw-\Lambda(r,s,t)\big].

Consider the continuous mapping
F:\R\times(0,\infty)^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
with

F(u,v,w):=\frac{u}{v^{1/p}w^{1/q}}.

We see that

\displaystyle F(\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|}{\big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\zeta_{i}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}\big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\big)^{1/q}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N,

where we used that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Hence, the contraction principle (see Lemma
2.1
) applied to the sequence
(F(\xi_{n}))_{n\in\N}
gives us an LDP for
(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)})_{n\in\N}
at speed
n
with GRF
\I:\R\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\I(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\Big\{\Lambda^{*}(u,v,w)\ :\ x=\frac{u}{v^{1/p}w^{1/q}}\Big\},&\text{if }x>0\\
+\infty&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

Now we assume that
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q},n\in\N
. For a closed set
A\subseteq\R
, by Lemma
3.3
, we get

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}\in A\big]=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})\leq-\inf_{x\in A}\mathbb{I}(x).

D_{n}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|}{||x||_{p}||y||_{q}}\in A\Big\}
is the set from Lemma
3.3
and we have used the large deviation upper bound which holds for
\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. This proves the large deviation upper bound for
\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}
. The lower bound can be shown analogously.
∎

3.4
Proof of Theorem
D

We now present the proof of the MDP.

Proof of Theorem
D
.

First, we assume that either,
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\U(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})\otimes\U(\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n})
or
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}
. We work with the sequence

\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N,

where
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}
is the quantity from Equation (
11
) and
m_{p,q}=p^{1/p}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}q^{1/q}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}
. Using the Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}
given in Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{1}{b_{n}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{b_{n}p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{b_{n}q}S_{n}^{(3)}+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

(29)

where
S_{n}^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}|-m_{p,q})
,
S_{n}^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\zeta_{i}|^{p}-1)
and
S_{n}^{(3)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|\eta_{i}|^{q}-1)
with iid
(\zeta_{i},\eta_{i})\sim\gamma_{p}\otimes\gamma_{q}
,
i\in\N
. First, we show that the quantity in (
29
) and

Y_{n}:=\frac{1}{b_{n}}S_{n}^{(1)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{b_{n}p}S_{n}^{(2)}-\frac{m_{p,q}}{b_{n}q}S_{n}^{(3)},\hskip 10.00002ptn\in\N

(30)

are exponentially equivalent on the scale
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
. To that end, for
\epsilon>0
, we consider

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}\left(\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}-m_{p,q}\right)-Y_{n}\right|>\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\epsilon\right].

Here, we take a closer look at the probability in the logarithm on the right-hand side. For sufficiently large
n\in\N
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\geq\delta\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}^{2}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}^{2}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{b_{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}^{2}>\frac{\epsilon}{M}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}\right],

where we used the properties of the function
R
from Lemma
3.2
. For a value
T\in(0,\infty)
, we have
\frac{\epsilon}{M}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}>T
for all sufficiently large
n\in\N
, since
\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
tends to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The sequence
\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{b_{n}}\right)_{n\in\N}
satisfies an MDP at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
by Proposition
2.4
with GRF
\mathbb{J}:\R^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, where

\mathbb{J}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}x\rangle,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\R^{3}.

(31)

\mathbf{C_{p,q}}\in\R^{3x3}
is the positive definite covariance matrix of the vector
\left(|\zeta_{1}\eta_{1}|,|\zeta_{1}|^{p},|\eta_{1}|^{q}\right)\in\R^{3}
(see the proof of Theorem
A
). Thus, by combining the upper bound of the MDP and the contraction principle (see Lemma
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\left|\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{b_{n}}\right)\right|\right|_{2}^{2}>T\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\inf\left\{x^{t}\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}x\ :\ ||x||_{2}^{2}>T\right\}

\displaystyle\leq-T\lambda_{min}(\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}),

where
\lambda_{min}(\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1})\in(0,\infty)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}
. Since the previous bound holds for any
T\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\frac{\sqrt{n}}{b_{n}}R\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right|>\epsilon\right]=-\infty,

showing the exponential equivalence at scale
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
as desired. Given that, we need to prove an MDP at rate
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
for the sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\N}
defined in Equation (
30
). For
n\in\N
, we have
Y_{n}=\left\langle\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{b_{n}}\right),d_{p,q}\right\rangle
, where
d_{p,q}=\left(1,-\frac{m_{p,q}}{p},-\frac{m_{p,q}}{q}\right)
. We recall that
\left(\frac{S_{n}^{(1)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(2)}}{b_{n}},\frac{S_{n}^{(3)}}{b_{n}}\right)_{n\in\N}
satisfies an MDP in
\R^{3}
at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
with GRF
\mathbb{J}:\R^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
. Now, we can apply the contraction principle (see Lemma
2.1
) in order to establish an MDP for the sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\N}
in
\R
at speed
(b_{n}^{2})_{n\in\N}
with GRF
\I:\R\rightarrow[0,\infty]
, where

\I(t):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}x\rangle\ :\ \langle d_{p,q},x\rangle=t\right\}.

(32)

We are able to give a closed form of
\I
by using the Lagrange method for optimization. For this, we fix
t\in\R
and consider the Lagrange function
L:\R^{4}\rightarrow\R
given by

L(x,\lambda):=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}x\rangle+\lambda(t-\langle d_{p,q},x\rangle),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\R^{3},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\R.

The directional derivatives are

\left(\frac{\partial L(x,\lambda)}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial L(x,\lambda)}{\partial x_{2}},\frac{\partial L(x,\lambda)}{\partial x_{3}}\right)=\mathbf{C_{p,q}}^{-1}x-\lambda d_{p,q}=0\in\R^{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}L(x,\lambda)=t-\langle d_{p,q},x\rangle=0.

This system of equations can be solved elementary and for the solution
x=x(t)
, we get

x(t)=\frac{\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}}{\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle}t,

where we mention that
d_{p,q}\neq(0,0,0)
and hence
\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle\in(0,\infty)
. Finally, our GRF
\I:\R\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is given as

\I(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle},\hskip 10.00002ptt\in\R,

where the quantity
\sigma_{p,q}^{2}=\langle d_{p,q},\mathbf{C_{p,q}}d_{p,q}\rangle\in(0,\infty)
is as claimed in Theorem
D
.

Now we consider the case when
(X^{(n)},Y^{(n)})\sim\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}
,
n\in\N
. We fix a closed set
A\subseteq\R
and denote
D_{n}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}\ :\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}y_{i}|}{||x||_{p}||y||_{q}}\in A\Big\}
. We then get

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{R}_{p,q}^{(n)}\in A\right]=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\sigma^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\sigma^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mu^{(n)}_{p}\otimes\mu^{(n)}_{q}(D_{n})\leq-\inf_{t\in A}\mathbb{I}(t),

where we have used Lemma
3.3
and the assumption
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{\log n}}=\infty
. This establishes the upper bound of the MDP. The lower bound can be shown analogously.
∎

Guo-Jie Li


Abstract




\ell
-Log-momotonic and Laguerre Inequality of P-recursive Sequences

Abstract.

We consider
\ell
-log-momotonic sequences and Laguerre inequality of order two for sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
such that

\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{r_{i}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha_{i}}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}\right),

where
m
is a nonnegative integer,
\alpha_{i}
are real numbers,
r_{i}(x)
are rational functions of
x
and

0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{m}<\beta.

We will give a sufficient condition on
\ell
-log-momotonic sequences and Laguerre inequality of order two for
n
sufficiently large.
Many P-recursive sequences fall in this frame. At last, we will give a method to find the
N
such that for any
n\geq N
, log-momotonic inequality of order three and Laguerre inequality of order two holds.

Keywords:
log-momotonic, Laguerre inequality, P-recursive sequence, asymptotic estimation.

AMS Classifications:
41A60, 05A20, 41A58.

1
Introduction

The Turán inequalities arise in the study
of Maclaurin coefficients of real entire functions in the Laguerre-Pólya class
[
15
]
.
A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of real numbers is said to satisfy the Turán inequalities
or to be log-concave, if

a_{n}^{2}-a_{n-1}a_{n+1}\geq 0,\quad\forall\,n\geq 1.

The Turán inequalities are also called Newton’s inequality
[
17
,
10
]
. For more results on the log-concavity, we refer to
[
3
,
9
,
13
]
.

Let
\varphi
be the operator defined on sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
by

\varphi\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}=\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},

where
b_{n}=a_{n+1}/a_{n}
. A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-monotonic of order
k
if for
r
odd and not greater than
k-1
, the sequence

\varphi^{r}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-concave and for
r
even and not great than
k-1
, the sequence
\varphi^{r}\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-convex.
A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is called infinitely log-monotonic if it is log-monotonic of order
k
for all integers
k\geq 1
.
Log-monotonic of order
\ell
is also called as
\ell
-log-monotonic.

A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is said to be ratio log-concave if
\{a_{n+1}/a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is log-concave. Chen, Guo and Wang
[
2
]

proved that under certain initial condition, the ratio log-concavity of a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq k}
of positive numbers implies that the sequence

\{\sqrt[n]{a_{n}}\}_{n\geq k}
is strictly log-concave.

A function
f
is said to be completely monotonic on an interval
I
if
f
has derivatives of all orders on
I
and

(-1)^{n}f^{(n)}(x)\geq 0

for
x\in I
and all integers
n\geq 0
. Chen, Guo and Wang
[
2
]
established a connection between completely momotonic functions and infinitely
log-monotonic sequences and showed the sequences of the Bernoulli numbers, the Catalan numbers and the central binomial coefficients are infinitely log-monotonic.

The Laguerre inequality
[
7
]
arises in the study of the real polynomials with only real zeros and the Laguerre-Pólya class consisting of real entire functions. Recall
that a real entire function

\varphi(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}

is said to be in the Laguerre-Pólya class, denoted by
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{LP}
, if it can be represented in the form

\varphi(x)=cx^{m}e^{-\alpha x^{2}+\beta x}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+x/x_{k})e^{-x/x_{k}},

where
c
,
\beta
,
x_{k}
are real numbers,
\alpha\geq 0
,
m
is a nonnegative integer and
\sum x_{k}^{-2}<\infty
. For more background on the theory of the

\mathcal{LP}
class, we refer to
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
.

Recall that if a polynomial
f(x)
satisfies

f^{\prime}(x)^{2}-f(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0,

then it is called to satisfy Laguerre inequality. Laguerre stated that if
f(x)
is a polynomial with only real zeros, then the Laguerre inequality holds for
f(x)
.
Gasper
[
4
]
used it as an important tool to deal with the positivity of special function.

In 1913, Jensen
[
6
]
found a
n
-th generalization of the Laguerre inequality

L_{n}(f(x)):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{n+k}\binom{2n}{k}f^{k}(x)f^{2n-k}(x)\geq 0,

where
f^{k}(x)
denotes the
k
-th derivative of
f(x)
. It yields the classical Laguerre inequality for
n=1
.

Yang and Wang
[
12
]
defined a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order
m
if

L_{m}(a_{n}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}(-1)^{k+m}\binom{2m}{k}a_{n+k}a_{2m-k+n}\geq 0.

For
m=1
, the above inequality reduces to log-concavity of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
. Yang and Wang
[
12
]
proved some combinatorial sequences satisfy the Laguerre inequality of order two. Wagner
[
16
]
recently proved that the partition function
p(n)
satisfied all of the Laguerre inequalities of order
m
as
n\to\infty
and proposed a conjecture
on the thresholds of the
m
-th Laguerre inequalities of
p(n)
for
m\leq 10
.

We focus on the behaviour of a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
when
n
is sufficiently large. We say a sequence satisfies Log-momotonic inequality and Laguerre inequality asymptotically if for
n
sufficiently large the Log-monotonic inequality and Laguerre inequality holds. We aim to give a criterion on the
\ell
-log-momotonic inequality and Laguerre inequality of order two for P-recursive sequences. A P-recursive sequence of order
d
satisfies a recurrence relation of the form

a_{n}=r_{1}(n)a_{n-1}+r_{2}(n)a_{n-1}+\cdots+r_{d}(n)a_{n-d},

where
r_{i}(n)
are rational functions of
n
, see
[
14
, Section 6.4]
.

By the asymptotic estimation given by Birkhoff and Trjitzinsky
[
1
]
and developed by Wimp and Zeilberger
[
18
]
, Hou and Li
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
proved that many P-recursive sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfy

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{r_{i}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha_{i}}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}\right),

(1.1)

where
m
is a nonnegative integer,
\alpha_{i}
are real numbers,
r_{i}(x)
are rational functions of
x
and

0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{m}<\beta.

Hou and Li
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
also give a estimation we will use later.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
r(x)
be a rational function of
x
and
K
be a positive integer. Then

r(\log(n+1))-r(\log n)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{r_{i}(\log n)}{n^{i}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{K}}\right),

for some rational functions
r_{i}(x)
. Similarly,

r(\log(n-1))-r(\log n)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\tilde{r}_{i}(\log n)}{n^{i}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{K}}\right),

for some rational functions
\tilde{r}_{i}(x)
. If
r(x)
is a polynomial, are the
r_{i}(x),\tilde{r}_{i}(x)
’s. Moreover,

r_{1}(x)=-\tilde{r}_{1}(x)=r^{\prime}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{2}(x)=\tilde{r}_{2}(x)=\frac{r^{\prime\prime}(x)-r^{\prime}(x)}{2}.

With the asymptotic form (
1.1
) and Lemma
1.1
, we are able to give a sufficient condition on the
\ell
-log-monotonic inequality in Section
2
. Then in Section
3
, we will give a sufficient condition on the asymptotic Laguerre inequality of order two.
We then give a method to find the
N
such that for any
n\geq N
the log-monotonic inequality of order three holds in section
4
. At last, we give a method
to find the
N
such that for any
n\geq N
the Laguerre inequality of order two holds in section
5
.

2
Asymptotically
\ell
-log-monotonic

In this section, we give a sufficient condition such that the sequence is
\ell
-log-momotonic.

We first give a lemma we will use later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r(x)
be a rational function of
x
,
\xi(n)=\frac{r(\log n)}{n^{\gamma}}
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
are real numbers and
\gamma>0
,
\alpha>0
, then we have the following estimation

(n+1)^{\alpha}\xi(n)-n^{\alpha}\xi(n+1)=r(\log n)n^{\alpha-\gamma-1}\left(\alpha+\gamma+o(1)\right),

and

\displaystyle\xi(n+1)(n-1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}+\xi(n-1)(n+1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}-2\xi(n)(n-1)^{\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(\log n)n^{2\alpha-\gamma-2}\big(\left(\alpha+\gamma\right)\left(\alpha+\gamma+1\right)+o(1)\big),

and

\displaystyle\xi(n-1)\xi(n+1)n^{2\alpha}-\xi^{2}(n)(n+1)^{\alpha}(n-1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}(\log n)n^{2\alpha-2\gamma-2}\left(\alpha+\gamma+o(1)\right).

Proof.
Since
n\to\infty
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r^{\prime}(\log n)}{r(\log n)}=0.

By Lemma
1.1
we have

\frac{r(\log(n+1))}{r(\log n)}=1+\frac{r^{\prime}(\log n)}{r(\log n)n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

and

\frac{r(\log(n-1))}{r(\log n)}=1-\frac{r^{\prime}(\log n)}{r(\log n)n}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Then we can deduce that

\displaystyle(n+1)^{\alpha}\xi(n)-n^{\alpha}\xi(n+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r(\log n)n^{\alpha}}{n^{\gamma}}\big[\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\alpha}-\frac{r(\log(n+1))}{r(\log n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-\gamma}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(\log n)n^{\alpha-\gamma-1}\left[\alpha+\gamma+o(1)\right].

And

\displaystyle\xi(n+1)(n-1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}+\xi(n-1)(n+1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}-2\xi(n)(n-1)^{\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(\log n)n^{2\alpha-\gamma}\big[\frac{r(\log(n+1))}{r(\log n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-\gamma}+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\frac{r(\log(n-1))}{r(\log n)}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-\gamma}-2\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(\log n)n^{2\alpha-\gamma-2}\left[\alpha(\alpha+1)+\gamma(\gamma+1)+2\alpha\gamma+o(1)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(\log n)n^{2\alpha-\gamma-2}\left[(\alpha+\gamma)(\alpha+\gamma+1)+o(1)\right].

And

\displaystyle\xi(n-1)\xi(n+1)n^{2\alpha}-\xi^{2}(n)(n+1)^{\alpha}(n-1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}(\log n)n^{2\alpha-2\gamma}\big[\frac{r(\log(n+1)r(\log(n-1)))}{r^{2}(\log n)}\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)^{-\gamma}-\left(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}(\log n)n^{2\alpha-2\gamma-2}\left[\alpha+\gamma+o(1)\right].

Now I will give the main Theorem of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence such that (
1.1
) holds.
Assume that
\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{1}\geq 1
and denote
\ell=\lfloor\alpha_{m}/\alpha_{1}\rfloor
. If
r_{1}(x)>0
for
x
sufficiently large,
then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the
\ell
-log-monotonic inequality asymptotically.

Proof.
Let

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}},\quad b_{n}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}.

Denote

\alpha_{1}=\alpha,\quad u_{n}=1+\frac{\xi(n)}{n^{\alpha}}.

Then
\xi(n)
has the form

\xi(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{r_{i}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha_{i}-\alpha}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta-\alpha}}\right).

Let
\varphi
be the operator defined on sequence
\{a_{n}\}
by

\varphi\{a_{n}\}=\{b_{n}\}=\{\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}}\},

It is easy to check that

\frac{\varphi\{a_{n-1}\}\varphi\{a_{n+1}\}}{(\varphi\{a_{n}\})^{2}}=\frac{b_{n-1}b_{n+1}}{b_{n}^{2}}=\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n}}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle u_{n}-u_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)^{\alpha}\xi(n)-n^{\alpha}\xi(n+1)}{n^{\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{1}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha+1}}\left(\alpha+o(1)\right)>0,\quad n\to\infty.

Then we have

\frac{\varphi\{a_{n-1}\}\varphi\{a_{n+1}\}}{(\varphi\{a_{n}\})^{2}}=1+\frac{u_{n+1}-u_{n}}{u_{n}}=1+(-1)\frac{r_{1}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha+1}}\left(\alpha+o(1)\right).

Repeating this argument, we have

\frac{\varphi^{k}\{a_{n-1}\}\varphi^{k}\{a_{n+1}\}}{(\varphi^{k}\{a_{n}\})^{2}}=1+(-1)^{k}\frac{r_{1}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha+k}}\left((\alpha)_{k}+o(1)\right),

where

(\alpha)_{k}=1
,
k=0
.

(\alpha)_{k}=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+k-1)
,
k>0
.

So we have
n\to\infty
,

\frac{\varphi^{k}\{a_{n-1}\}\varphi^{k}\{a_{n+1}\}}{(\varphi^{k}\{a_{n}\})^{2}}<1
,  if
k
is odd,

\frac{\varphi^{k}\{a_{n-1}\}\varphi^{k}\{a_{n+1}\}}{(\varphi^{k}\{a_{n}\})^{2}}>1
,  if
k
is even,

ending the proof of the Theorem.

Example 2.1
.

By Theorem
2.2
, when
u_{n}
are of the following form, the corresponding
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the asymptotic
\ell
-log-monotonic inequality.

1+\frac{1}{n},\quad 1+\frac{1}{n\log n},\quad 1+\frac{2}{n^{2}},\quad 1+\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}.

Noting that for
a_{n}=n!
, we have

u_{n}=\frac{n+1}{n}=1+\frac{1}{n}>0,\quad\forall\,n\geq 0.

Hence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the
\ell
-log-monotonic inequality for
n
sufficiently large.

3
Asymptotically Laguerre inequality of order two

In this section, we will give a sufficient condition such that the sequence is asymptotic Laguerre inequality of order two.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence such that (
1.1
) holds.
Assume that
\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{1}\geq 1
. If

r_{1}(x)>0
for
x
sufficiently large,

or

\alpha_{1}<2
,
r_{1}(x)<0
for
x
sufficiently large,

or

\alpha_{1}=2
,
r_{1}(x)<-1
for
x
sufficiently large,

then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two asymptotically.

Proof.
We only need to prove if
n\to\infty
, the following inequality holds

3a_{n+2}^{2}-4a_{n+1}a_{n+3}+a_{n}a_{n+4}>0.

(3.2)

Denote

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}},

dividing the equation (
3.2
) by
a_{n+2}^{2}
and by some simple calculation, we need to prove if
n\to\infty
, the following inequalities holds

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}^{2}u_{n+1}-4u_{n}+3>0.

(3.3)

Denote

\alpha_{1}=\alpha,\quad u_{n}=1+\frac{\xi(n)}{n^{\alpha}},

Then we have

\xi(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{r_{i}(\log n)}{n^{\alpha_{i}-\alpha}}+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta-\alpha}}\right).

Let

f(n)=u_{n-1}u_{n+1}^{2}u_{n+1}-4u_{n}+3,

then we have

f(n)=\frac{t_{1}(n)+t_{2}(n)+t_{3}(n)+t_{4}(n)}{(n-1)^{\alpha}n^{2\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}},

where

t_{1}(n)=\big[\xi(n+1)(n-1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}+\xi(n-1)(n+1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}-2\xi(n)(n-1)^{\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}\big]n^{\alpha},

and

\displaystyle t_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\xi(n-1)\xi(n+1)n^{2\alpha}+\xi(n)^{2}(n-1)^{\alpha}(n+1)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+2\xi(n-1)\xi(n)(n+1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}+2\xi(n+1)\xi(n)(n-1)^{\alpha}n^{\alpha},

and

t_{3}(n)=\big[2\xi(n-1)\xi(n+1)n^{\alpha}+\xi(n+1)\xi(n)(n-1)^{\alpha}+\xi(n-1)\xi(n)(n+1)^{\alpha}\big]\xi(n),

and

t_{4}(n)=\xi(n-1)\xi^{2}(n)\xi(n+1).

By Lemma
2.1
we have

t_{1}(n)=r_{1}(\log n)n^{3\alpha-2}\left(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+o(1)\right),

and

t_{2}(n)=6r_{1}^{2}(\log n)n^{2\alpha}\left(1+o(1)\right),

and

t_{3}(n)=4r_{1}^{3}(\log n)n^{\alpha}\left(1+o(1)\right),

and

t_{4}(n)=r_{1}^{3}(\log n)\left(1+o(1)\right).

Denote

t(n)=t_{1}(n)+t_{2}(n)+t_{3}(n)+t_{4}(n).

If
r_{1}(\log n)>0
, then
n\to\infty
,

t(n)>0
, then
f(n)>0
.

If
r_{1}(\log n)<0
, then
n\to\infty
:

If
\alpha<2
, the main term of
t(n)
is
t_{2}(n)
, we have

f(n)=\frac{6r_{1}^{2}(\log n)}{n^{2\alpha}}\left(1+o(1)\right)>0.

If
\alpha=2
, the main term of
t(n)
is
t_{1}(n)+t_{2}(n)
, we have

f(n)=\frac{6r_{1}(\log n)(1+r_{1}(\log n))}{n^{4}}\left(1+o(1)\right).

So if
r_{1}(\log n)<-1
, we have

f(n)>0.

Example 3.1
.

By Theorem
3.1
, when
u_{n}
are of the following form, the corresponding
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two asymptotically.

1\pm\frac{1}{n},\quad 1-\frac{1}{n\log n},\quad 1-\frac{2}{n^{2}},\quad 1-\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}.

Noting that for
a_{n}=n!
, we have

u_{n}=\frac{n+1}{n}=1+\frac{1}{n}.

Hence
\{n!\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two for
n
sufficiently large. In fact,

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}^{2}u_{n+1}-4u_{n}+3=\frac{6}{n(n-1)}>0,\quad\forall\,n\geq 2.

Similarly, if
a_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}
, we have

u_{n}=\frac{n}{n+1}=1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}+o(n^{-2-\delta}),\quad 0<\delta<1,

Hence
\{\frac{1}{n!}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two for
n
sufficiently large. In fact,

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}^{2}u_{n+1}-4u_{n}+3=\frac{6}{(n+1)(n+2)}>0,\quad\forall\,n\geq 2.

4
Log-momotonic equality of order three

In this section, we will give a method to find the explicit
N
such that log-momotonic equality of order three holds for

\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
when
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is P-recursive.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}.

If there exist an integer
N_{1}
, an upper bound
f_{n}
and a lower bound
g_{n}
of
u_{n}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,

0<g_{n}<u_{n}<f_{n},

and

g_{n}>1,\quad n\geq N_{2},

g_{n}-f_{n+1}>0,\quad n\geq N_{3},

g_{n-1}g_{n+1}-f^{2}_{n}>0,\quad n\geq N_{4}.

Let
N=\max\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3},N_{4}\}
, Then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
satisfies log-momotonic equality of order three.

Proof.
We only need to prove if
n\geq N
, the following three inequalities holds

\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}>1,

and

\frac{a_{n}}{a_{n-1}}\frac{a_{n+2}}{a_{n+1}}<\frac{a_{n+1}^{2}}{a_{n}^{2}},

and

\frac{a_{n-2}a_{n}}{a_{n-1}^{2}}\frac{a_{n}a_{n+2}}{a_{n+1}^{2}}>\left(\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

Denote

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}},

By some simple calculations, we only need to prove
n\geq N
, the following three inequalities holds.

u_{n}>1,

and

u_{n}-u_{n+1}>0,

and

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}-u_{n}^{2}>0.

If
n\geq N
, we have

u_{n}>g_{n}>1,

and

u_{n}-u_{n+1}>g_{n}-f_{n+1}>0,

and

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}-u^{2}_{n}>g_{n-1}g_{n+1}-f^{2}_{n}>0.

From Theorem
4.1
, we see the main thing we have to do is to find a appropriate upper bounds and lower bounds of
u_{n}
.
We will use the asymptotic estimation of P-recursive sequences given by Birkhoff and Trjitzinsky
[
1
]
and developed by Wimp and Zeilberger
[
18
]
. They showed that a P-recursive sequence is asymptotically equal to a linear combination of terms of the form

e^{Q(\rho,n)}s(\rho,n),

(4.4)

where

Q(\rho,n)=\mu_{0}n\log n+\sum_{j=1}^{\rho}\mu_{j}n^{j/\rho},

and

s(\rho,n)=n^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}(\log n)^{j}\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}b_{sj}n^{-s/\rho},

with
\rho
,
t
,
M
being positive integers and
\mu_{j}
,
r
,
b_{sj}
being complex numbers.

When
\{a_{n}\}
has the asymptotic expression (
4.4
) such that
t=1
, we are able to compute the bounds

f_{n}
and
g_{n}
by the HT algorithm. We implemented the HT algorithm in the package
P
-rec.m
which is available at
[
20
]
. For more information about the HT algorithm, we refer the author
to
[
5
]
.

Example 4.1
.

The central trinomial coefficients
\{b_{n}\}
, i.e., the coefficients of
x^{n}
in expansion of
(1+x+x^{2})^{n}
satisfies

b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}.

Then the sequence
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 8}
satisfies log-momotonic equality of order three.

Proof.
Use Zeilberger’s algorithm
[
19
]
we see that
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the following recurrence relation

(n+2)b_{n+2}-(2n+3)b_{n+1}-3(n+1)b_{n}=0,\quad n\geq 0,

with the initial values

b_{0}=1,\quad b_{1}=1.

By the algorithm HT, we have the following estimates:

1+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}-\frac{3}{8n^{3}}+\frac{9}{32n^{4}}-\frac{355}{256n^{5}}<\frac{b_{n+1}b_{n-1}}{b_{n}^{2}}<1+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}-\frac{3}{8n^{3}}+\frac{9}{32n^{4}}+\frac{157}{256n^{5}},\quad n\geq 12.

Let

u_{n}=\frac{b_{n+1}b_{n-1}}{b_{n}^{2}},

and

g_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}-\frac{3}{8n^{3}}+\frac{9}{32n^{4}}-\frac{355}{265n^{5}},

and

f_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}-\frac{3}{8n^{3}}+\frac{9}{32n^{4}}+\frac{157}{265n^{5}}.

Then we have

g_{n}>1,\quad n\geq 2.

g_{n}-f_{n+1}>0,\quad n\geq 2.

g_{n-1}g_{n+1}-f_{n}^{2}>0,\quad n\geq 4.

Checking the initial values, we finally get
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 8}
satisfies log-momotonic equality of order three.

5
Laguerre inequality of order two

In this section, we will give a method to find the explicit
N
such that Laguerre inequality of order two holds for

\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
when
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is P-recursive.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

u_{n}=\frac{a_{n-1}a_{n+1}}{a_{n}^{2}}.

If there exist an integer
N_{1}
, an upper bound
f_{n}
and a lower bound
g_{n}
of
u_{n}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,

0<g_{n}<u_{n}<f_{n},

and

g_{n-1}g_{n}^{2}g_{n+1}-4f_{n}+3>0,\quad N\geq N_{2}.

Let
N=\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, Then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq N}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two.

Proof.
If
n\geq N
, we have

u_{n-1}u_{n+1}^{2}u_{n+1}-4u_{n}+3>g_{n-1}g_{n}^{2}g_{n+1}-4f_{n}+3>0.

Example 5.1
.

Let
M_{n}
be the Motzkin numbers given by

(n+4)M_{n+2}-(2n+5)M_{n+1}-3(n+1)M_{n}=0,\quad n\geq 0,

with the initial values

M_{0}=1,\quad M_{1}=1.

Then the sequence
\{\frac{M_{n}}{n!}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two.

Proof.
By the algorithm HT, we derive

1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{47}{8n^{3}}<\frac{M_{n+1}M_{n-1}}{M_{n}^{2}}<1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{31}{8n^{3}},\quad n\geq 228.

Let

a_{n}=\frac{M_{n}}{n!},\quad u_{n}=\frac{a_{n+1}a_{n-1}}{a_{n}^{2}}.

Then we have

\frac{n}{n+1}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{47}{8n^{3}}\right)<u_{n}<\frac{n}{n+1}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{31}{8n^{3}}\right),\quad n\geq 228.

Let

g_{n}=\frac{n}{n+1}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{47}{8n^{3}}\right),

f_{n}=\frac{n}{n+1}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{2}}-\frac{31}{8n^{3}}\right).

Then we have

g_{n-1}g_{n}^{2}g_{n+1}-4f_{n}+3>0,\quad n\geq 2.

Checking the initial values, we finally get that
\{\frac{M_{n}}{n!}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies Laguerre inequality of order two.

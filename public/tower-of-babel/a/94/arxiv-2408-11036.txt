Distributional category of manifolds
Ekansh Jauhari

Abstract
. Recently, a new homotopy invariant of metric spaces, called the distributional category, was defined, which provides a lower bound to the Lusternik–Schnirelmann (LS) category. In this paper, we obtain several sufficient conditions for the distributional category ( 𝖽𝖼𝖺𝗍 \dcat ) of a closed manifold to be maximum, i.e., equal to its classical LS-category ( 𝖼𝖺𝗍 \cat ). These give us many new computations of 𝖽𝖼𝖺𝗍 \dcat , especially for some essential manifolds and (generalized) connected sums. In the process, we also determine the 𝖽𝖼𝖺𝗍 \dcat of closed 3 3 -manifolds having torsion-free fundamental groups and various closed geometrically decomposable 4 4 -manifolds. Finally, we extend some of our results to closed Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded below and discuss their 𝖼𝖺𝗍 \cat and 𝖽𝖼𝖺𝗍 \dcat in dimension 3 3 .

[

The LS-category of closed manifolds has been well-studied,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

,
[

[

Recently, in a joint work with Dranishnikov

,
[

[

Since the inception of this nascent invariant whose research is an emerging topic, the following tantalizing question has been around.

1.1 Question
.

For what spaces
X
do we have the equality
\dcat(X)=\cat(X)
?

To answer the above question, it is worthwhile to check if the techniques that are typically useful in determining LS-category remain somewhat useful in determining distributional category as well. One such technique is lower bounding the categories of a finite CW complex with its rational cup-length,
[
DJ
]
. Another example is the Eilenberg–Ganea technique
[
EG
]
of expressing the categories of the classifying space of a torsion-free discrete group in terms of the cohomological dimension of the group,
[
KW
]
.

As a result of these techniques, some spaces on which the distributional category agrees with the LS-category include closed surfaces (other than
{\mathbb{R}}P^{2}
), complex projective spaces, finite products of spheres, and classifying spaces of torsion-free discrete groups. Spaces on which the two notions of category don’t agree include real projective spaces and classifying spaces of finite groups,
[
DJ
]
,
[
KW
]
. These examples make Question
1.1
very interesting.

The main objective of this paper is to answer Question
1.1
by finding general classes of compact metric spaces (in particular, closed manifolds) on which the two notions of category coincide. We do this by presenting various sufficient conditions for the distributional category of a closed manifold to be equal to its classical LS-category. We show that careful use of some obstruction-theoretic methods turns out to be very helpful in computing the distributional category in various cases.

As a result of our sufficient conditions, we obtain analogs of some classical results on the LS-category of closed manifolds (especially the two results mentioned in Paragraph
2
above) for the distributional category. Our techniques also enable us to partially extend some of these sufficient conditions to a more general class of closed Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded below,
[
BGP
]
,
[

]

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Motivation for
\dcat

Given a pointed topological space
(Z,z_{0})
, let us define
P(Z)=\left\{\phi:[0,1]\to Z\mid\phi(1)=z_{0}\right\}
, and let
p_{Z}:P(Z)\to Z
be the evaluation fibration defined as
p(\phi)=\phi(0)
for all
\phi\in P(Z)
. For each
n\geq 1
, we define the
n
-th
Ganea space
, denoted
G_{n}(Z)
, to be the fiberwise iterated join of
n
copies of
P(Z)
along
p_{Z}
, i.e.,

G_{n}(Z)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}\hskip 2.84526pt\middle|\hskip 2.84526pt\phi_{i}\in P(Z),\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\phi_{i}(0)=\phi_{j}(0)\right\}.

Each element of
G_{n}(Z)
is a formal ordered linear combination where the terms with weights
\lambda_{i}=0
are dropped.
On this space, we define the
n
-th
Ganea fibration

p_{n}^{Z}:G_{n}(Z)\to Z
such that
p_{n}^{Z}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i})=\phi_{i}(0)
for any
i\leq n
such that
\lambda_{i}>0
.

The following theorem gives the Ganea–Schwarz characterization of the LS-category of pointed maps,
[
Sch
]
,
[
BG
]
,
[
Ja
]
,
[
CLOT
]
.

2.1 Theorem
.

Given a pointed map
f:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
,
\cat(f)<n
if and only if there exists a lift of
f
with respect to the fibration
p_{n}^{Y}:G_{n}(Y)\to Y
.

So,
\cat(f)
is the smallest integer
n
such that each
x\in X
is mapped to a formal
ordered
linear combination of at most
n+1
non-negatively weighted paths in
P(Y)
where each path has initial point
f(x)
and endpoint
y_{0}
.

One of the main motivations for defining the distributional version of this classical notion of LS-category is to think about an invariant that assigns to a given pointed map
f:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
the smallest integer
n
such that each
x\in X
is mapped to a formal
unordered
linear combination of at most
n+1
weighted paths in
P(Y)
having initial point
f(x)
and endpoint
y_{0}
(in
[
DJ
]
, we considered the special case when
f
is the identity map). Then, as in
[
DJ
]
, one can develop a theory of this new invariant parallel to the theory of the classical invariant
\cat
.

2.2.
Distributional category

For a metric space
Z
, let
\mathcal{B}(Z)
denote the set of probability measures
\mu=\sum\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
on
Z
, where
\sum\lambda_{i}=1
,
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
, and
\delta_{x_{i}}
is the point Dirac measure at
x_{i}\in Z
. For any
n\geq 1
, let

\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{B}(Z)\mid|\supp(\mu)|\leq n\right\}

denote the space of probability measures on
Z
supported by at most
n
points, equipped with the Lévy–Prokhorov metric
[
Pr
]
(or the Wasserstein distance metric), see also
[
DJ
, Section 3.1]
. Here,
\supp(\mu)
denotes the support of
\mu
. Given the pointed metric space
(Z,z_{0})
, for each
z\in Z
, let
P(z,z_{0})=\{\phi\in P(Z)\mid\phi(0)=z\}
.

2.2 Definition
.

The
distributional category
of a map
f:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})
, denoted
\dcat(f)
, is the smallest integer
n
for which there exists a continuous map
H:X\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n+1}(P(Y))
such that
H(x)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n+1}(P(f(x),y_{0}))
for all
x\in X
.

Upon taking
f
as the identity map, we get the notion of the distributional category of a space
X
, denoted
\dcat(X)
. For the following discussion, we refer to
[
DJ
, Sections 3.2 and 4.2]
.

Given a space
X
,
\dcat(X)
is a lower bound to its classical LS-category
\cat(X)
. It is easy to see that
\dcat(X)=0
if and only if
X
is contractible. Hence,
\dcat(S^{n})=1
for all
n
. Interestingly,
\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{n})=1
for all
n
, in contrast to the well-known equality
\cat({\mathbb{R}}P^{n})=n
. This result leads to various differences between the theories of
\cat
and
\dcat
, see
[
DJ
, Section 6.1]
and
[
J2
, Remark 4.13]
. In general,
\dcat(X)
can be arbitrarily large; indeed, the rational and the real cup-lengths of
X

are lower bounds to
\dcat(X)
. However, we note that, unlike the case of the classical LS-category, the
R
-cup-length of
X
, denoted
c\ell_{R}(X)
, is not necessarily a lower bound to
\dcat(X)
for a general ring
R
due to the example of
{\mathbb{R}}P^{n}
. Furthermore,
\dcat
is a homotopy invariant of spaces. So, for a finitely generated discrete group
\Gamma
, one can define
\dcat(\Gamma):=\dcat(B\Gamma)
, where
B\Gamma
denotes the unique (up to homotopy) classifying space of
\Gamma
.

The well-known result of Eilenberg and Ganea
[
EG
]
says that
\cat(\Gamma)=\cd(\Gamma)
, where
\cd(\Gamma)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid H^{k}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{A}})\neq 0\text{ for some }{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma\text{-module }{\mathcal{A}}\}
is the cohomological dimension of
\Gamma
. For any finite group
\Gamma
, while
\cat(\Gamma)=\cd(\Gamma)
is infinite, it follows from
[
KW
, Theorem 7.2]
that
\dcat(\Gamma)\leq|\Gamma|-1
(see also
[
J1
, Section 8.A]
). In fact, for any prime
p\geq 2
,
\dcat({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})=p-1
due to
[
Dr2
, Theorem 5.12]
(see also
[
DJ
]
for the case
p=2
). Therefore, an analog of the Eilenberg–Ganea theorem does not hold in the theory of distributional category for groups having torsion. However, such an analog holds for torsion-free discrete groups.

2.3 Theorem

(
[

, Theorem 7.4]
,
[

, Theorem 5.3]

If

\dcat(\Gamma)=\textup{acat}(\Gamma)=\cat(\Gamma)=\cd(\Gamma)
.

Here,

denotes the “analog category”, which is a new homotopy invariant of topological spaces introduced recently by Knudsen and Weinberger in
[

[

[

Recall the evaluation fibration
p_{Z}:P(Z)\to Z
defined as
p_{Z}(\phi)=\phi(0)
for all

. For each

, the result of the fiberwise application of the functor

P(Z)_{n}=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\mathcal{B}_{n}(p_{Z}^{-1}(z))=\left\{\mu\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(P(Z))\hskip 2.84526pt\middle|\hskip 2.84526pt\supp(\mu)\subset p_{Z}^{-1}(z),\hskip 2.84526ptz\in Z\right\}

and a continuous map

such that
\mathcal{B}_{n}(p_{Z})(\mu)=z
whenever

. We refer to

2.4 Proposition

The map

We note that the distributional category of a space is a special case of the general notion of the “distributional sectional category” of a fibration, denoted

Given a map
f:(X,x_{0})\to(Y,y_{0})

2.5 Proposition
.

(1)

(2)

\dcat(f)\leq\min\{\cat(f),\dcat(X),\dcat(Y)\}

(3)

For any map

(4)

The following characterization was stated in

2.6 Proposition

Given a pointed map

\dcat(f)<n

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{Y}):P(Y)_{n}\to Y

Proof.

If
\dcat(f)<n

H(x)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(P(f(x),y_{0}))
for each
x\in X
. In particular, the image of
H
is in
P(Y)_{n}
. By definition of
H
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{Y})(H(x))=f(x)
for all
x
. Hence,
H
is the required lift. Conversely, if
g:X\to P(Y)_{n}
is a lift of
f
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{Y})
, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{Y})(g(x))=f(x)
and thus, we must have
g(x)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(P(f(x),y_{0}))
for all
x\in X
. So, by definition,
\dcat(f)\leq n-1
.
∎

The following covering map inequality for
\dcat
(which is a direct analog of the corresponding inequality for
\cat
, see
[
CLOT
, Corollary 1.45]
) will be very useful in Sections
5.1
and
8.1
.

2.7 Theorem

(
[
DJ
, Theorem 3.7]
)
.

If
p:X\to Y
is a covering map, then
\dcat(X)\leq\dcat(Y)
.

2.3.
Topological basics

Given a discrete group
\Gamma
, its
Berstein–Schwarz class
, denoted
\beta_{\Gamma}
, is the first obstruction to a lift of the classifying space
B\Gamma
to its universal cover
E\Gamma
. If
I(\Gamma)
denotes the augmentation ideal of the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
, then
\beta_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(B\Gamma;I(\Gamma))
. We refer the reader to
[
DKR
, Section 2]
for a nice review of cohomology with local coefficients (see also
[
Ha
, Section 3.H]
).

The Berstein–Schwarz class is “universal” in some sense, see
[
Sch
]
and
[
DR
]
. The following well-known result, called the Berstein–Schwarz theorem, relates the cohomological dimension of a discrete group with its Berstein–Schwarz class.

2.8 Theorem

(
[
Sch
]
,
[
DR
]
)
.

For a discrete group
\Gamma
,
\cd(\Gamma)=\max\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\beta_{\Gamma}^{k}\neq 0\}
.

Given a fibration
f:E\to B
, where
B
is a CW complex and the fiber
F
is
(n-2)
-connected for some
n\geq 3
, the
primary obstruction
to a section of
f
is a cohomology class
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(B;H_{n-1}(F))
, where coefficients are in the
\pi_{1}(B)
-module
H_{n-1}(F)
.

For a discrete group
\Gamma
, it follows from
[
Sch
]
that the
n
-th power of its Berstein–Schwarz class,
\beta_{\Gamma}^{n}
, is the primary obstruction to a section of the
n
-th Ganea fibration
p_{\Gamma}:G_{n}(B\Gamma)\to B\Gamma
defined in Section
2.1
.

In our setting, for the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{X}):P(X)_{n}\to X
, the fiber is
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\Omega X)
for the loop space
\Omega X
and it is
(n-2)
-connected due to
[
Dr2
, Theorem 3.1]
whenever
X
is a CW complex. Hence, the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{X})
admits a section on the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
X
. So, the primary obstruction to a section of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{X})
, say
\kappa_{n}
, is a cohomology class of dimension
n
, i.e.,
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(X;{\mathcal{F}})
for a
\pi_{1}(X)
-module
{\mathcal{F}}
. If
\dim(X)=n
, then
\kappa_{n}
is the only obstruction to a section of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{X})
. Thus, in that case,
\kappa_{n}=0
if and only if
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{X})
admits a section.

Moving forward, we reserve the notation
\kappa_{n}
for such primary obstructions.

We end this section by recalling Poincaré duality. Given a closed manifold
M
of dimension
n
, the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
on
M
defines a fundamental class
[M]
of
M
such that for any local coefficients
{\mathcal{A}}
on
M
and integer
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the
k
-th homomorphism

\text{PD}_{k}:H^{k}(M;{\mathcal{A}})\to H_{n-k}(M;{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{M}),

that sends each
\alpha\in H^{k}(M;{\mathcal{A}})
to the cap product
[M]\frown\alpha\in H_{n-k}(M;{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{M})
, is an isomorphism, see
[
Bre
, Chapter V, Sections 9 and 10]
. If a mapping
f:M\to N
takes the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
, then the
degree
of
f
, denoted
\text{deg}(f)
, is the integer for which
f_{*}([M])=\text{deg}(f)[N]
. If
\text{deg}(f)=\pm 1
, then
f
induces an epimorphism
f_{\bullet}:\pi_{1}(M)\to\pi_{1}(N)
of the fundamental groups.

3.
For essential manifolds

Let
M
be a closed
n
-manifold and
\pi:=\pi_{1}(M)
. We follow Gromov
[
Gr
]
and say that
M
is
essential
if there exists a classifying map
f:M\to B\pi
that induces an isomorphism of the fundamental groups such that
f
cannot be deformed into the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
B\pi
(see also
[
BD
, Definition 3.1]
). Some obvious examples of essential manifolds include aspherical manifolds. Non-aspherical examples include
{\mathbb{R}}P^{n}
for each
n\geq 2
.

There are several equivalent definitions of essential manifolds.

3.1 Theorem

(
[
KR
]
)
.

For a closed
n
-manifold
M
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

M
is essential.

(2)

\cat(M)=\dim(M)=n
.

(3)

There exists a coefficient system
{\mathcal{A}}
and a map
f:M\to B\pi
such that the induced homomorphism
f^{*}:H^{n}(B\pi;{\mathcal{A}})\to H^{n}(M;f^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))
is non-trivial.

If
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{Q}}
in part (
3
) above, then
M
is called
rationally essential
.

Of course, if
\dcat(M)=\dim(M)=n
, then
M
is essential in view of the implication (
2
)
\implies
(
1
) above. However, we note that an analog of Theorem
3.1
does not hold for
\dcat
: even though
{\mathbb{R}}P^{n}
is essential for each
n
, we have
\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{n})=1<n
for all
n\geq 2
.

So, it is natural to look for essential manifolds whose distributional category is equal to the dimension of the manifold.

3.1.
Torsion-free fundamental groups

We now restrict our attention to closed essential manifolds having torsion-free fundamental groups. Let
M
be an essential
n
-manifold such that
\pi:=\pi_{1}(M)
is torsion-free, and
f:M\to B\pi
be a classifying map as above that cannot be deformed into the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
B\pi
. Since
M
is essential,
(f^{*}(\beta_{\pi}))^{n}\neq 0
for the Berstein–Schwarz class
\beta_{\pi}\in H^{1}(B\pi;I(\pi))
, see
[
CLOT
, Theorem 2.51]
and also
[
KR
, Theorem 4.1]
. This means
\beta_{\pi}^{n}\neq 0
and thus, by Theorem
2.8
,
\cd(\pi)\geq n
. Due to Theorem
2.3
, we get
\dcat(B\pi)\geq n
. Hence, by Proposition
2.4
, the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi}):P(B\pi)_{n}\to B\pi
does not admit a section. Therefore, if
\dim(B\pi)=n
, then the primary obstruction
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(B\pi;{\mathcal{F}})
to a section of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi})
is non-zero.

3.2 Definition
.

We say that
M
satisfies the
cap property
if
f_{*}([M])\frown\kappa_{n}\neq 0
.

3.3 Remark
.

Let us formalize the cap property. The orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
on
M
is uniquely determined by the kernel of the homomorphism
w:\pi\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}=\text{Aut}({\mathbb{Z}})
, where
w
is the first Stiefel–Whitney class of
M
, see
[
Ba
, Page 32]
. Hence, for
M
to have (co)homology with coefficients in
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
is the same as having (co)homology with coefficients in the twisted integers
{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}
. Since
[M]\in H_{n}(M;{\mathcal{O}}_{M})=H_{n}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
and
f:M\to B\pi
induces an isomorphism of the fundamental groups, we have that
f_{*}([M])\in H_{n}(B\pi;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
. Therefore,
f_{*}([M])\frown\kappa_{n}\in H_{0}(B\pi;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}\pi
-module.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Let
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(B\pi;{\mathcal{F}})
be the primary obstruction to a section of the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi}):P(B\pi)_{n}\to B\pi
and let
f:M\to B\pi
be a classifying map that cannot be deformed into the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
B\pi
. Then the image
f^{*}(\kappa_{n})\in H^{n}(M;f^{*}({\mathcal{F}}))
is the primary obstruction to a lift of
f
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi})
. Since
\dim(M)=n
, the only obstruction to such a lift of
f
is
f^{*}(\kappa_{n})
. Because
f
induces an isomorphism (and hence an epimorphism) of fundamental groups, the induced map

f_{*}:H_{0}(M;f^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))\to H_{0}(B\pi;{\mathcal{A}})

is an isomorphism for each coefficient system
{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore, due to the cap property,

f_{*}\left([M]\frown f^{*}(\kappa_{n})\right)=f_{*}([M])\frown\kappa_{n}\neq 0

in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{w}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
coefficients (see Remark
3.3
). Thus,
f^{*}(\kappa_{n})\neq 0
. So,
f
does not admit a lift with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi})
. Hence, by Proposition
2.6
, we get
\dcat(f)\geq n
. This gives

n\leq\dcat(f)\leq\dcat(M)\leq\cat(M)=\dim(M)=n

due to Proposition
2.5
(
2
) and Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.4 Corollary
.

Let
M
be a closed essential
n
-manifold with
\pi:=\pi_{1}(M)
. If
B\pi
is a closed
n
-manifold, then
\dcat(M)=\cat(M)=n
.

Proof.

Since
M
is essential, we have
f_{*}([M])\neq 0\in H_{n}(B\pi;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
for a classifying map
f:M\to B\pi
as above. This follows from
[
Ba
, Theorem 8.2]
. For the case when
M
is orientable, this is proved in
[
BD
, Proposition 3.2]
as well. We note that the proof of
[
BD
, Proposition 3.2]
also works if
M
is non-orientable since
f
takes the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\pi}
on the closed
n
-manifold
B\pi
to the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
on
M
. This happens because
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{\pi}
are obtained as pullbacks of the canonical
{\mathbb{Z}}
-bundle
{\mathcal{O}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}P^{\infty}
along the maps
w_{M}:M\to{\mathbb{R}}P^{\infty}
and
w_{\pi}:B\pi\to{\mathbb{R}}P^{\infty}
, respectively, that represent the respective first Stiefel–Whitney classes (see
[
Dr1
, Section 2.4]
), and
w_{\pi}\circ f=w_{M}
. In particular,
\text{deg}(f)\neq 0
. Then, since the primary obstruction
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(B\pi;{\mathcal{F}})
to a section of the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{\pi}):P(B\pi)_{n}\to B\pi
is also non-zero, we get that

f_{*}([M])\frown\kappa_{n}=\text{deg}(f)[B\pi]\frown\kappa_{n}=\text{deg}(f)\left([B\pi]\frown\kappa_{n}\right)\neq 0

due to Poincaré duality in
B\pi
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{\pi}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
coefficients. Thus,
M
satisfies the cap property. Hence, the conclusion follows from Theorem
A
.
∎

This recovers the following statement, which is a consequence of Theorem
2.3
.

3.5 Corollary
.

If
M
is a closed aspherical
n
-manifold, then
\dcat(M)=n
.

3.2.
Symplectically aspherical manifolds

To emphasize the utility of Corollary
3.4
, we will present in this section various examples of non-aspherical essential manifolds whose distributional category turns out to be maximum.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
such that
[\omega]^{n}\neq 0
, where
[\omega]\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{R}})
is the de Rham cohomology class corresponding to the non-degenerate symplectic
2
-form
\omega
. If
(M,\omega)
is simply connected, then clearly,
n\leq c\ell_{{\mathbb{R}}}(M)\leq\dcat(M)\leq\cat(M)\leq n
implies
\dcat(M)=n
. So, we assume that
M
is not simply connected. Let
c_{1}\in H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
denote the first Chern class of
M
and let
h:\pi_{2}(M)\to H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
denote the Hurewicz homomorphism. We follow
[
Ori
]
and call
(M,\omega)

spherically monotone
if there exists some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that the composition

([\omega]-\lambda c_{1})\circ h:\pi_{2}(M)\to H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}

is trivial. When
\lambda=0
, then
(M,\omega)
is called
symplectically aspherical
.
Examples of these kinds include symplectic manifolds whose second homotopy groups vanish, such as aspherical manifolds.

3.6 Corollary
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a spherically monotone
2n
-manifold with some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
([\omega]-\lambda c_{1})^{n}\neq 0
. If
B\pi_{1}(M)
is a closed
2n
-manifold, then
\dcat(M)=2n
.

Proof.

When
\lambda=0
, then
M
is essential due to
[
RO
, Corollary 4.2]
. In general,
M
is essential by
[
Ori
, Theorem 4.6]
if
([\omega]-\lambda c_{1})^{n}\neq 0
. So, Corollary
3.4
applies.
∎

3.7 Remark
.

We note that the LS-category of these symplectic manifolds was calculated in
[
RO
]
and
[
Ori
]
using the notion of category weight (see
[
St
]
,
[
R1
]
). For
\dcat
, the author has not been able to develop a “useful” notion of “distributional category weight” that can determine
\dcat(M)
. However, our current technique does give many new computations for
\dcat
of symplectically aspherical manifolds.

We now focus on closed symplectically aspherical (SA)
2n
-manifolds
(M,\omega)
for which
B\pi_{1}(M)
are closed
2n
-manifolds.

3.8 Example

(Aspherical)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
denote the class of closed aspherical manifolds that are symplectic. Interesting examples in this class include the following. For each
n\geq 1
, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{g_{i}}
, where
\Sigma_{g_{i}}
is the closed orientable surface of genus
g_{i}\geq 1
. Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
is an aspherical symplectic
2n
-manifold. Thus,
\dcat({\mathcal{P}}_{n})=2n
.

Let
H_{3}({\mathbb{R}})
and
H_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
denote, respectively, the real and the discrete Heisenberg groups. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{3}=H_{3}({\mathbb{R}})/H_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
is the Heisenberg
3
-manifold whose fundamental group is
H_{3}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Let
\mathcal{KT}={\mathcal{H}}_{3}\times S^{1}
denote the Kodaira–Thurston manifold. It is an aspherical symplectic
4
-manifold
[
RT
]
. Thus,
\dcat(\mathcal{KT})=4
.

Let
G
be a simply connected completely solvable Lie group of dimension
2n
and
\Gamma
be a co-compact lattice in
G
. Then the quotient
G/\Gamma
is a closed aspherical
2n
-manifold. If there exists
\alpha\in H^{2}(G/\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\alpha^{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}(G/\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
, then
G/\Gamma
has a symplectic structure by
[
IKRT
, Lemma 2.2]
and thus, it is SA. Hence,
\dcat(G/\Gamma)=2n
is obtained.

However, to get new computations of
\dcat
using Corollary
3.6
, we are interested in non-aspherical SA manifolds (while Gompf
[
Go
]
provides such examples in dimension
4
, we don’t know if they satisfy the cap property and so, their
\dcat
cannot be determined by our methods). Indeed, many such manifolds exist in all even dimensions
\geq 4
and we obtain new computations of
\dcat
in all those cases.

3.9 Example

(In the fourth dimension)
.

Due to
[
KRT
, Corollary 5.4]
, there exists an SA
4
-manifold
{\mathcal{M}}^{4}
such that
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{M}}^{4})={\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
and
\pi_{2}({\mathcal{M}}^{4})\neq 0
. Thus, we get
\dcat({\mathcal{M}}^{4})=4
from Corollary
3.6
. Let the class of such
4
-manifolds be denoted by
\mathscr{B}
. In Example
3.11
, we shall give such examples in all even dimensions.

3.10 Example

(Branched covers)
.

For each fixed
n\geq 2
, let
Y^{n}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}/\Lambda
be an abelian variety of complex dimension
n
for
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{2n}
(
Y^{n}
is a projective
2n
-torus). In particular,
Y^{n}
is SA and
\pi_{1}(Y^{n})={\mathbb{Z}}^{2n}
. In
[
Wa
, Section 3]
and
[
DCP
, Section 2]
, using an ample line bundle
L
of
Y^{n}
and choosing a smooth divisor
B^{n-1}
in the linear system
|2L|
, a degree two branched cover
f:X^{n}\to Y^{n}
is defined with
B^{n-1}
as the branch locus. These branched covers are interesting objects in algebraic geometry, see
[
Wa
]
,
[
DCP
]
. In particular,
X^{n}
is not aspherical due to
[
DCP
, Theorem 2.2]
. Here,
B^{n-1}
can be chosen to be a (real) codimension
2
symplectic submanifold of
Y^{n}
. So,
X^{n}
is an SA
2n
-manifold by
[
Go
, Lemma 1 and Theorem 3]
. Finally, we note that
\pi_{1}(X^{n})=\pi_{1}(Y^{n})={\mathbb{Z}}^{2n}
due to
[
PT
, Corollary 3.4]
(see also the proof of
[
DCP
, Theorem 2.2]
). Hence, Corollary
3.6
gives
\dcat(X^{n})=2n
. Let the class of such non-aspherical SA branched covers be denoted by
\mathscr{C}
.

3.11 Example

(Products)
.

For each
n\geq 1
, we compute
\dcat
of SA
(2n+4)
-manifolds
M
for which
\pi_{2}(M)\neq 0
. Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{n},G/\Gamma\in\mathscr{A}
and
\mathcal{M}^{4}\in\mathscr{B}
.

(1)

If
M={\mathcal{P}}_{n}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{4}
, then
B\pi_{1}(M)={\mathcal{P}}_{n}\times T^{4}
. Hence,
\dcat(M)=2n+4
.

(2)

If
M=G/\Gamma\times{\mathcal{M}}^{4}
, then
B\pi_{1}(M)=G/\Gamma\times T^{4}
. Hence,
\dcat(M)=2n+4
.

Here,
T^{4}
denotes the
4
-torus. In general, finite products of manifolds in classes
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{C}
, and their products with manifolds in class

, will give non-aspherical SA manifolds whose

.

3.12 Example

(From symplectic Lefschetz fibrations)
.

For
g\geq 1
, let
\Gamma_{g}:=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
. Note that
\Gamma_{g}
is finitely presented and the identity map
i_{g}:\Gamma_{g}\to\Gamma_{g}
is a finite presentation. Clearly, the induced map
i_{g}^{*}:H^{2}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(\Gamma_{g};{\mathbb{R}})
is non-zero. So, by
[
KRT
, Proposition 3.1]
, there exists a symplectic Lefschetz fibration

\Sigma_{h}\hookrightarrow X\xrightarrow{f}S^{2}

for some
h\geq g
with
\pi_{1}(X)=\Gamma_{g}
and
\Omega\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
such that
\langle\Omega,\phi_{*}[S^{2}]\rangle=0
and
\psi_{y}^{*}(\Omega)\neq 0
for all smooth maps
\phi:S^{2}\to X
and
\psi_{y}:f^{-1}(y)\hookrightarrow X
for
y\in S^{2}
. For any
k\geq 1
, let
Y_{k}=\Sigma_{h}\times\Sigma_{k}
. Then the Gompf symplectic fiber sum
X\#_{\Sigma_{h}}Y_{k}
(see
[
IKRT
, Section 3]
for its definition and also Section

for a general description of fiber sums) is SA by
[

, Proposition 3.3]
. Due to
[

,

\pi_{1}\left(X\#_{\Sigma_{h}}Y_{k}\right)=\Gamma_{g}\oplus\Gamma_{k}.

Hence, we apply Corollary
3.6
to get
\dcat(X\#_{\Sigma_{h}}Y_{k})=4
. We note that in general, the fiber sums

need not be aspherical.

We now explain how for any

, any direct sum of

-number of surface groups (i.e., fundamental groups of closed orientable surfaces
\Sigma_{g}
of genus

) can be realized as the fundamental group of a closed symplectically aspherical

-manifold

that is not aspherical but whose

.

3.13 Remark
.

For any
n\geq 2
, let
\Gamma^{n}:=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma_{g_{i}}
, where
\Gamma_{g_{i}}=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g_{i}})
for

. We saw in Examples

-manifold

such that

and
\dcat(M)=2n
. Now, let us assume that at most one summand in

. Then there exists

. In Example

, we take

and

.

4.
For connected sums

Let
M
and

-manifolds and

\cat(M\#N)=\max\{\cat(M),\cat(N)\}

from
[

]

, at least when both

and

have torsion — see
[
DJ

for a non-orientable example when

and Remark

for an orientable example when

. It is unclear if such a general connected sum formula holds for
\dcat
if

is torsion-free. In this section, we provide such a partial formula when

is essential and satisfies the cap property defined in Section
3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}
be the orientation sheaves on the closed
n
-manifolds
M
and
N
, respectively. Let
B
denote a closed
n
-disc common to
M
and
N
, whose interior is deleted from
M
and
N
to identify the resulting boundary spheres and form the connected sum
M\#N
. If
{\mathcal{O}}_{B}
is the orientation sheaf on
B
, then we can identify
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}
along
{\mathcal{O}}_{B}
to get the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
on
M\cup_{B}N
. Here,
M\cup_{B}N
denotes the union of
M
and
N
along the closed
n
-disc
B
. Consider the quotient map
\psi:M\cup_{B}N\to(M\cup_{B}N)/B=M\vee N
. Since
B
is contractible,
\psi
admits a homotopy inverse
\iota:M\vee N\to M\cup_{B}N
. Then the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
on
M\vee N
is obtained by pulling back
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
along
\iota
. If
q:M\#N\to M\cup_{B}N
denotes the inclusion, then the restriction of
{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
along

gives the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\#}
on
M\#N
. So, in particular, the map
\psi\circ q
takes

to

.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

We follow the above notations. Let
d:M\vee N\to N
and
\phi:M\#N\to N
be the maps collapsing
M
to a point, and let
g:N\to B\pi_{1}(N)
be a classifying map that cannot be deformed into the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
B\pi_{1}(N)
. Let us consider the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\#N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\cup_{B}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\pi_{1}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\vee N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}

By definition,
\phi_{*}([M\#N])=[N]
and
q_{*}([M\#N])=[M]\oplus[N]
. Thus,
q
induces an epimorphism of the fundamental groups. So, in view of the homotopy equivalence
\psi
, the induced map

(\psi\circ q)_{*}:H_{0}(M\#N;(\psi\circ q)^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))\to H_{0}(M\vee N;{\mathcal{A}})

is an isomorphism for each coefficient system
{\mathcal{A}}
. Note that
(\psi\circ q)^{*}({\mathcal{O}})={\mathcal{O}}_{\#}
. Let
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(B\pi_{1}(N);{\mathcal{F}})
be the primary obstruction to a section of the fibration

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p):P(B\pi_{1}(N))\to B\pi_{1}(N)
, see Paragraph 1 of Section
3.1
for details. Then

(g\circ\phi)^{*}(\kappa_{n})\in H^{n}(M\#N;(g\circ\phi)^{*}({\mathcal{F}}))

is the primary obstruction to the lift of
g\circ\phi
with respect to the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p)
. Since
\dim(M\#N)=n
, it is the only obstruction to such a lift of
g\circ\phi
. Clearly, we have
d^{*}(g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))=\alpha\oplus g^{*}(\kappa_{n})
for some cohomology class
\alpha
. Since
\phi=d\circ\psi\circ q
and
(\psi\circ q)_{*}([M\#N])=[M]\oplus[N]
, we get the following set of equalities in
{\mathcal{O}}\otimes\hskip 1.42262pt(g\circ d)^{*}({\mathcal{F}})
coefficients:

(\psi\circ q)_{*}\left([M\#N]\frown\phi^{*}(g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))\right)=(\psi\circ q)_{*}\left([M\#N]\frown(\psi\circ q)^{*}\left(d^{*}(g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))\right)\right)\\
=(\psi\circ q)_{*}([M\#N])\frown d^{*}(g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))=([M]\oplus[N])\frown(\alpha\oplus g^{*}(\kappa_{n})).

Let

be the first Stiefel–Whitney class and

be the twisted integers so that
[N]\in H_{n}(N;{\mathcal{O}}_{N})=H_{n}(N;{\mathbb{Z}}_{w})
, see Remark
3.3
. Then, by the cap property of

, we have in

g_{*}([N]\frown g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))=g_{*}([N])\frown\kappa_{n}\neq 0.

In particular,
[N]\frown g^{*}(\kappa_{n})\neq 0
. Therefore,
([M]\oplus[N])\frown(\alpha\oplus g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))\neq 0
and thus, the first equality above implies that
\phi^{*}(g^{*}(\kappa_{n}))=(g\circ\phi)^{*}(\kappa_{n})\neq 0
. Hence,
g\circ\phi
does not admit a lift with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p)
. By Propositions

(
2
) and
2.6
, we get that

n\leq\dcat(g\circ\phi)\leq\dcat(M\#N)\leq\cat(M\#N)=n.

Thus,
\dcat(M\#N)=n
.
∎

It follows from Theorem

and the proof of Corollary

that if

If

Let

4.4 Remark
.

For each
n\geq 1
, let
T^{n}
denote the
n
-torus. Due to Corollary
4.2
, we have
\dcat(T^{n}\#T^{n})=n
.
However,

is not a closed

is not true.

For non-aspherical spaces, their rational (or real) cup-lengths were typically used to determine their
\dcat
values in
[

, Section 6.1]
. Given a connected sum, the cup-lengths of its summands can be used to estimate its

\max\{c\ell_{\mathbb{F}}(M),c\ell_{\mathbb{F}}(N)\}\leq\dcat(M\#N)\leq\cat(M\#N),

[

For example,

But from this technique alone, we cannot prove that

or

-manifold

gives new computations

in the case when

(From non-orientable surfaces)
.

5.
For low-dimensional manifolds

In this section, we focus on closed manifolds of dimensions
n\leq 4
. In dimensions
n\leq 2
, the
\dcat
values are completely known,
[
DJ
, Section 6.1]
(see also Section
1
). So, the first interesting problem in determining
\dcat
of low-dimensional manifolds comes in dimension

.

5.1.
3
-manifolds

The classical LS-category of closed
3
-manifolds was computed first in
[
GLGA
]
and then in
[
OR
]
using category weight. The complete description based on the fundamental groups is as follows. Let
M
be a closed
3
-manifold. Then,

\cat(M)=\begin{cases}1&\textup{if }\pi_{1}(M)=0\\
2&\textup{if }\pi_{1}(M)\neq 0\text{ is free}\\
3&\textup{if }\pi_{1}(M)\text{ is not free}\end{cases}

Using some ideas from
[
OR
]
and Theorem
A
, we can compute the distributional category of any closed
3
-manifold
M
such that
\pi_{1}(M)
is torsion-free.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

First, let
M
be simply connected. In this case, we have that
\dcat(M)\leq\cat(M)=1\implies\dcat(M)=1
.

Now, let us assume that
\pi_{1}(M)\neq 0
is free. Let
M=M_{1}\#\cdots\#M_{k}
be the unique (up to permutation) decomposition of
M
into prime
3
-manifolds
M_{i}
, see
[
He
, Theorems 3.15 and 3.21]
. Since
\pi_{1}(M)\neq 0
is free,
\pi_{1}(M_{i})
is free for each
1\leq i\leq k
and there exists
j
such that
\pi_{1}(M_{j})\neq 0
. Due to
[
OR
, Corollary 4.6]
,
M_{j}
cannot be irreducible. Therefore,
M_{j}
must be an
S^{2}
-bundle over
S^{1}
by
[
He
, Lemma 3.13]
. Let us write
M=N\#M_{j}
, where
N
is the connected sum of the other prime components (if
k=1
, we take
N=S^{3}
). If
M_{j}
is orientable, then
M_{j}=S^{2}\times S^{1}
. Therefore,
2=c\ell_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(S^{2}\times S^{1})\leq\dcat(N\#M_{j})
. If
M_{j}
is non-orientable, then
S^{2}\times S^{1}
is a double cover of
M_{j}
. Hence,
N\#N\#(S^{2}\times S^{1})
is a cover of
N\#M_{j}
. Using the rational cup-length of
S^{2}\times S^{1}
in the same way as before and Theorem
2.7
, we get

2=c\ell_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(S^{2}\times S^{1})\leq\dcat(N\#N\#(S^{2}\times S^{1}))\leq\dcat(N\#M_{j}).

In any case, we have
2\leq\dcat(M)\leq\cat(M)=2
. Thus,
\dcat(M)=2
.

Finally, let
\pi_{1}(M)
be not free but torsion-free. Let
M=M_{1}\#\cdots\#M_{k}
be the (essentially unique) decomposition of
M
into prime
3
-manifolds
M_{i}
. Since
\cat(M)=3
, due to the connected sum formula
[
DS
]
, there exists
j
such that
\cat(M_{j})=3
. Since the LS-category of
S^{2}
-bundles over
S^{1}
is
2
,
M_{j}
must be irreducible by
[
He
, Lemma 3.13]
. Since
\pi_{1}(M)
is torsion-free, so is
\pi_{1}(M_{j})
. In particular,
\pi_{1}(M_{j})
is infinite. Thus,
M_{j}
is not a finite quotient of
S^{3}
. This and the irreducibility of
M_{j}
implies that
M_{j}
is aspherical.
Hence, we have
M=N\#M_{j}
, where
N
is the sum of the other summands and
M_{j}
is aspherical. So, we apply Corollary
4.2
to get
\dcat(M)=3
.
∎

5.1 Remark
.

In Theorem
C
, we cannot drop the torsion-free hypothesis on
\pi_{1}(M)
when
\pi_{1}(M)
is not free:
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{R}}P^{3})={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is not free but
\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{3})=1

The only remaining case is when

has torsion (this implies that

is not free). We say that

if

has torsion. To explore the possibility of determining
\dcat
of
3
-manifolds in the class

, we consider the following examples.

5.2 Example
.

Clearly,

. We can view
{\mathbb{R}}P^{3}\#{\mathbb{R}}P^{3}
as the quotient space

and

for all

is a double cover of

, we get that

.

5.3 Remark
.

Due to Example

,
\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{3})=1<2\leq\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{3}\#{\mathbb{R}}P^{3})
. Hence, the connected sum formula from
[

, Theorem 1]
does not hold for

even when all the summands are orientable manifolds.

5.4 Example
.

If

and

are two non-trivial

-dimensional lens spaces, then

. Depending on the lens spaces chosen, there exists some

such that

is a cover of

. Therefore,

.

5.5 Example
.

Let
M
be a
3
-manifold admitting the spherical geometry and let

be aspherical. Since
\pi_{1}(M)
is finite,

. However, Corollary

gives

. So, the converse of Theorem

is not true.

Because of

and the above examples, we see that manifolds in class

cannot have a constant

, then while

, one cannot estimate

in general. So, Theorem

is optimal in some sense.

Due to Thurston,
\mathbb{E}^{3}
,
{\mathbb{N}}il^{3}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{E}
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{SL}}
,
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}ol^{3}
,

, and

are the eight maximal

has one of the first six geometries, then

is aspherical, and thus,

. By Corollary

, if

appears in the prime decomposition of a closed

-manifold

, then
\dcat(K)=3
. If

has the spherical geometry

, then

and we do not know

in general. However, if

has

because there are only the following four possibilities for

•

. Then

by
[

, Proposition 6.7]
.

•

is the non-orientable

. Then

by the proof of Theorem

.

•

by Example

.

•

is a cover of

directly gives

.

So, if such a manifold

appears in the prime decomposition of a

, then

by the cup-length technique used in the proof of Theorem

.

5.6 Remark
.

Determining the exact value
\dcat({\mathbb{R}}P^{2}\times S^{1})
will be very interesting. If

, then since

) and disprove an analog of the famous Ganea conjecture (see

) for

.

5.2.

-manifolds with free fundamental group

Let

be a closed

-manifold such that

is free. First, let

. Then

by

5.7 Proposition
.

If

is a closed orientable
4
-manifold such that
\pi_{1}(M)\neq 0

\dcat(M)=\cat(M)=2

Proof.

As observed in
[

, Page 2]
and
[

, if

is free, then
\cat(M)\leq 2
. Since

is free of rank

is a free abelian group of rank
k\geq 1
. Because

is orientable,
H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})\neq 0

H^{3}(M;{\mathbb{Q}})\neq 0
and the cup product pairing

We note that

We will see more computations for

6.

6.1.
Sums along submanifolds

Let
M
and

be two closed smooth orientable
n
-manifolds for

be a closed orientable manifold of dimension at most
n

i:S\to M
and

with respective normal bundles
\mu
and
\nu
and an orientation-reversing bundle isomorphism

. Such a map

exists, in particular, when
\dim(S)=n-2
and
e(\mu)=-e(\nu)
for the Euler characteristics. Using smoothness, we can choose tubular neighborhoods

and
V\subset N
of
i(S)
and

and

are identified with the total spaces of
\mu
and
\nu

[

(M-i(S))\sqcup(N-j(S))

and identify

with

using

to obtain a closed orientable
n

of

along

Since

is a compact pseudomanifold (with a CW structure) of dimension

in the sense of
[

, Section 2.4]

{\mathcal{O}}_{N/L}
on

gives a fundamental class
[N/L]
that defines the Poincaré duality homomorphism
\text{PD}_{n}
from Section
2.3
in dimension

is an isomorphism for each coefficient system, see
[

Proof of Theorem
D
.

First, we note that since

are closed and orientable of dimension
n
,

has non-trivial cohomology in degree

. Since

is aspherical as well, we have
\cd(\pi_{1}(N/L))\geq n
. Hence,
\dcat(N/L)\geq n
by Theorem
2.3
. Due to Proposition
2.4
, the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p):P(N/L)_{n}\to N/L
does not admit a section. Hence, the primary obstruction
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(N/L;{\mathcal{F}})
to a section of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p)
is non-zero. Let us define the collapsing map

\phi:M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN\to(M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN)/M=N/L.

By definition,
\phi^{*}(\kappa_{n})\in H^{n}(M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN,\phi^{*}({\mathcal{F}}))
is the primary obstruction to a lift of
\phi
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p)
. Because
\dim(M\#_{L}\hskip 1.13809ptN)=n
, it is the only obstruction to such a lift of
\phi
. Due to orientability,
\phi
sends
{\mathcal{O}}_{N/L}
to the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
on
M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN
. Furthermore,
\text{deg}(\phi)=1
. Hence, in
{\mathcal{O}}_{N/L}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
coefficients, we get

\phi_{*}\left([M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN]\frown\phi^{*}(\kappa_{n})\right)=\phi_{*}([M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN])\frown\kappa_{n}=[N/L]\frown\kappa_{n}\neq 0

due to Poincaré duality in

and so, a lift of
\phi
does not exist with respect to

. Hence, using Propositions

(
2
) and
2.6
, we get the inequality

n\leq\dcat(\phi)\leq\dcat(M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN)\leq\cat(M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN)\leq\dim(M\#_{L}\hskip 0.56905ptN)=n.

∎

Such orientable aspherical pseudomanifolds can be obtained, for example, using the (relative) strict hyperbolization technique, see

]
,
[

]
,
[

. Let

be a compact orientable
n

6.2.
Doubles of punctured manifolds

We now consider a particular case of the generalized connected sum discussed above for compact manifolds. Let
M
be a compact oriented
n
-manifold with boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
. We take a copy
\overline{M}
of

with reverse orientation and glue

and
\overline{M}
along
\partial M
to form

\mathcal{D}M=M\#_{\partial M}\hskip 0.56905pt\overline{M}.

This is a closed oriented
n
-manifold which we call the

Recall that any

, a
k
-puncturing
of a closed orientable

-manifold

refers to the process of the removal of

n
-discs (or the interiors of closed
n
-discs) from

Proof of Theorem
E
.

Let
Z=\prod_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
and
m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}
. For each

, we can obtain

back from

by filling each of the
k_{i}
punctures in

by a suitable

-disc. Certainly, the inclusion
\mathcal{I}_{i}:X_{i}\hookrightarrow M_{i}
is an embedding. So, there is a canonical map
\tau^{\prime}:M\to Z
. Let
q_{1}:M\to M/\partial M
and
q_{2}:Z\to Z/\partial Z
be the quotient maps that collapse the respective boundaries. Since
Z
is closed,
q_{2}
is the identity map on
Z
. Thus,
\tau^{\prime}
produces a natural map
\tau:M/\partial M\to Z
. Let

\phi:\mathcal{D}M\to\mathcal{D}M/M=M/\partial M

be the collapsing map with
\text{deg}(\phi)=1
. Then, it follows that
(\tau\circ\phi)_{*}([\mathcal{D}M])=[Z]
. Since
Z
is aspherical of dimension
m
, the primary obstruction
\kappa_{m}\in H^{m}(Z;{\mathcal{F}})
to a section of the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(p_{Z}):P(Z)_{m}\to Z
is non-zero (see Section
3.1
). Clearly,

(\tau\circ\phi)^{*}(\kappa_{m})\in H^{m}(\mathcal{D}M;(\tau\circ\phi)^{*}({\mathcal{F}}))

is the primary obstruction to a lift of the map
\tau\circ\phi
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(p_{Z})
, and since
\dim(\mathcal{D}M)=m
, it is the only obstruction to such a lift of
\tau\circ\phi
. We note that
\tau\circ\phi
induces an isomorphism

(\tau\circ\phi)_{*}:H_{0}(\mathcal{D}M;(\tau\circ\phi)^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))\to H_{0}(Z;{\mathcal{A}})

for each coefficient system
{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
M
,
\mathcal{D}M
, and
Z
are all orientable manifolds,
\tau\circ\phi
sends the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
on
Z
to the orientation sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
on
\mathcal{D}M
. Thus, in
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
coefficients, we have that

(\tau\circ\phi)_{*}\left([\mathcal{D}M]\frown(\tau\circ\phi)^{*}(\kappa_{m})\right)=(\tau\circ\phi)_{*}([\mathcal{D}M])\frown\kappa_{m}=[Z]\frown\kappa_{m}\neq 0

due to Poincaré duality in

. Thus,
(\tau\circ\phi)^{*}(\kappa_{m})\neq 0
. Hence, we conclude that a lift of
\tau\circ\phi
does not exist with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{m}(p_{Z})
. Due to Propositions
2.5
(
2
) and
2.6
, we have that

m\leq\dcat(\tau\circ\phi)\leq\dcat(\mathcal{D}M)\leq m.

Therefore,
\dcat(\mathcal{D}M)=\dim(\mathcal{D}M)=m
.
∎

6.2 Example

If

is a punctured closed nilmanifold of dimension

In the notations of Theorem

6.3.
Geometrically decomposable

We follow Hillman

6.4 Theorem

(
[
Hi
, Theorem 7.2]
)
.

If a closed
4
-manifold

admits a geometric decomposition, then exactly one of the following is true.

(1)

M
is geometric.

(2)

M
is the total space of an
S^{2}
-orbifold bundle over a hyperbolic

-orbifold.

(3)

All components of

have geometry
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

(4)

The components of
M\setminus\cup S
have geometry
\hskip 0.56905pt\mathbb{H}^{4}
,
\mathbb{H}^{3}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{E}^{2}
, or
\hskip 1.42262pt\widetilde{\mathbb{SL}}\times\mathbb{E}^{1}
.

(5)

The components of
M\setminus\cup S
have geometry
\mathbb{H}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
or
\hskip 1.42262pt\mathbb{F}^{4}
.

From here onwards, we use the term
surface
to refer to a closed orientable
2
-manifold of genus
\geq 1
. For the facts used in the following discussion, we refer the reader to
[
Hi
, Sections 7.1 and 7.5]
.

Due to Filipkiewicz, there are nineteen maximal
4
-dimensional geometries, one of which is not realizable by any closed manifold. If a closed manifold
M
belongs to class (
1
) and
has one of the twelve aspherical geometries, then
\dcat(M)=4

3.5
. If
M
belongs to class (

) or (

is aspherical and thus,

\dcat

3

Up to finite covers, the

-manifolds having

i=1,2
, let us fix some integers

7.
For Alexandrov spaces

We now study A. D. Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded below. In this section, we will extend some of our results for closed manifolds from the previous sections to a more general class of closed Alexandrov spaces, thereby initiating a study of the LS-category of Alexandrov spaces.

7.1.
Some definitions

An Alexandrov space is a finite-dimensional, complete, locally compact length space with an inner length metric that locally satisfies a lower curvature bound in the sense of distance comparison. Alexandrov spaces are locally contractible metric ANR,
[
Wu
]
. We refer to
[
BGP
]
,
[

]
,
[
GGNZ
, Section 2]
, and
[

, Section 6]
for their precise definitions. Examples of Alexandrov spaces include orbit spaces of isometric compact Lie group actions on complete Riemannian manifolds whose sectional curvatures are uniformly bounded below. A non-manifold and non-orbifold example is the suspension of
{\mathbb{C}}P^{2}
, denoted
\s({\mathbb{C}}P^{2})
. For more examples and constructions, we refer the reader to
[
GGNZ
, Section 2.3]
.

Let

be an
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space without boundary. At each point

, one can define tangent directions and an angle distance between them, see
[
BGP
]
and
[
GGNZ
]
for details. The metric completion of the tangent directions gives the
space of directions at

p
, denoted
\varSigma_{p}
, which is an Alexandrov
(n-1)
-space. If

, then for each
p\in X
, either
\varSigma_{p}=S^{2}
or
\varSigma_{p}={\mathbb{R}}P^{2}
. If

, then
\varSigma_{p}=\s({\mathbb{R}}P^{2})
or
\varSigma_{p}
is a
3
-manifold having the spherical geometry,
[

, Corollary 2.3]
. The Euclidean cone over
\varSigma_{p}
yields the

p
, denoted

, which is an Alexandrov
n
-space. By Perelman’s conical neighborhood theorem, every
p\in X
has a neighborhood homeomorphic to
K_{p}
. A point
p\in X
is called

if
\varSigma_{p}=S^{n-1}
; otherwise, it is called

. For
n\geq 3
, the codimension of the set of singular points of

is at least

by a theorem of Perelman. So, when
n=3
and
X
is compact, the set of its singular points is finite. If all points of
X
are regular, then

is a manifold, also called an Alexandrov
n
-manifold. The converse is true when
n\leq 4
and not true otherwise
[
GGG
, Section 2.2]
.

When
n\leq 2
, an Alexandrov
n
-space is a manifold, see
[
BBI
, Corollary 10.10.3]
. So, the non-manifold cases arise when

. In these cases, we first want to define a notion of the “connected sum” of two closed Alexandrov
n
-spaces, which is a direct generalization of the notion introduced for
n=3
in
[

, Section 3.1]
.

Let
M
and
N
be two closed Alexandrov
n
-spaces. Let
p\in M
and

such that
\varSigma_{p}=\varSigma_{q}
. Using Perelman’s theorem, we choose neighborhoods

of

and
V\subset N
of

such that
U=K_{p}
and
V=K_{q}
. Since
\partial(M\setminus U)=\varSigma_{p}
and
\partial(N\setminus V)=\varSigma_{q}
, there exists a homeomorphism
\psi:\partial(M\setminus U)\to\partial(N\setminus V)
.

7.1 Definition
.

The
connected sum of
M
and
N
along
(p,q)
, denoted
M\#^{(p,q)}N
, is defined as the quotient space

((M\setminus U)\sqcup(N\setminus V))/\sim,

where
x\sim\psi(x)
for all
x\in\partial(M\setminus U)
.

Indeed,
M\#^{(p,q)}N
is a closed Alexandrov
n
-space with curvature bounded below — see, for example,
[
FGGGH
, Section 3]
and
[
GGNZ
, Page 9]
). Unlike the case of the connected sum of closed manifolds, in general, the sum

need not be homeomorphic to
M\#^{(p_{2},q_{2})}N
, even if

,
p_{1}=q_{1}
,
p_{2}=q_{2}
, and
\varSigma_{p_{1}}=\varSigma_{p_{2}}
, as shown in
[

, Theorem A]
for

. However, when
\varSigma_{p}=\varSigma_{q}=S^{n-1}
and there is no risk of confusion regarding

and

, we will write

to denote the usual connected sum of the Alexandrov spaces
M
and
N
along closed
n
-discs.

7.2.
Extension of some results

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the classical LS-category of closed Alexandrov spaces has not been well studied. In particular, it is not known whether the connected sum formula for closed manifolds
[
DS
, Theorem 1]
holds for the connected sum of closed Alexandrov spaces as well! Using our techniques from Section
4
, we provide in this section a partial such formula for
\dcat
in the special case when one of the Alexandrov spaces is aspherical. This extends the classical connected sum formula for
\cat
to closed non-manifold Alexandrov spaces in some cases.

The unified notion of orientability of Alexandrov spaces without boundary was studied by Mitsuishi in
[
Mit
]
(see also
[
HS
, Section 2]
). Let
R
be any principal ideal domain. If
X
is a closed
R
-orientable Alexandrov
n
-space, then
X
has a
R
-fundamental class
[X]_{R}\in H_{n}(X;R)
by
[
Mit
, Theorem 1.8 and Remark 2.11]
. We note that every closed Alexandrov space is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-orientable and
\s({\mathbb{R}}P^{2})
is not
{\mathbb{Z}}
-orientable, see
[
Mit
, Section 5.2]
.

7.2 Theorem

(
[
Mit
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain and
X
be a closed
R
-orientable Alexandrov
n
-space. Then for any
R
-module
G
, the Poincaré duality homomorphism
\textup{PD}_{n}:H^{n}(X;G)\to H_{0}(X;G)
is an isomorphism.

Here, the homomorphism
\text{PD}_{n}:H^{n}(X;G)\to H_{0}(X;G)
sends each cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{n}(X;G)
to the cap product
[X]_{R}\frown\alpha\in H_{0}(X;G)
.

Unlike the case of closed manifolds, the existence of fundamental class and the satisfaction of Poincaré duality is very restrictive in the case of closed Alexandrov spaces, so we will be careful while extending our results.

Let
M
and
N
be two closed Alexandrov
n
-spaces such that
N
is a manifold. We can choose regular points
p\in M
and
q\in N
(both
M
and

have infinitely many regular points) to form

, which we will write as

from here onwards.
Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}
be the orientation sheaf on

. We follow the conventions from Section
4
. The fundamental class
[M\#N]_{R}
always exists for either
R={\mathbb{Z}}
or
R={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
(a

-fundamental class exists when both

and

are
{\mathbb{Z}}
-orientable) and gets mapped to

under the homomorphism induced in homology by the map

that collapses

to a point. So, Theorem

, which produces some new computations for the classical LS-category (

), can now be proved.

Proof sketch of Theorem
F
.

The proof for
\dcat(M\#N)\geq n
is the same as that of Corollary
4.2
and uses Poincaré duality in the manifold

in

coefficients, where

is a

-module such that
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(N;{\mathcal{F}})
is the non-zero primary obstruction to a section of

due to the asphericity of

. Since any closed Alexandrov
n
-space

is an ANR, in particular, locally contractible

\dcat(M\#N)=\cat(M\#N)=n

∎

7.3 Example

(Flat manifolds)
.

Any flat

for any Alexandrov

In the orientable (or

Proof of Theorem

.

Since

is a closed orientable aspherical Alexandrov
n

and

and
[

, Theorem 1.8]
to get the equality

\dcat(N)=\cd(\pi_{1}(N))=n
. So, in view of Proposition

, the primary obstruction
\kappa_{n}\in H^{n}(N;{\mathcal{F}})
to a section of the fibration
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(p_{N}):P(N)_{n}\to N
is non-zero.
Due to orientability,
[M\#N]
and
[N]
exist such that
\phi_{*}([M\#N])=[N]
for the collapsing map
\phi:M\#N\to N
. Clearly,

induces an isomorphism of the zeroth homology groups in each coefficient system. Therefore, we have

\phi_{*}([M\#N]\frown\phi^{*}(\kappa_{n}))=\phi_{*}([M\#N])\frown\kappa_{n}=[N]\frown\kappa_{n},

where
\phi^{*}(\kappa_{n})\in H^{n}(M\#N;\phi^{*}({\mathcal{F}}))

with respect to

. Since

, it is the only obstruction to such a lift of

. Here,

is a

-module, hence an abelian group. By Poincaré duality in

), we have

. So,

does not admit a lift with respect to

. Thus, we get

in view of Propositions

(
2
) and
2.6
.
∎

.

We note that the orientability assumption is important to prove Theorem

7.5 Example

Following the notations of Example

8.
For non-manifold Alexandrov

We now focus on three-dimensional Alexandrov spaces that have recently been studied extensively in

]
,
[

]
,
[

]

In this section, using some well-known results from Alexandrov geometry (in particular, from the above papers), we shall make some observations regarding the classical and the distributional categories of these spaces.

8.1.
Initial observations

Let
X
be a closed Alexandrov
3
-space. Then we have
\dcat(X)\leq\cat(X)\leq 3
. If

is a simply connected CW complex, then by
[

, Theorem 1.50]

\dcat(X)=\cat(X)=1
. Hence, if
X
is a closed positively-curved Alexandrov
3
-space that is not a manifold, then we have
\dcat(X)=\cat(X)=1
because

due to
[
GGG
, Theorem 1.1]
and
\s({\mathbb{R}}P^{2})
is a simply connected CW complex.

Given a closed Alexandrov
3
-space

, let
M_{X}
be the compact non-orientable
3
-manifold with boundary obtained by removing disjoint regular neighborhoods of the finitely many singular points of
X
. So, the boundary of
M_{X}
is a finite collection of
{\mathbb{R}}P^{2}
. Let

M_{X}=M_{1}\#\cdots\#M_{n}\#l(S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1})

be the unique prime decomposition
[
He
]
of

, where

and
S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1}
denotes the non-orientable
S^{2}
-bundle over
S^{1}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\widehat{M_{i}}
be the Alexandrov
3
-space obtained from

by capping off its
{\mathbb{R}}P^{2}
boundary components. Then,

X=\widehat{M_{1}}\hskip 1.42262pt\#\cdots\#\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{M_{n}}\hskip 1.42262pt\#\hskip 1.42262ptl(S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1})

is the unique (up to permutation)

of

that exists due to
[
FGGGH
, Theorems B and C]
. Here, each
\widehat{M_{i}}
is a connected sum of prime Alexandrov

-spaces. The notions of prime and irreducible Alexandrov

[

, Section 3.2]
.

For some

, if
\widehat{M_{j}}
is orientable and aspherical, or if it is an aspherical manifold, then

in view of Theorems

and

is aspherical with no

8.1 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a closed Alexandrov
3
-space with a CW structure. If
X
has an
S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1}
summand in its normal prime decomposition, then
\dcat(X)>1
.

Proof.

We use the above notations. By our assumption,
l\geq 1
. Thus, we can write
X=Y\#(S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1})
, where

Y=\widehat{M_{1}}\#\cdots\#\widehat{M_{n}}\#(l-1)(S^{2}\hskip-1.42262pt\propto\hskip-1.42262ptS^{1}).

Since

is the connected orientable double cover of

, the space

is a cover of

. Since

is a CW complex, so is its cover

. Thus,
(Z,S^{2})
is a good pair in the sense of
[

]
. So, we can proceed using the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for cohomology of the pair

along the lines of
[

to get

2=c\ell_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(S^{2}\times S^{1})\leq c\ell_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(Z)\leq\dcat(Z).

Finally, we use Theorem

to conclude that

.
∎

8.2 Remark
.

While the classical LS-category of closed
3
-manifolds is completely known
[
GLGA
]
,
[
OR
]
, it is difficult to describe the
\cat
of closed Alexandrov
3
-spaces completely. We list some reasons why the techniques used for determining
\cat
of closed
3
-manifolds do not work for closed Alexandrov
3
-spaces in general.

•

In the case of closed non-manifold Alexandrov
n
-spaces, Poincaré duality in middle degrees may not hold, see
[
Mit
, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 5.2]
. If
M
is a closed
n
-manifold for
n\geq 3
such that
\pi_{1}(M)
is not free, then
\cat(M)\geq 3
due to
[
DKR
, Theorem 4.1]
. Also, if
M
is a closed
3
-manifold such that
\pi_{1}(M)
is infinite and
\pi_{2}(M)=0
, then
M
is aspherical (see
[
OR
, Proposition 2.6]
). Since the proofs of these results use Poincaré duality in middle degrees, they do not work if
M
is a non-manifold Alexandrov space.

•

A suitable analog of the “projective plane theorem” for closed
3
-manifolds (see
[
He
, Theorem 4.12]
) is not known for closed Alexandrov
3
-spaces in general. Thus, the proofs of
[
GLGA
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
OR
, Proposition 4.5]
do not work for the non-manifold cases.

•

In contrast to the
3
-manifold case, there are infinitely many closed non-irreducible prime Alexandrov
3
-spaces due to
[
FGGGH
, Theorem D]
. So, the proofs of
[
GLGA
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
OR
, Proposition 5.1]
do not work for general Alexandrov
3
-spaces
M
to give
\cat(M)\leq 2
when
\pi_{1}(M)
is free.

Due to these reasons, the subsequent results in
[
GLGA
]
and
[
OR
]
are not obtained, and thus, the LS-category of closed non-manifold Alexandrov
3
-spaces cannot be determined completely using just their fundamental groups.

In view of Remark

X=\#_{i=1}^{r}\s({\mathbb{R}}P^{2})\#\mathscr{M}

above.

Proof.

Since
X

c(X)\neq 3

∎

8.8 Remark
.

The statements of Propositions
8.4
and
8.7
hold true if the condition
\cat(X)>1
is replaced by the condition
\dcat(X)>1
since
\cat(X)\geq\dcat(X)

We end this paper by giving two instances where the converse of Proposition
8.7
holds true.

.

A Stefan-Sussmann theorem for normal distributions on manifolds with boundary
David Perrella, David Pfefferlé, Luchezar Stoyanov

Abstract




I
Abstract

An analogue of the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem on manifolds with boundary is proven for normal distributions. These distributions contain vectors transverse to the boundary along its entirety. Plain integral manifolds are not enough to “integrate” a normal distribution; the next best “integrals” are so-called neat integral manifolds with boundary. The conditions on the distribution for this integrability is expressed in terms of adapted collars and integrability of a pulled-back distribution on the interior and on the boundary.

II
Introduction

The foundations of integrable distribution theory on manifolds can be summarised by the following four programs.

1.

Define integrability of a distribution.

2.

Ask what “differential” conditions are equivalent to integrability.

3.

Determine the “regularity” of integral manifolds.

4.

Look at a notion of “maximal” for integral manifolds and show that there is a correspondence with foliations.

In the case of constant rank, the Frobenius Theorem addresses program two and is the main component of the foundations (although, as noted by Lavau
Lavau 2018
, the precise historical list of contributors to the foundations of this theory do include authors before Frobenius). In the case of non-constant rank, it was the independent works of Stefan
Stefan 1974
and Sussmann
Sussmann 1973
which gave rise to the completion of the foundations; culminating in the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem (which again addresses program two). What appears absent from the literature is an analogous theory on manifolds with boundary, where the integral manifolds become, in general, manifolds with boundary.

To address this, the present paper takes on the analogous first and second programs for
normal distributions
on manifolds with boundary. Normal distributions are those which contain vectors transverse to the boundary along its entirety. Our analogue of integrability is what we call
neat integrability
and the differential conditions constitute our main result, namely a Stefan-Sussmann theorem for normal distributions.

In a future paper, we will use the results obtained here to complete the remaining two analogous programs in the foundations. This will provide means of guaranteeing examples of
foliations by manifolds with boundary
on manifolds with boundary. A general notion of these foliations were considered by Noakes
Noakes 1981
where he generalised Bott’s Theorem
Bott 1970
as well as demonstrate how these foliations may be used to guarantee (ordinary) foliations.

This paper is structured as follows. Distributions and the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem are first reviewed in section
III
. We then discuss normal distributions and state our main result in section
IV
. Lastly, we give a proof of this result in section
V
.

III
Review of distributions and the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem

The definitions of the standard terms we use from smooth manifold theory coincide with that of Lee’s book
Lee 2013
and of Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
. In particular, a submanifold with or without boundary in general is not assumed to be embedded, only immersed. We compile the relevant theory of distributions from Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
using our own terminology. Let
M
be a smooth manifold.

For an arbitrary subset
D\subset TM
, a
\bm{D}
-section
is a vector field
X
on
M
such that for all
p\in M
,
X|_{p}\in D
. We also denote
D_{p}=D\cap T_{p}M
for
p\in M
. With this,
D
is called a
distribution on
\bm{M}
if for all
p\in M
,
D_{p}
is a vector subspace of
T_{p}M
and for any
v\in D_{p}
, there exists a
D
-section
X
with
X|_{p}=v
. The
rank of
\bm{D}
is the map
r:M\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
given by
r(p)=\dim D_{p}
. For a smooth map
f:S\to M
between manifolds, we set
f^{*}D=(Tf)^{-1}(D)
and call
f^{*}D
the
pullback of
\bm{D}
on to
\bm{S}
(where
Tf
denotes the tangent map).

For the following, let
D
be a distribution on
M
of rank
r
. An
integral manifold
of
D
is a connected submanifold
I
of
M
such that, for
p\in I
,
Ti|_{p}(T_{p}I)=D_{p}
(
i:I\subset M
). We say that
D
is
integrable
if through every point of
M
, there passes an integral manifold of
D
.

We now introduce the definitions needed to state the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem. To this end, a
local
D
-section
is a local section
X:U\to TM
(where
U
is an open submaifold of
M
) with
X|_{p}\in D
for
p\in U
. A distribution
D
on
M
is said to be:

1.

involutive
if its
D
-sections form a Lie subalgebra of the space of vector fields on
M
,

2.

homogeneous
(after Stefan
Stefan 1974
) if for all local
D
-sections
X
, for any point
(p,t)\in\mathcal{D}
in the (maximal) domain of the flow
\psi^{X}:\mathcal{D}\to M
, one has (after identifying tangent spaces)
T\psi^{X}_{t}|_{p}(D_{p})=D_{\psi^{X}(p,t)}
.

The final definition pertains to certain nice charts for
D
. Let
p\in M
and set
n=\dim M
. A chart
(U,\varphi)
on
M
is said to be
adapted to
\bm{D}
at
\bm{p}
if the following holds (where
(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})
denotes the local frame of
TM
induced by
\varphi
).

1.

\varphi(p)=0
and
\varphi(U)=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{n}
for some
\epsilon>0
,

2.

\partial_{1},...,\partial_{r(p)}
are local
D
-sections,

3.

for all
c\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{n-r(p)}
, denoting
U_{c}=\varphi^{-1}\left((-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{r(p)}\times\{c\}\right)
if
r(p)<n
and
U_{c}=U
otherwise, the rank
r
of
D
is constant along
U_{c}
.

We now state the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem (as formulated in Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
).

Theorem III.1

(Stefan-Sussmann Theorem)
.

Let
M
be a manifold (without boundary) and
D
a distribution on
M
. The following are equivalent.

1.

D
is integrable.

2.

D
is involutive and has constant rank along the integral curves of local
D
-sections.

3.

D
is homogenous.

4.

For every
p\in M
, there exists a chart adapted to
D
at
p
.

The purpose of this paper is to prove an analogous result for what we are calling
normal distributions on manifolds with boundary
. We now discuss this notion and state our main result.

IV
Normal distributions and the main result

The definition of distribution is precisely as before except on a manifold with boundary (including the notions of rank, pullback and involutivity). For the remainder of this section, let
D
be a distribution on a manifold with boundary
M
.

A connected submanifold
L
with boundary of
M
is called an
integral manifold with boundary of
\bm{D}
if for
p\in L
,
Tl|_{p}(T_{p}L)=D_{p}
(
l:L\subset M
). We will say that
D
is
integrable by boundaries
if through every point of
M
, there passes an integral manifold with boundary of
D
.

The distribution
D
is said to be
normal
if for every
p\in\partial M
, there exists a vector in
D_{p}
transverse to
\partial M
. To introduce our analogue of the Stefan-Sussmann Theorem (Theorem
III.1
), we introduce some definitions. For the following, let
D
be a normal distribution on
M
of rank
r
.

An integral manifold with boundary
L
of
D
is said to be
neat
if
\partial L\subset\partial M
. Accordingly,
D
is said to be
neatly integrable by boundaries
if through any point in
M
, there passes a neat integral manifold of
D
.

We now move to analogues of the hypotheses within the Stefan-Sussmann theorem. For the remaining of this section, we assume that
\partial M\neq\emptyset
. The analogues we present will be in terms of some collar; without mentioning any general vector field. This simplification is made by the hypotheses expressing integrability of some constructed distributions on the manifolds
\interior M
and
\partial M
. One can express our main result purely in terms of vector fields by applying the (standard) Stefan-Sussmann Theorem (Theorem
III.1
) and the Boundary Flowout Theorem (Theorem
V.2
).

Recall, a
collar

\Sigma:\partial M\times[0,1)\to M
is an embedding onto a neighbourhood of
\partial M
in
M
such that
\Sigma(p,0)=p
for all
p\in\partial M
. We will call a collar
\Sigma:\partial M\times[0,1)\to M
a
\bm{D}
-precollar
if, for all
(p,t)\in\partial M\times[0,1)
,

r(\Sigma(p,t))=r(p)\text{ and }\Sigma(p,\cdot)^{\prime}(t)\in D_{\Sigma(p,t)}.

The normal distribution
D
will be called
precollared
if a
D
-precollar exists. A collar
\Sigma:\partial M\times[0,1)\to M
will called a
D
-collar
if, denoting
\jmath:\partial M\subset M
, for all
(p,t)\in\partial M\times[0,1)
,

T\Sigma(\cdot,t)|_{p}((\jmath^{*}D)_{p})+T\Sigma(p,\cdot)|_{t}(T_{t}[0,1))=D_{\Sigma(p,t)}.

Accordingly, the normal distribution
D
will be called
collared
if a
D
-collar exists.

Our analogue of adapted charts is closest to the original. However, to ensure these charts account for the distribution being normal, we must use the local vector field generated by the last coordinate of the chart; since this is the only local vector field which is transverse to the boundary. We have arranged this by using the last few coordinates of the chart as opposed to the first few.

Let
p\in M
and set
n=\dim M
. Then, a chart
(U,\varphi)
is said to be
neatly adapted to
\bm{D}
at
\bm{p}
if the following holds.

1.

\varphi(p)=0
and
\varphi(U)=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{n}
if
p\in\interior M
and
\varphi(U)=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{n}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}
otherwise,

2.

\partial_{n-r(p)+1},...,\partial_{n}
are local
D
-sections,

3.

For all
c\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{n-r(p)}
, denoting
U_{c}=\varphi^{-1}\left(\{c\}\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{r(p)}\right)
if
r(p)<n
and
U_{c}=U
otherwise, the rank
r
of
D
is constant along
U_{c}
.

With these definitions, we may present our main result.

Theorem IV.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold with boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
D
be a normal distribution of rank
r
on
M
. Denote by
\imath:\interior M\subset M
and
\jmath:\partial M\subset M
the inclusions of the interior and the boundary into
M
. Then, the pullback
\imath^{*}D
is a distribution of rank
r|_{\interior M}
on
\interior M
and
\jmath^{*}D
is a distribution of rank
r|_{\partial M}-1
on
\partial M
. Moreover, the following are equivalent.

S1.

D
is neatly integrable by boundaries.

S2.

\imath^{*}D
is integrable and
D
is precollared.

S3.

\imath^{*}D
and
\jmath^{*}D
are integrable and
D
is collared.

S4.

For every
p\in M
, there exists a chart neatly adapted to
D
at
p
.

Using Theorem
IV.1
and Proposition
V.6
(found in the next section), a Frobenius theorem may be formulated as follows.

Corollary IV.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold with boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
D
be a normal distribution of constant rank. Then the following are equivalent.

F1.

D
is neatly integrable by boundaries.

F2.

D
is involutive.

F3.

For every
p\in M
, there exists a chart neatly adapted to
D
at
p
.

We will now prove Theorem
IV.1
.

V
Proof of main result

Our proof will establish five implications directly. Although this is not the optimal amount (four is optimal), three of our chosen implications are trivial, making for only two substantial implications. We will now establish some preliminary observations of distributions which we used throughout in proving these implications.

V.1
Preliminary observations of distributions and of normal distributions

We first state two additional results found in Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
for later use. The first is on the “regularity” of integral manifolds of distributions. They are in fact
weakly embedded
. We introduce some notation to express this and related notions to simplify subsequent proofs.

Let
A,B,M,N
be manifolds with boundary and
f:M\to N
be a map between manifolds with boundary.

1.

If
A\subset M
, the restriction is denoted
f|_{A}:A\to N
as usual.

2.

If
f(M)\subset B
, we will write the induced map as
f|^{B}:M\to B
. We call
f|^{B}
the
co-restriction of
\bm{f}
to
B
if
B\subset N
and call it the
co-extension of
\bm{f}
to
\bm{B}
if
B\supset Y
. If
f(A)\subset B
, we define
f|_{A}^{B}=(f|_{A})^{B}
for succinctness.

3.

If
A
is an submanifold with boundary in
M
and
f
is an injective immersion and
B
is the unique submanifold with boundary of
N
such that
f|_{A}^{B}
is a diffeomorphism, we denote
f(A)=B
.

4.

If
M
is a manifold and
A
is a submanifold of
M
, it is said that
\bm{A}
is weakly embedded in
\bm{M}
if for any smooth map
F:S\to M
between manifolds such that

, we have that the co-restriction
F|^{A}
is also smooth.

5.

We will denote by
\text{Pts}(A)
the set of points of
A
when it is necessary for distinction.

The following is Proposition 3.5.15 in Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
.

Proposition V.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
D
an integrable distribution on
M
. Then, all integral manifolds of
D
are weakly embedded in
M
.

Another important result found in Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
is on the existence of maximal integral manifolds. An integral manifold
I
of a distribution
D
on a manifold
M
is
maximal
if, for any integral manifold
H
of
D
with a point in common with
I
,
H
is an open submanifold of
I
. The following is Theorem 3.5.17 in Rudolf and Schmidt’s book
Rudolph and Schmidt 2012
adapted to our context.

Theorem V.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
D
an integrable distribution on
M
. Then, through every point of
M
, there passes a unique maximal integral manifold of
D
.

This gives rise to a convenient containment property of integral curves.

Proposition V.2
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
D
an integrable distribution on
M
. Let
X
be a
D
-section,
\gamma:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to M
be an integral curve of
X
and
I
be a maximal integral manifold of
D
with
\gamma\cap I\neq\emptyset
. Then,
\gamma\subset I
.

Proof.

First let
H
be a maximal integral manifold of
D
. We see that
X
is tangent to
H
. Hence, there is a
h
-related vector field
\tilde{X}
to
X
where
h:H\subset M
is the inclusion. Then consider
\gamma^{-1}(H)
. Let
t\in\gamma^{-1}(H)
. Then considering the integral curves of
\tilde{X}
, by uniqueness, there is a neighbourhood
(t-\delta,t+\delta)
of

for some
\delta>0
with
(t-\delta,t+\delta)\subset\gamma^{-1}(I)
. Hence,
\gamma^{-1}(I)
is open in the interval
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
.

By assumption
\gamma^{-1}(I)\neq\emptyset
. Hence, by the above,
\gamma^{-1}(I)
is open and nonempty in
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. Now consider the compliment
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\backslash\gamma^{-1}(I)=\gamma^{-1}(M\backslash I)
. By Theorem
V.1
, replacing each
p\in M
with the unique maximal integral manifold
L
of
D
containing
p
, we obtain a partition
\mathcal{C}
of
M
by the maximal integral manifolds of
D
. Hence,
M\backslash I=\cup_{H\in\mathcal{C}\backslash\{I\}}H
so that,

(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\backslash\gamma^{-1}(I)=\gamma^{-1}(M\backslash I)=\gamma^{-1}(\cup_{H\in\mathcal{C}\backslash\{I\}}H)=\cup_{H\in\mathcal{C}\backslash\{I\}}\gamma^{-1}(H).

Hence, using the above again,
\gamma^{-1}(M\backslash I)
is open in
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. Since
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
is connected, it follows that
\gamma^{-1}(I)=(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
. That is,
\gamma\subset I
.
∎

Proposition V.3

We first note that boundaries are essential for integrability of normal distributions by boundaries.

Proposition V.4
.

If

.

Proposition V.5
.

There exists an inward-pointing vector field

on

For each

We will also require some simple pull-back properties for distributions.

Proposition V.7
.

Let
M
be a manifold with boundary and
D
a distribution on
M
. Let
S
be a manifold with boundary of codimension
1
and
F:S\to M
an injective immersion. Let
\omega
be a
1
-form on
M
such that
F^{*}\omega=0
and
X
be a
D
-section with
\omega(X)=1
. Then,
F^{*}D
is a distribution on
S
of rank
r\circ F-1
where
r
is the rank of
D
.

Proof.

Let
p\in S
and denote
q=F(p)
. From
\dim{S}=\dim{M}-1
,
\omega|_{q}(X|_{q})=1
, and injectivity of
TF|_{p}:T_{p}S\to T_{q}M
, we have that
\mathop{\text{im}}{TF|_{p}}=\ker\omega|_{q}
. Now, consider the linear map
A_{q}:T_{q}M\to T_{q}M
given by
A_{q}(u)=u-\omega|_{q}(u)X|_{q}
. We claim that
TF|_{p}((F^{*}D)_{p})=A_{q}(D_{q})
.

Indeed, one readily sees that
(F^{*}D)_{p}=(TF|_{p})^{-1}(D_{q})
and
\ker\omega|_{q}=\mathop{\text{im}}{A_{q}}
so that,

TF|_{p}((F^{*}D)_{p})=TF|_{p}((TF|_{p})^{-1}(D_{q}))=\mathop{\text{im}}{TF|_{p}}\cap D_{q}=\ker\omega|_{q}\cap D_{q}=\mathop{\text{im}}{A_{q}}\cap D_{q}.

We now consider
\mathop{\text{im}}{A_{q}}\cap D_{q}
. For any
u,v\in T_{q}M
such that
v=A_{q}(u)
, we have
v=u-\omega|_{q}(u)X|_{q}
. Hence, since
X|_{q}\in D_{q}
and
D_{q}
is a vector subspace of
T_{q}M
, we have

if and only if
v\in D_{q}
. This shows that
\mathop{\text{im}}{A_{q}}\cap D_{q}=A_{q}(D_{q})
. Hence, in total,

TF|_{p}((F^{*}D)_{p})=\mathop{\text{im}}{A_{q}}\cap D_{q}=A_{q}(D_{q}),

as claimed.

To compute the dimension of
(F^{*}D)_{p}
, we consider the linear map
B_{q}=A_{q}|_{D_{q}}
. Since
\dim{\ker{B_{q}}}=1
, the vector subspace
TF|_{p}((F^{*}D)_{p})=A_{q}(D_{q})
has dimension
\dim\text{im}B_{q}=r(q)-1
. Hence, because
TF|_{p}
is a linear injection,
(F^{*}D)_{p}
is a vector subspace of
T_{p}S
of dimension
r(q)-1=(r\circ F-1)(p)
.

Now, let
v\in(F^{*}D)_{p}
. Then,
TF(v)\in D
and
v\in T_{p}S
so that
w=TF|_{p}(v)\in D_{q}
. Hence, there is a
D
-section
W
with
W|_{q}=w
. Consider the vector field
A(W)=W-\omega(W)X
. For

, we have that,

A(W)|_{F(p^{\prime})}=A_{F(p^{\prime})}(W|_{F(p^{\prime})})\in A_{F(p^{\prime})}(D_{F(p^{\prime})})=TF|_{p^{\prime}}((F^{*}D)_{p^{\prime}}),

so that
W|_{F(p^{\prime})}=TF|_{p^{\prime}}(u)
for some
u\in(F^{*}D)_{p^{\prime}}\subset T_{p^{\prime}}S
. Hence, since
F
is an immersion, there exists a unique map
Y:S\to TS
with
Y|_{p^{\prime}}\in T_{p^{\prime}}S
for

\imath^{*}D=\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{*}D_{1}=(\pi_{1}\circ\imath)^{*}D_{1}
, where
\tilde{\pi}_{1}:\interior M\to A\times(0,1)
is projection onto the first factor.

Then, consider
E=\pi_{1}^{*}(D_{1})
. We have from Lemma
V.9
that
E
is a neatly integrable distribution of rank
r_{1}\circ\pi_{1}+1
(where
r_{1}
is the rank of
D_{1}
). Hence,
E
is of constant rank along the curves
\gamma_{p}=\text{Id}(p,\cdot):[0,1)\to M
. Moreover, we see that
\imath^{*}E=\imath^{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}D_{1})=(\pi_{1}\circ\imath)^{*}D_{1}=\imath^{*}D
and hence
D_{(p,t)}=E_{(p,t)}
for all
(p,t)\in\interior M
.

Hence, by Corollary
V.4
, we must have that
D=E
and hence,
D
is neatly integrable by boundaries.
∎

With this, we derive implication
S2\Rightarrow S1
in Theorem
IV.1
.

Application of the product analysis

Lemma V.12
.

Let
M
be a manifold with boundary
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
D
be a normal distribution of rank
r
on
M
. Let
\imath:\interior M\subset M
and
\jmath:\partial M\subset M
denote the inclusions. Then we have the implication
S2\Rightarrow S1
in Theorem
IV.1
.

Proof.

Assume that
\imath^{*}D
is integrable and
D
is precollared.

Let
\Sigma:\partial M\times[0,1)\to M
be a
D
-precollar. Denote by
\sigma=\Sigma|^{V}
the co-restriction diffeomorphism onto
V=\Sigma(\partial M\times[0,1))
. The inclusions we require are denoted by
\iota:V\subset M
,
\tilde{\imath}:\interior V\subset V
,
\tilde{\jmath}:\partial V\subset V
and
\imath^{\prime}:\partial M\times(0,1)\subset\partial M\times[0,1)
. Lastly, we set
\tilde{D}=\tilde{\imath}^{*}D
.

First, notice that since
\tilde{D}=\tilde{\imath}^{*}D
is a distribution, it is clear that then
D^{\prime}=\sigma^{*}\tilde{D}
is a distribution on
\partial M\times[0,1)
since
\sigma
is a diffeomorphism. Moreover, since
\imath^{*}D
is integable, it follows easily that
\tilde{\imath}^{*}\tilde{D}
is integrable. Since again
\sigma
is a diffeomorphism, it is clear then that
\iota^{\prime*}D^{\prime}
is integrable.

Now, since
\Sigma
is a
D
-precollar, the vector field
\tilde{N}=T\sigma\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\circ\sigma^{-1}
is a
\tilde{D}
-section and
\tilde{D}
has constant rank along the curves
\sigma(p,\cdot)
for
p\in\partial V=\partial M
. Hence,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
is a
D^{\prime}
-section and
D^{\prime}
has constant rank along the curves
\gamma_{p}=\text{Id}(p,\cdot)
for
p\in\partial M
.

Hence, altogether, by Lemma
V.11
,
D^{\prime}
is neatly integrable by boundaries. Hence, since
\sigma
is a diffeomorphism, it easily follows that
\tilde{D}
is neatly integrable by boundaries. From this, since again
\imath^{*}D
is integrable by assumption, we obtain altogether that
D
is neatly integrable by boundaries.
∎

Let

\jmath^{*}D
at

(U^{\prime},\tilde{\varphi})

\text{id}:[0,\epsilon)\to[0,\epsilon)

\Sigma

(U,\varphi)
is neatly adapted to

.

III.1

Lemma V.14
.

Assume that at every point
p\in M

Nilpotent Higgs bundles and families of flat connections
Sebastian Schulz

Abstract




Abstract

We investigate
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-families of flat connections whose leading term is a nilpotent Higgs field. Examples of such families include real twistor lines and families arising from the conformal limit. We show that these families have the same monodromy as families whose leading term is a regular Higgs bundle and use this to deduce that traces of holonomies are asymptotically exponential in rational powers of the parameter of the family.

1
Introduction

1.1
Higgs bundles and the WKB method

The theory of Higgs bundles is intimately intertwined with the theory of families of flat connections. Given a Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
on a Riemann surface
C
, there are several
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-families

\nabla_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi+D+\varepsilon\psi

of flat connections naturally associated to it, for example through the
Non-Abelian Hodge Correspondence
and through the
Conformal Limit
construction. It is natural to ask how these the monodromy of these connections behave in the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit. The
exact WKB method

[
Vor83
]
is an important tool in tackling problems of this sort and has played a pivotal role in shedding light on a variety of geometric structures over the last decade.

Firstly, Gaiotto-Moore-Neitzke
[
GMN13
]
proposed a conjectural description of the asymptotics of the Hitchin system’s Hyperkähler metric that was motivated by the WKB method. Their conjecture has since been confirmed in many different settings
[
MSWW16
,
MSWW19
,
DN19
,
Fre20
,
FMSW20
]
.

Secondly, Iwaki-Nakanishi
[
IN14
,
IN16
]
have related the exact WKB method to the theory of
Cluster algebras
. Cluster algebras, first introduced by Fomin-Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
, have proven ubiquitous in Higher Teichmüller Theory thanks to the work of Fock-Goncharov
[
FG06
]
and are linked to a variety of facets of mathematics, see e.g.
[
Kel10
]
for a survey.

Thirdly, though not unrelated, WKB analysis is well-known to be related to the study of Donaldson-Thomas invariants and their wall-crossing structure
[
All19
,
AB20
,
Bri19
,
BS15
]
.

All of these applications are subject to a certain genericity condition, namely that the Higgs field can be diagonalized with distinct eigenvalues, at least away from a finite set of points of
C
. Under this condition, the exact WKB method gives the following: For any closed loop
\gamma
on
C
, the trace of the holonomy around
\gamma
grows exponentially in
\varepsilon^{-1}
in the
\varepsilon\searrow 0
limit
1
1

1

That is
\varepsilon\to 0
while
\varepsilon>0
.
. More precisely, there exists a constant
Z_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
,
\operatorname{Re}(Z_{\gamma})>0
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\big(\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla_{\varepsilon})\cdot\exp(-\varepsilon^{-1}Z_{\gamma})\big)

(1.1)

exists and is non-zero, see Section
2.7
for a more detailed discussion. The constant
Z_{\gamma}
admits a geometric interpretation as a period of the
spectral curve
, a certain (ramified) cover of
C
determined by the Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
.

A similar description without this genericity assumption has so far been unknown, and in this paper we deal with the most degenerate case of a
nilpotent Higgs bundle
, meaning one for which the eigenvalues of
\varphi
are all identically zero. The exact WKB method is not directly applicable in this situation because it is unclear what the analogue of the spectral curve should be. Moreover, the constant
Z_{\gamma}
in
1.1
is an integral of the eigenvalue of
\varphi
with the largest real part, which in this case would simply yield zero.

1.2
Families of flat connections

Before we proceed, let us briefly explain what kind of families of flat connections we consider. A Higgs bundle on a Riemann surface
C
is a pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
consisting of a holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
and a holomorphic endomorphism
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,End(E)\otimes K_{C})
where
K_{C}
denotes the holomorphic cotangent bundle of
C
. A Higgs bundle generically obeys a stability condition analogous to that for vector bundles, and for stable Higgs bundles there exists a unique hermitian metric
h
such that

\nabla_{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1}\varphi+D_{h}+\zeta\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}

(1.2)

is a flat connection for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, where
D_{h}
denotes the Chern connection of the pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},h)
. This is a consequence of the Non-Abelian Hodge Correspondence and
\nabla_{\zeta}
is known as a
real twistor line
; see Section
2.2
for more details.

We shall also be interested in a family of flat connections known as the
Conformal Limit
family, as follows: For any
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
there exists a hermitian metric
h_{R}
such that

\nabla(\zeta,R):=\zeta^{-1}R\varphi+D_{h_{R}}+\zeta R\varphi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}

(1.3)

is a flat connection for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. Letting
\hbar=R^{-1}\zeta
this becomes a family

\nabla_{\hbar,R}=\hbar^{-1}\varphi+D_{h_{R}}+\hbar R^{2}\varphi^{\dagger_{h_{R}}}.

(1.4)

The
R\to 0
limit of this family is known as the
\hbar
-
conformal limit

\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
of the Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
. We provide more details in Section
2.4
.

1.3
Main results

Our main theorems concern a weaker version of WKB theory that is not applicable to all curves
\gamma
but only to curves we call
WKB curves
, see section
2.7
. It is important to note that WKB curves exist on any Riemann surface
C
and for any non-nilpotent
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
on it, see Appendix
A
. Also, recall that the moduli space of Higgs bundles
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
admits a
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action given by
\xi\cdot(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)=(\overline{\partial}_{E},\xi\cdot\varphi)
. Then we prove that the holonomy of a real twistor line around
\gamma
grows exponentially in
\zeta^{-1/2}
. More precisely,

Theorem
3.7

For a nilpotent
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle that is not a fixed point of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action, there exists a closed curve
\gamma
on
C
and a
Z_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\operatorname{Re}(Z_{\gamma})>0
such that the limit

\lim_{\zeta\searrow 0}\big(\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla_{\zeta})\cdot\exp(-\zeta^{-1/2}Z_{\gamma})\big),

(1.5)

exists and is non-zero. Here,
\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}
denotes the holonomy around
\gamma
and
\nabla_{\zeta}
is the family of flat connections (
1.2
).

In fact, we will show that
Z_{\gamma}
is a period of the spectral curve
\Sigma^{\prime}
of a non-nilpotent Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
that is canonically obtained from
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
. Any curve
\gamma
that is a WKB curve with respect to
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
will obey (
1.5
).

Theorem
3.7
holds in slightly more generality then we have stated here. For example, we show that the analogous statement is true for the conformal limit family
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
if the vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is itself stable.

Let us explain how this
secondary Higgs bundle

(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
arises. If
\varphi\neq 0
then
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
defines a
C^{\infty}
-decomposition
E=K\oplus L
into line bundles, where
K=\ker\varphi
and
L=K^{\bot_{h}}
is the orthogonal complement with respect to the harmonic metric
h
. The matrix
g(\varepsilon)=\operatorname{\sf diag}(\varepsilon^{-1/4},\varepsilon^{1/4})
defines a gauge transformation via conjugation, and applied to the family
\nabla_{\varepsilon}
it yields a new family

\nabla^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=g(\varepsilon)^{-1}\cdot\nabla_{\varepsilon}\cdot g(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\Phi+\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}+\partial^{\prime}_{E}+O(\varepsilon^{1/2}).

(1.6)

We use this construction and prove the following

Proposition
3.4

Let
\nabla_{\zeta}
be the real twistor line of a stable, nilpotent Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
. Then there exists a gauge-equivalent family

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1/2}\Phi+\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}+\partial^{\prime}_{E}+O(\zeta^{1/2})

(1.7)

of flat connections such that
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a holomorphic, stable Higgs bundle. Moreover,
\Phi
is nilpotent if and only if
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is a fixed point of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action.

We prove the analogous statement for a conformal limit family
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
provided that the vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is itself stable (Proposition
3.5
below). It is instructive to note that the gauge transformation (
1.6
) does not necessarily preserve the
type
of the family. For example, if
\nabla_{\zeta}
represents a real twistor line then
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
does not because it is not real (Proposition
3.9
below). If
\nabla_{\hbar}
arises from the conformal limit, then
\nabla^{\prime}_{\hbar}
has terms of powers
1/2
,
1
and
3/2
in
\hbar
.

Proposition
3.4
is the key step to proving Theorem
3.7
, as it allows to use the WKB method on the family
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
. It is worth noting that the WKB method is typically employed on families that contain only integer powers of
\zeta
. There is no major obstruction to using the WKB method on families that contain rational powers of
\zeta
and this was recently described by Thomas
[
Tho21
]
as the
Rational WKB method
and used to construct a formal embedding of the cotangent bundle of the space of higher complex structures into the character variety. It would be very interesting to further investigate the connection between these two different appearances of the rational WKB method.

As an application of Theorem
3.7
we prove part of a conjecture of Simpson
[
Sim10
]
concerning a natural stratification of the moduli space of flat connections
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
known as the
partial oper stratification
. Simpson conjectured that the individual leaves of the stratification are closed inside of
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
which we answer affirmatively for the leaves inside of the unique open stratum in Corollary
3.8
in the
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
case. Note that this was already known for a minimal dimensional stratum known as the
space of opers
.

1.4
Generalizations

We subsequently discuss the generalization to higher rank Higgs bundles. A novelty is a certain integer
m
(
2\leqslant m\leqslant n
) that is an invariant of a nilpotent
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle which is not a fixed point of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action. We restrict our attention to the family of real twistor lines
\nabla_{\zeta}
and prove

Theorem
4.6

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
be a stable nilpotent
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle that is not a
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-fixed point. Then there exists an integer
m
,
2\leqslant m\leqslant n
, such that the associated real twistor line
\nabla_{\zeta}
from (
1.2
) is gauge-equivalent to a family of the form

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}=\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\Phi+\dots+\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}+\partial^{\prime}_{E}+\dots

(1.8)

and the pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a stable
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle that is not nilpotent.

As a consequence, we expect that asymptotically

\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla_{\zeta})=\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta})\sim\exp(\zeta^{(1-m)/m}Z_{\gamma})

(1.9)

for some constant
Z_{\gamma}
. This is based on the higher rank WKB method which is more complicated and less well-developed.

Lastly, we discuss parabolic
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundles and show that the statements of Proposition
3.4
and Theorem
3.7
hold for parabolic twistor lines. The main difficulty is that one has to make sure that the new family
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
behaves well near the puncture, i.e. that the constituents of
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
have the correct singular behavior.

Though we do not discuss it in detail here, the generalization to
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundles should be immediate. It would be interesting to see the generalization to
G_{\mathbb{C}}
-Higgs bundles for arbitrary reductive groups
G_{\mathbb{C}}
over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Structure:
This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we recall necessary background about the Hitchin system. In Section
3
we study the
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-case described above, this section contains most of the novel ideas of this work. We generalize our results to the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
in Section
4
and to parabolic
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundles in section
5
. We discuss the nilpotent cone of the moduli space of Higgs bundles on a four-punctured sphere, dubbed the
toy model
for its simplicity, in Section
6
and include in Appendix
A
a review on (Half-) Translation surfaces that is key to our construction of WKB curves.

2
The geometry of the Hitchin system

In this section we review the definition of Higgs bundles as well as the geometric properties of the moduli space of (stable) Higgs bundles. We will focus on the case
G={\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
and fix a closed, connected Riemann surface
C
whose canonical bundle is denoted by
K_{C}
, as well as a complex vector bundle
E\to C
of rank
n
together with a trivialization of its determinant line bundle
\Lambda^{n}E
.

2.1
The Hitchin system

Definition 2.1
.

An
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle is a pair
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
consisting of

(i)

a holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{E}
on
E
, together with a trivialization of its determinant line bundle
\det(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{C}
, and

(ii)

a traceless, holomorphic section
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes K_{C})
.

We will often denote holomorphic vector bundles by
\mathcal{E}=(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
.
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundles form a coarse moduli space, at least once the appropriate stability conditions have been imposed. To this end, recall that the
slope

\mu
of a complex vector bundle
E
is the quotient

\mu(E):=\frac{\deg E}{\operatorname{rk}E}

(2.1)

of the degree of
E
by its rank. The degree of a vector bundle is equal to the degree of its determinant line bundle, so the vector bundle underlying an
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle must have degree (and hence slope) zero.

Definition 2.2
.

A Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is called
stable
if

\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{E})

(2.2)

for every proper, holomorphic,
\varphi
-invariant subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{E}
. Here,
\varphi
-invariant means that
\varphi(\mathcal{F})\subset\mathcal{F}\otimes K_{C}
. A Higgs bundle is called
polystable
if it is the direct sum of stable Higgs bundles, all of which have the same slope.

Clearly, stability implies polystability. A morphism of Higgs bundles is one where the obvious diagram commutes, and stable (resp. polystable) Higgs bundles on
C
up to isomorphism form a moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}(C,n)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}^{ps}(C,n)
) known as the
Dolbeault moduli space
,
moduli space of stable Higgs bundles
or
Hitchin system
. When there is no confusion we write simply
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}:=\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}(C,n)
, and similarly
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}^{ps}
.
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}^{ps}
is a (singular) normal, quasiprojective variety of dimension
(n^{2}-1)(2g-2)
whose smooth locus is
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
. Moreover, it is an algebraically completely integrable system
[
Hit87a
]
which can be seen as follows: Note that the Higgs field
\varphi
locally on the surface is a (traceless,
K_{C}
-valued) endomorphism up to conjugation, so a more invariant way to describe it is in terms of its eigensystem. To this end, pick a homogeneous basis
p_{2},\dots,p_{n}
of the algebra of invariant polynomials
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{sl}(n,{\mathbb{C}})]^{{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})}
(e.g. the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial, or the polynomials
p_{k}(M)=\operatorname{Tr}(M^{k})
). In the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
the polynomials will have degrees
\deg p_{k}=k
, and we can recall the definition of the
Hitchin map

\displaystyle\begin{split}h:\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}(C,n)&\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(C,n):=\bigoplus_{k=2}^{n}H^{0}(C,K_{C}^{k}),\\
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)&\longmapsto(p_{2}(\varphi),\dots,p_{n}(\varphi)).\end{split}

(2.3)

The space
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(C,n)
is known as the
Hitchin base
, and
h
is a proper, surjective, holomorphic map whose fibers are special Lagrangian (hence generically complex, compact tori), turning
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}^{ps}
into an integrable system as previously advertised.

2.2
Non-abelian Hodge Theory

Recall that a Hermitian metric
h
on a holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
determines the
Chern connection

D
, the unique connection that is
h
-unitary and whose
(0,1)
-part agrees with the Dolbeault operator of the holomorphic bundle,
D^{(0,1)}=\overline{\partial}_{E}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
R\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. A
harmonic metric with parameter
R
for a Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is a Hermitian metric
h
on
\mathcal{E}
that induces the trivial metric on
\det\mathcal{E}
and which solves the (rescaled)
Hitchin’s equation

F_{D}+R^{2}\left[\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}\right]=0.

(2.4)

Here,
F_{D}
is the curvature of the Chern connection
D
, and
\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}
denotes the adjoint with respect to the metric
h
.

A fundamental result in the theory of Higgs bundles is the celebrated
Non-abelian Hodge Theorem
(NAHT), which dates back to Donaldson
[
Don87
]
and Hitchin
[
Hit87a
]
for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
, and to Corlette
[
Cor88
]
and Simpson
[
Sim88
]
for the general case. Before we state it, recall that a flat connection
\nabla
on a vector bundle
E
is called

•

completely reducible
if for every
\nabla
-invariant subbundle
F\subset E
there is a
\nabla
-invariant complement, and

•

irreducible
if there are no non-trivial
\nabla
-invariant subbundles of
E
.

Irreducible (resp. completely reducible) flat connections up to equivalence form a moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}:=\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}(n,C)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}^{cr}
), called the
de Rham moduli space
, which is diffeomorphic (resp. homeomorphic) to
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}^{ps}
) via the NAHT:

Theorem 2.1

(Non-abelian Hodge Theorem)
.

Fix
R\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. A Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
is polystable if and only if there exists a harmonic metric
h
with parameter
R
. Moreover, for this
h
,

\nabla_{\zeta,R}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi):=\zeta^{-1}R\varphi+D+\zeta R\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}

(2.5)

is a completely reducible flat connection for any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, which is irreducible if and only if
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
is stable. Conversely, a flat connection
\nabla
is completely reducible if and only if there exists a hermitian metric
h
such that in the unique decomposition of
\nabla
in the form of (
2.5
) with
\zeta=1
one has
\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi=0
. In that case,
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
is a polystable Higgs bundle which is stable if and only if
\nabla
is irreducible.

Of course,
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
are only diffeomorphic but not biholomorphic: While the former has compact, complex submanifolds, the latter is Stein. Nonetheless, both are Kähler with Riemannian metric
g
and complex structure
I
resp.
J
, and the product
K=IJ=-JI
forms another complex structure which completes that quadruple
(g,I,J,K)
that turns
\mathcal{M}
into a Hyperkähler manifold. In particular, there is a
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
of complex structures
I_{\zeta}
which is normalized so that
I_{0}=I
and
I_{1}=J
. We will mostly be concerned with complex structures
I
and
J
and will continue denoting the corresponding Kähler manifolds by
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
.

As with any Hyperkähler manifold, it can be convenient to consider its twistor space. It will suffice for us to define the twistor space over
{\mathbb{C}}
(rather than all of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
), which is known as the moduli space of Deligne’s
\lambda
-connections (
[
Del
]
,
[
Sim97
]
):

Definition 2.4
.

A
\lambda
-connection on
E
is a triple
(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\overline{\partial}_{E}
is a holomorphic structure on
E
and
\nabla_{\lambda}:\Omega^{0}(E)\rightarrow\Omega^{0,1}(E)
is a differential operator subject to

(i)

a
\lambda
-twisted Leibniz rule: for all
f\in C^{\infty}(C)
and
s\in\Omega^{0}(E)

\nabla_{\lambda}(fs)=\lambda\partial f\otimes s+f\nabla_{\lambda}(s),

(2.6)

(ii)

a holomorphicity condition
[\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda}]=0
.

When
\lambda=0
then
\nabla_{0}
becomes
C^{\infty}
-linear, i.e. a holomorphic Higgs field. If
\lambda\neq 0
, then
\nabla:=\lambda^{-1}\nabla_{\lambda}
is a holomorphic connection on
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
, or, equivalently,
D=\overline{\partial}_{E}+\nabla
is a flat connection on
E
. Equivalence classes
[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]
form the
Hodge moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\text{Hod}}
of (polystable)
\lambda
-connections. It comes equipped with a tautological map

\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\text{Hod}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\hskip 28.45274pt[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]\mapsto\lambda.

(2.7)

As we just described,
\pi^{-1}(0)\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
and
\pi^{-1}(\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
when
\lambda\neq 0
. The family (
2.5
) is a
real twistor line
: A holomorphic section of
\pi
that obeys the reality condition

\overline{\nabla_{-\overline{\zeta}^{-1}}}=g^{-1}\nabla_{\zeta}g=:\nabla_{\zeta}.g,

(2.8)

where
g
is a gauge transformation acting on connections in the usual way.

2.3
The
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action

The Hodge moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\text{Hod}}
comes equipped with a natural
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action

\xi\cdot[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]:=[(\xi\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\xi\nabla_{\lambda})],\hskip 28.45274pt\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}.

(2.9)

In particular,
\pi
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-equivariant:

\pi\left(\xi\cdot[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]\right)=\xi\cdot\lambda=\xi\cdot\pi[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})].

(2.10)

This
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action restricts to the fiber
\pi^{-1}(0)
of Higgs bundles, where it coincides with the usual action of rescaling the Higgs field. A fixed point of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action must necessarily have
\lambda=0
and is therefore a Higgs bundle. Such a fixed point is called a
variation of Hodge structure
(VHS) and must lie in the fiber
h^{-1}(0)=:N\subset\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
called the
nilpotent cone
. The
\xi\to 0
limit of any Higgs bundle exists by properness of the Hitchin map and is a VHS. To illustrate that the Higgs field of a fixed point can be non-zero, let
K_{C}^{1/2}
denote a choice of square root of the canonical bundle and define the following Higgs bundle:

\mathcal{E}_{n}=K_{C}^{\frac{1-n}{2}}\oplus K_{C}^{\frac{3-n}{2}}\oplus\dots\oplus K_{C}^{\frac{n-1}{2}},\hskip 28.45274pt\varphi_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&\\
&0&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&1\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.11)

This Higgs bundle represents the
uniformization point
in
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}(C,n)
, and it is easy to see that

(\mathcal{E}_{n},\xi\varphi_{n})=(\mathcal{E},g^{-1}(\xi)\varphi_{n}g(\xi))=:(\mathcal{E},\varphi_{n}).g(\xi)

(2.12)

where

g(\xi)=\mathrm{diag}\left(\xi^{\frac{1-n}{2}},\xi^{\frac{3-n}{2}},\dots,\xi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right).

(2.13)

In particular,

&0&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\varphi_{k-1,k}\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix},

(2.14)

where
\varphi_{i,i+1}:\mathcal{V}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{V}_{i}\otimes K_{C}

The conformal limit

We will be interested in the limit of the family

(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in V_{\alpha}

W^{i}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0}):=\{[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]\in W^{i}_{\alpha}:\lim_{\xi\to 0}\xi\cdot[(\lambda,\overline{\partial}_{E},\nabla_{\lambda})]=[(0,\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})]\}.

\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}(r\cdot(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi))=\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar/r}(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi).

2.5
Very stable Higgs bundles

In these notes, we will be interested in nilpotent Higgs bundles, particularly those that are not fixed by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action. We collect here a few of the results about their existence and structure.

Drinfeld
[
Dri81
]
and Laumon
[
Lau88
]
introduced the notion of a
very stable vector bundle
, which is a holomorphic vector bundle on a connected, compact Riemann surface that admits no non-zero nilpotent Higgs fields. For a surface of genus
g>1
these vector bundles are necessarily stable in the sense discussed previously, and they form a non-empty open subset in the moduli space of (stable) vector bundles
[
Lau88
]
. Pauly and Peón-Nieto
[
PPN19
]
proved that a stable vector bundle
[(\overline{\partial}_{0},0)]
is very stable if and only if the leaf
W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},0)
is closed inside
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
or, equivalently, if the restriction of the Hitchin map to the leaf is proper. A stable bundle that is not very stable is called
wobbly
.

Hausel and Hitchin
[
HH21
]
have recently generalized these definitions to the other components
V_{\alpha}
of the fixed locus inside the nilpotent cone
N
.

Definition 2.6
.

A stable Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in V_{\alpha}
is called very stable if

W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\cap N=\{[(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})]\}.

(2.25)

The theorem of Pauly and Peón-Nieto generalizes in the sense that a stable Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
is very stable if and only if
W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\subset\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
is closed. As in the case of vector bundles, a stable Higgs bundle is called
wobbly
if it is not very stable.

Wobbly Higgs bundles in
\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}(C,n)
exist for any choice of
(C,n)
because wobbly vector bundles do, though it is an open question which components
V_{\alpha}
admit wobbly Higgs bundles. Some partial progress has been made, e.g. for fixed points
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
whose underlying vector bundle is a direct sum of line bundles
L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{n}
. In this case,
\varphi_{0}^{n-1}
has a single non-trivial component
b\in H^{0}(C,L_{1}^{\ast}\otimes L_{n}\otimes K_{C}^{n-1})
and
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
is very stable if and only if
b
has no repeated zeroes
[
HH21
, Theorem 1.2]
. For example, the uniformizing Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi_{n})
is evidently very stable, and because it constitutes its own connected component
V_{\alpha}
this component does not contain wobbly points.

On the other hand, one might have naively expected that all components
V_{\alpha}
contain very stable Higgs bundle and that they would form a dense open subset. While this is true in the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, there is a component for the
{\mathrm{SL}}(3,{\mathbb{C}})
moduli space for which all Higgs bundles are wobbly
[
Got94
]
,
[
HH21
, Remark 5.12]
.

2.6
A parametrization of the stable manifold

Collier and Wentworth
[
CW19
]
give an explicit parametrization of the leaves
W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
which we want to quickly recall here.

First, note that any nilpotent Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
induces a filtration

0\subset\ker\varphi\subset\ker(\varphi^{2})\subset\dots\subset\ker(\varphi^{n})=\mathcal{E},

(2.26)

and the fixed points
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in V_{\alpha}
are precisely those for which there is a compatible holomorphic grading as in Proposition
2.2
, in the sense that
\ker(\varphi^{j})=\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{j}
. Such a splitting induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on the vector bundle
\operatorname{End}\mathcal{E}
via

\operatorname{End}_{j}\mathcal{E}:=\bigoplus_{i-l=j}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{V}_{l}),

(2.27)

where we set
\operatorname{End}_{j}\mathcal{E}=0
if
|j|>k-1
, and we define

N_{+}:=\bigoplus_{j>0}\operatorname{End}_{j}\mathcal{E},\hskip 14.22636ptN_{-}:=\bigoplus_{j<0}\operatorname{End}_{j}\mathcal{E},\hskip 14.22636ptL:=\operatorname{End}_{0}\mathcal{E}\cap\mathfrak{sl}(E).

(2.28)

Next, recall that for
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in V_{\alpha}
with harmonic metric
h
the connection
D=\varphi_{0}+\partial^{h}_{0}+\overline{\partial}_{0}+\varphi_{0}^{\dagger_{h}}
is flat, where
\partial_{0}^{h}+\overline{\partial}_{0}
is the Chern connection associated to the pair
(\overline{\partial}_{0},h)
. Let us define

D^{\prime}=\partial_{0}^{h}+\varphi_{0}^{\dagger_{h}},\hskip 14.22636ptD^{\prime\prime}=\overline{\partial}_{0}+\varphi_{0};

(2.29)

then
D=D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}
. Define furthermore the
BB-slice
through
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
as

\mathcal{S}^{+}_{(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})}:=\{(\beta,\varphi_{1})\in\Omega^{0,1}(N_{+})\oplus\Omega^{1,0}(L\oplus N_{+})|D^{\prime\prime}(\beta,\varphi_{1})+[\beta,\varphi_{1}]=0,D^{\prime}(\beta,\varphi_{1})=0\}.

(2.30)

Then the following proposition (Corollary 4.3 in
[
CW19
]
) gives a parametrization of the stable manifold
W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})
which generalizes the familiar one for the Hitchin section, the stable manifold of the uniformization point
(\mathcal{E}_{n},\varphi_{n})
:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in V_{\alpha}
be a stable VHS. Then the map

p_{H}:\mathcal{S}^{+}_{(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0})}\rightarrow W^{0}_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}_{0},\varphi_{0}),\hskip 14.22636pt(\beta,\varphi_{1})\mapsto\left[(\overline{\partial}_{0}+\beta,\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1})\right]

(2.31)

is a biholomorphism.

2.7
WKB analysis

The WKB method can be used to study the asymptotics of flat sections for a family of flat connections

\nabla_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi+D+\varepsilon\psi

(2.32)

on a holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a Riemann surface
C
which is not necessarily compact. These families may arise as the real twistor lines associated to a Higgs bundle via Non-abelian Hodge theory, or as the family defining the conformal limit. In either case,
\varphi
is a Higgs bundle,
D
is a connection, and
\varepsilon\psi
contains the terms that grow in positive powers with
\varepsilon
. We will be interested in the case where the leading term
\varphi
is nonzero and where
\psi
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Let
\gamma:I=[0,1]\rightarrow C
be a parametrized, closed loop in
C
, i.e.
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)=p
. After trivializing
\gamma^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
, flatness of a section
s
of
\gamma^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
under the pullback connection
\gamma^{\ast}\nabla_{\varepsilon}
is expressed by the equation

\left(dt\otimes\frac{d}{dt}+\varepsilon^{-1}\gamma^{\ast}\varphi+A+\varepsilon\gamma^{\ast}\psi\right)s(t)=0,

(2.33)

where
t
is the coordinate on
I
and
A
is a
1
-form representing the connection
\gamma^{\ast}D
.

Let us focus on the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
so that
\gamma^{\ast}\varphi
has two eigenvalues
\pm\mu(t)dt
. Everything that follows will depend only on the homology class of
\gamma
and hence we may assume that
\gamma^{\ast}\varphi
is zero-free. Moreover, we will be interested in the case that
\gamma
is a
WKB curve
, i.e. that

\operatorname{Re}(\mu(t))>0

(2.34)

for all
t
. Define the
period along
\gamma
to be

Z_{\gamma}:=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(t)dt,

(2.35)

then for a WKB curve we clearly have
\operatorname{Re}(Z_{\gamma})>0
. Since
\pm\mu(t)
are distinct, we can choose a splitting
\gamma^{\ast}\mathcal{E}=L_{-}\oplus L_{+}
that diagonalizes
\gamma^{\ast}(\varphi)
, i.e.
\gamma^{\ast}(\varphi)|_{L_{\pm}}=\pm(\mu)id_{L_{\pm}}
. Let us also write the connection 1-form
A
with respect to this decomposition as

A=A_{+}+A_{-}+A_{o},

(2.36)

where
A_{\pm}
are
1
-forms representing flat connections on
L_{\pm}
, and
A_{o}
represents the off-diagonal part of
A
, i.e. it is a section of
\left(\operatorname{Hom}(L_{+},L_{-})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(L_{-},L_{+})\right)\otimes T^{\ast}I
. The WKB method gives the following

Proposition 2.5
.

For a WKB curve
\gamma
, the holonomy of
\nabla_{\varepsilon}
around
\gamma
grows exponentially in
\varepsilon^{-1}
. More precisely, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\big(\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla_{\varepsilon})\cdot\exp(-\varepsilon^{-1}Z_{\gamma})\big)=\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(A_{+})\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},

(2.37)

where the limit is taken over
\varepsilon>0
.

Here we have implicitly fixed an identification of the fibers over the endpoints of the interval.

Remark 2.7
.

The statement of Proposition
2.5
goes back to
[
GMN13
]
, and the proof strategy that was outlined there has recently been formalized by Mochizuki
[
BDHH21
]
.

3
Nilpotent WKB analysis

3.1
A secondary Higgs field

We shall be interested in the small
\varepsilon
asymptotics of a family of flat connections

D_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi+D+\varepsilon\psi

(3.1)

where the leading term
\varphi
is a nilpotent Higgs field on
E
,
D
is a connection on
E
, and
\varphi
and
\psi
are
\operatorname{End}(E)
-valued
1
-forms of type
(1,0)
resp.
(0,1)
. Examples of these families include the real twistor lines
\nabla_{\zeta}
(
2.5
) and the families arising from the conformal limit
\mathcal{CL}_{\hbar}
(
2.19
). We will be using the parameter
\varepsilon
for a general family and observe that its flatness implies a set of Hitchin-like equations:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi+D+\varepsilon\psi
be a family of connections. Then flatness for all
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is equivalent to the following set of equations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[\varphi\wedge\varphi\right]=\left[\psi\wedge\psi\right]&=0,\\
D\varphi=D\psi&=0,\\
F_{D}+\left[\varphi\wedge\psi\right]&=0.\end{split}

(3.2)

Proof.

The proof is completely analogous to the one that relates Hitchin’s equations to the flatness of the real twistor line. One readily computes

F_{\nabla_{\varepsilon}}=\varepsilon^{-2}\left[\varphi\wedge\varphi\right]+\varepsilon^{-1}D\varphi+F_{D}+\left[\varphi\wedge\psi\right]+\varepsilon D\psi+\varepsilon^{2}\left[\psi\wedge\psi\right],

(3.3)

and its vanishing for all choices of
\varepsilon
is equivalent to (
3.2
).

Notice that in particular
\overline{\partial}_{E}\varphi=0
where
\overline{\partial}_{E}
is the
(0,1)
-part of
D
and thereby a holomorphic structure on
E
. We will be concerned with the scenario where
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
defines a stable, non-zero, nilpotent Higgs bundle, and will restrict our attention to the case of
G={\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
. By nilpotency,
\varphi
defines a holomorphic line subbundle
\mathcal{L}_{1}:=\ker\varphi
of
\mathcal{E}
and we can choose a
C^{\infty}
-complement
L_{2}
. There is a canonical choice for
L_{2}
which we will make by taking it to be the orthogonal complement with respect to the harmonic metric of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Since we work in a smooth setting, we will use
L_{1}
to denote the smooth bundle underlying
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. This splitting induces a decomposition of
D
of the form

D=A_{1}+D^{\prime}+A_{-1},

(3.4)

where
A_{1}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Hom}(L_{2},L_{1})\otimes\Omega^{1})
,
A_{-1}\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Hom}(L_{1},L_{2})\otimes\Omega^{1})
, and
D^{\prime}=D_{1}\oplus D_{2}
is the direct sum of connections on
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
. For a fixed
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
we can pick a square-root
\varepsilon^{1/2}
which defines an automorphism on
L_{1}
(resp.
L_{2}
) by multiplication by
\varepsilon^{1/2}
(resp.
\varepsilon^{-1/2}
) and hence an automorphism of
E
of the form

g(\varepsilon)=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{1/2}&0\\
0&\varepsilon^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.5)

Notice that the ambiguity in picking a square-root contributes an overall sign, which is central in
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, so the gauge transformation is well-defined. Conjugating by this (constant on
C
) transformation one obtains a new family

D^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}:=D_{\varepsilon^{2}}.g(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-1}(A_{-1}+\varphi)+D^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

(3.6)

Observe that we have rescaled the original family to be
D_{\varepsilon^{2}}
because
D_{\varepsilon}.g(\varepsilon{1/2})
is not single-valued in
\varepsilon
.

Remark 3.1
.

Notice that the connection
D^{\prime}
is diagonal, in particular its
(0,1)
-part
\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}
is. Thus, the underlying holomorphic vector bundle is a direct sum of line bundles
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}
, the associated graded to the filtered bundle defined by the nilpotent Higgs field
\varphi
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Phi=A_{-1}+\varphi
. The pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a stable Higgs bundle.

Proof.

Flatness of the family
D^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
implies a set of equations similar to (
3.2
), in particular

\left[\Phi\wedge\Phi\right]=2\left[A_{-1}\wedge\varphi\right]=0.

(3.7)

\varphi
is strictly upper-triangular with respect to the splitting we chose, while
A_{-1}
is strictly lower-triangular, so their commutator is a priori diagonal and non-zero. Since
\varphi
is of type
(1,0)
and non-zero, (
3.7
) implies that
A_{-1}^{(0,1)}
, the
(0,1)
-part of
A_{-1}
, must vanish. Moreover, since
D^{\prime}\Phi=0
and
\Phi
is of pure type
(1,0)
, this implies that
\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}\Phi=0
.

Lastly we need to show stability, for which we can adapt a proof from
[
BHR19
]
. Any holomorphic line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
induces a holomorphic line bundle homomorphism

\mathcal{L}\mapsto\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/\mathcal{L}_{2}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{1}

(3.8)

which is identically zero if and only if
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{2}
. Since
\varphi\neq 0
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is not an invariant subbundle, and hence for any invariant subbundle
\mathcal{L}
one obtains
\deg\mathcal{L}<\deg\mathcal{L}_{1}
, which together with
\deg\mathcal{L}_{1}<0
by stability of
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
completes the proof.

Remark 3.2
.

One important invariant of an
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle
\Phi
is the associated quadratic differential
q_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi^{2})\in H^{0}(C,K_{C}^{2})
. In the case at hand this has a very geometric meaning: Let
\partial_{E}
denote the
(1,0)
-part of
D
, and consider the composite map

Q_{2}:=\varphi\circ\partial_{E}:\mathcal{L}_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{E}}\mathcal{E}\otimes K_{C}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes K_{C}^{2}.

(3.9)

Lemma 3.3
.

Q_{2}
acts by multiplication by a holomorphic quadratic differential
q_{2}\in H^{0}(C,K_{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the previous proposition but it is enlightening to check it by hand. We need to prove two things: Firstly, that
Q_{2}
is linear over functions
f\in\mathcal{O}(C)
. Secondly, that
Q_{2}
is indeed holomorphic.

To see that
Q_{2}
is linear over functions, let
f\in\mathcal{O}(C)
and
s\in\Gamma(C,\mathcal{L})
, then

Q_{2}(f\cdot s)=\varphi(\partial f\cdot s+f\cdot\partial_{E}s)=\partial f\cdot\varphi(s)+f\cdot\varphi(\partial_{E}s)=f\cdot Q_{2}(s),

(3.10)

where we used
\varphi(s)=0
in the last step.

Next we check holomorphicity.
\varphi
is holomorphic by (

3.4

Let us take a closer look at the previous construction for the case that the family of flat connections is the twistor family

(4.9)

while
\varphi.g_{n}=\zeta\varphi
and
\varphi^{\dagger}.g_{n}=\zeta^{-1}\varphi^{\dagger}
. It therefore transforms the family as

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}:=\nabla_{\zeta^{n}}.g_{n}(\zeta)=\zeta^{1-n}(\varphi+A_{1-n})+\zeta^{2-n}A_{2-n}+\dots+\nabla^{\prime}+\zeta A_{1}+\dots+\zeta^{n-1}(A_{n-1}+\varphi^{\dagger}),

(4.10)

but the leading term is only regular if
A_{1-n}\neq 0
which is not necessarily true. We let
m\leqslant n
be the largest integer for which
A_{1-m}\neq 0
, and remark that
m>1
if and only if
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is not a fixed point which follows directly from Proposition
2.2
and unitarity of the Chern connection. We will assume
m>1
from now on and define

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}:=\nabla_{\zeta^{m}}.g_{n}(\zeta)=\zeta^{1-m}(\varphi+A_{1-m})+\zeta^{2-m}A_{2-m}+\dots+\nabla^{\prime}+\dots\zeta^{m-1}(A_{m-1}+\varphi^{\dagger})+\dots.

(4.11)

As in the rank
2
case, the constant term is diagonal and therefore its
(0,1)
-part
\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}
defines a holomorphic structure on
E
which turns the line bundles
E_{i}
into holomorphic line subbundles. Let
\Phi:=\varphi+A_{1-m}
and
\zeta_{m}=\exp(2\pi i/m)
, then

\Phi.g_{n}(\zeta_{m})=\zeta_{m}\varphi+\zeta_{m}^{1-m}A_{1-m}=\zeta_{m}\Phi,

(4.12)

i.e.
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
is fixed under a cyclic subgroup of order
m
. These bundles have been studied previously (e.g.
[
Sim09
,
Col16
]
) and are called
m
-cyclic Higgs bundles:

Definition 4.1
.

An
m
-cyclic
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle is an
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
of the special form

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{k},\hskip 14.22636pt\varphi=\left(\varphi_{i,j}\right)_{i,j=1}^{k},

(4.13)

such that
\mathcal{E}_{i}
are holomorphic vector bundles, and
\varphi_{i,j}\in H^{0}(C,\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})\otimes K_{C})
such that
\varphi_{i,i+1}
(
i=1,..,k-1
) are required to be nonzero,
\varphi_{m+i-1,i}
(
i=1,..,k+1-m
) are allowed to be nonzero, and
\varphi_{i,j}=0
otherwise.

These fixed points under the subgroup
\mu_{m}:=\langle\zeta_{m}\rangle\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
are a generalization of variations of Hodge structure, in the sense that the underlying bundle still carries a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, while the Higgs field is not necessarily strictly monotonic with respect to the grading but is allowed to form cycles of length
m
. A direct consequence of this structure is that the image of
\Phi
under the Hitchin map is rather special: Since the only cycles are of length
m
(or multiples thereof), the only nonzero holomorphic differentials associated to
\Phi
(with respect to a homogeneous basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{sl}(n)]^{{\mathrm{SL}}(n)}
such as
p_{k}(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X^{k})
) are the
m
-,
2m
-,
\dots
,
\lfloor n/m\rfloor m
-differentials.

Proposition 4.2
.

The pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a stable Higgs bundle. If
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is not a
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-fixed point, then
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
is not nilpotent.

Proof.

We need to check holomorphicity and stability. The former follows from the flatness of the family (
4.11
). Indeed, the curvature
F_{\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}}
can be decomposed by orders of
\zeta
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}}=&\zeta^{2-2m}\left[\Phi\wedge\Phi\right]+\zeta^{3-2m}\left[\Phi\wedge A_{2-m}\right]+\zeta^{4-2m}\left([\Phi\wedge A_{3-m}]+[A_{2-m}\wedge A_{2-m}]\right)+\dots+\\
&+\zeta^{1-m}\left(\nabla^{\prime}\Phi+\sum_{k=1}^{m-2}\left[A_{-k}\wedge A_{k+1-m}\right]\right)+\dots.\end{split}

(4.14)

The proof proceeds by iteratively analyzing the equations in orders of
\zeta
. The first term implies
0=[\Phi\wedge\Phi]=2[\varphi\wedge A_{1-m}]
and hence that
A_{1-m}
is of type
(1,0)
as we will show now. Using (
4.9
) one computes

0=\left([\varphi\wedge A_{1-m}]\right)_{i,j}=\Big(\varphi_{i}\wedge A_{i+1,i+2-m}+\varphi_{i+1-m,i+2-m}\wedge A_{i,i+1-m}\Big)\delta_{i,j+2-m},

(4.15)

where it is understood that any term vanishes which has a subscript that is not in
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Now we iterate in
i
: When
i<m
,
i+1-m\leqslant 0
and only the first term contributes, showing that
A_{2,3-m},\dots,A_{m,1}
are of type
(1,0)
. When
i=m
the second term is of type
(2,0)
and vanishes for degree reasons, so the equation implies that
A_{m+1,2}
is of type
(1,0)
. Continuing this way shows that all of the components of
A_{1-m}
are of type
(1,0)
, and then so is
\Phi
.

Vanishing of the next term in the
\zeta
-expansion similarly implies that
A_{2-m}
is of type
(1,0)
, and continuing in this fashion proves that
A_{-k}
is of type
(1,0)
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
. Geometrically, the vanishing of the
(0,1)
-pieces of
A_{-k}
for positive
k
reflects the fact that
\ker(\varphi^{j})
are holomorphic subbundles of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
. It follows that the bracket terms in the
\zeta^{1-m}
-constituent of
F_{\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}}
are of type
(2,0)
and vanish for degree reasons, so
\nabla^{\prime}\Phi=0
and the
(1,1)
piece demands that
\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}\Phi=0
.

For stability, we observe the following: If the underlying vector bundle of a Higgs bundle is the direct sum of holomorphic line bundles
\mathcal{E}_{i}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}<0
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and if the entries of the Higgs field in
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{E}_{i+1},\mathcal{E}_{i})\otimes K_{C}
(
i=1,\dots,n-1)
are nonzero, then the Higgs bundle is stable. Indeed, stability is an open condition that is invariant under the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action, and the above shows that the
\zeta\to 0
limit of
\zeta\cdot(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
is stable. The requirements are fulfilled for us because the original Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is stable, and
\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{E}_{i}=\ker(\varphi^{k})
is a
\varphi
-invariant subbundle, hence of negative degree.

Lastly, the fact that
\Phi
is not nilpotent follows directly from the construction: Since
A_{1-m}\neq 0
, there must be some nonzero entry
A_{i+m-1,i}
and the composition

\mathcal{V}_{i}\xrightarrow{A_{i+m-1,i}}\mathcal{V}_{i+m-1}\otimes K_{C}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{m-1}}\mathcal{V}_{i}\otimes K_{C}^{m}

(4.16)

is a nonzero, holomorphic morphism
M
of line bundles. In particular,
M^{k}\neq 0
is the
(i,i)
entry of
\Phi^{m\cdot k}
for all
k
, so
\Phi
is not nilpotent.

As in the rank two case, the section
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
is not real and consequently no real twistor line (notice that
\nabla^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}
is not single-valued and hence in particular not holomorphic in
\zeta
):

Proposition 4.3
.

The section
\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}
is not a real section, i.e. it is not gauge-equivalent to
\overline{\nabla^{\prime}}_{-\overline{\zeta}^{-1}}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}=\zeta^{1-m}(\varphi+A_{1-m})+\dots+\zeta^{m-1}(\varphi^{\dagger}+A_{m-1})

(4.17)

and by unitarity of the Chern connection we have
A_{m-1}=-A_{1-m}^{\dagger}
. A quick computation shows that

\overline{\nabla^{\prime}}_{-\overline{\zeta}^{-1}}=(-1)^{m-1}\zeta^{m-1}(\overline{\varphi}+\overline{A_{1-m}})+\dots+(-1)^{m-1}\zeta^{1-m}\left(\overline{\varphi}^{\dagger}-\overline{A_{1-m}}^{\dagger}\right),

(4.18)

so reality would imply in particular that
(\varphi+A_{1-m})
is gauge equivalent to

(-1)^{m}\left(\overline{\varphi}^{\dagger}-\overline{A_{1-m}}^{\dagger}\right)=(-1)^{m}(\varphi^{T}-A_{1-m}^{T}).

(4.19)

The easiest invariant at our disposal is the
m
-differential associated to the invariant polynomial
\operatorname{Tr}(X^{m})
, which is readily computed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\left[(-1)^{m-1}(\varphi^{T}-A_{1-m}^{T})\right]^{m}\right)&=(-1)^{m(m-1)}m\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi^{m-1}\circ(-A_{1-m}))\\
&=-m\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi^{m-1}\circ A_{1-m})=-\operatorname{Tr}\left((\varphi+A_{1-m})^{m}\right),\end{split}

(4.20)

hence the two cannot be gauge equivalent.

Remark 4.2
.

It would be desirable to find a more intrinsic characterisation of the integer
m
that is associated to a nilpotent Higgs bundle in this way. More generally, it would be interesting to know in which connected components of
N-(\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}})^{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}
, the complement of the Variations of Hodge structure inside the nilpotent cone, the Higgs bundles that attain the theoretical maximum for the integer
m
form a dense open subset. Note that for nilpotent Higgs bundles of type
(1,\dots,1)
that maximum is
n
, while for other types it is a strictly smaller integer as we will explain in the next section. It is relatively straightforward to see that a maximum is achieved on an open subset but it is less clear that this number coincides with the theoretical maximum.

Lastly, let us comment on the WKB theory for these families. Note that because
\Phi
is a
\mu_{m}
-fixed point, the leading term of the family

\nabla^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}:=\nabla_{\zeta}.g_{n}(\zeta)=\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\Phi+\zeta^{(2-m)/m}A_{2-m}+\dots=\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta^{1/m}}

(4.21)

is single-valued in
\zeta
up to equivalence, though the subleading terms may be multi-valued. Since (at least naively) the asymptotic behaviour of holonomies of families of flat connections is controlled by their leading constituent, there is hope in proving a WKB result analogous to that in
3.7
. However, the existence of a WKB curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to C
is even more restrictive in this case: For a fixed
\mathrm{Arg}(\zeta)
and a fixed
m
-th root
\zeta^{1/m}
, it is required that the eigenvalues
\mu_{i}(t)dt
of
\gamma^{\ast}\Phi
are distinct with well-ordered real parts

\operatorname{Re}(\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\mu_{1}(t))>\operatorname{Re}(\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\mu_{2}(t))>\dots>\operatorname{Re}(\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\mu_{n}(t))

(4.22)

for all
t
. We are unaware of a general existence theorem for WKB curves on a Riemann surface with a higher rank Higgs bundle, see also Appendix
A
. Nonetheless, if a WKB curve exists then it is expected that the analogous statement to Proposition
2.5
and hence to Theorem
3.7
should hold, namely that asymptotically

\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla_{\zeta})=\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Hol}_{\gamma}(\nabla^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta})\sim\exp(\zeta^{(1-m)/m}Z_{\gamma}),

(4.23)

where
Z_{\gamma}=\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{1}(t)dt\neq 0
. More generally one would expect that for any loop
\gamma
there is a constant
Z_{\gamma}
such that (
4.23
) holds, though this requires the more difficult theory of
higher rank exact WKB analysis
which is to the best of our knowledge currently still under development.

4.2
For general nilpotent Higgs fields

In this subsection we will study the case that the Higgs field is of type
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
where
n_{1}\geqslant\dots\geqslant n_{k}
and
\sum n_{i}=n
. In terms of the Jordan normal form this means that there are
n_{k}
blocks of size at least
k
. The general idea is to apply the procedure from the previous subsection “blockwise” and for this we start by describing a convenient frame for our computation.

As before,
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
defines holomorphic subbundles
\mathcal{E}_{j}:=\ker\varphi^{j}
of dimension
n_{1}+\dots+n_{j}
, and we can pick an orthogonal splitting
\mathcal{E}_{j}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{j}
using the associated graded
V_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
. We want to further decompose the
V_{i}
into line bundles for which we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Any hermitian vector bundle
(V,h)
on a smooth, connected 2-manifold admits an orthogonal decomposition into complex line bundles
V=L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{p}
, where
p=\operatorname{rk}V
.

Proof.

First, any vector bundle
V
of rank at least
2
on a surface
C
admits a line subbundle. Indeed, it is enough to construct a nowhere vanishing section
s
of
V
which is possible by first taking any section with discrete zeroes and then perturbing it smoothly to avoid zeroes. The latter is possible because
rk(V)>\dim(C)
.

Given any line subbundle
L_{1}\subset V
, the hermitian metric
h
defines an orthogonal complement
V_{1}
. Next, pick a line subbundle
L_{2}\subset V_{1}
which gives an orthogonal decomposition
V_{1}=L_{2}\oplus V_{2}
etc. Iteration of this process yields the desired orthogonal decomposition
V=L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{p}
.

We can apply the previous Lemma to
V_{k}
to obtain an orthogonal decomposition

V_{k}=L_{k}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k}^{(n_{k})}

(4.24)

into complex line bundles. Next,
k>1
since
\varphi\neq 0
and therefore
\varphi
does not annihilate the line bundles
L_{k}^{(i)}
, so we can inductively define line subbundles
L_{j}^{(i)}
(
i=1,..,n_{k};j=1,..,k-1)
of
V_{k-1}
via

\varphi(L_{j+1}^{(i)})=L_{j}^{(i)}\otimes\Omega^{1}.

(4.25)

So far we described a decomposition of the subbundles corresponding to the largest Jordan blocks, namely those of size
k
. Let
k_{1}<k
be the largest integer such that
n_{k_{1}}>n_{k}
, we use the hermitian metric to define an orthogonal complement of
L_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k_{1}}^{(n_{k})}\subset V_{k_{1}}
which we orthogonally split into line bundles using Lemma
4.4
to obtain

V_{k_{1}}=L_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k_{1}}^{(n_{k})}\oplus L_{k_{1}}^{(n_{k}+1)}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k_{1}}^{(n_{k_{1}})}.

(4.26)

As previously, if
k_{1}>1
then
\varphi
does not annihilate these line bundles and one can inductively define

L_{j}^{(i)}=\varphi(L_{j+1}^{(i)})\otimes\left(\Omega^{1}\right)^{-1},

(4.27)

where
i=n_{k}+1,..,n_{k_{1}}
, and
j=1,..,k_{1}-1
. We can continue in the same vein to orthogonally decompose the entire bundle
E
into line bundles, which proves the following

Proposition 4.5
.

Given a nilpotent Higgs field
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
of type
(n_{1},..,n_{k})
and a hermitian metric
h
, there is an orthogonal decomposition into complex line bundles

E=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\dots,k,\\
i_{j}=1,\dots,n_{j}\end{subarray}}L_{j}^{(i_{j})}

(4.28)

such that
\varphi(L_{1}^{(i)})=0
and for
j\neq 1
,

\varphi(L_{j}^{(i)})\subset L_{j-1}^{(i)}\otimes\Omega^{1}.

(4.29)

Moreover, the vector bundles

\mathcal{E}_{r}=\ker(\varphi^{r})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,..,r,\\
i_{j}=1,..,n_{j}\end{subarray}}L_{j}^{(i_{j})}

(4.30)

are holomorphic subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
for
r=1,..,k
.

The previous proposition shows that there is a global (over
C
) frame in which
\varphi
exhibits its Jordan canonical form. To be explicit, let
(k_{1},\dots,k_{l})
be the partition of
n
that is transpose to
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
and let

W^{(i)}:=\oplus_{j=1}^{k_{i}}L^{(i)}_{j}.

(4.31)

Then
\varphi
restricts to a nilpotent morphism
\varphi^{(i)}:W^{(i)}\to W^{(i)}\otimes\Omega^{1}
that is of type
(1,1,\dots,1)
. Notice that unless
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
is a fixed point, the
W^{(i)}
are not subbundles in the holomorphic sense. We can copy the strategy from Section
4.1
to each block
(W^{(i)},\varphi^{(i)})
to construct a gauge transformation
g(\zeta)
of
\nabla_{\zeta}
. Denote by
m_{i}\in\{2,\dots,k_{i}\}
the integer
m
in the block
i
, and by
A^{(i)}_{1-m_{i}}\neq 0
the part of the Chern connection restricted to
W^{(i)}
that has weight
1-m_{i}
with respect to the grading (
4.31
) of
W^{(i)}
. Then
g(\zeta)
is of the form

g(\zeta)=\operatorname{\sf diag}\left(\zeta^{(1-k_{1})/2m_{1}},\zeta^{(3-k_{1})/2m_{1}},\dots,\zeta^{(k_{1}-1)/2m_{1}},\zeta^{(1-k_{2})/2m_{2}},\dots,\zeta^{(k_{l}-1)/2m_{l}}\right)

(4.32)

and if we let
m:={\rm max}\{m_{i}\}
then the gauge-transformed connection takes the form

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}:=\nabla_{\zeta}.g(\zeta)=\zeta^{(1-m)/m}\Phi+\dots+\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}+\dots,

(4.33)

where

Phi=\sum_{i:m_{i}=m}\varphi^{(i)}+A^{(i)}_{1-m_{i}}

(4.34)

and the pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a stable Higgs bundle that is not nilpotent.

Let us comment a little more on the shape of the Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
.
\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}
is diagonal with respect to the grading (
4.28
).
\Phi
is block-diagonal with square blocks
\Phi^{(i)}
of size
k_{1}<\dots<k_{l}
, where
(k_{1},\dots,k_{l})
is the transpose partition to
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
.
\Phi^{(i)}=\varphi^{(i)}+A^{(i)}_{1-m_{i}}
is the sum of two pieces, where
\varphi^{(i)}
is a nilpotent Higgs field of rank
k_{i}
and type
(1,\dots,1)
and the only non-zero components of

have distance
m_{i}-1
from the diagonal. In particular,
\Phi^{(i)}
is the Higgs field of an
m_{i}
-cyclic Higgs bundle.

This proves the following

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
be a nilpotent Higgs bundle of type
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
,
n_{k}\neq 0
, which is not a
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-fixed point. Then there exists an integer
m\in\{2,\dots,k\}
and a gauge transformation

such that (
4.33
) holds and the pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a stable Higgs bundle that is not nilpotent.

5
The case of parabolic Higgs bundles

In this section we will describe how the constructions described thus far carry over to the setting of parabolic Higgs bundles. After reviewing some basic facts about parabolic Higgs bundles and their moduli spaces, a subject pioneered by Hitchin (
[
Hit87a
]
,
[
Hit87b
]
), we will turn our attention to the “toy model” which is a moduli space of Higgs bundles of the minimal possible real dimension, 4. In this section we restrict our attention to the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
and degree zero bundles.

5.1
Parabolic Higgs bundles

Fix a compact Riemann surface
C
with a divisor
D=p_{1}+\dots+p_{n}
, together with a pair
\{-\rho_{i},\rho_{i}\}
of parabolic weights where
0<\rho_{i}<1/2
for each puncture
p_{i}
. Fix furthermore a complex vector bundle
E\rightarrow C
of rank
2
and degree zero and an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow\det E
which induces a holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{\det E}
on the determinant bundle.

A parabolic
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
- Higgs bundle on
(C,D)
is a triple
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\mathcal{F}_{i},\varphi)
consisting of

•

a holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{E}
on
E
which induces the fixed holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{\det E}
on its determinant line bundle,

•

a complete flag
\mathcal{F}_{i}=F_{i}^{\bullet}=\{0=F_{i}^{0}\subset F_{i}^{1}\subset F_{i}^{2}=E_{p_{i}}\}
at each of the punctures,

•

a holomorphic section
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,\operatorname{End}((E,\overline{\partial}_{E}))\otimes K_{C}(D))
which is traceless and such that
\mathrm{res}\;\varphi(p_{i}):F^{j}_{i}\rightarrow F^{j-1}_{i}\otimes K_{C}(D)_{p_{i}},
i.e. the residue of the Higgs field is strictly upper triangular
1
1

1

Higgs fields with this condition are often called
strongly parabolic
but we will call them parabolic for simplicity as we will not deal with the weakly parabolic case here.
with respect to the filtration.

We will denote by
\mathcal{E}
the holomorphic vector bundle
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
together with the family of flags
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
, so a Higgs bundle is again a pair
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
. Its
parabolic degree
is defined as

\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{E}:=\deg\mathcal{E}+\sum(\rho_{i}-\rho_{i})=0

(5.1)

and so automatically vanishes. For a holomorphic line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}
we let
\alpha_{i}=\rho_{i}
if
\mathcal{L}_{p_{i}}=F^{1}_{i}
, and
\alpha_{i}=-\rho_{i}
otherwise. The parabolic structure of
\mathcal{E}
induces a parabolic structure on
\mathcal{L}
and the parabolic degree of
\mathcal{L}
is

\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{L}=\deg\mathcal{L}+\sum\alpha_{i}.

(5.2)

A line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}
is called
\varphi
-invariant
if
\varphi(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{L}\otimes K_{C}(D)
. We call a Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)

\vec{\rho}
-
stable
(resp.
\vec{\rho}
-
semistable
) if for every
\varphi
-invariant line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
we have

\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{L}=\frac{\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{rank}\mathcal{L}}<(\leqslant)\frac{\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{E}}{\mathrm{rank}\mathcal{E}}=0.

(5.3)

An isomorphism of parabolic Higgs bundles is an isomorphism of holomorphic vector bundles that preserves the flag structure and commutes with the Higgs field. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{H}
of isomorphism classes of
\vec{\rho}
-stable Higgs bundles is a smooth, quasi-projective variety that has been constructed through geometric invariant theory in
[
Yok93
]
. It comes equipped with the usual
Hitchin map
to a vector space of quadratic differentials

\displaystyle\begin{split}h:\mathcal{M}_{H}&\rightarrow\mathcal{B}=H^{0}(C,K_{C}^{2}(D))\\
\varphi&\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi^{2}),\end{split}

(5.4)

a proper map whose generic fiber is a compact complex torus. Note that since
\varphi
has at most simple poles along
D
and since its residue is nilpotent,
\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi^{2})
will also have at most simple poles.

5.2
A secondary Higgs bundle: The parabolic case

We want to describe how parabolic Higgs bundles give rise to families of flat connections through a parabolic version of the Non-Abelian Hodge Correspondence. For this, we must first recall what it means for a hermitian metric and a flat connection to be adapted to the parabolic structure. Locally, away from the punctures, nothing changes compared to the case discussed previously and it will suffice for us to describe the situation in a neighborhood of a puncture, cf.
[
BGP97
]
.

Near the puncture
p
with parabolic structure (
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{p},\pm\rho)
we fix a local coordinate
z
such that
z=0
corresponds to
p
. A hermitian metric
h
is called
adapted to the parabolic structure at p
if there is a local holomorphic decomposition
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}
compatible with the filtration, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{1}|_{p}=\mathcal{F}^{1}_{p}
, such that
h
is locally of the form

h=\begin{pmatrix}|z|^{2\rho}&\\
&|z|^{-2\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.5)

h
is called
adapted to the parabolic structure
, or just
adapted
, if it is adapted to the parabolic structure at every puncture
p
. The associated Chern connection locally takes the form

D_{h}=h^{-1}\partial h=d+\frac{1}{z}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\\
&-\alpha\end{pmatrix}dz.

(5.6)

Passing from a holomorphic frame
(e_{1},e_{2})
to a unitary one via
(\tilde{e}_{1},\tilde{e}_{2})=(|z|^{-\rho}e_{1},|z|^{\rho}e_{2})
this becomes

D_{h}=d+\begin{pmatrix}i\rho&\\
&-i\rho\end{pmatrix}d\theta

(5.7)

for
z=re^{i\theta}
. (Part of) the Non-Abelian Hodge Theorem then states that for every stable parabolic Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
there exists a unique adapted hermitian metric
h
such that

\nabla_{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1}\varphi+D_{h}+\zeta\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}

(5.8)

is a flat connection for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

[
Sim90
]
.

Now let us restrict our attention to the case that
\varphi
is non-zero and nilpotent. This induces a holomorphic filtration
0\subset\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{E}
as before, via
\mathcal{K}=\ker\varphi
. If
\varphi
has a pole at
p_{i}\in D
, then by definition we necessarily have
\mathcal{K}_{p_{i}}=F^{1}_{i}
, in other words the two flags necessarily coincide over the puncture. If on the other hand
\varphi
is regular at a puncture
p_{j}\in D
then there is no compatibility condition between the two flags.

We are now in the position to take a closer look at the effect of the gauge transformation
g(\zeta)=\operatorname{\sf diag}(\zeta^{1/4},\zeta^{-1/4})
. Decomposing
D=D_{h}=A_{1}+D^{\prime}+A_{-1}
as in (
3.4
) yields

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}:=\nabla_{\zeta}.g(\zeta)=\zeta^{-1/2}(A_{-1}+\varphi)+D^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{1/2}).

(5.9)

The discussion from section
3
carries over verbatim as long as we can ensure that the pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},A_{-1}+\varphi)
has the correct behaviour near the punctures to define a parabolic Higgs field. But (
5.6
) implies that
A_{-1}
has no poles, so
\Phi=A_{-1}+\varphi
has the same residues as
\varphi
. This proves the following

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{H}}
be a
\vec{\rho}
-stable nilpotent parabolic
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle that is not a fixed point of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-action. Then the real twistor line

\nabla_{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1}\varphi+\overline{\partial}_{E}+\partial_{E}^{h}+\zeta\varphi^{\dagger_{h}}

(5.10)

is gauge-equivalent for
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
to the family

\nabla^{\prime}_{\zeta}=\zeta^{-1/2}\Phi+\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E}+\partial^{\prime}_{E}+\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{1/2}),

(5.11)

and furthermore the pair
(\overline{\partial}^{\prime}_{E},\Phi)
defines a
\vec{\rho}
-stable parabolic Higgs bundle that is not nilpotent.

6
The toy model

In this section we study the so-called
toy model
, the moduli space of parabolic
{\mathrm{SL}}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundles over a four-punctured sphere, which has the minimal real dimension possible, namely four. We are particularly interested in the nilpotent cone, i.e. the fiber
h^{-1}(0)
of the Hitchin fibration. The non-singular part has been described in great detail, see e.g.
[
FMSW20
]
. We have not found the details below spelled out in detail in the literature and found them useful as a testing ground for the project carried out here.

Let
C={\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
and
D=p_{1}+\dots+p_{4}
for
p_{1}=0
,
p_{2}=1
,
p_{3}=\infty
and
p_{4}=p
, a configuration that can always be achieved through an appropriate Möbius transformation. We start with the trivial vector bundle
E=\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}^{2}\to C
, which endowed with a complex structure
\overline{\partial}_{E}
will be isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(-n)\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)
for some non-negative integer
n
. Notice that in our conventions of
|\rho_{i}|<1/2
we find for a line subbundle
\mathcal{L}
of degree
k
that

k-2<\mathrm{pdeg}\;\mathcal{L}<k+2

(6.1)

and hence any
\varphi
-invariant line subbundle of degree at least 2 would automatically destabilize a Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
by (
5.3
). A complete flag
\mathcal{F}_{i}
at each puncture is given by the choice of a line
L_{i}
. We will characterize nilpotent parabolic Higgs bundles in 2 cases, depending on whether the Higgs field vanishes or not.

Case 1:

\varphi=0
.

In this case any subbundle is
\varphi
-invariant and the stability condition reduces to that of parabolic vector bundles, hence we have the two subcases
n=0
and
n=1
.

If
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}(0)\oplus\mathcal{O}(0)
we can trivialize the bundle. Let us first note that if all 4 flags
L_{i}
were to coincide, the degree
0
line bundle passing through them would have positive parabolic degree
\sum\rho_{i}
and hence destabilize
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
. If three flags were to coincide (say
L_{i}=L_{j}=L_{k}\neq L_{l}
), the two degree zero line bundles passing through either line would have parabolic degree
\pm(\rho_{i}+\rho_{j}+\rho_{k}-\rho_{l})
and hence one would not be strictly negative. Similarly, one can rule out that there are two pairs of lines that agree. In summary, we can find a basis for
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}
in which the lines take the form

L_{1}={\mathbb{C}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},\;L_{2}={\mathbb{C}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\;L_{3}={\mathbb{C}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\;L_{4}={\mathbb{C}}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
1\end{pmatrix},

(6.2)

for some
w\in{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
by an appropriate coordinate transformation (and, if necessary, relabelling of the punctures
p_{i}
).

Next, note that a holomorphic line subbundle must have non-positive degree and a line subbundle of degree
\leqslant-2
automatically has negative parabolic degree. For the degree
-1
and degree
0
line subbundles, we must take a closer look at the flag structure as well as the parabolic weights. If
w\notin\{0,1,\infty\}
, a degree zero line subbundle can hit at most one of the flags and will not destabilize as long as

\rho_{\sigma(1)}<\rho_{\sigma(2)}+\rho_{\sigma(3)}+\rho_{\sigma(4)}

(6.3)

for any permutation
\sigma\in S_{4}
. Similarly, if
w\in\{0,1,\infty\}
then there is a unique degree zero line subbundle which meets two of the flags, and for it to not destabilize
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
requires
\rho_{1}+\rho_{4}<\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}
if
w=0
, and analogously for the other two cases. If
w\neq p
then a degree
-1
line subbundle can meet at most 3 flags (the tautological bundle for example meets the flags over
0
,
1
, and
\infty
), so stability requires

1+\rho_{\sigma(1)}>\rho_{\sigma(2)}+\rho_{\sigma(3)}+\rho_{\sigma(4)}

(6.4)

for all permutations
\sigma\in S_{4}
. If on the other hand
w=p
, then the tautological bundle meets all 4 lines and
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
is unstable unless
\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}+\rho_{4}<1
. From now on, we will assume that all of these conditions are fulfilled, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma(1)}<\,

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma(2)}+\rho_{\sigma(3)}+\rho_{\sigma(4)}<1+\rho_{\sigma(1)}\;\forall\sigma\in S_{4},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{\sigma(1)}+\rho_{4}<\rho_{\sigma(2)}+\rho_{\sigma(3)}\;\forall\sigma\in S_{3},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\rho_{3}+\rho_{4}<1,

(6.7)

which has solutions in an open subset of
(0,1/2)^{4}
that includes for example
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\rho_{3}=1/4
,
\rho_{4}=1/8
. As we have just seen, there is a
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
worth of parabolic bundles labelled by
w
.

In the case that
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
, one can proceed in a similar vein. Because of (
6.7
) this bundle is destabilized by a degree
1
holomorphic subbundle and will not appear in the moduli space.

Case 2:

\varphi\neq 0
.

As before,
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-n)\oplus\mathcal{O}(n)
and hence

\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{E})\simeq\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{O}}&{\mathcal{O}}(-2n)\\
{\mathcal{O}}(2n)&{\mathcal{O}}\end{pmatrix}

(6.8)

of which we aim to find a nontrivial section. Let us start by assuming that
\varphi
has non-vanishing residues at all 4 punctures. Since
\varphi
is furthermore nilpotent, we can trivialize the bundle away from a point, say
p_{3}=\infty
, and write it as

\varphi=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}a(z)\\
b(z)\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}-b(z)&a(z)\end{pmatrix}=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}-ab&a^{2}\\
-b^{2}&ab\end{pmatrix}

(6.9)

for some polynomials
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
. The condition that
\varphi
has a pole of first order at
z=\infty
puts some constraints on the order of
a
and
b
, which can be seen by switching to the
w=1/z
chart:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi&=\frac{-dw/w^{2}}{w(1-w)(1-wp)/w^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}-a(w^{-1})b(w^{-1})&w^{2n}a(w^{-1})^{2}\\
-w^{-2n}b(w^{-1})^{2}&a(w^{-1})b(w^{-1})\end{pmatrix}\\
&=-\frac{-dw}{w(1-w)(1-wp)}\begin{pmatrix}-w^{2}ab&w^{2n+2}a^{2}\\
-w^{-2n+2}b^{2}&w^{2}ab\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(6.10)

from which we conclude
\deg a\leqslant 1+n
,
\deg b\leqslant 1-n
. This leaves the two cases
n=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{E}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}
, and
n=1
, i.e.
\mathcal{E}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(1)
.

Let us start again with the case that
\mathcal{E}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}
. In this case both
a
and
b
are linear, say
a(z)=Az+B
and
b(z)=Cz+D
. If the two were linearly dependent, say
a=\lambda b
for some
\lambda
, then the line
{\mathbb{C}}\begin{pmatrix}1,-\lambda\end{pmatrix}^{T}
would be globally in the kernel, and the degree zero line bundle passing through it would destabilize
(\mathcal{E},\varphi)
. Hence,
a
and
b
are linearly independent, in which case the linear polynomial
a-\lambda b
cannot have more than one root for any choice of
\lambda
. This forces the 4 flags to be mutually distinct, so that we can use our gauge freedom to bring them into the same form as in (
6.2
). From there, it is easy to see that
a(z)=1,b(z)=z,w=p
and consequently

\varphi_{p}=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}z&-z^{2}\\
1&-z\end{pmatrix}

(6.11)

is the only solution (up to an overall scaling by
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
). The underlying vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
with parabolic structure
w=p
is called a
wobbly bundle
: A stable vector bundle that admits a non-zero nilpotent Higgs field.

For the case
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(1)
we proceed similarly:
a
is a quadratic polynomial while
b
has to be constant. Assume that
b\neq 0
, then there is a gauge transformation such that

\varphi=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}-b&a\\
0&1/b\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-ab&a^{2}\\
-b^{2}&ab\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-1/b&a\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}=\frac{dz}{z(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(6.12)

This is the limit
\zeta.({\mathcal{O}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}},\varphi_{p})
as
\zeta\to\infty
of the nilpotent Higgs bundle just discussed. Notice that the underlying parabolic vector bundle becomes unstable (as
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
is a subbundle of positive parabolic degree). Indeed, this Higgs bundle is known as the
uniformization point
: The point where the nilpotent cone and the Hitchin section intersect.

This is the full discussion if none of the residues vanish. Now let us assume that
\varphi
is regular at some puncture, say at
p_{3}=\infty
. In this case, the description (
6.9
) holds but the subsequent discussion shows that
\varphi
has odd order at
\infty
. Another option is that
\mathrm{res}\varphi=0
at two punctures, say
p_{2}=1
and
p_{3}=\infty
. Then
\varphi
takes the form

\varphi=\frac{dz}{z(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}-ab&a^{2}\\
-b^{2}&ab\end{pmatrix}

(6.13)

and regularity at
\infty
implies that
\deg a\leqslant n
and
\deg b\leqslant-n
, hence
n=0
. Thus the kernel is globally constant and there is a unique line bundle passing through it. Stability requires that the flags over the two punctures with vanishing residue of the Higgs field disagree with this line, in accordance with (
6.6
). In case that these two flags disagree with one another, we can bring the collection of flags into the general position of (
6.2
) with parabolic structure
w=0
, such that the Higgs bundle is

\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O},\,\varphi_{0}=\frac{dz}{z(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}=L_{4},L_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix},L_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right).

(6.14)

There is no gauge freedom left, so that there is a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
orbit of these given by rescaling
\varphi_{0}\mapsto\zeta\varphi_{0}
. There is also the case that the flags over
1
and
\infty
agree, in which case we can choose a gauge where that flag is
(1\;0)^{T}
. There is now some gauge freedom left which can undo the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action, so that there is in fact only a single point in the moduli space representing this equivalence class, and this point is given as the limit
\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\zeta.(\mathcal{E}_{0},\varphi_{0})
of the bundle just discussed. Notice that the underlying parabolic vector bundle is unstable: The degree 0 line subbundle given by the first summand destabilizes it.

By analogy, there are two more
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-families of Higgs bundles with nilpotent Higgs field, together with a point in the rescaling limit. Concretely, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O},\,\varphi_{1}=\frac{dz}{(z-1)(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix},L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},L_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}=L_{4},L_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right),

(6.15)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}_{\infty}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O},\,\varphi_{\infty}=\frac{dz}{(z-p)}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},L_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},L_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix},L_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=L_{4}\right).

(6.16)

with parabolic structure
w=1
resp.
w=\infty
.

To conclude our discussion, the nilpotent cone of the toy model has the following structure: There is a
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
worth of stable parabolic vector bundles parametrized by the parabolic structure
w
. For generic
w
, the bundle does not support a non-vanishing nilpotent Higgs field. However, for the special values
w\in D
there is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-family of nilpotent Higgs fields supported on the corresponding parabolic bundle, parametrized by some scaling parameter
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Moreover, the large
\zeta
limit exists and is another nilpotent Higgs bundle, where the underlying parabolic bundle is now unstable. Topologically, it hence has the structure of an affine
D_{4}
-configuration of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
’s (figure
1
).

Figure 1:

The locus of
{\mathbb{C}}
-VHS consists of the central
{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
(where the Higgs field is zero) as well as the four limit points at the tips of the external spheres. We know that to any other Higgs bundle inside of the nilpotent cone there is canonically associated a meromorphic quadratic differential, the space of which is one-dimensional and therefore uniquely determined up to a scalar
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
by

q_{2}=\frac{c\cdot dz^{2}}{z(z-1)(z-p)}

(6.17)

which indeed has a first-order pole at
z=\infty
. One could hope that there was an easy relationship between a
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
-orbit of a nilpotent Higgs field and this constant
c
but making a quantitative statement would require determining the Chern connection (or at least a certain component of it) which means solving Hitchin’s equations - a very difficult undertaking. The qualitative analysis from Proposition
3.6
still holds, meaning that the assignment is not holomorphic because the constant
c
is bounded.

Appendix A
WKB curves via translation surfaces

In this appendix we will discuss the existence of WKB curves on Riemann surfaces (with holomorphic quadratic differentials). For this, we will remind the reader of the interpretation of such surfaces as
half-translation surfaces
and construct a few explicit examples. Afterwards we will discuss the general theory. We do not claim any originality but were unable to find this discussed in the literature.

First, recall our definition of a WKB curve: Start with a connected Riemann surface
C
with an
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
such that
\varphi
is not nilpotent, then
\varphi
has eigenvalues
\pm\mu(z)dz
that are generically nonzero. These define a branched double-covering
\Sigma\subset T^{\ast}C
of
C
known as the
Spectral Curve
that keeps track of the two eigenvalues. Of course,
(\mu(z)dz)^{2}=1/2\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi^{2})
, so the eigenvalues only depend on the quadratic differential
q_{2}
associated to
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
via the Hitchin map, and then so does
\Sigma=\Sigma_{q_{2}}
. Around any point
z_{0}\in C
where
\mu(z_{0})\neq 0,\infty
one can define a local coordinate
w=w(z)
via

w(z):=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\mu(z)dz=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(\varphi(z)^{2})}\hskip 5.69046pt,

(A.1)

and a change in base point corresponds to a constant translation of this local coordinate, while a different choice of square root (i.e. the exchange
\mu\leftrightarrow-\mu
) corresponds to an overall change of sign of
w
.

These local coordinates give
C
the structure of a
half-translation surface
: Roughly speaking, a half-translation surface is a finite or countable collection of polygons in the Euclidean plane whose edges are identified by maps of the form
w\mapsto\pm w+a
called
half-translations
. Given such a half-translation surface, the inherited metric induces a complex structure on the glued surface, and the quadratic differential that is locally defined as
(dw)^{2}
is invariant under half-translations descends to a globally well-defined quadratic differential.

There is one more subtlety that is worth pointing out for our discussion, namely the role of zeroes or poles of the (meromorphic) quadratic differential. In the description above, we allow zeroes of arbitrary order but poles only of order 1. While the neighborhood of a regular point is Euclidean as described above, zeroes or poles correspond to conical singularities on the side of half-translation surfaces: An opening angle larger than
(2+k)\pi
corresponds to a zero of order
k
(
k\geqslant 1
), while an opening angle of
\pi
signifies a simple pole.

A special type of half-translation surface is a translation surface: All edge identifications preserve the orientation and are of the form
w\mapsto w+a
. These surfaces correspond to the case that the quadratic differential is globally the square of an Abelian differential
A\in H^{0}(C,K_{C})
. We give some examples of translation and half-translation surfaces in Figures
3
resp.
4
. Determining the genus of the corresponding surface is an easy exercise using the Euler characteristic: For example, the left surface in Figure
3
contains 2 vertices, 12 edges (7 external and 5 internal) and 6 faces, yielding a surface of Euler characteristic
2-2g=2-12+6=-4
and hence of genus
3
. Equivalently, one can count the order of zeroes and poles and determine this way the degree of the canonical bundle. Of course, the individual rectangles are not required to be of the same size (though sides that are to be identified need to be of the same length), and the complex structure of the resulting surface depends on these choices.

Figure 3:
Two translation surfaces. The labelling of the edges prescribes the gluing and induces an identification of the vertices which we have included for the convenience of the reader. A staircase composed of
2n
rectangles as on the left and one with
2n-1
as on the right both yield a surface of genus
n
.

Now we can come back to our original intention of constructing examples of WKB curves on Riemann surfaces
C
equipped with a quadratic differential. Upon translating the problem to the language of half-translation surfaces, a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow C
becomes a collection of curves
\gamma_{i}:[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\rightarrow P
where
P
is the set of polygons defining the half-translation structure, and
\gamma_{i}(t_{i}),\gamma_{i}(t_{i+1})\in\partial P
such that
\gamma_{i}(t_{i+1})
and
\gamma_{i+1}(t_{i+1})
are identified when gluing the edges of
P
in the prescribed fashion. With this understood, a WKB curve is one for which
\operatorname{Im}\gamma_{i}
is a strictly increasing function on
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
for all
i
. It is now easy to see that the examples we have provided in figures
3
and
4
have WKB curves given by vertical paths connecting two edges labeled by
A_{i}
.

Figure 4:
Two half-translation surfaces: Notice that the vertices
2
and
3
in the left figure (resp.
3
and
4
in the right one) have opening angle
\pi
and correspond to first-order poles of the quadratic differential. A configuration such as the one on the left with
2n+1
rectangles yields a surface of genus
n
, the same is true for a configuration such as the one on the right consisting of
2n
rectangles.

Let
D\subset C
be the set of zeroes and poles of
q_{2}
, and
C^{o}:=C-D
. We are interested in curves
\gamma
that evade the zeroes and poles of
q_{2}
and so we are really interested in homotopy classes of loops
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}(C^{o})
. In particular, this shows that two of examples considered in the previous paragraph are homotopic if and only if they connect the same two edges.

WKB loops have the special feature that their lift to the spectral curve
\Sigma
consists of two disjoint loops. The reason is that
\operatorname{Re}(\mu(z))>0
along a WKB curve, so the two sheets of the covering
\Sigma\to C
cannot mix in a neighborhood of
\gamma
. It is therefore convenient to consider the notion of WKB curves on
\Sigma
which is by itself a translation surface: The Liouville 1-form
\lambda
on
T*C
naturally restricts to an abelian differential on
\Sigma
.

Let us now discuss the general existence of WKB curves on half-translation surfaces where we will restrict our attention to quadratic differentials with at most simple poles. Notice that the foliation induced by
-q_{2}
is equivalent to the one coming from
q_{2}
(the segments are unoriented) but that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/[0,\pi]\simeq\mathbb{RP}^{1}
the horizontal foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}
coming from
e^{i\theta}q_{2}
is transverse to the original
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}
.

Now let
p\in C^{o}
, we want to construct a homologically non-trivial WKB loop that passes through
p
. It is a well-known fact (see e.g.
[
Str84
]
,
[
GMN13
]
) that there are at most countably many angles
\theta
for which the leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}
passing through
p
is non-recurrent so that generically the leaf forms a dense subset on
C
. It is then clear that in a small neighborhood of
p
, a leaf at a generic angle consists of parallel line segments and that one can smoothly connect a different line segment from the one that contains
p
via a small path that remains transverse to
\mathcal{F}
to form a loop. A sketch of this can be found in figure
5
.

Figure 5:
A minor detour connects two strands of a generic leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}
to create a closed loop that is everywhere transverse to the foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

This carries a natural orientation when interpreted via its lift to
\Sigma
. Observe that
\gamma
is necessarily homologically non-trivial as we shall explain now. If
\gamma
were to close up without the detour, one could easily compute its period

Z_{\gamma}=l_{\gamma}\times e^{i\theta}

(A.2)

where
l_{\gamma}
is the length of
\gamma
. Since the period is nonzero and depends linearly on the homology class, this implies that it is homologically nontrivial. If we include the detour then the computation of the period is slightly more cumbersome but only differs marginally from the previous outcome, so in particular remains non-zero. This proves the following

Proposition A.1
.

Let
C
be a compact Riemann surface and
q_{2}
a meromorphic quadratic differential on
C
that is non-zero and has at most simple poles. Then
(C,q_{2})
admits a WKB loop.

Lastly, let us comment on the existence of WKB curves in the case of a non-nilpotent
{\mathrm{SL}}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
-Higgs bundle
(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
. Let
u:=h(\overline{\partial}_{E},\varphi)
denote its projection to the Hitchin base and
\Sigma=\Sigma_{u}
the associated spectral curve. Recall that
\Sigma_{u}\to C
is a branched (n:1)-cover and that
(\Sigma_{u},\lambda)
is a translation surface.

Pick a class
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}(C)
that admits a representative
\gamma:[0,1]\to C
with the following properties:

1.

Its lift to
\Sigma_{u}
is the disjoint union of
n
loops;

2.

It has extension
\tilde{\gamma}
to a tubular neighborhood
U
of
[0,1]\subset{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\tilde{\gamma}:U\to C
is holomorphic.

U
is contractible and so
\tilde{\gamma}^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
is trivializable in a global coordinate
z
. The first condition implies that
\tilde{\gamma}^{\ast}\varphi
is diagonalizable on
U
with distinct eigenvalues
\mu_{i}(z)dz
. A WKB curve is one for which there is an ordering

\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{1})>\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{2})>\dots>\operatorname{Re}(\mu_{n})

(A.3)

for all
z\in U
. Every ordered pair
\{i,j|i>j\}\subset\{1,\dots,,n\}
determines an abelian differential
\lambda_{ij}=(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j})dz
on
U
and
\gamma
is a WKB curve if and only if it is a WKB curve with respect to each of the
\lambda_{ij}
. In other words, there are
\binom{n}{2}
different foliations
\mathcal{F}_{ij}
on
U
and
\gamma
has to be transverse to all of their leaves.

Effectively, the asymptotics considered around (
4.23
should be governed by the largest (by real part) eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
, so even if some of the smaller eigenvalues were to exchange order along
\gamma
it should not effect the asymptotic behavior. Consequently, the condition on
\gamma
being eligible for the WKB method would require it to be transverse to
(n-1)
foliations on
C
.

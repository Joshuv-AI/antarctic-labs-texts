1
Introduction

In this paper we study the existence and behaviour of radially symmetric solutions to the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=g(|x|,v(x))&&\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\
\Delta v&=f(|x|,|\nabla u(x)|)&&\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is either a ball centered at the origin with radius
R>0
or the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and for all
t\in\mathbb{R},R>0
we have

\lim\limits_{r\to R^{-}}g(r,t)<\infty\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\lim\limits_{r\to R^{-}}f(r,t)<\infty.

(2)

We assume that
f,g\in C^{1}([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty))
, are non-negative, and are increasing in both variables. The study of systems such as (
1
) has generally focused on the case where
f,g
are polynomial in both variables, motivating the above conditions. The case where
\Omega
is a ball,
g(|x|,v(x))=v(x)
and
f(|x|,|\nabla u|)=|\nabla u|^{2}
, that is

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=v&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\Delta v&=|\nabla u|^{2}&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

was first studied by Diaz, Lazzo and Schmidt in
[
6
]
. This choice of the functions
f
and
g
appears in the study of the dynamics of viscous, heat-conducting fluids. In
[
6
]
, the authors
obtained that the system (
3
) has one positive solution which blows up at the boundary and the authors also observed that in case of small dimensions
(N\leq 9)
, there exists one
sign-changing solution which also blows up at the boundary. In
[
7
,
8
]
, Diaz, Rakotoson, and Schmidt extended these results to time dependent systems.

In
[
23
]
, Singh considered system (
1
) in the case where
g=v^{p}
and
f=f(|\nabla u|)
, that is

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=v^{p}&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\Delta v&=f(|\nabla u|)&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

Here, the author considered the system (
4
) both in the case of a ball centered at the origin with positive radius and in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and classified all the positive radial solutions of (
4
) together with the behaviour of solutions at the boundary. The results obtained in
[
23
]
have since been extended in
[
5
]
to the following weighted system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=|x|^{a}v^{p}&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\Delta v&=|x|^{b}v^{q}f(|\nabla u|)&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(5)

where the authors again discussed the existence of positive radial solutions and boundary behaviour in case of a ball with positive radius, centered at the origin and in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Boundary blow-up problems have a long history and one could link them back to atleast a century ago. For example, in 1916, Bieberbach
[
3
]
had studied the boundary blow-up solutions for the equation
\Delta u=e^{u}
in a planar domain. Since then, there has been many new techniques which have been devised to tackle such solutions ( see
[
13
,
14
,
22
]
). Semilinear elliptic equations with nonlinear gradient terms have been studied extensively for such boundary blow-up solutions in last few decades ( for instance, see
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
20
]
).

In
[
11
]
, Ghergu, Giacomoni and Singh studied the following more general quasilinear elliptic system with nonlinear gradient terms

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{p}u&=v^{m}|\nabla u|^{\alpha}&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\Delta_{p}v&=v^{\beta}|\nabla u|^{q}&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6)

in which the authors classified all the positive radial solutions in case
\Omega
is a ball and also obtained the behaviour at infinity of such solutions.

We first consider the case when
\Omega=B_{R}
is a ball of radius
R>0
and centered at the origin. We obtained that in this case the system (
1
) has positive radially symmetric solutions
(u,v)
such that
u
or
v
( or both ) blow up around
\partial{\Omega}
if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{g\left(R,D^{-1}\left(C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(\rho,t)}dt\right)^{2}\right)\right)}<\infty,

(7)

as well as a (sometimes equivalent) necessary condition for this behaviour. Next, we study the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=|x|^{a}v^{p}&&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{R},\\
\Delta v&=|x|^{b}h(|\nabla u|)&&\quad\mbox{ in }B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

where
h\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
is an increasing function such that
h(t)>0
for all
t>0
. We obtained that the above system has positive radially symmetric solutions
(u,v)
such that
u
or
v
( or both ) blow up around the boundary of
B_{R}
if and only if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}H(t)dt\Big)^{p/(2p+1)}}<\infty,\quad\mbox{ where }\;H(t)=\int_{0}^{t}h(k)dk.

(8)

Finally, in case when
h(t)=t^{s}
,
s\geq 1
, then we are able to find the exact
rate at which the solution
(u,v)
blows up at the infinity. In order to do that, we have used the dynamical system techniques for cooperative systems with negative divergence to obtain the exact blow up rate at infinity.

The conditions (
7
) and (
8
) are similar to optimal conditions obtained by Keller
[
19
]
and Osserman
[
21
]
in 1950s while studying the existence of a solution to the boundary blow-up problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=f(u)&&\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=\infty&&\quad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(9)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain and
f\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
is a nonnegative increasing function. The authors obtained that (
9
) has
C^{2}(\Omega)
solutions if and only if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}}<\infty\quad\mbox{ where }\;F(s)=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)dt.

(10)

Equation (
10
) has also been seen in various other circumstances as it is related to the maximum principle for nonlinear elliptic inequalities. For example, if
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is nonnegative and satisfies
\Delta u\leq f(u)
in
\Omega
, then, if
u
vanishes at a point in
\Omega
, it must vanish everywhere in
\Omega
.

Main Results

Our first two results provide necessary and sufficient conditions under which positive radially symmetric solutions
(u,v)
to (
1

\partial{\Omega}
, where
\Omega
is a ball of radius
R

11

\displaystyle A_{\rho}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{g(\rho,t)}{\sqrt{t}}dt\qquad\mbox{for all}\ 0<\rho\leq R,

\displaystyle D(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}g^{2}(R,t)dt.

We assume for all

\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}A_{\rho}(s)=\lim\limits_{s\to\infty}D(s)=\infty.

Note that both functions defined above are strictly increasing in the variable
s

C

.

(i)

Suppose

(ii)

then there exists a positive, radial solution
(u,v)
to (
1
) satisfying

\lim\limits_{r\to R}v(r)=\infty.

(iii)

\lim\limits_{|x|\to R}u(x)=\infty

\mbox{u is bounded in}\ B_{R},

(14)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{sds}{g(R,A^{-1}_{r_{0}}\left(C\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(R,t)}dt\right)}<\infty.

Remark
.

Suppose our non-linearity
g(r,t)
satisfies

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{g(r,t)}{t^{p}}=\varphi(r)>0

(15)

for some
p>0
, then conditions (
11
) and (
12
) are equivalent. In this case we find, for all large enough

A_{R}(s)=A(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{g(R,t)}{\sqrt{t}}dt\geq\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{s}\frac{g(R,t)}{\sqrt{t}}dt\geq C\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{s}\frac{t^{p}}{\sqrt{t}}dt\geq Cs^{\frac{2p+1}{2}}.

In other words, we have for large enough
s
that
A^{-1}(Cs^{\frac{2p+1}{2}})\leq s
, which implies

\displaystyle g\left(R,A^{-1}\left(C\left(\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(R,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2p+1}}\right)^{\frac{2p+1}{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq g\left(R,\left(C\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(R,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2p+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(R,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p+1}}.

Now fix
0<\rho<R
. We see from (
15
) that there exists

D(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g^{2}(R,t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{N}g^{2}(R,t)dt+C\int_{N}^{s}t^{2p}dt\leq Cs^{2p+1}.

for all
s>N

we have that
s\leq D^{-1}(Cs^{sp+1})
, which then implies

\displaystyle g\left(R,D^{-1}\left(C\int_{0}^{2s}F(\rho,t)dt\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq g\left(R,D^{-1}\left(C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(\rho,t)}dt\right)^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=g\left(R,D^{-1}\left(\left(C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(\rho,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2p+1}}\right)^{2p+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq g\left(R,C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(\rho,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2p+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{f(\rho,t)}dt\right)^{\frac{2p}{2p+1}},

where the first inequality follows from Lemma 4.1 in Singh 2015. By
(i)
and
(ii)

for some
\rho\leq R
.

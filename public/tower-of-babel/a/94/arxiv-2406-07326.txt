2.2.
Preliminaries from algebraic geometry

Here we recall various basic results from algebraic geometry that will be instrumental for proving our main theorem. We will make use of the notions of dimension, degree and singularity of a variety, as can be found in standard textbooks of Algebraic Geometry, for example, the book of Harris
[
9
]
. We begin with the following definitions. A variety is said to be of
pure dimension
or
equidimensional
if all the irreducible components of the variety have equal dimension. Further, two equidimensional varieties
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
are said to
intersect properly
if
\mathrm{codim}(X\cap Y)=\mathrm{codim}\ X+\mathrm{codim}\ Y.
It turns out that good upper bounds for the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field, depend on the degree and dimension of the variety. Because of this reason, the following proposition from
[
9
]
will be indispensable for us.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
9
, Cor. 18.5]

Let
X
and
Y
be equidimensional varieties that intersect properly in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. Then

\deg(X\cap Y)\leq\deg X\deg Y
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
F,G\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
be nonconstant homogeneous polynomials having no common factors. Then

(a)

V(F)
and
V(G)
intersect properly.

(b)

V(F)\cap V(G)
is equidimensional of dimension
m-2
.

(c)

\deg(V(F)\cap V(G))\leq\deg F\deg G
.

Proof.

Part (a) is well-known in Algebraic Geometry and is generally proved using Krull’s principal ideal theorem. Part (b) is proved using Macaulay’s unmixedness theorem (See
[
15
, Chapter 7, Theorem 26]
). Part (c) follows straightaway from Proposition
2.10
.
∎

We conclude this subsection by recording a very important observation that follows from the two propositions above.

Remark 2.12
.

Suppose

is a Hermitian variety in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})

is any hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}

2.3.
Basic upper bounds

In this subsection, we recall some well-known upper bounds on the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with given degrees and dimensions. We begin with a result by J. -P. Serre
[
12
]
that is undoubtedly one of the fundamental results. This was independently proved by Sørensen
[

]
.

Theorem 2.13

(Serre’s inequality)
.

Let
F\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
be a nonconstant homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\leq q
. Then

|{\mathsf{V}}(F)|\leq dq^{m-1}+p_{m-2},

where
p_{m-2}=1+q+\dots+q^{m-2}
. Moreover,
equality holds if and only if
V(F)
is a union of
d
hyperplanes defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
all containing a common linear subspace of codimension
2
.

In particular, any plane curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of degree
d\leq q
has at most
dq+1
many
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rational points. Furthermore, a plane curve of degree
d
that admits exactly
dq+1
many
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rational points is a union of
d
lines passing through a common point. Among various other known bounds for plane curves defined over finite fields, we would make use of the following result
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
by Aubry and Perret.

Theorem 2.14
.

[
1
, Corollary 2.5]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a plane absolutely irreducible algebraic curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then
|\#C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})-(q+1)|\leq(d-1)(d-2)\sqrt{q}
, where
d=\deg\mathcal{C}
.

For nonsingular curves, the above bound is known as
Hasse-Weil bound
. However, the result by Aubry and Perret applies to a larger family of curves. To solve our problem, it is imperative that we consider bounds on varieties that are not necessarily hypersurfaces. To this end, we have the following result by Lachaud and Rolland.

Proposition 2.15
.

[
11

Let
X
be an equidimensional projective (resp. affine) variety defined over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Further assume that
\dim X=\delta
and
\deg X=d
. Then

|X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dp_{\delta}\ \ \ \ \ \ (\mathrm{resp.}\ \ |X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dq^{\delta}),

where
p_{\delta}=1+q+\dots+q^{\delta}
.

The following result from
[
11
]
on varieties that are irreducible over

, but not absolutely irreducible, will be very important.

Proposition 2.16
.

[
11

Let
X
be a projective variety defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, but not absolutely irreducible. Then
X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\mathrm{Sing}(X)({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, where
\mathrm{Sing}(X)
is the subset of
X
consisting of all the singular points of
X
in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
.

The above result shows, in particular, that if
X
is a projective variety defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, that is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
but not absolutely irreducible, then any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-rational point on
X
is a singular point of
X
. We conclude this section by stating a recent result that will be useful later.

Theorem 2.17
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
be a surface of degree
d
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
P\in Y({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Then one of the following holds:

(a)

Y
contains a plane defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,

(b)

Y
contains a cone over a plane curve defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with center at
P
,

(c)

\#\{\ell\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}\mid\ell\ \mathrm{is\ a\ line\ such\ that}\ P\in\ell\subset Y\}\leq d(d-1)
.

Remark 2.18
.

We explain the application of Theorem
2.17
in case of cubic surfaces. Suppose
Y
is a cubic surface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
that does not contain any plane defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
P\in Y({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. If
Y
contains a cone over a plane curve
Y^{\prime}
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with center at
P
, then
\deg Y^{\prime}=3
. Indeed, if
\deg Y^{\prime}=1
or
2
, then
Y
contains a plane defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which contradicts our assumption. Moreover, it is clear that
Y^{\prime}
must be irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The Theorem above thus implies that such a cubic surface
Y
is either a cone over a plane irreducible cubic curve
Y^{\prime}
with center at
P
, or
Y
may contain at most
6
lines passing through
P
.

3.
Intersection of a singular Hermitian surface and a surface of degree
d

Let
V_{2}^{\prime}
denote a singular Hermitian surface of rank
3
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. Geometrically, the Hermitian variety
V_{2}^{\prime}
is a cone over a plane nonsingular Hermitian curve
V_{1}
with center at a point
P
. Suppose
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
. Then
V(F)
is a surface of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
We note that any plane that does not pass through
P
intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at a non-degenerate Hermitian curve, and
V_{2}^{\prime}
is still a cone over that Hermitian curve with center at
P
. In this section, we are going to give an upper bound on the quantity
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{2}^{\prime}|
that will be instrumental in the proof of our main result. Throughout this section, we will assume that
d\leq q
. Since our main purpose of this article is to work towards a proof of Conjecture
1
, it is enough for us to assume that
d\leq q
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\Pi
is a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
that does not pass through
P
and
V(F)
does not contain
\Pi
, then
|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi|\leq d(q+1)
.

Proof.

As noted above, the curve
V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi
is a non-degenerate Hermitian curve in the plane
\Pi
and, in particular, an irreducible plane curve of degree
q+1
. By hypothesis, the curve
V(F)\cap\Pi
is a plane curve of degree
d
. The assertion now follows from Bézout’s Theorem.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\Pi
is a plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
P\not\in\Pi
and
\Pi\subset V(F)
, then
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C}|\leq(d-1)(q+1)q^{2}
, where
\Pi^{C}={\mathbb{P}}^{3}\setminus\Pi
.

Proof.

Since
\Pi\subset{\mathsf{V}}(F)
, it follows that
{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap\Pi^{C}
is an affine surface of degree at most
d-1
. Furthermore,
V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C}
is also an irreducible affine surface of degree
q+1
contained in
\Pi^{C}
. It follows that
(V(F)\cap\Pi^{C})
and
(V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C})
have no common components, and consequently, their intersection is a complete intersection of degree at most
(d-1)(q+1)
. Consequently,
\dim(V_{2}^{\prime}\cap V(F)\cap\Pi^{C})=1
and
it follows from Proposition
2.15
, that
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C}|\leq(d-1)(q+1)q^{2}
.
∎

We are ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

We have
|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}|\leq d(q+1)q^{2}+1
.

Proof.

Case 1:
V(F)
does not contain any plane
not
passing through
P
.
We may assume that
V(F)
intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational point
Q
other than
P
. Let
\ell
be a line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
only at
Q
. It is evident that
P\not\in\ell
.
Then there exists a unique plane, say
\Pi_{0}
, containing
\ell
that passes through
P
. Note that
\Pi_{0}\cap V_{2}^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}
, where
\ell^{\prime}
is the line joining
P
and
Q
. Indeed, the line
\ell^{\prime}\subseteq\Pi_{0}\cap V_{2}^{\prime}
since
P,Q\in\Pi_{0}\cap V_{2}^{\prime}
.
We write
\mathcal{B}(\ell)=\{\Pi_{0},\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{q^{2}}\}
, where
\mathcal{B}(\ell)
denotes the set of all planes containing
\ell
. It follows that,

(2)

|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi_{0}|\leq q^{2}+1.

Furthermore, the planes
\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{q^{2}}
are planes that do not contain
P
. As a consequence,
\Pi_{j}
intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at a nonsingular Hermitian curve for every
j=1,\dots,q^{2}
. Since
\Pi_{j}\not\subseteq V(F)
, we see that
|V(F)\cap\Pi_{j}|
is a plane curve of degree
d
for all
j=1,\dots,q^{2}
. From Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3)

|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi_{i}|\leq d(q+1)\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,q^{2}.

Note that
V_{2}^{\prime}
intersects
\ell
only at
Q
. Thus,
|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\ell|=1
. We have,

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{q^{2}}\left(|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi_{i}|-|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\ell|\right)+|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\ell|

\displaystyle\leq\left((q^{2}+1)-1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{q^{2}}\left(d(q+1)-1\right)+1

\displaystyle=d(q+1)q^{2}+1.

The inequality above, of course, follows from Equations (
2
) and (
3
).

Case 2:
V(F)
contains a plane
not
passing through
P
.
Suppose
V(F)
contains a plane
\Pi
not passing through
P
. Then
V_{2}^{\prime}
intersects
\Pi
at a nonsingular Hermitian curve
V_{1}
. Since
\Pi\subset{\mathsf{V}}(F)
, we have
{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi=V_{1}
. Note that,

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi|+|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C}|

\displaystyle=|V_{1}|+|{\mathsf{V}}(F)\cap V_{2}^{\prime}\cap\Pi^{C}|

\displaystyle\leq q^{3}+1+(d-1)(q+1)q^{2}

\displaystyle<d(q+1)q^{2}+1.

The inequality above follows from Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is not difficult to produce a surface of degree
d
that intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at exactly
d(q+1)q^{2}+1
many
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points. Take a plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, not passing through
P
. As noted above, the plane
\Pi
intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at a nonsingular Hermitian curve
V_{1}
. Take a plane curve
C
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of degree
d
in
\Pi
that intersects
V_{1}
at exactly
d(q+1)
many
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points. It is now evident that the cone over
C
, which is a surface of degree
d
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with center at
P
intersects
V_{2}^{\prime}
at exactly
d(q+1)q^{2}+1
many
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points. While our argument shows that the bound of the above theorem is strict, it does not classify all the surfaces of degree
d
that attain this bound. This problem could be interesting for further investigation.

4.
General results towards Edoukou’s conjecture

Throughout this section, we will denote by
\mathcal{X}
, a threefold of degree
d
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
contained in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. That is
\mathcal{X}=V(F)
, the set of all points
P\in{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
such that
F(P)=0
, for some homogeneous polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]
with
\deg F=d
. Since the linear sections of Hermitian threefolds are well-understood, we will be assuming that
1<d\leq q
. Also, as before, the non-degenerate Hermitian threefold will be denoted by
V_{3}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
does not contain a generator. Then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq d(q^{5}+1).

In particular, we have
|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Let us consider the incidence set

I=\{(P,\ell):P\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3},\ell\ \text{is a generator},P\in\ell\}.

We will count
|I|
in two ways. First, we see that,

(4)

|I|=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}}\#\{\ell:P\in\ell\}=|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|(q^{3}+1).

The fact that
\#\{\ell:P\in\ell,\ell\ \text{is a generator of }\ V_{3}\}=q^{3}+1
is already noted in Remark
2.5
. On the other hand, using the fact that
V_{3}
contains
(q^{5}+1)(q^{3}+1)
generators, we have

(5)

|I|=\sum_{\ell\subset V_{3}}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap\ell|\leq d(q^{5}+1)(q^{3}+1).

It follows from Equations (
4
) and (
5
) that

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq d(q^{5}+1)
.
∎

It is now natural to proceed to determine an upper bound for
|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|
in the case when
\mathcal{X}
contains a generator of
V_{3}
, but no planes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. However, we do not have a good bound in the general case and as a consequence, the complete solution to Conjecture
1
remains open. We will present some partial results under this hypothesis later in this section and we will study this case for cubic threefolds in the subsequent section. For the time being, let us deal with the case when
\mathcal{X}
contains a plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, but it does not contain a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, but no hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq(d-1)q^{5}+(d-1)q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1.

In particular,
|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Let
\Pi
be a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
contained in
\mathcal{X}
. Let us denote

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi):=\{\Sigma:\Pi\subset\Sigma,\Sigma\ \text{is a hyperplane defined over}\ \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}\}.

Note that for any plane
\Pi\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we have

|V_{3}\cap\Pi|\leq q^{3}+q^{2}+1.

Also, for any
\Sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi)
, using Proposition
2.15
, and the fact that
\Sigma\not\subset\mathcal{X}
, we may conclude that

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Sigma\setminus\Pi)|\leq(d-1)(q+1)q^{2}.

We have,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap\Pi|+\sum_{\Sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi)}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Sigma\setminus\Pi)|

\displaystyle\leq q^{3}+q^{2}+1+(q^{2}+1)(d-1)(q+1)q^{2}

\displaystyle=(d-1)q^{5}+(d-1)q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1.

The last inequality follows trivially since
d\leq q
.
∎

As mentioned above, it is difficult to find good bounds for
|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|
when
\mathcal{X}
contains a generator of
V_{3}
but does not contain a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Some partial results to this end are given below.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
contains no plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If there exists a plane
\Pi_{0}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\Pi_{0}\cap\mathcal{X}
has
d
generators of
V_{3}
, then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq dq^{5}-q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Let
\Pi_{0}\cap V_{3}
contain
d
generators of
V_{3}
. Since a plane containing a generator of
V_{3}
can intersect
V_{3}
at either one line or
q+1
concurrent lines, it follows that
V_{3}\cap\Pi_{0}
is a union of
q+1
lines
\ell_{0},\dots,\ell_{q}
passing through a common point, say
P\in V_{3}
. It is also easy to see that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi_{0})
contains exactly one hyperplane that is tangent to
V_{3}
, namely, the tangent hyperplane to
V_{3}
at
P
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi)
contains a tangent hyperplane
T_{Q}(V_{3})
where
Q\neq P
, then
\Pi_{0}=T_{P}(V_{3})\cap T_{Q}(V_{3})
and hence the lines
\ell_{0},\dots,\ell_{q}
are contained in
T_{Q}(V_{3})
. Consequently, the
q+1
lines meet at the point
Q
, implying
P=Q
, a contradiction. If
\Sigma=T_{P}(V_{3})
, then
\mathcal{X}\cap\Sigma
is a surface of degree
d
in
\Sigma
. It now follows from Theorem
3.3
that

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})|\leq d(q+1)q^{2}+1.

If
\Sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi_{0})
, but
\Sigma\neq T_{P}(V_{3})
, we conclude from Theorem
2.2
(b), that
\Sigma\cap V_{3}
is a non-degenerate Hermitian surface in
\Sigma
. Now Theorem
2.9
yields

|\Sigma\cap\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq d(q^{3}+q^{2}-q)+q+1.

However, the upper bound above is attained if and only if
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{X}
contains
d
planes that are tangent to
V_{3}
. Since, in our case, the threefold
\mathcal{X}
does not contain a plane, a strict bound
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
applies and we have

|\Sigma\cap\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq dq^{3}+(d-1)q^{2}+1.

Finally,
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
does not contain
\Pi_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}\cap\Pi_{0}
contains the union of
d
generators. It follows that
\mathcal{X}\cap\Pi_{0}
is exactly the union of
d
generators of
V_{3}
. Thus

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap\Pi_{0}|=dq^{2}+1.

We may thus conclude that, if
\Sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi_{0})
and
\Sigma\neq T_{P}(V_{3})
, then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Sigma\setminus\Pi_{0})|\leq dq^{3}+(d-1)q^{2}+1-(dq^{2}+1)=dq^{3}-q^{2}.

Following the same argument as in Proposition
4.2
, we see that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(\Pi_{0})\\
\Sigma\neq T_{P}(V_{3})\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Sigma\setminus\Pi_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq d(q+1)q^{2}+1+q^{2}(dq^{3}-q^{2})

\displaystyle=dq^{5}-q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1

The last strict inequality in the assertion follows trivially since
d\leq q
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
contains no plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathcal{X}\cap\Pi
contains at most one generator for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. Then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq(d-1)q^{5}+q^{2}+(d-1)q^{3}+(d-1)q+1<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Suppose that for all plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
,
\mathcal{X}\cap\Pi
does not contain any generator. This in particular implies that
\mathcal{X}
does not contain any generator. Then Proposition
4.1
applies and proves the assertion. Thus we may assume that
\mathcal{X}
contains a generator
\ell
of
V_{3}
.

Claim:
For every
\Pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell)
, we have
|\mathcal{X}\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)|\leq(d-1)q
.

Proof of claim:
Note that, for any
\Pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell)
the intersection

\Pi\cap V_{3}
consists of at most
q+1
generators. Since
\mathcal{X}\cap V_{3}
contains no generators of
V_{3}
other than
\ell
, it follows that
\mathcal{X}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)
and
V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)
are plane curves of degrees
d-1
and at most
q
respectively. Furthermore, the two affine curves have no common components. Using Bézout’s Theorem, we now conclude that

|\mathcal{X}\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)|=|\left(\mathcal{X}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)\right)\cap\left(V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)\right)|\leq(d-1)q.

Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\ell(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|+\sum_{\Pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell)}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell)|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{2}+1)+(q^{4}+q^{2}+1)(d-1)q

\displaystyle=(d-1)q^{5}+q^{2}+(d-1)q^{3}+(d-1)q+1

\displaystyle<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The first inequality follows from the claim, whereas the strict inequality is an immediate consequence of the hypothesis
d\leq q
.
∎

Before proceeding further, we show that the results derived above are enough to prove Edoukou’s bound. To this end, let us first prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\Sigma_{1}
be a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that is not a tangent to
V_{3}
, then for any hyperplane
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we have
|\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}\cap V_{3}|\geq q^{3}+1
.

Proof.

If
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma
, then
|\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}\cap V_{3}|=|\Sigma_{1}\cap V_{3}|=q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1>q^{3}+1
. We may thus assume that
\Sigma\neq\Sigma_{1}
. Thus
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}
is a plane contained in the projective space
\Sigma_{1}
. Since
\Sigma_{1}
is not a tangent to
V_{3}
, we see that
V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{1}
is a non-degenerate Hermitian surface in
\Sigma_{1}
. Note that
\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}
is a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
contained in
\Sigma_{1}
. We know that a planar section of a nondegenerate Hermitian surface is either a plane nonsingular Hermitian curve or a union of
q+1
lines passing through a common point. Thus
|\Sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}\cap V_{3}|\geq q^{3}+1
, proving the assertion.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Edoukou’s result on the intersection of a non-degenerate Hermitian threefold and a quadric threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
.

Corollary 4.6

(Edoukou’s Theorem)
.

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
Q
be a quadric threefold defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. Then

|V_{3}\cap Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\leq 2(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The bound is attained by a quadric
Q
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
Q
is a union of two hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, both not tangents to
V_{3}
, such that the plane
\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}
intersects
V_{3}
at a non-degenerate plane Hermitian curve.

Proof.

We distinguish the proof into two cases:

Case 1:
Suppose
Q
is reducible. Then
Q
must be a union of hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. From Theorem
2.2
, we see that for any hyperplane
\Sigma
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
,

|\Sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|=\begin{cases}q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1\ \ \text{if}\ \Sigma\ \text{is not a tangent to}\ V_{3}\\
q^{5}+q^{2}+1\ \ \text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

If
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
are both tangents to
V_{3}
, then

|V_{3}\cap Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\leq|V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{1}|+|V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{2}|\leq 2(q^{5}+q^{2}+1)<2(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

So we may assume without loss of generality that
\Sigma_{1}
is not a tangent to
V_{3}
. Thus

\displaystyle|V_{3}\cap Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|

\displaystyle=|V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{1}|+|V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{2}|-|V_{3}\cap\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 2(q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1)-(q^{3}+1)

\displaystyle=2(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The inequality above is a consequence of Lemma
4.5
. Note that here equality occurs if and only if both
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are not tangents to
V_{3}
and the plane
\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}
intersects
V_{3}
at a nonsingular plane Hermitian curve.

Case 2:
Suppose
Q
is irreducible. Thus
Q
can not contain a hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
Q
contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, Proposition
4.2
applies and establishes the assertion. We may assume that
Q
contains no plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
Q
contains no generators of
V_{3}
, then the assertion follows from Proposition
4.1
. Suppose
Q
contains a generator of
V_{3}
. If every plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
contains at most one generator of
V_{3}
, then Proposition
4.4
applies. Finally, if a plane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that contains two generators of
V_{3}
exists, then the assertion inequality is obtained from Proposition
4.3
. We note that all the inequalities in this case are strict. This completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section by showing that if
\mathcal{X}
contains a line
\ell
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that is tangent to
V_{3}
, then
|\mathcal{X}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\cap V_{3}|
satisfies the bound in Edoukou’s conjecture. To this end, we have the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
P\in V_{3}
and
\ell_{P}
is a tangent line to
V_{3}
passing through
P
. Then

(a)

there exist
q^{2}+1
planes containing
\ell_{P}
that are contained in
T_{P}(V_{3})
, and

(b)

if
\Pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell_{P})
and
\Pi\not\subset T_{P}(V_{3})
, then
\Pi\cap V_{3}
does not contain any generator of
V_{3}
.

Proof.

To prove part (a), it is enough to show that
\ell_{P}\subset T_{P}(V_{3})
. But the later is true since any line passing through
P
that is not contained in
T_{P}(V_{3})
intersects
V_{3}
at
q+1
points.
Suppose
\Pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell_{P})
and
\Pi
contains a generator, say
\ell
, of
V_{3}
. Now
\ell
and
\ell_{P}
are two distinct lines in
\Pi
and hence
\Pi
is the unique plane containing
\ell
and
\ell_{P}
. On the other hand, both the lines
\ell
and
\ell_{P}
are contained in
T_{P}(V_{3})
. It follows that
\Pi\subset T_{P}(V_{3})
.
This proves part (b).
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
contains a tangent line to
V_{3}
and
\mathcal{X}
does not contain any plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq(d-1)q^{5}+(d-1)q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}
contains a line
\ell_{P}
that is tangent to
V_{3}
at the point
P\in V_{3}
. Write

\mathcal{B}(\ell_{P})=\{\Pi_{0},\dots,\Pi_{q^{2}},\Pi_{q^{2}+1},\dots,\Pi_{q^{4}+q^{2}}\},

where
\Pi_{0},\dots,\Pi_{q^{2}}\subset T_{P}(V_{3})
. Note that

(6)

|\ell_{P}\cap\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|+\sum_{i=0}^{q^{2}}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell_{P})|=|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})|\leq d(q+1)q^{2}+1.

Note that the last inequality follows from Theorem
3.3
. Using Lemma
4.7
, we conclude that, for
j=q^{2}+1,\dots,q^{4}+q^{2}
, the plane
\Pi_{j}
intersects
V_{3}
at a plane non-degenerate Hermitian curve. Also the curve
\mathcal{X}\cap(\Pi_{j}\setminus\ell_{P})
is a plane curve of degree at most
d-1
.
From Bézout’s theorem,

(7)

|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell_{P})|\leq(d-1)(q+1).

Finally, using equations (
6
) and (
7
), we see that,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\ell_{P}\cap\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|+\sum_{j=0}^{q^{4}+q^{2}}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell_{P})|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})|+\sum_{j=q^{2}+1}^{q^{4}+q^{2}}|\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}\cap(\Pi\setminus\ell_{P})|

\displaystyle\leq d(q+1)q^{2}+1+q^{4}(d-1)(q+1)

\displaystyle=(d-1)q^{5}+(d-1)q^{4}+dq^{3}+dq^{2}+1<d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Intersection of cubic threefolds with Hermitian threefold

The main goal of this section is to determine the maximum number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points on the intersection of a cubic threefold and Hermitian threefolds in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. To this end, we will denote by
\mathcal{C}
a cubic threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
while we keep using
V_{3}
for denoting the non-degenerate Hermitian threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. Note that, we have dealt with threefolds that

(a)

contains no generators of
V_{3}
and

(b)

contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, but no hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

in the previous section. Thus, we will need to study the cases
when

(i)

\mathcal{C}
contains generators of
V_{3}
but no planes defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
and

(ii)

\mathcal{C}
contains a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

in this section. For ease of reading, we will divide the section into two subsections.

5.1.
Cubic threefolds containing a hyperplane

In this subsection, we will make use of Edoukou’s result (Proposition
2.8
) to show that if
\mathcal{C}
contains a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then the bound in Edoukou’s conjecture holds in the affirmative.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cubic threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that contains a hyperplane
\Sigma
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq 3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
contains a hyperplane
\Sigma
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. It follows immediately that
\mathcal{C}=\Sigma\cup Q
, where
Q
is a quadric threefold defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Now,

|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|\leq q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1
. We distinguish four cases.

Case 1:
Q
is of Type I.
Then
Q=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
, where
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, neither of them tangent to
V_{3}
. Using Proposition
2.8
we derive that
|Q\cap V_{3}|=2(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. We have,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2})\cap\Sigma\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma)\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1)+2(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1-(q^{3}+1)

\displaystyle=3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The last inequality above follows from Lemma
4.5
.

Case 2:
Q
is of Type II.
We have
Q=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
, where
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, neither of them tangent to
V_{3}
. Using Proposition
2.8
we derive that
|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|=2q^{5}+q^{2}+q^{3}+1
. We have,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2})\cap\Sigma\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma)\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1)+2q^{5}+q^{2}+q^{3}+1-(q^{3}+1)

\displaystyle=3q^{5}+q^{3}+2q^{2}+1<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The last inequality above follows from Lemma
4.5
.

Case 3:
Q
is of Type III.
Here
Q=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
, where
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Furthermore, the hyperplane
\Sigma_{2}
is not a tangent to
V_{3}
. Using Proposition
2.8
we derive that
|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|=2q^{5}+2q^{2}+q^{3}+1
. We have,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2})\cap\Sigma\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|-|(\Sigma_{2}\cap\Sigma)\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1)+2q^{5}+2q^{2}+1-(q^{3}+1)

\displaystyle=3q^{5}+3q^{2}+1<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Case 4:
Q
is not of Type I, Type II or Type III.
In this case, from Proposition
2.8
, we obtain that
|Q\cap V_{3}|\leq 2q^{5}+q^{2}+1
. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\Sigma\cap V_{3}|+|Q\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq q^{5}+q^{3}+q^{2}+1+2q^{5}+q^{2}+1

\displaystyle=3q^{5}+2q^{2}+q^{3}+2<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

For the sake of proving the inequality in the above Proposition, it is not really necessary to distinguish Case 1, Case 2, and Case 3. In each of these three cases, the presence of one nontangent hyperplane in the conic plays a pivotal role in establishing the required upper bound. However, the above distinction shows us that the upper bound could be attained by a cubic threefold
\mathcal{C}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and containing a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
will attain the upper bound if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{3}
are distinct hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

(b)

\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\Sigma_{3}
intersect at a common plane
\Pi
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
such that
\Pi\cap V_{3}
is a plane nonsingular Hermitian curve.

In fact, we will establish at the end of this article that if
q\geq 7
, then a cubic threefold
\mathcal{C}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
attains the upper bound of Edoukou’s conjecture if and only if
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the conditions mentioned above.

5.2.
Cubic threefolds containing no hyperplanes

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be an irreducible plane cubic curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is absolutely irreducible, then

||\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|-(q^{2}+1)|\leq 2q.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is not absolutely irreducible, then
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\leq 1
.

Proof.

(a)

Follows from Theorem
2.14
.

(b)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a plane cubic curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
but reducible in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. From
[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
, we see that if
Z
is an absolutely irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then
\deg(Z)\mid\deg\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Since
\deg\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=3
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a union of three lines passing through a common point, say
P
. Thus Proposition
2.16
implies that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\subseteq\{P\}
and hence the assertion is established.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
q\geq 7
and
\mathcal{C}
be a cubic threefold defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
P\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}
. If
\mathcal{C}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})
is a cone over a plane cubic absolutely irreducible curve
\mathcal{C}^{*}
with center at
P
, then
\mathcal{C}
contains a line that is tangent to
V_{3}
at
P
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{*}
is a plane absolutely irreducible curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, it follows from Lemma
5.3
that
|\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|\geq q^{2}+1-2q
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}^{*}\subset\Pi
, where
\Pi
is a plane, not passing through
P
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
which is contained in
T_{P}(V_{3})
. Now
V_{3}\cap\Pi
is a plane non-degenerate Hermitian curve and hence Bézout’s theorem implies

|(V_{3}\cap\Pi)\cap C^{*}|\leq 3(q+1)
. Since
q\geq 7
, it follows that
|\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})|>|(V_{3}\cap\Pi)\cap C^{*}|
and hence there is a point
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
that is not contained in
V_{3}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a cone over
C^{*}
with center at
P
, the line
\ell
joining
P
and
P^{\prime}
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\ell
is a line defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
passing through
P
, the line
\ell
must be either a tangent line or a generator. Since
P^{\prime}\not\in V_{3}
, it follows that
\ell
can not be a generator and the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
P\in V_{3}
and
\ell
be a generator of
V_{3}
containing
P
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cubic threefold defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the threefold
\mathcal{C}
contains at most two generators of
V_{3}
. If
\ell
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
, then one of the following holds:

(a)

T_{P}(V_{3})\cap\mathcal{C}
contains a cone over a plane curve with center at
P
,

(b)

|T_{P}(V_{3})\cap\mathcal{C}\cap V_{3}|\leq 2q^{3}+6q^{2}-3q-4
.

Proof.

Suppose (a) does not hold. Then, in view of Theorem
2.17
, we see that
T_{P}(V_{3})\cap\mathcal{C}
contains at most six lines passing though
P
. Let

\mathcal{S}:=\{\Pi\in\mathcal{B}_{T_{P}(V_{3})}(\ell):\mathcal{C}\cap\Pi\ \text{contains two generators of}\ V_{3}\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ s=|\mathcal{S}|.

It follows that
s\leq 5
and for any
\Pi\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
|\Pi\cap V_{3}\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|\leq q^{2}+q-1
.
On the other hand, for any
\Pi\in\mathcal{B}_{T_{P}(V_{3})}(\ell)\setminus\mathcal{S}
, using Bézout’s Theorem, we see that
|\Pi\cap V_{3}\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|\leq 2q
. Thus

\displaystyle|T_{P}(V_{3})\cap C\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\ell|+\sum_{\Pi\in\mathcal{S}}|\Pi\cap V_{3}\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|+\sum_{\Pi\in\mathcal{B}_{T_{P}(V_{3})}(\ell)\setminus\mathcal{S}}|\Pi\cap V_{3}\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{2}+1)+s(q^{2}+q-1)+(q^{2}+1-s)(2q)

\displaystyle=2q^{3}+q^{2}+2q+1+s(q^{2}-q-1)

\displaystyle\leq 2q^{3}+q^{2}+2q+1+5(q^{2}-q-1)\ \ (\text{since}\ q^{2}-q-1>0)

\displaystyle=2q^{3}+6q^{2}-3q-4.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
q\geq 7
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cubic threefold defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
that contains a generator of
V_{3}
but contains no plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Moreover, assume that for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the intersection
\mathcal{C}\cap\Pi
contains at most two generators of
V_{3}
. Then

|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
contains a generator
\ell
of
V_{3}
. Now, as discussed in Remark
2.18
, for every
P\in\ell
, either the cubic surface
\mathcal{C}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})
is a cone over a plane cubic curve
\mathcal{C}_{P}^{\prime}
with center at
P
or
\mathcal{C}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})
contains at most
6
lines passing through
P
.

Let
L=\{P\in\ell:\mathcal{C}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})\ \text{is a cone over a plane cubic}\ \mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}\ \text{with center at}\ P\}.

Observe that for each
P\in L
, if
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}
is reducible, then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}
contains a line defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and as a consequence,
\mathcal{C}
contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. We may thus assume that for each
P\in L
, the plane curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}
is irreducible. If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}
is absolutely irreducible, then it follows from Lemma
5.4
, that
\mathcal{C}
contains a line that is tangent to
V_{3}
. Here, Proposition
4.8
applies, and we obtain the assertion. We may thus assume from now that for every
P\in L
, the plane curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{P}
is irreducible but not absolutely irreducible. It now follows from Lemma
5.3
(b), that for each
P\in L
, we have
T_{P}(V_{3})\cap\mathcal{C}\cap V_{3}=\ell.
Consequently, we have the following:

(8)

|V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|=\begin{cases}0\ \ &\text{if}\ P\in L\\
\leq 2q^{3}+5q^{2}-3q-5\ \ &\text{if}\ P\in\ell\setminus L.\end{cases}

The last inequality follows from Lemma
5.5
. Since

{\mathbb{P}}^{4}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{P\in\ell}}T_{P}(V_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle|C(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|

\displaystyle=|\ell|+\sum_{P\in L}|V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|+\sum_{P\in\ell\setminus L}|V_{3}\cap T_{P}(V_{3})\cap(\mathcal{C}\setminus\ell)|

\displaystyle\leq(q^{2}+1)+|L|\cdot 0+(q^{2}+1-|L|)(2q^{3}+5q^{2}-3q-5)

\displaystyle=q^{2}+1+2q^{5}+5q^{4}-q^{3}-3q-5-|L|(2q^{3}+5q^{2}-3q-5)

\displaystyle\leq 2q^{5}+5q^{4}-q^{3}+q^{2}-3q-4

\displaystyle<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

The last inequality is true since
q\geq 7
, as per hypothesis. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{4}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree three and
V_{3}
be a non-degenerate Hermitian threefold in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
. If
q\geq 7
, then

|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq 3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1.

Moreover, this bound is attained by a cubic threefold
V(F)
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
, if and only if
V(F)
is a union of three non-tangent hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, such that each of the hyperplanes contains a common plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\Pi
intersects
V_{3}
at a non-degenerate plane Hermitian curve.

Proof.

Throughout, we will denote by
\mathcal{C}
the threefold given by the vanishing locus of
F
, that is
\mathcal{C}=V(F)
. We distinguish the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

\mathcal{C}
is reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. In this case, the threefold
\mathcal{C}
contains a hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. From Proposition
5.1
we see that
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq 3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. Also it follows from Remark
5.2
that the bound is attained in this case if and only if
V(F)
is a union of three non-tangent hyperplanes
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, such that each of the hyperplanes contains a common plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\Pi
intersects
V_{3}
at a non-degenerate plane Hermitian curve.

Case 2:

\mathcal{C}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
In this case,
\mathcal{C}
does not contain any hyperplane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. If
\mathcal{C}
does not contain any generator of
V_{3}
, then Proposition
4.1
shows that
|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. If
V(F)
contains a plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, then Proposition
4.2
applies and shows that
|\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. So, we may assume that
\mathcal{C}
contains a generator of
V_{3}
but no plane defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. It follows that for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the plane curve
V(F)\cap\Pi
can contain at most three generators of
V_{3}
. If there exists a plane
\Pi_{0}
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
V(F)\cap\Pi_{0}
has exactly three generators of
V_{3}
, then Proposition
4.3
implies that
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. Furthermore, if for any plane
\Pi
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the intersection
V(F)\cap\Pi
contains at most two generators of
V_{3}
, then Proposition
5.6
shows that
|V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1
. Thus, we see that a strict bound always holds in this case. This completes the proof.
∎

Edge Universality of Random Regular Graphs of Growing Degrees
Horng-Tzer Yau

Abstract
We consider the statistics of extreme eigenvalues of random d d -regular graphs, with N 𝔠 ⩽ d ⩽ N 1 / 3 − 𝔠 N^{\mathfrak{c}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{1/3-{\mathfrak{c}}} for arbitrarily small 𝔠 > 0 {\mathfrak{c}}>0 . We prove that in this regime, the fluctuations of extreme eigenvalues are given by the Tracy-Widom distribution. As a consequence, about 69% of d d -regular graphs have all nontrivial eigenvalues bounded in absolute value by 2 ​ d − 1 2\sqrt{d-1} .

Introduction

Expander graphs
are sparse graphs with strong connectivity property, and have numerous applications to the design of robust networks and the theory of error-correct coding
[
33
]
. The expansion property of a regular graph can be measured by its spectral gap.
Ramanujan graphs
are
d
-regular graphs with the largest spectral gap, i.e. all nontrivial eigenvalues are bounded in absolute value by
2\sqrt{d-1}
. They are the best possible expander graphs, at least as far as the spectral gap measure of expansion is concerned.

Ramanujan graphs were explicitly constructed when
d=p+1
with
p
a prime power, by Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak
[
47
]
and independently by Margulis
[
50
]
, using deep tools from number theory. More recently, in the breakthrough work
[
48
,
49
]
, Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava proved that there exist
bipartite Ramanujan graphs
of every degree. It still remains an interesting open problem whether there exist non-bipartite Ramanujan graphs of every degree. However, based on numerical simulation
[
52
]
, it was observed a positive portion of
random
d
-regular graphs
are Ramanujan.

We study the statistics of extreme eigenvalues of random
d
-regular graphs, when the degree
d
grows with the size of the graph. For random
d
-regular graphs on
N
vertices, it has been proven in
[
8
]
for
N^{2/9+{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{1/3-{\mathfrak{c}}}
and in
[
31
]
for
N^{2/3+{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant d\leqslant N/2
, the fluctuations of extreme eigenvalues are given by the
Tracy-Widom distribution
from random matrix theory. In this work we generalize these edge universality results to the sparser regime
N^{\mathfrak{c}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{1/3-{\mathfrak{c}}}
for arbitrarily small
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
. As a consequence, about 69% of
d
-regular graphs on
N
vertices with
d^{3}\ll N\leqslant d^{1/{\mathfrak{c}}}
have all nontrivial eigenvalues bounded in absolute value by
2\sqrt{d-1}
. This gives many Ramanujan graphs.

Universality for the edge statistics of Wigner matrices was first established by the moment method
[
57
,
54
,
39
]
under certain symmetry assumptions on the distribution of the matrix elements. The moment method was further developed in
[
53
,
29
]
and
[
56
]
. A different approach to edge universality for Wigner matrices based on the direct comparison with corresponding Gaussian ensembles was developed in
[
58
,
28
,
22
]
. And later, a necessary and sufficient condition for edge universality of Wigner matrices were discovered in
[
46
]
.

For the comparison argument, a key intermediate ingredient is the rigidity of extreme eigenvalues. For edge universality to be true, it is necessary that extreme eigenvalues fluctuate on scale
\OO_{\prec}(N^{-2/3})
. Main body of this paper is devoted to prove such optimal rigidity estimate. The proof is based on first constructing a
higher order self-consistent equation
for the Stieltjes transform of the empirical eigenvalue distribution, then computing high moments of the self-consistent equation by a recursive moment estimate, and proving it concentrates around zero. This approach was first introduced in
[
45
]
and further developed in
[
44
,
35
,
37
,
32
]
to study sparse Erdős-Rényi graphs and in
[
8
]
to study sparse random
d
-regular graphs.

For random
d
-regular graphs, the self-consistent equation has a
1/\sqrt{d}
asymptotic expansion. The main challenge for the above approach is to show high moments of the self-consistent equation are close to zero, which requires cancellations of each order in the asymptotic expansion.
[
8
]
was able to explore the cancellation up to
d^{-3/2}
, which implies the fluctuation of extreme eigenvalues is bounded by
\OO_{\prec}(d^{-3}+N^{-2/3})
. To get the optimal
\OO_{\prec}(N^{-2/3})
fluctuation bound, we need to have cancellations up to
d^{-{\mathfrak{a}}/2}
for arbitrary large
{\mathfrak{a}}\geqslant 0
. To achieve it, in this paper we combine the two steps, and construct the high order self-consistent equation and compute its high moments in one step. By construction, our high order self-consistent equation concentrates around zero. To identify the the self-consistent equation, we directly show the Stieltjes transform of the spectral measure of the
infinite
d
-regular tree
satisfies the same equation. Thus extreme eigenvalues concentrate around the spectral edge of the infinite
d
-regular tree, with optimal error
\OO_{\prec}(N^{-2/3})
.

The computation of the high order self-consistent equation boils down to estimate the following quantity (and its generalizations)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}RU],

(1.1)

where
{\mathcal{T}}
is a sub-tree graph (may not be connected),
A_{\mathcal{T}}
is the indicator function that
{\mathcal{T}}
is contained in the graph,
R
is a polynomial of the Green’s function entries, and
U
is any polynomial of the Stieltjes transform. To estimate (
1.1
), we resample each edge of the sub-tree
{\mathcal{T}}
, and use it as a discrete integration by part. This generalizes the simple switching procedure, introduced in
[
11
]
to prove the local law of random
d
-regular graphs.

For (
1.1
), there are three cases:
i) If
R
contains an off-diagonal Green’s function entry
G_{ij}
such that
i,j
are in different connected components of
{\mathcal{T}}
, we show (
1.1
) is negligible. ii) If
R
contains an off-diagonal Green’s function entry
G_{ij}
such that
i,j
are in the same connected component of
{\mathcal{T}}
, we show (
1.1
) is a finite linear combination of terms in the same form, but with some extra factors of
1/\sqrt{d}
. iii) If
R
contains only diagonal Green’s function entries, the leading term is obtained by replacing
R
by a power of the Stieltjes transform of the empirical measure, and the higher order terms are again in the form of (
1.1
) with some extra factors of
1/\sqrt{d}
. By repeating this procedure, we eventually obtain a polynomial of the Stieltjes transform of the empirical measure, which leads to the self-consistent equation.

To prove the convergence to the Tracy-Widom distribution, we took a dynamical approach, which was original developed to prove the bulk universality of Wigner matrices
[
24
,
25
,
19
,
41
,
42
,
17
,
21
,
26
]
.
We interpolate the adjacency matrix with the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE). The eigenvalues at each time slice have the same law as Dyson Brownian motion (DBM). For sufficiently regular initial data, it has been proven in
[
43
]
, after short time the eigenvalue statistics at the spectral edge of DBM agree with GOE. With the optimal edge rigidity as input,
[
43
]
implies the edge universality holds for adjacency matrix plus a small Gaussian component. Our main contribution is to show the edge statistics do not change along the interpolation, thus edge universality holds for the original adjacency matrix. The main challenge is to keep track the edge location
L_{t}
along the interpolation. At each time
t
, we can construct a high order self-consistent equation, which governs the spectral of the interpolated matrix. We discover that the time derivative of
L_{t}
is given by the spatial derivative of the corresponding high order self-consistent equation with a negligible error. This allows us to build a dynamical framework around
L_{t}
and show the edge statistics do not change.

It is worth to compare edge results of sparse Erdős–Rényi graphs and random
d
-regular graphs. Thanks to a series of papers
[
32
,
22
,
45
,
44
,
35
,
37
]
, the edge statistics of Erdős–Rényi graphs
G(N,p)
with
Np\geqslant N^{\mathfrak{c}}
are now well understood. When the average degree
pN\gg N^{1/3}
, extreme eigenvalues have Tracy-Widom fluctuations
[
22
,
45
]
. However, when
N^{{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant pN\ll N^{1/3}
the extreme eigenvalues have Gaussian fluctuations, given by the fluctuation of the total number of edges
[
35
,
32
]
. The higher order fluctuations are given by a sum of an infinite hierarchy of subgraph counting random variables which are asymptotically joint Gaussian
[
44
]
. Surprisingly, up to this explicit random shift, the fluctuations of extreme eigenvalues are still given by the Tracy-Widom distribution
[
37
]
.
For random
d
-regular graphs, the fluctuations of those subgraph counting
quantities are negligible. Extreme eigenvalues concentrate around a deterministic spectral edge and converge to the Tracy-Widom
distribution. In the same regime, bulk universality for Erdős–Rényi graphs and random
d
-regular graphs are also known
[
7
,
34
,
22
]
.

For Erdős–Rényi graphs in the sparser regime
Np=\OO(\ln N)
, there exists a critical value
b_{*}=1/(\ln 4-1)
such that if
Np\geqslant b_{*}\ln N/N
, the extreme eigenvalues of the normalized adjacency matrix converge to
\pm 2

[
13
,
4
,
59
,
14
]
and all the eigenvectors are delocalized
[
5
,
23
]
. For
(\ln\ln N)^{4}\ll Np<b_{*}\ln N/N
, there are outlier eigenvalues
[
59
,
4
]
.
The spectrum splits into three phases: a delocalized phase
in the bulk, a fully localized phase near the spectral edge, and a semilocalized phase in between
[
6
,
3
]
. Moreover, the joint fluctuations of the eigenvalues near the spectral edges form a Poisson point process.
For random
d
-regular graphs with any fixed degree
d\geqslant 3
, all the eigenvectors are delocalized
[
9
,
36
]
. The fluctuation of extreme eigenvalues is still conjectured to be the Tracy-Widom distribution
[
52
]
. This conjecture is still quite open, but we do know the extreme eigenvalues concentrate around
\pm 2\sqrt{d-1}
with an error
\OO((\ln\ln N/\ln N)^{2})

[
16
,
30
]
, which recently was improved by the two authors to a polynomial bound
\OO(N^{-{\mathfrak{c}}})

[
36
]
.

Main results

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the uniform probability measure on the set of
d
-regular graphs on
N
vertices. We identify a graph with its adjacency matrix
A=(A_{ij})\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}
, defined as
A_{ij}=1
if and only if
i
and
j
are adjacent. Thus,
\mathbb{P}
is the uniform probability measure on the set of symmetric matrices
A\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}
satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{ij}=d

A_{ii}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,N

Since
A
is
d
-regular, it is immediate that
A
has a trivial eigenvalue
d

(1,1,\dots,1)^{*}

d
. For convenience, we shall consider the normalized adjacency matrix

H\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A/\sqrt{d-1}.

(1.3)

We denote its eigenvalues by
\lambda_{1}=d/\sqrt{d-1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{N}\geqslant-d/\sqrt{d-1}

{\bm{u}}_{1},{\bm{u}}_{2},\cdots,{\bm{u}}_{N}
.

Our main result is about the limiting distribution of the extremal eigenvalues. For
1\ll d\ll N^{1/3}
, extreme eigenvalues of random
d
-regular graphs converge to the Tracy-Widom distribution.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix arbitrarily small
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
, and an integer
k\geqslant 1
, let
F:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded test function with bounded derivatives. For
N^{{\mathfrak{c}}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{1/3-{\mathfrak{c}}}
, there is a universal constant
{\mathfrak{e}}>0
so that,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\mathbb{E}_{H}[F(N^{2/3}(\lambda_{2}-2),\cdots,N^{2/3}(\lambda_{k+1}-2)]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{GOE}[F(N^{2/3}(\mu_{1}-2),\cdots,N^{2/3}(\mu_{k}-2))]+\mathcal{O}\left(N^{-{\mathfrak{e}}}\right),\end{split}

(1.4)

provided
N
is large enough.
The second expectation is with respect to a GOE matrix with eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geqslant\mu_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mu_{N}
.
Analogous results hold for the smallest eigenvalues.

2
Eigenvalue Rigidity Estimates

We consider the adjacency matrix
A
restricted to the subspace orthogonal to the vector
{\bf 1}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(1,\dots,1)^{*}
.
More precisely,
let
P_{\perp}\vcentcolon{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be the orthogonal projection onto
{\bf 1}^{\perp}
,
explicitly given by
P_{\perp}=I-{\bf 1}{\bf 1^{*}}/N
where
I
is the
N\times N
identity matrix.
Since
H
is the normalized adjacency matrix of a regular graph, the matrices
H
and
P_{\perp}
commute:
HP_{\perp}=P_{\perp}H
.

For a spectral parameter
z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\vcentcolon\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[z]>0\}
we define the
Green’s function
by

G(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=P_{\perp}(H-z)^{-1}P_{\perp}=\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{{\bm{u}}_{i}{\bm{u}}_{i}^{*}}{\lambda_{i}-z},

we remark that the projection
P_{\perp}
kills the largest eigenvalue.
Thus,
G
and
(H-z)^{-1}
agree on the image of
P_{\perp}
, which is the subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
perpendicular to
\bm{1}
.
The Green’s function satisfies the relation

\displaystyle G(z)H=HG(z)=zG(z)+P_{\perp}=zG(z)+(I-{\bf 1}{\bf 1^{*}}/N).

(2.1)

Moreover,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}G_{ij}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}G_{ij}(z)=0.

(2.2)

We denote the normalized trace of
G
, which is also the Stieltjes transform of the empirical spectral measure of
H|_{\bm{1}^{\perp}}
, by

m(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}G_{ii}(z)=\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-z}.

(2.3)

We refer to
m(z)
simply as the
Stieltjes transform
.
Our goal is to approximate
m(z)
by
m_{d}(z)
,
the Stieltjes transform of the Kesten–McKay law
[
38
]
,

\displaystyle m_{d}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\rho_{d}(x)}{x-z}{\rm d}x,\qquad\rho_{d}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{d-1}-\frac{x^{2}}{d}\right)^{-1}\frac{\sqrt{[4-x^{2}]_{+}}}{2\pi}.

The Kesten–McKay law
\rho_{d}
is the spectral measure at any vertex of the infinite
d
-regular tree (see, for example,
[
2
]

or
[
10
, Section 5]
). It has support
[-2,2]
in our normalization. The Stieltjes transform
m_{d}(z)
is explicitly given by

m_{d}(z)=-\left({z+\frac{d}{d-1}m_{\rm sc}(z)}\right)^{-1},

(2.4)

where
m_{\rm sc}(z)
is the Stieltjes transform of the Wigner semicircle law,

m_{\rm sc}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\rho_{\text{sc}}(x)}{x-z}{\rm d}x,\qquad\rho_{\text{sc}}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{\sqrt{[4-x^{2}]_{+}}}{2\pi},

(2.5)

satisfying the self-consistent equation

1+zm_{\rm sc}(z)+m_{\rm sc}^{2}(z)=0.

(2.6)

Later we shall use that, alternatively,
m_{d}(z)
can be characterized by the self-consistent equation, which follows from rearranging (
2.4
)

\displaystyle P_{\infty}(z,m_{d}(z))=0,\quad P_{\infty}(z,w)=1+zw+\frac{d}{d-1}w^{2}+\sum_{k\geqslant 2}\frac{(-2)^{k-1}(2k-3)!!}{k!}\,\frac{d}{(d-1)^{k}}\,w^{2k}.

(2.7)

We fix a large constant
{\mathfrak{K}}>0
and define the spectral domain

\mathbf{D}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta\vcentcolon-{\mathfrak{K}}\leqslant E\leqslant{\mathfrak{K}},0<\eta\leqslant{\mathfrak{K}},N\eta\sqrt{\min\{|E-2|,|E+2|\}+\eta}\geqslant N^{1/{\mathfrak{K}}}\}.

(2.8)

Here, and throughout the following, we use the notation

z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta

for the real and imaginary parts of
z
.
The local semicircle law for random regular graphs
[
12
]

shows that
m(z)
is approximated by
m_{d}(z)
to order
1/\sqrt{d}
(up to logarithmic corrections),
at least away from the edges
\pm 2
of the spectral measure. The next result follows from
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
. We remark that
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
gives a better estimate for
\Lambda_{\rm d}
for
z
away from the edge
\pm 2
, but the following weaker form is enough for our purpose.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, define the deterministic control parameters

\Lambda_{\rm o}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\eta}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}+\frac{d^{3/2}}{N},\quad\Lambda_{\rm d}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\eta}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}+\frac{d^{3/2}}{N}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.9)

Fix small
{\mathfrak{c}}>0
, we have, for
N^{\mathfrak{c}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{2/3-{\mathfrak{c}}}
and all
z\in\mathbf{D}
from (
2.8
)

\displaystyle\max_{i\neq j}|G_{ij}(z)|\prec\Lambda_{\rm o}(z),\quad\max_{i}|G_{ii}(z)-m_{\rm sc}(z)|\prec\Lambda_{\rm d}(z).

(2.10)

Averaging over the index
i
, the estimate (
2.10
) implies an
estimate on the Stieltjes transform
m(z)
. Using additional cancelations from this average,
in this paper we shall derive a more precise estimate
from which we obtain our results about the extremal eigenvalues.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
N^{\mathfrak{c}}\leqslant d\leqslant N^{1/3-{\mathfrak{c}}}
, uniform for
z=E+\mathrm{i}\eta\in\mathbf{D}
, the Stieltjes transform of random
d
-regular graphs satisfies

\displaystyle|m(z)-m_{d}(z)|\prec\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{N\eta}+\frac{d}{N\sqrt{\dist(z,\pm 2)}},&-2\leqslant E\leqslant 2,\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\dist(z,\pm 2)}}\left(\frac{1}{N\eta^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{(N\eta)^{2}}+\frac{d}{N}\right),&|E|\geqslant 2.\end{array}\right.

We denote the classical eigenvalue locations of the Kesten-McKay distribution
\rho_{d}
as
\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{3}>\cdots>\gamma_{N}
,

\displaystyle\frac{k-1/2}{N-1}=\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{2}\rho_{d}(x){\rm d}x,\quad 2\leqslant k\leqslant N.

Then we have the following rigidity estimates

\displaystyle|\lambda_{k}-\gamma_{k}|\prec\frac{d}{N}+\frac{1}{N^{2/3}\min\{k-1,N+1-k\}^{1/3}},\quad 2\leqslant k\leqslant N.

(2.13)

Remark 2.3
.

We remark the rigidity estimates (
2.13
) is not sharp for bulk eigenvalues because of the extra term

. However, since

The following concept of
forest
will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 3.1
.

(Forest).
By a forest we mean a finite simple graph which is a union of trees:
{\mathcal{T}}=({\bf i}({\mathcal{T}}),E({\mathcal{T}}))=({\bf i},E)
. Here
{\bf i}\in[\![{N}]\!]
is a finite set of distinct vertices,
E
is a finite set of edges, and each edge
e\in E
connects
\{i_{e},i^{\prime}_{e}\}\in{\bf i}
. We denote the number of connected components of
{\mathcal{T}}
as
\theta({\mathcal{T}})
. We remark that
{\mathcal{T}}
may contain singletons. See Figure
1
for an example.

Given a forest
{\mathcal{T}}=({\bf i},E)
, we denote

\displaystyle A_{{\mathcal{T}}}=\prod_{e\in E}A_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}},

If
{\mathcal{T}}
consists of only singletons, i.e.
E=\emptyset
, then we simply define
A_{{\mathcal{T}}}=1
.
In the rest of this section, we use switchings to estimate terms in the following forms

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}F(A)\right],\quad\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}A_{ik}F(A)\right],\quad\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}A_{jk}F(A)\right],\quad i\in{\bf i},j,k\not\in{\bf i}

where
F
is any function depending on the random graph
A
.
(Later, we shall take
F
to be a polynomial of the Green’s function entries
\{G_{ij}\}_{i,j\in[\![{N}]\!]}
and the Stieltjes transform
m
.)

Proposition 3.2
.

Given a forest
{\mathcal{T}}=({\bf i},E)
, and a function
F
which depends on the random graph
A
.
For each edge
e\in E
, we associate another edge
\{j_{e},j^{\prime}_{e}\}\in[\![{N}]\!]
, and denote
{\bf j}=\{j_{e},j^{\prime}_{e}\}_{e\in E}
.
If the indices
\bf i,\bf j
are distinct, we have the identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(A)\prod_{e\in E}\chi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}(A)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(A+\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}\right)\prod_{e\in E}\chi_{i_{e}j_{e}}^{i^{\prime}_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}(A)\right].

(3.3)

If the index
i\in{\bf i}
and the indices
{\bf i}jkm\ell
are distinct, we have the identities

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{ik}^{m\ell}(A)F(A)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{im}^{k\ell}(A)F(A+\xi_{ik}^{m\ell})\right],\\
&\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{jk}^{m\ell}(A)F(A)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{jm}^{k\ell}(A)F(A+\xi_{jk}^{m\ell})\right],\end{split}

(3.4)

where the indicator function
\chi
is as defined in (
3.2
).

Proof.

Define the sets of graphs

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\left\{A:\prod_{e\in E}\chi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}(A)=1\right\},\quad{\mathcal{G}}_{2}=\left\{A:\prod_{e\in E}\chi_{i_{e}j_{e}}^{i^{\prime}_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}(A)=1\right\}.\end{split}

By our assumption that the indices
{\bf i},{\bf j}
are distinct, there is a simple bijection between
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
, namely

\displaystyle A\in{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\mapsto A+\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{1}.

(3.5)

Since
\mathbb{P}
is the uniform probability measure on
d
-regular graphs, the claim follows from (
3.5
).

The two identities in (
3.4
) can be proven in the same way, we will only give the proof of the first one. We define the sets of graphs

{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\left\{A:A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{ik}^{m\ell}(A)=1\right\}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}=\left\{A:A_{{\mathcal{T}}}\chi_{im}^{k\ell}(A)=1\right\}.

By our assumption that the indices
{\bf i}km\ell
are distinct, there is a simple bijection between
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
:

A\in{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\mapsto A+\xi_{ik}^{m\ell}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{1},

and the claim follows.

∎

3.2
Discrete Integration by Part

We define the discrete derivative operators induced by the simple switchings, which will be used in the rest of this paper.

Definition 3.3
.

For any symmetric matrix
\xi=\sum_{a=1}^{b}\xi_{i_{a}j_{a}}^{k_{a}\ell_{a}}
(recall from (
3.1
)) for some fixed
b\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the vertex set
\bm{V}(\xi)=\cup_{a=1}^{b}\{i_{a},j_{a},k_{a},\ell_{a}\}
and define the discrete and continuous derivative operator as

D_{\xi}F(A)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=F(A+\xi)-F(A),\quad\partial_{\xi}F(A)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sqrt{d-1}\left.\partial_{t}F(A+t\xi)\right|_{t=0}.

(3.6)

For a sequence of matrices
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{c}
, we denote
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{c})
and

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{\xi}}=\partial_{\xi_{1}}\partial_{\xi_{2}}\cdots\partial_{\xi_{c}},\quad\bm{V}(\bm{\xi})=\cup_{a=1}^{c}\bm{V}(\xi_{a}),\quad|\bm{\xi}|=c.

(3.7)

Note that the operation
D_{\xi}
switches all edges in
\xi
at once. The continuous derivative

\partial_{\xi}
is the directional derivative in the direction
\xi
of the rescaled variable
H=A/\sqrt{d-1}
.
For the discrete derivative operator
D_{\xi}
, we have the discrete product rule and the commutative property

D_{\xi}(FG)=(D_{\xi}F)G+F(D_{\xi}G)+(D_{\xi}F)(D_{\xi}G),\quad D_{\xi_{1}}D_{\xi_{2}}F=D_{\xi_{2}}D_{\xi_{1}}F,

(3.8)

If
F
is continuous differentiable up to
{\mathfrak{b}}
-th order, the Taylor expansion with remainder gives

\displaystyle D_{\xi}F(A)=\sum_{n=1}^{{\mathfrak{b}}-1}\frac{\partial_{\xi}^{n}F(A)}{n!(d-1)^{n/2}}+\frac{\partial_{\xi}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}F(A+\theta\xi)}{{\mathfrak{b}}!(d-1)^{{\mathfrak{b}}/2}},

(3.9)

for some
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
.

In the switching in (
3.3
), the indicator function
\prod_{e\in E}\chi_{i_{e}j_{e}}^{i^{\prime}_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}(A)

enforces that the matrix
A+\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}
is again the adjacency matrix of a simple graph.
Note that without this indicator function, such as in the following corollaries and elsewhere throughout our proof,

A+\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}
is not necessarily the adjacency matrix of a simple graph, just a real symmetric matrix. This does however not affect our arguments, which should be viewed as operating with general symmetric matrices instead of adjacency matrices of simple graphs.

Proposition 3.4
.

Given a forest
{\mathcal{T}}=({\bf i},E)
, for each edge
e\in E
, we associate another edge
\{j_{e},j^{\prime}_{e}\}\in[\![{N}]\!]
, and denote
{\bf j}=\{j_{e},j^{\prime}_{e}\}_{e\in E}
.
For any function
F_{\bf i}(A)
depending on the vertices
{\bf i}
and the adjacency matrix
A
, we denote

\mathcal{C}(F,A)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{{\bf i}{\bf j}}\left({\left|F_{\bf i}(A)\right|+\left|F_{\bf i}\left(A+\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}\right)\right|}\right).

(3.10)

Then we have the discrete integration by parts formula (recall the summation
\sum^{*}
from (
1.2
))

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\phantom{{}={}}\frac{1}{N^{\theta({\mathcal{T}})}d^{|E|}}\sum^{*}_{{\bf i}}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}}F_{{\bf i}}(A)\right]=\frac{1}{N^{|{\bf i}|}}\sum^{*}_{{\bf i}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{{\bf i}}(A)\right]\\
&+\frac{1}{N^{\theta({\mathcal{T}}^{+})}d^{|E^{+}|}}\sum^{*}_{{\bf i}{\bf j}}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{{\mathcal{T}}^{+}}D_{{\mathcal{T}}^{+}}F_{{\bf i}}(A)\right]+\OO\left(\frac{d\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}(F,A)]}{N}\right),\end{split}

(3.11)

where
{\mathcal{T}}^{+}=({\bf i}{\bf j},E^{+})
with
E^{+}=\cup_{e\in E}\{i_{e},j_{e}\}\cup\{i_{e}^{\prime},j_{e}^{\prime}\}
,
\xi=\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}
,
and

D_{{\mathcal{T}}^{+}}=D_{\xi}

from (
3.6
).

Notice that the right hand side of (
3.11
) can be interpreted as the expectation over the switched graph, since
A_{{\mathcal{T}}^{+}}
is the indicator function that the graph
A
has edges
E^{+}=\cup_{e\in E}\{i_{e},j_{e}\}\cup\{i_{e}^{\prime},j_{e}^{\prime}\}
.
The matrix
\xi=\sum_{e\in E}\xi_{i_{e}i^{\prime}_{e}}^{j_{e}j^{\prime}_{e}}
is the indicator function for the difference of edges of the unswitched graphs and the switched graph.

Lemma 4.1

Our strategy is based on the now standard three-step approach of random matrix theory

We recall the constrained GOE

Malcev Yang-Baxter equation, weighted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Malcev algebras and post-Malcev algebras
F. Harrathi, S. Mabrouk, O. Ncib, S. Silvestrov

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to study the 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Malcev algebras and discuss the solutions of Malcev Yang-Baxter equation by 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators. Furthermore we introduce the notion of weighted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Malcev algebras, which can be characterized by graphs of the semi-direct product Malcev algebra. Then we introduce a new algebraic structure called post-Malcev algebras. Therefore, post-Malcev algebras can be viewed as the underlying algebraic structures of weighted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Malcev algebras. A post-Malcev algebra also gives rise to a new Malcev algebra. Post-Malcev algebras are analogues for Malcev algebras of post-Lie algebras and fit into a bigger framework with a close relationship with post-alternative algebras. Keywords : Alternative algebra, Malcev algebra, weighted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator, Rota-Baxter operator, representation, Malcev Yang-Baxter equation MSC2020 : 17D10, 17A01, 17A30, 17B10, 17B38

Introduction

Associative dialgebras were introduced by Loday and Pirashvili
[
36
]
as the universal
enveloping algebras of Leibniz algebras (a noncommutative analogue of Lie algebras).
Loday also defined dendriform dialgebras in his study of algebraic
K
-theory
[
35
]
.
Dendriform dialgebras have two operations whose sum is an associative operation.
Moreover, the operads associated to associative dialgebras and dendriform dialgebras are Koszul dual.
Loday and Ronco introduced trialgebras, a generalization of dialgebras, and dual to them, dendriform trialgebras
[
37
]
.
This means one can decompose an associative product into sums of two or three operations satisfying some compatibility conditions.

Aguiar
[
1
]
first noticed a relation between Rota-Baxter algebras and dendriform dialgebras.
He proved that an associative algebra with a Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{R}
of weight zero is a dendriform dialgebra
relative to operations
x\prec y=x\mathcal{R}(y)
and
x\succ y=\mathcal{R}(x)y
.
Ebrahimi-Fard
[
16
]
generalized this fact to Rota-Baxter algebras of arbitrary weight
and obtained dendriform dialgebras and dendriform trialgebras.
Universal enveloping Rota-Baxter algebras of weight
\lambda
for dendriform dialgebras and trialgebras were defined by
Ebrahimi-Fard and Guo in
[
18
]
.

This idea of splitting associative operations gave rise to different algebraic structures with multiple binary operations
such as quadri-algebras, ennea-algebras, NS-algebras, dendriform-Nijenhuis algebras or octo-algebras.
These constructions can be put into the framework of (black square) products of nonsymmetric operads
[
17
]

Analogues of dendriform dialgebras, quadri-algebras and octo-algebras for Lie algebras
[
5
,

]
,

[
4
,

]
, alternative algebras
[

]
or Poisson algebras
[

have been obtained.
They can be regarded as splitting the operations in these latter algebras.

Malcev algebras and Rota-Baxter operators.

Rota-Baxter operators were introduced by G. Baxter

\mathcal{R}:A\to A

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(x)\mathcal{R}(y)=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{R}(x)y+x\mathcal{R}(y)+\lambda xy),

(1.1)

for all

The notion of Malcev algebras was introduced by Malcev
[
39

, who called these objects Moufang-Lie algebras.

Definition 1.1
.

A
Malcev algebra
is a non-associative algebra
A
with an anti-symmetric multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot]
that satisfies the Malcev identity for
x,y,z\in A
,

J(x,y,[x,z])=[J(x,y,z),x],

(1.2)

where
J(x,y,z)=[[x,y],z]+[[z,x],y]+[[y,z],x]
is the Jacobian.

Expanding the Jacobian, the Malcev identity (
1.2
) is equivalent to Sagle’s identity
for
x,y,z,t\in A:

[[x,z],[y,t]]=[[[x,y],z],t]+[[[y,z],t],x]+[[[z,t],x],y]+[[[t,x],y],z].

In particular, Lie algebras are examples of Malcev algebras. Malcev algebras play an important role in the geometry of smooth loops. Just as the tangent
algebra of a Lie group is a Lie algebra, the tangent algebra of a locally analytic Moufang loop is a Malcev algebra

[
31
,
32
,
39
,
41
,
45
]
. The reader is referred to
[

40
,

]
for discussions about the relationships between Malcev algebras, exceptional Lie algebras and physics.

On the other hand, Madariaga
[
38
]
first established the following connection from Rota-Baxter operators on Malcev algebras
to pre-Malcev algebras.

Definition 1.2
.

A
pre-Malcev algebra
is a vector space
A
endowed with a bilinear
product
\rhd
satisfying the following identity
for
x,y,z,t\in A
,

[y,z]\rhd(x\rhd t)+[[x,y],z]\rhd t+y\rhd([x,z]\rhd t-x\rhd(y\rhd(z\rhd t))+z\rhd(x\rhd(y\rhd t))=0,

(1.3)

where
[x,y]=x\rhd y-y\rhd x.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra and
\mathcal{R}
a Rota-Baxter operator. Then the binary operation

x\vartriangleright y=[\mathcal{R}(x),y]

defines a pre-Malcev algebras.

Closely related to Malcev algebras are alternative algebras. An alternative algebra is an algebra whose associator is an alternating function. In particular, all associative algebras are alternative, but there are plenty of non-associative alternative algebras, such as the octonions. Roughly speaking, alternative algebras are related to Malcev algebras as associative algebras are related to Lie algebras. Indeed, as Malcev observed in
[
39
]
, every alternative algebra

is Malcev-admissible. There are many Malcev-admissible algebras that are not alternative; see, e.g.,
[
40
]

[
48
]
for applications of alternative algebras to projective geometry, buildings, and algebraic groups.

Malcev Yang-Baxter equation and
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

The notion of Malcev bialgebra was introduced by Vershinin in
[
51
]
as an analogue of a Lie bialgebra
(also see
[
23
]
). A class of Malcev bialgebras (coboundary
cases) are obtained from the solutions of an algebraic equation in a Malcev
algebra, which is an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (CYBE)
in a Lie algebra (
[
23
]
). It is called Malcev Yang-Baxter equation (MYBE)
for convenience. The CYBE arose from the study of inverse scattering theory in 1980s. Later it was recognized as the "semi-classical
limit" of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation which was encountered by C. N. Yang in the
computation of the eigenfunctions of a one-dimensional fermion gas with delta function
interactions
[
52
]
and by R. J. Baxter in the solution of the eight vertex model in statistical
mechanics
[
9
]
. The study of the CYBE is also related to classical integrable systems and
quantum groups (see
[
15
]
and the references therein).
An important approach in the study of the CYBE was through the interpretation of its
tensor form in various operator forms which proved to be effective in providing solutions of
the CYBE, in addition to the well-known work of Belavin and Drinfeld
[
10
]
.

In the case of Lie algebras, it was Semenov-Tian-Shansky who first introduced
certain operator form of the CYBE(
[
46
]
). Later Kupershmidt introduced
a notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator of a Lie algebra as a generalization of (the operator
form of) the CYBE in a Lie algebra
[
33
]
. Nevertheless, the
\mathcal{O}
-operators of
Lie algebras play more interesting roles in the study of the CYBE. In fact, a
skew-symmetric solution of the CYBE is exactly a special
\mathcal{O}
-operator (associated
to the coadjoint representation) and more importantly, there are a kind
of algebraic structures behind the
\mathcal{O}
-operators of Lie algebras and the related
CYBE, namely, pre-Lie algebras, in the following sense: the
\mathcal{O}
-operators of
Lie algebras provide a direct relationship between Lie algebras and pre-Lie
algebras and in the invertible cases, they provide a necessary and sufficient
condition for the existence of a compatible pre-Lie algebra structure on a Lie
algebra, as an immediate consequence, there are some solutions of the CYBE
in certain Lie algebras obtained from pre-Lie algebras
[
7
]
.

The notion of post-algebras goes back to Rosenbloom in

Post-Lie algebras have been introduced by Vallette in 2007

intrested in post-Lie algebra structures, motivated by the importance of

derivations.

Recently, Post-Lie algebras which are non-associative algebras played an
important role in different areas of pure and applied mathematics. They
consist of a vector space
A
equipped with a Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]

and a binary operation
\triangleright
satisfying the following axioms

\displaystyle x\triangleright[y,z]

\displaystyle=[x\triangleright y,z]+[y,x\triangleright z],

(1.4)

\displaystyle[x,y]\triangleright z

\displaystyle=as_{\triangleright}(x,y,z)-as_{\triangleright}(y,x,z).

(1.5)

If the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
is zero, we have exactly a pre-Lie
structure.
It is worth to note that, in spite the post-Lie product does not yield a Lie bracket by antisymmetrization,
the bilinear product
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:A\otimes A\to A
, defined for all
x,y\in A
by

\displaystyle\{x,y\}=x\vartriangleright y-y\vartriangleright x+[x,y].

(1.6)

defines on

another Lie algebra structure.
The varieties of pre- and post-Lie algebras play a crucial role
in the definition of any pre and post-algebra through black Manin operads
product, see details in
[
11
,
27
]

are intimately associated with euclidean geometry, post-Lie algebras occur
naturally in the differential geometry of homogeneous spaces, and are also
closely related to Cartan’s method of moving frames.
Ebrahimi-Fard, Lundervold and Munthe-Kaas
[
20
]

studied universal enveloping algebras of post-Lie algebras and the free
post-Lie algebra.

In Section 2, we study the relationship between
\mathcal{O}
-operators and Malcev Yang-Baxter equation.
We construct in section 3 alternative algebras structure associated to any post-alternative algebra. The multiplication is
given by

x\star y=x\prec y+y\succ x+x\cdot y.

In addition, we investigate the notion of a weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operator to construct a post-alternative algebra structure on the
A
-bimodule
\mathbb{K}
-algebra of an alternative algebra
(A,\cdot)
.
The Last section is devoted to introduce the notion of post-Malcev algebra and we show that weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operators can be used to construct post-Malcev algebras.
We also reveal
a relation between post-Malcev algebras and post-alternative algebras by the
commutative diagram (
4.19
).

Convention and notation.

Throughout this paper, all algebras are finite-dimensional and over a field
\mathbb{K}
of
characteristic zero.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an algebra. We use the following notations.

(a)

Denote the left and right multiplication operator by
L_{\cdot}(x)
and
R_{\cdot}(x)
respectively, that is,

L_{\cdot}(x)y=R_{\cdot}(y)x=x\cdot y
for any
x,y\in A
. We also simply denote them by
L(x)
and
R(x)

respectively without confusion. In particular, if
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Malcev algebra, we let
ad_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)=ad(x)

denote the adjoint operator, that is,
ad_{[\cdot,\cdot]}(x)y=ad(x)y=[x,y]
for any
x,y\in A
.

(b)

Let
r=\sumop\displaylimits\limits_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A^{\otimes 2}
, and denote

r_{12}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes 1,\quad r_{13}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}x_{i}\otimes 1\otimes y_{i},\quad r_{23}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}1\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i},

(1.32)

where 1 is a unit element if
(A,\cdot)
is unital or a symbol playing a similar role of
the unit for the nonunital cases.

(c)

Let
V
be a vector space. The
twisting operator

\sigma:V^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow V^{\otimes 2}
is defined for all
a,b\in V
by

\sigma(a\otimes b)=b\otimes a.

We call
r=\sumop\displaylimits\limits_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in V^{\otimes 2}

skew-symmetric
(resp.
symmetric
) if

r=-\sigma(r)
(resp.
r=\sigma(r)
). On the other hand,
r
can be regarded as a linear map from the dual space
V^{*}
to
V
in the following way, for all
a^{*},b^{*}\in V^{*}
,

\langle a^{*},r(b^{*})\rangle=\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*},r\rangle,

(1.33)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the canonical pairing between
V^{*}
and
V
.

(d)

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be two vector spaces and
T:V_{1}\to V_{2}
be a linear map. Denote the dual (linear) map by

T^{*}:V^{*}_{2}\to V^{*}_{1}

defined for all
a_{1}\in V_{1},a^{*}_{2}\in V^{*}_{2}
by

\langle T^{*}(a^{*}_{2}),a_{1}\rangle=\langle a^{*}_{2},T(a_{1})\rangle.

(1.34)

On the other hand,
T
can be identified as an element in
V_{2}\otimes V^{*}_{1}
by

\langle a_{2}^{*}\otimes a_{1},T\rangle=\langle a_{2}^{*},T(a_{1})\rangle,\quad\forall a_{1}\in V_{1},a^{*}_{2}\in V^{*}_{2}.

(1.35)

Note that (
1.33
) is exactly the case
V_{1}=V^{*}_{2}
of (
1.35
). Moreover, in
the above sense, any linear map
T:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is obviously an element in

(V_{2}\oplus V^{*}_{1})\otimes(V_{2}\oplus V^{*}_{1})
.

(e)

Let
A
be an algebra and
V
be a vector space. For any linear map
\rho:A\rightarrow End(V)
, define a linear map
\rho^{*}:A\to End(V^{*})
, for all

x\in A,a^{*}\in V^{*},b\in V
, by

\langle\rho^{*}(x)a^{*},b\rangle=-\langle a^{*},\rho(x)b\rangle.

(1.36)

Note that in this case,
\rho^{*}

is different from the one given by (
1.34
) which
regards
End(V)
as a vector space, too.

2
\mathcal{O}
-operators and Malcev Yang-Baxter equation

In this section,
we recall the classical result that a skew-symmetric solution
of CYBE in a Malcev algebra gives an
\mathcal{O}
-operator through a duality between tensor product and
linear maps. Not every
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Lie algebra comes from a solution of CYBE in this way. However, any

\mathcal{O}
-operator can be recovered from a solution of CYBE in a larger Malcev algebra.

We first
recall the concept of a representation
(
see
[
30
]
)
and construct the dual representation.

Definition 2.1

(
[
30
]
)
.

A
representation
(or
a module
) of a Malcev algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
on
a vector space
V
is a linear map
\rho:A\longrightarrow End(V)
such that, for all

x,y\in A
,

\rho([[x,y],z])=\rho(x)\rho(y)\rho(z)-\rho(z)\rho(x)\rho(y)+\rho(y)\rho([z,x])-\rho([y,z])\rho(x).

(2.1)

We denote this representation by
(V,\rho)
.

For all
x,y\in A
, define the map
ad:A\longrightarrow End(A)
by

ad_{x}(y)=[x,y].

Then
ad
is a representation of the Malcev algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
on
A
, which is called the adjoint representation.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(V,\rho)
be a representation of a Malcev algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
.
Then
(V^{*},\rho^{*})
defined by (
1.36
) is a representation of
A
, which is called the dual representation of
(V,\rho)
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), we have, for
x,y,z\in A
,

\rho([[y,x],z])=-\rho([[x,y],z])=\rho(z)\rho(y)\rho(x)-\rho(y)\rho(x)\rho(z)-\rho(x)\rho([z,y])+\rho([x,z])\rho(y).

So, for any
x,y,z\in A
,
a^{*}\in V^{*}
,
b\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\langle\rho^{*}([[x,y],z])a^{*},b\Big\rangle=-\Big\langle a^{*},\rho([[x,y],z])b\Big\rangle=-\Big\langle a^{*},-\rho([[y,x],z])b\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Big\langle a^{*},\big(\rho(y)\rho(x)\rho(z)-\rho(z)\rho(y)\rho(x)+\rho(x)\rho([z,y])-\rho([x,z])\rho(y)\big)b\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Big\langle\big(-\rho^{*}(z)\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)+\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}(z)+\rho^{*}([z,y])\rho^{*}(x)-\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}([x,z])\big)a^{*},b\Big\rangle.

Hence, since
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is nondegenerate, we obtain

\rho^{*}([[x,y],z])=\rho^{*}(z)\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)-\rho^{*}(x)\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}(z)+\rho^{*}([y,z])\rho^{*}(x)-\rho^{*}(y)\rho^{*}([z,x]).\qed

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
.
r
is called a solution of
Malcev Yang-Baxter equation in
A
if
r
satisfies

[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0,

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle[r_{12},r_{13}]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}[x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j},

\displaystyle[r_{13},r_{23}]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}\otimes[y_{i},y_{j}],

\displaystyle[r_{12},r_{23}]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes[y_{i},x_{j}]\otimes y_{j}.

Equation (
2.2
) gives the tensor form of Malcev Yang-Baxter equation. What we will do next is
to replace the tensor form by a linear operator satisfying some conditions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. Then
r
is
a skew-symmetric solution of Malcev Yang-Baxter equation in
A
if and only if
r
satisfies for all
x^{*},y^{*}\in A^{*}
,

[r(x^{*}),r(y^{*})]=r(ad^{*}r(x^{*})(y^{*})-ad^{*}r(y^{*})(x^{*})).

(2.3)

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i},...,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
A
and

\{e_{i}^{*},...,e_{n}^{*}\}
be its dual basis. Suppose that
[e_{i},e_{j}]=\sumop\displaylimits\limits_{p}c_{ij}^{p}e_{p}
and
r=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}{a_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}}
. Hence
a_{ij}=-a_{ji}
.
Now, we have

\displaystyle\left[r_{12},r_{13}\right]

\displaystyle=\Big[\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}a_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes 1,\sumop\displaylimits_{k,l}a_{kl}e_{k}\otimes 1\otimes e_{l}\Big]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j,k,l,p}a_{ij}a_{kl}c_{ik}^{p}e_{p}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{l},

\displaystyle\left[r_{13},r_{23}\right]

\displaystyle=\Big[\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}a_{ij}e_{i}\otimes 1\otimes e_{j},\sumop\displaylimits_{k,l}a_{kl}1\otimes e_{k}\otimes e_{l}\Big]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j,k,l,p}a_{ij}a_{kl}c_{jl}^{p}e_{i}\otimes e_{k}\otimes e_{p},

\displaystyle\left[r_{12},r_{23}\right]

\displaystyle=\Big[\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j}a_{ij}e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes 1,\sumop\displaylimits_{k,l}a_{kl}1\otimes e_{k}\otimes e_{l}\Big]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i,j,k,l,p}a_{ij}a_{kl}c_{jk}^{p}e_{i}\otimes e_{p}\otimes e_{l}.

Then
r
is a solution of the
Malcev Yang-Baxter equation in
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
if and only if (for
any
j,p,l
)

\sumop\displaylimits_{i,k}\Big({a_{ij}a_{kl}c_{ik}^{p}+a_{kp}a_{ij}c_{ki}^{l}+a_{pi}a_{kl}c_{ik}^{j}}\Big)e_{p}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{l}=0.

On the other hand, by (
1.33
), we get

r(e_{j}^{*})=\sumop\displaylimits\limits_{i}a_{ij}e_{i}.

Then, if we set
x^{*}=e_{j}^{*}
and
y^{*}=e_{l}^{*}
, by (
2.3
),

\sumop\displaylimits_{i,k}\Big({a_{ij}a_{kl}c_{ik}^{p}+a_{kp}a_{ij}c_{ki}^{l}+a_{pi}a_{kl}c_{ik}^{j}}\Big)e_{p}=0.

Therefore, it is easy to see that
r
is a solution of Malcev Yang-Baxter equation in
A
if and only
if
r
satisfies (
2.3
).
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra. A symmetric bilinear form

B
on
A
is called
\emph{invariant}
if,
for all
x,y,z\in A
,

B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z]).

(2.4)

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra.
A Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on
A
is
a linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\to A
satisfying for all
x,y\in A,

[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y)]=\mathcal{R}([\mathcal{R}(x),y]+[x,\mathcal{R}(y)]).

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. Assume
r
is skew-symmetric
and there exists a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form
B
on
A
. Define a linear
map
\varphi:A\to A^{\ast}
by
\langle\varphi(x),y\rangle=B(x,y)
for any
x,y\in A
. Then
r
is a solution of the Malcev
Yang-Baxter equation in
A
if and only if
\mathcal{R}=r\varphi:A\to A
is a Rota-Baxter operator.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in A
, we have

\langle\varphi(ad(x)y),z\rangle=B([x,y],z)=B(z,[x,y])=-B(y,[x,z])=\langle ad^{*}(x)\varphi(y),z\rangle.

Hence
\varphi(ad(x)y)=ad^{*}(x)\varphi(y)
for any
x,y\in A
.
Let
x^{*}=\varphi(x)
,
y^{*}=\varphi(y)
, then by Theorem
2.1
,
r
is a solution of the Malcev Yang-Baxter
equation in
A
if and only if

[r\varphi(x),r\varphi(y)]=[r(x^{*}),r(y^{*})]=r(ad^{*}r(x^{*})(y^{*})-ad^{*}r(y^{*})(x^{*}))=r\varphi\big([r\varphi(x),y]+[x,r\varphi(y)]\big).

Therefore the conclusion holds.
∎

Now, we introduce the notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator of a Malcev algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra and let
(V,\rho)
be a representation of
A
. A linear map
T:V\to A
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
\rho
if
for all
a,b\in V,

\displaystyle[T(a),T(b)]=T\big(\rho(T(a))b-\rho(T(b))a\big).

(2.5)

Example 2.1
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra. Then a Rota-Baxter operator
(of weight zero) is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
A
associated to the adjoint representation

(A,ad)
and a skew-symmetric solution of Malcev Yang-Baxtezr equation in
A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
A

associated to the representation
(A^{\ast},ad^{\ast})
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra. Let
\rho^{*}:A\rightarrow gl(V^{*})
be the dual representation
of the representation
\rho:A\rightarrow gl(V)
of the Malcev
algebra
A
.
A linear map
T:V\to A
can be identified as an
element in
A\otimes V^{*}\subset(A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}V^{*})\otimes(A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}V)

as follows.
Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
A
. Let

\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\}
be a basis of
V
and
\{v_{1}^{*},\cdots,v_{m}^{*}\}
be its dual basis, that is
v_{i}^{*}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Set

T(v_{i})=\sumop\displaylimits\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j},i=1,\cdots,m
. Since as
vector spaces,

{\rm Hom}(V,A)\cong A\otimes V^{*},
we
have

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}T(v_{i})\otimes v_{i}^{*}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{m}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j}\otimes v_{i}^{*}

\displaystyle\in A\otimes V^{*}\subset(A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}V^{*})\otimes(A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}V^{*}).

(2.6)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Malcev algebra. Then
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
A
associated to
(V,\rho)

if and only if
r=T-\sigma(T)
is a skew-symmetric solution of the Malcev Yang-Baxter equation
in
A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}V^{\ast}.

Proof.

From (
2
), we have

r=T-\sigma(T)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}T(v^{i})\otimes v^{\ast}_{i}-v^{\ast}_{i}\otimes T(v^{i}).

3.1
Some basic results on alternative algebras

Definition 3.1
.

An
alternative algebra

(A,\cdot)
is a vector space
A
equipped with a
bilinear operation
(x,y)\rightarrow x\cdot y
satisfying, for all
x,y,z\in A
,

as_{A}(x,x,y)=as_{A}(y,x,x)=0,

(3.1)

where
as_{A}(x,y,z)=(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)
is the

associator
.

Remark 3.1
.

If the characteristic of the field is not 2, then an alternative
algebra
(A,\cdot)
also satisfies the stronger axioms, for all
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle as_{A}(x,y,z)+as_{A}(y,x,z)

\displaystyle=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle as_{A}(z,x,y)+as_{A}(z,y,x)

\displaystyle=0.

(3.3)

Now, recall that an algebra
(A,\cdot)
is called admissible Malcev
algebra if
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Malcev algebra, where
[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
.

Example 3.1
.

Any alternative algebra is Malcev admissible. That is if
(A,\cdot)
be an
alternative algebra, then
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Malcev algebra, where
[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
, for
all
x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.2

(
[
44
]
)
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an alternative algebra and
V
be a vector space.
Let
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r}:A\to End(V)
be two linear maps. Then,
(V,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
is called a representation or
a bimodule of

if, for any
x,y\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(x)\mathfrak{r}(y)+\mathfrak{r}(y)\mathfrak{r}(x)-\mathfrak{r}(x\cdot y)-\mathfrak{r}(y\cdot x)=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(x\cdot y)+\mathfrak{l}(y\cdot x)-\mathfrak{l}(x)\mathfrak{l}(y)-\mathfrak{l}(y)\mathfrak{l}(x)=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{l}(x\cdot y)+\mathfrak{r}(y)\mathfrak{l}(x)-\mathfrak{l}(x)\mathfrak{l}(y)-\mathfrak{l}(x)\mathfrak{r}(y)=0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}(y)\mathfrak{l}(x)+\mathfrak{r}(y)\mathfrak{r}(x)-\mathfrak{l}(x)\mathfrak{r}(z)-\mathfrak{r}(x\cdot y)=0.

(3.7)

Definition 3.3
.

A pre-alternative algebra
is a triple
(A,\prec,\succ)

is a vector space,

\prec,\succ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A

are bilinear maps satisfying
for all

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec z-x\succ(y\prec z)+(y\prec x)\prec z-y\prec(x\cdot z)=0,

(3.8)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec z-x\succ(y\prec z)+(z\cdot x)\succ y-z\succ(x\succ y)=0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\succ z-x\succ(y\succ z)+(y\cdot x)\succ z-y\succ(x\succ z)=0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle(z\prec x)\prec y-z\prec(x\star y)+(z\prec y)\prec x-z\prec(y\cdot x)=0.

(3.11)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a pre-alternative algebra. Then the product

. Furthermore,

on

Let

be a pre-alternative algebra.
Then the product

.

Definition 3.4

(3.15)

Proposition 3.3

For any

Proposition 4.10
.

Let

A

\mathcal{O}

The following result establishes a close relation between a post-alternative algebra and a post-Malcev algebra.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T:V\to A
be a
\lambda
-weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operator of alternative algebra
(A,\cdot)
with respect to

(V,\cdot_{V},\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})
and
(V,\circ,\prec,\succ)
be the associated post-alternative algebra given in Theorem
3.2
. Then
T
is a
\lambda
-weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Malcev admissible algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
with respect to an
A
-module Malcev algebra
(V;[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})
.

Moreover, if

(V,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\rhd)
be a post-Malcev algebra associated to the Malcev admissible algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
on
(V;[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})
.
Then, the products
(\{\cdot,\cdot\},\rhd)
are related with
(\circ,\prec,\succ)
as follow, for all
a,b\in V
,

\{a,b\}=a\circ b-b\circ a,\quad a\rhd b=a\succ b-b\prec a.

(4.17)

Proof.

Using the condition of
\lambda
-weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operator in (
3.28
) and Proposition
4.2
,
for
a,b\in A
,

\displaystyle[T(a),T(b)]-T\big(\rho(T(a))b-\rho(T(b))a+\lambda[a,b]_{V}\big)

\displaystyle=T(a)\cdot T(b)-T(b)\cdot T(a)-T\big((\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})(T(a))b-(\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})(T(b))a+\lambda(a\cdot_{V}b-b\cdot_{V}a\big)=0.

Then
T
is a
\lambda
-weighted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Malcev admissible algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
with respect to an
A
-module Malcev algebra
(V;[\cdot,\cdot]_{V},\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})
.

On the other hand, from (
3.30
) of Theorem
3.2
and (
4.16
) of Theorem
4.2
that

\displaystyle\{a,b\}=\lambda[a,b]_{V}=\lambda a\cdot_{V}b-\lambda b\cdot_{V}a=a\circ b-b\circ a,

\displaystyle a\rhd b=(\mathfrak{l}-\mathfrak{r})(T(a))b=\mathfrak{l}(T(a))b-\mathfrak{r}(T(a))b=a\succ b-b\prec a.\qed

Corollary 4.4
.

\{x,y\}=x\circ y-y\circ x,\quad x\rhd y=x\succ y-y\prec x,

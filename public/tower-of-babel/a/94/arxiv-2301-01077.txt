Introduction

Locally conformally Kähler geometry
is probably the simplest complex geometry beyond
the Kähler which admits many examples, explicit
and non-explicit. A manifold is called
LCK
(locally conformally Kähler)
if it admits
a Kähler covering, with the deck group acting
by holomorphic homotheties (
2.1
).
Every Hopf manifold
is LCK, because its universal cover admits a Kähler
potential, with the deck transform multiplying
the potential by a number. A manifold with
this property is called
an LCK manifold with potential
;
an LCK manifold admits a potential if and only
if it can be realized as a complex submanifold
in a Hopf manifold (
[
OV3
]
).

Recall that
a diagonal
Hopf manifold
is a manifold of
form
\frac{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\backslash 0}{\langle A\rangle}
,
where
A
is a linear contraction which
can be diagonalized in appropriate basis.
A more special class of LCK manifolds
with potential are
Vaisman manifolds
,
Subsection
2.2.2
, which can
be characterized as complex manifolds
admitting a holomorphic embedding to a diagonal
Hopf manifold (
[
OV9
, Theorem 4.9]
).

Every Vaisman manifold
M
is equipped with an action of
the Lee and anti-Lee fields, which are Killing and holomorphic
(
2.2.2
).
In
[
Ve2
]
,
it was shown that any stable holomorphic bundle on
M

is equivariant with respect to this action.
From
2.2.2
, it
follows that any complex subvariety is invariant
under this action as well.

Let
G
be the group of holomorphic isometries
of a Vaisman manifold
M
obtained as the closure of the
group generated by the Lee and the anti-Lee flow.
In this paper, we prove that any
holomorphic tensor field on
M
is

G
-invariant.

For holomorphic vector fields and differential forms this result was obtained
by K. Tsukada in
[
T1
, Theorem 3.3, Theorem 4.2]
.

We prove a similar result for LCK manifolds with potential.
Let
M
be an LCK manifold with potential, and
\tilde{M}

its Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cover. We assume that
\tilde{M}
is an open
algebraic cone (
2.2.1
);
this is true automatically when
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M\geqslant 3

(
[
OV8
]
).
When
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=2
, this would follow if we assume
the GSS conjecture
(
[
OV7
, Section 25.6]
). Since
\tilde{M}
is algebraic,
it makes sense to speak of the Zariski closure of the

{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
\tilde{M}
. We consider the Zariski
closure

{\cal G}
of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action as the smallest algebraic
subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{M})
containing this
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action;
this group is clearly commutative and positive-dimensional. Since
the action of
{\cal G}
commutes with the action of the deck
transform group
{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\cal G}
on
\tilde{M}
,
the quotient
{\cal G}/{\mathbb{Z}}
naturally acts on
M
.

We prove that any holomorphic tensor field on
an LCK manifold with potential is
{\cal G}
-invariant
(
3.2
).

Let
H=\frac{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\backslash 0}{\langle A\rangle}

be a diagonal Hopf manfold, and
{\cal G}
the
Zariski closure of
{\langle A\rangle}
.
Let
\alpha_{i}
be the eigenvalues of
A
,
and
A_{1}
an operator which has the eigenvalues
|\alpha_{i}|

in the same basis. In
4
,
we show that the linear field
\log\!A_{1}
belongs to the
Zariski closure of
{\langle A\rangle}
, and
in
5
,
step 3, we prove that
\log A_{1}
can be obtained
as the Lee field of a Vaisman structure on
H
.

This implies that the Lee and anti-Lee field of a Vaisman
manifold belong to the Lie algebra of
{\cal G}

(the Zariski closure of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action).
Therefore, any holomorphic tensor field is Lee and anti-Lee
invariant.

2
Preliminaries

For the material in this section, we refer the reader to
[
OV7
]
.

2.1
Locally conformally Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.1:

A complex manifold
(M,I)
is called
locally conformally
Kähler
(LCK, for short) if it admits a
covering
(\tilde{M},I)
equipped with a Kähler metric

\tilde{\omega}
such that the deck group of the
cover acts on
(\tilde{M},\tilde{\omega})
by holomorphic
homotheties. An
LCK metric
on an LCK manifold
is an Hermitian metric on
(M,I)
such that its
pullback to
\tilde{M}
is conformal with
\tilde{\omega}
.

Proposition 2.2:
A Hermitian manifold
(M,I,g)
is
LCK if and only if there exists a closed 1-form
\theta
(called
the Lee form
) such that the fundamental form

\omega(x,y):=g(Ix,y)
satisfies

d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
.

Definition 2.3:
An LCK structure
(\omega,\theta)
on a complex manifold
(M,I)
is called
strict
if the Lee form is not exact. When the Lee form is exact, the structure
(\omega,\theta)
is called
globally conformally Kähler
(GCK).

Example 2.4:
Let
H:=({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus 0)/\langle A\rangle
,
A=\lambda\operatorname{Id}
,

|\lambda|>1
, be a
classical Hopf manifold
. It is
LCK because the flat Kähler metric
\tilde{g}_{0}=\sum dz_{i}\otimes d\overline{z}_{i}
is multiplied by 2 by the deck
group
{\mathbb{Z}}
. The Lee form on the covering
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus 0

is
\theta=-d\log|z|^{2}
. Since the Hopf manifold is diffeomorphic with
S^{1}\times S^{2n-1}
, the LCK structure is strict.

Remark 2.5:
All complex submanifolds of an LCK manifold are still LCK (but not necessarily strict).

2.2
LCK manifolds with potential

Definition 2.6:
An LCK manifold has
LCK potential
if it
admits a Kähler covering on which the Kähler metric
has a global and positive potential function
\psi
such that
the deck group multiplies
\psi
by a
constant.
1
1

1

In the sequel, a
differential form which is multiplied by a constant
factor by the action of the deck group is called
automorphic
.

In this case,
M
is called
LCK manifold with
potential
.

Example 2.7:
The diagonal Hopf manifold in
2.1
is LCK with potential because
\tilde{g}_{0}
has the global automorphic potential
\psi:=\sum|z_{i}|^{2}
.

By
[
OV3
]
, compact LCK manifolds with potential
are stable to small deformations of the complex
structure. This implies that
all
linear Hopf manifolds

({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\setminus 0)/\langle A\rangle
,
A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
,
with eigenvalues of absolute value
>1
, are LCK with potential.

Remark 2.8:
One can easily prove that all complex submanifolds of an LCK manifold with potential are still LCK with potential.

One of the most important properties of LCK manifolds with potential is the following Kodaira-type embedding result:

Theorem 2.9:
(
[
OV3
]
) Let
M
be a compact LCK manifold with potential of complex dimension at least 3. Then
M
admits a holomorphic embedding into a linear Hopf manifold.

The idea of the proof is that the Kähler cover of an LCK
manifold with potential is not a cone in the Riemannian sense
(it lacks the homothety), but is very close to the idea of
cone, because it can be completed with just one (singular)
point. This completion can be realized using a theorem by
Rossi and Andreotti-Siu which only works in dimension
greater than 3. In dimension 2, the result is true,
if one assumes the GSS conjecture (
[
OV6
, Theorem 5.6]
),
which is a long-standing conjecture, often assumed to be true.

Below, we give a precise description of the Kähler
cover of an LCK manifold with potential.

2.2.1
Algebraic cones

Definition 2.10:

(
[
OV4
]
)
A
closed algebraic cone
is an
affine variety
{\cal C}
admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action
\rho

with a unique fixed point
x_{0}
, called
the origin
or
the apex
,
and satisfying the following:

(i)

{\cal C}
is smooth outside of
x_{0}
, and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts freely
on
{\cal C}\backslash x_{0}
.

(ii)

\rho
acts on the Zariski tangent
space
T_{x_{0}}{\cal C}
with all eigenvalues

|\alpha_{i}|<1
.

An
open algebraic cone
is a complex manifold
which is biholomorphic to a closed algebraic
cone without the origin.

Theorem 2.11:
(
[
OV8
]
)
Let
M
be an LCK manifold with proper potential,
and
\tilde{M}
its Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cover.
Then
\tilde{M}
is an open algebraic cone.

Remark 2.12:
The restriction to proper potentials is not significant, since
any LCK metric with potential can be approximated in the
{C}^{\infty}
-topology (while keeping the complex structure fixed) by an LCK metric with proper potential (
[
OV2
,
OV5
]
).

As a topological space, the closed algebraic cone

\tilde{M}_{c}
is obtained as a one-point completion of an open
algebraic cone
\tilde{M}
. However, the complex structure
on a closed algebraic cone is not uniquely determined by

\tilde{M}
. We refer the reader to
[
OV8
]
for this
intricate and fascinating subject.

The manifold
\tilde{M}
does not define
the LCK manifold
M
uniquely:
M=\frac{\tilde{M}}{{\mathbb{Z}}}

is determined by the choice of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action which
may vary.

In
[
OV8
]
, we proved that an algebraic cone of an
LCK manifold with potential
is isomorphic to an affine cone over a projective orbifold.
This nearly transforms the geometry of LCK manifolds with
potential into a chapter of algebraic geometry.

We end these preliminaries by citing a result which gives a method of constructing LCK manifolds with potential once the algebraic cone which will be the covering is known. We first need the following notion.

Definition 2.13:
Let
\tilde{M}
be an open algebraic cone,
\tilde{M}_{c}

the corresponding closed cone,
and
\vec{r}\in T\tilde{M}_{c}
a holomorphic vector field
such that for all
t>0
the diffeomorphism
e^{t\vec{r}}

is a holomorphic contraction of
\tilde{M}_{c}
to the origin.
In this situation, the 1-parameter
family
e^{t\vec{r}}
is called
the flow of contractions
(or
contraction flow
).
A strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface
S\subset\tilde{M}
is called
a
pseudoconvex shell
if
S
intersects each orbit
of
e^{t\vec{r}}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, exactly once.

Pseudoconvex shells are used to obtain
automorphic plurisubharmonic functions, as follows.

Theorem 2.14:
(
[
OV4
]
)
Let
\tilde{M}

be an algebraic cone,
e^{t\vec{r}}
a contraction flow, and

S\subset\tilde{M}
a pseudoconvex shell. Then for each
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}

there exists a unique function
\varphi_{\lambda}
such that

\operatorname{Lie}_{\vec{r}}\varphi=\lambda\varphi
and
\varphi_{\lambda}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{S}}\right.}=1
.
Moreover,
such
\varphi_{\lambda}
is strictly
plurisubharmonic when
\lambda\gg 0
.

2.2.2
Vaisman manifolds

Among the LCK manifolds with potential, the best understood subclass is formed by the
Vaisman manifolds
. These are LCK manifolds
with the Lee form parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita
connection of the LCK metric.

Let
(M,I,\omega,\theta)
be a Vaisman manifold and

\pi\ :\ \tilde{M}\to M
a Kähler cover on which

\pi^{*}\theta
is exact. Then it can be seen that
the
\tilde{\omega}
-squared norm of
\pi^{*}\theta
is a
positive, automorphic potential for the Kähler metric

\tilde{\omega}
(
[
OV3
]
).

Example 2.15:
Some examples of Vaisman manifolds:

(i)

Diagonal Hopf manifolds
({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\backslash 0)/\langle A\rangle
where
A
is semi-simple and with eigenvalues
\alpha_{i}
of absolute value
>1
,
[
GO
,
OV4
]
.

(ii)

Elliptic complex surfaces (see
[
B
]
for the complete classification of Vaisman compact surfaces; see also
[
VVO
]
).

(iii)

All compact submanifolds of a Vaisman manifold (
[
Ve1
]
).

Remark 2.16:
The class of Vaisman manifolds is strict: neither
the LCK Inoue surfaces, nor the non-diagonal
Hopf
manifolds can bear Vaisman metrics (
[
B
]
,
[
OV4
]
).

The metric dual
\theta^{\sharp}
of the Lee form is called

the Lee field
.

Proposition 2.17:
(
[
Vai1
,
Vai2
]
) Let
(M,g,I,\theta)
be a Vaisman manifold. Then the Lee field

\theta^{\sharp}
is Killing and holomorphic; moreover,
it commutes with
I\theta^{\sharp}
.

Denote by
\Sigma
the holomorphic 1-dimensional foliation generated by
\theta^{\sharp}
and
I\theta^{\sharp}
. It is called
the canonical foliation
(the motivation is given in the next theorem).

Theorem 2.18:

Let
M
be a compact Vaisman manifold, and

\Sigma\subset TM
its canonical foliation. Then:

(i)

\Sigma
is
independent from the choice of the Vaisman metric (
[
T3
]
).

(ii)

d^{c}\theta=\omega-\theta\wedge I\theta
(
[
Vai2
]
) and the exact (1,1)-form
\omega_{0}:=d^{c}\theta
is semi-positive
(
[
Ve1
]
). Therefore,
\Sigma=\ker\omega_{0}
,
and
\omega_{0}
is transversally Kähler with respect to
\Sigma
.

The following result is almost obvious. We give its proof for consistency.

Lemma 2.19:

Let
M
be a compact Vaisman manifold, and
G

the closure of the Lie group generated by the
action of the Lee and the anti-Lee fields on
M
.
Then
G
is a compact torus.

Proof:
Since the Lee and anti-Lee fields are acting on
M

by isometries (
2.2.2
),
the group
G
is a closed subgroup of the
isometry group of
M
, and the latter is compact.
Then
G
is compact; since it is the closure of an
abelian group, it is abelian.

3
Holomorphic tensors on LCK manifolds with potential

The purpose of this section is to prove

3.2
.
For its proof, we shall recall some facts related
to coherent sheaves.

3.1
Extension of reflexive coherent sheaves

Given a coherent sheaf
\cal F
over
X
, let

{\cal F}^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\cal F,{\cal O}_{X})
be
the dual sheaf
,
that is, the sheaf of module homomorphisms to the ring
of regular functions. The natural morphism of sheaves

{\cal F}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\cal F}^{**}
does not need to be an isomorphism:
for example, its kernel contains the torsion of
{\cal F}
.
A coherent sheaf is called
reflexive

if the natural map
{\cal F}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\cal F}^{**}
is an isomorphism.
For an introduction to the reflexive sheaves,
see
[
OSS
]
. Here we state some results which
are relevant to our work.

First of all, notice that
the sheaf
{\cal F}^{*}
is already reflexive
(
[
OSS
]
, Ch. II, Lemma 1.1.8).
Moreover, the singular set of a reflexive sheaf
over a normal variety has codimension
\geqslant 3
;
in particular, a reflexive sheaf over a complex
surface is locally free (
[
OSS
]
, Ch. II, 1.1.10).
Also, a reflexive sheaf of rank one over a smooth manifold
is locally free (
[
OSS
]
, Ch. II, Lemma 1.1.15).

The most important property of reflexive sheaves is
normality
.
Let
Z\subset X
be a subvariety of a normal
complex variety,
\operatorname{codim}_{X}Z\geqslant 2
.
Consider the open embedding map

j:\;X\backslash Z\hookrightarrow X
, and let
j^{*}
and
j_{*}
be the
sheaf pullback and pushforward, For any sheaf
{\cal F}
, there exists
a natural sheaf morphism
{\cal F}{\>\longrightarrow\>}j_{*}j^{*}{\cal F}
taking a section
of
{\cal F}
to its restriction to
X\backslash Z
. A coherent sheaf

{\cal F}
is
normal
if
the natural map
{\cal F}{\>\longrightarrow\>}j_{*}j^{*}{\cal F}
is an isomorphism, for any
subvariety
Z\subset X
of codimension
\geqslant 2
.

The following theorem can be understood as
a sheaf version of the Hartogs extension
theorem.

Theorem 3.1:

A coherent sheaf
{\cal F}
over a normal complex variety
is normal if and only if it is reflexive.

Proof:
[
Se
, Proposition 7]
.

Theorem 3.2:

(
[
OV7
, Theorem 27.6]
)
Let
M
be a normal complex variety with isolated singularities,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M\geqslant 3
,
x\in M
a point,
and
M_{0}:=M\backslash\{x\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}M
.
Consider a reflexive sheaf
{\cal F}
on
M_{0}
.
Then
j_{*}{\cal F}
is a reflexive coherent sheaf.
Moreover,
j_{*}{\cal F}
is the unique coherent
reflexive sheaf on
M
which is isomorphic to
{\cal F}

on
M_{0}
.

Proof:
Uniqueness of a reflexive extension
follows from the fact that a coherent sheaf
{\cal F}
over a normal variety
is normal if and only if it is reflexive (
3.1
).
The existence of coherent extension of
{\cal F}
follows from

[
AS
, Proposition 6.1]
.

3.2
Invariance of holomorphic tensor fields

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.3:

Let
M
be a compact LCK manifold with potential,

\tilde{M}
its Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cover, considered as
an open algebraic cone, and

\Phi\in H^{0}(M,B)
be a holomorphic tensor field on
M
,
where
B=(\Omega^{1}M)^{\otimes k}\otimes TM^{\otimes l}
.
Denote by
\tilde{\Phi}

its lift to
\tilde{M}
, and let
{\cal G}
be the Zariski closure
of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
\tilde{M}
. Then
\tilde{\Phi}
is

{\cal G}
-invariant.

Proof. Step 1:
Consider a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on a finite-dimensional space
W
.
Then any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant vector
w\in W
is invariant under the
Zariski closure of
{\mathbb{Z}}
. This is why the statement
of
3.2

is not surprising. However, the space of
tensor fields on
\tilde{M}
is not
finite-dimensional, which makes
3.2

non-trivial. Denote by
{\mathfrak{m}}
the maximal ideal
of the origin in the closed algebraic cone
\tilde{M}_{c}
.
We think of the quotients
{\cal O}_{\tilde{M}_{c}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}

as of the spaces of jets of holomorphic (or algebraic)
functions. As in
[
OV8
]
,
we choose
\tilde{M}_{c}
normal, so that the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action
is extended to
\tilde{M}_{c}
. Then any

{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant jet
u\in{\cal O}_{\tilde{M}_{c}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}

is also
{\cal G}
-invariant.

Step 2:
From now on, we use the same letter
\Phi

to denote the lift of
\Phi
to
\tilde{M}
.
Consider
\Phi
as a

{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant section of the appropriate
tensor bundle
B=(\Omega^{1}M)^{\otimes k}\otimes TM^{\otimes l}
,
which is by construction
{\mathbb{Z}}
-equivariant.
Using
3.1
, we extend

B
to a reflexive coherent sheaf
B_{c}
on
\tilde{M}_{c}
.
Since coherent reflexive sheaves are normal
(
[
OSS
, Ch. II, Lemma 1.1.12]
,
[
Se
, Proposition 7]
), the section
\Phi

admits a holomorphic extension
to
\tilde{M}_{c}
, denoted as
\Phi_{c}\in H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},B_{c})
.
Consider the space
J_{B}^{k}:=\frac{B_{c}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1}B_{c}}

of
k
-jets of
B_{c}
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},B_{c})

preserves

H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}B_{c})\subset H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},B_{c})
.
Let
\Phi^{k}_{c}\in J_{B}^{k}
be the
k
-jet of
\tilde{\Phi}_{c}
.
Since
\Phi_{c}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant, and the space of
k
-jets
is finite-dimensional, the
k
-jet
\Phi^{k}_{c}

is
{\cal G}
-invariant. Let

\hat{B}_{c}:=\lim\limits_{\leftarrow}J_{B}^{k}

be the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion of
B_{c}
, and

\hat{\Phi}_{c}
the image of
\Phi_{c}
in this
completion. Since all
k
-jets
\Phi^{k}_{c}
are
{\cal G}
-invariant,
the completion
\hat{\Phi}_{c}
is also
{\cal G}
-invariant.
Since the ring of germs of holomorphic
functions on
\tilde{M}_{c}
in
c
is Noetherian
(
[
GN
, Chapter II, Theorem B.9]
), and
B_{c}
,
being reflexive, is torsion-free, the
completion map
H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},B_{c}){\>\longrightarrow\>}\hat{B}_{c}

is injective (
[
AM
, Theorem 10.17]
).
Therefore,
\Phi\in H^{0}(\tilde{M}_{c},B_{c})

is also
{\cal G}
-invariant.

4
Zariski closures and the Chevalley theorem

We recall the following famous theorem due to Chevalley.

Let
G\subset\operatorname{GL}(V)
be an algebraic
group, and
W=V^{\otimes k}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes l}

a tensor representation of
G
. A
G
-invariant
vector
v\in W
is called
a tensor invariant of
G
.
A point
x\in{\mathbb{P}}W
is called

a projective tensor invariant of
G

if it is
G
-invariant.

Theorem 4.1: (Chevalley theorem,

[
M
]
).

An algebraic
group is uniquely determined by the set
of its projective tensor invariants.

When an algebraic group is
reductive
(over
{\mathbb{C}}
, a group
is reductive if and only if it has a compact real form),
a stronger version of Chevalley theorem is available.

Theorem 4.2:

A reductive algebraic
group is uniquely determined by the set
of its tensor invariants.

Proof:
[
D
, Proposition 3.1 (c)]
.

We are going to prove the following result, used further
on to determine the Lee field action on a Vaisman manifold.

Theorem 4.3:

Let
A\in\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
be a diagonal linear operator
with the eigenvalues
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
,
and
A_{1}
an operator which is diagonal in the
same basis, with the eigenvalues
|\alpha_{1}|,...,|\alpha_{n}|
.
Denote by
{\cal G}
the Zariski closure of the
group
\langle A\rangle
in
\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Then
{\cal G}
contains
A_{1}
.

Proof:
Since the operator
A
is diagonalizable,
the algebraic closure of
\langle A\rangle

is a commutative group which contains only
semisimple elements. By

[
BT
, Proposition 1.5]
,
the connected component of
{\cal G}
is isomorphic
to
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{d}
, hence
{\cal G}
is reductive.
Then
4

implies that
{\cal G}
is the set of all

g\in\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
which preserve all

A
-invariant vectors

w\in W=V^{\otimes k}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes l}
.
The eigenvalues of
A
on
W

are products of
k
instances of
\alpha_{i}

and
l
instances of
\alpha_{i}^{-1}
.
The eigenvectors
w\in W

are
w=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}z_{m_{i}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{l}\zeta_{n_{i}}
, where

z_{n_{i}}
are the eigenvectors in
V
with the
eigenvalues
\alpha_{n_{i}}

and
\zeta_{m_{i}}
are the eigenvectors in
V^{*}
with the
eigenvalues
\alpha_{m_{i}}^{-1}
. Then

A(w)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{n_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\alpha^{-1}_{m_{i}}
.
The eigenvector
w
is
A
-invariant
if and only if
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{n_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\alpha^{-1}_{m_{i}}=1
.
This implies
\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\alpha_{n_{i}}|\prod_{i=1}^{l}|\alpha^{-1}_{m_{i}}|=1
,
hence
w
is
A_{1}
-invariant as well.

5
Holomorphic tensors on Vaisman manifolds

Let
M
be a Vaisman manifold, and
\theta^{\sharp}
its Lee
field. Then
\theta^{\sharp}
and
I(\theta^{\sharp})

are holomorphic Killing fields (
2.2.2
).
The closure
G
of the group

e^{t_{1}\theta^{\sharp}+t_{2}I(\theta^{\sharp})}
in the group of isometries of

M
is compact and commutative, hence it is a compact torus
(
2.2.2
).

Theorem 5.1:

Let
M
be a compact Vaisman manifold,

\Phi\in H^{0}(M,B)

a holomorphic tensor, where
B=(\Omega^{1}M)^{\otimes k}\otimes TM^{\otimes l}
,
and
G
the smallest closed Lie group containing the
flows generated by the Lee and the anti-Lee fields.
Then
\Phi
is
G
-invariant.

Proof. Step 1:
Choose a Vaisman metric on
M
with LCK rank 1, and
let
\tilde{M}
be the corresponding
Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cover of
M
, which is considered
as an open algebraic cone. Let
{\cal G}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{M})
be
the Zariski closure of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-action. Then
\Phi
is

{\cal G}
-invariant by
3.2
.
To finish the proof of
5
,
it would suffice to show that
G\subset{\cal G}
;
since
{\cal G}
is closed, this would follow if
we prove that the Lee field
\theta^{\sharp}

is tangent to
{\cal G}
.

Step 2:

Let
M{\>\longrightarrow\>}H
be a holomorphic embedding of
M
to a
diagonal Hopf manifold. By
2.2.2
, this embedding
commutes with the group generated by the
Lee and the anti-Lee flow, hence it would suffice
to show that
\theta^{\sharp}
is tangent to
{\cal G}
when
M=H
.
It remains to prove
5

assuming that
M
is a Hopf manifold. For this purpose,
we compute the groups
G
and
{\cal G}
for
a diagonal Hopf manifold. This can be done explicitly
using the expression for the Vaisman metric on a Hopf manifold
obtained in
2.2.1
via the pseudoconvex shells.

Step 3:
The rank 2 algebra generated by
the Lee and anti-Lee flows on a Vaisman
manifold
M
is uniquely determined by
the complex structure on
M
(
2.2.2
).
We express the Lee flow on a Hopf manifold
using a Vaisman structure
we shall construct explicitly, and
prove that it belongs to
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
.
This would imply that the original Lee flow
also belongs to
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
.

Let
H=\frac{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\backslash 0}{{\langle A\rangle}}
,
where
A\in\operatorname{GL}(n,{\mathbb{C}})
is a diagonal linear contraction with eigenvalues

\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
. Let
A_{1}
be the matrix which is
diagonal in the same basis as
A
, and has eigenvalues

|\alpha_{i}|
. Then
\vec{r}:=\log A_{1}

is a holomorphic contraction vector field,
and
e^{t\vec{r}}
is an
A
-invariant
contraction flow. The sphere
S^{2n-1}

is a pseudoconvex shell compatible with this contraction flow.
By
[
OV4
]
, we obtain a
e^{t\log A_{1}}
-automorphic
plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
. We are going to prove
that
\varphi
is
A
-automorphic.

Consider the diagonal
{\operatorname{U}}(1)^{n}
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
The plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
is
{\operatorname{U}}(1)^{n}
-invariant
because
\vec{r}
and
S^{2n-1}
are
{\operatorname{U}}(1)^{n}
-invariant.
Since
A\in{\operatorname{U}}(1)^{n}\cdot A_{1}
,
and
\varphi
is
A_{1}
-automorphic, it is
A
-automorphic.
Therefore,
\frac{dd^{c}\varphi}{\varphi}
defines a Vaisman metric
on
H
.
By definition, the Lee flow maps the level sets
of
\varphi
to the level sets of
\varphi
, with

\varphi(e^{t\theta^{\sharp}}z)=t\varphi(z)
.
By construction of
\varphi
,
the same is true for
e^{-t\log A_{1}}
, that is,

\varphi(e^{-t\log A_{1}}z)=t\varphi(z)
.
Therefore,
\theta^{\sharp}=-\log A_{1}
, where
\log A_{1}

is the diagonal matrix with the eigenvalues
\log|\alpha_{i}|

in the same basis. This is the Lee flow for the
Vaisman metric
\frac{dd^{c}\varphi}{\varphi}
.

The vector field

\log A_{1}
belongs to
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
,
because any power of
A_{1}
fixes all
tensor invariants of
{\cal G}

by
4
,
hence the corresponding 1-parametric
sugroup also belongs to
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
;
this gives
\log A_{1}\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
.

6
An application: the Kodaira dimension of Vaisman manifolds

Definition 6.1:
Let
M
be a compact complex manifold.
Its
pluricanonical bundle
is
the tensor power
K_{M}^{n}=K_{M}^{\otimes n}
,
n\geqslant 0
.
The
Kodaira dimension

\kappa(M)

is defined as
\kappa(M):=\limsup_{n}\frac{\log(\dim H^{0}(K_{M}^{n}))}{\log n}
.

Remark 6.2:
The Kodaira dimension is equal to
-\infty
if
H^{0}(K_{M}^{n})=0

for almost all
n>0
, and to 0 if
\dim H^{0}(K_{M}^{n})
is bounded, but non-zero
for infinitely many
n
.
If the function
n\mapsto\dim H^{0}(K_{M}^{n})

grows as a polynomial of degree
d
, the Kodaira dimension
of
M
is
d
.

Let
M
be a Vaisman manifold,

G
the closure of the group generated by the
Lee and the anti-Lee action,
\tilde{M}

a Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-covering of
M
, and
\tilde{G}
the lift
of
G
to
\tilde{M}
. Denote by
{\mathfrak{G}}
the Zariski
closure of
\tilde{G}
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{M})
, considered
as an algebraic variety (
[
OV8
]
).

Since
\tilde{G}
acts on
\tilde{M}
by homotheties,
and contains contractions, there is an open
set
U\subset{\mathfrak{G}}
in the connected component
of
{\mathfrak{G}}
such that for all
\gamma^{\prime}\in U
,
the quotient
M^{\prime}:=\frac{\tilde{M}}{\langle\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}

is LCK (
[
OV8
]
). The automorphism
\gamma
commutes with the action
of the group generated by the Lee and the anti-Lee
field, hence by
[
KO
]

M^{\prime}
is Vaisman.
1
1

1

As
shown in
[
KO
]
, an LCK manifold which admits
a holomorphic conformal
{\mathbb{C}}
-action is Vaisman, assuming
that this action cannot be lifted to an isometry of the
Kähler cover.

By
[
BT
, Proposition 1.5]
,
the connected component of
{\mathfrak{G}}
is
isomorphic to
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{k}
.
It is not hard to see that the union of all one-dimensional
closed subgroups is dense in any algebraic group, hence

U
contains a dense subset of
\gamma^{\prime}
which are
contained in a subgroup
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\subset{\mathfrak{G}}
.
Since
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}/\langle\gamma^{\prime}\rangle
is an elliptic
curve, the Vaisman manifold

M^{\prime}=\frac{\tilde{M}}{\langle\gamma^{\prime}\rangle}

is elliptically fibered.
2
2

2

Ellipticaly fibered Vaisman manifolds
are called
quasi-regular
.
As shown in

[
OV1
]
, the leaf space
X
is a
projective orbifold.

Theorem 6.3:

Let
M
be a Vaisman manifold,

M^{\prime}
its quasi-regular deformation obtained
above, and
X
the corresponding leaf space,
which is a projective orbifold. Then

\kappa(M)=\kappa(M^{\prime})=\kappa(X)
,
where
\kappa
denotes the Kodaira dimension.

Proof. Step 1:
The equality

\kappa(M^{\prime})=\kappa(X)
is clear from the adjunction
formula: since
\pi:\;M^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
is an elliptic
fibration, one has

H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})=H^{0}(K_{X}^{k})
for all
k
.

Step 2:

By
5
,
any section
h
of the pluricanonical bundle
K_{M}^{k}
on
M

is
{\mathfrak{G}}
-invariant. Then, for any

\gamma^{\prime}\in U
, the lift of
h
to
\tilde{M}

is
\gamma^{\prime}
-invariant, hence can be obtained
as a pullback of a section of
K_{M^{\prime}}^{k}
under
the quotient map
\tilde{M}{\>\longrightarrow\>}M^{\prime}
.
This defines an injective map

\Psi:\;H^{0}(K_{M}^{k}){\>\longrightarrow\>}H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})
.

Step 3:
To finish the proof
of
6
it remains
to show that
\Psi:\;H^{0}(K_{M}^{k}){\>\longrightarrow\>}H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})

is surjective. This would follow if we show that
any section
h^{\prime}\in H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})

is
{\mathfrak{G}}
-invariant. Since

H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})=H^{0}(K_{X}^{k})

(Step 1), the
{\mathfrak{G}}
-invariance
of
h^{\prime}
would follow if we prove that
the corresponding section of
K_{X}^{k}
is
invariant under the natural action
of
{\mathfrak{G}}
on
X
.

Recall that
the pluricanonical representation

is the natural action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
on
H^{0}(K_{X}^{k})
.
Since
X
is an orbifold, its singularities are klt
(
[
P
, Proposition 1.2.1]
).
By
[
KK
, Theorem 10.61]
,
the image of the pluricanonical representation
is finite; since
{\mathfrak{G}}
is connected,
it acts on
H^{0}(K_{X}^{k})
trivially.
This gives
H^{0}(K_{M}^{k})=H^{0}(K_{M^{\prime}}^{k})=H^{0}(K_{X}^{k})
.

As an application, we outline
the proof of the deformational stability
of Kodaira dimension for Vaisman manifolds.

Conjecture 6.4:

Let
M_{t},t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, be a smooth family of compact
Vaisman manifolds. Then the Kodaira dimension
of
M_{t}
is constant.

Sketch of a proof:

We sketch a proof of this conjecture, reducing it
to
6
below.

Let
\tilde{M}_{t}
be the family of open algebraic
cones obtained from each
M_{t}
by taking
a Kähler
{\mathbb{Z}}
-cover; this is possible to do by

[
OV1
]
. Each
\tilde{M}_{t}
is equipped
with a natural algebraic structure by
[
OV8
]
.

What follows is a conjecture, which most likely
can be proven by the methods used in
[
OV8
]
.

Let
\tilde{M}_{t},t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
be a smooth family of open algebraic cones.
As shown in
[
OV8
]
, for an
appropriate choice of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\tilde{M}_{t}

the quotient
\tilde{M}_{t}/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is isomorphic to a
complex projective orbifold
X_{t}
; this isomorphism
is extended to an isomorphism between
\tilde{M}_{t}

and
\operatorname{Tot}^{\circ}(L_{t})
,
where
L_{t}
is an ample line bundle over
X_{t}
.

Conjecture 6.5:

Let
\tilde{M}_{t},t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
be a smooth family of open algebraic cones.
Then for any sufficiently small open
subset
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, the choice of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action
as above can be performed in such a way that
the family
(X_{t},L_{t})

depends smoothly on
t\in U
.

Now, by
6
,
the Kodaira dimension of
M_{t}
is equal
to the Kodaira dimension of
X_{t}
, which
is constant, as shown by Siu in

[
Si
]
.

Typical representations, parabolic induction and the inertial local Langlands correspondence
Peter Latham

Abstract
. We prove a result which provides a link between the decomposition of parabolically induced representations and the Bushnell–Kutzko theory of typical representations. As an application, we show that there exists a well-defined inertial Langlands correspondence which respects the monodromy action of L L -parameters, under some standard conjectures regarding the local Langlands correspondence. To allow for potential applications of this inertial Langlands correspondence, we also provide a complete construction of the set of typical representations, giving a parametrization of these in terms of the structure of the Bruhat–Tits building of G G .

Introduction

In this paper, we show that there exists a well-defined “inertial local Langlands correspondence”, as long as one assumes some standard conjectures about the existence and properties of the local Langlands correspondence. Recall that the local Langlands correspondence is a conjectural parametrization of the irreducible representations of a
p

G
in terms of
L
-parameters
, which are objects closely related to continuous Galois representations (see Section
5
for a precise definition).

The inertial Langlands correspondence is a modification of the local Langlands correspondence which tracks only the information which occurs “on the compact level”—it replaces
L
-parameters with the restrictions to the compact inertia subgroup, and replaces irreducible

The existence of the inertial Langlands correspondence not only provides an affirmative answer to the natural question of whether the many available explicit constructions translate over the local Langlands correspondence, but is also of arithmetic significance. Most notably, the inertial Langlands correspondence is a key pre-requisite to the formation of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture
[
BM02

]
, which is a central problem in modern algebraic number theory, the partial solution of which has already found numerous applications—for example, Kisin’s proof of (many cases of) the 2-dimensional Fontaine–Mazur conjecture
[
Kis09
]
is largely based on establishing the corresponding cases of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture.

Each of these applications relies on the inertial Langlands correspondence for
\mathbf{GL}_{N}(F)
, the existence and fine properties of which have been understood for quite some time. However, if one wishes to investigate similar questions within the more general context of the Langlands programme for an arbitrary
p
-adic group, then one requires a similar understanding of the inertial Langlands correspondence for such groups. This is something of a problematic topic within the literature—the term “inertial Langlands correspondence” actually refers to
two
(closely related) folklore conjectures. The author is not aware of an exposition of either anywhere in the literature, and in fact one of the two is not provably well-defined, given the current state of the art.

To explain this distinction, recall that an
L
-parameter for
G
consists of a continuous representation of the Weil–Deligne group
W_{F}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
, taking image in the Langlands dual group
{}^{L}G
. The action of
\mathbb{C}
contained within such a representation is the
monodromy action
of the
L
-parameter; the role of the monodromy action is to distinguish between distinct irreducible representations with equivalent supercuspidal supports.

•

The most basic form of the inertial Langlands correspondence is a canonical map which assigns to each Bushnell–Kutzko type a map
I_{F}\rightarrow{}^{L}G
, where
I_{F}\subset W_{F}
is the inertia group. The existence of this map is not a problem; given the existence of the local Langlands correspondence, together with a construction of types, deducing the existence of this inertial correspondence is an exercise in the definitions. The issue here is that one forgets the monodromy action, which leads to a somewhat coarse correspondence—for applications such as the Breul–Mézard conjecture, one must keep track of the monodromy action. For this reason, we refer to this construction as the
weak inertial Langlands correspondence

•

One then expects the existence of the “strong” inertial Langlands correspondence. This is entirely conjectural aside from the cases of
\mathbf{GL}_{N}(F)

Theorem 2

This is intriguing in that, while not allowing for a complete description of the Jordan–Hölder series of a parabolically induced representation, it does allow for a combinatorial description of the irreducible components of such a representation, in terms of sets of typical representations.

In order to apply either this description of (semisimplified) parabolically induced representations, or to make a useful application of the above inertial local Langlands correspondence, one requires a workable description of the various typical representations. Our final result provides just this, showing that the isomorphism classes of typical representations admit a combinatorial-geometric description via Bruhat–Tits theory. \textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F},G^{\vee})}

Much of the content of this paper will be focused on imposing various equivalence relations on the set of irreducible representations of
G
via this diagram, and understanding the relationships between these diagrams. In particular, the local Langlands correspondence is expected to give a finite-to-one parametrization
\mathrm{rec}:\mathrm{Irr}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
; the finite fibres of this map give a partition of
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
into
L
-packets
. One can alternatively consider the fibres of
\varphi\circ\mathrm{rec}
, where
\varphi
is any of the maps in the above diagram. Many of our questions will be aimed towards giving a precise description of the fibres of the resulting equivalence relation; our ultimate goal is the construction of a set of objects which naturally parametrize the fibres of
\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\circ\mathrm{rec}
, which is essentially achieved by chasing around the diagram in the opposite direction.

The precise role of the action of
\mathbb{C}
associated to an
L
-parameter will play a particularly key role throughout the paper; we refer to this action as the
monodromy
of an
L
-parameter.

There are another family of useful maps on these cohomology sets which will be of use to us. Given a closed subgroup
\mathbf{H}
of
\mathbf{G}
, there is often a dual embedding
\iota:H^{\vee}\hookrightarrow G^{\vee}
, which extends to an embedding
\iota:{}^{L}H\hookrightarrow{}^{L}G
. Via this embedding, one obtains a pushforward
\iota_{*}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,H^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,G^{\vee})
whenever
\Gamma
is one of the above groups. While one requires some hypotheses on
\mathbf{H}
in order to guarantee that the dual embedding works as desired, it is known that one can construct such a dual embedding when
\mathbf{H}
is either a torus or a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
; this will be sufficient for our purposes. A description of the construction of these dual embeddings is given in
[
Kal19
, 5.1]
.

6.
Kaletha’s correspondence

In
[
Kal19
]
, Kaletha directly constructs an extremely strong candidate for the image under the local Langlands correspondence of the set of
regular supercuspidal representations
of
G
. While it is
not
known that Kaletha’s construction
is
the local Langlands correspondence (due to the fact that it is currently unclear exactly which properties should characterize the correspondence for arbitrary groups), it is widely expected that this is the case. For the remainder of the paper, we assume that this is so.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G
of inertial support
\mathfrak{s}
, and let
(J(\Sigma),\lambda_{\Sigma})
be an
\mathfrak{s}
-type arising from a datum
\Sigma=(\vec{G},M^{0},u,\sigma,\vec{r},\vec{\phi})
as in Section
4
. We say that
\pi
is
regular
if the depth-zero component
\sigma
is regular in the following sense: the restriction of
\sigma
to
G_{u,0}^{0}
is a sum of pairwise
G_{[u],0}^{0}
-conjugate representations; fix one such component
\sigma_{0}
. Then
\sigma_{0}
is inflated from a cuspidal irreducible representation of
G_{u,0:0+}^{0}
, a finite group of Lie type; we say that
\sigma
is regular if
\sigma_{0}
is a regular Deligne–Lusztig representation, i.e. there exists a minisotropic maximal torus
\mathsf{T}\subset G_{u,0:0+}^{0}
, and a character
\vartheta
of
\mathsf{T}
in general position, such that
\sigma_{0}=\pm\mathrm{R}_{\mathsf{T}}\theta
.

Kaletha shows that, when
\pi
is a regular
supercuspidal
representation of
G
, the information in the datum
\Sigma
may be encoded in a
regular elliptic pair

(S,\Theta)
; this is a pair consisting of an elliptic torus
S
, together with a character
\Theta
of
S
which satisfies a number of properties. In doing so, Kaletha obtains a canonical bijection between the set of regular supercuspidal representations of
G
and the set of
G
-conjugacy classes of regular elliptic pairs
(S,\Theta)
, which we denote by
\pi_{S,\Theta}\leftrightarrow(S,\Theta)
. For the most part, we are able to treat this construction as a black box, although we do require one basic property: by following Kaletha’s construction of the bijection
(S,\Theta)\rightarrow\pi_{S,\Theta}
, which proceeds by first associating a datum
\Sigma_{S,\Theta}
to
(S,\Theta)
, it is apparent that if
\chi
is a character of
G
such that
(S,\Theta\otimes\chi)
is also a regular elliptic pair, then one has
\pi_{S,\Theta\otimes\chi}=\pi_{S,\Theta}\otimes\chi
. In particular, this is always the case when
\chi
is an unramified character of
G
.

We write
\mathrm{Cusp}(G)
for the set of isomorphism classes of regular supercuspidal representations, i.e. the set of isomorphism classes of representations of the form
\pi_{S,\Theta}
for some regular elliptic pair
(S,\Theta)
. More generally, we will make use of the following definition throughout the remainder of the paper.

Definition 6.2
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is
regular
if its supercuspidal support
\mathrm{sc}(\pi)
is a regular representation of some Levi subgroup of
G
.

We write
\mathrm{Reg}(G)
for the set of isomorphism classes of regular representations of
G
.

Remark 6.3
.

This is a slightly non-standard notion of regularity within the context of the Langlands programme. More typically, there is a notion of a regular
L
-parameter, and one says that
\pi
is regular if the local Langlands correspondence assigns to it a regular
L
-parameter. It is widely expected that representations which are regular in our sense should also be regular in this sense, and our notion of regularity is much more natural from the
p
-adic group side of the correspondence, which is where much of our work will take place.

The major result of
[
Kal19
]
is the construction of a local Langlands correspondence for the regular supercuspidal representations.

Theorem 6.4

(
[
Kal19
]
)
.

There exists a natural map
\mathrm{rec}_{G}:\mathrm{Cusp}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{F},G^{\vee})
.

As discussed above, the remainder of the paper assumes that this map
\mathrm{rec}_{G}

is
the local Langlands correspondence.

Remark 6.5
.

Notice that the image of this map is contained in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{F},G^{\vee})
,
not
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
—in other words, every regular supercuspidal representation of
G
corresponds to a parameter with a trivial monodromy action.

As a consequence of this construction, one easily obtains an inertial Langlands correspondence. We write
\mathrm{Type}(G)
for the set of equivalence classes of cuspidal Kim–Yu types which are contained in regular supercuspidal representations, i.e. the set of pairs of the form
(J(\Sigma),\lambda_{\Sigma})
where
\Sigma
is some datum with regular Deligne–Lusztig depth-zero component. There is therefore a canonical surjective map
\mathrm{Cusp}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
, the image of which we denote by
\mathrm{Type}_{\mathrm{sc}}(G)
.

Corollary 6.6
.

There exists a natural map
\mathrm{iner}_{G}:\mathrm{Type}_{\mathrm{sc}}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F},G^{\vee})
such that the following diagram commutes.

\textstyle{\mathrm{Cusp}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{rec}_{G}}

Proof.

Given some
(J,\lambda)
representing a class in
\mathrm{Type}(G)
, since
(J,\lambda)

\mathfrak{s}
-type for some inertial equivalence class

∎

Remark 7.3

We refer to

8.
Typical representations

In order to construct our strong form of the inertial Langlands correspondence, we will need to work with a slightly more general class of objects than
\mathfrak{s}
-types.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
(J,\lambda)
be a pair consisting of an irreducible representation
\lambda
of a compact open subgroup
J
of
G
. We say that
(J,\lambda)
is
\mathfrak{s}
-typical if, whenever
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
contains
\lambda
, one has
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
.

Remark 8.2
.

In other words, this is close to asking that
\mathrm{Rep}_{\lambda}(G)
is a subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
(as opposed to being equal to
\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
, as would be the case if
(J,\lambda)
were an
\mathfrak{s}
-type). However, showing that this is equivalent to the above definition encounters some difficulties when one considers inadmissible representations of
G
, since if
(J,\lambda)
is
\mathfrak{s}
-typical but not an
\mathfrak{s}
-type, then
\mathrm{Rep}_{\lambda}(G)
is not a Serre subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
—in this case
\mathrm{Rep}_{\lambda}(G)
is not closed under formation of subquotients.

As long as one has a construction of types, it is easy to see that many typical representations exist.

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose that
(J,\lambda)
is an
\mathfrak{s}
-type and that
K\supset J
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
.

(i)

For any irreducible component
\tau
of
\mathrm{Ind}_{J}^{K}\ \lambda
, the pair
(K,\tau)
is
\mathfrak{s}
-typical.

(ii)

If
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)
contains
\lambda
, then there exists a pair
(K,\tau)
as above such that
\pi|K
contains
\tau
.

Proof.

Each of these is a straightforward consequence of Frobenius reciprocity.
∎

In particular, one obtains a collection of typical representations which are of the form
(K,\tau)
where
K\subset G
is a maximal compact subgroup and
\tau|_{J(\Sigma)}
contains
\lambda_{\Sigma}
for some datum
\Sigma
such that
J(\Sigma)\subset K
. We denote the set of
G
-conjugacy classes of such typical representations by
\mathrm{Typ}(G)
. Notice that there is a canonical map
\mathrm{Typ}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
, given by mapping a typical representation to its underlying equivalence class of types.

While there is not a canonical map
\mathrm{Irr}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Typ}(G)
, there
is
a canonical map from
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
to the power set
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{Typ}(G)
, namely the map which sends
\pi
to the set
X_{\pi}
of
G
-conjugacy classes of
\mathfrak{s}
-typical representations
(K,\tau)
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{K}(\tau,\pi)\neq 0
, as
K
ranges over a set of representatives of the conjugacy classes of maximal compact subgroups of
G
. Moreover, the canonical map
\mathrm{Typ}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
clearly extends to a partial map
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{Typ}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
, defined on the subset of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{Typ}(G)
consisting of sets of the form
X_{\pi}
.

9.
The inertial Langlands correspondence

With the above discussion in mind, we write
\varphi
for the canonical map
\mathrm{Reg}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{Typ}(G)
, defined by
\varphi(\pi)=X_{\pi}
. We write
\mathrm{Typ}_{\varphi}(G)
for the image of
\varphi
.

The key to constructing a “strong” form of the inertial Langlands correspondence is the following result.

Theorem 9.1
.

Suppose that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Reg}(G)
are such that
\mathrm{sc}(\pi_{1})=\mathrm{sc}(\pi_{2})
and
\varphi(\pi_{1})=\varphi(\pi_{2})
. Then
\pi_{1}\simeq\pi_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
(M,\rho)
denote a representative of
\mathrm{sc}(\pi_{1})=\mathrm{sc}(\pi_{2})
, and choose a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
with Levi factor

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
occur as irreducible subquotients of the representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}\ \rho
. It is clear that
\pi_{1}\simeq\pi_{2}
when

, so we may assume that
M
is a proper Levi subgroup of
G
.

Fix a datum
\Sigma=(\vec{G},M^{0},u,\sigma,\vec{r},\vec{\phi})
such that
(J(\Sigma),\lambda_{\Sigma})
is an
[M,\rho]_{M}

, we may associate to
\mathscr{F}
a Levi subgroup

of
G
lying in the
G
-orbit of

; without loss of generality we may take

. Fix an apartment
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{B}(G)
containing
\mathscr{F}
, corresponding to a maximal split torus
S\subset G
.

Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
denote the vertices of
\mathscr{F}
, so that one has

Since the type

Again using the fact that

Given

\varphi(\pi)=X

\mathrm{iner}_{G}(X)=\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)
.

Suppose that
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Reg}(G)
also satisfies
\varphi(\pi^{\prime})=X
. We claim that
\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})=\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)
. Since
\varphi(\pi)=\varphi(\pi^{\prime})
, projecting along the canonical map
\mathrm{Typ}_{\varphi}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
shows that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are inertially equivalent. In other words, if
(M,\rho)
is a representative of
\mathrm{sc}(\pi)
, then there exists an unramified character
\omega
of
M
such that
(M,\rho\otimes\omega)
is a representative of
\mathrm{sc}(\pi^{\prime})
. Since
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Reg}(G)
, by definition we may find regular elliptic pairs
(S,\Theta)
and
(S^{\prime},\Theta^{\prime})
in
M
such that
\rho=\pi_{S,\Theta}
and
\rho^{\prime}=\pi_{S^{\prime},\Theta^{\prime}}
; since
\rho^{\prime}=\rho\otimes\omega=\pi_{S,\Theta}\otimes\omega=\pi_{S,\Theta\omega}
, it follows that we may without loss of generality take
S=S^{\prime}
and
\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta\omega
.

Now consider the
L
-parameter
\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})
. By construction, the semisimplification of this is precisely the pushforward
\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{M}(\rho^{\prime})=\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{M}(\rho\otimes\omega)
along an appropriate embedding
\iota:{}^{L}M\hookrightarrow{}^{L}G
. Since both
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho\otimes\omega
are constructed from regular elliptic pairs defined on the same torus
S\subset M
, one finds that both
\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{M}(\rho)
and
\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{M}(\rho^{\prime})
have image in
{}^{L}S\hookrightarrow{}^{L}M\hookrightarrow{}^{L}G
.

Given an element
a
of the image of
\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})
, one may take the Jordan decomposition
a=su
with
s
semisimple and
u
unipotent, so that
s
is contained in the image of
\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{M}(\rho^{\prime})
, which is an abelian group. If
a=\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})(w)
, then
a
must commute with the semisimple element
b=\iota_{*}\mathrm{rec}_{T}(\omega)(w)
, and one finds that
(sb)u=(bs)u=u(bs)=u(sb)
is a Jordan decomposition of

. In particular, this implies that
\mathrm{red}_{T}(\omega)
extends to a 1-cocycle
c_{\omega}:W_{F}^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\vee}
which is trivial on both
I_{F}
(since
\omega
is unramified) and
\mathbb{C}
. Given a 1-cocycle
f
representing the class
\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)
, the pointwise product
fc_{\omega}
is a 1-cocycle whose cohomology class is independent of the choice of
f
; in this way we obtain a class
\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)\cdot c_{\omega}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
; by the above observations on the Jordan decomposition, we see that one must have
\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)\cdot c_{\omega}=\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})
.

We may therefore assume without loss of generality that
\mathrm{sc}(\pi)=\mathrm{sc}(\pi^{\prime})
, in which case the assumption that
\varphi(\pi)=\varphi(\pi^{\prime})
allows us to apply Theorem
9.1
to deduce that
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}
. As argued above, changing the supercuspidal support of
\pi^{\prime}
by an unramified twist does not affect
\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi^{\prime})
, and so we conclude that
\mathrm{Res}_{I_{F}^{\prime}}^{W_{F}^{\prime}}\mathrm{rec}_{G}(\pi)
is independent of the choice of
\pi
. In particular, this gives us our definition of
\mathrm{iner}_{G}:\mathrm{Typ}_{\varphi}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
, from which the commutativity of the first diagram is immediate.

The commutativity of the second diagram follows easily from Hypothesis
7.1
, along with the fact that the canonical map
\mathrm{Typ}_{\varphi}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{Type}(G)
simply maps each typical representation to its underlying type.

Remarks 9.3
.

One should note that, due to the hypotheses on
p
which are in force throughout the paper, we only claim to establish the existence of this inertial correspondence for the so-called
essentially tame representations
(which should be those corresponding to
L
-parameters for which the image of the wild inertia subgroup is abelian). It is worth pointing out the precise extent to which these hypotheses are required for the above proof to work. Most significantly, they guarantee the existence of
\mathfrak{s}
-types for each
\mathfrak{s}
; however, the existence of
\mathfrak{s}
-types is known even for wildly ramified representations in a number of cases, such as for general or special linear groups
[
BK93
,
BK94
]
, inner forms of general linear groups
[
SS08
]
, and classical groups when
p\neq 2

[
Ste08
]
. Once one has a construction of types, the major thing that one needs to know in order to follow the template of the above proofs is that the type is associated to a point in the building, and that the Levi subgroup associated to this point should be equal to the Levi subgroup defining the corresponding inertial equivalence class; this is the case in the known constructions. The other point at which we make use of our hypotheses is when defining regular representations as representations with regular supercuspidal support in the sense of
[
Kal19
]
. Such an approach cannot be taken in the non-tame case, meaning that a more complicated approach to regularity must be taken; it seems unlikely that this would be the source of any major technical issues, however. In short, this appears to be an approach which should go through
mutatis mutandis
in any situation where one has a satisfactory construction of
\mathfrak{s}
-types for each inertial equivalence class
\mathfrak{s}
. At the time of writing, however, the only such available constructions which are uniform in appearance as the group
G
varies are those leading to tame representations.

Remarks 9.4
.

As mentioned during the introduction, this strong form of the inertial Langlands correspondence has been something of a problematic topic within the literature, with varying statements and a rather complex trail of references to be followed if one wishes to see a proof (which, ultimately, boils down to a lengthy but largely straightforward exercise in the Bernstein–Zelevinsky classification
[
BZ76
]
). The case of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(G)
is essentially treated as
[
SZ99
, Prop. 6.2]
, although this is presented in a somewhat different language;
[
BC09
, Prop. 6.5.3]
describes how one may translate it into a statement closer to the viewpoint taken in this paper, and such an approach is also discussed in
[
EG14
, §4.1]
.

However, while it appears to be a common expectation that such results should generalize beyond
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(F)
, to the author’s knowledge the only other situation in which this has been fully worked out is that of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}(F)
in an unpublished note by Will Conley. At best, there is a widely believed folklore conjecture which predicts that there should be a canonical map
\mathrm{Typ}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
(or, more likely, a map defined on some well-chosen subset of
\mathrm{Typ}(G)
). Such a result would be a considerable refinement of the results of this section, but this appears to be considerably beyond the reach of our current knowledge. In particular, any construction of such a map appears to have several serious pre-requisites:

(i)

one must have a detailed understanding of how parabolically induced representations decompose into irreducible components;

(ii)

one must have a detailed understanding of the set of typical representations; and

(iii)

one must know that there exists a very precise correspondence between the set of typical representations and the set of representations with a given inertial support.

The first of these, in particular, is a serious obstacle, with very little progress having been made beyond the Bernstein–Zelevinsky classification, apart from for certain families of representations of classical groups.

Our approach largely consists of the observation that, by being willing to work with sets of typical representations rather than singletons, it becomes much easier to show that the map
\mathrm{iner}_{G}
is well-defined, with this problem reducing to a clear problem about the representation theory of
G
, which we resolve as Theorem
9.1
.

Nonetheless, one would hope that, at least in a great many cases, it is actually possible to refine the map
\mathrm{Typ}_{\varphi}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
to a (partially defined) map
\mathrm{Typ}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{F}^{\prime},G^{\vee})
and work with typical representations one-by-one. It would be extremely interesting to investigate this in some explicit situations, such as for classical groups where many parabolically induced representations have known decompositions.

10.
An explicit construction of typical representations

With the results of the previous section, we have a satisfactory construction of an inertial Langlands correspondence. However, if one wishes to apply this correspondence, then one must understand precisely the set
\mathrm{Typ}(G)
of typical representations. In this final section, we provide a geometric construction of these typical representations, using Bruhat–Tits theory.

We fix some notation for the remainder of the paper. Let
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{B}(G)

[

From now on, let

be a point contained in the simplicial closure of

Proposition 10.5

Suppose that
(x,\zeta)

\mathrm{Ind}_{J(\Sigma)}^{K}\ \lambda_{\Sigma}

\mathrm{Ind}_{J(\Sigma_{x,\zeta})}\ \lambda_{\Sigma_{x,\zeta}}
for some pair

which is maximal with respect to
\preceq

Proof.

We freely make use of the results of
[
KY17
, §8]
and
[
Yu01
, §15]

is a compact subgroup of

which contains

is maximal with respect to

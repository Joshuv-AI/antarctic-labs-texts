Singular quasilinear convective systems involving variable exponents

Abstract
. The paper deals with the existence of solutions for quasilinear elliptic systems involving singular and convection terms with variable exponents. Our approach combines the sub-supersolutions method and Schauder’s fixed point theorem.



AMS Subject Classifications: 35J75; 35J48; 35J92

Keywords:
p(x)
-Laplacian, variable exponents, fixed point, singular system, gradient estimate, regularity.

1. Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

(N\geq 2)
be a bounded domain with
smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Given
p_{i}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),

1<p_{i}^{-}\leq p_{i}^{+}<N
with

\begin{array}[]{l}p_{i}^{-}=\inf_{x\in\Omega}p_{i}(x)\text{ \ and \ }p_{i}^{+}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}p_{i}(x),\end{array}

we deal with the following quasilinear elliptic system

(\mathrm{P})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p_{i}(x)}u_{i}=f_{i}(x,u_{1},u_{2},\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{2})\text{
\ in }\Omega\\
u_{i}>0\text{ \ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }u_{i}=0\text{ \ on }\partial\Omega,\text{ }i=1,2,\end{array}\right.

where
-\Delta_{p_{i}(x)}
stands for the
p_{i}(x)
-Laplacian differential
operator defined by

-\Delta_{p_{i}(x)}u_{i}=-div(|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\nabla u_{i}),\text{
for }u_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,p_{i}(x)}(\Omega).

The nonlinear terms
f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2},\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{2})
and
f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2},\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{2})
which are often expressed as
dealing with convection terms, are of Carathéodory type. Namely, for every
(s_{1},s_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\star})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, we assume
that
f_{i}(\cdot,s_{1},s_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is Lebesgue measurable in
\Omega
, and, for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
f_{i}(x,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is
continuous in
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\star})^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. A solution of
(\mathrm{P})
is
understood in the weak sense, that is, a pair
(u_{1},u_{2})\in W_{0}^{1,p_{1}(x)}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,p_{2}(x)}(\Omega)
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\nabla u_{i}\nabla\varphi_{i}\ dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(x,u_{1},u_{2},\nabla u_{1},\nabla u_{2})\varphi_{i}\ dx,

(1.1)

for all
\varphi_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,p_{i}(x)}(\Omega)
.

Our main purpose is to establish the existence and regularity of solutions
for quasilinear singular convective system
(\mathrm{P})
satisfying the
assumption:

(
H
{}_{f})

There exist constants
M_{i},m_{i}>0
, and
functions
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i},\bar{\gamma}_{i}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, such that

m_{i}s_{1}^{\alpha_{i}(x)}s_{2}^{\beta_{i}(x)}\leq f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leq M_{i}(s_{1}^{\alpha_{i}(x)}s_{2}^{\beta_{i}(x)}+\left|\xi_{1}\right|^{\gamma_{i}(x)}+\left|\xi_{2}\right|^{\bar{\gamma}_{i}(x)}),

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, for all
s_{1},s_{2}>0
and all
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

i=1,2
.

The dependence of the right hand side terms on the solution and its gradient
deprives system
(\mathrm{P})
of a variational structure. Thereby
variational methods are not applicable. Moreover, due to the presence of
convection terms, even the so called topological methods as
sub-supersolutions and fixed points technique cannot be directly implemented.
Another important feature in studying problem
(\mathrm{P})
is that the
nonlinearities can exhibit singularities when the variables
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
approach zero. This occur through the following condition

(
H
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})

|\alpha_{i}^{\mp}|+|\beta_{i}^{\mp}|<p_{i}^{-}-1,

and

0\leq\min\{\gamma_{i}^{-},\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{-}\}\leq\max\{\gamma_{i}^{+},\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{+}\}<p_{i}^{-}-1,

where

\alpha_{i}^{\mp}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{i}^{-}&\text{if }\alpha_{i}(\cdot)>0\\
\alpha_{i}^{+}&\text{if }\alpha_{i}(\cdot)<0\end{array}\right.\text{ }\beta_{i}^{\mp}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta_{i}^{-}&\text{if }\beta_{i}(\cdot)>0\\
\beta_{i}^{+}&\text{if }\beta_{i}(\cdot)<0\end{array}\right.,\text{ }i=1,2.

Precisely, singularities appear in system whenever one of the exponents at
least is negative, that is,
\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}<0
. This represents a major hurdle to overcome. This difficulty is
heightened by the very emphasized singularity character of
(\mathrm{P})

that stems from
(
H
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})
when
\alpha_{i}^{\mp}+\beta_{i}^{\mp}<0
. In this case, hypothesis
(
H
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})
is strengthened by assuming

(
H̃
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})

If
\alpha_{i}^{\mp}+\beta_{i}^{\mp}<0
, we have

\left|\alpha_{i}^{\mp}\right|+\left|\beta_{i}^{\mp}\right|\leq\frac{1}{Np_{i}^{\prime+}},

and

0\leq\gamma_{i}(x)\leq\frac{p_{1}(x)}{Np_{i}^{\prime}(x)}\text{ \ and \ }0\leq\overline{\gamma}_{i}(x)\leq\frac{p_{2}(x)}{Np_{i}^{\prime}(x)},\text{
for }x\in\Omega.

Quasilinear convective system
(\mathrm{P})
has been rarely investigated in
the literature. Actually, according to our knowledge,
[
22
]
is the only
paper that has addressed this issue in the regular case, that is when all
exponents are positive. Existence result is obtained applying the recent
topological degree of Berkovits. The virtually non-existent works devoted to
the singular case of convective systems is partly due to the involvement of
the
p_{i}(x)
-Laplacian operator. This fact results in the lack of
properties such as homogeneity making it highly challenging task to
establish a control on solutions and especially on their gradient. When
p_{i}(x)
is reduced to be a constant,
-\Delta_{p_{i}(x)}
becomes the
well-known
p_{i}
-Laplacian operator. In this respect, by relying on the a
priori gradient estimate in
[
6
,
7
]
, the existence of solutions for
singular convective systems have been investigated in
[
8
,
10
]
. Still
in the context of constant exponents, for singular system
(\mathrm{P})

defined in whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we quote
[
16
]
while for the case of Neumann boundary condition
we refer to
[
15
]
. We also mention
[
2
]
focusing on a singular
system of type
(\mathrm{P})
corresponding to the semilinear case, that is
when
p_{i}(x)=2
(
i=1,2
).

When convection terms are canceled, singular system
(\mathrm{P})
was
recently examined in
[
1
,
3
]
. In this context, depending on the sign
of
\alpha_{i}(\cdot)
and
\beta_{i}(\cdot),
two complementary
structures for the system
(\mathrm{P})
appear: cooperative and competitive
structure (see
[
1
]
). Here, the important structural disparity of the
latter makes nonlinearities
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
(without gradient terms)
behaving in a drastically different way. This fact has led in
[
3
]
to
consider only the cooperative system involving logarithmic growth while in

[
1
]
, a separate study corresponding to each structure is required. We
emphasize that in the present work, neither cooperative nor competitive
structure on the system
(\mathrm{P})
is imposed. In fact, these both
complementary structures for the system
(\mathrm{P})
are handled
simultaneously without referring to them.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Assume (H
f
) holds. Then

\mathbf{(i)}
Under assumption
(
H
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})

with
\alpha_{i}^{\mp}+\beta_{i}^{\mp}>0
, system
(\mathrm{P})
has a
bounded (positive) solution
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
in
C_{0}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})\times C_{0}^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega}),
for certain
\tau\in(0,1),
satisfying

u_{i}(x)\geq c_{0}d(x),\text{ for a constant }c_{0}>0,\text{ }i=1,2,

(1.2)

\mathbf{(ii)}
Under assumption
(\mathrm{\tilde{H}}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})
, system
(\mathrm{P})
admits a (positive) solution
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
in
(W_{0}^{1,p_{1}(x)}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))\times(W_{0}^{1,p_{2}(x)}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega))
satisfying (
1.2
).

Our approach is chiefly based on Schauder’s fixed point theorem. In this
respect, comparison arguments as well as a priori estimates are crucial to
get the appropriate localization of the desired fixed point which is
actually solution of
(\mathrm{P})
. This is achieved through a control on
solutions and their gradient which in itself represents a significant
feature of our result. At this point, the choice of suitable functions with
an adjustment of adequate constants is crucial. However, this would not be
enough without making use of the new Mean Value theorem (cf. Theorem
2
in Appendix) that is decisive to offset the lack of homogeneity
property and to deal with the variable exponents attendance. It should be
noted that the Mean Value theorem is a key ingredient in getting the
gradient estimate thus generalizing that corresponding to the case of
constant exponents problems stated in
[
6
,
7
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Sections
2
and
3

establish gradient estimates and a priori bounds; Section
4
deals
with comparison properties; Section
5
presents the proof of the main
result while Section
6
contains the new Mean Value Theorem.

2. A priori estimates

Let
L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
be the generalized Lebesgue space that consists of
all measurable real-valued functions
u
satisfying

\rho_{p(x)}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty,

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}=\inf\{\tau>0:\rho_{p(x)}(\frac{u}{\tau})\leq 1\}.\end{array}

Recall for any
u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
it holds

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}^{p^{-}}\leq\rho_{p(x)}(u)\leq\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}^{p^{+}}\text{ \ if \ }\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}>1,\\
\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}^{p^{+}}\leq\rho_{p(x)}(u)\leq\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}^{p^{-}}\text{ \ if \ }\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}\leq 1,\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

and

\left\|u\right\|_{p(x)}=a\text{ \ if and only if }\rho_{p(x)}(\frac{u}{a})=1.

(2.2)

The variable exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega),
defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}W^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega):|\nabla u|\in L^{p(x)}(\Omega)\},\end{array}

is endowed with the norm
\left\|u\right\|_{1,p(x)}=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p(x)}
which makes it a Banach space. In the sequel,
d(x):=d(x,\partial\Omega)
denotes the euclidean distance of
x
with
respect to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

The next lemma is a slight modification of
[
22
, Lemma 5.1]
which will
be useful later on.

Lemma 1
.

Let
k,m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be two real and positive
functions with
m^{-}>0
. If
u\in L^{k(x)}\left(\Omega\right)
then
u^{m(x)}\in L^{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}\left(\Omega\right)
, and there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\||u|^{m(x)}\right\|_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}=\|u\|_{k(x)}^{m(x_{0})}.\end{array}

Proof.

On account of (
2.2
), Mean Value theorem
[
4
, Theorem 5]
ensures
the existence of
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}1=\rho_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}(\frac{|u|^{m(x)}}{\left\||u|^{m(x)}\right\|_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}})=\int_{\Omega}|\frac{u}{\|u\|_{k(x)}}|^{k(x)}\frac{\|u\|_{k(x)}^{k(x)}}{\left\||u_{1}|^{m(x)}\right\|_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}^{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}}dx\\
=\frac{\|u\|_{k(x)}^{k(x_{0})}}{\left\||u_{1}|^{m(x)}\right\|_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}^{\frac{k(x_{0})}{m(x_{0})}}}\rho_{k(x)}(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{k(x)}})=\frac{\|u\|_{k(x)}^{k(x_{0})}}{\left\||u|^{m(x)}\right\|_{\frac{k(x)}{m(x)}}^{\frac{k(x_{0})}{m(x_{0})}}},\end{array}

showing the desired identity.
∎

A priori gradient estimate is provided in the next lemma. It is a partial
extension of
[
6
, Lemma
1
]
to problems involving variable exponents.

Lemma 2
.

Let
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a nontrivial sign-constant
function and let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
be the weak solution of the
Dirichlet problem

-\Delta_{p(x)}u=h(x)\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ }u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

(2.3)

Then, there exists a constant
\bar{k}_{p}>0
, depending only on
p
,
N
,
and
\Omega
, such that

\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}\leq\bar{k}_{p}\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}-1}}

(2.4)

with

p^{\pm}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{-}&\text{if }\|h\|_{\infty}>1\\
p^{+}&\text{if }\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First, assume that
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1.
Multiplying problem (
2.3
) by
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p(.)}(\Omega),
with
\varphi\geq 0,

integrating over
\Omega
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\text{ }dx=\int_{\Omega}h(x)\varphi\text{ }dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\text{ }dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\xi|^{p(x)-2}\nabla\xi\nabla\varphi\text{ }dx,

where
\xi(x)
is the
p(x)
-torsion function defined by

-\Delta_{p(x)}\xi=1\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }\xi=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

The weak comparison principle implies
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\|\xi\|_{\infty}
while the regularity theorem in
[
12
]
ensures the
existence of constants
\tau\in(0,1)
and
\bar{k}_{p}>0
such that
\|u\|_{C^{1,\tau}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\bar{k}_{p}.

Now we deal with the case
\|h\|_{\infty}>1
. By Theorem
2
in the Appendix, there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-1}{p^{-}-1}}u)|^{p(x)-2}\nabla(\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-1}{p^{-}-1}}u)\nabla\varphi\text{ }dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-(p(x)-1)}{p^{-}-1}}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\text{ }dx\\
=\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-(p(x_{0})-1)}{p^{-}-1}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\text{ }dx=\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-(p(x_{0})-1)}{p^{-}-1}}\int_{\Omega}h(x)\varphi\text{ }dx\\
\leq\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-(p^{-}-1)}{p^{-}-1}}\int_{\Omega}h(x)\varphi\text{ }dx=\int_{\Omega}\|h\|_{\infty}^{-1}h(x)\varphi\text{ }dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi\text{ }dx.\end{array}

Thus, in view of the previous argument, it follows that

\|h\|_{\infty}^{\frac{-1}{p^{-}-1}}|u|_{1,\alpha}\leq\bar{k}_{p},

showing that (
2.4
) holds true. This ends the proof.
∎

The case when
h
in (
2.3
) is not an
L^{\infty}
-bounded function is
handled in the next lemma which provides an a priori
L^{\infty}
-estimate
of solutions for (
2.3
).

Lemma 3
.

Assume
h\in L^{p^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)\cap L^{N}(\Omega)
in (
2.3
). Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
N,p
and
\Omega
, such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}-1}},\end{array}

(2.5)

with

p^{\pm}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{-}&\text{if }\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}>1\\
p^{+}&\text{if }\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N},
consider the set
A_{k}=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>k\}
, where
u
is the
solution of (
2.3
). Thus, to prove (
2.5
) amounts to show that

|A_{k}|=0\,\,\mbox{ for any }\,\,k>k_{0},

where

k_{0}:=C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}-1}},

(2.6)

with a constant
C>0
that will be chosen later on. By contradiction assume
that there exists
k>k_{0}
such that
|A_{k}|\neq 0
. Testing (
2.3
)
with
(u-k)^{+}
leads to

\int_{A_{k}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p(x)}dx=\int_{A_{k}}h\left(x\right)(u-k)^{+}dx\leq\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\left\|(u-k)^{+}\right\|_{L^{N^{\prime}}(A_{k})}.

(2.7)

One has

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{A_{k}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p(x)}dx\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(A_{k}^{-})}^{p^{-}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}(A_{k}^{+})}^{p^{+}},\end{array}

(2.8)

where
A_{k}^{-}:=A_{k}\cap\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}
and
A_{k}^{+}:=A_{k}\cap\{|\nabla u|<1\}
. Hölder’s inequality together with classical Sobolev
and Lebesgue embeddings imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|(u-k)^{+}\right\|_{L^{N^{\prime}}(A_{k})}\leq C_{1}\int_{A_{k}}|\nabla u|dx\\
\leq C_{1}\left(|A_{k}^{-}|^{\frac{p^{-}-1}{p^{-}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(A_{k}^{-})}+|A_{k}^{+}|^{\frac{p^{+}-1}{p^{+}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}(A_{k}^{+})}\right),\end{array}

(2.9)

as well as

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{\pm}}(A_{k}^{\pm})}^{p^{\pm}}\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{\pm}}(A_{k}^{\pm})}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(A_{k}^{\pm})}|A_{k}^{\pm}|^{-\frac{p^{\pm}-1}{p^{\pm}}})^{p^{\pm}-1}\\
\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{\pm}}(A_{k}^{\pm})}(C_{1}^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{N^{\prime}}(A_{k}^{\pm})}|A_{k}^{\pm}|^{-\frac{p^{\pm}-1}{p^{\pm}}})^{p^{\pm}-1}\\
\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{\pm}}(A_{k}^{\pm})}(C_{1}^{-1}k|A_{k}^{\pm}|^{\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}-\frac{p^{\pm}-1}{p^{\pm}}})^{p^{\pm}-1},\end{array}

(2.10)

for a certain constant
C_{1}>0
. Then, gathering (
2.7
)-(
2.10
)
together, we infer that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(A_{k}^{-})}\left[(C_{1}^{-1}k|A_{k}^{-}|^{\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}-\frac{p^{-}-1}{p^{-}}})^{p^{-}-1}-C_{1}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}|A_{k}^{-}|^{\frac{p^{-}-1}{p^{-}}}\right]\\
+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}(A_{k}^{+})}\left[(C_{1}^{-1}k|A_{k}^{+}|^{\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}-\frac{p^{+}-1}{p^{+}}})^{p^{+}-1}-C_{1}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}|A_{k}^{+}|^{\frac{p^{+}-1}{p^{+}}}\right]\\
\leq 0.\end{array}

(2.11)

Now, fix
C>0
in (
2.6
) as follows

C:=\max\{C_{1}^{p^{-\prime}},C_{1}^{p^{+\prime}}\}|\Omega|^{1-\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}}.

Then, for
k>k_{0},
it follows that

\left[(C_{1}^{-1}k|A_{k}^{\pm}|^{\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}-\frac{p^{\pm}-1}{p^{\pm}}})^{p^{\pm}-1}-C_{1}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}|A_{k}^{\pm}|^{\frac{p^{\pm}-1}{p^{\pm}}}\right]\geq 0,

which contradicts (
2.11
). This completes the proof.
∎

3. An auxiliary system

For each
(z_{1},z_{2})\in W_{0}^{1,p_{1}(x)}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,p_{2}(x)}(\Omega),
we consider the auxiliary problem

(\mathrm{P}_{(z_{1},z_{2})})\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p_{i}(x)}u_{i}=f_{i}(x,z_{1},z_{2},\nabla z_{1},\nabla z_{2})\text{
\ in }\Omega\\
u_{i}=0\text{ \ on }\partial\Omega,\text{ }i=1,2.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 4
.

Assume
(
H̃
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})

holds. Suppose that

z_{i}(x)\geq\tilde{c}d(x)\text{ \ and \ }\|\nabla z_{i}\|_{p_{i}(x)}\leq\tilde{L},\,

(3.1)

for some constants
\tilde{c},\tilde{L}>0
independent on
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. Then, for
\tilde{L}
large enough, problem
(\mathrm{P}_{(z_{1},z_{2})})

admits a unique solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
W_{0}^{1,p_{1}(x)}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,p_{2}(x)}(\Omega)
satisfying

\|\nabla u_{i}\|_{p_{i}(x)}\leq\tilde{L},\,\,i=1,2.

(3.2)

Proof.

First, we claim that

f_{i}(.,z_{1},z_{2},\nabla z_{1},\nabla z_{2})\in L^{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)\cap L^{N}(\Omega),\,\text{for }i=1,2.

(3.3)

We only show that
f_{i}\in L^{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)
in (
3.3
) because
f_{i}\in L^{N}(\Omega)
can be justified similarly by
substituting
p_{i}^{\prime}(.)
with
N
in the argument below. By
(
H
{}_{f})
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|f_{i}\left(.,z_{1},z_{2},\nabla z_{1},\nabla z_{2}\right)\right\|_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}\\
\leq M_{i}\left(||z_{1}^{\alpha_{i}(.)}z_{2}^{\beta_{i}(.)}||_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}+\left\||\nabla z_{1}|^{\gamma_{i}(.)}\right\|_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}+\left\||\nabla z_{2}|^{\overline{\gamma}_{i}(.)}\right\|_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}\right).\end{array}

(3.4)

Lemma
1
together with
(
H̃
{}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})
imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\||\nabla z_{1}|^{\gamma_{i}(.)}\right\|_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}+\left\||\nabla z_{2}|^{\overline{\gamma}_{i}(.)}\right\|_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}=\left\|\nabla z_{1}\right\|_{\gamma_{i}(x)p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}^{\gamma_{i}\left(x_{0}^{i}\right)}+\left\|\nabla z_{2}\right\|_{\overline{\gamma}_{i}(x)p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}^{\overline{\gamma}_{i}\left(x_{1}^{i}\right)}\\
\leq C(\left\|\nabla z_{1}\right\|_{p_{1}(x)}^{\gamma_{i}\left(x_{0}^{i}\right)}+\left\|\nabla z_{2}\right\|_{p_{2}(x)}^{\overline{\gamma}_{i}\left(x_{1}^{i}\right)}),\end{array}

(3.5)

for certain
x_{0}^{i},x_{1}^{i}\in\Omega
and a constant
C>0
. From (
3.1
) we have

\begin{array}[]{l}||z_{1}^{\alpha_{i}(.)}z_{2}^{\beta_{i}(.)}||_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}||(\tilde{c}d(.))^{\alpha_{i}(.)+\beta_{i}(.)}||_{p_{i}^{\prime}(x)}&\text{if }\alpha_{i}^{+},\beta_{i}^{+}<0\\

\mathcal{K}_{C}
(resp.
\mathcal{\tilde{K}}_{\tilde{L}}
), Lemma
6
implies that (
1.2
) is fulfilled. This
completes the proof.

6. Appendix

Denoting
B_{1}
the unit ball and
S_{1}
the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
\Psi_{\epsilon},\Pi_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
be sequences of mollifiers defined for
\epsilon>0
by

\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{N}}\Psi\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right)\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\Pi_{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{N}}\Pi\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right),

(6.1)

with
\Psi:B_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a bump function satisfying
\Psi(x)=C^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}}
,
C=\int_{B_{1}}e^{-\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}}dx
, and
\Pi=\overline{C}^{-1}\pi
, where
\pi\in C_{0}^{1}(B_{1})
is the solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\pi=1&&\mbox{ in }B_{1}\\
&\pi=0&&\mbox{ on }S_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

and
\overline{C}=\int_{B_{1}}\pi(x)dx
. Both the functions
\Psi
and
\Pi
satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Psi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Pi(x)dx=1.

(6.2)

The Mean value theorem is stated as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
be the solution of a
nonlinear elliptic equation of the form

-\Delta_{p(x)}u=h(x)\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ }u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(6.3)

where
h
is a sign-constant function. Let
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz continuous function satisfying
-\infty<m\leq f(x)\leq M<\infty
for some constants
m,M
. Then, for any
sign-constant function
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega),
there exists a real

\gamma\in[m,M]
, depending on
\phi
, such that

\int_{\Omega}f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx=\gamma\int_{\Omega}h(x)\phi dx.

(6.4)

Remark 1
.

Actually, equality (
6.4
) shall be proved for any nonnegative function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and the generalization to
W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
is deduced by a density argument. Also, without loss of
generality, we will assume that
h
is nonnegative, because otherwise, instead of (
6.3
), we consider the problem

-\Delta_{p(x)}u=-h(x)\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

whose solution is
\hat{u}=-u
. Similarly, we will assume that
\phi
is nonnegative.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Inspired by
[
4
]
, the proof of (
6.4
) is divided in four steps:

\bullet

Step 1:
For any functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, benote by
\star
the classical convolution product defined by

\displaystyle f\star g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x-z)g(z)dz,\,\,\mbox{for any }x\in\Omega,

and set

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi_{\epsilon}=\phi\star\Pi_{\epsilon}\\
\Omega(y)=\left\{x\in\Omega\,|\,f(x)\leq y\right\},\,\,y\in\left[m,M\right]\\
\Omega_{\epsilon}=\left\{x\in\Omega\,|\,d(x,\partial\Omega)\geq\epsilon\right\}\\
a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega(y)\cap\Omega_{\epsilon}}\star\Psi_{\epsilon}.\end{array}

(6.5)

For
\epsilon>0,
let
\breve{F}_{\epsilon},F_{\epsilon}:[m,M]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the functionals defined for any nonnegative function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
by

\begin{array}[]{l}\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y)=\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}(x)\,f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx,\\
F_{\epsilon}(y)=\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx,\end{array}

where
u
refers to the solution of problem (
6.3
). We claim that

\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\left|\int_{m}^{M}d\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y)-\int_{m}^{M}ydF_{\epsilon}(y)\right|=0.

(6.6)

Indeed, let
P_{n}=\left\{m=y_{0}<...<y_{n}=M\right\}
be a partition of
the interval
\left[m,M\right]
such that
\mathcal{N}=y_{k}-y_{k-1}=\delta<\eta,
and let
y_{k}^{\prime}\in[y_{k-1},y_{k}]
. We
check that

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]-\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}^{\prime}\left[F_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-F_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]\right|\\
=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}(f(x)-y_{k}^{\prime})a_{\epsilon,\Omega_{k}}(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right|.\end{array}

(6.7)

with
\Omega_{k}=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,y_{k-1}<f(x)\leq y_{k}\}=\Omega(y_{k})\backslash\Omega(y_{k-1})
and
a_{\epsilon,\Omega_{k}}=a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y_{k})}-a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y_{k-1})}
. For any
x\in\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon}+B_{\epsilon}(0)
, there exists
(y,s)\in\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{\epsilon}\times B_{\epsilon}(0)
, such that
x=y+s
. From the Mean Value
theorem, and the
C
-Lipschitz regularity of
f
,
we infer that

\begin{array}[]{l}|f(x)-y_{k}^{\prime}|\leq\left|f(y+s)-f(y)\right|+\left|f(y)-y_{k}^{\prime}\right|\leq C|s|+|y_{k}-y_{k-1}|\leq C\epsilon+\eta.\end{array}

(6.8)

Since
a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y_{0})}=a_{\epsilon,\Omega(m)}=0
, and
a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y_{n})}=a_{\epsilon,\Omega(M)}=a_{\epsilon,\Omega}
, it holds

\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{\epsilon,\Omega_{k}}(x)=a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x).\end{array}

(6.9)

Gathering (
6.7
)-(
6.9
) together leads to

\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]-\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}^{\prime}\left[F_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-F_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]\right|\\
\leq\left(C\epsilon+\eta\right)\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-1}\left|\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right|dx.\end{array}

(6.10)

Observe that

\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega(y)},\,\forall y\in[m,M],\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}=\nabla\phi\,\,\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,

(6.11)

see for example
[
11
, Theorem 7, page 714]
. Thus, (
6.10
)-(
6.11
) together imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]-\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}^{\prime}\left[F_{\epsilon}(y_{k})-F_{\epsilon}(y_{k-1})\right]\right|=0.\end{array}

(6.12)

This achieves the proof of (
6.6
).

\bullet

Step 2:
After integrating by parts, we may write that

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{m}^{M}ydF_{\epsilon}(y)=\left[MF_{\epsilon}(M)-mF_{\epsilon}(m)\right]-\int_{m}^{M}F_{\epsilon}(y)dy,\end{array}

(6.13)

which implies, according to (
6.6
),

\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\left|\left(\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(M)-\breve{F}_{\epsilon}(m)\right)-\left(MF_{\epsilon}(M)-mF_{\epsilon}(m)-\int_{m}^{M}F_{\epsilon}(y)dy\right)\right|=0.\end{array}

(6.14)

By definition of
F_{\epsilon}
and
\breve{F}_{\epsilon}
, (
6.14
)
becomes

\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\left|\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right.\\
-M\,\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\\
\left.+\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right]dy\right|=0.\end{array}

(6.15)

By the way,
a_{\epsilon,\Omega(y)}=a_{\epsilon,\Omega}-a_{\epsilon,\Omega\backslash\Omega(y)}
, so from (
6.15
) we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\left|\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right.\\
-m\,\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\\
\left.-\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega\backslash\Omega(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right]dy\right|=0.\end{array}

(6.16)

\bullet

Step 3:
Set
A(y)=\Omega(y)
(resp.
A(y)=\Omega\backslash\Omega(y)
). Assume that
\phi\geq 0
is constant in
A(y)
. Then from the Dominated Convergence theorem and (
6.11
) we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,A(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right]dy\\
=\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx\right]dy=0.\end{array}

(6.17)

Now suppose that
\phi
is not constant in
A(y)
. For
(\epsilon,y,\phi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\star}\times[m,M]\times(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega))_{+}
fixed, we claim that there exists a nonnegative function
\Phi_{\epsilon,y}\in W_{0}^{1,p(x)}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi_{\epsilon,y}=a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\,\,\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

(6.18)

Indeed, consider the singular quasilinear elliptic problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=-|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}v^{-1}-2div\left(a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right)&&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&v>0&&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&v=0&&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.19)

Here, the divergence of the vector
a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}
must be interpreted in the classical sense, which is possible since the convolution
\phi_{\epsilon}
(resp.
a_{\epsilon,A(y)}
) involves the function
\phi
(resp.
\Psi_{\epsilon}
) which is infinitely differentiable. We are going to prove the existence of at least one solution for (
6.19
) in the sense of distributions, i.e., there exists
v_{\epsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{\epsilon}\nabla\xi dx=-\int_{\Omega}|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}v_{\epsilon}^{-1}\xi dx-2\int_{\Omega}div\left(a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right)\xi dx\end{array}

for every
\xi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Existence of a solution for (
6.19
):
Define

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{\epsilon}(x)=|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|\\
b_{\epsilon}(x)=div\left(a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right)\\
K_{\epsilon}=\left\{w\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\underline{v}_{\epsilon}\leq w\leq\overline{v}_{\epsilon}\right\}\\
\underline{v}_{\epsilon}=\lambda\epsilon^{-1/2}\psi\\
\overline{v}_{\epsilon}=\max\{\lambda\epsilon^{-1/2}\|\psi\|_{\infty},\|b_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\|\chi\|_{\infty}\}\\
f_{\epsilon}(x,z)=-a_{\epsilon}^{2}(x)z^{-1}-2b_{\epsilon}(x),\,\,z\in K_{\epsilon}\\
\hat{\phi}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\underset{\epsilon\in(0,1)}{\min\,}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy\end{array}

where
\psi,\chi\in C_{0}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
are respectively the solutions of the problems

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\psi=\hat{\phi}&&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&\psi=0&&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\chi=1&&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&\chi=0&&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.20)

see
[
23
, Proposition 2.1]
. The fact that
\phi\geq 0
is not constant in
A(y)
implies that
\hat{\phi}
is a positive nontrivial function, hence the existence of
\lambda>0
such that

d(x)\leq\lambda\psi(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all }\,\,x\in\Omega,

(6.21)

with
d(x):=d(x,\partial\Omega)
, see
[
24
, Theorems 1 and 2]
. Let
z\in K_{\epsilon}
, consider the auxiliary problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=f_{\epsilon}(x,z)&&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&v=0&&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.22)

Following the ideas of
[
23
]
, we prove that
f_{\epsilon}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. Indeed, due to the classical Mean Value theorem, the estimation
a_{\epsilon,A(y)}(x)\leq\|\nabla a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\|_{\infty}d(x)
with (
6.21
) imply

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{\epsilon}^{2}z^{-1}\leq a_{\epsilon}^{2}\underline{v}_{\epsilon}^{-1}=|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}(\lambda\epsilon^{-1/2}\psi)^{-1}\leq\epsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\|_{\infty}|\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}\in L^{q}(\Omega),\end{array}

(6.23)

for any
q>N
. Consequently,
f_{\epsilon}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, and from
[
23
, Proposition 2.1]
there exists
\check{v}_{\epsilon}\equiv Az\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying (
6.22
). Since the mapping
S:K_{\epsilon}\rightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
, defined by
Sz=f_{\epsilon}(x,z)
, is continuous and bounded, it follows from
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
that
A:K_{\epsilon}\rightarrow C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is compact. It remains to prove that
A:K_{\epsilon}\rightarrow K_{\epsilon}
. Indeed, let
z\in K_{\epsilon}

and
\check{v}_{\epsilon}=Az
. Then

-\Delta\check{v}_{\epsilon}\leq\|b_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}=-\Delta(\|b_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\chi)
,
\chi
defined in (
6.20
),
which implies according to the weak comparison principle that

\check{v}_{\epsilon}\leq\|b_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\chi\leq\overline{v}_{\epsilon}
.

Now to prove that
\underline{v}_{\epsilon}\leq\check{v}_{\epsilon}
, we proceed as in
[
17
]
. Let
B_{s}=\{x\in\Omega:f_{\epsilon}(x,.)<\lambda s\hat{\phi}(x)\}
(
s>0
) and let
v_{s,\epsilon}
be the solution of the problem

\displaystyle-\Delta v=s^{-1}f_{s,\epsilon}(x,\underline{v}_{\epsilon})\equiv s^{-1}\left\{\begin{aligned} &f_{\epsilon}(x,\underline{v}_{\epsilon})\,\,&&\mbox{in }B_{s}\\
&\lambda s\hat{\phi}(x)\,\,&&\mbox{in }\Omega\backslash B_{s}\end{aligned}\right.,v=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

Notice that

-\Delta\check{v}_{\epsilon}=f_{\epsilon}(x,z)\geq f_{s,\epsilon}(x,\underline{v}_{\epsilon})=-\Delta\left(sv_{s,\epsilon}\right)
, and from the weak comparison principle we have
\check{v}_{\epsilon}\geq sv_{s,\epsilon}
. Also, setting
v_{0}=\lambda\psi
,
\psi
defined in (
6.20
), then
v_{0}
satisfies
-\Delta v_{0}=\lambda\hat{\phi}
. From
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
, and using (
6.21
) and (
6.23
), there exists
C>0
independent of
v_{s,\epsilon}
and
v_{0}
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}|v_{s,\epsilon}-v_{0}|_{C^{1}}\leq C\left\|s^{-1}f_{s,\epsilon}-\lambda\hat{\phi}\right\|_{q}\\
=C\left(\int_{B_{s}}\left|s^{-1}[-a_{\epsilon}^{2}(x)\underline{v}_{\epsilon}^{-1}-2b_{\epsilon}(x)]-\lambda\hat{\phi}(x)\right|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\\
\leq C|B_{s}|^{1/q}\left(s^{-1}\left[\epsilon^{1/2}\|\nabla a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|b_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\right]+\lambda\|\hat{\phi}\|_{\infty}\right)\\
\leq|B_{s}|^{1/q}M_{s,\epsilon},\end{array}

(6.24)

where

M_{s,\epsilon}=C(s^{-1}[\epsilon^{-1/2}\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\epsilon^{-1}\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}+2\|\Delta\phi\|_{\infty}]+\lambda\|\phi\|_{\infty}|B_{1}|).

Also, from (
6.21
), we check that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\check{v}_{\epsilon}\geq sv_{s,\epsilon}\geq\lambda s\left(\lambda^{-1}v_{0}-\lambda^{-1}|v_{s,\epsilon}-v_{0}|_{C^{1}}d(x)\right)\geq\lambda s(1-|v_{s,\epsilon}-v_{0}|_{C^{1}})\psi.\end{array}

Now, consider the set
G=\{x\in\Omega:d(x,\partial[A(y)\cap\Omega_{\epsilon}])\geq\epsilon\}
. If
x\in G
,
b_{\epsilon}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\Delta\phi_{\epsilon}(x)
, and by definition of
\phi_{\epsilon}
it occurs

\begin{array}[]{l}-2b_{\epsilon}(x)=2\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\phi\star(-\Delta\Pi_{\epsilon})(x)=2\epsilon^{-2}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\int_{B_{1}(0)}(-\Delta\Pi)(y)\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy\\
=2\epsilon^{-2}\overline{C}^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy.\end{array}

(6.26)

Assuming that
\epsilon>0
is small enough so that
\epsilon^{-1/2}\geq\lambda\overline{C}+2^{-1}N\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\pi\|_{\infty}
, we deduce from (
6.26
) that

\begin{array}[]{l}-2b_{\epsilon}(x)\geq(2\lambda+\overline{C}^{-1}N\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\pi\|_{\infty})\epsilon^{-3/2}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy.\end{array}

(6.27)

Besides, considering that
\lambda>0
satisfies (
6.21
), for any
(x,z)\in G\times K_{\epsilon}
we get

\begin{array}[]{l}-a_{\epsilon}^{2}(x)z^{-1}\geq-|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}\underline{v}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\geq-|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}|^{2}(\epsilon^{-1/2}d(x))^{-1}\\
\geq-\overline{C}^{-1}N\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\pi\|_{\infty}\epsilon^{-3/2}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy.\end{array}

(6.28)

Therefore, combining (
6.27
) and (
6.28
), in the set
G
we end up with

\begin{array}[]{l}f_{\epsilon}(x,z)\geq 2\lambda\epsilon^{-3/2}\mathbbm{1}_{A(y)}(x)\int_{B_{1}(0)}\phi(x-\epsilon y)dy\geq\lambda s\hat{\phi}(x),\end{array}

with
s=2\epsilon^{-1/2}
. Consequently
B_{s}\subset\Omega\backslash G
, where
B_{s}=\{x\in\Omega:f_{\epsilon}(x,.)<\lambda s\hat{\phi}(x)\}
, and so
|B_{s}|\leq|\Omega\backslash G|<k_{N}\epsilon^{N}
for some constant
k_{N}>0
. Applying (
6.24
) with
s=2\epsilon^{-1/2}
and
q\in(N,(4/3)N)
, we may write, for
\epsilon>0
small enough,

|v_{s,\epsilon}-v_{0}|_{C^{1}}\leq\epsilon^{3/4}M_{s,\epsilon}<\epsilon^{1/4}\overline{M}<1/2,

(6.29)

where
\overline{M}>0
is some constant independent of
\epsilon
. We deduce from (
6
) and (
6.29
) that

\check{v}_{\epsilon}\geq\lambda(s/2)\psi=\lambda\epsilon^{-1/2}\psi=\underline{v}_{\epsilon}.

Therefore
\check{v}_{\epsilon}\in K_{\epsilon}
, and the existence of a solution of (
6.19
) follows from Schauder’s fixed point theorem.

Proof of (
6.18
):
Let
v_{\epsilon}\in K_{\epsilon}
be a solution of (
6.19
). We show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v_{\epsilon}-a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}dx=0.

Indeed, multiplying (
6.19
) by
v_{\epsilon}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we may write

\begin{array}[]{l}0=\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla v_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\left|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}+2div(a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon})v_{\epsilon}\right]dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla v_{\epsilon}|^{2}-2a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}.\nabla v_{\epsilon}+\left|a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}\right]dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v_{\epsilon}-a_{\epsilon,A(y)}\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}dx.\end{array}

As a consequence, we define
\Phi_{\epsilon,y}\equiv v_{\epsilon}
in (
6.18
).

\bullet

Step 4:
Since
u
is a solution of problem (
6.3
), and thanks to the
positivity of the functions
h
and
\Phi_{\epsilon,y}
, from (
6.18
) we have

\begin{array}[]{l}I_{\epsilon}=\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,A(y)}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\right]dy\\
=\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\Phi_{\epsilon,y}dx\right]dy\\
=\int_{m}^{M}\left[\int_{\Omega}h(x)\Phi_{\epsilon,y}dx\right]dy\geq 0.\end{array}

(6.30)

Besides, using the same argument as in (
6.17
), we prove that the sequence
I_{\epsilon}
converges as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Therefore from (
6.30
) we deduce that
\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim\,}I_{\epsilon}\geq 0
, and combining (
6.15
) and (
6.16
), we end up
with

\begin{array}[]{l}m\,\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\\
\leq\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx\\
\leq M\,\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\int_{\Omega}a_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)\,|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi_{\epsilon}dx.\end{array}

The Dominated Convergence theorem and (
6.11
) imply that

\begin{array}[]{l}m\int_{\Omega}h(x)\phi dx=m\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx\\
\leq M\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx=M\int_{\Omega}h(x)\phi dx.\end{array}

This leads to the existence of
\gamma\in[m,M]
, depending on
\phi
, such that

\int_{\Omega}f(x)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}\nabla u\nabla\phi dx=\gamma\int_{\Omega}h(x)\phi dx.

This completes the proof.

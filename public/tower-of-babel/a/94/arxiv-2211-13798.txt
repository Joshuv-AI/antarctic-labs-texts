On uniform estimates for ( n − 1 ) − (n-1)- form fully nonlinear partial differential equations on compact Hermitian manifolds


Abstract
We obtain a priori L ∞ L^{\infty} estimate for a general class of ( n − 1 ) − (n-1)- form fully nonlinear partial differential equations on compact Hermitian manifolds. Our method relies on the local version of comparison with auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations, developed earlier by B. Guo and D. H. Phong. The key is to find the appropriate elliptic operator such that the maximum principle applies.



Nikita Klemyatin, Shuang Liang and Chuwen Wang

Introduction

A priori estimates have always been fundamental in the study of partial differential equations. Among them,
L^{\infty}
estimates are especially important. As the most prominent example,
L^{\infty}
estimate for the Monge-Ampère equation obtained through Moser iteration was crucial in Yau’s solution to Calabi conjecture
[
26
]
. Kolodziej
[
21
]
improved it to a sharp version using pluripotential theory. A recent development initiated by Guo, Phong and Tong
[
7
]
provided a PDE-based proof to Kolodziej’s result using comparison with solutions to auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations. Moreover, the method extends to a wide class of non-linear equations satisfying a structural condition, which has been shown by Harvey and Lawson
[
20
]
to be quite large. This flexible method can also be applied to obtain stability estimates for Monge-Ampère and Hessian equations
[
8
]
;
L^{\infty}
estimates for Monge-Ampère equations on nef classes rather than just Kähler classes
[
9
]
; sharp modulus of continuity for non-Hölder solutions
[
10
]
; extensions to parabolic equations
[
4
]
; Regularization of
m-
subharmonic functions and hölder continuity
[
5
]
; lower bounds for the Green’s function
[
11
]
; uniform entropy estimates
[
13
]
; and diameter estimates and convergence theorems in Kähler geometry not requiring bounds on the Ricci curvature
[
15
,
16
]
. Sroka
[
22
]
applied the same method to obtain a sharp uniform bound for the quaternionic Monge-Ampére equation on hyperhermitian manifolds. For an exposition of these topics, we refer the readers to the recent survey paper by Guo and Phong
[
14
]
.

Recently, Guo and Phong
[
12
]
developed a local version of the comparison method to extend the
L^{\infty}
bound to equations on Hermitian manifolds. It also applies to
(n-1)-
form Monge-Ampère equations
[
12
]
, which was first solved by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
24
]
with regard to Calabi-Yau equation for Gauduchon metrics. A natural question is whether the comparison method in
[
12
]
applies to more general
(n-1)-
form fully nonlinear equations other than Monge-Ampère equations. In this paper, we generalize the
L^{\infty}
estimate to a wide class of
(n-1)-
form fully nonlinear equations satisfying structure conditions proposed in
[
7
]
. The key is to find the appropriate elliptic operator similar to the one defined in
[
24
, p.17]
and
[
12
, p.20]
, so that the maximum principle applies to the test function.

We now describe our results in detail.

Consider a positive function
f:\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}_{+}
such that

(1) the domain
\Gamma\subset{\bf R}^{n}
is an open symmetric cone satisfying

\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1};

(1.1)

Here
\Gamma_{k}:=\{\lambda\in{\bf R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\;1\leq j\leq k\}
is the Garding cones, where
\sigma_{j}(\lambda)
is the
j
-th symmetric polynomial in
\lambda
.

(2)
f(\lambda)
is symmetric in
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
and it satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\lambda_{j}\leq\beta f

(1.2)

for some positive constant
\beta>0
;

(3)
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}>0
for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
,
j=1,\ldots,n
;

(4) There exists
\gamma>0
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\geq\gamma.

(1.3)

for all
\lambda\in\Gamma
.

Remark that (
1.2
) slightly relaxes the conditions in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
, which require
f
to be homogeneous of degree one.

Suppose
(X,\omega)
is a compact Hermitian manifold without boundary and
\omega_{h}
is another Hermitian metric on
X
. For any
\varphi\in C^{2}(X)
, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}=\omega_{h}+\frac{1}{n-1}((\Delta_{\omega}\varphi)\omega-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)+\chi[\varphi],

(1.4)

where

\Delta_{\omega}\varphi=n\frac{i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}}

is the complex Laplacian of
\varphi
with respect to
\omega
and
\chi[\varphi]=x(z,\varphi,\partial\varphi,\overline{\partial}\varphi)
is the gradient term which may depend on the
\varphi
and its first order derivatives.

Let
h_{\varphi}:TX\to TX
be the relative endomorphism from
\omega
to
\tilde{\omega}
. In local coordinates,
(h_{\varphi})^{j}{}_{i}=g^{j\bar{k}}\tilde{g}_{\bar{k}i}
, where
(\tilde{g}_{\bar{k}i})
denotes the components of
\tilde{\omega}
and
(g^{i\bar{j}})
denotes the inverse of
\omega
. Let
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
be the (unordered) vector of eigenvalues of
h_{\varphi}
.

For a smooth function
F
on
X
, we consider the fully nonlinear partial differential equation

f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\mbox{ and }\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma.

(1.5)

with
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
and
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]\in\Gamma
on
X
.

We remark that our definition of relative endomorphism
h_{\varphi}:=\omega^{-1}\tilde{\omega}
is different from those in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
due to a different form of unknown metric (
1.4
). So the condition
\lambda\in\Gamma
means
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\omega}]\in\Gamma
, instead of
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)]\in\Gamma
, which imposes different condition on
\varphi
as in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. For example, when
\chi=0,\,\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, this requires
\varphi
being
(n-1)-
plurisubharmonic in the sense of
[
18
,
19
]
, instead of being plurisubharmonic.

The following two special cases is of particular interests. When
f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=\Big(\frac{\tilde{\omega}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\Big)^{1/n}
,
\chi=0
and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, the equation is the
(n-1)-
form Monge-Ampère equation considered by Tosatti-Weinkove
[

]
. They showed when
\omega
is Kähler, the equation is solvable if

is modified by a suitable additive constant, in which the

estimate is an essential step of their proof. When
f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=\Big(\frac{\tilde{\omega}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\Big)^{1/n}
,
\chi[\varphi]=*{\rm Re}\{\sqrt{-1}\partial\varphi\wedge\bar{\partial}\omega^{n-2}\}
and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, the equation is considered by Székelyhidi-Tosatti-Weinkove
[

]
in their celebrated solution to the Gauduchon conjecture.

Later in Guo-Phong
[

]
,
a priori

C^{0}
estimate of
\varphi
can be obtained for the
(n-1)-
form Monge-Ampère equation even when
\omega
is not Kähler. Here we generalize their results to arbitrary equations
f
satistying the conditions (1-4) above with the assumption the gradient term
\chi
being semi-positive.

Theorem 1

Assume
\chi\geq 0
, let
\varphi
be a
C^{2}
solution on a compact Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega)
of the equation

\displaystyle f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F}

(1.6)

where the operator
f(\lambda)
satisfies the conditions (1-4) spelled out in Section §1.

Fix any
p>n
, we have

\displaystyle||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq C

(1.7)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
X,\omega,\omega_{h},n,p
, and
\|e^{F}\|_{L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}}
. Here the
L^{1}(\,{\rm log}\,L)^{p}
norms are with respect to the volume form
\omega^{n}
.

Recently, Guo-Phong manage to prove
C^{0}
estimates for the gradient term
\chi
of a specific form without being semi-positive. Their method uses real auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation, we refer the interested readers to the most recent version of
[
12
]
.

2
The local estimate and proof of Theorem 1

We denote by
G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\partial\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}}
the coefficients of the linearization of the operator
\,{\rm log}\,f(\lambda[h])
.

It follows from the structure conditions of
f
that
G^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite at
h_{\varphi}
and

\displaystyle{\rm det}(G^{i\bar{j}})=\frac{1}{f^{n}}{\rm det}(\frac{\partial f(\lambda[h])}{\partial h_{ij}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{f(\lambda)^{n}}.

(2.1)

Fix any point
z_{0}\in X
, by simple linear algebra there is a smooth local frame
\{e^{i}\}
for the holomorphic cotangent bundle
{T^{*}}^{1,0}X
in a neighborhood of
z_{0}
, such that
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{j}e^{j}\wedge e^{\bar{j}}
and
\tilde{\omega}=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}e^{j}\wedge e^{\bar{j}}
. In particular,
(h_{\varphi})_{\bar{j}i}=\lambda_{j}\delta_{ij}
and
G^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\delta_{ij}
in this frame. Therefore, we have in the neighborhood of
z_{0}

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{G}\tilde{\omega}=\sum_{i,j}G^{i\bar{j}}\tilde{g}_{\bar{j}i}=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\lambda_{j}\leq\beta.

(2.2)

The last equality follows from (
1.2
). As
z_{0}
is arbitrary, we know that
{\rm tr}_{G}\tilde{\omega}\leq C
holds everywhere on
X
.

Define the following tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
by

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1}{n-1}\Big((G^{k\bar{l}}g_{\bar{l}k})g^{i\bar{j}}-G^{i\bar{j}}\Big).

Remark.
This tensor is similar to the tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
defined in
[
24
, p.17]
and
[
12
, p.20]
, except that we put
G^{i\bar{j}}
in place of
\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}
in order to deal with more general fully nonlinear equations.

We summarize the key properties of
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 1

The tensor
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
then satisfies the following: for
\lambda\in\Gamma
,

(a)

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}\leq\beta-G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}-G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{\bar{j}i}
and
G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i},G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{\bar{j}i}\geq 0
;

(b)

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite, and
{\rm det}(\Theta^{i\bar{j}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{f(\lambda)^{n}}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{G}\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{j}}\Big((g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}+\frac{1}{n-1}(g^{k\bar{l}}\varphi_{\bar{k}l}g_{\bar{j}i}-\varphi_{\bar{j}i})+\chi_{\bar{j}i}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}+G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{\bar{j}i}+\frac{1}{n-1}\Big((G^{k\bar{l}}g_{\bar{l}k})g^{i\bar{j}}-G^{\bar{j}i}\Big)\varphi_{\bar{j}i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}+G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{\bar{j}i}+\Theta^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{\bar{j}i}

where the first equality follows from the equation (
2.2
), the second equality follows from definition of
\tilde{\omega}
and the last equality follows from the definition of
\Theta
. Since
G^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite, we know
G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}\geq 0
. This proves (a).

We can choose holomorphic coordinates at a given point
p\in X
such that
g_{i\bar{j}}|_{p}=\delta_{ij}
and
G_{i\bar{j}}|_{p}=\mu_{i}\delta_{ij}
. Note that
\mu_{i}>0
for each
i=1,\cdots,n
since
G^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite. Then we have

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}|_{p}=\frac{1}{n-1}(\sum_{k\neq i}\mu_{k})\delta_{ij}

which is clearly positive definite. Moreover,

{\rm det}(\Theta^{i\bar{j}})|_{x_{0}}=\frac{1}{(n-1)^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{k\neq i}\mu_{k})\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\prod_{k\neq i}\mu_{k}\Big)^{1/(n-1)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}={\rm det}(G^{i\bar{j}})\geq\frac{\gamma}{f(\lambda)^{n}},

where the middle inequality follows from the arithmetic-geometric (AG) inequality and the last inequality follows from the equation (
2.1
). This proves (b). Q.E.D.

Let
x_{0}
be the point where
\varphi
attains its minimum. Without loss of generality, we assume
-\varphi(x_{0})\geq 2
. We then fix a local holomorphic coordinate
z
centered at
x_{0}
such that

\displaystyle{1\over 2}i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\leq\omega\leq 2i\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}\ {\rm in}\ B(x_{0},2r_{0})

(2.3)

where
B(x_{0},2r_{0})
is the Euclidian ball of radius
2r_{0}
in this coordinate. We denote
\Omega:=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
for simplicity.

Choose a small constant
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

\omega_{h}\geq\frac{2\epsilon^{\prime}}{n-1}({\rm tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h})\omega\ {\rm in}\ \Omega.

(2.4)

Define
s_{0}:=4\epsilon^{\prime}r_{0}^{2}
. Then for any
s\in(0,s_{0})
, we consider the following comparison function
u_{s}:\Omega\to{\bf R}
given by

u_{s}(z):=\varphi(z)-\varphi(x_{0})+\epsilon^{\prime}|z|^{2}-s,

(2.5)

Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}=\{z\in\Omega;u_{s}(z)<0\}.

(2.6)

be the sublevel set of
u_{s}
.
It’s easy to see from the definition that
u_{s}
is positive on
\partial\Omega
, so sub-level sets
\Omega_{s}

is relatively compact in
\Omega
.

Set

\displaystyle A_{s}=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(2.7)

To make the right hand side of our auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation smooth, we choose the following sequence of smooth positive functions
\tau_{k}:{\bf R}\to{{\bf R}}_{+}
such that

\tau_{k}(x)=x+\frac{1}{k},\quad\mbox{ when }x\geq 0,

(2.8)

and

\tau_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{2k},\quad\mbox{ when }x\leq-\frac{1}{k},

and
\tau_{k}(x)
lies between
1/2k
and
1/k
for
x\in[-1/k,0]
. Clearly
\tau_{k}
converge pointwise to
\tau_{\infty}(x)=x\cdot\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}(x)
as
k\to\infty
, where
\chi_{{\bf R}_{+}}
denotes the characteristic function of
{\bf R}_{+}
.

The auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation we consider is the following

\displaystyle(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}={\tau_{k}(-u_{s})\over A_{s,k}}e^{nF}\omega^{n}\ {\rm in}\ \Omega,\quad\psi_{s,k}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial\Omega

(2.9)

with
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k}\geq 0
, and
A_{s,k}
is defined by

\displaystyle A_{s,k}=\int_{\Omega}\tau_{k}(-u_{s})e^{nF}\omega^{n}.

(2.10)

By Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
2
]
, this Dirichlet problem admits a unique solution
\psi_{s,k}
which is of class
C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
, with
\psi_{s,k}\leq 0
. By the definition of
A_{s,k}
, we have
A_{s,k}\to A_{s}
as
k\to\infty
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=1.

(2.11)

Now we are ready to establish the following key comparison lemma

Lemma 2

Let
u_{s}
be a
C^{2}
solution of the fully non-linear equation (
1.6
) and
\psi_{s,k}
be the solutions of the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
2.9
) as defined above. Then we have

\displaystyle-u_{s}\leq\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{n\over n+1}\ {\rm on}\ \bar{\Omega}

(2.12)

where
\varepsilon
is the constant defined by
\varepsilon^{n+1}=A_{s,k}\gamma^{-1}\beta^{n+1}{(n+1)^{n}\over n^{2n}}
.

Proof.
We show that the function

\displaystyle\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{n\over n+1}-u_{s}

(2.13)

is always
\leq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}
. Let
x_{\max}\in\overline{\Omega}
be a maximum point of
\Phi
. If
x_{\max}\in\overline{\Omega}\backslash\Omega_{s}
, clearly
\Phi(x_{\max})\leq 0
by the definition of
\Omega_{s}
and the fact that
\psi_{s,k}<0
in
\Omega
. If
x_{\max}\in\Omega_{s}
, then we have
i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi(x_{\max})\leq 0
by the maximum principle. Since
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
is positive definite, we calculate at
x_{\max}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Theta^{i\bar{j}}\Phi_{\bar{j}i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\Theta^{i\bar{j}}(\psi_{s,k})_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{\varepsilon n}{(n+1)^{2}}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{n+2}{n+1}}\Theta^{i\bar{j}}(\psi_{s,k})_{i}(\psi_{s,k})_{\bar{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta+{\rm tr}_{G}\omega_{h}+{\rm tr}_{G}\chi-\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{n-1}({\rm tr}_{G}\omega\cdot{\rm tr}_{\omega}\omega_{{\bf C}^{n}}-{\rm tr}_{G}\omega_{{\bf C}^{n}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\Theta^{i\bar{j}}(\psi_{s,k})_{i\bar{j}}-\beta+{\rm tr}_{G}[\omega_{h}-\frac{2\epsilon^{\prime}}{n-1}({\rm tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h})\omega]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}({\rm det}\Theta^{i\bar{j}})^{1/n}[{\rm det}(\psi_{s,k})_{i\bar{j}}]^{1/n}-\beta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{\gamma^{1/n}}{f}\frac{(-u_{s})^{1/n}e^{F}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}-\beta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}\gamma^{1/n}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{(-u_{s})^{1/n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}-\beta.

Here the first equality follows from the definition of
\Theta^{i\bar{j}}
and part (a) of Lemma
1

\Theta^{i\bar{j}}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}\leq\beta-G^{i\bar{j}}(g_{h})_{\bar{j}i}-G^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{\bar{j}i}.

The third line follows from the choice of
\epsilon^{\prime}
in (
2.4
).
In the fourth line, we applied the standard arithmetic-geometric inequality. The fifth line follows from part (b) of Lemma
1
and the definition of
\psi_{s,k}
. The last line follows from the equation (
1.5
). By the choice of
\varepsilon
, this implies that
\Psi(x_{0})\leq 0
. Hence
\sup_{\Omega}\Psi\leq 0
. Q.E.D.

Along the same spirit in
[

]
, as long as we establish the comparison between
u_{s}
and
\psi_{s,k}
as in Lemma

, we could derive the
L^{\infty}
estimate, without refering to the differential equations satisfied by

Lemma 3

Assume the functions
u_{s}=\varphi-\varphi(x_{0})+q(z)-s
satisfies
u_{s}>0
on
\partial\Omega
and
u_{s}(x_{0})<0
. Let
\Omega_{s}
,
A_{s}
, and
A_{s,k}
be the corresponding notions defined above.

Assume that

\displaystyle-u_{s}\leq C(n,\gamma,\beta)\,A_{s,k}^{1\over n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{n\over n+1}\ {\rm on}\ \bar{\Omega}

(2.14)

for some constant
C(n,\gamma,\beta)
, where
\psi_{s,k}
are plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
such that
\int_{\Omega}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=1
and
\psi_{s,k}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Then
for any
p>n
, we have

\displaystyle-\varphi(x_{0})\leq C(n,\omega,\gamma,\beta,p,\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,\omega^{n})}).

(2.15)

Now, Theorem
1
follows from Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
, except that the a priori
L^{\infty}
bound of
\varphi
may rely on the
L^{1}
norm of
\varphi
. To remove this dependence, we cite the following lemma from
[
12
, Lemma 8]
.

Lemma 4

Let
\varphi\in C^{2}(X)
so that
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)]\in\Gamma_{1}
with
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, then

\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq C(n,\omega).

Remark.
We stress again that our definition of relative endomorphism
h_{\varphi}:=\omega^{-1}\tilde{\omega}
is different from those in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
due to a different form of unknown metric (
1.4
). So the condition
\lambda\in\Gamma
means
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\omega}]\in\Gamma
, instead of
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)]\in\Gamma
.

Since our assumption for
\varphi
is that
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\omega}]
lies in
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
, i.e.
{\rm tr}_{\omega}\tilde{\omega}\geq 0
. This implies that
\Delta_{\omega}\varphi\geq-{\rm tr}_{\omega}\omega_{h}\geq-C^{\prime}
by (
1.4
), for some positive constant
C^{\prime}
. Therefore
{\rm tr}_{\omega}(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}(\frac{n\varphi}{C^{\prime}}))\geq 0
. In other words,
\lambda[\omega^{-1}\cdot(\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}(\frac{n\varphi}{C^{\prime}}))]
is in
\Gamma_{1}
. Lemma
4
then yields the desired
L^{1}
bound of
\varphi
given our normalization
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
. The proof of Theorem
1
is complete.

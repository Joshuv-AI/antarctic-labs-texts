Roots, trace, and extendability of flat nonnegative smooth functions
Fushuai Jiang

Abstract
Building on the univariate techniques developed by Ray and Schmidt-Hieber, we study the class ℱ s ​ ( ℝ n ) \mathcal{F}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) of multivariate nonnegative smooth functions that are sufficiently flat near their zeroes, which guarantees that F r F^{r} has Hölder differentiability r ​ s rs whenever F ∈ ℱ s F\in\mathcal{F}^{s} . We then construct a continuous Whitney extension map that recovers an ℱ s \mathcal{F}^{s} function from prescribed jets. Finally, we prove a Brudnyi-Shvartsman Finiteness Principle for the class ℱ s \mathcal{F}^{s} , thereby providing a necessary and sufficient condition for a nonnegative function defined on an arbitrary subset of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} to be ℱ s \mathcal{F}^{s} -extendable to all of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} .

Introduction

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we use
\lfloor s\rfloor
to denote the largest integer strictly smaller than
s
.
For a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a real number
s>0
, let
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
denote the Hölder-Zygmund space of
\lfloor s\rfloor
-times continuously differentiable functions whose derivatives up to order
\lfloor s\rfloor
are bounded, and the
\lfloor s\rfloor
-th order derivatives are Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent
s-\lfloor s\rfloor
. The space becomes a Banach space when equipped with the norm

\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\max_{0\leq m<s}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|+\sup_{x\neq y,\,x,y\in\Omega}\frac{\left|\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(x)-\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}}.

Building on the univariate techniques developed by K. Ray and J. Schmidt-Hieber
[
35
]
, we study the smoothness property of the roots of multivariate nonnegative Hölder-differentiable functions. Furthermore, we provide a description of their trace to subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and provide criteria for their extendability from incomplete data.

The question of differentiability of square roots was first studied by G. Glaeser
[
23
]
,
who showed the sharp result that any nonnegative univariate function that is
2
-flat (
f(x)=0\Longrightarrow f^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
) admits a continuously differentiable square root. Additional flatness conditions then become necessary for a smooth function to have a square root with additional regularity. All the roots considered in this paper are nonnegative, in contrast with the notion “admissible roots” that are allowed to change signs
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we consider a particular positive cone
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
equipped with a seminorm

\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}&\text{ if }s\geq 1\\
0&\text{ if }0\leq s<1\end{cases},

such that
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
is a sufficient condition for
F^{r}
to have Hölder regularity
rs
. We define

\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
r\in(0,1]
,
s>0
, a convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and all nonnegative
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
,

\|{F^{r}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{rs}(\Omega)}\leq\|{F^{r}}\|_{\mathcal{F}^{rs}(\Omega)}\leq C(n,r,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}^{r}.

Note that the univariate version (
n=1
) of Theorem
1.1
was proved by Ray and Schmidt-Hieber
[
35
]
. The main difference between the proof of Theorem
1.1
and its univariate counterpart is in the analysis of the combinatorial form of the multivariate Faá di Bruno’s formula (Theorem
A.1
). We supply the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Appendix
A
for completeness.

In view of Theorem
1.1
,
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
is a sufficient condition for
F^{1/2}\in\mathcal{C}^{s/2}(\Omega)
, thereby overcoming the limitation described in
[
3
,
23
]
. The quantity
\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}
measures the flatness of
F
near its zeros in
\Omega
. In particular, if
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
and
F(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
, then
\nabla^{m}F(x_{0})=0
for all
0\leq m<s
. The class
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
contains, for example, constant functions, nonnegative
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
functions uniformly bounded away from zero on
\Omega
, and functions of the form
\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{s}G(x)
with
G
nonnegative and uniformly bounded away from zero. See Section
3
for other basic properties of
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
, and

[
35
]
for further comparison between
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
and other criteria considered in the literature.

The convexity assumption on
\Omega
in Theorem
1.1
can be relaxed, as long as a suitable variant of the mean value theorem holds. We will not pursue such generality in this paper.

Next, we consider two Whitney-type extension problems on how to characterize the restriction of
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
functions to a closed set (with no assumption on differentiability structures), and how to recover a function
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from partial data with
\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
as small as possible, up to a constant factor depending only on the dimension and the smoothness. We call such an
F
quasi-optimal.

First, we consider the classical jet-extension problem, i.e., reconstruction of an
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
function from given Taylor expansions.

Problem 1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set. For each
x\in E
, let
P_{x}
be a polynomial of degree at most
\lfloor s\rfloor:=\max\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:m<s\right\}
(a jet of order
\lfloor s\rfloor
) with
P_{x}(x)\geq 0
.

(A)

Does there exist an
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{J}_{x}F\equiv P_{x}
for all
x\in E
, i.e., the
\lfloor s\rfloor
-jet of
F
at
x
agrees with
P_{x}
for all
x\in E
?

(B)

If so, how do we find such an
F
taking all the prescribed jets, such that
\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is as small as possible, up to a multiplicative factor
C(n,s)
?

Let
\mathcal{J}_{E}F
denote the parameterized family of jets
(\mathcal{J}_{x}F)_{x\in E}
. Taylor’s theorem, taking flatness into consideration, states that
\|{\mathcal{J}_{E}F}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}\leq C(n,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, where
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}
is a norm on the positive cone
\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
of flat jets associated with the class
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
(see (
4.1
) below). Our solution to Problem
1
is captured by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set and let
(P_{x})_{x\in E}
be a field of jets of order
\lfloor s\rfloor
with
P_{x}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in E
. Then
(P_{x})_{x\in E}
extends to an
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function
if and only if
\|{(P_{x})_{x\in E}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}<\infty
. Moreover,
we can construct a continuous map
\mathcal{T}_{E}:\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)\to{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{J}_{E}\circ\mathcal{T}_{E}=Id
on
\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
.

See Theorem
4.2
(when
E
is finite) and Theorem
4.3
(when
E
is possibly infinite) for a more detailed statement. We shall prove Theorem
4.2
in Section
4
, and we will explain the necessary modification for proving Theorem
1.2
when
E
is possibly infinite.

Next, we consider the much harder function-extension problem.

Problem 2

(Whitney Extension Problem for
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set and let
f:E\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function.

(A)

Does there exist an
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F=f
on
E
?

(B)

If so,
how do we find such an
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F=f
on
E
and
\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is as small as possible, up to a multiplicative factor
C(n,s)
?

In this paper, we provide an answer to Problem
2
(A) in terms of the following “Finiteness Principle”.

Theorem 1.3

(Finiteness Principle for
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
)
.

Given
n\geq 1
and
s>0
, there exist constants
k^{\sharp}=k^{\sharp}(n,s)
and
C^{\sharp}=C^{\sharp}(n,s)
such that the following holds. Given an arbitrary
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(not necessarily finite or closed) and a function
f:E\to[0,\infty)
,
f
extends to a
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function if and only if

\sup_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}<\infty,

where
\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}:=\inf\left\{\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }F=f\text{ on }S\right\}
.
Moreover, we have the following equivalence of norms

(1.1)

\sup_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}\leq\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}\leq C^{\sharp}\cdot\sup_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}.

To wit, the only obstruction to the existence of a global extension is the lack of control in some local extensions.

2

Definitions and notations

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
always denotes a connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\lfloor s\rfloor
denote the largest integer
strictly
less than
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For a positive real
s>0
, we use
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
to denote the vector space of
\lfloor s\rfloor
-times continuously differentiable functions whose derivatives up to order
\lfloor s\rfloor
are bounded and
(s-\lfloor s\rfloor)
-Hölder continuous. We equip
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\max_{0\leq m<s}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|+\sup_{x\neq y,\,x,y\in\Omega}\frac{\left|\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(x)-\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}}.

Here,
\nabla^{m}F(x)
denotes the symmetric
m
-linear form and
\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|=\left(\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=k}\left|\partial^{\alpha}F(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
, where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
is a multi-index,
\left|\alpha\right|:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}
, and
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{t_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{t_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}
. We use the
\nabla^{m}F
notation when we want to emphasize on the number of derivatives taken. We will also use the homogeneous Hölder seminorm

\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{x\neq y,\,x,y\in\Omega}\frac{\left|\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(x)-\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}}.

We would like to point out the difference between
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
and the non-Hölder space
C^{m}(\Omega)
consisting of
m
-times continuously differentiable functions whose derivatives up order
m
are bounded and continuous, equipped with the norm
\|{F}\|_{C^{m}(\Omega)}:=\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq m}\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\left|\nabla^{k}F(x)\right|
. The closed unit ball of
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
is compact in the
C^{\lfloor s\rfloor}(\Omega)
topology whenever
\Omega
is bounded. We will use this fact in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For a nonnegative function
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
, we define

\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}&\text{ if }s\geq 1\\
0&\text{ if }0\leq s<1\end{cases},

where we adopt the conventions
\frac{0}{0}=0
and
\frac{a}{0}=\infty
for
a>0
. We use
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
to denote the collection of nonnegative functions
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
such that
\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}}<\infty
, and we define

\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}:=\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}.

We will see in Proposition
3.1
(A) that
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}
defines a seminorm on the positive cone
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
, thus justifying the notations above.
Since every
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
has well-defined values on the closure
\overline{\Omega}
, we can make sense of the spaces above if we replace
\Omega
by
K
with
\Omega\subset K\subset\overline{\Omega}
.

We use
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\lfloor s\rfloor}
to denote the space of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of degree no greater than
\lfloor s\rfloor
.

Let
F
be
\lfloor s\rfloor
-times continuously differentiable near
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use
\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}F
to denote the
\lfloor s\rfloor
-jet of
F
at
x_{0}
, which we identify with its Taylor polynomial

\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}F(x)\equiv\sum_{0\leq m<s}\nabla^{m}F(x_{0})\left(\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\otimes m}}{m!}\right)\equiv\sum_{0\leq\left|\alpha\right|<s}\partial^{\alpha}F(x_{0})\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}.

A cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of the form
c_{Q}+[-\delta_{Q}/2,\delta_{Q}/2)^{n}
, where
c_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the center of
Q
and
\delta_{Q}>0
is the sidelength of
Q
. If
A>0
, we use
AQ
to denote the concentric dilation of
Q
by a factor of
A
.
A
dyadic
cube is a cube of the form
2^{-k}z+[-2^{-k},2^{-k})
, where
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Each dyadic cube
Q
is contained in a unique dyadic cube with sidelength
2\delta_{Q}
, and that cube is denoted by
Q^{+}
.

We use
B(x,r)
to denote the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
x
and radius
r
, and we use
Q(c_{Q},\delta_{Q})
to denote the cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
c_{Q}
and sidelength
\delta_{Q}
.

Let
X
be a set of parameters, we use
C(X),c(X)
, etc., to denote constants that depend only on
X
. Their precise values may vary from line to line.

Let
S
be a finite set, we write
\#S
to denote the cardinality of
S
. If
S
is not finite, we define
\#S=\infty
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define

and
\mathrm{dist}({A},{B}):=\inf_{a\in A,b\in B}\left|a-b\right|
.

3
Basic results on the flat norm

We begin by summarizing some basic properties of the class
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
s>0
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex.

(A)

{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
is a positive cone in
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
, on which
\|{\cdot}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}
defines a norm. Moreover,
\|{FG}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C(n,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}\|{G}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}
for any
F,G\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
.

(B)

If
0<s^{\prime}\leq s
, then
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{F}^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, and
\|{\cdot}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq\max\left\{\|{\cdot}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)},\|{\cdot}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}
.

(C)

If
0<s^{\prime}\leq s
,
s>1
,
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
, and
\inf_{\Omega}F=0
, then
\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\geq\|{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\|{F}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}
.

(D)

If
F
is uniformly bounded away from zero on
\Omega
, then
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
if and only if
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\Omega)
.

(E)

Assume that
\Omega
is bounded.
Let
F
be nonnegative and continuously differentiable on
\Omega
, such that
\partial_{j}F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
for every
j=1,\cdots,n
. Then
F\in\mathcal{F}^{s+1}(\Omega)
with

\|{F}\|_{\mathcal{F}^{s+1}(\Omega)}\leq C(n,s,\diam\Omega)\cdot\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\|{\partial_{j}F}\|_{\mathcal{F}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},\,\|{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}.

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we directly apply the univariate argument in the proofs of Theorems 2,3, and 5 of
[
35
]
to all the partial derivatives.
One can also obtain similar wavelet estimates as in Proposition 1 of
[
35
]
.
We omit the details here.

Remark 3.1
.

The cone
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
inherits a notion of completeness from
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
. Suppose
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
-Cauchy sequence
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}\cap{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
that is also bounded in
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
, then the limit
F
of
F_{n}
in
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
is in
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
and
\|{F_{n}}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}}\to\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}}
.

The following lemma states that the flatness condition (at a single point) induces a lengthscale within which the change in derivatives can be controlled by the pointwise value. In particular, the function is locally constant. We will use this lemma repeatedly throughout the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
c_{0}>0
bounded from below by a constant determined only by
\epsilon,n,s

such that for any
0<c\leq c_{0}
, the following hold.

(A)

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
P\in\mathcal{P}
with
P(x_{0})\geq 0
and
M:=\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}<\infty
, and let
\delta_{x_{0},P}:=c\left(\frac{P(x_{0})}{M}\right)^{1/s}
. Then

\left|\nabla^{m}P(x)-\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|\leq\epsilon M^{\frac{m}{s}}P(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m}{s}}

for all
x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0},P})
and
0\leq m<s
.
In particular,

\left|P(x)-P(x_{0})\right|\leq\epsilon P(x_{0})\text{ for all }x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0},P}).

(B)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex open set, let
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
, and let
\delta_{x_{0},F}:=c\left(\frac{F(x_{0})}{\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}}\right)^{1/s}
. Then

\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)-\nabla^{m}F(x_{0})\right|\leq\epsilon\left(\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{m}{s}}F(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m}{s}}

for all
x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0},F})\cap\Omega
and
0\leq m<s
.
In particular,

\left|F(x)-F(x_{0})\right|\leq\epsilon F(x_{0})\text{ for all }x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0},F})\cap\Omega.

Proof.

If
s\leq 1
, there is nothing to prove. We assume that
s>1
. We further assume that
P(x_{0}),F(x_{0})>0
, for otherwise, all derivatives of
P
and
F
must vanish, and the estimates are trivial.

First, we prove (A). Write
\delta=\delta_{x_{0},P}
. Let
x\in B(x_{0},c\delta)
. We treat
P
as its own Taylor polynomial, so that

\nabla^{m}P(x)=\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})+\sum_{1\leq k<s-m}\nabla^{m+k}P(x_{0})\left(\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\otimes k}}{k!}\right).

Note that
\left|(x-x_{0})^{\otimes k}\right|\leq c^{k}\delta^{k}
.
By assumption,

\left|\nabla^{m+k}P(x_{0})\right|\leq P(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m-k}{s}}M^{\frac{m+k}{s}}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{m}P(x)-\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|\leq C(n,s)\sum_{1\leq k<s-m}P(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m-k}{s}}M^{\frac{m+k}{s}}\cdot c^{k}\cdot\frac{P(x_{0})^{\frac{k}{s}}}{M^{\frac{k}{s}}}.\end{split}

For
c_{0}
sufficiently small, we see that the right-hand side can be bounded by
\epsilon M^{\frac{m}{s}}P(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m}{s}}
. This proves (A).

We turn to (B). We set write
\delta=\delta_{x_{0},F}
. Let
P:\equiv\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}F
, i.e.,

P(x):=\sum_{0\leq m<s}\nabla^{m}F(x_{0})\left(\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\otimes m}}{m!}\right).

Then
\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\geq M:=\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}
. By the triangle inequality, Taylor’s theorem, and part (A) (with
\epsilon/2
in place of
\epsilon
),

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)-\nabla^{m}F(x_{0})\right|&\leq\left|\nabla^{m}P(x)-\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|+\left|\nabla^{m}(F-P)(x)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{m}{s}}F(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m}{s}}+\left|\nabla^{m}(F-P)(x)\right|.\end{split}

By Taylor’s theorem,

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{m}(F-P)(x)\right|&\leq C(n,s)\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\delta^{s-m}\\
&\leq C(n,s)c^{s-m}\cdot\left(\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{F(x_{0})}{\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}}\right)^{\frac{s-m}{s}}\\
&\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{m}{s}}F(x_{0})^{\frac{s-m}{s}}\end{split}

as long as
c_{0}
is sufficiently small. Lemma
3.1
(B) follows from the two inequalities above.
∎

If
s\in(0,1]
, then the flatness condition is trivial, and any function in
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
can be extended to a nonnegative
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function with comparable norm, via Kirszbraun’s formula or the Whitney extension operator (see Section
4
below). The next proposition, which improves Theorem 4 of
[
35
]
, says a similar phenomenon also occurs for
s\in(1,2]
. The scaling is consistent with the nonnegative (non-Hölder)
C^{2}
extension in
[
28
,
29
,
27
,
20
,
26
]
. Results of such type can be also used to study nonnegative Sobolev
L^{2}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
p>n
) extension, thanks to the embedding
L^{2}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{2-n/p}(\Omega)
for bounded
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See
[
25
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
s\in(1,2]
, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
P
be an affine polynomial with
P(x_{0})\geq 0
and
\frac{\left|\nabla P\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{1-s}}<\infty
. Set
\delta=\frac{P(x_{0})}{\left|\nabla P\right|}
. Then for any
\lambda>1
and nonnegative
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(B(x_{0},\lambda\delta))
with
\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}F\equiv P
, we have

(3.1)

\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(B(x_{0},\lambda\delta))}\geq\frac{s(\lambda-1)}{\lambda^{s}}\frac{\left|\nabla P\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{s-1}}.

In particular, if
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is nonnegative and
F_{\Omega}
is the restriction of
F
to
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
F_{\Omega}\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
and
\|{F_{\Omega}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2^{s}}{s}\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Proof.

If
\delta=0
, then
P(x_{0})=\left|\nabla P\right|=0
, and (
3.1
) is trivial. We assume that
\delta>0
.
Let
M:=\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(B(x_{0},\lambda\delta))}
. For any
x\in B(x_{0},\lambda\delta)
, let
\gamma
be the straight line segment from
x_{0}
to
x
, and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives

F(x)=P(x)+\int_{\gamma}(\nabla F-\nabla P).

The triangle inequality then implies that

(3.2)

\left|F(x)-P(x)\right|\leq s^{-1}\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{s}M.

Restricting
F
to the ray emanating from
x_{0}
in the direction of
-\nabla P
and re-parameterize, we see that (
3.2
) implies

(3.3)

s^{-1}Mt^{s}-\left|\nabla P\right|t+P(x_{0})\geq 0\text{ for all }t\in[0,\lambda\delta].

Plugging
t=\lambda\delta
into (
3.3
), we see that (
3.1
) follows.

For the last conclusion, since
F
is assumed to be globally nonnegative, we may set

\lambda=2
and apply (
3.3
) pointwise everywhere in
\Omega
.
∎

4
Whitney jets

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set. A
Whitney field
on
E
is a parametrized family of polynomials
\vec{P}=(P_{x})_{x\in E}
such that
P_{x}\in\mathcal{P}
for all
x\in E
. We define

(4.1)

\begin{split}\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(E)}&:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in E\\
0\leq m<s\end{subarray}}\left|\nabla^{m}P_{x}(x)\right|+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in E\\
x\neq y\\
0\leq m<s\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}(P_{x}-P_{y})(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s-m}},\\
\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(E)}&:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in E\\
1\leq m<s\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P_{x}(x)\right|^{s}}{\left|P_{x}(x)\right|^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m},\text{ and }\\
\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}&:=\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(E)}+\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(E)}.\end{split}

We use
\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
to denote the positive cone of Whitney fields
\vec{P}=(P_{x})_{x\in E}
such that
P_{x}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in E
and
\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}<\infty
.

Note that if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is any set and
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
F
generates a Whitney field

\mathcal{J}_{E}F:\equiv(\mathcal{J}_{x}F)_{x\in E}\in\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E).

Theorem 4.1

(Taylor’s theorem)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set and let
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
\mathcal{J}_{E}F\in\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
and

\|{\mathcal{J}_{E}F}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}\leq C(n,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

The Whitney extension map then is a continuous right inverse of
\mathcal{J}_{E}:{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
. See Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
below for the precise statement.

4.1
A model bump function

We fix a model bump function for the rest of the paper.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the standard bump function
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
defined by

(4.2)

g(x)=\begin{cases}\exp\left(1-\frac{1}{1-t^{2}}\right)&\text{ for }-1<t<1\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Then for all
s>0
, we have

\|{g}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(n,s).

Proof.

It suffices to check
\|{g}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}([-1,1])}
. For
t\in(-1,1)
, we have
g^{(m)}(t)=q_{m}(t)g(t)
where each term of
q_{m}
is a rational function, singular only at
\pm 1
. Then

\left(\left|g^{(m)}(t)\right|^{s}/g(t)^{s-m}\right)^{1/m}=\left|q_{m}(t)\right|^{s/m}\cdot g(t)\leq C(s,m)

since
g
vanishes faster than any polynomial near
\pm 1
.
∎

With
g
as in (
4.2
), we define the standard bump function
\varphi_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
by

(4.3)

\varphi_{0}:=g^{\otimes n},\quad(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{n}g(t_{j}).

It is clear that

\supp{\varphi_{0}}=[-1,1]^{n}\text{ and }\|{\varphi_{0}}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s).

Let
\delta>0
and let
\tau_{\delta}:x\mapsto\delta^{-1}x
. A direct computation yields

(4.4)

\|{\varphi\circ\tau_{\delta}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\delta^{-s}\|{\varphi}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\text{ for any }\varphi\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

4.2
Extension of a single jet

For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
M\geq 0
, we define

(4.5)

\Gamma(x_{0},M):=\left\{P\in\mathcal{P}:P(x_{0})\geq 0,\,\max_{0\leq m<s}\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|\leq M\text{ and }\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq M.\right\}

and

(4.6)

\Gamma(x_{0}):=\bigcup_{M\geq 0}\Gamma(x_{0},M).

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a map
\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}:\Gamma(x_{0})\to{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that for every
P\in\Gamma(x_{0},M)
, the following hold.

(A)

\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}\circ\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]\equiv P
.

(B)

\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]\geq 0
.

(C)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M
.

(D)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M
, and consequently,
\|{\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M
.

Proof.

Fix
P\in\Gamma(x_{0})
and let
M:=\max\limits_{1\leq m<s}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|^{s}}{P(x_{0})^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}
. If
P(x_{0})=0
, then
P
is the zero polynomial and
M=0
, so we can set
\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]\equiv 0
. From now on, we assume
P(x_{0})>0
. We may further assume that
M=1
.

We set

\delta:=P(x_{0})^{1/s}\text{ so that }P(x_{0})=\delta^{s}.

By the definition of
\Gamma
, we have

(4.7)

\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|\leq\delta^{s-m}\text{ for }1\leq m<s.

Let
c_{0}
be as in Lemma
3.1
with
\epsilon=1/2
, and let
Q:=x_{0}+[-c_{0}\delta,c_{0}\delta]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus,

(4.8)

P(x)\geq 1/2\delta^{s}\text{ for all }x\in Q.

From (
4.7
) and Taylor’s theorem, we also see that

(4.9)

\left|\nabla^{m}P(x)\right|\leq\delta^{s-m}C(n,s)\text{ for all $x\in Q$ and $0\leq m<s$.}

Combining (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we see that

(4.10)

\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x)\right|^{s}}{P(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq C(n,s)\text{ for all $x\in Q$ and $1\leq m<s$.}

Case I: suppose
\delta\geq 1
.

We let
\theta_{0}:=\varphi_{0}(\frac{x-x_{0}}{c_{0}})
with
\varphi_{0}
as in (
4.3
). Define

(4.11)

\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]:=\theta_{0}\cdot P.

Since
\theta_{0}\equiv 1
near
x_{0}
, conclusion (A) follows. Since
\supp{\theta_{0}}\subset x_{0}+[-c_{0},c_{0}]^{n}\subset Q
, conclusion (B) follows from (
4.8
). Thanks to Proposition
3.1
(A) and (
4.10
), we have

\|{\theta_{0}P}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}\leq C(n,s)\|{\theta_{0}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}\|{P}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}\leq C(n,s).

Conclusion (C) follows. Conclusion (D) is similar since
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
is an algebra. This concludes the analysis of Case I.

Case II: suppose
\delta<1
.

Let
\theta(x):=\varphi_{0}(\frac{x-x_{0}}{c_{0}\delta})
with
\varphi_{0}
as in (
4.3
). Then

(
\theta
-1)

\theta\equiv 1
near
x_{0}
,

(
\theta
-2)

\supp{\theta}\subset Q
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

(
\theta
-3)

\left|\nabla^{m}\theta\right|\leq\delta^{-m}\cdot C(n,s)
,

(
\theta
-4)

and
\|{\theta}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}\leq\delta^{-s}\cdot C(n,s)
, thanks to (
4.4
).

We define

(4.12)

\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]:=\theta\cdot P.

Conclusion (A) follows from property (
\theta
-1).

Conclusion (B) follows from (
4.8
) and properties (
\theta
-2) and (
\theta
-3).

Now we prove conclusion (C). Write
F:=\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P]
. For
1\leq m<s
and
x\in Q
,

(4.13)

\nabla^{m}F(x)=\sum_{k+l=m}C(k,l)\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}P(x)

For each summand, we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}P(x)\right|^{s}}{\theta(x)^{s-m}P(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}&\leq C(n,s)\left(\theta(x)^{l}P(x)^{k}\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\right|^{s}}{\theta(x)^{s-k}}\frac{\left|\nabla^{l}P(x)\right|^{s}}{P(x)^{s-l}}\right)^{1/m}\\
&\leq C(n,s)\theta(x)^{l/m}P(x)^{k/m}\|{\theta}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}^{k/m}\|{P}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(Q)}^{l/m}\\
\text{by ($\theta$-4) and \eqref{eq:lem:Whitney-single-Pflat3}}&\leq C(n,s)\delta^{sk/m}\delta^{-sk/m}\leq C(n,s).\end{split}

Combine (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we have

\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq C(n,s)\sum_{k+l=m}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}P(x)\right|^{s}}{\theta(x)^{s-m}P(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq C(n,s).

Since (
4.13
) holds for all
x\in Q
and
1\leq m<s
, conclusion (C) follows.

We turn to conclusion (D).

For
x,y\in Q
and
k,l
with
k+l=m=\lfloor s\rfloor
,

(4.15)

\begin{split}&\left|\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}P(x)-\nabla^{k}\theta(y)\otimes\nabla^{l}P(y)\right|\leq\\
&\quad\quad\quad\left|\nabla^{k}\theta(x)\otimes(\nabla^{l}P(x)-\nabla^{l}P(y))\right|+\left|(\nabla^{k}\theta(x)-\nabla^{k}\theta(y))\otimes\nabla^{l}P(y)\right|=:A_{1}+A_{2}\end{split}

Thanks to (
4.9
) and (
\theta
-4), we have

(4.16)

\begin{split}A_{1}&\leq C(n,s)\|{\nabla^{k}\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\|{\nabla^{l+1}P}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\left|x-y\right|\\
&\leq C(n,s)\delta^{-k}\delta^{s-l-1}\left|x-y\right|\\
&\leq C(n,s)\left|x-y\right|^{s-m}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(4.17)

\begin{split}A_{2}&\leq C(n,s)\|{\nabla^{k+1}\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\left|x-y\right|\|{\nabla^{l}P}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\\
&\leq C(n,s)\delta^{-(k+1)}\delta^{s-l}\left|x-y\right|\\
&\leq C(n,s)\left|x-y\right|^{s-m}.\end{split}

It follows from (
4.13
), (
4.15
)–(
4.17
) that

\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s).

Finally, using the assumption that
\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|\leq 1
for
0\leq m<s
, we can apply (
4.9
) and (
\theta
-4) to estimate (
4.13
) to obtain

\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|\leq C(n,s)\text{ for all $x\in Q$ and $0\leq m<s$}.

Conclusion (D) follows.

∎

4.3
Whitney Extension Theorem for finite sets

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set. A finite
Whitney decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to
E
is a collection of dyadic cubes
\Lambda_{E}
defined by

\Lambda_{E}:=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Lambda_{Q}\text{ where }\Lambda_{Q}:=\begin{cases}\left\{Q\right\}&\text{ if }\#(E\cap 3Q)\leq 1\\
\bigcup\left\{\Lambda_{Q^{\prime}}:\text{ $(Q^{\prime})^{+}=Q$}\right\}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

Given
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{E}
, we say that
Q
touches
Q^{\prime}
if their closures have nonempty intersection.
The cubes in
\Lambda
satisfy a nice geometric property:

(4.18)

1/2\delta_{Q}\leq\delta_{Q^{\prime}}\leq 2\delta_{Q}\text{ whenever $Q$ touches $Q^{\prime}$}.

As a consequence,

(4.19)

\#\left\{Q\in\Lambda_{E}:1.1Q\ni x\right\}\leq 2^{n}\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For each
Q\in\Lambda_{E}
, we set

\varphi_{Q}(x):=\varphi_{0}\left(2(x-c_{Q})/(1.1\delta_{Q})\right)

with
\varphi_{0}
as in (
4.2
).
Then

\supp{\varphi_{Q}}=1.1Q,\,\left|\nabla^{k}\varphi_{Q}(x)\right|\leq\delta_{Q}^{-k}\cdot C(n,k),\text{ and }\|{\varphi_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta_{Q}^{-s}\cdot C(n,s)\text{ for }0\leq k<s.

We define

\theta_{Q}(x):=\frac{\varphi_{1.1Q}(x)}{\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}}\varphi_{1.1Q}(x)}.

The denominator is a finite sum, thanks to (
4.19
).

\left\{\theta_{Q}:Q\in\Lambda_{E}\right\}
has the following properties.

(POU-1)

0\leq\theta_{Q}\leq 1
, and
\sum_{Q\in\Lambda}\theta_{Q}\equiv 1
;

(POU-2)

\supp{\theta_{Q}}\subset 1.1Q
for each
Q\in\Lambda_{E}
, so each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
lies in the support of at most
2^{n}
partition functions;

(POU-3)

\left|\nabla^{m}\theta_{Q}\right|\leq\delta_{Q}^{-m}\cdot C(n,s)
for
1\leq m<s
;

(POU-4)

\|{\theta_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta^{-s}\cdot C(n,s)
.

For each
Q\in\Lambda_{E}
, we define a map
\mathcal{T}_{Q}
as follows.

•

Suppose
E\cap 3Q\neq\varnothing
, we pick
x_{Q}\in E\cap 3Q
and set
\mathcal{T}_{Q}:=\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}
, with
\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}
as in Lemma
4.1
. We set
P_{Q}\equiv P_{x_{Q}}
.

•

Suppose
E\cap 3Q=\varnothing
and
\delta_{Q}<1
. Then
E\cap 3Q^{+}\neq\varnothing
. We fix any
x_{Q}\in E\cap 3Q^{+}
and set
\mathcal{T}_{Q}:=\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}
, with
\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}
as in Lemma
4.1
. We set
P_{Q}\equiv P_{x_{Q}}
.

•

Suppose
E\cap 3Q=\varnothing
and
\delta_{Q}=1
. We set
\mathcal{T}_{Q}:=
the zero map. We set
P_{Q}\equiv 0
.

Define
\mathcal{T}_{E}:\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)\to{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

(4.20)

\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}](x):=\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}}\theta_{Q}(x)\cdot\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}[P_{Q}](x).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set. The map
\mathcal{T}_{E}:\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)\to{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined in (
4.20
) satisfies the following properties.

(A)

\mathcal{J}_{E}\circ\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]\equiv\vec{P}
for all
\vec{P}\in\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
,

(B)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(E)}
, and

(C)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}
, and hence,
\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}
.

Proof.

Let
F:=\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]
and let
F_{Q}:=\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}[P_{Q}]
for
Q\in\Lambda_{E}
.

Since
F
is defined to be the convex combination of nonnegative functions, we have
F\geq 0
.

Conclusion (A) follows from Lemma
4.1
(A) and the fact that
\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}}\theta_{Q}\equiv 1
.

Conclusion (B) is almost identical to the proof of the classical Whitney Extension Theorem for
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
22
]
. The first minor difference is that we use a finite cutoff of the Whitney decomposition. Secondly,
we glue together
F_{Q}
instead of
P_{Q}
to preserve nonnegativity, which is harmless thanks to Taylor’s theorem.

Now we prove conclusion (C). Note that there is nothing to prove if
s\leq 1
. For the rest of the proof, we assume
s>1
. We set

M_{1}:=\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(E)}\text{ and }M_{2}:=\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(E)}.

For
1\leq m<s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Q(x)\in\Lambda_{E}
containing
x
, the product rule yields

(4.21)

\nabla^{m}F(x)=\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}}\theta_{Q}(x)\nabla^{m}F_{Q}(x)+\sum_{Q\text{ touches }Q(x)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+l=m\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}C(k,l)\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x).

Note that in (
4.21
), every sum has bounded number of summands, thanks to (POU-2) and (
4.19
). We will repeatedly use this fact without further referencing it.

Using (
4.21
), we see that to estimate
\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}}
, it suffices to estimate the following two types of terms:

A_{0,m}:=\left(\frac{\left|\theta_{Q}(x)\nabla^{m}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\text{ and }A_{k,l}:=\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m},

with the understanding that
Q
touches
Q(x)
and
k+l=m
.

For the first type, we have

(4.22)

A_{0,m}\leq\theta_{Q}(x)^{s/m}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{F_{Q}(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq\|{F_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M_{2}.

For the analysis of
A_{k,l}
, first we estimate
\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x)\right|
. By the triangle inequality,

(4.23)

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x)\right|&\leq\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-P_{Q})(x)\right|+\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q(x)}-P_{Q(x)})(x)\right|+\left|\nabla^{l}(P_{Q}-P_{Q(x)})(x)\right|.\end{split}

Thanks to Lemma
4.1
(B) and Taylor’s theorem, we have

(4.24)

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-P_{Q})(x)\right|&\leq\left|x-x_{Q}\right|^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)\|{F_{Q}}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{1},\text{ and }\\
\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q(x)}-P_{Q(x)})(x)\right|&\leq\left|x-x_{Q(x)}\right|^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)\|{F_{Q(x)}}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{1}.\end{split}

Recall that
x_{Q}\in E\cap 3Q^{+}
for every
Q\in\Lambda_{E}
. Thanks to (
4.18
) and Taylor’s theorem, we have

(4.25)

\left|\nabla^{l}(P_{Q}-P_{Q(x)})(x)\right|\leq\left|x_{Q}-x_{Q(x)}\right|^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{1}\leq\delta^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{1}.

Combining (
4.23
)–(
4.25
) above, we have

(4.26)

\left|\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x)\right|\leq\delta^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{1}.

We then consider two cases.

Case I:
F(x)\geq\delta^{s}\cdot M_{2}
.

Thanks to (
4.26
) and (POU-3), we have

(4.27)

\begin{split}A_{k,l}\leq\left(\delta^{-ks}\delta^{(s-l)s}\delta^{-(s-m)s}\right)^{1/m}C(n,s)(M_{1}+M_{2})=C(n,s)(M_{1}+M_{2}).\end{split}

Case II:
F(x)<\delta^{s}\cdot M_{2}

This implies that

(4.28)

F_{Q}(x)\leq\delta^{s}\cdot M_{2}\text{ for every $Q\in\Lambda_{E}$ with $1.1Q\ni x$.}

Since
\|{F_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M_{2}
by Lemma
4.1
, we see that

\left|\nabla^{l}F_{Q}(x)\right|\leq\delta^{s-l}\cdot C(n,s)M_{2}.

Therefore,

(4.29)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}&\leq\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{\theta_{Q}^{s-m}F_{Q}(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\\
&\leq C(n,s)\left(\theta_{Q}(x)^{l}F_{Q}(x)^{k}\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{\theta_{Q}(x)^{s-m}}\frac{\left|\nabla^{l}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{F_{Q}(x)^{s-l}}\right)^{1/m}\\
\text{\eqref{eq:FQ-small} and (POU-3)}&\leq C(n,s)\theta_{Q}(x)^{l/m}F_{Q}(x)^{k/m}\|{\theta_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{k/m}\|{F_{Q}}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{l/m}\\
&\leq\delta^{k/m}\delta^{-k/m}\cdot C(n,s)M_{2}=C(n,s)M_{2}.\end{split}

Since
Q
touches
Q(x)
,
\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\right|\leq C(n,s)\theta_{Q(x)}
. By a similar argument as in (
4.29
), we have

(4.30)

\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}F_{Q(x)}(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\leq C(n,s)M_{2}.

Combining (
4.29
) and (
4.30
), we can conclude that

(4.31)

A_{k,l}\leq C(n,s)M_{2}.

Thanks to (
4.22
), (
4.27
), and (
4.31
), we have

(4.32)

\begin{split}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}F(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}&\leq C(n,s)\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}}\left(\frac{\left|\theta_{Q}(x)\nabla^{m}F_{Q}(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\\
&\quad\quad\quad+C(n,s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\text{ touches }Q(x)\\
k+l=m\\
k\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}\theta_{Q}(x)\otimes\nabla^{l}(F_{Q}-F_{Q(x)})(x)\right|^{s}}{F(x)^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}\\
&\leq C(n,s)M_{2}.\end{split}

Since (
4.32
) holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0\leq m<s
, we see that conclusion (C) follows. This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

4.4
Whitney Extension Theorem for arbitrary closed sets

For this section, all the dyadic cubes are assumed to be closed.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set (not necessarily finite). Let
\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}
be the classical Whitney decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
defined by

\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}:=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Lambda_{Q}^{\infty}\text{ where }\Lambda_{Q}^{\infty}:=\begin{cases}\left\{Q\right\}&\text{ if }E\cap 3Q=\varnothing\\
\bigcup\left\{\Lambda_{Q^{\prime}}^{\infty}:\text{ $(Q^{\prime})^{+}=Q$}\right\}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

As before,

1/2\delta_{Q}\leq\delta_{Q^{\prime}}\leq 2\delta_{Q}\text{ whenever $Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}$ touch each other.}

Thus,
cubes in
\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}
have bounded overlap, and the partition of unity
\left\{\theta_{Q}:Q\in\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}\right\}
adapted to
\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}
satisfies (POU-1)–(POU-4).

For each
Q\in\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}
, we pick
x_{Q}\in E
(not necessarily unique) such that

\mathrm{dist}({x_{Q}},{E})=\mathrm{dist}({E},{Q}).

For
\vec{P}=(P_{y})_{y\in E}\in\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
We define

(4.33)

\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}](x)=\begin{cases}P_{x}(x)&\text{ if }x\in E\\
\sum_{Q\in\Lambda_{E}^{\infty}}\theta_{Q}(x)\mathcal{T}_{x_{Q}}[P_{x_{Q}}](x)&\text{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set. The map
\mathcal{T}_{E}:\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)\to{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined in (
4.33
) satisfies the following properties.

(A)

\mathcal{J}_{E}\circ\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]\equiv\vec{P}
for all
\vec{P}\in\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)
,

(B)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(E)}
, and

(C)

\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}
, and thus,
\|{\mathcal{T}_{E}[\vec{P}]}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}
.

The proof of Theorem
4.3
uses similar quantitative estimates as in Theorem
4.2
along with a convergence argument. The details can be found in
[
5
,
32
,
37
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

be a finite set. For each

, let

-Whitney convex

•

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]

x_{0}\in E
,

•

\left|\nabla^{m}(P_{1}-P_{2})(x_{0})\right|\leq M\delta^{s-m}

0\leq m<s

•

\left|\nabla^{m}Q_{j}(x_{0})\right|\leq\delta^{-m}
for

,

,

P:\equiv\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}\left(Q_{1}^{2}P_{1}+Q_{2}^{2}P_{2}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(x_{0},A_{0}M).

By adapting the proof of the Finiteness Principle for shape fields from

19
]

[

,
12
]
) to
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}

Theorem 5.1

(
{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
Finiteness Principle for Shape Fields)
.

There exists a large constant
k=k(n,s)
such that the following hold.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set. Let
(\Gamma_{0}(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
be a
(A_{0},\delta_{0})
-Whitney convex shape field. Let

and
M_{0}\geq 0
be given. Suppose for every
S\subset E
with
\#S\leq k

\vec{P}=(P_{x})_{x\in S}

P_{x}\in\Gamma_{0}(x,M)
for all
x\in S
, and
\|{\vec{P}}\|_{\mathcal{J}{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(S)}\leq M_{0}
. Then there exist
P_{0}\in\Gamma_{0}(x_{0},M_{0})
and
F_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}(Q_{0})

\mathcal{J}_{x}F_{0}\in\Gamma_{0}(x,C(n,s,A_{0})M_{0})

and

(C)

\left|\nabla^{m}F_{0}(x)\right|\leq C(n,s,A_{0})M
for all

and

.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set and let

. For each
x\in E
and
M\geq 0
, we define

(5.1)

\Gamma_{f}(x,M)=\left\{P\in\Gamma(x,M):P(x)=f(x)\right\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite set and let
f:E\to[0,\infty)
. Then

(\Gamma_{f}(x,M))_{x\in E,M\geq 0}
is a
(C,1)
-Whitney convex shape field for some

Proof.

It is clear that
\Gamma_{f}\left(x,M\right)
is convex and
\Gamma_{f}(x,M)\subset\Gamma_{f}(x,M^{\prime})
whenever
M^{\prime}\geq M
. It remains to establish
(C,1)
-Whitney convexity.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
, and let
x_{0}\in E

P_{1},P_{2}\in\Gamma_{f}(x_{0},M)
for some

and

\displaystyle P(x_{0})

(5.7)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq m<s}\left(\frac{\left|\nabla^{m}P(x_{0})\right|}{P(x_{0})^{s-m}}\right)^{1/m}

Note that (
5.7
) follows immediately from (
5.4
), (
5.5
), and the assumption that
P_{1},P_{2}\in\Gamma_{f}(x_{0},M)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}
be as in Lemma
4.1
, and we set
F_{j}:=\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}[P_{j}]
for
j=1,2
. Then

(5.9)

F_{j}\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\|{F_{j}}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M,\text{ and }\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}F_{j}\equiv P_{j}\text{ for }j=1,2.

Thanks to (
5.4
), we may assume (interchanging
Q_{1}
and

if necessary)

(5.10)

Q_{1}(x_{0})\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

For sufficiently small

(using a similar argument as in Lemma
3.1
(A)), (
5.3
) and (
5.10
) yields

(5.11)

Q_{1}(x)\geq\frac{1}{10}\text{ for }x\in Q(x_{0},c_{0}\delta),

a cube with center
x_{0}
and
\delta_{Q}=c_{0}\delta
.
Fix

as in (
5.11
). Let
\chi(x):=\varphi_{0}(\frac{x-x_{0}}{c_{0}\delta})
with
\varphi_{0}
as in (

). Then

(5.12)

0\leq\chi\leq 1,\,\supp{\chi}\subset Q(x_{0},c_{0}\delta),\,\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}\chi\equiv 1,\,\text{ and }\left|\nabla^{m}\chi\right|\leq C(n,x)\delta^{-m}\text{ for }0\leq m<s.

Define
\varphi_{1}:=\chi\cdot Q_{1}+(1-\chi)
and
\varphi_{2}=\chi\cdot Q_{2}
, and
\theta_{j}:=\frac{\varphi_{j}}{\sqrt{\varphi_{1}^{2}+\varphi_{2}^{2}}}
. Then

and

enjoy the following properties.

(

and

(

(

5.2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

First, we prove the finite-set version of Theorem
1.3
. Recall that, given
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f:S\to[0,\infty)
,

\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}:=\inf\left\{\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }F=f\text{ on }S\right\}.

Theorem 5.2

(Finiteness Principle for
{\mathcal{F}^{s}}
, finite set version)
.

There exist constants
k^{\sharp}=k^{\sharp}(n,s)
and
C^{\sharp}=C^{\sharp}(n,s)
such that the following holds. Given any finite subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f:E\to[0,\infty)
,

(5.18)

\max_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}\leq\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(E)}\leq C^{\sharp}\cdot\max_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}.

Proof.

The first inequality is immediate. We shall prove the second. We shall prove the case when
E\subset 0.5Q_{0}
where
\delta_{Q_{0}}=1
. The general case follows immediately if we apply a partition of unity adapted to a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by unit cubes.
Set

M_{0}:=2\max_{S\subset E,\,\#S\leq k^{\sharp}}\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}<\infty.

It suffices to exhibit a function
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
F=f
on
E
and
\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,s)M_{0}
.

Recall from (
5.1
) that

\Gamma_{f}(x,M)=\left\{P\in\Gamma(x,M):P(x)=f(x)\right\}\text{ for each }x\in E.

By the definition of
\|{f}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(S)}
, given any
S\subset E
with
\#S\leq k^{\sharp}
, there exists
F_{S}\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F_{S}=f
on
S
and
\|{F_{S}}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq M_{0}
. Let
\vec{P}_{S}:=\mathcal{J}_{S}F_{S}=(\mathcal{J}_{x}F_{S})_{x\in S}
.
By Taylor’s theorem
4.1
,

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, let
\Pi(\alpha)
denotes all the possible partition of
\alpha
, i.e.,

\Pi(\alpha)=\left\{\pi=\left\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{k}\right\}:k\in\mathbb{N},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\text{ for all }j,\text{ and }\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}=\alpha\right\}.

Theorem A.1

(Multivariate Faá di Bruno’s Formula,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an interval. Let
h:I\to\mathbb{R}
and
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to I
be sufficiently smooth. Then

(A.1)

\partial^{\alpha}(h\circ F)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(\alpha)}\left(h^{(\left|\pi\right|)}\circ F\right)\cdot\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F.

Here,
\left|\pi\right|
is the cardinality of
\pi
and
h^{(k)}
is the
k
-th derivative of
h
.

We are mainly interested in Theorem
A.1
for case
h(t)=t^{r}
for some
r\in(0,1)
. Thus,
h^{(k)}=C(k,r)t^{r-k}
for some
C(k,r)\neq 0
.

Lemma A.1
.

Given
r\in(0,1)
,
s>0
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
convex,
F\in{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)
, and
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

(A.2)

\left|\nabla^{m}F^{r}(x)\right|\leq C(n,r,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}^{m/s}F(x)^{r-m/s}\text{ for all $0\leq m<s$,}

and

(A.3)

\left|\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F^{r}(x)-\nabla^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F^{r}(y)\right|\leq C(n,r,s)\frac{\left(\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}(\Omega)}+\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}(\Omega)}\right)}{\min\left\{F(x)^{1-r},F(y)^{1-r}\right\}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-\lfloor s\rfloor}.

Moreover, if
F\geq\epsilon>0
, then

(A.4)

\|{F^{r}}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C(n,r,s)\epsilon^{r-1}\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Lemma 2 in
[
35
]
to the combinatorial form of the multivariate Faá di Bruno’s formula.

If
0<s\leq 1
, then (
A.2
) is trivial and (
A.3
) follows from the mean value theorem. From now on, we assume
s>1
, and we set

m_{0}:=\lfloor s\rfloor.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
F(y)\leq F(x)
and
\|{F}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}+\|{F}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}=1
.

First of all, by the definition of
\|{F}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{s}}
,

(A.5)

\left|\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(x)\right|\leq\prod_{\beta\in\pi}F(x)^{\frac{s-\left|\beta\right|}{s}}=F(x)^{\left|\pi\right|-\frac{m_{0}}{s}}.

Applying Faá di Bruno’s Formula (
A.1
) and (
A.5
), we see that

(A.6)

\left|\partial^{\alpha}F^{r}(x)\right|\leq C(n,r,s)\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(\alpha)}F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}F(x)^{\left|\pi\right|-\frac{m_{0}}{s}}\leq C(n,r,s)F(x)^{r-\frac{m_{0}}{s}}.

We see that (
A.2
) follows from (
A.6
).

For the rest of the proof, we fix an arbitrary multi-index
\alpha
with
\left|\alpha\right|=m_{0}
and a partition
\pi
of
\alpha
. Note that the following arguments hold for
\tilde{\alpha}
with
\left|\tilde{\alpha}\right|<m_{0}
after suitable simplification.

Case I: suppose
\left|x-y\right|\leq c_{0}F(x)^{1/s}

with
c_{0}
as in Lemma
3.1
with
\epsilon=1/2
.

Thus,
F(y)/2\leq F(x)\leq 3F(y)/2
, which we will use freely throughout rest of the proof.

By the triangle inequality,

(A.7)

\begin{split}&\left|F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(x)-F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right|\\
&\quad\quad\leq\left|\left(F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}-F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\right)\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(x)\right|+\left|F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\left(\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(x)-\prod_{\beta\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right)\right|\\
&=:M_{1}+M_{2}.\end{split}

Let
u=\frac{y-x}{\left|y-x\right|}
. By the mean value theorem applied to
F
along the
u
-direction, there exists a point
\xi=\xi_{x,y,\alpha}
on the open segment connecting
x
and
y
such that

(A.8)

\begin{split}&\left|F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}-F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\right|=\left|F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}-F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\right|^{(s-m_{0})+(1-(s-m_{0}))}\\
&\leq\left|(r-\left|\pi\right|)F(\xi)^{r-\left|\pi\right|-1}\nabla_{u}F(\xi)\left|x-y\right|\right|^{s-m_{0}}\cdot\left|F(x)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}+F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\right|^{1-(s-m_{0})}\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)\left|F(y)\right|^{-\left|\pi\right|-1+r+m_{0}/s}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.\end{split}

In light of (
A.5
) and (
A.8
), we see that

(A.9)

M_{1}\leq C(n,r,s)F(y)^{r-1}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.

Using a similar argument as in (
A.8
), we see that for
\left|\beta\right|\leq m_{0}-1

(A.10)

\begin{split}\left|\partial^{\beta}F(x)-\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right|&\leq\left|\nabla_{u}\partial^{\beta}F(\xi)\left|x-y\right|\right|^{s-m_{0}}\left|\partial^{\beta}F(x)-\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right|^{1-(s-m_{0})}\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)\left|F(\xi)\right|^{\frac{s-\left|\beta\right|-1}{s}(s-m_{0})}\left|F(y)\right|^{\frac{s-\left|\beta\right|}{s}(1-s+k)}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)\left|F(y)\right|^{\frac{m_{0}-\left|\beta\right|}{s}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.\end{split}

For
\left|\beta\right|=m_{0}
, since
F\in{\mathcal{C}^{s}}
, we have

(A.11)

\left|\partial^{\beta}F(x)-\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right|\leq\|{F}\|_{{\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{s}}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}\leq\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}=\left|F(y)\right|^{\frac{m_{0}-\left|\beta\right|}{s}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.

Now we estimate
M_{2}
.

Temporarily fix
\beta\in\pi
,
x,y\in\Omega
. We define two arrays
(z_{\gamma,\beta})_{\gamma\in\pi}
and
(w_{\gamma,\beta})_{\gamma\in\pi}
with the following properties:

•

z_{\beta,\beta}=x
and
w_{\beta,\beta}=y
;

•

Either
z_{\beta,\gamma}=w_{\beta,\gamma}=x
for all
\gamma\neq\beta
or
z_{\beta,\gamma}=w_{\beta,\gamma}=y
for all
\gamma\neq\beta
.

Using (
A.10
) and (
A.11
), we have

(A.12)

\begin{split}&F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\left|\prod_{\gamma\in\pi}\partial^{\gamma}F(z_{\beta,\gamma})-\prod_{\gamma\in\pi}\partial^{\gamma}F(w_{\beta,\gamma})\right|\\
&=F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\prod_{\gamma\in\pi,\gamma\neq\beta}\left|\partial^{\gamma}F(z_{\beta,\gamma})\right|\left|\partial^{\beta}F(x)-\partial^{\beta}F(y)\right|\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\left(\prod_{\gamma\in\pi,\gamma\neq\beta}F(z_{\beta,\gamma})^{\frac{s-\left|\gamma\right|}{s}}\right){F(y)}^{\frac{(s-\left|\beta\right|)-(s-m_{0})}{s}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)F(y)^{r-1}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.\end{split}

In the last step, we used the fact that
\sum_{\gamma\in\pi}\gamma=\left|\alpha\right|=m_{0}
.
By repeatedly applying triangle inequality and using (
A.12
), we have

(A.13)

\begin{split}M_{2}&\leq F(y)^{r-\left|\pi\right|}\sum_{\beta\in\pi}\left|\prod_{\gamma\in\pi}\partial^{\gamma}F(z_{\beta,\gamma})-\prod_{\gamma\in\pi}\partial^{\beta}F(w_{\beta,\gamma})\right|\leq C(n,r,s)F(y)^{r-1}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.\end{split}

In light of Faá di Bruno’s Formula (
A.1
), estimates (
A.7
), (
A.9
), and (
A.13
), we see that (
A.3
) holds in the case
\left|x-y\right|\leq c(n,s)F(x)^{1/s}
.

Case II: suppose
\left|x-y\right|>c_{0}F(x)^{1/s}

with
c_{0}
as in Lemma
3.1
with
\epsilon=1/2
. Equivalently,
F(x)\leq C(n,s)\left|x-y\right|^{s}
.

Since
F(y)\leq F(x)\leq C(n,s)\left|x-y\right|^{s}
, (
A.6
) implies

(A.14)

\begin{split}\left|\partial^{\alpha}F(x)-\partial^{\alpha}F(y)\right|&\leq C(n,r,s)\left(F(x)^{r-m_{0}/s}+F(y)^{r-m_{0}/s}\right)\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)\frac{1}{F(y)^{1-r}}F(x)^{(s-m_{0})/s}\\
&\leq C(n,r,s)\frac{1}{F(y)^{1-r}}\left|x-y\right|^{s-m_{0}}.\end{split}

We see that (
A.3
) follows from (
A.14
) in the case
\left|x-y\right|>c(n,s)F(x)^{1/s}
.

Thus, we have shown (
A.3
) for arbitrary

.

Finally, we turn to (
A.4
). Suppose

on
\Omega
. It follows from (
A.3
) and (
A.2
) that

(A.15)

\|{F^{r}}\|_{{\mathcal{C}^{s}}}\leq C(n,r,s)\epsilon^{r-1}\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}}

On the other hand, we see from (
A.2
) that

(A.16)

\begin{split}\left|\nabla^{m}F^{r}(x)\right|&\leq C(n,r,s)\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}}^{m/s}F(x)^{r-m/s}\left(F(x)/\epsilon\right)^{\frac{m(1-r)}{s}}\\
&=C(n,r,s)\left(\|{F}\|_{{\mathcal{F}^{s}}}/\epsilon^{1-r}\right)^{m/s}\left(F(x)^{r}\right)^{\frac{s-m}{s}}.\end{split}

In light of (
A.15
) and (
A.16
), we see that (
A.4
) holds.
∎

.

By rescaling, we may assume that

Effective Field Theory of Black Hole Perturbations
in Vector-Tensor Gravity

Abstract
We formulate the effective field theory (EFT) of vector-tensor gravity for perturbations around an arbitrary background with a timelike vector profile, which can be applied to study black hole perturbations. The vector profile spontaneously breaks both the time diffeomorphism and the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry, leaving their combination and the spatial diffeomorphism as the residual symmetries in the unitary gauge. We derive two sets of consistency relations which guarantee the residual symmetries of the EFT. Also, we provide the dictionary between our EFT coefficients and those of generalized Proca (GP) theories, which enables us to identify a simple subclass of the EFT that includes the GP theories as a special case. For this subclass, we consider the stealth Schwarzschild(-de Sitter) background solution with a constant temporal component of the vector field and study the decoupling limit of the longitudinal mode of the vector field, explicitly showing that the strong coupling problem arises due to vanishing sound speeds. This is in sharp contrast to the case of gauged ghost condensate, in which perturbations are weakly coupled thanks to certain higher-derivative terms, i.e., the scordatura terms. This implies that, in order to consistently describe this type of stealth solutions within the EFT, the scordatura terms must necessarily be taken into account in addition to those already included in the simple subclass.

2
General construction of the EFT

2.1
Symmetry breaking pattern and unitary gauge

In this Section, we will explain how to construct the EFT action of vector-tensor theories with a timelike vector field on an arbitrary background metric.
This kind of EFT will be particularly useful for describing the dynamics of black hole perturbations in the context of modified gravity theories and thus, e.g., determining their quasinormal mode spectrum.
As we have already mentioned, the EFT of vector-tensor theories on a cosmological background has been formulated in
[
50
]
, to which the EFT we are going to formulate in this paper must reduce on a very large scale.

Similar to the EFT on a cosmological background
[
50
]
, our starting point is the existence of a non-vanishing timelike vector field
v_{\mu}(x)
. This background vector field breaks part of the 4d spacetime diffeomorphism and defines a preferred threading of spacetime. In order to identify the symmetry breaking pattern of our EFT, it is more convenient to express the vector field as follows:

\displaystyle v_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}{\tilde{\tau}}+g_{M}A_{\mu}\,;\quad g_{M}\equiv\frac{g}{M_{\star}}\;,

(2.1)

where
{\tilde{\tau}}
is a scalar field,
A_{\mu}
is a vector field,
g
is a gauge coupling, and
M_{\star}
is a mass scale of the order of the Planck mass (which may be different from the observed Planck mass
M_{\rm Pl}
). The vector field (
2.1
) is invariant under the
U(1)
transformation

\displaystyle{\tilde{\tau}}\rightarrow{\tilde{\tau}}-g_{M}\chi\;,\quad A_{\mu}\rightarrow A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\chi\;,

(2.2)

where
\chi(x)
is an arbitrary scalar function. One may choose the gauge
\tilde{\tau}=0
in which (
2.1
) is simply a rescaling of
v_{\mu}
. In our setup, on the other hand, the vector field
v_{\mu}
is assumed to have a timelike expectation value, and another gauge choice is more appropriate to understand the symmetry breaking pattern.
Since
v_{\mu}
is timelike, it is convenient to choose a coordinate system such that
{\tilde{\tau}}
coincides with the time coordinate
\tau
. In this coordinate (or gauge) choice, the vector field (
2.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle v_{\mu}\big|_{{\tilde{\tau}}=\tau}=\delta_{\ \mu}^{\tau}+g_{M}A_{\mu}\equiv{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}\;.

(2.3)

We call this gauge the
unitary gauge
. Now, as
{\tilde{\tau}}
is identified with the time coordinate, the transformation (
2.2
) should be understood as the combination of the time diffeomorphism and the
U(1)
transformation.
Therefore, our EFT setup possesses the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
) on top of the usual 3d spatial diffeomorphism.
*2
*2

*2

This symmetry breaking pattern is the same as that of gauged ghost condensation
[
56
]
, as pointed out in
[
57
]
.

As it can be seen from (
2.1
) or (
2.3
),
v_{\mu}
is not hypersurface-orthogonal in general and determines a
preferred time direction (or threading)
. On the other hand, in the EFT of scalar-tensor theories (see e.g.,
[
43
,
45
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
), there is a
preferred time slicing
, defined by a hypersurface-orthogonal vector
n_{\mu}\propto\partial_{\mu}{\tilde{\tau}}
which is obviously not invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
).
*3
*3

*3

Typically, the preferred time slicing in the EFT of scalar-tensor theories can be defined via
n_{\mu}\propto\partial_{\mu}\bar{\Phi}
, where
\bar{\Phi}
is a time-dependent background of a scalar field.
Therefore, our EFT of vector-tensor theories with a timelike vector field is different than the usual EFT of scalar-tensor theories. Indeed, it is obtained from the EFT of shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theories
[
58
]
when the shift symmetry is gauged
[
56
]
.
Notice that, in the absence of the gauge coupling,
{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}
coincides with
\delta^{\tau}_{\ \mu}
, so that the EFT reduces to the one of scalar-tensor theories. Here and in what follows, bold quantities represent those with contributions of
A_{\mu}
.

Note that here we have not assumed any specific forms of both the background metric and the background gauge field. Our assumptions so far are (i) the existence of a preferred timelike vector field
{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}=v_{\mu}|_{{\tilde{\tau}}=\tau}
and (ii) the EFT in the unitary gauge is invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
) in addition to the 3d spatial diffeomorphism.

It would be worth mentioning that it should be also possible to formulate the EFT with a preferred
spacelike
vector field by following a similar procedure presented below. However, we focus on the timelike case in the present paper, so that our EFT can smoothly connect to the EFT on a cosmological background on large scales.

2.2
Building blocks

The tensors which are invariant under the 4d diffeomorphism and original
U(1)
symmetry like the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, 4d covariant derivative
\nabla_{\mu}
, 4d Riemann tensor
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
, and field strength of the vector field
F_{\mu\nu}
are trivial building blocks of our setup. In this Subsection, we look for the nontrivial building blocks which are invariant under the residual symmetries which consist of the 3d spatial diffeomorphism and the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
). The timelike vector
{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}
is covariant under the 3d diffeomorphism and invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry. Thus, its norm,

\displaystyle{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \nu}^{\tau}=g^{\tau\tau}+2g_{M}A^{\tau}+g_{M}^{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}\;,

(2.4)

which is negative for timelike
v_{\mu}
, is a building block. The unit future-directed timelike vector is defined by

\displaystyle{\bf\it n}_{\mu}\equiv-\frac{{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}}{\sqrt{-{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}}\;,\quad{\bf\it n}_{\mu}{\bf\it n}^{\mu}=-1\;.

(2.5)

Then, it is natural to define the projection tensor,

\displaystyle{\bf\it h}_{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}+{\bf\it n}_{\mu}{\bf\it n}_{\nu}\;,

(2.6)

which satisfies
{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \alpha}{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}_{\ \nu}={\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}_{\ \mu}{\bf\it n}_{\alpha}=0
.
The unit timelike vector and projection tensor allow one to define the quantities that are parallel or orthogonal to the vector
{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}
. However, there is no corresponding time slicing in general since
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}
is not hypersurface-orthogonal due to the non-vanishing vorticity which is provided by the transverse degrees of freedom in
A_{\mu}
. Therefore, the usual
3+1
ADM decomposition is not suitable here. Nevertheless, one can use
1+3
decomposition which takes into account the role of the vorticity. In Appendix A of
[
50
]
, the
1+3
decomposition is introduced and some details of calculations are presented. Here we only present the results.

The first derivative of the unit preferred vector is characterized by the expansion tensor, the vorticity tensor, and the acceleration vector defined respectively as

\displaystyle{\bf\it K}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}_{\ (\mu|}\nabla_{\alpha}{\bf\it n}_{|\nu)}\;,

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}_{\ [\mu|}\nabla_{\alpha}{\bf\it n}_{|\nu]}\;,

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\bf\it a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv{\bf\it n}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}{\bf\it n}_{\mu}\;,

(2.9)

where the symmetrization and the anti-symmetrization are denoted by
(\,)
and
[\,]
, respectively, and
\nabla_{\mu}
is the 4d covariant derivative.

The 4d covariant derivative
\nabla_{\mu}
can be also decomposed into the orthogonal and parallel components respectively as

\displaystyle\pounds_{{\bf\it n}}\;,\quad{\bf\it D}_{\mu}\equiv{\bf\it h}_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\;,

(2.10)

where
\pounds_{{\bf\it n}}
and
{\bf\it D}_{\mu}
denote the Lie derivative along
{{\bf\it n}}_{\mu}
and orthogonal covariant derivative, respectively.

Using the projection tensor, it is straightforward to define the orthogonal spatial curvature from the commutator of the orthogonal covariant derivative
[
50
]
.
*4
*4

*4

Note that there is no unique definition of the “spatial curvature” because of the absence of the spatial hypersurfaces, see Appendix B of
[
51
]
and references therein.
In practice, we can compute the orthogonal spatial curvature through the Gauss equation:

\displaystyle{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}{\bf\it h}^{\beta}{}_{\nu}{\bf\it h}^{\gamma}{}_{\rho}{\bf\it h}^{\delta}{}_{\sigma}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}={}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+2{\bf\it W}_{[\rho|\mu}{\bf\it W}_{|\sigma]\nu}\,,

(2.11)

where
{\bf\it W}_{\mu\nu}\equiv{\bf\it K}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}
and
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
is the 4d Riemann tensor. The orthogonal spatial Ricci curvature and its trace can be defined in a usual manner:

\displaystyle{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu}\equiv{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\alpha}_{\ (\mu|\alpha|\nu)}\;,\quad{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}\equiv{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\;.

(2.12)

Note that the indices are symmetrized to define the orthogonal spatial Ricci curvature. While the anti-symmetric part of
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\alpha}_{\ \mu\alpha\nu}
does not vanish differently from the case of the spatial curvature, it can be written in terms of
{\bf\it W}_{\mu\nu}
. Thus, it can be safely omitted from the building blocks of the EFT.

In the gauge field sector, the field strength tensor,

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}\;,

(2.13)

can be decomposed into the electric part
{\bf\it E}_{\mu}
and the magnetic part
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=-2{\bf\it E}_{[\mu}{\bf\it n}_{\nu]}+{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}\;,

(2.14)

where

\displaystyle{\bf\it E}_{\mu}\equiv{\bf\it n}^{\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}\;,\quad{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}\equiv{\bf\it h}^{\alpha}_{\ \mu}{\bf\it h}^{\beta}_{\ \nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\;.

(2.15)

For later convenience, we introduce a rank-2 tensor
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
via

\displaystyle{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\epsilon_{\mu\nu}^{\ \ \ \rho\alpha}{\bf\it n}_{\rho}{\bf\it E}_{\alpha}\;.

(2.16)

Here,
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\sqrt{-g}\,\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
denotes the covariant Levi-Civita tensor,
where
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the totally anti-symmetric Levi-Civita
symbol with
\varepsilon_{0123}=1
.
Note that we have the relation
{\bf\it E}_{\mu}=-\epsilon_{\mu}^{\ \ \nu\alpha\rho}{\bf\it n}_{\nu}{\bf\it E}_{\alpha\rho}/2
.
The reason why we have introduced this rank-2 tensor
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
will become clear in Subsection
2.3
.
From the definitions (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we explicitly see that the tensors
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
are anti-symmetric with respect to the indices
\mu
and
\nu
.
Also, among the tensors
F_{\mu\nu}
,
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
, and
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
, only two of them are independent, so throughout this paper we are going to only work with
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
.
Actually, it is useful to note that
F_{\mu\nu}
can be expressed in terms of the tensors
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}={\bf\it n}_{\lambda}{\bf\it E}_{\alpha\rho}\epsilon_{[\mu}^{\ \ \lambda\alpha\rho}{\bf\it n}_{\nu]}+{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}\;,\quad F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=-2{\bf\it E}^{\mu}{\bf\it E}_{\mu}+{\bf\it B}^{\mu\nu}{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}={\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it E}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it B}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;.

(2.17)

Additionally, from Eqs. (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), one finds that

\displaystyle{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{g_{M}}{2\sqrt{-{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}}{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}\;,

(2.18)

\displaystyle{\bf\it a}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{M}}{2\sqrt{-{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}}\epsilon_{\mu}^{\ \ \nu\alpha\beta}{\bf\it n}_{\nu}{\bf\it E}_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\partial_{\nu}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}{2{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\;.

(2.19)

The relations above imply that both
{\bf\it a}_{\mu}
and
{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}
are completely determined once
{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}
,
{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}
,
{\bf\it h}_{\mu\nu}
, and
{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}
are specified. Thus, we can safely disregard the presence of both
{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}
and
{\bf\it a}_{\mu}
since they are not independent building blocks. Indeed, in practice, working with
\{{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu},{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu}\}
instead of
\{{\bf\it a}_{\mu},{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}\}
is better since the limit
g_{M}\to 0
is manifest in this basis.

It is worth comparing the above
1+3
decomposition with the usual
3+1
ADM decomposition. In order to do so, we note that the normal vector
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}
does not define a constant-
\tau
hypersurface since there is a contribution coming from the transverse modes of
A_{\mu}
. Taking the limit
g_{M}\to 0
, we ignore the transverse modes and
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}
becomes hypersurface-orthogonal. Therefore, we should recover the usual well-known results of
3+1
ADM decomposition in the limit
g_{M}\to 0
. In this limit, from (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
), we see that
{\bf\it\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}\to\delta_{\ \mu}^{\tau}
,
{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\to g^{\tau\tau}
, and
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}\to n_{\mu}=-{\delta}_{\ \mu}^{\tau}/\sqrt{-{g}^{\tau\tau}}
. Accordingly, the projection tensor (
2.6
) reduces to the induced metric of the spatial hypersurfaces
{\bf\it h}_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+{n}_{\mu}{n}_{\nu}
. The expansion tensor reduces to the extrinsic curvature
{\bf\it K}_{\mu\nu}\to K_{\mu\nu}=h^{\alpha}_{\ (\mu|}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{|\nu)}
while, as expected, the vorticity tensor vanishes. Then,
\pounds_{{\bf\it n}}
and
{\bf\it D}_{\mu}
coincide with
\pounds_{{n}}
and the 3d spatial derivative
{D}_{\mu}
respectively and
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
reduces to the usual 3d curvature of the spatial hypersurfaces. It is interesting to note that in some particular situations, e.g., on a cosmological background, the geometrical quantities defined via
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}
and
{\bf\it h}_{\mu\nu}
coincide with the ones defined on the constant-
\tau
hypersurface. Such a situation
can be realized when the solution of
A_{\mu}
is assumed to be irrotational, i.e., the transverse modes are absent and the vorticity tensor
{\bf\it\omega}_{\mu\nu}
vanishes.

Now, we can easily write down the most general EFT action of vector-tensor theories that is invariant under the residual symmetries in the unitary gauge. On top of the 4d covariant building blocks
g_{\mu\nu}
,
\nabla_{\mu}
,
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
, and
F_{\mu\nu}
, we have found independent building blocks
\{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau},{\bf\it K}_{\mu\nu},\pounds_{{\bf\it n}},{\bf\it D}_{\mu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu},{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu}\}
which are covariant under the 3d diffeomorphism and invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
). As in the spatial curvature, the Weyl piece of
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
vanishes in
1+3
dimensions and we only need to use the Ricci part
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu}
as an independent building block. Therefore, the most general EFT action of vector-tensor theories in unitary gauge is given by

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~\mathcal{L}({\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu},{\bf\it K}_{\mu\nu},{\bf\it E}_{\mu\nu},{\bf\it B}_{\mu\nu},\pounds_{{\bf\it n}},{\bf\it D}_{\mu})\;,

(2.20)

where the contraction of indices, to construct scalar quantities, can be performed with respect to either the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
or the projection tensor
{\bf\it h}_{\mu\nu}
.
Note that here we use the orthogonal spatial Ricci curvature
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu}
, rather than the 4d Ricci curvature
R_{\mu\nu}
.
*5
*5

*5

As discussed in
[
50
]
, the 4d Ricci curvature
R_{\mu\nu}
contains time derivatives, which could result in an extra would-be ghostly state when one considers higher-order terms in the curvature. It was shown in
[
34
]
that the action of the GP theories, which does not contain a ghost state, can be written in terms of
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}_{\mu\nu}
.
It is important to emphasize that this EFT action in the unitary gauge can be generally applied to an arbitrary background geometry.
However, this form of the EFT action is not practically useful to describe the dynamics of both backgrounds and perturbations. In the next Subsection, we will expand such a covariant form of the EFT in terms of perturbations of the building blocks.

2.3
Consistency relations: expansion around an inhomogeneous background

In this Subsection, following the procedure done in
[
50
,
53
]
, we expand the general EFT action (
2.20
) in the unitary gauge around a non-trivial
\tau
- and
\vec{x}
-dependent background.
At the end, we will impose two sets of consistency relations among the EFT coefficients, which ensure the invariance of the EFT under both the 3d diffeomorphism and the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
).

In what follows, for simplicity, we shall focus on lower-derivative terms by assuming that terms involving the derivative operators
\pounds_{{\bf\it n}}
and
{\bf\it D}_{\mu}
are suppressed. We then obtain a simplified version of the EFT action (
2.20
) as follows:

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~\mathcal{L}({\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau},{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu})\;.

(2.21)

Here, the tensors are all rank-2 and always have one upper index and one lower index.
Written in this form, the contraction of the indices is always done by the Kronecker delta
\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, and hence the projection tensor
{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
does not appear explicitly in the Lagrangian.
This is why we have used the rank-2 tensor
{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
rather than the vector
{\bf\it E}_{\mu}
.
If we regarded
{\bf\it E}_{\mu}
as an independent variable, then the Lagrangian would have terms like
{\bf\it E}_{\mu}{\bf\it E}^{\mu}={\bf\it h}^{\mu\nu}{\bf\it E}_{\mu}{\bf\it E}_{\nu}
, which explicitly contains the projection tensor. Note that the way of the contraction does not matter because the rank-2 tensors are either symmetric or anti-symmetric in their indices, e.g.,
{\bf\it K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}{\bf\it K}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}={\bf\it K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}{\bf\it K}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}
.

We define perturbations around an arbitrary background metric

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\equiv{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}(\tau,\vec{x})\;,\quad\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(\tau,\vec{x})\;,\quad\delta{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-{}^{(3)}\!\bar{{\bf\it R}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(\tau,\vec{x})\;,\\
&\delta{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it E}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(\tau,\vec{x})\;,\quad\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(\tau,\vec{x})\;,\end{split}

(2.22)

where the background values are denoted with a bar and are generally
\tau
- and
\vec{x}
-dependent. Note that all the background quantities depend on the background of
A_{\mu}
.
Since here we are interested in the EFT on an arbitrary background metric, the background for the spatial components of
A_{\mu}
is allowed to be non-vanishing, which is in contrast to the case of cosmological background
[
50
]
.

One could separate
{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
into the trace and traceless parts with respect to
{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv\frac{{\bf\it K}}{3}{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,\quad{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv\frac{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}{3}{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,

(2.23)

respectively.
This decomposition is especially useful on a cosmological background where the background values of
{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
vanish (see
[
50
]
).
In any case, this is just a matter of notation and one could choose either
\{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\}
or
\{{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it K},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}\}
as a part of the EFT building blocks.
We will discuss more on the relation between the two conventions in Appendix
A
.

Let us proceed with the action (
2.21
).
The Taylor expansion of the action up to first order in perturbations defined in (
2.22
) is given by

\displaystyle S_{(1)}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\delta{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\delta{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bigg]\;,

(2.24)

where
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{X^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\equiv(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial X^{\mu}_{\ \nu})|_{\rm BG}
. The Taylor coefficients are made of the background quantities so they must have the same symmetries as the background if the background has. The first four terms in the action (
2.24
) were already present in the EFT of vector-tensor theories on the FLRW background
[
50
]
, whereas the last two terms with
\delta{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
are additionally present due to the fact that their coefficients
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
may acquire non-vanishing values in a general background which is not necessarily isotropic.
As usual, the above action up to the first order in perturbations is responsible for the background dynamics of both the metric and the gauge field.
Furthermore, the expansion of the action (
2.21
) at second order gives

\displaystyle S_{(2)}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}(\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau})^{2}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\bigg]\;,

(2.25)

where we have introduced a compact notation
{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\}
and defined
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}\equiv(\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}/\partial{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\partial{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta})|_{\rm BG}
etc.
Here and in what follows, we suppress the summation over the repeated indices
I
and
J
.

The time coordinate
\tau
and spatial coordinates
\vec{x}
are not the building blocks of the EFT and from the actions (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we see that both the residual symmetries of the EFT [i.e., the 3d diffeomorphism and the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
)] are apparently broken due to the fact that the Taylor coefficients evaluated on the non-trivial background generally depend on both
\tau
and
\vec{x}
. However, the fact that the whole Taylor series we have obtained is derived from the covariant action (
2.20
) which is invariant under the residual symmetries suggests that there must be a set of relations among the Taylor coefficients that guarantees invariance of the series expansions (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) under the residual symmetries.
Indeed, there are two sets of consistency relations: One is associated with the residual 3d diffeomorphism and the other with the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
).
Note that the former is similar to the one obtained in the context of the EFT of scalar-tensor theories on a generic background
[
53
]
, while the latter is similar to the one derived in the EFT of vector-tensor theories on a cosmological background
[
50
]
.

The first set of consistency relations which guarantees the 3d diffeomorphism invariance of the action is obtained by applying the chain rule with respect to the spatial derivatives.
Note that we use the fact that the Taylor coefficients may implicitly depend on
\vec{x}
through the background values.
Then, the chain rule applied to the derivative of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
with respect to
\vec{x}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{x}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\tau,\vec{x})=\

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\vec{x}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}({\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau},{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu})\bigg|_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial{}^{(3)}\!\bar{{\bf\it R}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it E}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\cdots\;,

(2.26)

where
\cdots
refers to higher-order terms in derivatives in case the more general action (
2.20
) involving the derivative operators
\pounds_{{\bf\it n}}
and
{\bf\it D}_{\mu}
is considered.
Note that on the RHS of the above equation, there is no spatial derivative acting on
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
since the action is not allowed to explicitly depend on
\vec{x}
.
Additionally, applying the chain rule to the spatial derivatives of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{x}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it Z}}^{J}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\cdots\;,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{x}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it Z}}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}{\partial\vec{x}}+\cdots\;.

(2.28)

Straightforwardly, one can apply the chain rule to the spatial derivatives of other higher-order terms (e.g.,
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
) to obtain their corresponding consistency relations.
In this way, we obtain a set of infinitely many consistency relations.
We see that these relations provide a connection among the Taylor coefficients order by order in perturbations; the coefficients cannot be arbitrarily chosen.
We now impose this set of consistency relations to make sure that the 3d diffeomorphism invariance is preserved.
In fact, we see that in the case where all the background quantities are independent of
\vec{x}
, e.g., cosmological background
[
50
]
, this set of relations is trivially satisfied.
In addition, when the gauge coupling is set to zero, this kind of consistency relations recovers the one found in
[
53
,
55
]
as expected.

Let us discuss the second set of consistency relations. This set is obtained by applying the chain rule to the time derivative of the Taylor coefficients in (
2.24
) and (
2.25
). Applying the chain rule to the time derivative of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\tau,\vec{x})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bar{\mathcal{L}}(\tau,\vec{x})=\

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\tau}\bar{\mathcal{L}}({\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau},{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu})\bigg|_{\rm BG}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial{}^{(3)}\!\bar{{\bf\it R}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it E}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\tau}+\cdots\;.

(2.29)

Notice that on the RHS of the above equation, there is no time derivative acting on
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
since the EFT action is not allowed to explicitly depend on
\tau
.
Similarly, the chain rule applied to the time derivatives of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it Z}}^{J}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{\partial\tau}+\cdots\;,

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}{\partial\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}\frac{\partial\bar{{\bf\it Z}}^{J}{}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}}{\partial\tau}+\cdots\;.

(2.31)

As before, one can straightforwardly apply the chain rule to the time derivatives of other terms, for instance,
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
, to obtain their corresponding consistency relations.
In this way, one again obtains a set of infinitely many consistency relations.
This set of consistency relations must be imposed on the Taylor coefficients in order for the EFT to be invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
).
We note that in the case of the EFT of scalar-tensor theories discussed in
[
53
,
55
]
where the gauge coupling
g_{M}
vanishes,
this set of consistency relations is trivially satisfied since the EFT action is allowed to explicitly depend on the time coordinate
\tau
unless the underlining theory has the shift symmetry.
Moreover, once the background is given by the FLRW metric, the relations (
2.29
)–(
2.31
) reduce to the ones obtained in
[
50
]
as expected.

In the next Subsection, we will write down the EFT action up to second order in perturbations and we will see that the two sets of consistency relations we have obtained are translated into relations among the EFT coefficients of vector-tensor theories on an arbitrary background.

2.4
EFT action

In this Subsection, we will write down the EFT action (
2.21
) expanded up to the second order in perturbations. We will see that this EFT action is apparently not invariant under the 3d spatial diffeomorphism and the residual
U(1)
(
2.2
) due to the fact that perturbations in (
2.22
) are defined around a general background which depends on time and spatial coordinates.
As pointed out in the previous Subsection, the two sets of consistency relations, Eqs. (
2.26
)–(
2.28
) and Eqs. (
2.29
)–(
2.31
) ensure that the EFT action is invariant under the residual symmetries. After we write down the EFT action, we will impose these relations among the EFT coefficients.

Expanding the action (
2.21
) around a general background with perturbations (
2.22
), we consider the following general EFT action in the unitary gauge up to second order in perturbations,

\displaystyle S_{\rm EFT}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}_{\star}}{2}f(y){\bf\it R}-\Lambda(y)-c(y){\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}-d(y){\bf\it K}-\alpha(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-\beta(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}

\displaystyle-\xi_{1}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it E}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-\xi_{2}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it B}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2}^{4}(y)\bigg(\frac{\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}{-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}\bigg)^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{I}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bigg(\frac{\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}{-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}\bigg)\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}^{IJ}(y)^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}\bigg]\;,

(2.32)

where
y\equiv\{\tau,\vec{x}\}
and
{\bf\it R}
denotes the 4d Ricci scalar without the total derivative term, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\bf\it R}\equiv{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}+{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+{\bf\it\omega}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it\omega}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-{\bf\it K}^{2}=R+2\nabla_{\mu}({\bf\it n}^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}{\bf\it n}^{\mu}-{\bf\it K}{\bf\it n}^{\mu})\;,

(2.33)

with
R
being the usual 4d Ricci scalar.
Note that
\alpha(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
and
\beta(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
are assumed to be traceless (i.e.,
\alpha^{\mu}_{\ \mu}=0
and
\beta^{\mu}_{\ \mu}=0
) so that the EFT coefficients can be defined uniquely.
*6
*6

*6

If
\alpha(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
and
\beta(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
had the trace parts, then they would be absorbed into
d(y)
and
f(y)
, respectively.

The EFT coefficients in (
2.32
) can generally depend on both time and spatial coordinates.
It is useful to note that, in the case of the EFT of vector-tensor theories on the FLRW background, all the EFT coefficients are functions only of time, coefficients
\xi_{1}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
\xi_{2}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
should vanish due to the isotropy of the background, and
\alpha(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
\beta(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
are redundant.
As usual, the Einstein-Hilbert term and the terms linear in perturbations affect the background dynamics for both the metric and the gauge field, resulting in the tadpole cancellation conditions (see Section
4
).

Let us comment on the second-order terms in (
2.32
).
In general, the coefficient
\lambda_{2}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
can be decomposed as
\lambda_{2}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=a^{I}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, where
a^{I}
is some function of
y
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{2}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
is the traceless part of
\lambda_{2}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
.
This ensures that the operators such as
\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\delta{\bf\it K}
and
\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
have been taken into account. Note that, when
\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
is taken to be either
\delta{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
or
\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, the terms that consist of the trace of
\delta{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
or
\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
automatically vanish.
As for the rank-4 tensor
\lambda_{3}^{IJ}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}

, it can be generally decomposed into

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}=c_{1}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}+c_{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \beta}\delta^{\alpha}_{\ \nu}+\hat{c}^{\alpha}_{\ (\beta}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu)}+\tilde{\lambda}_{3}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}\;,

(2.34)

where we have suppressed the indices
I
and
J
.
Here,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are functions of
y
, and
\hat{c}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}
is a rank-2 traceless tensor.
The remaining rank-4 piece is called
\tilde{\lambda}_{3}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}
.
Clearly, when the first two terms in (
2.34
) are contracted with
\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}
, it gives rise to the EFT operators such as
\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}
and
\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
.
Besides, the third term on the RHS of (
2.34
) once contracted with
\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}
generates the operators, e.g.,
\delta{\bf\it K}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
.
Finally, the contraction between the tensor
\tilde{\lambda}_{3}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}
and
\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}
gives the operators involving four indices, unlike the ones obtained from the first three terms in (
2.34
).
In what follows, we will not discuss an explicit form of the tensor
\tilde{\lambda}_{3}{}^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}
since it would not be relevant to our discussion in this paper.

Let us now proceed with the EFT action (
2.32
).
For notational simplicity, let us define the following quantities:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\;,\quad\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\;,

(2.35)

which correspond to the trace of the Taylor coefficients
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
, respectively.
*7
*7

*7

The coefficient of
1/4
in Eq. (
2.35
) has been introduced so that the traceless parts of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}
are given by
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}}\delta^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\delta^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
, respectively.

Then, the Taylor coefficients in (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) are related to the EFT coefficients via

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\star}^{2}f(y)&=2\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\;,\\
\Lambda(y)&=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}{}^{(3)}\!\bar{{\bf\it R}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\big(\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{2}-\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\bar{{\bf\it E}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,\\
c(y)&=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}+\frac{g_{M}^{2}}{4(-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau})^{2}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\\
d(y)&=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}}-\frac{3}{2}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bar{{\bf\it K}}\;,\quad\alpha(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it K}}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+2\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bigg(\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\frac{\bar{{\bf\it K}}}{4}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bigg)\;,\\
\beta(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}&=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\delta^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,\quad\xi_{1}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it E}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}}\;,\\
\xi_{2}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}&=-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it B}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}}+\frac{g_{M}^{2}}{2(-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau})}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,\quad m_{2}^{4}(y)=(-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau})^{2}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}-\frac{g_{M}^{2}}{2(-\bar{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}})}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\end{split}

(2.36)

where we recall that
\alpha(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
and
\beta(y)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
were assumed to be traceless.
Also, the terms with
\lambda_{2}^{I}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
\lambda_{3}^{IJ}(y)^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{2}^{I}(y)^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bigg(\frac{\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}}{-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau}}\bigg)\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}&=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}}\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-\frac{g_{M}^{2}}{2(-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau})^{2}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\bar{{\bf\it B}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}\delta{\bf\it B}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\\
\lambda_{3}^{IJ}(y)^{\mu\alpha}_{\ \ \ \nu\beta}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}&=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{I}{}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\delta{\bf\it Z}^{J}{}^{\beta}_{\ \alpha}+2\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}(\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}-\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu})\\
&\qquad-\frac{g_{M}^{2}}{2(-\bar{{\bf\it g}}^{\tau\tau})}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}}\delta{\bf\it B}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it B}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\end{split}

(2.37)

where we have defined
\delta{\bf\it K}\equiv{\bf\it K}-\bar{{\bf\it K}}

2.5
Stückelberg trick

In the previous Subsection, we have formulated a consistent EFT of vector-tensor theories in the unitary gauge on a general background.
In particular, we have found that the invariance of the EFT action under the residual symmetries imposes the consistency relations among the EFT coefficients.
In this Subsection, we will discuss the Stückelberg procedure of our EFT.
Specifically, this will be useful for analyzing the dynamics of the longitudinal mode of

4
Static and spherically symmetric background

In this Section, we apply our general vector-tensor EFT, which is applicable to any background as far as the vector field is non-vanishing and timelike, to the particular case of static and spherically symmetric background.

4.1
Background dynamics

We start with the static and spherically symmetric background metric in Schwarzschild coordinates,

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}=-A(r){\rm d}t^{2}+\frac{{\rm d}r^{2}}{B(r)}+r^{2}\gamma_{ab}{\rm d}x^{a}{\rm d}x^{b}\;,

(4.1)

where

and
B(r)
are functions of the areal radius

represents the metric on a unit two-sphere (i.e.,
\gamma_{ab}{\rm d}x^{a}{\rm d}x^{b}={\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\phi^{2}

a,b=\{\theta,\phi\}

Notice that here we do not assume the condition

[

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tau={\rm d}t+\sqrt{\frac{1-A}{AB}}~{\rm d}r\;,\qquad{\rm d}\rho={\rm d}t+\frac{{\rm d}r}{\sqrt{AB(1-A)}}\;.

(4.3)

5
Conclusions

We have constructed the effective field theory (EFT) of vector-tensor theories on an arbitrary background metric. The construction of the EFT in the unitary gauge is based on the fact that a
timelike
background vector field spontaneously breaks both the time diffeomorphism and
U(1)
symmetry, while leaving a combination of them invariant as a residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
), on top of the residual spatial diffeomorphism. In Section
2
, similar to the EFT of vector-tensor theories constructed on a cosmological background
[
50
]
, we have provided the EFT action (
2.20
) in the unitary gauge as a spacetime integral of the scalar function of quantities covariant under the residual symmetries, for example,
{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}
and
{\bf\it K}
.
This covariant form of the unitary-gauge EFT can be generally applied to an arbitrary background metric.
Then, expanding such an EFT action around a generic background geometry in terms of perturbations such as
\delta{\bf\it g}^{\tau\tau}
,
\delta{\bf\it K}
and perturbations of other building blocks gives rise to the EFT action for perturbations (
2.32
) or (
2.45
) with the coefficients depending on both time and spatial coordinates.
As pointed out in
[
50
]
, since in this setup the time coordinate is no longer a building block of the EFT (both
\tau
and
A_{\mu}
transform under the residual
U(1)
symmetry such that the EFT action remains invariant), one is required to impose a set of consistency relations with respect to the temporal direction in order for the EFT to be invariant under the residual
U(1)
symmetry (
2.2
). On top of that, as explained in
[
53
]
, another set of consistency relations with respect to the spatial directions must be imposed on the EFT coefficients. This is because the perturbations we have defined in the unitary gauge do not covariantly transform under the 3d spatial diffeomorphism since their background values explicitly depend on the spatial coordinates. We have found that a consistent way of constructing the EFT of vector-tensor theories on a generic background is to expand the unitary-gauge action around such an inhomogeneous background and to impose the two sets of consistency relations, which ensure that the residual symmetries are preserved at any orders in perturbation.
Therefore, we have obtained the EFT action of perturbations in the unitary gauge in the context of vector-tensor theories.
This EFT can be indeed generally applied to arbitrary backgrounds of both the metric and the gauge field, with the timelike background vector
{\bf\it n}_{\mu}
. In Section
3
, we have provided a dictionary between our EFT coefficients and those of generalized Proca (GP) theories.
It is important to emphasize that the dictionary we have derived holds for any backgrounds of both the metric and the gauge field. Also, this dictionary enables us to identify a simple subclass of the EFT that includes GP theories as a special case. In Section
4
, we have studied the background dynamics, assuming that the background metric is static and spherically symmetric and the background gauge field has a constant temporal component given by Eq. (
4.14
), based on the simple subclass that contains GP theories. Specifically, we have arrived at the conclusion that the parameter
c(r)
of the EFT action vanishes on this particular choice of backgrounds, which agrees with the result found in the similar EFT constructed on a cosmological background
[
50
]
.
Furthermore, in the case of stealth Schwarzschild(-de Sitter) background, we have analyzed the decoupling-limit action for the longitudinal mode
\pi
derived from the leading order EFT action (
4.20
), where the terms linear in the extrinsic curvature and its trace, i.e.,
\tilde{d}(r){\bf\it K}
and
\alpha(r)\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
, as well as the higher-derivative quadratic operators
\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}
and
\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
are absent.
The analysis shows that the sound speeds (
c_{\rho}^{2}
and
c_{\theta}^{2}
) of scalar fluctuations are vanishing everywhere, indicating that the system is infinitely strongly coupled. This is in sharp contrast to the case of gauged ghost condensate, in which perturbations are weakly coupled thanks to certain higher-derivative terms, i.e., the scordatura terms. This implies that, in order to describe this type of stealth solutions within the EFT, the scordatura terms must necessarily be taken into account in addition to those already included in the simple subclass.

There are several future directions we would like to investigate further. As already pointed out at the end of Subsection
4.2
, it is worth studying the decoupling limit of
\pi
on other backgrounds of
g_{\mu\nu}
or
A_{\mu}
. This would indeed shed light on whether or not the strong coupling we have found in the absence of scordatura terms is also present in other cases.
Another interesting prospect is to use our EFT to study the dynamics of both tensor and vector perturbations at least at quadratic order in the odd- and even-parity sectors. Especially, since the strong coupling problem we have found in Subsection
4.2
is expected to be absent in the odd sector as the longitudinal mode of the gauge field belongs to the even sector, the odd-sector analysis should be straightforwardly carried out. Finally, it would be phenomenologically interesting to use our EFT to study the quasinormal mode spectrum of perturbations as done in
[
73
,
81
]
for the case of EFT of scalar-tensor theories, and to compute the tidal Love number of black holes.

Appendix A
Mapping between different bases of EFT building blocks

In the main text, we chose
\{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\}
as a part of the EFT building blocks and we did not separate these tensors into the trace and traceless parts as in (
2.23
).
In this Appendix, we comment on the mapping of the EFT to the basis of
\{{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it K},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}\}
.

Let us first consider the following Lagrangian:

\displaystyle{\cal L}={\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,

(A.1)

which can be expressed in terms of
{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
and
{\bf\it K}
as

\displaystyle{\cal L}={\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{{\bf\it K}^{2}}{3}\;.

(A.2)

We now expand the Lagrangian about the background.
In terms of the perturbations defined as

\displaystyle\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},\qquad\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},\qquad\delta{\bf\it K}\equiv{\bf\it K}-\bar{{\bf\it K}}\;,

(A.3)

we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+2\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,

(A.4)

or

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+2\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{1}{3}\left(\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{2}+2\bar{{\bf\it K}}\delta{\bf\it K}+\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}\right),

(A.5)

depending on if we choose
{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
or
\{{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it K}\}
as independent building blocks.
Note that the perturbation of the projection tensor,
\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\equiv{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}-\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, does not show up in either (
A.4
) or (
A.5
).
This is because
{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
does not appear explicitly in the covariant Lagrangian (
A.1
) or (
A.2
).
However, the situation becomes non-trivial when we try to directly find the mapping between the Lagrangians (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) which are expressed in terms of perturbations.
Actually, since

\displaystyle\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\frac{\delta{\bf\it K}}{3}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\frac{{\bf\it K}}{3}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\;,

(A.6)

the last two terms in (
A.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}&=\left(\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\frac{\bar{{\bf\it K}}}{3}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\right)\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{\bar{{\bf\it K}}}{3}\delta{\bf\it K}+\frac{{\bf\it K}}{3}\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{\bar{{\bf\it K}}{\bf\it K}}{9}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\\
\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}&=\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{2}{3}{\bf\it K}\left(\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}+\frac{\delta{\bf\it K}}{3}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\right)\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\\
&\quad+\frac{{\bf\it K}^{2}}{9}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\end{split}

(A.7)

respectively, which involve
\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
explicitly.
Nevertheless, the unwanted
\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
’s can be removed from (
A.4
).
The point is that the above two terms show up in (
A.4
) in the combination
2\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
thanks to the fact that they are derived from the covariant Lagrangian (
A.1
).
Note that this is nothing but the consistency relation we have discussed in Subsection
2.3
for a more general Lagrangian.
Indeed, starting from (
A.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\bar{{\bf\it K}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it K}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}=2\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\frac{1}{3}\left(2\bar{{\bf\it K}}\delta{\bf\it K}+\delta{\bf\it K}^{2}\right)\;,

(A.8)

where we have used the following two identities to remove
\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
and
\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&={\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}=\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\bar{{\bf\it\sigma}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it\sigma}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;,\\
0&={\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}-\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}=2\bar{{\bf\it h}}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+\delta{\bf\it h}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\delta{\bf\it h}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}\;.\end{split}

(A.9)

Now, as expected, the perturbation of the projection tensor has been canceled out and we have recovered (the perturbative part of) Eq. (
A.5
).

The same thing should happen for a more general EFT action.
Namely, if we start from our EFT action (
2.45
) and rewrite it in terms of
\{{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it K},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}\}

Therefore, one could choose either
\{{\bf\it K}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\}
or
\{{\bf\it\sigma}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it r}^{\mu}_{\ \nu},{\bf\it K},{}^{(3)}\!{\bf\it R}\}
as a part of the EFT building blocks, and there exists a one-to-one correspondence between the EFT actions written in the two different manners.

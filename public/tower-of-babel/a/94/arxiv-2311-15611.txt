Some factorization results on polynomials having integer coefficients
Jitender Singh 1, Rishu Garg 2

Abstract
. In this article, we prove some factorization results for several classes of polynomials having integer coefficients, which in particular yield several classes of irreducible polynomials. Such classes of polynomials are devised by imposing some sufficiency conditions on their coefficients along with some conditions on the prime factorization of constant term or the leading coefficient of the underlying polynomials in conjunction with the information about their root location.

1.
Introduction.

The irreducibility criteria due to Schönemnn
[
11
]
, Eisenstein
[
2
]
, Perron
[
6
]
, and Dumas
[
3
]
are among the most popular classical results concerning irreducibility of polynomials having integer coefficients
[
1
]
. The irreducibility criteria due to Schönemnn and Eisenstein are easy consequences of the much more general factorization result of Dumas based on Newton-polygon approach. There have been many extensions and generalizations of the aforementioned irreducibility criteria for polynomials having integer coefficients, and the literature is vast; so for a quick survey, the reader may refer to see
[
9
]
.

In 2013, Weintraub proved the following generalization of the Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion which also rectifies the false claim made by Eisenstein himself in
[
2
]
.

Theorem
A

(Weintraub
[
10
]
)
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a polynomial and suppose there is a prime
p
such that
p
does not divide
a_{m}
,
p
divides
a_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
, and for some
k
with
0\leq k\leq m-1
,
p^{2}
does not divide
a_{k}
. Let
k_{0}
be the smallest such value of
k
. If
f(z)=g(z)h(z)
, a factorization in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
, then
\min\{\deg(g),\deg(h)\}\leq k_{0}
. In particular, for a primitive polynomial
f
, if
k_{0}=0
, then
f
is irreducible, and if
k_{0}=1
and
f
does not have a root in
\mathbb{Q}
, then
f
is irreducible.

The irreducibility criterion of Eisenstein is precisely the case
k=0
of Theorem
A
, and such results are based on factorization properties of the coefficients of the given polynomial with respect to a prime divisor.
On the other hand, the irreducibility criterion due to Perron is based on root location of the underlying polynomial. More precisely, the irreducibility criterion of Perron states that if the coefficients of the monic polynomial
f=z^{m}+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
,
m\geq 2
satisfy

\displaystyle|a_{m-1}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 1+|a_{m-2}|+|a_{m-3}|+\cdots+|a_{1}|+|a_{0}|,

then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. The aforementioned inequality enforces all but one zero of
f
to lie inside the open unit disk
|z|<1
in the complex plane, and the remaining zero to lie outside the closed unit disk
|z|\leq 1
. This information about the zeros of
f
can be utilized in order to deduce the irreducibility of
f
. Interestingly, the irreducibility criterion of Perron itself implies the following extension for non-monic polynomials as follows.

Theorem
B
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a primitive polynomial with
a_{0}a_{m}\neq 0
and
m\geq 2
, such that

\displaystyle|a_{m-1}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 1+|a_{0}||a_{m}|^{m-1}+|a_{1}||a_{m}|^{m-2}+\cdots+|a_{m-2}||a_{m}|.

Then
f
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
.

Proof.

Define
g(z)=a_{m}^{m-1}f(z/a_{m})
. It follows that
g\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
. Further, the polynomials
f
and
g
have the same number of irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. In fact we have

\displaystyle g=a_{0}a_{m}^{m-1}+a_{1}a_{m}^{m-2}z+\cdots+a_{m-2}a_{m}z^{m-2}+a_{m-1}z^{m-1}+z^{m},

which shows that
g
is a monic polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}
. By the hypothesis, the coefficients of
g
satisfy

|a_{m-1}|>1+|a_{0}a_{m}^{m-1}|+|a_{1}a_{m}^{m-2}|+\cdots+|a_{m-2}a_{m}|
.
Consequently, by the classical irreducibility criterion of Perron, the polynomial
g
is irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
, and thus so does the polynomial
f
.
∎

Perron
[
6
]
gave a multivariate version of his irreducibility criterion for polynomials over arbitrary fields, whose detailed proof may be found in
[
5
]
. As a generalization of the irreducibility criterion of Perron, the authors in
[
7
]
proved a factorization result for a class of polynomials having integer coefficients.

In this article, we obtain factorization results for polynomials having integer coefficients satisfying the conditions analogous to that of the conditions analogous to those of Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion which depend upon prime factorization of the coefficients of the underlying polynomial, or the irreducibility criterion of Perron which are purely based on root location, or the conditions on the prime factorization of the constant term or the leading coefficient in connection with the root location. Our main results are stated in Sec.
2
, where at the end some examples based on the main results are discussed. Finally, the proofs are presented in Sec.
3
.

2.
Main results

Analogous to Theorem
A
, we have another generalization of Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion in a somewhat different setup as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a polynomial and suppose there exists a prime
p
and coprime positive integers
k
and

such that
p
does not divide
a_{j}
,
p^{k}
divides
a_{i}
for

, and
p^{k+1}
does not divide
a_{0}
. Then any factorization
f(z)=g(z)h(z)
of

in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
has a factor of degree less than or equal to
k_{0}
, where
k_{0}\in\{0,m-j\}
. In particular, for a primitive polynomial
f
, if
j=m
, then
f
is irreducible, and if
j=m-1
and
f
does not have a root in
\mathbb{Q}
, then
f
is irreducible.

Theorem
1
for

yields a well known irreducibility criterion which may be found in
[
8
, Theorem B]
.

Our next result uses prime factorization of the constant term of

in connection with the root location of the polynomial

, which also generalizes one of the irreducibility criteria proved in
[
8

.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a primitive polynomial for which
a_{0}=\pm p^{k}d
for some positive integers
k
and

not dividing

such that the zeros of
f
lie outside the closed disk

j

1\leq j\leq m
for which
p
does not divide

is a product of at most
\min\{k,j\}
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. In particular, if
k=1
or
j=1
, then
f
is irreducible.

If instead the prime factorization of the leading coefficient of

is used, then we may have the following factorization result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a primitive polynomial for which

is a product of at most

irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. In particular, if
k=1
or
j=1
, then
f
is irreducible.

As a generalization of Theorem
B
to include the class of polynomials in which a coefficient other than the
(m-1)

Theorem 4
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a primitive polynomial with
a_{0}a_{m}\neq 0
and
m\geq 2
. Let
b
be a positive divisor of

and

\displaystyle|a_{j}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i<j}|a_{i}||a_{m}|^{j-i}+\sum_{j<i\leq m}|a_{i}|\delta^{i-j},

where the summation over empty set is understood to be zero. Then

is a product of at most
m-j
irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
. In particular, if
j=m-1
, then
f
is irreducible.

The following corollaries are immediate from Theorem

Corollary 5
.

Let
f=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a primitive polynomial with
a_{0}a_{m}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle|a_{j}|

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i<j}|a_{i}|b^{j-i}\gamma^{j-i}+\sum_{j<i\leq m}\frac{|a_{i}|}{|a_{m}|^{i-j}}\gamma^{i-j},

The Corollary

[

]
for

Taking

Corollary 6

Let

\displaystyle|a_{j}|

\displaystyle|a_{j+1}/a_{m}|+\sum_{0\leq i<j}|a_{i}||a_{m}|^{j-i}+\sum_{j+1<i\leq m}|a_{i}||a_{m}|^{-(i-j)},

In

The following result complements their irreducibility criterion and also improves upon the corresponding factorization result proved by the authors in

Corollary 7

Let

\displaystyle p^{N}|a_{j}|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle|a_{m}a_{j-1}|p^{2s}+\sum_{i=2}^{j}|a_{m}^{i}a_{j-i}|p^{is}+\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}\frac{|a_{i}|}{|a_{m}|^{i-j}}.

Then

shows that
p
must divide each
\alpha_{i}
. This proves that
r\leq k
.

Now on the contrary, assume that
r>j
. We may write each

so that
\alpha_{i}=a_{i\deg(f_{i})}
. Since
f(z)=f_{1}(z)\cdots f_{r}(z)
, we have
m=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\deg(f_{i})
and so,

\displaystyle a_{m-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{t=1}^{r}i_{t}=m-j}a_{1i_{1}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}}=\sum_{\sum_{t=1}^{r}(\deg(f_{t})-i_{t})=j}a_{1i_{1}}\cdots a_{ri_{r}},

where
0\leq i_{t}\leq\deg(f_{t})
and

for each index

, it follows that at least one of the numbers

, and so,

∎

Lemma 8
.

If
g=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[z]

,

f
lie within the open disk
|z|<1/|a_{m}|
and
m-j
zeros lie outside the closed disk
|z|\leq 1
, then
f
is a product of at most
m-j
irreducible factors in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
.

Proof.

Suppose that

is the product of
r
nonconstant irreducible polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
,
1\leq r\leq m
. Then
1\leq|a_{0}|=|g(0)|=|g_{1}(0)|\cdots|g_{r}(0)|
, and so,
|g_{i}(0)|\geq 1
for each

. Assume on the contrary that
r>m-j\geq 1
. Then

and there exists an index

. So, we may write

, which is a contradiction.

.

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i<j}a_{i}b^{m-1-i}z^{i}+(a_{j}b^{m-1-j}z^{j})+\sum_{j<i<m}a_{i}b^{m-1-i}z^{i}+(a_{m}/b)z^{m}.

Since

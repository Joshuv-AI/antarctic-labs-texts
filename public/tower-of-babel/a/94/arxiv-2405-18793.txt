Policy Zooming: Adaptive Discretization-based Infinite-Horizon Average-Reward Reinforcement Learning
Avik Kar, Rahul Singh

Abstract
We study the infinite-horizon average-reward reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous space Lipschitz MDPs in which an agent can play policies from a given set Φ \Phi . The proposed algorithms efficiently explore the policy space by “zooming” into the “promising regions” of Φ \Phi , thereby achieving adaptivity gains in the performance. We upper bound their regret as 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T 1 − d eff. − 1 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(T^{1-d_{\text{eff.}}^{-1}}\big) , where d eff. = d z Φ + 2 d_{\text{eff.}}=d^{\Phi}_{z}+2 for model-free algorithm PZRL-MF and d eff. = 2 ​ d 𝒮 + d z Φ + 3 d_{\text{eff.}}=2d_{\mathcal{S}}+d^{\Phi}_{z}+3 for model-based algorithm PZRL-MB . Here, d 𝒮 d_{\mathcal{S}} is the dimension of the state space, and d z Φ d^{\Phi}_{z} is the zooming dimension given a set of policies Φ \Phi . d z Φ d^{\Phi}_{z} is an alternative measure of the complexity of the problem, and it depends on the underlying MDP as well as on Φ \Phi . Hence, the proposed algorithms exhibit low regret in case the problem instance is benign and/or the agent competes against a low-complexity Φ \Phi (that has a small d z Φ d^{\Phi}_{z} ). When specialized to the case of finite-dimensional policy space, we obtain that d eff. d_{\text{eff.}} scales as the dimension of this space under mild technical conditions; and also obtain d eff. = 2 d_{\text{eff.}}=2 , or equivalently 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T}) regret for PZRL-MF , under a curvature condition on the average reward function that is commonly used in the multi-armed bandit (MAB) literature.

Reinforcement Learning (RL)

To illustrate the intuition behind zooming, we revisit its origin in the simpler setting of Lipschitz MABs
(
Kleinberg et al. 2008
)
.

Lipschitz MABs: The Zooming Algorithm.
The agent maintains a set of ‘‘active arms,’’ and their ‘‘confidence balls’’ whose radii are equal to the confidence radii associated with the corresponding arm’s estimated reward. Thanks to Lipschitz continuity, rewards of nearby arms can be inferred from the active ones.
2
2

2

Let
a
be an active arm with confidence radius
\eta
and empirical mean
\hat{\mu}_{a}
. Then, for any arm
a^{\prime}
in its confidence ball, the mean reward lies in
[\hat{\mu}_{a}-(1+L)\eta,\hat{\mu}_{a}+(1+L)\eta]
with high probability.
New arms are activated only if not covered by existing confidence balls. The agent selects the arm with the highest upper confidence bound (UCB) index. Because the confidence radius shrinks with the number of plays, the algorithm “zooms in” on promising regions, those with high UCB index. This adaptive behavior yields an effective dimension of
d_{z}+2
. A similar idea has been explored in
(
Bubeck et al. 2011
)
.

1.1
Challenges

For MABs, the zooming algorithm plays only from amongst the active arms since the UCB index of an active arm turns out to be an optimistic estimate of the mean reward of each arm lying inside its confidence ball. For policies, however, rewards are not unbiased estimates of the long-term average unless the controlled Markov process (CMP) is at stationarity, making confidence radius design and optimism proofs more involved. Moreover, to our knowledge, model-free UCB indices have not been explored for average reward RL, not even in tabular MDPs. Another challenge lies in selecting an appropriate norm for measuring distances between policies (note that since the policy space is not finite-dimension, all norms are not equivalent).

1.2
Contributions

1. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to provide finite-time regret bounds for average reward RL in general state-action space MDPs with
d>1
. Prior works
(
Ortner and Ryabko 2012
;
Qian et al. 2019
;
Wei et al. 2021
;
He et al. 2023
)
are either limited to finite action spaces or assume
d_{\mathcal{S}}=1
.

2. We develop two algorithms,
PZRL-MF
(model-free) and
PZRL-MB
(model-based), that use policy-based zooming and UCB methods
(
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
)
. Our main novelty is a new complexity measure for average reward RL: the zooming dimension
d_{z}^{\Phi}
, defined via policy covers of
\Phi
. We show regret bounds of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(T^{1-d_{\text{eff.}}^{-1}}\big)
with
d_{\text{eff.}}=d^{\Phi}_{z}+2
for
PZRL-MF
, and
d_{\text{eff.}}=2d_{\mathcal{S}}+d^{\Phi}_{z}+3
for
PZRL-MB
. Importantly, a small
d_{z}^{\Phi}
does not imply the MDP belongs to nice class of MDPs, such as linear MDPs or tabular MDPs. When
\Phi
is parameterized over
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{w}}
, we show
d_{z}^{\Phi}\leq d_{w}
under mild assumptions. For MDPs with bi-Lipschitz average reward functions, we get
d_{\text{eff.}}=2
and hence an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T})
regret for
PZRL-MF
. Note that the bi-Lipschitz assumption is common in structured bandits
(
Cope 2009
;
Yu and Mannor 2011
;
Combes et al. 2020
)
.

3. Along the way, we prove a novel sensitivity result (Theorem
4.1
) for Markov processes on general state spaces
(
Meyn and Tweedie 2012
)
. We bound the distance between the stationary distributions of Markov chains in terms of a weighted distance measure (
13
) between the transition kernels, improving over the existing results
(
Mitrophanov 2005
;
Mouhoubi 2021
)
that bounds the same quantity in terms of the sup distance between the transition kernels.

4. Existing algorithms for general state spaces are often computationally intractable without linearity
(
Ayoub et al. 2020
)
or deterministic dynamics
(
Wu et al. 2024
)
. In contrast, our methods are efficient, and in fact,
PZRL-MF
has the same computational complexity as zooming for MABs.

5. In Section
5
, we demonstrate the applicability of our framework via a transmission scheduling problem. This MDP is neither tabular nor linear; however, the optimal policy is known to belong to a known class of policies that can be described by finitely many parameters. Simulation results show the practical relevance of our algorithms.

1.3
Past Works

Lipschitz episodic MDPs
:

Domingues et al. (2021)
uses smoothing kernels to estimate the transition kernel, and obtains a regret upper bound with
d_{\text{eff.}}=2d+1
.
Cao and Krishnamurthy (2020)
performs adaptive discretization and zooming and achieves regret upper bound with
d_{\text{eff.}}=d_{z}+2
, where
d_{z}
is the zooming dimension defined specifically for the episodic case.
Sinclair et al. (2023)
also obtains adaptivity gains with
d_{\text{eff.}}=d_{z}+d_{\mathcal{S}}
for a model-based algorithm. The same work also shows a regret lower bound of
\Omega(K^{1-(d_{z}+2)^{-1}})
. This lower bound is worse than the
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{K})
dependence that is achievable for the tabular case or the function approximation techniques. This is not surprising since Lipschitz MDPs are a broader class of MDPs
(
Maran et al. 2024a
;
Maran et al. 2024b
)
; See Figure
1
. Even though function approximation techniques yield an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{K})
regret, this comes at the expense of a larger prefactor in the regret bound as compared to Lipschitz MDPs. Moreover, function approximation techniques are computationally inefficient unless the underlying MDP is linear, and the feature maps are
known
. The knowledge of feature maps seems to be a restrictive assumption since learning features efficiently is an active topic in itself
(
Modi et al. 2024
)
.

Average reward RL
: Tabular MDPs are well-studied by now, and popular algorithms with a tight
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{DSAT})
regret bound exist
(
Jaksch et al. 2010
;
Tossou et al. 2019
)
; where
D
is the MDP diameter. In contrast, continuous MDPs have only recently gained attention.
(
Wei et al. 2021
)
uses function approximation, in which the relative value function is a linear function of the features, and obtains a
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret.
He et al. (2023)
uses function approximation techniques and obtains a regret bound of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\textit{poly}(d_{E},B)\sqrt{d_{F}T})
, where
B
is the span of the relative value function. When the transition kernel of the underlying MDP is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and infinitely often smoothly differentiable, then
Ortner and Ryabko (2012)
obtains a regret upper bound with
d_{\text{eff.}}=(2d+2\alpha)/\alpha
.
Kar and Singh (2025)
performs adaptive discretization and zooming and achieves regret upperbound with
d_{\text{eff.}}=2d_{\mathcal{S}}+d_{z}+3
for Lipschitz MDPs with compact state-action spaces.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}(T;\psi)

(1)

(Lipschitz continuity)

(i)

Assumption 2.2

(Ergodicity)

Let

Zooming dimension.
Suboptimality of a policy

\displaystyle d^{\Phi}_{z}:=\inf\Big\{

\displaystyle d^{\prime}>0~|~\mathcal{N}_{\frac{\gamma}{c_{z}}}\left(\Phi_{\gamma}\right)\leq c_{z_{1}}\gamma^{-d^{\prime}},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\frac{\gamma}{c_{z}}}\left(\Phi_{\leq\gamma}\right)\leq c_{z_{2}}\gamma^{-d^{\prime}},~\forall\gamma\geq 0\Big\},

Remark 2.3
.

We note that even if the policy class is high-dimensional, the zooming dimension could be small, as it is a measure of the size of the set of near-optimal policies.

3
Algorithm

We propose a model-free algorithm
PZRL-MF
and a model-based algorithm
PZRL-MB
. Both the algorithms combine policy-based zooming with the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty
(
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
)
. They maintain a set of active policies, compute their UCB indices, and then play an active policy with the highest UCB index in the current episode. Its zooming component zooms in and activates only those policies from
\Phi
, for which it is not possible to generate a good estimate of its performance using the performance estimates of nearby active policies. However, they differ in the way in which they compute UCB indices and activate new policies and hence are discussed separately. The algorithms are summarized in Algorithm
1
.

3.1
PZRL-MF

Policy Diameter.
Diameter at time
t
is defined as,

\displaystyle\mbox{diam}^{(f)}_{t}\left(\phi\right):=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\left(\sqrt{\frac{c^{(f)}_{d}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}}{1\vee N_{t}(\phi)}}+\frac{1+K_{t}(\phi)}{1\vee N_{t}(\phi)}\right),\phi\in\Phi,

(4)

where

is the number of plays of the policy
\phi

t

\phi

Active Policies.

\Phi^{act.}_{t}

\displaystyle B_{\phi,t}:=\left\{\phi^{\prime}\in\Phi:\rho_{\Phi}(\phi^{\prime},\phi)\leq\mbox{diam}^{(f)}_{t}\left(\phi\right)\right\}.

(5)

Since the confidence ball associated with a policy shrinks when it is played, in the possible event that

\displaystyle\rho_{\Phi,\infty}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}{\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\phi_{1}(s),\phi_{2}(s))},~\forall\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\Phi.

Let the MDP

The detailed proofs of the above two results are delegated to Appendix
G
. Here, we provide a generic

:

Proof sketch.

We decompose the regret as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}(T;\psi)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K(T)}{\sum_{t=\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k+1}-1}{J_{\mathcal{M},\Phi}^{\star}-J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{k})}}}_{(a)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{K(T)}{\sum_{t=\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k+1}-1}{J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{k})-r(s_{t},\phi_{k}(s_{t}))}}}_{(b)},

where
K(T)
denotes the total number of episodes till time
T
. We bound the terms (a) and (b) separately.

Bounding
(a): This term is further decomposed into the sum of the regrets arising due to playing policies from the sets
\Phi_{\gamma}
, where
\gamma
assumes the values
2^{-i},~i=1,2,\ldots,\left\lceil\log{\left(1/\epsilon\right)}\right\rceil
, and
\epsilon=T^{-d_{\text{eff.}}^{-1}}
. Cumulative regret arising from playing policies not in the set
\cup_{i=1}^{\left\lceil\log(1/\penalty\epsilon)\right\rceil}\Phi_{2^{-i}}
is bounded by
\epsilon T
. Regret due to playing policies from
\Phi_{\gamma}
is bounded in the following three steps:

(1) First, we derive a condition under which a
\gamma
-suboptimal policy is no longer played.

(2) Then, we deduce an upper bound of the number of plays of a policy
\phi
in terms of its suboptimality gap by concluding that the condition stated in (1) holds when
\phi
has been played sufficiently many times.

(3) Then, we establish an upper bound on the number of policies that are activated by the algorithms from
\Phi_{\gamma}
.

The product of two upper bounds discussed in (2) and (3), when multiplied with
2\gamma
, yields regret from playing policies in
\Phi_{\gamma}
. We then add these regret terms corresponding to different sets
\Phi_{\gamma}
, where
\gamma=2^{-i}
and
i=1,2,\ldots,\left\lceil\log{\left(1/\epsilon\right)}\right\rceil
; to this we add the regret arising due to playing policies with suboptimality less than
\epsilon
, which is bounded by
\epsilon T
.

Bounding
(b): Upper bound on the term
(b)
is derived in Proposition
G.1
, and shows that we must pay a constant penalty in regret each time we switch to playing a different policy; hence this term is bounded as
\mathcal{O}(K(T))
. Then we show that the total number of episodes for
PZRL-MF
as well as
PZRL-MB
are bounded above by
\mathcal{O}\Big(T^{d^{\Phi}_{z}/d_{\text{eff.}}}\Big)
, where
d_{\text{eff.}}=d^{\Phi}_{z}+2
and
d_{\text{eff.}}=2d_{\mathcal{S}}+d^{\Phi}_{z}+3
, respectively.

We obtain the desired regret bound after summing the upper bounds on (a) and (b).
∎

Remark 4.6

(Discontinuous Policies)
.

The regret analysis of
PZRL-MB
extends to policies with discontinuities, provided all discontinuities lie on the boundaries of active cells. This condition can be enforced by defining the cells in such a way that the policy discontinuities lie only on the cell boundaries. For simplicity, we define the cells using dyadic cubes in our exposition (Definition

).

Remark 4.7

(Regarding Assumption
4.4
)
.

We need Assumption
4.4
in order to ensure a sufficiently large number of visits to regions of
\mathcal{S}
which have been assigned positive measure under the stationary distribution. This condition ensures that the model-based UCB bonuses diminish to

when the corresponding policy is played infinitely often. Similar assumptions appear in prior works. For example,
Ormoneit and Glynn (2002)
assumes the transition kernel has a strictly positive Radon-Nikodym derivative.
Wang et al. (2024)
requires the
m
-step transition kernel to be lower bounded. In yet another work,
Wei et al. (2021)
bounds the regret for average reward RL that uses linear function approximation. They assume that under every policy, the integral of cross-product of the feature vectors w.r.t. the stationary measure has all the eigenvalues bounded away from zero. This assumption ensures that upon playing any policy, the confidence ball shrinks in each direction. All serve a purpose analogous to Assumption
4.4
.

6
Conclusion

The central idea of zooming-based algorithms is to capitalize on the adaptive nature of the algorithm. We identify an important problem encountered while employing zooming to infinite horizon average reward RL setup. To rectify this, we define the zooming dimension for this setup in terms of coverings of the policy space, and also allow it to depend upon the class of policies
\Phi
used by the agent. We propose zooming-based algorithms
PZRL-MF
and
PZRL-MB
, and prove that they exhibit adaptivity gains. We show that their regret can be bounded as
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{1-d_{\text{eff.}}^{-1}})
where
d_{\text{eff.}}=d_{z}^{\Phi}+2
and
d_{\text{eff.}}=2d_{\mathcal{S}}+d_{z}^{\Phi}+3
, respectively. Simulation results confirm our theoretical findings.

Definition A.1

A cell is intersection of the state space and a dyadic cube with vertices from the set
\{(N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{d_{\mathcal{S}}})+2^{-\ell}(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{d_{\mathcal{S}}}):v_{j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{\ell}\},N_{j}\in\mathbb{N},j=1,2,\ldots,d_{\mathcal{S}}\}

\ell\in\mathbb{N}

\ell

\ell

. For a cell

to be a point from

The cells are interrelated through a structure called the “partition tree.”

Definition A.2

(Partition tree)
.

A partition tree of depth
\ell
is a tree in which
(i) Each node at depth
m
, where
m\leq\ell
, of the tree is a cell of level
m
. (ii) If
\zeta
is a cell of level
m
, where
m<\ell
, then, a) all the cells of level

that collectively generate a partition of
\zeta

\zeta
. The corresponding cells are called child cells, and we use
\textit{Child}(\zeta)
to denote the child cells of
\zeta

\zeta
is called the parent cell of these child nodes. Any ancestor node of cell
\zeta
is called an ancestor of
\zeta
.

PZRL-MB
maintains a set of “active cells” for every active policy. The following rule is used for activating and deactivating cells corresponding to an active policy
\phi
.

Definition A.3

(Activation rule)
.

For a cell
\zeta
denote,

\displaystyle N_{\max}(\zeta):=\frac{c^{(b)}_{d}2^{d_{\mathcal{S}}+2}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right)^{d_{\mathcal{S}}+2}},\mbox{ and }N_{\min}(\zeta):=\begin{cases}~~0&\mbox{ if }\ell(\zeta)=0\\
\frac{c^{(b)}_{d}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right)^{d_{\mathcal{S}}+2}},&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(16)

where
c^{(b)}_{d}>0
is a constant that is discussed in Lemma
D.2
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
is the confidence parameter. The number of visits to
\zeta
while playing policy
\phi
is denoted
N_{\phi,t}(\zeta)
and is computed using the following rule:

1.

The initial active partition for policy
\phi
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\phi,0}
is a partition of
\mathcal{S}
made of cells of diameter
1
.

2.

N_{\phi,t}(\zeta):=0
for every cell
\zeta
if
\phi
not active at time
t
.

3.

A cell
\zeta
is said to be active if
N_{\min}(\zeta)\leq N_{\phi,t}(\zeta)<N_{\max}(\zeta)
.

4.

N_{\phi,t}(\zeta)
is defined as the number of times either
\zeta
, or any of its ancestors, has been visited while being active until time
t
by the policy
\phi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle N_{\phi,t}(\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}{\mathbb{I}_{\left\{s_{i}\in\zeta_{i},a_{i}=\phi(s_{i})\right\}}},

(17)

where
\zeta_{i}
is the unique cell that is active at time
i
and satisfies
\zeta\subseteq\zeta_{i}
.

Denote the set of active cells at time
t
corresponding to the policy
\phi
by
\mathcal{P}_{\phi,t}
.

Denote the set of the representative points of the cells in
\mathcal{P}_{\phi,t}
by
S_{\phi,t}
.
q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)
denotes the cell in
\mathcal{P}_{\phi,t}
that contains

. Denote
\ell^{\max}_{\phi,t}
to be the level of the smallest cell in

and denote

to be the set of representative points of the cells in

A.2
Discretized Transition Kernel Estimate

Let
\bar{S}
be the set of representative points of a set of cells that partitions the state space. Then, for a continuous transition kernel
\tilde{p}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]
and for any
S\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, we define
\wp_{S\to\bar{S},\tilde{p}}(s,\cdot):S\mapsto[0,1]^{\bar{S}}
as follows,

\displaystyle\wp_{S\to\bar{S},\tilde{p}}(s,s^{\prime}):=\tilde{p}(s,q^{-1}(s^{\prime})),~\forall s\in S,s\in\bar{S}.

(18)

The kernel
\wp_{S\to\bar{S},\tilde{p}}
can be viewed as a discretization of
\tilde{p}
. Denote the transition kernel induced by the policy
\phi
by
p_{\phi}
. The discretized transition kernel
\wp_{\mathcal{S}\to\bar{S}_{\phi,t},p_{\phi}}
will be of interest to us.

Let
\tilde{S}_{\phi,t}(s)
denote the set of representative states of the cells of level
\ell(q^{-1}_{t}(s))
, i.e.,
\tilde{S}_{\phi,t}(s):=\{q(\xi):\xi\in\mathcal{P}^{(\ell(q^{-1}_{t}(s)))}\}
. Now, we are all set to introduce the discrete estimate of the transition probability kernel of the CMP induced under the application of policy
\phi
.

\displaystyle\hat{p}^{(d)}_{\phi,t}(s,s^{\prime}):=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}{\mathbb{I}_{\left\{(s_{i},s_{i+1})\in{q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)}_{i}\times q^{-1}(s^{\prime}),\phi_{i}=\phi\right\}}}}{\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}{\mathbb{I}_{\left\{s_{i}\in{q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)}_{i},\phi_{i}=\phi\right\}}}},~\forall s\in\mathcal{S},s^{\prime}\in\tilde{S}_{\phi,t}(s).

(19)

Recall that
{q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)}_{i}
is the cell that was active at
i
-th time step (
i\leq t
) and that contains
q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)
. We denote a continuous extension of
\hat{p}^{(d)}_{\phi,t}
by
\hat{p}_{\phi,t}
and it is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\phi,t}(s,B):=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\tilde{S}_{\phi,t}(s)}{\frac{\lambda(B\cap q^{-1}(s^{\prime}))}{\lambda(q^{-1}(s^{\prime}))}\hat{p}^{(d)}_{t}(s,s^{\prime})},~\forall s\in\mathcal{S},B\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}.

(20)

Here
\lambda(\cdot)
is the Lebesgue measure on
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}})
. In Lemma
D.2
, we bound the total variation distance between
\wp_{\mathcal{S}\to\bar{S}_{\phi,t},p_{\phi}}(s,\cdot)
and
\wp_{S_{\phi,t}\to\bar{S}_{\phi,t},\hat{p}_{\phi,t}}(q(\zeta),\cdot)
where
s\in\zeta
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{P}_{\phi,t}
.

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{0}(s)

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i+1}(s)

s\in S_{\phi,t}
,

We recall that for

Extended MDP.
Recall that
\bar{S}_{\phi,t}
is the discrete state space at time

\phi

\displaystyle A_{\phi,t}:=\{\{\phi(s)\}:s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}\}.

Consider the following modified reward function

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{\phi,t}(s)

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\phi\in\Phi
, and let

on
\bar{S}_{\phi,t}

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\phi,t}}(\tilde{\phi}):=\max_{\tilde{p}\in\mathcal{C}_{\phi,t}}{\left\{J_{\tilde{M}}(\tilde{\phi}):\tilde{M}=(\bar{S}_{\phi,t},A_{\phi,t},\tilde{p},\tilde{r}_{\phi,t})\right\}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}{\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i+1}(s)-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)\right)}=J_{\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\phi,t}}(\tilde{\phi}).

Proof.

Consider the
i
-th step of the iteration in (
11

\displaystyle\theta_{i}\in\underset{\theta\in\mathcal{C}_{\phi,t}}{\arg\max}\left\{\tilde{r}_{\phi,t}(s)+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{\theta(s,s^{\prime})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s^{\prime})}\right\}.

Let

. Then since

that is permissible in the set

\Theta=\{\Theta_{s,s^{\prime}}\}_{s,s^{\prime}\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}

)
.
∎

A.5
Properties of
PZRL-MB

In this section, we show that the model-based UCB index (
12
) of an active policy is an optimistic estimate of its average reward; we derive an upper bound of the span of the iterates in (
11
), and lastly, we derive an upper bound on the index of the active policies in terms of their average reward and the diameter.

Lemma A.6

(Optimism of Model-based UCB Index)
.

On the set
\mathcal{G}^{(\mbox{Model})}
,

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi^{\prime})\leq\text{Index}^{(b)}_{t}(\phi),~t\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\},\mbox{ where }\phi\in\Phi^{act.}_{t},\phi^{\prime}\in B_{\phi,t},

and where
\text{Index}^{(b)}_{t}(\phi)
is defined in (
12
).

Proof.

Consider the policy evaluation algorithm for the true MDP, i.e.,

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{i+1}(s)

\displaystyle=r(s,\phi(s))+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{p(s,\phi(s),s^{\prime})V^{\phi}_{i}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}}.

Note that the value of the policy
\phi
evaluated on the MDP
\mathcal{M}
is

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)=\lim_{i\to\infty}{\left(V^{\phi}_{i+1}(s)-V^{\phi}_{i}(s)\right)},~\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.

As the limit of a sequence is equal to the limit of its Cesàro mean, we can write,

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)=\lim_{i\to\infty}{\frac{1}{i}V^{\phi}_{i}(s)},~\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.

Similarly, we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}{\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i+1}(s)-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)\right)}=\lim_{i\to\infty}{\frac{1}{i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)},~\forall s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t},

where
\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}\}
is the sequence defined in (
11
). We will first show that

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{i}(s^{\prime})\leq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s),~\forall(s,s^{\prime})\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}\times\mathcal{S}\mbox{ such that }s^{\prime}\in q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s),~\forall i\in\mathbb{N},

(24)

using mathematical induction. The base case is trivially true as
V^{\phi}_{i}(s)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)=0
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Let us assume that

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{j}(s^{\prime})\leq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{j}(s),~\forall(s,s^{\prime})\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}\times\mathcal{S}\mbox{ such that }s^{\prime}\in q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s),\forall,j=0,1,\ldots,i.

(25)

Then we have,

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i+1}(s)

\displaystyle=r(s,\phi(s))+(1+L_{\phi})L_{r}~\mbox{diam}\left(q_{\phi,t}^{-1}(s)\right)+\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{C}_{\phi,t}}{\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{\theta(q(q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s)),s^{\prime\prime})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s^{\prime\prime})}}

\displaystyle\geq r(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}))+\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{p(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}),q^{-1}_{t}(s^{\prime\prime}))\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\geq r(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}))+\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{p(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}),q^{-1}_{t}(s^{\prime\prime}))\max_{\bar{s}^{\prime\prime}\in q^{-1}_{\phi,t}(s^{\prime\prime})}{V^{\phi}_{i}(\bar{s}^{\prime\prime})}}

\displaystyle\geq r(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}))+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{p(s,\phi(s),s^{\prime})V^{\phi}_{i}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=V^{\phi}_{i+1}(s),

where the first inequality follows from Assumption
2.1
(
i
) and from Lemma
D.2
, while the second inequality follows from (
25
). Upon dividing both sides of (

i
, and then taking limit

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}{\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i+1}(s)-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)\right)},s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}

Upon invoking Theorem
4.1
we obtain the following for each

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi)+L_{J}\mbox{diam}^{(b)}_{t}\left(\phi\right)

Lemma A.7
.

Consider the iterates in (
11

\mathcal{G}^{(\mbox{Model})}

\displaystyle~\textit{sp}\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}\right)\leq C_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu},~\forall i\in\mathbb{N},t\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle C_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}

\displaystyle:=\max{\left\{\frac{\bar{m}(\bar{m}+5)}{2}+\frac{6}{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\frac{C_{\eta,\phi}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}(m^{\star}+1)}{1-\alpha}\right)^{d_{\mathcal{S}}}+\frac{2(m^{\star}+1)}{1-\alpha},\frac{1+\frac{L_{r}(1-\alpha)}{(m^{\star}+1)C_{\eta,\phi}}}{(1-\alpha)\left(1-\alpha^{\frac{1}{m^{\star}}}\right)}\right\}},

\displaystyle\bar{m}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\log_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}{\left(\frac{2C}{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\frac{C_{\eta,\phi}(m^{\star}+1)\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}}{1-\alpha}\right)^{d_{\mathcal{S}}}\right)}\right\rceil,\mbox{ and }

(28)

\displaystyle m^{\star}

\displaystyle:=\left\lceil\log_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}{(C)}\right\rceil+1.

(29)

\displaystyle~\textit{sp}\left(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}\right):=\max_{s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)}-\min_{s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}}{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)}.

Proof.

We first note that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muV\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\phi,t}_{i}(s)

\displaystyle\max{\{\mbox{diam}\left(q_{\phi,t}^{-1}(s)\right):s\in\bar{S}_{\phi,t}\}}\geq\frac{1-\alpha}{C_{\eta,\phi}\left(m^{\star}+1\right)},

(30)

while the second occurs when (
30

Case 1: Let
\zeta
be the cell with the largest diameter from the set
\mathcal{P}_{\phi,t}
. We first show that
\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
, the CMP induced by the transition kernel

\phi

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{m}(\bar{m}+5)}{2}+\frac{6}{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\frac{C_{\eta,\phi}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}(m^{\star}+1)}{1-\alpha}\right)^{d_{\mathcal{S}}}

is as defined in (
28
). From Assumption
2.2
, Assumption
4.4

\displaystyle\mu^{(i)}_{\phi,p,s^{\prime}}(\zeta)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi,p}(\zeta)\mbox{ and }\mu^{(i)}_{\phi,p,s^{\prime}}(\zeta)\leq\frac{3}{2}\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi,p}(\zeta)~\forall i\geq\bar{m}.

Now, consider the process
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
that is independent across time;
x_{i}
assumes the value
1
with a probability
\mu^{(i)}_{\phi,p,s^{\prime}}(\zeta)
, and

with a probability

. Define the following random variables

and
T^{(s)}_{\zeta,s^{\prime}}
,

\displaystyle T^{(x)}_{\{1\}}

\displaystyle:=\inf{\{i\geq 0\mid x_{i}=1\}},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle T^{(s)}_{\zeta,s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\inf{\{i\geq 0\mid s_{i}\in\zeta,s_{0}=s^{\prime}\}}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[T^{(x)}_{\{1\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{i\cdot\mu^{(i)}_{\phi,p}(\zeta)\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}{\left(1-\mu^{(j)}_{\phi,p,s}(\zeta)\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{m}(\bar{m}-1)}{2}+\sum_{i=\bar{m}}^{\infty}{\frac{3i}{2}\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi,p}(\zeta)\prod_{j=\bar{m}}^{i-1}{\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi,p}(\zeta)\right)}}

denote the distributions of
s_{1,i}
and
s_{2,i}
, respectively. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\left\lVert\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}}\leq\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\left\lVert\mu^{(0)}_{1}-\mu^{(0)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV},

where
m^{\star}=\left\lceil\log_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}{(C)}\right\rceil+1
.

Proof.

From Lemma
B.1
, we have that,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}\leq\alpha\left\lVert\mu^{(0)}_{1}-\mu^{(0)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV},\mbox{ for }i\geq m^{\star}.

(46)

Also, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle=2~\sup_{A\subseteq\mathcal{S}}{\left\{(\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2})(A)\right\}}

\displaystyle=2~\sup_{A\subseteq\mathcal{S}}{\left\{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{p(s,\phi(s),A)~d(\mu^{(i-1)}_{1}-\mu^{(i-1)}_{2})(s)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\mathcal{S}\\
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}{\left\{p(s,\phi(s),A)-p(s^{\prime},\phi(s^{\prime}),A)\right\}}\left\lVert\mu^{(i-1)}_{1}-\mu^{(i-1)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mu^{(i-1)}_{1}-\mu^{(i-1)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV},

where the first step follows from the definition of the total variation norm, and the third step follows from Lemma
I.6
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}\leq\left\lVert\mu^{(0)}_{1}-\mu^{(0)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV},~\forall i\in\mathbb{N}

(47)

Using (
46
) iteratively, and using (
47
), we can write,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\left\lVert\mu^{(i)}_{1}-\mu^{(i)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{m^{\star}-1}{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\left\lVert\mu^{(m+i\cdot m^{\star})}_{1}-\mu^{(m+i\cdot m^{\star})}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\left\lVert\mu^{(0)}_{1}-\mu^{(0)}_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}.

where
m^{\star}:=\left\lceil\log_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}{(C)}\right\rceil+1
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition B.3

(Sensitivity)
.

Consider a MDP
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},p,r)
that satisfies Assumption

(

2.2
. Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\Phi
be two stationary deterministic policies. Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{1},p}-\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{2},p}\right\rVert_{TV}\leq\frac{m^{\star}L_{p}}{1-\alpha}\rho_{\Phi,\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{1},p}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\leq\frac{m^{\star}L_{p}}{1-\alpha}\rho_{\Phi,\infty}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}).

Proof.

First note that
\rho_{\Phi,\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{1},p}}(\cdot,\cdot)\leq\rho_{\Phi,\infty}(\cdot,\cdot)
. It remains to prove the first inequality. We introduce a few shorthand notation to simplify the presentation. Denote
\mu^{(m)}_{\phi_{i},p}
by
\mu^{(m)}_{i}
,
\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{i},p}
by
\mu_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Let
p_{i}
denote the Markov transition kernel induced by policy
\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Denote
\Delta p:=p_{1}-p_{2}
and
\delta^{(m)}:=\mu^{(m)}_{1}-\mu^{(m)}_{2}
. Note that
\int{p_{i}~d\mu^{(m)}_{i}}
are the distributions at
m+1
. We have the following,

\displaystyle\delta^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\int{p_{1}~d\mu^{(m-1)}_{1}}-\int{p_{2}~d\mu^{(m-1)}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-1)}_{1}}+\int{p_{2}~d\delta^{(m-1)}}

\displaystyle=\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-1)}_{1}}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-2)}_{1}}\right)}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\delta^{(m-2)}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-1)}_{1}}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-2)}_{1}}\right)}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu^{(m-3)}_{1}}\right)}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\delta^{(m-3)}}\right)}\right)}.

(48)

Note that if the difference between the distributions of states at time
m-3
is
\delta^{(m-3)}
, then the last term is the difference between distributions of states at time
m
of two CMPs whose evolutions are governed by transition kernel
p_{2}
from time

\displaystyle\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\delta^{(m-3)}}\right)}\right)}=\mathbb{P}_{\phi_{2}}(s_{m}\mid s_{m-3}\sim\mu^{(m-3)}_{1})-\mathbb{P}_{\phi_{2}}(s_{m}\mid s_{m-3}\sim\mu^{(m-3)}_{2}),

(49)

where

denotes the probability measure associated with the CMP with transition kernel
p_{2}
. Letting

in (

k

\displaystyle\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu}_{1}\right)}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}\right)}\right)}+\ldots

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\mathbb{P}_{\phi_{2}}(s_{m}\mid s_{m-k}\sim\mu_{1})-\mathbb{P}_{\phi_{2}}(s_{m}\mid s_{m-k}\sim\mu_{2})\right).

(50)

Now, letting

\displaystyle\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}=\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu}_{1}\right)}+\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{p_{2}~d\left(\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}\right)}\right)}+\ldots.

(51)

Note that
\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}=\int{p_{1}~d\mu_{1}}-\int{p_{2}~d\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\left\lVert\int{\Delta p~d\mu_{1}}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\sup_{A\subseteq\mathcal{S}}{\left\{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\Delta p(s,A)~d\mu_{1}(s)}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\sup_{A\subseteq\mathcal{S}}{\{\Delta p(s,A)\}}~d\mu_{1}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\int{\left\lVert\Delta p\right\rVert_{TV}~d\mu_{1}}.

(52)

Also,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{1},p}-\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{2},p}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{\star}}{1-\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\left\lVert p(s,\phi_{1}(s),\cdot)-p(s,\phi_{2}(s),\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}~d\mu_{1}(s)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{\star}L_{p}}{1-\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\phi_{1}(s),\phi_{2}(s))~d\mu_{1}(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{\star}L_{p}}{1-\alpha}\rho_{\Phi,\mu^{(\infty)}_{\phi_{1},p}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}),

where the second inequality follows from Assumption
2.1
(
ii
). This concludes the proof.

Lemma B.4

(Bound on the span of relative value function)

Proof.

Lemma B.6

(Bound on the span of policy evaluation iterates)
.

Consider an MDP
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},p,r)
such that
p
satisfies Assumption
2.2
, and consider the policy evaluation algorithm for policy
\phi\in\Phi
on
\mathcal{M}
i.e.,

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle V^{\phi}_{i+1}(s)

\displaystyle=r(s,\phi(s))+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{p(s,\phi(s),s^{\prime})V^{\phi}_{i}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}}.

Appendix C
Metric Space of Policies

The following definitions are some widely used notions of the size of sets in a metric space, and closely follow Definition
1
of
Cao and Krishnamurthy (2020)
.

Definition C.1
.

Let
(X,\rho_{X})
be a metric space.

•

An
\epsilon
-covering of
X
is a collection of subsets of
X
such that each element of the collection has a diameter at most
\epsilon
. The cardinality of the smallest
\epsilon
-covering is called the
\epsilon
-covering number, denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X)
.

•

The covering dimension of
X
is defined as
d_{X}:=\inf\{d\geq 0:\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X)\leq c~\epsilon^{-d}~\forall\epsilon>0,\mbox{ for some constant }c>0\}
.

•

A
\epsilon
-packing is a collection of points
\bar{X}\subset X
such that
\min_{x\neq x^{\prime}\in\bar{X}}{\rho_{X}(x,x^{\prime})}\geq\epsilon
. The cardinality of the largest
\epsilon
-packing is called the
\epsilon
-packing number, denoted by
\mathcal{N}^{\mbox{pack}}_{\epsilon}(X)
.

•

An
\epsilon
-net of
X
is an
\epsilon
-packing
X_{\epsilon}\subset X
for which
\{\{x^{\prime}\in X:\rho_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\leq\epsilon\};x\in X_{\epsilon}\}
covers
X
.

•

The doubling constant of
(X,\rho_{X})
is defined as
\Lambda(X):=\max_{\epsilon>0,x\in X}\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon/2}(\{x^{\prime}\in X:\rho_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\leq\epsilon\})
, which is the maximum
\epsilon/2
-covering number of balls of radius
\epsilon
in
X
.

We note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\mbox{pack}}_{2\epsilon}(X)\leq\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\mbox{pack}}_{\epsilon}(X).

Next, we shall show that the metric
\rho_{\Phi_{SD},\nu}(\cdot,\cdot)
(
13
) induces a metric space on the set of stationary deterministic policies,
\Phi_{SD}
.

Lemma C.2
.

(\Phi_{SD},\rho_{\Phi_{SD},\nu})
is a metric space, where two policies
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are equivalent if
\rho_{\Phi_{SD},\nu}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=0
, i.e.,

1.

Non-negativity: For any

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}{\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{t}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}\left(J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})-r(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}))\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}{\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{t}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\left(\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{t-1}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t-1})-\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{t}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})\right)p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{0}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}~\textit{sp}\left(h_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot;\phi_{0})\right)+\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\phi_{\tau}\in\Phi_{i}\right\}}~\textit{sp}\left(h_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot;\phi_{\tau})\right).

(53)

Consider the first term in the r.h.s. of (

\eta_{t}:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C~\textit{sp}\left(r\right)}{1-\alpha}(1+K_{\tau}(\Phi_{i})),\mbox{ where }\tau\in\{1,2,\ldots\},i=1,2,\ldots,N

; after performing some algebraic calculations, we conclude that the radius of
B_{t}(\phi)
will never drop below
\Delta_{\Phi}(\phi)/(2+L_{J})
. Since initially at
t=0
there is only a single active policy, each set in
\Phi^{(f)}_{\mbox{cover}}
contains at most one active policy for every
\tau\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
. This concludes the proof of
1
.

Now we will prove the second statement. For the diameter of a policy
\phi\in\Phi_{2^{-i}}
to go below
\Delta_{\Phi}(\phi)/(2+L_{J})
, it enough to play it
N
times where

is the smallest natural number that satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\left(\sqrt{\frac{c^{(f)}_{d}}{N}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}}+\frac{1+\log_{2}{(N)}}{N}\right)\leq 2^{-i}/(2+L_{J}),

or,

\displaystyle N\geq c^{(f)}_{d}\left(\frac{2C(2+L_{J})}{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\delta}\right)}~2^{2i}.

This proves the second claim.
∎

Appendix G
Regret Analysis

Regret decomposition:

Recall the regret decomposition of regret (
1
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}(T;\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{J^{\star}_{\mathcal{M},\Phi}-r(s_{t},a_{t})}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\left(J^{\star}_{\mathcal{M},\Phi}-J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})\right)}}_{(a)}+\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\left(J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})-r(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}))\right)}}_{(b)}.

(84)

The term (a) captures the regret arising due to the gap between the optimal value of the average reward and the average reward of the policies
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
that are actually played in different episodes, while (b) captures the sub-optimality arising since the distribution of the induced Markov chain does not reach the stationary distribution in finite time. (a) and (b) are bounded separately. We firstly bound the term (b); this bound is the same for both the algorithms
PZRL-MF
and
PZRL-MF
. The bounds on the term (a) vary for
PZRL-MF
and
PZRL-MB
, and hence are derived separately.

Proposition G.1
.

Consider,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})-r(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}))}\leq\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}\log{\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)}}+\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)}(1+K(T)),

(85)

where
K(T)
denotes the total number of episodes until time
T
. Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(\mbox{Fluc.})}_{\delta}:=\left\{\omega:~\eqref{bdd:fluctuation}\mbox{ holds }\right\}.

(86)

For both the algorithms PZRL-MF and PZRL-MB we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{G}^{(\mbox{Fluc.})}_{\delta}\right)\geq 1-\delta,~\delta\in(0,1).

(87)

Proof.

We begin by converting the Markovian noise to a martingale difference sequence, using a similar technique that was used in the proof of Lemma
D.1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})-r(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}),ds)}-\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{0})p(s_{0},\phi_{0}(s_{0}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{0},\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\left(h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t-1})\right)p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{T-1})p(s_{T-1},\phi_{T-1}(s_{T-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{0},\phi_{0}).

(88)

Now consider the first term in the r.h.s. of (
88
). Denote
\eta_{t}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})
. Noting that for both
PZRL-MF
as well as
PZRL-MB
,
\phi_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}

\displaystyle=0.

Hence,
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}
is a martingale difference sequence. Also, from the bound on the span of
h_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot;\phi)
that was derived in Lemma
B.4
, we have that
\eta_{t}\in\left[-\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)},\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)}\right]
. An application of Azuma-Hoeffding inequality (Lemma
I.1
), yields the following: for each
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{h_{\mathcal{M}}(s;\phi_{t})p(s_{t-1},\phi_{t-1}(s_{t-1}),ds)}-h_{\mathcal{M}}(s_{t},\phi_{t})}\leq\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}\log{\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)}}.

Now, consider the second term in the r.h.s. of (
88
). The
t
-th element in this summation can assume a non-zero value only when a new episode starts at time
t
. So, the second term can be upper-bounded by
\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)}K(T)
using Lemma
B.4
, where
K(T)
denotes the number of episodes that have been started until time
T
by the learning algorithm. Again by using Lemma
B.4
, the third term can be bounded by
\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)}
.

Putting all the individual bounds together, we have that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}{J_{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{t})-r(s_{t},\phi_{t}(s_{t}))}\leq\frac{C}{1-\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}\log{\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)}}+\frac{C}{2(1-\alpha)}(1+K(T)).

(89)

This concludes the proof.
∎

In the next two consecutive lemmata, we derive upper-bounds on
K(T)
for
PZRL-MF
and
PZRL-MB
, respectively.

Lemma G.2
.

On the set
\mathcal{G}^{(\mbox{MF})}
, the number of total episodes played by PZRL-MF is bounded above as

Appendix H

In this section, we derive some useful properties of the algorithm that are used in the proof of regret upper bound. The first lemma shows that for any active cell
\zeta
at time
t
, the quantity
\frac{1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}(\zeta)}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta_{t_{i}}\right)}}
is bounded above by

. We use this in concentration inequality for the transition kernel estimate.

Lemma H.1
.

For all
t\in[T-1]
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
, let
t_{i}
denote the time instance when
\zeta
or any of its ancestor was visited by PZRL-MB for the
i
-th time. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}(\zeta)}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta_{t_{i}}\right)}\leq 3~\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right).

Proof.

By the activation rule (
A.3
), a cell
\zeta^{\prime}
can be played at most
N_{\max}(\zeta^{\prime})-N_{\min}(\zeta^{\prime})=\tilde{c}_{1}2^{2\ell(\zeta^{\prime})}+\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}}{3}\mathbb{I}_{\left\{\zeta^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\right\}}
times while being active, where
\tilde{c}_{1}=3c_{1}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}^{-2}\log{\left(\frac{T}{\epsilon\delta}\right)}~\epsilon^{-d_{\mathcal{S}}}
. We can write,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}(\zeta)}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta_{t_{i}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\min}(\zeta)}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta_{t_{i}}\right)}+\frac{1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\sum_{i=N_{\min}(\zeta)+1}^{N_{t}(\zeta)}{\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta_{t_{i}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}}{3N_{t}(\zeta)}+\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell(\zeta)-1}{2^{\ell}}+\frac{N_{t}(\zeta)-N_{\min}(\zeta)-1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\sqrt{d_{\mathcal{S}}}}{N_{t}(\zeta)}2^{\ell(\zeta)}+\frac{N_{t}(\zeta)-N_{\min}(\zeta)-1}{N_{t}(\zeta)}\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(N_{t}(\zeta)+2N_{\min}(\zeta)-1)~\mbox{diam}\left(\zeta\right)}{N_{t}(\zeta)}

\displaystyle f(x)=a_{0}x-a_{1}\sqrt{x}-a_{2}.

Then for all
x\geq\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{0}}\right)^{2}+3\frac{a_{2}}{a_{0}}
,
f(x)\geq 0
.

Proof.

Taking
y=x^{2}
and applying quadratic formula, we have that

\displaystyle a_{0}y^{2}-a_{1}y-a_{2}\geq 0,

if
y\geq\frac{a_{1}+\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+4a_{0}a_{2}}}{2a_{0}}
. Therefore,
f(x)\geq 0
if
x\geq\left(\frac{a_{1}+\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+4a_{0}a_{2}}}{2a_{0}}\right)^{2}>\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{0}^{2}}+\frac{3a_{2}}{a_{0}}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma I.6
.

Let
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
be two probability measures on
Z
and let
v
be an
\mathbb{R}
-valued bounded function on
Z
. Then, the following holds.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Z}{v(z)(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(dz)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}~\textit{sp}\left(v\right).

Proof.

Denote
\lambda(\cdot):=\mu_{1}(\cdot)-\mu_{2}(\cdot)
. Now let
Z_{+},Z_{-}\subset Z
be such that
\lambda(B)\geq 0
for every
B\subseteq Z_{+}
and
\lambda(B)<0
for every
B\subseteq Z_{-}
. We have that

\displaystyle\lambda(Z)=\lambda(Z_{+})+\lambda(Z_{-})=0.

(100)

Also,

\displaystyle\lambda(Z_{+})-\lambda(Z_{-})=\left\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}.

(101)

Combining the above two, we get that

\displaystyle\lambda(Z_{+})=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}.

(102)

Now,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Z}{v(z)\lambda(dz)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Z_{+}}{v(z)\lambda(dz)}+\int_{Z_{-}}{v(z)\lambda(dz)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\lambda(Z_{+})\sup_{z\in Z}{v(z)}+\lambda(Z_{-})\inf_{z\in Z}{v(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=\big|\lambda(Z_{+})\sup_{z\in Z}{v(z)}-\lambda(Z_{+})\inf_{z\in Z}{v(z)}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda(Z_{+})\inf_{z\in Z}{v(z)}+\lambda(Z_{-})\inf_{z\in Z}{v(z)}\big|

\displaystyle=\lambda(Z_{+})\left(\sup_{z\in Z}{v(z)}-\inf_{z\in Z}{v(z)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right\rVert_{TV}~\textit{sp}\left(v\right).

Hence, we have proven the lemma.
∎

Lemma I.7
.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be two transition probability kernels of two Markov chains with common state space
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}{\left\lVert\theta_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}}\leq c
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\theta^{(m)}_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta^{(m)}_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}\leq m\cdot c,~\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

where
\theta^{(m)}_{i}
is the
m
-step transition kernel of the Markov chain with one-step transition kernel
\theta_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

We shall prove this using mathematical induction. The base case is given. Let us assume that,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\theta^{(i)}_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta^{(i)}_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}\leq i\cdot c,~\forall i=1,2,\ldots,m-1.

See that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\theta^{(m)}_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta^{(m)}_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{1}(s,s^{\prime})\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},\cdot)ds^{\prime}}-\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},\cdot)ds^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},\cdot)ds^{\prime}}-\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\theta_{2}(s^{\prime},\cdot)ds^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{TV}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\left(\theta^{(m-1)}_{1}(s,s^{\prime})-\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\right)\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},A)ds^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}}{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\left(\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},A)-\theta_{2}(s^{\prime},A)\right)ds^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\theta^{(m-1)}_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}}{~\textit{sp}\left(\theta_{1}(\cdot,A)\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathcal{S}}{\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,s^{\prime})\left\lVert\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},\cdot)-\theta_{2}(s^{\prime},\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}ds^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\theta^{(m-1)}_{1}(s,\cdot)-\theta^{(m-1)}_{2}(s,\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}+\max_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}{\left\lVert\theta_{1}(s^{\prime},\cdot)-\theta_{2}(s^{\prime},\cdot)\right\rVert_{TV}},

where the first inequality follows from triangle inequality and from the definition of total variation distance, the second inequality follows from Lemma
I.6
and by taking the supremum inside integration. This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

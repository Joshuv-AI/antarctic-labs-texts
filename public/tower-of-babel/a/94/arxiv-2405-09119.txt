Molecular order induced charge transfer in a C 60 -topological insulator moiré heterostructure
Ram Prakash Pandeya, Konstantin P. Shchukin, Yannic Falke, Gregor Mussler, Jalil Abdur Rehman, Nicolae Atodiresei, Alexander V. Fedorov, Boris V. Senkovskiy, Daniel Jansen, Giovanni Di Santo, Luca Petaccia, Alexander Grüneis

Abstract
We synthesize and spectroscopically investigate monolayer C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} on the topological insulator (TI) Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . This C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} / Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} heterostructure is characterized by excellent translational order in a novel ( 4 × 4 4\times 4 ) C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} superstructure on a ( 9 × 9 9\times 9 ) unit of Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} . We measure the full two-dimensional energy band structure of C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} / Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} using angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES). We find that C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} accepts electrons from the TI at room temperature but no charge transfer occurs at low temperatures. We unravel this peculiar behaviour by Raman spectroscopy of C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} / Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and density functional theory (DFT) calculations of the electronegativity of C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} . Both methods are sensitive to orientational order of C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} . At low temperatures, Raman spectroscopy shows a dramatic intensity increase of the C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} Raman signal, evidencing a transition to a rotationally ordered state. DFT reveals that the orientational order of C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} at low temperatures has a higher electron affinity than at high temperatures. These results neatly explain the temperature-dependent charge transfer observed in ARPES. Our conclusions are supported by the appearance of a strong photoluminescence from C 60 \text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}} / Bi 4 ​ Te 3 \text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} at low temperatures.

Introduction

Organic thin films deposited on metal or semiconductor substrates can form interfaces with exciting opto-electronic properties that are controlled by the charge transfer between the molecule and the substrate and the molecular order. The large variety of organic molecules allows for a flexible modification of the interface properties.
Chugh et al. (2019)
Two intertwined aspects for obtaining interfaces with defined properties are the molecular order and the molecule-substrate-interaction.
Gruznev et al. (2013)
A strong molecule-substrate interaction can imprint molecular order, however it is detrimental to inheriting the molecular properties to the interface electronic states because it alters the molecular character of the electronic states at the surface. Thus, one wishes to fine-tune the strength of the molecule-substrate interaction versus molecule-molecule interaction by choice of the substrate and molecule. The surfaces of bulk metals and also semiconductors often form covalent bonds to molecules because dangling bonds are present on their surface. Moreover, the interface structure is complicated by surface reonstructions, change in molecular shape and geometrical frustration.
Alfonso-Moro et al. (2023)
E.g. electron doping of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
on the reconstructed Cu surface is optimal for obtaining superconductivity, i.e. C
{}_{60}^{3+}
(Ref.
4
) and a large
7\times 7
superstructure forms for
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
on Au.
Schull and Berndt (2007)
On the other hand, van-der-Waals substrates have weaker interaction to molecules due to absence of dangling bonds. For example, for pentacene on graphene, a coverage dependent molecular orientation has been oberserved that could further be tuned by doping of the graphene substrate.
Liu et al. (2013)
Similarly, it has been observed that the charge transfer between
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and graphene can be controlled by a gate voltage applied to graphene; this charge transfer in turn controls the growth mode and molecular order of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
.
Nguyen et al. (2020)
Unbiased graphene becomes p-doped when covered by
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
which has been revealed by an upshift in the Raman active 2D band of graphene and a shift of the conductance minimum as a function of gate voltage.
Qin et al. (2020)
;
Matković et al. (2017)
Van-der-Waals epitaxy and interaction of organic molecules has been oberserved for PTDCA on graphene
Wang and Hersam (2009)
,
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}

;
Guo et al. (2020)

\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}

, PDCTA on borphene

Huang et al. (2020)

;

;
Kleemann et al. (2020)

Much less is known about the interaction of organic molecules with topological insulators (TIs). The organic layer/TI system allows for studying the stability of topologically protected electronic states and provides a new substrate for growth. To that end the molecule-TI interactions have been investigated for monolayers of MnPc, CoPc, and CuPc
Sessi et al. (2014)
;
Bathon et al. (2015)
and PTCDA.
Yang et al. (2015)
Highly ordered
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
in a hexagonal structure with a thickness of 50
Å
has been synthesized on Bi
2
Se
3
topological insulator.
Latzke et al. (2019)
Yet, the rather large thickness of the organic
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
layers prevented a study of the structure of the interface by surface science methods such as photoemission or scanning probe techniques. In particular, the charge transfer between the TI to the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
, the change of the topologically protected surface state, the molecular orientation and the optical properties of the interface are not known.
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
stands out from other organic molecules as a highly symmetric molecule with a triply degnerate HOMO and LUMO level which exhibits strong electron-photon interaction and correlated electronic ground statee when alkali-metal doped.
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
The
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecule acts as a cage for endohedral encapsulation of atoms along with a high degree of available functionalizations.
Yan et al. (2014)
;
Li et al. (2015)
;
Carrillo-Bohórquez
et al. (2021)
;
Komatsu et al. (2005)
;
Whitener et al. (2008)

For single crystal
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and thick films of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
, a structural phase transition occurs for
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
at 250 K from face centered cubic (fcc) to simple cubic (sc).
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
This phase transition is related to the orientation of adjacent
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules. For fcc, neighbouring molecules have different orientation whereas for sc there the orientation of neighouring
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
is identical.
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
This phase transition also has a large impact on the Raman spectra. That is, the intense Ag(2) mode, or pentagonal pinch mode hardens and becomes sharper below the transition temperature. It is clear that for a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
monolayer on a substrate that phase transition can be modified or even absent. Yet, until now, there are no reports on the effect of substrate interaction on that transition despite it might be important for the charge transfer of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
to the substrate.
Here, we investigate the properties of monolayer (ML)
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
on the novel topological insulator
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
Yamana et al. (1979)
;
Chagas et al. (2020)
;
Chagas et al. (2022)
;
Nabok et al. (2022)
Interestingly, ML
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
grows in a (
4\times 4
)
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
superstructure on a (
9\times 9
) grid with regards to the underlying TI. This structure results in a sharp moiré pattern observed in low energy electron diffraction (LEED). Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) reveals that the TI becomes hole doped upon
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
deposition. Interestingly, the molecular order of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
is crucial to the hole doping. That is, when the temperature is lowered to to 80 K by cooling with liquid N
2
, the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules change the angle of orientation and the hole doping effect is reversed. Resonance Raman spectroscopy of the pentagonal pinch mode of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
is shown to be highly sensitive to changes in molecular ordering: upon cooling to 80 K, that Raman mode increases dramatically in intensity and its linewidth narrows. Calculations using density functional theory (DFT) are able to explain the changes in charge transfer with the molecular orientation.

2.3
Structural phase transition in
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
: electronic and optical properties

Raman spectroscopy of bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
evidences a structural phase transition from fcc to sc around 250 K which is a result of rotational ordering of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
.
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
In the sc structure of bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
, all the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules have the same orientation. At low temperatures in the sc bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
phase, the Ag(2) Raman mode of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
increases in intensity, shifts to higher frequency and drastically reduces its linewidth.
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
In the following we investigate this phase transition in the limit of 0.5 and one ML
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. Figure 3a depicts the Raman spectra recorded at 300 K and at 80 K for a clean
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, one ML of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and 0.5 ML of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. It can be seen that
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
has a broad background at 1450 cm
-1
from which the Ag(2) Raman peak of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
emerges. At a temperature of 85 K, the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
Raman spectra in the vicinity of the Ag(2) mode drastically change. The
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
Ag(2) Raman mode shifts to higher frequency, becomes sharper and more intense. The behaviour of the Raman modes in
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
upon cooling is very similar to the fcc to sc transition observed in bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
.
Matus et al. (1991)
;
Dresselhaus et al. (1996)
However, as we will show in the following, the details of the structural transition in rotational order of monolayer
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
on
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is somewhat different from that observed in bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. Below we show via ARPES that this structural transition in rotational order of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
goes hand in hand with a change in the charge transfer between
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. Figures 3b and 3c depict ARPES of a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
heterostructure recorded at 30 K in the energy range of the Dirac cone of
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and the molecular bands of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
, respectively. Figure 3d shows a comparative MDC analysis of the ARPES spectra of pure
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
at RT and
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
at 30 K carried out on the same sample. This
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
surface was prepared on a different
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
film than the surface shown in Figure 1 and therefore has a slightly different carrier concentration. The analysis in Figure 3d affirms the previous conclusion from Figure 1, namely that upon
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
deposition, the Fermi surface size of
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
shrinks. From Figure 3d we also observe that, upon cooling the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
heterostructure to 30 K, the Fermi surface size increases almost back to the original value of pristine
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. We relate this observation to a temperature-dependent charge transfer between
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

at two different momenta. It can be seen that the HOMO and HOMO-1 bands shift to higher binding energy by 60 meV and by 110 meV, respectively when cooling down the sample from RT to 30 K. Importantly, this shift is not related to electron doping of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
because calculations below show that the LUMO (not visible in ARPES) also shifts to a higher energy when the sample is cooled. That is, the band gap between HOMO and LUMO for a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
film increases as we cool from 300 K to 30 K. As a consequence, we have charge neutrality of the
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
heterostructure despite the electron transfer from
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
to
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
upon cooling from 300 K to 30 K.

In order to rationalize the observed findings (i.e. the change of charge transfer between
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
as a function of temperature), we perform calculations of the total energy and the electron affinity
E_{A}
by DFT. Note that
E_{A}
is defined as the difference of the total energy between the charged
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and the neutral
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. For the case of strong electron acceptors as
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
, the charged
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
(with one extra electron in the LUMO) has a lower total energy which leads to negative values of
E_{A}
. We model the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
ML by considering a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
dimer and calculate its total energy and
E_{A}
as a function of the relative angle between the two
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules that form the dimer. The dimer is an excellent qualitative approximation to our system in which the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
-
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
interactions dominate over the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
-
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
interaction. As a consequence, their electronegativity depends on the relative rotation of neighbouring
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
.
Figure 3f depicts the total energy of the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
dimer as a function of the rotation angle between them. There is one global minimum at a relative rotational angle of about 70
∘
(
\alpha
phase). Another local minimum is located at a relative rotational angle of about 20
∘
(
\beta
phase). We therefore expect that at low temperatures, all
{}\mathrm{C}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{60}}}{}
are in the
\alpha
phase and that at increased temperature some of the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules occupy the
\beta
phase. Let us consider the electron affinity
E_{A}
of these two phases in order to rationalize the observed changes in charge transfer to the
{}{}\mathrm{Bi}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{4}}}\mathrm{Te}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{3}}}
substrate. Figure 3g depicts the calculated
E_{A}
as a function of relative rotational angle of the two
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules to one another. For the
\alpha
phase, the
E_{A}=-3.123
eV and for the
\beta
phase,
E_{A}=-3.126
eV. The smaller value of
E_{A}
for the
\beta
phase means that in this phase, the energy gain per
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
is larger, if an electron is accepted from
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. That is, if more
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
dimers are in the
\beta
phase, the electron transfer from
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
to
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
will be larger. This nicely explains the experimental observation that, at 300 K we have a smaller
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
Fermi surface than at 30 K. The calculations were done for a
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
dimer and that the calculated difference in
E_{A}
between
\alpha
and
\beta
phases is 3 meV per
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. Yet, in the experiment, each
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
has six neighbours and the total energy difference will be enhanced compared to the calculated value which holds for the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
dimer.

The changes in charge transfer and
E_{A}
due to rotational ordering at low temperature result also profoundly affect the optical properties of the heterostructure. To that end, we have recorded the changes of the photoluminescence of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
upon cooling. Figure 3h depicts PL spectra at different temperatures between room temperature and 80 K. It can be seen that upon cooling the luminescence at 1.7 eV increases in intensity. We observe the appearance of PL at temperatures below
\sim
250 K. This is likely related to the increased rotational order which goes together with the absence of electron transfer from the
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
to
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. The PL increase at low temperatures may partially be a result of the supressed trion formation in charge neutral
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
molecules which decay non-radiatively and due to other supressed non-radiative decay mechanisms. The luminescence spectra of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
are modified by electron-phonon coupling. That is, the luminescence has side peaks that are red-shifted from the main peak by one or several phonon energies (magnified spectrum in Figure 3b). This observation for the case of ML
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
is not surprising. Indeed, a strong electron-phonon coupling in bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
has been oberved
Reber et al. (1991)
and is closely related to superconductivity in alkali metal doped
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
such as
Rb_{3}C_{60}
.
Finally, we summarize the results obtained in Figure 3i showing
E_{A}
as a function of temperature. At 30 K, only the
\alpha
phase contributes and dimers in this phase have a high
E_{A}
. As a consequence of the high
E_{A}

\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
to
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}

\beta

\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
to
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. This simple picture neatly explains the experimental observations based on the rotational ordering of
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
on
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
rotational order is a means to control charge transfer and optical properties of the
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
-
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
moiré heterostructure.

3
Conclusion and outlook

We have synthesized a weakly interacting interface between
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and a highly ordered monolayer
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}

and investigated its structural, electronic and optical properties. We have found via LEED measurements that
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}

crystallizes with long-range order that is different from thick
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
films on TI and has not yet been reported for a TI–organic interface. We have observed a structural phase transition in the rotational order of monolayer
{}\mathrm{C}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{60}}}
that is reminiscent to the structural transition in bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. However, in the case of ML
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
/
\text{Bi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{Te}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, theory suggests a different rotational angle for neighbouring
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
in the low temperature phase than for bulk
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
. This rotational ordering goes hand-in-hand with a modified charge transfer. Our work illustrates the complex temperature-dependent interplay between charge transfer and rotational order in heterostructures between
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{60}}}
and TI. Our observations are explained by DFT calculations of the total energy and electron affinity of
{}\mathrm{C}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{60}}}{}
. Our work highlights that topological insulator / organic heterostructures are interesting material systems with tunable optoelectronic properties that can be grown by van-der-Waals epitaxy. We expect that similar results may be achieved for 2D material / organic heterostructures. This research direction could e.g. be followed up in one of the following ways. First, magnetic fullerenes on TI can be grown as a material system to study the effects of Zeeman splitting on the spin-polarized bands of the TI. Second, the van der Waals epitaxy of

Concentration of norms of random vectors with independent p p -sub-exponential coordinates


Abstract
We present examples of p p -sub-exponential random variables for any positive p p . We prove two types of concentration of standard p p -norms ( 2 2 -norm is the Euclidean norm) of random vectors with independent p p -sub-exponential coordinates around the Lebesgue L p L^{p} -norms of these p p -norms of random vectors. In the first case p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , our estimates depend on the dimension n n of random vectors. But in the second one for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , with an additional assumption, we get an estimate that does not depend on n n . In other words, we generalize some know concentration results in the Euclidean case to cases of the p p -norms of random vectors with independent p p -sub-exponential coordinates.

1
Introduction and results

Let
p
be a positive number. We consider random variables with
p
-
sub-exponential tail decay
, i.e., random variables
X
for which there exists two positive constants
c,C
such that

\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq c\exp\big(-(t/C)^{p}\big)

for all
t\geq 0
. Such random variables we will call
p
-
sub-exponential
.

Example 1.1
.

The exponentially distributed random variable
X\sim Exp(1)
has exponential tail decay that is

\mathbb{P}(X\geq t)=\exp(-t)
.
It is the example of random variable with
1
-sub-exponential tail decay;
c=C=1
.
Consider a random variable
Y_{p}=\theta X^{1/p}
for some
p,\theta>0
. Observe that for
t\geq 0

\mathbb{P}(Y_{p}\geq t)=\mathbb{P}\big(\theta X^{1/p}\geq t\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(X\geq(t/\theta)^{p}\big)=\exp\big(-(t/\theta)^{p}\big).

The random variable
Y_{p}
has
p
-sub-exponential tail decay;
c=1
and
C=\theta
. Let us note that
Y_{p}
has the Weibull distribution with the shape parameter
p
and the scale parameter
\theta
. One can say that random variables with Weibull distributions form model examples of r.v.s with
p
-sub-exponential tail decay.

Because it is know that random variables with the Poisson and the geometric distributions have
1
-sub-exponential tail decay then, in similar way as above, we can form another families of
p
-sub-exponential random variables for any
p>0
.

A more interesting case, which is independently interesting, occurs when we start with Gaussian distributions.

Example 1.2
.

Let
g
denote a random variable with the standard normal distribution. It is know that for tails of such variables hold the estimate:

\mathbb{P}(|g|\geq t)\leq\exp(-t^{2}/2),

for
t\geq 0
(see for instance
[
4
, Prop.2.2.1]
). Defining now
Y_{p}=\theta|g|^{2/p}
, by the above estimate, we get

\mathbb{P}(Y_{p}\geq t)=\mathbb{P}\big(\theta|g|^{2/p}\geq t\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(|g|\geq(t/\theta)^{p/2}\big)\leq\exp\Big(-\big[t/(2^{1/p}\theta)\big]^{p}\Big).

In other words we obtain another family of r.v.s with
p
-sub-exponential tail decay;
c=1
and
C=2^{1/p}\theta
.

Define now a symmetric random variable
g_{p}
such that
|g_{p}|=|g|^{2/p}
. One can calculate that
its density function has the form

f_{p}(x)=\frac{p}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}|x|^{p/2-1}e^{-|x|^{p}/2}.

Let us emphasize that for
p=2
we get the density of the standard normal distribution.
Observe that
\mathbb{E}g_{p}=0
and
\mathbb{E}|g_{p}|^{p}=\mathbb{E}g^{2}=1
.
For any
p>0
we will call the random variable
g_{p}
the
standard
p
-normal
(
p
-
gaussian
)
and write
g_{p}\sim\mathcal{N}_{p}(0,1)
, where the first parameter denote the mean value but the second one the absolute
p
-th moment of
g_{p}
.

The
p
-sub-exponential random variables can be characterized by finiteness of
\psi_{p}
-norms defined as follows

\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}:=\inf\big\{K>0:\;\mathbb{E}\exp(|X/K|^{p})\leq 2\big\};

according to the standard convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
We will call the above functional
\psi_{p}
-norm but let us emphasize that only for
p\geq 1
it is a proper norm. For
0<p<1
it is so-called quasi-norm. It do not satisfy the triangle inequality (see Appendix A in
[
5
]
for more details).

Let us emphasize that
p
-sub-exponential random variables
X
satisfy the following
p
-sub-exponential tail decay:

\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\Big(-(t/\|X\|_{\psi_{p}})^{p}\Big);

see for instance
[
9
, Lem.2.1]
.

For
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p\geq 1
, let
|x|_{p}
denote the
p
-norm of
x
, i.e.,
|x|_{p}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p})^{1/p}
. For a random variable
X
, by
\|X\|_{L^{p}}
we will denote the Lebesgue norm of
X
, i.e.,
\|X\|_{L^{p}}=(\mathbb{E}|X|^{p})^{1/p}
.

From now on let
X=(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
denote a random vector with real coordinates.
We will be interested in the concentration of the norm
|X|_{p}
around
\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p})^{1/p}
in spaces of
p
-sub-exponential random variables.
In other words we will be interested in an estimate of the norm
\||X|_{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\|_{\psi_{p}}
.

I owe the first result of this type to the anonymous reviewer of the previous version of this paper, to whom I hereby express my thanks.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
X=(X_{1},...,X_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a random vector with
independent
p
-sub-exponential coordinates.Then

\||X|_{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq n^{1/(2p)}C^{1/p}K_{p},

where
K_{p}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}
and
C
is some universal constant.

Let us emphasize that, for
p\geq 2
, we can remove on the right hand side the factor
n^{1/(2p)}
but under an additional assumption that
p
-th moments of coordinates are the same, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{p}=\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p}
,
i=2,3,...,n
. Let us note that then

\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}=n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}
.
The main theorem of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
X=(X_{1},...,X_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a random vector with independent
p
-sub-exponential coordinates
X_{i}
that satisfy
\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{p}=\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p}
,
i=2,3,...,n
. Then

\||X|_{p}-n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq 6^{1/p}C\Big(\frac{K_{p}}{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}}\Big)^{p-1}K_{p},

where
K_{p}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}
and
C
is an universal constant.

Remark 1.5
.

The above theorem is the generalization of the concentration of
\psi_{2}
-norm of random vectors with independent sub-gaussian coordinates
(see Vershynin
[
8
, Th.3.1.1]
) to the case of
\psi_{p}
-norm of vectors
with
p
-sub-exponential coordinates, for
p\geq 2
.

Norms are Lipschitz function on given normed spaces. Concentration of Lipschitz functions on Gauss space is one of a basic example of concentration of measure phenomenon; see e.g.
[
2
, Th. 5.6]
,
[
1
, Cor. 3.2.6]
or, in a general form,
[
7
, Th. 5.3]
.

In Gauss space are investigated Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the Euclidean norm. In our approach we investigate the
p
-norms. Because Orlicz spaces are Banach lattices then we can immediately formulate some form of concentration for Lipschitz functions
f
with respect to
p
-norms of random vectors with
p
-sub-exponential coordinates, i.e.,

\big\|f(X)-\|f\|_{Lip}\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq\|f\|_{Lip}\big\||X|_{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{\psi_{p}},

where
\|f\|_{Lip}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|_{p}}
.
Let us note that in our approach a distribution of
f(X)
does not concentrate around of a median of
f
or a mean of
f(X)
but around the value

\|f\|_{Lip}\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}
.

-sub-exponential random variables (Section

2
Spaces of
p
-sub-exponential random variables

The
p
-sub-exponential random variables characterize the following lemma whose proof, for
p\geq 1
, one can find in
[
9
, Lem.2.1]
. Let us emphasize that this proof is valid for any positive
p
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a random variable and
p>0
. There exist positive constants
K,L,M

such that
the following conditions are equivalent:

1.
\mathbb{E}\exp(|X/K|^{p})\leq 2

(K\geq\|X\|_{\psi_{p}})
;

2.
\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-(t/L)^{p})
for all
t\geq 0
;

3.
\mathbb{E}|X|^{\alpha}\leq 2M^{\alpha}\Gamma\big(\frac{\alpha}{p}+1\big)
for all
\alpha>0
.

Remark 2.2
.

The definition of
\psi_{p}
-norm is based on condition 1. Let us notice that if condition 2 is satisfied with some constant
L
then

\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq 3^{1/p}L
(compare
[
9
, Rem.2.2]
).

Let
L_{0}
denote the space of all random variables defined on a given probability space. By
L_{\psi_{p}}
we will denote the space of random variables with finite
\psi_{p}
-norm:

L_{\psi_{p}}:=\{X\in L_{0}:\;\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}<\infty\}.

For
\psi_{p}
-norms one can formulate the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p,r>0
and
X\in L_{\psi_{pr}}
then
|X|^{p}\in L_{\psi_{r}}
and

\||X|^{p}\|_{\psi_{r}}=\|X\|^{p}_{\psi_{pr}}
.

Proof.

Let
K=\|X\|_{\psi_{pr}}>0
. Then

2=\mathbb{E}\exp(|X/K|^{pr})=\mathbb{E}\exp\big(\big||X|^{p}/K^{p}\big|^{r}),

which is equivalent to the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Let us emphasize that if we known the moment generating function of a given random variable
|X|
then we can calculate the
\psi_{p}
-norm of
|X|^{1/p}
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
X\sim Exp(1)
. The moment generating function of
X
equals
\mathbb{E}\exp(tX)=1/(1-t)
for
t<1
. Let us observe that

\mathbb{E}\exp(X/K)=\frac{1}{1-1/K}\leq 2

if
K\geq 2
. It means that
\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}=2
. In consequance, Weibull distributed random variables, with the shape parameter
p
and the scale parameter
\theta
, have the
\psi_{p}
-norms:

\|\theta X^{1/p}\|_{\psi_{p}}=\theta\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}^{1/p}=\theta 2^{1/p}.

Let us note that starting with the moment generating function of
g^{2}
of the form
(1-2t)^{-1/2}
(
t<1/2
), similarly as above, one can calculate that

\|g^{2}\|_{\psi_{1}}=\|g\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}=8/3
and
\|g_{p}\|_{\psi_{p}}=(8/3)^{1/p}
.

Let us notice that by Jensen’s inequality we get, for
p\geq 1
, that the
\psi_{p}
-norm of the expected value of
p
-sub-exponential random variable is not less than the
\psi_{p}
-norm of this random variable itself, since

2=\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(\big|X/\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\big|^{p}\Big)\geq\exp\Big(\big|\mathbb{E}X/\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\big|^{p}\Big)=\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(\big|\mathbb{E}X/\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\big|^{p}\Big),

which means that
\|\mathbb{E}X\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}
. In consequence, for
p
-sub-exponential random variable, we have

\|X-\mathbb{E}X\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq 2\|X\|_{\psi_{p}}\quad(p\geq 1).

Example 2.5
.

If

Since

In the following lemma it is shown that sub-exponential random variables possess the
approximate rotation invariance property
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X_{1},...,X_{n}
be independent sub-exponential random variables. Then

\tau_{\varphi_{1}}^{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{\varphi_{1}}^{2}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i}).

Proof.

Denote
\tau_{\varphi_{1}}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})
by
K_{i}
,
i=1,...n
. For independent centered sub-exponential r.v.s we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(t\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\exp\big(t(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\big)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\varphi_{\infty}(K_{i}t)=\exp\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{\infty}(K_{i}t)\Big).

Observe that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{\infty}(K_{i}t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}^{2})t^{2}&{\rm if}&t\leq 1/\max_{i}K_{i},\\
\infty&{\rm otherwise.}&\end{array}\right.

Since
\max_{i}K_{i}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}^{2}}
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{\infty}(K_{i}t)\leq\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}t\Big).

By the above, the estimate (
3
) and the definition of
\tau_{\varphi_{1}}
-norm we obtain that

\tau_{\varphi_{1}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)\leq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{\varphi_{1}}^{2}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)^{1/2}.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Let us note that if
X_{i}
are sub-exponential then
|X_{i}|
are sub-exponential too.
The above lemma implies that

\tau_{\varphi_{1}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|\Big)=\tau_{\varphi_{1}}\Big(|X|_{1}-\||X|_{1}\|_{1}\Big)\leq\sqrt{n}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\tau_{\varphi_{1}}\Big(|X_{i}|-\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|\Big)

(4)

In the following example it is shown that the factor
\sqrt{n}
on the right hand side is necessary

Example 2.8
.

Let
X_{i}\sim Exp(1)
,
i=1,...,n
, be independent random variables. Note that the cumulant generating function of their centered sum equals
nC_{X-1}

(
X\sim Exp(1)

\ln\mathbb{E}\exp[t(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-n)]=nC_{X-1}(t)=-nt-n\ln(1-t).

As in Example

we get

nC_{X-1}(t)=\frac{n}{2}C^{\prime\prime}_{X-1}(\theta_{t}t)t^{2}\quad(|t|<1)

and the
\tau_{\varphi_{1}}

equals
\sqrt{n}+1\sim\sqrt{n}
.

Because the norms
\tau_{\varphi_{1}}(\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{1}}
are equivalent on the space of centered sub-exponential random variables and

\||X_{i}|-\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq 2\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}
,

, then we can rewrite the inequality (

\||X|_{1}-\||X|_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq C\sqrt{n}K_{1},

where
K_{1}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}
and

is an universal constant.

The proof of the following proposition is similar to the proof of the upper bound in the large deviation theory (see for instance
[
6
, 5.11(4)Theorem. Large deviation]
) but with one difference. Instead of the cumulant generating function of a given random variable we use its upper estimate by the function
\varphi_{\infty}
and, in consequence, the convex conjugate
\varphi_{\infty}^{\ast}=\varphi_{1}
on its tail estimate (see
[
9
, Lem. 2.6]
), where

\varphi_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\frac{1}{2}x^{2}&{\rm if}&|x|\leq 1,\\
|x|-\frac{1}{2}&{\rm if}&|x|>1.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
, be independent sub-exponential random variables. Then

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big|\geq t\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-n\varphi_{1}\Big(\frac{t}{2C_{1}K}\Big)\Big),

where
K:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}
and
C_{1}
is the universal constant such that
\tau_{\varphi_{1}}(\cdot)\leq C_{1}\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{1}}
.

Proof.

The moment generating function of
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
can be estimated as follows

\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(u\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(u\frac{1}{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\Big(\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\tau_{\varphi_{1}}((X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i}))u\Big)\Big)

\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\Big(\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}C_{1}\|X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}u\Big)\Big)\leq\exp\Big(n\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku\Big)\Big).

The convex conjugate of the function
f(u):=n\varphi_{\infty}(\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku)
equals

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\Big\{tu-n\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku\Big)\Big\}=\sup_{u>0}\Big\{tu-n\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\sup_{u>0}\Big\{\frac{t}{2C_{1}K}\frac{2C_{1}Ku}{n}-\varphi_{\infty}\Big(\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku\Big)\Big\}=n\sup_{v>0}\Big\{\frac{t}{2C_{1}K}v-\varphi_{\infty}(v)\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\varphi_{1}(t/2C_{1}K);

the second equality holds since
\varphi_{\infty}
is the even function, the fourth one by the substituting
v=\frac{2}{n}C_{1}Ku
and the last one by definition of the convex conjugate for even functions and the equality
\varphi_{\infty}^{\ast}=\varphi_{1}
.
Thus we get

f^{\ast}(t)=n\varphi_{1}(t/2C_{1}K).

Similarly as in
[
3
, Lem. 2.4.3]
(formally
f
and
f^{\ast}
are not
N
-function, but the proof is the same also for these functions), we get

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big|\geq t\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-n\varphi_{1}\Big(\frac{t}{2C_{1}K}\Big)\Big).

∎

Remark 2.10
.

Let us emphasize that because

\varphi_{1}\big(t/(2C_{1}K)\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\big\{t^{2}/(4C_{1}^{2}K^{2}),t/(2C_{1}K)\big\},

then the above estimate implies a form of Bernstein’s inequality for averages:

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mathbb{E}X_{i})\Big|\geq t\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-\frac{n}{2}\min\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{4C_{1}^{2}K^{2}},\frac{t}{2C_{1}K}\Big\}\Big);

compare Vershynin
[
8
, Cor.2.8.3]
.

3
Proofs of the results

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Because, for
a\geq 0
, the function
a^{1/p}
is concave on the nonnegative half-line of real numbers, then the following inequality

\big|a-b\big|\geq\big|a^{1/p}-b^{1/p}\big|^{p}

(6)

holds for any
a,b\geq 0
.

If
X_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
, are
p
-sub-exponential random variables then
|X_{i}|^{p}
are the sub-exponential ones. Let
Y_{i}
denotes
|X_{i}|^{p}
and
Y
be a vector
(Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. By Lemma
2.3
we have
\|Y_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}=\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}^{p}
. Moreover
|Y|_{1}=|X|_{p}^{p}
and

\||Y|_{1}\|_{L^{1}}=\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}
. Substituting in (
5
)
Y
instead of
X
we get

\||Y|_{1}-\||Y|_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|_{\psi_{1}}=\||X|_{p}^{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq C\sqrt{n}K_{p}^{p},

where
K_{p}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}
and
C
is the universal constant.

By the definition of
\psi_{1}
-norm and inequality (
6
) with
a=|X|_{p}^{p}
and
b=\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}
we obtain

2\geq\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(\frac{\big||X|_{p}^{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\big|}{C\sqrt{n}K_{p}^{p}}\Big)\geq\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(\frac{\big||X|_{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\big|^{p}}{\big[(C\sqrt{n})^{1/p}K_{p}\big]^{p}}\Big),

which means that

\big\||X|_{p}-\||X|_{p}\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq(C\sqrt{n})^{1/p}K_{p}.

It finishes the proof of Proposition
1.3
.

The structure of the proof of Theorem
1.4
is similar to the proof in Vershynin
[
8
, Th. 3.1.1]
but, apart from Proposition
2.9
and Lemma
2.1
, we also use the following two technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x,\delta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
. If
|x-1|\geq\delta
then
|x^{p}-1|\geq\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}
.

Proof.

Under the above assumption on
x
and
p
we have:
|x^{p}-1|\geq|x-1|
. It means that if
|x-1|\geq\delta
then
|x^{p}-1|\geq\delta
. For
0\leq\delta\leq 1
we have
\delta^{p}\leq\delta
. In consequence

|x^{p}-1|\geq\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}
for
0\leq\delta\leq 1
.

Suppose now that
\delta>1
. The condition
|x-1|\geq\delta
is equivalent to

x\geq\delta+1
if
x\geq 1
or
x\leq 1-\delta
if
0\leq x\leq 1
. Let us observe that the second opportunity is not possible for
\delta>1
and
x\geq 0
. The first one gives
x^{p}\geq(\delta+1)^{p}\geq\delta^{p}+1
(
p\geq 1
) that is equivalent to
x^{p}-1\geq\delta^{p}
for
x\geq 1
. Summing up we get

|x^{p}-1|\geq\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}
for
x,\delta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
p\geq 2
then
\varphi_{1}(\max\{\gamma,\gamma^{p}\})\geq\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{p}
for
\gamma\geq 0
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\varphi_{1}
we have

\varphi_{1}(\max\{\gamma,\gamma^{p}\})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}&{\rm if}&0\leq\gamma\leq 1,\\
\gamma^{p}-\frac{1}{2}&{\rm if}&1<\gamma.\end{array}\right.

If
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
then
\varphi_{1}(\max\{\gamma,\gamma^{p}\})=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{p}
for
p\geq 2
.

If
1<\gamma
then the inequality
\varphi_{1}(\max\{\gamma,\gamma^{p}\})=\gamma^{p}-\frac{1}{2}>\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{p}
also holds.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Let us observe that the expression

\frac{1}{n\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}|X|_{p}^{p}-1=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{|X_{i}|^{p}}{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}-1\Big)

is the sum of independent and centered sub-exponential random variables. Moreover, by condition (
1
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\||X_{i}|^{p}-1\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq 2\||X_{i}|^{p}\|_{\psi_{1}}=2\|X_{i}\|^{p}_{\psi_{p}}\leq 2K^{p}_{p}.

Now, by virtue of Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
2.9
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}}|X|_{p}-1\Big|\geq\delta\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}|X|_{p}^{p}-1\Big|\geq\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}\Big)

(7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp\Big(-n\varphi_{1}\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}}{2C_{1}K^{p}_{p}}\Big)\Big),

for any
C\geq 2C_{1}
.

The inequality

2=\mathbb{E}\exp\Big(\frac{|X_{i}|}{\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}}\Big)^{p}\geq 1+\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{|X_{i}|}{\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}}\Big)^{p}

implies that
\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{p}}^{p}\geq\mathbb{E}|X_{i}|^{p}=\|X_{1}\|_{p}^{p}
,
i=1,...,n
, and, in consequence,

K_{p}^{p}\geq\|X_{1}\|_{p}^{p}
.
Since
C_{1}>1
, we have that
\|X_{1}\|_{p}^{p}/2C_{1}K^{p}_{p}
is less than
1
.
Under this condition we have

\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}}{2C_{1}K^{p}_{p}}\geq\max\Big\{\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{2C_{1}K_{p}^{p}},\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{2C_{1}K_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big\}.

By the definition of
\varphi_{1}
and Lemma
3.2
with
\gamma=\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta/(2C_{1}K_{p}^{p})
we get

\varphi_{1}\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\max\{\delta,\delta^{p}\}}{2C_{1}K^{p}_{p}}\Big)\geq\varphi_{1}\Big(\max\Big\{\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{CK_{p}^{p}},\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{CK_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big\}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{CK_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}.

Rearranging (
7
) and applying the above estimate we obtain the following

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big||X|_{p}-n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\Big|\geq n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\delta\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}}|X|_{p}-1\Big|\geq\delta\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp\Big(-n\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{CK_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\exp\Big(-\Big(\frac{n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\delta}{2^{1/p}CK_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big).

Changing variables to
t=n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\delta
, we get the following
p
-sub-exponential tail decay

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big||X|_{p}-n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\Big|\geq t\Big)\leq 2\exp\Big(-\Big(\frac{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}t}{2^{1/p}CK_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}\Big).

By Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.2
we obtain

\big\||X|_{p}-n^{1/p}\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq 6^{1/p}C\Big(\frac{K_{p}}{\|X_{1}\|_{L^{p}}}\Big)^{p-1}K_{p},\quad{\rm for}\;p\geq 2,

which finishes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Example 3.3
.

Let

{\bf g}_{p}=(g_{p,1},...,g_{p,n})

be a random vector with independent standard
p
-normal coordinates (
g_{p,i}\sim\mathcal{N}_{p}(0,1)
).
Recall that
\|g_{p,i}\|_{L^{p}}=1
and
\|g_{p,i}\|_{\psi_{p}}=(8/3)^{1/p}
, for
i=1,...,n
. Thus
K_{p}^{p}=8/3
. By Theorem
1.4
we get

\||{\bf g}_{p}|_{p}-n^{1/p}\|_{\psi_{p}}\leq\frac{8}{3}C6^{1/p}\quad{\rm for}\;p\geq 2.

Remark 3.4
.

Many problems deal with sub-gaussian and sub-exponential random variables may be considered in the spaces of

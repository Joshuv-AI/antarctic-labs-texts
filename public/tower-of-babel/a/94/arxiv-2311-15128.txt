Quickest Change Detection with Post-Change Density Estimation
Yuchen Liang, Venugopal V. Veeravalli

Abstract
The problem of quickest change detection in a sequence of independent observations is considered. The pre-change distribution is assumed to be known, while the post-change distribution is unknown. Two tests based on post-change density estimation are developed for this problem, the window-limited non-parametric generalized likelihood ratio (NGLR) CuSum test and the non-parametric window-limited adaptive (NWLA) CuSum test. Both tests do not assume any knowledge of the post-change distribution, except that the post-change density satisfies certain smoothness conditions that allows for efficient non-parametric estimation. Also, they do not require any pre-collected post-change training samples. Under certain convergence conditions on the density estimator, it is shown that both tests are first-order asymptotically optimal, as the false alarm rate goes to zero. The analysis is validated through numerical results, where both tests are compared with baseline tests that have distributional knowledge.



I
Introduction

The problem of quickest change detection (QCD) is of fundamental importance in mathematical statistics (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
]
for an overview). Given a sequence of observations whose distribution changes at some unknown change-point, the goal is to detect the change in distribution as quickly as possible after it occurs, while controlling the false alarm rate.
In classical formulations of the QCD problem, it is assumed that the pre- and post-change distributions are known, and that the observations are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) in the pre- and post-change regimes. However, in many practical situations, while it is reasonable to assume that we can accurately estimate the pre-change distribution, the post-change distribution is rarely completely known.

There have been extensive efforts to address pre- and/or post-change distributional uncertainty in QCD problems. In the case where both distributions are not fully known, one approach is to assume that the distributions are parametrized by a (low-dimensional) parameter that comes from a pre-defined parameter set, and to employ a generalized likelihood ratio (GLR) approach for detection. This approach was first introduced in
[
4
]
and later analyzed in more detail in
[
5
]
.
In particular, in
[
5
]
, it is assumed that the pre-change distribution is known and that the post-change distribution comes from a parametric family, with the parameter being finite-dimensional. A window-limited GLR test is proposed, which is shown to be asymptotically optimal under certain smoothness conditions.
This work has recently been extended to non-stationary post-change settings
[
6
]
. For the setting considered in
[
5
]
, a window-limited adaptive approach to constructing a QCD test was developed in recent work
[
7
]
. This adaptive test is also shown to achieve first-order asymptotic optimality
[
7
]
. In this paper, one of the test constructions for the case where the post-change is completely unknown is based on extending techniques introduced in
[
7
]
.

We assume complete knowledge of the pre-change distribution, while not making any parametric assumptions about the post-change distribution.
There has been prior work along these lines. One approach is to replace the log-likelihood ratio by some other useful statistic for distinguishing between distributions in constructing tests. Examples of this approach include the use of kernel M-statistics
[
8
,
9
]
, one-class SVMs
[
10
]
, nearest neighbors
[
11
,
12
]
, and Geometric Entropy Minimization
[
13
,
14
]
. In
[
8
]
, a test is proposed that compares the kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) within a window to a given threshold. A way to set the threshold is also proposed that meets the false alarm rate asymptotically
[
8
]
. Another approach is to estimate the log-likelihood ratio and thus the CuSum test statistic through a pre-collected training dataset. This include direct kernel estimation
[
15
]
and, more recently, neural network estimation
[
16
]
.
However,
the tests proposed in
[
8
]
–
[
16
]
lack explicit performance guarantees on the detection delay.

Our contributions are as follows:

1.

We propose a window-limited non-parametric generalized likelihood ratio (NGLR) CuSum test and a non-parametric window-limited adaptive (NWLA) CuSum test, both of which do not assume any knowledge of the post-change distribution (except that the post-change density satisfies certain smoothness conditions that allows for efficient non-parametric estimation), and do not require any post-change training data.

2.

We characterize a generic class of density estimators that enable detection.

3.

For both tests, we provide a way to set the test threshold to meet false alarm constraints (asymptotically).

4.

We show that both proposed tests are first-order asymptotically optimal with the selected thresholds, as the false alarm rate goes to zero.

5.

We validate our analysis through numerical results, in which we compare both tests with baseline tests that have distributional knowledge.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
, we describe some properties required of the density estimators for asymptotically optimal QCD. In Section
III
, we propose the NGLR-CuSum test and analyze its theoretical performance. In Section
IV
, we study the performance of the NWLA-CuSum test. Both tests are analyzed under the assumption that the post-change distribution is completely unknown.
In Section
V
, we present numerical results that validate the theoretical analysis.
In Section
VI
, we provide some concluding remarks.

A preliminary version of the results in this paper for the NGLR-CuSum test appeared in
[
1
]
.

II
Density Estimators for Quickest Detection

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be i.i.d. observations drawn from an unknown distribution, with probability density function (or
density
)
p
with respect to some dominating measure
\mu
, and let
\mathrm{supp}(p)
be the support of
p
. Let
\mathrm{E}_{p}
and
\mathrm{V}_{p}
denote, respectively, the expectation and variance operator on the sequence of observations, when the density corresponding to each observation is
p
. For two densities
p
and
q
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to
\mu
, the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is defined as:

D(p||q):=\int_{\mathrm{supp}(p)}\log(p(x)/q(x))p(x)d\mu(x).

Define
X^{[k,n]}:=X_{k},\dots,X_{n}
. Let
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
be a density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to
\mu
that is estimated using
X^{[k,n]}_{-i}:=X_{k},\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{n}
, where the subscript
-i
represents that
X_{i}
, with
k\leq i\leq n
, is the observation that is left out from
X^{[k,n]}
. We refer to
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
as a leave-one-out (LOO) estimator. Note that
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
and
X_{i}
are independent for each
1\leq k\leq i\leq n
.

With some possible abuse of notation, we also define

\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}:=\widehat{p}^{n,n-w}_{-n}

to be the LOO estimate of
p
obtained from the past
w
i.i.d. samples from
p
.

Suppose that, for large enough
w
, there exist constants
0<\beta_{1},C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
and
0<\beta_{2}<2
(that depend only on the density
p
and the estimation procedure) such that the KL loss
[
17
]
of the density estimator satisfies

\text{KL-loss}(\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}):=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[D(p||\widehat{p}^{w}_{n})\right]\leq\frac{C_{1}}{w^{\beta_{1}}}

(1)

where the KL divergence and the expectation operator
\mathrm{E}_{p}
are taken over the randomness of
X_{n}
and
\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}
, respectively.
Also, the second moment satisfies

\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\left(\log\frac{p(X_{n})}{\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}(X_{n})}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C_{2}}{w^{\beta_{2}}}.

(2)

Here the expectation operator
\mathrm{E}_{p}
is taken over the randomness of both
X_{n}
and
\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}
. Recall that
X_{n}
is independent of
\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}
.

Similar assumptions to (
1
) and (
2
) are imposed for general
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
as follows. When
n-k
is large enough, for each
k\leq i\leq n
,

\text{KL-loss}(\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[D(p||\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})\right]\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(n-k)^{\beta_{1}}}

(3)

and

\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\left(\frac{1}{(n-k+1)}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(n-k+1)^{\beta_{2}}}.

(4)

A typical loss measure for a density estimator is the mean-integrated squared error (MISE), defined as (see, e.g.,
[
18
, Chap. 2]
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{w}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\int(\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}(x_{n})-p(x_{n}))^{2}d\mu(x_{n})\right]=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}-p\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right].

(5)

The following lemma connects the MISE measure with the bounds in (
1
)–(
4
). The proof is given in the Appendix.

Lemma II.1
.

Suppose that there exist
\overline{\zeta},\underline{\zeta}
such that

0<\underline{\zeta}\leq p(x),\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}(x)\leq\overline{\zeta}<\infty,~\forall x\in\mathrm{supp}(p).

(6)

If the estimator achieves

\mathrm{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{w}_{n})\leq\frac{C_{3}}{w^{\beta_{3}}},

(7)

for all
w
large enough and for some constants
0<\beta_{3},C_{3}<\infty
, then
(
1
)-(
4
)
are satisfied with

C_{1}=\frac{C_{3}}{\underline{\zeta}},\quad C_{2}=\frac{\overline{\zeta}rC_{3}}{\underline{\zeta}^{2}},\quad\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\beta_{3}

where

r:=\left(\frac{\log(\underline{\zeta}/\overline{\zeta})}{(\underline{\zeta}/\overline{\zeta})-1}\right)^{2}.

(8)

In the following, for any positive functions
g(w),h(w)
, the notation
h(w)=O(g(w))
means that
\frac{h(w)}{g(w)}\xrightarrow{w\to\infty}L<\infty
, and
h(w)=\Omega(g(w))
means that
\frac{h(w)}{g(w)}\xrightarrow{w\to\infty}L>0
.

Corollary II.1.1
.

Suppose that (
6
) is satisfied with

\overline{\zeta}=\overline{\zeta}_{w}=O(w^{\overline{\beta}}),\quad\underline{\zeta}=\underline{\zeta}_{w}=\Omega(w^{-\underline{\beta}})

such that

\underline{\beta}<\beta_{3}/2,\quad\overline{\beta}<\beta_{3}-2\underline{\beta}.

Suppose that the estimator still achieves (
7
). Then, (
1
)–(
4
) are still satisfied, with

\beta_{1}=\beta_{3}-\underline{\beta},\quad\beta_{2}=\beta_{3}-2\underline{\beta}-\overline{\beta}-\varrho

where
\varrho>0
is a small constant such that
\beta_{2}
is still positive.

The proof of this corollary is given in the Appendix.

An example of a density estimator that satisfies (
1
)–(
4
) (under condition (
6
) and when the density satisfies some smoothness condition) is the
kernel
density estimator (KDE).

Example II.1

(Kernel Density Estimator (KDE))
.

Suppose
d=1
and the dominating measure
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Given observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{w}
, the kernel density estimator (KDE) is defined as

\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}(x_{n})=\frac{1}{wh}\sum_{j=n-w}^{n-1}K\left(\frac{x_{n}-X_{j}}{h}\right)

(9)

where
K(\cdot)\geq 0
is a kernel function and
h>0
is a smoothing parameter.
Define the
\gamma
-Hölder density class as

{\cal H}_{\gamma}:=\left\{p:\int p(x)dx=1,\exists L>0,\left|p^{(\ell)}(x_{1})-p^{(\ell)}(x_{2})\right|\leq L\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\gamma-\ell},~\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathrm{supp}(p)\right\}.

Here
\gamma>0
and
\ell=\left\lfloor\gamma\right\rfloor
. Further, if the kernel function
K(\cdot)
satisfies

\int K(u)du=1,\quad\int u^{j}K(u)du=0,~j=1,\ldots,\ell.

(10)

Then, as shown in
[
19
]
, with a properly chosen
h=h^{w}
, the KDE satisfies

\sup_{p\in{\cal H}_{\gamma}}\mathrm{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{w}_{n})=O(w^{-\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+1}}).

Therefore, if the condition (
6
) is further satisfied, from Lemma
II.1
, conditions (
1
)–(
4
) are satisfied with

\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+1}.

(11)

For the case where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a product kernel can be used to estimate the density, and the corresponding KDE is

\widehat{p}^{w}_{n}(\bm{x}_{n})=\frac{1}{w\prod_{i=1}^{d}h^{(i)}}\sum_{j=n-w}^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}K\left(\frac{x_{n}^{(i)}-X_{j}^{(i)}}{h^{(i)}}\right)

where
x^{(i)},~i=1,\dots,d
is the
i
-th element of a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\bm{h}
is a vector for smoothing parameter.
With a properly chosen
\bm{h}
, it can be shown that
[
20
]
:

\sup_{p\in{\cal H}_{\gamma}}\mathrm{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{w}_{n})=O(w^{-\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+d}}).

Therefore, if the condition (
6
) is further satisfied, we have

\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma+d}.

(12)

We note that the actual choices of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
do not affect the first-order asymptotic optimality results given in Thm
III.3
and Thm
IV.6
.

III
QCD with NGLR-CuSum Test

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n},\dots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a sequence of independent random variables (or vectors), and let
\nu
be a change-point. Assume that
X_{1},\dots,X_{\nu-1}
all have density
p_{0}
with respect to some dominating measure
\mu
. Furthermore, assume that
X_{\nu},X_{\nu+1},\dots
have densities
p_{1}
also with respect to
\mu
. Here
p_{0}
is assumed to be completely known. Regarding
p_{1}
, we only assume that (
3
) and (
4
) are satisfied. Let
({\cal F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the filtration, with
{\cal F}_{0}=\{\Omega,\varnothing\}
and
{\cal F}_{n}=\sigma\left\{X_{\ell},1\leq\ell\leq n\right\}
being the sigma-algebra generated by the set of
n
observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. Furthermore, let
{\cal F}_{\infty}=\sigma(X_{1},X_{2},\dots)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
denote the probability measure on the entire sequence of observations when the change-point is
\nu
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{\nu}
denote the corresponding expectation.
The change-time
\nu
is assumed to be unknown but deterministic. The problem is to detect the change quickly, while controlling the false alarm rate. Let
\tau
be a stopping time
[
21
]
defined on the observation sequence associated with the detection rule, i.e.
\tau
is the time at which we stop taking observations and declare that the change has occurred.

We employ standard notations as follows:

\displaystyle o(x)~\text{as}~x\to x_{0}~\text{for a function $h(x)\geq 0$ such that}~\limsup_{x\to x_{0}}\left|\frac{h(x)}{x}\right|=0

\displaystyle\omega(x)~\text{as}~x\to x_{0}~\text{for a function $h(x)\geq 0$ such that}~\liminf_{x\to x_{0}}\left|\frac{h(x)}{x}\right|=\infty

\displaystyle O(x)~\text{as}~x\to x_{0}~\text{for a function $h(x)\geq 0$ such that}~\limsup_{x\to x_{0}}\left|\frac{h(x)}{x}\right|<\infty

\displaystyle\Theta(x)~\text{as}~x\to x_{0}~\text{for a function $h(x)\geq 0$ such that}~\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\left|\frac{h(x)}{x}\right|=L\in(0,\infty)

and
A_{\alpha}\sim B_{\alpha}
is equivalent to
A_{\alpha}=B_{\alpha}(1+o(1))
. If not explicitly specified,
x\to x_{0}
refers to
\alpha\to 0
or
b\to\infty
.

III-A

QCD Problem Formulation and Classical Results

When
p_{1}
is known, Lorden
[
4
]
proposed solving the following optimization problem to find the best stopping time
\tau
:

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right)

(13)

where

\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right):=\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\esssup\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\left(\tau-\nu+1\right)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

(14)

characterizes the worst-case delay, and the constraint set is

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}:=\left\{\tau:\mathrm{FAR}\left(\tau\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

(15)

with
\mathrm{FAR}\left(\tau\right):=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\tau\right]}
,
which guarantees that the false alarm rate of the algorithm does not exceed
\alpha
. Here,
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\cdot\right]
is the expectation operator when the change never happens, and
(\cdot)^{+}:=\max\{0,\cdot\}
.

Lorden also showed that Page’s Cumulative Sum (CuSum) algorithm
[
22
]
whose test statistic is given by:

W(n)=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}=\left(W(n-1)\right)^{+}+\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{n})}{p_{0}(X_{n})}

solves the problem in (
13
) asymptotically as
\alpha\to 0
.
The CuSum stopping rule is given by:

\tau_{\text{Page}}\left(b\right):=\inf\{n:W(n)\geq b\}.

(16)

It was shown by Moustakides
[
23
]
that the CuSum test is exactly optimal for the problem in (
13
) with some threshold
b_{\alpha}
that meets the false alarm constraint exactly, where
b_{\alpha}\sim\left|\log\alpha\right|
. Thus, we have the first-order asymptotic approximation as:

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right)=\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau_{\text{Page}}\left(b_{\alpha}\right)\right)\sim\frac{\left|\log\alpha\right|}{I}

(17)

as
\alpha\to 0
. Here we define

I:=D(p_{1}||p_{0}).

When the post-change distribution has parametric uncertainties, Lai
[
5
]
generalized this performance guarantee with the following assumptions.
Let
\theta\in\Theta
be the post-change parameter, and denote the post-change density as
p_{1}^{\theta}
. Define
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}_{\nu}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_{\nu}
to be the probability and expectation operator on the sequence, respectively, when the true post-change density is
p_{1}^{\theta}
. For fixed
\theta\in\Theta
, define the worst-case average detection delay as:

\mathrm{WADD}^{\theta}\left(\tau\right):=\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\esssup\mathbb{E}_{\nu}^{\theta}\left[\left(\tau-\nu+1\right)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right].

(18)

Under parametric uncertainty, the goal is to find a test that belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
(see (
15
) and achieves

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}^{\theta}\left(\tau\right)

(19)

for every
\theta\in\Theta
.

Define
I^{\theta}:=D(p_{1}^{\theta}||p_{0})
. Suppose that
p_{0}
and
p_{1}^{\theta}
satisfy

\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\theta}\left\{\max_{t\leq n}\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+t}\log\frac{p_{1}^{\theta}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}\geq(1+\delta)nI^{\theta}\right\}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

(20)

for any
\delta>0
, and

\sup_{t\geq\nu}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\theta}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n}\log\frac{p_{1}^{\theta}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}\leq(1-\delta)nI^{\theta}\right\}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

(21)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
. Also, suppose that the window size
m_{\alpha}
satisfies

\liminf m_{\alpha}/\left|\log\alpha\right|>\frac{1}{I^{\theta}}\quad\text{ and }\log m_{\alpha}=o(\left|\log\alpha\right|).

Then, under some smoothness conditions
[
5
]
, the window-limited GLR-CuSum test:

\tilde{\tau}_{\text{GLR}}\left(b\right):=\inf\left\{n\geq 1:\max_{(n-m_{\alpha})^{+}<k\leq{n}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p_{1}^{\theta}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}\geq b\right\}

(22)

with test threshold
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|(1+o(1))
solves the problem in (
19
) asymptotically as
\alpha\to 0
, for every
\theta\in\Theta
. The asymptotic performance is

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}^{\theta}\left(\tau\right)\sim\mathrm{WADD}^{\theta}\left(\tilde{\tau}_{\text{GLR}}\left(b_{\alpha}\right)\right)\sim\frac{\left|\log\alpha\right|}{I^{\theta}}.

(23)

III-B

Non-parametric GLR CuSum Test

For the case when
p_{1}
is unknown, we define the non-parametric GLR statistic as

\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}=\log\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})},\ \forall k\leq i\leq n.

(24)

We remind readers of the definition of
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
from Section
II
. The non-parametric generalized likelihood ratio (NGLR) CuSum stopping rule is defined as

\widehat{\tau}(b):=\inf\left\{n>1:\max_{(n-m_{b})^{+}<k\leq n-1}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}\geq b\right\}.

(25)

Here the window size
m_{b}
is designed to satisfy

\liminf m_{b}/b\geq\frac{\eta}{I}

(26)

where
\eta>1
is an arbitrary constant.

In Lemma
III.1
, we show that
\widehat{\tau}
with a properly chosen density estimator and threshold
b=b_{\alpha}
satisfies the false alarm constraint asymptotically in (
15
).
In Lemma
III.2
, we establish an asymptotic upper bound on
\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b)\right)
. The proofs of the lemmas are given in the Appendix. Finally, in Theorem
III.3
, we combine the lemmas and establish the first-order asymptotic optimality of the NGLR-CuSum test.

Lemma III.1
.

Suppose that the estimator is chosen such that
\exists~\varsigma>0
,

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\max_{(k,n):k\leq n\leq k+m_{b}}\prod_{i=k}^{n}\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}\right]\leq b^{\varsigma}

(27)

for any large enough
b
.
Let
b_{\alpha}
satisfy

b_{\alpha}-\varsigma\log b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|+\log 8.

(28)

Then,

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})\right]\geq\alpha^{-1}(1+o(1)).

We will elaborate on condition (
27
) in Section
V-A
.
Intuitively, this condition is satisfied when the density estimator converges to the true density fast enough (
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
-almost surely). Since the pre-change distribution is known, we can numerically verify condition (
27
) with the chosen window size and density estimator.

Lemma III.2
.

Suppose
b
is large enough such that condition (
26
) holds. Suppose that (
3
) and (
4
) hold.
Further, suppose (
21
) holds for the true log-likelihood ratio.
Then,

\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b)\right)\leq\frac{b}{I}(1+o(1)),~\text{as }b\to\infty.

Theorem III.3
.

Suppose that conditions (
20
) and (
21
) hold for the true log-likelihood ratio, and suppose that the window size satisfies (
26
).
Suppose that (
27
) is satisfied for the chosen estimator. Let
b_{\alpha}
be so selected according to equation (
28
) such that

\mathrm{FAR}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})\right)\leq\alpha(1+o(1))
, where also
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|(1+o(1))
. Then
\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})
solves the problem in (
13
) asymptotically as
\alpha\to 0
, and

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right)\sim\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}\left(b_{\alpha}\right)\right)\sim\frac{\left|\log\alpha\right|}{I}.

Proof of Theorem
III.3
.

The asymptotic lower bound on the delay follows from (
21
) using
[
5
, Thm. 1]
. The asymptotic optimality of
\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})
follows from Lemma
III.1
and Lemma
III.2
.
∎

IV
QCD with NWLA CuSum Test

Define the non-parametric window-limited adaptively-estimated log-likelihood ratio as

\widehat{Z}^{w}_{n}=\log\frac{\widehat{p}_{n}^{w}(X_{n})}{p_{0}(X_{n})},\ \forall n>w

(29)

where
\widehat{p}_{n}^{w}
is the output of the density estimator given input
X^{[n-w,n-1]}
. Note that
\widehat{Z}^{w}_{n}
is independent of
{\cal F}_{n-w-1}
.
Define the non-parametric window-limited adaptive (NWLA) CuSum statistic as:

\overline{W}^{w}(n)=\left(\overline{W}^{w}(n-1)\right)^{+}+\widehat{Z}^{w}_{n},\quad n>w

(30)

and
\overline{W}^{w}(1)=\dots=\overline{W}^{w}(w)=0
.
The corresponding stopping rule is

\overline{\tau}(b):=\inf\left\{n>w:\overline{W}^{w}(n)\geq b\right\}.

(31)

Here
b=b_{\alpha}>0
is a threshold depending on the false alarm rate

Lemma IV.3

Define

U_{n}=U_{n-1}+\widehat{Z}^{w}_{n},\quad\forall n>w

Proof of Lemma
IV.3

The proof is similar to

U_{w}=0=\overline{W}(w)

Lemma IV.4

If

Proof of Lemma
IV.4

First, for any

\sum_{i=w+1}^{w+wk}\widehat{Z}^{w}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{w}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\widehat{Z}^{w}_{w\ell+j}

Note that

Comparison between NGLR-CuSum Test and NWLA-CuSum Test

We now compare the performance between the NGLR-CuSum test and the parallel-NWLA-CuSum test. First, we compare the computational complexity of both tests if the KDE is used for density estimation. For the NGLR-CuSum test, at each input observation
X_{n}
, for all hypothesized change-points
k\in((n-m)^{+},n-1]
(where
m
is the window size), the pair-wise kernel value

for each pair of

is calculated and a LOO kernel estimate is evaluated at each point

) is compared to the given threshold. Thus, the computation complexity of the NGLR-CuSum test at each time is

w
) is compared to the given threshold. Therefore, the computation complexity of the parallel-NWLA-CuSum test is
\Theta((n\wedge W_{\text{max}})^{2})
. Also note that in practice,

VI
Conclusion

We studied a window-limited non-parametric generalized likelihood ratio (NGLR) CuSum test and a non-parametric window-limited adaptive (NWLA) CuSum test for QCD. Both tests do not assume any explicit knowledge of the post-change distribution, and do not require post-change training samples ahead of time. We characterized a generic class of density estimators that enable detection. For both tests, we provided a way to set the test thresholds to meet false alarm constraints, and we showed that the tests are first-order asymptotically optimal with the selected thresholds, as the false alarm rate goes to zero.
We validated our analysis through Monte-Carlo simulations, in which we compared both tests with baseline tests that have distributional knowledge.

n>w
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\left|(R_{n+1}-(n+1))-(R_{n}-n)\right|\Big|{\cal F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\left|R_{n+1}-R_{n}-1\right|\Big|{\cal F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[R_{n+1}|{\cal F}_{n}\right]+(R_{n}+1)

\displaystyle=2(R_{n}+1)

\displaystyle\leq 2(e^{b}+1)

almost surely on the event
\{\overline{\tau}_{R}(b)>n\}
. Therefore, we can apply the optional sampling theorem and obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[R_{\overline{\tau}_{R}(b)}-\overline{\tau}_{R}(b)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[R_{w+1}-(w+1)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[e^{\widehat{Z}^{w}_{w+1}}\right]-(w+1)=-w,

where
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[e^{\widehat{Z}^{w}_{w+1}}\right]=1
because
\widehat{p}^{w}_{w+1}
is a density given
{\cal F}_{w}
. Finally, we arrive at

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\overline{\tau}(b)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\overline{\tau}_{R}(b)\right]=w+\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[R_{\overline{\tau}_{R}(b)}\right]\geq e^{b}.\qed

Proof of Lemma
IV.2
.

The proof is similar to
[
7
, Lemma 4]
. Define a helping stopping time

\overline{\tau}_{\nu}(b):=\inf\{n\geq\nu+w:\overline{W}_{\nu}(n)\geq b\}

where

\overline{W}_{\nu}(n)=\left(\overline{W}_{\nu}(n-1)\right)^{+}+\widehat{Z}^{w}_{n},\quad n\geq\nu+w

with
\overline{W}_{\nu}(n)=0,\forall n<\nu+w
. Note that
\overline{W}(\nu+w)\geq\overline{W}_{\nu}(\nu+w)
. Now, if
\overline{W}(k)\geq\overline{W}_{\nu}(k)
, we have

\overline{W}(k+1)=\left(\overline{W}(k)\right)^{+}+\widehat{Z}^{w}_{k+1}\geq\left(\overline{W}_{\nu}(k)\right)^{+}+\widehat{Z}^{w}_{k+1}=\overline{W}_{\nu}(k+1)

as long as
\overline{W}(k)<b
. Thus, by induction,

\overline{W}(n)\geq\overline{W}_{\nu}(n),~\forall n\geq\nu+w

on the event
\left\{\overline{W}(n)<b\right\}
, which implies that
\overline{\tau}(b)\leq\overline{\tau}_{\nu}(b)
almost surely under
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
. In the remainder of the proof, we omit “
(b)
” in the descriptions of the stopping times for notational brevity.

Since
\overline{\tau}-\nu+1=w+\left(\overline{\tau}-\nu-w+1\right)\leq w+(\overline{\tau}-\nu-w+1)^{+}
, we have

(\overline{\tau}-\nu+1)^{+}\leq w+(\overline{\tau}-\nu-w+1)^{+}\quad\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\text{--}a.s.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[(\overline{\tau}-\nu+1)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq w+\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[(\overline{\tau}-\nu-w+1)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle=w+\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[(\overline{\tau}-\nu-w+1)^{+}|X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1},{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}w+\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[(\overline{\tau}_{\nu}-\nu-w+1)^{+}|X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1},{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{=}}w+\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\overline{\tau}_{\nu}-\nu-w+1|X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1},{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

where
(*)
holds because
\overline{\tau}\leq\overline{\tau}_{\nu}
almost surely (under
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
), and
(**)
holds because
\overline{\tau}_{\nu}\geq\nu+w-1\geq 0
almost surely (under
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
).

Now,
\forall\nu\geq 1
, given the information of
X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1}
, the event
\left\{\overline{\tau}_{\nu}\geq\nu+w\right\}
is independent of
{\cal F}_{\nu-1}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\esssup\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\overline{\tau}_{\nu}-\nu-w+1|X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1},{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\overline{\tau}_{\nu}-\nu-w+1|X_{\nu},\dots,X_{\nu+w-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\overline{\tau}_{1}-w|X_{1},\dots,X_{w}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\overline{\tau}-w\right]

The last line holds because
\overline{\tau}_{1}=\overline{\tau}
almost surely (under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
). The proof is now complete.
∎

Proof of Lemma
IV.5
.

The proof consists of two parts. In the first part, we use a similar technique as in
[
26
, Thm 1.1]
to obtain an extension of Wald’s identity to the case where the samples are
w
-dependent. In the second part, we upper bound the overshoot using results from renewal theory. For notational brevity, we omit the dependence on
w
and write
\widehat{Z}^{w}_{i}=\widehat{Z}_{i}
.

Define

Y_{i}:=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{i}-(i-w)\widehat{I}|{\cal F}_{i-w}\right],~\forall i\geq w

and note that
Y_{w}=0
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[Y_{i+1}|{\cal F}_{i-w}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{i+1}-(i+1-w)\widehat{I}|{\cal F}_{i+1-w}\right]|{\cal F}_{i-w}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{i}+\widehat{Z}_{i+1}-(i-w)\widehat{I}-\widehat{I}|{\cal F}_{i-w}\right]

\displaystyle=Y_{i}+\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\widehat{Z}_{i+1}-\widehat{I}|{\cal F}_{i-w}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}Y_{i}+\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\widehat{Z}_{i+1}-\widehat{I}\right]

\displaystyle=Y_{i},~\forall i>w,~\mathbb{P}_{1}\text{--}a.s.

where
(*)
follows from independence between
\widehat{Z}^{w}_{i+1}
and
{\cal F}_{i-w}
. This implies that
\left\{(Y_{i},{\cal F}_{i-w})\right\}_{i\geq w}
is a martingale. Therefore, for any finite
k>w
,
\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}\leq k<\infty
, and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}-\widehat{I}(\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}-w)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\tau_{u}=m\right\}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\min\left\{m,k\right\}}-\widehat{I}(\min\left\{m,k\right\}-w)|{\cal F}_{\min\left\{m,k\right\}-w}\right]\big|\tau_{u}=m\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\tau_{u}=m\right\}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[Y_{\min\left\{m,k\right\}}|\tau_{u}=m\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[Y_{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[Y_{w}\right]=0

where
(*)
follows from optional sampling theorem. This implies that

\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\right]=\widehat{I}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}\right]-w).

(58)

Note that
\tau_{u}<\infty
with probability 1 under
\mathbb{P}_{1}
by Lemma
IV.4
.
For
i>w
, let
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}:=\max\{0,\widehat{Z}_{i}\}
and
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}:=-\min\{0,\widehat{Z}_{i}\}
. Note that
\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+},\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}\geq 0
,
\widehat{Z}_{i}=\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}
, and
U_{n}=\sum_{i=w+1}^{n}\left(\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}-\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}\right),\forall n>w
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}\right]-\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=w+1}^{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=w+1}^{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\tau_{u}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{+}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\tau_{u}}\widehat{Z}_{i}^{-}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}}\right]

where
(i)
follows from the monotone convergence theorem, and
(ii)
is due to the fact that
\tau_{u}<\infty
with probability 1. Also by the monotone convergence theorem,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\lim_{k\to\infty}\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right].

Thus, taking the limit of
k
on both sides of (
58
),

\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}}\right]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}}\right]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{I}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\min\left\{\tau_{u},k\right\}\right]-w)=\widehat{I}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]-w).

(59)

Now, denote

L_{i}:=\log\frac{\widehat{p}_{i}^{w}(X_{i})}{p_{1}(X_{i})},\quad\forall i>w.

By definition we have
\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\widehat{Z}_{i}-L_{i}\right]=I,\forall i>w
. The proof of (
59
) is also applicable to
L^{2}_{\tau_{u}}
, which gives us

\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\tau_{u}}L^{2}_{i}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[L^{2}_{w+1}\right](\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]-w).

(60)

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}}-b\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}-1}-b+\widehat{Z}_{\tau_{u}}\right]<\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\widehat{Z}_{\tau_{u}}\right]=I+\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[L_{\tau_{u}}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}I+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[L_{\tau_{u}}^{2}\right]}\leq I+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\sum_{i=w+1}^{\tau_{u}}L_{i}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{=}}I+\sqrt{\left(\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]-w\right)\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[L_{w+1}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(iii)}}{{\leq}}I+\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{w^{\beta_{2}}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]-w\right)}

(61)

for sufficiently large
w
. Here
(i)
follows from Jensen’s inequality,
(ii)
follows from (
60
), and
(iii)
follows from assumption (
2
). Denote
c_{w}:=C_{2}w^{-\beta_{2}}
and
x:=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]-w
. The goal below is to get an upper bound for
x
. Combining (
Proof of Lemma
.
) with (
59
), we obtain

\sqrt{c_{w}x}+I\geq\widehat{I}x-b

which implies that

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2(b+I)\widehat{I}+c_{w})+\sqrt{(2(b+I)\widehat{I}+c_{w})^{2}-4\widehat{I}^{2}(b+I)^{2}}}{2\widehat{I}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(b+I)\widehat{I}+c_{w}}{\widehat{I}^{2}}.

Plugging this bound into (
Proof of Lemma
.
) gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}}-b\right]

\displaystyle\leq I+\sqrt{\frac{2(b+I)c_{w}\widehat{I}+c_{w}^{2}}{\widehat{I}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq I+\frac{2\sqrt{(b+I)c_{w}\widehat{I}}+\sqrt{2}c_{w}}{\widehat{I}}

where in the last inequality we use the fact that
\sqrt{u+v}\leq\sqrt{2u}+\sqrt{2v}
for any
u,v>0
. Therefore, combining with (
59
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}\right]

\displaystyle=w+\widehat{I}^{-1}\left(b+\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[U_{\tau_{u}}-b\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq w+\widehat{I}^{-1}(b+I)+\left(4C_{2}(b+I)w^{-\beta_{2}}\widehat{I}^{-3}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{2}\widehat{I}^{-2}C_{2}w^{-\beta_{2}}.

The proof is now complete since
\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\overline{\tau}(b)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\tau_{u}(b)\right]
by Lemma
IV.3
. ∎

On the monophonic convexity in complementary prisms
Neethu P. K, Ullas Chandran S. V, Julliano R. Nascimento

Abstract
A set S S of vertices of a graph G G is monophonic convex if S S contains all the vertices belonging to any induced path connecting two vertices of S S . The cardinality of a maximum proper monophonic convex set of G G is called the monophonic convexity number of G G . The monophonic interval of a set S S of vertices of G G is the set S S together with every vertex belonging to any induced path connecting two vertices of S S . The cardinality of a minimum set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) whose monophonic interval is V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) is called the monophonic number of G G . The monophonic convex hull of a set S S of vertices of G G is the smallest monophonic convex set containing S S in G G . The cardinality of a minimum set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) whose monophonic convex hull is V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) is called the monophonic hull number of G G . The complementary prism G ​ G ¯ G\overline{G} of G G is obtained from the disjoint union of G G and its complement G ¯ \overline{G} by adding the edges of a perfect matching between them. In this work, we determine the monophonic convexity number, the monophonic number, and the monophonic hull number of the complementary prisms of all graphs.

Introduction

A
finite convexity space
is a pair
(V,{\cal C})
consisting of a non-empty finite set
V
and a collection
{\cal C}
of subsets of
V
such that
\emptyset\in{\cal C}
,
V\in{\cal C}
and
{\cal C}
is closed under intersections. The elements of
\mathcal{C}
are called
convex sets
, see
[
19
]
. Different convexities associated with the vertex set of a graph are well-known. The most natural convexities in graphs are path convexities defined by a family of paths
\mathcal{P}
, in a way that a set
S
of vertices of
G
is
\mathcal{P}
-
convex
if
S
contains all vertices of every path of
\mathcal{P}
between vertices of
S
. An extensive survey of different types of path convexities can be found in
[
18
]
. The well-known
geodetic convexity
has
\mathcal{P}
as the family of all shortest paths
[
1
,
2
,
12
]
.

In this paper, we consider the
monophonic convexity
in graphs. In this convexity,
\cal P
is based on the family of induced paths. A
chord
of a path
P:u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
is an edge
u_{i}u_{j}
, with
j\geq i+2
. A
u,v
-path
P
is
monophonic
(
m
-path
, for short) or
induced
if it is a chordless path. A set of vertices
S
of a graph
G
is a
monophonic convex set
(
m
-
convex set
for short) if
S
contains all vertices belonging to any induced path connecting two vertices of
S
. The cardinality of a maximum proper convex set of
G
is called the
monophonic convexity number
(
m
-
convexity number
for short) of
G
, denoted by
\c_{m}(G)
.

The
monophonic interval

J_{G}[u,v]
between
u
and
v
is the set of vertices of all
u,v
-monophonic paths in
G
. For
S\subseteq V(G)
we fix
J_{G}[S]=\bigcup_{{}_{u,v\in S}}J_{G}[u,v]
. Thus, a set
S
is
monophonic convex
if
J_{G}[S]=S
.
A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is called a
monophonic set
(
m
-set, for short) if
J_{G}[S]=V(G)
. The
monophonic number
of
G
, denoted by
\m(G)
, is the cardinality of a minimum monophonic set of
G
.

The
m
-convex hull

[S]_{G}
of a set
S
in
G
is the smallest convex set containing
S
in
G
. The convex hull
[S]_{G}
can be formed from the sequence
J^{p}_{G}[S]
, where
p
is a non-negative integer,
J^{0}_{G}[S]=S
,
J^{1}_{G}[S]=J_{G}[S]
, and
J^{p}_{G}[S]=J_{G}[J_{G}^{p-1}[S]]
, for
p\geq 2
.
A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is called an
m
-hull set
if
[S]_{G}=V(G)
. The
m
-hull number
of
G
, denoted by
\h_{m}(G)
, is the cardinality of a minimum
m
-hull set of
G
.

The monophonic convexity was introduced in 1982, by Jamison
[
13
]
. For additional topics of interest concerning to the monophonic convexity we refer to the reader
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. In particular, Dourado, Protti and Szwarcfiter
[
8
]
proved that the decision problems associated with the monophonic convexity number and the monophonic number are NP-complete on arbitrary graphs. On the other hand, they prove that the monophonic hull number of an arbitrary graph can be computed in polynomial time.

If
G
is a graph and
\overline{G}
its complement, then the
complementary prism

G\overline{G}
of
G
is the graph formed from the disjoint union of
G
and
\overline{G}
by adding the edges of a perfect matching between the corresponding vertices of
G
and
\overline{G}
, see
[
14
,
15
]
. For example,
C_{5}\overline{C}_{5}
is the Petersen graph. The geodesic convexity number of complementary prisms has been investigated recently in
[
3
]
. In
[
3
]
, the authors proved that the decision problem associated to the convexity number is NP-complete even restricted to complementary prisms
G\overline{G}
when both
G
and its complement
\overline{G}
are connected. In the same work, the authors found the geodetic convexity number of
G\overline{G}
when
G
or
\overline{G}
is disconnected. In
[
16
]
, the authors found a formula for the geodesic convexity number of complementary prisms of all trees, a rich subclass of the above-stated class. Other related topics studied recently on complementary prisms include geodetic hull number
[
4
,
7
]
, general position number
[
17
]
, and domination
[
15
]
. To our knowledge, there is no literature on the monophonic convexity number of the complementary prisms. In this paper, we derive formulas for the monophonic convexity number, monophonic number, and monophonic hull number of the complementary prisms of all graphs. In Section
2
we fix the notations, terminologies and present some initial remarks. In Sections
3
,
4
, and
5
we discuss our results on the monophonic convexity number, monophonic number, and monophonic hull number, respectively.

2
Preliminaries

Graphs in this paper are finite, simple, and undirected. For basic graph terminologies, we follow
[
5
]
. Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, the
open neighborhood

N_{G}(v)

is the set of neighbors of
v
, while the
closed neighborhood

N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
. The

degree
of
v
is
deg_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
. A vertex
v
is
simplicial
if
N_{G}(v)
induces a clique. For
S\subseteq V(G)
, we fix
N_{G}[S]=\bigcup_{v\in S}N_{G}[v]
; and
N_{G}(S)=(N_{G}[S])\backslash S
. A vertex
v\in V(G)
is
pendant
if
deg_{G}(v)=1
. The
distance
between two vertices
u
and
v
, denoted
d_{G}(u,v)
, is the length of a shortest path joining them if any; otherwise
d_{G}(u,v)=\infty
. The
eccentricity
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
, denoted
ecc_{G}(v)
, is the maximum distance from
v
to any vertex in
G
. We may simplify the notations above by omitting the index
G
whenever
G
is clear from the context. The
diameter
of a graph
G
, denoted
\diam(G)
, is the maximum eccentricity among the vertices of
G
.
The
order
of a graph
G
is the number of vertices in
G
, denoted by
n(G)
. A connected component
G^{\prime}
in a disconnected graph
G
is called
trivial
if
n(G^{\prime})=1
, and
non-trivial
, otherwise.
The
independent number

\alpha(G)
is the cardinality of a largest set of nonadjacent vertices of
G
. Whereas
the
clique number

\omega(G)
is the cardinality of a largest clique of
G
. For a set
S\subseteq V(G)
,
S^{c}
denotes the compliment of the set
S
in
G
. i.e.,
S^{c}=V(G)\setminus S
.

If
u\in V(G)\cap V(G\overline{G})
, then the unique neighbour of
u
in
V(G\overline{G})\cap V(\overline{G})
is denoted by
\overline{u}
, called the
partner
of
u
in
\overline{G}
. If
S\subseteq V(G)
, then the set of all partners of the vertices from
S
is denoted by
\overline{S}
. For a positive integer
k
, we use the notation
[k]=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.

Some remarks that arise from monophonic convexity definitions can be found in
[
8
]
. The next three are useful for our purposes.

Remark 2.1
(
[
8
]
)

Every graph with a simplicial vertex satisfies
\c_{m}(G)=n-1
.

Remark 2.2
(
[
8
]
)

Every monophonic set or
m
-hull set of a graph
G
must contain all of its simplicial vertices.

Remark 2.3
(
[
8
]
)

For any graph
G
,
\h_{m}(G)\leq\min\{\m(G),\c_{m}(G)+1\}
.

Furthermore, when dealing with monophonic intervals on complementary prisms, the next proposition will be useful.

Proposition 2.4

Let
G
be a graph. If
P:u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
is an induced path in
G
, then
V(P)\cup V(\overline{P})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[u_{1},u_{4}]
.

Proof.

Given that
u_{1},u_{2},\overline{u_{2}},\overline{u_{4}},u_{4}
and
u_{1},\overline{u_{1}},\overline{u_{3}},u_{3},u_{4}
are monophonic paths in
G\overline{G}
, the conclusion is direct.

3
Monophonic convexity number

In this section, we determine the monophonic convexity number of complementary prisms
G\overline{G}
. First, we consider the case when both
G
and
\overline{G}
are connected, in Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
G
be a connected graph such that
\overline{G}
is connected, then
\c_{m}(G\overline{G})=\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
.

Proof.
Let
S
be a monophonic convex set of
G\overline{G}
with
|S|=\c_{m}(G\overline{G})
and fix
S_{G}=S\cap V(G)
and
S_{\overline{G}}=S\cap V(\overline{G})
. Since
n(G)\geq 2
, we have that
|S|\geq 2
. Consider the following three cases.

Case 1 :

S_{G}\neq\emptyset
and
S_{\overline{G}}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, we prove that
|S|=2
.

First, we prove the following claim.

Claim 1:

S_{\overline{G}}=\overline{S_{G}}
. Let
u\in S_{G}
. Choose
v\in S_{G}
such that
\overline{v}\in S_{\overline{G}}
. Let
x\in S_{G}
. Let
\overline{P}
be a
\overline{v},\overline{x}
-monophonic path in
\overline{G}
. Then
\overline{P}
together with the edge
\overline{x}x
is a
x,\overline{v}
-monophonic path containing
\overline{x}
in
G\overline{G}
. So
\overline{x}\in S
. Hence
\overline{S_{G}}\subseteq S_{\overline{G}}
. On the other hand, let
\overline{y}\in S_{\overline{G}}
and
P
be a
u,y
-monophonic path in
G
. Then
P
together with the edge
y\overline{y}
is a
u,\overline{y}
–monophonic path containing
y
in
G\overline{G}
. Hence
y\in S_{G}
. This shows that
S_{\overline{G}}\subseteq\overline{S_{G}}
.

Claim 2:

S_{G}
is a clique or an independent set in
G
. If
|S_{G}|\leq 2
, then there is nothing to prove. So assume that
|S_{G}|\geq 3
. Suppose there exists
u,v\in S_{G}
with
u
and
v
adjacent in
G
. Let
w
be any vertex distinct from
u
and
v
in
G
. Suppose
w
is nonadjacent to both
u
and
v
. Then
\overline{u},\overline{w},\overline{v}
is an monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
containing
\overline{w}
. And hence by Claim 1,
w,\overline{w}\in S
. Also if
w
is adjacent to exactly one of
u
or
v
, say
u
, then
u,w,\overline{w},\overline{v}
is an
u,\overline{v}
-monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
containing both
w
and
\overline{w}
. Hence
w,\overline{w}\in S
. The above observations show that

[N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)]^{c}\subseteq S_{G}.

(1)

Moreover
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)\neq\emptyset
, since
S\neq V(G\overline{G})
. Let
w\in N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
. We first show that any vertex
z\in S_{G}
is adjacent to both
u
and
v
. This in turn implies that
S_{G}
is a clique, since
u
and
v
are arbitrary adjacent vertices in
S_{G}
. Suppose
z
is non adjacent to
u
. First consider the case
zw\notin E(G)
. Then
v,w,\overline{w},\overline{z}
is an
m
-path connecting
v
and
\overline{z}
. This shows that
V(G\overline{G})=S
. This is not possible as
S
is a proper subset of
G
. Thus
zw\in E(G)
. In this case
z,w,u,\overline{u}
is an
m
-path connecting
z
and
\overline{u}
containing
w
, and so
w\in S_{G}
. This again shows that
S=V(G\overline{G})
. Which is not possible. Thus
uz\in E(G)
and
vz\in E(G)
. Hence
S_{G}
must be a clique in
G
and
S_{\overline{G}}
is an independent set in
\overline{G}
.

Now, if
S_{G}
contains two nonadjacent vertices
u
and
v
, then replacing
G
by
\overline{G}
in the above arguments we get that
S_{\overline{G}}
is a clique.

Note that an independent set in a connected graph is
m
-convex only if it contains exactly one vertex. Thus in this case
|S|=2
.

Case 2:

S_{\overline{G}}=\emptyset
. Let
u,v\in S_{G}
. If
uv\notin E(G)
, then
u,\overline{u},\overline{v},v
is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
. Since
S_{\overline{G}}=\emptyset
,
S_{G}
must be a clique in
G
. Hence
|S|\leq\omega(G)
.

Case 3:

S_{G}=\emptyset
. In this case, replacing
G
by
\overline{G}
in Case 1, we get that
|S|\leq\omega(\overline{G})=\alpha(G)
.

\square

Next, we find the monophonic convexity number of disconnected graphs.

Theorem 3.2

Let
G
be a disconnected graph with components
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in[r]}
, and
k
be the order of a minimum component of
G
. Then

\c_{m}(G\overline{G})=\begin{cases}2n(G)-1\hskip 56.9055pt;$ if $k=1\,,\\
\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)+2-(\min_{i\in[r]}\{\alpha(G_{i})\})\hskip 8.5359pt;$ otherwise$\,.\end{cases}

Proof.
If
k=1
, then the single vertex
v
in a minimum component of
G
will be a simplicial vertex of
G\overline{G}
. Hence
V(G\overline{G})\setminus\{v\}
is a convex set of
G\overline{G}
and so
\c_{m}(G\overline{G})=2n(G)-1
. Now suppose that
k\geq 2
. Let
S
be a monophonic convex set of
G\overline{G}
with
|S|=\c_{m}(G\overline{G})
. Fix
S_{G}=S\cap V(G)
and
S_{\overline{G}}=S\cap V(\overline{G})
. Since
k\geq 2
, we have
|S|\geq 2
. Consider the following cases.

Case 1:

S_{\overline{G}}=\emptyset
. Let
u,v\in S_{G}
. If
uv\notin E(G)
, then
u,\overline{u},\overline{v},v
is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
. This shows that
\overline{u},\overline{v}\in S
, which is impossible. Thus
S_{G}
must be a clique in
G
. Hence
|S|\leq\omega(G)
.

Case 2:

S_{G}=\emptyset
. In this case, replacing
G
by
\overline{G}
in Case 1, we get
|S|\leq\alpha(G)
.

Case 3:

S_{G}\neq\emptyset
and
S_{\overline{G}}\neq\emptyset
. Let
u\in S_{G}
and
\overline{v}\in S_{\overline{G}}
. We first prove the following two claims.

Claim 1:
If
u
and
v
are in the same component of
G
, say in
G_{1}
, then

\overline{S_{G}\cap G_{1}}=S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{1}}.

(2)

Let
x\in S_{G}\cap G_{1}
. Let
\overline{P}
be a
\overline{v},\overline{x}
-monophonic path in
\overline{G}
. Then
\overline{P}
together with the edge
\overline{x}x
is a
x,\overline{v}
-monophonic path containing
\overline{x}
in
G\overline{G}
. Hence
\overline{x}\in S
and so
\overline{S_{G}\cap G_{1}}\subseteq S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{1}}
. On the other hand, let
\overline{y}\in S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{1}}
and let
P
be a
u,y
-monophonic path in
G_{1}
. Then
P
together with the edge
y\overline{y}
is a
u,\overline{y}
-monophonic path containing
y
in
G\overline{G}
. Hence
y\in S_{G}
. This shows that
S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{1}}\subseteq\overline{S_{G}\cap G_{1}}
.

Claim 2:
For any component,
G_{i}
of
G
, with
S_{G}\cap G_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{i}}\neq\emptyset
,

{\rm either\ }|S_{G}\cap G_{i}|=1{\rm\ or\ }|S_{\overline{G_{i}}}\cap\overline{G_{i}}|=1.

(3)

Suppose that there exist distinct vertices
x_{1},x_{2}
in
S_{G}\cap G_{i}
and distinct vertices
\overline{y_{1}}
and
\overline{y_{2}}
in
S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{i}}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
i=1
; and also we may assume that
x_{1}\neq y_{1}
. If
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
are adjacent vertices in
G
, then
V(\overline{G})\setminus V(\overline{G_{1}})\subseteq S_{\overline{G}}
. Since
\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{1}}\in S_{\overline{G}}
by Claim 1 and so
V(\overline{G})\setminus V(\overline{G_{1}})\subseteq J_{\overline{G}}[\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{1}}]
. Since the order of any component is greater than 2, we get
V(\overline{G})\subseteq S
. Now recall that for any pair of adjacent vertices
z
and
w
in
V(G_{i})
, with
i\in[r]
,
\overline{z},z,w,\overline{w}
is an
m
-path. Since for any
z\in V(G_{i})
,
\deg_{G}(z)\geq 1
, for any

,
V(G_{i})\subseteq S
as
V(\overline{G_{i}})\subseteq S
. Hence
V(G\overline{G})\subseteq S
. This is not possible, as
S
is a proper subset of
G\overline{G}
. Hence
S_{G}\cap G_{1}
must be independent. But then

cannot be convex, since
G_{1}
is connected. Hence the claim follows.

In the following, we prove that
|S_{G}|=1
. Assume the contrary, that there exist two distinct vertices
u_{1},u_{2}\in S_{G}
. Suppose first that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are in the same component of
G
, say in
G_{1}
. Then by Claim 1 and Claim 2, we have that
S_{\overline{G}}\cap V(\overline{G_{1}})=\emptyset
. Choose
\overline{x}\in S_{\overline{G}}
. Then
\overline{x}\notin\overline{G_{1}}
and
u_{1},\overline{u_{1}},\overline{x}
is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
. Thus this case is not possible as
S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{1}}=\emptyset
. Thus
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
must be from different components, say
u_{1}\in G_{1}
and
u_{2}\in G_{2}
. Let
x
be any vertex in
G_{2}
distinct from
u_{2}
and let
P
be a monophonic path connecting
x
and
u_{2}
. Then the path
u_{1},\overline{u_{1}},\overline{x},x
together with the path

is a monophonic path connecting
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
containing
x
and
\overline{x}
. Hence
x,\overline{x}\in S
. Also by Claim 1,
\overline{u_{2}}\in S
. This contradicts Claim 2. Thus
|S_{G}|=1
.

Claim 3:

S_{\overline{G}}
is a clique. If possible suppose that
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in S_{\overline{G}}
, for some
i\in[r]
with
x
and

adjacent in
G
. Then the path
\overline{x},x,y,\overline{y}
is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
. Thus

. This is impossible by Claims 1 and Claim 2. Thus

is a clique.

Let
S_{G}=\{u\}
and without loss of generality we may assume that
u\in V(G_{1})
. Also, let

. Then by Claim 1 and Claim 2,
|S_{\overline{G}}\cap\overline{G_{i}}|=1
. Thus
|S|\leq\alpha(G)+2-(\alpha(G_{i}))
. This shows that
|S|\leq\alpha(G)+2-l
.

Finally, we show that
G\overline{G}
contains a
m
-convex set of order
\alpha(G)+2-l
.
For that, let
I
be an independent set in
G
with
|I|=\alpha(G)
. Choose a vertex
u
in
I
such that
u\in V(G_{i})
and
\alpha(G_{i})=l
. Consider the set
H=\{u,\overline{u}\}\cup C
, where
C=\overline{I}\setminus V(\overline{G_{i}})
. Then
|H|=\alpha(G)+2-l
. We claim that
H
is a
m
-convex set of
G\overline{G}
. We remark that
\overline{I}
is
m
-convex since it is a clique in
G\overline{G}
. Now let
\overline{x}
be a vertex in
\overline{I}
which is distinct from
\overline{u}
. Then
x
and
u
are in different components of
G
and
u,\overline{u},\overline{x}
is the unique
m
-path connecting
u
and
\overline{x}
. This shows that the set

must be
m
-convex in
G\overline{G}
.
This in turn implies that
\c_{m}(G\overline{G})=\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)+2-l\}
.

\square

4
Monophonic number

In this section, we are concerned with the monophonic number of complementary prisms. As done in Section
3
, the results are separated according to the connectedness of
G
. We begin when
G
is a disconnected graph.

Theorem 4.1

Let
G
be a disconnected graph with
r\geq 2
connected components. Then
\m(G\overline{G})=r
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r}
be the components of
G
.
Consider
S=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{r}\}
such that
v_{i}
is any vertex of
V(G_{i})
, for every
i\in[r]
. We show that
S
is a monophonic set. Since
G_{i}
is connected, there is a monophonic path
P:v_{i},\dots,u
in
G_{i}
, for every
u\in V(G_{i})
. (
P
can be the shortest path between
v_{i}
and
u
, for instance). Then,
P
together with the edges
u\bar{u}
,
\bar{u}\bar{v}_{j}
,
\bar{v}_{j}v_{j}
is a
v_{i},v_{j}
-monophonic path containing
\{u,\overline{u}\}
, for every
u\in V(G_{i})
, for every
1\leq i<j\leq r
. Then,
J_{G\overline{G}}[S]=V(G\overline{G})
and
\m(G\overline{G})\leq r
.

On the other hand, to show that
r
is the minimum order of a monophonic set of
G\overline{G}
, we prove Claim 1.

Claim 1:

If
S
is a monophonic set of
G\overline{G}
, then
V(G_{i}\overline{G}_{i})\cap S\neq\emptyset
, for every
i\in[r]
.

Let
t\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}
be the number of trivial components in
G\overline{G}
. If
t\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq t
, the conclusion that
V(G_{i}\overline{G}_{i})\cap S\neq\emptyset
is direct, since
v_{i}
is simplicial. Then, consider that
t+1\leq i\leq r
, for
t\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
.

For a contradiction, suppose that
V(G_{i}\overline{G}_{i})\cap S=\emptyset
, for some
t+1\leq i\leq r
. Let
v\in V(G_{i})
. Since
S
is a monophonic set,
v
lies in a
x,y
-monophonic path
P
in
G\overline{G}
, for
x,y\in V(G\overline{G})\setminus V(G_{i}\overline{G}_{i})
. Since
G_{i}
is a nontrivial component and
P
is an
m
-path, we have that
|V(P)|\geq 6
as well as
|V(P)\cap V(\overline{G}_{i})|\geq 2
. Then, let
P:x,\dots,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},\dots,y^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime},\dots y
with
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in V(\overline{G})\setminus V(\overline{G}_{i})
,
x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in V(\overline{G}_{i})
, and
x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}\in E(\overline{G})
.
Notice that
x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}
is an edge in
\overline{G}
. Since
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime\prime}
are not consecutive vertices in
P
, then
x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}
is a chord, a contradiction.

By Claim 1, we conclude that
\m(G\overline{G})\geq r
and the proof is done.

\square

In the following, we consider both
G
and
\overline{G}
be connected graphs. Recall that
n(G)\geq 4
.

Lemma 4.2

Let
G
be a connected graph such that
\overline{G}
is connected. If
\diam(G)\geq 3
, then
\m(G\overline{G})=2
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in V(G)
such that
d_{G}(u,v)=3
and
S=\{u,v\}
. Since
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
, we have that, for every
x\in N_{G}(u)
,
\overline{x}\in N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
. Then,
u,x,\overline{x},\overline{v},v
is a monophonic path and
x,\overline{x}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[u,v]
. Similarly, for every
y\in N_{G}(v)
, it holds that
v,y,\overline{y},\overline{u},u
is a monophonic path and
y,\overline{y}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[u,v]
.

Next, we show that for every
z\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v))
, the vertices
z,\overline{z}
lie in an
u,v
-monophonic path. For that, we choose
w\in\{u,v\}
such that
d_{G}(z,w)
is minimum and let
P
be a shortest
z,w
-path in
G
. Let
w^{\prime}\in\{u,v\}\setminus\{w\}
. We show that
P
together with the edges
\{z\overline{z},\bar{z}\bar{w}^{\prime},\overline{w}^{\prime}w^{\prime}\}
is an
u,v
-monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
.

Given that
P
is a shortest path in
G
, as well as in
G\overline{G}
, it holds that
P
is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
. Notice that
P
with
\{z\overline{z},\bar{z}\bar{w}^{\prime}\}
is also a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
, since
\bar{z}\notin N_{G\overline{G}}(P\setminus\{z\})
and
\bar{w}^{\prime}\notin N_{G\overline{G}}(P)
. Recall that
w^{\prime}\notin N_{G\overline{G}}(\overline{z})
, then, remains to show that
w^{\prime}\notin N_{G\overline{G}}(P)
. That conclusion holds since
N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)=\emptyset
and
P
is a shortest
z,w
-path in
G
implies that
V(P)\cap N_{G}(w^{\prime})=\emptyset
.

Hence,
z,\overline{z}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[u,v]
and
S
is a monophonic set of
G\overline{G}
.

\square

Recall that Lemma
4.2
treats the case
\diam(G)\geq 3
or
\diam(\overline{G})\geq 3
. Then, from now on, we consider
\diam(G)=\diam(\overline{G})=2
. With such a restriction, the following remark will be useful.

Remark 4.3
(
[
6
]
)

Let
G
be a graph. If
\diam(G)=\diam(\overline{G})=2
, then for every edge
uv\in E(G)
,
ecc_{G}(u)=ecc_{G}(v)=2
and
V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v))\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 4.4

Let
G
be a graph. If
G=C_{5}
, then
\m(G\overline{G})=3
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a cycle graph on
5
vertices, with
V(G)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{5}\}
and
E(G)=\{u_{i}u_{i+1}:1\leq i\leq 4\}\cup\{u_{5}u_{1}\}
. Consider
S=\{u_{1},u_{4},\overline{u}_{5}\}
.
Since
u_{1},u_{5},u_{4}
is an
m
-path in
G
,
\overline{u}_{5}\in S
, and by Proposition
2.4
, for every
i\in[4]
,
u_{i},\overline{u}_{i}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[u_{1},u_{4}]
, we conclude that
S
is a monophonic set of
G\overline{G}
.

Next, we show that
\m(G\overline{G})\geq 3
. Suppose, by contradiction, that
\m(G\overline{G})=2
. Let
S=\{x,y\}
be a monophonic set of
G\overline{G}
. Since
|V(G)|>1
, we have that
xy\notin E(G\overline{G})
. We consider two cases:
(i)

x,y\in V(G)
and
(ii)

x\in V(G)
and
y\in V(\overline{G})
.

(i)
Since for every pair of nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in V(G)
,
d_{G}(x,y)=2
, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
x=u_{1}
and
y=u_{4}
. Since
S
is an
m
-set of
G\overline{G}
,
\overline{u}_{5}
lies in a monophonic path
P:u_{1},\dots,\overline{u}_{5},\dots,u_{4}
. It is clear that
u_{5}\notin V(P)
, otherwise an edge from
\{u_{1},u_{5},u_{4}u_{5},u_{5}\overline{u}_{5}\}
would be a chord. This implies that
\overline{u}_{2}\overline{u}_{5},\overline{u}_{3}\overline{u}_{5}\in E(P)
. If
u_{2}\in V(P)
, then
u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}\overline{u}_{2}\in E(P)
. Then,
u_{3}\notin V(P)
, consequently
u_{4}\overline{u}_{4}\in E(P)
. But
\overline{u}_{2}\overline{u}_{4}
is a chord in
P
, a contradiction. So, consider that
u_{2}\notin V(P)
. Then,
u_{1}\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{1}\overline{u}_{3}\in E(P)
. This implies that
\overline{u}_{4}\notin V(P)
, consequently
u_{3}\in V(P)
as
u_{4}u_{3},u_{3}\overline{u}_{3}\in E(P)
. But
\overline{u}_{1}\overline{u}_{3}
is a chord in
P
, contradiction. Hence,
\overline{u}_{5}\notin J_{G\overline{G}}[u_{1},u_{4}]
, a contradiction.

(ii)
Let
x\in V(G)
and
y\in V(\overline{G})
with
xy\notin E(G)
. Since
d_{G\overline{G}}(x,y)=2
, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
x=u_{1}
and
y=\overline{u}_{2}
. Since
S
is an
m
-set of
G\overline{G}
,
u_{3}
lies in a monophonic path
P:u_{1},\dots,u_{3},\dots,u_{4}
. Notice that
u_{2}\notin V(P)
, otherwise an edge from
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{2}u_{3},u_{2}\overline{u}_{2}\}
would be a chord. Then
u_{3}u_{4},u_{3}\overline{u}_{3}\in E(P)
. If
u_{5}\in V(P)
, then
u_{1}u_{5},u_{4}u_{5}\in E(P)
. Consequently
\overline{u}_{5}\notin V(P)
and
\overline{u}_{2}\overline{u}_{4}\in E(P)
. But in this case,
u_{4}\overline{u}_{4}
is a chord of
P
, a contradiction. Then, consider
u_{5}\notin V(P)
. This implies that
u_{1}\overline{u}_{1}\in E(P)
, but
\overline{u}_{1}\overline{u}_{3}
is a chord of
P
, also a contradiction. Therefore,
\m(G\overline{G})\geq 3
.
\square

Lemma 4.5

Let
G
be a graph such that
\diam(G)=\diam(\overline{G})=2
. If
G\neq C_{5}
, then
\m(G\overline{G})=2
.

Proof.

Let
G\neq C_{5}
be graph with
\diam(G)=\diam(\overline{G})=2
. We present two claims for the proof.

Claim 1:

If there exists
uv\in E(G)
such that
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|\geq 2
, then
\m(G\overline{G})=2
.

Let
uv\in E(G)
such that
N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and
S=\{u,\overline{v}\}
.
Given that
u,v,\overline{v}
is an
m
-path, the conclusion that
v\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
is immediate. By Remark
4.3
, we know that there exists
x\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v))
. Then,
\overline{u},\overline{v}\in N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{x})
. This implies that
u,\overline{u},\overline{x},\overline{v}
is an
m
-path and
\overline{u}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
.

Recall that
u_{1},u_{2}\notin N_{G}(v)
. Then
\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2}\in N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
. Consequently,
u,u_{i},\overline{u}_{i},\overline{v}
, for every
i\in\{1,2\}
, is a monophonic path in
G\overline{G}
and
u_{i},\overline{u}_{i}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
. This in turn implies that
(N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v))\cup(\overline{N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
.

Now, let
v_{1}\in N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)
. Notice that
there is no monophonic
u,v_{1}
-path containing both
u_{1},u_{2}
, otherwise either
uu_{1}
or
uu_{2}
would be a chord. In addition, since
d_{G}(v_{1},u_{i})\leq 2
, for every
i\in\{1,2\}
, a shortest
v_{1},u_{i}
-path in
G
does not contain
v
. Then, we select
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
v_{1},\dots,u_{i}
is a shortest path in
G
of minimum length.
Thus,
u,u_{i},\dots,v_{1},\overline{v}_{1},\overline{u}_{3-i},\overline{v}
is a monophonic
u,\overline{v}
-path in
G\overline{G}
. Consequently
v_{1},\overline{v}_{1}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
. Furthermore, this implies that
(N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u))\cup(\overline{N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
.

Next, let
y\in N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)
. By Remark
4.3
, there exists
w\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(y))
. If
w\notin N_{G}(v)
, then
\overline{w}\in N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
, consequently
u,y,\overline{y},\overline{w},\overline{v}
is an
m
-path in
G\overline{G}
. Otherwise,
vw
being an edge in
G
, Remark
4.3
implies that there exists
w^{\prime}\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(v)\cup N_{G}(w))
. Thus,
\overline{v},\overline{w}\in N_{\overline{G}}(\overline{w}^{\prime})
. Hence,
u,y,\overline{y},\overline{w},\overline{w}^{\prime},\overline{v}
is an
m
-path in
G\overline{G}
.
In both cases, we conclude that
y,\overline{y}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
, which implies that
(N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v))\cup(\overline{N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
.

Finally, let
x\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v))
. Since
d_{G}(u,x)=2
, there exists
u^{\prime}\in N_{G}(x)
such that
u^{\prime}x\in E(G)
. Then,
u,u^{\prime},x,\overline{x},\overline{v}
is an
m
-path in
G\overline{G}
. Thus
x,\overline{x}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
and
(V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v)))\cup(\overline{V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v))})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[S]
.

By the above,
S=\{u,\overline{v}\}
is an
m
-set of
G\overline{G}
, therefore
\m(G\overline{G})=2
.

Recall that Claim 1 considers the existence of
uv\in E(G)
such that
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|\geq 2
. Then, for the rest of the proof, we consider that there is no such an edge, i.e., for every
uv\in E(G)
,
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|\leq 1
. We proceed with Claim 2.

Claim 2:

If there exists a clique of order
3
in
G
, then
\m(G\overline{G})=2
.

Let
C=\{a,b,c\}
be a clique in
G
. Since
ab\in E(G)
, Remark
4.3
, implies that there exists
x\in V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(a)\cup N_{G}(b))
. Since
\diam(G)=2
, there exists
y\in N_{G}(a)\cup N_{G}(b)
such that
xy\in E(G)
.

Recall that
ax,bx\notin E(G)
. If
y=c
, then
cx
is an edge with
|N_{G}(c)\setminus N_{G}(x)|\geq 2
and the conclusion follows by Claim 1.
Otherwise, w.l.o.g., suppose that
y\in N_{G}(a)\setminus N_{G}(b)
. If
cy\notin E(G)
, given that
by\notin E(G)
, we have that
ay
is an edge with
|N_{G}(a)\setminus N_{G}(y)|\geq 2
and, again, the conclusion follows by Claim 1. So, suppose that
cy\in E(G)
. Since
ax,cx\notin E(G)
,
yx
is an edge with
|N_{G}(y)\setminus N_{G}(x)|\geq 2
. Once applying Claim 1, the conclusion holds.

By Claim 2, we may assume that
G
contains no
K_{3}
as an induced subgraph, that is,
G
is
triangle-free
. Recall that for every
uv\in E(G)
,
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|\leq 1
. If
G
is triangle-free and there exists
uv\in E(G)
, with
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|=0
, then
deg_{G}(u)=1
. Since
\diam(G)=2
, for every
x\in V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}
,
d_{G}(u,x)=2
. But this implies that
N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v)=V(G)
, which contradicts Remark
4.3
. Then
G
has no pendant vertices. Finally, since for every
uv\in E(G)
,
|N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{G}(v)|=1
,
\diam(G)=\diam(\overline{G)}=2
implies that
G=C_{5}
, which was proved in Lemma
4.4
.

\square

Putting everything together, we close this section with Theorem

.

Theorem 4.6

Let
G
be a connected graph such that
\overline{G}
is connected, then

\m(G\overline{G})=\begin{cases}3\hskip 11.38092pt;$ if $G=C_{5}\,,\\
2\hskip 11.38092pt;$ otherwise$\,.\end{cases}

The conclusion follows by Lemmas
4.2
,
4.4
, and
4.5
.

\square

5
Monophonic hull number

In this section, we determine the monophonic hull number of complementary prisms. As mentioned in Introduction, in
[
8
]
the authors prove that the monophonic hull number of an arbitrary graph can be computed in polynomial time. Although they presented an
O(n^{3}m)
-time algorithm for computing such a parameter on a graph
G
with
n
vertices and
m
edges, we present here closed formulas for
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})
that depend solely on the number of trivial components of
G
. This leads to a linear time algorithm for computing the hull number of complementary prisms.

Theorem 5.1

Let
G
be a disconnected graph with
t
trivial components. Then

\h_{m}(G\overline{G})=\begin{cases}2\hskip 28.45274pt;$ if $t=0\,,\\
t+1\hskip 11.38092pt;$ otherwise$\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has no trivial components. Denote by
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r}
the components of
G
. Let
S=\{\overline{u},\overline{v}\}
such that
uv\in E(G_{1})
. We show that
S
is an
m
-hull set of
G\overline{G}
. Since
\bar{u}\bar{v}\notin E(\overline{G})
and, for every
w\notin V(G_{1})
,
\bar{u}\bar{w},\bar{v}\bar{w}\in E(\overline{G})
, we have that
\bar{u},\bar{w},\bar{v}
is a monophonic path in
\overline{G}
. Then
\bigcup_{i=2}^{r}V(\overline{G}_{i})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[\overline{u},\overline{v}]
. Since
G_{2}
is nontrivial, there exists
x,y\in V(G_{2})
such that
xy\in E(G)
. By the same argument, we get
V(\overline{G}_{1})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[\overline{x},\overline{y}]
. Finally, given that
V(\overline{G})\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
and, for every
i\in[r]
, there exist
x,y\in V(G_{i})
such that
xy\in E(G)
,
x,y\in J_{G\overline{G}}[\overline{x},\overline{y}]
. By applying Claim 1 of proof of Theorem
3.2
we get that
V(G)\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
.

Now, suppose that
G
has
t\geq 1
trivial components. Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{r}
denote the components of
G
, where
V(G_{i})=\{v_{i}\}
, for
1\leq i\leq t
, and
n(G_{i})\geq 2
, for
t+1\leq i\leq r
. Notice that
v_{i}
is a simplicial vertex, for every
1\leq i\leq t
. Then, Remark
2.2
implies that
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{t}
belongs to any
m
-hull set of
G\overline{G}
and
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})\geq t
. For every
i,j\in[t]
, we know that
\overline{v}_{i}\overline{v}_{j}
is a chord for every
v_{i},v_{j}
-path in
G\overline{G}
. Then,
[\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{t}\}]_{G\overline{G}}=\{v_{i},\overline{v}_{i}:1\leq i\leq t\}
, which implies that
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})\geq t+1
.

Let
u
be any vertex in a nontrivial component of
G
, say
G_{t+1}
. We show that
S=\{u,v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{t}\}
is an
m
-hull set of
G\overline{G}
. We know that
u,\overline{u},\overline{v}_{i},v_{i}
(resp.
u^{\prime},\overline{u}^{\prime},\overline{v}_{i},v_{i}
) is a monophonic path between
u
(resp.
u^{\prime}
, for
u^{\prime}\in N_{G}(u)
) and
v_{i}
, for every
i\in[t]
. Then
(\{v_{i},\overline{v}_{i}:1\leq i\leq t\}\cup N_{G}[u]\cup\overline{N_{G}[u]})\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
. Let
u^{\prime}\in N_{G}(u)
. Since
\overline{u}\overline{u^{\prime}}\notin E(G)
, we have that
\bigcup_{i=t+2}^{r}V(\overline{G}_{i})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[\overline{u},\overline{u^{\prime}}]
. Given that
G_{t+2}
is nontrivial,
V(\overline{G}_{t+1})\subseteq J_{G\overline{G}}[V(\overline{G}_{t+2})]
, which implies that
V(\overline{G})\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
. Finally, using a similar argument as in the case of
t=0
, we conclude that
V(G)\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
.

\square

Theorem 5.2

Let
G
be a connected graph such that
\overline{G}
is connected, then
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})=2
.

Proof.

Since
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})
is upper bounded by
\m(G\overline{G})
(cf. Remark
2.3
), when
G\neq C_{5}
, Theorem
4.6
imples that
\h_{m}(G\overline{G})=2
. Then, it is enough to prove that
\h_{m}(C_{5}\overline{C}_{5})=2
.

Let
G
be a cycle graph on
5
vertices, with
V(G)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{5}\}
and
E(G)=\{u_{i}u_{i+1}:1\leq i\leq 4\}\cup\{u_{5}u_{1}\}
. Consider
S=\{u_{1},u_{4}\}
.
Proposition
2.4
implies that, for every
i\in[4]
,
u_{i},\overline{u}_{i}\in J_{G\overline{G}}[S]\subseteq[S]_{G\overline{G}}
. Since
u_{1},u_{5},u_{4}
is an
m
-path in

Compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras
Apurba Das

Abstract
A compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra is a graded vector space together with two compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra structures on it. Given a graded vector space, we construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra structures on it. We provide examples of compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras arising from Nijenhuis operators, compatible V V -datas and compatible Courant algebroids. We define the cohomology of a compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra and as an application, we study formal deformations. Next, we classify ‘strict’ and ‘skeletal’ compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras in terms of crossed modules and cohomology of compatible Lie algebras. Finally, we introduce compatible Lie 2 2 -algebras and find their relationship with compatible L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras.

Notations.

All (graded) vector spaces, (multi)linear maps, tensor products, wedge products are over a field

Compatible Lie algebras

2.1 Definition
.

A compatible Lie algebra is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with two Lie brackets
[~,~]
and
[~,~]^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the compatibility

(1)

\displaystyle[[x,y]^{\prime},z]+[[x,y],z]^{\prime}=[[x,z]^{\prime},y]+[[x,z],y]^{\prime}+[x,[y,z]^{\prime}]+[x,[y,z]]^{\prime},\text{ for }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Note that the compatibility condition (
1
) is equivalent to say that
[~,~]+[~,~]^{\prime}
is also a Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
.

2.2 Definition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
be a compatible Lie algebra. A representation of it consists of a triple
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
in which
(V,\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
, and
(V,\rho^{\prime})
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]^{\prime})
satisfying additionally the following compatibility

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]^{\prime})+\rho^{\prime}([x,y])=[\rho(x),\rho^{\prime}(y)]+[\rho^{\prime}(x),\rho(y)],~\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Any compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
can be regarded as a representation
(\mathfrak{g},\rho=\mathrm{ad}_{[~,~]},\rho^{\prime}=\mathrm{ad}_{[~,~]^{\prime}})
of itself. This is called the adjoint representation.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
be a representation of it. Consider the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex
\{\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g},V),\partial\}
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho)
, where the coboundary
\partial:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},V)
, for
n\geq 0
, is given by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}f([x_{i},x_{j}],x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly, we can consider the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex
\{\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g},V),\partial^{\prime}\}
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]^{\prime})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho^{\prime})
. It has been observed in
[
15
]
that
\partial
and
\partial^{\prime}
satisfies the compatibility
\partial\circ\partial^{\prime}+\partial^{\prime}\circ\partial=0
. Therefore, we are now able to construct a new cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V),\partial_{c}\}
, where

\displaystyle C^{0}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{v\in V|~\rho(x)v=\rho^{\prime}(x)v,\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}~~~\text{ and }~~~C^{n\geq 1}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\underbrace{\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)}_{n\text{ times}}.

The coboundary map
\partial_{c}:C^{n}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, for
n\geq 0
, given by

\displaystyle\partial_{c}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=(\partial f_{1},\ldots,\underbrace{\partial f_{i}+\partial^{\prime}f_{i-1}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,\partial^{\prime}f_{n}).

The cohomology groups of the complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V),\partial_{c}\}
are called the cohomology of the compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
.

2.3 Remark
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Suppose
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)
are two elements that satisfies
\partial f=\partial^{\prime}f^{\prime}=\partial f^{\prime}+\partial^{\prime}f=0
. Then the element
(f,\underbrace{f+f^{\prime},\ldots,f+f^{\prime}}_{n-2\text{ times}},f^{\prime})\in C^{n}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a
n
-cocycle as

\displaystyle\partial_{c}(f,f+f^{\prime},\ldots,f+f^{\prime},f^{\prime})=\big(\partial f,\partial f+\partial f^{\prime}+\partial^{\prime}f,\ldots,\underbrace{\partial(f+f^{\prime})+\partial^{\prime}(f+f^{\prime})}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,\partial^{\prime}f^{\prime}\big)=0.

Such
n
-cocycles are called induced by compatible Lie algebra cocycles. We will use such
3
-cocycles in Section
6
to classify skeletal compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

L_{\infty}
-algebras

2.4 Definition
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a pair
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
consisting of a graded vector space
L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i}
together with a collection
\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of graded skew-symmetric multilinear maps
l_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L
with
\text{deg }(l_{k})=2-k
, for
k\geq 1
, satisfying the following set of identities:

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}\chi(\sigma)(-1)^{i(j-1)}~l_{j}\big(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)=0,

for
n\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in L.

The identities (
2
) are called the higher Jacobi identities. For small values on
n
, the meaning of the higher Jacobi identities can be found in
[
16
]
.

2.5 Remark
.

Any differential graded Lie algebra
(L,\partial,[~,~])
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra in which
l_{1}=\partial
,
l_{2}=[~,~]
and
l_{k}=0
, for
k\geq 3
.

Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Take
V=s^{-1}L
the desuspension of
L
, where
V_{i}=(s^{-1}L)_{i}=L_{i+1}
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We define degree
1
maps
\rho_{k}:V^{\otimes k}\rightarrow V
, for
k\geq 1
, by

\displaystyle\rho_{k}:=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}~s^{-1}\circ l_{k}\circ s^{\otimes k}.

It turns out that the map
\rho_{k}
is graded symmetric, for
k\geq 1
. Note that the map
l_{k}
can be reconstructed from
\rho_{k}
by
l_{k}=s\circ\rho_{k}\circ(s^{-1})^{\otimes k}
.

For each
k\geq 1
, the map
\rho_{k}
uniquely lifts to a degree
1
coderivation
\widetilde{\rho_{k}}:SV\rightarrow SV
on the symmetric coalgebra
SV
, given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho_{k}}(v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(k,n-k)}\epsilon(\sigma)~\rho_{k}(v_{\sigma(1)}\odot\cdots\odot v_{\sigma(k)})\odot v_{\sigma(k+1)}\odot\cdots\odot v_{\sigma(n)},~\text{ for }n\geq k

and zero otherwise. Note that the graded space of coderivations
\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV)
on the symmetric coalgebra
SV
is a graded Lie algebra with the commutator bracket
[~,~]_{C}
.

2.6 Proposition
.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on a graded vector space
L
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})
, where
V=s^{-1}L
.

3
Compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section, we introduce compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras as the homotopy analogue of compatible Lie algebras. Given a graded vector space
L
, we also construct a graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are given by compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra structures
L
.

3.1 Definition
.

A compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a triple
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
in which
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(L,\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
are both
L_{\infty}
-algebras additionally satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(i,n-i)}\chi(\sigma)(-1)^{i(j-1)}~\bigg\{

\displaystyle l_{j}\big(l_{i}^{\prime}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)

\displaystyle+l_{j}^{\prime}\big(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}\big)\bigg\}=0,

for
n\geq 1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in L.

In this case, we often say that the
L_{\infty}
-algebras
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(L,\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
are compatible.
Note that the compatibility condition (
3
) is equivalent to the fact that the pair
(L,\{l_{k}+l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

3.2 Example
.

(i) Any compatible Lie algebra is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose underlying graded vector space is concentrated in degree
0
. Any compatible differential graded Lie algebra
(L,\{\partial,[~,~]\},\{\partial^{\prime},[~,~]^{\prime}\})
is also a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra with
l_{k}=l^{\prime}_{k}=0
, for
k\geq 3
. See also Proposition
4.1
.

(ii) Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is obviously a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

(iii) Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then
(L,\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
,
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}+l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(L,\{l_{k}+l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
are all compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Some nontrivial examples of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras are provided in Sections
4
and
6
. In the next, we give some characterization results for compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{n},[~,~])
be a graded Lie algebra and
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
be two Maurer-Cartan elements. They are said to be compatible if they satisfies
[\theta,\theta^{\prime}]=0
. The following result gives a characterization of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra structures on a graded vector space
L
in terms of compatible Maurer-Cartan elements in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})
, where
V=s^{-1}L
.

3.3 Theorem
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra structures on a graded vector space
L
and compatible Maurer-Cartan elements in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})
, where
V=s^{-1}L
.

Proof.

Let
\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be two collections of multilinear maps
l_{k},l^{\prime}_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L
with
\mathrm{deg}(l_{k})=\mathrm{deg}(l^{\prime}_{k})=2-k
, for
k\geq 1
. We consider the collections
\{\rho_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{\rho^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of degree
1
maps
\rho_{k},\rho^{\prime}_{k}:V^{\otimes k}\rightarrow V
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{k}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}~s^{-1}\circ l_{k}\circ s^{\otimes k}~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\rho^{\prime}_{k}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}~s^{-1}\circ l^{\prime}_{k}\circ s^{\otimes k},~\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Define two degree
1
coderivations
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)
by
D=\sum_{k\geq 1}\widetilde{\rho_{k}}
and
D^{\prime}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\widetilde{\rho^{\prime}_{k}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle[D,D]_{C}=0~~~\leftrightsquigarrow~~~(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})\text{ is an }L_{\infty}\text{-algebra},

\displaystyle[D^{\prime},D^{\prime}]_{C}=0~~~\leftrightsquigarrow~~~(L,\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})\text{ is an }L_{\infty}\text{-algebra},

\displaystyle[D,D^{\prime}]_{C}=0~~~\leftrightsquigarrow~~~\text{the compatibility condition }(\ref{hji-comp}).

This completes the proof.
∎

L_{\infty}
-algebra structures on

\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(SV)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Coder}^{n}_{c}(SV)

The graded Lie bracket

on

induces a bracket on

\displaystyle\llbracket(D_{1},\ldots,D_{m+1}),

\displaystyle(D^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,D^{\prime}_{n+1})\rrbracket

\displaystyle:=\big([D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime}]_{C},\ldots,\underbrace{[D_{1},D_{i}^{\prime}]_{C}+[D_{2},D^{\prime}_{i-1}]_{C}+\cdots+[D_{i},D^{\prime}_{1}]_{C}}_{i\text{-th place}},\ldots,[D_{m+1},D^{\prime}_{n+1}]_{C}\big),

and

There is a one-to-one correspondence between compatible

-algebra structures on

\displaystyle\leftrightsquigarrow

\displaystyle(\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})\text{ on }L

\displaystyle D,D^{\prime}\in(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})

\displaystyle(D,D^{\prime})\in(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(SV),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket).

Compatible dgLa induced from a compatible Lie algebra

We have seen in Remark

that a differential graded Lie algebra can be considered as an
L_{\infty}

4.1 Proposition
.

Let
(L,\partial.[~,~])
and
(L,\partial^{\prime},[~,~]^{\prime})
be two differential graded Lie algebras. Then the triple
(L,\{\partial,[~,~]\},\{\partial^{\prime},[~,~]^{\prime}\})
is a compatible differential graded Lie algebra if and only if

•

\partial\circ\partial^{\prime}+\partial^{\prime}\circ\partial=0
,

•

[[x,y],z]^{\prime}+[[x,y]^{\prime},z]=(-1)^{|y||z|}[[x,z],y]^{\prime}+(-1)^{|y||z|}[[x,z]^{\prime},y]+[x,[y,z]]^{\prime}+[x,[y,z]^{\prime}],

•

\partial[x,y]^{\prime}+\partial^{\prime}[x,y]=[\partial(x),y]^{\prime}+[\partial^{\prime}(x),y]+(-1)^{|x|}[x,\partial(y)]^{\prime}+(-1)^{|x|}[x,\partial^{\prime}(y)]
, for
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

In the following, we construct an example of a compatible differential graded Lie algebra from a compatible Lie algebra.

4.2 Example
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
be a compatible Lie algebra. Consider the graded vector space
L=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
of all skew-symmetric multilinear maps on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
. It carries a graded Lie bracket (Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket) given by

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\mathsf{NR}}:=~

\displaystyle f\diamond g-(-1)^{mn}g\diamond f,~\text{ where }

\displaystyle(f\diamond g)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n+1})=~

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in Sh(n+1,m)}(-1)^{\sigma}~f\big(g(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n+1)}),x_{\sigma(n+2)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n+1)}\big),

for
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Let the compatible Lie brackets
[~,~]
and
[~,~]^{\prime}
correspond to elements
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
in
L_{1}=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Then
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
satisfies

\displaystyle[\mu,\mu]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0,~\quad~~~[\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0~~~~\text{ and }~~~~[\mu,\mu^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0.

This implies that the differential graded Lie algebras
(L,\partial_{\mu}=[\mu,-]_{\mathsf{NR}},[~,~]_{\mathsf{NR}},)
and
(L,\partial_{\mu^{\prime}}=[\mu^{\prime},-]_{\mathsf{NR}},[~,~]_{\mathsf{NR}},)
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
4.1
. In other words,
(L,\{\partial_{\mu},[~,~]_{\mathsf{NR}}\},\{\partial_{\mu^{\prime}},[~,~]_{\mathsf{NR}}\})
is a compatible differential graded Lie algebra.

Compatible
A_{\infty}
-algebras

The notion of
A_{\infty}
-spaces and
A_{\infty}
-algebras (strongly homotopy associative algebras) was introduced by Stasheff in the study of group-like topological spaces
[
28
]
. They have important applications in homotopy theory and combinatorial study of associahedra.

4.3 Definition
.

An
A_{\infty}
-algebra is a pair
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
consisting of a graded vector space
A=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
together with a collection
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of graded multilinear maps
\mu_{k}:A^{\otimes k}\rightarrow A
with
\mathrm{deg}(\mu_{k})=2-k
, for
k\geq 1
, satisfying for
n\geq 1
and homogeneous elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{j}(-1)^{\lambda(i+1)+i(|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{i-1}|)}\mu_{j}\big(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},\mu_{i}(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+k-1}),\ldots,a_{n}\big)=0.

Let
W=s^{-1}A
. Note that a collection
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of graded multilinear maps
\mu_{k}:A^{\otimes k}\rightarrow A
with
\mathrm{deg}(\mu_{k})=2-k
induces a collection
\{\tau_{k}:W^{\otimes k}\rightarrow W\}_{k\geq 1}
of degree
1
multilinear maps, where
\tau_{k}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}s^{-1}\circ\mu_{k}\circ s^{\otimes k}
, for
k\geq 1
. Hence we obtain a degree
1
coderivation
E=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{\tau_{k}}:TW\rightarrow TW
on the tensor coalgebra
TW
, where
\widetilde{\tau_{k}}:TW\rightarrow TW
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau_{k}}(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}(-1)^{|w_{1}|+\cdots+|w_{i-1}|}~w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\tau_{k}(w_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{i+k-1})\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{n},

for
n\geq k
and zero otherwise. Consider the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(TW),[~,~]_{C})
on the graded vector space
\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(TW)
of coderivations on the tensor coalgebra
TW
. Then
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra if and only if
E\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(TW)
satisfies
[E,E]_{C}=0
. The following result has been proved in
[
16
]
.

4.4 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be an
A_{\infty}
-algebra. Then
(A,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}(-1)^{\sigma}\mu_{k}(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(k)}),~\text{ for }k\geq 1.

The collection
\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is called the skew-symmetrization of
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
.

4.5 Definition
.

A compatible
A_{\infty}
-algebra is a triple
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
in which
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
,
(A,\{\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(A,\{\mu_{k}+\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
are all
A_{\infty}
-algebras.

It is easy to prove that (similar to Theorem
3.4
) a compatible
A_{\infty}
-algebra structure on a graded vector space
A
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(TW),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
, where
W=s^{-1}A
.

4.6 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be a compatible
A_{\infty}
-algebra. Then
(A,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where
\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are the skew-symmetrizations of
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, respectively.

Proof.

Since
(A,\{\mu_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(A,\{\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
are
A_{\infty}
-algebras, it follows that
(A,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(A,\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
are
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Further,
(A,\{\mu_{k}+\mu^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra implies that
(A,\{l_{k}+l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra (by Proposition
4.4
). Thus, we get that
(A,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.
∎

Nijenhuis operators on
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this subsection, we state the main result from
[
1
]
about Nijenhuis operators on
L_{\infty}
-algebras, and observe that Nijenhuis operators induce compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras. Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Consider the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element
D\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})
, where
V=s^{-1}L
.

Let
\{N_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L\}_{k\geq 1}
be a collection of skew-symmetric multilinear maps with
\mathrm{deg}(l_{k})=1-k
, for
k\geq 1
. Consider the collection
\{\mathcal{N}_{k}:V^{\otimes k}\rightarrow V\}_{k\geq 1}
of degree
0
symmetric multilinear maps on
V
given by
\mathcal{N}_{k}=(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}s^{-1}\circ N_{k}\circ s^{\otimes k}
, for
k\geq 1
. This collection of symmetric multilinear maps lifts to a degree
0
coderivation
N=\sum_{k\geq 1}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}_{k}}\in\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)
on the symmetric coalgebra
SV
.

4.7 Definition
.

A collection
\{N_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L\}_{k\geq 1}
of skew-symmetric multilinear maps (with
\mathrm{deg}(N_{k})=1-k
, for
k\geq 1
) is said to be a Nijenhuis operator on the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
if there exists a degree
0
coderivation
{P}\in\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)
such that

\displaystyle[N,[N,D]_{C}]_{C}=[P,D]_{C}~~~~\text{ and }~~~~[N,P]_{C}=0.

Such a
P
is called a square of
N
. The following result has been proved in
[
1
, Theorem 2.4]
.

4.8 Proposition
.

Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra with the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element
D\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}(SV),[~,~]_{C})
, where
V=s^{-1}L
. Suppose
\{N_{k}:L^{\otimes k}\rightarrow L\}_{k\geq 1}
is a Nijenhuis operator with the corresponding coderivation
N\in\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)
. Then the coderivation
D_{N}:=[N,D]_{C}\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)
satisfies
[D_{N},D_{N}]_{C}=0
and
[D,D_{N}]_{C}=0
. Hence
D_{N}
corresponds to an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure
(L,\{l_{k}^{N}\}_{k\geq 1})
on the same graded vector space
L
and
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{N}\}_{k\geq 1})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Compatible
V
-datas

Let
L
be a graded Lie algebra (with the bracket
[~,~]
),
\mathfrak{a}\subset L
be an abelian Lie subalgebra,
P:L\rightarrow L
be a projection such that
\mathrm{im}(P)=\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathrm{ker}(P)\subset L
be a Lie subalgebra. Let
\triangle\in\mathrm{ker}(P)_{1}
be an element that satisfies
[\triangle,\triangle]=0
. Such an element is called a ‘square-zero odd element’. In this case, the quadruple
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
is called a
V
-data
[
31
]
.

Given a
V
-data
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
, the graded vector space
\mathfrak{a}
carries some degree
1
symmetric multilinear maps given by

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}):=P[\cdots[[\triangle,a_{1}],a_{2}],\ldots,a_{k}],\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Moreover, the pair
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
31
]
, where
s\mathfrak{a}
is the suspension of
\mathfrak{a}
and
l_{k}
’s are given by
l_{k}=s\circ\rho_{k}\circ(s^{-1})^{\otimes k},
for
k\geq 1
.

4.9 Definition
.

Let
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
and
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle^{\prime})
be two
V
-datas with the same underlying structures except the square-zero odd elements. They are said to be compatible if
[\triangle,\triangle^{\prime}]=0
.

4.10 Proposition
.

Let
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
and
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle^{\prime})
be two compatible
V
-datas with the corresponding
L_{\infty}
-algebras
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
and
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
. Then
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Proof.

Since
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
and
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle^{\prime})
are two compatible
V
-datas, it follows that
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle+\triangle^{\prime})
is a
V
-data. If
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l^{*}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is the
L_{\infty}
-algebra corresponding to the
V
-data
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle+\triangle^{\prime})
, then it is easy to see that
l^{*}_{k}=l_{k}+l^{\prime}_{k}
, for
k\geq 1
. This implies that
(s\mathfrak{a},\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.
∎

In the following, we give an example of compatible
V
-datas and the use of the corresponding compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
V
be two vector spaces. Consider the graded Lie algebra

\displaystyle L=\big(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[~,~]_{\mathsf{NR}}\big)

on the space of all skew-symmetric multilinear maps on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
with the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket. Then
\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}V,\mathfrak{g})\subset L
is an abelian Lie subalgebra. Let
P:L\rightarrow L
be the projection onto the subspace
\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose there are two skew-symmetric bilinear brackets
[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and bilinear maps
\rho,\rho^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\times V\rightarrow V
. Consider the elements

\triangle,\triangle^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
defined respectively by

\displaystyle\triangle((x,u),(y,v))=([x,y],\rho(x,v)-\rho(y)u)~\text{ and }~\triangle^{\prime}((x,u),(y,v))=([x,y]^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}(x)v-\rho^{\prime}(y)u),

for
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
. Then we have the following.

4.11 Proposition
.

With the above notations,
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
is a compatible Lie algebra and
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a representation if and only if
[\triangle,\triangle]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
,
[\triangle^{\prime},\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
and
[\triangle,\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
.

Proof.

For any
(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
, we have

\displaystyle[\triangle,\triangle]_{\mathsf{NR}}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=2\big([[x,y],z]-[[x,z],y]+[[y,z],x],~\rho([x,y])w-[\rho(x),\rho(y)]w-\rho([x,z])v+[\rho(x),\rho(z)]v

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho([y,z])u-[\rho(y),\rho(z)]u\big),

\displaystyle[\triangle^{\prime},\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=2\big([[x,y]^{\prime},z]^{\prime}-[[x,z]^{\prime},y]^{\prime}+[[y,z]^{\prime},x]^{\prime},~\rho^{\prime}([x,y]^{\prime})w-[\rho^{\prime}(x),\rho^{\prime}(y)]w-\rho^{\prime}([x,z]^{\prime})v+[\rho^{\prime}(x),\rho^{\prime}(z)]v

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\rho^{\prime}([y,z]^{\prime})u-[\rho^{\prime}(y),\rho^{\prime}(z)]u\big)

and

\displaystyle[\triangle,\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=\big([[x,y]^{\prime},z]-[[x,z]^{\prime},y]+[[y,z]^{\prime},x]+[[x,y],z]^{\prime}-[[x,z],y]^{\prime}+[[y,z],x]^{\prime},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\rho([x,y]^{\prime})w-[\rho(x),\rho^{\prime}(y)]w-\rho([x,z]^{\prime})v+[\rho(x),\rho^{\prime}(z)]v+\rho([y,z]^{\prime})u-[\rho(y),\rho^{\prime}(z)]u

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\rho^{\prime}([x,y])w-[\rho^{\prime}(x),\rho(y)]w-\rho^{\prime}([x,z])v+[\rho^{\prime}(x),\rho(z)]v+\rho^{\prime}([y,z])u-[\rho^{\prime}(y),\rho(z)]u\big).

This shows that
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
is a compatible Lie algebra and
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a representation if and only if
[\triangle,\triangle]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
,
[\triangle^{\prime},\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
and
[\triangle,\triangle^{\prime}]_{\mathsf{NR}}=0
.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
be a representation. Then it follows from the above Proposition that
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle)
and
(L,\mathfrak{a},P,\triangle^{\prime})
are compatible
V
-datas. Therefore, the space
s\mathfrak{a}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}V,\mathfrak{g})
carry compatible graded Lie brackets (as higher brackets are zero for degree reason) given by

\displaystyle\{\![P,Q]\!\}=(-1)^{m}[[\triangle,P]_{\mathsf{NR}},Q]_{\mathsf{NR}}~~~\text{ and }~~~\{\![P,Q]\!\}^{\prime}=(-1)^{m}[[\triangle^{\prime},P]_{\mathsf{NR}},Q]_{\mathsf{NR}},

for
P\in(s\mathfrak{a})_{m}=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m}V,\mathfrak{g})
and
Q\in(s\mathfrak{a})_{n}=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}V,\mathfrak{g})
. In other words,
(s\mathfrak{a},\{\![~,~]\!\},\{\![~,~]\!\}^{\prime})
is a compatible graded Lie algebra. We will use this graded Lie algebra to characterize relative Rota-Baxter operators on compatible Lie algebras.

4.12 Definition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
be a representation. A linear map
R:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a relative Rota-Baxter operator if it satisfies

\displaystyle[R(v),R(w)]=R\big(\rho(R(v))w-\rho(R(w))v\big)~~\text{ and }~~[R(v),R(w)]^{\prime}=R\big(\rho^{\prime}(R(v))w-\rho^{\prime}(R(w))v\big),~\text{ for }v,w\in V.

We have the following result about the characterization of relative Rota-Baxter operators.

4.13 Proposition
.

A linear map
R:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if
R
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the compatible graded Lie algebra
(s\mathfrak{a},\{\![~,~]\!\},\{\![~,~]\!\}^{\prime})
, i.e.
R
satisfies
\{\![R,R]\!\}=\{\![R,R]\!\}^{\prime}=0
.

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation (see
[
30
]
), we get that

\displaystyle\{\![R,R]\!\}(u,v)=~

\displaystyle 2\big(R(\rho(R(v))w)-R(\rho(R(w))v)-[R(v),R(w)]\big),

\displaystyle\{\![R,R]\!\}^{\prime}(u,v)=~

\displaystyle 2\big(R(\rho^{\prime}(R(v))w)-R(\rho^{\prime}(R(w))v)-[R(v),R(w)]^{\prime}\big),

for
v,w\in V
. This shows that
R
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if
\{\![R,R]\!\}=\{\![R,R]\!\}^{\prime}=0
.
∎

Compatible Courant algebroids

Courant algebroids were introduced in
[
18
]
as a double object of Lie bialgebroids. A Courant algebroid over a smooth manifold
M
consists of a quadruple
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]_{E},\rho)
of a smooth vector bundle
E
over
M
, a symmetric nondegenerate
C^{\infty}(M)
-valued pairing
\langle~,~\rangle:\Gamma E\times\Gamma E\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
, a bilinear bracket
[~,~]_{E}:\Gamma E\times\Gamma E
and a bundle map
\rho:E\rightarrow TM
satisfying the following set of identities:

•

[X,[Y,Z]_{E}]_{E}=[[X,Y]_{E},Z]_{E}+[Y,[X,Z]_{E}]_{E}
(left Leibniz identity),

•

[X,fY]_{E}=f[X,Y]_{E}+\rho(X)(f)Y,

•

\rho[X,Y]_{E}=[\rho(X),\rho(Y)],

•

[X,X]_{E}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\langle X,X\rangle,

•

\rho(X)\langle Y,Z\rangle=\langle[X,Y]_{E},Z\rangle+\langle Y,[X,Z]_{E}\rangle,

for
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma E
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Here
\mathcal{D}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow\Gamma E
is the composition
C^{\infty}(M)\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}(M)\xrightarrow{\rho^{*}}\Gamma E^{*}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Gamma E
, where the last isomorphism is induced by the pairing
\langle~,~\rangle
.

In
[
26
]
Roytenberg and Weinstein showed that a Courant algebroid
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]_{E},\rho)
associates an
L_{\infty}
-algebra on the graded vector space
L(E):=C^{\infty}(M)\oplus\Gamma E
(where
C^{\infty}(M)
is concentrated in degree
-1
and
\Gamma E
is concentrated in degree
0
) whose structure maps are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}=\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle l_{2}(X,Y)=\lfloor X,Y\rfloor_{E}:=[X,Y]_{E}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\langle X,Y\rangle,

\displaystyle l_{2}(X,f)=-l_{2}(f,X)=\frac{1}{2}\langle X,\mathcal{D}f\rangle,

\displaystyle l_{3}(X,Y,Z)=-\frac{1}{6}\langle\lfloor X,Y\rfloor_{E},Z\rangle+c.p.

(and the higher multilinear maps are vanish), for
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma E
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Here we followed the convension of
[
27
]
. We denote this
L_{\infty}
-algebra by
(L(E),l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
.

4.14 Definition
.

Two Courant algebroids
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]_{E},\rho)
and
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]^{\prime}_{E},\rho^{\prime})
on the underlying same vector bundle
E
and pairing
\langle~,~\rangle
are said to be compatible if the following conditions are hold:

(4)

\displaystyle[X,[Y,Z]_{E}]^{\prime}_{E}+[X,[Y,Z]^{\prime}_{E}]_{E}=~

\displaystyle[[X,Y]_{E},Z]^{\prime}_{E}+[[X,Y]^{\prime}_{E},Z]_{E}+[Y,[X,Z]_{E}]^{\prime}_{E}+[Y,[X,Z]^{\prime}_{E}]_{E},

(5)

\displaystyle\rho([X,Y]_{E}^{\prime})+\rho^{\prime}([X,Y]_{E})=~

\displaystyle[\rho(X),\rho^{\prime}(Y)]+[\rho^{\prime}(X),\rho(Y)],

for
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma E
. Note that the conditions (
4
) and (
5
) is equivalent to the fact that the new quadruple
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]_{E}+[~,~]_{E}^{\prime},\rho+\rho^{\prime})
is also a Courant algebroid.

In
[
14
]
, Kosmann-Schwarzbach introduced Nijenhuis operators on a given Courant algebroid and show that certain Nijenhuis operators induced a deformed Courant algebroid structure. Moreover, the deformed one is compatible with the given one. In the following, we prove that compatible Courant algebroids give rise to compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

4.15 Proposition
.

Let
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]_{E},\rho)
and
(E,\langle~,~\rangle,[~,~]^{\prime}_{E},\rho^{\prime})
be two compatible Courant algebroids. Consider the corresponding
L_{\infty}
-algebras
(L(E),l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
and
(L(E),l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3})
on the graded vector space
L(E)
. Then
(L(E),\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
(L(E),l_{1}^{*},l_{2}^{*},l_{3}^{*})
be the
L_{\infty}
-algebra associated to the Courant algebroid
(E,[~,~]_{E}+[~,~]_{E}^{\prime},\langle~,~\rangle,\rho+\rho^{\prime})
. It is then easy to see that

(6)

\displaystyle l_{1}^{*}=l_{1}+l^{\prime}_{1},~~~~l_{2}^{*}=l_{2}+l^{\prime}_{2}~~\text{ and }~~l_{3}^{*}=l_{3}+l^{\prime}_{3}.

It follows from (
6
) that
(L(E),\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
is a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.
∎

5
Cohomology and deformations of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section, we first introduce the cohomology of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then we study formal and finite order deformations of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra in terms of cohomology.

Cohomology of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

Let
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. In Theorem
3.4
, we have seen that this compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
L
corresponds to a Maurer-Cartan element
(D,D^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}_{c}(SV)
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(SV),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
, where
V=s^{-1}L
. We will use this Maurer-Cartan element to define the cohomology of the compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
.

For each
n\geq 1
, we define the
n
-th cochain group
C^{n}_{c}(L,L)
by

\displaystyle C^{n}_{c}(L,L):=\mathrm{Coder}^{(n-1)}_{c}(SV)=\underbrace{\mathrm{Coder}^{(n-1)}(SV)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{Coder}^{(n-1)}(SV)}_{n\text{ times}},

and a map
\delta_{c}:C^{n}_{c}(L,L)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{c}(L,L)
, for
n\geq 1
, defined by
\delta_{c}(-):=\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),-\rrbracket.

Then it is easy to see that
(\delta_{c})^{2}=0
. In other words,
\{C^{\bullet}_{c}(L,L),\delta_{c}\}
is a cochain complex.
The corresponding cohomology groups are called the cohomology of the compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
, and they are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{c}(L,L).

Deformations of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

Let
L=(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra with the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element
(D,D^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}_{c}(SV)=\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)\oplus\mathrm{Coder}^{1}(SV)
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(SV),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
.

5.1 Definition
.

A formal deformation of the compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
consists of a formal power series

(7)

\displaystyle(D,D^{\prime})_{t}:=(D_{0},D_{0}^{\prime})+t(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})+t^{2}(D_{2},D_{2}^{\prime})+\cdots\text{ in }\mathrm{Coder}_{c}^{1}(SV)[[t]]

(with
(D_{0},D_{0}^{\prime})=(D,D^{\prime})
) that satisfies
\llbracket(D,D^{\prime})_{t},(D,D^{\prime})_{t}\rrbracket=0
.

Thus, (
7
) defines a formal deformation of
L
if and only if

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\llbracket(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}),(D_{j},D_{j}^{\prime})\rrbracket=0,~\text{ for }n\geq 0.

This system of equations are called deformation equations. Note that (
8
) holds for
n=0
as
(D,D^{\prime})
corresponds to the given compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
L
. However, for
n=1
, we get

\displaystyle\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})\rrbracket=0,

which implies that
(D_{1},D^{\prime}_{1})\in C^{2}_{c}(L,L)
is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
L
. This is called the infinitesimal of the formal deformation
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}.
In particular, if
(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})=\cdots=(D_{p-1},D_{p-1}^{\prime})=0
and
(D_{p},D_{p}^{\prime})
is the first nonzero term, then
(D_{p},D_{p}^{\prime})\in C^{2}_{c}(L,L)
is a
2
-cocycle.

5.2 Definition
.

Let
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime})
and
\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}t^{i}(\overline{D}_{i},\overline{D}_{i}^{\prime})
be two formal deformations of the compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
. They are said to be equivalent if there is a formal power series

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}:=\Phi_{0}+t\Phi_{1}+t^{2}\Phi_{2}+\cdots\text{ in }\mathrm{Coder}^{0}_{c}(SV)[[t]]=\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)[[t]]

(with
\Phi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
) satisfying
\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}=\Phi_{t}\circ(D,D^{\prime})_{t}.

It follows that
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
and
\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}
are equivalent if and only if

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}(\overline{D}_{i},\overline{D}_{i}^{\prime})\circ\Phi_{j}=\sum_{i+j=n}\Phi_{i}\circ(D_{j},D_{j}^{\prime}),\text{ for }n\geq 0.

Observe that (
9
) holds automatically for
n=0
as
\Phi_{0}=\mathrm{id}
. For
n=1
, we get
(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})-(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})=\delta_{c}(\Phi_{1})
. This shows that the infinitesimals corresponding to equivalent deformations are cohomologous. Hence, they gives rise to the same cohomology class in
H^{2}_{c}(L,L)
.

Next, we consider rigidity of compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

5.3 Definition
.

(i) A deformation
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
is said to be trivial if it is equivalent to the undeformed one
(D,D^{\prime})
.

(ii) A compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
is said to be rigid if any deformation of
L
is trivial.

The following result provides a sufficient condition for the rigidity of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

5.4 Theorem
.

Let
L
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. If
H^{2}_{c}(L,L)=0
then
L
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
be any deformation of
L
given by

\displaystyle(D,D^{\prime})_{t}=(D,D^{\prime})+\sum_{i\geq p}t^{i}(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}),~\text{ for some }p\geq 1.

Then we know that the first nonzero term
(D_{p},D_{p}^{\prime})
is a
2
-cocycle. By the hypothesis, we have
(D_{p},D_{p}^{\prime})=\delta_{c}(\Phi_{p})
, for some
\Phi_{p}\in C^{1}_{c}(L,L)=\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)
. We consider
\Phi_{t}=\mathrm{id}+t^{p}\Phi_{p}\in\mathrm{Coder}^{0}(SV)[[t]]
, and define

\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}=\Phi_{t}^{-1}\circ(D,D^{\prime})_{t}\circ\Phi_{t}.

Then
\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}
is a deformation equivalent to
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
, and of the form

\displaystyle\overline{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}=(D,D^{\prime})+\sum_{i\geq p+1}t^{i}(\overline{D}_{i},\overline{D}_{i}^{\prime}).

By repeating the same process, we can show that
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
is equivalent to
(D,D^{\prime})
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Finite order deformations.
Here we consider finite order deformations of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra and their extensions. Let
L=(L,\{l_{k}\}_{k\geq 1},\{l_{k}^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 1})
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra given by a Maurer-Cartan elements
(D,D^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}_{c}(SV)
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathrm{Coder}^{\bullet}_{c}(SV),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket)
.

5.5 Definition
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be a natural number. An order
N
deformation of
L
consists of a finite polynomial

\displaystyle(D,D^{\prime})_{t}=(D_{0},D_{0}^{\prime})+t(D_{1},D_{1}^{\prime})+\cdots+t^{N}(D_{N},D_{N}^{\prime})\text{ in }\text{ Coder}^{1}_{c}(SV)[[t]]/(t^{N+1})

(with
(D_{0},D_{0}^{\prime})=(D,D^{\prime})
) that satisfies
\llbracket(D,D^{\prime})_{t},(D,D^{\prime})_{t}\rrbracket=0
(
mod
t^{N+1}
).

Thus, in an order
N
deformation, the equations in (
8
) hold for
n=0,1,\ldots,N
. These equations can be equivalently written as

(10)

\displaystyle\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),(D_{n},D_{n}^{\prime})\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n;i,j\geq 1}\llbracket(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}),(D_{j},D_{j}^{\prime})\rrbracket,~\text{ for }n=0,1,\ldots,N.

5.6 Definition
.

An order
N
deformation
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}t^{i}(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime})
is said to be extensible if there exists an element
(D_{N+1},D_{N+1}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Coder}^{1}_{c}(SV)
such that
\widetilde{(D,D^{\prime})}_{t}=(D,D^{\prime})_{t}+t^{N+1}(D_{N+1},D_{N+1}^{\prime})=\sum_{i=0}^{N+1}t^{i}(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime})
is an order
N+1
deformation.

Thus, together with equations in (
10
), one more equation need to be satisfied, namely,

(11)

\displaystyle\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),(D_{N+1},D_{N+1}^{\prime})\rrbracket=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1;i,j\geq 1}\llbracket(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}),(D_{j},D_{j}^{\prime})\rrbracket.

The right hand side of the above equation does not contain the term
(D_{N+1},D_{N+1}^{\prime})
. Therefore, it depends only on the given orden
N
deformation
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
. It is called the obstruction to extend the deformation
(D,D^{\prime})_{t}
.

5.7 Proposition
.

The obstruction is a
3
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
L
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\delta_{c}\big(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1;i,j\geq 1}\llbracket(D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}),(D_{j},D_{j}^{\prime})\rrbracket\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1;i,j\geq 1}\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),\llbracket(D_{i},D^{\prime}_{i}),(D_{j},D^{\prime}_{j})\rrbracket\rrbracket

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1;i,j\geq 1}\big(\llbracket\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),(D_{i},D^{\prime}_{i})\rrbracket,(D_{j},D^{\prime}_{j})\rrbracket~-~\llbracket(D_{i},D^{\prime}_{i}),\llbracket(D,D^{\prime}),(D_{j},D^{\prime}_{j})\rrbracket\rrbracket\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+j=N+1;i_{1},i_{2},j\geq 1}\llbracket\llbracket(D_{i_{1}},D^{\prime}_{i_{1}}),(D_{i_{2}},D^{\prime}_{i_{2}})\rrbracket,(D_{j},D^{\prime}_{j})\rrbracket

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i+j_{1}+j_{2}=N+1;i,j_{1},j_{2}\geq 1}\llbracket(D_{i},D^{\prime}_{i}),\llbracket(D_{j_{1}},D^{\prime}_{j_{1}}),(D_{j_{2}},D^{\prime}_{j_{2}})\rrbracket\rrbracket\qquad(\text{form }(\ref{fin-def-eq}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j+k=N+1;i,j,k\geq 1}\llbracket\llbracket(D_{i},D^{\prime}_{i}),(D_{j},D^{\prime}_{j})\rrbracket,(D_{k},D^{\prime}_{k})\rrbracket=0.

Hence the proof.
∎

Therefore, it follows that the obstruction induces a cohomology class in
H^{3}_{c}(L,L)
. This is called the obstruction class. As a consequence of (
11
), we get the following.

5.8 Theorem
.

A finite order deformation of a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra
L
is extensible if and only if the corresponding class in
H^{3}_{c}(L,L)
is trivial.

5.9 Corollary
.

Let
L
be a compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. If
H^{3}_{c}(L,L)=0
then any finite order deformation of
L
is extensible.

Classification of some compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section, we will consider compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras whose underlying graded vector space is concentrated in degrees
-1
and
0
. We call them
2
-term compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras. We first recall
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras
[
2
]
.

6.1 Definition
.

(i) A
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a tuple
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
consisting of a graded vector space
L_{-1}\oplus L_{0}
(concentrated in degrees
-1
and
0
) together with a linear map
l_{1}:L_{-1}\rightarrow L_{0}
, skew-symmetric bilinear maps
l_{2}:L_{i}\times L_{j}\rightarrow L_{i+j}
(
-1\leq i,j,i+j\leq 0
) and a skew-symmetric trilinear map
l_{3}:L_{0}\times L_{0}\times L_{0}\rightarrow L_{-1}
satisfying for
w,x,y,z\in L_{0}
and
h,k\in L_{-1},

(12)

\displaystyle l_{1}(l_{2}(x,h))=l_{2}(x,l_{1}(h)),

(13)

\displaystyle l_{2}(l_{1}(h),k)=l_{2}(h,l_{1}(k)),

(14)

\displaystyle l_{1}(l_{3}(x,y,z))=-l_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),z)+l_{2}(l_{2}(x,z),y)+l_{2}(x,l_{2}(y,z)),

(15)

\displaystyle l_{3}(l_{1}(h),x,y)=-l_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),h)+l_{2}(l_{2}(x,h),y)+l_{2}(x,l_{2}(y,h)),

(16)

\displaystyle l_{2}(l_{3}(w,x,y),z)+l_{2}(l_{3}(w,y,z),x)-l_{2}(l_{3}(w,x,z),y)-l_{2}(l_{3}(x,y,z),w)=l_{3}(l_{2}(w,x),y,z)

\displaystyle-l_{3}(l_{2}(w,y),x,z)+l_{3}(l_{2}(w,z),x,y)+l_{3}(l_{2}(x,y),w,z)-l_{3}(l_{2}(x,z),w,y)+l_{3}(l_{2}(y,z),w,x).

(ii) Let
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
and
(M_{-1}\oplus M_{0},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
be
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras. A morphism between them is a triple
(f_{-1},f_{0},f)
consisting of linear maps
f_{-1}:L_{-1}\rightarrow M_{-1}
and
f_{0}:L_{0}\rightarrow M_{0}
, and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
f:L_{0}\times L_{0}\rightarrow M_{-1}
satisfying for
x,y,z\in L_{0}
and
h\in L_{-1}
,

(17)

\displaystyle f_{0}\circ l_{1}=m_{1}\circ f_{-1},

(18)

\displaystyle m_{1}(f(x,y))=f_{0}(l_{2}(x,y))-m_{2}(f_{0}(x),f_{0}(y)),

(19)

\displaystyle f(x,l_{1}(h))=f_{-1}(l_{2}(x,h))-m_{2}(f_{0}(x),f_{-1}(h)),

(20)

\displaystyle m_{2}(f(x,y),f_{0}(z))+f(l_{2}(x,y),z)+f_{-1}(l_{3}(x,y,z))=m_{3}(f_{0}(x),f_{0}(y),f_{0}(z))

\displaystyle+m_{2}(f_{0}(x),f(y,z))+m_{2}(f(x,z),f_{0}(y))+f(x,l_{2}(y,z))+f(l_{2}(x,z),y)).

6.2 Definition
.

A
2
-term compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a tuple
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
in which
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
and
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3})
are
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras satisfying for
w,x,y,z\in L_{0}
and
h,k\in L_{-1},

(21)

\displaystyle l_{1}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h))+l^{\prime}_{1}(l_{2}(x,h))=l_{2}(x,l^{\prime}_{1}(h))+l^{\prime}_{2}(x,l_{1}(h)),

(22)

\displaystyle l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{1}(h),k)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{1}(h),k)=l_{2}(h,l^{\prime}_{1}(k))+l^{\prime}_{2}(h,l_{1}(k)),

(23)

\displaystyle l_{1}(l^{\prime}_{3}(x,y,z))+l^{\prime}_{1}(l_{3}(x,y,z))=-l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),z)-l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),z)+l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,z),y)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{2}(x,z),y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+l_{2}(x,l^{\prime}_{2}(y,z))+l^{\prime}_{2}(x,l_{2}(y,z)),

(24)

\displaystyle l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{1}(h),x,y)+l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{1}(h),x,y)=-l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),h)-l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),h)+l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h),y)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{2}(x,h),y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+l_{2}(x,l^{\prime}_{2}(y,h))+l^{\prime}_{2}(x,l_{2}(y,h)),

(25)

\displaystyle l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{3}(w,x,y),z)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{3}(w,x,y),z)+l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{3}(w,y,z),x)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{3}(w,y,z),x)-l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{3}(w,x,z),y)

\displaystyle-l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{3}(w,x,z),y)-l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{3}(x,y,z),w)-l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{3}(x,y,z),w)=l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(w,x),y,z)+l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(w,x),y,z)

\displaystyle-l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(w,y),x,z)-l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(w,y),x,z)+l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(w,z),x,y)+l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(w,z),x,y)+l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),w,z)

\displaystyle+l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(x,y),w,z)-l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,z),w,y)-l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(x,z),w,y)+l_{3}(l^{\prime}_{2}(y,z),w,x)+l^{\prime}_{3}(l_{2}(y,z),w,x).

.

Let
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})

L_{\infty}

) - (

) is equivalent to the fact that

-term

.

Let
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
and
(M_{-1}\oplus M_{0},\{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\},\{m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2},m^{\prime}_{3}\})

.

6.6 Definition
.

A
2

Crossed modules of compatible Lie algebras and strict compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this subsection, we introduce crossed module of compatible Lie algebras and show that they are closely related to strict compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

6.7 Definition
.

A crossed module of compatible Lie algebras is tuple
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},t,t^{\prime},\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
consisting of compatible Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
and
\mathfrak{h}=(\mathfrak{h},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{h}},[~,~]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}})
, homomorphisms
t:(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])\rightarrow(\mathfrak{h},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
t^{\prime}:(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]^{\prime})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{h},[~,~]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}})
of Lie algebras, and bilinear maps
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying for
m,n\in\mathfrak{g}
and
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(i)

t(\alpha(x,m))=[x,t(m)]_{\mathfrak{h}}
,

t^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}(x,m))=[x,t^{\prime}(m)]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}}
,

t(\alpha^{\prime}(x,m))+t^{\prime}(\alpha(x,m))=[x,t^{\prime}(m)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[x,t(m)]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}},

(ii)

\alpha(t(m),n)=[m,n]
,

\alpha^{\prime}(t^{\prime}(m),n)=[m,n]^{\prime}
,

\alpha(t^{\prime}(m),n)+\alpha^{\prime}(t(m),n)=\alpha(m,t^{\prime}(n))+\alpha^{\prime}(m,t(n))
,

(iii)

\alpha(x,[m,n])=[\alpha(x,m),n]+[m,\alpha(x,n)],

\alpha^{\prime}(x,[m,n]^{\prime})=[\alpha^{\prime}(x,m),n]^{\prime}+[m,\alpha^{\prime}(x,n)]^{\prime},

\alpha(x,[m,n]^{\prime})+\alpha^{\prime}(x,[m,n])=[\alpha(x,m),n]^{\prime}+[\alpha^{\prime}(x,m),n]+[m,\alpha(x,n)]^{\prime}+[m,\alpha^{\prime}(x,n)],

(iv)

\alpha([x,y],m)=\alpha(x,\alpha(y,m))-\alpha(y,\alpha(x,m)),

\alpha^{\prime}([x,y]^{\prime},m)=\alpha^{\prime}(x,\alpha^{\prime}(y,m))-\alpha^{\prime}(y,\alpha^{\prime}(x,m)),

\alpha([x,y]^{\prime},m)+\alpha^{\prime}([x,y],m)=\alpha(x,\alpha^{\prime}(y,m))+\alpha^{\prime}(x,\alpha(y,m))-\alpha(y,\alpha^{\prime}(x,m))-\alpha^{\prime}(y,\alpha(x,m))
.

6.8 Theorem
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between strict compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras and crossed module of compatible Lie algebras.

Proof.

Let
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l_{1},l_{2},0\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},0\})
be a strict compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Since
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l_{1},l_{2},0)
is a strict
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
2
]
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}=L_{-1}
and
\mathfrak{h}=L_{0}
both carries Lie brackets given by

\displaystyle[h,k]:=l_{2}(l_{1}(h),k)=l_{2}(h,l_{1}(k)),~\text{ for }h,k\in L_{-1}~~~\text{ and }~~~[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}:=l_{2}(x,y),~\text{ for }x,y\in L_{0}.

Similarly,
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},0)
is a strict
L_{\infty}
-algebra implies that
\mathfrak{g}=L_{-1}
and
\mathfrak{h}=L_{0}
equipped with another Lie brackets

\displaystyle[h,k]^{\prime}:=l_{2}^{\prime}(l^{\prime}_{1}(h),k)=l^{\prime}_{2}(h,l^{\prime}_{1}(k)),\text{ for }h,k\in L_{-1}~~~\text{ and }~~~[x,y]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}}:=l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),~\text{ for }x,y\in L_{0}.

The compatibility conditions (
23
) and (
24
) implies that
(\mathfrak{g}=L_{-1},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
and
(\mathfrak{h}=L_{0},[~,~]_{\mathfrak{h}},[~,~]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}})
are compatible Lie algebras. Finally, we define maps

\displaystyle t=l_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h},~~~~t^{\prime}=l^{\prime}_{1}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h},~~~~\alpha=l_{2}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}~~~\text{ and }~~~\alpha^{\prime}=l^{\prime}_{2}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}.

Then it is easy to verify that
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},t,t^{\prime},\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
is a crossed module of compatible Lie algebras.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},t,t^{\prime},\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
be a crossed module of compatible Lie algebras. Then it is easy to verify that
(\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}}_{-1}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak{h}}_{0},\{l_{1},l_{2},0\},\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},0\})
is a strict compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{1}=t,~~~~l_{2}(x,y)=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}},~~~~l_{2}(x,h)=-l_{2}(h,x):=\alpha(x,h),~\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{h},~h\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{1}=t^{\prime},~~~~l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y)=[x,y]^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{h}},~~~~l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h)=-l^{\prime}_{2}(h,x):=\alpha^{\prime}(x,h),~\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{h},~h\in\mathfrak{g},

Finally, the above two constructions are inverses to each other. This completes the proof.

Skeletal compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras

6.9 Theorem
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between skeletal compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras and tuples
(\mathfrak{g},V,(\theta,\theta+\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}))
, where
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
is a compatible Lie algebra,
V=(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a representation and
(\theta,\theta+\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\in C^{3}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a
3
-cocycle induced by compatible Lie algebra cocycles.

Proof.

Let
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{0,l_{2},l_{3}\},\{0,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
be a skeletal compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Since
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},0,l_{2},l_{3})
is a skeletal
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
2
]
, it follows from (
14
) that
\mathfrak{g}=L_{0}
is a Lie algebra with the bracket
[x,y]:=l_{2}(x,y)
, for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}=L_{0}
. Moreover, if follows from (
15
) that
V=L_{-1}
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
with the action map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
,
\rho(x)(h):=l_{2}(x,h)
, for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h\in V
. Finally, the condition (
16
) says that the skew-symmetric trilinear map
l_{3}:L_{0}\times L_{0}\times L_{0}\rightarrow L_{-1}
(i.e.
l_{3}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g},V)
) is a
3
-cocycle in the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho)
, i.e.
\delta(l_{3})=0
. Similarly,
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},0,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3})
is a skeletal
L_{\infty}
-algebra implies that
(\mathfrak{g}=L_{0},[~,~]^{\prime})
is a Lie algebra,
(V=L_{-1},\rho^{\prime})
is a representation, where

\displaystyle[x,y]^{\prime}:=l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\rho^{\prime}(x)(h)=l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h),~\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}=L_{0}\text{ and }h\in V=L_{-1}.

And the skew-symmetric trilinear map
l^{\prime}_{3}:L_{0}\times L_{0}\times L_{0}\rightarrow L_{-1}
(i.e.
l_{3}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g},V)
) is a
3
-cocycle in the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~]^{\prime})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho^{\prime})
, i.e.
\delta^{\prime}(l^{\prime}_{3})=0
.

Finally, the condition (
23
) implies that
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
is a compatible Lie algebra, the condition (
24
) implies that
(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
is a representation of the compatible Lie algebra. Lastly, the condition (
25
) is equivalent to
\delta(l^{\prime}_{3})+\delta^{\prime}(l_{3})=0
. Therefore, by Remark
2.3
, we have
\delta_{c}(l_{3},l_{3}+l^{\prime}_{3},l^{\prime}_{3})=0
. As a summary, we get a required triple
(\mathfrak{g},V,(l_{3},l_{3}+l^{\prime}_{3}+l^{\prime}_{3}))
.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},V,(\theta,\theta+\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}))
be a triple consisting of a compatible Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],[~,~]^{\prime})
, a representation
V=(V,\rho,\rho^{\prime})
and a
3
-cocycle
(\theta,\theta+\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})\in C^{3}_{c}(\mathfrak{g},V)
induced by compatible Lie algebra cocycles. Then it is easy to verify that
(\underbrace{V}_{-1}\oplus\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\{0,l_{2},l_{3}\},\{0,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
is a skeletal compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

\displaystyle l_{2}(x,y):=[x,y],~~~~l_{2}(x,h)=-l_{2}(h,x)=\rho(x)(h),~~~~l_{3}=\theta,

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y):=[x,y]^{\prime},~~~~l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h)=-l^{\prime}_{2}(h,x)=\rho^{\prime}(x)(h),~~~~l^{\prime}_{3}=\theta^{\prime},

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h\in V
. The above two correspondences are inverses to each other. Hence the proof.
∎

7
Compatible Lie
2
-algebras

In this section, we introduce compatible Lie
2
-algebras as the categorification of compatible Lie algebras. We show that the category of compatible Lie
2
-algebras is equivalent to category
{\bf 2CompL^{1}_{\infty}}
introduced in Section
6
(Notation
6.5
).

Like a (compatible) Lie algebra has an underlying vector space, a (compatible) Lie
2
-algebra has an underlying
2
-vector space. A
2
-vector space is basically a category internal to the category
Vect
of vector spaces. More precisely, a
2
-vector space
\mathcal{C}
is a category with a vector space of objects
\mathcal{C}_{0}
and a vector space of morphisms
\mathcal{C}_{1}
such that the source and target maps
s,t:\mathcal{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{0}
, the identity assigning map
i:\mathcal{C}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{1}
, and the composition map
\circ:C_{1}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{1}
are all linear maps. Given
2
-vector spaces
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, a morphism
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is just a functor in the category
Vect
.

7.1 Definition
.

[
2
]
A Lie
2
-algebra is a
2
-vector space
\mathcal{C}
together with a skew-symmetric bilinear functor
[~,~]:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
and a skew-symmetric trilinear natural isomorphism (called the Jacobiator)

\displaystyle J_{x,y,z}:[[x,y],z]\rightarrow[[x,z],y]+[x,[y,z]],\text{ for }x,y,z\in\mathcal{C}_{0}

which makes the following diagram commutative: for

,

\textstyle{[[[w,x],y],z]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J_{[w,x],y,z}}
\scriptstyle{[J_{w,x,y},z]}
\textstyle{[[[w,y],x],z]+[[[w,[x,y]],z]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J_{[w,y],x,z}+J_{w,[x,y],z}}
\textstyle{[[[w,x],z],y]+[[w,x],[y,z]]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[J_{w,x,z},y]+J_{w,x,[y,z]}}
\textstyle{{\begin{subarray}{c}[[[w,y],z],x]+[[w,y],[x,z]]\\
~~~+[[w,z],[x,y]]+[w,[[x,y],z]]\end{subarray}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

A Lie

-algebra as above may be denoted by the triple

.

Let

In the following, we introduce compatible Lie

7.3 Definition
.

A compatible Lie
2
-algebra is a tuple
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J,[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime},J^{c})
in which
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J)
and
(\mathcal{C},[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime})
are Lie

-algebras and

is a trilinear skew-symmetric natural isomorphism

\displaystyle J^{c}_{x,y,z}:[[x,y],z]^{\prime}+[[x,y]^{\prime},z]\rightarrow[[x,z],y]^{\prime}+[[x,z]^{\prime},y]+[x,[y,z]]^{\prime}+[x,[y,z]^{\prime}]

which makes the triple
(\mathcal{C},[~,~]+[~,~]^{\prime},J+J^{\prime}+J^{c})
into a Lie
2
-algebra.

7.4 Definition
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J,[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime},J^{c})
and
(\mathcal{D},\lfloor~,~\rfloor,K,\lfloor~,~\rfloor^{\prime},K^{\prime},K^{c})
be two compatible Lie
2
-algebras. A morphism between them is a triple
(F,G,G^{\prime})
consisting of a linear funtor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
on the underlying
2
-vector spaces, and two skew-symmetric bilinear natural transformations

\displaystyle G_{x,y}:\lfloor F(x),F(y)\rfloor\rightarrow F[x,y]~~~~\text{ and }~~~G^{\prime}_{x,y}:\lfloor F(x),F(y)\rfloor^{\prime}\rightarrow F[x,y]^{\prime}

such that
(F,G)
is a morphism of Lie
2
-algebras from
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J)
to
(\mathcal{D},\lfloor~,~\rfloor,K)
, and
(F,G^{\prime})
is a morphism of Lie
2
-algebras from
(\mathcal{C},[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime})
to
(\mathcal{D},\lfloor~,~\rfloor^{\prime},K^{\prime}).

Compatible Lie
2
-algebras and morphisms between them forms a category. We denote this category by
CompLie2
.

7.5 Theorem
.

The categories
2CompL
{}^{1}_{\infty}
and
CompLie2
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
L=(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l,l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})
be an element in
2CompL
{}^{1}_{\infty}
. We define a
2
-vector space
\mathcal{C}
whose set of objects
\mathcal{C}_{0}=L_{0}
and the set of morphisms
\mathcal{C}_{1}=L_{-1}\oplus L_{0}
, and structure maps

\displaystyle s(h,x)=x,~~~~t(h,x)=l(h)+x~~\text{ and }~~i(x)=(0,x).

Define a skew-symmetric bilinear functor
[~,~]:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
and a skew-symmetric trilinear natural isomorphism
J
by

\displaystyle[(h,x),(k,y)]:=(l_{2}(h,y)+l_{2}(x,k)+l_{2}(dh,k),~l_{2}(x,y))~~\text{ and }~~J_{x,y,z}:=\big(l_{3}(x,y,z),~l_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),z)\big).

Since
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l,l_{2},l_{3})
is a
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra, it follows from
[
2
]
that
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J)
is a Lie
2
-algebra. Similarly,
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3})
is a
2
-term
L_{\infty}
-algebra implies that
(\mathcal{C},[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime})
is a Lie
2
-algebra, where

\displaystyle[(h,x),(k,y)]^{\prime}:=(l^{\prime}_{2}(h,y)+l^{\prime}_{2}(x,k)+l^{\prime}_{2}(dh,k),~l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y))~~\text{ and }~~J^{\prime}_{x,y,z}:=\big(l^{\prime}_{3}(x,y,z),~l^{\prime}_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),z)\big).

Finally, we define another skew-symmetric trilinear natural isomorphism
J^{c}
by

\displaystyle J^{c}_{x,y,z}:=(l_{3}(x,y,z)+l^{\prime}_{3}(x,y,z),~l_{2}(l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y),z)+l^{\prime}_{2}(l_{2}(x,y),z)).

By various conditions (
21
)-(
25
) of the compatibility of the
L_{\infty}
-algebras
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l,l_{2},l_{3})
and
(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},l,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3})
, we deduce that
(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J,[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime},J^{c})
is a compatible Lie
2
-algebra. We denote this compatible Lie
2
-algebra by
\Phi(L).

Next, let
(f_{-1},f_{0},f):\underbrace{(L_{-1}\oplus L_{0},\{l,l_{2},l_{3}\},\{l,l^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{3}\})}_{L}\rightsquigarrow\underbrace{(M_{-1}\oplus M_{0},\{m,m_{2},m_{3}\},\{m,m^{\prime}_{2},m^{\prime}_{3}\})}_{M}
be a morphism of
2
-term compatible
L_{\infty}
-algebras. We define a morphism
(F,G,G^{\prime}):\Phi(L)\rightsquigarrow\Phi(M)
of compatible Lie

\displaystyle F((h,x))=(f_{-1}(h),f_{0}(x)),~~G_{x,y}=(f(x,y),~l_{2}(f_{0}(x),f_{0}(y)))~~\text{ and }~~G^{\prime}_{x,y}=(f(x,y),~l^{\prime}_{2}(f_{0}(x),f_{0}(y))).

One can also show that such construction preserves identity morphisms and the composition of morphisms. Thus, we obtain a functor
\Phi:{\bf 2CompL^{1}_{\infty}}\rightarrow{\bf CompLie2}
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J,[~,~]^{\prime},J^{\prime},J^{c})
be a compatible Lie
2
-algebra. We define two vector spaces
L_{-1},L_{0}
and a linear map

\displaystyle L_{-1}=\mathrm{ker}(s),~~~L_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}~~~\text{ and }~~~l=t|_{\mathrm{ker}(s)}.

Define a skew-symmetric bilinear map

L_{\infty}

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{2}(x,y)=[x,y]^{\prime},~~~l^{\prime}_{2}(x,h)=-l^{\prime}_{2}(h,x)=[i(x),h]^{\prime}~~\text{ and }~~l^{\prime}_{3}(x,y,z)=\mathrm{pr}_{1}(J^{\prime}_{x,y,z}).

(\mathcal{C},[~,~],J)

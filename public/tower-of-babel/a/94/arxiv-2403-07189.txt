A multiscale cavity method for sublinear-rank symmetric matrix factorization
Jean Barbier 1 , Justin Ko 2 , and Anas A. Rahman 1

Abstract
We consider a statistical model for symmetric matrix factorization with additive Gaussian noise in the high-dimensional regime where the rank M M of the signal matrix to infer scales with its size N N as M = o ⁡ ( N 1 / 10 ) M={\rm o}(N^{1/10}) . Allowing for an N N -dependent rank offers new challenges and requires new methods. Working in the Bayes-optimal setting, we show that whenever the signal has i.i.d. entries the limiting mutual information between signal and data is given by a variational formula involving a rank- one replica symmetric potential. In other words, from the information-theoretic perspective, the case of a (slowly) growing rank is the same as when M = 1 M=1 (namely, the standard spiked Wigner model). The proof is primarily based on a novel multiscale cavity method allowing for growing rank along with some information-theoretic identities on worst noise for the Gaussian vector channel. We believe that the cavity method developed here will play a role in the analysis of a broader class of inference and spin models where the degrees of freedom are large arrays instead of vectors.

Introduction

Reconstructing a low-rank signal matrix observed through a noisy channel with highest possible accuracy is of fundamental importance across a range of disciplines including statistical inference, machine learning, signal processing, and information theory. This task encompasses a variety of models that have attracted considerable attention over the last decade, such as sparse principal components analysis and

Focusing on the Bayesian viewpoint, various applications of (the adaptive) interpolation methods, the cavity method, and the Approximate Message Passing (AMP) algorithm have been used to study the spiked Wigner model and its generalizations. Studies commenced in the rank

II
Setting and main result

We study the free entropy
F_{N}(\lambda)
(
2
) of the spiked Wigner model (
1
) in the
N\to\infty
regime with rank
M={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
and i.i.d. signal
\bm{X}_{0}=(\bm{x}_{0,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{M})_{i\leq N}=(\bm{x}_{0,ij}\in\mathbb{R})_{i\leq N,j\leq M}
, i.e., the prior distribution
\pP_{X,N,M}
on
\bm{X}_{0}
fully factorizes:

\pP_{X,N,M}(\bm{X}_{0})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\pP_{X,M}(\bm{x}_{0,i})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\pP_{X}(\bm{x}_{0,ij}).

(5)

In addition, we take
\pP_{X}
to be a centered distribution with
D
-bounded support
:
\mathrm{supp}\,\pP_{X}\subseteq[-D,D]
for some
0\leq D<\infty
.

An application of the (non-rigorous) replica method
[
43
]
suggests that
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(\lambda)
is given by a variational formula written in terms of the rank-
M
replica symmetric potential
F^{\rm RS}_{M}:\mathcal{S}_{M}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
defined by (with
\mathcal{S}_{M}
the set of symmetric positive semidefinite
M\times M
matrices)

\displaystyle F^{\rm RS}_{M}(\bm{Q},\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{M}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\bm{x}_{0}}\ln Z_{M}^{\rm RS}-\frac{\lambda}{4M}\operatorname{Tr}\bm{Q}^{2},

(6)

\displaystyle Z_{M}^{\rm RS}

\displaystyle:=\int e^{\sqrt{\lambda}\bm{x}^{\intercal}\sqrt{\bm{Q}}\bm{z}+\lambda\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\bm{x}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}}\,d\pP_{X,M}(\bm{x}),

where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
is a standard Gaussian vector, and
\bm{x}_{0}\sim\pP_{X,M}

[
19
,
20
]
. However, we find that it is instead given by a formula in terms of the simpler rank-one analogue

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{z,x_{0}}\ln Z_{1}^{\rm RS}-\frac{\lambda}{4}q^{2},

(7)

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{\rm RS}

\displaystyle:=\int e^{\sqrt{\lambda q}zx+\lambda qx_{0}x-\frac{1}{2}\lambda qx^{2}}\,d\pP_{X}(x),

with
z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and
x_{0}\sim\pP_{X}
. Thus, we have as our main result that, under our hypotheses above in addition to a further hypothesis on the number of non-analytic points (phase transitions) in
\lambda
of
\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
, the
N\to\infty
limit of the free entropy
F_{N}(\lambda)
is given by the rank-one replica symmetric potential
F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
at its supremum over
q
.

Theorem 1

(Rank-one replica formula for the growing-rank spiked Wigner model)
.

Let
\mathbb{N}\ni M=M_{N}={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
, let the prior distribution on
\bm{X}_{0}
fully factorize according to (
5
), set
\rho:=\mathbb{E}_{\pP_{X}}X^{2}
, and take
\pP_{X}
to be a centered distribution with
D
-bounded support. Moreover, assume that
\pP_{X}
is such that for all
m\leq M
,
\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}F_{m}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
is analytic in
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
except for possibly one critical point
0<\lambda_{c,m}<\infty
. Then, the limiting free entropy (
2
) of the spiked Wigner model (
1
) is given in terms of the replica symmetric potential (
7
) by

\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N,M_{N}}(\lambda)=\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(8)

As a consequence we have the following formula for the minimum mean-square error for any
\lambda
such that the maximizer
q^{*}(\lambda):={\rm argmax}_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
is unique:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2}M_{N}}\mathbb{E}\|\bm{X}_{0}\bm{X}_{0}^{\intercal}-\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{0}\bm{X}_{0}^{\intercal}\mid\bm{Y}]\|_{\rm F}^{2}=\rho^{2}-q^{*}(\lambda)^{2}.

The statement on the minimum mean-square error follows from the I-MMSE formula
[
44
]
and the arguments of
[
14
]
.

We comment on our hypotheses:

•

We require
M={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
to achieve concentration of the overlap matrix
\bm{R}_{10}:=\frac{1}{N}\bm{X}^{\intercal}\bm{X}_{0}
w.r.t. the posterior law of the signal and so that error terms arising from the cavity computation vanish in the large
N
limit. We believe that this growth rate for the rank is not fundamental and is rather a limitation of the concentration proofs. We conjecture that Theorem
1
holds for
M=N^{\alpha},\alpha<1
.

•

Symmetry properties of
\pP_{X,N,M}
owing to its factorized nature encourage the thermal concentration of
\bm{R}_{10}
onto a scalar multiple of the identity, which will in turn induce an equivalence between
\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(qI_{M},\lambda)
and
\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
. The factorization of the prior is thus key to reduce the dimensionality of the variational formula.

•

Requiring
\pP_{X}
to have
D
-bounded support is not very restrictive and is only a technical constraint; prior distributions
\pP_{X}
with unbounded support can be treated by truncating them to be
D
-bounded, working in the finite
D
regime as in this paper, and then taking
D\to\infty
at the final stage, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
20
]
.

•

It is believed that
\lim_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(\lambda)
exhibits one phase transition for many natural choices of prior
\pP_{X}
, see
[
45
]
and references therein. This corresponds to a unique non-analytic point
\lambda
. We translate this assumption on the model onto one for the replica formula
\sup_{\bm{Q}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
.

Theorem
1
clearly manifests equivalence with its rank-one analogue, rigorously analyzed in
[
18
,
14
,
20
]
, due to the
M
-independence of the right-hand side of (

). Results of the same nature stating that “sub-linear rank behaves as finite rank” exist for mesoscopic spiked perturbations of random matrices
[
46
]
and for spherical integrals
[
22
]
. Our results complement these in the context of Bayesian inference.

In the remainder of this paper, we summarize four key ingredients that constitute a proof of Theorem
1
. In Section
III
, we show how the rank-
M
and rank-one replica symmetric potentials are related. Then, in Section
IV
, we show how an interpolation argument gives a lower bound on
F_{N}(\lambda)
. The corresponding upper bound is derived in Section

, wherein we show concentration of the overlap matrix
\bm{R}_{10}
and use this result to carry out a multiscale application of the cavity method prescribed by the Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme. Namely, we are able to treat sequences

indexed by
two
growing dimensions instead of one, as is usually the case.

For convenience of later analysis, it is helpful to re-express the replica symmetric potential in terms of the mutual information of the
M
-dimensional vector Gaussian channel:

F^{\rm RS}_{M}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=-\frac{1}{M}I(\bm{x}_{0};\sqrt{\lambda\bm{Q}}\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{z})\\
-\frac{\lambda}{4M}\|\bm{Q}-\rho I_{M}\|_{\rm F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda\rho^{2}}{4},

(9)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\rm F}
is the Frobenius norm,
\bm{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{M})
is standard Gaussian noise, and
\bm{x}_{0}\sim\pP_{X,M}
.

Remark 1

(Properties of the replica potential)
.

We are going to use that the mutual information
I(\bm{x}_{0};\sqrt{\lambda\bm{Q}}\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{z})
is, in a well-defined sense, a concave, non-decreasing monotone function of
\bm{Q}

[
44
,
47
]
, while
\|\bm{Q}-\rho I_{M}\|^{2}_{\rm F}
is a convex paraboloid with minimum at
\bm{Q}=\rho I_{M}
— the problem of maximizing
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
over
\bm{Q}
translates to one of comparing these two competing terms. As a function of
\lambda
,
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)-\lambda\rho^{2}/4
is non-increasing, monotone, continuous, and convex for each
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
. Thus, so too is
\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)-\lambda\rho^{2}/4
. This is true for any
M
including
M=1
.

III
From rank-
M
to rank-one potential

We link the supremum of the rank-
M
replica symmetric potential
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
to its rank-one equivalent through a series of intermediary results. The first two are of particular interest due to their being a statement on the worst possible additive Gaussian noise in a vector channel with i.i.d. inputs.

Lemma 1

(Trimming the off-diagonal part of the noise covariance reduces information)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
,
\bm{x}_{0}\sim\pP_{X,M}
,
x_{0}\sim\pP_{X}
,
\bm{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{M})
, and
z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. Then, for
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
,

I(\bm{x}_{0};\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{M}I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}}z).

(10)

This inequality is an equality if
\bm{\Sigma}
is diagonal.

Proof.

The chain rule for mutual information, the independence of the entries of
\bm{x}_{0},\bm{z}
, and the definition of conditional entropy implies that

I(\bm{x}_{0};\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{M}I(\bm{x}_{0,i};\bm{x}_{0,i}+(\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})_{i}).

As
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
, we may diagonalize it as
\bm{\Sigma}=\bm{O}\bm{\sigma}\bm{O}^{\intercal}
with
\bm{\sigma}={\rm diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{M})
. Then, we have in law that

(\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})_{i}\overset{\rm d}{=}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{M}O_{ij}^{2}\sigma_{j}\Big)^{1/2}z=\sqrt{\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}}z.

Observing also that
\bm{x}_{0,i}\overset{\rm d}{=}x_{0}
, we obtain the result.
∎

Corollary 1

(Worst Gaussian noise with fixed covariance trace)
.

Let
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
be such that
\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}\geq D^{2}
for each
1\leq i\leq M
and denote its normalized trace by
\sigma:=\operatorname{Tr}\bm{\Sigma}/M
. Then, in the setting of Lemma
1
, we have that

I(\bm{x}_{0};\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})\geq MI(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\sigma}z).

(11)

This inequality is an equality if
\bm{\Sigma}=\sigma I_{M}
.

Proof.

As
\pP_{X}
has
D
-bounded support, we have from
[
48
]
that
I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{t}z)
is a convex function of
t
for all
t\geq D^{2}
. Thus, the result follows from applying Jensen’s inequality to the RHS of (
10
) in order to bring the sum under the square root.
∎

Now, to maximize
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
over
\bm{Q}
, it is essential to note that we are allowed to restrict our search to points satisfying a certain criticality condition.

Lemma 2

(A criticality condition)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
and diagonalize
\mathcal{S}_{M}\ni\bm{Q}=\bm{O}\bm{q}\bm{O}^{\intercal}
with
\bm{q}={\rm diag}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{M})
. Then, any maximizer
\bm{Q}
of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
must be such that the following system of equations, solved in particular by any critical point of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
, is satisfied:

q_{i}^{1/2}\left(\bm{O}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\bm{x}_{0}}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}\bm{O}_{i}-q_{i}\right)=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq M,

(12)

where
\bm{O}_{i}
is the
i
th
column of the orthogonal matrix
\bm{O}
and

\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}:=\frac{1}{Z_{M}^{\rm RS}}\int\,\cdot\,e^{\sqrt{\lambda}\bm{x}^{\intercal}\sqrt{\bm{Q}}\bm{z}+\lambda\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\bm{x}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}}\,d\pP_{X,M}(\bm{x}).

(13)

In particular, if
q_{1},\ldots,q_{M}>0
, this system simplifies to

\bm{Q}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\bm{x}_{0}}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}(\bm{Q}),

(14)

which corresponds to the criticality condition
\nabla_{\bm{Q}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}=(0)
.

Proof.

Interpreting
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
as a function of the square roots of the eigenvalues of
\bm{Q}
at fixed eigenvectors and writing
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{M})
, the
\sqrt{\bm{q}}
-derivative of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
in the direction of
\bm{\gamma}
is

\nabla_{\bm{\gamma}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i}q_{i}^{1/2}\left(\bm{O}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\bm{x}_{0}}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}\bm{O}_{i}-q_{i}\right)

for any
\bm{\gamma}
, so the criticality condition (
12
) holds for any critical point. To discard the possibility of maxima on the boundary of
\mathcal{S}_{M}
that do not satisfy (
12
), note that being on the boundary means that at least one of the
q_{i}
, say
q_{1}
, must either be zero or tend to infinity. In the latter case, one can check from (
9
) that
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
tends to
-\infty
. In the former, we note that
\nabla_{(1,\ldots,1)}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
and
\nabla_{(1,-1,\ldots,-1)}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
only agree in sign if the system (
12
) is satisfied.
∎

Restricting our search to points satisfying the criticality condition (
12
) allows us to write

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}(\mathcal{I})}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda),

where
\mathcal{S}_{M}(\mathcal{I})
is the set of symmetric positive semidefinite
M\times M
matrices with eigenvalues in
\mathcal{I}\subseteq[0,\infty)
and we may tune
\mathcal{I}
by choosing to work in either the high or low SNR regime. This is enabled by the following lemma and corollary.

Lemma 3

(A continuity property)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
and define
E_{i}(\lambda):=\bm{O}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\bm{x}_{0}}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}\bm{O}_{i}
with
\bm{Q}
in (
13
) set to be the maximizer of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
. Then, for each
1\leq i\leq M
,
E_{i}(\lambda)
is continuous except possibly at
\lambda_{c,M}
and

E_{i}(0)=0,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}E_{i}(\lambda)=\rho.

Proof.

Continuity of
E_{i}(\lambda)
follows from applying Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem to
\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}
as specified by (
13
) — all potential discontinuities arise from the (unique by hypothesis) phase transition of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
at
\lambda=\lambda_{c,M}
. At
\lambda=0
, there is no coupling between
\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}_{0}
in (
13
), so

\mathbb{E}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}}\langle\bm{x}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}\mathbb{E}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}=\mathbf{0},

because
\pP_{X}
is centered. The
\lambda\to\infty
limit of
E_{i}(\lambda)
can be surmised from the finite rank analysis of
[
14
]
.
∎

We are now in position to show that when the SNR is sufficiently low or high, the eigenvalues of the maximizer of the potential can be restricted; this will soon be crucial in order to exploit certain convexity properties of parts of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
holding only for these subsets of
\mathcal{S}_{M}
.

Corollary 2

(Low and high SNR regimes)
.

In the setting of Lemma
3
with
\rho^{\prime}:=2\rho/3
,

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}[0,\rho]}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)

(15)

and, moreover, there exists
0<\lambda_{S},\lambda_{L}<\infty
such that

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}[0,\rho^{\prime}]}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda),&\lambda<\lambda_{S},\\
\sup\limits_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}[\rho^{\prime},\rho]}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda),&\lambda>\lambda_{L}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality together with the tower rule for conditional expectation (also known as the Nishimori identity, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
) shows that
\mathbb{E}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}
has eigenvalues bounded by
\rho
. By Lemma
2
, any
\bm{Q}
maximizing
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
must have eigenvalues zero or in agreement with those of
\mathbb{E}\langle\bm{x}\bm{x}_{0}^{\intercal}\rangle_{{\rm RS},M}
, so such a
\bm{Q}
must have eigenvalues in
[0,\rho]
. The existence of a suitable
\lambda_{S}
is established by observing that
E_{i}(0)=0
and
E_{i}(\lambda)
is continuous away from
\lambda_{c,M}
. Likewise, when
\lambda\to\infty
, the maximizer of
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
can be shown to have non-zero eigenvalues, so combining (
14
) with Lemma
3
establishes existence of
\lambda_{L}
.
∎

Now, since
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
, so too is
(\lambda\bm{Q})^{-1}
, which we denote as
\bm{\Sigma}
from here on out. It is then immediate that Lemma
1
induces decoupling in (
9
) when
\bm{\Sigma}
has the form
\sigma I_{M}
. This is indeed the form of
\bm{\Sigma}
for which
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
is maximized. We first treat the low and high SNR regimes separately.

Lemma 4

(Low SNR rank-one reduction)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
. For
\lambda<\min\{D^{-2}/\rho,\lambda_{S}\}
we have

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sup_{q\in[0,\rho^{\prime}]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(16)

Proof.

From (
9
), computing the left-hand side of (
16
) is equivalent to the minimization problem

\inf_{\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}\Big\{I(\bm{x}_{0};\bm{x}_{0}+\bm{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{z})+\frac{\lambda}{4}\|\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}/\lambda-\rho I_{M}\|^{2}_{\rm F}\Big\},

where
\bm{\Sigma}:=(\lambda\bm{Q})^{-1}
. As
\lambda<\lambda_{S}
, Corollary
2
says that the eigenvalues, hence diagonal entries
\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}=\lambda^{-1}\sum_{j}\bm{O}_{ij}^{2}\tilde{q}_{j}
(where we write
\tilde{q}_{i}=q_{i}^{-1}
for the inverses of the eigenvalues of
\bm{Q}
), are bounded below by
1/(\lambda\rho^{\prime})
. Since we also have that
\lambda<D^{-2}/\rho
, this means that
\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}>D^{2}\rho/\rho^{\prime}>D^{2}
. Thus, by Corollary
1
, the above infimum is bounded below by

\inf_{\tilde{q}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{q}_{M}\in[1/\rho^{\prime},\infty)}\Big\{MI(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\sigma}z)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{M}(1/\tilde{q}_{i}-\rho)^{2}\Big\},

where
\sigma:=\sum_{i\leq M}\tilde{q}_{i}/(\lambda M)
. The critical points (discarding the possibility of extrema on the boundary due to the same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
2
) of the above symmetric expression w.r.t. the
(\tilde{q}_{i})
occur at solutions of

\tilde{q}_{i}=\nu^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\sigma}z)\Big),\quad 1\leq i\leq M,

where
\nu(\tilde{q})=\lambda(\rho\tilde{q}-1)/(2\tilde{q}^{3})
is a bijection for
\tilde{q}\geq 1/\rho^{\prime}
(thus the choice of
\rho^{\prime}
). This system of equations is satisfied only if
\tilde{q}_{1}=\cdots=\tilde{q}_{M}
, at which point our infimum decouples over the
\tilde{q}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{q}_{M}
into

M\inf_{\tilde{q}\in[1/\rho^{\prime},\infty)}\Big\{I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\tilde{q}/\lambda}z)+\tfrac{\lambda}{4}(1/\tilde{q}-\rho)^{2}\Big\}.

Substituting this into the left-hand side of (
16
) and noting that the infimum is attained at
\bm{Q}=I_{M}/\tilde{q}
produces the right-hand side, as desired.
∎

Lemma 5

(High SNR rank-one reduction)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
. We have for
\lambda>\lambda_{L}
that

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sup_{q\in[\rho^{\prime},\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(17)

Proof.

We are interested in the same minimization problem as before, but notice that it is now bounded below by the infimum

\inf_{\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}[1/(\lambda\rho),1/(\lambda\rho^{\prime})]}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{M}I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}}z)\\
+\frac{\lambda M}{4}\Big(\frac{M}{\lambda\operatorname{Tr}\bm{\Sigma}}-\rho\Big)^{2}\Big\},

where we have used Lemma
1
to simplify the mutual information term and Jensen’s inequality to simplify the squared Frobenius norm — the latter term is a convex function of the eigenvalues of
\bm{\Sigma}
for
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}[1/(\lambda\rho),1/(\lambda\rho^{\prime})]
. Interpreting this as the infimum of a symmetric function of the diagonal entries
\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}
of
\bm{\Sigma}
and noting that
\partial_{\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}}I(x_{0};x_{0}+\sqrt{\bm{\Sigma}_{ii}}z)
is a monotone function, we see that this infimum is attained at
\bm{\Sigma}_{11}=\cdots=\bm{\Sigma}_{MM}
and it therefore decouples as in the proof of Lemma
4
so that the result follows.
∎

We point out that the proofs of lemmas
4
and
5
are somehow dual to each other. In comparing the mutual information to the squared Frobenius norm, we require one to be convex to form a trace and the other to have monotone derivative in order to force decoupling over the relevant parameters. In the low SNR regime, these parameters are the eigenvalues of
\lambda\bm{\Sigma}
, while in the high SNR regime, we work with the diagonal entries of
\bm{\Sigma}
. Finally, we use analytic continuation to extend these lemmas to the entire positive real line.

Proposition 1

(Rank-one reduction for all non-negative SNR)
.

Let
\pP_{X,M}=\pP_{X}^{\otimes M}
. We have for all
\lambda\geq 0
that

\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(18)

Proof.

That
\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
has one non-analytic point
\lambda_{c,M}
is a hypothesis of Theorem
1
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that the non-analytic point of
\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
is at
\lambda_{c,1}\leq\lambda_{c,M}
. Decreasing
\lambda_{S}
(increasing
\lambda_{L}
) if needed, we then have that these two suprema are analytic functions of
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)\setminus\{\lambda_{c,1},\lambda_{c,M}\}
that agree on
[0,\lambda_{S})\cup(\lambda_{L},\infty)
. Thus, by analytic continuation, they agree on
[0,\lambda_{c,1})\cup(\lambda_{c,M},\infty)
. By analyticity and Remark
1
, they must also agree on
(\lambda_{c,1},\lambda_{c,M})
, hence on all of
[0,\infty)
.
∎

IV
Free entropy lower bound: interpolation

The standard (finite rank) interpolation argument allows us to establish the desired lower bound on the free entropy
F_{N}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 2

(Free entropy lower bound)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, but with
M,N\geq 1
free, we have that

F_{N}(\lambda)\geq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(19)

Proof.

This argument is standard and can be found in
[
14
]
(apart from the rank-one reduction given by Proposition
1
used at the end). We recall the main ideas. First, we compare
F_{N}(\lambda)
to
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
by introducing an appropriate interpolating Hamiltonian
H_{N}(\bm{X},t)
such that (cf. (
6
))

\displaystyle H_{N}(\bm{X},0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda}\bm{x}_{i}^{\intercal}\sqrt{\bm{Q}}\bm{z}_{i}+\lambda\bm{x}_{0,i}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}_{i}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\bm{x}_{i}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}\bm{x}_{i},

\displaystyle H_{N}(\bm{X},1)

\displaystyle=H_{N}(\bm{X}),

where each
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
is the
i\textsuperscript{th}
row of
\bm{X}
and for
i=1,\ldots,N
, the
\bm{z}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{M})
are i.i.d. vectors. Letting
\varphi(t)
denote the corresponding interpolating free entropy, applying Gaussian integration by parts and the Nishimori identity shows that

\varphi^{\prime}(t)\geq-\frac{\lambda}{4M}\operatorname{Tr}\bm{Q}^{2}.

But then, for all
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
,

F_{N}(\lambda)-F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)=\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)+\frac{\lambda}{4M}\operatorname{Tr}\bm{Q}^{2}\geq 0.

Taking the supremum over
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}
and using Proposition
1
to replace said supremum then completes the proof.
∎

V
Free entropy upper bound:

the multiscale cavity method

The goal in this section is to show that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(\lambda)\leq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

Combining this result with Proposition
2
would then prove Theorem
1
. This bound is obtained via an adaptation of the cavity method that allows for increments in both
M
and
N
. An essential result for carrying out said cavity computation is the concentration of the overlap matrix.

V-A

Concentration of the overlap matrix

Due to the symmetries of our model, we are able to add small side information to induce concentration of the overlap matrix
\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}\ni\bm{R}_{10}:=\frac{1}{N}\bm{X}^{\intercal}\bm{X}_{0}
. This side information is negligible in the large
N
limit and is incorporated through the introduction of the perturbed Hamiltonian (cf. (
4
))

\displaystyle H_{N}^{(\varepsilon)}(\bm{X})

\displaystyle:=H_{N+1}(\bm{X})

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{Tr}\Big(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\bm{X}^{\intercal}\tilde{\bm{Z}}+\varepsilon\bm{X}^{\intercal}\bm{X}_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\bm{X}^{\intercal}\bm{X}\Big),

(20)

where
\tilde{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}
is a standard Ginibre matrix consisting of i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries independent of everything else and
\varepsilon\in[s_{N},2s_{N}]
is a perturbation parameter with
(s_{N})_{N\geq 1}
a decreasing sequence of real numbers in
(0,1)
that tend slowly to zero as
N\to\infty
; note the
(N+1)
-dependence of the first term in the perturbed Hamiltonian. The perturbation Hamiltonian (trace term above) can be understood as the log-likelihood corresponding to a side Gaussian channel
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\bm{X}_{0}+\tilde{\bm{Z}}
. The resulting
H_{N}^{(\varepsilon)}(\bm{X})
thus still corresponds to the log-likelihood of an inference problem in the Bayes-optimal setting and so-called Nishimori identities hold which, in turn, induce concentration-of-measure
[
15
,
16
]
. Note the particularly simple form of side information/perturbation used here compared to
[
15
,
20
,
21
]
. This is possible due the fact that in the cavity method we only need concentration w.r.t. the posterior law conditional on the data (
\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_{N,\varepsilon}
defined below) instead of the stronger statements proved in the aforementioned references that include concentration w.r.t. the data as well. Another key point of this simple perturbation is that it maintains the exchangeability of the variables
(\bm{x}_{ij})
under the quenched Gibbs measure
\mathbb{E}\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_{N,\varepsilon}
: no statistical symmetries present in the original model are broken by the perturbation apart from the trivial sign ambiguity
\bm{X}\to-\bm{X}
. These two points allow us to obtain concentration for higher regimes of rank than what we would obtain using more complicated perturbations. As a concrete consequence, we are able to reach
M={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
compared to the
M={\rm o}(N^{1/20})
obtained in
[
21
]
. Yet, there is room for improvement in order to reach
M=N^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha<1
, the regime for which we believe Theorem
1
to hold.

Let the partition function and Gibbs (posterior) average with respect to the perturbed Hamiltonian be

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{N,M}

\displaystyle:=\int e^{H_{N}^{(\varepsilon)}(\bm{X})}\,d\pP_{X,N,M}(\bm{X}),

\displaystyle\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle_{N,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\tilde{Z}_{N,M}}\int\,\cdot\,e^{H_{N}^{(\varepsilon)}(\bm{X})}\,d\pP_{X,N,M}(\bm{X}).

In analogy with (
2
), the perturbed free entropy reads

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)=\tilde{F}_{N,M}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{NM}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{Z},\bm{X}_{0}}\ln\tilde{Z}_{N,M}.

(21)

Proposition 3

(Overlap concentration)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on properties of
\pP_{X}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{N}}\int_{s_{N}}^{2s_{N}}d\varepsilon\,\mathbb{E}\langle\|\bm{R}_{10}-\langle\bm{R}_{10}\rangle_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{\rm F}^{2}\rangle_{N,\varepsilon}\leq\Gamma_{N,M}:=\frac{CM^{2}}{\sqrt{Ns_{N}}}.

This proposition follows from a modification of an argument of
[
20
,
15
]
, whereby one uses Gaussian intergration by parts and the Nishimori identity to observe that concentration of
\bm{R}_{10}
is governed by the concentration of the simpler auxiliary function
\mathcal{L}:=-N^{-1}\partial_{\varepsilon}H_{N}^{(\varepsilon)}(\bm{X})
.

The negligibility of our side information is made precise in the following lemma, which follows from an interpolation argument akin to the proof of Proposition
2
.

Lemma 6

(The perturbation is negligible)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, we have uniformly for
\varepsilon\in[s_{N},2s_{N}]
that

F_{N}(\lambda)\leq\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)+{\rm O}(s_{N}).

Due to this lemma, we may henceforth supplant our task of establishing an upper bound on
F_{N}(\lambda)
with that of bounding
\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)
, which enables use of Proposition
3
.

V-B

Multiscale cavity method and proof of Theorem
1

The main methodological novelty starts now. In the multiscale setting, the rank
M=M_{N}
grows as a function of
N
. Therefore, there are two growing interacting scales that need to be considered jointly: the row and column dimensions. It is thus no longer amenable to the standard Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme
[
39
,
14
]
. We show that in the multiscale setting, the free entropy can be expressed as a convex combination of a normalization of the limits of the finite differences

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(n,m)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n+1,m}-\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m},

\displaystyle\Delta_{M}(n,m)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m+1}-\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m}.

To simplify notation, we define
\Delta_{N}:=\Delta_{N}(N,M_{N+1})
and
\Delta_{M}:=\Delta_{M}(N,M_{N})
.
We now provide generic bounds for multiscale free entropies indexed by two growing dimensions. Here, the dependency between
M,N
is generic.

Theorem 2

(Multiscale Aizenman–Sims–Starr identity)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\gamma\geq 0
, and
M=M_{N}=\lfloor\alpha N^{\gamma}\rfloor
. Then,

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{N}}{M}+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N},

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{N}}{M}+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}.

This result is sharp in the sense that if the limits on the right hand side exist, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{N,M_{N}}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{N}}{M}+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}.

Proof.

We focus on proving the upper bound, since the lower bound will follow from obvious modifications. It also suffices to consider the case that
0<\gamma\leq 1
, since the case
\gamma=0
is the usual Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme and the
\gamma>1
case can be deduced from the case when
\gamma\leq 1
by interchanging the
N
and
M
indices.

Let
\epsilon>0
. We define

m_{n}=\lfloor\alpha n^{\gamma}\rfloor\text{ and }n_{m}=\lfloor\alpha^{-1}m^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\rfloor

to denote the
m
index as a function of
n
and the
n
index as a function of
m
; these dependencies are the same as between
M_{N}
and
N
. We begin as in the standard proof by expressing the log partition function as a (truncated) telescoping sum,

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}\Big(\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n+1,m_{n+1}}-\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m_{n}}\Big)\!+\!{\rm o}_{N}(T)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NM}\Big(\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}\tilde{\Delta}_{N}(n)+\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}\tilde{\Delta}_{M}(n)\Big)\!+\!{\rm o}_{N}(T),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{N}(n)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n+1,m_{n+1}}-\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m_{n+1}},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{M}(n)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m_{n+1}}-\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{n,m_{n}},

and the error term
o_{N}(T)
tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
for any fixed
T
.
Notice that
\tilde{\Delta}_{M}(n)=0
if
m_{n+1}=m_{n}
, so we only keep the terms in the second sum when the
m_{n}
coordinate increases. The truncation
T=T(\epsilon)
is chosen sufficiently large such that for all
n\geq T
,

\frac{\tilde{\Delta}_{N}(n)}{m_{n}}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{N}}{M}+\epsilon

and (considering only
n\geq T
for which
m_{n+1}=m_{n}+1
)

\frac{\tilde{\Delta}_{M}(n)}{n}\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}+\epsilon.

Substituting these bounds into the telescoping sum gives the upper bound

\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)\leq\Big(\frac{\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}m_{n}}{NM}\Big)\Big(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{N}}{M}+\epsilon\Big)\\
\quad+\Big(\frac{\sum_{m=M_{T}}^{M_{N-1}}n_{m}}{NM}\Big)\Big(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}+\epsilon\Big)+{\rm o}_{N}(T).

(22)

The desired upper bound now follows from the integral approximations

\frac{\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}m_{n}}{NM}\leq\int_{T}^{N}\frac{\alpha k^{\gamma}}{NM}\,dk=\frac{1}{1+\gamma}+{\rm o}_{N}(T)

and

\frac{\sum_{m=M_{T}}^{M_{N-1}}n_{m}}{NM}\leq\int_{M_{T}}^{M_{N-1}+1}\frac{\alpha^{-1}k^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{NM}\,dk=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}+{\rm o}_{N}(T).

Taking the limit superior of both sides of (
22
) and then taking
\epsilon\to 0
completes the proof of the upper bound.

It is worth mentioning that the integral approximations are asymptotically sharp in the sense that

\frac{\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}m_{n}}{NM}\geq\int_{T-1}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha k^{\gamma}}{NM}\,dk\asymp\frac{1}{1+\gamma}

and

\frac{\sum_{m=M_{T}}^{M_{N-1}}n_{m}}{NM}\geq\int_{M_{T}-1}^{M_{N-1}-1}\frac{\alpha^{-1}k^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{NM}\,dk\asymp\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma},

so the lower bound of the theorem follows from a similar argument to that just presented.
∎

Remarkably, thanks to the rank-one reduction described in Proposition
1
, it turns out that both of the limit suprema in the RHS of the upper bound in Theorem
2
reduce to the RHS of (
8
). Hence, so too does their displayed convex sum, as desired. This drastic simplification is however not expected to hold in the linear scaling
M=\Theta(N)
.

We have stated here the multiscale Aizenman–Sims–Starr identity in terms of the (perturbed) free entropy for convenience, but it actually holds for
any
generic sequence with two growing indices. Indeed, it is a general fact about sequences and uses no properties of the log partition function, as can be seen from the proof. The parameter
\alpha
does not appear in the convex combinations displayed in Theorem
2
, but it does appear in the
\Delta_{M}
terms and can thus have an effect on the limit of the differences. Furthermore, if
\tilde{F}_{N}
remains finite in the limit, then
\Delta_{N}
is an order
M
quantity and
\Delta_{M}
is an order
N
quantity, so the normalized differences in the spin and rank coordinates are both of constant order and both have non-negligible contribution to the limiting value. In other words, even if
M_{N}\ll N
so that adding a column occurs much less frequently than rows when the system size grows, the free entropy difference due to column addition is much larger than the one due to row addition because columns are of length
N
, while rows are of length
M
. These two effects perfectly compensate each other so that both row and column addition contributes the same order in the multiscale Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme.

The multiscale cavity method is a powerful tool because the limit of a two-indices sequence with nonlinear asymptotic growth rate can be computed by finding the limits of the differences where one of the coordinates is
fixed
, instead of having to deal with increments in both coordinates simultaneously. In other words, the
\Delta_{N}
and
\Delta_{M}
can be computed independently of one another to isolate the effects of adding one spin or adding one rank coordinate. In statistical physics language, this reduces the problem to computing a cavity in the spins for fixed rank dimension and a cavity in the rank for fixed spin dimension in the thermodynamic limit. The
\Delta_{N}
is the standard cavity studied in classical models, while
\Delta_{M}
is a term that only appears when examining multiscale models.

V-B
1
The
\Delta_{N}
cavity bound

Carrying out the standard Aizenman–Sims–Starr procedure
[
39
]
,
[
14
, Section 4.3]
, which relies crucially on the concentration of the overlap matrix given in Proposition
3
, yields the following.

Lemma 7

(
\Delta_{N}
cavity bound)
.

For
M,N\geq 1
and
\pP_{X,N,M}
fully factorized with
\rho<\infty
, we have that

\frac{\Delta_{N}(N,M)}{M}\leq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)+{\rm O}(M\Gamma_{N,M})+{\rm o}_{N}(1).

(23)

The error terms vanish as
N\to\infty
when
M={\rm o}(N^{1/6})
.

V-B
2
The
\Delta_{M}
cavity bound

Recall
\Delta_{M}:=\Delta_{M}(N,M_{N})
. We now present the analogue of Lemma
7
for
\Delta_{M}
with a shortened proof due to its novelty. We will do a comparison of a rank
M+1
and a rank
M
system. The rank one equivalence of the rank
M
model (after normalization) suggests to write

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{N,M_{N}+1}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{N,M_{N}}

\displaystyle\approx(M_{N}+1)\sup_{q}F_{1}^{\rm RS}-M_{N}\sup_{q}F_{1}^{\rm RS}=\sup_{q}F_{1}^{\rm RS}.

This intuition is the correct one and is formalized as follows.

Lemma 8

(
\Delta_{M}
cavity bound)
.

For
M_{N}={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
and
\pP_{X,N,M}
fully factorized with
\rho<\infty
, we have that

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}\leq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

Proof.

Define

\hat{\Delta}_{N}(n):=\Delta_{N}(n,M_{N}+1)

in order to track the increment in the
N
index with fixed
M_{N}+1
index. We treat the two terms in the definition of
\Delta_{M}=\Delta_{M}(N,M_{N})

separately. For the first, we consider a telescoping series over the
N
-index to see that

\frac{\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{N,M_{N}+1}}{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=T}^{N-1}\hat{\Delta}_{N}(n)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}\hat{\Delta}_{N}(n)\\
\leq\Big(1-\frac{T}{N}\Big)\sup_{n\geq T}\hat{\Delta}_{N}(n)+\frac{T}{N}\sup_{n<T}\hat{\Delta}_{N}(n),

where
T
is a truncation parameter to be decided.
Due to Lemma
7
, the first supremum is bounded above, up to an error term decreasing with
T
, by
(M_{N}+1)\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
. A cavity argument shows that the second supremum is bounded above by
CM_{N}^{2}
for some constant
C
depending only on
\lambda
and
\pP_{X}
. For the second term in the definition of
\Delta_{M}
, simply observe from Proposition
2
that

-\frac{\mathbb{E}\ln\tilde{Z}_{N,M_{N}}}{N}\leq-M_{N}\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

(24)

Summing the two bounds thus gives

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{M}}{N}\leq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)+{\rm error}(N,M_{N},T).

(25)

Taking
T=N^{4/5-\epsilon(\gamma)}
for some small constant
\epsilon(\gamma)
and
M_{N}=o(N^{1/10})
is sufficient for the combination of all error terms in (
25
) to vanish when taking the limit superior.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1

Combining the last two lemmas together with Theorem
2
(noting that setting
M=M_{N+1}
within (
23
) and multiplying both sides by
M_{N+1}/M_{N}
shows that
\Delta_{N}/M_{N}
is bounded above by
\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
plus error terms vanishing at large
N

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{N}(\lambda)\leq\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda).

Lemma
6
then shows that
\limsup_{N\to\infty}F_{N}(\lambda)
obeys the same upper bound. As this matches the lower bound of Proposition
2
, we are done.

VI
Conclusion

We studied the spiked Wigner model with growing rank
M={\rm o}(N^{1/10})
. We have derived results on worst additive Gaussian noise and used them to show equivalence between
\sup_{\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
and
\sup_{q\in[0,\rho]}F_{1}^{\rm RS}(q,\lambda)
. This reduction was used inside a standard interpolation argument, followed by the novel multiscale cavity method, in order to prove that the limiting free entropy remains the same as in the case of rank equal to one for signals with i.i.d. entries.

In terms of novelty, we used information-theoretic arguments to reduce our rank-
M
replica symmetric potential to its rank-one counterpart. In addition to yielding a tractable final result, this allowed us to apply a generalization of the Aizenman–Sims–Starr scheme. Using the cavity method instead of, say, the adaptive interpolation method
[
20
,
21
]
means that we required only posterior concentration of the overlap rather than full (quenched) concentration. This in turn enabled us to employ a scalar perturbation parameter (as opposed to a matrix), leading to more tractable computations and better rates of convergence.

The rank-one reduction follows from the factorized form of the prior and the induced exchangeability, implying that
F_{M}^{\rm RS}(\bm{Q},\lambda)
is maximized at a matrix proportional to the identity. This is similar to recent simplifications of vector spins Parisi formulae obtained in
[
36
,
37
]
through the utilization of underlying symmetries in the Potts spin glass. Combining these simplifications with our multiscale cavity method has the potential to extend the corresponding finite-rank vector spin models to sublinear-rank multiscale models, which is a natural step towards understanding the challenging extensive rank regime
M=\Theta(N)
, see
[
49
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
. In this vein, we foresee that the relative tractability of our computations will also enable us to push the theory of tensor factorization
[

,
19
,
20
,

,

,

]
into the growing rank regime.

Introduction

1.1.
Overview and main result

Consider a convex and coercive
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
. The aim of this paper is to prove Lipschitz regularity of the local minimisers of the standard integral of Calculus of Variations

(1.1)

J(u,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}F(Du)\,dx

i. e. of those
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
such that
F(Du)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and for any open
{\mathcal{O}}\Subset\Omega
it holds

J(u,{\mathcal{O}})\leq J(u+w,{\mathcal{O}})\qquad\forall\,w\in W^{1,1}_{0}({\mathcal{O}}).

The integrands
F
we are interested in satisfy for some
H<\infty

(1.2)

\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))\leq H\,\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\qquad\text{for a.\,e.\,$z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

where here and in the following
\lambda_{\rm max}(M)
and
\lambda_{\rm min}(M)
denote respectively the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix
M
. In this introductory paragraph, the integrands obeying (
1.2
) will be called
uniformly elliptic
(even if the term is ubiquitous and thus may cause some confusion). As will be clarified later, most of the available Lipschitz regularity results for local minimisers prescribe that the singular set, where
\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))
blows up, and the degeneracy set, where
\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))
vanishes, are both bounded. As will be clarified in the following discussion, condition (
1.2
) alone instead allows for both sets to be simultaneously unbounded (even dense) and sizeable (in the sense of Hausdorff dimension).

In order to motivate the uniform ellipticity condition (
1.2
), let us review some well known facts regarding local minimisers of (
1.1
).
If
F
fulfils a polynomial upper bound, these are finite energy solutions of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.3)

{\rm div}\,(DF(Du))=0

in
\Omega
.
Existence of minimisers is ensured by standard methods once a superlinearity condition on
F
at
\infty

is imposed, however well known examples (see
[
21
,
33
]
) show that under these sôle conditions (sub-polynomial growth and superlinearity) they may fail to be locally bounded. Regularity of local minimisers is a classical topic dating back to Hilbert’s XIX problem, solved through Schauder and DeGiorgi-Nash-Moser theories under the
strong ellipticity
assumption

0<\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))<\infty.

Since the solution of Hilbert’s problem, much effort has been dedicated to weaken this assumption. A natural quantity arising in the aforementioned regularity theories is the
ellipticity ratio
(also called the
linear dilatation
of
D^{2}F(z)
), namely

{\rm e}(z)=\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))}.

The closure of smooth, strongly elliptic integrands with respect to pointwise (or, equivalently,
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
) convergence, when coupled with a uniform bound on the ellipticity ratio, turns out to be a cone consisting of

•

Affine functions, which are the extremals of the cone

•

Convex superlinear
F\in C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{2,N}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
obeying (
1.2
) a. e.  for some
H<\infty

(see Corollary
2.7
).
The second type of integrands have been studied in the seminal papers
[
29
,
30
]
(to which we refer for further details) and are called

quasiuniformly convex

It turns out, quite conveniently we may say, that in dimension

\begin{split}{\rm Sing}_{F}&=\left\{\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\esslimsup_{z\to\bar{z}}\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))=\infty\right\},\\

If

Despite the natural appearance of q. u. c integrands as limits (in the sense described above) of strongly elliptic ones, the corresponding regularity theory for (

rather than on the actually controlled quantity

\sup_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(Du))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(Du))}

and a naive limiting argument is bound to fail.

Nevertheless, some classes of q. u. c. where the standard regularity theory can still produce results are already well known. An important type of q. u. c. integrands are for instance those with
Uhlenbeck structure
, i. e. depending only on the modulus of the gradient. Given a non-decreasing
G:[0,\infty[\to[0,\infty[
, the function

is q. u. c. if and only if
G\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap W^{2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and there exists a constant

such that

(1.6)

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{t\,G^{\prime\prime}(t)}{G^{\prime}(t)}\leq C\qquad\text{for a.\,e.\,$t>0$}.

In particular, for a q. u. c. function with Uhlenbeck structure, it always holds

(1.7)

{\rm Sing}_{F}\subseteq\{0\}\quad\text{as well as}\quad{\rm Deg}_{F}\subseteq\{0\}

which, since obviously
{\rm Sing}_{F}\cap{\rm Deg}_{F}=\emptyset
for a q. u. c.
F
, justifies the traditional dichotomy between the singular or degenerate case of (
1.3
).

The regularity theory for solutions of (
1.3
) when
F
is a Uhlenbeck q. u. c. integrand is well developed, even when non-homogenous terms with low summability are included and no variational setting it available. See e. g.
[
12
]
and the literature therein for a survey on the available results for solutions of

(1.8)

{\rm div}\,\left(\frac{G^{\prime}(|Du|)}{|Du|}\,Du\right)=f

up to the Lipschitz scale, depending on the summability properties of

. Regarding higher regularity, in
[
32
]
minimisers have been proved to be

for integrands which are even more general than the Uhlenbeck ones, but still fulfil a uniform

on the ellipticity ratio. By this we mean that the main assumption for the

regularity is the existence of two radial functions
\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\rm max}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
obeying

\lambda_{\rm min}(|z|)\leq\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z)),\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(|z|)\geq\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))

and

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(t)}{\lambda_{\rm min}(t)}<\infty

so that these integrands are q. u. c.. In this framework both
{\rm Sing}_{F}
and
{\rm Deg}_{F}
are anyway restricted to have radial symmetry, but since neither of those can contain circles, (
1.7
) holds again.

Notice that when

1.2.
The rôle of the Uhlenbeck structure

Let us discuss briefly the rôle of a radial control on the ellipticity ratio in proving a-priori gradient bounds for finite energy solutions of (
1.3
) with a convex
F
. The standard strategy to this end, which goes back to Bernstein,
is to find a suitable coercive function
G:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and coefficients
a_{ij}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(1)

The matrix
A(z)=(a_{ij}(z))
has controlled ellipticity for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

(2)

The function
G(Du)
solves

(1.12)

{\rm div}\,(A(Du)\,D(G(Du)))\geq 0.

Once these tasks are achieved,
linear
regularity theory can be applied to
G(Du)
. If, for instance,
A
turns out to be strongly elliptic, then solutions of (
1.12
) fulfil

\|G(Du)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq C\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}G(Du)\,dx

and we are reduced to control the integrand on the right (or variants of it) by
F(Du)
.
More generally, once suitable refinements of the linear elliptic regularity step are developed, this scheme is flexible enough to deal with choices of
A
which may not be strongly elliptic, as long as their ellipticity ratio does not blow up too fast for
z\to\infty
(see e. g.
[
6
]
and the literature therein for the non-uniformly elliptic setting).

For the sake of this discussion, we will suppose that
F
and
u
are smooth and that we have chosen
\lambda_{\rm min},\lambda_{\rm max}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
in such a way that for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
it holds

(1.13)

0\leq\lambda_{\rm min}(z)\leq\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z)),\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(z)\geq\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z)),\qquad\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(z)}{\lambda_{\rm min}(z)}\leq H.

The standard approach to construct the couple
(A,G)
is to multiply each equation in (
1.4
) by
\partial_{\alpha}u
and sum, to obtain

(1.14)

{\rm div}\,\Big(D^{2}F(Du)\,\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}(D\partial_{\alpha}u)\,\partial_{\alpha}u\Big)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}D^{2}F(Du)\,D\partial_{\alpha}u\,D\partial_{\alpha}u\geq 0

where the last inequality follows from the convexity of
F
. With this choice, however, we are committing to a radial control on the ellipticity ratio, since

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}(D\partial_{\alpha}u)\,\partial_{\alpha}u=D\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}

and a natural choice for
A
is

A(z)=\frac{D^{2}F(z)}{\lambda_{\rm min}(z)},

which is strongly elliptic if the ellipticity ratio is uniformly bounded, while in the meantime (
1.14
) reads

{\rm div}\,\left(A(Du)\,\lambda_{\rm min}(Du)\,D\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 0.

In order to construct
G
we must impose

(1.15)

DG(z)=\lambda_{\rm min}(z)\,D\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}=\lambda_{\rm min}(z)\,z

but this relation forces
G
, and thus
\lambda_{\rm min}
, to be radial functions.
If this is so, i. e.
\lambda_{\rm min}(z)=\lambda_{\rm min}(|z|)
, we can indeed set

G(z)=\int_{0}^{|z|}\lambda_{\rm min}(s)\,s\,ds

which fulfils (
1.15
).
Notice finally that, since
F(0)=0
and
DF(0)=0
, for
|\omega|=1
and

it holds

F(t\,\omega)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(D^{2}F(s\,\omega)\,\omega,\omega\right)(t-s)\,ds\geq\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{\rm min}(s)\,(t-s)\,ds\geq\int_{0}^{t/2}\lambda_{\rm min}(s)\,s\,ds=G(t/2)

which can be used to bound
G(Du)
in terms of the natural integrand
F(Du)
.
3
3

3

As we will see, if
{\rm e}
is uniformly bounded it always holds
F(2\,z)\leq C\,F(z)
.

Without a radial control on
\lambda_{\rm min}
or
\lambda_{\rm max}
, however, the previous approach fails.

Remark 1.3
.

It may be worth noting that multiplying (
1.4
) by
\partial_{\alpha}F(Du)
and summing up, one finds

{\rm div}\,\big(D^{2}F(Du)D(F(Du))\big)={\rm Tr}\,\big(D^{2}F(Du)\,D^{2}u\,D^{2}F(Du)\,D^{2}u).

As will be quantified in the following paragraph, the convexity of

ensures that the right-hand side above is still nonnegative, so that we have found another couple

fitting the previous scheme, namely,

A=D^{2}F(Du),\qquad G=F.

The issue, however, is that

fails to be strongly elliptic and is only uniformly elliptic. Needless to say, the linear theory for solutions of

under the uniform ellipticity condition

is very poor (as can be seen by simple one-dimensional examples) and does not provide boundedness of solutions. Nevertheless, estimate (

behaves “as if” the operator

1.3.
Outline of the proof

The possibility of tackling the regularity problem for finite energy solutions of (
1.3
)
in dimension
N\geq 3
under the general uniform ellipticity condition (
1.2
) has been considered for the first time in
[
23
]
. The approach adopted therein can be seen in the framework of
nonlinear differential inclusions
, originally rooted in the works
[
5
,
37
,
18
]
and well developed nowadays, thanks to the significant applications investigated in
[
34
,
28
,
39
,
15
]
, to name a few. Specifically, one can look at (
1.3
) focusing only on the
stress field

DF(Du)=V
. Formally deriving the equation, we find the system

(1.16)

{\rm Div}\,(DV^{t})=0

where
{\rm Div}
is the row-wise divergence operator acting on matrix-valued functions and
M^{t}
denotes the transpose of the matrix
M
. Notice that, contrary to the Laplacian operator

\Delta V={\rm Div}\,(DV),

the operator in (
1.16
) is far from being elliptic: its kernel contains all compactly supported solenoidal vector fields and there is no hope to prove regularity of solutions to system (
1.16
). Ellipticity, however, can be restored by constraining
DV
to pointwise belong to a suitable cone. The natural and fruitful one in our framework, as found in
[
23
]
, is given by

{\mathcal{K}}_{H}=\left\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}:{\rm Tr}\,(M\,M)\geq\frac{1}{H}\,{\rm Tr}\,(M\,M^{t})\right\},

for
H
given in (
1.2
). The fact that
DV
must belong to such a cone depends only on the structural condition

DV=D^{2}F(Du)\,D^{2}u,

i. e.
DV
must be the product of a positive definite symmetric matrix with linear dilatation bounded by
H
with a symmetric matrix (see Lemma
5.1
below). Note that, as
H\searrow 1
, we have
{\mathcal{K}}_{H}\searrow{\mathcal{K}}_{1}={\rm Sym}_{N}
, that is the linear space of symmetric
N\times N
matrices, hence the solutions of the differential inclusion

(1.17)

\begin{cases}{\rm Div}\,(DV^{t})=0\\
DV\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\end{cases}

are just the gradients of harmonic functions.

For
H>1
, system (
1.17
) is elliptic at the
L^{2}
level, but this kind of restored ellipticity has a limited effect at finer regularity scales.
Indeed, stress fields of
p
-harmonic equation certainly solve the previous nonlinear differential inclusion for
p=1+1/H
, and for
p\neq 2
there exists (see
[
27
]
) in the plane a
p
-harmonic function whose stress field vanishes only at the origin and is homogenous of degree

d=\frac{1}{6}\left(p+\sqrt{p^{2}+12\,p-12}\right)=\frac{1}{6\,H}\left(H+1+\sqrt{H^{2}+14\,H+1}\right).

Since the last expression is always less than
1
for
H>1
, the best regularity one can expect from solutions of (
1.17
) is at most
C^{\alpha}
, for
\alpha=\alpha(H)\in\ [1/3,1[
and for any
H>1
. We stress here that we were
not
able to prove that solutions of (
1.17
) are bounded, which would imply the qualitative part of Theorem
1.1
and would be meaningful in light of current lines of research on differential inclusions (see e. g.
[
2
,
24
]
). In fact, we had to resort to additional structure possessed by the original equation (
1.3
). More precisely, by still denoting
V=DF(Du)
for a solution of (
1.3
) and by using the differential inclusion
DV\in{\mathcal{K}}_{H}
, we prove a family of

of the form

(1.18)

\int_{B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)>k\}}|DV|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C_{H}}{(R-r)^{2}}\int_{B_{R}\cap\{F(Du)>k\}}|V|^{2}\,dx

for arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{R}
and for
R>r>0
.
Then we translate this vectorial Caccioppoli inequality into a family of Caccioppoli inequalities on suitable scalar functions intrinsically defined as suitable Minkowski functionals associated to

G(z)=F(DF^{-1}(z))

(which is well defined since
DF
is invertible), namely,

(1.19)

g_{k}(z)=\inf\left\{t>0:G(z/t)>k\right\}

for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Notice that, since
V=DF(Du)
, then

\{F(Du)>k\}=\{G(V)>k\}=\{g_{k}(V)>1\}

and the quasiuniform convexity of
F
is pivotal to prove that (
1.18
) translates to

(1.20)

\int_{B_{r}}|D(g_{k}(V)-1)_{+}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C_{H}}{(R-r)^{2}}\,\int_{B_{R}}|g_{k}(V)|^{2}\,dx.

Additional fine properties of the family of
1
-homogeneous functions
\{g_{k}\}
allow to adapt the classical De Giorgi method for proving boundedness of subsolutions. Notice that (
1.20
) exhibits two main differences with respect to a standard Caccioppoli inequality. On the one hand, due to the vectorial nature of (
1.18
), its dependence on the level
k
is encapsulated rather implicitly in the family of
1
-homogeneous functions
\{g_{k}\}
rather than directly on
V
. More substantially, its right-hand side is quite bigger than the one usually found for scalar problems, which would provide an integrand of the form
(g_{k}(V)-1)_{+}
on the right instead of
g_{k}(V)
. Nevertheless, the De Giorgi method can still be adapted to this weaker setting, providing an
L^{\infty}
bound on
F(Du)
in terms of the
L^{2}
norm of
V
. It is quite fortunate that this estimate implies, through a refinement of the results in
[
23
]
, the natural bound (
1.11
).

1.4.
Related results

In the uniformly elliptic setting the result in Theorem
1.1
has, as already remarked, been previously obtained in
[
32
]
under an Uhlenbeck type control of the form (
1.13
), with
\lambda_{\rm min}(z)
and
\lambda_{\rm max}(z)
depending only on the modulus of
z
. In
[
32
]
, actually,
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity is proved under these assumptions. Lipschitz regularity has been proved in
[
13
]
for solutions of (
1.8
) coupled with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, with optimal regularity assumptions both on
f
and on the domain. The corresponding regularity for solutions of systems with Uhlenbeck structure is treated in
[
14
]
.

Notice that in
[
32
,
12
,
14
]
the authors assume that the function
G^{\prime}
appearing in (
1.8
) (or in the lower/upper controls on
D^{2}F(z)
) belongs to
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, while we admit
G^{\prime}\in{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(this regularity seems optimal due to
[
23
, Example 3.5]
).
However, inspecting the proofs in
[
12
,
14
]
shows that the results therein hold under this more general assumption.

Our result covers in particular the Finslerian anisotropic setting, which we will now briefly describe due to its relevance in recent research trends. The integrand considered in this framework are of the form

F(z)=G(h(z))

where
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap W^{2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is increasing, convex and fulfils (
1.6
) (see
[
23
, Example 3.7]
),
while
h
is a
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
convex, positive,
1
-homogeneous function (not necessarily symmetric) such that the principal curvatures of
\partial\{h<1\}
are bounded from below by a positive constant, see
[
23
, Example 3.7]
for more details.
The arguments in
[
11
, Section 3]
show that
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity holds true for the corresponding minimisers when
G
has more stringent controls of
p
-growth type and
h\in C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.

While the non-uniformly elliptic case is not treated in this research, it is worth mentioning that there are many instances where Lipschitz regularity can be obtained even if the ellipticity ratio is unbounded. Generally speaking, in order to get Lipschitz continuity of local minimisers, one usually requires a growth control on the ellipticity ratio outside a bounded set, but in most cases the resulting Lipschitz bound critically depends on the diameter of the aforementioned set. The literature in this area is huge and, regarding local minimisers, we refer to
[
6
]
for some recent results and for a rather comprehensive description of this research topic. Lipschitz regularity can be obtained for rather wild functionals by the so called Hilbert-Haar method, adapted to the Calculus of Variations by Stampacchia and Hartman
[
36
,
25
]
. This allows to obtain Lipschitz regularity of minimisers having a prescribed boundary value obeying the so-called bounded slope condition, under very loose conditions on the integrand. We refer to
[
8
]
and the literature therein for more details on this approach. Another class of non-uniformly elliptic integrands are the so-called
orthotropic
ones, where both
{\rm Sing}_{F}
and
{\rm Deg}_{F}
are unbounded, being union of hyperplanes
\{z_{i}=0\}
. In this setting, assuming a-priori boundedness of the minimiser is the key to infer Lipschitz regularity. We refer to
[
9
]
and the literature therein for further details on this class of integrands.

2.
Quasiuniformly convex integrands

Definition 2.1
.

A map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
K
-quasiconformal if it is a
W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
homeomorphism, it is a. e. differentiable and the inequality

\frac{1}{K}\,\sigma_{\rm max}(D\Phi)^{N}\leq|J\,\Phi|\leq K\,\sigma_{\rm min}(D\Phi)^{N}

holds a. e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Let
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
denote the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By
[
38
, Theorem 17.3]
any quasiconformal map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
can be extended to a homeomorphism of
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
by setting
\Phi(\infty)=\infty
, in the meantime keeping its (geometric) quasiconformality constant
K
unaltered.
Moreover,
\Phi\in W^{1,N}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and is
quasisymmetric
, that is, there are an increasing homeomorphism
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a constant
C>0
such that

(2.1)

\frac{\left|\Phi(z)-\Phi(z_{0})\right|}{|\Phi(w)-\Phi(z_{0})|}\leq C\,\eta\left(\frac{|z-z_{0}|}{|w-z_{0}|}\right)

for all
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{z_{0}\}
.
Set, for
\alpha>0
and
t\geq 0
,

(2.2)

\eta_{\alpha}(t)=\max\bigl\{t^{\alpha},t^{1/\alpha}\bigr\}.

By
[
1
, Theorems 3.18 and 5.1]
, any
K
-quasiconformal map
\Phi
fulfils (
2.1
) with

C=C(K),\qquad\eta=\eta_{K^{1/(N-1)}}.

The
maximal linear dilatation
of
\Phi
is defined as the (finite) number

H=\esssup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\sigma_{\rm max}(D\Phi(z))}{\sigma_{\rm min}(D\Phi(z))}.

Elementary linear algebra shows that such a quasiconformal
\Phi
is actually
H^{N-1}
-quasiconformal, hence it fulfils the distortion estimate

(2.3)

\frac{\left|\Phi(z)-\Phi(z_{0})\right|}{|\Phi(w)-\Phi(z_{0})|}\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|z-z_{0}|}{|w-z_{0}|}\right)

for all
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and all
z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{z_{0}\}
.
Notice that the constant
C
actually depends on
N
as well. Finally, recall that if
\Phi
is quasiconformal then so is
\Phi^{-1}
and the maximal linear dilatations of
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
coincide. Hence (
2.3
) holds true for
\Phi^{-1}
as well.

Definition 2.2
.

A map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
\delta
-monotone for some
\delta\in\,]0,1]
if

\left(\Phi(z)-\Phi(w),z-w\right)\geq\delta\,|\Phi(z)-\Phi(w)|\,|z-w|.

Kovalev’s theorem
[
29
]
shows that any non-constant
\delta
-monotone map is quasiconformal. In particular it is a homeomorphism and, directly from the definition, its inverse is
\delta
-monotone as well.
Kovalev theorem has a quantitative version proved in
[
3
, Theorem 1]
. It states that any
\delta
-monotone map has a maximal linear dilatation obeying

H\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}{1-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}

and this bound is sharp, i. e. it reduces to an equality for the
\delta
-monotone linear map
v\mapsto A\,v
, where

(2.4)

A=\begin{pmatrix}1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}&0\\
0&1-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

The opposite implication is in general not true. To see this, consider the linear map
v\mapsto B\,v
, with
B
given by

B=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\
2&-1\end{pmatrix}

which is quasiconformal with
H=3
but not even monotone. However, full (even quantitatively) equivalence of the two concepts holds true in the class of
gradient mappings
.

Definition 2.3
.

A differentiable function
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is
H
-quasiuniformly convex (briefly,
H
-q. u. c.) if it is convex and its gradient map is quasiconformal, with maximal linear dilatation bounded by
H
.

More explicitly (and by making use of Aleksandrov’s theorem),
F
is
H
-q. u. c. if

(i)

F
is convex,
C^{1}
and
W^{2,1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and not affine;

(ii)

It holds

(2.5)

\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))\leq H\,\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))

for a. e. point
z
of second order differentiability.

By
[
30
, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.2]
, any q. u. c. function is strictly convex and coercive, thus it has a unique minimum point.
Since, given two symmetric matrices
M_{1},M_{2}
, it holds

(2.6)

\lambda_{\rm min}(M_{1}+M_{2})\geq\lambda_{\rm min}(M_{1})+\lambda_{\rm min}(M_{2}),\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(M_{1}+M_{2})\leq\lambda_{\rm max}(M_{1})+\lambda_{\rm max}(M_{2}),

the set of
H
-q. u. c. functions turns out to be a convex cone, i. e. it is closed by sum and positive scalar multiples. Moreover, it is also closed by isometric change of variables, as well as dilations. Finally, summing an affine function to a
H
-q. u. c. function still gives a
H
-q. u. c. function. For these reasons we will frequently
normalize
q. u. c. integrands by requiring that

(2.7)

F(z)\geq F(0)=0

and that
i_{F}=1
, where

(2.8)

i_{F}:=\inf_{|z|=1}|DF(z)|,

simply by considering

(2.9)

\tilde{F}(z)=\frac{1}{i_{F}}\,\left(F(z+\bar{z})-F(\bar{z})\right),\qquad\text{where }{\rm Argmin}\,(F)=\{\bar{z}\}.

Example 2.4
.

Some examples of q. u. c. integrands have already been discussed in the previous section. Here, given two points
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we construct a q. u. c. function
F
such that
{\rm Sing}_{F}=\{z_{1}\}
and
{\rm Deg}_{F}=\{z_{2}\}
, the latter sets being as in (
1.5
).

Without loss of generality, assume
z_{1}=0
and
z_{2}=w
, for fixed
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and set
r=|w|
. For
p>2>q>1
choose

d(z)=\frac{|z|^{p}}{p},\qquad s(z)=\frac{|z-w|^{q}}{q},

so that a direct computation shows (cf.
[
23
, Example 3.6]
)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}d(z))

\displaystyle=|z|^{p-2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}d(z))

\displaystyle=(p-1)\,|z|^{p-2},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}s(z))

\displaystyle=(q-1)\,|z|^{q-2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}s(z))

\displaystyle=|z|^{q-2}.

Choose
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{w\})
such that

\varphi(z)=\begin{cases}s(z)&\text{if $|z-w|\leq r/4$}\\
1&\text{if $|z-w|\geq r/2$}\end{cases}

and consider the (positive and finite) numbers

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}d(z)):|z|\geq r/2\right\},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{|D^{2}\varphi(z)|_{2}:|z-w|\geq r/4\right\},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}d(z)):|z-w|\leq r/4\right\},

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}s(z)):|z-w|\geq r/4\right\}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the function
F(z)=d(z)+\varepsilon\,\varphi(z)
belongs to
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{2,N}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and furthermore
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{w\})
, so that
{\rm Sing}_{F}\subseteq\{w\}
.

For
z\in B_{r/2}(0)
we have

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))=\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}d(z)),\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}(F(z))=\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}d(z)),

which shows through (
2.10
) that

\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))}\leq p-1\qquad\text{in $B_{r/2}(0)$}

and
\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\to 0
for
z\to 0
.
From (
2.6
) we infer that for all
z\in B_{r/4}(w)

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\geq\alpha+\varepsilon\,\lambda_{{\rm min}}(D^{2}s(z)),\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))\leq\gamma+\varepsilon\,\lambda_{{\rm max}}(D^{2}s(z)),

so that by (
2.11
) it follows

\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},\frac{1}{q-1}\right\}\qquad\text{in $B_{r/4}(w)$}

and
\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\to\infty
for
z\to w
.
Finally, for
z\notin B_{r/2}(0)\cup B_{r/4}(w)
we have

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\geq\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}d(z))-\varepsilon\,\beta,\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))\leq\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}d(z))+\varepsilon\,\beta,

so that for
\varepsilon\,\beta<\alpha/2
it holds
\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\geq\alpha/2
, while (
2.10
) yields

\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))}\leq 3\,(p-1)\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\big(B_{r/2}(0)\cup B_{r/4}(w)\big)$}.

All in all, for
\varepsilon<\alpha/(2\,\beta)
,
F
is q. u. c. with
{\rm Sing}_{F}=\{w\}
,
{\rm Deg}_{F}=\{0\}
.

Many properties of q. u. c. functions have been studied in
[
29
,
30
]
, to which we refer for further details and characterisations. Here we gather the ones that are needed in the proof of our main result.
We start by a converse of Kovalev’s theorem relating the
\delta
-monotonicity to the quasiconformality in a quantitative form.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable and not affine. Then,
F
is
H
-q. u. c. if and only if
DF
is
\delta
-monotone, where

\delta=\frac{2\,\sqrt{H}}{H+1},\qquad\text{or}\qquad H=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}{1-\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}},

and the bounds are sharp.

Proof.

The fact that if
DF
is
\delta
-monotone and non-constant then
F
is
H
-q. u. c. has been proved, as mentioned before, in
[
3
, Theorem 1]
. To prove the opposite implication, suppose
F
is
H
-q. u. c., so that
DF
is quasiconformal. By
[
29
, Section 3]
, the
\delta
-monotonicity of
DF
is equivalent to

(2.12)

\left(D^{2}F(z)\,v,v\right)\geq\delta\,|D^{2}F(z)\,v|\,|v|

at a. e. points of second order differentiability where (
2.5
) holds true. Note that we can assume that
D^{2}F
is symmetric, as this follows from Alexandrov’s theorem (see
[
35
, Corollary 2.9]
), and that it is strictly positive definite, thanks to the quasiconformality of
DF
. Since (
2.12
) is invariant by orthogonal change of variables, we can assume that
D^{2}F
is diagonal with positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\dots<\lambda_{N}
. We are therefore reduced to find, for
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{N})
, the value of

I:=\inf_{v\neq 0}\frac{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\,v_{i}^{2}}{\Big({\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\,v_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big({\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}}.

In order to determine the latter we invoke Cassels inequality (see
[
40
, Appendix]
), which reads as

\frac{\sum_{i}w_{i}\,a^{2}_{i}\sum_{i}w_{i}\,b_{i}^{2}}{\left(\sum_{i}w_{i}\,a_{i}\,b_{i}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{(M+m)^{2}}{4\,M\,m}

for any choice of
w_{i}\geq 0
not all identically equal to zero and
a_{i},b_{i}>0
such that

0<m<a_{i}/b_{i}<M.

Indeed, it suffices to choose
b_{i}=1
,
a_{i}=\lambda_{i}
and
w_{i}=v_{i}^{2}
to obtain that

I\geq\frac{2\,\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\,\lambda_{N}}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{N}}

and actually the equality holds true for the vector

\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{N}}},0,\dots,0,\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{N}}}\right).

Since
\lambda_{N}\leq H\,\lambda_{1}
by assumption and the map
t\mapsto 2\sqrt{t}/(t+1)
is decreasing, we infer that

I\geq\frac{2\,\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\,\lambda_{N}}}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{N}}=\frac{2\,\sqrt{\lambda_{N}/\lambda_{1}}}{\lambda_{N}/\lambda_{1}+1}\geq\frac{2\,\sqrt{H}}{H+1}

so that (
2.12
) is proved for
\delta=2\,\sqrt{H}/(H+1)
, as claimed.
The optimality of this estimate follows by choosing
F(z)=(A\,z,z)
with
A
given in (
2.4
) (here we use the fact that
A
is symmetric).
∎

For further reference, we regroup the previous discussion and some other useful properties of
H
-q. u. c. functions in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
H
-q. u. c. function. The following holds:

(1)

F
is strictly convex, coercive and
W^{2,N}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(2)

Both
DF
and
DF^{-1}
are
\eta_{H}
-quasisymmetric and
2\sqrt{H}/(H+1)
-monotone.

(3)

There exists
C=C(H,N)>0
such that, if
F(\bar{z})=\min_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F
, then

(2.13)

\displaystyle|DF(z)-DF(\bar{z})|\,|z-\bar{z}|\leq C\,|F(z)-F(\bar{z})|

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{F(w)-F(\bar{z})}{F(z)-F(\bar{z})}\leq C\,\frac{|w-\bar{z}|}{|z-\bar{z}|}\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|w-\bar{z}|}{|z-\bar{z}|}\right)

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{\bar{z}\}
.

(4)

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N};[0,+\infty))
the function
F*\varphi(z)+\mu\,|z|^{2}
is
H
-q. u. c. Moreover, if
\|\varphi\|_{1}=1
, for all sequences
(\varepsilon_{n})
,
(\mu_{n})
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
,
\mu_{n}\downarrow 0
, the sequence

(2.15)

F_{n}(z)=F*\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}(z)+\frac{\mu_{n}}{2}\,|z|^{2},\qquad\text{with }\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}(z)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}^{N}}\,\varphi\left(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{n}}\right),

is such that
F_{n}\to F
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
DF_{n}^{-1}\to DF^{-1}
in
C^{0}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(5)

For any
\delta>0
, the Moreau-Yoshida reguarization
F_{\delta}
of
F
, defined as

F_{\delta}(z)=\inf_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big\{F(w)+\frac{1}{2\,\delta}|w-z|^{2}\Big\},

is
H
-q. u. c. and it holds
\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F_{\delta}(z))\leq 1/\delta
.

Proof.

The first two properties have already been discussed, so we focus on
(3)
. We will prove it by exploiting the
\delta
-monotonicity and the quasisymmetry of
DF
. To this aim we note that, by considering
F(z+\bar{z})-F(\bar{z})
, we can assume
\bar{z}=0
and
F(z)\geq F(0)=0
.
To prove (
2.13
), we start by noticing that the
\delta
-monotonicity of
DF
gives

(2.16)

F(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\left(DF(t\,z),z),z\right)\,dt\geq\delta\,|z|\,\int_{1/2}^{1}|DF(t\,z)|\,dt.

On the other hand, inequality (
2.3
) applied to
DF
with
z_{0}=0
(and since
DF(0)=0
, too) gives

\frac{|DF(z)|}{|DF(t\,z)|}\leq C\,\eta_{H}(1/t)\leq C\,2^{H}\qquad\forall\,t\geq 1/2,

so that (
2.16
) can be estimated as

F(z)\geq\frac{\delta}{C\,2^{H+1}}\,|DF(z)|\,|z|.

Inequality (
2.14
) is proven similarly. It holds

F(w)\leq\int_{0}^{1}|DF(t\,w)|\,|w|\,dt\leq|w|\,\sup\left\{|DF(x)|:|x|\leq|w|\right\}

which combined with (
2.13
) yields

\frac{F(w)}{F(z)}\leq C\,\frac{|w|}{|z|}\,\sup\left\{\frac{|DF(x)|}{|DF(z)|}:|x|\leq|w|\right\}.

Thanks to the quasisymmetry (
2.3
) of
DF
we get

\frac{|DF(x)|}{|DF(z)|}\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|x|}{|z|}\right)\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|w|}{|z|}\right)

whenever
|x|\leq|w|
, and thus (
2.14
) follows.

The first part of assertion
(4)
has been proved in
[
23
, Proposition 2.3]
, while the
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
convergence of
F_{n}
to
F
is trivial. Since
DF_{n}
are homeomorphisms which are locally uniformly converging to
DF
, the same is true for
DF^{-1}
thanks to Arens’ theorem (see
[
17
]
).
Finally, part (5) has been proved in
[
23
, Proposition 2.3-(iv)]
, where it in particular it is shown that if
P_{\delta}=\big({\rm Id}+\delta\,DF^{-1}\big)^{-1}
, for a. e.
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
it holds

\begin{split}\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F_{\delta}(z))&=\frac{\lambda_{\rm min}\big(D^{2}F(P_{\delta}(z))\big)}{1+\delta\,\lambda_{\rm min}\big(D^{2}F(P_{\delta}(z))\big)},\\
\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F_{\delta}(z))&=\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}\big(D^{2}F(P_{\delta}(z))\big)}{1+\delta\,\lambda_{\rm max}\big(D^{2}F(P_{\delta}(z))\big)}.\end{split}

Since

\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t}{1+\delta\,t}=\frac{1}{\delta},

the claimed bound follows.
∎

Recall that by a smooth
strongly elliptic integrand
we mean an
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\geq\lambda,\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))\leq\Lambda

for some
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
. Clearly, strongly elliptic integrands are
H
-q. u. c. with
H=\Lambda/\lambda
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
H\geq 1
. The cone

{\rm C}_{H}=\{F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ s.\,t.\,$F$ is $H$-q.\,u.\,c.\,or affine}\}

is closed with respect to point-wise a. e. convergence and the smooth, strongly elliptic integrands in
{\rm C}_{H}
are dense in it. Moreover, point-wise a. e. convergence of
(F_{n})_{n}
in
{\rm C}_{H}
implies
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
convergence of
(F_{n})_{n}
and
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
convergence of
({\rm det}\,D^{2}F_{n})_{n}
.

Proof.

By
[
30
, Lemma 2.5]
we know that the point-wise limit of q. u. c. functions is either q. u. c. or affine and that the point-wise a. e. convergence implies the
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
one. Therefore it suffices to show that

F\mapsto\esssup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(z))}{\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))}

is lower semicontinuous with respect to
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
convergence on the cone of q. u. c. functions. Let
F_{n}\in{\rm C}_{H}
be such that
F_{n}\to F
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with none of the
F_{n}
affine. By Lemma
2.5
,
DF_{n}
is
2\,\sqrt{H}/(H+1)
-monotone hence, by passing to the limit in the definition od
\delta
-monotonicity, so is
DF
. By Lemma
2.5
again, this implies that
F
is
H
-q. u. c., giving the claimed lower semicontinuity.

To prove the density statement, let
F
be
H
-q. u. c. for some
H
. For
\delta_{n},\varepsilon_{n},\mu_{n}\downarrow 0
, the functions

F_{n}(z)=F_{\delta_{n}}*\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}(z)+\frac{\mu_{n}}{2}|z|^{2}

(here
F_{\delta_{n}}
denotes the Moreau-Yoshida regularisation of
F
) constructed through Proposition
2.6
, points
(4)
and
(5)
, are
H
-q. u. c. integrands that approximate
F
point-wise. Using (
2.6
) together with basic properties of convolution and Proposition
2.6
-
(5)
, yields

\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F_{n}(z))\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{n}}+\mu_{n},\qquad\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F_{n}(z))\geq\mu_{n}

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, hence
F_{n}
are strongly elliptic integrands.

If instead
F
is an affine function of the form
F(z)=(w,z)+c
for fixed
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we can consider
G(z)=|z|^{2}/2
and set

F_{n}(z)=n\big(G(w+z/n)-G(w)\big)+c,\qquad DF_{n}(z)=DG(w+z/n),\qquad D^{2}F_{n}=\frac{{\rm Id}}{n}.

Clearly
F_{n}\to\ell
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the
F_{n}
are
1
-q. u. c. and strongly elliptic. The last stated property on the

-convergence of the determinants is contained in
[
30
, Lemma 2.5]
.
∎

Actually (see again
[
30
, Lemma 2.5]
), the pointwise convergence in the previous corollary can be weakened to hold on a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 2.8
.

The previous corollary, as outlined in its proof, implies the lower semicontinuity of the maximal linear dilatation of monotone, quasiconformal gradient maps. Lower semicontinuity of the maximal linear dilatation in the larger class of quasiconformal maps holds true for

, but fails for

, due to a famous counterexample of Iwaniec

.

We conclude this section by introducing a generalisation of the function

given in (

), which will appear in many subsequent computations. Given

, the auxiliary functions

are increasing homeomorphisms of

to itself, and it holds

.
Since

we see that

\eta_{a,b}^{-1}(t)=\min\bigl\{t^{1/a},t^{1/b}\bigr\}.

We collect in the following proposition some elementary properties of the function

and of its inverse.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

. Then,

(1)

For all

(2.18)

\eta_{a,b}(s\,t)\leq\eta_{a,b}(s)\,\eta_{a,b}(t)\quad\text{as well as}\quad\eta_{a,b}^{-1}(s\,t)\geq\eta_{a,b}^{-1}(s)\,\eta_{a,b}^{-1}(t)

(2)

For all

\eta_{a,b}^{-1}(t)\,\eta_{1/a,1/b}(1/t)=1.

(3)

For all

and

it holds

\frac{1}{C}\,\eta_{a,b}(t)\leq\eta_{a,b}(\sigma\,t)\leq C\,\eta_{a,b}(t),

for positive constants

. The same estimate holds true for

as well.

(4)

For

and

\eta_{a,b}\circ\eta_{c,d}=\eta_{ac,bd}

and the same formula holds true for the inverses.

The proof of these facts is elementary and is therefore omitted.

3.
Local minimisers and Sobolev regularity of their stress field

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be open. For all
\mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega
let us consider the functionals

J\colon W^{1,1}({\mathcal{O}})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.1)

J(w,{\mathcal{O}})=\int_{\mathcal{O}}F(Dw)\;dx.

We say that a function
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is a local minimiser of
J
in
\Omega
if
F(Du)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and for any open
{\mathcal{O}}\Subset\Omega
it holds

J(u,{\mathcal{O}})=\inf\left\{J(w,{\mathcal{O}}):\,w\in u+W^{1,1}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})\right\}.

If
F
is
C^{1}
, strictly convex and coercive, let
\{\bar{z}\}={\rm Argmin}\,(F)
. It is readily checked that any local minimiser
u
of
J
defines a local minimiser
\tilde{u}(x)=u(x)-(\bar{z},x)
for the functional
\tilde{J}
given as in (
3.1
), but with
\tilde{F}(z)
defined as in (
2.9
). In particular, we can assume that (
2.7
) holds true, i. e. that
\bar{z}=0
. If
F
is q. u. c., which will be assumed henceforth, using (
2.14
) for
\bar{z}=0
gives

F(z)\geq\frac{1}{C}\,F(w)\,|z|\,\eta^{-1}_{H}(|z|)\qquad\forall\,|w|=1,

so that for all
|z|>1
it holds

(3.2)

F(z)\geq\frac{1}{C}\sup_{|w|=1}F(w)\,|z|^{1+\frac{1}{H}}.

In particular, any local minimiser for
J
in
\Omega
belongs to
W^{1,1+\frac{1}{H}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

More precisely, despite the fact that q. u. c. integrands are
per se
anisotropic, they enjoy, together with their derivative, an isotropic growth control allowing to treat minimisation problems for
J
in standard Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Indeed, after normalising
F
so that (
2.7
) holds true, we can let for
t\geq 0

a(t)=\sup_{|z|\leq t}|DF(z)|,\qquad A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)\,d\tau.

Notice that
a
is continuous on
[0,\infty[
and positive for
t>0
. Given
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}
, since
DF
is a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
DF(B_{t_{2}})
is an open neighbourhood of the closed set
DF(\overline{B_{t_{1}}})
, thus
a(t_{2})>a(t_{1})
, i. e.
a
is strictly increasing.
By the quasisymmetry of
DF
, choosing
z_{0}=0
and
|w|=t
in (
2.3
), we obtain that for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
it holds

|DF(z)|\leq C\,a(t)\,\eta_{H}(|z|/t),

so that for any
k\geq 0
,
t\geq 0
, the monotonicity of
\eta_{H}
grants

(3.3)

a(k\,t)\leq C\,\eta_{H}(k)\,a(t).

Therefore
A
is a Young function, i. e. it is convex, increasing and fulfils

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{A(t)}{t}=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t}=\infty,

where the second equality follows from (
3.3
) for
k=1/t
and using
\eta_{H}(1/t)^{-1}=t^{1/H}
for
t\geq 1
.
The inverse of
a
is readily checked to be

a^{-1}(s)=\inf_{|z|\geq s}|DF^{-1}(z)|

which obeys a similar estimate as (
3.3
).
It follows that
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition

A(2\,t)\leq C\,A(t)\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0

for
C=C(H,N)
, and the same holds true for its Young conjugate defined as

A^{*}(s)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(s\,t-A(t)\right)=\int_{0}^{s}a^{-1}(\sigma)\,d\sigma

(see the proof of
[
4
, eq. (16.94)-(16.95)]
).

Clearly

F(z)=\int_{0}^{|z|}(DF(s\,z/|z|),z/|z|)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{|z|}a(s)\,ds=A(|z|)

while the
\delta
-monotonicity and quasisymmetry of
DF
give

F(z)\geq\delta\,\int_{0}^{|z|}|DF(s\,z/|z|)|\,ds\geq\frac{\delta}{C}\,\int_{0}^{|z|}a(s)\,ds,

so that

(3.4)

\frac{1}{C}\,A(|z|)\leq F(z)\leq C\,A(|z|).

The map
DF
enjoys similar isotropic bounds, namely,

\frac{1}{C}\,a(|z|)\leq|DF(z)|\leq a(|z|),

where the first inequality follows from (
2.3
) as before, and it is furthermore possible to show that

(3.5)

\frac{1}{C}\,A(|z|)\leq A^{*}(DF(z))\leq C\,A(|z|)

(see the proof of
[
4
, eq. (16.106)]
).

Given an open set
{\mathcal{O}}\Subset\Omega
, it follows from (
3.4
) that

J(u,{\mathcal{O}})<\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad A(|Du|)\in L^{1}({\mathcal{O}}).

Using the fact that
{\mathcal{O}}
has finite measure and
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition, the summability of
A(|Du|)
can be equivalently stated as

u\in W^{1,A}({\mathcal{O}}):=\left\{v\in W^{1,1}({\mathcal{O}}):A(|v|),A(|Dv|)\in L^{1}({\mathcal{O}})\right\},

equipped with the Luxembourg norm

\|v\|_{L^{A}({\mathcal{O}})}+\||Dv|\|_{L^{A}({\mathcal{O}})},\qquad\|g\|_{L^{A}({\mathcal{O}})}=\inf\left\{s>0:\int_{\mathcal{O}}A(|g|/s)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Thanks to the
\Delta_{2}
condition on
A
, the so-called Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}({\mathcal{O}})
so defined turns out to be a Banach space.
Existence of local minimiser given, say, a boundary datum
\varphi\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
for
\Omega
bounded is thus granted by minimising
J
on
u+W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
, the latter space being the closed vector space of those
v\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
whose extension at zero outside
\Omega
belongs to
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, thanks to the validity of the
\Delta_{2}
condition on both
A
and
A^{*}
, the Banach space
W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is reflexive by
[
19
, p. 54]
, while
J
is convex and coercive by (
3.4
). Conversely, a local minimiser
u
on
\Omega
minimises
J(\cdot\,,{\mathcal{O}})
on
u+W^{1,A}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})
for any
{\mathcal{O}}\Subset\Omega
and thus standard methods (see

[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
) ensure the validity of the Euler-Lagrange equation

(3.6)

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(DF(Du),Dw)\,dx=0\qquad\forall\,w\in W^{1,A}_{0}({\mathcal{O}}).

Notice that for any
u\in W^{1,A}({\mathcal{O}})
the inequalities in (
3.5
) imply that
|DF(Du)|\in L^{A^{*}}({\mathcal{O}})
(thus
a fortiori

|DF(Du)|\in L^{1}({\mathcal{O}})
) and therefore Hölder’s inequality in Orlicz spaces ensures that

W^{1,A}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})\ni w\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{O}}(DF(Du),Dw)\,dx

is a well defined continuous linear functional. Thus, if for
{\mathcal{O}}\Subset\Omega
a function
u\in W^{1,A}({\mathcal{O}})
fulfils

{\rm div}\,(DF(Du))=0

in the distributional sense, it does so also in the weak sense (
3.6
), thanks to the density of
C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})
in
W^{1,A}_{0}({\mathcal{O}})
granted by
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
(here the
\Delta_{2}
condition on
A
alone is sufficient).

We summarise the previous discussion in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
F
be a q. u. c. integrand. A function
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is a local minimiser for
J
in (
3.1
) if and only if
F(Du)\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
and

{\rm div}\,(DF(Du))=0

in the distributional sense.

The next proposition is essentially contained in
[
23
, Theorem 3.3]
, but we will need the more explicit estimates given in its present form. It revolves around the Sobolev regularity of the
stress field

(3.7)

V(x)=DF(Du(x)).

.

Let

) and let

be as in (

4.
A regularising procedure

As customary, it will be convenient to work under the assumption that both the integrand and the minimiser under scrutiny are smooth. In order to do so, we introduce a family of regularised problems having the required smoothness and whose integrands and corresponding minimisers converge to the original problem.

Lemma 4.1

(Regularised problems)
.

Let
F
be a
H
-q. u. c. function fulfilling (
2.7
) and let
u
be a local minimiser for
J
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Then, for any ball
B_{2R}\Subset\Omega
there exists a sequence
F_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
H
-q. u. c. integrands obeying (
2.7
) and

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F_{n}(z))>0

and a sequence of corresponding minimisers
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}(B_{2R})
of

J_{n}(w,B_{2R})=\int_{B_{2R}}F_{n}(Dw)\,dx

such that

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}F_{n}(Du_{n})\,dx

\displaystyle\to\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in $W^{1,1+1/H}(B_{R})$},

(4.1c)

\displaystyle DF_{n}(Du_{n})

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup DF(Du)\quad\text{in $W^{1,2}(B_{R})$}.

Proof.

We set for brevity
B=B_{2R}
and
p=1+1/H
. Fix an even
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1};[0,+\infty[)
such that
\|\varphi\|_{1}=1
and use the notation of Proposition
2.6
-(4). Notice that for any such
\varphi
it holds

z=\int(z-y)\,\varphi(y)\,dy

so that Jensen inequality gives

(4.2)

F(z)\leq(\varphi\ast F)(z).

Set

F_{n}(z)=(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F)(z)+\frac{\mu_{n}}{2}\,|z|^{2},

with
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
and
\mu_{n}\downarrow 0
to be chosen, so that
F_{n}
fulfils for each
n
all the conditions stated in the lemma.
Notice that thanks to (
3.2
) and the convergence
F_{n}\to F
in
C^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have

(4.3)

F_{n}(w)\geq\frac{|w|^{p}}{C}\quad\text{as well as}\quad\frac{1}{C}\leq\inf_{|z|=1}|DF_{n}(z)|\leq C,

respectively, for constants
C
independent of
n
.

Let, for sufficiently large
n
,

\psi_{n}=u*\varphi_{1/n}\in C^{\infty}(B)

and consider the minimisation problem

(4.4)

\inf\{J_{n}(w):w\in{\rm Lip}(\overline{B}),w=\psi_{n}\ \text{on $\partial B$}\}.

According to
[
36
, Theorem 9.2]
, there is a solution
u_{n}
of (
4.4
), which (see
[
10
, p. 5923]
and (
3.2
)) also solves

J_{n}(u_{n})=\inf\{J_{n}(w):w\in\psi_{n}+W^{1,p}_{0}(B)\}.

By
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
there are constants
A_{n}\geq 1
(depending only on
B
and on the regularity of
\psi_{n}
, but not on
\varepsilon_{n}
,
\mu_{n}
) such that

{\rm Lip}(u_{n})\leq\frac{A_{n}}{\mu_{n}}.

The integrand
F
is therefore strongly elliptic on the range of
Du_{n}
since
F_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F_{n}(Du_{n}))\geq\mu_{n},\qquad\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F_{n}(Du_{n}))\leq C<\infty

on
\overline{B}
, so that standard regularity theory gives
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{B})
.
We first choose
\mu_{n}\downarrow 0
so that

(4.5)

\lim_{n}\mu_{n}\int_{B}|D\psi_{n}|^{2}\,dx=0.

Set

M_{n}=1+\sup_{B}|D\psi_{n}|+\frac{A_{n}}{\mu_{n}}

and observe that, since
\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F\to F
in
C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0

and
M_{n}
is independent of
\varepsilon_{n}
, we can pick
(\varepsilon_{n})\subseteq(0,1)
,
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
, so that

(4.6)

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F-F\|_{C^{1}(B_{M_{n}})}\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Clearly, it still holds
F_{n}\to F
in
C^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We claim that (
4.1
) holds true with such choices.

By the minimality of
u_{n}
, it holds
J_{n}(u_{n})\leq J_{n}(\psi_{n})
, while from (
4.5
) we get

(4.7)

\varlimsup_{n}J_{n}(u_{n})\leq\varlimsup_{n}J_{n}(\psi_{n})=\varlimsup_{n}\int_{B}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F)(D\psi_{n})\,dx.

In order to estimate the rightmost term of (
4.7
) we use (
4.6
) to have

(4.8)

\int_{B}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F)(D\psi_{n})\,dx\leq\frac{|B|}{n}+\int_{B}F(D\psi_{n})\,dx,

while the vector-valued Jensen inequality implies

\begin{split}\int_{B}F(D\psi_{n})\,dx&=\int_{B}F\left(\int\varphi_{1/n}(x-y)\,Du(y)\,dy\right)\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{B}\left(\int\varphi_{1/n}(x-y)F(Du(y))\,dy\right)dx\\
&\leq\int_{B}\varphi_{1/n}*F(Du)\,dx.\end{split}

Therefore from (
4.8
) we have

\varlimsup_{n}\int_{B}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{n}}*F)(D\psi_{n})\,dx\leq\varlimsup_{n}\int_{B}\varphi_{1/n}*F(Du)\,dx=\int_{B}F(Du)\,dx

which, inserted into (
4.7
), gives

(4.9)

\varlimsup_{n}J_{n}(u_{n})\leq J(u).

In particular,
J_{n}(u_{n})
is bounded and (
4.3
) then implies a uniform bound on
Du_{n}
in
L^{p}(B)
.
Moreover, since
u_{n}-\psi_{n}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
, then Poincaré’s inequality gives

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B)}&\leq\|u_{n}-\psi_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B)}\\
&\leq C\,\big(\|D(u_{n}-\psi_{n})\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B)}\big)\\
&\leq C\,\big(\|Du_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B)}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(B)}\big),\end{split}

so that
(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{p}(B)
as well. Therefore
(u_{n})
possesses a (not relabeled) subsequence weakly converging in
W^{1,p}(B)
to some
v
and it is readily checked that
v\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B)
. This in turn implies that (
4.1b
) is satisfied.

Now, since the map

w\mapsto\int_{B}F(Dw)\,dx

is weakly lower semicontinuous in
W^{1,p}(B)
and
F_{n}\geq F
thanks to (
4.2
), we get

(4.10)

J(v)\leq\varliminf_{n}\int_{B}F(Du_{n})\,dx\leq\varliminf_{n}\int_{B}F_{n}(Du_{n})\,dx=\varliminf_{n}J_{n}(u_{n}).

Coupling the latter with (
4.9
) gives
J(v)\leq J(u)
, implying
v=u
by the strict convexity of
F
.
In particular,
up to subsequences,

(4.11)

Du_{n}\rightharpoonup Du\qquad\text{in $L^{p}(B)$}.

Therefore, from (
4.10
) and (
4.9
) we infer

(4.12)

\int_{B}F(Du_{n})\,dx\to\int_{B}F(Du)\,dx,

that is (
4.1a
). We now apply Proposition
3.3
to
DF_{n}(Du_{n})
, thus obtaining

\|DF_{n}(Du_{n})\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{R})}\leq C,

where the uniform bound holds thanks to equations (
4.3
) and (
4.12
), being
B=B_{2R}
.
Setting

(4.13)

V_{n}=DF_{n}(Du_{n})

we can pick a subsequence such that
V_{n}\to V
weakly in
W^{1,2}(B_{R})
, strongly in
L^{2}(B_{R})
, and pointwise a. e. in
B_{R}
,
for a suitable
V\in W^{1,2}(B_{R})
. Since
DF_{n}^{-1}\to DF^{-1}
locally uniformly thanks to Proposition
2.6
-(4), we infer that
DF_{n}^{-1}(V_{n})\to DF^{-1}(V)
a. e.. Then (
4.13
) implies that
Du_{n}\to DF^{-1}(V)
a. e., and in turn (
4.11
) allows the identification
V=DF(Du)
. This shows (
4.1c
) and the proof is thus complete.
∎

5.
Caccioppoli inequality

In this section we prove a Caccioppoli-type inequality. To this aim, let us first show the following result, which is a simplification of
[
23
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P
and
S
be
N\times N
symmetric matrices, with
P
positive definite. Then,

(5.1)

\left(P\,S,S\,P\right)_{2}\geq\frac{\lambda_{\rm min}(P)}{\lambda_{\rm max}(P)}|P\,S|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Let us observe that both sides of (
5.1
) are invariant by orthogonal change of basis, so we can assume that
P
is diagonal, with positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
obeying

\lambda_{j}\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\,\lambda_{i},\quad\text{where }\beta=\frac{\lambda_{\rm max}(P)}{\lambda_{\rm min}(P)},

for any
i,j=1,\dots,N
.
Then, if
S=(a_{ij})
, we have

\left(P\,S,S\,P\right)_{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\,a_{ij}\,\lambda_{j}\,a_{ji}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\,\lambda_{j}\,a_{ij}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}^{2}\,a_{ij}^{2}=\frac{1}{\beta}\,|P\,S|_{2}^{2},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Caccioppoli Inequality)
.

Let
F
be a smooth
H
-q. u. c. function and let
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R})
.
For
V
given by (
3.7
), assume that

(5.2)

{\rm div}\,V=0\qquad\text{in $B_{R}$}.

Moreover, for a given affine function
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, and all
\rho<R
, set

(5.3)

A(\ell,\rho)=\left\{x\in B_{\rho}:F(Du(x))\geq\ell(Du(x))\right\}.

Then, for all
\rho<R
, it holds

(5.4)

\int_{A(\ell,\rho)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq\left(\frac{\pi\,H}{R-\rho}\right)^{2}\,\int_{A(\ell,R)}|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dx.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and set
V_{\varepsilon}=V+\varepsilon\,Du=DF(Du)+\varepsilon\,Du
. Differentiating the latter gives

DV_{\varepsilon}=\big(D^{2}F(Du)+\varepsilon\,{\rm Id}\big)\,D^{2}u,

where the matrices
D^{2}F(Du)+\varepsilon\,{\rm Id}
and
D^{2}u
are symmetric positive definite and symmetric, respectively. By (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(Du)+\varepsilon\,{\rm Id})&\leq\lambda_{\rm max}(D^{2}F(Du))+\varepsilon\\
&\leq H\,\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(Du))+\varepsilon\\
&\leq H\,\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(Du)+\varepsilon\,{\rm Id})\end{split}

hence (
5.1
) gives
|DV_{\varepsilon}|_{2}^{2}\leq H\,(DV_{\varepsilon},DV_{\varepsilon}^{t})_{2}
.
Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
provides

|DV|_{2}^{2}\leq H\,(DV,DV^{t})_{2}.

We want to integrate the above inequality over
A(\ell,r)
, for
r<R
, assuming for the moment that
\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}
is a
C^{1}
hypersurface in
B_{R}
.
Using the divergence theorem gives

(5.5)

\begin{split}\int_{A(\ell,r)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx&\leq H\,\int_{A(\ell,r)}\left(DV,DV^{t}\right)_{2}\,dx\\
&=H\,\int_{A(\ell,r)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})_{i}\,dx\\
&=H\,\int_{A(\ell,r)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Big(V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\Big)_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,\Div V_{j}\,dx\\
&=H\,\int_{\partial A(\ell,r)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,n^{i}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1},\end{split}

where
n
denotes the exterior normal to
\partial A(\ell,r)
and where we used the fact that (
5.2
) implies
\Div V_{j}=0
for all
j=1,\dots,N
.
To compute the last integral in (
5.5
), notice that

\partial A(\ell,r)=\bigl(\partial B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)\geq\ell(Du)\}\bigr)\bigcup\,\bigl(B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}\bigr),

so that

n(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x}{|x|}&\text{if $x\in\partial B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)\geq\ell(Du)\}$}\\[15.0pt]
-\dfrac{D\big((F-\ell)(Du(x))\big)}{|D\big((F-\ell)(Du(x))\big)|}&\text{if $x\in B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}$}.\end{cases}

Let
x\in B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}
and compute

U^{i}(x):=\big((F-\ell)(Du(x))\big)_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(V^{k}-\ell_{k})\,u_{ki}\quad\text{as well as}\quad V^{i}_{j}=\sum_{h=1}^{N}F_{ih}(Du)\,u_{hj},

so that

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,n^{i}&=-\frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,(V^{k}-\ell_{k})\,u_{ki}\\
&=-\frac{1}{|U|}\,\sum_{i,j,k,h=1}^{N}F_{ih}(Du)\,u_{hj}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,u_{ik}\,(V^{k}-\ell_{k})\\
&=-\frac{1}{|U|}\,\big(D^{2}F(Du)\,U,U\big)<0,\end{split}

being
D^{2}F
non-negative definite.
Then the previous inequality implies that the rightmost integral in (
5.5
) evaluated on
B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}
has a negative sign and hence (
5.5
) reduces to

(5.6)

\begin{split}\int_{A(\ell,r)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx&\leq H\,\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)\geq\ell(Du)\}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}V^{i}_{j}\,(V^{j}-\ell_{j})\,n^{i}\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}\\
&\leq H\,\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)\geq\ell(Du)\}}|DV|_{2}\,|V-D\ell|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1},\end{split}

where in the last step we used the Schwarz inequality.

Let now
\zeta
be the function given by

\zeta(r)=\int_{A(\ell,r)}|DV|_{2}\,|V-D\ell|\,dx\quad\text{for all }0<r<R,

with first derivative

(5.7)

\zeta^{\prime}(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\{F(Du)\geq\ell(Du)\}}|DV|_{2}\,|V-D\ell|\,d{\mathcal{H}}^{N-1}.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,R])
with
\varphi(R)=0
.
We multiply (
5.6
) by
\varphi^{2}
and integrate in
[0,R]
. Taking (
5.7
) into account, integrating by parts and using the fact that
\varphi(R)=\zeta(0)=0
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}\varphi^{2}\,|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dr&\leq H\,\int_{0}^{R}\varphi^{2}\,\zeta^{\prime}\,dr\\
&=H\left(\varphi^{2}(R)\,\zeta(R)-\varphi^{2}(0)\,\zeta(0)\right)-2\,H\,\int_{0}^{R}\varphi\,\varphi^{\prime}\,\zeta\,dr\\
&\leq 2\,H\,\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}|\varphi||\varphi^{\prime}||DV|_{2}|V-D\ell|\;dx\,dr\\
&\leq 2\,H\left({\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}\varphi^{2}\,|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dxdr}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip-3.0pt\left({\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dxdr}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

which simplifies to

\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}\varphi^{2}\,|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dr\leq 4\,H^{2}\,\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dx\,dr.

Fix
0<\rho<R
. Taking advantage of the monotonicity of the functions

r\mapsto\int_{A(\ell,r)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx,\qquad r\mapsto\int_{A(\ell,r)}|V-D\ell|_{2}^{2}\,dx

we then have

\begin{split}\int_{A(\ell,\rho)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,\int_{\rho}^{R}\varphi^{2}\,dr&\leq\int_{\rho}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}\varphi^{2}\,|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dr\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}\varphi^{2}\,|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\,dr\\
&\leq 4\,H^{2}\,\int_{0}^{R}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{A(\ell,r)}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dx\,dr\\
&\leq 4\,H^{2}\int_{A(\ell,R)}|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dx\,\int_{0}^{R}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,dr,\end{split}

which implies

\int_{A(\ell,\rho)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq 4\,H^{2}\,I(R,\rho)\,\int_{A(\ell,R)}|V-D\ell|^{2}\,dx

for

I(R,\rho):=\inf\left\{\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{R}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\,dr}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\rho}^{R}\varphi^{2}\,dr}}:\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,R])\setminus\{0\},\varphi(R)=0\right\}.

A minimiser for this problem is found through elementary considerations as

\varphi(r)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $r\leq\rho$}\\[5.0pt]
\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\dfrac{r-\rho}{R-\rho}\right)&\text{if $r\in[\rho,R]$},\end{cases}

so that an explicit computation gives

I(R,\rho)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\,(R-\rho)^{2}},

and (
5.4
) follows.

To remove the assumption that the set
\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)\}
is
C^{1}
in
B_{R}
, we invoke Sard’s theorem,
which ensures that this property is satisfied by

\{F(Du)=\ell(Du)+k\}
, for a. e.
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Given
k_{n}\downarrow 0
, we can repeat the same argument as before, starting from (
5.5
), thus arriving to

(5.8)

\int_{A(\ell+k_{n},r)}|DV|_{2}^{2}\;dx\leq\left(\frac{\pi\,H}{R-\rho}\right)^{2}\int_{A(\ell+k_{n},R)}|V-D\ell|^{2}\;dx.

Notice that
A(\ell+k_{n},\cdot)\searrow A(\ell,\cdot)
, so we can pass to the limit in (
5.8
) by monotone (or dominated) convergence, and in turn (
5.4
) holds true also in this case.
∎

We report the following consequence, already present in
[
23
, proof of Theorem 3.2]
, for the sake of completeness.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
F
be a
H
-q. u. c. function, let
u
be a local minimiser for
J
in
\Omega
in the sense of Definition
3.1
and let
V
be given by (
3.7
). Then,

(5.9)

\int_{B_{R}}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{R^{N+2}}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}|V|\,dx\right)^{2}

for
C=C(H,N)
and all balls
B_{R}
such that
B_{4R}\subseteq\Omega
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
4.1
on
B_{2R}
we can suppose that both
F
and
u
are smooth, since for the regularised problems the left-hand side of (
5.9
) is lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak convergence in
W^{1,2}(B_{R})
, while the right-hand side is (up to subsequences) convergent. We thus apply Lemma
5.2
for
\ell(z)\equiv\min F
to get

(5.10)

\int_{B_{\rho}}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\,\int_{B_{r}}|V|^{2}\,dx

for all
0<\rho<r<2R
. Let
E
be a continuous extension operator

E:W^{1,2}(B_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{2};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
31
, Theorem 12.83]
ensures that

\|E(U)\|_{2}\leq C\,\|E(U)\|_{1}^{\frac{2}{N+2}}\,\|DE(U)\|_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{N+2}}\qquad\forall\,U\in W^{1,2}(B_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})

for
C=C(N)
, so that by the continuity of
E

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}|U|^{2}\,dx&\leq\int_{B_{2}}|E(U)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\,\left(\int_{B_{2}}|E(U)|\,dx\right)^{{\frac{4}{N+2}}}\left(\int_{B_{2}}|DE(U)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{{\frac{N}{N+2}}}\\
&\leq C\,\left(\int_{B_{1}}|U|\,dx\right)^{\frac{4}{N+2}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}|DU|^{2}\,dx\right)^{{\frac{N}{N+2}}}\end{split}

for a bigger constant depending on the operator norms of
E:L^{1}(B_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{1}(B_{2};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
E:W^{1,2}(B_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{2};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
we apply to the previous estimate Young’s inequality with exponents
(N+2)/2
and
(N+2)/N
to get

\int_{B_{1}}|U|^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon^{2}\,\int_{B_{1}}|DU|^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{N}}\left(\int_{B_{1}}|U|\,dx\right)^{2},

which rescales to

(5.11)

\int_{B_{r}}|U|^{2}\,dx\leq(\varepsilon\,r)^{2}\,\int_{B_{r}}|DU|^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{(\varepsilon\,r)^{N}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|U|\,dx\right)^{2}\qquad\forall\,U\in W^{1,2}(B_{r};\mathbb{R}^{N})

for some
C=C(N)>0
.
Choose

\varepsilon^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(r-\rho)^{2}}{\widetilde{C}\,r^{2}}

and apply (
5.11
) with
U=V
and such
\varepsilon
to (
5.10
) to get

\int_{B_{\rho}}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{B_{r}}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{(r-\rho)^{N+2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|V|\,dx\right)^{2}

for all
0<\rho<r<2R
. An application of
[
20
, Lemma 3.1, Ch. 5]
shows that then

\int_{B_{\rho}}|DV|_{2}^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{(r-\rho)^{N+2}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}|V|\,dx\right)^{2}

which, for
r=2R
,
\rho=R
, provides (
5.9
).
∎

6.
A family of
1
-homogeneous functions

Let
F
be a
H
-q. u. c. function. In this section we gather the basic properties of the function

(6.1)

G(z)=F(DF^{-1}(z))

and of the family of Minkowski functionals related to it, namely,

(6.2)

g_{k}(z)=\inf\{t>0:\,G(z/t)<k\},

for any
k\geq 0
. Note that, since
DF
is a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the function in (
6.1
) is well defined.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
F
be a
H
-q. u. c. function fulfilling (
2.7
).
Then,
G
is coercive and
for any
k\geq 0
the set
\{G\leq k\}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin.

Proof.

We apply (
2.13
) with the vector
DF^{-1}(z)
and use (
2.7
) to have

G(z)\geq|DF^{-1}(z)|\,|z|/C.

Furthermore, the quasisymmetry (
2.3
) of
DF^{-1}
together with
DF^{-1}(0)=0
give

|DF^{-1}(z)|\geq\frac{\sup_{|v|=1}|DF^{-1}(v)|}{C\,\eta_{H}(1/|z|)}.

Gathering the previous inequalities and making use of (
2.19
) and (
2.17
) we have

G(z)\geq\frac{\sup_{|v|=1}|DF^{-1}(v)|}{C}\,|z|^{1+1/H}\quad\text{for all }|z|>1,

thus proving the coercivity of
G
. In particular for any
k>0
the set
\{G\leq k\}

is compact with nonempty interior.

Let us now show that
\{G\leq k\}
is star shaped with respect to the origin. Since
G(0)=0
by the normalisation (
2.7
), it suffices to show that for any
z\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
the function
t\mapsto G(t\,z)
is nondecreasing. To this end, suppose first that
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
fulfils

(6.3)

\lambda_{\rm min}(D^{2}F(z))\geq\mu

for some
\mu>0
independent of
z
. Then
DF
is a diffeomorphism and it holds

(6.4)

DG(z)=z\,D(DF^{-1})(z)=(D^{2}F)^{-1}(DF^{-1}(z))\,z,

therefore

\frac{d}{dt}G(t\,z)=\left(DG(t\,z),z\right)=t\left((D^{2}F)^{-1}(DF^{-1}(t\,z))\,z,z\right)\geq 0,

proving the claim. In the general case, thanks to Proposition
2.6
-(4), we know that any
H
-q. u. c. function
F
can be approximated in
C^{1}
by a sequence
(F_{n})
of
C^{2}
and
H
-q. u. c. functions obeying (
6.3
), and such that
DF_{n}^{-1}\to DF^{-1}
locally uniformly. Hence,
G_{n}=F_{n}\circ DF_{n}^{-1}\to G
locally uniformly, too. It follows that
t\mapsto G(t\,z)
is nondecreasing, being the pointwise limit of a sequence of nondecreasing functions. The proof is thus complete.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In general, the sets
\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:G(w)\leq k\}=\{DF(z):F(z)\leq k\}
are not convex, so that the functions
g_{k}
defined in (
6.2
) may fail to be norms. Moreover, by employing the approximation in (
2.15
) in the previous proof, we see that the
H
-q. u. convexity assumption is not needed to prove the second statement of the previous proposition and, given any convex
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
obeying (
2.7
), the sets
\{DF(z):F(z)\leq k\}
, while not convex in general, are always star-shaped with respect to the origin.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
F
be a

and

) and

(2)

For some

(6.7)

Proof.

Since
DG
given in (
6.4
) doesn’t vanish outside the origin, the implicit function theorem implies that the boundary

is a

-hypersurface

is

outside the origin. By construction

is positively
1

\displaystyle\big(Dg_{k}(z),z\big)

\displaystyle=g_{k}(z),\quad\forall\,z\neq 0,

(6.9)

\displaystyle Dg_{k}(\lambda\,z)

\displaystyle=Dg_{k}(z),\quad\forall\,\lambda>0,z\neq 0,

From the last property, in particular, it holds

on
\{G=k\}
, and therefore the Lagrange multiplier rule gives

for some

and all

.

This, together with (

), implies that on
\{G=k\}
we have

1=g_{k}(z)=(Dg_{k}(z),z)=\alpha(z)\,(DG(z),z),

and in turn

Let

. Thanks to (
6.4
) we get

(6.11)

|Dg_{k}(z)|=\frac{|DG(z)|}{\big(DG(z),z\big)}=\frac{|z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w)|}{\big(z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w),z\big)},\qquad\text{with }w=DF^{-1}(z).

Let
\Lambda(w)
and

be the maximum and minimum eigenvalues
of

at

, respectively. Then it holds

|z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w)|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda(w)}|z|\quad\text{as well as}\quad\big(z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w),z\big)\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda(w)}\,|z|^{2}.

Therefore, thanks to (
6.11
), (
6.12
), and (
2.5
) we have

(6.13)

|Dg_{k}(z)|=\frac{|z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w)|}{\big(z\,(D^{2}F)^{-1}(w),z\big)}\leq\frac{|z|\Lambda(w)}{|z|^{2}\lambda(w)}\leq\frac{H}{|z|}\qquad\text{on $\{G=k\}$}.

Using (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) gives

|Dg_{k}(z)|=\left|Dg_{k}\left(\frac{z}{g_{k}(z)}\right)\right|.

Therefore we can use (
6.13
) with the vector
\displaystyle\frac{z}{g_{k}(z)}
, thus obtaining

This proves (
6.5
).
To verify (
6.6
), notice that under the stated assumptions on

the map

is strictly increasing on

, hence
\{g_{k}=1\}=\{G=k\}
.
By the

, it then holds

\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}g_{k}=\sup_{\{G=k\}}|z|\quad\text{as well as}\quad\inf_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}g_{k}=\inf_{\{G=k\}}|z|.

Since
\{G\leq k\}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, we can choose
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
in

such that

By (

) applied to

and (

) we have

(6.14)

\frac{\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}g_{k}}{\inf_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}g_{k}}=\frac{|\bar{x}|}{|\bar{y}|}=\frac{|DF(DF^{-1}(\bar{x}))|}{|DF(DF^{-1}(\bar{y}))|}\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|DF^{-1}(\bar{x})|}{|DF^{-1}(\bar{y})|}\right).

By the definition of

, it holds

, hence (

) gives

so that

\frac{|DF^{-1}(\bar{x})|}{|DF^{-1}(\bar{y})|}\leq\frac{1}{\eta_{1+H,1+1/H}^{-1}(1/C)}.

Inserting this estimate into (
6.14
) gives us the conclusion.

Finally, to prove the last assertion, let us fix
k>h
, choose
\tilde{x}
such that

and set

Then in holds

, so that
\tilde{y}\in\{G=h\}
. Set furthermore

(6.15)

x:=DF^{-1}(\tilde{x})\in\{F=k\}\quad\text{as well as}\quad y:=DF^{-1}(\tilde{y})\in\{F=h\}.

By the definition of
\tilde{x}
and

and the

(6.16)

\inf_{\{G\geq k\}}(g_{h}-1)=\frac{g_{h}(\tilde{x})-g_{h}(\tilde{y})}{g_{h}(\tilde{y})}{=\frac{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|}{|\tilde{y}|}.}

Using the distortion estimate (

) to the map

\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|}{|\tilde{y}|}\right)

) for the function

\frac{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}|}{|\tilde{y}|}\geq\eta_{H}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{C}\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right)\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\eta_{H}^{-1}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right),

which inserted into (
6.16
) reads as

\inf_{\{G\geq k\}}(g_{h}-1)\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\eta^{-1}_{H}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right).

By (

) we have

\begin{split}k-h&=F(x)-F(y)\leq\int_{0}^{1}|DF(y+t\,(x-y))|\,|x-y|\,dt\\
&\leq|x-y|\,\left[\int_{0}^{1}|DF(y+t\,(x-y))-DF(y)|\,dt+|DF(y)|\right].\end{split}

To estimate the rightmost integral in the above inequality we use (

) for the map

&\leq C\,|DF(y)|\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right)\,\int_{0}^{1}\eta_{H}(t)\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{CH}{H+1}\,|DF(y)|\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right).\end{split}

Therefore (

On the other hand, (

h=F(y)\geq|y|\,|DF(y)|/C.

Gathering the previous two inequalities gives

(6.19)

\frac{k-h}{h}\leq C\,\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\,\left[1+\eta_{H}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\right)\right].

Since

t\,(1+\eta_{H}(t))\leq 2\,\eta_{1,H+1}(t),

we can rewrite (
6.19
) as

\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}\geq\eta_{1,H+1}^{-1}\left(\frac{k-h}{C\,h}\right)

and recalling (
6.17
) gives

\inf_{\{G\geq k\}}(g_{h}-1)\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\eta^{-1}_{H}\left(\eta_{1,H+1}^{-1}\left(\frac{k-h}{h}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{C}\,\eta^{-1}_{1/H,H(H+1)}\left(\frac{k-h}{h}\right),

where we used (
2.20
), (
2.21
) to clean up the estimate.
Using (
2.17
) gives (
6.7
) and then the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 7.1
.

be a
H

) and let

such that,

\sup_{B_{R/2}}|F(Du)|\leq C\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx.

Proof.

Let us suppose for the moment that both

and

.
Moreover, by considering

\begin{split}\left(\int_{A(k,r)}|g_{k}(V)-1|^{2^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}&\leq\left(\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}|\varphi\,(g_{k}(V)-1)_{+}|^{2^{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\\

) with

2.20
) as

M\geq C\,\eta_{H+1,1+\frac{1}{H}}\left(\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{R}}|V|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Now recall that by (
3.8
) it holds

\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{R}}|V|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\,\eta_{\frac{H}{H+1},\frac{1}{H+1}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx\right).

Then, thanks to (
2.21
) and again (
2.20
) it is sufficient to require

(7.17)

M\geq C\,\eta_{H+1,1+\frac{1}{H}}\circ\eta_{\frac{H}{H+1},\frac{1}{H+1}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx\right)=C\,\eta_{H}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx\right).

Choosing
M
such that the equality in (
7.17
) holds true gives, thanks to (
7.16
), that
F(Du)\leq M
in
B_{R/2}
, with the estimate

\sup_{B_{R/2}}|F(Du)|\leq C\,\eta_{H}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx\right).

We can then remove assumption (
7.2
) to get, for a general
i_{F}>0
,

(7.18)

\sup_{B_{R/2}}|F(Du)|\leq C\,i_{F}\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{1}{i_{F}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx\right).

We next take advantage of the invariances of this estimate. For any
\lambda>0
, the function
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda\,u(x)
is a local minimiser in
\Omega
for the functional
J_{\lambda}
having integrand

F_{\lambda}(z)=F(z/\lambda).

In particular, (
7.18
) holds true also for such
F_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}
, that is,

(7.19)

\sup_{B_{R/2}}|F_{\lambda}(Du_{\lambda})|\leq C\,i_{F_{\lambda}}\,\eta_{H}\left(\frac{1}{i_{F_{\lambda}}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F_{\lambda}(Du_{\lambda})\,dx\right).

Notice that
F_{\lambda}
is still
H
-q. u. c. and that

(7.20)

i_{F_{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\inf_{|z|=1}\left|DF\Big(\frac{z}{\lambda}\Big)\right|.

For any
z,w
of unit norm, the distortion estimate (
2.3
) applied first to the vectors
z/\lambda,w
and then to
w,z/\lambda
gives

\left|DF\Big(\frac{z}{\lambda}\Big)\right|\leq C\,|DF(w)|\,\eta_{H}(1/\lambda)\quad\text{as well as}\quad|DF(w)|\leq C\,\left|DF\Big(\frac{z}{\lambda}\Big)\right|\,\eta_{H}(\lambda),

respectively, so that

\frac{i_{F}}{C\,\eta_{H}(\lambda)}\leq\left|DF\Big(\frac{z}{\lambda}\Big)\right|\leq C\,i_{F}\,\eta_{H}(1/\lambda)

for all
|z|=1
. Taking the infimum for such
z
’s and using (
7.20
) give

\frac{i_{F}}{C\,\eta_{H}(\lambda)\,\lambda}\leq i_{F_{\lambda}}\leq C\,i_{F}\,\frac{\eta_{H}(1/\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Since
DF
is
H^{N-1}
-quasiconformal, it is also locally
1/H
-Hölder continuous and so is the map
t\mapsto|DF(t\,z)|
. It follows that
\lambda\mapsto i_{F_{\lambda}}
is continuous on
]0,+\infty[
, being an infimum of equicontinuous functions. Moreover, it fulfils

\begin{split}\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}i_{F_{\lambda}}&\geq\frac{i_{F}}{C}\,\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\eta_{H}(\lambda)\,\lambda}=\infty\quad\text{as well as}\quad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\infty}i_{F_{\lambda}}\leq C\,i_{F}\,\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\infty}\frac{\eta_{H}(1/\lambda)}{\lambda}=0.\end{split}

Thanks to the intermediate value theorem we can then choose
\lambda
so that

\frac{1}{i_{F_{\lambda}}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F_{\lambda}(Du_{\lambda})\,dx=\frac{1}{i_{F_{\lambda}}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx=1.

For such
\lambda
and corresponding
u_{\lambda}
,
F_{\lambda}
, (
7.19
) becomes (
7.1
), since

\sup_{B_{R/2}}F_{\lambda}(Du_{\lambda})=\sup_{B_{R/2}}F(Du)\quad\text{as well as}\quad i_{F_{\lambda}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F(Du)\,dx,

and

\eta_{H}\left(\frac{1}{i_{F_{\lambda}}}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F_{\lambda}(Du_{\lambda})\,dx\right)=\eta_{H}(1)=1.

This gives us the conclusion in the smooth case.

To remove the smoothness assumption on
F
and
u
, let
F_{n}
and
u_{n}
be the regularised approximations obtained thanks to Lemma
4.1
. Then we can apply the same argument as before to
F_{n},u_{n}
, thus obtaining

(7.21)

\sup_{B_{R/2}}|F_{n}(Du_{n})|\leq C\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B_{2R}}F_{n}(Du_{n})\;dx.

Now observe that (
4.1
) allows to assume (up to subsequences) that
DF_{n}(Du_{n})\to DF(Du)
pointwise a. e. in
B_{2R}
, while Proposition
2.6
-
(4)
ensures that
DF_{n}^{-1}\to DF^{-1}
locally uniformly, so that both
Du_{n}\to Du
and
F(Du_{n})\to F(Du)
, pointwise a. e. in
B_{R}
. This observation and the first estimate in (
4.1
) allow to pass to the limit in (
7.21
), thus obtaining again the conclusion.
∎

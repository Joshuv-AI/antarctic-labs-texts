Optimal Hardy-weights for elliptic operators with mixed boundary conditions
Yehuda Pinchover, Idan Versano

Abstract
. We construct families of optimal Hardy-weights for a subcritical linear second-order elliptic operator ( P , B ) (P,B) with degenerate mixed boundary conditions. By an optimal Hardy-weight for a subcritical operator we mean a nonzero nonnegative weight function W W such that ( P − W , B ) (P-W,B) is critical, and null-critical with respect to W W . Our results rely on a recently developed criticality theory for positive solutions of the corresponding mixed boundary value problem. 2000 ​ Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary ​ 35A23; Secondary 35B09, 35J08, 35J25. Keywords: Green function, Hardy inequality, minimal growth, Robin boundary condition.



1.
Introduction

The classical Hardy inequality states that if
n\geq 3
, then

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\phi|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

It is well known that the constant
((n-2)/2)^{2}
in (
1.1
) is sharp but not achieved in the appropriate Hilbert space.
In the past four decades,
Hardy-type inequalities were developed for vast classes of operators with different types of boundary conditions, see for example,
[
5
,
13
,
11
,
14
,
6
,
9
]
and references therein.
In
[
5
]
the authors established a general method to generate an explicit
optimal Dirichlet-Hardy-weight for a general (not necessarily symmetric) subcritical linear elliptic
operators either in divergence form or in non-divergence form defined on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
or on a noncompact manifold. In some definite
sense, an optimal Hardy-weight is ‘as large as possible’ Hardy-weight.

In the present paper, we utilize the approach developed in
[
5
,
19
]
to produce families of optimal Hardy-inequalities
for a general linear, second-order, elliptic operator with degenerate mixed boundary conditions. Our approach relies on
criticality theory of positive weak solutions for elliptic operators with mixed boundary conditions which has been recently established in
[
19
]
. Though our methods are similar to those in
[
5
,
19
]
, where (generalized) Dirichlet boundary conditions are considered, a more intricate treatment is required when mixed boundary conditions are considered.

We remark that in
[
14
]
(resp.,
[
9
]
) a variational approach was taken in order to produce Hardy-inequalities in a Lipschitz (resp.,
C^{2}
-convex) domain for the Laplace (resp.,
p
-Laplace) operator with degenerate Robin boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
.
Our approach does not assume convexity of the domain, and provides, in particular, a nontrivial improvement of
a certain (Robin) Hardy-inequality obtained in
[
14
]
for the Laplacian in the exterior of the unit ball (see Example
4.2
). See also
[
7
]
for related results.

We recall some essential definitions and results which
are discussed in detail in
[
20
]
.
Let
P
be a second-order, linear, elliptic operator with real measurable coefficients which is defined on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We assume that
P
is in the divergence form

(1.2)

Pu:=-\mathrm{div}\,\left[A(x)\nabla u+u{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}(x)\right]+{\mathbf{\bar{b}}}(x)\cdot\nabla u+c(x)u\qquad x\in\Omega.

Let
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
be a relatively open
C^{1}
-portion of
\partial\Omega
, and consider the oblique boundary operator

(1.3)

Bu:=\beta(x)\big(A(x)\nabla u+u{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}(x)\big)\cdot\vec{n}(x)+\gamma(x)u\qquad x\in\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}},

where
\vec{n}(x)
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
x\in\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
,
\xi\cdot\eta
denotes the Euclidean inner product of
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\beta,\gamma
are real measurable functions defined on
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
such that
\beta>0
on
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
.
The boundary of
\Omega
is then naturally decomposed to a disjoint union of its Robin part
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
, and its Dirichlet part
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
. That is,
\partial\Omega=\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\cup\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
, where
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\cap\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}=\emptyset
.
If further
{\mathbf{\bar{b}}}={\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}
in
\Omega
, we say that
(P,B)
is
symmetric
in
\Omega
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider the operator
(P,B)
defined on
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
, where
P
and
B
are of the forms (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), respectively.

•

We say that
(P,B)
is nonnegative in
\Omega
(in short
(P,B)\geq 0
in
\Omega
) if there exists a positive weak solution to the mixed boundary value problem

(
P,B
)

\begin{cases}Pu=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
Bu=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}.\end{cases}

•

We say that
W\gneqq 0
is a Hardy-weight for
(P,B)
in
\Omega
if

(P-W,B)\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

•

A Hardy-weight
W
is said to be a Dirichlet-Hardy-weight if
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}=\emptyset
.

•

A nonnegative operator
(P,B)
in
\Omega

is said to be subcritical (resp., critical) in
\Omega
if
(P,B)
admits (resp., does not admit) a Hardy-weight for
(P,B)
in
\Omega
.

By
[
20
, Theorem 5.19]
,
(P,B)
is subcritical in
\Omega
if and only if
(P,B)
admits a positive minimal Green function
G_{P,B}^{\Omega}(x,y)
in
\Omega
. On the other hand,
(P,B)
is critical in
\Omega
if and only if the equation
(P,B)u=0
admits (up to a multiplicative constant) a unique positive supersolution
\phi
in
\Omega
. In fact,
\phi
is a positive solution, called the
(Agmon) ground state
(for the definition of a ground state see Definition
2.28
). Furthermore,
(P,B)
is critical in
\Omega
if and only if
(P^{\star},B^{*})
is critical in
\Omega
, where
(P^{\star},B^{*})
is the formal adjoint of
(P,B)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)

[
20
, Corollary 5.20]
.

Next we recall the notion of optimal Hardy-weight and optimality at
\infty

[
5
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
W
be a Hardy-weight for
(P,B)
in
\Omega
. A Hardy-weight
W
is said to be optimal in
\Omega
if
(P-W,B)
is critical in
\Omega
and
\int_{\Omega}\phi\phi^{*}W\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty
, where
\phi
and
\phi^{*}
are respectively, the ground states of
(P-W,B)
and
(P^{\star}-W,B^{*})
in
\Omega
. In this case we say that
(P-W,B)
is null-critical with respect to the weight
W
.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a Hardy-weight
W
is optimal at infinity in
\Omega
if for any
K\Subset\overline{\Omega}
,

\partial K\cap\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}=\emptyset
, and
\partial K\cap\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\Subset\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
with respect to the relative topology on
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
(in short,
K\Subset_{R}\Omega
), we have

\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mid(P-\lambda W,B)\geq 0\mbox{ in }\Omega\setminus K\}=1.

Remark 1.4
.

The definition of an optimal Hardy-weight in
[
5
]
includes the requirement that
W
should be optimal at infinity in
\Omega
. But, for the case
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}=\emptyset
, it is proved in
[
15
]
that if
P-W
is null-critical with respect to
W
in
\Omega
, then
W
is also optimal at infinity. The same proof applies to the more general case considered in the present paper, hence, in Definition
1.2
we avoid the requirement of optimality at infinity.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(P,B)
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
, and let
G(x,y):=G^{\Omega}_{P,B}(x,y)
the corresponding positive minimal Green function. Fix
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
The Green potential with a density
\varphi
is the function

G_{\varphi}(x):=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Next, we define the notion of a function vanishing near the Dirichlet boundary part and at
\infty
.
We first need to define an exhaustion of
\Omega
subordinated to
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
.

Definition 1.6
.

A sequence
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega

is called an exhaustion of
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
if it is
an increasing sequence of Lipschitz domains such that
\Omega_{k}\Subset_{R}\Omega_{k+1}\Subset_{R}\Omega
, and
\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{\Omega_{k}}=\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
.
For
k\geq 1
we denote :

\partial\Omega_{k,\mathrm{Rob}}:=\mathrm{int}(\partial\Omega_{k}\cap\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}})\quad\partial\Omega_{k,\mathrm{Dir}}:=\partial\Omega_{k}\setminus\partial\Omega_{k,\mathrm{Rob}},\quad\Omega_{k}^{*}:=(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}})\setminus\overline{\Omega_{k}}.

Remark 1.7
.

For the existence of such an exhaustion see the appendix in
[
20
]
. We note that the construction of
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
ensures that
\partial\Omega^{*}_{k,\mathrm{Rob}}
is Lipschitz as well.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
K\Subset\Omega
and
f\in C((\overline{\Omega\setminus K})\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}})
.
We say that

(1.4)

\lim_{x\to\infty_{\mathrm{Dir}}}f(x)=0

if for any
\varepsilon>0
and any exhaustion
\{\Omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
, there exists
k_{0}
such that

|f(x)|<\varepsilon
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k_{0}}
.

Remark 1.9
.

(1)

In the case
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\!=\!\emptyset
, (
1.4
) is equivalent to the condition

\lim\limits_{x\to\overline{\infty}}f(x)=0
, where
\overline{\infty}
denotes the ideal point in the one-point compactification of
\Omega
.

(2)

Let
K\Subset\Omega
, then
f\in C((\overline{\Omega\setminus K})\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}})
satisfying (
1.4
) is bounded in
\Omega\setminus K
.

The first main result of the paper provides an explicit family of optimal Hardy weights. It reads as follows:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let Assumptions
2.5
hold in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, (
n\geq 2
) containing
x_{0}
. Let
(P,B)
be a subcritical operator in
\Omega
and let
G(x):=G_{P,B}^{\Omega}(x,x_{0})
be its minimal positive Green function with singularity at
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let
G_{\varphi}
be the Green potential with a density
0\lneqq\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Assume that there exists
u\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{P,B}(\Omega)
satisfying Ancona’s condition

(1.5)

\lim_{x\to\infty_{\mathrm{Dir}}}\dfrac{G(x)}{u(x)}=0.

Let

0\leq a\leq\dfrac{1}{\sup\limits_{\Omega}{(G_{\varphi}/u)}}\,,\quad f_{w}(t):=\sqrt{2t-at^{2}},\quad\mbox{and }\;\;W:=\frac{P\left(uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)\right)}{uf_{w}\left(G_{\varphi}/u\right)}\,.

Then
(P-W,B)
is critical in
\Omega
, and

(1.6)

W=\frac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}(2-aG_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\qquad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi).

Furthermore, assume that one of the following regularity conditions are satisfied.

(1)

(P,B)
is symmetric,
A\in C^{0,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}},\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
,
{\mathbf{\bar{b}}}={\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}\in C^{\alpha}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
c\in L^{\infty}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}})
, and
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\in C^{1,\alpha}
.

(2)

\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
,
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
are both relatively open and closed sets,
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}
is bounded and admits a finite number of connected components, and the coefficients of
P
are smooth functions in
\Omega
(or more generally,
A\in C^{\lceil(3n-2)/2\rceil,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}})
,
{\mathbf{\tilde{b}}}\in C^{\lceil(3n-2)/2\rceil,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
{\mathbf{\bar{b}}}\in C^{\lceil(3n-2)/2\rceil-1,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
c\in C^{\lceil(3n-2)/2\rceil-1,1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
).

Then
W
is an optimal Hardy-weights for
(P,B)
in
\Omega
.

Remark 1.11
.

Theorem
1.10
is proved in
[
5
]
for the specific (classical) case

\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}=\emptyset
and
a=0
. Moreover, Theorem
1.10
with
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}=\emptyset
and
a>0
was proved in
[
19
]
, and provides greater Hardy-weights than the optimal Hardy-weight

given in
[
5
]
, in the sense that

W=\dfrac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}\big(2-a(G_{\varphi/u})\big)^{2}}>W_{\mathrm{class}}:=\dfrac{|\nabla(G_{\varphi}/u)|_{A}^{2}}{4(G_{\varphi}/u)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{in }\Omega\setminus(\mbox{\rm supp}\,(\varphi)\cup\mathrm{Crit}(G_{\varphi}/u)).

Clearly, the above inequality holds true also in the case
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 3.1

(Khas’minskiĭ-type criterion)
.

Let

is a Lipschitz subdomain. Assume that

\lim_{x\to\infty_{\mathrm{Dir}}}\frac{u_{0}(x)}{u_{1}(x)}=0.

Then

is a a ground state of
(P,B)w=0
in
\Omega
, and therefore,
(P,B)
is critical in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We need to prove that
u_{0}
has minimal growth at infinity. Let

be an exhaustion of

\overline{\Omega}\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}
and a Lipschitz subdomain

such that

, and

is Lipschitz (see Remark

).
Fix

and let

having minimal growth in a neighborhood of infinity in
\Omega
.
Let

be fixed such that

(3.2)

C^{-1}G(x)\leq u_{0}(x)\leq CG(x)\qquad\mbox{for all~}x\in\overline{\Omega_{1}\setminus K^{\prime}}.

The minimal growth of

implies

(3.3)

C^{-1}G\leq u_{0}\qquad\mbox{in~}\Omega\setminus K^{\prime}.

Furthermore, (
3.1
) implies that for any

, the exists

such that

u_{0}\leq\varepsilon u_{1}\leq CG+\varepsilon u_{1}\qquad\mbox{on~}\partial{\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega}.

Notice that

and

belong to

C(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Dir}}\cup K))\cap\mathcal{SH}(\Omega\setminus K)

Consider the set

.
Then, Corollary
2.4
and (

The generalized maximum principle (Lemma

then implies

u_{0}\leq CG+\varepsilon u_{1}\qquad\mbox{in~}D_{k}.

Letting

u_{0}\leq CG+\varepsilon u_{1}\qquad\mbox{in~}\Omega\setminus K^{\prime}.

Letting

and (

) imply,

u_{0}\asymp G
in
\Omega\setminus K^{\prime}
, namely,

is a ground state, and therefore,

is critical in
\Omega
.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma

v:=\sqrt{(|x|-1+\varepsilon)|x|^{1-n}}\in\mathcal{RSH}_{P,B}(\Omega)

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta v-\dfrac{(n-1)(n-3)v}{4|x|^{2}}-\dfrac{v}{4(|x|-1+\varepsilon)^{2}}=0&\mbox{in~}\Omega,\\[17.07164pt]
\nabla v\cdot\vec{n}+\gamma v=\dfrac{-1+\varepsilon(n+2\gamma-1)}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\geq 0&\mbox{on~}\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}.\end{cases}

Hence, the
AP
theorem
[
20
]
implies the Hardy-type inequality in
H^{1}(\Omega)

(4.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}}\!\!\gamma\phi^{2}\!\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\geq\int_{\Omega}\left[\dfrac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4|x|^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{4(|x|-1+\varepsilon)^{2}}\right]\!\phi^{2}\!\,\mathrm{d}x.

Remark 4.3

(Improved Hardy-inequality in the exterior of the unit ball)
.

Assume further that
\gamma\geq 0
is constant.
In
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
, (
4.1
) has been obtained for the case
\varepsilon=(2\gamma)^{-1}
.
Obviously, by letting

\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\gamma}:=(n-1+2\gamma)^{-1}
in (
4.1
) we obtain an improvement of the Hardy-type inequality in
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
. In particular, the function

v_{\gamma}:=\sqrt{(|x|-1+\varepsilon_{\gamma})|x|^{1-n}}

is a positive solution of the equation

(4.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v-\dfrac{v(n-1)(n-3)}{4|x|^{2}}-\dfrac{v}{4(|x|-1+\varepsilon_{\gamma})^{2}}=0&\mbox{in~}\Omega,\\
\nabla v\cdot\vec{n}+\gamma v=0&\mbox{on~}\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{Rob}}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, the function
w_{\gamma}:=v_{\gamma}\log(|x|-1+\varepsilon_{\gamma})
is a positive solution of the equation

(4.3)

-\Delta v-\dfrac{v(n-1)(n-3)}{4|x|^{2}}-\dfrac{v}{4(|x|-1+\varepsilon_{\gamma})^{2}}=0

in a neighborhood of
\infty
, satisfying

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{v_{\gamma}}{w_{\gamma}}=0.

By Lemma
3.1
, any positive solution
\phi
of (
4.3
) having minimal growth at
\infty
satisfies
\phi\asymp v_{\gamma}
in a neighborhood of
\infty
. As a result,
v_{\gamma}
is the ground state of (
4.2
). Moreover, it is easy to see that (
4.2
) is also null-critical, implying that the function

W:=\dfrac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4|x|^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{4(|x|-1+\varepsilon_{\gamma})^{2}}

is an optimal Hardy-weight of
(P,B)
in
\Omega
.
For an explicit formula for
G_{P,B}^{\Omega}(x,y)
in the cases
\gamma=0,(n-2)/2
, see
[
21
]
.

Lattices over finite group schemes and stratification
Tobias Barthel, Dave Benson, Srikanth B. Iyengar, Henning Krause, and Julia Pevtsova

Abstract
. This work concerns representations of a finite flat group scheme G G , defined over a noetherian commutative ring R R . The focus is on lattices, namely, finitely generated G G -modules that are projective as R R -modules, and on the full subcategory of all G G -modules projective over R R generated by the lattices. The stable category of such G G -modules is a rigidly-compactly generated, tensor triangulated category. The main result is that this stable category is stratified and costratified by the natural action of the cohomology ring of G G . Applications include formulas for computing the support and cosupport of tensor products and the module of homomorphisms, and a classification of the thick ideals in the stable category of lattices.

1.
Introduction

In recent years, there has been a surge of interest in the structure and properties of tensor triangulated categories arising in algebra, algebraic geometry, and topology. In the algebraic context the starting point is the groundbreaking contributions by Hopkins
[
29
]
and Neeman
[
37
,
39
]
. Since then, the subject has expanded into modular representation theory for finite groups, finite group schemes over fields, Lie algebras, rings of differential operators, and equivariant homotopy theory, among others. A central problem is the classification of thick ideals in essentially small tensor triangulated categories, along with the more challenging task of identifying localising and colocalising ideals in the associated big tensor triangulated categories.

In this work we provide such classification for the big category of representations of finite groups and finite group schemes over commutative noetherian rings. The tensor triangulated category we introduce serves as a unifying framework for two contexts that have been studied extensively. Specialised to the trivial group it is the derived category of a commutative noetherian ring, and the corresponding classification is due to Neeman. On the other end of the spectrum, when the coefficient ring
R
is a field, one gets the stable module category of a finite group or a finite group scheme. Here is our main theorem.

Theorem A

(Theorems
9.1
,
10.1
)
.

For
G
a finite flat group scheme over a commutative noetherian ring
R
, the tensor triangulated category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
is stratified and costratified by the canonical action of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,R)
.

The category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
arises as a suitable ind-completion of the bounded derived category
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
of
lattices
: representations of
G
that are finitely generated and projective over
R
. We provide two complementary constructions of this category: In Sections
2
and
4
, we use Frobenius exact categories to give a homological model of
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
. In Section
5
we study
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
as the ind-completion in the
\infty
-categorical sense. The ring
H^{*}(G,R)
, which identifies with
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{RG}(R,R)
, is a graded-commutative noetherian
R
-algebra, by recent work of van der Kallen
[
44
]
.

Group representations over rings are pervasive, encompassing representations over rings of integers of algebraic number fields; see for example
[
22
, Chapter 3]
. In this context the representations of interest are the torsion-free ones, that is to say, lattices, and these have become objects of study in their own right
[
40
,
42
,
45
]
. Lattices appear already in the work of Brauer and Nesbitt
[
20
]
in the context of integral representations of finite groups as an intermediary between ordinary and modular representations. While classification of indecomposable modules in such categories is usually out of reach, Theorem
A
provides structural information on the category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
. Namely, the stratification by
H^{*}(G,R)
that it gives can be restated as a natural bijection between the nonzero localising ideals of
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
, the nonzero colocalising ideals of
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
, and subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,R)
:

\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{localising ideals}\\
\text{of $\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)$}\end{gathered}\,\right\}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\;\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{subsets of}\\
\text{$\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,R)$}\end{gathered}\,\right\}\,\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\;\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{colocalising ideals}\\
\text{of $\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)$}\end{gathered}\,\right\}\,.

The general results in
[
14
,
16
]
allow us to deduce several other desirable consequences from Theorem
A
; see Section
10
for details.
These include the calculation of the Balmer spectrum
[
1
]
of
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
:

(1.1)

\operatorname{Spc}(\operatorname{rep}(G,R))\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,R)\,.

This isomorphism encodes a natural bijection between the nonzero thick ideals of
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
and specialisation closed subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,R)
. This is the restriction of the bijection above for the big category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
to the small category
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
. In view of the fact that classification of indecomposable lattices is generally a hopeless task, the parametrisation of thick tensor ideals offers a partial solution; it classifies
G
-lattices over
R
up to homological operations: shifts, extensions, retracts, and tensoring.

) for

]
.
The first result on classifying localising ideals in a big
category is due to Benson, Iyengar, and Krause
[

]
;
their classification recovers the case

of
Theorem
A
where

is a field and
G
is a finite group.
This was generalised by the last four authors to finite group schemes
in
[
18
]
.

Moving to more recent history, and in fact to the motivating development
for this work, we arrive at a theorem of Lau
[
33
]
who established
the calculation (
1.1
) for
G
a finite group and
R
any
commutative noetherian ring. Hence, Theorem
A
generalises
Lau’s work in two directions: from finite groups to finite group schemes,
and from
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
to its ind-completion
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
.Keeping in line with earlier work on representations over a field, both of
these passages are far from routine, and require new ideas and methods.

For a start, while the objects in
\operatorname{rep}(G,R)
can be described in simple terms and have been studied for long, as far as we know the category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
has not appeared in the literature before, and even the objects in it are mysterious, when

is not a regular ring. Our first task is to establish basic structural properties of
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
, and the paragraph below outlines some of our key findings.

The category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)

Throughout
G
is a finite flat group scheme over a noetherian commutative ring
R
. The group algebra
RG
is a cocommutative Hopf
R
-algebra that is a finitely generated and projective as an
R
-module. We consider representations of
G
over
R
which can be identified with
RG
-modules.

Let
\operatorname{Mod}(G,R)
be the category of
RG
-modules that are projective as
R
-modules, and
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
its full subcategory consisting of the finitely generated modules, in other words, lattices. We write
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
for the subcategory of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
consisting of complexes that are
K
-projective when restricted to
R
. For our purposes the more pertinent object is a subcategory of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
whose objects satisfy a local finiteness condition; this is the category
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
. When
R
is regular,
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
coincides with
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
, and this case is discussed in
[
5
,
17
]
. However, these categories differ for general rings. In Section
11
we discuss an example that illustrates this, based on the representation theory of
\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2
.

The key features of these homotopy categories and the associated stable categories are described in Sections
2
–
4
, including a recollement relating them; see Corollary
2.15
. The constructions apply more generally to any Frobenius
R
-algebra and the results of Section
2
are stated in that greater generality.

By way of motivation, we state fundamental properties satisfied by
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
that make it a suitable homotopy category to consider:

(1)

\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
is a rigidly-compactly generated symmetric tensor triangulated category with product
\otimes_{R}
, unit
R
, and
\operatorname{End}^{*}(R)\cong H^{*}(G,R)
.

(2)

The compact objects in
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
identify with

\operatorname{rep}(G,R)=\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))\,,

the bounded derived category of lattices.

Inspired by work in
[
33
]
, Barthel
[
4
]
proposed a homotopy theoretic model for
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
, proved that it satisfies the properties above, and that it is stratified by
H^{*}(G,R)
, when
R
is a Dedekind domain. Subsequently, this result was generalised to all regular rings
R
, from a homotopical
[
5
]
and a homological
[
17
]
perspective, but the methodology of neither work readily generalised to arbitrary noetherian coefficients. The current work provides a model that unifies all these frameworks.

In Section
5
we describe how one can enhance the structure on
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
so it becomes a symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category. This allows us to compare the model of this paper to the
\infty
-category of derived representations of a finite group
G
studied in
[
4
,
5
]
. The latter is a natural candidate suggested by the
\infty
-category context and that extends well to other ring spectra; see
[
6
]
. We prove that the two categories are equivalent in the strongest possible sense, thereby reconciling the homological with the homotopical approach; see Proposition
5.8
. This section serves as motivation and as a bridge from modular representation theory to higher algebra; the rest of the paper is independent of it.

Next we describe other key ingredients that go into the proof of Theorem
A
.

Reduction to fibres

As noted above,
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
is a rigidly-compactly generated symmetric tensor triangulated category. Heuristically, it can be viewed as a sheaf of categories over
\operatorname{Spec}R
with fibres
\operatorname{Rep}(G_{k(\mathfrak{p})},k(\mathfrak{p}))
, for
\mathfrak{p}
ranging over the prime ideals of
R
and
G_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
denoting the group scheme base-changed along
R\to k(\mathfrak{p})
. This perspective is crucial for Lau’s approach to computing the Balmer spectrum of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))
for a finite group
G
, and the approach to stratification in
[
17
,
28
]
.

Let
X_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})
denote the base change of an object
X\in\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
along
R\to k(\mathfrak{p})
. The results below illustrate how the global structure of
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
is controlled by its fibres.

Theorem B

(Theorem
8.1
)
.

Let
G
be a finite flat group scheme over a noetherian commutative ring
R
. The following statements hold.

(1)

(Detection) For any
X\in\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
one has

X=0\quad\iff\quad\text{$X_{k(\mathfrak{p})}=0$ for all $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R$.}

(2)

(Building) The following conditions are equivalent for
X,Y\in\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
:

(a)

X\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}(Y)
in
\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)
;

(b)

{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}({Y}_{k(\mathfrak{p})})

in
\operatorname{Rep}(G_{k(\mathfrak{p})},k(\mathfrak{p}))
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
.

As we explain in Section
3
, ‘detection’ holds for any Frobenius algebra; see Proposition
3.6
. Part (2), given in Theorem
8.1
is a generalisation of
[
17
]
from regular to arbitrary noetherian coefficients; it immediately implies (1), but from a conceptual point of view it is useful to separate these two conditions.

Another crucial result is that the spectrum of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,R)
is also controlled by the fibres.

Theorem C

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

For each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
base change along
R\to k(\mathfrak{p})
induces a homeomorphism

\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k(\mathfrak{p}))\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}(H^{*}(G,R)\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

The proof of this theorem occupies Section
6
. The ideas that go into it are rather different from those involved in proving Theorem
B
. They build on earlier work of Benson and Habegger
[
12
]
, and Lau
[
33
]
that establish the result for finite groups. The critical new inputs are recent results of van der Kallen
[
44
]
, concerning torsion and finite generation of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,R)
for a finite flat group scheme
G
over
R
; see Section
6.8
.

Theorems
B
and
C
allow us to employ the general theory of
[
13
,
14
]
for describing localising ideals in this context; we recall the relevant notions in Section
7
. One of the key insights of
[
17
]
is that stratification for regular rings may be checked fibrewise, thereby
reducing the problem to the case of field coefficients. After the preparation we have done so far, this now extends verbatim to arbitrary noetherian rings and finite group schemes, leading to the stratification part of Theorem
A
. The costratification is then a formal
consequence, by combining the abstract bootstrap theorem of
[
7
]
with fibrewise detection; see Sections

2.
Frobenius algebras

In this section we introduce the various module categories and homotopy categories of complexes that arise in our work. While our focus is on finite group schemes, some basic constructions and results apply more generally to Frobenius algebras. This is the content of this section. The methods build on those in
[
31
]
; we take this work, and also
[

]
, as our standard references for the material below.

Throughout
R
is a commutative ring (not necessarily noetherian) and
A
a
finite projective
R
-algebra
; that is to say, an
R
-algebra that is finitely generated and projective as an
R
-module. As usual
\operatorname{Mod}A
stands for the category of all left
A
-modules, but we drop the term ‘left’ in the sequel. We write
\operatorname{Proj}A
for the full subcategory of projective
A
-modules. Our focus is on the subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}A
consisting of modules that are projective as
R
-modules; we denote it
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
. We write
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
for the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
consisting of modules that are finitely generated over
R
.

Lemma 2.1
.

In
\operatorname{Mod}A
the subcategory
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
is extension closed and so carries a natural exact structure, given by the short exact sequences of
A
-modules.∎

Let
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))
be the full subcategory of projective objects in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
, and
\operatorname{Inj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))
the full subcategory of injective objects. The duality functor is an equivalence:

(2.2)

D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,R)\colon(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{mod}(A^{\mathrm{op}},R)\,.

For all
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
and
N\in\operatorname{mod}(A^{\mathrm{op}},R)
one has

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,DN)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}(N,DM)\,.

It follows that the exact category
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
has enough projective
and enough injective objects. We set

\omega_{A/R}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,R)\,;

this is the
dualising bimodule
of the
R
-algebra
A
.

For any
A
-module
M
let
\operatorname{add}M
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}A
consisting of direct summands of finite direct sums of copies of
M
, and
\mathrm{Add}\,M
denotes the corresponding subcategory where all direct sums are permitted. When
M
is in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
so is every module in
\mathrm{Add}\,M
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The exact category
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
has enough projective and enough
injective objects, with

\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))=\mathrm{Add}\,A=\operatorname{Proj}A\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Inj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))=\mathrm{Add}\,\omega_{A/R}\,.

Proof.

We view
\operatorname{Proj}R
as an exact category; in it all objects are projective and injective. The
forgetful functor
u\colon\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)\to\operatorname{Proj}R
is exact. Thus its
left adjoint
u_{\lambda}=A\otimes_{R}-
preserves projectivity, while
its right adjoint
u_{\rho}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,-)
preserves injectivity. For

M\in\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
the counit
\varepsilon_{M}\colon u_{\lambda}u(M)\to M

provides an epimorphism such that
u_{\lambda}u(M)
is projective,
while the unit
\eta_{M}\colon M\to u_{\rho}u(M)
provides a
monomorphism such that
u_{\rho}u(M)
is injective. The map

u(\varepsilon_{M})
is a split epimorphism, while
u(\eta_{M})
is a
split monomorphism. Thus
\varepsilon_{M}
and
\eta_{M}
provide exact
sequences in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
.

The equality
\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))=\operatorname{Proj}A
is clear since
u_{\lambda}
maps the category of free
R
-modules onto the category of free

A
-modules.

Given that
u_{\rho}
preserves injectives and that it also preserves all direct sums, since
A
is finitely generated as an
R
-module, it follows that the objects in
\operatorname{Inj}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))
are precisely the direct summands of direct sums of copies of
\omega_{A/R}
.
∎

We obtain a pair of functors

\Sigma,\Omega\colon\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)\to\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
by completing, for
any module
M
in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
, the morphisms
\eta_{M}
and
\varepsilon_{M}
to
exact sequences

(2.4)

\begin{gathered}0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\eta_{M}}u_{\rho}u(M)\longrightarrow\Sigma M\longrightarrow 0\\
0\longrightarrow\Omega M\longrightarrow u_{\lambda}u(M)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{M}}M\longrightarrow 0\,.\end{gathered}

Iterating this process yields an injective and projective resolution of

, which we denote
\mathbf{i}M
and
\mathbf{p}M
, respectively.

Derived categories

We write
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{A})
to denote the derived category of an exact category

Proposition 2.5
.

The inclusion
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}A
of exact categories induces a quotient functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}

with kernel given by the complexes in

Proof.

Consider the localising subcategory of

Proposition 2.6
.

The inclusions

The composite of functors in question and also the first one are fully faithful, because
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
is

cofinal

\Omega_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Coker}(P_{n+1}\to P_{n})

for each

Example 2.7

\cdots\xrightarrow{\ t\ }A\xrightarrow{\ t\ }A\xrightarrow{\ t\ }A\xrightarrow{\ t\ }\cdots\,.

Frobenius algebras

We are interested in the case when the exact category
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
is
Frobenius
, that is to say, it has enough projective and enough injective objects, and these coincide.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
A
a finite projective
R
-algebra. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

There is an equality
\operatorname{add}\omega_{A/R}=\operatorname{add}A
.

(2)

The exact category
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
is a Frobenius category.

(3)

The exact category
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
is a Frobenius category.

Proof.

The equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) is clear since
\operatorname{add}\omega_{A/R}
equals the category of injective objects in

\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
, while
\operatorname{add}A
equals the category of projectives.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

A ring
A
is a
Frobenius
R
-algebra
if it is a finite projective
R
-algebra satisfying the conditions in the result above; see the work of Scheja and Storch
[
41
, §14]
, where this notion is introduced via condition (1). When
R
is noetherian, this condition can be detected in terms of the fibres of
A
over
R
; see Lemma
3.3
. Evidently
A
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra if and only if so is
A^{\mathrm{op}}
, because of the duality (
2.2
).

The stable module category

For any Frobenius category
\mathcal{A}
we write
\operatorname{St}\mathcal{A}
for the

stable category
, obtained from
\mathcal{A}
by annihilating
the projective and injective objects; this carries a natural
triangulated structure
[
32
, Section 3.3]
. We denote
\Sigma
the suspension in

\operatorname{St}\mathcal{A}
, which for any
M\in\mathcal{A}
is given by an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow\Sigma M\longrightarrow 0

such that
Q
is an injective, and so also a projective, object. For
M,N
in
\mathcal{A}
we set

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)/\mathrm{PHom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)\,,

where
\mathrm{PHom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)
denotes the subgroup of morphisms
M\to N
that
factor through a projective object.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. We set

\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{St}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{St}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))\,,

and consider these categories with their natural triangulated structure.

The homotopy category of projectives

Let
\Lambda
be a ring and
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda)
its homotopy
category of (unbounded) complexes of projective
\Lambda
-modules. Consider the canonical functor
\mathbf{q}\colon\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda)\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
which inverts the quasi-isomorphisms. Taking projective resolutions yields a left adjoint
adjoint
X\mapsto\mathbf{p}X
that identifies the derived category
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}\Lambda)
with the full subcategory of
K
-projective
complexes
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}\Lambda)
.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. We write
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
for the full subcategory of complexes in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
that are
K
-projective when restricted to
R
; this is a localising subcategory. Let
\mathbf{Ac}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
denote the full subcategory of complexes in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
that are acyclic; this is again a localising subcategory. The term ‘acyclic’ refers to the exact structure of the ambient exact category, which is either
\operatorname{Mod}A
or
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
. In both cases we obtain the same class of acyclic complexes. It is clear that a complex is acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}A
if it is acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
. On the other hand, if a complex is acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}A
and also
K
-projective over
R
, then it is contractible over
R
and therefore acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
.

Recall that each
M
in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
has an injective and a projective resolution, denoted
\mathbf{i}M
and
\mathbf{p}M
; see (
2.4
). A
complete resolution
of an
M
in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
is an acyclic complex
\mathbf{t}M
of projective
A
-modules with
\operatorname{Coker}(d^{-1}_{\mathbf{t}M})=M
; here,
d^{-1}_{\mathbf{t}M}
denotes the
(-1)
st differential in the complex
\mathbf{t}M
. Then
\mathbf{i}M
and
\mathbf{t}M
are
K
-projective over
R
, since
A
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra; the complex
\mathbf{p}M
is always
K
-projective over
R
.

Lemma 2.9
.

When
A
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra the assignment
X\mapsto\operatorname{Coker}(d^{-1}_{X})
induces an
R
-linear triangle equivalence

\mathbf{Ac}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)\,.

Proof.

Each
X
in
\mathbf{Ac}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
is contractible when restricted to
R
. Thus the assignment

X\mapsto\operatorname{Coker}(d^{-1}_{X})
is well-defined. The quasi-inverse
takes
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
to a complete resolution
\mathbf{t}M
in

\operatorname{Proj}A
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

When
A
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra the canonical functor

\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)

induces a localisation sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Ac}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{p}}

The left adjoint
\mathbf{p}\colon\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\to\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)

. For any

.
∎

Compact generation

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. We write
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
for the localising subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
generated by the modules in
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
. By a
localising
subcategory
we mean a full subcategory closed under all coproducts and
satisfying the
two-out-of-three property
: for any exact sequence

0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow 0

if any two of
X,Y
, or
Z
is in the subcategory so is the third. The subcategory
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
contains all the projective objects in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
, and so is itself a Frobenius category, with induced exact structure. When
R
is regular
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)=\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
, by Lemma
2.16
, but not always; see Section
11
. Set

\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{St}(\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R))\,.

Here are the key properties of this triangulated category.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. The triangulated category
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
is compactly generated and its
subcategory of compact objects identifies with the idempotent
completion of
\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)
.

Proof.

The canonical functor
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)\to\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
preserves coproducts and hence
\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)
consists of compact objects. They generate
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
as a triangulated category by definition so an arbitrary compact object is a direct summand of an object in
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
; see
[
32
, Proposition 3.4.15]
.
∎

2.12
.

The category
\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)
is idempotent complete when
R
is a complete local ring. This need not be the case for a general
R
.

Indeed, the local ring
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{(x,y)}/(x^{2}-y^{2}(y+1))
, viewed as an algebra over
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{C}[x]_{(x)}
, is a Frobenius
R
-algebra; this can be checked easily. Since
R
is a DVR, a finitely generated module over it is projective (equivalently, free) if and only it is torsion-free. Thus an
A
-module is in
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
if and only if it has positive depth, that is to say, it is maximal Cohen–Macaulay. Thus
\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)
is the stable category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay
A
-modules. This category is not idempotent complete; see
[
32
, Lemma 6.2.12]
for a proof.

For an example where
A
is the group algebra of a finite group, see forthcoming work of J. Grodal and A. Krause.

Set
\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
. For a full additive subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
we write
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{C})
for the homotopy category of chain complexes. We set

\mathbf{K}^{+,b}(\mathcal{C})=\{X\in\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{C})\mid H^{n}X=0\textrm{ for }|n|\gg 0\}

where
\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathcal{C})
denotes the complexes
X
with
X^{n}=0
for
n\ll 0
, and the condition
H^{n}X=0
means that
d_{X}^{n-1}
can be written as the
composite

X^{n-1}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Ker}d^{n}_{X}\rightarrowtail X^{n}

of an admissible epimorphism and an admissible monomorphism in
\mathcal{A}
. In
particular, the subcategory
\mathbf{K}^{+,b}(\mathcal{C})
depends on the ambient
category
\mathcal{A}
, even though it is not part of the notation.
The subcategory
\mathbf{K}^{-,b}(\mathcal{C})
is defined analogously.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. For an object
X
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

X
is compact in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and
K
-projective over
R
.

(2)

X
fits into an extension
0\to X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to 0
such that

X^{\prime}=\mathbf{i}M
for some
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
and
X^{\prime\prime}
is perfect.

(3)

X
is in
\mathbf{K}^{+,b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
.

Moreover, such an
X
is perfect over
R
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Let
X
be compact and
K
-projective over
R
. The restriction functor
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)\to\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}R)
preserves compactness, since its right adjoint
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(A,-)
preserves coproducts. Thus
X
is compact over
R
. Since it is also
K
-projective over
R
we deduce that it is perfect over
R
. So
X
belongs to
\mathbf{K}^{+}(\operatorname{proj}A)
by
[
38
, Proposition 7.6]
,
and
H^{i}(X)=0
for all
i\geq n
and some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We truncate in degree
n
and write
X
as an
extension
0\to X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to 0
such that
X^{\prime}
is concentrated
in degrees
i\geq n
and
X^{\prime\prime}
is concentrated in degrees
i<n
. Clearly,
X^{\prime\prime}
is perfect and hence
X^{\prime}
is
K
-projective
over
R
. Splicing
X^{\prime\prime}
together with a projective resolution of

M\coloneqq\operatorname{Ker}d^{n}_{X}
, yields a complex over
R
that is acyclic and

K
-projective, and hence contractible. Thus

X^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{i}M
with
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) It suffices to verify that
X^{\prime\prime}
and
X^{\prime}
are compact and
K
-projective over
R
. Clearly
X^{\prime\prime}
has these properties. Also
X^{\prime}
is

K
-projective over
R
. For any
Y
in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
one has an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(\mathbf{i}M,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}(A)}(M,A)\,,

by
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
, so
X^{\prime}
is compact.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) This is clear.

The last assertion was verified in proving (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
∎

We write
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
for the localising subcategory of
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
that is generated by the objects
satisfying the equivalent conditions in Lemma
2.13
. Thus one has inclusions

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\subseteq\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\subseteq\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)

which become equalities when the ring
R
is regular; cf. Proposition
2.16
. In fact, it is not hard to verify that equality holds on the right only if
R
is regular. Equality holds on the left when, for instance,
A=R
, and also in some other cases, but not always; see Section
11
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. The triangulated category
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
is compactly generated and the canonical functor

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
induces a triangle
equivalence

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)^{c}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\,\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))\,.

Proof.

The triangulated category
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
is compactly generated by
construction. For the description of the compact objects recall
that
\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
is an exact category with enough injective
objects. Thus we have a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{K}^{+}(\operatorname{proj}A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))
which restricts to an
equivalence

\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)^{c}=\mathbf{K}^{+,b}(\operatorname{proj}A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R)).\qed

We write
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(A)
for the category of perfect complexes, which identifies with the full subcategory
of compact objects in
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
A
be a Frobenius
R
-algebra. Then the canonical functor
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
induces a recollement

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{p}}

and the pair of left adjoints
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{t})
induces (when restricted
to compact objects) a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))/\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(A)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{stmod}(A,R)\,.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{p}
is left adjoint to
\mathbf{q}\colon\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. The latter preserves coproducts so it admits a right adjoint, by Brown representability. This yields the right half of the recollement; the left half is a consequence.

Indeed, we have the full subcategory of acyclic complexes in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
on the left, that identifies with a localising subcategory of
\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)
by Lemma
2.9
. This subcategory is generated by the modules from

\operatorname{mod}(A,R)
since
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
is generated by their injective
resolutions, and therefore it equals
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
.

As to the description of the compact objects,
\mathbf{q}
preserves coproducts so the left adjoint
\mathbf{p}
preserves compactness. The compacts in
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
identify with the category
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(A)
of perfect complexes, while the compacts in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
identify with

\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(A,R))
by Proposition
2.14
. This yields the
last assertion, since for any Frobenius category
\mathcal{A}
with full
subcategory of projectives
\mathcal{P}
we have

\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathcal{A})/\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mathcal{P})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{St}\mathcal{A}\,,

for example by
[
32
, Proposition 4.4.18]
.
∎

Regular base

A commutative ring
R
is
regular
if it is noetherian and the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
has finite global dimension for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
; when the Krull dimension of
R
is finite,
R
is regular if and only if its global dimension is finite; see
[
21
, Section 2.2]
. In what follows, we often use the following characterisation of regularity: A noetherian commutative ring
R
is regular if and only if every complex of projective
R
-modules is
K
-projective; equivalently, every acyclic complex of projective modules is contractible; see, for instance,
[
30
, Section 3]
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
A
a Frobenius
R
-algebra. There are equalities

\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)=\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)\,.

Moreover, there is an equality
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}(A,R))=\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.

Proof.

By
[
38
, Proposition 7.14]
, the triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
is compactly generated. From Lemma
2.13
and regularity of
R
, it follows that each compact object
belongs to
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)
. Thus
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.
Comparing the diagrams in Proposition
2.10
and
Corollary
2.15
and using Lemma
2.9
, we conclude that
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)=\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)
. Therefore, any
M\in\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
may be represented by an object in
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
up to a projective summand. Since projectives are in
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
, it follows that
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)=\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
. The last equality follows from Proposition
2.5
once one observes that for any complex in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
being acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}A
implies contractible in
\operatorname{Mod}R
and therefore also acyclic in
\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)
.
∎

3.
Fibrewise detection

Let
R
be a noetherian commutative ring and
A
a finite projective
R
-algebra. In this section we prove that certain homological properties of
A
-modules can be detected locally. To that end, for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R
, let
k(\mathfrak{p})
denote the residue field of
R
at
\mathfrak{p}
, and for any
A
-complex
X
, set

{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\otimes_{R}k(\mathfrak{p})\,.

The assignment
X\mapsto{X}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
induces exact
fibre functors

\displaystyle\operatorname{Mod}(A,R)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}A_{k(\mathfrak{p})}=\operatorname{Mod}A_{k(\mathfrak{p})}

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A,R)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k(\mathfrak{p})},k(\mathfrak{p}))=\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A_{k(\mathfrak{p})})\,.

We prove that these functors are
jointly conservative
when

\mathfrak{p}
runs through
\operatorname{Spec}R
; see Proposition
3.6
. This requires some preparations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a noetherian commutative ring,
A
a flat
R
-algebra,
and
F
an
A
-module that is flat over
R
. If for each

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
the
A_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
-module
F_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
is flat, then the

A
-module
F
is flat.

Proof.

The argument follows Deligne’s proof of

[
24
, Prop. 5.5.23]
.

We begin with a general observation. For any ideal
I
of
R
we write

V(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid I\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}

for the corresponding closed subset. Then for
V=V(I)
and any
A
-module
M
its submodule
of sections

\varGamma_{V}M=\{x\in M\mid I^{n}x=0\text{ for some }n>0\}

equals the maximal
A
-submodule
N\subseteq M

supported on
V
, that is

N_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for
\mathfrak{p}\not\in V
. Note that
M=\operatorname*{colim}_{V}\varGamma_{V}M
where

V
runs through all closed subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

We prove by noetherian induction on closed subsets
V
of
\operatorname{Spec}R

that for each (right)
A
-module
M
supported on
V
one has

\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,F)=0\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

Say
V=V(I)
for some reduced ideal
I
in
R
. For each integer
n\geq 0
the subset

M_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in M\mid I^{n}x=0\}

is an
A
-submodule of
M
, and since
M
is supported on
V
we get that

M=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
.
Since
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,F)=\operatorname*{colim}_{n}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M_{n},F)
, it suffices to verify the desired vanishing for
M
such that
I^{n}M=0
. Then
M
has a finite filtration

0=I^{n}M\subseteq I^{n-1}M\subseteq\cdots\subseteq IM\subseteq M

by
A
-submodules and each subquotient is annihilated by
I
. Thus we can replace
M
by one of these subquotients and assume

A ring

if injective and projective

is also self-injective and the ring is artinian; cf.

Lemma 3.3

Let

Proof.

Since

An algebra

Suppose

4.
Finite flat group schemes

From now on
R
is a noetherian commutative ring and
G
a finite flat group scheme over
R
. The coordinate algebra
R[G]
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module and a commutative Hopf
R
-algebra. The group algebra
RG=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R[G],R)
is then a finitely generated projective
R
-module and a cocommutative Hopf algebra. Hence,
RG
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra, so the results of the previous sections apply. Examples of finite flat groups schemes are given at the end of this section.

There is an equivalence of categories between
R
-linear representations of
G
and
RG
-modules. Since
RG
is a Hopf algebra there is more structure associated to its various module categories. This is the topic of this section. In what follows, by a
G
-module we mean an
RG
-module, and we write
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
for
\operatorname{mod}(RG,R)
,
\operatorname{Proj}G
instead of
\operatorname{Proj}RG
, and so forth.

Tensor structure

Given
G
-modules
X
and
Y
, there is a natural diagonal
G
-module structure on
X\otimes_{R}Y
, obtained by restricting its

(G\times G)
-module structure along the coalgebra map
\Delta\colon RG\to RG\otimes_{R}RG
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
P,Q
be
G
-modules. If the
G
-module
P
is projective and
Q
is projective over
R
, then the
G
-module
P\otimes_{R}Q
is projective.

Proof.

This follows from the standard adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(P\otimes_{R}Q,-)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(P,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,-))\,.\qed

The preceding result implies that
\otimes_{R}
induces a tensor product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
, the homotopy category of projective
G
-modules.

Lemma 4.2
.

The triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
is tensor triangulated, with product
\otimes_{R}
and unit the injective resolution
\mathbf{i}R
in
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
.

Proof.

We have seen that
\otimes_{R}
provides a tensor product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
. It remains to verify the assertion about the unit; for this see
[
19
, Proposition 5.3]
, whose proof extends to finite flat group schemes, and also
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
.
∎

Rigidity

The tensor product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
induces one on
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
, the subcategory introduced following Lemma
2.13
. By construction, it is compactly generated and our aim is to show that it is also rigidly-compactly generated, in the sense recalled below.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated tensor triangulated category,
with tensor product
\otimes
and unit
\mathds{1}
. We assume that
\mathds{1}
is
compact. Being a compactly generated tensor triangulated category,

\mathcal{T}
has an
internal function object
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(-,-)
, defined
by the property that

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(Y,Z))

for
X,Y
and
Z
in
\mathcal{T}
. There is a natural map

(4.3)

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,\mathds{1})\otimes Y\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(X,Y)

and
X
is
rigid
if this map is an isomorphism for all

Y
. Since
\mathds{1}
is compact, every rigid object is compact, and one
says that
\mathcal{T}
is rigidly-compactly generated when the converse holds: compact
objects and rigid objects coincide. It is straightforward to verify that this property holds if and only if:

(1)

the subcategory of compact objects is closed under
\otimes
, and

(2)

(
4.3
) is an isomorphism when
X,Y
are compact.

We get back to the tensor triangulated category
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
with product
\otimes_{R}
, where
G
is finite flat group scheme over
R
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The tensor product
\otimes_{R}
induces on
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
a tensor-triangulated structure that is rigidly-compactly generated.

Proof.

We claim that the tensor product on
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
restricts to a product on
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
. Because
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
is compactly
generated it suffices to show that for any compact objects
X,Y
in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
, the complex
X\otimes_{R}Y
is again in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
. For the rigidity we need to verify that
X\otimes_{R}Y
is also compact and that (
4.3
) is an
isomorphism. We use the identification of the compact objects in
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
with the objects in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))
; see
Proposition
2.14
. For
M,N
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))
it is clear that the complex
M\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}N
is in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))
since
M
and
N
are perfect complexes over
R
. Now we can identify
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(M,N)=\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M,N)
and it remains to observe that the map

\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M,R)\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}N\longrightarrow\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M,N)

of
G
-complexes is an isomorphism, because
M
is perfect over
R
.
∎

Cohomology

With
G
acting trivially on
R
, we consider the cohomology algebra
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{G}(R,R)
of
G
. Since
G
is a group scheme, standard arguments yield that
S
is a graded-commutative
R
-algebra. For any
G
-module

set

H^{*}(G,M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}_{G}^{*}(R,M)\,.

This is a graded module over
S
, under cup-products; see
[
9
]
. Friedlander and Suslin
[
27
]
prove that
S
is finitely generated—equivalently, noetherian—when
R
is a field; this result has been extended by van der Kallen
[
44
]
to cover any noetherian commutative ring
R
; see
6.8
.

For any map
R\to R^{\prime}
we write
G_{R^{\prime}}
for the group scheme over
R^{\prime}
obtained by base change of
G
along
R\to R^{\prime}
. This induces a map of graded-commutative rings

H^{*}(G,R)\longrightarrow H^{*}(G_{R^{\prime}},R^{\prime})\,.

For any
R^{\prime}
-module
M^{\prime}
one has an isomorphism of
H^{*}(G,R)
-modules

H^{*}(G,M^{\prime})\cong H^{*}(G_{R^{\prime}},M^{\prime})\,,

where
M^{\prime}
is viewed as a
G
-module via restriction, as usual. This is compatible with the map of graded algebras
H^{*}(G,R)\to H^{*}(G_{R^{\prime}},R^{\prime})
.

Examples

We list examples of finite flat group schemes over rings; the purpose is to indicate that there is a plethora of such things. We are grateful to Sean Cotner for sharing his expertise on this topic.

Example 4.5
.

For
R
a noetherian commutative ring (satisfying mild conditions) Oort and Tate
[
43
]
classified finite group schemes with coordinate algebra
R[x]/(x^{p})
. When
R
is a complete local ring with residue field of characteristic
p
, there is such a group scheme for any factorisation
p=ac
in
R
. Two such group schemes are isomorphic if the pairs
(a,c)
and
(a^{\prime},c^{\prime})
differ by multiplication by a unit in
R
.

When
R
contains
\mathbb{F}_{p}
the group algebra of such a group scheme is
R[x]/(x^{p}-\lambda x)
for
\lambda\in R
, and two such group schemes are isomorphic if the
\lambda
’s differ by a unit.

Example 4.6
.

To construct examples of higher rank, consider an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
R
and the additive group scheme
\mathbb{G}_{a,R}
over
R
. Its coordinate algebra is
R[x]
. Let

f\colon\mathbb{G}_{a,R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a,R}

be a map of group schemes defined by the map on coordinate algebras

f^{*}\colon R[x]\longrightarrow R[x],\quad f^{*}(x)=x^{p^{n}}+a_{p^{n}-1}x^{p^{n-1}}+\ldots+a_{1}x

for some
n\geq 1
, and elements
a_{i}
in
R
. This is a Hopf algebra map since
f^{*}(x)
is primitive by construction and hence defines a map of group schemes. Let
G
be the scheme theoretic kernel of
f
. Then
G
is a finite flat group scheme over
R
with coordinate algebra
R[x]/(f^{*}(x))
, of rank
p^{n}
.

For example, take
R=k[[t]]
, with
k
a field of positive characteristic
p
, and
f^{*}(x)=x^{p}-t^{p-1}x
. The fibres of
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}f
over
R
are different group schemes. The special fibre, at
t=0
, has the coordinate algebra
k[x]/(x^{p})
, so
G_{k}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a(1)}
, the first Frobenius kernel of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. On the other hand, the generic fibre
G_{k((t))}
has coordinate algebra
k((t))[x]/(x^{p}-t^{p-1}x)
, which splits into
p
copies of
k((t))
. Hence it is the constant finite group scheme
\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Another family of commutative finite flat group schemes is given by the (scheme-theoretic)
p
-torsion subgroups of supersingular abelian varieties defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras; see
[
34
, Chapter 13]
. The next family is again based on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
but consists of noncommutative group schemes.

Example 4.7
.

Set
R=k[t]/(t^{p})
where
k
is a field of characteristic
p
. Consider the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/p
with a generator
\sigma
acting on
\mathbb{G}_{a,R}
via multiplication by
(t+1)
:

\sigma\circ s\mapsto(t+1)s

for any
s\in\mathbb{G}_{a,R}
. On the level of the coordinate algebra
R[\mathbb{G}_{a}]\cong R[x]
, the
R
-algebra automorphism
\sigma
is defined by
\sigma(x)=(t+1)x
. This action commutes with the Frobenius homomorphism on
\mathbb{G}_{a,R}
and, hence, restricts to the Frobenius kernels
\mathbb{G}_{a(r),R}
. We consider finite flat groups schemes over
R
which are semi-direct products with respect to this action:
G_{r}=\mathbb{G}_{a(r),R}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Explicitly, the group algebra
RG_{r}
is the smash product:
RG_{r}\cong R\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\#R\mathbb{Z}/p
. The coproduct is inherited from that on
R\mathbb{G}_{a(r)}\cong R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]/(x_{1}^{p},\ldots,x_{r}^{p})
and
R\mathbb{Z}/p\cong R[\sigma]/(\sigma^{p}-1)
and is cocommutative. The product is twisted with the action of
\sigma
:

\displaystyle(1\#\sigma)(x_{1}\#1)

\displaystyle=(t+1)x_{1}\#\sigma;

\displaystyle(1\#\sigma)(x_{i}\#1)

\displaystyle=x_{i}\#\sigma,\quad 1<i\leq r.

These are noncommutative and finite flat over
R
, with
RG_{r}
a free
R
-module of rank
p^{r+1}
. The smallest one,
G_{1}
, cannot be obtained via base change from a group scheme defined over any integral domain. A proof of this claim was communicated to us by Sean Cotner.

With

Let

1

1

\mathcal{D}(RG)
be the derived
\infty
-category of
RG
-modules with symmetric monoidal structure coming from the coproduct on the cocommutative Hopf algebra
RG
, as above; its homotopy category is
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}(RG))
. The next result is folklore, so we only sketch the proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
R
a commutative ring. There is an equivalence

\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R))\simeq\mathcal{D}(RG)
of symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories, which restricts to a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(R))\simeq\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))\,.

Proof.

The
R
-linear dual
A=\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(RG,R)
of
RG
has the structure of a dualisable commutative algebra in
\mathcal{D}(G,R)
. Induction along the unit map
R\to A
identifies with the restriction functor
\mathrm{Res}\colon\mathcal{D}(RG)\to\mathcal{D}(R)
and the corresponding cosimplicial descent diagram takes the form

\Delta\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty},\qquad\bullet\mapsto\textstyle\prod_{G^{\times\bullet}}\mathcal{D}(R)\coloneqq(\mathcal{D}(R)\rightrightarrows\prod_{G}\mathcal{D}(R)\Rrightarrow\ldots)\,.

We thus obtain a symmetric monoidal exact comparison functor to the totalisation,

(5.4)

\mathcal{D}(RG)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Tot}\textstyle\prod_{G^{\times\bullet}}\mathcal{D}(R)\,.

On the one hand, since
\mathrm{Res}
is conservative, it follows from the Barr–Beck–Lurie theorem
[
36
, Theorem 4.7.3.5]
that the functor (
5.4
) is an equivalence. On the other hand, the standard simplicial bar resolution of
BG
implies that the totalisation in (
5.4
) recovers the category of local systems on
BG
with coefficients in
\mathcal{D}(R)
,

\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R))\simeq\mathrm{Tot}\textstyle\prod_{G^{\times\bullet}}\mathcal{D}(R)\,,

equipped with its pointwise symmetric monoidal structure. Composing these two symmetric monoidal equivalences, we obtain the first part of the claim:

(5.5)

\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R))\simeq\mathcal{D}(RG)\,.

The equivalence of (
5.5
) restricts to an equivalence on dualisable objects:

(5.6)

\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R))^{\mathrm{dbl}}\simeq\mathcal{D}(RG)^{\mathrm{dbl}}\,.

On the one hand, since the tensor product is pointwise, the left side of (
5.6
) identifies with
\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R)^{\mathrm{dbl}})\simeq\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R)^{\mathrm{perf}})
. On the other hand, the conservative functor
\mathcal{D}(RG)\to\mathcal{D}(R)
detects dualisable objects, so the right side of (
5.6
) is naturally equivalent to
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))
by Proposition
2.6
, as claimed.
∎

5.7
.

Let
i^{*}R\in\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}(R))
be the local system arising from pushing
R
forward along the inclusion of a chosen basepoint
i\colon\ast\to BG
. Unwinding the construction, the equivalence of Lemma
5.3
is determined by sending
i^{*}R
to the regular representation
RG\in\mathcal{D}(RG)
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
R
a noetherian commutative ring. The equivalence of Lemma
5.3
induces an equivalence

\operatorname{\mathcal{R}\!\!\;\mathit{ep}}(G,R)\simeq\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)

of symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories.

Proof.

Recall that both sides of the desired equivalence are rigidly-compactly generated
\infty
-categories. Combining Lemma
5.3
with (
5.1
) thus yields equivalences

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{R}\!\!\;\mathit{ep}}(G,R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Fun}(BG,\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{perf}}(R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Ind}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(G,R))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Ind}(\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)^{c})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)

of symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-categories.
∎

5.9
.

One feature of the
\infty
-category
\operatorname{\mathcal{R}\!\!\;\mathit{ep}}(G,R)
is that its construction extends to any topological group
G
and any symmetric monoidal coefficient
\infty
-category, such as perfect modules over commutative ring spectra. This allows one to transport questions from modular representation theory to higher algebra and, vice versa, apply homotopical techniques to problems in representation theory; see
[
6
]
.

Cohomology and fibres

As in Section
4
, we fix a noetherian commutative ring
R
and a finite flat group scheme
G
over
R
.
For each map of rings
R\to k
with
k
a field, consider the

-algebra

S(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}_{G_{k}}^{*}(k,k)\,.

The functor
-\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}k
induces a map of graded

and hence a map of graded

(6.1)

\phi_{k}\colon S\otimes_{R}k\longrightarrow S(k)\,.

The source and target are graded-commutative and finitely generated. Consider the induced map on spectra:

(6.2)

\operatorname{Spec}(\phi_{k})\colon\operatorname{Spec}S(k)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_{R}k)\,.

In
[
33
]
it is proved that this map is a homeomorphism when

is a finite group. The argument, going back to
[
12
]
, extends to finite flat group schemes and yields the result below. The key new input is work of van der Kallen
[
44
]
, recalled in
6.8
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The map
\operatorname{Spec}(\phi_{k})
is a homeomorphism.

When the characteristic of
k
is zero, the source and target of the map
\phi_{k}
are zero in non-negative degrees, by a result of van der Kallen’s recalled below
6.8
, so
\operatorname{Spec}(\phi_{k})
is trivially a homeomorphism. The real content of the result is in the case when the characteristic of

is positive. We deduce it from a more general result, Theorem
6.11
; see
6.12
. Its statement and proof are modeled on those in Benson–Habegger
[

]
and Lau
[

, Section 8]
, and require some preparation.

Universal homeomorphisms

A map
\varphi\colon A\to B
of (possibly graded) commutative rings is a
universal homeomorphism
if for each map
A\to A^{\prime}
of rings the map on spectra induced by the base change map
A^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}\otimes_{A}B
, is a homeomorphism:

\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\prime}\otimes_{A}B)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}A^{\prime}\,.

In particular
\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A
is a homeomorphism. A universal homeomorphism is universally bijective (in the obvious sense), and the converse holds if
\varphi
is finite, but not in general: consider the map
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{Q}
for some prime number
p
.

Universal bijectivity is easier to track. Indeed, when
\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A
is surjective so is the map induced by base change. So the critical point is that the one-to-one property is preserved under base change. It is not hard to verify that the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is universally bijective;

(2)

\varphi\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})\colon k(\mathfrak{p})\to B\otimes_{A}k(\mathfrak{p})
is universally bijective for
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
;

(3)

\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_{A}k)
is a point for each map
A\to k
of rings, where
k
is a field.

This observation is used repeatedly in what follows, as is the following criterion to detect universal homeomorphisms; see
[
23
, Lemma 10.46.7]
for a proof.

6.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon A\to B
be a map of commutative rings and
p
be a prime number. Assume the following conditions hold:

(1)

B
is generated as an
A
-algebra by elements
b
such that there exist an
n\geq 1
for which

p^{n}\cdot b
and
b^{p^{n}}
are in
\mathrm{Image}(\varphi)
;

(2)

each
a\in\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)
is nilpotent.

Then
\varphi
is a universal homeomorphism. In particular, if
A
contains a field of positive characteristic
p
and
\varphi
is an
F
-isomorphism, it is a universal homeomorphism.

6.5
.

Let
G
be a finite flat group scheme over
R
. Any map
f\colon R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
of
R
-algebras induces a map

H^{*}(f)\colon H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,R^{\prime\prime})

of graded
R^{\prime\prime}
-algebras. Consider the collection

U(G)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\text{$f$ a map of $R$-algebras}\mid\text{$H^{*}(f)$ is a universal homeomorphism}\}.

The following statements are easy to verify, given the characterisations of universal homeomorphisms and universal bijective maps.

Lemma 6.6
.

The collection
U(G)
is closed under compositions and contains all flat maps. When
K
is a commutative ring,
R
a
K
-algebra, and
f
a map of
R
-algebras, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
is in
U(G)
;

(2)

f\otimes_{K}K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is in
U(G)
for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}K
.∎

Here is a variation of the result above concerning universal bijectivity.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
f\colon R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
be a map of commutative rings and
\overline{f}_{\mathfrak{q}}
the composite
R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}\to k(\mathfrak{q})
, where
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime\prime}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

H^{*}(f)
is universally bijective;

(2)

H^{*}(\overline{f}_{\mathfrak{q}})
is universally bijective for each
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime\prime}
.∎

6.8
.

Recall that
R
is a noetherian commutative ring. Van der Kallen
[
44
, Theorem 1]
proves the following results:

(1)

There exists an integer
d\geq 1
with

d\cdot H^{\geqslant 1}(G,-)=0\quad\text{on $\operatorname{Mod}R$}\,.

(2)

The
R
-algebra
H^{*}(G,R)
is finitely generated, and hence noetherian, and the
H^{*}(G,R)
-module
H^{*}(G,M)
is finitely generated whenever
M
is a finitely generated
G
-module.

These results are crucial to all that follows. When
G
is a finite group, one can take
d
to be the order of the group. The statement is also obvious when
d\cdot R=0
, so the essential case is when the structure map
\mathbb{Z}\to R
is one-to-one.

Lemma 6.9
.

Any surjective map
f
of
R
-algebras with
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
nilpotent is in
U(G)
.

Proof.

It suffices to check that
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
is in
U(G)
for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R^{\prime}
, by Lemma
6.6
, so we may assume
R^{\prime}
is a local ring. In particular, it contains
\mathbb{Q}
or its residue field is of positive characteristic
p
and
R^{\prime}
is
p
-local, that is to say, the natural map
R^{\prime}\to\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R^{\prime}
is bijective.

In the former case
H^{\geqslant 1}(G,-)=0
on
\operatorname{Mod}R^{\prime}
, and there is nothing left to verify; this is by
6.8
(1). So we may assume
R^{\prime}
is
p
-local. Then, again by
6.8
(1), one gets

p^{n}\cdot H^{\geqslant 1}(G,-)=0\quad\text{on $\operatorname{Mod}R^{\prime}$ and some $n\geq 1$.}

Set
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(f)
; the hypothesis is that
I^{s}=0
for some
s\geq 1
. It suffices to verify the statement when
s=2
, and to that end we verify that
H^{*}(f)
satisfies the conditions in
6.4
. Condition (2) clearly holds as
I
is nilpotent. The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow R^{\prime}\longrightarrow R^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0

of
R^{\prime}
-modules induces an exact sequence

H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ H^{*}(f)\ }H^{*}(G,R^{\prime\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }H^{*}(G,I)\,.

Arguing as in the proof
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
, or
[
33
, Lemma 8.17]
, yields that the connecting map
\delta
is a derivation: For any
x,y
in
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime\prime})
, one has

\delta(x\cup y)=\delta(x)\cup y+(-1)^{|x|}x\cup\delta(y)\,.

Fix
x\in H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime})
. When
|x|
and
p
are both odd,
x^{2}=0
by the graded-commutativity of
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
. So we can assume
|x|
is even or
p=2
. Either way, it follows from the fact that
\delta\colon H^{*}(G,R^{\prime\prime})\to H^{*+1}(G,I)
is a derivation that
\delta(x^{i})=ix^{i-1}\delta(x)
. Since
p^{n}\cdot H^{\geqslant 1}(G,-)=0
, we deduce that
\delta(x^{p^{n}})=0
and hence
x^{p^{n}}
is in the image of
H^{*}(f)
. Thus condition (1) of
6.4
is also satisfied.
∎

A surjective map
R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
is a
complete intersection
if its kernel can be generated by a regular sequence.

Lemma 6.10
.

Any surjective complete intersection map
f\colon R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime\prime}
of
R
-algebras with
R^{\prime}
noetherian is in
U(G)
.

Proof.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
6.9
we can assume
R^{\prime}
is local, with residue field of positive characteristic
p
and that
p^{n}\cdot H^{\geqslant 1}(G,-)=0
on
\operatorname{Mod}R^{\prime}
. Moreover, since
U(G)
is closed under compositions it suffices to consider the case
R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}/t
, where
t
is not a zero-divisor in
R^{\prime}
. At this point one can argue as in the proof of
[
33
, Lemma 8.21]
to deduce the desired statement. Here is a sketch of the argument.

For each integer
i\geq 1
set

J_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ t^{i}\ }H^{*}(G,R^{\prime\prime}))\,.

The ring
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
is noetherian, by van der Kallen’s result
6.8
, so the chain of ideals
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}\subseteq\cdots
stabilises. Fix an integer
s
such that
J_{i}=J_{i+1}
for
i\geq s
. Since the map
R^{\prime}/t^{s}\to R^{\prime}/t
is in
U(G)
, by Lemma
6.9
, it suffices to verify that the map
R^{\prime}\to R^{\prime}/t^{s}
is in
U(G)
. Thus, replacing
t
by
t^{s}
, we can assume
J_{1}=J_{i}
for
i\geq 1
. The sequence of
G
-modules

0\longrightarrow R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ t\ }R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ f\ }R^{\prime}/t\to 0

yields the exact sequence of
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
-modules

0\longrightarrow R^{\prime}/t\otimes_{R^{\prime}}H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ H^{*}(f)\ }H^{*}(G,R^{\prime}/t)\xrightarrow{\ \delta\ }J_{1}\longrightarrow 0\,.

It suffices to verify
\delta(x^{p^{n}})=0
for
x\in H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime}/t)=0
; see
6.4
.

Let
P
be a projective resolution of
R^{\prime}
as a
G_{R^{\prime}}
-module. As
t
is not a zero-divisor on
R^{\prime}
, the complex
P/tP
is a projective resolution of
R^{\prime}/t
as a
G_{R^{\prime}/t}
-module, and the exact sequence above is induced by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{G}(P/tP)\longrightarrow 0\,.

The product on
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime})
and
H^{*}(G,R^{\prime}/t)
is induced by the composition product on the differential graded algebras
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)
and
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P/tP)
, respectively, and the map
H^{*}(f)
is induced by the map
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)\to\operatorname{End}_{G}(P/tP)
of dg algebras.
Fix a cycle
z
in
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P/tP)
and a pre-image
\tilde{z}
in
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)
; then
\delta([z])=[d(\tilde{z})/t]
. The graded-commutativity of the ring
H^{*}(G,R)
and the fact that
p^{n}H^{\geqslant 1}(G,R^{\prime})=0
yields
\delta([z]^{p^{n}})=[tw]
for some

; see
[
33
, Lemma 8.21]
for details. Since
td(w)=d(tw)=0
in
\operatorname{End}_{G}(P)
and

is not a zero-divisor,
d(w)=0
. Then

t^{2}[w]=t[tw]=t\delta([z]^{p^{n}})=0

since
t\cdot\operatorname{End}_{G}(P/tP)=0
. So
[w]
is in
J_{2}
. Since
J_{2}=J_{1}
we deduce that
t[w]=0
, that is to say, that
\delta([z]^{p^{n}})=0

∎

A map
R\to R^{\prime}
of commutative rings is said to be
essentially of finite type
if
R^{\prime}
is a localisation of a finitely generated

-algebra.

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
R
be a noetherian commutative ring,
G
a finite flat group scheme over
R
, and
f\colon R\to R^{\prime}
a map of commutative rings. The following statements hold.

(1)

H^{*}(f)
is universally bijective;

(2)

H^{*}(f)
is a universal homeomorphism if
f
is essentially of finite type.

Proof.

(1) Given Lemma
6.7
it suffices to consider the case when
R^{\prime}=k
, a field. We claim that then the map
H^{*}(f)
is even a universal homeomorphism. To see this, set
\mathfrak{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(f)
, a prime ideal in
R
. The map
f\colon R\to k
factors as

R\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow k(\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow k\,,

where the first two maps are the natural ones. Since both maps
R\to R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
k(\mathfrak{p})\to k
are flat and hence in
U(G)
, it suffices to verify that the map
R_{\mathfrak{p}}\to k(\mathfrak{p})
is in
U(G)
. We can thus assume
R
is local and
f\colon R\to k
is the quotient map, with
k
the residue field of
R
.

We induct on
\dim R
to deduce that
f
is in
U(G)
, and in particular universally bijective. The base case
\dim R=0
is covered by Lemma
6.9
for then the ideal
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
is nilpotent.

Suppose
\dim R\geq 1
and set
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{(0)}
the nilradical of
R
; it is a nilpotent ideal, since
R
is noetherian. Thus the map
R\to R/I
is in
U(G)
. Since the map
R\to k
factors as
R\to R/I\to k
, it suffices to verify that
R/I\to k
is in
U(G)
. So we can replace
R
by
R/I
and assume it is reduced. Then, since
\dim R\geq 1
, the maximal ideal of
R
is not an associated prime and hence contains an element
t
that is not a zero-divisor. The map
R\to k
factors as

R\longrightarrow R/tR\longrightarrow k\,.

The map on the left is in
U(G)
, by Lemma
6.10
, and the one on the right is in
U(G)
by the induction hypothesis, since

\dim(R/tR)=\dim R-1
. We conclude that
f
is in
U(G)
.

(2) Since
f
is essentially of finite type, it factors as
R\to R^{\prime\prime}\to R^{\prime}
where
R\to R^{\prime\prime}
is the localisation of a finitely generated polynomial ring over
R
, and
R^{\prime\prime}\to R^{\prime}
is surjective. Since
R\to R^{\prime\prime}
is flat it is in
U(G)
. We may thus replace
R
by
R^{\prime\prime}
and suppose
f
is surjective, and hence a finite map. It follows that the map
H^{*}(f)
is also finite, by van der Kallen’s theorem
6.8
(2), and hence the induced map
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(f)
is universally closed. Since
H^{*}(f)
is universally bijective, by part (1), we conclude that it is a universal homeomorphism.
∎

6.12
.

Theorem
6.3
is the special case of Theorem
6.11
where

is a field. On the other hand, as can be seen from the proof, the latter result is proved by (a simple) reduction to the case where
R^{\prime}
is a field.

Local cohomology and support

This section is a reminder about local cohomology functors and a general local-to-global principle for triangulated categories; for details, see
[
13
,
14
]
.

Localisation and local cohomology

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with suspension
\Sigma
. Given
objects
X
and
Y
in
\mathcal{T}
, consider the graded abelian groups

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,Y)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,X)\,.

Composition makes
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X)
a graded ring and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,Y)
a left-
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(Y)

right-
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X)
module.

Let
S
be a graded-commutative noetherian ring. In what follows we
will only be concerned with homogeneous elements and ideals in
S
. In
this spirit, ‘localisation’ will mean homogeneous localisation, and

\operatorname{Spec}S
will denote the set of homogeneous prime ideals in
S
.

We say that a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is
S
-linear
if for
each
X
in
\mathcal{T}
there is a homomorphism of graded rings

\phi_{X}\colon S\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X)
such that the induced left and
right actions of
S
on
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,Y)
are compatible in the
following sense: For any
r\in S
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)
, one
has

\phi_{Y}(r)\alpha=(-1)^{|r||\alpha|}\alpha\phi_{X}(r)\,.

An exact functor
F\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{U}
between
S
-linear triangulated
categories is
S
-linear
if the induced map

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}^{*}(FX,FY)

of graded abelian groups is
S
-linear for all objects
X,Y
in

\mathcal{T}
.

In what follows, we fix a compactly generated
S
-linear triangulated
category
\mathcal{T}
and write
\mathcal{T}^{c}
for its full subcategory of compact
objects.

Let
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}S
be a specialisation closed subset. An

S
-module
M
is
V
-torsion
if
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}
in

\operatorname{Spec}S\setminus V
. Analogously, an object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
is

V
-torsion
if the
S
-module
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}(C,X)
is

V
-torsion for all
C\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
. For an ideal
I\subseteq S
we use
the more common term
I
-torsion
instead of
V(I)
-torsion.
The full subcategory of
V
-torsion objects

\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T}\coloneqq\{X\in\mathcal{T}\mid X\text{ is $V$-torsion}\}

is localising and the inclusion
\varGamma_{V}\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{T}

Lemma 7.1

Let

be compactly generated

Proof.

(1)

the images of the maps
(\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}\coloneqq\operatorname{Spc}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}))_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R}

\operatorname{Spc}(\operatorname{rep}(G,R))

(2)

(3)

\operatorname{Rep}(G,R)

11.
An example

In this section we give an example, promised in Section
2
of a group
G
and a noetherian commutative ring
R
with the property that the category
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
is properly contained in
\operatorname{Mod}(G,R)
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
2
, set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[t]/(t^{2})
and
G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Z}/2
, the elementary abelian
2
-group of rank one. Setting
x\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g-1
, where
g
is the generator of
G
, one gets an isomorphism

RG\cong\frac{k[t,x]}{(t^{2},x^{2})}\,,

which identifies with the group algebra over
k
of
E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
, the Klein
4
-group. The cohomology algebra is

H\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H^{*}(E,k)\cong k[t^{*},x^{*}]\,,

where the basis
t^{*},x^{*}
of
H^{1}
is dual to the basis
t,x
of
(t,x)/(t,x)^{2}
. One has

\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}(G,R)\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}(E,k)\,.

This induces full and faithful inclusions

(11.1)

\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)\subseteq\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)\subseteq\operatorname{StMod}(E,k)\,.

Here is a key observation about these categories.

Lemma 11.2
.

Both
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
and
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
are localising subcategories of
\operatorname{StMod}(E,k)
, with

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{H}\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Proj}H\setminus\{(t^{*})\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{H}\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Proj}H\setminus\{(0),(t^{*})\}\,.

Proof.

A
kE
-module
M
is in
\operatorname{Mod}(G,R)
if and only if it is projective when restricted to
R
; equivalently, its support does not contain
(t^{*})
. This justifies the claim about the support of
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
.

The support of any finite dimensional
kE
-module
M
is closed, so
M
is in
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
if and only if the subset
\{(0),(t^{*})\}
is not in its support. Thus this subset is not in the support of any module that is in the localising subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(G,R)
generated by
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
. This justifies the second equality.
∎

It follows from the result above that the
generic module
in
\operatorname{StMod}(E,k)
, that is to say, the
kE
-module
\varGamma_{(0)}k
is in
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
but not in
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
.

The inclusions in (
11.1
) do not respect the tensor triangular structures, for the product on the two categories on the left is
-\otimes_{R}-
whereas for that on the right is
-\otimes_{k}-
. This is an important point, and is discussed further below.

Thick and localising ideals

We continue with a description of thick and localising ideals of the stable categories
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
and
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
.

The ideal
(t)\subset RG
is isomorphic to
R
as an
RG
-module, so the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(t)\longrightarrow RG\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow 0

defines an element in
H^{1}(G,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{RG}(R,R)
; denote it
\eta
. Since
H^{*}(G,R)
is a commutative
R
-algebra, one gets a morphism of
R
-algebras

R[\eta]\longrightarrow H^{*}(G,R)\,.

A simple computation yields:

Lemma 11.3
.

The map above is an isomorphism. ∎

Thus
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,R)
consists of a single point, in contrast with
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(E,k)=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. This means that there is a single nonzero thick
ideal
in
\operatorname{stmod}(G,R)
, whereas thick subcategories are aplenty, and parameterised by specialisation closed subsets of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\setminus\{(0)\}
. Here is an example: Assume
k
is algebraically closed and set

M_{[a,b]}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}RG/(at+bx)\quad\text{for $[a,b]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}$.}

Observe that these modules are in
\operatorname{mod}(G,R)
, except the one for
[a,b]=[1,0]
. Since there is only a single thick ideal in
\operatorname{stmod}(G,R)
, these modules must tensor-generate each other. Indeed as long as
[a,b]\neq[1,0]
, as
G
-modules one has

M_{[a,b]}\otimes_{R}M_{[-a,b]}\cong M_{[0,b]}\cong R\,.

We deduce also that
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
has no nontrivial localising ideals. This is another measure of the difference between this category and
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
: The modules in the latter are precisely those
kE
-modules not supported on the closed point
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{[1,0]\}
, so

\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)=L_{V}\operatorname{StMod}(E,k)\,.

It follows that
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
is also compactly generated, with compact objects generated by
L_{V}k
. Moreover the minimal localising subcategories of
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
are parameterised by the points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\setminus V
. This set consists of closed points and the generic point
\{0\}
. The localising subcategories corresponding to the closed points are compactly generated and hence contained in
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
. Thus there are only two proper localising ideals in
\operatorname{StMod}(G,R)
, namely,
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(G,R)
and the localising subcategory generated by the
G
-module
\varGamma_{(0)}k
supported at
(0)
.

A similar analysis can be made of the categories
\mathbf{K}^{\mathrm{lf}}(\operatorname{Proj}G,R)
and
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
. Now there is one more minimal localising ideal to take into account, namely the
K
-projective complexes in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Proj}G)
.

11.4
.

The preceding construction can be generalised as follows to yield Frobenius
R
-algebras
A
for which
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
is smaller than
\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)
.

Let
G
be a commutative finite group scheme defined over a field
k
of positive characteristic
p
, such that the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G,k)
has Krull dimension at least
2
. Choose a
\pi

\alpha\colon R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[t]/(t^{p})\longrightarrow kG\mathrel{{=}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}}A

such that the corresponding point
[\alpha]
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
is closed and has codimension
\geq 1
. Then
A
is a Frobenius
R
-algebra and
\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)
consists of those
G
-modules whose support does not contain
[\alpha]
. Choose a non-closed point
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
whose closure contains
[\alpha]
; it exists because the codimension of
[\alpha]
is positive. The module
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k
is then in
\operatorname{StMod}(A,R)
but not in
\operatorname{StMod}^{\mathrm{lf}}(A,R)
.

The construction above carries over to any finite flat group scheme
G
and
\pi
-point
\alpha
whose image is central in
kG
.

Wave-front tracking for a quasi-linear scalar conservation law with hysteresis
Fabio Bagagiolo, Stefan Moreti

Abstract
In this article we deal with the Cauchy problem for the quasi-linear scalar conservation law u t + ℱ ​ ( u ) t + u x = 0 , u_{t}+\mathcal{F}(u)_{t}+u_{x}=0, where ℱ \mathcal{F} is a specific hysteresis operator, namely the Play operator. Hysteresis models a rate-independent memory relationship between the input u u and its output. Its presence in the partial differential equation gives a particular non-local feature to the latter allowing us to capture phenomena that may arise in some application fields. Riemann problems and the interactions between shock lines are studied and via the so-called Wave-Front Tracking method a solution to the Cauchy problem with bounded variation initial data is constructed. The solution found satisfies an entropy-like condition, making it the unique solution in the class of entropy admissible ones.



Introduction

In this work we deal with the Cauchy problem for a conservation law with hysteresis nonlinearities as follows:

\begin{cases}u_{t}+w_{t}+u_{x}=0&\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}\times[0,T),\\
w(x,t)={\cal F}[u(x,\cdot),w^{0}(x)](t)&\quad\forall\ t\in]0,T[,\ \text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)&\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1)

where:
\cal F
is a so-called hysteresis operator, namely representing a memory-dependent input-output relationship between the pair of scalar functions
(t\mapsto u(x,t),t\mapsto w(x,t))
, one for almost every
x
;
u_{0}
is the initial datum for the solution
u
;
w^{0}
is a suitable space-dependent function for the initial values of the output
w
. The memory dependence represented by
\cal F
is rate-independent, which is the main characterization of the hysteresis phenomena. We are going to consider the case where
\cal F
is the so-called Play operator.

The presence of the hysteretic term

gives (
1

More precisely, due to the rate-independent memory, the functional input-output relationship
u\mapsto w={\cal F}[u,w^{0}]
is highly non-linear and non-differentiable and this fact leads to abrupt changes of characteristics after any possible time

(u,w)
reaches suitable regions of the phase-space

. In the case of the Play operator, such abrupt changes can be somehow seen as the case of a discontinuity in the derivative of the flux function
f
for a generic conservation law

. For the Riemann problem, (

depends on the hysteretic relationship

Our main goal is the study of a generic Cauchy problem for (

) with initial data
u_{0},w_{0}
just bounded variation functions (

u_{0}
and
w_{0}
by piece-wise constant functions, solve the corresponding Riemann problems and, suitably adapting the wave-front tracking method, we pass to the limit. In doing that we face several non-standard problems:

i) with respect to the classical wave-front tracking method in our case the number of discontinuity waves after an interaction may increase, while the total variation of the solution remains constant.

ii) for the Riemann problem the solution
u
is discontinuous in time and this is in general a problem for the hysteresis relation which is usually defined for continuous inputs
u
; we need to extend the definition of the Play operator to time-piece-wise constant inputs in the spirit of the so-called regulated functions (see Brokate-Sprekels
[
3
]

[

[

iii) for the Cauchy problem, being the solution only

[
20
]
). In particular, such variational inequality will be maintained to the limit, giving the existence of a weak solution for the Cauchy problem with hysteresis.

Our main results are the existence of a solution of (

In the article

In our article instead we use a different approach which is more constructive and gives a concrete idea on how the discontinuities behave and how the solution evolves in time.

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}:~&\tilde{\cal L}\subset C^{0}([0,T])\times\mathbb{R}\to C^{0}([0,T])\\

Proposition 1.3

Let

Proof.

First notice that, since

|Dw|((0,t))=\sum_{\{i~|~t_{i}<t\}}|w_{i+1}-w_{i}|.

(1.10)

(1\Rightarrow 2)
By definition of

\int\limits_{(0,t)}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{w})~d(Dw)=(\tilde{u}(t_{1})-\tilde{w}(t_{1}))(w_{2}-w_{1})

a|Dw|((0,t))=a|w_{2}-w_{1}|.

Now since

2
Solution to scalar Riemann problems with piecewise linear flux

In this section we will briefly recall some results for the Riemann problem for a conservation law with piecewise linear flux function which will be used in Section
4
. Let us deal with the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0&\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(2.1)

with
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous and piecewise linear and

u_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{l}\quad x<0,\\
u_{r}\quad x>0.\end{cases}

With
u_{0}
as above, (
2.1
) is usually called Riemann problem.

A weak solution for (
2.1
), even for more general
f
and
u_{0}
(at least integrable), is here defined.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
u:\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
,
u\in L^{1}_{loc}
, is a weak solution of (
2.1
) if

\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}[u\phi_{t}+f(u)\phi_{x}]~dx~dt+\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u_{0}(x)\phi(x,0)~dx=0

(2.2)

for every
C^{1}
function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support.

It is known that a weak solution with a jump discontinuity along a curve
C
parametrized by
t\mapsto s(t)
with
s
derivable, should satisfy the so called Rankine-Hugoniot condition (see e.g.
[
7
]
):

f(u_{-}(t))-f(u_{+}(t))=s^{\prime}(t)(u_{-}(t)-u_{+}(t))

(2.3)

on
C,
where
u_{-}(t),u_{+}(t)
are respectively the limits from the left and right parts of the domain separated by discontinuity curve
C
.

In general for conservation laws there is not uniqueness of the weak solution. The introduction of the so-called entropy condition allows us to select a unique solution. In particular one can require that
u
satisfies

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\{|u-k|\phi_{t}+sign(u-k)(f(u)-f(k))\phi_{x}\}~dxdt\geq 0,

(2.4)

for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
and for every non negative smooth
\phi
with compact support. It is proven,
[
14
]
(see also
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
), that for a scalar conservation law there exists one and only one weak solution satisfying (
2.4
). Such unique solution is usually called the entropy solution.

For piece-wise constant weak solutions, using also the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
2.3
), the entropy condition (
2.4
) simply reduces to (see
[
2
]
)

\frac{f(u)-f(u_{+})}{u-u_{+}}\leq\frac{f(u_{+})-f(u_{-})}{u_{+}-u_{-}}\leq\frac{f(u)-f(u_{-})}{u-u_{-}}

(2.5)

for every value
u
between
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
.

For the case of piecewise linear flux, the entropy solution of the Riemann problem can be explicitly constructed, see
[
6
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
. Such solution is piecewise constant with a finite number of values.
For the purposes of next sections we sketch here such construction.

We recall that
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is piecewise linear if
f
is continuous and there is a finite family of disjoint intervals, covering
\mathbb{R}
, where
f
is affine. Moreover
u:\mathbb{R}\times[0,+\infty[\to\mathbb{R}
is piecewise constant if the domain can be partioned in a finite family of subsets where
u
is constant.

In (
2.1
), let us suppose
u_{l}<u_{r}
, and consider
f_{c}
the greatest convex minorant of
f
on the interval
[u_{l},u_{r}]
, which we recall to be

f_{c}:=\sup\{h\text{ convex in }[u_{l},u_{r}]~|~h\leq f\}.

(2.6)

It is easy to see that
f_{c}
is a piecewise linear function hence the graph is a polygonal curve. Let us denote the vertices in the following way:
(u_{l},f(u_{l}))
,
(u_{1},f(u_{1}))
, …,

3
Weak formulation for the Cauchy problem with Play hysteresis

In this section we consider the following problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+w_{t}+u_{x}=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{T},\\
w=[\mathcal{F}(u,w_{0})],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
\mathbb{R}_{T}:=\mathbb{R}\times[0,T)
, and the state-dependent hysteresis operator is defined as in Remark
1.5
, starting from the Play operator (with amplitude
a>0
) applied to
t\mapsto u(x,t)
for almost every
x
fixed.

Definition 3.1
.

A couple of
L^{1}_{loc}
functions
(u,w)
with
u,w:\mathbb{R}_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is a weak solution to (
3.1
) if:

i)

it satisfies the following weak formulation of the PDE

\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}[(u+w)\phi_{t}+u\phi_{x}]~dx~dt+\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(u_{0}(x)+w_{0}(x))\phi(x,0)~dx=0,

(3.2)

for every
C^{1}
function
\phi
with compact support in
\mathbb{R}_{T}
;

ii)

for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{t}
it holds

|u(x,t)-w(x,t)|\leq a;

(3.3)

iii)

for almost every
x
, the distributional derivative
\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}_{T}
(denoted in the same way) that satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u(x,t)^{2}-u_{0}(x)^{2})~dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(w(x,t)^{2}-w_{0}(x)^{2})~dx\leq-a\Big|\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}\Big|(\mathbb{R}\times(0,t)),

(3.4)

for almost every
t\in(0,T)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Similarly to
[
20
]
, equation (
3.4
) can be interpreted as an equivalent formulation of (
1.8
), in the case of
H^{1}
functions which also depend on the state
x
. Indeed suppose that both
u,w
are in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{T})
solution of the PDE (
3.1
) then (
3.4
) reads as follows

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}u_{t}u+w_{t}w~dtdx+a\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|w_{t}|~dtdx\leq 0

so, by the PDE,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}-(w_{t}u+u_{x}u)+w_{t}w~dtdx+a\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|w_{t}|~dtdx\leq 0.

Since for almost every
t
,
u
is in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
so

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u_{x}u~dx=0\text{ for almost every }t,

we can conclude that

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(w-u)w_{t}~dtdx+a\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{t}|w_{t}|~dtdx\leq 0,

which is indeed (
1.8
) extended to space-dependent
H^{1}
functions. Finally, as in Section
1
for time-dependent piecewise constant functions, and arguing as in
[
21
]
and
[
20
]
, (
3.4
) can be seen to be a suitable weak extension of the Play operator to time-space dependent integrable functions.

If the weak solution has a jump discontinuity on a curve
(s(t),t)
, e.g. say
u_{-}(t)\not=u_{+}(t)
or
w_{-}(t)\not=w_{+}(t)
then we get the following extended Rankine-Hugoniot condition

\frac{u_{-}(t)-u_{+}(t)}{u_{-}(t)-u_{+}(t)+w_{-}(t)-w_{+}(t)}=s^{\prime}(t).

(3.5)

The entropy condition (
2.4
) is also extended to the following one

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{T}}(|u-k|+|w-\hat{k}|)\phi_{t}+|u-k|\phi_{x}~dxdt\geq 0,

(3.6)

for every
\phi
non negative, smooth and with compact support in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
and for every couple
(k,\hat{k})\in{\cal L}
.

We can now rewrite (

) as follows

\lambda[w_{l}-w_{r}]+(\lambda-1)(u_{l}-u_{r})=0.

(6.6)

So if
u_{l}=u_{r}
then
\lambda=0
, and the left-hand-side of (
6.2
) becomes
0
, independently from
(k,\hat{k})
. So the couple
(u,w)
is still an entropy solution and we imply case
\ref{list3})
.

If
w_{l}=w_{r}
then
\lambda=1
so we infer case
\ref{list4})
. Notice that even in this case (
6.2
) is trivially satisfied.

Suppose now instead
w_{r}\not=w_{l}
and
u_{r}\not=u_{l}
and recall that
u_{l}-u_{r}+w_{l}-w_{r}
is either positive or negative. Keeping this in mind and recalling (

) as follows

\frac{(u_{l}-u_{r})(|w_{r}-\hat{k}|-|w_{l}-\hat{k}|)+(w_{r}-w_{l})(|u_{r}-k|-|u_{l}-k|)}{u_{l}-u_{r}+w_{l}-w_{r}}\geq 0.

(6.7)

Now, for
(k,\hat{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we study the function

h(k,\hat{k}):=(u_{l}-u_{r})(|w_{r}-\hat{k}|-|w_{l}-\hat{k}|)+(w_{r}-w_{l})(|u_{r}-k|-|u_{l}-k|),

which is the numerator appearing in (
6.7
). We can split the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
9

k

u_{l},u_{r}
combined with the cases
\hat{k}
above, below or between
w_{l},w_{r}
, see Figure

.
It is easy to prove that
h
is constant in
R_{1},R_{3},R_{7}
and
R_{9}
as in these parts both
k
and
\hat{k}
cancel out. In particular, in
R_{1}

which is strictly positive if the jump between
u_{l}
and
u_{r}

w_{l}
and
w_{l}
and strictly negative otherwise. In
R_{9}
instead

, and in
R_{3}
and
R_{7}

. It is easy so see then that in the other regions

is affine, connecting continuously the constant parts, so we can deduce that it has the same constant sign in
R_{2}
and
R_{4}
and the opposite one in
R_{6}
and
R_{8}
. In
R_{5}
we have a change of sign on the segment
AB
connecting
(\min(u_{l},u_{r}),\min(w_{l},w_{r}))
to
(\max(u_{l},u_{r}),\max(w_{l},w_{r}))
.

With this in mind we then conclude that if the jump in
w
has opposite sign with respect to the jump in
u
then this violates our condition (
6.2
). This is because both the pairs
(u_{r},w_{r})
and
(u_{l},w_{l})
are contained in
\mathcal{L}
and, see Figure

, we can find multiple pairs in
\mathcal{L}

(u_{l},w_{l})
,
(u_{r},w_{r})
themselves) such that
h
is either positive or negative. Since the denominator
u_{l}-u_{r}+w_{l}-w_{r}\not=0
has fixed sign this means that the inequality (
6.2
) cannot be true for all
(k,\hat{k})\in\mathcal{L}
.

So we can only have jumps in
w
with the same sign of jumps in
u
. If
u_{l}>u_{r}
then the denominator
u_{l}-u_{r}+w_{l}-w_{r}
is positive, hence it must be

for all
(k,\hat{k})\in\mathcal{L}
. But
the only possibility is that the points

)

\cal L
. Otherwise at least one of them has a neighborhood containing points
(k,\hat{k})
in
\cal L
where

is negative.

\cal L
. Similarly, if
u_{l}<u_{r}
, then the points must lie on the upper boundary of
\cal L
. So negative jumps occur only on the upper boundary of
\cal L
. Also note that, in both cases,

and

must have a jump of the same amplitude.

So we checked that for

and

, (
6.2
) implies
\lambda=1/2
(see (

)) and either case
\ref{list5})
or
\ref{list6})
.

As there are no other possible choices of

\ref{list2})
.

(\impliedby)

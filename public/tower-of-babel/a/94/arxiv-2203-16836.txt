Exponential convergence of a dissipative quantum system towards finite-energy grid states of an oscillator
Lev-Arcady Sellem, Philippe Campagne-Ibarcq, Mazyar Mirrahimi, Alain Sarlette, Pierre Rouchon

Abstract
Based on the stabilizer formalism underlying Quantum Error Correction (QEC), the design of an original Lindblad master equation for the density operator of a quantum harmonic oscillator is proposed. This Lindblad dynamics stabilizes exactly the finite-energy grid states introduced in 2001 by Gottesman, Kitaev and Preskill for quantum computation. Stabilization results from an exponential Lyapunov function with an explicit lower-bound on the convergence rate. Numerical simulations indicate the potential interest of such autonomous QEC in presence of non-negligible photon-losses.



1
Introduction

Quantum Error Correction (QEC) represents a much sought-after target in the road towards large-scale quantum computations.
Indeed, decoherence affecting early quantum computing platforms
limits their ability to carry out interesting computations.
However, the
threshold theorem

[
1
]
states
that the use of quantum error correcting codes
could allow for arbitrarily long reliable quantum computations,
provided the noise levels affecting the hardware
could be kept below a threshold depending on the considered code.
A major issue for QEC is the huge resource overhead associated with the use of error correcting
codes
[
2
]

and recent years have seen a growing number of encoding proposals aim at reducing this overhead,
such as the so-called
cat code
[
3
,
4
]
,
binomial code
[
5
]

or GKP code
[
6
]
.
In particular, recent experiments in
superconducting circuits
[
7
]

and trapped ions
[
8
]

demonstrated the generation
and stabilization
of the finite-energy grid states
underlying the GKP encoding,
sparking a renewed interest for its use for quantum computation (see e.g. recent reviews
[
9
,
10
]
).
From a control theoretical perspective, QEC is a feedback-loop. Usual QEC is a discrete-time process based on a static output-feedback where the measured error syndrome (a classical output signal) indicates which correcting unitary transformation has to be applied via a specific short time-pulse on the classical control-input signal; in that case the controller is a classical system.
On the other hand, autonomous QEC or reservoir engineering QEC is a continuous-time process where the controller is a dissipative quantum system coupled to the system storing quantum information.
The idea of exploiting quantum dissipation
goes back to optical pumping
[
11
]
.
In
[
12
]
the potential interest of such dissipation engineering is highlighted for quantum state preparation and computation.

Continuous-time stabilization through dissipation engineering
has been experimentally demonstrated for cat codes
(see e.g.
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
)
and theoretically contemplated for GKP states in
[
16
]

where numerical simulations based on a Lindblad master equation with two dissipation operators indicate the potential interest of this approach;
however,
the authors did not investigate convergence rates of the proposed dynamics
or energy boundedness along trajectories.
Here we go further and propose a set of four dissipation Lindblad operators
exponentially
stabilizing
finite-energy GKP states introduced in
[
6
]
.
In section
2
, we develop, for a square lattice,
a heuristic method to design Lindblad dynamics
stabilizing finite-energy GKP states.
Its adaptation to other lattices such as the hexagonal one is straightforward.
It is inspired by the stabilizer formalism widely used in quantum error correction (see e.g.
[
1
, chapter 10]
).
Theorem
1
of section
3
provides
an exponential Lyapunov function
for the proposed Lindblad dynamics (
3
)
with an explicit lower bound on the convergence rate.
Section
4
,
devoted to numerical simulations with photon-loss error,
indicates the interest of these Lindblad dissipators for autonomous QEC. Possible further developments and issues are gathered in section
5
. The detailed and technical calculations are in appendix.

2
Lindblad dissipators derived from infinite-energy stabilizer generators

Set
\eta=2\sqrt{\pi}
and consider the Hermitian phase-space operators of a quantum harmonic oscillator
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{P}
satisfying
[\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{P}]=i
.
By Glauber identity
e^{\pm i\eta\mathbf{Q}}
and
e^{\pm i\eta\mathbf{P}}
commute.
The four commuting operators

e^{i\eta\mathbf{Q}}
,
e^{-i\eta\mathbf{Q}}
,
e^{i\eta\mathbf{P}}
and
e^{-i\eta\mathbf{P}}

are called the infinite-energy GKP stabilizers
and their common eigenspace associated to the eigenvalue
+1

is called
the infinite-energy GKP codespace.
These four stabilizer operators are the independent generators
of the stabilizer group
\{e^{in\eta\mathbf{Q}}e^{im\eta\mathbf{P}}~|~(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
.

In the
q
-representation,

\mathbf{P}\equiv-i\frac{d}{dq}

hence
e^{\pm i\eta\mathbf{P}}\equiv e^{\pm\eta\frac{d}{dq}}

corresponds to a constant shift of
\pm\eta
on
q
. Thus,
e^{in\eta\mathbf{Q}}e^{im\eta\mathbf{P}}
applied on the wave function
\left|\psi\right\rangle\equiv(\psi(q))_{q\in\mathbb{R}}
reads

e^{in\eta\mathbf{Q}}e^{im\eta\mathbf{P}}\left|\psi\right\rangle\equiv\left(e^{in\eta q}\psi(q+m\eta)\right)_{q\in\mathbb{R}}.

(1)

Solving for the
+1
-eigenstates of (
1
),
we find that the infinite-energy GKP codespace is of dimension
2
and spanned by two Dirac combs, the even comb

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\big(q-2k\tfrac{2\pi}{\eta}\big)

located at even multiples of
\tfrac{2\pi}{\eta}=\sqrt{\pi}

and the odd comb

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\big(q-(2k+1)\tfrac{2\pi}{\eta}\big)

located at odd multiples of
\tfrac{2\pi}{\eta}

(
\delta
stands for the Dirac distribution).

As in
[
6
, section V]
(see also
[
10
]
for a recent exposure), consider
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}=e^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(\mathbf{Q}^{2}+\mathbf{P}^{2})}
, a regularizing Hermitian operator with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. In the
q
-representation
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}
corresponds to the convolution with the Mehler kernel

K(q,q^{\prime},\varepsilon)=\tfrac{\exp\left(-\frac{\tanh\varepsilon}{2}(q^{\prime})^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sinh\varepsilon}}\exp\left(-\tfrac{(q-q^{\prime}/\cosh\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\tanh\varepsilon}\right).

Thus,
\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\left|\psi\right\rangle
reads
\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(q,q^{\prime},\varepsilon)\psi(q^{\prime})\,dq^{\prime}
and applied to the even and odd combs yields
the following coherent superpositions of Gaussian squeezed states of finite-energy (average photon-number around
1/(2\varepsilon)
):

\displaystyle\left|\text{even}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\sum_{k}\tfrac{e^{-\frac{\pi\tanh\varepsilon}{2}(2k)^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sinh\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{(q-2k\sqrt{\pi}/\cosh\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\tanh\varepsilon}}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\left|\text{odd}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\sum_{k}\tfrac{e^{-\frac{\pi\tanh\varepsilon}{2}(2k+1)^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sinh\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{(q-(2k+1)\sqrt{\pi}/\cosh\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\tanh\varepsilon}}\right)_{q\in\mathbb{R}}.

With
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
these two finite-energy and smooth quantum states
approximate generators of the infinite-energy GKP codespace.
We introduce an orthonormal basis of their span, defined by

\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\propto\left|\text{even}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
and
\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\propto\left|\text{odd}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle-\tfrac{\left\langle\text{even}_{\varepsilon}|\text{odd}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\text{even}_{\varepsilon}|\text{even}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle}\left|\text{even}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle.

By construction,
\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
and
\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
belong to the kernel of the following four non-Hermitian operators derived from the infinite-energy stabilizer operators:

\mathbf{V}_{1}=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{Q}}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathbf{I},~\mathbf{V}_{2}=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-i\eta\mathbf{Q}}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathbf{I},\\
\mathbf{V}_{3}=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{P}}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathbf{I},~\mathbf{V}_{4}=\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,e^{-i\eta\mathbf{P}}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathbf{I}.

Using

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}+i\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\triangleq\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{E}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=-i\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}+\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\triangleq\mathbf{S}

these four operators read

\mathbf{V}_{1}=e^{i\eta\mathbf{R}}-\mathbf{I},~\mathbf{V}_{2}=e^{i\eta\mathbf{S}}-\mathbf{I},~\mathbf{V}_{3}=e^{-i\eta\mathbf{R}}-\mathbf{I},~\mathbf{V}_{4}=e^{-i\eta\mathbf{S}}-\mathbf{I}.

(2)

Since
[\mathbf{R},\mathbf{S}]=i
and
\eta^{2}=4\pi
, for any
k,\ell
,
\mathbf{V}_{k}
and
\mathbf{V}_{\ell}
commute.
Then any density operator
\rho
having his support in
\text{span}\{\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle,\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\}
is a steady state of the following Lindblad master equation:

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}(\rho)\triangleq\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)

(3)

where

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{V}}(\rho)\triangleq\mathbf{V}\rho\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}-(\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}\rho+\rho\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}\mathbf{V})/2.

Next section provides a first formal analysis ensuring the exponential convergence of the above dynamical system towards the finite-energy GKP codespace, i.e. towards the set of density operators
\rho
with range in
\text{span}\{\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle,\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\}
.

3
Exponential convergence

The rigorous functional analysis framework is not addressed here:
calculations are led as if the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space were finite.
The
a priori
estimate we obtain
constitutes a first step towards
a fully rigorous mathematical analysis
that we plan to develop in future publications.

Theorem 1
.

Consider
\mathbf{W}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}_{k}
where the
\mathbf{V}_{k}
are given by (
2
).
Then for any time-varying density operator
\rho(t)
satisfying (
3
), we have, for
\eta=2\sqrt{\pi}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2\eta}]
:

\frac{d}{dt}\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(t)\right)\leq-\kappa(\varepsilon,\eta)\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(t)\right)

with
\kappa(\varepsilon,\eta)>0
given by

\kappa(\varepsilon,\eta)=\big(\sinh(\eta^{2}s)-\sin(\eta^{2}c)\big)\big(1-e^{-3\eta^{2}s/2}\big)\\
-\big(\cosh(\eta^{2}s)-\cos(\eta^{2}c)\big)\big(1+e^{-3\eta^{2}s/2}\big)

(4)

where
s=\sinh(2\varepsilon)
and
c=\cosh(2\varepsilon)
.
For
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
\eta=2\sqrt{\pi}
, we have
\kappa(\varepsilon,\eta)=2\eta^{4}\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})
.

The detailed proof is quite technical. It is given in appendix
A
and relies on Glauber identity.
This theorem implies that,
for any initial density operator
\rho(0)
,

0\leq\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(t)\right)\leq\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(0)\right)e^{-\kappa(\varepsilon,\eta)t}
.
Thus,
\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(t)\right)=0
.
Since, for all
t\geq 0
,

\rho(t)\geq 0
and
\mathbf{W}\geq 0
,
the support of
\rho(t)
converges to

\ker\mathbf{W}=\text{span}\{\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle,\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\}
,
the finite-energy GKP codespace.
Since any operator with support in
\ker\mathbf{W}

belongs to
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
,

\rho(t)
exponentially converges to a steady state of (
3
).

Moreover,
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}

coincides with operators having their support in
\ker\mathbf{W}
.
Thus,
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is of real dimension
4
,
spanned by density operators with support on
\text{span}\{\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle,\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\}
.

Remark 1
.

The a priori estimate of theorem
1
is also valid when
\eta=\sqrt{2\pi}
. Then
\ker\mathbf{W}
is spanned by
a single wave function corresponding to the regularization of the Dirac comb
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\big(q-k\sqrt{2\pi}\big)
and colinear to

\sum_{k}\tfrac{e^{-k^{2}\pi\tanh\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sinh\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{(q-k\sqrt{2\pi}/\cosh\varepsilon)^{2}}{2\tanh\varepsilon}}.

Such grid states are certainly to be considered as interesting resources in metrology to measure simultaneously the commuting modular observables derived from
e^{\pm i\eta\mathbf{Q}}
and
e^{\pm i\eta\mathbf{P}}
and thus to avoid the Heisenberg uncertainty principle attached to measurements of
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{P}
(see
[
17
, chapter V, section 4]
).

Consider

\mathbf{S}_{0}=\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|+\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 1_{\varepsilon}\right|,~\mathbf{S}_{x}=\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|+\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 1_{\varepsilon}\right|,\\
\mathbf{S}_{y}=i\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|-i\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 1_{\varepsilon}\right|,~\mathbf{S}_{z}=\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|-\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 1_{\varepsilon}\right|.

Since
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
is of real dimension
4
, the kernel
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
of its adjoint
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
for the Frobenius product is also of dimension
4
. It is spanned by four independent Hermitian invariant operators,
\mathbf{I}
(conservation of the trace) and

\mathbf{J}_{\xi}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{*}}(\mathbf{S}_{\xi}),~\xi=x,y,z.

Since the spectra of
\mathbf{S}_{x}
,
\mathbf{S}_{y}
and
\mathbf{S}_{z}
are
\{-1,0,1\}
, the spectra of

\mathbf{J}_{x}
,
\mathbf{J}_{y}
and
\mathbf{J}_{z}
are inside
[-1,1]

[
18
]
.
Then for any operator
\rho
, we have (see e.g.
[
19
]
):

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}}(\rho)=\tfrac{\mathbf{S}_{0}+\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{x}\rho\right)\mathbf{S}_{x}+\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{y}\rho\right)\mathbf{S}_{y}+\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho\right)\mathbf{S}_{z}}{2}.

The quantities

\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{x}\rho\right),\,\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{y}\rho\right),\,\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho\right)

can be seen as the Bloch coordinates
of a logical qubit encoded in the density operator
\rho
,
as they always satisfy

(\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{x}\rho\right))^{2}+(\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{y}\rho\right))^{2}+(\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho\right))^{2}\leq 1.

4
Simulations with photon-loss errors

Figure 1:
Simulations between
t_{0}=0
and
t_{f}=1/\kappa_{1}
, starting from
\rho(0)=\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|
(implying
\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho(0)\right)=1
) for different value of
\varepsilon
, with (label ”on”) and without (label ”off”) the autonomous error correction based on (
3
), including a non-negligible error rate of
\kappa_{1}=\varepsilon/5
associated to photon-losses (single-photon life-time of
1/\kappa_{1}
). The ”on” error-rate is estimated as
\kappa_{1}(1-\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho(1/\kappa_{1})\right))
. When
\varepsilon
decreases, the corresponding decrease of the photon-loss rate
\kappa_{1}
is compensated for by the choice of time horizon
t_{f}=1/\kappa_{1}
. We observe that for
\varepsilon=1/10
(resp.
1/20
and
1/30
), the ”on” error-rate is approximately
1/7
(resp.
1/80
and
1/1000
) of the bare ”off” error-rate
\kappa_{1}
.

The above formulae are used in our simulations to compute numerically
\mathbf{S}_{\xi}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\xi}
just by numerical time integration of (
3
) and of its adjoint. A Galerkin approximation is used with Fock subspace
\Big\{\left|n\right\rangle\Big\}_{0\leq n\leq n^{*}}
where
\left|n\right\rangle
is the state with
n
photons
[
20
]
. Since the average number of photons on the finite-energy GKP codespace is around
1/(2\varepsilon)
,
n^{*}
has to be much larger than
1/\varepsilon
. We have observed numerically that taking
n^{*}
around
20/\varepsilon
is enough since higher values do not change the results. On figure
1
, we have performed simulations for
\varepsilon=1/10
,
1/20
and
1/30
.
All simulations start with

\rho_{0}=\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle\left\langle 0_{\varepsilon}\right|

on the finite-energy GKP codespace,
i.e. with logical coordinates

\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{x}\rho_{0}\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{y}\rho_{0}\right)=0

and

\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{z}\rho_{0}\right)=1
.
All simulations include photon-loss errors at a rate
\kappa_{1}=\varepsilon/5
scaled as 10% of the inverse of the average number of photon in
\left|0_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
and
\left|1_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle
.
The Lindblad master equations numerically solved are of two kinds:

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(\rho),&\hbox{ curve label ''on'';}\\
\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(\rho),&\hbox{curve label ''off'',}\end{array}\right.

where
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{Q}+i\mathbf{P})/\sqrt{2}
is the annihilation operator.
We observe a strong suppression of errors in presence of the engineered dissipation
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
.
Other simulations not presented here with local phase-space operators (polynomial of low-order in
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
) such as
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{Q}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{P}}
instead of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{a}}
, exhibit a similar strong decrease of the decoherence rate when
\varepsilon
is decreased.

5
Concluding remarks

The guarantee of exponential stability
provided by theorem
1
,
combined with the
numerically observed
efficient protection
against local errors in phase space, motivates the following issue:
how to physically implement the autonomous stabilization scheme
attached to the Lindblad master equation (
3
)?
Quantum superconducting circuits
[
7
]

and trapped ions
[
8
]

appear as promising platforms
for this task.

The strong impact of
\varepsilon
close to
0^{+}
on the decoherence rates is an indication of some exponential behaviour in the protection against local errors. This point will be investigated in
future works.

Notice the analogy between the Lyapunov function
\mathbf{W}
in theorem
1

and the Lyapunov function

(\mathbf{a}^{r}-\alpha^{r}\mathbf{I})^{\dagger}(\mathbf{a}^{r}-\alpha^{r}\mathbf{I})

introduced in
[
21
]

for the Lindblad master equation corresponding to multi-photon pumping and cat-qubits
[
3
]
:

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{a}^{r}-\alpha^{r}\mathbf{I}}(\rho)
with

r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.
As already done in
[
21
]
for cat-qubits, we expect to provide in forthcoming publications a fully rigorous and functional analysis proof of well-posedness and exponential convergence of the infinite-dimensional initial-value problem (
3
).

Appendix A
Proof of theorem
1

Elementary numerical computations combined with the asymptotics
2\eta^{4}\varepsilon^{2}
around
0^{+}
ensure that
\kappa>0
when

\eta=2\sqrt{\pi}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2\eta}]
.

Formally
\frac{d}{dt}\rm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{W}\rho(t)\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\rho(t)\sum_{k=1}^{4}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}(\mathbf{W})\right)
where the adjoints
\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}
of the super operators
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}(\mathbf{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{V}_{k}-(\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}_{k}\mathbf{W}+\mathbf{W}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathbf{V}_{k})/2

\displaystyle\equiv(\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathbf{W},\mathbf{V}_{k}]+[\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger},\mathbf{W}]\mathbf{V}_{k})/2.

Since for any
k
and
\ell
,
[\mathbf{V}_{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{k}]=0
, we have
[\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}_{\ell}\mathbf{V}_{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{k}]=[\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}_{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{k}]\mathbf{V}_{\ell}
. Thus

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{W}\triangleq\sum_{k=1}^{4}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\mathbf{V}_{k}}(\mathbf{W})=\sum_{k,\ell}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}_{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{k}]\mathbf{V}_{\ell}.

Let us introduce the notation

\mathbf{R}_{1}=\mathbf{R},\,\mathbf{R}_{2}=\mathbf{S},\,\mathbf{R}_{3}=-\mathbf{R},\,\mathbf{R}_{4}=-\mathbf{S}

such that

\mathbf{V}_{k}=e^{i\eta\mathbf{R}_{k}}-\mathbf{I}.

Exploiting Glauber identity, we get

e^{i\eta\mathbf{A}}e^{i\eta\mathbf{B}}=e^{-\tfrac{\eta^{2}}{2}[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]}\,e^{i\eta(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})}

for operators
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
such that
[\mathbf{A},[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]]=[\mathbf{B},[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]]=0
,
from which

\left[e^{i\eta\mathbf{A}},\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{B}}\right]=e^{i\eta\mathbf{B}}\,\left(e^{-\eta^{2}[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]}-\mathbf{I}\right)\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{A}}.

With
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\{\mathbf{R},\mathbf{S},\mathbf{R}^{\dagger},\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}\}
and commutations

[\mathbf{R},\mathbf{R}^{\dagger}]=[\mathbf{S},\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}]=\sinh(2\varepsilon)\mathbf{I}\text{ and }[\mathbf{R},\mathbf{S}^{\dagger}]=i\cosh(2\varepsilon)\mathbf{I},

we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathbf{V}_{\ell}^{\dagger},\mathbf{V}_{k}]\mathbf{V}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\,[e^{-i\eta\mathbf{R}_{\ell}^{\dagger}},\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{R}_{k}}]\,\mathbf{V}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell}\mathbf{V}_{k}^{\dagger}\,e^{i\eta\mathbf{R}_{k}}\,\left(e^{\eta^{2}\left[\mathbf{R}_{\ell}^{\dagger},\mathbf{R}_{k}\right]}-\mathbf{I}\right)\,e^{-i\eta\mathbf{R}_{\ell}^{\dagger}}\mathbf{V}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell}\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\dagger}T_{k,\ell}\mathbf{W}_{\ell}

where

\mathbf{W}_{k}\triangleq e^{-i\eta\mathbf{R}_{k}^{\dagger}}\,\mathbf{V}_{k}

and

T_{k,\ell}\triangleq e^{\eta^{2}[\mathbf{R}_{\ell}^{\dagger},\mathbf{R}_{k}]}-1

are scalar coefficients
forming the entries of the Hermitian circulant matrix:

T=\begin{pmatrix}-1+e^{-\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{-i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{i\eta^{2}c}\\
-1+e^{i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{-\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{-i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{\eta^{2}s}\\
-1+e^{\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{-\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{-i\eta^{2}c}\\
-1+e^{-i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{\eta^{2}s}&-1+e^{i\eta^{2}c}&-1+e^{-\eta^{2}s}\\
\end{pmatrix}

with
s=\sinh(2\varepsilon)
and
c=\cosh(2\varepsilon)
.
This matrix admits the spectral decomposition

T=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}w_{k}^{\dagger}w_{k}

where

\displaystyle w_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}&\tfrac{-1}{2}&\tfrac{1}{2}&\tfrac{-1}{2}\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }\lambda_{1}=2(\cosh(\eta^{2}s)-\cos(\eta^{2}c))

\displaystyle w_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}&\tfrac{1}{2}&\tfrac{1}{2}&\tfrac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }\lambda_{2}=2(\cosh(\eta^{2}s)+\cos(\eta^{2}c)-2)

\displaystyle+

Appendix B
An operator inequality

Lemma 1
.

Take two operators Hermitian
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{P}
such that
[\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{P}]=i\mathbf{I}
. Then

\forall\eta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}\quad\quad e^{-\tfrac{3\eta^{2}|\sinh(2\varepsilon)|}{4}}\cosh\big(3\eta\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\big)\geq\pm\cos\big(\eta\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}\big).

Proof.

Set
\mathbf{R}=\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}+i\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}

and
\Lambda_{\pm}=e^{-i\eta\mathbf{R}^{\dagger}}e^{i\eta\tfrac{\mathbf{R}}{2}}\pm e^{i\eta\mathbf{R}^{\dagger}}e^{-i\eta\tfrac{\mathbf{R}}{2}}
,
then usual computations based on Glauber identity yield

\Lambda_{\pm}^{\dagger}\Lambda_{\pm}=2e^{-\eta^{2}s/8}\left(\cosh\big(3\eta\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\big)\pm e^{-3\eta^{2}s/4}\cos\big(\eta\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}\big)\right)

with
s=\sinh(2\varepsilon)
.
Thus, for any
\eta
and
\varepsilon
, the operators

\cosh\big(3\eta\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\big)\pm e^{-3\eta^{2}s/4}\cos\big(\eta\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}\big)

are non-negative. This means that

e^{3\eta^{2}s/4}\cosh\big(3\eta\sinh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{P}\big)\geq\pm\cos\big(\eta\cosh(\varepsilon)\mathbf{Q}\big).

We conclude by changing
\varepsilon
to
-\varepsilon
.
∎

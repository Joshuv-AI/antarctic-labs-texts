Introduction

The numerical methods of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) have been
extensively studied in the past decades, and by now it is still an active
research area; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
25
,
41
,
42
]
and the reference therein.
However, the majority of numerical analysis efforts in this domain have primarily focused on the Hilbert space
framework, while investigations within the context of Banach spaces remain relatively limited.

We summarize some related works in this field that have come to our attention.
Regarding abstract stochastic Cauchy problems, Cox and van Neerven
[
9
,
10
]
established
pathwise Hölder convergence for both the splitting scheme and the implicit-linear Euler scheme.
Blömker and Jentzen
[
2
]
conducted a detailed analysis of Galerkin approximations
for the one-dimensional stochastic Burgers equation, focusing on the spatial
L^{\infty}
-space setting.
Bréhier et al.
[
5
]
analyzed semidiscrete splitting approximations for the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with additive noise under general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.
Recently, van Neerven and Veraar
[
35
]
developed an elegant framework for establishing pathwise uniform convergence for time discretisation schemes for a broad class of SPDEs.
Additionally, Klioba and Veraar
[
21
]
analyzed temporal approximations of stochastic evolution equations with irregular nonlinearities within the
2
-smooth Banach spaces setting.

Despite these significant advancements, the numerical analysis of SPDEs in the broader context
of general Banach spaces remains an underdeveloped area.
This motivates us to analyze the stability and convergence of the Euler scheme for the stochastic linear
evolution equations in Banach spaces, which is one of the most popular
temporal discretization scheme in this realm.

Firstly, we establish a discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate.
Maximal
L^{p}
-regularity is of fundamental importance for the deterministic
evolution equations; see, e.g.,
[
11
,
26
,
33
,
40
]
.
In the past twenty years, the discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity of deterministic
evolution equations has also attracted great attention; see, e.g.,

[
3
,
22
,
24
,
23
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
Utilizing the techniques of
H^{\infty}
-calculus,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness,
and square function estimates, van Neerven et al.
[
38
]
have established the
following seminal stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate:

\displaystyle\Biggl[\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\biggl\lVert A^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\biggr\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\Biggr]^{1/p}\leqslant c\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}.

In this inequality,
p\in(2,\infty)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
,
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded domain in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geqslant 2
),
H
is a separable Hilbert space,
W
represents an
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion,

S(\cdot)
is the analytic semigroup generated by a sectorial operator
A
on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
,
and
f
is an
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
-valued stochastic process adapted to the underlying filtration.
Building on the methodology laid out in
[
38
]
, we establish the following discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate:

\displaystyle\Biggl[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\biggl\lVert A^{1/2}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{k\tau}^{k\tau+\tau}(I+\tau A)^{k-j}f_{k}\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\biggr\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\Biggr]^{1/p}\leqslant c\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lVert{f_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p},

where
\tau
represents the time step. This result is presented rigorously in Theorem
3.2
.
For comparison, we provide the deterministic discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate as follows (see
[
22
]
):

\displaystyle\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\tau\biggl\lVert A\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{k\tau}^{k\tau+\tau}(I+\tau A)^{k-j}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\Biggl]^{1/p}\leqslant c\lVert g\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}+;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))},\quad\forall g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}+;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})).

In contrast to the deterministic case, the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate exhibits
inferior spatial regularity, a discrepancy that can be attributed to the presence of Brownian motion.
Notably, when
p=q=2
and
A
corresponds to the negative Laplacian on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
with
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, the aforementioned discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
estimate is well-established and can be derived using a simple energy argument.
Furthermore, analogous estimates have been obtained in the Hilbert space setting by Kazashi
[
28
]
.
Although our numerical analysis assumes that
A
is a sectorial operator on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
, the findings can be extended to the case where
A
is the negative Stokes operator.

Secondly, we establish a sharp error estimate in the norm

\lVert{\cdot}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}
, with
p,q\in[2,\infty)
.
Previous research, including the works of
[
9
,
10
,
35
]
,
has yielded various error estimates involving general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.
However, the convergence in the specific norm
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
has not been thoroughly investigated.
This type of error estimate is particularly significant for the numerical analysis of stochastic optimal control problems that involve stochastic evolution equations.
In this study, by employing a duality argument and assuming that the process
f
is piecewise constant in time, we derive the following sharp error estimate:

\left[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}\lVert{y(t)-Y_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p}\leqslant c\tau^{1/2}\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert{f(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p},

where
y
denotes the mild solution of a stochastic linear evolution equation,
and
(Y_{j})_{j=0}^{J}
represents its temporal approximation via the Euler scheme.
This result is formally stated in
Theorem

4.1
.
It is widely recognized that for the Euler scheme, achieving a convergence rate of
1/2
under general spatial

L^{q}
-norms is typically unattainable when dealing with rough data.
However, it is noteworthy that under the additional assumption of piecewise constant temporal behavior for
the process
f
, the convergence rate of
1/2
can still be achieved.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces
necessary notation alongside the concepts of
\gamma
-radonifying operators,
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness,

H^{\infty}
-calculus, and stochastic integral. In Section
3
,
we establish the discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity. Finally, Section
4
provides
a sharp error estimate.

Conventions
.
Throughout this paper, we will use the following conventions:
For any Banach spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
,
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
denotes the space of all bounded linear operators from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
, and
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{1})
is abbreviated to
\mathcal{L}(E_{1})
;
The symbol
I
denotes the identity operator;
For each
p\in[1,\infty]
, its conjugate exponent is denoted by
p^{\prime}
;
For any measure space
(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
, any Banach space
E
,
and any
p\in[1,\infty]
, we use
L^{p}(X;E)
to denote a standard Bochner space (see
[
18
, Chapter 1]
);
Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\,(d\geqslant 2)
be a bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary;
The imaginary unit is denoted by
i
;
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, its argument

\operatorname{Arg}z
is restricted to the interval
(-\pi,\pi]
;
The symbol
c
denotes a generic positive constant, which is independent of the time step
\tau
but may differ in different places.
In addition, for any
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we define the sector

\Sigma_{\theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid-\theta<\operatorname{Arg}z<\theta\}.

\gamma
-Radonifying operators
.
For any Banach space
E
and Hilbert space
U
with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{U}
, define

\mathcal{S}(U,E):=\text{span}\big\{u\otimes e\mid\,u\in U,\,e\in E\},

where
u\otimes e\in\mathcal{L}(U,E)
is defined by

(u\otimes e)(v):=(v,u)_{U}e,\quad\forall v\in U.

Let
\gamma(U,E)
denote the completion of
\mathcal{S}(U,E)

with respect to the norm

\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi_{n}\otimes e_{n}}\Big\rVert_{\gamma(U,E)}:=\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}e_{n}}\Big\rVert_{E}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, all orthonormal systems

(\phi_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
of
U
, all sequences
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
E
,
and all sequences
(\gamma_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
of independent standard Gaussian
random variables. Here,
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation operator
associated with the probability space on which
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N}

are defined.
It is noteworthy that when
E
is a Hilbert space,
\gamma(U,E)
is identical to the space of
all Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
U
to
E
, equipped with the same norm.
Furthermore, of particular significance is the isometric isomorphism between
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
and

\gamma(H,L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
for any
q\in[1,\infty)
.
For a comprehensive study of
\gamma
-radonifying operators, the reader is directed
to
[
19
, Chapter 9]
.

\mathcal{R}
-boundedness
. For any two Banach spaces

E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, an operator family
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
is said to be
\mathcal{R}
-bounded if there exists a constant
C>0

such that

\int_{0}^{1}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)B_{n}x_{n}}\Big\rVert_{E_{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant C\int_{0}^{1}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)x_{n}}\Big\rVert_{E_{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
N\geqslant 1
, all sequences
(B_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
\mathcal{A}
,
all sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in
E_{1}
, and all sequences
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}

of independent symmetric
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables on
[0,1]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{A})
the infimum of these
C
’s.
For a comprehensive treatment of
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness, the reader is referred to

[
19
, Chapter 10]
and
[
33
, Chapter 4]
.

H^{\infty}
-calculus
.
A sectorial operator
A
with an angle of analyticity
\omega_{A}
on a Banach space
E
is said to possess a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus if there exists
\sigma\in(\omega_{A},\pi]
such that

\Big\lVert{\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\sigma}}\varphi(z)(z-A)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z}\Big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(E)}\leqslant C\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\sigma}}\lvert{\varphi(z)}\rvert

holds for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
, where
C
is a positive constant that is independent of
\varphi
. The space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\sigma})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\varphi:\Sigma_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{C}\mid\,\text{the function $\varphi$ is analytic and there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\sigma}}\left(\frac{1+\lvert{z}\rvert^{2}}{\lvert{z}\rvert}\right)^{\varepsilon}\lvert{\varphi(z)}\rvert<\infty\right\}.

The infimum of all such
\sigma
values is referred to as the angle of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
A
.
It is worth mentioning that a wide array of partial differential operators admits a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, including the negative Laplacian and the negative Stokes operator; see Section 9 of
[
27
]
. For an exhaustive treatment of the
H^{\infty}
-calculus, the reader is directed to Chapter 5 of
[
17
]
and Chapter 10 of
[
19
]
.

Stochastic integral
.
Assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a given complete probability
space equipped with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
.
On this space, we are given a sequence of independent
\mathbb{F}
-adapted Brownian motions

(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
0\leqslant s<t<\infty
and
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the increment
\beta_{n}(t)-\beta_{n}(s)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
.
In the sequel, we will
use
\mathbb{E}
to denote the expectation operator associated with the
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space with inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
and an orthonormal basis
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion

W
is defined such that for each
t\geqslant 0
,
W(t)
is an element of
\mathcal{L}(H,L^{2}(\Omega))
,
given explicitly by

W(t)h=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(h,h_{n})_{H}\beta_{n}(t),\quad\forall h\in H.

The reader is referred to
[
36
]
for the theory of stochastic integrals with respect to

in UMD Banach spaces.
For any
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, let

L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)))

denote the space of all
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)
-valued processes in

L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)))
.
The stochastic integral has the following essential isomorphism feature;
see, e.g.,
[
37

.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, there exist two positive constants

c_{0}
and

c_{0}\mathbb{E}\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))}^{p}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big\lVert{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\leqslant c_{1}\mathbb{E}\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H))}^{p}

(2.1)

for all
f\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H)))
.

Discrete spaces
.
For any Banach space
E
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, define

\ell^{p}(E):=\Big\{(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Bigl|\,\,\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert{v_{j}}\rVert_{E}^{p}<\infty\Big\},

and endow this space with the norm

\lVert{(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(E)}:=\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert{v_{j}}\rVert_{E}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\quad\text{for all }(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{p}(E).

For any
v\in\ell^{p}(E)

v_{j}
,

3
Stability estimates

Let
0<\tau<1
be a fixed time step. For any
p,q,r\in[1,\infty)
, let
\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
denote the space

\left\{v\in\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))\mid v_{j}\text{ is }\mathcal{F}_{j\tau}\text{-measurable for all }j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

It is well-known that
\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
forms a Banach space when endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))}
.
For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for any
v\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))
with
p,q\in[2,\infty)
that is
\mathcal{F}_{j\tau}
-measurable, we introduce the shorthand notation
v\delta W_{j}
to represent the stochastic integral

\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}v\,\mathrm{d}W(t).

This notation is adopted for the sake of brevity and clarity.

This section studies the stability of the following Euler scheme: seek
Y:=(Y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}+\tau AY_{j+1}=f_{j}\delta W_{j},\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

(3.1a)

\displaystyle Y_{0}=0,

(3.1b)

where the sequence
f:=(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is given.

The main result of this section are the following two theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p,q,r\in(1,\infty)
. Assume that
A
is a densely defined
sectorial operator on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
and

\{z(z-A)^{-1}\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta_{A}}}\}

is
\mathcal{R}
-bounded in
\mathcal{L}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
,
where
\theta_{A}\in(0,\pi/2)
.
Let
Y
be the solution to the discretization
Eq.

3.1
with

f\in\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))).

Then the following stability estimate holds:

\left[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\frac{Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}}{\sqrt{\tau}}}\Big\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant c\lVert{f}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))},

(3.2)

where
c
is a constant independent of the time step
\tau
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
. Assume that
A
is a
densely defined sectorial operator on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
satisfying the
following conditions:

•

A
has a dense range in
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
;

•

there exists
\theta_{A}\in(0,\pi/2)
such that

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},\,\lvert{\operatorname{Arg}z}\rvert\geqslant\theta_{A}}\,\lvert{z}\rvert\lVert{(z-A)^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}<\infty;

(3.3)

•

A
admits a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle less than
\theta_{A}
.

Let
Y
be the solution to the discretization
Eq.

3.1
with

f\in\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))).

Then the following discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate holds:

\lVert{A^{1/2}Y}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}\leqslant c\lVert{f}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))},

(3.4)

where
c
is a constant independent of
\tau
.

Remark 3.1
.

The discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate established in
Theorem

3.2

is useful for the numerical analysis of significant nonlinear SPDEs, including the stochastic Allen-Cahn
equations and the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations.
Here, we present a concise application of Theorem
3.2
to the numerical analysis of nonlinear
stochastic parabolic equations as follows. Let
T>0
be a fixed time horizon, and let

\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary

\partial\mathcal{O}
. Consider a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of continuous functions from
\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
that satisfy the following growth condition for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

|f_{n}(x,y)|\leqslant C_{F}(1+|y|)\quad\text{for all $x\in\mathcal{O}$ and $y\in\mathbb{R}$},

where
C_{F}
is a constant independent of
n
. Additionally, let
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative real numbers such that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}<\infty.

We investigate the following nonlinear stochastic parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t,x)-\Delta y(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}f_{n}(x,y(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{n}(t),&t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathcal{O},\\
y(t,x)=0,&t\in(0,T],\,x\in\partial\mathcal{O},\\
y(0,x)=0,&x\in\mathcal{O},\end{cases}

where
(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
represents a sequence of independent standard Brownian motions as defined in Section
2
.
It is well-known that the negative Laplace operator with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions meets
the conditions of Theorem
3.2
; see, for instance,
[
27
, Proposition 9.8]
.
Let
J
be a positive integer and set
\tau=\frac{T}{J}
. We consider the following temporal semidiscretization:

\begin{cases}Y_{j+1}(x)-Y_{j}(x)-\tau\Delta Y_{j+1}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}f_{n}(x,Y_{j}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{n}(t),&0\leqslant j<J,\,x\in\mathcal{O},\\
Y_{j}(x)=0,&1\leqslant j\leqslant J,\,x\in\partial\mathcal{O},\\
Y_{0}(x)=0,&x\in\mathcal{O}.\end{cases}

For brevity, we henceforth refer to a quantity as “bounded” to imply uniform boundedness with respect to
\tau
.
For any
p\in(2,\infty)
, a routine computation reveals that

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|Y_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\big]
is bounded,
which implies by Theorem
3.2
that

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\nabla Y_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\big]
is also bounded.
Applying Sobolev’s embedding theorem, we further obtain that

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|Y_{j}\|_{L^{6}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\big]
is
bounded. Hence, invoking Theorem
3.2
once more yields that
\mathbb{E}\big[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\nabla Y_{j}\|_{L^{6}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\big]

is bounded.
By iterating this argument, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}\left[\tau\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\nabla Y_{j}\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\right]\quad\text{is bounded for all $p,q\in(2,\infty)$}.

This methodology was utilized in
[
32
]
; interested readers are referred there for additional details.
Furthermore,
Theorem

3.1
implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tau\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left\lVert\frac{Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\right]\quad\text{is bounded for all $p,q\in(2,\infty)$}.

Analogous stability results can be straightforwardly derived when the initial value is non-zero.

3.1
Proof of
Theorem

3.1

Let
\widetilde{A}
be the natural extension of

in

It is straightforward to verify that
\widetilde{A}
is a sectorial operator on
L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
.
Let
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an arbitrary sequence of independent symmetric
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables on
[0,1]
. For any
N\geqslant 1
,
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta_{A}}}
, and
(v_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(i)}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle c\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-\widetilde{A})^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle c\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-\widetilde{A})^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle c\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-A)^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(ii)}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle c\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-A)^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{r/2}\right]^{1/r},

[

By the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of the set

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-\widetilde{A})^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\mathcal{R}\left(\{z(z-A)^{-1}\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta_{A}}}\}\right)\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{r/2}\right]^{1/r}.

Applying the Kahane-Khintchine inequality once more, we obtain

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{r/2}\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle=c\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/2}.

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)z_{n}(z_{n}-\widetilde{A})^{-1}v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\mathcal{R}\big(\{z(z-A)^{-1}\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\theta_{A}}}\}\big)\left[\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}(t)v_{n}\Bigr\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/2}.

It follows that

\mathcal{R}

[

\displaystyle\Big\lVert{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\theta_{A}}}z^{1/2}\lambda^{1/2}(z-\lambda)^{-1}(\lambda-A_{\epsilon})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\Big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}

Thus, an elementary calculation using property (b) yields the following additional property:

(d)

For any given

,

(3.25)

\displaystyle I_{j,\epsilon}^{(2)}

.

.

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}+\tau AY_{j+1}=\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad 0\leqslant j<J,

(4.2a)

\displaystyle Y_{0}=0.

(4.2b)

.

Let
v
be an arbitrary but fixed element of

{}^{0}H^{1,p^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T;D(A^{*}))
.
Using standard techniques (see, e.g.,
[
39
, Lemma 5.5]
), we have, almost surely,

\int_{0}^{T}\langle{y(t),\,-v^{\prime}(t)+A^{*}v(t)}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),\,f(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W(t),

where the duality pairing on the left-hand side is between
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
and

L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})
, and the pairing on the right-hand side denotes an

\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})
-valued process, as detailed in Remark
4.2
.
Furthermore, a direct computation yields, almost surely,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),\,f(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,v({j\tau+\tau})\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{t}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\,v^{\prime}(t)\biggr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, almost surely,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle{y(t),\,-v^{\prime}(t)+A^{*}v(t)}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,v({j\tau+\tau})\biggr\rangle-\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{t}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\,v^{\prime}(t)\biggr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t.

For any measurable set
C\in\mathcal{F}
, multiplying both sides of the above equality by the indicator
function
\mathbbm{1}_{C}
for
C
and taking expectations, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle{y(t),\,-z^{\prime}(t)+A^{*}z(t)}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

(4.4)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Biggl\{\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,z({j\tau+\tau})\biggr\rangle-\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{t}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,z^{\prime}(t)\biggr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Biggr\}

holds for
z=v\mathbbm{1}_{C}
. Since both sides of
Eq.

4.4
act as bounded linear functionals on

L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;{}^{0}H^{1,p^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T;D(A^{*})))

with respect to
z
, as can be readily verified by
Lemma

2.1
, and
considering the density of the linear span

\text{span}\left\{v\mathbbm{1}_{C}\mid v\in{}^{0}H^{1,p^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T;D(A^{*})),\,C\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

within this space, we can apply a density argument to conclude that
Eq.

4.4
holds
for all

z\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;{}^{0}H^{1,p^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))\cap L^{p^{\prime}}(0,T;D(A^{*}))).

Step 2.

Let
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))
be given arbitrarily.
According to Theorem 4.2 in
[
40
]
, there exists a process
z
such that
z
solves the
backward evolution equation almost surely:

\begin{cases}-z^{\prime}(t)+A^{*}z(t)=g(t),&0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
z(T)=0,\end{cases}

and satisfies the regularity estimate

\lVert{z^{\prime}}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}+\lVert{A^{*}z}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}\leqslant c\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

(4.5)

Define the sequence
(Z_{j})_{j=0}^{J}
almost surely by

\displaystyle Z_{j}-Z_{j+1}+\tau A^{*}Z_{j}=\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad 0\leqslant j<J,

(4.6a)

\displaystyle Z_{J}=0.

(4.6b)

By adopting an approach analogous to that presented in
[
23
, Theorem III]
,
we can establish the following well-known inequality:

\biggl[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\tau\lVert{z({j\tau})-Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}^{p^{\prime}}\biggr]^{1/p^{\prime}}\leqslant c\tau\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

(4.7)

Next, using
Eq.

4.2
and
Eq.

4.6
, a straightforward computation gives that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\big\langle{Y_{j},\,g(t)}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,Z_{j+1}\biggr\rangle.

Furthermore, the identity
Eq.

4.4
implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle y(t),\,g(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,z({j\tau+\tau})\biggr\rangle-\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{t}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,z^{\prime}(t)\biggr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg\}.

Combining these equalities, we derive the following identity:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\langle y(t)-Y_{j},g(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,z({j\tau+\tau})-Z_{j+1}\biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\biggl\langle\int_{{j\tau}}^{t}f({j\tau})\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\,z^{\prime}(t)\biggr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\left\|\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}f(j\tau)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\left\|z(j\tau+\tau)-Z_{j+1}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\left\|\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}f(j\tau)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:z-Z-omega})}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\tau^{\frac{p}{2}}\left\|f(j\tau)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:integral})}

\displaystyle=c\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert{f}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Similarly, for
I_{2}
, using Hölder’s inequality, the regularity estimate
Eq.

4.5
,
and
Lemma

2.1
, we deduce that

I_{2}\leqslant c\tau^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

By combining these bounds, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\langle y(t)-Y_{j},\,g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant c\tau^{1/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Given that
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))
is arbitrary,
we can invoke the principle of duality to directly achieve the desired error estimate
Eq.

4.3
.
This concludes the proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.2
.

In Step 1 of the preceding proof, the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),f(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W(t)
is
interpreted as follows. By virtue of the
\gamma
-Fubini isomorphism
(cf.
[
19
, Theorem 9.4.8]
), it is established that
f
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted

\gamma(H,L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))
-valued process. Furthermore, for each
t\in[0,T]
,
v(t)
can
be viewed as a bounded linear functional from
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
to
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
by applying the ideal property of
\gamma
-radonifying operators (see
[
19
, Theorem 9.1.10]
),

\langle v(\cdot),f(\cdot)\rangle
is identified as an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})
-valued process. This identification comes with the estimate

\lVert{\langle v(t),f(t)\rangle}\rVert_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\lVert{v(t)}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\lVert{f(t)}\rVert_{\gamma(H,L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}\leqslant c\lVert{v(t)}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\lVert{f(t)}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)},\quad t\in[0,T].

Therefore, the stochastic integral
\int_{0}^{T}\langle v(t),f(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W(t)
is understood
as the stochastic integral of an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})
-valued process.

Remark 4.3
.

To provide further insight into the derivation of
Eq.

4.7
, we start by noting that, almost surely,

\displaystyle\big(z(j\tau)-Z_{j}\big)-\big(z(j\tau+\tau)-Z_{j+1}\big)+\tau A^{*}\big(z(j\tau)-Z_{j}\big)=A^{*}\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}z(j\tau)-z(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Utilizing the deterministic discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimate (see
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\tau\lVert{z(j\tau)-Z_{j}}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}^{p^{\prime}}\right]^{1/p^{\prime}}\leqslant c\left[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}\lVert{z(t)-z(j\tau)}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p^{\prime}}.

The desired inequality
Eq.

4.7
then follows from the standard estimate:

\left[\mathbb{E}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{j\tau}^{j\tau+\tau}\lVert{z(t)-z(j\tau)}\rVert_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})}^{p^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p^{\prime}}\leqslant c\tau\lVert{z^{\prime}}\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Appendix A
Some technical estimates

In this section, we employ the notation established in
Section

3
.
Let
\mu
denote the Lebesgue measure on the domain
\mathcal{O}
.
We also recall that
c
represents a generic positive constant,
which is independent of the time step
\tau
, though its value may vary from one instance to another.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
p,q\in[2,\infty)
. Consider the curve
\Upsilon
constructed in Subsection

3.2
. Let
\mathcal{I}(z)
be defined for
z\in\Upsilon
as in
Eq.

3.14
.
We assert that
\mathcal{I}(z)
is uniformly bounded in the norm of the space

\mathcal{L}\left(\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))),\,\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))\right)

with respect to both
z\in\Upsilon
and the time step
\tau
.

Proof.

Fix any
z\in\Upsilon
and let
\xi=e^{-z}-1
.
Using
Lemma

2.1
, Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality,
we obtain, for any
g\in\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{I}(z)g}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{p}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}g_{k}\delta W_{k}/\sqrt{\tau}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\lVert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}g_{k}}\rVert_{H}^{2}\Big)^{q/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr]^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\lVert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}g_{k}}\rVert_{H}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big)^{2/q}\biggr]^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\left[\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\big\lvert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}}\big\rvert^{2}\lVert{g_{k}}\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)}^{p}\right]\big(I(j,\xi)\big)^{p/2-1}\Bigg\},

where

\displaystyle I(j,\xi):=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\big\lvert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}}\big\rvert^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\big\lvert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{-k}}\big\rvert^{2}.

It follows that, for any
g\in\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{I}(z)g}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle cI(\infty,\xi)^{p/2-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\big\lvert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}}\big\rvert^{2}\lVert{g_{k}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}^{p}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle cI(\infty,\xi)^{p/2-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\big\lvert{\xi^{1/2}(1+\xi)^{k+1-j}}\big\rvert^{2}\lVert{g_{k}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle cI(\infty,\xi)^{p/2}\lVert{g}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))}^{p}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle cI(\infty,e^{-z}-1)^{p/2}\lVert{g}\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))}^{p},

by the fact
\xi=e^{-z}-1
. This leads to the bound

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{I}(z)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}\big(\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))),\,\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))\big)}\leqslant cI(\infty,e^{-z}-1)^{1/2}.

Therefore, the desired claim follows from the fact
I(\infty,0)=0
and the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\Upsilon\setminus\{0\}}\,I(\infty,e^{-z}-1)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\Upsilon\setminus\{0\}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big\lvert{(e^{-z}-1)^{1/2}e^{kz}}\big\rvert^{2}=\sup_{z\in\Upsilon\setminus\{0\}}\,\frac{\lvert{e^{-z}-1}\rvert}{1-\lvert{e^{2z}}\rvert}<\infty,

which is easily verified by the construction of
\Upsilon
as detailed in Subsection
3.2
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
r\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(1,\infty]
. For any
g\in\ell^{r}(L^{s}(\mathcal{O}))
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{1+m}\sum_{k=j}^{j+m}\lvert{g_{k}}\rvert}\Big\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\leqslant c\lVert{g}\rVert_{\ell^{r}(L^{s}(\mathcal{O}))}^{r}.

Proof.

Define

G(t):=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(1+m)\tau}\int_{t}^{t+(1+m)\tau}\widetilde{g}(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta.

where
\widetilde{g}(t):=\lvert{g_{j}}\rvert
for all
t\in[{j\tau},{j\tau+\tau})

and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
j\geqslant 1
, it is easily verified that

\displaystyle G({j\tau})(x)\leqslant\frac{c}{\tau}\int_{j\tau-\tau}^{{j\tau+\tau}}G(t)(x)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{O},

which implies, through Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, that

\displaystyle\lVert{G({j\tau})}\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{-r}\biggl[\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\int_{j\tau-\tau}^{{j\tau+\tau}}G(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr]^{r/s}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{-r}\biggl[\int_{j\tau-\tau}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\lVert{G(t)}\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{-1}\int_{j\tau-\tau}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\lVert{G(t)}\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Summing over
j
, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lVert{G({j\tau})}\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lVert{G(t)}\rVert_{L^{s}(\mathcal{O})}^{r}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant c\tau^{-1}\lVert{\widetilde{g}}\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{s}(\mathcal{O}))}^{r}\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Neerven2012}{}{}, Proposition~3.4]})}

\displaystyle=c\lVert{g}\rVert_{\ell^{r}(L^{s}(\mathcal{O}))}^{r}.

The desired inequality then follows from the fact

G({j\tau})=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{1+m}\sum_{k=j}^{j+m}\lvert{g_{k}}\rvert,\quad\forall j\geqslant 1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that
p,q\in[2,\infty)
. Define

\{\Pi_{m}\mid\,m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathcal{L}\big(\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H))),\,\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))\big)

as follows: for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

g\in\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
,

\displaystyle(\Pi_{m}g)_{0}:=0,

(A.1a)

\displaystyle(\Pi_{m}g)_{j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}\sum_{k=(j-1-m)\vee 0}^{j-1}g_{k}\frac{\delta W_{k}}{\sqrt{\tau}},\quad j\geqslant 1.

(A.1b)

Then
\mathcal{R}(\{\Pi_{m}\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\})
is uniformly bounded
with respect to the time step
\tau
.

Proof.

Following the proof of
[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
, we only present
a brief derivation.
Consider an arbitrary positive integer
N
. Let
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
denote a sequence of
independent, symmetric
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables defined on the probability
space
\Omega_{r}
. We denote the expectation with respect
to this probability space by
\mathbb{E}_{r}
. Given a sequence
(g^{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in

\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
and a sequence
(m_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
in

\mathbb{N}
, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}\Pi_{m_{n}}g^{n}\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}\Pi_{m_{n}}g^{n}\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{r}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{1+m_{n}}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}g_{k}^{n}\delta W_{k}/\sqrt{\tau}\Bigr\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}^{p}\biggr]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{r}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{1+m_{n}}}\mathbbm{1}_{j-1-m_{n}\leqslant k}g_{k}^{n}\Bigr\|_{H}^{2}\Big)^{q/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Big]^{p/q}\biggr]^{1/p},

where the first inequality utilizes the Kahane-Khintchine inequality (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Theorem 6.2.4]
),
and the final inequality employs Lemma
2.1
.
Here,
\mathbbm{1}_{j-1-m_{n}\leqslant k}
denotes the indicator
function, which equals
1
if
j-1-m_{n}\leqslant k
, and
0
otherwise.
Applying the Kahane-Khintchine inequality again, we obtain, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}\biggl[\int_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{r_{n}}{\sqrt{1+m_{n}}}\mathbbm{1}_{j-1-m_{n}\leqslant k}g_{k}^{n}\Bigr\|_{H}^{2}\Big)^{q/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr]^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}\|g_{k}^{n}\|_{H}^{2}\Bigr\|_{L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O})}^{p/2}.

Combining the above estimates, we arrive at

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}\Pi_{m_{n}}g^{n}}\Big\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}

(A.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{1/p}.

For any
Z\in\ell^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(\Omega;L^{(q/2)^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})))
,
a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\langle{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2},\,Z_{j}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+1+m_{n}}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\Big\langle{\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2},\,Z_{j}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big\langle{\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2},\,\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+1+m_{n}}Z_{j}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\langle{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2},\,\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{1+m}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+1+m}\lvert{Z_{j}}\rvert}\Big\rangle,

where
\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))

and
L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(\Omega;L^{(q/2)^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}))
.
By Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

A.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\langle{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2},\,Z_{j}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{2/p}\times\lVert{Z}\rVert_{\ell^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(\Omega;L^{(q/2)^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})))}.

Invoking the duality principle then yields

\displaystyle\biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+m_{n}}\sum_{k=j-1-m_{n}\vee 0}^{j-1}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{2/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{2/p}.

In conjunction with
Eq.

A.2
, this leads to

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}\Pi_{m_{n}}g^{n}}\Big\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}\leqslant c\biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert{g_{k}^{n}}\rVert_{H}^{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{1/p}.

On the other hand, using the Kahane-Khintchine inequality gives

\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}g^{n}\Bigr\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}\geqslant c\biggl[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Bigl\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|g_{j}^{n}\|_{H}^{2}\Bigr\|_{L^{p/2}(\Omega;L^{q/2}(\mathcal{O}))}^{p/2}\biggr]^{1/p}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}\Pi_{m_{n}}g^{n}}\Big\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O})))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}\leqslant c\biggl[\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big\lVert{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r_{n}g^{n}}\Big\rVert_{\ell^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))}^{2}\biggr]^{1/2}.

Since the above generic positive constant
c
is independent of
\tau
,
and given that
N
is an arbitrary positive integer
while
(g^{n})_{n=1}^{N}
is an arbitrary sequence in

\ell_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(L^{p}(\Omega;L^{q}(\mathcal{O};H)))
,

(m_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
is an arbitrary sequence in
\mathbb{N}
,
and
(r_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
is an arbitrary sequence of independent symmetric

\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables,
it follows that
\mathcal{R}(\{\Pi_{m}\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\})

is uniformly bounded with respect to
\tau
.
This completes the proof.
∎

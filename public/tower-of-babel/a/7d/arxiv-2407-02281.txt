Abstract
Zero-error coding encompasses a variety of source and channel problems where the probability of error must be exactly zero. This condition is stricter than that of the vanishing error regime, where the error probability goes to zero as the code blocklength goes to infinity. In general, zero-error coding is an open combinatorial question. We investigate two unsolved zero-error problems: the source coding problem with side information and the channel coding problem. We focus our attention on families of independent problems for which the probability distribution decomposes into a product of probability distributions. A crucial step is the additivity property of the optimal rate, which does not always hold in the zero-error regime, unlike in the vanishing error regime. When the additivity holds, the concatenation of optimal codes is optimal. We derive a condition under which the additivity of the complementary graph entropy H ¯ \overline{H} for the AND product of graphs and for the disjoint union of graphs are equivalent. Then we establish the connection with a recent result obtained by Wigderson and Zuiddam and by Schrijver, for the zero-error capacity C 0 C_{0} . As a consequence, we provide new single-letter characterizations of H ¯ \overline{H} and C 0 C_{0} , for example when the graph is a product of perfect graphs, which is not perfect in general, and for the class of graphs obtained by the product of a perfect graph G G with the pentagon graph C 5 C_{5} . By building on Haemers result for C 0 C_{0} , we also show that the additivity of H ¯ \overline{H} does not hold for the product of the Schläfli graph with its complementary graph.

Results from the Literature and Open Problems

We investigate the source coding problem depicted in Fig.

Remark 2

(Zero-error source and channel duality at the code-level)
.

We interpret the formula in Theorem

and

represent in bits, respectively, the minimum number of colors and the maximum size of an independent set. A color class, i.e. the set of vertices to which the same color is assigned, is an independent subset of vertices. In the case where all color classes have the same size, we would need
n\cdot\log\alpha(G)
bits to describe the source sequence within its color class. Thus,
n\cdot C(G,P_{X})
can be seen as the average number of bits needed to specify the index of the source sequence in its color class. These two quantities sum up to
n\cdot H(X)
, which is the information needed to describe the source sequence with zero-error. However, this interpretation does not allow to conclude about the source and channel duality at the code level. In particular it does not guarantee that all sources are covered by a codeword, i.e. a color and an index within that color class, which is required in the source-coding problem.

We study the class of vertex-transitive graphs, where all the vertices play the same “role”. For these graphs, we show that the uniform distribution achieves the zero-error capacity.

In

V
Conclusion

We have shown the equivalences of
additivity properties between
C_{0}
,
C
, and
\overline{H}
for the AND product (
\wedge
) and for the disjoint union (
\sqcup
) of graphs, as depicted in Fig.
3
.
For zero-error problems with same characteristic graphs and capacity-achieving distributions, we show that it is optimal to code separately for independent channels if and only if it is optimal to code separately for independent sources with side-information.
We also state the following open questions:

-

As pointed out in Theorem
8
, for all capacity-achieving
distributions
of a product graph, the product of its marginals is also capacity-achieving. Are these marginals capacity-achieving for the respective graphs in the product, and conversely, if we consider the product of capacity-achieving distributions of graphs, is this distribution capacity-achieving for the product of graphs? In other words,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\star}\left(\bigwedge_{a\in\mathcal{A}}G_{a}\right)\cap\bigotimes_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Delta(\mathcal{X}_{a})\overset{?}{=}\bigotimes_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G_{a}).

(85)

Theorem
9
partially answer this question
in the sense that inclusion holds when the
additivity holds for the AND product.

-

Theorem
9
and Theorem
10
show
that the
additivity
of
C_{0}
holds if and only if the
additivity
of
C
holds
when
P_{X}
is capacity-achieving.
Can we find graphs
G
such that the
additivity
of
C(G,P_{X})
holds when
P_{X}
is capacity-achieving, but does not hold for some
P_{X}
that is not capacity-achieving? A negative answer would imply that the
additivity
of
C_{0}
is equivalent to the
additivity of
C(G,P_{X})
and
\overline{H}(G,P_{X})

for all

P_{X}
, similarly to perfect graphs.

-

In the vanishing error regime, Wyner showed in
[
74
, Sec. VI.C]
that a linear channel code can be used to construct a source code with side information, where the encoder transmits the syndrome. Similarly in Theorem
7
, the index of a source sequence can be decomposed into a color class and an index within the color class. Does such a source-channel duality hold at the code level in the zero-error regime? Can we show that the decomposition into the color classes is optimal for both zero-error problems?

-

Finally, we have seen in Corollary
3
that
\overline{H}\big(G\wedge C_{5},P_{X}\otimes P_{X_{5}}\big)

is additive
when
G
perfect. Is the non-
additivity
of
\overline{H}
tied to specific non-perfect induced subgraphs in each graph in the product? And if so, can we find a minimal
sub-family for
these graphs?

, the function
P_{X}\mapsto C(G,P_{X})
is concave. Let
P_{X},P^{\prime}_{X}\in\Delta(\mathcal{X})
and
\beta\in[0,1]
. Let
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of integers such that
\frac{b_{n}}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}\beta
.

By Lemma
5
, there exists two sequences
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that satisfy the following:

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star},\;\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n}\text{ are independent in }G^{\wedge n};

(184)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C(G,P_{X}),

\displaystyle\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}|}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C(G,P^{\prime}_{X}),

(185)

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\|T_{x^{n}}-P_{X}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}}\|T_{x^{n}}-P_{X}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(186)

Let us build a sequence of codebooks
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}}
adapted to the distribution
\beta P_{X}+(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X}
by using a time-sharing between
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}}
and
(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}}
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\doteq\mathcal{C}_{n}^{b_{n}}\times\mathcal{C}^{\prime n-b_{n}}_{n}.

(187)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n^{2}}
is independent in
G^{\wedge n^{2}}
as a product of independent sets.

The rate associated to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}|}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{n}\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|+(n-b_{n})\log|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}|}{n^{2}}

(188)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{n}}{n}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}{n}+\frac{n-b_{n}}{n}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}|}{n}

(189)

\displaystyle\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}\beta C(G,P_{X})+(1-\beta)C(G,P^{\prime}_{X});

(190)

and the types of the codewords in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n^{2}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}}\bigg\|T_{x^{n^{2}}}-\beta P_{X}-(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}

(191)

\displaystyle=\>

\displaystyle\max_{x^{nb_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\max_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}}\bigg\|\frac{nb_{n}}{n^{2}}T_{x^{nb_{n}}}+\frac{n(n-b_{n})}{n^{2}}T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\beta P_{X}-(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}

(192)

\displaystyle\leq\>

\displaystyle\max_{x^{nb_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\bigg\|\frac{b_{n}}{n}T_{x^{nb_{n}}}-\beta P_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\max_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}}\bigg\|\frac{n-b_{n}}{n}T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}-(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}

(193)

\displaystyle=\>

\displaystyle\beta\max_{x^{nb_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\bigg\|T_{x^{nb_{n}}}-P_{X}+o(1)T_{x^{nb_{n}}}\bigg\|_{\infty}+(1-\beta)

\displaystyle\times\max_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}}\bigg\|T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}-P^{\prime}_{X}+o(1)T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}\bigg\|_{\infty}

(194)

\displaystyle\leq\>

\displaystyle\beta\max_{x^{nb_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\bigg\|T_{x^{nb_{n}}}-P_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}+o(1)\bigg\|T_{x^{nb_{n}}}\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+(1-\beta)\max_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}}\bigg\|T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}-P^{\prime}_{X}\bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+o(1)\bigg\|T_{x^{\prime n(n-b_{n})}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(195)

By Lemma
5
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{n}|}{n^{2}}\leq C(G,\beta P_{X}+(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X})
, thus

\displaystyle\beta C(G,P_{X})+(1-\beta)C(G,P^{\prime}_{X})\leq C(G,\beta P_{X}+(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{X}).

(196)

The function
P_{X}\mapsto C(G,P_{X})
is concave on the convex compact set
\Delta(\mathcal{X})
, therefore its set of maximizers
\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)=\argmax_{P_{X}\in\Delta(\mathcal{X})}C(G,P_{X})
is convex. Furthermore, by Lemma
6
, the set
\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)
is nonempty and satisfies

\displaystyle\forall P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G),\;C(G,P_{X})=C_{0}(G).

(197)

Proof of Theorem
8

Let us start by showing that Theorem
8
is true when
\mathcal{A}
has two elements. Let
G=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{E})
, and
G^{\prime}=(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{E^{\prime}})
be two graphs, and let
P_{X,X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G\wedge G^{\prime})
. We will prove that
P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}}
is also capacity-achieving by building an adequate sequence of codebooks.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}^{\prime})^{n}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is an independent set in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime}),

(198)

\displaystyle\max_{(x^{n},x^{\prime n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\|T_{x^{n},x^{\prime n}}-P_{X,X^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(199)

The existence of such a sequence is given by Lemma
5
, and Proposition
6
. Let

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}_{X,X^{\prime}}\doteq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}\sum_{(x^{n},x^{\prime n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}T_{x^{n},x^{\prime n}}.

(200)

An immediate observation is that

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}_{X,X^{\prime}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}P_{X,X^{\prime}}

(201)

as a consequence of (
199
).

Let us build a sequence of codebooks with asymptotic rate
C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime})
, such that the type of their codewords converge uniformly to
P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}\doteq\mathcal{T}^{n^{3}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}})\cap\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n};

(202)

where

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\doteq\|Q^{(n)}_{X}\otimes Q^{(n)}_{X^{\prime}}-P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}+\textstyle\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{n}};

(203)

and where for all
t\leq n
, the shifted codebook
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{(t)}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\doteq

\displaystyle\Big\{\Big((x_{t},x_{t+1},...,x_{n},x_{1},...,x_{t-1}),x^{\prime n}\Big)\>\Big|\>(x^{n},x^{\prime n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}\Big\}.

(204)

By construction,
\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{n^{3}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}})
thanks to (
202
), and
\varepsilon_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0

203
) and (
201

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n^{3}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n}}\|T_{x^{n^{3}}}-P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(205)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}
is an independent set in
(G\wedge G^{\prime})^{\wedge n^{3}}
, as it is contained in the product independent set
\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}
; note that this holds because the shifted codebook
\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}
is an independent set in
(G\wedge G^{\prime})^{\wedge n}

t\leq n
.

Now let us prove that
\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}|}{n^{3}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime})
. Let us draw a codeword uniformly from
\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle C^{n^{3}}\doteq(C^{n^{2}}_{1},...,C^{n^{2}}_{n}),

(206)

where for all
t\leq n

is a random
n\times n

\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}
. We want to prove that
C^{n^{3}}\in\mathcal{T}^{n^{3}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}})

On one hand we have to determine the average type of the random variables

which are iid copies of
C^{n^{2}}=(C^{n}_{1},...,C^{n}_{n})
; where each
C^{n}_{t}
is drawn uniformly from
\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}
, and the

are mutually independent.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[T_{C^{n^{2}}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left[T_{C^{n}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t\leq n}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}|}\sum_{(x^{n},x^{\prime n})\in\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}}T_{x^{n},x^{\prime n}}

(209)

(210)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}\sum_{(x^{n},x^{\prime n})\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}T_{x^{n}}\otimes T_{x^{\prime n}}

(211)

\displaystyle=Q^{(n)}_{X}\otimes Q^{(n)}_{X^{\prime}},

(212)

where
\sigma_{t}(x^{n})=(x_{t},x_{t+1},...,x_{n},x_{1},...,x_{n-1})

209
) comes from the construction of

204

211

\displaystyle\sum_{t\leq n}T_{\sigma_{t}(x^{n}),x^{\prime n}}=\sum_{t\leq n}\sum_{s\leq n}T_{x_{s+t},x^{\prime}_{s}}=\sum_{s\leq n}\sum_{t\leq n}T_{x_{s+t},x^{\prime}_{s}}

(214)

where the index
s+t
is taken modulo
n
.

On the other hand we have

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}|}{\left|\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}\right|}

(215)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\mathcal{T}^{n^{3}}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}})\cap\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}\right|}{\left|\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}\right|}

(216)

(221)

(222)

(223)

where (
217
) and (
218
) come from the construction of
C^{n^{3}}
; (
220
) comes from the construction of
\varepsilon_{n}
; (
221

222

212

223

, as the random variables
T_{C_{t}^{n^{2}}}(x,x^{\prime})
are iid and takes values in
[0,1]
. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}|}{n^{3}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\left|\left(\textstyle\prod_{t\leq n}\mathcal{C}^{(t)}_{n}\right)^{n}\right|}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}{n}=C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime});

where the second equality holds as the shifted codebooks

5

\displaystyle C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}^{\star}_{n^{3}}|}{n^{3}}\leq C(G\wedge G^{\prime},P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}})

\displaystyle C_{0}(G\wedge G^{\prime}),

(225)

hence

.

Therefore, Theorem

is proved when
\mathcal{A}
has two elements:

\displaystyle P_{X,X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G\wedge G^{\prime})\Longrightarrow P_{X}\otimes P_{X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G\wedge G^{\prime}).

(226)

\mathcal{A}
has a cardinality greater than 2. Let

. By considering the product graphs

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge_{a\in\mathcal{A}}G_{a}=\Big(\bigwedge_{1\leq i<i^{\star}}G_{i}\Big)\wedge\Big(\bigwedge_{i^{\star}\leq i\leq|\mathcal{A}|}G_{i}\Big);

for all

Proof of Proposition

Let
G
be a vertex-transitive graph, and let
P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)
. Let
\psi\in\Aut(G)
, we first prove that
P_{\psi(X)}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)

\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)

Let
(\mathcal{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}}
be a sequence such that

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star},\;\mathcal{C}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{n}\text{ is an independent set in }G^{\wedge n},

(231)

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}}\|T_{x^{n}}-P_{X}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,

(232)

\displaystyle\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C(G,P_{X})=C_{0}(G).

(233)

5
. Note that the last equality in (
233
) comes from the assumption
P_{X}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)
.

n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}

\displaystyle\psi(\mathcal{C}_{n})\doteq\{(\psi(x_{1}),...,\psi(x_{n}))\>|\>x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}\}

G^{\wedge n}

\psi

\displaystyle\max_{x^{n}\in\psi(\mathcal{C}_{n})}\|T_{x^{n}}-P_{\psi(X)}\|_{\infty}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

Furthermore, since
\psi
is a bijection we have
|\psi(\mathcal{C}_{n})|=|\mathcal{C}_{n}|

\displaystyle\frac{\log|\psi(\mathcal{C}_{n})|}{n}=\frac{\log|\mathcal{C}_{n}|}{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}C_{0}(G).

(236)

\displaystyle P_{\psi(X)}\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G).

(237)

x\neq x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, denote by
\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\subseteq\Aut(G)
the set of automorphisms that map
x^{\prime}

x
; note that this set is nonempty thanks to the vertex-transitivity of
G
. We have for all
x\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\Aut(G)=\textstyle\bigsqcup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}.

(238)

Furthermore, for all
x\in\mathcal{X}

(\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x})_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow x}\circ\psi_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{\psi\in\Aut(G)}P_{\psi(X)}\qquad\in\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)

(241)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{\psi\in\Aut(G)}P_{X}(\psi^{-1}(x))\bigg)_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

(242)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}|\mathcal{S}_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}|P_{X}(x^{\prime})\bigg)_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

(243)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{|\Aut(G)|}{|\mathcal{X}|}P_{X}(x^{\prime})\bigg)_{x\in\mathcal{X}}

(244)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\Unif(\mathcal{X});

(245)

where (
241
) comes from the convexity of
\mathcal{P}^{\star}(G)
given by Proposition
6
and (
237
); (
243
) comes from (
238

244

7

Let
(w_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}
,
and maximize

\displaystyle\zeta:P_{A}\mapsto H(P_{A})+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{A}(a)w_{a}.

(246)

It can be easily observed that
\zeta
is strictly concave, hence the existence and uniqueness of the maximum. We have

\displaystyle\nabla\zeta(P_{A})=\left(-\log P_{A}(a)-\frac{1}{\ln 2}+w_{a}\right)_{a\in\mathcal{A}},

hence

C^{\prime}
can be deduced from the fact that

is the normalization constant

and we have

\displaystyle\zeta(P^{\star}_{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\ P^{\star}_{A}(a)\left(\log\left(\frac{\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}2^{w_{a^{\prime}}}}{2^{w_{a}}}\right)+w_{a}\right)

(252)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}}2^{w_{a^{\prime}}}\right).

Aline Roumy

is a Research Director at the Inria Center at Rennes University, France, where she leads a research group on data compression.

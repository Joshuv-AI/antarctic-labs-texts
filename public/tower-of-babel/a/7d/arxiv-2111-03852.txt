1.
Introduction

Given
0\leq\alpha<n
and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cap\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n},+\infty\right)
, we consider the operator
T_{\alpha,m}
defined by

(1)

T_{\alpha,m}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-A_{1}y|^{-\alpha_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot|x-A_{m}y|^{-\alpha_{m}}f(y)\,dy,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where the
\alpha_{j}

\alpha_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\alpha_{m}=n-\alpha
and the
A_{j}
’s are

n\times n
invertible matrices. For the case
\alpha=0
, we require that the matrices
A_{j}
satisfy the following additional condition:

A_{i}-A_{j}
are invertible for
i\neq j
,
1\leq i,j\leq m
.

The germ of this operator appears in
[
11
]

L^{p}(\mathbb{H}_{1})

1<p\leq+\infty

\mathbb{H}_{1}

boundedness of the operator

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x-y|^{\alpha-1}|(\beta-1)x-\beta y|^{-\alpha}f(y)\,dy,

for
\beta\neq 0,1
and
0<\alpha<1
. A generalization of this operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was studied by T. Godoy and M. Urciuolo in

[
3

If
0<\alpha<n
and
m\geq 1
, then the operator
T_{\alpha,m}
has the same behavior that the Riesz potential on

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this pointwise inequality implies that
T_{\alpha,m}
is a bounded operator from

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\frac{n}{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and it is of type weak
(1,n/n-\alpha)
. We observe that if

for all

T_{\alpha,m}
is the Riesz potential

I_{\alpha}

and
m\geq 1

is a kind of generalization of the Riesz potential.

It is well known that the Riesz potential

2.
Preliminaries

A weight is a non-negative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that takes values in
(0,\infty)
almost everywhere, i.e. : the weights are allowed
to be zero or infinity only on a set of Lebesgue measure zero.

Given a weight
w
and
0<p<\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the spaces of all functions
f
satisfying
\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx<\infty
. When
p=\infty
, we have that
L^{\infty}_{w}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{w}}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
. If
E
is a measurable set, we use the notation
w(E)=\int_{E}w(x)dx
.

Let
f
be a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The function

M(f)(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|dy,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
containing
x
, is called the uncentered Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
f
.

We say that a weight
w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
if there exists
C>0
such that

M(w)(x)\leq Cw(x),\,\,\,\,\,a.e.\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

the best possible constant is denoted by
[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
. Equivalently, a weight
w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
if there exists
C>0
such that for every ball
B

(4)

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\leq C\,ess\inf_{x\in B}w(x).

Remark 3
.

If
w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
0<r<1
, then by Hölder inequality we have that
w^{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

For
1<p<\infty
, we say that a weight
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
if there exists
C>0
such that for every ball
B

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}[w(x)]^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dx\right)^{p-1}\leq C.

It is well known that
\mathcal{A}_{p_{1}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{p_{2}}
for all
1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
. Also, if
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
, then there exists
1<q<p
such that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
. We denote by
\widetilde{q}_{w}=\inf\{q>1:w\in\mathcal{A}_{q}\}

the critical index of

.

Lemma 14
.

For
0\leq\alpha<n
and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cap(1-\frac{\alpha}{n},+\infty)
, let
T_{\alpha,m}
be the operator   defined by

(\ref{Talfa})
, where the

’s are
n\times n
invertible matrices. If

|T_{\alpha,m}a(x)|\lesssim[w(B)]^{-1/p}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\chi_{R_{k}}(x)\left(M_{\frac{\alpha n}{n+d+1}}\left(\chi_{B}\right)(A_{k}^{-1}x)\right)^{\frac{n+d+1}{n}},\,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,x\in R.

Proof.

We denote
k(x,y)=\left|x-A_{1}y\right|^{-\alpha_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\left|x-A_{m}y\right|^{-\alpha_{m}}.
In view of the moment condition of
a(.)
we have

T_{\alpha,m}a(x)=\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}k(x,y)a(y)dy=\int\limits_{B(x_{0},r)}\left(k(x,y)-q_{d}\left(x,y\right)\right)a(y)dy,\,\,\,(x\in R),

where
q_{d}
is the degree
d
Taylor polynomial of the function
y\rightarrow k(x,y)
expanded around
x_{0}
. By the standard estimate of the
remainder term of the Taylor expansion, there exists
\xi
between
y
and

x_{0}
such that

\left|k(x,y)-q_{d}\left(x,y\right)\right|\lesssim\left|y-x_{0}\right|^{d+1}\sum\limits_{k_{1}+...+k_{n}=d+1}\left|\frac{\partial^{d+1}}{\partial y_{1}^{k_{1}}...\partial y_{n}^{k_{n}}}k(x,\xi)\right|

\leq C\left|y-x_{0}\right|^{d+1}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|x-A_{i}\xi\right|^{-\alpha_{i}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{m}\left|x-A_{l}\xi\right|^{-1}\right)^{d+1}.

Now, we decompose
R=\bigcup_{k=1}^{m}R_{k}
where

R_{k}=\{x\in R:|x-A_{k}x_{0}|\leq|x-A_{i}x_{0}|\,\,for\,\,all\,\,i\neq k\}.

x\in R
then

, since
\xi\in B
it follows that
|A_{i}x_{0}-A_{i}\xi|\leq Mr\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-A_{i}x_{0}|

If
x\in R
, then
x\in R_{k}
for some
k
and since
\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{m}=n-\alpha
we obtain

\left|k(x,y)-q_{d}\left(x,y\right)\right|\leq C\left|y-x_{0}\right|^{d+1}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|x-A_{i}x_{0}\right|^{-\alpha_{i}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{m}\left|x-A_{l}x_{0}\right|^{-1}\right)^{d+1}

\leq Cr^{d+1}\left|x-A_{k}x_{0}\right|^{-n+\alpha-d-1},

this inequality allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\left|T_{\alpha,m}a(x)\right|\leq C\left\|a\right\|_{1}r^{d+1}\left|x-A_{k}x_{0}\right|^{-n+\alpha-d-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left|B\right|^{1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left\|a\right\|_{p_{0}}r^{d+1}\left|x-A_{k}x_{0}\right|^{-n+\alpha-d-1},

since

, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\left|T_{\alpha,m}a(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{r^{n+d+1}\left|x-A_{k}x_{0}\right|^{-n+\alpha-d-1}}{[w(B)]^{1/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left(M_{\frac{\alpha n}{n+d+1}}\left(\chi_{B}\right)(A_{k}^{-1}x)\right)^{\frac{n+d+1}{n}}}{[w(B)]^{1/p}},\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,x\in R_{k}.

This completes the proof.

Yangians, Mirabolic Subalgebras, and Whittaker Vectors
Artem KALMYKOV ab

Abstract
We construct an element in a completion of the universal enveloping algebra of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{gl}_{N} , which we call the Kirillov projector, that connects the topics of the title: on the one hand, it is defined using the evaluation homomorphism from the Yangian of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{gl}_{N} , on the other hand, it gives a canonical projection onto the space of Whittaker vectors for any Whittaker module over the mirabolic subalgebra. Using the Kirillov projector, we deduce some categorical properties of Whittaker modules, for instance, we prove a mirabolic analog of Kostant’s theorem. We also show that it quantizes a rational version of the Cremmer–Gervais r r -matrix. As application, we construct a universal vertex-IRF transformation from the standard dynamical R R -matrix to this constant one in categorical terms.

Introduction

Solutions to the
quantum Yang–Baxter equation
(QYBE for short)

R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}
,
the so-called
quantum
R
-matrices
, have many applications in mathematical physics and theory of quantum groups. The most well-studied example is the
standard solution
, associated to any reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, that leads to Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups
[
15
,
28
]
.

The starting point for the paper is a particular non-standard solution in type A which is called the
Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix
. Originally, it appeared in
[
12
]
and was later studied, for instance, in
[
9
,
27
]
. This solution is special among other non-standard solutions in at least two ways (which might be connected to each other).

First, it is related to the so-called
vertex-IRF transformation
. Namely, there is another important equation in mathematical physics, which is a generalization of the QYBE, called
quantum dynamical Yang–Baxter equation

R_{12}(\lambda)R_{13}(\lambda+h_{2})R_{23}(\lambda)=R_{23}(\lambda+h_{1})R_{13}(\lambda)R_{12}(\lambda+h_{3}),

where
\lambda
is a parameter on the dual space
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
to an abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and
h_{i}
are weight operators, see Definition
2.8
. As explained by Felder
[
22
]
, it is closely related to the star-triangle relation for face-type statistical models
[
5
]
. Moreover, it naturally appears in the description of the Liouville and Toda field theories
[
25
]
. As in the non-dynamical case, it leads to the theory of
dynamical quantum groups
, and for any reductive
\mathfrak{g}
, there are canonically defined standard solutions, see
[
17
,
19
]
. It turns out that in type A, it is possible to gauge the standard dynamical
R
-matrix to a constant one which is exactly the Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix (in fact, this is how it was discovered in
[
12
]
). What is even more interesting is that such kind of gauge transformations (which are called
vertex-IRF
transformations) is unique, that is, it exists only in type A and the only constant
R
-matrix we can get is the Cremmer–Gervais one, see
[
4
]
. So, it is natural to ask: what is a representation-theoretic explanation of this fact? Or, at least, what is a representation-theoretic meaning of
the
vertex-IRF transformation?

This brings to the second special feature of the Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix: its relation to W-algebras. In principle, the exchange algebra of the Toda field theory (which is related to affine W-algebras) is “controlled” by the standard dynamical
R
-matrix
[
25
]
, but, as we mentioned before, it was shown in
[
12
]
that is possible to gauge away the dependence on the dynamical parameter. Likewise, it was observed in
[
10
]
that the Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix can be constructed using the
Sevostyanov characters

[
42
]
which are used to define a
q
-analog of the principal finite W-algebra. So, one may ask: what is a direct relation between the Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix and W-algebras? This paper is an attempt to answer these questions in a somewhat simpler situation of the rational analog of the Cremmer–Gervais solution.

Let us recall Konstant’s construction of the finite principal W-algebra
[
36
]
. Let
G
be a simple Lie group with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Choose a character
\psi\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{*}
of the negative nilpotent subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}_{-}\subset\mathfrak{g}
taking nonzero values on simple root generators and denote by
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}=\{x-\psi(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}\}
the shift of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
along
\psi
in the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
. Its quotient by the left action
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
has a right
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-action; let
\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})\bigr)^{N_{-}}
be the space of
Whittaker vectors
, i.e., the elements on which
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
acts with character
\psi
. It is an example of a quantum Hamiltonian reduction, and as such, it has a natural product. By the result of loc. cit., this algebra is isomorphic to the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
.

There is a categorical analog of this construction involving the category
\mathrm{HC}(G)
of
Harish-Chandra bimodules
. Those are
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules with certain
G
-integrability condition, in particular,
\mathrm{HC}(G)
is a monoidal category. A similar assignment
X\mapsto\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\bigr)^{N_{-}}
for
X\in\mathrm{HC}(G)
can be enhanced to a functor to the category of
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}}
-bimodules that has a natural “multiplicative” (lax monoidal) structure analogous to the standard product construction on quantum Hamiltonian reductions

\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\bigr)^{N_{-}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{g}}\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash Y\bigr)^{N_{-}}\rightarrow\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\otimes_{\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})}Y\bigr)^{N_{-}},\qquad[x]\otimes[y]\mapsto[x\otimes y].

This is the functor
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\psi}
of the
Kostant–Whittaker reduction
studied in
[
7
]
. By the results of loc. cit., the lax monoidal maps above are in fact isomorphisms.

There is a version for the trivial character where one can consider the space
(X/\mathfrak{n})^{N}
of the invariant vectors under the left action of
\mathfrak{n}
in the quotient
X/\mathfrak{n}
. In particular, for
X=\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, the result is isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h})
of the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of the maximal torus
H\subset G
. According to
[
30
]
, this assignment can be enhanced to a functor
{\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathrm{HC}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}(H)}
of
parabolic restriction
which, upon extending the scalars to the fraction field of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h})
(that we ignore in the introduction), also becomes monoidal.

In fact, there is more to the latter construction. For any
G
-representation
V
, the free right
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
can be endowed with a left action making it an object of
\mathrm{HC}(G)
. This assignment can be upgraded to a monoidal functor
\mathrm{free}\colon\Rep(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}(G)
of
free
Harish-Chandra bimodules. According to the classical results of
[
44
]
and
[
21
]
, the parabolic restriction functor “trivializes” on such bimodules, in other words, there is a natural isomorphism
{\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{free}(V))\cong V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h})}
after extending the scalars. By loc. cit., the image of the braiding on
\Rep(G)
under the monoidal isomorphisms of
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}
gives a solution to the QDYBE.

The goal of this paper is to adapt this construction to the Whittaker setting for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
. The only missing ingredient is a trivialization isomorphism
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}^{\psi}(\mathrm{free}(V))\cong V\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. To define it, we follow the approach of
[
44
]
that uses the so-called
extremal projector
in the setting of parabolic restriction introduced in
[
3
]
. It is an element
P
lying in a certain completion of
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
localized by rational functions on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
; in particular, its action is well defined only for those
\mathfrak{n}
-integrable
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules whose weights are generic. For such modules, it projects to
\mathfrak{n}
-invariants along the image of the action of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
. In particular, an isomorphism
V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h})\rightarrow\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{free}(V))
can be constructed by applying
P
to
v\otimes 1
in
V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{n}
(again, upon extending the scalars).

The main result of the paper is the construction of an analogous element
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
depending on a vector of parameters
\vec{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N-1})
that we call the
Kirillov projector
as it bears some resemblance to the Kirillov model from the theory of
p
-adic groups
[
34
]
, see Definition
5.2
. For instance, it is defined not just for
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, but for the so-called
mirabolic
subalgebra
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
of the mirabolic subgroup
\mathrm{M}_{N}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}
of matrices preserving the last basis vector in
\mathbf{C}^{N}
. Theorem
5.3
is the main theorem of the paper, here
E_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
are the standard matrix unit generators.

Theorem A
.

For any right Whittaker module over
\mathfrak{m}_{N}
, the Kirillov projector defines a unique linear operator satisfying

\displaystyle P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})^{2}=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}),\qquad P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})\cdot(E_{ij}-\delta_{i,j+1})=0,\qquad 1\leq j<i\leq N,

\displaystyle(E_{ij}+\delta_{ij}u_{j})\cdot P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad 1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1,

and acting by identity on the space of Whittaker vectors.

Let us indicate main features of the construction. First, contrary to the case of the extremal projector whose action requires certain genericity assumption on the weights, the Kirillov projector does not involve any denominators, thus has a well-defined action on
any
right Whittaker module. Second, it is constructed inductively using a sequence of mirabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{m}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{2}\subset\dots\subset\mathfrak{m}_{N}
embedded via lower right corner, at each step projecting to the space of Whittaker vectors for the corresponding
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
(and this was another motivation for the name). Third, its construction involves the evaluation homomorphism to
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
from the
Yangian

\mathrm{Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
. More precisely, at each step, the Kirillov projector is defined using certain
quantum minors
, and the proof of Theorem
A
uses extensively their properties expounded in
[
39
]
. Unfortunately, as we mentioned, the evaluation homomorphism is crucial for the proofs, and we do not know if there is a purely Yangian analog of the Kirillov projector.

Recall that by Kostant’s result
[
36
]
, the category
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathfrak{g})
of
Whittaker modules
, i.e., the
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules on which
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}
acts locally nilpotently, is equivalent to the category of
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules. Moreover, the following
translation
property holds: for any
G
-representation
V
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})\bigr)^{N_{-}}\cong V\otimes\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})\bigr)^{N_{-}}=V\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{g}.

With the help of the Kirillov projector, we show a mirabolic analog of this result for the category
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})
of (right) Whittaker modules over the mirabolic subalgebra, see Theorem
6.1
.

Theorem B
.

The functor
(-)^{N_{-}}\colon\mathrm{Wh}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}
of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}
-invariants is an equivalence of categories such that for any representation
V
of the mirabolic subgroup
\mathrm{M}_{N}
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash V\otimes\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{N})\bigr)^{N_{-}}\cong V
.

In particular, the natural tensor structure on the mirabolic analog
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}
of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction discussed above induces a tensor structure on the composition

\Rep(\mathrm{M}_{N})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{free}}\mathrm{HC}(\mathrm{M}_{N})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}}\mathrm{Vect},

which, in particular, produces a solution to the QYBE. We show that, up to a small difference between the mirabolic subgroup in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and the maximal parabolic subgroup of matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{N}
preserving the linear span of the last basis vector, this construction produces a deformation of the rational analog of the Cremmer–Gervais solution as in
[
16
]
. Extending this construction to
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, the other main result of the paper can be summarized as follows, see Theorem
6.17
and Section
6.3
.

Theorem C
.

For every vector of parameter
\vec{u}
, upon trivialization induced by the Kirillov projector
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})
, the monoidal structure on the functor
\Rep(\mathrm{GL}_{N})\rightarrow{}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{BiMod}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}
of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction restricted to free Harish-Chandra bimodules produces a constant solution
R^{\mathrm{CG}}(\vec{u})
to the QYBE such that for
\vec{u}=(N-1,\dots,1)
, it coincides with the rational analog of the Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrix.

More precisely, for
\vec{u}=(N-1,\dots,1)
, we show that its semiclassical limit is the inverse of trace pairing with the
principal nilpotent element

\mathfrak{m}_{N}\wedge\mathfrak{m}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbf{C},\qquad x\wedge y\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(e\cdot[x,y]),\qquad e:=E_{12}+\dots+E_{N-1,N},

that coincides (up to the aforementioned difference) with the degeneration of the classical Cremmer–Gervais
r
-matrix by the results of
[
23
]
. Moreover, in Section
6.3
we compute explicitly the quantum
R
-matrix for the dual of the vector representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and show that it also coincides with the rational Cremmer–Gervais solution, now on the quantum level
[
16
]
. Surprisingly, this calculation is essentially based on the action of the
degenerate affine Hecke algebra
on certain Harish-Chandra bimodules, for instance, the resulting
R
-matrix can be simply interpreted as the action of the permutation operator on its polynomial representation.

For an arbitrary vector of parameters, this construction produces a family of constant solutions to QYBE that we call
h
-deformed rational Cremmer–Gervais
R
-matrices, see Definition
6.8
. Its semiclassical limit is obtained by replacing
e
with
e-h
, where
h=u_{1}E_{11}+\dots+u_{N-1}E_{N-1,N-1}
, in the definition above.

Finally, as application, we construct a categorical version of the vertex-IRF transformation. It turns out that it can be interpreted via a finite analog of the
Miura transform

[
26
]
: using the Harish-Chandra homomorphism
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
, one can construct the maps

\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\bigr)^{N_{-}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{g}}}\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{h})\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n}\leftarrow(X/\mathfrak{n})^{N},

that become isomorphisms after extending the scalars to the rational functions on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Moreover, the resulting map
\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{g}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{g}}(X)
is compatible with the tensor structure. In particular, for
{G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}}
, we have the following result, see Theorems
7.8
and
7.13
.

Theorem D
.

The natural transformation between the functor of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction and the parabolic restriction induces a gauge transformation between the standard solution of the QDYBE and the
h
-deformed rational Cremmer–Gervais solution.

We compute this gauge transformation for the special vector of parameters
\vec{u}=(N-1,\dots,1)
and for the dual of the vector representation. In accordance with the
earlier constructions
[
4
,
9
,
12
]
, it turns out that, up to a diagonal factor, it is given by the Vandermonde matrix.

We expect that there should be a
q
-deformation of the constructions of the paper that would produce the actual Cremmer–Gervais solution. Indeed, there is a notion of quantum Harish-Chandra bimodules (for instance, see
[
30
, Section 2.5]
and references therein) that involves the quantum group
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
as well as a generalization of the parabolic restriction. We expect that, similarly to Sevostyanov’s construction
[
42
]
of the center of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
via a
q
-analog of the Whittaker reduction, there should be a
q
-analog of the Kostant–Whittaker reduction functor as well as a
q
-analog of the Kirillov projector that uses the evaluation homomorphism from the quantum affine algebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{N}\bigr)
. Then the tensor structure on the former under the trivialization of the latter should produce the original Cremmer–Gervais solution.

The properties of the Kirillov projector were predicted using SageMath
[
41
]
. The author’s code that implements the action of the projector on certain Harish-Chandra bimodules is available here:
https://github.com/art-kalm/whittaker
.

Structure of the paper.
In Section
2
, we give a general setup for the paper and review the categorical approach to (standard) dynamical quantum groups via the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules and parabolic restriction functor as in
[
30
]
. Proofs are mostly omitted unless there is a version that we need later in the paper and it is different from loc. cit. In Section
3
, we define the mirabolic subgroup and a family of
r
-matrices that deform the degenerate version of the Cremmer–Gervais
r
-matrix. In Section
4
, we review Yangians and related constructions such as quantum minors. We prove various technical results that we use later in the paper. In Section
5
, we introduce the Kirillov projector and prove its defining properties. In Section
6
, we recall the Kostant–Whittaker reduction functor, introduce its mirabolic version, and show some properties thereof. We also obtain quantization of the family of rational Cremmer–Gervais solutions. In Section
7
, we define a categorical version of the vertex-IRF transformation and show that an equivalence between parabolic restriction and Kostant–Whittaker reduction gives the vertex-IRF transformation in the classical sense.

All the objects and constructions in the paper involve the asymptotic parameter
\hbar
; to simplify the exposition, we have set
\hbar=1
in the introduction.

2
Background: dynamical quantum groups

In this section, we will give a brief overview of the classical extremal projector and its relation to dynamical quantum groups through the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules.

2.1
General setup

For the rest of the paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{C}
of characteristic zero. All categories and functors we will consider are
\mathbf{C}
-linear; likewise, unless otherwise stated, all tensor products are taken over
\mathbf{C}
. Often, we will use the algebra of polynomial functions
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
in variable
\hbar
and denote by
{\mathrm{Mod}}_{\mathbf{C}[\hbar]}
the corresponding monoidal category of
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
-modules with the standard tensor product
\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\hbar]}
over
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
. For any vector space
V
over
\mathbf{C}
, we denote by
{V[\hbar]:=V\otimes_{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{C}[\hbar]}
.

Throughout this paper, we work with locally presentable categories (we refer to
[
1
]
and
[
8
, Section 2]
for more details). We will also use Deligne’s tensor product of locally presentable categories introduced in
[
13
]
.

2.2
Harish-Chandra bimodules

For details and proofs in a more general setting, we refer the reader to
[
30
, Section 2]
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic group and
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra with bracket
[\,,\,]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
asymptotic universal enveloping algebra

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a tensor algebra over
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
generated by the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with the relations

xy-yx=\hbar[x,y]
,
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The
non-asymptotic universal enveloping algebra

\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathbf{C}
-algebra generated by
\mathfrak{g}
with the relations
{xy-yx=[x,y]}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In principle, most of the cited literature deals with the non-asymptotic version
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
. However, unless specified otherwise, all the constructions and proofs can be translated
mutatis mutandis
to the asymptotic setting.

Denote by
\Rep(G)
the braided monoidal category of
G
-representations over
\mathbf{C}
, where the braiding is given by the permutation map

\sigma_{VW}\colon\ V\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes V,\qquad v\otimes w\mapsto w\otimes v.

For any
V\in\Rep(G)
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\ad_{\xi}\colon V\rightarrow V
the differential of the
G
-action along
\xi
. The adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
extends to an action on
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
, in particular, the latter is naturally an object of
\Rep(G)
such that the differential is

\ad_{\xi}(x)=\frac{\xi x-x\xi}{\hbar},\qquad\xi\in\mathfrak{g},x\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}).

Definition 2.3

(
[
7
]
)
.

An
asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodule
is a left
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
X
in the category
\Rep(G)
. In other words, it has a structure of a
G
-representation and a left
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-module such that the action morphism

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes X\rightarrow X

is a homomorphism of
G
-representations. The category of Harish-Chandra bimodules is denoted by
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)
.

In what follows, we will refer to asymptotic Harish-Chandra bimodules as simply Harish-Chandra bimodules.

There is a natural right
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-module structure on any Harish-Chandra bimodule
X
. Namely, for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, define

x\xi:=\xi x-\hbar\ad_{\xi}(x),\qquad x\in X,

(2.1)

and extend it to a right
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-action. Therefore, the category
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)
is a subcategory of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-bimodules, hence is equipped with a tensor structure

X\otimes^{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)}Y:=X\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}Y
.

Remark 2.4
.

The subcategory of
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)
where
\hbar
acts by identity is identified with the standard category of Harish-Chandra bimodules
[
6
, Definition 5.2]
. The one where
\hbar
acts by zero can be identified with the category of
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

There is a natural functor of the so-called
free
(
right
)
Harish-Chandra bimodules

\mathrm{free}\colon\Rep(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G),\qquad V\mapsto V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}),

such that the
right

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-action is free and the
G
-action is diagonal. By
[
30
, equation (28)]
, it is isomorphic to the functor of free
left
Harish-Chandra bimodules
V\mapsto\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes V
; however, in what follows, we will use the right version. One can check that this functor is monoidal. In fact, all Harish-Chandra bimodules can be “constructed” from the free ones.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
30
, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

The category
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)
is generated by
\mathrm{free}(V)
for
V\in\Rep(G)
.

2.3
Constant and dynamical
\boldsymbol{R}
-matrices

For introduction to the theory of dynamical quantum groups, see
[
19
]
; for the interpretation in terms of the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules, see
[
30
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

•

Let
F_{VW}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbf{C}[\hbar]}(V[\hbar]\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\hbar]}W[\hbar])
be a collection of invertible
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
-linear maps natural in
V,W\in\Rep(G)
. The
twist
equation is

F_{U\otimes V,W}\circ(F_{UV}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})=F_{U,V\otimes W}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes F_{VW}),

Remark 2.9
.

In particular, a quantum

It turns out that there is a Tannakian interpretation of dynamical
R
-matrices in terms of functors to the category of Harish-Chandra bimodules. Namely, consider the category
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)
of Harish-Chandra bimodules over

. Since
\mathfrak{h}
is commutative, the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
is equal to the algebra of polynomial functions
\mathbf{C}\bigl[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr]
with a trivial
H
-action, where
\mathbb{A}^{1}
corresponds to
\hbar
. Representations of
H
are completely reducible with irreducibles being one-dimensional parameterized by weights

; in particular, as a plain category,

\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\mu\in\Lambda(H)}\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}[\mu],

where
\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}
is the category of right

-modules and

simply indicates the corresponding graded component. For

, the left
\mathbf{C}\bigl[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr]
-action is

f(\lambda)\otimes x\mapsto xf(\lambda+\hbar\mu).

(2.8)

For every graded component, the tensor product is

\displaystyle\otimes^{\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)}\colon\ \mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}[\mu]\otimes\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}[\nu]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}[\mu+\nu],

\displaystyle(X[\mu],Y[\nu])

\displaystyle\mapsto(X\otimes_{\mathbf{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}]}Y)[\mu+\nu].

(2.9)

In what follows, we will need a
generic
version of this category. Denote by

\bigl(\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr)^{\mathrm{gen}}=\bigl\{(\lambda,\hbar)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\mid\bigl\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\bigr\rangle\not\in\hbar\mathbb{Z},\,\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\bigr\}

the set of generic weights, where

is the set of positive roots, and let
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\subset\mathrm{Frac}\bigl(\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr)\bigr)
be the algebra of rational functions regular on
\bigl(\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\bigr)^{\mathrm{gen}}
. Define

(2.11)

with the left action and the tensor product given by obvious generalization of formulas (
2.8
) and (

), respectively. Similarly to non-generic version, there is a functor of free Harish-Chandra bimodules

,
such that if

4.1
General definition and properties

The
(

)
Yangian

\mathrm{Y}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
is an associative algebra generated over
\mathbf{C}[\hbar]
by the elements

, where
1\leq i,j\leq N
and

. In what follows, we also define
t_{ij}^{(0)}:=\delta_{ij}
. Introduce the formal Laurent series in

t_{ij}(u):=\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}t_{ij}^{(r)}u^{-r}.

We combine them into a generating matrix

T(u):=(t_{ij}(u))\in\mathrm{Y}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)\bigl[\bigl[u^{-1}\bigr]\bigr].

Let
P\in\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)
be the permutation matrix. Consider the
Yang
R
-matrix

R(u-v)=\mathrm{id}-\frac{\hbar P}{u-v}\in\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{N}\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr).

Then the defining relation of
\mathrm{Y}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
can be presented in the form

R(u-v)T_{1}(u)T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)T_{1}(u)R(u-v),

where the equality takes place in
\mathrm{Y}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\otimes\mathrm{End}\bigl(\mathbf{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbf{C}^{N}\bigr)
and
T_{1}(u):=T(u)\otimes\mathrm{id}
,
T_{2}(v)=\mathrm{id}\otimes T(v)
. Equivalently, it is given by

(u-v)[t_{ij}(u),t_{kl}(v)]=\hbar\frac{t_{kj}(u)t_{il}(v)-t_{kj}(v)t_{il}(u)}{u-v}.

(4.1)

Consider the following
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})

E=\begin{pmatrix}E_{11}&\dots&E_{1,n-1}&E_{1N}\\

E_{N1}&\dots&E_{N,N-1}&E_{NN}\end{pmatrix}.

One can define a homomorphism
\mathrm{Y}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
, called the

, by
{T(u)\mapsto\mathrm{id}+u^{-1}E}
.

Under the evaluation homomorphism, we will be mainly dealing with a slightly different version of the
T
-matrix. Namely, define
{L(u)=(L_{ij}(u)):=u\cdot\mathrm{id}+E}
.
Obviously, we have

. Almost all the properties of

that we use in the paper are satisfied

by

; we will explicitly indicate the cases where it is not true or not trivial.

\displaystyle={}

Similarly, we can define a quantum minor for

\displaystyle L_{b_{1}\dots b_{m}}^{a_{1}\dots a_{m}}(u):={}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\sigma}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)L_{a_{1}b_{\sigma(1)}}(u-m\hbar+\hbar)\cdots L_{a_{m-1}b_{\sigma(m-1)}}(u-\hbar)L_{a_{m}b_{\sigma(m)}}(u).

(4.2)

Observe that

(4.3)

(1)

where we used

Lemma 5.9
.

For
c\leq d\leq b<a
and any
k\geq 0
, we have

\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})\bigl(L_{1\dots c}^{a,2,\dots,c}(v)+(-1)^{c}\psi(E_{ac})\bigr)=\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}\frac{L_{1\dots c}^{1\dots c}(v)-L_{1\dots c}^{1\dots c}(u)}{v-u}.

In particular,

\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})\partial_{u}^{k}L_{1\dots c}^{a,2,\dots,c}(u)+(-1)^{c}\delta_{k,0}\psi(E_{ac})\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})=\frac{1}{k+1}\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}\bigl(\partial_{u}^{k+1}L_{1\dots c}^{1\dots c}(u)\bigr),

where by
\partial_{u}
we mean the derivative with respect to
u

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
,

L_{1\dots c}^{a,2,\dots,c}(v)=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{c}(-1)^{l-1}E_{al}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}^{2\dots c}(v).

Since
E_{al}=E_{al}^{\psi}+\psi(E_{al})
and
\psi(E_{al})=0
except for, possibly,
l=c=a-1
, we can rewrite the sum as

\sum\limits_{l=1}^{c-1}(-1)^{l-1}E^{\psi}_{al}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}^{2\dots c}(v)+(-1)^{c-1}\psi(E_{ac})L_{1\dots c-1}^{2\dots c}(v).

Therefore, by Lemma
5.8
,

\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})L_{1\dots c}^{a,2,\dots,c}(v)=\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{c}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u)L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}^{2\dots c}(v)+(-1)^{c-1}\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})\psi(E_{ac})L_{1\dots c-1}^{2\dots c}(v).

Observe that
[E_{a1},E_{ij}]=0
for any
c\geq i>j
. By Proposition
4.8
, the classes of
L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u)
and
L_{1\dots c-1}^{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}(u)
in the left quotient by the shift
\mathrm{span}(E_{ij}-\psi(E_{ij})\mid c\geq i>j\geq 1)
are equal for every
l
; therefore, by Proposition
5.7
,

\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u)=\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}L_{1\dots c-1}^{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}(u)

and the right-hand side is equal to

\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u})E_{a1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{c}L_{1\dots c-1}^{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}(u)L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}^{2\dots c}(v)+(-1)^{c-1}\psi(E_{ac})\mathcal{P}_{d}(\vec{u}).

Recall Proposition

\sum\limits_{l=1}^{c}L_{1\dots c-1}^{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}(u)L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots c}^{2\dots c}(v)=\frac{L_{1\dots c-1}^{2\dots c}(v)L_{1\dots c}^{1\dots c}(u)-L_{1\dots c-1}^{2\dots c}(u)L_{1\dots c}^{1\dots c}(v)}{u-v}.

The second part of the lemma follows by setting

∎

Then we continue the process by pushing the derivative to the right. For instance,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)-k\hbar(-E_{b+1,1})\partial_{u}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)-k\hbar(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}\partial_{u}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(k\hbar)^{2}\partial_{u}L_{1,2,\dots,l}^{b+1,2,\dots,l}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)-(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}k\hbar\partial_{u}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}+\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(k\hbar)^{2}(-E_{b+1,1})\frac{\partial_{u}^{2}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)}{2}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}.

In the end, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-k\hbar)^{j}}{j!}\partial_{u}^{j}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots l}^{1\dots l}(u-k\hbar),

where the sum is finite.
∎

Therefore,

\displaystyle A={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})E_{b+1,b}^{\psi}P_{b+1,1}^{\psi}(u)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\hbar^{-k}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u-k\hbar)\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{k!}.

(5.11)

Now let us study
B
of (
5.10
). Consider the term

\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1,\dots,b-1,b}^{1,\dots,b-1,b+1}(u).

By Proposition
4.1
, we have

L_{1,\dots,b-1,b}^{1,\dots,b-1,b+1}(u)=(-1)^{b+1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{b}(-1)^{l-1}E_{b+1,l}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots b}^{1\dots b-1}(u).

Recall that
E_{b+1,,b}=E_{b+1,b}^{\psi}+1
. By Lemmas
5.8
and
5.11
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1,\dots,b-1,b}^{1,\dots,b-1,b+1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{b}E_{b+1,l}^{\psi}(-1)^{b+l}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots b}^{1\dots b-1}(u)\right)+(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-1)^{b+2}(-E_{b+1,1})\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{b}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots b}^{1\dots b-1}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}+(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-1)^{b+2}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{b}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u-k\hbar)L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots b}^{1\dots b-1}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}+(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u).

By Propositions
4.8
and
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}\sum\limits_{l=1}^{b}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(u-k\hbar)L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots b}^{1\dots b-1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}\frac{L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u-k\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)-L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-k\hbar)L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u)}{k\hbar}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle B={}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}(-1)^{(b+2)k}\hbar^{-k+1}L_{1,\dots,b-1,b}^{1,\dots,b-1,b+1}(u)\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{(k-1)!}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\frac{\hbar^{-k}}{k!}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u-k\hbar)\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\frac{\hbar^{-k}}{k!}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u)\prod_{i=1}^{k}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}(-1)^{(b+2)k}\frac{\hbar^{-(k-1)}}{(k-1)!}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u)\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar).

By comparing the second and the third rows, we see that their sum is
\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})
. Likewise, recall (
5.11
) that

A=-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\hbar^{-k}L_{1\dots b-1}^{1\dots b-1}(u-k\hbar)\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{k!}.

In particular, the sum of
A
and the first row of
B
is equal to
-\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u})(-E_{b+1,1})
. The proposition follows.
∎

5.2
Kirillov projector: left
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{b}^{\vec{u}}}
-invariance

In this subsection, we will show the second property from Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem 5.13
.

The Kirillov projector satisfies

\displaystyle(E_{ii}+u_{i})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad 1\leq i\leq N-1,\qquad E_{ij}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq N-1.

We will prove this statement by induction on dimension
N
. The base case is
N=2
.

Proposition 5.14
.

The Kirillov projector
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{2}}^{\psi}(u_{1})
satisfies

(E_{11}+u)P_{21}^{\psi}(u)=0
.

Proof.

First, observe that

E_{21}P^{\psi}_{21}(u-\hbar)=P_{21}(u).

(5.12)

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle P_{21}^{\psi}(u)E_{21}={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k}\hbar^{-k}\frac{L_{1}^{1}(u)\cdots L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar k+\hbar)}{k!}E_{21}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle E_{21}\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k}\hbar^{-k}\frac{L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar)\cdots L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar k)}{k!}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle E_{21}P_{21}^{\psi}(u-\hbar).

But by Theorem
5.13
, we have
P_{21}^{\psi}(u)E_{21}=P_{21}^{\psi}(u)
.

Then, we will use the following commutation relation:

\bigl[E_{11},\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k}\bigr]=-k\hbar E_{21}\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(E_{11}+u)P_{21}^{\psi}={}

\displaystyle E_{11}\sum\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k}\hbar^{-k}\frac{L_{1}^{1}(u)\cdots L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar k+\hbar)}{k!}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k}\hbar^{-k}(E_{11}+u)\frac{L_{1}^{1}(u)\cdots L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar k+\hbar)}{k!}

\displaystyle-E_{21}\sum\bigl(E_{21}^{\psi}\bigr)^{k-1}\hbar^{1-k}\frac{L_{1}^{1}(u)\cdots L_{1}^{1}(u-\hbar k+\hbar)}{(k-1)!}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle P_{21}^{\psi}(u)L_{1}^{1}(u)-E_{21}P_{21}^{\psi}(u-\hbar)L_{1}^{1}(u)=0

by (
5.12
).
∎

Now we make an induction step. Recall the notation
{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}
from (
5.6
). Assume we proved Theorem
5.13
for
P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})
. Observe that

P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1})\cdots P_{N1}^{\psi}(u_{1}).

Then the statement is reduced to

\displaystyle(E_{11}+u_{1})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad E_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad 1<k\leq N-1.

The first property follows from Proposition
5.14
, as
E_{11}
commutes with
P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})
. For the second property, let us show first an a priori weaker statement.

Proposition 5.15
.

We have

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})=0
.
In other words, the equality
E_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}(\vec{u})\allowbreak=0
holds in the left quotient by
{}_{1}\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}
.

Proof.

By induction on
N
and by Proposition
5.19
, we have

E_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{b}}(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{b-1})=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{b}}(u_{1}-\hbar,u_{2},\dots,u_{b-1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u_{1}).

We claim that nothing changes in the quotient when we pass to a larger algebra, namely,

\displaystyle P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1b}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1})\cdots P_{b1}^{\psi}(u_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1}-\hbar)\cdots P_{b1}^{\psi}(u_{1}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u_{1}).

It follows from the following technical, but in fact simple lemma.

Lemma 5.16
.

Assume that
x=\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}E_{1,b-i}\alpha_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}({}_{b-1-i}\mathfrak{gl}_{b-1})
. Then

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})xP_{b-l+1,b-l}^{\psi}(u_{b-l})\cdots P_{b,b-l}(u_{b-l})\in\sum_{i=0}^{l}E_{1,b-i}\cdot({}_{b-1-i}\mathfrak{gl}_{b-1})

and

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})xP_{b+1,b-l+1}^{\psi}(u_{b-l+1})\cdots P_{N,b-l+1}^{\psi}(u_{b-l+1})=P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})x.

Proof.

Let us only show the second property. The condition
\alpha_{i}\in({}_{b-1-i}\mathfrak{gl}_{b-1})
means that
\alpha_{i}
is the sum of products of elements of the form
E_{\alpha\beta}
with
b-l+1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq b-1
. It is clear that for any such
\alpha,\beta
, the commutator
[E_{j,b-l+1},E_{\alpha\beta}]
for
j>b
will be a nilpotent element of the form
E_{jc}
with
b-l+1\leq c\leq b-1
, in particular, the value of
\psi
on it is zero. Therefore, we have

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})\mathrm{ad}_{E_{j,b-l+1}}(x)=0
.
Since

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})x\xi^{\psi}=P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}(x)

for any
\xi
in the negative nilpotent subalgebra of
{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}
, we conclude that every
P_{j,b-l+1}^{\psi}(u_{b-l+1})
for
j>b
acts on
P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})x
by identity. Hence,

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})xP_{b+1,b-l+1}^{\psi}(u_{b-l+1})\cdots P_{N,b-l+1}^{\psi}(u_{b-l+1})(u_{b-l+1})=P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})x.

The first property follows by similar analysis of commutators and is left to the reader.
∎

Therefore, the claim of the proposition would follow from

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}^{\psi}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1}-\hbar)\dots P_{b1}^{\psi}(u_{1}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u_{1})P_{b+1,1}^{\psi}(u_{1})=0.

For brevity, denote by
\vec{u}-\hbar:=(u_{1}-\hbar,u_{2},\dots,u_{N-1})
and
u=u_{1}
. Adopting notations of Section
5.1
, it can be reformulated as

\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)P_{b+1,1}^{\psi}(u)=0.

Observe that
b\geq 2
, in particular,
E_{b+1,1}=E_{b+1,1}^{\psi}
. We need two facts. The first one: by Lemma
5.9
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u_{1})(-E_{b+1,1})

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)+\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1,2,\dots,b}^{b+1,2,\dots,b}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)+\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)\hbar E_{b+1,1}\frac{L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)-L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)}{\hbar}

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}-(-1)^{b+2}\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)-(-1)^{b+2}\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar).

The second one: by the same lemma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)+k\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1,2,\dots,b}^{b+1,2,\dots,b}(u-\hbar)(-E_{b1})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)-k\hbar(-E_{b+1,1})\partial_{u}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}-(-1)^{b+2}k\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
5.11
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-k\hbar-\hbar)-(-1)^{b+2}k\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}

Combining the two facts, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-k\hbar-\hbar)-(-1)^{b+2}(k+1)\hbar\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)P_{b+1,1}^{\psi}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)\left(1+\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\hbar^{-k-1}\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{k!}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}+\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k+1}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-k\hbar-\hbar)(-1)^{(b+2)(k+1)}\hbar^{-k-1}\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{(k+1)!}

\displaystyle\phantom{\qquad=}{}-\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}(-E_{b+1,1})^{k}(-1)^{(b+2)k}\hbar^{-k}\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{k}L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u-i\hbar)}{k!}.

Term-by-term comparison gives that this sum is indeed zero, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}_{b}(\vec{u}-\hbar)L_{1\dots b}^{1\dots b}(u)P_{b+1,1}^{\psi}(u)=0,

and the proposition follows.
∎

It turns out that the weaker version implies the stronger one.

Proposition 5.17
.

For
2\leq b\leq N-1
, we have

E_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=0
,
where the equality is understood as of linear operators on any Whittaker module.

Proof.

Let
W
be a Whittaker module and
w\in W
. Observe that
\mathrm{span}(E_{1i}\mid 2\leq i\leq N-1)
can be naturally identified with the dual of the vector representation of
{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}
. Therefore, we can apply Remark
6.6
and conclude that

\displaystyle wE_{1b}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N-1}w_{i}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1i}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})

for some
w_{i}\in W
. Hence,

\displaystyle wE_{1b}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})={}

\displaystyle wE_{1b}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1})\cdots P_{N1}^{\psi}(u_{1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N-1}w_{i}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1i}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})P_{21}^{\psi}(u_{1})\cdots P_{N1}^{\psi}(u_{1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N-1}w_{i}P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1i}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})

for some
\{w_{i}\}\subset W
. Therefore, to prove that it is zero, it is enough to show that

P_{{}_{1}\mathfrak{m}_{N}}({}_{1}\vec{u})E_{1i}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=0

for every
2\leq i\leq N-1
. But this is exactly Proposition
5.15
.
∎

5.3
Kirillov projector: other properties

In what follows, we denote

\vec{u}-\hbar e_{i}:=(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1},u_{i}-\hbar,u_{i+1},\dots,u_{N-1})
.

Proposition 5.18
.

The Kirillov projector satisfies

L_{i\dots N-1}^{i+1\dots N}(u_{i}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{i})=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}).

for every
1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Proof.

By inductive construction of the Kirillov projector, it is enough to prove the statement for
i=1
. Recall Theorem
5.3
: the operator
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})
, satisfying

\displaystyle P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})(x-\psi(x))=0,\qquad x\in\mathfrak{n}_{-},

\displaystyle(E_{ij}+\delta_{ij}\cdot u_{j})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=0,\qquad 1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1

together with the normalization condition
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})\big|_{W^{N_{-}}}=\mathrm{id}
, is unique. Therefore, it would be enough to prove that the left-hand side satisfies these properties. The first property is clear, and the normalization condition follows from Proposition
4.8
. For
2\leq i\leq j\leq N-1
, the second property follows from

\bigl[E_{ij},L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)\bigr]=0
.
Therefore, it is enough to consider the case
i=1
. Let
j=1
. Then

\bigl[E_{11},L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)\bigr]=-\hbar L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar),

hence

\displaystyle(E_{11}+u_{1})L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})

\displaystyle=L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)(E_{11}+u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})

\displaystyle=0.

Let
j>1
, then

\bigl[E_{1j},L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)\bigr]=(-1)^{j}\hbar L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots\hat{j}\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar).

Since

L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots\hat{j}\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N-1}(-1)^{l-1}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots N-1}^{2\dots\hat{j}\dots N}(u_{1}-2\hbar)L_{1l}(u_{1}-\hbar)

and each term
L_{1l}(u_{1}-\hbar)
acts by zero on
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar)
, we conclude that

E_{ij}L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar)=0.

Therefore, the element

L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar)

satisfies the conditions of Theorem
5.3
, thus coincides with
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})
.
∎

Corollary 5.19
.

We have

E_{1N}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1}).

Proof.

Since
L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1})
is central, we have

P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1})=L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1}).

By Proposition
4.1
, we have

L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1})=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}(-1)^{l-1}L_{1\dots\hat{l}\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)L_{1l}(u_{1}).

By Theorem
5.13
, the only terms that act non-trivially are for
i=1
and
i=N
. The former gives

\displaystyle L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)L_{11}(u_{1})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})={}

\displaystyle L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)(L_{11}(u_{1}-\hbar)+\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\hbar L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1}),

while the latter

(-1)^{N-1}L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)E_{1N}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1}).

We also have

\displaystyle L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)E_{1N}={}

\displaystyle E_{1N}L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)+\hbar(-1)^{N-1}L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots N-1}(u_{1}-\hbar)

\displaystyle-\hbar(-1)^{N-1}L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar).

Since

L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots N-1}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})=0,

we have

\displaystyle L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(u_{1})P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})={}

\displaystyle\bigl[\hbar L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)+(-1)^{N-1}E_{1N}L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)

\displaystyle+\hbar L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots N-1}(u_{1}-\hbar)-\hbar L_{2\dots N}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)\bigr]P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(-1)^{N-1}E_{1N}L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar e_{1}).

Therefore, the claim of the proposition follows from

L_{1\dots N-1}^{2\dots N}(u_{1}-\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}-\hbar)=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}),

which is Proposition
5.18
.
∎

It seems that there is no closed expression for the action of
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})
on the last column
\mathfrak{u}_{N}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}

\mathfrak{u}_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&0&*\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&*\end{pmatrix}

except for
E_{1N}
. However, it turns out that in the quotient by the ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
, its image is essentially given by the coefficients of the quantum characteristic polynomial that we normalize as

L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v+N\hbar-\hbar)=v^{N}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(-1)^{N-i}A_{i}v^{i}.

Proposition 5.20
.

(1)

For a special choice of parameters
u_{i}=(N-i)\hbar
, we have

E_{kN}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(N\hbar-\hbar,\dots,\hbar)\equiv A_{k-1}\qquad\bigl(\mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\bigr).

(2)

For arbitrary parameters
\vec{u}
, there are functions
\alpha_{kj}=\alpha_{kj}(\vec{u})
and
\beta_{kj}=\beta_{kj}(\vec{u})
such that

E_{kN}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})\equiv(A_{k-1}-\beta_{kk})+\sum_{j<k}\alpha_{kj}(A_{j-1}-\beta_{kj})\qquad\bigl(\mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\bigr).

(3)

The map

\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})/\mathfrak{b}^{\vec{u}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}

induced by the Kirillov projector, is an isomorphism of vector spaces. Here
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
is the center of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
.

Proof.

In what follows, we use “
\equiv
” to denote equivalence modulo the ideal
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
.

Since
L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)
is central,

L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)\equiv P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)=L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}).

By Proposition
4.1
, we have

L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-l}L_{1\dots N-1}^{1\dots\hat{l}\dots N}(v)L_{lN}(v-\hbar N+\hbar).

By Proposition
4.8
, we get

\displaystyle L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)\equiv{}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-l}L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(v)L_{lN}(v-\hbar N+\hbar)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}(-1)^{N-l}L_{lN}(v-\hbar N+\hbar)L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(v).

Using Proposition
4.1
, one can show that

\displaystyle L_{1\dots l-1}^{1\dots l-1}(v)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=(v-u_{1})(v-u_{2}-\hbar)\cdots(v-u_{l-1}-\hbar l+2\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}),

therefore,

\displaystyle L_{1\dots N}^{1\dots N}(v)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})\equiv{}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left((-1)^{N-l}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}(v-u_{i}-\hbar i+\hbar)\right)E_{lN}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})

\displaystyle+(v-N\hbar+\hbar)\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(v-u_{i}-\hbar i+\hbar)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}).

The first and the second part of the proposition then follow by comparing the coefficients of
v
on both sides. As for the third part, it follows from Remark
6.6
that for any
x,y\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{u}_{N})
,

\displaystyle xyP_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})=xP_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})yP_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})+(K_{(1)}\cdot x)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u})(K_{(2)}\cdot y)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(\vec{u}),

where
K=K_{(1)}\otimes K_{(2)}
is some nilpotent matrix acting on
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{u}_{N})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{u}_{N})
, where we identify
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{u}_{N})
with the double quotient
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})/\mathfrak{b}^{\vec{u}}
. In particular, we can invert
1+K
. It follows from Theorem
6.11
that
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
is a free commutative algebra on generators
\{A_{i}\}
. Since we know that the transformation induced by the Kirillov projector is invertible on generators, the third part follows by induction on the degree of generators.
∎

Remark 5.21
.

Classically, the first part of Proposition
5.20
can be interpreted as follows. Denote by

P_{\hbar}:=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(N\hbar-\hbar,\dots,\hbar)

the Kirillov projector for this special choice of parameters. It gives a linear map

P_{\hbar}\colon\ \mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\rightarrow\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\bigr)^{N_{-}}.

Recall that by Kostant’s theorem
[
36
]
, the target is isomorphic to the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. Composing with the quotient map
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
, we get a homomorphism

\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
.
Taking limit
\hbar\rightarrow 0
and identifying
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we obtain a linear map

P_{0}\colon\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{N}\bigr)

where we identified
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathbb{A}^{N}\bigr)
with the classical limit of
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
using the coefficients
A_{k}
as the generators. Observe that they become the coefficients of the usual characteristic polynomial. Moreover, it follows from Remark
6.6
that this is an algebra map, since

xyP_{\hbar}=xP_{\hbar}yP_{\hbar}+O(\hbar)
.
In particular, it corresponds to a map between varieties
\mathbb{A}^{N}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
. It is not hard to determine which one: since
P_{0}
acts as

\displaystyle(E_{ij}-\psi(E_{ij}))P_{0}=0,\qquad i>j,\qquad E_{kl}P_{0}=0,\qquad k\leq l,

\displaystyle E_{kN}P_{0}=A_{k-1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq N,

this map is given by

(A_{1},\dots,A_{N})\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&0&A_{0}\\
1&\dots&0&A_{N-1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&1&A_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}

(up to transposition induced by the isomorphism
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{*}
). Therefore, the Kirillov projector quantizes the companion matrix which is a form of a Kostant slice.

\displaystyle\sum_{j>k}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}\phi_{j}E_{jk}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j>k}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(E_{jk}\phi_{j}-\hbar\ad_{E_{jk}}(\phi_{j}))P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}\phi_{k+1}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}+\hbar(N-k)P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}\phi_{k}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}.

Therefore, the total sum is
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}\phi_{k+1}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}
as required.
∎

Proposition 6.20

The endomorphisms

Proof.

We only need to check the relation

\displaystyle\phi_{k}\otimes\phi_{l}\otimes z

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi_{l}\otimes\phi_{k}\otimes z\mapsto-\bigg(\sum_{i,j}\ad_{E_{ij}}(\phi_{l})\otimes E_{ji}\bigg)\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})}(\phi_{k}\otimes z)

7.1
Generalized gauge transformation

Let

Definition 7.1

Gauge transformations preserve the set of quantum dynamical

In the setting of Section

(
[

The data of a gauge transformation between dynamical twists

\textstyle{\Rep(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\scriptstyle J_{1}(\lambda)}
\textstyle{\scriptstyle J_{2}(\lambda)}

It is also possible to interpret generalized gauge transformations in this language. There is a forgetful functor
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)\rightarrow\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})}

of
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)

Definition 7.4

Let

\mathcal{M}

7.2
Categorical vertex-IRF transformation

Recall a monoidal functor
\Rep(\mathrm{GL}_{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)^{\mathrm{gen}}
from Section
2.5
obtained from parabolic restriction whose monoidal structure morphisms are given by the standard dynamical twist of Definition
2.20
. In particular, there is an action of
\Rep(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
on the category
\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
of right
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
-modules, whose structure morphisms are given by the standard dynamical twist. Likewise, there is an action of
\Rep(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
on the category
\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}
of right modules over the center
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
whose structure morphisms are given by the rational Cremmer–Gervais twist of Definition
6.8
. Also recall the Harish-Chandra homomorphism
\mathbf{A}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\rightarrow\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
. To show that there is a vertex-IRF transformation between them, it is enough to prove by Theorem
7.5
(or, rather, generic version thereof) that the natural functor
-\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\colon\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
is a functor of module categories.

Recall that the parabolic restriction
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
is a functor
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})\rightarrow\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)^{\mathrm{gen}}
. The Harish-Chandra category
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(H)^{\mathrm{gen}}
is a subcategory of
{}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}\mathrm{BiMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
, and there is restriction functor from the latter to
{}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{BiMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
. In what follows, we consider
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
to take values in
{}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{BiMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
.

Proposition 7.6

(
[
29
, Corollary 2.7.7]
)
.

There is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\rightarrow\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}^{\psi}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}

of functors
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(G)\rightarrow{}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{BiMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}}
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
. Consider a sequence of maps

\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\bigr)^{N_{-}}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\xrightarrow{p_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})}}\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n}\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{act}}\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n},

(7.1)

where
p_{X}
is the projection and
\mathrm{act}
is the right action of
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
on
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n}
. By
[
26
, Lemma 6.2.1]
, it is an isomorphism. On the other hand, there is a map

(X/\mathfrak{n})^{N}\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n}.

(7.2)

We claim that after extension of scalars to
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
, it is an isomorphism. Indeed, since
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
is generated by free Harish-Chandra bimodules by Proposition
2.5
and both functors in question are colimit-preserving by Theorems
2.17
and
6.14
, it is enough to check the statement for free Harish-Chandra bimodules
X=\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes V
(here, it is convenient to take
left
free modules). There is an
\mathfrak{n}
-stable filtration on
V
with one-dimensional quotients; in particular, we can choose a (weight) basis
\{v_{\mu}\}
and a partial order on it such that
\ad_{\mathfrak{n}}(v_{\mu})>v_{\mu}
.

By the PBW theorem, we have a right
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
-module isomorphism

\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X/\mathfrak{n}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\otimes V
,
given by the generators
\{1\otimes v_{\mu}\}
. Likewise, the extremal projector gives an isomorphism

P\colon\ \mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes V\rightarrow(X/\mathfrak{n})^{N}

as in Theorem
2.18
(upon identifying
V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes V
). It follows from definition of
P
that

P(1\otimes v_{\mu})=1\otimes v_{\mu}+\sum_{\lambda>\mu}f_{\lambda}\cdot v_{\lambda}\in(X/\mathfrak{n})\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{b})^{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes V,

where
f_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{b})
are some elements. In particular, its class in the left quotient by
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}
is given by some upper-unitriangular transformation in the basis
\{1\otimes v_{\mu}\}
with coefficients in
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
, hence is invertible.

Therefore, composing (
7.1
) with the inverse of (
7.2
), we conclude.
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

The functor
\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\rightarrow\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}},\ M\mapsto M\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
is a functor of
\mathrm{HC}_{\hbar}(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
-module categories, in particular of
\Rep(\mathrm{GL}_{N})
-module categories.

Proof.

Since the category
\mathrm{RMod}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}
is generated by free modules, it is enough to check compatibility of actions on
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}
. By Proposition
7.6
, we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\rightarrow\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(X)

(for brevity, we omit the index “gen”). Therefore, we only need to check the pentagon axiom:

\textstyle{\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(Y)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(X\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}Y)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(X/\mathfrak{n})^{N}\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}\mathfrak{h}}(Y/\mathfrak{n})^{N}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(X\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}\mathfrak{g}}Y/\mathfrak{n})^{N}.}

In terms of elements, let
{}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[y]\in\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash Y\bigr)^{N_{-}}
and
{}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[x]\in\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash X\bigr)^{N_{-}}
. We denote their image in the double quotient
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash Y/\mathfrak{n}
by
{}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[y]_{\mathfrak{n}}
(same for
X
). Let
F
be the inverse of (
7.2
). Then we need to show that

F({}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[x]_{\mathfrak{n}})\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}\mathfrak{g}}F({}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[y]_{\mathfrak{n}})=F({}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[x\otimes_{\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}y]_{\mathfrak{n}}).

We show that the projections of both sides to

F({}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\psi}_{-}}[y]_{\mathfrak{n}})\in[y]_{\mathfrak{n}}+\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}Y/\mathfrak{n}

The element

is
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}

and so the projections to both sides coincide.

In particular, it implies the following.

from Definition

7.3
Vertex-IRF transformation

In what follows, we will use the notations of Example
2.21
and Section
6.3
; in particular, we denote
\lambda_{i}=E_{ii}+\hbar(N-i)
. Recall that the canonical homomorphism
X
commutes with both
\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
-actions on
V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})
, in particular, it induces an action on the parabolic reduction
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))

Lemma 7.9
.

The action of
X
on
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))\cong V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
is given by

P\phi_{i}P\mapsto P\phi_{i}P\cdot\lambda_{i},

where
P
is the extremal projector of Section
2.4

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle PX(\phi_{i})P

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{a,b}P\ad_{E_{ab}}(\phi_{i})E_{ba}P=\sum\limits_{b}P\phi_{b}E_{bi}P

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{b>i}P\phi_{b}E_{bi}P+P\phi_{i}PE_{ii}+\sum\limits_{b<i}P\phi_{b}E_{bi}P

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{b>i}PE_{bi}\phi_{b}P+\hbar(N-i)P\phi_{i}P+P\phi_{i}PE_{ii}+0=P\phi_{i}P(E_{ii}+N\hbar-i\hbar)

\displaystyle=P\phi_{i}P\cdot\lambda_{i},

where we used the properties of the extremal projector from Theorem
2.12
.
∎

Denote by

\phi_{i}^{\psi}:=\phi_{i}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{N}}(N\hbar-\hbar,\dots,\hbar)\in\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})),\qquad\bar{\phi}_{i}:=P\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))

the basis elements of the corresponding reductions. Recall that by construction of Proposition
7.6
, we need to explicitly compute the isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{res}^{\psi}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}}\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})^{\mathrm{gen}}/\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})).

(7.3)

We identify the middle module with
V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})^{\mathrm{gen}}
using PBW theorem.

Lemma 7.10
.

Under the projection map
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}(V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}))\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})/\mathfrak{n}
, we have

\bar{\phi}_{i}\mapsto\sum\limits_{j=i}^{N}\phi_{j}\prod_{k=i+1}^{j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})^{-1}.

Proof.

Recall the formula (
2.21
). It is clear that in order to be nonzero in the left quotient by
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}
, the indices
\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{s}\}
should be
\{i+1<\dots<j\}
for some

. The result follows.
∎

In what follows, we identify the elements
\bigl\{\phi_{i}^{\psi}\bigr\}
as well as
\bigl\{\bar{\phi}_{i}\bigr\}
with their images in the double quotient
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\psi}\backslash V\otimes\mathrm{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N})/\mathfrak{n}
.

Lemma 7.11
.

We have

\phi_{1}^{\psi}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\phi}_{i}\prod_{j<i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{-1}.

Proof.

We need to show that the coefficient in front of each
\phi_{k}
for
k=2,\dots,N
on the right-hand side is zero. It is equal to
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j\neq i,j\leq k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{-1}
. But this is the sum of all residues with respect to variable
u
of the rational function
\prod_{j=1}^{k}(u-\lambda_{j})^{-1}
which is zero.
∎

Corollary 7.12
.

For any

, we have

\phi_{k}^{\psi}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bar{\phi}_{i}\cdot\lambda_{i}^{k-1}\prod_{j<i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{-1}.

Proof.

Observe that the sequence (
7.3
) commutes with the canonical homomorphism
X
. The corollary follows from Proposition
6.18
and Lemma
7.9
.
∎

In particular, we see that the transition matrix from the Kostant–Whittaker reduction of

to its parabolic reduction is essentially given by the Vandermonde matrix

S(\lambda)=D(\lambda)\cdot V(\lambda),\qquad V(\lambda)=\bigl(\lambda_{i}^{j-1}\bigr)_{ij},\qquad D(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}\Big(\prod_{j<i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{-1}\Big)_{i=1}^{N},

(7.4)

which agrees (up to a diagonal factor) with a rational analog of the original construction
[

]
(see also

]
) studied in

]
.

Theorem 7.13

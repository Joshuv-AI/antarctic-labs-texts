Game Semantics for Higher-Order Unitary Quantum Computation
Samson Abramsky, Radha Jagadeesan

Abstract
We develop a symmetric monoidal closed category of games, incorporating sums and products, to model quantum computation at higher types. This model is expressive, capable of representing all unitary operators at base types. It is compatible with base types and realizable by unitary operators.

Our understanding of deterministic higher-order computation emerges from the tight linkage between types/formulas and programs/proofs. Acknowledging that quantum processes will inevitably yield new proofs, we adhere to the following principle to preserve the connection with logic:

Discussion

The category
{\cal V}
is closely related to the one derived from the GOI-construction on finite-dimensional vector spaces with tensor products, as discussed in
Abramsky and Coecke [2003]
. Specifically, the composition of functions on strategies within
{\cal V}
aligns with their definition of composition. This makes
{\cal V}
a refinement of their category, specifically tailored to games. While their original category is compact-closed,
{\cal V}
does not exhibit
\star
-autonomy due to its restriction to games initiated by the Opponent. Moreover,
{\cal V}
distinguishes between the two multiplicatives based on considerations of the direction of information flow, which are inherent to the game structure.

This refined approach is exploited in subsequent sections, where we focus
{\cal V}
on reversible and unitary strategies. Within these discussions, the uniqueness of composition witnesses, guaranteed by lemma
18
and lemma
19
, allow us to demonstrate that reversibility and unitarity properties are conserved under composition.

Universality.

We conclude this section by demonstrating universality in a couple of different ways.

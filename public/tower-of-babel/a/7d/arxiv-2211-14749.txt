Characteristic functions for (r, n)-free and (f, g)-free elements
Himangshu Hazarika, Dhiren Kumar Basnet

Abstract
. For a prime power q q , 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} denotes the finite field of order q q , and for m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} denotes the extension field of degree m m . We establish a characteristic function for the set of ( r , n ) (r,\,n) -free elements of finite cyclic R R -module for the Euclidean domain R R . Furthermore, we explore ( f , g ) (f,\,g) -freeness through polynomial values and finally give an expression for the characteristic function for the set of ( f , g ) (f,\,g) -free elements.

For a prime power

\mathbb{F}_{q}

q

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

The problem of constructing an algorithm to find a primitive element of a finite field is one of the major open problems in finite field theory. Hence, researchers focus on relatively unchallenging problem, i.e., to create an algorithm to find an element of higher multiplicative order. This problem becomes more useful as the elements of higher multiplicative order may replace primitive elements in various applications. One of the notable work of Gao
[
3

3.
Finite cyclic
R
-module

Let
R
be an Euclidean domain and
\mathfrak{M}
be a finite cyclic
R
-module, under the rule
r\,o\,\alpha
, where
r\in R
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
. Let
g\in\mathfrak{M}
be a generator of
\mathfrak{M}
. Since
\mathfrak{M}
has similar properties as abelian group, hence
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}
can be defined as an
R
-module, under the rule,
r\,o\,\psi\,:\,\alpha\,\mapsto\,\psi(r\,o\,\alpha)
, for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
and
\psi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}
. In this article, whenever a
sum runs through the divisors of some element of
R
or when a condition is applicable to all the members of a conjugacy class of
R/\sim
, we will consider just one representative, as an element of
R
and will treat as same with its conjugates that hold the equivalence relation
r\sim s\Leftrightarrow r=us
, for some
u\in R^{*}
.

For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
, from the properties of
R
and
\mathfrak{M}
, we have that the annihilator of
\alpha
is an ideal of
R
, hence it has a unique generator. This annihilator is called the
order
of
\alpha
and we denote it with
\mathrm{ord}(\alpha)
. We set
m:=ord(g)
.

For
d\in R
, the Euler function is defined as
\phi(d)=|(R/dR)^{*}|
.

For
a,b\in R
, we denote
a_{(b)}
as

a_{(b)}=\frac{a}{gcd(a,b)}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r\in R
and
b
is a divisor of
m
. An element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
is of the form
r\circ\beta
with
ord(\beta)=b
if and only if
ord(\alpha)=b_{(n)}
, where
n=\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,m)
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
be of the form
r\circ\beta
with
ord(\beta)=b
. Then
b
is the generator of the ideal (ideal of annihilators of
\beta
) in R. Then
\alpha=r\circ\beta
has order
\frac{b}{\mathrm{gcd}(b,\,r)}=\frac{b}{\mathrm{gcd}(b,\,n)}=b_{(n)}
.

(\Leftarrow)
Let
\mathrm{ord}(\alpha)=b_{(n)}
, where
n=\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,m)
. To show
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
is of the form
r\circ\beta
with
\mathrm{ord}(\beta)=b
.

Consider the set
A_{b}=\{r\circ\beta|\mathrm{ord}(\beta)=b\}.
Then the cardinality of the set is
\phi(b_{(n)})
. Now for any element
\delta
of order
b
, the elements of
A_{b}
are
i\circ(r\circ\delta)=(ir)\circ\delta
, where
\mathrm{gcd}(i,\,b)=1
. So we have,
(ir)\circ\delta=(jr)\circ\delta
if and only if
i=j+yb_{(n)}
, for some
y\in R
, i.e.,
i\equiv j(\mathrm{mod}(b_{(n)}))
. (This
\mathrm{mod}
is analogous to

Therefore the cardinality of the set
A_{b}
is the number of in-congruent
x(\mathrm{mod}(b_{(n)}))
, as
x\in R
such that
\mathrm{gcd}(x,\,b)=1
. Since
b_{(n)}
divides
b
, hence cardinality is
\phi(b_{(n)})
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2

For

If

When

(iii)

3.1.

Following is the definition of Generalised

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be two divisors of
m

-free if

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
r,\,n
be two divisors of
m
. Let
a\in\mathfrak{M}
be such that
ord(a)|\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}
. Then
a
is
(r,\,n)
-free if and only if
\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)=\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,\frac{m}{ord(a)})
.

Proof.

Clearly it suffices to prove that
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)ord(a)}\right)=1
. We take
b\in\mathfrak{M}
of order
\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,m)}
. Then
a=k\circ b
for some
k\in R
, while
ord(a)=\left(\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)\mathrm{gcd}\left(k,\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}\right)}\right).

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
a
be
(r,\,n)
-free such that
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)ord(a)}\right)\neq 1
.

Then
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\mathrm{gcd}\left(k,\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}\right)\right)\neq 1
i.e.,
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},k,\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}\right)\neq 1,
i.e.
k=k_{0}k_{1},
for some
k_{0}\in R
such that
k_{0}\neq 1
and
k_{0}\,|\,\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}\right).

Thus
a=k\circ b=(k_{0}k_{1})\circ b=k_{0}\circ(k_{1}\circ b).
Since
a
is
(r,\,n)
-free, this implies

a contradiction.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)ord(a)}\right)=1
. Take some
\beta\in\mathfrak{M}
such that
ord(\beta)|\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)},

a=d\circ\beta
and
d|r_{(n)}.
It suffices to show that

.

First we have
\beta=i\circ b
, for some

. It follows that
a=k\circ b=d\circ(i\circ b)=(di)\circ b
. Now
ord(b)=\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}
and
k\circ b=(di)\circ b
, where
b\in\mathfrak{M}
and
k,di\in R.
Since
R
is ED, there exists

such that
k=di+t\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,n)}
. Since

and

, hence

. We have
\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)ord(a)}\right)=1

\mathrm{gcd}\left(r_{(n)},k,\frac{m}{\mathrm{gcd}(r,\,n)}\right)=1

Lemma 3.7

\Lambda_{r,n}(x)=\begin{cases}1,\,\mbox{ if }x\mbox{ is }(r,\,n)\mbox{-free},\\

Consider the case

\Lambda_{p^{s},n}(\omega)=\frac{\phi(p^{s}_{(n)})}{|(R/p^{s}R)|}\underset{i=0}{\overset{s}{\sum}}\frac{\mu(p^{i}_{(n)})}{\phi(p^{i}_{(n)})}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{i}}{\sum}\psi(\omega).

Now

\displaystyle\underset{i=0}{\overset{s}{\sum}}\frac{\mu(p^{i}_{(n)})}{\phi(p^{i}_{(n)})}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{i}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)=

\displaystyle\underset{i=0}{\overset{t}{\sum}}\frac{\mu(p^{i}_{(n)})}{\phi(p^{i}_{(n)})}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{i}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)+\frac{\mu(p^{t+1}_{(n)})}{\phi(p^{t+1}_{(n)})}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{t+1}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{i=0}{\overset{t}{\sum}}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{i}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)-\frac{1}{\phi(p)}\underset{ord(\phi)=p^{t+1}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{ord(\psi)|p^{t}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)-\frac{1}{\phi(p)}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{t+1}}{\sum}\psi(\omega).

Hence

\Lambda_{p^{s},n}(\omega)=\frac{\phi(p^{s}_{(n)})}{|(R/p^{s}R)|}\left(\underset{ord(\psi)|p^{t}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)-\frac{1}{\phi(p)}\underset{ord(\psi)=p^{t+1}}{\sum}\psi(\omega)\right).

(3.1)

Now, let

Next assume that

\mathfrak{F}\,o\,x\,:=\,\underset{i=0}{\overset{n}{\sum}}F_{i}x^{q^{i}}
, where
\mathfrak{F}(X)=\underset{i=0}{\overset{n}{\sum}}F_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. From the normal basis theorem, it is clear that the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
-module is cyclic, and normal elements or free elements are generators of the module.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, the unique monic polynomial polynomial
g
of the least degree dividing
x^{m}-1
is said to be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order of
\alpha
if
g\circ\alpha=0
.
Throughout this section, we denote
Ord(\alpha)
as the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order of
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. For any element
\alpha
in some extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
(x^{m}-1)\circ\alpha=\alpha^{q^{m}}-\alpha=0
if and only if
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. If we set
I_{\alpha}=\{g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\mid g\circ\alpha=0\}
, then
I_{\alpha}
is an ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and hence generated by a polynomial, say
h_{\alpha}(x)
(we require
h_{\alpha}(x)
to be monic). Then
h_{\alpha}(x)
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order of
\alpha
. It is clear that
Ord(\alpha)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
Ord(\alpha)|x^{m}-1
.

For
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, we denote
f_{(g)}
as

f_{(g)}=\frac{f}{\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,g)}.

If
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,
f=\underset{i=0}{\overset{s}{\sum}}a_{i}x^{i}
, we define
L_{f}(x)=\underset{i=0}{\overset{s}{\sum}}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}
as
q
-associate of
f
. Also for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, let
f\circ\alpha=L_{f}(\alpha)=\underset{i=0}{\overset{s}{\sum}}a_{i}\alpha^{q^{i}}
. For
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, the following hold.

(i)

L_{f}(L_{g}(x))=L_{fg}(x).

(ii)

L_{f}(x)+L_{g}(x)=L_{f+g}(x).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a divisor of
x^{m}-1
. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is of the form
f\circ\beta
with
Ord(\beta)=g
if and only if
Ord(\alpha)=g_{(h)}
, where
h=\mathrm{gcd}(f,x^{m}-1)
.

Proof of this lemma is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, then we have

T(f,g):=\underset{t|f}{\sum}\frac{|\mu(t_{(g)})|}{\Phi(t_{(g)})}.\Phi(t)=\left|\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{l\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}\right|.W\left(\left|\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{h\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}\right|\right)=\left(q^{deg(l)}\right).W\left(q^{deg(h)}\right),

where
l=\mathrm{gcd}(f,g)
and
h=\mathrm{gcd}(f,f_{(g)})
,
W(n)
denotes the number of square free divisors of
n
.

Proof of this lemma is similar to that of Lemma
3.2
.

5.
(f,\,g)
-free element

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be divisors of
x^{m}-1
. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called
(f,\,g)
-free if
Ord(\alpha)\mid\frac{x^{m}-1}{\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,g)}
and
\alpha=h\circ\beta
, for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with
Ord(\beta)\mid\frac{x^{m}-1}{\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,g)}
and
h|f_{(g)}
, imply
h=1
.

Then we have the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f,\,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be divisors of
x^{m}-1
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
be such that
Ord(\alpha)\mid\frac{x^{m}-1}{\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,g)}
. Then
\alpha
is
(f,\,g)
-free if and only if
\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,g)=\mathrm{gcd}(f,\,\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(\alpha)})
.

The proof is similar to Lemma
3.5
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
t|x^{m}-1
and we take some
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Then

\underset{Ord(\psi)|t}{\sum}\psi(\omega)=\begin{cases}\left|\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{<t>}\right|\,\mbox{or }\,q^{deg(t)},\,\mbox{ if }\,t\mid\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(\omega)},\\
0,\,\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 5.2
.

Now we define the function

\Omega_{f,g}(\omega)=\frac{\Phi(f_{(g)})}{q^{deg(f)}}\underset{h|f}{\sum}\frac{\mu(h_{(g)})}{\Phi(h_{(g)})}\underset{Ord(\psi)=h}{\sum}\psi(\omega),\,\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

and prove it to be characteristic function for
(f,\,g)
-free elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, then

\Omega_{f,g}(\omega)=\begin{cases}1,\,\mbox{ if }\omega\mbox{ is }(f,\,g)\mbox{-free},\\
0,\,\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The proof is similar as Lemma
3.7
.

6.
(f,\,g)
-freeness through polynomial values

For divisors
f,\,F,\,g,\,G
of
x^{m}-1
and polynomials
h,\,H\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, we study the number of pairs
(h(y),\,H(y))
such that
h(y)
is
(f,\,g)
-free and
H(y)
is
(F,\,G)
-free with
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. This number can be zero if
h(x)
and
H(x)
have certain additive dependence with respect to the polynomials
h,\,H
.

Following is the definition of dependence for polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x].

Definition 6.1
.

Let
f,\,F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be divisors of
x^{m}-1
and let
h,\,H\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be non constant polynomials. The pair
(h,\,H)
is
(f,\,F)
-independent if for every
t|f
and
T|F
with
l=\mathrm{lcm}(t,\,T)\neq 1
(we choose
t,\,T
to be monic) and
1\leq deg(c)\leq deg(t)
and
1\leq deg(C)\leq deg(T)
with
\mathrm{gcd}(t,\,c)=\mathrm{gcd}(T,\,C)=1
, the polynomial

L_{\frac{lc}{t}}(h(x))+L_{\frac{lC}{T}}(H(x))
is not of the form
L_{l}(g(x))+k
, for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.

We continue with the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

With the terms and conditions as above, if
\mathfrak{G}(x)=L_{\frac{lc}{t}}(h(x))+L_{\frac{lC}{T}}(H(x))
is not of the form
L_{l}(g(x))+k
for any
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and for any
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, then
\mathfrak{G}(x)
is definitely not of the form
a(x)^{p}-a(x)+b
, for any
a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, where
q=p^{n}
for some positive integer
n
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f,\,F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be divisors of
x^{m}-1
. If the pair
(h,\,H)
is not
(f,\,F)
-independent, then there exist divisors
r,\,R\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
of
x^{m}-1
in a way
that there is no element
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
h(\theta)
is
(f,\,r)
-free and
H(\theta)
is
(F,\,R)
-free.

Proof.

Since
(h,\,H)
is not
(f,\,F
)-independent, there exist some
t|f
and
T|F
with
l=\mathrm{lcm}(t,\,T)\neq 1
and
1\leq deg(c)\leq deg(t)
and
1\leq deg(C)\leq deg(T)
with
\mathrm{gcd}(t,\,c)=\mathrm{gcd}(T,\,C)=1
, such that for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
and
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]

L_{\frac{lc}{t}}(h(x))+L_{\frac{lC}{T}}(H(x))=L_{l}(g(x))+k.

Then for every
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
we have

\left(\frac{lc}{t}\right)\circ h(\theta)+\left(\frac{lC}{T}\right)\circ H(\theta)=l\circ g(\theta)+k.

Then multiplying by
\frac{x^{m}-1}{l}
we have

\frac{(x^{m}-1)c}{t}\circ h(\theta)+\frac{(x^{m}-1)C}{T}\circ H(\theta)=\left(\frac{x^{m}-1}{l}\right)\circ k.

(6.1)

First we consider the case
t\neq T
(we have already chosen
t,T
as monic). From the Equation (
6.1
), we have

\frac{T(x^{m}-1)c}{t}\circ h(\theta)=\frac{T(x^{m}-1)}{l}\circ k
and
\frac{t(x^{m}-1)C}{T}\circ H(\theta)=\frac{t(x^{m}-1)}{l}\circ k

Since right hand sides are inverses of one another, hence we have

\displaystyle\frac{Ord(h(\theta))}{\mathrm{gcd}\left(Ord(h(\theta)),\frac{x^{m}-1}{t}.T.c\right)}=\frac{Ord(H(\theta))}{\mathrm{gcd}\left(Ord(H(\theta)),\frac{x^{m}-1}{T}.t.C\right)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow t.\mathrm{gcd}\left(T,\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(H(\theta))}.t.C\right)=T.\mathrm{gcd}\left(t,\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(h(\theta))}.T.c\right).

We divide both the parts of above by
\mathrm{gcd}(t,\,T)
and get

t.\mathrm{gcd}\left(T_{(t)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(H(\theta))}.t_{(T)}.C\right)=T.\mathrm{gcd}\left(t_{(T)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(h(\theta))}.T_{(t)}.c\right).

Since
\mathrm{gcd}(t_{(T)},T_{(t)}.c)=\mathrm{gcd}(T_{(t)},t_{(T)}.C)=1
, the above equation becomes

t.\mathrm{gcd}\left(T_{(t)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(H(\theta))}\right)=T.\mathrm{gcd}\left(t_{(T)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(h(\theta))}\right).

(6.2)

If possible, let there be some
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, such that
h(\theta)
is
(f,r)
-free and
H(\theta)
is
(F,R)
-free, for some divisors
r,R\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
of
x^{m}-1
. Using Lemma
5.1
, we have
\mathrm{gcd}\left(f,\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(h(\theta))}\right)=\mathrm{gcd}(f,r)
. Then combining this with the fact that
t_{(T)}|f
, we have that
\mathrm{gcd}\left(t_{(T)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(h(\theta))}\right)=\mathrm{gcd}(t_{(T)},r).
Similarly we have
\mathrm{gcd}\left(T_{(t)},\frac{x^{m}-1}{Ord(H(\theta))}\right)=\mathrm{gcd}(T_{(t)},R).
Then Equation (
6.2
) gives

t.\mathrm{gcd}(T_{(t)},R)=T.\mathrm{gcd}(t_{(T)},r).

(6.3)

Since
t\neq T
, without loss of generality we assume that there is some non constant monic polynomial
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
such that
d|t_{(T)}
. Now we choose
R=T_{(t)}
and
r=\frac{t_{(T)}}{d}
, then Equation (
6.3
) yields
\frac{tT}{\mathrm{gcd}(t,T)}=\frac{tT}{d\,\mathrm{gcd}(t,T)}
, a contradiction.

Now we have the case
t=T
. In this case, Equation (
6.1
) becomes

\frac{(x^{m}-1)c}{l}\circ h(\theta)+\frac{(x^{m}-1)C}{l}\circ H(\theta)=\frac{(x^{m}-1)}{l}\circ k
.

Let us set
r=R=l
. In this case if
h(\theta)
is
(f,\,l)
-free, then its
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order divides
\frac{x^{m}-1}{l}
, hence
\frac{(x^{m}-1)c}{l}\circ h(\theta)=0
. Similarly
\frac{(x^{m}-1)C}{l}\circ H(\theta)=0
and hence their sum must be zero i.e.,
\frac{x^{m}-1}{l}\circ k=0
. Then identical arguments lead us to Equation (
6.3
), i.e.,

t.\mathrm{gcd}(T,\,R)=T.\mathrm{gcd}(t,\,r).

Since
\mathrm{lcm}(t,\,T)
is non constant monic polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, hence without loss of generality we may assume that there exists some non constant
e\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
such that
e|t
. Now we choose
R=T
and
r=\frac{t}{e}
, then above yields
tT=\frac{tT}{e}
, a contradiction.

This completes the proof.

∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f,\,F,\,r,\,R\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
be divisors of
x^{m}-1
and for
h,\,H\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}[x]
, the pair
(h,\,H)
is
(f,\,F)
-independent. Let
D+1\geq 1
be the number of distinct roots of
h(x)+H(x)
in its splitting field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The number
\mathfrak{N}_{h,\,H}=\mathfrak{N}_{h,\,H}(f,F,r,R)
of elements
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that
h(\theta)
is
(f,\,r)
-free and
H(\theta)
is
(F,\,R)
-free satisfies

\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}=\frac{\Phi(f_{(r)})\Phi(F_{(R)})}{q^{deg(f)}q^{deg(F)}}\left(q+M(f,r,F,R)\right),

with
|M(f,r,F,R)|\leq Dq^{m/2}Q_{f,r}Q_{F,R}
, where
Q_{g,h}:=\left|\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{<k>}\right|.W\left(\left|\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{<y>}\right|\right)
with
k=gcd(g,h)
,
y=\mathrm{gcd}(g,g_{(h)})
.

Proof.

By definition we have,
\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}=\underset{\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}{\sum}\Omega_{f,r}(h(\omega))\Omega_{F,R}(H(\omega))
.
From the characteristic function
\Omega
, for

A=\frac{\Phi(f_{(r)})\Phi(F_{(R)})}{q^{deg(f)}q^{deg(F)}}

, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}/A

\displaystyle=\underset{\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}{\sum}\left(\underset{t|f}{\sum}\frac{\mu(t_{(r)})}{\Phi(t_{(r)})}\underset{Ord(\psi)}{\sum}\psi(h(\omega))\right)\left(\underset{T|F}{\sum}\frac{\mu(T_{(R)})}{\Phi(T_{(R)})}\underset{Ord(\kappa)}{\sum}\kappa(H(\omega))\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{\underset{T|F}{t|f}}{\sum}\frac{\mu(t_{(r)})\mu(T_{(R)})}{\Phi(t_{(r)})\Phi(T_{(R)})}\underset{Ord(\kappa)=T,Ord(\psi)=t}{\sum}G_{h,H}(\psi,\kappa),

where
G_{h,H}=\underset{\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}{\sum}\psi(h(\omega))\kappa(H(\omega))
.

Fix
t|f
and
T|F
(we choose
t,T
to be monic), let
\psi,\kappa
be additive characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-orders
t
and
T
respectively, and set
l=\mathrm{lcm}(t,\,T)
. Then

\psi(h(\omega))\kappa(H(\omega))=\widetilde{\psi}\left(\left(\frac{cl}{t}\right)\circ h(\omega)+\left(\frac{Cl}{T}\right)\circ H(\omega)\right),

for some additive character
\widetilde{\psi}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order
l
and some polynomials
c,C
such that
1\leq deg(c)\leq deg(t)
and
1\leq deg(C)\leq deg(T)
with
\mathrm{gcd}(t,\,c)=\mathrm{gcd}(T,\,C)=1
.

Since the pair
(h,\,H)
is
(f,\,F)
-independent, the polynomial
\mathfrak{G}(x)=L_{\frac{cl}{t}}(h(x))+L_{\frac{Cl}{T}}(H(x))
is of the form
L_{l}(g(x))+k
if and only if
l=1
i.e.
t=T=1
. Therefore from Weil’s theorem we have
|G_{h,H}(\psi,\kappa)|\leq Dq^{m/2}
whenever
(t,\,T)=(1,\,1)
.

For
t=T=1
, we observe that
\psi
and
\kappa
are just the trivial additive characters and so
G_{h,H}(\psi,\kappa)=q^{m}-\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is the number of the roots of
h(x)+H(x)
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Since
\varepsilon\leq D+1
, we have

\left|\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}/A-q^{m}\right|\leq D+1+Dq^{m/2}E,

where

E=\underset{\underset{(t,T)\neq(1,1)}{t|f,T|F}}{\sum}\frac{\mu(t_{(r)})\mu(T_{(R)})}{\Phi(t_{(r)})\Phi(T_{(R)})}\underset{Ord(\kappa)=T}{\underset{Ord(\psi)=t}{\sum}}1=T(f,\,r)T(F,\,R)-1
.

Where
T(f,\,g)
is as in Lemma
4.2
and according to the Lemma
6.2
, we have the equality
T(f,\,g)=Q_{f,\,g}
and so

\left|\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}/A-q^{m}\right|\leq D+1+Dq^{m/2}(Q_{f,r}Q_{F,R}-1)<Dq^{m/2}Q_{f,r}Q_{F,R}

from where the result follows.

∎

Corollary 6.3.1
.

Let
f,\,F,\,r,\,R,\,h,\,H
as in the above theorem.

If
q^{m/2}\geq DQ_{f,r}Q_{F,R}
, then
\mathfrak{N}_{h,H}>0.

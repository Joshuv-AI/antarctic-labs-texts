1.
Introduction

A partition of an integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers (called the “parts” of the partition) whose sum is
n
. The partition function
p(n)
is defined as the number of partitions of
n
. In 1921, Ramanujan
[
Ram21
]
showed that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(1)

\displaystyle p(5n+4)\equiv 0\pmod{5}

(2)

\displaystyle p(7n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{7}

(3)

\displaystyle p(11n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Ramanujan’s discoveries sparked interest among number theorists in the arithmetic properties of
p(n)
, as well as similar partition functions. An
r
-colored partition of
n
is a partition of
n
for which each part is assigned one of a fixed set of
r
colors. We let
p_{r}(n)
denote the number of
r
-colored partitions of
n
for positive integers
n
. Congruences for
p_{r}(n)
modulo powers of 11 similar to Ramanujan’s congruences were found by Gordon
[
Gor83
]
. Kiming and Olsson
[
KO92
]
, Boylan
[
Boy04
]
, and Dawsey and Wagner
[
DW17
]
discovered families of congruences of the form

(4)

p_{r}(\ell n+t)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}

for primes
\ell
. We refer to congruences of the form (
4
) where
\ell
is prime as
Ramanujan-type congruences
.

Ahlgren and Boylan
[
AB03
]
showed that
p(n)
has no Ramanujan-type congruences other than (
1
)-(3). On the other hand, the work of Ono and Ahlgren (
[
Ono00
]
,
[
AO01
]
) shows that
p(n)
has many congruences of the form
p(An+t)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. Treneer
[
Tre06
]
extended this to all integer sequences which arise as Fourier coefficients of a weakly holomorphic modular form, including the functions
p_{r}(n)
. These results show that for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and prime
\ell\geq 5
, there are infinitely many primes
Q
such that for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

p_{r}(\ell Q^{4}n+t)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Despite all this progress, there is no known explicit classification of all the congruences of
p_{r}(n)
. Finding new types of congruences, and showing various types of congruences do not exist, remains an active area of research.

Ahlgren, Raum, and the first author
[
ABR20
]
investigated congruences of the form

(5)

p_{r}(\ell mn+t)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}

with
r=1
and
m
cube-free and co-prime to
\ell
. Of course, if
\ell\in\{5,7,11\}
and
r=1
, then one has (
5
) for any
m
by the Ramanujan Congruences (
1
)-(3). When
r=1
,
[
ABR20
]
shows that there are no non-trivial instances of
5
where
\ell,m<10,000
and
m
is prime, and they proved that for any
\ell\geq 5
, congruences of the type (
5
) are rare in a precise sense unless a certain modular form has many coefficients divisible by
\ell
.

We investigate congruences of the form (
5
) for
r>1
and
m
prime, and we call such congruences
theta-type congruences
. We say that a theta-type congruence is nontrivial if it does not follow from a Ramanujan-type congruence. Keeping with the method of
[
ABR20
]
and other papers on partition congruences, we use the following generating functions:

f_{r,\ell,0}(z):=\sum_{N}p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell N+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{N}{24}}=q^{-\frac{r\ell}{24}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{r}\left(\ell n-\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}\right)q^{n}

and

f_{r,\ell,-1}(z):=\sum_{\left(\frac{-rN}{\ell}\right)=-1}p_{r}\left(\frac{N+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{N}{24}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq\ell-1\\

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present the necessary background from the theory of modular forms.

2.1.
Modular forms

Throughout,
\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
denotes the upper half plane and
z
represents an element of
\mathbb{H}
. We let
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
and
q^{n}:=e(nz)
for
n\in\mathbb{Q}
.

We will use the action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
on
\mathbb{H}
given by fractional linear transformation:

\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\cdot z:=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

If
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a function on the upper half-plane,
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
,
we define

\displaystyle\left(f\big|_{k}\gamma\right)(\tau):=(\det\gamma)^{\frac{k}{2}}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma\tau),

where we always choose the principal branch of the square root.

The Dedekind eta function defined in Section 1 satisfies the transformation law

\eta|_{1/2}\gamma=\nu_{\eta}(\gamma)\eta

for every
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, with
\nu_{\eta}
given explicitly in
[
Kno70
, §4.1]
.

We will require the congruence subgroups

\Gamma_{0}(N):=\Big\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\Big\}.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
. We say that a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
modular form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and level
N
with respect to the multiplier system
\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}
if

(10)

f\big|_{k}\gamma=\chi(d)\nu_{\eta}^{r}(\gamma)f\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ \gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N)

and if
f\big|_{k}\sigma
has a Fourier expansion of the form

f\big|_{k}\sigma=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\sigma}(n)q^{n/24}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ \sigma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

Such a function is a
cusp form
if
\lim_{y\to\infty}f(\sigma(x+iy))=0
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. On the other hand, a
weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and level
N
with respect to
\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}
is a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
which satisfies (
10
) and the property that for each
\sigma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, there is a polynomial
P_{\sigma}
such that
\lim_{y\to\infty}f(\sigma z)-P_{\sigma}(q^{\frac{-1}{24N}})=0
. We denote by
M_{k}\left(N,\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
,
S_{k}\left(N,\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
, and
M^{!}_{k}\left(N,\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
the complex vector spaces of modular forms, cusp forms, and weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight
k
and level
N
with respect to
\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}
.

It is easy to check that any
f\in M_{k}^{!}\left(N,\chi\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
has a Fourier expansion of the form

(11)

f=\sum_{n\equiv r\pmod{24}}a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}.

2.6.
Modular properties of
f_{r,\ell,\delta}

The generating functions
f_{r,\ell,\delta}
have been used in several papers on partition congruences going back to the work of Ono
[
Ono00
]
and Ahlgren-Ono
[
AO01
]
for

, more recently in
[

]
, as well as
[

]
for

. From these previous works, it is easy to see that

is always a weakly holomorphic modular form, but we include a statement and proof of this fact here for completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

We assume
\ell\geq 5
is prime, and that
r
is an odd integer co-prime to
\ell
. Then

f_{r,\ell,0}\equiv\frac{\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|U_{\ell}}{\eta^{r\ell}}\pmod{\ell}.

It follows that
f_{r,\ell,0}
is congruent modulo
\ell
to a weakly holomorphic modular form in
M_{k_{r,\ell,0}}^{!}(\nu_{\eta}^{-r\ell})
, where

k_{r,\ell,0}=\frac{r(\ell^{2}-\ell-1)}{2}.

Proof.

Let
a(n)
be the coefficients of
\Delta^{\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)r}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}a(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=:\Delta^{\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)r}

\displaystyle=\eta^{r(\ell^{2}-1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{n}(1-q^{n})^{\ell^{2}r}\right)\cdot q^{r\ell^{2}/24}\eta^{-r}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{n}(1-q^{n})^{\ell^{2}r}\right)\cdot\sum_{n}p_{r}(n)q^{n+r\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{n}(1-q^{n})^{\ell^{2}r}\right)\sum_{n}p_{r}\left(n-r\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)\right)q^{n}.

Applying the
U_{\ell}
operator, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n}a(\ell n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=:\Delta^{\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)r}|U_{\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv(1-q^{n})^{\ell r}\sum_{n}p_{r}\left(\ell n-\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)r\right)\pmod{\ell}.

Multiplying both sides of the last relation by
\eta^{-r\ell}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{r(\ell^{2}-1)/24}|U_{\ell}}{\eta^{\ell r}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum p_{r}(\ell n-r(\ell^{2}-1)/24)q^{n-(r\ell/24)}\pmod{\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum p_{r}(\ell n-r(\ell^{2}-1)/24)q^{\frac{24n-r\ell}{24}}\pmod{\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{N}p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell N+r}{24}\right)q^{N/24}\pmod{\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv f_{r,\ell,0}\pmod{\ell}.

Finally, we observe that
\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|U_{\ell}\equiv\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|T_{\ell}\in M_{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{2}}
and
\eta^{r\ell}\in S_{r\ell/2}(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r\ell})
, and it follows that their ratio lies in
M_{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)-r\ell}{2}}^{!}(1,\nu^{-r\ell})
.
∎

For

Lemma 2.7
.

We assume
\ell\geq 5
is prime, and that

Let

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\prod_{n}(1-q^{n})^{\ell^{2}r}\right)\sum_{n}\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)p_{r}\left(n-r\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)\right)q^{n}\pmod{\ell}.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}-\left(\frac{-r}{\ell}\right)\Theta^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\Delta^{r\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\right)}

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

First we prove case (1). From Lemma
2.6
, we have

f_{r,\ell,0}\equiv\frac{\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|U_{\ell}}{\eta^{r\ell}}\pmod{\ell}.

First we explain why our assumptions imply that
f_{r,\ell,0}
is congruent to a cusp form. The order of vanishing of the numerator is at least
\lceil\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}\rceil
, and the order of vanishing of the denominator is
\frac{r\ell}{24}
. We have

\displaystyle\Big\lceil\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}\Big\rceil

\displaystyle=\Big\lceil\frac{r\ell}{24}-\frac{r}{24\ell}\Big\rceil

\displaystyle=\Big\lceil\frac{r\ell}{24}-\frac{a}{24}+\frac{a+1}{24\ell}\Big\rceil

\displaystyle=\Big\lceil\frac{r\ell-a}{24}+\frac{a+1}{24\ell}\Big\rceil

\displaystyle\geq\Big\lceil\frac{r\ell}{24}\Big\rceil

\displaystyle>\frac{r\ell}{24}.

In the last two steps, we used the assumptions that
r\ell\equiv 23\pmod{24}
and
0<a<23
.

By Lemma
2.3
, the form
\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|U_{\ell}
is congruent to a form in
S_{w}
, where

(19)

w=(\ell-1)\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{\ell-1}\cdot\left(\ell+\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)-2}{2\ell}\right)\Big\rfloor.

We compute
w
:

\displaystyle\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{\ell-1}\cdot\left(\ell+\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)-2}{2\ell}\right)\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=\Big\lfloor\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}+\frac{r(\ell+1)}{2\ell}-\frac{1}{\ell(\ell-1)}\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=\Big\lfloor\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{\ell(\ell-1)}+\frac{r}{2}+\frac{r}{2\ell}\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=\Big\lfloor\frac{\ell+1}{\ell}+\frac{a(\ell-1)}{2}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{2}-\frac{(a+1)}{2\ell}\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=1+\frac{a(\ell-1)}{2}+\frac{a-1}{2}+\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{(a+1)}{2\ell}\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=\frac{a\ell+1}{2}+\Big\lfloor\frac{1-a}{2\ell}\Big\rfloor

\displaystyle=\frac{a\ell-1}{2}.

In the last step, we used the assumption that
\ell\geq\frac{a-1}{2}
.

Now we have
4|w
, in particular,
w\not\equiv 2\pmod{12}
, so
\operatorname{dim}(S_{w})=\Big\lfloor\frac{w}{12}\Big\rfloor
. We compute

\Big\lfloor\frac{w}{12}\Big\rfloor=\Big\lfloor\frac{a\ell^{2}-a\ell-\ell+1}{24}\Big\rfloor

and

\frac{r\ell}{24}=\frac{a\ell^{2}-a\ell-\ell}{24}.

From
a\ell^{2}-a\ell-\ell+1\equiv 0\pmod{24}
and
r\ell/24\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, we deduce

\Big\lfloor\frac{w}{12}\Big\rfloor>\frac{r\ell}{24}.

Using the Miller basis, there exists a form in
S_{w}
congruent to
\Delta^{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}}|U_{\ell}
with order of vanishing greater than
\frac{r\ell}{24}
.

We now have

. It follows from the calculation of

above that

.

Thus we’ve shown

. By Lemma

, we have

for some integer

. Comparing the coefficients of

in the Fourier expansions of

.

The second case is basically identical to the first case. Once again, we have

We can once again compute that under these assumptions,

is congruent to a cusp form in

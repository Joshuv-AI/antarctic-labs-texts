THE LIPSCHITZ SATURATION OF A MODULE
Terence Gaffney and Thiago F. da Silva

Abstract
. In this work we extend the concept of the Lipschitz saturation of an ideal defined in [ 5 ] to the context of modules in some different ways, and we prove they are generically equivalent.

Introduction

The definition of the Lipschitz saturation of an ideal appears in
[
5
]
, in the context of bi-Lipschitz equisingularity. The study of bi-Lipschitz equisingularity was started by Zariski
[
17
]
, Pham and Teissier
[
14
,
18
]
, and was further developed by Lipman
[
16
]
, Mostowski
[
19
,
20
]
, Parusinski
[
21
,
22
]
, Birbrair
[
23
]
and others.

The Lipschitz Saturation and the double of an ideal
I
, denoted
I_{S}
and
I_{D}
, respectively, were defined in
[
5
]
, where
I
is a sheaf of ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, the analytic local ring of an analytic variety
X
. The ideal
I_{S}
consists of elements in
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
whose quotient of its pullback by the blowup-map, with a local generator of the pullback of
I
is Lipschitz. The double
I_{D}
is the submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{2}
generated by
(h\circ\pi_{1},h\circ\pi_{2})
,
h\in I
, where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:X\times X\rightarrow X
are the projections. Theorem 2.3 of
[
5
]
gives an equivalence between
I_{S}
and the integral closure of
I_{D}
.

In
[
3
]
the authors generalize the notion of the double for a sheaf of modules
{\mathcal{M}}
, and the Genericity Theorem is generalized for an arbitrary family of analytic varieties. They also get an interesting decomposition of the stalk of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
, as the direct sum of the stalks
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{x^{\prime}}
, as long
x\neq x^{\prime}
. If
{\mathcal{M}}
is the jacobian module of a family of analytic varieties, the stalks at
x
and
x^{\prime}
determine the tangent hyperplanes at these two points. Since, to control the Lipschitz behavior of the tangent hyperplanes to
X
, it is natural to look for a sheaf on
X\times X
whose stalks determine the tangent hyperplanes at each pair of distinct points, it is natural to consider the double of the jacobian module.

This paper is organized as follows:

In section
1
we recall some basic background material.

In section
2
we present some definitions for the Lipschitz Saturation of a module, inspired by Theorem 2.3 of
[
5
]
, and also motivated by characterizations of the integral closure of modules coming from the integral closure of ideals. Despite all these definitions of Lipschitz saturation agree on the ideal case, we show these notions are not the same anymore in the module case. Nevertheless, we prove all the definitions of Lipschitz saturation of modules are generically equivalent.

In section
3
we prove the Necessity theorem, and we relate the infinitesimal Lipschitz condition A coming from the first Lipschitz saturation with the strongly bi-Lipschitz equisingularity and the Mostowski’s conditions for a Lipschitz stratification. A new proof of the Genericity Theorem arises using the Necessity Theorem and the Mostowski’s results about Lipschitz stratifications.

1.
Background on Lipschitz Saturation of Ideals and Integral Closure of Modules

The Lipschitz saturation of a local ring was defined by Pham and Teissier in
[
14
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
,
SB_{I}(X)
the saturation of the blow-up and
\pi_{S}:SB_{I}(X)\rightarrow X
the projection map. The
Lipschitz saturation
of the ideal
I
is denoted
I_{S}
, and is the ideal
I_{S}:=\{h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}\mid\pi_{S}^{*}(h)\in\pi_{S}^{*}(I)\}
.

Since the normalization of a local ring
A
contains the Lipschitz Saturation of
A
, it follows that
I\subseteq I_{S}\subseteq\overline{I}
. In particular, if
I
is integrally closed then
I_{S}=\overline{I}
.

This definition can be given an equivalent statement using the theory of integral closure of modules. Since Lipschitz conditions depend on controlling functions at two different points as the points come together, we should look for a sheaf defined on
X\times X
. We describe a way of moving from a sheaf of ideals on
X
to a sheaf on
X\times X
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:X\times X\rightarrow X
be the projections to the i-th factor, and let
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
. Define
h_{D}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X,(x,x)}^{2}
as
(h\circ\pi_{1},h\circ\pi_{2})
, called the double of
h
. We define the double of the ideal
I
, denoted
I_{D}
, as the submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X,(x,x)}^{2}
generated by
h_{D}
, where
h
is an element of
I
.

We can see in
[
5
]
, the following result gives a link between Lipschitz saturation and integral closure of modules.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 2.3)
.

Suppose
(X,x)
is a complex analytic set germ,
I\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
and
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
.
Then
h\in I_{S}
if, and only if,
h_{D}\in\overline{I_{D}}
.

Using the Lipschitz saturation of ideals (and doubles), in
[
4
]
, we defined the infinitesimal Lipschitz conditions for hypersurfaces.

Let us recall two results about the integral closure of modules which will inspire good definitions for Lipschitz saturation of modules.

The ideal sheaf
\rho({\mathcal{M}})
on
X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{p-1}
associated to a submodule sheaf
{\mathcal{M}}
of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
(see
[
11
]
)
: Given
h=(h_{1},...,h_{p})\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
and
(x,[T_{1},...,T_{p}])\in X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{p-1}
, with
T_{i}\neq 0
, we define
\rho(h)
as the germ of the analytic map given by
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}h_{j}(z)\frac{T_{j}}{T_{i}}
which is well-defined on a Zariski open subset of
X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{p-1}
that contains the point
(x,[T_{1},...,T_{p}])
. We define
\rho({\mathcal{M}})
as the ideal generated by
\{\rho(h)\mid h\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
. The next result, proved in
[
11
]
, gives a strong relation between the integral closure of modules and ideals.

Proposition 1.3

(
[
11
]
, Proposition 3.4)
.

Let
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
. Then
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
if, and only if,
\rho(h)\in\overline{\rho({\mathcal{M}})}
at all point
(x,[T_{1},...,T_{p}])\in V(\rho({\mathcal{M}}))
.

In
[
6
]
there is another way to make a link between the integral closure of modules and ideals, using minors of a matrix of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a submodule sheaf of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
, and
[{\mathcal{M}}]
a matrix of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
. For each
k
, let
J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
denote the ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
generated by the
k\times k
minors of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
. If
h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
, let
(h,{\mathcal{M}})
be the submodule generated by
h
and
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proposition 1.4

(
[
6
]
, Corollary 1.8)
.

Suppose
(X,x)
is a complex analytic germ with irreducible components
\{V_{i}\}
. Then,
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
if, and only if,
J_{k_{i}}((h,{\mathcal{M}}_{i}))\subseteq\overline{J_{k_{i}}({\mathcal{M}}_{i})}
at
x
, where
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
is the submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{V_{i},x}^{p}
induced from
{\mathcal{M}}
and
k_{i}
is the generic rank of
(h,{\mathcal{M}}_{i})
on
V_{i}
.

Now we recall some basic results about the double of a module developed in
[
3
]
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an analytic space, and let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
. Consider the projection maps
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:X\times X\rightarrow X
. We assume that
{\mathcal{M}}
is finitely generated by global sections.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
. The double of
h
is defined as the element

h_{D}:=(h\circ\pi_{1},h\circ\pi_{2})\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{2p}
.

The double of
{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
, and is defined as the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{2p}
generated by
\{h_{D}\mid h\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
.

Consider
z_{1},...,z_{n}
the coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The next lemma is a useful tool to deal with the double.

Lemma 1.6

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Suppose
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and
h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
. Then:

(1)

(\alpha h)_{D}=-(0,(\alpha\circ\pi_{1}-\alpha\circ\pi_{2})(h\circ\pi_{2}))+(\alpha\circ\pi_{1})h_{D}
;

(2)

(0,(\alpha\circ\pi_{1}-\alpha\circ\pi_{2})(h\circ\pi_{2}))\in{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
, for all
h\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
;

(3)

\alpha\circ\pi_{1}-\alpha\circ\pi_{2}\in I(\Delta(X))=(z_{1}\circ\pi_{1}-z_{1}\circ\pi_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\circ\pi_{1}-z_{n}\circ\pi_{2})
, for all
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
;

(4)

(g+h)_{D}=g_{D}+h_{D}
, for all
g,h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
.

In
[
3
]
we see that it is possible to obtain a set of generators for
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
from a set of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proposition 1.7

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.3)
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
is generated by
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\}
. Then, the following sets are generators of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
:

(1)

{\mathcal{B}}=\{(h_{1})_{D},\ldots,(h_{r})_{D}\}\cup\{(0_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{p}},(z_{i}\circ\pi_{1}-z_{i}\circ\pi_{2})(h_{j}\circ\pi_{2}))

|

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}=\{(h_{1})_{D},\ldots,(h_{r})_{D}\}\cup\{((z_{i}\circ\pi_{1}-z_{i}\circ\pi_{2})(h_{j}\circ\pi_{1}),0_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{p}}))

|

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
.

(3)

{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime\prime}=\{(h_{1})_{D},\ldots,(h_{r})_{D}\}\cup\{(z_{i}h_{j})_{D}

|

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\}
.

The following proposition gives a link between the integral closure of the double of a module and the integral closure of the original module.

Proposition 1.8

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.9)
.

Let
h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
.

(1)

If
h_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
then
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
and
x^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
h_{D}\in({\mathcal{M}}_{D})^{\dagger}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
then
h\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\dagger}
at
x
and
x^{\prime}
.

In the next theorem we compute the generic rank of the double of a module.

Theorem 1.9

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.5)
.

Let
(X,x)
be an irreducible analytic complex germ of dimension
d\geq 1
, and
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
a submodule of generic rank
k
. Then
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
has generic rank
2k
at
(x,x)
.

Corollary 1.10

(
[
3
]
, Corollary 2.6)
.

Let
\{V_{i}\}
be the irreducible components of
(X,x)
. For each
i
, if
{\mathcal{M}}
has generic rank
k_{i}
on
V_{i}
then
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
has generic rank
2k_{i}
on
V_{i}\times V_{i}
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{M}}
has generic rank
k
on each component of
X
then
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
has generic rank
2k
on each component of
X\times X
.

We end this section recalling an important result which states that the double of a sheaf of modules
{\mathcal{M}}
carries all the information at
(x,x^{\prime})
as the stalks of
{\mathcal{M}}
do at
x
and
x^{\prime}
, as long
x\neq x^{\prime}
.

Proposition 1.11

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.11)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
be a sheaf of submodules. Consider

(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X
with
x\neq x^{\prime}
. Then:

{\mathcal{M}}_{D}=({\mathcal{M}}_{x}\circ\pi_{1})\oplus({\mathcal{M}}_{x^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{2})

at
(x,x^{\prime})
.

Proposition
1.11
provides additional motivation for the idea of the double: In order to control the Lipschitz behavior of pairs of tangent planes at two different points
x
and
x^{\prime}
of a family
{\mathcal{X}}
, it is helpful to have each module which determines the tangent hyperplanes at each point as part of the construction. Furthermore, this proposition shows that
JM({\mathcal{X}})_{D}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
contains both
JM({\mathcal{X}})_{x}
and
JM({\mathcal{X}})_{x^{\prime}}
.

2.
The Lipschitz Saturation of a Module

We want extend to modules the notion of Lipchitz saturation that was defined in
[
5
]
for ideals. Motivated by some equivalent descriptions that the Lipschitz saturation has in the ideal case, we define corresponding versions of the Lipschitz saturation for modules, and explore the extent to which they are equivalent.

The main motivation of the next definition is Theorem 2.3 of
[
5
]
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an analytic set and
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
1-Lipschitz saturation of
{\mathcal{M}}
at
x\in X
is denoted by
({\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}})_{x}
, and is defined by

({\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}})_{x}:=\{h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}\mid h_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}\mbox{ at }(x,x)\}.

In the family case, the
1-Lipschitz saturation of
{\mathcal{M}}
at
x\in X
relative to
Y
is defined as above taking the double relative to
Y
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
submodules of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
.

(1)

{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
;

(2)

{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
. In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}
is a reduction of
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
and
e({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}})=0
.

The same result holds in the family case.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be an arbitrary point.

(1) Let
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
and
h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
at
x
. Since
h_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
then by Proposition
1.8
(1) we have that
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
. Thus,
(0,(\alpha\circ\pi_{1}-\alpha\circ\pi_{2})(h\circ\pi_{2}))\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
. Hence

(\alpha h+h^{\prime})_{D}=(\alpha\circ\pi_{1})h_{D}+(0,(\alpha\circ\pi_{1}-\alpha\circ\pi_{2})(h\circ\pi_{2}))+h^{\prime}_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
.

(2) If
h\in{\mathcal{M}}
at
x
then
h_{D}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{D}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
at
(x,x)
, so
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
at
x
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
.

Furthermore, if
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
at
x
then
h_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
at
(x,x)
, and by Proposition
1.8
(1) we have that
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.
∎

In order to define the second Lipschitz saturation, let us fix some notations.

For each
\psi:X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathbb{C}})
,
\psi=(\psi_{1},...,\psi_{p})
and
h=(h_{1},...,h_{p})\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
, we define
\psi\cdot h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
given by
(\psi\cdot h)(z):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\psi_{i}(z)h_{i}(z)
. We define
\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}
as the ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
generated
\{\psi\cdot h\mid h\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(1)

\psi\cdot(\alpha g+h)=\alpha(\psi\cdot g)+(\psi\cdot h)
,
\forall g,h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
and
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{M}}
is generated by
\{h_{1},...,h_{r}\}
then
\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}
is generated by
\{\psi\cdot h_{1},...,\psi\cdot h_{r}\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that (2) is a straightforward consequence of (1). Now, write
g=(g_{1},...,g_{p})
and
h=(h_{1},...,h_{p})
. Then, for every
z
we have
(\psi\cdot(\alpha g+h))(z)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\psi_{i}(z)(\alpha(z)g_{i}(z)+h_{i}(z))=\alpha(z)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\psi_{i}(z)g_{i}(z)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\psi_{i}(z)h_{i}(z)=(\alpha(\psi\cdot g)+(\psi\cdot h))(z)
.
∎

Let us fix some notation for the minors of a matrix. Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
A
be a matrix. If
I=(i_{1},...,i_{k})
and
J=(j_{1},...,j_{k})
are
k
-indexes,
A_{IJ}
is defined as the
k\times k
submatrix of
A
formed by the rows
i_{1},...,i_{k}
and columns
j_{1},...,j_{k}
of
A
. We denote
J_{IJ}(A):=\det(A_{IJ})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
has generic rank
k
in each component of
X
. If
I=(i_{1},...,i_{k})
and
J=(j_{1},...,j_{k})
are indexes with
j_{1}=1
then there exists
\psi:X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathbb{C}})
such that:

(1)

\psi\cdot h=J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})
,
\forall h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
;

(2)

\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

Let us fix a matrix of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
,
[{\mathcal{M}}]=

\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\
g_{1}&\ldots&g_{r}\\
|&&|\end{bmatrix}
. We have
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})=\det[h,{\mathcal{M}}]_{IJ}
, where

[h,{\mathcal{M}}]_{IJ}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{i_{1}}&g_{i_{1},j_{2}-1}&\ldots&g_{i_{1},j_{k}-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
h_{i_{k}}&g_{i_{k},j_{2}-1}&\ldots&g_{i_{k},j_{k}-1}\end{bmatrix}

for all
h=(h_{1},...,h_{p})
. Let
G_{i_{l},j_{s}-1}
be the
(l,s)
-cofactor of
[h,{\mathcal{M}}]_{IJ}
, for all
l,s\in\{1,...,k\}
. Notice that the
(l,1)
-cofactors
G_{i_{l},0}
do not depend of
h
. Then,
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})=\det[h,{\mathcal{M}}]_{IJ}=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}G_{i_{l},0}\cdot h_{i_{l}}
, for all
h
. Take
\psi:X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathbb{C}})
given by
(\psi_{1},...,\psi_{p})
where
\psi_{i_{l}}=G_{i_{l},0}
, for all
l\in\{1,...k\}
, and
\psi_{j}=0
, for every index
j
off
I
. Thus, for all
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
we get
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})=\psi_{I}\cdot h_{I}=\psi\cdot h
.

Now, let
g\in{\mathcal{M}}
arbitrary. Then
(g,{\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{M}}
. By (1) we have
\psi\cdot g=J_{I,J}(g,{\mathcal{M}})\in J_{k}((g,{\mathcal{M}}))=J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
, hence
\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
.
∎

Remark:
It is enough work with indexes
I=(i_{1},...,i_{k})
and
J=(j_{1},...,j_{k})
such that
i_{1}<...<i_{k}
and
j_{1}<...<j_{k}
.

Propositions
1.3
and
1.4
are characterizations for the integral closure of modules using integral closure of ideals. Notice the next proposition is another characterization of the same type.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
and suppose
{\mathcal{M}}
has generic rank
k
on each component of
X
. Then,
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
at
x
if, and only if,
\psi\cdot h\in\overline{\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}}
, at
x
,
\forall\psi:X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

(\Longrightarrow)
It is a straightforward consequence of the curve criterion.

(\Longleftarrow)
The proof now use 1.7 and 1.8 of
[
6
]
.

By these results it is enough to check that
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})\in\overline{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}
, for all indexes
I
and
J
. Write
I=(i_{1},...,i_{k})
and
J=(j_{1},...,j_{k})
. Let
[{\mathcal{M}}]=

\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\
g_{1}&\ldots&g_{r}\\
|&&|\end{bmatrix}
be a matrix of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Write
h=(h_{1},...,h_{p})
. Then,
[h,{\mathcal{M}}]=

\begin{bmatrix}|&|&&|\\
h&g_{1}&\ldots&g_{r}\\
|&|&&|\end{bmatrix}
. If
j_{1}>1
then
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})
is a
k\times k
minor taken only among the generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
, hence
J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}})\in J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})\subseteq\overline{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}
.

Now suppose that
j_{1}=1
. By Lemma
2.4
there exists
\psi:X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\psi\cdot h=J_{I,J}(h,{\mathcal{M}})
and
\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
. By the hypothesis we have
\psi\cdot h\in\overline{\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}}
, hence
J_{I,J}(h,{\mathcal{M}})=\psi\cdot h\in\overline{\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}}}\subseteq\overline{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}
.
∎

Given
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
and

Proposition 2.7

Let

{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}

Proof.

Let

Proposition 2.8

Proposition 2.10

{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}

k

Proof.

Suppose

[
5

(J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}\subseteq\overline{(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}}

(J_{IJ}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}

Suppose

(J_{I,J}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}=(\psi\cdot h)_{D}\in\overline{(\psi\cdot{\mathcal{M}})_{D}}\subseteq\overline{(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}}

∎

The next result shows the third saturation is closely related to the pullback of the saturated blow-up map.

Proposition 2.12
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{M}}
has generic rank
k
on every component of
X
. Let
\pi:SB_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X)\rightarrow B_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X)
,
p:B_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X)\rightarrow X
and
\pi_{S}=p\circ\pi
be the projections maps. Let
h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
. Then:

h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
if and only if
\pi_{S}^{*}(h)\in\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

Fix a set of generators
\{g_{1},...,g_{r}\}
of
{\mathcal{M}}
.
We work at
x^{\prime}\in E
,
E=\pi^{-1}(E_{B})
,
E_{B}
the exceptional divisor of
B_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X)
.

Suppose first that
J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}})\subseteq(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{S}
. Let
\pi_{S}^{*}(J_{I,J}({\mathcal{M}}))
be a local generator of the principal ideal
\pi_{S}^{*}(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))
. Then, by Cramer’s rule we can write

[formula]

(J_{I,J}({\mathcal{M}})\circ\pi_{S})(h_{I}\circ\pi_{S})=\sum\limits_{j\in J}(J_{I,j}(h_{I},{\mathcal{M}}_{I})\circ\pi_{S})(m_{I,j}\circ\pi_{S})

with
m_{j}\in{\mathcal{M}}
. We claim in fact that

(J_{I,J}({\mathcal{M}})\circ\pi_{S})(h\circ\pi_{S})=\sum\limits_{j\in J}(J_{I,j}(h_{I},{\mathcal{M}}_{I})\circ\pi_{S})(m_{j}\circ\pi_{S}).

To see this pick a curve
\phi:({\mathbb{C}},0)\rightarrow(SB_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X),x^{\prime})
and choose
\phi
so that the rank of
\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}})|_{\phi}
is generically
k
. Since by hypothesis
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
then
h\circ\pi_{S}\circ\phi\in\phi^{*}(\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}}))
. So, the element

\star=h\circ\pi_{S}\circ\phi-\sum\limits_{j\in J}(\frac{J_{I,j}(h_{I},{\mathcal{M}}_{I})\circ\pi_{S}}{J_{I,J}({\mathcal{M}})\circ\pi_{S}}\circ\phi)(m_{j}\circ\pi_{S}\circ\phi)\in\phi^{*}(\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}}))

because the above quotients by hypothesis are regular functions on
SB_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X)
. By equation (1), the above element has
0
for the entries indexed by
I
. So, if
\star
is not zero then
\phi^{*}(\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}}))
has rank at least
k+1
, which is a contradiction. Since the images of
\phi
fill up a Z-open set it follows that
\star
is locally zero. Therefore,

h\circ\pi_{S}=\sum\limits_{j\in J}(\frac{J_{I,j}(h_{I},{\mathcal{M}}_{I})\circ\pi_{S}}{J_{I,J}({\mathcal{M}})\circ\pi_{S}})(m_{j}\circ\pi_{S})\in\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}}).

Conversely, suppose that
\pi_{S}^{*}(h)\in\pi_{S}^{*}({\mathcal{M}})
. Then we can write

[formula]

h\circ\pi_{S}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}(g_{j}\circ\pi_{S})

where
\alpha_{j}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}_{B_{J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})}(X),x^{\prime}}
,
\forall j\in\{1,...,r\}
.

Let
c
be an arbitrary generator of
J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}})
. Then we can write
c=\det[h,{\mathcal{M}}]_{IJ}
for some
k
-indexes
I
and
J
. If the
k
-index
J
does not pick the first column of
[h,{\mathcal{M}}]
, then
c
is a
k\times k
minor of the matrix
[{\mathcal{M}}]
, hence
c\in J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})\subseteq(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{S}
and we are done. Thus we may assume that
j_{1}=1
. Then, we can write

c=\det\left[\begin{matrix}h_{I}&(g_{j_{1}})_{I}&...&(g_{j_{k-1}})_{I}\end{matrix}\right].

Applying the pullback of the projection map we have

\pi_{S}^{*}(c)=\det\left[\begin{matrix}h_{I}\circ\pi_{S}&(g_{j_{1}})_{I}\circ\pi_{S}&...&(g_{j_{k-1}})_{I}\circ\pi_{S}\end{matrix}\right].

By the equation (2), if we look only to the entries of the
k
-index
I
, we get

h_{I}\circ\pi_{S}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}((g_{j})_{I}\circ\pi_{S}).

Using the linearity of the determinant in the first column, we conclude that
\pi_{S}^{*}(c)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{j}(v_{j}\circ\pi_{S})
, where
v_{j}:=\det\left[\begin{matrix}(g_{j})_{I}&(g_{j_{1}})_{I}&...&(g_{j_{k-1}})_{I}\end{matrix}\right],
for all
j\in\{1,..,r\}
. Clearly,
v_{j}\in J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})
,
\forall j\in\{1,...,r\}
. Hence,
\pi_{S}^{*}(c)\in\pi_{S}^{*}(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))
and by the definition of the Lipschitz saturation of an ideal, we conclude that
c\in(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{S}
.
∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
with generic rank

on each component of

,

and

. Suppose that there exists an ideal

of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X,(x,x)}^{2p}
such that

IJ_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\subseteq\overline{J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})}

J_{2k}(h_{D},{\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq\overline{IJ_{2}((J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D})}

at

h\in({\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}})_{x}\Leftrightarrow h\in({\mathcal{M}}_{S_{2}})_{x}\Leftrightarrow h\in({\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}})_{x}

Proof.

The first implication follows from Proposition
2.8
. The second one follows from Proposition
2.11
.

Suppose
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
at

. Then,
(J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}\subseteq\overline{(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}}

\overline{(J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}}=\overline{(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}}

\phi^{*}((J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D})=\phi^{*}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})

\phi^{*}(J_{2k}(h_{D},{\mathcal{M}}_{D}))\subseteq\phi^{*}(I\cdot J_{2}((J_{k}(h,{\mathcal{M}}))_{D}))\\
=\phi^{*}(I)\cdot J_{2}(\phi^{*}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}))=\phi^{*}(I\cdot J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}))\subseteq\phi^{*}(J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D}))

By Theorem 2.9 of
[

1.4

. ∎

Lemma 2.15

Let

(z_{t_{1}}-z_{t_{1}}^{\prime})...(z_{t_{k}}-z_{t_{k}}^{\prime})\det({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ})\det({\mathcal{M}}_{KL}^{\prime})\in J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x)

k

Write
{\mathcal{M}}_{KL}=(g_{rs})

\left[\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}&|&0_{k\times k}\\
--------&&------------\\

The next result gives us the first condition of Proposition

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
be a submodule and

a)

I_{\Delta}^{k}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\subseteq J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})

is principal then

at

Proof.

(a) We have that

[(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}]=\left[\begin{matrix}\det({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ})&...&0\\
\det({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}^{\prime})&...&(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})\det({\mathcal{M}}_{KL}^{\prime})\\
\end{matrix}\right]

varying the
k
-indexes

and

. Thus, the desired inclusion is a straightforward consequence of the previous lemma.

(b) Since

is principal then there exist
k
-indexes

such that

and

is generated by

.Thus we can write

[(J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D}]=\left[\begin{matrix}g&0\\
g^{\prime}&(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})g^{\prime}\\
\end{matrix}\right]

varying

[(z_{t_{1}}-z_{t_{1}}^{\prime})...(z_{t_{k-1}}-z_{t_{k-1}}^{\prime})].[g.g^{\prime}(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})]\in J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D}),

Therefore,

∎

Lemma 2.17
.

{\mathcal{M}}
be an

{\mathcal{J}}_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x).

Proof.

It suffices to prove that each generator of
{\mathcal{J}}_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
belongs to

The columns of

-indexes

The next result ensures that any free submodule satisfies the second condition of Proposition

Lemma 2.18

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
of rank
k
. Then

J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x).

Proof.

We have that

[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]=\left[\begin{matrix}[{\mathcal{M}}]_{p\times k}&|&0\\
------&|&-------\\
[{\mathcal{M}}]^{\prime}_{p\times k}&|&(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})[{\mathcal{M}}]^{\prime}\par\end{matrix}\right]

varying
i\in\{1,...,n\}
.

Let
d\in J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
at
(x,x)
be an arbitrary generator. Then
d=\det N
where
N
is a
2k\times 2k
submatrix of
[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]
.

(i) Suppose first that there are
k+t
columns of
N
taken on the part

\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})[{\mathcal{M}}]^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]

of
[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]
, with
1\leq t\leq k
. Then we can write

N=\left[\begin{matrix}({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ})_{k\times(k-t)}&|&0_{k\times({k+t})}\\
--------&|&--------\\
({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ})^{\prime}_{k\times(k-t)}&|&(\tilde{{\mathcal{M}}})_{KL}\end{matrix}\right]

=\left[\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}&0_{k\times t}&|&0_{k\times k}\\
---&---&|&------\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}^{\prime}&*&|&**\end{matrix}\right]

for some matrices * and **, where ** is a square matrix of size
k\times k
. So in this case we have that
d=\det N=\det\left[\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}&0_{k\times t}\end{matrix}\right].\det(**)=0\in I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})
.

(ii) Now suppose that we have exactly
k
columns of
N
taken on the part

\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
(z_{i}-z_{i}^{\prime})[{\mathcal{M}}]^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]

of
[{\mathcal{M}}_{D}]
. Then we can write

N=\left[\begin{matrix}{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}&|&0_{k\times k}\\
------&|&------\\
{\mathcal{M}}_{IJ}^{\prime}&|&\tilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{KL}\end{matrix}\right].

Thus,
d=\det({\mathcal{M}}_{IJ})\det(\tilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{KL})\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})
and by previous lemma we conclude that
d\in I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous lemma, we have a result which states the second condition of Proposition
2.14
holds locally in a dense Zariski open subset of
X
in a sheaf of submodules of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
of generic rank
k
on each component of
X
. Then there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U
of
X
such that
U\cap V
is a dense Zariski open subset of
V
,
\forall V
component of
X
,
J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
and

J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x),

for all
x\in U
.

Proof.

Consider
[{\mathcal{M}}]
a matrix of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Using the Cramer’s rule, we can choose
k

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear independents columns of
[{\mathcal{M}}]
such that these columns generates
{\mathcal{M}}
in a such dense Zariski open subset
U
of
X
and
J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
along
U
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{k}
be the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
generated by the columns chosen above. Thus, given
x\in U
, we have that
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}=({\mathcal{M}}_{k})_{x}
is a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
of rank

and the desired inclusion is a consequence of the previous lemma.
∎

Before we state the main theorem of this section, we prove that the generic rank of
(h,{\mathcal{M}})
is the same as the generic rank of
{\mathcal{M}}
, provided
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{p}
of generic rank
k
on each component of
X
. If
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
then
(h,{\mathcal{M}})
also has generic rank
k
on each component of
X
.

Proof.

Since
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{x}}
then
h\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{x^{\prime}}}
, for all
x^{\prime}
in an open neighborhood
U
of
x
in
X
. Suppose the rank of
(h,{\mathcal{M}})
is
k+1
. Then
h(x^{\prime})\notin{\mathcal{M}}(x^{\prime})
. But,
x^{\prime}
is a constant curve, so
h\notin\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{x^{\prime}}}
, contradiction.
∎

The next theorem gives us the desired equivalence.

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
of generic rank
k
on each component of
X
. Then there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U
of
X
such that

{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}={\mathcal{M}}_{S_{2}}={\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}

along
U
.

Proof.

Since
X\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is an analytic complex variety then
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is a noetherian sheaf of rings, hence
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
is a noetherian sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules. Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
is a sheaf of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules then
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
is finitely generated by global sections
h_{1},...,h_{r}
. Since
h_{i}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
,
\forall i\in\{1,...,r\}
then by previous lemma
(h_{i},{\mathcal{M}})
also has generic rank
k
on each component of
X
,
\forall i\in\{1,...,r\}
. By Lemma
2.19
, for each
i\in\{1,...,r\}
there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U_{i}
of
X
such that
U_{i}\cap V
is a dense Zariski open subset of
V
, for all
V
component of
X
, and

J_{2k}((h_{i})_{D},{\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}(h_{i},{\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x),

\forall x\in U_{i}
.

Also by Lemma
2.19
there exists a dense Zariski open subset
U_{0}
of
X
such that
U_{0}\cap V
is a dense Zariski open subset of
V
, for all component
V
of
X
, and
J_{k}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
which is principal at
x
,
\forall x\in U_{0}
. From Lemma
2.16
(b), we conclude that

I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\subseteq J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x),

\forall x\in U_{0}
.

Take
U:=\bigcap_{j=0}^{r}U_{j}
. Then
U
is a dense Zariski open subset of
X
and
U\cap V
is a dense Zariski open subset of
V
, for all
V
component of
X
.

We already know that
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{2}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
at any point of
X
, in particular, along
U
. Let us prove that
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
along
U
. In fact, let
x\in U
. Given an arbitrary
i\in\{1,...,r\}
, since
x\in U_{0}
and
x\in U_{i}
then:

—

I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}({\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\subseteq J_{2k}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x)
;

—

J_{2k}((h_{i})_{D},{\mathcal{M}}_{D})\subseteq I_{\Delta}^{k-1}J_{2}((J_{k}(h_{i},{\mathcal{M}}))_{D})\mbox{ at }(x,x)
.

Since
h_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
at
x
then by Proposition
2.14
we have that
h_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
at
x
,
\forall i\in\{1,...,r\}
. Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
at
x
is generated by
h_{1},...,h_{r}
at

, then
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
at

.
∎

In the next example we show that in general
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
is not a submodule of
{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
.

Example 2.22
.

Consider the submodule
{\mathcal{M}}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{2}^{2}
given by

[{\mathcal{M}}]=\left[\begin{matrix}x&0&y\\
y&x&0\end{matrix}\right]
and let

.

Thus,
J_{2}({\mathcal{M}})=\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle=J_{2}(h,{\mathcal{M}})
. In particular,
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{3}}
.

Consider the curve
\Phi:({\mathbb{C}},0)\rightarrow({\mathbb{C}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2},(0,0))
given by
\Phi(t)=(t,\alpha t,t,\beta t)
.

We have that

[\Phi^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{D})]=\left[\begin{matrix}t&0&\alpha t&0&0&0\\
\alpha t&t&0&0&0&0\\
t&0&\beta t&(\alpha-\beta)t^{2}&0&\beta(\alpha-\beta)t^{2}\\
\beta t&t&0&\beta(\alpha-\beta)t^{2}&(\alpha-\beta)t^{2}&0\end{matrix}\right]

and
[\Phi^{*}(h_{D})]=\left[\begin{matrix}t\\
3\alpha t\\
t\\
3\beta t\end{matrix}\right]
. Let
D
be the first
4\times 3
submatrix of
[\Phi^{*}(M_{D})]
. The complementar submatrix has degree 2, so we can ignore its terms. Notice that:

[\Phi^{*}(h_{D})]-D\cdot e_{1}=\left[\begin{matrix}0\\

. Therefore,

.

3.
The Necessity Theorem

In this section we prove the Necessity theorem, and we relate the infinitesimal Lipschitz condition coming from the first Lipschitz saturation with the strongly bi-Lipschitz equisingularity and the Mostowski’s conditions for a Lipschitz stratification.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an analytic variety. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
be the sheaf of locally Lipschitz functions on
X
. Clearly
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is a subsheaf of
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule. We define
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
as the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
given by
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}:={\mathcal{L}}_{X}{\mathcal{M}}\cap{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
. The module
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
is called
strong Lipschitz saturation of
\mathbf{{\mathcal{M}}}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Necessity Theorem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
generated by global sections
\{h_{1},...,h_{r}\}
,
x\in X
. If
h\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}
at
x
then
h_{D}\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}_{D}}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
, for any
x^{\prime}\in X
. In particular,
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{S_{1}}
.

Proof.

The generators of generators of
{\mathcal{M}}_{D}
are given by the set

B=\{(h_{i})_{D}\}_{i=1}^{r}\cup\{(0,(z_{j}\circ\pi_{1}-z_{j}\circ\pi_{2})(h_{i}\circ\pi_{2}))\}_{i,j=1}^{r,n}.

We will use the criterion for integral closure of modules obtained in Proposition 1.11 of
[
6
]
. For each
i\in\{1,...,r\}
we can write

h_{i}=(h_{i1},...,h_{ip})
.

Let
\varphi:X\times X\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{2p},{\mathbb{C}})
be an arbitrary global section of
\Gamma(\mbox{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}^{2p},{\mathbb{C}}),X\times X)
with components
\varphi_{1},...,\varphi_{2p}
.

For each
(z,z^{\prime})\in X\times X
let

M(z,z^{\prime}):=\sup\{{\parallel}\varphi(z,z^{\prime})\cdot s(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}\mid s\in B\}.

Since
h\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
then
h_{D}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}^{2p}
. By hypothesis, for each
i\in\{1,...,r\}
there exists
C_{i}>0
such that

{\parallel}\alpha_{i}(z)-\alpha_{i}(z^{\prime}){\parallel}\leq C_{i}\underset{j\in\{1,...,n\}}{\sup}\{{\parallel}z_{j}-z^{\prime}_{j}{\parallel}\}

\forall(z,z^{\prime})\in X\times X
.

Take
C_{0}:=\max\{C_{1},...,C_{r}\}
. For each
i\in\{1,...,r\}
,
\alpha_{i}
is locally bounded, so there exists
D_{i}>0
and
U_{i}
neighborhood of
x
in
X
such that
{\parallel}\alpha_{i}(z){\parallel}\leq D_{i}
,
\forall z\in U_{i}
. Take
U:=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}U_{i}
and
D:=\max\{D_{1},...,D_{r}\}
.

Let
(z,z^{\prime})\in U\times X
be arbitrary. Then:
{\parallel}\varphi(z,z^{\prime})\cdot h_{D}(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}{\parallel}\alpha_{i}(z){\parallel}{\parallel}\varphi(z,z^{\prime})\cdot(h_{i})_{D}(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}{\parallel}\alpha_{i}(z)-\alpha_{i}(z^{\prime}){\parallel}{\parallel}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{p}\varphi_{p+\ell}(z,z^{\prime})h_{i\ell}(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}\\
\leq DrM(z,z^{\prime})+C_{0}r\underset{\begin{smallmatrix}i\in\{1,...,r\}\\
j\in\{1,...,n\}\end{smallmatrix}}{\sup}\{{\parallel}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{p}\varphi_{p+\ell}(z,z^{\prime})(z_{j}-z^{\prime}_{j})h_{i\ell}(z^{\prime}){\parallel}\}\\
\leq(Dr+C_{0}r)M(z,z^{\prime})
. Taking
C:=C_{0}r+Dr>0
we just proved that

{\parallel}\varphi(z,z^{\prime})\cdot h_{D}(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}\leq C\underset{s\in B}{\sup}\{{\parallel}\varphi(z,z^{\prime})\cdot s(z,z^{\prime}){\parallel}\}

for all
(z,z^{\prime})\in U\times X
, where
B
is a generator set of
M_{D}
and
U\times X
is a neighborhood of
(x,x^{\prime})
in
X\times X
. Therefore,
h_{D}\in\overline{M_{D}}
at
(x,x^{\prime})
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If the elements of
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}
were meromorphic functions the above theorem would follow easily. However we assume nothing about them other than that they are Lipschitz.

Remark 3.4
.

The Necessity Theorem still works for the real analytic case. One can use the Proposition 4.2 of
[
6
]
and the proof is the same.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}
be an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-submodule of
{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{p}}
. Then,
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
and, in particular,
e({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{L}}})=0
.

In
[
1
]
the authors defined the notion of
strongly bi-Lipschitz equisingularity
for a family of analytic complex functions.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
U
be a connected open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
and let
(X,0)\subseteq({\mathbb{C}}^{n},0)
be a germ of an analytic variety. Consider a family of analytic functions
F:X\times U\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
with
F(0,y)=0
and
f_{y}(z)=F(z,y)
. We say that
F
is
strongly bi-Lipschitz equisingular (sLe)
if there exists a continuous map
v:X\times{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
v_{y}(z):=v(z,y)
, such that each vector field
v_{y}:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is Lipschitz,
\forall y\in U
and

\frac{\partial f_{y}}{\partial y}(z)=D(f_{y})_{z}(v_{y}(z))

for every
y\in U
and
z
near to the origin in
X
. Notice the last equation can be rewritten as

\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\frac{\partial F}{\partial z_{i}}

where
v_{1},...,v_{n}
are the components of
v
.

So the sLe condition is equivalent to asking that the partial derivative of
F
is an element of the strong Lipschitz saturation of the jacobian ideal
J(F)
.

In
[
7
]
the author defined a condition for a family of functions analogous to the
iL_{A}
condition for sets (see
[
3
,
4
]
).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a family of analytic fucntions with
F(0,y)=0
and
f_{y}(z)=F(z,y)
. Denote the parameter space
Y=0\times{\mathbb{C}}
and assume that
Y
is the singular locus of
F
. Here we work with the double relative to
Y
, i.e, defined by the projections
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
restricted to the fiber product
({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}})\underset{Y}{\times}({\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}})
. We say that
F
is
infinitesimally Lipschitz equisingular (iLe)
at a point of the fiber product if

(JM(F)_{Y})_{D}\subseteq\overline{(JM_{z}(F))_{D}}

at this point.

Using the Necessity Theorem, we get the following

Corollary 3.8
.

If
F:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a strongly bi-Lipschitz equisingular family of functions then
F
is infinitesimally Lipschitz equisingular.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an analytic variety. If
S
is a stratum of the Mostowski stratification of
X
then the
iL_{A}
condition holds along
S
.

Example 3.10

(Fernandes and Ruas example)
.

Consider the family of functions given by
F(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{3}x^{3}-t^{2}xy^{3n-2}+y^{3n}
. In
[
2
]
the authors proved this family is not strongly bi-Lipschitz trivial. Here we use our criterion to get the same conclusion. In fact, we have:

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=-2txy^{3n-2}

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}=x^{2}-t^{2}y^{3n-2}

\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}=(2-3n)t^{2}xy^{3n-3}+3ny^{3n-1}
.

Let us prove that
iL_{A}
does not hold for the family
F
. Consider the curve
\phi(t)=(ty^{\frac{3n-2}{2}},y,t,-ty^{\frac{3n-2}{2}},y,t)
,
y=t^{2}
, which is formed by the two parametrizations
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
of the two branches of the polar curve defined by
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}=0
.

Since
(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x})_{D}\circ\phi=(0,0)
and
y\circ\phi_{1}-y\circ\phi_{2}=0
then
\phi^{*}((J_{z}(F))_{D})
has two generators
(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{1},\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{2})
and
(0,(x\circ\phi_{1}-x\circ\phi_{2})(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{2}))
. Thus, if
F
satisfies
iL_{A}
then we nust have

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\circ\phi_{2}-\left((\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\circ\phi_{1})\left(\frac{\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{2}}{\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{1}}\right)\right)\in((\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\circ\phi_{2})(x\circ\phi_{1}-x\circ\phi_{2}))
. This implies that

2t^{2}y^{\frac{9n-6}{2}}\left(1+\frac{3n+(3n-2)t^{3}y^{\frac{3n-6}{2}}}{3n+(2-3n)t^{3}y^{\frac{3n-6}{2}}}\right)\in(ty^{\frac{9n-4}{2}})=(ty^{\frac{9n-6}{2}}.y)

which is a contradiction. Hence,
F
does not satisfy
iL_{A}
condition. Hence,
F
is not strongly bi-Lipschitz trivial.

Remark 3.11
.

Since Mostowski showed that any complex analytic has a Mostowski stratification, when coupled with our necessity result, this gives another proof of the genericity of the
iL_{A}
condition.

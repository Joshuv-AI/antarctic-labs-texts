A conservative stochastic Dirac-Klein-Gordon system
Evgueni Dinvay, Sigmund Selberg

Abstract
. Considered herein is a particular nonlinear dispersive stochastic system consisting of Dirac and Klein-Gordon equations. They are coupled by nonlinear terms due to the Yukawa interaction. We consider a case of homogeneous multiplicative noise that seems to be very natural from the perspective of the least action formalism. We are able to show existence and uniqueness of a corresponding Cauchy problem in Bourgain spaces. Moreover, the regarded model implies charge conservation, known for the deterministic analogue of the system, and this is used to prove a global existence result for suitable initial data.

1.
Introduction

Consideration is given to the following
Dirac-Klein-Gordon equations, one-dimensional in the space variable,
containing homogeneous multiplicative noise of the Stratonovich type,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(-i\partial_{t}-i\alpha\partial_{x}+M\beta\right)\psi&=\phi\beta\psi+\beta\psi\xi_{1},\\
\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}+m^{2}\right)\phi&=\psi^{*}\beta\psi+\phi\xi_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with initial data

\psi(0,x,\omega)=\psi_{0}(x,\omega),\qquad\phi(0,x,\omega)=\phi_{0}(x,\omega),\qquad\partial_{t}\phi(0,x,\omega)=\phi_{1}(x,\omega).

(1.2)

The unknowns are random processes
\psi(t,x,\omega)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\phi(t,x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}
, for
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega
in a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Here
m,M\geqslant 0
are constants and
the
2\times 2
Dirac matrices
\alpha,\beta
satisfy

\alpha=\alpha^{*},\,\beta=\beta^{*},\,\alpha^{2}=\beta^{2}=I

and

\alpha\beta+\beta\alpha=0.

For simplicity we choose the particular representation

\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let
W
be the cylindrical Wiener process defined by
a complete orthonormal sequence

\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

in

L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

and a sequence

\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

of independent real-valued Brownian motions on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is an associated filtration of
\mathcal{F}
.
We assume that the noise is of the form

\xi_{j}=\frac{d\mathcal{W}_{j}}{dt},\qquad\mathcal{W}_{j}=\mathfrak{K}_{j}W\qquad(j=1,2),

where the
\mathfrak{K}_{j}
are convolution operators

\mathfrak{K}_{j}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{k}_{j}(x-y)f(y)dy

(1.3)

with real-valued kernels
\mathfrak{k}_{j}\in H^{\sigma_{j}}(\mathbb{R})
. Here
\sigma_{j}\geq 0
will be chosen depending on the Sobolev regularity of the initial data.

Interpreting the stochastic integrals in the Stratonovich sense, we can then write (
1.1
) as

\left\{\begin{gathered}d\psi=(-\alpha\partial_{x}-iM\beta)\psi\,dt+i\phi\beta\psi\,dt+i\beta\psi\mathfrak{K}_{1}\circ dW,\\
d\phi=\dot{\phi}\,dt,\\
d\dot{\phi}=(\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2})\phi\,dt+\psi^{*}\beta\psi\,dt+\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}\circ dW,\end{gathered}\right.

(1.4)

where
\dot{\phi}=\partial\phi/\partial t
and
\psi\mathfrak{K}_{1}
,
\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}
are understood as compositions of the convolution operators
\mathfrak{K}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{K}_{2}
with the multiplication operators given by
\psi
,
\phi
. Thus

(\psi\mathfrak{K}_{1})f(x)=\psi(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{k}_{1}(x-y)f(y)dy

and similarly for
\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}
. Depending on the regularity of the initial data, the Sobolev regularity
\sigma_{j}
of the kernel
\mathfrak{k}_{j}
will be chosen so that the above compositions are Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
into suitable Sobolev spaces.

By introducing noise in the Stratonovich sense we respect two of the key physical
properties of the original deterministic Dirac-Klein-Gordon system:
the principle of least action and the conservation of the charge,
\int\left|\psi(t,x)\right|^{2}\,dx
.

From an analysis perspective it is more convenient to work
with the Itô stochastic integral. The Itô form of the above system is

\left\{\begin{gathered}d\psi=(-\alpha\partial_{x}-iM\beta)\psi\,dt+i\phi\beta\psi\,dt-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\psi\,dt+i\beta\psi\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW,\\
d\phi=\dot{\phi}\,dt,\\
d\dot{\phi}=(\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2})\phi\,dt+\psi^{*}\beta\psi\,dt+\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW,\end{gathered}\right.

(1.5)

where
M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}=(1/2)\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. To see this, write (
1.4
) in the abstract form

dX=AXdt+\mathcal{N}(X)dt+\mathcal{M}(X)\circ dW,

where

X=\begin{pmatrix}\psi\\
\phi\\
\dot{\phi}\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha\partial_{x}-iM\beta&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{M}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}i\beta\psi\mathfrak{K}_{1}\\
0\\
\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, at least formally, the corresponding Itô form is (see e.g.
[
19
]
)

dX=AXdt+\left(\mathcal{N}(X)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\mathcal{M}_{k}(X)\right)\right)\,dt+\mathcal{M}(X)dW,

where
\mathcal{M}_{k}(X)=\mathcal{M}(X)e_{k}
and we calculate

\mathcal{M}_{k}\left(\mathcal{M}_{k}(X)\right)=\begin{pmatrix}(i\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{k}\beta)^{2}\psi\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-(\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{k})^{2}\psi\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}

and note that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{k}\left(\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{k}(x)\right)^{2}=\sum_{k}\left\langle\,\mathfrak{k}_{1}(x-\cdot),e_{k}\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Parseval’s identity. Formally, this verifies the conversion from (
1.4
) to (
1.5
).

Our aim is to prove existence and uniqueness for (
1.5
)
with initial data
(\psi,\phi,\dot{\phi})(0)=(\psi_{0},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
in the spaces

\psi_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)\right),\qquad\phi_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,H^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right),\qquad\phi_{1}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,H^{r-1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\right),

(1.6)

for a certain range of Sobolev indices
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, using the charge conservation we will prove global existence when
s=0

and
1/4<r<1/2
,
under the additional assumption that
\psi_{0}\in L^{p}\left(\Omega,L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right)

for a sufficiently large
p\geq 4
, depending on
r
.

Assuming for the moment that
m>0
, then by a rescaling we may take
m=1
. Applying the linear transformation
(\psi,\phi,\dot{\phi})\mapsto(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+},\phi_{-})
given by

\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{+}\\
\psi_{-}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\phi=\phi_{+}+\phi_{-},\qquad\phi_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi\pm\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}i\dot{\phi}\right),

where
D_{x}=-i\partial_{x}
and
\left\langle\,\cdot\,\right\rangle=\left(1+\left|\cdot\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
,
the Cauchy problem (
1.5
), (
1.6
) then transforms to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-id\psi_{+}+D_{x}\psi_{+}\,dt=-M\psi_{-}\,dt+\phi\psi_{-}\,dt+\psi_{-}\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW+iM_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\psi_{+}\,dt,\\
&-id\psi_{-}-D_{x}\psi_{-}\,dt=-M\psi_{+}\,dt+\phi\psi_{+}\,dt+\psi_{+}\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW+iM_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\psi_{-}\,dt,\\
&-id\phi_{+}+\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle\phi_{+}dt=+\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\re\left(\overline{\psi_{+}}\psi_{-}\right)dt+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW,\\
&-id\phi_{-}-\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle\phi_{-}dt=-\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\re\left(\overline{\psi_{+}}\psi_{-}\right)dt-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

with

\psi_{\pm}(0)=f_{\pm}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\right),\qquad\phi_{\pm}(0)=g_{\pm}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,H^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\right),\qquad\overline{g_{+}}=g_{-}.

(1.8)

Here
\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\phi\mathfrak{K}_{2}
is understood as a composition of operators. We remark that
\overline{\phi_{+}}=\phi_{-}
. Thus
\phi=\phi_{+}+\overline{\phi_{+}}
and it suffices to solve for
\psi_{+}
,
\psi_{-}
and
\phi_{+}
.

, and this opened the way for improvements in the low-regularity local well-posedness theory by using Bourgain’s Fourier restriction norm spaces, see

,

and the references therein. Global existence in space dimension

was proved in

, and for

in

]
for small data. Global existence below the charge in space dimension

The optimal low-regularity result for the deterministic case in space dimension

was obtained in
[
26
]
; it states that the problem is locally well posed for

and

, and that this range is optimal since some form of ill-posedness holds outside it. In the present work we are primarily interested in getting a global result for

in the presence of noise, and not so much in reaching the lowest possible regularity. Therefore, we restrict attention to the range

, which corresponds to the results in

for the deterministic case. In those papers, the local existence proof is based on contraction in Bourgain spaces

, and that is also the approach we follow. However, the presence of noise introduces some further technical issues that have to be dealt with, including the fact that one has to work with

(instead of

in the deterministic case), and that one has to introduce cutoffs to deal with the lack of uniform bounds with respect to the probabilistic variable
\omega
.
In order to deal with these issues and obtain local well-posedness of the stochastic extension
(

developed in

and based on analysis in Bourgain spaces.
To prove global existence we take advantage of the charge conservation,

and that is why we need a Stratonovich noise,

We work in Bourgain spaces of low time regularity (

In the classical field theory one can determine the equations of motion
by the principle of least action.
As we restrict ourselves to the one dimensional space,
the action is an integral functional

\mathcal{S}=\int\mathcal{L}dtdx,

where the Lagrangian density, depending on the field and time,
is defined by the physical system under consideration.
The deterministic analogue of Equations (
1.1
)
is related to a particular choice of
the density
\mathcal{L}(\psi,\phi,t)
,
as explained in

[
5
,
32
]
.
We recall very briefly the corresponding physical background
and show how the noise can be naturally introduced here.
In particle physics, the Yukawa interaction
[
32
]

explains how forces between nucleons are mediated
by massive particles called mesons.
Mathematically, this is described by the action integral

\mathcal{S}(\psi,\phi)

defined by the Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}(\psi,\phi)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\psi)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{meson}}(\phi)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}(\psi,\phi).

Here
\psi
is a spinor field (the fermion field) and

\phi
is a real scalar field (the meson field)
whose free-field dynamics are determined by the Lagrangians

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\psi)=\psi^{*}\left(i\partial_{t}+i\alpha\partial_{x}-M\beta\right)\psi,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{meson}}(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2},

corresponding to the free Dirac and Klein-Gordon equations. Here

m,M\geq 0
are masses and

\psi^{*}
denotes the complex conjugate transpose of
\psi
.
The interaction is determined by the Yukawa coupling term

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}(\psi,\phi)=\phi\psi^{*}\beta\psi.

The corresponding system of Euler-Lagrange equations
is the deterministic Dirac-Klein-Gordon system.
We introduce the noise by adding

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{noise}}(\psi,\phi,x,t)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{\mathrm{noise}}(\psi,x,t)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{meson}}^{\mathrm{noise}}(\phi,x,t)=\psi^{*}\beta\psi\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2}\xi_{2}.

One can think about
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}^{\mathrm{noise}}

as stochastic fluctuations of the initial Dirac potential

M\psi^{*}\beta\psi.

Similarly,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{meson}}^{\mathrm{noise}}

represents a noisy extension of the potential

m^{2}\phi^{2}/2

in the Klein-Gordon model.
Thus using the new Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}(\psi,\phi,x,t)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}(\psi)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{meson}}(\phi)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}(\psi,\phi)+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{noise}}(\psi,\phi,x,t)

one arrives at a stochastic variational principle
leading to the system (
1.1
).
Note that the Stratonovich calculus obeys normal differentiation rules,
and so the derivation of the Euler-Lagrange equations
works out as in the deterministic case.

2.1.
Random variables

We fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
admitting an independent sequence
\{B_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathbb{E}(X)=\int_{\Omega}X(\omega)\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)

A stochastic process
X(t)
, defined on a time interval
I=[S,T]
or
I=[S,\infty)
, where
S\geq 0
, and taking values in a separable Hilbert space
H
, is said to be

(or just

is
(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathcal{B}_{H})

-measurable for all

. Here
\mathcal{B}_{H}
denotes the Borel

-algebra of

.

A process

is a

of

a.s.
We assume that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains all sets in

with measure zero,
so that any modification of an adapted process is itself adapted.
Moreover, the filtration is supposed to be right-continuous,
i.e.

\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal{F}_{s}=\mathcal{F}_{t}

for any
t\geq 0
.

The process

is

if for each

the map

, from

Progressive measurability implies adaptedness (see

), and the converse holds if the process has continuous paths (see
[

or Lemma

If

[S,T]
into

\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega,Z)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega,Z)\colon\text{$u$ is progressively measurable}\right\}.

2.2.
Stopping times

A
stopping time
is a random variable
\tau\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty]
such that for all
t\geq 0
, the set

\{\tau\leq t\}=\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\tau(\omega)\leq t\}

is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable. Then also
\{\tau<t\}
,
\{\tau>t\}
and
\{\tau\geq t\}
have this property, of course. Note that any constant
\tau\geq 0
is a stopping time.

In the next three lemmas we establish some facts about stopping times.
We only consider strictly positive stopping times.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\tau\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
be a stopping time. Then the set

E=\left\{(s,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times\Omega\colon 0\leq s<\tau(\omega)\right\}

belongs to the product
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}_{[0,\infty)}\otimes\mathcal{F}
. For any
T>0
, the set

E_{T}=E\cap\left([0,T]\times\Omega\right)

belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{[0,T]}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{T}
.

Proof.

Let
A_{n}
be the set of numbers
iT/2^{n}
,
i=0,\dots,2^{n}
. Then

E_{T}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{t\in A_{n}}[0,t]\times\{t<\tau\}
,
and of course
E=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}E_{N}
.
∎

As a consequence of this lemma, if
u
is a random variable defined on
E
(so it is defined up to the time
\tau
), then it makes sense to ask whether
u
is
\mathcal{B}_{[0,\infty)}\otimes\mathcal{F}
-measurable. That is, whether the trivial extension of
u
has this property. Similarly, one can ask whether
u
restricted to
E_{t}
is
\mathcal{B}_{[0,t]}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for all
t\geq 0
, which amounts to progressive measurability of the trivial extension. The next lemma gives sufficient conditions for this to hold.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\tau\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
be a stopping time, and let
E
and
E_{t}
, for
t\geq 0
, be as in Lemma
1
. Let
u\colon E\to H
, where
H
is a separable Hilbert space. Assume that
u
has continuous paths, in the sense that

\text{$t\mapsto u(t,\omega)$ is continuous on $[0,\tau(\omega))$, for each $\omega$},

(2.2)

and assume that
u
is adapted, in the sense that, for each
t\geq 0
such that
\left\{t<\tau\right\}
is non-empty, we have

\omega\mapsto u(t,\omega)
, defined for
\omega\in\{t<\tau\}
, is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.

(2.3)

Then
u|_{E_{t}}
is
\mathcal{B}_{[0,t]}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for all
t\geq 0
. In other words, the trivial extension of

Proof.

Let
U=\mathbb{1}_{E}u
be the trivial extension of
u
to
[0,\infty)\times\Omega
. Then (
2.3
) says that
U(t)
is adapted for every
t\geq 0
. Now fix
t\geq 0
. For

let
t_{i}=it/2^{n}
for

, and define

Then

is
\mathcal{B}_{[0,t]}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable by the adaptedness of

, and (
2.2
) implies that

converges pointwise to

(and therefore in
[0,t]\times\Omega

∎

.

Let

\tau_{R}\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
by

\tau_{R}(\omega)=\sup\left\{t\in[0,\tau(\omega))\colon\text{$f(s,\omega)<R$ for $0\leq s\leq t$}\right\}.

Then
\tau_{R}
is a stopping time. Moreover, for each
\omega
,

\lim_{R\to\infty}\tau_{R}(\omega)=\tau(\omega),

(2.6)

\text{$0\leq t\leq\tau_{R}(\omega)$ and $t<\tau(\omega)$}\implies f(t,\omega)\leq R

(2.7)

and

\tau_{R}(\omega)<\tau(\omega)\implies f(\tau_{R}(\omega),\omega)=R.

(2.8)

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
.
Note that, since
\tau_{R}\leq\tau
,

\left\{\tau_{R}>t\right\}=\left\{\tau_{R}>t\right\}\cap\left\{\tau>t\right\}.

Using (
2.4
),
and the compactness of the interval
[0,t]
,
we write

\left\{\tau_{R}>t\right\}\cap\left\{\tau>t\right\}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,t)}\left(\left\{f(s,\cdot)<R-\frac{1}{n}\right\}\cap\left\{\tau>t\right\}\right),

which is evidently
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
Indeed, for any
\omega
belonging to the set on the right hand side
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
s\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,t)

we have

f(s,\omega)<R-1/n.

Since
t<\tau(\omega)
, the continuity now implies

f(s,\omega)\leqslant R-1/n

for any
s\in[0,t]
, hence

f(s,\omega)<R

for
s
in some larger interval
[0,t+\delta]
.
This implies
\tau_{R}(\omega)>t

and
\omega
belongs to the set on the left hand side.
Conversely, if
\omega
belongs to the set on the left hand side, then

f(s,\omega)<R

for any
s\in[0,t]
.
Hence there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(s,\omega)<R-1/n

for any
s\in[0,t]
,
and in particular,
for any
s\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,t)
.
Therefore
\omega
belongs to the set on the right hand side as well.

If
0<a<\tau(\omega)
, then taking
R>\sup_{0\leq s\leq a}f(s,\omega)
gives
\tau_{R}(\omega)\geq a
, proving (
2.6
). Finally, the properties (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) are immediate from the definition of
\tau_{R}
, and this concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Stochastic integrals

In this section, let
K
and
H
be separable Hilbert spaces, with orthonormal bases
\{e_{k}\}
and
\{f_{j}\}
, respectively.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(K,H)
the space of bounded linear operators from
K
into
H
, with the operator norm, and by
\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)
the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators

\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)=\left\{T\in\mathcal{L}(K,H)\colon\trace(T^{*}T)<\infty\right\},

which is a separable Hilbert space with the norm and inner product

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)}=\trace(T^{*}T)^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{k}\left\|Te_{k}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\langle\,S,T\,\rangle_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)}=\trace(T^{*}S).

One can think of Hilbert-Schmidt operators as infinite-dimensional matrices. Indeed, defining

T_{jk}=\langle\,Te_{k},f_{j}\,\rangle_{H},

then
T\mapsto\{T_{jk}\}
is an isometry from
\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)
onto
l^{2}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})
.

For later use we note the fact that if
S\in\mathcal{L}(H,H^{\prime})
, where
H^{\prime}
is a Hilbert space, and
T\in\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)
then the composition
ST
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H^{\prime})
and

\left\|ST\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H^{\prime})}\leq\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H,H^{\prime})}\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)}.

(2.9)

Consider now the cylindrical Wiener process

W(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}(t)e_{k},

where the sum is formal. Given
T>0
, the
H
-valued Itô integral of an adapted process

F\in L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)\right)

is a natural generalisation of the
n
-dimensional Itô integral. It can be defined by

\int_{0}^{T}F(t)\,dW(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{T}F_{jk}(t)\,dB_{k}(t)\right)f_{j},

where the integrals on the right hand side are ordinary Itô integrals and
F_{jk}=\langle\,Fe_{k},f_{j}\,\rangle_{H}
are the matrix entries of
F
. The sum converges in
L^{2}(\Omega;H)
and the Itô isometry holds:

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{T}F(t)\,dW(t)\right\|_{H}^{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|F(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H)}^{2}\,dt\right).

(2.10)

Moreover, the
H
-valued random variable
I(t,\omega)=\int_{0}^{t}F(s,\omega)\,dW(s,\omega)
is adapted and we can assume that it has continuous paths, since it has a modification with this property. Further,
I
is a martingale, so by Doob’s maximal inequality (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
),

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}F(s)\,dW(s)\right\|_{H}^{2}\right)\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{T}F(s)\,dW(s)\right\|_{H}^{2}\right).

(2.11)

2.4.
Function spaces

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
First,
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
denotes the usual Sobolev space with norm

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s}\bigl|\widehat{f}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2},

(2.12)

where
\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle=\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
and the Fourier transform is defined by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx\qquad\left(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right).

Then the inverse transform is given by
\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\xi}g(\xi)\,d\xi
, the Plancherel identity reads
\left\|\mathcal{F}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=(2\pi)^{d/2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and we have
\widehat{fg}=(2\pi)^{-d}\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}
and

\widehat{f*g}=\widehat{f}\,\widehat{g}

for
f,g\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

We recall the Sobolev product law (see Theorem 2.2 in
[
13
]
)

\left\|fg\right\|_{H^{-s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.13)

which holds for all Schwartz functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}\geq\frac{d}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{i\neq j}(s_{i}+s_{j})\geq 0,\quad\text{which are not both equalities}.

(2.14)

For
d=1
and
0<b<1
we will make use of the norm equivalence
(see
[
28
, Lemma 3.15]
)

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\sim\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{S^{b}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(2.15)

Here
\left\|f\right\|_{S^{b}(\Omega)}
denotes the Slobodeckij seminorm on an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
,

\left\|f\right\|_{S^{b}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|f(t)-f(r)\right|^{2}}{\left|t-r\right|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt.

On any finite time interval
I=(S,T)
there is a similar norm equivalence (see
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
)

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b}(I)}\sim_{I}\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{S^{b}(I)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.16)

but with the caveat that the constants depend on the interval.
The norm on the left hand side is the restriction norm, defined as the infimum
of
\left\|g\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}
taken over
g\in H^{b}(\mathbb{R})
with
g=f
on
I
.

Second, given a function
h\in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
,
we denote by
X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
the Bourgain space with norm

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s}\left\langle\,\tau+h(\xi)\,\right\rangle^{2b}\left|\widehat{u}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\tau\,d\xi\right)^{1/2},

(2.17)

where
\widehat{u}=\mathcal{F}_{(t,x)}u
is the space-time Fourier transform.
The restriction to a time-slab
(S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted
X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)
,
and is equipped with the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}=\inf\left\{\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}\colon\text{$u(t)=v(t)$ for $t\in(S,T)$}\right\}.

(2.18)

The symbol
\tau+h(\xi)
in (
2.17
) is associated to the linear PDE

-i\partial_{t}u+h(D_{x})u=0
with the group

S_{h(\xi)}(t)=e^{-ith(D_{x})},

whose action on
f(x)
is given by
\mathcal{F}_{x}\left\{S_{h(\xi)}(t)f\right\}(\xi)=e^{-ith(\xi)}\widehat{f}(\xi)
. Here we recall that
D_{x}=-i\partial_{x}
, so that
\widehat{D_{x}f}(\xi)=\xi\widehat{f}(\xi)
.
Note that we can also write (
2.17
) as

\left\|u\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}}^{2}=\int\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\left\|e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}u(t,\xi)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}d\xi.

(2.19)

We now mention some well-known properties of Bourgain norms; we refer to
[
18
]
and
[
31
]
for more details.
First, we note the obvious conjugation property

\left\|\overline{u}\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}=\left\|u\right\|_{X_{-h(-\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}

(2.20)

valid also on the whole line
\mathbb{R}
, of course.
By
L^{2}
duality and Plancherel’s theorem it is clear that

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}=(2\pi)^{d+1}\sup_{\left\|v\right\|_{X^{-s,-b}_{h(\xi)}}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)\overline{v(t,x)}\,dt\,dx\right|

(2.21)

and

(2\pi)^{d+1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t,x)\overline{v(t,x)}\,dt\,dx\right|\leq\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{-s,-b}_{h(\xi)}}.

(2.22)

Now let
\theta(t)
be any smooth, compactly supported function.
From (
2.19
) it is clear that

\left\|\theta(t)S_{h(\xi)}(t)f\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}=\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for $b\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(2.23)

Moreover,

\left\|\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S_{h(\xi)}(t-t^{\prime})F(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}\leqslant C_{b}\left(\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\left\|F\right\|_{X^{s,b-1}_{h(\xi)}}\quad\text{for $b>\frac{1}{2}$},

(2.24)

which by (
2.19
) reduces to the inequality

\left\|\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{b}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C_{b}\left(\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b-1}(\mathbb{R})}\quad\text{for $b>\frac{1}{2}$}.

(2.25)

The latter can be proved by using Fourier inversion on
f
as follows, cf.
[
18
]
.
Assuming
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

and using Plancherel’s theorem we can rewrite the integral of
f
as

\int_{0}^{t}f(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}\left(\mathbb{1}_{(0,t)}\right)(\tau)}\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\tau)\frac{1}{\tau}\left(e^{it\tau}-1\right)\,d\tau.

Note that the right hand side here is well defined for any

f\in H^{b-1}(\mathbb{R})
with
b>1/2
,
as we shall see below.
It serves as a definition for the left hand side.
We need to calculate the
L^{2}
-norm of

J(\lambda)=\langle\lambda\rangle^{b}\mathcal{F}_{t}\left(\theta(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right)(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(\tau)\frac{\langle\lambda\rangle^{b}}{\tau}\left(\widehat{\theta}(\lambda-\tau)-\widehat{\theta}(\lambda)\right)\,d\tau.

At first we split this integral into integrals over

|\tau|<1
and
|\tau|\geqslant 1
.
On the second domain we can just bound

|\tau|\geqslant\langle\tau\rangle/2

and then return to the integration over the whole line
\mathbb{R}
.
On the first domain we use the fundamental theorem of calculus for the difference
in brackets

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\widehat{\theta}(\lambda-\tau)-\widehat{\theta}(\lambda)\right)=i\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{F}_{t}(t\theta(t))(\lambda-\mu\tau)\,d\mu.

Hence

\left|J(\lambda)\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\langle\lambda\rangle^{b}\left|\mathcal{F}_{t}(t\theta(t))(\lambda-\mu\tau)\right|\,d\tau d\mu+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\frac{\langle\lambda\rangle^{b}}{\langle\tau\rangle}\left(\left|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda-\tau)\right|+\left|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda)\right|\right)\,d\tau.

In the first integral
we can bound

\left\langle\,\lambda\,\right\rangle^{b}\lesssim\left\langle\,\lambda-\mu\tau\,\right\rangle^{b}+1\lesssim\left\langle\,\lambda-\mu\tau\,\right\rangle^{b},

so up to a constant its
L^{2}
-norm gives rise to the term

\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\,d\tau\lesssim\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{b-1}\,d\tau\lesssim\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b-1}}

by Minkowski’s integral inequality.
Now noticing

\left\langle\,\lambda\,\right\rangle^{b}\lesssim\left\langle\,\lambda-\tau\,\right\rangle^{b}+\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{b}

the second integral can be split, up to a constant, into the following three integrals

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\frac{\langle\lambda\rangle^{b}}{\langle\tau\rangle}\left|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda)\right|\,d\tau+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\frac{\langle\lambda-\tau\rangle^{b}}{\langle\tau\rangle}\left|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda-\tau)\right|\,d\tau+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{f}(\tau)\right|\langle\tau\rangle^{b-1}\left|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda-\tau)\right|\,d\tau=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.

Here
J_{3}
is a convolution,
hence

\left\|J_{3}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\left\|\widehat{\theta}\right\|_{L^{1}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\tau)\langle\tau\rangle^{b-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\left\|\langle\tau\rangle^{-b}\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{b}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b-1}},

where the
L^{1}
-norm was estimated by Hölder.
Similarly,

\left\|J_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|J_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant 2\left\|\widehat{\theta}(\lambda)\left\langle\,\lambda\,\right\rangle^{b}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\tau)\langle\tau\rangle^{-1}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant 2\left\|\langle\tau\rangle^{-b}\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{2}}\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{b}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{b-1}}

that finishes the proof of (
2.25
).

From (
2.23
) and (
2.24
),
one immediately obtains the corresponding restriction norm inequalities
on any time interval
(0,T)
,
by choosing a bump function
\theta
such that
\theta(t)=1
for
|t|\leq 1
.
If
T\in(0,1]
, we apply (
2.23
) and (
2.24
) with
\theta(t)
,
while if
T>1
we apply them with
\theta(t/T)
instead of
\theta(t)
,
and use the fact that

\left\|\theta(t/T)\right\|_{H^{b}_{t}}\leqslant\sqrt{T}\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{\max(b,0)}}

and

\left\|t\theta(t/T)\right\|_{H^{b}_{t}}\leqslant T^{3/2}\left\|t\theta(t)\right\|_{H^{\max(b,0)}_{t}}

for
T>1
.
This gives

\left\|S_{h(\xi)}(t)f\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\leq C_{b}\left(1+\sqrt{T}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for $b\in\mathbb{R}$ and $T>0$}

(2.26)

and

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{h(\xi)}(t-t^{\prime})F(t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\leq C_{b}\left(1+T^{3/2}\right)\left\|F\right\|_{X^{s,b-1}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\quad\text{for $b>\frac{1}{2}$ and $T>0$},

(2.27)

where
C_{b}
depends on
b
but not on
T
.

Further, one has (see Lemma 2.11 in
[
31
]
)

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\leq C_{b,b^{\prime}}T^{b^{\prime}-b}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\quad\text{for $-\frac{1}{2}<b<b^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}$ and $0<T\leq 1$}

(2.28)

and

b>\frac{1}{2}\implies X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)\hookrightarrow C\left([0,T],H^{s}\right)\quad\text{with}\quad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{b}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)},

(2.29)

where the last inequality follows by applying the Sobolev embedding

H^{b}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, for
b>1/2
,
to the function

t\mapsto e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)

staying in

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s}\left|e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\xi,

and recalling first (
2.19
), then (
2.18
).

We will also need the trivial fact that

b\geq 0\implies X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)\hookrightarrow L^{2}\left([0,T],H^{s}\right)\quad\text{with}\quad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{s})}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}.

(2.30)

In particular, this implies that the space

\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}\right)

is well-defined when
b\geq 0
, as in (
2.1
).

Finally, we recall that for
-1/2<b<1/2

the restriction norm on
H^{b}(S,T)
is equivalent to the

H^{b}(\mathbb{R})
-norm
of the trivial extension.
More precisely, for all
\phi\in H^{b}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}\leqslant\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(S,T)}\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C_{b}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}\quad\text{for $-\frac{1}{2}<b<\frac{1}{2}$},

(2.31)

where we emphasise that
C_{b}
is independent of
(S,T)
.
Similarly, for all
u\in X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}\leqslant\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(S,T)}u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}}\leqslant C_{b}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}\quad\text{for $-\frac{1}{2}<b<\frac{1}{2}$.}

(2.32)

Here
\mathbb{1}_{(S,T)}(t)
is the characteristic function
of the interval
(S,T)
. Note that the left inequalities in(
2.31
) and (
2.32
) hold trivially by the definition the restriction norm; for a proof of the right inequalities, by an argument relying on the Slobodeckij seminorm, see Lemma 4 in
[
7
]
(alternatively one can use the Fourier transform, applying the ideas used to prove Lemma 3.2 in
[
10
]
).

Combining (
2.31
) and (
2.32
) with (
2.19
), we get the restriction norm equivalence

\left\|u\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}\sim\left(\int\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\left\|e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}u(t,\xi)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(S,T)}^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{for $-\frac{1}{2}<b<\frac{1}{2}$},

(2.33)

with constants depending on
b
but not on
(S,T)
.
With an additional effort one can get a stronger result.

Lemma 4

(Bourgain isometry)
.

For any
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, interval
(S,T)

and

h\in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)

we have the following

\left\|u\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}=\left(\int\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\left\|e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}u(t,\xi)\right\|_{H_{t}^{b}(S,T)}^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

(2.34)

We find the proof of this lemma instructive and not completely straightforward,
so we put it in a separate section
9
.
Moreover, we could not find it presented anywhere else.
As a matter of fact, even the weaker result (
2.33
)
would serve all our needs below.
So Lemma
4
together with its proof
given in Section
9
can be regarded as a complementary material.

Another immediate consequence of (
2.32
) is that functions on adjacent time intervals can be glued together.

Lemma 5
.

Let
-1/2<b<1/2
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{b}
such that if
u\in X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{0},t_{1})
and
v\in X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{1},t_{2})
, where
t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}
, then the glued function

[u,v](t)=\begin{cases}u(t)&t_{0}<t<t_{1}\\
v(t)&t_{1}<t<t_{2}\end{cases}

belongs to
X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{0},t_{2})
and

\left\|[u,v]\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{0},t_{2})}\leq C_{b}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{0},t_{1})}+\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(t_{1},t_{2})}\right).

Proof.

This follows from (
2.32
) and the triangle inequality since
\mathbb{1}_{(t_{0},t_{2})}=\mathbb{1}_{(t_{0},t_{1})}+\mathbb{1}_{(t_{1},t_{2})}
a.e.
∎

2.5.
Cutoffs and a modified Bourgain norm

As usual with a multiplicative noise,
we have to truncate the nonlinearity in order to prove existence by iteration.
In the corresponding cutoffs we will use, for technical reasons,
not the restriction norm (
2.18
)
but an equivalent norm, defined by

\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{(T-S)^{2b}}\int_{S}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{S}^{T}\frac{|U(t,\xi)-U(r,\xi)|^{2}}{|t-r|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)d\xi,

(2.35)

where

U(t,\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}u(t,\xi).

It is thoroughly studied below in Sections
10
and
11
.
Here it is crucial that the norm equivalence is uniform
with respect to the time interval to which we restrict.
To be precise, for any
T_{0}>0
and
b\in(0,1/2)
there exists a constant
C_{T_{0},b}

such that (see Lemma
21
)

C_{T_{0},b}^{-1}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}\leq C_{T_{0},b}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}

(2.36)

for all
u\in X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)
with
0<S<T\leq T_{0}
.

The idea of exploiting the Slobodeckij seminorm,
the double integral over
(S,T)
in (
2.35
),
comes from
[
15
]
.
However, we point out that the factor
1/(T-S)^{2b}

in front of the
L^{2}
-norm
turns out to be important to claim the uniform equivalence (
2.36
); see Remark
6
in Section
11
for a further discussion of this.
Moreover, the necessity of this factor becomes clear
when one calculates these norms on a concrete element,
say

u(t)=S_{h(\xi)}(t)f,

where
f\in H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.
In fact, by (
2.34
) it follows immediately that

\left\|S_{h(\xi)}(t)f\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(0,T)}=\left\|1\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}

for all
T>0
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and it is easy to see that

\left\|1\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)}\sim T^{1/2-b}\quad\text{for $0\leqslant b<\frac{1}{2}$ and $0<T\leqslant 1$.}

(2.37)

Indeed, recalling (
2.31
), we can calculate the equivalent norm

\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\right\|_{H^{b}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\tau}dt\right|^{2}\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{2b}d\tau=T\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{2}{\tau}\sin\frac{\tau}{2}\right|^{2}\left\langle\,\frac{\tau}{T}\,\right\rangle^{2b}d\tau,

which is comparable to
T^{2}+T^{1-2b}\sim T^{1-2b}
(for
0<T\leq 1
and
0\leq b<1/2
) as one can see by splitting the last integration into
|\tau|\leq T
and
|\tau|>T
.
On the other hand, it is easily seen from (
2.35
), with
U(t,\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, that

\left\|S_{h(\xi)}(t)f\right\|_{\widetilde{X}_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(0,T)}=T^{1/2-b}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}.

For the modified restriction norm (
2.35
) we have the following key estimates,
proved in Section
11
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
T_{0}>0
and
b\in(0,1/2)
. Let
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth, compactly supported function and set
\theta_{R}(x)=\theta(x/R)
for
R>0
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and for
1\leq i\leq n
let
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
h_{i}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
and
u_{i},v_{i}\in X^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,T_{0})
. Then for
T\in(0,T_{0}]
,
R>0
and
1\leq j\leq n
we have the estimates

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,t)}^{2}\right)u_{j}(t)\right\|_{X^{s_{j},b}_{h_{j}(\xi)}(0,T)}\leqslant C\sqrt{R},

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,t)}^{2}\right)u_{j}(t)-\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|v_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,t)}^{2}\right)v_{j}(t)\right\|_{X^{s_{j},b}_{h_{j}(\xi)}(0,T)}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|u_{i}-v_{i}\right\|_{X^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,T)},

where
C
depends only on
b
,
T_{0}
and
\theta
.

The modified norm (
2.35
) can be formulated also for functions
\phi(t)
depending only on the time variable
t
, and this gives cutoff estimates for functions in
H^{b}(0,T)
,
0<b<1/2
. See Section
11.6
.

3.
Main results

We consider the mild form of (
1.7
), which reads

\psi_{\pm}(t)=S_{\pm\xi}(t)f_{\pm}-iM\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-\sigma)\psi_{\mp}(\sigma)\,d\sigma+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-\sigma)(\phi\psi_{\mp})(\sigma)\,d\sigma\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-\sigma)\psi_{\mp}(\sigma)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW(\sigma)-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-\sigma)\psi_{\pm}(\sigma)\,d\sigma

(3.1)

and

\phi_{+}(t)=S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t)g_{+}+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-\sigma)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\re\left(\overline{\psi_{+}}\psi_{-}\right)(\sigma)\,d\sigma\\
+\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-\sigma)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\phi(\sigma)\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW(\sigma),

(3.2)

where

\phi=\phi_{+}+\overline{\phi_{+}}=2\re\phi_{+}.

We will look for solutions

\psi_{\pm}\in X_{\pm\xi}^{s,b}(0,T),\qquad\phi_{+}\in X_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{r,b}(0,T),

(3.3)

where
b<1/2
is taken sufficiently close to
1/2
, depending on
s
and
r
.
Note that by the conjugation property (
2.20
)
we have

\overline{\phi_{+}}\in X_{-\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{r,b}(0,T).

(3.4)

It will be convenient to define

\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\times H^{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})

(3.5)

and

\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),b}(0,T)=X_{+\xi}^{s,b}(0,T)\times X_{-\xi}^{s,b}(0,T)\times X_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{r,b}(0,T),

(3.6)

with the product norms.

We now state our main results.

Theorem 1

(Local existence)
.

Assume that
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

s>-\frac{1}{4},\qquad\left|s\right|\leq r\leq s+1,\qquad 0<r<1+2s.

Assume further that the kernels
\mathfrak{k}_{j}
, defining the convolution operators
\mathfrak{K}_{j}
, have the regularity

\mathfrak{k}_{1}\in H^{\left|s\right|}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),\qquad\mathfrak{k}_{2}\in H^{\max(0,r-1)}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

Then for any
b<1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
, the following holds. Assume that

(f_{+},f_{-},g_{+})\in L^{2}\bigl(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}\bigr)\quad\text{is $\mathcal{F}_{0}$-measurable}.

Then there exists a stopping time
\tau\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
and a random process

(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+})(t)\in\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}\quad\text{for $0\leq t<\tau$}

such that for
0<t<\tau
, (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold,

(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+})(t)\colon\{t<\tau\}\to\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}\quad\text{is $\mathcal{F}_{t}$-measurable}

and

(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+})\in C\left([0,t],\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}\right)\cap\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),b}(0,t).

Moreover, the solution is maximal in the sense that

\tau<\infty\implies\limsup_{t\nearrow\tau}\left\|(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),b}(0,t)}=\infty,

and it is unique in the sense that if
(\Psi_{+},\Psi_{-},\Phi_{+})
is a solution with the same initial data, and satisfying the same assumptions but with a stopping time
\tau^{\prime}
,
then almost surely

(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi_{+})(t)=(\Psi_{+},\Psi_{-},\Phi_{+})(t)\quad\text{for $0\leq t<\min(\tau,\tau^{\prime})$}.

Further, if
s\geq 0
, then the charge is almost surely conserved:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left|\psi_{+}(t,x)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi_{-}(t,x)\right|^{2}\right)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\psi_{0}(x)\right|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{for $0\leq t<\tau$},

where
\psi_{0}=(f_{+},f_{-})
.

This theorem is a consequence of the abstract well-posedness theory presented in Section
6
.
The existence follows from Theorem
3
and the uniqueness from Theorem
4
. The necessary assumptions stated in Section
6
are verified here on account of the bounds stated in Lemmas
6
,
7
,
8
and
9
below; see Section
6.10
for the details. The charge conservation is proved in Section
7
.

Using the charge conservation, we will then deduce the following global result.

Theorem 2

(Global existence)
.

Let
s=0
and
1/4<r<1/2
. Let
\max(r,1-2r)<b<1/2
. Given initial data as in Theorem
1
,
we impose the additional condition
f_{+},f_{-}\in L^{p}\left(\Omega,L^{2}\right)
,
where

p\geq\max\left(4,\frac{2b+2r-1}{b+2r-1}\right)

Then the solution in Theorem
1
extends globally in time. That is,
\tau=\infty
.

The proof is given in Section
8
.

Although the local result will, as mentioned, be deduced from the abstract framework expounded in some detail in Section
6
, we find it worthwhile to present here a broad outline of the key ideas behind the proof.

Existence for a short time interval
(0,T)
will be proved by iteration in

\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),b}(0,T)\right)\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,C([0,T],\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)})\right),

(3.7)

and the first thing to notice is that we cannot expect the stochastic integrals to be in this space unless
b
is strictly less than
1/2
, the reason being that the paths of any one-dimensional Brownian motion belong to
H^{b}(0,T)
if and only if
b<1/2
, as shown in
[
4
]
.

For
b<1/2
the stochastic integrals can indeed be controlled in (
3.7
), provided that we know that the linear operators

f\mapsto M_{1}(f)=f\mathfrak{K}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad g\mapsto M_{2}(g)=\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}g\mathfrak{K}_{2}

(3.8)

map
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively, into Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively. Thus, we need the following.

Lemma 6
.

Let
s,r
be as in Theorem
1
. Assume that
\mathfrak{k}_{1}\in H^{\left|s\right|}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{k}_{2}\in H^{\max(0,r-1)}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that the linear operators
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
defined by (
3.8
) satisfy

\left\|M_{1}(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{s})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|M_{2}(g)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{r})}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{H^{r}}

for all
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
.

The proof of this lemma is given in Section
4
, and in Section
6
we show how it is applied to control the stochastic integrals.

Now let us turn our attention to the deterministic terms in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Here there is a difference from the purely deterministic case, where one works with
b>1/2
, see e.g.
[
29
]
. Since now we are forced to take
b<1/2
, the required bilinear estimates are a bit tighter. We will prove the following bilinear bounds, extending those obtained in
[
29
,
30
]
to the case where
b
is less than, but close to,
1/2
.

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

s>-\frac{1}{4},\qquad\left|s\right|\leq r\leq s+1,\qquad 0<r<1+2s.

Then for any
b<1/2
sufficiently close to
1/2
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{+\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{-\xi}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{+\xi}},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\psi}\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{r-1,-b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{+\xi}}\left\|\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}},

(3.11)

for all Schwartz functions
\psi
,
\psi^{\prime}
and
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
. In particular, in the case
s=0
and
1/4<r<1/2
, relevant for Theorem
2
, the above estimates hold for all
b>1/4
.

This lemma is proved in Section
5
. The method of proof does not differ significantly from that used in
[
29
,
30
]
for the case
b>1/2
. Also, we remark that studying bilinear space-time estimates in Bourgain norms with
b<1/2
is nothing new. For example, general product estimates for wave-type spaces were studied in
[
12
,
13
]
.

With
b
as in the last lemma, and choosing
0<\varepsilon<1/2-b
, set

B=-b+1-\varepsilon.

Then
B>1/2
,
so we can apply (
2.27
) and (
2.28
)
to control the deterministic integrals in
\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),B}(0,T)
.
And then, crucially, by (

C\left([0,T];\mathbf{H}^{(s,s,r)}\right)

(and of course also in
\mathbf{X}^{(s,s,r),b}(0,T)
, since

).
For example,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\xi}(t-\sigma)(\phi_{+}\psi_{-})(\sigma)\,d\sigma\right\|_{X^{s,B}_{+\xi}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\phi_{+}\psi_{-}\right\|_{X^{s,B-1}_{+\xi}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\text{by \eqref{Bourgain-2b}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\varepsilon}\left\|\phi_{+}\psi_{-}\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{+\xi}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\text{by \eqref{Bourgain-3}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\varepsilon}\left\|\phi_{+}\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(0,T)}\left\|\psi_{-}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\text{by Lemma \ref{DKG-bilinear-lemma}},

and similarly for the other bilinear terms,
and also for the linear ones, for which we use the following.

Lemma 8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\geq 0
. Then

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{h(\xi)}}\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{t},H^{s})}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{g(\xi)}}

for all Schwartz functions
u
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
and any choice of
g,h\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

This is obvious from the definitions (
2.17
) and (
2.12
), and Plancherel’s theorem.
∎

The bounds are pointwise in
\omega
, and for the iteration in the space (
3.7
) one must now take the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm of them. So for example, one needs to control

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\phi_{+}\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(0,T)}^{2}\left\|\psi_{-}-\Psi_{-}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}(0,T)}^{2}\right),

where
\psi_{-}
and
\Psi_{-}
represent different iterates. The only reasonable way to estimate this, seems to be

\left(\sup_{\omega}\left\|\phi_{+}\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(0,T)}^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\psi_{-}-\Psi_{-}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}(0,T)}^{2}\right),

so one needs to control the norms of the iterates uniformly in
\omega
. Here, a further difference from the deterministic case becomes apparent, since there one usually chooses
R>0
and considers initial data whose norm is at most
R
. Then for
T>0
small enough depending on
R
, the Bourgain norms in the iteration are all bounded by
R
times some constant. This will not work in the stochastic case, since a bound in the space (
3.7
) does not imply a pointwise bound in
\omega
. Instead, as is usual in stochastic problems with multiplicative noise, one must truncate the equations. Following the approach in
[
15
]
, for a given
R>0
we consider a truncated version of (
3.1
), (
3.2
) where in the deterministic integrals, each unknown is multiplied by the cutoff

\Theta(t)=\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\psi_{+}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}_{+\xi}(0,t)}^{2}+\left\|\psi_{-}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}_{-\xi}(0,t)}^{2}+\left\|\phi_{+}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{r,b}_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(0,t)}^{2}\right),

(3.12)

where
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is any smooth, compactly supported cutoff function with
\theta(t)=1
for

, and we define
\theta_{R}(x)=\theta(x/R)
. So for example, the integral term

\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\xi}(t-\sigma)(\phi_{+}\psi_{-})(\sigma)\,d\sigma,

considered above, is replaced by

\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\xi}(t-\sigma)\left(\Theta\phi_{+}\Theta\psi_{-}\right)(\sigma)\,d\sigma

and similarly for the other bilinear terms. For technical reasons, inside the cutoffs we do not use the Bourgain restriction norm as defined in (
2.17
), (
2.18
), but rather the equivalent norm (
2.36
), discussed in detail in Section
10
.

With the truncation, and making use of Proposition
1
, the bilinear terms can be controlled in the space (
3.7
), and by iteration one can prove existence up to a small time

depending only on
R
. Repeating this argument one obtains existence on a time interval of any size. Letting
R
tend to
\infty
, this implies the existence of a maximal solution of the original, non-truncated problem. The uniqueness requires a separate argument. The details are shown in Section
6
in an abstract framework.
As mentioned, the necessary assumptions in Section
6
are verified
on account of the bounds in Lemmas
6
,
7
and
8
, as well as Lemma

below. The details are discussed in Section

Lemma 9
.

Assume that

This follows from the Sobolev product law (

4.
Bounds for Hilbert-Schmidt operators

Our main aim in this section is to prove Lemma
6
.

To this end, we require the following lemma. It corresponds to Lemma 2.6 in
[
15
]
, but we remove an additional assumption made there, namely that the convolution kernel
\mathfrak{k}
is in
L^{1}\cap L^{2}
.

Lemma 10
.

Let

\mathfrak{k}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right),

v\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}\right)

and let

\mathfrak{K}

be the convolution operator defined by

\mathfrak{K}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{k}(x-y)f(y)dy.

(4.1)

Then for any orthonormal basis

\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

of

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)

we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{F}\left(v\mathfrak{K}e_{j}\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\right|^{2}d\eta<\infty

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Set
f_{j}(x)=e_{j}(-x)
. Then
\{f_{j}\}
is also an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\overline{\widehat{f_{j}}(\xi)}=\widehat{e_{j}}(\xi)
, by the assumption that
e_{j}
is real valued.

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\right|^{2}d\xi\,d\eta=\left\|\widehat{v}\,\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=(2\pi)^{2d}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\mathfrak{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

it follows that

\left\|F_{\xi}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\right|^{2}d\eta<\infty\quad\text{for a.e.~$\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$},

where
F_{\xi}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{\eta\mapsto\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\right\}
. Applying Parseval’s identity we have, for a.e.
\xi
,

\left\|F_{\xi}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\langle\,F_{\xi},f_{j}\,\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left\langle\,\widehat{F_{\xi}},\widehat{f_{j}}\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\widehat{e_{j}}(\eta)\,d\eta\right|^{2}<\infty.

To finish the proof we only have to notice that
v\mathfrak{K}e_{j}
belongs to
L^{2}
(the operator
\mathfrak{K}
maps
L^{2}
into
L^{\infty}
, by Hölder’s inequality) and has Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}(v\mathfrak{K}e_{j})(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}*e_{j}}(\eta)\,d\eta=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\widehat{e_{j}}(\eta)\,d\eta.

We claim that this equality holds in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence for a.e.
\xi
. To prove the claim, approximate the
L^{2}
functions
v
,
\mathfrak{k}
and
e_{j}
by Schwartz functions, for which the equality clearly holds, and then pass to the limit using the fact that
(2\pi)^{-d/2}\mathcal{F}
is an isometry on
L^{2}
, and the bound

\left\|\int\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\widehat{e_{j}}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\leq\left\|\widehat{v}\right\|_{L^{2}}\int\left|\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\widehat{e_{j}}(\eta)\right|\,d\eta\leq\left\|\widehat{v}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widehat{e_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}},

where we applied Minkowski’s integral inequality and Hölder’s inequality.
∎

Remark 2
.

Integrating both sides of the equality in Lemma
10
,
one recovers the well-known fact
that
v\mathfrak{K}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\left\|v\mathfrak{K}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},L^{2})}=\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Corollary 1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{k}\in H^{\left|s\right|}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then the convolution operator
\mathfrak{K}
defined by (
4.1
) satisfies

\left\|v\mathfrak{K}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(L^{2},H^{s}\right)}\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}\right\|_{H^{\left|s\right|}}\quad\text{for all $v\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)$},

where the constant depends only on
s
. Thus the map
v\mapsto v\mathfrak{K}

extends to a bounded linear map from
H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

into
\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

Integrating both sides of the equality in Lemma

10
with respect to

\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s}d\xi

one obtains

\left\|v\mathfrak{K}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{s})}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widehat{v}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}(\eta)\right|^{2}\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s}d\xi\,d\eta.

Applying the inequality

\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s}\leq C_{s}\left\langle\,\xi-\eta\,\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\,\eta\,\right\rangle^{\left|s\right|}\quad\text{for all $\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $s\in\mathbb{R}$},

(4.2)

the claimed bound follows immediately.
∎

With this corollary in hand, we can now prove Lemma
6
.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

The bound on
M_{1}(f)=f\mathfrak{K}_{1}
is immediate from Corollary
1
. For
M_{2}(g)=\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}g\mathfrak{K}_{2}
we write

\left\|M_{2}(g)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{r})}=\left\|g\mathfrak{K}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{r-1})}

and use again the corollary; if
0\leq r\leq 1
, we bound by

\left\|g\mathfrak{K}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},L^{2})}\leq C\left\|\mathfrak{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}},

while if
r\geq 1
we bound by

C\left\|\mathfrak{k}\right\|_{H^{r-1}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{r-1}}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.
Bilinear bounds

In this section we prove Lemma
7
, and we prove some additional null form bound, stated in two lemmas at the end of the section, that will be needed in the proof of global existence. Throughout this section the space dimension is
d=1
.

We first note the following basic product law for Bourgain norms.

Lemma 11
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\geq 0
, and assume that

•

s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}\geq 1/2
, and

•

\min_{i\neq j}(s_{i}+s_{j})\geq 0
, and

•

the two preceding inequalities are not both equalities, and

•

b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}>1/2
.

Then there is a constant
C
such that the bound

\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{-s_{1},-b_{1}}_{h_{1}(\xi)}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s_{2},b_{2}}_{h_{2}(\xi)}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3},b_{3}}_{h_{3}(\xi)}}

(5.1)

holds for all Schwartz functions
u
and
v
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
and any choice of
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By Plancherel’s theorem and
L^{2}
duality, (
5.1
) can be reformulated as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{f_{1}(\tau,\xi)f_{2}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta)f_{3}(\lambda,\eta)\,d\lambda\,d\eta\,d\tau\,d\xi}{\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s_{1}}\left\langle\,\xi-\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{2}}\left\langle\,\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{3}}\left\langle\,\tau+h_{1}(\xi)\,\right\rangle^{b_{1}}\left\langle\,\tau-\lambda+h_{2}(\xi-\eta)\,\right\rangle^{b_{2}}\left\langle\,\lambda+h_{3}(\eta)\,\right\rangle^{b_{3}}}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}},

where the
f_{i}
are non-negative. By symmetry, it suffices to consider the region where
\left\langle\,\lambda+h_{3}(\eta)\,\right\rangle
is the minimum among
\left\langle\,\tau+h_{1}(\xi)\,\right\rangle
,
\left\langle\,\tau-\lambda+h_{2}(\xi-\eta)\,\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\,\lambda+h_{3}(\eta)\,\right\rangle
, and then the left side is bounded by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{f_{1}(\tau,\xi)f_{2}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta)f_{3}(\lambda,\eta)\,d\lambda\,d\eta\,d\tau\,d\xi}{\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s_{1}}\left\langle\,\xi-\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{2}}\left\langle\,\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{3}}\left\langle\,\lambda+h_{3}(\eta)\,\right\rangle^{b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}},

where we used the assumption
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\geq 0
. This shows that it is enough to prove

\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{-s_{1},0}_{h_{1}(\xi)}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s_{2},0}_{h_{2}(\xi)}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3},b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}_{h_{3}(\xi)}}.

(5.2)

But by the assumptions on
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
we can apply the product law (
2.13
) to get

\left\|u(t)v(t)\right\|_{H^{-s_{1}}}\leq C_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s_{2}}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s_{3}}}

for all
t
. Taking now the
L^{2}
norm with respect to
t
of both sides, and using (
2.29
) to bound

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s_{3}}}\leq C_{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3},b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}_{h_{3}(\xi)}},

we then obtain (
5.2
), and this concludes the proof.
∎

Now we apply the above lemma to obtain estimates of the form

\left\|uv\right\|_{X_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{-s_{1},-b_{1}}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s_{2},b_{2}}_{+\xi}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3},b_{3}}_{-\xi}}.

(5.3)

But here we can gain some regularity compared to the generic case, due to the opposite signs in the dispersion relations on the right hand side; the symbols
\tau+\xi
and
\tau-\xi
correspond to transport equations with propagation in transverse directions. Thus, (
5.3
) is a
null form
estimate.

Let us denote by
b_{\mathrm{min}},b_{\mathrm{med}},b_{\mathrm{max}}
the minimum, median and maximum, respectively, of the three numbers
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
.
We then have the following result.

Lemma 12
.

Suppose
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\geq 0
. Then the following conditions are sufficient for the null form estimate (
5.3
) to hold for all Schwartz functions
u
and
v
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
:

•

s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+b_{\mathrm{min}}\geq 1/2
, and

•

\min\left(s_{2}+s_{3}+b_{\mathrm{min}},s_{1}+s_{2},s_{1}+s_{3}\right)\geq 0
, and

•

the two preceding inequalities are not both equalities, and

•

b_{\mathrm{med}}+b_{\mathrm{max}}>1/2
.

Proof.

We reformulate (
5.3
) as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{f_{1}(\tau,\xi)f_{2}(\tau-\lambda,\xi-\eta)f_{3}(\lambda,\eta)\,d\lambda\,d\eta\,d\tau d\xi}{\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s_{1}}\left\langle\,\xi-\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{2}}\left\langle\,\eta\,\right\rangle^{s_{3}}\left\langle\,\tau\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle\,\right\rangle^{b_{1}}\left\langle\,\tau-\lambda+(\xi-\eta)\,\right\rangle^{b_{2}}\left\langle\,\lambda-\eta\,\right\rangle^{b_{3}}}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}},

where the
f_{i}
are non-negative. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle 2\left|\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\tau+\xi\right|+\left|\tau-\lambda+(\xi-\eta)\right|+\left|\lambda-\eta\right|,

\displaystyle 2\left|\xi-\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\tau-\xi\right|+\left|\tau-\lambda+(\xi-\eta)\right|+\left|\lambda-\eta\right|,

implying

\min\left(\left\langle\,\eta\,\right\rangle,\left\langle\,\xi-\eta\,\right\rangle\right)\leq C\max\left(\left\langle\,\tau\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle\,\right\rangle,\left\langle\,\tau-\lambda+(\xi-\eta)\,\right\rangle,\left\langle\,\lambda-\eta\,\right\rangle\right),

since
\left\langle\,\tau\pm\left|\xi\right|\,\right\rangle
is comparable to
\left\langle\,\tau\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle\,\right\rangle
. Thus, we can reduce (
5.3
) to estimates

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{X_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{-s_{1},-B_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s_{2}+b_{\mathrm{min}},B_{2}}_{+\xi}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3},B_{3}}_{-\xi}},

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{X_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}^{-s_{1},-B_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s_{2},B_{2}}_{+\xi}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{s_{3}+b_{\mathrm{min}},B_{3}}_{-\xi}},

where
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\geq 0
and
B_{1}+B_{2}+B_{3}=b_{\mathrm{med}}+b_{\mathrm{max}}
. Applying Lemma
11
we therefore obtain the claimed result.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
7
.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

We start with (
3.11
), which reads

\left\|\overline{\psi}\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{r-1,-b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{+\xi}}\left\|\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}}.

By (
2.20
),
\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{+\xi}}=\|\overline{\psi}\|_{X^{s,b}_{+\xi}}
, so we can remove the conjugation on
\psi
. Now we apply Lemma
12
with
s_{1}=1-r
,
s_{2}=s_{3}=s
and
b_{1}=b_{2}=b_{3}=b
, and conclude that (
3.11
) holds if

b>\frac{1}{4},\quad 2s+b\geq 0,\quad 1-r+s\geq 0,\quad 1-r+2s+b>\frac{1}{2}.

(5.4)

It remains to consider (
3.9
) (the proof of (
3.10
) is similar). We have to show

\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{+\xi}}\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}}.

By (
2.21
),

\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{s,-b}_{+\xi}}=(2\pi)^{2}\sup_{\left\|\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{-s,b}_{+\xi}}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi\psi\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\,dt\,dx\right|,

where by (
2.22
),

(2\pi)^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi\psi\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\,dt\,dx\right|\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\left\|\psi\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\right\|_{X^{-r,-b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}.

Thus we have reduced to obtaining a bound

\left\|\overline{\psi^{\prime}}\psi\right\|_{X^{-r,-b}_{\pm\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}}\leq C\left\|\psi^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{-s,b}_{+\xi}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{-\xi}}.

Applying Lemma
12
with
s_{1}=r
,
s_{2}=-s
,
s_{3}=s
and
b_{1}=b_{2}=b_{3}=b
, we conclude that (
3.9
) holds if

b>\frac{1}{4},\quad r+b>\frac{1}{2},\quad r-s\geq 0,\quad r+s\geq 0.

(5.5)

If we take
b=1/2-\delta
with
\delta>0
sufficiently small, then both (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) are satisfied if

s>-\frac{1}{4},\qquad\left|s\right|\leq r\leq s+1,\qquad 0<r<1+2s,

proving the main part of Lemma
7
. In the special case
s=0
,
r\in(1/4,1/2)
, mentioned at the end of Lemma
7
, it is clear that (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) hold for any
b>1/4
.
∎

We conclude this section with some additional null form estimates, given in the next two lemmas.

Lemma 13
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
.
Then for all Schwartz functions
u
and
v
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}

the following bound holds true

\left\|uv\right\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{+\xi}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{-\xi}}\quad\text{where}\quad b=\begin{cases}1-1/p&\text{if $1\leq p<2$},\\
1/2+\varepsilon&\text{if $p=2$}.\end{cases}

(5.6)

Here
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small;
C
depends on
p
, and on
\varepsilon
if
p=2
.

Proof.

In null coordinates
(s,y)=(t+x,t-x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the desired inequality reads

\left\|uv\right\|_{L^{p}_{s,y}}\leq C\left\|\left\langle\,D_{s}\,\right\rangle^{b}u\right\|_{L^{2}_{s,y}}\left\|\left\langle\,D_{y}\,\right\rangle^{b}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{s,y}}.

But by Hölder’s inequality, letting
q\in[2,\infty]
be defined by
1/p=1/2+1/q
,

\left\|uv\right\|_{L^{p}_{s,y}}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{s}(L^{2}_{y})}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}_{s}(L^{q}_{y})}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{y}(L^{q}_{s})}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}_{s}(L^{q}_{y})},

where we used Minkowski’s integral inequality in the last step. The desired inequality now follows by applying the Sobolev embedding
H^{b}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
, which holds for
b=1/2+\varepsilon
if
q=\infty
, and for
b=1/2-1/q
if
2\leq q<\infty
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
r>0
and
h\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
. Then for all Schwartz functions
u
and
v
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
, we have the estimate

\left\|uv\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{-r,-r}}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{+\xi}}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{-\xi}},\quad\text{where}\quad b=\begin{cases}1/2-r&\text{if $0<r<1/2$},\\
\varepsilon&\text{if $r=1/2$},\\
0&\text{if $r>1/2$}.\end{cases}

(5.7)

Here
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small;
C
depends on
r
, and on
\varepsilon
if
r=1/2
, but not on
h
.

Proof.

Define
p=p(r)\in[1,2)
by (i)
1/p=r+1/2
if
0<r<1/2
, (ii)
1/p=1-\varepsilon
if
r=1/2
, and (iii)
p=1
if
r>1/2
. Here we can take any
0<\varepsilon<1/2
.
Then the Sobolev embedding

L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow H^{-r}(\mathbb{R})

(5.8)

holds.
Setting
F(t,\xi)=\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{-r}e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{uv}(t,\xi)
, we then have

\left\|uv\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{-r,-r}}=\left\|\left\|F(t,\xi)\right\|_{H^{-r}_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\leq C\left\|\left\|F(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\leq C\left\|\left\|F(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}}=C\left\|\left\|uv\right\|_{H^{-r}_{x}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}}\leq C\left\|uv\right\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}},

where we applied (
5.8
) twice, to get the first and third inequalities, and we used Minkowski’s integral inequality to get the second inequality. The proof can now be concluded by appealing to Lemma
13
; we are in the case
1\leq p<2
, so (
5.6
) holds with
b=1-1/p
.
∎

As a consequence we get the following result used later in Section
8
.

Corollary 2
.

Let

0<r<b<\frac{1}{2},\quad 0<\mu<1,\quad\frac{1}{2}-r=\mu b.

Then for any
h\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and
T>0

we have the estimates

\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{0,-b}_{\pm\xi}(0,T)}\leqslant C\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{\mp\xi}(0,T)}^{\mu}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}^{1-\mu},

where
C
depends on
r,b
but neither on
h
nor on
T
.

Proof.

Firstly, by (
2.32
) we can substitute the restriction norm
with the norm of trivial extension

\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{0,-b}_{\pm\xi}(0,T)}\leqslant\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\phi\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\psi\right\|_{X^{0,-b}_{\pm\xi}}

and without loss of generality we will write simply
\phi,\psi

while meaning in fact the trivial extensions

\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\phi,\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\psi.

Secondly,
we apply Lemma
14
via duality (
2.21
), (
2.22
) as follows.
Given an arbitrary test function with

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{\pm\xi}}=1

consider the integral

(2\pi)^{2}\left|\int\phi\psi\overline{u}\,dtdx\right|\leqslant\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,r}_{h(\xi)}}\left\|\overline{\psi}u\right\|_{X^{-r,-r}_{h(\xi)}}\leqslant C_{r}\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{h(\xi)}}\left\|\overline{\psi}\right\|_{X^{0,1/2-r}_{\mp\xi}}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0,1/2-r}_{\pm\xi}}\leqslant C_{r}\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{h(\xi)}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,\mu b}_{\mp\xi}},

where
C_{r}
comes exactly from the previous lemma.
Here we have used the conjugation property (
2.20
)
and obvious embeddings of Bourgain spaces.
Appealing to the interpolation argument
one obtains

\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,\mu b}_{\mp\xi}}\leqslant\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{\mp\xi}}^{\mu}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,0}_{\mp\xi}}^{1-\mu}=(2\pi)^{1-\mu}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{\mp\xi}}^{\mu}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{1-\mu}.

Now taking the supremum of the above integral over test functions
and recalling that
\phi,\psi
stand for the trivial extensions

\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\phi,\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\psi

one obtains the needed statement

\left\|\phi\psi\right\|_{X^{0,-b}_{\pm\xi}(0,T)}\leqslant(2\pi)^{1-\mu}C_{r}C_{b}^{1+\mu}\left\|\phi\right\|_{X^{r,b}_{h(\xi)}(0,T)}\left\|\psi\right\|_{X^{0,b}_{\mp\xi}(0,T)}^{\mu}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}^{1-\mu}

by (
2.21
) and (
2.32
).
Finally,
the interpolation used above is justified as

\left\|f(\tau,\xi)\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{\mu b}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi}}\leqslant\left\|f^{\mu}(\tau,\xi)\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{\mu b}\right\|_{L^{2/\mu}_{\tau,\xi}}\left\|f^{1-\mu}(\tau,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2/(1-\mu)}_{\tau,\xi}}

by Hölder’s inequality with

f(\tau,\xi)=\left|\widehat{\psi}(\tau\pm\xi,\xi)\right|

and the Bourgain norm definition (
2.17
).

∎

6.
Abstract well-posedness for dispersive PDE systems with noise

Let
W(t)
be a cylindrical Wiener process, as in Section
2
.

6.1.
Notation and definitions

Let
d,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
define

\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=H^{s_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\times\dots\times H^{s_{n}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

with the product norm

\left\|\mathbf{f}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=\left(\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{H^{s_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}+\dots+\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{H^{s_{n}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{for}\quad\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Given
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, define the Fourier multiplier
\mathbf{h}(D_{x})
and the group
\mathbf{S}(t)
by

\mathbf{h}(D_{x})\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}(D_{x})f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{n}(D_{x})f_{n}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{h_{1}(\xi)}(t)f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
S_{h_{n}(\xi)}(t)f_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where the latter is then an isometry of
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
. Further, given
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
we define

\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)=X_{h_{1}(\xi)}^{s_{1},b}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\times\dots\times X_{h_{n}(\xi)}^{s_{n},b}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

with the product norm. The restriction to
(S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)
.

The following space will play a key role.

Definition 1
.

For
0\leq S<T
let, with notation as in (
2.1
),

Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)=\mathbb{L}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\cap C\left([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)\right)

with norm

\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}=\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\right)}+\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega,C\left([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)\right)}.

Note that this space is complete.

Remark 3
.

From the embedding

\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)}\leq\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}\quad\text{if $b\geq 0$},

(6.1)

we infer that
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\hookrightarrow L^{2}\left([S,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
for
b\geq 0
.

By Lemma
5
, we see immediately that the
Z
-space has the following gluing property.

Lemma 15
.

Let
0<S<S^{\prime}
and
-1/2<b<1/2
.
Given
\mathbf{u}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)
and
\mathbf{v}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
, define
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]
by

[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}](t)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}(t)&\text{for $0\leq t\leq S$}\\
\mathbf{v}(t)&\text{for $S<t\leq S^{\prime}$}.\end{cases}

Then

\left\|[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})}\leq C_{b}\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\right),

where
C_{b}
depends only on
b
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{u}(S)=\mathbf{v}(S)
, then
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})
.

6.2.
Initial value problem

Now consider the Cauchy problem for a system of dispersive nonlinear stochastic PDE,

-id\mathbf{u}(t)+\mathbf{h}(D)\mathbf{u}(t)\,dt=\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(t))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(t))\right]\,dt+\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(t))\,dW(t),\qquad\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},

(6.2)

where the unknown is a random variable
\mathbf{u}(t)
taking values in
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
for a given
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathbf{u}_{0}\colon\Omega\to\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\quad\text{is $\mathcal{F}_{0}$-measurable},

(6.3)

and the operators

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f})=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}(\mathbf{f})\\
\vdots\\
M_{n}(\mathbf{f})\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{f})=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}(\mathbf{f})\\
\vdots\\
L_{n}(\mathbf{f})\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{f})=\begin{pmatrix}N_{1}(\mathbf{f})\\
\vdots\\
N_{n}(\mathbf{f})\end{pmatrix},

acting only in the space variable
x
, are assumed to have the following properties:

\mathbf{M}\colon\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\to\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})\quad\text{is linear, with a bound}\quad\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{f}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}.

(6.4)

Further,

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}

(6.5)

and

\mathbf{N}\colon\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}\to\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b^{\prime}}\quad\text{is locally Lipschitz, for some $-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}<0<b<\frac{1}{2}$},

(6.6)

with the bound, for some constants
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
,

\left\|\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b^{\prime}}}\leq C\left(1+\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}}\right)^{p-1}\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}}.

(6.7)

These estimates of course imply the corresponding ones with time restriction to any slab
(S,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Finally, we assume that with the same
b,b^{\prime}
as above,

\mathbf{L}\colon\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}\to\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b^{\prime}}\quad\text{is linear, with a bound}\quad\left\|\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b^{\prime}}}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}}.

(6.8)

We emphasise that for the examples we have in mind,
\mathbf{N}
may fail to map
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
into itself, hence the deterministic integral in (
6.12
) below may not make sense as a Bochner integral in
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
. However, one expects that this obstruction disappears at sufficiently high regularity. We therefore add the assumption

\text{there exists $\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, with $s_{i}^{\prime}\geq s_{i}$, such that
$\mathbf{N}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ map $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}$ continuously into $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}$}.

(6.9)

This will be used to establish measurability properties,
and to regularise (
6.2
).

Remark 4
.

The reason for separating the linear part
\mathbf{L}
from the nonlinear part
\mathbf{N}
is that this allows us to avoid truncating the linear terms; see (
6.16
). This is not essential for the arguments used to prove existence and uniqueness in this section, but is used in the proof of conservation of charge in Section
7
.

Remark 5
.

It is easy to construct operators satisfying (
6.4
).
Taking
K=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
, we consider

M_{i}(\mathbf{f})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\,D\,\right\rangle^{-\sigma_{i,j}}f_{j}\mathfrak{K}_{i,j},

where
\sigma_{i,j}
are real numbers and
\mathfrak{K}_{i,j}
are of the form (
4.1
) with kernels
\mathfrak{k}_{i,j}
. Then by Corollary
1
,

\left\|M_{i}(\mathbf{f})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{s_{i}})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|f_{j}\mathfrak{K}_{i,j}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},H^{s_{i}-\sigma_{i,j}})}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{H^{s_{j}}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{i,j}\right\|_{H^{|s_{j}|}}

provided that
s_{i}-\sigma_{i,j}\leq s_{j}

and
\mathfrak{k}_{i,j}\in H^{|s_{j}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Let us now define precisely what we mean by a solution of (
6.2
).

Definition 2
.

Let
\tau\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
be a stopping time. By a
solution of (
6.2
), (
6.3
) up to time
\tau
, we mean a random variable

\mathbf{u}(t,\omega)\in\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\quad\text{for $0\leq t<\tau(\omega)$}

such that

\mathbf{u}(t)\colon\{t<\tau\}\to\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\quad\text{is $\mathcal{F}_{t}$-measurable},

(6.10)

and such that, almost surely,

\mathbf{u}\in C\left([0,t],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)\cap\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,t)\quad\text{for $0<t<\tau$}

(6.11)

and

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right]\,ds\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)

(6.12)

for
0\leq t<\tau
. So in particular,
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
almost surely.

Some remarks are in order.
First, by the assumptions on
\mathbf{N}
, the first integral in (
6.12
) is well defined pointwise in
\omega
, and belongs to
C\left([0,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
and
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
for any
0<T<\tau(\omega)
, as we show in Section
6.5
. We emphasise, however, that it may not make sense as an
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
-valued Bochner integral, but only when interpreted in the Bourgain space, where dispersive effects are taken into account.

Second, to see that the stochastic integral exists, define

f(t)=\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,t)}^{2}\quad\text{for $0<t<\tau$},\qquad f(0)=0.

(6.13)

By Lemmas
23
and
24
, proved in Section
10
,
f
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
3
, hence

\tau_{R}(\omega)=\sup\left\{t\in[0,\tau(\omega))\colon\text{$f(s,\omega)<R$ for $0\leq s\leq t$}\right\}

(6.14)

is a stopping time for each
R>0
, and
\lim_{R\to\infty}\tau_{R}(\omega)=\tau(\omega)
.
Now consider the process

\mathbb{1}_{t\leq\tau_{R}(\omega)}\mathbf{u}(t,\omega),

that is, the trivial extension beyond the time
\tau_{R}
. It is progressively measurable by Lemma
2
, so by (
6.1
) it belongs to
L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
for all
T>0
, and is
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
-adapted, hence

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{s\leq\tau_{R}}(s)\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s)

exists in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
for all
t\geq 0
, by the assumption (
6.4
).
Thus the Itô integral appearing in (
6.12
) is well defined
by the localisation procedure.

6.3.
Existence and uniqueness

We now formulate the main existence and uniqueness results that will be proved.

Theorem 3

(Maximal local existence)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0<b<1/2
. Assume that (
6.4
)–(
6.9
) hold and that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable.
Then the problem (
6.2
), (
6.3
) has a solution
\mathbf{u}(t)
in the sense of Definition
2
, with a stopping time
\tau\colon\Omega\to(0,\infty]
. The solution is maximal in the sense that, almost surely,

\tau<\infty\implies\limsup_{t\nearrow\tau}\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,t)}=\infty.

(6.15)

The proof is given at the end of this subsection.

Theorem 4

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0<b<1/2
. Assume that (
6.4
)–(
6.9
) hold and that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable.
Suppose
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are solutions of (
6.2
), (
6.3
), as in Definition
2
, up to stopping times
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
, respectively, and with the same initial datum
\mathbf{u}_{0}
.
Then almost surely

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)\quad\text{for $0\leq t<\min(\tau,\tau^{\prime})$}.

This is proved in Section
6.8
. For the proof, we must be able to compare the two solutions on slabs
[0,T]\times\Omega
. For this reason, we require also the following extension theorem.

Theorem 5

(Extension)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0<b<1/2
. Assume that (
6.4
)–(
6.9
) hold and that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable.
Suppose
\mathbf{u}
is a solution of (
6.2
), (
6.3
), as in Definition
2
, with stopping time
\tau
. Define the conditional stopping times
\tau_{R}
as in (
6.13
), (
6.14
). Then for any
R>0
and
T>0
the equation

\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right]\,ds\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{U}(s))\,dW(s)

has a unique solution
\mathbf{U}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
such that, almost surely,
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\min(T,\tau_{R})
.

This is proved in Section
6.7
.

Now let us return to the existence result, Theorem
3
. To prove it, we consider a truncated version of (
6.12
), depending on a parameter
R>0
,

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s),

(6.16)

where we use the notation

\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(t)=\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s_{i},b}_{h_{i}(\xi)}(0,t)}^{2}\right).

(6.17)

Here
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth and compactly supported function with
\theta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
, and we write
\theta_{R}(x)=\theta(x/R)
. Inside the cutoffs we use the norm (
2.35
).

We shall prove the following global result for the truncated problem.

Theorem 6

(Global existence with truncation)
.

Let
R>0
,
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0<b<1/2
. Assume that (
6.4
)–(
6.9
) are satisfied. Assume that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable.
Then the truncated problem (
6.16
) has a unique global solution
\mathbf{u}^{R}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{R}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
for each
T>0
. Moreover, for each
T>0
we have

\left\|\mathbf{u}^{R}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)}\leq C_{T,R,b}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{s})},

(6.18)

and if
\mathbf{U}^{R}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
is the solution with
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable data
\mathbf{U}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
, then

\left\|\mathbf{u}^{R}-\mathbf{U}^{R}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)}\leq C_{T,R,b}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{U}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{s})}.

(6.19)

Granting this last result for the moment, we can prove the local result, Theorem
3
. Define

f_{R}(t)=\left\|\mathbf{u}^{R}\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,t)}^{2}\quad\text{for $t>0$},\qquad f_{R}(0)=0.

By Lemmas
23
and
24
, proved in Section
10
, this function satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
3
, hence

\tau_{R}(\omega)=\sup\left\{t\in[0,\infty)\colon\text{$f_{R}(s,\omega)<R$ for $0\leq s\leq t$}\right\}.

is a stopping time. Up to this time,
\mathbf{u}^{R}
is a solution of the non-truncated problem (
6.12
), and we let
R\to\infty
to get a maximal solution. To this end, we use the following.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\mathbf{u}^{R}
be as in Theorem
6
, and define the stopping time
\tau_{R}
as above. Then

\mathbf{u}^{R}(t)=\mathbf{u}^{R^{\prime}}(t)\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq\min(\tau_{R},\tau_{R^{\prime}})$}

(6.20)

and

R\leq R^{\prime}\implies\tau_{R}\leq\tau_{R^{\prime}}.

(6.21)

Moreover,

\text{$R<R^{\prime}$ and $\tau_{R^{\prime}}<\infty$}\implies\tau_{R}<\tau_{R^{\prime}}.

(6.22)

Proof.

First, (
6.20
) follows from Theorem
4
(proved in Section
6.8
, and independently of Theorem
6
and the present lemma).

Now let us prove (
6.22
) (which of course implies (
6.21
)). Suppose that
R<R^{\prime}
and
\tau_{R^{\prime}}<\infty
. To get a contradiction, assume that
\tau_{R^{\prime}}\leq\tau_{R}
. Then by (
6.20
),
f_{R}(t)=f_{R^{\prime}}(t)
for
0<t\leq\tau_{R^{\prime}}
. But by (
2.8
),
f_{R^{\prime}}(\tau_{R^{\prime}})=R^{\prime}
. Thus
f_{R}(\tau_{R^{\prime}})=R^{\prime}>R
, contradicting (
2.7
). Hence we must have
\tau_{R}<\tau_{R^{\prime}}
.
∎

In view of the last lemma, setting

\tau=\sup_{R}\tau_{R},

which is a stopping time, we can consistently define
\mathbf{u}(t)
for
t\in[0,\tau)
by setting
\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{u}^{R}(t)
for
t\in[0,\tau_{R}]
. By (
2.8
) we have

\tau_{R}<\infty\implies\left\|\mathbf{u}^{R}\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,\tau_{R})}^{2}=R,

and by the estimate (
10.4
) in Lemma

, this implies

\tau_{R}<\infty\implies\left\|\mathbf{u}^{R}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,\tau_{R})}^{2}\geq CR,

(6.23)

where
C>0
depends only on
b
. Then (
6.15
) follows. Thus we have shown that Theorem
3
is a consequence of Theorem
6
(and of Theorem
4
, which is used to prove the above lemma).

6
,
5
and

, in that order. We also prove a regularisation result for the truncated system, in Section

. Finally, in Section

we show how the existence and uniqueness parts of Theorem

follow from the abstract results.

In preparation for the proofs, we discuss in the next two subsections some key consequences of the assumptions made on the operators
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{N}
.

6.4.
Properties of
\mathbf{M}

Assume that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
is
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
-adapted. Then by (
6.4
),

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right)\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{u}(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}^{2}\,ds\right),

(6.24)

so for
0\leq t\leq T
the Itô integral

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)

is well defined in
L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
, is
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
-adapted and pathwise continuous, and by the Itô isometry,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}^{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right).

(6.25)

By the maximal inequality (
2.11
),

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}^{2}\right)\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right).

(6.26)

Moreover, the stochastic integral belongs to
L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)\right)
, as we now show.

Lemma 17
.

Let
T>0
. Assume that
\mathbf{M}
satisfies (
6.4
), and that
0\leq b<1/2
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)}^{2}\right)\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right)

(6.27)

for all
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
-adapted
\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
. Here the constant
C
depends on
b
, but not on
T
.

Proof.

Set

\mathbf{I}(t)=\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s),

where the operator

\mathbf{f}\mapsto\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{f}=\begin{pmatrix}\left\langle\,D\,\right\rangle^{s_{1}}f_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\left\langle\,D\,\right\rangle^{s_{n}}f_{n}\end{pmatrix}

is an isometry of
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
onto
L^{2}_{x}=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{n}\right)
. Thus
\mathbf{I}(t)\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,L^{2}_{x}\right)

and the left side of (
6.27
) equals

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{b}(0,T)}^{2}\,d\xi\right),

(6.28)

where

\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t)=\mathcal{F}\mathbf{I}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,L^{2}_{\xi}\right)

and
\mathcal{F}\colon L^{2}_{x}\to L^{2}_{\xi}
is the Fourier transform in
x
. Using repeatedly (
2.9
), we see that

\left\|\mathcal{F}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}\left(K,L^{2}_{\xi}\right)}\leq(2\pi)^{d/2}\left\|\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,L^{2}_{x})}\\
\leq\left\|\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}=\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}.

(6.29)

Thus the Itô isometry gives, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}^{2}\right)\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right).

Integrating this over
0\leq t\leq T
and using Tonelli’s theorem gives

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi,\omega)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\xi\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)\leq CT\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right),

implying that

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi,\omega)\right|^{2}\,dt<\infty

for a.e.
(\xi,\omega)
. So extending
\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t)
by zero outside the interval
(0,T)
, its Fourier transform with respect to
t
is well defined:

\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}(\tau,\xi,\omega)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\,\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi,\omega)\,dt,

and

\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{2b}\left|\widetilde{I}_{j}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\tau.

(6.30)

Now we calculate

\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}(\tau,\xi)=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\,\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right)\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\,dt\right)\mathcal{F}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s),

where we used the stochastic Fubini’s theorem (see
[
17
]
). This is justified on account of the bound (
6.29
). Combining that bound with

\left|\int_{s}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\,dt\right|\leq C\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{-1},

where
C
is independent of
s
and
T
, we obtain

\left\|\left(\int_{s}^{T}e^{-it\tau}\,dt\right)\mathcal{F}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{\mathbf{s}}\mathbf{S}(-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,L^{2}_{\xi})}\leq C\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{-1}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})},

hence by Itô’s isometry

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\widetilde{I}_{j}(\tau,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}^{2}\right)\leq C\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{-2}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right)

for
1\leq j\leq n
. Multiplying both sides by
\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{2b}
, integrating in
\tau
, and using Tonelli’s theorem and (
6.28
) and (
6.30
), we conclude that the left side of (
6.27
) equals

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{I}}(t,\xi)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{b}(0,T)}^{2}\,d\xi\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{2b}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\widetilde{I}_{j}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\,d\xi\right)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle\,\tau\,\right\rangle^{2b-2}\,d\tau\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right),

completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Combining (
6.26
), the last lemma and the embedding (
2.28
), we obtain the following key fact.

Corollary 3
.

Let
0\leq S<T\leq S+1
. Assume that
\mathbf{M}
satisfies (
6.4
), and that
0\leq b<1/2
. Then we have the bound

\left\|\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}\leq C(T-S)^{b}\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\right)}

(6.31)

for all
\mathbf{u}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)
,
where the constant
C
depends on
b
, but not on
T
or
S
.

Proof.

Extend
\mathbf{u}
by zero outside
S<t<T
. Then
\mathbf{u}
belongs to
L^{2}\left([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
(see Remark
3
), and applying (
6.26
), (
6.24
) and Lemma
17
we get

\left\|\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s)\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([S,T]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)}.

Applying now (
2.28
), we obtain (
6.31
).
∎

6.5.
Properties of
\mathbf{N}
and
\mathbf{L}

Recalling that
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0
, choose
0<\varepsilon<b^{\prime}+1/2
and set

B:=b^{\prime}+1-\varepsilon>\frac{1}{2}.

Let
0\leq S<T\leq S+1
and assume that
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)
. Applying (
2.29
), (
2.27
), (
2.28
) and the assumption (

) we get

\sup_{S\leq t\leq T}\left\|\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{v}(s))\right]\,ds\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}\leq C\left\|\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{v}(s))\right]\,ds\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},B}(S,T)}\\
\leq C\left\|\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},B-1}(S,T)}\leq C(T-S)^{\varepsilon}\left\|\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b^{\prime}}(S,T)}\\
\leq C(T-S)^{\varepsilon}\left(1+\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}\right)^{p-1}\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)}

(6.32)

and (taking
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}
and using the assumption (
6.5
))

t\mapsto\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds\quad\text{belongs to $C([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})$}.

(6.33)

Note that in (
6.32
) the constants
C
depend only on
b
,
b^{\prime}
and
B
. Moreover, we claim that

\text{$\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\cap C([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})$ is $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}$-adapted}\implies\text{$t\mapsto\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,ds$ is $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}$-adapted}.

(6.34)

To see this, let
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}
be as in (
6.9
) and use mollification in the

-variable to obtain a sequence
\mathbf{u}_{m}
such that

•

\mathbf{u}_{m}\in\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},b}(S,T)\cap C([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
,

•

\mathbf{u}_{m}
is
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
-adapted,

•

\mathbf{u}_{m}\to\mathbf{u}
in
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,T)\cap C([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
as

.

Then by the assumption (
6.9
),
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}_{m})\in C([S,T],\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}})
is adapted, and therefore progressively measurable, hence the
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
-valued integral

),

converges, as

, in

to the integral appearing in (

), thereby proving that the latter is adapted. Finally, we note that (
6.32
)–(

) of course also hold for

, but then with

in (

).

6.6.
Existence for the truncated problem

We now prove Theorem
6
. To simplify the notation, instead of
\mathbf{u}^{R}
we simply write
\mathbf{u}
.

Note that (
6.16
), with the cutoff given by (
6.17
), is equivalent to

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds\\
+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s)

(6.35)

for
0\leq S\leq t\leq T
. By Proposition
1
, for
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
we have, for
S\in[0,T]
,

\left\|\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(t)\mathbf{u}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)}\leq C\sqrt{R},

(6.36)

\left\|\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(t)\mathbf{u}(t)-\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{v}}(t)\mathbf{v}(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)}

(6.37)

where the constant depends on
b
and
T
. By Lemma
24
, the cutoffs
\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(t)
and
\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{v}}(t)
are adapted.

Now fix a target time
T>0
, and divide
[0,T]
into
N
subintervals of length
\delta=T/N
, where
N
will be chosen large enough depending on
R
and
T
. On each subinterval
[0,\delta],[\delta,2\delta],\dots
we prove existence by a contraction argument in the
Z
-space.

Proceeding inductively, let us assume that for some
0\leq j<N
we have proved existence up to time
S=j\delta
, so
\mathbf{u}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S)
(for
S=0
this just means that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
). Set
S^{\prime}=S+\delta
. Then for
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
we must solve

\mathbf{v}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-\sigma)\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}(\sigma)\mathbf{v}(\sigma)\right)\,d\sigma+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-\sigma)\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{v}(\sigma))\,d\sigma\\
+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-\sigma)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}(\sigma))\,dW(\sigma),

(6.38)

where
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]
is defined as in Lemma
15
. If we can show that (
6.38
)
has a unique solution
\mathbf{v}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
, then by Lemma
15
we have
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})
. Renaming the latter function
\mathbf{u}
, we have then extended the solution to
[0,S^{\prime}]
, and by induction this proves Theorem
6
.

To solve (
6.38
) on
[S,S^{\prime}]=[S,S+\delta]
, we set up a contraction argument in
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
for the operator

\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{v})(t)=\text{r.h.s.}\eqref{induction1-truncated}=:\mathfrak{T}_{0}(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{2}(\mathbf{v})(t)+\mathfrak{T}_{3}(\mathbf{v})(t),\quad\text{for $S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}$}.

So now let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
. We will prove that

\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{v})\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})

(6.39)

and

\left\|\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{v})-\mathfrak{T}(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}

(6.40)

provided that
S^{\prime}-S=\delta>0
is taken sufficiently small, depending on
R
and
T
. Thus
\mathfrak{T}
is a contraction on
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
, so it has a unique fixed point
\mathbf{v}
in that space.

We now prove (
6.39
) and (
6.40
) for each of the terms constituting
\mathfrak{T}
.

6.6.1.
The term
\mathfrak{T}_{0}

By the induction hypothesis,
\mathbf{u}(S)
belongs to
L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)

and is
\mathcal{F}_{S}
-measurable, hence the same is true of
\mathfrak{T}_{0}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)
for
t\geq S
. By (
2.26
) and (
2.29
),

\sup_{t\in[S,S^{\prime}]}\left\|\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},1}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}(S)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}},

implying
\mathfrak{T}_{0}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},1}(S,S^{\prime})
. This verifies (
6.39
) for the term
\mathfrak{T}_{0}
.

6.6.2.
The term
\mathfrak{T}_{1}

Applying (
6.32
) on
[S,S^{\prime}]
to the difference

\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})-\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{w})=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-\sigma)\left[\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}(\sigma)\mathbf{v}(\sigma)\right)-\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]}(\sigma)\mathbf{w}(\sigma)\right)\right]\,d\sigma

yields

\sup_{t\in[S,S^{\prime}]}\left\|\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})(t)-\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{w})(t)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}}\leq C\left\|\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})-\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},B}(S,S^{\prime})}\\
\leq C(S^{\prime}-S)^{\varepsilon}\left(1+\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}+\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\right)^{p-1}\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}\mathbf{v}-\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}.

But by (
6.36
),

\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})}\leq C\sqrt{R},

where
C
depends on
T
and
b
. The same holds with
\mathbf{w}
instead of
\mathbf{v}
. Similarly, (
6.37
) gives

\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}\mathbf{v}-\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq\left\|\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]}[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]-\Theta_{R}^{[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]}[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})}\\
\leq C\left\|[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]-[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}]\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})},

(6.41)

where we used Lemma
15
in the last step. Taking the

-norm we therefore obtain

\left\|\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})-\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C(S^{\prime}-S)^{\varepsilon}\left(1+\sqrt{R}\right)^{p-1}\left\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))},

where
C
depends on
T
and
b
. Taking
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}
, the bounds above also imply that
\mathfrak{T}_{1}(\mathbf{v})
belongs to
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
, by (
6.33
) and (
6.34
).

6.6.3.
The term
\mathfrak{T}_{2}

The arguments used for
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
apply also here, but simplify since we take
p=1
and there is no cutoff.

6.6.4.
The term
\mathfrak{T}_{3}

By Remark
3
,
\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}\left([S,S^{\prime}]\times\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}\right)
. Extending
\mathbf{v}
by zero outside
[S,S^{\prime}]
, the considerations in Section

, and in particular (

), (

) and Lemma

, show that
\mathfrak{T}_{3}(\mathbf{v})
belongs to

.
Moreover, by Corollary

and the linearity of

we have

\left\|\mathfrak{T}_{3}(\mathbf{v})-\mathfrak{T}_{2}(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C(S^{\prime}-S)^{b}\left\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})\right)},

which proves (
6.40
) for the term
\mathfrak{T}_{3}
, if

is small enough. This concludes the proof of (
6.39
) and (
6.40
).

6.6.5.
The bounds (
6.18
) and (
6.19
)

Taking
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}
, the above bounds show that the fixed point
\mathbf{v}
satisfies

\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}(S)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})},

where

where

. This proves (

) (let the

.

6.7.
Extension

Here we prove Theorem
5
.

Assume that
0\leq S<T
and that we have found
\mathbf{U}
, with the desired properties, on
[0,S]
(for
S=0
this just means that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
). Set
S^{\prime}=S+\delta
, where
\delta>0
will be chosen sufficiently small, depending on
T
and
R
. For
t\in[S,S^{\prime}]
we must then solve

\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{U}(S)+i\int_{S\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\mathbf{S}(t-\sigma)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right]\,ds+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{V}(s))\,dW(s).

(6.42)

The solution
\mathbf{V}
should be in
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
, it should satisfy, almost surely,

\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t)\quad\text{for $S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}\wedge\tau_{R}$},

(6.43)

and it should be the only solution with these properties.

For
\mathbf{V}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
define

\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{V})(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{U}(S)+i\int_{S\wedge\tau_{R}}^{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right]\,ds+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}([\mathbf{u},\mathbf{V}](s))\,dW(s),

where

[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{V}](t)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}(t)&\text{for $0\leq t\leq S^{\prime}\wedge\tau_{R}$}\\
\mathbf{V}(t)&\text{for $S^{\prime}\wedge\tau_{R}<t\leq S^{\prime}$}.\end{cases}

Now observe that, almost surely,

S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}\wedge\tau_{R}\implies\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{V})(t)=\mathbf{u}(t),

(6.44)

since for such
t
we have
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{V}](s)=\mathbf{u}(s)
for
S\leq s\leq t
, and by (
6.12
),

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\mathbf{u}(S)+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{u}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right]\,ds+i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\,dW(s),

which equals
\mathbf{\Phi}(\mathbf{V})(t)
since
S\leq t\leq\tau_{R}
and
\mathbf{u}(S)=\mathbf{U}(S)
.

So if
\mathbf{V}
is a fixed point of
\mathbf{\Phi}
, then by (
6.44
) we have, almost surely,
[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{V}]=\mathbf{V}
on
[S,S^{\prime}]
, hence
\mathbf{V}
satisfies (
6.42
) and (
6.43
). Conversely, if
\mathbf{V}
satisfies (
6.42
) and (
6.43
), it is clearly a fixed point. Thus it only remains to prove that
\mathbf{\Phi}
has a unique fixed point in
Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})
. But this follows as in the proof of Theorem
6
, if
\delta=S^{\prime}-S>0
is small enough. This concludes the proof of Theorem
5
.

6.8.
Uniqueness

Here we prove Theorem
4
.

Fix
R>0
and
T>0
and define the conditional stopping time
\tau_{R}
as in (
6.13
), (
6.14
).
Similarly define
\tau_{R}^{\prime}
for
\mathbf{v}
.
It is enough to prove that, almost surely,
\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\min(T,\mu)
,
where
\mu=\min(\tau_{R},\tau_{R}^{\prime})
.

Note that if
\mu_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}
is the conditional stopping time
defined by the pair
(\mu,\mathbf{u})
,
then

\mu_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}=\mu.

Similarly,

\mu_{R}^{\mathbf{v}}=\mu.

Therefore, by Theorem
5

there exist
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
such that, almost surely,
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t)
and
\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\min(T,\mu)
, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mu}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{U}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{U}(s))\right]\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{U}(s))\,dW(s),

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t\wedge\mu}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{V}(s))+\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{V}(s))\right]\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{V}(s))\,dW(s).

for
0\leq t\leq T
.

Then it is enough to prove that, almost surely,
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{V}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq T
. We know this holds for
t=0
. As in the proof of Theorem
6
we now cut
[0,T]
into short intervals of length
\delta
and proceed inductively. Assume that
0\leq S<T
and that we have proved that, almost surely,
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{V}(t)
for
t\in[0,S]
. Then we prove that this is true also on
[S,S^{\prime}]
, where
S^{\prime}=S+\delta
.

To this end, write

\mathbf{U}(t)-\mathbf{V}(t)=\Delta_{1}(t)+\Delta_{2}(t)+\Delta_{3}(t)\quad\text{for $S\leq t\leq T$},

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S\wedge\mu}^{t\wedge\mu}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{U}(s))-\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{V}(s))\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S\wedge\mu}^{t\wedge\mu}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{U}(s))-\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{V}(s))\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{U}(s))-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{V}(s))\right]\,dW(s).

We are going to first estimate
\Delta_{1}(t)
pointwise in
\omega
, and then take the
L^{2}
norm with respect to
\omega
. So for the pointwise estimate we may restrict to
\omega
at which
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t)
and
\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbf{v}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\min(T,\mu)
. We may also assume
S\leq\mu
, as otherwise the integral
\Delta_{1}(t)
vanishes. Write

\Delta_{1}(t)=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{1}_{s\leq\mu}\mathbf{U}(s))-\mathbf{N}(\mathbb{1}_{s\leq\mu}\mathbf{V}(s))\right]\,ds.

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
. Observe that

\left\|\mathbb{1}_{t\leq\mu}\mathbf{U}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,\mu)}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,\mu)}\leq C\sqrt{R},

since
\mu\leq\tau_{R}
. Here
C
depends on
T
and
b
. The same holds for
\mathbf{V}
, since
\mu\leq\tau_{R}^{\prime}
. Thus by (
6.32
) we get the bound, pointwise a.e. in
\omega
,

\left\|\Delta_{1}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left(1+\sqrt{R}\right)^{p-1}\delta^{\varepsilon}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})},

which we then square and integrate with respect to
\omega
. The same estimate holds for
\Delta_{2}
, but with
p=1
. Finally, we bound
\Delta_{3}
. By Corollary
3
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\Delta_{3}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}^{2}\right)\leq C\delta^{2b}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}^{2}\right).

Combining the above bounds, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}^{2}\right)\leq C(1+R)^{p-1}\delta^{2\min(\varepsilon,b)}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}^{2}\right),

where
C
depends on
T
and
b
. So for
\delta>0
small enough,

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}^{2}\right)=0,

hence, almost surely,
\mathbf{U}(t)=\mathbf{V}(t)
for
S\leq t\leq S^{\prime}
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
4
.

6.9.
Regularisation and Itô’s formula

For
\mu\geq 1
let
P_{\mu}
be the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\theta_{\mu}(\xi)
, that is, for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\widehat{P_{\mu}f}(\xi)=\theta\left(\frac{\xi}{\mu}\right)\widehat{f}(\xi).

We assume that
\supp\theta\subset[-2,2]
,
hence
\widehat{P_{\mu}f}
is supported in
[-2\mu,2\mu]
, so
P_{\mu}f(x)
is smooth.
We also assume that
|\theta|\leq 1
.
Then
P_{\mu}
is bounded, with operator norm
\leq 1
, on
H^{s}
and
X^{s,b}
for any
s,b
.
Moreover,
P_{\mu}
maps
H^{s}
into
H^{N}
for arbitrarily large
N
.
Finally, we assume that
\theta=1
on
[-1,1]
,
so that
P_{\mu}
converges strongly to the identity operator
in
H^{s}
and
X^{s,b}
as
\mu\to\infty
.

Now consider the following frequency-truncated version of (
6.16
):

\mathbf{u}(t)=P_{\mu}\left(\mathbf{S}(t)\mathbf{u}_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{L}\left(P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds\right.\\
\left.+i\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{M}\left(P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s)\right),

(6.45)

and let us write
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\mu}=P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}_{0}
. By the dominated convergence theorem,

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$}.

(6.46)

To prove that the solutions of the regularised problem converge, we need the fact that

\lim_{\mu\to\infty}\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}=0

(6.47)

for all
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
.
For any orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}
of
K

we have, for each component
M_{i}(\mathbf{f})
,

\left\|(1-P_{\mu})M_{i}(\mathbf{f})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H^{s_{i}})}^{2}=\sum_{j}\left\|(1-P_{\mu})M_{i}(\mathbf{f})e_{j}\right\|_{H^{s_{i}}}^{2}=\sum_{j}\int\left(1-\theta_{\mu}(\xi)\right)^{2}\left|\widehat{m}_{i,j}(\xi)\right|^{2}\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s_{i}}d\xi,

where
m_{i,j}=M_{i}(\mathbf{f})e_{j}\in H^{s_{i}}
and

\sum_{j}\int\left|\widehat{m}_{i,j}(\xi)\right|^{2}\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{2s_{i}}d\xi=\left\|M_{i}(\mathbf{f})\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,H^{s_{i}})}^{2}<\infty.

The dominated convergence theorem therefore implies (
6.47
).

We shall prove the following.

Theorem 7

(Regularised global existence)
.

Let
R>0
,
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-1/2<b^{\prime}<0<b<1/2
. Assume that (
6.4
)–(
6.9
) are satisfied. Assume that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable. Then for all
\mu\geq 1
and
T>0
the regularised problem (
6.45
) has a unique solution
\mathbf{u}^{\mu}\in Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)
, with initial value
\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\mu}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{x}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}
is supported in
[-2\mu,2\mu]
. Moreover,

\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)}\leq C_{T,R,b}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{s})},

(6.48)

where the constant is independent of
\mu
.
Finally, letting
\mathbf{u}
be as in Theorem
6
(where it is denoted
\mathbf{u}^{R}
),
we have

\lim_{\mu\to\infty}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T)}=0.

(6.49)

Before proving the above theorem, let us remark that the main reason for regularising is that we can then apply Itô’s formula, as formulated in Theorem 2.10 in
[
17
]
; it does not apply directly to the problem (
6.16
), since the deterministic integral may not make sense as a Bochner integral in
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
. But in the frequency-truncated problem (
6.45
), the corresponding integral makes sense even in the more regular space
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
, by the assumption (
6.9
) and the fact that
P_{\mu}
maps
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}}
into
\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}
. Then
[
17
, Theorem 2.10]
can be applied (after applying
\mathbf{S}(-t)
on both sides of (
6.45
), and passing
P_{\mu}
inside the integrals). Passing to the limit
\mu\to\infty
, one can then hope to get Itô’s formula also for (
6.16
). Indeed, this works out in a case of particular interest to us here, namely the conservation of charge for the stochastic Dirac-Klein-Gordon system. The details of this are shown in Section
7
.

We now prove Theorem
7
.
The existence and uniqueness works by a fixed point argument as in the proof of Theorem
6
, up to some obvious modifications, since
P_{\mu}
is bounded on all the spaces involved. From (
6.45
) it is obvious that
\mathcal{F}_{x}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}
is supported in
[-2\mu,2\mu]
. So it remains to prove (
6.49
). As in the proof of Theorem
6
we cut
[0,T]
into short intervals of length
\delta
, where
\delta>0
is chosen sufficiently small depending on
R
and
T
(but not on
\mu
). Suppose that we have proved (
6.49
) on
[0,S]
for some
0\leq S<T
; if
S=0
, we appeal to (
6.46
). Now we must prove (
6.49
) on
[S,S^{\prime}]
with
S^{\prime}=S+\delta
. To this end, write

\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(t)-\mathbf{u}(t)=\Delta_{1}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{2}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{3}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{4}^{\mu}(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq S$},

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}(t-S)\left(\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(S)-\mathbf{u}(S)\right),

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[P_{\mu}\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}^{\mu}}(s)P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(s)\right)-\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[P_{\mu}\mathbf{L}\left(P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(s)\right)-\mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{4}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[P_{\mu}\mathbf{M}\left(P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(s)\right)-\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\right]\,dW(s).

First, by (
2.26
) and the induction hypothesis,

\left\|\Delta_{1}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(S)-\mathbf{u}(S)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$}.

(6.50)

Second, write

\Delta_{2}^{\mu}(t)=\Delta_{2,1}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{2,2}^{\mu}(t),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{2,1}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=iP_{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left[\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}^{\mu}}(s)P_{\mu}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}(s)\right)-\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\right]\,ds,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2,2}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i(P_{\mu}-1)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\mathbf{N}\left(\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}(s)\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,ds.

Estimating as in the proof of Theorem
6
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{2,1}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+R)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\delta^{\varepsilon}\left\|P_{\mu}\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}^{\mu}}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))}

(6.51)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+R)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\delta^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\|P_{\mu}\left[\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}^{\mu}}\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{u}\right]\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))}+\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\Theta_{R}^{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+R)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(\delta^{\varepsilon}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))}+\rho(\mu,S)\right),

where

\rho(\mu,S)=\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,S))}+\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T))}\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$},

(6.52)

by the induction hypothesis and the dominated convergence theorem. Write
\Delta_{2,2}^{\mu}=(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{v}
, where
\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},B}(0,T)\right)
, by the proof of Theorem
6
. Then
by (
2.29
) and dominated convergence,

\left\|\Delta_{2,2}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},B}(0,T))}\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$}.

(6.53)

The estimates for
\Delta_{2}
apply also to
\Delta_{3}
(with
p=1
).

Finally, we split

\Delta_{4}^{\mu}(t)=\Delta_{4,1}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{4,2}^{\mu}(t)+\Delta_{4,3}^{\mu}(t),

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{4,1}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)P_{\mu}\mathbf{M}\left(P_{\mu}\left[\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right](s)\right)\,dW(s),

\displaystyle\Delta_{4,2}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)P_{\mu}\mathbf{M}\left([P_{\mu}-1]\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s),

\displaystyle\Delta_{4,3}^{\mu}(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{S}^{t}\mathbf{S}(t-s)\left(P_{\mu}-1\right)\mathbf{M}\left(\mathbf{u}(s)\right)\,dW(s).

Then as the proof of Theorem
6
, and using the boundedness of
P_{\mu}
,

\left\|\Delta_{4,1}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\delta^{b}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime}))}.

(6.54)

By the dominated convergence theorem,

\left\|\Delta_{4,2}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{s},b}(0,T))}\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$}.

(6.55)

By (
6.26
) and Lemma
17
,

\left\|\Delta_{4,3}^{\mu}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq C\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s))\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(K,\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{s}})}^{2}\,ds\right)\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$},

(6.56)

using (
6.47
) and the dominated convergence theorem.

Combining (
6.50
)–(
6.56
), we conclude that

\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\mu}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Z^{\mathbf{s},b}(S,S^{\prime})}+o(1)\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$},

for
\delta=S^{\prime}-S
small enough. Together with the induction hypothesis this implies (
6.49
) on the interval
[0,S^{\prime}]
, and by induction we then obtain the convergence on the whole interval
[0,T]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
7
.

7.
Charge conservation

Let
s=0
and
0<r<1/2
. We now prove the statement in Theorem
1
about charge conservation of the local solution
(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi)
, almost surely for
0\leq t<\tau
. Let
R>0
. As explained in Section
6
, the solution equals, up to the conditional stopping time
\tau_{R}
, the solution
(\psi_{+}^{R},\psi_{-}^{R},\phi^{R})
of the
R
-truncated problem, obtained in Theorem
6
via the set-up in Section
6.10
. Since
\tau_{R}\to\tau
as
R\to\infty
, it clearly suffices to prove the charge conservation for
(\psi_{+}^{R},\psi_{-}^{R},\phi^{R})
.

In the remainder of this section we fix
R>0
, and to simplify the notation we drop the superscript
R
on the solution. Thus,
(\psi_{+},\psi_{-},\phi)
denotes the global solution of the truncated versions of (
3.1
), (
3.2
):

\psi_{\pm}(t)=S_{\pm\xi}(t)f_{\pm}-iM\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)\psi_{\mp}(s)\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)(\Theta\phi\Theta\psi_{\mp})(s)\,ds\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)\psi_{\mp}(s)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW(s)-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)\psi_{\pm}(s)\,ds,

(7.1)

and

\phi_{+}(t)=S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t)g_{+}+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-s)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\re\left(\Theta\overline{\psi_{+}}\Theta\psi_{-}\right)(s)\,ds\\
+\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-s)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}\phi(s)\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW(s).

(7.2)

Here
\phi=2\re\phi_{+}
and
\Theta(t)
is defined as in (
3.12
), with
s=0
. We assume that
\theta
is even, so that
P_{\mu}f
is real-valued if
f
is.

Set
\psi=(\psi_{+},\psi_{-})
and
\psi_{0}=(f_{+},f_{-})
. We will prove that the charge is almost surely conserved:

\left\|\psi(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

To this end, we want to apply Itô’s formula with the functional
\mathcal{H}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathcal{H}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u(x)\right|^{2}\,dx.

However, as discussed in Section
6.9
, it is necessary to first regularise the problem. So for
\mu\geq 1
we consider the solution
(\psi_{+}^{\mu},\psi_{-}^{\mu},\phi^{\mu})
, obtained in Theorem
7
, of the frequency-truncated equations

\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(t)=S_{\pm\xi}(t)P_{\mu}f_{\pm}-iM\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)P_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s)\,ds+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)P_{\mu}\left(\Theta P_{\mu}\phi^{\mu}\cdot\Theta P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}\right)(s)\,ds\\
+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)P_{\mu}\left(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s)\right)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\,dW(s)-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\pm\xi}(t-s)P_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(s)\,ds,

(7.3)

and

\phi_{+}^{\mu}(t)=S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t)P_{\mu}g_{+}+i\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-s)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}P_{\mu}\re\left(\Theta P_{\mu}\overline{\psi_{+}^{\mu}}\cdot\Theta P_{\mu}\psi_{-}^{\mu}\right)(s)\,ds\\
+\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{+\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle}(t-s)\left\langle\,D_{x}\,\right\rangle^{-1}P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\phi^{\mu})(s)\mathfrak{K}_{2}\,dW(s),

(7.4)

where
\phi^{\mu}=2\re\phi_{+}^{\mu}
. Set
\psi^{\mu}=\left(\psi_{+}^{\mu},\psi_{-}^{\mu}\right)
.
Then by Theorem
7
we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\|\psi^{\mu}(t)-\psi(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\phi^{\mu}(t)-\phi(t)\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\right)\right)\to 0\quad\text{as $\mu\to\infty$}

(7.5)

for any
T>0
. Moreover, the spatial Fourier transform of
\left(\psi_{+}^{\mu},\psi_{-}^{\mu},\phi^{\mu}\right)
is supported in
[-2\mu,2\mu]
.

Notice that

\mathcal{H}(\psi^{\mu})=\mathcal{H}(\psi_{+}^{\mu})+\mathcal{H}(\psi_{-}^{\mu})

and that the first and second derivatives of the functional
\mathcal{H}
are given by the linear form

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(u)=2\re\langle\cdot,u\rangle_{L^{2}}

and the bilinear form

\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}(u)=2\re\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}.

In terms of
X_{\pm}(t)=S_{\pm\xi}(-t)\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(t)
we can rewrite (
7.3
) as

X_{\pm}(t)=X_{\pm}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s)\,dW(s),

where

\Psi(t)=-iMS_{\pm\xi}(-t)P_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(t)+iS_{\pm\xi}(-t)P_{\mu}(\Theta P_{\mu}\phi^{\mu}\cdot\Theta P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu})(t)-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}S_{\pm\xi}(-t)P_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(t)

and

\Phi(t)=iS_{\pm\xi}(-t)P_{\mu}\left(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(t)\right)\mathfrak{K}_{1}.

Applying now Itô’s formula, as stated in
[
17
, Theorem 2.10]
, we get

\mathcal{H}(X_{\pm}(t))-\mathcal{H}(X_{\pm}(0))\\
=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(X_{\pm}(s))\Psi(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(X_{\pm}(s))\Phi(s)\,dW(s)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\trace\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}(X_{\pm}(s))\left(\Phi(s),\Phi(s)\right)\,ds.

Using the fact that the group
S_{\pm\xi}(t)
is unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the above works out to be

\mathcal{H}(\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(t))-\mathcal{H}(\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(0))\\
=\int_{0}^{t}2\re\left\langle\,-iMP_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s)+iP_{\mu}(\Theta P_{\mu}\phi^{\mu}\cdot\Theta P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu})(s)-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}P_{\mu}^{2}\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(s),\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(s)\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}2\re\left\langle\,iP_{\mu}\left(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s)\right)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\cdot,\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(s)\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\,dW(s)\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\re\left\langle iP_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{j},iP_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}ds\\
=I_{\pm}+II_{\pm}+III_{\pm}.

Since
M,M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}
,
P_{\mu}\phi^{\mu}
and
\Theta
are real-valued, and
P_{\mu}
is hermitian, it is clear that

I_{+}+I_{-}=-M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}2\re\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\langle\,P_{\mu}\psi_{+}^{\mu}(s),P_{\mu}\psi_{+}^{\mu}(s)\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}+\left\langle\,P_{\mu}\psi_{-}^{\mu}(s),P_{\mu}\psi_{-}^{\mu}(s)\,\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\right)\,ds.

Thus

I_{+}+I_{-}=-2M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds=-\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)\right)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}ds,

where
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2},L^{2})
and the last equality holds by Remark
2
, since
2M_{\mathfrak{K}_{1}}=\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Next we notice that

II_{\pm}=-2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\im\left\langle(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{j},P_{\mu}\psi_{\pm}^{\mu}(s)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\,dB_{j}(s).

Thus

II_{+}+II_{-}=0,

since
\mathfrak{K}_{1}e_{j}
is a real-valued function.
Finally we notice that

III_{\pm}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi_{\mp}^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}ds,

hence

III_{+}+III_{-}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}ds,

where the operator inside the norm is regarded as a composition
of three operators (first apply
\mathfrak{K}_{1}
, then multiplication by
P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}
and finally
P_{\mu}
).

Summing the contributions, we arrive at

\mathcal{H}(\psi^{\mu}(t))-\mathcal{H}(\psi^{\mu}(0))=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\|P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}-\left\|\left(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)\right)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}^{2}\right)\,ds,

(7.6)

and letting
\mu\to\infty
we get the charge conservation for
\psi
,

\mathcal{H}(\psi(t))-\mathcal{H}(\psi(0))=0\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$}.

Indeed, let
T>0
. By (
7.5
) we know that for some sequence
\mu_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
, we have, almost surely,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\psi^{\mu_{k}}(t)-\psi(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}.

Thus, along
\mu=\mu_{k}
, the left hand side of (
7.6
) converges to
\mathcal{H}(\psi(t))-\mathcal{H}(\psi(0))
for
0\leq t\leq T
. Moreover, the right hand side converges to zero by the dominated convergence theorem. Indeed,
for
\mu
large enough we have the bounds, uniformly in
s\in[0,T]
,

\left\|P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq\left\|(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq\left\|\psi^{\mu}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\left\|\psi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}+1\right)\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}},

and

0\leq\left\|(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}-\left\|P_{\mu}(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\\
\leq C\left(\left\|\psi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}+1\right)\left\|(1-P_{\mu})(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}},

so it only remains to check that
\left\|(1-P_{\mu})(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}
tends to zero along
\mu=\mu_{k}
as
k\to\infty
. But

\left\|(1-P_{\mu})(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leq\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\psi(s)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}+\left\|(1-P_{\mu})(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)-\psi(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\\
\leq\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\psi(s)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}+\left\|(P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)-\psi(s))\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}},

since
\left\|1-P_{\mu}\right\|\leq 1
.
Now the first term
\left\|(1-P_{\mu})\psi(s)\mathfrak{K}_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\to 0
by (
6.47
),
and the last term equals

\left\|P_{\mu}\psi^{\mu}(s)-\psi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\psi^{\mu}(s)-\psi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|(P_{\mu}-1)\psi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathfrak{k}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}

that tends to zero along
\mu=\mu_{k}
as
k\to\infty
.
This concludes the proof of charge conservation.

9.
Bourgain isometry

This section is devoted to the proof of Lemma
4
.
We start with a very general result related to Bochner spaces.
It may be difficult to find it in the existing literature,
so we provide here a complete proof.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mu
be a
\sigma
-finite complete measure on
Y

and
G
be a separable Banach space.
Consider a closed subspace
G_{0}
in
G
with the quotient projection

\pi_{0}:G\to G/G_{0}.

The trivial case
G_{0}=G
is excluded, of course.
Then there exists a unique linear operator
\Phi
making the following diagram commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi:f\mapsto\bigl(y\mapsto\pi_{0}(f(y))\bigr)\;\;}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi=\Phi^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

(9.1)

where
P
is the quotient projection and
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Moreover,
\Phi
is invertible and

\left\|\Phi\right\|=\left\|\Phi^{-1}\right\|=1.

Proof.

We split the proof in several steps starting with the implied correctness of the diagram (
9.1
).

(1)

Clearly,

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})

is a closed subspace in

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G).

Hence
P
is well defined as the corresponding quotient projection,
in particular,

\left\|P\right\|=1.

(2)

For any

f\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

the value
\pi(f)
is the composition

Y\xrightarrow{f}G\xrightarrow{\pi_{0}}G/G_{0}.

Here
G
and
G/G_{0}
are endowed with the Borel
\sigma
-algebras,
whereas
f
is measurable and
\pi_{0}
is continuous.
Therefore
\pi(f)
is measurable.
Moreover,
\pi
is linear with
\left\|\pi\right\|\leqslant 1
.
Indeed,

\left\|\pi f\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}^{p}=\int_{Y}\left\|\pi_{0}f(y)\right\|_{G/G_{0}}^{p}d\mu(y)\leqslant\int_{Y}\left\|f(y)\right\|_{G}^{p}d\mu(y)=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}^{p}.

(3)

An important claim implied in the statement is surjectivity of
\pi
.
Let

h\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0}).

We need to find an

f\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

such that
\pi f=h
.
A direct pointwise construction may not necessarily lead even to a measurable function
f
on
Y
.
So we approximate
h
by a sequence
h_{n},n\in\mathbb{N},
of simple functions in

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0}).

Then for any
y\in Y
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
f_{n}(y)\in G
such that

\pi_{0}(f_{n}(y))=h_{n}(y)

and
f_{n}
is simple.
Note that
f_{n}
may not converge anywhere.
Let us choose a subsequence in
\{h_{n}\}
, while keeping the same notation,
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|h_{n+1}-h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}<\infty.

Making use of the fact that
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite,
that is

Y=\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}Y_{k}

with disjoint
Y_{k}
having
\mu(Y_{k})<\infty
,
we can introduce a measurable function

\chi:Y\to(0,\infty)

taking at most countably many values
(in fact we need below that it is constant on each
Y_{k}
)
and normalized by

\int\chi d\mu=1.

Pointwisely, we have

\left\|h_{n+1}(y)-h_{n}(y)\right\|_{G/G_{0}}=\inf\left\{\left\|F\right\|_{G}\colon F-(f_{n+1}(y)-f_{n}(y))\in G_{0}\right\}.

Now let

\left\{Y_{l_{n}}^{n}\right\}

be the partition associated with
h_{n}
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can approximate the infimum on the finest partition

\left\{Y_{l_{n+1}}^{n+1}\bigcap Y_{l_{n}}^{n}\bigcap Y_{k}\right\}

of
Y
as follows

\left\|h_{n+1}(y)-h_{n}(y)\right\|_{G/G_{0}}^{p}+\frac{\chi(y)}{2^{pn}}>\left\|F_{n}(y)\right\|_{G}^{p},

where

F_{n}(y)-(f_{n+1}(y)-f_{n}(y))\in G_{0}

and
F_{n}
is constant on each

Y_{l_{n+1}}^{n+1}\bigcap Y_{l_{n}}^{n}\bigcap Y_{k}.

One sets
F_{0}=f_{1}
and so
\pi F_{0}=h_{1}
, in particular.
Integrating the above inequality one obtains the estimate

\left\|h_{n+1}-h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\geqslant\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)},

implying

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|h_{n+1}-h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}+1<\infty.

Thus

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}

converges in

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

to an
F
satisfying
\pi F=h
.
Indeed,

\pi\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}F_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(h_{n+1}-h_{n})+h_{1}=h_{N+1}\to h\ \text{ as }\ N\to\infty.

Therefore
\pi
is surjective.

(4)

Finally, we can define the linear mappings
\Phi
and
\Psi
in the diagram (
9.1
).
Indeed, by the previous step for any

h\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})

there is an

f_{h}\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

such that
\pi f_{h}=h
.
We set

\Phi(h)=Pf_{h}.

If we have two elements such that

\pi f_{h}^{1}=\pi f_{h}^{2}=h

then

\int_{Y}\left\|\pi_{0}f_{h}^{1}(y)-\pi_{0}f_{h}^{2}(y)\right\|_{G/G_{0}}^{p}d\mu(y)=\left\|\pi f_{h}^{1}-\pi f_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}^{p}=0.

Hence for a.e.
y
the difference

f_{h}^{1}(y)-f_{h}^{2}(y)\in G_{0}

and so

f_{h}^{1}-f_{h}^{2}\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})

implying the equality

Pf_{h}^{1}=Pf_{h}^{2}

in
L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})
.
Thus
\Phi
is a well defined linear operator.

Similarly, for any

h\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})

there is obviously an

f_{h}\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

such that
Pf_{h}=h
.
We can set

\Psi(h)=\pi f_{h}.

If we have two elements satisfying

Pf_{h}^{1}=Pf_{h}^{2}=h

then their difference

f_{h}^{1}-f_{h}^{2}\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0}).

Hence for a.e.
y
the difference

f_{h}^{1}(y)-f_{h}^{2}(y)\in G_{0}

and so

\left(\pi f_{h}^{1}-\pi f_{h}^{2}\right)(y)=\pi_{0}\left(f_{h}^{1}(y)-f_{h}^{2}(y)\right)=0\ \text{ in }\ G/G_{0}

implying the equality

\pi f_{h}^{1}=\pi f_{h}^{2}

in
L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})
.
Thus
\Psi
is well defined as well.

(5)

Clearly, so defined
\Phi
and
\Psi
are the only linear operators
making the diagram (
9.1
) commutative.
Moreover, it is straightforward to check that the compositions

\Psi\Phi
and
\Phi\Psi
are identities in the spaces

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})

and

L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0}),

respectively.
Therefore
\Psi=\Phi^{-1}
.

(6)

In this step we prove the bound
\left\|\Psi\right\|\leqslant 1

which in turn will automatically imply that
\Phi
is bounded as well and
\left\|\Phi\right\|\geqslant 1
.
Let

h\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})

then by steps
2
,
4

we have

\left\|\Psi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}=\left\|\pi f_{h}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|f_{h}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}.

In other words,

\left\|\Psi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}

for every
f
belonging to the preimage
P^{-1}\{h\}
.
Therefore passing to infimum over this preimage one recovers
the quotient norm of
h
,
so that

\left\|\Psi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})},

which proves the claim
\left\|\Psi\right\|\leqslant 1
due to the arbitrary choice of
h
.

(7)

In order to complete the proof of the lemma it is only left to get the bound
\left\|\Phi\right\|\leqslant 1
.
Here we need to be careful again about measurability of pointwise quotient norm approximations.
So we will check that the bound

\left\|\Phi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}

holds for every simple
h
.
It is enough since
\Phi
is already known to be bounded, by the previous step.

Similarly to the previous step, from the identity
\left\|P\right\|=1
one deduces

\left\|\Phi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)},\quad f\in\pi^{-1}\{h\}.

(9.2)

Let
\varepsilon>0
and function
\chi
be as in step
3
.
Then

\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}^{p}+\varepsilon=\int_{Y}\left(\inf_{g\in\pi_{0}^{-1}\{h(y)\}}\left\|g\right\|_{G}^{p}+\varepsilon\chi(y)\right)d\mu(y),

and so there exists
g_{\varepsilon}:Y\to G
such that

\inf_{g\in\pi_{0}^{-1}\{h(y)\}}\left\|g\right\|_{G}^{p}+\varepsilon\chi(y)\geqslant\left\|g_{\varepsilon}(y)\right\|_{G}^{p},\quad\pi_{0}(g_{\varepsilon}(y))=h(y)

and it takes at most countably many values,
its partition being the finest one for
h
and
\chi
.
In particular,

g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)

and

\pi(g_{\varepsilon})=h,

which because of (
9.2
) implies

\left\|\Phi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})}^{p}\leqslant\left\|g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)}^{p}\leqslant\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})}^{p}+\varepsilon.

Passing to the limit one obtains

\left\|\Phi h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G)/L^{p}(Y,\mu;G_{0})}\leqslant\left\|h\right\|_{L^{p}(Y,\mu;G/G_{0})},

completing the proof.

∎

We formulate the following simple lemma, that can be either found in
[
22
]

or easily proved with the help of an argument similar to one used in step
6
of the previous proof.

Lemma 20
.

Consider nontrivial closed subspaces
E_{0},F_{0}
in normed spaces
E,F
, respectively.
Let
\mathcal{T}:E\to F
be a linear bounded operator satisfying
\mathcal{T}(E_{0})\subset F_{0}
.
Then there exists a unique linear
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
making the following diagram commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E/E_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F/F_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}\;\;}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\;\;P_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}

(9.3)

Moreover,

\left\|\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}\right\|\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{T}\right\|.

We take

and

We introduce
\mathcal{T}
as the isometric extension of

e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}

defined on the Schwartz space

\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\right)

with the property (
2.19
),
so that

\left\|u\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}}^{2}=\int\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\left\|\mathcal{T}u(\xi)\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}d\xi.

(9.4)

Now let us consider an interval
I=(S,T)

and the closed subspaces

G_{0}=\left\{u\in H^{b}(\mathbb{R})\colon u=0\text{ on }I\right\},

E_{0}=\left\{u\in X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}\colon u=0\text{ on }I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}.

It is known that

and

endowed with the quotient norms, of course.

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
making the following diagram commutative

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}d\xi;H^{b}(I)\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}d\xi;H^{b}(\mathbb{R})\right)/L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}d\xi;G_{0}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}=e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}\;\;}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{2}\;\;}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{-1}}

(9.5)

This commutative diagram makes a complete sense of and proves the identity
(
2.34
).
In other words, the term

e^{ith(\xi)}\mathcal{F}_{x}

staying in (
2.34
)
should be understood as the composition

\Phi^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}

having operator norm equal to unity,
namely,

\left\|u\right\|_{X_{h(\xi)}^{s,b}(S,T)}=\left(\int\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\left\|\Phi^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}u(\xi)\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

(9.6)

In order to appeal to the previous two lemmas, and to prove the claim,
one needs only to show the inclusions

(

10.
A modified Bourgain norm

In preparation for the proof of the cutoff estimates, we now investigate more closely the modified Bourgain norm defined in (
2.35
), and derive some of its key properties.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
b\in(0,1/2)
and
h\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
. Write the norm (
2.17
) as

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|U(t,\xi)\right\|_{H^{b}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2},

where the transform
u(t,x)\mapsto U(t,\xi)
is defined by

U(t,\xi)=\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s}e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi).

By (
2.15
), the above norm is equivalent to, with constants depending only on
b
, the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{\widehat{X}^{s,b}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\left\|U(t,\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\|U(t,\xi)\right\|_{S^{b}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

Inserting here the characteristic function
\mathbb{1}_{(S,T)}(t)
of a time interval
(S,T)
, we compute

\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(S,T)}u\right\|_{\widehat{X}^{s,b}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{S}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{b(t-S)^{2b}}+\frac{1}{b(T-t)^{2b}}\right)\,dt+\left\|U(t,\xi)\right\|_{S^{b}_{t}(S,T)}^{2}\right)\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

(10.1)

By (
2.32
), (
2.34
)
the latter is equivalent to, with constants depending only on
b
,
the restriction norm

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}(S,T)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|U(t,\xi)\right\|_{H^{b}_{t}(S,T)}^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}.

(10.2)

The advantage of the norm (
10.1
) is that it has an explicit expression; there are no restriction norms involved. It is still a bit tricky to work with, however. We will use instead the simpler, modified norm

\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}(S,T)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{(T-S)^{2b}}\int_{S}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{S}^{T}\frac{|U(t,\xi)-U(r,\xi)|^{2}}{|t-r|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)\,d\xi\right)^{1/2},

(10.3)

which is the same as (
2.35
), and turns out to be equivalent to the two previous norms.

In fact, we have the following.

Lemma 21
.

Let
T_{0}>0
and
0<b<1/2
. Consider an interval
(S,T)
with length at most
T_{0}
. Then the norms defined on
X^{s,b}(S,T)
by (
10.1
), (
10.2
) and (
10.3
) are pairwise equivalent, with constants depending only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Moreover, for all
T>0
we have the estimate

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}(0,T)}\geq C\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}(0,T)},

(10.4)

where
C>0
depends only on
b
.

Proof.

By translation invariance we may consider the interval
(0,T)
, where
0<T\leq T_{0}
. By (
2.32
) we already know that (
10.1
) and (
10.2
) are equivalent, uniformly in
T
(of any size). The equivalence of (
10.1
) and (
10.3
) reduces to proving the equivalence of the following norms on
H^{b}(0,T)
:

M_{T}(\phi)=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\phi(t)\right|^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{bt^{2b}}+\frac{1}{b(T-t)^{2b}}\right)\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\left|\phi(t)-\phi(r)\right|^{2}}{\left|t-r\right|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)^{1/2}

and

N_{T}(\phi)=\left(T^{-2b}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\phi(t)\right|^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\left|\phi(t)-\phi(r)\right|^{2}}{\left|t-r\right|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

First, since
0<t<T
implies
T^{-2b}<t^{-2b}
, it is clear that

N_{T}(\phi)\leq b^{1/2}M_{T}(\phi)\quad\text{for all $T>0$}.

(10.5)

It remains to show

M_{T}(\phi)\leq CN_{T}(\phi).

We claim that this holds for
T=T_{0}
. Granting this for the moment, it follows that the inequality holds also for
0<T\leq T_{0}
, since setting
g(s)=\phi\left(sT/T_{0}\right)
and rescaling yields

M_{T}(\phi)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\right)^{1-2b}M_{T_{0}}(g)^{2}\leq C^{2}\left(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\right)^{1-2b}N_{T_{0}}(g)^{2}=C^{2}N_{T}(\phi)^{2}.

It remains to prove the claim, namely
M_{T_{0}}(g)\leq CN_{T_{0}}(g)
for
g\in H^{b}(0,T_{0})
, with
C
depending on
T_{0}
and
b
. But on the one hand, (
2.15
) implies
M_{T_{0}}(g)=\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(0,T_{0})}g\right\|_{H^{b}(\mathbb{R})}
. On the other hand,
N_{T_{0}}(g)\sim_{b,T_{0}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T_{0})}
by (
2.16
). By (
2.31
) it now follows that
M_{T_{0}}(g)\sim_{b,T_{0}}N_{T_{0}}(g)
.

Finally, (
10.4
) is immediate from (
10.5
). This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Before proceeding with the proof of the cutoff estimates, we mention some properties of the modified Bourgain norm (
10.3
).

Lemma 22
.

Let
T_{0}>0
and
0<b<1/2
. Then for all
0<T\leq T_{0}
and all

we have the bounds

(10.6)

and

\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}(0,t)}\leq C\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}(0,r)}+\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}(r,t)}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq r<t\leq T,

(10.7)

where the constants only depend on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
21
. First, (

) follows via the equivalence of (
10.3
) with the restriction norm (
10.2
), and the fact that the latter is increasing with respect to the interval to which we restrict. Second, (
10.7
) follows via the equivalence of (

10.1
), writing
\mathbb{1}_{(0,t)}=\mathbb{1}_{(0,r)}+\mathbb{1}_{(r,t)}
(a.e.) and applying the triangle inequality.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
0<b<1/2
. Assume that
u\in X^{s,b}(0,T)
and set

Then
f
is continuous on
(0,T]
. Moreover, if we additionally assume that

, then

\lim_{t\searrow 0}f(t)=0,

so

extends to a continuous function on

.

Proof.

For

,

(10.8)

Continuity follows from the dominated convergence theorem. It remains to prove
\lim_{t\searrow 0}f(t)=0
. For the second term in (

), this is clear by dominated convergence, and the first term we bound by

t^{-2b}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|u(r)\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\,dr\leq t^{1-2b}\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}\left\|u(r)\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

which tends to zero as
t\searrow 0
if
u\in C([0,T],H^{s})
.
∎

Lemma 24
.

Assume that
u\in X^{s,b}(0,T)\cap C([0,T],H^{s})
is an

-adapted random variable. Then the continuous function
f\colon[0,T]\to[0,\infty)
from Lemma
23
is adapted.

Proof.

For both terms in (

),
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurability follows by Tonelli’s theorem, since

is progressively measurable.

11.
Cutoff estimates in
H^{b}
and
X^{s,b}

Here we prove Proposition
1
, which we restate below for convenience. Let
0<b<1/2
. Fix a smooth, compactly supported function
\theta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and write
\theta_{R}(x)=\theta(x/R)
.

Via the transform
u(t,x)\mapsto U(t,\xi)=\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s}e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)
, and recalling Lemma
21
, we can identify
X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)
with the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{b}(S,T))
with norm (this corresponds to (
10.3
))

\left\|U\right\|_{(S,T)}=\left\|\left(\frac{1}{(T-S)^{2b}}\int_{S}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{S}^{T}\frac{|U(t,\xi)-U(r,\xi)|^{2}}{|t-r|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.

If
S=0
, we simply write
\left\|U\right\|_{T}
. This norm is associated to the inner product

\langle\,U,V\,\rangle_{T}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{T^{2b}}\int_{0}^{T}U(t,\xi)\overline{V(t,\xi)}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\left[U(t,\xi)-U(r,\xi)\right]\overline{\left[V(t,\xi)-V(r,\xi)\right]}}{\left|t-r\right|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\right)\,d\xi.

For a vector
\mathbf{U}=(U_{1},\dots,U_{n})
we write

\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|U_{i}\right\|_{T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

With this notation, and taking into account Lemma
21
, we can now restate Proposition
1
as follows.

Proposition 2
.

Let
T_{0}>0
. Then for all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimates

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}\leqslant C\sqrt{R},

(11.1)

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}\leqslant C\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T},

(11.2)

where the constant
C
depends only on
b
,
T_{0}
and
\theta
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of this result. For convenience, and without loss of generality, we assume that
\left|\theta\right|\leq 1
and
\theta
is supported in
[-2,2]
. Throughout we assume that
T_{0}>0
,
b\in(0,1/2)
and that functions
U,V
etc. are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{b}(S,T))
, and similarly for vectors
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}
etc.

We note the bound, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\theta_{R}(x)-\theta_{R}(y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}\left|x-y\right|.

(11.3)

This holds with
C=\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

We first prove some preliminary lemmas.

11.1.
Preliminary estimates

Lemma 25
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\left\langle\,V,W\,\right\rangle_{t}-\langle\,V,W\,\rangle_{r}\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi\leq C\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2},

(11.4)

where
C
depends only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

We write

\langle\,V,W\,\rangle_{t}=\mu(t)+\nu(t),

where

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=t^{-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}V(s,\zeta)\overline{W(s,\zeta)}\,ds\,d\zeta,

\displaystyle\nu(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left[V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right]\overline{\left[W(s,\zeta)-W(\sigma,\zeta)\right]}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta.

The left side of (
11.4
) is therefore bounded by
2(I+J)
, where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\mu(t)-\mu(r)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\nu(t)-\nu(r)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi.

To estimate
J
we note that

\nu(t)-\nu(r)=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left[V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right]\overline{\left[W(s,\zeta)-W(\sigma,\zeta)\right]}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta,

so by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\left|\nu(t)-\nu(r)\right|^{2}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left|V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta\\
\times 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left|W(s,\zeta)-W(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta,

where the last line is bounded by
\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}
. Thus

J\leq\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\\
\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left|V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta\right)\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

(11.5)

and Lemma
26
below yields the desired estimate
J\leq C\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}
.

In
I
we split the innermost integral as
\int_{0}^{t/2}+\int_{t/2}^{t}
and write
I=I_{1}+I_{2}

accordingly. The term
I_{1}
is easy to handle since
t-r\sim t
. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz and (
10.6
) to bound
\left|\mu(t)\right|\leq\left\|V\right\|_{t}\left\|W\right\|_{t}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}\left\|W\right\|_{T}
, we then simply estimate

I_{1}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}t^{-2b}\,dt\,d\xi\right)\leq C^{\prime}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2},

where we used Lemma
21
in the last step.

It remains to consider
I_{2}
. Writing

\mu(t)=t^{1-2b}f(t),\quad\text{where}\quad f(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds,\quad g(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(s,\zeta)\overline{W(s,\zeta)}\,d\zeta,

we expand

\displaystyle\mu(t)-\mu(r)

\displaystyle=(t^{1-2b}-r^{1-2b})f(t)+r^{1-2b}[f(t)-f(r)]

\displaystyle=(t^{1-2b}-r^{1-2b})f(t)+r^{1-2b}\left[\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{r}\right)\int_{0}^{r}g(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{t}\int_{r}^{t}g(s)\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{1-2b}-r^{1-2b}}{t^{1-2b}}\mu(t)+\frac{r-t}{t}\mu(r)+\frac{r^{1-2b}}{t}\int_{r}^{t}g(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=:\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}.

Thus
I_{2}\leq 3(K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3})
, where

K_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{t/2}^{t}\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi\qquad(j=1,2,3).

For
K_{2}
we use once more the bound
\left|\mu(r)\right|\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}\left\|W\right\|_{T}
and get

K_{2}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}t^{-2}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-r)^{1-2b}\,dr\right)\,dt\,d\xi\\
\leq C^{\prime}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}t^{-2b}\,dt\,d\xi\leq C^{\prime\prime}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2}.

The same bound is obtained for
K_{1}
, since there
t^{1-2b}-r^{1-2b}\sim t^{-2b}(t-r)
.

For
K_{3}
we bound, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

(t-r)^{-2b}\int_{r}^{t}\left|g(s)\right|\,ds\leq\left\|V\right\|_{(r,t)}\left\|W\right\|_{(r,t)}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}\left\|W\right\|_{T},

where the last inequality follows from (
10.6
). Thus

K_{3}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}t^{-4b}\left(\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-r)^{2b-1}\,dr\right)\,dt\,d\xi\\
\leq C^{\prime}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}t^{-2b}\,dt\,d\xi\leq C^{\prime\prime}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|W\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following lemma was used in the above proof.

Lemma 26
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{r}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\left|V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta\right)\,dr\,dt\,d\xi\\
\leq C\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2},

with a constant
C
depending only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

Here
0<r<s<t<T
and
0<\sigma<s
. Rearranging the order of the integrations, we rewrite the integral as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{dr}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\right)\,dt\,d\xi\right)\frac{\left|V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{1}{2b(t-s)^{2b}}\,dt\,d\xi\right)\frac{\left|V(s,\zeta)-V(\sigma,\zeta)\right|^{2}}{(s-\sigma)^{1+2b}}\,d\sigma\,ds\,d\zeta.

But by Lemma
21
and (
10.6
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{T}\left|U(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{1}{2b(t-s)^{2b}}\,dt\,d\xi\leq C\left\|U\right\|_{(s,T)}^{2}\leq C\left\|U\right\|_{T}^{2},

and the claimed inequality then follows.
∎

We will also need the following double mean value theorem.

Lemma 27
.

For all
x,y,X,Y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\theta(x)-\theta(y)-\theta(X)+\theta(Y)\right|\\
\leq\left\|\theta^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\min\left(\left|x-y\right|,\left|X-Y\right|\right)\max\left(\left|x-X\right|,\left|y-Y\right|\right)+\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|x-y-X+Y\right|.

Proof.

Fix
x,y,X,Y
. By symmetry we may assume
\left|x-y\right|\leq\left|X-Y\right|
. Defining
\kappa(t)=y+t(x-y)
and
\rho(t)=Y+t(X-Y)
, we write

\theta(x)-\theta(y)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\theta^{\prime}\left(\kappa(t)\right)\,dt\right)(x-y)=:I_{1}(x-y)

and

\theta(X)-\theta(Y)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\theta^{\prime}\left(\rho(t)\right)\,dt\right)(X-Y)=:I_{2}(X-Y).

Then

\theta(x)-\theta(y)-\theta(X)+\theta(Y)=(I_{1}-I_{2})(x-y)+I_{2}(x-y-X+Y).

Clearly,
\left|I_{2}\right|\leq\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, so it only remains to check that

\left|I_{1}-I_{2}\right|\leq\left\|\theta^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|x-X\right|+\left|y-Y\right|\right).

But this is clear since

I_{1}-I_{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\theta^{\prime\prime}\left(\rho(t)+s\left[\kappa(t)-\rho(t)\right]\right)\,ds\right)\left[\kappa(t)-\rho(t)\right]\,dt

and

\left|\kappa(t)-\rho(t)\right|=\left|y-Y+t(x-y-X+Y)\right|\leq(1-t)\left|y-Y\right|+t\left|x-X\right|.

∎

With these preliminary results in hand, we are now ready to start the proof of Proposition
2
. We split the argument into several steps.

11.2.
Cutoff estimate, version I

Lemma 28
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimate

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)V\right\|_{T}\leq C\left(1+R^{-1}\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}^{2}\right)\left\|V\right\|_{T},

(11.6)

with a constant
C
depending only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

Setting
\psi(t)=\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})
, the square of the left side of (
11.6
) equals
A+2B
, where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T^{-2b}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\psi(t)V(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\xi,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\psi(t)V(t,\xi)-\psi(r)V(r,\xi)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

and
\left|\psi(t)\right|\leq 1
implies
A\leq\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}
. Clearly,
B\leq 2(B_{1}+B_{2})
,
where

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\psi(r)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|V(t,\xi)-V(r,\xi)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|V(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\psi(t)-\psi(r)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

where
\left|\psi(r)\right|\leq 1
implies
B_{1}\leq\left\|v_{j}\right\|_{T}^{2}
. In
B_{2}
we estimate, using (
11.3
),

\left|\psi(t)-\psi(r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}\left|\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{r}^{2}\right|,

(11.7)

and obtain
B_{2}\leq R^{-2}\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}^{4}\left\|V\right\|_{T}^{2}
as a consequence of Lemma
25
.
∎

Remark 6
.

The estimate (
11.6
), using the equivalent norm (
10.1
) instead of (
10.3
), is claimed in
[
15
]
, but there is a gap in the proof. To explain the problem, let us denote by
|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{T}
the norm used in
[
15
]
, that is, the norm given by (
10.1
):

|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{T}=\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\widehat{X}^{s,b}},\quad\text{where $\mathbf{U}(t,\xi)=\left\langle\,\xi\,\right\rangle^{s}e^{ith(\xi)}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(t,\xi)$.}

Then by the triangle inequality we have, for
0<r<t
,

\left||\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{t}-|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{r}\right|\leq\left\|\mathbb{1}_{(r,t)}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\widehat{X}^{s,b}}=|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{(r,t)}.

(11.8)

Combining (
11.8
) with the analogue of (
11.7
) for the norm
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{t}
yields

\left|\psi(t)-\psi(r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{(r,t)}|\!|\!|\mathbf{U}|\!|\!|_{t},

(11.9)

which is essentially what was used in
[
15
]
, instead of (
11.7
). But it is easy to see that (
11.9
) is not enough to prove the estimate for
B_{2}
. Indeed, take
\mathbf{U}
and
V
both to be the function
\mathbb{1}_{(0,T)}(t)f(\xi)
, where
0<T<1
and
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Then by (
2.37
) we have
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{(r,t)}\sim(t-r)^{1/2-b}
and
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}\sim t^{1/2-b}
, so if we estimate
B_{2}
by using (
11.9
), then we get

B_{2}\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}t^{1-2b}\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-4b}\,dr\,dt.

But the right hand side equals
+\infty
unless
b<1/4
.

11.3.
Cutoff estimate, version II

As a corollary to Lemma
28
, we obtain the following variation on that result, proving the bound (
11.1
) in Proposition
2
, as well as (
11.2
) in the case
\mathbf{V}=0
.

Lemma 29
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimates

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)V\right\|_{T}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T},

(11.10)

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}\leq C\sqrt{R},

(11.11)

with a constant
C
depending only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

We first prove (
11.10
). Recall the assumption
\supp\theta\subset[-2,2]
. So if
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\geq 2R
for all
t\in(0,T)
, then the left side of the inequality equals zero. It remains to consider the case where
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}<2R
for some
t\in(0,T)
. Define

T_{\mathbf{U}}=\sup\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}<2R\right\}.

Then

\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}^{2}\leq 2R,

(11.12)

by the continuity of
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}
with respect to
t>0
(see Lemma
23
). And if
T_{\mathbf{U}}<T
, then
\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})=0
for
t\in[T_{\mathbf{U}},T]
. So (
10.7
) yields (if
T_{\mathbf{U}}=T
, this holds trivially)

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)V\right\|_{T}\leq C\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)V\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}},

and by Lemma
28
the right hand side is dominated by

C\left(1+R^{-1}\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}^{2}\right)\left\|V\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}\leq 3C\left\|V\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}},

where we used (
11.12
). By (
10.6
),

\left\|V\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}\leq C\left\|V\right\|_{T}
, which proves (
11.10
). Taking now
V=U_{j}
and using the bound (
11.12
), we get (
11.11
).
∎

11.4.
Difference estimate, version I

Lemma 30
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimates

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}\leq C\left(1+\frac{M^{2}}{R}+\frac{M^{4}}{R^{2}}\right)\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T},

(11.13)

where

M=\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T}+\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}

and the constant
C
depends only on
b
and
T_{0}

Proof.

Setting
\psi(t)=\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})
and
\chi(t)=\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})
, we reduce to proving, for
1\leq j\leq n
,

\left\|[\psi(t)-\chi(t)]U_{j}\right\|_{T}\leq\mathrm{r.h.s.}\eqref{difference}

and

\left\|\chi(t)(U_{j}-V_{j})\right\|_{T}\leq C\left\|U_{j}-V_{j}\right\|_{T}.

The latter holds by (
11.10
), so we concentrate on the former, whose left hand side squared equals
A+2B
, where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T^{-2b}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\psi(t)-\chi(t)\right|^{2}\left|U_{j}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\,dt\,d\xi,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|[\psi(t)-\chi(t)]U_{j}(t,\xi)-[\psi(r)-\chi(r)]U_{j}(r,\xi)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi.

Using (
11.3
) we estimate

\left|\psi(t)-\chi(t)\right|\leq\frac{C}{R}\left|\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right|.

(11.14)

Writing

\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\langle\,U_{i}-V_{i},U_{i}\,\rangle_{t}+\langle\,V_{i},U_{i}-V_{i}\,\rangle_{t}\right)

(11.15)

and applying Cauchy-Schwarz yields

\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}\leq CM^{2}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2}\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$},

(11.16)

implying
A\leq CR^{-2}M^{4}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2}
, as desired.

It remains to bound the term
B
. Defining

\kappa=\psi-\chi

we write
B\leq 2(B_{1}+B_{2})
, where

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\kappa(r)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|U_{j}(t,\xi)-U_{j}(r,\xi)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi,

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U_{j}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\kappa(t)-\kappa(r)\right|^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi.

By (
11.14
) and (
11.16
),
\left|\kappa(r)\right|^{2}\leq CR^{-2}M^{2}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2}
,
so

B_{1}\leq CR^{-2}M^{4}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2}.

In
B_{2}
we write out

\kappa(t)-\kappa(r)=\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})-\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})-\left[\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{r}^{2})-\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{r}^{2})\right]

and apply Lemma
27
to get

B_{2}\leq C\left(\frac{I}{R^{4}}+\frac{J}{R^{2}}\right),

where

I=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U_{j}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{r}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{r}^{2}\right)^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi

and

J=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|U_{j}(t,\xi))\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{r}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{r}^{2}\right)^{2}}{(t-r)^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt\,d\xi.

By (
11.16
) and Lemma
25
,

I\leq C_{b}M^{8}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2},

which is acceptable. Finally, using (
11.15
) and Lemma
25
,

J\leq C_{b}M^{4}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}^{2},

and this concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

11.5.
Difference estimate, version II

We are now in a position to finish the proof of Proposition
2
, by proving the bound (
11.2
).

Lemma 31
.

For all
T\in(0,T_{0}]
and
R>0
we have the estimate

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T},

(11.17)

where the constant
C
depends only on
b
and
T_{0}
.

Proof.

First, if
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\geq 2R
and
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\geq 2R
for all
t\in[0,T]
, then the left side equals zero.

Second, consider the hybrid case where
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}\geq 2R
for all
t\in[0,T]
, whereas
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}<2R
for some
t\in[0,T]
. Then
\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})=0
for
t\in[0,T]
. Defining
T_{\mathbf{V}}
as in the proof of Lemma
29
, we find

\mathrm{l.h.s.}\eqref{difference2}=\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T}\leq C\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2}\right)\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\leq C\sqrt{2R},

by Lemma
29
. So if
\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\geq\sqrt{R}
, we are done. If, on the other hand,
\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{v}}}\leq\sqrt{R}
, then by the triangle inequality,
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\leq(1+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{R}
, since
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{2}\leq 2R
. Then (
11.17
) follows by applying Lemma
30
to

\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{v}}}=\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}.

The other hybrid case is symmetric, so we are only left with the case where
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2}<2R
for some
t\in[0,T]
and
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{s}^{2}<2R
for some
s\in[0,T]
. Define
T_{\mathbf{U}}
and
T_{\mathbf{V}}
as in the proof of Lemma
29
, so
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}^{2}\leq 2R
and
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{2}\leq 2R
. By symmetry we may assume
T_{\mathbf{U}}\leq T_{\mathbf{V}}
, and then (
10.7
) yields

\mathrm{l.h.s.}\eqref{difference2}\leq C\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}.

If
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{2}\leq 8R
, we now obtain (
11.17
) by Lemma
30
. If, on the other hand,
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{2}>8R
, then
\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\geq\sqrt{2R}
, and using (
10.7
) we obtain

\mathrm{l.h.s.}\eqref{difference2}\leq C\left(\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{U}-\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}+\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{(T_{\mathbf{U}},T_{\mathbf{V}})}\right).

The first term on the right can be handled by Lemma
30
, since
\left\|\mathbf{U}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}^{2}\leq 2R
and
\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{U}}}^{2}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{2}\leq C2R
. For the second term, we get by (
10.6
) and Lemma
29
,

\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{(T_{\mathbf{U}},T_{\mathbf{V}})}\leq C\left\|\theta_{R}(\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{t}^{2})\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}},

where we used
\left\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}\geq\sqrt{2R}\geq\left\|\mathbf{V}\right\|_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}
.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

11.6.
Sobolev–Slobodeckij norm on
H^{b}(0,T)

All the properties discussed above in this section
are valid for usual Sobolev norms,
for functions depending only on the time variable
t
.
The proofs can be repeated directly without much of a difference.
However, we can get also those properties easily by
considering

u(t)=\phi(t)S_{h(\xi)}(t)f

with
\phi\in H^{b}(S,T)
and
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Indeed, from (
2.34
) and (
2.35
) it is clear that

\left\|u\right\|_{X^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}=\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|u\right\|_{\widetilde{X}^{s,b}_{h(\xi)}(S,T)}=\left\|\phi\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(S,T)}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}},

where

\left\|\phi\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(S,T)}=\frac{1}{(T-S)^{2b}}\int_{S}^{T}\left|\phi(t)\right|^{2}\,dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{S}^{T}\frac{\left|\phi(t)-\phi(r)\right|^{2}}{\left|t-r\right|^{1+2b}}\,dr\,dt.

Normalising by taking
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}=1
, we then get from Lemma
21
, for
b\in(0,1/2)
,

C_{T_{0},b}^{-1}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(S,T)}\leq C_{T_{0},b}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H^{b}(S,T)}\qquad(0\leq S<T\leq T_{0}),

(11.18)

and Proposition
1
has the following analogue.

Proposition 3
.

Let
T_{0}>0
and
b\in(0,1/2)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that
\phi_{i},\Phi_{i}\in H^{b}(0,T_{0})
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Then for
T\in(0,T_{0}]
,
R>0
and
1\leq j\leq n
we have the estimates

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(0,t)}^{2}\right)\phi_{j}(t)\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)}\leqslant C\sqrt{R},

\left\|\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(0,t)}^{2}\right)\phi_{j}(t)-\theta_{R}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\Phi_{i}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{b}(0,t)}^{2}\right)\Phi_{j}(t)\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\phi_{i}-\Phi_{i}\right\|_{H^{b}(0,T)},

where
C
depends only on
b
,
T_{0}
and
\theta
.

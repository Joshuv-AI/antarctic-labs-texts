The weighted ambient metric and the singular Ricci flow
Ayush Khaitan

Abstract
. We prove the existence and uniqueness of a weighted analogue of the Fefferman-Graham ambient metric for manifolds with density. We then show that this ambient metric forms the natural geometric framework for the singular Ricci flow: given a singular gradient Ricci flow spacetime in the Kleiner-Lott sense, we construct a unique global ambient half-space from it. We also prove the converse, that every global ambient space contains a singular gradient Ricci flow spacetime, thereby completing the correspondence. Our main application is the construction of infinite families of fully non-linear analogues of Perelman’s ℱ \mathcal{F} and 𝒲 \mathcal{W} functionals. We extend Perelman’s monotonicity result to these two families of functionals under several conditions, including for shrinking solitons and Einstein manifolds. We do so by constructing a “Ricci flow vector field” in the ambient space, which may be of independent research interest. We also prove that the weighted GJMS operators associated with the weighted ambient metric are formally self-adjoint, and that the associated weighted renormalized volume coefficients are variational.

Introduction

The Fefferman–Graham ambient space
[
10
]
is a formally Ricci flat space canonically associated to a given conformal manifold.
Among its many applications are the classification of local scalar conformal invariants
[
1
,
12
]
and the construction of a family of conformally covariant operators, called GJMS operators, with leading-order term a power of the Laplacian
[
13
]
.

A
manifold with density

(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
is a Riemannian manifold with a smooth measure
e^{-\phi}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
, and may be thought of as a smooth metric measure space with
m=\infty
. Among its applications is the realization of the
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{W}
gradient Ricci flows
[
17
]
. A manifold with density
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
is a
gradient Ricci soliton
if
\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi=\lambda g
.

In
[
7
,
6
]
, Case constructs two higher fully non-linear analogues of the
\mathcal{W}
-functional,
\mathcal{W}_{2,\phi}
and
\mathcal{W}_{3,\phi}
, that are also conformally invariant, and proves that they are variational. We bring this program to a completion by constructing the weighted ambient space for manifolds with density, which allows us to systematically construct infinite families of fully non-linear analogues of both the
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{W}
functionals. We prove that these families are variational, and also construct the associated weighted GJMS operators, and weighted renormalized volume coefficients.

Moreover, we establish a dual correspondence between the weighted ambient metric and the singular Ricci flow by proving that given a singular gradient Ricci flow spacetime
\mathcal{H}
in the Kleiner-Lott sense
[
16
]
, we can construct a unique global ambient half-space structure on
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}
. We also prove that every global ambient space contains a singular Ricci flow spacetime, thereby completing the correspondence between the two.

The main application of our results is the construction of infinite families of fully non-linear analogues of Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
–functional and
\mathcal{W}
–functional. We also prove that, under suitable conditions, these families of functionals are monotone increasing or decreasing under the singular Ricci flow. We prove that the renormalized volume coefficients, which are the integrands of these fully non-linear analogues of Perelman’s functionals, are variational, and prove that shrinking solitons are stable points of the
\mathcal{W}
functional in a conformal class.

Our constructions require us to construct a vector field in the weighted ambient space, along which we have a solution to the gradient Ricci flow. By the use of this vector field, we are also able to provide short proofs of the monotonicity of Perelman’s
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{W}
functionals that, to our knowledge, are not present in the literature. See
Lemmas

5.2
and
6.2
for more details. This vector field may be of independent research interest.

2.
Background

We first recall the definition of a manifold with density
[
6
]
, and then discuss some weighted invariants.

Definition 2.1
.

A
manifold with density
is a tuple
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
, consisting of a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, a smooth measure
e^{-\phi}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
determined by the function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and the Riemannian volume element
\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
, and a parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

The
weighted Bianchi identity
for
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
is

(2.1)

2(\delta_{\phi}\text{Ric}_{\phi})-\nabla R_{\phi}=0.

The
weighted Bach tensor

B_{\phi}
is defined as
[
7
, Equation (1.1)]

(2.2)

\begin{split}B_{\phi}=&\delta_{\phi}dP_{\phi}+\mathrm{Rm}\cdot P_{\phi},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\phi}dP_{\phi}\right)(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nabla_{E_{i}}P_{\phi}\left(E_{i},X,Y\right)-dP_{\phi}(\nabla\phi,X,Y),

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Rm}\cdot P_{\phi}\right)(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{Rm}\left(E_{i},X,E_{j},Y\right){P_{\phi}}\left(E_{i},E_{j}\right)

for all
p\in M
and all
X,Y\in T_{p}M
, where
\left\{E_{i}\right\}\subset T_{p}M
is an orthonormal basis.

Definition 2.2
.

Two metric measure structures
(g,\phi)
and
(\widehat{g},\widehat{\phi})
are defined to be
pointwise conformally equivalent
if
(\widehat{g},\widehat{\phi})=(g,\phi+\omega)
for some
\omega\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

We have the following conformal transformation formulas
[
8
, Lemma (3.3)]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,\phi\lambda)
be a manifold with density, and let
\widehat{\phi}=\phi+\omega
. Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=R_{\phi}+2\Delta\omega-2g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\omega)+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+2\lambda\omega,

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=F_{\phi}+\Delta\omega-2g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\omega)+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+2\lambda\omega,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ric}_{\phi}+\nabla^{2}\omega,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=Y_{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}(-2g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\omega)+|\nabla\omega|^{2}-2\lambda\omega).

Definition 2.4
.

A manifold with density
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
is weighted
locally conformally flat

[
6
]
if
g
is a flat metric.

Note that if
g
is flat and
\lambda\neq 0
, there need not exist a
\phi
such that
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
is a gradient Ricci soliton.

Definition 2.5
.

Gradient Ricci solitons are manifolds with density, denoted as
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
, such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle P_{\phi}

\displaystyle=0.

Lemma 2.6
.

For a gradient Ricci soliton, we have
F_{\phi}=c
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We obtain from
Equation

2.4
and the Bianchi identity that

(2.5)

|\nabla\phi|^{2}+R-2\lambda\phi=c

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Subtracting
Equation

2.5
from the trace of
Equation

2.4
implies that
F_{\phi}
is constant.
∎

3.
Weighted ambient metric

Let
(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)
be a manifold with density. Denote by
\mathcal{E}
the trivial line bundle over
M
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the ray subbundle of
\mathrm{S}^{2}(T^{*}M)\oplus\mathcal{E}
consisting of all triples
(x,g_{x},l)
such that
l=\phi(x)+v
for some
v\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in M
. We define the dilation
\delta_{s}\colon\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathcal{G}
by
(x,g_{x},l)\mapsto(x,g_{x},l+s)
. Let
T:={\left.\kern-1.2pt\frac{d}{ds}\delta_{s}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{s=0}}}
denote the infinitesimal generator of dilations. Also, let
\pi\colon(x,g_{x},l)\mapsto x
be the projection from
\mathcal{G}
to
M
. There is a canonical metric measure structure
(\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi})
on
\mathcal{G}
defined by
\boldsymbol{g}(X,Y)=g_{x}(\pi_{*}X,\pi_{*}Y)
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}(x,g_{x},l)=l
.

Fixing
(g,\phi)
, we define a coordinate chart

\mathcal{G}\mapsto\mathbb{R}\times M:(x,g_{x},\phi(x)+v)\mapsto(-v,x)

for
v\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we have the embedding

\iota\colon\mathcal{G}\ \hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{R},(-v,x)\mapsto(-v,x,0).

Note that if
(x^{1},\dots x^{n})
denote the local coordinates on
M
, then we consider
(v,x^{1},\dots,x^{n},u)
to be the local coordinates on
\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We use
0
to label the
v
-component,
\infty
to label the
u
-component, lower case Latin to label coordinates on
M
and upper case Latin to label coordinates on
\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We choose the sign convention
(-v,x,0)
instead of
(v,x,0)
for the embedding, in order to ensure consistency with
[
5
,
10
]
. For example, if the embedding was of the form
(v,x,0)
, then
{\left.\kern-1.2pt(\partial_{u}g,\partial_{u}\phi)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=0}}
would be
(-2P_{\phi},Y_{\phi})
, which would be inconsistent with the formulas
{\left.\kern-1.2pt(\partial_{\rho}g,\partial_{\rho}\phi)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\rho=0}}=(2P_{\phi},-Y_{\phi})
in
[
5
]
, and the formula
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\partial_{\rho}g\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\rho=0}}=2P
in
[
10
]
.

We extend the dilation to
\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
as
\delta_{s}(v,x,u)=(v+(\lambda u-1)^{-1}s,x,u)
. Hence, we have

(3.1)

T=(\lambda u-1)^{-1}\partial_{v},

where
T
is the infinitesimal generator of dilations
\delta_{s}
.

Given
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we define

[\phi]:=\{\phi+\omega\mid\omega\in C^{\infty}(M)\}

to be the conformal structure of
\phi
.
We now define pre-ambient spaces and special cases thereof.

Definition 3.1
.

(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},\lambda)
is called a
pre-ambient space
of the conformal manifold
(M^{n},g,[\phi],\lambda)
if

(i)

\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a dilation-invariant neighborhood of
\mathcal{G}\times\{0\}
;

(ii)

\widetilde{g}
is a smooth metric of signature
(n+1,1)
and
\widetilde{\phi}
a smooth function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
;

(iii)

\delta_{s}^{*}\widetilde{g}=\widetilde{g}
and
\delta_{s}^{*}\widetilde{\phi}=\widetilde{\phi}+s
;

(iv)

(\iota^{*}\widetilde{g},\iota^{*}\widetilde{\phi})=(\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi})
; and

(v)

for each
p\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, the dilation orbit
s\mapsto\delta_{s}p
is formally a geodesic for
\widetilde{g}
to first order, i.e.
\nabla_{\partial_{v}}\partial_{v}=0\bmod\,O(u)
.

By the homogeneity requirements of
(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi})
,
\widetilde{g}
is independent of
v
, and
\widetilde{\phi}
is of the form
\phi_{u}-v+\lambda uv
, where
\phi_{u}
is independent of
v
and
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\phi_{u}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{u=0}}=\phi
.

We now define
normal
pre-ambient spaces.

Definition 3.2
.

A pre-ambient space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},\lambda)
is said to be in
normal form
relative to
(g,\phi)
if

(i)

for each fixed
z\in\mathcal{G}
, the set of
u\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(z,u)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
is an open interval
I_{z}
containing
0
;

(ii)

for each
z\in\mathcal{G}
, the curve on
I_{z}
defined as
u\mapsto(z,u)
is a geodesic in
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
; and

(iii)

on
\mathcal{G}
, it holds that
\widetilde{g}=\boldsymbol{g}+2dudv
.

Note that
Item

(v)
is generally not included as a part of the definition of a pre-ambient metric (cf.
[
10
]
*Definition 2.7), and that
Definition

3.1
is actually the definition of a pre-ambient metric that is
straight
(cf.
[
10
]
) to first order. We introduce
Item

(v)
to remove the indeterminacy in the values of
\widetilde{g}_{00},\widetilde{g}_{0i}
in a normal ambient metric. Our focus in the current paper is on a specific class of metrics for which we observe gradient Ricci flow; i.e. we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle(L_{X}g_{u})_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2[(\mathrm{Ric}_{\phi})_{u}]_{ij}\bmod O(u^{\infty}),

\displaystyle L_{X}\widetilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=-(R_{u}+\Delta\phi_{u})\bmod O(u^{\infty})

for some vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and ambient metrics that satisfy
Item

(v)
suffice for our purposes.

Also note that
Item

(v)
is no longer equivalent to
\nabla T=\mathrm{Id}
, or that
\widetilde{g}(2T,\cdot)=d(\widetilde{g}(T,T))
, as it is in
[
9
, Proposition (2.4)]
. This is clear from
Equation

3.1
.

We now show that for a normal ambient metric,
\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}=1
, and
\widetilde{g}_{\infty i}=\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},\lambda)
be a pre-ambient space such that for each
z\in\mathcal{G}
, the set of all
u\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(z,u)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
is an open interval containing
0
. Then
(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi})
is in normal form if and only if
\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}=1
and
\widetilde{g}_{\infty i}=\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}=0
.

Proof.

A normal pre-ambient space has

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\mathcal{G}}}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{g}_{i\infty}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\left.\kern-1.2pt\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle=1.

The
u
-lines are geodesics if and only if the Christoffel symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\infty\infty I}
,
I\in\{0,i,\infty\}
, vanish. Taking
I=\infty
gives
\partial_{u}\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}=0
, and hence
\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}=0.
Now taking
I=i
and
I=0
gives
\widetilde{g}_{\infty i}=0
and
\widetilde{g}_{\infty 0}=1
.

The converse follows similarly.
∎

Definition 3.4

A weighted ambient space for the conformal manifold

such that at

, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\equiv O(u^{\infty}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\equiv O(u^{\infty}).

Definition 3.5
.

We say that two pre-ambient spaces

\mathcal{U}_{2}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{2}

(i)

\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
both contain
\mathcal{G}\times\{0\}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
are dilation-invariant and
\psi
commutes with dilations;

(iii)

the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathcal{G}\times\{0\}
is the identity map; and

(iv)

(\widetilde{g}_{1}-\psi^{*}\widetilde{g}_{2},\widetilde{\phi}_{1}-\psi^{*}\widetilde{\phi}_{2})\equiv O(u^{\infty})

Recall that
\phi
determines the fiber coordinate

and the horizontal subbundle
\mathrm{ker}(dv)\subset T\mathcal{G}
.

A key step in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is the construction and classification of normal weighted ambient metrics (cf.
[

]

Theorem 3.6
.

Given
(M^{n},g,[\phi],\lambda)
,

(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},\mu)
for it. Moreover, if

(\widetilde{g}_{1}-\widetilde{g}_{2},\widetilde{\phi}_{1}-\widetilde{\phi}_{2})\equiv O(u^{\infty})

We prove

in

.
We now show that any ambient space is ambient-equivalent to a normal ambient space. In order to do so, we first construct a
g
-transversal vector
V_{z}
. Recall that
\phi
determines the fiber coordinate

may be viewed as a subspace of

via the inclusion

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists a vector
V_{z}\in T_{(z,0)}(\mathcal{G}\times\mathbb{R})
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(V,T)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(V,X)

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{M}_{z},

\displaystyle=0.

Moreover,

is transverse to

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=V^{0}\partial_{v}+V^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}+V\partial_{u},

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{g}_{ij}(x)dx^{i}dx^{j}+2\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}dvdu+2\widetilde{g}_{i\infty}dx^{i}du+\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}(du)^{2}.

Equation

\displaystyle-\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}V^{0}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle 2\widetilde{g}_{0\infty}V^{0}V^{\infty}+\widetilde{g}_{ij}V^{i}V^{j}+2\widetilde{g}_{i\infty}V^{i}V^{\infty}+\widetilde{g}_{\infty\infty}\left(V^{\infty}\right)^{2}

We can use

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},\lambda)
be a weighted ambient space for the conformal manifold
(M^{n},g,[\phi],\lambda)
. Then it is ambient-equivalent to a normal ambient space for
(M^{n},g,[\phi],\lambda)
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathcal{G}
, let
V_{z}
be the
g
-transversal for
\widetilde{g}
at

. Let

be a geodesic for
\widetilde{g}
, with initial conditions

needn’t be geodesically complete,

is defined only for

into

for all

\widetilde{g}
, with initial conditions

be an interval containing 0. Assume that
(z,t)\in\mathcal{U}_{1}
for all

is a geodesic for
\widetilde{g}
, with initial conditions

Next, we define

containing 0. It follows that for each fixed

\widetilde{g}
.

Since

, so is

. For each fixed

\psi^{*}\widetilde{g}

\displaystyle\left(\psi^{*}\widetilde{g}\right)\left(\partial_{t},T\right)=1,

\displaystyle\left(\psi^{*}\widetilde{g}\right)\left(\partial_{t},X\right)=0\text{ for }X\in TM,

6.
The
\mathcal{W}
–gradient Ricci flow

Consider the following system of equations studied by Perelman
[
17
]
*(3.3)

(6.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{Ric}_{ij},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=-R-\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{n}{2\tau},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau

\displaystyle=-1.

Pulling
(g,\phi,\tau)
in
Equation

6.1
back by the family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\nabla\phi
, we get the
\mathcal{W}
–gradient Ricci flow, defined as

(6.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2(\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=-R-\Delta\phi+\frac{n}{2\tau},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau

\displaystyle=-1.

Perelman proves that the
\mathcal{W}
functional is monotone increasing under the Ricci flow, as defined by
Equation

6.1
. He does so by proving that
\mathcal{W}
is monotone increasing under the
\mathcal{W}
–gradient Ricci flow defined by
Equation

6.2
, and that it is unchanged when we pull back
(g,\phi,\tau)
by the one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms generated by
\nabla\phi
.

Let
\tau>0
.
We now show that there exists a vector field in the space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),(2\tau(s))^{-1})\times(-\epsilon,\tau)
, along which we have a solution
\mathcal{W}
–gradient Ricci flow.

Proof of
Theorem

1.11
.

In an ambient space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\phi},(2\tau)^{-1})
, consider the vector field

X:=(1-(2\tau)^{-1}u)^{-1}(v_{1,\phi})_{u}\partial_{v}-(1-(2\tau)^{-1}u)\partial_{u},

where
(v_{1,\phi})_{u}=-\frac{1}{2}[R+2\Delta\phi-|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\tau^{-1}(\phi-n)]
.
We have

(6.3)

\displaystyle L_{X}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2(\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi)+\frac{1}{\tau}g,

\displaystyle L_{X}\phi

\displaystyle=-R-\Delta\phi+\frac{n}{2\tau}.

Now consider
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),{(2\tau(s))}^{-1})\times(-\epsilon,\tau)
, with
s\in(-\epsilon,\tau)
being the parameter. For each
s
, we have that
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),({2\tau(s)})^{-1})\times\{s\}
is a normal global weighted ambient space. Given a manifold with density

(M^{n},g,\phi,\lambda)\subset(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),(2\tau(s))^{-1}),

we have local normal coordinates
\{v,x^{i},u(s)\}
, and a vector
X
, such that
Equation

6.3
is satisfied. In terms of the set of local coordinates
\{v,x^{i},u(s),s\}
on
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),{(2\tau(s))}^{-1})\times(-\epsilon,\tau)
, let

(6.4)

\displaystyle\tau(s)

\displaystyle=(1-s/\tau)\tau,

\displaystyle g_{u(s)}(s)

\displaystyle=(1-s/\tau)g_{u},

\displaystyle\phi_{u(s)}(s)

\displaystyle=\phi_{u}.

Note that
(g_{u(s)}(s),\phi_{u(s)}(s))
does indeed help form the normal ambient metric for each
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),(2\tau(s))^{-1})

scales as
(1-s/\tau)u
, while

remain constant with

. We have from
Equation

6.4
,

(6.5)

\displaystyle{\partial_{s}}\tau(s)

\displaystyle=-1,

\displaystyle{\partial_{s}}g_{u(s)}(s)

\displaystyle=-g_{u}=-\frac{1}{\tau(s)}g_{u(s)}(s).

From
Equation

6.5
, for

Y:=X+\partial_{s},

we have for all

\displaystyle L_{Y}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2(\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^{2}\phi),

\displaystyle L_{Y}\phi

\displaystyle=-R-\Delta\phi+\frac{n}{2\tau},

\displaystyle L_{Y}\tau

\displaystyle=-1,

which is the same as
Equation

6.1
. Therefore, the gradient Ricci flow is generated by

in
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\times(-\epsilon,\tau)
.
∎

Note that the coordinate
u\leq 0
in these ambient half-spaces. Hence,
(1-(2\tau(s))^{-1}u)\geq 1
, and the vector
Y
makes sense throughout the space.
Also, observe that the gradient Ricci flow spacetime, as defined in

1.5
, lies along
\partial_{t}
and hence transverse to each ambient space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}},\widetilde{g}(s),\widetilde{\phi}(s),(2\tau(s))^{-1})

We have

\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}=\int\tau^{k}v_{k,\phi}(4\pi\tau)^{-n/2}e^{-\phi}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}.

Perelman proved
[

\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}

Lemma 6.1
.

We have

(6.6)

\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}(cg,\phi,c\tau)=\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}(g,\phi,\tau)

Proof.

Consider the re-scaling

We infer from

\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}((1-s/\tau)g,\phi,(1-s/\tau)\tau)=\mathcal{W}_{k,\phi}(g,\phi,\tau).

–functional remains unchanged along

of

to

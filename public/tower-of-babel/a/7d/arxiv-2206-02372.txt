On the Rationality of moduli spaces of vector bundles over chain-like curves
Suhas B. N., Praveen Kumar Roy and Amit Kumar Singh

Abstract
. Let C C be a chain-like curve over ℂ \mathbb{C} . In this paper, we investigate the rationality of moduli spaces of w w -semistable vector bundles on C C of arbitrary rank and fixed determinant by putting some restrictions on the Euler characteristics.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14D20, 14E08

1.
Introduction

The moduli space of vector bundles (of fixed rank and degree) on curves is one of the most important and well explored topics in algebraic geometry. Over a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g
, it is not possible to parametrize the set of all isomorphism classes of vector bundles of a fixed rank and degree by means of an algebraic variety or a scheme. To address this issue, David Mumford introduced the concept of stable and semistable vector bundles and proved that over a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
, the isomorphism classes of stable bundles have the structure of a smooth quasi-projective variety
[
Mumford-GIT-1965
]
. A natural compactification of this variety was then given by Seshadri by first introducing the S-equivalence relation on semistable vector bundles and then including the S-equivalence classes of semistable bundles in the moduli space. This compactified moduli space of semistable bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
, turns out to be a normal irreducible projective variety of dimension
r^{2}(g-1)+1
. When
(r,d)=1
, the notion of semistability coincides with that of stability, and so in this case,
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
becomes a smooth projective variety. One also has a canonical determinant map
\det:\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{C}(1,d)
, which sends an equivalence class
[E]\in\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
to its determinant class
[\Lambda^{r}E]\in\mathcal{U}_{C}(1,d)
. The fibre under this map of any arbitrary element
L\in\mathcal{U}_{C}(1,d)
is denoted by
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(r,L)
, and is called a fixed determinant moduli space.

The question of rationality of the fixed determinant moduli space of stable vector bundles over smooth projective curves have been quite well explored. In 1966, Tjurin first proved that the moduli space
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(2,L)
is a rational variety, for any line bundle
L
on
C
of odd degree
[
Tjurin-1966
, Theorem 14]
. Subsequently in 1975, Newstead generalised the result of Tjurin and proved that
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(r,L)
is a rational variety provided
(r,d)=1
and some other conditions hold. His technique was to construct a family of vector bundles of the required type, parametrized by a Zaraski open subset
S
of an affine space such that the induced map from
S
to
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,L)
is birational
[
Newstead-1975
,
Newstead-1980
]
. After a couple of decades, it was proved by King and Schofield in
[
King-Schofield-1999
]
that
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
is birationally equivalent to
\mathcal{U}_{C}(h,0)\times\mathbb{A}^{(r^{2}-h^{2})(g-1)}
, where
h=(r,d)
. Further, they also proved that
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(r,L)
is a rational variety when
(r,d)=1
, thereby settling the rationality question completely for the case when rank and degree are coprime. Nevertheless, the question is still mostly open for the non-coprime case. Narasimhan and Ramanan have proved the rationality of
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(2,L)
when the curve
C
is of genus 2 and the line bundle
L
is of even degree
[
Narasimhan-Ramanan-1969
]
.

In the case when the base curve
C
is an irreducible nodal curve of arithmetic genus
p_{a}(C)\geq 2
, one has to include torsion-free sheaves and not just vector bundles in order to get a compact moduli space (cf.
[
Seshadri-1982
, Chapter VII]
,
[
Newstead-2011
, Chapter V]
). Let
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
denote the moduli space of semistable torsion-free sheaves on
C
of rank
r
and degree
d
. Then, motivated by
[
King-Schofield-1999
]
, Bhosle and Biswas proved that
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
is birational to
\mathcal{U}_{C}(h,0)\times\mathbb{A}^{(r^{2}-h^{2})(p_{a}(C)-1)}
, and that
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(r,L)
is rational if
h=1

[
Bhosle-Biswas-2014
, Theorem 1.2]
. Further, they also proved that
\mathcal{SU}_{C}(r,L)
is rational when
p_{a}(C)=2=r

[
Bhosle-Biswas-2014
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Suppose
C
is a reducible nodal curve with
n
smooth components
C_{i}
of genus
g_{i}\geq 2
and
n-1
nodes
p_{i}
such that
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
, whenever
|i-j|>1
and
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}=\{p_{i}\}
, for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. Such curves are called
chain-like curves
. In this case, to get a compact moduli space, one replaces the vector bundles by pure sheaves of dimension one. Also one needs to consider weights of the components (called polarization in the literature) in order to define semistability. Let
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)
denote the moduli space of
w
-semistable pure sheaves of dimension one and Euler characteristic
\chi
. In
[
Bigas91
]
, Teixidor identifies the irreducible components of
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)
(for further details, see section 2). Now suppose
L
is a fixed line bundle on
C
of suitable Euler characteristic. Let
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,r,\chi,L)}
denote the closure of the collection of all vector bundles in
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)
with determinant
L
. For the case when
n=2=r
, it was proved in
[
Barik-Dey-Suhas-2018
]
by Barik, Dey and Suhas that
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,2,\chi,L)}
is rational whenever
(2,\chi)=1
. More recently, Favale and Brivio have proved it for higher rank under some extra assumption on the Euler characteristic
[
Brivio-Favale-VB-2020
]
. For
n\geq 2
and
r=2
, it has been shown in
[
Dey-Suhas-2018
]
that
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,2,\chi,L)}
is rational provided
(2,\chi)=1
.

In this article, we investigate the rationality of
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,r,\chi,L)}
for
r\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
under some conditions on the Euler characteristic. Though we use the technique employed in
[
Brivio-Favale-VB-2020
]
, increase in the number of components of the base curve and hence the complexity involved, makes it more challenging. The main result of this article is the following theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
C
be a chain-like curve as mentioned above. Fix
r\geq 2
, and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(r,d_{i})=1
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Let
\chi_{i}=d_{i}+r(1-g_{i})
and
\chi=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}-(n-1)r
. For any line bundle
L
of multidegree
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
and any polarization
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
satisfying the conditions

(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}w_{i})\chi-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}\chi_{i}+r(j-1)<\chi_{j}<(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}w_{i})\chi-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}\chi_{i}+rj,

for
1\leq j\leq n-1
,
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,r,\chi,L)}
is a rational variety.

The coprimality condition in the above theorem is very strong. For example, if
r=2
, then there is at most one
choice for the
\chi_{j}
satisfying the coprimality assumptions and the inequalities (exactly one for generic
w
). This means
that Theorem
covers only one of the
2^{n-1}
components of the moduli space.
Note that this result is very different from
that of
[
Dey-Suhas-2018
]
, where the authors prove the rationality of all the components when
(2,\chi)=1
. However,
when
n
and
\chi
are both even,
[
Dey-Suhas-2018
]
gives no information, whereas there is a component with
(2,d_{i})=1

for all
i
.

The proof of Theorem
involves the construction of a birational model for
\overline{\mathcal{SU}_{C}(w,r,\chi,L)}
and indeed for
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}

(Theorem
4.3
and Corollary
4.6
). In fact, this model can be constructed
without the coprimality assumptions (see Remark
3.2
).

2.
Preliminaries

Let
C
be a reduced nodal curve having
n
smooth irreducible components
C_{i}
of genus
g_{i}
with
C_{i}\cap C_{i+1}=\{p_{i}\}
and
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
whenever
|i-j|>1
. Such a curve
C
is called a chain-like curve. Let
\nu:\tilde{C}\rightarrow C
be the normalisation of
C
such that
\nu^{-1}(p_{i})=\{q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}\}
, where
q_{i}\in C_{i}
and
q_{i}^{\prime}\in C_{i+1}
.
On such a curve
C
, we have the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\rightarrow 0.

(2.1)

Here
\mathcal{T}
is supported only at the nodal point(s).
Also, it is not hard to show that each
\mathcal{T}_{p_{i}}
is a one dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
and that
h^{0}(\mathcal{T})=n-1
.
From the exact sequence (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C_{j}})-\chi(\mathcal{T})

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}-(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}.

So, if we let
p_{a}(C)=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})
be the arithmetic genus of
C
, then
p_{a}(C)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}
.
Now, suppose
E
is a vector bundle of rank
r
on
C
. Tensoring the exact sequence (
2.1
) with
E
, denoting
E_{|_{C_{j}}}
by
E_{j}
and the cokernel by
\mathcal{T}_{E}
, we obtain

0\rightarrow E\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{E}\rightarrow 0.

(2.3)

We also have

\displaystyle\chi(E)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\chi(E_{j})-r(n-1).

(2.4)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F
be a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules. We call
F
a pure sheaf of dimension one if; for every nonzero proper
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-subsheaf
G\subset F
, the dimension of the support of
G
is equal to one.

Vector bundles on
C
are examples of pure sheaves of dimension one. Suppose
F
is a pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
. Let
F_{j}=\frac{F_{|_{C_{j}}}}{\text{Torsion}(F_{|_{C_{j}}})}
for each
j
, where
\text{Torsion}(F_{|_{C_{j}}})
is the torsion subsheaf of
F_{|_{C_{j}}}
. Then
F_{j}
, if non-zero, is torsion-free and hence locally free on
C_{j}
. Let
r_{j}
denote the rank of
F_{j}
and let
d_{j}
denote the degree of
F_{j}
for each
j
. We call the n-tuples
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
and
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
respectively, the
multirank
and the
multidegree
of
F
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
be an n-tuple of rational numbers such that
0<w_{j}<1
for each
j
and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}=1
. We call such an n-tuple a polarization on
C
.

We now define the slope of a pure sheaf of dimension one on a chain-like curve
C
with respect to a given polarization
w
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F
be a pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
of multirank
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
. Then the slope of
F
with respect to a polarization
w
, denoted by
\mu_{w}(F)
, is defined by
\mu_{w}(F)=\frac{\chi(F)}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}r_{j}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
E
be a pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
. Then,
E
is said to be
w
-
semistable
(resp.
w
-
stable
) if for any proper subsheaf
F\subset E
, one has
\mu_{w}(F)\leq\mu_{w}(E)
(resp.
\mu_{w}(F)<\mu_{w}(E)
). If
E
is not
w
-semistable for any polarization
w
on
C
, we call it a
strongly unstable
.

Now, let
E
be a
w
-semistable pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
. Then there is a finite filtration of pure sheaves of dimension one on
C

\displaystyle 0=E^{0}\subset E^{1}\subset\cdots\subset E^{k-1}\subset E^{k}=E,

(2.5)

such that, for each
1\leq j\leq k
, the quotient
E^{j}/E^{j-1}
is a
w
-stable pure sheaf of dimension one on
C
with
\mu_{w}(E^{j}/E^{j-1})=\mu_{w}(E)
. Such filtration is known as a
Jordan-Hölder filtration
of
E
. Given a Jordan Hölder filtration (
2.5
), define the
graded sheaf
associated to
E
as

\displaystyle Gr_{w}(E)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}E^{j}/E^{j-1},

(2.6)

which depends on the isomorphism class of
E
. Two
w
-semistable pure sheaves
E
and
F
, each of dimension one on
C
are said to be
S_{w}
-equivalent if

\displaystyle Gr_{w}(E)=Gr_{w}(F).

If
E
and
F
are
w
-stable sheaves, then
S_{w}
-equivalence is equivalent to an isomorphism.

The following result from
[
Bigas91
, Theorem-1, Steps-1,2]
will be useful in subsequent theorems.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
C
is a chain-like curve and
E
is a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
. Let
E_{i}
denote the restriction of
E
to the component
C_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
w
denote a polarization on
C
. Then

(1)

if
E
is
w
-semistable,
\chi
is the Euler characteristic of
E
and
\chi_{j}
the Euler characteristic of
E_{j}
for each
j
, then

(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}w_{i})\chi-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}\chi_{i}+r(j-1)\leq\chi_{j}\leq(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}w_{i})\chi-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}\chi_{i}+rj,

(2.7)

where
j
varies from
1
to
n-1
;

(2)

if
E_{i}
is a semistable vector bundle for
i=1,\dots,n
and the Euler characteristics of

and
E_{i}
satisfy the inequalities (

), then
E
is
w
-semistable. Further, if the inequalities (
2.7
) are strict for
i=1,\dots,n-1
and at least one

is stable, then
E
is
w
-stable.

(3)

The irreducible components of
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)
are indexed by the multidegrees
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
for
which
\chi_{i}=d_{i}-r(g_{i}-1)
for
1\leq i\leq n
, such that the
\chi_{i}
satisfy the strict inequalities (
2.7
) for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
\sum_{i}^{n}\chi_{i}-r(n-1)=\chi
.

From now on, we denote the irreducible components of
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)
by
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
.

3.
Construction of pure sheaves of dimension one

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over the base field
\mathbb{C}
with genus
g\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)
be the moduli space of semistable vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
on a curve
C
. It is a quasi-projective variety and smooth projective when
r
and
d
are co-prime
[
Seshadri-1982
]
. Moreover,

\dim\mathcal{U}_{C}(r,d)=r^{2}(g-1)+1

(see
[
Seshadri-1982
]
). Note that in the co-prime case, the notions of stability and semistability coincide.

Let
C
be a chain-like curve having
n
smooth components
C_{i}
with
(n-1)
nodes
p_{i}
. Let
r
and
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
be integers with
r\geq 1
.
In this section, we construct a fibre product of projective bundles
\mathbb{P}
over
\mathcal{U}_{C_{1}}(r,d_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{C_{n}}(r,d_{n})
and then we also prove the existence of a polarization
w
(see
) such that the induced map from
\mathbb{P}
to
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
is birational.
Here,
\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
denotes the irreducible component of the moduli space of
w
-semistable vector bundles of rank
r
and multidegree
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
on a chain-like curve
C
with Euler characteristic
\chi
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C
be a chain-like curve having
n
smooth irreducible components
C_{i}
of genus
g_{i}\geq 1
with nodes
p_{i}
and
\nu^{-1}(p_{i})=\{q_{i},q_{i}^{\prime}\}
. Fix
r\geq 2
, and
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
r
is pairwise co-prime with
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
. Then there is a locally trivial map

\pi:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{C_{1}}(r,d_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{C_{n}}(r,d_{n}),

where
\mathbb{P}
is a fibre product of projective bundles, such that the fibre at
([E_{1}],\dots,[E_{n}])
is

(\mathbb{P}(\text{Hom}(E_{1,q_{1}},E_{2,q_{1}^{\prime}})),\dots,\mathbb{P}(\text{Hom}(E_{n-1,q_{n-1}},E_{n,q_{n-1}^{\prime}}))).

Here
E_{i,q_{i}}
(resp.
E_{i+1,q_{i}^{\prime}}
) denotes the fibre of
E_{i}
(resp.
E_{i+1}
) at
q_{i}
(resp.
q_{i}^{\prime}
).

Proof.

The proof follows the constructions of Lemma 3.1 of
[
Brivio-Favale-VB-2020
]

Since
r
is pairwise co-prime with

, for each

\mathcal{P}_{i}
for the moduli space of semistable vector bundles on
C_{i}
of rank
r
and degree
d_{i}
. In other words, for each
i
, we have the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}_{i}
on
\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})\times C_{i}
with
\mathcal{P}_{i}|_{[E_{i}]\times C_{i}}\cong E_{i}
via the identification

. Let

\displaystyle\iota_{i}:\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})\times q_{i}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})\times C_{i},

\displaystyle\iota_{i}^{\prime}:\mathcal{U}_{C_{i+1}}(r,d_{i+1})\times q_{i}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{C_{i+1}}(r,d_{i+1})\times C_{i+1},

be the natural inclusions, and
\iota_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i})
,
{\iota^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1})
the pullbacks under these inclusions of the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, where
i=1,\dots,n-1
. Note that
\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})\times q_{i}\cong\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})
, and
\mathcal{U}_{C_{i+1}}(r,d_{i+1})\times q_{i}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{U}_{C_{i+1}}(r,d_{i+1})
, (1
\leq i\leq n-1)
. Therefore, the vector bundle
\iota_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i})
(resp.
{\iota^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1})

\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})
(resp.
\mathcal{U}_{C_{i+1}}(r,d_{i+1})

for

. Moreover, the fibre of
\iota_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i})
at

is
E_{i,q_{i}}
, and the fibre of
{\iota^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1})

The product

\pi_{i}:\mathcal{U}_{C_{1}}(r,d_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{C_{n}}(r,d_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}(r,d_{i})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}:=\mathcal{H}om(\pi^{*}_{i}(\iota^{*}_{i}(\mathcal{P}_{i})),\pi^{*}_{i+1}({\iota^{\prime}}^{*}_{i}(\mathcal{P}_{i+1}))).

(3.1)

Let

denote the fibre product of

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
\mathbb{P}
can be defined without the coprimality conditions in the above theorem.
Let
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}^{N_{i}}/P_{i})
denote the Grothendieck’s Quot scheme parameterising
the quotients of
\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}^{N_{i}}
having fixed Hilbert polynomial
P_{i}
. Then, for a suitable choice of
N_{i}
and
P_{i}
, there is an open subset
\mathcal{R}_{i}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{i}
such that the action of
\text{GL}(N_{i})
on it descends to an action of
\text{PGL}(N_{i})
, and the
quotient is
\mathcal{U}_{C_{i}}^{s}(r,d_{i})
(the moduli space of stable bundles of rank
r
and degree
d_{i}
on
C_{i}
). Also, there exists a projective Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}_{i}
on

\mathcal{R}_{i}\times C_{i}
, whose restriction on
\{E\}\times C_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}(E)
for all
E\in\mathcal{R}_{i}

(see
[
Biswas-Paz-Newstead
]
for details).
Now, we can construct the bundles
\mathcal{F}_{i}
on
\mathcal{R}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{R}_{n}
and the action of

\text{GL}(N_{1})\times\cdots\times\text{GL}(N_{n})
on
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
descends to an action of

\text{PGL}(N_{1})\times\cdots\times\text{PGL}(N_{n})
. By taking the fibre product of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
,
we obtain
\mathbb{P}
as a bundle on
\mathcal{U}_{C_{1}}^{s}(r,d_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{C_{n}}^{s}(r,d_{n})
,
with the same fibre as in Theorem
3.1
. It is to be noted that this
bundle is no longer locally trivial in the Zariski topology. However, it does provide a birational model for

\mathcal{U}_{C}(w,r,\chi)_{d_{1},...,d_{n}}
.

Throughout this paper, by

A note on alternating minimization algorithms: Bregman frame
Tao Sun, Lizhi Cheng

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a Bregman frame for several classical alternating minimization algorithms. In the frame, these algorithms have uniform mathematical formulation. We also present convergence analysis for the frame algorithm. Under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property, stronger convergence is obtained.



Keywords: Alternating minimization algorithms, Bregman distance, nonconvex, Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property

Mathematical Subject Classification
90C30, 90C26, 47N10

Introduction

In this paper, we are devoted to solving the following problem

\min_{y,z}\Phi(y,z)=f(y)+H(y,z)+g(z),

(1)

where
f
,
g
and
H
are all closed, and the function
H
is continuously differentiable over
\textrm{dom}(f)\times\textrm{dom}(g)
.
It is natural to consider using the alternating minimization method for (
1
), i.e., minimizing only one variable and fixing other ones in each iteration. Mathematically, the
A
lternating
M
inimization (AM) can be presented as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{z}H(y^{k+1},z)+g(z).

A. Beck first proves the convergence of AM when both functions
f
and
g
are convex
[
3
]
. The sublinear convergence rate is derived provided
\Phi
is further convex
[
3
]
. In each iteration, the AM algorithm need to solve two subproblems which may not have explicit solutions. For practical perspective, paper
[
4
]
proposes the
P
roximal
L
inearized
A
lternating
M
inimization (PLAM) algorithm which reads as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{y}\langle\nabla_{y}H(y^{k},z^{k}),y-y^{k}\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|y-y^{k}\|_{2}^{2}+f(y),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{z}\langle\nabla_{z}H(y^{k+1},z^{k}),z-z^{k}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|z-z^{k}\|_{2}^{2}+g(z).

If the proximal maps of
f
and
g
are easy to calculate, so are
y^{k+1}
and
z^{k+1}
. The the global convergence of PLAM is proved under several reasonable assumptions
[
4
]
. The convergence results under convex case are also proved
[
11
]
. Besides AM and PLAM, the
A
ugmented
A
lternating
M
inimization (AAM) is also considered by literature
[
1
]
; the scheme of AAM can be described as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y)+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|y-y^{k}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{z}H(y^{k+1},z)+g(z)+\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|z-z^{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

The augmented terms in AAM is actually to derive a sufficient descend condition of the algorithm. The convergence results of AAM are presented in
[
1
]
.

In applications, we may need to combine the algorithms above. The following problem, which is provided an example to illustrate the hybrid ideal, which reads as

\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}},z\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}}\lambda_{1}\|y\|_{1}+\|Ay-z\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{2}\|z\|_{1,2},

(2)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
, and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,2}
are the
\ell_{1}
norm and
\ell_{1,2}
norm, respectively. When
z
is fixed, minimizing the function above needs iterations; therefore, we use the PALM strategy. While when
y
is fixed, the minimization of the function is quite simple. This algorithm can be generalized as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{z}\langle\nabla_{z}H(y^{k+1},z^{k}),z-z^{k}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|z-z^{k}\|_{2}^{2}+g(z).

We call scheme (
1
) as AM-PLAM. Under the hybrid ideal, we can see that there are at least 8 classes of alternating minimization algorithms for two block case. As the the number of the blocks increases, more alternating minimization algorithms are generated.

In this note, we propose a framework for the algorithms mentioned above (including the hybrid ones). The frame is based on the Bregman distance. In the frame, all the algorithm enjoy the same mathematical formulation. Convergence results are also provided for the frame.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the framework. Section 3 contains the convergence results. Section 4 concludes the paper.

2
Bregman frame

To propose the framework, we need recall the definition and properties of the Bregman distance.
The Bregman distance which is an extension of the squared Euclidean distance was proposed by
[
bregman1967relaxation
]
. In recent years, it has been used for various models and algorithms in signal processing and machine learning research
[
6
,
12
,
13
]
. For a convex differential function
\phi
, the Bregman distance is defined as

B_{\phi}(x,y):=\phi(x)-\phi(y)-\langle\nabla\phi(y),x-y\rangle.

(3)

If
\phi(x)=\|x\|_{2}^{2}
, we have that
B_{\phi}(x,y)=\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}
. If
\phi=x^{\top}Mx
, where
M
is a positive semidefinite matrix, then,
B_{\phi}(x,y)=\|M^{\frac{1}{2}}(x-y)\|_{2}^{2}=(x-y)^{\top}M(x-y)
.

Proposition 1

Let
\phi
be a differentiable convex function and
B_{\phi}(x,y)
is the Bregman distance. Then,

1.

B_{\phi}(x,y)\geq 0
,
B_{\phi}(x,x)=0
, for any
x,y\in dom(\phi)
.

2.
For a fixed
y
,
B_{\phi}(x,y)
is convex.

3.
If
\phi
is strongly convex with
\delta
, then,
B_{\phi}(x,y)\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}
, for any
x,y\in dom(\phi)
.

In this paper, the convex function
\phi
and
\psi
are assumed to have Lipschitz gradients with
L_{\phi},L_{\psi}>0
. And function
H(y,z)
satisfies the following assumption

\|\nabla_{y}H(\overline{y},z)-\nabla H(y,z)\|_{2}\leq L_{1}(\|\overline{y}-y\|_{2})

(4)

for any fixed
z\in\textrm{dom}(g)
, and

\|\nabla_{z}H(y,\overline{z})-\nabla H(y,z)\|_{2}\leq L_{2}(\|\overline{z}-z\|_{2})

(5)

for any fixed
y\in\textrm{dom}(f)
.
The Bregman frame for the algorithm can be described as

\displaystyle y^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y)+B_{\phi^{k}}(y,y^{k}),

\displaystyle z^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{z}H(y^{k+1},z)+g(z)+B_{\psi^{k}}(z,z^{k}),

where
\{\phi^{k},\psi^{k}\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
are differentiable functions.

Algorithm 1

Bregman Alternating Minimization Algorithm

0:

parameters
\alpha>0
, functions
\Phi,\Psi
Initialization
:
y^{0},z^{0}
for

k=0,1,2,\ldots

y^{k+1}\in\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y)+B_{\phi^{k}}(y,y^{k})

z^{k+1}\in\textrm{arg}\min_{z}H(y^{k+1},z)+g(z)+B_{\psi^{k}}(z,z^{k})

end for

We explain that AM, PLAM and AAM all can be regarded as the special case of Algorithm 1.

AM
: In Algorithm 1, we set
\phi^{k}=\psi^{k}\equiv 0
. Then, Algorithm 1 reduces to the AM algorithm.

PLAM
: Let
\phi^{k}(y)=\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|y\|_{2}^{2}-H(y,z^{k})
, then,
\phi^{k}
is differentiable. If
\alpha_{k}>L_{1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{k}(\overline{y})-\phi^{k}(y),\overline{y}-y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\|\overline{y}-y\|_{2}^{2}-\langle\nabla_{y}H(\overline{y},z^{k})-\nabla_{y}H(y,z^{k}),\overline{y}-y\rangle

(6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k}-L_{1})\|\overline{y}-y\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0.

Therefore,
\phi^{k}
is convex. And it can be easily verified that calculating
y^{k+1}
equals to

\textrm{arg}\min_{y}H(y,z^{k})+f(y)+B_{\phi^{k}}(y,y^{k}).

(7)

Similarly, letting
\psi^{k}(z)=\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}-H(y^{k+1},z)
, computing
z^{k+1}
equals to

\textrm{arg}\min_{z}H(y^{k+1},z)+g(y)+B_{\psi^{k}}(z,z^{k}).

(8)

AAM
: Letting
\phi^{k}(y)=\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|y\|_{2}^{2}
and
\psi^{k}(z)=\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|z\|_{2}^{2}
, Algorithm 1 reduces to the AAM algorithm.

We can easily see that the hybrid algorithms can also be generalized as the Bregman form. Thus, Algorithm 1 provides a unified mathematical formulation for the alternating minimization algorithms.

Convergence analysis

3.1
Preliminary tools

Here, we collect some basic definitions and propositions in the following.

Definition 1
(subdifferentials
[
7
,
9
]
)

Let
J:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper and lower semicontinuous function.

1.

For a given
x\in dom(J)
, the Fr
\acute{e}
chet subdifferential of
J
at
x
, written as
\hat{\partial}J(x)
, is the set of all vectors
u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
which satisfy

\lim_{y\neq x}\inf_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{J(y)-J(x)-\langle u,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|_{2}}\geq 0.

When
x\notin dom(J)
, we set
\hat{\partial}J(x)=\emptyset
.

2.

The (limiting) subdifferential, or simply the subdifferential, of
J
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, written as
\partial J(x)
, is defined through the following closure process

\partial J(x):=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\exists x^{k}\rightarrow x,J(x^{k})\rightarrow J(x)~\textrm{and}~u^{k}\in\hat{\partial}J(x^{k})\rightarrow u~\textrm{as}~k\rightarrow\infty\}.

It is easy to verify that the Fr
\acute{e}
chet subdifferential is convex and closed while the subdifferential is closed. When
J
is convex, the definition agrees with the one in convex analysis
[
rockafellar2015convex
]
as

\partial J(x):=\{v:J(y)\geq J(x)+\langle v,y-x\rangle~~\textrm{for}~~\textrm{any}~~y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}.

Let
\{(x^{k},v^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
(x^{k},v^{k})\in\textrm{graph }(\partial J)
. If
(x^{k},v^{k})
converges to
(x,v)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
and
J(x^{k})
converges to
v
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
, then
(x,v)\in\textrm{graph }(\partial J)
. This indicates the following simple proposition.

Proposition 2

If
v^{k}\in\partial J(x^{k})
, and
\lim_{k}v^{k}=v
and
\lim_{k}x^{k}=x
. Then, we have that

v\in\partial J(x).

(9)

A necessary condition for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be a minimizer of
J(x)
is

\textbf{0}\in\partial J(x).

(10)

When
J
is convex, (
10
) is also sufficient. A point that satisfies (
10
) is called (limiting) critical point. The set of critical points of
J(x)
is denoted by
\textrm{crit}(J)
.
We call
J
is strongly convex with
\nu
if for any
x,y\in dom(J)
and any
v\in\partial J(x)
, it holds that

J(y)\geq J(x)+\langle v,y-x\rangle+\frac{\nu}{2}\|y-x\|_{2}^{2}.

Proposition 3
(
[
nesterov2004introductory
]
)

Assume that
J(x)
is strongly convex with
\nu
and
x^{*}\in\textrm{arg}\min J(x)
. Then, for any
x\in dom(J)
, we have

J(x)\geq J(x^{*})+\frac{\nu}{2}\|x^{*}-x\|_{2}^{2}.

(11)

Proposition 4

If
x^{*}=(y^{*},z^{*})
is a critical point of
\Phi
, it must hold that

\displaystyle-\nabla H_{y}(y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial f(y^{*}),

\displaystyle-\nabla H_{z}(y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial g(z^{*}).

(12)

Definition 2
(
[
4
,
2
]
)

(a) The function
J:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is said to have the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property at
\overline{x}\in dom(\partial J)
if there
exist
\eta\in(0,+\infty]
, a neighborhood
U
of
\overline{x}
and a continuous function
\varphi:[0,\eta)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

1.

\varphi(0)=0
.

2.

\varphi
is
C^{1}
on
(0,\eta)
.

3.

for all
s\in(0,\eta)
,
\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}(s)>0
.

4.

for all
x
in
U\bigcap\{x|J(\overline{x})<J(x)<J(\overline{x})+\eta\}
, the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality holds

\varphi^{{}^{\prime}}(J(x)-J(\overline{x}))\textrm{dist}(\textbf{0},\partial J(x))\geq 1.

(13)

(b) Proper lower semicontinuous functions which satisfy the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality at each point of
dom(\partial J)
are called KL functions.

3.2
Convergence results

Theorem 1

For any
k
, we have that

\Phi(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})\leq\Phi(y^{k+1},z^{k})\leq\Phi(y^{k},z^{k}).

(14)

Proof 1

The scheme of Algorithm 1 gives that

H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+f(y^{k+1})+B_{\phi^{k}}(y^{k+1},y^{k})\leq H(y^{k},z^{k})+f(y^{k})+B_{\phi^{k}}(y^{k},y^{k}).

(15)

Note that
B_{\phi^{k}}(y^{k+1},y^{k})\geq 0
and
B_{\phi^{k}}(y^{k},y^{k})=0
, we can also have

H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+f(y^{k+1})\leq H(y^{k},z^{k})+f(y^{k}).

(16)

Then, we can obtain

\Phi(y^{k+1},z^{k})\leq\Phi(y^{k},z^{k}).

(17)

Similarly, we can derive the other inequality.

Lemma 1

If the following condition holds

\min\{\nu_{\phi^{k}},\nu_{\psi^{k}}\}>0,

(18)

then, there exist
\rho>0
such that

\Phi(x^{k})-\Phi(x^{k+1})\geq\rho\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}.

(19)

Proof 2

Letting
\rho=\min\{\nu_{\phi^{k}},\nu_{\psi^{k}}\}
,
we easily see that

B_{\phi^{k}}(y^{k+1},y^{k})\geq\rho\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|_{2}^{2}

(20)

Then, from (
15
), we can have that

H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+f(y^{k+1})+\rho\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq H(y^{k},z^{k})+f(y^{k}).

(21)

Similarly, we can obtain that

H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})+g(z^{k+1})+\rho\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+g(z^{k}).

(22)

Summing (
21
) and (
22
), we can obtain the result.

Lemma 2

If the sequence
\{x^{k}=(y^{k},z^{k})\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
generated by Algorithm 1 is bounded and condition (
18
) holds and

\max\{L_{\phi^{k}},L_{\psi^{k}}\}<+\infty,

(23)

then we have

\lim_{k}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2}=0.

(24)

For any cluster point
x^{*}=(y^{*},z^{*})
, it is also a critical point of
\Phi
.

Proof 3

The continuity of
\Phi
indicates that
\{\Phi(x^{k})\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
is bounded. From Lemma
1
,
\Phi(x^{k})
is decreasing. Thus, the sequence
\{\Phi(x^{k})\}_{k=0,1,2,\ldots}
is convergent, i.e.,
\lim_{k}[\Phi(x^{k})-\Phi(x^{k+1})]=0
. With Lemma
1
, we have

\lim_{k}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|_{2}\leq\lim_{k}\frac{\Phi(x^{k})-\Phi(x^{k+1})}{\rho}=0.

(25)

For any cluster point
(y^{*},z^{*})
, there exists
\{k_{j}\}_{j=0,1,2,\ldots}
such that
\lim_{j}(y^{k_{j}},z^{k_{j}})=(y^{*},z^{*})
. Then, we also have that
\lim_{j}(y^{k_{j}+1},z^{k_{j}+1})=(y^{*},z^{*})
.
From the scheme of Algorithm 1, we have the following conditions

\displaystyle-\nabla_{y}H(y^{k_{j}+1},z^{k_{j}})+\nabla\phi^{k_{j}}(y^{k_{j}})-\nabla\phi^{k_{j}}(y^{k_{j}+1})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial f(y^{k_{j}+1}),

\displaystyle-\nabla_{z}H(y^{k_{j}+1},z^{k_{j}+1})+\nabla\psi^{k_{j}}(z^{k_{j}})-\nabla\psi^{k_{j}}(z^{k_{j}+1})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial g(z^{k_{j}+1}).

Letting
j\rightarrow+\infty
, with closedness of the subdifferential, we have that

\displaystyle-\nabla H_{y}(y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial f(y^{*}),

\displaystyle-\nabla H_{z}(y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\partial g(z^{*}).

From Proposition
4
,
x^{*}
is a critical point of
\Phi
.

Lemma 3

If condition (
23
) holds, for any
k
, there exists
L>0
such that

\textrm{dist}(\textbf{0},\partial\Phi(x^{k+1}))\leq L\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2}.

(27)

Proof 4

The subdifferential of
\Phi(x^{k+1})
can be presented as

\partial\Phi(x^{k+1})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{y}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})+\partial f(y^{k+1})\\
\nabla_{z}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})+\partial g(z^{k+1})\end{array}\right).

(28)

Recalling the optimization conditions of each iteration, we have

v^{k+1}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{y}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})-\nabla_{y}H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+\nabla\phi^{k}(y^{k})-\nabla\phi^{k}(y^{k+1})\\
\nabla_{z}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})-\nabla_{z}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})+\nabla\psi^{k}(z^{k})-\nabla\psi^{k}(z^{k+1})\end{array}\right)\in\partial\Phi(x^{k+1}).

(29)

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\|v^{k+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y}H(y^{k+1},z^{k+1})-\nabla_{y}H(y^{k+1},z^{k})+\nabla\phi^{k}(y^{k})-\nabla\phi^{k}(y^{k+1})\|_{2}

(30)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi^{k}(z^{k})-\nabla\psi^{k}(z^{k+1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{y}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|_{2}+L_{\phi^{k}}\|y^{k}-y^{k+1}\|_{2}+L_{\psi^{k}}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}(L_{y}+\max\{L_{\phi^{k}},L_{\psi^{k}}\})\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|_{2}.

Letting
L=\sqrt{2}(L_{y}+\max\{L_{\phi^{k}},L_{\psi^{k}}\})
, we can finish the proof.

4
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we proposed a framework algorithm for alternating minimization algorithms. Thus, quite various alternating minimization algorithms have a same mathematical formulation. We also provide the convergence results of the proposed abstract algorithm.

In fact, the proposed algorithm can be extended to multi-block case. The multi-block minimization model can be described as

\min_{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=H(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i}),

(32)

where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
. The multi-block Bregman alternating minimization algorithm then can be presented as

\displaystyle x_{i}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\textrm{arg}\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}H(x_{1}^{k+1},x_{2}^{k+1},\ldots,x_{i-1}^{k+1},x,x_{i+1}^{k},\ldots,x_{n}^{k})+f_{i}(x)+B_{\phi_{i}^{k}}(y,y^{k}).

(33)

The convergence analysis is similar to the one of the two-block case.

Steiner systems S ⁡ ( 2 , 4 , 2 m ) S(2,4,2^{m}) supported by a family of extended cyclic codes
Qi Wang

Abstract
. In [C. Ding, An infinite family of Steiner systems S ⁡ ( 2 , 4 , 2 m ) S(2,4,2^{m}) from cyclic codes, J. Combin. Des. 26 (2018), no.3, 126–144], Ding constructed a family of Steiner systems S ⁡ ( 2 , 4 , 2 m ) S(2,4,2^{m}) for all m ≡ 2 ( mod 4 ) ≥ 6 m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\geq 6 from a family of extended cyclic codes. The objective of this paper is to present a family of Steiner systems S ⁡ ( 2 , 4 , 2 m ) S(2,4,2^{m}) for all m ≡ 0 ( mod 4 ) ≥ 4 m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\geq 4 supported by this family of extended cyclic codes. The main result of this paper complements the previous work of Ding, and the results in the two papers will show that there exists a binary extended cyclic code that can support a Steiner system S ⁡ ( 2 , 4 , 2 m ) S(2,4,2^{m}) for all even m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 . Furthermore, this paper also determines the parameters of other 2 2 -designs supported by this family of extended cyclic codes.

1.
Introduction

A (finite)
incidence structure
is a triple
(V,{\mathcal{B}},I)
such that
V
is a finite set of
v
elements called
points
,
{\mathcal{B}}
is a set of
k
-subsets of
V
with
1\leq k\leq v
, and
I\subseteq V\times{\mathcal{B}}
is an incidence relation between
V
and
{\mathcal{B}}
. The incidence relation
I
is given by membership, i.e., a point
p\in V
and a block
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
are incident if and only if
p\in B
. In the following, we may simply denote the incidence structure
(V,{\mathcal{B}},I)
by a
pair
(V,{\mathcal{B}})
. A
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)

design
(or
t
-design, for short) with
t\leq k
is an incidence structure
(V,{\mathcal{B}})
such that any
t
-subset of
V
is contained in exactly
\lambda
blocks
[
3
]
. We denote the number of blocks of a
t
-design by
b
, and a
t
-design is called
symmetric
if
v=b
. A
t
-design that has no repeated blocks is called
simple
. Throughout this
paper, we consider only simple
t
-designs with
t<k<v
. By a simple counting argument, we have the following identity, which restricts the parameters of a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design.

(1)

b{k\choose t}=\lambda{v\choose t}.

In particular, a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design is referred to as a
Steiner system
if
t\geq 2
and
\lambda=1
, and is traditionally denoted by
S(t,k,v)
.

In the literature, the most studied Steiner systems are those with parameters
S(2,3,v)
(
Steiner triple systems
),
S(3,4,v)
(
Steiner quadruple systems
), and
S(2,4,v)
. It was proved in
[
15
]
that a Steiner system
S(2,4,v)
exists if and only if
v\equiv 1
or
4\pmod{12}
. Infinite families of Steiner systems
S(2,4,v)
include:
S(2,4,4^{n})
, where
n\geq 2
(affine geometries);
S(2,4,3^{n}+\cdots+3+1)
, where
n\geq 2
(projective geometries);
S(2,4,2^{s+2}-2^{s}+4)
, where
s>2
(Denniston designs). For surveys on such Steiner systems, the reader is referred to
[
5
,
23
]
. It is open if the Steiner systems
S(2,4,v)
listed above are supported by a linear code. We answer this problem affirmatively by investigating the Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})
supported by a family of extended cyclic code for all even
m\geq 4
.

Recently, Ding constructed an infinite family of Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})

for all
m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\geq 6
from a family of extended cyclic codes, which
are affine-invariant
[
7
]
. As a follow-up of
[
7
]
, in this paper, we prove that a
family of extended binary cyclic codes hold Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})
for all

m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\geq 4
, and also determine the parameters of other
2
-designs supported
by this family of extended cyclic codes. The result of this paper together with the work
of
[
7
]
shows that there is an extended binary cyclic code that can support a
Steiner system
S(2,4,2^{m})
for all even
m\geq 4
. Hence, the work of this paper
complements that of
[
7
]
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we will
recall linear codes, and introduce two general methods in constructing designs from linear codes, the latter of which will be employed in Section
3
. After recalling a type of affine-invariant codes, we will determine the weight distribution of their dual codes, and then explicitly compute the parameters of the underlying Steiner systems from these codes. In Section
4
, we will conclude the paper and present several open problems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce some basics of linear codes and cyclic codes, and then summarize two generic constructions of designs from linear codes.

2.1.
Linear codes and cyclic codes

An
[n,\kappa,d]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a
\kappa
-dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
with minimum nonzero Hamming weight
d
, where
q
is a power of a prime
p
. As a linear subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, the linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
can be represented as the span of a minimal set of codewords. These codewords are collated as the rows of a matrix
G
, also known as a
generator matrix
of the code
{\mathcal{C}}
. The matrix
H
, whose null space is
{\mathcal{C}}
, is called the
parity-check
matrix of
{\mathcal{C}}
. The
dual code
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is the code whose generator matrix is the parity-check matrix
H
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
, and the
extended code
is the code having one extra parity element, denoted by
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}
. A linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called
cyclic
if each codeword
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})
in
{\mathcal{C}}
implies that the cyclic shift
(c_{n-1},c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2})
is also a codeword in
{\mathcal{C}}
. We may identify each vector
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
with

c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle,

then each code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
corresponds to a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. It is well-known that the linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is cyclic if and only if the corresponding subset in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
is an ideal of the ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle g(x)\rangle
, where the generator
g(x)
of the ideal has the least degree. Here,
g(x)
is called a
generator polynomial
and
h(x)=(x^{n}-1)/g(x)
is referred to as a
parity-check polynomial
of

{\mathcal{C}}
. A cyclic code of length
n=q^{m}-1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for some positive integer
m
is called a
primitive cyclic code
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Let
s
be an integer with
0\leq s<n
. The
q
-cyclotomic coset of
s
modulo
n
is defined by

C_{s}:=\{s,sq,sq^{2},\ldots,sq^{\ell_{s}-1}\}\pmod{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{N}},

where
\ell_{s}
is the smallest positive integer such that
s\equiv sq^{\ell_{s}}\pmod{n}
, and is exactly the size of the
q
-cyclotomic coset. The smallest integer in
C_{s}
is called the
coset leader
of
C_{s}
. Let
\Gamma_{(n,q)}
denote the set of all coset leaders. It is easy to see that all distinct
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
partition
{\mathcal{N}}
, i.e.,
\cup_{s\in\Gamma_{(n,q)}}C_{s}={\mathcal{N}}
. The generator polynomial of the cyclic code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
can be expressed as

g(x)=\prod_{t\in T}(x-\alpha^{t}),

where
\alpha
is a generator of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
,
T
is a subset of
{\mathcal{N}}
and a union of some
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
. The set
T
is called a
defining set
of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to
\alpha
.

For a
[v,\kappa,d]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, let
A_{i}:=A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})
denote the number of codewords with Hamming weight
i
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, where
0\leq i\leq v
. Then the sequence
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{v})
is called the
weight distribution
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, and
\sum_{i=0}^{v}A_{i}z^{i}
is referred to as the
weight enumerator
of
{\mathcal{C}}
. The weight distribution is quite important
not only for measuring the error-correcting capability of a code, but also for determining the parameters of designs supported by linear codes.

The following lemma, a variant of the MacWilliams Identity
[
28
]
, reveals how to compute the weight enumerator of the dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
from that of the original code
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 1
.

[
28
, p. 41]

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
[v,\kappa,d]
code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with weight enumerator
A(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{v}A_{i}z^{i}
and let

A^{\perp}(z)
be the weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
. Then

A^{\perp}(z)=q^{-\kappa}\Big(1+(q-1)z\Big)^{v}A\Big(\frac{1-z}{1+(q-1)z}\Big).

2.2.
Designs from linear codes

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a linear code, and let
(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{v})
be its weight distribution. For each
k
with
A_{k}\neq 0
, by
{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
we denote the set of supports of all codewords with Hamming weight
k
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, where the coordinates of a codeword are indexed by
(0,1,2,\ldots,v-1)
. Let
V=\{0,1,2,\ldots,v-1\}
be the point set. The pair
(V,{\mathcal{B}}_{k})
might be a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design for some positive integers
\lambda
and
t
. In this case, we call this design a

support design
of the code
{\mathcal{C}}
, and say that the code
{\mathcal{C}}
holds a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design.

In the literature, a lot of progress has been made in constructing designs from the codewords of a fixed weight in some linear codes (for example, see
[
1
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
26
,
27
,
25
,
8
,
11
,
29
,
24
]
). There are two general methods, which specify certain sufficient conditions for constructing
t
-designs from linear codes. The first method, given by the following theorem, was developed by Assmus and Mattson
[
1
,
16
]
.

Theorem 2

(Assmus-Mattson Theorem)
.

[
1
,
16
]

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
[v,\kappa,d]
code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
d^{\perp}
denote the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
.
Let
w
be the largest integer satisfying
w\leq v
and

w-\left\lfloor\frac{w+q-2}{q-1}\right\rfloor<d.

Define
w^{\perp}
analogously using
d^{\perp}
. Let
(A_{i})_{i=0}^{v}
and
(A_{i}^{\perp})_{i=0}^{v}
denote
the weight distribution of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
, respectively. Fix a positive integer
t
with
t<d
, and
let
s
be the number of
i
with
A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0
for
0\leq i\leq v-t
. Suppose
s\leq d-t
. Then

•

the codewords of weight
i
in
{\mathcal{C}}
hold a
t
-design provided
A_{i}\neq 0
and
d\leq i\leq w
, and

•

the codewords of weight
i
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
hold a
t
-design provided
A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0
and

d^{\perp}\leq i\leq\min\{v-t,w^{\perp}\}
.

We note that the key sufficient condition in the Assmus-Mattson theorem is that
s\leq d-t
, where
s
denotes the number of nonzero Hamming weights in the dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
, and
d
denotes the minimum Hamming weight in the code
{\mathcal{C}}
. In other words,
d-s
is the upper bound for
t
when a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
holds a
t
-design. Recently, the Assmus-Mattson theorem was explicitly employed to construct infinite families of
t
-designs, see
[
6
,
9
]
.

The other method is to consider the automorphism group of a linear code.
If the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
of a linear code is
t
-transitive, then the codewords of weight greater than
t
holds a
t
-design. The set of coordinate permutations that map a code
{\mathcal{C}}
to itself forms a group. This group is called the
permutation automorphism group
of
{\mathcal{C}}
and is denoted by
{\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})
. The
automorphism group
of a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
, denoted by
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
, is the set of maps of the form
DP\gamma
,
where
D
is a diagonal matrix,
P
is a permutation matrix, and
\gamma
is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, that map
{\mathcal{C}}
to itself. Clearly, we have
{\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\leqslant{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
. The automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
is said to be
t
-transitive
if for every pair of
t
-element ordered subsets of the index set
V=\{0,1,...,v-1\}
, there is an element
DP\gamma
of the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
, such that its permutation part
P
sends the first set to the second set. The following theorem summarizes this approach, whose proof can be found in
[
16
, p. 308]
.

Theorem 3
.

[
16
]

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a linear code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
where
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
is
t
-transitive. Then the codewords of any weight
i\geq t
of
{\mathcal{C}}
hold a
t
-design.

To apply this method, one has to find a subgroup of the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})
that is

t
-transitive on
{\mathcal{C}}
. Note that it is in general very hard to determine the parameters of such
t
-design supported by a linear code. Some examples of
t
-designs obtained by this method can be found, for example, in
[
4
,
21
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The following result states that the dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
also holds
t
-designs
[
22
]
if
{\mathcal{C}}
does so.

Proposition 4
.

[
22
]

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,\kappa,d]
binary linear code with
\kappa>1
, such that for each weight
w>0
the supports of the codewords of weight
w
form a
t
-design, where
t<d
. Then the supports of the codewords of each nonzero weight in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
also form a
t
-design.

3.
A type of affine-invariant codes and their designs

In this section, we first recall a type of affine-invariant codes investigated in
[
7
]
, together with some known results therein, and then show that a family of the codes supports
Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})
for all
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\geq 4

3.1.
A type of affine-invariant codes

An
affine-invariant
code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is an extended primitive cyclic code
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}
of length
q^{m}
such that the
affine permutation group
, denoted by
{\mathrm{AGL}}(1,q^{m})
, is a subset of
{\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})
, where
{\mathrm{AGL}}(1,q^{m})
is defined by

{\mathrm{AGL}}(1,q^{m})=\{\sigma_{(a,b)}(y)=ay+b:\ a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*},\ b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\}.

Note that
{\mathrm{AGL}}(1,q^{m})
acts doubly transitively on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}

3
that an affine-invariant code
{\mathcal{C}}
holds
2
-designs
[
7
]

[

]

[

[

3.3.
Discussion on isomorphism

As stated in Theorem
12
, the supports of all the codewords with Hamming weight
4
in the code
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{E}}
form a Steiner system
S(2,4,2^{m})
, where
m\geq 4
is even. The points and lines in the affine geometries are the classical Steiner systems with the same parameters. Indeed, the Steiner systems discussed in this paper are in fact isomorphic to those from affine geometries. To see this, we denote by
\alpha
a primitive element in
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and
let
m^{\prime}=m/2
for even
m\geq 4
. Then we consider two sets
\mathcal{B}_{4}
and
PL
, where

\mathcal{B}_{4}
denotes the set of supports of all codewords with Hamming weight
4
in the code
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{E}}
, and
PL
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle PL

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{x,x+(x+y),x+\alpha(x+y),x+\alpha^{2}(x+y)\}:\ x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{4^{m^{\prime}}},\ x\neq y\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{x,y,\alpha^{2}x+\alpha y,\alpha x+\alpha^{2}y\}:\ x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{4^{m^{\prime}}},\ x\neq y\}.

In fact, the set
PL
is exactly the set of lines in the affine space
\mathrm{AG}(m^{\prime},\mathbb{F}_{4})
, and is thereby the set of blocks in the classical Steiner system
S(2,4,4^{m^{\prime}})
. We now prove that
PL=\mathcal{B}_{4}
. On the one hand, we have

|PL|=\frac{{4^{m^{\prime}}\choose 2}}{{4\choose 2}}=\frac{2^{m-1}(2^{m}-1)}{6}=|\mathcal{B}_{4}|.

On the other hand, it is checked that

\mathrm{Tr}(x)+\mathrm{Tr}(y)+\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha x+\alpha^{2}y)+\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha^{2}x+\alpha y)=0,

and

\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{e}+1})+\mathrm{Tr}(y^{2^{e}+1})+\mathrm{Tr}((\alpha x+\alpha^{2}y)^{2^{e}+1})+\mathrm{Tr}((\alpha^{2}x+\alpha y)^{2^{e}+1})=0.

Then by the definition of the generator polynomial of the code
\mathcal{C}_{E}
in (
2
), it follows that the set
PL
is a subset of
\mathcal{B}_{4}
. Therefore, the two sets
\mathcal{B}_{4}
and
PL
are identical, and the isomorphism is thereby proved.

4.
Concluding remarks

The contribution of this paper is to prove that a family of extended cyclic
codes supports Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})
for all
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}\geq 4
. The result of this paper is a follow-up of
[
7
]
, and also complements that of
[
7
]
. Although the Steiner systems
S(2,4,2^{m})
presented in this paper and
[
7
]
are isomorphic to those of points and lines in the affine geometries, the contributions of this paper and
[
7
]
discovered a coding-theoretic construction of the classical geometric Steiner systems.

The key motivation of this paper comes from the following challenging research problems:

•

Is a given
t
-design a support design of a linear code? In other words,
is there a linear code that holds a given
t
-design?

•

Construct a specific linear code that holds a given
t
-design.

•

Construct a specific linear code with minimum dimension that holds a given
t
-design.

•

Construct new
t
-designs from known or new linear codes.

It would be nice if a coding-theoretic construction of known Steiner systems can be worked out.
The reader is cordially invited to attack this problem.

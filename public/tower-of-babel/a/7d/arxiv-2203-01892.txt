Incompressible polar active fluids with quenched disorder in dimensions d > 2 d>2
Leiming Chen, Chiu Fan Lee, Ananyo Maitra, John Toner

Abstract
We present a hydrodynamic theory of incompressible polar active fluids with quenched disorder. This theory shows that such fluids can overcome the disruption caused by the quenched disorder and move coherently, in the sense of having a non-zero mean velocity in the hydrodynamic limit. However, the scaling behavior of this class of active systems can not be described by linearized hydrodynamics in spatial dimensions between 2 and 5. Nonetheless, we obtain the exact dimension-dependent scaling exponents in these dimensions.

Hydrodynamic description.—
We start with the hydrodynamic equation of motion (EOM) of a generic incompressible polar active fluid with both quenched and annealed fluctuations constructed using symmetry arguments
Toner and Tu 1995
;
Toner and Tu 1998

Furthermore, because the system is non-equilibrium, many terms forbidden in equilibrium are allowed here as well

These considerations imply the following EOM

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\bf\nabla})\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\bf\nabla}P-(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\bf\nabla}P_{1})\mathbf{v}+U(|\mathbf{v}|)\mathbf{v}

(3)

\displaystyle+\mu_{1}\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}+\mu_{2}(\mathbf{v}\cdot{\bf\nabla})^{2}\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{f}_{{}_{Q}}+\mathbf{f}_{{}_{A}}\ ,

where the “pressure”

and
\mathbf{f}_{{}_{A}}
are respectively the quenched and annealed noises, which have zero means and correlations of the form

\displaystyle\langle f_{{}_{Q}}^{i}(\mathbf{r},t)f_{{}_{Q}}^{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=2D_{{}_{Q}}\delta_{ij}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle\langle f_{{}_{A}}^{i}(\mathbf{r},t)f_{{}_{A}}^{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=2D_{{}_{A}}\delta_{ij}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})\,,

(4b)

where the indices

We focus on the broken-symmetry moving phase, and consider the local velocity deviation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}=

\displaystyle-{\bf\nabla}_{{}_{\perp}}P-\gamma\partial_{x}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}-\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\cdot{\bf\nabla}_{{}_{\perp}})\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}+\mathbf{f}_{{}_{Q}}^{\perp}+\mathbf{f}_{{}_{A}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle-{\alpha\over v_{0}}\left(u_{x}+{u^{2}\over 2v_{0}}\right)\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}+\mu_{{}_{\perp}}\nabla^{2}_{{}_{\perp}}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}+\mu_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\,.

In this DRG analysis, we first decompose the field

Next we rescale the time, lengths, and the field as follows:

\displaystyle{{\rm d}\mu_{{}_{\perp}}\over{\rm d}\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(z-2+\eta_{{}_{\perp}}\right)\mu_{{}_{\perp}}\,,

\displaystyle{{\rm d}\mu_{x}\over{\rm d}\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(z-2\zeta\right)\mu_{x}\,,

\displaystyle{{\rm d}\gamma\over{\rm d}\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(z-\zeta\right)\gamma\,,

Summary & Outlook.—
We have considered the effects of quenched disorder in incompressible polar active fluids in the flocking phase, and showed that quenched disorder
make the scaling behavior of the system very different from that predicted by linearized hydrodynamics, and from that of an incompressible polar active fluid with only annealed disorder.
While this work focuses on an one-component active fluid in the incompressible limit,
an interesting future direction would be to consider the hydrodynamic behavior of active suspensions,
which are two-component (swimmers and solvent) systems that are only incompressible as a whole.

Appendix B
Detailed argument for non-renormalisation of
\lambda_{1}

In this section, we present our argument that
\lambda_{1}
gets no graphical corrections.
Consider first, by way of illustration, the
homogeneous
Navier-Stokes equation (i.e., one with no noise or other external force), but with a parameter
\lambda_{1}\neq 1
introduced (say, to allow us to do rescaling in the DRG
FNS
). “Galilean invariance” means that if we have a solution
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r},t)
of the Navier-Stokes equation, which now reads

\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nu\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}={\bf 0}\,,

(33)

then there’s a family of other solutions parameterized by a vector
\mathbf{w}
given by

\mathbf{v}^{(\mathbf{w})}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)+\mathbf{w}

(34)

which also satisfies the same equation, with the same value of
\lambda_{1}
.

But now, if we consider the
in
homogeneous Navier-Stokes equation

\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r},t)+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r},t)-\nu\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r},t),

(35)

and have a solution
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r},t)
to this equation, then

\mathbf{v}^{(\mathbf{w})}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)+\mathbf{w}

(36)

is not a solution of (
35
). Indeed, if we plug (
36
) into the left hand side of (
35
), we get

\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)-\nu\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)

(37)

which, according to equation (
35
), equals
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)
. Thus, plugging our trial solution (
36
) into (
35
) leads to

\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r},t)

(38)

which is clearly not true in general.

To say this another way, if
{\bf v}^{(1)}
obeys (
33
), then
\mathbf{v}^{(\mathbf{w})}(\mathbf{r},t)
obeys

\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)-\nu\nabla^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)

(39)

which is a different equation, because it has a different force on the right hand side.

However, Galilean invariance
does
work in a statistical sense: if we look at the correlations of the noise in (
39
) (i.e.,
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t))
, and assume that
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r},t)
satisfies local white noise (annealed) statistics

\langle f_{i}(\mathbf{r},t)f_{j}(\mathbf{r},t^{\prime})\rangle=D\delta_{ij}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})

(40)

then

\langle f_{i}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)f_{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=D\delta_{ij}\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]\delta(t-t^{\prime})

(41)

However, since the
\delta(t-t^{\prime})
in this expression is only non-zero when
t=t^{\prime}
, it follows that we can set
t-t^{\prime}=0
in the
\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]
without changing anything (since doing so only changes the value of that delta function when
t\neq t^{\prime}
, in which case
\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]
is multiplied by zero anyway.
So, setting
t-t^{\prime}=0
in the
\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]
, Eq. (
41
) becomes

\langle f_{i}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)f_{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=D\delta_{ij}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime})

(42)

which has the same statistics (
40
) as the noise in the original equation. So the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes equation with annealed noise is Galilean invariant in a statistical sense.

However, this argument does not work if the noise is quenched. In this case, going through the above manipulations leads to the replacement of the original quenched noise statistics

\langle f_{i}(\mathbf{r},t)f_{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=D\delta_{ij}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

(43)

with

\langle f_{i}(\mathbf{r}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t,t)f_{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}t^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=D\delta_{ij}\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]

(44)

and now there’s no convenient second delta function
\delta(t-t^{\prime})
to allow us to set
t=t^{\prime}
in
\delta[\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{w}(t-t^{\prime})]
. Therefore, the statistics (
44
) really are different from the statistics (
43
), so we can not argue for even statistical Galilean invariance.

However, despite this lack of Galilean invariance (and consequently, pseudo-Galilean invariance
NL
),
\lambda_{1}
does not
get
receive any graphical correction even in the presence of quenched noise.

To see this, let us consider the steady-state equation of motion, which is sufficient since the static quenched noise dominates. This looks very much like the Navier-Stokes equation, with
x
playing the role of time, and
r_{\perp}
playing the role of space. To be precise, it reads

\gamma\partial_{x}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)+\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)\cdot\nabla_{\perp})\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)-\mu_{\perp}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)=\mathbf{f}_{Q}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)

(45)

with
\mathbf{f}_{{}_{Q}}
having statistics

\langle f_{{}_{Q}}^{i}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)f_{{}_{Q}}^{j}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x^{\prime})\rangle=D_{{}_{Q}}\delta_{ij}\delta(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp})\delta(x-x^{\prime})\,.

(46)

In writing equation (
45
), we have neglected the term
\mu_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)
that we retain in the main text, because that term proves to be irrelevant for the quenched fluctuations that dominate the graphical corrections.
Now assume we have a solution
\mathbf{u}_{\perp}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)
of (
45

Then the “very-pseudo Galilean boosted” vector function obtained by treating

the way we treated time in the Navier-Stokes equation, namely

\mathbf{u}_{\perp}^{(\mathbf{w})}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{u}_{\perp}^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x,x)+\mathbf{w}

(47)

satisfies

\gamma\partial_{x}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)+\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)\cdot\nabla_{\perp})\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)-\mu_{\perp}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)-\mu_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathbf{u}_{\perp}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp},x)=\mathbf{f}_{Q}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x,x)\ .

(48)

The statistics of the noise on the right hand side are the same as as those of the original noise, by an argument almost identical to the one used above for the annealed Navier-Stokes equation. Specifically, we have

\langle f_{{}_{Q}}^{i}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x,x)f_{{}_{Q}}^{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=D_{{}_{Q}}\delta_{ij}\delta[\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}(x-x^{\prime})]\delta(x-x^{\prime})

(49)

As in the Navier-Stokes case, here we can replace
x-x^{\prime}
with zero in
\delta[\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}(x-x^{\prime})]
, since
\delta(x-x^{\prime})
will vanish when

.

Making that replacement, (
49
) becomes

\langle f_{{}_{Q}}^{i}(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x,x)f_{{}_{Q}}^{j}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp}-(\lambda_{1}/\gamma)\mathbf{w}x^{\prime},x^{\prime})\rangle=D_{{}_{Q}}\delta_{ij}\delta(\mathbf{r}_{\perp}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{\perp})\delta(x-x^{\prime})

(50)

which are exactly the same statistics (
46
) as the original noise. Hence, the static equation of motion has the “very-pseudo-Galilean invariance” (
47
) in a statistical sense. Since this invariance involves
\lambda_{1}/\gamma
, it follows that
\lambda_{1}/\gamma
can not get any graphical corrections.

We now note that the relevant part of the nonlinear term
(\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\cdot{\bf\nabla}_{{}_{\perp}})\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}
is a total “
\perp

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\cdot{\bf\nabla}_{{}_{\perp}})\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{\perp}\left(u_{i}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\right)-\left(\nabla_{{}_{\perp}}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\right)\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{\perp}\left(u_{i}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\right)+\left(\partial_{x}u_{x}\right)\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\approx\partial_{i}^{\perp}\left(u_{i}^{\perp}\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}\right)\,,

where in the second equality we have used the incompressibility constraint

, and in “

\left(\partial_{x}u_{x}\right)\mathbf{u}_{{}_{\perp}}
since it is irrelevant compared to

Since this sole relevant non-linearity is a total

-derivative, there are no graphical corrections to

Since we have already established that

{\mathbf{k}}_{{}_{\perp}}\equiv\mathbf{q}_{{}_{\perp}}(r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda)\,,~~~k_{{}_{x}}\equiv q_{{}_{x}}(r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda)^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,,~~~\Omega\equiv\omega^{\prime}(r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda)^{z^{\prime}}

we obtain

\displaystyle C_{{}_{A}}(\mathbf{r},t)=r_{{}_{\perp}}^{2\chi^{\prime}}G_{{}_{A}}\left({|x-\gamma_{0}t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}},{|t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{z^{\prime}}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle G_{{}_{A}}\left({|x-\gamma_{0}t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}},{|t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{z^{\prime}}}\right)\equiv{2(d-2)D_{{}_{A}}\Lambda^{2\chi^{\prime}}\over(2\pi)^{d+1}}\int{\rm d}\Omega{\rm d}^{d}k\,{{{\exp\left\{-{\rm i}\left[{\Omega t\over(r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda)^{z^{\prime}}}-{\mathbf{k}_{{}_{\perp}}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{{}_{\perp}}\over r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda}-{(x-\gamma t)k_{x}\over(r_{{}_{\perp}}\Lambda)^{\zeta^{\prime}}}\right]\right\}}\over\Omega^{2}+\left[\mu_{x}k_{x}^{2}+\mu_{{}_{\perp 0}}k_{{}_{\perp}}^{2}(k_{x}/\Lambda^{\prime})^{-{\eta_{{}_{\perp}}\over\zeta}}\right]^{2}}}\,,

(65)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}={1\over 2}\left[z^{\prime}-\zeta^{\prime}-(d-1)\right]\,,

(66)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=2\zeta^{\prime}\,,

(67)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}={2\zeta\over 2\zeta+\eta_{{}_{\perp}}}\,.

(68)

\zeta^{\prime}
, and
\chi^{\prime}

Appendix E
Calculation of the exponent
\theta

We begin with the scaling form (
65
) for the annealed part of the correlation function, which
contains all of the time dependence. If we sit at fixed, large
\mathbf{r}_{\perp}
(i.e.,
x=0
, large
\mathbf{r}_{\perp}
), at long times the two
arguments of the scaling function in equation (
65
) go like
|t|/r_{\perp}^{z^{\prime}}
and
|t|/r_{\perp}^{\zeta^{\prime}}
, respectively. The correlation function will be controlled by the larger of these
two, which, for large
r_{\perp}
, is
|\gamma t|/r_{\perp}^{\zeta^{\prime}}
, since
\zeta^{\prime}<z^{\prime}
. Therefore, for large time,

G_{{}_{A}}\left({|\gamma t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}},{|t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{z^{\prime}}}\right)\approx G_{{}_{A}}\left({|\gamma t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}},0\right)\equiv F_{{}_{A}}\left({|\gamma t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}}\right)\,.

(69)

We expect that, at sufficiently large time
t
(i.e.,
|t|\gg r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}
),
the correlation function (
64
) should become independent of
\mathbf{r}_{{}_{\perp}}
. This implies that the scaling function
F_{{}_{A}}
in (
69
) must scale like
r_{\perp}^{-{2\chi^{\prime}}}
, to cancel off the
r_{\perp}^{{2\chi^{\prime}}}
prefactor in (
64
). But since the scaling function
F_{{}_{A}}(u)
in (
69
) depends only on the scaling combination
u\equiv{|\gamma t|\over r_{{}_{\perp}}^{\zeta^{\prime}}}
, this can only be achieved if

F_{{}_{A}}(u)\propto u^{\theta}

(70)

with

\theta=\frac{2\chi^{\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}=-\left[{d^{2}+4d-9\over 2(d+1)}\right]\,.

(71)

Requiring this scaling of
F_{{}_{A}}(u)
clearly implies, from (
64
), that

C_{{}_{A}}(x=0,\mathbf{r}_{{}_{\perp}}={\mathbf{0}},t)\propto|t|^{\theta}\,,

(72)

at large time, with
\theta
given by (
71
), which is the result quoted in the main text.

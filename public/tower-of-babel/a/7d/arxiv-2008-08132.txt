Subharmonic Solutions in Reversible Non-Autonomous Differential Equations
Izuchukwu Eze, Carlos García-Azpeitia, Wieslaw Krawcewicz, Yanli Lv

Abstract
We study the existence of subharmonic solutions in the system u ¨ ​ ( t ) = f ​ ( t , u ​ ( t ) ) \ddot{u}(t)=f(t,u(t)) , where u ⁡ ( t ) ∈ ℝ k u(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{k} and f f is an even and p p -periodic function in time. Under some additional symmetry conditions on the function f f , the problem of finding m ​ p mp -periodic solutions can be reformulated in a functional space as a Γ × ℤ 2 × D m \Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times D_{m} -equivariant equation, where the group Γ × ℤ 2 \Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} acts on the space ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} and D m D_{m} acts on u ⁡ ( t ) u(t) by time-shifts and reflection. We apply Brouwer equivariant degree to prove the existence of an infinite number of subharmonic solutions for the function f f that satisfies additional hypothesis on linear behavior near zero and the Nagumo condition at infinity. We also discuss the bifurcation of subharmonic solutions when the system depends on an extra parameter.

Reversible Non-Autonomous Differential Equations

We are interested in studying the existence of the so-called

subharmonic
periodic solutions to (
1
), i.e. in
finding non-constant solutions, which for some integer
m\geq 3

u(t)=u(t+{2\pi m}),\;\;\dot{u}(t)=\dot{u}(t+{2\pi m}).

(3)

We also consider a subgroup
\Gamma\leq S_{k}

\sigma x=\sigma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k})^{T}:=(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)})^{T}.

(4)

Clearly, the space
V:=\mathbb{R}^{k}
equipped with this
\Gamma
-action is
an orthogonal
\Gamma
-representation. As it is also of our interest to
study the impact of the symmetries
\Gamma
on the existence of subharmonic
solutions to (
1
), we introduce the following condition:

(
A_{4}
)

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

\sigma\in\Gamma
, we have
f(t,\sigma x)=\sigma f(t,x)
.

The condition (

) implies that

is
\Gamma
-equivariant, i.e. the

1
) admits
\Gamma
-symmetries.

2.1
Reformulation of (
1
) in Functional
Spaces

Consider the Banach space
\mathbb{F}:=C_{2\pi m}(\mathbb{R},V)
of all
2\pi m
-periodic continuous
V

\|\varphi\|_{\infty}:=\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(t)|,\;\varphi\in\mathbb{F},

2\pi m

\|u\|=\|u\|_{2,\infty}:=\max\{\|u\|_{\infty},\|\dot{u}\|_{\infty},\|\ddot{u}\|_{\infty}\},\quad u\in\mathbb{E}.

Notice that the natural injection operator
\mathfrak{j}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}

(\mathfrak{j}(u))(t):=u(t)

N_{f}(\varphi)(t)=f(t,\varphi(t))

(

) is equivalent to the following operator equation

Lu=N_{f}(\mathfrak{j}(u))-\mathfrak{j}(u),\quad u\in\mathbb{E}.

(6)

Since the operator

u=L^{-1}\Big(N_{f}(\mathfrak{j}(u))-\mathfrak{j}(u)\Big),\quad u\in\mathbb{E}.

Define the map
\mathscr{F}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}
, by

\mathscr{F}(u):=u-L^{-1}\Big(N_{f}(\mathfrak{j}(u))-\mathfrak{j}(u)\Big),\quad u\in\mathbb{E}.

(7)

Then
u\in\mathbb{E}
is a solution to (
1
) if and only if

\mathscr{F}(u)=0.

(8)

One can easily observe that, by compactness of
\mathfrak{j}
, the map

\mathscr{F}
is a completely continuous field on
\mathbb{E}
.

Obviously, by the condition (
A_{3}
), we have
f(t,0)=0
for all

, thus
\mathscr{F}(0)=0
, i.e. the zero function is the

trivial solution
to (
1
). In what follows we are
interested in finding non-trivial (i.e. non-constant)
2\pi m
-periodic
solutions to (
1
). The group
G:=\Gamma\times D_{m}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
acts on the space
\mathbb{E}
by

\displaystyle(\sigma,\gamma^{j},\pm 1)u(t)

\displaystyle:=\pm\sigma u(t+2\pi j),\quad j=0,1,\dots,m-1,\;\;\sigma\in\Gamma,\;\gamma=e^{\frac{i2\pi}{m}},

\displaystyle(\sigma,\kappa,\pm 1)u(t)

\displaystyle:=\pm\sigma u(-t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\;\;u\in\mathbb{E},

thus
\mathbb{E}
is an isometric Banach
G
-representation. One can easily
verify that the properties (
A_{1}
)—(
A_{4}
) imply that
\mathscr{F}
is
G
-equivariant.

Proposition 2.1

2.3
Linearization of Equation (

) at

We make the following additional assumption

(
A_{5}
)

There exists a

A:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(t,x)-Ax}{|x|}=0

(10)

uniformly with respect to

, and for all integers

and

\mu\in\sigma(A)
,

\frac{j^{2}}{m^{2}}+\mu\not=0.

(11)

We define the linear operator
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}

(associated with
A:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
) by

\mathscr{A}u:=u-L^{-1}\Big(N_{A}(\mathfrak{j}(u)-\mathfrak{j}(u)\Big),\quad u\in\mathbb{E},

(12)

where
N_{A}(\varphi)(t):=A(\varphi(t))
,

,
\varphi\in C_{2\pi m}(\mathbb{R};V)
. Under the assumption (
A_{5}
) the operator

\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}
given by (

) is an
isomorphism and
D\mathscr{F}(0)=\mathscr{A}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
satisfies the conditions (
A_{1}
)—(
A_{5}
). Then there
exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the
G
-map
\mathscr{F}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}
(given by (
7
)) and
\mathscr{A}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}
(given by (
12
)) are
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
-admissibly
G
-homotopic (here
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=B_{\varepsilon}(0)
in

\mathbb{E}
).

Proof.

Define the linear homotopy
\mathfrak{H}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}

as
\mathfrak{H}(\lambda,u):=(1-\lambda)\mathscr{A}u+\lambda\mathscr{F}(u)
,

u\in\mathbb{E}
, and suppose for contradiction that there exists a sequence

\{\lambda_{n},u_{n}\}
such that
u_{n}\not=0
,
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{o}

and
u_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, then we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{H}(\lambda_{n},u_{n})=(1-\lambda_{n})\mathscr{A}u_{n}+\lambda_{n}F(u_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}(u_{n})+\lambda_{n}(\mathscr{F}(u_{n})-\mathscr{A}u_{n}).

Put
v_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}
. Then

0=\mathscr{A}v_{n}+\lambda_{n}\frac{\mathscr{F}(u_{n})-\mathscr{A}u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}.

Since
\|u_{n}\|\to 0
and
\lambda_{n}
is bounded, thus

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr{F}(u_{n})-\mathscr{A}u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|}=0,

which implies

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathscr{A}v_{n}).

On the other hand, since
\mathscr{A}=\id-\mathscr{K}
, where

\mathscr{K}:=L^{-1}(A\mathfrak{j}-\mathfrak{j})
is a compact operator, one
can assume (by passing to a subsequence) that
\mathscr{K}v_{n}\to v_{o}
,
which implies
v_{n}\to v_{0}
and
\|v_{o}\|=1
, so
v_{o}\in\text{{Ker\,}}\mathscr{A}
, but this is a contradiction with (
A_{5}
).

\square

2.4

The following condition is often referred to as the

condition
:

(
A_{6}
)

There exists a constant

such that

\forall_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\;\;\forall_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\;\;|x|\geq M\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;f(t,x)\bullet x>0.

We consider the following parametrized (by

)
modification of system (
1

\begin{cases}\ddot{u}(t)=\lambda f(t,u(t))+(1-\lambda)u(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\;u(t)\in V,\\
u(t)=u(t+{2\pi m}),\;\dot{u}(t)=\dot{u}(t+{2\pi m}).\end{cases}

(13)

Then we have:

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
f:\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a continuous function satisfying conditions (
A_{1}
)—(
A_{4}
) and (

-periodic function of

(where

)

Proof.

Assume for the contradiction that

is a solution while
\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|u(t)|\geq M

\phi(t):=\frac{1}{2}|u(t)|^{2}

. However, by condition (

G\mbox{\rm-}\deg(T,B(V))=\prod_{i=0}^{r}G\mbox{\rm-}\deg(T_{i},B(V_{i}))=\prod_{i=0}^{r}\prod_{\mu\in\sigma_{-}(T)}\left(\deg_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}\right)^{m_{i}(\mu)}

(41)

where
T_{i}=T|_{V_{i}}
and
\sigma_{-}(T):=\left\{\mu\in\sigma(T):\mu<0\right\}
denotes the real negative
spectrum of
T
.

Notice that the basic degrees can be effectively computed from
(
40
), i.e. formula

\displaystyle\deg_{\mathcal{V}_{i}}=\sum_{(H)}n_{H}(H),

where

n_{H}=\frac{(-1)^{\dim\mathcal{V}_{i}^{H}}-\sum_{H<K}{n_{K}\,n(H,K)\,\left|W(K)\right|}}{\left|W(H)\right|}.

(42)

a<b

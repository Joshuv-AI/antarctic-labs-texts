Variety of general position problems in graphs
Jing Tian, Sandi Klavžar

Abstract
Let X X be a vertex subset of a graph G G . Then u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) are X X -positionable if V ⁡ ( P ) ∩ X ⊆ { u , v } V(P)\cap X\subseteq\{u,v\} holds for any shortest u , v u,v -path P P . If each two vertices from X X are X X -positionable, then X X is a general position set. The general position number of G G is the cardinality of a largest general position set of G G and has been already well investigated. In this paper a variety of general position problems is introduced based on which natural pairs of vertices are required to be X X -positionable. This yields the total (resp. dual, outer) general position number. It is proved that the total general position sets coincide with sets of simplicial vertices, and that the outer general position sets coincide with sets of mutually maximally distant vertices. It is shown that a general position set is a dual general position set if and only if its complement is convex. Several sufficient conditions are presented that guarantee that a given graph has no dual general position set. The total general position number, the outer general position number, and the dual general position number of arbitrary Cartesian products are determined.



Keywords:
general position; total general position; outer general position; dual general position; Cartesian product of graphs; strong resolving graph; convex subgraph

Introduction

General position sets were introduced to graph theory independently in
[
3
,
18
]
, but in the special case of hypercubes, these sets had been studied previously in
[
15
]
. Finding a largest general position set in a graph

[
18
]
. In
[
2
]
, a characterization of general position sets in graphs was proved and the general position number for the complement of bipartite graphs, the complement of trees, and the complement of hypercubes was determined. Afterwards, general position sets received a wide attention, see
[
7
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
19
]
for a selection of recent developments.

Mutual-visibility sets are closely related to general position sets and were recently introduced by Di Stefano
[

. While investigating mutual-visibility sets in strong products, there was a natural need to introduce total mutual-visibility sets
[
5
]
. This has led to the variety of mutual-visibility sets which was formalized in
[
4
]
. Motivated by the variety of mutual-visibility sets, the main purpose of this paper is to introduce and study the corresponding variety of general position sets. It consists of general position sets, total general position sets, outer general position sets, and dual general position sets.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the rest of the introduction we give definitions needed. In the next section we introduce the variety of the general position sets and characterize total general position sets and outer general position sets. In Section
3
we focus on dual general position sets. We first observe that a general position set is a dual general position set if and only if its complement is convex. Then we consider graphs with small dual general position numbers. In particular, several sufficient conditions are presented that guarantee that a given graph has no dual general position set at all. In Section
4
we determine the total general position number, the outer general position number, and the dual general position number of arbitrary Cartesian products.

In this section we first introduce the announced variety of general position sets. After that we characterize total general position sets and outer general position sets.

We now characterize total general position sets as follows.

Theorem 2.1

Let

Assume first that

Suppose that

, which means that there exists a shortest

-positionable, a contradiction. As a consequence, we conclude that

must belong to

and then

.

To prove the converse, assume that
X\subseteq S(G)
. Consider any two vertices

and

from

and let

be a shortest

-path of

. If

and hence

are

-positionable. Assume next that

. Let

be the path

. Then

.
Suppose that

, where

3
Dual general position sets

From Theorem
2.1
it follows that if
X
is a total general position set of
G
, then each subset of
X
is also a total general position set of
G
. Similarly, by Theorem
2.3
this hereditary property also holds for outer general position sets. In addition, this property is also known to hold for general position sets. A bit surprisingly, if
X
is a dual general position set of
G
and
Y\subseteq X
, then

need not be a dual general position set of
G
. To see this, consider two adjacent vertices of
C_{5}
which form a dual general position set, however, one vertex of
C_{5}
does not form such a set. So each of the properties of being in general position, being in total general position, and being in outer general position is hereditary, but the property of being in dual general position property is not.

The above consideration indicates that the dual general position is intrinsically different from the other three invariants. In this section we have a closer look to it. We first characterize which general position sets are dual general position sets. Then we respectively consider graphs
G
with
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)=0
,
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)=1
, and
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)\geq 2
.

Let
X
be a dual general position set in a graph

. Then, clearly,
X
is a general position set. Hence to find a largest dual general position set in
G
if suffices to check all general position sets in
G
and check if they are also dual. For this task, the following result is useful.

Theorem 3.1

Let
X
be a general position set of a graph
G
. Then
X
is a dual general position set if and only if
G-X
is convex.

Proof.
Assume that
X
is a dual general position set. Let
x,y\in V(G)\setminus X
and let
P
be a shortest
x,y
-path. Since
X
is a dual general position set, the vertices
x
and
y
are
X
-positionable which in turn implies that
V(P)\cap X=\emptyset
. It follows that
G-X
is convex.

Conversely, assume that
X
is a general position set and
G-X
is convex. If
x,y\in X
, then
x
and
y
are
X
-positionable since
X
is a general position set. Consider next
x,y\in V(G)\setminus X
. Since
G-X
is convex, each shortest
x,y
-path lies in
G-X
hence no such path contains vertices from
X
. We can conclude that
X
is a dual general position set.

\square

For the later use we state explicitly the following consequence of Theorem

.

Corollary 3.2

If

is a graph and

, then

is a dual general position set.

Proof.
Since

we see that

\square

Theorem 3.9

If

and

are two adjacent vertices of a graph

(i)

is a dual general position set of

(ii)

(iii)

For each

Proof.
Let

and

.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If

is a dual general position set of

that
G-\{x,y\}
is convex.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Assume that

is convex. Then

is complete. Indeed, for otherwise two nonadjacent vertices

and
x^{\prime\prime}
from

are not
\{x,y\}
-positionable. Analogously,

is complete. Furthermore, if

and

. But if

, then

is not convex, hence we conclude that

for any

.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
:
Let
1\leq d_{G}(u,v)\leq 2
for any two vertices
u,v\in N_{G}(x)\cup N_{G}(y)
, and let
G[X]
and

be complete. Set

and

from

4
The variety in Cartesian products

To determine the general position number of Cartesian product graphs is a difficult problem and has been already widely investigated. It took several intermediately steps before the general position number of integer lattices (alias Cartesian products of a finite number of paths) has been determined
[
13
]
. Bounds on the general position number of Cartesian products of arbitrary graphs were independently proved in
[
7
,
22
]
. Special Cartesian products were studied in
[
23
]
(products of two trees), in
[
11
]
(products of paths and cycles), and in
[
11
,
16
]
(products of two cycles).

In this section we determine the total general position number, the outer general position number, and the dual general position number of arbitrary Cartesian products. For this purpose, we need some additional notation and terminology on Cartesian products. Let
G
and
H
be graphs and consider
G\,\square\,H
. Given a vertex
h\in V(H)
, the subgraph of
G\,\square\,H
induced by the set of vertices
\{(g,h):\ g\in V(G)\}
, is a
G
-layer
and is denoted by
G^{h}
.
H
-layers
{}^{g}H
are defined analogously. Each
G
-layer and each
H
-layer is isomorphic to
G
and
H
, respectively.
If
X\subseteq V(G\,\square\,H)
, the
projection

p_{G}(X)
of
X
to
G
is the set
\{g\in V(G):\ (g,h)\in X\ {\rm for\ some}\ h\in V(H)\}
. The projection
p_{H}(X)
of
X
to
H
is defined analogously.

For total general position sets, Corollary
2.2
implies:

Corollary 4.1

If
G
and
H
are connected graphs of order at least
2
, then
{\rm gp}_{\rm t}(G\,\square\,H)=0
.

Proof.
It is straightforward to see that
G\,\square\,H
contains no simplicial vertices.

\square

For outer general position sets, the following result is useful.

Theorem 4.2

[
21
, Theorem 3]

If
G
and
H
are connected graphs, each of order at least
2
, then
(G\,\square\,H){}_{\rm SR}\cong G{}_{\rm SR}\times H{}_{\rm SR}
.

Theorem 4.3

If
G
and
H
are two connected graphs, each of order at least
2
, then

{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G\,\square\,H)=\min\{{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G),{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(H)\}.

Proof.
By Theorem
2.3
we have
{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G\,\square\,H)=\omega((G\,\square\,H){}_{\rm SR})
.
Theorem
4.2
implies that
\omega((G\,\square\,H){}_{\rm SR})=\omega(G{}_{\rm SR}\times H{}_{\rm SR})
.
Since
\omega(G\times H)=\min\{\omega(G),\omega(H)\}
, see
[
8
, Exercise 16.1]
, we have
{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G\,\square\,H)=\min\{\omega(G{}_{\rm SR}),\omega(H{}_{\rm SR})\}
.
Using Theorem
2.3
again we conclude that

{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G\,\square\,H)=\min\{{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G),{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(H)\}
.

\square

Corollary 4.4

If
m,n\geq 3
, then
{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(K_{m}\,\square\,K_{n})=\min\{m,n\}
.

For dual general position sets, the following result that characterizes convex subgraphs of Cartesian product graphs will be applied.

Lemma 4.5

[
8
, Lemma 6.5]

A subgraph
W
of
G=G_{1}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,G_{k}
is convex if and only if
W=U_{1}\,\square\,\cdots\,\square\,U_{k}
, where each
U_{i}
is convex in
G_{i}
.

Theorem 4.6

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, each of order at least
2
. Then
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G\,\square\,H)>0
if and only if one factor is complete and the other factor has a simplicial vertex. Moreover,

(i)

{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{m})=\max\{n,m\}
, and

(ii)

if
H
is not complete and contains a simplicial vertex, then
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(K_{n}\,\square\,H)=n
.

Proof.
Set
V(G)=[n]
and
V(H)=[m]
. So we have
V(G\,\square\,H)=[n]\times[m]
.

Assume first that
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G\,\square\,H)>0
and let
X
be a dual general position set of
G\,\square\,H
. Then
X
is clearly a general position set of
G\,\square\,H
and hence by Theorem
3.1
,
K=(G\,\square\,H)-X
is a convex subgraph of
G\,\square\,H
. Consequently, by Lemma
4.5
we infer that
K=G^{\prime}\,\square\,H^{\prime}
, where
G^{\prime}
is convex in
G
and
H^{\prime}
is convex in
H
. Suppose that
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
are proper subgraphs of
G
and
H
, respectively. Then there exist vertices
g\in V(G)\setminus V(G^{\prime})
and
h\in V(H)\setminus V(H^{\prime})
, such that
g
has a neighbor
g^{\prime}\in V(G)
and
h
has a neighbor
h^{\prime}\in V(H)
. Then
(g^{\prime},h),(g,h),(g,h^{\prime})
is an induced path of
G\,\square\,H
, where all its vertices are from
X
, a contradiction.

By the above contradiction,
p_{G}(X)=V(G)
or
p_{H}(X)=V(H)
. We may without loss of generality assume that
p_{G}(X)=V(G)
. Since layers in Cartesian products are convex, this implies that
V(G)
is a general position set in
G
which in turn implies that
G
is a complete graph. Moreover, since
X
is a general position set we also see that
|p_{H}(X)|=1
and let
h
be the unique vertex of
H
to which

projects. Now, if

is not a simplicial vertex of

, then there exist vertices
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in N_{H}(h)
such that
h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}\notin E(H)
. But then

is clearly not convex, hence

must be a simplicial vertex of

.

To complete the argument we observe that if
G
is complete and
h\in V(H)
is a simplicial vertex, then by Theorem
3.1
the set
V(G)\times\{h\}
is a dual general position set.

The two formulas in the cases when
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G\,\square\,H)>0
follow directly from the above discussion.

\square

In
[

, Theorem 3.8]
it is proved that
{\rm gp}(K_{m}\,\square\,K_{n})=m+n-2
. Combining this result with Corollary
4.4
and Theorem

we see that the four general position invariants considered can vary arbitrary. For instance, for any

we have:

\displaystyle{\rm gp}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2n})

\displaystyle=3n-2,

\displaystyle{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2n})

\displaystyle=2n,

\displaystyle{\rm gp}_{\rm o}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2n})

\displaystyle=n,

\displaystyle{\rm gp}_{\rm t}(K_{n}\,\square\,K_{2n})

\displaystyle=0.

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we have introduced the variety of general position sets. We have completely described the total general position sets and the outer general position sets. On the other hand, we have observed that the dual general position sets are not hereditary. This fact makes the investigation of dual general position sets quite tricky. For instance, we have given a sufficient condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)=0
, yet we were not been able to characterize graphs
G
with this property. The same problems remains open for the case
{\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)=1
.

We have seem that if
G
is a block graph, then
{\rm gp}(G)={\rm gp}_{\rm o}(G)={\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)={\rm gp}_{\rm t}(G)
. It would be an interesting project to characterize the graphs for which this holds. Moreover, the same question can be posed for each subsets of the involved invariants, for instance, to characterize the graphs
G
for which
{\rm gp}(G)={\rm gp}_{\rm d}(G)
holds.

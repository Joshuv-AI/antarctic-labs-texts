A note on the Kuznetsov component of the Veronese double cone
Marin Petković, Franco Rota

Abstract
. This note describes moduli spaces of complexes in the derived category of a Veronese double cone Y Y . Focusing on objects with the same class κ 1 \kappa_{1} as ideal sheaves of lines, we describe the moduli space of Gieseker stable sheaves and show that it has two components. Then, we study the moduli space of stable complexes in the Kuznetsov component of Y Y of the same class, which also has two components. One parametrizes ideal sheaves of lines and it appears in both moduli spaces. The other components are not directly related by a wall-crossing: we show this by describing an intermediate moduli space of complexes as a space of stable pairs in the sense of Pandharipande and Thomas.

2.1.
Stability conditions

Here we give a short review of Bridgeland stability conditions, with the main purpose of fixing the notation for what follows. We direct the interested reader to the seminal work of Bridgeland
[
Bri07
]
and to the survey
[
MS17
]
and references therein for a thorough description.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and let
K(\mathcal{A})
be its Grothendieck group. A
(weak) stability function
is a group homomorphism
Z:K(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\Im Z(E)>0\text{ or }\Im Z(E)=0\text{ and }\Re Z(E)<(\leq)0

for any
0\neq E\in\mathcal{A}
. To a (weak) stability function
Z
, we associate a
slope function

\mu(E)=\begin{cases}\frac{-\Re Z(E)}{\Im Z(E)}&\text{if }\Im Z(E)\neq 0\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

We say that
E\in\mathcal{A}
is
stable
if for all quotients
E\twoheadrightarrow F
in
\mathcal{A}
we have

\mu(E)<\mu(F).

Similarly,
E
is said to be
semistable
if only the non-strict inequality
\mu(E)\leq\mu(F)
holds.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{T}
be a triangulated category and
v:K(\mathsf{T})\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda
a surjection from the Grothendieck group of
\mathsf{T}
to a finite rank lattice. A
(weak) stability condition
on a triangulated category
\mathsf{T}
(with respect to
v
) is a pair
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
consisting of

–

a heart of a bounded
t
-structure
\mathcal{A}

–

a (weak) stability function
K(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{v}\Lambda\xrightarrow{Z}\mathbb{C}

satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(Harder-Narasimhan filtration) Any
E\in\mathcal{A}
has a filtration in
\mathcal{A}
with semistable quotients with decreasing slopes.

(ii)

(Support property) There exists a quadratic form
Q
on
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
which is negative definite on
\ker Z
and for all semistable
E\in\mathcal{A}
we have
Q(E)\geq 0
.

We say an object
E\in\mathsf{T}
is
\sigma
-(semi)stable if
E[k]\in\mathcal{A}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
E[k]
is semistable with respect to
Z

4
4

4

Given a stability condition
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
and
\phi\in(0,1]
, let
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
be the category of
\sigma
-semistable objects
E\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying
Z(v(E))\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}e^{i\pi\phi}
. Then, define
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
for all
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
by imposing
\mathcal{P}(\phi+1)\coloneqq\mathcal{P}(\phi)[1]
.
The collection
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{P}(\phi)\}_{\phi\in\mathbb{R}}
defines a

of
\mathsf{T}
. The datum of a slicing and a compatible stability function is in fact equivalent to that of a stability condition

, Prop. 5.3]
, and it is sometimes convenient to identify
\sigma
with the pair
(Z,\mathcal{P})
.
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
be a weak stability condition on
\mathsf{T}
. For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define subcategories

\mathcal{A}_{\mu\leq\beta}

The tilt of

That is, objects

are complexes with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\mathcal{A}}(E)\in\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{\sigma}\leq\beta}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathcal{A}}(E)\in\mathcal{A}_{\mu_{\sigma}>\beta}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\mathcal{A}}(E)=0,\text{ for }i\neq-1,0.

For a smooth projective variety

2.1.1.

2.1.2.

For a class

Definition 2.4
.

A

(

Fix

–

, where

Let
Y

The derived category of

Definition 3.1

A similar definition holds for lines and conics in

A curve

Lines and conics in

are described in

, Sec. 3]

are the closure of fibers of the projection

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a degree 1 curve in

. Then the image

is a conic in

, which intersects

in three (possibly coinciding) points with multiplicity 2. There are two possibilities:

–

is a line, and

is also a line.

–

:

is a doubled line. Then

is a smooth curve of genus 1, or a singular rational curve.

Proof.

Since

is induced by the linear series

,

must be a conic on

. Note first of all that if

is reducible then so is

. Hence,

If

.
So

If

We can now classify Gieseker-semistable sheaves of class

Proposition 3.4
.

on

are exactly ideal sheaves of subschemes

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

is an ideal sheaf. This is a standard argument: since

is the degree of the Hilbert polynomial, so

.

(i)
,
(ii)
, and

. Observe moreover that Proposition

implies the following:

Proposition 3.5
.

The moduli space

is isomorphic to the Hilbert scheme of lines

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of

∎

4.

For the rest of this note,

6.1.
P(\kappa_{1})
as a generalized Quot scheme

In Section
3
we showed that

is isomorphic to the Hilbert scheme of lines on
Y
. Here, we give a similar interpretation for the moduli space
P(\kappa_{1})
of
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable objects as quotients of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
in an appropriate heart of
D^{b}(Y)
.

Consider the sheaves of the form
\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)
, where
p\in Y
(possibly
p=y_{0}
) and
C=C_{x}
for some
x\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
. By Riemann-Roch we also have

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)(t))=1+t.

However, the
\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)
are not sheaf quotients of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
and do not represent points of the Hilbert scheme of lines of
Y
.

In this section, we consider a different space of quotients, and show that the distinguished triangles

(22)

\begin{split}I_{\ell}\to&\;\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{\ell}\\
F_{p}[-1]\to&\;\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)\\

are all short exact sequences in an appropriate abelian category (the notation here is the same as that of Theorem
5.1
).

More precisely, define
\mathcal{B}^{\theta}_{\alpha,\beta}
as follows: pick
(\alpha,\beta)\in V
so that the chain of inequalities

\frac{\beta}{-\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}}{2}+1\right)}=\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(F_{p})>0=\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(p))>\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})=\frac{2\beta}{-(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})}

, we may pick

F
is any unstable object of class
-\kappa_{1}
, then any destabilizing quotient

satisfies
\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(G)<\theta\coloneqq\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(F)-\epsilon
.

Then, consider the torsion pair in
\Coh^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(Y)
consisting of the categories
\Coh^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(Y)_{\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}\leq\theta}
and
\Coh^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(Y)_{\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}>\theta}
generated by
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable objects of slope
\leq\theta
and
>\theta
respectively. Denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\theta}_{\alpha,\beta}
the (shift of the) corresponding tilt:

\mathcal{B}^{\theta}_{\alpha,\beta}\coloneqq\left[\Coh^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(Y)_{\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}\leq\theta},\Coh^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(Y)_{\mu^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}>\theta}[-1]\right].

\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}

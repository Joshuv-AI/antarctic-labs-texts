Abstract
. We discuss relations between different notions of ranks for multilinear forms. In particular we show that the Schmidt and the analytic ranks for trilinear forms are essentially proportional.



1.
Introduction

In recent years there is growing interest in properties of polynomials which are independent of the number of variables.
To study such properties various notions were introduced for measuring the complexity of polynomials.
In this paper we compare three notion of rank of polynomials
P
over a field
{\mathbf{k}}
- the Schmidt rank
r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
, the slice rank
s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
both defined for arbitrary fields
{\mathbf{k}}
, and the analytic rank
a_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
defined for finite fields
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}
be a field,
\mathbf{V}
be a finite-dimensional k-vector space,
V=\mathbf{V}({\mathbf{k}})
, let

\mathcal{S}_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mathcal{S}^{d}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
be the graded algebra of polynomial functions on
\mathbf{V}
and let

P\in\mathcal{S}^{d}_{\mathbf{k}}
.

(1)

The
Schmidt rank

r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
is the minimal number
r
such that
P
can be written in the form
P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}R_{i}
, where
Q_{i},R_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
are polynomials on
V
of degrees
<d
.

(2)

The
slice rank

s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
is the minimal number
r
such that
P
can be written in the form
P=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Q_{i}R_{i}
, where
\deg(Q_{i})=1
.

(3)

In the case when
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field and

\psi:{\mathbf{k}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
a non-trivial additive character
we write
A_{{\mathbf{k}},\psi}(P):=\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\psi(P(v))}{q^{\mathrm{dim}(V)}}
. We define the
analytic rank

a_{{\mathbf{k}},\psi}(P):=-\log_{q}(|A_{\mathbb{F}_{q},\psi}(P)|)

These three notions of complexity of polynomials play an important role in many problems in number theory, additive combinatorics, and algebraic geometry.
The Schmidt rank, also called the
h
-invariant, was first introduced by Schmidt in his paper on integer points in varieties defined over the rationals. In his paper Schmidt showed that over the complex field the Schmidt rank of a polynomials is proportional to the codimension of the singular locus of the associated variety. The same notion of complexity was reintroduced in the work of Ananyan and Hochster
[
1
]
as the
strength
of a polynomial and was used in the proof of the Stillman conjecture. The notion of slice rank played an important role in the arguments for the cap set problem in combinatorics
[
3
,
6
,
15
]
. Finally the notion of analytic rank for polynomials over finite fields was introduced in Gower and Wolf
[
10
]
as a tool for studying the CS-complexity of systems of linear equations.

Remark 1.2
.

It is clear that
r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
and
r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)=s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
if
d\leq 3.

In this paper we consider only the case of multilinear polynomials. Namely
V=V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}
is a product of
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces and
P:V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
is a multilinear polynomial.

We introduce two additional definitions.

Definition 1.3
.

(1)

In the case when
V=V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}
is a product of
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector spaces and

P:V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}
is a multilinear polynomial we define a
{\mathbf{k}}
-subvariety
{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}\subset\mathbf{V}_{2}\times\dots\times\mathbf{V}_{d}
by
{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}:=\{(v_{2},\dots,v_{d})\in\mathbf{V}_{2}\times\dots\times\mathbf{V}_{d}|P(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d})=0,\forall v_{1}\in V_{1}\}
.

(2)

For a subspace
L\subset(V_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{d})^{\vee}
we define

r_{\mathbf{k}}(L)
as the minimum of
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathrm{codim}(W_{i})
over the set of subspaces
W_{i}\subset V_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, such that
l_{|W_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes W_{d}}\equiv 0
for all
l\in L
.

Claim 1.4
.

For a multilinear polynomial

P:V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbf{k}}

the slice rank
s_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P)
is equal to the minimum of

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\operatorname{codim}_{V_{i}}(W_{i})
where

W_{i}\subset V_{i},1\leq i\leq d
are subspaces such that

P_{|W_{1}\times\dots\times W_{d}}\equiv 0
. It follows that if
L
is one dimensional
L={\mathbf{k}}P
then
r_{{\mathbf{k}}}(L)=s_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P)
.

Lemma 1.5
.

In the case when
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
we have
A_{{\mathbf{k}},\psi}(P)=\frac{|{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|}{q^{\sum_{i=2}^{d}\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})}}
.

Proof.

For
(v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in V_{2}\times\ldots\times V_{d}
we define
\psi_{v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}:V_{1}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\star}
by

\psi_{v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}(v_{1}):=\psi(P(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n})).

It is clear that
\psi_{v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}
is an additive character of
V_{1}
which trivial if and only if
(v_{2},\dots,v_{n})\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Therefore
\sum_{v_{1}\in V_{1}}\psi_{v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}(v_{1})=0
if
v_{1}\not\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\sum_{v_{1}\in V_{1}}\psi_{v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}(v_{1})=q^{\mathrm{dim}(V_{1})}
if
v_{1}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
∎

Corollary 1.6
.

The analytic rank of a multilinear polynomial
P
does not depend on the choice of the non-trivial additive character
\psi
. We will denote it by
a_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
.

Fro a field
{\mathbf{k}}
we denote by
\bar{\mathbf{k}}
the algebraic closure. Obviously we have
r_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(P)\leq r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
. We conjecture that the reverse inequality is essentially holds as well.

Conjecture 1.7

(d)
.

For any
d\geq 2
there exists
\kappa_{d}>0
such that

r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq\kappa_{d}r_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(P)
for any field
{\mathbf{k}}
and a multilinear
{\mathbf{k}}
-polynomial
P
of degree
d
, where
\bar{\mathbf{k}}
is the algebraic closure of
{\mathbf{k}}
.

Remark 1.8
.

It is easy to see that Conjecture
1.7
holds for
d=2
with
\kappa_{2}=1
.

In
[
4

Derksen introduced the notion of the

-stable rank
of a multilinear polynomial (denoted

) defined in terms of Geometric Invariant Theory and proved the following

(1)

and

(2)

\frac{2s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)}{d}\leq r_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{G}(P)\leq s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)

This immediately implies
the inequality
s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq\frac{3}{2}s_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(P)
for trilinear polynomials
P
.
Since
s_{\mathbf{k}}(P)=r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
for trilinear polynomials

we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.9

(Dersken)
.

For trilinear polynomials we have
r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)\leq\frac{3}{2}r_{\bar{\mathbf{k}}}(P)
.

Quantitative estimates for the relation between analytic rank and Schmidt rank provide a means for obtaining quantitative bounds for important problems in additive combinatorics number theory and algebraic geometry.

(d)

For any

such that
r_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(P)\leq c_{d}a_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(P)

for any multilinear polynomial of degree

A lemma on the codimension

3.2.

For

{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{n}
be the corresponding hypersurfaces and define
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}:={\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\cap(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i})
.

Claim 3.4
.

Each component
{\mathbf{C}}
of
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}
is of dimension
m-j
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction in
j
. The statement obviously is true for
j=0
.

Any component
{\mathbf{C}}
of
{\mathbf{B}}_{j+1}
is a component of an intersection

{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{j+1}
for some component
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
of
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}
. By induction
\mathrm{dim}({\mathbf{C}}^{\prime})=m-j
. So not all the functions
h_{j}
vanish on
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
. Hence by the genericity of the choice of linear combinations
\{H_{j}\}
we see that

H_{j+1}
does not vanish on
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
and therefore
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{j+1}
is
of pure dimension
m-j-1
.
∎

As follows from Claim
3.4
the intersection

{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}
has dimension
0
. Therefore the Bézout’s theorem implies that
|{\mathbf{Y}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots{\mathbf{Z}}_{n-c}|\leq q^{m}d^{c}
. Since
{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})={\mathbf{Y}}\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Z}}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{X}}\cap{\mathbf{Y}}_{1}\cap\dots\cap{\mathbf{Y}}_{n-c}
we see that

|{\mathbf{Y}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\leq q^{m}d^{c}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

(1)

Proof.

Let

5.
The adaptation of the Schmidt’s result for fields of finite characteristic in the case when
d=3
.

We use notations from Definitions
1.1
,
1.3
. In
[
14
]
W. Schmidt proved the following result.

Theorem 5.1

(Schmidt)
.

For any
d\geq 2
there exists

D_{d}
such that for any complex multilinear polynomial

P:V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
we have
r_{\mathbb{C}}(P)\leq D_{d}g
where
g:=\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbf{V}_{2}\times\dots\times\mathbf{V}_{d}}{\mathbf{Z}}_{P}

His proof extends to any algebraically closed field, and we sketch it here for the three-dimensional case.

In this section we fix an algebraically closed field
{\mathbf{k}}
and write
r(P)
instead of
r_{\mathbf{k}}(P)
. Let
{\mathbf{X}}
be an algebraic
{\mathbf{k}}
-variety.
Since our field
{\mathbf{k}}
is algebraically closed any constructible subset

Y\subset X
defines uniquely a subset
{\mathbf{Y}}\subset{\mathbf{X}}
such that
Y={\mathbf{Y}}({\mathbf{k}})
. We say that a constructible subset
Y\subset X
is open if the subset
{\mathbf{Y}}\subset{\mathbf{X}}
is open and we define
\dim(Y):=\dim({\mathbf{Y}})

For a trilinear polynomial
P:U\times V\times W\to{\mathbf{k}}

the
constructible subset of points
(v,w)
such that
P(u,v,w)=0

.

The main goal of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

For any trilinear polynomial
P:U\times V\times W\to{\mathbf{k}}

r(P)\leq 2g
where
g:=\mathrm{codim}_{V\times W}Z_{P}
.

Before proving Theorem
5.2
we remind the reader of some results from Algebra.

5.1.
Linear Algebra

Definition 5.3
.

For a bilinear form
B:U\times V\to{\mathbf{k}}
we write

(1)

S_{U}(B):=\{u\in U|B(u,v)=0,\forall v\in V\}
.

(2)

S_{V}(B):=\{v\in V|B(u,v)=0,\forall u\in U\}
.

Remark 5.4
.

r(B)=\operatorname{codim}_{U}S_{U}(B)=\operatorname{codim}_{V}S_{V}(B)
.

Let
X
be a smooth curve over
{\mathbf{k}}
, let
t_{0}\in X
and let
B(t):U\times V\to{\mathbf{k}},t\in X
be a family of bilinear forms. We write
B:=B(t_{0})
,
S_{U}:=S_{U}(B)
,
S_{V}:=S_{V}(B)
, and
C:=(\partial B(t)/\partial t)_{t=t_{0}}
.

Claim 5.5
.

If
r(B(t))\leq r(B),t\in X
then

C_{|S_{U}\times S_{V}}\equiv 0
.

Proof.

We show that the assumption that
C_{|S_{U}\times S_{V}}\not\equiv 0
leads to a contradiction.
To simplify notations we assume that
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{A}}
and
t_{0}=0
.

To start with we choose bases
e_{i},f_{j}
, where
1\leq i\leq\dim(U),1\leq j\leq\dim(V)
of
U
and
V
such that

(1)

B(e_{i},f_{j})=\delta_{i,j},1\leq i,j\leq r(B)
.

(2)

B(e_{i},f_{j})=0
if either
i>r(B)
or
j>r(B)
.

Then
S_{U}
is the span of
\{e_{i}\}
, where
r(B)+1\leq i\leq\dim(U)
and
S_{V}
is the span of
\{f_{j}\}
, where
r(B)+1\leq j\leq\dim(V)
.
Since
C_{|S_{U}\times S_{V}}\not\equiv 0
there exist a pair
(i_{0},j_{0})
such that
i_{0},j_{0}>r(B)
and
C(e_{i_{0}},f_{j_{0}})\neq 0
.

Let
U^{\prime}\subset U
,
\dim(U^{\prime})=r(B)+1
be the span of
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq r(B)}
and of
e_{i_{0}}
and
V^{\prime}\subset V
,
\dim(V^{\prime})=r(B)+1
be the span of
\{f_{j}\},_{1\leq j\leq r(B)}
and of
f_{j_{0}}
.

We denote by
\Delta(t)
be the determinant of the
bilinear form of
B(t)_{U^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}}
with respect to chosen bases of
U^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
. The condition
r(B(t))\leq r(B),t\in{\mathbf{k}}
implies that
\Delta(t)\equiv 0
. On the other hand

\Delta(t)\equiv tC(e_{i_{0}},f_{j_{0}})\mod(t^{2})
.

This contradiction proves Lemma
5.5
.

∎

5.2.
Transcendence degree

Definition 5.6
.

(1)

For a field extension

{\mathbf{k}}(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\hookrightarrow K

(2)

For a point

we denote by
{\mathbf{k}}(w)\subset K
the subfield generated by
{\mathbf{k}}
and the coordinates of

tr_{\mathbf{k}}(w):=tr({\mathbf{k}}(w)/{\mathbf{k}})

(3)

Let
{\mathbf{X}}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{N}
be an irreducible
{\mathbf{k}}
-subvariety. A point

w\in{\mathbf{X}}(K)
is generic if it is not contained in any proper
{\mathbf{k}}
-subvariety of
X
.

Claim 5.7
.

(1)

Let
{\mathbf{X}}\subset{\mathbf{A}}^{M}
be an irreducible
{\mathbf{k}}

be generic point. Then

(2)

Let

be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear map,

Proof of Theorem
5.2

Proof.

Let
P:U\times V\times W\to{\mathbf{k}}
be as in Theorem

5.2
and

be an irreducible component of the maximal
dimension,
g:=\operatorname{codim}_{V\times W}Z

.

Let

be a generic point of

, and let

be the projection of

on

. For

we denote by

the bilinear forms on
V
given by

.

Let
S_{U}=\{u\in U|P_{w_{0}}(u,v)=0,\forall v\in V\}
,
and let
S_{V}=\{v\in V|P_{w_{0}}(u,v)=0,\forall u\in U\}
. Since
w_{0}
is a generic point of
Y

r(P_{y})\leq r(P_{w_{0}})
for all

.
Since

and we can define

the tangent space at

..

It follows from

Proposition 5.8

(1)

P_{|S_{U}\times S_{V}\times S_{W}}\equiv 0

(2)

\operatorname{codim}_{U}(S_{U})+\operatorname{codim}_{W}(S_{W})+\operatorname{codim}_{V}(S_{V})\leq 2g
.

Proof.

To show that

we have to show that for

the restriction of the bilinear form

is

Choose a smooth curve on

passing through

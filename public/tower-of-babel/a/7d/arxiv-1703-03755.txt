The extremal function for geometry minors of matroids over prime fields
Peter Nelson, Zach Walsh

Abstract
. A frame template over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} describes the precise way in which a given 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -representable matroid is close to being a frame matroid. Our main result determines the maximum-rank projective or affine geometry that is described by a given frame template over a prime field. Subject to the matroid minors hypothesis of Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle, we use our result to determine, for each projective or affine geometry N N over a prime field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , a best-possible upper bound on the number of elements in a simple 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -representable matroid M M of sufficiently large rank with no N N -minor.



1.
Introduction

Frame templates were introduced by Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle [
2
] as a way to describe the specific structure of classes of matroids representable over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
.
A frame template describes the precise way in which a given
\mathbb{F}
-representable matroid is close to being a frame matroid.
For this reason, the definition of frame template is quite technical, and we defer its statement to Section 4.

Our main result, Theorem
7.1
, states that every frame template over a prime field describes a class of matroids that either contains a large projective geometry or affine geometry, or is degenerate.
Before proving this result, we present a simpler definition of a frame template, and prove in Lemma
5.6
that it is equivalent to the definition in [
2
].
Our definition from an earlier version of this paper has since been further simplified in [
6
], and applied in [
1
] and [
4
] to study classes of ternary and quaternary matroids, respectively.

Our motivation for studying frame templates is the matroid minors hypothesis of Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle [
2
], whose proof has been announced but not yet written.
Subject to this hypothesis (Hypothesis
4.1
), we show that our main result implies the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If
M
is a simple binary matroid of sufficiently large rank with no
\PG(t+2,2)
-minor, then

|M|\leq 2^{t}\tbinom{r(M)-t+1}{2}+2^{t}-1.

We also show that this bound is best-possible, and characterize the unique example where equality holds for each sufficiently large rank.
In fact, we solve the analogous problems for excluding arbitrary projective and affine geometries over any prime field.

For a class
\mathcal{M}
of matroids, let
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)
denote the maximum number of elements in a simple matroid in
\mathcal{M}
of rank at most
n
. (If
\mathcal{M}
is a nonempty subclass of the
\GF(q)
-representable matroids then
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)
is always defined, with
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)\leq\tfrac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
for all
n
.) We call
h_{\mathcal{M}}
the
extremal function
of
\mathcal{M}
. This is often referred to as the
size
or
growth rate
function; our terminology here is an attempt to agree with the broader combinatorics literature. A simple matroid
M\in\mathcal{M}
with
|M|=h_{\mathcal{M}}(r(M))
is
extremal
in
\mathcal{M}
. A nonempty class
\mathcal{M}
of matroids is
minor-closed
if it is closed under both minors and isomorphism. The following is a simplified version of the growth rate theorem of Geelen, Kung and Whittle [
3
].

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\mathbb{F}
is a prime field and
\mathcal{M}
is a proper minor-closed subclass of the
\mathbb{F}
-representable matroids, then either

•

there is some
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)\leq\alpha n
for all
n
, or

•

\mathcal{M}
contains all graphic matroids and there is some
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\binom{n+1}{2}\leq h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)\leq\alpha n^{2}
for all
n
.

We call classes of the latter type
quadratic
.
The motivation for the applications of our main result is that they may help solve the difficult problem of classifying the extremal functions of quadratic classes of representable matroids exactly.

To state our results, we first introduce the terminology used to naturally describe the extremal functions and the extremal matroids which will occur. Fix a finite field
\mathbb{F}
and a subgroup
\Gamma
of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}^{*}
. The
weight
of a vector is its number of nonzero entries. A
unit vector
is a weight-
1
vector whose nonzero entry is
1
. A
\Gamma
-frame matrix
is an
\mathbb{F}
-matrix in which each column is either a weight-
0
vector, a unit vector, or a weight-
2
vector of the form
\gamma e_{j}-e_{i}
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
and distinct unit vectors
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
. A matroid represented by a
\Gamma
-frame matrix is a
\Gamma
-frame matroid.
The class of
\Gamma
-frame matroids is well-known to be minor-closed; see [
12
] for a comprehensive reference.

Write
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
for the class of
\Gamma
-frame matroids, and
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
for the class of matroids having a representation
{{P}\brack{A}}
for some
\mathbb{F}
-matrix
P
with at most
t
rows and some
\Gamma
-frame matrix
A
. (In this notation
\mathbb{F}
is implicit.) Note that
\mathcal{G}(\{1\})^{0}
is the class of graphic matroids. We will see that
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
is minor-closed, and has extremal function
f_{|\mathbb{F}|,|\Gamma|,t}(n)
defined by

f_{|\mathbb{F}|,|\Gamma|,t}(n)=|\mathbb{F}|^{t}\left(|\Gamma|\tbinom{n-t}{2}+n-t\right)-\tfrac{|\mathbb{F}|^{t}-1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1}

for all
n\geq t
. We refer to this function
f_{q,g,t}(n)
, which is quadratic in
n
with leading term
\tfrac{1}{2}gq^{t}n^{2}
, frequently throughout. For each
n\geq t
, there is a unique rank-
n
extremal matroid
M
in
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
given by
M\cong\si\left(M{{P}\brack{A}}\right)
, where
{{P}\brack{A}}
includes all possible columns for which
P
has
t
rows and
A
is a
\Gamma
-frame matrix with
n-t
rows. We call this extremal matroid
\DG(n,\Gamma)^{t}
; it will be discussed later in more detail.

We can now fully state the main applications of our main result (Theorem
7.1
) for excluding projective and affine geometries over all prime fields. The
p\leq 3
case differs from the general case; this is essentially because rank-
3
projective/affine geometries can be binary/ternary frame matroids but are not frame matroids over larger fields. These results are all subject to Hypothesis
4.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
N
be one of
\PG(t+2,2)
or
\AG(t+3,2)
. If
\mathcal{M}
is the class of binary matroids with no
N
-minor, then

h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f_{2,1,t}(n)=2^{t}\tbinom{n-t+1}{2}+2^{t}-1

for all sufficiently large
n
. Moreover, if
M
is extremal in
\mathcal{M}
and
r(M)
is sufficiently large, then
M\cong\DG(r(M),\{1\})^{t}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the class
\mathcal{M}
of ternary matroids with no
\AG(t+2,3)
-minor satisfies

h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f_{3,2,t}(n)=3^{t}(n-t)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(3^{t}-1)

for all sufficiently large
n
. Furthermore, if
M
is extremal in
\mathcal{M}
and
r(M)
is sufficiently large, then
M\cong\DG(r(M),\GF(3)^{*})^{t}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
N
be either
\PG(t+1,p)
for some prime
p\geq 3
or
\AG(t+1,p)
for some prime
p\geq 5
. If
\mathcal{M}
is the class of
\GF(p)
-representable matroids with no
N
-minor, then

h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f_{p,(p-1)/2,t}(n)=p^{t}\left(\tfrac{p-1}{2}\tbinom{n-t}{2}+n-t\right)+\tfrac{p^{t}-1}{p-1}

for all sufficiently large
n
. Moreover, if
M
is extremal in
\mathcal{M}
and
r(M)
is sufficiently large, then
M\cong\DG(r(M),\Gamma)^{t}
, where
\Gamma
is the index-
2
subgroup of
\GF(p)^{*}
.

Theorem
1.3
was previously known only for
t=0
and, in the case of projective geometries,
t=1
(see [
5
,
7
,
8
]); Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
were unknown for all
t
.
They will all follow from a more general result, Theorem
8.1
, which is also subject to Hypothesis
4.1
; we state a simplified version here.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\GF(p)
be a prime field. If
\mathcal{M}
is a quadratic minor-closed class of
\mathbb{F}
-representable matroids then there exists
\Gamma\leq\mathbb{F}^{*}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(n)+O(n)
. Furthermore, either

•

for all sufficiently large
n
we have
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(n)
and
\DG(n,\Gamma)^{t}
is the unique extremal rank-

\mathcal{M}
, or

•

for all sufficiently large

contains a simple rank-

.

Most of our material, when specialised to binary matroids, was originally proved in [

].

2.
Preliminaries

We use the notation of Oxley [
10
], and also write
|M|
for
|E(M)|
and
\varepsilon(M)
for
|\si(M)|
for a matroid
M
. The rows and columns of matrices and the co-ordinates of vectors will always be indexed by sets, and thus have no inherent ordering. We write
0_{A}
and
\mathbf{1}_{A}
for the zero and all-ones vector in
\mathbb{F}^{A}
respectively, and
0_{A\times B}
for the zero matrix in
\mathbb{F}^{A\times B}
, and we identify
\mathbb{F}^{A}\times\mathbb{F}^{B}
with
\mathbb{F}^{A\cup B}
for disjoint
A
and
B
. For a matrix
P=\mathbb{F}^{A\times B}
, we write
P[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]
for the submatrix with rows in
A^{\prime}
and columns in
B^{\prime}
, and write
P[A^{\prime}]
for
P[A^{\prime},B]
and
P[B^{\prime}]
for
P[A,B^{\prime}]
where there is no ambiguity. If
|A|=|B|
but
A\neq B
then the ‘determinant’ of a matrix
P\in\mathbb{F}^{A\times B}
is only defined up to sign, and identity matrices do not make sense, but nonsingularity and
P^{-1}
(where it exists) are well-defined. We refer to any square matrix in
\mathbb{F}^{A\times B}
whose columns are distinct unit vectors as a
bijection matrix
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{E}
. For a vector
u\in U
and a set
X\subseteq E
, we write
u[X]
for the co-ordinate projection of
u
onto
X
, and
U[X]=\{u[X]\colon u\in U\}
. For a set
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{F}
(typically a multiplicative subgroup), write
\Gamma U=\{\gamma u\colon u\in U,\gamma\in\Gamma\}
. For a matrix
P\in\mathbb{F}^{E\times E}
we denote
\{Pu\colon u\in U\}
by
PU
. If
U
and
W
are additive subgroups of
\mathbb{F}^{E}
then we say
U
and
W
are
skew
if
U\cap W=\{0\}
, and if they are skew subspaces with
U+W=\mathbb{F}^{E}
then they are
complementary
; a pair of complementary subspaces gives rise to a well-defined projection map
\psi\colon\mathbb{F}^{E}\to W
for which
\psi(u+w)=w
for all
u\in U
and
w\in W
.

Represented Matroids

Most of our arguments involve manipulation of matrices; for this purpose we will use a formalised notion of a matroid representation. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
E
be a finite set. We say two subspaces
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}^{E}
are
projectively equivalent
if
U_{1}=U_{2}D
for some nonsingular diagonal matrix
D
.
For a field
\mathbb{F}
, we define an
\mathbb{F}
-represented matroid
to be a pair
(E,U)
where
E
is a finite set and
U
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{E}
; two represented matroids
(E_{1},U_{1})
and
(E_{2},U_{2})
are equal if
E_{1}=E_{2}
and
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are projectively equivalent, and are
isomorphic
if there is a bijection
\varphi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
such that
\{(u_{\varphi(e)}\colon e\in E_{1})\colon u\in U_{1}\}
is projectively equivalent to
U_{2}
.

A
representation
of
M
is an
\mathbb{F}
-matrix
A
whose row space is projectively equivalent to
U
(that is, its rowspace is
U
after some set of nonzero column scalings); we write
M=M(A)
. For each
X\subseteq E
, we write
r(X)
for the dimension of the subspace
U[X]
, or equivalently
\rank(A[X])
for any representation
A
of
M
. Note that
r(\cdot)
is invariant under projective equivalence so is well-defined. The pair
\tilde{M}=(E,r)
is a matroid in the usual sense, and we call this the
abstract matroid
associated with
M
; an abstract matroid
N
is thus
\mathbb{F}
-representable if and only if there is some
\mathbb{F}
-represented matroid
M
with
N=\tilde{M}
.

From here on we will be working with represented matroids exclusively, abbreviating them as just
matroids
. To be precise, we define
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
in this new context to be the class of
\mathbb{F}
-represented matroids of the form
M{{P}\brack{A}}
for some
\Gamma
-frame matrix
A
and some matrix
P
with at most
t
rows.

The
dual
of a represented matroid
M=(E,U)
is defined to be
M^{*}=(E,U^{\perp})
, and for a set
X\subseteq E
we define
M\!\setminus\!X=(E-X,U[E-X])
and
M/X=(M^{*}\!\setminus\!X)^{*}
and define minors of
M
accordingly; these are well-defined, and agree with the usual notions of minors and duality in the abstract matroid. An
extension
of a represented matroid
M
is a matroid
M^{+}
such that
M^{+}\!\setminus\!e=M
for some
e\in E(M^{+})
, or equivalently a matroid having a representation obtained from one of
M
by appending a new column. Any invariant property or parameter of abstract matroids can easily be extended to represented matroids, and we define (co-)simplicity, (co-)simplification, the parameter
\varepsilon(M)
, and the extremal function
h_{\mathcal{M}}
for a class
\mathcal{M}
of represented matroids in the obvious way.

Connectivity

Write
\lambda_{M}(A)=r_{M}(A)+r_{M}(E(M)-A)-r(M)
for each
A\subseteq E(M)
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, a matroid
M
of rank at least
k
is
vertically
k
-connected
if for every partition
(A,B)
of
E(M)
with
\lambda_{M}(A)<k-1
, either
A
or
B
is spanning in
M
. (This definition is somewhat nonstandard but equivalent to the usual one.)
We require a theorem from [
9
], which roughly states that the highly-connected matroids exemplify the densest members of any quadratic class. The version we state is both simplified and specialised to matroids over prime fields.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a prime field and let
f(x)
be a real quadratic polynomial with positive leading coefficient. If
\mathcal{M}
is a quadratic minor-closed class of
\mathbb{F}
-represented matroids with
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)>f(n)
for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there is a vertically
k
-connected matroid
M\in\mathcal{M}
with
r(M)\geq k
and
\varepsilon(M)>f(r(M))
.

To obtain the equality characterisation in Theorem
1.1
as well as the bound, we need a lemma that is a variant of the above.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field and
f(x)
be a real quadratic polynomial with positive leading coefficient, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a restriction-closed class of
\mathbb{F}
-represented matroids and
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
, then for all sufficiently large
r
, every rank-
r
matroid
M\in\mathcal{M}
with
\varepsilon(M)=f(r)
is vertically
k
-connected.

Proof.

Say
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+c
where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
. Set
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
so that
n_{0}\geq 2k+a^{-1}
, while
h_{\mathcal{M}}(n)=f(n)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, and
f
is increasing on
[n_{0},\infty)
. Let
n_{1}=\max(2n_{0},f(k),\tfrac{1}{2a}(|\mathbb{F}|^{n_{0}}+a-b))
; we show that every
M\in\mathcal{M}
with
r(M)\geq n_{1}
and
\varepsilon(M)=f(r(M))
is vertically
k
-connected.

Let
M\in\mathcal{M}
satisfy
r(M)=r\geq n_{1}
and
\varepsilon(M)=f(r)
. If
M
is not vertically
k
-connected, then there is a partition
(A,B)
of
E(M)
for which
1\leq r_{M}(A)\leq r_{M}(B)\leq r-1
and
r\leq r_{M}(A)+r_{M}(B)<r+k
. Let
r_{B}=r_{M}(B)
and
r_{A}=r_{M}(A)
; note that
r_{B}\geq\tfrac{r}{2}\geq n_{0}
so we have
\varepsilon(M|B)\leq f(r_{B})\leq f(r-1)=a(r-1)^{2}+b(r-1)+c
.

If
r_{A}\leq n_{0}
then
\varepsilon(M|A)<|\mathbb{F}|^{n_{0}}
so

\displaystyle ar^{2}+br+c

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(M)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(M|A)+\varepsilon(M|B)

\displaystyle<|\mathbb{F}|^{n_{0}}+a(r-1)^{2}+b(r-1)+c.

This implies that
r<\tfrac{1}{2a}\left(|\mathbb{F}|^{n_{0}}+a-b\right)\leq n_{1}
, a contradiction.

If
r_{A}>n_{0}
then
\varepsilon(M|A)\leq f(r_{A})
and we have

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(M|A)+\varepsilon(M|B)

\displaystyle\leq a(r_{A}^{2}+r_{B}^{2})+b(r_{A}+r_{B})+2c

\displaystyle=a(r_{A}+r_{B})^{2}+b(r_{A}+r_{B})+2c-2ar_{A}r_{B}

\displaystyle<a(r+k)^{2}+b(r+k)+2c-2an_{0}(\tfrac{r}{2})

\displaystyle=f(r)+f(k)+ra(2k-n_{0}),

where we use
r_{A}\geq n_{0}
and
r_{B}\geq\tfrac{r}{2}
. This gives
ra(n_{0}-2k)<f(k)
so, using
n_{0}\geq 2k+a^{-1}
, we have
r<f(k)\leq n_{1}
, again a contradiction.
∎

3.
Frame matroids and extensions

In this section we define the extremal matroids in classes
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
, and consider certain slightly larger classes.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field,
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
B_{0}
be an
n
-element set and
b_{1},\dotsc,b_{n}
be the unit vectors in
\mathbb{F}^{B_{0}}
. Let

W(n)=\{b_{i}\colon i\in[n]\}\cup\{-b_{i}+\gamma b_{j}\colon i,j\in[n],i<j,\gamma\in\Gamma\},

and
A\in\mathbb{F}^{B_{0}\times E}
be a matrix whose set of columns is
W(n)
. We write
\DG(n,\Gamma)
for any matroid isomorphic to
M(A)
(this is a
Dowling geometry
over
\Gamma
), and call any matrix obtained from such an
A
by column scalings a
standard
representation for
\DG(n,\Gamma)
. Given any
\Gamma
-frame matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{B\times F}
with
\rank(A^{\prime})=n
, we can remove redundant rows, rename rows, and rescale columns to obtain a matrix whose columns are all in
W(n)
; it follows that any rank-
n
extremal matroid in
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
is isomorphic to
\DG(n,\Gamma)
.

For each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
n\geq t
, let
X
be a
t
-element set and
B_{0}
be an
(n-t)
-element set, and
A^{t}\in\mathbb{F}^{(B_{0}\cup X)\times E}
be a matrix whose set of columns is

W^{t}(n)=(\mathbb{F}^{X}\times W(n-t))\cup(U\times\{0_{B_{0}}\}),

where
U
is a maximal set of pairwise non-parallel nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{F}^{X}
. We write
\DG(n,\Gamma)^{t}
for any matroid isomorphic to
M(A^{t})
for such an
A^{t}
, and call any matrix obtained from such an
A^{t}
by column scalings a
standard
representation of
\DG(n,\Gamma)^{t}
. It is not hard to check that, given a standard representation, rescaling rows in
B_{0}
by elements of
\gamma
yields another standard representation. Moreover, given any rank-
n
matroid
M\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
with
n\geq t
, we can remove/append/rename rows then rescale columns to find a representation for
M
whose columns are all in
W^{t}(n)
; therefore every rank-
n
extremal matroid in
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
is isomorphic to
\DG(n,\Gamma)^{t}
. We can thus determine the extremal function
h_{\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}}(n)=|W^{t}(n)|
; we have
|W(n)|=|\Gamma|\binom{n}{2}+n
and so

h_{\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}}(n)=|W^{t}(n)|=|\mathbb{F}|^{t}|W(n-t)|+\tfrac{|\mathbb{F}|^{t}-1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1}=f_{|\mathbb{F}|,|\Gamma|,t}(n),

which justifies our earlier claims.

The next lemma’s proof uses the fact that
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)
is minor-closed.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\GF(q)
be a finite field and
\Gamma\leq\mathbb{F}^{*}
. For all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the class
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
is minor-closed.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
, so there exist
P\in\mathbb{F}^{T\times E}
with
t
rows and a
\Gamma
-frame matrix
Q\in\mathbb{F}^{B\times E}
, such that
M=M(A)
for
A={{P}\brack{Q}}
. Let
e\in E
be a nonloop of
M
. Clearly
M(A)\!\setminus\!e\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
. If
Q[e]=0
then we can perform row-operations within
P
and remove a row of
P
to contract
e
and we have
M(A)/e\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t-1}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
. If
Q[e]\neq 0
then
A
is row-equivalent to a matrix
{{P^{\prime}}\brack{Q}}
for which
P^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}^{T\times E}
satisfies
P^{\prime}[e]=0
. Then
M(A)/e=M{{P^{\prime}}\brack{Q^{\prime}}}
for some
\Gamma
-frame matrix
Q^{\prime}
that represents
M(Q)/e
. Therefore
M(A)/e\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
; it follows that
\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
is minor-closed.
∎

Extensions

If
x\in\mathbb{F}^{*}-\Gamma
, then let
\DG^{(x)}(n,\Gamma)^{t}
denote a matroid of the form

5.
Taming Templates

In this section we prove that templates can be substantially simplified with no loss of generality. Our first few lemmas give basic ways to manipulate templates without changing the class of conforming matroids. The first allows us to generically contract an appropriately structured subset of

Lemma 5.1

Let

and

is an
(E,Z,Y_{1})
-shift matrix. Note that
r(M)\geq\rank(AS)-|C\cup Y_{1}|\geq\rank(A)-c
, and since
\rank(A)
is at least the number of distinct columns in the submatrix
A[B-X,Z]
, we see that
A[B-X,Z]
has at most
r(M)+c
distinct columns. Every column of
(AS)[Z]
is the sum of a column of
A[Y_{1}]
and a column of
A[Z]
, so
(AS)[Z]
has at most
|Y_{1}|(r(M)+c)
distinct columns and
(AS)[Z\cup Y_{0}]
has at most
|Y_{1}|(r(M)+c)+|Y_{0}|\leq c(r(M)+c)
distinct columns. Thus
\varepsilon(M|(Y_{0}\cup Z))\leq\varepsilon(M(AS)|(Y_{0}\cup Z))\leq c(r(M)+c)
. Let
F=E-(C\cup Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}\cup Z)
. We have
M(AS)|F\in\mathcal{G}(\Gamma)^{t}
, so
\varepsilon(M|F)\leq\varepsilon(M(AS)|F)\leq f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(\rank(AS))\leq f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(r(M)+c)
. Now for all
x
we have

\displaystyle f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(x+c)-f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(x)

\displaystyle=p^{t}|\Gamma|((x-t)c+\tfrac{1}{2}(c^{2}+c))

\displaystyle\leq p^{t}(p-1)c(x+c),

since
|\Gamma|\leq p-1
and
c\leq c^{2}
. Combining the above estimates we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon(M)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(M|F)+\varepsilon(M|(Y_{0}\cup Z))

\displaystyle\leq f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(r(M)+c)+c(r(M)+c)

\displaystyle\leq f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(r(M))+p^{t}(p-1)c(r(M)+c)+c(r(M)+c)

\displaystyle\leq f_{p,|\Gamma|,t}(r(M))+p^{t+1}c(r(M)+c),

giving the bound.
∎

For the dual bound, which is linear in rank, we need an easy lemma bounding the density of the dual of a frame matroid. (The lemma applies to any
\Gamma
-frame matroid over any field.)

Lemma 6.2
.

If
M^{*}
is a frame matroid, then
\varepsilon(M)\leq 3r(M)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
M
is simple. Let
A
be a frame representation of
M^{*}
with
r^{*}(M)
rows. If some row of
A
has weight less than
3
then
M^{*}
has a coloop or series pair so
M
is not simple. Thus
A
has at least
3r^{*}(M)
nonzero entries, so the number

of columns of

is at least
\tfrac{3}{2}r^{*}(M)
. Therefore
\tfrac{r(M)}{\varepsilon(M)}=\tfrac{|M|-r^{*}(M)}{|M|}=1-\tfrac{r*(M)}{|M|}\geq\tfrac{1}{3}
.
∎

We now prove our main result; a reduced template in which

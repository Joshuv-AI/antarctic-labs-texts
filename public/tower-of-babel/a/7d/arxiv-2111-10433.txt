Qubit Decoherence and Symmetry Restoration through Real-Time Instantons
Foster Thompson 1 and Alex Kamenev 1,2

Abstract
A parametrically driven quantum oscillator, stabilized by a nonlinear dissipation, exhibits a spontaneous breaking of the parity symmetry. It results in the quantum bi-stability, corresponding to a Bloch sphere of dark states. This makes such a driven-dissipative system an attractive candidate for a qubit. The parity symmetry breaking is exact both on the classical level and within the quantum mechanical perturbation theory. Here we show that non-perturbative quantum effects lead to the symmetry restoration and result in exponentially small but finite qubit decoherence rate. Technically the symmetry restoration is due to real time instanton trajectories of the Keldysh path integral, which represents the Lindbladian evolution of the driven-dissipative oscillator.

To exemplify this, consider a harmonic oscillator subject to an external drive that periodically modulates its resonant frequency
\hat{H}=\omega_{0}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+2\lambda\cos(2\omega_{p}t)(\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}+\hat{a}^{2})
, where
\hat{a}
is a Bose annihilation operator,
[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger}]=1
. In addition to the coherent drive we allow the oscillator to dissipate through interaction with its environment, modelled as a bath of linear oscillators in thermal equilibrium at zero temperature. To ensure the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\hat{a}\to\hat{P}\hat{a}\hat{P}^{\dagger}=-\hat{a}

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}=\sum_{s}\frac{g_{s}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{s}}}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{s}\hat{a}^{2}+\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\hat{b}_{s})

\hat{b}_{s}

\omega_{s}

In the rotating frame of the external drive, ignoring counter-rotating terms, the Hamiltonian becomes effectively time independent:

\hat{H}(\hat{a},\hat{a}^{\dagger})\simeq\Delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\lambda(\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}+\hat{a}^{2}),

(1)

where
\Delta=\omega_{0}-\omega_{p}
. Integrating out the bath degrees of freedom, leads to a dissipative dynamics, described by the Lindblad equation for the reduced density matrix of the oscillator:

\partial_{t}\rho=\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}\rho=-i[\hat{H},\rho]+\gamma\Big(\hat{a}^{2}\rho\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\hat{a}^{2},\rho\}\Big),

Both

\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{+}\rho=\hat{P}\rho

\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{-}\rho=\rho\hat{P}

[\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{\pm},\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}]=0

When the drive amplitude

\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}\rho=\gamma\Big((\hat{a}^{2}-\phi_{0}^{2})\rho(\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}-\phi_{0}^{*2})-\frac{1}{2}\big\{(\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}-\phi_{0}^{*2})(\hat{a}^{2}-\phi_{0}^{2}),\rho\big\}\Big),

It exhibits two oscillatory states, distinguished by their phases relative to the external drive, forming a qubit basis. Such qubit is, however, subject to a precession and decoherence due to the non-perturbative
symmetry restoration. We found that the semiclassical description of this phenomenon relies on the real-time instanton trajectories in the complexified (and Keldysh doubled) phase space of the oscillator. This put the corresponding decoherence mechanism in the universality class of the

\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{q}}=\phi^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{q}*}
. As a consequence, any non-static solutions will require
\phi^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{q}}
and
\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{q}}
to be regarded as independent complex variables. The four equations of motion from Eq.s (
15
) and their four complex conjugates specify eight equations of motion on an eight real-dimensional complexified phase space with the four holomorphic and four anti-holomorphic coordinates
(\phi^{\mathrm{c}},\phi^{\mathrm{q}},\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}},\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}},\phi^{\mathrm{c}*},\phi^{\mathrm{q}*},\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}*},\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}*})
. These EoM are Hamilton’s equations generated by the Hamiltonian
K+K^{*}
. The corresponding Poisson structure has a bracket with holomorphic and antiholomorphic parts:

\displaystyle\{A,B\}=-iA\big(\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}}}+\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}*}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}*}}+\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}*}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}*}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}*}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}*}}+\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}*}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}*}}\big)B=0.

(19)

The holomorphic part of the Poisson bracket kills any anti-holomorphic function and vice-versa. As a consequence, holomorphic and anti-holomorphic functions always commute. Moreover, two holomorphic functions
A
and
B
commute when

\displaystyle 0=A\big(\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{c}}}+\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}}}\vec{\partial}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}}-\reflectbox{$\vec{\reflectbox{$\partial$}}$}_{\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}}\vec{\partial}_{\phi^{\mathrm{q}}}\big)B.

(20)

In such cases, there conjugates will also commute with one another, yielding a set of four commuting functions
A,B,A^{*},B^{*}
. It is this fact that explains why the complexified motion in the problem considered here is integrable. There is an additional integral of motion
M
commutes with
K
:

M=\bigg(2\phi_{0}^{2}(\phi^{\mathrm{c}2}+\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}2})+(\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi^{\mathrm{q}}-\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}\phi^{\mathrm{c}}+2\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi^{\mathrm{c}})\Big(\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}\phi^{\mathrm{c}}+\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi^{\mathrm{q}}+\frac{2i\Delta}{\gamma}\Big)\bigg)\\
\bigg(2\phi_{0}^{2}(\phi^{\mathrm{q}2}+\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}2})-(\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi^{\mathrm{q}}-\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}\phi^{\mathrm{c}}-2\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}\phi^{\mathrm{q}})\Big(\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{q}}\phi^{\mathrm{c}}+\bar{\phi}^{\mathrm{c}}\phi^{\mathrm{q}}+\frac{2i\Delta}{\gamma}\Big)\bigg).

(21)

This implies the existence of a total of three additional mutually Poisson-commuting functions
M,M^{*},K-K^{*}
alongside the Hamiltonian
K+K^{*}
.

II
Keldysh Path Integral Details

In the conventional construction of the Keldysh path integral, the object
1=\Tr(\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}})\rho)
is converted into a functional integral by insertion of compete sets of coherent states on both the left and the right of the density matrix, as discussed in
Diehl
. Instead, we consider the trace of the time evolution superoperator
\Tr(\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}))
. This trace can be expressed as the integral over the Hilbert-Schmidt operator norm of coherent states:

\Tr\big(\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}})\big)=\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\phi_{0}^{+}\text{d}^{2}\phi_{0}^{-}}{\pi^{2}}e^{-\bar{\phi}_{0}^{+}\phi_{0}^{+}-\bar{\phi}_{0}^{-}\phi_{0}^{-}}\tr\Big(\ket{\phi_{0}^{+}}\bra{\phi_{0}^{-}}^{\dagger}\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}})\ket{\phi_{0}^{+}}\bra{\phi_{0}^{-}}\Big).

(22)

The trace in right expression can be brought into the form of a path integral in a conventional way. The remaining insertions
\ket{\phi_{0}^{+}}\bra{\phi_{0}^{-}}
will set the boundary conditions at the beginning and end of the time contour. At this step, the integral in front of the operator trace in (
22
) can be absorbed into the path integral measure to an integral over the loop space of the Keldysh phase space:

\Tr\big(\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}})\big)=\int\limits_{\phi^{\pm}(-T/2)=\phi^{\pm}(T/2)}\text{D}\phi^{\pm}\text{D}\bar{\phi}^{\pm}e^{iS}.

(23)

The three character-valued partition functions
\Tr(\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{\pm}\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}))
and
\Tr(\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{+}\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{P}}}}_{-}\exp(T\hat{\vphantom{\rule{1.0pt}{5.71527pt}}\smash{\hat{\mathcal{L}}}}))
can be massaged into path integrals in the same way. The parity superoperators will act to the left on the tensor of coherent states, replacing
\phi_{0}^{\pm}
with
-\phi_{0}^{\pm}
on the left or on the right, changing the boundary conditions on the fields. The corresponding path integral must be taken over the space twisted loops on the Keldysh phase space. The boundary conditions corresponding to the different twisting superoperators are:

\mathcal{P}_{+}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \phi^{\pm}(-T/2)=\mp\phi^{\pm}(T/2)

(24a)

\mathcal{P}_{-}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \phi^{\pm}(-T/2)=\pm\phi^{\pm}(T/2)

(24b)

\mathcal{P}_{+}\mathcal{P}_{-}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \phi^{\pm}(-T/2)=-\phi^{\pm}(T/2)

(24c)

For the rescaled Keldysh-rotated fields
X
and
P
from (
10
), these become:

\mathcal{P}_{\pm}\ \longleftrightarrow\ X(-T/2)=\mp P(T/2),\ P(-T/2)=\mp X(T/2)

(25a)

\mathcal{P}_{+}\mathcal{P}_{-}\ \longleftrightarrow\ X(-T/2)=-X(T/2),\ P(-T/2)=-P(T/2)

(25b)

Either of the first two can be used to isolate the imaginary part of the smallest finite Lindbladian eigenvalue
\Lambda
; the third is used to isolate the real part.

III
Numerics

Numerical calculations were obtained by truncating and then vectorizing the operator Hilbert space. The Hilbert space was was truncated to finite dimension
N
; Boson creation/annihilation operators were replaced with
N\times N
matrices. Vectorization was done in the standard way by replacing simple tensor operators by vectors
\ket{\psi}\bra{\varphi}\to\ket{\psi}\otimes\ket{\varphi}
and superoperators by operators acting on different tensor factors
A\cdot B\to A\otimes B^{\mathrm{T}}
. After vectorization, the Lindbladian superoperator is transformed into an
N^{2}\times N^{2}
matrix. Its spectrum is obtained by exact diagonalization. All numerical data shown was obtained using
N=50
. Variation in the data was unnoticeable when going to larger values of
N
for the range of parameters studied in the main text, meaning the results shown are effectively independent of
N
. The low-lying part of the spectrum of the diagonalized truncated Lindbladian had two zero eigenvalues and two exponentially small eigenvalues; the plots in the main text show one of the two small eigenvalues.

Parameterisation of Reasoning on Temporal Markov Logic Networks
Victor David 1, Raphaël Fournier-S’niehotta 1, Nicolas Travers 1,2

Abstract
We aim at improving reasoning on inconsistent and uncertain data. We focus on knowledge-graph data, extended with time intervals to specify their validity, as regularly found in historical sciences. We propose principles on semantics for efficient Maximum A-Posteriori inference on the new Temporal Markov Logic Networks (TMLN) which extend the Markov Logic Networks (MLN) by uncertain temporal facts and rules. We examine total and partial temporal (in)consistency relations between sets of temporal formulae. Then we propose a new Temporal Parametric Semantics, which may combine several sub-functions, allowing to use different assessment strategies. Finally, we expose the constraints that semantics must respect to satisfy our principles.

Reasoning on large data sets to extend knowledge is an open challenge

,

,

]
.
Most approaches model information with

(KGs)

Then,

For historians, reasoning under uncertainty and inconsistency is at the basis of methodology: the validity of any fact remains questionable. Temporal information is crucial: outside of a temporal interval, a fact becomes false.
Using facts, temporal and uncertainty information, a historian may obtain several consistent levels of knowledge.

Recent works on KG reasoning do not focus on both time and uncertainty.
Several models with
Markov Logic Networks
(MLN) have been proposed

However, those representations cannot handle a fully uncertain universe where any fact or rule may be uncertain.

We propose to generalize this approach, by
i) introducing a new representation of knowledge, called
temporal MLN
(TMLN), built on (a temporal) many-sorted logic, for reasoning on both time and uncertainty,
ii) enlarging the notion of uncertainty to rules, and
iii) adapting reasoning, to deal with both uncertain facts and rules. We define a new temporal semantics and a temporal extension to
Maximum A-Posteriori
(MAP) inference
[
16
]

This MAP inference produces
instantiations

i.e.,

Finally, we generalise the temporal semantics by a Parametric Semantics, which may combine several sub-functions for various consistency validations.

Background

In a seminal work
[
4
]
, Chekol et al. formalise the UTKG approach, which integrates both time and uncertainty in Knowledge Graphs to reach a
certain world maximisation
.
However, they do not take into account the possibility to have uncertain rules.
We put

Secondly, our novel representation of TMLN allows for easier manipulations and better analyses.

2.1
Many-Sorted First Order Logic

We start by presenting what is a Many-Sorted First-Order Logic (introduced in
[

]

Definition 1
(Many-Sorted FOL)

Let
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{o}}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
be a set of sorts.
A
Many-Sorted First-Order Logic

\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
, is a set of formulae built up by induction from:
a set
{\mathbf{C}}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{l}\}
of constants,
a set
{\mathbf{V}}=\{x^{s},y^{s},z^{s},\ldots\mid s\in{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{o}}\}
of variables,
a set
{\mathbf{P}}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}\}
of predicates,
a function
\mathtt{ar}:{\mathbf{P}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
which tells the arity of any predicate,
a function
\mathtt{sort}
s.t. for
P\in{\mathbf{P}}
,
\mathtt{sort}(P)\in{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{o}}^{\mathtt{ar}(P)}
, and for
c\in{\mathbf{C}}
,
\mathtt{sort}(c)\in{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{o}}
,
the usual connectives (
\neg
,
\vee
,
\wedge
,
\rightarrow
,
\leftrightarrow
), Boolean constants (
\top
and
\bot
) and quantifier symbols (
\forall,\exists
).
A
ground formula
is a formula without any variable.

Lowercase (resp. uppercase) greek letters like
\phi,\psi
(resp.
\Phi,\Psi
) denote formulae (resp. sets of formulae).
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
is the set of all formulae.

Definition 3
(Interpretation)

An
interpretation
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}
over a structure
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}
assigns to elements of the
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
vocabulary some values in the structure
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}
. Formally,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(s_{i})=D_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
(each sort symbol is assigned to a domain),

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(P_{i})=R_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
(each predicate symbol is assigned to a relation),

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(a_{i})=c_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
(each constant symbol is assigned to a value).

Then, satisfying formulae is recursively defined by:

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models P_{i}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
iff
({\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(a_{1}),\dots,{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(a_{k}))\in R_{i}
,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\exists x^{s_{i}}\phi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}},x^{s_{i}}\leftarrow v}\models\phi
for some
v\in D_{i}
,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\forall x^{s_{i}}\phi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}},x^{s_{i}}\leftarrow v}\models\phi
for each
v\in D_{i}
,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi\wedge\psi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi
and
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\psi
,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi\vee\psi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi
or
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\psi
,

–
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\neg\phi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\not\models\phi
.

where
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}},x^{s_{i}}\leftarrow v}
is a modified version of
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}
s.t. the variable
x^{s_{i}}
is replaced by a value
v
in the domain
D_{i}
corresponding to the sort symbol
s_{i}
.
Finally, if
\Phi
is a set of formulae, then
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\Phi
iff
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi
for each
\phi\in\Phi
.

Definition
3
does not target the satisfaction of implications and equivalences, while they can be defined by:
(\phi\rightarrow\psi)\equiv(\neg\phi\vee\psi)
, and
(\phi\leftrightarrow\psi)\equiv(\phi\rightarrow\psi)\wedge(\psi\rightarrow\phi)
.
For instance, the set of interpretations of the formula
P(a)\vee P(b)
is equal to
\{\{P(a)\},\{P(b)\},\{P(a),P(b)\}\}
and for
P(a)\wedge P(b)
is
\{\{P(a),P(b)\}\}
.

With these notions of structures and interpretations on TMLNs, we may define the consequence relations and logical consequences over
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
formulae.

Definition 4
(Consequence Relation)

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be two
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
formulae. We say that
\psi
is a consequence of
\phi
, denoted by
\phi\vdash\psi
, if for any structure
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}
, and any interpretation
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}
over
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}
,
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\phi
implies
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}\models\psi
.

Definition 5
(Logical Consequences -
{\mathtt{Cn}}
)

Let
\phi\in\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
.
The function
{\mathtt{Cn}}(\phi)
is the set of all logical consequences of
\phi
, i.e.
{\mathtt{Cn}}(\phi)=\{\psi\in\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}\ |\ \phi\vdash\psi\}.

The function
{\mathtt{Cn}}
returns an infinite set of formulae, but for clarity we consider only one formula per equivalent class and only the predicates and constants appearing in the original formulae.
Such as,
{\mathtt{Cn}}(P(a)\vee P(b))=\{P(a)\vee P(b)\}
and

{\mathtt{Cn}}(P(a)\wedge P(b))=\{P(a),P(b),P(a)\wedge P(b),P(a)\vee P(b)\}\}
.

3
Temporal and Uncertain Knowledge Representation

Markov Logic Networks (MLNs) combine Markov Networks and First-Order Logic (FOL) by attaching weights to ﬁrst-order formulae and treating them as feature templates for Markov Networks
[
15
]
.
We extend this framework to temporal information by resorting to Many-Sorted First-Order Logic (
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
).

3.1
Temporal Markov Logic Networks

Let start by defining what we call a Temporal Many-Sorted First-Order Logic
\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
.

Definition 6
(Temporal Many-Sorted FOL)

A
\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
evaluated by a structure
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}
is a constrained
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
where
|{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{o}}|\geq 2
, for any interpretation
{\mathbf{I}}_{{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{t}}}(s_{1})=Time
, any predicate
P_{i}\in\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
has
\mathtt{ar}(P_{i})\geq 3
with the sort of the last two parameters belonging to
s_{1}
and
t_{min}
and
t_{max}
are time constants indicating the minimum and maximum time points for any pre-order between the time constants.

Using this constrained
\mathtt{MS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
accompanied with a temporal domain (named
Time

Finally, Temporal Markov Logic Networks (TMLN) extend
\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
(resp. Markov Logic Networks) by associating a degree of certainty to each formula (resp. by adding a temporal validity to the predicates).

(TMLN)

A Temporal Markov Logic Network

-
\mathtt{FOL}

…

\forall i\in\{1,
…
,n\}

\phi_{i}\in\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
such that it is a ground formula and
w_{i}\in[0,1]
,

–
{\mathbf{R}}=\{(\phi^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{1}),
…
,(\phi^{\prime}_{k},w^{\prime}_{k})\}
with
\forall i\in\{1,
…
,k\}
,
\phi^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
such that it is not a ground formula and in the form (premises,conclusion), i.e.,
(\psi_{1}\wedge
…
\wedge\psi_{l})\rightarrow\psi_{l+1}
where
\forall j\in\{1,
…
,l+1\}
,
\psi_{j}\in\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}
, and
w_{i}\in[0,1]
.

The universe of all TMLNs is denoted by

Let

\mathtt{MI}({\mathbf{M}})

Formally, to define the set of instantiations, we have to define two useful notions. Firstly, we denote by

Secondly, we define the function

\mathtt{W}(\phi,{\mathbf{M}})=\mathtt{max}(\mathtt{min_{w}}({\mathbf{M}}_{1}),\ldots,\linebreak\mathtt{min_{w}}({\mathbf{M}}_{m}))

\mathtt{min_{w}}({\mathbf{M}}_{i}=\{(\psi_{1},w_{1}),\ldots,(\psi_{l},w_{l})\})=\mathtt{min}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{l})

4
Temporal and Uncertain Knowledge Reasoning

Computing the MAP inference means “finding the most probable state of the world”
[
4
]
. In order to do that, we integrate semantics on TMLN, before examining principles governing those semantics and (in)consistency relations.

4.1
Temporal MAP Inference

Semantics

are methods
which compute the strength of a TMLN state. We denote the universe of all semantics by
\mathtt{Sem}
, such that for any
{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathtt{Sem}
,

{\mathcal{S}}:\mathtt{TMLN^{*}}\rightarrow[0,+\infty[.

We compute a strength above 0, instead of a probability between 0 and 1.
One semantics may maximise the amount of information, while another may maximise the quality.

Temporal Maximum A-Posteriori (MAP) Inference

in TMLN returns the most probable, temporally consistent, and expanded state
w.r.t.
a given semantics.
Given
{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathtt{TMLN}
and
{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathtt{Sem}
, a method solving a MAP problem is denoted by:

\mathtt{map}:\mathtt{TMLN}\times\mathtt{Sem}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(\mathtt{TMLN^{*}})
, where
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
denote the powerset of X, such that:

\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})=\{I\mid I\in\underset{I~\subseteq~\mathtt{MI}({\mathbf{M}})}{\text{argmax}}~{\mathcal{S}}(I)\text{ and }\nexists I^{\prime}\in\underset{I^{\prime}~\subseteq~\mathtt{MI}({\mathbf{M}})}{\text{argmax}}~{\mathcal{S}}(I^{\prime})\text{ s.t. }I\subset I^{\prime}\}
.

To determine a MAP inference we need to define semantics, however not all methods are desirable. Below we present some principles that a semantics should satisfy.

4.2
Principles for semantics in Temporal MAP Inference

Our first principle states that adding new weightless information does not change the strength of the MAPs of a TMLN, whatever the temporality is.
Given that the same set of predicates or formulae instantiated on different temporalities are not equivalent, we homogenises
\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}

Principle 1
(Temporal Neutrality)

Let
{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathtt{TMLN}
,
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{M}}\cup\{(\phi,w)\}

\phi\in\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL}

–
\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\mathtt{TF}({\mathbf{M}}))\not\vdash\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\{\phi\})

–
w=0

A semantics

\forall I\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})
and
\forall I^{\prime}\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{S}})

{\mathcal{S}}(I)={\mathcal{S}}(I^{\prime})

The next principle ensures that one cannot decrease the strength of the MAPs of a TMLN by adding new and consistent information.
The temporal consistency
\mathtt{Con}:{\mathcal{P}}(\mathtt{TS}
-
\mathtt{FOL})\rightarrow\{\top,\bot\}
denotes a relation of consistence for a set of temporal formulae.

Principle 2
(Consistency Monotony)

Let a relation of consistence
\mathtt{Con}
,
{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathtt{TMLN}
and
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{M}}\cup\{(\phi,w)\}
where
(\phi,w)
is a weighted temporal formula such that:

–
\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\mathtt{TF}({\mathbf{M}}))\not\vdash\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\{\phi\})

–
\forall I\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})

\mathtt{Con}(\{\phi\}\cup\mathtt{TF}(I))
is true and
I\subset\mathtt{MI}(\{(\phi,w)\}\cup I)
.

A semantics
{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathtt{Sem}
satisfies consistency monotony iff:

\forall I\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})
and
\forall I^{\prime}\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{S}})

{\mathcal{S}}(I)\leq{\mathcal{S}}(I^{\prime})
.

The last principle states that if we add a new temporal fact to a TMLN such that it is consistent with each instantiation, then the fact will be present in each new instantiation.

Principle 3
(Invariant Consistent Facts)

Let a relation of consistence
\mathtt{Con}
,
{\mathbf{M}}\in\mathtt{TMLN}
and
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{M}}\cup\{(\phi,w)\}
where
(\phi,w)
is a TMLN fact such that:

–
\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\mathtt{TF}({\mathbf{M}}))\not\vdash\scalebox{1.2}{$\tau$}(\{\phi\})
, and

–
\forall I\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})
,
\mathtt{Con}(\{\phi\}\cup\mathtt{TF}(I))
is true.

A semantics
{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathtt{Sem}
satisfies invariant consistent facts iff,

\forall I\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}},{\mathcal{S}})
,
I\cup\{(\phi,w)\}\in\mathtt{map}({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{S}})
.

5
Conclusion

Reasoning on Knowledge Graphs have recently been approached with MLNs, to find the most probable state of the world. However, they were limited to a strict temporal inconsistency.
We extend UTKGs with a fully-formalized model of TMLN, capable of combining consistency and inconsistency for facts and rules, allowing to reason with the MAP inference using new principles and relations between partial and total aspects of temporal (in-)consistency.
Moreover, we introduce temporal parametric semantics which offer flexibility to tailor semantics reasoning to one’s needs.

As a perspective, we first wish to extend this work to existential rules in order to capture more real data (see Section
3.2
).
Secondly, some relationships between formulae in a TMLN are not yet exploited and could be represented with argumentation graphs (
e.g.,
the notion of support
[
6
]
or similarity between pieces of information
[
1
,
2
]
), to enhance the weight of inferred knowledge.
Thirdly, as we can see in Table
3
, each parameter of the
\mathtt{TPS}
have an impact on the temporal MAP inferences and its possible conclusions. It will be useful to investigate some properties to describe the strategies and specific behaviours of the parametrisation functions.
Finally, implementing our results and confronting them with real data will likely prove very useful, at least to generate new research hypotheses in historical science.

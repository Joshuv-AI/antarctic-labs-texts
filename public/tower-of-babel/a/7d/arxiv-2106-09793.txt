Abstract
. We establish necessary or sufficient conditions to guarantee that skew Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt extensions are NI or NJ rings. Our results extend those corresponding for skew polynomial rings and establish similar properties for other families of noncommutative rings such as universal enveloping algebras and examples of differential operators.

1.
Introduction

Several kinds of rings are defined in terms of their set of nilpotent elements. For example, a ring
R
is called
NI
if its set
N(R)
of nilpotent elements coincides with its upper radical
N^{*}(R)

(the sum of all its nil ideals of
R
). If
N(R)=J(R)
, where
J(R)
is the Jacobson radical of
R
, then
R
is called
NJ
.
R
is said to be
2-primal
if
N(R)
is equal to the prime radical
N_{*}(R)
of
R
(the intersection of all prime ideals of
R
).
R
is called
weakly 2-primal
if
N(R)
coincides with its Levitzki
radical
L(R)
(the sum of all locally nilpotent ideals of
R
).

The NI and NJ rings have recently been investigated by several authors. For instance, Hwang et al.
[
16
]
studied the structure of NI rings
related to strongly prime ideals and showed that minimal strongly
prime ideals can be lifted in NI rings (
[
16
]
, Theorem 2.3). They proved that for an NI ring
R
,
R
is
weakly
pm
(every strongly prime ideal of
R
is contained in a unique maximal ideal of
R
) if and only if the topological space of maximal ideals of
R
is a retract of the topological space of strongly prime ideals of
R
, or equivalently, if the topological space of strongly prime ideals of
R
is normal (
[
16
]
, Theorem 3.7). Also, they proved that
R
is weakly pm if and only if
R
is
pm
(every prime ideal of
R
is contained in a unique maximal ideal of
R
) when
R
is a symmetric ring (that is,
rst=0
implies
rts=0
, where
r,s,t\in R
) (
[
16
]
, Theorem
3.8).

Concerning skew polynomial rings (also known as Ore extensions) introduced by Ore
[
35

[

studied the Jacobson radical of skew polynomial rings of

is an algebra with no

[

[

gave necessary and sufficient conditions for a

has no nonzero nil ideals (

is a
\delta

[

[

Later, Nasr-Isfahani

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, the term ring means an associative ring with
identity not necessarily commutative. For a ring
R
, as we saw above, we fix the following notation:
N_{*}(R)
is its
prime radical
,
N^{*}(R)
is its
upper radical
,

N(R)
is its
set of nilpotent elements
,
J(R)
is its
Jacobson radical
, and
L(R)
is its
Levitzki radical
. It is well-known that the following relations hold:
N_{*}(R)\subseteq L(R)\subseteq N^{*}(R)\subseteq N(R)
and
N_{*}(R)\subseteq L(R)\subseteq N^{*}(R)\subseteq J(R)
(see
[
19
]
,
[
30
]
or
[
31
]
, for more details).

R
is called
nil-semisimple
if it has no nonzero nil ideals. Recall that nil-semisimple rings are semiprime (
[
16
]
, p. 187). If
P
is a prime ideal of
R
,
P
is called
completely prime
if
R/P
is a domain, and
P
is said to be
strongly prime
if
R/P

is nil-semisimple. Note that maximal ideals and completely prime ideals are strongly prime; any
strongly prime ideal contains a minimal strongly prime ideal;
N^{*}(R)
is the unique maximal nil ideal of
R
;
N^{*}(R)=\{a\in R\mid RaR\text{ is a nil ideal of }R\}=\bigcap\{P\mid P\text{
is a strongly prime ideal of }R\}
(see
[
16
]
or
[
19
]
for more details).

For a ring
R
, 2-primal implies
weakly 2-primal, whence NI and NJ are examples of NI rings.
R
is
reduced (i.e., without nonzero nilpotent elements) if and only if
R
is nil-semisimple and NI, or equivalently,
R
is semiprime and 2-primal (
[
16
]
, p. 187). As we can check,
R
is 2-primal if and only if
N_{*}(R)=N^{*}(R)=N(R)
. Shin
[
44
]
, Proposition 1.11, proved that the set of nilpotent elements of a ring
R
coincides with the prime radical
if and only if every minimal prime ideal of
R
is completely prime. Thus,
R
is 2-primal if and only if every minimal prime ideal of

R
is completely prime, or equivalently,
R/N_{*}(R)
is reduced.

A ring
R
is said to be
semicommutative
if for every pair of
elements
a,b\in R
, we have that
ab=0
implies
aRb=0
. Shin
[
44
]
, Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 1.5, established that semicommutative rings are 2-primal, and hence semi-commutative rings are NI. Note that domains are reduced rings, reduced rings are symmetric, symmetric rings are reversible (
R
is said to be
reversible
if
ab=0
implies
ba=0
, where
a,b\in R
), and reversible rings are semicommutative, but the converses are not true in general (see
[
30
]
). A ring is called

right
(left)
duo
if every right (left) ideal of it is
two-sided. Shin
[
44
]
, Lemma 1.2, showed that right (left) duo rings are semicommutative. Therefore, NI rings contain several families of rings such as domains, reduced rings, symmetric rings, semi-commutative rings, reversible rings, one-sided duo rings, 2-primal rings and NJ rings (for more details, see
[
29
]
). Köthe’s
conjecture (the upper nilradical contains every nil left
ideal) holds for NI rings (Hwang et al.,
[
16
]
, p. 192).

Equivalent definitions for NI rings are presented in Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
16
]
, Lemma 2.1)
.

For a ring
R
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

R
is NI.

(ii)

N(R)
is an ideal.

(iii)

Every subring (possibly without identity) of
R
is NI.

(iv)

Every minimal strongly
prime ideal of
R
is completely prime.

(v)

R/N^{*}(R)
is a reduced ring.

(vi)

R/N^{*}(R)
is a symmetric ring.

Some examples of NJ rings are nil rings, division rings, Boolean
rings, commutative Jacobson rings, commutative affine algebras over a field
\mathbb{K}
, semi-Abelian
\pi
-regular rings, locally finite Abelian rings (Jiang et al.,
[
17
]
, Example 2.5). Every reduced ring is NJ (
[
17
]
, Proposition
2.11). Note that
R=\mathbb{Z}[[x]]
is a domain and
hence NI, with
N(R)=\{0\}
, but
J(\mathbb{Z}[[x]])=x\mathbb{Z}[[x]]\neq\{0\}
and so

is not
an NJ ring (
[
17
]
, Example 2.2). This example shows that NI are not included in NJ rings.

(

.

Let

(iv)

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

From Definition
2.2
(iv), it is clear that skew PBW extensions are more general than iterated skew polynomial rings (cf.
[
9
]
). For example, universal enveloping algebras of finite dimensional Lie algebras and some 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebras in the sense of Bell and Smith
[
4
]
(see also Rosenberg
[
43
]
) cannot be expressed as iterated skew polynomial rings but are skew PBW extensions. For quasi-commutative skew PBW extensions, these are isomorphic to iterated Ore extensions of endomorphism type (
[
26
]
, Theorem 2.3).

(ii)

Skew PBW extensions of endomorphism type are more general than iterated Ore extensions of endomorphism type. Let us illustrate the situation with two and three indeterminates.

For the iterated Ore extension of endomorphism type
R[x;\sigma_{x}][y;\sigma_{y}]
, if
r\in R
then we have the following relations:
xr=\sigma_{x}(r)x
,
yr=\sigma_{y}(r)y
, and
yx=\sigma_{y}(x)y
. Now, if we have
\sigma(R)\langle x,y\rangle
a skew PBW extension of endomorphism type over
R
, then for any
r\in R
, Definition
2.2
establishes that
xr=\sigma_{1}(r)x
,
yr=\sigma_{2}(r)y
, and
yx=d_{1,2}xy+r_{0}+r_{1}x+r_{2}y
, for some elements
d_{1,2},r_{0},r_{1}
and
r_{2}
belong to
R
. From these relations it is clear which one of them is more general.

If we have the iterated Ore extension
R[x;\sigma_{x}][y;\sigma_{y}][z;\sigma_{z}]
, then for any
r\in R
,
xr=\sigma_{x}(r)x
,
yr=\sigma_{y}(r)y
,
zr=\sigma_{z}(r)z
,
yx=\sigma_{y}(x)y
,
zx=\sigma_{z}(x)z
,
zy=\sigma_{z}(y)z
. For the skew PBW extension of endomorphism type
\sigma(R)\langle x,y,z\rangle
,
xr=\sigma_{1}(r)x
,
yr=\sigma_{2}(r)y
,
zr=\sigma_{3}(r)z
,
yx=d_{1,2}xy+r_{0}+r_{1}x+r_{2}y+r_{3}z
,
zx=d_{1,3}xz+r_{0}^{\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime}x+r_{2}^{\prime}y+r_{3}^{\prime}z
, and
zy=d_{2,3}yz+r_{0}^{\prime\prime}+r_{1}^{\prime\prime}x+r_{2}^{\prime\prime}y+r_{3}^{\prime\prime}z
, for some elements
d_{1,2},d_{1,3},d_{2,3},r_{0},r_{0}^{\prime},r_{0}^{\prime\prime},r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime},r_{1}^{\prime\prime},r_{2},r_{2}^{\prime},r_{2}^{\prime\prime},r_{3}
,
r_{3}^{\prime},r_{3}^{\prime\prime}
of
R
. As the number of indeterminates increases, the differences between both algebraic structures are more remarkable.

(iii)

From Definition

\mathbb{N}
-graded ring in a non-trivial sense. With this in mind, Proposition

-graded rings, then a map

. For

.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
45
]
, Proposition

.

Let

be a bijective skew PBW extension over an

-graded algebra

. If the
following conditions hold:

is a graded ring homomorphism and
\delta_{i}:R(-1)\to R

-derivation, for all

-graded algebra with graduation given by

-space generated by the set

\Bigl\{r_{t}x^{\alpha}\mid t+|\alpha|=p,\ r_{t}\in R_{t}\text{ and }x^{\alpha}\in{\rm Mon}(A)\Bigr\}.

Definition 2.6

(
[
45
]
, Definition 2.6)

Let

We present some remarkable examples of graded skew PBW extensions.

Example 2.7

The Jordan plane, homogenized enveloping algebras, and some classes of diffusion algebras (

3.

This section contains the original results of the paper about NI property for skew PBW extensions. We start with Proposition
3.1
which follows directly from
[
28
]
, Theorem 3.9, and
[
42
]
, Proposition 4.4. Recall that a ring

is

if every finite subset in

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a skew PBW
extension over

. If

satisfies one of the following conditions,

(i)

is 2-primal and
(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible, or

(ii)

is locally finite,

-compatible and

Proposition 3.2
.

If

is an NI skew PBW

, then

is an NI ring,

,

, and therefore

Proof.

From Definition

(i) we know that

is a
subring of

. Since

is NI, Proposition

implies that

is NI. Now, since the Jacobson

we have that

and

are nil ideals of

and

, respectively. Hence,

and

. As

.
∎

From Propositions

and

we deduce that if

is

-compatible and

is NI. This result has been proved by Reyes and Suárez

, Theorem

Since weak

-compatible rings are more general than

-compatible rings, and NI rings are more general than 2-primal rings, the following theorem generalizes
[

, Theorem 4.11, and some other results of

]
and

Theorem 3.3
.

If

is a skew PBW extension over a weak

-compatible ring

is NI if and only if

is NI.

Proof.

Suppose that

is an NI ring.
Let us first see that

is a

-invariant. By Proposition

,

is an ideal of

. For a fixed

\sigma_{i}(r^{k})=\sigma_{i}(r)^{k}=0

. Thus

\sigma_{i}(N(R))\subseteq N(R)

is weak

(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible, whence

-invariant ideal or

Let us show that

, Theorem 4.6,
f=\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}X_{i}\in N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle

.
Therefore,

is an ideal of

, and by Proposition

is an NI ring.

Conversely, if

is an NI ring, then by Proposition
3.2

we have that

is an NI ring.
∎

If

is a skew PBW extension over a
(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible ring

, then

is
\Sigma
-rigid. Indeed, for

For the next result, recall that a ring

Proposition 3.4
.

If

is an NI skew PBW extension over

(i)

and

are completely semiprime.

(ii)

(i)

Since

is an ideal
of

Remark 3.5

is a
\Sigma
-ideal of

.

With the aim of showing that

N(A)=N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, before consider the following facts.

Note that
N(R)
is

\Delta
-invariant; indeed, if

, then

. Since
N(R)
is a
\Sigma
-ideal, then
\sigma_{i}(r)\in N(R)\subseteq N(A)
, and so

\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}\in N(A)
, which implies that
\delta_{i}(r)\in N(A)
, that
is,
\delta_{i}(r)\in N(R)
. By
[
22
]
, Proposition 2.2 (i), the system of endomorphisms and

\Sigma
-derivations
(\Sigma,\Delta)
induces over
R/N(R)
a system
(\overline{\Sigma},\overline{\Delta})
of endomorphisms and
\Sigma
-derivations defined by

\overline{\sigma}_{i}(\overline{r}):=\overline{\sigma_{i}(r)}
and

\overline{\delta}_{i}(\overline{r}):=\overline{\delta_{i}(r)}
,
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
N(R)
is proper,
[
22
]
, Proposition
2.6 (ii), implies that
A/N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
is a skew PBW extension over
R/N(R)
. Note that
R/N(R)

is a
\overline{\Sigma}
-rigid ring, since if

\overline{r}\overline{\sigma}^{\alpha}(\overline{r})=0
, then

\overline{r\sigma^{\alpha}(r)}=0
, and so
r\sigma_{i}(r)\in N(R)
. Having in mind that
N(R)
is

\Sigma
-rigid,
r\in N(R)
and so
\overline{r}=0
. By
[
38
]
, Theorem 4.4,
A/N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
is a reduced ring (cf.
[
7
]
, Theorem 6.1.9).

Let us see that
N(A)\subseteq N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. If
f\in N(A)
, then

, for some positive

. Thus
\overline{f^{k}}=\overline{f}^{k}=0

in

, and so

. Hence

, that is,

.

For the another inclusion, if

, where
r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{t}\in N(R)\subseteq N(A)
, since

is an ideal of

, then
r_{0},r_{1}X_{1},\dots,r_{t}X_{t}\in N(A)
, and
therefore
f=r_{0}+r_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+r_{t}X_{t}\in N(A)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
r\in N(R)\subseteq N(A)
. Then
rx_{i}\in N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, for
1\leq i\leq n
, whence
x_{i}r=\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}+\delta_{i}(r)\in N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle=N(A)
. By assumption
N(R)
is a
\Sigma
-ideal, so
\sigma_{i}(r)\in N(R)
and thus
\delta_{i}(r)\in N(R)
, i.e.,

N(R)
is
\Delta
-invariant. Since
N(R)
is proper,

[
22
]
, Proposition 2.6 (i), implies that

is an ideal of
A
and
AN(R)\subseteq N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. Using that
N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle=N(A)
, we obtain that

is an ideal
of
A
, and hence

is NI.

To show that

is

\Sigma
-rigid it is enough to see that for

and

,

implies that

. If for

,

,

, then

,

, and so
\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}r=\sigma_{i}(r)\sigma_{i}(r)x_{i}+\sigma_{i}(r)\delta_{i}(r)\in N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle

is
\Sigma
-ideal. Then

.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) If

, and by

,

. From implication (i)

, and so
N^{*}(A)=N(A)=N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle=N^{*}(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Suppose that

is a
\Sigma
-rigid ideal of

. By the same argument as in the proof of (i)
\Rightarrow

is a
\Sigma
-ideal of

. If

, then

, and since

then

, for

, which shows that

is
\Delta
-invariant. Using the same argument as in

. By assumption

, whence

.
∎

4.
NJ skew PBW extensions

In this section, we present the original results of the paper about NJ property for skew PBW extensions.

We start with the following assertion that follows directly from definitions of NI and NJ rings.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
A=\sigma(R)\langle x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rangle
is an NJ skew PBW extension over a ring
R
, then
R
is NI and
J(A)\cap R=N(R)
.

From
[
7
]
, Propositions 3.2.1 and 3.2.3, it follows the next result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Skew PBW extensions over a domain
R
are NJ rings, and hence, NI rings.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
A
is a graded skew PBW extension over
R=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}R_{n}
, then
A
is NJ if and only if
A
is NI and

J(A)\cap R_{0}
is a nil ideal.

Proof.

Suppose that
A
is NI and
J(A)\cap R_{0}
is nil. By
[
45
]
, Remark
2.10 (i),
R_{0}=A_{0}
, whence
J(A)\cap R_{0}=J(A)\cap A_{0}
is nil. From
[
32
]
, Theorem 2.4,
J(A)=N(A)
, i.e.,
A
is an NJ ring.

Conversely, if
A
is an NJ ring, then
A
is NI. Since
R_{0}
is a subring,
J(A)\cap R_{0}
is an
ideal, and as
A
is an NJ ring,
J(A)=N(A)
. In this way,
J(A)\cap R_{0}\subseteq J(A)=N(A)
, and hence
J(A)\cap R_{0}
is a nil ideal.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If
A
is a graded skew PBW extension over a connected algebra
R=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}R_{n}
, then
A
is NJ if and only if
A

is NI.

Proof.

Since
R
is connected,
[
45
]
, Remark 2.10 (i), implies that
A_{0}=R_{0}=\mathbb{K}
, whence
J(A)\cap A_{0}=\{0\}
is nil. The result follows from Theorem

4.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
A
be a quasi-commutative skew PBW extension over a ring
R
. Then
A
is NJ if and only if
A
is NI and
J(A)\cap R
is a nil
ideal of
R
.

Proof.

From
[
46
]
, Proposition 2.6, we know that quasi-commutative skew PBW extensions where
R
has the the trivial graduation are graded skew PBW extensions, so
[
45
]
, Remark 2.10
(i), guarantees that
A_{0}=R_{0}=R
. Thus, the assertion follows from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If
A
is an NJ quasi-commutative skew PBW extension over a ring
R

and
J(R)\subseteq J(A)
then
R
is NJ.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
we have that
J(A)\cap R=N(R)
. Since
J(R)\subseteq J(A)
then
J(R)\subseteq N(R)
. Since

A
is NJ then it is NI, so by Proposition

3.2
we have that
N(R)\subseteq J(R)
.
Therefore
R
is NJ.
∎

The next theorem presents similar results to
[

]
, Theorem 3.10 (1).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

is a weakly 2-primal

Since

for

. Thus

is weak

\Delta
-compatible. If

is weakly 2-primal then it is NI, and by Theorem

,

is NI. Let us show that

is nil. If

, then

. From
[

]
, Proposition
2.6,

is a graded skew PBW extension with the trivial graduation of

, and so

is a homogeneous component of

with degree 1. By
[

]
, Lemma 2.3
(2),

. In this way,
[
41
]
, Theorem 4.6, implies that

. The assertion follows from
Corollary
4.5
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let
A=\sigma(R)\langle x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\rangle
be a skew PBW extension over a ring

.

(i)

If

is 2-primal and

-skew Armendariz,

, Proposition 4.9, implies that

[

]
, Proposition 2.5, guarantees that

(iii)

shows that

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
A=\sigma(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
be a skew PBW
extension of derivation type.

(i)

If
A
is NI and
R
is
\Delta
-compatible and right duo, then

is
NJ.

(ii)

is NI and

if and only if

is NJ, where

is
the set of all coefficients of all terms of all polynomials of

Proof.

(i) If
A
is NI then

. For the other

. Since

is
(\Sigma,\Delta)
-compatible and right duo,
[

]
, Theorem 4.7, guarantees that

. So,

. As

is NI, Proposition
3.7
(iv) implies that

, that is,

.

(ii) Suppose that
A
is NI and

. By
Proposition
3.7
(iv),
N(A)=N(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. If

, then by hypothesis

, for

, whence

, for every

. Since
A
is NI, then
N(A)
is an ideal of

, and hence

.

For the converse,
suppose that
A
is NJ. Then
A
is NI, and so Proposition

3.7
(iv) implies that

, then for some

such that

. Since

, it follows that

.
∎

.

is NI if and only if

By Proposition
4.9

5.
Future work

In this article, we have established necessary or sufficient conditions to ensure that skew PBW extensions are either NI or NJ rings. Now, since the notion of weak compatibility introduced in
[
41
]
is more general than the notion of compatibility defined in
[
12
]
and
[
39
]
, a first task is to extend the results obtained here that used the compatibility condition, now assuming the weak compatibility condition.

On the other hand, since skew PBW extensions are part of a more general family of noncommutative algebraic structures such as the
semi-graded rings
introduced by Lezama and Latorre
[
25
]
, which have been studied recently from the point of view of noncommutative algebraic geometry (topics such as Hilbert series and Hilbert polynomial, generalized Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, non-commutative schemes, and Serre-Artin-Zhang-Verevkin theorem, see Lezama et al.,
[

and
[
24
]
), and having in mind that semi-graded rings are more general than
\mathbb{N}
-graded rings (of course, we mean rings that do not admit a non-trivial
\mathbb{N}
-graduation), the natural task is to investigate NI and NJ properties for semi-graded rings with the aim of extending results established by Nasr-Isfahani
[
32
]
in the context of graded rings.

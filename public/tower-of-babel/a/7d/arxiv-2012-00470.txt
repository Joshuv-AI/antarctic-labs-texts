Improved Performance Guarantees for Orthogonal Group Synchronization via Generalized Power Meth
Shuyang Ling

Abstract
Given the noisy pairwise measurements among a set of unknown group elements, how to recover them efficiently and robustly? This problem, known as group synchronization, has drawn tremendous attention in the scientific community. In this work, we focus on orthogonal group synchronization that has found many applications, including computer vision, robotics, and cryo-electron microscopy. One commonly used approach is the least squares estimation that requires solving a highly nonconvex optimization program. The past few years have witnessed considerable advances in tackling this challenging problem by convex relaxation and efficient first-order methods. However, one fundamental theoretical question remains to be answered: how does the recovery performance depend on the noise strength? To answer this question, we study a benchmark model: recovering orthogonal group elements from their pairwise measurements corrupted by additive Gaussian noise. We investigate the performance of convex relaxation and the generalized power method (GPM). By applying the novel leave-one-out technique, we prove that the GPM with spectral initialization enjoys linear convergence to the global optima to the convex relaxation that also matches the maximum likelihood estimator. Our result achieves a near-optimal performance bound on the convergence of the GPM and improves the state-of-the-art theoretical guarantees on the tightness of convex relaxation by a large margin.

Group synchronization aims to recover group elements

g_{ij}=g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}+\text{noise},\quad(i,j)\in{\cal E}

However, it is often highly challenging to solve the group synchronization problem in the presence of noise. When the noise exists, sequential recovery no longer works as it would amplify the noise.

In this work, we are interested in studying how to recover orthogonal group elements
\bm{G}_{i}\in\Od(d)
from the pairwise measurements corrupted by Gaussian noise,

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\bm{G}_{i}\bm{G}_{j}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}_{ij},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Od(d)

\displaystyle:=\{\bm{O}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}:\bm{O}\bm{O}^{\top}=\bm{O}^{\top}\bm{O}=\bm{I}_{d}\},

Why is (

Orthogonal group is a generalization of many useful groups such as
\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{2}:=\{1,-1\}
and unitary group
U(1):=\{e^{\mi\theta}:\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}
, and contains permutation groups and special orthogonal group as subgroups. Moreover, the

[

Given this statistical model (
1.1
), one common approach is maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) which is equivalent to finding the least squares estimator. Minimizing the least squares cost function is a potentially NP-hard problem in general since it includes graph max-cut
[
29
]
as a special case
d=1
. On the other hand, many numerical simulations and previous theoretical works indicate that despite its seeming NP-hardness, the
\Od(d)
synchronization is solvable with an efficient algorithm if noise level is relatively low. The focus of this paper is analyzing the performance of semidefinite relaxation and generalized power method in solving (
1.1
). More precisely, we attempt to answer two questions:

When does convex relaxation recover the least squares estimator?

The first question concerns tightness: the solution to the relaxed convex program is not necessarily equal to the least squares estimator in general. When are they the same?

Can we design an efficient algorithm to solve this orthogonal group synchronization?

The second question is about efficient recovery: in practice, we do not often use the SDP (semidefinite programming) relaxation due to its expensive computational costs. As a result, finding an efficient algorithm with global convergence will be very favorable. In particular, we will study the convergence of generalized power method applied to the
\Od(d)
synchronization
[
9
,
40
,
41
,
63

The answers to both questions are closely related to the noise strength, namely
\sigma

\sigma

2
Preliminaries

Recall the orthogonal group synchronization under Gaussian noise:

\bm{A}_{ij}=\bm{G}_{i}\bm{G}_{j}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}_{ij}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}

where
\bm{G}_{i}\in\Od(d)
and
\bm{W}_{ij}
is a
d\times d
Gaussian random matrix.
Let
\bm{G}^{\top}=[\bm{G}^{\top}_{1},\cdots,\bm{G}^{\top}_{n}]\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times nd}
. Then we write the model into a more convenient block matrix form

\bm{A}=\bm{G}\bm{G}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W},\quad\bm{W}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}

(2.1)

where
\bm{W}=[\bm{W}_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is an
nd\times nd
symmetric Gaussian random matrix whose entries are i.i.d. standard normal.
The task is to recover the group elements from their pairwise measurements
\bm{A}_{ij}
.

Among many existing approaches for
\Od(d)
synchronization, one popular approach is maximum likelihood estimation (MLE). Under Gaussian noise, the likelihood function is equivalent to the least squares cost function:

\min_{\bm{R}_{i}\in\Od(d)}\sum_{i<j}\|\bm{R}_{i}\bm{R}_{j}^{\top}-\bm{A}_{ij}\|_{F}^{2}

which is equivalent to

\max_{\bm{R}_{i}\in\Od(d)}\sum_{i<j}\langle\bm{R}_{i}\bm{R}_{j}^{\top},\bm{A}_{ij}\rangle.

(2.2)

Directly maximizing (
2.2
) is not an easy task since (
2.2
) is a highly nonconvex optimization program and potentially NP-hard. Convex relaxation is a powerful approach to overcome the nonconvexity. Let
\bm{R}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
be a matrix of size
nd\times d
whose
i
th block is
\bm{R}_{i}.
Note that every element in
\{\bm{R}\bm{R}^{\top}:\bm{R}_{i}\in\Od(d)\}
is positive semidefinite and its diagonal block equals
\bm{I}_{d}.
The convex relaxation is obtained by “lifting” the feasible set:

\max~\langle\bm{A},\bm{X}\rangle\quad\text{ such that }\quad\bm{X}\succeq 0,~\bm{X}_{ii}=\bm{I}_{d}.

(2.3)

This semidefinite relaxation is a generalization of Goemans-Williamson relaxation
[
29
]
for graph max-cut. The global maximizer of (
2.3
) can be recovered by using a standard convex program solver
[
30
,
44
]
. However, due to the relaxation, it is likely that the solution to (
2.3
) does not match that to (
2.2
). Therefore, one major theoretical question is the tightness, i.e., when (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) share the same global maximizer. More generally, we are interested in when (
2.2
) is solvable by an algorithm with polynomial time complexity.
The answer depends on the value of
\sigma
: for (
2.2
) with small
\sigma
, it is more likely to obtain the optimal solution.

Our discussion focuses on answering two questions: (a) when is the SDP relaxation tight? How does the tightness depend on
\sigma
?; (b) if the SDP is tight, does there exist an efficient algorithm to recover the optimal solution to (
2.3
) and (
2.2
)? Now we introduce the generalized power method: the algorithm consists of a two-step procedure widely employed in nonconvex optimization literature
[
14
,
42
,
63
]
: first we find a good initialization by using spectral method and then show the global convergence of the generalized power method. Before proceeding to the algorithm, we give a few useful definitions.

Definition 2.1

(Generalized “phase”)
.

The generalized “phase” of a matrix
\bm{\Psi}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
is defined as

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Psi}):=\bm{U}\bm{V}^{\top}+\bm{U}_{\perp}\bm{V}_{\perp}^{\top},\qquad\bm{\Psi}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top},

(2.4)

where
\bm{\Psi}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}
is the compact SVD of
\bm{\Psi}
, i.e.,
\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U}=\bm{V}^{\top}\bm{V}=\bm{I}_{r}
and
r=\rank(\bm{\Psi})
. Here
\bm{U}_{\perp}
and
\bm{V}_{\perp}
are
d\times(d-r)
matrices satisfying
\bm{U}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{U}=\bm{V}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{V}=0
and
\bm{U}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{U}_{\perp}=\bm{V}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{V}_{\perp}=\bm{I}_{d-r}
.

For a block matrix
\bm{X}^{\top}=[\bm{X}_{1}^{\top},\cdots,\bm{X}_{n}^{\top}]\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times nd}
with
\bm{X}_{i}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
, we define
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\cdot)
as an operator
\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}\rightarrow\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
:

\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{X}):=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{P}(\bm{X}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{P}(\bm{X}_{n})\end{bmatrix}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}.

The operator
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
is also known as the
matrix sign function

[
2
,
31
]
.
Indeed,
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Psi})
is not unique because there are multiple choices of
\bm{U}_{\perp}
and
\bm{V}_{\perp}
if
\bm{\Psi}
is not full rank. Therefore, it is more reasonable to treat
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
as a set-valued function and

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Psi})
outputs one representative from the set

\{\bm{U}\bm{V}^{\top}+\bm{U}_{\perp}\bm{V}_{\perp}^{\top}:\bm{U}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{U}=\bm{V}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{V}=0\},\qquad\bm{\Psi}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top},

which is a subset of
\Od(d)
.
With a bit abuse of notation, a possibly simpler alternative way of defining
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
is to let
\bm{\Psi}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}
be any full SVD of
\bm{\Psi}
and

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Psi}):=\bm{U}\bm{V}^{\top}\in\Od(d)

where
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
are both
d\times d
orthogonal matrices.
In particular, if
\bm{\Psi}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
is invertible, then

\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Psi}):=\bm{\Psi}(\bm{\Psi}^{\top}\bm{\Psi})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=(\bm{\Psi}\bm{\Psi}^{\top})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Psi}

is uniquely determined.

The operator
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is essentially the projection operator which maps a matrix of
\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
to
\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
since for each block
\bm{X}_{i}
,
\mathcal{P}(\bm{X}_{i})
is the orthogonal matrix closest to
\bm{X}_{i}
:

\mathcal{P}(\bm{X}_{i}):=\argmin_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{Q}-\bm{X}_{i}\|_{F}^{2}.

In our discussion, we also need to introduce a new distance function between two matrices
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
in
\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
.
Remember that for orthogonal group synchronization, the solution is equivalent up to a global group action, i.e., for any fixed
\bm{G}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n},

\bm{G}
and
\bm{G}\bm{Q}
are equivalent for any
\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)
. This fact needs to be taken into consideration and we define the following distance function for two matrices in
\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
:

d_{F}(\bm{Y},\bm{X}):=\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{Y}-\bm{X}\bm{Q}\|_{F},

(2.5)

where
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{Y})
minimizes
\|\bm{Y}-\bm{X}\bm{Q}\|_{F}
since

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{Y},\bm{X})

\displaystyle=\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\sqrt{\|\bm{X}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}-2\langle\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{Y},\bm{Q}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\|\bm{X}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}-2\|\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{Y}\|_{*}}.

This distance function satisfies triangle inequality. For three arbitrary
nd\times d
matrices
\bm{X},\bm{Y},
and
\bm{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle=\|\bm{X}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{ZX}\|_{F}\leq\|\bm{X}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{ZY}\bm{Q}_{YX}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q}_{YX}\|_{F}+\|\bm{Y}\bm{Q}_{YX}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{ZY}\bm{Q}_{YX}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})+d_{F}(\bm{Y},\bm{Z})

where
\bm{Q}_{ZX}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{X})
,
\bm{Q}_{ZY}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{Y})
, and
\bm{Q}_{YX}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X})
all belong to
\Od(d).

Now we are ready to present the algorithm, which has been proposed in
[
41
,
63
]
.

Algorithm 1

Generalized power methods for orthogonal group synchronization

1:

Compute the top
d
eigenvectors
\bm{\Phi}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
of
\bm{A}
with
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}=n\bm{I}_{d}.

2:

Compute
\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Phi}_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
where
\bm{\Phi}_{i}
is the
i
th block of
\bm{\Phi}
.

3:

Initialize
\bm{S}^{0}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{\Phi}).

4:

\bm{S}^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t})
,
t=0,1,\cdots

5:

Stop when the iteration stabilizes.

The algorithm begins with a spectral initialization which first computes the top
d
eigenvectors of
\bm{A}
and then projects them onto
\Od(d)^{\otimes n}.
After that,
each step of the algorithm is essentially one step of the power iteration followed by projection. In order to better understand this algorithm, we first take a closer look at this algorithm and its possible fixed point. Let
\bm{S}_{i}^{t}
be the
i
th block of
\bm{S}^{t}
and the update rule for the
i
th block is

\bm{S}_{i}^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{ij}\bm{S}_{j}^{t}\right)={\bm{U}_{i}^{t}}(\bm{V}_{i}^{t})^{\top},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

where
\bm{U}_{i}^{t}
and
\bm{V}_{i}^{t}
are the left and right singular vectors of
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{ij}\bm{S}_{j}^{t}
.
Note that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{ij}\bm{S}_{j}^{t}=\bm{U}_{i}^{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{i}^{t}(\bm{V}_{i}^{t})^{\top}=\bm{U}_{i}^{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{i}^{t}(\bm{U}_{i}^{t})^{\top}\cdot\bm{U}_{i}^{t}(\bm{V}_{i}^{t})^{\top}=\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}^{t}\bm{S}_{i}^{t+1}

(2.6)

where

\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}^{t}:=\bm{U}_{i}^{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{i}^{t}(\bm{U}_{i}^{t})^{\top}\succeq 0.

In other words,
\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}^{t}=\left([\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t}]_{i}[\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t}]_{i}^{\top}\right)^{1/2}
is the matrix square root of
[\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t}]_{i}[\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t}]_{i}^{\top}
and is uniquely determined.
A more compact form of the update rule is

\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t}=\bm{\Lambda}^{t}\bm{S}^{t+1}

where
\bm{\Lambda}^{t}=\blkdiag(\bm{\Lambda}_{11}^{t},\cdots,\bm{\Lambda}_{nn}^{t})
is a block-diagonal matrix whose diagonal blocks consist of
\{\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}^{t}\}_{i=1}^{n}.

Suppose
\{\bm{S}^{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
finally converges under
d_{F}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
is
likely
to satisfy (we will justify why it holds later):

\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}

(2.7)

where
\bm{\Lambda}=\blkdiag(\bm{\Lambda}_{11},\cdots,\bm{\Lambda}_{nn})\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}\succeq 0
. In fact, (
2.7
) arises again when we want to characterize the global optimality of
\bm{S}^{\infty}(\bm{S}^{\infty})^{\top}
in (
2.3
): if
\bm{S}^{\infty}
satisfies (
2.7
) and
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0
, then
\bm{S}^{\infty}(\bm{S}^{\infty})^{\top}
is a global maximizer to (
2.3
), which follows from the duality theory in convex optimization.

3
Main theorems

The first result improves the state-of-the-art bound for the tightness of the SDP relaxation to solve orthogonal group synchronization.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider orthogonal group synchronization problem (
2.1
) under additive Gaussian noise and its convex relaxation (
2.3
).
The SDP relaxation is tight, i.e., the globally optimal solution
\bm{X}
to (
2.3
) is rank-
d
and can be factorized into
\bm{X}=\bm{S}\bm{S}^{\top}
where
\bm{S}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
equals the maximum likelihood estimator (
2.2
), if

\sigma<\frac{c_{0}\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})}

(3.1)

with high probability for a small constant
c_{0}.

Moreover, its solution is unique and satisfies

\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\widehat{\bm{G}}_{i}-\bm{G}_{i}\bm{Q}\|_{F}\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{n^{-1}d}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})

where
\widehat{\bm{G}}_{i}
is the MLE and the solution to (
2.3
).

Here we have several remarks on Theorem
3.1
. The maximum likelihood estimator
\widehat{\bm{G}}
does not have an explicit form and is not equal to the ground truth
\bm{G}
. The best known bound on
\sigma
for the tightness before this result is
\sigma<c_{1}n^{1/4}/d^{3/4}
for some small constant
c_{1}
in
[
36
]
. The bound (
3.1
) greatly improves the scaling on
n
from
n^{1/4}
to a near-optimal dependence
\sqrt{n}
.
Note that in random matrix theory, it has been extensively studied when the top eigenvectors of
\bm{A}
are correlated with the planted signals (low-rank matrix), see e.g.
[
7
,
48
]
. In fact, once the noise level
\sigma
reaches
\sigma>\sqrt{n/d}
, spectral methods fail to provide useful information about the planted signal. Moreover, spectral methods (PCA) achieve the optimal detection threshold under certain natural priors for the spike.
Therefore, the optimal bound on
\sigma
should scale like
\sqrt{n/d}
up to a logarithmic factor and our bound differs from this threshold by a factor of
\sqrt{d}
.

Another relevant approach to solve the
\Od(d)
synchronization uses spectral relaxation. Note that
[
35
]
implies that spectral relaxation provides a spectral estimator
\widehat{\bm{G}}_{i,\spec}
of
\bm{G}_{i}
which satisfies
\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\max\|\widehat{\bm{G}}_{i,\spec}-\bm{G}_{i}\bm{Q}\|\lesssim\sigma\sqrt{n^{-1}d}
with a near-optimal bound on
\sigma
, i.e.,
\sigma\lesssim\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}).

However, the solution
\widehat{\bm{G}}_{\spec}
given by spectral relaxation is often not equal to the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE).

The second result concerns establishing an efficient algorithm with guaranteed global convergence. Despite that the spectral methods fail to give the MLE directly, it can be used as an initialization step since
\bm{S}^{0}=\widehat{\bm{G}}_{\spec}
is very close to
\bm{G}
even in block-wise error bound. Using this idea, we have the following theorem which provides a convergence analysis of the generalized power method.

Theorem 3.2
.

Consider orthogonal group synchronization problem (
2.1
) with additive Gaussian noise.
Suppose

\sigma<\frac{c_{0}\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})}

for some small constant
c_{0}
,
then the spectral initialization gives
\bm{S}^{0}
which satisfies
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{G})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
for
\epsilon<1/(32\sqrt{d})
and
the sequence
\{\bm{S}^{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
from the generalized power method converges linearly to a limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
, i.e.,

d_{F}(\bm{S}^{t},\bm{S}^{\infty})\leq 2^{-t}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{S}^{\infty}),\quad\forall t\geq 0,

with probability at least
1-O(n^{-2})
. Moreover,
\bm{S}^{\infty}(\bm{S}^{\infty})^{\top}
is the unique global maximizer of the SDP relaxation (
2.3
) and
\bm{S}^{\infty}
equals the maximum likelihood estimator, i.e., the global maximizer to (
2.2
).

Theorem
3.2
is a generalization of the convergence analysis of the GPM on phase synchronization (U(1) group)
[
63
]
. This extension is nontrivial since U(1) group considered in
[
63
]
is commutative while
\Od(d)
group is non-commutative for
d\geq 3
. The resulting technical difference can be seen later in Lemma
4.5
and
4.6
which are used to show that the GPM behaves like a contraction mapping on the iterates.

Now we discuss a few future directions.
As discussed before, the dependence on
n
is near-optimal but the scaling of
d
remains suboptimal by
\sqrt{d}
.
In fact, the suboptimal dependence of performance bound on the rank
d
is a general issue in the convergence analysis of nonconvex approaches in many signal processing and machine learning problems
[
17
,
34
]
. It is an open problem to have an exact recovery guarantee of the MLE via convex/nonconvex approach provided that

\sigma=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}\right)

holds modulo a log-factor. Resolving this open problem will potentially lead to a substantial improvement of performance guarantees in many examples of low-rank matrix recovery via nonconvex approach.
For the
\Od(d)
synchronization, one possible solution is to derive a performance bound in terms of
d_{2}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}):=\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{Y}-\bm{X}\bm{Q}\|
, which is significantly more challenging since the norm is no longer equipped with an inner product. This will create technical difficulties in proving that the Algorithm 1 is actually a contraction mapping within a basin of attraction, more precisely discussed in Lemma
4.7
and
4.8
.

Another future direction is about the optimization landscape associated with the Burer-Monteiro factorization of (
2.2
). Empirical studies in the author’s earlier work
[
36
]
indicate that even without spectral initialization, the generalized power method still outputs the global maximizer to the SDP relaxation. Therefore, it would be very interesting to see how the optimization landscape changes with respect to the noise strength
\sigma
or to justify why random initialization works well enough for this synchronization problem.
For the algorithmic aspect, another commonly-used approach for the angular (phase) synchronization is the GPM with a certain stepsize
[
9
,
40
]
. Instead of running
\bm{S}^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{A}\bm{S}^{t})
, one wants to recover the group elements by

\bm{S}^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{n}\left((\bm{I}_{nd}+\alpha\bm{A}/n)\bm{S}^{t}\right)

for some
\alpha>0
. In particular, as
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
, this algorithm becomes the generalized power method studied in this paper. It would be an interesting research problem to explore whether one can establish the global convergence of this proposed approach.

Finally, note that our current manuscript only deals with the additive Gaussian noise in (
2.1
). It is natural to consider general subgaussian noise. Examples include uniform corruption model
[
61
]
, i.e.,
\bm{A}_{ij}=\xi_{ij}\bm{G}_{i}\bm{G}_{j}^{\top}+(1-\xi_{ij})\bm{O}_{ij}
,
i<j
where
\xi_{ij}
is an independent Bernoulli(
p
) random variable and
\bm{O}_{ij}
is a random matrix sampled uniformly from the Haar measure on
\Od(d)
,
and also general additive block-wise independent subgaussian noise, i.e.,
\bm{A}_{ij}=\bm{G}_{i}\bm{G}_{j}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{\Delta}_{ij}
where
\{\bm{\Delta}_{ij}\}_{i\leq j}
are centered independent random matrices with subgaussian tail on
\|\bm{\Delta}_{ij}\|
. The techniques introduced in this paper could be extended to both scenarios but a tight performance bound requires a more careful analysis of the operator norm of the noise matrix.

4
Proofs

This section is devoted to the proof of the main theorems. Our proof will follow a similar route in
[
63
]
. However, since
\Od(d)
is non-commutative, many technical parts need different treatments from those in
[
63
]
. Also we will use some supporting results in
[
35
]
, especially regarding spectral initialization, without giving the detailed proofs. To make this manuscript more self-contained, the proof idea of each cited result will be discussed.

Instead of studying (
2.1
), we will focus on a statistically equivalent model.
Note that orthogonal transform will not change the distribution of Gaussian random matrix. It suffices to consider

\bm{A}=\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}

after performing a simple change of variable to (
2.1
) where
\bm{Z}^{\top}:=[\bm{I}_{d},\cdots,\bm{I}_{d}]\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times nd}.

Our main goal is to show the tightness of the SDP relaxation (
2.3
), i.e., the global maximizer to the SDP relaxation is of rank
d
, which corresponds to the global maximizer to (
2.2
). Similar to phase synchronization, the global maximizer to (
2.2
) does not have an explicit form (
\bm{Z}
is not the global maximizer in general), which makes it harder to characterize the global maximum. To resolve this issue,
[
63
]
developed a smart way of characterizing the global maximizer by treating it as the limiting point of the sequences generated by the generalized power method. We will show that the sequence
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
always stays in the intersection of the following two sets in Section
4.1
and
4.2
:

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{S}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}:d_{F}(\bm{S},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}\right\},

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{\xi,\infty}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\bm{S}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}:\left\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{S}\right\|_{F}\leq\xi\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}),~1\leq i\leq n\right\},

where
\xi=2+3\epsilon>0
is a constant determined in (
4.11
),
\epsilon
is another constant which satisfies

\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{32\sqrt{d}}\Longleftrightarrow\epsilon^{2}d\leq\frac{1}{1024}

and
\bm{W}_{i}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}
is the
i
th block column of noise matrix
\bm{W}=[\bm{W}_{1},\cdots,\bm{W}_{n}].

Note that both
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
are closed and compact sets in the Euclidean space. Then if we can prove that
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
converges to a limiting point, the limit also stays in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
. Moreover, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose the generalized power iteration converges to a limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
which satisfies
\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}
where
\bm{\Lambda}=\blkdiag(\bm{\Lambda}_{11},\cdots,\bm{\Lambda}_{nn})\succeq 0
is a symmetric block-diagonal matrix with
\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
. Moreover, if
\bm{S}^{\infty}
is located in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
, then
\bm{X}^{\infty}=\bm{S}^{\infty}(\bm{S}^{\infty})^{\top}
is the unique global maximizer to (
2.3
) if

\sigma<\frac{1-3\epsilon^{2}d/2}{\xi+3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})}.

(4.1)

Therefore, our task becomes (a): to show that the sequence
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
is located in the
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
and converges to a limiting point; (b): to justify why the limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
is the global maximizer.

Now we first focus on completing the task (b) by providing a sufficient condition of the global optimality in (
2.3
). Then we will show that if the limiting point is a fixed point of the power iteration and is located in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
, then it must satisfy the following global optimality condition. Finally, we will verify the assumptions in Theorem
4.1
hold in Section
4.3
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The matrix
\bm{X}=\bm{S}\bm{S}^{\top}
with
\bm{S}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
is a global optimal solution to (
2.3
) if there exists a block-diagonal matrix
\bm{\Lambda}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}
such that

\bm{A}\bm{S}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S},\quad\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0.

(4.2)

Moreover, if
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}
is of rank
(n-1)d
, then
\bm{X}
is the unique global maximizer.

This optimality condition can be found in several places including
[
36
, Proposition 5.1]
and
[
51
, Theorem 7]
.
The derivation of Theorem
4.2
follows from the standard routine of duality theory in convex optimization. In fact, the block-diagonal matrix
\bm{\Lambda}
corresponds exactly to the dual variable in (
2.3
). The first equation in (
4.2
) seems relatively simple to achieve as we have seen that the fixed point of power iteration naturally satisfies this condition (
2.6
). The more challenging part arises from achieving
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0
which reduces to finding a tight lower bound for the smallest eigenvalue of
\bm{\Lambda}.
We will prove that a tight lower bound of
\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Lambda})
can be obtained if the limiting point is inside
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}.
Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we introduce two useful supporting facts.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\bm{W}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}
is a symmetric Gaussian random matrix, then

\|\bm{W}\|\leq 3\sqrt{nd}

(4.3)

with probability at least
1-\exp(-nd/2).

This is a quite standard result in random matrix theory, which can be found in many places
[
5
,
63
,
58
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\bm{S}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
, all the singular values of
\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}
satisfy

\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n\leq\sigma_{i}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S})\leq n,\quad 1\leq i\leq d.

Proof: .

Note that

\displaystyle d_{F}^{2}(\bm{S},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle=\|\bm{S}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{S}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\bm{Z}\|_{F}^{2}-2\langle\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S},\bm{Q}\rangle

\displaystyle=2(nd-\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}\|_{*})\leq\epsilon^{2}nd

where
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S})
. Since
\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}\|\leq n
implies
0\leq\sigma_{i}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S})\leq n
,
1\leq i\leq d
, we have

\frac{\epsilon^{2}nd}{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d}(n-\sigma_{i}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}))\geq n-\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S})\Longrightarrow\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n.

∎

This bound is actually quite conservative due to the extra
d
factor in the lower bound. It is also the reason why we obtain a sub-optimal scaling on
\sigma
in the main theorem. In fact, numerical experiments indicate that this additional
d
factor is not tight. Therefore, we leave this sub-optimality issue to the future work.
Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Suppose the fixed point
\bm{S}^{\infty}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
satisfies
\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}
for some positive semidefinite block-diagonal matrix
\bm{\Lambda}=\blkdiag(\bm{\Lambda}_{11},\cdots,\bm{\Lambda}_{nn})\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}
. Then we have

\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}=[\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}]_{i}(\bm{S}_{i}^{\infty})^{\top}\succeq 0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

where
[\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}]_{i}=\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}\bm{S}_{i}^{\infty},1\leq i\leq n
. Remember our goal is to prove that
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0
and its
(d+1)
th smallest eigenvalue is strictly positive.

We first obtain a lower bound of the smallest eigenvalue of
\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}
as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Lambda}_{ii})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\min}(\bm{\Lambda}_{ii})=\sigma_{\min}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{ij}\bm{S}_{j}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}+\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{W}_{ij}\bm{S}_{j}^{\infty}\right)

(Weyl’s theorem)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\min}\left(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}\right)-\sigma\left\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}\right\|_{F}

(Lemma
4.4
)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n-\sigma\xi\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

where
\bm{\Lambda}_{ii}\succeq 0
is symmetric for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\bm{S}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
. Note that
(\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A})\bm{S}^{\infty}=0
means at least
d
eigenvalues are 0. To show that
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0
with rank
(n-1)d
, it suffices to ensure the
(d+1)
-th smallest eigenvalue of
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}
is strictly positive, i.e.,
\bm{u}^{\top}(\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A})\bm{u}>0
for any nonzero unit vector
\bm{u}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd}
such that
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}=0
. It holds that

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{u}+\sigma\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{u}^{\top}(\bm{S}^{\infty}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|^{2}+\sigma\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{W}\bm{u}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{S}^{\infty}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|^{2}+\sigma\|\bm{W}\|

\displaystyle(\text{Use}~\eqref{eq:gaussnorm})\quad

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}nd+3\sigma\sqrt{nd}

where
\|\bm{W}\|\leq 3\sqrt{nd}
,
\|\bm{S}^{\infty}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|\leq\|\bm{S}^{\infty}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
and
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty})
. Thus if (
4.1
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{\top}(\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A})\bm{u}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Lambda}_{ii})-\max_{\bm{u}:\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}=0,\|\bm{u}\|=1}\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{u}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n-\sigma\xi\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})-\left(\epsilon^{2}nd+3\sigma\sqrt{nd}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{3\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n-\sigma(\xi+3)\sqrt{nd}\left(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}\right)>0.

Then we have
\bm{\Lambda}-\bm{A}\succeq 0
with the
(d+1)
th smallest eigenvalue strictly positive and the bottom
d
eigenvalues equal to zero.
∎

Roadmap
:
Now we provide an overview of what to do in the next few sections.
To prove the main results Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
, Theorem
4.1
indicates that we need to show the iterates
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
stay in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
for all
\ell\in\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and moreover, it will converge to a limiting point which satisfies
\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}
for some positive semidefinite block-diagonal matrix
\bm{\Lambda}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times nd}.
We will justify why
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
stay in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
in Section
4.1
and
4.2
. The existence and convergence of limiting point, and its global optimality will be given in Section
4.3
. In Section
4.4
, we will prove why the spectral initialization provides a good starting point within
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
. All the following results hold with high probability under the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
.

4.1
Contraction mapping on
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}

The tricky part is that we do not know the explicit form of the MLE which is not necessarily equal to
\bm{Z}
. In order to show the convergence of iterates to the MLE, we start with proving the power iteration essentially gives a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
.
Define

{\cal L}:=n^{-1}\bm{A},\quad{\cal L}\bm{S}:=\frac{1}{n}\bm{A}\bm{S}=\frac{1}{n}(\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W})\bm{S}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{nd\times d}.

(4.4)

Then the power iteration is equivalent to:

\bm{S}^{t+1}=\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{t}).

Now, we will prove that
\mathcal{P}_{n}\circ{\cal L}
is a contraction mapping, by investigating
{\cal L}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
individually.
The main result in this subsection is Lemma
4.8
.
Ideally, if we manage to prove that
\mathcal{P}_{n}\circ{\cal L}
maps
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
to itself, then the proof is done which follows from the contraction mapping theorem. However, it remains unclear how to prove/disprove this statement. Instead, we will show that the iterates
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
from the projected power method stay in the basin of attraction. We will address this issue in the next subsection.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}\in\Od(d)^{\otimes n}
with
d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
and
d_{F}(\bm{Y},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
, it holds

d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})\leq\rho^{\prime}\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}),

where

\rho^{\prime}=2\epsilon\sqrt{d}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

Under the assumption

\epsilon\sqrt{d}\leq\frac{1}{32},\quad\sigma\leq\frac{1}{72}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}},

then

\rho^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{9}.

We give the proof of this lemma at the end of this subsection. Note that Lemma
4.5
generalizes Lemma 12 in
[
63
]
to the non-commutative scenario. The major difficulty in proving Lemma
4.5
comes from estimating the error
\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|
for
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X})
, which is very different from the proof of Lemma 12 in
[
63
]
.
Next, we turn our focus to
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For two invertible matrices
\bm{X}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
and
\bm{Y}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}(\bm{X})-\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})+\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})}.

If only
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})
(or
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})
) is known, then we can use

\|\mathcal{P}(\bm{X})-\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y})\|_{F}\leq 2\sigma^{-1}_{\min}(\bm{X})\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}.

Lemma
4.6
is a generalization of Lemma 13 in
[
63
]
from
d\leq 2
to
d\geq 3
, which says the matrix sign function (generalized phase) in (
2.4
) is a Lipschitz continuous function in the region where the input matrices have their smallest singular values bounded away from 0.
We defer the proof of Lemma
4.6
to the end of this section. The main idea of the proof follows from applying the famous Davis-Kahan theorem to the augmented matrices of
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
.

Now it seems combining Lemma
4.5
with
4.6
justifies the contraction of
\mathcal{P}_{n}\circ{\cal L}
. The only missing piece is to ensure that each block
[{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i}
has a lower bound on its smallest singular value so that we can apply Lemma
4.6
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
\bm{X}\in{\cal N}_{\epsilon}\cap{\cal N}_{\xi,\infty}
, then

\sigma_{\min}\left([{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i}\right)\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}d

provided that

\sigma\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2\xi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})}.

One can easily realize that the dependence of
\sigma
on
d
is sub-optimal by a factor of
\sqrt{d}.
This is where the bottleneck is.

Lemma 4.8

(Contraction mapping)
.

Suppose
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
are in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
, then we have

\displaystyle d_{F}(\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{X}),\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{Y}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\cdot d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\left(2\epsilon\sqrt{d}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\right)\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}).

We define

\rho=\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\left(2\epsilon\sqrt{d}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\right).

Under

\epsilon\sqrt{d}\leq\frac{1}{32},\quad\sigma\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{72}\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}},~\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2\xi}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})}\right\},

then
\rho<1/2.

Proof of Lemma
4.7
and
4.8
.

Over
\bm{X}\in\mathcal{N}={\cal N}_{\epsilon}\cap{\cal N}_{\xi,\infty}
, we have
{\cal L}\bm{X}
and its
i
th block as

{\cal L}\bm{X}=\frac{1}{n}(\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W})\bm{X},\quad[{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{X}+\frac{\sigma}{n}\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{X}.

Note that if
d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
,
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{X})\geq\left(1-\epsilon^{2}d/2\right)n
holds.
As a result,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}([{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{X})-\frac{\sigma}{n}\left\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{X}\right\|

\displaystyle(\text{Lemma}~\ref{lem:sigmamin},~\bm{X}\in\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty})\quad

\displaystyle{\geq}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)-\frac{\sigma}{n}\cdot\xi\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}d

when

\sigma\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}\sqrt{nd}}{2\xi(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})}.

Similarly, we have
\sigma_{\min}([{\cal L}\bm{Y}]_{i})\geq 1-\epsilon^{2}d.

Now we are ready to obtain the contraction property of
\mathcal{P}_{n}\circ{\cal L}
on
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
. We pick
\bm{Q}
as the orthogonal matrix
\bm{Q}
which minimizes

d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})=\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|{\cal L}\bm{X}-{\cal L}\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}.

Then

\displaystyle d_{F}(\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{X}),\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{Y}))

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{X})-\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{P}([{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i})-\mathcal{P}([{\cal L}\bm{Y}\bm{Q}]_{i})\|_{F}^{2}}

(Lemma
4.6
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|[{\cal L}\bm{X}]_{i}-[{\cal L}\bm{Y}\bm{Q}]_{i})\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\|{\cal L}\bm{X}-{\cal L}\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})

(Lemma
4.5
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\left(2\epsilon\sqrt{d}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\right)d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}).

∎

Now we present the proof of Lemma
4.5
and
4.6
which are used to justify Lemma
4.8
.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Under
d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
and
d_{F}(\bm{Y},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
, it holds that

\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{Z})\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n,\quad\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{Z})\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}\right)n.

Also by triangle inequality and Lemma
4.4
, we have

d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})\leq 2\epsilon\sqrt{nd},\quad\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X}^{\top}\bm{Y})\geq\left(1-2\epsilon^{2}d\right)n.

Now we set
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X})
which minimizes
\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{R}\|_{F}
over
\bm{R}\in\Od(d)
.

\displaystyle d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\|\bm{A}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\left\|\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\right\|_{F}+\sigma\|\bm{W}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})

where
\bm{A}=\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}
,
\|\bm{Z}\|=\sqrt{n}
, and
\|\bm{W}\|\leq 3\sqrt{nd}.

The more tricky part is the estimation of
\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}.

Let
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X})
,
\bm{Q}_{\bm{X}}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{X})
, and
\bm{Q}_{\bm{Y}}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{Y})
. Then

\displaystyle\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\bm{Y}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{\bm{Y}})^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}+\|\bm{Y}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}+\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}-n\bm{Q}\|_{F}

where
\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{\bm{Y}}\|\leq\|\bm{Y}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}_{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}.

For the second term,

\displaystyle\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}-n\bm{Q}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}\|_{F}^{2}-2n\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}\|_{*}+n^{2}d

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(n-\sigma_{i}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n-\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}))\cdot(nd-\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}\|_{*})

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}nd\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}^{2}

where
0\leq n-\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X})\leq 2\epsilon^{2}nd
follows from Lemma
4.4
and
\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}^{2}=2(nd-\|\bm{Y}^{\top}\bm{X}\|_{*})
.
As a result,

\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q})\|_{F}\leq 2\epsilon\sqrt{nd}\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\bm{Q}\|_{F}=2\epsilon\sqrt{nd}\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}).

To sum up, we have

\displaystyle d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{X},{\cal L}\bm{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot 2\epsilon\sqrt{nd}\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\epsilon\sqrt{d}+3\sigma\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\right)\cdot d_{F}(\bm{X},\bm{Y}).

∎

Now we present the proof of Lemma
4.6
which relies on the famous Davis-Kahan theorem.

Theorem 4.9

(Davis-Kahan theorem
[
23
]
)
.

Suppose
\bm{\Psi}
and
\bm{\Psi}_{E}
are the top
d
normalized eigenvectors of
\bm{X}
and
\bm{X}_{\bm{E}}=\bm{X}+\bm{E}
respectively, then

\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{E}}-\bm{\Psi}\bm{Q}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\|(\bm{I}-\bm{\Psi}\bm{\Psi}^{\top})\bm{\Psi}_{E}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\|\bm{E}\bm{\Psi}_{E}\|}{\delta}

where
\bm{\Psi}^{\top}\bm{\Psi}=\bm{\Psi}_{E}^{\top}\bm{\Psi}_{E}=\bm{I}_{d}
and

\delta=\lambda_{d}(\bm{X})-\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{X}_{\bm{E}})>0
is the spectral gap. The bound also holds when replacing
\|\cdot\|
by
\|\cdot\|_{F}
.

In particular, in order to obtain a meaningful bound, we would hope to have
\delta>\|\bm{E}\|
such that
\|(\bm{I}-\bm{\Psi}\bm{\Psi}^{\top})\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{E}}\|<1.

By Theorem
4.9
, we can establish a useful result about the projection operator
\mathcal{P}(\cdot).

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

The proof follows from the Davis-Kahan theorem.
Consider the augmented matrix:

\widetilde{\bm{X}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\bm{X}\\
\bm{X}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\bm{Y}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\bm{Y}\\
\bm{Y}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Let
(\bm{U}_{X},\bm{V}_{X})
and
(\bm{U}_{Y},\bm{V}_{Y})
be the left and right singular vectors of
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
respectively. Then

\bm{M}_{X}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{X}\\
\bm{V}_{X}\end{bmatrix}
and
\bm{M}_{Y}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{Y}\\
\bm{V}_{Y}\end{bmatrix}
are the eigenvectors associated with the top
d
eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\bm{X}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{Y}}
respectively which are also equal to the singular values of
\bm{X}
and
\bm{Y}
.
Applying the Davis-Kahan Theorem
4.9
gives

\displaystyle\|(\bm{I}_{2d}-\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}^{\top})\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{d}(\widetilde{\bm{X}})-\lambda_{d+1}(\widetilde{\bm{Y}})}\cdot\|(\widetilde{\bm{X}}-\widetilde{\bm{Y}})\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})+\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})}\cdot\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}\|\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})+\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})}.

Here the spectral gap equals
\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})+\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})
since all the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\bm{X}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{Y}}
are
\{\pm\sigma_{\ell}(\bm{X})\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
and
\{\pm\sigma_{\ell}(\bm{Y})\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
respectively. Therefore, we have
\lambda_{d}(\widetilde{\bm{X}})=\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})
and
\lambda_{d+1}(\widetilde{\bm{Y}})=-\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y}).

Using the definition of
\bm{M}_{X}
and
\bm{M}_{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle(\bm{I}_{2d}-\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}^{\top})\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}&-\bm{U}_{X}\bm{V}_{X}^{\top}\\
-\bm{V}_{X}\bm{U}_{X}^{\top}&\bm{I}_{d}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{Y}\\
\bm{V}_{Y}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-\bm{I}_{d}&0\\
0&\bm{V}_{X}\bm{U}_{X}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{X}\bm{V}_{X}^{\top}-\bm{U}_{Y}\bm{V}_{Y}^{\top}\\
\bm{U}_{X}\bm{V}_{X}^{\top}-\bm{U}_{Y}\bm{V}_{Y}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\bm{V}_{Y}.

Note that
\bm{U}_{X},\bm{V}_{X},\bm{U}_{Y},
and
\bm{V}_{Y}
are all orthogonal. Therefore, it holds that

\|(\bm{I}_{2d}-\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}^{\top})\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{U}_{X}\bm{V}_{X}^{\top}-\bm{U}_{Y}\bm{V}_{Y}^{\top}\|_{F}=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{P}(\bm{X})-\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y})\|_{F}.

Now we obtain the distance between
\mathcal{P}(\bm{X})
and
\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y}):

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}(\bm{X})-\mathcal{P}(\bm{Y})\|_{F}

\displaystyle=2\|(\bm{I}_{2d}-\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}\bm{M}_{\bm{X}}^{\top})\bm{M}_{\bm{Y}}\|_{F}\leq\frac{2\|\bm{X}-\bm{Y}\|_{F}}{\sigma_{\min}(\bm{X})+\sigma_{\min}(\bm{Y})}.

∎

4.2
Keep the iterates in the basin of attraction

The challenging part is to keep the iterates
\bm{S}^{\ell}
in the basin of attraction
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
. In particular, it is not trivial to estimate the “correlation” between
\bm{W}_{m}
and
\bm{S}^{\ell}
since
\bm{S}^{\ell}
depends on
\bm{W}_{m}
implicitly for all
1\leq m\leq n
. The remedy is to use the very popular
leave-one-out
technique by introducing an auxiliary sequence which is the output of the generalized power method based on the data matrix:

\bm{A}^{(m)}_{ij}:=\begin{cases}\bm{I}_{d}+\sigma\bm{W}_{ij},&\text{ if }~i\neq m\text{ and }j\neq m,\\
\bm{I}_{d},&\text{ if }~i=m\text{ or }j=m,\end{cases}

for every
1\leq m\leq n.
The matrix
\bm{A}^{(m)}
is exactly equal to
\bm{A}
except its
m
th block row and column. In other words,

\bm{A}^{(m)}:=\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}^{(m)}

(4.5)

where

\bm{W}^{(m)}_{ij}=\begin{cases}\bm{W}_{ij},&\text{ if }~i\neq m\text{ and }j\neq m,\\
0,&\text{ if }~i=m\text{ or }j=m.\end{cases}

Denote
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
as the
\ell
th iterate from the generalized power method with

{\cal L}^{(m)}:=n^{-1}\bm{A}^{(m)}=n^{-1}\left(\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}^{(m)}\right),

which means

\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m}=\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}^{(m)}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}),\quad 1\leq m\leq n,\quad\ell\geq 0.

By construction,
\{\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
is
statistically independent
of
\bm{W}_{m}
and thus applying the concentration inequality to
\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}
yields a tight bound. The missing piece is the difference between
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
and
\bm{S}^{\ell}
for all
1\leq m\leq n
and
\ell\geq 0.
Note that
\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}^{(m)}
differ up to only one block row/column and thus we believe
\bm{S}^{\ell}
and
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
are very close for all
\ell\in\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
1\leq m\leq n
.
As long as
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell,m})
is small (in particular, of order
\sqrt{d}
), then
\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{F}
is of order
\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}).

Now we first start with bounding
\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}
by using the fact that
\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}^{2}
satisfies chi-square distribution.

Lemma 4.10
.

For
X\sim\chi^{2}_{k}
, we have

\hbox{\msbm{P}}(X\geq k+t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(t-k\log\left(1+\frac{t}{k}\right)\right)\right)

for
t>0.
In particular, it holds

\hbox{\msbm{P}}(\sqrt{X}\geq\sqrt{k}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})\leq n^{-\gamma^{2}/2},\quad\gamma>0.

Proof: .

This is a quite standard result which can be easily proven by using moment generating function and Markov inequality. For any
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle\hbox{\msbm{P}}(X\geq k+t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda(k+t)}\E e^{\lambda X}=e^{-\lambda(k+t)}(1-2\lambda)^{-k/2}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\lambda(k+t)-\frac{k}{2}\log(1-2\lambda)\right).

The upper bound is minimized when choosing
\lambda=\frac{t}{2(k+t)}
, which gives the bound.

If
t=2\gamma\sqrt{k\log n}+\gamma^{2}\log n
, then

\displaystyle\hbox{\msbm{P}}(X\geq(\sqrt{k}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(2\gamma\sqrt{k\log n}+\gamma^{2}\log n-k\log\frac{(\sqrt{k}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})^{2}}{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle(\text{use}~\log(1+x)\leq x,x>0))\quad

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(2\gamma\sqrt{k\log n}+\gamma^{2}\log n-2k\cdot\frac{\gamma\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=n^{-\gamma^{2}/2}

(4.6)

for any
\gamma>0.

∎

By construction of
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
, we know that
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
is independent of
\bm{W}_{m}
for all
\ell
. Thus by taking a union, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.11
.

It holds uniformly that

\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})

(4.7)

for
1\leq\ell\leq L
with probability at least
1-Ln^{-\gamma^{2}/2+1}
. In particular, we choose
L=n
and
\gamma=4.

Proof: .

For any fixed
\ell
and
m
, we know that each entry of
\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
is an i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,n)
random variable. Thus

n^{-1}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{d^{2}}.

Using Lemma
4.10
with
k=d^{2}
and
t=2\gamma d\sqrt{\log n}+\gamma^{2}\log n
gives

n^{-1}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}^{2}\leq(d+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})^{2}

with probability at least
1-n^{-\gamma^{2}/2}
. Now taking a union bound over
1\leq m\leq n
and
1\leq\ell\leq L
, we have

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L,1\leq m\leq n}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{n}\left(d+\gamma\sqrt{\log n}\right)\leq\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})

with probability at least
1-Ln^{-\gamma^{2}/2+1}=1-n^{-\gamma^{2}/2+2}.

∎

Now we can see that in order to show that
\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{F}
is small, it suffices to bound the error between
\bm{S}^{\ell}
and
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
for all
1\leq m\leq n
and
1\leq\ell\leq L
. In fact, for the initialization
\bm{S}^{0}
and
\bm{S}^{0,m}
, we have the following result which will be proven in Section
4.4
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Under

\sigma<c_{0}\min\left\{\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n}{d}},\frac{\kappa\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}}\right\}

for a sufficiently small constant
c_{0}
, then the initialization satisfies:

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{nd}}{2},

(4.8)

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{S}^{0,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2},\quad 1\leq m\leq n,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

(4.10)

uniformly for all
1\leq m\leq n.

Note that if we only want to prove the tightness of the SDP relaxation, the initialization step is not necessary since we can simply pick
\bm{S}^{0}=\bm{Z}
for the purpose of proof. The initialization step works mainly for the global convergence of the efficient projected generalized power method.
We defer the proof of this lemma in Section
4.4
. From now on, we set

\xi=2+\frac{3\kappa}{2},\qquad\kappa=2\epsilon,\qquad\epsilon\sqrt{d}<\frac{1}{32}

(4.11)

in
\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}.
Under this set of parameters, we can see all the lemmas in this section hold under the assumption of Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Conditioned on (
4.3
) and (
4.7
), we have for
1\leq\ell\leq L
,

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2},\quad 1\leq m\leq n,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}),

(4.13)

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{Z})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{nd}}{2},

(4.14)

if

\sigma<\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{(3\kappa+2)\sqrt{d}},\frac{\kappa}{24}\right\}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}},\quad\kappa=2\epsilon

which is guaranteed by (
3.1
) in Theorem
3.1
.

Combined with Lemma
4.8
, we have

d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell-1})\leq 2^{-\ell}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}^{0}),\quad 1\leq\ell\leq L-1

(4.15)

since
\bm{S}^{\ell}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty},~\forall 0\leq\ell\leq L.

Proof of Lemma
4.13
.

The proof is based on induction. First of all, the initial value
\bm{S}^{0}
and
\bm{S}^{0,m}
satisfy (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.14
) in Lemma
4.12
.
Suppose all the three inequalities (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.14
) hold true for
0,1,\cdots,\ell
, we will prove the case for
\ell+1
with
\ell\leq L-1.

Step 1: Proof of (
4.12
).

Note that
\bm{S}^{\ell}
is in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
implies
\sigma_{\min}([{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}]_{i})>1-\epsilon^{2}d
for all
1\leq i\leq n
which follows from Lemma
4.7
. Applying Lemma
4.8
gives

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},{\cal L}^{(m)}\bm{S}^{\ell,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{4}d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},{\cal L}^{(m)}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}),\quad 1\leq m\leq n,

where
\epsilon\sqrt{d}\leq 1/32.

We proceed by using triangle inequality:

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{4}\left(d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell,m})+d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell,m},{\cal L}^{(m)}\bm{S}^{\ell,m})\right)

Since

hold, then

\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}.

Then Lemma

implies that

d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell,m})\leq\frac{1}{9}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell,m}).

Note that the
j
th block of

[(\bm{W}-\bm{W}^{(m)})\bm{S}^{\ell,m}]_{j}=\begin{cases}\bm{W}_{jm}\bm{S}_{m}^{\ell,m},&j\neq m,\\
\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m},&j=m,\end{cases}

where
\bm{S}_{m}^{\ell,m}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
, the
m
th block of
\bm{S}^{\ell,m}
, is orthogonal.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|(\bm{W}-\bm{W}^{(m)})\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq{\|\bm{W}_{m}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}_{m}\|_{F}}+\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle(\text{use Lemma~\ref{lem:gauss}})\quad

\displaystyle\leq{\|\bm{W}_{m}\|_{F}}+\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\leq 3\sqrt{nd}\cdot\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle<4\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}).

(4.18)

Then combining (
4.12
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (

) together gives

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell,m})+\frac{9\sigma}{4n}\|(\bm{W}-\bm{W}^{(m)})\bm{S}^{\ell,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2}+\frac{9\sigma}{4n}\cdot 4\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2}

Step 2: Proof of (

).

Let
\bm{R}

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell+1}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1}-\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m}\bm{R})\right\|_{F}+\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle(\text{use Lemma~\ref{lem:gauss}})\quad

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{W}_{m}\|\cdot d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell+1,m})+\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\leq 3\sqrt{nd}\cdot\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2}+\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n}).

Step 3: Proof of (
4.14

Since both
\bm{S}^{\ell}
and
\bm{Z}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle=d_{F}(\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}),\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{Z}))\leq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon^{2}d}\cdot d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9}{2}\cdot d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{Z}),\qquad\bm{S}^{\ell}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}

which follows from Lemma
4.8

For

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|[{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}]_{i}-\bm{R}\|_{F}
for some orthogonal matrix
\bm{R}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell})\in\Od(d).
We look at the
i

{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}:

[{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}]_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}+\frac{\sigma}{n}\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}

Then

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{R}\in\Od(d)}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|[{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}]_{i}-\bm{R}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\bm{R}\in\Od(d)}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}-\bm{R}\right\|_{F}+\frac{\sigma}{n}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{F}.

\min_{\bm{R}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{S}^{\ell}-\bm{Z}\bm{R}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}/2

2nd-2\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{*}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}nd}{4}\Longrightarrow\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{*}\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}\right)nd.

\displaystyle\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}-n\bm{R}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\sigma_{i}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell})-n)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(nd-\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{*})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{4}n^{2}d^{2}}{64}

where
0\leq\sigma_{i}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell})\leq n.
This implies

\|\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}-n\bm{R}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon^{2}nd/8.

Then

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{R}\in\Od(d)}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|[{\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}]_{i}-\bm{R}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\bm{R}\in\Od(d)}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}-\bm{R}\right\|_{F}+\frac{\sigma}{n}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\ell}\|_{F}

\displaystyle(\text{use}~\eqref{eq:kappa2})\quad

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{8}+\frac{\sigma}{n}\cdot\left(1+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{8}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{2}<\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{d}}{9}

where
\epsilon

and

\sigma\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}d}{3\kappa+2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}(\sqrt{d}+4\sqrt{\log n})}.

Therefore, it holds

d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{Z})\leq\frac{9}{2}\cdot d_{F}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{Z})\leq\frac{9}{2}\cdot\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{nd}}{9}\leq\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{nd}}{2}.

Now we have proven
\bm{S}^{\ell+1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
. By induction, we have
\bm{S}^{\ell}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq L
.
Now we can immediately invoke Lemma
4.8
and get

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell})

\displaystyle=d_{F}(\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}),\mathcal{P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell-1}))\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell-1}).

∎

Proof of Theorem

and

.

The proofs of Theorem

and

are exactly the same except the initialization part. For the tightness of SDP, it suffices to let
\bm{S}^{0}=\bm{Z}
since we only need to show the global optimum of the SDP is exactly the MLE. For the generalized power method, we show that spectral initialization provides a sufficiently good starting point which is addressed in Section
4.4
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
, it suffices to show that the iterates have a limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
which satisfy
\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}
and is also located in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
, according to Theorem
4.1
.

Step 1:
The existence and convergence of the limiting point in
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
.

Conditioned on Lemma
4.14
, it holds that
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell},\bm{S}^{\ell-1})\leq 2^{-\ell+1}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}^{0})
for all
\ell\in\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

Then
\{\bm{S}^{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+k},\bm{S}^{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=\ell}^{\ell+k-1}2^{-t}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}^{0})\leq 2^{-\ell+1}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}^{0}),\quad\forall k\in\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{\geq 0}

(4.22)

is arbitrarily small for any sufficiently large
\ell.

Note that
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
is a closed and compact set in the Euclidean space.
The distance function
d_{F}(\cdot,\cdot)
is equivalent to the Frobenius norm on the quotient set
\Od(d)^{\otimes n}/\Od(d)
where
\Od(d)
is also a compact set.
Thus since
\bm{S}^{\ell}
is a Cauchy sequence, we know there must exist a limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}.
From (
4.22
), we also have the linear convergence of
\bm{S}^{\ell}
to the limiting point:

d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\infty},\bm{S}^{\ell})\leq 2^{-\ell+1}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}_{0}).

(4.23)

Now we want to prove that
\bm{S}^{\infty}(\bm{S}^{\infty})^{\top}
is the unique global maximizer to (
2.3
) by using Theorem
4.1
.

Note that (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) hold for all
\ell
. Therefore, sending
\ell
to
\infty

\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{\infty}\|_{F}\leq\left(2+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}),\quad d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\infty},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}

(4.24)

since
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}
are compact sets. This implies
\bm{S}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{N}_{\xi,\infty}.

Step 2:
The limiting point
\bm{S}^{\infty}
satisfies
\bm{A}\bm{S}^{\infty}=\bm{\Lambda}\bm{S}^{\infty}
for some block-diagonal positive semidefinite matrix
\bm{\Lambda}.

Note that

d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\infty}),\bm{S}^{\ell})\leq d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\infty}),{\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}))+d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}),\bm{S}^{\ell}).

By letting
\ell
go to
\infty
and using Lemma
4.8
, it holds

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\infty}),{\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}))\leq\frac{1}{2}\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\infty},\bm{S}^{\ell})=0.

For

, we have

d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\ell}),\bm{S}^{\ell})=d_{F}(\bm{S}^{\ell+1},\bm{S}^{\ell})\leq 2^{-\ell}d_{F}(\bm{S}^{1},\bm{S}^{0})\rightarrow 0,\quad\ell\rightarrow\infty,

which follows from Lemma
4.13
.
Therefore,

d_{F}({\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\infty}),\bm{S}^{\infty})=0

and we have
{\cal P}_{n}({\cal L}\bm{S}^{\infty})=\bm{S}^{\infty}\bm{O}
for some orthogonal matrix

(4.25)

), we know that

. Applying Theorem

\bm{S}^{\infty}
satisfies (

which follows from Lemma
4.6
and (

).

\bm{A}=\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}+\sigma\bm{W}
; (b) round each
d\times d
block
\bm{\Phi}_{i}
of
\bm{\Phi}
into an orthogonal matrix and use
\bm{S}_{i}^{0}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Phi}_{i})
as the initialization. Note that Lemma
4.12
requires the initial value
\bm{S}^{0}
to be very close to the ground true
\bm{Z}
and also not highly “aligned” with the noise
\bm{W}_{m}
. It is a seemingly simple task to justify that:
if the noise is small, then applying Davis-Kahan theorem would provide a tight bound of
\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|
. Then we believe that
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{\Phi})
is also close to
\bm{Z}
.
However, due to the rounding procedure in the initialization, we actually need a more careful analysis of the eigenvectors
\bm{\Phi}
.

In order to obtain
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
, we actually need to establish a block-wise bound between
\bm{\Phi}
and
\bm{Z}
, i.e.,

\min_{\bm{Q}\in\Od(d)}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-\bm{Q}\|.

As a result, what we need is much more than classical eigenspace perturbation bound given by Davis-Kahan theorem
[
23
]
which provides an error bound in
\|\cdot\|
or
\|\cdot\|_{F}
.

The block-wise perturbation bound on the eigenvectors is a natural generalization of recent entrywise error bound on eigenvectors arising from spectral clustering,
\hbox{\msbm{Z}}_{2}
-synchronization, and matrix completion. The author’s early work
[
35
]
uses the popular leave-one-out technique to obtain such a block-wise error bound in several applications, including the scenario discussed in this paper. One core result for the initialization reads as follows:

Theorem 4.15

(Theorem 5.1 in
[
35
]
)
.

Suppose
(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\Lambda})
are the top
d
eigenvectors and eigenvalues of
\bm{A}
satisfying
\bm{A}\bm{\Phi}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Lambda}
,
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}=n\bm{I}_{d}
and
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{\Phi})
. Let

\eta:=\sigma n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}),\quad\sigma<C_{0}^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})^{-1}

(4.26)

where
C_{0}>0
is an absolute large constant.
Then with probability at least
1-O(n^{-1}d^{-1})
, it holds

\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|\lesssim\eta\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|,

(4.27)

uniformly for all
1\leq i\leq n
where
[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}
is the
i
th block of
\bm{A}\bm{Z}
. Moreover, we have

|\sigma_{j}(\bm{\Phi}_{i})-1|\lesssim\eta,\qquad\forall 1\leq i\leq n,~1\leq j\leq d.

(4.28)

Interested readers may refer to
[
35
]
for more details and we do not repeat the proof again here. For the completeness of presentation, we will discuss the main idea of proving (
4.27
) and how to derive (
4.28
) from (
4.27
). Note that our goal is to estimate each block of
\bm{\Phi}
. The idea is to apply one step power iteration and use the outcome to approximate
\bm{\Phi}
:

\bm{\Phi}-\bm{A}\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}=\bm{A}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}

where
{\bm{A}\bm{\Phi}=\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Lambda}}.
Therefore, the error bound on the
i
th block is

\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|\leq\|[\bm{A}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})]_{i}\|\|\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|

Here
\lambda_{\min}(\bm{\Lambda})\geq n-\sigma\|\bm{W}\|>n/2
follows from Weyl’s theorem and thus
\|\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|\leq 2/n.

It suffices to bound
\|[\bm{A}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})]_{i}\|
. However, the
i
th block column of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{\Phi}
are
statistically dependent
and thus we cannot apply concentration inequality directly to obtain a tight bound. To overcome this issue, we apply the leave-one-out technique again: approximate
\bm{\Phi}
with
\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}
which consists of the top
d
eigenvectors of
\bm{A}^{(i)}
defined in (
4.5
):

\displaystyle\|\bm{A}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|

\displaystyle=\|(\bm{Z}+\sigma\bm{W}_{i})^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{Z}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(i)})\|+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n}\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}\|\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}\|+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}(\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|.

The first term is bounded by using standard Davis-Kahan argument; for the second term, we first use Davis-Kahan theorem again to bound
\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}\|
which is quite small and then control the term by
\|\bm{W}_{i}\|\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}\|
; for the third term, due to the independence between
\bm{W}_{i}
and
\bm{\Phi}^{(i)}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}
, one can also get a tight bound by using concentration inequality of a Gaussian random matrix
[
58
]
. This finishes the proof of (
4.27
).

With (
4.27
), we can derive (
4.28
). First of all,
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|=O(1)
holds since (
4.27
) gives

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|\lesssim\frac{1}{1-C^{\prime}\eta}\|[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|\leq C_{0}\|[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\|\|\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|\leq 4C_{0}.

Here
C_{0}
and
C^{\prime}
are two constants, and the bound on
\|[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\|
follows from

\|[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\|=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{I}_{d}+\sigma\bm{W}_{ij})\right\|\leq n+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{Z}\|\leq 2n

if
\sigma<c_{0}\sqrt{n}/(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})
where
\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{Z}\|=O(\sqrt{n}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}))
holds with high probability for all
1\leq i\leq n
implied by
[
58
, Theorem 4.4.5]
. Now we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-\bm{Q}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|+\|[\bm{A}\bm{Z}]_{i}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}-\bm{Q}\|

(use (
4.27
))

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta+\|(n\bm{I}_{d}+\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{Z})\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}-\bm{Q}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|+\|\bm{Q}(n\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}-\bm{I}_{n})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta+\sigma\|\bm{W}_{i}^{\top}\bm{Z}\|\|\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|+\|\bm{\Lambda}-n\bm{I}_{d}\|\|\bm{\Lambda}^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta+\sigma\sqrt{n}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})\cdot\frac{2}{n}+\sigma\|\bm{W}\|\cdot\frac{2}{n}\lesssim 6\eta

where
\|\bm{\Lambda}-n\bm{I}_{d}\|\leq\sigma\|\bm{W}\|
.
These arguments lead to (
4.28
) in Lemma
4.15
.

The next lemma is used to estimate the distance
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{S}^{0,m})
in (
4.9
). In fact, it suffices to estimate the distance between
\bm{\Phi}
and
\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}
, i.e., the top
d
eigenvectors of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}^{(m)}
respectively. Note that
\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}^{(m)}
differ only by one block column and row, and thus their eigenvectors are also very close, following from Davis-Kahan theorem.

Lemma 4.16

(Lemma 5.9 in
[
35
]
)
.

Let
\bm{\Phi}
and
\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}
be the top
d
eigenvectors of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}^{(m)}
with
\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}=(\bm{\Phi}^{(m)})^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}=n\bm{I}_{d}
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\bm{Q}_{m}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\|(\bm{I}-n^{-1}\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top})\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\|\lesssim\sigma n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})\cdot\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|.

where
\bm{Q}_{m}=\mathcal{P}((\bm{\Phi}^{(m)})^{\top}\bm{\Phi})
.
In other words,

n-\sigma_{\ell}\left(\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\right)\lesssim\sigma(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq d

and

\|(\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}(\bm{\Phi}^{(m)})^{\top}\bm{\Phi})^{1/2}-n\bm{I}_{d}\|\lesssim\sigma(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}\|.

Proof: .

The proof follows from setting
\bm{X}=\bm{A}
and
\bm{X}_{\bm{E}}=\bm{A}^{(m)}
and then applying Davis-Kahan perturbation bound in Theorem
4.9
.
∎

Now we will show that the initialization step produces
\bm{S}^{0}
which satisfies (
4.8
), (
4.9
), and (
4.10
).

Proof of Lemma
4.12
.

By the Davis-Kahan theorem with
\bm{X}=\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top}
and
\bm{X}_{\bm{E}}=\bm{X}+\sigma\bm{W}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\left\|\left(\bm{I}_{nd}-\frac{1}{n}\bm{\Phi}\bm{\Phi}^{\top}\right)\bm{Z}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n-\sigma\|\bm{W}\|}\cdot\|(\bm{A}-\bm{Z}\bm{Z}^{\top})\bm{Z}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma n^{-1}\cdot\|\bm{W}\bm{Z}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma n^{-1}\cdot 3\sqrt{nd}\cdot\sqrt{nd}\lesssim\sigma d

where
\|\bm{Z}\|_{F}=\sqrt{nd}
and
\bm{Q}=\mathcal{P}(\bm{Z}^{\top}\bm{\Phi})
.
Note that all the blocks of
\bm{\Phi}
have smallest singular values greater than 1/2, following from Lemma
4.15
. Thus

\displaystyle d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{Z})

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{\Phi})-\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{Z}\bm{Q})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{P}(\bm{\Phi}_{i})-\mathcal{P}(\bm{Q})\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bm{\Phi}_{i}-\bm{Q}\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{Z}\bm{Q}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma d

where the third equality follows from Lemma
4.8
.
Note that if

\sigma\leq c_{0}\epsilon\sqrt{n/d},

for some small constant
c_{0},
then
d_{F}(\bm{S}^{0},\bm{Z})\leq\epsilon\sqrt{nd}
which gives (
4.8
).

Also note that Lemma
4.16
implies

\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\bm{Q}_{m}\|_{F}\lesssim\sigma n^{-1/2}d^{1/2}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})

where
\bm{Q}_{m}=\mathcal{P}((\bm{\Phi}^{(m)})^{\top}\bm{\Phi})
and
|\sigma_{j}(\bm{\Phi}_{i})-1|\lesssim\sigma n^{-1/2}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})<1/2
for all
1\leq j\leq d
and
1\leq i\leq n.
As a result, we have (
4.9
) which follows from

\displaystyle\|\bm{S}^{0}-\bm{S}^{0,m}\bm{Q}_{m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{\Phi})-\mathcal{P}_{n}(\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\bm{Q}_{m})\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 8\|\bm{\Phi}-\bm{\Phi}^{(m)}\bm{Q}_{m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sigma n^{-1/2}d^{1/2}(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2}

provided that

\sigma<c_{0}\frac{\kappa\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\log n}}

for some small constant
c_{0}.

Then for the correlation between
\bm{W}_{m}
and
\bm{S}^{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}(\bm{S}^{0}-\bm{S}^{0,m}\bm{Q}_{m})\|_{F}+\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{W}_{m}\|\cdot\|\bm{S}^{0}-\bm{S}^{0,m}\bm{Q}_{m}\|_{F}+\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0,m}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sqrt{nd}\cdot\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{2}+\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})

with probability at least
1-n^{-\gamma^{2}/2}
following from Lemma
4.10

where all entries of
n^{-1/2}\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0,m}\in\hbox{\msbm{R}}^{d\times d}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables and
n^{-1}\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0,m}\|_{F}^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{d^{2}}.
Now taking the union bound over
1\leq m\leq n
gives

\displaystyle\|\bm{W}_{m}^{\top}\bm{S}^{0}\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{3\kappa}{2}\right)\sqrt{nd}(\sqrt{d}+\gamma\sqrt{\log n})

holds uniformly for all
1\leq m\leq n
with probability at least
1-n^{-\gamma^{2}/2+1}
with
\gamma=4.

∎

Rational Homotopy Type Of Complements Of Submanifold Arrangements
Alexander Zakharov

Abstract
In this paper we provide an explicit cdga controlling the rational homotopy type of the complement X − ∪ i Z i X-\cup_{i}Z_{i} where X X is a compact smooth algebraic variety and { Z i } \{Z_{i}\} is a collection of subvarieties such that all set-theoretical intersections are smooth. This generalizes the corresponding result of J. Morgan in the case where { Z i } \{Z_{i}\} forms a divisor with normal crossings. The model is given in terms of the cohomology of all intersections of Z i Z_{i} ’s, and the natural maps induced by the inclusions.



Introduction

0.1
Main results

Definition 0.1.1
.

An
arrangement

(X,Z_{*})

is a smooth compact algebraic variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}

and a collection
\{Z_{i}\subset X,i\in N\}
of subvarieties parametrized by
a finite set
N
such that the set-theoretical
intersections

Z_{I}=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}Z_{i},I\subset N
and
Z_{\emptyset}=X

are smooth
C^{\infty}
-submanifolds. (In other words, the corresponding
scheme-theoretic intersections with the reduced scheme structure are smooth subschemes.)
The complement of the arrangement
(X,Z_{*})
is the
variety
U=X-\cup_{i}Z_{i}
.

Let
A_{\PL}(-)\colon\Top\to\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}
denote the Sullivan functor (e.g.
[
FHT01
, §10]
).
A (cdga)
model
of
U
is a cdga
B^{\bullet}
quasi-isomorphic to

A_{\PL}(U)
in

\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}
, meaning there is a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms
B^{\bullet}\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)
.
In this paper, by the rational, respectively complex homotopy type of a space
X
we mean the quasi-isomorphism type of
A_{\PL}(X)
, respectively of its complex analog.

Definition 0.1.2
.

A collection
\{D_{i}\subset X\}
is called an
NCD

if all
D_{i}
are smooth and
\cup D_{i}
is a
divisor with normal crossings.

Consider an arrangement
(X,D_{*})
where
D_{*}
is an NCD.
Let
U=X-\cup_{i}D_{i},i\leq n
and
j\colon U\to X
be the inclusion.
Denote by
\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
the constant sheaf over
X
.
The following theorem
is due to J. Morgan
[
Mor78
]
.
It describes the
\mathbb{Q}
-homotopy type of the complement of
an NCD-arangement:

Theorem 0.1.3

(Morgan)
.

Let
E^{pq}_{2}=H^{p}(X;R^{q}j_{*}j^{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})
be
the second sheet of the Leray spectral sequence.
Then
E^{**}_{2}
is a model of
U
, i.e.
there is a quasi-isomorphism

E^{**}_{2}\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)\in\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

In particular Theorem
0.1.3
implies that

E_{3}=E_{\infty}
.
Note that if
(X,Z_{*})
is a smooth
arrangement with some
Z_{i}

of codimension
>1
, then
the Leray spectral sequence does not
degenerate at
E_{3}
. An example is

(\mathbb{P}^{2},pt)
.

In this work we will generalize
Theorem
0.1.3
to the case of an arbitrary
smooth arrangement

(X,Z_{*})
in a compact algebraic
X
,
providing a model for the
\mathbb{Q}
-homotopy type of
the complement
U=X-\cup_{i}Z_{i}
.
The corresponding model turns out to be functorial in the arrangement

(X,Z_{*})
(see Definition
2.5.3
).

To state the results let us introduce some notation.
In order to simplify sign issues
and make the product structure in what follows clear,
it will be convenient to order the set
N
and to
use the monomials

d\nu_{I}=d\nu_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge d\nu_{i_{k}}
for

I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\}
, viewed as degree
-|I|
elements of
the Grassmann algebra
\Lambda\langle d\nu_{i}\mid i\in N\rangle
.
Let
\iota_{i}
be the derivation of

\Lambda\langle d\nu_{i}\mid i\in N\rangle

of degree
1

determined by

\iota_{i}d\nu_{j}=\delta_{ij}
.
Note that the one dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}

and the derivation

\iota_{i}
of
\Lambda\langle d\nu_{i}\mid i\in N\rangle

are independent of the ordering of
N
.

The default coefficient ring for cohomology groups is

\mathbb{Q}
and will usually be omitted from notation.
Let
(X,Z_{*})
be a smooth compact algebraic
arrangement.
For
i\in I\subset N
, set
H^{*}_{Z_{I}}(X)=H^{*}(X,X-Z_{I})
, and let

g_{I-\{i\},I}:H^{*}_{Z_{I}}(X)\to H^{*}_{Z_{I-\{i\}}}(X)

denote the natural map induced by the embedding

Z_{I}\ari Z_{I-\{i\}}
.
The main result is the following

Theorem 0.1.4

(On the homotopy type of the complement,
Corollary
5.3.2
)
.

There is a second quadrant
multiplicative spectral sequence
(E^{pq}_{r},d_{r})
with these properties:

1.

We have
E^{pq}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{|I|=-p}H^{q}_{Z_{I}}(X)
, and

E^{**}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits_{I}H^{*}_{Z_{I}}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I},

is a cdga with the differential

d_{1}=\sum\limits^{i,I}_{i\in I}g_{I-\{i\},I}\otimes\iota_{i},

and the straightforward multiplication obeying the usual
sign rule.

2.

The terms
(E_{r},d_{r})
are functorial
in the arrangement
(X,Z_{*})
for
r>0
.

3.

We have
E_{2}=E_{\infty}
.

4.

There is a functorial quasi-isomorphism

E^{**}_{1}\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)\in\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}},

where both sides are considered as
functors of
(X,Z_{*})
.

See Example
B.0.2
of Appendix
B
for an explicit computation using this result.

To clarify the functoriality of the result,
assume
f\colon X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}X
is a morphism
and
g\colon N\xrightarrow{}N^{\prime}
is an injective map such that

f^{-1}Z_{i}\subset Z^{\prime}_{g(i)}
for all
i\in N
.
This data provides a morphism of arrangements
(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{*})\xrightarrow{}(X,Z_{*})
(see Definition
2.5.3
), inducing a natural morphism
E^{**}_{1}(X,Z_{i\in N})\xrightarrow{}E^{**}_{1}(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}_{i\in N^{\prime}})
.
On the other hand
f
restricts to a map of complements
U^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}U
.
The zig-zag provided by the Theorem
commutes with these morphisms.
In particular, if
(X,Z_{*})
admits a group action
which permutes the arrangement
Z_{*}
,
we obtain an expression for
the action on the rational homotopy type.

In the case of an NCD-complement this spectral sequnce
appears already in
[
Mor78
]
and
is related to the Leray spectral sequence in Theorem
0.1.3

via décalage (see Proposition
1.2.10
).

It is natural to look for a similar model for the rational homotopy type
which would better reflect
the combinatorics of the arrangement. One way to do this is
to consider for example the corresponding intersection poset.
The notion of a locally geometric lattice
was applied in
[
CLW21
]
to describe the cohomology ring of some
arrangement complements in terms of the Orlik-Solomon algebra and
the cohomology of smooth intersections.
We will prove here similar results about the rational homotopy type and without requiring
the intersections to be clean, i.e. to locally look like a
vector subspace arrangement.
Let
[X,L,\Mu]
be a smooth compact algebraic

multiplicative arrangement

on
X
given by a presheaf
(L,\leq)^{\op}\xrightarrow{}2^{X}
,
and suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a corresponding
OS-algebra

see Definitions
2.5.1
and
2.4.5
.
For simplicity one can assume
(L,\leq)
is a geometric lattice,
and take
\Mu=\OS(L)
to be the usual Orlik-Solomon algebra of
(L,\leq)

viewed as an
L
-graded object.
Let us mention that arrangements form a category
(Definition
2.5.3
).

An example of
(L,\leq)
is the cubical arrangement
\mathcal{C}

of all intersections
\mathcal{C}_{I}=\cap_{i\in I}Z_{i}

as in Theorem
0.1.4
.
For
x\in L
we denote by
L_{x}
the value of the presheaf on
x
. Note that
L_{0}=X
and
x\leq y\in L
implies
L_{x}\supset L_{y}\subset X
.
We require that

(L,\leq)
should be graded by a rank function
r

such that
r(0)=0
and

r(x)=r(y)+1
if
x
lies over
y
. The last condition is
denoted by

x:>y
.
For each
y<x\in L
there is a morphism

g_{yx}:H^{*}_{L_{x}}(X)\to H^{*}_{L_{y}}(X)
corresponding to
the embedding
L_{x}\hookrightarrow L_{y}\subset X
, and for each

y<:x
we have the
structure map

\partial_{yx}:\mathcal{M}_{x}\to\mathcal{M}_{y}
.

Set
U=X-\bigcup\limits^{x\in L}_{x>0}L_{x}
to be the complement. The following result is similar to Theorem 1.3 in
[
CLW21
]
,
and it also appears in the case of a clean hypersurface arrangement
in
[
Dup15
]
:

Theorem 0.1.5

(On the cohomology of the complement, theorem

5.2.1
)
.

There is a multiplicative spectral sequence,
functorial in the multiplicative arrangement
[X,L,\Mu]
,
of pure Hodge structures with
E^{pq}_{1}
of weight
q

with these properties:

1.

We have

E^{pq}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits^{x\in L}_{r(x)=-p}H^{q}_{L_{X}}(X)\otimes\mathcal{M}_{x},

and
(E^{**}_{1},d_{1})
is a cdga
with the differential given by

d_{1}=\sum\limits^{y,x}_{y<:x}g_{yx}\otimes\partial_{yx}

2.

We have
E_{2}=E_{\infty}
.

3.

There is a natural multiplicative isomorphism
of Hodge structures:

E^{pq}_{2}\simeq\Gr^{\Dec\ W}_{q}H^{p+q}(U)
.

4.

There is a multiplicative splitting

H^{*}(U)\simeq\Gr^{\Dec W}H^{*}(U)
of algebras.
In particular, there is an isomorphism of algebras

E^{**}_{2}\simeq H^{*}(U)
which
is functorial in

[X,L,\Mu]

Remark 0.1.6
.

Here
\Gr^{\Dec\ W}_{q}H^{n}(U)
denotes the usual
weight
q
piece of the mixed Hodge structure on

H^{n}(U)
, see Remark
1.2.7
for the
filtration décalée
\Dec\ W
.

This spectral sequence is called the
Mayer-Vietoris
spectral sequence
of the arrangement
[X,L,\Mu]
, and it also exists
in general topological setting (see Theorem
4.2.3
).
We will refer to the spectral sequence in Theorem
0.1.4

as the
cubical
Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence.

Remark 0.1.7
.

The above theorem is analogous to the fact that
the Leray spectral sequence of an open embedding
has a mixed Hodge structure (see
[
Ara05
]
).
Both results agree in the above-mentioned case of an open embedding of the
complement of an NCD arrangement.
In the general case the weight condition implies that the cdg-algebra
E^{**}_{r},r>0

can be considered as a pure Hodge
complex (a Hodge diagram in fact).
Considering
H^{*}(U)
as a mixed Hodge structure one can rephrase the statement of the theorem by saying that
there is a natural isomorphism of Hodge structures

E^{**}_{2}\simeq\Gr^{\Dec W}H^{*}(U)
.
We emphasize however that
H^{*}(U)
itself is
rarely pure, and one cannot in general recover
the mixed Hodge structure on
H^{*}(U)

from our model.

Assume the arrangement
[X,L,\OS(L)]
is locally geometric
(Definition
2.5.2
).
Our next result is an analog of
Theorem
0.1.4
that gives a more economical model which captures
the combinatorial structure of the given arrangement
(L,\leq)
.
We state it as an extension of Theorem
0.1.5
.

Theorem 0.1.8

(The lattice model for the homotopy type of the complement,
Theorem
5.3.3
)
.

We keep the notation of Theorem
0.1.5
. For a
locally geometric arrangement
[X,L]
, there is a quasi-isomorphism:

E^{**}_{1}\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)\in\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}

which is functorial in
[X,L]
.

We illustrate this result in Example
B.0.1
of Appendix
B
.

Since the cubical lattice is a geometric arrangement,
Theorem
0.1.4
is a formal corollary of
Theorem
0.1.8
.
In the course of the proof we will do the opposite and deduce
Theorem
0.1.8
from
Theorem
0.1.4
.

The proof is based on the notion of a mixed Hodge diagram
introduced by Morgan in
[
Mor78
]
and then developed in
a functorial form by Navarro Aznar in
[
Nav87
]
.
It is the cdga version of the mixed Hodge complex invented by
Deligne
[
Del71
]
.

A non-trivial property of a mixed Hodge diagram
(K,F,W)

is that it
produces a mixed Hodge

structure

(M,F,\Dec W)
on the minimal model
M
of
the cdga
K

[
Mor78
]
,
[
Cir15
]
.
In particular its weight
filtration admits
a natural Deligne splitting (Proposition
1.2.8
)
over
\mathbb{C}
, thus providing a natural
quasi-isomorphism
E_{1}(K_{\mathbb{C}},W)\zgqis E_{0}(K_{\mathbb{C}},\Dec W)
with

E_{0}(M,\Dec W)\otimes\mathbb{C}\simeq M\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
By a general result due to Morgan-Sullivan (e.g.
[
Mor78
, Theorem 10.1]
)
we have a non-canonical quasi-isomorphism

E_{1}(K_{\mathbb{Q}},W)\zgqis K_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

It is possible however to avoid using minimal models and
upgrade the statement to a lax symmetric
monoidal quasi-isomorphism
of functors
(K,F,W)\xrightarrow{}E_{1}(K_{\mathbb{Q}},W)
and
(K,F,W)\xrightarrow{}K_{\mathbb{Q}}

from
(\MHC,\otimes)
to
(\Ch(\Vect_{\mathbb{Q}}),\otimes)
.
We will adopt this approach due to Cirici and Horel
[
CH20
]
, see
Theorem
1.2.12
.

Let
j\colon U\to X
be the inclusion of the complement, let

\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}
denote the constant sheaf over
X
and let
[X,L,\Mu]

be a multiplicative arrangement (e.g. a cubical one).
The sheaf
Rj_{*}j^{*}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}
has a natural
resolution
\MV(L)\in\Mon(\Ch^{\leq 0}(\Sh_{X}))
in terms of the arrangement,
called the Mayer-Vietoris
complex (Definition
4.1.2
). The Mayer-Vietoris complex
is equipped with
the
rank filtration

W^{\prime}
, such that
E^{pq}_{1}(\MV(L),W^{\prime})

is described by Theorem
0.1.8
.
Though
E^{pq}_{1}(\MV(L),W^{\prime})

is a cdga,
\MV(L)
is non-commutative, and it does not admit a
structure of a Hodge complex on the nose.

The initial aim of this work
is to construct a Hodge diagram
(\MVHD,F,W^{\prime})
based on this resolution
and such that
E^{pq}_{1}(\MVHD,W^{\prime})=E^{pq}_{1}(\MV(L),W^{\prime})
.
Once this is done, Theorem
0.1.8
follows from Theorem
1.2.12
.

Let us mention some other approaches and related difficulties.
First of all, there is no available analog for
the log-de Rham complex for
an inclusion
X-\cup Z_{i}\to X
when
\codim Z_{i}/X>1
.
On the other hand, Dupont
[
Dup15
]
defined a version of the
log-de Rham complex

\Omega^{\bullet}\langle D_{i}\rangle\subset\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}

in the case
\codim D_{i}/X=1
and a hypersurface arrangement
D_{i}

is clean but not neccessary an NCD, i.e.

\bigcup D_{i}\subset X
locally looks like an
arrangement of hyperplanes.

In other concrete examples of interest, such as configuration spaces

U=F(M,n)\subset M^{n}
(see
[
FM94
]
,
[
Kri94
]
),
there is a direct approach which is to construct a compactification

\overline{U}
of
U
with an NCD complement

\overline{U}-U=\cup D_{i}
explicitly.
In this case the complex part of
the Hodge diagram is given by the usual
log-de Rham complex
\Omega^{*}(\ln\overline{U}-U)
.

Note that in general
a realization of
U=X-\cup Z_{i}
as an NCD-complement
\overline{U}-\cup D_{j}

uses
Hironaka’s resolution of singularities.
In the case of clean intersections one can also use wonderful compactification
[
DP95
]

introduced by de Concini-Procesi. These are more explicit in principle but still difficult to handle in examples.

To summarize, our approach is homological in nature and
does not use an
explicit construction of the resolution.
Thus in the case of an arbitrary arrangement with smooth
set-theoretical intersections
(i.e. regardless of whether or not the intersection poset
is locally geometric) we have a model for
the rational homotopy type of the complement
given by Theorem
5.3.2
.

0.2
Applications

We will now describe a few applications of our results. The first one generalizes the formality of the complement
of a vector
hyperplane arrangement, which can be traced back to works
of Arnold, Brieskorn, Orlik-Solomon and others, see
[
OS80
]
,
[
Bri73
]
.
Consider an arrangement of affine subspaces in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let
(L,\leq)
be the corresponding intersection poset
and
U=\mathbb{C}^{n}-\bigcup\limits_{x>0}L_{x}
be the complement.
Assume that
(L,\leq)
is a locally geometric lattice.
This is equivalent to
requiring that for all
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
the lattice

\{x\in L\mid L_{x}\ni p\}\subset L
is geometric.

Note that Theorem
0.1.5
provides an expression for
H^{*}(U)
.
We will prove the following

Theorem 0.2.1

(Theorem
6.1.1
)
.

The complement
U
is
formal.

Remark 0.2.2
.

In the case of a central arrangement, i.e. when
L
is a geometric lattice,
the theorem was proved by Feichtner-Yuzvinsky in
[
FY07
]
.
Note that an arrangement given by affine hyperplanes is always
locally geometric; this case was covered
by Dupont in

[
Dup16
, Theorem 3.7]
.

The next theorem generalizes the Kriz-Totaro model (see
[
Kri94
]
)
for the
rational homotopy type
of the configuration space of

n
-tuples of points on
a smooth compact algebraic variety
M
.
Namely, for any finite unoriented graph
G
one can consider the

chromatic
configuration space

F(M,G)
of points

x_{i}\in M
labeled by
vertices

i\in V(G)
with the condition
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
for each edge
\{ij\}\in E(G)
.
Let
\Delta_{ab}\subset M^{n}
be the diagonal
x_{a}=x_{b}

and
[\Delta_{ab}]\in H^{2\dim M}(M^{n})
the corresponding
cohomology class.

Remark 0.2.3
.

The word “chromatic” is justified by the fact that

\chi(F(M,G))=p_{G}(\chi(M))
, where
p_{G}
is the chromatic polynomial of

G
and
\chi
is any motivic measure on the Grothendieck ring of varieties.

Theorem 0.2.4

(Theorem
6.3.1
)
.

The rational homotopy type of
F(M,G)

has a model equal to the cdga over
H^{*}(M^{V(G)})
given by the quotient

H^{*}(M^{V(G)})\otimes\Lambda^{*}\langle\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}\rangle/\mathcal{J}

where
\langle\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}\rangle
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by generators

\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}
of degree

2\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M-1
for all
ordered
pairs
(a,b)\in E(G)
,
and
\mathcal{J}
is the ideal generated by the following relations

1.

\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}=\tilde{\Delta}_{ba}
;

2.

\partial(\tilde{\Delta}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{\Delta}_{i_{k-1}i_{k}})=0

for each simple cycle
(i_{1},i_{2}),\ldots,(i_{k-1},i_{k}),(i_{k},i_{1})\in E(G)
in
G
;

3.

p^{*}_{a}(\gamma)\cdot\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}=p^{*}_{b}(\gamma)\cdot\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}
for all
\gamma\in H^{*}(M)
and
(a,b)\in E(G)
.

The differential
d
is zero on
H^{*}(M^{V(G)})
, and we have

d\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}=[\Delta_{ab}]\in H^{2\dim M}(M^{V(G)})
where
[\Delta_{ab}]

is the Poincaré dual class of the diagonal
[\Delta_{ab}]
.

We define an action of
Aut(G)
on our model by setting

\sigma.\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}=\tilde{\Delta}_{\sigma(a)\sigma(b)},\sigma\in Aut(G)
and using the permutation action on
M^{V(G)}
. With this definition, the model is
Aut(G)
-equivariant in the sense that there is a zig-zag of
Aut(G)
-equivariant quasi-isomorphisms connecting the model and
A_{PL}(F(M,G))
.

Remark 0.2.5
.

Assume the action of
Aut(G)
on
F(M,G)
is free, i.e.
G
is the complete graph on
n=|V(G)|
elements, and so
F(M,G)=F(M,n)
, the configuration space of ordered
n
-tuples of points of
M
. Then our model allows one to recover the
\Sigma_{n}
-equivariant rational homotopy type of
F(M,n)
, see
[
Tri82
]
.

0.3
Organization of the paper

The work is organized as follows. In Section
1
we recall
basic facts about mixed Hodge complexes and
state a result about
E_{1}
-formality of these, see
Theorem
1.2.12
.
Then, in Section
2
we review and/or prove a few facts about lattices which we need in order to
treat the general case of our theorem beyond the case of the cubical lattice.
There we introduce
(L,\leq)
-chain algebras and define
Orlik-Solomon algebras in this context.
In Section
3
we describe a functorial construction
of mixed Hodge diagram
(\mathcal{K}_{U/X},F,W)\in\MHD(X)
associated with
a variety
U
over a smooth proper
X

following Navarro Aznar
[
Nav87
]
.

In Section
4
we construct the Mayer-Vietoris resolution
in the topological setting and describe the corresponding spectral sequence
in Theorem
4.2.3
. In Section
5.1
we apply the
\Cech
construction from Section
4.3
to construct a mixed Hodge diagram
\MVHD(X,\mathcal{C})\in\MHD(X)
, which we use to prove the main Theorem
5.3.2
. Finally, in Section
6
we prove the applications which we described above.

Here we recall some general preliminaries which

Let

Define

\displaystyle Z^{pq}_{r}

\displaystyle:=\ker[W^{p}K^{*}\to K^{*}/W^{p+r}K^{*}]

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle B^{pq}_{r}

\displaystyle:=dW^{p-r+1}+W^{p+1}.

(1.1.2)

We note that:

dZ^{pq}_{r}\subset Z^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}

dB^{pq}_{r}\subset B^{p+r,q-r+1}_{r}

associated with filtration

\displaystyle{}_{W}E^{pq}_{r}=\frac{Z^{pq}_{r}}{Z^{pq}_{r}\cap B^{pq}_{r}}

(1.1.3)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{Z^{pq}_{r}+B^{pq}_{r}}{B^{pq}_{r}}.

(1.1.4)

with differential

One has a natural isomorphism:

H^{*}(E_{r},d_{r})\simeq E_{r+1}.

(1.1.5)

A non trivial observation is that the realization of

in

Namely,

.

Let

be another filtration on

.

induced by

(

) and (

) respectively.
On the other hand, since

one can define a filtration

on each

inductively using

.
This defines three natural filtrations on

(

[

2.

(on three filtrations;
[

[

Assume

•

Proof.

We will use the idea of the following proof in the sequel, so we reproduce it here. It is enough to show that

Note that

So

Hence

Let

(L,\leq)
-objects (respectively

-filtered complexes).

Theorem 2.4.6

(Orlik-Solomon;

If

Proof.

For all

Following

]

Then

Proposition 2.4.7

(Functoriality of OS-complexes)
.

Let
[L,\Mu],[L^{\prime},\Mu^{\prime}]
be locally graded posets with OS-complexes

\Mu,\Mu^{\prime}
respectively.
For a rank preserving map
g\colon(L,\leq)\xrightarrow{}(L^{\prime},\leq)
,
there is a unique homomorphism
g_{*}\Mu\xrightarrow{}\Mu^{\prime}
of
(L^{\prime},\leq)
-chain
complexes inducing
\id\colon g_{*}\Mu_{0}=\Bbbk\to\Mu_{0}=\Bbbk
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4.3
,

\Hom(g_{*}\Mu,\Mu^{\prime})=\Hom((g_{*}\Mu)_{0},\Mu^{\prime}_{0})
.
Clearly
(g_{*}\Mu)_{0}=\Mu^{\prime}_{0}=\Bbbk
.
∎

In particular, if
(L,\leq)
admits an OS-complex
\Mu
, then it is
unique up to a canonical isomorphism.
One can say that
g\colon L\xrightarrow{}L^{\prime}
induces a natural
morphism

[L,\Mu]\xrightarrow{}[L^{\prime},\Mu^{\prime}]
.

Corollary 2.4.8
.

Assume
g\colon L\xrightarrow{}L^{\prime}
is a
homomorphism of locally geometric lattices.
Then there is a natural homomorphism of
(L^{\prime},\leq)
-chain algebras

g_{*}\OS(L)\xrightarrow{}\OS(L^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to consider the case of globally geometric lattices.
We have the induced homomorphism

G\colon\Lambda\langle\Atoms(L)\rangle\xrightarrow{g}\Lambda\langle\Atoms(L^{\prime})\rangle.

Since
g
maps
\Atoms(L)
to
\Atoms(L^{\prime})

(see Remark
2.1.16
),

G
commutes with the Koszul differential
\partial
.
Following the proof of Theorem
2.4.6
, let

\mathcal{J}=\langle dep\rangle+\partial\langle dep\rangle
, and
similarly for
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
.
On the level of algebras we have

\Tot(g_{*}\OS(L))=\Tot(\OS(L))=\Lambda\langle\Atoms(L)\rangle/\mathcal{J},

and
\Tot(\OS(L^{\prime}))=\Lambda\langle\Atoms(L^{\prime})\rangle/\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
.
Clearly
g
preserves dependent subsets of atoms, hence

g\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
.
We obtain a homomorphism of cdga’s

G\colon\Tot(g_{*}\OS(L))\xrightarrow{}\Tot(\OS(L^{\prime}))
.
On the other hand,
G
is
L^{\prime}
-graded, so
it follows that
G
is induced by
a morphism of
(L^{\prime},\leq)
-chain algebras

G_{\bullet}\colon g_{*}\OS(L)\xrightarrow{}\OS(L^{\prime})
.
Since
G_{\bullet}
satifies
G_{0}=\id_{\Bbbk}
, this
completes the proof.
∎

Hence a homorphism
g\colon(L,\leq)\xrightarrow{}(L^{\prime},\leq)
of locally geometric lattices
induces a
multiplicative
morphism
[L,\OS(L)]\xrightarrow{}[L^{\prime},\OS(L^{\prime})]
.

3.2
Thom-Whitney-Sullivan totalization functor

Recall that we use

to denote any of several similar categories of (multi)filtered complexes in

If

is additive symmetric monoidal.
We will now discuss the Thom-Whitney-Sullivan functor
s_{TW}

As a motivation consider the Moore complex functor

s\colon\cs{\ChF(\Sh_{X})}\to\ChF(\Sh_{X})
.
The Alexander-Whitney formula
makes

a lax (non-symmetric) monoidal functor, in particular we get a functor

s\colon\cs\CMon(\ChF(\Sh_{X}))\to\Mon(\ChF(\Sh_{X})),

which takes values in non-commutative monoids.
The functor

can be though as a symmetric monoidal refinement of

.
Below we summarize its main properties.

Proposition 3.2.1

(
[
Nav87
, 2.2, 3.3, 6.12]
)
.

There is a lax symmetric monoidal functor

s_{TW}\colon(\cs\ChF(\Vect_{\mathbb{Q}}),\otimes)\to(\ChF(\Vect_{\mathbb{Q}}),\otimes),

and a natural filtered quasi-isomorphism

s_{TW}\zgqis s
.
The functor
s_{TW}
is exact on chain complexes.
This induces a lax symmetric
monoidal functor

s_{TW}\colon\cs\ChF(\mathbb{Q}-\Sh_{X})\to\ChF(\mathbb{Q}-\Sh_{X})

defined by
(s_{TW}\mathcal{F})(U)=s_{TW}(\mathcal{F}(U))
for each

\mathcal{F}\in\ChF(\mathbb{Q}-\Sh_{X})
and any open
U\subset X
.

For continous

the exactness of

implies
s_{TW}\circ f^{*}=f^{*}\circ s_{TW}

and
s_{TW}\circ f_{*}=f_{*}\circ s_{TW}

Let

denote the Godement
cosimplicial construction (
[
God58
, Appendice]
).
Then
G=s\circ G_{\cs}
is the Godement resolution.
If

is continuous, then there is natural morphism

G_{\cs}f_{*}\to f_{*}G_{\cs}
and by the exactness of
f^{*}
,
a natural quasi-isomorphism
f^{*}G_{\cs}\to G_{\cs}f^{*}

Definition 3.2.2
.

Set

\displaystyle G_{TW}

\displaystyle=s_{TW}\circ G_{\cs},

\displaystyle R_{TW}f_{*}

\displaystyle=s_{TW}\circ f_{*}\circ G_{\cs}=f_{*}\circ G_{TW},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{TW}

\displaystyle=s_{TW}\circ\Gamma\circ G_{\cs}=\Gamma\circ G_{TW}.

Note that in case when
j
is an open inclusion
we have a natural isomorphism
j^{*}G_{\cs}\simeq G_{\cs}j^{*}
,
in particular
R_{TW}j_{*}j^{*}\simeq j_{*}j^{*}G_{TW}
.

Let us call a complex

filtered soft

for any filtration

,
the filtered components

.

Proposition 3.2.3

(
[
Nav87
, 4.5, 4.7, 6.13]
)
.

The functor

G_{TW}\colon(\ChF(\mathbb{Q}-\Sh_{X}),\otimes)\to(\ChF(\mathbb{Q}-\Sh_{X}),\otimes)

is lax symmetric monoidal and provides
a filtered soft resolution,
i.e. there is a natural

quasi-isomorphism

for all

.

Remark 3.2.4
.

In particular given morphism

induces a natural morphism

where the middle arrow is induced by

Remark 3.2.5

The quasi-isomorphism

However for

are the natural homomorphisms for
x^{\prime}<:x\in L
and

\partial_{x^{\prime}x}
are the structure maps of
\Mu

3.

({}_{W^{\prime}}E^{**}_{r},d_{r}),r>0

is a complex of Hodge structures
with
({}_{W^{\prime}}E^{pq}_{r},F),r>0

a pure Hodge structure of weight
q
.

4.

There is a natural isomorphism of
pure Hodge structures of weight
q
:

{}_{W^{\prime}}E^{pq}_{2}={}_{W^{\prime}}E^{pq}_{\infty}\simeq\Gr^{\Dec W}_{q}H^{p+q}(U).

5.

The spectral sequence is
multiplicative with respect to the
mixed Hodge structures.

6.

There is a functorial isomorphism of algebras:

({}_{W^{\prime}}E^{**}_{2},W^{\prime})\simeq(H^{*}(U;\mathbb{Q}),W).

Proof.

Let
\MVHC:=\MVHC(X,L)
,
\MV:=\MV((i_{*}i^{!})G(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}),\Mu)
.
By Proposition
4.5.8

we have a natural
W^{\prime}
-filtered quasi-isomorphism:

(\MVHC,W^{\prime})\zgqis(\MV,W^{\prime})\in\Ch(\Sh_{X}).

By Theorem
1.2.18
we have

{}_{W^{\prime}}E^{**}_{2}\MVHC={}_{W^{\prime}}E^{**}_{\infty}\MVHC
.
The terms
E^{pq}_{r}(\MV,W^{\prime}),r>0
are described by
Theorem
4.2.3
, providing
a natural isomorphism

{}_{W^{\prime}}E^{pq}_{\infty}(\MV)\simeq\Gr^{p}_{W^{\prime}}H^{p+q}(U;\mathbb{Q})

which is automatically
multiplicative
.

By Lemma
5.1.7
the filtrations
W
and
W^{\prime}

on

coincide, hence we have
a natural multiplicative isomorphism

{}_{W^{\prime}}E^{pq}_{2}(\MV)\simeq\Gr^{p}_{W}H^{p+q}(U)=\Gr^{\Dec W}_{q}H^{p+q}(U)

which is compatible with the Hodge filtration
F
on both sides.

Finally, the splitting provided by
Corollary
1.2.14
implies a functorial
multiplicative isomorphism

\Gr^{\Dec W}H^{*}(U;\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{*}(U,\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

Remark 5.2.2
.

Assertions 1-6 of the previous Proposition hold without assuming
\Mu
has a multiplicative structure,
i.e. additively it is sufficient for
\Mu
to be an OS-complex.

This result is also stated in
[

]
.
We want to note that the

of

fact based on Theorem

due to Cirici and Horel.
For example over
\mathbb{R}

while the Morgan-Sullivan theorem
[

a priori provides
only a non-canonical splitting.

Assume

\Gamma_{TW}(\MVHD(X,\mathcal{C}))\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)\in\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}.

(5.3.1)

By the naturality of the construction,

(\MVHD(X,\mathcal{C}),F,W^{\prime})
and

\MHD(X)

Recall that

Theorem 5.3.2

(The main theorem)
.

There is a multiplicative spectral sequence
E^{pq}_{r}

with the following properties.

1.

We have
E^{pq}_{1}=\bigoplus_{|I|=-p}H^{q}_{Z_{I}}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}

and

The multiplication

4.

There is a functorial quasi-isomorphism

E^{**}_{1}\zgqis A_{\PL}(U)\in\CDGA_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Proof.

Let
E_{r}={}_{W^{\prime}}E_{r}(\MVHD(X,\mathcal{C}))
.

Finally, assume

We have

(The main theorem for lattices)
.

For the multiplicative spectral sequence

in Theorem

.

6.1
Formality of a subspace arrangement with locally geometric intersection poset

Consider a collection
\{Z_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
of affine subspaces.
Let
(L,\leq)
be the corresponding intersection poset, i.e. the elements of
L
are given by
all
different
non-empty intersections
Z_{I}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},I\subset\Atoms(L)
.

Assume
(L,\leq)
is locally geometric.
This is equivalent to
requiring that for all
p\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
the lattice

\{x\in L\mid L_{x}\ni p\}\subset L
is geometric (Definition
2.1.12
).
In this section we will prove the following

Theorem 6.1.1
.

The complement
U=\mathbb{C}^{n}-\bigcup\limits_{i}Z_{i}
is
formal.

Note that, since for all
x\in L
, the mixed Hodge structure
H^{k}_{L_{x}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is pure of weight
k
, the result trivially follows by Remark
5.3.4
.
However, for completeness we give a direct proof based on Theorem
5.3.2
.

Let

X:=\mathbb{P}^{n}\supset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and

\tilde{L}
be the intersection poset obtained by taking
the closure of
\{Z_{i}\}
in
X

and adding the hyperplane at infinity

\tilde{L}_{\infty}:=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
So
\Atoms(\tilde{L})=\Atoms(L)\sqcup\{\infty\}
.
Clearly,

U=\mathbb{P}^{n}-\bigcup\limits_{x\in\tilde{L}}\tilde{L}_{x}=\mathbb{C}^{n}-\bigcup\limits_{x\in L}L_{x}.

Remark 6.1.2
.

Note that
\tilde{L}
is not locally geometric in general.
For example consider the intersection poset
(L,\leq)
given by
a plane and a line in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
with empty intersection.
In this case
\tilde{L}
is not graded and its description is given by
Part 5 of Example
2.5.7
.

Let us first describe a simple model for the
\mathbb{Q}
-homotopy type of
U

without any assumptions on
(L,\leq)
using the cubical model.
By Theorem
5.3.2
,
the following cdga models the rational
homotopy type of
U
:

K:=\left(\bigoplus\limits_{I\subset\Atoms(\tilde{L})}H^{*}_{\tilde{L}_{I}}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\cdot d\nu_{I},\partial\right).

Recall that
d\nu_{I}
has degree
-|I|
and the
differential
\partial=\sum\limits_{i}\iota_{i}
is
the whole Koszul differential.

Since
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})=\mathbb{Q}[t]/t^{n+1},\deg t=2

and
\tilde{L}_{I}

is a projective subspace, one can identify
the cohomology with support
H^{*}_{\tilde{L}_{I}}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})

with

t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}/\mathbb{P}^{n}}H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
as a graded
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
-module, where by definition

\codim\emptyset/\mathbb{P}^{n}=n+1
.
This description is compatible with the algebra structure on
K
.
The element

t^{\codim L_{I}}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})

corresponds to the Thom class
\tau_{I}\in H^{*}_{\tilde{L}_{I}}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
.

Further, since taking the direct product with an affine space
does not change the homotopy type of the affine complement
and the codimension of the subspaces in the arrangement,
one may assume from the beginning that
for any
x\in L\subset\tilde{L}
, we have

\tilde{L}_{x}\cap\tilde{L}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, i.e. all
subspaces
L_{x}
are at least one dimensional. Note that for
I\subset\Atoms(L)\subset\Atoms(\tilde{L})
we have by definition
\tilde{L}_{I}=\cap_{i\in I}\tilde{L}_{i}
, which could be non-empty even if
L_{I}=\emptyset
.

Consider the following complexes over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
,

1.

K=\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(\tilde{L})}t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\mathbb{Q}[t]/t^{n+1}\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}
,

2.

K^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(\tilde{L})}_{\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset}t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\mathbb{Q}[t]\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}
,

3.

K^{\prime\prime}=\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(\tilde{L})}_{\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset}t^{n+1}\mathbb{Q}[t]\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}

equipped with the Koszul differential.
We define a cdga structure on
K^{\prime}
by setting
d\nu_{I}\cdot d\nu_{J}=s(I,J)d\nu_{IJ}

where
s(I,J)=0
if
\tilde{L}_{IJ}=\emptyset
and
the sign of the shuffle
(I,J)
in
IJ
otherwise. The multiplication
is well defined since

\codim L_{I}+\codim L_{J}\geq\codim L_{IJ}
.

Clearly the natural projection
K^{\prime}\xrightarrow{}K
is a morphism of cdga’s
with the kernel
K^{\prime\prime}
.

Lemma 6.1.3
.

The complex
K^{\prime\prime}
is acyclic.

Proof.

Note that for
I\subset\Atoms(L)
, we have
\tilde{L}_{I}=\emptyset

iff
\tilde{L}_{I\infty}=\emptyset
.
Indeed, if
\tilde{L}_{I}\cap\tilde{L}_{\infty}=\emptyset
and
\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset
,
then
\tilde{L}_{I}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
\tilde{L}_{I}=L_{I}
, but by our
assumption
L_{I}\neq\emptyset
is at least one dimensional.

We can write

K^{\prime\prime}=\left[\bigoplus\limits_{I\subset\Atoms(L)}d\nu_{I\infty}\xrightarrow{\iota_{\infty}}\bigoplus\limits_{I\subset\Atoms(L)}d\nu_{I}\right]\cdot t^{n+1}\mathbb{Q}[t]

as a bicomplex with two columns.
Since the horizontal differential
\iota_{\infty}
is acyclic, the claim follows.
∎

Let
s\colon K^{\prime}\to K^{\prime}[-1]
be multiplication by
the element

td\nu_{\infty}\in K^{\prime}
.
Note that in general
sK^{\prime}\not\subset tK^{\prime}
,
(e.g.
d\nu_{\infty}\not\in K^{\prime}
).

Lemma 6.1.4
.

1.

The commutator
[s,\partial]\colon K^{\prime}\to K^{\prime}

is multiplication by
t
.

2.

The subcomplex
sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime}\subset K^{\prime}
is an acyclic ideal.

Proof.

1. This is straighforward.

2. Clearly,
sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime}
is a subcomplex and an ideal of
K^{\prime}
.
Let
sc_{1}+tc_{2},c_{1},c_{2}\in K^{\prime}
be a cocycle, i.e.

0=\partial(sc_{1}+tc_{2})=-s\partial c_{1}+tc_{1}+t\partial c_{2}.

Then, since
s^{2}=0
, we have
t(sc_{1}+s\partial c_{2})=0
, and hence

sc_{1}+s\partial c_{2}=0
.
So

\partial(sc_{2})=-s\partial c_{2}+tc_{2}=sc_{1}+tc_{2}

is
a coboundary.
∎

By the lemmas above, the rational homotopy type of
U

is given by the cdga
K^{\prime}/(sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime})
.

Lemma 6.1.5
.

There is an isomorphism of complexes:

K^{\prime}/(sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime})\simeq\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(L)}_{\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset}\mathbb{Q}\cdot t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I}\oplus\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(L)}_{\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset,L_{I}=\emptyset}\mathbb{Q}\cdot t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I\infty}.

(6.1.6)

Proof.

Assume
\tilde{L}_{I}\neq\emptyset
for some
I\subset\Atoms(L)
.
There are two cases. If
L_{I}=\emptyset
, then
\tilde{L}_{I}=\tilde{L}_{I\infty}
, and

s(t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}d\nu_{I})=\pm t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I\infty}+1}d\nu_{I\infty}.

If
L_{I}\neq\emptyset
, then
\codim\tilde{L}_{I}=1+\codim\tilde{L}_{I\infty}
, and

s(t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I})=\pm t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I\infty}}d\nu_{I\infty}.

This immediately implies the required description for
K^{\prime}/(sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime})
and also equips
the RHS in (
6.1.6
) with an obvious cdga structure.
∎

To proceed let
\mathcal{Q}
denote the locally cubical lattice corresponding to
L
, and let

p\colon(\mathcal{Q},\leq)\xrightarrow{}(L,\leq)
be the natural
projection (Proposition
2.4.11
).
Since
(L,\leq)
is an intersection poset,
the vertices of
\mathcal{Q}
are given by
I\in\mathcal{Q}
, where

I\subset\Atoms(L)
are such that
L_{I}\neq\emptyset
.
The cdga
\Tot\big(p^{*}H_{L_{\bullet}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\OS(\mathcal{Q})\big)
is given by

\bigoplus\limits^{I\subset\Atoms(L)}_{L_{I}\neq\emptyset}t^{\codim L_{I}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}\cdot d\nu_{I},

where the product of
t^{\codim L_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I}
with
t^{\codim L_{J}}\cdot d\nu_{J}

is zero unless
\codim L_{I}+\codim L_{J}=\codim L_{IJ}
.

Finally, for all sup-lattices
L
we obtain the following:

Proposition 6.1.7
.

There is a quasi-isomorphism of cdga’s:

\phi\colon K\xrightarrow{}\Tot\big(p^{*}H_{L_{\bullet}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\OS(\mathcal{Q})\big).

Proof.

Note that
H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
-module where

t\in H^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
acts by zero.
The required homomorphism of algebras is given by

1.

\phi(t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I})=0

if
I\ni\infty
or
L_{I}=\emptyset
, and

2.

\phi(t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I})=t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I}

otherwise.

So
\phi
factorizes through
\phi^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}/(sK^{\prime}+tK^{\prime})\xrightarrow{}p^{*}H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})*\OS(\mathcal{Q})
.
In the notation of (
6.1.6
) we have:

\ker\phi^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits^{I\in\mathcal{Q}}_{L_{I}=\emptyset}t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I}\oplus\bigoplus\limits^{I\in\mathcal{Q}}_{L_{I}=\emptyset}t^{\codim\tilde{L}_{I}}\cdot d\nu_{I\infty}.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
6.1.3
, it is easy to see that
\ker\phi^{\prime}
is acyclic,
and the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1.1
.

Now assume
(L,\leq)
is a geometric lattice.
By the above we have a chain of quasi-isomorphisms:

K\xrightarrow{\phi}\Tot(p^{*}H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}\otimes\OS(\mathcal{Q}))=\Tot(p_{*}(p^{*}H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}\otimes\OS(\mathcal{Q})))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}\Tot(H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}\otimes p_{*}\OS(\mathcal{Q}))\xrightarrow{}\\
\Tot(H^{*}_{L_{\bullet}}\otimes\OS(L))=:\mathcal{T},

where the equality is tautological, and in last two arrows we have used the projection formula

2.2.26
and the formality
of the atomic complex
2.4.14
.

It remains to observe that the differential in
\mathcal{T}
vanishes since
H^{*}_{L_{x}}
is concentrated in degree
2\codim L_{x}
,
while
y<:x\in L
implies
\codim L_{x}>\codim L_{y}
.

Hence the rational homotopy type of
U

is modeled by the following cdga with trivial differential

\mathcal{T}=\bigoplus\limits_{x\in L}\tau^{\codim L_{x}}\cdot\OS(L)_{x}.

Here the algebra structure on the RHS is uniquely determined by
the algebra structure of the OS-algebra
\OS(L)
, and
by requiring that we have

\tau^{\codim L_{x}}\OS(L)_{x}\cdot\tau^{\codim L_{y}}\OS(L)_{y}=0
for any
non-tranversal
x,y\in L
, i.e. for
x,y
such that

\codim L_{x}\cap L_{y}<\codim L_{x}+\codim L_{y}
.
∎

6.2
Chromatic configuration spaces

For a topological space
X
and a finite (non-oriented) graph
G
with the set of
vertices
V(G)=\{1,\ldots,n\}
we define the
chromatic configuration space

F(X;G)
by setting

F(X;G):=X^{n}-\bigcup\limits_{\{a,b\}\in E(G)}\Delta_{\{ab\}}

where
E(G)
is the set of edges of
G
and

\Delta_{\{ab\}}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X^{n}\mid x_{a}=x_{b}\}
.
We have a natural action of
Aut(G)
on
F(X;G)
.
As an immediate application of Theorem
4.2.3

let us describe a spectral sequence converging to
H^{*}(F(X;G);\mathbb{Z})
.

Following part 2 of Example
2.4.9
we consider the intersection poset

\Delta:=\Delta^{G}=(\Delta_{*},\leq)
with
\Atoms(\Delta)
given by

\Delta_{\{ab\}}
for each
\{a,b\}\in E(G)
.
An element
x=\Delta_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}}\in\Delta
is an unordered partition

\sqcup_{i\leq k}S_{i}=V(G)
such that each
S_{i}\subset G
is a non-empty and connected subset.
If
T=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}\subset E(G)
is a collection of edges,
then
\sup(T)\in\Delta
corresponds to a partition of
V(G)
given by the
vertices of the connected components of the graph
G_{T}:=(V(G_{T})=V(G),E(G_{T})=T)
.

The rank function
r
is defined by
r(\Delta_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}})=\sum\limits_{i\leq k}(|S_{i}|-1)
.
A set
\{\Delta_{e_{1}},\ldots,\Delta_{e_{k}}\}
of atoms for
e_{i}\in E(G)=\Atoms(\Delta)
is independent iff
the union of the edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
contains no cycles.

Recall that
\OS(\Delta)
is the quotient
\Lambda\langle\Delta_{\{ab\}}\rangle/\mathcal{J}
where
\deg(\Delta_{\{ab\}})=-1
and
\mathcal{J}
is the ideal generated by the elements

\partial(\Delta_{\{i_{1}i_{2}\}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\Delta_{\{i_{k}i_{1}\}}),

for all cycles given by a sequence of edges
\{i_{1},i_{2}\},\ldots,\{i_{k-1},i_{k}\},\{i_{k},i_{1}\}\in E(G)
.

By Theorem
4.2.3
,
for a paracompact, locally contractible topological space
X
and
for any coefficient ring
\Bbbk
we have an
Aut(G)
-equivariant spectral sequence

E^{pq}_{1}=\bigoplus\limits^{x\in\Delta}_{r(x)=-p}H^{q}_{\Delta_{x}}(X^{n};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\OS(\Delta)\Rightarrow H^{*}(F(X;G);\Bbbk).

Now assume
\Bbbk
is a field and
X
is a
\Bbbk
-oriented topological manifold
of dimension

K\sm I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}=K\sm I\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}\mbox{ }\supset\mbox{ }p\mbox{ }\sqcup\mbox{ }(KK^{\prime}\sm II^{\prime})=KK^{\prime}\sm(I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }I^{\prime})

K-p\sm I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}=K\sm I\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}\mbox{ }\supset\mbox{ }p\mbox{ }\sqcup\mbox{ }(KK^{\prime}\sm II^{\prime})=(K-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime})\sm(I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }I^{\prime}).

In particular
m(\alpha_{p}-\beta_{p})=(\iota_{p}d\mu_{I}d\mu_{I^{\prime}})^{KK^{\prime}}-(\iota_{p}d\mu_{I}d\mu_{I^{\prime}})^{(K-p)\cup K^{\prime}}=0
since
K-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}=KK^{\prime}
.

If
p\not\in K^{\prime}
, then using

K\sm I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}=p\sqcup(K\sm I\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime})\mbox{ }\supsetneq\mbox{ }p\sqcup(KK^{\prime}\sm II^{\prime})=KK^{\prime}\sm(I-p\mbox{ }\sqcup\mbox{ }I^{\prime})

we get
m(\alpha_{p})=0
, while

K-p\sm I-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}=K\sm I\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime}\sm I^{\prime}\mbox{ }\supsetneq\mbox{ }KK^{\prime}\sm II^{\prime}=(K-p\mbox{ }\cup\mbox{ }K^{\prime})\sm(I-p\mbox{ }\sqcup\mbox{ }I^{\prime})

implies
m(\beta_{p})=0
.
Similarly we obtain
m(\alpha^{\prime}_{p})=m(\beta^{\prime}_{p})=0
.
Thus
A=0=B
.

This completes the proof that
\hat{A}^{N}
is a monoid in
\ChF
.
The component
\hat{m}_{I\subset K;I^{\prime}\subset K^{\prime}}

takes values in
W^{\prime}_{|KK^{\prime}|}\hat{A}^{N}
.
Hence
\Gr^{W^{\prime}}\hat{m}
is well defined and it is easy to see that
it is equal to the associated graded of
the convolution multiplication (
4.3.4
).

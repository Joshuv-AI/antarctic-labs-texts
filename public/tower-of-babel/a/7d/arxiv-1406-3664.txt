Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\tau\geq 0
and
\sigma>0
. Let
Q\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\tau}
be a function that is
real-valued on
\mathbb{R}
and let
Z_{Q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{t_{n}\}
denote the real and distinct
zeros of
Q
. Assume that if
\operatorname{card}Z_{Q}>1
then
\inf_{k\neq\ell}\left|t_{k}-t_{\ell}\right|>0
. Let

A_{s}(Q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\left[Q^{\prime}(t)\right]^{2}+s^{2}\left[Q(t)\right]^{2}\right),\qquad s\geq 0\,.

(1.4)

Then the following two statements hold true.

(a)
If
f\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
, then we have

|f(x)|\leq(\sigma+\tau)\left(A_{\sigma+\tau}(Q)\right)^{-1/2}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(1.5)

(b)
If equality holds in (
1.5
) for a point
x=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
for a function
f\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
that is real-valued on
\mathbb{R}
with
f\not\equiv 0
, then either
\left|(Qf)(x_{0})\right|=\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}
or
there exist two real constants
S
and
C
such that
|S|+|C|>0
and

Q(x)\,f(x)\equiv S\sin\left((\sigma+\tau)\,x\right)+C\cos\left((\sigma+\tau)\,x\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(1.6)

.

If equality holds in (

2.
Some Lemmas

We will discuss properties of
Q
and of other entire functions of
exponential type that are needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We
start with two simple properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

A_{s}(Q)
defined by (
1.4
) is a right-continuous nondecreasing function of
s\in[0,\infty)
.

(b)
If
Q
satisfies condition (
1.9
), then
\limsup_{x\to\infty}|Q(x)|>0

and
\limsup_{x\to-\infty}|Q(x)|>0
.

Proof.

(a) Clearly,
A_{s}(Q)
is a nondecreasing function of
s\in[0,\infty)
. Let

c\in[0,\infty)
be a fixed number. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there
exists
u_{0}=u_{0}(\varepsilon,c)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

A_{c}(Q)\geq\left[Q^{\prime}(u_{0})\right]^{2}+c^{2}\left[Q(u_{0})\right]^{2}-\varepsilon\,,

and, therefore,

A_{c}(Q)\leq A_{s}(Q)\leq\left[Q^{\prime}(u_{0})\right]^{2}+s^{2}\left[Q(u_{0})\right]^{2}\leq A_{c}(Q)+(s^{2}-c^{2})\left[Q(u_{0})\right]^{2}+\varepsilon\,,\qquad s\in(c,\infty)\,,

from which

A_{c}(Q)\leq\lim_{s\to c+}A_{s}(Q)\leq A_{c}(Q)+\varepsilon\,,

and, letting here
\varepsilon\to 0+
, proves right-continuity of
A_{s}(Q)
at
c
.

(b) Fix
s\geq\theta
. If
\limsup_{x\to\infty}|Q(x)|=0
, i.e.,

\lim_{x\to\infty}Q(x)=0
, then, for
x>0
and by the mean value
theorem,

|x|\inf_{t\in[x,\infty)}|Q^{\prime}(t)|\leq|Q(2x)-Q(x)|\,,

so that, dividing both sides by
|x|
and letting
x\to\infty
, we
get
4
4

4

Actually,
Q(x)=o(|x|)
as
x\to\infty
is sufficient here.

\liminf_{x\to\infty}|Q^{\prime}(x)|\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|Q(2x)-Q(x)|}{|x|}=0\,,

and then

\liminf_{x\to\infty}\left(\left[Q^{\prime}(x)\right]^{2}+s^{2}\left[Q(x)\right]^{2}\right)\leq\liminf_{x\to\infty}\left[Q^{\prime}(x)\right]^{2}+s^{2}\lim_{x\to\infty}\left[Q(x)\right]^{2}=0\,,

that contradicts (
1.9
). If
\limsup_{x\to-\infty}|Q(x)|=0
, then
apply the previous part to
Q(-x)
that also satisfies the conditions.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<\delta\leq L<\infty
. A measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is called

(L,\delta)
-dense if for every interval
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}
with
m(\Delta)=L
, we have
m\left(E\cap\Delta\right)\geq\delta
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<\delta\leq L<\infty
and
\sigma>0
. If the measurable set

E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is
(L,\delta)
-dense, then there is a constant
C_{1}>0

such that for every
f\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
the Remez-type inequality

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{1}\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(E)}

(2.1)

holds.

Lemma
2.3
is a special case Katsnelson’s
[
2
, Theorem 3,
p. 43]
where he proved it for polysubharmonic functions on

\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

5

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\tau\geq 0
and
\theta\geq\tau
. If
Q\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\tau}
satisfies
conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
), then there exists
\alpha^{\ast}>0

such that the set
E^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|Q(x)|\geq\alpha^{\ast}\right\}
is

(d/2,d/4)
-dense, where
d
is defined in (
1.10
).

Proof.

Let
s\geq\theta
, see (
1.9
). It follows immediately from
(
1.9
) that there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\left[Q^{\prime}(x)\right]^{2}+s^{2}\left[Q(x)\right]^{2}\geq C_{2}^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(2.2)

Next, given
\alpha>0
, the open set
E_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|Q(x)|<\alpha\}
can be represented as
E_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}}I
,
where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
is a family of pairwise disjoint open intervals
I
.
Then (
2.2
) shows that for
\alpha\in(0,C_{2}/s]
the set
E_{\alpha}
does
not contain zeros of
Q^{\prime}
. Therefore,
Q
is strictly monotone on each
interval
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
.

If
\alpha\in(0,C_{2}/s]
and
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
is a bounded interval then
|Q|

takes the same value at the endpoints of
I
so that
I
contains precisely
one zero of
Q
.

If
\alpha\in(0,C_{2}/s]
and
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
is an unbounded interval, say

(a,\infty)
, then,
Q
being monotone on
I
, the finite or infinite
\Gamma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\lim_{x\to\infty}Q(x)
exists and, by Lemma
2.1
(b),
\Gamma\neq 0
. Hence, if
\alpha>0
is sufficiently small, say,
0<\alpha<\alpha_{1}\leq C_{2}/s
then
I\not\subset E_{\alpha}
so that
I\not\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
. In
other words, if
0<\alpha<\alpha_{1}\leq C_{2}/s
then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
has no
unbounded components.

Summarizing the above, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in(0,C_{2}/s]
such that, for all

\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}]
, each interval
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
is bounded and it
contains precisely one zero
t_{I}
of
Q
. In particular, if
Q
has no real
zeros, then
E_{\alpha}=\emptyset
for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}]
.

It remains to estimate the length of each interval
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}
. Let

\alpha_{2}\in(0,C_{2}/s]
be the unique positive solution of the equation

\alpha/\sqrt{C_{2}^{2}-\alpha^{2}s^{2}}=d/8
.
Let
\alpha^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\min\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\}
.
Taking account of (
2.2
) and using the mean value theorem, we see that
for each
x\in I
there exists
\xi\in I
such that

|x-t_{I}|=\frac{|Q(x)|}{|Q^{\prime}(\xi)|}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{C_{2}^{2}-\alpha^{2}s^{2}}}\leq d/8,

whenever
0<\alpha\leq\alpha^{\ast}
. Hence
m(I)\leq d/4
for

\alpha\in(0,\alpha^{\ast}]
. Therefore, due to condition (
1.10
), for every
closed interval
\Delta
of length
d/2
, we have
m\left(\Delta\cap E_{\alpha^{\ast}}\right)\leq d/4
. This shows that
E^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\mathbb{R}\setminus E_{\alpha^{\ast}}
is
(d/2,d/4)
-dense.

Now we are in the position to prove the following Schur-type inequality.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\tau\geq 0
and
\theta\geq\tau
. Let
Q\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\tau}
satisfy
conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
). Let
\sigma>0
. Then there is
a constant
C_{3}>0
such that for every
f\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
we have

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{3}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Let the number
\alpha^{\ast}>0
and the
(d/2,d/4)
-dense set
E^{\ast}\subset\mathbb{R}
be chosen with the help of Lemma
2.4
. Then

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(E^{\ast})}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{\ast}}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}

(2.4)

so that (
2.3
) follows from Lemma
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(a).

Let
\tau
,
\sigma
, and
Q
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
f\in\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
and assume
f\not\equiv 0

\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty

The proof will consist of three short steps. Using Lemma

\theta=\sigma+\tau

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty

this will be used in all three steps.

Step 1a.
First, we assume that
f
is real-valued on
\mathbb{R}
and that
there exists
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|f(x^{\ast})|=\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}

Since

to get

\left[(Qf)^{\prime}(x)\right]^{2}+\left(\sigma+\tau\right)^{2}\left[(Qf)(x)\right]^{2}\leq(\sigma+\tau)^{2}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}\,,

see
[
1
, Theorem II, p. 555]
. Since
f^{\prime}(x^{\ast})=0
,
we can set

in (

) to arrive at

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=f^{2}(x^{\ast})\leq\frac{(\sigma+\tau)^{2}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}{\left[Q^{\prime}(x^{\ast})\right]^{2}+(\sigma+\tau)^{2}\left[Q(x^{\ast})\right]^{2}}\,,

that implies (
1.5
) immediately.

Step 2a.
Second, we assume that
f
is real-valued on
\mathbb{R}
but

|f|
doesn’t necessarily take its maximum value on
\mathbb{R}
. Given
\varepsilon>0

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left|f(x_{\varepsilon})\right|+\varepsilon

,

\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma+\delta}

\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}-\varepsilon\leq\left|f(x_{\varepsilon})\right|

\displaystyle(\sigma+\tau+\delta)\left(A_{\sigma+\tau+\delta}(Q)\right)^{-1/2}\left\|QF_{\delta}\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}

First, letting here

, we get

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq(\sigma+\tau+\delta)\left(A_{\sigma+\tau+\delta}(Q)\right)^{-1/2}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})},

) for

\mathbb{R}
. Then

\operatorfont{B\/}_{\sigma}
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, pick

\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left|f(x_{\varepsilon})\right|+\varepsilon

. Then

is
real-valued on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfies the relations
|G(x)|\leq|f(x)|
for

x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\left|G(x_{\varepsilon})\right|=\left|f(x_{\varepsilon})\right|
. Applying
(
1.5

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}-\varepsilon\leq\left|f(x_{\varepsilon})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|G(x_{\varepsilon})\right|\leq\left\|G\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}\leq

\displaystyle(\sigma+\tau)\left(A_{\sigma+\tau}(Q)\right)^{-1/2}\left\|QG\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq(\sigma+\tau)\left(A_{\sigma+\tau}(Q)\right)^{-1/2}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})},

and, letting

, we again arrive at (
1.5
) for

as well.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
(a) has fully been proved.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(b).

Let
\tau
,
\sigma
, and

If there exists

such that equality holds in (

|f(x_{0})|=\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty

\displaystyle\left[(Qf)^{\prime}(x_{0})\right]^{2}+\left(\sigma+\tau\right)^{2}\left[(Qf)(x_{0})\right]^{2}=\left(\left[Q^{\prime}(x_{0})\right]^{2}+\left(\sigma+\tau\right)^{2}\left[Q(x_{0})\right]^{2}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=

\displaystyle(\sigma+\tau)^{2}\frac{\left[Q^{\prime}(x_{0})\right]^{2}+\left(\sigma+\tau\right)^{2}\left[Q(x_{0})\right]^{2}}{A_{\sigma+\tau}(Q)}\left\|Qf\right\|_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\geq(\sigma+\tau)^{2}||Qf||_{\operatorfont{C\/}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Summarizing,

If

Thus, Theorem
1.1
(b) has fully been proved as well.

Pure measure of bending for soft plates
Epifanio G. Virga

Abstract
This paper, originally motivated by a question raised by Wood and Hanna [ Soft Matter , 15 , 2411 (2019)], shows that pure measures of bending for soft plates can be defined by introducing the class of bending-neutral deformations, finite incremental changes of the plate’s shape bearing no further bending. This class of deformations is subject to a geometric compatibility condition, which is fully characterized. A tensorial pure measure of bending, which is accordingly invariant under bending-neutral deformations, is described in details. As shown by an illustrative class of examples, the general notion of pure measure of bending could be of use to formulate direct theories for soft plates, where stretching and bending energies are kept separate.



I
Introduction

It has been remarked that a
pure
measure of bending for extensible rods
cannot
be provided by the curvature of its centerline, as this can easily been shown to be affected by a superimposed dilation
Wood and Hanna 2019
. Actually, within the nonlinear theory of extensible elastic rods, this issue has long be known, at least since the work of Antman
Antman 1968
and others who soon followed in his footsteps
Reissner 1972
;
Whitman and DeSilva 1974
. It has also consequences on the choice of the appropriate form of the bending content of the strain energy, when in the direct approach one wishes that it be nicely separated from the stretching content.

This was indeed the criterion advocated in
Antman 1968
for a planar extensible
elastica
: if
\vartheta
denotes the centerline’s deflection angle (relative to a fixed direction in the plane) of a naturally straight rod, the appropriate quadratic bending energy independent of stretching would be proportional to
(\partial_{x}\vartheta)^{2}
, and not
(\partial_{s}\vartheta)^{2}
, where
x
and
s
denote the arc-length coordinate in the reference and present configurations, respectively. As also remarked in
Antman 1968
, a theory measuring the bending energy of an extensible rod by
(\partial_{s}\vartheta)^{2}
, such as that proposed in
Tadjbakhsh 1966
, would in general be more complicated.
1
1

1

It seems indeed simplicity the major advantage of Antman’s proposed constitutive law, as witnessed by the neat analysis that ensued in
Antman 1968
.

Other attempts to derive the balance equations for an extensible elastic rod have been based in
Magnusson et al. 2001
and
Oshri and Diamant 2017
on Biot’s
nominal
strain tensor
Biot 1939
(see also
Truesdell 1952
and
Biot 1965
); they corroborated the approach purported in
Antman 1968
. A similar corroboration came from
Irschik and Gerstmayr 2009
, which followed yet another avenue, designed according to the canons of modern continuum mechanics.

Similarly, for a plate whose planar midsurface
S
in the reference configuration gets deformed into the surface
\mathscr{S}
embedded in three-dimensional space, the invariants of the curvature tensor
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}
, where
\bm{\nu}
is the outer unit normal to
\mathscr{S}
and
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}
denotes the surface gradient, cannot be pure measures of bending, as also neatly illustrated in
Vitral and Hanna 2023a
.

In analogy with the theory of stretchable rods, proposals for the bending energy of plates and shells (i.e., plates curved in their natural state) have been advanced that are not affected by one or another form of stretching. The lines of thought followed to achieve this goal are disparate, as are the conclusions reached; controversies abound, especially for shells, which I deliberately leave out of the scope of this paper.

In an alternative approach to geometric elasticity as formulated in
Efrati et al. 2009
, a theory for deformable, geometrically incompatible sheets was derived in
Oshri and Diamant 2017
from a three-dimensional model phrased in terms of Biot’s strain measures. The outcome is in contrast with
Efrati et al. 2009
, also in the rod-limit case, where the alternative energy is closer to Antman’s. The theory of
Oshri and Diamant 2017
has been applied to a soft matter system in
Oshri et al. 2019
; it effectively employs a measure of bending for plates that has a distant antecedent in
Atluri 1984
.

More recently, a dimension reduction method following the general lines proposed in
Steigmann 2007
;
Steigmann 2008
;
Steigmann 2012
;
Steigmann 2013
was applied in
Vitral and Hanna 2023b
to a three-dimensional isotropic quadratic strain energy
Vitral and Hanna 2022
formulated in terms of Biot’s strain tensor to justify a model for shells. In particular, in the limiting case of plates, the one that concerns us here, two two-dimensional bending measures arose naturally from the method employed in
Vitral and Hanna 2023b
, one invariant under arbitrary superimposed stretchings, and the other invariant only under superimposed dilations, both reducing to Antman’s in the rod-limit.

The questions addressed in this paper will be of a pure kinematic nature. First, we need to identify a criterion of
bending-neutrality
for soft (highly deformable) plates: we must single out the incremental deformations that, although changing the shape of
\mathscr{S}
, do so without any further bending. Thus,
pure
measures of bending will be defined as the ones
invariant
under bending-neutral deformations. I shall focus on one isotropic such measure,
\mathbf{A}
, tensorial in character, alongside with its scalar invariants and contrast them with both similar and dissimilar measures from the literature. It will be shown that
\mathbf{A}
reduces to Antman’s measure for extensible rods.

Identifying the pure measure of bending appropriate to a class of materials should prelude to a proposal for a direct theory of plates for which stretching and bending modes are kept separate from one another.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we define the class of bending-neutral deformations and present an isotropic measure of pure bending associated with them. Section
III
is devoted to the compatibility condition that a bending-neutral deformation must obey; we shall see that this class contains both pure tangential stretching deformations and planar
drill
rotations, characterized separately in
Szwabowicz 2008
and
Mohammadi Saem et al. 2021
. Finally, in Sec.
IV
, we summarize the conclusions of this study and comment briefly about its possible extensions.

Pure Measure of Bending

\mathbb{A}(\bm{y})

4

4

7

7

II.2
Impure Measures of Bending

It is interesting to link the invariants of
\mathbf{A}
to other invariant, but impure measures of bending, namely, the mean curvature
H
and the Gaussian curvature
K
of
\mathscr{S}
, defined as

H:=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})\quad\text{and}\quad K:=\det(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}).

(21)

We begin by computing
\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A}
. It
readily follows from (
18
) that

\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A}=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{B}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})^{2})=2H\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{B}\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})-K\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{B},

(22)

where the last equality follows from an identity proven in
Ozenda and Virga 2021
(see their equation (14)).

To obtain
\det\mathbf{A}
, we start from
\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A}^{2}
, which by (
18
)
also reads as

\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A}^{2}=(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})^{2}\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{B}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})^{2},

(23)

where
\cdot
denotes the inner product of tensors.
8
8

8

For generic tensors
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{L}\cdot\mathbf{M}:=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{L}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M})
.
Letting
\mathbf{B}
be represented as

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=B_{11}\bm{n}_{1}\otimes\bm{n}_{1}+B_{12}(\bm{n}_{1}\otimes\bm{n}_{2}+\bm{n}_{2}\otimes\bm{n}_{1})+B_{22}\bm{n}_{2}\otimes\bm{n}_{2}

(24)

in the eigenframe of
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}
in (
16
),
we easily obtain from (

\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A}^{2}=\kappa_{1}^{4}B_{11}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{4}B_{22}^{2}+2\kappa_{1}^{2}\kappa_{2}^{2}B_{12}^{2}=(\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{A})^{2}-2K^{2}\det\mathbf{B},

(25)

which by the Cayley-Hamilton theorem applied to
\mathbf{A}
implies that

\det\mathbf{A}=K^{2}\det\mathbf{B}=K^{2}\det\mathbf{C}.

(26)

Thus, equations (
22

26

III
Bending-Neutrality Compatibility

For
\bm{y}^{\prime}
to be a bending-neutral deformation, neither
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}
nor
\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}
can be arbitrary. They must be subject to a compatibility condition, which we shall discuss in some detail here along with its solutions. The special case where
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}=\mathbf{P}(\bm{\nu})
has been considered and fully solved in
Mohammadi Saem et al. 2021
.

Compatibility arises from requiring that the curvature tensor
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast}
of
\mathscr{S}^{\ast}
be symmetric, as it should (see also
Mohammadi Saem et al. 2021
). By writing (
17
) for
\bm{\nu}^{\ast}
(and
\bm{y}^{\ast}
) instead of
\bm{\nu}
(and
\bm{y}
), we have that

\nabla\bm{\nu}^{\ast}=(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast})(\nabla\bm{y}^{\ast}).

(56)

On the other hand, by (
12
) and (
13
), we can also write

\nabla\bm{\nu}^{\ast}=\mathbf{R}_{0}\nabla\bm{\nu}=\mathbf{R}_{0}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})(\nabla\bm{y}),

(57)

which, combined with (
56
), leads us to

\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast}=\mathbf{R}_{0}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})(\mathbf{U}^{\prime})^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{R}_{0}^{\mathsf{T}},

(58)

once use is also made of (
6
), (
3
), and (
8
).
It follows from (
58
) that
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast}
is symmetric if and only if
(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})(\mathbf{U}^{\prime})^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}^{\mathsf{T}}
is so. This latter condition will be written in the following equivalent way,

\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}=\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}(\mathbf{U}^{\prime})^{-1}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}\mathbf{U}^{\prime}.

(59)

Tensors on both sides of (
59
) act on the local tangent plane of
\mathscr{S}
. It is clear that every solution
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}
of (
59
) is defined to within a multiplicative surface dilation,
\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{P}(\bm{\nu})
, with arbitrary
\lambda^{\prime}>0
.

Two special classes of solutions of (
59
) deserve notice. They are better illustrated by use of the classical Euler-Rodriguez formula to represent
\mathsf{SO}(\bm{\nu})
(see also
Palais et al. 2009
for an updated account),

\mathbf{R}(\alpha):=\mathbf{I}+\sin\alpha\mathbf{W}(\bm{\nu})-(1-\cos\alpha)\mathbf{P}(\bm{\nu}),\quad\alpha\in]-\pi,\pi],

(60)

where
\mathbf{W}(\bm{\nu})
is the skew-symmetric tensor associated with
\bm{\nu}
, so that
\mathbf{W}(\bm{\nu})\bm{v}=\bm{\nu}\times\bm{v}
, for all vectors
\bm{v}
.

First, if either
\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}=\mathbf{R}(0)=\mathbf{I}
or
\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}=\mathbf{R}(\pi)=-\mathbf{I}+2\bm{\nu}\otimes\bm{\nu}
, the latter acting as
-\mathbf{I}
on the plane tangent to
\mathscr{S}
, then (
59
) reduces to requiring that
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}
commute with
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}
, which just amounts to say that
\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}
and
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}
must have the same eigenframe (see also
Szwabowicz 2008
).

Second, for
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}\mathbf{P}(\bm{\nu})
, with
\lambda^{\prime}>0
, (
59
) becomes

\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}=\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu})\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}.

(61)

Taking the trace of both sides of (
61
), and using both (
16
) and (
60
), we arrive at

2H(1-\cos 2\alpha)=0,

(62)

which for

reduces to either
\alpha=0
or
\alpha=\pi
, delivering again the first special solution of (
59
) considered above. For
H=0
, which is the case of
minimal
surfaces, (
62
) is identically satisfied and direct inspection of (
61
) with the aid of (
60
) shows that it is solved by all
\mathbf{R}_{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathsf{SO}(\bm{\nu})
. For
\lambda^{\prime}=1
, this result was already proved in
Mohammadi Saem et al. 2021
.

10

10

(an equivalent family would be obtained by setting

\mathscr{S}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{tr}(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast})=\cos\alpha\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\right)=0,

\displaystyle:=\det(\nabla\!_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\nu}^{\ast})=-\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}=-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}.

We have considered two distinct conditions that must be met for

to be a bending-neutral deformation, one compatibility condition and one integrability condition. Whenever

is such that either one or the other condition is not met, any incremental

will necessarily bring additional bending.

IV
Conclusions

The primary object of this paper is answering a question posed in
Wood and Hanna 2019
:“How do we wish to define a measure of bending, and by extension a bending energy and the concept of pure stretching deformation, for a thin structure”?

We (the patient reader and I) caracolled our way toward a possible answer through the notion of bending-neutral deformation, an incremental deformation that does not affect bending, but it is more than a pure stretching as
it may include normal twisting.

Quite naturally, a pure measure of bending was then introduced as a deformation measure unaffected by bending-neutral deformations. An explicit, tensorial example of such a measure was given and contrasted against other measures known for plates.

Common wisdom has it that plates (and shells, for that matter) are rather unresponsive to twists exciting local drill rotations (see, for example,
Szwabowicz 2008
). This may well be the case for conventional,
hard
plates, but it is likely not to be so for
soft
, polymeric plates, especially those with an activable internal structure, such as nematic elastomers (described in the landmark textbook
Warner and Terentjev 2003
).

These latter material surfaces are
anisotropic
, as the nematic director field
\bm{n}
on
\mathscr{S}
breaks the rotational symmetry about the normal
\bm{\nu}
. It might be interesting to find anisotropic pure measure of bending fit for these systems; they might be suggestive of direct theories where stretching and bending energy contents are kept separate, oblivious to whether they can be derived by dimension reduction of established three-dimensional strain energies or not.
11
11

11

The role of bending energy in the theory for nematic elastomer plates has been highlighted in
Ozenda et al. 2020
; applications are presented in
Singh and Virga 2023a
;
Singh and Virga 2023b
and
Sonnet and Virga 2022

Whether the answer presented in this paper to the opening question is satisfactory or not is not for me to tell. In the second part of the paper, my interest shifts toward the legitimacy of bending-neutral deformations. They have been characterized by the fulfillment of a necessary kinematic compatibility condition, which generates a three-dimensional set of solutions. Of course, this only represents the admissible bending-neutral deformations; which ones do actually exist, given a surface
\mathscr{S}
they are meant to transform, is decided, at least locally, by the fulfillment of an integrability condition. The study of this condition and its solutions will be the subject of a future paper.

Tensor PCA from basis in tensor space
Claudio Turchetti, Laura Falaschetti

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to present a mathematical framework for tensor PCA. The proposed approach is able to overcome the limitations of previous methods that extract a low dimensional subspace by iteratively solving an optimization problem. The core of the proposed approach is the derivation of a basis in tensor space from a real self-adjoint tensor operator, thus reducing the problem of deriving a basis to an eigenvalue problem. Three different cases have been studied to derive: i) a basis from a self-adjoint tensor operator; ii) a rank-1 basis; iii) a basis in a subspace. In particular, the equivalence between eigenvalue equation for a real self-adjoint tensor operator and standard matrix eigenvalue equation has been proven. For all the three cases considered, a subspace approach has been adopted to derive a tensor PCA. Experiments on image datasets validate the proposed mathematical framework.

More formally, PCA derives an orthogonal linear transformation to extract a low-dimensional representation from high-dimensional data. There are two used approaches to perform the PCA: i) the eigenvalue decomposition of data covariance matrix; and ii) the SVD of data matrix. In both cases an orthogonal matrix

As this concept is quite general can be profitably used to extend PCA from vector-space to tensor-space. The aim of this paper is to develop a mathematical framework for tensor PCA. The core of the proposed approach is the derivation of a basis in tensor space from a real
self-adjoint operator

It will be shown that tensors with a symmetrical structure belong to this important class of operators, can be easily derived as tensor products from tensor data, similar to the way in which Gram-matrix are derived from data matrix. In this way the problem of deriving a basis is equivalent to an eigenvalue problem. As final objective, tensors PCA is obtained as the projection of tensor data on the subspace spanned by the coordinates corresponding to the highest eigenvalues. In the paper three different problems will be carried out to derive: i) a basis from a real self-adjoint operator; ii) a rank-1 basis from a set of tensors; iii) a basis in a subset of tensors. For all these three cases a subspace approach will be adopted to derive a tensor PCA. In particular, with reference to the first issue, the properties of a self-adjoint tensor operator will be studied and the equivalence between eigenvalue equation for this operator and standard matrix eigenvalue equation will be proven. C. Basis of a subspace

Let us suppose a set

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}=\{\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n},\hskip 5.69046ptn=1:N\}

(76)

of N order-
d
tensors
\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i}},

is given, with

N<L=I_{1}I_{2}\ldots{I_{d}}
. Assuming
r
tensors within this set are linearly independent, thus this subset spans a subspace in tensor space

\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\ldots\times I_{d}}
,
whose dimension is
r\leq{N}
. The problem of deriving a basis from this subset is equivalent to the QR problem
[
76
]
, that is well known in matrix algebra, which aims to derive an orthonormal set of vectors, from a set of independent vectors. In tensor space the problem can be formulated as follows. Assume every tensors in
\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}
can be represented as linear combination of
r
orthogonal tensors

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l},\hskip 2.84544ptl=1:r
. Formally we have

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}r_{n,l},\hskip 5.69046ptl=1:r

(77)

where
r_{n,l}
is the
(N\times{r})
matrix of coefficients in the linear combination (
77
).

The tensor product of
\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}
with itself along the vector index
i
, gives

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}r_{n,l}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}r_{m,l^{\prime}}=r_{n,l}r_{m,l^{\prime}}\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}=r_{n,l}r_{m,l},\hskip 5.69046pt

(78)

where the orthogonality of

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}
, i.e.

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}
, is used. The tensor product (
78
) represents a real nonnegative self-adjoint operator in the space
R^{N}
, due to symmetry of the
(N\times{N})
matrix
r_{n,l}r_{m,l}
, thus ensuring a set of nonnegative eigenvalues exists.

It can be easily shown that the rank of matrix
[R]_{n,l}=r_{n,l}
is
r
. In fact due to the orthogonality of
\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}
,
it results

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}r_{n,l}=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}r_{n,l}=r_{n,l}

(79)

and

r_{n,l}r_{n,l^{\prime}}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}=

\big\|\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}\big\|^{2}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l^{\prime}}=\big\|\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}\big\|^{2}\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}

(80)

(
80
) shows that two generic columns
r_{n,l}
and

r_{n,l^{\prime}}
of matrix
R
are orthogonal each other and, as a consequence
r=rank(R)
.

Due to symmetry, the
(N\times{N})
matrix

can be decomposed as

r_{n,l}r_{m,l}=U_{n,p}\Sigma^{2}_{p,q}U^{T}_{q,m}=U_{n,p}\Sigma^{2}_{p,q}U_{m,q}=

U_{n,r}\Sigma_{p,q}\Sigma_{p,q}U_{m,q}=U_{n,p}\Sigma_{p,l}(U_{m,q}\Sigma_{q,l})

(81)

The matrix on the left
r_{n,l}r_{m,l}=[RR^{T}]_{n,m}
is the Gram-matrix of
R^{T}
, and it is well known from matrix algebra that
rank(RR^{T})=rank(R)
. This implies that the matrix
\Sigma_{p,q}
is an
(r^{\prime}\times{r^{\prime}})
diagonal matrix
\Sigma=diag(\sigma_{1}\ldots{\sigma_{r}})
, whose elements
\sigma_{j}
are the singular values, with
r^{\prime}=rank(R^{T}R)=rank(R)=r
. It is worth to notice that any other matrix

, whith
V
orthogonal, satisfies (
81
), thus
RR^{T}
can be decomposed in a non unique way as

r_{n,l}=U_{n,p}\Sigma_{p,l}

(82)

Combining (
77
) and (

) gives

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}U_{n,p}\Sigma_{p,l}

(83)

and the tensor product of
\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}
with matrix

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}U_{n,m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\Sigma_{p,l}U_{n,p}U_{n,m}=

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\Sigma_{p,l}\delta_{p,m}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}\Sigma_{l,m}

(84)

due to the orthogonality of
U
.
The matrix
\Sigma_{p,l}
is diagonal full rank, thus
\Sigma^{-1}_{p,l}
exists and from (
84
)

\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}U_{n,m}\Sigma^{-1}_{m,l}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}

(85)

or

\mathchoice{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\textbf{i},l}=\mathchoice{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}{\mathcal{X}}_{\textbf{i},n}b_{n,l},\hskip 5.69046ptl=1:r

(86)

where

(87)

(N\times{N})

Conclusion

Tensor PCA is a multilinear extension of PCA, one of the most popular and efficient subspace techniques used in matrix analysis to reduce the dimensionality of a dataset. In this paper a mathematical framework for tensor PCA has been developed by deriving a basis in tensor space and by projecting data on a subspace. In particular the problem of deriving a basis from a self-adjoint tensor operator has been studied and the equivalence between eigenvalue equation for this operator and standard matrix eigenvalue equation has been proven.

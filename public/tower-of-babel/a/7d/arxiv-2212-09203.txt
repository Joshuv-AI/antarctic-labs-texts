A local gap theorem for Ricci shrinkers
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
We prove a local gap theorem for Ricci shrinkers, which states that if the local μ \mu -functional at scale 1 1 on a large ball centered at the minimum point of the potential function is close enough to 0 0 , then the shrinker must be the flat gaussian shrinker. In relation to our result, Yokota [ Yo09 , Yo12 ] proved the same result assuming the global μ \mu -functional to be close enough to 0 0 . Our result shows an aspect of how the local geometry of a shrinker controls the global geometry, which is also discussed in [ LW19 , LW20 , LW21 ] .



1
Introduction

A shrinking gradient Ricci soliton, or Ricci shrinker for short, is a tuple
(M^{n},g,f)
of a smooth Riemannian manifold and a smooth function called the
potential function
, satisfying the equation

\Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\tfrac{1}{2}g.

In our consideration of a Ricci shrinker, we normalize the function
f
so that

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=f.

(1.1)

As an important subfield in the Ricci flow, the Ricci shrinker has been studied extensively by far,
leading to many good results which have been facilitating the study of the Ricci flow in general, and its singularity formation in particular.

Surprisingly, Perelman
[
Per02
]
shows that a shrinker is a critical point of his
\mathcal{W}
-functional. Because of the monotonicity of the
\mathcal{W}
-functional under the Ricci flow and the conjugate heat flow (namely, positive solutions to the conjugate heat equation), a shrinker limit can be obtained by proper scaling on the one hand (c.f.
[
CZ11
,
MM15
]
), and a shrinker and its potential function must be strongly related to the conjugate heat equation and (logarithmic) Sobolev inequalities on the other hand (c.f.
[
CN09
,
LW20
]
); the latter shall be our chief viewpoint in this paper.

Indeed, the potential function
f
can be regarded as a singular conjugate heat kernel on the canonical form of the shrinker, which, due to Cao-Zhou
[
CZ10
]
, always admits gaussian upper and lower estimates. Precisely,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Big({\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)-5n\Big)_{+}^{2}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big({\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)+\sqrt{2n}\,\Big)^{2},

(1.2)

where
o
is a fixed
minimum point
of
f.

Another important geometric quantity of the shrinker is the
f
-volume
\displaystyle\int_{M}e^{-f}dg
, which also reveals many of the shrinker’s properties. For instance, by implementing the
f
-volume, Wylie
[
W08
]
proved that the fundamental group of a shrinker must be finite. Since the
f
-volume of a shrinker is always finite
[
CZ10
]
, we may define the quantity
\mu_{g}
as its logarithm, namely,

\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg=e^{\mu_{g}}.

The significance of
\mu_{g}
is sufficently explored by Carrillo-Ni
[
CN09
]
and Li-Wang
[
LW20
]
. Indeed, if we let

\displaystyle\tilde{f}:=f+\mu_{g},

(1.3)

then

\displaystyle\int(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}dg=1,

(1.4)

and
\tilde{f}
is exactly the minimizer of Perelman’s functional
\mathcal{W}(g,\cdot,1)
. In fact, much more can be said:

\displaystyle 2\Delta\tilde{f}-|\nabla\tilde{f}|^{2}+R+\tilde{f}-n\equiv\mu_{g},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{g}=\mathcal{W}(g,\tilde{f},1)=\mu(g,1)=\nu(g).

The definitions of the
\mathcal{W}
,
\mu
, and
\nu
functionals are found in
[
Per02
]
. For this reason, the quantity
\mu_{g}
is often called the
shrinker entropy
.

By applying his techniques of estimating the reduced volume, Yokota
[
Yo09
,
Yo12
]
proved a gap theorem showing how the shrinker entropy is involved with the global geometry.

Theorem 1.1

(Yokota’s gap theorem
[
Yo12
]
)
.

There is a positive number
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n)>0
depending only on the dimension
n
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker and let
\mu_{g}
be the shrinker entropy. Assume that
\mu_{g}\geq-\varepsilon
, then
(M^{n},g,f)
must be the flat gaussian shrinker.

The above theorem of Yokota states that the
\mu_{g}
of a nonflat shrinker cannot be too close to zero, which is tantamount to saying that, if the global logarithmic Sobolev constant is close enough to zero, then there must be a global control of the curvature, that is, the curvature must be zero. Its local version, namely, the local logarithmic Sobolev constant controlling the local geometry, was first developed by Perelman in his famous pseudolocality theorem
[
Per02
]
. However, on a special object such as a Ricci shrinker, the local geometry can sometimes control the global geometry; this is seen, for instance, in
[
LW19
,
LW20
,
LW21
]
. The theorem which we prove in this paper is of this sort.

We shall consider the local version of Perelman’s
\mu
-functional on a smooth manifold
M^{n}
, which was studied in
[
W18
]
. For any open set
\Omega\subset M
and any smooth metric
g
, define

\displaystyle\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g,u,\tau)\,\bigg|\,\int_{M}u^{2}\,dg=1,\ \ u\in C_{0}^{0,1}(\Omega)\right\},

\displaystyle\nu(\Omega,g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\mu(\Omega,g,s)\,\big|\,s\in(0,\tau]\right\},

where

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g,u,\tau):=\int_{M}\left(\tau\big(4|\nabla u^{2}|+Ru^{2}\big)-u^{2}\log u^{2}\right)\,dg-n-\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi\tau.

It is well-known that
\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)
is a local logarithmic Sobolev constant. So long as
\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)>-\infty
, there is a logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\Omega
, namely,

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dg+n+\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi\tau+\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)\leq\int_{M}\tau\big(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2}\big)\,dg

(1.6)

for any
u\in C_{0}^{0,1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{2}=1
.

If the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is complete and noncompact, and if
\Omega
is precompact, then the functional
\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)
reflects only the geometric property within
\Omega
; it is plausible that anything could happen near the spatial infinity of the manifold while
\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)
remains close to zero. However, in the next theorem we shall prove that this observation is not true on a shrinker. If the geometry in a large ball is good enough (in the sense that the local
\mu
functional is close enough to
0
), then the whole shrinker must be Euclidean.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any dimension
n
there is a positive constant
\delta(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete Ricci shrinker. Let
o\in M
be a minimum point of
f
. If

\mu(B(o,1/\delta),g,1)\geq-\delta,

then
(M^{n},g,f)
is the flat gaussian shrinker.

It is worth noting that the local
\mu
-functional is not a priori involved with curvature. Presumably, a manifold could have very good isoperimetic constant (and hence very nice logarithmic Sobolev constant) with arbitrarily large curvature. To make this point clear, we present the following corollary of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any dimension
n
there is a positive constant
\delta(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete Ricci shrinker. Let
o\in M
be a minimum point of
f
. If

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{n}\geq(1-\delta)n^{n}\omega_{n}\left(\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\Omega)\right)^{n-1}\qquad\text{ for any regular }\Omega\subset B(o,1/\delta),

(1.7)

then
(M^{n},g,f)
is the flat gaussian shrinker.

As an application of the above theorem and corollary, we prove another local gap theorem for Ricci shrinkers. To state our result, we review the notion of curvature scale.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold, the curvature scale at
x\in M
is defined as

\displaystyle r_{\Rm}(x):=\sup\big\{r>0\,\big|\,|{\Rm}|\leq r^{-2}\ \text{ on }\ B(x,r)\big\}.

(1.8)

Similarly, if
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
is a Ricci flow, then the curvature scale at
(x,t)\in M\times I
is defined as

\displaystyle r_{\Rm}(x,t):=\sup\big\{r>0\,\big|\,|{\Rm}|\leq r^{-2}\ \text{ on }\ B_{g_{t}}(x,r)\times[t-r^{2},t]\big\}.

(1.9)

Theorem 1.5
.

For any dimension
n
there is a positive number
\varepsilon(n)>0
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete shrinker. Let
o\in M
be a minimum point of
f
. If
r_{\Rm}(o)\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
, then
(M^{n},g,f)
is the flat gaussian shrinker.

To conclude the introduction, we point out that Theorem
1.2
, Corollary
1.3
, and Theorem
1.5
are in the spirit of
[
LW20
, Corollary 7]
. Corollary
1.3
can even be directly derived from the techniques in
[
LW20
]
(see Section 3 below). However, Theorem
1.2
does not follow from
[
LW20
, Corollary 7]
as a straightforward corollary. While the latter highly depends on
[
LLW21
]
, our approach is totally different. Other gap theorems for shrinkers under global conditions are also seen in
[
CCL22
,
MW11
,
Zh18
,
Zh20
]
.

2
The proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We first show a coarse uniform estimate for the local and global entropies using the volume estimates in
[
LLW21
]
. The shrinker entropy estimate is then refined by localizing its minimizer
\tilde{f}
. The refined estimate implies that
\mu_{g}
is close to
0
provided the local
\mu
entropy is sufficiently small.
The argument is similar to the proof of the local monotonicity formula for the Ricci flow, see
[
W20
,
TZ21
,
CMZ21
]
.
Theorem
1.2
then follows from Theorem
1.1
(see also
[
Yo09
,
Yo12
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker normalized as in (
1.1
). Let
o
be a minimum point of
f
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)\geq c(n)\exp\left(\mu\big(B(o,1),g,1\big)\right),

where
c(n)
is a dimensional constant.

Proof.

The proof is a slight modification of
[
LLW21
, Lemma 2.4]
, we include it here for the convenience of the reader.

Applying
[
WW09
, Theorem 1.2]
to the concentric balls
B(o,1)
and
B(o,1/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{B(o,1)}e^{-f}dg}{\int_{B(o,1/2)}e^{-f}dg}\leq C(n)\exp\left(\sup_{B(o,1)}|\nabla f|\right),

(2.1)

where
C(n)
is a dimensional constant. By (
1.1
), (
1.2
), and the fact that
R\geq 0
(c.f.
[
Che09
]
), we also have that

\displaystyle 0\leq f(x)\leq n,\qquad|\nabla f|\leq\sqrt{n},\qquad\text{ for all }\qquad x\in B(o,1).

(2.2)

It follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1/2)\right)\geq c(n)\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right).

(2.3)

Now, we consider a smooth cutoff function
\eta
, which is compactly supported in
B(o,1)
with

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta\big|_{B(o,1/2)}\equiv 1,\qquad|\nabla\eta|\leq 4.

Letting

L:=\int_{M}\eta^{2}\,dg,\qquad\varphi=L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta,

then we have

\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{0,1}_{0}(B(o,1)),\qquad\int_{M}\varphi^{2}=1,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)\geq L\geq\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1/2)\right)\geq c(n)\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right).

Applying the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
1.6
) to
\varphi
with
\tau=1
and
\Omega=B(o,1)
, we have

\displaystyle n+\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi+\mu\big(B(o,1),g,1\big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{64}{L}\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)+\sup_{B(o,1)}R\right)-\int_{M}\varphi^{2}\log\varphi^{2}\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{64\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)}{c(n)\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)}+n\right)+\log L-\frac{1}{L}\int_{M}\eta^{2}\log\eta^{2}\,dg

\displaystyle\leq C(n)+\log L+\frac{1}{eL}\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log L+C(n),

where we have applied (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and the fact that
R\leq f
; this finishes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker normalized as in (
1.1
). Let
o
be a minimum point of
f
. Then we have

\mu_{g}\geq\mu(B(o,1),g,1)-C(n),

where
C(n)
is a dimensional constant.

Proof.

[
LLW21
, Lemma 2.5]
shows that

\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,1)\right)\leq C(n)e^{\mu_{g}}.

In combination with the above lemma, we complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a constant to be determined. Assume
(M^{n},g,f)
is a Ricci shrinker normalized as in (
1.1
), satisfying

\displaystyle\mu\big(B(o,1/\delta),g,1\big)\geq-\delta,

where
o
is the minimum point of
f
.

Next, we shall use the function
(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}
to create a test function that can be properly applied to the logarithmic Sobolev inequality determined by
\mu\big(B(o,1/\delta),g,1\big)
, where
\tilde{f}
is defined in (
1.3
), normalized in the way that

\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg=1.

Two things are to be attended to in the construction of the test function. One is that the function should have unit
L^{2}
-norm, the other is that the function should be compactly supported in
B(o,1/\delta)
. Let us deal with these two points one by one.

By a volume estimate proved by Munteanu-Wang
[
MW14
, Theorem 1.4]
,

\displaystyle\Vol_{g}\left(B(o,r)\right)\leq C_{n}r^{n}\qquad\text{ for all }\qquad r>0.

(2.5)

It follows that, for any
r\geq 40n,

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B(o,r)}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\int_{M\setminus B(o,r)}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{4}\big({\rm dist}(x,o)-5n\big)_{+}^{2}-\mu_{g}\right)\,dg

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}e^{-\mu_{g}-\frac{1}{5}r^{2}}\leq C_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{5}r^{2}},

where in the last inequality above we applied Lemma
2.2
and the fact that
\mu\big(B(o,1),g,1\big)\geq\mu\big(B(o,1/\delta),g,1\big)\geq-\delta\geq-1
.

Let
\eta
be a standard cutoff function on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\eta|_{(-\infty,1/2]}=1,\quad\eta|_{[1,\infty)}=0,\quad-3\sqrt{\eta}\leq\eta^{\prime}\leq 0.

Let

\phi(x)=\eta\left(\tfrac{{\rm dist}(x,o)}{\delta^{-1}}\right).

Then we obviously have that

\displaystyle\phi\in C_{0}^{1}\big(B(o,1/\delta)\big)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|\nabla\phi|\leq 3\delta.

(2.7)

By (
2.6
), if we take
\delta\leq\bar{\delta}(n)
, then we have

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:=\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\phi^{2}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg\geq\int_{B\big(o,\frac{1}{2}\delta^{-1}\big)}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

(2.8)

\displaystyle=1-\int_{M\setminus B\big(o,\frac{1}{2}\delta^{-1}\big)}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg\geq 1-C_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{20}\delta^{-2}}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}.

We shall use

u:=\phi\sqrt{(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}/V}

as the test function. Since
\mu(B(o,1/\delta),1)\geq-\delta
and
\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{2}\,dg=1
, applying (
1.6
) to
u
with
\tau=1
and
\mu(\Omega,g,\tau)=-\delta
, we have

\displaystyle-\delta

\displaystyle\leq\int\left(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2}\right)\,dg-\int_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}\,dg-\tfrac{n}{2}\ln(4\pi)-n

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\int\left(4\left|\nabla\log\phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\tilde{f}\right|^{2}u^{2}+Ru^{2}\right)\,dg-\int_{M}\left(\log\phi^{2}-\tfrac{n}{2}\log 4\pi-\tilde{f}-\log V\right)u^{2}\,dg-\tfrac{n}{2}\ln(4\pi)-n

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(4\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}-4\phi\left\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\tilde{f}\right\rangle\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg+\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{f}\right|^{2}+R+\tilde{f}-n\right)u^{2}\,dg+\log V

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(4\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+2\phi^{2}\Delta\tilde{f}-2\phi^{2}\left|\nabla\tilde{f}\right|^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg+\int_{M}\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{f}\right|^{2}+R+\tilde{f}-n\right)u^{2}\,dg+\log V

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(2\Delta\tilde{f}-\left|\nabla\tilde{f}\right|^{2}+R+\tilde{f}-n\right)u^{2}\,dg+\log V

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{4}{V}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

\displaystyle=\mu_{g}+\log V+\frac{4}{V}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg-\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg,

where
\mu_{g}
is the constant defined in (
1.5
). For the last two terms in the above inequality, we first apply (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4}{V}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg\leq 72\delta^{2}\int(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg=72\delta^{2}.

(2.10)

Secondly, since, by (
1.4
),
d\nu:=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg
is a probability measure, then, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\left(\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}\right)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}}\,dg

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{M}\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}d\nu\geq\frac{1}{V}\left(\int_{M}\phi^{2}\,d\nu\right)\log\left(\int_{M}\phi^{2}\,d\nu\right)

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\cdot V\log V

\displaystyle=\log V.

Combining (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle\mu_{g}\geq-\delta-72\delta^{2}.

(2.12)

Finally, taking
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}(n)
, the conclusion of our theorem follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3
A pseudolocality point of view

The proof of Theorem
1.2
in the previous section, though very concise, does not reflect much of the Ricci flow mechanism developed by Hamilton, Perelman, and others. Indeed, a Ricci shrinker
(M^{n},g,f)
generates an ancient Ricci flow in the following way. Let

\displaystyle\tau_{t}:=1-t,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{\tau_{t}}\nabla_{g}f\circ\phi_{t},\qquad\phi_{0}=\operatorname{id},\qquad g_{t}=\tau_{t}\phi_{t}^{*}g.

(3.1)

Then
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
satisfies the Ricci flow equation, and is called the
canonical form
of the Ricci shrinker. If we let
\tilde{f}_{t}=f\circ\phi_{t}+\mu_{g}
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t}=(4\pi\tau_{t})^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}_{t}}

(3.2)

is a solution to the conjugate heat equation. In the current and following section, we shall view a Ricci shrinker dynamically as a Ricci flow by considering its canonical form, and show how the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
is related to the classical theory of Ricci flow. First of all, we recall the pseudolocality theorem of Li-Wang, which is an adaptation of Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem (c.f.
[
W20
,
LW20
]
) to the canonical form of a Ricci shrinker.

Theorem 3.1

(Pseudolocality
[
LW20
, Theorem 24]
)
.

There exist positive numbers
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
and
\delta_{0}(n)>0
with the following properties. Let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
be the canonical form of a Ricci shrinker. Suppose
t_{0}\in(-\infty,1)
and
B_{g_{t_{0}}}(x_{0},r)\subset M
is a geodesic ball satisfying

\nu\big(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r),g_{0},r^{2}\big)>-\delta_{0}.

Then for each
t\in(t_{0},\min\{t_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}r^{2},1\})
and
x\in B_{g_{t}}(x_{0},\frac{1}{2}r)
, we have

\displaystyle|{\Rm}|(x,t)\leq(t-t_{0})^{-1},

\displaystyle\inf_{\rho\in(0,\sqrt{t-t_{0}}\,]}\rho^{-n}\Vol_{g_{t}}\left(B_{g_{t}}(y,\rho)\right)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{n}.

We shall show how Theorem
3.1
implies Corollary
1.3
. However, it is worth pointing out that Theorem
1.2
itself cannot be immediately reduced to a corollary of Theorem
3.1
. One can nevertheless apply Theorem
3.1
to prove a weaker version of Theorem
1.2
, namely, with the
\mu\big(B(o,1/\delta),g,1\big)>-\delta
condition replaced by
\nu\big(B(o,1/\delta),g,1\big)>-\delta
. We recall the following technical lemma due to Wang
[
W18
, Lemma 3.5]
, which, in combination with Theorem
1.2
, already leads to a proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
W18
, Lemma 3.5]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature. Assume that there are
o\in M
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{n}\geq(1-\delta)n^{n}\omega_{n}\left(\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\Omega)\right)^{n-1}\qquad\text{ for any regular }\Omega\subset B(o,1/\delta).

(3.3)

Then we have

\displaystyle\nu(B(o,1/\delta),g,1/\delta^{2})\geq n\log(1-\delta).

A proof of Corollary
1.3
via Theorem
3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be the Ricci shrinker in the statement of Corollary
1.3
and let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
be its canonical form. We take
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}(n)
, such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}/\delta^{2}\geq 1,\qquad n\log(1-\delta)\geq-\delta_{0},

where
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
are the constants in Theorem
3.1
. Due to Lemma
3.2
, we can apply Theorem
3.1
to
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
at
(o,0)
with
r=\delta^{-1}
. This leads to

\displaystyle R(o,t)\leq\frac{1}{t},\qquad\text{ for all }t\in(0,1).

(3.4)

On the other hand, since
o
is the minimum point of the potential function
f
, we have that
o
is a static point under the flow
\phi_{t}
which defines the canonical form (
3.1
). Thus, we have

\displaystyle R(o,t)=\frac{1}{\tau_{t}}R_{g}(\phi_{t}(o))=\frac{1}{1-t}R_{g}(o),\qquad\text{ for all }t\in(-\infty,1).

(3.5)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle R_{g}(o)\leq\frac{1-t}{t},\qquad\text{ for all }t\in(0,1).

Taking
t\to 1
, we have

R_{g}(o)=0.

Due to
[
Che09
]
and the strong minimumm principle, the Ricci shrinker must be Ricci flat, and the shrinker equation

\nabla^{2}f=\tfrac{1}{2}g

immediately implies that the shrinker is gaussian (c.f.
[
PRS11
, Theorem 1]
).
∎

In this section, we shall present a proof of Theorem

4.1
Conjugate heat kernel and Nash entropy

Bamler
[
Bam20a
]
studied the
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance between conjugate heat kernels. For two probability measures
\mu
,
\nu
in a metric space
(X,d)
, the
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{X}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{f}\left(\int_{X}f\,d\mu-\int_{X}f\,d\nu\right),

(4.1)

where the infimum is taken over all bounded
1
-Lipschitz functions.

Now we consider a Ricci flow
(M,g_{t})_{t\in I}
with bounded curvature within each compact time interval. Let
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
be conjugate heat flows, namely,

\mu_{t}=u(\cdot,t)\,dg_{t},\qquad\nu_{t}=v(\cdot,t)\,dg_{t},

where
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
are positive solutions to the conjugate heat equation with unit integral. By applying a simple gradient estimate for the heat equation, Bamler
[
Bam20a
, Lemma 2.7]
shows that

t\to{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{t}}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})

is an increasing function in
t
.

In practice, we shall mostly consider conjugate heat kernels as conjugate heat flows, and denote by

d\nu_{x,t\,|\,s}=K(x,t\,|\,\cdot,s)\,dg_{s}

the conjugate heat kernel based at
(x,t)
. The Nash entropy based at
(x,t)
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(\tau)=-\int_{M}\log K(x,t\,|\,\cdot,t-\tau)\,d\nu_{x,t\,|\,t-\tau}-\frac{n}{2}\log 4\pi\tau-\frac{n}{2}.

For any point
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,1)
and any
s<t
, Bamler
[
Bam20a
]
shows that there is always an
H_{n}
-center
(z,s)
of
(x,t)
, such that
\nu_{x,t\,|\,s}
is close to
\delta_{z}
, for instance, in the sense of
W_{1}
-Wasserstein distance. Precisely, we have (c.f.
[
Bam20a
, Corollary 3.8]
)

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{s}}\big(\nu_{x,t\,|\,s},\delta_{z}\big)\leq\sqrt{H_{n}(t-s)},

(4.2)

where
H_{n}=\frac{(n-1)\pi^{2}}{2}+4
. It is clear from
[
Bam20a
]
and
[
CMZ21
]
that the Nash entropy is strongly involved with the local geometry at the base point as well as at the
H_{n}
-center.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
CMZ21
, Theorem 1.10]
)
.

Assume that
[-r^{2},0]\subseteq I
. Furthermore, assume that

(4.3)

where

and

is a dimensional constant.

Next, we recall the following

-regularity theorem due to Bamler, which states that if on a Ricci flow the Nash entropy based at a point is small enough, then the curvature scale

with the following property. Assume that

Another proof of Theorem

properly in space-time of the canonical form defined in (

(x,0)

(c.f.

Let

(4.4)

where

Lemma 4.3

(Continuous dependence of
\mu
-functional on the scale)
.

Let

\tau
.

Proof.

This result follows directly from the proof of

is precompact and the scalar curvature is positive on

\displaystyle\left(\int u^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dg\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{S}\int\left(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2}\right)\,dg,\qquad\text{ for all }\ u\in C_{0}^{0,1}(\Omega).

(4.5)

The crux of the proof in

With the validity of (

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the assumption (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{t}}(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\delta_{o})\leq C(n)\sqrt{\tau_{t}},\qquad\text{ for all }\ t\in(-\infty,1).

(4.6)

Proof.

Since
o
is static under the flow
\phi_{t}
defined in (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{t}}(\tilde{\mu}_{t},\delta_{o})={\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{\tau_{t}\phi_{t}^{*}g}(\phi_{t}^{*}\tilde{\mu}_{0},\phi_{t}^{*}\delta_{o})=\sqrt{\tau_{t}}{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g}(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\delta_{o}),

where the last equation above is from the fact that

is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
g
if and only if
\sqrt{\tau_{t}}h\circ\phi_{t}
is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
g_{t}
. Thus, if suffices to obtain an estimate like

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g}(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\delta_{o})\leq C(n).

By the assumption (
4.4
) and Lemma

, we have a lower bound of
\mu_{g}
depending only on the dimension. Thus, (
1.2
) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left({\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)-C(n)\right)_{+}^{2}\leq\tilde{f}(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left({\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)+C(n)\right)^{2}.

(4.7)

On the other hand, for any bounded
1
-Lipschitz function

(with respect to

), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}h\,d\tilde{\mu}_{0}-\int_{M}h\,d\delta_{o}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\big(h(x)-h(o)\big)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}(x)}\,dg(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}{\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}(x)}\,dg(x).

It follows immediately from (
2.5
), (

), and (

) that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g}\big(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\delta_{o}\big)\leq\int_{M}{\rm dist}_{g}(x,o)(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\tilde{f}(x)}\,dg(x)\leq C(n).

We have finished the proof of the Lemma.

Another proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
(M,g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,1)}
be the canonical form defined in (

) of a shrinker with bounded curvature satisfying (

). Let

be a minimum point of

.

In order to deal with the diffeomorphism in the definition of the canonical form, we shall consider sub-level-sets of

\displaystyle D_{t}(r):=\left\{f_{t}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}r^{2}\right\},\qquad t\in(-\infty,1),\ \ r^{2}>0.

Then, by (

\displaystyle B_{g_{t}}\Big(o,(r-\sqrt{2n}\,)\sqrt{\tau_{t}}\Big)\subset D_{t}(r)\subset B_{g_{t}}\Big(o,(r+5n)\sqrt{\tau_{t}}\Big),\qquad\text{ for all }\ t\in(-\infty,1)\ \text{ and }\ r>\sqrt{2n}.

Thus, if we take
\delta\leq\overline{\delta}(n)\ll 1
, then (
4.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mu\big(D_{0}(1/\delta),g_{0},1\big)\geq-\delta.

(4.9)

In addition,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}:\Big(D_{t}(r),g_{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\to\Big(D_{0}(r),\tau_{t}g\Big)

is obviously an isometry, which implies that

\displaystyle\mu\big(D_{t}(1/\delta),g_{t},s\tau_{t}\big)\equiv\mu\big(D_{0}(1/\delta),g,s\big),\qquad\text{ for all }\ t\in(-\infty,1)\text{ and }s>0.

(4.10)

Next, we fix an arbitrary point

. Let
r\geq 1
and let
(z,-r^{2})
be an

-center of
(x,0)
.
By the monotonicity of the

-Wasserstein distance and Lemma

, we may estimate

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{g_{-r^{2}}}(z,o)

\displaystyle={\rm dist}^{g_{-r^{2}}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{z},\delta_{o})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm dist}^{g_{-r^{2}}}_{W_{1}}(\delta_{z},\nu_{x,0\,|\,-r^{2}})+{\rm dist}^{g_{-r^{2}}}_{W_{1}}(\tilde{\mu}_{-r^{2}},\nu_{x,0\,|\,-r^{2}})+{\rm dist}^{g_{-r^{2}}}_{W_{1}}(\tilde{\mu}_{-r^{2}},\delta_{o})

\displaystyle\leq\left(C(n)+\frac{{\rm dist}_{W_{1}}^{g_{0}}(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\delta_{x})}{\sqrt{\tau_{-r^{2}}}}\right)\sqrt{\tau_{-r^{2}}}.

By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma

,

\mathcal{N}_{x,0}(r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon(n),\qquad\text{ for all }\ r\geq\overline{r}(n,x,\delta).

We may conclude by Theorem
4.2
that

. Hence the shrinker in question must be flat.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
. We first provide a local injectivity radius estimate at the minimum point
o
of the potential function. Then, we apply a result by Hamilton
[
Ham95
]
to show an almost Euclidean isoperimetric inequality on a ball of large radius centered at
o
. Theorem
1.5
then follows from Corollary
1.3
. Indeed, it can be seen from the proof of the Gromoll-Meyer theorem
[
GM69
]
that,
on a complete Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature bounded from above by a positive constant
K
, if there is a proper
C^{2}
strictly convex function, then the injectivity radius of
M
is bounded from below by
\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}
(see also
[
CD04
, Theorem B.65]
); a local versinon of this statement is sufficient for our purpose.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold. Let
r>0
,
p_{0}\in M
, and
F:B(p_{0},r)\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly convex function. Assume that
the sectional curvature is bounded from above by
r^{-2}
on
B_{g}(p_{0},r)
, and that
\{F\leq s\}\subseteq B_{g}(p_{0},r/2)
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
x\in\{F\leq s\}
, the injectivity radius at
x
satisfies

\operatorname{inj}_{g}(x)\geq\frac{r}{2}.

Proof.

Let
A:=\{F\leq s\}
. By the Rauch comparison theorem, we have that the conjugate radius at each
x\in A
is bounded from below by
\min\{\pi r,r/2\}=r/2

[
CD04
, Theorem B.20]
. Suppose by contradiction that the conclusion of the lemma fails. Then

\iota_{0}:=\inf\{\operatorname{inj}_{g}(y):y\in A\}<\frac{r}{2}.

By the compactness of
A
and the conjugate radius bound, we have
\iota_{0}>0
. Let
\{p_{m}\}
be a sequence in
A
such that
\text{inj}_{g}(p_{m})\to\iota_{0}
as

. By passing to subseqence and by a result of Klingenberg
[
K59
, Lemma 1]
(see also
[
doC92
, Prop 2.12, Ch.13]
),
p_{m}\to p_{\infty}\in A
and there is a sequence of unit-speed geodesics
\gamma_{m}:[0,2I_{m}]\to M
, where
I_{m}=\text{inj}_{g}(p_{m})
, with
\gamma_{m}(0)=\gamma_{m}(2I_{m})=p_{m}
such that
q_{m}:=\gamma_{m}(I_{m})
is a point on the cut locus of
p_{m}
that realizes the distance between
p_{m}
and its cut locus. By the compactness of
A
, we may further suppose that
q_{m}\to q_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{m}\to\gamma_{\infty}
, where
\gamma_{\infty}:[0,2\iota_{0}]\to M
satisfies
\gamma_{\infty}(0)=\gamma_{\infty}(2\iota_{0})=p_{\infty}
, and
q_{\infty}=\gamma_{\infty}(\iota_{0})
is a point on the cut locus of
p_{\infty}
which realizes the distance between
p_{\infty}
and its cut locus. Note that in general
\gamma_{m}
may have corner at
p_{m}
, i.e.
\gamma_{m}^{\prime}(0)\neq\gamma^{\prime}_{m}(2I_{m})
. We will prove that this doesn’t happen in the limit
\gamma_{\infty}
. As

and
\iota_{0}<r/2
, we have that
\gamma_{\infty}
is contained in
B_{g}(p_{0},r)
. We then show that
\gamma_{\infty}
lies inside
A
. The function
F\circ\gamma_{\infty}
has positive second derivative, and hence, by the property of convex functions, we have

F\circ\gamma_{\infty}(t)\leq F\circ\gamma_{\infty}(0)=F\circ\gamma_{\infty}(2\iota_{0})=F(p_{\infty})\leq s.

As a consequence,
\gamma_{\infty}
and, in particular,
q_{\infty}
lies in
A
. By the minimality of
\iota_{0}
, we may apply the same argument as in
[
doC92
, Prop 2.12, Ch.13]
to

to see that
\gamma^{\prime}_{\infty}(0)=\gamma^{\prime}_{\infty}(2\iota_{0})
, and thus
\gamma_{\infty}
is a closed smooth geodesic loop without any corner. This implies that the function
F\circ\gamma_{\infty}(t)
can be extended to a smooth nonconstant
2\iota_{0}
periodic function on
\mathbb{R}
, and thus attains its maximum value, which is impossible for a strictly convex function on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

\Omega\subset B_{g}(o,c^{\prime}/\varepsilon)
, it holds that

(1-c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\operatorname{Area}_{g_{E}}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\leq\operatorname{Area}_{g}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\leq(1+c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\operatorname{Area}_{g_{E}}\left(\partial\Omega\right)

(1-c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n}{2}}\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{E}}\left(\Omega\right)\leq\operatorname{Vol}_{g}\left(\Omega\right)\leq(1+c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n}{2}}\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{E}}\left(\Omega\right).

Thus, if we take
0<c^{\prime}\leq\overline{c^{\prime}}(n,\delta)\ll 1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\partial\Omega)\right)^{n}}{\left(\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\Omega)\right)^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}{(1+c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}\frac{\left[\operatorname{Area}_{g_{E}}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\right]^{n}}{\left[\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{E}}\left(\Omega\right)\right]^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}{(1+c_{n}c^{\prime 2})^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}n^{n}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)n^{n}\omega_{n},

where we also used the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality in the second line; (
5.2
) is proved. Lastly, we pick
\varepsilon\leq\overline{\varepsilon}(n,c^{\prime},\delta)
such that
\frac{c^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}>\frac{1}{\delta}
; note that the upper bound of
\varepsilon
depend only on
n
, since our choice of
c^{\prime}
and
\delta
also depend only on
n
. This shows that the lower bound of the isoperimetric constant of
B_{g}(o,1/\delta)
is
\delta
-close to that of the Euclidean space.
By Corollary
1.3
,
the shrinker is the flat gaussian soliton. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

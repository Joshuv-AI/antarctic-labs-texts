Abstract
Résumé In the Proceedings of the AMS Boulder conference in 1965 Langlands states a combinatorial lemma involving families of characteristic functions attached to ordered partitions of an obtuse basis in a finite dimensional euclidean vector space. Langlands does not give any indication about the proof of the lemma which is said to be a “pleasant exercise”. Since we did not find a proof in the literature we decided to give one. We believe it of some interest for the history of the subject.

Some months ago Bill Casselman observed that there does not seem to exist any proof

This is not immediate since ordered partitions used by Langlands never occur in Arthur’s statement.
At least from an historical point of view, it seems useful to give a proof for the Boulder lemma,
to relate it to the modern treatment of the combinatorics and, in particular, to establish
the equivalence alluded to above.

and
\boldsymbol{\widehat{{\theta}}}{}^{\Lambda}

Proof 3.10

. But,

Application to the Boulder lemma

Where ordered partitions disappear

Consider an ordered partition
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\Delta_{P}^{R}

such that
F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}=\Delta_{P}^{R}\backslash\Delta_{P}^{Q}
with
P\subsetneq Q\subsetneq R
.
Let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be the ordered partition of
\Delta_{P}^{Q}
defined by

F_{\mathfrak{q}}^{i}=F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i+1}\!\!\quad\hbox{for}\quad\!\!1\leq i\leq r({\mathfrak{q}})=r({\mathfrak{p}})-1\,\,.

Then, Langlands’ characteristic functions
\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}
may be rewritten :

\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}={\theta}_{Q,R}^{\Lambda}\,\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\Lambda}\!\!\quad\hbox{and}\quad\!\!\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}=\tau_{Q}^{R}\,\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\Lambda}\,\,.

[formula]

Moreover

b_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}=b_{Q,R}^{\Lambda}+b_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\Lambda}\!\!\quad\hbox{and}\quad\!\!\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{q}}+a_{P}^{Q}+1+b_{Q,R}^{\Lambda}\,\,.

[formula]

The next proposition shows how to replace, up to a sign, alternating sums
over ordered partitions of functions

\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}
by a single characteristic function.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\widehat{b}}^{\Lambda}_{P,R}
be the number of
\lambda\in\Delta_{P}^{R}
such that
\lambda(\Lambda)\leq 0
.
Then,

\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta_{P}^{R})}(-1)^{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}=(-1)^{{\widehat{b}}^{\Lambda}_{P,R}}\widehat{{\theta}}_{P,R}^{\Lambda}\,\,.

Proof 4.2
.

The above remarks
(\star)
and
(\star\star)
show that

\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta_{P}^{R})}(-1)^{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}=\sum_{P\subset Q\subsetneq R}(-1)^{a_{P}^{Q}+1+b_{Q,R}^{\Lambda}}\,\,{\theta}_{Q,R}^{\Lambda}\sum_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta_{P}^{Q})}(-1)^{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{q}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\Lambda}\,\,.

The equivalence of Proposition
4.1

and Corollary
3.9
now follows by induction on the cardinal of

\Delta_{P}^{R}
.

Proof of the Boulder lemma

Proposition
2.1
claims an identity that in view of
(\star)
can be written

\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta_{P}^{R})}(-1)^{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Lambda}(H)=d_{\Delta_{P}^{R}}(\Lambda)-\sum_{P\subset Q\subsetneq R}\tau_{Q}^{R}(H)\sum_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta_{P}^{Q})}(-1)^{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{\mathfrak{q}}}\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\Lambda}(H)\,\,.

To prove it we have to show that, thanks to Proposition
4.1
,

(-1)^{{\widehat{b}}^{\Lambda}_{P,R}}\widehat{{\theta}}_{P,R}^{\Lambda}(H)=d_{\Delta_{P}^{R}}(\Lambda)\,-\!\!\!\sum_{P\subset Q\subsetneq R}\!\!(-1)^{{\widehat{b}}_{P,Q}^{\Lambda}}\tau_{Q}^{R}(H)\widehat{{\theta}}_{P,Q}^{\Lambda}(H)

but this is nothing than Corollary
3.11
.

Truncated Eisenstein series

Series
E^{\prime\prime}
versus truncated Eisenstein series

Here we borrow the notation from
[
5
]
.
Let
G
be a reductive group over a number field
F
with minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{0}
.
Let
P
be a standard parabolic subgroup with Levi
M
,

S
an associate standard parabolic subgroup and
\Phi
a cuspidal form on
\mathbf{X}_{P}

and
T
some parameter in
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
. Let
\Delta=\Delta_{P}^{G}

and consider
\Lambda\in V\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\Re(\Lambda)
is far enough inside the positive Weyl chamber (with respect to
\Delta
).
Langlands introduces in
[
6
, §9]
functions

F^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{S}(x,\Phi,\Lambda)=\sum_{s\in W(\mathfrak{a}_{P},\mathfrak{a}_{S})}\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta)}(-1)^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Re(s\Lambda)}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Re(s\Lambda)}(H_{S}(x)-T_{S})(M(s,\Lambda)\Phi)(x,s\Lambda)

where, by definition,

\Phi(x,\Lambda)=e^{<\Lambda+\rho_{P},H_{P}(x)>}\Phi(x)\,\,.

The reader is warned that
the dependence on
T
(denoted
H_{0}
by Langlands in
[
6
]
) does not appear
explicitly on the left hand side.
Then, Langlands introduces

E^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x,\Phi,\Lambda)=\sum_{S}\sum_{\xi\in S(F)\backslash G(F)}F_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\xi x,\Phi,\Lambda)

where the sum over
S
runs over parabolic subgroups associated to
P
.
On the other hand Arthur has defined in
[
3
]
a truncation operator denoted
\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}
.
When applied to the Eisenstein series

E(x,\Phi,\Lambda)=\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash G(F)}\Phi(\gamma x,\Lambda)

then, according to
[
5
, Proposition 5.4.1]
,
one obtains the following expression

\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}\!E(x,\Phi,\Lambda)\!=\!\sum_{S}\!\!\sum_{s\in W(\mathfrak{a}_{P},\mathfrak{a}_{S})}\sum_{\xi\in S(F)\backslash G(F)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(-1)^{a(s)}\!\phi_{M,s}(s^{-1}\!(H_{S}(\xi x)-T_{S}))(M(s,\Lambda)\Phi)(\xi x,s\Lambda)

where, by definition,

\phi_{M,s}(H)=\widehat{{\theta}}_{P,G}^{\,\,\Re(s\Lambda)}(sH)

and the integer
a(s)
is the number of simple roots
\lambda\in\Delta_{S}
such that
\lambda(s\Lambda)<0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

E^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x,\Phi,\Lambda)=\mathbf{\Lambda}^{T}\!E(x,\Phi,\Lambda)\,\,.

Proof 5.2
.

It suffices to observe that if
\Re(\Lambda)
is in the positive chamber,
then for
H\in\mathfrak{a}_{P}
and

one has

\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Delta)}(-1)^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Re(s\Lambda)}}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\Re(s\Lambda)}(sH)=(-1)^{a(s)}\phi_{M,s}(H)\,\,.

This assertion is a particular case of Proposition
4.1

where
\Delta_{0}
is the set of simple roots of
G

with respect to the minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{0}
and
\Delta=\Delta_{P}^{G}
.

On scalar products

A formula for the scalar product of two series

Arthur proves a formula

As already observed this equivalence is not obvious since ordered partitions

]

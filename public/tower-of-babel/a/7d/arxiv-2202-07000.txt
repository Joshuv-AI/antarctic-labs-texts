Abstract
. We define the Eulerian ideal of a k k -uniform hypergraph and study its degree and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. The main tool is a Gröbner basis of the ideal obtained combinatorially from the hypergraph. We define the notion of parity join in a hypergraph and show that the regularity of the Eulerian ideal is equal to the maximum cardinality of such a set of edges. The formula for the degree involves the cardinality of the set of sets of vertices, T T , that admit a T T -join. We compute the degree and regularity explicity in the cases of a complete k k -partite hypergraph and a complete hypergraph of rank 3 3 .

1.
Introduction

Eulerian ideals of graphs were introduced in
[
13
]
, motivated
by the notion of vanishing ideals of projective toric sets parameterized by graphs, the study of which started in
[
14
]
.
Both the Eulerian ideal of a graph and the vanishing ideal of the toric subset parameterized by a graph
are homogenous binomial ideals of the polynomial ring on the edges of the graph and yield one-dimensional, Cohen–Macaulay
quotients. These properties are favourable to the study of the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and the degree
of these ideals. In the case of the vanishing ideal of the projective
toric set parameterized by a graph, there has been substantial progress
in the computation of these invariants (
cf
.
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
15
]
) and
results indicate that they involve the cardinality of the base field.
On the other hand, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.8]
we known that in the case of the Eulerian ideal,
the regularity and the degree do not depend on the base field and therefore they reflect the combinatorics in a clear way.
The degree of the Eulerian ideal has a simple formula involving
the number of connected components of
G
and the bipartite property (
cf
.
[
13
, Proposition 2.11]
).
As for the formula of the regularity, the key combinatorial notion, introduced in
[
9
]
,
is that of
parity join
. These are subsets of edges of the graph which, given any even subgraph of the graph with an
even

number of edges (for example, two edge-disjoint triangles), have no more than half the number of edges of that
subgraph in common with it (
cf
. Definition
5.4
, below). Together with
a re-interpretation of this invariant in terms of
T
-joins with cardinalities of fixed parity, these
have enabled a combinatorial formula for the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of the Eulerian ideal (or, equivalently,
the quotient it yields) for a general graph (
cf
.
[
9
, Theorem 4.13]
).

The purpose of the present article is to generalize the notions and results of
[
13
,
9
]

to hypergraphs. To obtain a homogeneous ideal without changing the original definition,
we will restrict to
k
-uniform hypergraphs, i.e., hypergraphs whose edges have cardinality equal to
k
.
We describe a Gröbner basis for the Eulerian ideal of a
k
-uniform hypergraph and from this basis
we derive combinatorial formulas for the degree and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. Finally, we apply
these to the explicit computation of these invariants in the case of complete hypergraphs.

The contents are the following. Sections
2
and
3
are devoted to
preliminary material; we give the definition of the Eulerian ideal of a hypergraph
(Definition
2.1
), a characterization of the homogeneous binomials in the ideal
(Proposition
2.3
) and we
define
T
-joins and even subsets in a hypergraph (Definition
3.1
). In
Section
4
we describe a Gröbner basis of the ideal (Theorem
4.4
) which
we use in Section
5
to describe the Hilbert function of the quotient
by the ideal in terms of reduced parity joins (Definition
5.4
and
Theorem
5.7
). In Sections
6
and
7

we give combinatorial formulas for the invariants regularity (Theorem
6.5
) and
degree (Theorem
7.2
). In Section
8
, we
compute explicitly these invariants for some families of complete hypergraphs.

2.
The ideal

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. More precisely, let
\mathcal{H}=(V_{\mathcal{H}},E_{\mathcal{H}})
where

V_{\mathcal{H}}
is a set (of vertices) and
E_{\mathcal{H}}
is a set
of subsets of
V_{\mathcal{H}}
, the elements of which we call edges of
\mathcal{H}
.
All hypergraphs in this article are assumed to be finite, with nonempty edge set and

k
-uniform, with
k\geq 2
, i.e.,
their edges have common cardinality equal to
k
.
Simple graphs are identified with
2
-uniform hypergraphs.
Fix
K
a field and let
K[V_{\mathcal{H}}]=K[x_{v}:v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}]
and
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]=K[t_{e}:e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}]
be polynomial rings.
Define a homomorphism of graded rings,
\varphi\colon K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]\to K[V_{\mathcal{H}}]
, by

\textstyle\varphi(t_{e})=\prod_{v\in e}x_{v}
,
for all
e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Throughout we will use the multi-index notation for monomials, i.e., whenever
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{\mathcal{H}}}
is a nonnegative integer
valued function of the set of edges,
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\in K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
will denote the monomial:

\textstyle\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}=\prod_{e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}}t_{e}^{\alpha(e)}.

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{E_{\mathcal{H}}}
is an integer-valued function of the edge set of
\mathcal{H}
,
we will use
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
to denote its support, i.e.,

\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)=\left\{e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}:\alpha(e)\not=0\right\}
.
Similarly for functions of the vertex set
\gamma
and monomials
\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}\in K[V_{\mathcal{H}}]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph. With the above notations, the Eulerian ideal of
\mathcal{H}
is the ideal

I_{\mathcal{H}}=\varphi^{-1}(x^{2}_{v}-x^{2}_{w}:v,w\in V_{\mathcal{H}})
.

Since
\varphi
is a graded homomorphism and
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the preimage of a homogeneous binomial ideal,
by a standard elimination argument, the Eulerian ideal of
\mathcal{H}
is also a homogeneous binomial ideal
(
Cf
., for instance,
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
).
In the graph case, the name Eulerian ideal comes from the characterization of a Gröbner basis of this ideal
in terms of the Eulerian subgraphs of the graph (
cf
.
[
9
, Theorem 3.3]
).
In the hypergraph case, the role of Eulerian subgraphs will be taken by the even subsets
of edges (
cf
. Definition
3.1
), as we will see later.
This is equivalent in the case of graphs and, in the case of
hypergraphs, is a direct approach that avoids considering any of the several notions of connectivity.
The motivation for this is the next result, which is a characterization
of the binomials in the Eulerian ideal, along the lines of
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
.
Let us first fix some notation.

Definition 2.2
.

(i) If
C\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
and
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
, let
\deg_{C}(v)
denote
\sum_{e\in C}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|
.
(ii) If
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{E_{\mathcal{H}}}
, let

\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha)=\left\{e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}:\alpha(e)\equiv_{2}1\right\}\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
be homogeneous and
let
C=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)
. Then
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in I_{\mathcal{H}}

if and only if, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\deg_{C}(v)
is even.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})=\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{x}^{\mu}
,
for some
\gamma,\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{V_{\mathcal{H}}}
.
Then, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\deg_{C}(v)=\sum_{e\in C}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|\\
\phantom{\deg_{C}(v)}\equiv_{2}\sum_{e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|\cdot(\alpha(e)-\beta(e))\\
\phantom{\deg_{C}(v)}\equiv_{2}\gamma(v)-\mu(v).\end{array}

Suppose that
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{x}^{\mu}\in(x_{v}^{2}-x_{w}^{2}:v,w\in V_{\mathcal{H}})
and therefore
there exist

g_{vw}\in K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
such that

\textstyle\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{x}^{\mu}=\sum\limits_{v,w\in E_{\mathcal{H}}}g_{vw}(x_{v}^{2}-x_{w}^{2}).

Fix
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
. Setting all variables but
x_{v}
, in the expression above, equal to
1
we get:

x_{v}^{\gamma(v)}-x_{v}^{\mu(v)}=h(x_{v}^{2}-1)

for some
h\in K[x_{v}]
. Writing
h
as linear combination of monomials in
x_{v}
and
working out the product on the left explicitly, we deduce that there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such
that
\gamma(v)=\mu(v)+2m
, as required.

Conversely, suppose that
\gamma(v)-\mu(v)
is even, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Then there exists
\rho\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{V_{\mathcal{H}}}
such that
\gamma=2\gamma^{\prime}+\rho
and
\mu=2\mu^{\prime}+\rho

for some
\gamma^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{V_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Let us fix
u\in V_{\mathcal{H}}

and let us work with the relations
x_{v}^{2}\equiv x_{u}^{2}
modulo the ideal
(x_{v}^{2}-x_{w}^{2}:v,w\in V_{\mathcal{H}})
. Then

\textstyle\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{x}^{\mu}\equiv x_{u}^{2|\gamma^{\prime}|}\mathbf{x}^{\rho}-x_{u}^{2|\mu^{\prime}|}\mathbf{x}^{\rho},

where
|\gamma^{\prime}|=\sum_{v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}}\gamma^{\prime}(v)
and
|\mu^{\prime}|=\sum_{v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}}\mu^{\prime}(v)
.
As
\mathbf{x}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{x}^{\mu}
is homogeneous (because
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
is), we get

|\gamma^{\prime}|=|\mu^{\prime}|
and thus the last binomial above is equal to zero.
We conclude that
\varphi(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})\in(x_{v}^{2}-x_{w}^{2}:v,w\in V_{\mathcal{H}})
, i.e.,
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
∎

3.
T
-joins

The characterization of the binomials in
I_{\mathcal{H}}
given in Proposition
2.3

leads us to the notion of
T
-join in hypergraphs. This notion will play a key role in the computation of the regularity and the
degree of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

(i) If
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
and
J\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
, we say that
J
is a
T
-join if and only if

\textstyle\deg_{J}(v)=\sum_{e\in J}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|\;\;\text{is odd}\iff v\in T.

In particular, a
\emptyset
-join is a subset of edges
J\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\deg_{J}(v)

is even, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
. These subsets of edges are called
even
.
(ii) Let us denote the set of all even subsets of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
by
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
.
(iii) Let us denote by
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
the set of all
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}

for which there exists at least a
T
-join.

Given
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
, the question of existence of a
T
-join is pertinent.
When
T=\emptyset
or when
T
is the set of vertices of a single edge, then
a
T
-join always exists, namely the empty set and the singleton of the edge in question,
respectively. In general, not every
subset
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
admits a
T
-join.
It is well-known that for
k=2
, i.e., for graphs, if a
T
-join exists for a given
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}

then
|J\cap E_{\mathcal{C}}|
must be even, for every connected component
\mathcal{C}

of the graph
\mathcal{H}
. The converse also holds (
cf.
[
7
, Proposition 12.7]
).
The situation for odd
k
is different. For example in the hypergraph of Example
2.4

the set of edges
J=\left\{\left\{1,2,4\right\},\left\{1,3,4\right\},\left\{2,3,4\right\}\right\}
, corresponding to the pyramid on the top,
is a
\left\{4\right\}
-join.

The following is a minimal set of restrictions on the elements of
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
, which we will use
in the remainder of this article.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and let
J
be a
T
-join.
(i) If
k
is even then
|T|
is even.
(ii) If
k
is odd then
|T|\equiv_{2}|J|
.
In particular, when
k
is odd there are no elements of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
with odd cardinality.

Proof.

Since

\textstyle\sum_{v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}}\deg_{J}(v)=\sum_{v\in T}\deg_{J}(v)+\sum_{v\not\in T}\deg_{J}(v)=k|J|,

if
k
is even, then
|T|\equiv_{2}0
and if
k
is odd, then

|T|\equiv_{2}|J|
.
∎

Many properties of
T
-joins in graphs extend to hypergraphs. One example
is the relation of
T
-joins with the symmetric difference of sets, which we denote
by
\triangle
. Namely, the fact that if
J_{1}
is a
T_{1}
-join and
J_{2}
is a
T_{2}
-join then
J_{1}\triangle J_{2}

is a
(T_{1}\triangle T_{2})
-join. This follows from the observation that, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
,

\textstyle\deg_{J_{1}\triangle J_{2}}(v)=\sum_{e\in J_{1}}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|+\sum_{e\in J_{2}}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|-2\sum_{e\in J_{1}\cap J_{2}}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|.

In particular,
\mathcal{E}(E_{H})
, the set of even subsets of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
is closed
under the symmetric difference.

4.
A Gröbner basis

Definition 4.1
.

(i) Denote
\mathscr{T}=\{t_{e}^{2}-t_{f}^{2}:e,f\in E_{\mathcal{H}}\}
. (ii)
A binomial
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
, is said an Eulerian binomial if

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
are square-free, coprime, of same degree and

\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
.
Let
\mathscr{E}
denote the finite set of all Eulerian binomials.
(iii) Let us denote
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
.

Note that, here, the even subset
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
has even cardinality, since

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
are square-free, coprime and of the same degree.

Proposition 4.2
.

\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}\subset I_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{G}
and denote
C=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)
.
By Proposition
2.3
, we must show that

C
is even. If
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{T}
then
C=\emptyset

and therefore
\deg_{\emptyset}(v)=0
, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
(
cf
. Definition
2.2
).
If
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{E}

then

\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)=\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\beta)

which, by definition, is even.
∎

From now on, fix a total order on the set
E_{\mathcal{H}}
and consider the associated
graded reverse lexicographic order on
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
. The next result gives a sufficient
condition for a binomial to reduce to zero modulo the set of binomials

\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
be homogeneous. If

\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)

is even, then, with respect to the
graded reverse lexicographic order,

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
.

Proof.

We will use complete induction on the degree of
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
. If
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})=0
, then

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}=0
and there is nothing to show.
Assume that
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})>0
.
Let
\mathbf{t}^{\delta}=\gcd(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha},\mathbf{t}^{\beta})
and assume that
\mathbf{t}^{\delta}\not=1
.
Let us write
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}=\mathbf{t}^{\delta}\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}=\mathbf{t}^{\delta}\mathbf{t}^{\mu}
.
Then

\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\gamma-\mu).

As
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{t}^{\mu})<\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})
, by induction,

\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{t}^{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.
We may thus restrict to the case when
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
are coprime.

Assume, without loss of generality, that the leading term of
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
is
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
.
If
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
are both square-free, then
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{E}
and we have finished.
Suppose that
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
is square-free but there exists
\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon}\not=1

such that
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}=(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})^{2}\mathbf{t}^{\eta}
, for suitable square-free
\mathbf{t}^{\eta}
.
Let
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
denote the product of the first
d
edges in
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
, where
d=\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})
, and consider

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{t}^{-\sigma}-\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
. Since
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})=\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\beta})
we deduce that

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{t}^{-\sigma}-\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
is homogeneous.
Furthermore, since
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{t}^{-\sigma}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
are square-free, coprime monomials and since

\operatorname{supp}(\alpha-\sigma)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\eta+\sigma)=\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\eta)=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-2\epsilon-\eta)

which, by assumption, is even, we conclude that
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{t}^{-\sigma}-\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
is, in fact, an Eulerian binomial.
As
\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
is the product of a set of least edges in
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
, its leading term is
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{t}^{-\sigma}
,
which divides
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
, the leading term of
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})^{2}\mathbf{t}^{\sigma}
. Using this Eulerian binomial in a one-step
reduction, we get:

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})^{2}\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{E}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbf{t}^{\eta}\bigl[(\mathbf{t}^{\sigma})^{2}-(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})^{2}\bigr].

Hence we may reduce to the case that
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
is not square-free (since the leading term of
(\mathbf{t}^{\sigma})^{2}-(\mathbf{t}^{\epsilon})^{2}

is certainly not square-free). Suppose then that there exists
e\in E_{\mathcal{H}}
such that
t_{e}^{2}\mid\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
. Let
f\in E_{G}
be an edge, least to

e
, such that
t_{f}\mid\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
. Then
t_{e}^{2}-t_{f}^{2}
has leading term
t_{e}^{2}
. Using this element
of
\mathscr{T}
in a one-step reduction, we get:

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{T}}}{{\longrightarrow}}t_{f}(t_{f}\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}t_{e}^{-2}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}t_{f}^{-1}).

If
\gamma,\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
are such that
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}=t_{f}\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}t_{e}^{-2}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\mu}=\mathbf{t}^{\beta}t_{f}^{-1}

then

\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\gamma-\mu)=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta).

Hence, by induction
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{t}^{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.
∎

Next, let us use Propositions
2.3
and

4.3
to show that

\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
is a Gröbner basis for the Eulerian ideal with
respect to the graded reverse lexicographic order.
This result generalizes
[
9
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

With respect to the graded reverse lexicographic order,
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
is a Gröbner basis
of the Eulerian ideal
I_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

We will use Buchberger’s criterion and we start by showing that
I_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathscr{G})
.
By Proposition
4.2
,
(\mathscr{G})\subset I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
To prove the reverse inclusion, we will use the fact that
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is generated by homogeneous binomials.
Let
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
be a homogeneous binomial in
I_{\mathcal{H}}
and let

C=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)
. Then, by Proposition
2.3
,

C
is even and hence, by Proposition
4.3
,

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
reduces to zero modulo
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{T}\cup\mathscr{E}
, which implies that

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in(\mathscr{G})
.

If
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{G}

then
\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta)
is even. To prove that
S(f,g)
reduces to
zero modulo
\mathscr{G}
this property of
f,g\in\mathscr{G}
will suffice.
Assume, without loss of generality, that

f=\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
and
g=\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}-\mathbf{t}^{\mu}

with
\operatorname{lt}(f)=\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
,
\operatorname{lt}(g)=\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
. Let us denote

C_{1}=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\alpha-\beta),\quad C_{2}=\operatorname{supp}_{2}(\gamma-\mu)

and let

5.
The Hilbert function

As is well-known, a Gröbner basis of

K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}

Definition 5.1

From now on, let us denote by

\mathcal{H}

.
Let us first make use of the Gröbner basis,

If

Given that

Definition 5.4
.

(i) If

Theorem 5.5
.

The map
\mathcal{B}_{d}(I_{\mathcal{H}},t_{\ell}^{2})\to\mathcal{J}_{d}^{\mathrm{r}}(\mathcal{H})
defined by
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}\mapsto\operatorname{supp}(\gamma)

is well-defined and a bijection.

Proof.

Let
C\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
be an even set of even cardinality. Suppose that
J=\operatorname{supp}(\gamma)

satisfies
|J\cap C|>\frac{|C|}{2}
. Let
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
be the product of the first
\frac{|C|}{2}

J
and let
\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
denote the product of the remaining edges. Then

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}\in\mathscr{E}
has leading term equal to
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
.
This is also the case if
|J\cap C|=\frac{|C|}{2}
and
J
does not contain the least edge in

.
Both cases lead to a contradiction. Since
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
is square free and thus

|J|=\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\gamma})=d
, we deduce that

. Hence the map is well-defined.
Using again the square-free property of the elements of
(I_{\mathcal{H}},t^{2}_{\ell})

that the map is injective.

Let us now prove that the map is surjective. Let
J\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
be a reduced parity join and let
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}

be the product of the edges in
J
. Since
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
is square-free, it suffices to show that

\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
is not divisible by the leading term of an element in
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathscr{G}
.
Let us consider an Eulerian binomial
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
and assume,

\operatorname{lt}(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}-\mathbf{t}^{\beta})=\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
.
Then, this means that
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)
contains the least edge of the corresponding
even set

C=\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\sqcup\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}.

Assume, with a view to a contradiction that
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\mid\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
. Then

|J\cap C|\geq\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})=\frac{|C|}{2}
.
Since
J
is a parity join, we deduce that
|J\cap C|=\frac{|C|}{2}

and therefore

. But then, as
J

is a reduced parity join, the least edge of

it belongs to
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)

∎

Before we use Theorem

The proof can be taken almost

Lemma 5.6
.

If
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}\in K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
is a monomial, then
\mathbf{t}^{\gamma}
is
\bigl(K[E_{H}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)
-regular.

Proof.

Given the definition
of

(

. Definition

), it suffices to
show that if

is any variable in

and

, then

Fix a total order of the vertices of
\mathcal{H}
for which

is the least vertex.
Then

\mathfrak{g}=\left\{x_{v}^{2}-x_{u}^{2}:v\in V_{\mathcal{H}},v\not=u\right\}

Assume

. As no leading term of an element of

,
we conclude that

is a factor of every one-step reduction in the division algorithm, which is to say that

.

Theorem 5.7
.

\dim_{K}\bigl(K[E_{G}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)_{d}=\sum_{j\geq 0}|\mathcal{J}_{d-2j}^{\mathrm{r}}(\mathcal{H})|
.

Proof.

Since

is

the following is a short exact sequence of graded homomorphisms of

(3)

0\to\frac{K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]}{I_{\mathcal{H}}}[-2]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot t_{\ell}^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]}{I_{\mathcal{H}}}\to\frac{K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]}{(I_{\mathcal{H}},t^{2}_{\ell})}\to 0.

.

.

Let

and, for every

Let us denote by

6.
Regularity

As we saw in the previous section, the Hilbert function
of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{H}
is related to reduced parity joins.
We will show that the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of this graded ring is related to the
maximum cardinality of a reduced parity join. However there is a way to give this
result in terms of the simpler notion of parity join and
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})
and that is what we will
do. The way to do this is to exploit the relation between parity joins and
T
-joins.
The next result, which we use in the computation of the maximum cardinality
is the analogue for hypergraphs of
[
9
, Lemmas 4.6 and 4.12]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and let
J
be a
T
-join.
(i) There exist
T
-joins
J_{1},J_{2}
with
|J_{1}|\not\equiv_{2}|J_{2}|
if and
only if there exists
C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
with
|C|
odd. (ii)

J
is a parity join if and only if
J
has minimum cardinality among all
T
-joins

J^{\prime}
such that
|J^{\prime}|\equiv_{2}|J|
.

Proof.

(i) Let
J_{1},J_{2}\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
be
T
-joins with
|J_{1}|\not\equiv_{2}|J_{2}|
.
Then
C=J_{1}\triangle J_{2}\subset E_{G}
is an even set of odd cardinality. Conversely,
if
C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
and
|C|
is odd then
J\triangle C
is another
T
-join and

|J\triangle C|\not\equiv_{2}|J|
.

(ii) Reasoning as before we deduce that

(5)

\left\{J\triangle C:C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})\text{ and $|C|$ is even}\right\}.

is the set of all
T
-joins,
J^{\prime}\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
, with
|J^{\prime}|\equiv_{2}|J|
.
If
C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
with
C
even, then

\textstyle|J\cap C|\leq\frac{|C|}{2}\iff|J\triangle C|\geq|J|.

Therefore
J
is a parity join if and only if
|J|
is the minimum cardinality of an element of (
5
).
∎

Let us denote the power set of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
by
\mathcal{P}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
.
Since a set of edges may be identified with the corresponding monomial of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
,
the graded reverse lexicographic order induces a total order on
\mathcal{P}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
.
Let us define it explicitely. Let
J_{1}\not=J_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{\mathcal{H}})

and denote by
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\not=\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
the square-free monomials
obtained as the products of the edges of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, respectively.
Then
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\succ\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
, in the graded reverse lexicographic order, if and only
if either

\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})\succ\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\beta})\iff|J_{1}|>|J_{2}|

or
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)
contains the last edge of
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha-\beta)
, i.e.,

J_{2}
contains the last edge of the symmetric difference
J_{1}\triangle J_{2}
.

Definition 6.2
.

If
J_{1}\not=J_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
are elements of the power set of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
, we
set
J_{1}\succ J_{2}
if
|J_{1}|>|J_{2}|
or, if
|J_{1}|=|J_{2}|
and

J_{2}
contains the last edge in
J_{1}\triangle J_{2}
.

It is clear that the partial order
\succeq
defined by the above is a total order and, in particular,
every (finite) subset of
\mathcal{P}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
has a minimum element

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and let
J
be a
T
-join. Then

J
is a reduced parity join if and only if
J

is the minimum with respect to
\succeq
of the set

(6)

\left\{J^{\prime}:\text{$J^{\prime}$ is a $T$-join and $|J^{\prime}|\equiv_{2}|J|$}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
J^{\prime}
be another
T
-join with
|J^{\prime}|\equiv_{2}|J|
and denote

the corresponding even cardinality element of

\textstyle|J\cap C|=\frac{|C|}{2}\iff|J^{\prime}|=|J|.

Hence, if

is a reduced parity join and
|J^{\prime}|=|J|
then

contains the last

which implies that

is the minimum of (
6
).
Conversely if

is the minimum of this set then, by Proposition

,

is a parity join. Suppose that there exists

is another

Proposition

gives the existence of a reduced parity join of cardinality equal to that of any given

,
as the maximum cardinality of a reduced parity join (

Corollary 6.4
.

\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})
is the maximum cardinality of a reduced parity join.

The next theorem is a combinatorial formula for the regularity of

.
This result is the generalization for hypergraphs of
[

.

Theorem 6.5
.

The regularity of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is equal to
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})-1
.

Proof.

Since
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is a
1
-dimensional, Cohen–Macaulay graded module,
its regularity is equal to its index of regularity, i.e., the smallest degree

such that
H(d)=P(d)
, for all

, where

and

denote the Hilbert function
and the Hilbert polynomial of

, respectively (
cf
.
[

, Corollary 4.8]
).
In turn, the index of regularity is equal to the degree of the Hilbert series plus one (

.
[

, Corollary 5.1.9]
).
Since, by Corollary

, the degree of
the Hilbert series of

is equal to the maximum cardinality of a reduced
parity join minus two and, in turn, by Propositions

and

, using Corollary

,
the regularity of

is equal to

.
∎

7.
Degree

Since
\mathcal{K}[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is a
1
-dimensional
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
-module its degree
coincides with its Hilbert polynomial, which, as the Hilbert series, (
4
), indicates
is related to the cardinality of the set of reduced parity joins,
\sqcup_{d=0}^{s}\mathcal{J}_{d}^{\mathrm{r}}(\mathcal{H})
.
In the case of graphs, because we know exactly which sets belong to
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
, the degree
of this module may be given in terms of the connected components of the graph and the bipartite property.
(
Cf
.
[
13
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
9
, Proposition 4.10]
.) In the general
case, an alternative combinatorial characterization of the degree involves the cardinality
of
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
.

Proposition 7.1
.

The map
\sqcup_{d=0}^{s}\mathcal{J}_{d}^{\mathrm{r}}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
sending a
reduced parity join,
J\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
, to the set

\left\{v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}:\deg_{J}(v)\text{ is odd}\right\}
is a surjection.
If all elements of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
have even cardinality then
it is a bijection, otherwise, it is
2
-to-
1
.

Proof.

Fix
T\in\mathcal{T}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
. By Proposition
6.3
, there exists
a reduced parity join
J
that maps to
T
. If all elements
of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
have even cardinality then, by Proposition
6.1

the set (
6
) is the full set of
T
-joins and, using again Proposition
6.3
,
we deduce that there exists only one
T
-join which is a reduced parity join. If there exists

C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
of odd cardinality then
J
and
J\triangle C
are
T
-joins with
|J\triangle C|\not\equiv_{2}|J|
.
Hence we may apply Proposition
6.3
twice to find two
reduced parity joins mapping to
T
. As any other
T
-join must belong to one of the two
corresponding sets of
T
-joins (the even cardinality ones and the odd cardinality ones) and,
by Proposition
6.3
, only one in each set is a reduced parity join,
we deduce that the preimage of
T
consists of exactly two reduced parity joins.
∎

Theorem 7.2

If
s=|E_{\mathcal{H}}|

is equal to

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=0}^{s}|\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{r}}_{d}(\mathcal{H})|

\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
has odd cardinality, then

\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})|

|\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})|

Proof.

Since
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is

may be obtained by multiplying its Hilbert series by

.
Using the rational form of the Hilbert series given in (
4
), we deduce that

\textstyle\deg K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{s}j_{d}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=0}^{s}|\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{r}}(\mathcal{H})|.

The rest of the statement follows from Proposition
7.1
.
∎

8.
Complete Hypergraphs

In this section we compute the degree and the regularity of

K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{H}
when
\mathcal{H}
is a complete
k
-partite graph or a
3
-uniform complete hypergraph.
The computations rely on the fact that in these cases the set
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and
the set of
T
-joins, for any given
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
, can be analysed explicitly, without
using a notion of hypergraph connectivity.

Definition 8.1
.

A hypergraph is called a complete
k
-partite hypergraph if
the vertex set,
V_{\mathcal{H}}
, is endowed with a
k
-partition,
V_{\mathcal{H}}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
, with

|V_{i}|=a_{i}>0
, such that
E_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\right\}:v_{i}\in V_{i},\text{ for every }i=1,\dots,k\right\}
.
Let us denote a complete
k
-partite hypergraph by
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
.

The next result is the analogue of the well-known result that a bipartite graph contains no cycles of
odd cardinality. It is also an important characteristic of a complete
k
-partite hypergraph as regards
the results of the previous sections.

Proposition 8.2
.

If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}^{k}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
and
C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
, then
|C|
is even.

Proof.

If
C\subset\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
, then,
since every edge in
C
contains a single vertex of
V_{1}
,

\textstyle|C|=\sum_{v\in V_{1}}\sum_{e\in C}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e|=\sum_{v\in V_{1}}\deg_{C}(v).

Since
\deg_{C}(v)
is even, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
, this implies that
|C|
is even.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}^{k}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
and
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
.
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
if and only if

T_{i}=T\cap V_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, have cardinalities of equal parity.
Moreover, if
T
satisfies this condition, then the minimum cardinality of a
T
-join
is
\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
.

Proof.

If
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and if
J
is a
T
-join then,
for every
i=1,\dots,k
,

(7)

\textstyle|J|=\sum_{v\in T_{i}}\deg_{J}(v)+\sum_{v\in V_{i}\setminus T_{i}}\deg_{J}(v)\equiv_{2}|T_{i}|.

Hence
|T_{i}|
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, have equal parity.
Conversely, let
T\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
be such that
T_{i}=T\cap V_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, have cardinalities
of equal parity. Denote
r=\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
. Consider the sequence of elements of

T_{i}
written as
w_{1}^{i},\dots,w_{r}^{i}
, where, the first
|T_{i}|
in this sequence
are the members of
T_{i}
(without repetitions) and, if
|T_{i}|<r
,
the last
r-|T_{i}|
of them are equal to
w^{i}_{1}
.
Consider the set of
r
edges

J=\left\{\left\{w^{1}_{i},\dots,w^{k}_{i}\right\}:i=1,\dots,r\right\}.

If
v\not\in T
then, clearly
\deg_{J}(v)=0
. If
v=w^{i}_{j}\in T_{i}
, with
2\leq j\leq|T_{i}|
then

\deg_{J}(v)=1
. If
v=w^{i}_{1}\in T_{i}
then, because

|T_{i}|\equiv_{2}r
,
\deg_{J}(v)=r-|T_{i}|+1\equiv_{2}1
.
We deduce that
J
is a
T
-join of cardinality
r=\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
.
It remains to be proved that this is the minimum cardinality of a
T
-join.
If
J^{\prime}
any
T
-join, then, applying the equality in (
7
) to
J^{\prime}
,
we get

\textstyle|J^{\prime}|\geq\sum_{v\in T_{i}}\deg_{J^{\prime}}(v)\geq|T_{i}|,

for every
i=1,\dots,k
. We conclude that
|J^{\prime}|\geq\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
.
∎

Theorem 8.4
.

If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}^{k}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}
, then

\begin{cases}\log_{2}\bigl(\deg K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{H}\bigr)=\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\bigr)-k\\
\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

As for the degree, using Theorem
7.2
, we only need to show that

(8)

\textstyle\log_{2}|\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})|=(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i})-k+1.

But this is now straightforward from Proposition
8.3
.
Let us now deal with the statement on the regularity of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
By Theorem
6.5
we must show that
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})=\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
.
Let
J\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}
be a parity join and let
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
be the set of vertices,
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
,
such that
\deg_{J}(v)
is odd. Since by Proposition
8.2

there are no elements of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
of odd cardinality, using
Proposition
6.1
,
we deduce that
|J|
is the minimum cardinality of a
T
-join. Since we showed
in Proposition
8.3
that the minimum cardinality
of a
T
-join is
\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
, where, recall,
T_{i}=T\cap V_{i}
, we conclude
that
|J|\leq\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
. This shows that

\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})\leq\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}.

To prove the opposite inequality, it suffices to show that there exists a parity join of
cardinality
\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
. Arguing as before, this is the same as showing that
there exists
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
such that
\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}=\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
.
Let
1\leq m\leq k
be such that
a_{m}=\max\left\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\}
. If
a_{m}
is even, fix

T=V_{m}
, if
a_{m}
is odd, then, choosing
v_{i}\in V_{i}
, for all
i\not=m
, set

T=\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m-1}\right\}\cup V_{m}\cup\left\{v_{m+1},\dots,v_{k}\right\}
. In both cases

T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and
a_{m}=\max_{i=1}^{k}|T_{i}|
.
∎

Definition 8.5
.

A hypergraph is called complete of rank
k
if
|V_{\mathcal{H}}|\geq k
and

E_{\mathcal{H}}
is the set of cardinality
k
subsets of
V_{\mathcal{H}}
. Let us denote a complete
hypergraph of rank
k
, with
n
vertices, by
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{n}
.

Recall that we are assuming throughout this article that
k\geq 2
.

Proposition 8.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}^{k}_{n}
. (i) If
n=k
then
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})=\left\{\emptyset,V_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}
.
(ii) If
n>k
and
k
is odd, then
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathcal{P}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
.
(iii) If
n>k
and
k
is even, then
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
is equal to the set of elements of
\mathcal{P}(V_{\mathcal{H}})

of even cardinality.

Proof.

(i) If
n=k
and
V_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\right\}
then
E_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\right\}\right\}

and therefore a
T
-join exists if and only if
T=\emptyset
or
T=\left\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\right\}=V_{\mathcal{H}}
.
(ii) Assume that
n>k
and
k
is odd. To show that
\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathcal{P}(V_{\mathcal{H}})

it suffices to show that
\left\{v\right\}\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
, for all
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Fix
v_{0}\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
and let
V
be any subset of
V_{\mathcal{H}}\setminus\left\{v_{0}\right\}
with cardinality

k
. Consider:

J=\left\{\left\{v_{0}\right\}\cup J^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\subset V\text{ and }|J^{\prime}|=k-1\right\}\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}.

As
\deg_{J}(v_{0})=\binom{k}{k-1}=k
is odd,

\deg_{J}(w)=\binom{k-1}{k-2}=k-1
is even, for every
w\in V
, and

\deg_{J}(v)=0
for every
v\not\in V\cup\left\{v_{0}\right\}
, we deduce that

\deg_{J}(v)\text{ is odd}\iff v\in\left\{v_{0}\right\}.

In other words,
J
is
\left\{v_{0}\right\}
-join. (iii) Assume that
n>k
and
k
is even. Let
T\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
and let
J
be a
T
-join.
By Proposition
3.2
,
|T|
is even. To prove that

\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
is equal to the set of subsets of
V_{\mathcal{H}}
of even cardinality, it suffices
to show that, for every pair of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
,

\left\{v_{1},v_{2}\right\}\in\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
. Let us fix
v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Choose
V\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}\setminus\left\{v_{1},v_{2}\right\}
of
cardinality
k-1
. Consider

J=\left\{\left\{u,v\right\}\cup J^{\prime}:J^{\prime}\subset V\text{ and }|J^{\prime}|=k-2\right\}\subset E_{\mathcal{H}}.

Arguing as before we deduce that
J
is a
\left\{v_{1},v_{2}\right\}
-join.
∎

Corollary 8.7
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}_{n}^{k}
. (i) If
n=k
, then
\deg K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{H}
is equal to
1
.
(ii) If
n>k
, then
\deg K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{H}
is equal to
2^{n-1}
.

Proof.

(i) If
n=k
then
|E_{H}|=1
hence
I_{\mathcal{H}}=(0)
and
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]
is
1
-dimensional.
(ii) There are two cases. Assume first that
k
is odd. Then, by Proposition
3.2
,
no element of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
has odd cardinality. Therefore, by Theorem
7.2
,
the degree of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is equal to
\frac{|\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})|}{2}
, which, by Proposition
8.6
,
is equal to
2^{n-1}
. Let us now assume that
k
is even. Let
V\subset V_{\mathcal{H}}
be a subset of
vertices of cardinality
k+1
and
let
J
be the set of edges with vertices in
V
. Then

\textstyle\deg_{J}(v)=\binom{k}{k-1}=k

is even, for every
v\in V
while
\deg_{J}(v)=0
, for every
v\in V_{\mathcal{H}}\setminus V
.
In other words,
J
is an even subset of edges. Since
|J|=\binom{k+1}{k}=k+1
we conclude that

\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
contains elements of odd cardinality. Hence the degree of
K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}
is

|\mathcal{T}(E_{\mathcal{H}})|
, which, by Proposition
8.6
, is equal to

2^{n-1}
.
∎

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{K}_{n}^{3}
. If
n=3
, then
\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=0
.
If
n=4
, then
\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=4
.
If
n\geq 5
, then
\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor
.

Proof.

If
n=3
then
|E_{\mathcal{H}}|=1
and therefore, by Theorem
4.4
,
I_{\mathcal{H}}=(0)
,
and hence
\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=0
. Assume now that
n=4
and let
C\in\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
.
By Proposition
3.2
,
|C|
must be even and hence

|C|=0
,
2
or
4
. A set of two distinct edges yields two vertices of degree

2
and two of degree
1
. A set of four edges is the whole of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
,
which yields all vertices of degree
3
. Hence we must have
|C|=0
. In other words,

\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})=\left\{\emptyset\right\}
and, consequently, all subsets of
E_{\mathcal{H}}
are parity joins.
Therefore
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})=|E_{\mathcal{H}}|=4
and, by Theorem
6.5
,

\operatorname{reg}K[E_{\mathcal{H}}]/I_{\mathcal{H}}=3
.

Let us now assume that

. As no element of
\mathcal{E}(E_{\mathcal{H}})
has odd cardinality,
by Proposition
6.1
, parity

-joins. We will use this to show
that
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathcal{H})=\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor+1
. Note that, by Proposition
8.6
,

\mathcal{T}(V_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathcal{P}(V_{\mathcal{H}})
. Given

let us denote by
\tau(\mathcal{H},T)
the minimum cardinality
of a
T
-join. If
T=\emptyset
then
\tau(\mathcal{H},T)=0
. If
|T|=1
, say

, then

\tau(\mathcal{H},T)\leq 3
since there exists a
T
-join of cardinality
3
, as is shown in Figure
2
.

Assume now

\textstyle\tau(\mathcal{H},T)\leq\lfloor\frac{|T|+1}{3}\rfloor+1.

is a

and

.

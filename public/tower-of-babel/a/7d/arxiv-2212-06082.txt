Modified propagation of gravitational waves from the early radiation era
and Axel Brandenburg

Abstract
We study the propagation of cosmological gravitational wave (GW) backgrounds from the early radiation era until the present day in modified theories of gravity. Comparing to general relativity (GR), we study the effects that modified gravity parameters, such as the GW friction α M \alpha_{\rm M} and the tensor speed excess α T \alpha_{\rm T} , have on the present-day GW spectrum. We use both the WKB estimate, which provides an analytical description but fails at superhorizon scales, and numerical simulations that allow us to go beyond the WKB approximation. We show that a constant α T \alpha_{\rm T} makes relatively insignificant changes to the GR solution, especially taking into account the constraints on its value from GW observations by the LIGO–Virgo collaboration, while α M \alpha_{\rm M} can introduce modifications to the spectral slopes of the GW energy spectrum in the low-frequency regime depending on the considered time evolution of α M \alpha_{\rm M} . The latter effect is additional to the damping or growth occurring equally at all scales that can be predicted by the WKB approximation. In light of the recent observations by pulsar timing array (PTA) collaborations, and the potential observations by future detectors such as SKA, LISA, DECIGO, BBO, or ET, we show that, in most of the cases, constraints cannot be placed on the effects of α M \alpha_{\rm M} and the initial GW energy density ℰ GW ∗ \mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*} separately, but only on the combined effects of the two, unless the signal is observed at different frequency ranges. In particular, we provide some constraints on the combined effects from the reported PTA observations.

\displaystyle\,h_{*}(k)\cos k\tilde{c}_{\rm T}(\eta{\cal H}_{*}-1)

\displaystyle\,+\frac{c_{\rm T}^{*}h_{*}^{\prime}(k)+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\bigl(c_{\rm T}^{*}\,\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}+{c_{\rm T}^{\prime}}^{*}\bigr)h_{*}(k)}{k{c_{\rm T}^{*}}^{2}}\sin k\tilde{c}_{\rm T}(\eta{\cal H}_{*}-1)\biggr].

(3.7)

The general solution reduces to the following when
c_{\rm T}=1

at all times,

\chi(k,\eta)=h_{*}(k)\cos k(\eta\mathcal{H}_{*}-1)+\frac{h_{*}^{\prime}(k)+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}\,h_{*}(k)}{k}\sin k(\eta\mathcal{H}_{*}-1).

(3.8)

This expression holds both in GR with
h(k,\eta)=\chi(k,\eta)
and

\alpha_{\rm M}=0
,
and in MG theories with
h(k,\eta)=e^{-\mathcal{D}}\chi(k,\eta)
and
\alpha_{\rm M}\neq 0
.

3.2
Limitations of the WKB approximation

To obtain the WKB solution, we have neglected two terms in equation (
3.3
), which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{A^{\prime\prime}}{A}-\frac{\tilde{a}^{\prime\prime}}{\tilde{a}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{\rm T}^{\prime\prime}}{2c_{\rm T}}+\frac{3c_{\rm T}^{\prime 2}}{4c_{\rm T}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm M}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm M}\Big)\mathcal{H}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}\mathcal{H}-\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm M}\Big),

(3.9)

where we have used
{\cal H}^{\prime}=a^{\prime\prime}/a-{\cal H}^{2}
.
Hence, the WKB assumption could break down when at least one
of these terms is not negligible when compared to
k^{2}c_{\rm T}^{2}
.
First, we obtain the limiting values of
k
due to
c_{\rm T}
and its derivatives in the first two terms on the right-hand side of equation (
3.9
)

k_{\rm lim,c_{\rm T}^{\prime\prime}}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{|c_{\rm T}^{\prime\prime}|}{2c_{\rm T}^{3}}},\quad k_{\rm lim,c_{\rm T}^{\prime}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{3}|c_{\rm T}^{\prime}|}{2c_{\rm T}^{2}}.

(3.10)

Note that in this paper we only consider
c_{\rm T}
to be constant in time,
i.e.,
k_{\rm lim,c_{\rm T}^{\prime\prime}}
and
k_{\rm lim,c_{\rm T}^{\prime}}
are zero.
We defer the study of time-varying
c_{\rm T}
and its effects to future work.

In addition,
we have one term that depends on
\alpha_{\rm M}
, one term that
depends on the time evolution of
\alpha_{\rm M}
, and one term
that depends on
a^{\prime\prime}/a
.
The latter appears also in GR when WKB is used to approximate the
solution for the scaled strains, which do not decay (note that
the decay of the physical strains is already absorbed by the
scale factor), as shown in equation (
3.8
).
This term is given in equation (
2.9
) and its upper
bound can be found using Friedmann equations

\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\lesssim\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Omega_{{\rm mat},0}}{\Omega_{{\rm rad},0}}\frac{g_{*}}{g_{*}^{0}}\biggl(\frac{g_{\rm S}}{g_{\rm S}^{0}}\biggr)^{-{4\over 3}}{\cal H}\,\frac{a_{*}}{a_{0}}\lesssim 10^{4}\,h^{2}\,{\cal H}\,\frac{a_{*}}{a_{0}},

(3.11)

where the first inequality is an approximation valid during
the RD era and it decays during MD.
Hence, the upper bound is valid at all times and its specific value depends
on
a_{*}/a_{0}
.
For example, for GWs generated at the EWPT or at the QCDPT, this leads to the
following limiting wave numbers (for
h=0.67
), at which the WKB approximation might
break down,

\displaystyle\,k_{{\rm lim},\,a^{\prime\prime}}^{\rm EW}\sim 1.87\times 10^{-6}\,{\cal H}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bigl|1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}\bigr|}{c_{\rm T}}}\leq 1.87\times 10^{-6}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bigl|1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}\bigr|}{c_{\rm T}}},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\,k_{{\rm lim},\,a^{\prime\prime}}^{\rm QCD}\sim 5.90\times 10^{-5}\,{\cal H}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bigl|1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}\bigr|}{c_{\rm T}}}\leq 5.90\times 10^{-5}\,\sqrt{\frac{\bigl|1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}\bigr|}{c_{\rm T}}}.

(3.13)

In both cases, this term is subdominant up to very large
superhorizon scales and it is bounded by the values in equations (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) since
{\cal H}=\eta_{*}/\eta

during RD era as can be seen using equation (
2.9
).
This limit can be modified by the inclusion of
\alpha_{\rm M}
and
\alpha_{\rm T}

but unless they take large values these modifications are negligible.

On the other hand, two additional limitations to
the WKB approximation appear due to the
\alpha_{\rm M}
parameter,

\displaystyle k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}}

\displaystyle\,\sim\frac{{\cal H}}{c_{\rm T}}\sqrt{\biggl|\frac{\alpha_{\rm M}}{2}\Bigl(1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}\Bigr)\biggr|}\lesssim\frac{1}{c_{\rm T}}\sqrt{\frac{|\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}|}{2}},

(3.14)

\displaystyle k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\,\sim\frac{1}{c_{\rm T}}\sqrt{\frac{{\cal H}\,|\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}|}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{\rm T}}\sqrt{\frac{|\alpha^{{}^{\prime}*}_{\rm M}|}{2}},

(3.15)

where we can neglect the term
{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}
in front of 1 in the
first limit
for small values of
\alpha_{\rm M}
.
Hence, the WKB limit can break down in MG
around the horizon or at larger scales.
In general, we expect the limit from
\alpha_{\rm M}
to be more
restrictive than that from
\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}
and to dominate
at the initial time when
{\cal H}=1
.
However, this depends on the parameterization of
\alpha_{\rm M}
,
which can give different results for both limits; see section
4
.

3.3
GW spectrum using the WKB approximation

The spectrum of GW energy density can be expressed as

\Omega_{\rm GW}(k,\eta)=\frac{1}{\rho_{{\rm crit},0}}\frac{{\rm\,d}\rho_{\rm GW}}{{\rm\,d}\ln k}=\frac{1}{6}\biggl(\frac{H_{*}}{H_{0}}\biggr)^{2}\biggl(\frac{a_{*}}{a_{0}}\biggr)^{4}kS_{h^{\prime}}(k,\eta),

(3.16)

where
2S_{h^{\prime}}
is the spectrum
6
6

6

Following ref.
[
73
]
, we define the spectrum

S_{h^{\prime}}(k,\eta)
from the
+
and
\times
polarization
modes, giving an extra factor of 2 due to the property

\tilde{h}^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\eta)\,\tilde{h}^{{}^{\prime}*}_{ij}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}^{\prime},\eta)=2\bigl[h^{\prime}_{+}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\eta)\,h^{{}^{\prime}*}_{+}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}^{\prime},\eta)+h^{\prime}_{\times}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\eta)\,h^{{}^{\prime}*}_{\times}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}^{\prime},\eta)\bigr].

of the scaled strain

\langle\tilde{h}_{ij}^{\prime}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{},\eta)\,\tilde{h}_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}*}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}^{\prime},\eta)\rangle=(2\pi)^{6}\delta^{3}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle k$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle k$}}{}^{\prime})\frac{2S_{h^{\prime}}(k,\eta)}{4\pi k^{2}}.

(3.17)

Taking
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm M}\mathcal{H}
,
the modified GW energy spectrum can be obtained using the WKB
solution for MG, given in equation (

),
and assuming that at time

such that
|h^{\prime}_{*}(k)|=k\,|h_{*}(k)|

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}

\displaystyle\,(k,\eta)=e^{-2\mathcal{D}}\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm GR}(k,\eta)\,\xi(k,\eta),

(3.18)

where
\xi(k,\eta)
corresponds to the spectral modifications with

\mathcal{D}
and assuming constant-in-time
c_{\rm T}

(see appendix
B
for details and, in particular, equation (
B.9
) for generic time-dependent
c_{\rm T}
),

\xi(k,\eta)=1+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\rm T}+\frac{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}}{2k}+\frac{1}{8k^{2}}\Biggl[\alpha_{\rm M}^{2}{\cal H}^{2}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{c_{\rm T}^{2}}\biggr)+\alpha_{\rm M}^{*2}\Biggr]+\frac{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}\alpha_{\rm M}^{2}{\cal H}^{2}}{8k^{3}c_{\rm T}^{2}}+\frac{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*2}\alpha_{\rm M}^{2}{\cal H}^{2}}{32k^{4}c_{\rm T}^{2}}.

(3.19)

Note that we have averaged over oscillations in time
\eta
,
such that the expression is valid at
k\,\tilde{c}_{\rm T}\gtrsim(\eta{\cal H}_{*}-1)^{-1}
.
At late times,
{\cal H}^{2}=(\eta_{*}/\eta)^{2}
becomes small, so we can neglect the
terms that dilute with
{\cal H}^{2}
,

\xi(k,\eta\gg\eta_{*})={\textstyle{1\over 2}}\bigl(1+\alpha_{\rm T}\bigr)+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}}{2k}\Bigr)^{2}.

(3.20)

From equation (
3.20
), we can identify the IR and UV
limiting ranges of the spectrum,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm IR}=\Omega_{\rm GW}\Bigl(k\ll{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}^{*},\eta\gg\eta_{*}\Bigr)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}}^{2}}{8k^{2}}\,e^{-2\mathcal{D}}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm GR}(k,\eta),

(3.21)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm UV}=\Omega_{\rm GW}\Bigl(k\gg{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm M}^{*},\eta\gg\eta_{*}\Bigr)=

\displaystyle\,\Bigl(1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm T}\Bigr)\,e^{-2\mathcal{D}}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm GR}(k,\eta).

(3.22)

Note that the factor
{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}}^{2}
is part of the IR limit,
indicating that the IR enhancement of the form
k^{-2}
holds
regardless of the sign of
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}
.
The critical
k_{\rm crit}={\textstyle{1\over 2}}|\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}|
indicates where the IR regime begins,
i.e., where the
k^{-2}
term in equation (
3.20
) becomes dominant.

Equation (
3.19
) shows that the GW spectrum in the IR regime can
present up to
k^{-4}
modifications to the
GR spectrum but this and other terms vanish as time
evolves since they are proportional to
{\cal H}^{2}
.
Hence, at late times, we end up with the GR spectrum
amplified (or diluted) for negative (or positive)
\mathcal{D}
by
e^{-2\mathcal{D}}
at all wave numbers
with a
k^{-2}
enhancement in the IR regime,
proportional to the additional
{\textstyle{1\over 8}}{\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}}^{2}

factor, as shown in equation (
3.21
).
In the UV regime, we find the
e^{-2\mathcal{D}}
enhancement (or depletion) and an
additional factor
1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm T}
, which is, in general, a function of
\eta
.
The sign of
\alpha_{\rm T}
determines if the GW spectrum is
amplified or decreased with respect to that obtained from
GR.

We have found that, according to the WKB approximation,
the parameter
\alpha_{\rm M}
introduces changes in the spectral shape
at
k\leq k_{\rm crit}={\textstyle{1\over 2}}|\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}|
that do not dilute as time
evolves and depend on the value of
\alpha_{\rm M}
only at the time of
GW generation.
On the other hand, note that, using equations (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we can estimate
the WKB approximation to break down around

k\sim\max(k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}},k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}})
.
This means that when
{\cal H}\sim 1
, the spectral changes occur at wave numbers around and below the
critical
k_{\rm crit}
,
where the WKB approximation might not be valid (for
c_{\rm T}\sim 1
).

We investigate the resulting spectra in MG using
numerical simulations in section
5

with the objective to test the validity of the WKB
approximation and its potential limitations and to confirm the resulting GW spectra
when MG parameters are introduced.

4
Phenomenological parameterizations

In section
3.3
, we noted that, according to the WKB
approximation, a constant
\alpha_{\rm T}\neq 0
induces modifications in the total GW energy density, but not
in its spectral shape as long as
\alpha_{\rm T}\ll 1
.
Realistically, the tensor speed excess at the present day is observationally constrained to be
\alpha_{\rm T,0}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-15})

by the binary neutron star merger GW170817 and its gamma-ray burst GRB 170817A
[
64
]
.
This constraint can be circumvented if
\alpha_{\rm T}
is either frequency- or time-dependent, such that larger deviations could hide outside the LIGO–Virgo frequency
band
[
69
,
90
]

or in the past
[
91
,
92
]
.
In this study, we show that even using larger values of

\alpha_{\rm T}
, constant in time and frequency, the modifications to
the GW spectrum are negligible, which is seen from the WKB
approximation and can be confirmed with numerical simulations.
Hence, from now on, we will focus on two cases:

(i)

constant
\alpha_{\rm T}
with
\alpha_{\rm M}=0
,
and
(ii)

\alpha_{\rm T}=0
allowing
\alpha_{\rm M}
to take
different values as a function of time.

In particular, we are interested in the perspectives of negative values of
\alpha_{\rm M}
,
as they would enhance the amplitude of the GW spectrum.
Various constraints on the present-day value
\alpha_{\rm M,0}
exist in
the literature,
but are significantly less stringent than those on
\alpha_{\rm T}
.
Assuming the cosmic acceleration due to scalar-tensor theories
of gravity, it can be shown that
|\alpha_{\rm M}|\lesssim 0.5
at
late times (see figure 4 in ref.
[
54
]
).
This is compatible with the numerical results of ref.
[
59
]
(see their figure 2), giving
|\alpha_{\rm M,0}|\lesssim 1
.
On the other hand, the Planck Collaboration reports
a slightly tighter constraint on
\alpha_{\rm M,0}\gtrsim-0.1

(see table 8 of ref.
[
6
]
).
Note that they parameterize
\alpha_{\rm M}=\alpha_{\rm M,0}\,(a/a_{0})^{n}
with

n\in(0.5,1)
.
In addition, to avoid having an infinite number of GW sources in the early Universe,

d_{\rm GW}
is required to increase monotonically at earlier times,
i.e.,
{\rm\,d}(d_{\rm GW})/{\rm\,d}a\leq 0
, as argued in ref.
[
57
]
.
Hence,
using equations (
2.7
)–(
2.8
), one gets a bound for
\alpha_{\rm M}
,

\alpha_{\rm M}(a)\geq\frac{a_{0}}{a\sqrt{\Omega(a)}}\Bigg[\int_{a}^{a_{0}}\frac{a_{0}\,da^{\prime}}{a^{\prime 2}\sqrt{\Omega(a^{\prime})}}\Bigg]^{-1}-1,

(4.1)

which is satisfied by all positive values
of
\alpha_{\rm M}
and for negative values with
|\alpha_{\rm M}|\leq|\alpha_{\rm M}(a_{*})|\lesssim 1
.
Taking these different constraints into account,
we explore a range of
\alpha_{\rm M,0}\in[-0.5,0.3]
.

Besides the simplest consideration of a constant
\alpha_{\rm M}
in time,
which we call choice 0,
its time-dependent forms can be written in accordance with
specific gravity models
[
93
]
.
Following refs.
[
94
,
77
,
95
]
, we choose phenomenological forms of
\alpha_{\rm M}

as simplified parameterizations motivated by effective descriptions
of modified gravity
[
96
]
.
Specifically:

\alpha_{\rm M}(\eta)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\rm M,0}\biggl[\frac{a(\eta)}{a_{0}}\biggr]^{n}&(\text{choice I}),\\
\alpha_{\rm M,0}\frac{1}{\Omega(\eta)}&(\text{choice II}),\\
\alpha_{\rm M,0}\frac{1-\Omega_{\rm mat}(\eta)/\Omega(\eta)}{1-\Omega_{{\rm mat},0}}&(\text{choice III}),\end{cases}

(4.2)

where choices II and III give a value of
\alpha_{\rm M}
proportional
to the percentage of dark energy density
and the combination of dark and radiation
energy densities, respectively, at each time
\eta
compared to
their relative amounts at the present time.
The exponent
n
in choice I needs to satisfy the following stability conditions

[
97
]
:

\mbox{stability for:}\;\begin{cases}0<n<{\textstyle{3\over 2}}\,\Omega_{{\rm mat},0}\simeq{\textstyle{1\over 2}},\ &\mbox{if}\;\alpha_{\rm M,0}>0,\\
n>{\textstyle{3\over 2}},\ &\mbox{if}\;\alpha_{\rm M,0}<0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

To provide an intuition on the aforementioned
\alpha_{\rm M}
parameterizations,
we show in figure
1
(left panel), for the different time dependencies
given in equation (
4.2
), the evolution of
\alpha_{\rm M}\mathcal{H}
, which
characterizes the growth or damping produced by
\alpha_{\rm M}
, according to
the WKB approximation [see equation (
3.4
)].
We also show the time evolutions of
k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}}\,c_{\rm T}
and

k_{{\rm lim},\,\alpha_{\rm M}^{\prime}}\,c_{\rm T}
(right panel), which correspond to the wave numbers
at which the WKB approximation might break down,
according to equations (
3.14
) and (
3.15
).
Figure
1

of generation (e.g., EWPT) up to present time.
We see that for choices 0 and III,

\Lambda

On the other hand, for choices I and II,
\alpha_{\rm M}{\cal H}
is negligible
for all of RD and most of MD and rapidly increases later on, especially during
\Lambda

For all parameterizations, the GW friction converges to the value
\alpha_{\rm M,0}

Note that, since many efforts of modifying gravity are aimed at
addressing the late-time acceleration of the universe, the

This is indeed the case for choices I and II here.
If one considers only the MD and
\Lambda

However, here we explore the entire history of the universe from well within RD,

\alpha_{\rm M}\mathcal{H}

(note, however, a brief discussion of
\alpha_{\rm M}=-1

[
101
]
).
Hence,
we emphasize the aim of this work to provide an
understanding of the phenomenological behavior of the GW spectrum due to
\alpha_{\rm M}

for signals produced during RD, although the GW background computed in section
3.3
for MG is relevant in the most general case.

5
Numerical solutions

To explore the limits and validity of the WKB approximation, we use
the
Pencil Code
to numerically solve the GW equation under
MG, given in equation (
2.3
).
The
Pencil Code
is a highly parallelized modular code that can be
used to solve various differential equations
[
72
]
.
In the context of cosmological GWs, it has previously been used to study GWs generated by hydrodynamic and
MHD stresses in the early universe
[
79
]
.
It uses a GW solver that advances the strains at each time step
sourced by the anisotropic stresses that are separately computed as the
solution to the MHD equations
[
73
]
.
Previous numerical work solved the GW equation under GR while, in the
present work, we have extended the code to solve equation (
2.3
); see
appendix
C
for further details on the numerical scheme.

5.1
Initial condition and time stepping schemes

In the current work, we focus primarily on the propagation rather than the production of GWs.
Therefore, we have adapted the
Pencil Code
to evolve an initial GW spectrum
in the absence of sources
with a spectral shape and amplitude based on those obtained in previous studies (see, e.g., refs.
[
70
,
84
]
).
Hence, equation (
2.3
) is solved in one dimension
(i.e., in
k>0
) in order to improve the efficiency
of the code when studying the propagation of a GW background
along the history of the universe.
We take the initial spectrum for the time derivative of the strains,
defined in equation (
3.17
),
to be a smoothed double broken power law described by

S_{h^{\prime}}(k,\eta_{*})=S_{h^{\prime}}^{*}\frac{\biggl[1+\Bigl(\frac{k_{\ast}}{k_{\rm b}}\Bigr)^{a-b}\biggr]\Big(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\Big)^{a}}{\bigg[1+\Big(\frac{k}{k_{\rm b}}\Big)^{(a-b)\alpha_{1}}\bigg]^{1\over\alpha_{1}}\bigg[1+\Big(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\Big)^{(b+c)\alpha_{2}}\bigg]^{1\over\alpha_{2}}},

(5.1)

where
S_{h^{\prime}}^{*}
and
k_{*}
are approximately the initial amplitude and position of the peak, respectively,

\eta_{*}\approx{\cal H}_{*}^{-1}
corresponds to the time of GW production
during the RD era,
and
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=2
are fixed smoothness parameters.
We similarly initialize the spectrum of the strains
S_{h}(k,\eta_{*})=S_{h^{\prime}}(k,\eta_{*})/k^{2}
.
We choose the slope in the IR range to be
k^{2}
, set by
a=2

(as is expected for causal sources
of GWs like the ones produced
during a phase transition)
up to the break wave number

5.2
Time evolution

For constant
\alpha_{\rm T}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{\rm M}=0

stays constant in time and its magnitude is
modified by the specific value of
\alpha_{\rm T}

1+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\alpha_{\rm T}

\alpha_{\rm T}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-1})

1

which would become much smaller if we restrict
\alpha_{\rm T}
to the present-time

7
Conclusions

GWs in modified gravity exhibit features in their
energy spectra different from what can be expected from GR.
We have explored the significance of such features in terms of the spectral
slopes and amplitudes in different frequency ranges, under different
time-dependent functional forms and values of two modification
parameters — the GW friction
\alpha_{\rm M}
and the tensor speed excess
\alpha_{\rm T}
.

We have computed the expected GW energy density spectra using the WKB
approximation, and have found that the GW energy density depends on the
time evolution of
\alpha_{\rm T}
and
\alpha_{\rm M}
, while its spectral shape only
depends on
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}
at the time of
GW generation,and the time dependence of
\alpha_{\rm T}
.
However, a number of limiting scales below which the WKB approximation might
not hold have been
identified in equations (
3.12
)–(
3.15
),
motivating numerical studies to go beyond the WKB estimate,
especially at superhorizon scales.
For this purpose, we have performed numerical simulations using the

Pencil Code
to study the propagation of GWs along the
history of the universe from their time of generation, assumed to be
during the RD era, e.g., at the EWPT or at the QCDPT, until the present
time.

The current-day value of the tensor speed excess is already tightly constrained by the precise measurement of the speed of GWs to
\alpha_{\rm T,0}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-15})
.
In this paper, we have found that the modifications
on the GW energy density spectra introduced by
a constant

\alpha_{\rm T}
are negligible,
even when
\alpha_{\rm T}
takes larger values (as it might be allowed where
it is not constrained, e.g., in the past or at frequencies out of the
LIGO–Virgo band).
In addition,
\alpha_{\rm T}
can modify the dispersion relation, leading
to a shift in
\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)
by
c_{\rm T}
.
On the other hand,
\alpha_{\rm M}
effectively acts as an (anti-)damping term in the GW equation
and can impart changes to the energy spectrum of GWs
with potential implications to future observations.
Considering sources of GWs at the EWPT and QCDPT,
the present-day GW spectrum is expected to peak in the mHz and nHz frequency bands, respectively.

Compared to the standard GW spectra obtained in GR,
the changes due to
\alpha_{\rm M}
are characterized by an overall boost or
depletion factor
e^{-2\mathcal{D}}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is given in equation (
3.4
).
However, the GW energy density at present time due to modified gravity is degenerate with the produced density at the time of generation
\mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*}
.
Therefore, potential observations or non-observations of the GW
spectra by PTA, SKA, LISA, BBO, DECIGO, ET, or others, could only
place bounds on the quantity
e^{-2\mathcal{D}}(\eta_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*}
and not on
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}

or
\mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*}
separately if we only consider the amplitude at the peak of the GW spectrum.
For example, we have provided some upper bounds on
e^{-2\mathcal{D}}(\eta_{0})\,\mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*}

using the reported observations of a common-process spectrum over several
pulsars by the different PTA collaborations; see section
6
.

In addition, the GW spectrum also presents
spectral modifications
\xi(k)
around the horizon and at superhorizon scales.
If
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}<0
, the modification leads to an enhancement of the
GW spectrum
\xi(k)\sim k^{-\beta_{0}}
with
\beta_{0}\in(0,2)
, which flattens the low-frequency part of the spectrum; see table
2
.
The enhancement found from the numerical simulations is different than
the one predicted by the WKB
estimate (
\beta_{0}=2
) but it still
increases the detection prospects of the low-frequency
tail of the GW spectra.
We also find more moderate modifications to the spectral shape
around the horizon scale for all
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}
and at all scales when
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}>0
.
These modifications allow us to characterize the resulting spectra due to MG.

The degeneracy between
\alpha_{\rm M}
and

\mathcal{E}_{\rm GW}^{*}
can be broken by observing the
signal at multiple frequency bands,
as
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}
leads to a low-frequency spectral
enhancement, while under GR
such enhancement does not appear.
This implies that, for example, a GW spectrum peaking in the mHz band can
potentially have its tail in the nHz band, above PTA or SKA sensitivities.
The observation of such a low-frequency tail would then be a direct indication
of a non-zero
\alpha_{\rm M}
at the time at which the GW signal has been produced
since the slope and amplitude would only depend on
\alpha_{\rm M}^{*}
.

Overall, a cosmological GW spectrum spanning many orders of magnitudes in frequency has the potential to constrain the parameter space of modified theories of gravity,
especially if the modifications occur in the early universe such as the RD era.

Appendix A
Friedmann equations

Friedmann equations can be expressed in

and a smooth equation of state (EOS)

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{1}{2}H_{0}^{2}\,\Omega(a)\bigl[1+3w(a)\bigr],\quad\biggl(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\biggr)^{2}=H_{0}^{2}\,\Omega(a),

\displaystyle\Omega(a)=\frac{\rho(a)}{\rho_{{\rm crit},0}}=

\displaystyle\,\Omega_{\rm rad}(a)+\Omega_{\rm mat}(a)+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\biggl({a\over a_{0}}\biggr)^{-4}\,\frac{g_{*}}{g_{*}^{0}}\biggl(\frac{g_{\rm S}}{g_{\rm S}^{0}}\biggr)^{-{4\over 3}}\,\Omega_{{\rm rad},0}+\biggl({a\over a_{0}}\biggr)^{-3}\,\Omega_{{\rm mat},0}+\Omega_{\Lambda,0}.

(A.2)

[

]
,

Appendix B
GW energy spectrum via WKB approximation

In this section, we present the derivation of the GW spectrum obtained using the WKB approximation introduced in section

.
We start with the WKB solution for
\chi=e^{\mathcal{D}}h
, given in equation (
3.7

11
11

11

We neglect here the effects of MG during the GW production and focus on the MG effects after the GWs start to propagate. Otherwise,

Appendix C
Numerical scheme

Following refs.
[
73
,
86
]
,
we implement a matrix solver for the modified GW equation.
Equation (
2.3
) can be expressed as

h_{ij}^{\prime\prime}+\sigma h_{ij}^{\prime}+\omega^{2}h_{ij}=0,

(C.1)

where

\sigma\equiv\alpha_{\rm M}\mathcal{H}\,,\,\,\omega^{2}\equiv c_{\rm T}^{2}k^{2}-\alpha_{\rm M}\mathcal{H}^{2}-\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}.

(C.2)

Then, assuming a solution of the type
h_{ij}=e_{ij}Ae^{\lambda\eta}
gives
the characteristic equation

\lambda^{2}+\sigma\lambda+\omega^{2}=0.

(C.3)

The eigenvalues
\lambda
can be obtained as

\lambda_{1,2}=-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sigma\mp\sqrt{\sigma^{2}-4\omega^{2}}\Big).

(C.4)

For
\delta\eta\ll 1
,
we approximate the solution by assuming that
\lambda_{1,2}^{\prime}\approx\lambda_{1,2}^{\prime\prime}\approx 0
during the time interval within time steps of the numerical solver.
Then, the solution for the strains in equation (
C.1
) takes the form

\displaystyle h_{ij}(\eta+\delta\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{ij}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}+\mathcal{D}_{ij}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta},

(C.5)

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\prime}(\eta+\delta\eta)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{ij}\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}+\mathcal{D}_{ij}\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta},

(C.6)

where
\mathcal{C}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{D}_{ij}
are constant amplitude coefficients evaluated at
\eta
.
Equivalently, in matrix form,
the solution can be rewritten as

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta+\delta\eta}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\\
\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}\\
\mathcal{D}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}.

(C.7)

Hence, the amplitude coefficients can be obtained as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}\\
\mathcal{D}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\\
\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta+\delta\eta}

(C.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})e^{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\delta\eta}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}&-e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\\
-\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta+\delta\eta},

(C.9)

and, in the limit
\delta\eta\rightarrow 0
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\eta\rightarrow 0}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}\\
\mathcal{D}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{2}&-1\\
-\lambda_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta},

(C.10)

where we assume that
\mathcal{C}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{D}_{ij}
are time-independent within
time steps.
Therefore, the time evolution of the relevant quantities
can be obtained via a matrix multiplication as

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta+\delta\eta}=\mathcal{M}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}_{ij}^{\eta},

(C.11)

where

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}&e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}-e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\\
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}-e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta})&\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}\delta\eta}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}\delta\eta}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.12)

In the case of
\sigma=0
and
\omega^{2}=k^{2}
,
the eigenvalues become
\lambda_{1,2}=\pm ik

and the evolution matrix reduces to the one relevant for GR,

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos{k\delta\eta}&\sin{k\delta\eta}\\
-\sin{k\delta\eta}&\cos{k\delta\eta}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.13)

Appendix D
Numerical accuracy

In this section, we study the numerical accuracy of the runs.
The GW solver in the
Pencil Code
has been made accurate to second order
in time in the presence of a source
T_{ij}
under GR.
However, the GW equation under modified theories of gravity lead to an
equation that can be approximated within small but finite time steps under
the assumption that the coefficients are constant in time,
leading to the numerical solver described in section
C
.
This method is accurate up to first order in the duration of the time step.
As the coefficient variations in time become smaller, the error is
also smaller, but the accuracy of the solver is still of first order.
This is demonstrated in figure
10
,
where we show on the left panel the relative errors in the GW energy during RD and MD,
and on the right panel the error during
\Lambda
D.
Recall that, as introduced in section
5.1
, during RD and MD, we use increasing time steps, represented by
n_{\rm incr}
here.
During
\Lambda
D, we use linear time steps, with the interval
shown as
\eta_{\rm incr}
in figure
10
.

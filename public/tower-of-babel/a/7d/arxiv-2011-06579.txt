Abstract
. This article extends our study of the geometry of the p p -adic eigencurve at a point defined by a weight 1 1 cuspform f f irregular at p p and having complex multiplication, and the implications in Iwasawa and in Hida theories. The novel results include the determination of the Fourier coefficients of certain non-classical p p -adic modular forms belonging to the generalized eigenspace of f f , in terms of p p -adic logarithms of algebraic numbers. We also compute the “mysterious” cross-ratios of the p p -ordinary filtrations of the Hida families containing f f .



Introduction

0.1.
Historical background

In the 1960s Kubota and Leopoldt used Kummer’s congruences involving Bernoulli numbers to define the
p
-adic zeta function
\zeta_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
and Iwasawa formulated his Main Conjecture postulating that
\zeta_{p}
generates the characteristic ideal of a certain
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
-module controlling the class group growth of the number fields contained in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Around the same time Serre
[
43
]
observed that ordinary Eisenstein series vary
p
-adic analytically in the weight, hence can
be interpolated over the
p
-adic analytic weight space
\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{C}_{p})=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
,
thus providing a modular interpretation of the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic zeta as a constant term of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
-adic Eisenstein series.

In the 1980s H. Hida gave a new impulse to the subject by
p
-adically interpolating in
[
36
]
ordinary cuspforms of weight at least
2
.
He introduced a space of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
-adic cuspform which is in a
perfect duality of finite free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
-modules with the corresponding Hecke algebra and proved a Control Theorem (see
[
37
]

when
p
is odd and
[
29
]
when
p=2
). Furthermore, Hida showed that every eigenform as above is the specialization of a unique, up to Galois conjugacy,

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
-adic eigenform, also called a Hida family. The Galois theoretic properties of these families, namely the restriction at the decomposition group at
p
, were studied by B. Mazur and A. Wiles who also made a crucial, for us, extension of Hida Theory to allow classical weight one forms.

Coming back to Iwasawa theory, Eisenstein families play a prominent role in the proof of the Main Conjecture over
\mathbb{Q}
by Mazur and Wiles. They proved one divisibility using Ribet’s Eisenstein ideal method and then deduced the equality via the class number formula. In their own words, while being ‘explicit’ from a certain modular perspective, the approach does not allow to determine whether or not
\zeta_{p}
admits multiple zeros (one merely knows that if that were the case then the characteristic series of the Iwasawa module would have a zero of the same multiplicity). Instead, they relied on the Ferrero–Greenberg Theorem
[
27
]
showing that
the ‘trivial’ zeros of
\zeta_{p}
are simple.

Another remarkable class of Hida families have complex multiplication (CM) by an imaginary quadratic field
K
,
and are obtained by
p
-adic interpolation of classical theta series. Their adjoint
p
-adic
L
-functions
is essentially equal to a Katz anti-cyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-function, and the corresponding anti-cyclotomic
Main Conjecture over
K
has been proven by K. Rubin and independently by H. Hida and J. Tilouine. Similarly to the Eisenstein case, both proofs proceed by proving one divisibility and by invoking the class number formula, but without determining the possible zeros and their multiplicities. The question, analogous to the Ferrero–Greenberg Theorem, of whether the trivial zeros of Katz’ anti-cyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-functions are simple, has remained open
while being reformulated in terms of certain Iwasawa modules, via the mysterious bridge envisioned by Iwasawa. In
[
8
]
we use Hida Theory together with Mazur’s Galois Deformations Theory, to
show that these trivial zeros are indeed simple, provided that a certain anti-cyclotomic
\mathscr{L}
-invariant does not vanish,
as predicted by the Four Exponentials Conjecture in Transcendence Theory.

Explicit results in Hida theory have been notoriously difficult to obtain since the Hecke-Hida algebras are finite and flat over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rsem
, a complete local algebra of Krull dimension
2
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem X\rsem
by a theorem of J.P. Serre.
What has made our approach successful is the passage to the discrete valuation ring
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\lsem X\rsem
, and even to its strict henselization
\Lambda=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\lsem X\rsem
, allowing the use of the
rigid geometry tools brought to the modular world by R. Coleman and B. Mazur in the 1990s.
A central object in this theory is the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve
\mathscr{C}\to\mathcal{W}
first introduced in

[
16
]
(under some technical assumptions which were later removed by K. Buzzard
[
12
]
) as the rigid analytic curve parametrizing systems of Hecke eigenvalues of overconvergent
p
-adic modular forms of finite slope (see §
1
for a detailed presentation).

0.3.
Overconvergent generalized eigenforms

Fourier coefficients of classical eigenforms are related to arithmetic functions such as the partition function or the Dedekind eta function, and are motivic in nature as the corresponding two-dimensional Galois representations occur in the étale cohomology of proper smooth varieties.
Amongst non-classical overconvergent forms, the closest in nature to a classical eigenform
f
, are those belonging
to its generalized eigenspace
S_{\sw(f)}^{\dagger}\lsem f\rsem
.
The very exclusive club of genuine overconvergent generalized eigenforms
S_{\sw(f)}^{\dagger}\lsem f\rsem_{0}

is a natural supplement of the classical subspace in
S_{\sw(f)}^{\dagger}\lsem f\rsem

[
2

1

to attach
p
-adic
L
-functions to classical eigenforms of critical slope, J. Bellaïche classified the possible such examples
in weight
k\geqslant 2
, and concluded that conjecturally the only genuine overconvergent generalized eigenforms
are critical CM forms, whose Fourier coefficients were recently computed by Hsu
[
38
]
.
The first to take up the task in weight
1
were H. Darmon, A. Lauder and V. Rotger
[
19
]

who expressed the Fourier coefficients of a certain
p
-adic overconvergent weight
1
generalized eigenform
in terms of
p
-adic logarithms of algebraic numbers in ring class fields of real quadratic fields.
The underlying classical weight
1
form has real multiplication (RM) and, according to
[
5
]
, it
is the only case in which a
p
-regular weight
1
form admits a genuine overconvergent generalized eigenspace.
Such generalized eigenforms could provide an approach to Hilbert’s twelfth problem
asking for an “explicit Class Field Theory” for real quadratic fields (analogous to the theory of complex multiplication for imaginary quadratic fields).

by sending

The space

\{\mid z-1\mid_{p}<1\}

For any

there exists a (unique) character

extending the universal character
\kappa_{m}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{B}_{m})^{\times}

The invertible sheaf

The fibers of the rigid analytification

For

In

on

specializing, for any

on

.
Namely, he constructed an open
\mathcal{J}_{m}
of
\mathcal{I}^{\an}_{\mid X(v_{m})}
endowed with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
-action such that

\omega_{m}=\left(\pi^{\an}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{J}_{m}}\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}_{m}}\right)[\kappa_{m}],

where
[\kappa_{m}]
means the
\kappa_{m}
-equivariant sections for the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.
More precisely, by

\mathcal{J}_{m}
is locally isomorphic for the étale topology on

to the union of disks

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cdot(1+p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})=\underset{y\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}{\bigcup}\mathrm{B}(\tilde{y},p^{-m})\subset\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times},

where

\mathrm{B}(\tilde{y},p^{-m})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}_{p},\mid z-\tilde{y}\mid_{p}\leqslant p^{-m}\}

By definition

X^{\ord}
to the eigenspace of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{B}_{m})
-valued locally analytic functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cdot(1+p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
which are
\kappa_{m}
-equivariant for the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

\tilde{\kappa}_{m}

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open affinoid of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
which is contained in some
\mathcal{B}_{m}

For

\omega_{\mathcal{U}}

X(v)\times\mathcal{U}
.
The correspondence
U_{p}
is defined on the locus

where the canonical subgroup of

and the resulting endomorphism of the

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-Banach module

As

The weight space

can be admissibly covered by countably many open affinoids

1.2.
q
-expansions of Coleman families

In this subsection we describe how one can attach to a Coleman family a geometric
q
-expansion which interpolates the
q
-expansions of its classical specializations and satisfies the
q
-expansion Principle.

The generalized elliptic curve
\mathrm{Tate}(q)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem q\rsem
, with
1
-gon special fiber and natural
\Gamma_{1}(N)
-level structure defines a morphism
\Spec(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem q\rsem)\to\mathscr{X}
(the section
q=0
corresponds to
\infty
). Let
d^{\times}t
be the canonical differential of
\mathrm{Tate}(q)
,
i.e.
, a canonical
\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X},\infty}^{\wedge}
-basis of the completed stalk
{\omega}_{\infty}^{\wedge}
of
\omega
along the section
\infty:\Spec(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathscr{X}
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, one can identify
(\omega_{\infty}^{\wedge})^{\otimes k}
with

\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X},\infty}^{\wedge}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem q\rsem
using the canonical basis

, allowing one to geometrically define the
q
-expansion of an overconvergent modular form.

Proposition 1.2
.

There exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
for the étale topology of the cusp
\infty\in X^{\ord}
and a generator of
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{U})
specializing at any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\cap\mathcal{U}
to the canonical differential
(d^{\times}t)^{\otimes k}
. In particular, one can attach to any Coleman family
\mathscr{F}\in M^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
a
q
-expansion
\sum_{n\geqslant 0}a_{n}(\mathscr{F})q^{n}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\lsem q\rsem
interpolating the
q
-expansions of its classical specializations. Moreover, the
q
-expansion Principle holds, i.e., the
q
-expansion map is injective.

Proof.

As already observed,
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}
is locally isomorphic for the étale topology on
X^{\ord}\ni\infty
to the eigenspace of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-valued locally analytic functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cdot(1+p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})
which are
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
-equivariant with respect to the action of the
universal character
\kappa_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})^{\times}
, a basis being given by the locally analytic character

\tilde{\kappa}_{\mathcal{U}}
. Here
m
is chosen so that
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{B}_{m}
.

Since
\mathrm{Tate}(q)
is ordinary at
p
, one can choose a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
for the étale topology of the cusp
\infty
with a local trivialization of
\omega
given by

and such that the section
\tilde{\kappa}_{\mathcal{U}}
generates
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{U})
. The specialization of
\tilde{\kappa}_{\mathcal{U}}
at any

corresponds under this construction to the canonical differential

generating
\omega^{\otimes k}(\mathcal{V})
, thus providing the sought-for
p
-adic analytic interpolation. This yields the desired trivialization
\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{U})\simeq\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{V})\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
together with the natural injection
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{V})\widehat{\otimes}\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\lsem q\rsem
given by the localization

at

a

interpolating the

-expansions of its classical specializations, and thus satisfying a

-expansion Principle.
∎

1.3.
Basic global properties of
\mathscr{C}

The eigencurve
\mathscr{C}
of tame level
N
is admissibly covered by the affinoids attached to the
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-algebras
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
generated by the Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
,
\langle\ell\rangle
,
\ell\nmid Np
and
U_{p}

acting on
M^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
, where

is arbitrary and the open affinoids
\mathcal{U}
form an admissible cover of
\mathcal{W}_{p}
as in §
1.1
.

Henceforth we will use the weight space
\mathcal{W}
representing the continuous homomorphisms:

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m},

which is endowed with
shifted
forgetful map to
\mathcal{W}_{p}
and is locally generated over the latter by the diamond operators
\langle a\rangle
,

a\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. The shift is made so that

henceforth corresponds to the character
x\mapsto x^{k-1}
of

\mathcal{W}
, and on the level of Iwasawa algebras is given by the automorphism of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\lsem 1+2p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rsem
sending
[1+2p]
to
(1+2p)[1+2p]
.

The eigencurve
\mathscr{C}
is reduced, and it follows from its construction that there exists a flat and locally finite morphism
\sw:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
, called the weight map. Moreover
\sw
is proper by
[
24
]
.
Thanks to the above shift, the classical weight
1
forms, which are the focal point of our study,
map under
\sw
to finite order characters equal to the determinant of the corresponding Galois representation (pre-composed with the Artin reciprocity map).

By construction of
\mathscr{C}
, there exist bounded global sections
\{T_{\ell},U_{p}\}_{\ell\nmid Np}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{C}}^{+}(\mathscr{C})
such that the usual application “system of eigenvalues”

x\in\mathscr{C}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\mapsto\{T_{\ell}(x),U_{p}(x)\}_{\ell\nmid Np}

is injective, and produces all systems of eigenvalues for
\{T_{\ell},U_{p}\}_{\ell\nmid Np}
acting on the space of overconvergent forms with coefficients in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, of tame level
N
, having weight in
\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
and a non-zero
U_{p}
-eigenvalue.

A fundamental arithmetic tool in the study of the geometry of
\mathscr{C}
is the universal
2
-dimensional pseudo-character

(2)

\tau_{\mathscr{C}}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{C}}(\mathscr{C}),

which is unramified at all
\ell\nmid Np
and
such that
\tau_{\mathscr{C}}
maps an arithmetic Frobenius
\Frob_{\ell}
to
T_{\ell}
.
This pseudo-character interpolates
p
-adically the traces of semi-simple
p
-adic Galois representations attached to the classical points of
\mathscr{C}
. While these Galois representations are De Rham at
p
, the semi-simple
p
-adic Galois representation attached to an arbitrary specialization of
\tau_{\mathscr{C}}
is only trianguline at
p
.

1.4.
Classical points of
\mathscr{C}

A point of
\mathcal{W}
is said to be classical if its restriction to some open subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

is given by the homomorphism
(x\mapsto x^{k-1})
,
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
(such characters are locally algebraic).
The subset
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
of classical weights
is very Zariski dense in the sense that for any affinoid
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{W}
, the intersection

\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}\cap\mathcal{U}
is either empty or is Zariski dense in
\mathcal{U}
. A classical point of
\mathscr{C}
always maps to a point of
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, but the converse in not necessarily true.

In order to describe the classical points of
\mathscr{C}
let us first recall the notion of a
p
-stabilization.
Let
f(z)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}a_{n}e^{2i\pi nz}
be a primitive normalized eigenform
of weight
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
, central character

\varepsilon
, and level
\Gamma_{1}(Mp^{t})
, with
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
M
dividing
N
. We distinguish the following two cases.

(i)

If
t=0
, then the Hecke polynomial
X^{2}-a_{p}X+\varepsilon(p)p^{k-1}
of
f
at
p
has two (necessarily non-zero, but not necessarily distinct) roots, denoted

\alpha
and
\beta
. The corresponding
p
-stabilizations
f_{\alpha}(z)=f(z)-\beta f(pz)
and

f_{\beta}=f(z)-\alpha f(pz)
both have level
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
and define points
f_{\alpha}
and
f_{\beta}
in
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. If those points are distinct, we call them
p
-regular, if not,
p
-irregular. By an abuse of language we sometime say that
f
itself is regular or irregular at
p
.

(ii)

If
t>0
, then
f
is already a
U_{p}
-eigenvector with eigenvalue
\alpha=a_{p}
. If
f
has finite slope (
i.e.
,
\alpha\neq 0
), then it defines a point
f_{\alpha}=f\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. As the Hecke polynomial equals
X(X-\alpha)
, the point

f
is regular at
p
, .

The set of classical points
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\mathscr{C}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
consists of all
points
f_{\alpha}
as above (considered with coefficients in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
via
\iota_{p}
) as
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
M
dividing
N
vary. By Coleman’s Control Theorem
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is very Zariski dense in
\mathscr{C}
.

The
p
-adic valuation of the
U_{p}
-eigenvalue of a point in
\mathscr{C}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is called its

. The slope of a classical weight
k

and is called

when the equality is reached.
The points having slope

are called

. The locus
\mathscr{C}^{\ord}
of
\mathscr{C}
where
\mid U_{p}\mid_{p}=1
is open and closed in
\mathscr{C}
, and is called the ordinary locus. A formal model of
\mathscr{C}^{\ord}
is given by the universal
p
-ordinary reduced Hida Hecke algebra of tame level
N
generated by the Hecke operators
T_{\ell}
for all primes
\ell\nmid Np
and
U_{p}
.

1.5.
Hecke operators at primes dividing the level

For each
\ell\mid N
the module
M^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
is endowed with a
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-linear operator
U_{\ell}
, commuting with
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
.
It can be either defined geometrically as a correspondence between modular curves of levels prime to
p
, or by the usual formulas on
q
-expansions,
i.e.
, using
p
-adic interpolation of classical forms. By adding those operators to

one can define the full eigencurve

, whose ordinary part is directly related to the Hida Hecke algebras in their most classical definition. The advantage of working with the full Hecke algebra would become transparent in §
1.6
. The disadvantage is that this bigger algebra is not necessarily reduced. Luckily one does not have to choose when working in a neighborhood of a classical point corresponding to newform, as the next proposition shows that the two are locally isomorphic

Proposition 1.4
.

Any
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}
corresponding to a newform of tame level
N
has an affinoid neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
such that
any
g\in\mathcal{V}\cap\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{cl}}
corresponds to a newform of tame level
N
. Moreover,
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{full}}

As
\rho_{f}
is irreducible, by standard arguments (see for example
[
5

\mathcal{V}
of

in

and a continuous representation

whose trace equals the pseudo-character
\tau_{\mathcal{V}}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{V})
obtained from (
2
).

Fix a prime
\ell
dividing
N
and recall that

is new at
\ell

and
\rho_{g}
to the inertia subgroup

at

\mathcal{V}
containing

\mathcal{V}
is irreducible.
Let
(r_{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{V}})
be the Weil-Deligne representation attached to

in
[
4

, and similarly let

denote the Weil-Deligne representation attached to
\rho_{g\mid\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}}

By
[
4

If

\rho_{f}(\mathrm{I}_{\ell})

If

It follows that any classical

One deduces then from

either

\bullet

In particular, all points of

By Coleman’s Control Theorem and the Strong Multiplicity One Theorem for

which are not cuspidal, but still are cuspidal-overconvergent,

belong to the cuspidal eigencurve
\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}
defined in
§

. If

has weight

. If

constructed by

A case which is particularly piquant is that of the unique weight

\Gamma_{0}(\ell)

1.6.
Cuspidal Hida duality

The
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-submodule of
M^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
of cuspidal Coleman families is defined as

S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}=\varinjlim_{v>0}\rH^{0}(X(v)\times\mathcal{U},\omega_{\mathcal{U}}(-D))^{\leqslant s},

where
D
is the cuspidal divisor of the ordinary locus
X^{\ord}
.
Note that it is
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
-stable and one defines
the cuspidal Hecke
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-algebra
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
as the quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
acting faithfully on it.
Using
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
instead of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
one defines the cuspidal eigencurve

\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}
which is endowed with a closed immersion
\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
of reduced flat rigid curves over
\mathcal{W}
.

Let
f
be a classical cuspidal point in
\mathscr{C}
. As recalled in §
1.1
,
\sw(f)
has a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
such that

S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
are both free of finite rank as
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-modules.

Proposition 1.6
.

Hida’s
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-linear pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}\times S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})

sending
(T,\mathscr{G})\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}\times S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
to
\langle T,\mathscr{G}\rangle=a_{1}(T(\mathscr{G}))
is a perfect duality.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.4
and the abstract recurrence relations between Hecke operators,
one know that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
one has
T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
.
As

\langle T,T_{n}(\mathscr{G})\rangle=\langle T_{n}T,\mathscr{G}\rangle=a_{1}(T_{n}T(\mathscr{G}))=a_{n}(T(\mathscr{G}))

for any
n\geqslant 1
, the
q
-expansion Principle from Proposition
1.2
shows that the following natural
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-linear maps are injective

(3)

\displaystyle S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\text{-mod}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s},\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U}))\text{ , }

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}\mapsto(T\mapsto\langle T,\mathscr{G}\rangle)

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})\text{-mod}}(S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U}))\text{ , }

\displaystyle T\mapsto(\mathscr{G}\mapsto\langle T,\mathscr{G}\rangle).

In particular, if
\mathcal{U}=\{\sw(f)\}
then the above maps are isomorphisms, as
S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\sw(f)}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\sw(f)}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
are vector spaces of finite dimension.
For general
\mathcal{U}
, as
S^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}_{\mathcal{U}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\cusp,\leqslant s}
are free
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
-modules of finite rank,
we proceed by localization at
\sw(f)
. By Nakayama’s lemma, it suffices to show that the cokernel of (
3
) vanishes residually, which follows from the surjectivity in the particular case
\mathcal{U}=\{\sw(f)\}
that we already considered.
∎

Remark 1.7

Similar techniques were used in

to establish a perfect duality between
M_{\mathcal{U}}^{{\dagger},\leqslant s}
and

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant s}
locally at a point corresponding to a weight

2.
Generalized eigenforms at classical points of the eigencurve

2.1.
Geometry of
\mathscr{C}
at classical points of weight at least
2

Recast in rigid geometry, Hida’s famous Control Theorem
[
37
]
states that any point of
\mathscr{C}^{\ord}
having classical weight
k\geqslant 2
is classical.
Note that ordinary forms of weight with
k\geqslant 2
are always regular at
p
and, when a second
p
-stabilization exists, it necessarily has critical slope.
Coleman’s Control Theorem generalizing Hida’s result, states that any non-critical point of
\mathscr{C}
having weight in
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is classical
(see
[
17
]
). Those results imply that
\mathscr{C}
is étale over
\mathcal{W}
at any non-critical,
p
-regular classical point of weight
k\geqslant 2
, hence it is also smooth at these points. There are no known examples of classical forms of weight
k\geqslant 2
which are
p
-irregular,
i.e.
, for which the Hecke polynomial at
p
has a double root.
There are only three cases in which
\sw
could fail to be étale at a classical point
f
of weight
k\geqslant 2
and regular at
p
,
potentially providing a cuspidal-overconvergent generalized eigenform.

(i)

Critical (a.k.a. evil) Eisenstein points.
Bellaïche and Chenevier proved in
[
3
]
that
\mathscr{C}
is smooth
and is conjecturally étale over
\mathcal{W}
at such points.

(ii)

Critical CM points.
Bellaïche showed in
[
1
]
that the eigencurve is smooth, although ramified over the weight space, at such points. He further showed that Jannsen’s conjecture in Galois cohomology implies that the ramification degree is precisely
2
.

(iii)

Critical non-CM points.
Breuil and Emerton proved in
[
11
]
(see also
[
28
]
for a partial
result and
[
6
]
for a different proof) that
\sw
ramifies at a classical weight
k\geqslant 2
point of critical slope
if, and only if, there exists an ordinary companion form, in which case the restriction to
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of the attached
p
-adic Galois representation splits. It is a folklore conjecture, attributed to R. Coleman and to R. Greenberg, that such non-CM points should not exist (see for example
[
14
]
).

Note that if a
p
-irregular classical weight
k\geqslant 2
points were to exist, they would be of non-critical slope
and Coleman’s Classicality Theorem
[
17
]
would imply that the corresponding generalized eigenspace consists only of classical
forms. Thus the second case above is (conjecturally) the only one yielding cuspidal-overconvergent generalized eigenforms, and their
q
-expansions have recently been computed by Hsu
[
38
]
(note that the technical condition preceding Theorem 1.1 in
loc. cit.
appears to be superfluous).

2.2.
Geometry of
\mathscr{C}
at classical points of weight
1
and Hida theory

Classical weight
1
points in
\mathscr{C}
all belong to
\mathscr{C}^{\ord}
and all have critical slope, hence Hida’s Control Theorem cannot be applied them and a specializations of a Hida family in weight
1
need not a classical eigenform.
According to a result of E. Ghate and V. Vatsal
[
30
]
, only Hida families with complex multiplication (CM)
admit infinitely many classical weight
1
specializations. An explicit bound for the number of classical weight
1
specializations of a non-CM family can be found in
[
26
]
.

Deligne and Serre attached in
[
23
, Proposition 4.1]
to any weight
1
newform
f
an irreducible Galois representation
\rho_{f}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\iota_{p}}\GL_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
having
finite image. As well-known (see for example
[
26
]
), the projective image is either dihedral, or else is isomorphic to one of the groups
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
or
A_{5}
in which case the form (or the corresponding point on
\mathscr{C}
) is referred to as exotic.
When the projective image is dihedral, then
f
has real multiplication (RM) or
complex multiplication (CM), depending on whether the corresponding quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is real or imaginary.
Note that, when the projective image is the Klein four group, then
f
has multiplication by three quadratic fields (two imaginary and one real).

One motivation for studying the geometry of the eigencurve at weight
1
points arises from the question of determining whether there is a unique, up to Galois conjugacy, Hida family specializing to a given weight
1
eigenform
f
. By
Proposition
1.4
the
minimal primes in Hida’s
p
-ordinary Hecke algebra which are contained in the height
1
prime attached to
f
are in bijection with the irreducible components of
\mathscr{C}
containing
f
. It follows that if
\mathscr{C}
is smooth at
f
, then there exists a unique, up to Galois conjugacy, Hida family specializing to
f
.
It is observed in
[
26
]
that any classical weight
1
form of Klein type and irregular at
p
is contained in two Hida families having CM by different imaginary quadratic fields, for which reason they cannot be Galois conjugates.
It turns out that this is part of a more general phenomenon. Indeed, it is proven in

[
8
]
that any weight
1
form irregular at
p
and having CM by
K
belongs to exactly

3
or
4
irreducible components of
\mathscr{C}
, exactly
2
out of which have CM by
K
.
In particular the uniqueness, up to Galois conjugacy, of the Hida family systematically fails at such weight
1
classical points.

While conjecturally all classical points of weight
k\geqslant 2
are expected to be
p
-regular (see
[
18
]
), the Chebotarev Density theorem applied to

\rho_{f}
, shows that for each classical weight
1
point
f
there are infinitely many irregular primes
p
, providing many classical points at which
\sw
is not étale (and not even smooth if
f
has CM).

The main result of
[
5
]
asserts that
\mathscr{C}
is smooth at any classical weight
1
point
f
which is regular at
p
.
Furthermore, the weight map
\sw
is not étale at
f
if, and only if,
f
has real multiplication (RM) by a quadratic field in which
p
splits. The next subsection is devoted to this case.

2.3.
Regular RM case

Let
f
be a
p
-stabilization of a
p
-regular weight
1
newform of level
N
having multiplication by a real quadratic field in which

splits,
and let

denote the corresponding maximal ideal of the Hecke algebra.
In this case the classical subspace of the generalized eigenspace

As

over
\mathcal{W}
at

(
i.e

at

[
5
]
show that

is a line, having a basis

[
5
]
,
H. Darmon, A. Lauder and V. Rotger determine in
[

]
the precise
q
-expansion of this genuine overconvergent generalized eigenform

We use the present opportunity to observe that, contrarily to what was claimed in
[
5
]
, based on a
misinterpretation of a result by Cho and Vatsal
[
15
]
, the expected equality

remains an open question. It is equivalent to showing that the ramification index of

at

equals
2
(see
[
7

for a thorough study of this case).

The geometry of the eigencurve is expected to be more intricate at a weight

points irregular at

and to have
fascinating applications in Iwasawa theory.

2.4.
Eisenstein case

We refer to
[
9
]
for a detailed study of this case.
A
p
-regular weight
1
Eisenstein point belongs to a unique irreducible component of
\mathscr{C}
which is
Eisenstein and étale over the weight space. A
p
-irregular weight
1
Eisenstein point
f
is
attached to an odd primitive Dirichlet character
\phi:(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
such that
\phi(p)=1
.
In contrast with the
p
-regular case,
f
belongs to exactly two Eisenstein components. Moreover
f
is cuspidal-overconvergent,
i.e.

vanishes at all cusps of the multiplicative ordinary locus of the modular curve
X(\Gamma_{0}(p)\cap\Gamma_{1}(N))
corresponding to the
\Gamma_{0}(p)
-orbit of
\infty
, hence
f
belongs to the cuspidal eigencurve
\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}
. It is
shown in
loc.cit.
that
\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}
is étale at
f
over the weight space, hence there exists a unique, up to a Galois conjugacy, cuspidal Hida family
\mathscr{F}
specializing to
f
.
It is also shown that
\mathscr{C}^{\cusp}
intersects transversally each of the two Eisenstein components containing
f

(note that the evil weight Eisenstein series of weight
\geqslant 2
do not belong to Eisenstein components).
Furthermore, one computes in
loc.cit.
the
q
-expansions of a basis
\{f^{\dagger}_{1,\phi},f^{\dagger}_{\phi,1}\}

of the space of genuine overconvergent generalized eigenforms in terms of
p
-adic logarithms of algebraic numbers,
and one remarks that these forms are
not
cuspidal-overconvergent. Finally, the expression of the constant term

a_{0}(f^{\dagger}_{1,\phi})=(\mathscr{L}(\phi)+\mathscr{L}(\phi^{-1}))\frac{L(\phi,0)}{2}
, where
\mathscr{L}(\phi)
denotes the cyclotomic
\mathscr{L}
-invariant
appearing in the derivative at a trivial zero of the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic

-function

, allows to give a geometric flavored proof of Gross’ formula
L_{p}^{\prime}(\phi\omega_{p},0)=\mathscr{L}(\phi)\cdot L(\phi,0)
(see
[
9
, §5]
).

2.5.
The

-irregular weight one case

Let us first say that this case is still the subject of active research.
A weight one newform which is irregular at
p
yields a unique point
f
on
\mathscr{C}

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the completed local ring of
\mathscr{C}
at

and

is an admissible affinoid of

\mathcal{T}\times S^{{\dagger}}_{\mathfrak{m}}\to\Lambda,\text{ given by }\langle h,\mathscr{G}\rangle=a_{1}(T\cdot\mathscr{G})\in\Lambda,

Let us observe that the classical subspace

2.6.
The

-irregular CM case

We will use without recalling the notations and assumptions introduced in the paragraph preceding Theorem

Since
\psi\neq\bar{\psi}

\rho_{f\mid\mathrm{G}_{K}}=\psi\oplus\bar{\psi}

Using the complex conjugation

\rho_{f\mid\mathrm{G}_{K}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\psi&0\\

\psi(\tau\cdot)&0\end{matrix}\right).

The local-global compatibility for

(9)

a_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\psi(\mathfrak{l})+\psi(\bar{\mathfrak{l}})&,\text{ if }(\ell)=\mathfrak{l}\bar{\mathfrak{l}}\neq(p)\text{ splits in }K,\\

which together with the usual recurrence relations

.
The above formulas are understood with the convention that
\psi(\mathfrak{l})=0

\mathfrak{l}
divides the conductor of
\psi
, and similarly

, if

divides the conductor of
\rho_{f}

It has been shown in
[
8
]
that in addition to belonging to two components of
\mathscr{C}

also belongs to one or two other components,

,

In our situation, we are in the presence of a so-called “trivial” zero and, prior to

-adic

.
By using

A corollary of the main results of

Infinitesimal non-CM Hida families

Proposition 3.1

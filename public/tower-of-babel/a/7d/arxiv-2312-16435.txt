Probing Ultralight Tensor Dark Matter with the Stochastic Gravitational-Wave Background from Advanced LIGO and Virgo’s First Three Observing Runs
Rong-Zhen Guo, Yang Jiang, Qing-Guo Huang

Abstract
Ultralight bosons are attractive dark-matter candidates and appear in various scenarios beyond standard model. They can induce superradiant instabilities around spinning black holes (BHs), extracting the energy and angular momentum from BHs, and then dissipated through monochromatic gravitational radiation, which become promising sources of gravitational wave detectors. In this letter, we focus on massive tensor fields coupled to BHs and compute the stochastic gravitational wave backgrounds emitted by these sources. We then undertake a search for this background within the data from LIGO/Virgo O1 ∼ \sim O3 runs. Our analysis reveals no discernible evidence of such signals, allowing us to impose stringent limits on the mass range of tensor bosons. Specifically, we exclude the existence of tensor bosons with masses ranging from 4.0 × 10 − 14 4.0\times 10^{-14} to 2.0 × 10 − 12 2.0\times 10^{-12} eV at 95 % 95\% confidence level.

Superradiant instability of ultralight tensor dark matter.

The dynamics of tensor dark matter can be described by spin-2 field
H_{\alpha\beta}
on BH background with initial mass
M_{i}
and initial dimensionless angular momentum
\chi_{i}=J_{i}/M_{i}^{2}
. This field can be regarded as linear massive tensor perturbation in the ghost-free bi-metric gravity theory
Brito et al. 2020
;
Babichev et al. 2016b
, which equation of motion on Ricci-flat background reads:

\displaystyle\square H_{\alpha\beta}+2R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta}H^{\gamma\delta}-m_{b}^{2}H_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}H_{\alpha\beta}=0,\quad H^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0,

where the box operator, Riemann tensor
R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta}
, contractions are constructed with the background metric, and
m_{b}
is the mass of the spin-2 field.

For ultralight (
m_{b}M\ll 1
) tensor dark matter, there are several different mechanisms for triggering instability. There exists unstable monopolar (
m=0
) modes, which are also existed in the spherical BH case. Such instabilities appear first, affecting the spacetime geometry of BHs and eventually forming hairy BHs
East and Siemonsen 2023
;
Brito et al. 2013
;
Babichev and Fabbri 2013
. This instability has an extremely short characteristic timescale (
\tau_{\textrm{mono}}\sim m_{b}^{-1}
)
Brito et al. 2013
, and due to its monopolarity, no GW produces during this process. Therefore, the impact of this process can be ignored. And subsequent superradiant instability leads to the formation of the quasi-bound state, which characteristic frequency is complex
Brito et al. 2013
;
Dias et al. 2023
:

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{R}}{m_{b}}

\displaystyle\simeq 1-\frac{(m_{b}M)^{2}}{2(\ell+n+S+1)^{2}},

(2)

\displaystyle\omega_{I}

\displaystyle\propto(m_{b}M)^{4\ell+5+2S}\left(\omega_{R}-m\Omega_{\mathrm{H}}\right),

where
\ell\geq 0,n\geq 0
, and the integer
S=(0,\pm 1,\pm 2)
are the mode total angular momentum, overtone number, and polarization with
m\in[-\ell,\ell]
. We use the result on Kerr background, because these modes are significant support at
r\sim M(m_{b}M)^{-2}

Brito et al. 2013
, the deformation compared to the Kerr BH for the hairy BH is therefore negligible under ultralight limit. It is clearly that for
m\geq 1
cases, if
\omega_{R}-m\Omega_{H}<0
, these states would grow exponentially until reaching the saturation point,
\omega_{R}\sim m\Omega_{H}
in timescale
\tau_{\text{inst }}\equiv 1/\omega_{I}
. In this letter, we concentrate on two dominant unstable appearing in non-relativistic approximation, a dipole mode (
l=m=1,S=0
) and a quadrapole mode (
l=m=2,S=-2
). Although they share the comparable timescale, it has been shown
Ficarra et al. 2019
that the dipole mode can be reabsorbed by the BH, giving back almost all its mass and spin. The loss of mass and angular momentum is thus almost entirely attributable to the quadrapole mode itself.

According to the conservation of mass and angular momentum, we can get the relationship between initial BH mass
M_{i}
, spin
J_{i}
and the mass
M_{f}
and spin
J_{f}
when superradiant instability saturated at
\omega_{R}=m\Omega_{H}
:

J_{f}=J_{i}-\frac{m}{\omega_{R}}\left(M_{i}-M_{f}\right)=J_{i}-\frac{m}{\omega_{R}}M_{T}^{\mathrm{max}},

(3)

M_{f}=\frac{m^{3}-\sqrt{m^{6}-16m^{2}\omega_{R}^{2}\left(mM_{i}-\omega_{R}J_{i}\right)^{2}}}{8\omega_{R}^{2}\left(mM_{i}-\omega_{R}J_{i}\right)},

(4)

where
M_{T}^{\mathrm{max}}
is the maximal total mass of tensor dark matter. This quasi-bound state dissipates its energy by GWs in a typical timescale
\tau_{\mathrm{GW}}
:

\tau_{\mathrm{GW}}=M_{f}\left(\frac{d\tilde{E}}{dt}\frac{M_{T}^{\max}}{M_{f}}\right)^{-1},

(5)

where
d\tilde{E}/dt
is the reduced GW flux. In the qradrapole case, it reads
Brito et al. 2020

\tau_{\mathrm{GW}}\sim 290\left(\frac{M_{f}}{30M_{\odot}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3M_{\odot}}{M_{T}^{\mathrm{max}}}\right)\left(\frac{m_{b}M_{f}}{0.2}\right)^{-10}\ \textrm{sec}.

(6)

Since the hierarchy of timescales
\tau_{\mathrm{GW}}\gg\tau_{\text{inst }}\gg M
, we can get the total GW energy of the whole system emitted in a duration time
\Delta t=t_{0}-t_{f}
by quasi-adiabatic approximation
Brito et al. 2017b

E_{\text{GW}}=\frac{M_{T}^{\mathrm{max}}\Delta t}{\Delta t+\tau_{\mathrm{GW}}},

(7)

where
t_{0}
is the age of the Universe and
t_{f}
characterizes the time when the hairy
\mathrm{BH}
is formed.

SGWB from boson clouds.

In this section, we aim to model the SGWB from the superradiant instability. As described in Eq. (
8
)
Allen and Romano 1999

\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)=\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\mathrm{d}\ln f},

(8)

it is characterized by the energy density per logarithm frequency and
\rho_{c}
is the critical energy density required for a spatially flat Universe. To figure out this background, we need to sum the GWs radiated by all the possible sources. In our work, it is the superposition of all the individual BH-boson system triggering superradiance. The general formula is

\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)=\frac{f}{\rho_{c}}\int\mathrm{d}z\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}z}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{\theta}\;\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta};z)\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{s}}{\mathrm{d}f_{s}}(\bm{\theta};m_{b},f),

(9)

where
\mathrm{d}t/\mathrm{d}z
is the derivative of lookback time over redshift. It is determined by the
\Lambda\text{CDM}
cosmology model
Condon and Matthews 2018
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}z}=\frac{1}{(1+z)H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{M}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\Lambda}}}.

(10)

\mathcal{R}(\bm{\theta};z)
is the formation rate of BH with parameter
\bm{\theta}
per comoving volume at redshift
z
.
\mathrm{d}E_{s}/\mathrm{d}f_{s}
is the energy spectrum of a single event in the source frame. Since the GW radiation is nearly monochromatic, the spectrum is well approximated by

\frac{\mathrm{d}E_{s}}{\mathrm{d}f_{s}}=E_{\text{GW}}\delta(f_{s}-f_{0})=E_{\text{GW}}\delta[f(1+z)-f_{0}],

(11)

where
f_{0}=\omega_{R}/\pi
and we replace the source-frame frequency
f_{s}
by detected frequency due to the redshift of the sources.

Two kinds of BH populations are considered in our work, which are isolated stellar origin BHs (SBHs) and binary SBH merging remnants. For isolated BH channel, the formation rate is

\mathcal{R}^{\text{iso}}(\chi,M;z)=p(\chi)R^{\text{iso}}(M;z).

(12)

By writing down Eq. (
12

p(\chi)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\chi_{\mathrm{u}}-\chi_{\mathrm{l}}},&\chi_{\mathrm{l}}\leq\chi\leq\chi_{\mathrm{u}}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(13)

The remaining part can be determined by astrophysical model

R^{\text{iso}}(M;z)=\psi(z_{f})\frac{\xi(M_{*})}{M_{*}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{*}}{\mathrm{d}M},

(14)

where
M_{*}

Data analysis and results.

SGWB will cause a correlation in the output of detector pairs. An estimator can be constructed with Fourier transform of the strain data as follows
Romano and Cornish 2017

\hat{C}_{IJ}(f)=\frac{2}{T}\frac{\text{Re}[\tilde{s}_{I}^{*}(f)\tilde{s}_{J}(f)]}{\gamma_{IJ}(f)S_{0}(f)},

(20)

where
S_{0}(f)=(3H_{0}^{2})/(10\pi^{2}f^{3})
.
\gamma_{IJ}(f)
is the overlap reduction function
Allen and Romano 1999
of detector pair
IJ
and
T
denotes observing time. Eq. (
20
) has been normalized to
\langle\hat{C}_{I}J(f)\rangle=\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)
and the variance is

\sigma_{IJ}^{2}(f)=\frac{1}{2T\Delta f}\frac{P_{I}(f)P_{J}(f)}{\gamma_{IJ}^{2}(f)S_{0}^{2}(f)}.

(21)

P_{I,J}(f)
appeared in Eq. (
21
) are the power spectral densities of the interferometers. To evaluate the mass of spin-2 boson, we perform Bayesian analysis. After glitch gating and non-stationary rejection, the noise of detectors is well approximated by uncorrelated Gaussian signal. Therefore, the total likelihood is

p(\bm{\hat{C}}|\bm{\theta})\propto\exp\left[-\sum_{IJ}\sum_{f}\frac{\left(\hat{C}_{IJ}(f)-\Omega(f;\bm{\theta})\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{IJ}(f)}\right],

(22)

where
\bm{\theta}
denotes a sequence of parameters to be determined by analysis and we have multiplied the likelihood from all the frequency bins and detector pairs. The ratio of model evidence

\mathcal{B}_{\text{\,noise}}^{\text{\,model}}=\frac{p(\bm{\hat{C}}\,|\,\text{Model of signal})}{p(\bm{\hat{C}}\,|\,\text{Pure noise})},

(23)

so-called Bayes factor illustrates the statistical significance of the presence of the signal.

Discussions and conclusions.

In this work, we search for the SGWB signal produced by ultralight tensor dark matter around both isolated SBHs and binary SBHs remnants in LIGO/Virgo O1
\sim
O3 runs. We find no evidence to suggest the presence of the signal then we place constraints on the mass of tensor bosons. Assuming a uniform distribution of spin between
[0,\,1],\;[0,\,0.5]
and
[0.5,\,1]
for isolated SBHs, we exclude the mass range
10^{-13.4}\sim 10^{-11.7}\text{eV}
. The relationship between the excluded mass range and the spin distribution is not very tightly connected.

It is noteworthy that we notice recent advances in the purely numerical spectral analysis of ultralight tensor dark matter
East and Siemonsen 2023
;
Dias et al. 2023
. These studies contain several new modes beyond the hydrogenic approximation we used in the work. Since these modes stem from nonseparability of the field equations, their dynamical impact becomes very difficult to compute. This aspect remains a subject for future investigations and warrants careful consideration in subsequent research endeavors.

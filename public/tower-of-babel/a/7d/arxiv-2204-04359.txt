Abstract
. Permutation-partition pairs were introduced by Stahl in 1980. These pairs are generalizations of graphs and graphs on surfaces. They were used to solve some problems for orientable embeddings of graphs. In this paper, we introduce a particular type of permutation-partition pair, called permutation-bipartition pair, which can be seen as generalizations of signed graphs and signed graph embeddings. Some applications are given.

Basic Terminology and Background

A
graph

G=(V(G),E(G))
is permitted to have loops and multiple edges. We use
S
to denote a

without regard to orientability. An

(or a map

into a closed surface

. A

\Sigma=(G,\sigma)
is a graph

together with a mapping

which assigns

or

to each edge of the graph

If

we call the edge

, otherwise

and the edge is

. In this paper, the negative edges and positive edges in
\Sigma
are represented by dashed lines and solid lines respectively.

A

of a graph

is a cyclic ordering of the edge-ends incident at

. A
rotation system

\rho
of a graph
G
is an assignment of a rotation at every vertex of
G
. It is well-known that there is an one-to-one
correspondence between the rotation systems and orientable embeddings. A
2-cell embedding
of a signed graph
\Sigma
can be described combinatorially by a
signed rotation system

(\rho,\sigma),
where
\rho
is a rotation system of

and
\sigma
is the

\sigma(e)=1
, then the edge

is twisted; otherwise

is untwisted. It is obvious that if

, for all

, then the signed rotation system
(\rho,\sigma)
is equivalent to a rotation system. Recall that any embedding of

into a surface
S
can be described by a signed rotation system. A signed rotation system is also known as a general rotation system in topological graph theory.
A
switch
of a signed rotation system at a vertex

means reversing the rotation about
v
and changing the sign on each of its incident edges. Two signed rotation system are
equivalent
if one can be transformed into another by a sequence of switches. For terms and definitions that are not explained here, we refer to the monograph on topological graph theory by Gross and Tucker
[
9
]
.

Signed graphs were introduced by F. Harary in 1950s to model social relations involving disliking, indifference, and liking. There are many researches on signed graph embeddings, and we only list a few results here. T. Zaslavsky considered orientation embedding of signed graphs, gave a characterization for the projective-planar signed graph (characterized by six small forbidden minors or eight small forbidden topological

For more topics, we refer to the survey paper of Zaslavsky

A
permutation-partition pair

(P,\Pi)
consists of an arbitrary permutation
P
and an arbitrary partition
\Pi,
both defined over some common underlying set
S.
It can be seen as a combinatorial generalization of graphs and graph embeddings. The notion of a permutation-partition pair was introduced by Stahl in 1980
[
23
]
. It is a useful tool to tackle orientable embeddings of a graph, such as minimum genus, maximum genus, average genus, and genus distribution, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
, etc.

In this paper, we introduce the permutation-bipartition pair which is a generalization of signed graphs and signed graph embeddings. It can also be thought of as a generalization for permutation-partition pair to the non-orientable case. Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give a definition for permutation-bipartition pair, and introduce its embedding, region, and Euler-genus. We also prove a region (genus) Walkup reduction for embeddings of the permutation-bipartition pairs. It is then used to calculate region distributions (or Euler-genus polynomials) of linear signed graph families. The expected genus is discussed in Section 3.

Lemma 2.4

given by

is a bijection such that

\|PQ\|=\|P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}+\delta_{\theta aP_{a},\theta b},

where

is the Knonecker delta function.

Proof.

We first show that the function

is a bijection. Recall that for any

has
(b)
and
(\theta b)
as singletons, and hence

is injective. On the other hand, for any

maps

to
b
and
\theta b
to
\theta a,
and we have

f((ab)Q_{a,\theta a}(\theta a,\theta b))=Q_{a,\theta a}.

Thus

is surjective.

Recall that

\displaystyle P_{a}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}P(babP)/b&\hbox{if $a\neq bP\neq b$ ;}\\
P(ba)/b,&\hbox{if $a=bP\neq b$;}\\

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\theta a,\theta b,\theta bP_{a}^{-1})P_{a}/\theta b&\hbox{if $\theta a\neq\theta bP_{a}^{-1}\neq\theta b$ ;}\\

P_{a}/\theta b,&\hbox{if $\theta bP_{a}^{-1}=\theta b.$}\end{array}\right.

By Lemma 1.1 in
[
25
]
, we have

\|PQ\|=\|P_{a}Q_{a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}.

If
\theta a\neq\theta bP_{a}^{-1}\neq\theta b,
note that
b
(
\theta b
) is in the domain of neither
P_{a}
(
P_{a,\theta a}
) nor

(

\displaystyle\|PQ\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P_{a}Q_{a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\theta bP_{a}^{-1},\theta b,\theta a)P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}(\theta a,\theta b)\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\theta b,\theta aP_{a}^{-1})P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}.

Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\|PQ\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P_{a}Q_{a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\theta b,\theta a)P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}(\theta a,\theta b)\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle\|(\theta b)P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}

\displaystyle\|P_{a,\theta a}Q_{a,\theta a}\|+\delta_{a,bP}+1.

2.4.
The genus version of Walkup reduction

We call a permutation-bipartition pair
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)

non-orientable
, if there exits a permutation
Q_{i}
on
\Pi_{i},
and a permutation
Q_{j}
on
\theta\Pi_{j},
such that the action of
\langle P,Q_{i},Q_{j}\rangle
on
\Pi_{i}\cup\theta\Pi_{j}
is transitive for
1\leq i,j\leq k
. Otherwise, the pair
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)
is an
orientable permutation-bipartition pair
. The
Euler characteristic
of the embedding
(P,Q)
of the pair
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)
is

\chi(P,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|P\|+\|Q\|+\|PQ\|-2n\right),

where
2n
is the number of elements in
S\cup S_{\theta}.

We denote
c(P,Q)
as the number of
orbits
that the group generated by
P
and
Q.
It is easy to see that if
a
and
b
are distinct and
a\equiv b(mod\ \Pi_{i}),
then

c(P,Q)+1\geq c(P_{a},Q_{a})\geq c(P,Q)

Moreover, if
a
and
b
belong to distinct cycles of
P
or
a=bP,
then
c(P_{a},Q_{a})=c(P,Q).
Similarly, if
\theta a
and
\theta b
are distinct and
\theta a\equiv\theta b(mod\ \theta\Pi_{i}),
then

c(P_{a},Q_{a})+1\geq c(P_{a,\theta a},Q_{a,\theta a})\geq c(P_{a},Q_{a}).

Again, if
\theta a
and
\theta b
belong to distinct cycles of
P_{a}
or
\theta aP_{a}=\theta b,
then
c(P_{a},Q_{a})=c(P_{a,\theta a},Q_{a,\theta a}).

The
Euler-genus
of the embedding
(P,Q)
of non-orientable pair
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)
is given by

\gamma^{E}(P,Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2c(P,Q)-\chi(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi),&\hbox{if $(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)$ is non-orientable};\\
c(P,Q)-\chi(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi),&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For
k\geq 0
, let
\gamma^{E}(P,Q)(k)
be the number of embeddings of
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)
that have Euler-genus
k.
The
Euler-genus polynomial
of the permutation-bipartition pair
(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)
is

\mathcal{E}_{(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{(P,\Pi\cup\theta\Pi)}(k)z^{k}.

The next two corollaries are based on two lemmas in Subsection 2.2. We will refer to these two corollaries as the genus version of Walkup reduction in the sequel. The reason for this is that it is more convenient to represent genus polynomials of signed graphs, just like genus polynomials of graphs.

Corollary 2.9
.

The expected genus

Let
\Sigma

\Sigma
is given by

\gamma_{E}(G)=\frac{\sum_{i\geq 0}i\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}(i)}{\prod_{v\in V(\Sigma)}(d_{v}-1)!}.

In other words, we have
\gamma_{E}(\Sigma)=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}(1)^{{}^{\prime}}}{\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma}(1)}.
We shall frequently use this property between expected genus and genus polynomial in the following discussion.

In
[

]

[

calculated expected genus for the Ringel ladder graph. Recall that ladders and cobblestone paths are two classes of linear graph families. Using permutation-partition pairs and Peron-Frobenius theory of stochastic matrices, Stahl
[

gave the asymptotic result for expected genus of linear graph families. By using a different approach, we now slightly generalize Stahl’s result to signed graphs.

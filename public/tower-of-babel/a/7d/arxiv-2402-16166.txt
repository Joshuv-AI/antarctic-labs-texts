Projective dimension and regularity of 3-path ideals of unicyclic graphs
Nguyen Thu Hang, Thanh Vu

Abstract
. We compute the projective dimension and regularity of 3 3 -path ideals of arbitrary graphs having at most one cycle.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
05E40, 13D02, 13F55

1.
Introduction

Let
S=\mathrm{k}[v\mid v\in V]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over an arbitrary field
\mathrm{k}
on a set of indeterminates
V
. Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)=V
and edge set
E(G)\subset V(G)\times V(G)
. Conca and De Negri
[
CD
]
introduced the
t
-path ideal of
G
. Note that
I_{2}(G)
is the usual edge ideal of
G
. When
G
is a tree, they showed that the generators of
I_{t}(G)
form an
M
-sequence. Jacques
[
J
]
described the Betti numbers of the edge ideals of cycles and gave a recursive formula for the Betti numbers of edge ideals of forests. For the
t
-path ideals, Erey
[
E
]
gave a formula for the Betti numbers of path ideals of line and star graphs, Alilooee and Faridi
[
AF2
]
gave a formula for the Betti numbers of path ideals of line and cycle graphs. Bouchat, Ha, and O’Keefe
[
BHO
]
, He and Van Tuyl
[
HV
]
, Kiani and Madani
[
KM
]
, and Bouchat and Brown
[
BB
]
obtained some partial results for the Betti numbers of directed rooted trees. We note that the path ideals of a directed rooted tree are generally smaller than those of the underlying undirected trees associated with it. Banerjee
[
B
]
proved that when
G
is gap-free, one has
\reg(S/I_{3}(G))\leq\max\{\reg(S/I_{2}(G)),2\}
.

Note that if
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are disjoint, then
I_{t}(G)=I_{t}(G_{1})+I_{t}(G_{2})
is the sum of ideals in a different set of variables. By
[
NV2
]
, we can deduce homological invariants of (powers) of
I_{t}(G)
from those of
I_{t}(G_{1})
and
I_{t}(G_{2})
. Thus, we may assume that
G
is connected. Even when
G
is a tree, we do not have a complete answer for the projective dimension and regularity of
I_{t}(G)
.

Recently, Kumar and Sarkar
[
KS
]
showed that
\reg(S/I_{3}(G))=2\nu_{3}(G)
when
G
is a tree and when
G
has a unique cycle then

2\nu_{3}(G)\leq\reg\left(S/I_{3}(G)\right)\leq 2\nu_{3}(G)+2

where
\nu_{3}(G)
is the
3
-path induced matching number of
G
. Our motivation in this work is to give a formula for the regularity of
I_{3}(G)
when
G
has a unique cycle in terms of the
3
-path induced matching number of
G
.

We now describe our main results. All graphs in this work are undirected simple graphs. We assume that
G
has at most one cycle
C
of length
m
on the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
. When
G
is a tree, we set
m=1
. We first establish certain Betti splittings for the
3
-path ideals of
G
. We then deduce efficient recursive formulae for the projective dimension and regularity of
I_{3}(G)
, see Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
,
4.4
,
4.6
for more details. Our results are new even when
G
is a tree. From that, we deduce a formula for the regularity of
I_{3}(G)
in terms of its
3
-path induced matching number, completing the work of Kumar and Sarkar
[
KS
]
.

Let
\partial(C)=N(C)\setminus C
, the boundary of
C
in
G
, be the set of neighbors of vertices of
C
that are not in
C
. Let
\Gamma_{G}(C)
be the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus\partial(C)
. Motivated by a result of Alilooee, Beyarslan, and Selvaraja
[
ABS
, Corollary 3.9]
, we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
G
is a connected simple graph with at most one cycle
C
of length
m
. Then

\reg(S/I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}2\nu_{3}(G)+2,&\text{ if }m>3,m\equiv 3\pmod{4}\text{ and }\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C)),\\
2\nu_{3}(G)+1,&\text{ if }m>3,m\equiv 2\pmod{4}\text{ and }\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C)),\\
2\nu_{3}(G),&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We structure the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we set up the notation, provide some background, and prove some properties of
3
-induced matching numbers of graphs. In Section
3
, we establish certain Betti splittings involving
3
-path ideals of graphs having at most one cycle. In Section
4
, we compute the projective dimension and regularity of
3
-path ideals of graphs having at most one cycle. We then deduce Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this section, we let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over an arbitrary field
\mathrm{k}
.

2.1.
Projective dimension and regularity

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. The
(i,j)
-graded Betti number of
M
is defined by

\beta_{i,j}(M)=\dim_{\mathrm{k}}\Tor_{i}^{S}(\mathrm{k},M)_{j}.

The
i
th Betti number of
M
is
\beta_{i}(M)=\dim_{\mathrm{k}}\Tor_{i}^{S}(\mathrm{k},M)
. We define the projective dimension and regularity of
M
as follows.

\displaystyle\pd_{S}(M)

\displaystyle=\sup\{i\mid\beta_{i}(M)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\reg_{S}(M)

\displaystyle=\sup\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\}.

For simplicity of notation, we often write
\pd(M)
and
\reg(M)
instead of
\pd_{S}(M)
and
\reg_{S}(M)
. We have the following well known facts.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x
be a variable and
I
a nonzero homogeneous ideal of
S
. Then

(1)

\pd(I)=\pd(S/I)-1
,

(2)

\reg(I)=\reg(S/I)+1
,

(3)

\pd(xI)=\pd(I)
,

(4)

\reg(xI)=\reg(I)+1
.

2.2.
Betti splittings

Betti splittings was introduced by Francisco, Ha, and Van Tuyl
[
FHV
]
for monomial ideals. It has regained interest recently in some work
[
CF
,
CFL
]
. We recall the definition and the following results about Betti splittings from
[
NV2
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
P,I,J
be proper nonzero homogeneous ideals of
S
such that
P=I+J
. The decomposition
P=I+J
is called a Betti splitting if for all
i\geq 0
we have
\beta_{i}(P)=\beta_{i}(I)+\beta_{i}(J)+\beta_{i-1}(I\cap J)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
P=I+J
is a Betti splitting of ideals of
S
. Then

\displaystyle\pd_{S}(P)

\displaystyle=\max\{\pd_{S}(I),\pd_{S}(J),\pd_{S}(I\cap J)+1\},

\displaystyle\reg_{S}(P)

\displaystyle=\max\{\reg_{S}(I),\reg_{S}(J),\reg_{S}(I\cap J)-1\}.

Proof.

See
[
FHV
, Corollary 2.2]
or
[
NV2
, Lemma 3.7]
.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi:M\to N
be a morphism of finitely generated graded
S
-modules. We say that
\varphi
is
\Tor
-vanishing if for all
i\geq 0
, we have
\Tor_{i}^{S}(\mathrm{k},\varphi)=0
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I,J
be nonzero homogeneous ideals of
S
and
P=I+J
. The decomposition
P=I+J
is a Betti splitting if and only if the inclusion maps
I\cap J\to I
and
I\cap J\to J
are
\Tor
-vanishing.

Proof.

See
[
NV2
, Lemma 3.5]
.
∎

For a monomial ideal
I
of
S
, we denote by
\partial^{*}(I)
the ideal generated by elements of the form
f/x_{i}
, where
f
is a minimal monomial generator of
I
and
x_{i}
is a variable dividing
f
. We have

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
I\subseteq J
be monomial ideals of
S
. Assume that
\partial^{*}(I)\subseteq J
. Then the inclusion map
I\to J
is
\Tor
-vanishing.

Proof.

Applying
[
NV2
, Lemma 4.2, Proposition 4.4]
, we deduce the conclusion.
∎

2.3.
Graphs and their path ideals

Let
G
denote a finite simple graph over the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
x\in V(G)
, let the neighborhood of
x
be the subset
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
. The closed neighborhood of
x
is
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
. A leaf is a vertex having a unique neighbor.

A simple graph
H
is a subgraph of
G
if
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
.
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
if it is a subgraph of
G
and
E(H)=E(G)\cap V(H)\times V(H)
.

For a subset
U\subset V(G)
, we denote by
N_{G}(U)=\cup\left(N_{G}(x)\mid x\in U\right)
and
N_{G}[U]=\cup\left(N_{G}[x]\mid x\in U\right)
. When it is clear from the context, we drop the subscript
G
from the notation
N_{G}
. The induced subgraph of
G
on
U
, denoted by
G[U]
, is the graph such that
V(G[U])=U
and
E(G[U])=E(G)\cap U\times U
. The notation
G\setminus U
denotes the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus U
.

A cycle
C
of length
m
is the graph on
V(C)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
whose edges are
\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\ldots,\{x_{m-1}x_{m}\},\{x_{m},x_{1}\}
.

A graph
G
is called unicyclic if
G
has a unique cycle.

A tree is a connected graph without any cycle.

Let
t\geq 2
be a natural number. A
t
-path of
G
is a sequence of distinct vertices
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of
G
such that
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
,
\ldots,\{x_{t-1},x_{t}\}
are edges of
G
. A
t
-path matching in a graph
G
is a subgraph consisting of pairwise disjoint
t
-paths. If the subgraph is induced, then
t
-path matching is said to be a
t
-path induced matching of
G
. The
t
-path induced matching number of
G
, denoted by
\nu_{t}(G)
, is the largest size of a
t
-path induced matching of
G
.

From the definition, we have

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a simple graph. We have

(1)

Assume that
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
. Then
\nu_{t}(H)\leq\nu_{t}(G)
.

(2)

Assume that
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are disjoint. Then
\nu_{t}(G)=\nu_{t}(G_{1})+\nu_{t}(G_{2})
.

The
t
-path ideal of
G
is defined to be

I_{t}(G)=\left(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}\mid x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{t}}\text{ is a }t\text{-path of }G\right)\subseteq S.

For simplicity of notation, we often write
x_{i}\in G
instead of
x_{i}\in V(G)
. By abuse of notation, we also call a monomial
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}\in I_{t}(G)
a
t
-path of
G
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
H
be an induced subgraph of
G
. Then
\reg(I_{t}(H))\leq\reg(I_{t}(G))
and
\pd(I_{t}(H))\leq\pd(I_{t}(G))
. In particular,
\reg(I_{t}(G))\geq(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)+1
.

Proof.

Since
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
,
I_{t}(H)
is the restriction of
I_{t}(G)
on
V(H)
. The conclusion follows from
[
OHH
, Corollary 2.5]
.

Now let
H
be a maximum
t
-induced matching of
G
. Then
I_{t}(H)
is the complete intersection of
\nu_{t}(G)
forms of degree
t
. Hence,
\reg(I_{t}(H))=(t-1)\nu_{t}(G)+1
. The conclusion follows.
∎

2.4.
3-path induced matching numbers

In this subsection, we give a recursive formula for computing the
3
-path induced matching numbers of graphs having at most one cycle. Let
G
be a connected graph having at most one cycle of length
m
on the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
. When
G
is a tree, we set
m=1
. Attached to each
v_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, we have a tree
T_{i}
, where we may consider
T_{i}
as a rooted tree with root
v_{i}
. Note that
E(T_{i})
might be empty. For each vertex
y\in T_{i}
, the level of
y
, denoted by
\level(y)
, is the length of the unique path from
v_{i}
to
y
. Fix an
i
such that
E(T_{i})
is non-empty. Let
z_{0}\in T_{i}
be a leaf of
G
of the highest level among all leaves in
T_{i}
. Let
y_{0}
be the unique neighbor of
z_{0}
.

First, assume that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. Then
y_{0}\notin\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
has a unique neighbor that is not a leaf. We assume that
N(y_{0})=\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\}\cup\{x_{0}\}
, where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
are leaves and
x_{0}
is not a leaf of
G
. We define the following graphs

(2.1)

G_{z_{0},1}=G\setminus\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{s}\},G_{z_{0},2}=G\setminus N[y_{0}],\text{ and }G_{z_{0},3}=G\setminus N[\{y_{0},x_{0}\}].

Lemma 2.9
.

With the notations above, we have

\nu_{3}(G)=\begin{cases}\max\{\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1}),1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1}),1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},3})\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
, the left-hand side is at least the right-hand side. Let
H
be an induced
3
-path matching of
G
of maximum size. If
V(H)\cap\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{s}\}=\emptyset
then
H
is an induced
3
-path matching of
G_{z_{0},1}
. Thus, we may assume that
z_{0}\in V(H)
. If
t=0
then the
3
-path must be
z_{0}y_{0}x_{0}
and we have
\nu_{3}(G)=1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},3})
. If
t>0
then the
3
-path is either
z_{0}y_{0}z_{j}
for some
j>0
or
z_{0}y_{0}x_{0}
. In either cases,
\nu_{3}(G)\leq 1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Now assume that
\level(z_{0})=1
. We may assume that
v_{1}
is the unique neighbor of
z_{0}
. We assume that
N(v_{1})=\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\}\cup\{v_{2},v_{m}\}
. Note that
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
are leaves of level
1
. We define the following graphs

(2.2)

\begin{split}G_{z_{0},1}&=G\setminus\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{s}\},G_{z_{0},2}=G\setminus N[v_{1}],\\
G_{z_{0},3}&=G\setminus N[\{v_{1},v_{2}\}],G_{z_{0},4}=G\setminus N[\{v_{1},v_{m}\}].\end{split}

Lemma 2.10
.

With the notations above, we have

\nu_{3}(G)=\begin{cases}\max\{\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1}),1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1}),1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}),1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},4})\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
2.9
with a remark that when
t=0
and
z_{0}\in H
where
H
is a maximum
3
-induced matching of
G
then either
z_{0},v_{1},v_{2}
or
z_{0},v_{1},v_{m}\in H
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We now compute the
3
-path induced matching numbers of paths and cycles.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
P_{m}
be a path on the vertices
\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then
\nu_{3}(P_{m})=\lfloor\frac{m+1}{4}\rfloor
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
m
. The base case
m\leq 3
is obvious. Now, assume that
m\geq 4
. By Lemma
2.9
, we have

\nu_{3}(P_{m})=\max\left\{\nu_{3}(P_{m-1}),1+\nu_{3}(P_{m-4})\right\}.

By induction on
m
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
C_{m}
be a cycle of length
m\geq 4
. Then
\nu_{3}(C_{m})=\lfloor\frac{m}{4}\rfloor
.

Proof.

We may assume that
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
is in a maximum
3
-induced matching of
C_{m}
. Then
\nu_{3}(C_{m})=1+\nu_{3}(P_{m-5})
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.11
.
∎

2.5.
Proximal graphs

Let
G
be a connected simple graph with a unique cycle
C
on the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
where
m\geq 3
. Let
\partial(C)=N_{G}(C)\setminus C
be the boundary of
C
in
G
and
\Gamma_{G}(C)
be the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus\partial(C)
.

Definition 2.13
.

We say that
G
is
t
-proximal if
\nu_{t}(G)=\nu_{t}(\Gamma_{G}(C)).

We now prove some properties regarding
3
-induced matching numbers of
3
-proximal graphs. We keep the notations as in subsection
2.4
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
z_{0}
be a leaf of the highest level of
G
with the unique neighbor
y_{0}
. Assume that
N(y_{0})=\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\}\cup\{x_{0}\}
and
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. Assume that
G
is not
3
-proximal.

(1)

If
G_{z_{0},1}
is
3
-proximal then
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})<\nu_{3}(G)
.

(2)

If
s\geq 1
and
G_{z_{0},2}
is
3
-proximal then
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})+1<\nu_{3}(G)
.

(3)

If
s=0
and
G_{z_{0},3}
is
3
-proximal then
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},3})+1<\nu_{3}(G)
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by contradiction.

For (1), assume that
G
is not
3
-proximal,
G_{z_{0},1}
is
3
-proximal and
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})=\nu_{3}(G)
. Note that
\Gamma_{1}(C)
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma_{G}(C)
, where
\Gamma_{1}(C)=\Gamma_{G_{z_{0},1}}(C)
. By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{1}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(G).

Hence,
\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))
. In other words,
G
is
3
-proximal, a contradiction.

For (2), assume that
s\geq 1
,
G
is not
3
-proximal,
G_{z_{0},2}
is
3
-proximal and
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})+1=\nu_{3}(G)
. Since
G_{z_{0},2}
is
3
-proximal, it contains
C
. Let
\Gamma_{2}(C)=\Gamma_{G_{z_{0},2}}(C)
. Note that
\Gamma_{2}(C)\cup\{z_{0}y_{0}x_{0}\}
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma_{G}(C)
. By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\nu_{3}(G)=1+\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})=1+\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{2}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(G).

Hence,
\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))
, a contradiction.

One can prove Part (3) similarly to Part (2).
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
z_{0}
be a leaf of the highest level of
G
with
\level(z_{0})=1
. Assume that
v_{1}
is the unique neighbor of
z_{0}
and
N(v_{1})=\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\}\cup\{v_{2},v_{m}\}
. Assume that
G
is not
3
-proximal and
G_{z_{0},1}
is
3
-proximal. Then
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})<\nu_{3}(G)
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})=\nu_{3}(G)
. Let
\Gamma_{1}(C)=\Gamma_{G_{z_{0},1}}(C)
. Note that
\Gamma_{1}(C)
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma_{G}(C)
. By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{1}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))\leq\nu_{3}(G).

Hence,
\nu_{3}(G)=\nu_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C))
, a contradiction.
∎

3.
Betti splittings for 3-path ideals of graphs with at most one cycle

In this section, we establish certain Betti splittings for the
3
-path ideals of graphs with at most one cycle. First, we prove some general results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with a leaf
z_{0}
. Let
y_{0}
be the unique neighbor of
z_{0}
. Then

I_{3}(G)=z_{0}y_{0}\left(x\mid x\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\}\right)+I_{3}(G_{1}),

where
G_{1}=G\setminus\{z_{0}\}
is the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus\{z_{0}\}
.

Proof.

If a
3
-path of
G
does not contain
z_{0}
then it must be a
3
-path of
G_{1}
. Now, assume that
p
is a
3
-path of
G
that contains
z_{0}
. Since
z_{0}
is a leaf with unique neighbor
y_{0}
, we must have
p
is of the form
z_{0}y_{0}x
with
x\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph and
y
be a vertex of
G
. Then

I_{3}(G):y=(uv\mid u\neq v\in N(y))+(uw\mid u\in N(y),w\in N(u)\setminus N[y])+I_{3}(G_{2}),

where
G_{2}
is the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus N[y]
.

Proof.

We denote by
L
the ideal on the right-hand side. Note that
yuv
and
yuw
with
u\neq v\in N(y)
and
w\in N(u)\setminus N[y]
are
3
-paths of
G
. Hence, the left-hand side contains the right-hand side.

Now, let
p
be a
3
-path of
G
. If
y\in p
then
p
must be of the form
yuv
and

as before, and we have

. Thus, we may assume that

. If

and we are done. Thus, we may assume that

. In particular, we have

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a simple graph with a leaf

. Let

be the unique neighbor of
z_{0}
. We denote by

\displaystyle J(z_{0})

\displaystyle=(x\mid x\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\}),

\displaystyle L(z_{0})

\displaystyle=(uv\mid u\neq v\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\})+(uw\mid u\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\},w\in N(u)\setminus N[y_{0}])

\displaystyle+\sum_{u\in N(y_{0})\setminus\{z_{0}\}}uI_{3}(G_{u}),

where

is the induced subgraph of

on

. Then the decomposition

is a Betti splitting and

z_{0}y_{0}J(z_{0})\cap I_{3}(G_{1})=z_{0}y_{0}L(z_{0}).

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, in the proof, we use
J
for

and
L
for
L(z_{0})
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
I_{3}(G)=z_{0}y_{0}J+I_{3}(G_{1})
. By

, Corollary 4.12]
and the fact that

is a Betti splitting. Now, we have

z_{0}y_{0}J\cap I_{3}(G_{1})=z_{0}y_{0}\left(J\cap(I_{3}(G_{1}):y_{0})\right).

By Lemma
3.2
,

I_{3}(G_{1}):y_{0}=(uv\mid u\neq v\in N(y_{0}))+(uw\mid u\in N(y_{0}),w\in N(u)\setminus N[y_{0}])+I_{3}(G_{2})

where

is the induced subgraph of

on

. We need to prove that

From the definition of

and

. It remains to prove that for any

. There exist

. Note that

. Thus,

∎

.

4.
Projective dimension and regularity of 3-path ideals of graphs with at most one cycle

Let
G
be a connected graph having at most one cycle
C
on the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
. With the Betti splittings established in Section
3
, one can deduce a recursive formula for computing the Betti numbers of
3
-path ideals of
G
. Nonetheless, the formula is a bit cumbersome, so we focus on deriving the formulae for the projective dimension and regularity. We then deduce Theorem
1.1
.

We first have some preparation lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
be variables of
S
and
P
a nonzero monomial ideal of
S
such that
z_{i}\notin\supp P
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Let
Q_{s}=(z_{i}z_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq s)+(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})P
. Assume that
s\geq 1
. Then

(1)

\pd_{S}(Q_{s})=\pd_{S}(P)+s-1
,

(2)

\reg_{S}(Q_{s})=\reg_{S}(P)+1
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
s
. The base case
s=1
is clear. Assume that the statements hold for
s\geq 1
. Let
B=z_{s+1}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})+z_{s+1}P
. Then we have
Q_{s+1}=Q_{s}+B
and
Q_{s}\cap B=z_{s+1}Q_{s}
. By Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that the decomposition
Q_{s+1}=Q_{s}+B
is a Betti splitting. By induction and Lemma
2.3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\pd_{S}(Q_{s+1})

\displaystyle=\max\{\pd_{S}(Q_{s})+1,\pd_{S}(B)\}=\pd_{S}(P)+s,

\displaystyle\reg_{S}(Q_{s+1})

\displaystyle=\max\{\reg_{S}(Q_{s}),\reg_{S}(B)\}=\reg_{S}(P)+1.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
be variables of
S
and
P
a non-zero monomial ideal of
S
such that
z_{i}\notin\supp P
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Let
Q=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})+P
. Then

(1)

\pd_{S}(Q)=\pd_{S}(P)+s
,

(2)

\reg_{S}(Q)=\reg_{S}(P)
.

Proof.

Let
Z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
. The conclusion follows from
[
NV2
, Proposition 3.11]
and the facts that
\pd_{S}(Z)=s-1
and
\reg_{S}(Z)=1
.
∎

We recall the following notations in Section
2
. Let
z_{0}
be a leaf of
G
of the highest level and
y_{0}
its unique neighbor. First, assume that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. Let
N(y_{0})=\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\}\cup\{x_{0}\}
, where
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}
are leaves of
G
having the same level as
z_{0}
and

is the unique neighbor of

of smaller level. We denote by

,
G_{z_{0},2}=G\setminus N[y_{0}]
and
G_{z_{0},3}=G\setminus N[\{y_{0},x_{0}\}]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. With the notations above, we have

\pd(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{p_{1},p_{2}+s+1,p_{3}+|N(x_{0})|+s\},&\text{ if }s>0\\
\max\{p_{1},p_{3}+|N(x_{0})|\},&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

where
p_{1}=\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),p_{2}=\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))
, and
p_{3}=\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))
.

Proof.

For each
i=0,\ldots,s+1
, let
G_{i}=G\setminus\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{i-1}\}
. We have
G_{0}=G
and
G_{s+1}=G_{z_{0},1}
. For each
i=0,\ldots,s
, we define the following ideals

\displaystyle U_{i}

\displaystyle=(z_{j}z_{k}\mid i<j<k\leq s)+(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{s})I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})

\displaystyle V_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{0}(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{s},u\in N(x_{0})\setminus\{y_{0}\})+x_{0}I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3})

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle=U_{i}+V_{i}.

By Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

(4.1)

\pd(I_{3}(G_{i}))=\max\{s-i,\pd(I_{3}(G_{i+1})),\pd(L_{i})+1\}.

Note that
U_{s}=(0)
. By Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.4
, we have

(4.2)

\pd(L_{i})=\begin{cases}\max\{\pd(U_{i})+1,\pd(V_{i})\},&\text{ if }i<s,\\
\pd(V_{i}),&\text{ if }i=s.\end{cases}

By Lemma
4.1
,
\pd(U_{i})=\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+s-i-1
for

. By Lemma

,
\pd(V_{i})=\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+|N(x_{0})|+s-i-1
. Hence,

(4.3)

\pd(L_{i})=\begin{cases}\max\{p_{2}+s-i,p_{3}+|N(x_{0})|+s-i-1\},&\text{ if }i<s,\\
p_{3}+|N(x_{0})|-1,&\text{ if }i=s.\end{cases}

The conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. With the notations above, we have

\reg(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+2\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+2\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

We use the notations as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
. By Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

(4.4)

\reg(I_{3}(G_{i}))=\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{i+1})),\reg(L_{i})+1\}.

By Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.4
, we have

(4.5)

\reg(L_{i})=\max\{\reg(U_{i}),\reg(V_{i})\}.

Note that
U_{s}
is the zero ideal. By Lemma
4.1
,
\reg(U_{i})=\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+1
if

. By Lemma
4.2
,
\reg(V_{i})=\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+1

. Since

is an induced subgraph of

, we have that
\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))\leq\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))

∎

Now, assume that

,

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
\level(z_{0})=1
. With the notations above, we have

\pd(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{p_{1},p_{2}+s+1,p_{3}+n_{2}+s+1,p_{4}+n_{m}+s+1\},&\text{ if }s>0\\
\max\{p_{1},p_{3}+n_{2}+1,p_{4}+n_{m}+1\},&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

,

,

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle=(z_{j}z_{k}\mid i<j<k\leq s)+(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{s})I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2})

By Lemma

, Lemma

\displaystyle\pd_{S}(L_{i})

\displaystyle=\max\{\pd(X_{i}+Y_{i})+1,\pd(Z_{i})\}

\displaystyle=\pd_{S}(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},4}))+|N(v_{m})\setminus N[v_{1}]|+s-i.

The conclusion follows.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that

\reg(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+2\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+2,\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},4}))+2\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\reg(L_{i})

\displaystyle=\max\{\reg(X_{i}+Y_{i}),\reg(Z_{i})\}

\displaystyle\pd(I_{3}(G))

\displaystyle=\max\{\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{1},1})),\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{1},2}))+4,\pd(I_{3}(G_{z_{1},3}))+8\},

\displaystyle\reg(I_{3}(G))

\displaystyle=\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{1},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{1},2}))+2\}.

Let

for simplicity of notations. Then
z_{5}
is a leaf of the highest level in

\displaystyle I(H_{z_{5},1})

\displaystyle=(v_{1}v_{2},\ldots,v_{5}v_{6},v_{1}v_{6})+v_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+v_{2}(x_{5},x_{6})

\displaystyle+x_{1}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{4})+x_{5}(y_{5},y_{6}),

\displaystyle I(H_{z_{5},3})

\displaystyle=(v_{3}v_{4},\ldots,v_{5}v_{6},v_{1}v_{6})+v_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{1}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{4}).

By Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\pd(I_{3}(H))

\displaystyle=\max\{\pd(I_{3}(H_{z_{5},1})),\pd(I_{3}(H_{z_{5},3}))+3\},

\displaystyle\reg(I_{3}(H))

\displaystyle=\max\{\reg(I_{3}(H_{z_{5},1})),\reg(I_{3}(H_{z_{5},3}))+2\}.

The graphs

and

are relatively small, and we can compute the projective dimension and regularity of their

-path ideals using Macaulay2

]

and
\reg(I_{3}(H))=7
. We can also apply Theorem
4.3
and Theorem

or compute the projective dimension and regularity of the

\pd(I_{3}(G))=12

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We prove by induction on
|G\setminus C_{m}|
. If
G=C_{m}
the conclusion follows from
[

]
. Thus, we may assume that there exists a leaf
z_{0}\notin\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
of some tree
T_{i}
attached to
v_{i}
. We do not assume that
m>1
, i.e.,
G
might be a tree. We choose
z_{0}
of the highest level in
T_{i}
. First, assume that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. By Theorem
4.4
, we have

(4.6)

\reg(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+2\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+2\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

If
\level(z_{0})=1
, by Theorem
4.6
, we have

(4.7)

\reg(I_{3}(G))=\begin{cases}\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},2}))+2\}&\text{ if }s\geq 1,\\
\max\{\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},1})),\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},3}))+2,\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},4}))+2\}&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

For ease of reading, we divide the proof into several steps. The first step is the result of Kumar and Sarkar
[
KS
]
. We give a different proof here to illustrate our method.

Step 1.
Assume that
G
is a tree. Then
\reg(I_{3}(G))=2\nu_{3}(G)+1
.

If
I_{3}(G)=0
then
\nu_{3}(G)=0
and the conclusion is vacuous. Now assume that
I_{3}(G)
is nonzero. We can always choose a vertex
v
acting as the root of
G
so that
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
. The conclusion then follows from induction, Eq. (
4.6
), and Lemma
2.9
.

Step 2.

\reg(I_{3}(G))\leq 2\nu_{3}(G)+(\reg(I_{3}(C_{m}))-2\nu_{3}(C_{m}))
.

Since the graphs
G_{z_{0},j}
for

are induced subgraphs of
G
, by induction and Step 1, we deduce that
\reg(I_{3}(G_{z_{0},j}))\leq 2\nu_{3}(G)+(\reg(I_{3}(C_{m}))-2\nu_{3}(C_{m}))
. If
\level(z_{0})\geq 2
, the conclusion follows from Eq. (
4.6
) and Lemma
2.9
. If
\level(z_{0})=1
, the conclusion follows from Eq. (
4.7
) and Lemma
2.10
.

Step 3.
Assume that
G
is unicyclic and
m=3
or
m\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
. Then
\reg(I_{3}(G))=2\nu_{3}(G)+1
.

Indeed, the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.8
, Step 2, and
[
AF1
, Corollary 5.5]
.

Step 4.
Assume that
G
is unicyclic with the unique cycle
C
on
m

. Assume further that

and

is

-proximal. Then
\reg(I_{3}(G))=2\nu_{3}(G)+\reg(I_{3}(C))-2\nu_{3}(C)
.

Indeed, we have
\Gamma_{G}(C)
is the disjoint union of
C
and a forest

. By Step 1, we deduce that
\reg(I_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C)))=\reg(I_{3}(C))+2\nu_{3}(F)+1
. By Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
2.8
, and Step 2, we have

\begin{split}2\nu_{3}(G)&+(\reg(I_{3}(C_{m}))-2\nu_{3}(C_{m}))=\reg(I_{3}(\Gamma_{G}(C)))\leq\reg(I_{3}(G))\\
&\leq 2\nu_{3}(G)+(\reg(I_{3}(C_{m}))-2\nu_{3}(C_{m})).\end{split}

The conclusion follows.

Step 5.
Assume that
G
is unicyclic with the unique cycle

m

By Lemma

Step 6.
Conclusion step. By Step 1 and Step 3, we may assume that

.

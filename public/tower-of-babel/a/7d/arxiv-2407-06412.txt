On Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -Instantons and the Fourier-Mukai Transform of Complex Lagrangians
Jesse Madnick, Emily Autumn Windes

Abstract
The real Fourier-Mukai (RFM) transform relates calibrated graphs to so-called “deformed instantons” on Hermitian line bundles. We show that under the RFM transform, complex Lagrangian graphs in ℝ 2 ​ n × T 2 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{2n}\times T^{2n} correspond to Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instantons over ℝ 2 ​ n × ( T 2 ​ n ) ∗ \mathbb{R}^{2n}\times(T^{2n})^{*} . In other words, the deformed Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instanton equation coincides with the usual Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instanton equation. Motivated by this observation, we study Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instantons on hyperkähler manifolds X 4 ​ n X^{4n} , with an emphasis on conical singularities. First, when X = C ⁡ ( M ) X=C(M) is a hyperkähler cone, we relate Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instantons on X X to tri-contact instantons on the 3 3 -Sasakian link M M and consider various dimensional reductions. Second, when X X is an asymptotically conical (AC) hyperkähler manifold of rate ν ≤ − 2 3 ​ ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) \nu\leq-\frac{2}{3}(2n+1) , we prove a Lewis-type theorem to the following effect: If the set of AC Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instantons is non-empty, then every AC Hermitian Yang-Mills connection over X X with sufficiently fast decay at infinity is an Sp ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{Sp}(n) -instanton.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background and Motivation

Gauge-theoretic equations are a potent tool in geometry and topology. As such, mathematicians and physicists have begun considering certain gauge-theoretic PDE in the context of Ricci-flat manifolds with special holonomy, i.e., those Riemannian manifolds with holonomy group

H=\textstyle\mathrm{SU}(n),\ \ \mathrm{Sp}(n),\ \ \mathrm{G}_{2},\ \ \mathrm{Spin}(7).

Perhaps the simplest such PDE are the
H
-instanton equations
, which generalize the anti-self-dual (ASD) equations familiar from the theory of
4
-manifolds.

Historically, the study of these four instanton equations has proceeded along rather different lines. For example, the
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-instanton equation is precisely the
primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills
(pHYM) condition, a highly well-studied PDE that enjoys deep ties to algebraic geometry via the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau Theorem. Where
\mathrm{G}_{2}
- and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons are concerned, influential papers of Donaldson–Thomas
[
13
]
and Donaldson–Segal
[
12
]
suggest that such connections might play a role in defining an enumerative invariant of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
- and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifolds.

The
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation, on the other hand, has received comparably less attention. In the literature, vector bundles equipped with an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton are called
hyperholomorphic
, as they carry a triple of holomorphic structures that satisfy the quaternionic relations. Such bundles have been studied mathematically by, for example, Verbitsky
[
43
,
45
]
, Kaledin–Verbitsky
[
27
]
, Feix
[
17
]
, Haydys
[
23
]
, Hitchin
[
24
]
, Ionaş
[
25
]
, Meazzini–Onorati
[
33
]
, among others, and arise in physics via
4
-dimensional supersymmetric field theories
[
34
]
. Given the importance of ASD connections (which are nothing other than
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-instantons) in the study of
4
-manifolds, we expect that the moduli space of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons will prove useful in the study of hyperkähler
4n
-manifolds.

A second motivation for this work comes from mirror symmetry. Roughly speaking, mirror symmetry predicts the existence of deep relationships between the calibrated geometry of a special holonomy manifold
X
, and gauge theory on its “mirror”
X^{*}
. In the Calabi-Yau setting, Leung–Yau–Zaslow
[
31
]
showed that under certain simplifying assumptions, the
real Fourier-Mukai (RFM) transform
provides such a relationship. Later, Lee–Leung
[
30
]
studied this transform in the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
settings, the latter of which was explored further by Kawai–Yamamoto
[
29
]
. In all three contexts, the RFM transform relates calibrated graphs to
deformed
H
-instantons
, a nonlinear analogue of the
H
-instanton equation.

Calabi-Yau geometry:

special Lagrangian graph

\displaystyle\ \longleftrightarrow\text{ deformed HYM connection}

\mathrm{G}_{2}
geometry:

co/associative graph

\displaystyle\ \longleftrightarrow\text{ deformed $\mathrm{G}_{2}$-instanton}

\mathrm{Spin}(7)
geometry:

Cayley graph

\displaystyle\ \longleftrightarrow\text{ deformed $\mathrm{Spin}(7)$-instanton}

In hyperkähler
4n
-manifolds, perhaps the most natural class of calibrated submanifolds consists of the
complex Lagrangians
, those
2n
-dimensional submanifolds that are both complex with respect to
I
and Lagrangian with respect to
J
and
K
. Thus, by analogy with the Calabi-Yau,
\mathrm{G}_{2}
, and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
settings, we would like to say that a connection arising from the RFM transform of a graphical submanifold
S
satisfies the
deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation
if and only if
S
is complex Lagrangian.

Our first result is a derivation of this putative deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation. Surprisingly, we find that it coincides with the ordinary
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation. To be precise:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be an open set, and equip
X=B\times T^{2n}
with a product hyperkähler structure
(g,(I,J,K))
such that
B\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}\subset X
is a complex Lagrangian with respect to
I
. Let
h\colon B\to T^{2n}
be a smooth function, let
S_{h}\subset X
be its graph, and let
\nabla^{h}
be its real Fourier-Mukai transform on
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to B\times(T^{n})^{*}
. Then:

S_{h}\text{ is complex Lagrangian with respect to }I\ \iff\ \nabla^{h}\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton.}

Perhaps the most striking feature of Theorem
1.1
is the linearity of the deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation. Indeed, since the complex Lagrangian condition is defined by the vanishing of a pair of
2
-forms, one expects that the deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation will be quadratic in the curvature rather than linear. (The reason for this linearity is explained in the proof.) From the point of view of mirror symmetry, it is tempting to interpret the above result as an instance of the philosophy that hyperkähler manifolds are in some sense “self-mirror”
[
44
]
.

1.2
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-Instantons

We are thus motivated to study
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons on hyperkähler manifolds
(X,g,(I,J,K))
. Essentially by definition,

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton}\ \iff\ A\text{ is primitive HYM with respect to }I,J,\text{ and }K.

In this work, we will study the situation in which
X
is a cone or asymptotically conical.

Suppose first that
X^{4n+4}=\mathrm{C}(M)
is a hyperkähler cone with
n\geq 1
, and let
M^{4n+3}
be its
3
-Sasakian link. In Proposition
6.2
, we show that conical
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instantons on
X
are modeled by
tri-contact instantons
on
M
, connections that are contact instantons in an
S^{2}
-family of ways. This raises the question of how to construct tri-contact instantons. To this end, we establish two dimensional reductions. Specifically, fixing one of the projections
p\colon M\to Z^{4n+2}
to the
(4n+2)
-dimensional (Kähler-Einstein) twistor space
Z
, and letting
h\colon M\to Q^{4n}
denote the projection to the
4n
-dimensional quaternionic-Kähler quotient, in
\lx@sectionsign
6.3
we show that:

\displaystyle p^{*}A\text{ is a tri-contact instanton on }M^{4n+3}

\displaystyle\iff A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton on }Z^{4n+2}

(1.1)

\displaystyle h^{*}A\text{ is a tri-contact instanton on }M^{4n+3}

\displaystyle\iff A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton on }Q^{4n}.

(1.2)

That the notion of “
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton” makes sense on the
(4n+2)
-dimensional twistor space
Z
is explained in
\lx@sectionsign
6.3
. These generalize results that are well-known when
n=1
.

Returning to the setting of smooth hyperkähler manifolds, we consider the obvious implication

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton }\implies A\text{ is primitive HYM with respect to }I,

and ask to what extent the converse might hold. If
X
is compact and at least one
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton exists, then the converse can be deduced from results of Verbitsky
[
43
]
. More precisely:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X^{4n},g,(I,J,K))
be a compact hyperkähler
4n
-manifold. Let
P\to X
be a principal
G
-bundle, where
G
is a compact Lie group. Suppose that
P
admits an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton. For any connection
A
on
P
, we have

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton }\iff A\text{ is primitive HYM with respect to }I.

In
\lx@sectionsign
7.1
, we will give a self-contained proof of Theorem
1.2
with an eye towards the non-compact setting. That is, if
X
is asymptotically conical (AC), and assuming that at least one AC
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton exists, we expect by analogy that AC primitive HYM connections with sufficiently fast decay ought to be
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons. Indeed, in
\lx@sectionsign
7.2
, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P\to(X^{4n},g,(I,J,K))
be a principal
G
-bundle over an AC hyperkähler manifold
X
of rate
\nu<0
, where
G
is a compact Lie group. Let
\Sigma^{4n-1}
be the asymptotic link of
X
, and fix a tri-contact instanton
A_{\infty}
on
Q\to\Sigma
, where
Q
is an asymptotic framing of
P
.

Suppose that
\nu\leq-\frac{2}{3}(2n+1)
, and that
P
admits an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton asymptotic to
A_{\infty}
with decay rate less than
\nu+1
. Then every
I
-primitive HYM connection on
P
that is asymptotic to
A_{\infty}
with sufficiently fast decay is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton.

The term “sufficiently fast decay” is explained in the more precise statement of Theorem
7.15
.

2.1
\Omega
-ASD Connections

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with
n\geq 4
, and fix a smooth
(n-4)
-form
\Omega\in\Omega^{n-4}(M)
with comass at most
1
. A connection
A
on a vector bundle
E\to M
is called
\Omega
-anti-self-dual
(or
\Omega
-ASD
) if it satisfies

\ast\!F_{A}=-\Omega\wedge F_{A}.

(2.1)

By applying
d_{A}
and the Bianchi identity, one sees that any such connection
A
satisfies the
\Omega
-Yang-Mills equation
(or the
Yang-Mills with torsion equation
):

d_{A}(\ast F_{A})=-d\Omega\wedge F_{A}.

(2.2)

An equivalent condition is
d_{A}^{*}(F_{A}+\ast(F_{A}\wedge\Omega))=0
. It is well-known that the
\Omega
-Yang-Mills equations are the Euler-Lagrange equations of the functional

\mathrm{YM}_{\Omega}(A)=\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol}-\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\Omega.

(2.3)

Moreover,
A
is an absolute minimizer of
\mathrm{YM}_{\Omega}
if and only if it is
\Omega
-ASD.

Now, if
\Omega
is closed, then the
\Omega
-Yang-Mills equation reduces to the usual
Yang-Mills equation

d_{A}(\ast F_{A})=0.

(2.4)

If, in addition,
M
is compact, then the second term in (
2.3
) is topological, and so the critical points of
\mathrm{YM}_{\Omega}
are equivalent to those of the usual Yang-Mills functional
\mathrm{YM}(A)=\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol}
.

Example 2.1
.

The classical situation is that of
n=4
and
\Omega=1
. In that case, the
\Omega
-ASD equation (
2.1
) reduces to the familiar ASD equation
\ast F_{A}=-F_{A}
.

We reiterate that if
\Omega
is not closed, then
\Omega
-ASD connections are
\Omega
-Yang Mills, but not necessarily Yang-Mills. Nevertheless, for certain special choices of (non-closed)
\Omega
, it can happen that
\Omega
-ASD connections are automatically Yang-Mills. For example, this phenomenon occurs when
(M,g)
is the (Einstein) link of a Ricci-flat cone with special holonomy
H
. More precisely:

•

(
H=\mathrm{SU}(n)
) If
M^{2n-1}
is a Sasaki-Einstein manifold with
n\geq 3
, then the
contact instanton equation
(see
\lx@sectionsign
5.2
) is equivalent to an
\Omega
-ASD equation for a particular non-closed
\Omega\in\Omega^{2n-5}(M)
. In this context, contact instantons are Yang-Mills
[
4
]
,
[
39
]
.

•

(
H=\mathrm{G}_{2}
) If
M^{6}
is a strict nearly-Kähler
6

Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation
(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.3.1
) is equivalent to an
\Omega
-ASD equation for a particular non-closed
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
. In this context, HYM connections are Yang-Mills
[

]
.

•

(
H=\mathrm{Spin}(7)
) If

2.2
H
-Structures

Let
M
be a smooth
n
-manifold, and let
H\leq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a Lie subgroup. An
H
-structure on
M
is an
H
-subbundle of the general frame bundle of
M
. In most cases of interest, the data of an
H
-structure is equivalent to a collection of tensors on
M
satisfying suitable algebraic conditions. For instance:

Example 2.2
.

Let
M^{2n}
be a
2n
-manifold. A
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structure
on
M
is equivalent to a triple
(g,J,\omega)
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
, a
g
-orthogonal almost-complex structure
J
, and the non-degenerate
2
-form
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
. A
Kähler structure
is a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structure with
\nabla\omega=0
(or, equivalently,
\nabla J=0
), where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
M
.

We will be particularly interested in those
H
-structures where
H
is one of the Ricci-flat holonomy groups on Berger’s list, i.e.:

\textstyle\mathrm{SU}(n),\ \ \ \mathrm{Sp}(n),\ \ \ \mathrm{G}_{2},\ \ \ \mathrm{Spin}(7).

(2.5)

In each of the following,
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of the given Riemannian metric.

1.

Let
M^{2n}
be a
2n
-manifold. An
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-structure

(g,J,\omega,\Upsilon)
on
M
is equivalent to a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structure
(g,J,\omega)
together with a a complex volume form
\Upsilon\in\Omega^{n,0}(M)
, normalized so that

c_{n}\Upsilon\wedge\overline{\Upsilon}=\frac{1}{n!}\omega^{n},\ \ \ \ c_{n}=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(i/2)^{n}.

A
Calabi-Yau structure
is an
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-structure satisfying
\nabla\omega=0
and
\nabla\Upsilon=0
(or, equivalently,
\nabla J=0
and
\nabla\Upsilon=0
). It is well-known that if
(g,J,\omega,\Upsilon)
is Calabi-Yau, then
\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(g)\leq\mathrm{SU}(n)
, and hence
g
is Ricci-flat. Conversely, every Riemannian metric with holonomy contained in
\mathrm{SU}(n)
arises from a Calabi-Yau structure.

2.

Let
M^{4n}
be a
4n
-manifold. An
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-structure
on
M
is equivalent to a pair
(g,(I,J,K))
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g
and a triple
(I,J,K)
of
g
-orthogonal almost-complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations
IJ=K
. In this case, we often write
\omega_{I}:=g(I\cdot,\cdot)
, and similarly for
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
. Note that an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-structure on
M
induces an
\mathrm{SU}(2n)
-structure
(g,I,\omega_{I},\Upsilon_{I})
on
M
, where
\Upsilon_{I}:=\frac{1}{n!}(\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K})^{n}
.

A
hyperkähler structure
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-structure satisfying
\nabla I=\nabla J=\nabla K=0
. It is well-known that if
(g,(I,J,K))
is hyperkähler, then
\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(g)\leq\mathrm{Sp}(n)
, and hence
g
is Ricci-flat. Conversely, every Riemannian metric with holonomy contained in
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
arises from a hyperkähler structure.

3.

Let
M^{7}
be a
7
-manifold. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
on
M
is equivalent to a
3
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
such that at each

, there exists a coframe
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{7})\colon T_{x}M\to\mathbb{R}^{7}
for which

\left.\varphi\right|_{x}=e^{123}+e^{145}+e^{167}+e^{246}-e^{257}-e^{347}-e^{356}

where

. Every
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
induces a canonical Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
on
M
. It is well-known that if
\nabla\varphi=0
, then
\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(g_{\varphi})\leq\mathrm{G}_{2}
, and hence
g_{\varphi}
is Ricci-flat. Conversely, every Riemannian metric with holonomy contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
arises in this way.

4.

Let
M^{8}
be an
8
-manifold. A
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
on
M
is equivalent to a
4
-form
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
such that at each
x\in M
, there exists a coframe
(e^{1},\ldots,e^{8})\colon T_{x}M\to\mathbb{R}^{8}

\left.\Phi\right|_{x}=e^{1234}+(e^{12}+e^{34})\wedge(e^{56}+e^{78})+(e^{13}-e^{24})\wedge(e^{57}-e^{68})-(e^{14}+e^{23})\wedge(e^{58}+e^{67})+e^{5678}

and similarly for

. Every
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi
induces a canonical Riemannian metric

on

\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(g_{\Phi})\leq\mathrm{Spin}(7)

2.3
H
-Instantons

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold equipped with an
H
-structure, where
H\leq\mathrm{SO}(n)
is a closed subgroup. Letting
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)
denote the Lie algebra of
H
, we may orthogonally split
\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}\cong\mathfrak{so}(n)=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
with respect to the Killing form. Accordingly, the bundle of
2
-forms on
M
splits as

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}M)\cong\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}}

Let
E\to M
be a vector bundle with structure group
G
, where
G
is a compact Lie group, and let
\mathrm{ad}_{E}\to M
denote the adjoint bundle. Then the bundle of
\mathrm{ad}_{E}
-valued
2
-forms decomposes as

\displaystyle\Lambda(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\cong\left(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\right)\oplus(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}).

We let
\pi_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}}
denote the corresponding projection maps from
\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}M)\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}
onto its respective components. The following definition is due to Reyes Carrión
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A connection
A
on the vector bundle
E\to M
is called an
H
-instanton
if its curvature
2
-form
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(M;\mathrm{ad}_{E})
takes values in
\mathfrak{h}
, in the sense that:

\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(F_{A})=F_{A}.

An equivalent condition is
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}}(F_{A})=0
.

We emphasize that this condition is linear. More precisely, if
A
is a connection on the trivial complex line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to M
, then the
H
-instanton equation is a linear condition on
F_{A}
, and is therefore a first-order linear PDE for
A
.

2.3.1
Hermitian Yang-Mills Connections

Let
M^{2n}
be a
2n
-manifold with a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structure
(g,J,\omega)
, and let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle of complex rank
r
. By the above discussion, a connection
A
on
E\to M
is a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton
provided that any of the following equivalent conditions holds:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{u}(n)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \iff\ F_{A}\in[\Lambda^{1,1}]\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}.

Extending
A
by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity (and denoting this extension by
A
), the
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton condition becomes:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{1,1}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \iff\ F_{A}^{0,2}=0.

Note that if
A
is a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton, then

F_{A}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\langle F_{A},\omega\rangle\,\omega^{n},

where
\langle F_{A},\omega\rangle\in\Omega^{0}(M;\mathfrak{u}(r))
is a matrix-valued function. A
Hermitian Yang-Mills (HYM) connection
is a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton
A
satisfying
\langle F_{A},\omega\rangle=\lambda_{E}\,\mathrm{Id}
for some constant
\lambda_{E}\in\mathbb{C}
. In this case, if
M
is a compact Kähler manifold, then it is well-known that

\lambda_{E}=-\frac{2\pi i}{n!\,\mathrm{vol}(M)}\frac{\deg(E)}{r}.

The HYM connections with
\lambda_{E}=0
are called
primitive HYM connections
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
E\to M^{2n}
be a Hermitian vector bundle, and suppose
M
is Kähler. In this setting, there is a well-known relationship between
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instantons and holomorphic structures. Indeed, if
E
has a holomorphic structure, then there exists a unique
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton
A
(the
Chern connection
) compatible with the holomorphic structure. Conversely, if
A
is a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-instanton on
E
, then by the Koszul-Malgrange Theorem,
E
admits a holomorphic structure compatible with
A
.

2.3.2
Examples of
H
-instantons

We will primarily be concerned with
H
-instantons where
H
is a Ricci-flat holonomy group on Berger’s list (
2.5
). As we now recall, in each of these cases, the
H
-instanton equation is equivalent to the
\Omega
-ASD equation for a suitable choice of
\Omega\in\Omega^{n-4}(M)
.

1.

(
H=\mathrm{SU}(n)
) Let
M^{2n}
be an oriented
2n
-manifold with an
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-structure
(g,J,\omega,\Upsilon)
, and let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle. It is well-known that the
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-instanton equation can be rephrased as follows

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{su}(n)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \iff\ F_{A}\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}]\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}

This is equivalent to requiring that the
\mathbb{C}
-linear extension of
A
(which we continue to call
A
) satisfies:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \iff\ \begin{cases}F_{A}^{0,2}=0\\
F_{A}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0.\end{cases}\ \iff\ \ast F_{A}=-\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\omega^{n-2}\wedge F_{A}.

In particular, (
\mathbb{C}
-linear extensions of)
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-instantons are precisely the
primitive
Hermitian Yang-Mills connections, which are in turn precisely the
\Omega
-ASD connections for
\Omega=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\omega^{n-2}\in\Omega^{2n-4}(M)
.

2.

(
H=\mathrm{Sp}(n)
) Let
M^{4n}
be an oriented
4n
-manifold with an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-structure
(g,(I,J,K))
, and let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle. Then the
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation can be rephrased as follows:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \iff\ F_{A}\in\left([\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,K}]\right)\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E},

where
\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,L}\to M
denotes the bundle of
2
-forms that are type
(1,1)
and primitive with respect to
L\in\{I,J,K\}
.

Thus, a connection
A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton if and only if (its
\mathbb{C}
-linear extension) is
hyper-holomorphic
, meaning that
A
is primitive HYM with respect to
I,J,K
simultaneously. In particular, hyper-holomorphic connections are those that are simultaneously
\Omega_{I}
-,
\Omega_{J}
-, and
\Omega_{K}
-ASD, where
\Omega_{L}=\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\omega_{L}^{2n-2}\in\Omega^{4n-4}(M)
for
L\in\{I,J,K\}
. We will explore these further in
\lx@sectionsign
3
.

3.

(
H=\mathrm{G}_{2}
) Let
M^{7}
be an oriented
7
-manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, and let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle. It is well-known that the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instanton equation can be rephrased as follows:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \ \iff\ \ F_{A}\wedge\ast\varphi=0\ \ \iff\ \ \ast F_{A}=-\varphi\wedge F_{A}.

In particular,
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-instantons are
\Omega
-ASD for the choice
\Omega=\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
.

4.

(
H=\mathrm{Spin}(7)
) Let
M^{8}
be an oriented
8
-manifold with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
, and let
E\to M
be a Hermitian vector bundle. It is well-known that the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instanton equation can be rephrased as follows:

F_{A}\in\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{spin}(7)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}\ \ \iff\ \ \ast F_{A}=-\Phi\wedge F_{A}.

In particular,
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-instantons are
\Omega
-ASD for the choice
\Omega=\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
.

Remark 2.5

(Representation dependence)
.

Despite the terminology, the notions of
H
-structure and
H
-instanton depend not only on the abstract Lie group
H
, but
also
on the representation
H\leq\mathrm{SO}(n)
.

Remark 2.6

(Generalization)
.

The notion of
H
-instanton actually makes sense for a more general class of geometric structures. Indeed, let
H\leq\mathrm{SO}(n)
be a closed subgroup, let
N(H)\leq\mathrm{SO}(n)
denote its normalizer in
\mathrm{SO}(n)
, and assume that
N(H)
is connected. Note that
N(H)
acts on
\mathfrak{so}(n)
via the adjoint action, and it can be shown
[
40
]
that this
N(H)
-action preserves the subspace
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{so}(n)
. Consequently, “
H
-instanton” is a well-defined concept on manifolds equipped with simply an
N(H)
-structure. This generalization is relevant to several contexts:

1.

If
H=\mathrm{SU}(2)\leq\mathrm{SO}(4)
, then
N(H)=\mathrm{SO}(4)
, so the notion of an
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-instanton makes sense on any oriented Riemannian
4
-manifold. In fact, it coincides with the classical SD or ASD equation, depending on the conjugacy class of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
in
\mathrm{SO}(4)
(see Remark
2.5
).

2.

If
H=\mathrm{SU}(n)\leq\mathrm{SO}(2n)
with
n\geq 3
, then
N(H)=\mathrm{U}(n)
, so the notion of an
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-instanton makes sense on any
2n
-manifold (for
2n\geq 6
) with a
\mathrm{U}(n)
-structure.

3.

If
H=\mathrm{Sp}(n)\leq\mathrm{SO}(4n)
, then
N(H)=\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
, so the notion of an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton makes sense on any
4n
-manifold with a
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure. We will return to this point in
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
.

2.4
The Real Fourier-Mukai Transform

Let
X
denote a Calabi-Yau,
\mathrm{G}_{2}
, or
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-manifold. Roughly speaking, mirror symmetry predicts the existence of a geometric functor relating the calibrated geometry and gauge theory of
X
to that of its “mirror”
X^{*}
. When
(X,X^{*})
is a mirror pair of Calabi-Yau manifolds that satisfies certain conditions, Leung–Yau–Zaslow
[
31
]
proposed such a functor, known as the
real Fourier-Mukai (RFM) transform
. Later, Lee–Leung
[
30
]
considered the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
and
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
analogues of the RFM transform, the latter of which was revisited by Kawai–Yamamoto
[
29
]
. Here, we will review this transform in the simplest idealized setting. Our discussion closely follows
[
29
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
. The reader desiring more details might consult
[
29
]
as well as
[
14
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.2.1]
,
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
.

Let
T^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{m}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{m})
denote the
m
-torus, and let
(T^{m})^{*}=(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{*}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{m})^{*}
denote its dual torus. Every point
p\in T^{m}
yields a flat connection
\nabla^{p}
on the trivial Hermitian line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to(T^{m})^{*}
in the following way. First, each point
p=(p^{1},\ldots,p^{m})\in T^{m}
can be identified with the homomorphism

\mu_{p}=e^{-i\langle\cdot,p\rangle}\colon((2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{m})^{*}\to\mathrm{U}(1),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is the natural dual pairing. Identifying
((2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{m})^{*}\cong\pi_{1}((T^{m})^{*})
, we can view
\mu_{p}\colon\pi_{1}((T^{m})^{*})\to\mathrm{U}(1)
as a unitary representation of the fundamental group of
(T^{m})^{*}
. From this representation and the principal
\pi_{1}((T^{m})^{*})
-bundle
(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{*}\to(T^{m})^{*}
, we obtain an associated Hermitian line bundle over
(T^{m})^{*}
, namely

E_{p}:=(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{*}\times_{\mu_{p}}\mathbb{C}\to(T^{m})^{*},

along with a flat Hermitian connection
\widetilde{\nabla}^{p}
on
E_{p}
. It turns out that
E_{p}
admits a non-vanishing global section, so that
E_{p}
is trivializable, and hence there is a bundle isomorphism
E_{p}\cong\underline{\mathbb{C}}
. Via this isomorphism, we obtain a flat connection called
\nabla^{p}
on
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to(T^{m})^{*}
, as desired. In fact, letting
(y^{1},\ldots,y^{m})
denote the standard coordinates on
(T^{m})^{*}
, we have

\nabla^{p}=d+i\sum_{a=1}^{m}p^{a}dy^{a}.

Next, we shift our perspective and view
T^{m}
as a fiber of the trivial bundle
X:=B\times T^{m}\to B
, where here
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is an open set with coordinates
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{k})
. Notice that every smooth function
h\colon B\to T^{m}
provides a section
(\mathrm{Id},h)\colon B\to B\times T^{m}
whose image is the graph of
h
. On the other hand, by the above discussion, the family of points
\{h(x)\in T^{m}\colon x\in B\}
yields a family of connections
\nabla^{h(x)}
, which altogether produces a connection
\nabla^{h}
on the trivial line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to X^{*}:=B\times(T^{m})^{*}
.

In summary, to every smooth function
h\colon B^{k}\to T^{m}
, we may associate two objects:

1.

A submanifold
S_{h}\subset X
given by the graph of
h
:

S_{h}:=\text{Graph}(h)=\{(x,h(x))\colon x\in B\}\subset B^{k}\times T^{m}

2.

A connection
\nabla^{h}
on the trivial complex line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to X^{*}:=B\times(T^{m})^{*}
given by:

\nabla^{h}:=d+i\sum_{a=1}^{m}h^{a}dy^{a}.

We refer to
\nabla^{h}
as the
real Fourier-Mukai (RFM) transform
of
S_{h}
.

2.4.1
Examples

The real Fourier-Mukai transform provides a correspondence between graphs
S_{h}\subset X
and connections
\nabla^{h}
on
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to X^{*}
. In particular, requiring that
S_{h}
satisfy a geometric PDE is equivalent to requiring that
\nabla^{h}
satisfy a “mirror” geometric PDE.

For example, suppose that
X^{k+m}=B^{k}\times T^{m}
is equipped with a product
H
-structure in such a way that
B\times\{\text{pt}\}\subset X
is a
\phi
-calibrated submanifold for a suitable calibration
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. Then the condition that
S_{h}\subset X
be
\phi
-calibrated is equivalent to requiring that
\nabla^{h}
satisfy a so-called “deformed
H
-instanton equation.” In general, the latter is a nonlinear first-order PDE system given by a polynomial condition (of degree at most
k
) on the curvature of
\nabla^{h}
.

For illustration, we now recall some examples of this correspondence in which
H\leq\mathrm{SO}(k+m)
is a Ricci-flat holonomy group on Berger’s list (
2.5
).

1.

(
H=\mathrm{SU}(n)
) Let

\displaystyle(F_{A})^{0,2}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}((\omega+F_{A})^{n})

\displaystyle=0.

2.

(
H=\mathrm{Sp}(n)
) Let
(k,m)=(2n,2n)
, and equip

with a product hyperkähler structure
(g,(I,J,K))
such that
B\times\{\text{pt}\}\subset X
is complex Lagrangian with respect to

h\colon B\to T^{2n}
be a smooth function. Then we may consider a

\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation

S_{h}\text{ is complex Lagrangian w.r.t. }I\iff\nabla^{h}\text{ is a deformed }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton.}

In Theorem

3.

h\colon B\to T^{4}
be a smooth function. Then according to
[

]
,
[

is

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{1}{6}F_{A}^{3}+F_{A}\wedge\ast\varphi=0.

If one instead takes

4.

(

\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(X)

B\times\{\text{pt}\}\subset X

[

3.1
\mathrm{Sp}(n)

3.1.1
Linear Algebra

A
quaternionic-Hermitian vector space
is a
4n
-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V
equipped with a pair
(g,\mathcal{F})
, where
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a positive-definite inner product, and
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathrm{End}(V)
is a
3
-dimensional subspace that admits a basis
\{I,J,K\}
of
g
-orthogonal complex structures satisfying the quaternionic relations
IJ=K
, etc. Such a basis is called
admissible
. For each
k\geq 1
, the
fundamental
4k
-forms
are

\textstyle\Pi_{k}:=\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}(\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{J}^{2}+\omega_{K}^{2})^{k}\in\Lambda^{4k}(V^{*}).

Each
\Pi_{k}
is well-defined (independent of the admissible basis) and has co-mass one.

It is well-known that the space of real
2
-forms
\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})
decomposes into three
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-irreducible submodules, say

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})=U\oplus W\oplus Y,

where
W\cong\mathfrak{sp}(n)
and
Y\cong\mathfrak{sp}(1)
. In particular,

\displaystyle\dim(U)

\displaystyle=6n^{2}-3n-3

\displaystyle\dim(W)

\displaystyle=2n^{2}+n

\displaystyle\dim(Y)

\displaystyle=3.

To be more explicit, let
\{I,J,K\}
be an admissible basis of
V
, let
\{I^{*},J^{*},V^{*}\}
be the dual basis of
V^{*}
, and let
\omega_{L}=g(L\cdot,\cdot)\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})
for
L\in\{I,J,K\}
. Note that the
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-invariant endomorphism
T\in\mathrm{End}(\Lambda^{2}(V^{*}))
given by
T=I^{*}\otimes I^{*}+J^{*}\otimes J^{*}+K^{*}\otimes K^{*}
is independent of the choice of basis. Then according to
[
7
]
, the map
T
has eigenvalues
3
and
-1
, and moreover

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}]=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon T\beta=3\beta\},

\displaystyle U\oplus Y

\displaystyle=[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}]\!]+[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]+[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{K}]\!]=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon T\beta=-\beta\}\!,

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}),

where
\Lambda^{p,q}_{L}\subset\Lambda^{p+q}(V^{*};\mathbb{C})
denotes the
(p,q)
-forms on
V
with respect to
L\in\{I,J,K\}
. Alternatively, according to
[
19
, Theorem 2.2]
, one has

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon\ast\!\beta=3\,\beta\wedge\Pi_{n-1}\right\}

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon\ast\!\beta=-\beta\wedge\Pi_{n-1}\right\}

(3.1)

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\textstyle\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon\ast\!\beta=\frac{3}{2n+1}\,\beta\wedge\Pi_{n-1}\right\}\!.

3.1.2
Quaternionic-Kähler Manifolds

Let
Q^{4n}
be an oriented
4n
-manifold. An
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure
on
Q
is equivalent to a pair
(g_{Q},\mathcal{F})
consisting of a Riemannian metric
g_{Q}
and a rank
3
subbundle
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathrm{End}(TQ)
such that:

1.

At each
q\in Q
, there exists a local frame
(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})
of
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the quaternionic relations
j_{1}j_{2}=j_{3}
and
j_{1}^{2}=j_{2}^{2}=j_{3}^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
.

2.

Every
j\in\mathcal{F}
acts by isometries:
g_{Q}(jX,jY)=g_{Q}(X,Y)
for all
X,Y\in TQ
.

Equivalently, an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure may be defined as a
4
-form
\Pi\in\Omega^{4}(Q)
such that at each
q\in Q
, there exists a coframe
u\colon T_{q}Q\to\mathbb{R}^{4n}
in which
\Pi|_{q}=\frac{1}{6}u^{*}(\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}+\omega_{3}^{2})
, where here
\{\omega_{I},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\}
is the standard hyperkähler triple on
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
.

For
n\geq 2
, a
quaternionic-Kähler structure
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-structure
(g_{Q},\mathcal{F})
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathrm{End}(TQ)
is a
\nabla
-parallel subbundle, where
\nabla
is the connection induced by
g_{Q}
. An equivalent condition is that the
4
-form
\Pi\in\Omega^{4}(Q)
is
g_{Q}
-parallel. For
n=1
, we say that
(Q^{4},g_{Q})
is
quaternionic-Kähler
provided that

3.2.1
Linear Algebra

A
hyper-Hermitian vector space
is a
4n
-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V
equipped with a pair
(g,(I,J,K))
, where
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a positive-definite inner product, and
I,J,K\in\mathrm{End}(V)
are
g
-orthogonal complex structures that satisfy the quaternionic relations
IJ=K
, etc. In other words, a hyper-Hermitian vector space is a quaternionic-Hermitian vector space together with a choice of admissible basis. As such, there is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-irreducible splitting

\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})=U\oplus W\oplus Y

(3.2)

as discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
3.1.1
. We aim to refine this decomposition further into
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-irreducible pieces.

For
L\in\{I,J,K\}
, define a non-degenerate
2
-form
\omega_{L}\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})
by
\omega_{L}=g(L\cdot,\cdot)
. Next, letting
\Lambda^{p,q}_{L}\subset\Lambda^{p+q}(V;\mathbb{C})
denote the space of
(p,q)
-forms with respect to
L
, and letting
\Lambda^{p,p}_{0,L}\subset\Lambda^{2p}(V^{*};\mathbb{C})
denote the primitive
(p,p)
-forms, we have a
\mathrm{U}(2n)
-irreducible decomposition

\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})=[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{L}]\!]\oplus[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,L}]\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{L}.

Concretely,

\displaystyle[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{L}]\!]

\displaystyle=\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon\beta(LX,LY)=-\beta(X,Y)\right\}

\displaystyle\dim[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{L}]\!]

\displaystyle=4n^{2}-2n

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{1,1}_{L}]

\displaystyle=\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\colon\beta(LX,LY)=\beta(X,Y)\right\}

\displaystyle\dim[\Lambda^{1,1}_{L}]

\displaystyle=4n^{2}.

Note that there are relationships between the various subspaces
[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{L}]\!]
and
[\Lambda^{1,1}_{L}]
for
L\in\{I,J,K\}
. For example, it is easy to check that
\omega_{K}\in[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}]\!]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]
, and in fact

\displaystyle[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}]\!]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]

\displaystyle=[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{K}]\!]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}],

and similarly for cyclic permutations of
(I,J,K)
. Moreover, one can check that

[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}]=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I,0}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J,0}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K,0}].

(3.3)

To prove the last equality, let
\beta\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}]
, and write
\beta=\gamma+r\omega_{I}
for some
\gamma\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I,0}]
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\beta\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]
and
r\omega_{I}\in[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]
, we have
r=\langle\beta,r\omega_{I}\rangle=0
, so that
\beta\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I,0}]
.

Now, using the hyper-Hermitian structure, we can refine (
3.2
) even further.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a hyper-Hermitian vector space. Then there is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-irreducible decomposition

\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})\cong U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus U_{K}\oplus W\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{I}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{J}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{K},

where

\displaystyle U_{I}

\displaystyle=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,I}]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{K}]\!]

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,K}].

\displaystyle U_{J}

\displaystyle=[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}]\!]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,J}]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{K}]\!]

\displaystyle U_{K}

\displaystyle=[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}]\!]\cap[\![\Lambda^{2,0}_{J}]\!]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,K}]

Moreover,

\displaystyle\dim(U_{I})=\dim(U_{J})=\dim(U_{K})

\displaystyle=2n^{2}-n-1,

\displaystyle\dim(W)

\displaystyle=2n^{2}+n.

Proof.

First, note that every pairwise intersection among
\{U_{I},U_{J},U_{K},W\}
is equal to
\{0\}
, and so
U_{I}+U_{J}+U_{K}+W=U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus U_{K}\oplus W
. Next, set
Y:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})
, and let
Y^{\perp}\subset\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})
be its orthogonal complement. It is clear that if
\beta\in U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus W
, then
\beta\in Y^{\perp}
. Conversely, every
\beta\in Y^{\perp}
can be written

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\beta_{I}+\beta_{J}+\beta_{K}+\beta_{W},

where

\displaystyle\beta_{I}

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}(\beta+I^{*}\beta-J^{*}\beta-K^{*}\beta)

\displaystyle\beta_{W}

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}(\beta+I^{*}\beta+J^{*}\beta+K^{*}\beta).

\displaystyle\beta_{J}

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}(\beta-I^{*}\beta+J^{*}\beta-K^{*}\beta)

\displaystyle\beta_{K}

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}(\beta-I^{*}\beta-J^{*}\beta+K^{*}\beta)

It is straightforward to check that
\beta_{L}\in U_{L}
for
L\in\{I,J,K\}
and
\beta_{W}\in W
. This proves that
U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus U_{K}\oplus W=Y^{\perp}
.

We now count dimensions. Since
W=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}]=\mathrm{u}(V,I)\cap\mathrm{u}(V,J)\cap\mathrm{u}(V,K)
, where
\mathrm{u}(V,L)
denotes the Lie algebra of the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(V,L)
, we see that
W\cong\mathfrak{sp}(n)
, and hence
\dim(W)=\binom{2n+1}{2}=2n^{2}+n
. Now, since
\dim(U_{I})=\dim(U_{J})=\dim(U_{K})
for symmetry reasons, we find that

\textstyle 8n^{2}-2n=\binom{4n}{2}=\dim[\Lambda^{2}(V^{*})]=3\dim(U_{I})+\dim(W)+\dim(Y).

Substituting
\dim(W)=2n^{2}+n
and
\dim(Y)=3
yields
\dim(U_{I})=2n^{2}-n-1
.

Finally, to see that
U_{I},U_{J},U_{K}
are
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-irreducible, we argue as follows. Let
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2n}=\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the standard complex
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-representation given by left-multiplication by quaternionic matrices, and let
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{H}
be the complex
\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-representation given by right-multiplication by unit quaternions. Then it is well-known that there is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)\mathrm{Sp}(1)
-equivariant isomorphism
U\cong[\Lambda^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathcal{H})]
, from which we obtain
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-equivariant isomorphisms
U_{I}\cong U_{J}\cong U_{K}\cong[\Lambda^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{E})]
. Since
[\Lambda^{2}_{0}(\mathcal{E})]
is
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-irreducible, this establishes the claim.
∎

The following algebraic fact is useful when studying of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons on complex line bundles. To set notation, for a
2
-form
F\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*};\mathbb{C})
, we let
F^{p,q}_{L}
denote its type
(p,q)
-component with respect to
L\in\{I,J,K\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F\in\Lambda^{2}(V^{*};i\mathbb{R})
be an
i\mathbb{R}
-valued
2
-form. The following are equivalent:

1.

F^{0,2}_{J}=0
and
F^{0,2}_{K}=0
.

2.

F^{0,2}_{I}=0
and
F^{0,2}_{J}=0
and
F^{0,2}_{K}=0
.

3.

F
satisfies the following four equations:

\displaystyle F^{0,2}_{J}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F^{0,2}_{K}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F\wedge\omega_{K}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

4.

F
satisfies the following six equations:

\displaystyle F^{0,2}_{I}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F^{0,2}_{J}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F^{0,2}_{K}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F\wedge\omega_{I}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F\wedge\omega_{K}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0.

Proof.

It is clear that (iv)
\implies
(iii)
\implies
(i), and that (iv)
\implies
(ii)
\implies
(i). Thus, it remains to prove (i)
\implies
(iv). Suppose that
F
satisfies (i). Since
F
is
i\mathbb{R}
-valued, we have
F^{2,0}_{J}=-\overline{F^{0,2}_{J}}=0
and
F^{2,0}_{K}=-\overline{F^{0,2}_{K}}=0
, so that
F\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}\cap\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}
. The result now follows from (
3.3
).
∎

3.2.2
Hyperkähler Manifolds

Let
X^{4n}
be a hyperähler manifold. Since each tangent space
T_{x}X
has a natural hyper-Hermitian structure, Proposition
3.2
implies that the bundle of
2
-forms on
X
admits an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-invariant decomposition

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)\cong U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus U_{K}\oplus W\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{I}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{J}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{K},

where now
W=[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,I}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0,K}]
, along with
U_{I},U_{J},U_{K}
, etc., refer to vector bundles (rather than vector spaces).

Now, let
E\to X
be a Hermitian vector bundle. An
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton
is a connection
A
on
E
satisfying either of the following equivalent conditions:

F_{A}\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}_{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})\ \iff\ F_{A}\in\Gamma(W\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E}).

More explicitly, the linear algebraic discussion above provides the following characterizations:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A
be a connection on a Hermitian vector bundle
E\to X
. The following are equivalent:

1.

A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton (i.e.,
F_{A}\in\Gamma(W\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{E})
).

2.

A
is
\Omega
-ASD for
\Omega=\frac{1}{(2n-1)!}(\omega_{I}^{2}+\omega_{J}^{2}+\omega_{K}^{2})^{n-1}
.

3.

A
is simultaneously
\Omega_{I}
-,
\Omega_{J}
-, and
\Omega_{K}
-ASD, where
\Omega_{L}=\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\omega_{L}^{2n-2}\in\Omega^{4n-4}(X)
.

4.

A
is a primitive HYM connection with respect to
I
,
J
, and
K
:

\displaystyle(F_{A})^{0,2}_{I}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(F_{A})^{0,2}_{J}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle(F_{A})^{0,2}_{K}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F_{A}\wedge\omega_{I}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F_{A}\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle F_{A}\wedge\omega_{K}^{2n-1}

\displaystyle=0.

Moreover, if
E
is a Hermitian line bundle (i.e., if
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)=1
), then the above conditions are equivalent to:

5.

(F_{A})^{0,2}_{J}=0
and
(F_{A})^{0,2}_{K}=0
.

3.2.3
Remark on Low Dimensions

In low dimensions,
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons on hyperkähler manifolds have relationships with other natural gauge-theoretic objects. Indeed:

Example 3.5

(Dimension
4
)
.

Let
X^{4}
be a hyperkähler
4
-manifold, let
E\to X
be a Hermitian vector bundle, and let
A
be a connection on
E
. Then

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(1)\text{-instanton}\ \iff\ A\text{ is primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills}\ \iff\ A\text{ is ASD.}

In this way,
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons are seen to be natural generalizations of the classical ASD equations to
4n
dimensions.

Example 3.6

(Dimension
8
)
.

Let
X^{8}
be a hyperkähler
8
-manifold, let
E\to X
be a Hermitian vector bundle, and let
A
be a connection on
E
. The hyperkähler structure on
X
induces a torsion-free
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(X)
. Then:

A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(2)\text{-instanton}\,\implies\,A\text{ is primitive Hermitian Yang-Mills}\,\implies\,A\text{ is a }\mathrm{Spin}(7)\text{-instanton.}

If
X
is compact and
E\to X
admits at least one
\mathrm{Sp}(2)
-instanton, then both converses hold: see Corollary
7.2
.

3.2.4
Remark on Bubbling Loci

We now briefly consider the bubbling of
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons on hyperkähler manifolds. In general, the bubbling loci of sequences of
\Omega
-ASD are
\Omega
-calibrated. The algebraic reason for this is the following fact, whose proof can be found in
[
16
, Prop 2.88]
:

Proposition 3.7
.

Equip
(\mathbb{R}^{4n},g_{0})
with the flat metric, where
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathbb{R}^{4n}=V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
be an orthogonal decomposition, and let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{4n}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be the corresponding projection. Let
E\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a principal
G
-bundle, where
G
is a compact Lie group, and let
A
be a non-flat connection on
E
. Diagrammatically:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{4n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}^{4}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Let
\Omega\in\Lambda^{4n-4}(\mathbb{R}^{4n})^{*}
be a calibration. Then:

\pi^{*}A\text{ is an }\Omega\text{-ASD instanton}\iff\begin{cases}A\text{ is an ASD instanton, and}\\
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{4n}\text{ is }\pm\!\Omega\text{-calibrated.}\\
\end{cases}

Let
A
be a non-flat connection on
E\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By the above proposition,
\pi^{*}A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton on
\pi^{*}E\to\mathbb{R}^{4n}
if and only if

is an ASD instanton and the
(4n-4)
-dimensional subspace

is simultaneously calibrated by
\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\omega_{I}^{2n-2},\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\omega_{J}^{2n-2}
, and

,

, and

Accordingly, on a hyperkähler manifold

, we expect that the bubbling locus of a sequence of

-dimensional submanifold that is

-,

-, and

]

,

4
Complex Lagrangians and Gauge Theory

In hyperkähler
4n
-manifolds
(X,g,(I,J,K))
, one of the most important classes of submanifolds is the
complex Lagrangians
,
2n
-dimensional submanifolds that are simultaneously complex with respect to
I
and Lagrangian with respect to both
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
. They have been studied extensively by algebraic geometers, often in the context of fibrations of complex symplectic manifolds. In this section, we make two observations relating complex Lagrangians in
X
to gauge theoretic objects on bundles over
X
.

First, in
\lx@sectionsign
4.1
, we prove Theorem
1.1
, which says that complex Lagrangian graphs correspond to
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instantons via the real Fourier-Mukai transform. Second, in
\lx@sectionsign
4.2
, we observe that when
X
is equipped with a distribution of complex Lagrangian
2n
-planes, there exists a simple first-order PDE system whose solutions are deformed HYM connections. Subsections
4.1
and
4.2
are independent from one another, and also from the rest of the paper.

4.1
The Real Fourier-Mukai Transform of Complex Lagrangians

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X^{4n},g,(I,J,K))
be a hyperkähler
4n
-manifold with symplectic forms
\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}
. Consider the following three conditions on a
2n
-dimensional submanifold
L\subset X
:

1.

L
is complex with respect to
I

2.

L
is Lagrangian with respect to
\omega_{J}

3.

L
is Lagrangian with respect to
\omega_{K}

If
L
satisfies any two of these conditions, then
L
also satisfies the third. In this case,
L
is called an
I
-complex Lagrangian
.

Remark 4.2
.

We remark that complex Lagrangian submanifolds are, in fact, special Lagrangians. Indeed, recall that
X
has complex volume forms
\Upsilon_{J}:=\frac{1}{n!}(\omega_{K}+i\omega_{I})^{n}
and
\Upsilon_{K}=\frac{1}{n!}(\omega_{I}+i\omega_{J})^{n}
. If
L\subset X
is an
I
-complex Lagrangian, then it is easy to check that
L
is calibrated by both
\mathrm{Re}((-i)^{n+1}\Upsilon_{J})
and
\mathrm{Re}(\Upsilon_{K})
. By definition, this means that
L
is
special Lagrangian
for
\Upsilon_{J}
with phase
i^{n+1}
, and also special Lagrangian for
\Upsilon_{K}
of phase
1
.

Recall from
\lx@sectionsign
2.4.1
that the the real Fourier-Mukai (RFM) transform of a special Lagrangian graph
S_{h}\subset B^{n}\times T^{n}
is a deformed HYM connection. By analogy, we will say that the RFM transform of a complex Lagrangian graph is a
deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton
. As we now prove, this deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation coincides with the usual
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be an open set, and equip
X=B\times T^{2n}
with a product hyperkähler structure
(g,(I,J,K))
such that
B\subset X
is a complex Lagrangian with respect to
I
. Let
h\colon B\to T^{2n}
be a smooth function, let
S_{h}\subset X
be its graph, and let
\nabla^{h}
be its real Fourier-Mukai transform on
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to B\times(T^{n})^{*}
. Then:

S_{h}\text{ is complex Lagrangian with respect to }I\ \iff\ \nabla^{h}\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n)\text{-instanton.}

This result is surprising for two reasons. First, it stands in stark contrast to the situation for the other Ricci-flat holonomy groups (
H=\mathrm{SU}(n),\mathrm{G}_{2},\mathrm{Spin}(7)
). In each of those cases, the deformed
H
-instanton equation is more complicated than the ordinary
H
-instanton equation. Second, since the complex Lagrangian condition is equivalent to the vanishing of the
2
-forms
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
, one expects that the deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation would be quadratic in the curvature of
\nabla
. Despite this, Theorem
4.3
shows that the equation is
linear
in the curvature.

4.1.1
Setup

We now prove Theorem
4.3
. To begin, let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be an open set, and let
T^{2n}
be a
2n
-torus. We introduce:

\displaystyle\text{coordinates }(z^{1},\ldots,z^{n})

\displaystyle=(x^{1}+iy^{1},\ldots,x^{n}+iy^{n})\text{ on }B^{2n}

\displaystyle\text{coordinates }(w^{1},\ldots,w^{n})

\displaystyle=(u^{1}+iv^{1},\ldots,u^{n}+iv^{n})\text{ on }T^{2n}

We use index ranges
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq a,b\leq n
. Let us equip the product
X^{4n}=B\times T^{2n}
with the flat metric
g=\sum(dx^{a})^{2}+(dy^{a})^{2}+(du^{a})^{2}+(dv^{a})^{2}
and complex structures
I,J,K
defined by the following requirements:

\displaystyle I\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}

\displaystyle J\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{a}}

\displaystyle K\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{a}}

\displaystyle I\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{a}}

\displaystyle J\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}

\displaystyle K\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{a}}

One can check that
IJ=K
. Finally, we note that the induced Kähler forms
\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}
, defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{I}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g(IX,Y)

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g(JX,Y)

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=g(KX,Y)

are given in coordinates by

\displaystyle\omega_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum dx^{a}\wedge dy^{a}+du^{a}\wedge dv^{a},

\displaystyle\omega_{J}

\displaystyle=\sum dx^{a}\wedge du^{a}+dv^{a}\wedge dy^{a},

\displaystyle\omega_{K}

\displaystyle=\sum dx^{a}\wedge dv^{a}+dy^{a}\wedge du^{a}.

In particular,
\omega_{J}
and
\omega_{K}
contain no terms of the form
du^{a}\wedge dv^{b}
. As we are about to demonstrate, the absence of such terms is the reason why the “deformed
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton equation” is linear in the first derivatives (rather than quadratic).

4.1.2
The graph
S_{h}

Let
h\colon B\to T^{2n}
be a smooth function, say
h=(f^{1},\ldots f^{n},g^{1},\ldots,g^{n})
. We denote the graph of
h
by

\displaystyle S_{h}

\displaystyle=\{(z,h(z))\colon z\in B\}=\{(x,y,f^{a}(x,y),g^{a}(x,y))\colon(x,y)\in B\}\subset B\times T^{2n}.

For ease of notation, we define functions

\displaystyle A_{aj}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f^{a}}{\partial x^{j}}

\displaystyle B_{aj}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f^{a}}{\partial y^{j}}

\displaystyle C_{aj}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial g^{a}}{\partial x^{j}}

\displaystyle G_{aj}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial g^{a}}{\partial y^{j}}.

Then the tangent space to
S_{h}
is spanned by the
2n
vectors
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
given by

\displaystyle X_{j}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}+\sum_{a}A_{aj}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{a}}+\sum_{a}C_{aj}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{a}},

\displaystyle Y_{j}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}+\sum_{a}B_{aj}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{a}}+\sum_{a}G_{aj}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{a}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle dx^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ai}

\displaystyle dy^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle du^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=A_{ai}

\displaystyle dv^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=C_{ai}

\displaystyle dx^{a}(Y_{i})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle dy^{a}(Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ai}

\displaystyle du^{a}(Y_{i})

\displaystyle=B_{ai}

\displaystyle dv^{a}(Y_{i})

\displaystyle=G_{ai}.

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},X_{j})

\displaystyle=A_{ij}-A_{ji}

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(X_{i},X_{j})

\displaystyle=C_{ij}-C_{ji}

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(Y_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=G_{ji}-G_{ij}

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(Y_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=B_{ij}-B_{ji}

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=B_{ij}+C_{ji}

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=G_{ij}-A_{ji}.

Proof.

Omitting the summation sign, we compute

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},X_{j})

\displaystyle=dx^{a}(X_{i})du^{a}(X_{j})-dx^{a}(X_{j})du^{a}(X_{i})+dv^{a}(X_{i})dy^{a}(X_{j})-dv^{a}(X_{i})dy^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ai}A_{aj}-\delta_{aj}A_{ai}+C_{ai}\cdot 0-C_{aj}\cdot 0

\displaystyle=A_{ij}-A_{ji}

and

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(Y_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=dx^{a}(Y_{i})du^{a}(Y_{j})-dx^{a}(Y_{j})du^{a}(Y_{i})+dv^{a}(Y_{i})dy^{a}(Y_{j})-dv^{a}(Y_{j})dy^{a}(Y_{i})

\displaystyle=0\cdot B_{aj}-0\cdot B_{ai}+G_{ai}\delta_{aj}-G_{aj}\delta_{ai}

\displaystyle=G_{ji}-G_{ij}

and

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=dx^{a}(X_{i})du^{a}(Y_{j})-dx^{a}(Y_{j})du^{a}(X_{i})+dv^{a}(X_{i})dy^{a}(Y_{j})-dv^{a}(Y_{j})dy^{a}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ai}B_{aj}-0\cdot A_{ai}+C_{ai}\delta_{aj}-G_{aj}\cdot 0

\displaystyle=B_{ij}+C_{ji}

Analogous computations yield the expressions for
\omega_{K}|_{S_{h}}
.
∎

4.1.3
The connection
\nabla^{h}

By the discussion in
\lx@sectionsign

(4.1)

We now decompose

(p,q)

Lemma 4.5

Let

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\left.\sum_{j}2A_{jj}\,d\zeta^{j}\wedge d\overline{\zeta}^{j}-\sum_{a<j}\left(A_{aj}+A_{ja}\right)\left(d\zeta^{a}\wedge d\overline{\zeta}^{j}+d\zeta^{j}\wedge d\overline{\zeta}^{a}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\left.\sum_{j}2G_{jj}\,d\eta^{j}\wedge d\overline{\eta}^{j}+\sum_{a<j}\left(G_{aj}+G_{ja}\right)\left(d\eta^{a}\wedge d\overline{\eta}^{j}+d\eta^{j}\wedge d\overline{\eta}^{a}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\sum_{a,j}B_{aj}\,dy^{j}\wedge du^{a}+C_{aj}\,dx^{j}\wedge dv^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left[\sum_{a,j}(B_{aj}+C_{ja})\left(d\zeta^{a}\wedge d\eta^{j}+d\overline{\zeta}^{a}\wedge d\overline{\eta}^{j}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{a,j}\left(B_{aj}-C_{ja}\right)\left(-d\zeta^{a}\wedge d\overline{\eta}^{j}+d\eta^{j}\wedge d\overline{\zeta}^{a}\right)\right]

4.1.4
Completing the proof of Theorem
4.3

The condition that
S_{h}
be complex Lagrangian with respect to
I
is equivalent to requiring that both
\left.\omega_{J}\right|_{S_{h}}=0
and
\left.\omega_{K}\right|_{S_{h}}=0
. This is equivalent to requiring that for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},X_{j})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(X_{i},X_{j})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(Y_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(Y_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\omega_{J}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle=0.

By Lemma
4.4
, this is equivalent to the first-order linear PDE system

\displaystyle A_{ij}-A_{ji}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle C_{ij}-C_{ji}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle G_{ji}-G_{ij}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle B_{ij}-B_{ji}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle B_{ij}+C_{ji}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle G_{ij}-A_{ji}

\displaystyle=0.

By Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
, we see that this system is equivalent to requiring that
F\in[\Lambda^{1,1}_{J}]\cap[\Lambda^{1,1}_{K}]
. Finally, by Lemma
3.3
, this last condition is equivalent to
\nabla^{h}
being an
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-instanton. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

4.2
Complex Lagrangian Distributions and dHYM Connections

Recall from
\lx@sectionsign
2.4.1
that a connection
A
on a Hermitian line bundle
L\to X
over a Calabi-Yau
2n
-manifold
(X^{2n},(g,\omega_{I},I,\Upsilon_{I}))
is a
deformed HYM connection
if it satisfies

\displaystyle(F_{A})^{0,2}

\displaystyle=0

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}((\omega_{I}+F_{A})^{n})

\displaystyle=0.

(4.3)

As the PDE system (
4.2
)-(
4.3
) is nonlinear, examples of dHYM connections are non-trivial to construct. In this subsection, we point out that when
X
is a
hyperkähler
manifold equipped with a complex Lagrangian distribution, there exists a considerably simpler PDE whose solutions are deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills.

So, let
(X,g,(I,J,K))
be a hyperkähler
4n
-manifold equipped with a distribution
\mathsf{B}\subset TX
of
I
-complex Lagrangian
2n
-planes. That is, we assume that
X
carries an orthogonal splitting
TX=\mathsf{B}\oplus\mathsf{F}
, where
\mathsf{B}\subset TX
is a rank
2n
-subbundle consisting of
I
-complex Lagrangian
2n
-planes, and
\mathsf{F}:=\mathsf{B}^{\perp}
. Notice that the data of
\mathsf{B}\subset TX
preferences a particular complex structure in the hyperkähler triple (namely
I
). In this context, the bundle of
2
-forms on
X
decomposes into two subbundles of “pure forms,” and one subbundle of “mixed forms”:

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)=\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\oplus(\mathsf{B}^{*}\otimes\mathsf{F}^{*})\oplus\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{F}^{*}).

We will say that a
2
-form
F\in\Omega^{2}(X)
is
mixed
if
F\in\Gamma(\mathsf{B}^{*}\otimes\mathsf{F}^{*})
. This condition arises naturally in connection with the RFM transform:

Example 4.7
.

Let
X=B\times T^{2n}
, where
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is an open subset, equip
X
with the (flat) product hyperkähler structure, and let
\mathsf{B}=TB
and
\mathsf{F}=T(T^{2n})
. Given a smooth function
h\colon B\to T^{2n}
, the RFM transformed connection
\nabla^{h}
on the trivial line bundle
\underline{\mathbb{C}}\to B\times(T^{2n})^{*}
has a curvature
2
-form
F_{h}
that is mixed.

We now observe:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
L\to X
be a Hermitian line bundle over a hyperkähler manifold
X
, and let
A
be a connection on
L
. Suppose that
X
is equipped with a distribution
\mathsf{B}\subset TX
of
I
-complex Lagrangian
2n
-planes.

If
A
is a
\mathrm{U}(2n)
-instanton (with respect to
I
) whose curvature
F_{A}
is mixed (w.r.t.
I
), then
A
is deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills (w.r.t.
I
).

Proof.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{U}(2n)
-instanton with respect to
I
whose curvature
2
-form
F_{A}
is mixed. Since
A
is a
\mathrm{U}(2n)
-instanton, we have
(F_{A})^{0,2}_{I}=0
, so it remains to verify (
4.3
). Since
F_{A}\in\Omega^{2}(X;\mathfrak{u}(1))\cong\Omega^{2}(X;i\mathbb{R})
, we may write
F_{A}=iF
for some real
2
-form
F\in\Omega^{2}(X)
. Abbreviating
\omega:=\omega_{I}
, we expand

(\omega+iF)^{2n}=\textstyle\binom{2n}{0}\omega^{2n}+\binom{2n}{1}\omega^{2n-1}\wedge iF+\cdots+\binom{2n}{2n-1}\omega\wedge i^{2n-1}F^{2n-1}+\binom{2n}{2n}i^{2n}F^{2n}

so that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}[(\omega+iF)^{2n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\textstyle(-1)^{k+1}\binom{2n}{2k-1}\,\omega^{2n-(2k-1)}\wedge F^{2k-1}.

Thus,
\mathrm{Im}[(\omega+iF)^{2n}]
is a linear combination of
(p+q)
-forms
\omega^{p}\wedge F^{q}
, where
p,q\geq 1
are odd integers with
p+q=2n
. Now, since
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\oplus\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{F}^{*})
, we have

\omega^{p}\in\Lambda^{2p}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\oplus\left(\Lambda^{2p-2}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\otimes\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{F}^{*})\right)\oplus\cdots\oplus\left(\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\otimes\Lambda^{2p-2}(\mathsf{F}^{*})\right)\oplus\Lambda^{2p}(\mathsf{F}^{*}).

On the other hand, since
F_{A}
is mixed, we have
F^{q}\in\Lambda^{q}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\otimes\Lambda^{q}(\mathsf{F}^{*})
, and hence

\ast(F^{q})\in\Lambda^{2n-q}(\mathsf{B}^{*})\otimes\Lambda^{2n-q}(\mathsf{F}^{*}).

Thus,
\omega^{p}
and
\ast(F^{q})
lie in orthogonal subspaces of
\Lambda^{2p}(T^{*}X)
, so that
\omega^{p}\wedge F^{q}=\langle\omega^{p},\ast(F^{q})\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}_{X}=0
for all odd integers
p,q\geq 1
with
p+q=2n
. This verifies (
4.3
).
∎

Remark 4.9
.

In the language of
H
-structures, the (hyperkähler)
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
-structure on
X
together with the complex Lagrangian distribution
\mathsf{B}\subset TX
constitutes a
\mathrm{U}(n)_{\Delta}
-structure, where
\mathrm{U}(n)_{\Delta}
is the image of the usual diagonal embedding
\mathrm{U}(n)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(n)\leq\mathrm{SO}(4n)
.

5
Gauge Theory on Calabi-Yau Cones and Sasaki-Einstein Links

We now turn our attention to gauge theory on Calabi-Yau cones
C^{2n+2}=\mathrm{C}(M)
with
n\geq 2
. In Proposition
5.3
, we recall that conically singular pHYM connections over

. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
5.3
we consider a dimensional reduction of the contact instanton equation. More precisely, we recall in Proposition

how pHYM connections on the Kähler-Einstein twistor space

yield simple examples of contact instantons on

The results of this section are not new (see e.g.,
[
39
]
,
[

]
for the case of

5.1
Calabi-Yau Cones and Sasaki-Einstein Links

Let
C^{2n+2}
be a Calabi-Yau cone,
n\geq 2
. By the discussion in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
,
C
carries an
\mathrm{SU}(n+1)
-structure
(g_{C},J_{C},\omega_{C},\Upsilon_{C})
, where
(g_{C},J_{C},\omega_{C})
is a Kähler structure and
\Upsilon_{C}\in\Omega^{n+1,0}(C)
is a complex volume form. Since
C
is a cone, we have that

(C^{2n+2},g_{C})=(M^{2n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{M})

for some Sasaki-Einstein manifold
(M,g_{M})
. Going forward, we identify
M
with the hypersurface
M\times\{1\}\subset C
.

On
M
, we define a
1
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, a vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
, an endomorphism
\mathsf{J}_{M}\in\mathrm{End}(TM)
, and an
n
-form
\Psi\in\Omega^{n}(M;\mathbb{C})
as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle:=\left.\left(r\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{C}\right)\right|_{M}

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{\sharp}

\displaystyle\mathsf{J}_{M}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}J_{C}&\mbox{on }\mathrm{Ker}(\alpha)\\
0&\mbox{on }\mathbb{R}\xi\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle:=\left.\left(r\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Upsilon_{C}\right)\right|_{M}.

We refer to
\alpha
as the
contact form
, and to
\xi
as the
Reeb field
. The data
(g_{M},\alpha,\mathsf{J}_{M},\Psi)
comprises an
(\mathrm{SU}(n)\times\{1\})
-structure.

Note that the tangent bundle of
M
splits orthogonally as

\displaystyle TM

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}\xi\oplus\mathsf{D},

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Ker}(\alpha),

the endomorphism
\left.\mathsf{J}_{M}\right|_{\mathsf{D}}\colon\mathsf{D}\to\mathsf{D}
is a
g_{M}
-orthogonal complex structure on
\mathsf{D}
, and the restriction
\Psi|_{\mathsf{D}}
is an
(n,0)
-form with respect to
\mathsf{J}_{M}|_{\mathsf{D}}
. Altogether, the
2n
-plane field
\mathsf{D}\subset TM
carries the Hermitian structure
(g_{M},\mathsf{J}_{M},\sigma)
, where
\sigma:=g_{M}(\mathsf{J}_{M}\cdot,\cdot)
is the non-degenerate
2
-form, as well as the complex volume form
\Psi|_{\mathsf{D}}
. In particular, the metric
\left.g_{M}\right|_{\mathsf{D}}
and orientation on
\mathsf{D}
induce a natural Hodge star operator

\displaystyle\ast_{\mathsf{D}}\colon\Lambda^{k}(\mathsf{D}^{*})

\displaystyle\to\Lambda^{2n-k}(\mathsf{D}^{*}).

We will say that a
k
-form
\beta\in\Lambda^{k}(T^{*}M)
is
transverse
if
\beta\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathsf{D}^{*})\subset\Lambda^{k}(T^{*}M)
. Note that if
\beta
is transverse, then

\ast\!\beta=(\ast_{\mathsf{D}}\beta)\wedge\alpha.

(5.1)

Finally, noting that
\omega_{C}=r\,dr\wedge\alpha+r^{2}\sigma
, we observe that
d\omega_{C}=0
implies
d\alpha=2\sigma
.

5.2
Contact Instantons on Sasaki-Einstein Manifolds

Let
M^{2n+1}
be a Sasaki-Einstein manifold with
n\geq 2
as above. The bundle of
2
-forms on
M
decomposes into
(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\{1\})
-irreducible subbundles as follows:

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}M)=\left(\alpha\otimes\mathsf{D}^{*}\right)\oplus[\![\Lambda^{2,0}]\!]\oplus[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}]\oplus\mathbb{R}\sigma

where here

\displaystyle[\![\Lambda^{2,0}]\!]

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{D}^{*})\colon\beta(\mathsf{J}_{M}X,\mathsf{J}_{M}Y)=-\beta(X,Y)\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathsf{D}\}

\displaystyle[\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}]

\displaystyle=\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{D}^{*})\colon\beta(\mathsf{J}_{M}X,\mathsf{J}_{M}Y)=\beta(X,Y)\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathsf{D}\text{ and }\langle\beta,\sigma\rangle=0\}.

We now define the notion of a (anti-self-dual) contact instanton.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
A
be a connection on a principal bundle
P\to M
. We say that
A
is a
contact instanton
if either of the following equivalent conditions hold:

1.

A
is an
(\mathrm{SU}(n)\times\{1\})
-instanton (i.e.,
F_{A}\in\Gamma([\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}]\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P})
).

2.

A
is
\Omega
-ASD for
\Omega=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\alpha\wedge\sigma^{n-2}
.

Proof.

Note that if
A
satisfies (i), then
F_{A}
is a transverse
\mathrm{ad}_{P}
-valued
2
-form. Similarly, if
A
satisfies (ii), then

\ast F_{A}=-\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\alpha\wedge\sigma^{n-2}\wedge F_{A}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{R}\alpha\otimes\Lambda^{2n-2}(\mathsf{D}^{*})\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P}),

and hence
F_{A}=\ast(\ast F_{A})
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}(\mathsf{D}^{*})\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P}
. Thus, in either case,
F_{A}
is transverse. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle F_{A}\in\Gamma([\Lambda^{1,1}_{0}]\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P})

\displaystyle\iff\ast_{\mathsf{D}}F_{A}=-\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\sigma^{n-2}\wedge F_{A}

\displaystyle\iff\alpha\wedge\ast_{\mathsf{D}}F_{A}=-\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\alpha\wedge\sigma^{n-2}\wedge F_{A}

\displaystyle\iff\ast F_{A}=-\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\alpha\wedge\sigma^{n-2}\wedge F_{A},

where the last equivalence uses (
5.1
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\Omega=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\alpha\wedge\sigma^{n-2}\in\Omega^{2n-3}(M)
. One can check that the
\mathrm{SU}(n)
-equivariant linear operator
\Lambda^{2}(T_{x}^{*}M)\to\Lambda^{2}(T_{x}^{*}M)
given by
\beta\mapsto\ast(\beta\wedge\Omega)
has eigenvalues
0
,
1
,
-1
, and
(n-1)
, with respective eigenspaces

Proof.

\displaystyle dr\wedge(\ast_{M}F_{B_{r}})=\ast_{C}F_{A}

7.1

7.2
The Asymptotically Conical Case

This section is aimed at stating and proving Theorem

Our approach follows that of Papoulias
[

]
, who obtains an analogous result for AC

-manifolds. Preparation of this section was also aided by helpful discussions in
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
, and
[
20
]
.

\mu_{j}=\mu_{j}(r)
for which

\displaystyle g_{C}

\displaystyle=(e^{1})^{2}+\cdots+(e^{N})^{2},

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}h_{X}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}^{2}(e^{1})^{2}+\cdots+\mu_{N}^{2}(e^{N})^{2}.

Repeating the argument in the proof of Lemma
7.8
shows that each
\mu_{j}=O(1)
. Now, in terms of the basis
\{e_{j}\}
, we may write
\Psi^{*}\gamma=\sum_{I}A_{I}e^{I}
, say, where we sum over multi-indices
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
and write
e^{I}=e^{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{k}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\Psi^{*}\gamma\right|_{g_{C}}^{2}=\sum_{I}A_{I}^{2}=\sum_{I}\frac{1}{\mu_{I}^{2}}A_{I}^{2}\mu_{I}^{2}=O(1)\sum_{I}\frac{1}{\mu_{I}^{2}}A_{I}^{2}=O(1)\left|\Psi^{*}\gamma\right|^{2}_{\Psi^{*}h_{X}}=O(1)\left|\gamma\right|^{2}_{h_{X}}.

∎

7.2.2
Asymptotically conical hyperkähler manifolds

Definition 7.10
.

Let
(X^{4n+4},h_{X},(I,J,K),(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}))
be a hyperkähler
(4n+4)
-manifold,
n\geq 1
. We say that
X
is
asymptotically conical (AC)
with
rate
\nu<0
if there exists a compact set
K\subset X
, a constant
R>0
, a compact connected
3
-Sasakian
(4n+3)
-manifold
(\Sigma^{4n+3},g_{\Sigma})
, and a diffeomorphism

\Psi\colon(R,\infty)\times\Sigma\to X-K

such that for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{k}_{g_{C}}(\Psi^{*}\omega_{L}-\beta_{L})\right|_{g_{C}}

\displaystyle=O(r^{\nu-k})\ \ \ \text{as }r\to\infty

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{k}_{g_{C}}(\Psi^{*}L-L_{C})\right|_{g_{C}}

\displaystyle=O(r^{\nu-k})\ \ \ \text{as }r\to\infty

where
C=\mathrm{C}(\Sigma)=(R,\infty)\times\Sigma
is the metric cone over
\Sigma
, and
(g_{C}
,
(I_{C},J_{C},K_{C})
,
(\beta_{I},\beta_{J},\beta_{K}))
is the natural conical hyperkähler structure on
C
. We call
\Sigma
the
asymptotic link
of
X
.

It is well-known that AC hyperkähler
(4n+4)
-manifolds of rate
\nu
are AC Riemannian
(4n+4)
-manifolds of rate
\nu
, meaning that we have the estimate

\left|\nabla^{k}_{g_{C}}(\Psi^{*}h_{X}-g_{C})\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\nu-k})\ \ \ \text{as }r\to\infty

See
[
9
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.3]
for a proof. From now on, we assume that
X
is an AC hyperkähler manifold of rate
\nu
.

As in
\lx@sectionsign
7.1.2
, for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying both
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=0
and
\lambda_{1}-2\lambda_{2}-2\lambda_{3}>0
, we define a
4n
-form
\Phi_{\lambda}\in\Omega^{4n}(X)
by

\Phi_{\lambda}=\textstyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}(\lambda_{1}\omega_{I}^{2n}+\lambda_{2}\omega_{J}^{2n}+\lambda_{3}\omega_{K}^{2n}).

The following algebraic fact will be used repeatedly in the sequel. To state it, recall the decomposition

\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)=U_{I}\oplus U_{J}\oplus U_{K}\oplus W\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{I}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{J}\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{J}

of Proposition
3.2
. For a
2
-form
F\in\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)
, we let
F_{I}
and
F_{W}
denote its components in
U_{I}
and
W
, respectively.

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
F\in\Lambda^{2}(T^{*}X)
. Then:

1.

F_{W}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=0
.

2.

F_{I}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=(-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})\ast\!F_{I}
.

Proof.

(a) Note that
F_{W}\wedge\frac{1}{(2n)!}\omega_{L}^{2n}=-\ast\!F_{W}
for each
L\in\{I,J,K\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{W}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})\ast\!F_{W}=0.

(b) Note that
F_{I}\wedge\omega_{I}^{2n}=-\ast\!F_{I}
, whereas
F_{I}\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n}=F_{K}\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n}=\ast F_{I}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{I}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{(2n)!}\left(\lambda_{1}\,F_{I}\wedge\omega_{I}^{2n}+\lambda_{2}\,F_{I}\wedge\omega_{J}^{2n}+\lambda_{3}\,F_{I}\wedge\omega_{K}^{2n}\right)

\displaystyle=(-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})\ast\!F_{I}.

∎

7.2.3
The Chern-Simons functional

Let
X^{4n+4}
be an AC hyperkähler manifold of rate
\nu<0
. To study gauge-theoretic objects over
X
, we consider a class of bundles that respects the asymptotic geometry of
X
.

Definition 7.12
.

A principal bundle
P\to X^{4n+4}
is
asymptotically framed
if there exists a principal bundle
Q\to\Sigma
(called an
asymptotic framing
) such that
\Psi^{*}P\cong\pi^{*}Q
, where
\pi\colon C=(R,\infty)\times\Sigma\to\Sigma
is the projection onto the second factor.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}Q\cong\Psi^{*}P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X-K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Fix a connection
A_{\infty}
on
Q\to\Sigma
. A connection
A
on
P\to X
is called
asymptotically conical (AC)
of
rate
\alpha

with respect to
A_{\infty}
provided that

\left|\nabla^{k}_{g_{C}}(\Psi^{*}A-\pi^{*}A_{\infty})\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\alpha-k})\ \ \text{ as }r\to\infty

for all
k\geq 0
. We let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{AC connections on }P\text{ of rate }\alpha\text{ with respect to }A_{\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\left\{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})\colon A\text{ is an }\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)\text{-instanton}\right\}\!.

We now seek AC analogues of Lemma
7.5
and Corollary
7.6
, which as stated apply only to
compact
hyperkähler manifolds. To this end, by using the truncations
X_{r}=\{x\in X\colon\rho(x)\leq r\}
and their boundaries
S_{r}=\{x\in X\colon\rho(x)=r\}
, we introduce the following functional.

Definition 7.13
.

Let
P\to X^{4n+4}
be an asymptotically framed principal bundle, and let
A_{0}
be an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton on
P
. For a connection
A
on
P
, its
Chern-Simons functional
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A]\colon[R,\infty)

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)

\displaystyle:=\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left(F_{A}\wedge(A-A_{0})-\frac{1}{3}(A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}.

The key properties of the CS functional are summarized in the following proposition. To state it, recall once more that if
A
is a connection on
P\to X
, then its curvature
2
-form
F:=F_{A}
decomposes as

F=F_{I}+F_{J}+F_{K}+F_{W}+\mu_{I}\omega_{I}+\mu_{J}\omega_{J}+\mu_{K}\omega_{K}.

In particular,
A
is
I
-primitive HYM if and only if
F=F_{I}+F_{W}
, and
A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton if and only if
F_{I}=0
.

Proposition 7.14
.

Let
A
be a connection on
P
.

1.

We have

\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)=\sum_{L\in\{I,J,K\}}\int_{X_{r}}\left(a_{L}|F_{L}|^{2}+b_{L}|\mu_{L}\omega_{L}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{X_{r}},

where the constants
a_{L}
and
b_{L}
are as in (
7.5
). In particular,
\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A]
is independent of the choice of
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton
A_{0}
.

2.

If
A
is
I
-primitive HYM, then

\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)=a_{I}\int_{X_{r}}|F_{I}|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol}_{X_{r}}.

Thus,
A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton if and only if
\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)=0
for all sufficiently large
r\geq R
.

Proof.

(a) Let
A
be a connection on
P
, let
A_{0}
be an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton on
P
, and let
F
and
F_{0}
be their respective curvature forms. It is well-known that

d\left[\mathrm{tr}\!\left((F+F_{0})\wedge(A-A_{0})-\frac{1}{3}(A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\right]=\mathrm{tr}(F^{2})-\mathrm{tr}(F_{0}^{2}).

Wedging with
\Phi_{\lambda}
, integrating over
X_{r}
, and then applying Stokes’ Theorem gives:

\displaystyle\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left((F+F_{0})\wedge(A-A_{0})-\frac{1}{3}(A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{r}}\mathrm{tr}(F^{2})\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}-\int_{X_{r}}\mathrm{tr}(F_{0}^{2})\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in\{I,J,K\}}\int_{X_{r}}\left(a_{L}|F_{L}|^{2}+b_{L}|\mu_{L}\omega_{L}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{X_{r}},

where in the last step we applied Lemma
7.5
to both integrals. To simplify the left side, note that since
A_{0}
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton, we have
F_{0}\in\Gamma(W\otimes\mathrm{ad}_{P})
. Therefore, Lemma
7.11
(a) gives
F_{0}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=0
, and so

\mathrm{tr}(F_{0}\wedge(A-A_{0}))\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=\mathrm{tr}(F_{0}\wedge(A-A_{0})\wedge\Phi_{\lambda})=0.

Therefore,

\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left(F\wedge(A-A_{0})-\frac{1}{3}(A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=\sum_{L\in\{I,J,K\}}\int_{X_{r}}\left(a_{L}|F_{L}|^{2}+b_{L}|\mu_{L}\omega_{L}|^{2}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{X_{r}}.

(b) This follows immediately from part (a).
∎

7.2.4
The asymptotically conical Lewis theorem

We are finally in a position to state and prove the AC analogue of Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem 7.15
.

Let
P\to X^{4n+4}
be an asymptotically framed principal
G
-bundle over an asymptotically conical hyperkähler manifold
X
of rate
\nu<0
, where
G
is a compact Lie group. Let
\Sigma^{4n+3}
be the
3
-Sasakian asymptotic link of
X
, let
Q\to\Sigma
be an asymptotic framing, and fix a tri-contact instanton
A_{\infty}
on
Q\to\Sigma
.

Suppose that
\nu\leq-2-\frac{4}{3}n
, and that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})
is non-empty for some
\alpha<\nu+1
. If
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})
is an asymptotically conical
I
-primitive HYM connection satisfying

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi^{*}F_{I}\right|_{g_{C}}

\displaystyle=O(r^{\beta})\ \ \text{ where }\beta<-4n-\nu-4,

(7.14)

where
F=F_{A}
is the curvature of
A
, then
A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})
be an asymptotically conical
I
-primitive HYM connection satisfying (
7.14
). Since
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(P,A_{\infty})
is non-empty, there exists an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton
A_{0}
satisfying

|\Psi^{*}A_{0}-\pi^{*}A_{\infty}|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\alpha}).

Consequently,

\left|\Psi^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{C}}\leq\left|\Psi^{*}A-\pi^{*}A_{\infty}\right|_{g_{C}}+\left|\Psi^{*}A_{0}-\pi^{*}A_{\infty}\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\alpha}).

In particular, suppressing pullbacks via the inclusions
S_{r}\hookrightarrow X-K
from the notation, Lemma
7.9
(a) gives

\displaystyle\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq r\left|\Psi^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\alpha+1})

(7.15)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi_{r}^{*}F_{I}\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq r\left|\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi^{*}F_{I}\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{\beta+1}).

(7.16)

Now, following the technique of
[
37
]
, we split

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left(F\wedge(A-A_{0})-\frac{1}{3}(A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}\leq\left|I_{1}(r)\right|+\left|I_{2}(r)\right|\!,

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left(F\wedge(A-A_{0})\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda},

\displaystyle I_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left((A-A_{0})^{3}\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}.

We aim to establish the following two estimates:

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}(r)\right|

\displaystyle=O\!\left(r^{4n+3+\alpha+\beta}\right)

(7.17)

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}(r)\right|

\displaystyle=O\!\left(r^{4n+3+3\alpha}\right)\!.

(7.18)

Note that these two bounds imply the result. To see this, observe that our assumptions imply that
4n+3+\alpha+\beta<0
and
4n+3+3\alpha<0
. Therefore, the Monotone Convergence Theorem, followed by Proposition
7.14
(b), followed by the above estimates gives

a_{I}\int_{X}|F_{I}|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol}=\lim_{r\to\infty}a_{I}\int_{X_{r}}|F_{I}|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol}_{X_{r}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathrm{CS}_{\lambda}[A](r)\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}(|I_{1}(r)|+|I_{2}(r)|)=0,

whence
F_{I}=0
, and
A
is an
\mathrm{Sp}(n+1)
-instanton.

We begin by proving (
7.17
). Set
\tau:=-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}
. Since
A
is
I
-primitive HYM, Lemma
7.11
implies that
F\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}=\tau\ast_{X}\!F_{I}
, and hence

\left|I_{1}(r)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left(F\wedge(A-A_{0})\right)\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}\right|=\left|\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}((A-A_{0})\wedge\ast_{X}F_{I})\right|.

Now, setting
\widehat{\partial}_{\rho}:=\frac{1}{\left|\partial_{\rho}\right|}\partial_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}((A-A_{0})\wedge\ast_{X}F_{I})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}((A-A_{0})\wedge\ast_{S_{r}}(\widehat{\partial}_{\rho}\,\lrcorner\,F_{I}))=-\frac{1}{|\partial_{\rho}|}\left\langle A-A_{0},\,\partial_{\rho}\,\lrcorner\,F_{I}\right\rangle_{h_{r}}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{r}}.

Moreover, since
X
is asymptotically conical of rate
\nu
, we have

\left|h_{X}(\partial_{\rho},\partial_{\rho})-1\right|=\left|(\Psi^{*}h_{X})(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})-g_{C}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})\right|=O(r^{\nu}),

which implies that
\frac{1}{|\partial_{\rho}|}=O(1)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}(r)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\frac{1}{|\partial_{\rho}|}\left\langle A-A_{0},\,\partial_{\rho}\,\lrcorner\,F_{I}\right\rangle_{h_{r}}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{r}}\right|

\displaystyle=O(1)\left|\int_{\Sigma}\Psi_{r}^{*}\!\left(\left\langle A-A_{0},\,\partial_{\rho}\,\lrcorner\,F_{I}\right\rangle_{h_{r}}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{r}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=O(1)\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0}),\,\Psi_{r}^{*}(\partial_{\rho}\,\lrcorner\,F_{I})\right\rangle_{\Psi_{r}^{*}h_{r}}\mathrm{vol}_{\Psi_{r}^{*}h_{r}}\right|

\displaystyle=O(r^{4n+1})\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0}),\,\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi_{r}^{*}F_{I}\right\rangle_{g_{\Sigma}}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}\right|

where in the last step we used (
7.11
) and (
7.13
). Finally, from the bounds (
7.15
) and (
7.16
), we obtain

\left|\left\langle\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0}),\,\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi_{r}^{*}F_{I}\right\rangle_{g_{\Sigma}}\right|\leq\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}\left|\partial_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\Psi_{r}^{*}F_{I}\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}=O(r^{\alpha+\beta+2}),

which gives (
7.17
).

It remains to prove (
7.18
). For this, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}(r)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{6}\int_{S_{r}}\mathrm{tr}\!\left[(A-A_{0})^{3}\wedge\Phi_{\lambda}\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{tr}\!\left[(\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0}))^{3}\wedge\Psi_{r}^{*}\Phi_{\lambda}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\int_{\Sigma}\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}^{3}\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}\Phi_{\lambda}\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}\,\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}.

Now, the bound (
7.15
) gives
\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}(A-A_{0})\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}^{3}=O(r^{3\alpha+3})
. Moreover, Lemma
7.9
(a) and (b) give

\left|\Psi_{r}^{*}\Phi_{\lambda}\right|_{g_{\Sigma}}\leq r^{4n}\left|\Psi^{*}\Phi_{\lambda}\right|_{g_{C}}=O(r^{4n})\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right|_{h_{X}}.

Finally, since
\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}=0
, we have
\left|\Phi_{\lambda}\right|_{h_{X}}=\text{constant}
. Thus, we conclude that
\left|I_{2}(r)\right|=O(r^{4n+3\alpha+3})
as claimed.
∎

u^{\alpha}(x)=0\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right\}\alpha=1,\ldots,n

(1.1)

as well as of other boundary value problems for those systems (see Section

Here and in the following repeated indices are to be summed over
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\ldots=1,\ldots,n
and
i,j,k,\ldots=1,2
,
and
\varepsilon>0
is the small homogenization parameter.
We assume that

\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ and }a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(\cdot+z)=a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}\mbox{ for all }z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

(1.3)

\displaystyle u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto(b_{i}^{\alpha}(\cdot,u),b^{\alpha}(\cdot,u))\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ is $C^{1}$-smooth}

(1.4)

and

\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}(y)\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)\partial_{y_{j}}\varphi^{\beta}(y)dy:\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)dy=1\right\}>0.

(1.5)

The components of the homogenized diffusion tensor are, by definition,

\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}:=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\left(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(y)+a^{\alpha\gamma}_{ik}(y)\partial_{y_{k}}v^{\gamma\beta}_{j}(y)\right)dy,

(1.6)

where the

,
\beta=1,\ldots,n
,

are defined by the
2n
cell problems

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{y_{i}}\left(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(y)+a^{\alpha\gamma}_{ik}(y)\partial_{y_{k}}v^{\gamma\beta}_{j}(y)\right)=0\mbox{ for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
v^{\alpha\beta}_{j}(\cdot+z)=v^{\alpha\beta}_{j}\mbox{ for }z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\;\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)^{2}}v^{\alpha\beta}_{j}(y))dy=0,\end{array}\right\}\alpha=1,\ldots,n.

(1.7)

It is well-known (as a consequence of

1.5
) and

[

) are uniquely weakly solvable
and that the homogenized diffusion coefficients
\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}
satisfy the coercivity condition
(
1.5
) as well, i.e.

\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\hat{a}_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)\partial_{y_{j}}\varphi^{\beta}(y)dy:\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)\partial_{y_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(y)dy=1\right\}>0.

(1.8)

Let us formulate our main result. It concerns existence and local uniqueness of
weak solutions
u=u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.1
) with
\varepsilon\approx 0
, which are close to a given non-degenerate weak solution
u=u_{0}
to
the homogenized problem

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{x_{j}}\Big(\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)=b^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
u^{\alpha}(x)=0\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right\}\alpha=1,\ldots,n

(1.9)

as well as the rate of convergence to zero for

Here and in what follows we denote

\|u\|_{\infty}:=\max_{\alpha=1,\ldots,n}\operatorname*{ess\phantom{|}\!sup}_{x\in\Omega}|u^{\alpha}(x)|\mbox{ for }u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.10)

As usual, a vector function

\int_{\Omega}\left(\Big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(x/\varepsilon)\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(x)+b^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\varphi^{\alpha}(x)\right)dx=0

,
and similarly for (

Suppose (

(1.11)

Our paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
we consider abstract nonlinear parameter depending equations of the type

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=0.

(1.14)

Here
\varepsilon>0
is the parameter. We prove a result on existence and local uniqueness of a family of solutions
u=u_{\varepsilon}\approx\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.14
) with
\varepsilon\approx 0
, where
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
is a family of approximate solutions to (
1.14
), i.e. a family with

F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
, and we estimate the norm of the error
u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
by the norm of the discrepancy
F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
.
This type of generalized implicit function theorems has been successfully applied to singularly perturbed nonlinear ODEs and PDEs and to homogenization of nonlinear ODEs.
Contrary to the classical implicit function theorem it is not supposed that
the linearized operators
F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)
converge for
\varepsilon\to 0

with respect to the uniform operator norm. And, indeed, in the
applications to singularly perturbed problems as well as to homogenization
problems they do not converge for
\varepsilon\to 0

with respect to the uniform operator norm (cf. Remark
3.2
below).
Hence, the present paper introduces an application
to semilinear elliptic PDE systems of a common approach to existence, local uniqueness and error estimates for singularly perturbed problems and for homogenization problems.
Another application to periodic homogenization of quasilinear ODE systems
of the type

a(x,x/\varepsilon,u(x),u^{\prime}(x))^{\prime}=f(x,x/\varepsilon,u(x))

has been presented in Part I
[
22
]
.

In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
by means of
the results of Section
2
.
Here the main work is to construct
appropriate families of approximate solutions
to (
1.1
) with
\varepsilon\approx 0

with small discrepancies in appropriate function space norms.
In order to apply implicit function theorems mainly one needs isomorphism properties
of the linearized operators. In the setting of Section
3
they follow from
K. Grögers result
[
13
]

about maximal regularity of boundary value problems for elliptic systems
with non-smooth data in the pair of Sobolev spaces
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p\approx 2
.
In order to apply implicit function theorems one needs also
C^{1}
-smoothness of the appearing nonlinear superposition operators. In the
setting of Section
3
these operators have to be well-defined and
C^{1}
-smooth on the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

with
p>2
, but
p\approx 2
, and therefore we have to suppose that the space dimension is two.

Finally, in Section
4
we show that Theorem
1.1
is true also in the case of mixed boundary conditions. More exactly, we consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on one part of
\partial\Omega
and inhomogeneous natural (i.e. nonlinear Robin) boundary conditions on the remaining part, and the two boundary parts are allowed to touch.

2
An abstract result of implicit function theorem type

Let
U
and
V
be a Banach spaces with norms
\|\cdot\|_{U}
and
\|\cdot\|_{V}
, respectively.
For
\varepsilon>0
let be given

\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\in U\mbox{ and }F_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(U;V).

In this section we consider the abstract equation

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=0.

(2.1)

Roughly speaking, we will show the following:
If the elements
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy (
2.1
) approximately for
\varepsilon\approx 0
, i.e. if
\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}\to 0
for
\varepsilon\to 0
,
and if they are non-degenerate solutions
(cf. assumption (
2.2
) below), then
for
\varepsilon\approx 0
there exists exactly one solution
u=u_{\varepsilon}
to (
2.1
) with
\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\approx 0
, and
\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}=O(\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V})
for
\varepsilon\to 0
.
For that we do not suppose any convergence of the operators
F_{\varepsilon}
or
F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)
or of the elements
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Remark that in the classical implicit function theorem one cannot omit, in general, the assumption, that
F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u)
converges for
\varepsilon\to 0
with respect to the uniform operator norm (cf.
[
15
, Section 3.6]
).

Theorem 2.1

Suppose that

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{there exist $\varepsilon_{0}>0$ and $c>0$ such that for all $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]$ the operators $F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})$ are}\\
\mbox{Fredholm of index zero from }$U$\mbox{ into }$V$,\mbox{and }\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))u\|_{V}\geq c\|u\|_{U}\mbox{ for all }u\in U\end{array}\right\}

(2.2)

and

\sup_{\|v\|_{U}\leq 1}\|(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+u)-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))v\|_{V}\to 0\mbox{ for }\varepsilon+\|u\|_{U}\to 0

(2.3)

and

\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}\to 0\mbox{ for }\varepsilon\to 0.

(2.4)

Then there exist
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
\delta>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]

there exists exactly one solution
u=u_{\varepsilon}
to (
2.1
) with
\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\delta
, and

\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\frac{2}{c}\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))\|_{V}.

(2.5)

Proof

Take
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.
Because of assumption (
2.2
) the linear operator
F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
is an isomorphism from
U
onto
V
, and, hence,
equation (
2.1
) is equivalent to the fixed point problem

u=G_{\varepsilon}(u):=u-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))^{-1}F_{\varepsilon}(u).

Take
u_{1},u_{2}\in U
. Then

\displaystyle\|G_{\varepsilon}(u_{1})-G_{\varepsilon}(u_{2})\|_{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su_{1}+(1-s)u_{2})\right)ds(u_{1}-u_{2})\right\|_{U}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\,\max_{0\leq s\leq 1}\|\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su_{1}+(1-s)u_{2})\right)(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{V}.

Here we used that (
2.2
) yields that

c\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})^{-1}v\|_{U}\leq\|v\|_{V}

for all
v\in V
.

Denote
{\cal B}^{r}_{\varepsilon}:=\{u\in U:\;\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq r\}
.
If
u_{1},u_{2}\in{\cal B}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
,
then also
su_{1}+(1-s)u_{2}\in{\cal B}^{r}_{\varepsilon}

for all
s\in[0,1]
. Therefore it follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.6
) that there exist
r_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]

the maps
G_{\varepsilon}
are strictly contractive with contraction constant
1/2
on the closed balls
{\cal B}^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
and
u\in{\cal B}^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|G_{\varepsilon}(u)-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{U}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|G_{\varepsilon}(u)-G_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{U}+\left\|G_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{U}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r_{0}}{2}+\left\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))^{-1}F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{U}\leq\frac{r_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{c}\left\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{V},

and (
2.4
) yields that
G_{\varepsilon}
maps
{\cal B}^{r_{0}}_{\varepsilon}
into itself if
\varepsilon_{1}
is taken sufficiently small.

Now, Banach’s fixed point principle yields the existence and uniqueness assertions of Theorem
2.1
, and the estimate (
2.5
)
follows as above:

\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\|G_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-G_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}+\|G_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{V}+\frac{1}{c}\left\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{V}.

Remark 2.2

In

,

,

,

,

,

,

For another result of the type of Theorem

If one applies Theorem

and

and of the family

of should be ”simple” (for example,

should be

to determine the exact solution

for
\varepsilon\to 0
should be high. The norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
should be weak and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{U}
should be strong such that the error estimate
(
2.5
)
is strong. But at the same time
the norm
\|\cdot\|_{U}
should be weak such that the domain of local uniqueness, which contains all
u\in U
with
\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\delta
, is large.
These criteria are contradicting, of course. Hence, in any application of Theorem
2.1

the choices of
U
,
V
,
\|\cdot\|_{U}
,
\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
are compromises according to the requirements of the application.

One way to find such compromises is described in Corollary
2.3
below. It delivers existence and local uniqueness of solutions
u=u_{\varepsilon}
to the equation
F_{\varepsilon}(u)=0
with
\varepsilon\approx 0
and
\|u-u_{0}\|\approx 0
, where
\|\cdot\|
is another norm in
U
, which is allowed to be much weaker
than the norm
\|\cdot\|_{U}
,
and
where
\|F_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})\|_{V}
does not converge to zero for
\varepsilon\to 0
, in general. The price for that is that the estimate (
2.11
) of the error
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
is with respect to the weaker norm
\|\cdot\|
, only.

Corollary 2.3

Suppose
(
2.4
). Further, let be given
u_{0}\in U

such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{there exist $\varepsilon_{0}>0$ and $c>0$ such that for all $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]$ the operators $F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0})$ are}\\
\mbox{Fredholm of index zero from }$U$\mbox{ into }$V$,\mbox{and }\|F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0}))u\|_{V}\geq c\|u\|_{U}\mbox{ for all }u\in U,\end{array}\right\}

(2.7)

and let be given a norm
\|\cdot\|
in
U

such that

\displaystyle d:=\sup\{\|u\|:\;u\in U,\|u\|_{U}\leq 1\}<\infty,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|\to 0\mbox{ for }\varepsilon\to 0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|v\|_{U}\leq 1}\|(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+u)-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}))v\|_{V}\to 0\mbox{ for }\varepsilon+\|u\|\to 0.

(2.10)

Then
there exist
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
\delta>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]

there exists exactly one solution
u=u_{\varepsilon}
to (
2.1
) with
\|u-u_{0}\|\leq\delta
,
and

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|\leq\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|+\frac{3d}{c}\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))\|_{V}.

(2.11)

Proof

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
u,v\in U
we have

\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+u)-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}))v\|_{V}\leq\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0}+(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})+u)-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0}))v\|_{V}+\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0})-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0}+(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})))v\|_{V}.

Hence, assumptions (
2.8
)-(
2.10
) imply that (
2.3
) is satisfied.
Similarly, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
v\in U
we have

\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})v\|_{V}\geq\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0})v\|_{V}-\|(F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0})-F_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{0}+(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})))v\|_{V}.

Therefore assumptions (
2.7
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
)
imply that (
2.2
) is satisfied
(with another
\varepsilon_{0}
in (
2.2
) than in (
2.7
) and with arbitrary smaller
c
in (
2.2
)
than in (
2.7
)).
Hence, Theorem
2.1
yields the existence assertion of Corollary
2.3
and the error estimate

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|\leq\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|+d\|u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|+\frac{3d}{c}\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}.

Now let us prove the local uniqueness assertion of Corollary
2.3
.
Take
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
and a solution
u\in U
to (
2.1
). Then

0=F_{\varepsilon}(u)=F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\int_{0}^{1}\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su+(1-s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right)(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})ds,

i.e.

u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}=-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})^{-1}\left(F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})+\int_{0}^{1}\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su+(1-s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right)(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})ds\right),

i.e.

\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}\leq\frac{1}{c}\left(\|F_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}+\max_{0\leq s\leq 1}\|\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su+(1-s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right)(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}\right).

(2.12)

But (
2.10
) yields that

\max_{0\leq s\leq 1}\|\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su+(1-s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right)(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}=o(\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U})\mbox{ for }\varepsilon+\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|\to 0.

Therefore (
2.9
) implies that

\max_{0\leq s\leq 1}\|\left(F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(su+(1-s)\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right)(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\|_{V}=o(\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U})\mbox{ for }\varepsilon+\|u-u_{0}\|\to 0.

Hence, if
\varepsilon
and
\|u-u_{0}\|
are sufficiently small, then (
2.12
) yields that
\|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{U}
is sufficiently small, and the local uniqueness assertion of Theorem (
2.1
) implies that
u=u_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.4

For

\rho(x)\geq 0\mbox{ and }\rho(x)=\rho(-x)\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;\rho(x)=0\mbox{ for }x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\geq 1,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(x)dx=1,

Lemma 3.3

(i) For all
r\geq 1
and

we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)-[S_{\delta}u](x)|^{r}dx=0

(3.18)

(ii) For all
r\geq 1
there exists
c_{r}>0
such that for all
\delta>0

and
u\in L^{r}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|[\partial_{x_{i}}S_{\delta}u](x)\right|^{r}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{r}}{\delta^{r}}\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)\right|^{r}dx\mbox{ for }i=1,2,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left|[S_{\delta}u](x)\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{r}}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)\right|^{r}dx.

(3.20)

Proof

Assertion (i) is proved e.g. in

[
1
, Lemma 1.1.1]
.

In order to prove assertion (ii) take
\delta>0
,

with
1/r+1/s=1
, and take

.
Then the Hölder inequality implies that for all

we have

|[S_{\delta}u](x)|=\left|\int_{\Omega}u(y)\rho_{\delta}(x-y)^{1/r}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)^{1/s}dy\right|\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{r}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)dy\right)^{1/r}.

Here we used that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(z)dz=1.

It follows that

|[S_{\delta}u](x)|^{r}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{r}\rho((x-y)/\delta)dy\leq\mbox{const }\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{r}dy,

where the constant does not depend on
\delta
and
u
.
Hence, (
3.20
) is proved.

Further, because of

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial_{x_{i}}\rho_{\delta}(x-y)|dx=\frac{1}{\delta^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial_{x_{i}}\rho((x-y)/\delta)|dy=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial_{x_{i}}\rho(z)|dz

we have that

\displaystyle|[\partial_{x_{i}}S_{\delta}u](x)|

3.3
Verification of (
3.9
)

The linear operators
A_{\varepsilon}
are isomorphisms from

onto

(cf. Theorem
3.1
), and the linear operators

are bounded from

into

W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

and, hence, compact from
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
into

. Hence, condition (
3.9
) is satisfied if there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0

such that

\inf\left\{\|(A_{\varepsilon}+B^{\prime}(u_{0}))u\|_{-1,p}:\;\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}],u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|u\|_{1,p}=1\right\}>0.

Suppose that this is not true. Then there exist sequences
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\ldots>0
and
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\varepsilon_{l}\to 0
for
l\to\infty
and

\lim_{l\to\infty}\|(A_{\varepsilon_{l}}+B^{\prime}(u_{0}))u_{l}\|_{-1,p}=0,

(3.35)

but

\|u_{l}\|_{1,p}=1\mbox{ for all }l.

(3.36)

Because
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is reflexive and because it is compactly embedded into
C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, without loss of generality we may assume that there exists

such that

u_{l}\rightharpoonup u_{*}\mbox{ for $l\to\infty$
weakly in }W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\mbox{ and }\lim_{l\to\infty}\|u_{l}-u_{*}\|_{\infty}=0.

(3.37)

From (
3.37
) follows that

\|B^{\prime}(u_{0})(u_{l}-u_{*})\|_{-1,p}\to 0
for

. Therefore (

) implies that

\lim_{l\to\infty}\|A_{\varepsilon_{l}}u_{l}+B^{\prime}(u_{0})u_{*}\|_{-1,p}=0.

(3.38)

Now we use the following theorem, which is well-known in periodic homogenization theory for linear elliptic equations and systems with

-coefficients (see, e.g.
[
9
, Lemma 8.6]

[

Theorem 3.6

Let be given

and

and sequences

Then

Because of (
3.37
), (
3.38
) and Theorem

4
Nonlinear mixed boundary conditions

In this section we generalize the results of Theorem
1.1
to the case of boundary value problems of the type

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{x_{i}}\Big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(x/\varepsilon)\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)=b^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
\Big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(x/\varepsilon)\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)\nu_{i}(x)=b_{0}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in\Gamma,\\
u^{\alpha}(x)=0\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma.\end{array}\right\}\alpha=1,\ldots,n.

(4.1)

Here
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the outer unit normal vector field on the boundary
\partial\Omega
,
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto b_{0}(\cdot,u)\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{n})

-smooth.

The reasons, why similar to Theorem
1.1
results are true also for the boundary value problem (
4.1

First, Theorem
3.1
is true (in an appropriate reformulation) also for mixed boundary conditions. Second, in
(

)
we did not need that the test functions
\varphi
vanish on
\partial\Omega
. And third, in Theorem
3.6
no boundary conditions are imposed on the functions
\tilde{u}_{l}
.

We assume that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that the Robin condition boundary part
\Gamma

is a subset of
\partial\Omega
such that

either
\Gamma=\emptyset

or
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
or the relative boundary of

\Gamma
in
\partial\Omega
consists of finitely many points.

Our assumptions concerning
the diffusion coefficients
a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}
and the drift and reaction functiond
b_{i}^{\alpha}
and

are as in Section
1
with one exception: If
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
, i.e. in the case of pure Robin boundary conditions, in place of condition (

) we assume the stronger condition (

).

The homogenized diffusion coefficients
\hat{a}_{ij}
are as in
Section

, i.e. defined in
(

)
via the cell problems (

),
and the homogenized boundary value problem, corresponding to (

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{x_{i}}\Big(\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)=b^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
\Big(\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)\nu_{i}(x)=b^{\alpha}_{0}(x,u(x))\mbox{ for }x\in\Gamma,\\
u^{\alpha}(x)=0\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma.\end{array}\right\}\alpha=1,\ldots,n.

(4.2)

A vector function

is called weak solution to the boundary value problem (

) if it vanishes on

and if it satisfies the variational equation

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\Big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}(x/\varepsilon)\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\Big)\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi^{\alpha}(x)+b^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\varphi^{\alpha}(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}b_{0}^{\alpha}(x,u(x))\varphi^{\alpha}(x)d\mu(x)\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in W_{\Gamma}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{x_{i}}\Big(\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+\partial_{u^{\gamma}}b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u_{0}(x))u^{\gamma}(x)\Big)=\partial_{u^{\gamma}}b^{\alpha}(x,u_{0}(x))u^{\gamma}(x)\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega,\\
\Big(\hat{a}^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}u^{\beta}(x)+\partial_{u^{\gamma}}b_{i}^{\alpha}(x,u_{0}(x))u^{\gamma}(x)\Big)\nu_{i}(x)=\partial_{u^{\gamma}}b_{0}^{\alpha}(x,u_{0}(x))u^{\gamma}(x)\mbox{ for }x\in\Gamma,\\
u^{\alpha}(x)=0\mbox{ for }x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma\end{array}\right\}

(4.3)

for
\alpha=1,\ldots,n
. Similar to Theorem
1.1
, we get the following

Theorem 4.1

Let
u=u_{0}
be a weak solution to (
4.2

Remark 4.2

Let us draw attention to the following technical detail:

In Section
3
, i.e. in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, the reason for using the cut-off functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.21
) and (
3.34
) is that the approximate solutions
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
should satisfy the Dirichlet boundary conditions. There
the cut-off functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}
are not needed
to avoid
boundary integrals in (
3.29
) after partial integration, because there the test function
\varphi
could have compact support.

But now, in Section
4
, i.e. in the case of Robin boundary conditions, the reason for using the cut-off functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.21
) and (
3.34
) is to avoid boundary integrals in (
3.29
) after partial integration, because now the test function
\varphi
is not allowed to have compact support. Here the cut-off functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}

are not needed for
certain boundary condition to be satisfied (because the approximate solutions
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
are not obliged to satisfy any boundary conditions).

This technical detail is important because of the following: If it would be possible to improve the choices (
3.21
) and (
3.34
)
of the approximate solutions appropriately (for example, to avoid the use of cut-off functions or to use other smoothing operators), then, perhaps, it would be possible to prove better than (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) error estimates.

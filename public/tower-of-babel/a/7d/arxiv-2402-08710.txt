Averages of multiplicative functions along equidistributed sequences
S. Chan, P. Koymans, C. Pagano, E. Sofos

Abstract
. For a general family of non-negative functions matching upper and lower bounds are established for their average over the values of any equidistributed sequence.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11N37 (11A25, 11N56).

1.
Introduction

Averaging multiplicative functions over integer sequences has a long history in number theory.
Nair
[
6
]
studied the average over the values of an irreducible integer polynomial
and this was later greatly generalised by Nair–Tenenbaum
[
7
]

and Henriot
[
4
]
. When it comes to polynomials in two variables it was later extended to binary forms by
La Bretèche–Browning
[
1
]
and to principal ideals by Browning–Sofos
[
2
]
.

Wolke
[
9
]
had worked on averages of a multiplicative function
f\geqslant 0
over the values of an increasing integer sequence, i.e.

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,T]}f(c_{a}),

under the assumption that the sequence is ‘equi-distributed’ along arithmetic progressions. With an eye to
certain applications to arithmetic statistics and Diophantine equations we aim to study sums that are more general and under weaker assumptions
on equidistribution. Omitting certain details for now, we shall work with sums of the form

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}f(c_{a})\chi(c_{a}),

where
\mathcal{A}
is any countable set,

\chi:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty)
is any function of finite support,
c_{a}
is an integer sequence,
and

f
is a non-negative arithmetic function with certain multiplicative properties.
We will give upper bounds in Theorem
1.9
and matching lower bounds in Theorem
1.13
.

1.1.
The upper bound

We introduce the necessary notation for the statement of the upper bound.

Definition 1.1

(Density functions)
.

Fix
\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K>0
. We define
\mathcal{D}(\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K)
as the set of multiplicative functions

h:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
having the properties

•

for all
B<w<z
we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\ \mathrm{prime}\\
w\leqslant p<z\end{subarray}}(1-h(p))^{-1}\leqslant\left(\frac{\log z}{\log w}\right)^{\kappa}\left(1+\frac{K}{\log w}\right),

•

for every prime
p>B
and integers
e\geqslant 1
we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle h(p^{e})\leqslant\frac{B}{p},

•

for every prime
p
and
e\geqslant 1
we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle h(p^{e})\leqslant p^{-e\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}.

In order to state a result that is sufficiently general but easy to use we use the following set-up from
[
3
, §2.2]
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an infinite set and for each
T\geqslant 1
let
\chi_{T}:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty)
be any function for which

(1.4)

\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\chi_{T}(a)>0\}\ \textrm{ is finite for every }T\geqslant 1.

We also assume that

(1.5)

\lim_{T\to+\infty}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)=+\infty.

Assume that we are given a sequence of strictly positive integers

(c_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
indexed by
\mathcal{A}
and denoted by

\mathfrak{C}:=\{c_{a}:a\in\mathcal{A}\}.

We will be interested in estimating sums of the form

(1.6)

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)f(c_{a}),

where
f
is an arithmetic function
with the following properties:

Definition 1.2

(A class of functions)
.

Fix
A\geqslant 1,\epsilon>0,C>0
. The set
\mathcal{M}(A,\epsilon,C)
of functions

f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is defined by the property that for all coprime
m,n
one has

f(mn)\leqslant f(m)\min\{A^{\Omega(n)},Cn^{\epsilon}\}.

Example 1.3
.

If
c_{n}
is a sequence of positive integers then

\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant T}f(c_{n})

is of type (
1.6
) by taking

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{N}
and
\chi_{T}(n)=\mathds{1}_{[1,T]}(n)
.

Example 1.4
.

If
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is bounded and
Q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
an integer polynomial then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap T\mathcal{D}\\
Q(\mathbf{x})\neq 0\end{subarray}}f(|Q(\mathbf{x})|)

is of type (
1.6
) by taking

\mathcal{A}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{x})\neq 0\}
and
\chi_{T}(\mathbf{x})=\mathds{1}_{T\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{x})
.

Example 1.5
.

If
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are integer polynomials in
n
variables then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}\cap[-T,T])^{n}\\
Q_{1}(\mathbf{x})=0,Q_{2}(\mathbf{x})\neq 0\end{subarray}}f(|Q_{2}(\mathbf{x})|)

is of type (
1.6
) when

\chi_{T}(\mathbf{x})=\mathds{1}_{[0,T]}(\max|x_{i}|)
,

\mathcal{A}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:Q_{1}(\mathbf{x})=0,Q_{2}(\mathbf{x})\neq 0\}
.

We will need the following notion of ‘regular’ distribution of the values of the integer sequence
c_{a}
in arithmetic progressions. For a non-zero integer
d
and any
T\geqslant 1
, let

C_{d}(T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A}\\
c_{a}\equiv 0\left(\textnormal{mod}\ d\right)\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}(a).

Definition 1.6

(Equidistributed sequences)
.

We say that
\mathfrak{C}
is equidistributed if there exist
positive real numbers
\theta,\xi,\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K
with
\max\{\theta,\xi\}<1
,
a function
M:\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 1}

and a function
h_{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K)

such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle C_{d}(T)=h_{T}(d)M(T)\Bigg\{1+O\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\leqslant M(T)\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg\}+O(M(T)^{1-\xi})

for every
T\geqslant 1
and every
d\leqslant M(T)^{\theta}
, where the implied constants are independent of
d
and
T
.

It is worth emphasizing that in this definition the constants

\theta,\xi,\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K
are all assumed to be independent of
T
. For example, the bound
h_{T}(p^{e})=O(1/p)

in (
1.2
) holds with an implied constant that is independent of
e,p
as well as
T
.

From now on we shall abuse notation by writing
M
for
M(T)
.

Remark 1.7
.

The function
M(T)
can be chosen freely in any way that makes

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}(a)=M(T)\Bigg\{1+O\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\leqslant M(T)\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg\}+O(M(T)^{1-\xi})

hold. It particular, it is necessary that it satisfies

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{M(T)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{A}\end{subarray}}\chi_{T}(a)=1.

One could simply take

M(T):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)
, however, in certain applications it is helpful
to have the freedom to choose instead a smooth approximation to
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)
as a function of
T
.

Example 1.8
.

In the setting of Example
1.3
define
c_{n}=n
. Then

C_{d}(T)=\sharp\{1\leqslant n\leqslant T:d\mid n\}=\frac{T}{d}+O(1),

thus, one can choose
h_{T}(d)=1/d
,
M(T)=T
and
\xi=9/10
. It is important to note that the choice of
M(T)
and
\xi
is not unique: one may, for example, alternatively take
M(T)=T+T^{0.4}
and
\xi=1/2
.

We are now ready to state the main upper bound of this paper.

Theorem 1.9

(The upper bound)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an infinite set and for each
T\geqslant 1
define
\chi_{T}:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty)
to be any function such that both (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) hold.
Take a sequence of strictly positive integers
\mathfrak{C}=(c_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{C}
is equidistributed with respect to some positive constants
\theta,\xi,\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K
and functions

M(T)
and
h_{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\kappa,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},B,K)

as in Definition
1.6
.
Fix any
A>1
and assume that
f
is a function
such that for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
C>0
for which

f\in\mathcal{M}(A,\epsilon,C)
, which is introduced in Definition
1.2
.
Assume that there exists
\alpha>0
and
\widetilde{B}>0
such that for all
T\geqslant 1
one has

(1.8)

\sup\{c_{a}:a\in\mathcal{A},\chi_{T}(a)>0\}\leqslant\widetilde{B}M^{\alpha},

where
M=M(T)
is as in Definition
1.6
.
Then for all
T\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)f(c_{a})\ll M\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\leqslant M\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant M\end{subarray}}f(a)h_{T}(a),

where the implied constant is allowed to depend
on
\alpha,A,B,\widetilde{B},\theta,\xi,K,\kappa
,
\lambda_{i}
, the function
f
and the implied constants in (
1.7
),
but is independent of
T
and
M
.

Remark 1.10

(Wolke’s density function assumption)
.

Note that
[
9
, Assumption
{(\textrm{A}_{2})}
]
states that there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}

such that for all
e\geqslant 1
and primes
p
one has

h_{T}(p^{e})\leqslant C_{1}e^{C_{2}}p^{-e}
. We replace this with (
1.3
) which is a lighter assumption for large
e
.
This is of high significance in applications
where
c_{a}
is the sequence of values obtained by a multivariable polynomial,
as in this case
h_{T}(p^{e})
is the density of zeros modulo
p^{e}
and one cannot hope for a bound with
\lambda_{1}\geqslant 1
.

Remark 1.11

(Wolke’s level of distribution assumption)
.

Let us
comment that
[
9
, Assumption
{(\textrm{A}_{4})}
]
implies that

C_{1}(T)-h_{T}(1)M\ll\frac{M}{(\log M)^{D_{1}}}

holds for every positive fixed constant
D_{1}
, i.e. it demands an arbitrary logarithmic saving.
Our assumption in Definition
1.6
is lighter in the sense that it
essentially only requires this for a fixed power of
\log M
.
To see this, note that when
d=1
, Definition
1.6
states that

C_{1}(T)-h_{T}(1)M\ll M\prod_{B<p\leqslant M}(1-h_{T}(p))^{2}+M^{1-\xi}.

In typical applications this is of size
M/(\log M)^{\kappa}
, where
\kappa
is as in (
1.1
).

Remark 1.12

(Wolke’s growth assumption)
.

Let us note that Wolke assumes that
the function
f
is multiplicative, which is relaxed in our work by demanding that it is submultiplicative as in
Definition
1.2
. Furthermore,
[
9
, Assumption
{(\textrm{F}_{1})}
]
states that
f(p^{e})
is only allowed to grow polynomially in
e

for a fixed prime
p
, whereas, Definition
1.2

relaxes this by assuming that
f(p^{e})
is allowed to grow subexponentially in
e
.

1.2.
The lower bound

We shall see that if
f
is not too close to
0
, then matching lower bounds hold.
This is a generalization of the work of Wolke
[
9
, Satz 2]
, where the main difference lies in the fact that the density functions in
Definition
1.1
are now allowed to grow with larger freedom. Furthermore, Wolke’s condition that
f(p^{m})\geqslant C_{0}^{m}
for some strictly positive real constant
C_{0}
is replaced by the more general condition (
1.9
).

Theorem 1.13

(The lower bound)
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of Theorem
1.9
.
Assume, in addition, that
f:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
is a multiplicative function for which

(1.9)

\mathrm{for\ each}\ L\geqslant 1\ \mathrm{one\ has}\ \inf\{f(m):\Omega(m)\leqslant L\}>0

and that the error term in Definition
1.6
satisfies

C_{d}(T)=h_{T}(d)M(T)\left\{1+o_{T\to\infty}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\leqslant M(T)\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{2}\right)\right\}+O(M(T)^{1-\xi})

whevever
d\leqslant M(T)^{\theta}
. Then for all
T\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)f(c_{a})\gg M(T)\prod_{p\leqslant M(T)}(1-h_{T}(p))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant M(T)\end{subarray}}f(a)h_{T}(a),

where the implied constants are
independent of
T
and
M
.

.

For a non-zero integer
m
define

\Omega(m):=\sum_{p\mid m}v_{p}(m),

where
v_{p}
is the standard

and

1
.
Throughout the paper we shall also make use of the convention that when iterated logarithm functions
\log t,\log\log t
, etc., are used, the real variable
t
is assumed to be sufficiently large to make the iterated logarithm well-defined.

The work of
[
8
, Lemma 1]
gives an upper bound on the density of integers all of whose prime factors are relatively small.
We shall need a variation of this result where the integers are weighted by a multiplicative function. In the applications it will be important that the bound
is of the form
O(x^{o(1)}z^{-c})
for some positive constant
c
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix any positive real numbers
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
and assume that
F:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
is a multiplicative function such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle F(p^{e})\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{c_{0}}{p},\frac{p^{c_{1}}}{p^{ec_{2}}}\right\}

for all primes
p
and
e\geqslant 1
. Define

c:=\min\left\{\frac{c_{2}}{2},\frac{1}{1+[2c_{1}/c_{2}]}\right\}\ \textrm{ and }\ c^{\prime}:=\frac{c+2(c_{0}+c)}{c}.

Then for all
x,z\geqslant 2
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\cap(z,x]\\
p\mid n\Rightarrow p\leqslant(\log x)(\log\log x)\end{subarray}}F(n)\ll z^{-c}\exp\left(\frac{c^{\prime}\log x}{(\log\log x)^{1/2}}\right),

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

Let
c_{4}
be a positive constant that will be optimised later. Then the sum over
n
is

\leqslant\frac{1}{z^{c_{4}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
p\mid n\Rightarrow p\leqslant y\end{subarray}}F(n)n^{c_{4}},

where
y=(\log x)(\log\log x)
. By Rankin’s trick we get the following bound for any
\delta>0
:

\leqslant\frac{x^{\delta}}{z^{c_{4}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
p\mid n\Rightarrow p\leqslant y\end{subarray}}F(n)\frac{n^{c_{4}}}{n^{\delta}}=\frac{x^{\delta}}{z^{c_{4}}}\prod_{p\leqslant y}\left(1+\sum_{e\geqslant 1}F(p^{e})p^{e(c_{4}-\delta)}\right).

For an auxiliary positive integer
e_{0}
we shall control the contribution of the
range
e\leqslant e_{0}
and
e>e_{0}
using the bounds
F(p^{e})\leqslant c_{0}/p
and

F(p^{e})\leqslant p^{c_{1}-ec_{2}}
respectively. Assume that

c_{4}\geqslant\delta
so that the contribution of the former range contributes

\leqslant 1+\sum_{e=1}^{e_{0}}\frac{c_{0}}{p}\frac{p^{ec_{4}}}{p^{e\delta}}\leqslant 1+c_{0}e_{0}p^{e_{0}(c_{4}-\delta)-1}.

Now assume that
c_{4}e_{0}\leqslant 1

so that the bound becomes

\leqslant 1+\frac{c_{0}}{c_{4}}p^{-\delta e_{0}}\leqslant 1+\frac{c_{0}}{c_{4}}p^{-\delta}\leqslant 1+\frac{c_{0}}{c_{4}}\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}.

The remaining range contributes

\leqslant p^{c_{1}}\sum_{e\geqslant 1+e_{0}}p^{e(c_{4}-\delta-c_{2})}.

Making the additional assumption that
c_{4}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}c_{2}
we can bound this by

\leqslant p^{c_{1}}\sum_{e\geqslant 1+e_{0}}p^{-e(\delta+c_{2}/2)}\leqslant\frac{p^{c_{1}}}{p^{e_{0}(\delta+c_{2}/2)}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j(\delta+c_{2}/2)}}\leqslant\frac{p^{c_{1}}}{p^{e_{0}c_{2}/2}}\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}.

Further assuming that
2c_{1}\leqslant e_{0}c_{2}
shows that this is
\leqslant\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}
.
Putting the bounds together leads to

1+\sum_{e\geqslant 1}F(p^{e})p^{e(c_{4}-\delta)}\leqslant 1+\frac{c_{0}+c_{4}}{c_{4}}\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1},

subject to the conditions

\delta\leqslant c_{4},c_{4}e_{0}\leqslant 1,c_{4}\leqslant\frac{c_{2}}{2},\frac{2c_{1}}{c_{2}}\leqslant e_{0}.

Putting
e_{0}=1+[2c_{1}/c_{2}]
shows that
these conditions are met for any
\delta\in(0,c_{4})
where

c_{4}:=\min\left\{\frac{c_{2}}{2},\frac{1}{1+[2c_{1}/c_{2}]}\right\}=c.

Hence, the overall bound becomes

\frac{x^{\delta}}{z^{c_{4}}}\prod_{p\leqslant y}\left(1+\frac{c_{0}+c_{4}}{c_{4}}\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}\right)\leqslant\frac{x^{\delta}}{z^{c_{4}}}\prod_{p\leqslant y}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}\right)^{\hskip-2.84544pt\frac{c_{0}+c_{4}}{c_{4}}}\hskip-8.5359pt\leqslant z^{-c_{4}}\exp\left(\delta(\log x)+\frac{c_{0}+c_{4}}{c_{4}}T\right),

where

T:=\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{p^{\delta}-1}\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{p\leqslant y}\frac{1}{\log p}\leqslant\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{2y}{(\log y)^{2}},

owing to the inequality
\mathrm{e}^{t}-1\geqslant t
with
t=\delta\log p
and the prime number theorem.
Putting
\delta=(\log\log x)^{-1/2}
we see that when
y=(\log x)(\log\log x)
one gets the bound

z^{-c_{4}}\exp\left(\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{1/2}}+\frac{(c_{0}+c_{4})}{c_{4}}(\log\log x)^{1/2}\frac{2(\log x)(\log\log x)}{(\log\log x)^{2}}\right),

which is sufficient. ∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Keep the setting of Lemma
2.1

and fix any
\beta_{0}>0
. For all
T\geqslant 2
with
\log T>4\beta_{0}/c_{2}
, for all
A>1,c\in\mathbb{N}
, and for any
\beta>0
with

\beta\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{c_{2}}{2},\frac{\beta_{0}}{\log T}\right\},

the product

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant T\\
p\nmid c\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geqslant 1\\
j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\min\{C^{\prime}p^{(i+j)c_{2}/2},A^{i+j}\}F(p^{i+j})(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\Big(\frac{\mathds{1}[p>c_{0}]}{(1-F(p))}+\mathds{1}[p\leqslant c_{0}]\Big)\Big)

is
O(\mathrm{e}^{\nu\beta\log T})
, where
\nu
is a positive constant that depends at most on
\beta_{0}
,
c_{i}
and
A
. Furthermore, the implied constant depends at most on

A,C^{\prime},c_{0},c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

Proof.

Define
p_{0}
to be the least prime satisfying
2A\leqslant p_{0}^{c_{2}/4}
. We will bound the sum over
i,j
for every individual
prime
p\geqslant p_{0}
and in the end we shall piece the bounds together for all primes
p\leqslant T
.

Step (1).

We start with the contribution of large
i
, in which case the bound

F(p^{e})\leqslant p^{c_{1}-ec_{2}}
and the crude estimate

p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)}\leqslant p^{\beta i}
will suffice.
Define

i_{1}:=1+\left[\frac{4(5+c_{1})}{c_{2}}\right].

The contribution of
i\geqslant i_{1}
is

\leqslant\sum_{i\geqslant i_{1}}A^{i}p^{\beta i}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}A^{j}F(p^{i+j})\leqslant p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant i_{1}}A^{i}p^{(\beta-c_{2})i}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}(Ap^{-c_{2}})^{j}\leqslant 2p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant i_{1}}(Ap^{(\beta-c_{2})})^{i}

because
Ap^{-c_{2}}\leqslant 1/2
, a fact that follows from

p\geqslant p_{0}
. Now we use the assumptions

\beta\leqslant c_{2}/2
and
2A\leqslant p_{0}^{c_{2}/4}\leqslant p^{c_{2}/4}
to see that

Ap^{(\beta-c_{2})}\leqslant Ap^{-c_{2}/2}\leqslant 1/2
.
Hence,

2p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant i_{1}}(Ap^{(\beta-c_{2})})^{i}\leqslant 4p^{c_{1}}(Ap^{(\beta-c_{2})})^{i_{1}}\leqslant 4p^{c_{1}-i_{1}c_{2}/4}\leqslant p^{2+c_{1}-i_{1}c_{2}/4}.

This is
\leqslant p^{-3}
because our choice for
i_{1}
makes sure that

5+c_{1}\leqslant i_{1}c_{2}/4.
We have thus shown that for all
p\geqslant p_{0}
one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geqslant i_{1}\\
j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}A^{i+j}F(p^{i+j})(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant p^{-3}.

Step (2).
Let us now bound the contribution of the
i,j
that satisfy

1\leqslant i<i_{1}\ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ i+j\geqslant i_{1}.

We have

\sum_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}\sum_{j\geqslant i_{1}-i}A^{i+j}F(p^{i+j})(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}A^{i}p^{-ic_{2}}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\sum_{j\geqslant i_{1}-i}(Ap^{-c_{2}})^{j}.

Using the inequality
Ap^{-c_{2}}\leqslant 1/2
to bound the sum over
j
results in the inequality

\leqslant 2p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}A^{i}p^{-ic_{2}}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})(Ap^{-c_{2}})^{i_{1}-i}=2p^{c_{1}}A^{i_{1}}p^{-c_{2}{i_{1}}}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)}),

which is at most
2p^{c_{1}}(Ap^{(\beta-c_{2})})^{i_{1}}

that has been previously shown to be at most
\leqslant 1/p^{3}
.

We have thus proved that for all
p\geqslant p_{0}
one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i<i_{1}\\
j\geqslant i_{1}-i\end{subarray}}A^{i+j}F(p^{i+j})(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant p^{-3}.

Step (3).

It remains to study the contribution of cases with

i+j<i_{1}
. For these we use the assumption
F(p^{e})\leqslant c_{0}/p
that leads to the bound

\leqslant\frac{c_{0}}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geqslant 1,j\geqslant 0\\
i+j<i_{1}\end{subarray}}A^{i+j}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant\frac{c_{0}}{p}\sum_{1\leqslant i<i_{1}}(2A)^{i}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant j<i_{1}-i\end{subarray}}(2A)^{j}.

Now since
A>1
we have
2A>2
. For all
m\geqslant 1
we have

1+(2A)+(2A)^{2}+\ldots+(2A)^{m-1}\leqslant\frac{(2A)^{m}}{2A-1}\leqslant(2A)^{m}.

This gives the bound

\leqslant\frac{c_{0}}{p}\sum_{1\leqslant i<i_{1}}(2A)^{i}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})(2A)^{i_{1}-i}=\frac{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}}{p}\sum_{1\leqslant i<i_{1}}(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant\frac{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}}{p}(p^{\beta i_{1}}-1).

The assumption

\beta\log T\leqslant\beta_{0}

shows that
\beta i_{1}\log T\leqslant\beta_{0}(1+\frac{4(5+c_{1})}{c_{2}})
, hence, for primes
p\leqslant T
we infer
\beta i_{1}\log p\leqslant\beta_{0}(1+\frac{4(5+c_{1})}{c_{2}})
.
On the other hand, the function
(-1+\mathrm{e}^{t})/t
is bounded in the interval

0\leqslant t\leqslant\beta_{0}(1+\frac{4(5+c_{1})}{c_{2}})
, thus,

p^{\beta i_{1}}-1=\exp((\log p)\beta i_{1})-1\leqslant\beta_{1}(\log p)\beta i_{1},

for a positive
constant
\beta_{1}
that depends on
\beta_{0}
and
c_{1},c_{2}
. Thus,
the contribution of cases with
i+j<i_{1}
is

\leqslant\frac{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}}{p}(p^{\beta i_{1}}-1)\leqslant\left\{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\beta i_{1}\right\}\frac{\log p}{p}.

In conclusion, we saw that for all primes
p\in(p_{0},T]
one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geqslant 1\\
j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}A^{i+j}F(p^{i+j})(p^{\beta i}-p^{\beta(i-1)})\leqslant 2p^{-3}+\left\{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\beta i_{1}\right\}\frac{\log p}{p}.

Step (4).
Using the last inequality with the bound

1+x_{p}\leqslant\exp(x_{p})
, valid for all
x_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
,
shows that, once restricted in the range
p>\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}
,
the product in the lemma is

\leqslant\exp\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}<p\leqslant T\\
p\nmid c\end{subarray}}(1-F(p))^{-1}\Big(2p^{-3}+\left\{c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\beta i_{1}\right\}\frac{\log p}{p}\Big)\Big).

Ignoring the condition
p\nmid c
will produce a larger bound.
Using the inequality
F(p)\leqslant c_{0}/p

we obtain

\ll\exp\Big(c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\beta i_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}<p\leqslant T\end{subarray}}(1-F(p))^{-1}\frac{\log p}{p}\Big),

where the implied constant depends at most on
c_{0}
. Using the inequality
F(p)\leqslant c_{0}/p

and the estimate
\sum_{p\leqslant y}(\log p)/p\ll\log y
leads to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}<p\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{(1-F(p))p}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}<p\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}\left(1+O_{c_{0}}\left(\frac{c_{0}}{p}\right)\right)\ll O_{c_{0}}(1)+\log T,

where the implied constant is absolute. Hence, the previous bound becomes

\ll_{c_{0}}\exp\Big(c_{0}(2A)^{i_{1}}\beta_{1}\beta i_{1}\log T\Big).

Since
i_{1}
is a function of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

we can thus write the bound as
\ll_{c_{0}}\exp(\nu\beta\log T)
for some
\nu=\nu(\beta_{0},c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},A)
.
To conclude the proof of the lemma
we must deal with the contribution of the primes
p\leqslant\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}
. Note that for every prime
p
the corresponding factor in the product of the lemma is

\leqslant 1+\Big(\frac{1}{1-F(p)}+1\Big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geqslant 1\\
j\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\min\{C^{\prime}p^{(i+j)c_{2}/2},A^{i+j}\}F(p^{i+j})p^{\beta i}.

Using the bound for
F
in the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
and the bound
\beta\leqslant\beta_{0}/\log T
we see that
the sum over
i,j
is at most

C^{\prime}p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant 1}p^{(-c_{2}/2+\beta_{0}/\log T)i}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}p^{-jc_{2}/2}.

Our assumption
4\beta_{0}/c_{2}<\log T
ensures that
\beta_{0}/\log T<c_{2}/4
, hence, we obtain the bound

C^{\prime}p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant 1}p^{-ic_{2}/4}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}p^{-jc_{2}/2}\leqslant C^{\prime}p^{c_{1}}\sum_{i\geqslant 1}2^{-ic_{2}/4}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{-jc_{2}/2}=O_{c_{2}}(C^{\prime}p^{c_{1}}).

Taking the product of this quantity over all primes
p\leqslant\max\{p_{0},c_{0}\}
gives an implied constant
that depends on
p_{0},c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
and
C^{\prime}
. Since
p_{0}=p_{0}(A,c_{2})
we see that the implied constant also depends on
A
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix any positive constants
C,C^{\prime},\epsilon
and assume that we are given a function
G:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)

such that for all coprime positive integers
a,b
one has

G(ab)\leqslant G(a)\min\{C^{\Omega(b)},C^{\prime}b^{\epsilon}\}.

Then for all coprime positive integers
a,b
we have
G(ab)\leqslant G(a)H(b)
, where
H
is the multiplicative function
defined as
H(p^{e})=\min\{C^{e},C^{\prime}p^{\epsilon e}\}
for all
e\geqslant 1
and primes
p
.

Proof.

We will prove this with induction on
\omega(b)
. When
\omega(b)=0
then
b=1
, hence, the statement clearly holds.
Assume that
k\geqslant 0
and that the statement holds for all
b\in\mathbb{N}
with
\omega(b)=k
.
Now let
n,n^{\prime}
be coprime and assume that
\omega(n)=k+1
. We shall show that
G(n^{\prime}n)\leqslant G(n^{\prime})H(n)
.
Writing
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}p_{k+1}^{\alpha_{k+1}}
where each
\alpha_{i}
is strictly positive and the
p_{i}
are
distinct primes, we let
a=n^{\prime}p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
and
b=p_{k+1}^{\alpha_{k+1}}
so that

G(n^{\prime}n)=G(ab)\leqslant G(a)H(p_{k+1}^{\alpha_{k+1}})

by assumption. Now
a
can be written as
n^{\prime}
multiplied by an integer that is coprime to
n^{\prime}

and with exactly
k
distinct prime factors, thus, our inductive hypothesis
shows that

G(a)\leqslant G(n^{\prime})\prod_{i=1}^{k}H(p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}).

Combining the two inequalities gives

G(n^{\prime}n)\leqslant G(n^{\prime})\prod_{i\leqslant k+1}H(p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})=G(n^{\prime})H(n)
.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

2.1
, fix any

\beta_{0}
. For any

\varpi>0

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>\Upsilon\\

Lemma 2.5
.

\nu_{1}/p
, where

.

\hskip-11.38092pt\prod_{p\in(V^{\epsilon},V)}\!\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{C^{j}F(p^{j})}{1-F(p)}\right)\leqslant\prod_{p\in(V^{\epsilon},V)}\!\left(1+\frac{\nu_{1}/p}{1-c_{0}/p}\right)\ll\prod_{p\in(V^{\epsilon},V)}\!\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\nu_{1}}\!\!\ll\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\nu_{1}}},

.

) to bound

) and noting that the sum over

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F
be as in Lemma
2.1
and
G
be as in Lemma
2.4
.

\gamma
and assume that for every prime
p

Then for all
T\geqslant 1

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant T\end{subarray}}F(a)G(a)\prod_{p\mid a}(1+c(p))\leqslant 2^{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant T\end{subarray}}F(a)G(a),

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid a\end{subarray}}(1+c(p))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid a\end{subarray}}\mu(d)^{2}c(d).

This turns the sum in the lemma into

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leqslant T\end{subarray}}F(a)G(a)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid a\end{subarray}}\mu(d)^{2}c(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}bd\leqslant T\end{subarray}}\mu(d)^{2}c(d)F(bd)G(bd).

By assumption there exists
C^{\prime}
such that
G(ab)\leqslant G(a)\min\{C^{\Omega(b)},C^{\prime}b^{c_{2}/2}\}
.
By Lemma
2.3
with
\epsilon=c_{2}/2

G(n^{\prime}n)\leqslant G(n^{\prime})H(n)
for all coprime
n,n^{\prime}
, where

H
is the multiplicative function given by
H(p^{e})=\min\{C^{e},C^{\prime}p^{ec_{2}/2}\}
for

and primes
p
.
We factor

hence, the sum is

with
\alpha_{1}=B
,
x^{\alpha_{2}}=z=M^{v}
,
x^{\alpha_{3}}=M
and
a=d
,
and taking advantage of the fact that
v\leqslant 1
, we infer that

\ll\prod_{B<p\mid d}(1+h_{T}(p))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}M^{v}<p<M\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p<M^{v}\\
p\nmid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p)),

that is at most

\ll\prod_{B<p\mid d}(1-h_{T}(p))^{-2}\prod_{B<p<M}(1-h_{T}(p)).

To treat the main term
sum
\sum_{m\mid\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{m}^{-}h_{T}(m)
we use
[
5
, Equation (6.40)]
, which is
a more precise version of
[
5
, Equation (6.48)]
in the case of
\lambda_{m}^{-}
. Specifically,
[
5
, Equation (6.40)]
states that

\sum_{m\mid\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{m}^{-}h_{T}(m)>(1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta-s}K^{10})\prod_{p\leqslant z}(1-h_{T}(p)),

where
\beta=1+9\kappa
and
s=(\log y)/(\log z)
.
In our case one has
s=\epsilon\theta/v
and a simple calculation shows that our definition of
v
ensures that
1-\mathrm{e}^{\beta-s}K^{10}\geqslant 1/2
, thus,

\sum_{m\mid\mathcal{P}}\lambda_{m}^{-}h_{T}(m)\gg\prod_{p\leqslant z}(1-h_{T}(p)).

Injecting our estimates in (
4.1
) gives

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)f(c_{a})\gg M\prod_{p\leqslant M^{v}}(1-h_{T}(p))\sum_{d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d)+o(M\mathcal{T})+O(M^{1-\xi/2}),

where

\mathcal{T}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\leqslant M^{v}\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\mid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{-2}.

Letting
c(p)=(1-h_{T}(p))^{-2}-1
and applying
Lemma
2.7
we obtain

\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}B<p\mid d\end{subarray}}(1-h_{T}(p))^{-2}\ll\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d).

This leads to

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\chi_{T}(a)f(c_{a})\gg M\prod_{p\leqslant M^{v}}(1-h_{T}(p))\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d)+O(M^{1-\xi/2}).

Since
h_{T}(p)\in[0,1)
for
p>M^{v}
and using that
v\leqslant 1
,
the product over
p\leqslant M^{v}
is at least
\prod_{p\leqslant M}(1-h_{T}(p))
. It thus remains to prove

\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M^{v_{0}}}f(d)h_{T}(d)\gg\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M}f(d)h_{T}(d).

Using the fact that
f
and
h_{T}
are both multiplicative we can write

\sum_{1\leqslant d\leqslant M}f(d)h_{T}(d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant b\leqslant M\\
P^{+}(b)\leqslant M^{v_{0}}\end{subarray}}f(b)h_{T}(b)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant M/b\\
P^{-}(c)>M^{v_{0}}\end{subarray}}f(c)h_{T}(c)

and it suffices to prove that the sum
over
c
is bounded independently of
M
.
We apply Lemma
2.11
to get the upper
bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant c\leqslant M/b\\
P^{-}(c)>M^{v_{0}}\end{subarray}}f(c)h_{T}(c)\ll\exp\left(\sum_{M^{v_{0}}<p\leqslant M}f(p)h_{T}(p)\right).

Recall that
f(p)\leqslant A
and
h_{T}(p)\leqslant B/p
, so the sum over
p
is

\ll\sum_{M^{v_{0}}<p\leqslant M}\frac{1}{p}=O(1),

thus, concluding the proof.

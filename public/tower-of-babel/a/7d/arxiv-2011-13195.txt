Average rank of quadratic twists with a rational point of almost minimal height
Joachim Petit

Abstract
. Given a family of quadratic twists of a fixed elliptic curve defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , we investigate the average rank in the subfamily of twists having a nontorsion rational point of almost minimal height. We show in particular that the average analytic rank is greater than one.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11D45, 11G05, 11G50

1.
Introduction

Fix
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
4A^{3}+27B^{2}\neq 0
.
Let
E
denote the elliptic curve defined over the rationals by the equation

E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

and for every square-free integer
d
, let
E_{d}
be the quadratic twist of
E
defined over the rationals by the equation

E_{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}dy^{2}=x^{+}Ax+B.

Write
\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})
for the analytic rank of
E_{d}
, that is the order of vanishing of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function
L(E_{d},s)
at
s=1
, and
\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))
for the algebraic rank of
E_{d}
, which is the rank of the finitely generated abelian group
E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})
.
Recall that the Parity Conjecture asserts that

\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})\equiv\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\bmod 2,

while the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture states that the two ranks are actually equal.
For
X\geq 1
, denote the set of square-free integers up to
X
by

\mathcal{S}(X)=\{1\leq d\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mu(d)\neq 0\}.

It has been conjectured by Goldfeld
[
Gol79
]
that the average analytic rank of quadratic twists, when ordered by the size of
d
, is exactly one half.

Conjecture 1

(Goldfeld)
.

Let
\nu\in\{0,1\}
. One has

\#\{d\in\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})=\nu\}\sim\frac{1}{2}\#\mathcal{S}(X),

as
X\to\infty
.

Note that the same result is conjectured to hold with the algebraic rank replacing the analytic rank as a consequence of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture.

Conjecture
1
has been studied extensively for curves
E
having full rational
2
-torsion, starting with the work of Heath-Brown
[
HB93
]
,
[
HB94
]
on the congruent number curve
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}=x^{3}-x
.
These results were later extended by Kane
[
Kan13
]
to any curve
E
with full rational 2-torsion having no rational cyclic subgroup of order four.
For such curves, Smith
[
Smi17
]
has recently shown that

\#\{1\leq d\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 2\}=o(X),

achieving significant progress towards Conjecture
1
for the algebraic rank.
Building upon this result, it was proven by Kriz
[
Kri20
]
that Conjecture
1
holds under the additional assumption that
E
has complex multiplication by
\mathbb{Q}(i)
or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
.

We also mention that the prediction of Conjecture
1
has since been extended, following the heuristics of Katz and Sarnak
[
KS99
]
, to state that amongst all elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, the density of rank zero curves and the density of rank one curves are both one half.
This broader version of the conjecture is supported by the recent work of Bhargava and Shankar
[
BS15a
]
,
[
BS15b
]
,
[
BS13a
]
,
[
BS13b
]
.

Le Boudec
[
LB18
]
has recently investigated the analogue of Conjecture
1
when the twists are ordered using a quantity coming from the geometry of their Mordell–Weil lattice.
Recall that each twist
E_{d}
comes equipped with a canonical height function
\hat{h}_{E_{d}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
(see Section
2
for its definition). Following the work of Le Boudec
[
LB16
]
, we consider the quantity
\eta_{d}(A,B)
defined through

(1.1)

\log\eta_{d}(A,B)=\min\{\hat{h}_{E_{d}}(P)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P\in E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})\setminus E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})_{\tors}\},

if
\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 1
, and by
\eta_{d}(A,B)=\infty
if
\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))=0
.
Le Boudec
[
LB18
]
showed that when the twists are ordered by the size of
\eta_{d}(A,B)
, then the average analytic rank is larger than one, and under the Parity Conjecture the same holds for the algebraic rank.

Remark that if
\eta_{d}(A,B)<\infty
, then
\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 1
which, by the work of Kolyvagin
[
Kol88
]
and Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor
[
BCDT01
]
, implies that
\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})\geq 1
.
Thus, given the prediction of Conjecture
1
, one might have expected the average rank to be one.

The goal of the present work is to provide a further example of a subfamily of the family of all quadratic twists for which the average analytic rank is also greater than one.
The quantity
\eta_{d}(A,B)
defined in (
1.1
) satisfies the sharp lower bound (see for instance
[
LB16
, Section 2.2]
)

\eta_{d}(A,B)\gg d^{1/8},

and we are interested in the quadratic twists for which this bound is almost attained.
For
\alpha>0
, we define the set

\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)=\{d\in\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\eta_{d}(A,B)\leq d^{1/8+\alpha}\}.

We note that the author
[
Pet20
, Theorem 1]
has recently established an asymptotic formula for the cardinality of this set when
\alpha\in(0,1/120)
. Our main result investigates the average analytic rank of the quadratic twists of
E
as
d
runs over
\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/120)
and assume that the polynomial
x^{3}+Ax+B
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Z}
. One has

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})>1.

We have decided to restrict ourselves to the case of an irreducible polynomial in order to keep the proof concise, but we expect our method to be robust enough to handle the general case.

For a prime number
p
, let
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d})
be the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group of
E_{d}
. There is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d})\longrightarrow\Sha(E_{d})[p^{\infty}]\longrightarrow 0,

where
\Sha(E_{d})
is the Tate–Shafarevich group of
E_{d}
and
\Sha(E_{d})[p^{\infty}]
is its
p
-primary part.
Let
\corank(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d}))
be the number of copies of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d})
. The above short exact sequence implies the inequality

(1.2)

\corank(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d}))\geq\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})),

while the Tate–Shafarevich Conjecture implies that these quantities are actually equal.
By (
1.2
), we have
\corank(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d}))\geq 1
for all
d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
. Moreover, the
p
-Parity Theorem of Dokchitser and Dokchitser
[
DD10
, Theorem 1.4]
states that
\corank(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d}))
and
\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})
share the same parity.

During the proof of Theorem
1
, we actually show that there exists a positive proportion of twists with analytic rank even and at least two, which implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, one has

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\corank(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E_{d}))>1.

In addition, we also clearly obtain the following conditional result about the average algebraic rank.

Corollary 3
.

Assume the Parity Conjecture. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, one has

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}\rank(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))>1.

Organisation of the paper

The proof of Theorem
1
follows the strategy of Le Boudec
[
LB18
]
and is the subject of Section
2
, where it is realised assuming the validity of Propositions
4
and
5
.
The purpose of Section
3
is to establish Proposition
4
, using a result of Salberger
[

based on the determinant method and which improves on the celebrated work of Heath-Brown
[

]
.
In Section

2.
The proof of Theorem
1

Throughout this paper, we view
A
,
B
and
\alpha
as fixed and every implicit constant may depend on all of them.
Any other dependence is specified.

We begin by recalling the definitions of various height functions. Let
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
denote the logarithmic Weil height and let
h_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be defined by

h_{x}(x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z)=h(x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z)

if
(x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z)\neq(0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0)
, and
h_{x}(0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0)=0
.
The canonical height
\hat{h}_{E_{d}}
on
E_{d}
is defined for
P\in E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})
by

\hat{h}_{E_{d}}(P)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}}h_{x}(2^{n}P).

One can show (see for instance
[
LB16
, Lemma 3]
) the existence of two constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending only on
A
and
B
such that for every
P\in E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})
, one has

(2.1)

c_{1}<\hat{h}_{E_{d}}(P)-\frac{1}{2}h_{x}(P)<c_{2}.

Write

(2.2)

F(x,z)=x^{3}+Axz^{2}+Bz^{3},

and recall that, under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, this polynomial is irreducible over the integers.
We introduce the polynomial

(2.3)

Q(u,v,w)=vF(u,vw^{2}).

For
(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{3}
satisfying
Q(u,v,w)\geq 1
and
\mu(Q(u,v,w))\neq 0
, the point
P
with coordinates
(uvw\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v^{2}w^{3})
lies in
E_{Q(u,v,w)}(\mathbb{Q})
. The square-free assumption on
Q(u,v,w)
ensures that
\gcd(u,vw)=1
, which implies
h_{x}(P)=\log\max\{u,vw^{2}\}
. Thus, if
\max\{u,vw^{2}\}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-2c_{1}}
, the first inequality in (
2.1
) implies
\hat{h}_{E_{d}}(P)\neq 0
, so
P
is not a torsion point. In this case, we have

\log\eta_{d}(A,B)\leq\hat{h}_{E_{d}}(P)<\frac{1}{2}h_{x}(P)+c_{2},

which leads to

\eta_{d}(A,B)<\mathrm{e}^{c_{2}}\max\{u,vw^{2}\}^{1/2}.

We introduce the region

(2.4)

\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)=\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrm{e}^{-2c_{1}}\leq\max\{u,vw^{2}\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2c_{2}}Q(u,v,w)^{1/4+2\alpha}\},

and define the number of representations

(2.5)

r_{Q}(\alpha;d)=\#\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Q(u,v,w)=d\}.

By the above discussion, if
d
is square-free, the condition
r_{Q}(\alpha;d)\geq 1
ensures
\eta_{d}(A,B)\leq d^{1/8+\alpha}
. In other words, the set

(2.6)

\mathcal{Q}(\alpha;X)=\{d\in\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}r_{Q}(\alpha;d)\geq 1\}

is a subset of
\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
.

Let
\omega(E_{d})
denote the sign of the functional equation of
L(E_{d},s)
. As mentioned in Section
1
, the analytic rank of
E_{d}
is at least one for
d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
, so that
\rank_{\mathrm{an}}(E_{d})
is at least 2 and even if and only if
\omega(E_{d})=1
.
For
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
, we define

\Omega_{\nu}(\alpha;X)=\frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega(E_{d})=\nu\}}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}.

To prove Theorem
1
, we establish that for
\alpha\in(0,1/120)
and
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
, one has

(2.7)

\liminf_{X\to\infty}\Omega_{\nu}(\alpha;X)>0.

Letting
d
range over the set
\mathcal{Q}(\alpha;X)
defined in (
2.6
) instead of
\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
provides the lower bound

(2.8)

\Omega_{\nu}(\alpha;X)\geq\frac{\#\{d\in\mathcal{Q}(\alpha;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega(E_{d})=\nu\}}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)}.

We introduce the second moment

R_{Q}^{(2)}(\alpha;X)=\sum_{d\leq X}r_{Q}(\alpha;d)^{2},

as well as the modified first moment

S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{S}(X)\\
\omega(E_{d})=\nu\end{subarray}}r_{Q}(\alpha;d).

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, combined with the lower bound (
2.8
), yields

(2.9)

\Omega_{\nu}(\alpha;X)\geq\frac{S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)^{2}}{\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)R_{Q}^{(2)}(\alpha;X)}.

The cardinality of the set
\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)
is the subject of a recent article by the author, where it is shown
[
Pet20
, Theorem 1]
that for
\alpha<1/120
, there exists a positive constant
c(\alpha)
such that one has

(2.10)

\#\mathcal{D}_{A,B}(\alpha;X)\sim c(\alpha)X^{1/2}\log X.

In Section
3
, we establish the following upper bound for the second moment.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/120)
. One has

R_{Q}^{(2)}(\alpha;X)\ll X^{1/2}\log X.

In Section
4
, we prove the following lower bound for the modified first moment.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/56)
and
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
. One has

S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)\gg X^{1/2}\log X.

Putting together the inequality (
2.9
), the estimate (
2.10
) and Propositions
4
and
5
, we obtain (
2.7
), which completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

3.
The second moment estimate

In this section, we establish Proposition
4
.
We begin with an easy preliminary lemma concerning the range of the triples in
\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/24)
. For
(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)
satisfying
Q(u,v,w)\leq X
, one has
w\ll X^{4\alpha}
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)
in (
2.4
), one has

\max\{u,vw^{2}\}\ll Q(u,v,w)^{1/4+2\alpha}\ll(v\max\{u,vw^{2}\}^{3})^{1/4+2\alpha},

so that

\max\{u,vw^{2}\}^{1-24\alpha}\ll v^{1+8\alpha}
. This implies
w\ll v^{16\alpha/(1-24\alpha)}
, and it suffices to note that one has
vw^{2}\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}
to complete the proof.
∎

We now turn to the computation of
R_{Q}^{(2)}(\alpha;X)
. Consider the diagonal term

m_{Q}(\alpha;d)=\#\left\{((u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2}))\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{150.65012pt}{$(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)$ \\
$Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})=Q(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})=d$ \\
$(u_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{1}w_{1}^{2})=(u_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}w_{2}^{2})$ }\right\},

where the extra condition is an equality in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, and let

(3.1)

a_{Q}(\alpha;d)=r_{Q}(\alpha;d)^{2}-m_{Q}(\alpha;d).

Let

(3.2)

\displaystyle M_{Q}(\alpha;X)=\sum_{d\leq X}m_{Q}(\alpha;d),

\displaystyle A_{Q}(\alpha;X)=\sum_{d\leq X}a_{Q}(\alpha;d).

We now provide upper bounds for these two quantities, in the form of two lemmas.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/24)
. One has

M_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll X^{1/2}\log X.

Proof.

For a fixed value of
d\geq 1
, let
((u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2}))
be a pair counted in
m_{Q}(\alpha;d)
.
Write
u=\gcd(u_{1},u_{2})
,
u_{1}=ux
and
u_{2}=uy
, where
x
and
y
are coprime positive integers.
The projective condition defining
m_{Q}(\alpha;d)
dehomogenises to

\displaystyle xu_{2}=yu_{1},

\displaystyle xv_{2}w_{2}^{2}=yv_{1}w_{1}^{2}.

From these expressions, we obtain

Q(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})=\frac{y^{3}v_{2}}{x^{3}v_{1}}Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),

and since
Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})=d\geq 1
, the equation
Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})=Q(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})
in the definition of
m_{Q}(\alpha;d)
becomes
x^{3}v_{1}=y^{3}v_{2}
, or equivalently
x^{2}w_{2}=y^{2}w_{1}
.
From the coprimality of
x
and
y
, it follows that
x^{2}
divides
w_{1}
and that
x^{3}
divides
v_{2}
.
Writing
w_{1}=wx^{2}
and
v_{2}=vx^{3}
for some positive integers
v
and
w
, we obtain
w_{2}=wy^{2}
and
v_{1}=vy^{3}
and therefore, we have

((u_{1},v_{1},w_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2}))=((ux,vy^{3},wx^{2}),(uy,vx^{3},wy^{2})),

for some 5-tuple
(u,v,w,x,y)
of positive integers. This brings about the new expression

m_{Q}(\alpha;d)\leq\#\left\{(u,v,w,x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{5}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{169.14857pt}{$(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2}),(uy,vx^{3},wy^{2})\in\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)$ \\
$Q(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2})=d$ }\right\},

and Lemma
6
implies
wx^{2},wy^{2}\ll X^{4\alpha}
.
Summing over
d
, we reach the upper bound

(3.3)

M_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{wx^{2},wy^{2}\ll X^{4\alpha}}\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{165.52765pt}{$1\leq Q(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2})\leq X$ \\
$ux,uy,vw^{2}x^{3}y^{4},vw^{2}x^{4}y^{3}\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}$ }\right\}.

An elementary application of the classical lattice-point counting method of Davenport
[
Dav51
]
shows

\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{115.5552pt}{$1\leq Q(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2})\leq X$ \\
$ux,uy,vx,vy\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}$ }\right\}\ll\iint_{0<Q(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2})\leq X}\dif u\dif v+\frac{X^{1/4+2\alpha}}{\max\{x,y\}}.

The change of variables
(u,v)\mapsto(uvX^{1/4}w^{1/2}(xy)^{3},vX^{1/4}w^{-3/2})
yields

\iint_{0<Q(ux,vy^{3},wx^{2})\leq X}\dif u\dif v=\frac{X^{1/2}(xy)^{3}}{w}\iint_{0<(xy)^{12}v^{4}F(u,1)\leq 1}v\dif u\dif v.

The integral over
v
has size
2^{-1}F(u,1)^{-1/2}(xy)^{-6}
and, since
F(u,1)
has three distinct roots, integrating over
u
gives a constant times
(xy)^{-6}
.
Summing over
x
,
y
and
w
in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle M_{Q}(\alpha;X)

\displaystyle\ll\mathop{\sum\sum\sum}_{wx^{2},wy^{2}\ll X^{4\alpha}}\left\{\frac{X^{1/2}}{w(xy)^{3}}+\frac{X^{1/4+2\alpha}}{\max\{x,y\}}\right\}

\displaystyle\ll X^{1/2}\log X+X^{1/4+6\alpha},

which concludes the proof since
\alpha<1/24
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/120)
. One has

A_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll X^{1/2}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
6
and carrying out the summation over
d
, we find in particular that

A_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll\mathop{\sum\sum}_{w_{1},w_{2}\ll X^{4\alpha}}\#\left\{(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{132.11203pt}{$1\leq u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}$ \\
$Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})=Q(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2})$ \\
$(u_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{1}w_{1}^{2})\neq(u_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{2}w_{2}^{2})$ }\right\}.

For fixed
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, write
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2})
and let
V_{\mathbf{w}}
be the projective surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
defined by the equation

Q(u_{1},v_{1},w_{1})=Q(u_{2},v_{2},w_{2}).

Let
H
denote the usual exponential Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{Q})
and set

a_{Q,\mathbf{w}}(k;\alpha;X)=\#\left\{(u_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}u_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{4})\in V_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbb{Q})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{136.19191pt}{$H(u_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}u_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{4})\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}/k$ \\
$(u_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{3}w_{1}^{2})\neq(u_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{4}w_{2}^{2})$ }\right\}.

Summing over
k=\gcd(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})
, we find

A_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll\mathop{\sum\sum}_{w_{1},w_{2}\ll X^{4\alpha}}\sum_{k\ll X^{1/4+2\alpha}}a_{Q,\mathbf{w}}(k;\alpha;X).

Let
L_{\mathbf{w}}
denote the Zariski closed subset of
V_{\mathbf{w}}
defined as the union of the lines contained in
V_{\mathbf{w}}
.
Since the polynomial
F(x,z)
is assumed to be irreducible, meaning that the curve
E
and all its twists
E_{d}
have trivial 2-torsion, a recent result by the author
[
Pet20
, Corollary 4.6]
states that if a point
(u_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}u_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{4})\in V_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbb{Q})
lies in
L_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbb{Q})
, then
(u_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{3}w_{1}^{2})=(u_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v_{4}w_{2}^{2})
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. Such points are excluded here and a result of Salberger
[
Sal08
, Theorem 0.1]
therefore shows that for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

a_{Q,\mathbf{w}}(k;\alpha;X)\ll_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{1/4+2\alpha}}{k}\right)^{13/8+\epsilon}.

It follows that

A_{Q}(\alpha;X)\ll_{\epsilon}X^{13/32+45\alpha/4+\epsilon},

and the assumption
\alpha<1/120
concludes the proof.
∎

We finally note that combining the equality (
3.1
) with the definitions in (
3.2
) and with Lemmas
7
and
8
, we immediately complete the proof of Proposition
4
.

4.
The square-free sieve

This section is dedicated to establishing Proposition
5
.
Recall that
N_{E}
denotes the conductor of the curve
E
.

For
d\geq 1
and square-free, let
D
be the discriminant of the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
and let
\chi_{d}
be the associated quadratic character. When
\gcd(D,N_{E})=1
, the sign of the functional equation of
L(E_{d},s)
is determined by (see
[
RS07
, Section 4]
)

\omega(E_{d})=\chi_{d}(-N_{E})\omega(E).

Using this equality and of the fact that
-N_{E}
is not a square, which implies that
\chi_{d}(-N_{E})
attains both signs as
d
varies, it follows from quadratic reciprocity that there exists
d_{0}\in(\mathbb{Z}/4N_{E}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that

S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{S}(X)\\
d\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}r_{Q}(\alpha;d).

Replacing
r_{Q}(\alpha;d)
by its definition in (
2.5
), this inequality becomes

S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)\geq\#\left\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{112.00252pt}{$Q(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}(X)$ \\
$Q(u,v,w)\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

It is immediate from the definition of
Q(u,v,w)
in (
2.3
) that
Q(1,d_{0},0)=d_{0}
, so defining

(4.1)

N(\alpha;X)=\#\left\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{130.76363pt}{$Q(u,v,w)\in\mathcal{S}(X)$ \\
$(u,v,w)\equiv(1,d_{0},0)\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\},

we obtain the inequality

S_{Q,\nu}(\alpha;X)\geq N(\alpha;X).

The remainder of Section
4
is dedicated to the proof of Propositon
9
below, which implies Proposition
5
.

Proposition 9
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1/56)
. One has

N(\alpha;X)\gg X^{1/2}\log X.

We point out that a square-free sieve argument has already been employed by Gouvêa and Mazur
[
GM91
]
, and at the same time but independently by Greaves
[
Gre92
]
, to show that integral binary forms, under certain assumptions, represent the expected number of square-free integers.
This is similar to the statement of Proposition
9
, the difference being that in our case the variables range over the region defined in (
2.4
) instead of over a box as they do in both aforementioned articles.
For this reason, the sieving process must be carried out in full here.

4.1.
Preliminary results

This section presents several results required later on.
Recall the definition (
2.2
) of the binary cubic form
F(x,z)
and let
\Delta_{F}=-(4A^{3}+27B^{2})
denote the discriminant of the polynomial
F(x,1)
.
Define the arithmetic function

(4.2)

\rho(n)=\#\{u\bmod n\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F(u,1)\equiv 0\bmod n\}.

This function is multiplicative and satisfies the upper bound
[
Ste91
, Corollary 2]

(4.3)

\rho(p^{k})\leq 2p^{v_{p}(\Delta_{F})/2}+1,

for any prime
p
and any
k\geq 1
.
Moreover, it follows from Hensel’s lemma that for
p\nmid\Delta_{F}
and
k\geq 1
, one has

(4.4)

\rho(p^{k})=\rho(p).

Together, (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) imply
\rho(n^{k})\ll\rho(n)
. By way of an estimate for the summatory function of
\rho(n)
(e.g.
[
Fom98
, Equation 4]
), this demonstrates the upper bound

(4.5)

\sum_{n\leq X}\rho(n^{k})\ll X.

Finally, we give an elementary lemma about a sum of a certain arithmetic function involving
\varphi^{*}(n)=\varphi(n)/n
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
a,q,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
. One has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\gcd(n,m)=1\\
n\equiv a\bmod q\end{subarray}}\mu(n)^{2}\varphi^{*}(n)=\frac{C_{0}(q)C_{1}(m;q)}{q}X+O(X^{1/2}),

with

\displaystyle C_{0}(q)=\prod_{p\nmid q}\left(1-\frac{2}{p^{2}}+\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right),

\displaystyle C_{1}(m;q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid m\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Write

f(n)=\begin{cases}\mu(n)^{2}\varphi^{*}(n),&\text{if }\gcd(n,m)=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and denote the quantity of interest by

A(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a\bmod q\end{subarray}}f(n).

This sum can be expressed as

\displaystyle A(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a\bmod q\end{subarray}}\sum_{d\mid n}(f\ast\mu)(d)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\geq 1\\
\gcd(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(f\ast\mu)(d)}{d}+O\left(\sum_{d\leq X}|(f\ast\mu)(d)|\right)+O\left(\frac{X}{q}\sum_{d>X}\frac{|(f\ast\mu)(d)|}{d}\right).

Remark that for
k\geq 1
, one has

(f\ast\mu)(p^{k})=\begin{cases}0,&p\nmid m\text{ and }k\geq 3\text{ or }p\mid m\text{ and }k\geq 2,\\
-1/p,&p\nmid m\text{ and }k=1,\\
-(1-1/p),&p\nmid m\text{ and }k=2,\\
-1,&p\mid m\text{ and }k=1,\end{cases}

so the main term is as claimed.
Remark that the computation of
(f\ast\mu)(p^{k})
shows, by multiplicativity, that one has

|(f\ast\mu)(d)|\leq|(f\ast\mu)(d^{\prime})|,

for all
d\geq 1
, where

d^{\prime}=\prod_{p\nmid m}p^{v_{p}(d)}.

One can therefore assume
m=1
when estimating the error terms, which we now do.

Let
d\geq 1
cube-free and write
d=d_{1}d_{2}^{2}
with
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
square-free and coprime. One has

\displaystyle|(f\ast\mu)(d_{1})|=1/d_{1},

\displaystyle|(f\ast\mu)(d_{2}^{2})|\leq 1,

and so, the sums appearing in the error terms are bounded as

\sum_{d\leq X}|(f\ast\mu)(d)|\leq\mathop{\sum\sum}_{d_{1}d_{2}^{2}\leq X}\frac{1}{d_{1}}\ll X^{1/2},

and

\sum_{d>X}\frac{|(f\ast\mu)(d)|}{d}\leq\mathop{\sum\sum}_{d_{1}d_{2}^{2}>X}\frac{1}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}\ll X^{-1/2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Setup for the square-free sieve

In this section, we establish a lower bound for the quantity
N(\alpha;X)
defined in (
4.1
) by transforming the region in which the triples
(u,v,w)
range.

Impose the additional condition
u\geq c_{3}vw^{2}
for some
c_{3}\geq 1
, so the maximum in the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{Q}(\alpha)
in (
2.4
) is simply
u
, and let
c_{4}=1+|A|+|B|
. For
c_{3}
large enough,
one has

\frac{1}{2}u^{3}\leq F(u,vw^{2})\leq c_{4}u^{3}.

These bounds allow us to strengthen the two conditions
Q(u,v,w)\leq X
and
u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2c_{2}}Q(u,v,w)^{1/4+2\alpha}
to

\displaystyle c_{4}u^{3}v\leq X,

\displaystyle u\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2c_{2}}\left(\frac{u^{3}v}{2}\right)^{1/4+2\alpha}.

Letting
c_{5}=\mathrm{e}^{-8c_{2}}2^{-(1+8\alpha)}
, the last condition can be rewritten as
u^{1-24\alpha}\leq c_{5}v^{1+8\alpha}
and we therefore obtain the inequality

N(\alpha;X)\geq\#\left\{(u,v,w)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{130.76363pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u$, $c_{4}u^{3}v\leq X$ \\
$u^{1-24\alpha}\leq c_{5}v^{1+8\alpha}$ \\
$\mu(Q(u,v,w))\neq 0$ \\
$(u,v,w)\equiv(1,d_{0},0)\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

Note that for any triple counted in this set, one has

c_{5}uv^{1+8\alpha}X^{8\alpha}\geq c_{3}u^{1-24\alpha}vw^{2}(c_{4}u^{3}v)^{8\alpha},

and that setting
c_{6}=c_{3}^{-1/2}c_{4}^{-4\alpha}c_{5}^{1/2}
, this inequality simplifies to

w\leq c_{6}X^{4\alpha}.

Let

(4.6)

U_{w}=c_{3}^{1/4}c_{4}^{-1/4}X^{1/4}w^{1/2}.

We reduce the range of
u
by imposing the additional restriction
u\leq U_{w}
, making the condition
c_{4}u^{3}v\leq X
redundant and thus giving

N(\alpha;X)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq c_{6}X^{4\alpha}\\
w\equiv 0\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{109.44653pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$u^{1-24\alpha}\leq c_{5}v^{1+8\alpha}$ \\
$\mu(Q(u,v,w))\neq 0$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(1,d_{0})\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

Next, we replace the summand in this estimate by the slightly larger quantity

(4.7)

N_{w}(X)=\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{109.44653pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$\mu(Q(u,v,w))\neq 0$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(1,d_{0})\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\},

which differs from said summand by an error term of size at most

\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{69.86986pt}{$u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$u^{1-24\alpha}\gg v^{1+8\alpha}$ }\right\}\ll(X^{1/2}w)^{(1-8\alpha)/(1+8\alpha)}.

We finally obtain

(4.8)

N(\alpha;X)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq c_{6}X^{4\alpha}\\
w\equiv 0\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}N_{w}(X)+O(X^{1/2}).

4.3.
Applying inclusion-exclusion

We now fix
w
divisible by
4N_{E}
and estimate the corresponding quantity
N_{w}(X)
defined in (
4.7
).
From the definition of
Q(u,v,w)
in (
2.3
), it ensues that the condition
\mu(Q(u,v,w))\neq 0
appearing in (
4.7
) can be reformulated as

\mu(v)\mu(F(u,vw^{2}))\neq 0,

and we will apply a square-free sieve argument to the condition
\mu(F(u,vw^{2}))\neq 0
.
Note that this condition implies
\gcd(u,vw)=1
, a condition we will keep throughout the sieving process.

We remove the square-free condition on
F(u,vw^{2})
by summing over the integers
\ell\geq 1
whose square divides
F(u,vw^{2})
.
The inequalities
vw^{2}\leq c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}
imply
F(u,vw^{2})\leq c_{4}U_{w}^{3}
, which restricts the range of
\ell
to
\ell^{2}\leq c_{4}U_{w}^{3}
.
Let

(4.9)

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{113.09016pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$\mu(v)\neq 0$, $\gcd(u,vw)=1$ \\
$F(u,vw^{2})\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(1,d_{0})\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\},

and note that for every pair
(u,v)
counted in this expression, one has
\gcd(\ell,w)=1
.
An application of the inclusion-exclusion principle now gives

(4.10)

N_{w}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{2}\leq c_{4}U_{w}^{3}\\
\gcd(\ell,w)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(\ell)N_{w,\ell}(X).

Note that all
v
counted in (
4.9
) are coprime to
\ell
so summing over residue classes, that expression becomes

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\bmod\ell^{2}\\
F(a,bw^{2})\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}\\
\gcd(b,\ell)=1\end{subarray}}\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{113.09016pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$\mu(v)\neq 0$, $\gcd(u,vw)=1$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(a,b)\bmod\ell^{2}$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(1,d_{0})\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

Shifting
a
by a factor
bw^{2}
simplifies the congruence condition and we arrive at

(4.11)

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod\ell^{2}\\
F(a,1)\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{113.09016pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq u\leq U_{w}$ \\
$\mu(v)\neq 0$, $\gcd(u,vw)=1$ \\
$u\equiv avw^{2}\bmod\ell^{2}$ \\
$(u,v)\equiv(1,d_{0})\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

4.4.
Reducing the range of
\ell

In this section, we show that the range of
\ell
in (
4.10
) can be reduced at a negligible cost.
Recall the definition of
\rho(n)
in (
4.2
). We begin with a simple upper bound on the size of
N_{w,\ell}(X)
.

Lemma 11
.

One has

N_{w,\ell}(X)\ll\left(\frac{X^{1/2}}{w\ell^{2}}+1\right)\rho(\ell^{2}).

Proof.

Abandoning some of the conditions in (
4.11
) and extending the range of
u
and
v
yields

\displaystyle N_{w,\ell}(X)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod\ell^{2}\\
F(a,1)\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{85.38326pt}{$1\leq u,c_{3}vw^{2}\leq U_{w}$ \\
$\gcd(u,v)=1$ \\
$u\equiv avw^{2}\bmod\ell^{2}$ }\right\}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq\ell^{2}\\
F(a,1)\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}\end{subarray}}\#\left\{(u,v,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{77.1932pt}{$|u|\leq U_{w}$ \\
$|v|\ll U_{w}w^{-2}$ \\
$|k|\ll U_{w}\ell^{-2}$ \\
$\gcd(u,v,k)=1$ \\
$u-avw^{2}-k\ell^{2}=0$ }\right\}

A result of Heath-Brown
[
HB84
, Lemma 3]
shows that the last summand has size at most

\frac{U_{w}^{2}}{w^{2}\ell^{2}}+1,

which, combined with the definition of
U_{w}
in (
4.6
), concludes the proof.
∎

An immediate consequence is the following result.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\theta>0
. One has

\sum_{X^{\theta}<\ell\ll U_{w}^{3/2}}\mu(\ell)N_{w,\ell}(X)\ll\frac{X^{1/2-\theta}}{w}+X^{3/8}w^{3/4}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
11
, we find

\sum_{X^{\theta}<\ell\ll U_{w}^{3/2}}\mu(\ell)N_{w,\ell}(X)\ll\frac{X^{1/2}}{w}\sum_{\ell>X^{\theta}}\frac{\rho(\ell^{2})}{\ell^{2}}+\sum_{\ell\leq U_{w}^{3/2}}\rho(\ell^{2}).

Using (
4.5
) and, for the first sum, Abel summation, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell>X^{\theta}}\frac{\rho(\ell^{2})}{\ell^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{X^{\theta}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\ll U_{w}^{3/2}}\rho(\ell^{2})\ll X^{3/8}w^{3/4},

which completes the proof.
∎

Going back to (
4.10
), Lemma
12
shows that for any
\theta>0
, we have

(4.12)

N_{w}(X)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<X^{\theta}\\
\gcd(\ell,w)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(\ell)N_{w,\ell}(X)+O\left(\frac{X^{1/2-\theta}}{w}\right)+O(X^{3/8}w^{3/4}).

4.5.
Estimating
N_{w,\ell}(X)

This section is dedicated to establishing a precise estimate for the quantity
N_{w,\ell}(X)
defined in (
4.9
) when
\gcd(\ell,w)=1
.
Removing the coprimality condition on
u
in (
4.11

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\bmod\ell^{2}\\
F(a,1)\equiv 0\bmod\ell^{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{3}vw^{2}\leq U_{w}\\
\gcd(v,\ell)=1\\
v\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\mu(v)^{2}\sum_{f\mid vw}\mu(f)\#\left\{u_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{83.6195pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq fu_{1}\leq U_{w}$ \\
$fu_{1}\equiv avw^{2}\bmod\ell^{2}$ \\
$fu_{1}\equiv 1\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}.

One sees that
f
can be inverted modulo

, and also modulo
\ell^{2}
as
f
divides
vw
, which is coprime to
\ell
.
Recall that we assume that
4N_{E}
divides

\gcd(\ell,4N_{E})=1
.
Together, these observations show that the two congruence conditions on

reduce to a single condition on
u_{1}
modulo

and we obtain the estimate

\#\left\{u_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\parbox{83.6195pt}{$c_{3}vw^{2}\leq fu_{1}\leq U_{w}$ \\
$fu_{1}\equiv avw^{2}\bmod\ell^{2}$ \\
$fu_{1}\equiv 1\bmod 4N_{E}$ }\right\}=\frac{U_{w}-c_{3}vw^{2}}{4N_{E}\ell^{2}f}+O(1).

Recalling the definition of
\rho(n)
in (
4.2
), we arrive at

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\rho(\ell^{2})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{3}vw^{2}\leq U_{w}\\
\gcd(v,\ell)=1\\
v\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\mu(v)^{2}\sum_{f\mid vw}\mu(f)\left\{\frac{U_{w}-c_{3}vw^{2}}{4N_{E}\ell^{2}f}+O(1)\right\}.

We now define

(4.13)

V_{w,\ell}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{3}vw^{2}\leq U_{w}\\
\gcd(v,\ell)=1\\
v\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\mu(v)^{2}\varphi^{*}(vw)(U_{w}-c_{3}vw^{2}),

and obtain

(4.14)

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\frac{\rho(\ell^{2})}{4N_{E}\ell^{2}}V_{w,\ell}(X)+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{1/4+\epsilon}}{w^{3/2}}\right),

when
\gcd(\ell,w)=1
, and zero otherwise. Here we have used the estimates
\rho(\ell^{2})\ll_{\epsilon}\ell^{\epsilon}\ll X^{\epsilon}
and
\#\{f\mid vw\}\ll_{\epsilon}(vw)^{\epsilon}\ll X^{\epsilon}
to simplify the error term, and replaced

by its definition (

).

Define the two arithmetic functions

(4.15)

\displaystyle f_{1}(n)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1},

\displaystyle f_{2}(n)=\varphi^{*}(w)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{p}{p^{2}+2p-1}\right)^{-1},

where the prime indicates that the product is restricted to

.
We compute
V_{w,\ell}(X)
in the following lemma.

Lemma 13
.

Assume
\gcd(\ell,w)=1
. There exists a constant
c_{7}>0

V_{w,\ell}(X)=\frac{c_{7}f_{2}(w)f_{1}(\ell)}{w}X^{1/2}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{3/8}}{w^{1/4-\epsilon}}\right).

Proof.

Taking
g=\gcd(v,w)
out, writing

and noting that
g
and
\ell
are necessarily coprime since we assume

, the expression (

) becomes

V_{w,\ell}(X)=\sum_{g\mid w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{3}gv_{1}w^{2}\leq U_{w}\\

gv_{1}\equiv d_{0}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\mu(gv_{1})^{2}\varphi^{*}(gv_{1}w)(U_{w}-c_{3}gv_{1}w^{2}).

Note that one has
\gcd(v_{1},g)=1
for every nonzero summand.
Furthermore, recall from the beginning of Section
4.2
that
d_{0}
is invertible modulo
4N_{E}
, meaning that the sum over
v_{1}
is zero unless
\gcd(g,4N_{E})=1
.
Noting that one has
\varphi^{*}(gv_{1}w)=\varphi^{\ast}(v_{1}w)=\varphi^{\ast}(v_{1})\varphi^{*}(w)
as

divides

and
\gcd(v_{1},w)=1
whenever
\mu(v_{1})\neq 0
, we reach the new expression

V_{w,\ell}(X)=\varphi^{*}(w)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{g\mid w}\mu(g)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{3}gv_{1}w^{2}\leq U_{w}\\
\gcd(v_{1},g\ell)=1\\
v_{1}\equiv d_{0}\bar{g}\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\mu(v_{1})^{2}\varphi^{*}(v_{1})(U_{w}-c_{3}gv_{1}w^{2}).

Here, the prime indicates that the sum is restricted to values of
g
coprime to
4N_{E}
, and
\bar{g}
denotes the inverse of
g
modulo
4N_{E}
.
Applying Lemma
10
and using Abel summation for the second summand, we find that the sum over
v_{1}
is

\frac{C_{0}(4N_{E})C_{1}(g\ell;4N_{E})}{8N_{E}c_{3}gw^{2}}U_{w}^{2}+O\left(\frac{U_{w}^{3/2}}{g^{1/2}w}\right),

with
C_{0}(4N_{E})
and
C_{1}(g\ell;4N_{E})
as in Lemma
10
. Let

c_{7}=\frac{C_{0}(4N_{E})}{8N_{E}(c_{3}c_{4})^{1/2}}>0,

and remark that the arithmetic function
f_{1}(n)
in (
4.15
) is chosen to have
C_{1}(n;4N_{E})=f_{1}(n)
.
Replacing
U_{w}
by its expression (
4.6
), the value of the sum over
v_{1}
becomes

\frac{c_{7}f_{1}(g\ell)}{gw}X^{1/2}+O\left(\frac{X^{3/8}}{g^{1/2}w^{1/4}}\right).

Recall that
\ell
and
w
are coprime and that
g
divides
w
so by multiplicativity,
f_{1}(g\ell)=f_{1}(g)f_{1}(\ell)
. Summing over
g
, we arrive at

V_{w,\ell}(X)=\frac{c_{7}\varphi^{*}(w)f_{1}(\ell)}{w}X^{1/2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{g\mid w}\frac{\mu(g)^{2}f_{1}(g)}{g}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{3/8}}{w^{1/4-\epsilon}}\right).

The sum over
g
satisfies

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{g\mid w}\frac{\mu(g)^{2}f_{1}(g)}{g}=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid w}\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(p)}{p}\right)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid w}\left(1+\frac{p}{p^{2}+p-1}\right)=\frac{f_{2}(w)}{\varphi^{*}(w)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Write
c_{8}=c_{7}/4N_{E}
. When
\gcd(\ell,w)=1
, the equation (
4.14
) and Lemma
13
merge into

(4.16)

N_{w,\ell}(X)=\frac{c_{8}f_{2}(w)\rho(\ell^{2})f_{1}(\ell)}{w\ell^{2}}X^{1/2}+O(X^{3/8}).

Here, the error term slightly worse than the one actually provided by the lemma. This is only to improve readability and has no consequence whatsoever, as the limitation on the range of
\alpha
will be determined by a different error term.

4.6.
Estimating
N(\alpha;X)

We now have all the necessary estimates to compute a lower bound for
N_{w}(X)
when
4N_{E}
divides
w
, and thus also for
N(\alpha;X)
using (
4.8
).
Combining (
4.12
) and (
4.16
) gives, for any
\theta>0
, the inequality

(4.17)

N_{w}(X)\geq\frac{c_{8}f_{2}(w)}{w}X^{1/2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell<X^{\theta}\\
\gcd(\ell,w)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\ell)\rho(\ell^{2})f_{1}(\ell)}{\ell^{2}}+O\left(\frac{X^{1/2-\theta}}{w}\right)+O(X^{3/8+\theta})+O(X^{3/8}w^{3/4}).

As previously, the prime indicates that the summation is restricted to indices coprime to
4N_{E}
.

Let

(4.18)

L(w)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\gcd(\ell,w)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\ell)\rho(\ell^{2})f_{1}(\ell)}{\ell^{2}}.

A simple computation shows that
L(w)
is finite and satisfies

(4.19)

L(w)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq X^{\theta}\\
\gcd(\ell,w)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(\ell)\rho(\ell^{2})f_{1}(\ell)}{\ell^{2}}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{X^{\theta-\epsilon}}\right).

We show that
L(w)
does not vanish.

Lemma 14
.

There exists a constant
c_{9}>0
satisfying
L(w)\geq c_{9}
for all
w\geq 1
.

Proof.

Expanding (
4.18
) as an Euler product gives

L(w)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\nmid w}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p^{2})f_{1}(p)}{p^{2}}\right)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\nmid w}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p^{2})}{p^{2}+p-1}\right),

with the second equality following from the definition of
f_{1}(p)
in (
4.15
).
Recall that
\Delta_{F}
denotes the discriminant of the polynomial
F(x,1)
, as defined at the beginning of Section
4.1
. We further separate this product as

L(w)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\nmid\Delta_{F}}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p^{2})}{p^{2}+p-1}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid\Delta_{F}}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p^{2})}{p^{2}+p-1}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\mid w}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p^{2})}{p^{2}+p-1}\right)^{-1}.

The product over the primes dividing
w
consists only of terms at least one, and the product over the prime divisors of
\Delta_{F}
is a positive constant. Combined with (
4.4
), this shows

L(w)\gg\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\prod}_{p\nmid\Delta_{F}}\left(1-\frac{\rho(p)}{p^{2}+p-1}\right).

Since
\rho(p)\leq 3
, the right-hand side does not vanish and the proof is complete.
∎

Gathering (
4.17
), (
4.19
) and Lemma
14
, we obtain

N_{w}(X)\geq\frac{c_{8}c_{9}f_{2}(w)}{w}X^{1/2}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{f_{2}(w)}{w}X^{1/2-\theta+\epsilon}\right)+O\left(\frac{X^{1/2-\theta}}{w}\right)+O(X^{3/8+\theta})+O(X^{3/8}w^{3/4}).

For any choice of
\theta\in(0,1/8-4\alpha)
, we return to (
4.8
) and sum this inequality over
w
. From the definition of
f_{2}(n)
in (
4.15
), it is immediate that
\varphi^{*}(w)\leq f_{2}(w)\ll_{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}
, and we obtain

N(\alpha;X)\geq c_{8}c_{9}X^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq c_{6}X^{4\alpha}\\
w\equiv 0\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi^{*}(w)}{w}+O(X^{1/2}),

since we assume
\alpha<1/56
.
Abel summation shows that the sum over
w
satisfies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq c_{6}X^{4\alpha}\\
w\equiv 0\bmod 4N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi^{*}(w)}{w}\gg\sum_{x\ll X^{4\alpha}}\frac{\varphi^{*}(x)}{x}\gg\log X,

and we obtain the lower bound
N(\alpha;X)\gg X^{1/2}\log X
, thus completing the proof of Proposition
9
.

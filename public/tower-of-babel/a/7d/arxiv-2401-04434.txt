Abstract
Gravitational plane waves (when Ricci flat) belong to the VSI family. The achronym VSI stands for vanishing scalar invariants , meaning that all scalar invariants built out of Riemann tensor and its derivatives vanish, although the Riemann tensor itself does not. In the particular case of plane waves many interesting phenomena have been uncovered for strings propagating in this background. Here we comment on gravitons propagating in such a spacetime.

1
Introduction.

The exact solutions of Einstein equations that can be in some sense interpreted as gravitational waves are interesting in their own right. What is more, the simplest of those, namely plane waves, is in some sense the limit of an arbitrary spacetime in the vicinity of a null geodesic (this is called the
Penrose limit
)
[
28
]
.

Into the plane waves, there is an interesting subfamily of spacetimes
[
24
]
and
[
14
]
, to wit, that of a special class of exact gravitational waves, namely the
plane-fronted parallel waves with parallel rays
(PP waves) that belong to the
vanishing scalar invariants
(VSI) class of spacetimes. That is, all scalars constructed out of the metric, the Riemann tensor and its derivatives vanish identically. This is obviously true also in flat spacetime where Riemann tensor vanishes. The interesting thing is that can be also true in
non flat spacetimes
, with a non vanishing Riemann tensor.

This fact means that there are no allowed counterterms
[
20
]
in the Einstein-Hilbert action when considering gravitons propagating in such a background. Which has led some authors to claim that there are no divergences whatsoever, and quantum gravity must be finite around such spaces. This is not so, and it is enough to remember that Minkowski is just one such space. Quantum gravity in flat space (and its divergences) has been investigated by Capper and coworkers a long time ago
[
11
]
.

The S-matrix elements (on shell divergences) however should be finite
[
25
]
. There is a general statement which asserts that divergences that vanish on the equations of motion for the quantum fields (as they do in VSI) indicate that modifications of the BRST algebra are necessary at each loop order, but we are not elaborating on that in this paper.

Our aim here instead is to consider some aspects of the physics of such gravitons propagating in a plane gravitational wave.

We review some usuals definitions (see Appendix
A
for more details). The standard
pp-wave
metric reads

\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}u\text{d}v-F[u,x^{c}]\text{d}u^{2}-\delta_{ab}\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}

(1)

where
u\equiv t-z
,
v\equiv t+z
, and the transverse coordinates are
x^{c}=(x,y)
.
These spacetimes belong to the Kerr-Schild class
[
26
]
. This means that the metric can be written as

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+Fl_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(2)

with

l_{\mu}=-\delta_{u\mu}

(3)

and the additional facts that
l^{2}=0
,
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}l^{\sigma}=0
and
\pounds(l)R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0
. An interesting fact proved in
[
21
]
is that Einstein equations can be written in a linear form and the pseudoenergy tensor vanishes.

A
plane wave
is a classical pp-wave for which the characteristic function
F
is quadratic in
x^{c}
,
F[u,x^{c}]=H_{ab}x^{a}x^{b}

\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}u\text{d}v-H_{ab}[u]x^{a}x^{b}\text{d}u^{2}-\delta_{ab}\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}

(4)

in harmonic (often called Brinkmann) coordinates.

This metric has an only nonvanishing component of the Riemann tensor

R_{uaub}=-H_{ab}[u]

(5)

and the Ricci tensor, in turn, has reduced to

R_{uu}=-\delta^{ab}H_{ab}[u]\equiv-H^{a}_{a}[u]

(6)

Obviously the manifold is Ricci flat whenever the transverse matrix
H_{ab}
is traceless, and the matrix is usually written as

H_{pq}[u]x^{p}x^{q}\equiv a_{+}[u](x^{2}-y^{2})+2a_{\times}[u]xy

(7)

here the functions
a_{+}[u]
and
a_{\times}[u]
are the
polarization profiles
of the wave.

In the non-Ricci-flat case, there is a source

T_{uu}={1\over 4}\left(T_{tt}+T_{zz}\right)-{1\over 2}T_{tz}=-{1\over\kappa^{2}}H^{a}_{a}[u]

(8)

Finally, an especially interesting case are the
impulsive waves

[
19
]
and
[
27
]
, which we will study in the next section

\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}u\text{d}v-\frac{1}{\lambda_{a}}\delta(u)\,\delta_{ab}x^{a}x^{b}\text{d}u^{2}-\delta_{ab}\text{d}x^{a}\text{d}x^{b}

(9)

this metric is Ricci flat if

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0

(10)

We shall see in a moment that in some cases, it is convenient to transform to a set of
group coordinates
or Rosen coordinates
[
19
]
, where the metric reads

\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}u\text{d}V-h_{ab}[u]\text{d}X^{a}\text{d}X^{b}

(11)

The relationship between the two sets of coordinates is given by

\displaystyle v=V+\frac{1}{2}\dot{h}_{ab}(u)X^{a}X^{b}

\displaystyle x^{a}=P^{a}_{b}(u)X^{b}

(12)

with

h_{ab}(u)=\delta_{ij}P_{a}^{i}(u)P_{b}^{j}(u)

(13)

where the matrix
P_{a}^{b}(u)
is determined by solving the differential equation

\delta_{ij}\ddot{P}^{i}_{a}(u)=H_{kj}(u)P^{k}_{a}(u)

(14)

with initial conditions satisfying the constraint

\delta_{ij}\left(\dot{P}^{i}_{a}(u)P^{j}_{b}(u)-\dot{P}^{i}_{b}(u)P^{j}_{a}(u)\right)=0

(15)

where the overdot means derivative with respect to
u
.

These coordinates do not cover the whole manifold because the determinant of the transverse metric vanishes at some point
u=\overline{u}
.
The only nonvanishing component of Ricci tensor in this coordinates reads

R_{uu}=-{1\over 2}h^{ab}\ddot{h}_{ab}-{1\over 4}\dot{h}^{ab}\dot{h}_{ab}

(16)

There is a theorem, first pointed out in
[
23
]
, which states that imposing additional Ricci flatness, then all higher order corrections to the equation of motion also vanish. This means the exact equation of motion of the full perturbative effective action.

The most important aspect of VSI spacetimes is that all the DeWitt-Schwinger coefficients of the short time expansion of the heat kernel do necessarily vanish. We will expose this in some detail in the correspondent section.

Is there any physical meaning in the fact that such an action vanishes on shell?
In fact there is none. For example, any theory characterized by a bilinear Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\sum\phi_{i}M_{ij}\phi_{j}

(17)

however complicated the kernel
M_{ij}
also vanishes on shell (like Dirac Lagrangian for example).

In addition, contrary to popular belief,
this is
not
a generic property of covariant actions. A simple example, the Weyl squared action

S\equiv\int d^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}W_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}W^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

(18)

which equation of motion is the vanishing of Bach tensor, but they do not imply
W_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}^{2}=0
. That is, the Lagrangian of conformal gravity does not vanish on shell.

The fact that it does for Einstein is just an accident.
Another point is that of course this does
not
mean that the equation of motion are trivial.

The preceding theorem shows that

\displaystyle\left.\Gamma[g]\right|_{on\,shell}=\Gamma_{class}[g]=-{1\over 2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{|g|}R

\displaystyle\left.{\delta\Gamma[g]\over\delta g_{\alpha\beta}(x)}\right|_{on\,shell}=0

(19)

We have just asserted that this last fact has been shown to be true in VSI backgrounds by Horowitz and Steif
[
23
]
; it physically means that the VSI spacetime is the consistent vacuum of quantum gravity.
But of course the 1PI two point correlator is different to zero.

\left.{\delta^{2}\Gamma[g]\over\delta g_{\alpha\beta}(x)\delta g_{\beta\gamma}(x^{\prime})}\right|_{on\,shell}\neq 0

(20)

In the next sections we will show our most relevant results about VSI spacetimes and we will do some illustrative examples.

2
The heat kernel for a plane wave.

The most useful tool in order to compute both the propagator as well as the effective action is the heat kernel
[
3
]
. In order to do this calculation it pays to work in Rosen coordinates

ds^{2}=dudV-h_{ab}[u]dX^{a}dX^{b}

(21)

We shall see in a moment that the propagator is the inverse of the plane wave d’Alembertian operator. In fact, the effective action will be determined by the determinant of the same operator. To be specific the d’Alembertian for VSI spacetime reads

\displaystyle\Box=\frac{\dot{h}_{ab}}{h_{ab}}\partial_{v}+\partial_{u}\partial_{v}-h^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}

(22)

There is a theorem by DeWitt
[
3
]
on the existence of a small proper time expansion for the heat kernel of any operator which can be obtained by a deformation of the d’Alembertian, given by

K\left(\tau;x,y\right)=K_{0}\left(\tau;x,y\right)\penalty\ \sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\penalty\ a_{p}\left(x,y\right)\tau^{p}

(23)

with

a_{0}(x,y)=1

(24)

and the
K_{0}\left(\tau;x,y\right)
is the flat space heat kernel

K_{0}\left(\tau;x,y\right)={1\over(4\pi\tau)^{n\over 2}}\,e^{-{\sigma(x-x^{\prime})\over 2\tau}}

(25)

Here
\sigma(x,x^{\prime})
is the world function corresponding to the points
x
and
x^{\prime}
,
[
30
]
.

The important thing for our purposes is that the so called DeWitt-Schwinger coefficients,
a_{p}
are given by integrals of pointwise scalars constructed out of Riemann tensor and its covariant derivatives contracted with the metric tensor. But in all VSI metrics, including plane waves, there are none of those. We are forced to conclude that

a_{p}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if}\,p\neq 0

(26)

and the heat kernel reduces to flat space one

K\left(\tau;x,y\right)=K_{0}\left(\tau;x,y\right)

(27)

this is a
exact
expression for the heat kernel.

We want to emphasize that we only need to obtain the world function to know the
exact
heat kernel.

In order to compute the world function, we consider the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{du}{d\tau}\frac{dV}{d\tau}-h_{ab}[u]\frac{dX^{a}}{d\tau}\frac{dX^{b}}{d\tau}

(28)

where
\tau
is the proper time. The canonical momenta read

\displaystyle p_{u}=\frac{dV}{d\tau}

\displaystyle p_{V}=\frac{du}{d\tau}

\displaystyle p_{a}=-2h_{ab}\frac{dX^{b}}{d\tau}

(29)

The Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to the coordinate
V
implies the conservation of
p_{V}
. We can then write

u-u_{0}=p_{V}(\tau-\tau_{0})

(30)

The transverse momentum can be written as

p_{a}=-2h_{ab}p_{V}\frac{dX^{b}}{du}

(31)

therefore

X^{a}=X_{0}^{a}-\frac{p_{b}}{2p_{V}}\int^{u}_{u_{0}}h^{ab}[u^{\prime}]du^{\prime}

(32)

The first integral of the timelike geodesic equations

g_{\mu\nu}{dx^{\mu}\over d\tau}{dx^{\nu}\over d\tau}=1

(33)

implies

\frac{du}{d\tau}\frac{dV}{d\tau}-h_{ab}\frac{dX^{a}}{d\tau}\frac{dX^{b}}{d\tau}=\frac{dV}{du}p^{2}_{V}-\frac{1}{4}h^{ab}p_{a}p_{b}=1

(34)

so that

\displaystyle V=V_{0}+\frac{u-u_{0}}{p_{V}^{2}}+\frac{p_{a}p_{b}}{4p_{V}^{2}}\int^{u}_{u_{0}}h^{ab}[u^{\prime}]du^{\prime}

(35)

Finally, the world function reads

\displaystyle\sigma(x,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}=\frac{1}{2p_{V}^{2}}(u-u_{0})^{2}=

(36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(V-V_{0})(u-u_{0})-\frac{p_{a}p_{b}}{8p_{V}^{2}}(u-u_{0})\int h^{ab}[u^{\prime}]du^{\prime}=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(V-V_{0})(u-u_{0})-\frac{1}{2}(X^{a}-X^{a}_{0})C_{ab}[u,u_{0}](X^{b}-X^{b}_{0})(u-u_{0})

where, we can define
C_{ab}
as

C_{ab}[u,u_{0}]\int_{u_{0}}^{u}h^{cb}[u^{\prime}]du^{\prime}=\delta_{a}^{c}

(37)

In conclusion, with the characteristic function
h_{ab}[u]
of the metric, we can obtain the world function and therefore the heat kernel. We show an explicit example

Impulsive Gravitational Plane Waves.

A particular case worth considering is the

•

\displaystyle S_{\text{\tiny{EH}}}

5
The Unimodular gauge

In the particular case of plane waves (as in all VSI spacetimes), the equations of motion of unimodular gravity are equivalent to the ones of Einstein General Relativity, because the Ricci tensor is already traceless to begin with.
As a matter of fact, in the unimodular gauge the plane wave metric, in Rosen coordinates, reads

ds^{2}={2dUdV\over\sqrt{h[u]}}-h_{ab}[u]dX^{a}dX^{b}

(82)

where

h[u]\equiv\det\,h_{ab}[u]

(83)

and
U
is a function of
u
given by

U(u)\equiv\,\frac{1}{2}\int^{u}\sqrt{h(x)}\,dx

(84)

Next, we only need the world function for obtain the propagator and the effective action. The geodesics are determined by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{h[u]}}\frac{dU}{d\tau}\frac{dV}{d\tau}-h_{ab}[u]\frac{dX^{a}}{d\tau}\frac{dX^{b}}{d\tau}

(85)

where
\tau
is the proper time, the momentum about
V
yields

\displaystyle p_{V}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{h[u]}}\frac{dU}{d\tau}

(86)

the Euler-Lagrange equation about
V
implies the conservation of
p_{V}
, then we can write

\frac{dU}{d\tau}=\frac{\sqrt{h[u]}}{2}p_{V}

(87)

which implies

\tau-\tau_{0}=\frac{2}{p_{V}}\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]

(88)

The function
\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]
is then given by

\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]\equiv\int^{U}_{U_{0}}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{h[u[x]]}}

(89)

this formula yields the function
\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]
through the implicit function theorem.

The transverse momentum can be written as

p_{a}=-h_{ab}p_{V}\sqrt{h[u]}\frac{dX^{b}}{dU}

(90)

then

X^{a}(\tau)=X^{a}_{0}-{p_{b}\over p_{V}}\int_{U_{0}}^{U}{h^{ab}(x)\over\sqrt{h(x)}}dx=X^{a}_{0}-{p_{b}\over p_{V}}\mathcal{C}^{ab}[U,U_{0}]

(91)

where we define

\mathcal{C}^{ab}[U,U_{0}]\equiv\int_{U_{0}}^{U}{h^{ab}(x)\over\sqrt{h(x)}}dx

(92)

with
\mathcal{C}_{ab}\mathcal{C}^{bc}=\delta_{a}^{c}
.

Imposing now the first integral

\frac{2}{\sqrt{h[u]}}\frac{dU}{d\tau}\frac{dV}{d\tau}-h_{ab}[u]\frac{dX^{a}}{d\tau}\frac{dX^{b}}{d\tau}=1

(93)

it follows that

\displaystyle V=V_{0}+\frac{2}{p_{V}^{2}}\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]+\frac{p_{a}p_{b}}{2p_{V}^{2}}\mathcal{C}^{ab}[U,U_{0}]

(94)

and world function in the unimodular gauge reads

\displaystyle\sigma(x,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}=\frac{2}{p_{V}^{2}}\mathcal{F}^{2}[U,U_{0}]=

(95)

\displaystyle=(V-V_{0})\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]-\frac{1}{2}(X^{a}-X^{a}_{0})\mathcal{C}_{ab}[U,U_{0}](X^{b}-X^{b}_{0})\mathcal{F}[U,U_{0}]

It is to be concluded that with the world function, we can obtain the propagator and the effective action in the unimodular background.

6
Conclusions.

In this paper we have determined the exact one loop propagator (albeit in a formal way) as well as the effective action for a graviton propagating in a plane wave. Gravitational plane waves are roughly similar to electromagnetic plane waves, but for the crucial point that a general gravitational wave cannot be written as some superposition of gravitational plane waves, owing to the nonlinear character of Einstein’s equations. There are some exceptions to this whenever the waves are in the Kerr-Schild family in which case Ricci flatness is equivalent to Fierz-Pauli
[
2
]
.

This work has been possible owing to the fact that plane waves belong to the family of VSI spacetimes (all geometric scalar invariants vanish).
Then the Schwinger-DeWitt expansion is trivial, and the heat kernel corresponding to the full laplacian is exact once the world function (one half the squared geodesic distance) is known, which is just an elementary exercise. There is a quadratic coupling to the Riemann tensor, which is like a position dependent mass, and looks like the manifestation of the
tail terms
is this formalism.

We have also pointed out that for Ricci flat spacetimes (our plane waves are such) are examples of instances where the equations of motion of unimodular gravity are the same as the general relativistic ones in the unimodular gauge.

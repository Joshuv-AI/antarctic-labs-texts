Classical spin liquid state in the emergent honeycomb-lattice material TbBO 3 Preprint: APS
J. Khatua, D. Tay, T. Shiroka, M. Pregelj, U. Jena, M. Barik, K. Kargeti, S. K. Panda, G. B. G. Stenning, P. Manuel, M. D. Le, D. T. Adroja, P. Khuntia

Abstract
The classical spin liquid state, owing to spin frustration between classical Ising spins on triangular motifs and interplay between competing degrees of freedom, is characterized by a macroscopically degenerate ground state and topological excitations linked to emergent gauge theories. However, experimental realization of such a state remains elusive. Here, by using thermodynamic- and local-probe measurements down to 16 mK, we demonstrate the exotic magnetism and spin dynamics in the nearly perfect honeycomb-lattice material TbBO 3 . The latter embodies a frustrated lattice with a superimposed triangular lattice, constituted by additional Tb 3+ ions at the center of each hexagon. Thermodynamic experiments reveal the presence of dominant antiferromagnetic exchange and significant dipolar interactions. Despite sizable antiferromagnetic exchange interactions between the Tb 3+ moments, muon-spin relaxation does not detect any signatures of long-range magnetic order or spin-freezing down to 16 mK, corroborating the specific heat and ac susceptibility down to 45 mK. This suggests that the spin-orbit driven anisotropic exchange interaction engender a strong frustration, crucial to induce a persistent spin dynamics. The scaling of muon relaxation rate as a function of the characteristic energy scale for several spin-liquid candidates, including TbBO 3 , demonstrates that a common underlying mechanism is at play. This is consistent with the NMR results on TbBO 3 and reminiscent of a universal spin-liquid behavior, here attributed to dominant antiferromagnetic short-range spin correlations, confirmed by the presence of a broad magnetic diffuse scattering in the elastic and low-energy inelastic neutron scattering at Q ≈ 1.03 Q\approx 1.03 Å -1 at low temperatures. Our results demonstrate that TbBO 3 hosts a classical spin liquid induced by spin-orbit driven anisotropy on a frustrated honeycomb lattice antiferromagnet.

Classical spin liquid (CSL) states, the classical
counterparts of QSLs, have garnered comparatively less attention. A
CSL is characterized by a macroscopic ground-state degeneracy and
by short-range spin correlations

an algebraic or a topological spin correlations with a finite gap in

Isakov et al. 2004

The algebraic CSL is expected to display a Coulomb phase and significant low-energy excitations associated with an emergent

gauge field, manifesting
as a power-law behavior in the thermodynamic and microscopic observables

;
Henley 2010
. Furthermore, competing exchange interactions in frustrated motifs have been predicted to stabilize emergent quantum phases, such as fracton phases,
whose exotic excitations may have implications in other
subfields, such as quantum gravity and elasticity

The exotic features of the CSL state, such as zero-point entropy, emergent gauge fields, and monopole excitations, have been identified in real 3D frustrated pyrochlore systems, where thermal fluctuations and dipolar interactions play a significant role

Petit et al. 2016

In this work, we investigated the ground state of a stuffed honeycomb-like lattice antiferromagnet TbBO
3
by muon spin relaxation (µSR), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), inelastic neutron scattering (INS), neutron powder diffraction (NPD), and thermodynamic measurements. Our experiments along with density functional theory + Hubbard U (DFT + U) calculations suggest the presence of a significant spin-orbit driven magnetic anisotropy and a frustrated spin-lattice. The
ac
susceptibility, specific-heat, and NPD data down to 50 mK rule out the presence of spin freezing and magnetic phase transitions. Below the broad maximum around 5 K, the specific heat follows a near-quadratic behavior down to 0.5 K, then it transitions to a sublinear power-law, possibly mimicking a gapless excitation spectra. However, INS experiments reveal a tiny gap of
\sim 3.5
K in the low-energy excitation spectra

;
Yan et al. 2024a
.
Despite the sizable antiferromagnetic interaction between Tb
3+
moments, µSR experiments detect no signatures of long-range magnetic order or spin-freezing down to 16 mK. The enhanced µSR relaxation rate below 3 K is attributed to the slowing down of spin dynamics on cooling and it levels off below 1 K, typical of spin liquid candidates,
thus suggesting a dynamic ground state

. The low-energy inelastic excitations at 0.3 and 0.5 meV show a broad maximum at
Q\approx 1.03
Å

The broad diffuse scattering both in elastic and inelastic channels

\sim
10 Å. Combined thermodynamic, µSR, NMR, INS, and neutron diffraction results uncover short-range spin correlations and persistent spin dynamics well below the interaction energy scale, indicative of a CSL in this frustrated magnet.

long-range magnetic order or spin freezing down to 50 mK, corroborated by NPD experiments down to 50 mK.
The INS experiments and CEF calculations reproduce the thermodynamic results reasonably well
and suggest that the ground state is well separated from the excited state.
DFT calculations suggest the presence of a significant spin-orbit
driven magnetic anisotropy. NMR experiments reveal the existence of
short-range spin correlations between the Tb
3+
ions, consistent
with the thermodynamic results. The µSR experiments
rule out
a spin freezing or a magnetic phase transition, but confirm the
persistence of spin dynamics down to 16 mK. The emergence of
a broad magnetic diffuse contribution in the elastic and low-energy inelastic scattering at
Q=1.03
Å
-1
that is enhanced at low-temperature
T\sim|\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}|

implies the presence of dominant
antiferromagnetic spin correlations and a CSL with short-range spin correlations,
in line with the NMR and thermodynamic results. The low-energy INS experiments detect a tiny gap of approximately 3.5 K, possibly reflecting topological spin correlations in the host honeycomb spin-lattice. Our results suggest
the emergence of a classical spin-liquid state stabilized by spin-orbit driven anisotropy inducing frustration in a stuffed honeycomb lattice with significant dipolar interactions, thus providing an
alternative route for achieving and studying the classical counterpart of quantum spin liquids.
The insights gleaned through this work may put forward
a strong impetus for the experimental realization of emergent many-body phenomena with exotic excitations in a broad range of frustrated lattices.

Axi-symmetric solutions for active vector models generalizing 3D Euler and electron–MHD equations
Dongho Chae, Kyudong Choi, In-Jee Jeong

Abstract
We study systems interpolating between the 3D incompressible Euler and electron–MHD equations, given by { ∂ t B + V ⋅ ∇ B = B ⋅ ∇ V , V = − ∇ × ( − Δ ) − a B , ∇ ⋅ B = 0 , \left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}B+V\cdot\nabla B=B\cdot\nabla V,\\ &V=-\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-a}B,\\ &\nabla\cdot B=0,\end{aligned}\right. where B B is a time-dependent vector field in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . Under the assumption that the initial data is axi-symmetric without swirl, we prove local well-posedness of Lipschitz continuous solutions and existence of traveling waves in the range 1 / 2 < a < 1 1/2<a<1 . These generalize the corresponding results for the 3D axisymmetric Euler equations and should be useful in the study of stability and instability for axisymmetric solutions.



1
Introduction

1.1
Active vector system

In this paper, we investigate the following active vector systems introduced in
[
11
]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}B+V\cdot\nabla B=B\cdot\nabla V,\\
&V=-\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-a}B,\\
&\nabla\cdot B=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
B(t,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a time-dependent divergence free vector field defined in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Alternatively, the system can be written in the more compact form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}B+\nabla\times((\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-a}B)\times B)=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot B=0.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

The system (
1.1
) generalizes three important physical models, namely the three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations, the electron magneto-hydrodynamics (E–MHD) system, and the entire family of generalized surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equations; see §
1.4
below for details. The recent paper
[
11
]
gave local well-posedness of smooth solutions for (
1.1
) in the range
\frac{1}{2}\leq a\leq 1
, and more generally when
(-\Delta)^{-a}B
is replaced with
\Gamma[B]
where
\Gamma
is a Fourier multiplier with a radial symbol which lies between
(-\Delta)^{-1}
and
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. In the current work, we focus on the case
(-\Delta)^{-a}
with
\frac{1}{2}<a<1
and initiate the study of
axi-symmetric
solutions, towards a better understanding of the issue of global regularity versus finite-time singularity formation. The study of axisymmetric Euler equation (corresponding to the case
a=1
) is a very classical subject, and the axisymmetric system in the E–MHD case (
a=0
) was recently studied in
[
13
,
12
,
37
,
46
]
. We shall provide several related works and motivations for the study of axi-symmetric solutions in §
1.4
after stating our main results in §
1.3
.

1.2
Axi-symmetric systems

By axi-symmetry, we mean solutions which are left invariant under all rotations fixing an axis; employing the cylindrical coordinate system
(r,\theta,z)
, we say that a vector field
B
is axi-symmetric if it has the form

\begin{split}B=b^{r}(r,z)e_{r}+b^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}+b^{z}(r,z)e_{z}.\end{split}

Then, we similarly have

\begin{split}V=v^{r}(r,z)e_{r}+v^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}+v^{z}(r,z)e_{z}.\end{split}

It will be useful to introduce the variable
U
defined by the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\times U=B,\\
&\nabla\cdot U=0\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

decaying at infinity. This is precisely the fluid velocity in the case of 3D Euler, namely when
a=1
. Under the assumption of axi-symmetry, we write

\begin{split}U=u^{r}(r,z)e_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z)e_{z}.\end{split}

Then, it turns out that (
1.1
) can be written as a system of equations for
b^{\theta}
and
u^{\theta}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}b^{\theta}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})b^{\theta}=\frac{v^{r}}{r}b^{\theta}-\frac{b^{r}}{r}v^{\theta}+(b^{r}\partial_{r}+b^{z}\partial_{z})v^{\theta},\\
&\partial_{t}u^{\theta}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})u^{\theta}=-\frac{v^{r}}{r}u^{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

The system is closed in terms of
(b^{\theta},u^{\theta})
since
u^{\theta}
at each moment of time determines
b^{r}
and
b^{z}
via
b^{r}=-\partial_{z}u^{\theta}
and
b^{z}=r^{-1}\partial_{r}(ru^{\theta}),
which follows from (
1.3
). In turn, components of
B
determine those of
V
, see (
1.7
) below. We have that
u^{\theta}\equiv 0
if and only if
v^{\theta}\equiv 0
, and therefore one may observe that the ansatz
u^{\theta}\equiv 0
propagates in time for (
1.4
). Under this assumption, we obtain the simpler
no–swirl
equation:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b^{\theta}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})b^{\theta}=\frac{v^{r}}{r}b^{\theta}.\end{split}

(1.5)

For convenience of the reader, we provide a derivation of (
1.4
) below. For 3D axi-symmetric Euler equations, such a formulation is well-known; see for instance
[
48
, Chap. 5]
.

1.3
Main results

The main results of this paper are local well-posedness in the Lipschitz function class and existence of traveling wave solutions in the same function class, in the range
\frac{1}{2}<a\leq 1
. The latter result generalizes known solutions in the three-dimensional Euler equations
(a=1)
and in particular shows that there are at least some non-trivial global strong solutions to the generalized systems (
1.1
).

To state our results, we define a space of Lipschitz continuous functions:

\begin{split}X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left\{f\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,:\,\nabla f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\}.\end{split}

For axisymmetric no-swirl equation, we provide local existence and uniqueness of solutions in the class
X^{1}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Well-posedness in the Lipschitz class)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<a<1
. Assume that
B_{0}\in X^{1}
is a divergence-free vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then, for any
T>0
, there is at most one solution to (
1.1
) belonging in
L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})
corresponding to
B_{0}
. Furthermore, if we assume in addition that
B_{0}
takes the form
b^{\theta}_{0}(r,z)e_{\theta}
, there exist
T=T(\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}})>0
and a solution
B=b^{\theta}e_{\theta}
to (
1.1
) in
L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})
, where
b^{\theta}
solves (
1.5
).

We emphasize that while the
existence
could be shown only for axisymmetric and no-swirl initial data, the
uniqueness
results holds not only within the axisymmetric equation but for the full system (
1.1
) (or equivalently (
1.2
)). This in particular rules out the possibility of non-axisymmetric Lipschitz solutions corresponding to an axisymmetric Lipschitz data.

Our second main result proves existence of non-trivial traveling wave solutions which belong to the above well-posedness class. For this, it will be convenient to introduce the
relative vorticity

\xi
: if
B(t,\mathbb{x})=b^{\theta}(t,r,z)e_{\theta}
, we define
\xi(t,r,z)=b^{\theta}(t,r,z)/r.
Then, by interpreting
\xi(t,\cdot)
as an axi-symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, (
1.5
) simply becomes

\partial_{t}\xi+V\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\xi=0,\quad t>0,\quad\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(1.6)

for the divergence free velocity
V=-(-\Delta)^{-a}\nabla\times B
.

Theorem 1.2

(Lipschitz continuous traveling waves)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<a<1
. Then, there exists a non-trivial traveling wave solution
\xi(t,\mathbb{x})=\bar{\xi}(x-Wte_{z})
for some
W>0
of (
1.6
) such that

\begin{split}\bar{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\mbox{ Lipschitz continuous, non-negative, and compactly supported.}\\
\end{split}

The proof is based on a variational approach. In particular, we shall follow the classical variational setup of Friedman–Turkington
[
35
, Theorem 2.2]
, see §
3.2
.

1.4
Discussion

Let us discuss several motivations to investigate traveling wave solutions of the system (
1.1
).

1.

Existence and stability of traveling waves
. For the axi-symmetric Euler equations, there is a huge body of literature on the existence and stability of traveling wave solutions, which are often referred to as
vortex rings
(see
[
36
,
50
,
49
,
35
,
3
,
6
,
10
,
15
]
and references therein). They correspond to ring-shaped vortex structures which can be observed in nature. Furthermore, such traveling waves can exist with non-zero
swirl
component of the velocity (
[
51
,
34
,
1
]
), which is interesting since they provide non-trivial examples of global strong solutions to the 3D Euler equations. The proof of existence and stability is usually achieved by a variational argument: after defining an appropriate admissible class with an energy function, the basic strategy is to solve the maximization problem. When a maximum exists, we have a traveling wave, and when the maximum exists uniquely, we obtain its stability. For the two-dimensional case, traveling waves have been studied for instance in
[
5
,
4
,
7
,
2
]
for the Euler equations and
[
8
,
9
]
for the generalized SQG equations.

Since the system (
1.1
) is a simultaneous generalization of 3D Euler and 2D SQG equations, our result on the existence of traveling waves can be considered as a simultaneous extension of existing works as well.

2.

Well-posedness issues
. The system (
1.1
) gives rise to challenging local and global well-posedness issues. The pioneering work
[
13
]
obtained finite-time singularity formation for smooth data in the case
a=0
, assuming well-posedness for axisymmetric data. Remarkably, the axisymmetric system in this case is simply given by the inviscid Burgers equation. While
[
37
]
proves that the initial value problem for (
1.1
) in the case
a=0
is
strongly ill-posed
in Sobolev (and even in any Gevrey) spaces, it does not exclude the possibility that there is a very special type of local well-posedness near the initial data used in
[
13
]
. The ill-posedness is expected to hold as well in the range
a<1/2
, but this seems to be a very technically challenging problem. On the other hand,
[
11
]
obtained local well-posedness in sufficiently regular Sobolev spaces when
a\geq 1/2
. The uniqueness criterion is improved to the Lipschitz class in the current work, assuming
a>1/2
.

3.

Infinite norm growth via instability of traveling waves
. In the past decade, there have been significant progress on the problem of
infinite norm growth
, or in other words, creation of arbitrarily small scales for fluid models (
[
22
,
24
,
25
,
23
,
41
,
43
,
53
,
52
,
42
,
31
,
39
,
38
,
44
,
20
,
18
,
16
,
17
,
21
,
26
,
30
,
29
,
32
,
28
,
14
]
). We refer the interested reader to the excellent survey papers
[
45
,
40
,
27
]
. A common feature of these works is that the instability result is based on a stability statement of the “background” solution in a weaker topology. In unbounded domains like
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, traveling waves constructed by variational principles are perfect candidates for such background flows; they are expected to be stable in some
L^{p}
norms of the vorticity, which gives a pointwise control on the velocity perturbation; see
[
19
,
16
]
.

Therefore, it is expected that the traveling waves constructed in this work can be applied to produce infinite growth results for smooth solutions to (
1.1
).

1.5
Derivation of axi-symmetric system

We briefly discuss how to arrive at the system (
1.4
). From the following formula for the convective derivative

\begin{split}(V\cdot\nabla)B&=\left(v^{r}\partial_{r}b^{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z}b^{r}-r^{-1}v^{\theta}b^{\theta}\right)e_{r}+\left(v^{r}\partial_{r}b^{\theta}+v^{z}\partial_{z}b^{\theta}+r^{-1}v^{\theta}b^{r}\right)e_{\theta}+\left(v^{r}\partial_{r}b^{z}+v^{z}\partial_{z}b^{z}\right)e_{z},\end{split}

we obtain the equation of
b^{\theta}
in (
1.4
) by taking the
\theta
–component of the equation of
B
in (
1.1
). Next, by “integrating” the form (
1.2
), we obtain the following equation of
U

\begin{split}\partial_{t}U+(\nabla\times U)\times V+\nabla q=0,\end{split}

with some scalar-valued function
q
. Then, taking the
\theta
–component of this equation gives the equation of
u^{\theta}
in (
1.4
). Next, let us demonstrate the relationship between
V
,
U
, and
B
, which allows us to close the system (
1.4
) in terms of
(b^{\theta},u^{\theta})
.

Relation between
V
and
U
.
From the relation between “two velocities”

V=\Gamma\Delta U=-(-\Delta)^{1-a}U
, we have

\begin{split}V(x)=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{U(x)-U(y)}{|x-y|^{5-2a}}\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Taking the second component, evaluating at
x=(r,0,z)
and switching to the cylindrical coordinates,

\begin{split}v^{\theta}(r,z)=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{u^{\theta}(r,z)-u^{\theta}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})\cos(\theta^{\prime})}{(r^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\theta^{\prime})+(r^{\prime})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}-a}}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}.\end{split}

Similarly, taking
x=(r,0,z)
and the first and third components give

\begin{split}v^{r}(r,z)=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{u^{r}(r,z)-u^{r}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})\cos(\theta^{\prime})}{(r^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\theta^{\prime})+(r^{\prime})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}-a}}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}v^{z}(r,z)=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{u^{z}(r,z)-u^{z}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})}{(r^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\theta^{\prime})+(r^{\prime})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}-a}}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime},\end{split}

respectively.

Relation between
V
and
B
.

From
V=-(-\Delta)^{-a}\nabla\times B
, we have

\begin{split}V(x)=-\frac{C_{a}}{2(1-a)}\int\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{-2a+5}}\times B(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

(1.7)

In the case
a=\frac{1}{2}
, this integral must be defined in the sense of principal value. Taking the first component of (
1.7
) and evaluating at
x=(r,0,z)
, we obtain the following useful relation between
b^{\theta}
and
v^{r}
:

\begin{split}v^{r}(r,z)=\frac{C_{a}}{2(1-a)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(z-z^{\prime})\cos(\theta^{\prime})b^{\theta}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})}{(r^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\theta^{\prime})+(r^{\prime})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}-a}}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}.\end{split}

Organization of the paper

We shall provide proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in §
2
and
3
, respectively.

2
Local well-posedness

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1. A priori estimates
. We assume that there is a solution
b^{\theta}
to (
1.5
) satisfying
B=b^{\theta}e_{\theta}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. To begin with, since
\tilde{v}
is divergence free in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|{\frac{v^{r}}{r}b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|{\frac{v^{r}}{r}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{1}}.\end{split}

We estimate

\begin{split}\|{\frac{v^{r}}{r}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|{\nabla V}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-2a}\|{\nabla B}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2a}\leq C\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}.\end{split}

This gives

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{1}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we may obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}\|{b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\end{split}

holds. Next, we write down the equations for
r^{-1}b^{\theta}
,
\partial_{r}b^{\theta}
, and
\partial_{z}b^{\theta}
:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\frac{b^{\theta}}{r}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})\frac{b^{\theta}}{r}=0,\end{split}

(2.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\partial_{r}b^{\theta}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})\partial_{r}b^{\theta}=-\frac{v^{r}}{r}\frac{b^{\theta}}{r}+\partial_{r}v^{r}\frac{b^{\theta}}{r}+\frac{v^{r}}{r}\partial_{r}b^{\theta}-\partial_{r}\widetilde{v}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}b^{\theta},\end{split}

(2.2)

and

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\partial_{z}b^{\theta}+(v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{z}\partial_{z})\partial_{z}b^{\theta}=\partial_{z}v^{r}\frac{b^{\theta}}{r}+\frac{v^{r}}{r}\partial_{z}b^{\theta}-\partial_{z}\widetilde{v}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}b^{\theta}.\end{split}

Using the fact that
\|{\nabla B}\|_{L^{\infty}}
is equivalent, up to constants, with

\begin{split}\|{r^{-1}b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\partial_{z}b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\partial_{r}b^{\theta}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\end{split}

and estimating the right hand sides of (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) gives

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|{\nabla B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\leq C\|{\nabla V}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\nabla B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}\|{\nabla B}\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

Combining the estimates, we arrive at

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}\leq C\|{B}\|_{X^{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

(2.3)

In particular, there is
T>0
depending only on
\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}}
such that
\|{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})}\leq 2\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}}
.

2. Existence
. To prove the existence of a solution in
L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})
, we consider the sequence of mollified initial data
\left\{B_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right\}
. (Here, we can take the convolution with a radial mollifier, which guarantees that the mollified sequence of data is axisymmetric without swirl.) Since each
B_{0}^{\varepsilon}
is smooth and decaying at infinity, there exists a unique corresponding local smooth solution to (
1.5
) (
[
11
]
), which we denote by
B^{\varepsilon}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, the a priori estimate (
2.3
) can be justified, which gives a uniform time
T>0
depending only on
\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}}
such that the solution
B^{\varepsilon}
remains smooth on
[0,T]
and satisfies the uniform bound

\begin{split}\|{B^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})}\leq 2\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Here, we used that the Lipschitz norm of
B^{\varepsilon}
is a blow-up criterion for (
1.5
). We can then extract a subsequence, still denoted by
\{B^{\varepsilon}\}
, such that
B^{\varepsilon}
is locally uniformly convergent in
C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Denoting the limit by
B
, we first see that
B(t=0)=B_{0}
. Next, from the uniform bound (
2.4
) and pointwise convergence, we obtain that

\begin{split}\|{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})}\leq 2\|{B_{0}}\|_{X^{1}}.\end{split}

Lastly, using a weak formulation of (
1.5
), it is not difficult to see that
B
is a weak solution to (
1.5
). This finishes the proof of existence.

3. Uniqueness
. To prove uniqueness, we assume that there are two solutions
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
to (
1.1
) which belong to
L^{\infty}([0,T];X^{1})
for some
T>0
, corresponding to the same initial data
B_{0}\in X^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We need to used the velocity formulation: denote the corresponding velocity equations by

\begin{split}\partial_{t}U_{i}+(\nabla\times U_{i})\times V_{i}+\nabla q_{i}=0,\end{split}

for
i=1,2
. We write the equation for the difference
D=U_{1}-U_{2}
:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}D+(\nabla\times D)\times V_{1}+(\nabla\times U_{2})\times(V_{1}-V_{2})+\nabla(q_{1}-q_{2})=0.\end{split}

Taking the dot product of both sides with
D
and integrating,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=I+II+III,\end{split}

with

\begin{split}I=-\int((\nabla\times D)\times V_{1})\cdot D,\end{split}

\begin{split}II=-\int((\nabla\times U_{2})\times(V_{1}-V_{2}))\cdot D,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}III=-\int\nabla(q_{1}-q_{2})\cdot D=0.\end{split}

Note that

\begin{split}I=\int(D\times(\nabla\times D))\cdot V_{1},\end{split}

and the pointwise identity

\begin{split}D\times(\nabla\times D)=\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{i}(D_{j}^{2})\right)-\partial_{i}(D_{i}D_{j})+D_{j}\partial_{i}D_{i}\right).\end{split}

The last term can be written as

\begin{split}\sum_{i\neq j}D_{j}(\partial_{i}D_{i})=\sum_{i,j}D_{j}(\partial_{i}D_{i})-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}(D_{i})^{2}=-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}(D_{i})^{2}\end{split}

using
\sum_{i}\partial_{i}D_{i}=0
. Therefore, we can integrate by parts to obtain

\begin{split}\left|I\right|\leq C\|{\nabla V_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we rewrite
II
as

\begin{split}II=\int(B_{2}\times(-\Delta)^{1-a}D)\cdot D\end{split}

by recalling
B_{2}=\nabla\times U_{2}
. This can be further written as

\begin{split}II=\frac{1}{2}\int[B_{2}\times,(-\Delta)^{1-a}]D\cdot D,\end{split}

and we see that

\begin{split}[B_{2}\times,(-\Delta)^{1-a}]D\,(x)=C_{a}\int\frac{(B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)}}\times D(y)dy.\end{split}

To estimate this function, we introduce a cutoff
0\leq\chi(|x-y|)\leq 1
which is equal to 1 for
|x-y|<R
, 0 for
|x-y|>2R
for some
R>0
to be determined. Then, we estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int\chi(|x-y|)\frac{(B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)}}\times D(y)dy\right|\leq C\|{\nabla B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int\frac{\chi(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)-1}}|D(y)|dy,\end{split}

using the mean value theorem for
B_{2}
. Then, the kernel is integrable, and we obtain that

\begin{split}\left\|\int\frac{\chi(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)-1}}|D(y)|dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq CR^{2a-1}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

using Young’s inequality. Next, we estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int\frac{(B_{2}(x)-B_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)}}\left(1-\chi(|x-y|)\right)\times D(y)dy\right|\leq C\|{B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int\frac{1-\chi(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)}}|D(y)|dy\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left\|\int\frac{1-\chi(|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{3+2(1-a)}}|D(y)|dy\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq CR^{2a-2}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Combining the estimates and choosing
R=\|{B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}/\|{\nabla B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\|{[B_{2}\times,(-\Delta)^{1-a}]D}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{a}\|{\nabla B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2-2a}\|{B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2a-1}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore, using the bounds for
I
and
II
,

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|\leq C_{a}\left(\|{\nabla V_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2-2a}\|{B_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2a-1}\right)\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{a}(\|{B_{1}}\|_{X^{1}}+\|{B_{2}}\|_{X^{1}})\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Using Gronwall’s inequality with
D(t=0)=0
gives
\|{D}\|_{L^{2}}(t)=0
, which concludes the proof of uniqueness. ∎

3
Existence of traveling waves

In this section, we prove existence of Lipschitz traveling wave solutions of (
1.6
). In the sequel,
C_{a}
is some positive constant depending only on the value of
a
.

3.1
Stream function and energy

For

B(\mathbb{x})=b^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}
, the corresponding vector stream
\Phi:=(-\Delta)^{-a}B
has the form

\Phi(\mathbb{x})=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{y}|^{3-2a}}B(\mathbb{y})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{y}=\phi^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}(\theta).

We define the (scalar) stream
\psi
and the (relative) vorticity
\xi
by

\psi(r,z):=r\phi^{\theta}(r,z)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\xi(r,z)=b^{\theta}(r,z)/r.

Then we have for
\Pi:=\{(r,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,r>0\},

\begin{split}\psi(r,z)&=C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\left[\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{r\bar{r}\cos(\theta)}{\left({r^{2}-2r\bar{r}\cos(\theta)+\bar{r}^{2}+(z-\bar{z})^{2}}\right)^{(3-2a)/2}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right]\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\\
&=:C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\left[G_{a}(r,z,\bar{r},\bar{z})\right]\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}=:\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi].\end{split}

(3.1)

Denoting

\begin{split}s(r,\bar{r},z,\bar{z}):=\left(\frac{(r-\bar{r})^{2}+(z-\bar{z})^{2}}{r\bar{r}}\right)\end{split}

gives

\begin{split}\psi(r,z)&=C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\left[{(r\bar{r})^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{F}_{a}(s)\right]\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\end{split}

(3.2)

with

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{a}(s)&:=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\cos(\theta)}{(2(1-\cos(\theta))+s)^{\frac{3-2a}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad s>0.\end{split}

(3.3)

We have the following elementary estimate on
\mathcal{F}_{a}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for
0\leq a<1
;

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{a}(s)\lesssim_{\tau,a}s^{-\tau},\qquad\tau\in[1-a,(5/2)-a].\end{split}

(3.4)

Indeed, it can be proved by modifying the argument of
[
33
]
, which covers the case
a=1
(the axisymmetric Euler).

The velocity
V=v^{r}e_{r}+v^{z}e_{z}
is given by

\begin{split}v^{r}(r,z)=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{z}\psi(r,z),\qquad v^{z}(r,z)=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\psi(r,z),\end{split}

(3.5)

and one may check directly the divergence free condition
\partial_{r}(rv^{r})+\partial_{z}(rv^{z})=0.

If we interpret
\xi
as an axi-symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(
i.e.

\xi(\mathbb{x})=\xi(r,z)
), it solves

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\xi+V\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\xi=0,\end{split}

which gives the fact that any
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
norm is preserved:
\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|\xi_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},
for any
t\geq 0
. In addition,
we have conservation of the impulse:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi(t,\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi_{0}(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}.

From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we obtain expressions

\begin{split}v^{r}(r,z)=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{z}\psi(r,z)=C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\frac{\bar{z}-z}{r(r\bar{r})^{\frac{3}{2}-a}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(s)\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z},\end{split}

\begin{split}v^{z}(r,z)=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\psi(r,z)=C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\left(\frac{2(r-\bar{r})}{r(r\bar{r})^{\frac{3}{2}-a}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(s)+\frac{(r\bar{r})^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{2}}\left[(a-\frac{1}{2})\mathcal{F}(s)-s\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(s)\right]\right)\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}.\end{split}

(3.6)

We define the
energy of the flow

\begin{split}E[\xi]&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi(\mathbb{x})\xi(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}=C_{a}\int_{\Pi}\int_{\Pi}\left[{(r\bar{r})^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{F}_{a}(s)\right]\xi(\bar{r},\bar{z})\bar{r}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,\xi(r,z)r\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

(3.7)

3.2
Variational framework and key proposition

We fix some
\frac{1}{2}<a<1
and adopt the variational setting of Friedman–Turkington
[
35
]
: For
\mu>0
, we consider the
admissible class

\begin{split}K_{\mu}&:=\left\{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad|\quad\xi=\xi(r,z)\geq 0,\quad\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\mu,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq 1\right\},\end{split}

and try to maximize the quantity

\begin{split}E_{2}[\xi]&:=E[\xi]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}.\end{split}

We define the maximum value and the maximizer set respectively by

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\mu}&:=\sup_{\xi\in K_{\mu}}E_{2}[\xi],\qquad S_{\mu}:=\{\xi\in K_{\mu}\quad|\quad E_{2}[\xi]=I_{\mu}\}.\end{split}

We now introduce the key proposition, which implies Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu>0
. There exists
\xi\in S_{\mu}
such that there exists a unique pair
(W,\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
W>0
,
\gamma\geq 0
satisfying

\xi=(\Psi)_{+},

(3.8)

where

\Psi(\mathbb{x}):=\psi(\mathbb{x})-\frac{1}{2}Wr^{2}-\gamma\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi:=\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi].

Moreover,
\xi
is non-negative, bounded, Lipshitz continuous, and compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and for each
r>0
,
\xi(r,\cdot_{z})
is nonincreasing as a function of
z>0
and

\xi(r,z)=\xi(r,-z)
.

Remark 3.2
.

1.

The relation (
3.8
) implies

\nabla^{\perp}_{r,z}\Psi\cdot\nabla_{r,z}\xi=0.

Thus the above proposition with (
3.5
) guarantees the existence of
\xi
satisfying

v^{r}\partial_{r}\xi+(v^{z}-W)\partial_{z}\xi=0

As a result,
\xi(\mathbb{x}-Wte_{z})

is
a traveling wave solution
of (
1.6
) for Theorem
1.2
. Lipschitzness of
\xi
follows from the relation (
3.8
)
since both
\psi=\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi]
(for
a>1/2
) and the map
s\mapsto s_{+}
are Lipschitz.

2.

In order to get more regular
\xi
than Proposition
3.1
does, one has to consider more regular vorticity function (e.g. see
[
9
]
for 2D generalized SQG equations). Indeed, in the proof of the proposition, we used Lipschitz vorticity
f(s)=s^{+}
. Instead, we may use smoother
f
such as
f(s)=(s^{+})^{1+\varepsilon},\varepsilon>0
.

3.

If one just want to prove existence of traveling waves for
a\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then one may follow the approach of
[
8
]
where the authors proved the existence of traveling dipoles for 2D generalized SQG equations. However, when
a\leq\frac{1}{2}
, it is a difficult question whether the obtained
\xi
is Lipschitz or not since regularity of the stream
\Phi=(-\Delta)^{-a}B
may not be Lipschitz.

3.3
Elementary estimates

We first estimate the stream
\psi
for
\xi\in L^{2}\cap L^{1}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For axi-symmetric
\xi\in\left(L^{2}\cap L^{1}\right)(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the stream function
\psi=\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi]
satisfies

|\psi(r,z)|\lesssim_{a}r^{1+a}\|\xi\|_{L^{3/(a+1)}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+r^{2a}\|\xi\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad\forall(r,z)\in\Pi.

(3.9)

Proof.

We decompose

|\psi(r,z)|\lesssim\int_{\Pi}G_{a}(r,z,r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|\xi(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}=\int_{t<r/2}+\int_{t\geq r/2}=:I+II,

for

t:=\sqrt{(r-r^{\prime})^{2}+(z-z^{\prime})^{2}}.

For the term
I
, by using (
3.4
) with

\tau=1-a
, and by taking any
p>1
satisfying
\tau p<1
, Hölder’s inequality
with
(1/p)+(1/p^{\prime})=1

implies that

\begin{split}I&\lesssim\int_{t<r/2}\frac{(rr^{\prime})^{a-\frac{1}{2}+\tau}}{t^{2\tau}}|\xi(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}\lesssim\left(\int_{t<r/2}\frac{(rr^{\prime})^{p(a-\frac{1}{2}+\tau)}}{t^{2\tau p}}r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}\right)^{1/p}\|\xi 1_{\{t<r/2\}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\lesssim r^{2a-1+\frac{3}{p}}\|\xi 1_{\{t<r/2\}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used
r\sim r^{\prime}
.
Thus by setting

\frac{1}{p}=1-a+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,a-\frac{1}{2}]

we have

I\lesssim\ r^{2-a+3\varepsilon}\|\xi 1_{\{t<r/2\}}\|_{L^{1/(a-\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

We can set
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{3}(2a-1)
so that
0<\varepsilon<\min\{\frac{a}{3},a-\frac{1}{2}\}
whenever
\frac{1}{2}<a<1
. It gives the first term in (
3.9
).

For the term
II
, we take
\tau=\frac{5}{2}-a
in (
3.4
). Since
r^{\prime}\leq 3t
for
t\geq r/2
, we obtain

\begin{split}II&\lesssim\int_{t\geq r/2}\frac{(rr^{\prime})^{2}}{t^{5-2a}}|\xi(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}\lesssim\int_{t\geq r/2}\frac{r^{2}}{t^{3-2a}}|\xi(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}\\
&\lesssim r^{2}\left(\int_{t\geq r/2}\frac{1}{t^{(3-2a)3}}r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}\right)^{1/3}\|\xi\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim r^{2a}\|\xi\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\end{split}

We have proved
(
3.9
). ∎

Now we estimate the energy
E
for
\xi\in L^{1}_{w}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{1}
, where we say that
\xi
lies on
L^{1}_{w}
if

\|\xi\|_{L^{1}_{w}}:=\|r^{2}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}|\xi|\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}<\infty.

Lemma 3.4
.

For axi-symmetric
\xi\in\left(L^{1}_{w}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{1}\right)(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

|E[\xi]|\leq E[|\xi|]\lesssim_{a}\left(\|\xi\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\xi\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)(\|\xi\|_{L^{1}_{w}}+\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}).

(3.10)

Proof.

We first use (
3.9
) to estimate for axi-symmetric
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\left(L^{1}_{w}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{1}\right)
,

\begin{split}\int_{\Pi}\left(\int_{\Pi}G_{a}(r,z,r^{\prime}z^{\prime})|\xi_{1}(r,z)|rdrdz\right)|\xi_{2}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}dr^{\prime}dz^{\prime}&\lesssim\left(\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}}\right)\int_{\Pi}[1+(r^{\prime})^{2}]|\xi_{2}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})|r^{\prime}drdz\\
&\lesssim\left(\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\|\xi_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}}\right)(\|\xi_{2}\|_{L^{1}_{w}}+\|\xi_{2}\|_{L^{1}}).\end{split}

Then, the estimate (
3.10
) follows by setting
\xi_{1}\equiv\xi_{2}
.
∎

In the next lemma, we show regularity of the stream
\Phi
when
\xi
is bounded and supported in
\{r<R\}
for some
R<\infty
. The result will be used to prove that a maximizer
\xi\in S_{\mu}
is Lipschitz and compactly supported.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\xi\in\left(L^{1}_{w}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{1}\right)(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be axi-symmetric. If we assume further

\xi\in L^{\infty}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mbox{spt}\,(\xi)\subset\{r<R\}\quad\mbox{for some}\quad R<\infty,

(3.11)

then

\Phi:=(-\Delta)^{-a}B\quad\mbox{lies on}\quad C^{1,\alpha}\cap W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.12)

for some
\alpha=\alpha(a)>0
and
q=q(a)>1
with decay

\frac{\Phi(\mathbb{x})}{r}=\frac{\Phi(\mathbb{x})}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad|\mathbb{x}|\to\infty.

(3.13)

Moreover, the energy of
\xi
defined by (
3.7
) is nonnegative with the identity

E[\xi]=C_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{a/2}B|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}\geq 0.

(3.14)

Proof.

The assumption (
3.11
) gives
B=B(\mathbb{x})=r\xi(r,z)e_{\theta}(\theta)\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Since
\nabla\Phi=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-a}B
with
a>1/2
,
we have

\nabla\Phi\in L^{q}\cap C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.15)

for any
q>\frac{3}{4-2a}
and for any
0<\alpha<2a-1

by Sobolev embedding.
Similarly,

\Phi=(-\Delta)^{-a}B\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any
q>\frac{3}{3-2a}
.
Hence, we get

\Phi\in W^{1,q}\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\mbox{for any}\quad q>\frac{3}{3-2a}\quad\mbox{and for any}\quad\alpha\in(0,2a-1).

To show the decay (
3.13
),
we recall (
3.15
).
By Hardy’s inequality,
\nabla\Phi\in L^{q}
implies

\Phi/r\in L^{q}
. On the other hand,

\Phi\in C^{1,\alpha}
with
\Phi|_{r=0}=0
implies

\Phi/r\in C^{\alpha}
. Hence we obtain the decay (
3.13
).

For the identity (
3.14
), we observe that
\Gamma^{1/2}B
lies on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for any

p>\frac{3}{3-a}
. In particular, we get

\Gamma^{1/2}B\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, the identity follows from (
3.7
) by integration by parts:

\displaystyle E[\xi]

\displaystyle=\int B\cdot\Phi\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\int(-\Delta)^{-a}B\cdot B\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\int|(-\Delta)^{-a/2}B|^{2}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}.\qed

3.4
Proof of existence of a maximizer (Proposition
3.1
)

As we defined
K_{\mu},I_{\mu},
and
S_{\mu}
, we additionally define

\begin{split}K_{\mu}^{\prime}&:=\left\{\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad|\quad\xi=\xi(r,z)\geq 0,\quad\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq\mu,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi(\mathbb{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq 1\right\},\\
I_{\mu}^{\prime}&:=\sup_{\xi\in K_{\mu}^{\prime}}E_{2}[\xi],\\
S_{\mu}^{\prime}&:=\{\xi\in K_{\mu}^{\prime}\quad|\quad E_{2}[\xi]=I_{\mu}^{\prime}\}.\end{split}

Since
0\in K_{\mu}^{\prime}
, we simply note
K_{\mu}\subsetneq K_{\mu}^{\prime}
and
I_{\mu}\leq I_{\mu}^{\prime}
. In the sequel, we will prove

I_{\mu}^{\prime}=I_{\mu}\in(0,\infty)\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{\mu}^{\prime}=S_{\mu}\neq\emptyset.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mu>0
. Then we have

0<I_{\mu}\leq I_{\mu}^{\prime}<\infty,

(3.16)

and the value is strictly increasing :

I_{\mu_{1}}<I_{\mu_{2}}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad 0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\infty.

(3.17)

Proof.

By (
3.10
) and Young’s inequality, we have, for any
\xi\in K^{\prime}_{\mu}
,

\begin{split}E[\xi]&\leq C\left(\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\xi\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)(\|\xi\|_{L^{1}_{w}}+\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})\leq C(\mu+1)+C(\mu+1)\|\xi\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq C(\mu+1)+C(\mu+1)\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(\mu+1)+C(\mu+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\end{split}

(3.18)

It implies

E_{2}[\xi]=E[\xi]-\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(\mu+1)+C(\mu+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(\mu+1)^{2}<\infty,

which gives
I_{\mu}^{\prime}<\infty
.

To prove
I_{\mu}>0
, we set
\xi_{1}=1_{B_{a}(0)}
for
B_{a}(0)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ |\ |x|<a\}
and choose
a>0
so that
\frac{1}{2}\int r^{2}\xi_{1}(x)\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\mu
. We set
\xi_{\sigma}(x)=\sigma^{5}\xi_{1}(\sigma x)
for
0<\sigma<1
, and observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi_{\sigma}(x)\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi_{1}(x)\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\mu,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi_{\sigma}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\sigma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi_{1}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq 1,

\displaystyle E_{2}[\xi_{\sigma}]=\sigma^{5-2a}\left(E[\xi_{1}]-\sigma^{2+2a}\int\xi_{1}^{2}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}\right).

Thanks to
\xi\geq 0
, we have
\psi\geq 0
. Then the definition of the energy (
3.7
) gives
E[\xi_{1}]>0
. Thus
for sufficiently small
0<\sigma\ll 1
,
\xi_{\sigma}\in K_{\mu}
satisfies

\displaystyle I_{\mu}\geq E_{2}[\xi_{\sigma}]>0,

which proved (
3.16
).

For monotonicity in
\mu>0
, we fix any
0<\sigma<1
, take any
\xi\in K_{\mu}
and set

\xi^{\sigma}(x)=\sigma^{\frac{5}{2}+a}\xi(\sigma x)
. It gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r^{2}\xi^{\sigma}(x)\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}+a}\mu>\mu,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi^{\sigma}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq\sigma^{a-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\xi^{\sigma})^{2}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}=\sigma^{2+2a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\xi)^{2}\textrm{d}\mathbb{x},

\displaystyle E[\xi^{\sigma}]=E[\xi].

So we have
\xi^{\sigma}\in K_{\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}+a}\mu}
and

\begin{split}I_{\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}+a}\mu}\geq E_{2}[\xi^{\sigma}]&=E[\xi]-\sigma^{2+2a}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=E_{2}[\xi]+\underbrace{(1-\sigma^{2+2a})}_{>0}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}>E_{2}[\xi].\end{split}

(3.19)

By taking
\sup_{\xi\in L_{\mu}}
, we get

I_{\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}+a}\mu}\geq I_{\mu}.

For strictness in the monotonicity (
3.17
), let’s assume

I_{\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}+a}\mu}=I_{\mu}

(3.20)

for a contradiction. Then take a sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset K_{\mu}
satisfying
E_{2}[\xi_{n}]\to I_{\mu}
. This hypothesis (
3.20
) together with (
3.19
) implies

\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\to 0.

The estimate (
3.10
) of Lemma
3.4
gives

\begin{split}|E[\xi_{n}]|&\leq C\left(\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{a+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)(\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{w}}+\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})\\
&\leq C(\mu+1)\left(\|\xi_{n}\|^{\frac{1}{3}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\xi_{n}\|^{\frac{2}{3}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\xi_{n}\|^{\frac{2a-1}{3}}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\xi_{n}\|^{\frac{4-2a}{3}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\quad\to\quad 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.\end{split}

These two convergences mean
I_{\mu}=\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{2}[\xi_{n}]=0
, which contradicts the fact
I_{\mu}>0
from (
3.16
). As a result, we obtain the strict monotonicity (
3.17
). ∎

We observe that any maximizing sequence is bounded in
L^{2}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mu>0
. Then any sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset K_{\mu}
satisfying
E_{2}[\xi_{n}]\to I_{\mu}
satisfies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\xi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty.

Proof.

By using the estimate (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=E[\xi_{n}]-E_{2}[\xi_{n}]\leq C(\mu+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}-E_{2}[\xi_{n}],

which implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\xi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(\mu+1)^{2}-I_{\mu}\leq C(\mu+1)^{2}<\infty.\qed

The following lemma is useful when we need convergence of the energy for a weak-convergent sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}
when the energy of each member
\xi_{n}
is uniformly concentrated in a fixed bounded set.

Lemma 3.8
.

For non-negative axi-symmetric functions
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\left(L^{1}_{w}\cap L^{2}\cap L^{1}\right)(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and for axi-symmetric set

Lemma 3.9

\displaystyle||\xi^{*}||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=||\xi||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\quad 1\leq q\leq 2,\quad||r^{2}\xi^{*}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=||r^{2}\xi||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad\mbox{and}\quad E[\xi^{*}]\geq E[\xi].

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of axi-symmetric non-negative functions on

such that

\displaystyle\xi_{n}=(\xi_{n})^{*}\quad\mbox{for each}\quad n,\qquad\sup_{n}\left\{||\xi_{n}||_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}+||r^{2}\xi_{n}||_{1}\right\}<\infty,\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\xi_{n}\rightharpoonup\xi\quad\textrm{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\textrm{as}\quad n\to\infty\quad\mbox{for some non-negative axi-symmetric}\quad\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

as

Proof.

First, we observe, by the weak convergence
\xi_{n}\rightharpoonup\xi
in

,

\|\xi\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}+\|r^{2}\xi\|_{1}\leq C\quad\mbox{for some }\,C>0.

We set a bounded domain

for
R\geq 1
and
A\geq 1
.
Then, by (

\begin{split}\left|E[\xi_{n}]-E[\xi]\right|&\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\left|\int_{{Q}}\int_{{Q}}{G}_{a}(x,y)\Big(\xi_{n}(x)\xi_{n}(y)-\xi(x)\xi(y)\Big)\,dxdy\right|+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{Q}}\xi_{n}\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi_{n}]\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus{Q}}\xi\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi]\,dx.\end{split}

Since
\xi_{n}
satisfies the monotonicity condition (
3.22
) for each

, so does
\xi
. Thus
we can estimate, by (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash Q}\xi\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi]\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}+\frac{1}{R^{1-a}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash Q}\xi_{n}\mathcal{G}_{a}[\xi_{n}]\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}+\frac{1}{R^{1-a}}\right).

On the other hand, we can simply check that the kernel

is locally square integrable

Lemma 3.12

Proof.

By the energy inequality, we know

Then, taking the limit

, we get

.
Since
\{\xi^{*}_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in

On the other hand, by taking

It proves

.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\psi-\xi)\eta\textrm{d}\mathbb{x}\leq 0.

On Induced Subgraphs of the Hamming Graph
Dingding Dong

Abstract
In connection with his solution of the Sensitivity Conjecture, Hao Huang (arXiv: 1907.00847, 2019) asked the following question: Given a graph G G with high symmetry, what can we say about the smallest maximum degree of induced subgraphs of G G with α ⁡ ( G ) + 1 \alpha(G)+1 vertices, where α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) denotes the size of the largest independent set in G G ? We study this question for H ⁡ ( n , k ) H(n,k) , the n n -dimensional Hamming graph over an alphabet of size k k . Generalizing a construction by Chung et al. (JCT-A, 1988), we prove that H ⁡ ( n , k ) H(n,k) has an induced subgraph with more than α ⁡ ( H ⁡ ( n , k ) ) \alpha(H(n,k)) vertices and maximum degree at most ⌈ n ⌉ \lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil . Chung et al. proved this statement for k = 2 k=2 (the n n -dimensional cube).

Preliminaries and notation

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, define
\Delta(G)
to be the maximum degree of
G
. For a subset
W\subseteq V
, define
G[W]
to be the induced subgraph of
G
on vertex set
W
. It will be convenient to take
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}={\mathbb{Z}}/k{\mathbb{Z}}
as the alphabet for the Hamming graph, so the set of vertices of
H(n,k)
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}
. We view the elements
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}
as functions from
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
and set
v=(v(1),\dots,v(n))
. It is not hard to see that for all
k,n\geq 1
, we have
\alpha(H(n,k))=k^{n-1}
.

3
Constructing a family of induced subgraphs

In this section, we construct a family
\mathcal{A}
of
k^{2}
induced bipartite subgraphs of
H(n,k)
. In Section
4
we show that (a) each member of
\mathcal{A}
is bipartite (Proposition
4.1
), (b) each member of
\mathcal{A}
has maximum degree at most
\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil
(Proposition
4.5
), and (c) at least one member of
\mathcal{A}
has more than
\alpha(H(n,k))
vertices (Proposition
4.7
). Theorem
1.2
is an immediate consequence of these statements.

Following Chung et al.
[
1
]
, we note that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a partition
[n]=F_{1}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}\dots\overset{\cdot}{\cup}F_{q}
such that
|q-\sqrt{n}|<1
and each
||F_{j}|-\sqrt{n}|<1
. We fix such a partition for the rest of this paper and use it to define partitions of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}
, the set of vertices.

Definition 3.1
.

We partition the vertex set of
H(n,k)
as

{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}=X\overset{\cdot}{\cup}Y

where

X:=\left\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:F_{j}\subseteq v^{-1}(0)\text{ for some }j\in[q]\right\},

(1)

Y:=\left\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:F_{j}\not\subseteq v^{-1}(0)\text{ for all }j\in[q]\right\}.

(2)

Definition 3.2
.

We further partition
X
and
Y
as

X=X_{0}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}X_{1}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}\dots\overset{\cdot}{\cup}X_{k-1},

Y=Y_{0}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}Y_{1}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}\dots\overset{\cdot}{\cup}Y_{k-1}

where

X_{i}:=X\cap\left\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}v(\ell)=i\right\},

(3)

Y_{i}:=Y\cap\left\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}v(\ell)=i\right\}.

(4)

We consider the following family of
k^{2}
induced subgraphs of
H(n,k)
.

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}]:i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}\right\}.

(5)

4
Analysis of the construction

In this section, we prove properties (a), (b) and (c) of the family
\mathcal{A}
indicated in the first paragraph of Section
3
. We first address (a).

Proposition 4.1
.

Each of the sets
X_{i}
,
Y_{i}

(i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k})
is independent in
H(n,k)
. In particular, for all
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
, the induced subgraph
H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}]
is bipartite.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the coordinates of vertices in each of the sets
X_{i}
,
Y_{i}
have the same sum.
∎

4.1
Maximum degree bound

We prove that for all
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
, the maximum degree of
H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}]
is at most
\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil
. First notice that if
v\in X
has a neighbor in
Y
, then
v^{-1}(0)
contains exactly one of
F_{1},\dots,F_{q}\,
. By contradiction, if
v^{-1}(0)
contains more than one of
F_{1},\dots,F_{q}\,
, then any neighbor of
v
still contains some
F_{j}
and therefore cannot be in
Y
. This allows us to make the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

For any
v\in X
with a neighbor in
Y
, define
j(v)
to be the unique
j\in[q]
such that
F_{j}\subseteq v^{-1}(0)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Fix
v_{2}\in Y
. If
v_{1},v_{1}^{\prime}\in X
are neighbors of
v_{2}
and
j(v_{1})=j(v_{1}^{\prime})
, then
v_{1}=v_{1}^{\prime}\,
. In particular, each vertex in
Y
has at most
q
neighbors in
X
.

Proof.

Suppose
v_{1}\in X
is a neighbor of
v_{2}
. Since
F_{j(v_{1})}\subseteq v_{1}^{-1}(0)
and
F_{j(v_{1})}\not\subseteq v_{2}^{-1}(0)
, there exists some
\ell_{0}\in F_{j(v_{1})}
such that
\left(v_{2}^{-1}(0)\cap F_{j(v_{1})}\right)\overset{\cdot}{\cup}\{\ell_{0}\}=F_{j(v_{1})}\,
. Hence
v_{1}
is the only element of

\left\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:v\sim v_{2},\hskip 2.84544ptF_{j(v_{1})}\subseteq v^{-1}(0)\right\},

which we obtain from
v_{2}
by changing the image of
\ell_{0}
from
v_{2}(\ell_{0})
to 0.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If
v_{1}\in X_{i_{1}}
and
v_{2}\in Y_{i_{2}}
are neighbors, then there
exists
\ell_{0}\in F_{j(v_{1})}
such that the following hold.

1.

v_{1}(\ell_{0})=0
;

2.

v_{2}(\ell_{0})=i_{2}-i_{1}\,
;

3.

v_{1}(\ell)=v_{2}(\ell)
for all
\ell\in[n]\setminus\{\ell_{0}\}
.

In particular, for all
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
, each vertex in
X_{i_{1}}
has at most
\displaystyle\max_{j\in[q]}|F_{j}|
neighbors in
Y_{i_{2}}
.

Proof.

Items 1 and 3 follow from the fact that
v_{1}\in X
,
v_{2}\in Y
and
v_{1}\sim v_{2}\,
. To see item 2, notice that

i_{2}-i_{1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(v_{2}(\ell)-v_{1}(\ell)\right)=v_{2}(\ell_{0})-0.

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For all
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
,

\Delta(H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}])\leq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil.

Proof.

Combining Propositions
4.1
,
4.3
and
4.4

\Delta(H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}])\leq\max\left\{\max_{j\in[q]}|F_{j}|,q\right\}\leq\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil.

4.2
Size greater than the independence number

We now prove that there exist
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
such that the induced subgraph
H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}]
has size greater than
\alpha(H(n,k))
.

Lemma 4.6
.

|X|\equiv(-1)^{q+1}\pmod{k}
. In particular,
|X|
is not divisible by
k
.

Proof.

The proof generalizes the inclusion-exclusion argument of Chung et al.
[
1
]
. By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

|X|=\sum_{j\in[q]}k^{n-|F_{j}|}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},j_{2}\in[q]\\
j_{1}<j_{2}\end{subarray}}k^{n-|F_{j_{1}}\cup F_{j_{2}}|}+\dots+(-1)^{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{q-1}\in[q]\\
j_{1}<\dots<j_{q-1}\end{subarray}}k^{n-|F_{j_{1}}\cup\dots\cup F_{j_{q-1}}|}+(-1)^{q+1}.

Since all terms but the last are divisible by
k
, the statement follows.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

There exist
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
such that
H(n,k)[X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}]
has size greater than
\alpha(H(n,k))
.

Proof.

Choose
i_{1},i_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
such that
\displaystyle|X_{i_{1}}|=\max_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|X_{i}|
and
\displaystyle|Y_{i_{2}}|=\max_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|Y_{i}|
. By Lemma
4.6
, it follows that

|X_{i_{1}}|>\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|X_{i}|.

Since also

|Y_{i_{2}}|\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|Y_{i}|,

we have

|X_{i_{1}}\cup Y_{i_{2}}|=|X_{i_{1}}|+|Y_{i_{2}}|>\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|X_{i}|+\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}}|Y_{i}|\right)=\frac{1}{k}(|X|+|Y|)=\frac{1}{k}|{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}|=k^{n-1}=\alpha(H(n,k)).

∎

Combine Propositions

and

5
Open questions on the lower bound

It is natural to ask whether
\sqrt{n}
is the true order of magnitude of
f(H(n,k))
for
k\geq 3
. Huang
[
5
]
proved the lower bound
f(H(n,2))\geq\sqrt{n}
using
linear algebra. We were unable to generalize his argument to Hamming graphs
with larger alphabets. Unfortunately we were unable even to obtain a lower bound of logarithmic order like the Chung et al. lower bound for
k=2
. We were able to generalize one of their lemmas toward this lower bound; this result might be of interest in its own right.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
S=(V(S),E(S))
is a subgraph of
H(n,k)
with average degree
\overline{d}
, then
\log|V(S)|\geq\frac{\overline{d}}{k-1}
.

Proof.

The statement is clear for
n=1
. Inductively, split
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}
into
k
copies of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n-1}
:

{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}=Z_{1}\overset{\cdot}{\cup}\dots\overset{\cdot}{\cup}Z_{k},

Z_{i}=\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}^{n}:v(1)=i\}.

For every
i\in[k]
set
V_{i}=V(S)\cap Z_{i}
. By throwing away the empty
V_{i}
and permuting the indices, we may assume
|V_{1}|\leq\dots\leq|V_{K}|
with all
V_{i}
nonempty.

Let
s_{1}
be the number of edges between
V_{1}
and
\bigcup_{j=2}^{K}V_{j}
in
G
,
s_{2}
the number of edges between
V_{2}
and
\bigcup_{j=3}^{K}V_{j}
in
G
,
\dots
,
s_{K-1}
the number of edges between
V_{K-1}
and
V_{K}
in
G
. This gives
|V_{i}|\geq\frac{s_{i}}{k-1}
for all
i\in[K-1]
.

The inductive hypothesis gives

|V_{i}|\log|V_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\deg_{G[V_{i}]}(v)\right),

so that

\sum_{i=1}^{K}|V_{i}|\log|V_{i}|+\frac{2}{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}s_{i}\right)\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{v\in V}\deg_{G}(v)\right).

(7)

Notice that for
0<p\leq q
, we always have

(p+q)\log(p+q)\geq p\log p+q\log q+2p.

(8)

Combining (7) and (8) gives

(|V_{1}|+\dots+|V_{K}|)\log(|V_{1}|+\dots+|V_{K}|)\geq|V_{1}|\log|V_{1}|+(|V_{2}|+\dots+|V_{K}|)\log(|V_{2}|+\dots+|V_{K}|)+2|V_{1}|

\geq\dots\geq\sum_{i=1}^{K}|V_{i}|\log|V_{i}|+2\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}|V_{i}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{K}|V_{i}|\log|V_{i}|+\frac{2}{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}s_{i}\right)\geq\frac{1}{k-1}\left(\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\deg_{G[V_{i}]}(v)\right).

∎

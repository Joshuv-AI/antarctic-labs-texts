Endoscopic decompositions and the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture
Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen

Abstract
. We construct natural operators connecting the cohomology of the moduli spaces of stable Higgs bundles with different ranks and genera which, after numerical specialization, recover the topological mirror symmetry conjecture of Hausel–Thaddeus concerning SL n \mathrm{SL}_{n} - and PGL n \mathrm{PGL}_{n} -Higgs bundles. This provides a complete description of the cohomology of the moduli space of stable SL n \mathrm{SL}_{n} -Higgs bundles in terms of the tautological classes, and gives a new proof of the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture, proven recently by Gröchenig–Wyss–Ziegler via p-adic integration. Our method is to relate the decomposition theorem for the Hitchin fibration, using vanishing cycle functors, to the decomposition theorem for the twisted Hitchin fibration whose supports are simpler.



0.
Introduction

0.1.
Overview

Throughout, we work over the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
C
be a nonsingular projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Let
n,d
be integers with
n>0
and
\mathrm{gcd}(n,d)=1
.

The cohomology of the moduli space
\widetilde{{\mathcal{N}}}_{n,d}
of rank
n
degree
d
stable vector bundles on
C
has been studied intensively for decades. By
[
1
,
2
]
, the cohomology
H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{N}}}_{n,d},{\mathbb{C}})
is generated by the
tautological classes
— the Künneth factors of the Chern characters of a universal family. Relations between the tautological classes were explored in
[
27
,
14
]
.

A natural moduli space closely related to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{N}}}_{n,d}
is the moduli of stable
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-bundles

{\mathcal{N}}_{n,L}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{N}}}_{n,d}

which parameterizes rank
n
stable vector bundles with fixed determinant
L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)
. The finite abelian group

\Gamma=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[n]

acts on
{\mathcal{N}}_{n,L}
via tensor product, which induces a
\Gamma
-action on the cohomology
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})
. The
\Gamma
-invariant part
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})^{\Gamma}
recovers the cohomology of the quotient
{\mathcal{N}}_{n,L}/\Gamma
, which can be viewed as the moduli space of stable
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-bundles. The tautological classes associated with a universal family generate the
\Gamma
-invariant cohomology
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})^{\Gamma}
.

The following theorem by Harder–Narasimhan
[
19
]
shows that every class in
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

Theorem 0.1

(Harder–Narasimhan
[
19
]
)
.

The
\Gamma
-action on
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})
is trivial.

As a consequence of Theorem
0.1
, we obtain immediately that the tautological classes generate the total cohomology
H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})
.

The purpose of this paper is to study the
\Gamma
-action on the cohomology of the moduli space of stable
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-Higgs bundles from the viewpoint of the Hausel-Thaddeus conjecture
[
21
]
. We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
the (coarse) moduli space parameterizing stable Higgs bundles

({\mathcal{E}},\theta:{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\otimes\Omega_{C}):\quad\mathrm{det}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq L,\quad\mathrm{trace}(\theta)=0

on the curve
C
. It is a nonsingular quasi-projective variety admitting a natural hyper-Kähler structure
[
25
,
32
]
. As in the case of vector bundles, the group
\Gamma
acts on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
via tensor product

{\mathcal{L}}\cdot({\mathcal{E}},\theta)=({\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}},\theta),\quad{\mathcal{L}}\in\Gamma.

The induced
\Gamma
-action on
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})
yields the following canonical decomposition

(1)

H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}/\Gamma,{\mathbb{C}})\oplus\bigoplus_{\kappa\neq 0}H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}

where
\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}=\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
runs through all nontrivial characters of
\Gamma
, and
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}
denotes the
\kappa
-isotypic component. By
[
31
]
(and the isomorphism
[
8
, (70)]
), the tautological classes associated with a universal family of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
generate the
\Gamma
-invariant cohomology

H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}/\Gamma,{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})^{\Gamma}.

However, contrary to Theorem
0.1
, the
\Gamma
-variant part of (
1
) is nontrivial, and carries a rich structure, predicted by topological mirror symmetry
[
21
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the structure of
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}
for
\kappa\neq 0
. We introduce natural operators which determine
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}
in terms of the cohomology of the moduli space of stable
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
-Higgs bundles on a certain curve for some
r\leq n
. These operators respect the perverse and the Hodge filtrations, and, upon specialization to Hodge polynomials, they recover the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture
[
21
]
. In particular, this gives a new proof using perverse sheaves of the topological mirror symmetry conjecture of Hausel-Thaddeus, which was recently proven by
[
17
]
using p-adic integration.

0.2.
Hitchin fibrations

The moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
carries a Lagrangian fibration

(2)

h:{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 2}H^{0}(C,{\Omega_{C}^{\otimes i}})

given by Hitchin’s integrable system, which is now referred to as the
Hitchin fibration
. The
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
is fiberwise with respect to (
2
). There are two types of moduli spaces closely related to the cohomological study of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
from the perspective of mirror symmetry
[
21
]
and representation theory
[
33
,
34
]
.

The moduli spaces of the first type are the fixed loci of an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
the
\gamma
-fixed subvariety, which maps to the Hitchin base via

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{h_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}=\mathrm{Im}(h|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}})\xhookrightarrow{i_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{A}}.

The
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
induces a
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
.

The second type of moduli spaces are associated with a cyclic Galois cover
\pi:C^{\prime}\to C
of the original curve given by
\gamma\in\Gamma
. We assume
\mathrm{deg}(\pi)=\mathrm{ord}(\gamma)=m
and
n=mr
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}(\pi)
be the moduli space parameterizing rank
r
stable Higgs bundles
({\mathcal{E}},\theta)
on
C^{\prime}
such that

\mathrm{det}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{E}})\simeq L,\quad\mathrm{trace}(\pi_{*}\theta)=0.

It admits a Hitchin fibration

h_{\pi}:{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}(\pi)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)

with a fiberwise
\Gamma
-action; see Section
1
for more details on these moduli spaces. From the viewpoint of representation theory, the moduli spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}(\pi)
are related to the study of the corresponding endoscopic groups for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
over
C

[
34
]
. They are nonsingular but disconnected. The Galois group

G_{\pi}=\mathrm{Aut}(\pi)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}

acts on both the source
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}(\pi)
and the target
{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)
, whose quotients recover
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}
respectively. We denote by

q_{\mathcal{A}}:{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}

the quotient map of the base
{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)
. We also consider the largest open subset
{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)^{*}\subset{\mathcal{A}}(\pi)
upon which the
G_{\pi}
-action is free, and set
A_{\gamma}^{*}:={\mathcal{A}}(\pi)^{*}/G_{\pi}\subset A_{\gamma}
.

0.3.
Endoscopic decompositions

In order to understand the decomposition (
1

\mathrm{Rh}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\in D_{c}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}),\quad\mathrm{Rh_{\gamma}}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\in D_{c}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma}),\quad\mathrm{Rh_{\pi}}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\in D_{c}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}(\pi))

{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}

\Gamma
-action and a character

\kappa

\Gamma
-action. The Weil pairing identifies canonically the group

and its dual,

(3)

\hat{\Gamma}=\Gamma;

Our first result is the following theorem, which relates the stable part of the endoscopic cohomology with the pullback of the
\kappa
-isotypic contribution for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}

[

]
in the case of

(Theorem

)
.

Let

Theorem 0.3

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
be identified by (

d_{\gamma}=\mathrm{codim}_{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma})
. We have an isomorphism

(5)

c_{\kappa}:\left(\mathrm{Rh}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\right)_{\kappa}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{i_{\gamma}}_{*}\left(\mathrm{Rh_{\gamma}}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\right)_{\kappa}[-2d_{\gamma}]\in D_{c}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})

The construction of the operator

c_{\kappa}:\left(\mathrm{Rh}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\right)_{\kappa}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{i_{\gamma}}_{*}\left(\mathrm{Rh_{\gamma}}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\right)_{\kappa}[-2d_{\gamma}]\in D_{c}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})

-case over the total Hitchin base; see
[

0.4.
Structure of the cohomology of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}

Let
\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
be identified by (

\pi:C^{\prime}\to C

0.5.
The Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture

In
[
21
]
, Hausel and Thaddeus showed that the moduli spaces of stable
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
- and
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-Higgs bundles are mirror partners in the sense of the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow mirror symmetry. As a consequence, these two moduli spaces should have identical Hodge numbers.

As explained in
[
21
]
, the moduli space of degree
d
stable
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
-Higgs bundles can be realized as the quotient
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}/\Gamma
which is naturally a Deligne–Mumford stack. Therefore, Hausel–Thaddeus conjectured that, for any two line bundles
L,L^{\prime}
with

\mathrm{deg}(L)=d,~~~\mathrm{deg}(L^{\prime})=d^{\prime},\quad\mathrm{gcd}(d,n)=\mathrm{gcd}(d^{\prime},n)=1,

the Hodge numbers of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
are the same as the stringy Hodge numbers of the stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L^{\prime}}/\Gamma]
(twisted by a certain gerbe
\alpha
):

(6)

h^{i,j}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L})=h_{\mathrm{st}}^{i,j}([{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L^{\prime}}/\Gamma],\alpha);

see
[
21
, Section 4]
for precise definitions of the gerbe
\alpha
and the stringy Hodge numbers. Later, Hausel further conjectured a refinement of (
6
): the Hodge numbers of
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}
coincide with the Hodge numbers of the gerby sector
[({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L^{\prime}})_{\gamma}/\Gamma]
. Here
({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L^{\prime}})_{\gamma}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L^{\prime}}
is the
\gamma
-fixed subvariety, and
\kappa
and
\gamma
are matched via (
3
). We refer to
[
23
, Conjecture 4.5]
concerning Hodge numbers and
[
23
, Conjecture 5.9]
for a further refinement involving perverse filtrations.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of Theorem
0.3
which proves the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture and a refinement of it; see the formulation (1.2.2) and the appendix in
[
29
]
for an explanation of how the right-hand side is equivalent to the gerby description above.

0.6.
Idea of the proof

Our approach proceeds in two steps.
We first show analogs of Theorems
0.2
and
0.3
for the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
of
D
-twisted
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-Higgs bundles with
\mathrm{deg}(D)
even and greater than
2g-2
(see Section
1
for precise definitions). As mentioned earlier, one expects this case to be simpler than the original setting, due to work of Chaudouard–Laumon
[
12
]
and de Cataldo
[
6
]
, which determines the supports appearing in the decomposition theorem for the twisted Hitchin map.
After proving the corresponding support theorem for endoscopic moduli, we study the endoscopic decomposition of Ngô
[
34
]
and Yun
[
39
]
over the elliptic locus, and extend it over the full twisted Hitchin base.

Unfortunately, this approach is not sufficient when
D=K_{C}
, since the supports of the Hitchin map remain mysterious
[
7
]
. Moreover, although
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
embeds inside
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
for certain effective divisor
D
with
\mathrm{deg}(D)>2g-2
, we cannot simply pull back equation (
5
).

Instead, we realize
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
as the critical locus of a regular function

(9)

\mu_{\pi,{\mathcal{M}}}:{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1};

see Theorem
4.5
. This allows us to express the cohomology of
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
as the vanishing cohomology of (
9
). In addition, since the function
\mu_{\pi,{\mathcal{M}}}
factors through the Hitchin base, we can use the vanishing cycles functor to relate the decomposition theorem for
{\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}
in terms of that for
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
. By applying this technique to the twisted version of equation (
5
), we obtain the full result.

0.7.
Relation to other work

As discussed in Section
0.5
, the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture as well as its refinements has been proven by Gröchenig–Wyss–Ziegler
[
17
]
via p-adic integration. Using similar approach, they also gave a new proof of Ngô’s geometric stabilization theorem
[
18
]
which plays a crucial role in Ngô’s proof of the fundamental lemma of the Langlands program
[
34
]
. Our approach goes along the inverse direction — we prove the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture by extending Ngô’s method
[
34
,
39
]
in the proof the geometric stabilization theorem via perverse sheaves and the support theorems. This carries out the proposal of Hausel in
[
23
, Section 5.4]
. A benefit of the sheaf-theoretic approach is that it allows us to construct concrete geometric operators which realize the Hausel–Thaddeus conjecture (Theorems
0.2
and
0.3
) and which provide better understanding of
H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}
for each non-trivial
\kappa
.

Throughout, we work over the complex numbers

1.1.

For our purpose, it is important to consider generalized Higgs bundles

From now on, all

1.3.
Weil pairing and cyclic covers

Recall from Section
0.1
that the group
\Gamma=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[n]
acts on the
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
via tensor product. For
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the
\gamma
-fixed subvariety
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{\gamma}\subset{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
carries an induced Hitchin map

{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{\gamma}\xrightarrow{h^{D}_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{A}}^{D}_{\gamma}=\mathrm{Im}(h^{D}|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma}})\xhookrightarrow{i^{D}_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{A}}^{D}.

As indicated by Theorem
0.3
, the cohomology of
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{\gamma}
is related to a
\kappa
-isotypic component of the cohomology of
{\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{n,L}
with respect to the
\Gamma
-action.

In order to describe this connection, we need a correspondence (
3
) between an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
and a character
\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}
, which we review in the following.

Let
\mu_{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
denote the finite group of the
n
-th roots of unity. We have the

on the group of
n
-torsion points of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)
,

\langle~~,~~\rangle_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow\mu_{n}.

Under the identification

\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[n]=H_{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}),

the Weil pairing coincide with the intersection pairing on
H_{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
. In particular, it is non-degenerate, which induces a character

\kappa=\langle\gamma,~~\rangle_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mu_{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. This gives the identification (
3
).

We also note that an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
naturally corresponds to a cyclic Galois cover of
C
whose degree is the order of
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In fact, for fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we denote by
L_{\gamma}
the
n
-torsion line bundle associated with
\gamma
, and let
m
be its order which divides

. Taking the
m
-th roots of unity fiberwise inside the total space of

with respect to the zero section
C
, we obtain a cyclic Galois cover

\pi:C^{\prime}\rightarrow C

with the Galois group
G_{\pi}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
. Conversely, every degree
m
étale cyclic Galois cover arises this way.

1.4.
Characters

In this section we give a concrete description for the character
\kappa
in terms of the Prym variety associated with the corresponding Galois cover
\pi:C^{\prime}\to C

As before, we assume that
\pi:C^{\prime}\to C
is a degree
m
cyclic Galois cover associated with

as in Section
1.3
, and we assume that

. The character
\kappa\in\hat{\Gamma}
matches with

via (

\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C)=\mathrm{Ker}\left(\mathrm{Nm}:\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)\right)

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}.

For an

-torsion line bundle

\mathrm{Nm}(\pi^{*}{\mathcal{L}})={\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes m}\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[r].

In particular, the line bundle

represents a point in
\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C))

\Gamma\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C)),\quad{\mathcal{L}}\mapsto[\pi^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes r}]\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C)).

The morphism (

\Gamma=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[n]\xrightarrow{[r]}\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Prym}(C^{\prime}/C)).

The first map above is the multiplication by

to the equivalent class of the line bundle

, and its surjectivity is given by the proof of
[

. Hence (

Recall that
\gamma\in\Gamma
is of order
m

\gamma\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]\subset\Gamma.

The following lemma is obtained by viewing the Weil pairing on
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[i]
for any

, via Poincaré duality, as the intersection pairing

H_{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}/i{\mathbb{Z}})\times H_{1}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}/i{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/i{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\mu_{i}.

Lemma 1.2
.

Assume
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. Let
\langle~~,~~\rangle_{\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]}
denotes the Weil pairing on
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]
, and we view

naturally as an element in
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]
. We have

\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle\gamma,r\gamma^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)[m]}.

(corresponding to

that the character

∎

2.
Support theorems for Hitchin moduli spaces

2.1.
Supports

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism between nonsingular quasi-projective varieties. The decomposition theorem by Bernstein, Beilinson, Deligne, and Gabber
[
3
]
implies that the direct image complex
\mathrm{Rf}_{\ast}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}
is (non-canonically) isomorphic to a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves,

(32)

\mathrm{Rf}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{Z_{i}}\mathrm{IC}_{Z_{i}}(L_{i})[d_{i}]\in D^{b}_{c}(Y),

where
d_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
Z_{i}\subset Y
is an irreducible subvariety, and
L_{i}
is a local system on an open subset
U_{i}
of
Z_{i}
. Every
Z_{i}
above is called a
support
of
f:X\to Y
. We say that a direct summand
{\mathcal{F}}
of the object

\mathrm{Rf}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}={\mathcal{F}}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}

has
full support
if each perverse constituent
\mathrm{IC}_{Z_{i}}(L_{i})[d_{i}]
of
{\mathcal{F}}
has support
Z_{i}=Y
.

In this section, we analyze the supports of various Hitchin fibrations introduced in Section 1 when
\mathrm{deg}(D)>2g-2
.

2.2.
Support theorems

For the Hitchin fibration

(resp.

), we define the elliptic locus of the Hitchin bases

(resp.
{\mathcal{A}}^{D}(\pi)
), denoted by

(resp.

, to be the open subset consisting of integral spectral curves.

Following the methods of Ngô
[
34
]
and Chaudouard–Laumon
[

, de Cataldo showed in

that all the supports for the

-Hitchin fibration (

Theorem 2.1

(
[

)
.

Let

be an effective divisor on

Now we consider the
\Gamma

, which induces a canonical decomposition of the direct image complex

see
[

. We define the

Combining Theorem

The trivial character

We fix a character

We have similarly a

Theorem 2.3

2.3.
Weak abelian fibrations

We recall the notion of weak abelian fibration introduced in
[
34
]
, which is modelled on the properties of Hitchin fibrations.

We follow closely the exposition of
[
6
, Section 2.6]
. Let
A
be an irreducible nonsingular variety. Assume that

are morphisms of the same relative dimensions
\mathrm{dim}(h)=\mathrm{dim}(g)
satisfying the following properties:

(a)

The map
g:P\to A
is a smooth commutative group scheme with connected fibers;

(b)

The map
h
is proper, and
M
is nonsingular;

(c)

The group scheme
P
acts on
M
fiberwise with affine stabilizers for every geometric point of

We call a triple
(M,A,P)
as above a

, if the Tate module

T_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}}(P)=\mathrm{R^{2d_{g}-1}g}_{!}\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}(d_{g})

as an
l
-adic sheaf is polarizable; see
[
6
, Section 2.6]
the paragraph following Lemma 2.6.2.

Over a closed point
a\in A
, we consider the Chevalley decomposition for the restricted group scheme
P_{a}
,

0\to P_{a}^{\mathrm{aff}}\to P_{a}\to P_{a}^{\mathrm{ab}}\to 0,

where
P_{a}^{\mathrm{aff}}
is the maximal connected affine linear subgroup of
P_{a}
and
P_{a}^{\mathrm{ab}}
is an abelian variety.

We recall in the following the support inequality of Ngô
[
34
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
34
, Theorem 7.2.2]
)
.

Let (M,A,P) be a weak abelian fibration. Assume the irreducible subvariety
Z\subset A
is a support of
h:M\to A
, then

(34)

\mathrm{dim}(h)-\mathrm{dim}(A)+\mathrm{dim}(Z)\geq d_{Z}^{\mathrm{ab}}(P).

Here
d_{Z}^{\mathrm{ab}}(P)=\mathrm{dim}(P_{a}^{\mathrm{ab}})
with
a\in Z
a general point.

Proof.

It is clear that (a) and (c) in Section

By

(

Let

d^{\mathrm{ab}}_{Z}\left(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\geq\sum_{i}\left({d_{\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}}-d_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}\right)+\mathrm{dim}(Z)+\left(\mathrm{deg}(D)-g+1\right).

Here
{d_{\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}}

-Hitchin fibration (

and the divisor

Proof.

When

, the subspace

(42)

{\mathcal{A}}^{D}(\mathrm{id})=\{\mathrm{char}(\theta)\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D}:~~~\mathrm{trace}(\theta)=0\}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D}

2.5.
Proof of Theorem
2.3
(a)

The proof of Theorem
2.3
(a) is parallel to the proofs of the main theorems in
[

,
6
]
, which we provide in the following for the reader’s convenience. The crucial ingredient is to combine Ngô’s support inequality (

) and the multi-variable inequality (

).

We assume

is an irreducible support of (
35
) whose general points have types
(\underline{m},\underline{n})
. By Theorem
2.4
, Proposition

, and Proposition

, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{dim}({h^{D}_{\pi}})-&\mathrm{dim}({A^{D}(\pi)})+\mathrm{dim}(Z)\geq d^{\mathrm{ab}}_{Z}\left(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{C}}\right)-g\\
&\geq\sum_{i}\left({d_{\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}}-d_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}\right)+\mathrm{dim}(Z)+(\mathrm{deg}(D)-g+1)-g\end{split}

where we use

(44)

\mathrm{dim}({h^{D}_{\pi}})-\mathrm{dim}({{\mathcal{A}}^{D}(\pi)})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left({d_{\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}}-d_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D^{\prime}}_{n_{i}}(C^{\prime})}\right)+(\mathrm{deg}(D)-2g+1).

Here we recall that

\mathrm{GL}_{*}
-Hitchin fibrations
[
6
, Section 6.1]
to compute both sides of (

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}

\displaystyle=\left({d_{\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}_{r}(C^{\prime})}}-d_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D^{\prime}}_{r}(C^{\prime})}\right)+\left(\mathrm{dim}H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(D))-g\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-r\mathrm{deg}(D^{\prime})+2r(g^{\prime}-1)+1\right)+\left(\mathrm{deg}(D)-2g+1\right)

where
g^{\prime}
is the genus of

and we used the last formula of

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\left(-n_{i}\mathrm{deg}(D^{\prime})+2n_{i}(g^{\prime}-1)+1\right)+\left(\mathrm{deg}(D)-2g+1\right)

\displaystyle=\left((-\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i})\left((\mathrm{deg}(D^{\prime})-2(g^{\prime}-1)\right)+s\right)+\left(\mathrm{deg}(D)-2g+1\right).

In particular, (

) implies that

(45)

Since

\mathrm{deg}(D^{\prime})-(2g^{\prime}-2)=\mathrm{deg}(\pi)(\mathrm{deg}(D)-(2g-2))>0

(a). ∎

For any Hitchin-type moduli space

Proposition 5.2
.

The quotient map (
119
) induces a canonical isomorphism

(121)

H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}

satisfying that

(122)

P_{k}H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{i+j=k}H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})\otimes P_{j}H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}.

Here the stable part
(-)_{\mathrm{st}}
denotes the
\Gamma
-invariant part of the cohomology.

Proof.

The first isomorphism is induced by (
120
). It suffices to show the compatibility (
122
) of the perverse filtrations.

We notice that the quotient map (
119
) is compatible with the Hitchin fibrations, and we have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C)\times{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(D))\times{\mathcal{A}}^{D}(\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{h}^{D}(C)\times h_{\pi}^{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the bottom arrow is a canonical identification. The pullback morphism
\widetilde{q}^{*}
for the cohomology is induced sheaf-theoretically by the canonical morphism

(123)

{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}\to\widetilde{q}_{*}{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}

where the first and the second
{\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}}
denote the trivial local systems on the target and the source of
\widetilde{q}
respectively. By applying the perverse truncation functor to the pushforward of (
123
) along
\widetilde{h}^{D^{\prime}}
, we obtain that the first map of (
120
) satisfies

(124)

P_{k}H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}P_{k}H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C)\times{\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})^{\Gamma}.

We conclude (
122
) from (
124
), the Künneth decomposition, and the fact that the perverse filtration on

H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})

coincides with the cohomological filtration
H^{*\leq k}
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
5.2
, we obtain a canonical operator given by the projection

(125)

\mathfrak{p_{1}}:H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow H^{i}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}},\quad\forall i\geq 0

sending a class in
H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})
to its projection to the direct summand component

H^{0}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{i}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}=H^{i}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}.

with respect to the decomposition (
121
). The identity above is induced by the fundamental class
1\in H^{0}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})

Corollary 5.3
.

The operator (
125

\mathfrak{p_{1}}\left(P_{k}H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\right)=P_{k}H^{i}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}.

Proof.

Since the fundamental class

lies in
P_{0}H^{0}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{D}_{1,0}(C),{\mathbb{C}})
, the corollary follows from (

∎

5.4.
Operators

Let
\pi:C^{\prime}\to C

\begin{split}H^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}&H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{st}}=H^{*}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}\\

Here the first morphism is (

, the third morphism is the projection to the

-invariant part, the fourth isomorphism is given by Lemma

, and the last isomorphism follows from Theorem

Theorem 5.4
.

We have

\mathfrak{p}_{\kappa}\left(P_{k}H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{D^{\prime}}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\right)=P_{k+d^{D}_{\gamma}}H^{i+2d^{D}_{\gamma}}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}^{D},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.3
, the morphism
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
preserves the perverse filtrations. All the other morphisms in (

The last morphism of (

P_{k}H^{i}({\mathcal{M}}^{D}_{\gamma},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}\xrightarrow{\simeq}P_{k+d^{D}_{\gamma}}H^{i+2d^{D}_{\gamma}}({\mathcal{M}}_{r,L}^{D}(\pi),{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}.\qed

Question 5.5

Is it true that

\mathfrak{p}^{B}_{\kappa}\left(W_{2k}H^{i}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{r,d}^{B}(C^{\prime}),{\mathbb{C}})\right)=W_{2k+2d_{\gamma}}H^{i+2d_{\gamma}}({\mathcal{M}}_{n,L}^{B},{\mathbb{C}})_{\kappa}?

Abstract
. We generalize some fundamental results for noncompact Riemannian manfolds without boundary, that only require completeness and no curvature assumptions, to manifolds with boundary: let M M be a smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary ∂ M \partial M and let C ^ c ∞ ​ ( M ) \hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M) denote the space of smooth compactly supported cut-off functions with vanishing normal derivative, Neumann cut-offs . We show, among other things, that under completeness: • C ^ c ∞ ​ ( M ) \hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M) is dense in W 1 , p ​ ( M ̊ ) W^{1,p}(\mathring{M}) for all p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) ; this generalizes a classical result by Aubin [ 2 ] for ∂ M = ∅ \partial M=\emptyset . • M M admits a sequence of first order cut-off functions in C ^ c ∞ ​ ( M ) \hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M) ; for ∂ M = ∅ \partial M=\emptyset this result can be traced back to Gaffney [ 7 ] . • the Laplace-Beltrami operator with domain of definition C ^ c ∞ ​ ( M ) \hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M) is essentially self-adjoint; this is a generalization of a classical result by Strichartz [ 20 ] for ∂ M = ∅ \partial M=\emptyset .

First order Sobolev density results, and first order sequences of Neumann cut-off functions

Let

on

(\cdot,\cdot)
. We consider

as a metric space with respect to its geodesic distance

, where the infimum is taken over all piecewise smooth curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to M

[
1
]

Remark 1.1
.

Assume

is an open connected smooth subset of a smooth complete Riemannian manifold without boundary. Then

is a complete smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary. This follows from the fact that the (intrinsic!) geodesic distance on

induces the topology on

We denote by

in the sense of manifolds with boundary. Following the notation of

The symbol

We understand all

-scalar products with respect to the Riemannian volume measure

Let

We recall that for every smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary

It has been shown in

is dense in

.

The Neumann realization of

in

is defined as the nonnegative self-adjoint operator

associated with the regular, strongly local Dirichlet form

\left\langle Hf_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla f_{1},\nabla f_{2}\right\rangle\quad\text{ for all $f_{1}\in\mathrm{Dom}(H)$, $f_{2}\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})$.}

In particular, for all

.

Remark 1.2

It is a standard fact that the intrinsic distance

2.
Essential self-adjointness of the Neumann-Laplacian

The main result of this section is:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be complete. Then
-\Delta
with domain of definition
\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}(M)
; in particular,
H
is the unique self-adjoint extension of this operator.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first result that deals with the essential self-adjointness of the Laplacian on a noncompact Riemannian manifold with boundary. The only mildly related result we are aware of is
[
15
]
, which deals with complex Riemannian manifolds that have a certain symmetry and that satisfy some additional analytic assumptions; there, the authors prove - with completely different methods - the essential self-adjointness of the Laplacian induced by the
\overline{\partial}
operator.

In the proof of Theorem
2.1
we will use the following lemma, whose content is that the domain of
H
is invariant under multiplication by functions in
\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{Dom}(H)
and let
\chi\in\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
. Then
f\chi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
,
\Delta(f\chi)=\chi\Delta f+2(\nabla f,\nabla\chi)+f\Delta\chi\in L^{2}(M)
and, for all
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
,

\int_{M}\phi\Delta(\chi f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{M}(\nabla\phi,\nabla(\chi f))\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

It follows that
\chi f\in\mathrm{Dom}(H)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Clearly
\chi f\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
and a computation shows that, for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathring{M})
,

\begin{split}\int_{M}\chi f\Delta\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu&=-\int_{M}(\nabla\phi,\chi\nabla f+f\nabla\chi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&=-\int_{M}(\nabla(\chi\phi),\nabla f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{M}\phi(\nabla\chi,\nabla)\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{M}\phi\operatorname{div}(f\chi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&=\int_{M}\phi[\chi\Delta f+2(\nabla\chi,\nabla f)+f\Delta\chi]\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\end{split}

where we have used that
\chi\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M}),

f\in\mathrm{Dom}(H)
and that
f\nabla\chi
is a
W^{1,2}
vector field with compact support in
\mathring{M}.
Thus

\Delta(\chi f)=\chi\Delta f+2(\nabla\chi,\nabla f)+f\Delta\chi\in L^{2}(M).

Next let
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
; using the fact that
\chi f\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
,
f\phi\in W^{1,1}(\mathring{M})
and that
\partial_{\nu}\chi=0
we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{M}\phi\Delta(\chi f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu&=\int_{M}(\chi\phi)\Delta f+2(\nabla f,\phi\nabla\chi)+f\phi\Delta\chi]\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&=\int_{M}[-(\nabla(f,\nabla(\chi\phi)+2(\nabla f,\phi\nabla\chi)-(\nabla(f\phi),\nabla\chi)]\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\partial M}f\phi\partial_{\nu}\chi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&=-\int_{M}(\nabla\phi,\nabla(\chi f))\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\end{split}

as required to conclude the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We are going to show that
\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
is an operator core for
H
. To this end, our proof will follow Chernoff’s philosophy
[
3
]
of using the wave equation for proving essential self-adjointness (see also
[
10
,
9
]
). However, in the presence of boundary, some additional care has to be taken in order obtain enough regularity for functions in the intersection of the spaces
\mathrm{Dom}(H^{k})
.

Step 1: For all
t>0
,
f\in L^{2}(M)
one has
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}f\in C^{\infty}(M)
with
\partial_{\nu}(\mathrm{e}^{-tH}f)=0
.

Proof of step 1: By spectral calculus we have

h:=\mathrm{e}^{-tH}f\in\bigcap^{\infty}_{k=0}\mathrm{Dom}(H^{k})\subset\{f\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M}):\Delta^{k}f\in L^{2}(M)\quad\text{for all $k$}\},

and it satisfies the weak Neumann boundary condition

\left\langle\Delta h,\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\phi\right\rangle\>,

for all
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
.

Clearly
h
is smooth in
\mathring{M}
due to local interior elliptic regularity and Sobolev embedding. We need to prove that
h
is smooth up to the boundary and that
\partial_{\nu}h=0
. So we fix an arbitrary
x\in\partial M
and pick a relatively compact coordinate neighbourhood
N^{\prime}\subset M
of
x
such that
N:=\overline{N^{\prime}}
is a smooth manifold with boundary. Let also
V\Subset N^{\prime}
be a neighborhood of
x
and let
\chi,\rho\in C^{\infty}(M)
by such that
\chi=1
on
V
,
\rho=1
on
\text{supp\,}\chi
and
\text{supp}\,\rho\Subset N^{\prime}
. Since
h\in\mathrm{Dom}(H)
, by Lemma
2.2
,

h_{0}:=\chi h\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M}),\quad\Delta h_{0}=:h_{1}\in L^{2}(M),

and the weak Neumann boundary condition holds

\left\langle\Delta h_{0},\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla h_{0},\nabla\phi\right\rangle\quad\text{for all $\phi\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})$}.

Thus, given
\phi_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{N})
,
\rho\phi_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\mathring{M})
and we deduce that

\int_{N}\phi_{0}h_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{M}\Delta h_{0}(\rho\phi_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{M}(\nabla h_{0},\nabla(\rho\phi_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{N}(\nabla h_{0},\nabla\phi_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

with
h_{1}=\Delta h_{0}\in L^{2}(N)

It follows from
[
14
, Theorem 5.9]
that
h_{0}\in W^{2,2}(\mathring{N})
. Bootstrapping this argument leads to
h_{0}\in W^{k,2}(\mathring{N})
for all
k
, and so
h_{0}\in C^{\infty}(N)
by Sobolev embedding and so
h\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Finally, the weak Neumann boundary condition and Green’s formula in combination with the second part of Remark
1.6
give
\partial_{\nu}h=0
.

Step 2: The space

\mathrm{Dom}_{c}(H):=\{f\in\mathrm{Dom}(H):\text{$f$ has a compact support}\}

is an operator core for
H
, that is,
\mathrm{Dom}_{c}(H)
is dense in the norm
\left\|f\right\|_{H}:=\left\|f\right\|+\left\|Hf\right\|
in
\mathrm{Dom}(H)
.

Proof of step 2: Note first that by Remark
1.2
, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
we get that for all
U_{1},U_{2}\subset M
open and disjoint,
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(M)
with
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\subset U_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and all
0<s<\varrho(U_{1},U_{2})
, one has

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\cos(s\sqrt{H})f_{1},f_{2}\rangle=0.

The latter fact implies that if
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset B(x_{0},r)
for some
x_{0}\in M
,
r>0
, then for any
t>0
one
has

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\cos(t\sqrt{H})f)\subset\overline{B(x,r+t)},

and this closed ball is compact due to the completeness of
M
(since completeness on
M
is equivalent to the Heine-Borel property of
M

[
17
]
). Now the claim follows from an abstract functional analytic fact (cf. Theorem
3.2
below).

Step 3:
\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
is dense in
\mathrm{Dom}_{c}(H)
in the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H}
.

Proof of step 3: let
f\in\mathrm{Dom}_{c}(H)
. Pick a function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
with
\partial_{\nu}\chi=0
and
\chi=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(f)
. By step 1 we have
\chi e^{-tH}f\in\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and

\left\|\chi e^{-tH}f-f\right\|=\left\|\chi\left(e^{-tH}f-f\right)\right\|\to 0

as
t\to 0+
. Moreover

\displaystyle H(\chi e^{-tH}f-f)

\displaystyle=H\left[\chi\left(e^{-tH}f-f\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\chi\left(He^{-tH}f-Hf\right)-2(\nabla\chi,\nabla(e^{-tH}f-f))-(H\chi)(e^{tH}f-f),

which shows that
\left\|H(\chi e^{-tH}f-f)\right\|\to 0
as

, completing the proof.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let us show that one cannot drop completeness in Theorem
2.1
. Pick
M:=[1,\infty)
,
\psi:=1
, and fix the Riemannian metric on
M
as
g(x)\coloneqq 1/x^{4}
. Then, the metric distance between two points
p,q
is given by
\varrho(p,q)=\operatorname{sgn}(q-p)(1/p-1/q)
and
M
is not complete (the sequence
p_{n}=n
is Cauchy but does not converge to any point in
M
). The Laplace-Beltrami operator is given in this case by

-\Delta f=-x^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^{2}f\right).

Chose
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\hat{f}(x)\coloneqq c_{1}\cosh(1/x)+c_{2}\sinh(1/x)

satisfies
\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{f}}{\mathrm{d}x}_{|x=1}=0
. It is straightforward to check that
-\Delta\hat{f}=-\hat{f}
. By the boundedness of
\hat{f}
, it follows that
\hat{f},\Delta\hat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathring{M})
, and by the Neumann boundary condition at
x=1
it follows that

\langle-\Delta h,\hat{f}\rangle=\langle h,-\Delta\hat{f}\rangle\quad\text{ for every $h\in\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)$}.

That is, for
H_{0}
defined as
-\Delta
with domain of definition
\hat{C}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
one has
\hat{f}\in\mathrm{Dom}(H^{*}_{0})
and
H_{0}^{*}\hat{f}=-\Delta\hat{f}
. As a consequence,
(H_{0}+1)^{*}\hat{f}=0
. Therefore,
(H_{0}+1)^{*}=H_{0}^{*}+1
has a nontrivial kernel and we conclude that
H_{0}
is not essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}(M)
.

Attribute-Guided Encryption with Facial Texture Masking
Chun Pong Lau, Jiang Liu, Rama Chellappa

Abstract
The increasingly pervasive facial recognition (FR) systems raise serious concerns about personal privacy, especially for billions of users who have publicly shared their photos on social media. Several attempts have been made to protect individuals from unauthorized FR systems utilizing adversarial attacks to generate encrypted face images to protect users from being identified by FR systems. However, existing methods suffer from poor visual quality or low attack success rates, which limit their usability in practice. In this paper, we propose Attribute Guided Encryption with Facial Texture Masking (AGE-FTM) that performs a dual manifold adversarial attack on FR systems to achieve both good visual quality and high black-box attack success rates. In particular, AGE-FTM utilizes a high fidelity generative adversarial network (GAN) to generate natural on-manifold adversarial samples by modifying facial attributes, and performs the facial texture masking attack to generate imperceptible off-manifold adversarial samples. Extensive experiments on the CelebA-HQ dataset demonstrate that our proposed method produces more natural-looking encrypted images than state-of-the-art methods while achieving competitive attack performance. We further evaluate the effectiveness of AGE-FTM in the real world using a commercial FR API and validate its usefulness in practice through an user study.

2.2
Off- and On-Manifold Attacks

Recently, several works aim to use a low-dimensional underlying data manifold to attack neural networks by creating on-manifold adversarial samples
[
Jalal et al.(2017)Jalal, Ilyas, Daskalakis, and
Dimakis
,
Song et al.(2018)Song, Shu, Kushman, and Ermon
,
Stutz et al.(2019)Stutz, Hein, and Schiele
,
Lin et al.(2020)Lin, Lau, Levine, Chellappa, and Feizi
]
. On-manifold adversarial samples are adversarial samples constrained to lie on data manifolds and are obtained by perturbing inputs in the latent space learned by generative models. Adversarial samples computed in the image space are considered as off-manifold
[
Stutz et al.(2019)Stutz, Hein, and Schiele
]
. On-manifold adversarial samples have been used to break models trained by adversarial training
[
Song et al.(2018)Song, Shu, Kushman, and Ermon
]
as well as deep generative model-based defense methods
[
Chen et al.(2020)Chen, Xie, and Zhu
]
such as DefenseGAN
[
Samangouei et al.(2018)Samangouei, Kabkab, and
Chellappa
]
, Analysis by Synthetics
[
Schott et al.(2018)Schott, Rauber, Bethge, and
Brendel
]
and MoG-VAE
[
Ghosh et al.(2019)Ghosh, Losalka, and Black
]
. Compared to off-manifold attacks, on-manifold attacks generate more natural-looking and less conspicuous adversarial samples as they come from a learned natural image manifold.
[
Lau et al.(2021)Lau, Liu, Souri, Lin, Feizi, and
Chellappa
]
proposed Joint Space Threat Model, which is a dual-manifold attack that combines both on-manifold and off-manifold perturbations.

3
Attribute Guided Encryption with Facial Texture Masking (AGE-FTM)

3.1
Problem Formulation

In this section, we formulate the problem of adversarial attacks on FR systems. Suppose the face images
x\in{\mathcal{X}}:=\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}
are drawn from an underlying distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, where
H
,
W
and
C
are the height, width and the number of channels of the image respectively. Let
f_{\theta}
be a parameterized model which maps any image in
{\mathcal{X}}
to a feature vector
y
in
f_{\theta}({\mathcal{X}})
. An
accurate
FR system can map two images
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
with the same identity to features that are very close in the feature space, i.e.
{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(x_{1}),f_{\theta}(x_{2}))\leq\tau
, where
{\mathcal{D}}
is a distance function and
\tau
is a threshold. A successful attack fools the FR system to map an adversarial image
\hat{x}
with the same identity as
x
to a feature that is far away from the feature of
x
, i.e.
{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(\hat{x}),f_{\theta}(x))\geq\tau
. Another type of successful attack is the targeted attack, which is also called the impersonation attack in FR. It aims to perturb the source image such that it is identified as the target identity, i.e.
{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(\hat{x}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))\leq\tau
where
x_{t}
is the target image. From
[
Lin et al.(2020)Lin, Lau, Levine, Chellappa, and Feizi
]
,
exact manifold assumption (EMA)
is that there exists a generative model
G
such that there exists a latent representation
z
in the latent space
{\mathcal{Z}}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every image
x
.

Off- and On-Manifold Robustness

To encrypt the facial information against malicious FR models in a practical scenario, we consider the impersonation attack in a black-box setting. We use multiple FR models to craft the attack and denote the set of FR systems as
{\mathcal{A}}_{f}
. Formally, we aim to solve the following problem:

\min_{\hat{x}}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(\hat{x}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))=\sum_{f_{\theta}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{f}}1-\cos{(f_{\theta}(\hat{x}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))},

(1)

where
x_{t}
is the target image and
{\mathcal{D}}
is the cosine similarity loss.
We consider the adversarial samples perturbed within the image space and the latent space as off-manifold and on-manifold adversarial samples respectively. Mathematically,

\min_{\delta\in\Delta}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(x+\delta),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))~\text{and}~\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(G(z+\lambda)),f_{\theta}(x_{t})),

(2)

where
x+\delta
and
G(z+\lambda)
are the off-manifold and on-manifold adversarial samples respectively,
\Delta=\{\delta:\lVert\delta\rVert_{p}<\epsilon\}
and
\Lambda=\{\lambda:\lVert\lambda\rVert_{p}<\eta\}
.

3.2
Facial Texture Masking Attack

Off-manifold attacks in the image space such as PGD
[
Madry et al.(2017)Madry, Makelov, Schmidt, Tsipras, and
Vladu
]
are usually strong and imperceptible. However, when it comes to FR, in particular for black-box attacks, it becomes less effective. Moreover, it is easier to perceive the existence of the attack when it comes to face images than in other images. According to
[
Tan and Gan(2015)
]
, human perception systems are less sensitive to changes in high-frequency components compared to low-frequency components. In other words, edges or textures of the face image are less disturbing to the human perception systems. On the other hand, faces of people tend to be smooth piecewise regions. It is more noticeable when there is a perturbation in those regions. This motivates us to propose Facial Texture Masking (FTM) Attack, which consists of two components.

The first one is that we do not attack the whole image but only the textured regions in the image. This makes the attack hard to notice from the perspective of the human visual system. There are many methods to extract the textured regions in the images, such as edge detection algorithms or truncation in the frequency domain using Fourier transform or Wavelet transform. To reduce computational cost, we are intentionally not using a deep learning approach to extract textures. Instead, we apply
unsharp masking
technique to extract the textures. Suppose
{\mathcal{B}}
is a Gaussian blur operator. We can obtain the binary mask
M_{\mathcal{H}}
by

M_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbbm{1}\{{\mathcal{H}}(x)>\gamma\},~{\mathcal{H}}(x)=|x-{\mathcal{B}}(x)|

(3)

where
\mathbbm{1}
is the characteristic function,
{\mathcal{H}}
is the high frequency component that generally consists of edges, and
\gamma
is some predefined threshold. Then we can restrict the off-manifold perturbation within the high frequency components. Taking the PGD attack as an example, we only perturb the pixels that are in the high frequency regions, i.e.
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{H}}=\{x_{ij}:M_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{ij})=1\}
. The iteration steps become:

\delta_{k+1}=\epsilon_{iter}\cdot sign\left(\nabla_{\delta_{k}|{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{H}}}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(x+\delta_{k}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))\right),~\nabla_{\delta_{k}|{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{H}}}(x)=\nabla_{\delta_{k}}(x)\odot M_{\mathcal{H}},

(4)

where
\epsilon_{iter}
is the attack step size at each iteration and
\odot
is the Hadamard product. We denote this attack as Texture Masking Attack (TMA).

Although the TMA significantly reduces the perceptibility of the attack, we can still notice the perturbations at the face portion if we zoom in and look carefully. To further improve the imperceptibility of the attack, we observe that perturbations within the hair region are barely noticable. Therefore, we leverage a face parsing algorithm to locate the hair region. Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
be the face parsing algorithm, which is a function
{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
that maps each pixel
x_{ij}
in a face image
x
in
{\mathcal{X}}
to a discrete component label
c
in
{\mathcal{C}}:=\{1,\cdots,|{\mathcal{C}}|\}
. Then the new binary mask
M
is

M=\mathbbm{1}\left\{\{{\mathcal{H}}(x)>\gamma\}\cap\{x_{ij}:{\mathcal{F}}(x_{ij})=c_{\text{hair}}\}\right\},

(5)

where
\{x_{ij}:{\mathcal{F}}(x_{ij})=c_{\text{hair}}\}
is the set containing the pixels in the hair portion. Denote
{\mathcal{A}}=\{x_{ij}:M(x_{ij})=1\}
. The formulation of the FTM attack becomes

\min_{\delta\in\Delta}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(x+\delta\odot M),f_{\theta}(x_{t})),

(6)

and the iteration steps in the FTM attack will be

\delta_{k+1}=\epsilon_{iter}\cdot sign\left(\nabla_{\delta_{k}|{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(x+\delta_{k}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))\right).

(7)

3.3
Attributed Guided Encryption

Makeup attacks such as
[
Yin et al.(2021)Yin, Wang, Yao, Guo, Kong, Ding, Li, and
Liu
,
Hu et al.(2022)Hu, Liu, Zhang, Li, Zhang, Jin, and
Wu
]
are shown to be effective for FR systems. These attacks can synthesize makeup in some parts of the face, such as the eye region in
[
Yin et al.(2021)Yin, Wang, Yao, Guo, Kong, Ding, Li, and
Liu
]
or the whole face in
[
Hu et al.(2022)Hu, Liu, Zhang, Li, Zhang, Jin, and
Wu
]
. However, there are some drawbacks. Makeup attacks usually require a makeup dataset for training. This leads to better visual performance for female because of the imbalance of gender in the makeup dataset. Moreover, in order to increase the attack strength, makeup attacks usually produce heavy makeup on the original images which may be undesirable. Therefore, we propose the Attributed Guided Encryption (AGE), which uses the attribute vectors in the generator to guide the on-manifold attack. We apply HFGI
[
Wang et al.(2022)Wang, Zhang, Fan, Wang, and Chen
]
, which is a state-of-the-art method that uses a high fidelity GAN and can invert and edit high resolution face images effectively. HFGI consists of an encoder
E
to encode the images and uses a generator
G
to edit the high resolution face images. Then the AGE formulation is

\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(G(E(x)+\lambda;{\bm{a}})),f_{\theta}(x_{t})),

(8)

To obtain the perturbation, we optimize it by

\lambda_{k+1}=\eta_{iter}\cdot sign\left(\nabla_{\lambda_{k}}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(G(E(x)+\lambda_{k};{\bm{a}}_{k}),f_{\theta}(x_{t}))\right),

(9)

where
{\bm{a}}_{k}=\dfrac{k{\bm{a}}}{N}
,
\eta_{iter}
is the attack step size at each iteration, and
N
is the total iteration number. For each step, intuitively the latent vector
z
, which represents the general latent representation, is perturbed by
\lambda_{k}
and guided by
{\bm{a}}_{k}
, the specific semantic latent direction, such as attributes "age" and "smile". Therefore, combining FTM and AGE, we propose the AGE-FTM, which is a dual manifold attack consisting of both off- and on-manifold attacks,

{\boxed{\begin{split}&\text{\bf AGE-FTM optimization:}\\
&\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda,~\delta\in\Delta}{\mathcal{D}}(f_{\theta}(G(E(x)+\lambda;{\bm{a}})+\delta\odot M,f_{\theta}(x_{t})).\end{split}}}

(10)

We compare the performance of the proposed method with different adversarial attacks specifically for FR: 1) Perturbation-based attack TIP-IM

4.6
Limitations and Future direction

AGE-FTM involves two components, AGE and FTM. AGE perturbs the latent space guided with attribute vectors to perform an on-manifold attack. However, it requires a generator that has a semantic latent space and generates a high-quality face image. A good face alignment is necessary for these face image GAN models. In other words, AGE does not work without face alignment. This would be a future direction that uses a different generator, such as flow-based or diffusion models that do not require face alignment.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose AGE-FTM that performs dual manifold adversarial attack on FR systems for facial privacy protection. AGE-FTM utilizes the image manifold information learned by GAN models to create
natural
changes to the source images guided by facial attributes, and adds
imperceptible
perturbations through facial texture masking. Our extensive experiments on the CelebA-HQ dataset demonstrate that AGE-FTM achieves state-of-the-art black-box attack performance and good visual quality, which can be useful for facial privacy protection in real-world applications.

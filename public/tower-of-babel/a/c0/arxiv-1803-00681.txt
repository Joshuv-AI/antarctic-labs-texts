Reedy Model Structures in Families
Edouard Balzin

Abstract
Given a family of model categories ℰ → ℛ \cal E\to\cal R over a Reedy category, we outline a set of conditions which lead to the existence of a Reedy model structure on the category of sections 𝖲𝖾𝖼𝗍 ⁡ ( ℛ , ℰ ) {\sf Sect}(\cal R,\cal E) . We prove that for a wide class of examples, this model structure serves as a strictification of the ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category of sections of the higher-categorical family associated to ℰ → ℛ \cal E\to\cal R .

Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a model category. Given a small category
{\mathcal{C}}
, the associated
functor category
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
can be endowed with objectwise weak equivalences:
take those natural transformations
X\to Y
such that for each
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
the value
X(c)\to Y(c)
is a weak equivalence in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
For a general
[
25
]
choice of
{\mathcal{M}}
(assuming small (co)limits, see Definition
2.2
) and
{\mathcal{C}}
,
there is no known way to complete the class of objectwise weak equivalences on
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})

into a model structure.
One option to treat this issue is to assume
additional requirements about
{\mathcal{M}}
, such as being cofibrantly generated, or further, combinatorial.
Another way concerns the assumptions on
{\mathcal{C}}
.

The notion of a Reedy category is attributed to
[
26
]
. To fix a definition, a

Reedy category
is a small category
{\mathcal{R}}

possessing two subcategories
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}\subset{\mathcal{R}}\supset{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
with

{\rm Ob}\,{\mathcal{R}}_{-}={\rm Ob}\,{\mathcal{R}}={\rm Ob}\,{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
,
and a degree function

deg:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
taking values in natural numbers, such that

1.

the isomorphisms of
{\mathcal{R}}
are identities,

2.

the non-identities of
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
lower the value of
deg
, and the non-identities of
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
raise the value of
deg
,

3.

any morphism
f:x\to y
factors uniquely as
f=f^{+}\circ f_{-}
, where

f_{-}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
and
f^{+}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
.

Many elementary diagram categories are naturally Reedy: for example, the two-arrow category

a\longleftarrow b\longrightarrow c
appearing in the calculus of pullbacks,
can be endowed with at least two Reedy structures; in the first one, both maps raise the degree,
in the other, both maps lower the degree. Similarly,
the category

[n]=0\longrightarrow...\longrightarrow n

admits two different Reedy structures: for the first structure,

deg(0)=0
and all maps advance the degree, for the second,
deg(0)=n
and all
maps lower the degree. Taking all
[n]
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
defines a
full subcategory
\Delta\subset{\rm\bf Cat}
of the category of small categories. The surjections,
the injections and
deg([n])=n
make
\Delta
(and
\Delta^{\sf op}
) into a Reedy category.
Furthermore, for any category
{\mathcal{C}}
, denote by
\Delta{\mathcal{C}}
the category of functors
\mathbf{c}:[n]\to{\mathcal{C}}
,
with morphisms
\mathbf{c}\to\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
given by functors
[n]\to[m]
in the overcategory

{\rm\bf Cat}/{\mathcal{C}}
. The Reedy structure on
\Delta
induces a Reedy structure
on
\Delta{\mathcal{C}}

[
8
, 22.10]
. This example shows that Reedy categories exist
in a great variety.

The classical result (see e.g.
[
18
]
) asserts that given a Reedy category

{\mathcal{R}}
and a model category
{\mathcal{M}}
, there exists an explicitly-constructed model structure
on
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{M}})
, with objectwise weak equivalences. The existence of a model structure
on functors from a Reedy category permits the computation of various homotopy limits,
even for a general category
{\mathcal{C}}
: the assignment

(\mathbf{c}:[n]\to{\mathcal{C}})\mapsto\mathbf{c}(n)

defines a (homotopy cofinal) functor
[
8
, 22.11]

p_{t}:\Delta{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
. We can use
the pull-back along
p_{t}
to embed
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}(\Delta{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
, and then use
the Reedy model structure on
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}(\Delta{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
for homotopy colimit computations.

Sections over a Reedy category

In this paper, we study families of model categories and their associated categories
of sections. In light of the Grothendieck construction
[
11
,
33
]
, a natural way to treat
a family of categories indexed by
{\mathcal{C}}
is to consider a functor
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
together
with additional conditions (that we survey in Section
1
).

Given a family
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
, we can associate to it the category
of sections
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{E}})
, consisting of those functors

S:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
such that
p\circ S=id_{\mathcal{C}}
. The case of functors
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{M}}

can be recovered by considering the trivial family, given by projection

pr_{\mathcal{C}}:{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
: the sections of
pr_{\mathcal{C}}
are identified with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
.

For an example of a nontrivial model-categorical family, denote by
{\rm\bf CRing}
the category of commutative rings and consider
a small diagram
A:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\rm\bf CRing}
. For a ring
R
, denote by

C^{\bullet}({\rm\bf Mod}_{R})
the
category of chain complexes of modules over
R
, that can be endowed with projective
model structure
[
18
]
.
Define
{\rm\bf Mod}_{A}
to be the category consisting of pairs
(c,M)
where
c
is in
{\mathcal{C}}
and

M
is a complex of
A(c)
-modules. A morphism
(c,M)\to(c^{\prime},N)
in
{\mathcal{E}}
is given by
a map
f:c\to c^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
and a chain map
M\to N|_{A(c)}
of complexes of
A(c)
-modules, where
we restrict
N
along the map
A(f):A(c)\to A(c^{\prime})
; by adjunction, it is the same data as a map of
complexes of
A(c^{\prime})
-modules
A(c^{\prime})\otimes_{A(c)}M\to N
.
This example of algebras and modules can be generalised
[
31
,
16
]
,
with the abstraction being the notion of a Quillen presheaf
[
17
,
3
]
, which
is the same data as
a functor from a small category
{\mathcal{C}}
to model categories and Quillen adjunctions.

When
{\mathcal{C}}=\Delta
,
the category of sections
{\sf Sect}(\Delta,{\rm\bf Mod}_{A})
plays an important role in
the questions of cohomological descent: inside
{\sf Sect}(\Delta,{\rm\bf Mod}_{A})
, there is
a full subcategory denoted
{\sf Sect}_{{\mathbb{L}}cart}(\Delta,{\rm\bf Mod}_{A})
consisting of sections
S
that send

[n]\to[m]
in
\Delta
to a quasi-isomorphism
A(m)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A(n)}S(n)\to S(m)
. As
explained in
[
31
]
, the category
{\sf Sect}_{{\mathbb{L}}cart}(\Delta,{\rm\bf Mod}_{A})
is the category
of descent data in derived algebraic geometry, see also Remark
3.48
.

To work with sections homotopically, one would like to, just as in the case of functors
to a model category, have a model structure. We thus pose the following problem: given a family

p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
over a Reedy category
{\mathcal{R}}
,
formulate the requirements on
p
for
the category
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})
to have a model structure, that specialises
to the Reedy model structure in the case of a trivial family.

Our findings are summarised by Theorem
2.11
in the main text:

Theorem 1

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a Reedy category and
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
an
admissible model semifibration
.
Then the category of sections
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})
has a model structure with objectwise
weak equivalences.

Let us explain the conditions imposed on
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
.

The functor
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
cannot be completely general: as it turns out, the appropriate condition
on
p
is that of a
semifibration
, which is a
mixture of the conditions of Grothendieck fibration and opfibration.
In detail, the semifibration property consists in requiring
that the restriction
{\mathcal{E}}|_{{\mathcal{R}}_{-}}\to{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
is a Grothendieck prefibration; the restriction

{\mathcal{E}}|_{{\mathcal{R}}_{+}}\to{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
is dually assumed to be a preopfibration. Denoting
{\mathcal{E}}(c):=p^{-1}(c)
, these two conditions
imply that for each
f_{-}:x\to y
in
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
, there is a functor
f_{-}^{*}:{\mathcal{E}}(y)\to{\mathcal{E}}(x)
,
and dually for each
f^{+}:x\to y
in
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
, there is a functor

f^{+}_{!}:{\mathcal{E}}(x)\to{\mathcal{E}}(y)
. The final requirement of a semifibration relates these
two classes of functors, see Definition
1.38
and
Proposition
1.40
for detail.

Given an object
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
of a Reedy category
{\mathcal{R}}
, denote
Lat(x)\subset{\mathcal{R}}/x
the
“latching category”, consisting of all
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
-maps
y\to x
without the identity
map. Similarly, the “matching category”
Mat(x)\subset x\backslash{\mathcal{C}}
consists
of all
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
-maps
x\to y
minus the identity.
The semifibration property of
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
implies the existence of two restriction functors

L_{x}:{\mathcal{E}}|_{Lat(x)}\to{\mathcal{E}}(x)
and
R_{x}:{\mathcal{E}}|_{Mat(x)}\to{\mathcal{E}}(x)
. Given a section

S:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
, the latching and the matching object functors are defined as follows:

\mathscr{Lat}_{x}S:=\operatorname{\varinjlim}_{Lat(x)}L_{x}S|_{Lat(x)},\,\,\,\mathscr{Mat}_{x}S:=\operatorname{\varprojlim}_{Mat(x)}R_{x}S|_{Mat(x)}

(we assume that all needed (co)limits exist).
Given a section

S:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
of the semifibration
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
, its value
S(x)
fits into a diagram

\mathscr{Lat}_{x}S\to S(x)\to\mathscr{Mat}_{x}S

and just as in the classical Reedy case, such diagrams for different
x
completely control the behaviour of
S
.

The semifibration
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
being
model
means that each fibre
{\mathcal{E}}(x)
is a model category,
and that we also require the transition functors
f^{+}_{!}:{\mathcal{E}}(x)\to{\mathcal{E}}(y)

along
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
-maps to preserve cofibrations and
trivial cofibrations (we do not require the preservation of colimits, which permits
to consider such functors as tensor products, and consequently the examples of
algebras and TQFT
[
20
]
).
A dual condition of preserving fibrations and trivial fibrations should hold for
f^{*}_{-}:{\mathcal{E}}(y)\to{\mathcal{E}}(x)
.
For each map
of sections
S\to T
, there is a naturally induced diagram

{diagram}

We define
S\to T
to be a
Reedy cofibration

if for each
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
, the map
\mathscr{Lat}_{x}T\coprod_{\mathscr{Lat}_{x}S}S(x)\to T(x)
is a cofibration in
{\mathcal{E}}(x)
.
Dually,
S\to T
is a
Reedy fibration
if for each
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
, the
map
S(x)\to\mathscr{Mat}_{x}S\prod_{\mathscr{Mat}_{x}T}T(x)
is a fibration in
{\mathcal{E}}(x)
. The Reedy weak equivalences are defined objectwise.

The defined classes of maps give a model structure on
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})
, provided
that the model semifibration
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies the
admissibility
condition, Definition
2.9
.
The admissibility guarantees that the functor
S\mapsto\mathscr{Lat}_{x}S
sends (trivial) Reedy cofibrations to
(trivial) cofibrations, and dually for
\mathscr{Mat}_{x}
. The examples of admissible families are covered by Lemma

2.10
.
A Quillen presheaf is admissible,
since its transition functors preserve (co)limits.
A Grothendieck fibration in model categories and right derivable functors over
[n]
is also admissible,
which follows from the simple structure of the matching categories of
[n]
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is similar to the one of the classical case.
The explicit
description of the model structure permits to verify various properties,
such as being cofibrantly generated under reasonable assumptions, Propositions

2.30
and
2.32
.

Comparison with higher-categorical sections

The second part of our work establishes a relation with the higher category theory
(that we model using the language of quasicategories
[
19
,
6
]
).
To begin, recall that given a category
{\mathcal{M}}
with weak equivalences
{\mathcal{W}}
,
its
(\infty,1)
-localisation is an
(\infty,1)
-functor
F:{\mathcal{M}}\to L_{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{M}}
,
such that for any
(\infty,1)
-category
{\mathcal{Z}}
,
the induced
(\infty,1)
-functor
F^{*}:\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}(L_{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{Z}})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{Z}})
is full and faithful,
and its essential image consists of
(\infty,1)
-functors that send
{\mathcal{W}}
to equivalences
of
{\mathcal{Z}}
. For model categories, the
(\infty,1)
-localisation enjoys various
special properties
[
12
]
. In particular, Cisinski
[
6
]
has shown that
given any model category
{\mathcal{M}}
and a small category
{\mathcal{C}}
, the natural

(\infty,1)
-functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},L_{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{M}})
induces an

(\infty,1)
-equivalence

L\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Fun}}({\mathcal{C}},L_{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{M}})

where on the left, we localise with respect to the objectwise weak equivalences.

Thanks to the various literature on quasicategories
[
19
,
6
,
9
,
12
,
21
]
there is now a well-developed theory of Grothendieck fibrations
of
(\infty,1)
-categories. In some cases
[
12
,
21
]
, it is known how to
(\infty,1)
-localise
a family
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
to get a properly behaved higher-categorical family
L{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
.
We thus ask how the localisation
L{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})
of the model structure of
Theorem
1
is compared with the

(\infty,1)
-sections
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},L{\mathcal{E}})
.

We treat the comparison issue in the following generality.
A functor
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
is a
left model Reedy fibration
if it is a
Grothendieck opfibration, a Grothendieck fibration over
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
, and is an admissible
model semifibration. Localising
{\mathcal{E}}
along the union
\cup_{c}{\mathcal{W}}(c)
of the
fibrewise weak equivalences yields an
(\infty,1)
-functor
L{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
that
is a coCartesian fibration (in the sense of
[
19
, Definition 2.4.2.1]
) and a Cartesian fibration
over
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
. The natural functor
{\mathcal{E}}\to L{\mathcal{E}}
induces the
(\infty,1)
-functor

{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})\to{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},L{\mathcal{E}})
. The following result is Theorem
3.37
:

Theorem 2

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
be a left model Reedy fibration. Then
the induced infinity-functor
L{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{E}})\to{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{R}},L{\mathcal{E}})

is an equivalence of quasicategories.

The origins of Theorem
2
lie in the paper
[
17
]
. However,
the proof of
[
17
, Théorème 18.2]
is incomplete: the authors do not
provide proofs for the needed higher-categorical arguments, and the model-categorical
considerations of
[
17
, Théorème 18.2]
contain a mistake (it is assumed
that the latching object functor preserves fibrant objects). Nonetheless, our proof of Theorem
2

shows that the infinity-categorical
Reedy induction can be carried out
along the general lines of
[
17
]
.
There are other comparison results
[
14
,
29
]
that work under more assumptions on the family
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
.
For example,
[
14
]
bypasses the Reedy induction, yet is only valid
for Quillen presheaves in the combinatorial model setting.

The example of Quillen presheaves has a particularly good strictification result, conjectured
in
[
17
]

and outlined in Proposition
3.42
:

Proposition 3

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
be a Quillen presheaf over a small category
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then localising
{\mathcal{M}}
along the
fibrewise weak equivalences yields an equivalence of
(\infty,1)
-categories

L{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{C}},L{\mathcal{M}}).

where we treat
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{M}})
as a category equipped with objectwise weak equivalences, with no model
structure.

Semifibrations

1.1
Cartesian arrows, prefibrations, sections

Let
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
be a functor. For
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
, denote by
{\mathcal{E}}(c)
the fibre
category
p^{-1}(c)
over
c
. It thus consists of all
X\in{\mathcal{E}}
with

p(X)=c
and all the maps
X\to X^{\prime}
with
p(X\to X^{\prime})=id_{c}
.

Definition 1.1

A morphism
\alpha:X\to Y
of
{\mathcal{E}}

•

is
p
-cartesian
, or simply cartesian, if for any other map

\beta:X^{\prime}\to Y
with
p(\beta)=p(\alpha)
there exists a unique
morphism
\gamma:X^{\prime}\to X
in
{\mathcal{E}}(p(X))
which
factors
\beta
as
\alpha\circ\gamma
.

•

is
p
-opcartesian
, or simply opcartesian,
if for any other map
\delta:X\to Y^{\prime}
with
p(\delta)=p(\alpha)

there exists a unique morphism
\eta:Y\to Y^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{E}}(p(Y))
which factors

\delta
as
\eta\circ\alpha
.

A
p
-cartesian or
p
-opcartesian morphism
\alpha:X\to Y
is
covering the morphism
f:c\to c^{\prime}
iff
p(\alpha)=f
.

In our definition of cartesian and opcartesian morphisms, we are faithful to
the original terminology of
[
11
]
. Today, a different definition of
(op)cartesian maps is presented in many sources
[
33
,
20
]
,
with the definition of
[
11
]
referred to as
“locally cartesian” morphism.

Definition 1.2

A functor
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a

•

prefibration iff for any
f:x\to y
of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{E}}(y)

there exists a cartesian morphism
\alpha:X\to Y
covering
f
,
that is,
p(\alpha)=f
.

•

preopfibration iff for any
f:x\to y
of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
X\in{\mathcal{E}}(x)

there exists a cartesian morphism
\delta:X\to Z
covering
f
,
that is,
p(\alpha)=f
.

Lemma 1.3

If
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a prefibration, then

p^{\sf op}:{\mathcal{E}}^{\sf op}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\sf op}
is a preopfibration.
\square

Notation 1.4

If
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a prefibration,
f:x\to y
is a morphism and

Y\in{\mathcal{E}}(y)
, we shall usually denote a chosen cartesian lift by

f^{*}Y\to Y
. The same applies when
p
is a preopfibration,
where for
X\in{\mathcal{E}}(x)
, we denote by
X\to f_{!}X

the chosen opcartesian lift.

Definition 1.5

A prefibration or preopfibration
q:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is small if both
{\mathcal{C}}

and
{\mathcal{E}}
are small categories. A prefibration or preopfibration
q

is
\emph{discrete}
if for each
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
, the category
{\mathcal{E}}(c)

has no non-identity maps (in other words, it is isomorphic to a set).

Lemma 1.6

Let
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
be a discrete prefibration.
Then the composition of cartesian morphisms of
{\mathcal{E}}
is cartesian.
The dual is true for preopfibrations.

Proof.

Clear, due to the lack of fibre maps.
\square

In general, not any pre(op)fibration has the property described in the previous
lemma. Those which have it, are called Grothendieck (op)fibrations.

Definition 1.7

A prefibration
p:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is, furthermore, a
Grothendieck
fibration
iff the composition of cartesian maps is cartesian. The
definition for
Grothendieck opfibrations
is dual.

Discrete pre(op)fibrations are thus automatically (op)fibrations.
The examples of non-discrete fibrations are, however, abundant.

Remark 1.8

It is not necessarily the case that the category
{\mathcal{E}}
is “bigger”
than
{\mathcal{C}}
. For example, the functor
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}\coprod{\mathcal{D}}

is a fibration and an opfibration.

Remark 1.9

In what follows, (op)fibrations will be considered as special cases of
pre(op)fibra-tions, with additional remarks where necessary.
Otherwise, any definition or a result given for a pre(op)fibration
implies the same for an (op)fibration.

Construction 1.10

Given a functor
E
from
{\mathcal{C}}
to categories, we produce an opfibration,
which we denote
\int E\to{\mathcal{C}}
and call the
Grothendieck construction

[
33
]
of
E
. An object of
\int E
is a pair
(c,X)
of
c\in{\mathcal{C}}

and
X\in E(c)
, and a morphism
(c,X)\to(c^{\prime},X^{\prime})
consists of

f:c\to c^{\prime}
together with a map
\alpha:E(f)(X)\to X^{\prime}
in
E(c^{\prime})
.

Dually, for a contravariant category-valued functor
F
defined on
{\mathcal{C}}
,
its Grothendieck construction is a fibration
\int F\to{\mathcal{C}}
with same pairs

(c,Y)
serving as objects, but with maps given by pairs of
f:c\to c^{\prime}
and

\beta:Y\to F(f)Y^{\prime}
in
F(c)
.

Grothendieck construction motivates the following perspective.
Consider a prefibration

1.3
Factorisation systems and semifibrations

Definition 1.34

A
factorisation system
on a category
{\mathcal{C}}
consists of a
pair of subcategories
\mathscr{L},\mathscr{R}\subset{\mathcal{C}}
containing all
isomorphisms of
{\mathcal{C}}
, such that any morphism
f:c\to c^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{C}}

can be decomposed as

f:c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l}}{{\longrightarrow}}c^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\longrightarrow}}c^{\prime}

(
1.5
)

with
l\in{\rm Mor}\,\mathscr{L}
and
r\in{\rm Mor}\,\mathscr{R}
. This factorisation must be
moreover unique up to unique isomorphism.

In this work, a
factorisation category
will denote a triple
({\mathcal{C}},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{R})
of a category together with a factorisation system
(\mathscr{L},\mathscr{R})
.

When clear, we shall simply refer to a factorisation category
({\mathcal{C}},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{R})
as
{\mathcal{C}}
. Due to the isomorphism condition
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{R}
contain all the objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
. We shall often refer to
\mathscr{L}
as the left class of maps, and to
\mathscr{R}
as the right class of maps.

Definition 1.35

A strict
factorisation functor

F:({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},\mathscr{L}^{\prime},\mathscr{R}^{\prime})\to({\mathcal{C}},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{R})
is a functor
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{C}}
such that
F(\mathscr{L}^{\prime})\subset\mathscr{L}
and
F(\mathscr{R}^{\prime})\subset\mathscr{R}
. We shall occasionally denote by
F_{L}:\mathscr{L}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{L}
and
F_{R}:\mathscr{R}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{R}
the induced functors.

An important class of factorisation categories is given by Reedy categories. To repeat,

Definition 1.36

A
Reedy category

{\mathcal{R}}
is a factorisation category
({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{R}}_{-},{\mathcal{R}}_{+})
together with a degree function

taking values in natural numbers, such that

1.

the isomorphisms of
{\mathcal{R}}
are identities,

2.

the non-identities of
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
lower the value of
deg
,

3.

the non-identities of
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
raise the value of
deg
,

It is implied that
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}
is locally Noether and
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}
is locally Artin.
For the literature concerning Reedy categories, see
[
19
,
13
,
18
,
27
]
.
We assume the degree function to be taking values in natural numbers. While this suffices
for most practical examples, our choice excludes from consideration the case
{\mathcal{R}}=\beta

for an arbitrary ordinal

. The latter has some importance for the theory-building
of Section

, but is a relatively mild case and will be treated
by hand once needed.
Henceforth, we shall also be implicit about the degree

2.

Remark 3.22

A result of Hinich
[

is similar to that of Mazel-Gee
[

with a distinction: aside from inverting also the maps in the base, the fibration
localiser

is assumed to be saturated. The way we construct

does not usually produce a saturated localiser,

In many cases,

saturates to a bigger class of weak equivalences: those maps

which projection

If we a dealing with, say, a Quillen presheaf

equivalences only if restricted to the subcategories of cofibrant or fibrant objects,
depending on the direction.
To study what happens if we localise

in this case, let us first introduce some tools.

A.2
Normalised model structure

For a variation of the argument of Theorem
2.11
, let us look
at the following.
Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a
\Delta
-indexed category. The subcategory
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}\subset\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
controls degenerations. Recasting the usual definition,

Definition A.10

An object
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
is
degenerate
if there exists a non-identity degree-raising map
y\to x
of
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}
. An object
x
is thus non-degenerate iff
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}/x=\{id:x\to x\}
, or, equivalently,
Lat(x)=\emptyset
.

For the purposes of this subsection, consider a functor
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
such that

1.

It is a semifibration over the Segal factorisation system.

2.

The induced semifibration over the Reedy factorisation system
({\mathcal{X}}_{-},{\mathcal{X}}_{+})
is an admissible model Reedy semifibration.

3.

It is normalised, that is its restriction
{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
is a locally constant fibration,
a fibration for which all the transition functors are equivalences.

Given a section
X\in{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{E}})
of such
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
, we can thus conclude that
\mathscr{Lat}_{x}X
is the initial object of
{\mathcal{E}}(x)
for each non-degenerate
x
.

Definition A.11

A section
X
is
normalised
iff it takes any arrow of
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}
to an opcartesian arrow of
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
.

Lemma A.12

A section
X
is
normalised
iff for any degenerate object
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
, the latching map
\mathscr{Lat}_{y}X\to X(y)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Given the definition of a normalised section, we have that for each
f:x\to y
in
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}/y
, the map
f_{!}X(x)\to X(y)
is an isomorphism. One then checks that the latching category
Lat(y)\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{+}/y
is connected
and so the colimit of a constant
Lat(y)
-diagram with value
X(y)
gives
X(y)
.
\square

Remark A.13

If we take
x\to y
to be an ordinary degeneracy (if projected to

) then
X(x)\to X(y)
is an isomorphism (note that
{\mathcal{E}}(x)\cong{\mathcal{E}}(y)
.

Lemma A.14

The category
{\sf Sect}_{N}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{E}})
admits limits and colimits, which are calculated in
{\sf Sect}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

The colimit part is trivial and is left to the reader. For the limit part, we will use the Segal factorisation system on
{\mathcal{X}}
to calculate limits. For the proof, recall also the functor
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta^{\sf op}
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{X}}

x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}
. Given that on the level of
\Delta
, the maps of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}
are interval inclusions, and so we have an equivalence
x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\pi(x)\in\Delta\subset{\rm\bf Cat}
.

Now, consider a morphism
f:x\to x^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{X}}_{+}
. It also means that
f
belongs to
\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{X}}
, but in any case, the factorisation system
(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}},\mathscr{A}_{\mathcal{X}})
defines a functor

\bar{f}:x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\to x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}

by projecting to
\Delta

f^{*}

x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\pi x^{\prime}
and
x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\pi x

\pi(f):\pi x^{\prime}\to\pi x

by projection to
\Delta^{\sf op}
.
In all, we constructed the following diagram

{diagram}

If we note by
p_{x}:x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
and
p_{x^{\prime}}:x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}
the natural projections, then the map
\bar{f}^{*}p_{x}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\to p_{x^{\prime}}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}
(cf Proposition
1.45
) of prefibrations over
x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}}

p_{x}\bar{f}\to p_{x^{\prime}}

{\sf Sect}(x\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}},p_{x}^{*}{\mathcal{E}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{f}^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf Sect}(x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}},\bar{f}^{*}p_{x}^{*}{\mathcal{E}})\cong{\sf Sect}(x^{\prime}\backslash\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{X}},p^{*}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}})

\pi f

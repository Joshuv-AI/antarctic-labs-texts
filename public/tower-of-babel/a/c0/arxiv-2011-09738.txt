Localised time-periodic solutions of discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon systems with convex on-site potentials
Dirk Hennig

Abstract
Abstract: The existence of nonzero localised periodic solutions for general one-dimensional discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon systems with convex on-site potentials is proved. The existence problem of localised solutions is expressed in terms of a fixed point equation for an operator on some appropriate function space which is solved by means of Schauder’s Fixed Point Theorem.



Non-uniform structures in nonlinear discrete lattice systems in the form of spatially
localised and time-periodically varying states, referred to as breather solutions, have attracted considerable interest.
Rigorous results regarding proofs of existence and stability of intrinsic localised time-periodic modes (also known as breathers) were
provided in
MacKay
-
b12
. Several methods and techniques were used such as continuation of (trivially localised) solutions from the anti-integrable
of zero couplings for sufficiently weak couplings, center manifold reduction methods, Lyapunov-Schmidt decomposition and continuum approximation techniques, variational approaches, perturbative arguments for the characteristic exponents of the Floquet monodromy matrices and normal form theorems and Nekoroshev-type proofs.
With this work we present a comparatively concise existence proof for breathers
in general discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon systems with convex on-site potential.

We discuss the existence of localised time-periodic solutions of
general discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon systems on
infinite lattices given by the following set of coupled oscillator equations

\frac{d^{2}q_{n}}{dt^{2}}=\kappa\,[q_{n+1}-2q_{n}+q_{n-1}]-V^{\prime}(q_{n}),\,\,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(1)

where the
q_{n}(t)
describe the coordinate of the
n
th oscillator at time
t
and
the prime
′
in the last term on the r.h.s. of (
1
) stands for the derivative with respect to
q_{n}
.
Each oscillator interacts with its neighbours to the left and right and the
strength of the interaction
is determined by the value of the parameter
\kappa
.
This system has a Hamiltonian structure related to the energy

H=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{n}^{2}+V(q_{n})+\frac{\kappa}{2}(q_{n+1}-q_{n})^{2}\right),

(2)

and it is time-reversible with respect to the involution
p\mapsto-p
.

Assume that the following condition on the on-site potential
V(x)
holds:

A:

V:\,\,{\mathbb{R}}\,\rightarrow\,{\mathbb{R}}
is even,
V(x)=V(-x)
,
non-negative and at least twice continuously
differentiable and uniformly convex which is characterised by the following properties:
There exists two constants
0<\underline{K}<\overline{K}<\infty

such that

\underline{K}\leq V^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq\overline{K},\,\,\,\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3)

Then, one has

\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}\,x^{2}\leq V(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\overline{K}\,x^{2},\,\,\,|V^{\prime}(x)|\leq\overline{K}|x|,\,\,\,\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(4)

Further assume that

V(0)=V^{\prime}(0)=0,\,\,\,V^{\prime\prime}(0)=1.

(5)

That is, the unique equilibrium at
x=0
is a global minimum of
V(x)
and
V^{\prime}(x)
can be expressed as

V^{\prime}(x)=x+W^{\prime}(x),

(6)

with non-negative
W(x)
and
\sign(V^{\prime}(x))=\sign(W^{\prime}(x))
.
Hence,

V(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+W(x).

(7)

The solutions of the system obtained by linearising equations (
1
) around the equilibrium
q_{n}=0
are superpositions of plane wave solutions (phonons)

q_{n}(t)=\exp(i(kn-\omega t)),

(8)

with frequencies

\omega^{2}(k)=1+4\kappa\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right),\,\,\,k\in[-\pi,\pi].

(9)

These (extended) states disperse. Therefore the frequency
\Omega
of a localised time-periodic solution must satisfy the non-resonance condition
\Omega\neq|\omega(k)|/m
for any integer
m\geq 1
. This requires

\Omega^{2}>1+4\kappa
as a necessary condition for the existence of localised time-periodic solutions of system (
1
).

In the following we prove
the existence of localised periodic
solutions of system (
1
) on infinite lattices.

To this end, some appropriate function spaces are introduced on which the original
problem is presented as a fixed point problem for a corresponding operator.
Utilising Schauder’s
Fixed Point Theorem we establish the existence of localised solutions in a similar vein to the approach in
SIAM

treating the existence of travelling wave solutions in finite arrays of coupled Josephson junction systems.

With concern to the existence of localised periodic solutions to system (
1
) we state:

Theorem:
Let
A
hold. Then
there exist nonzero sequences
q\equiv\{q_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in H^{2}([0,T];l^{2})
with zero time average
\int_{0}^{T}q_{n}(t)dt=0,
that are localised time-periodic solutions of system (
1
)
with period
T=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}

provided

\Omega^{2}>1+4\kappa.

(10)

For the proof of the assertions of the Theorem the strong version of Schauder’s Fixed Point Theorem is used
Schauder
:

Let
G
be a closed convex subset of a
Banach space and
f
a continuous map of
G
into a compact subset of
G
. Then
f
has a fixed point.

Proof:
For the following considerations Eq. (
1
) is suitably re-written as:

\ddot{q}_{n}+q_{n}-\kappa[q_{n+1}-2q_{n}+q_{n-1}]=-W^{\prime}(q_{n})

(11)

Thus only the r.h.s. of (
11
) features terms nonlinear in
q
. Ultimately,
(
11
) shall be re-formulated as a fixed point equation in
q
.

We introduce appropriate function spaces.
Denote by

{X}^{0}=\left\{q\in L^{2}_{per}((0,T);{l}^{2})\,|\,\int_{0}^{T}q_{n}(t)dt=0,\,\,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}

(12)

the space of
T-
periodic square integrable
functions of time with zero time average assuming values in
l^{2}
.
X^{0}
is a closed convex subset of
L^{2}_{per}((0,T);{l}^{2})
. We introduce further the Sobolev space
X^{2}=\{q\in H^{2}_{per}((0,T);{l}^{2})\}

containing
T-
periodic
functions of time assuming values in
l^{2}
which,
together with their weak derivative
D^{\alpha\leq 2}
up to order

2
, are in
X^{0}
. The following norms are used:

\displaystyle\parallel q\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\,\parallel q(t)\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}dt=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\hat{\tilde{q}}_{k,m}\right|^{2}dk

(13)

\displaystyle\parallel q\parallel_{X^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\,\left(\parallel q(t)\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}+\parallel D{q}(t)\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}+\parallel D^{2}{q}(t)\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}\right)dt=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1+(m\Omega)^{2}+(m\Omega)^{4}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\hat{\tilde{q}}_{m,k}|^{2}dk,

(14)

where
\hat{q}_{n,k}
determines the temporal Fourier-transform:

\displaystyle q_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\hat{q}_{n,m}\exp\left(i\Omega mt\right),\,t\in[0,T],\,\,\,\,\,\,{\hat{q}}_{n,m}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}q_{n}(t)\exp(-i\Omega mt)dt,

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{n,m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{q}}_{n,-m},

(16)

and
\tilde{q}_{k}
is the spatial Fourier-transform:

\displaystyle q_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{q}_{k}(t)\exp(ikn),\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{q}_{k}(t)=\sum_{n}q_{n}(t)\exp(-i\,k\,n),

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\tilde{q}}_{-k},

(18)

of
q_{n}(t)
, respectively, and
\overline{u}
denotes the conjugate of

u
.

The Hilbert space of square-summable sequences,
{l}^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, is endowed with the standard scalar product:

\left<q,p\right>:=\sum_{n}\,q_{n}\,p_{n}\,,\qquad\parallel q\parallel^{2}_{l^{2}}:=\left<q,q\right>\,.

(19)

For later use we note that
X^{2}
is compactly embedded in
X^{0}
(
X^{2}\Subset X^{0}
).

We relate the l.h.s. of (
11
) to the
linear mapping:
M\,:\,X^{2}\,\rightarrow\,X^{0}
:

M(u_{n})=\ddot{u}_{n}+{u}_{n}-\kappa[u_{n+1}-2u_{n}+u_{n-1}].

(20)

Using the relations for the temporal and spatial Fourier-transforms (
16
),(
17
),
one obtains the representation

u_{n}(t)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\hat{\tilde{u}}}_{k,m}\exp(ikn)dk\exp(i\Omega mt).

(21)

Applying the operator
M
to the Fourier elements
\exp(ikn)\exp(i\,\Omega mt)

in (
21
), results in

M\exp(ikn)\exp(i\,\Omega mt)=\nu_{m}(k)\exp(ikn)\exp(i\,\Omega mt),

(22)

where

\nu_{m}(k)=-\Omega^{2}\,m^{2}+1+4\,\kappa\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right).

(23)

As the hypothesis (
10
) guarantees that
\nu_{m}(k)\neq 0
,
\forall\,\,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\forall\,\,k\in[0,2\pi]
, the mapping
M
possesses an inverse obeying

M^{-1}\exp(i\,\Omega lt)=(1/\nu_{l})\exp(i\,\Omega lt)
and for the norm of the linear operator
M^{-1}:\,\,X^{0}\rightarrow X^{2}
one derives using the hypothesis (
10
) the upper bound:

\displaystyle\parallel M^{-1}\parallel_{X^{0},X^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq q\in X^{0}}\frac{\parallel M^{-1}\,q\parallel_{X^{2}}}{\parallel q\parallel_{X^{0}}}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq q\in X^{0}}\frac{\left(\int_{0}^{T}\,\left[\parallel M^{-1}q(t)\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}+\parallel D{M^{-1}q(t)}\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}+\parallel D^{2}{M^{-1}q(t)}\parallel^{2}_{{l}^{2}}\right]dt\right)^{1/2}}{\parallel q\parallel_{X^{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq q\in X^{0}}\frac{\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\hat{\tilde{q}}_{k,m}}{\nu_{m}(k)}\right|^{2}dk+\sum_{m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{i\Omega m\hat{\tilde{q}}_{k,m}}{\nu_{m}(k)}\right|^{2}dk+\sum_{m^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{(i\Omega m)^{2}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{k,m}}{\nu_{m}(k)}\right|^{2}dk\right)^{1/2}}{\parallel q\parallel_{X^{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{k\in[0,2\pi]}{\frac{1}{|\nu_{m}(k)|}}\cdot\frac{\left(\sum_{m^{\prime}}\left(1+(\Omega\,m)^{2}+(\Omega\,m)^{4}\right)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\hat{\tilde{q}}_{k,m}\right|^{2}dk\right)^{1/2}}{\parallel q\parallel_{X^{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}-(1+4\,\kappa)}<\infty,

verifying boundedness of
M^{-1}
,
and we used the notation
\sum_{l^{\prime}}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}
.

Furthermore associated with the r.h.s. of (
11
) we introduce
the nonlinear operator
N\,:\,X^{0}\,\rightarrow\,X^{0}
, as

(N(u))_{n}=-W^{\prime}(u_{n}).

(25)

In order to show that the operator
N

is continuous on
X^{0}
we prove that

N
is Frechet differentiable at any
u
with bounded derivative. In detail we have

N^{\prime}(u):\,h\in X^{0}\,\mapsto N^{\prime}(u)[h]=-W^{\prime\prime}(u)h\in X^{0},

(26)

so that

\displaystyle\parallel N^{\prime}(u)[h]\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\,-W^{\prime\prime}(u_{n}(t))\,h_{n}(t)\right|^{2}\,dt

(27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|1-\,V^{\prime\prime}(u_{n}(t))|^{2}\,|h_{n}(t)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\overline{K}^{2})\,\parallel h\parallel^{2}_{X^{0}},

proving the (uniform) boundedness of the differential. Hence,

\parallel N^{\prime}(u)\parallel_{{\cal{L}}(X^{0},X^{0})}\leq\sqrt{1+\overline{K}^{2}},

(28)

viz. the derivative is bounded and for the range of
N
in
X^{0}
we get the bound

\displaystyle\parallel N(u)\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\,-W^{\prime}(u_{n}(t))|^{2}\,dt

(29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\,u_{n}(t)-V^{\prime}(u_{n}(t))|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\overline{K}^{2})\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|u_{n}(t)|^{2}\,dt\leq(1+\overline{K}^{2})\parallel u\parallel^{2}_{X^{0}}.

Finally, we express the problem (
11
)
as a fixed point equation in terms of a mapping
X^{0}\,\rightarrow\,X^{2}\Subset X^{0}
:

q=M^{-1}\circ N(q)\equiv S(q).

(30)

We verify that the range of
S
is contained in a compact subset of
X^{0}
.
Representing
N(q)\in X^{0}
in terms of its spatial and temporal Fourier-transforms as

(N(q))_{n}(t)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{\tilde{N}}_{k,m}\exp(ikn)dk\exp\left(i\Omega mt\right),

(31)

the Fourier coefficients of
M^{-1}(N(q))
fulfill
\forall q\in X^{0}
,
\forall k\in[0,2\pi],\,\,\,\forall m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}

\left|\frac{\hat{{\tilde{N}}}_{k,m}}{-\Omega^{2}\,m^{2}+1+4\,\kappa\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\hat{{\tilde{N}}}_{k,m}\right|^{2}dk}{m^{4}[\Omega^{2}-(1+4\,\kappa)]^{2}}=\frac{\parallel N(q)\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}}{m^{4}[\Omega^{2}-(1+4\,\kappa)]^{2}}.

(32)

Exploiting the coercivity of the conserved total energy
E=H
in (
2
) and the expression (
7
) one can
bound the
l^{2}
-norm of
q=\{q_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
as follows
\parallel q(t)\parallel_{l^{2}}^{2}\leq 2E
for every
t\in[0,T]
, so that

\parallel{q}\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}\leq 2E,

(33)

from which, using (
29
), we deduce

\parallel N(q)\parallel_{X^{0}}^{2}\leq 2(1+\overline{K}^{2})E.

(34)

Hence, from (
32
) and (
34
) one concludes that
S
maps
X^{0}
into the subset

Y^{0}=\left\{p=\left\{p\right\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in X^{0},\,p_{n}(t)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{{\tilde{p}}}_{k,m}\,\exp(ikn)dk\exp\left(i\Omega mt\right)\,\left|\,|\hat{{\tilde{p}}}_{k,m}|\leq\frac{M}{m^{2}}\right.\right\},

(35)

which is, for every

M=\frac{\sqrt{2(1+\overline{K}^{2})E}}{\Omega^{2}-(1+4\,\kappa)}>0

(36)

compact in
X^{0}
.

That is, the operator
S
maps closed convex subsets
X^{0}
of
L_{per}^{2}((0,T);l^{2})
into compact subsets
Y^{0}
of
X^{0}
.

Clearly
S
is continuous on
X^{0}
as the relations (
24
) and (
28
) establish that its constituents
M^{-1}
and
N
are continuous.
Schauder’s fixed point theorem
implies then that the fixed point equation
q=S(q)
has at least one solution.
Moreover, by the hypothesis (
10
) the values of the
frequency of oscillations
\Omega
lie above the upper edge
of the continuous (phonon) spectrum determined by
1+4\kappa
. Thus the corresponding solutions
have to be anharmonic which necessitates nonzero nonlinearity.
Only the latter facilitates amplitude-depending tuning of the frequency of oscillations so that the breather frequency
\Omega

lies outside of the phonon spectrum.
Thus it must hold that

\parallel N(q)\parallel_{Y^{0}}=\parallel-W^{\prime}(q)\parallel_{Y^{0}}\not\equiv 0
, which is by
A
satisfied if and only if

q\not\equiv 0
.
That is, the fixed point equation (
30
) possesses a nonzero
solution and the proof is finished.

\square

Remarks:
(i) As the obtained time-periodic
H^{2}
fixed-point-solutions are by Sobolev embeddings
C^{1}
in time and since the operator
q\mapsto W^{\prime}(q)
maps
C^{1}
into itself, one concludes from Eq. (
11
) that
\ddot{q}\in C^{1}
so that the solutions are in fact
C^{3}
in time and hence, are classical solutions.

(ii) The localised solutions on the infinite lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
are represented
by (infinite) square-summable sequences, viz. decay of the states for
|n|\rightarrow\infty
takes place in the sense of the

l^{2}
norm. This approach allows for general localised patterns (e.g. multi-site breathers).
Regarding so-called single-site breathers, their exponential decay can be directly incorporated in our existence proof by using an exponentially weighted norm instead of the standard one in (
19
) and the corresponding existence proof proceeds in the same manner as the general one above. (See also the relevant discussion in
MacKay
.)

In summary, we have proven the existence of nonzero localised periodic solutions
for general Klein-Gordon systems with convex on-site potentials on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The existence problem has been
reformulated
as a fixed point problem for an operator on a function space
which is solved with the help of Schauder’s Fixed Point Theorem.
The method employed in this paper can be straightforwardly extended to treat also the general Klein-Gordon systems
on lattices of higher dimension.

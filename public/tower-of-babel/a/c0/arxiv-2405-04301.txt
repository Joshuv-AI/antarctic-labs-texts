Classification of solutions to the isotropic horospherical p p -Minkowski problem in hyperbolic plane
Haizhong Li, Yao Wan

Abstract

[
18

Problem 1.1

(The horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem)
.

Let
p

defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}

, such that there exists a smooth uniformly h-convex bounded domain

for which its horospherical
p
-surface area measure has density
f

\displaystyle dS_{p}(K,z)=f(z)d\sigma.

That is, finding a smooth positive solution to

\displaystyle\varphi^{-p}(z)\det(A[\varphi(z)])=f(z),

such that the matrix

\displaystyle A_{ij}[\varphi(z)]=D_{j}D_{i}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|D\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\sigma_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi-\frac{1}{\varphi}\right)\sigma_{ij}>0

for all

\mathbb{S}^{n}

2.
The period function

Let
\varphi(\theta)
be a smooth positive solution to the isotropic horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem in hyperbolic plane, that is,
\varphi(\theta)
satisfies

(2.1)

\varphi^{-p}\left(\varphi_{\theta\theta}-\frac{\varphi_{\theta}^{2}}{2\varphi}+\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{-1}}{2}\right)=\gamma,

where
\gamma
is a positive constant. Due to Theorem
1.1
, we only consider the case where
p\leq-1
and
u(\tau)
is not constant from now on.

Define
u(\tau)=\sqrt{\varphi(2\tau)}
, then (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

u^{1-2p}(u_{\tau\tau}+u-u^{-3})=2\gamma.

Since
p<0
, then (
2.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(u_{\tau}^{2}+u^{2}+u^{-2})_{\tau}=\frac{2\gamma}{p}(u^{2p})_{\tau}.

Thus a first integral of (
2.2
) is given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle u_{\tau}^{2}+u^{2}+u^{-2}-\frac{2\gamma}{p}u^{2p}=E,

for some constant
E
.

Since the solution
\varphi
to (
2.1
) is a
2\pi
-periodic positive function of
\theta
, then the corresponding solution
u
to (
2.2
) is a
\pi
-periodic positive function of
\tau
, and its maximum
u_{+}
and minimum values
u_{-}
are determined by
p,\gamma
and
E
. Define a function

E_{\gamma}(u)=u^{2}+u^{-2}-\frac{2\gamma}{p}u^{2p},

then it follows from (
2.3
) that
E=E_{\gamma}(u_{\pm})
. Note that

E_{\gamma}^{\prime}(u)=2u-2u^{-3}-4\gamma u^{2p-1},

and

E_{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(u)=2+6u^{-4}-4\gamma(2p-1)u^{2p-2}>0,

thus
E_{\gamma}(u)
is convex, and decreasing for
u\in(0,u_{\gamma})
and increasing for
u\in(u_{\gamma},+\infty)
, where
u_{\gamma}
satisfies
E_{\gamma}^{\prime}(u_{\gamma})=0
. Moreover,
E_{\gamma}(u)
has the minimum value
E_{\gamma}^{*}=E_{\gamma}(u_{\gamma})
, and
E_{\gamma}(u)
tends to infinity as
u
tends to zero or infinity. Consequently, we obtain

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u_{+}>u_{-}
be the two positive roots of the equation
E_{\gamma}(u)=E
, where
E>E_{\gamma}^{*}
. Then
u_{+}
increases to infinity and
u_{-}
decreases to zero as
E
increases to infinity.

Furthermore, if
\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{2}>0
and
E>E_{\gamma_{1}}^{*},E>E_{\gamma_{2}}^{*}
, denote
u_{+}^{(i)},u_{-}^{(i)}
by the two roots of the equation
E_{\gamma_{i}}(u)=E
for
i=1,2
, then
u_{+}^{(2)}>u_{+}^{(1)}>u_{-}^{(1)}>u_{-}^{(2)}>0
.

Let
\tau_{-},\tau_{+}\in[0,\pi)
be two consecutive critical points such that
u(\tau_{\pm})=u_{\pm}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\tau_{+}>\tau_{-}
. Using (
2.3
), we have

u_{\tau}=\sqrt{E+\frac{2\gamma}{p}u^{2p}-u^{2}-u^{-2}}>0

for
\tau\in(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
. Then

(2.4)

\tau_{+}-\tau_{-}=\int_{\tau_{-}}^{\tau_{+}}d\tau=\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}\frac{du}{u_{\tau}}.

Thus we consider the period function

(2.5)

\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E):=\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}\frac{du}{\sqrt{E+\frac{2\gamma}{p}u^{2p}-u^{2}-u^{-2}}},

where
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
satisfy
E_{\gamma}(u_{-})=E_{\gamma}(u_{+})=E
.

If
u(\tau)
is a
\pi
-periodic non-constant solution to (
2.2
), then it must have an even number of critical points in
[0,\pi)
. It follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) that there exists a positive integer
m
such that
\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{2m}
. We obtain

Lemma 2.2
.

(i)

If
\frac{\pi}{4}<\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)<\frac{\pi}{2}
for any
E>E_{\gamma}^{*}
, then the solution to (
2.1
) must be constant. Moreover, the solution to (
2.1
) is unique.

(ii)

If
E>E_{\gamma}^{*}
and
\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{2m}
for some integer
m
, then there exists a
\frac{2\pi}{m}
-periodic non-constant solution to (
2.1
).

For convenience, we provide two new expressions of
\Theta_{p}
. First, using
E_{\gamma}(u_{-})=E_{\gamma}(u_{+})=E
, we get

(2.6)

-\frac{2\gamma}{p}=\frac{(u_{+}^{2}-u_{-}^{2})(u_{+}^{-2}u_{-}^{-2}-1)}{u_{+}^{2p}-u_{-}^{2p}},

and

(2.7)

E=\frac{u_{+}^{2p}(u_{-}^{2}+u_{-}^{-2})-u_{-}^{2p}(u_{+}^{2}+u_{+}^{-2})}{u_{+}^{2p}-u_{-}^{2p}}.

It follows from
p\leq-1
and (
2.6
) that
\gamma>0
if and only if
u_{+}u_{-}>1
. We can then consider
\Theta_{p}
as a function of
u_{-}
and
u_{+}

(2.8)

\Theta_{p}[u_{-},u_{+}]:=\Theta_{p}(\gamma(u_{-},u_{+}),E(u_{-},u_{+}))=\int_{u_{-}}^{u_{+}}\frac{du}{\sqrt{G_{p}(u,u_{-},u_{+})}},

where

(2.9)

\begin{split}G_{p}(u,u_{-},u_{+})=\frac{u_{-}^{2p}-u^{2p}}{u_{-}^{2p}-u_{+}^{2p}}(u_{+}^{2}+u_{+}^{-2})+\frac{u^{2p}-u_{+}^{2p}}{u_{-}^{2p}-u_{+}^{2p}}(u_{-}^{2}+u_{-}^{-2})-u^{2}-u^{-2}\end{split}

for
(u_{-},u_{+})
satisfying
u_{+}>u_{-}>0
and
u_{+}u_{-}>1
.

Next, let
r=\frac{u_{+}}{u_{-}}\in(1,\infty)
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{u_{+}u_{-}}\in(0,1)
, and make the change of variable
s=\frac{u}{u_{-}}
. We define

(2.10)

\Theta_{p}\{\alpha,r\}:=\Theta_{p}[\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}r^{-\frac{1}{2}},\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}]=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F_{p}(s,\alpha,r)}},

where

(2.11)

\begin{split}F_{p}(s,\alpha,r)&=\frac{1-s^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}(r^{2}+\alpha^{2})+\frac{s^{2p}-r^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}(1+\alpha^{2}r^{2})-s^{2}-\alpha^{2}r^{2}s^{-2}\\
&=\frac{1-s^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}(r^{2}-1)-(s^{2}-1)-\alpha^{2}\left[\frac{1-s^{2p}}{1-r^{2p}}(r^{2}-1)-r^{2}(1-s^{-2})\right]\end{split}

for
(\alpha,r)
satisfying
0<\alpha<1<r
.

In the following sections, we will study the asymptotic behavior and monotonicity of the period function
\Theta_{p}
.

3.
Special values and asymptotic behaviors

Let
p\leq-1
. Denote
D_{1}=\{(\gamma,E):\ \gamma>0,\ E>E_{\gamma}^{*}\}
and
D_{2}=\{(\alpha,r):\ 0<\alpha<1<r\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\Theta_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\Theta_{p}\{\cdot,\cdot\}
are continuous on
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, respectively.
\Theta_{p}
is continuous with respect to
p
, and has following limiting values:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\to-\infty}\Theta_{p}\{\alpha,r\}=\arccos\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}-\alpha^{2}}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 1}\Theta_{p}\{\alpha,r\}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{(2-2p)+(2+2p)\alpha^{2}}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to+\infty}\Theta_{p}\{\alpha,r\}=\frac{\pi}{2},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\gamma\to 0}\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{E\to+\infty}\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{2},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{E\to(E_{\gamma}^{*})^{+}}\Theta_{p}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{(2-2p)+(2+2p)u_{\gamma}^{-4}}},

where
u_{\gamma}\in(1,+\infty)
is given by

(3.7)

u_{\gamma}^{2-2p}-u_{\gamma}^{-2-2p}=2\gamma.

Moreover, we also have

(3.8)

\Theta_{-1}(\gamma,E)=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\quad(\gamma,E)\in D_{1},

(3.9)

\Theta_{p}\{1,r\}=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\quad\quad\quad r>1.

The following lemma is needed in the proof of (
3.6
), see
[
17
, Corollary 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\delta
be a positive real number and let
f:(u_{0}-\delta,u_{0}+\delta)\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that
f(u_{0})=f^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})=-2a<0
. If for any small
c>0
,
u_{1}(c)<u_{0}<u_{2}(c)
are such that
f(u_{1}(c))+c=0=f(u_{2}(c))+c
, then

\lim\limits_{c\to 0^{+}}\int_{u_{1}(c)}^{u_{2}(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{f(u)+c}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a}}.

For the sake of completeness, let us give a proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

For any
b>a
, we define the function
h(u)=f^{\prime}(u)+2b(u-u_{0})
. Since
h^{\prime}(u_{0})=2(b-a)>0
, there exists a
\epsilon(b)\in(0,\delta)
such that
h^{\prime}(u)>0
for any
u\in[u_{0},u_{0}+\epsilon]
. Then it follows from
h(u_{0})=0
that
h(u)>0
for
u\in(u_{0},u_{0}+\epsilon]
.

For any small
c>0
such that
u_{2}(c)<u_{0}+\epsilon
, the function

g(u)=f(u)+c+b(u-u_{2}(c))(u+u_{2}(c)-2u_{0})

satisfies
g(u_{2}(c))=0
and
g^{\prime}(u)=h(u)>0
. Thus
g(u)<0
for
u\in[u_{0},u_{2}(c))
. We get

0<f(u)+c<b(u_{2}(c)-u)(u+u_{2}(c)-2u_{0}),\quad\text{for any}\ u\in(u_{0},u_{2}(c))

and then

\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{b}}=\int_{u_{0}}^{u_{2}(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{b(u_{2}(c)-u)(u+u_{2}(c)-2u_{0})}}<\int_{u_{0}}^{u_{2}(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{f(u)+c}}.

On the other hand, for any
0<b<a
and small
c>0
, the same argument shows

\int_{u_{0}}^{u_{2}(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{f(u)+c}}<\int_{u_{0}}^{u_{2}(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{b(u_{2}(c)-u)(u+u_{2}(c)-2u_{0})}}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{b}}.

Therefore, we obtain

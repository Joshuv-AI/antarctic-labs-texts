Šoltés’ hypergraphs
Stijn Cambie

Abstract
More than 30 30 years ago, Šoltés observed that the total distance of the graph C 11 C_{11} does not change by deleting a vertex, and wondered about the existence of other such graphs, called Šoltés graphs. We extend the definition of Šoltés’ graphs to Šoltés’ hypergraphs, determine all orders for which a Šoltés’ hypergraph exists, observe infinitely many uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs, and find the Šoltés’ hypergraph with minimum size (spoiler: it is not C 11 C_{11} ).



1
Introduction

The total distance, which is proportional with the average distance, is one of the most important distance-based graph parameters. For a graph
G
, we denote the total distance with
W(G)
. Here
W(G)=\sum_{u,v\in V(G)}d(u,v)
is the sum of distances between any (unordered) pair of vertices in a graph
G
.
We will examine one of the most easy-to-state questions related to the total distance.

In 1991, Šoltés
[
7
]
observed that

W(C_{11})=W(C_{11}\setminus u)
for every vertex
u\in V(C_{11})
,
and asked whether there are other graphs
G
(nowadays called Šoltés’ graphs) satisfying
W(G)=W(G\setminus u)
for all vertices
u
of
G
.
It has been conjectured that
C_{11}
is the only Šoltés’ graph (
[
3
, conj. 51]
).

One can generalize the notion of Šoltés’ graphs to Šoltés’ hypergraphs.
A hypergraph
H=(V,E)
, with
E\subset 2^{V}
, is a pair consisting of a set of vertices and a set of edges, where an edge is a set that can contain any number of vertices (we will assume at least
2
, as edges of size
1
do not matter in computing the total distance).
More precise terminology is given in Subsection
1.1
.
A Šoltés’ hypergraph is a connected hypergraph
H
for which
W(H)=W(H\setminus v)
(which needs to be finite) for every
v\in V(H).

Here
W(H)
is the sum of distances over all pairs of vertices, where the distance
d(u,v)
is the minimum number of edges in a connected subhypergraph of
H
containing both
u
and
v
.
By convention,
K_{1}
is not considered as a Šoltés’ hypergraph.
In
Section

2
, we prove that there exists a Soltés’ hypergraph of order
n
if and only if
n\geq 5
. The latter trivially implies that there are infinitly many Soltés’ hypergraphs.

In
Section

3
, we consider the problem for uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs. Those are hypergraphs for which all edges have the same cardinality (graphs are
2
-uniform hypergraphs).
We give two infinite examples of families of uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs,
indicating the abundance of solutions.
By the discovery of a few examples of uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs which have larger diameter (arguably, they behave like graphs in this context), the original version of Šoltés’ problem may expected to be hard and one can doubt that
C_{11}
might be the only Šoltés’ graph.

There still remain natural subproblems, such as determining the smallest uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs.
We find two uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs of order
10
(hence
C_{11}
is not the smallest uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph); the hemi-dodecahedron (see
Fig.

4
) and the dual of
K_{5}^{(3)}
(see
Fig.

5
). We prove that the latter is the unique Soltés’ hypergraph of minimum size.

1.1
Notation and Terminology

A hypergraph
H=(V,E)
, where
E\subset 2^{V}
, consists of a set of vertices and a set of hyperedges. A hyperedge is defined as a set containing at least two vertices
1
1

1

Edges with a single vertex would not influence the total distance.
and called an edge when no confusion can arise. The number of vertices and edges are called the order and size respectively.
For a vertex
v\in V
,
H\setminus v
denotes the hypergraph
(V\setminus v,E^{\prime})
, where
E^{\prime}
includes all hyperedges in
E
not containing
v
. In essence,
H\setminus v
is obtained by removing
v
and all hyperedges containing
v
.
The dual
H^{\star}=(E,V^{\star})
of a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
, is the hypergraph whose vertices are the edges of
H
, and whose hyperedges correspond with the tuples of all edges incident to a vertex of
V.
A clique
K_{n}^{(r)}
is the
r
-uniform hypergraph with a vertex set of order
n
, containing all possible hyperedges with
r
elements.
So up to isomorphism,
K_{n}^{r}=\left([n],\binom{[n]}{r}\right)
where
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.

The degree of a vertex
v
, denoted by
\deg(v)
, is the number of hyperedges containing
v
. The minimum and maximum degrees, denoted as
\delta
and
\Delta
, represent the smallest and largest degrees in the hypergraph.

A path in
H
is represented by a sequence
(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},v_{2},\ldots,e_{r},v_{r})
, where each
e_{i}
is a hyperedge containing vertices
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i}
for every
i
,
v_{0}=u
, and
v_{r}=v
. No edge or vertex appears multiple times in the path, and
r
denotes the length of the path.
The distance
d(u,v)
between two vertices
u,v
in a hypergraph
H
is defined as the length of the shortest path between them.

For a hypergraph
H
, the total distance or Wiener index,
W(H)
, is the sum of distances between any pair of vertices in
H
, i.e.,
W(H)=\sum_{u,v\in V(H)}d(u,v)
. Equivalently,
W(H)
equals the total distance
W(G^{\prime})
of the underlying graph
G^{\prime}=(V,E^{\prime})
, where
E^{\prime}=\large\cup\{\binom{e}{2}\mid e\in E\}
comprises all
2
-uniform edges that are subsets of a hyperedge in
E
.

Next, we generalize the folklore result on the extrema of the Wiener index for graphs of a given order. This will be used afterwards to prove that no Šoltés’ hypergraph can have order bounded by

Proposition 2
.

For a connected hypergraph
H
on
n
vertices, we have

\binom{n}{2}=W(K_{n})\leq W(H)\leq W(P_{n})=\binom{n+1}{3}.

The upper bound is strict if
H\not=P_{n}
, while there are many hypergraphs attaining the minimum.

Proof.

The lower bound is trivial, since all distances are at least one.
The upper bound is by induction.
Let
S
be a minimum set of (twin-)vertices such that
H\setminus S
(remaining hypergraph after removing
S
and edges incident with
S
) is connected. The existence of such a set can be concluded by starting from a vertex and adding overlapping edges one at a time.
Let
\lvert S\rvert=s.

Then by induction, we have that

\displaystyle W(H)

\displaystyle\leq H(H\setminus S)+s\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n-s}i

\displaystyle=\binom{n-s+1}{3}+s\binom{n-s+1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n-s+1}{3}+\sum_{i=n-s+1}^{n}\binom{i}{2}=\binom{n+1}{3}.

For equality to be possible, it is necessary that
s=1
and
H\setminus S=P_{n-1}
(by induction). From here one easily concludes that
H=P_{n}
, since the distances between the vertex
S
and the vertices in
P_{n-1}
have to be exactly
1
up to
n-1.

∎

3
Uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs

In this section, we prove that there are (infinitely many) uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs. We do so by constructing two infinite families; one with increasing uniformity and one with fixed uniformity.

Proposition 4
.

There are infinitely many values of
k
for which there exists a
k
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph.

Proof.

Let
m\geq 4,

k=\binom{m}{2}
and
n=\binom{m+1}{2}+1.

Let
H
be the hypergraph with
V=[n]
and
E
be the edges consisting of
k
consecutive (modulo
n
) numbers in
[n]
.
Then
H
is vertex-transitive and
W(H)=\binom{n}{2}
, as
diam(H)=1
due to
2k-1\geq n
). For any
v\in V
,

W(H\setminus v)=\binom{n-1}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}i=\binom{n}{2}.

The latter since
H\setminus v
has diameter
2
and it is equal to a path for which the first
m
vertices have resp.
m,m-1\ldots,1
non-neighbours in
V\setminus v.

∎

Proposition 5
.

There are infinitely many
4
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs.

Proof.

Consider a Steiner
2
-design
H=S(2,4,n)
with block size
4
, for some
n\equiv 1,4\pmod{12}
and
n\geq 13
. They exist for all such
n
, and there are many of them for large
n
, see
[
6
]
.

Every
2
vertices share exactly one edge, thus
diam(H)=1
. If
u,w
are two vertices such that
\{u,v,w\}
are part of an edge
e
in
H
, then for a vertex
z\not\in e
, there are two disjoint edges (in
G\setminus v
which contain
\{z,u\}
and
\{z,w\}
.
This implies that for every vertex
v\in V(H),

W(H\setminus v)=\binom{n-1}{2}+\frac{n-1}{3}\binom{3}{2}=\binom{n-1}{2}+(n-1)=\binom{n}{2}=W(H).

Thus
H
is indeed a Šoltés’ hypergraph, which has diameter
1
and is
4
-uniform.
∎

Proposition 6
.

There are no
3
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs with diameter
1.

Proof.

Let
H
be a
3
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph with diameter
1.

We associate with every pair of vertices
(v_{i},v_{j})
in
\binom{V}{2},
all vertices
v^{\prime}\in V\setminus\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
for which
\{v_{i},v_{j},v^{\prime}\}\in E.

For every vertex
v^{\prime}
, count the number
x_{v^{\prime}}
of pairs associated with
v^{\prime}
and no other vertex.
Since there are
\binom{n}{2}
pairs in total, and
n
vertices, there is a vertex

for which this number is bounded by

Then it is easy to see that

has diameter
2
as follows. For every
2

which are not adjacent in

there are at most

So they have at least

(we only need to consider

Since

we conclude that

, contradicting the choice of

∎

Theorem 10
.

The dual of
K_{5}^{(3)}
is the unique uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph of minimum size.

Proof.

Note that a Šoltés’ hypergraph has minimum degree at least
2
, since if not, a vertex
v
of degree
1
has a neighbour whose removal disconnects
v
from the remaining vertices.
Also the size
m
and maximum degree satisfy
m\geq\Delta+2,
as otherwise removing a vertex of maximum degree leaves either a disconnected hypergraph, or a clique (with one hyperedge).

Now
2\leq\delta\leq\Delta\leq m-2
implies that
m\geq 4.

We first exclude
m=4.

Claim 11
.

A Šoltés’ hypergraph cannot have size
4
or less.

Proof.

In this case,
\Delta=\delta=2
and by
Theorem

3

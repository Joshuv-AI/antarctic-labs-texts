Random Reed-Solomon Codes and Random Linear Codes are Locally Equivalent
Matan Levi, Jonathan Mosheiff, Nikhil Shagrithaya

Abstract
We establish an equivalence between two important random ensembles of linear codes: random linear codes (RLCs) and random Reed-Solomon (RS) codes. Specifically, we show that these models exhibit identical behavior with respect to key combinatorial properties—such as list-decodability and list-recoverability—when the alphabet size is sufficiently large. We introduce monotone-decreasing local coordinate-wise linear (LCL) properties, a new class of properties tailored for the large alphabet regime. This class encompasses list-decodability, list-recoverability, and their average-weight variants. We develop a framework for analyzing these properties and prove a threshold theorem for RLCs: for any LCL property 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , there exists a threshold rate R 𝒫 R_{\mathcal{P}} such that RLCs are likely to satisfy 𝒫 \mathcal{P} when R < R 𝒫 R<R_{\mathcal{P}} and unlikely to do so when R > R 𝒫 R>R_{\mathcal{P}} . We extend this threshold theorem to random RS codes and show that they share the same threshold R 𝒫 R_{\mathcal{P}} , thereby establishing the equivalence between the two ensembles and enabling a unified analysis of list-recoverability and related properties. Applying our framework, we compute the threshold rate for list-decodability, proving that both random RS codes and RLCs achieve the generalized Singleton bound. This recovers a recent result of Alrabiah, Guruswami, and Li (2023) via elementary methods. Additionally, we prove an upper bound on the list-recoverability threshold and conjecture that this bound is tight. Our approach suggests a plausible pathway for proving this conjecture and thereby pinpointing the list-recoverability parameters of both models. Indeed, following the release of a prior version of this paper, Li and Shagrithaya (2025) used our equivalence theorem to show that random RS codes are near-optimally list-recoverable.

1.2
Local Code Properties and the LCL Framework

Our notion of LCL properties extends a general framework, initially introduced in
[
28
]
, for studying list-decodability and list-recoverability as part of a broader class of code properties. A set of words in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
that all lie within the same Hamming ball of radius
\rho n
is called
\rho\text{-clustered}
. A set of words
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is said to be
\left(\rho,\ell\right)\text{-recovery-clustered}
if there exists a sequence of sets
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
, each of size at most
\ell
, such that for every
x\in X
, the condition
\left|\{i\in[n]\mid x_{i}\notin Z_{i}\}\right|\leq\rho n
holds. Thus, a code
\mathcal{C}
is
\left(\rho,L\right)\text{-list-decodable}
(or
\left(\rho,\ell,L\right)\text{-list-recoverable}
) if and only if it does
not
contain a
\rho\text{-clustered}
(or
\left(\rho,\ell\right)\text{-recovery-clustered}
) set of size
L+1
. We interpret a clustered (or recovery-clustered) set of codewords in
\mathcal{C}
as a
witness
to the code’s non-list-decodability (or non-list-recoverability). This leads to the observation that list-decodability and list-recoverability have short witnesses, drawing a natural analogy to
co-NP
languages or
\Pi_{1}
logical formulas.

In
[
28
]
, this viewpoint was used to study list-decodability and list-recoverability within a newly defined class of
(monotone-decreasing) local code properties
. A property is said to be
local
if it has short witnesses, like clustered sets for list-decodability. The main finding of
[
28
]
is that
Gallagher codes
, a random ensemble of LDPC codes, possess local properties similar to those of RLCs, meaning they are just as list-decodable and list-recoverable (with high probability). Later,
[
20
]
extended this framework to show that
randomly punctured low-bias codes
also share local properties with RLCs. These results can be seen as
reductions
between code ensembles.
5
5

5

Note that
[
28
,
20
]
only prove one-sided reductions, transferring monotone-decreasing local properties from RLCs to other ensembles. In contrast, the reduction in the present paper goes both ways.

The reduction results in
[
28
]
and
[
20
]
stem from the
threshold theorem for local properties for RLCs

[
28
, Theorem 2.8]
. This theorem states that for any local property
\mathcal{P}
, there is a threshold rate
R_{\mathcal{P}}
such that RLCs with a rate below
R_{\mathcal{P}}
are likely to satisfy
\mathcal{P}
, whereas those with a rate above
R_{\mathcal{P}}
are unlikely to do so. An analogous theorem for plain random codes is proven in
[
19
]
.

The threshold theorem crucially relies on the property
\mathcal{P}
having short witnesses. For instance, if
\mathcal{P}
is the property of
(\rho,L)
-list-decodability, the witnesses are
\rho\text{-clustered}
sets of size
L+1
. To apply the theorem, subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}
(where
b=L+1
) are classified into types, and the theorem uses the expected number of these witnesses in a code
\mathcal{C}
to determine whether
\mathcal{C}
is likely to satisfy
\mathcal{P}
. However, the number of types grows exponentially in
q^{b}
, which is manageable for fixed
q
but becomes unwieldy when
q
increases with
n
. For a more formal treatment of this technique, see
[
29
]
.

This exponential growth in the number of types motivates the need for a refined approach. Our framework of
LCL properties
avoids explicitly enumerating types and can handle cases where
q
is large, often keeping the complexity independent of
q
(see
Remark

3.3
).

Notably, random RS codes arise as random puncturings of the
full RS code

\mathsf{RS}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q};k\right)
, which is known to have low bias, suggesting that the results of
[
20
]
could apply to their analysis. However, the methods developed in
[
28
,
20
]
are tailored to codes over relatively small alphabets and do not extend to RS codes, where
q\geq n
is required. Our LCL framework fills this gap by extending the local properties approach to the large-alphabet regime.

Informally, a property
\mathcal{P}
is
b
-LCL if it is witnessed by a small set of codewords
x^{1},\dots,x^{b}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
such that, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the vector
(x^{1}_{i},\dots,x^{b}_{i})
satisfies certain linear constraints. For example,
\rho
-clusteredness is characterized by a large number of equality constraints (which are, in particular, linear) among
x^{1}_{i},\dots,x^{b}_{i}
for many coordinates
1\leq i\leq n
. We provide a formal treatment in
Section

2.3
.

As mentioned, the LCL framework encompasses list-decodability and list-recoverability, along with their average-weight variants. It also naturally captures other properties, such as average pairwise distance (defined in
[
8
]
) and list-decodability from burst errors (e.g.,
[
30
]
) under a unified lens.

1.4
Open Problems

1.4.1
List-Recovery and Non-Local Properties

Determining the threshold for list-recovery in the large
q
regime remains a significant open problem, as it would reveal the list-recovery parameters of both random RS codes and RLCs. As noted earlier, we conjecture this threshold to be
\left(1-\frac{1}{\log_{\ell}(L+1)}\right)\left(1-\frac{L+1}{L+1-\ell}\cdot\rho\right)
.

Another challenge arises in the small
q
regime, such as when
q=\mathrm{poly}(\ell)
. Our current methods cannot directly address list-recoverability in this setting due to the exponential dependence of
q
on locality. Relaxing this dependence is an important goal, as it could deepen our understanding of list-recovery in this regime.

Furthermore, extending our framework to analyze highly non-local properties would be valuable. Consider the following example: fix a large
q
and let
0<R<1
,
q^{(1-R)}<\ell<q
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\mathcal{P}
represent the property of
\left(0,\ell,L\right)
-list-recoverability for codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
, where
L=\ell^{n}\cdot q^{-(1-R)n}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)
. Note that
\mathcal{P}
is not a local property, since
L
is typically exponential in
n
, but it is a natural one to consider.

Notice that
\ell^{n}\cdot q^{-(1-R)n}
is the expected size of the set
\mathcal{C}\cap Z_{1}\times Z_{2}\times\dots\times Z_{n}
, where
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
is a fixed code of rate
(1-R)
, and
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}
are independent uniformly random subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of size
\ell
. Thus, a code
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}
if its maximum intersection with any product set
Z_{1}\times Z_{2}\times\dots\times Z_{n}
is at most
(1+\varepsilon)
times this expectation. From a pseudorandomness perspective, a code satisfying
\mathcal{P}
can be said to
fool product sets (also known as combinatorial rectangles)
within a
(1+\varepsilon)
factor.

Consider the question of whether any linear code satisfies this property. In particular, do RLCs satisfy it? Both questions were essentially answered in the affirmative by
[
27
]
when
R>\frac{1}{2}
and
q
is a large prime. For
R\leq\frac{1}{2}
, these questions remain open. We conjecture that the answer is again positive, provided that
q
is a sufficiently large prime.

1.4.2
An Alphabet-Uniform Framework

As discussed in

Currently, RLCs are shown to achieve the Elias bound for list-decodability in two cases: when

1.4.3

In the large

Conjecture 1.1

(Optimality of RLCs and random RS codes for LCL properties)

For every
\varepsilon>0

and

Following the original publication of this paper,

2.2
Average-Weight List-Decodability and Average-Weight List-Recoverability

As mentioned in

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a set of words. If there exists some

, we say that

\left(\rho,\ell\right)\text{-average-weight-recovery-clustered}
.

A code is said to be
\left(\rho,L\right)\text{-average-weight list-decodable}
if it does not contain a
\rho\text{-average-weight-clustered}

. A code is said to be
\left(\rho,\ell,L\right)\text{-average-weight list-recoverable}
if it does not contain a
\left(\rho,\ell\right)\text{-average-weight-recovery-clustered}
set of size larger than
L
.

Note that the average-weight variants are stronger than the plain versions of these properties. Specifically, if a code is
\left(\rho,L\right)\text{-average-weight list-decodable}
, it is also
\left(\rho,L\right)\text{-list-decodable}

The notion of

[

(see also
[
16

The fundamental observation underlying these works is that any code that is not list-decodable must contain a subset of codewords that exhibit significant agreement with some fixed vector in the ambient space. Equivalently, these codewords must agree with one another on a substantial number of coordinates.
[

leveraged this insight by representing codewords as columns of a matrix, where coordinate-wise agreements among codewords manifest as linear constraints on the matrix rows. (Reduced) Intersection Matrices serve as symbolic matrices that encode such agreement constraints. The concept of a

This generalization plays a crucial role in establishing the threshold theorem for RLCs and in demonstrating the equivalence between RLCs and random RS codes.

As shown by the following proposition, this framework allows us to capture natural code properties such as list-decodability and list-recoverability.

4
LCL Properties of Random Linear Codes

The following is a basic property of an RLC.

Lemma 4.1

(Probability that an RLC contains a set)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
be an RLC of rate
R
and let
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times b}
. Then

\mathrm{Pr}\left[A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]=q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\operatorname{rank}A}

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{C}=\ker P
where
P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{(1-R)n\times n}
is uniformly random. Now,

\mathrm{Pr}\left[A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]=\mathrm{Pr}\left[\forall i\in[(1-R)n]~~{P}_{i*}\cdot A=0\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{(1-R)n}\mathrm{Pr}\left[{P}_{i*}\cdot A=0\right]=q^{-(1-R)\cdot n\cdot\operatorname{rank}A}\eperiod

∎

We are interested in the probability that an RLC
\mathcal{C}
of rate
R
satisfies a given
b
-LCL property
\mathcal{P}
. In particular, we would like to estimate
\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }\mathcal{V}\right]
for a given linear profile
\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)^{n}
. As a first-order estimate, it makes sense to first compute the expectation

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}^{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\mid A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}\right|\right]=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}^{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}}\mathrm{Pr}\left[A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}^{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}}q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\operatorname{rank}A}\ecomma

(5)

where the last transition is due to
Lemma

4.1
. As we shall see, to understand the likelihood of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\mathcal{V}
we will need a more nuanced expectation argument, in which we classify the matrices in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}
according to their row span. For each
U\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)
we define

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{row-span}}(A)=U\right\}.

If there exists
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}
such that
A\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
contains
(\mathcal{V},U)
.
Let

\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)=\left\{U\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\mid\forall 1\leq i<j\leq b\>\>\exists x\in U\textrm{ such that }x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right\}

and note that a matrix
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}^{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{row-span}}(A)\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)
. Hence,

\max_{U\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)}\mathrm{Pr}\left[\exists A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U},~A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }\mathcal{V}\right]\leq\sum_{U\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)}\mathrm{Pr}\left[\exists A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U},~A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]\eperiod

Observe that the left-hand and right-hand sides differ by a factor of at most
\left|\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\right|\leq q^{b^{2}}
. For constant
b
, this is merely polynomial in
q
and will thus end up being negligible.
We therefore turn to estimating the probability that
\mathcal{C}
contains
(\mathcal{V},U)
for a given fixed
U\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)
. Analogously to
Eq.

5
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\mid A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}\right|\right]=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}}q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\operatorname{rank}A}=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}}q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U}=\left|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\right|\cdot q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U}\eperiod

(6)

To bound the right-hand side we need to bound the term
\left|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\right|
. To this end, we define

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}}\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{row-span}}(A)\subseteq U\right\}

and note that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}
. Notice that even when

U\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)
, the set
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}
will also include some matrices with non-distinct columns, in contrast to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}
. In particular, observe that the all-zero matrix is always an element of
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}
.

The cardinality of
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}
is easier to compute than that of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}
since the former is a linear subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times b}
. Indeed, a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times b}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}
if and only if
{A}_{i*}\in\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U
for all
i\in[n]
. Hence,

\dim\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U\right)\eperiod

(7)

Defining the potential function
11
11

11

Although
n
does not appear explicitly as a parameter to
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)
, it is given implicitly as the length of the profile
\mathcal{V}
. Thus,
Eq.

8
is indeed a proper definition.

\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U\right)-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U\ecomma

(8)

we conclude from
Eq.

6
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\mid A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\right|\cdot q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U}\leq\left|\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}\right|\cdot q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U}

\displaystyle=q^{\dim\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U}}\cdot q^{-(1-R)n\cdot\dim U}

\displaystyle=q^{\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)}\eperiod

(9)

When
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)\geq\Omega(n)
we shall say that the potential is
large
, whereas a potential satisfying
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)\leq-\Omega(n)
will be called
small
. In our informal discussion we will ignore the middle case in which a potential is neither large nor small. To justify this dichotomy, observe that a potential is “almost always” either large or small. Namely, if
-\omega(n)\leq\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)\leq\omega(n)
then for any fixed
\varepsilon
we have
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R+\varepsilon)=\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)+\varepsilon n\cdot\dim U\geq\Omega(n)
and
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R-\varepsilon)=\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)-\varepsilon n\cdot\dim U\leq-\Omega(n)
.

Eq.

9
yields an immediate upper bound on the probability that
\mathcal{C}
contains
(\mathcal{V},U)
. Namely, Markov’s bound yields

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\exists A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U},~A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right]=\mathrm{Pr}\left[\left|\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\mid A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}\right|\geq 1\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\{A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},U}\mid A\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}\right|\right]\leq q^{\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)}\eperiod

(10)

Hence,
\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)
being large is a necessary condition for
\mathcal{C}
to be likely to contain
(\mathcal{V},U)
. One immediately wonders whether this is also a sufficient condition. The answer turns out to be no. In the following example,
\mathcal{C}
is very unlikely to contain
(\mathcal{V},U)
despite
\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)
being large.

Example 4.2
.

Let
b=2
and
U={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Define
\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\right)^{n}
by

\mathcal{V}_{i}=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}\\
\left\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\mid x_{1}=x_{2}\right\}&\text{if }\frac{n}{2}\leq i\leq n\eperiod\end{cases}

Then,

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2},R\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\right)-(1-R)n\cdot\dim{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\right)-2(1-R)n

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\cdot 2+\frac{n}{2}\cdot 1-2(1-R)n=n\cdot\left(2R-\frac{1}{2}\right)\eperiod

In particular, taking
R=\frac{1}{3}

\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2},R\right)=\frac{n}{6}\geq\Omega(n)
, which is large.

We claim that, in spite of the above, an RLC
\mathcal{C}
of rate
\frac{1}{3}
is very unlikely to contain
(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2})
. Indeed, suppose that
\mathcal{C}
contains a matrix
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}}
. Let
A^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times 1}
consist of the difference between the two columns of
A
. Observe that
A^{\prime}
must belong to the set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1}}
, where
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1}\right)^{n}
is given by

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{i}=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{F}}_{q}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}\\
\{0\}&\text{if }\frac{n}{2}\leq i\leq n\eperiod\end{cases}

Indeed, since
A
satisfies
\mathcal{V}
, the matrix
A^{\prime}
must satisfy
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, and since
\operatorname{\mathsf{row-span}}(A)={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, it must hold that
\operatorname{\mathsf{row-span}}(A^{\prime})={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Thus, to contain
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2},\mathcal{V})
, the code
\mathcal{C}
must also contain
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1},\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
. However,
Eq.

10
bounds that probability of the latter event by
q^{\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1},R\right)}
, where

\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1},R\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim\left(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{i}\right)-(1-R)n=\frac{n}{2}-(1-R)n=n\cdot\left(R-\frac{1}{2}\right)\eperiod

Taking
R=\frac{1}{3}
yields

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2})\right]\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1})\right]\leq q^{\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1},\frac{1}{3}\right)}=q^{-\frac{n}{6}}\leq q^{-\Omega(n)}\eperiod

In
Example

4.2
, the expectation bound based on
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)
is not tight because, informally, the constraints presented by
\mathcal{V}
are
skewed
towards a certain part of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
(in this case, the difference between the two coordinates). Thus, a tighter bound is obtained by considering the potential
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},U^{\prime},R)
(here
U^{\prime}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{1}
), which we informally think of as
implied
by
(\mathcal{V},U)
. As we show in the following
threshold proposition
, considering
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U)
as well as
\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},U^{\prime})
for all
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},U^{\prime})
implied by
(\mathcal{V},U)
, yields a necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{C}
to contain
(\mathcal{V},U)
with high probability.

Proposition 4.3

(RLC Thresholds for linear profiles)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
q
be a prime power (which may depend on
n
). Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
be an RLC of rate
R\in[0,1]
.

Fix
b\in\mathbb{N}
and
U\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\setminus\left\{\left\{0\right\}\right\}
and let
\mathcal{V}\in\left(\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\right)^{n}
. Let

M=\min\left\{\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)-\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},W,R\right)\mid W\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\textrm{ and }W\subsetneq U\right\}\eperiod

The following then holds.

1.

If
M<0
then
\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},U)\right]\leq q^{M}

2.

If
M>0

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},U)\right]\geq 1-q^{-M+b^{2}}
.

Before proving
Proposition

4.3

\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},\left\{0\right\},R\right)=0
for every
\mathcal{V}
and

\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)=\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},U,R\right)-\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},\left\{0\right\},R\right)>0
. As we have already seen in

9

\mathcal{C}
to be likely to contain
(\mathcal{V},U)
.

Let us now revisit
Example

4.2

Proposition

4.3
. In that example, consider the vector space
W=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\mid x_{1}=x_{2}\right\}
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},W,R\right)=Rn
, which is larger than
\mathsf{\Phi}\left(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2},R\right)=\left(2R-\frac{1}{2}\right)n

Proposition

4.3
thus implies that an RLC of rate

is unlikely to contain
\left(\mathcal{V},{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\right)
. In

4.2
we reached a similar conclusion by projecting the rows of a matrix

is exactly

. The proof of the first part of

4.3
generalizes this method. The second part of the proposition says that this method is essentially tight.

We refer to

4.3

since, given
U\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\setminus\left\{\left\{0\right\}\right\}

\mathcal{V}\in\left(\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\right)^{n}

(\mathcal{V},U)

(\mathcal{V},U)
. The threshold rate is given by

R_{\mathcal{V},U}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\\
W\subsetneq U\end{subarray}}\left\{1-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U\right)-\dim\left(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap W\right)\right)}{n\cdot\left(\dim U-\dim W\right)}\right\}\eperiod

(11)

Indeed, one can verify that the term

. In other words,

R_{\mathcal{V},U}=\min\left\{R\in[0,1]\mid\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)\geq\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},W,R)~\textrm{ for every linear subspace }W\subseteq U\right\}\eperiod

(12)

Recall that a code
\mathcal{C}
is said to

(13)

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a

-LCL property whose associated profile family is

\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}

R_{\mathcal{P}}:=\min_{\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{F}}\left\{R_{\mathcal{V}}\right\}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{F}\\
U\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\end{subarray}}\left\{R_{\mathcal{V},U}\right\}\eperiod

Theorem 4.4

(More detailed version of

3.1
)

\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)^{n}

\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}

In particular, if

\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle M:=\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)-\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},W,R)=g-(1-R)n\cdot d\geq g-(1-R_{\mathcal{V},U}-\varepsilon)n\cdot d\geq\varepsilon nd\geq\varepsilon n

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ satisfies }\mathcal{P}\right]\geq\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},U)\right]\geq 1-q^{-M+b^{2}}\geq 1-q^{-\varepsilon n+b^{2}}\eperiod

\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{F}
and

.
Hence,

M_{\mathcal{V},U}\leq\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U,R)-\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},W,R)=g-nd(1-R)\leq-\varepsilon nd\ecomma

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},U)\right]\leq q^{-\varepsilon nd}\leq q^{-\varepsilon n}

Now,

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ satisfies }\mathcal{P}\right]\leq\sum_{\mathcal{V}\in{\mathbb{F}}}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)}\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }(\mathcal{V},U)\right]\leq|\mathcal{F}|\cdot\left|\mathcal{L}_{{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\right|\cdot q^{-\varepsilon n}=|\mathcal{F}|\cdot q^{-\varepsilon n+b^{2}}\eperiod

Finally, the third claim follows immediately from the second claim.
∎

We turn to proving
Proposition

4.3

Proof of
Proposition

4.3
.

We begin with the first statement. Suppose that
M<0

\mathsf{\Phi}(V,U,R)-\mathsf{\Phi}(V,W,R)=M

Since
\mathcal{C}

\left|\mathcal{C}^{b}\cap M_{\mathcal{V}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}\right|\geq\left|K\right|-\sum_{u,v}\left|K_{u,v}\right|\geq q^{d}-\binom{b}{2}\cdot q^{d-1}>0\ecomma

where the last inequality is by our assumption that

\mathcal{C}^{b}\cap M_{\mathcal{V}}^{\operatorname{\mathsf{Dist}}}\neq\emptyset\ecomma

so
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{V}
and thus satisfies
\mathcal{P}
.

We turn to bound the probability of
E_{u,v}
for some fixed
u,v
. To this end, we find a necessary condition for this event. Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathsf{RS}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n};k-t\right)

Observe that
\mathcal{D}
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times U_{u,v}}

\mathcal{C}^{b}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times U_{u,v}}

\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U_{u,v}}\right)\geq\dim\left(\mathcal{C}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U_{u,v}}\right)-t(b-1)=\dim K_{u,v}-t(b-1)\eperiod

Therefore, the event
E_{u,v}
implies that
\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U_{u,v}}\right)\geq 1

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b-1}
be the projection map onto the coordinate set
[b]\setminus\{u\}
. Define the
(b-1)
-local profile
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b-1})^{n}
by
\mathcal{T}_{i}=\psi(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U_{u,v})
. Note that
\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U_{u,v}}\right)=\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b-1}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\right)

W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b-1})

\psi(W)=W^{\prime}
. Then, denoting

, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{T},W^{\prime},R_{\mathcal{V}}-\delta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim(\mathcal{T}_{i}\cap W^{\prime})-\left(1-(R_{\mathcal{V}}-\delta)\right)\cdot n\cdot\dim W^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap W)-\left(1-(R_{\mathcal{V}}-\delta)\right)\cdot n\cdot\dim W

\displaystyle=\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},W,R_{\mathcal{V}}-\delta)=\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},W,R_{\mathcal{V}})-\delta\cdot n\cdot\dim W

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},\{0\},R_{\mathcal{V}})-\delta\cdot n\cdot\dim W\leq-\delta\cdot n\cdot\dim W\ecomma

where the first inequality is due to
Eq.

26

Proposition

6.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left[E_{u,v}\right]\leq\mathrm{Pr}\left[\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{V},U_{u,v}}\right)\geq 1\right]=\mathrm{Pr}\left[\dim\left(\mathcal{D}^{b}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\right)\geq 1\right]

\displaystyle\leq(2^{b}-1)\cdot\left(\frac{(4b)^{4b}(k-t)}{\delta q}\right)^{\frac{\delta n}{2b}}

\displaystyle\leq(2^{b}-1)\cdot\left(\frac{(4b)^{4b}k}{\varepsilon q}\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon n}{2b}}\eperiod

Eq.

25
follows since

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\mathcal{C}\textrm{ contains }\mathcal{V}\right]\geq 1-\mathrm{Pr}\left[\bigcup_{u,v}E_{u,v}\right]\geq 1-\binom{b}{2}\cdot(2^{b}-1)\cdot\left(\frac{(4b)^{4b}k}{\varepsilon q}\right)^{\frac{\varepsilon n}{2b}}\eperiod

∎

7.1
Local Polynomial Profiles

We require some notation. Fix

Observe that

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear space. If

For

Let

-linear but not

The following fact will be useful.

Lemma 7.1

(Dimension of
S(\alpha)
)
.

Let
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{b}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear space and write

1.

\dims S(\alpha)\leq D
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

2.

If
S
is
k
-bounded (

) then

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mathsf{U}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}\left[\dims S(\alpha)=D\right]\geq 1-\frac{Dk}{q}\eperiod

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{|S|\times b}
be a matrix (possibly with infinitely many rows) whose rows are the elements of
S
. Denote
t:=\dims S(\alpha)=\ranks A(\alpha)
. Note that
A
contains a minor
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{t\times t}

\ranks M(\alpha)=t

\left(\det M\right)(\alpha)=\det\left(M(\alpha)\right)\neq 0\ecomma

implying that

The first claim follows.

We turn to the second claim. Suppose that

is
k
-bounded. Let

\rankl M^{\prime}=D

\det M^{\prime}\neq 0

are polynomials of degree at most

. Suppose that this event holds. Then,

D\geq t=\ranks A(\alpha)\geq\ranks M^{\prime}(\alpha)\geq D\ecomma

\dims S(\alpha)=t=D
.
∎

We turn to define the notion of a
local polynomial profile
.

Definition 7.2
.

An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear map
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{a}

\psi\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{b}\right)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{a}
. In other words,
\psi
is
polynomial
if it is represented in the standard basis by a matrix with polynomial entries. We use
\deg\psi
to denote the maximum degree (as a polynomial) of an entry in this matrix.

Definition 7.3
.

A
b
-local polynomial profile
is a sequence
\Psi=\left(\psi_{1},\dots\psi_{n}\right)
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear polynomial maps
\psi_{i}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b_{i}}
(for some
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{N}

\deg\Psi=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\deg\psi_{i}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. A tuple of polynomials
(P_{1},\dots,P_{b})
is said to
satisfy

(\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
if for every

\left(\psi_{i}\left(P_{1},\dots,P_{b}\right)\right)(\alpha_{i})=0\eperiod

(27)

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear space
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{b}
, we denote

S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=\left\{\boldsymbol{P}\in S\mid\boldsymbol{P}\textrm{ satisfies }(\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha})\right\}\eperiod

Note that
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear space.

Polynomial profiles provide a framework for directly analyzing and manipulating message vectors, which, in the case of random RS codes, correspond to the coefficient vectors of low-degree polynomials. This generalization enables one to define profiles that are well-suited for the stochastic process to proceed until completion (see
Section

7.4
for further details). Additionally, we require a notion that is related to the concept of
\mathsf{\Phi}
, which is defined for subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}
.

Definition 7.4

(Strength)
.

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear subspace
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}
and a polynomial map
\psi
, we denote
\mathsf{strength}_{U}(\psi)\coloneqq\diml\psi(U)
. Note that when
U={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}
, we have
\mathsf{strength}_{U}(\psi)=\rankl\psi
.

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear subspace
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}
and a polynomial profile
\Psi=\left(\psi_{1},\dots\psi_{n}\right)
, we denote
\mathsf{strength}_{U}(\Psi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{strength}_{U}(\psi_{i})
.

Definition 7.5
.

Given
T,U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}
, where
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear subspace, we say that

is

\spnl(T\cap U)=U
. Otherwise, we say that
U
is
T
-dead
.

Our goal is to rephrase

in terms of local polynomial profiles. We require the following lemma to do so.

Lemma 7.6

Let

with

a prime power. Let
\mathcal{V}=\left(V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathcal{L}\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\right)\right)^{n}

Furthermore, for every
Q_{k,b}

For

\varphi_{i}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{b-\dims V_{i}}

-linear map with

\psi_{i}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b-\dims V_{i}}

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear map whose restriction to

\varphi_{i}(\boldsymbol{P}(\alpha))=\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{P})(\alpha)

\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

\boldsymbol{P}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{b}

Finally, take

Now, suppose that
\mathcal{C}:=\mathsf{RS}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n};k\right)

-linear space and is

\Pr_{\alpha\sim\mathsf{U}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}\left[\dims S(\alpha)=D\right]\geq 1-\frac{Dk}{q}\geq 1-\frac{bk}{q}>0\eperiod

In particular, we may fix some

\displaystyle\dim(U^{\prime}\cap V_{i})

\displaystyle=D-\dim\varphi_{i}(U^{\prime})

\displaystyle=D-\dim\left\{\varphi_{i}(\boldsymbol{P}(\alpha))\mid\boldsymbol{P}\in S\right\}

\displaystyle=D-\dim\left\{\psi_{i}(\boldsymbol{P})(\alpha)\mid\boldsymbol{P}\in S\right\}

The first inequality above follows from the first item of

-live. Hence,

\mathsf{strength}_{U}(\Psi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\diml\psi_{i}(U)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(D-\dims\left(U^{\prime}\cap V_{i}\right)\right)\eperiod

∎

We give some intuition for

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Phi}(\mathcal{V},U^{\prime},R)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\dims(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U^{\prime})-(1-R)n\dims U^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-(\sum_{i}\dims U^{\prime}-\dims(\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap U))+Rn\dims U^{\prime}

Lemma 7.7

The rest of this section is devoted to proving

. The main ingredient in our proof is a potential function, which we now define.

7.2
A Potential Function for Local Polynomial Profiles

Let
\Psi=\left(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\right)
be a
b
-local polynomial profile and let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\right)
be a uniformly random element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. To prove
Lemma

7.7
we need to bound the probability that
Q_{k,b}[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\supsetneq\left\{\boldsymbol{0}\right\}
. To this end, we will tackle a more general question: given a finite
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear space
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]^{b}
, how likely is it that
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\supsetneq\left\{\boldsymbol{0}\right\}
? To help answer this question, we define a potential function
\mathsf{\varPi}
. Given an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear space
W\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)^{b}
, we denote

d_{W}(S):=\dims\left(S\cap W\right)

and

\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}\left(S,\Psi\right):=d_{W}(S)-\mathsf{strength}_{W}(\Psi)\eperiod

To motivate our definition of
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}
, consider an iterative stochastic process in which the random evaluation points
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
are revealed one by one. For
0\leq i\leq n
, denote
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i}}=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i}\right)
. Also,
\Psi_{1\cdots{i}}=\left(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{i}\right)
and
\Psi_{{i}\cdots n}=\left(\psi_{i},\dots,\psi_{n}\right)
. Note that
S[\Psi_{1\cdots{i}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i}}]
is fully known after the
i
-th step of the stochastic process. Moreover,

S=S[\Psi_{1\cdots{0}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{0}}]\supseteq S[\Psi_{1\cdots{1}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{1}}]\supseteq\dots\supseteq S[\Psi_{1\cdots{n}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{n}}]=S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\eperiod

As we show below (
Lemma

7.9
), the decrease in dimension in a single step, namely,

d_{W}(S[\Psi_{1\cdots{i-1}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i-1}}])-d_{W}(S[\Psi_{1\cdots{i}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i}}])
, is at most
\diml\psi_{i}(W)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{W}(S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}])

\displaystyle=d_{W}(S)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{W}(S[\Psi_{1\cdots{i-1}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i-1}}])-d_{W}(S[\Psi_{1\cdots{i}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1\cdots{i}}])\right)

\displaystyle\geq d_{W}(S)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\diml\psi_{i}(W)\right)

\displaystyle=d_{W}(S)-\mathsf{strength}_{W}(\Psi)=\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}(S,\Psi))

(31)

Hence, if
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}(S,\Psi)
is positive then
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\cap W
is guaranteed to have positive dimension, implying, in particular, that
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\supsetneq\{0\}
.

We turn to the examine the opposite case, namely, when
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}(S,\Psi)
is very negative (that is, much smaller than zero). Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is sampled uniformly at random from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. One may hope to show that
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\cap W=\{0\}
holds in this setting with high probability. However, this is not always true. Indeed, a construction along the lines of
Example

4.2
yields a setting in which
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}(S,\Psi)
is very negative, but
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W^{\prime}}(S,\Psi)
is positive for some nontrivial
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(X)
-linear subspace
W^{\prime}\subseteq W
. By
Eq.

31
, we then have
S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\cap W\supseteq S[\Psi,\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\cap W^{\prime}\supsetneq\{0\}
.

In
Lemma

7.8
we manage to show that a somewhat weaker probabilistic phenomenon does always occur whenever
\mathsf{\varPi}_{W}(S,\Psi)
is very negative. Namely, we show that

When Does A Three-Dimensional Chern-Simons-Witten Theory Have A Time Reversal Symmetry?
Roman Geiko and Gregory W. Moore

Abstract
In this paper, we completely characterize time-reversal invariant three-dimensional Chern-Simons gauge theories with torus gauge group. At the level of the Lagrangian, toral Chern-Simons theory is defined by an integral lattice, while at the quantum level, it is entirely determined by a quadratic function on a finite Abelian group and an integer mod 24 24 . We find that quantum time-reversally symmetric theories can be defined by classical Lagrangians defined by integral lattices which have self-perpendicular embeddings into a unimodular lattice. We find that the quantum toral Chern-Simons theory admits a time-reversal symmetry iff the higher Gauss sums of the associated modular tensor category are real. We conjecture that the reality of the higher Gauss sums is necessary and sufficient for a general non-Abelian Chern-Simons to admit quantum T-symmetry.

Introduction

Three-dimensional Chern-Simons theory is one of the most interesting and well-studied topological quantum field theories. It has a wide range of applications, from the mathematics of invariants of three-manifolds and knots to the physics of the fractional quantum Hall effect and topological quantum computation. In a series of papers beginning with
Seiberg2016
it was observed that some Chern-Simons theories admit nontrivial time-reversal symmetries

The basic idea of the classification is very simple (it is reviewed with some more care in the next section). It was demonstrated in

\mathscr{D}
(which can be viewed as the group of anyons, or as the group of so-called “one-form symmetries”), together with a quadratic function on it

\hat{q}:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

b(x,y):=\hat{q}(x+y)-\hat{q}(x)-\hat{q}(y)+\hat{q}(0)

(1)

is bilinear form on
\mathscr{D}

The pair

The result of

The time-reversal invariant theories are simply those such that

The main result is that a pair is time-reversal if and only if all the higher Gauss sums associated to the quadratic function

It is of course interesting to extend the result to Chern-Simons theories with non-Abelian gauge group.
We propose that the proper criterion is that the associated modular tensor category is equivalent to

Toral Chern-Simons-Witten Theory and Finite Forms

In this letter, we analyze how the toral Chern-Simons theories behave under the time-reversal transformation and study the condition for this family of topological quantum field theories to be time-reversal invariant. The definition of the
U(1)^{N}
Chern-Simons gauge theory requires a choice of a rank
N
integral symmetric bilinear form
K
such that the CS functional informally reads

CS_{\Lambda,K}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{X}K_{IJ}A_{I}\wedge dA_{J}\;,

(2)

where
K_{IJ}=K(e_{I},e_{J})
is the matrix of
K
in the given basis for a lattice
\Lambda
. The gauge fields are normalized so that
F/2\pi
has integral periods. This theory requires a choice of spin-structure for
X
if
K
is odd and does not require this extra choice if
K
is even. In the former case, the theory is called spin, while in the latter, it is called bosonic. For a proper definition of the functional (
2
), and related matters, see
Belov:2005ze
;
stirling2008abelian
. Ultimately,
K
is an element of
H^{4}(BT,{\mathbb{Z}})
where
T
is the Abelian torus. Due to the isomorphism between
H^{4}(BT,{\mathbb{Z}})
and the space of integral symmetric bilinear forms, will mean by
K
the corresponding object in the space forms.

To begin, we set some notation. By a lattice
(\Lambda,K)
, we mean a finitely generated free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
\Lambda
together with the bilinear form
K
on it. Here and everywhere below, the form
K
is assumed to be integral, symmetric, generically odd, and non-degenerate. If it does not lead to confusion, the lattice is denoted simply by
K
. As a gauge theory,
CS_{\Lambda,K}
does not depend on the choice of basis for
\Lambda
, it rather depends on the isomorphism class of
(\Lambda,K)
in the space of integral lattices. Classification of integral lattices is a notoriously complicated task, however, as we shall review, the full quantum theory defined by
(\Lambda,K)
depends on a set of invariants that is much coarser than the isomorphism class. We proceed to a review of the classification of quantum toral Chern-Simons theories and associated lattice invariants first discussed in
Belov:2005ze
.

As a free module,
\Lambda
can be embedded in the dual module
\Lambda^{*}=\mbox{Hom}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{Z}})
by using the non-degenerate form
K
: in terms of the original lattice, the dual lattice reads
\Lambda^{*}=\{x\in\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Q}\,|\,K(x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,\;\mbox{for all}\;\mu\in\Lambda\}
. Further, the form
K
can be extended to the whole
\Lambda^{*}
by
\mathbb{Q}
-linearity, giving rise to a form on the dual module:

\displaystyle K^{*}:\;\Lambda^{*}\times\Lambda^{*}\to\mathbb{Q}\;.

(3)

Then, we define the discriminant group
\mathscr{D}=\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda
, it is a finite Abelian group of order
|\mathscr{D}|=|\det K|
which can be equipped with the following torsion form:

\displaystyle b:\mathscr{D}\times\mathscr{D}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}/{\mathbb{Z}}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle b(x+\Lambda,y+\Lambda)

\displaystyle=K^{*}(x,y)\;\mbox{mod}\;1\,.

(5)

We call the pair consisting of a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
b
and a finite Abelian group
\mathscr{D}
simply by a finite bilinear form: the discriminant form on
\mathscr{D}
is a finite bilinear form as long as
K
is non-degenerate.

Given a lattice
(\Lambda,K)
, a quadratic refinement for it is a function
\hat{Q}:\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{Q}(\mu+\nu)-\hat{Q}(\mu)-\hat{Q}(\nu)+\hat{Q}(0)=K(\mu,\nu)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mu,\nu\in\Lambda\,.

(6)

A quadratic form is a quadratic refinement also satisfying
Q(n\mu)=n^{2}Q(\mu)
for all
n
. Analogously, we define a quadratic refinement for finite bilinear form
(\mathscr{D},b)
: this is a function
\hat{q}:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{Q}/{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\hat{q}(x+y)-\hat{q}(x)-\hat{q}(y)+\hat{q}(0)=b(x,y)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x,y\in\mathscr{D}\,,

(7)

while a quadratic form
q
for
b
is a quadratic refinement that also satisfies
q(nx)=n^{2}q(x)
(alternatively, it must satisfy
q(-x)=q(x)
and
q(0)=0
). Note, the quadratic refinements that are not necessarily quadratic forms will be distinguished by a hat, like
\hat{Q}
and
\hat{q}
, while quadratic forms are always unhatted, like
Q
and
q
. The standard way to obtain a quadratic form for the even lattice
K
is to define
Q(\mu)=K(\mu,\mu)/2
. If
K
is odd, then
K(\mu,\mu)/2
is not always an integer, and quadratic form refining
K
does not exist. In order to deal with this problem, we define a quadratic refinement by picking a characteristic vector
W\in\Lambda^{*}
, i.e., a vector satisfying

\displaystyle K(W,\mu)\equiv K(\mu,\mu)\;\mbox{mod}\;2\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mu\in\Lambda\;.

(8)

For any choice of
W
,
K(\mu,\mu-W)/2
is a quadratic refinement of
K
. Moreover, we can shift this refinement by any constant. It is convenient to define the standard quadratic refinement for
K
odd as
\hat{Q}_{W}(\mu)=K(\mu,\mu\!-\!W)/2+K(W,W)/8
. This definition depends on the choice of
W
in a very mild way. One checks that for any
W^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{*}
, there exists an element
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
Q_{W+2W^{\prime}}(\mu)=Q_{W}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})
. Therefore, up to a shift of the argument, quadratic refinements defined above depend only on the class
[W]\in\Lambda^{*}/2\Lambda^{*}
. In the bosonic (even) case, this class can be chosen
0
, while in the spin (odd) case, a choice of non-trivial class
[W]
is required. The refinement
\hat{Q}_{W}(\mu)
descends to a refinement
\hat{q}_{W}(x)
on the discriminant group in the full analogy with the discriminant bilinear form.

Another invariant of the integral lattice
K
is its signature
\sigma
– the difference between the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues of
K
diagonalized over
\mathbb{Q}
. The Gauss-Milgram formula (see, e.g.
Taylor
;
Hopkins2005
) states that the Gauss sum for the standard quadratic refinement
\hat{q}_{W}
, obtained from the lattice
(\Lambda,K)
, satisfies

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathscr{D}}}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{D}}e^{2\pi i\hat{q}_{W}(x)}=e^{2\pi i\sigma/8}\,.

(9)

Let us define relations that will play a key role in our further discussion. Notice that a quadratic (bilinear) form precomposed with a group automorphism is another quadratic (bilinear) form. Since it is unreasonable to distinguish between isomorphic groups, we give the following definition of isomorphic quadratic (bilinear) forms on lattices and finite Abelian groups.

Definition 2.1
.

Integral lattices
(\Lambda,K)
and
(\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime})
are isomorphic
(\Lambda,K)\cong(\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime})
if there exists an isomorphism of free modules
F:\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
K=K^{\prime}\circ F
.

Definition 2.2
.

Finite bilinear forms
(\mathscr{D},b)
and
(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},b^{\prime})
are isomorphic
(\mathscr{D},b)\cong(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},b^{\prime})
if there exists a group isomorphism
f:\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
such that
b=b^{\prime}\circ f
.

Finite quadratic forms
(\mathscr{D},q)
and
(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},q^{\prime})
are isomorphic
(\mathscr{D},q)\cong(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},q^{\prime})
if there exists a group isomorphism
f:\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
such that
q=q^{\prime}\circ f
.

There is an extra transformation which is allowed for quadratic refinements but not for quadratic forms. Notice that the shift of the argument maps a quadratic form to a quadratic refinement. On the contrary, a shift of the argument of a quadratic refinement

\hat{q}(x)\mapsto\hat{q}^{\prime}(x)=\hat{q}(x-\delta)=\hat{q}(x)+\hat{q}(-\delta)+b(x,-\delta)-\hat{q}(0)

(10)

gives another quadratic refinement refining the same bilinear form
b
as
\hat{q}
refines. Motivated by this observation, we introduce the following equivalence relation

Definition 2.3
.

Quadratic refinements
\hat{q}
and
\hat{q}^{\prime}
defined on
\mathscr{D}
are equivalent
\hat{q}\;\sim\;\hat{q}^{\prime}
if there exists
\delta\in\mathscr{D}
such that
\hat{q}(x)=\hat{q}^{\prime}(x+\delta)
for all
x\in\mathscr{D}
. The equivalence class of
\hat{q}
is denoted by
\{\hat{q}\}
.

With this new notation, we reformulate the previous result: given an integral lattice
(\Lambda,K)
, the class of the quadratic refinement
\{q_{W}\}
does not depend on the choice of the representative of the class
[W]\in\Lambda^{*}/2\Lambda^{*}
.

2.1
Quantization of lattices

In the previous section, we defined various invariants associated with integral lattices. Let us review the role those invariants play in the description of the quantum Abelian Chern-Simons-Witten theory.

It was shown in
Belov:2005ze
that the classical spin Chern-Simons theory, defined by the lattice
(\Lambda,K)
, upon quantization becomes a theory which depends only on the quartet of lattice invariants
(\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}_{W}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\,24)
— all topological invariants of manifolds assigned by this TQFT can be expressed through these four invariants. Note that
Belov:2005ze
only classified torus CSW theory as a non-extended
\operatorname{2-3}
spin TQFT, while torus CSW as a fully-extended
\operatorname{0-1-2-3}
TQFT was analyzed in
Freed:2009qp
;
Henriques
.

Observe that keeping
b
in the quartet is not necessary as
b
can always be reconstructed from
\hat{q}
according to (
7
). Nevertheless, we keep this redundant data in the quartet for a reason: many functions can refine the same finite form, and we will be finding refinements after fixing a finite form. In other words, we keep the redundancy to be aligned with the logic of this paper.

The process of quantization of a toral CS theory can be seen as the following mapping:

\displaystyle(\Lambda,K)\mapsto({\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}_{W}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\,24})\,,

(11)

called “quantization of lattice” in
stirling2008abelian
. While the quantization is far from injective, it is surjective. It was shown by Wall in
Wall1963QuadraticFO
that any finite form
(\mathscr{D},b)
is a discriminant form for some integral lattice. Moreover, it was shown in Proposition 3.60 of
stirling2008abelian
that there exists a unique class
[W]
such that
\{q\}=\{q_{W}\}
. Therefore, any abstract quartet subject to (
9
) lifts to a (generically odd) lattice. If the quartet contains a refinement that is also a quadratic form, then such a quartet lifts to an even lattice. See
Wang2020
for examples of such a reconstruction in the bosonic case.

In summary, quantum toral CSW theory is defined by a quartet
({\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}_{W}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\,24})
. The following isomorphism relation is natural.

Definition 2.4
.

Quartets
(\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\;24)
and
(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},\,b^{\prime},\{\,\hat{q}^{\prime}\},\,\sigma^{\prime}\,\mbox{mod}\;24)
are isomorphic if there exists a group isomorphism
f:\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
such that
\{\hat{q}\}=\{\hat{q}^{\prime}\circ f\}
,
b=b^{\prime}\circ f
, and
\sigma\equiv\sigma^{\prime}
mod
24
.

In the next section, we define the action of time-reversal on classical data and formulate the condition for quantum and classical time-reversal invariance.

2.2
Time-reversal transformation

Classically, a time-reversal transformation (
\mathsf{T}
-transformation) acts on the CS functional (
2
) by changing the sign of the volume form. Equivalently, the
\mathsf{T}
-transformation acts as a negation of the bilinear form:

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}:(\Lambda,K)\mapsto(\Lambda,-K)\,.

(12)

The
\mathsf{T}
-transformation can be accompanied with a lattice automorphism, and, in some cases, the former can be undone with the latter.

Definition 2.5
.

The classical CS functional defined through the (isomorphism class) of lattices
(\Lambda,K)
is
classically
time-reversal invariant (
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariant) if
(\Lambda,K)\cong(\Lambda,-K)
as integral lattices.

If
(\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\,24)
is the quantization of
(\Lambda,K)
, then
(\mathscr{D},-b,\{-\hat{q}\},-\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\,24)
is the quantization of
(\Lambda,-K)
. Along with the classical
{\mathsf{T}}
invariance, we can introduce the quantum
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance defined solely in terms of the lattice invariants.

Definition 2.6
.

The quantum toral CS theory specified by the quartet
(\mathscr{D},\,b,\,\{\hat{q}\},\,\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\;24)
is
quantum

\mathsf{T}
-invariant if this quartet is isomorphic to
(\mathscr{D},\,-b,\,\{-\hat{q}\},\,-\sigma\,\mbox{mod}\;24)
. Such a quartet is called
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric.

A
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric quartet, in particular, consists of a finite form
(\mathscr{D},b)
and a refinement
\hat{q}
that are isomorphic to their negation. These terms deserve their own definition.

Definition 2.7
.

A finite bilinear form
(\mathscr{D},b)
is a bilinear
{\mathsf{T}}
-form if
(\mathscr{D},b)\cong(\mathscr{D},-b)
. A quadratic refinement
\hat{q}
on

is a

such that
\{\hat{q}\circ\gamma\}=\{-\hat{q}\}

Clearly, a quadratic

-refinement refines a bilinear

-form.

Finite Bilinear Forms

In this section, we review the classification of bilinear forms on finite Abelian groups and apply it to finding isomorphism classes of
\mathsf{T}
-invariant forms. As a general reference for this section, we recommend the book by Miranda

Miranda2009
. The main tool for the analysis of finite forms is localization to their prime and homogeneous subgroups. For a finite Abelian group
\mathscr{D}
, its
p
-subgroup
\mathscr{D}_{p}
(a.k.a. Sylow
p
-subgroup) is the maximal subgroup of
\mathscr{D}
consisting of elements whose order is a power of
p

p
-subgroups:

\mathscr{D}\cong\bigoplus_{p}\mathscr{D}_{p}\,.

(13)

Further, each
\mathscr{D}_{p}
in this decomposition is isomorphic to a sum of homogeneous
p
-groups of exponent

\mathscr{D}_{p}\cong\bigoplus_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k})^{d_{p}^{k}}\,.

(14)

Both splittings are orthogonal in the sense that any form

\mathscr{D}
admits an orthogonal decomposition into the direct sum of forms on prime and homogeneous components:

\displaystyle b\cong\bigoplus_{p}b_{p}\,,\quad b_{p}\cong\bigoplus_{k}b_{p}^{k}.

(15)

Thus, any finite form admits a splitting
(\mathscr{D},b)\equiv\sum_{p}\sum_{k}(({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k})^{d_{p}^{k}},b_{p}^{k})
.

The finite forms on cyclic groups.

The non-degeneracy condition implies that finite forms on

\displaystyle b(x,y)=\alpha xy/p^{r}\,\quad\mbox{where}\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}\,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r})^{\times}\,.

4.2
The Witt group of quadratic forms.

In this section, we review the notion of the Witt group of quadratic forms, denoted by
{\cal W}_{pt}
, and discuss the interplay between the Witt relation of quadratic forms and time-reversal symmetry. The Witt equivalence relation of quadratic forms answers the physically-motivated question of when two quantum Abelian CS TQFTs in three dimensions share a topological interface
Saulina2011
.
One should not confuse the Witt group of bilinear forms
W
defined in
3.3
with the Witt group
{\cal W}_{pt}
we shall define here. The Witt group we define below is coarser than
W
or its quadratic analog, while being more standard in the physics literature. In Theorem
3.3
above we found a simple criterion for
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance of a finite bilinear form expressed in terms of
W
. One might expect a similar statement for the
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance of quadratic forms expressed in terms of
{\cal W}_{pt}
, but the situation turns out to be more subtle.

Let us review the standard notion of the Witt group of quadratic forms
Drinfeld:2010
(compared to the Witt group of bilinear forms
W
defined in Section
3.3
). We assume that quadratic and bilinear forms are non-degenerate unless specified otherwise. Let
(\mathscr{D},q)
be a quadratic form,
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
be an isotropic subgroup, and
\mathcal{H}^{\perp}
be the subgroup orthogonal to
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the bilinear form refined by
q
. It is straightforward to check that
(\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H},q|_{\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H}})
is a non-degenerate quadratic form.
We say that
(\mathscr{D},q)
and
(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},q^{\prime})
are Witt-equivalent if there exist isotropic subgroups
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
such that
(\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H},q|_{\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H}})\cong(\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}\perp}/\mathcal{H}^{\prime},q|_{\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}\perp}/\mathcal{H}^{\prime}})
. We denote the Witt equivalence class of
(\mathscr{D},q)
by
[(\mathscr{D},q)]
. The monoid of isomorphism classes of quadratic forms modulo the Witt equivalence relation forms an Abelian group denoted by
{\cal W}_{pt}
: the inverse to
[(\mathscr{D},q)]
is
[\mathscr{D},-q]
. One can check that the following relations hold for all
k\geqslant 1
:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}=[\lambda_{p}^{\pm 1}]\,,\quad[\lambda_{p^{2k}}^{\pm 1}]=[0]\,,\quad[\phi_{2^{k}}]=[0]\,,\quad[\psi_{2^{2k}}]=[0]\,,\quad[\psi_{2^{2k+1}}]=[\psi_{2}]\,,\\
[\omega_{2^{2k+1}}^{+1}]=[\omega_{2^{2k+1}}^{+5}]=[\omega_{2}^{+1}]\,,\quad[\omega_{2^{2k+1}}^{-1}]=[\omega_{2^{2k+1}}^{-5}]=[\omega_{2}^{-1}]\,,\\
[\omega_{2^{2k+2}}^{+1}]=[\omega_{4}^{+1}]\,,\quad[\omega_{2^{2k+2}}^{+5}]=[\omega_{4}^{+5}]\,,\quad[\omega_{2^{2k+2}}^{-1}]=[\omega_{4}^{-1}]\,,\quad[\omega_{2^{2k+2}}^{-5}]=[\omega_{4}^{-5}]\,.\end{gathered}

Clearly, any quadratic form is Witt equivalent to some anisotropic quadratic form, i.e., having no isotropic subgroups after reduction along the maximal isotropic subgroup (the choice of the maximal isotropic subgroup does not matter). Therefore, any Witt class can be represented by an anisotropic quadratic form. For completeness, we write down presentations for
{\cal W}_{pt}
:

\displaystyle({\cal W}_{pt})_{p}\cong\langle[\lambda_{p}^{+1}],[\lambda_{p}^{-1}]\,|\,2[\lambda_{p}^{+1}]=2[\lambda_{p}^{-1}]=0\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/2\,,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\;\;p\equiv 1\;\mbox{mod}\;4\,,\;

\displaystyle({\cal W}_{pt})_{p}\cong\langle[\lambda_{p}^{+1}],[\lambda_{p}^{-1}]\,|\,2[\lambda_{p}^{+1}]=2[\lambda_{p}^{-1}],\;[\lambda_{p}^{+1}+\lambda_{p}^{-1}]=0]\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/4\,,\quad\quad\;\;p\equiv 3\;\mbox{mod}\;4\,,\;

\displaystyle({\cal W}_{pt})_{2}\cong\langle[\omega_{2}^{+1}],[\omega_{2}^{+1}+\omega_{4}^{-1}]\,|\,8[\omega_{2}^{+1}]=0,2[\omega_{2}^{+1}+\omega_{4}^{-1}]=0\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/8\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/2\,.\;

A standard argument demonstrates that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathscr{D}|}}\tau_{1}(q)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H}|}}\tau_{1}(q|_{\mathcal{H}^{\perp}/\mathcal{H}})
for any isotropic subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
, see e.g.,
Miranda2009
. The argument works for any
\tau_{n}
as long as
n
is coprime to
|\mathscr{D}|
. As a consequence,
(\mathscr{D},q)
represents the trivial element of
{\cal W}_{pt}
if and only if
\frac{\tau_{n}(q)}{|\tau_{n}(q)|}=1
for all
n
such that
gcd(n,|\mathscr{D}|)=1
. Indeed, if
(\mathscr{D},q)
contains a Lagrangian subgroup, then
\frac{\tau_{n}(q)}{|\tau_{n}(q)|}=\tau_{n}(0)=1
for
gcd(n,|\mathscr{D}|)=1
. To prove the opposite direction, we reduce any quadratic form to the anisotropic one, e.g. to the ones listed in (
4.2
) and check that all non-zero anisotropic forms satisfy this property. A similar proof can be found in
NG2019
.

Let us compare the Witt groups
W
and
{\cal W}_{pt}
. We notice that the class of
(\mathscr{D},q)
in
{\cal W}_{pt}
contains much less information than the class of
(\mathscr{D},b)
in
W
. We have proved that any bilinear form representing an order two element
W
is
{\mathsf{T}}
symmetric in Theorem
3.3
. In sharp contrast, the condition that
(\mathscr{D},q)
represents an order two element in
{\cal W}_{pt}
is
not
sufficient for
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry. A good example, pointed out to us by T. Johnson-Freyd, of a
{\cal W}_{pt}
-trivial quadratic form which is not
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric is
\lambda_{3}^{+1}+\lambda_{27}^{-1}
. This has a Lagrangian subgroup, so it is trivial in
{\cal W}_{pt}
, but it is not
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric, as one can check by noting that the higher Gauss sums are not real.

The
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry need not be preserved by the Witt equivalence. Indeed, if
(\mathscr{D},q)
is a quadratic
{\mathsf{T}}
-form with the
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry realized by
\gamma\in Aut(\mathscr{D})
and
{\cal H}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
is an isotropic subgroup, then
\gamma({\cal H})
is isomorphic to
{\cal H}
, but it is in general a different subgroup of
\mathscr{D}
.

Any quadratic
{\mathsf{T}}
-form
(\mathscr{D},q)
represents an order two element in
{\cal W}_{pt}
by definition:
[\mathscr{D}\oplus\mathscr{D},q\oplus q]=[\mathscr{D}\oplus\mathscr{D},q\oplus-q]=[0]
. On the other hand, any order two element of
{\cal W}_{pt}
can be presented by an anisotropic quadratic form which must be
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric as can be seen in (
4.2
). In principle, one should be able to define an analog of the Witt relation which would preserve the
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry, perhaps, by requiring that the isotropic subgroup is fixed by at least one time reversal. We leave this question for future work.

4.3
Generalized isometries

Let us conclude the discussion of quadratic

For any quadratic form

\displaystyle Isom^{+}(q)=\{\gamma\in Aut(\mathscr{D})\,|\;q\circ\gamma=q\}\,.

The isometry groups of the standard quadratic forms are known, see, e.g.

\displaystyle Isom^{+}(\phi_{2^{r}})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\

\displaystyle Isom^{+}(\psi_{2^{m}})=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&-c\\

Further, we can define the set

\displaystyle Isom^{-}(q)=\{\gamma\in Aut(\mathscr{D})\,|\,q\circ\gamma=-q\}\,.

By definition, the set

is non-empty for a quadratic

\displaystyle Isom^{\pm}(q)=\{\gamma\in Aut(\mathscr{D})\,|\,q\circ\gamma=\pm q\}\,.

For cyclic groups, we obtain the abstract groups of generalized isometries:

\displaystyle Isom^{\pm}(\omega_{2}^{\pm 1})=\mbox{Id}\,;\;Isom^{\pm}(\omega^{t}_{2^{m}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/4\,;\;Isom^{\pm}(\lambda^{\pm 1}_{p^{r}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/4

One of the main goals of this paper is to discuss the necessary conditions for the existence of quantum time-reversal symmetry. Assuming that the quantum Chern-Simons admits a

For all other bilinear forms, we find that for

Let us summarize our findings. The bilinear

Given an isomorphism class of a bilinear

5
Lattices

At the classical level, any concrete toral CS functional is specified by a lattice
(\Lambda,K)
. In this section, we discuss the lifting of quantum data corresponding to
\mathsf{T}
-invariant quantum CS theories to lattices. Clearly, isomorphic lattices have isomorphic discriminant forms and quadratic refinements. In general, the interplay between isomorphism classes of finite forms and lattices is described by the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

(
Kneser1953QuadratischeFU
,
DURFEE1977
)

Two lattices
(\Lambda,K)
and
(\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime})
have isomorphic discriminant forms if and only if they are stably equivalent, that is
K\oplus U\cong K^{\prime}\oplus U^{\prime}
where
U
and
U^{\prime}
are unimodular lattices, even if
K
and
K^{\prime}
are even, and odd if
K
and
K^{\prime}
are odd.

Thus, finite
\mathsf{T}
-forms lift to lattices that are classically
\mathsf{T}
-invariant up to the addition of a unimodular lattice. In principle, we can describe lattices corresponding to
\mathsf{T}
-invariant quartets in terms of their
p
-adic reductions, also known as the genus. However, there is a more constructive description in terms of lattice embeddings.

5.1
Lattice embeddings

Let us review some of the results obtained by Nikulin in
Nikulin1980
. An embedding of lattices
\phi:\Lambda\hookrightarrow M
is called primitive if the quotient
M/\Lambda
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module, i.e., another lattice. Note that, in general, embeddings of lattices need not be primitive. For example,
\Lambda/2\Lambda
is a pure torsion, while for primitive embeddings,
M/\Lambda
must be torsion-free.

5
5

5

In practice, the primitiveness of the given embedding can be determined by a criterion found by Sertöz
sertoz2005singular
(Theorem 6): a lattice embedding is primitive if and only if the greatest common divisor of the maximal minors of the embedding matrix with respect to any choice of basis is 1. This criterion was rederived in
Levin2012
.

For a primitive embedding
\Lambda\hookrightarrow M
, we can consider the orthogonal complement
\Lambda^{\perp}\equiv(\Lambda)_{M}^{\perp}
with respect to the bilinear form of
M
. We say that two lattices
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
are perpendicular
\Lambda\!\perp\!\Lambda^{\prime}
if there exists a unimodular lattice
M
and a primitive embedding of
\Lambda
into a
M
such that
\Lambda^{\perp}\cong\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

(Nikulin)

Let
(\mathscr{D},b)
and
(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},b^{\prime})
be the discriminant forms of
(\Lambda,K)
and
(\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime})
respectively. Then:

1) A primitive embedding of
\Lambda
into a unimodular lattice
M
such that
\Lambda^{\perp}\cong\Lambda^{\prime}
is determined by an isomorphism
\gamma:\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
for which
b^{\prime}\circ\gamma=-b
.

2)
(\Lambda,K)\!\perp\!(\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime})
if and only if
(\mathscr{D},b)\cong(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},-b^{\prime})
.

It is straightforward to see that any lattice is orthogonal to its negation:
(\Lambda,K)\!\!\perp\!\!(\Lambda,-K)
trivially holds. However, the lattice need not be perpendicular to itself. As follows from the proposition above,
(\Lambda,K)\!\perp\!(\Lambda,K)
if and only if
(\mathscr{D},b)
is a T-form. This gives us an alternative description of
\mathsf{T}
-forms: these are discriminant forms of self-perpendicular lattices.

Remark.

Physically, the condition
\Lambda\!\perp\!\Lambda^{\prime}
for lattices means that there are three toral Chern-Simons theories:
CS_{\Lambda,K}
,
CS_{\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime}}
, and
CS_{M,U}
. Since
(M,U)
is a unimodular lattice,
CS_{M,U}
is a “trivial” theory. Note, the primitivity of the embedding is crucial; otherwise,
M/\Lambda
has a torsion and does not define a toral gauge theory. The “trivial” theory has no anyons, yet it is still a toral gauge theory with the lattice of charges and we can pick off charges constituting the non-trivial theory
CS_{\Lambda,K}
such that the quotient theory is
CS_{\Lambda^{\prime},K^{\prime}}
. In its turn, if
CS_{\Lambda,K}
is
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariant upon quantization, then it can be embedded into a trivial theory such that the quotient is the same theory. There is a close relation to the folding trick
Saulina2011
, where folded theory corresponds to
(\Lambda\oplus\Lambda,K\oplus(-K))
. Here, the pair
CS_{\Lambda,K}
and
CS_{\Lambda,-K}
is the simplest example of theories embeddable in the “trivial” theory.

5.2
Classical vs quantum time-reversal

In our approach to Abelian CS, the quantum time-reversal symmetry is much more common than the classical one. For the former, it suffices to find a
\mathsf{T}
-form, while for the latter, there must be an equivalence between the lattice and its negation. Clearly, a positive-definite lattice is never classically
\mathsf{T}
-invariant. Let
t_{+}
and
t_{-}
be the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, we might expect
(\Lambda,K)
to be classically
\mathsf{T}
-invariant only if
t_{+}=t_{-}
. Let
l(\mathscr{D})
be the minimal number of generators of
\mathscr{D}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

(Nukulin) An even lattice
(\Lambda,K)
with invariants
(t_{+},t_{-},q)
is unique up to isomorphism if
t_{+}+t_{-}\geqslant 2+l(\mathscr{D})
,
t_{+}\geqslant 1
,
t_{-}\geqslant 1
.

This proposition gives sufficient conditions for an even lattice to be
\mathsf{T}
-invariant. A classical Abelian CS theory defined by an even indefinite lattice
(\Lambda,K)
is classically
\mathsf{T}
-invariant if
t_{+}=t_{-}
, its discriminant form
(\mathscr{D},b)
is a
\mathsf{T}
-form,
q
is a
\mathsf{T}
-refinement that is also a quadratic form, and
2t_{+}\geqslant 2+l(\mathscr{D})
. This condition eliminates
A_{2}
, as for this
\mathsf{T}
-form, there does not exist a quadratic
\mathsf{T}
-form. We expect that a similar condition can be formulated for odd lattices, and leave this question for the future.

6
Modular Tensor Categories

We discussed the quantum data defining quantum Abelian Chern-Simons-Witten theory as a result of canonical quantization. Every (spin) CSW theory defines a 3d TQFT, known as a Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev theory
Witten1989
and, by the correspondence with 2d RCFT, it also defines a modular tensor category
Moore:1988qv
;
Moore:1989vd
. In the spin case it should be a super-MTC.
We shall view the (super-) MTC as the quantum data associated to a quantum CSW theory.
In this section we briefly review how the quantum data of torus CSW theory fits into the categorical picture.
We then define time-reversal invariance in terms of (super-) MTCs and consider some implications of this definition.

We begin by setting the most necessary notation
7
7

7

See Appendix
D
for quick definitions of other terms from category theory used here.
. Recall that a modular tensor category
{\cal C}
is, in particular, a non-degenerate braided fusion category
Drinfeld:2010
equipped with a ribbon structure, see
Rowell2018
;
Kong:2022cpy
for recent reviews. The braided structure is a set of isomorphisms permuting objects in the monoidal product

\displaystyle c_{x,y}:x\otimes y\xrightarrow{\sim}y\otimes x\,,

(56)

while the ribbon structure is a set of isomorphisms
\theta_{x}:x\xrightarrow{\sim}x
, called twists, compatible with the braiding, i.e., satisfying the balancing condition:

\displaystyle\theta_{x\otimes y}=c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}(\theta_{x}\otimes\theta_{y})

(57)

and such that
\theta_{\mathbf{1}}=id_{\mathbf{1}}
where
\mathbf{1}
is the monoidal unit.

8
8

8

The physical origin of this condition is that, if
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}
are (holomorphic parts of) primary fields in 2d CFT, of conformal dimensions
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
, then the coefficient of
\phi_{3}(w)
in the operator product of
\phi_{1}(z)
and
\phi_{2}(w)
depends on
z,w
as a constant times
(z-w)^{h_{3}-h_{2}-h_{1}}
.

Since we are interested in super-MTCs arising from (spin) Chern-Simons theories, all of the categories below are assumed to be unitary.
The spherical structure allows us to define the trace, such that the modular S-matrix is defined through the braiding as follows

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{x,y}={\rm Tr}[c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}]\,

(58)

where
x
and
y
are simple objects of
{\cal C}
.

A unitary premodular category
{\cal C}
is an MTC if and only if its
S
-matrix is non-degenerate. Working with spin TQFTs requires introduction of super-MTCs, see
Bruillard2017
;
Bruillard2020
for reviews. We say that a premodular category
{\cal C}
is a super-MTC if its Müger center is equivalent the category of super-vector spaces
sVec

S

{\cal S}_{sVec}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
and

0&-1\end{pmatrix}

sVec

S
-matrix of any super-MTC has the form
{\cal S}={\cal S}_{sVec}\otimes{\cal S}_{nd}
where
{\cal S}_{nd}
is some non-degenerate
S
-matrix
Bruillard2017
.

Further, we introduce the notion of time-reversed (super-) MTC analogous to the time-reversed quantum data of Abelian Chern-Simons. The time-reversed version of a unitary (super-) MTC
{\cal C}
, denoted by
{\cal C}^{rev}
, also known as the reversed category, is a category with the same objects as
{\cal C}
but with the reversed braiding
c_{x,y}^{rev}=c_{y,x}^{-1}
. This definition of time-reversed category was also used in
Lee:2018eqa
;
Kong:2022cpy

Definition 6.1
.

The unitary (super-) MTC
{\cal C}
is
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric if there exists a braided monoidal equivalence
{\cal F}:{\cal C}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\cal C}^{rev}
.

If such an
{\cal F}
exists, then
\bar{\theta}_{x}=\theta_{{\cal F}(x)}
and
\bar{\cal S}_{x,y}={\cal S}_{{\cal F}(x),{\cal F}(y)}
automatically

for all simple objects
x
and
y
. Here, the overline means complex conjugation and we are identifying
Hom(x,x)\cong\mathbb{C}
for simple objects
x
.

Remark.

The condition for the time-reversal invariance is somewhat similar to the conditions used for gluing left and right movers into modular invariant correlation functions
Frohlich:2009gb
;
Moore:1988ss
.
In RCFT, the physical Hilbert space is a finite sum of irreducible representations of chiral and anti-chiral algebras
{\cal H}=\oplus_{i=1}{\cal H}_{i}\otimes{\cal H}_{\bar{i}}
. The partition function of the full theory is
Z=\sum_{i,j}\chi_{i}\,h_{i,j}\,\overline{\chi_{j}}
where
\chi_{i}
is the character of the
i

are non-negative integers characterizing the field content of the theory. The set of numbers

is subject to algebraic constraints ensuring the modular invariance

Dijkgraaf:1988tf
;
Frohlich:2009gb

need not exist at all.

6.1
(Super)-MTC for Abelian Chern-Simons

A prototypical example of an MTC corresponding to bosonic toral CS is the twisted group category
{\cal C}(\mathscr{D},q)
introduced in
JOYAL199320
: objects are vector spaces labeled by the elements of the finite Abelian group
\mathscr{D}

stirling2008abelian

\displaystyle\theta_{x}=e^{2\pi iq(x)}\,,\quad{\cal S}_{x,y}=e^{2\pi ib(x,y)}\,,\quad x,y\in\mathscr{D}\,.

(59)

Any finite quadratic form defines a pointed MTC

and all pointed MTCs are of this form

.

9
9

9

In physical language a “pointed” MTC has a fusion rule algebra that is group-like, i.e., there is exactly one simple object in the fusion product of two simple objects. The simple objects can be chiral fields in RCFT or

According to the connection between finite quadratic forms and braiding on

In the case of spin CS, the category assigned to a bounding circle (NS sector) is a super-MTC, while the category assigned to a non-bounding circle (R sector) is a module category for the super-MTC associated with the NS sector. According to Corollary A.19 of

In the spin case, the quadratic refinement

6.3
Higher Gauss sums

Analogously to the Abelian case, we can define the higher Gauss sums for any unitary (super-) MTC
{\cal C}
. Let
d_{x}={\rm Tr}[id_{x}]
be the quantum dimension of
x\in\mbox{Irr}({\cal C})
where
id_{x}:x\to x
is the identity morphism of
x
and
\mbox{Irr}({\cal C})
is the set of irreducible objects of
{\cal C}
. For any

, we define the
n
-th Gauss sum of
{\cal C}
as

\displaystyle\tau_{n}({\cal C})=\sum_{x\in\scalebox{0.7}{Irr}({\cal C})}d_{x}^{2}\;\theta_{x}^{n}\,.

(60)

Assuming
\tau_{n}({\cal C})\neq 0
, the
n
-th multiplicative central charge of
{\cal C}

NG2019
is

\displaystyle\xi_{n}({\cal C})=\frac{\tau_{n}({\cal C})}{|\tau_{n}({\cal C})|}\,.

(61)

Let us review the key properties of the higher Gauss sums and central charges. The first central charge
\xi_{1}({\cal C})
of any MTC is a root of unity
Anderson:1988
, while the first Gauss sum of a super-MTC is always zero
Bruillard2017
. In general, if
{\cal C}
is a only a premodular category, i.e., a category with all the precursors of the MTC except for the non-degeneracy condition, then the following relations hold

1) If
\tau_{n}({\cal C})\neq 0
, then
\xi_{n}({\cal C}^{rev})=\xi_{-n}({\cal C})=\overline{\xi_{n}({\cal C})}
,

2) For all

,
\tau_{n}({\cal C}\boxtimes{\cal D})=\tau_{n}({\cal C})\tau_{n}({\cal D})
,

3) If
\tau_{n}({\cal C})\tau_{n}({\cal D})\neq 0
, then
\xi_{n}({\cal C}\boxtimes{\cal D})=\xi_{n}({\cal C})\xi_{n}({\cal D})
,

4) If
{\cal C}
is an MTC and
{\cal D}({\cal C})
is its Drinfeld center (see Appendix
D
for definitions), then
\xi_{n}({\cal Z}({\cal C}))=1
for all

such that
\tau_{n}({\cal C})\neq 0
.

The higher central charges, whenever defined, are multiplicative maps which take the unit value on the Drinfeld centers. We shall see that the higher central charges can be seen as homomorphisms from the Witt group
{\cal W}_{un}
of pseudo-unitary non-degenerate braided fusion categories to the group of roots of unity. For future convenience, let us introduce a notation for the subset of natural numbers coprime to the Frobenius-Schur exponent

of

\displaystyle D({\cal C})\equiv\{l\in\mathbb{N}\,|\;gcd(n,FSExp({\cal C}))=1\}\,.

(62)

Note, if
{\cal C}
is an MTC, then the Frobenius-Schur exponent of
{\cal C}
coincides with the order of
\theta

such that

for all

According to Theorem 4.1 of

, if

is a root of unity. Therefore, for any unitary MTC

(Theorem 4.4 of

Anti-unitary symmetries in TQFT.

In the present paper, we approach the question of when Chern-Simons admits an anti-unitary symmetry. Our approach is rather algebraic – we look for the condition on the quantum data to admit a time-reversal symmetry. A different approach to the definition of time-reversal invariant
3\!-\!2
TQFT should reflect the fact that such TQFT is not sensitive to changing the orientation of bordisms and
2
-manifolds. Therefore, to ensure
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance, there must exist a lift of the corresponding tangential structure to a non-oriented one. For simplicity, consider a non-anomalous oriented bosonic TQFT
\mathcal{F}^{SO}
, i.e., a monoidal functor from the category of oriented bordisms
\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{SO}
to the category of vector spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{SO}:\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{SO}\longrightarrow\mbox{Vec}\,.

(63)

Time-reversal invariance can be seen as the existence of a lift of
\mathcal{F}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{O}\to\mbox{Vec}
such that the following diagram with the forgetful functor
\kappa:\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{SO}\to\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{O}
commutes (in the 2-categorical sense)
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{SO}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{O}}
Vec
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}

Similarly, a non-anomalous
3\!-\!2
spin TQFT is a functor to the category of super vector spaces

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}:\mbox{Bord}_{3,2}^{Spin}\longrightarrow\mbox{sVec}\,.

(64)

and
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance condition is the existence of a lift of this functor. Depending on the realization of the time-reversal transformation, the
Spin
structure lifts to
Pin^{+}
or
Pin^{-}
structure, see
Debray2022
for analysis of this question for the case of Chern-Simons with a finite Abelian gauge group.

In terms of topological invariants, it was pointed out in
NG2019
that the Witten-Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants of the lens space
L(n,1)
and that of its orientation reversal are related as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{WRT_{{\cal C}}(L(n,1))}{WRT_{{\cal C}}(-L(n,1))}=\frac{\tau_{n}({\cal C})}{\tau_{-n}({\cal C})}\frac{\tau_{-1}({\cal C})}{\tau_{1}({\cal C})}

(65)

assuming that the relevant Gauss sums do not vanish. This relation is consistent with our conjecture, as we expect that time-reversal invariant MTCs provide us with invariants non-sensible to the orientation.

In the present paper, we analyse the algebraic data coming from the value
\mathcal{F}(S^{1})
. It would be interesting to investigate how the time-reversal symmetry is reflected on the value
\mathcal{F}(pt)

Freed:2009qp
;
Henriques
.

Time-reversal symmetry vs. time-reversal duality

In
Freed2021
, it was shown that Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev theory admits a non-trivial boundary theory iff it is a Turaev-Viro theory. Recall, a 3d TQFT always admits a gapless boundary e.g., through the CS/WZW correspondence, while the existence of gapped boundary theory is a non-trivial condition. For Turaev-Viro theory, the value of the TQFT functor on a 3-framed circle is a Drinfeld center. Therefore, with TQFTs admitting gapped boundaries, we associate Witt-trivial theories. This statement was rederived for Abelian Chern-Simons in
kaidi2021higher
– triviality of a certain subset of higher central charges suffices for bosonic Abelian Chern-Simons to admit a gapped boundary.

Being Witt-trivial is necessary and sufficient for the theory to admit a gapped boundary. Let us discuss what it means for the theory to represent an order two element in the Witt group. If
[{\cal C}(G_{k})]^{2}=1
, then there exists a braided equivalence

\displaystyle{\cal C}(G_{k})\boxtimes{\cal C}(G_{k})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\cal Z}({\cal A})

(66)

for some fusion category
{\cal A}
(Corollary 5.9 of
davydov2010Witt
). Physically, this means that the theory
{\cal C}(G_{k})
stacked with itself is equivalent to a Witt-trivial theory, a theory admitting a gapped boundary. By unfolding this construction, we get that
{\cal C}(G_{k})
satisfying (
66
) admits a topological interface with its time-reversal: one can say that such a theory admits a time-reversal
duality
. Topological interfaces in Abelian Chern-Simons were discussed in
Kapustin:2010if
and studied in more detail in the context of general 3d TQFTs in
Fuchs:2013
. There is a nice mathematical interpretation of such an interface pointed out to us by Christoph Schweigert: One can define a higher version of the Morita 2-category of (algebras, bimodules, intertwiners) called BrTens. It is a 4-category which is suitable for the Morita theory of braided fusion categories, and is spelled out in detail in
Brochier2018
. A topological interface between
{\cal C}
and
{\cal C}^{rev}
can be seen as a 1-morphism between the corresponding objects in the higher Morita category BrTens of braided tensor categories.

Let us comment more on the analogy between pairing left- and right-movers in RCFT and the existence of time-reversal duality in CS (or self-duality in RCFT according to
Moore:1989vd
). The equivalence
{\cal C}(G_{k})\boxtimes{\cal C}(G_{k})^{rev}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\cal Z}({\cal C}(G_{k}))
, holding for any MTC
{\cal C}(G_{k})
, is related to the existence of at least one sesqui-linear modular invariant pairing on
\mbox{Irr}({\cal C}(G_{k}))
, namely the diagonal modular invariant. In its turn, condition (
66
) is equivalent to the existence of a bilinear pairing on objects such that the total theory is “condensable to the vacuum” in the sense of
Kong2014AnyonCA
.

{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry and Witt relations.

In Section
3.3
, we discussed the Witt group of bilinear forms and its relation to the Kawauchi and Kojima invariants. The latter invariants are given by the higher Gauss sums of a specific quadratic form associated to the given bilinear form. We noticed that the class of a bilinear form in
W
depends on all the higher Gauss sums of the associated quadratic form (though, not all of them are independent). This must be contrasted with the fact that the class of an abstract quadratic form in
{\cal W}_{pt}
depends only on a subset of its higher Gauss sums (related to the Frobenius-Schur exponent). One might ask if there is an analog of
W
for pseudo-unitary braided fusion categories that depends on
all
of the higher Gauss sums of a class representative. We proved that being order two in
W
is necessary and sufficient for a bilinear form to be
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric in Theorem
3.3
. If the conjecture made in Section
Conjecture
is correct, then one might ask if being order two in the hypothetical analog of
W
is sufficient for
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariance.

We noted that a quadratic
{\mathsf{T}}
-form can be Witt equivalent to a non-
{\mathsf{T}}
-invariant quadratic form. Similarly, the
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry of a unitary MTC is not preserved by the Witt relation given in Definition
D.1
. It is an intriguing question if one can define an analog of
{\cal W}
which would consist of
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetric uMTCs modulo a Witt-like relation preserving the
{\mathsf{T}}
-symmetry.

“Real” fusion categories.

According to the standard definition, a fusion category is, in particular, a tensor category over
\mathbb{C}
. A complex fusion category
{\cal C}_{\mathbb{C}}
is “real” if it is equivalent to a product of a tensor category
{\cal C}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
defined over
{\mathbb{R}}
and
Vec_{\mathbb{C}}
:
{\cal C}_{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\cal C}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\boxtimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}Vec_{\mathbb{C}}
. This is the standard framework for the theory of Galois descent, which is discussed in the categorical context in
Etingof2012
and
Sanford2022
. When working over
{\mathbb{R}}
, we allow simple objects to have non-trivial endomorphism algebras. Almost trivial examples are
{\cal C}(\phi_{2})
and
{\cal C}(\psi_{2})
: their real versions are fusion categories with two objects whose endomorphism algebras are simply
{\mathbb{R}}
. In general,
{\cal C}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
{\cal C}_{\mathbb{C}}
need not have the same sets of simple objects. It would be interesting to understand if the anomaly partition function
Barkeshli:2016mew
;
Lee:2018eqa
can detect the splitting of
{\cal C}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Appendix D
The Witt Group of Non-Degenerate Braided Fusion Categories

In this appendix, we collect some relevant definitions and give a very brief review of the Witt group of braided fusion categories. We assume the reader is familiar with the notion of a fusion category, see
Etingof:2005
and
EGNO
for the general reference and the original paper by A. Davydov et al.
davydov2010Witt
on the Witt groups.

We say that a fusion category is pointed if all its simple objects are invertible, i.e., the fusion of simple objects is group-like. A fusion category
{\cal C}
is simple if the only proper fusion subcategory of
{\cal C}
is Vec. For a braided fusion category
{\cal C}
, we introduce the Müger center
{\cal Z}_{2}({\cal C})
as the full subcategory whose objects have trivial double braiding with all objects in
{\cal C}
:

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{2}({\cal C})=\{x\in{\cal C}\;|\;c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}=id_{x\otimes y},\;\mbox{for all}\;y\in{\cal C}\}\,.

(76)

We say that braided fusion category
{\cal C}
is non-degenerate if
{\cal Z}_{2}({\cal C})\cong\mbox{Vec}
, and slightly-degenerate if
{\cal Z}_{2}({\cal C})\cong\mbox{sVec}
. We define a premodular category to be a braided fusion category equipped with a ribbon structure. The ribbon and braided structures are sets of isomorphisms (
56
) and
\theta_{x}:x\xrightarrow{\sim}x
subject to (
57
).

There are some useful types of categories with less structure than premodular categories.
A
pivotal category
is a monoidal category with duals equipped with a morphism
\epsilon(x):\mathbf{1}\mapsto x\otimes x^{\vee}
for each object
x
with
x^{\vee}
being the dual of
x
. The set of morphisms is subject to the conditions listed in
Barrett:1993zf
. Finally, right and left traces are maps defined for any object
x
and morphism
f:x\mapsto x
as
{\rm Tr}_{R}[f]\equiv\epsilon(x^{\vee})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes f)\circ(\epsilon(x^{\vee}))^{\vee}
and
{\rm Tr}_{L}[f]\equiv\epsilon(x)\circ(f\otimes\mathbf{1})\circ(\epsilon(x))^{\vee}
respectively. Note these are in
{\rm End}(\textbf{1})\cong\mathbb{C}
and hence can be identified with complex numbers. A
spherical category
is a pivotal category where left and right traces are the same for all objects
Barrett:1993zf
.
Returning to premodular categories,
an alternative definition to the one given above, one could define a
premodular category as a spherical braided fusion category.

In this paper, we are interested in unitary categories coming from CS theories. Any unitary category is, in particular, pseudo-unitary. We refer the reader to, e.g.,
davydov2010Witt
for the definition of pseudo-unitary categories. It is expected that the notions of unitarity and pseudo-unitarity are equivalent
Kong:2022cpy
. We use the term pseudo-unitary to be consistent with the math literature keeping in mind the conjectured equivalence.

One of the results of
Etingof:2005
states that any pseudo-unitary fusion category admits a unique spherical structure. Next, for any braided fusion category, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the spherical and ribbon structures
Yetter:1992
. Therefore, unitary braided fusion categories, the categories of our interest, are premodular in a canonical way.

For any premodular category, we define the modular S-matrix:

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{x,y}={\rm Tr}[c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}]\,

(77)

where
x
and
y
are simple objects of
{\cal C}
and
{\rm Tr}
is the trace corresponding to the ribbon structure. The non-degeneracy of the S-matrix is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of the braiding.

Let
{\cal C}

Simple deterministic 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log n) algorithm finding a solution of Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem.
Seokhwan Choi, Hanpil Kang, Dongjae Lim

Abstract
Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem is a famous theorem in additive number theory, which states any sequence of 2 ​ n − 1 2n-1 integers contains a subsequence of n n elements, with their sum being a multiple of n n . In this article, we provide a deterministic algorithm finding a solution of Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem in 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log n) time. This is the first known deterministic 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log n) time algorithm finding a solution of Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem.

Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem and its proof

Let

be a proposition which states for any sequence of
2n-1

A=\{{a}_{1},{a}_{2},\cdots,{a}_{2n-1}\}

holds for any positive integer

.

We give an elementary proof of this theorem based on the original work of Erdős, Ginzburg, and Ziv.

First, we will handle the case where
n
is prime.

Theorem 1
.

EGZ(p)
holds for prime
p
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{2p-1}
be sorted sequence of
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{2p-1}
according to their remainder divided by
p
. In other words, for some permutation
\pi
of
\{1,2,\cdots,2p-1\}
,
a_{\pi(i)}=b_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq 2p-1
) and
(b_{j}\bmod p)\leq(b_{j+1}\bmod p)
(
1\leq j\leq 2p-2
). Here, remainder of

divided by

is denoted as
x\bmod y
, and
0\leq(x\bmod y)<y
.

If index
i
(
1\leq i\leq p-1
) exists such that
b_{i}\equiv b_{i+p}\pmod{p}
, then
b_{i}\equiv b_{i+1}\equiv\cdots\equiv b_{i+p-1}\pmod{p}
. Therefore
\{b_{i},b_{i+1},\cdots,b_{i+p-1}\}
is subset of
a
, with length
p
and sum of elements in subsequence is divisible by
p
.

If such index does not exist, the difference
d_{i}=b_{i+p}-b_{i}
is not multiple of
p
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
. We will consider submultisets of
A
in the form of
\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{p}\}
with
c_{j}\in\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}
for
1\leq j\leq p-1
and
c_{p}=b_{p}
. We can find a submultiset whose sum is multiple of
p
among these subsets.

We will define a sequence of set
S_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq p
) as all possible remainders of sum of elements for the subsets, which follows the above constraints but
c_{j}=b_{j}
for
j\geq i
.
S_{i}
can be written as follows:

S_{i}=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\right)\bmod p\ \middle|\ c_{j}\in\begin{array}[]{ll}\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}&j<i\\
\{b_{j}\}&j\geq i\end{array}\right\}

We will find recursive formula for
S_{i}
. Here, we denote
X\oplus_{p}Y=\{(x+y)\bmod p\mid x\in X,y\in Y\}
.

Lemma 1
.

For
2\leq i\leq p
,
S_{i}=S_{i-1}\oplus_{p}\{0,d_{i-1}\}

\displaystyle S_{i}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\right)\bmod p\ \middle|\ c_{j}\in\begin{array}[]{ll}\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}&j<i\\
\{b_{j}\}&j\geq i\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\right)\bmod p\ \middle|\ c_{j}\in\begin{array}[]{ll}\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}&j<i-1\\
\{b_{j}\}&j=i-1\\
\{b_{j}\}&j\geq i\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\cup\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\right)\bmod p\ \middle|\ c_{j}\in\begin{array}[]{ll}\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}&j<i-1\\
\{b_{j+p}\}&j=i-1\\
\{b_{j}\}&j\geq i\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=S_{i-1}\cup\left\{\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\right)+b_{i+p-1}-b_{i-1}\right)\bmod p\ \middle|\ c_{j}\in\begin{array}[]{ll}\{b_{j},b_{j+p}\}&j<i-1\\
\{b_{j}\}&j=i-1\\
\{b_{j}\}&j\geq i\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle=S_{i-1}\cup(S_{i-1}\oplus_{p}\{b_{i+p-1}-b_{i-1}\})=S_{i-1}\oplus_{p}\{0,d_{i-1}\}

∎

Now, we will prove that
\lvert S_{i}\rvert
is strictly increasing until it becomes

Lemma 2
.

For non-empty subset
S
of
\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
and integer

p

S\subsetneq S\oplus_{p}\{0,a\}

S=S\oplus_{p}\{0\}
, so it is trivial that
S\subset S+_{p}\{0,a\}
and it is sufficient to prove

.

If
S=S\oplus_{p}\{0,a\}
,
s\in S\rightarrow(s+a)\bmod p\in S+_{p}\{0,a\}\rightarrow(s+a)\bmod p\in S
.

S
is non-trivial subset of
\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
, so there exists

such that
x\in S
and
y\not\in S
. For positive

and
S\subsetneq S+_{p}\{0,a\}
. ∎

Lemma 3
.

For
1\leq i\leq p
,
|S_{i}|\geq i
.

We will use mathematical induction on
i
.

For base case
i=1
,
S_{i}=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_{j}\right)\bmod p\right\}
, so
|S_{1}|=1\geq 1
.

For inductive case

\lvert S_{i-1}\rvert=p
, then

.

If

. So
|S_{i}|\geq|S_{i-1}|+1\geq(i-1)+1=i
. ∎

|S_{p}|\geq p

and

. This implies

, and proves existence of subsequence of length

, whose sum is divisible by
p
. ∎

Now, we will handle the case where

Theorem 2
.

EGZ(p)\wedge EGZ(q)\rightarrow EGZ(pq)

We will find
2p-1
disjoint subsets

of

with
|B_{i}|=q
and sum of elements in

is multiple of

.

is a subset of size

. If

,
q(2p-(i-1))-1\geq 2q-1
, so
EGZ(q)
can be used to find

.

We will construct sequence

of length
2p-1
, where
s_{i}=\frac{\sum B_{i}}{q}
. By applying
EGZ(p)
, we can select distinct
p
indices
k_{1},\cdots,k_{p}
where
p\mid\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_{k_{i}}
.
\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}B_{k_{i}}
is subset with size
pq
and
\sum\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}B_{k_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}B_{k_{i}}=q\sum_{i=1}^{p}s_{k_{i}}
, which is multiple of
pq
. ∎

Now, we will prove Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem.

Theorem 3
.

EGZ(n)
holds for any positive integer
n
.

We will use strong mathematical induction on
n
. For base case,
EGZ(1)
holds since we can select whole sequence as subsequence. Length of such subsequence is
1
and every number is divisible by

.

For inductive case

n
is prime,

holds by Theorem 1. if

is composite (

)
EGZ(a)
and

holds by induction hypothesis, and
EGZ(ab)
holds by Theorem 2. ∎

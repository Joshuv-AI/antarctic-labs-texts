Abstract
. In this paper, we study the relations between trace inequalities(Sobolev and Moser-Trudinger type), isocapacitary inequalities and the regularity of the complex Hessian and Monge-Ampère equations with respect to a general positive Borel measure. We obtain a quantitative characterization for these relations through properties of the capacity minimizing functions.



1.
introduction

Sobolev and Moser-Trudinger type inequalities play an important role in both PDE and geometry. On one hand, these inequalities are widely used in the study of existence and regularity of solutions to partial differential equations. On the other hand, people also discovered that they are equivalent to isoperimetric and isocapacitary inequalities
[
Ma
]
. Despite the classical Sobolev and Moser-Trudinger inequalities, the analogous inequalities for a series of fully nonlinear equations with variational structure have been developed, including both real and complex Hessian equations
[
W1
,
TrW
,
TiW
,
AC20
]
. In particular, the Moser-Trudinger type inequality for the complex Monge-Ampère equations has been established
[
BB
,
C19
,
GKY
,
AC19
,
WWZ1
]
.
The Moser-Trudinger type inequality can also be related to the Skoda integrability of plurisubharmonic functions
[
DNS
,
DN
,
Ka
,
DMV
]
.
In this paper, we study the relations between trace inequalities(Sobolev and Moser-Trudinger type), isocapacitary inequalities and the regularity of the complex Hessian and Monge-Ampère equations with
respect to a general nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
. Our results generalize the classical trace inequalities
[
AH
]
. Here ‘trace’ refers to that
\mu
lives on a domain
\Omega
and is a surface measure on a smooth submanifold in
\Omega
. The trace and isocapacitary inequalities for the real Hessian equations were obtained by
[
XZ
]
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be a pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\omega
be the Kähler form associated to the standard Euclidean metric.
Let
\mathcal{PSH}_{k}(\Omega)
be the
k
-plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
and
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
be the set of functions in
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)

with vanishing boundary value. Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}(\Omega)
be set of
k
-plurisubharmonic functions which can be decreasingly approximated by functions in
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}(\Omega)

[
C06
]
. For
u\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(\Omega)
,
denote the
k
-Hessian energy by

\mathcal{E}_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

and the norm by

\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}.

In particular, when
k=n
, we write
\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{0}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{n,0}(\Omega)}
for simplicity.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Borel measure with finite mass on
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
We will consider the trace inequalities and
isocapacitary inequalities with respect to
\mu
as in the classical case
[
AH
]
. We recall the capacity for plurisubharmonic functions.
The relative capacity for plurisubharmonic functions was introduced by Bedford-Taylor
[
BT
,
B
]
.
For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
, the
k
-capacity
is defined as

\text{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\left.\int_{E}(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\,\right|\,v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k}(\Omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

Throughout the context, we will use
|\cdot|
to denote the Lebesgue measure of a Borel subset.
We follow
[
Ma
]
to define the
capacity minimizing function
with respect to
\mu

\nu_{k}(s,\Omega,\mu):=\inf\left\{\left.\text{Cap}_{k}(K,\Omega)\,\right|\,\mu(K)\geq s,K\Subset\Omega\right\},\ \ \ 0<s<\mu(\Omega).

Then we denote

(1.1)

\displaystyle I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu):=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\mu(\Omega)}\left(\frac{s}{\nu_{k}(s,\Omega,\mu)}\right)^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}\,ds,&0<p<k+1,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{\frac{t}{\nu_{k}(t,\Omega,\mu)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}}\right\},&p\geq k+1,\end{cases}

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\beta,\Omega,\mu):=\sup\left\{s\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{\nu_{n}(s,\Omega,\mu)^{\frac{q}{n+1}}}\right):\ 0<s<\mu(\Omega)\right\}.

Note that if
\mu(P)>0
for a
k
-pluripolar subset
P\subset\Omega
, then
I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
I_{n}(\beta,\Omega,\mu)=+\infty
. Therefore, we only need to consider those measures which charge no mass on pluripolar subsets.
The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is a smooth,
k
-pseudoconvex domain, where
1\leq k\leq n
.

(i)

The Sobolev type trace inequality

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\frac{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)}}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}}:u\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(\Omega),0<\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\}<+\infty

holds if and only if
I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)<+\infty
. Moreover, the Sobolev trace map

Id:\,{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega,\mu),\ 1<p<\infty

is a compact embedding if and only if

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{s}{\nu_{k}(s,\Omega,\mu)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}}\to 0.

(ii)

When
k=n
, for
q\in[1,\frac{n+1}{n}]
and
\beta>0
, the Moser-Trudinger type trace inequality

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\beta\left(\frac{-u}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{0}(\Omega)}}\right)^{q}\right)\,d\mu:u\in\mathcal{F}_{n}(\Omega),0<\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{0}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\}

holds if and only if
I_{n}(\beta,\Omega,\mu)<+\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

(a) For
p\geq k+1
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mu(K)\leq I_{n}(\beta,\Omega,\mu)\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\beta}{\text{Cap}_{n}(K,\Omega)^{\frac{q}{n+1}}}\right)

for

.
By

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq

(1.9)

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\beta,n,\Omega}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\beta}{\text{Cap}_{n}(E,\Omega)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right),\ 0<\beta<2n.

Let

(i)

There exist

such that for any Borel subset

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\chi_{E}\,d\mu,\ \ \ &\text{in }\Omega,\\

admits a continuous solution

\displaystyle\|u_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq C\mu(E)^{\delta}.

(ii)

(1) The conclusion from (ii) to (i) in the above theorem also holds with general continuous boundary value.

(2) When

2.
On relatively capacities and relatively extremal functions

In this section, we recall the relative capacity for plurisubharmonic functions
[
BT
,
B
]
.

For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
, the
k
-capacity
is defined as

\text{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\left.\int_{E}(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\,\right|\,v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k}(\Omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

It is well known that the
k
-capacity can be characterized by
the
relatively
k
-extremal function

u_{k,E,\Omega}^{*}
, which is the upper regularization of

u_{k,E,\Omega}:=\sup\left\{v\,\left|v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k}(\Omega),v\leq-1\text{ on }E,v<0\text{ on }\Omega\right.\right\}.

We will usually write
u_{k,E}^{*}
for simplicity if there is no confusion.
When
E=K
is a compact subset, we have
-1\leq u_{k,K}^{*}\leq 0
, the complex Hessian measure
(dd^{c}u_{K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0
on
\Omega\setminus K
. Moreover,
\text{Cap}_{k}(K,\Omega)=0
if
u_{k,K}^{*}>-1
on
K
.
The following well-known fact shows
u_{k,K}^{*}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
K\subset\Omega
is a compact subset,
we have

u_{k,K}^{*}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

Proof.

By
[
KR
, Proposition 1.2]
, there exists an exhaustion function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
.
By the maximum principle we have
-a:=\sup_{K}\psi<0
. Let
\hat{\psi}:=\frac{\psi}{a}\leq-\chi_{K}
, then we get
\hat{\psi}\leq u_{k,K}^{*}
on
\Omega
. By the fact
\hat{\psi}(\xi)\to 0
as
\xi\to z\in\partial\Omega
, we get the result.
∎

Suppose
\Omega
is
k
-hyperconvex so that
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
is non-empty.
Inspired by
[
XZ
]
for the real Hessian equations, we consider several capacities defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

(i) Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Omega
. Define

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\left.\int_{K}(-v)(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\,\right|\,v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,2}(K,\Omega):=\inf\left\{\left.\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\,\right|\,v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega),v\big|_{K}\leq-1\right\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,3}(K,\Omega):=\inf\left\{\left.\int_{\Omega}(-v)(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\,\right|\,v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega),v\big|_{K}\leq-1\right\}.

(ii) For an open subset
O\subset\Omega
and
j=1
,
2
,
3
, let

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,j}(O,\Omega):=\sup\left\{\left.\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,j}(K,\Omega)\,\right|\,\text{compact }K\subset O\right\}.

(iii) For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
and
j=1
,
2
,
3
, let

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,j}(E,\Omega):=\inf\left\{\left.\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,j}(O,\Omega)\,\right|\,\text{open $O$ with }E\subset O\subset\Omega\right\}.

In order to show the equivalence of capacities defined above, we need the following well-known comparison principle.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a
k
-hyperconvex domain with
C^{1}
-boundary.
Let
u
,
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)
. If
\lim_{z\to\xi\in\partial\Omega}(u-v)\geq 0
,
u\leq v
in
\Omega
, then there hold

\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\geq\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},\ \ \ \int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\geq\int_{\Omega}(-v)(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of the integration by parts and the smooth approximation for functions in
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
1\leq k\leq n
, and
K\subset\Omega
is a compact subset. Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega)=\int_{K}(-u_{k,K}^{*})(dd^{c}u_{k,K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Proof.

First, by definition we have

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega)\geq\int_{K}(-u_{k,K}^{*})(dd^{c}u_{k,K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

To reach the reversed inequality,
we choose
\{K_{j}\}
to be a sequence of compact subsets of
\Omega
with smooth boundaries
\partial K_{j}
such that

K_{j+1}\subset K_{j},\ \ \ \bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}K_{j}=K.

Using the smoothness of
\partial K_{j}
, the relatively extremal function

u_{j}:=u_{k,K_{j}}^{*}=u_{k,K_{j}}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.
Note that
u_{j}\uparrow v
and
u_{k,K}^{*}=v^{*}
.
Then for
u\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
such that
-1\leq u\leq 0
, we have
u\geq u_{j}
on
K_{j}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{u_{j}\leq u\}}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{u_{j}\leq u\}}(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{K_{j}}(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Taking
j\to\infty
, we obtain

\int_{K}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{\Omega}(-u_{k,K}^{*})(dd^{c}u_{k,K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{K}(-u_{k,K}^{*})(dd^{c}u_{k,K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

This yields

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega)\leq\int_{K}(-u_{K}^{*})(dd^{c}u_{K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

thereby completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For any Borel set
E\subset\Omega
, we have

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,j}(E,\Omega)=\text{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega),\ \ \ j=1,2,3.

Proof.

By definition, it suffices to prove the equalities when
E=K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.
Note that for
u
,
v\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
such that
-1\leq u\leq 0
,
v\big|_{K}\leq-1
, we have

\int_{K}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{K}(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{K}(dd^{c}u_{k,K}^{*})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Hence we obtain

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega)\leq\text{Cap}_{k}(K,\Omega)\leq\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,2}(K,\Omega).

It suffices to prove

(2.5)

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,2}(K,\Omega)\leq\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,3}(K,\Omega)\leq\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K,\Omega).

We still choose
\{K_{j}\}
to be a sequence of compact subsets in
\Omega
with smooth boundaries
\partial K_{j}
such that

K_{j+1}\subset K_{j},\ \ \ \bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}K_{j}=K.

Then
u_{j}:=u_{k,K_{j}}^{*}=u_{k,K_{j}}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,
u_{j}\uparrow v
with
v^{*}=u_{k,K}^{*}
, and
u_{j}\big|_{K_{j}}\equiv-1
. For any
j
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,2}(K,\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{\Omega}(-v_{j})(dd^{c}v_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

where
v_{j}
is an arbitrary function in
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
such that
v_{j}\big|_{K_{j}}\leq-1
. This implies
\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,2}(K,\Omega)\leq\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,3}(K_{j},\Omega)
. Then by Lemma
2.3
, we have

\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,3}(K_{j+1},\Omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{k,1}(K_{j},\Omega)=\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{j},\Omega).

Letting
j\to\infty
, (
2.5
) holds by the convergence of
\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{j},\Omega)
and the weak continuity of
(-u_{j})(dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
.
∎

3.
Capacitary estimates for level sets of plurisubharmonic functions

In this section, we establish a capacitary estimate for level sets of
k
-plurisubharmonic functions, which will play
an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The analogous estimate for the real Hessian equation was obtained by
[
XZ
]
. This estimate is a generalization of the capacitary estimates for the Wiener capacity
[
AH
, Chaper 7]
.

First, we recall the
capacitary weak type inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle t^{k+1}\text{Cap}_{k}(\left\{z\in\Omega\,\left|\,u(z)\leq-t\right.\right\},\Omega)\leq\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1},\ \ \ \forall t>0.

This inequality was proved by
[
ACKZ
]
for
k=n
, and by
[
Lu
]
for general
k
. We prove the
capacitary strong type inequality
as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
u\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
. For any
A>1
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\text{Cap}_{k}(\left\{z\in\Omega\,\left|\,u<-t\right.\right\},\Omega)\,dt\leq\left(\frac{A}{A-1}\right)^{k+1}\log A\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}.

Proof.

We use similar arguments as
[
Ma
]
for the Laplacian and
[
XZ
]
for the real Hessian case.
For
t>0
, denote

K_{t}:=\left\{z\in\Omega\,\left|\,u(z)\leq-t\right.\right\},\ \Omega_{t}:=\left\{z\in\Omega\,\left|\,u(z)<-t\right.\right\}

and
v_{t}:=u_{k,K_{t}}^{*}
. For a Borel subset
E\subset\Omega
, we denote

\phi(E):=\frac{\int_{E}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}.

For
A>1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At})\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(K_{t}\setminus K_{At})\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{At}\frac{d\phi(K_{s})}{ds}\,ds\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{s}^{\frac{s}{A}}\frac{dt}{t}\right)\frac{d\phi(K_{s})}{ds}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log A\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\phi(K_{s})}{ds}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\phi(K_{t})\cdot\log A

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log A.

This implies

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|u\cdot\chi_{\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At}}\right\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At}}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right)\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}\log A.

Then for
\forall t>0
, we consider

u^{t}:=\frac{u+t}{(A-1)t},\ \ \ \tilde{u}^{t}:=\max\{u^{t},-1\}.

It is clear that
u^{t},\tilde{u}^{t}\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega_{t})\cap C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega}_{t})
and
\tilde{u}^{t}=-1
on
K_{At}
.
We have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{t}\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega_{t})}^{k+1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}(-\tilde{u}^{t})(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}d\tilde{u}^{t}\wedge d^{c}\tilde{u}^{t}\wedge(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At}}d\tilde{u}^{t}\wedge d^{c}\tilde{u}^{t}\wedge(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At}}\left(-\frac{u}{(A-1)t}\right)(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A-1)^{-k-1}t^{-k-1}\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At}}\left(-u\right)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

where we have used
\frac{\partial\tilde{u}^{t}}{\partial\nu}\geq 0
almost everywhere on
\partial K_{At}
for
t>0
at the fourth line. That is,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}(-\tilde{u}^{t})(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq(A-1)^{-k-1}t^{-k-1}\phi(\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At})\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}.

Now we denote by
\hat{v}_{t}:=u_{k,K_{At},\Omega_{t}}^{*}
the relatively extremal function of
K_{At}
with respect to
\Omega_{t}
. Note that
\hat{v}_{t}\geq\tilde{u}^{t}
in
\Omega_{t}
, and
\hat{v}_{t}=\tilde{u}^{t}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{t}
. By comparison principle, we have

\displaystyle\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{At},\Omega_{t})=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{At}}(-\hat{v}_{t})(dd^{c}\hat{v}_{t})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\int_{\Omega_{t}}(-\tilde{u}^{t})(dd^{c}\tilde{u}^{t})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(A-1)^{-k-1}t^{-k-1}\phi(\Omega_{t}\setminus K_{At})\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}.

Finally, by (
3.3
), (
3
) with
\lambda=At
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{\lambda},\Omega)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A^{k+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{At},\Omega_{t})\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A^{k+1}(A-1)^{-k-1}\log A\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{k+1}.

∎

In this section, we are going to prove the trace inequalities in Theorem

4.1.
The Sobolev type trace inequality(the case
0<p<k+1
).

First, we show
I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)<+\infty

implies the Sobolev type inequality.
For
u\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(\Omega)
, denote

S_{k,p}(\mu,u):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{[\mu(K_{2^{j}}^{u})-\mu(K_{2^{j+1}}^{u})]^{\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}}}{\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{2^{j}}^{u},\Omega)^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}},\ \ \ K_{s}^{u}:=\{u\leq-s\}.

By the elementary inequality
a^{c}+b^{c}\leq(a+b)^{c}
for
a
,
b\geq 0
and
c\geq 1
, we get

\displaystyle S_{k,p}(\mu,u)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{[\mu(K_{2^{j}}^{u})-\mu(K_{2^{j+1}}^{u})]^{\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}}}{[\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)]^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{[\mu(K_{2^{j}}^{u})]^{\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}}-[\mu(K_{2^{j+1}}^{u})]^{\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}}}{[\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)]^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[\nu_{k}(s,\Omega,\mu)]^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}}\,d\left(s^{\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}}\right)=\frac{k+1}{k+1-p}I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu).

Then by the strong capacitary inequality (
3.2
) with
A=n
and by integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u)^{p}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{[0,-u]}(s^{p})\,d\left(s^{p}\right)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(K_{s}^{u})d\left(s^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p}\,d\mu(K_{s}^{u})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{(j+1)p}\cdot[\mu(K_{2^{j}}^{u})-\mu(K_{2^{j+1}}^{u})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{S_{k,p}(\mu,u)}^{\frac{k+1-p}{k+1}}\cdot\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{j(k+1)}\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{2^{j(k+1)}}^{u},\Omega)\right)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(k+1){S_{k,p}(\mu,u)}^{\frac{k+1-p}{k+1}}\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k}\text{Cap}(K_{s}^{u},\Omega)\,ds\right)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,k,\mu,p)\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{p},

thereby completing the proof.∎

On the other hand, suppose there exists
p\in(0,k+1)
such that the inequality (
1.3
) holds.
That is, for any
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)

\sup_{t>0}\{t\mu(K_{t}^{u})^{\frac{1}{p}}\}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}.

By the definition of
\nu_{k}
, for any integer
j<\frac{\log\mu(\Omega)}{\log 2}
, we can choose a compact subset
K_{j}\subset\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\mu(K_{j})\geq 2^{j},\ \text{Cap}_{k}(K_{j},\Omega)\leq 2\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu).

Then by Lemma
2.5
, we can choose
u_{j}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle u_{j}\big|_{K_{j}}\leq-1,\ {\mathcal{E}}_{k}(u_{j})\leq 2\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{j},\Omega).

Then for integers
i
,
m
with
-\infty<i<m
and
\frac{\log\mu(\Omega)}{\log 2}\in(m,m+1]
, let

\gamma_{j}:=\left(\frac{2^{j}}{\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)}\right)^{\frac{1}{k+1-p}},\ \ \forall j\in[i,m]\text{\ and \ }u_{i,m}:=\sup_{i\leq j\leq m}\{\gamma_{j}u_{j}\}.

Since
u_{i,m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(u_{i,m})\leq C_{n,k}\sum_{i}^{m}\gamma_{j}^{k+1}{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(u_{j})\leq C_{n,k}\sum_{i}^{m}\gamma_{j}^{k+1}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu).

Note that for
i\leq j\leq m
we have

2^{j}<\mu(K_{j})\leq\mu(K_{\gamma_{j}}^{u_{i,m}}).

This implies

\displaystyle\|u_{i,m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-p}C_{n,k,p}\int_{\Omega}|u_{i,m}|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\inf\left\{t\,\left|\,\mu(K_{t}^{u_{i,m}})\leq s\right.\right\}\right)^{p}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j=i}^{m}\left(\inf\left\{t\,\left|\,\mu(K_{t}^{u_{i,m}})\leq 2^{j}\right.\right\}\right)^{p}\cdot 2^{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\sum_{j=i}^{m}\gamma_{j}^{p}2^{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\frac{\sum_{j=i}^{m}\gamma_{j}^{p}2^{j}}{\sum_{j=i}^{m}\gamma_{j}^{k+1}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}}\|u_{i,m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\frac{\sum_{j=i}^{m}2^{\frac{j(k+1)}{k+1-p}}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{-\frac{p}{k+1-p}}}{\left(\sum_{j=i}^{m}2^{\frac{j(k+1)}{k+1-p}}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{-\frac{p}{k+1-p}}\right)^{\frac{p}{k+1}}}\|u_{i,m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{j=i}^{m}2^{\frac{j(k+1)}{k+1-p}}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{-\frac{p}{k+1-p}}\right)^{\frac{k+1-p}{k+1}}\|u_{i,m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to-\infty}\sum_{j=i}^{m}2^{\frac{(j+1)(k+1)}{k+1-p}}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{-\frac{p}{k+1-p}}+\int_{2^{m}}^{\mu(\Omega)}\left(\frac{s}{\nu_{k}(s,\Omega,\mu)}\right)^{\frac{p}{k+1-p}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\mu(\Omega))\lim_{i\to-\infty}\sum_{j=i}^{m}2^{\frac{(j+1)(k+1)}{k+1-p}}\nu_{k}(2^{j},\Omega,\mu)^{-\frac{p}{k+1-p}}<+\infty.\qed

4.2.
The Sobolev type trace inequality(the case
p\geq k+1
).

First, we assume
I_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)<+\infty
and prove the Sobolev type inequality.
For

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u)^{p}\,d\mu=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(K_{t})t^{p-1}\,dt

\displaystyle pI_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)\int_{0}^{\infty}{\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{k+1}}t^{p-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle pI_{k,p}(\Omega,\mu)\cdot\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{p-1-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k}\text{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega)\,dt

u=u_{k,K}^{*}
the relatively capacitary potential with respect to a compact set

(c),

4.3.
Compactness

In this section, we are going to consider the compactness of the embedding induced by inequalities (
1.3
).
First, we recall the Poincaré type inequality for complex Hessian operators.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Hou
,
AC20
]

Suppose
\Omega
is a pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary, and
1\leq l<k\leq n
. Then there exists a uniform constant
C>0

depending on
k
,
l
and
\Omega
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{l,0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)},\ \ \ \forall u\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega).

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
with smooth boundary
\partial K
, and
1\leq l<k\leq n
. Then we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{Cap}_{l}(K,\Omega)\leq C\cdot{\text{Cap}_{k}(K,\Omega)}^{\frac{l+1}{k+1}}.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)
such that
u\leq-\chi_{K}
. By
\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{PSH}_{l,0}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Cap}_{l}(K,\Omega)=\widetilde{\text{Cap}}_{l,3}(K,\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{l}\wedge\omega^{n-l}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\int_{\Omega}(-u)(dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\right]^{\frac{l+1}{k+1}}.

Then (
4.2
) follows by taking the infimum.
∎

Now, we are in position to show the compactness of the Sobolev trace inequality.
First we need the following compactness theorem for classical Sobolev embedding.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
Ma
]

Suppose
p\geq 2
, the embedding

Id:\,W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)

is compact if and only if

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{s}{\nu_{1}(s,\mu)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\to 0.

By Corollary
4.2
, condition (
1.4
) implies (
4.3
).

Proof of compactness in Theorem
1.1
(i).

First, we consider the ’if’ part.
For a sequence
u_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(\Omega)
with bounded

.

, we can find a subsequence
\{u_{j_{l}}\}

converges to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{j_{l}}-u|^{p}\,d\mu\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{j_{l}}-u|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{j_{l}}-u|^{\frac{3p-2}{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{j_{l}}-u\|_{L^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(\Omega,\mu)}\cdot(\|u_{j_{l}}\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3p-2}{4}}+\|u\|^{\frac{3p-2}{4}}_{\mathcal{PSH}_{k,0}(\Omega)})\to 0,

For the ’only if’ part, we assume

is compactly embedded into

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\lambda\left(\frac{-u}{{\mathcal{M}}[u]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{\frac{nq}{n+1}}\right)\,d\mu:u\in\mathcal{F}_{n}(\Omega),0<\|u\|_{\mathcal{PSH}_{0}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\}<+\infty

Therefore,

Then for every

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}e^{(\lambda-\varepsilon)\frac{(-u)^{\frac{nq}{n+1}}}{{\mathcal{M}}[u]^{\frac{q}{n+1}}}}\,d\mu

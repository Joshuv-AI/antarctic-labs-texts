On the maximum principle for general linear elliptic equations. Thanks: Research supported by Australian Research Council Grants (DP170100929, DP230100499)
Neil S. Trudinger

Abstract
. We consider maximum principles and related estimates for linear second order elliptic partial differential operators in n-dimensional Euclidean space, which improve previous results, with H-J Kuo, through sharp L p L^{p} dependence on the drift coefficient b b . As in our previous work, the ellipticity is determined through the principal coefficient matrix 𝒜 \mathcal{A} lying in sub-cones of the positive cone, which are dual cones of the Gårding k k -cones, for k = 1 , ⋯ , n k=1,\cdots,n . Our main results are maximum principles for bounded domains, which extend those of Aleksandrov in the case k = n k=n , together with extensions to unbounded domains, depending on appropriate integral norms of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , and corresponding local maximum principles. We also consider applications to local estimates in the uniformly elliptic case, including extensions of the Krylov-Safonov Hölder and Harnack estimates.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with maximum principles for general linear second order partial differential elliptic operators of the form,

(1.1)

Lu=\mathcal{A}.D^{2}u+b.Du=a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b_{i}D_{i}u,

where
\mathcal{A}=[a^{ij]}
is a positive measurable mapping from a domain
\Omega
in Euclidean
n
-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the linear space of
n\times n
real symmetric matrices, and
b=(b_{1},\cdots b_{n})
is a measurable mapping from
\Omega
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, we improve the corresponding results of Kuo and Trudinger in
[
7
]
, with respect to the conditions assumed on the drift coefficient
b
, together with their applications to corresponding improvements of the Pucci conjecture bounds in
[
15
]
.

In accordance with
[
7
]
, the crucial algebraic quantities in our maximum principles are the elementary symmetric polynomials
S_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

(1.2)

=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad k=1,\cdots,n,

and their associated cones,

(1.3)

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ S_{j}[\lambda]>0,\ \forall j=1,\cdots,k\},

and closed dual cones,

(1.4)

\bar{\Gamma}^{*}_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\ \lambda.\mu\geq 0,\ \forall\mu\in\Gamma_{k}\}.

Normalised dual functions
\rho^{*}_{k}
are defined on
\bar{\Gamma}^{*}_{k}
as follows:

(1.5)

\rho^{*}_{k}(\lambda)=\inf\{\frac{\lambda.\mu}{n}\ |\ \mu\in\Gamma_{k},\ S_{k}(\mu)\geq\binom{n}{k}\}.

As with the corresponding normalised elementary symmetric functions
\rho_{k}
on
\bar{\Gamma}_{k}
, given by

\rho_{k}=\{S_{k}(\lambda)/\binom{n}{k}\}^{1/k}
, the dual functions
\rho^{*}_{k}
are nondecreasing along rays and concave.

Our dual cones, corresponding to the open cones
\Gamma_{k}
, are then given by

(1.6)

\Gamma^{*}_{k}:=\{\lambda\in\bar{\Gamma}^{*}_{k}|\rho^{*}_{k}(\lambda)>0\}

Note that it is readily shown by approximation that
\bar{\Gamma}^{*}_{k}
is indeed the closure of
\Gamma^{*}_{k}
and that
from the concavity of
\rho_{k}
, it also follows that
\Gamma^{*}_{k}
is open in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
k>1
; (see Remark 1.1).

Clearly,
\Gamma_{k}\subset\Gamma_{l}
for
k\geq l
and
\Gamma_{1}
is the half-space
\{\sum\lambda_{i}>0\}
, while
\Gamma_{n}
is the positive cone. Consequently the dual cones
\Gamma^{*}_{k}\subset\Gamma^{*}_{l}
for
k\leq l
, with
\Gamma^{*}_{1}
the open ray through
(1,\ldots,1)
and
\Gamma^{*}_{n}=\Gamma_{n}
.
Note also that our notation here differs from that in
[
7
]
, where we have used
\Gamma^{*}_{k}
to denote the closed cone
\bar{\Gamma}^{*}_{k}
.

Next we say that a symmetric
n\times n
matrix
A\in\Gamma_{k},(\Gamma^{*}_{k},\bar{\Gamma}_{k},\bar{\Gamma}_{k})
if its eigenvalues
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k},(\Gamma^{*}_{k},\bar{\Gamma}_{k},\bar{\Gamma}_{k})
and define
\rho^{*}_{k}(A)=\rho^{*}_{k}(\lambda)
. It follows that
\rho^{*}_{k}(A)\geq\rho^{*}_{l}(A)
for
k\geq l
,
A\in\Gamma^{*}_{k}
while

\rho^{*}_{n}A)=(\text{det}A)^{1/n}
and
\rho^{*}_{1}(A)=\lambda_{min}(A)
, where

\lambda_{min}(A)
denotes the minimum eigenvalue of
A
. Expressing the eigenvalues
\lambda(\mathcal{A})=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
in non-decreasing order, so that
\lambda_{min}=\lambda_{1}
, we also have the following estimate, for
\mathcal{A}\in\Gamma^{*}_{k}
:

(1.7)

\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\leq\frac{k}{n}{\binom{n}{k}}^{1/k}(\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{k})^{1/k},

with equality in the cases
k=1
and
k=n
. To prove (
1.7
), we fix a vector
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
in the definition (
1.5
) by setting

\mu_{i}={\binom{n}{k}}^{1/k}(\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{k})^{1/k}\lambda^{-1}_{i},

for
i=1,\dots k
, and
\mu_{i}=0
for
i=k+1,\dots n
.

Remark 1.1
.

We remark here that the inequality (
1.7
) will also be sharp for
1<k<n
, as equality will hold there along the ray passing through the point where
\lambda_{i}=1
, for
i\leq k
and
\lambda_{i}=k
, for
i>k
. This can be proved by showing that the above choice of
\mu
will then be the unique critical point for the associated minimisation problem, which is a consequence of the concavity of
\rho_{k}
. Similarly we can use the uniqueness of an optimal
\mu=\mu(\lambda)
in (
1.5
) for
\lambda\in\Gamma^{*}_{k}
to verify the openness of
\Gamma_{k}^{*}
.

The following maximum principle, for bounded
\Omega
, now extends that in Theorem 1.1 of
[
7
]
to general linear operators
L
, in the cases,
k\geq n/2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
) with coefficients
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\subset\Gamma^{*}_{k}
,
b/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for
k\geq n/2
and some
p>n
, (
\geq n
if
k=n
). Then if
u\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
,
(Lu)^{-}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, for
q\geq k
if
k>n/2
and
q>n/2
if
k=n/2
, we have the estimate,

(1.8)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u+C(\text{diam}\Omega)^{2-n/q}||\frac{(Lu)^{-}}{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})}||_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

where
C
is a constant depending on
n,k,p,q
and
(\text{diam}\Omega)^{1-n/p}||b/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

The case
k=n
is the well known maximum principle, due originally to Aleksandrov for operators in the general form (
1.1
); (see for example
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
).

By adapting the proof of Corollary 4.2 in
[
7
]
, we can obtain, from the proof of Theorem 1.1, the following variant, where the dependence on the diameter of
\Omega
is replaced by that on the
L_{q}
norm of
\mathcal{A}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})
, thereby providing an extension to unbounded domains. To fit better with later applications, we will express our assumed bounds on
\mathcal{A}
in terms of
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A}):=\text{trace}\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume additionally in Theorem 1.1, that
k>n/2
and
\mathcal{A}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\in L^{k}(\Omega)
.
Then we have the estimate

(1.9)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u+CR^{2-n/q}||\frac{(Lu)^{-}}{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})}||_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

where
R=||\mathcal{T}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})||^{k/n}_{L^{k}(\Omega)}
and
C
is a constant depending on
n,k,p
and
R^{1-n/p}||b/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

We will consider the cases,
k<n/2
in Theorem 1.1 and
k\leq n/2
in Theorem 2.2, in conjunction with our treatment in Sections 2 and 3. We just remark here that Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 extend to these cases with
q>n/2
and the conditions on the coefficient
b
replaced by
b/\sqrt{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{min}(\mathcal{A})}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p>n
.

As further consequences of Theorem 1.1, we also obtain, in Section 4, corresponding improvements of the local estimates for uniformly elliptic operators in
[
7
]
which then provide further extensions of the fundamental Hölder and Harnack estimates of Krylov and Safonov to the ellipticity cones
\Gamma^{*}_{k}
. The reader is also referred to
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
for extensions of the case
k=n
to exponents
q\leq n
, with
q
at least close to
n
and
p=n
.

Finally, we remark here that Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, extend immediately to more general operators of the form,

(1.10)

Lu=a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b_{i}D_{i}u+cu,

with scalar coefficient
c\leq 0
satisfying
c/\rho^{*}(\mathcal{A})\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

Theorem 1.1 is a consequence of a sharper version of the special case of operators
L_{0}
of the form,

(2.1)

L_{0}u:=\mathcal{A}.D^{2}u,

proved in Section 2 of
[
7
]
, and the gradient estimate from Theorem 4.1 in
[
17
]
. First we recall the upper
k
-contact set of
a function
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is defined by

(2.2)

\Omega^{+}_{k}=\{x_{0}\in\Omega|\ \exists\ k\text{-convex}\ v\in C^{2}(\Omega),\ \text{satisfying}\ u\leq-v\ \text{in}\ \Omega,\ u(x_{0})=-v(x_{0})\}

where a function
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is called
k
-convex if
D^{2}v\in\bar{\Gamma}_{k}(\Omega)
. Then from Section 2 of
[
7
]
, it follows that if
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies
u\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega
, and
\Omega_{0}
is a domain containing

\Omega
, with
u
extended to vanish on
\Omega_{0}-\Omega
, so that
\Omega^{+}_{0,k}\subset\Omega
, we have the estimate,

(2.3)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq C(\text{diam}\Omega_{0})^{2-n/q}||\frac{(L_{0}u)^{-}}{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})}||_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{0,k})},

where
C
is a constant depending on
n,k
and
q
.

To proceed further we need the extended notion of
k
-convex function introduced in
[
17
]
, namely an upper semi-continous function
v
in a domain
\Omega
is called
k
-convex if any quadratic polynomial
\varphi
for which the difference
v-\varphi
has a local maximum in
\Omega
is
k
-convex. As in
[
17
]
we denote the linear space of
k
-convex functions in
\Omega
by
\Phi^{k}(\Omega)
. Equivalent characterisations are also provided in Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 in
[
17
]
. From Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 2.3 in
[
17
]
, it then follows that
\Phi^{k}(\Omega)
is contained in the Sobolev space
W^{1,r}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
for
r<\frac{nk}{n-k}
and moreover, we have the estimate:

(2.4)

||Dv||_{L^{r}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C(\text{diam}\Omega)^{(n-r)/r}\sup_{\Omega}|v|

for any
v\in\Phi^{k}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}
satisfying dist
(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)\geq\kappa(\text{diam}\Omega)
, for some
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
, with constant
C
depending on
n,k,r
and
\kappa
.

Now, defining the upper
k
-envelope of
u
on
\Omega_{0}
by

(2.5)

u_{0}=\inf\{-v|v\in\Phi^{k}(\Omega_{0}),u\leq-v\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{0}\},

we then have
u_{0}\in\Phi^{k}(\Omega_{0})
,
0\leq u\leq u_{0}
in
\Omega_{0}
and
u_{0}=u
in
\Omega^{+}_{0,k}
. To apply the estimate
(
2.3
), we suppose
\Omega
lies in a ball of radius
R
and take
\Omega_{0}
to be the concentric ball of radius
2R
. Using the coordinate transformation
x\rightarrow(x-x_{0})/R
, where
x_{0}
denotes the centre of
\Omega_{0}
, we can then assume
R=1
. For convenience, by dividing
L
by
\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})
, we can also assume
\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})=1
. From the estimate (
2.4
), applied to the function
u_{0}
in
\Omega_{0}
, with
\kappa=1/4
and
r
chosen so that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p},

we then have the estimate

(2.6)

||b.Du||_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{0,k})}\leq C||b||_{L^{p}(\Omega^{+}_{0,k})}\sup u,\\

where
C
is a constant depending on
n,k,p
and
q
. By combining (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), we can obtain the desired estimate (
1.6
), in the case when
||b||_{p}
is sufficiently small. To proceed to the general case, we adapt the argument used by Pucci
[
9
]
in his treatment of the case
k=n
. Writing
M=\sup_{\Omega}u
, we let
\mathcal{U}_{m}=\mathcal{U}_{m,k}
, for
m\in[0,M)
denote the
k
-upper contact set of the function
u-m
in
\Omega_{0}
, so that
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\Omega^{+}_{0,k}
in our previous notation. For
m=M
, we set
\mathcal{U}_{M}=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ u=M\}
. For a sufficiently large positive integer
N
, we then let
m_{0},\dots,m_{N}
be an increasing sequence in
[0,M]
, such that
m_{0}=0
,
m_{N}=M
and

(2.7)

\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i-1}-\mathcal{U}_{i}}|b|^{p}=\frac{1}{N^{p/q}}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{0}-\mathcal{U}_{M}}|b|^{p}

for
i=1,\dots,N
, and
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{m_{i}}
. Now we define

y_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i-1}-\mathcal{U}_{i}}|b.Du|^{q},

so that

\Sigma^{N}_{j=i}y_{j}=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i-1}}|b.Du|^{q}

for
i=1,\dots,N
, since
Du=0
a.e on
\mathcal{U}_{N}
. Applying the estimates, (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), to the functions
u-m_{i}
and using (
2.7
), we then obtain, for
i=1,\dots,N
, the estimates

(2.8)

y_{i}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{N}||b||^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}\{\Sigma^{N}_{j=i}y_{j}+||(Lu)^{-}||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}\},

for a further constant
C_{0}
depending on
n,k,p
and
q
. Now setting

\theta=C_{0}||b||^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{U}_{0})},\ \alpha=\theta/(N-\theta),\ \mu=||(Lu)^{-}||^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}

and taking
N>\theta
, we can rewrite (
2.8
) as

y_{i}\leq\alpha(\Sigma^{N}_{j=i+1}y_{j}+\mu)

In accordance with the discrete Gronwall inequality, we then have, by iteration,

y_{i}\leq\alpha(1+\alpha)^{(N-i)}\mu,

for
i=1,\dots,N
. Summing over
i
, we now obtain

\int_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}|b.Du|^{q}\leq\{(1+\alpha)^{N}-1\}\mu=\{(\frac{N}{N-\theta})^{N}-1\}\mu

so that letting
N\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the estimate

(2.9)

||b.Du||_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}\leq(e^{\theta}-1)^{1/q}||(Lu)^{-}||_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}.

Note that we can also infer (
2.9
) directly from (
2.8
) and the Lemma in Section 3 of
[
9
]
, which corresponds to a cruder version of our preceding estimate with finite
N>\theta
.

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.9
) and returning to our original coordinates, with
u
replaced by
u-\sup_{\partial\Omega}u
, we obtain the following sharper version of our desired estimate (
1.7
) for functions
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
,

(2.10)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u\ +\ Cd^{2-n/q}\exp(C_{0}d^{(1-n/p)q}||b/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A}))||^{q}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{+}_{k})})||\frac{(Lu)^{-}}{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})}||_{L^{q}(\Omega^{+}_{k})},

where
d=\text{diam}\Omega
,
C
and
C_{0}
are constants depending respectively on
n,k,q
and
n,k,p,q
. By following the approximation argument given, for example, in the proof of the case
k=n
in
[
3
]
, we may extend the estimate to functions
u\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
, thereby completing the proof of Theorem 1.1.

We can extend the estimate (
2.10
) to the cases
k<n/2
through a completely different approach by adapting that used for divergence structure operators in
[
11
]
. Under our previous normalisations,
R=\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})=1
, we consider the function
w
given by

(2.11)

w=\log\frac{M+\mu}{M-u+\mu},

where
\mu=||(Lu)^{-}||_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
for some
q>n/2
, so that

\displaystyle L_{0}w

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}Dw.Dw-b.Dw+\frac{1}{M-u+\mu}Lu

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{M-u+\mu}(Lu)^{-}-\frac{|b|^{2}}{4\lambda_{min}(\mathcal{A})}.

Applying the estimate (
2.3
), with
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
, we then obtain

\sup w\leq C(1+||b^{2}/4\lambda_{min}(\mathcal{A})||_{L^{q}(\Omega)})

and hence, from (
2.11
) and taking account of our normalisations, we obtain, in place of (
2.10
),

(2.12)

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u\ +\ d^{2-n/q}\exp C\{1+d^{2q-n)}||b^{2}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{min}(\mathcal{A})||^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega}\}||\frac{(Lu)^{-}}{\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})}||_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

for all
k
and any
q>n/2
, where
C
is a constant depending on
n
,
k
, and
q
.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Unlike the case
L=L_{0}
in
[
7
]
, Theorem 1.2 does not immediately follow from the corresponding local maximum principle. However the approach is still similar and we can combine the proofs, with the resultant local maximum principle formulated in the next section as Theorem 3.1.
Accordingly we fix a ball
B:=B_{R}:=B_{R}(y)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of centre
y
and radius
R
, such that the intersection
\Omega\cap B
is non-empty. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.2 and our previous normalisations,
B=B_{1}(0)
and
\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})=1
, we define a cut-off function
\eta
by

(3.1)

\eta=[(1-|x|^{2})^{+}]^{\beta}

for some
\beta\geq 1
, to be chosen. Then setting
v=\eta(u^{+})^{2}
, for
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfying
u\leq 0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B
, we now compute, in
\Omega\cap B\cap\{u>0\}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle L_{0}v:=a^{ij}D_{ij}v

\displaystyle=u^{2}a^{ij}D_{ij}\eta+4ua^{ij}D_{i}\eta D_{j}u+2\eta a^{ij}D_{i}uD_{j}u+2\eta uL_{0}u

\displaystyle\geq-C\mathcal{T}\eta^{1-2/\beta}u^{2}-\eta b.Du^{2}+2\eta uLu,

where
C
is a constant depending on
n
and
\beta
. Now we fix the point
x_{0}
so that
u(x_{0})=\sup_{\Omega}u=\sup_{\Omega}v
and take
\beta=2
. Applying the estimate (
2.3
) with
\Omega_{0}=B_{2}
, we then obtain

(3.3)

\sup_{\Omega}v\leq C||(\eta^{1-2/\beta}\mathcal{T}u^{2}+\eta(b.Du^{2})^{-}+\eta u(Lu)^{-}||_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}

for a constant
C
depending on
n,k,q
and
\beta
, where now
\mathcal{U}_{0}\subset B\cap\Omega
denotes the upper
k
-contact set of the function
v
in
\Omega_{0}
.

To prove the global estimate, (
1.8
), we now fix the point
x_{0}
so that
u(x_{0})=\sup_{\Omega}u=\sup_{\Omega}v
and take
\beta=2
in (
3.3
). Consequently, if

(3.4)

||\mathcal{T}||_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq 1/2C,

where
C
is the constant in (
3.3
), with
\beta=2
, we have

\sup_{\Omega}v\leq 2C||\eta(b.Du^{2})^{-}+\eta u(Lu)^{-}||_{L^{q}(\mathcal{U}_{0})}

so that we can adapt the proof of Theorem 1.1 to handle the coefficient
b
. Here we let
\mathcal{U}_{m}=\mathcal{U}_{m,k}
, for
m\in[0,M)
, denote the
k
-upper contact set of the function
v_{m}:=\eta(u^{2}-m^{2})
in
\Omega_{0}
and define

y_{i}:=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i-1}-\mathcal{U}_{i}}(\eta|b.Du^{2}|)^{q},

so that now

\Sigma^{N}_{j=i}y_{j}=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i-1}}(\eta|b.Du^{2}|)^{q}

for
i=1,\dots,N
.

To proceed further we also need to estimate
D\eta/\eta
in
\mathcal{U}_{m}
. Letting
v_{0}
denote the upper
k
-envelope of
v_{m}
in
\Omega_{0}
, it follows that

(1-(|x|/2)^{2-n/k}\sup v_{m}\leq v_{0}\leq\eta\leq(1-|x|^{2})\sup v_{m}

in
\mathcal{U}_{m}
, which implies

|D\eta|/\eta\leq\frac{\beta}{1-(1/2)^{(2k/n)-1}}:=\gamma

in
\mathcal{U}_{m}
.

Now denoting
v^{i}=v_{m_{i-1}}
, we then have, in the upper contact set

,

\displaystyle\eta|b.Du^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|b|\{|Dv^{i}|+|D\eta|(u^{2}-m^{2}_{i-1})\}

\displaystyle\leq|b|\{|Dv^{i}|+\gamma\sup v^{i}\},

so that using the estimates (
2.3
) and (

) we obtain again the fundamental inequality (

), for a constant

depending on
n,k,p,q
and
\beta
. We remark that the case

-upper envelope of

to estimate
|Dv^{i}|\leq v^{i}
in
\mathcal{U}_{i-1}
. The proof can then be further simplified by taking

.

To conclude the estimate (
1.8
), we then need to fix
\beta=2
and an appropriate radius

satisfying the condition (
3.4
) in our original coordinates which is done by taking

(3.5)

R=C||\mathcal{T}/\rho^{*}_{k}(\mathcal{A})||^{q/n}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for a further constant
C
depending on
n,k
and

.

Accordingly we obtain an estimate of the form (
2.10
), with the diameter

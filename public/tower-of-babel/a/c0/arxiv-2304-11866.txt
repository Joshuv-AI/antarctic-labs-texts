Some results on Continuous dependence of fractal functions on the Sierpiński gasket
*Vishal Agrawal, Ajay Prajapati, Abhilash Sahu, Tanmoy Som

Abstract
. In this article, we show that α \displaystyle\alpha -fractal functions defined on Sierpiński gasket (denoted by △ \displaystyle\triangle ) depend continuously on the parameters involved in the construction. In the latter part of this article, the continuous dependence of parameters on α \displaystyle\alpha -fractal functions defined on △ \displaystyle\triangle is shown graphically.

1.
Introduction

In the field of Numerical Analysis, the computational implications of interpolation have been a major concern for many years. The theory of interpolation has evolved throughout the development of classical approximation theory. Various interpolation techniques are used in Numerical Analysis and Classical Approximation Theory, and they are based on polynomial, trigonometric, spline, and rational functions. Based on the underlying idea of the model under investigation, these techniques can be applied to a specific data set. While nonrecursive interpolation techniques in the literature almost always produce smooth interpolants, it should be emphasized that they are not all recursive. There are a great number of real-world phenomena and experimental signals that are confusing, and sometimes their traces appear smooth. Due to their non-differentiability and complexity, a simple mathematical framework may not adequately describe their smallest geometric complexity. In order to produce an interpolant with a more complex geometric structure, it is necessary to develop a novel interpolation approach. Univariate real-valued interpolation functions constructed on a compact interval in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

A number of studies have exposed several significant characteristics of FIFs, including their smoothness, stability, one-sided approximation property and constraint approximation property, as well as their box dimension and Hausdorff dimension. Consider a set
\displaystyle V_{0}=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right\}
such that points in
\displaystyle V_{0}
have equal distances from each other. Corresponding to each point of
\displaystyle V_{0}
, define the contraction map
\displaystyle u_{i}
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
as follows:

u_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(t+x_{i}\right),

where
\displaystyle i=1,2,3
. Then, three contraction maps together with the plane constitute an IFS, which produces
\displaystyle\triangle
as an attractor, i.e.,

\triangle=\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}u_{i}(\triangle).

Define
\displaystyle V_{1}
by
\displaystyle V_{1}=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},u_{1}\left(x_{2}\right),u_{2}\left(x_{3}\right),u_{3}\left(x_{1}\right)\right\}
. Let us consider a continuous function
\displaystyle f:\triangle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let
\displaystyle U=\triangle\times\mathbb{R}
and define maps
\displaystyle H_{i}:U\rightarrow U
by

H_{i}(t,x)=\left(u_{i}(t),M_{i}(t,x)\right),i=1,2,3,

where
\displaystyle M_{i}(t,x):\triangle\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a contraction map in the last variable, that is,

\left|M_{i}(.,x)-M_{i}\left(.,x^{\prime}\right)\right|\leq c\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|

with
\displaystyle M_{i}\left(p_{j},f\left(p_{j}\right)\right)=f\left(u_{i}\left(p_{j}\right)\right)
. In particular, we take

M_{i}(t,x)=\alpha_{i}x+f\left(u_{i}(t)\right)-\alpha_{i}b(t),

where
\displaystyle b\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
is base function and
\displaystyle f\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
original function such that
\displaystyle b\left(p\right)=f\left(p\right)~\forall~p\in V_{0}
, and scale vector
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{i}\right|_{\infty}<1
. Thus, We have an IFS
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}:=\left\{U,H_{i}:i=1,2,3\right\}
.

3.
Continuous dependence on parameter
\displaystyle\alpha
AND
\displaystyle b

Let
\displaystyle f\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
. Define a map
\displaystyle W
from
\displaystyle S
to
\displaystyle C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
by

W(\alpha)=f_{b}^{\alpha},

where
\displaystyle S=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\alpha|_{\infty}\leq r<1\right.
and
\displaystyle r
is a fixed number
\displaystyle\}
and
\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}
is
\displaystyle\alpha
-fractal function associated with
\displaystyle f
with respect to
\displaystyle b
and the scale vector
\displaystyle\alpha
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For fixed
\displaystyle f\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
and for suitable
\displaystyle b\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
, the map
\displaystyle W:S\rightarrow C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
is continuous.

Proof.

The fixed point theory says that for a fixed a scale vector
\displaystyle\alpha
, and
\displaystyle b
, the map
\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}
is unique. Further, being fixed point of RB- operator,
\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}
satisfies the functional equation:

f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}.

It is obvious that
\displaystyle W
is well defined. Let
\displaystyle\alpha\in S
, then from the above functional equation, we have

W(\alpha)(t)=f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}

and for
\displaystyle\beta\in S
,

W(\beta)(t)=f_{b}^{\beta}(t)=f(t)+\beta_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\beta}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}.

We shall show that
\displaystyle W
is continuous at
\displaystyle\alpha
. For this, subtract one from other of the above two equations, for
\displaystyle t\in u_{i}(\triangle)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)-f_{b}^{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right]-\left[f(t)+\beta_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\beta}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right]

(2)

\displaystyle=\left[\alpha_{i}f_{b}^{\alpha}\left(u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right)-\beta_{i}f_{b}^{\beta}\left(u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right)\right]+\left(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\right)b\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\alpha_{i}f_{b}^{\alpha}-\beta_{i}f_{b}^{\alpha}+\beta_{i}f_{b}^{\alpha}-\beta_{i}f_{b}^{\beta}\right]\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)+\left(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\right)b\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}\right)f_{b}^{\alpha}+\beta_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right)\right]\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)+\left(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\right)b\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t).

Now, using triangle inequality and definition of uniform norm, we have

\displaystyle\left|f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)-f_{b}^{\beta}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left[\left(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}\right)f_{b}^{\alpha}+\beta_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right)\right]\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right|+\left|\left(\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\right)b\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right|

(3)

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha-\beta|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+|\beta|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right\|_{\infty}+|\beta-\alpha|_{\infty}\|b\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=|\alpha-\beta|_{\infty}\left(\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}\right)+|\beta|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right\|_{\infty}.

It follows that, for all
\displaystyle t\in\triangle
, we get

\left|f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)-f_{b}^{\beta}(t)\right|\leq|\alpha-\beta|_{\infty}\left(\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}\right)+|\beta|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right\|_{\infty}.

(4)

The above implies that

\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq|\alpha-\beta|_{\infty}\left(\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}\right)+|\beta|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\right\|_{\infty}.

(5)

Using
\displaystyle 1-|\beta|_{\infty}\geq 1-r
, finally we have

\|W(\alpha)-W(\beta)\|_{\infty}=\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{b}^{\beta}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|\alpha-\beta|_{\infty}}{1-r}\left(\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}\right).

(6)

Since
\displaystyle\alpha
is fixed and
\displaystyle\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}
is bounded, we have
\displaystyle W
is continuous at
\displaystyle\alpha
. Since
\displaystyle\alpha
was taken arbitrarily, hence,
\displaystyle W
is continuous on
\displaystyle S
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
and scale vector
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\displaystyle|\alpha|_{\infty}<1
and
\displaystyle X_{f}=\left\{b\in C(\triangle,\mathbb{R}):\left.b\right|_{V_{0}}=\left.f\right|_{V_{0}}\right\}
. Then the map
\displaystyle T:X_{f}\rightarrow C(\triangle,\mathbb{R})
defined by
\displaystyle T(b)=f_{b}^{\alpha}
is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

We know that for a scale vector
\displaystyle\alpha
and a suitable function
\displaystyle b:\triangle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the function
\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}
is unique. Further, being fixed point of
\displaystyle\mathrm{RB}
- operator,
\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}
satisfies the functional equation:

f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}.

It is obvious that
\displaystyle T
is well defined. Let
\displaystyle b,c\in X_{f}
then from the above functional equation, we have

T(b)(t)=f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}.

and

T(c)(t)=f_{c}^{\alpha}(t)=f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{c}^{\alpha}-c\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t),~\forall~t\in u_{i}(\triangle),i\in\{1,2,3\}.

On subtracting one from other of the above two equations, we get for
\displaystyle t\in u_{i}(\triangle)

\displaystyle f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)-f_{c}^{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-b\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right]-\left[f(t)+\alpha_{i}\left(f_{c}^{\alpha}-c\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)+\alpha_{i}(c-b)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t).

Now using triangle inequality and definition of uniform norm, we have

\displaystyle\left|f_{b}^{\alpha}(t)-f_{c}^{\alpha}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)+\alpha_{i}(c-b)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\alpha_{i}\left(f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right|+\left|\alpha_{i}(c-b)\circ u_{i}^{-1}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+|\alpha|_{\infty}\|c-b\|_{\infty}.

The above inquality holds for all
\displaystyle t\in\triangle
, therefore, we write

\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}\leq|\alpha|_{\infty}\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}+|\alpha|_{\infty}\|c-b\|_{\infty}.

This can be recasted as,
\displaystyle\left\|f_{b}^{\alpha}-f_{c}^{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|\alpha|_{\infty}}{1-|\alpha|_{\infty}}\|b-c\|_{\infty}
. It follows that

\|T(b)-T(c)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|\alpha|_{\infty}}{1-|\alpha|_{\infty}}\|b-c\|_{\infty},

which shows that
\displaystyle T
is a Lipschitz continuous map with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|_{\infty}}{1-|\alpha|_{\infty}}
.
∎

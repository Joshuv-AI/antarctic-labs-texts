A sufficient family of necessary inequalities for the compatibility of quantum marginals
Thomas C. Fraser

Abstract
The quantum marginal problem is concerned with characterizing which collections of quantum states on different subsystems are compatible in the sense that they are the marginals of some multipartite quantum state. Presented here is a countable family of inequalities, each of which is necessarily satisfied by any compatible collection of quantum states. Additionally, this family of inequalities is shown to be sufficient: every incompatible collection of quantum states will violate at least one inequality belonging to the family.



I
Introduction

The quantum marginal problem (QMP) is interested in characterizing the space of reduced/marginal states of a multipartite quantum state, and is widely regarded as being an important, albeit challenging problem to solve.

Connections and applications of the QMP to other topics in quantum theory (and beyond) include multipartite entanglement and separability
Coffman et al. 2000
;
Walter et al. 2013
, quantum error correction
Ocko et al. 2011
;
Huber 2017
;

, entropic constraints

;

;

;

, other forms of quantum compatibility problems

;

;

, the asymptotics of representations of the symmetric groups

;

;
Christandl et al. 2018a
, the asymptotic restriction problem for tensors
Christandl et al. 2018b
, random matrix theory
Collins and McSwiggen 2021
;
Christandl et al. 2014
, and many-body physics

.

The quantum marginal problem, and its associated terminology, is derived from an analogous problem in probability theory called the
classical
marginal problem. The classical marginal problem is concerned with characterizing the relationships between the various marginal distributions of a joint, multivariate distribution

;
Vorob’ev 1962
;
Malvestuto 1988
. As a joint probability distribution and its marginals can always be faithfully represented by the eigenvalues of a joint quantum state and its marginals using a product eigenbasis, the quantum marginal problem subsumes the classical marginal problem, and consequently, any of its applications
Fritz and Chaves 2012
;
Liang et al. 2011
;
Abramsky and Brandenburger 2011
;
Fraser and Wolfe 2018
.

One of the earliest formulations of the problem dates back to the early 1960s, when, for the purposes of simplifying calculations involving the atomic and molecular structure, quantum chemists became interested in characterizing the possible reduced density matrices of a system of
N
interacting fermions
Coulson 1960
;
Coleman 1963
. This version of the problem, referred to as the
N
-representability problem, has a long history

;
Coleman 2001
;
Lude et al. 2013
;

;

;

;
Mazziotti 2012b
;
Klyachko 2006
.

Now the QMP comes in a variety of flavours which can be broadly organized by considering any additional assumptions or constraints that are imposed on either i) the properties of the joint state, and/or ii) the properties of the set of candidate density operators
Tyc and Vlach 2015
.

When focusing on the joint state, specializations of the QMP exist where the joint state is assumed to be fermionic
Coleman and Yukalov 2000
, bosonic
Wei et al. 2010
, Gaussian
Eisert et al. 2008
;
Vlach 2015
, separable
Navascués et al. 2021
, or having symmetric eigenvectors
Aloy et al. 2020
. Generally speaking, the QMP is difficult in the sense that it is a QMA-complete problem

;
Liu et al. 2007
;
Wei et al. 2010
;
Bookatz 2012
. For the purposes of this paper, the only restriction imposed on joint states will be that they live in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, with a finite and fixed number of subsystems.

Regarding the list of candidate density operators, e.g.,
(\rho_{AB},\rho_{BC},\rho_{AC})
, the list of subsystems they correspond to, e.g.,
(AB,BC,AC)
, is known as the
marginal scenario
, while the individual elements, e.g.,
AB
,
BC
, or
AC
, will be referred to as
marginal contexts
. A key consideration for understanding previous work on the QMP is whether the marginal contexts are disjoint. When the marginal contexts are disjoint, a complete solution to the QMP is known
Klyachko 2004
;
Klyachko and Shumovsky 2006
. For a given specification of Hilbert space dimensions, this solution takes the form of a finite list of linear inequality constraints on the spectra of the candidate density operators. These solutions furthermore recover earlier results pertaining to low dimensional Hilbert-spaces
Higuchi et al. 2003
;
Higuchi 2003
;
Bravyi 2003
;
Han et al. 2005
.

In contrast, when the marginal scenario involves overlapping marginal contexts, existing results are comparatively more sporadic and typically weaker, being only applicable to low-dimensional systems, small numbers of parties, or only yielding necessary but insufficient constraints
Chen et al. 2014
;
Carlen et al. 2013
;
Butterley et al. 2006
;
Hall 2007
;
Chen et al. 2016
. One promising approach, developed in
Christandl et al. 2018a
for relating marginal spectra to the recoupling theory of the symmetric group, appears limited to situations where the marginal contexts do not overlap too much.

Whenever a candidate set of density operators is explicitly given, one strategy to decide their compatibility is to use convex optimization techniques, e.g., semidefinite programming
Vandenberghe and Boyd 1996
. If the joint state is not necessarily pure, compatibility can be decided with a single semidefinite program
Hall 2007
. Additionally, when the joint state is assumed pure, compatibility can still be decided by an infinite hierarchy of semidefinite programs
Yu et al. 2021
. In either case, analytic inequality constraints that serve as witnesses for incompatibility can be extracted from the outputs of such semidefinite programs
Hall 2007
.

The objective of this paper is to improve our understanding of the QMP, in particular for the case of overlapping marginal contexts, by i) deriving inequality constraints that are necessarily satisfied by all compatible collections of density operators, and ii) proving that if a collection of density operators satisfies these inequalities, then they are compatible. This paper begins by formally defining the QMP and then reformulating it from a different perspective. The primary advantage of this reformulation of the QMP is that it exposes an implicit symmetry of the problem which is helpful in deriving our main result.

IV
Conclusion

This paper makes progress toward an analytic solution to the quantum marginal problem (QMP) by constructing an countably-infinite family of necessary operator inequalities whose satisfaction by a given tuple of density operators is sufficient to conclude their compatibility.
The primary advantage of this approach is its generality: for any finite Hilbert space dimension(s) and any number of subsystems with arbitrary overlap, the corresponding family of necessary inequalities is shown to be sufficient.
The results of this paper, therefore, constitute the first quantifier-free solution to the QMP for overlapping marginal contexts.
However, the characterization of compatible density operators produced by this approach is not finite, and thus inherently more challenging to compute.
Evidently, further insights will be required to produce a finite set of necessary and sufficient conditions for the overlapping QMP.

Appendix D
Pure vs. Full QMP

One might wonder why the version of the QMP considered in this paper (
1
) seems to be exclusively interested in the existence of joint states that are
pure
,
\psi_{J}\in\mathcal{P}(H_{J})
, instead of the more general
density operator
,
\rho_{J}\in\mathcal{D}(H_{J})
. In order to distinguish between these two types of QMP, the former is sometimes called the
pure
QMP, while the latter is sometimes called the
full
QMP. Of these two variants, the full QMP is arguably a much closer analogy to the classical marginals problem
Fritz and Chaves 2012
.

The distinction between these two variants is strongest when the marginal scenario under consideration,
\mathcal{M}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{m})
, has disjoint marginal contexts, i.e.
S_{i}\cap S_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
, or equivalently
H_{\mathcal{M}}=H_{S_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{S_{m}}\cong H_{J}
. Under the assumption of disjoint marginal contexts, the full QMP becomes trivial; every collection of density operators
(\rho_{S_{1}},\ldots,\rho_{S_{m}})
are the
\mathcal{M}
-marginals of the density operator
\rho_{J}=\rho_{S_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{S_{m}}
. On the other hand, under this assumption, the pure QMP remains non-trivial.

At the level of generality considered in this paper, wherein the marginal scenario
\mathcal{M}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{m})
is permitted to contain overlapping marginal contexts, e.g.,
S_{i}\cap S_{j}\neq\emptyset
, the distinction becomes less important because the marginals of a mixed state can equivalently be viewed as the marginals of any of its purifications. Specifically, there exists a density operator
\sigma_{J}\in\mathcal{D}(H_{J})
with marginals
\rho_{S}=\mathrm{Tr}_{J\setminus S}(\sigma_{J})
for all
S\in\mathcal{M}
if and only if there exists a joint pure state
\psi_{JJ^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}(H_{J}\otimes H_{J^{\prime}})
(where
H_{J^{\prime}}\cong H_{J}
) such that
(\mathrm{Tr}_{J\setminus S}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{J^{\prime}})(\sigma_{J})=\rho_{S}
for all
S\in\mathcal{M}
. Consequently, the techniques developed in this paper, which directly apply to the pure QMP, can also be applied to any instance of the full QMP without substantial modification.

Appendix G
The Bipartite QMP

This section considers the bipartite marginal scenario,
\mathcal{M}=(A,B)
, for the joint context
J=AB
. For this scenario, the QMP is fully solved and admits of a simple solution:
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are the marginals of a joint pure state
\psi_{AB}\in\mathcal{P}(H_{AB})
if and only if they have the same spectrum
Tyc and Vlach 2015
;
Klyachko 2004
. A natural question arises: how does
3
recover this well-known result?

To answer this question, first let
a=\dim(H_{A})
,
b=\dim(H_{B})
, and let
\ell=\min(a,b)
. For this scenario,
Eq.

13
becomes

(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})^{\otimes n}\leq(\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{A})^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{AB}^{(2n)}).

(75)

Now let
s_{A}\in\mathbb{S}^{a}
and
s_{B}\in\mathbb{S}^{b}
be the spectra of
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
. Using the results of
Appendix

J
(or essentially the spectral estimation theorem
Keyl and Werner 2005
;
Christandl and Mitchison 2006
), together with
Eq.

40
, it is possible to show that the exponential factor in
Eq.

16
,
\Omega(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
, depends only on
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
and is equal to:

\displaystyle\Omega(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\inf_{r\in\mathbb{S}^{\ell}}(D\!\left(s_{A}\!\parallel\!r\right)+D\!\left(s_{B}\!\parallel\!r\right)),

(76)

where
D\!\left(p\!\parallel\!q\right)
is the relative entropy
D\!\left(p\!\parallel\!q\right)=\sum_{i}p_{i}(\ln p_{i}-\ln q_{i})
. Since
D\!\left(p\!\parallel\!q\right)
only vanishes if
p=q
,
\Omega(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})
only vanishes if
s_{A}=s_{B}
. Therefore, we conclude that
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are the
(A,B)
-marginals of a pure state
\psi_{AB}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{b})
if and only if they have equal spectra.
Additionally, using Pinsker’s inequality
Reid and Williamson 2009
,
\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq 2D\!\left(p\!\parallel\!q\right)
, and the triangle inequality for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\lVert s_{A}-s_{B}\right\rVert_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3(\left\lVert s_{A}-r\right\rVert_{1}^{2}+\left\lVert s_{B}-r\right\rVert_{1}^{2})

(77)

\displaystyle\leq 6(D\!\left(s_{A}\!\parallel\!r\right)+D\!\left(s_{B}\!\parallel\!r\right)).

(78)

Therefore,
\Omega(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\geq\left\lVert s_{A}-s_{B}\right\rVert_{1}^{2}/6
.

A more direct consequence of
Eq.

75
is the following proposition.

Proposition 7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{a}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{b}
be partitions. If
\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
satisfies
Eq.

75
, then

s_{\alpha}(r_{A})s_{\beta}(r_{B})\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{2n}^{\ell}}c^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\dim(V_{\lambda}^{a})\dim(V_{\lambda}^{b})}{\dim(W_{\lambda})},

(79)

where
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
are the spectra of
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
respectively. Additionally,
s_{\alpha}
and
s_{\beta}
are Schur functions
Sra 2016
;
Sagan 2013
and
c^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}
is the Littlewood-Richardson coefficient
Littlewood and Richardson 1934
;
Fulton 2000
;
Pak et al. 2019
.

Proof.

One of the most powerful tools for decomposing bipartite Hilbert spaces, specifically the symmetric subspace of a bipartite system
\vee^{k}(H_{A}\otimes H_{B})\cong V^{ab}_{(k)}
, is known as
\mathrm{GL}(a)\times\mathrm{GL}(b)
-duality
Howe 1987
(see also
(
Walter 2014
, Eq. (2.25))
):

V^{ab}_{(k)}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{k}^{\ell}}V_{\lambda}^{a}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{b},

(80)

where
\ell=\min(a,b)
.
Using this result, and applying
\Pi_{A}^{\alpha}\otimes\Pi_{B}^{\beta}
to the right-hand-side of
Eq.

13
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{Tr}_{AB}^{\otimes n}\{(\Pi_{A}^{\alpha}\otimes\Pi_{B}^{\beta})(\mathrm{Tr}_{B}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{A}^{\otimes n})(\Pi_{AB}^{(2n)})\}\\
&\quad=\dim(W_{\alpha})\dim(W_{\beta})\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}^{\ell}_{2n}}\hskip-5.0ptc^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\dim(V_{\lambda}^{a})\dim(V_{\lambda}^{b})}{\dim(W_{\lambda})},\end{split}

(81)

where
c^{\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}
counts the multiplicity of the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}
irreducible representation space
W_{\alpha}\otimes W_{\beta}
inside
W_{\lambda}
under the restriction of
\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. By comparison, applying
\Pi_{A}^{\alpha}\otimes\Pi_{B}^{\beta}
to the left-hand-side of
Eq.

13
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}((\Pi_{A}^{\alpha}\otimes\Pi_{B}^{\beta})(\rho_{A}^{\otimes n}\otimes\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}))

(82)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{A}^{\alpha}\rho_{A}^{\otimes n})\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{B}^{\beta}\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}),

(83)

\displaystyle\quad=s_{\alpha}(r_{A})\dim(W_{\alpha})s_{\beta}(r_{B})\dim(W_{\beta}).

(84)

Therefore,
Eq.

75
implies
Eq.

79
and thus the claim holds.
∎

Corollary 8
.

Let

be a subspace of a joint Hilbert space,

. An

-product state,

Appendix I
Counting Solutions to the QMP

Whenever a given
\mathcal{M}
-product state,
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}=\rho_{S_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{S_{m}}
, is shown to be compatible, a natural follow-up problem is to determine whether or not the joint state,
\psi_{J}
, satisfying
Eq.

9
is unique. For the bipartite marginal scenario,
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, if the common spectrum of
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
is
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r},0,\ldots,0)
, with positive values distinct, i.e.
s_{1}>\cdots>s_{r}>0
, then the
unique
pure state,
\psi_{AB}\in\mathcal{P}(H_{J})
, satisfying
Eq.

9
is
\psi_{AB}=\ket{\psi_{AB}}\bra{\psi_{AB}}
where

\ket{\psi_{AB}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{s_{i}}\ket{\phi_{A}^{(i)}}\otimes\ket{\phi_{B}^{(j)}},

(86)

where
\{\phi_{A}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
and
\{\phi_{B}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{r}
are the eigenvectors of
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
. If, however, the common spectrum,
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r},0,\ldots,0)
, is degenerate in the sense that some of its values are identical, then the solution to
Eq.

9
may not be unique. A familiar example of this phenomenon, for the two-qubit Hilbert space
H_{J}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
, are the four Bell states all sharing the same pair of maximally-mixed, single-qubit marginals,
(\frac{I}{2},\frac{I}{2})
.

The following result generalizes
Eq.

17
by considering the possibility that an
\mathcal{M}
-product may be compatible with multiple, orthogonal, joint states.

Corollary 9
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}=\rho_{S_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{S_{m}}
be an
\mathcal{M}
-product state and
\{\psi_{J}^{(1)},\ldots,\psi_{J}^{(v)}\}
be a set of joint pure states, satisfying i) for all
1\leq j,k\leq v

\mathrm{Tr}(\psi_{J}^{(j)}\psi_{J}^{(k)})=|\braket{\psi_{J}^{(j)}|{\psi_{J}^{(k)}}}|^{2}=\delta_{j,k},

(87)

and ii) for all
1\leq i\leq m
, and
1\leq k\leq v

\rho_{S_{i}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{J\setminus S_{i}}(\psi_{J}^{(k)}).

(88)

Then, the following inequality holds:

v^{nm}\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes n}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{nm}^{v}}\mathrm{Tr}_{mJ\setminus\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes n}(\Pi_{J}^{\lambda}).

(89)

Proof.

Let
P_{V}
be the orthogonal projection operator onto the subspace of
H_{J}
spanned by
\{\psi_{J}^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{v}
, i.e.,

P_{V}=\sum_{k=1}^{v}\psi_{J}^{(k)}.

(90)

Since each
\psi_{J}^{(k)}
has marginals
(\rho_{S_{1}},\ldots,\rho_{S_{m}})
, we conclude

\mathrm{Tr}_{mJ\setminus\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes n}(P_{V}^{\otimes nm})=v^{nm}\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes n}.

(91)

Furthermore,

commutes with

for all
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{nm}
and thus commutes with
\Pi_{J}^{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{nm}^{d_{J}}
. In fact, we obtain

P_{V}^{\otimes nm}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{nm}^{v}}\Pi_{J}^{\lambda}P_{V}^{\otimes nm}\Pi_{J}^{\lambda}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{nm}^{v}}\Pi_{J}^{\lambda},

(92)

because i)
\Pi_{J}^{\lambda}P_{V}^{\otimes nm}=0
for all
\lambda
with length
\ell(\lambda)>v
, and ii)
P_{V}\leq I_{J}
. Applying
\mathrm{Tr}_{mJ\setminus\mathcal{M}}^{\otimes n}
yields
Eq.

89
.
∎

Note that
Eq.

89
is equivalent to
Eq.

17

Corollary 12
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
have spectrum
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and let
U\in\mathrm{U}(d)
be a unitary such that
\rho=U\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})U^{\dagger}
and let
\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. Then

\frac{\Delta_{s}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)}{\Delta_{s}(\mathrm{diag}(s))}=\exp(-K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right))

(99)

where
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)
is defined as

K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)={\sum}_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}\ln s_{i}-\delta_{i}(s)\ln\mathrm{pm}_{i}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U),

(100)

where
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)\in[0,\infty]
and
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)=0
if and only if
\sigma=\rho
.

Notice that if
\rho
and
\sigma
are simultaneously diagonalized by
U
so that
U^{\dagger}\sigma U=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})
, the quantity
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)
simplifies to the classical relative entropy,
D\!\left(s\!\parallel\!t\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}(\ln s_{i}-\ln t_{i})
,
also known as Kullback-Liebler divergence
Kullback 1997
. Also note that, in general,
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)
does not equal the quantum relative entropy
S\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\sigma))
, but is nevertheless bounded by
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)\leq S\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)

Keyl 2006
. For these reasons, we refer to the quantity
K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)
as
Keyl-divergence
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{d}

n\in\mathbb{N}

\bra{\phi^{U}_{\lambda}}\tau_{\lambda}(\sigma^{\otimes\left|\lambda\right|})\ket{\phi^{U}_{\lambda}}=\Delta_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U).

(101)

Proof.

This result is noted by
Keyl 2006
as
(
Keyl 2006

with reference to

∎

Henceforth, define the projection operator
\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{\otimes\left|\lambda\right|})

\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\coloneqq\iota_{\lambda}(\ket{\phi_{\lambda}^{U}}\bra{\phi^{U}_{\lambda}}\otimes I_{W_{\lambda}})\iota_{\lambda}^{\dagger},

(102)

so that,

\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{U}\rho^{\otimes\left|\lambda\right|})=\dim(W_{\lambda})\Delta_{\lambda}(U^{\dagger}\rho U).

(103)

Corollary 14
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and let
U\in\mathrm{U}(d)
diagonalize
\rho
, i.e.
\rho=U\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})U^{\dagger}

\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi^{U}_{nqs}\sigma^{\otimes nq})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi^{U}_{nqs}\rho^{\otimes nq})}=\exp(-nqK\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)).

(104)

Proof.

The proof follows from

. When

Theorem 15
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, let
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
be the spectrum of
\rho
, and let
U\in\mathrm{U}(d)
diagonalize
\rho
, such that
\rho=U\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})U^{\dagger}
.
Then there exists a sequence,

of partitions such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi^{U}_{\lambda^{n}}\sigma^{\otimes n})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi^{U}_{\lambda^{n}}\rho^{\otimes n})}\leq D(s)\exp(-(n-\tbinom{d+1}{2}+1)K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right)).

(105)

where
D(s)
is a constant depending only on

.

Proof.

The proof relies on an explicit construction of a sequence,

, that satisfies the claim.
For each non-negative integer

\mu^{k}
be the partition characterized by
\delta_{i}(\mu^{k})=\lceil\delta_{i}(ks)\rceil

Next, apply

\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi_{\mu^{k}}^{U}\sigma^{\otimes\left|\mu^{k}\right|})}{\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi_{\mu^{k}}^{U}\rho^{\otimes\left|\mu^{k}\right|})}\leq\frac{\exp(-kK\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right))}{\Delta_{\mu^{1}}(\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\ldots s_{d}))}.

Therefore, from

where

Corollary 16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
be compact. Define

\Omega(\rho)\coloneqq\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma\right).

(114)

Then
\Omega(\rho)=0
if and only if
\rho\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

If
\rho\in\mathcal{C}
, then
12
implies
\Omega(\rho)=K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\rho\right)=0
. Otherwise if
\rho\not\in\mathcal{C}
, then consider, for each fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d;\downarrow}
and
U\in\mathrm{U}(d)
, that the function
\sigma\mapsto\Delta_{x}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)\in[0,1]
is continuous as
\mathrm{pm}_{i}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U)
is a polynomial in the coefficients of
\sigma
. The compactness of
\mathcal{C}
guarantees (using the extreme value theorem) the supremum is attained by some
\sigma_{x}^{U}\in\mathcal{C}
:

\Delta_{x}(U^{\dagger}\sigma_{x}^{U}U)=\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\Delta_{x}(U^{\dagger}\sigma U).

(115)

Since
\Omega(\rho)=K\!\left(\rho\!\parallel\!\sigma_{x}^{U}\right)
and
\rho\neq\sigma_{x}^{U}
, we conclude, from
12
, that
\Omega(\rho)\neq 0
.
∎

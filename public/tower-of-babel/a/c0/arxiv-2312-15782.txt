Cancellation and splitting of Symplectic modules in the critical range and Euler class group
Rakesh Pawar, Husney Parvez Sarwar

Abstract
. In this paper, we discuss the cancellation and splitting of the symplectic modules. The symplectic cancellation result presented here can be thought of as an analog of the projective module cancellation result of Fasel. The symplectic splitting is similar to Murthy’s splitting theorem. To prove the cancellation and splitting, we carefully analyze the Postnikov towers in the 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -homotopy category. Then we prove the vanishing of top cohomology with coefficients in some homotopy sheaf. As another application of the vanishing results, we answer partially a question of Mrinal Das about the isomorphism of ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -th Euler class group and ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -th Chow group, where d d is the dimension of the underlying smooth affine variety.

Introduction

Let us begin with two classical results. Let

[
24
]
and Suslin
[
28
]
proved the following two results on splitting and cancellation respectively. Let
R
be a smooth affine algebra over an algebraically closed field of dimension

-module with

is the top Chern class of

in the Chow group

[

[

Fasel’s result is also optimal in the view of Mohan Kumar’s example

]
. Similar to Suslin’s cancellation conjecture, there is a conjecture for the splitting called Murthy’s splitting conjecture

-torsion. (Actually the 2nd and 3rd maps are isomorphism). The third map is isomorphism as it follows from the isomorphism of sheaves

.
To see the 2nd map is isomorphism, consider the fiber sequence

Breaking the long exact sequence as

{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d}(GL_{d+1})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{d+1}-0))\to A\to 0

and

0\to A\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d+1}).

H^{d}(X,A)
is
d!
-torsion. By
[

the cohomology group

is uniquely divisible. Hence

and thus,

H^{d}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d}))\to H^{d}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d+1}))

is an isomorphism.

For the 1st map to see that

{\rm Ker\ }(H^{d}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d-1}))\to H^{d}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d})))

is
(d-1)!
-torsion, consider the fiber sequence

GL_{d-1}\to GL_{d}\to\mathbb{A}^{d}-0

hence the long exact sequence

{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d}(GL_{d})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d}(\mathbb{A}^{d}-0)\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d-1})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{d-1}(GL_{d}).

is
(d-1)!
. Thus, the claim is proved.

Further consider the maps from

\mathbb{A}^{d-1}-0\xrightarrow{u_{d-1}^{\prime}}GL_{d-1}\xrightarrow{r_{d-1}}\mathbb{A}^{d-1}-0

Proposition 3.10

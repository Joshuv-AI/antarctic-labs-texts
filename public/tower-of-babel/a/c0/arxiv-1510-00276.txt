Introduction

In what follows, the unit ball of a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})

is denoted
B_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\{x\in X:\ \|x\|_{X}<1\}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and

n\in\mathbb{N}
, the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the
\ell_{p}
norm is
denoted as usual by
\ell_{p}^{n}
. Given two metric spaces
(U,d_{U})

and
(V,d_{V})
, the Lipschitz constant of a mapping
f:U\to V
is
denoted
\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
. Throughout this article, given
a,b\in(0,\infty)
, the notations

a\lesssim b
and
b\gtrsim a
mean that
a\leqslant cb
for some
universal constant
c\in(0,\infty)
. The notation
a\asymp b

stands for
(a\lesssim b)\wedge(b\lesssim a)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let
r_{n}(\varepsilon)\geqslant 0
be the supremum
over those
r\in[0,1)
such that for every Lipschitz function

f:B_{\ell_{2}^{n}}\to\ell_{2}
there exists a linear mapping

T:\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}
, a vector
a\in\ell_{2}
, and a sub-ball

B^{*}=x_{0}+\rho B_{\ell_{2}^{n}}\subseteq B_{\ell_{2}^{n}}
of radius
\rho\geqslant r
, such that

\forall\ x\in B^{*},\qquad\frac{\|f(x)-(a+Tx)\|_{\ell_{2}}}{\rho}\leqslant\varepsilon\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

(1)

Thus, all the Hilbert space-valued
1
-Lipschitz functions on the
Euclidean unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are guaranteed to be
\varepsilon
-close to
some affine function on some sub-ball of radius at least
r_{n}(\varepsilon)
,
where
\varepsilon
-closeness is measured relative to the scale of the
sub-ball. A lower bound on
r_{n}(\varepsilon)
corresponds to a
differentiation-type theorem asserting that any such function is macroscopically close to being affine rather than being
infinitesimally affine. Crucially, the macroscopic lower bound on
the scale of affine approximability is independent of the given
function.

The basic question in which we are interested is that of determining
the asymptotic behavior of
r_{n}(\varepsilon)
as
n\to\infty
.
Qualitatively, we ask for an asymptotic understanding of those
Hilbert space-valued Lipschitz functions on
B_{\ell_{2}^{n}}
that are
hardest to approximate by affine functions.

There is a big gap between the known upper and lower bounds on

r_{n}(\varepsilon)
. We have
r_{n}(\varepsilon)\leqslant e^{-cn/\varepsilon^{2}}
, where
c\in(0,\infty)
is a universal
constant; see Section
2.2
below.
The only known lower bound
[
57
]
on
r_{n}(\varepsilon)
is
r_{n}(\varepsilon)\geqslant e^{-(n/\varepsilon)^{Cn}}
, where
C\in(0,\infty)
is a universal constant.
For concreteness, by choosing, say,
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{4}
and denoting

r_{n}=r_{n}(\frac{1}{4})
, the best known bounds on
r_{n}
become

e^{-n^{Kn}}\leqslant r_{n}\leqslant e^{-\kappa n},

(2)

for some universal constants
\kappa,K\in(0,\infty)
. An illustrative special case of the main result that is obtained here (to be described in full below) is the following asymptotic
improvement over the leftmost inequality (
2
), which holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for some universal constant
K\in(0,\infty)
.

r_{n}\geqslant e^{-e^{Kn}}.

(3)

1.1
The modulus of
L_{p}
affine approximabilty

Despite the fact that the above question was phrased in the context
of Hilbert spaces, a setting which arguably best highlights its
fundamental nature, it is important to study it in the context of
mappings between more general normed spaces; it is in this setting,
for example, that it becomes relevant to Bourgain’s discretization
problem
[
13
,
35
]
, as explained in
[
57
, Section 1.1]
(see Remark
3
below).

Definition 1
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(X=\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{X})
be an
n
-dimensional
Banach space. Also, let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be an inifinite
dimensional Banach space. For
p\in(0,\infty]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

define
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
to be the supremum over those

r\geqslant 0
with the following property. For every Lipschitz function

f:B_{X}\to Y
there exists
y\in X
and
\rho\in[r,\infty)
such
that
y+\rho B_{X}\subseteq B_{X}
, and there exists
a\in Y
and a
linear operator
T:X\to Y
whose operator norm satisfies

\|T\|_{X\to Y}\leqslant 3\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
, such that

\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(x+\rho B_{X})}\int_{y+\rho B_{X}}\left\|f(z)-(a+Tz)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\varepsilon\rho\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

(4)

We call
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\cdot)
the modulus of
L_{p}
affine
approximability corresponding to
X
and
Y
.

Using
the notation of Definition
1
, the quantity
r_{n}(\varepsilon)
that we defined above can be written
as

r_{n}(\varepsilon)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}r_{\infty}^{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}}(\varepsilon).

Indeed, the
L_{\infty}
requirement (
1
)
implies that
\|T\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}}\leqslant(1+2\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leqslant 3\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
. For finite
p
, the
L_{p}
bound (
4
)
does not automatically imply a bound on
\|T\|_{X\to Y}
, which is
the reason why we added the requirement
\|T\|_{X\to Y}\leqslant 3\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
as part of the definition of
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
.

The case
p=\infty
of Definition
1
, for which we shall use
below the simpler notation

r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}r_{\infty}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon),

was introduced by Bates, Johnson, Lindenstrauss, Preiss and
Schechtman
[
6
]
, who proved that
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)>0
for all

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
if and only if
Y
admits an equivalent uniformly
convex norm; see
[
6
]
for beautiful geometric applications of this result. The best known lower bound on
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
(in
terms of
n
,
\varepsilon
and the modulus of uniform convexity of
Y
) was
obtained in
[
57
]
. This bound is

r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant e^{-(n/\varepsilon)^{c(Y)n}},

(5)

where
c(Y)\in(0,\infty)
depends only the modulus of uniform
convexity of the target Banach space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
. An explicit
estimate on
c(Y)
appears in
[
57
, Thm. 1.1]
. Here we obtain
an estimate that is asymptotically better than (
5
) as
n\to\infty
provided that
Y
-valued martingale differences are
unconditional (
Y
is a UMD Banach space). Note that all the classical
reflexive Banach spaces have this property, but one can
construct
[
67
]
uniformly convex Banach spaces that are not UMD.

Formally, a Banach space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is said to be a UMD
Banach space if there exists
\beta\in(1,\infty)
such that if

\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is a
Y
-valued square-integrable martingale
defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every choice of signs
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
we have

\int_{\Omega}\Big\|M_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{n}\left(M_{j}-M_{j-1}\right)\Big\|_{Y}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\leqslant\beta^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left\|M_{n}\right\|_{Y}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}.

(6)

If
Y
is a UMD Banach space then the infimum over those
\beta\in(1,\infty)
for which (
6
) holds true for every
square-integrable
Y
-valued martingale
\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is denoted below by

\beta(Y)
. Examples of UMD Banach spaces include all
L_{p}(\mu)

spaces when
p\in(1,\infty)
, in which case
\beta(L_{p}(\mu))\asymp p^{2}/(p-1)
. See
[
14
]
and the references therein for
more information on UMD spaces.

Theorem
2
below asserts an improved lower bound
on the modulus of affine approximability
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
, provided
that
Y
is a UMD space.

Theorem 2
.

There is a universal constant
c\in[1,\infty)
such that for
every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in[2,\infty)
, if
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is an

n
-dimensional normed space and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space
satisfying
\beta(Y)\leqslant\beta
then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
we have

r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant\exp\left(-\frac{(\beta n)^{c\beta}}{\varepsilon^{c(n+\beta)}}\right).

Remark 3
.

By substituting Theorem
2
into equation (12) of
[
57
]
one obtains a bound on Bourgain’s discretization modulus in the special case of UMD targets that improves over the bound that was deduced in
[
57
, Section 1.1]
and matches Bourgain’s original bound
[
13
]
. Specifically, one obtains the refined estimate that appears in equation (2) of
[
35
]
. This yields a new proof of the best known general bound in Bourgain’s discretization problem via an approach that is entirely different from Bourgain’s method, albeit in the special (though still very general) case of UMD targets. We note that due to the recent progress in
[
35
]
a stronger bound is available here when the target is
L_{p}
.

The main reason why we study here the modulus of
L_{p}
affine
approximability
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
is that it relates to
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
through Lemma
4
below, whose simple
proof appears in Section
2.1
. The advantage
of working with finite
p
is that it allows us to use a variety of
analytic tools, such as vector-valued Littlewood–Paley theory and complex
interpolation.

Lemma 4
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Suppose that
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})

and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
are Banach spaces with
\dim(X)=n
. Then for
every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we have

r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant r_{p}^{X\to Y}\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{9}\right)^{1+\frac{n}{p}}\right).

Due to Lemma
4
, Theorem
2

is a consequence of Theorem
5
below, which is
our main result. Its proof is based on a vector-valued variant of an
argument of Dorronsoro
[
26
]
, combined with a wide variety of
additional analytic and geometric ingredients of independent interest.

Theorem 5
.

There exist universal constants
\kappa,C\in[1,\infty)
such that
for every
\beta\in[2,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is an

n
-dimensional normed space and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space
satisfying
\beta(Y)\leqslant\beta
, then for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we have

r_{\kappa\beta}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant\exp\left(-\frac{(\beta n)^{C\kappa\beta}}{\varepsilon^{\kappa\beta}}\right).

1.2
Previous work

Over the past several decades, research on quantitative differentiation has proceeded roughly along two lines of inquiry, one of which arising from functional analysis and metric geometry and the other arising from rectifiability questions in harmonic analysis. The present work belongs to the former direction, but its main contribution is the use of methods from the latter direction in this new context while incorporating various Banach space theoretic tools.

Bates, Johnson, Lindenstrauss, Preiss and Schechtman studied
[
6
]
quantitative differentiation in order to prove the rigidity of certain classes of Banach spaces under nonlinear quotients. The same notion was used in
[
57
]
for metric embeddings, namely as an alternative approach to Bourgain’s discretization problem
[
13
]
when the target Banach space is uniformly convex. The methods in this context fall under the category of (extensions of) “approximate midpoint arguments,” as initiated by Enflo
[
7
]
to prove that
L_{1}
is not uniformly homeomorphic to
\ell_{1}
, and further developed in
[
13
,
27
,
46
,
65
]
(see also Chapter 10 of
[
8
]
). As examples of the many related applications to quantitative embedding theory and rigidity questions, see also
[
60
,
54
,
20
,
27
,
19
,
64
,
55
,
56
]
.

Parallel developments of a different nature arose in harmonic analysis, as part of the quest to develop a quantitative theory of rectifiability, with applications to singular integrals. Notable contributions along these lines include classical works of Stein (see the monograph
[
72
]
), through the works of Dorronsoro
[
26
]
, Jones
[
48
,
49
]
, David and Semmes
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
, as well as the more recent work of Azzam and Schul
[
2
]
. The work of Dorronsoro
[
26
]
directly influenced the present article, and we were also greatly inspired by the works of David and Semmes
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. These works introduced and studied quantities that correspond to Definition
1
when
X
is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y
is the real line. Such methods also yield results for mappings from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, but with statements that include implicit parameters that are allowed to depend on
m,n
. In
[
24
]
David and Semmes compare their work to that of Bates, Johnson, Lindenstrauss, Preiss and Schechtman
[
6
]
, noting that the latter methods are different, and even yield results for infinite dimensional spaces.

Our contribution here follows the harmonic-analytic methodology, while overcoming several difficulties. Firstly, the literature on quantitative rectifiability ignores the dependence on the dimension
n
, while this dependence is the main topic of interest in the present context. In fact, a direct examination of the dependence on
n
that is implicit in the above cited works reveals that it is insufficient for the purpose of obtaining improved bounds on
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
. Secondly, in our setting the domain
X
is a general
n
-dimensional normed space
X
rather than a Euclidean space, and our arguments address this point. A final important difference is that we treat infinite dimensional Banach spaces
Y
as targets. Overcoming this requires substantial effort, because the infinite dimensional arguments of Bates, Johnson, Lindenstrauss, Preiss and Schechtman
[
6
]
do not seem to be applicable in our setting. The present work yields an infinite dimensional version of an inequality that Dorronsoro obtained
[
26
]
for real-valued functions. Such an infinite dimensional extension of Dorronsoro’s work is not routine, and in particular it does not hold true for arbitrary infinite dimensional Banach space targets
Y
. In fact, the geometry of
Y
influences the structure of the inequality thus obtained, while stronger inequalities hold true for real-valued functions. The assumption that
Y
is UMD is used several times in our argument through a rich UMD-valued Fourier-analytic toolkit that has been developed by many authors over the past four decades.

1.3
Open questions

We list below some open questions that arise naturally from our work.

Question 6

(Asymptotics of
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
)
.

Obviously, the most tantalizing open question in the present context is to determine the rate at which
r_{n}
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
, even roughly: say, is this rate exponential, doubly exponential, or of some intermediate behavior? More importantly for potential applications, it remains open to obtain sharp bounds on the quantity
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
when
X
is a finite dimensional Banach space,
Y
is a uniformly convex Banach space or belongs to some important class of Banach spaces (e.g. UMD spaces or uniformly convex lattices), and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Question 7

(Infinite dimensional domains)
.

The question of characterizing those pairs of Banach space
X,Y
for which
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)>0
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
was solved in
[
6
]
also when
\dim(X)=\infty
and
\dim(Y)<\infty
: this happens if and only if
X
admits an equivalent uniformly smooth norm.
It remains open to understand the asymptotic behavior of
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
in the setting of uniformly smooth infinite dimensional domains and finite dimensional ranges. In particular, the rate at which
r^{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}^{n}}(\frac{1}{4})
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
is unknown. An explicit lower bound on
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
in terms of the modulus of uniform smoothness of
X
and
\dim(Y)
can be deduced from an examination of the proof in
[
6
]
, but we believe that this bound is far from being optimal.

Question 8

(Uniformly convex targets)
.

As stated earlier, by
[
6
]
we know that if
\dim(X)=n<\infty
and
\dim(Y)=\infty
then
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)>0
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
if and only if
Y
admits and equivalent uniformly convex norm. However, the best known lower bounds on
r^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
in this (maximal) generality remain those of
[
57
]
, and these bounds are weaker than Theorem
2
(as
n\to\infty
), which does not cover all uniformly convex ranges
Y
because Pisier
[
67
]
proved that there exist uniformly convex Banach spaces that are not UMD (even such uniformly convex Banach lattices exist, as shown by Bourgain in
[
11
]
; see also the recent example by Qiu
[
69
]
). It would be interesting to obtain an improved bound as in Theorem
2
under the weaker assumption that
Y
is uniformly convex. Our proof of Theorem
2
definitely uses properties of
Y
that imply the UMD property (e.g., we rely on the boundedness of the
Y
-valued Hilbert transform, which was shown by Bourgain
[
11
]
to imply that
Y
is UMD). However, the conclusion of Theorem
2
, or even Theorem
5
, may be valid when
Y
is uniformly convex, and the same holds true for some of our other results, such as Theorem
19
below. Certain aspects of vector-valued Littelwood–Paley theory are known to hold true for uniformly convex targets (see e.g.
[
59
]
), so it would be interesting to investigate the extent to which the UMD property is needed for our results. If, on the other hand,
Theorem
5
or Theorem
19
imply the UMD property then this would be a new characterization of UMD spaces.

Question 9

(Asymptotics of
r^{X\to Y}_{p}(\varepsilon)
for finite
p
)
.

It would be interesting to understand the asymptotic behavior of the modulus of
L_{p}
affine approximability
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
, even in the special case when
X
and
Y
are both Hilbert spaces,
p=2
and, say,
\varepsilon=1/2
. A careful examination of the proof of Theorem
5
in this Hilbertian setting (in which case some of the steps that we perform below are not needed, and several estimates can be easily improved) reveals that for some
c\in(0,\infty)
,

\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\ \forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),\qquad r_{2}^{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}}(\varepsilon)\gtrsim e^{-\frac{c(n\log n)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}}.

(7)

We do not know the extent to which (
7
) is best possible; it seems plausible that with more work one could improve the dependence on
n
in the exponent, but we do not presently have an upper bound that comes close to the lower bound in (
7
). Note that while the moduli
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
are interesting in their own right, we do not have geometric applications of them as in the case
p=\infty
. So, as a more amorphous research direction, it would be interesting to find geometric applications of bounds on
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
(other than as a tool to bound
r_{\infty}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
, which is the application that we present here).

2
Geometric preliminaries

Fix from now on an integer
n
and an
n
-dimensional normed space

(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
. We shall also fix a normed space

(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
. In later sections we will need
Y
to be a UMD
Banach space, but the statements of the present section hold true when

Y
is a general normed space.

By John’s theorem
[
45
]
there exists a scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
X
with respect to which we can identify
X
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we have

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\|x\|_{2}\leqslant\|x\|_{X}\leqslant\sqrt{n}\cdot\|x\|_{2},

(8)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}=\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}}
. We shall also use the standard notation
B^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}B_{\ell_{2}^{n}}
and
S^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\partial B^{n}
. This Euclidean structure will be fixed from now on. Despite the fact that
X
is now endowed with two metrics (those induced by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
), we shall tacitly maintain throughout the ensuing discussion the convention that whenever
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f:\Omega\to Y
then
\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\Omega)}
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
f
with respect to the metric induced by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, i.e.,

\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{Y}}{\|x-y\|_{X}}.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
we shall also use the shorter notation
\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

We shall use standard notation for vector-valued
L_{p}
spaces. Specifically, for every measurable subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive Lebesgue measure and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we let
L_{p}(\Omega,Y)
denote the space of all measurable functions
f:\Omega\to Y
such that

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\|f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Given
f:\Omega\to Y
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
f^{x}:\Omega-x\to Y
the translate of
f
by
x
, i.e.,

\forall\,y\in\Omega-x,\qquad f^{x}(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(x+y).

(9)

For
u\in(0,\infty)
and
f\in L_{1}(uB^{n},Y)
let
T_{u}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
be the linear operator defined by

\forall\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad T_{u}f(w)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{n+2}{{V}_{n}u}\int_{B^{n}}\langle z,w\rangle f(uz)\mathrm{d}z,

(10)

where

V_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{vol}(B^{n})=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}.

The operator norm of
T_{u}f
can be bounded in terms of the Lipschitz constant of
f
as follows.

Lemma 10
.

Fix
u\in(0,\infty)
and a Lipschitz map
f:uB^{n}\to Y
. Then

\|T_{u}f\|_{X\to Y}\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(uB^{n})}.

Proof.

By rescaling we may assume that
u=1
. Take
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
consider the following affine hyperplane.

H_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \left\langle y,\frac{w}{\|w\|_{2}}\right\rangle=t\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then by the definition (
10
) and Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle T_{1}f(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2)\|w\|_{2}}{V_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t\int_{H_{t}\cap B^{n}}f(z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2)\|w\|_{2}}{V_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t\int_{H_{t}\cap B^{n}}\left(f(z)-f\left(z-\frac{2tw}{\|w\|_{2}}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|T_{1}f(w)\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(n+2)\|w\|_{2}}{V_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t\int_{H_{t}\cap B^{n}}\left\|f(z)-f\left(z-\frac{2tw}{\|w\|_{2}}\right)\right\|_{Y}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2(n+2)\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(B^{n})}\|w\|_{X}}{V_{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2}\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}\left(H_{t}\cap B^{n}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+2)\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(B^{n})}\|w\|_{X}}{V_{n}}\int_{B^{n}}x_{1}^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(B^{n})}\|w\|_{X}.\qed

We also record for future use the following simple estimate.

Lemma 11
.

For every
u\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
we have the following operator norm bound.

\|T_{u}\|_{L_{p}(uB^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(uB^{n},Y)}\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}.

Proof.

By rescaling we may assume that
u=1
. If
f\in L_{p}(B^{n},Y)
then

\displaystyle\|T_{1}f\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n+2}{V_{n}}\int_{B^{n}}\left\|w\mapsto\langle z,w\rangle f(z)\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}\mathrm{d}z

(11)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{V_{n}}\bigg(\int_{B^{n}}|w_{1}|^{p}dw\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{B^{n}}\|z\|_{2}\cdot\|f(z)\|_{Y}\mathrm{d}z

(12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}\|f\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)},

(13)

where in (
11
) we used the definition (
10
) and the triangle inequality in
L_{p}(B^{n},Y)
, in (
12
) we used rotation invariance, and (
13
) follows from Hölder’s inequality combined with the following fact, which can be verified by a direct computation and is also, say, a special case of inequality (4) in
[
5
]
.

\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{n}}\int_{B^{n}}|w_{1}|^{p}\mathrm{d}w\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\asymp\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}},1\right\}.\qed

For
u\in(0,\infty)
and
f\in L_{1}(uB^{n},Y)
define

P^{0}_{u}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{V_{n}}\int_{B^{n}}f(uz)\mathrm{d}z\in Y.

(14)

Thus, for every
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in L_{1}(\Omega,Y)
, if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in(0,\infty)
satisfy
x+uB^{n}\subseteq\Omega
then the vector
P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\in Y
is the mean of
f
over
x+uB^{n}
. The following simple estimate will be used later.

Lemma 12
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Every measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\leqslant\frac{2^{p}}{n+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(y)-f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+q}}\mathrm{d}y.

Proof.

Recalling (
14
), it follows from the triangle inequality in
L_{p}(B^{n},Y)
and convexity that

\forall\,v\in Y,\qquad\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant 2^{p}\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-v\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y.

By choosing here
v=f(x)
, we see that for every
u\in(0,\infty)
we have

\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant 2^{p}\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-f(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y.

Hence, denoting the surface measure on
S^{n-1}
by
\sigma
, by integrating in polar coordinates we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-f(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S^{n-1}}r^{n-1}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(urw)-f(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}r^{n-1}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(sw)-f(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{(s/r)^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(w)\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{r}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p}}{n+q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(y)-f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+q}}\mathrm{d}y.

∎

Define an affine mapping
P^{1}_{u}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
by

P^{1}_{u}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}P^{0}_{u}f+T_{u}f.

(15)

By a simple change of variable, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\left(P^{1}_{u}f^{x}\right)^{-x}(y)=P^{0}_{u}f^{x}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{y_{j}-x_{j}}{\int_{x+uB}(w_{j}-x_{j})^{2}\mathrm{d}w}\int_{x+uB}(z_{j}-x_{j})f(z)\mathrm{d}z.

(16)

Consequently, if
f
were a real-valued function then
(P^{1}_{u}f^{x})^{-x}
would be the orthogonal projection in
L_{2}(x+uB^{n})
of
f
onto the subspace consisting of all the affine mappings. Lemma
13
below shows that for every
p\in[1,\infty]
, if
f\in L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)
then the distance between
f
and
(P^{1}_{u}f^{x})^{-x}
in
L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)
is controlled by the distance of
f
to the subspace of
L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)
consisting of all the affine mappings. Such a statement was previously proved in
[
26
]
, but since the dependence on
n
and
p
is important in the present context, and is only implicit in
[
26
]
, we include its proof.

Lemma 13
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty]
,
u\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f\in L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)
and that
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is affine. Then

\left\|f^{x}-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{L_{p}(uB^{n},Y)}=\left\|f-\left(P^{1}_{u}f^{x}\right)^{-x}\right\|_{L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)}\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}\cdot\left\|f-\Lambda\right\|_{L_{p}(x+uB^{n},Y)}.

Proof.

By translation and a rescaling we may assume that
x=0
and
u=1
. Since
P_{1}^{1}\Lambda=\Lambda
,

\displaystyle\left\|f-P^{1}_{1}f\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|f-\Lambda\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}+\left\|P^{1}_{1}(f-\Lambda)\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def P0}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle\left\|f-\Lambda\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}+\left\|P^{0}_{1}(f-\Lambda)\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}+\left\|T_{1}(f-\Lambda)\right\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}.

It remains to note that
\|P^{0}_{1}(f-\Lambda)\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}\leqslant\|f-\Lambda\|_{L_{p}(B^{n},Y)}
since
P_{1}^{0}
is an averaging operator, and to apply Lemma
11
.
∎

We end this section by recording for ease of later reference two consequences of Lemma

.

Corollary 14
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Every measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}}{(q+n)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(y)-f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+q}}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

Fix
u\in(0,\infty)
. Lemma
13
implies that every affine mapping
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies

\left(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}\left(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(17)

An application of (
17
) when
\Lambda
is the constant
P^{0}_{u}f^{x}
shows that for every
u\in(0,\infty)
we have

\left(\int_{B^{n}}\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}\left(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{0}_{u}f^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This implies the desired estimate due to Lemma
12
.∎

Corollary 15
.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in(p,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is smooth. Then

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim\frac{p\min\left\{\sqrt{pn},n\right\}}{q(n+q-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x+y)-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+q-p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(18)

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in(0,\infty)
. For every
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
define

a_{j}(u,x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{1}P^{0}_{su}f_{j}^{x}\mathrm{d}s\in Y,\qquad\mathrm{where}\qquad f_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}.

(19)

Also, define an affine function
\Lambda_{x,u}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
by setting

\forall\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\Lambda_{x,u}(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}a_{j}(u,x).

(20)

An application of (
17
) with
\Lambda=\Lambda_{x,u}
shows that

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim\min\{\sqrt{pn},n\}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda_{x,u}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(21)

Observe that

\displaystyle f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda_{x,u}(uy)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def Lambda choice}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}f^{x}(suy)\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=1}^{n}uy_{j}a_{j}(u,x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def aj}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}uy_{j}\big(f_{j}^{x}(suy)-P^{0}_{su}f_{j}^{x}\big)\mathrm{d}s.

By the triangle inequality in
L_{p}(B^{n},Y)
, this implies that for every
u\in(0,\infty)
,

\left(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda_{x,u}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}u\bigg(\int_{B^{n}}|y_{j}|^{p}\left\|f_{j}^{x}(suy)-P^{0}_{su}f_{j}^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}s\\
\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{u}\bigg(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f_{j}^{x}(ty)-P^{0}_{t}f_{j}^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{u}h_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}t,

(22)

where we used the crude estimate
\max_{y\in B^{n}}\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}|y_{j}|\leqslant 1
and introduced the notation

\forall\,(j,t)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\times(0,\infty),\qquad h_{j}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigg(\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f_{j}^{x}(ty)-P^{0}_{t}f_{j}^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(23)

A combination of (
22
) with the triangle inequality and Hardy’s inequality
[
72
, Section A.4]
yields

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda_{x,u}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{u}h_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{q+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{p}{q}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{h_{j}(u)^{p}}{u^{q-p+1}}\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def hj}}}{{=}}\frac{p}{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f_{j}^{x}(ty)-P^{0}_{t}f_{j}^{x}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{q-p+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(24)

By applying Lemma
12
to each of the functions
\{h_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
(with
q
replaced by
q-p
), we conclude from (
24
) that the following estimate holds true.

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-\Lambda_{x,u}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{q+1}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{p}{q(n+q-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|f_{j}^{x}(y)-f_{j}(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+q-p}}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Recalling the definition of
f_{j}
in (
19
), the desired estimate (
18
) now follows from (
21
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
4

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and denote
\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(\varepsilon/9)^{1+n/p}
. Fix also

0<r<r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\delta)
and a
1
-Lipschitz function
f:B_{X}\to Y
. By
the definition of
r_{p}^{X\to Y}(\delta)
, we can find
y\in X
and

\rho\geqslant r
such that
y+\rho B_{X}\subseteq B_{X}
, and there exists an
affine mapping
\Lambda:X\to Y
with
\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leqslant 3
, such
that

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\rho B_{X})}\int_{y+\rho B_{X}}\left\|f(u)-\Lambda(u)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}u\leqslant\delta^{p}\rho^{p}=\frac{\varepsilon^{p+n}\rho^{p}}{9^{p+n}}.

(25)

We claim that (
25
), combined with the fact that

\|f-\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}+\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leqslant 4
, yields

\forall\,z\in y+\rho B_{X},\qquad\left\|f(z)-\Lambda(z)\right\|_{Y}\leqslant\varepsilon\rho,

(26)

thus completing the proof of Lemma
4
.

Assume for the sake of obtaining a contradiction
that (
26
) fails. Then there exists

z\in y+\rho B_{x}
such that
\left\|f(z)-\Lambda(z)\right\|_{Y}>\varepsilon\rho
. Write
\lambda=\varepsilon/9
and define
w=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)z
.
Observe that we have
\|w-z\|_{X}=\lambda\|y-z\|_{X}\leqslant\lambda\rho
. Supposing that

u\in X
satisfies
\|u-w\|_{X}\leqslant\lambda\rho
, we therefore obtain

\|u-y\|_{X}=\left\|(u-w)-(1-\lambda)(y-z)\right\|_{X}\leqslant\|u-w\|+(1-\lambda)\|y-z\|_{X}\leqslant\rho.

(27)

Moreover, since
\|f-\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leqslant 4
we have

\|f(u)-\Lambda(u)\|_{Y}\geqslant\|f(z)-\Lambda(z)\|_{Y}-4\|u-z\|_{X}\\
>\varepsilon\rho-4\|u-w\|_{X}-4\|w-z\|_{X}\geqslant\varepsilon\rho-8\lambda\rho=\lambda\rho.

(28)

It follows from (
27
) that
w+\lambda\rho B_{X}\subseteq y+\rho B_{X}
, and it follows from (
28
) that for
u\in w+\lambda\rho B_{X}
the integrand in the
left hand side of (
25
) is strictly larger than

\lambda\rho
. Hence, (
25
) yields the following
contradiction.

\lambda^{p+n}\rho^{p}=\frac{\varepsilon^{p+n}\rho^{p}}{9^{p+n}}>\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\lambda\rho B_{X})}{\mathrm{vol}(\rho B_{X})}\lambda^{p}\rho^{p}=\lambda^{p+n}\rho^{p}.

∎

2.2
An upper bound on the modulus of affine approximability

The example that is constructed below was obtained in collaboration with Charles Fefferman; we thank him for agreeing that we include it here. A simple construction from
[
57
]
shows that if
p\in[2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
then

r^{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}(\ell_{p})}(\varepsilon)\lesssim\frac{e^{-1/(c\varepsilon)^{p}}}{\sqrt{n}},

(29)

where
c\in(0,\infty)
is a universal constant. We shall now show how the example of
[
57
]
can be tensorized so as to yield an improved dependence on
n
, and we shall also briefly discuss the problem of bounding
r_{q}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)
for finite
q\geqslant 1
. The following lemma is an
L_{q}
-variant of Lemma 4.1 of
[
57
]
.

Lemma 16
.

There exists a universal constant
C\in(0,\infty)
with the following property. For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty)
there exists a
1
-Lipschitz function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}
such that for every
q\in[1,\infty]
and every affine mapping
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}
, if
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
-1\leqslant a<b\leqslant 1
then

\frac{b-a}{2}\geqslant 4e^{-(C/\varepsilon)^{p}}\implies\left(\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}>\varepsilon\cdot\frac{b-a}{2}.

(30)

Consequently,

r_{q}^{\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}}(\varepsilon)\lesssim e^{-(C/\varepsilon)^{p}}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the piecewise affine (“sawtooth”) function defined by
\varphi(2{{\mathbb{Z}}})=\{0\}
and
\varphi(1+2{{\mathbb{Z}}})=\{1\}
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
that will be determined later. Denoting the standard basis of
\ell_{p}^{m}
by
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
, define
f:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}^{m}
by setting for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{m^{1/p}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\varphi\left(2^{k}x\right)}{2^{k}}e_{k}.

(31)

Since
\varphi
is
1
-Lipschitz, it follows from (
31
) that also
f
is
1
-Lipschitz.

Fix
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
-1\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant 1
and
b-a\geqslant 4/2^{m}
. There exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
4/2^{k}\leqslant b-a\leqslant 8/2^{k}
. Hence there is
j\in\{0,\ldots,2^{k-1}-1\}
for which
[j/2^{k-1},(j+1)/2^{k-1}]\subseteq[a,b]
. Then, since
b-a\leqslant 8/2^{k}
, for every affine mapping
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}^{m}
we have

\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}x\gtrsim 2^{k}\int_{j/2^{k-1}}^{(j+1)/2^{k-1}}\left\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}x.

(32)

Writing
\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m})
, where
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{m}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are affine, it follows from (
31
) and (
32
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\gtrsim 2^{k}\int_{j/2^{k-1}}^{(j+1)/2^{k-1}}\left|\frac{\varphi\left(2^{k}x\right)}{m^{1/q}2^{k}}-\Lambda_{k}(x)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{q/p}2^{qk}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\varphi(y)-\mathscr{L}(y)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}y,

(33)

where
\mathscr{L}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the affine function given for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
by
\mathscr{L}(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}m^{1/p}2^{k}\Lambda_{k}((y+2j+1)/2^{k})
. Recalling (
14
) and (
16
), we have
P_{1}^{1}\varphi\equiv 1/2
. Hence, by Lemma
13
,

\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\varphi(y)-\mathscr{L}(y)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\gtrsim\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\varphi(y)-\frac{1}{2}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\gtrsim 1.

(34)

Since
b-a\asymp 2^{-k}
, by combining (
2.2
) and (
34
) we see that

\left(\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\left\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\right\|_{p}^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geqslant\frac{\eta}{m^{1/p}}\cdot\frac{b-a}{2},

(35)

where
\eta\in(0,\infty)
is a universal constant. Suppose that
\varepsilon<\eta
and choose
m
to be the largest positive integer such that
m<(\eta/\varepsilon)^{p}
. Then (
35
) implies the conclusion of (
30
). The requirement here is that
b-a\geqslant 4/2^{m}
, and since
m+1\geqslant(\eta/\varepsilon)^{p}
, this requirement is satisfied if
b-a\geqslant 8/2^{(\eta/\varepsilon)^{p}}
, which follows from
(b-a)/2\geqslant 4e^{-(\eta/(2\varepsilon))^{p}}
. Thus (
30
) holds true with
C=\eta/2
. Note that, with this choice of
C
, the implication (
30
) holds vacuously when
\varepsilon\geqslant\eta
.
∎

Lemma
17
below tensorizes Lemma
16
to improve over (
29
), obtaining exponential decay.

Lemma 17
.

There exists universal constants
K,\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, every
p\in[1,\infty)
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

r^{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}(\ell_{p})}(\varepsilon)\leqslant e^{-n(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}.

(36)

Proof.

By Lemma
16
(with
q=\infty
) we can fix
K,\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
there exists a
1
-Lipschitz mapping
f:[-1,1]\to\ell_{p}
such that for every interval
[a,b]\subseteq[-1,1]
with
(b-a)/2>e^{-(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
and for every affine mapping
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}
there exists
x\in[a,b]
such that
\|f(x)-\Lambda(x)\|_{p}>\varepsilon(b-a)/2
.

Define
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p})
by setting for every
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

F(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left(f\left(x_{1}\right),\frac{f\left(e^{(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}x_{2}\right)}{e^{(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}},\ldots,\frac{f\left(e^{(n-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}x_{n}\right)}{e^{(n-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}}\right)\in\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p}).

By applying the
1
-Lipschitz condition for
f
coordinate-wise, we see that
F
is
1
-Lipschitz as a mapping from
\ell_{2}^{n}
to
\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p})
. Fix
x\in B^{n}
and
r\in(0,1)
such that
x+rB^{n}\subseteq B^{n}
. Suppose from now on that
r>e^{-n(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
and let
j\in\mathbb{N}
be the largest integer for which
r\leqslant e^{-(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
. Then
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}r>e^{-(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
. Let
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p})
be an affine mapping, and write
\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})
, where
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ell_{p}
are affine. Consider the affine mapping
\Lambda_{j}^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}\to\ell_{p}
given by
\Lambda_{j}^{\prime}(y)=\Lambda_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},y,x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Set
a=x_{j}e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}-re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
and
b=x_{j}e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}+re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
. Then

\frac{b-a}{2}=re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}>e^{-(K/\varepsilon)^{p}},

so by our assumption on
f
there exists
w\in\left[x_{j}e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}-re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}},x_{j}e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}+re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}\right]
with

\left\|f(w)-e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}\Lambda_{j}^{\prime}\left(we^{-(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}\right)\right\|_{p}>\varepsilon re^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}.

Setting
y=we^{-(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
, this is the same as asserting that there exists
y\in[x_{j}-r,x_{j}+r]
with

\left\|e^{-(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}f\left(e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}y\right)-\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}(y)\right\|_{p}>\varepsilon r.

Hence, writing
z=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},y,x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n})
, we have that
z\in x+rB^{n}
and

\left\|F(z)-\Lambda(z)\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p})}\geqslant\left\|e^{-(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}f\left(e^{(j-1)(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}y\right)-\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}(y)\right\|_{p}>\varepsilon r,

(37)

Since (
37
) holds true for every affine mapping
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ell_{2}^{n}(\ell_{p})
whenever
x+rB^{n}\subseteq B^{n}
and
r>e^{-n(K/\varepsilon)^{p}}
, the proof of (
36
) is complete.
∎

Remark 18
.

An upper bound on
r_{p}^{\ell_{2}^{n}\to\ell_{2}(\ell_{p})}(\varepsilon)
is a consequence of Lemma
17
and Lemma
4
. It seems likely that a significantly stronger upper bound holds true, but the above tensorization procedure does not seem to yield such an improvement. We leave the investigation of upper bounds on the modulus of
L_{p}
affine approximability as an interesting question for future research.

3
A UMD-valued Dorronsoro-type estimate

The main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
5
(hence also Theorem
2
) is the following result.

Theorem 19
.

There exists a universal constant
\kappa\in[2,\infty)
with the following property. Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space and write
\beta=\beta(Y)
. Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is a Lipschitz and compactly supported function. Then

\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{n}}\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}}{u^{1+\kappa\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\lesssim\beta^{15}n^{\frac{5}{2}}\big(\mathrm{vol}(\operatorname{supp}(f))\big)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

(38)

In the case of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such a statement was first proved by Dorronsoro
[
26
]
, with an implicit dependence on the dimension
n
.
Extensions and variants of Dorronsoro’s theorem have been further studied within the theory of functions spaces, where norms like the one that appears on the left of (
38
) define what is called
local approximation spaces
. See
[
75
, Section 1.7]
for some discussion of the subsequent history and
[
75
, Section 3.5.1]
for a different proof of a similar statement. There, the dependence on
n
is perhaps even more implicit, since it also depends on a non-canonical choice of resolutions of identity used to define some general function spaces.

This dependence on dimension is crucial for us here; specifically, we desire polynomial growth in
n
in the right hand side of (
38
), while a naïve examination of the proof in
[
26
]
reveals that it yields a much worse (super-exponential) dependence on
n
. Note also that in
[
26
]
the
L_{\kappa\beta}
norm that appears in (
38
) can be replaced by an
L_{p}
norm for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, while in the present vector-valued setting the geometry of the target space
Y
influences the value of
p
. In fact, we shall prove a more refined (and stronger) version of Theorem
19
; see Theorem
40
below, in which the
L_{\kappa\beta}
norm that appears in (
38
) can be replaced by an
L_{p}
norm provided
Y
is a UMD Banach space of Rademacher cotype
p
(see Section
4.4
below). These refinements are not important for our purposes, i.e., for proving Theorem
5
, but they do imply sharper results, e.g. when
Y
is itself an
L_{q}(\mu)
space. In the same vein, the exponent
15
of
\beta
in (
38
) is not sharp (for the purpose of Theorem
5
we only desire a polynomial dependence on
\beta
).

The bulk of the ensuing discussion is devoted to the proof of Theorem
19
. Our argument roughly follows the strategy of Dorronsoro in
[
26
]
, combined with substantial additions and modifications in order to obtain good dependence on
n
and also overcome difficulties that arise in the vector-valued setting and are not present in the real-valued setting of
[
26
]
. As explained in the Introduction, these complications reflect a genuine difference between the vector-valued setting and the real-valued setting, as such results do not hold true for general Banach space targets
Y
, so the geometry of
Y
must somehow enter into the argument. We did not investigate the extent to which Theorem
19
(and Theorem
40
below) are sharp in terms of the assumptions that are required from
Y
and the
L_{p}
norm that could appear in (
38
) (or (
137
) below).

3.1
Proof of Theorem
5
assuming the validity of Theorem
19

Here we shall prove Theorem
5
while using Theorem
19
. This will allow us to focus later on Theorem
19
itself in order complete the justification of Theorem
5
, and hence, by Lemma
4
, also (
3
) and Theorem
2
.

Recalling our setting, we are given a
n
-dimensional normed space
(X=\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{X})
such that (
8
) holds true. We are also given a normed space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
that satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
19
. Suppose that
f:B_{X}\to Y
is
1
-Lipschitz. Without loss of generality assume also that
f(0)=0
.

Define
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

\forall\,u\in[0,\infty),\qquad\phi(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mathrm{if}\ u\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},\\
n+1-n^{\frac{3}{2}}u&\mathrm{if}\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}<u\leqslant\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ u>\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\phi
is supported in
[0,(1+1/n)/\sqrt{n}]
and it is elementary to verify the following inequalities.

\forall\,u\in(0,\infty),\qquad\max\big\{\phi(u),\sqrt{n}u\phi(u)\big\}\leqslant 1.

(39)

and

\forall\,u,v\in(0,\infty),\qquad\big|\phi(u)-\phi(v)\big|\cdot\min\{u,v\}\leqslant(n+1)|u-v|.

(40)

Since by (
8
) we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\subseteq B_{X}
, by (
39
) we know that every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies
\phi(\|x\|_{2})x\in B_{X}
. We can therefore define
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
by
F(x)=f(\phi(\|x\|_{2})x)
. Then,
F(x)=f(x)
if
\|x\|_{2}\leqslant 1/\sqrt{n}
, and
F(x)=0
if
\|x\|_{2}\geqslant(1+1/n)/\sqrt{n}
. Also, every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|y\|_{X}\leqslant\|x\|_{X}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|F(x)-F(y)\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(B_{X})}\big\|\phi(\|x\|_{2})x-\phi(\|y\|_{2})y\big\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\phi(\|x\|_{2})\|x-y\|_{X}+\big|\phi(\|x\|_{2})-\phi(\|y\|_{2})\big|\|y\|_{X}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:john posiiton}\wedge\eqref{eq:phi upper}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{X}+\big|\phi(\|x\|_{2})-\|\phi(\|y\|_{2})\big|\sqrt{n}\|y\|_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:phi lip}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{X}+\sqrt{n}(n+1)\|x-y\|_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:john posiiton}}}{{\leqslant}}

\displaystyle\left(n^{\frac{3}{2}}+\sqrt{n}+1\right)\|x-y\|_{X}.

Thus
\|F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim n^{\frac{3}{2}}
. Since
\operatorname{supp}(F)\subseteq\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}
, it therefore follows from Theorem
19
that

\displaystyle\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|F^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}F^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}}{V_{n}u^{1+\kappa\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant K\beta^{15}n^{\frac{5}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{n}{\kappa\beta}}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle<eK\beta^{15}n^{4}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}},

(41)

where
K\in(0,\infty)
is a universal constant. Note that

(x,u)\in\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\right)\times\left(0,\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)\implies x+uB^{n}\subseteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}.

Since
F
coincides with
f
on
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}
, it follows that

(x,y,u)\in\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\right)\times B^{n}\times\left(0,\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)\implies P^{1}_{u}F^{x}=P^{1}_{u}f^{x}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad F^{x}(uy)=f^{x}(uy).

These observations in combination with (
3.1
) imply that

\bigg(\iiint_{\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\right)\times B^{n}\times\left(0,\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}}{V_{n}u^{1+\kappa\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}<K\beta^{15}n^{4}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}.

(42)

Fix
M\in(1,\infty)
whose precise value will be specified later. It follows from (
42
) that there exist

u\in\left(\frac{1}{Mn^{2/3}},\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad x\in\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n},

(43)

such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{n}}\int_{B^{n}}\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\leqslant\frac{2eK\beta^{15}n^{4}u}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}.

(44)

Indeed, we would otherwise have

\bigg(\iiint_{\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\right)\times B^{n}\times\left(0,\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\right)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}}{V_{n}u^{1+\kappa\beta}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\\
>\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{n}{\kappa\beta}}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\left(\int_{\frac{n^{-3/2}}{M}}^{n^{-3/2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\frac{2eK\beta^{15}n^{4}}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}\geqslant eK\beta^{15}n^{4}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}},

thus contradicting (
42
). Observe that by Lemma
10
we have

\|P_{u}^{1}f^{x}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def P1}}}{{=}}\|T_{u}f^{x}\|_{X\to Y}\leqslant\|f^{x}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant 1.

Hence, if we set
\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}P^{1}_{x}f^{x}
then
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is affine,
\|\Lambda\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant 1
, and by a change of variable one can rewrite (
44
) as follows.

\bigg(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(x+uB^{n})}\int_{x+uB^{n}}\left\|f(z)-\Lambda(z)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\leqslant\frac{2eK\beta^{15}n^{4}u}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}.

(45)

This is not quite the type of conclusion that we desire, because the averaging in (
45
) occurs over a Euclidean ball rather than a ball in
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{X})
. We overcome this via another averaging step.

Observe that

x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\subseteq x+uB^{n}\subseteq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}B^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:john posiiton}}}{{\subseteq}}B_{X}.

(46)

Indeed, if
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
\|a\|_{2}\leqslant(1-1/n)u
and
\|b\|_{X}\leqslant u/n
then,

\|a+b\|_{2}\leqslant\|a\|_{2}+\|b\|_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:john posiiton}}}{{\leqslant}}\|a\|_{2}+\|b\|_{X}\leqslant\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)u+\frac{u}{n}=u,

Now, define
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows.

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{(y,w):\ y\in x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}\ \wedge\ w\in y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(y,w):\ w\in x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\ \wedge\ y\in\left(x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}\right)\cap\left(w+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right)\right\}.

By (
46
),
f(w)
is well-defined for every
(y,w)\in A
. It therefore follows from Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\iint_{A}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}}\mathrm{d}y\int_{y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\int_{x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}}\mathrm{vol}\left(\left(x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}\right)\cap\left(w+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right)\right)\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{vol}\left(\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right)\int_{x+uB^{n}}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w.

Hence,

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}\left(x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}\right)}\int_{x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{vol}\left(y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right)}\int_{y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w\\
\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}\left(x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}\right)}\int_{x+uB^{n}}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w\leqslant\frac{\left(2eK\beta^{15}n^{4}u\right)^{\kappa\beta}}{\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}\log M}\leqslant\frac{\left(4eK\beta^{15}n^{4}u\right)^{\kappa\beta}}{\log M}.

This implies that there exists
y\in x+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)uB^{n}
such that

\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}\left(y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}\right)}\int_{y+\frac{u}{n}B_{X}}\|f(w)-\Lambda(w)\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}w\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\leqslant\frac{4eK\beta^{15}n^{4}u}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}=\frac{4eK\beta^{15}n^{5}}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}\cdot\frac{u}{n}.

Recalling Definition (
1
), since by (
43
) we are ensured that
u\geqslant 1/(Mn^{3/2})
, it follows that

\forall\,M\in(1,\infty),\qquad r_{\kappa\beta}^{X\to Y}\left(\frac{4eK\beta^{15}n^{5}}{\left(\log M\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}}\right)\geqslant\frac{1}{Mn^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(47)

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
choose
M=e^{(4eK\beta^{15}n^{5}/\varepsilon)^{\kappa\beta}}
in (
47
), thus yielding Theorem
5
as follows.

\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),\qquad r_{\kappa\beta}^{X\to Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(4eK\beta^{15}n^{5})^{\kappa\beta}}{\varepsilon^{\kappa\beta}}\right).

∎

4
Preliminaries on UMD Banach spaces

This section is devoted to the presentation of several analytic properties of UMD Banach spaces that will be used extensively in the proof of Theorem
5
.

Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a UMD Banach space and fix
p\in(1,\infty)
. Denote (as usual) by
\beta_{p}(Y)
the infimum over those
\beta\in(1,\infty]
such that if

\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is a
Y
-valued
p
-integrable martingale
defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
we have

\int_{\Omega}\Big\|M_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}\left(M_{j}-M_{j-1}\right)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\leqslant\beta^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left\|M_{n}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}.

(48)

Thus, using the notation of the Introduction, we have
\beta_{2}(Y)=\beta(Y)
. The following inequality is well-known; see
[
17
]
for its proof.

\beta_{p}(Y)\lesssim\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}\beta(Y).

(49)

We also record for future use that (
48
) implies (see
[
53
, Thm. 4.4]
) that for every
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\Omega}\Big\|a_{0}M_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\left(M_{j}-M_{j-1}\right)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\leqslant\left(\max_{j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}|a_{j}|^{p}\right)\beta_{p}(X)^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left\|M_{n}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}.

(50)

In
[
33
]
Garling introduced two parameters
\beta_{p}^{+}(Y),\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)
, defined to be the best constants in the following inequalities, which are required to hold true for every martingale
\{M_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
as above.

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\bigg[\int_{\Omega}\Big\|M_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{n}\left(M_{j}-M_{j-1}\right)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\bigg]\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left\|M_{n}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P},

and

\int_{\Omega}\left\|M_{n}\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\leqslant\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)^{p}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\bigg[\int_{\Omega}\Big\|M_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{n}\left(M_{j}-M_{j-1}\right)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\bigg],

where
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation with respect to
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n})
chosen uniformly at random from
\{-1,1\}^{n}
. Garling’s inequalities are weaker than (
48
), which is required to hold for
every

\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
rather than only in expectation with respect to
\varepsilon
. Hence,

\max\left\{\beta_{p}^{+}(Y),\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)\right\}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y),

(51)

but there are examples of Banach spaces
Y
for which
\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)
or
\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)
is markedly smaller than
\beta_{p}(Y)
; see
[
33
,
34
]
. Some of the ensuing estimates can be stated in terms of the parameters
\beta_{p}(Y),\beta_{p}^{+}(Y),\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)
, but in order to avoid cumbersome expressions we will sometimes state our bounds in terms of the quantity
\beta(Y):=\beta_{2}(Y)
, by invoking (
49
) and (
51
). In fact, in our setting we will always choose
2\leqslant p\asymp\beta(Y)
, in which case by using (
49
) and (
51
) we will sometimes bound from above the quantities
\beta_{p}(Y),\beta_{p}^{+}(Y),\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)
by a universal constant multiple of
p\,\beta(Y)\asymp\beta(Y)^{2}
.
This choice has the advantage of simplifying some of the ensuing discussion, but it yields bounds that could be improved for some (quite exotic) UMD Banach spaces
Y
by a straightforward inspection of our proofs.

4.1
\mathscr{R}
-boundedness

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be Banach spaces. The space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
is denoted
\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
. Following
[
10
,
21
]
, a set of operators
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
is said to be
\mathscr{R}
-bounded if

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}T_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant C^{p}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{X}^{p}\bigg],

(52)

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in Y
and every
T_{1},\ldots,T_{N}\in\mathcal{T}
, and some (equivalently, by Kahane’s inequality
[
50
]
, for all)
p\in[1,\infty)
. The infimum over those
C
in (
52
) is denoted
\mathscr{R}_{p}(\mathcal{T})
.

The following result is due to Bourgain
[
12
]
; see also
[
30
]
for a proof.

Proposition 20

(Bourgain’s vector-valued Stein inequality)
.

Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space and fix
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\{\mathscr{F}_{j}\}_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
be an increasing sequence of sub-
\sigma
-algebras on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
. For every
j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
let
\mathscr{E}_{j}\in\mathscr{L}(L_{p}(\mathbb{P},Y),L_{p}(\mathbb{P},Y))
be the conditional expectation operator corresponding to
\mathscr{F}_{j}
, i.e.,
\mathscr{E}_{j}f=\mathbb{E}[f|\mathscr{F}_{j}]
for every
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{P},Y)
. Then

\mathscr{R}_{p}\left(\{\mathscr{E}_{j}\}_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\right)\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y).

By a classical representation theorem for positive self-adjoint semigroups of contractions due to Rota
[
70
]
(see also
[
73
, Sec. VI]
or
[
59
, Thm. 2.5]
), Proposition
20
implies the following dimension independent
\mathscr{R}
-boundedness estimate for the heat semigroup. Here and in what follows,
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Corollary 21

(
\mathscr{R}
-boundedness of the heat semigroup)
.

Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a UMD Banach space,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\mathscr{R}_{p}\left(\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t\in(0,\infty)}\right)\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y).

Proof.

For any
\delta>0
, the operator
Q=e^{\frac{1}{2}\delta\Delta}
satisfies the assumptions of Rota’s theorem as formulated in
[
73
, Sec. VI]
or
[
59
, Thm. 2.5]
. Thus there exists a measure space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mu)
with an increasing sequence of sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\{\mathscr{F}_{j}\}_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
and yet another sub-
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
with the corresponding conditional expectation operators
\mathscr{E}_{j}
and
{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}
such that

\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\qquad e^{j\delta\Delta}=Q^{2j}=J^{-1}{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}\mathscr{E}_{-j}J,

where
J:L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime},Y)
is an isometric isomorphism. Thus

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{p}\big(\{e^{j\delta\Delta}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{R}_{p}\big(\{J{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}\mathscr{E}_{-j}J\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|J^{-1}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\cdot\|{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mu)\to L_{p}(\Omega,\mathscr{F^{\prime}},\mu)}\cdot\mathscr{R}_{p}\big(\{\mathscr{E}_{-j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\big)\cdot\|J\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime},Y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta_{+}(Y),

where we used Proposition
20
together with easy properties of
\mathscr{R}
-bounds and the contractivity of conditional expectations.

Using basic properties of
\mathscr{R}
-bounds (cf.
[
76
, Proposition 9.5]
), if
\mathscr{T}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{T}_{k}
is a union of an increasing sequence
\mathscr{T}_{k}\subseteq\mathscr{T}_{k+1}
, and
\overline{\mathscr{T}}
is the closure of
\mathscr{T}
in the strong operator topology, then
\mathscr{R}_{p}(\overline{\mathscr{T}})=\mathscr{R}_{p}(\mathscr{T})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathscr{R}_{p}(\mathscr{T}_{k})
. With
\mathscr{T}_{k}=\{e^{j2^{-k}\Delta}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
, we have
\overline{\mathscr{T}}=\{e^{t\Delta}\}_{t\in(0,\infty)}
by the strong continuity of
t\mapsto e^{t\Delta}
, and this proves the claim.
∎

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|V_{j}(t)\|_{Y}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]<\infty

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}B_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)^{p}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}C_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg],

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}C_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg]\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)^{p}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}B_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg].

Continuing with the notation of Theorem
22
, for every (operator-valued) measurable

We record for future use the following simple estimate.

Lemma 23
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\{B(t)=(B_{1}(t),\ldots,B_{n}(t))\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
be a Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, starting at
0
. For every Banach space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
, every
p\in(1,\infty)
and every smooth and compactly supported
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
with a finite-dimensional range, we have

\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{\tau-t}{2}\Delta}h(B(t)+x)\mathrm{d}B_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(58)

Proof.

If
\varphi\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(B(t)+x)]=e^{\frac{t}{2}\Delta}\varphi(x)
for every
t\in[0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}[\varphi(B(t)+x)]\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\Delta}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(59)

For
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
, let

u(x,t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\Delta}h(x)

denote the heat extension of
h
. By Itô’s formula (see
[
51
, Theorem 3.3.6]
) applied to the function
t\mapsto u(B(t)+x,\tau-t)
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}(B(t)+x,\tau-t)\mathrm{d}B_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=u(B(\tau)+x,0)-u(x,\tau)-\int_{0}^{\tau}\bigg(-\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{x}u\bigg)(B(t)+x,\tau-t)\mathrm{d}t

(60)

\displaystyle=h(B(\tau)+x)-e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h(x).

(61)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{\tau-t}{2}\Delta}h(B(t)+x)\mathrm{d}B_{j}(t)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use ito}}}{{\leqslant}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h(B(\tau)+x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\right]\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:markov property}}}{{=}}\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}+\left\|e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(62)

To deduce the desired bound (
58
) from (
62
), it remains to note that

\forall\,p\in(1,\infty),\qquad\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\|e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}=0.

(63)

Indeed, by Young’s inequality we have the following point-wise estimate

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\left\|e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h(x)\right\|_{Y}=\left\|k_{\frac{\tau}{2}}*h(x)\right\|_{Y}\leqslant\left\|k_{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},

(64)

where
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is the heat kernel and
q=p/(p-1)
. Since
q\in(0,\infty)
, the
L_{q}
-norm of the heat kernel
k_{\tau/2}
converges to
0
as
\tau\to\infty
. Moreover,
\|e^{\frac{\tau}{2}\Delta}h\|_{Y}
is dominated point-wise by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function
M\|h\|_{Y}\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence (
63
) follows from (
64
) by an application of the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of the present section: it establishes an estimate that will be used several times in the ensuing discussion.

Theorem 24
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Suppose that for
t\in(0,\infty)
we are given a bounded operator
A(t):Y\to Y
such that the mapping
A:(0,\infty)\to\mathscr{L}(Y,Y)
is bounded and measurable. Also, suppose that
T:L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
is a linear operator that has the following dual representation. For every sufficiently nice
f
in a dense subspace of
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
, and
g^{*}
in a dense subspace of
L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y^{*})
, where
q=p/(p-1)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{*}(x)\left(Tf(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\Delta}g^{*}(x)\bigg)\bigg(A(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\Delta}f(x)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

(65)

Then, recalling the notation (
55
),

\|T\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)\mathscr{R}_{p}(A).

(66)

Moreover, in the special case
A:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
we have

\|T\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)\left\|A\right\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\infty)}.

(67)

Proof.

By duality and the identity (
65
), the desired estimate (
66
) would follow if we show that for every sufficiently nice
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
and
g^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y^{*})
,

\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{s}{2}\Delta}g^{*}(x)\right)\left(A(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}e^{\frac{s}{2}\Delta}f(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\\
\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)\mathscr{R}_{p}(A)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\|g^{*}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y^{*})}.

(68)

Let us consider
f,g
smooth and compactly supported, and taking values in finite-dimensional subspaces of

and

, respectively.

For every

4.2
Vector-valued multipliers

Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a Banach space. The Fourier transform of
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
will be denoted below by
\mathcal{F}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
, where we use the normalization

\mathcal{F}f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\langle x,y\rangle}f(x)\mathrm{d}y.

A possible formulation of Parseval’s identity in this vector-valued setting is to say that for functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
and
g^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y^{*}
that are either smooth and compactly supported, or Fourier transforms of such functions, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}g^{*}(x)(\mathcal{F}f(x))\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{*}(x)(f(-x))\mathrm{d}x.

(75)

If
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is an additional Banach space and
\mathfrak{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
is measurable then the multiplier associated to
\mathfrak{m}
is defined as usual by considering for every smooth and compactly supported
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
(or the Fourier transform of such a function) the function
T_{\mathfrak{m}}f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
given by

T_{\mathfrak{m}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{m})*f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(x\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(x)\mathcal{F}f(x)\right).

If
\mathfrak{m}
is smooth and locally bounded at least away from the coordinate hyperplanes, and the Fourier transform of
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
is smooth and compactly supported away from these hyperplanes, then also
T_{\mathfrak{m}}f
has a smooth and compactly supported Fourier transform. Hence, for such
f
and smooth and compactly supported
g^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y^{*}
(or the Fourier transform of such a function), Parseval’s identity applies and gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{*}(x)(T_{\mathfrak{m}}f(x))\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}g^{*}(x)\left(\mathfrak{m}(-x)\mathcal{F}f(-x)\right)\mathrm{d}x.

(76)

Also, under our choice of normalization of the Fourier transform,
-\Delta=T_{\mathfrak{m}}
for
\mathfrak{m}(x)=\|x\|_{2}^{2}
.

Theorem
24
can be used to bound the following multipliers, which arise as Laplace transforms of
-\Delta
. (Another approach to such multipliers appears in the recent survey
[
25
, Section 2.2.1]
; while it is presented there for scalar-valued functions, it is based on principles that are valid in any UMD space.) Suppose that
A:(0,\infty)\to\mathscr{L}(Y,Y)
is measurable and define
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}(Y,Y)
by

\forall\,y\in Y,\qquad\alpha(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}s\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}A(t)y\mathrm{d}t.

Then Theorem
24
applies to
\alpha(-\Delta)
, i.e., to the operator
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
where
\mathfrak{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathscr{L}(Y,Y)
is given by

\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Y,\qquad\mathfrak{m}(x)y=\alpha(\|x\|_{2}^{2})y=\|x\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\|x\|_{2}^{2}}A(t)y\mathrm{d}t.

(77)

Indeed, by Parseval’s identity (
76
), the representation (
65
) is a direct consequence of (
77
). We therefore have the following dimension independent bound, which holds true for every
p\in(1,\infty)
.

\left\|\Delta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta}A(t)\mathrm{d}t\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\mathscr{R}_{p}(A),

where we recall the notation (
55
). Also, if
A
takes values in
\mathbb{C}
then

\left\|\Delta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{t\Delta}A(t)\mathrm{d}t\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)\|A\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\infty)}.

(78)

Later we shall use (
78
) as a source of dimension-independent bounds for multipliers that correspond to imaginary powers of the Laplacian. Specifically, for every
s\in(0,\infty)
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

s^{iu}=s^{-(1-iu)}s=\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-iu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-iu}e^{-st}\mathrm{d}t.

It therefore follows from (
78
) that

\left\|(-\Delta)^{iu}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\frac{\beta_{p}(Y)}{|\Gamma(1-iu)|}\asymp\beta_{p}(Y)\frac{e^{|u|\arctan|u|}}{\sqrt{1+|u|}}\asymp\beta_{p}(Y)\frac{e^{\frac{\pi|u|}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|u|}},

(79)

where the penultimate step in (
79
) is a consequence of Stirling’s formula. For ease of later reference, we record the bound that we have just proved as Corollary
25
below. The reverse implication, i.e., that the boundedness
(-\Delta)^{iu}
on
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
implies that
Y
is UMD, is also true; in fact it was pointed out in
[
41
]
that
[
36
]
implicitly contains the estimate
\beta_{p}(Y)\leqslant\liminf_{u\to 0}\|(-\Delta)^{iu}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
.

Corollary 25
.

Suppose that
p\in(1,\infty)
and
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space. Then for every
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\left\|(-\Delta)^{iu}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\beta_{p}(Y)\frac{e^{\frac{\pi|u|}{2}}}{\sqrt{1+|u|}}.

Our next corollary of Theorem
24
is a dimension-independent bound for a multiplier that will be used in the ensuing proof of Theorem
5
. We shall use below the following integral representation.

\forall(\theta,\alpha)\in(0,1)\times[0,\infty),\qquad\frac{1}{(1+\alpha)^{\theta}}=\frac{\sin(\pi\theta)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{1-\theta}(1-s)^{\theta}(1+\alpha s)}.

(80)

To verify the validity of (
80
), simply apply the change of variable
s=t/(1+\alpha-\alpha t)
.

Corollary 26
.

Fix
a\in(0,2]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
\mathfrak{m}_{a}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting

\forall,x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\},\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{a}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{|x_{1}|^{a}}{\|x\|_{2}^{a}}.

(81)

Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space and that
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\|T_{\mathfrak{m}_{a}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)^{2}\beta_{p}^{-}(Y)\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{3}.

(82)

Remark 27
.

When
Y=\mathbb{C}
in Corollary
26
, Bañuelos and Bogdan
[
3
]
obtained the bound

\|T_{\mathfrak{m}_{a}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\max\left\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\right\}-1.

(83)

Note that
\beta_{p}(\mathbb{C})=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}-1
, by a theorem of Burkholder
[
16
]
. We are unable to recover the better estimate (

) for the scalar-valued case of Corollary
26
using our method.

Proof of Corollary
26
.

Write each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})
, where
x^{\prime}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. For every
s\in(0,1]
define
\gamma_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\gamma_{s}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{x_{1}^{2}+s\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x_{1}^{2}e^{-(x_{1}^{2}+s\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2})t}\mathrm{d}t.

(84)

Then
\gamma_{1}=\mathfrak{m}_{2}
, and if
a\in(0,2)
then by (
80
) with
\theta=a/2\in(0,1)
and
\alpha=\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}/x_{1}^{2}
we have

\mathfrak{m}_{a}=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma_{s}d\mu_{a}(s),

where
\mu_{a}
is the probability measure on
(0,1)
whose density is proportional to
s^{\frac{a}{2}-1}(1-s)^{-\frac{a}{2}}
. Therefore, in order to prove the desired estimate (
82
) it suffices to show that for every
s\in(0,1)
,

\|T_{\gamma_{s}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)^{2}\beta_{p}^{-}(Y).

Consider the UMD Banach space
Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},Y)
. By the identification
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\cong L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)
, the multiplier
T_{\gamma_{s}}
can be thought of as an operator from
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)
to
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)
; this is how Theorem
24
will be applied next, i.e., with
Y
replaced by
Z
, while noting that
\beta_{p}^{\pm}(Z)=\beta_{p}^{\pm}(Y)
.

We consider a test function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
that is finite linear combination of functions of the form
x\mapsto f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})\cdots f_{n}(x_{n})y
, where
y\in Y
and each
f_{i}
has a smooth Fourier transform, compactly supported away from
0
, and a similar function
g^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y^{*}
. Note that such functions are dense in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)\cong L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
and in
L_{q}(\mathbb{R},Z^{*})=L_{q}(\mathbb{R},L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},Y^{*}))\cong L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y^{*})
, respectively, where
q=p/(p-1)
.
Then by the Parseval identity (
76
) we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{*}(x_{1})(T_{\gamma_{s}}f(x_{1}))\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{*}(x)(T_{\gamma_{s}}f(x))\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}g^{*}(x)\left(\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{x_{1}^{2}+s\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}\mathcal{F}f(-x)\right)\mathrm{d}x.

Consequently, by the second equality in (
84
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{*}(x_{1})(T_{\gamma_{s}}f(x_{1}))\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x_{1}e^{-\frac{t}{2}x_{1}^{2}}\mathcal{F}g^{*}(x)\left(e^{-ts\|x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}}x_{1}e^{-\frac{t}{2}x_{1}^{2}}\mathcal{F}f(-x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}}g^{*}\right)(x)\left(\mathcal{F}\left(-e^{ts\Delta^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}e^{\frac{t}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}}f\right)\right)(-x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

where
\Delta^{\prime}
denotes the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, i.e., with respect to the variable
x^{\prime}
. By the vector-valued Parseval identity (
75
), we therefore have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{*}(x_{1})(T_{\gamma_{s}}f(x_{1}))\mathrm{d}x_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}e^{\frac{u}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}}g^{*}(x_{1})\right)\left(A(u)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}e^{\frac{u}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}}f\right)(x_{1})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}u,

where
A(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}-e^{us\Delta^{\prime}}:L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)
. Recalling Corollary
21
, it therefore follows from Theorem
24
that

\|T_{\gamma_{s}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}=\|T_{\gamma_{s}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Z)}\\
\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Z)\beta_{p}^{-}(Z)\mathscr{R}_{p}\left(\left\{e^{t\Delta^{\prime}}:L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},Y)\right\}_{t\in(0,\infty)}\right)\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)^{2}\beta_{p}^{-}(Y).

4.3
Littlewood–Paley decomposition

We need to introduce notation for the usual multi-scale bump functions that occur in Littlewood–Paley decompositions.
Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be smooth and supported on
[-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2]
; for concreteness we can take

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\phi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{-\frac{1}{(|x|-1/2)(2-|x|)}}&\mathrm{if}\ |x|\in(1/2,2),\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ |x|\in[0,1/2]\cup[2,\infty).\end{array}\right.

(85)

For
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
define
\psi_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\psi_{k}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\phi(2^{k}x)}{\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\phi(2^{j}x)}.

(86)

We also define
\omega_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\omega_{k}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\psi_{k-1}(x)+\psi_{k}(x)+\psi_{k+1}(x).

(87)

Thus
\omega_{k}\psi_{k}=\psi_{k}
, and therefore the corresponding multipliers satisfy the identity
T_{\omega_{k}}T_{\psi_{k}}=T_{\psi_{k}}
.

For every
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
define
\vartheta_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
by

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\vartheta_{k}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\sin^{4}(2^{k}x)}{(2^{k}x)^{2}}.

(88)

Like
\psi_{k}
, the function
\vartheta_{k}
is roughly localized around
|x|\asymp 2^{-k}
, but unlike
\psi_{k}
, it has long tails that are supported over all of
\mathbb{R}
. The importance of the special “pseudo-bump functions”
\{\vartheta_{k}\}_{k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
stems from the fact that they can be directly related to averages of dyadic martingales, which leads to the following form of the Littlewood–Paley inequality with a good constant.

Proposition 28
.

Suppose that
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a UMD Banach space. Then

\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{{{\mathbb{Z}}}}}\Big\|\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\varepsilon_{j}T_{\vartheta_{j}}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\beta_{p}^{+}(Y)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

Proposition
28
is due implicitly to Bourgain
[
12
]
, where it is proved as an intermediate step towards a more usual form of the Littlewood–Paley inequality involving the localized bump functions
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
in place of
\{\vartheta_{k}\}_{k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
, but with a more complicated dependence on the UMD constant. The above formulation of Proposition
28
appears explicitly as the special case
h=k=\mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2]}-\mathbf{1}_{[1/2,1]}
of Proposition 5.10 in
[
40
]
(where we are using here the notation of
[
40
, Proposition 5.10]
).

In subsequent arguments we shall use Proposition
28
in addition to some auxiliary estimates concerning the bump functions
\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\in Z}
, which are valid for arbitrary Banach space targets. These estimates rely on the fact that if
\mathfrak{m}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
then by Young’s inequality we have

\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}=\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}\right)*f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\leqslant\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(89)

Lemma 29
.

Suppose that
p\in[1,\infty]
and that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a Banach space. Then for every
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,

\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad\|T_{\psi_{k}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\lesssim\|T_{\vartheta_{k}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(90)

Proof.

Since for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
we have
\vartheta_{k}(x)=\vartheta_{0}(2^{k}x)
and
\psi_{k}(x)=\psi_{0}(2^{k}x)
, we also have
T_{\vartheta_{k}}f(x)=T_{\vartheta_{0}}(y\mapsto f(2^{k}y))(2^{-k}x)
and
T_{\psi_{k}}f(x)=T_{\psi_{0}}(y\mapsto f(2^{k}y))(2^{-k}x)
. Consequently, it suffices to prove (
90
) when
k=0
. Since
\vartheta_{0}
is nonzero on the support of
\psi_{0}
, we can write
T_{\psi_{0}}f=T_{\psi_{0}/\vartheta_{0}}T_{\theta_{0}}f
. The function
\psi_{0}/\vartheta_{0}
is smooth and compactly supported, therefore its inverse Fourier transform
g=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\psi_{0}/\vartheta_{0})
belongs to the Schwartz class of test functions
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, and in particular
g\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus (
90
) follows from (
89
) with
\mathfrak{m}=\psi_{0}/\vartheta_{0}
and
T_{\vartheta_{0}}f
in place of
f
.
∎

In order to facilitate the next two applications of (
89
), we record the following simple observation. If
\mathfrak{m}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
is smooth then for every
a\in(0,\infty)
we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{1+(ax)^{2}}\bigg)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+a^{2}x^{2})\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathfrak{m}\right)(x)\right|\\
=\frac{\pi}{a}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}-a^{2}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime})\right\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{a}\left\|\mathfrak{m}-a^{2}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{a}\left\|\mathfrak{m}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\pi a\left\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

(91)

Choosing
a=\sqrt{\|\mathfrak{m}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}/\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}}
in (
91
) and substituting the resulting estimate into (
89
) yields

\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\leqslant 2\pi\sqrt{\|\mathfrak{m}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\cdot\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(92)

Lemma 30
.

Fix
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a Banach space. Then for every
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|T_{\psi_{k}}f(x+y)-T_{\psi_{k}(x)}f(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\min\left\{1,\frac{|y|}{2^{k}}\right\}\left\|T_{\psi_{k}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(93)

Proof.

Write
g(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}T_{\psi_{k}}f(x+y)-T_{\psi_{k}}f(x)
. The fact that the norm of
g
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
is at most twice the norm of
T_{\psi_{k}}f
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
follows from the triangle inequality in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
. For the second estimate in (
93
), note that
g=T_{\rho_{y}\omega_{k}}T_{\psi_{k}}f
, where
\rho_{y}(x)=e^{-ixy}-1
. Hence, it remains to show that for every
h\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
and
y\in[-2^{k},2^{k}]
we have
\|T_{\rho_{y}\omega_{k}}h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\lesssim|y|2^{-k}\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}
. By (
92
), this will follow if we prove that
\|\rho_{y}\omega_{k}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot\|(\rho_{y}\omega_{k})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim(2^{-k}y)^{2}
. Since
\rho_{y}(x)\omega_{k}(x)=(\rho_{2^{-k}y}\omega_{0})(2^{k}y)
and for every smooth
\mathfrak{m}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
the product
\|x\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(\lambda x)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot\|(x\mapsto\mathfrak{m}(\lambda x))^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}
is independent of
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, it suffices to show that

\forall\,z\in[-1,1],\qquad\|\rho_{z}\omega_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim|z|\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\|(\rho_{z}\omega_{0})^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim|z|.

(94)

The point-wise estimate
|\rho_{z}(x)|\leqslant|zx|
combined with the fact that the function
x\mapsto x\omega_{0}(x)
is in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
implies the first assertion in (
94
). For the second assertion in (
94
), compute directly that

(\rho_{z}\omega_{0})^{\prime\prime}(x)=-z^{2}e^{-ixz}\omega_{0}(x)-i2ze^{-ixz}\omega_{0}^{\prime}(x)+(e^{-ixz}-1)\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x).

We therefore have the following point-wise estimate, which holds true whenever
|z|\leqslant 1
.

|(\rho_{z}\omega_{0})^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leqslant|z|^{2}|\omega_{0}(x)|+2|z\omega_{0}^{\prime}(x)|+|zx\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leqslant|z|\big(|\omega_{0}(x)|+2|\omega_{0}^{\prime}(x)|+|x\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)|\big).

The second assertion in (
94
) is now a consequence of the fact that the three functions
\omega_{0},\omega_{0}^{\prime}
and
x\mapsto x\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)
all belong to
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
30
∎

Lemma 31
.

Fix
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a Banach space. Then for every smooth and compactly supported
f:\mathbb{R}\to Y
we have

\left\|T_{\psi_{k}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\lesssim(1+\alpha)2^{2\alpha(k+2)}\left\|T_{\psi_{k}}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(95)

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
\omega_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
that is given in (
87
), since
T_{\omega_{k}}T_{\psi_{k}}=T_{\psi_{k}}

we have
T_{\psi_{k}}f=(-\Delta)^{-\alpha}T_{\omega_{k}}T_{\psi_{k}}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}f=T_{\xi_{k}}T_{\psi_{k}}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}f
, where

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\{0\},\qquad\xi_{k}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{k}(x)}{|x|^{2\alpha}}=2^{2k\alpha}\frac{\omega_{0}(2^{k}x)}{|2^{k}x|^{2\alpha}}=2^{2k\alpha}\xi_{0}(2^{k}x).

It therefore suffices to show that
\xi_{0}
is the Fourier transform of a function in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
of norm at most a constant multiple of
(1+\alpha)2^{4\alpha}
. To this end, we will again apply the estimate (
92
).
First, since
|x|^{-1}\leqslant 4
on the support of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{0}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\|\xi_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{4\alpha}
.
Also,

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\{0\},\qquad\xi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{2\alpha(2\alpha+1)}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\omega_{0}(x)}{x^{2}}-\frac{4\alpha}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\omega_{0}^{\prime}(x)}{|x|}+\frac{\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{|x|^{2\alpha}}.

Using again that
|x|^{-1}\leqslant 4
on the support of
\omega_{0}
, combined with the fact that the three functions
\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}
,
x\mapsto\omega_{0}(x)/x^{2}
and
x\mapsto\omega_{0}^{\prime}(x)/x
are all in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that
\|\xi_{0}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim(2\alpha+1)^{2}2^{4\alpha}
. So,

\|T_{\xi_{0}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:easy multiplier2}}}{{\lesssim}}\sqrt{\|\xi_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|\xi_{0}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R})}}\leqslant\sqrt{4^{2\alpha}\cdot(2\alpha+1)^{2}4^{2\alpha}}\lesssim(1+\alpha)4^{2\alpha}.\qed

4.4
Type and Cotype

For
p\in[1,2)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
, the type
p
constant and the cotype
q
constant of a Banach space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
, denoted
T_{p}(X)
and
C_{q}(Y)
, respectively, are defined to be the infimum over those
T,C\in[1,\infty]
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in Y
,

\frac{1}{C}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{Y}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{Y}\bigg]\leqslant T\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|x_{j}\|_{Y}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(96)

where the expectation is with respect to
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
chosen uniformly at random. The smallest
T,C\in(0,\infty]
for which (
96
) holds true are denoted
T_{p}(Y),C_{q}(Y)
, respectively. Any UMD Banach space
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
admits an equivalent uniformly convex norm
[
61
,
1
]
, and hence it has finite cotype
[
29
,
66
]
. The following lemma makes this qualitative statement quantitative in the case of cotype. A similar (and simpler) argument yields a quantitative bound in the case of type as well (see Remark
33
below), but in what follows only the case of cotype will be used.

Lemma 32

(Cotype in terms of
\beta(Y)
)
.

There exists a universal constant

Proof.

The proof below is a (somewhat tedious) combination of several results that appear in the literature. The key step is an examination of the proof of Pisier’s quantitative version
[

of the Maurey–Pisier theorem
[

for stable type. For

be the stable type

. Namely,

(97)

where in (

p
-stable random variables

, i.e., the characteristic function of

\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[e^{it\theta_{1}}\right]=e^{-|t|^{p}}.

(98)

It follows from (

; see e.g.
[

. By Jensen’s inequality and Kahane’s inequality, this implies that

(99)

Suppose from now on that

(in the argument below we only use that

.
It follows from the proof of the main theorem of

that there exists a universal constant

(100)

then

, the dependence on

Thus, recalling that

Note that

\beta(\ell_{p}^{m})\gtrsim\min\left\{q,\left(\log m\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}=\min\left\{q,\left(\log m\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\right\}.

(101)

While (

be the largest integer such that

. Let

be the uniform probability measure on the discrete hypercube

, and think of
\ell_{p}^{m}
as containing an isometric copy of

. Define

by setting

and for

defining

\forall\,\varepsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}^{k},\qquad M_{j}(\varepsilon)(\delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(1+\varepsilon_{\ell}\delta_{\ell})=2^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{j})=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{j})\}}.

Then

is a martingale with respect to the natural coordinate filtration of

Observe that

\bigg(\int_{\{-1,1\}^{k}}\left\|M_{k}(\varepsilon)\right\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\varepsilon)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=2^{\frac{k(p-1)}{p}}=2^{\frac{k}{q}}.

(102)

For every

S(\varepsilon,\delta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(M_{j}(\varepsilon)(\delta)-M_{j-1}(\varepsilon)(\delta)\right)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\asymp 2^{j(\varepsilon,\delta)}-1.

(103)

\mu\times\mu\left(\left\{(\varepsilon,\delta)\in\{-1,1\}^{k}\times\{-1,1\}^{k}:\ j(\varepsilon,\delta)=j\right\}\right)\asymp\frac{1}{2^{j}}.

(104)

Hence, using the triangle inequality in

\bigg(\int_{\{-1,1\}^{k}}\int_{\{-1,1\}^{k}}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta_{j}\left(M_{j}(\varepsilon)-M_{j-1}(\varepsilon)\right)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mu)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\varepsilon)\mathrm{d}\mu(\eta)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\gtrsim\bigg(\int_{\{-1,1\}^{k}}\int_{\{-1,1\}^{k}}S(\varepsilon,\delta)^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(\varepsilon)\mathrm{d}\mu(\delta)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:S lower}\wedge\eqref{eq:product measure estimate j}}}{{\gtrsim}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j(p-1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\asymp 2^{\frac{k}{q}}\cdot\min\left\{q,k^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\},

which, when contrasted with (

101
).

A combination of (

) and (

For every

4.5
UMD-valued Riesz potentials, Sobolev spaces and interpolation

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s,p\in(0,\infty)
. Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a Banach space. If
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is smooth and compactly supported then its homogeneous
(s,p)
-Riesz potential (semi)norm is defined as usual by

\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}=\left\|T_{\xi\mapsto\|\xi\|_{2}^{s}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(105)

(
\|\cdot\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
is sometimes denoted
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
, but we use a simpler notation since nonhomogeneous Riesz potentials do not occur in what follows.) The Banach space
H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
is the completion of the smooth and compactly support functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
.

Throughout the ensuing discussion we shall use standard notation and basic facts from complex interpolation theory, as appearing in
[
9
]
. The following lemma provides quantitative control on the behavior of the spaces
H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
under complex interpolation when
Y
is a UMD Banach space.

Lemma 34
.

Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a UMD Banach space. Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s,\sigma\in(0,\infty)
with
s<\sigma
. Suppose also that
\theta\in(0,1)
and define
t=(1-\theta)s+\theta\sigma
. Then every
f\in H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
satisfies

\left\|f\right\|_{[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}}\lesssim\beta_{p}(Y)e^{\frac{\pi(\sigma-s)}{4}\sqrt{\theta(1-\theta)}}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

Proof.

Consider the strip
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ \Re z\in(0,1)\}
. For every
M\in(0,\infty)
define an auxiliary mapping
\Phi_{M}:\overline{S}\to H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)+H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
by

\forall\,z\in\overline{S},\qquad\Phi_{M}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}e^{M(z(z-1)-\theta(\theta-1))}(-\Delta)^{\frac{t-s-z(\sigma-s)}{2}}f.

Then
\Phi_{M}
is holomorphic on
S
and satisfies
\Phi_{M}(\theta)=f
. By the definition of the complex interpolation space
[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}
, we therefore have

\left\|f\right\|_{[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}}\leqslant\inf_{M>0}\sup_{b\in\mathbb{R}}\max\left\{\|\Phi_{M}(ib)\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},\|\Phi_{M}(1+ib)\|_{H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\right\}.

(106)

For every
(a,b)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
we have

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s+a(\sigma-s)}{2}}\Phi_{M}(a-bi)=e^{Ma(a-1)+M\theta(1-\theta)-Mb^{2}-M(2a-1)bi}(-\Delta)^{\frac{ib(\sigma-s)}{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f.

Recalling (
105
), we therefore obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{M}(a

\displaystyle+bi)\|_{H_{s+a(\sigma-s),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{Ma(a-1)+M\theta(1-\theta)-Mb^{2}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{ib(\sigma-s)}{2}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\|f\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{p}(Y)e^{Ma(a-1)+M\theta(1-\theta)}\cdot e^{-Mb^{2}+\frac{\pi|b|(\sigma-s)}{4}}\|f\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},

(107)

where in (
4.5
) we used Corollary
25
. The function
b\mapsto-Mb^{2}+\pi|b|(\sigma-s)/4
attains its maximum on
\mathbb{R}
at
b=\pi(\sigma-s)/(8M)
. It therefore follows from (
4.5
) that

\sup_{b\in\mathbb{R}}\max\left\{\|\Phi_{M}(ib)\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},\|\Phi_{M}(1+ib)\|_{H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\right\}\lesssim\beta_{p}(Y)e^{M\theta(1-\theta)+\frac{\pi^{2}(\sigma-s)^{2}}{64M}}\|f\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

In combination with (
106
) we therefore have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{p}(Y)\left(\inf_{M\in(0,\infty)}e^{M\theta(1-\theta)+\frac{\pi^{2}(\sigma-s)^{2}}{64M}}\right)\|f\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{p}(Y)e^{\frac{\pi(\sigma-s)}{4}\sqrt{\theta(1-\theta)}}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},

(108)

where for (
4.5
) the optimal choice of
M\in(0,\infty)
is
M=\pi(\sigma-s)/(8\sqrt{\theta(1-\theta)})
.
∎

Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open (for our purposes
\Omega
will always be either a multiple of
B^{n}
or all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). If
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a Banach space and
p\in[1,\infty]
then for every smooth
f:\Omega\to Y
denote

\|f\|_{W_{1,p}(\Omega,Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,Y)}.

(109)

Thus
\|\cdot\|_{W_{p,1}(\Omega,Y)}
is the (homogeneous) first order Sobolev (semi)norm of
f
. The corresponding Sobolev space
W_{p,1}(\Omega,Y)
is the completion of the space of all smooth and compactly supported functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{p,1}(\Omega,Y)}
. For
(s,p)\in(0,1)\times[1,\infty)
, the order
s
fractional (homogeneous) Sobolev (semi)norm of
f:\Omega\to Y
is defined by

\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\Omega,Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigg(\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|x-y\|_{2}^{n+ps}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(110)

While the notation
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}_{s,p}(\Omega,Y)}
is sometimes used in the literature, we shall use the above simpler notation because nonhomogeneous Sobolev norms do not occur in what follows.

Our next goal is to relate UMD-valued Riesz potentials to Sobolev norms. The following lemma treats the case of first order Sobolev norms, and also contains a comparison between Riesz potentials that will be needed later; it is a simple consequence of the boundedness of the Hilbert transform on UMD Banach spaces, combined with the method of rotations.

Lemma 35
.

Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then every
f\in W_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
satisfies

\|f\|_{H_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\|f\|_{W_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

Moreover, if
s\in(1,\infty)
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
then for every smooth
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
we have

\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{H_{s-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(111)

Proof.

Suppose that
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
is odd, continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and positively homogeneous of order
-n
, i.e.,
K(tx)=t^{-n}K(x)
for every
t\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}
. For
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},X)
consider the corresponding Calderòn–Zygmund singular integral

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad T_{K}f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x-y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y.

(112)

It follows from the method of rotations, as presented by Iwaniec and Martin in
[
44
]
, that

\|T_{K}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}|K(z)|\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\right)\|H\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)},

(113)

where
\sigma
is the surface area measure on the Euclidean sphere
S^{n-1}
and
H:L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
is the Hilbert transform, i.e.,

\forall\,\varphi\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad H\varphi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi(y)}{x-y}\mathrm{d}y.

(114)

The integrals in (
112
) and (
114
) exist in the sense of principal values. The estimate (
113
) is presented in
[
44
, Proposition 5.1]
in the case
Y=\mathbb{C}
, but the same proof applies to the Banach space-valued setting without any change (the proof is based on an integral identity that is estimated using convexity of the norm. As such, the vector-valued and scalar-valued cases are identical; here the Banach space
Y
can be general and the UMD property isn’t used).

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
consider the Riesz transform given by

\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad R_{j}f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}}{\|x-y\|_{2}^{n+1}}f(y)\mathrm{d}y.

By (
113
) we have,

\displaystyle\|R_{j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\pi\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})\int_{S^{n-1}}|z_{1}|\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)}{2\pi^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\|H\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}

\displaystyle=\|H\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{2},

(115)

where the bound
\|H\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}
that was used in (
4.5
) is implicit in the important work of Burkholder
[
15
]
and explicit in
[
32
, Theorem 3]
.

Note that for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have
R_{j}=(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
, as follows directly by computing the Fourier transform (see e.g.
[
72
, Chapter III]
)). Consequently,

\|f\|_{H_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bessel}}}{{=}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Delta f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Wp1 def}\wedge\eqref{eq:use IM}}}{{\leqslant}}\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\|f\|_{W_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

Finally, to deduce (
111
), proceed as follows.

\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{H_{s-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bessel}}}{{=}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}=\left\|(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\\
=\left\|R_{j}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use IM}}}{{\leqslant}}\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bessel}}}{{=}}\beta_{p}(Y)^{2}\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

The following theorem asserts a useful comparison between UMD-valued fractional Sobolev norms and the corresponding Riesz potentials.

Theorem 36
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. Suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space of cotype
p
. Then every
f\in H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
satisfies

\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\frac{C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}(nV_{n})^{\frac{1}{p}}}{s(1-s)}\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},

(116)

Proof of Theorem
36
.

The proof below proceeds via a reduction to a slightly stronger statement in the one dimensional case
n=1
. So, assume for the moment that we already proved that

\forall\,g\in H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y),\qquad\|g\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\lesssim\frac{C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)}{s(1-s)}\|g\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(117)

We shall now deduce the desired estimate (
116
) from (
117
), and then proceed to prove (
117
).

For every
z\in S^{n-1}
and
w\in z^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
define
g_{z,w}:\mathbb{R}\to Y
by setting

\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad g_{z,w}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f(w+tz).

(118)

By changing to polar coordinates we see that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}^{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:s sobolev}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|f(x+y)-f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{\|y\|_{2}^{n+ps}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{S^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|f(x+rz)-f(x)\|_{Y}^{p}}{|r|^{1+ps}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:s sobolev}\wedge\eqref{eq:def gzw}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{n-1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\int_{z^{\perp}}\|g_{z,w}\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\mathrm{d}w.

Hence, using (
117
) we deduce that

\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\frac{C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)}{s(1-s)}\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)\int_{z^{\perp}}\|g_{z,w}\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\mathrm{d}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(119)

For every
z\in S^{n-1}
,
w\in z^{\perp}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
denote

h_{z,w}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left(-\langle z,\nabla\rangle^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(w+tz)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def gzw}}}{{=}}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}g_{z,w}(t).

With this notation, for every
z\in S^{n-1}
we have

\int_{z^{\perp}}\|g_{z,w}\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\mathrm{d}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bessel}}}{{=}}\int_{z^{\perp}}\left\|h_{z,w}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\mathrm{d}w=\left\|\left(-\langle z,\nabla\rangle^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}^{p}\\
=\left\|\left(-\langle z,\nabla\rangle^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bessel}}}{{\leqslant}}\left\|T_{m_{z}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}^{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}^{p},

(120)

where we set
m_{z}(x)=|\langle z,x\rangle|^{s}/\|x\|_{2}^{s}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in S^{n-1}
. By rotation invariance, it follows from Corollary
26
that the norm of the multiplier
T_{m_{z}}
as an operator from
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
to itself is at most a constant multiple of
\beta_{p}(Y)^{3}
. By combining this bound with (
119
) and (
120
), we get

\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\frac{C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}\sigma(S^{n-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}}{s(1-s)}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

Since
\sigma(S^{n-1})=nV_{n}
, this concludes the deduction of (
116
) from (
117
).

It remains to prove (
117
). The case
Y=\mathbb{R}
of (
117
) is given in
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.5]
, without explicit dependence on the relevant parameters. The proof of (
117
) below consists of an adaptation of the argument of
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.5]
to the UMD-valued setting, while tracking the bounds.

Recalling the Littlewood–Paley partition of unity
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
given in (
86
), for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|g(x+y)-g(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g(x+y)-T_{\psi_{j}(x)}g(x)\right\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(121)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\min\Big\{1,\frac{|y|}{2^{j}}\Big\}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)},

(122)

where in (
121
) we used the fact that
\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{j}\equiv 1
and the triangle inequality in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
, and in (
122
) we used Lemma
30
. Now,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use localization}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{r\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\int_{2^{r}}^{2^{r+1}}\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\min\Big\{1,\frac{|y|}{2^{j}}\Big\}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\Big)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{1+ps}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(123)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{r\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\Big(\sum_{u\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\frac{\min\left\{1,2^{u}\right\}}{2^{su}}\cdot 2^{-s(r-u)}\left\|T_{\psi_{r-u}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(124)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\Big(\sum_{r\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\frac{\min\left\{1,2^{pu}\right\}}{2^{psu}}\cdot 2^{-ps(r-u)}\left\|T_{\psi_{r-u}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}2^{-psj}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\sum_{u\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\frac{\min\left\{1,2^{u}\right\}}{2^{su}}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}2^{-psj}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(126)

where for (
124
) use the fact that for each
r\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
in the integrand of the corresponding summand that appears in the right hand side of (
123
) we have
|y|\asymp 2^{r}
, and make the change of variable
u=r-j
, and for (
125
) use the triangle inequality in
\ell_{p}({{\mathbb{Z}}})
.

Recalling the functions
\{\vartheta_{j}\}_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
given in (
88
), an application of Proposition
28
shows that

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{{{\mathbb{Z}}}}}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\varepsilon_{j}T_{\vartheta_{j}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\bigg]\leqslant\beta_{p}(Y)\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}=\beta_{p}(Y)\|g\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(127)

At the same time, since
L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
has cotype
p
with constant
C_{p}(Y)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{{{\mathbb{Z}}}}}\bigg[\Big\|\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\varepsilon_{j}T_{\vartheta_{j}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}\bigg]\gtrsim\frac{1}{C_{p}(Y)}\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\left\|T_{\vartheta_{j}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\gtrsim\frac{1}{C_{p}(Y)}\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{C_{p}(Y)}\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}2^{-psj}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(128)

where in the penultimate step of (
128
) we used Lemma
90
and in the final step of (
128
) we used Lemma
31
. By combining (
127
) and (
128
), we see that

\Big(\sum_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}2^{-psj}\left\|T_{\psi_{j}}g\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)\|g\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}.

(129)

A substitution of (
129
) into (
126
) now yields the desired estimate (
117
).
∎

Remark 37
.

The left side of (
129
) is the Besov norm
\|g\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)}
. Hence (
129
) asserts a quantitative embedding of
H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
into
B^{s}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R},Y)
when
Y
is a UMD space of cotype
p
. A qualitative embedding statement of this type was recently established by Veraar
[
79
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Remark 38
.

One can prove a reverse inequality to that of Theorem
36
under the assumption that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space of type
p\in(1,2]
, in which case one obtains an upper bound on
\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
in terms of
\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
. Specifically, we have the following estimate for
s>0
.

\forall f\in W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),\qquad\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\frac{T_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}n^{\frac{s}{2}}}{(p-1)(nV_{n})^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(130)

Since (
130
) is not needed below, we omit its proof (which is available on request). By Remark
33
, one can take in (
130
)
p-1\asymp 1/\beta(Y)
, in which case
T_{p}(Y)\asymp 1
and, by (
49
),
\beta_{p}(Y)\lesssim\beta(Y)^{2}
. Note that, by Kwapien’s theorem
[
52
]
, unless
Y
is isomorphic to a Hilbert space, its type and cotype do not coincide, and therefore by combining Theorem
36
with (
130
) one does not obtain an equivalence between the norms
\|\cdot\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}
for non-Hilbertian targets
Y
. See
[
38
]
for a related characterization of Hilbert space.

Recalling the notation that was introduced in Section
2
, we end this section by deducing a corollary of Theorem
36
that will be very important in what follows.

Corollary 39
.

Fix
p\in[2,\infty)
and
(s,\sigma)\in(0,1)\times(1,2)
. Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
be a UMD Banach space of cotype
p
. Then every measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{V_{n}u^{ps+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{pn}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}}{s(1-s)}\|f\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)},

(131)

and

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{V_{n}u^{p\sigma+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{p}n^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{6}}{(\sigma-1)(2-\sigma)}\|f\|_{H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(132)

Proof.

By integrating the conclusion of Corollary
14
(with
q=ps
) over
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we see that

\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{n}}\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{ps+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{pn}}{(nV_{n})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{W_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(133)

A substitution of the conclusion of Theorem
36
into (
133
) yields (
131
).

Next, by integrating the conclusion of Corollary
15
(with
q=p\sigma>p
) over
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we see that

\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{n}}\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\|f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy)\|_{Y}^{p}}{u^{p\sigma+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{pn}}{(nV_{n})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{W_{\sigma-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(134)

By Theorem
36
applied to
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and with
s
replaced by
\sigma-1\in(0,1)
,

\forall\,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{W_{\sigma-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\frac{C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}(nV_{n})^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(\sigma-1)(2-\sigma)}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{H_{\sigma-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(135)

Moreover, by the second assertion of Lemma
35
we have

\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\left\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{H_{\sigma-1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\lesssim\beta_{p}^{2}(Y)\left\|f\right\|_{H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(136)

By substituting (
136
) into (
135
), and then substituting the resulting estimate into (
134
), we obtain the desired estimate (
132
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
19

We shall prove here the following theorem.

Theorem 40
.

Fix
p\in[2,\infty)
and suppose that
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space of cotype
p
. Then every smooth
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
satisfies

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{V_{n}u^{p+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\lesssim\sqrt{pn}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{7}\log(\beta_{p}(Y)n)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(137)

Note that Theorem
19
follows from Theorem
40
. Indeed, if
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
is a UMD Banach space with
\beta=\beta(Y)
then by Lemma
32
there exists a universal constant
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
such that if we set
p=\kappa\beta
then
C_{p}(Y)\lesssim 1
(in particular we necessarily have
\kappa\beta\geqslant 2
).
Moreover, by (
49
) we have
\beta_{p}(Y)\lesssim\beta^{2}
. To deduce Theorem
19
, take
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to Y
that is Lipschitz and compactly supported. By convolving
f
with a smooth bump function whose support has small diameter we may also assume that
f
is smooth. It now follows from Theorem
40
that

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}}{V_{n}u^{\kappa\beta+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\\
\lesssim\beta^{\frac{29}{2}}\sqrt{n}\log(\beta n)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\Big\|_{Y}^{\kappa\beta}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\leqslant\beta^{15}n^{\frac{5}{2}}\big(\mathrm{vol}(\operatorname{supp}(f))\big)^{\frac{1}{\kappa\beta}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}},

where we used the fact that, due to (
8
), for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

\Big\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\Big\|_{Y}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|f(x+te_{j})-f(x)\|_{Y}}{|t|}\leqslant\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\|e_{j}\|_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:john posiiton}}}{{\leqslant}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\sqrt{n}.

Proof of Theorem
40
.

For every
s\in(0,\infty)
let
\nu_{s}
be the measure on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}
whose density is given by

\forall(u,x,y)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n},\qquad\varphi_{s}(u,x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{V_{n}|u|^{ps+1}}.

(138)

Define a linear operator
\mathscr{S}:H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\nu_{s},Y)
by setting for every
f\in H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
,

\forall(u,x,y)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n},\qquad\mathscr{S}f(u,x,y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f^{x}(uy)-P^{1}_{u}f^{x}(uy),

(139)

where we recall the notations (
9
) and (
15
) for
f^{x}
and
P^{1}_{u}
, respectively. In what follows we let
M_{s}
denote the norm of
\mathscr{S}
as an operator from
H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
to
L_{p}(\nu_{s},Y)
, i.e.,

\forall\,s\in(0,\infty),\qquad M_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\|\mathscr{S}\|_{H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)\to L_{p}(\nu_{s},Y)}.

Suppose that
s,\sigma,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

(s,\sigma,\theta)\in(0,1)\times(1,2)\times(0,1)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad 1=(1-\theta)s+\theta\sigma.

Then
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{s}^{1-\theta}\varphi_{\sigma}^{\theta}
, so by Stein’s interpolation theorem
[
71
, Theorem 2]
for every
f
in the complex interpolation space
[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}
we have

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{V_{n}u^{p+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def density nus}\wedge\eqref{eq:def operator S}}}{{=}}\|\mathscr{S}f\|_{L_{p}(\nu_{1},Y)}\leqslant M_{s}^{1-\theta}M_{\sigma}^{\theta}\|f\|_{[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}}.

(140)

We note that Stein’s interpolation theorem is stated in
[
71
]
for real-valued function spaces, but the standard proofs of this theorem (see also
[
74
]
or
[
9
, Section 5.4]
) work without additional effort for vector-valued spaces as well, which is what we are using here. Alternatively, the vector-valued setting is treated explicitly by Calderón in
[
18
, Section 13.6]
. Every
f\in W_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)
satisfies

\|f\|_{[H_{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y),H_{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)]_{\theta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\lesssim}}\beta_{p}(Y)\|f\|_{H_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{\lesssim}}\beta_{p}(Y)^{3}\|f\|_{W_{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},Y)}.

(141)

where in
(*)
we used Lemma
34
and in
(**)
we used Lemma
35
. Hence,

\bigg(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B^{n}\times(0,\infty)}\frac{\left\|f^{x}(uy)-P_{u}^{1}f^{x}(uy)\right\|_{Y}^{p}}{V_{n}u^{p+1}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}u\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Wp1 def}\wedge\eqref{eq:interpolation ineq S}\wedge\eqref{eq:use comparisons of interpolation}}}{{\lesssim}}\beta_{p}(Y)^{3}\bigg(\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(s,\sigma,\theta)\in(0,1)\times(1,2)\times(0,1)}}{{(1-\theta)s+\theta\sigma=1}}}M_{s}^{1-\theta}M_{\sigma}^{\theta}\bigg)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\Big\|_{Y}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(142)

Corollary
39
asserts that

\forall(s,\sigma)\in(0,1)\times(1,2),\qquad M_{s}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{pn}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}}{s(1-s)}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad M_{\sigma}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{p}n^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{6}}{(\sigma-1)(2-\sigma)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(s,\sigma,\theta)\in(0,1)\times(1,2)\times(0,1)}}{{(1-\theta)s+\theta\sigma=1}}}M_{s}^{1-\theta}M_{\sigma}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(s,\sigma,\theta)\in(0,1)\times(1,2)\times(0,1)}}{{(1-\theta)s+\theta\sigma=1}}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{pn}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}}{s(1-s)}\bigg)^{1-\theta}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{p}n^{\frac{3}{2}}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{6}}{(\sigma-1)(2-\sigma)}\bigg)^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{pn}C_{p}(Y)\beta_{p}(Y)^{4}\log(\beta_{p}(Y)n),

(143)

where (
5
) arises by choosing
s=1-1/\log(\beta_{p}(Y)n)
and
\sigma=2-1/\log(\beta_{p}(Y)n)
, in which case necessarily
\theta=1/\log(\beta_{p}(Y)n)
. We note that these choices essentially yield the best possible estimate in (
5
) (up to constant factors), but one could also choose here, say,
s=1/2
and
\sigma=3/2
, yielding a worse dependence on
n
which is of lesser importance for our present purposes. A substitution of (
5
) into (
142
) yields the desired estimate (
137
).
∎

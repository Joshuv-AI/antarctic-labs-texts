Partial-wave projection of the one-particle exchange in three-body scattering amplitudes
Andrew W. Jackura, Raúl A. Briceño

Abstract
As the study of three-hadron physics from lattice QCD matures, it is necessary to develop proper analysis tools in order to reliably study a variety of phenomena, including resonance spectroscopy and nuclear structure. Reconstructing the three-particle scattering amplitude requires solving integral equations, which can be written in terms of data-constrained dynamical functions and physical on-shell quantities. The driving term in these equations is the so-called one-particle exchange, which leads to a kinematic divergence for particles on-mass-shell. A vital component in defining three-particle amplitudes with definite parity and total angular momentum, which are used in spectroscopic studies, is to project the one-particle exchange into definite partial waves. We present a general procedure to construct exact analytic partial wave projections of the one-particle exchange contribution for any system composed of three spinless hadrons. Our result allows one full control over the analytic structure of the projection, which we explore for some low-lying partial waves with applications to three pions.

III
Partial Wave Projection

Our first task is to define the generic partial wave projection for
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
scattering amplitudes. The scheme we follow is similar to that of Ref.
Mikhasenko et al. 2020
, where we first couple the three-particle system to a definite total angular momentum
J
through the helicity framework. Then, we re-couple the helicity partial wave to ones of definite parity using spin-orbit or
LS
coefficients. The reason for going through this two-step process is that helicity transforms simply under Lorentz transformations compared to spin-projections against some space-fixed
z
-axis. Doing so makes the projection of the exchange amplitude simpler, as the angles in the total CM frame are simply related to those in either pair rest frame.

III.1
Helicity Projection

The starting point is to project the amplitude in
helicity partial waves
, that is partial waves of definite total angular momentum where the pairs have their spin projected quantized along their momentum direction. Following the decomposition used in Refs.
Hansen and Sharpe 2014
;
Hansen and Sharpe 2015
;
Jackura 2023

\displaystyle\ket{P,\mathbf{k},\mathbf{a}}\propto\sqrt{4\pi}\,\sum_{\ell,\lambda}\ket{P,\mathbf{k},\ell\lambda}\,Y_{\ell\lambda}^{*}(\mathbf{\hat{a}}_{k}^{\star})\,,

III.2
Spin-Orbit Projection

Spin-orbit amplitudes are those of definite spatial parity. These amplitudes are important to construct for the spectroscopy as hadrons appear as resonant states of amplitudes, and hadrons have definite spin-parity
J^{P}
. Given the helicity partial wave projections for the
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
amplitude in Sec.
III.1
, we can easily construct amplitudes of definite parity by taking appropriate linear combinations. We use the fact that the amplitudes
\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}
can be interpreted as a quasi-two-body amplitude where one particle has a helicity. We can therefore use standard techniques
Jacob and Wick 1959
from the partial wave projection of a two-body helicity state to spin-orbit state to obtain

\displaystyle\ket{P,k,^{2S+1}\!L_{J},m_{J},\ell}\propto\sum_{\lambda}\ket{P,k,Jm_{J},\ell\lambda}\,\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})\,,

which when applied to our helicity amplitude yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{(\ell^{\prime})}(^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J})\,\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}^{J}(p,k)\,\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})\,.

(20)

Here
S
is the total intrinsic spin of the pair-spectator system,
L
is the orbital angular momentum between an initial state pair and its spectator in their total CM frame, and
S^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime}
are similarly defined for the final state. The total angular momentum
J
of the three-body system therefore has values
\lvert L-S\rvert\leq J\leq L+S
and
\lvert L^{\prime}-S^{\prime}\rvert\leq J\leq L^{\prime}+S^{\prime}
. The parity of the three-particle state with a total angular momentum
J
is

\displaystyle P=\eta(-1)^{S+L}=\eta(-1)^{S^{\prime}+L^{\prime}}\,,

where
\eta
is the product of the intrinsic parities of the three particles, e.g. for three pseudoscalar pions the product of intrinsic parities is
\eta=(-1)^{3}=-1
. Since the strong interaction conserves parity, only transitions where
S+L
and
S^{\prime}+L^{\prime}
are both even or both odd are allowed.

To couple the helicity basis to the spin-orbit basis, we have introduced the
spin-orbit coupling coefficients

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}
, which are defined in terms of Clebsch-Gordan coefficients as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})=\sqrt{\frac{2L+1}{2J+1}}\,\braket{J\lambda|L0,S\lambda}\,\delta_{\ell S}\,.

(21)

The Kronecker delta enforces that the total spin is equal to that of the pair,
S=\ell
, as expected for our three spinless particles.

12
12

12

In anticipation of extensions for external particles with spin we define the spin-orbit coupling coefficients with the redundant
\ell=S
, which in the case for particles with spin the Kronecker delta will be replaced with an additional Clebsch-Gordan coefficient which couples the pair and spectator spins to total
S
.

From the completeness relation of the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, one immediately sees that the spin-orbit couplings satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L^{\prime}_{J})\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})=\delta_{L^{\prime}L}\,.

(22)

The spin-orbit amplitudes describe the transition
{}^{2S+1}L_{J}\,\to\,^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J}
for the quasi-two-body reaction
\xi_{k}^{(\ell)}+\varphi_{k}\to\xi_{p}^{(\ell^{\prime})}+\varphi_{p}
. Therefore, for fixed
\sigma_{k}
and
\sigma_{p}
, the amplitudes have the usual threshold behavior from orbital angular momentum barrier suppression,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)\sim p^{L^{\prime}}k^{L}\,,

as
p,k\to 0
for fixed
\sigma_{k},\sigma_{p}
.

As an example of the form of spin-orbit coupling coefficients, let us consider a system of three pions. For a pair of pions in relative
S
wave, then
\ell=0
and the only allowed
L
is
L=J
. So, the spin-orbit coefficient is simply

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(0)}(^{1}J_{J})=\delta_{\lambda 0}\,.

(23)

If the pair of pions is in an angular momentum state
\ell=1
, i.e. the resonant
P
wave channel, then the pair-spectator system is then a triple state with
S=1
. For some target total angular momentum
J
, the allowed orbital angular momenta are
L=J-1,J,J+1
. Therefore, the spin-orbit coefficients can be simplified to the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(1)}(^{3}L_{J})=\begin{cases}\,\sqrt{\dfrac{J+1}{2(2J+1)}}\,\lvert\lambda\rvert+\sqrt{\dfrac{J}{2J+1}}\,\delta_{\lambda,0}\,,&L=J-1\,,\\[20.0pt]
\,-\dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\,,&L=J\,,\\[20.0pt]
\,\sqrt{\dfrac{J}{2(2J+1)}}\,\lvert\lambda\rvert-\sqrt{\dfrac{J+1}{2J+1}}\,\delta_{\lambda,0}\,,&L=J+1\,.\end{cases}

(24)

Since the pion is a pseudoscalar, the product of the intrinsic parities is
\eta=-1
, and therefore the total parity of the system is
P=(-1)^{L}
. For a target
J^{P}=1^{+}
and the initial and final pairs both being vectors
\ell=\ell^{\prime}=1
, then only
S
and
D
waves contribute giving an two-dimensional amplitude with
{}^{3}S_{1}
and
{}^{3}D_{1}

The OPE is a

\displaystyle u_{pk}

\displaystyle=(P-k-p)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}+m_{p}^{2}-2E_{k}\omega_{p}-2pk\,z_{pk}\,,

\displaystyle\zeta_{pk}

\displaystyle\equiv z_{pk}\,\Big\rvert_{u_{pk}=m_{e}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle=-1-\frac{m_{e}^{2}-u^{(0)}_{pk}}{2pk}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{2s(\sigma_{k}+m_{p}^{2}-m_{e}^{2})-(s+\sigma_{k}-m_{k}^{2})(s+m_{p}^{2}-\sigma_{p})}{\lambda^{1/2}(s,\sigma_{k},m_{k}^{2})\lambda^{1/2}(s,\sigma_{p},m_{p}^{2})}\,,

contains potentially square-root singularities depending on the system of helicities. In order to obtain an analytic representation for the OPE, we isolate the singular dependencies in

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\xi_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(z_{pk})\,\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})\,,

, with the helicities

, thus the spin-helicity function factorizes into the non-analytic half-angle factor and a regular function in

It is further useful to define the
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\equiv\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})\,\Big\rvert_{z_{pk}=\zeta_{pk}}\,,

(43)

which allows us to express the function as a term at the propagator pole and a term which is the difference of the pole and non-pole term.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)+\left[\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})-\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\right]\,,

(44)

Near the pole, the difference vanishes as
(\zeta_{pk}-z_{pk})

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta_{pk}-z_{pk}}\,\left[\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})-\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\right]\,.

(45)

The introduction of the
\mathcal{B}
coefficient allows us to completely isolate the analytic behavior of the OPE in
z_{pk}
into the generic form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2pk}\xi_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}(z_{pk})\,\left[\,\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)}{\zeta_{pk}-z_{pk}}+\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})\,\right]\,.

(46)

As discussed in the beginning of this section, the singular behavior of
\mathcal{G}
in the variable
z_{pk}=\cos\theta_{pk}
comes from two locations. First there is the overall half-angle factor, hidden in the spin-helicity function, which exhibits kinematic singularities due to the spin of the initial and final state pairs. Second, the OPE is singular where the exchange particle goes on its mass-shell, which is encoded in the pole. The remaining
z_{pk}
behavior is analytic in the physical region we consider.

The
\mathcal{B}
coefficients are constructed to be regular functions of
z_{pk}
on the interval
-1\leq z_{pk}\leq+1
, thus for a fixed
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda
we can freely expand it into Legendre polynomials as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)\,\mathcal{B}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\,P_{j}(z_{pk})\,,

(47)

where by the orthogonality of the Legendre polynomials we can obtain the projected coefficients

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z_{pk}\,P_{j}(z_{pk})\,\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k,z_{pk})\,.

(48)

We stress here that although Eq. (
47

\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell
only a finite number of projected coefficients Eq. (
48

J
. In Sec.

, we give explicit examples of these coefficients for

Since the OPE is a known function, we can easily tabulate the
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients for the particular scattering channels of interest by the procedure outlined above. While at first, this may seem like extra computational steps given that
\mathcal{G}
has a known form, we find that this decomposition allows us to write down a generic analytic representation for the definite-parity partial wave amplitudes of the OPE. In doing so, we arrive at an exact result that isolates all the known singular structures of the OPE in the
(\sigma_{p},s,\sigma_{k})
variables, and a set of coefficients which are determined from the identified
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients.

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
are

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j,L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)\equiv\frac{1}{2pk}\,(2j+1)\,\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{(\ell^{\prime})}(^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J})\,\mathcal{C}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{J}\,\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\,\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})\,.

(61)

We stress here that Eq. (
59
) is a generic result for an exchange of a spinless particle between pairs with any angular momentum that couple to some total
J^{P}
. The matrices
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
are regular functions of the energies in the physical region.

It is important to note that Eq. (
59
) is not a unique decomposition, as the Legendre functions of the
2\textsuperscript{nd}
-kind can be written, cf. Eqs. (
102
) and (
103
) in App.
A
, as

\displaystyle Q_{j}(z)=P_{j}(z)\,Q_{0}(z)-W_{j-1}(z)\,,

(62)

where
P_{j}
are the Legendre polynomials and
W_{j-1}(z)
is polynomial in
z
, defined for
j>0
by

\displaystyle W_{j-1}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{j}\frac{1}{n}\,P_{n-1}(z)\,P_{j-n}(z)\,,

(63)

whereas for
j=0
,
W_{-1}=0
. This relation allows one to shift the definitions of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
to absorb/remove terms regular in the kinematic variables, which can further simplify expressions for the partial wave projected OPE. Exploiting this relation, we reduce our result for the partial wave OPE to one involving only
Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)=\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G},L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)+\mathcal{T}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)\,Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})

(64)

which is the expression claimed in Eq. (
1
) as our primary result. The matrix elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G},L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G},L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)-\sum_{j}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j,L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)\,W_{j-1}(\zeta_{pk})\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2pk}\,\sum_{j}(2j+1)\,\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{(\ell^{\prime})}(^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J})\,\mathcal{C}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{J}\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left[\,\mathcal{B}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)-W_{j-1}\,\mathcal{A}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\,\right]\,\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})\,,

(65)

and the
\mathcal{T}
coefficient is the sum over the
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
coefficients weighted by Legendre polynomials,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{j}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j,L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell),J}(p,k)\,P_{j}(\zeta_{pk})\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2pk}\,\sum_{j}(2j+1)P_{j}(\zeta_{pk})\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{(\ell^{\prime})}(^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J})\,\mathcal{C}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{J}\,\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(p,k)\,\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(\ell)}(^{2S+1}L_{J})

(66)

Equation (
64
), along with (
IV.2
) and (
IV.2
), are the main result of this work. The only remaining task for the user is to construct the
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{T}
matrices by identifying the appropriate
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients. In the next section, we illustrate the procedure for some low-spin cases.

V
Case Studies

In this section we examine the consequence of our main result, Eq. (
59
), for three cases of low-spin systems: pairs with
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=0
,
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1
, and the transition process
\ell^{\prime}=1,\ell=0
. Any higher spin system can be found by following the procedure outlined in this section. This procedure is easily amenable to symbolic computation with software such as
Mathematica
, and such a notebook with examples is supplied in the Supplemental Material.

The main tasks are to identify the
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients as defined in Eqs. (
43
) and (
45
), respectively, given for each case. Once these coefficients are determined, we compute the
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
functions, Eqs. (
IV.2
) and (
61
), respectively. These matrices are then fed into Eqs. (
IV.2
) and (
IV.2
) for
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{T}
, respectively, giving the analytic projection shown in Eq. (
64
).

Throughout this section, we denote
\eta=\eta_{k}\eta_{p}\eta_{e}
as the product of intrinsic parities of the initial and final spectators, and the exchange particle. As our focus is on hadronic processes, we also assume parity-conserving reactions with
P=\eta(-1)^{L+S}=\eta(-1)^{L^{\prime}+S^{\prime}}
. Additionally, since
S^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}
and
S=\ell
always in this work, we introduce a convenient notation for the partial wave OPE,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{2S^{\prime}+1}L^{\prime}_{J}|^{2S+1}L_{J})\equiv\mathcal{G}_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime},LS}^{(S^{\prime}S),J}(p,k)\,,

where the dependencies on kinematic variables are left implicit.

V.1
Pairs with
\boldsymbol{\ell^{\prime}=\ell=0}

The simplest case is when both the incoming and outgoing pairs are in relative
S
waves, so that
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=0
. The complications with the spin-helicity function are eliminated since
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda=0
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(00)}=\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}0}\delta_{\lambda 0}
. This in turn indicates that the coefficients are
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(00)}=\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}0}\delta_{\lambda 0}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(00)}=0
. Therefore, the exchange propagator (
46
) reduces to the simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0\lambda^{\prime},0\lambda}=\frac{1}{2pk}\,\frac{\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}0}\delta_{\lambda 0}}{\zeta_{pk}-z_{pk}}\,.

(67)

Recoupling to spin-orbit amplitudes is also trivial as only
S=\ell=0
and
S^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}=0
, restricting the allowed orbital angular momentum to be
J=L=L^{\prime}
. The parity of the system is then
P=\eta(-1)^{J}
.

From Eq. (
IV.2
), we see that
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}(^{1}J_{J}|^{1}J_{J})=0
because
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(00)}=0
for this case. Using Eq. (
61
) and the identities for
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(0)}(^{1}J_{J})=\delta_{\lambda 0}
and
\,\mathcal{C}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{J}=\delta_{Jj}\delta_{\lambda^{\prime}0}\delta_{\lambda 0}/(2J+1)
found from Eqs. (
21
) and (
53
), respectively, one finds that the partial wave OPE for a target
J^{P}
simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{1}J_{J}|^{1}J_{J})=\frac{1}{2pk}\,Q_{J}(\zeta_{pk})\,.

(68)

As detailed in Eqs. (
64
), (
IV.2
), and (
IV.2
), we can express this amplitude in terms of only
Q_{0}
. Explicitly, the
J=0
, and
1
partial wave OPE amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{1}S_{0}|^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2pk}\,Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})\,,

(69)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{1}P_{1}|^{1}P_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2pk}+\frac{\zeta_{pk}}{2pk}\,Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})\,.

(70)

The
J=0
OPE agrees with the well-known result found in many works on three-body scattering processes, e.g. Refs.
Jackura et al. 2019
;
Dawid and Szczepaniak 2021
;
Jackura et al. 2021
;
Dawid et al. 2023a
.

We perform a simple check of Eq. (
68
) by verifying it satisfies the expected behavior near threshold. As discussed in Sec.
III.1
, we can recognize that
\mathcal{G}
can be thought of as an effective two-body amplitude by interpreting
\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}
and
\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}
as effective masses of the external states. As a result, one would expect that such amplitude
\mathcal{G}(^{1}J_{J}|^{1}J_{J})
scales as
(pk)^{J}
in the vicinity of the nearest pair-spectator threshold
\sqrt{s}\sim\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}+m_{p}\sim\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}+m_{k}
, cf. Sec.
III.2
.

To reproduce this behavior, we note that near this threshold both
p
and
k
are small,
p,k\to 0
. Fixing
\sigma_{k}>\sigma_{k}^{(\mathrm{thr}.)}
and
\sigma_{p}>\sigma_{p}^{(\mathrm{thr}.)}
, we see from the definition of
\zeta_{pk}
, Eq. (
IV.1
), that near threshold
\zeta_{pk}
diverges as
1/pk
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{pk}=\frac{\mathcal{N}}{2pk}+O\!\left(pk^{-1},p^{-1}k\right)\,,

where
\mathcal{N}=(\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}-m_{p})^{2}-m_{e}^{2}
is a positive constant. From the behavior of Legendre function for large arguments, Eq.
105
, we see that near threshold
Q_{J}(\zeta_{pk})\to(pk)^{J+1}
. Therefore, near threshold Eq. (
68
) satisfies the expected behavior of
\mathcal{G}(^{1}J_{J}|^{1}J_{J})\sim(pk)^{J}
. Explicitly, we write the threshold expansions of Eqs. (
69
) and (
70
) by using the asymptotic expansion of
Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})
for
\zeta_{pk}\to\infty
as given in Eq. (
106
),

\displaystyle Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{pk}}+\frac{1}{3\zeta_{pk}^{3}}+O\!\left(\zeta_{pk}^{\,-5}\right)\,.

(71)

Since
\zeta_{pk}
appears as a reciprocal in this expansion, we can write the Taylor series for
1/\zeta_{pk}
for small
p
and
k
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{pk}}=\frac{2pk}{\mathcal{N}}+O\!\left(p^{2}k,pk^{2}\right)\,.

Then, the explicit threshold behavior for the
{}^{1}S_{0}\to^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}\to^{1}P_{1}
amplitudes is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{1}S_{0}|^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}+O\!\left(p,k\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{1}P_{1}|^{1}P_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3\mathcal{N}^{2}}\,pk+O\!\left(p^{2}k,pk^{2}\right)\,,

which is consistent with the expected behavior.

V.2
Pairs with
\boldsymbol{\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1}

We turn to the case where the pairs carry non-zero angular momenta
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1
. In this case, the spin-helicity matrix has a non-trivial structure, and we must work out the
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients. Recall that given the elements of the spin-helicity matrix
\mathcal{H}
, we can solve for the
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients using Eqs. (
43
) and (
45
), respectively. Most readily, the
\mathcal{A}
coefficient is given by first isolating
\mathcal{A}(z_{pk})
from Eq. (
42
),

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k,z_{pk})=\frac{1}{\xi_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(z_{pk})}\,\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,,

(72)

and then setting
z_{pk}=\zeta_{pk}
. For convenience, we introduced the
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1
spin-helicity function
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}
and
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}
coefficient which has a common factor removed,

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}{3pk}\,\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}\,,

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}{3pk}\,\mathcal{A}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}\,.

When both initial and final state pairs are in relative
P
wave, the spin structure is such that there are only five independent functions which results from the reflection property Eq. (
32
). There is an accidental symmetry relating the
\lambda^{\prime},\lambda=+1,+1
and
+1,-1
components, yielding only four independent functions. In terms of the polar angles previously defined, we obtain for the spin-helicity function

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{\pm 1\pm 1}^{(11)}=-\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{\pm 1\mp 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k_{p}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}}{pk}\,\sin\chi_{p}^{\star}\sin\chi_{k}^{\star}\,,

(73a)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{\pm 10}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{k_{p}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}}{pk}\,\sin\chi_{p}^{\star}\cos\chi_{k}^{\star}\,,

(73b)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{0\pm 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{k_{p}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}}{pk}\,\cos\chi_{p}^{\star}\sin\chi_{k}^{\star}\,,

(73c)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{H}}}_{00}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{p}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}}{pk}\,\cos\chi_{p}^{\star}\cos\chi_{k}^{\star}\,.

(73d)

The Lorentz transformations Eqs. (
37
) and (
38
) relate the polar angles
\chi_{k}^{\star}
and
\chi_{p}^{\star}
to the total CM frame polar angle
\theta_{pk}
. Note that if we compare Eq. (
73
) to expressions in Ref.
Kamal 1965
, we find disagreement with respect to overall phase factors. This is due to the azimuthal angle of
\pi
for one of the momenta as detailed in Eq. (
IV.1
), which was neglected by the author of Ref.
Kamal 1965
. One can convince themselves that this phase factor is necessary and consistent with an alternative model for the OPE which replaces the spin-helicity matrix as given in Eq. (
31
) with polarization Lorentz tensors contracted with momenta, e.g.
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}\propto(\varepsilon^{*}(P_{p},\lambda^{\prime})\cdot k)(\varepsilon(P_{k},\lambda)\cdot p)
, which can be seen by considering an effective Lagrangian of vector field
V_{\mu}
coupling to two scalars, e.g.
\mathcal{L}\supset-ig_{p}V_{p}^{\mu}\,\varphi_{e}\partial_{\mu}\varphi_{k}-ig_{k}V_{k}^{\mu}\,\varphi_{e}\partial_{\mu}\varphi_{p}
where
g_{p}
,
g_{k}
are effective couplings to the scalar
\varphi
fields.

Recalling that
z_{pk}\equiv\cos\theta_{pk}
and
\sin\theta_{pk}=\sqrt{1-z_{pk}^{2}}
, the half-angle factor for each helicity combination is given by

\displaystyle\xi_{\pm 1\pm 1}(z_{pk})

\displaystyle=\frac{1+z_{pk}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\xi_{\pm 1\mp 1}(z_{pk})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z_{pk}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\xi_{\pm 10}(z_{pk})=\xi_{0\pm 1}(z_{pk})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{1-z_{pk}^{2}}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\xi_{00}(z_{pk})

\displaystyle=1\,.

Combining Eqs. (
72
) and (
73
) and using the boost relations Eqs. (
38
) and (
37
) gives for the
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}(p,k,z_{pk})
coefficients

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\pm 1\pm 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-1+z_{pk}\,,

(74a)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\pm 1\mp 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=1+z_{pk}\,,

(74b)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\pm 10}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{k}\,\left(\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+z_{pk}\right)\,,

(74c)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{0\pm 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\mp\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{p}\,\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+z_{pk}\right)\,,

(74d)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{00}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{p}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+z_{pk}\right)\left(\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+z_{pk}\right)\,.

(74e)

The on-shell coefficients
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}(p,k)\equiv\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}(p,k,\zeta_{pk})
are given by Eq. (
74
) with the substitution
z_{pk}\to\zeta_{pk}
. We again define the
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}
coefficients in terms of
\mathcal{B}
coefficients as

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}{3pk}\,\mathcal{B}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}\,,

which are related to
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}
through Eq. (
45
),

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k,z_{pk})=\frac{1}{\zeta_{pk}-z_{pk}}\,\left[\,\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k,z_{pk})-\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{A}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k)\,\right]\,,

Evaluating the difference and dividing by the pole gives
\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}(p,k,z_{pk})

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\pm 1\pm 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-1\,,

(75a)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\pm 1\mp 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-1\,,

(75b)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\pm 10}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\mp\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{k}\,,

(75c)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{0\pm 1}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{2}\,\gamma_{p}\,,

(75d)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{00}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{p}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+z_{pk}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\,.

(75e)

Finally, we require the projected
\mathcal{B}
coefficients defined by Eq. (
48
), repeated here for convenience,

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z_{pk}\,P_{j}(z_{pk})\,\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}(p,k,z_{pk})\,.

Upon substituting Eq. (
75
) we find only two non-zero terms in the expansion

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{0,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-|\lambda\,\lambda^{\prime}|\,-\sqrt{2}\gamma_{k}\,\lambda^{\prime}\,\delta_{\lambda,0}+\sqrt{2}\gamma_{p}\,\lambda\,\delta_{\lambda^{\prime},0}\,

\displaystyle\qquad-\gamma_{p}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\,\delta_{\lambda^{\prime},0}\delta_{\lambda,0}\,,

(76a)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{1,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\,\gamma_{p}\gamma_{k}\,\delta_{\lambda^{\prime},0}\delta_{\lambda,0}\,,

(76b)

\displaystyle\,\,\overline{\!\!{\mathcal{B}}}_{j,\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(11)}

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,j>1\,.

(76c)

Having found the
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
coefficients for
\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1
, we now construct
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{T}
matrices for some target
J^{P}
. Trivially
S^{\prime}=S=1
, therefore if
J=0
only
L^{\prime}=L=1
contributes, while for
J>0
the allowed orbital angular momenta are
L=J-1,J,J+1
and
L^{\prime}=J-1,J,J+1
for the initial and final states, respectively.

V.2.1
Total
\boldsymbol{J=0}

Let us first consider a target
J=0
, where only
L^{\prime}=L=1
is allowed and the corresponding parity is
\eta
. Thus, we need only compute a single
{}^{3}P_{0}
amplitude with the spin-orbit recoupling from Eq. (
21
) being
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(1)}(^{3}P_{0})=-\delta_{\lambda,0}
. Evaluating the expression for
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
using Eqs. (
IV.2
) and (
61
) respectively, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}(^{3}P_{0}|^{3}P_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\gamma_{k}\gamma_{p}}{2q_{k}^{\star}q_{p}^{\star}}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}(^{3}P_{0}|^{3}P_{0})

\displaystyle=\delta_{j0}\,\frac{3\gamma_{k}\gamma_{p}}{2q_{k}^{\star}q_{p}^{\star}}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\left(\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\,.

Since only
j=0
contributes for this case, feeding these matrices into Eqs. (
IV.2
) and (
IV.2
) gives trivially
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{G}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\mathcal{T}=\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
. Adding these contributions together as dictated by Eq. (
64
), one finds

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{0}|^{3}P_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\gamma_{k}\gamma_{p}}{2q_{k}^{\star}q_{p}^{\star}}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+\zeta_{pk}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{3\gamma_{k}\gamma_{p}}{2q_{k}^{\star}q_{p}^{\star}}\left(\frac{\beta_{p}\omega_{k}}{k}+\zeta_{pk}\right)\left(\frac{\beta_{k}\omega_{p}}{p}+\zeta_{pk}\right)Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk}).

(77)

As before, we check the threshold behavior of this amplitude by fixing
\sigma_{k}
and
\sigma_{p}
and expanding for small
p
and
k
. Just as in Sec.
V.1
,
\zeta_{pk}
diverges as
\zeta_{pk}=\mathcal{N}/2pk
as
p,k\to 0
, thus
Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})
admits an expansion as Eq. (
71
). Moreover,
\gamma_{p}=1+O(p^{2})
,
\omega_{p}=m_{p}+O(p^{2})
, and
E_{p}=\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}+O(p^{2})
as
p\to 0
, with similar expansions for variables of the
k
spectator as
k\to 0
. Near the pair-spectator thresholds, one finds that the expansion of Eq. (
V.2.1
) for
p,k\to 0
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{0}|^{3}P_{0})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\left(1+\frac{3m_{p}m_{k}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}}\right)\,pk+O\!\left(p^{2}k,pk^{2}\right)\,,

where the relative momenta
q_{p}^{\star}
and
q_{k}^{\star}
are finite positive constants since
\sigma_{p}
and
\sigma_{k}
are fixed above their respective thresholds. Therefore, as expected
\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{0}|^{3}P_{0})\sim pk
near threshold.

V.2.2
Total
\boldsymbol{J=1}
, Parity
\boldsymbol{\eta}

Next let us consider a target
J=1
with a parity
\eta
, which enforces
L^{\prime}=L=1
, i.e.
{}^{3}P_{1}\to^{3}P_{1}
. From Eq. (
21
) the spin-orbit coupling is
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{(1)}(^{3}P_{1})=-\lambda/\sqrt{2}
. From Eq. (
IV.2
) we find that

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{G}}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=0
, while from Eq. (
61
) the
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
factors are

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{j}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=-\frac{1}{2q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\left(\,2\delta_{j0}-3\zeta_{pk}\,\delta_{j1}+\delta_{j2}\,\right)\,,

with all coefficients
j>2
being zero. Thus, the partial wave OPE Eq. (
59
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=-\frac{1}{q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})+\frac{3\zeta_{pk}}{2q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}Q_{1}(\zeta_{pk})-\frac{1}{2q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}Q_{2}(\zeta_{pk})\,.

(78)

Before applying the simplifications of Eqs. (
IV.2
) and (
IV.2
), we first perform an intermediary manipulation of Eq. (
78
) by using the Bonnet recursion relation for the Legendre function
Q_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=-\frac{1}{2q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\Big[\,Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})-Q_{2}(\zeta_{pk})\,\Big]\,.

(79)

\pi

\pi\pi\to\rho
subsystems. The difference in overall sign is due to the error in Ref.
Kamal 1965
from not considering the correct azimuthal angle of one of the momenta as discussed in the beginning of Sec.
V.2
. Finally, we use
Q_{2}(\zeta_{pk})=P_{2}(\zeta_{pk})Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})-3\zeta_{pk}/2
to express Eq. (
79

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=-\frac{3}{4q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\zeta_{pk}+\frac{3}{4q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\left(\,\zeta_{pk}^{2}-1\,\right)\,Q_{0}(\zeta_{pk})\,.

(80)

Near threshold we expect
\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})\sim pk
, which is verified by following the same procedure as in the previous cases, and finding

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}P_{1}|^{3}P_{1})=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\,pk+O\left(p^{2}k,pk^{2}\right)\,,

which agrees with the expected behavior.

Examining the threshold behavior as in previous cases, we find that the
{}^{3}S_{1}\to{{}^{3}S_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}S_{1}|^{3}S_{1})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\left(\frac{m_{p}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}}\,k^{2}+\frac{m_{k}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}}\,p^{2}\right)+O\!\left(p^{3},k^{3},p^{2}k,pk^{2}\right)\,.

If we fix
\sigma_{p}<\sigma_{k}
, then the threshold we approach first is
\sqrt{s}\sim\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}+m_{k}
. As we approach this threshold, then the amplitude does approach a constant, as
p
will be finite this threshold. However, if both
k
and
p
approach threshold simultaneous, e.g. in the case where
m_{p}=m_{k}
and
\sigma_{p}=\sigma_{k}

S

Repeating this exercise for
{}^{3}S_{1}\to{{}^{3}D_{1}}
and
{}^{3}D_{1}\to{{}^{3}D_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}D_{1}|^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{2}m_{k}}{\mathcal{N}q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}}\,p^{2}+O\!\left(p^{3},p^{2}k\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(^{3}D_{1}|^{3}D_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{15\mathcal{N}^{2}q_{p}^{\star}q_{k}^{\star}}\left(1+\frac{10m_{p}m_{k}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{p}}\sqrt{\sigma_{k}}}\right)\,p^{2}k^{2}+O\!\left(p^{3}k^{2},p^{2}k^{3}\right)\,,

where the threshold expansion of the
{}^{3}D_{1}\to{{}^{3}S_{1}}
amplitude is found by interchanging
k\leftrightarrow p
on
\mathcal{G}(^{3}D_{1}|^{3}S_{1})

\ell^{\prime}=\ell=1
. Next, we will look at examples for transitions between
\ell=0

\ell^{\prime}=1

\boldsymbol{\ell^{\prime}=1}

Here we consider an initial pair with spin

Following the same procedure as in the previous cases, we have for

Our final example is for

\sigma_{k}

VII
Summary

We have shown a generic procedure to project relativistic scattering amplitudes of three spinless particles to definite
J^{P}
partial waves, with focus on it application to the kinematic singularity arising from on-shell particle exchanges between two-body sub-channel scattering processes. The procedure as presented in Sec.
IV
, specifically in the final projection results shown in Eqs. (
64
), (
IV.2
), and (
IV.2
) allow one to systematically compute the contribution from the one-particle exchange, which was illustrated in Sec.
V
for some low-lying spins of immediate interest, e.g. in the scattering of three pions as discussed in Sec.
VI
. These results can then be supplied into the corresponding integral equations
Hansen and Sharpe 2015
;
Jackura 2023
, along with some parameterized and constrained three-body
K
matrix, e.g. ones constrained from lattice QCD calculations with finite-volume quantization conditions, to reconstruct the complete on-shell
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
hadronic scattering amplitude.

Our resulting analytic representation for the one-particle exchange of definite
J^{P}
allows one to avoid performing a numerical integration over a singular function, which is generally slowly convergent, and allows the practitioner to have full control over the analytic behavior in the complex
s
-,
\sigma_{p}
-, and
\sigma_{k}
-planes. Controlling the analytic structure of aspects of three-body amplitudes has been shown to be important for the analytic continuation of the
\mathbf{3}\to\mathbf{3}
amplitude, e.g. for searching for resonant structures in hadron spectroscopy
Dawid et al. 2023a
;
Dawid et al. 2023b
. Looking forward, our results can be immediately used in the community in further theoretical and phenomenological studies of three-hadron resonance production. Furthermore, they can be extended to accomadate a more general class of reactions, such as those with external particles with arbitrary spin.

Appendix A
Recapitulation of Angular Momentum Functions

This appendix is devoted to collecting useful identities and properties of the Legendre functions and Wigner rotation matrix elements which we use throughout this work. While these relations can be found in the literature (which we refer to as appropriate), we feel that this summary serves to assist the reader in understanding the technical aspects of our work.

A.1
Legendre Functions of the
\boldsymbol{1\textsuperscript{st}}
-kind

The Legendre functions of the 1
st
kind,
P_{\ell}(z)
, are the regular solutions of Legendre’s differential equation
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
, which can be expressed explicitly for
\ell\in\mathbbm{N}_{0}
and
-1\leq z\leq+1
by the Rodrigues’ formula,

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\,\ell!}\,\frac{\textrm{d}^{\ell}}{\textrm{d}z^{\ell}}(z^{2}-1)^{\ell}\,.

(94)

We consider only
\ell\in\mathbbm{N}_{0}
, therefore the functions are analytic in
z\in\mathbbm{C}
for each
\ell
. The Legendre functions form an orthogonal set of functions over the interval
-1\leq z\leq+1
,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}\textrm{d}z\,P_{\ell^{\prime}}(z)P_{\ell}(z)=\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\,\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\,.

(95)

The first few Legendre functions are

\displaystyle P_{0}(z)=1\,,\quad P_{1}(z)=z\,,\quad P_{2}(z)=\frac{1}{2}(3z^{2}-1)\,.

Given
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
, all remaining
P_{\ell}
can be generated through the Bonnet recursion relation for
\ell>1
,

\displaystyle\ell P_{\ell}(z)=z(2\ell-1)\,P_{\ell-1}(z)-(\ell-1)\,P_{\ell-2}(z)\,.

(96)

There are many additional properties and identities which can be found in Ref.
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
. Here we state one integral relation,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z\,z^{\ell}P_{\ell}(z)=\frac{2^{\ell+1}(\ell!)^{2}}{(2\ell+1)!}\,,

(97)

which is useful in the asymptotic expansion of the Legendre functions of the 2
nd
kind as discussed next in section.

A.2
Legendre Functions of the
\boldsymbol{2\textsuperscript{nd}}
-kind

A second class of solutions of Legendre’s differential equation are the Legendre functions of the 2
nd
kind,
Q_{\ell}(z)
. For every
\ell\in\mathbbm{N}_{0}
, the
Q_{\ell}
functions are related to the
P_{\ell}
functions through the Neumann relation
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
,

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z^{\prime}\,\frac{P_{\ell}(z^{\prime})}{z-z^{\prime}}\,.

(98)

The integral has endpoint singularities, leading to branch points in
Q_{\ell}
in the complex
z
-plane at
z=\pm 1
for each
\ell
. We choose to orient the branch cut such that the function is analytic on
z\in\mathbbm{C}\,/\,\{z\,|\,-1\leq z\leq+1\}
, which is equivalent to choosing
z\to z+i\epsilon
in Eq. (
98
) and taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
after integration. The Neumann relation (
98
) allows us to easily identify the discontinuity of
Q_{\ell}
across the branch cut,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Disc}\,Q_{\ell}(z)=-i\pi P_{\ell}(z)\,\Theta(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\,,

(99)

where
\Theta
is the Heaviside step function.

The first few Legendre functions of the 2
nd
kind are given by

\displaystyle Q_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{z+1}{z-1}\right)\,,\quad Q_{1}(z)=P_{1}(z)Q_{0}(z)-1\,,\quad Q_{2}(z)=P_{2}(z)Q_{0}(z)-\frac{3z}{2}\,.

(100)

Combining the Neumann relation (
98
) with the Bonnet recursion relation for the
P_{\ell}
functions, Eq. (
96
), yields a recursion relation for integer
\ell>1
given
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
,

\displaystyle\ell Q_{\ell}(z)=z(2\ell-1)\,Q_{\ell-1}(z)-(\ell-1)\,Q_{\ell-2}(z)\,.

(101)

One can then construct an explicit expression for
Q_{\ell}
for any
\ell\in\mathbbm{N}_{0}
and
z
on the cut plane,

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}(z)=P_{\ell}(z)\,Q_{0}(z)-W_{\ell-1}(z)\,,

(102)

where
W_{\ell-1}
is defined for
\ell>0
as

\displaystyle W_{\ell-1}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{n}P_{n-1}(z)\,P_{\ell-n}(z)\,,

(103)

with the
\ell=0
case defined as
W_{-1}=0

Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
.

The behavior of
Q_{\ell}(z)
as
z\to\infty
can be found by expanding the Neumann relation, Eq. (
98
), for large
z
,

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z^{\prime}\,(z^{\prime})^{n}\,P_{\ell}(z^{\prime})\,.

(104)

The integral is identically zero for
n<\ell
, thus the leading asymptotic behavior is given when
n=\ell
, which from Eq. (
97
) gives

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}(z)\xrightarrow[z\to\infty]{}\frac{2^{\ell}(\ell!)^{2}}{(2\ell+1)!}\,\frac{1}{z^{\ell+1}}\,.

(105)

By direct evaluation of Eq. (
104
), the explicit asymptotic expansion for the
\ell=0
function is given by

\displaystyle Q_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-2n-1}}{2n+1}=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{3z^{3}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{z^{5}}\right)\,.

(106)

A.3
Spherical Harmonics

For spinless particles, orbital angular momentum states are represented by the spherical harmonics
Varshalovich et al. 1988
(with the Condon–Shortley phase convention)

\displaystyle Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\braket{\theta,\varphi|\ell m}\,,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)(\ell-m)!}{4\pi(\ell+m)!}}\,P_{\ell}^{m}(\cos\theta)\,e^{im\varphi}\,,

(107)

where
P_{\ell}^{m}
are the associated Legendre functions,

\displaystyle P_{\ell}^{m}(z)=(1-z^{2})^{m/2}\,\frac{\textrm{d}^{m}}{\textrm{d}z^{m}}P_{\ell}(z)\,.

(108)

See Ref.
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
for properties of
P_{\ell}^{m}
. The spherical harmonics for
m\geq 0
and
\ell\leq 2
are explicitly

\displaystyle Y_{00}=\phantom{-}\sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi}}\,,

\displaystyle Y_{10}=\phantom{-}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\,\cos\theta\,,

\displaystyle Y_{11}=-\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi}}\,e^{i\varphi}\,\sin\theta\,,

\displaystyle Y_{20}=\phantom{-}\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}\,(3\cos^{2}\theta-1)\,,

\displaystyle Y_{21}=-\sqrt{\frac{15}{8\pi}}\,e^{i\varphi}\,\sin\theta\cos\theta\,,

\displaystyle Y_{22}=\phantom{-}\sqrt{\frac{15}{32\pi}}\,e^{2i\varphi}\,\sin^{2}\theta\,,

where
m<0
components are given by the reflection property

\displaystyle Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\theta,\varphi)=(-1)^{m}\,Y_{\ell\,-m}(\theta,\varphi)\,.

(109)

The spherical harmonics are orthonormal over the entire solid angle,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\textrm{d}\varphi\int_{-1}^{+1}\textrm{d}\cos\theta\,Y_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{*}(\theta,\varphi)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)=\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\,\delta_{m^{\prime}m}\,,

(110)

and satisfy the spherical addition theorem

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(z)=\frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\,Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)\,,

(111)

where
z=\cos\theta\cos\theta^{\prime}+\sin\theta\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos(\varphi^{\prime}-\varphi)
and
P_{\ell}
are the Legendre functions of the 1
st
-kind. Note that if
m=0
for all
\ell\in\mathbbm{N}_{0}
, then the spherical harmonics are related to the Legendre functions as

\displaystyle Y_{\ell 0}(\theta,\varphi)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}\,P_{\ell}(z)\,.

A.4
Wigner Rotation Matrix Elements

Here we summarize some useful properties of Wigner rotation matrix elements. A detailed review can be found in Ref.
Varshalovich et al. 1988
. A rotation of
\theta
about an axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}
is given by the unitary operator
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{\hat{n}}}(\theta)=e^{-i\theta\,\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{n}}}
where
\mathbf{J}
is the angular momentum operator. For a generic rotation about the Euler angles
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\equiv\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{\hat{z}}}(\alpha)\cdot\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}(\beta)\cdot\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{\hat{z}}}(\gamma)\,.

Wigner
D
matrix elements of
\mathcal{R}
in a basis
\ket{jm}
, with representation
j
and projection
m
spanning
-j\leq m\leq j
, are defined as

\displaystyle D_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)

\displaystyle\equiv\bra{jm}\mathcal{R}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\ket{jm^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle=e^{-im\alpha}\,d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta)\,e^{-im^{\prime}\gamma}\,,

(112)

where
d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}
are the Wigner “little”
d
matrix elements. The
d
matrix elements can be expressed in terms of the Jacobi polynomials
Varshalovich et al. 1988

\displaystyle d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta)=(-1)^{\eta}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma!(\sigma+\mu+\nu)!}{(\sigma+\mu)!(\sigma+\nu)!}}\,\xi_{mm^{\prime}}(\cos\beta)\,P_{\sigma}^{(\mu,\nu)}(\cos\beta)\,,

(113)

where
\mu=\lvert m-m^{\prime}\rvert
,
\nu=\lvert m+m^{\prime}\rvert
,
\sigma=j-(\mu+\nu)/2
, and the phase power
\eta=0
if
m^{\prime}\geq m
and
\eta=m^{\prime}-m
if
m^{\prime}<m
. The function
\xi_{mm^{\prime}}
is known as the
half-angle factor

Collins 1971
and is defined as

\displaystyle\xi_{mm^{\prime}}(z)=\left(\frac{1-z}{2}\right)^{\lvert m-m^{\prime}\rvert/2}\left(\frac{1+z}{2}\right)^{\lvert m+m^{\prime}\rvert/2}\,,

(114)

which is singular at
z=\pm 1
and symmetric under the interchange
m\leftrightarrow m^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\xi_{mm^{\prime}}(z)=\xi_{m^{\prime}m}(z)\,.

The Jacobi polynomials
P_{\sigma}^{(\mu,\nu)}(z)
are regular functions of real
z
,

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}^{(\mu,\nu)}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\sigma}\frac{(\sigma+\mu)!(\sigma+\nu)!}{n!(\sigma+\mu-n)!(\nu+n)!(\sigma-n)!}\,\left(\frac{z-1}{2}\right)^{\sigma-n}\,\left(\frac{z+1}{2}\right)^{n}\,,

(115)

where we require
\sigma
,
\sigma+\mu
,
\sigma+\nu
, and
\sigma+\mu+\nu
are non-negative integers
Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
.

The Wigner
d
matrix elements themselves have numerous symmetry relations, most importantly for this work

\displaystyle d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}=(-1)^{m^{\prime}-m}d_{m^{\prime}m}^{(j)}=d_{-m^{\prime}-m}^{(j)}\,,

and

\displaystyle d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta)=d_{m^{\prime}m}^{(j)}(-\beta),\ ,

(116)

which are discussed in Ref.
Varshalovich et al. 1988
.

Like the spherical harmonics, the Wigner
d
matrix elements respect an orthogonality condition over the interval
-1\leq\cos\beta\leq+1
,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}\textrm{d}\cos\beta\,d_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta)\,d_{\bar{m}\bar{m}^{\prime}}^{(\bar{j})}(\beta)=\frac{2}{2j+1}\,\delta_{j\bar{j}}\,\delta_{m\bar{m}}\,\delta_{m^{\prime}\bar{m}^{\prime}}\,

(117)

which can be seen by the definition (
113
) and using the orthogonality condition of the Jacobi polynomials over the same domain

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}z\,

\displaystyle(1-z)^{\mu}(1+z)^{\nu}\,P_{\sigma}^{(\mu,\nu)}(z)P_{\rho}^{(\mu,\nu)}(z)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\mu+\nu+1}}{2\sigma+\mu+\nu+1}\,\frac{(\sigma+\mu)!(\sigma+\nu)!}{\sigma!\,(\sigma+\mu+\nu)!}\,\delta_{\sigma\rho}\,,

(118)

for
\mu,\nu>-1

Abramowitz and Stegun 1964
. The addition theorem for Wigner
d
matrix elements
Varshalovich et al. 1988
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}}d_{mm^{\prime\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta_{1})\,d_{m^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\beta_{2})\,e^{-im^{\prime\prime}\varphi}=D_{mm^{\prime}}^{(j)}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,,

(119)

where the Euler angles
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\gamma
are given by the following relations
Varshalovich et al. 1988

\displaystyle\cot\alpha

\displaystyle=\cos\beta_{1}\cot\varphi+\cot\beta_{2}\,\frac{\sin\beta_{1}}{\sin\varphi}\,,

(120a)

\displaystyle\cos\beta

\displaystyle=\cos\beta_{1}\cos\beta_{2}-\sin\beta_{1}\sin\beta_{2}\cos\varphi\,,

(120b)

\displaystyle\cot\gamma

\displaystyle=\cos\beta_{2}\cot\varphi+\cot\beta_{1}\,\frac{\sin\beta_{2}}{\sin\varphi}\,.

(120c)

The sign of
\alpha
and
\gamma
can be fixed by the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta_{1}}=\frac{\sin\gamma}{\sin\beta_{2}}=\frac{\sin\varphi}{\sin\beta}\,.

(121)

Finally, the Wigner
D
matrix elements are related to the spherical harmonics as

\displaystyle Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}\,D_{m0}^{(\ell)\,*}(\varphi,\theta,0)\,.

(122)

axes are associated with body-fixed coordinate systems which are defined fixed to the momenta
\mathbf{P}_{k}
and
\mathbf{P}_{p}
, respectively.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{k}
and
\mathbf{P}_{p}
be the initial and final pair momenta in the total CM frame defined with respect to some generic space-fixed coordinate system
XYZ
. Then, the polar and azimuthal angles of
\mathbf{P}_{k}
are
\theta_{k}
and
\varphi_{k}
, respectively, while the polar and azimuthal angles of
\mathbf{P}_{p}
are
\theta_{p}
and
\varphi_{p}
, respectively. These orientations are depicted in Fig.
13
, where we also introduce the CM frame effective scattering angle
\theta_{pk}
defined with the usual addition of spherical angles

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{pk}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k}\,,

\displaystyle=\cos\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{k}+\sin\theta_{p}\sin\theta_{k}\cos(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})\,,

(130)

which follows from decomposing
\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p}
and
\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k}
into Cartesian components with respect to the space-fixed coordinate system. The reaction plane in the CM frame is now defined with a unit normal vector

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{n}}=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{k}\times\mathbf{P}_{p}}{\lvert\mathbf{P}_{k}\times\mathbf{P}_{p}\rvert}\,,

and is still used to define the azimuthal angles of the initial and final state pair rest frames as discussed in Sec.
II.1
. Note that when we orient
\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k}=\mathbf{\hat{Z}}
, we recover that
\theta_{p}=\theta_{pk}
and
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=\mathbf{\hat{Y}}
, as is chosen in the main text.

The partial wave expansion proceeds as in Sec.
III.1
, up through Eq. (
16
), which we repeat here for convenience,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{J=J_{\min}}^{\infty}(2J+1)\,\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}^{J}(p,k)\,\sum_{m_{J}=-J}^{J}D_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p})\,D_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k})\,.

The task now is to simplify the sum on
m_{J}
. Similar to spherical harmonics, the Wigner
D
matrix elements have an addition theorem which allows us to reduce the composition of two rotation functions to a single rotation, cf. App.
A
. For the rotation functions in Eq. (
16
), we find the following
Pendleton 2003

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{J}}D_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p})D_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{J}}D_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\varphi_{p},\theta_{p},0)D_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\varphi_{k},\theta_{k},0)\,,

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{J}}d_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(\theta_{p})\,d_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\theta_{k})\,e^{-im_{J}(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}\,,

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{J}}d_{\lambda m_{J}}^{(J)}(-\theta_{k})d_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(\theta_{p})\,e^{-im_{J}(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}\,,

(131)

where in the last line we used the symmetry identity Eq. (
116
). From the addition theorem, we find that we can express this sum as a single Wigner
D
matrix element,

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{J}}D_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p})D_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{J}}d_{\lambda m_{J}}^{(J)}(-\theta_{k})d_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(\theta_{p})\,e^{-im_{J}(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}\,

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda\varphi_{pk}}\,d_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(\theta_{pk})\,e^{i\lambda^{\prime}\psi_{pk}}\,,

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\varphi_{pk},\theta_{pk},\psi_{pk})\,,

(132)

where the resulting Euler angles are given by the usual addition of rotation matrices as summarized in App.
A
. Explicitly, the total CM frame scattering angle
\theta_{pk}
is given by
\cos\theta_{pk}\equiv\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k}
as is defined in Eq. (
C
), while the azimuthal angles
\varphi_{pk}
and
\psi_{pk}
are fully specified by the following relations

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\cot\varphi_{pk}=-\cos\theta_{k}\,\cot(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})+\cot\theta_{p}\,\frac{\sin\theta_{k}}{\sin(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}\,,\\[5.0pt]
\cot\psi_{pk}=-\cos\theta_{p}\,\cot(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})+\cot\theta_{k}\,\frac{\sin\theta_{p}}{\sin(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}\,,\\[5.0pt]
-\frac{\sin\varphi_{pk}}{\sin\theta_{p}}=\frac{\sin\psi_{pk}}{\sin\theta_{k}}=\frac{\sin(\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{p})}{\sin\theta_{pk}}\,.\end{gathered}

After using the addition theorem on the Wigner
d
matrix elements, Eq. (
C
), we find that the helicity partial wave expansion Eq. (
C
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})=\sum_{J}(2J+1)\,\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}^{J}(p,k)\,D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\varphi_{pk},\theta_{pk},\psi_{pk})\,,

(136)

which can be inverted to project a helicity amplitude into its partial waves

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}^{J}(p,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\textrm{d}\psi_{pk}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\textrm{d}\varphi_{pk}\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\int_{-1}^{+1}\!\textrm{d}\cos\theta_{pk}\,D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(\varphi_{pk},\theta_{pk},\psi_{pk})\,\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,.

(137)

Comparing to the kinematics outlined in Sec.
II
, it seems that there are additional independent variables in the form of the azimuthal dependencies
\varphi_{pk}
and
\psi_{pk}
. However, these azimuthal angles are non-dynamical in the sense that they orient the reaction plane with respect to our arbitrary external coordinate system. To see this, first consider the simple limit where
\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{P}}}_{k}=\mathbf{\hat{Z}}
. Therefore,
\theta_{k}=\varphi_{k}=0
. From Eq. (
C
) we find that as
\theta_{k}\to 0
and
\varphi_{k}\to 0
, that the Euler angles
\varphi_{pk}\to\varphi_{p}
,
\theta_{pk}\to\theta_{p}
, and
\psi_{pk}\to 0
. Thus, we have removed one of the azimuthal angles, and the angular momentum composition rule Eq. (
C
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{J}}D_{m_{J}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p})D_{m_{J}\lambda}^{(J)}(\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{k})

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)\,*}(\varphi_{p},\theta_{p},0)\,.

The remaining angle
\varphi_{pk}=\varphi_{p}
is not dynamical, as it only orients the reaction plane with respect to the external coordinate system,
\cos\varphi_{p}=\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{p}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{X}}
. Therefore, we can rotate the system about the helicity quantization axis by and angle of
-\varphi_{p}
, which preserves the helicity
Jacob and Wick 1959
, to eliminate this redundant angle and arrive at our result in Eq. (
17
).

In a similar manner, we can simultaneously rotate away both azimuthal angles in Eq. (
136
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}D_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}^{(J)}(0,0,\varphi_{pk})\,\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime},\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,D_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{(J)}(\psi_{pk},0,0)=\mathcal{M}_{\ell^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime},\ell\bar{\lambda}}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\,\Big\rvert_{\varphi_{pk}=\psi_{pk}=0}\,.

(138)

This transformation leaves the magnitude of the pair momenta invariant as they are aligned with their respective quantization axes. We conclude that the angles
\psi_{pk}
and
\varphi_{pk}
are non-dynamical variables and are arbitrary rotations which arise from the definitions of the three-particle states with respect to the spatial coordinate system. Therefore, if one rotates the system as in Eq. (
138
) to remove the
\varphi_{pk}
and
\psi_{pk}
angles, we arrive exactly at the expansion as originally presented in Eq. (
18
) of Sec.
III.1
.

Finally, we consider in detail the consequence of a generic reference frame to the OPE. In particular, we show that the dependence on the non-dynamical angles explicitly cancels in performing the partial-wave projection of the OPE. We begin by rewriting Eq. (
31
) here for convenience,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}^{(\ell^{\prime}\ell)}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{k})\equiv\left(\frac{k_{p}^{\star}}{q_{p}^{\star}}\right)^{\ell^{\prime}}4\pi\,Y_{\ell^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}^{*}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{k}}}_{p}^{\star})Y_{\ell\lambda}(\mathbf{\hat{\mathbf{p}}}_{k}^{\star})\left(\frac{p_{k}^{\star}}{q_{k}^{\star}}\right)^{\ell}\,.

We need to decompose the vectors
\mathbf{k}_{p}^{\star}
and
\mathbf{p}_{k}^{\star}
with respect to the general coordinate system, as well as describe their Lorentz transformations. In the total CM frame, the spectator momenta are anti-parallel to the pair momenta, e.g. for the final spectator

(139a)

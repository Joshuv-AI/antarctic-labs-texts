1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to understand the large-scale behaviour of homogeneous and inhomogeneous
p
-Sobolev functions, with
1\leq p<+\infty
, when
(X,d,\mu)
is an Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric measure space, with
1<Q<+\infty
, which supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality, as defined in Section
2.3
. In particular, we explore whether the limit

(1.1)

\lim_{x\to{+\infty}}u(x)

exists for such Sobolev functions
u
. Here
x\to{+\infty}
means that
|x|\to{+\infty}
where
|x|:=d(O,x)
for a given
O\in X
.
For
1\leq p<+\infty
, the homogeneous Sobolev space, denoted
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X):=\dot{N}^{1,p}(X,d,\mu)
, is the space of locally integrable functions that have a
p
-integrable upper gradient on
X
, where the notion of upper gradients is given in Section
2.2
. As usual, the Sobolev space
N^{1,p}(X):=N^{1,p}(X,d,\mu),

1\leq p<+\infty
, is the collection of all
p
-integrable functions on
(X,d,\mu)
belonging to
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
.

Recently, the second and third authors established the existence and uniqueness of limits at infinity of functions in
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
for
1\leq p<+\infty
, along infinite curves outside a zero modulus family. This is under the assumption that the space is equipped with a doubling measure which supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality, and that the space satisfies the annular chain property, see
[
KN23
]
and Section
2.4
.
In particular, it was shown that for every
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X),
there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, depending only on
u
, such that

(1.2)

\lim_{t\to{+\infty}}u(\gamma(t))\text{\rm\ \ exists and is equal to $c$ for $p$-almost every $\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}$}.

Here,
\Gamma^{+\infty}
is the collection of all infinite curves, defined in Section
2.1
. By
p
-almost every curve, we mean that the conclusion holds for every curve outside a family with zero
p
-modulus, see Section
2.2
.
If there exists

such that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(\gamma(t))=c
, then we say that

has a limit along
\gamma
. If
c\in\mathbb{R}
, then we say that
u
has a finite limit along
\gamma
. For a recent thorough investigation of when
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)=N^{1,p}(X)+\mathbb{R}
in metric measure spaces of uniformly locally controlled geometry, see
[
GKS24
]
. For each
1\leq p<\infty
, there exists a function
f\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)

such that, although
the limit at infinity of

along
p
-almost every infinite curve exists and equals to a finite constant by (
1.2
), the function

may fail to be in
N^{1,p}(X)+\mathbb{R}
by
[
GKS24
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Moreover, we refer the readers to
[
LN24
]
for a discussion on (
1.2
) for bounded variation functions, and that the conditions of doubling and Poincaré inequalities are
necessary for the existence of limits at infinity along
1
-almost every infinite curve of bounded variation functions.

Under the assumptions of Ahlfors
Q
-regularity, we first show that we can improve
the above result of
[
KN23
]
when
1\leq p<\infty
is strictly less than
Q
and
X
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality. For
1\leq p\leq Q
, we define the notion of a

-thin set, which roughly speaking has sufficiently small Hausdorff content in dyadic annuli at infinity. See Section

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
and let
1\leq p<Q
. Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu

p
-Poincaré inequality. Then for every
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
, there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
and a
p
-thin set

such that

\lim_{E\not\ni x\to+\infty}u(x)=c.

Furthermore, there is only one such
c\in\mathbb{R}
for which the above holds.

To prove this theorem, we use the result from
[
KN23
]
which states that

has a limit at infinity along a family of infinite curves with positive
p
-modulus. By using this family of curves, we can construct the stated
p
-thin set. However, when
p\geq Q
, the

-modulus of
\Gamma^{+\infty}
is zero, see for instance
[
HKST15
, Chapter 5]
, and so we cannot use this line of reasoning to guarantee the existence of limits of functions in
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
along infinite curves in this case. In fact, we show in Example
6.1
that there are functions in
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
, for both
p=Q
and
p>Q
, which fail to have a limit at infinity along any curve
\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}
.
For this reason, we consider the following question for the case
p\geq Q
: if a function in
\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
has a limit along some infinite curve, must it have limits along many such curves, and are these limits unique?

When
p>Q
, we see that uniqueness is not guaranteed, even when limits exist along a large collection of infinite curves. Indeed, in Example
6.4
, we construct a function in
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p>n
which has a limit at infinity along every radial curve, but these limits do not coincide. For this reason, the critical case
p=Q
is one of the primary focuses of this paper. It turns out that when
p=Q
, the limits at infinity of Sobolev functions are more well-behaved: in Lemma
3.2
, we show that if a function
u\in\dot{N}^{1,Q}(X)
has a limit along some infinite curve, then it obtains the same limit at infinity everywhere outside of a
Q
-thin set.
Furthermore, we show that a function in
\dot{N}^{1,Q}(X)
cannot have more than one such limit at infinity, in contrast to the case when
p>Q
.

A natural question is whether the existence of limits at infinity outside of a
Q
-thin set implies the existence of limits along infinite curves. To answer this question, we introduce the notion of a
thick
set, defined in Section
2.7
. Roughly speaking, thick sets have Hausdorff content uniformly bounded away from zero in sufficiently large dyadic annuli. One of the main ideas of this paper, shown in Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.6
, is that if
u\in\dot{N}^{1,Q}(X)
has a limit at infinity along some thick set, then there exists a large family of infinite curves along which
u
attains the same limit. We recall that any family of infinite curves in this setting will have
Q
-modulus zero, and so
Q
-modulus gives us no information on the size of this family. However, this family of curves has large
Q
-modulus in each of the considered annuli. As such, we show that inside any sufficiently large ball, the curves in this family have length proportional to the radius of the ball, and the union of their trajectories inside the ball has measure proportional to the measure of the ball. That is to say, this family of curves fills up a significant portion of each large ball, but each curve is not too long. In Lemma
3.3
, we show that if
E\subset X
is a
Q
-thin set, then
X\setminus E
is a thick set. This completes the picture for the following main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty

(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, unbounded metric measure space with metric

and Ahlfors

There exists an infinite curve

\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(\gamma(t))=c
.

There is a thin set
E

\lim_{E\not\ni x\to+\infty}u(x)=c

III.

There is a thick set

\lim_{F\ni x\to+\infty}u(x)=c

There is a family

\mu(B(O,R)\cap|\Gamma|)\simeq\mu(A_{R}\cap|\Gamma|)\simeq\mu(B(O,R))

Is there a bound on the rate at which

. A curve
\gamma
is said to be a
rectifiable curve
if its length is finite. Similarly,
\gamma
is a
locally rectifiable curve
if its restriction to each compact subinterval of
I
is rectifiable. Each rectifiable curve
\gamma
will be parameterized by arc length and hence the
line integral
over
\gamma
of a Borel function
f
on
X
is

\int_{\gamma}fds=\int_{0}^{\ell(\gamma)}f(\gamma(t))dt.

If
\gamma
is locally rectifiable, then we set

\int_{\gamma}fds=\sup\int_{\gamma^{\prime}}fds

where the supremum is taken over all rectifiable subcurves
\gamma^{\prime}
of
\gamma
. Let
\gamma:[0,{+\infty})\to X
be a locally rectifiable curve, parameterized by arc length. Then

\int_{\gamma}fds=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(\gamma(t))dt.

A locally rectifiable curve
\gamma
is an
infinite curve
if
\gamma\setminus B\neq\emptyset
for all balls
B
. Then
\int_{\gamma}ds={+\infty}.

We denote by
\Gamma^{+\infty}
the collection of all infinite curves.

2.2.
Modulus and capacities

A triple
(X,d,\mu)
is called a metric measure space if
(X,d)
is a separable metric space and
\mu
is a nontrivial locally finite Borel-regular measure on
X
. For every set
A
, we set

\mu(A):=\inf\{\mu(U):A\subset U,U\ \text{\rm is a $\mu$-measurable subset of $X$}\}.

By
[
HKST15
, Proposition 3.3.37]
, the above
\mu
-measurable subset
U
can be replaced by an open subset.

Let
\Gamma
be a family of curves in a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
. Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. The
p
-modulus
of
\Gamma
, denoted
\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma)
, is defined by

\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma):=\inf\int_{X}\rho^{p}d\mu

where the infimum is taken over all Borel functions
\rho:X\to[0,{+\infty}]
satisfying

\int_{\gamma}\rho ds\geq 1
for every locally rectifiable curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
. A family of curves is called
p
-exceptional
if it has
p
-modulus zero. We say that a property holds for
p
-a.e curve
(or
p
-almost every curve) if the collection of curves for which the property fails is
p
-exceptional.

Let
E,F
be two subsets of a set
\Omega
. We define

(2.1)

\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(E,F,\Omega):=\inf\int_{\Omega}\rho^{p}d\mu

where the infimum is taken over all Borel functions
\rho:\Omega\to[0,+\infty]
satisfying
\int_{\gamma}\rho ds\geq 1
for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
in
\Omega
connecting
E
and
F
. If
\Omega=X
, we set
\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(E,F):=\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(E,F,X)
.

Let
u
be a locally integrable function on
X
. A Borel function
\rho:X\to[0,{+\infty}]
is said to be an
upper gradient
of
u
if

(2.2)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds

for every rectifiable curve
\gamma
connecting
x
and
y
. Then we have that (
2.2
) holds for all compact subcurves of
\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}
. For
1\leq p<+\infty
, we say that
\rho
is a
p
-weak upper gradient
of
u
if (
2.2
) holds for
p
-a.e rectifiable curve. In what follows, we denote by
\rho_{u}
the
minimal
p
-weak
upper gradient of
u
, which is unique up to sets of measure zero and which is minimal in the sense that
\rho_{u}\leq\rho
a.e. for every
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient
\rho
of
u
. In
[
H03
]
, the existence and uniqueness of such minimal
p
-weak upper gradient are given.
The notion of upper gradients is due to Heinonen and Koskela
[
HK98
]
, and we refer interested readers to
[
BB11
,
H03
,
HK98
,
N00
]
for a more detailed discussion on upper gradients.

Let
\Omega\subseteq X
. We denote
\dot{N}^{1,p}(\Omega):=\dot{N}^{1,p}(\Omega,d,\mu)
, with
1\leq p<+\infty
, the collection of all locally integrable functions for which an upper gradient is
p
-integrable on
(\Omega,d,\mu)
. We set
N^{1,p}(\Omega):=\dot{N}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}_{\mu}(\Omega)
where
1\leq p<+\infty
and
L^{p}_{\mu}(\Omega)
consists of all
p
-integrable functions on
(\Omega,\mu)
.

Let
K\subset X
be a subset. The
Sobolev
p
-capacity
of
K
, denoted
C_{p}(K)
is defined by

(2.3)

C_{p}(K):=\inf\left(\int_{X}|u|^{p}d\mu+\int_{X}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu\right),

where the infimum is taken over all functions
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
with the
p
-integrable minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
\rho_{u}
such that
u|_{K}\equiv 1
.

Given two subsets
E,F
of a set
\Omega
, we set

(2.4)

\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(E,F,\Omega):=\inf\int_{\Omega}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu

where the infimum is taken over all functions
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with the
p
-integrable minimal
p
-weak upper gradient
\rho_{u}
such that
u|_{E}\equiv 1
and
u|_{F}\equiv 0
. If
\Omega=X,
we set
\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(E,F):=\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(E,F,\Omega)
.

By
[
HKST15
, Lemma 6.2.2]
, one can replace the minimal
p
-weak upper gradients
\rho_{u}
in (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) with upper gradients of the function
u
.

We have from
[
HK98
, Proposition 2.17]
that for two subsets
E,F
of a set
\Omega
,

(2.5)

\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(E,F,\Omega)=\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(E,F,\Omega).

2.3.
Ahlfors regular measures and Poincaré inequalities

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A Borel regular measure
\mu
is called
doubling
if every ball in
X
has finite positive measure and if there exists a finite constant
C\geq 1
such that for all balls
B(x,r)
with radius
r>0
and center at
x\in X
,

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r))<+\infty.

Let
1<Q<{+\infty}
. A Borel regular measure
\mu
is said to be
Ahlfors
Q
-regular
if there exists a finite constant
C_{Q}\geq 1
such that for all balls
B(x,r)
with radius
r>0
and center at
x\in X
,

\frac{r^{Q}}{C_{Q}}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{Q}r^{Q}.

Hence if
\mu
is Ahlfors
Q
-regular where
1<Q<{+\infty}
, then
\mu
is a doubling measure. Here
C_{Q}
is said to be the
Ahlfors
Q
-regularity constant
of
\mu
.

Let
1\leq p<{+\infty}
. We say that a measure
\mu
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality
if every ball in
X
has finite positive measure and if there exist finite constants
C>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|u-u_{B(x,r)}|d\mu\leq Cr\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}\rho^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}

for all balls
B(x,r)
with radius
r>0
and center at
x\in X
, and for all pairs
(u,\rho)
satisfying (
2.2
) such that
u
is integrable on balls. Here
\lambda
is called the
scaling constant
or
scaling factor
of the
p
-Poincaré inequality.

We will use the following Morrey embedding theorem, see
[
HKST15
, Theorem 9.2.14]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
and let
Q<p<+\infty
. Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, unbounded, Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric measure space supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality. Then there exists a finite constant
C\geq 1
such that given a ball
B\subset X
and a function
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
, we have that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\text{\rm rad}(B)^{Q/p}d(x,y)^{1-Q/p}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{4\lambda B}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}

for all
x,y\in B
. Here
\lambda
is the scaling factor of the Poincaré inequality, and
\text{\rm rad}(B)
is the radius of
B
.

For more on Poincaré inequalities, we refer the interested readers to
[
HK00
,
HK95
]
.

We will use the following
p
-modulus and
p
-capacity estimates for annular regions. For proof, see for instance
[
HK98
, Proposition 2.17 and Lemma 3.14]
and
[
HKST15
, Theorem 9.3.6]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
and let
1\leq p<+\infty
.
Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is an unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu
supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality.
We assume that
X
is complete.
Then for
0<2r<R<+\infty
, we have that

\text{\rm Mod}_{p}(B(O,r),X\setminus B(O,R))\approx\text{\rm Cap}_{p}(B(O,r),X\setminus B(O,R))\simeq\begin{cases}r^{Q-p},&p<Q;\\
\left(\log\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)\right)^{1-Q},&p=Q;\\
R^{Q-p},&p>Q;\end{cases}

for a given
O\in X
.

2.4.
Chain conditions

In this paper, we employ the following annular chain property which is given in
[
KN23
, Section 2.5]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\geq 1
. We say that
X
satisfies an annular
\lambda
-chain condition at
O
if the following holds. There are finite constants
c_{1}\geq 1,c_{2}\geq 1,\delta>0
and a finite number
M<{+\infty}
so that given
r>0
and points
x,y\in B(O,r)\setminus B(O,r/2)
, one can find balls
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{k}
with the following properties:

1.

k\leq M
.

2.

B_{1}=B(x,r/(\lambda c_{1}))
,
B_{k}=B(y,r/(\lambda c_{1}))
and the radius of each
B_{i}
is
r/(\lambda c_{1})
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

3.

B_{i}\subset B(O,c_{2}r)\setminus B(O,r/c_{2})
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

4.

For each
1\leq i\leq k-1
, there is a ball
D_{i}\subset B_{i}\cap B_{i+1}
with radius
\delta r
.

If
X
satisfies an annular
\lambda
-chain condition at
O
for every
\lambda\geq 1
, we say that
X
has the annular chain property.

By
[
KN23
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have the following sufficient criterion guaranteeing the annular chain property holds:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mu
be doubling on an unbounded metric space
(X,d)
.
Suppose that there is a finite constant
c_{0}\geq 1
so that for every
r>0
, each pair
x,y
of points in
B(O,r)\setminus B(O,r/2)
can be joined by a curve in
B(O,c_{0}r)\setminus B(O,r/c_{0})
. Then
X
has the annular chain property.

By
[
K07
, Theorem 3.3]
, a complete, Ahlfors
Q
-regular metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality with
1\leq p\leq Q
satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
2.4
, and so such a metric measure space has the annular chain property. Furthermore, from the proof of
[
KN23
, Lemma 2.3]
, we see that in such spaces, only the constants
M
and
\delta
in Definition
2.3
depend on
\lambda
, whereas the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are independent of
\lambda
. In such spaces, we also have the following modification of the annular chain property:

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
X
is complete and
\mu
is an Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality on an unbounded metric space
(X,d)
where
1<Q<+\infty
and
1\leq p\leq Q
. Then
there are finite constants
\overline{c_{1}}\geq 1,\overline{c_{2}}\geq 1,\delta>0
and a finite number
2\leq\overline{M}<{+\infty}
so that given
+\infty>r_{2}\geq 2r_{1}>0
and two balls
B
and
B^{\prime}
with radius
\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}
in
B(O,\overline{c_{1}}r_{2})\setminus B(O,r_{1}/\overline{c_{1}})
, one can find balls
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{k}
with the following properties:

1.

k\leq\overline{M}
.

2.

B_{1}=B
,
B_{k}=B^{\prime}
and the radius of each
B_{i}
is comparable to
r_{2}/r_{1}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

3.

B_{i}\subset B(O,\overline{c_{2}}r_{2})\setminus B(O,r_{1}/\overline{c_{2}})
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

4.

For each
1\leq i\leq k-1
, there is a ball
D_{i}\subset B_{i}\cap B_{i+1}
with radius
\delta\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}
.

Proof.

Since
X
is complete and
\mu
is an Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure supporting a
p
-Poincaré inequality where
1<Q<+\infty
and
1\leq p\leq Q
, we have that the assumption of Proposition
2.4
holds, see for instance in
[
K07
, Theorem 3.3]
. In particular, there is a finite constant
c_{0}\geq 1
so that for every
+\infty>r_{2}\geq 2r_{1}>0
, each pair
x,y
of points in
B(O,r_{2})\setminus B(O,r_{1})
can be joined by a curve in
B(O,c_{0}r_{2})\setminus B(O,r_{1}/c_{0})
. Hence the claim follows by an analog of the proof of Proposition
2.4
(see
[
KN23
, Lemma 2.3]
).
∎

2.5.
Loewner spaces

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space. For
1<Q<+\infty
, we define the
Loewner function

\phi_{X,Q}:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
of
X
by

\phi_{X,Q}(t)=\inf\{\text{\rm Mod}_{Q}(E,F,X):\Delta(E,F)\leq t\}

where
E
and
F
are disjoint non-degenerate continua in
X
with

\Delta(E,F)=\frac{\text{\rm dist}(E,F)}{\min\{\text{\rm diam}E,{\rm diam}F\}}

designating their relative distance in
X
. Here
{\rm dist}(E,F):=\inf\{d(x,y):x\in E,y\in F\}
and
{\rm diam}E:=\sup\{d(x,y):x,y\in E\}
. If one can not find two disjoint continua in
X
, it is understood that
\phi_{X,Q}(t)\equiv 0
. By the definition, the function
\phi_{X,Q}
is decreasing.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
, a pathwise connected metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
is said to be a
Loewner space
of exponent
Q
or a
Q
-Loewner space
, if the Loewner function
\phi_{X,Q}(t)
is positive for all
t>0
.

In fact, we have the following theorems.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
.
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu
supporting a
Q
-Poincaré inequality.
Then
X
is a
Q
-Loewner space.

Proof.

By Theorem 9.6 in
[
Hei01
]
, we have that if
(X,d,\mu)
is quasiconvex, proper, Ahlfors
Q
-regular and supports a
Q
-Poincaré inequality then
X
is a
Q
-Loewner space. Since
X
is complete and Ahlfors
Q
-regular supports a
Q
-Poincaré inequality, we obtain that
X
is proper (see for instance
[
HKST15
, Lemma 4.1.14]
) and quasiconvex (see for instance
[
HKST15
, Theorem 8.3.2]
). The proof completes.
∎

Theorem 2.7

(Section 3 in
[
HK98
]
)
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
.
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a complete, unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu
supporting a
Q
-Poincaré inequality.
Let
0<r<+\infty
, and let
E,F
be disjoint non-degenerate continua which are subsets of
B(O,Cr)\setminus B(O,r/C)
, for a given
+\infty>C>1
, such that
\min\{\text{\rm diam}E,\text{\rm diam}F\}\geq r/8
. Then there is a finite constant
\delta>0
independent of
r
so that

{\rm Mod}_{Q}(E,F,B(O,Cr)\setminus B(O,r/C))\geq\delta.

2.6.
Polar coordinate systems

The following weak polar coordinate system was introduced in
[
KN23
]
.

Let
\mathbb{S}
be an abstract set of indices with a Radon probability measure
\sigma
and a metric
d_{\mathbb{S}}
.
Given a point
\mathcal{O}
, we consider collections
\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S})
of infinite curves with parameter set
\mathbb{S}
starting from
\mathcal{O}
, namely

\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S})=\{\gamma^{\mathcal{O}}_{\xi}\in\Gamma^{+\infty}:\gamma_{\xi}^{\mathcal{O}}(0)=\mathcal{O},\xi\in\mathbb{S}\}.

We say that
X:=(X,d,\mu)
has a
weak polar coordinate system
at the
coordinate point

\mathcal{O}
if there is a choice of a pair
(\mathbb{S},\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}))
with a Radon probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{S}
, a
coordinate weight

h:X\to\mathbb{[}0,{+\infty})
, and a finite constant
\mathcal{C}>0
such that

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{S}}\int_{\gamma^{\mathcal{O}}_{\xi}}|f|\ h\ dsd\sigma\leq\mathcal{C}\int_{X}|f|d\mu\text{\rm\ \ for every integrable function $f$ on $(X,d,\mu)$.}

Each infinite curve
\gamma_{\xi}^{\mathcal{O}}\in\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S})
is called a
radial curve
with respect to
\xi\in\mathbb{S}
(starting from
\mathcal{O}
). We refer the interested readers to
[
KN23
, Section 2.1]
for more discussion on general and concrete polar coordinates.

By a slight abuse of notation, we will also denote by
\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S})
the subset of
X
which is the union of all radial curves.

A collection of infinite curves is said to be
pairwise disjoint (at infinity)
if there is a ball
B
such that for any two distinct curves
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
in the collection,
(\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime})\setminus B=\emptyset
. Given a weak polar coordinate system
(\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}),\sigma,d_{\mathbb{S}}):=(\mathbb{S},\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}),h,\sigma,d_{\mathbb{S}})
at
\mathcal{O}
,
a subfamily
\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
of pairwise disjoint infinite curves with
\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})>0
, where
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{S}
, is said to admit a
dyadic Lipschitz projection (at infinity)
if there are finite constants
C>0,r_{0}>0
such that there is a mapping
p:(\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}),d)\to(\mathbb{S}^{\prime},d_{\mathbb{S}})
, from
x\in\gamma_{\xi}^{\mathcal{O}}\in\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
to the corresponding parameter
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
, being
C/r
-Lipschitz on each
\Gamma^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})\cap B(\mathcal{O},r)\setminus B(\mathcal{O},r/2)
with
r>r_{0}
.
For example, this hold for the Muckenhoupt-weighted space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
1<n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.7.
Hausdorff measures, thin sets, and thick sets

Let
0\leq\beta<{+\infty}
and
0<R\leq{+\infty}
.
Let
(X,d)
be an arbitrary metric space. The
(\beta,R)
-Hausdorff content
of a subset
E
, denoted
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R,d}(E)
, is given by

\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R,d}(E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}r_{k}^{\beta}:E\subset\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B_{k}\text{\rm\ \ and \ \ }0<r_{k}<R\right\}

where
B_{k}
is a ball with radius
r_{k}
. The
\beta
-Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\beta}
of a subset
E
is

\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\beta}(E):=\lim_{R\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R,d}(E).

For brevity, we will omit the metric
d
when referring to a given metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, and denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R}:=\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R,d}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}:=\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\beta}
. In this paper,
to emphasize the“spherical” metric space
(\mathbb{S},d_{\mathbb{S}})
defined in Section
2.6
, we denote the Hausdorff contents and Hausdorff measures defined with respect to this space as
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R,d_{\mathbb{S}}}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{d_{\mathbb{S}}}
, respectively.

If
\mu
is Ahlfors
Q
-regular,
1<Q<+\infty
, then for arbitrary set
A\subset X
,
\mu(A)\simeq\mathcal{H}^{Q}(A)
.
When the measure
\mu
is only assumed to be doubling, and not necessarily Ahlfors
Q
-regular, it is more natural to define the
codimension
t
Hausdorff content
of a subset
E
, for
0<t<+\infty
and
0<R\leq+\infty
, as follows:

\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{R}(E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu(B_{k})}{r_{k}^{t}}:E\subset\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<r_{k}<R\right\}.

The
codimension
t
Hausdorff measure
of a subset
E
is then defined by

\mathcal{H}^{-t}(E):=\lim_{R\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{R}(E).

If
\mu
is Ahflors
Q
-regular, then
\mathcal{H}^{-t}_{R}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{Q-t}_{R}
and
\mathcal{H}^{-t}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{Q-t}
for
0<t\leq Q
.

We have two following results, the first of which can be found in
[
HKM06
, Lemma 2.25]
. The second is obtained by connectedness of infinite curves, see
[
Fe69
, Corollary 2.10.12]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
0<\beta<{+\infty}
,
0<R\leq{+\infty}
. Then for
E\subset X,

\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_{R}(E)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{\beta}(E)=0
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
+\infty>R>0
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+\infty}(\gamma\bigcap B(O,2R)\setminus B(O,R))\geq R/2
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}
such that
\gamma\bigcap B(O,R)\neq\varnothing
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
and let
1\leq p\leq Q
. Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is Ahlfors
Q
-regular, and for

Proof.

\frac{\mu(G)}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}\leq\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}\simeq\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}r_{k}^{Q}}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r_{k}^{p-\alpha}}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p-\alpha}}r_{k}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}.

If

for all

then

\frac{\mu(G)}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}r_{k}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}\text{\rm\ \ for all }0<\alpha<p.

If there is an index

such that

, then the Ahlfors
Q
-regular properties give that

\frac{\mu(G)}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}\leq\frac{\mu(A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}})}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{p}}\simeq\frac{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{Q-(p-\alpha)}}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{r_{k_{0}}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{(\lambda 2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}r_{k}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}\text{\rm\ \ for all }0<\alpha<p.

In both cases, we obtain the desired inequality, since

in the previous display. We have that the
b_{k}
are strictly positive, non-increasing, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}b_{k}=0
. If
k<n_{1}
, we define
b_{k}=1
. Finally,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}a_{k}b_{k}^{-p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}a_{k}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=n_{i}}^{n_{i+1}}ia_{k}<\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}a_{k}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}<+\infty.\qed

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
, and let
\rho_{u}
be a
p
-integrable upper gradient of
u
. Since
(X,d,\mu)
is complete, Ahlfors
Q
-regular, and satisfies a
p
-Poincaré inequality with
p<Q
, it follows from
[
K07

that the annular chain property holds. Therefore, we have from
[
KN23
]
, that there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(\gamma(t))=c\quad\text{for $p$-a.e.\ }\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
denote the family of curves in
\Gamma^{+\infty}
for which this holds. As

, it follows that
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma^{+\infty})>0
, see
[
KN23
, Theorem 1.2]
for example, and so there exists a finite constant
C_{1}>0
such that

(3.2)

{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{1})\geq C_{1}>0.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\Gamma_{j,k}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{1}:|u(x)-c|<2^{-k}\text{ for all }x\in\gamma\setminus B(O,\lambda 2^{j})\}.

We then have that
\Gamma_{1}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{j,k}
, and so by subadditivity of the
p
-modulus,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-j}C_{1}=C_{1}\leq{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{1})\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{j,k}).

Hence there exists
j_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{j_{k},k})\geq 2^{-j_{k}}C_{1}>0.

Then for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{j_{k},k}
,

|u(x)-c|<2^{-k}{\rm\ \ for\ all\ }x\in\gamma\setminus B(O,\lambda 2^{j})\ {\rm and\ for\ }j\geq j_{k}.

Since
\rho_{u}\in L^{p}_{\mu}(X)
, we also choose
i_{k}\geq j_{k}
sufficiently large so that

(3.4)

2^{j_{k}}2^{kp}\int_{X\setminus B(O,2^{i_{k}}/c_{2})}\rho_{u}^{p}\,d\mu<2^{-k}.

With sequences
\{i_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
now fixed, we define a sequence
\{a_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
by setting
a_{l}=2^{-k}
for
i_{k}\leq l<i_{k+1}
.
Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

(3.5)

|u(x)-c|<a_{l}\text{\rm\ \ for all $x\in\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}$, $i_{k}\leq l<i_{k+1},\gamma\in\Gamma_{j_{k},k}$}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{k}\leq l<i_{k+1}
, we define

\text{$F_{l}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{j_{k},k}}(\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}})$ \ and\ }E_{l}:=\{x\in A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}:|u(x)-c|>2a_{l}\}.

We consider three cases. Firstly, suppose that there exist
x\in E_{l}
and
y\in F_{l}\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}
such that
|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|\leq a_{l}/5
and
|u(y)-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|\leq a_{l}/5
. Then, by Lemma
2.19

(3.6)

1\lesssim a_{l}^{-p}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}\,d\mu=2^{kp}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}\,d\mu.

For the second case, suppose that
|u(y)-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|\geq a_{l}/5
for all

. Let

. Then by Lemma
2.10
and Lemma
2.20
, it follows that

\frac{\mu(F_{l})}{(2^{l+1})^{p}}\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{H}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}_{+\infty}(F_{l})}{(\lambda 2^{l+1})^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}})^{p}}2^{kp}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu.

Since we are considering
i_{k+1}\geq l\geq i_{k}\geq j_{k}
, we have that
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{j_{k},k})\leq\mu(F_{l})/(\lambda 2^{l+1})^{p}
because for all measurable subsets
A\supset F_{l}
,
\chi_{A}/(\lambda 2^{l+1})
is admissible for computing
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{j_{k},k})
. Hence, from (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.7)

C_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}})^{p}}2^{j_{k}}2^{kp}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu.

For the third case, suppose that
|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|\geq a_{l}/5
for all

. Then by Lemma
2.20
, we have that

(3.8)

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}_{+\infty}(E_{l})}{(\lambda 2^{l+1})^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}})^{p}}2^{kp}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu.

By (
3.4
), we have that

(3.9)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}2^{j_{k}}2^{kp}\int_{X\setminus B(O,2^{i_{k}}/c_{2})}\rho_{u}^{p}\,d\mu<+\infty.

Therefore, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, sufficiently large, so that the two estimates (

) and (

) fail for all

and

. Thus, for all

and

) holds.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}_{+\infty}(E\cap A_{\lambda 2^{n+1}})}{(\lambda 2^{n+1})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}})^{p}}\sum_{k=k_{m}}^{+\infty}\sum_{l=i_{k}}^{i_{k+1}-1}2^{kp}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{p}d\mu

\lim_{m\to+\infty}\sum_{n=m}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{Q-(p-\alpha)}_{+\infty}(E\cap A_{\lambda 2^{n+1}})}{(\lambda 2^{n+1})^{\alpha}}=0{\rm\ \ for\ all\ 0<\alpha<p},

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
.
Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete, unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu
supporting a
Q
-Poincaré inequality.
Let

and suppose that there is an infinite curve
\gamma\in\Gamma^{+\infty}
so that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}u(\gamma(t))=c_{0}\in[-\infty,+\infty]

such that

(3.10)

\lim_{E\not\ni x\to{+\infty}}u(x)=c_{0}.

Proof.

We recall that
+\infty>\lambda\geq 1
is
the scaling factor of the
Q
-Poincaré inequality, and that
\rho_{u}
is a
Q
-integrable upper gradient of
u
. We also recall that we use the notation

c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}}:=B(O,c_{2}\lambda 2^{j+3})\setminus B(O,2^{j}/(c_{2}\lambda))
for

, where
c_{2}
is as in Definition
2.3
. Here the annular chain property is given since
X
is complete, see Section
2.4
.

We first prove (
3.10
) for the case of
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since
u\in\dot{N}^{1,Q}(X)
, there exists by Lemma
3.1
, a non-increasing sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{j}>0
such that

(3.11)

\lim_{j\to{+\infty}}a_{j}=0,\ \sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}a_{j}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu<{+\infty},\text{\rm\ \ and\ \ }|u-c_{0}|<a_{j}/2\text{\rm\ \ on \ }{\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}}}\ {\rm for\ }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
E_{l}:=\{x\in A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}:|u(x)-c_{0}|>a_{l}\}
for

. We set
E:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{+\infty}E_{l}
. It is clear that
\lim_{x\to{+\infty},x\notin E}u(x)=c_{0}
. We now only need to prove that

is a thin set. Since
a_{l}/2\leq|u(x)-c_{0}|-|u(y)-c_{0}|\leq|u(x)-u(y)|

and

3.11
), we have that

(3.12)

a_{l}/2\leq|u(x)-u(y)|\leq|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|+|u_{B(x,2^{l})}-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|+|u_{B(y,2^{l})}-u(y)|

for all
x\in E_{l}
and
y\in\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}
.
Using Lemma
2.19
and Lemma
2.20
, we consider the following cases:

If there exist
x\in E_{l}
and
y\in\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}
such that
|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|\leq\frac{a_{l}}{10}
and
|u(y)-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|\leq\frac{a_{l}}{10}
, then by estimate (
2.11
) from Lemma
2.19
, we have that

(3.13)

1\lesssim a_{l}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu.

Let
0<\alpha<Q
.
If
|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|>\frac{a_{l}}{10}
for all
x\in E_{l}
, then estimate (
2.12
) from Lemma
2.20
gives

(3.14)

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(E_{l})}{(2^{l})^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{Q}})^{Q}}a_{l}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu.

Likewise, if
|u(y)-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|>\frac{a_{l}}{10}
for all
y\in\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}
, then the same estimate from Lemma
2.20
, with

, gives

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+\infty}(\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}})}{2^{l}}\lesssim a_{l}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu.

By Lemma
2.9
,
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+\infty}(\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}})\gtrsim 2^{l}
for all sufficiently large

, and so in this case, we also have that

(3.15)

1\lesssim a_{l}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu

for all sufficiently large
l
.

By (
3.11
), there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that (

) does not hold for any

, and so for all

. From (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we then have that (
3.14
) holds true for all

.
Summing (
3.14
) over

(3.16)

\lim_{k\to{+\infty}}\sum_{l=k}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{{+\infty}}(E_{l})}{(2^{l})^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-2^{-\frac{\alpha}{Q}})^{Q}}\lim_{k\to{+\infty}}\sum_{l=k}^{+\infty}a_{l}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu=0,

and so

Now suppose that

. We use similar arguments as above. By Lemma
3.1
and since
u\in\dot{N}^{1,p}(X)
, there exists a non-increasing sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{j}>0
such that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}a_{j}=0,\ \sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}a_{j}^{-Q}\int_{c_{2}\cdot A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}}}\rho_{u}^{Q}d\mu<+\infty,\text{\rm\ \ and \ \ $u-\frac{1}{a_{j}}>a_{j}/2$ on $\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{j+1}}$ for $j\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Letting
E_{l}:=\{x\in A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}:u(x)-\frac{1}{a_{l}}<\frac{a_{l}}{4}\}
for

, we again set

. It is clear that

. Moreover,
0<a_{l}/4\leq(u(y)-\frac{1}{a_{l}})-(u(x)-\frac{1}{a_{l}})=u(y)-u(x)\leq|u(y)-u(x)|
for all
x\in E_{l}
and

. Hence

a_{l}/4\leq|u(x)-u(y)|\leq|u(x)-u_{B(x,2^{l})}|+|u_{B(x,2^{l})}-u_{B(y,2^{l})}|+|u_{B(y,2^{l})}-u(y)|

for all

and
y\in\gamma\cap A_{\lambda 2^{l+1}}
. Repeating the above arguments from (

) to (

), we obtain that

is a thin set.
Similarly, it is easy to check that the claim (
3.10
) holds when
c_{0}=-\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<Q<+\infty
.
Suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is an unbounded metric measure space with metric
d
and Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure
\mu
. Let
E
be a thin set. Then

is a thick set.

Proof.

Let
E
be a
Q
-thin set. Let
0<\alpha<Q
. Then there is a finite constant

such that

(3.17)

\lim_{k\to{+\infty}}\sum_{j=k}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(E\cap A_{C2^{j+1}})}{(C2^{j+1})^{\alpha}}=0.

By Lemma
2.11
, there is a constant

such that

(3.18)

\mathcal{H}_{+\infty}^{\alpha}(A_{C2^{j+1}})\geq C_{1}(2^{j+1})^{\alpha}\text{\rm\ \ for all $j\in\mathbb{N}$},

), there is an index

), we obtain from subadditivity of

\frac{3}{5}\leq|u_{B(x,2^{j})}-u_{B(y,2^{j})}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{{\overline{M}}-1}|u_{B_{i}+1}-u_{B_{i}}|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{\overline{M}}\left(\int_{\lambda\cdot B_{i}}g^{Q}d\mu\right)^{1/Q}

1&\text{\rm\ \ if \ \ $x\in B(O,s_{j})\setminus B(O,s_{j}/2)$},\\
1-\min\left\{1,\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{O,x}}\int_{\gamma}g_{r_{j+1},s_{j}}ds\right\}&\text{\rm\ \ if \ \ $x\in B(O,r_{j+1})\setminus B(O,s_{j})$},\end{cases}

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
g
is an upper gradient of
u
and hence
u\in\dot{N}^{1,Q}(X)
. Moreover,
u(x)=0
for all
x
such that
d(O,x)=r_{j}
.

For each
10\leq j\in\mathbb{N}
, we pick
x_{j}\in X
so that
B(x_{j},2^{-j})\subset B(O,2^{j+1})\setminus B(O,2^{j}))
, and so that

(6.6)

B(x_{j},2^{-j})\cap\left(\bigcup_{i}\{x:|x|=r_{i}\}\right)=\varnothing.

Since our space is connected by the Poincaré inequality, then we can find such an
x_{j}
by the definition of the
r_{i}
. For each
j\geq 10
, define

B_{j}:=B(x_{j},2^{-j^{\frac{2}{Q-1}}}\times 2^{-j}),

and let
F:=\bigcup_{j=10}^{+\infty}B_{j}
. By Lemma
2.10
and Ahlfors
Q
-regularity, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(F\cap B(O,2^{j+1})\setminus B(O,2^{j}))}{(2^{j})^{\alpha}}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(B_{j})}{(2^{j})^{\alpha}}\gtrsim\frac{\mu(B_{j})}{(2^{j})^{Q}}\simeq\frac{(2^{-j^{\frac{2}{Q-1}}}\times 2^{-j})^{Q}}{(2^{j})^{Q}}=:\delta_{j}\to 0

as
j\to+\infty
for all
0<\alpha<Q
. Thus,
F
is almost thick as in Definition
2.15
. However,
F
is not thick since

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(F\cap B(O,2^{j+1})\setminus B(O,2^{j}))}{(2^{j})^{\alpha}}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{+\infty}(B_{j})}{(2^{j})^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{(2^{-j^{\frac{2}{Q-1}}}\times 2^{-j})^{\alpha}}{(2^{j})^{\alpha}}\to 0

as
j\to+\infty
for all
0<\alpha<Q
.

For each
j\geq 10
, we use Theorem
2.2
to choose a function
0\leq\psi_{j}\leq 1
which is admissible for computing
\text{\rm Cap}_{Q}(B_{j},X\setminus B(x_{j},2^{-j}))
such that

\int_{X}g_{\psi_{j}}^{Q}d\mu\lesssim\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{2^{-j^{\frac{2}{Q-1}}}}\right)\right)^{1-Q}\approx j^{-2},

where
g_{\psi_{j}}
is the minimal
Q
-weak upper gradient of
\psi_{j}
. By this estimate and the fact that each
\psi_{j}
is supported in
B(x_{j},2^{-j})
, it follows that
\sum_{j=10}^{+\infty}\psi_{j}\in N^{1,Q}(X)
.

Set
f:=\min\{1,u+\sum_{j=10}^{+\infty}\psi_{j}\}
. Since
\psi_{j}\equiv 1
on
B_{j}
, it follows that
f|_{F}\equiv 1
, and by (
6.6
), we have that
f|_{\{x:|x|=r_{i}\}}\equiv 0
for all
i
. Therefore (
6.4
) follows, completing the proof.
∎

Example 6.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be so that
n\geq 2
. We denote
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1))
the classical homogeneous
p
-Sobolev space where
n<p<+\infty
and
B(O,1)
the ball with radius
1
and center at the origin
O
. Then
there exists a continuous function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1))
such that its limit at infinity along all radial curves exists but is not unique.

Proof.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1)
. We define

u(x)=\frac{x_{n}}{|x|}\text{\rm\ \ for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1)$}

where
|x|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{2}_{i}
.
Since
||x|^{2}-x_{n}^{2}|\leq|x|^{2}
and
|x_{n}x_{i}|\lesssim|x|^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we obtain that for
p>n
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1)}|\nabla u|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1)}\left({\left|\frac{|x|^{2}-x_{n}^{2}}{|x|^{{3}}}\right|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left|\frac{x_{n}x_{i}}{|x|^{{3}}}\right|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(O,1)}\frac{1}{|x|^{p}}dx<{+\infty}.

Let
x=(r,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})
in the spherical coordinate. That is,

\begin{cases}x_{1}=r\cos(\xi_{1}),\\
x_{i}=r\sin(\xi_{1})\sin(\xi_{2})\ldots\sin(\xi_{i-2})\cos(\xi_{i-1}),\rm where\ 2\leq i\leq n-1,\\
x_{n}=r\sin(\xi_{1})\sin(\xi_{2})\ldots\sin(\xi_{n-2})\sin(\xi_{n-1}),\end{cases}

where
\xi_{n-1}\in[0,2\pi)
,
\xi_{i}\in[0,\pi]
for
1\leq i\leq n-2
.
Hence for
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\ldots,\xi_{n-1})
in the unit sphere,

\lim_{r\to{+\infty}}u(r\xi)=\sin(\xi_{1})\sin(\xi_{2})\ldots\sin(\xi_{n-2})\sin(\xi_{n-1}).

∎

Non-Abelian fractional quantum Hall states at filling factor 3 / 4 3/4
Kai-Wen Huang, Ying-Hai Wu

Abstract
Fractional quantum Hall states have been observed at filling factor ν = 3 / 4 \nu=3/4 in three platforms. General theoretical analysis of topological orders at ν = 3 / 4 \nu=3/4 revealed that four types of non-Abelian states with Ising anyons have ground state degeneracy 12 12 on the torus. The properties of ν = 3 / 4 \nu=3/4 states can be analyzed using two complementary approaches. In the first one, they are treated as particle-hole conjugate of ν = 1 / 4 \nu=1/4 Moore-Read types states. In the second one, they are mapped to composite fermions with reverse flux attachment at effective filling factor 3 / 2 3/2 , whose integral part realizes an integer quantum Hall state and the fractional part realizes ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 Moore-Read type states. For the specific case of bilayer graphene, numerical calculations demonstrate that strong Landau level mixing could generate a gapped state at ν = 3 / 4 \nu=3/4 with 12 fold ground state degeneracy on the torus. Its chiral graviton spectral functions has one low energy peak with negative chirality and one high energy peak with positive chirality. This points to a specific member of the Moore-Read type states and agrees with the deduction based on daughter states.



I
Introduction

Fractional quantum Hall states constitute an prominent example of strongly correlated topological phases
Halperin and Jain 2020
. In the most common setting, electrons are confined to two dimensional space and exposed to a perpendicular magnetic field such that dispersionless Landau levels (LLs) arise. An intricate interplay between the single-particle states and electron correlations stabilize many fascinating states. While experimental systems have underlying lattice structures, it is usually sufficient to employ the continuum description without worrying about lattice effects because Landau orbitals are much larger than crystalline unit cells. Thouless et al. uncovered the topological underpinning of quantum Hall physics as they linked the Hall conductance of non-interacting electrons to the Chern number of occupied energy bands
Thouless et al. 1982
. This description is independent from LLs and can be extended in various ways
Haldane 1988
. In principle, FQH states without magnetic field can be realized in energy bands that satisfy certain conditions
Liu and Bergholtz 2024
. A few ground breaking experiments on twisted MoTe
2
and rhombohedral multilayer graphene have indeed achieved this goal
Cai et al. 2023
;
Zeng et al. 2023
;
Park et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023
;
Lu et al. 2024
;
Xie et al.
.

With or without magnetic fields, FQH states exhibit many remarkable universal properties that are described in the framework of topological order (TO)
Wen 1989
. It was predicted shortly after the original observation of FQH states that they posses anyonic excitations
Arovas et al. 1984
and compelling evidence has been reported in recent years
Bartolomei et al. 2020
;
Nakamura et al. 2020
;
Nakamura et al. 2023
;
Werkmeister et al.
;
Samuelson et al.
. The existence of non-Abelian anyons was also proposed in the context of FQH states
Moore and Read 1991
;
Wen 1991
and has drawn considerable attention for their intriguing nature and potential utitlity in topological quantum computation
Kitaev 2003
;
Nayak et al. 2008
. The Moore-Read Pfaffian state and its variants
Levin et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2007
;
Son 2015
;
Zucker and Feldman 2016
are an important class of non-Abelian states that may be realized in multiple platforms
Ma et al. 2024
. A dramatic manifestation of these states is that they occur at filling factors
\nu=p/q
with denominator
q=2
whereas most other FQH states are found at odd
q

Willett et al. 1987
. In three experiments about GaAs hole systems
Wang et al. 2022
, rhombohedral multilayer graphene
Xie et al.
, and bilayer graphene (BLG)
Kumar et al.
,
\nu=3/4
FQH states have been reported. These results are very intriguing and call for detailed investigations.

The
3/4
state has already been analyzed in the experimental papers, but we shall provide additional results on this system. One can place strong constrains on the possible TOs at
\nu=3/4
using a bootstrap analysis
Musser et al.
. In particular, there are four types of non-Abelian TOs whose ground state degeneracy (GSD) on the torus is
12
. It is quite plausible that the two experiments with magnetic fields have the same fundamental mechanism that can be understood in two complementary ways
Wang et al. 2022
;
Kumar et al.
. In the first approach, one construct
\nu=1/4
FQH states from the usual Moore-Read type states and then perform particle-hole conjugation to obtain
3/4
states. In the second approach, electrons are turned into composite fermions (CFs) with effective filling factor
3/2
such that the
1/2
part form Moore-Read type states
Jain 1989a
. Numerical calculations are performed in the context of BLG to substantiate our analysis. Using a microscopic model that takes into account both the orbital characters of LLs and strong level mixing, 12 quasi-degenerate ground states is found on the torus in a certain parameter regime. An inspection of the chiral graviton spectral functions
Yang 2016
;
Liou et al. 2017
;
Nguyen and Son 2021a
indicates that this system realizes the anti-Pfaffian type TO.

II
Non-Abelian Candidates

It is helpful to begin with the extensively studied non-Abelian states at
\nu=1/2
. The original Moore-Read Pfaffian wave function written in the disk geometry is

\displaystyle{\rm Pf}\left(\frac{1}{z_{j}-z_{k}}\right)\prod_{j<k}(z_{j}-z_{k})^{2}.

(1)

For an isolated LL with two-body interaction, there is an exact particle-hole symmetry that relates filling factors
\nu
and
1-\nu
. The exact solution of a many-body system at
\nu=1/2
should be transformed to itself under this symmetry, but the Pfaffian wave function does not satisfy this condition. This has motivated the construction of the anti-Pfaffian
Levin et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2007
and the particle-hole symmetric Pfaffian (PHS-Pfaffian) state
Son 2015
;
Zucker and Feldman 2016
. Some wave functions have been proposed for the PH-Pfaffian state
Yang
;
Mishmash et al. 2018
;
Rezayi et al. 2021
but the anti-Pfaffian state has no simple real space wave function. It is possible to construct a parton wave function that has the same universal properties as the anti-Pfaffian state
Balram et al. 2018
. For our purpose, it is convenient to denote all of them collectively as
\Psi^{\rm MR}_{1/2}
whenever there is no need to specify which one is under investigation. If they are multiplied by another Jastrow factor, we would obtain
\nu=1/4
states

\displaystyle\Psi^{\rm I}_{1/4}=\Psi^{\rm MR}_{1/2}\prod_{j<k}(z_{j}-z_{k})^{2}.

(2)

This wave function can also be interpreted in the parton framework
Jain 1989b
that would be useful in later discussions. One electron is decomposed to one fermion and one boson. The fermions form
\nu=1/2
Moore-Read type states and the bosons form
\nu=1/2
Laughlin state
Laughlin 1983
. For each
\nu=1/4
state, there is a counterpart at
\nu=3/4
obtained through particle-hole conjugation

\displaystyle\Psi^{\rm I}_{3/4}=\mathcal{PH}\Psi^{\rm I}_{1/4}.

(3)

As we shall demonstrate below in BLG, gapped state only arise at
\nu=3/4
when LL mixing is strong. In such cases,
\nu
and
1-\nu
are not related by exact particle-hole symmetry. Nevertheless, the universal properties of
3/4
states can still be analyzed in this way.

We can also construct
\nu=3/4
states using the CF theory
Jain 1989a
. An electron binds two fluxes to become CF. FQH states of electrons are interpreted as integer quantum Hall (IQH) states of CFs. This scenario does not work for the
\nu=1/2
Moore-Read type states, but they can be attributed to CF pairing
Read and Green 2000
;
Scarola et al. 2000
. To obtain a
\nu=3/4
state, the CFs should have filling factor
\nu^{*}=-3/2
according to the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{*}}{2\nu^{*}+1}=3/4.

(4)

The minus sign means that the CFs form Landau-like levels in negative effective magnetic field as shown in Fig.
1
. An IQH state is realized in the fully occupied lowest level whereas Moore-Read type states appear in the half-filled second level. This analysis leads to the wave function

\displaystyle\Psi^{\rm II}_{3/4}=\left[\Psi^{\rm MR}_{1/2}\otimes\Psi^{\rm IQH}_{1}\right]^{*}\prod_{j<k}(z_{j}-z_{k})^{2}.

(5)

A similar picture has been proposed to understand the FQH states at
\nu=3/8
and
3/10

Mukherjee et al. 2012
;
Mukherjee and Mandal 2015
. This approach does not rely on particle-hole transformation but requires the presence of residual interaction between the CFs.

The final states generated by these two approaches should have a one-to-one correspondence with each other. This equivalence has been discussed for Abelian FQH states with
\nu\geq 2/3
. For example, the
4/5
states can be obtained as particle-hole conjugate of the
\nu=1/5
Laughlin state, or from interacting CFs in partially filled levels
Balram 2016
. It has also been pointed out in Ref.
Kumar et al.
based on the results on edge physics. The state
\Psi^{\rm I}_{3/4}
that is constructed from Eq. (
2
) with the Pfaffian factor corresponds to the state
\Psi^{\rm II}_{3/4}
in which the half-filled CFs form the Pfaffian state, and similarly for the other two members. Thanks to the equivalence, the three states can be named as
\frac{3}{4}
-Pf,
\frac{3}{4}
-aPf, and
\frac{3}{4}
-PHS-Pf if Eq. (
2
) contains the Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian, and PHS-Pfaffian wave function, respectively. Chiral central charges for them are
-1/2
,
3/2
, and
3/2

Kumar et al.
.

For a specific system that supports half- or quarter-filled FQH states, considerable efforts are required to determine if they are indeed Moore-Read type states and distinguish between the three members. One fruitful probe is the thermal Hall conductance measurement
Banerjee et al. 2018
;
Dutta et al. 2022
;
Melcer et al. 2024
that directly reveal the chiral central charge of edge states
Kitaev 2006
. It is also helpful to analyze the daughter states associated with a particular non-Abelian state
Levin and Halperin 2009
;
Yutushui et al.
;
Zheltonozhskii et al.
. The existence of daughter states provides indirect yet powerful evidence
Kumar et al.
. Here we study the spectral functions of chiral gravitons. This concept was introduced to describe the long wavelength limit of neutral excitations in FQH states that arise from dynamical oscillation of intrinsic metrics
Yang 2016
;
Liou et al. 2017
;
Nguyen and Son 2021a
. These excitations have spin-2 and definite chiralities that could reveal the nature of FQH states. For some two-flux CF states in GaAs, they have been observed in experiments using circularly polarized light
Liang et al. 2024
. It was confirmed that graviton of the states with
\nu<1/2
(
\nu>1/2
) has negative (positive) chirality. Light scattering is a bulk probe and very suitable for studying the
\nu=1/2
Moore-Read type states
Haldane et al. 2021
. The dominant chirality is negative (positive) for the Pfaffian (anti-Pfaffian) state whereas both chiralities should appear in the PHS-Pfaffian state. In the vicinity of
\nu=1/4
, the graviton physics becomes even more intricate: the CF states at
\nu<1/4
have two graviton modes with negative chirality, the CF states at
\nu>1/4
have one graviton mode for each of the positive and negative chiralities, and the
1/4
CF liquid has one graviton mode with negative chirality
Nguyen and Son 2021b
;
Nguyen et al. 2022
;
Balram et al. 2022
;
Wang and Yang 2023
. It has been proposed that four-flux CF states can be recast as parton states such that the higher energy mode corresponds to excitations of bosonic partons
Balram et al. 2022
. These results shall provide valuable guidance when we analyze the graviton modes of the
\nu=3/4
states below.

III
Numerical Results

This section presents our numerical results about the
3/4
state in BLG that supports its non-Abelian nature. It is certainly desirable if an accurate model for BLG is employed, but the essential physics can be captured in a simplified model that is more amenable to numerical investigations. The two layers in BLG are labeled as
1,2
and each one hosts two sublattice sites
A
and
B
. In the tight-binding description of BLG, each lattice site contributes one state so we have a four-dimensional Hamiltonian
Jung and MacDonald 2014
. Four hopping parameters
\gamma_{i}
(
i=0,1,3,4
) are used according to the Slonczewski-Weiss-McClure scheme. An additional parameter
\delta
is introduced to characterize the onsite potential of
B_{1}
and
A_{2}
sites. In the presence of a displacement field, there is a potential difference between the top and bottom layers. The band structure has two valleys
\mathbf{K}_{\pm}
and the Landau problem can be studied in each valley. We would like to simplify this model for two reasons. Firstly, the single-particle eigenstates are complicated four-dimensional vectors whose elements are infinite summations over non-relativistic Landau orbitals. The existence and properties of FQH states are strongly affected by the single-particle wave functions. While the parameters
\gamma_{i}
and
\delta
have been deduced from ab initio calculations
Jung and MacDonald 2014
, they may not be perfectly accurate for a specific sample. It would be premature to rule out the existence of a FQH state simply because numerical calculations using these parameters fail to produce this state. As the
3/4
state only appear in a small regime in the experiment
Kumar et al.
, a more reasonable approach is to search for the parameters that can accomplish this. Secondly, this model does not have full rotational symmetry due to the trigonal wrapping term
\gamma_{3}
. While the existence of FQH states does not require rotational symmetry, it is often more convenient to explore the nature of a state when it does posses this symmetry.

The filling factor range
[-4,4]
in BLG is spanned by two LLs with a small splitting from each valley and spin. It is reasonable to assume that the
3/4
state is spin-valley polarized given the experimental results and previous experience. In our simplified model, there are two LLs with single-particle eigenstates

IV
Discussions

In summary, we have studied the possible TOs of
\nu=3/4
FQH states and revealed that the one observed in BLG is of the
\frac{3}{4}
-aPf type based on numerical results. One may wonder if our results also shed some light on GaAs hole system
Wang et al. 2022
. It has an intricate LL diagram with many crossings and LL mixing is strong in the vicinity of
\nu=3/4
. The single-particle eigenstates of these LLs are also complicated multi-component vectors. A simple estimate shows that LL mixing is likely to have significant impact and at least two LLs should be included when studying the
3/4
state. The lower level that is relevant here is also primarily made of the non-relativistic lowest LL orbitals
MacDonald and Ekenberg 1989
;
Yang et al. 1990
. If the higher level can be approximated in the way that we have considered, then the
3/4
state in GaAs hole system should be essentially the same as the one in BLG. As the stability of the
3/4
state depends quite sensitively on
c_{0}
, one should perform numerical calculations using microscopic models of GaAs hole system to address this issue. The rhombohedral hexalayer graphene problem is more difficult
Xie et al.
. For the related pentalayer case, several theoretical investigations have appeared but consensus has yet to emerge
Dong et al. a
;
Zhou et al.
;
Dong et al. b
;
Guo et al.
;
Kwan et al.
. Nevertheless, if numerical calculations confirm that the GSD on the torus is 12, we would conclude that the state is non-Abelian.

There was also an observation of
\nu=1/4
state in GaAs hole systems
Wang et al. 2023
. Numerical investigations suggest that it may also arises from CF pairing
Zhao et al. 2023
;
Sharma et al. 2024
. No clear plateaus was found at
\nu=3/4
in this experiment, so one tentatively conclude that particle-hole symmetry is absent. It would be very helpful if a microscopic calculation that uses accurate single-particle eigenstates and takes into account LL mixing can be done. More generally, LL mixing could play an important role in many van der Waals materials. For Bernal stacking trilayer graphene, half-filled FQH states have been observed
Chen et al. 2024
. Their nature depends on the details of LL mixing and intriguing evolution is expected when LLs are tuned by external fields. In rhombohedral
N
-layer graphene, there could be
N
nearly degenerate LLs in certain parameter regimes, which is favorable for realizing certain non-Abelian FQH states given by the parton construction
Wu et al. 2017
;
Timmel and Wen
.

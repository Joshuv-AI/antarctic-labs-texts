The asymptotic mean action and the asymptotic linking number for pseudo-rotations
David Bechara Senior, Patrice Le Calvez, Abror Pirnapasov

Abstract
. We establish that the asymptotic mean action and the asymptotic linking number of irrational pseudo-rotations remain well-defined everywhere and constant for every C 1 C^{1} irrational pseudo-rotation that behaves as a rotation on the boundary. As a consequence, we demonstrate that the isotopy of irrational pseudo-rotations with a positive rotation number is a right-handed isotopy in the sense of Ghys.



1.
Introduction and Main Results

1.1.
The asymptotic mean action of an irrational pseudo-rotation

We consider
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbbm{R}^{2}
the unit closed two disk centered at the origin with polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
. Let
f
be an area-preserving smooth diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
isotopic to
\mathrm{id}
via an area-preserving isotopy
\tilde{f}=\{f_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
. Let
\beta
be a primitive of the standard symplectic form
\omega=\frac{r}{\pi}dr\wedge d\theta.

The
action
of
f
with respect to
\beta
and the given isotopy is the unique smooth function
a_{\tilde{f}}(x):\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}da_{\tilde{f}}=f^{*}\beta-\beta\\
a_{\tilde{f}}(x)=\int_{\tilde{f}(x)}\beta\qquad\text{for every}\ x\in\partial\mathbb{D},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{f}(x)
is the path
t\mapsto f_{t}(x)
.

We define the corresponding Calabi invariant by

\mathrm{CAL}(\tilde{f}):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\ \omega.

The quantity
a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)
is called the
asymptotic mean action of

x
with respect to
f
, whenever following limit exists

a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{\tilde{f}}({f}^{i}(x)).

A straightforward application of Birkhoffs’ ergodic theorem implies that
a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)
is well defined for Lebesgue a.e.
x\in\mathbb{D}
, furthermore it is independant of the primitive
\beta
of
\omega
. If
x
is periodic or
x\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, then

a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)
is well defined. We will denote
\mathrm{Per}(f)
the set of periodic points of
f
. Moreover
a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)
is equal to
\rho(\tilde{f}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{D}})
for every
x\in\partial\mathbb{D}
where
\rho(\tilde{f}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{D}})
is the rotation number of the isotopy
\tilde{f}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\{f_{t}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
restricted to the boundary of the disc
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

In
[
Hut16
]
Hutchings discovered a very interesting relation between the Calabi invariant and the asymptotic mean action of periodic points of smooth area-preserving disk maps which are a rotation on the boundary.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Hut16
]

Let
f
be a smooth area-preserving diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}

that is a rigid rotation near the boundary and isotopic to
\mathrm{id}
via
\tilde{f}
. If

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{CAL}(\tilde{f})<\rho(\tilde{f}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}),

then

(1.3)

\displaystyle\inf\left\{a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)\,|\,x\in\mathrm{Per}(f)\right\}\leq\mathrm{CAL}(\tilde{f}).

Weiler
[
Wei21
]
established a similar result for area-preserving diffeomorphisms of the closed annulus. The third author
[
Pir21
]
proved Theorem
1.1
without the assumption of the map being a rotation near the boundary by using preliminary extensions. The third author and Prasad
[
PP22
]
proved the validity of inequality (
1.3
) for generic Hamiltonian maps of surfaces with boundaries by proving the generic equidistribution theorem for Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of surfaces with boundary. Recently, in
[
NW23
]
, Nelson and Weiler established a nongeneric version of Theorem
1.1
on higher-genus surfaces but with stronger assumptions.

For the particular case of irrational pseudo-rotations the third author showed that the Calabi invariant of smooth irrational pseudo-rotations is equal to the rotation number(cf.
[
Pir21
]
), this was proven by using the general case of Theorem
1.1
. This result was subsequently reaffirmed by the second author using finite-dimensional methods for
C^{1}
irrational pseudo-rotations which are conjugated to a rotation on the boundary.

The following question, posed in
[
BSHS21
]
in the context of three-dimensional Reeb dynamics, addresses the relationship between the asymptotic mean action of periodic points and the asymptotic mean action of all points:

Question 1.2
.

Does the following equality hold for all area-preserving disk maps?

(1.4)

\overline{\big\{a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x):\ x\in\mathbb{D}\big\}}=\overline{\big\{a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x):\ x\in\mathrm{Per}(f)\big\}}

Equality (
1.4
) implies Theorem
1.1
because the set
\overline{\big\{a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x):\ x\in\mathbb{D}\big\}}
contains the Calabi invariant. In his thesis
[
BS23
]
, the first author showed that the equality (
1.4
) holds for all diffeomorphisms generated by an autonomous Hamiltonian of the disc.

Definition 1.3
.

An area-preserving diffeomorphism
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is called an irrational pseudo-rotation if
\#\mathrm{Per}(f)=1
.

In the following theorem, we affirmatively answer question
1.2
for
C^{1}
irrational pseudo-rotations, which are conjugate to a rotation when restricted to the boundary.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a
C^{1}
irrational pseudo-rotation that is
C^{1}
conjugate to a rotation on the boundary, then the sequence of functions

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{\tilde{f}}({f}^{i}(x))}{n}

uniformly converges to
\rho(\tilde{f})
. In particular, the asymptotic mean action
a^{\infty}_{\tilde{f}}(x)={\rho(\tilde{f})}
, where
\rho(\tilde{f})
is the Poincare rotation number of the isotopy restricted to the boundary of the disk.

Remark 1.5
.

We prove Theorem
1.4
for irrational pseudo-rotations that fix
0
and coincide with a rotation on the boundary. This is merely a technical condition because if
x\in\mathbb{D}
is a fixed point of an irrational pseudo-rotation
f
, then there exists a compactly supported Hamiltonian
g
such that
g(0)=x
. This implies that
g\circ f\circ g^{-1}
is also an irrational pseudo-rotation and
0
is its fixed point. We also note that every
C^{1}
diffeomorphism of
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
can be extended as a
C^{1}
area preserving diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
. This is the reason why we can suppose that
f
is
C^{1}
conjugate to a rotation on
\partial\mathbb{D}
in Theorem 1.3.

In his thesis
[
BS23
]
, the first author proved Theorem
1.4
for
C^{0}
-rigid irrational pseudo-rotation using Joly’s winding number bound [Lemma 5.3 in
[
Jol21
]
] for
C^{0}
-rigid irrational pseudo-rotations. This statement also holds for minimally ergodic irrational pseudo-rotations
[
FK04
]
, which are irrational pseudo-rotations having only three ergodic invariant measures: the Dirac measure at the fixed point, the Lebesgue measure on the boundary, and the Lebesgue measure on the disc. Together with the Calabi identity result in
[
Pir21
]
, minimally ergodicity implies that the integral of the action function with respect to any invariant probability measure is equal to the rotation number. This implication leads to the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
using a Krylov-Bogolyubov type argument. In the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we also prove that the integral of the action function with respect to any invariant measure is equal to the rotation number using Le Calvez’s finite-dimensional methods.

1.2.
The asymptotic orbit linking number of an irrational pseudo-rotation

Let
f\in\text{Diff}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
and let
\tilde{f}=\{f_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a Hamiltonian isotopy such that
f^{0}=\mathrm{id}
and
f^{1}=f
. Here,
\text{Diff}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
is the set of
C^{1}
diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
which preserves
\omega_{0}
.

Definition 1.6
.

The
winding number

W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)
of a pair of distinct points
x,y\in\mathbb{D}
is the real number

W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y):=\frac{{\theta}(1)-{\theta}(0)}{2\pi},

where
\theta:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
is a continuous function such that

(1.5)

\frac{f_{t}(y)-f_{t}(x)}{||f_{t}(y)-f_{t}(x)||}=e^{i\theta(t)}.

The value of
W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)
does not depend on the isotopy joining
f
to the identity if isotopies have the same rotation number on the boundary. This holds because any two paths joining
f
to the identity are homotopic if the isotopies have the same rotation number when restricted to the boundary, which implies that the winding number is the same. The map
W_{\tilde{f}}
is smooth and bounded on
(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D})\setminus\Delta
, where
\Delta
denotes the diagonal
\{(x,x)|x\in\mathbb{D}\}
in
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
. To see this, let
K
be indeed the blow-up of the diagonal in
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
. In more concrete terms,
K
is the compact set formed by replacing
\Delta
with the unit tangent bundle
T^{1}\mathbb{D}
). Of course
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\setminus\Delta
naturally embeds into
K
as a dense open set. To be more precise a sequence
(x_{n},y_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\setminus\Delta
converges to a point
(x,\xi)\in T^{1}\mathbb{D}\simeq\mathbb{D}\times S^{1}
whenever
x_{n},y_{n}\rightarrow x
and if the sequence of oriented vectors
\frac{y_{n}-x_{n}}{||y_{n}-x_{n}||}=e^{i\theta_{n}}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
converges to
\xi
. We assert that if
f
is of class
C^{1}
, the function
W_{\tilde{f}}
extends to
K
as a continuous function, and in particular,
W_{\tilde{f}}
is bounded. This is, in fact, an extension of the definition of
W_{\tilde{f}}
to element the form
(x,\xi)\in T^{1}\mathbb{D}
. Let
(f_{t})
be the isotopy between the identity and
f
among the
C^{1}
-diffeomorphisms of
\text{Diff}^{1}(\mathbb{D},\omega_{0})
. We then define
W_{\tilde{f}}(x,\xi)
as the variation in the angle of the vector
df_{t}(\xi)\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}
, images of
\xi
under the differential
df_{t}
, as
t
varies from
0
to
1
. This indeed defines the desired continuous extension of
W_{\tilde{f}}
to
K
.

Define the set
\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
as follows:

\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{D}}\times{\mathbb{D}}\,|\enskip f^{i}(x)\not=f^{j}(y)\enskip\mathrm{for\enskip every\enskip}(i,i^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\}.

Definition 1.7
.

Suppose
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
, the number

L_{\tilde{f}}^{\infty}(x,y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(y))}{n^{2}}

is called the
asymptotic orbit linking number
of
(x,y)
if the limit exists.

In the next theorem, we prove that the asymptotic linking number exists and is equal to the rotation number of the isotopy for every pair of points that are not in the same orbit of the map.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a
C^{1}
irrational pseudo-rotation that is
C^{1}
conjugate to a rotation on the boundary and
\tilde{f}
is an isotopy from
\mathrm{id}
to
f
, then the sequence

\left(\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(y))}{n^{2}}\right)_{n\geq 0}

converges uniformly to
\rho(\tilde{f})
for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular,
the asymptotic mean linking number
L_{\tilde{f}}^{\infty}(x,y)
is defined for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
and equal to
\rho(\tilde{f})
.

Definition 1.9
.

The isotopy
\tilde{f}
is called a right-handed isotopy, if

•

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
if holds that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(y))>0.

•

for every
x\in\mathrm{Per}(f)
if holds that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
f^{i}(x)\neq f^{j}(x)\end{subarray}}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(x))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=0\\
f^{i}(x)=f^{j}(x)\end{subarray}}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),df^{j}(\xi))\right)>0.

Remark 1.10
.

The isotopy
\tilde{f}
is called left-handed if both limits are negative in Definition
1.9
.

In
[
Ghy09
]
, Ghys introduced right-handed flows and demonstrated that any finite collection of periodic orbits admits a global surface of section. Subsequently, in
[
FH21
]
, Florio and Hryniewicz provided a sufficient condition for Reeb flows to be right-handed. Prasad
[
Pra22
]
showed that volume-preserving right-handed flows are conformally equivalent to Reeb flows. In her thesis
[
Lhu18
]
, Lhuissier showed that definition
1.9
is equivalent to the definition of right-handedness due to Ghys if one looks at the suspension of the disk map as a flow (see
[
Lhu18
]
Theorem 7.3.2).

Corollary 1.11
.

If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a
C^{1}
irrational pseudo-rotation that is
C^{1}
conjugate to a rotation on the boundary, and
\tilde{f}
is an area-preserving isotopy from the identity map
\mathrm{id}
to
f
such that the rotation number of the isotopy is (negative) positive, then the isotopy
\tilde{f}
is (left-handed) right-handed.

Finally we proceed to prove Corollary
1.11
using Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Corollary
1.11
.

It is enough to prove the theorem in the case were the isotopy is right-handed. Assume that the rotation number of the isotopy is positive. By using Theorem
1.8
, we can conclude that for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
, the asymptotic orbit linking number is defined and equal to the rotation number and by assumption, it is positive. That is

L_{\tilde{f}}^{\infty}(x,y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(y))}{n^{2}}=\rho(\tilde{f})>0

for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{O}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now we check the second condition of Definition
1.9
. Since the fixed point
0
of
f
is the only periodic point of
f
, the sum in the second condition simplifies to the following sum:

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(0,df^{i}(\xi))>0.

But, this is the linearized rotation number of
0
through the isotopy. By the classical result of Franks’
[
Fra88
]
and also of the second author
[
LC01
]
, the linearized rotation number of the fixed point is also equal to
\rho(\tilde{f})
hence it is also positive.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.11
.
∎

Structure of the article:

In Section 2, we recall basic notions relevant to the article, such as weak convergence of probability measures, radial foliations, and their properties. In Section 3, we introduce radial foliations and isotopies for irrational pseudo-rotations whose study has been developed by the second author in
[
LC23a
]
; then, we show that the measure between any leaf of a radial foliation and its image is equal to the rotation number of the map. By combining this with properties of the foliation, we show that the integral of the winding number with respect to the product of any two invariant probability measures is also equal to the rotation number. In Section 4, we use the integral of the winding number together with a Krylov-Bogolyubov argument to prove Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.8
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce necessary results in ergodic theory and also in the theory of radial foliations specifically in the case of irrational pseudo-rotations. In order to state several of the inequalities needed to prove the main theorems, we require different quantities coming from measurements of topological angles.

2.1.
Invariant measures and pointwise ergodic theorem.

Assume
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a homeomorphism. For
x\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac measure supported at
x
. We will denote:

\mu_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\delta_{f^{i}(x)}

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be two sequences in
\mathbb{D}
and
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
an increasing sequence of positive integers. Then there exists a subsequence
(n_{k_{j}})_{j\geq 0}
such that the sequences of measures
(\mu_{n_{k_{j}}}(x_{k_{j}}))_{j\geq 0}
and
(\mu_{n_{k_{j}}}(y_{k_{j}}))_{j\geq 0}
converge for the weak* topology and their limits are invariant probability measures of
f
.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
is very similar to the proof of the Krylov-Bogolyubov Theorem and is based on the compactness of the space of Borel probability measures furnished with the weak
∗
topology and the fact that every limit point of a sequence
(\mu_{n_{k}}(x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
is invariant.
The next result is straightforward.

Proposition 2.2
.

If sequences of probability measures
(\mu_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converge for the weak
∗
topology to
\nu
and
\mu
, then the sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{k}\times\nu_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converges for the weak
∗
topology to
\nu\times\mu
.

The following theorem is a direct implication of Templemann’s pointwise ergodic theorem. For more information, please refer to Theorem 2.6 in Section 2.3.3 of
[
Sar09
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
\mu
and
\nu
are invariant probability measures of
f
. If
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D},\mu\times\nu)
, then

S_{n}\phi(z):=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi(f^{i}(x),f^{j}(y))

converges for
(\mu\times\nu)
-almost every
z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
.

2.2.
Properties of radial foliations and topological angles

Let us begin with some notations and definitions, specially regarding radial foliations on a disk. Let
D:=\{z\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}\,|\enskip\|z\|<r\}
be an open disk and set
D^{*}=D\setminus\{0\}
the punctured disk, lastly let us write
\tilde{D}
for the universal covering space of
D^{*}
. We will consider the sets

W=\{(z,z^{\prime})\in D^{*}\times D^{*}\ |\ z\neq z^{\prime}\}

and

\tilde{W}=\{(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{D}\times\tilde{D}\ |\ \tilde{z}\neq\tilde{z}^{\prime}\}.

Definition 2.4
.

A
radial foliation
is an oriented topological foliation of
D^{*}
such that every leaf
\phi
is a
ray
, meaning that it satisfies

\alpha(\phi)=\{0\},\qquad\omega(\phi)\subset\overline{D}\setminus D\quad\text{(here the closure is taken in }\mathbb{R}^{2})

In other words,
\phi
tends to
0
in the past and to
\partial D
in the future. We denote
{\mathfrak{F}}
the set of radial foliations. The group
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
of homeomorphisms of
D^{*}
that are isotopic to the identity, acts naturally on
{\mathfrak{F}}
: if
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
, then the foliation
f(\mathscr{F})
, whose leaves are the images by
f
of the leaves of
\mathscr{F}
, is a radial foliation. Moreover,
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
acts transitively on
\mathfrak{F}
. If
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
is endowed with the
C^{0}
topology, meaning the compact-open topology applied to maps and the inverse maps, the stabilizer
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})
of
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
. Consequently, the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})/\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})

\displaystyle\to{\mathfrak{F}}

\displaystyle f\,\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})

\displaystyle\mapsto f(\mathscr{F})

is bijective and
{\mathfrak{F}}
can be furnished with a natural
C^{0}
topology. It is the topology, induced from the quotient topology defined on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})/\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})
by this identification map. Note that this topology does not depend on
\mathscr{F}
.
It is well known that the fundamental groups of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
and
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})
are infinite cyclic and that the morphism
i_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*}),\mathrm{Id})\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*}),\mathrm{Id})
induced by the inclusion map
i:\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathscr{F}}(D^{*})\to\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
is bijective. It implies that
{\mathfrak{F}}
is simply connected.
In the whole article, when
\mathscr{F}
is a radial foliation, the notation
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
means the lift of
\mathscr{F}
to the universal covering space
\tilde{D}
. Note that
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
is a trivial foliation. In particular, every leaf
\tilde{\phi}
of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
is an oriented line of
\tilde{D}
that separates
\tilde{D}
into two connected open sets, the component of
\tilde{D}\setminus\tilde{\phi}
lying on the left of
\tilde{\phi}
will be denoted
L(\tilde{\phi})
and the component lying on the right will be denoted
R(\tilde{\phi})
. We get a total order
\preceq_{\mathscr{F}}
on the set of leaves of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
as follows:

\tilde{\phi}\preceq_{\mathscr{F}}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow R(\tilde{\phi})\subset R(\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}).

We can also define a partial order
\leq_{{\mathscr{F}}}
on
\tilde{D}
: denote
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
the leaf of
\tilde{\mathscr{F}}
that contains
\tilde{z}
and write
\tilde{z}<_{{\mathscr{F}}}\tilde{z}^{\prime}
if
\tilde{z}\not=\tilde{z}^{\prime}
, if
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}=\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
and if the segment of
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
beginning at
\tilde{z}
and ending at
\tilde{z}^{\prime}
inherits the orientation of
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
.

converge, the first one to

the second one to

Moreover,

•

p_{*}

\int_{\mathbb{D}^{*}_{a/b}}p_{*}\penalty\ d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{*}_{a/b}}p_{1}\circ\tilde{f}_{2m}-p_{1}\penalty\ d\mu,

•

\int_{\mathbb{D}^{*}_{a/b}}\pi_{*}\penalty\ d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{D}^{*}_{a/b}}\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{f}_{2m}-\pi_{1}\penalty\ d\mu.

We know that

\displaystyle p_{*}(z)-\pi_{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}-p_{1})\circ f^{i}_{2mb}(z)-(\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}-\pi_{1})\circ f^{i}_{2mb}(z)}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i+1}-p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i})(z)-(\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i+1}-\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i})(z)}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}(p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i+1}-\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i+1})(z)-(p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i}-\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{i})(z)}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(p_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{n_{k}}-\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{{f}}_{2mb}^{n_{k}})(z)-(p_{1}-\pi_{1})(z)}{n_{k}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(p_{1}-\pi_{1})\circ f^{n_{k}}_{2mb}(z)-(p_{1}-\pi_{1})(z)}{n_{k}}=0

for
\mu
-almost every
z
. This completes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Remark 3.5

4.
Proof of Main Results

In this section, we use the last result from the previous section together with a Krylov-Bogolyubov type argument to prove Theorems

and

In his thesis, the first author established the inequality (

[

[

. By repeating the same argument, we could also show that it works for

Theorem 4.1
.

[
BS23
]

If

is a

(4.1)

\Big|\frac{a_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)}{n}\Big|\leq\frac{8}{n}.

Here
\omega=\frac{r}{\pi}dr\wedge d\theta
.

We will now prove Theorem
1.4
by invoking Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
3.9
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem
4.1
implies the following claim:

Claim
: For any invariant probability measure
\mu
, we have

\int_{D}a_{\tilde{f}}(x)\penalty\ d\mu(x)=\rho(\tilde{f}).

Proof:
Using inequality (
4.1
), we obtain the following inequality:

-\frac{8}{n}\leq\frac{a_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)}{n}\leq\frac{8}{n}.

First, we integrate both sides of the inequality with respect to the measure
\mu
, and then apply the following two identities:

(4.2)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}(x)\penalty\ d\mu(x)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x)\penalty\ d\mu(x)}{n},

(4.3)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\mu(x)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\mu(x)}{n}.

Therefore, we have:

-\frac{8}{n}\leq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}^{n}}\penalty\ d\mu(x)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}^{n}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\mu(x)}{n}\leq\frac{8}{n}.

The last inequality together with the equalities (
4.2
) and (
4.3

-\frac{8}{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\mu(x)-\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\mu(x)\leq\frac{8}{n}.

By taking the limit from both sides of the inequality, we obtain:

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\mu(x).

We can write the measure
\mu
in the following form
b\delta_{0}+(1-b)\mu_{1}
where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac measure at
0
,
b\in[0,1]
, and the measure
\mu_{1}
satisfies
\mu_{1}(\{0\})=0
. Now we rewrite the equality (
4.4

\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\mu(x)=b\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times d\delta_{0}(x)+(1-b)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}W_{\tilde{f}}(x,y)\penalty\ d\omega(y)\times\mu_{1}(x).

By Theorem
3.9
and the equality (

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\mu(x)=b\int_{\mathbb{D}}a_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\delta_{0}(x)+(1-b)\rho(\tilde{f}).

By combining of Theorem
4.1
and Theorem

, together with the last equality this gives the desired equality

∎

Assume the sequence

\left(\frac{\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}a_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x))}{n}\right)_{n\geq 0}

does not converge uniformly, so there exist

\epsilon>0

\big|\frac{\sum^{n_{k}-1}_{i=0}a_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x_{{k}}))}{n_{k}}-\rho(\tilde{f})\big|\geq\epsilon.

We now choose a subsequence

Proof of Theorem

Let us begin by assuming that the sequence

\left|\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n_{k}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{k}-1}W_{\tilde{f}}(f^{i}(x_{{k}}),f^{j}(y_{{k}}))}{n_{k}^{2}}-\rho(\tilde{f})\right|\geq\epsilon.

We may assume, up to passing to a further sub-sequence that the sequence
(n_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbbm{N}}

Define

Define

\displaystyle\int_{K}W_{\tilde{f}}\penalty\ d\lambda

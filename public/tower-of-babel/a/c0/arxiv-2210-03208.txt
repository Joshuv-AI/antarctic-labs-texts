Single Inclusive Hadron Production in DIS at Small x x : Next to Leading Order Corrections
Filip Bergabo, Jamal Jalilian-Marian

Abstract
We calculate the one-loop corrections to single inclusive hadron production in Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS) at small x x in the forward rapidity region using the Color Glass Condensate formalism. We show that the divergent parts of the next to leading order (NLO) corrections either cancel among each other or lead to x x (rapidity) evolution of the leading order (LO) dipole cross section according to the JIMWLK evolution equation and DGLAP evolution of the parton-hadron fragmentation function. The remaining finite parts constitute the NLO ( α s \alpha_{s} ) corrections to the LO single inclusive hadron production cross section in DIS at small x x .

Here we calculate the next to leading order corrections to single inclusive hadron production in DIS at small
x
in the forward rapidity region using the Color Glass Condensate formalism. To do so we use our recent results for next to leading order corrections to dihadron production
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b
in DIS and integrate out one of the final state partons. As expected we encounter various divergences which appear when we integrate over the phase space of the final state parton. We show that UV and soft divergences cancel among each other while the collinear divergences associated with radiation of a massless parton are absorbed into the parton-hadron fragmentation function. We show that all quadrupole terms appearing in the intermediate steps of the calculation cancel among various terms and one is left with dipoles (and squared dipoles) only. The rapidity divergences arising from integrating over longitudinal phase space of the final state parton are absorbed into evolution of the dipoles describing the target dynamics and lead to JIMWLK evolution of the leading order cross section. The remaining terms are finite and constitute the
O(\alpha_{s})

x

IV
Divergences

As in the case of dihadron production in
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b
there are
four categories of divergences;
UV, soft, rapidity and collinear. The cancellation of UV and soft divergences
proceeds as follows. UV divergences appear as
\mathbf{x}_{3}\to\mathbf{x}_{2}
in
\sigma_{6}
, as
\mathbf{x}_{3}\to\mathbf{x}_{1}
in
\sigma_{3\times 3}
, as
\mathbf{k}\to\infty
in
\sigma_{10}
, as
\mathbf{k}_{2}\to\infty
in
\sigma_{11}
and
\sigma_{12}
, and as
\mathbf{k}_{1}\to\infty
in
\sigma_{14(1)}
and
\sigma_{14(2)}
. We find that these UV divergences all cancel according to Eq.
27
. Note that all other UV divergent terms were already canceled in the first two lines of Eq.
26
.

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{6}+\differential\sigma_{12}\right]_{\text{UV}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{3\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{11}+\differential\sigma_{10}+\differential\sigma_{14(1)}+\differential\sigma_{14(2)}\right]_{\text{UV}}=0.

(27)

Soft divergences occur when
\mathbf{k}
and
z
both go to zero (in coordinate space this becomes
\mathbf{x}_{3}\to\infty
and
z\to 0
). The cancellation of soft divergences in all remaining terms proceeds identically to that in dihadron production
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b
with

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{2\times 2}+2\,\differential\sigma_{10}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{1\times 2}+\differential\sigma_{13(1)}+\differential\sigma_{13(2)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{3\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{4\times 4}+2\,\differential\sigma_{3\times 4}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{2\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{2\times 4}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{6}+\differential\sigma_{8}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{11}+\differential\sigma_{14(1)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{12}+\differential\sigma_{14(2)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0.

(28)

Rapidity divergences appear when
z\rightarrow 0
at finite (non-zero) transverse momentum.
To isolate those it is customary to introduce a rapidity factorization scale
z_{f}

and write the
z

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z}{z}f(z)=\left\{\int_{0}^{z_{f}}\frac{\differential z}{z}+\int_{z_{f}}^{1}\frac{\differential z}{z}\,\right\}\,f(z).

(29)

so that the rapidity divergences will come from the first integral while the cross
section in the second integral will contain no rapidity divergence. Therefore we first
focus on the first integration region containing the rapidity divergence. As all soft
divergences have already been canceled only terms of the form

\frac{\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{3i}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3j}^{2}}
with
i\neq j
remain (see
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b

for the explicit expressions). These can be added to give

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{L}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}}{(2\pi)^{7}}\,\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{1})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{1})

\displaystyle\bigg\{\frac{N_{c}\,\alpha_{s}}{2\pi^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{z_{f}}\frac{\differential z}{z}\int\differential^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}\bigg[\frac{\mathbf{x}_{22^{\prime}}^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(S_{23}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{22^{\prime}}\right)-\frac{\mathbf{x}_{12}^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}\left(S_{13}S_{32}-S_{12}\right)-\frac{\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(S_{13}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{12^{\prime}}\right)\bigg]\bigg\}.

(30)

Comparing this to the LO result in Eq.
5

it is clear that the terms inside the curly bracket
correspond to the BK/JIMWLK evolution

Balitsky 1996
;
Kovchegov 2000
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1997b
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1998a
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1998b

of the dipoles that appear in the LO cross section.
As the contribution of the second term in the

z

be absorbed into BK/JIMWLK evolution of the LO cross section. The contribution of the

\int_{z_{f}}^{1}\differential z
region is now free of divergences (after absorbing the collinear divergences

correction to the LO result.

Collinear divergences are identical to the case of dihadrons and were treated in full detail
in
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b
, therefore here we will skip some details. When we
integrate out quarks there remains collinear divergences involving the antiquark when
its transverse momentum
\mathbf{q}
becomes parallel to the loop transverse momentum

\mathbf{k}
(
\theta\rightarrow 0

\cos\theta\equiv\frac{\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{q}||\mathbf{k}|}

One can write the hadronic cross section in terms of the partonic cross section as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to\bar{q}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{2}}D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h}).

Here we have included only the case where the quark is integrated out and the antiquark with momentum
q
fragments into a hadron
h

z_{h}q

D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h},\mu^{2})
can be written in terms of the
bare
fragmentation function
D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}
and higher order corrections.

\displaystyle D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h},\mu^{2})=D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})

(33)

The
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})

\sigma_{2\times 2}
(Eq.
7
) and
\sigma_{10}
(Eq.
16
). Using Eq.
32
with the leading order partonic cross section (longitudinal part of Eq.
5
) and the two relevant corrections (in momentum space), we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})\frac{2e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg[4z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}+\frac{2g^{2}N_{c}}{(2\pi)}\int\frac{\differential z}{z}\frac{(1-z_{2}-z)^{2}(z_{2}+z)^{2}[z_{2}^{2}+(z_{2}+z)^{2}]}{z_{2}}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{2}}\mathbf{q}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2g^{2}N_{c}}{(2\pi)}\int_{0}^{z_{2}}\frac{\differential z}{z}z_{2}(1-z_{2})^{2}[z_{2}^{2}+(z_{2}-z)^{2}]\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{2}}\mathbf{q}\right)^{2}}\Bigg].

Here the first term in the square brackets is the leading order contribution, the second term is the real correction
\sigma_{2\times 2}
, and the last term is the virtual correction
\sigma_{10}
(doubled here since it’s a cross term). Next we follow identical steps to what was done for dihadrons and rewrite this expression using

and relax the large-
N_{c}
approximation inside the square brackets taking
N_{c}\to 2C_{F}
. We also define a new variable
\xi

\xi=z_{2}/(z_{2}+z)
and for the virtual correction
\xi=(z_{2}-z)/z_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg[(1-z_{2})^{2}+2\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi^{5}}\frac{(1-z_{2}/\xi)^{2}(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{q}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle-2(1-z_{2})^{2}\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(\mathbf{k}-(1-\xi)\mathbf{q}\right)^{2}}\Bigg].

Now, we want to regulate the divergences in the two integrals over
\mathbf{k}

Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b

\displaystyle\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{q}\right)^{2}}\to\frac{e^{i\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\,\,\,\,\,\epsilon=d-2>0.

(36)

\displaystyle\left[\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}^{2}}\right]_{IR}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{IR}}-\log\left(e^{\gamma_{E}}\pi\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon).

(37)

\displaystyle\left[\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}^{2}}\right]_{UV}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}+\log\left(e^{\gamma_{E}}\pi\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon).

(38)

Here Eq.
36
is used for the real integral. We also make the approximation
\xi\approx 1
inside the exponential on the right side to ignore it, this we motivate by noting that there is a
1-\xi
in the denominator so the integral is dominated by the region
\xi\approx 1
. Eq.
37
and
38
are used for the virtual correction, after a shift on the
\mathbf{k}
integral. This shift makes the collinear divergence look infrared, but this is distinct from the soft divergences discussed earlier and is in fact the collinear divergence. The UV divergence is canceled against other virtual corrections (Eq.
27
). Therefore what remains here is the finite part of Eq.
38
which remains as part of the finite NLO corrections, and Eq.
37
which can now be added to the real correction by setting
\epsilon_{IR}=-\epsilon
.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg[(1-z_{2})^{2}+\left\{\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi^{5}}\frac{(1-z_{2}/\xi)^{2}(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}-\frac{(1-z_{2})^{2}\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\right\}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]\Bigg]

(39)

Next, let’s note that
z_{2}
is no longer an independent variable, it can be written in terms of
z_{h}
using

\displaystyle z_{2}=\frac{q_{h}^{+}}{z_{h}l^{+}}.

(40)

Writing all the
z_{2}
’s this way, and rearranging the result we have

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(q_{h}^{+})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{5}(l^{+})^{3}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{\left(1-\frac{q_{h}^{+}}{z_{h}l^{+}}\right)^{2}}{z_{h}^{5}}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})+\left\{\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi^{5}}\frac{(1-\frac{q_{h}^{+}}{z_{h}l^{+}\xi})^{2}(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\frac{D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})}{z_{h}^{5}}-\frac{(1-\frac{q_{h}^{+}}{z_{h}l^{+}})^{2}\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\frac{D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})}{z_{h}^{5}}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]\Bigg].

(41)

Now that all the
z_{h}
dependence is explicit, let’s perform a substitution on the
z_{h}
integral only in the real correction term (first term in the curly brackets). We’ll define
z_{h}^{\prime}=\xi z_{h}
, and once the substitution is complete we’ll remove the prime. This yields

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg[D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})+\left\{\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int_{z_{h}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h}/\xi)-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})\right\}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]\Bigg].

(42)

Here we have written things back in terms of
z_{2}
for aesthetics. Finally, the real and virtual corrections can be combined using the antiquark-antiquark splitting function
\mathcal{P}_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}(\xi)=C_{F}\left[\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)_{+}}+\frac{3}{2}\delta(1-\xi)\right]

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\int_{z_{h}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h}/\xi)\Bigg[\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}P_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}(\xi)\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]\Bigg].

(44)

For more details on these calculations, see
Bergabo and
Jalilian-Marian 2022b
. So, we are now able to define the DGLAP evolved fragmentation function

\displaystyle D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h},\mu^{2})=\int_{z_{h}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h}/\xi)\Bigg[\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}P_{\bar{q}\bar{q}}(\xi)\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\log\left(\pi e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu|\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2}|\right)\right]\Bigg],

(45)

in terms of which our result becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}_{\text{LO}+2\times 2+10}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z_{h}}{z_{h}^{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}}{(2\pi)^{5}}\int\differential^{6}\mathbf{x}[S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{12^{\prime}}+1]e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12^{\prime}}|Q_{2})D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h}).

(46)

Thus we have shown that all divergences appearing in our next-to-leading order results are either canceled or absorbed into evolution of dipoles and fragmentation functions. The full result for the single inclusive cross section at next-to-leading order can be written schematically as

\displaystyle\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to hX}=\differential\sigma_{LO}\otimes\text{JIMWLK}+\differential\sigma_{LO}\otimes D_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h},\mu^{2})+\differential\sigma_{NLO}^{\text{finite}}.

(47)

Here we imply the presence of a bare fragmentation function
D^{0}_{h/\bar{q}}(z_{h})
in the first and last terms.

In summary we have derived the next to leading order corrections to single inclusive hadron production in the forward rapidity region in DIS at small
x
. We have shown that all divergences either cancel among various terms or can be absorbed into BK/JIMWLK evolution of dipoles and DGLAP evolution of the parton-hadron fragmentation function.

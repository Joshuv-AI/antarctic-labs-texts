FZZ formula of boundary Liouville CFT via conformal welding
Morris Ang MIT Guillaume Remy Columbia University Xin Sun University of Pennsylvania

Abstract
Liouville Conformal Field Theory (LCFT) on the disk describes the conformal factor of the quantum disk, which is the natural random surface in Liouville quantum gravity with disk topology. Fateev, Zamolodchikov and Zamolodchikov (2000) proposed an explicit expression, the so-called FZZ formula, for the one-point bulk structure constant for LCFT on the disk. In this paper we give a proof of the FZZ formula in the probabilistic framework of LCFT, which represents the first step towards rigorously solving boundary LCFT using conformal bootstrap. In contrast to previous works, our proof is based on conformal welding of quantum disks and the mating-of-trees theory for Liouville quantum gravity. As a byproduct of our proof, we also obtain the exact value of the variance for the Brownian motion in the mating-of-trees theory. Our paper is an essential part of an ongoing program proving integrability results for Schramm-Loewner evolutions, LCFT, and in the mating-of-trees theory.

Introduction

Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) first appeared in theoretical physics in A. Polyakov’s seminal work
[
Pol81
]
where he proposed a theory of summation over the space of Riemannian metrics on a given two dimensional surface. The fundamental building block of his framework is the Liouville conformal field theory (LCFT),
which describes the law of the conformal factor of the metric tensor of a surface of fixed complex structure.
LCFT was first made rigorous in probability theory in the case of the Riemann sphere in
[
DKRV16
]
, and then in the case of a simply connected domain with boundary in
[
HRV18
]
; see also
[
DRV16
,
Rem18
,
GRV19
]
for the case of other topologies.

On surfaces without boundary, solving Liouville theory amounts to computing the three-point function on the sphere - which is given by the DOZZ formula proposed in
[
DO94
,
ZZ96
]
- and arguing that correlation functions of higher order or in higher genus can be obtained from it using the conformal bootstrap method of
[
BPZ84
]
.
Recently, two major breakthroughs have been achieved, namely the rigorous proof of the DOZZ formula
[
KRV20
]
and of the conformal bootstrap on the sphere
[
GKRV20
]
.
A similar program can be pursued for surfaces with boundary, where the most basic correlation function is the bulk one-point function on the disk with expression given by the Fateev-Zamolodchikov-Zamolodchikov (FZZ) formula proposed in
[
FZZ00
]
.
In this paper we will prove the FZZ formula, which represents the first step towards rigorously solving boundary LCFT.

Our approach is completely different from the one used in
[
KRV20
,
Rem20
,
RZ22
]

which is based on the BPZ equations and on the operator product expansion of
[
BPZ84
]
.
As explained in Section
1.1
, that approach has essential obstructions to proving the FZZ formula.
Instead, we rely on the rich interplay between LCFT and the random geometry corresponding to LQG.
In particular, we use the idea of the
quantum zipper
, which says that the conformal welding of two LQG type random surfaces
gives a LQG type surface decorated with a Schramm Loewner evolution (SLE). Building on the original work of
[
She16
,
DMS21
]

and the recent work of the first and the third authors with Holden
[
AHS20
,
AHS21
]
, we prove a new quantum zipper result and use it to obtain the FZZ formula.
As an intermediate step in our proof, we also obtain the exact value of the variance of the Brownian motion in the mating-of-trees theory by Duplantier, Miller and Sheffield
[
DMS21
]
.

Besides its intrinsic interest and its relevance to conformal bootstrap,
the FZZ formula yields integrability results on Gaussian multiple chaos on the unit disk or upper half plane; see Section
1.3
.
Moreover, it is a crucial input to the paper
[
AS21
]
of the first and the third authors on the integrability of conformal loop ensemble on the sphere.
We will discuss these aspects and related ongoing projects and open questions in Section
1.4
, after stating our main result in Section
1.1
and summarizing the proof strategy in Section
1.2
.

1.1
Boundary Liouville conformal field theory and the FZZ formula

In the physics literature LCFT is defined by a formal path integral. We work on a simply connected domain with boundary, which by conformal invariance can equivalently be the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
or the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. For almost all of this paper we will work with
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\mathrm{Im}(z)>0\}
with boundary given by the real line
\mathbb{R}
. The most basic observable of Liouville theory is the correlation function of
N
marked points in the bulk
z_{i}\in\mathbb{H}
with associated weights
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
M
marked points on the boundary
s_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
with associated weights
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. The physics path integral definition of this correlation function is then

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{\alpha_{i}\phi(z_{i})}\prod_{j=1}^{M}e^{\frac{\beta_{j}}{2}\phi(s_{j})}}}\right\rangle=\int_{X:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}}DXe^{-S_{L}(X)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{\alpha_{i}X(z_{i})}\prod_{j=1}^{M}e^{\frac{\beta_{j}}{2}X(s_{j})}}}\right),

(1.1)

where
DX
is a formal uniform measure over the space of all maps
X
from the domain
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathbb{R}
and
S_{L}(X)
is the
so-called Liouville action which has expression given by:

\displaystyle S_{L}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\partial^{g}X|^{2}+QR_{g}X+4\pi\mu e^{\gamma X}}}\right)g(x)d^{2}x+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(QK_{g}X+\mu_{B}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}X}}}\right)g(x)^{1/2}dx.

(1.2)

Here the background metric is
g=g(x)dx^{2}
,
R_{g}
and
K_{g}
are respectively the Ricci and geodesic curvatures on
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{R}
,

\gamma\in(0,2)
is the coupling parameter for LCFT,
Q=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{2}{\gamma}
is called the
background charge
,
and
\mu,\mu_{B}>0
are called
cosmological constant
s. They tune respectively the interaction strength of the Liouville potentials
e^{\gamma X}
and
e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}X}
.
Although the definition of
S_{L}(X)
depends on a choice of background metric
g
, the correlation functions depend trivially on this choice thanks to the Weyl anomaly proved in
[
HRV18
]
.

As a conformal field theory, it is well known that the (bulk) one-point correlation function
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle
of LCFT
must have the following form

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle=\frac{U(\alpha)}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im z}}\right|^{2\Delta_{\alpha}}}\quad\textrm{ for }z\in\mathbbm{H},

(1.3)

where
U(\alpha)
is called the
structure constant
and
\Delta_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
is called the
scaling dimension
.
In
[
FZZ00
]
, the following exact formula for
U(\alpha)
was proposed

U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha):=\frac{4}{\gamma}2^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{2^{\gamma\alpha}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}}}\right)^{\frac{Q-\alpha}{\gamma}}\Gamma(\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}-1)\cos((\alpha-Q)\pi s),

(1.4)

where the parameter
s
is related to the ratio of cosmological constants
\frac{\mu_{B}}{\sqrt{\mu}}
through the relation:

\cos\frac{\pi\gamma s}{2}=\frac{\mu_{B}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\sqrt{\sin\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4}},\quad\text{with}\quad\begin{cases}s\in[0,\frac{1}{\gamma}),\quad\text{when}\quad\frac{\mu^{2}_{B}}{\mu}\sin\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4}\leq 1,\\
s\in i[0,+\infty),\quad\text{when}\quad\frac{\mu^{2}_{B}}{\mu}\sin\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4}\geq 1.\end{cases}

(1.5)

Notice
U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
depends non-trivially on
\mu,\mu_{B}
only through the ratio
\frac{\mu_{B}}{\sqrt{\mu}}
,
the dependence being encoded in the intricate relation (
1.5
) defining the parameter
s
.

The main result of our paper is a proof that
U(\alpha)=U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
where
U(\alpha)
is defined in the rigorous probabilistic framework.
Let us now outline the procedure of
[
DKRV16
]
adapted to the case of
\mathbb{H}
in
[
HRV18
]

that allows to give a rigorous meaning to (
1.1
) and thus for
U(\alpha)
.
All definitions will be precisely restated in Section
2.2
. The first step is to interpret the
DX
of (
1.1
) combined with the gradient squared term
|\partial^{g}X|^{2}
of
S_{L}(X)
as giving the law of the Gaussian Free Field (GFF). Concretely let
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
be the probability measure corresponding to the free-boundary GFF on
\mathbbm{H}
normalized to have average zero on the upper half unit circle
\partial\mathbbm{D}\cap\mathbbm{H}
. Define now the infinite measure
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}(d\phi)
obtained by sampling
(h,\mathbf{c})

according to
P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{-Qc}\,dc]
and setting
\phi(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\mathbf{c}
, where
|z|_{+}:=\max(|z|,1)
.
The
\mathbf{c}
is known as the zero mode in physics, which comes from the fact that the gradient
|\partial^{g}X|^{2}
only defines the field up to a global constant, and one must integrate over this degree of freedom.
This construction corresponds to choosing
g(x)=\max(1,|x|)^{-4}
as the background metric in the Liouville action
S_{L}(X)
in (
1.1
).
The term
2Q\log|z|_{+}
comes form the curvature terms of
S_{L}(X)
. As explained in
[
HRV18
]
and
[
RZ22
]
, the choice of the background metric only affects the law of field by an explicit multiplicative constant given by the Weyl anomaly.

To make sense of the effect of
e^{\alpha\phi(z)}
, we let
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\alpha^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\phi_{\varepsilon}(z)}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}(d\phi)
,
where
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is a suitable regularization at scale
\epsilon
of
\phi
. By virtue of the Girsanov theorem,
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z)}
can be realized as a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}
plus an
\alpha
-log singularity at
z
. Lastly to handle the Liouville potentials
e^{\gamma X}
and
e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}X}
present in
S_{L}(X)
,
define the bulk and boundary Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC) measures of
\phi
as the limits (see e.g.
[
Ber17
,
RV14
]
):

\mu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{H})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\int_{\mathbbm{H}}e^{\gamma\phi_{\epsilon}(z)}d^{2}z,\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\phi_{\epsilon}(z)}dz.

(1.6)

Now for
\gamma\in(0,2)
,
\mu,\mu_{B}>0
, set

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle:=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z)}[e^{-\mu\mu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{H})-\mu_{B}\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})}-1],\quad\textrm{for }z\in\mathbbm{H}.

(1.7)

We will explain in Section
2.2
that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle}}\right|<\infty
when
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,
thanks to the
-1
in (
1.7
).
Moreover, for
\Delta_{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
, the quantity
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im z}}\right|^{2\Delta_{\alpha}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle

does not depend on
z\in\mathbbm{H}
. For concreteness, we take
z=i
and set

U(\alpha):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(i)}}}\right\rangle.

(1.8)

Now we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\gamma\in(0,2)
,
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,
\mu,\mu_{B}>0
, we have
U(\alpha)=U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
.

The condition
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
is required for (
1.7
) to be finite, but one can extend the probabilistic definition of
U(\alpha)
and the result to
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
; see Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
4.19
. So far in the probability literature the exact formulas on LCFT have all been derived by implementing the BPZ equations and the operator product expansion of
[
BPZ84
]
,
as first performed in
[
KRV20
]
proving the DOZZ formula. In the setup of a domain with boundary the same technique has been applied in the works
[
Rem20
,
RZ20
,
RZ22
]
, which all compute different cases of boundary Liouville correlations with
\mu=0,\mu_{B}>0
. This method has a major obstruction to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Indeed, in order to define an observable satisfying the BPZ equation, the range of
\alpha
needs to contain an interval of length strictly greater than
\frac{2}{\gamma}
. The best range of
\alpha
for a GMC definition of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle
is
(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
- see Corollary
4.19
- which has length exactly
\frac{2}{\gamma}
and thus is not sufficient. Another less fundamental but technically challenging issue is to reveal the intricate dependence on
\mu,\mu_{B}
in
U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
.
In the next subsection, we explain our strategy based on the conformal welding of quantum surfaces that circumvents these difficulties.

1.2
A proof strategy based on the conformal welding of quantum surfaces

By definition, the FZZ formula describes the joint law of
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})
and
\mu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{H})
in (
1.6
) where
\phi
is a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
.
Here although
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
is an infinite measure we adopt the probability terminology such as “sample” and “law”.
The law of
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})
is encoded in the limiting case of the FZZ formula where
\mu=0
and
\mu_{B}>0
, which has been obtained in
[
Rem20
]
.
Given this result, it turns out that the FZZ formula is equivalent to the statement that conditioning on
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})=1
, the conditional law of
\mu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{H})
is the inverse gamma distribution with certain parameters. Here the inverse gamma distribution with shape parameter
a
and scale parameter
b
has the following density:

1_{x>0}\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\frac{1}{x^{a+1}}e^{-b/x}
.
The crux of this paper is to derive the desired inverse gamma distribution using conformal welding of quantum surfaces.
In this section we sketch this strategy.

Quantum surfaces are the generalization of 2D Riemannian manifolds in the LQG random geometry.
For a fixed
\gamma\in(0,2)
, consider triples
(D,h,z)
where
D
is a domain,
h
is a variant of Gaussian free field on
D
, and
z\in D
.
We say that
(D,h,z)
is equivalent to
(\widetilde{D},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{z})
if there exists a conformal map

\psi:\widetilde{D}\to D
such that
\widetilde{h}=h\circ\psi+Q\log|\psi^{\prime}|
and
\psi(z)=\widetilde{z}
. Under this equivalence relation, the intrinsic geometric quantities in
\gamma
-LQG such
as the quantum area and length measures transform covariantly under conformal maps. Here the quantum area and length are defined by Gaussian multiplicative chaos as in (
1.6
).
A quantum surface with one interior marked point is an equivalence class under this equivalence relation. We can similarly define quantum surfaces with more marked points or decorated with other natural structures such as curves.

For
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
, sample
\phi
from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
and condition on
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})=\ell>0
.
(This conditioning makes sense; see Lemma
4.4
.)
We write the conditional law of the quantum surface corresponding to
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i)
as
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)^{\#}
.
With this notion, the FZZ formula can be reduced to the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)

the law of the quantum area of a sample from
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;1)^{\#}
is the inverse gamma distribution with shape
\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\alpha)
and scale
\frac{1}{4\sin\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4}}
.

When
\alpha=\gamma
, by
[
Cer21
]
and
[
AHS21
]
,
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;1)^{\#}
describes the law of the so-called
quantum disk
with unit boundary length and one interior marked point. In this case, based on the mating-of-trees theory of Duplantier, Miller, and Sheffield
[
DMS21
]
,
Gwynne and the first author of this paper
[
AG21
]
proved that the law of the quantum area
is the inverse gamma distribution with shape
\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\gamma)
and scale
\frac{1}{2\mathbbm{a}^{2}\sin^{2}(\pi\gamma^{2}/4)}
, where
\mathbbm{a}^{2}

is the unknown variance in the mating-of-trees theory, which first appeared in
[
DMS21
, Theorem 8.1]
.

Let us review the mating-of-trees theory.
In our proofs we will only use some of its consequences that can be stated without explicit reference to it.
Hence we will keep our discussion brief and refer to the survey
[
GHS19
]
for more background, especially on its fundamental role in the recent development on the scaling limit of random planar maps.
Recall that the Schramm-Loewner evolution (
\SLE_{\kappa}
) with a parameter
\kappa>0
is a canonical family of conformal invariant random planar curves
discovered by Schramm
[
Sch00
]
. In a nutshell, mating-of-trees theory says that if we run a space-filling variant of an
\SLE_{16/\gamma^{2}}

curve on top of an independent
\gamma
-LQG surface, then this curve-decorated quantum surface can be encoded by a two-dimensional correlated Brownian motion

(L_{t},R_{t})
such that

\Var[L_{1}]=\Var[R_{1}]=\mathbbm{a}^{2}\textrm{ and }\Cov(L_{1}R_{1})=-\mathbbm{a}^{2}\cos(\frac{\gamma^{2}\pi}{4}).

(1.9)

Here the
mating-of-trees variance

\mathbbm{a}^{2}
is an unknown function of the parameter
\gamma
. As a first step towards proving Theorem
1.2
, we identify the value of
\mathbbm{a}^{2}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\gamma\in(0,2)
, the mating-of-trees variance
\mathbbm{a}^{2}=\mathbbm{a}^{2}(\gamma)
is given by

\mathbbm{a}^{2}=\frac{2}{\sin(\frac{\pi\gamma^{2}}{4})}.

We will prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
3
.
Our proof has two ingredients: a systematic understanding of the relation between canonical quantum surfaces and LCFT
developed by the first and the third authors with Holden in
[
AHS21
]
; the explicit boundary LCFT correlation functions computed by the second author in
[
Rem20
]
and in his joint work
[
RZ22
]
with Zhu.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we use the idea of conformal welding which we recall now.
For
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\kappa=\gamma^{2}\in(0,4)
,
if we run an independent
\SLE_{\kappa}
on top of a certain type of

\gamma
-LQG quantum surface, the two quantum surfaces on the two sides of the SLE curve are independent quantum surfaces.
Quantum boundary lengths from the two sides agree on the curve, defining an unambiguous notion of quantum length on the SLE curve.
Moreover, the original curve-decorated quantum surface can be recovered by gluing the two smaller quantum surfaces according to the quantum boundary lengths.
This recovering procedure is called
conformal welding
.
Such results were first established by Sheffield
[
She16
]
and later extended in
[
DMS21
,
AHS20
]
.

In this paper we prove a new conformal welding result that enables us to derive the inverse gamma distribution in Theorem
1.2
.
It asserts the existence of an
\SLE_{\kappa}
type curve
\eta
with the following properties. See Figure
1
for an illustration.

•

\eta
is a simple closed random curve surrounding
i
that visits 0 and otherwise is in
\mathbbm{H}
.

•

Suppose
\phi
is independent of
\eta
and is sampled from the conditional law of
\mathrm{LF}^{(\alpha,i)}_{\mathbbm{H}}
conditioning on
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})=1
.
Let
D_{\eta}(0)
and
D_{\eta}(\infty)
be the bounded and unbounded component of
\mathbbm{H}\setminus\eta
, respectively.
Then as quantum surfaces with marked points,

(D_{\eta}(0),\phi,i,0)
and
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})
are conditionally independent given the quantum length of
\eta
.

•

The conditional law of
(D_{\eta}(0),\phi,i)
conditioning on the quantum length
\ell
of
\eta
is
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)^{\#}
.

•

The law of the quantum area and quantum lengths of the two boundary arcs of
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})
can be
explicitly described in terms of the mating-of-trees Brownian motion
(L_{t},R_{t})
.

Figure 1:

Left:
A curve
\eta
independent of a sample
\phi
from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
.
Right:
The quantum surfaces
(D_{\eta}(0),\phi,i,0)
and
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})
are conditionally independent given the quantum length of
\eta
.
Moreover,
(\phi,\eta)
can be recovered from the two quantum surfaces by conformal welding.

This result is stated as Theorem
4.6
and proved in Sections
4.2
and
5
.
It relies on the conformal welding results for finite area quantum surfaces proved in
[
AHS20
]
.
The law of
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})
is given by a variant of the so-called
two-pointed quantum disk with weight
\gamma^{2}/2
, whose quantum length and area distribution is obtained in
[
AHS20
]
in terms of the mating-of-trees Brownian motion; see Section
2.5
.

Using the above conformal welding result, we can obtain a recursive relation on the law of the quantum area of a sample from
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;1)^{\#}
. Using a path decomposition for Brownian motion in cones, we prove that the only solution to this recursion is the inverse gamma distribution in Theorem
1.2
, which is identified as the law of the duration of a certain Brownian motion in cones.
For technical reasons, we carry out this argument for
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q-\frac{\gamma}{4})
first. This gives Theorem
1.1
for

\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q-\frac{\gamma}{4})
. Since
\frac{\gamma}{2}<\frac{2}{\gamma}<Q-\frac{\gamma}{4}
,
by the analyticity of
U(\alpha)
in
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,
we obtain Theorem
1.1
in its full range
(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,
which in turn gives Theorem
1.2
in its full range
(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
.
It also allows us to extend Theorem
1.1
to
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
for a suitably defined
U(\alpha)
; see Corollary
4.19
.
See Section
4
for the detailed argument.

1.3
Exact solvability for moments of a Gaussian multiplicative chaos

It is natural to look at the limits
\mu\rightarrow 0
or
\mu_{B}\rightarrow 0
in the exact formula of Theorem
1.1
. These limits have the effect of deleting one of the two Liouville potentials in (
1.2
) and the probabilistic expression for
U(\alpha)
then reduces up to an explicit prefactor to a moment of GMC either on the bulk or on the boundary of the domain. In the case of
\mu\rightarrow 0
, see equation (
2.5
) for this reduction. Our main result then reduces in this case to Proposition
2.8
giving an exact formula for the moment of GMC on
\mathbb{R}
. This formula was derived in
[
Rem20
]
and is actually used in our proof of Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand the limit
\mu_{B}\rightarrow 0
provides a novel result on the moments of GMC on
\mathbb{H}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
,
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
, and
\widetilde{\phi}=h-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbb{H}}(z,i)
,
where
h
is a sample from

P_{\mathbb{H}}
. Then

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mu_{\widetilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{H})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\alpha)}}}\right]=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\pi}{2^{\gamma\alpha+2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}}}\right)^{\frac{Q-\alpha}{\gamma}}\Gamma(\frac{\gamma\alpha}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}-\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}})\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\pi\frac{\alpha-Q}{\gamma}}}\right).

(1.10)

A further corollary can be derived if one assumes the moment
\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\alpha)
of the above GMC to be an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is a well-known simple Gaussian computation that a positive integer moment of GMC reduces to a Selberg type integral, see
[
FW08
]
for a review on these integrals.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<n<\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}
. Then the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\prod_{i<j}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{\gamma^{2}}|z_{i}-\overline{z}_{j}|^{\gamma^{2}}}}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{|z_{i}-\overline{z}_{i}|^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}}\frac{1}{|z_{i}^{2}+1|^{\gamma Q-n\gamma^{2}}}}}\right)

(1.11)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{-\pi}{2^{4+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}(1-2n)}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4})}}}\right)^{n}\Gamma(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}n)\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-n).

The integral (
1.11
) ressembles the Dotsenko-Fateev integral of
[
DF84
]
appearing in the context of CFT on the Riemann sphere, with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
being replaced by
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. To the best of our knowledge the above evaluation of (
1.11
) was not known.
Notice that for
\gamma\geq\sqrt{2}
, there are no valid
n
as the first moment of the GMC on
\mathbb{H}
is not finite. For any
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2})
, there are finitely many
n
that satisfy
0<n<\frac{2}{\gamma^{2}}
. Lastly let us note by conformal invariance it is possible to write both of these results on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, see for instance
[
RZ22
, Section 5.3]
for how to link moments of GMC on
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{H}
.

1.4
Perspectives and outlook

In this section we describe several perspectives, ongoing projects, and future directions related to the FZZ formula.

1.4.1
Integrability and the conformal bootstrap for boundary LCFT

In order to carry out the conformal bootstrap for LCFT on Riemann surfaces with boundary, along with the bulk one-point function that we obtained in Thoerem
1.1
, one needs to compute three other correlation functions for LCFT on
\mathbbm{H}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi(s)}}}\right\rangle,\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi(s_{1})}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\phi(s_{2})}}}\right\rangle,\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\phi(s_{1})}e^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\phi(s_{2})}e^{\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}\phi(s_{3})}}}\right\rangle,

(1.12)

where
z\in\mathbb{H}
,
s,s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For the boundary two-point and three-point functions one also has the freedom to choose
\mu_{B}
as a function defined on the boundary
which is constant on each arc in between boundary insertions. See
[
RZ22
, Figure 1]
for a summary.

Along with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle
, these three correlation functions are the “basic” correlations as thanks to conformal invariance they depend trivially on
z,s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
. On the other hand their dependence on
\gamma,\mu,\mu_{B},\alpha,\beta,\beta_{i}
is non-trivial. Explicit formulas for these functions have been proposed in the physics papers
[
FZZ00
,
Hos01
,
PT02
]
.
In the limiting case where
\mu=0
, they reduce to moments of the boundary GMC measure and they have been explicitly computed in the work
[
RZ22
]
. In a work in progress with Zhu, we plan to verify the proposed formulas for all three correlations for general
\mu>0
.
The techniques will be a combination of the tools of the present paper and of
[
RZ22
]
.
Indeed, the approach in
[
RZ22
]
is still applicable to the second and third correlation functions in (
1.12
) as there is no bulk insertion.
By a conformal welding statement similar to the one in this paper, we will get the first one from the second one and the FZZ formula.

Once the above correlations have been evaluated, the next natural step is to compute a correlation function with more marked points or on a non simply connected surface with boundary. For this purpose one needs to implement the conformal bootstrap procedure first proposed in physics in
[
BPZ84
]
. At the level of mathematics this has been achieved to compute an
N
-point function on the sphere in the recent breakthrough
[
GKRV20
]
. One can expect to adapt the methods of
[
GKRV20
]
to the case of a surface with boundary, where the FZZ formula along with the correlations (
1.12
) are a crucial input. More concretely in
[
Mar03
]
a bootstrap equation involving the FZZ formula is proposed to compute the partition function of LCFT on an annulus. We state it here as a conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Consider an annulus represented as a cylinder of length
\pi\tau
and of radius
1
. Let
q=e^{-2\pi\tau}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{Annulus}}
be the partition function (no insertion points) of LCFT on this annulus defined probabilistically in an analogous way to (
1.7
), see also
[
Rem18
]
. Then the following equality should hold:

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{Annulus}}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(Q+iP)U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(Q-iP)q^{P^{2}-\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})dP.

(1.13)

One has the degree of freedom to choose different values of
\mu_{B}
for each of the two boundaries of the annulus, in which case the two
U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}
functions in the right hand side of (
1.13
) must be computed respectively with those two values. The
\mu
parameter is the same for
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{Annulus}}
and both
U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}
functions. Lastly the contour of integration
\mathcal{C}
is a suitable deformation of
\mathbb{R}
avoiding the pole at
P=0
.

1.4.2
Interplay between three types of integrability in conformal probability

Our paper is part of an ongoing program of the first and the third authors
to prove integrable results for SLE, LCFT, and mating-of-trees via two connections between these subjects:
1. equivalent but complementary descriptions of canonical random surfaces in the path integral (e.g.
[
DKRV16
]
) and mating-of-trees (e.g.
[
DMS21
]
) perspectives;
2. conformal welding of these surfaces with SLE curves as the interface.
We now describe other aspects in this program that are closely related to this paper.

•

Our paper relies on the recent work of the first and the third authors with Holden
[
AHS20
,
AHS21
]
.
In particular, our conformal welding result is built on the one proved in
[
AHS20
]
for two-pointed quantum disks.
Our evaluation of the mating-of-trees variance uses the technique from
[
AHS21
]
on the LCFT description of quantum surfaces.

One of the main results in
[
AHS21
]
is an exact formula for a variant of SLE curves called the chordal
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
(see Section
2.4
).
Its proof shares the same starting point with our proof of the FZZ formula: a conformal welding result for quantum disks.

[
AHS21
]
demonstrated how to get integrability results for SLE using LCFT, mating-of-trees and conformal welding.
Using the same methodology and taking the FZZ formula as a crucial input, the first and the third authors proved two integrable results
on the conformal loop ensemble in
[
AS21
]
.
The first relates the three-point correlation function of the conformal loop ensemble on the sphere
[
IJS16
]
with the DOZZ formula.
The other addresses a conjecture of Kenyon and Wilson (recorded in
[
SSW09
, Section 4]
) on its electrical thickness.

•

Our proof of the FZZ formula does not rely on conformal field theoretical techniques such as the BPZ equations and operator product expansion,
except for two exact formulas from
[
Rem20
]
and
[
RZ22
]
; see (
2.6
) and (
3.4
).
In an ongoing project with Da Wu, the first and the third authors aim at using the conformal welding and stochastic calculus methods in SLE to recover these two formulas.
Combined with our paper, this will provide a proof of the FZZ formula which is fully based on SLE, mating-of-trees, and conformal welding.
It is an interesting open question whether such a proof can be given for the DOZZ formula.

•

The first and the third authors are working on verifying our belief
that in a very general sense the conformal welding of two quantum surfaces defined by LCFT can be described by LCFT, where
the conformal welding result (Theorem
4.1
) in this paper is a special case.
For another instance of this statement,
consider a pair of independent Liouville fields on a disk with three boundary insertions. If we conformally weld them along the three boundary arcs,
then the resulting surface should be given by a Liouville field on the sphere with three bulk insertions.
Moreover, the magnitude of an insertion on the sphere is determined by the local rule for conformal welding described in
[
DMS21
]
.
More precisely, in the terminology of
[
DMS21
]
, if we zoom in around an insertion on the sphere the picture looks like the conformal welding of two
quantum wedge
s into a
quantum cone
, which is understood in
[
DMS21
]
. In our present case, if we zoom in around
0
in Figure
1
, the picture will converge to
the conformal welding of three quantum wedges into a single quantum wedge as established in
[
DMS21
]
.

1.4.3
FZZT branes and related models

We review here several models related to the FZZ formula in the theoretical physics literature. Boundary Liouville theory admits two kinds of boundary states, the one studied in the present paper being the so-called Fateev-Zamolodchikov-Zamolodchikov-Teschner (FZZT) brane
[
FZZ00
]
,
[
Tes00
]
. The other is the Zamolodchikov-Zamolodchikov (ZZ) brane which corresponds to LCFT on a disk with the hyperbolic metric as background metric; see
[
ZZ01
]
. In this ZZ brane setup there is also a formula for the bulk one-point function, see
[
ZZ01
, Equation (2.16)]
. Both the FZZT and ZZ branes play a role in the relation between matrix models, integrable heirarchies, and Liouville quantum gravity; see for instance
[
AB19
]
and references therein. Lastly one can consider LCFT on a non-orientable surface, the simplest model being the projective plane, see
[
Hik03
, Equation (3.10)]
for the analogue of
U(\alpha)
in that case.
We hope to adapt our methods to study these directions in the future, see also the review
[
Nak04
]
for more details.

Organization of the paper.
After providing background in Section
2
, we obtain the mating of trees variance in Section
3
.
Then in Section
4
, we carry out the strategy outlined in Section
1.2
modulo the proof of the conformal welding result, whose proof is supplied in Section
5
.

2.1
Notations and conventions

Throughout the paper we assume
\gamma\in(0,2)
is the LQG coupling constant. Moreover,

Q=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{2}{\gamma}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\kappa=\gamma^{2}.

(2.1)

We will work with planar domains including the upper half plane
\mathbbm{H}=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:\Im(z)>0\}
, the unit disk
\mathbbm{D}=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:|z|<1\}
,
and the horizontal strip
\mathcal{S}=\mathbbm{R}\times(0,\pi)
. For a domain
D\subset\mathbbm{C}
, we write
\partial D
of
D
as the set of prime ends of
D

and call it the
boundary
of
D
. For example,
\partial\mathbbm{H}=\mathbbm{R}\cup\{\infty\}

and
\partial\mathcal{S}=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:\Im z=0\textrm{ or }1\}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.

We write
\Gamma
as the unique meromorphic function such that
\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x}dx
for
\alpha>0
.
For
a>0
and
b>0
, the inverse gamma distribution with shape parameter
a
and scale parameter
b
has the following density:

1_{x>0}\frac{b^{a}}{\Gamma(a)}\frac{1}{x^{a+1}}e^{-b/x}.

(2.2)

We will frequently consider non-probability measures and extend the terminology of probability theory to this setting.
In particular, suppose
M
is a measure on a measurable space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
such that
M(\Omega)
is not necessarily
1
, and
X
is a
\mathcal{F}
-measurable function.
Then we say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
is a sample space,
X
is a random variable. We call the pushforward measure
M_{X}=X_{*}M
the
law
of
X
.
We say that
X
is
sampled
from
M_{X}
. We also write
\int f(x)M_{X}(dx)
as
M_{X}[f]
for simplicity.
For a finite positive measure
M
, we denote as its total mass by
|M|

and write
M^{\#}=|M|^{-1}M
as the corresponding probability measure.

Let
g
be a smooth metric on
\mathbbm{H}
such that
the metric completion of
(\mathbbm{H},g)
is a compact Riemannian manifold.
Let
H^{1}(\mathbbm{H},g)
be the Sobolev space whose norm is the sum of the Dirichlet energy and the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to
(\mathbbm{H},g)
.
Let
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
be the dual space of
H^{1}(\mathbbm{H},g)
.
Then the function space
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
and its topology do not depend on the choice of
g
, and it is a Polish (i.e. complete separable metric) space. All random functions on
\mathbbm{H}
considered in this paper will belong to
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
.

2.2
Liouville conformal field theory on the upper half plane

For convenience we present LCFT on
domains conformally equivalent to a unit disk, with the upper half plane
\mathbbm{H}
as our base domain.
Let
h
be the centered Gaussian process on
\mathbbm{H}
with covariance kernel given by

\mathbbm{E}[h(x)h(y)]=G_{\mathbbm{H}}(x,y):=\log\frac{1}{|x-y||x-\bar{y}|}+2\log|x|_{+}+2\log|y|_{+},

(2.3)

where
|x|_{+}:=\max(|x|,1)
and in the sense that
\mathbbm{E}[(h,f)(h,g)]=\iint f(x)\mathbbm{E}[h(x)h(y)]g(y)dxdy
, for smooth test functions
f,g
.
Let
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
be the law of
h
. Using the arguments in
[
She07
,
Dub09
]
it can be shown that
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
is a probability measure on the space
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
defined in Section
2.1
.
For smooth test functions
g
and
f
such that
\int_{\mathbbm{H}}f(z)\,d^{2}z=\int_{\mathbbm{H}}g(z)\,d^{2}z=0
, we have
\mathbbm{E}[(h,f)(h,g)]=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbbm{H}}\nabla g\cdot\nabla f\,d^{2}z
. This is the characterizing property of the free boundary Gaussian free field, which is only uniquely defined modulo additive constant.
The field
h
is the particular variant where the additive constant is fixed by requiring the average around the upper half unit circle to be 0.

Given a function
f\in H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
and
z\in\mathbbm{H}\cup\mathbbm{R}
, let
f_{\varepsilon}(z)
be the average of
f
over
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(z)\cap\mathbbm{H}
.
For
h\sim P_{\mathbbm{H}}
, define the random measures
\mu_{h}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}}d^{2}z
and
\nu_{h}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\gamma^{2}/4}e^{\gamma h_{\varepsilon}/2}dz
, where the convergence holds in probability in weak topology. See
[
Ber17
,
RV14
]
and references therein for more details on this construction.
We call
\mu_{h}
and
\nu_{h}
the quantum area and quantum boundary length measures, respectively, corresponding to
\phi
.
For functions which can be written as a sum of the GFF and a continuous function, quantum length and area can be defined similarly.

Definition 2.1

(Liouville field)
.

Sample
(h,\mathbf{c})
from the measure
P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{-Qc}\,dc]

on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})\times\mathbbm{R}
, and let
\phi(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\mathbf{c}
be a random field on
\mathbbm{H}
.
Let
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}
be the measure on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
which describes the law of
\phi
. We call a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}

a Liouville field on
\mathbbm{H}
.

The following lemma defines Liouville fields with insertions by making sense of
e^{\alpha\phi(z_{0})}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}(d\phi)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
and
z_{0}\in\mathbbm{H}
, the limit
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z_{0})}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\alpha^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\phi_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}(d\phi)
exists in the vague topology. Moreover, sample
(h,\mathbf{c})
from
(2\Im z_{0})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}|z_{0}|_{+}^{-2\alpha(Q-\alpha)}P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{(\alpha-Q)c}dc]
and let

\phi(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,z_{0})+\mathbf{c}\quad\textrm{for }z\in\mathbbm{H}.

Then the law of
\phi
is given by
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z_{0})}
.

Proof.

Sample
h
from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
, fix
c\in\mathbbm{R}
and set
\widetilde{\phi}(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+c
. For a compactly supported continuous function
F
on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})\times\mathbb{R}
, Girsanov’s theorem and
\Var h_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})=-\log\varepsilon-\log(2\Im z_{0})+4\log|z_{0}|_{+}+o_{\varepsilon}(1)
give

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\varepsilon^{\alpha^{2}/2}e^{\alpha\widetilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})}F(h,c)}}\right]

\displaystyle=(2\Im z_{0})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}|z_{0}|_{+}^{-2\alpha(Q-\alpha)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha c}e^{\alpha h_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbbm{E}[h_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})^{2}]}F(h,c)}}\right]

\displaystyle=(2\Im z_{0})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}|z_{0}|_{+}^{-2\alpha(Q-\alpha)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha c}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h(\cdot)+\alpha\mathbbm{E}[h(\cdot)h_{\varepsilon}(i)],c}}\right)}}\right]

\displaystyle=(2\Im z_{0})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}|z_{0}|_{+}^{-2\alpha(Q-\alpha)}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha c}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h(\cdot)+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i),c}}\right)}}\right].

Integrating over
[e^{-Qc}dc]
yields the lemma.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

We call a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z)}

For

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im z_{0}}}\right|^{-2\Delta_{\alpha}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}

[

Suppose

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}[f]=\int f(\phi)\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(d\phi)

Definition 2.5
.

For
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\mu,\mu_{B}}:=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z)}[e^{-\mu\mu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{H})-\mu_{B}\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})}-1]\quad\textrm{for }z\in\mathbbm{H}.

Lemma 2.6

Suppose
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
,
\mu\geq 0
and
\mu_{B}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{B})>0

|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(i)}}}\right\rangle|<\infty

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im z}}\right|^{2\Delta_{\alpha}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(z)}}}\right\rangle

Proof.

By Lemma

\widetilde{\phi}(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)
where

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(i)}}}\right\rangle}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}dce^{(\alpha-Q)c}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1-e^{-\mu e^{\gamma c}\mu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{H})-\mu_{B}e^{\frac{\gamma c}{2}}\nu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{R})}}}\right]}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q-\alpha}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}dce^{(\alpha-Q)c}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mu\gamma e^{\gamma c}\mu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{H})+\mu_{B}\frac{\gamma}{2}e^{\frac{\gamma c}{2}}\nu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)e^{-\mu e^{\gamma c}\mu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{H})-\mu_{B}e^{\frac{\gamma c}{2}}\nu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{R})}}}\right]}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{H})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}(Q-\alpha)}}}\right]+c_{2}\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\nu_{\tilde{\phi}}(\mathbb{R})^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(Q-\alpha)}}}\right].

In the last line above

2.3
Quantum surface and quantum disks

In this section we review the definition of a few variants of quantum disks that will be used in our paper.
Let
\mathcal{D}\mathcal{H}=\{(D,h)\>:\>D\subset\mathbbm{C}\text{ open, }h\text{ a distribution on }D\}

We say

SLE and conformal welding of quantum disks

We now review the conformal welding result proved in
[
AHS20
]

, introduced in
[

]
and studied in e.g.
[

MS16

.
The parameter range relevant for us is
\kappa\in(0,4)
,
\rho_{-}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}-2

\rho_{+}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}-2

In this range, the

on

is a probability measure on simple curves on

from
0
to

, which does not touch
\partial\mathbbm{H}

except at the endpoints. For a general simply connected domain
D
with boundary points
a
and
b
, let
\psi:\mathbbm{H}\rightarrow D
be a conformal map such that
\psi(0)=a
and

\psi(\infty)=b
. The
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
on
(D,a,b)
is defined as the pushforward by
\psi
of the
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
on
(\mathbbm{H},0,\infty)
.
Although there is a degree of freedom in choosing
\psi
, this definition is independent of such choices.
We omit the definition of
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
via the Loewner equation as it will not be needed.

For
W\geq\gamma^{2}/2
, recall
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\ell,\ell^{\prime})
from (
2.8
).
For a fixed

, let

\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W;\cdot,\ell)

3.1

In this section we prove Theorem

[

. Before recalling this result, we first give a concrete realization of a Haar measure on the group of conformal automorphisms of
\mathbbm{H}

Sample
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{r})
from the measure
|(p-q)(q-r)(r-p)|^{-1}\,dp\,dq\,dr
restricted to triples
(p,q,r)
which are oriented counterclockwise on
\mathbbm{R}=\partial\mathbbm{H}

\mathfrak{g}
be the conformal map with
\mathfrak{g}(0)=\mathbf{p},\mathfrak{g}(1)=\mathbf{q},\mathfrak{g}(-1)=\mathbf{r}

\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}

\mathfrak{g}\bullet_{\gamma}\phi=\phi\circ\mathfrak{g}^{-1}+Q\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|(\mathfrak{g}^{-1})^{\prime}}}\right|
from (
2.7

As explained in
[

]

is a (left and right invariant) Haar measure on the conformal automorphism group
\mathrm{conf}(\mathbbm{H})
of
\mathbbm{H}

Proposition 3.5

(Proposition 2.39 of
[
AHS21
]
)
.

Let
M
be a measure on fields
\phi
such that the law of
(\mathbbm{H},\phi)
is
\mathrm{QD}
. If we sample
(\phi,\mathfrak{g})
from
M\times\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}

\mathfrak{g}\bullet_{\gamma}\phi
is

\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}.

Due to the invariance of the Haar measure, the law of
\mathfrak{g}\bullet_{\gamma}\phi
does not depend on the choice of

. This is called a uniform embedding of
\mathrm{QD}
via

in

We will need to following basic fact on

Lemma 3.6

For

where the density of
\mathfrak{g}(i)
is transparent.

Lemma 3.7
.

Define three measures
A,N,K
on the conformal automorphism group
\mathrm{conf}(\mathbbm{H})
on
\mathbbm{H}
as follows. Sample
\mathbf{t}
from
\mathbf{1}_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\,dt
and set
a:z\mapsto\mathbf{t}z
, sample
\mathbf{s}
from the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbbm{R}
and set
n:z\mapsto z+\mathbf{s}
, and sample
\mathbf{u}
from
\mathbf{1}_{-\frac{\pi}{2}<u<\frac{\pi}{2}}du
and set
k:z\mapsto\frac{z\cos\mathbf{u}-\sin\mathbf{u}}{z\sin\mathbf{u}+\cos\mathbf{u}}
. Let
A,N,K
be the laws of
a,n,k
respectively.
Then the law of
a\circ n\circ k
under
A\times N\times K
equals
\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}
.

Proof.

Set
\mathfrak{g}=a\circ n\circ k
.
Then the law of
\mathfrak{g}
under
A\times N\times K
is a Haar measure on
\mathrm{conf}(\mathbbm{H})
; see e.g.
[
DE14
, Theorem 11.1.3]
.
By the uniqueness of the Haar measure (see e.g.
[
Far08
, Theorem 5.1.1]
), the law of
\mathfrak{g}
is
C\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}
for some
C\in(0,\infty)
.
We now check that
C=1
.

Define
E_{\varepsilon}:=\{\mathfrak{g}(0)\in(0,1),\mathfrak{g}(-1)<\mathfrak{g}(0)<\mathfrak{g}(1),\frac{\mathfrak{g}(1)-\mathfrak{g}(0)}{\mathfrak{g}(0)-\mathfrak{g}(-1)}\in(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon),\mathfrak{g}(0)-\mathfrak{g}(-1)\in(1,2)\}
, then by the definition of
\mathbf{m}_{\mathbbm{H}}
we get

A\times N\times K[E_{\varepsilon}]=C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{p-2}^{p-1}\int_{p+(p-r)(1-\varepsilon)}^{p+(p-r)(1+\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{(p-r)(q-p)(q-r)}\,dq\,dr\,dp=C(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1))\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Let

\underline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\{\mathbf{u}\in(-(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2},(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),\mathbf{t}\in(1+\delta,2-\delta),\mathbf{s}\in(0,\frac{1}{\mathbf{t}})\}
and
\overline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\{\mathbf{u}\in(-(1+\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2},(1+\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),\mathbf{t}\in(1-\delta,2+\delta),\mathbf{s}\in(0,\frac{1}{\mathbf{t}})\}
. Since
\frac{z\cos u-\sin u}{z\sin u+\cos u}=z-u-uz^{2}+O(u^{2})
as
u\to 0
with error uniform for
z\in\{-1,0,1\}
, it is easy to check that for fixed
\delta
and sufficiently small
\varepsilon
we have
\underline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}\subset E_{\varepsilon}\subset\overline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
, and

\displaystyle A\times N\times K[\underline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}]

\displaystyle=(1+\delta)\varepsilon\int_{2-\delta}^{1-\delta}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\,dt=(1-\delta)(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{2-\delta})\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\quad A\times N\times K[\overline{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}]

\displaystyle=(1+\delta)(\frac{1}{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{2+\delta})\varepsilon.

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\frac{C}{2}\in[(1-\delta)(\frac{1}{1+\delta}-\frac{1}{2-\delta}),(1+\delta)(\frac{1}{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{2+\delta})]
. Sending
\delta\to 0
,
we get
C=1
. ∎

We first show that:

(3.5)

Then by Girsanov’s theorem (see e.g.

Now,

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}

is an embedding of a sample from
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
.
Let
T\in\mathbbm{R}
be sampled from the Lebesgue measure
dt
independently of
\phi
.
Let
\widetilde{\phi}(z)=\phi(z+T)
.
Then the law of
\widetilde{\phi}
is given by
\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\beta,\pm\infty)}
where
\beta=Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{W}{\gamma}
.

When a sample from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(W)
is embedded as
(\mathcal{S},\phi,i\mathbf{\theta},\pm\infty)
for some
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
then
(\phi,\theta)
are uniquely determined by the marked quantum surface structure. The following lemma is a straightforward variant of Theorem
3.12
that describes the joint law of
(\phi,\theta)
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Fix
W>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}
and
\beta=Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{W}{\gamma}
. When a sample from
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(W)
is embedded as
(\mathcal{S},\phi,i\theta,+\infty,-\infty)
, then the law of
(\phi,\theta)
is
\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\beta,\pm\infty),(\gamma,u)}(d\phi)1_{\theta\in(0,\pi)}d\theta
.

Proof.

By Girsanov’s theorem as in (
3.5
) in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we have

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\beta,\pm\infty)}(d\phi)\mu_{\phi}(d^{2}z)=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\gamma,z),(\beta,\pm\infty)}(d\phi)d^{2}z.

(3.8)

Let
M
be the law of
\phi
in Theorem
3.12
so that the law of the marked quantum surface
(\mathcal{S},\phi,+\infty,-\infty)
is
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
.
Now we sample
(\phi,\mathbf{z},T)
according to
\mu_{\phi}(d^{2}z)M(d\phi)dt
, where
dt
corresponding to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, let
\widetilde{\phi}(z)=\phi(z+T)
and
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{z}-T
.
Then by Theorem
3.12
and the definition of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(W)
,
the law of
(\widetilde{\phi},\mathbf{u})
is

\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\beta,\pm\infty)}(d\phi)\mu_{\phi}(d^{2}z)
.
By (
3.8
) this equals

\frac{\gamma}{2(Q-\beta)^{2}}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\gamma,t+i\theta),(\beta,\pm\infty)}(d\phi)\times 1_{\theta\in(0,\pi)}d\theta dt.

(3.9)

Let
S
be the marked quantum surface
(\mathcal{S},\widetilde{\phi},\mathbf{u},-\infty,\infty)
. Since
\Re u=\Re\mathbf{z}+T
and
dt
is translation invariant,
the joint law of
(S,\Re\mathbf{u})
is
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(W)\times dt
.
Comparing to (
3.9
), we see that for each
t\in\mathbbm{R}
, if
(\phi,\theta)
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\gamma,t+i\theta),(\beta,\pm\infty)}(d\phi)1_{0<\theta<\pi}d\theta
, then the law of
(\mathcal{S},\phi,t+i\theta,+\infty,-\infty)
viewed as a marked quantum surface is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(W)
. Setting
t=0
we conclude.
∎

The following lemma allows us to transfer the LCFT description of
\mathcal{M}_{1,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
from
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathbbm{H}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\Re u=0
. Let
\exp:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbbm{H}
be the map
z\mapsto e^{z}
.
If
\phi
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\alpha,u)}
then the law of
\exp\bullet_{\gamma}\phi
is
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,e^{u})}
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that for
z\in\mathcal{S}
we have
C_{\mathcal{S}}^{(0,\pm\infty),(\alpha,z)}=e^{-\alpha(Q+\alpha)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re z}}\right|+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Re z}(2\Im e^{z})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}
, so for
u\in\mathcal{S}
with
\Re u=0
we have
C_{\mathcal{S}}^{(0,\pm\infty),(\alpha,u)}=C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,e^{u})}
.
Here
C_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z_{0})}=(2\Im z_{0})^{-\alpha^{2}/2}|z_{0}|_{+}^{-2\alpha(Q-\alpha)}
is the prefactor in front of
P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{(\alpha-Q)c}dc]

in the description of
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,z_{0})}
from Lemma
2.2
.
Finally, since the law of
\widetilde{h}:=h\circ\exp^{-1}
is
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
and
G_{\mathcal{S}}(z,w)=G_{\mathbbm{H}}(e^{z},e^{w})
, we see that

\exp\bullet_{\gamma}(h-Q\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Re\cdot}}\right|+\alpha G_{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot,z))=\widetilde{h}-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,e^{z}).

Adding a random constant
\mathbf{c}
sampled from
e^{(\alpha-Q)c}dc
completes the proof.
∎

We only record the LCFT description of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(2+\gamma^{2})
since it is particularly simple,
and it is also the only case we need for our conformal welding result Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Sample
(\phi,\mathbf{x})
from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}\times dx
where
dx
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbbm{R}
.
Then the law of
(\mathbbm{H},h,i,\infty,\mathbf{x})

viewed as marked quantum surface is
\frac{2Q^{2}}{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(2+\gamma^{2})
.

Proof.

Since
W=2+\gamma^{2}
, we have
\beta=Q+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{W}{\gamma}=0
.
By Lemma
3.13
, there is a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that if
(\phi_{1},\theta)
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathcal{S}}^{(\gamma,\theta i)}(d\phi_{1})\mathbf{1}_{0<\theta<\pi}d\theta
, then the law of the marked quantum surface
(\mathcal{S},\phi_{1},\theta i,+\infty,-\infty)
is
\frac{2Q^{2}}{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(2+\gamma^{2})
. By Lemma
3.14
, if we set
\phi_{2}:=\exp\bullet_{\gamma}\phi_{1}
, then the law of
(\phi_{2},\theta)
is
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,e^{i\theta})}(d\phi_{2})\mathbf{1}_{0<\theta<\pi}d\theta
, and the law of marked quantum surface
(\mathbbm{H},\phi_{2},e^{i\theta},\infty,0)
is
\frac{2Q^{2}}{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(2+\gamma^{2})
.

Finally, let
f_{\theta}:\mathbbm{H}\to\mathbbm{H}
be the conformal automorphism fixing
\infty
and sending
e^{i\theta}\mapsto i
(i.e.
f_{\theta}(z)=\frac{z}{\sin\theta}-\cot\theta
).
Setting
\phi:=f_{\theta}\bullet_{\gamma}\phi_{2}
and
\mathbf{x}=f_{\theta}(0)
, the law of
(\phi,\theta)
is
\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}(d\phi)\mathbf{1}_{0<\theta<\pi}d\theta
by Lemma
2.4
, so it is a calculus exercise to check that the law of
(\phi,\mathbf{x})
is
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}\times dx
. On the other hand,
the law of the marked quantum surface
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i,\infty,\mathbf{x})
is
\frac{2Q^{2}}{\gamma}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,2}(2+\gamma^{2})
.
∎

4
Proof of the FZZ formula

In this section we carry out the strategy outlined in Section
1.2
to prove Theorem
1.1
.
In Section
4.1
we make a precise statement (Theorem
4.1
)
about the conformal welding results for
\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma^{2}/2)
that we discussed in Section
1.2
.
We postpone its proof to Section
5
.
In Section
4.2
, we prove the extension of it where the weight of the bulk insertion is generic.
Based on this, for
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q-\frac{\gamma}{4})
we identify the inverse gamma distribution for the area law in Section
4.3
.
In Section
4.4
, by relating the inverse gamma distribution and FZZ formula, we
prove that
U(\alpha)=U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q-\frac{\gamma}{4})
.
In Section
4.5
we show that
U(\alpha)
has an analytic extension
on a complex neighborhood of
(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
, which implies
U(\alpha)=U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{\gamma},Q)
, and hence the inverse gamma law for this range also.
Finally in Corollary
4.19
we extend the probabilistic definition of
U(\alpha)
to the range
\alpha\in(\frac{\gamma}{2},Q)
in a way that matches
U_{\mathrm{FZZ}}(\alpha)
.

4.1
SLE bubble zipper with a quantum typical bulk insertion

Let
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
be the space of counterclockwise simple loops on
\mathbbm{H}
that pass through
0
and surround
i
.
More precisely, an oriented simple closed
loop
\eta
on
\mathbbm{C}
is in
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
if and only if
0\in\eta
,
(\eta\setminus\{0\})\subset\mathbbm{H}
,

i
is inside the bounded component of
\mathbbm{H}\setminus\eta
, and
\eta
surrounds
i
counterclockwise.
For
\eta\in\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
, let
D_{\eta}(i)
and
D_{\eta}(\infty)
be the bounded and unbounded components of
\mathbbm{H}\setminus\eta
, respectively.
The point
0
corresponds to two boundary points on
D_{\eta}(\infty)
which we denote by
0^{-}
and
0^{+}
such that
\eta
goes from
0^{+}
to
0^{-}
.

Recall
\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}(\ell)
and
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2};\ell,\cdot)
as defined in Sections
2.3
and
2.4
.
Our proof of the FZZ formula relies on the following conformal welding equation in the same spirit as Theorem
2.13
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a unique probability measure
\mathsf{m}
on
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
such that the following holds.
Suppose
(\phi,\eta)
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}\times\mathsf{m}
. Then the law of
(D_{\eta}(0),\phi,i,0)
and
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})

viewed as a pair of marked quantum surfaces is given by

C\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}(r)\times\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2};\cdot,r)\,dr\textrm{ for some constant }C\in(0,\infty).

Similarly as in Theorem
2.13
, Theorem
4.1
says that
if we conformally weld
\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
, we get the curve decorated quantum surface whose embedding can be described by
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}\times\mathsf{m}
.
The proof of Theorem
4.1
uses ideas which are orthogonal to the rest of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Therefore we postpone the proof of Theorem
4.1
to Section
5
. As part of that proof we will show that the measure
\mathsf{m}

comes from collapsing the end points of a family of
\SLE_{\kappa}(\rho_{-};\rho_{+})
curves while conditioning on surrounding
i
.
For the purpose of proving Theorem
4.1
, we do not need such a detailed description of
\mathsf{m}
.

4.2
SLE bubble zipper with a generic bulk insertion

In this section we extend Theorem
4.1
from the
\gamma
-bulk insertion to the case of a generic one.
We start by defining quantum disks with an
\alpha
-bulk insertion.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
, let
\phi
be sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
. We write

\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)
as the infinite measure describing the law of
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i)
as a marked quantum surface. Similarly, when
\phi
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i),(\gamma,0)}
as defined in Lemma
3.8
, we write
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,1}(\alpha)
as the law of
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i,0)
as a marked quantum surface.

Remark 4.3
.

(Choice of parametrization)
Weight and log-singularity are two different parameterizations of vertex insertions for quantum surfaces; see
[
DMS21
, Tables 1.1 and 1.2]
for more choices of parameters. We parametrize
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
using weight
W
because the most important property we need for
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(W)
is the conformal welding identity, where the weights are additive.
We parameterize
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)
by the log singularity
\alpha
because this is the one used in the FZZ formula and it behaves nicely under Girsanov transform; see Lemma
4.7
.

By Proposition
3.9
,

\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma)=C\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}
for some constant
C
.
Therefore, Theorem
4.1
says that
modulo a multiplicative constant, conformally welding samples from
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\gamma)
and
\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
,
we get a sample from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}

decorated with an independent SLE curve.
In this section we show that the same holds with
\gamma
replaced by
\alpha
.

We first give an explicit description of the disintegration of
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)
over the boundary length.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
and
h
sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
, let
\widetilde{h}(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)
and
L=\nu_{\widetilde{h}}(\mathbbm{R})
.
For
\ell>0
, let
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)
be the law of
\widetilde{h}+\frac{2}{\gamma}\log\frac{\ell}{L}
under the reweighted measure
2^{-\alpha^{2}/2}\frac{2}{\gamma}\frac{\ell^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)-1}}{L^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)}}P_{\mathbbm{H}}
,
and let
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)
be the measure on quantum surfaces
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i)
with
\phi
sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)
is a measure on quantum surfaces with boundary length
\ell
, and

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell,\quad\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)\,d\ell.

(4.1)

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)
-a.e. we have
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})=\ell
.
To see that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
, we note that for any nonnegative measurable function
F
on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int F(\widetilde{h}+\frac{2}{\gamma}\log\frac{\ell}{L})2^{-\alpha^{2}/2}\frac{2}{\gamma}\frac{\ell^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)-1}}{L^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)}}P_{\mathbbm{H}}(dh)\,d\ell=\int\int_{\mathbbm{R}}F(\widetilde{h}+c)2^{-\alpha^{2}/2}e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc\,P_{\mathbbm{H}}(dh)

using Fubini’s theorem and the change of variable
c=\frac{2}{\gamma}\log\frac{\ell}{L}
.
This yields
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell=\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
, since the left hand side of the above equation characterizes
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell
and the right hand side gives
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
thanks to Lemma
2.2
. The second identity in (
4.1
) then follows from definition.
∎

We state without proof the variant of this lemma for
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
and
h
sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
, let
\widetilde{h}(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\alpha G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)+\frac{\gamma}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,0)
and
L=\nu_{\widetilde{h}}(\mathbbm{R})
.
For
\ell>0
, let
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)
be the law of
\widetilde{h}+\frac{2}{\gamma}\log\frac{\ell}{L}
under the reweighted measure
2^{-\alpha^{2}/2}\frac{2}{\gamma}\frac{\ell^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)}}{L^{\frac{2}{\gamma}(\alpha-Q)+1}}P_{\mathbbm{H}}
,
and let
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)
be the measure on quantum surfaces
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i)
with
\phi
sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell)
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)
is a measure on quantum surfaces with boundary length
\ell
, and

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i),(\gamma,0)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i),(\gamma,0)}(\ell)\,d\ell,\quad\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{1,1}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\alpha;\ell)\,d\ell.

(4.2)

To generalize Theorem
4.1
, we also need to deform our measure on curves.
Given
\eta\in\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
, let
\psi_{\eta}:\mathbbm{H}\to D_{\eta}(i)
be the unique conformal map fixing
i
and
0
.
Let
\mathsf{m}
be the measure on
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
in Theorem
4.1
and
\Delta(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})
. Let

\mathsf{m}_{\alpha}
be the measure on
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)
obtained by reweighting
\mathsf{m}
as follows:

\frac{d\mathsf{m}_{\alpha}}{d\mathsf{m}}(\eta)=|\psi_{\eta}^{\prime}(i)|^{2\Delta_{\alpha}-2}.

(4.3)

We now are ready to state the bubble zipper result for quantum disk with
\alpha
-bulk insertion. We consider only
\alpha>\frac{\gamma}{2}
; for such
\alpha
we have
|\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(1)|<\infty
by Lemma
2.7
.

Theorem 4.6
.

There exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that the following holds. For
\alpha>\frac{\gamma}{2}
,
suppose
(\phi,\eta)
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(1)\times\mathsf{m}_{\alpha}
.
Then the law of
(D_{\eta}(0),\phi,i,0)
and
(D_{\eta}(\infty),\phi,0^{-},0^{+})

viewed as a pair of marked quantum surfaces is given by

C\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{disk}}_{1,1}(\alpha;r)\times\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2};1,r)\,dr.

(4.4)

The proof of Theorem
4.6
is similar in spirit to that of
[
AHS21
, Proposition 4.5]
.
We begin by explaining how a Liouville field with specified boundary length changes under a suitable reweighting.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{\gamma}{2}
. For any
\ell>0
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for any nonnegative measurable function
f
on
H^{-1}(\mathbbm{H})
for which
\phi\mapsto f(\phi)
only depends on
\phi|_{\mathbbm{H}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(i)}
,

\int f(\phi)\times\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2})}e^{(\alpha-\gamma)\phi_{\varepsilon}(i)}d\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}(\ell)=\int f(\phi)d\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}(\ell).

(4.5)

Moreover, the same holds when we replace
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
and
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i)}
with
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i),(\gamma,0)}
and
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i),(\gamma,0)}
.

Proof.

We only explain the proof of (
4.5
) since the same argument works for
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i),(\gamma,0)}
and
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\alpha,i),(\gamma,0)}
.
For a GFF
h
sampled from
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
, let
\widetilde{h}:=h-2Q\log|\cdot|_{+}+\gamma G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i)
.
Let
\theta_{\varepsilon}
be the uniform probability measure on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(i)
and let
\delta:=(2\log|\cdot|_{+},\theta_{\varepsilon})
,
where
(\cdot,\cdot)
means pairings of generalized functions hence
(\cdot,\theta_{\varepsilon})
means average over
\theta_{\varepsilon}
.
Recall
G_{\mathbbm{H}}(x,y)=\mathbbm{E}[h(x)h(y)]
from (
2.3
). We see that
(G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i),\theta_{\varepsilon})=-\log(2\varepsilon)+\delta
.
Thus,
\widetilde{h}_{\varepsilon}(i)=h_{\varepsilon}(i)+(\gamma-Q)\delta-\gamma\log(2\varepsilon)
. Let
\mathbbm{E}=\mathbbm{E}_{\mathbbm{H}}
be the expectation over
P_{\mathbbm{H}}
.
By the description of
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
from Lemma
2.2
,
we have

\displaystyle\int f(\phi)\times\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2})}e^{(\alpha-\gamma)\phi_{\varepsilon}(i)}d\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}=2^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2})}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)(\widetilde{h}_{\varepsilon}(i)+c)}f(\widetilde{h}+c)]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}}(2\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-\gamma)^{2}}e^{(\alpha-\gamma)(\gamma-Q)\delta}\int_{\mathbbm{R}}{\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)h_{\varepsilon}(i)}f(\widetilde{h}+c)]}e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc.

(4.6)

Define
G_{\mathbbm{H}}^{\varepsilon}(z,i):=\mathbbm{E}[h(z)h_{\varepsilon}(i)]=(G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,\cdot),\theta_{\varepsilon})
. Then
G_{\mathbbm{H}}^{\varepsilon}(z,i)|_{\mathbbm{H}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(i)}=G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)|_{\mathbbm{H}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(i)}+\delta
.
By the Girsanov Theorem and the fact that
f(\phi)
only depends on
\phi|_{\mathbbm{H}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(i)}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbbm{R}}{\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)h_{\varepsilon}(i)}f(\widetilde{h}+c)]}e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc=\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)h_{\varepsilon}(i)}]\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}[f(\widetilde{h}+(\alpha-\gamma)G_{\mathbbm{H}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,i)+c)]e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)h_{\varepsilon}(i)}]\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}[f(\widetilde{h}+(\alpha-\gamma)G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i)+(\alpha-\gamma)\delta+c)]e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc.

(4.7)

Since
\Var(h_{\varepsilon}(i))=\iint G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,w)\theta_{\varepsilon}(dz)\theta_{\varepsilon}(dw)=-\log(2\varepsilon)+2\delta
, we have

\mathbbm{E}[e^{(\alpha-\gamma)h_{\varepsilon}(i)}]=(2\varepsilon)^{-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-\gamma)^{2}}e^{(\alpha-\gamma)^{2}\delta}.

(4.8)

On the other hand, with the change of variable
c^{\prime}=(\alpha-\gamma)\delta+c
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbbm{R}}\mathbbm{E}[f(\widetilde{h}+(\alpha-\gamma)G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i)+(\alpha-\gamma)\delta+c)]e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle T_{a}(x)

Lemma 4.17

Let

satisfying

,

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\;y^{b}e^{-\lambda y}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t^{c}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-y^{2}t-\frac{a^{2}}{t}}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}a^{-n+2c-b+1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{b}{2}-c-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{1}{2}).

Proof.

To compute the double integral we expand

and then perform the change of variable

,

. Hence
y=\frac{\sqrt{uv}}{a}
,

,
dydt=\frac{a}{2v\sqrt{uv}}dudv
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\;y^{b}e^{-\lambda y}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t^{c}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-y^{2}t-\frac{a^{2}}{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\;y^{b+n}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;t^{c}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-y^{2}t-\frac{a^{2}}{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dudv\frac{a}{2v\sqrt{uv}}a^{2c}v^{-c}a^{-b-n}(uv)^{\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{2}}e^{-u-v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}a^{-n+2c-b+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}dudv\;u^{\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}v^{\frac{b}{2}+\frac{n}{2}-c-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-u-v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{n}}{n!}a^{-n+2c-b+1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{b}{2}-c-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{b}{2}+\frac{1}{2}).

The conditions on the parameters are such to make the above integrals and series converge. For
n=0
in the sum, the integrals over
u
and
v
converge respectively if
b>-1
and
c<\frac{b}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
. The condition
\lambda>0
is obvious. The condition
\lambda<2a
is required for the series over

to converge, as can be checked on the last line.
∎

Proposition 4.18

For any compact set

Proof.

To start we use the following identity coming from Lemma

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(2i)}}}\right\rangle=\frac{U(\alpha)}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Im 2i}}\right|^{2\Delta_{\alpha}}},

(4.25)

to place the bulk insertion at the location

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle e^{\alpha\phi(2i)}}}\right\rangle

\alpha

Fix a
r_{0}

h
be the free boundary GFF on
\mathbb{H}
, which is normalized to have zero average over the upper half unit circle.
Thanks to the Markov property of the field, see for instance
[
BP
, Theorem 5.5]
, we can write the decomposition
h=h_{1}+h_{2}
, where
h_{1}
is a Dirichlet GFF on
B(2i,e^{-r_{0}})
and
h_{2}
is a field which is independent of
h_{1}
and smooth in a neighborhood of
2i

2i

Consider now the one-dimensional process

obtained by taking the mean of

2i

(h(z))_{z\in\mathbb{H}_{r}}

\mathbb{H}_{r}:=\mathbb{H}\backslash B(2i,e^{-r})

\mathbbm{E}[h_{1,r}(2i)^{2}]=r-r_{0}
and the notation
h_{r}:=h_{1,r}+h_{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{1,r+1}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{1,r+1}(2i)^{2}]}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((h(z))_{z\in\mathbb{H}_{r}}}}\right)}}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{1,r}(2i)^{2}]-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{1,r+1}(2i)^{2}]}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{1,r+1}(2i)-\alpha h_{1,r}(2i)}}}\right]\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{1,r}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{1,r}(2i)^{2}]}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((h(z))_{z\in\mathbb{H}_{r}}}}\right)}}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{r}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{r}(2i)^{2}]}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((h(z))_{z\in\mathbb{H}_{r}}}}\right)}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{r+1}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{r+1}(2i)^{2}]}F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((h(z))_{z\in\mathbb{H}_{r}}}}\right)}}\right].

,

\displaystyle U_{r}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dce^{(\alpha-Q)c}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{r}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{r}(2i)^{2}]}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-e^{\gamma c}\mu_{h}(\mathbb{H}_{r})-e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)-1}}\right)}}\right].

\alpha

V

K

\displaystyle|U_{r+1}(\alpha)-U_{r}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dc\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|e^{(\alpha-Q)c}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\alpha h_{r+1}(2i)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[h_{r+1}(2i)^{2}]}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-e^{\gamma c}\mu_{h}(\mathbb{H}_{r+1})}}\right)-\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-e^{\gamma c}\mu_{h}(\mathbb{H}_{r})}}\right)}}\right)}}\right]}}\right|

E_{n}=\{\nu_{h}(0,y_{n})\in(y_{n}^{\frac{\gamma Q}{2}+2\varepsilon_{n}},y_{n}^{\frac{\gamma Q}{2}-2\varepsilon_{n}})\}
. By the tail bounds,
we have

P_{\mathbbm{H}}[E_{n}^{c}]\leq\mathbbm{P}[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\sqrt{2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y_{n}}}\right|}G}\not\in(y_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}},y_{n}^{-\varepsilon_{n}})]+P_{\mathbbm{H}}[\nu_{h}(0,1)\not\in(y_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}},y_{n}^{-\varepsilon_{n}})]\leq 2e^{-O(\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y_{n}}}\right|)}+O(y_{n}^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}\varepsilon_{n}}).

Here, the first term is bounded from above by the standard Gaussian tail bound.
We conclude that
\sum_{n}P_{\mathbbm{H}}[E_{n}^{c}]<\infty
, so the Borel-Cantelli lemma yields that a.s.
E_{n}
occurs for all sufficiently large
n
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y_{n+1}}}\right|}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y_{n}}}\right|}=1
, this gives that
\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y}}\right|}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log\nu_{h}(0,y)}}\right|}=\frac{2}{\gamma Q}
a.s.
∎

We now give an asymptotic estimate for
\log x_{\delta}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

M
-almost everywhere,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log x_{\delta}}{\log\delta^{-1}}=\frac{2}{\gamma Q}
.

Proof.

Recall from Definition
2.3
that
\phi
sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
is given by
\phi=h-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\gamma G_{\mathbbm{H}}(\cdot,i)+\mathbf{c}
where
(h,\mathbf{c})
is sampled from
2^{-\gamma^{2}/2}P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{(\gamma-Q)c}dc]
.
Thus
\frac{\nu_{\phi}(0,y)}{e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbf{c}}\nu_{h}(0,y)}\in(e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}G_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,1)},1)
for all
y\in(0,1)
, and so
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
-almost everywhere
\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\nu_{\phi}(0,y)}{\log\nu_{h}(0,y)}=1
.
Therefore by the last assertion of Lemma
5.6
, we have

\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y}}\right|}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log\nu_{\phi}(0,y)}}\right|}=\frac{2}{\gamma Q}\quad\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}\text{-almost everywhere}.

(5.11)

By Lemma
2.4
applied to the inversion map
z\mapsto-z^{-1}
, this yields that
\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\log y}{-\log\nu_{\phi}(y,\infty)}=\frac{2}{\gamma Q}
. Setting
y=x_{\delta}
so that
\nu_{\phi}(y,\infty)=\delta
, we get

\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
-almost everywhere
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{\log x_{\delta}}{\log\delta^{-1}}=\frac{2}{\gamma Q}
.
Hence the same limit holds for
M
by restricting to
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})\in(1,2)
. ∎

The following tail bound is quite loose, but suffices for our purposes.

Lemma 5.8
.

For
\zeta\in(0,1)
, there exists
C_{\zeta}\in(0,\infty)
such that

M[\{x_{t}\geq y\}]<C_{\zeta}(t^{4/\gamma^{2}}y)^{-(1-\zeta)}\quad\text{for all }t>0\text{ and }y>1.

(5.12)

Proof.

By the definition of
x_{t}
, (
5.12
) is equivalent to
M[\{\nu_{\phi}(y,\infty)>t\}]<C_{\zeta}(t^{4/\gamma^{2}}y)^{-(1-\zeta)}
for all
t>0
and
y>1
. Using Lemma
2.4
with the inversion map
z\mapsto-z^{-1}
, this is equivalent to

M[\{\nu_{\phi}(0,y)>t\}]<C_{\zeta}(t^{-4/\gamma^{2}}y)^{(1-\zeta)}\quad\text{for all }t>0\text{ and }y\in(0,1).

(5.13)

Recall that
M
is
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
restricted to
\{\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})\in(1,2)\}
. By Definition
2.3
,
\phi
sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}
is
\phi(z)=h(z)-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\gamma G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)+\mathbf{c}
where
(h,\mathbf{c})
is sampled from
2^{-\gamma^{2}/2}P_{\mathbbm{H}}\times[e^{(\gamma-Q)c}dc]
. Since
-2Q\log|z|_{+}+\gamma G_{\mathbbm{H}}(z,i)\in(-\log 2,0)
for
z\in[-1,1]
, we have
2^{-\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbf{c}}\nu_{h}(I)<\nu_{\phi}(I)<e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbf{c}}\nu_{h}(I)
for any
I\subset[-1,1]
. Since
M
-a.s. we have
\nu_{\phi}(-1,0)<2
, for any
r\in\mathbbm{R}
we have

\displaystyle 2^{\gamma^{2}/2}M[\{\nu_{\phi}(0,y)>t\}]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbbm{R}}P_{\mathbbm{H}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(0,y)>t\text{ and }2^{-\gamma^{2}/2}e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(-1,0)<2}}\right]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{r}P_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(0,y)>t]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc+\int_{r}^{\infty}P_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(-1,0)<2^{\gamma^{2}/2+1}]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc.

The first term can be bounded using the second assertion in Lemma
5.6

\int_{-\infty}^{r}P_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(0,y)>t]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{r}t^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}ye^{\frac{2}{\gamma}c}\cdot e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc=\frac{2}{\gamma}t^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}ye^{\frac{\gamma}{2}r},

where
C\in(0,\infty)
is a constant that can change from line to line.
For the second term, by Lemma
5.6
for any
p>0
we have:

\int_{r}^{\infty}P_{\mathbbm{H}}[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}\nu_{h}(-1,0)<2^{\gamma^{2}/2+1}]e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc\leq C\int_{r}^{\infty}2^{(\gamma^{2}/2+1)p}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}pc}e^{(\gamma-Q)c}\,dc\leq Ce^{(\gamma-Q-\frac{\gamma}{2}p)r}.

Combining the two bounds gives:

M[\{\nu_{\phi}(0,y)>t\}]\leq Ct^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}ye^{\frac{\gamma}{2}r}+Ce^{(\gamma-Q-\frac{\gamma}{2}p)r}.

Choosing
r=\frac{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}\log t+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\log y}}\right|}{\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{2}p}
gives (
5.13
) with
\zeta=\frac{\frac{\gamma}{2}}{\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{2}p}
. Varying
p\in(0,\infty)
, we get all
\zeta\in(0,1)
.
∎

The next lemma allows us to control
f(x_{1/2})
.

Lemma 5.9
.

We have
M[|f(x_{1/2})|]<\infty
.

Proof.

We first show there is a constant
C>0
such that

f(y)<C\log y\quad\text{ for all }y>2.

(5.14)

Write
p=M[\{x_{1/2}<0\}]
. By Lemma
5.7
, for
\delta
small enough,
M[\{x_{\delta}<\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}\}]<\frac{p}{2}
.
Let
F_{\delta}=\{x_{\frac{1}{2}}<0\text{ and }x_{\delta}>\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}\}
so that
M[F_{\delta}]\geq\frac{p}{2}
. By Lemma
5.4
we have
|M_{\delta}|=M[f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2})]\geq M[1_{F_{\delta}}(f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2}))]
.
Since
f
is increasing and
f(0)=0
, we have

M[1_{F_{\delta}}(f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2}))]\geq f(\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}})M[F_{\delta}]\geq\frac{p}{2}f(\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}).

By Lemma
5.5
,
|M_{\delta}|=(1+o_{\delta}(1))\mathfrak{C}\log\delta^{-1}
. Therefore there exists
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

f(\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}})\leq C\log\delta^{-1}
for all
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. Setting
y=\delta^{-\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}
and possibly choosing
C
larger
we obtain (
5.14
).

Now we bound
M[|f(x_{1/2})|]
.
By (
5.3
)
f
is an increasing odd function, so

M[1_{x_{1/2}>1}f(x_{1/2})]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M[1_{2^{n}\geq x_{1/2}>2^{n-1}}f(x_{1/2})]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(2^{n})M[\{x_{1/2}>2^{n-1}\}].

(5.15)

By (
5.14
) and the tail bound of
x_{1/2}
in Lemma
5.8
, for a possibly larger
C
we have

M[1_{x_{1/2}>1}f(x_{1/2})]\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C\log 2^{n}M[\{x_{1/2}>2^{n-1}\}]\leq C^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n2^{-n/2}<\infty.

Since
0<f(t)<f(2)
for
t\in(0,2)
, we have
M[1_{1\geq x_{1/2}\geq 0}f(x_{1/2})]\leq f(2)|M|<\infty

hence
M[1_{x_{1/2}\geq 0}|f(x_{1/2})|]=M[1_{x_{1/2}\geq 0}f(x_{1/2})]<\infty
. As
M
-almost everywhere
\nu_{\phi}(\mathbbm{R})\geq 1
, we conclude

M[x_{1/2}<-y]=M[\nu_{\phi}(-y,\infty)\leq\frac{1}{2}]\leq M[\nu_{\phi}(-\infty,-y)\geq\frac{1}{2}]=M[x_{1/2}>y]\textrm{ for }y>0.

Since
f
is an odd function, we have that
M[1_{x_{1/2}<0}|f(x_{1/2})|]\leq M[1_{x_{1/2}\geq 0}|f(x_{1/2})|]<\infty
.
∎

Now, we give the asymptotic upper bound on
f
.

Lemma 5.10
.

For any
s>0
we have
\limsup_{\delta\to 0}|M|f(\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s})/|M_{\delta}|\leq 1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
, we have

\displaystyle|M_{\delta}|

\displaystyle=M[f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2})]\geq M[1_{\{x_{\delta}>\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s}\}}(f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2}))]

\displaystyle\geq f(\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s})(|M|-M[\{x_{\delta}\leq\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s}\}])-M[|f(x_{1/2})|].

Since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}M[\{x_{\delta}\leq\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s}\}]=0
by Lemma
5.7
,
|M_{\delta}|\to\infty
by Lemma
5.5
and
M[|f(x_{1/2})|]<\infty
by Lemma
5.9
we are done.
∎

We next obtain the matching asymptotic lower bound on
f
.

Lemma 5.11
.

For any
s>0
we have
\liminf_{\delta\to 0}|M|f(\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}-s})/|M_{\delta}|\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
E_{\delta}=\{x_{\delta}>\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}-s}\}
. By the monotonicity of
f
we may assume
\frac{2}{\gamma Q}+s<\frac{8}{\gamma^{2}}
, and by (
5.14
) and monotonicity we have

\displaystyle|M_{\delta}|

\displaystyle=M[f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2})]\leq M[1_{E_{\delta}}f(x_{\delta})]+M[1_{E_{\delta}^{c}}f(x_{\delta})]+M[|f(x_{1/2})|]

\displaystyle\leq CM[1_{E_{\delta}}\log(x_{\delta})]+f(\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}-s})(|M|-M[E_{\delta}])+M[|f(x_{1/2})|].

We claim that
M[1_{E_{\delta}}\log x_{\delta}]=o(\log\delta^{-1})
. To see this, we write

M[1_{E_{\delta}}\log x_{\delta}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}M[E_{\delta}\cap\{\log x_{\delta}>w\}]\,dw\leq\frac{8}{\gamma^{2}}\log\delta^{-1}M[E_{\delta}]+\int_{\frac{8}{\gamma^{2}}\log\delta^{-1}}^{\infty}M[\{\log x_{\delta}>w\}]\,dw.

By Lemma
5.7
the first term is
o(\log\delta^{-1})
, and for the second term, let
\zeta\in(0,1)
and use
Lemma
5.8
:

\int_{\frac{8}{\gamma^{2}}\log\delta^{-1}}^{\infty}M[\{\log x_{\delta}>w\}]\,dw\leq\int_{\frac{8}{\gamma^{2}}\log\delta^{-1}}^{\infty}C_{\zeta}(\delta^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}e^{w})^{-1+\zeta}\,dw=(1-\zeta)^{-1}C_{\zeta}\delta^{\frac{(1-\zeta)4}{\gamma^{2}}}.

Thus
M[1_{E_{\delta}}\log x_{\delta}]=o(\log\delta^{-1})
. Moreover,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}M[E_{\delta}]=0
by Lemma
5.7
,
|M_{\delta}|\gtrsim\log\delta^{-1}
by Lemma
5.5
and
M[|f(x_{1/2})|]<\infty
by Lemma
5.9
. This gives the desired result.
∎

Now we are ready to conclude the proof of Proposition
5.3
. We start by the marginal law of
\phi
.

Lemma 5.12
.

The
M^{\#}_{\delta}
-law of
\phi
converges to
M^{\#}
in total variational distance as
\delta\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
R_{\delta}(\phi)=\frac{dM_{\delta}^{\#}}{dM^{\#}}(\phi)
be the Radon derivative of the marginal of
\phi
under
M^{\#}_{\delta}
with respect to
M
.
By Lemma
5.4
, we have

R_{\delta}(\phi)=\frac{|M|}{|M_{\delta}|}(f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2}))\textrm{ almost surely in }M^{\#}.

By Lemmas
5.7
,
5.10
and
5.11
, we see that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{|M|}{|M_{\delta}|}f(x_{\delta})=1
a.s. in
M^{\#}
. Since
\lim_{\delta\to 0}|M_{\delta}|=\infty
and
-\infty<f(x_{1/2})<\infty
a.s. in
M^{\#}
, we see that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}R_{\delta}=1
a.s. in
M^{\#}
. Since
M^{\#}[R_{\delta}(\phi)]=1
for each
\delta
, we have
\lim_{\delta\to 0}M^{\#}[|R_{\delta}(\phi)-1|]=0
. Therefore, with the supremum taken over measurable sets
A
,
\sup_{A}|M_{\delta}^{\#}[A]-M^{\#}[A]|=\sup_{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|M^{\#}[1_{A}(R_{\delta}(\phi)-1)]}}\right|\leq M^{\#}[|R_{\delta}(\phi)-1|]\to 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

We now deal with the joint law of
(\phi,\mathbf{x})
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\hat{m}_{\delta}=1_{\{0<x<\delta^{-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}}\}}p(x)\,dx
.
The
M_{\delta}^{\#}
-law of
(\phi,\mathbf{x})
is
o_{\delta}(1)
-close in total variational distance to

.

Proof.

The conditional law of
\mathbf{x}
given

is
1_{x_{1/2}<x<{x_{\delta}}}(f(x_{\delta})-f(x_{1/2}))^{-1}p(x)dx
. Let
d_{\delta}(\phi)
be the total variational distance between this distribution and
\hat{m}^{\#}_{\delta}
. Let
E_{\delta}=\{|\frac{x_{\delta}}{\log\delta^{-1}}-\frac{2}{\gamma Q}|<1\}
. By Lemmas
5.7
and
5.12
,
\lim_{\delta\to 0}M_{\delta}[E_{\delta}^{c}]=0
. Moreover, by Lemmas
5.5
,
5.10
and
5.11
,
\lim_{y\to\infty}(\log y)^{-1}f(y)
is a positive constant, and hence

\lim_{\delta\to 0}M_{\delta}[1_{E_{\delta}}d_{\delta}(\phi)]=0
. We conclude that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}M_{\delta}[d_{\delta}(\phi)]=0
as needed.
∎

Since the conditional law of

Let

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\psi(z)=-z^{-1}
and
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,\infty)=\{\eta:\psi(\eta)\in\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,0)\}
. By Proposition

,
the law of
(\phi_{\mathbbm{D}}\circ f+Q\log|f^{\prime}|,f^{-1}\circ\eta_{\mathbbm{D}})
can be written as the probability measure proportional to
\int_{1}^{2}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell\times\mathsf{m}^{\infty}

where

is the probability measure on
\mathrm{Bubble}_{\mathbbm{H}}(i,\infty)
describing the marginal law of

. Therefore for some

we have

\int_{1}^{2}\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}(\ell)\,d\ell\times\mathsf{m}^{\infty}=C\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}(r)\times\mathcal{M}_{0,2}^{\mathrm{disk}}(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2};\ell,r)\,dr\,d\ell,

(5.18)

in the sense that when a field and curve are sampled from the left hand side of (
5.18
), the law of the two quantum surfaces cut out by the curve is the right hand side of (
5.18
).

A scaling argument shows that (
5.18
) holds when the integration interval

is replaced by
[e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c},2e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}c}]
for any

2.2
and the change of coordinates
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=\mathbf{c}+c

\phi
is sampled from
\mathrm{LF}_{\mathbbm{H}}^{(\gamma,i)}(\ell)
then the law of

is

. Similarly, if
(\mathbbm{H},\phi,i,0)
has law
\mathrm{QD}_{1,1}(r)
then

, and if

Appendix A
Brownian motion in cones

For
\phi\in(0,2\pi)
let
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}=\{z\in\mathbbm{C}:\arg z\in(0,\phi)\}
be the cone of angle
\phi
. Following
[
LW04
, Section 3]
we will define various measures corresponding to Brownian motion in
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
conditioned on certain probability zero events via limiting procedures. These constructions are in the same spirit as
[
LW04
, Section 3]
, but we also consider Brownian path measures in a cone with an endpoint at its vertex. For more details on the validity of these constructions see
[
LW04
, Section 3]
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the collection of continuous planar curves
Z
defined on a finite time interval
[0,T_{Z}]
, where
T_{Z}
is the
duration
of the curve. Endow
\mathcal{K}
with the metric
d_{\mathcal{K}}(Z_{1},Z_{2})=\inf_{\theta}\{\sup_{0<t<T_{Z_{1}}}|t-\theta(t)|+|Z_{1}(t)-Z_{2}(\theta(t))|\}
with the infimum taken over increasing homeomorphisms
\theta:[0,T_{Z_{1}}]\to[0,T_{Z_{2}}]
. Our Brownian path measures will be non-probability measures on
\mathcal{K}
equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra associated to
d_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Let
\mathsf{m}(z,w;t)
denote the measure on
\mathcal{K}
such that
|\mathsf{m}(z,w;t)|=\frac{1}{2\pi t}e^{-|z-w|^{2}/2t}
and
\mathsf{m}(z,w;t)^{\#}
is the Brownian bridge from
z
to
w
with duration
t
. Let
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(z,w;t)
be the restriction of
\mathsf{m}(z,w;t)
to paths staying in
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
, and let
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(z,w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(z,w;t)\,dt
.

Lemma A.1
.

For each
w\in\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
and
t>0
, pick any
\psi\in(0,\phi)
and define the measure
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(w,0;t):=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{s^{-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}}{\sin(\frac{\pi\psi}{\phi})}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(w,se^{i\psi};t)
. This limit exists and does not depend on the choice of
\psi
. Moreover there is a constant
C=C(\phi)
such that for all
w,t
,

|\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(w,0;t)|=Ct^{-1-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}|w|^{\frac{\pi}{\phi}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2t}}\sin(\frac{\pi}{\phi}\arg w).

(A.1)

Proof.

This result follows from
[
Shi85
, Theorem 2]
except that theorem is about the Brownian motion in
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}
which starts at 0 and ends at
w
.
Our statement follows from the time reversal symmetry of Brownian path.
∎

Define
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(w,0):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(w,0;t)\,dt
and, for
u>0
,
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u,0):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u+i\varepsilon,0)
.

Corollary A.2
.

Suppose
\phi\in(0,2\pi),\theta\in(0,\phi)
and
r>0
. There is a constant
c_{\phi,\theta}\in(0,\infty)
such that

|\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(re^{i\theta},0)|=c_{\phi,\theta}\cdot r^{-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}
. Moreover, the law of the duration of a sample from
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(re^{i\theta},0)^{\#}
is the inverse gamma distribution with shape
\frac{\pi}{\phi}
and scale
\frac{r^{2}}{2}
(recall (
2.2
)).
Similarly, for
u>0
,
|\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u,0)|=c_{\phi}u^{-\frac{\pi}{\phi}-1}
. Moreover, the law of the duration of a sample from
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u,0)^{\#}
is the inverse gamma distribution with shape
\frac{\pi}{\phi}
and scale
\frac{u^{2}}{2}
.

Proof.

These claims are immediate from Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Suppose
\theta\in(0,\phi)
, we describe a path decomposition for
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u,0)^{\#}
where we split the path at the first time it hits the ray
e^{i\theta}\mathbbm{R}_{+}
.
For
z\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, let
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(z)
denote the probability measure corresponding to Brownian motion started at
z
and killed when it exits
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. For
y>0
, let
E_{y,\varepsilon}
be the event that Brownian motion exits
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
on the boundary interval
(ye^{i\theta},(y+\varepsilon)e^{i\phi})
, and let
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(z,ye^{i\theta})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(z)|_{E_{y,\varepsilon}}
. For
x>0
define
\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(x,ye^{i\theta})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(x+\varepsilon i,ye^{i\theta})
.

Lemma A.3
.

For
0<\theta<\phi<2\pi
and
u>0
, we have

\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u,0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(u,re^{i\theta})\times\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(re^{i\theta},0)\,dr

in the sense that when a sample from the right hand side is concatenated to obtain a path from
u
to
0
, the law of this concatenated path is the left hand side.

Proof.

Let
\psi=\frac{\phi+\theta}{2}
.
For
\delta,\varepsilon>0
, by the strong Markov property of Brownian motion we have

\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(u+\varepsilon i,\delta e^{i\psi})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(u+\varepsilon i,re^{i\theta})\times\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(re^{i\theta},\delta e^{i\psi})\,dr.

Multiplying both sides by
\frac{\delta^{-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}}{\sin(\frac{\pi\psi}{\phi})}\varepsilon^{-1}
and sending
\delta,\varepsilon\to 0
yields the assertion.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

For
0<\theta<2\pi
, there exists a constant
C
such that

|\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(u,re^{i\theta})|=C\frac{(ur)^{\frac{\pi}{\theta}-1}}{(u^{\frac{\pi}{\theta}}+r^{\frac{\pi}{\theta}})^{2}}\quad\text{for }u,r>0.

Proof.

This is equivalent to
[
AHS20
, Lemma C.2]
after the shear transform
\Lambda
in Proposition
2.14
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Suppose
0<\theta<\phi<2\pi
. Let
L
be sampled as in (
4.15
). Then for
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{\phi})
we have
\mathbbm{E}[(\frac{L}{u})^{\varepsilon}]<1
.

Proof.

By (
4.14
), Corollary
A.2
and Lemma
A.4
the law of
\frac{L}{u}
is proportional to

1_{x>0}|\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(u,uxe^{i\theta})||\mathsf{m}_{\mathcal{C}_{\phi}}(uxe^{i\theta},0)|dx\;\propto\;1_{x>0}x^{\pi/\theta-1}(1+x^{\pi/\theta})^{-2}\cdot x^{-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}\,dx.

Set
f(x):=\frac{x}{\sin x}
. When
p\in(-1,\frac{2\pi}{\theta}-1)
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{(1+x^{\pi/\theta})^{2}}\,dx=\frac{\theta}{\pi}\frac{\pi-\theta(p+1)}{\sin(\pi-\theta(p+1))}=\frac{\theta}{\pi}f(\pi-\theta(p+1)).

Therefore

\mathbbm{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{L}{u}}}\right)^{\varepsilon}}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\frac{\pi}{\theta}-1-\frac{\pi}{\phi}+\varepsilon}}{(1+x^{\pi/\theta})^{2}}\,dx\bigg/\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\frac{\pi}{\theta}-1-\frac{\pi}{\phi}}}{(1+x^{\pi/\theta})^{2}}\,dx=\frac{f(\frac{\pi\theta}{\phi}-\theta\varepsilon)}{f(\frac{\pi\theta}{\phi})}.

Since
f(x)
is increasing on
(0,\pi)
and
\frac{\pi\theta}{\phi}\in(0,\pi)
, we obtain the lemma.
∎

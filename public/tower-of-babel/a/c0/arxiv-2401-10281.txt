Chaotic properties of an FIR filtered Hénon map Journal: Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation
Vinícius S. Borges, Magno T. M. Silva, Marcio Eisencraft

Abstract
When chaotic signals are used in practical communication systems, it is essential to control and eventually limit the spectral bandwidth occupied by these signals. One way to achieve this goal is to insert a discrete-time filter into a nonlinear map that generates chaotic signals. However, this can completely change the dynamic properties of the original map. Considering this situation, this paper presents a series of numerical experiments aimed at obtaining the Lyapunov exponents of the signals generated by the two-dimensional Hénon map with a set of prototypical finite impulse response (FIR) filters added in the feedback loop. Our results show that the number of filter coefficients and the location of the zeros have a significant and complex impact on the behavior of the generated signals. Therefore, FIR filters should be carefully designed to preserve or suppress chaos in practical applications.

Introduction

A chaotic signal has three main characteristics: it is bounded, aperiodic, and exhibits a sensitive dependence on initial conditions
Alligood2000
. These properties often result in large bandwidth and have stimulated proposals for the application of chaotic signals in Telecommunications and Signal Processing, such as modulation
kennedy2000digital
,
Souza2019
,
Baptista2021
, image encryption
ismail2020novel
,
asgari2019novel
,
zhu2022stable
, 5G technology standards
mohammed2022efficient
,
reena2021chaotic
, ultra-wideband communications
eisencraft2010spectral
, among many others.

Since transmission channels are usually limited in bandwidth
Lathi2009
, knowing and controlling the spectrum of transmitted signals is a relevant problem when using chaotic signals in communication systems. With this in mind, the authors of
Eisencraft2009b
proposed the use of a discrete-time finite impulse response (FIR) filter
Oppenheim2009
in a chaotic signal generator as a way to control the spectral content of the generated signals.

Linear filters have been used to denoise or detect chaotic signals
Milosavljevic2020
,
Werner2017
,
Wang2011
,
Carroll1995
,
Butusov2018
. But in general, their output is not fed back into the generating system. Thus, they can be designed and analyzed in a way that is essentially separated from the chaos generator itself. However, in
Eisencraft2009b
,
Fontes2016a
the filter is inserted into the chaos generator, and its output is fed back through the nonlinear system and through the filter itself. In this way, although the filter is actually linear, the resulting system is highly nonlinear, and the general behavior of the filtered chaos generator cannot be easily predicted.

Subsequently, it was shown that insertion of this filter does not affect chaotic synchronization
Fontes2016a
, which is essential for chaos-based communication. However, an important question has remained open: Since the inserted filters significantly alter the original map, are the generated signals still chaotic?

After the results of
Fontes2016a
, the authors of
borges2022f
analyzed the case of a two-coefficient FIR filter inserted in the feedback loop of a Hénon map. Using Lyapunov exponents, the authors have shown that, despite the simplicity of the filter considered, it can induce complicated phenomena such as cascades of bifurcations, coexistence of attractors, crises, and “shrimps”
varga2020route
,
pati2020bifurcations
. Thus, the design of filters combined with the chaotic map can show dynamics that are difficult to predict and handle.

In this paper, we extend the research of
borges2022f
by considering filters with a larger number of coefficients and investigating the effect of order and zero positions on the resulting dynamics. Due to the methodological challenges of considering many parameters in a dynamical system, we divide our analysis into five numerical experiments considering different prototypical FIR filters.

The paper is organized as follows. The dynamical system under consideration is introduced in Section
2
. The computation of its fixed points and a discussion of the stability of the orbits are presented in Section
3
. The numerical experiments with different FIR filters are presented in Section
4
. Section
5
closes the paper with the main conclusions.

2
The filtered Hénon map

The Hénon map is used as a paradigm to generate chaotic two-dimensional discrete-time signals
Henon1969
,
henon1976two
. It can be expressed by

\begin{cases}x_{1}(n+1)=\alpha-(x_{1}(n))^{2}+\beta x_{2}(n)\\
x_{2}(n+1)=x_{1}(n)\end{cases},

(1)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are real parameters and
x_{1}(n)
and
x_{2}(n)
are the state variables. In particular, in this paper, we consider
\alpha=1.4
and
\beta=0.3
as in
henon1976two
. These parameter values are known to produce chaotic signals, as shown in Figure
1
(a)
, where the state variable
x_{1}(n)
is plotted considering two close initial conditions. As can be seen, the two signals shown in Figure
1
(a)
are bounded, aperiodic and present a sensitive dependence on initial conditions
Alligood2000
.
Figures
1
(b) and (c) show the phase space and the power spectral density of the orbit with initial condition equal to zero, respectively, highlighting the presence of a strange attractor and the aperiodic nature of the orbit.

Figure 1:
(a) Example of chaotic signals generated by (1) with
\alpha=1.4
and
\beta=0.3
. In solid line
x_{1}(0)=x_{2}(0)=0
and in dashed line
x_{1}(0)=x_{2}(0)=0.0001
;
(b) phase space; and (c) power spectral density of the orbit with zero initial conditions
.

To limit the spectral content of the signals generated by the Hénon map, a causal FIR filter
Oppenheim2009
with
N_{z}
zeros and coefficients
c_{j}
,
j=0,1,\cdots,N_{z}
,
N_{z}\geq 1
,
c_{0}\neq 0
, can be applied to
x_{1}(n)
, providing

x_{3}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{N_{z}}c_{j}x_{1}(n-j).

(2)

This filtered signal
x_{3}(n)
is then fed back into the dynamic system, resulting in the
filtered Hénon map

Eisencraft2009b
,
borges2022f

\begin{cases}x_{1}(n+1)=\alpha-(x_{3}(n))^{2}+\beta x_{2}(n)\\
x_{2}(n+1)=x_{1}(n)\\
x_{3}(n+1)=\sum_{j=0}^{N_{z}}c_{j}x_{1}(n-j+1)\end{cases},

(3)

where the state vector is
\mathbf{x}(n)\triangleq\left[x_{1}(n),x_{2}(n),x_{3}(n)\right]
.

In the analysis performed in
borges2022f
, the simplest case
N_{z}=1
was considered in detail, which already leads to complex phenomena, e.g., islands of periodicity in a sea of chaos, known as “shrimps”
varga2020route
,
pati2020bifurcations
. Our goal here is to further extend the results presented there to more general FIR filters. The task is not straightforward due to the intrinsic nonlinearity of the system and the increase in dimensionality with
N_{z}
.

To systematize the analysis, we will write the FIR filter in factored form and define its gain.
Thus, the transfer function
H(z)
of the FIR filter (
2
) can be written as
Oppenheim2009

H(z)\triangleq\sum_{j=0}^{N_{z}}c_{j}z^{-j}=c_{0}\prod_{k=1}^{N_{z}}\frac{(z-z_{k})}{z},

(4)

where
z_{k}
,
k=1,2,\cdots,N_{z}
are the zeros of the filter, i.e., the roots of the polynomial
z^{N_{z}}H(z)
.
Defining the
filter gain
as

G\triangleq H(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{N_{z}}c_{j}=c_{0}\prod_{k=1}^{N_{z}}{(1-z_{k})}

(5)

and assuming that
z_{k}\neq 1
,
1\leq k\leq N_{z}
,
it will be convenient for our analysis to express (
4
) as

H(z)=G\prod_{k=1}^{N_{z}}\frac{(z-z_{k})}{z(1-z_{k})}.

(6)

We begin our analysis by studying the fixed points of (
3
) and their stability in the following section.

3
Fixed points and their stability

To compute the fixed points
\mathbf{p}=\left[p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\right]
of (
3
), we have to solve for

\begin{cases}p_{1}&=\alpha-(p_{3})^{2}+\beta p_{2}\\
p_{2}&=p_{1}\\
p_{3}&=p_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{N_{z}}c_{j}\end{cases}.

(7)

Using (
5
) and replacing
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
in the first equation, we get

G^{2}p_{1}^{2}+(1-\beta)p_{1}-\alpha=0.

(8)

Thus, for
G\neq 0
,

p_{1}^{+,-}=\frac{-(1-\beta)\pm\sqrt{(1-\beta)^{2}+4\alpha G^{2}}}{2G^{2}}

(9)

where
p_{1}^{+}>0
and
p_{1}^{-}<0
. Replacing (
9
) in (
7
), we obtain the fixed points

\mathbf{p}^{+}=\left[p_{1}^{+},p_{1}^{+},Gp_{1}^{+}\right]

(10)

and

\mathbf{p}^{-}=\left[p_{1}^{-},p_{1}^{-},Gp_{1}^{-}\right].

(11)

Interestingly, fixed points depend only on the map parameters (
\alpha
and
\beta
) and on the filter gain
G
, and not on the individual filter coefficients.
The dependence of fixed points on the sum of the coefficients
G
of (
5
) agrees with the results of
borges2022f
where a special case of (
9
) was obtained for
N_{z}=1
.
We also note that for the particular case
G=0
,
(
8
) reduces to a linear equation, and there is only one fixed point at
\left[p_{0},p_{0},0\right]
where

p_{0}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}=2,

(12)

for
\alpha=1.4
and
\beta=0.3
, as we consider here. Figure
2
shows
p_{1}^{+}
and
p_{1}^{-}
as a function of
G
.

Figure 2:
Plots of
p_{1}^{+}
and
p_{1}^{-}
of (
9
). Note that
p_{1}^{-}
diverges and
p_{1}^{+}\rightarrow p_{0}
for
G=0
.

To determine the linear stability of the fixed points, we need to transform (
3
) into a system of
N_{z}+1
first-order difference equations. Then, it can be obtained from the absolute value of the eigenvalues of the associated Jacobian matrix
\mathbf{J}_{N_{z}}\left(\mathbf{x}(n)\right)
evaluated at
\mathbf{p}^{+}
and
\mathbf{p}^{-}

Alligood2000
,
strogatz2018nonlinear
. The calculation of
\mathbf{J}_{N_{z}}\left(\mathbf{x}(n)\right)
is presented in
A
.

Although the coordinates of the fixed points depend only on
G
, our calculations in
A
show that
\mathbf{J}_{N_{z}}\left(\mathbf{x}(n)\right)
, and consequently their linear stability, is more subtle, depending on the full set of filter coefficients. Our numerical analysis shows that
\mathbf{p}^{-}
is always linearly unstable. This is not the case for
\mathbf{p}^{+}
. Therefore, in Section
4
, we numerically investigate its stability by considering different prototype filters that lead to different sets of coefficients.
If
\mathbf{p}^{+}
is unstable, the orbits of (
3
) can converge to a periodic or chaotic attractor or diverge to infinity. To distinguish bounded orbits as periodic or chaotic, we numerically estimate their largest Lyapunov exponent
\Lambda
using the tangent map technique
Alligood2000
. An orbit with
\Lambda>0
is considered a chaotic orbit, and an orbit with
\Lambda<0
is considered to converge to a periodic or quasiperiodic attractor.

5
Conclusions

In recent years, several applications of dynamic systems that generate chaotic signals have appeared in engineering and applied physics. In some of them, limiting the frequency content of chaotic signals is fundamental, which can be achieved by inserting linear filters in their description. However, the influence of these filters on the asymptotic behavior of the trajectories is not fully understood. In this sense, in this work, we present numerical experiments
using the recently proposed filtered Hénon map
borges2022f
to evaluate which characteristics (gain, position, parity, and number of zeros) of an FIR filter are relevant in terms of asymptotic dynamics.

Interestingly, we found that the fixed points of the filtered Hénon map using a generic FIR filter depend only on the filter coefficients through the filter gain
G
. Specific values of
c_{j}
do not affect them. However, their stability is more subtle and depends on the composition of the filter coefficients. Our numerical experiments show that for the different filters tested, there is a range of
G
that produces chaotic and periodic orbits. Above this range, the orbits diverge and below it, the fixed points are stable.

In summary, the incorporation of linear filters into a dynamical system can significantly change the nature of the generated signals.
When using maps that produce signals known to be chaotic, it cannot be assumed that this property will be maintained when filters, even linear ones, are introduced in the system. This variation in dynamics as a function of the filters employed warns of the need to carefully evaluate proposals for bandlimited chaos-based communication systems.

This article leaves several possibilities of future research. For instance: i)
the effects of the introduction of filters on
the properties associated with the generation of pseudorandom sequences using the Hénon map
MeranzaCastillon2019
,
Kocarev2003
; ii) the possibility of using a symmetric version of the Hénon map
Butusov2018a
,
tutueva2022fast
; iii) the influence of digital noise and truncation on the chaotic properties of the orbits; and iv) the effects of the insertion of infinite impulse response (IIR) or nonlinear filters into the chaos generator.

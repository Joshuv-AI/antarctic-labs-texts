Group synchrony, parameter mismatches, and intragroup connections
Shirin Panahi, Francesco Sorrentino

Abstract
Group synchronization arises when two or more synchronization patterns coexist in a network formed of oscillators of different types, with the systems in each group synchronizing on the same time-evolution, but systems in different groups synchronizing on distinct time-evolutions. Group synchronization has been observed and characterized when the systems in each group are identical and the couplings between the systems satisfy specific conditions. By relaxing these constraints and allowing them to be satisfied in an approximate rather than exact way, we observe that stable group synchronization may still occur in the presence of small deviations of the parameters of the individual systems and of the couplings from their nominal values. We analyze this case and provide necessary and sufficient conditions for stability through a master stability function approach, which also allows us to quantify the synchronization error. We also investigate the stability of group synchronization in the presence of intra group connections and for this case, extend some of the existing results in the literature. Our analysis points out a broader class of matrices describing intra group connections for which the stability problem can be reduced in a low-dimensional form.



I
Introduction

A large literature has considered synchronization in networks of coupled oscillators, see for example the review in Ref.
Arenas et al. 2008
. In the case of identical oscillators, stability of the synchronous solution for arbitrary network topologies can be investigated through the master stability function approach, introduced in Ref.
Pecora and Carroll 1998
. More complex patterns of synchronous dynamics may arise in networks formed of different types of oscillators.
Group synchronization in networks of coupled oscillators was first analyzed in
Sorrentino and Ott 2007
, followed by further analysis in Ref.
Dahms et al. 2012
and an experimental realization of group synchronization
Williams et al. 2013
. Moreover, group synchronization in networks of fractional-order chaotic oscillators was investigated in Ref.
Yang 2015
.

According to the definition in Ref.
Sorrentino and Ott 2007
, a
group
is a set of oscillators characterized by the same (uncoupled) dynamics, with oscillators of different types belonging to different groups. Group synchronization is achieved when the oscillators in each group synchronize on the same time evolution, with these synchronized dynamics being different from group to group. Cluster synchronization can be considered as a particular case of group synchronization, but for which the oscillators in different clusters are of the same type. Most recently, a canonical transformation for simultaneous block diagonalization of matrices has been proposed to decouple the cluster synchronization stability problem into subproblems of minimal dimensionality
Panahi et al. 2021
. The more general case of cluster synchronization in multilayer networks formed of oscillators of different types has been studied in Ref.
Della Rossa et al. 2020
.

An experimental realization of group synchronization was performed in Ref.
Williams et al. 2013
, where stable group synchronization was observed under different configurations for a small network of four coupled optoelectronic oscillators. This observation suggests that group synchronization is robust with respect to small parametric mismatches that are inevitable in experiments. The analysis of stability of the group-synchronous solution in the presence of such small parametric mismatches (affecting both the individual nodes being coupled and the strengths of the couplings between them) is studied in this paper. The effects of parameter mismatches in a network of
identical
oscillators achieving complete synchronization have been studied in Refs.
Restrepo et al. 2004
;
Sun et al. 2009
;
Sorrentino and Porfiri 2011
and achieving cluster synchronization in Refs.
Sorrentino and
Pecora 2016
;
Cho 2019
. A second order expansion to study complete synchronization in networks of identical nodes with parameter mismatches has been studied in Refs.
Acharyya and
Amritkar 2012
;
Acharyya and
Amritkar 2015
. However, to the best of our knowledge, no paper has investigated the role of parameter mismatches in networks formed of systems of different types, which is the main focus of this paper. The other subject of this paper is the study of the conditions under which group synchronization may arise in the presence of intragroup connections, which was first considered in Ref.
Dahms et al. 2012
.

II.1
Spectrum of the matrix
\tilde{M}

We have already stated that the singular values of the matrix
\tilde{A}
can be computed from the eigenvalues of the matrix
\tilde{M}
. We want to show that there is a direct relation between the eigenvalues of the matrix
\tilde{M}
and those of the
nominal matrix

M^{NOM}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A^{NOM}\\
{A^{NOM}}^{T}&0\end{array}\right),

(36)

where here we retain the assumption (with no loss of generality) that the sum of the entries in the rows of the matrix
A^{NOM}
is equal to
1
. This also implies that the sum of the entries in the columns of the matrix
A^{NOM}
is equal to
1/e
.
We know from Ref.
Sorrentino and Ott 2007
that the spectrum of the matrix

M^{NOM}
is characterized by the following properties: (i) the spectrum is symmetric with respect to the real and imaginary axes, (ii) at least

d=(N_{x}-N_{y})
eigenvalues are equal to zero, (iii) two eigenvalues are equal to
+\sqrt{e^{-1}}
and
-\sqrt{e^{-1}}
and (iv) the eigenvectors of the matrix
M^{NOM}
,

\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}=[\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa}\quad\boldsymbol{v}^{y\kappa}]^{T}
can be of either one of two different types:

•

Type I eigenvectors associated with eigenvalues
\pm\sqrt{e^{-1}}
,
\boldsymbol{v}^{1}=[\underbrace{1,1,...,1}_{\text{$N_{x}$ times}},\underbrace{0,0,...,0}_{\text{$N_{y}$ times}}]
and
\boldsymbol{v}^{2}=[\underbrace{0,0,...,0}_{\text{$N_{x}$ times}},\underbrace{1,1,...,1}_{\text{$N_{y}$ times}}]
. These correspond to modes parallel to the synchronization
manifold.

•

Type II eigenvectors associated with the remaining
(N_{x}+N_{y}-2)
eigenvalues, for which
\sum_{l=1}^{N_{x}}v^{x\kappa}_{l}=0
and
\sum_{l=1}^{N_{y}}v^{y\kappa}_{l}=0
,
\kappa=3,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
. These correspond to modes orthogonal to the synchronization
manifold.

In the first part of this Section we have seen that, in case of parametric mismatches, the master stability function (
32
) and the extended master stability function (
33
)
depend on the eigenvalues of the matrix
\tilde{M}
rather than those of the nominal matrix
M^{NOM}
. We want to show that the spectrum of the matrix
\tilde{M}
is closely related to that of the matrix
M^{NOM}
, namely,

M^{NOM}\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}=\varsigma^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}\Leftrightarrow\tilde{M}\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}=\vartheta^{k}\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa},

(37)

where (a)
\vartheta^{1}=\vartheta^{2}=0
and (b)
\vartheta^{\kappa}=\varsigma^{\kappa}
,
\kappa=3,..,N_{x}+N_{y}
.

To prove proberty (a) we just need to observe that from the structure of the matrix
\tilde{M}
in (
18
) and the property that the sums over the entries in the rows and in the columns of the matrix
\tilde{A}
is equal to zero, it follows that
\tilde{M}\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{0}
for
\kappa=1,2
. Hence,
\boldsymbol{v}^{1}
and
\boldsymbol{v}^{2}
are still eigenvectors for the matrix
\tilde{M}
but with associated eigenvalue
0
.

To prove property (b), we see that the left eigenvalue equation for the matrix
\tilde{M}
corresponds to the following equations:
\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa^{T}}\tilde{A}=\varsigma^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}^{y\kappa^{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{v}^{y\kappa^{T}}\tilde{A}^{T}=\varsigma^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa^{T}}
. We then note that from Eq. (
14
) we can write that the matrix
\tilde{A}=(A^{NOM}-\Delta)
, where the entries over column
j
of the matrix
\Delta
are all the same and equal to
a_{j}
,
j=1,...,N_{y}
. Then for
\kappa=3,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
,
\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa^{T}}\tilde{A}=\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa^{T}}({A}^{NOM}-\Delta)=\varsigma^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{v}^{y\kappa^{T}}
since
\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa^{T}}\Delta=\boldsymbol{0}
from the property that the sum over the entries of the vector
\boldsymbol{v}^{x\kappa}
is equal to zero, for
\kappa=3,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
.
Hence, for
\kappa=3,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
,
\boldsymbol{v}^{\kappa}
is still an eigenvector for the matrix
\tilde{M}
with associated eigenvalue
\varsigma^{\kappa}
.

III
Couplings among members of the same group

So far we have only considered coupling from the
\boldsymbol{X}
group to the
\boldsymbol{Y}
group and vice versa. While in general group synchronization is possible in the presence of intra group connections, stability of the group synchronous solution when also intra group connections are present has not been fully elucidated. This problem was investigated in Ref.
Dahms et al. 2012
, where conditions were presented for the dimensionality reduction of the stability problem. Here we will extend some of the results in Ref.
Dahms et al. 2012
and we will show that the stability of group synchronization can be reduced in a low-dimensional form for a broader class of networks.

Consider two groups of coupled oscillators, described by the following equations:

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{i}

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}A_{ij}H(\boldsymbol{y}_{j})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{x}}C_{i\ell}R(\boldsymbol{x}_{\ell}),\quad i=1,\ldots,N_{x},

(44a)

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{y}}_{j}

\displaystyle=G(\boldsymbol{y}_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}B_{ji}L(\boldsymbol{y}_{i})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{y}}D_{j\ell}S(\boldsymbol{y}_{\ell}),\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{y},

(44b)

where the output functions
R:\mathbf{R}^{n_{x}}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n_{x}}
and
S:\mathbf{R}^{n_{y}}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{n_{y}}
and the
N_{x}
-square matrix
C
and the
N_{y}
-square matrix
D
define the intra group connections of group
\boldsymbol{X}
and group
\boldsymbol{Y}
, respectively.

If
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}A_{ij}=a\neq 0
and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{x}}C_{i\ell}=c\neq 0
independent of
i
and
\sum_{i}^{N_{x}}B_{ji}=b\neq 0
and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{y}}D_{j\ell}=d\neq 0
independent of
j
, a synchronous solution exists
x_{1}=x_{2}=...=x_{N_{x}}=x_{s}
,

y_{1}=y_{2}=...=y_{N_{y}}=y_{s}
, obeying,

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{s}

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})+aH(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})+cR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})

(45a)

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{y}}_{s}

\displaystyle=G(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})+bL(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})+dS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s}).

(45b)

Note that by appropriately rescaling the functions
\boldsymbol{H},\boldsymbol{L},\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{S}
it is always possible to set
a=b=c=d=1
. Therefore, without loss of generality, in what follows we will proceed under this assumption.

In order to analyze stability, we linearize (
44
) about the synchronous solution,

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{i}

\displaystyle=DF(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{x}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}A_{ij}DH(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{y}_{j}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{x}}C_{i\ell}DR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{x}_{\ell},\quad i=1,...,N_{x}

(46a)

\displaystyle\delta\dot{\boldsymbol{y}}_{j}

\displaystyle=DG(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{y}_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{y}}B_{ji}DL(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{x}_{i}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{y}}D_{j\ell}DS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\delta\boldsymbol{y}_{\ell},\quad j=1,...,N_{y}.

(46b)

In
Dahms et al. 2012
it was shown that Eq. (
46
) can be reduced in a low-dimensional form, provided that the matrix
N=\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
0&D\end{bmatrix}
has the same set of eigenvectors as the matrix
M=\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\
B&0\end{bmatrix}
. It was thus concluded in
Dahms et al. 2012
that if this condition is satisfied, in a similar way to the case of bipartite topologies, stability of the synchronous solution can be reduced in the following low dimensional form,

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DF(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+{\lambda^{\kappa}}DH(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\eta^{\kappa}DR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}

(47a)

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DG(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\lambda^{\kappa}DL(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+\eta^{\kappa}DS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa},

(47b)

\kappa=1,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
, where
\lambda^{\kappa}
and
\eta^{\kappa}
are the eigenvalues associated with the same eigenvector of the matrices
M
and
N
, respectively. By construction, there are always two
\kappa
eigenvectors which are associated with perturbations tangent to the synchronization manifold, and the corresponding pairs of eigenvalues
(\lambda^{\kappa},\eta^{\kappa})
do not need to be considered in order to assess stability. Without loss of generality, we label
\kappa=1
and
\kappa=2
the two tangent eigenmodes and
\kappa=3,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
the remaining transverse eigenmodes.

In what follows we extend the results in
Dahms et al. 2012
and show that a reduction in a low dimensional form is possible for any pair of matrices

\displaystyle C=C^{\prime}+J^{x},

(48a)

\displaystyle D=D^{\prime}+J^{y}.

(48b)

where (i) the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}C^{\prime}&0\\
0&D^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}
has the same set of eigenvectors as the matrix
M
and (ii)
J^{x}
and
J^{y}
are any two
N_{x}
-square and
N_{y}
-square matrices whose columns are composed of entries that are all the same. This form of the matrices
J^{x}
and
J^{y}
corresponds to a particular coupling configuration for which for any pair of nodes
(i,j)
in either group
\boldsymbol{X}
or group
\boldsymbol{Y}
, the coupling strength from node
i
to node
j
is a function of
i
but not of
j
.

Using the ansatz
\delta\boldsymbol{x}_{i}(t)=p_{i}^{\kappa}\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}(t)
and
\delta\boldsymbol{y}_{j}(t)=q_{j}^{\kappa}\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}(t)
in (
46
), we obtain,

\displaystyle{p_{i}^{\kappa}}\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DF(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}){p_{i}^{\kappa}}\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}A_{ij}{q_{j}^{\kappa}}DH(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{x}}C_{i\ell}DR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})p_{\ell}^{\kappa}}\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}

(49a)

\displaystyle{q_{j}^{\kappa}}\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DG(\boldsymbol{y}_{s}){q_{j}^{\kappa}}\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}B_{ji}{p_{i}^{\kappa}}DL(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{y}}D_{j\ell}DS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})q_{\ell}^{\kappa}}\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}

(49b)

We now consider the left eigenvalue equation,

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\kappa^{T}}M=\lambda^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\kappa^{T}},

(50)

where the eigenvectors

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\kappa^{T}}=\Big(p_{1}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},p_{2}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},\dots,p_{N_{x}}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},q_{1}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},q_{2}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},\dots,q_{N_{y}}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}\Big)
.
By multiplying (
49
a) by
p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}
, summing over
i
, and dividing by
(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}{p_{i}^{\kappa}})
[by multiplying (
49
b)
by
q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}
, summing over
j
, and dividing by
(\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}{q_{j}^{\kappa}})
], we obtain,

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DF(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+{\lambda^{\kappa}}DH(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\eta^{\kappa}DR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+\frac{\Big(\sum_{\ell}\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}J^{x}_{i\ell}p_{\ell}^{\kappa}\Big)}{\Big(\sum_{i}{p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}}{p_{i}^{\kappa}}\Big)}DR(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa},

(51a)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=DG(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\lambda^{\kappa}DL(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\kappa}+\eta^{\kappa}DS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa}+\frac{\Big(\sum_{\ell}\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}J^{y}_{j\ell}q_{\ell}^{\kappa}\Big)}{\Big(\sum_{j}{q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}}{q_{j}^{\kappa}}\Big)}DS(\boldsymbol{y}_{s})\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\kappa},

(51b)

\kappa=1,...,N_{x}+N_{y}
.

We shall now show that
\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}J^{x}_{i\ell}=0
, for
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
in Eq. (
51
a) and
\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}J^{y}_{j\ell}=0
, for
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
in Eq. (
51
b). Since by definition the matrices
J^{x}
and
J^{y}
have columns whose entries are all the same, it suffices to show that
\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}=0
, for
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
and
\sum_{j}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}=0
, for
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
. We will prove that this latter property is indeed satisfied by the
(N_{x}+N_{y}-2)
eigenvectors that are associated with the transverse eigenmodes of the matrix

.

Note that the left eigenvalue equation
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\kappa^{T}}{M}=\lambda^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\kappa^{T}}
implies the following two equations:
\boldsymbol{q}^{\kappa^{T}}{B}=\lambda^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{p}^{\kappa^{T}}
and
\boldsymbol{p}^{\kappa^{T}}{A}=\lambda^{\kappa}\boldsymbol{q}^{\kappa^{T}}
where the vectors
\boldsymbol{p}^{\kappa^{T}}=\Big(p_{1}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},p_{2}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},\dots,p_{N_{x}}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}\Big)
and
\boldsymbol{q}^{\kappa^{T}}=\Big(q_{1}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},q_{2}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}},\dots,q_{N_{y}}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}\Big)
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}{B}_{ji}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\kappa}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}

(52a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}{A}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\kappa}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}.

(52b)

By summing Eq. (
52
a) over
i=1,...,N_{x}
and Eq. (
52
b) over
j=1,...,N_{y}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\kappa}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}

(53a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}p_{i}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\kappa}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}.

(53b)

By plugging (
53

53

\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}=(\lambda^{\kappa})^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}
, which can be satisfied in two possible ways: either
\lambda^{\kappa}=\pm 1
, which applies to the parallel eigenmodes
\kappa=1,2
of the matrix
M
or
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}q_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}=0
, which applies to the transverse eigenmodes
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
of the matrix
M
. Analogously, by plugging (
53

53

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}p_{j}^{\kappa^{\mathit{l}}}=0
for
\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
.

We can thus conclude that Eq. (

47

\kappa=3,...,(N_{x}+N_{y})
[while in general it does not reduce to (
47

\kappa=1,2
]. This implies that when intra group connections are in the general form of Eq. (
48

47
).

Finally, we note that the analysis of parameter mismatches presented in Sec.
II
can be extended to the case that small mismatches affect the intra group connections. In particular, if the intra group connections are affected by small mismatches but are in a form that approximately satisfies Eq. (
48
), then the low-dimensional system (
47
) can still be used to predict stability of the synchronous state.

IV
Conclusions

The main contribution of this paper is to extend the analysis of the effects of parameter mismatches on the stability of the network synchronous solution
Restrepo et al. 2004
;
Sun et al. 2009
;
Sorrentino and Porfiri 2011
;
Cho 2019
;
Sorrentino and
Pecora 2016
;
Acharyya and
Amritkar 2012
;
Acharyya and
Amritkar 2015

to the case of networks formed of systems of different types. An important reference for our work is Ref.
Sun et al. 2009
which introduced a master stability function to characterize the synchronization error in networks with parameter mismatches.

Group synchronization was first studied in
Sorrentino and Ott 2007
and has been the subject of both theoretical
Dahms et al. 2012
and experimental
Williams et al. 2013

In this paper, we have defined a master stability function that describes stability of the group synchronization solution in the presence of mismatches on the individual parameters of the network oscillators and on the couplings between the oscillators in the groups.

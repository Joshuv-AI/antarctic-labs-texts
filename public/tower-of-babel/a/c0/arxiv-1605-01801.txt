Abstract
. In this article we present an L p L_{p} -theory ( p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 ) for the time-fractional quasi-linear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) of type ∂ t α u = L ⁡ ( ω , t , x ) ​ u + f ⁡ ( u ) + ∂ t β ∑ k = 1 ∞ ∫ 0 t ( Λ k ​ ( ω , t , x ) ​ u + g k ​ ( u ) ) ​ d ​ w t k , \partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=L(\omega,t,x)u+f(u)+\partial^{\beta}_{t}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int^{t}_{0}(\Lambda^{k}(\omega,t,x)u+g^{k}(u))dw^{k}_{t}, where α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) , β < α + 1 2 \beta<\alpha+\frac{1}{2} , and ∂ t α \partial^{\alpha}_{t} and ∂ t β \partial^{\beta}_{t} denote the Caputo derivative of order α \alpha and β \beta respectively. The processes w t k w^{k}_{t} , k ∈ ℕ = { 1 , 2 , ⋯ } k\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\cdots\} , are independent one-dimensional Wiener processes defined on a probability space Ω \Omega , L L is a second order operator of either divergence or non-divergence type, and Λ k \Lambda^{k} are linear operators of order up to two. The coefficients of the equations depend on ω ( ∈ Ω ) , t , x \omega(\in\Omega),t,x and are allowed to be discontinuous. This class of SPDEs can be used to describe random effects on transport of particles in medium with thermal memory or particles subject to sticking and trapping. We prove uniqueness and existence results of strong solutions in appropriate Sobolev spaces, and obtain maximal L p L_{p} -regularity of the solutions. Our result certainly includes the L p L_{p} -theory for SPDEs driven by space-time white noise if the space dimension d < 4 − 2 ​ ( 2 ​ β − 1 ) ​ α − 1 d<4-2(2\beta-1)\alpha^{-1} . This is a quite interesting result since in case of the classical SPDE, i.e. α = β = 1 \alpha=\beta=1 , d d must be one.

First we introduce some elementary facts related to the fractional calculus.

For
\varphi\in L_{1}((0,T))

I^{n}_{t}\varphi(t):=\int^{t}_{0}(I^{n-1}\varphi)(s)ds),\quad\quad(I^{0}_{t}\varphi:=\varphi).

In general, the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral
of the order
\alpha\geq 0
is defined as

I_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\varphi(s)ds,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

By Jensen’s inequality, for

,

\left\|I_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi\right\|_{L_{p}(0,T)}\leq N(T,\alpha)\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{p}(0,T)}.

is well-defined and finite for almost all

. This inequality shows that if

and
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
L_{p}([0,T])
, then

also converges to
I^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi
in
L_{p}([0,T])
. The inequality for
p=\infty
implies that if

converges in probability uniformly in

[0,T]
then so does
I^{\alpha}_{t}f_{n}
.

Using Fubini’s theorem one can easily show for any
\alpha,\beta\geq 0

I^{\alpha}_{t}I^{\beta}_{t}\varphi=I^{\alpha+\beta}_{t}\varphi,\quad\text{$(a.e.)$}\,\,t\leq T.

(2.2)

It is known that if

and
\alpha-\frac{1}{p}\notin\mathbb{N}
then (see

\displaystyle\|I_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}([0,T])}\leq N(p,T,\alpha)\|\varphi\|_{L_{p}(0,T)}.

(2.3)

\alpha\geq 0

3.
Parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality

In this section we obtain a sharp
L_{p}
-estimate for solutions to the model equation

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=\Delta u+\partial^{\beta}_{t}\int^{t}_{0}g^{k}dw^{k}_{s}.

(3.1)

For this, we prove the parabolic Littlewood-Paley inequality related to the equation. For the classical case
\alpha=\beta=1
we refer to
[
19
,
20
,
24
]
.

Consider the fractional diffusion-wave equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x),\quad u(0)=u_{0},\quad 1_{\alpha>1}u^{\prime}(0)=0.

(3.2)

By taking the Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform with respect to
x
, we formally find that
u(t)=p(t)*u_{0}
is a solution to this problem if
p(t,x)
satisfies

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}\mathcal{F}(p)=-|\xi|^{2}\mathcal{F}(p),\quad\mathcal{F}(p)(0,\xi)=1,\quad 1_{\alpha>1}\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}\right)(0,\xi)=0.

(3.3)

It turns out that (see
[
13
,
18
]
or Lemma
3.1
below) there exists a function
p(t,x)
, called the fundamental solution, such that it satisfies (
3.3
). It is also true that
p
is infinitely differentiable in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\partial^{n}p(t,x)}{\partial t^{n}}=0
if
x\neq 0
.
Define

q_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x):=\begin{cases}I_{t}^{\alpha-\beta}p(t,x)&:\alpha\geq\beta\\
D_{t}^{\beta-\alpha}p(t,x)&:\alpha<\beta,\end{cases}

(3.4)

and

q(t,x):=q_{\alpha,1}(t,x).

Note that
q_{\alpha,\beta}
is well defined due to above mentioned properties of
p
.
Moreover
D_{t}^{\beta-\alpha}p(t,x)=\partial_{t}^{\beta-\alpha}p(t,x)
since
p(0,x)=0
if
x\neq 0
.

In the following lemma we collect some important properties of
p(t,x)
,
q(t,x)
, and
q_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)
taken from

[
13
]
and
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
,

\beta<\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
, and
\gamma\in[0,2)
.

(i)
There exists a fundamental solution
p(t,x)
satisfying above mentioned properties. It also holds that
for any
t\neq 0
and
x\neq 0
,

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}p(t,x)=\Delta p(t,x),\quad\frac{\partial p(t,x)}{\partial t}=\Delta q(t,x),

(3.5)

and for each
x\neq 0
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(t,x)\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
.
Moreover,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(t,\cdot)
is integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
uniformly on

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(ii)
If

,

is integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
uniformly on

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(iii)
There exist constants

and

such that if

,

\displaystyle\left|p(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N|x|^{-d}\exp\left\{-c|x|^{\frac{2}{2-\alpha}}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}\right\}.

(3.6)

(iv)

It holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\{D_{t}^{\sigma}q_{\alpha,\beta}(t,\cdot)\}(\xi)=t^{\alpha-\beta-\sigma}E_{\alpha,1+\alpha-\beta-\sigma}(-|\xi|^{2}t^{\alpha}),

(3.7)

where
E_{a,b}(z)
,

, is the Mittag-Leffler function defined as

E_{a,b}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(ak+b)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(v)

There exists a constant
N=N(d,\gamma,\alpha,\beta)
such that

\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|+\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}\partial_{t}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|\leq N\left(|x|^{-d+2-\gamma}\wedge|x|^{-d-\gamma}\right)

if

,
and

\displaystyle\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|+\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}\partial_{t}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq N\left(\{|x|^{1-\gamma}(1+\ln|x|1_{\gamma=1})\}\wedge|x|^{-1-\gamma}\right)

if
d=1
.
Furthermore, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}D_{x}^{n}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|+\left|D_{t}^{\sigma}D_{x}^{n}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}\partial_{t}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,x)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq N(d,\gamma,\alpha,\beta,n)\left(|x|^{-d+2-\gamma-n}\wedge|x|^{-d-\gamma-n}\right).

(3.8)

(vi)
The scaling properties hold:

q_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)=t^{-\frac{\alpha d}{2}+\alpha-\beta}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,xt^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}),

(3.9)

D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)=t^{-\sigma-\frac{\alpha(d+\gamma)}{2}+\alpha-\beta}D_{t}^{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,xt^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}).

(3.10)

Proof.

(i), (ii), (iii), and (v) are easily obtained from Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.3 of
[
18
]
.
The proof of (iv) can be found in Seciotn 6 of
[
18
]

For the scaling property (vi), see
[

, (5.2)]
.

The following result is well-known, for instance if
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
For the completeness of the article, we give a proof.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x-y)f(y)dy

converges to
f(x)
uniformly as
t\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

By (
3.7
), for any

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,y)dy=\mathcal{F}p(0)=E_{\alpha,1}(0)=1.

Also (

) shows that

is a constant function of

. For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x-y)f(y)dy-f(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,y)(f(x-y)-f(x))dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|y|<\delta}\left|p(t,y)(f(x-y)-f(x))\right|dy+\int_{|y|>\delta}\left|p(t,y)(f(x-y)-f(x))\right|dy

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}(\delta)+\mathcal{J}(\delta).

, for any

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{c_{1}/2}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(T)}

\displaystyle:=\left[\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}T_{t-s}^{\alpha,\beta}g(s,\cdot)(x)\right|_{H}^{2}ds\right]^{1/2},

Proof.

For a real-valued measurable function
h
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define
the maximal function

\mathbb{M}_{x}h(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|h(y)|dy=\sup_{r>0}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{r}(x)}|h(y)|dy.

The Hardy-Littlewood maximal theorem says

\|\mathbb{M}_{x}h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N(d,p)\|h\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\forall p>1.

(3.20)

For a function
h(t,x)
, set

\mathbb{M}_{x}h(t,x)=\mathbb{M}_{x}\left(h(t,\cdot)\right)(x),\quad\mathbb{M}_{t}h(t,x)=\mathbb{M}_{t}\left(h(\cdot,x)\right)(t),

and

\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}h(t,x)=\mathbb{M}_{t}\left(\mathbb{M}_{x}h(\cdot,x)\right)(t).

To evaluate
\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}h(t,x)
, we first fix
t
and estimate
(\mathbb{M}_{x}h(t,\cdot))(x)
. After this, we fix
x
and regard
(\mathbb{M}_{x}h(t,\cdot))(x)

as a function of
t
only to estimate the maximal function with respect to
t
.

Denote

Q_{0}:=[-2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},0]\times[-1,1]^{d}.

(3.21)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1};H)

and assume that
g=0
outside of
[-4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}]\times B_{3d}
.
Then for
(t,x)\in Q_{0}
,

\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy\leq N\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x),

where

.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
,

(t,x)\in Q_{0}

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1};H)

and assume that
g(t,x)=0
for
|t|\geq 4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
. Then for any
(t,x)\in Q_{0}
,

\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy\leq N(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x).

Proof.

Take
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\zeta=1

in
B_{2d}
and
\zeta=0
outside
B_{3d}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{T}
is a sublinear operator, and therefore

\mathcal{T}g\leq\mathcal{T}(\zeta g)+\mathcal{T}((1-\zeta)g).

Since
\mathcal{T}(\zeta g)
can be estimated by Lemma
3.7
,
we may assume that
g(t,x)=0
for
x\in B_{2d}
. Let
0>s>r>-4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
.
Then by (
3.10
),

\displaystyle\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}T_{s-r}^{\alpha,\beta}g(r,\cdot)(y)\right|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha d}{2}+\alpha-\beta-\frac{\alpha{c_{1}}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}y)\right|\left|g(r,y-z)\right|_{H}dz

\displaystyle=(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}(1,(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}y)\right|\left|g(r,y-z)\right|_{H}dz.

(3.22)

To proceed further we use the following integration by parts formula : if
F
and
G
are smooth enough then for any
0<\varepsilon<R<\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon\leq|\eta|\leq R}F(\eta)G(|\eta|)d\eta

\displaystyle=-\int_{\epsilon}^{R}G^{\prime}(\rho)\left[\int_{|\eta|\leq\rho}F(\eta)d\eta\right]d\rho

\displaystyle\qquad+G(R)\int_{|\eta|\leq R}F(\eta)d\eta-G(\epsilon)\int_{|\eta|\leq\epsilon}F(\eta)d\eta.

(3.23)

Indeed, (
3.23
) is obtained by applying integration by parts to

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}G(\rho)\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\int_{B_{\rho}(0)}F(z)\,dz\right)d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}G(\rho)\left(\int_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}F(s)\,dS_{\rho}\right)d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{R\geq|z|\geq\varepsilon}F(z)G(|z|)\,dz.

Observe
that if
(s,y)\in Q_{0}
and
\rho>1
, then

|x-y|\leq 2d,\quad B_{\rho}(y)\subset B_{2d+\rho}(x)\subset B_{(2d+1)\rho}(x),

(3.24)

whereas if
\rho\leq 1
then for
z\in B_{\rho}(0)
,

|y-z|\leq\sqrt{d}+1\leq 2d
and thus
g(r,y-z)=0
.
Therefore by (
3.23
) and(
3.8
),

\displaystyle(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}q_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1,(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}y\right)\right|\left|g(r,y-z)\right|_{H}dz

\displaystyle\leq N(s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left((s-r)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho\right)^{-d-1-{c_{1}}}\left[\int_{|z|\leq\rho}\left|g(r,y-z)\right|_{H}dz\right]d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N(s-r)^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{-d-1-{c_{1}}}\left[\int_{|z|\leq\rho}|g(r,y-z)|_{H}dz\right]d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N(s-r)^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho^{-1-{c_{1}}}\left[-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{B_{3\rho}(x)}\left|g(r,z)\right|_{H}dz\right]d\rho

\displaystyle\leq N(s-r)^{\alpha-\beta}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}(r,x).

Then due to the fact that
(\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H})^{2}\leq\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{0}}

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy=\int_{Q_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}\left|(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}T_{s-r}^{\alpha,\beta}g(r,\cdot)(y)\right|_{H}^{2}drdsdy

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{Q_{0}}\int_{-4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}^{s}\left[\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(r,x)(s-r)^{2(\alpha-\beta)}\right]drds

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{-4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}^{0}\left(\int_{r}^{0}(s-r)^{2(\alpha-\beta)}ds\right)\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(r,x)dr\leq N\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x).

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 3.9

(s+R^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},y)=(0,0)
.

Also, due to (
3.18
) (or (
3.19

\mathcal{T}g(c^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\cdot,c\cdot)(t,x)=\mathcal{T}g(c^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}t,cx).

Since dilations do not affect averages, it suffices to prove (
3.29
) with

, i.e.

Q=Q_{0}=[-2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},0]\times[-1,1]^{d}.

Now we take a function
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}

such that
\zeta=1
on
[-3^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},3^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}]
,

\zeta=0
outside of
[-4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}},4^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}]
,
and
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
. We also choose a function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

such that
\eta=1
on
B_{2d}
,
\eta=0
outside of
B_{3d}
,
and
0\leq\eta\leq 1
. Set

Observe that

and

(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}T_{t-s}^{\alpha,\beta}g_{1}(s,y)=\zeta(s)(-\Delta)^{{c_{1}}/2}T_{t-s}^{\alpha,\beta}g(s,y),

\mathcal{T}g\leq\mathcal{T}g_{1}+\mathcal{T}(g_{2}+g_{3}),

(3.30)

\mathcal{T}g_{3}\leq\mathcal{T}(g_{2}+g_{3})\leq\mathcal{T}g.

(3.31)

(
3.30
) is because
\mathcal{T}
is sublinear (see (
3.14
)), and (
3.31
) comes
from the facts
g_{3}=(1-\eta)(g_{2}+g_{3})
,
g_{2}+g_{3}=(1-\zeta)g
,

, and

Hence for any constant

|\mathcal{T}g-c|\leq|\mathcal{T}g_{1}|+|\mathcal{T}(g_{2}+g_{3})-c|

(3.32)

Indeed, (
3.32
) is from (

, and if

then it follows from

) is obvious if

, and

we use

.

Therefore, for any

,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)-c\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{T}g_{1}(s,y)\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}(g_{2}+g_{3})(s,y)-c\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{T}g_{1}(s,y)\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}g_{2}(s,y)\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}g_{3}(s,y)-c\right|,

and by (

\displaystyle-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)-\left(\mathcal{T}g\right)_{Q_{0}}\right|^{2}dsdy\leq 4-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g(s,y)-c\right|^{2}dsdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{1}(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy+16-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{2}(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy+16-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{3}(s,y)-c\right|^{2}dyds.

Note
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
3.8
and
3.9
, respectively, and thus

\displaystyle-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{1}(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy+-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{2}(s,y)\right|^{2}dsdy

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g_{1}\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x)+\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g_{2}\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x)\right)\leq N\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x).

The second inequality above is due to
|g_{i}|\leq|g|
(

). Take

and note that

-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{3}(s,y)-\left(\mathcal{\mathcal{T}}g_{3}\right)_{Q_{0}}\right|^{2}dsdy\leq-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}-\hskip-10.81218pt\int_{Q_{0}}\left|\mathcal{T}g_{3}(s,y)-\mathcal{T}g_{3}(r,z)\right|^{2}drdzdsdy.

(3.34)

Note also, on
Q_{0}
,
\mathcal{T}g_{3}
does not depend on the values of

for

. Hence the above two integrals do not change

by

, where

so
that

,
\xi=1
for

, and
\xi=0
for

t\geq 2^{2/{\alpha}}
. Now it is easy to check that

satisfies the assumptions of Lemma

, and
therefore the right hand side of (

Hence (

) is finally proved.

We continue the proof of the theorem.
By (

) and Jensen’s inequality

(\mathcal{T}g)^{\#}(t,x)\leq N\left(\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}(t,x)\right)^{1/2}.

Therefore by Fefferman-Stein theorem (
[
43
, Theorem 4.2.2]
) and

[
43
, Theorem 1.3.1]
),

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}g\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\|(\mathcal{T}g)^{\#}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\|\mathbb{M}_{t}\mathbb{M}_{x}\left|g\right|_{H}^{2}\|^{1/2}_{L_{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\|\mathbb{M}_{x}|g|^{2}_{H}\|^{1/2}_{L_{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq N\||g|_{H}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

This proves the theorem if

) does not depend on the value of

∎

4.
Model Equation

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\beta\in\left(-\infty,\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)
. In this section we obtain the uniqueness, existence, and sharp estimate of strong solutions to the model equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\partial_{t}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}g^{k}(s,x)dw_{s}^{k},\quad t>0

(4.1)

with the zero initial condition
u(0,x)=0
(additionally
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=0
if
\alpha>1
).

The following lemma is used to estimate solutions to the equation when
\beta<1/2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
p>2
,
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
, and
g\in\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(T,l_{2})
. Then
for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\partial_{t}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{r}g^{k}(s,\cdot)dw_{s}^{k}\right\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}}^{p}dr\leq N(d,p,\beta,T)I_{t}^{1-2\beta}\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(\cdot,l_{2})}^{p}(t).

In particular,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\partial_{t}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{r}g^{k}(s,\cdot)dw_{s}^{k}\right\|_{H_{p}^{\gamma}}^{p}dr\leq N\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\gamma}(t,l_{2})}^{p}.

Proof.

Due to the isometry
(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}:H^{\gamma}_{p}\to L_{p}
, we only need to prove the case
\gamma=0
.
By Lemma
2.2
(iii),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\beta}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{r}g^{k}(s,x)dw_{s}^{k}\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}g^{k}(s,x)dw_{s}^{k},

for almost all
t\leq T
(a.s.). By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy
inequality and the Hölder inequality, for all
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-\beta}g^{k}(s,\cdot)dw_{s}^{k}\right\|_{L_{p}}^{p}dr

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-2\beta}\left|g(s,x)\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{p/2}drdx

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-2\beta(\frac{2}{p}+\frac{p-2}{p})}\left|g(s,x)\right|_{l_{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{p/2}drdx

\displaystyle\leq N\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{-2\beta}\left|g(s,x)\right|_{l_{2}}^{p}dsdrdx

\displaystyle=N\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-2\beta}\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(s,l_{2})}^{p}ds=NI_{t}^{1-2\beta}\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(\cdot,l_{2})}^{p}(t).

The lemma is proved.
∎

A version of Lemma
4.2
can be found in
[
6
]
for
p=2
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
. But solutions spaces are slightly different, and our proof is more rigorous.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
and
define

\displaystyle u(t,x):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{\alpha,\beta}(t-s,x-y)g^{k}(s,y)dydw_{s}^{k}.

(4.2)

Then
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{p}(T)
and satisfies (
4.1
) with the zero initial conditon in the sense of distributions
(see Definition
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Set

v(t,x):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}g^{k}(s,x)dw_{s}^{k},\quad w(t,x):=I_{t}^{\alpha-\beta}v(t,x),

where
I_{t}^{\alpha-\beta}v=D_{t}^{\beta-\alpha}v
if
\alpha<\beta
. Note that since
g\in\mathbb{H}^{\infty}_{0}(T,l_{2})
, by the
Kolmogorov continuity theorem

v\in C^{1/2-\varepsilon}([0,T],H^{m}_{p})

for any
\varepsilon>0
and
m
.
Thus
w\in C^{\delta}([0,T],H_{p}^{m})
for some
\delta>0
(see (
2.8
)).

By Fubini’s theorem if
\alpha\geq\beta
and fractional integration by parts (e.g.
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
) if
\alpha<\beta
,

\int^{t}_{0}I_{s}^{\alpha-\beta}p(s,x-y)(\int_{0}^{t-s}g^{k}(r,y)dw^{k}_{r})ds=\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)I_{s}^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{0}^{s}g^{k}(r,y)dw_{r}^{k}ds.

Here
I^{\alpha}_{s}p(s,x-y)
and
I^{\alpha-\beta}_{s}\int^{s}_{0}g^{k}(r,y)dw^{k}_{r}
are used to denote
(I^{\alpha-\beta}_{t}p(\cdot,x-y))(s)
and

(I^{\alpha-\beta}_{t}\int^{\cdot}_{0}g^{k}(r,y)dw^{k}_{r})(s)
, respectively. Thus, using the stochastic Fubini theorem (see
[
23
, Lemma 2.7]
) we get, for each
(t,x)
,
(a.s.)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}u(s,x)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}I_{s}^{\alpha-\beta}p(s,x-y)\int_{0}^{t-s}g^{k}(r,y)dw^{k}_{r}dsdy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)I_{s}^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{0}^{s}g^{k}(r,y)dw_{r}^{k}dyds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)w(s,y)dyds.

Due to
the continuity with respect to
t
, for each
x
we get

\int_{0}^{t}u(s,x)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)w(s,y)dyds,\quad\forall t\leq T~\text{$(a.s.)$}

and therefore
(a.s.)

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)w(s,y)dyds,~\quad(a.e.)\,\,t\leq T.

(4.3)

In other words, the above equality holds
(a.e.)
on
\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Next we claim that

\displaystyle u(t,x)-w(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(t-s,x-y)\Delta w(s,y)dyds

(4.4)

(a.e.)
on
\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By the definition of the differentiation, for each
(\omega,t,x)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t-s,x-y)w(s,y)dyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(p(t+h-s,x-y)\right)w(s,y)dyds

\displaystyle+\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{p(t+h-s,x-y)-p(t-s,x-y)}{h}\right]w(s,y)dyds.

By the mean value theorem, the integration by parts, and Lemma
3.1
(i) and (ii),

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{p(t+h-s,x-y)-p(t-s,x-y)}{h}\right]w(s,y)dyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(t+\theta h-s,x-y)w(s,y)dyds,\quad\theta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(t+\theta h-s,x-y)\Delta w(s,y)dyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(t-s)\Delta w(s,y)dyds.

For the last equality above we used the
L_{1}
-continuity of the integrable function
[
37
, Theorem 9.5]
, which implies that
for any
f\in L_{1}([0,t+\varepsilon])
, where
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}|f(s+h)-f(s)|dt=0
.

On the other hand, due to Corollary
3.2
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(p(t+h-s,x-y)\right)w(s,y)dyds=w(t,x).

Thus (
4.4
) is proved due to (
4.3
), and
from (
4.4
) it easily follows that
u
has a

H^{2}_{p}
-valued continuous version since
g\in\mathbb{H}_{0}^{\infty}(T,l_{2})
.
It only remains to show that
u
satisfies (
4.1
). By representation formula (
4.4
), it follows
that
u-w\in\mathbf{H}^{\alpha,2}_{p,0}(T)

(a.s.)
, and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(u-w)+\Delta w(t,x)

(4.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta u

in
\mathbf{L}_{p}(T)
. See Remark
2.9
for spaces

\mathbf{H}^{\alpha,2}_{p,0}(T)
and
\mathbf{L}_{p}(T)
. Actually in (
[
17
, Lemma
3.5]
), it is proved that (
4.4
) gives the unique
solution to (
4.5
) in the space

\mathbf{H}^{\alpha,2}_{p,0}(T)
if
\Delta w
is sufficiently smooth.
However one can easily check that this representation holds even if

\Delta w\in L_{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
by using an approximation argument.
It follows from (
2.14
) and Remark
2.9
that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
(a.s.)

(u(t)-w(t),\phi)=I^{\alpha}(\Delta u,\phi),\quad\text{$(a.e.)$}\,\,t\leq T.

Taking
(w(t),\phi)
to the right hand side of the equality and
using the continuity of
u
with respect to
t
,
we get

(u(t),\phi)=I^{\alpha}_{t}(\Delta u,\phi)+I^{\alpha-\beta}_{t}\int^{t}_{0}(g^{k},\phi)dw^{k}_{s},\quad\,\forall t\leq T~\text{$(a.s.)$}.

Therefore
u
is a solution to (
4.1
) in the
sense of distributions
because
u
itself is an
H^{2}_{p}
-valued continuous process.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Recall, for

Step 2

In this section we study the SPDE

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=\left(a^{ij}u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}u_{x^{i}}+cu+f(u)\right)+\partial^{\beta}_{t}\int^{t}_{0}h(u)\,dB_{t}

are functions depending on

(\omega,t,x)
, the functions
f
and

depend on
(\omega,t,x)
and the unknown

Let

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume

{\kappa_{0}}\in\left(\frac{d}{2},d\right],\quad 2\leq 2r\leq p,\quad 2r<\frac{d}{d-{\kappa_{0}}},

(7.4)

and

are functions of

and

|h(x,u)-h(x,v)|\leq\xi(x)|u-v|

For

\|g(u)-g(v)\|_{H^{-{\kappa_{0}}}_{p}(l_{2})}\leq N\|\xi\|_{L_{2s}}\|u-v\|_{L_{p}},

where

is the conjugate of
r
and
N=N(r)<\infty
. In particular, if

and
\xi
is bounded, then

\|g(u)-g(v)\|_{H^{-{\kappa_{0}}}_{p}(l_{2})}\leq N\|u-v\|_{L_{p}}.

Proof.

It is well-known (e.g.
[

, p.132]
,
[
22
, Exercise 12.9.19]
) that there exists a Green function

, which decays exponentially fast at infinity and behaves like

so that the equality holds:

\|g(u)-g(v)\|_{H^{-{\kappa_{0}}}_{p}(l_{2})}=\|\bar{h}\|_{L_{p}},

where

\displaystyle\bar{h}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(x-y)|^{2}|h(y,u(y))-h(y,v(y))|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(x-y)|^{2}\xi^{2}(y)|u(y)-v(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}=:\tilde{h}(x).

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\tilde{h}(x)|\leq\|\xi\|_{L_{2s}}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(x-y)|^{2r}|u(y)-v(y)|^{2r}dy\right)^{1/(2r)}.

Note that

since

.
Therefore applying Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\|\tilde{h}\|_{L_{p}}\leq N\|\xi\|_{L_{2s}}\|G\|_{2r}\|u-v\|_{L_{p}}\leq N\|\xi\|_{L_{2s}}\|u-v\|_{L_{p}}.

The lemma is proved.

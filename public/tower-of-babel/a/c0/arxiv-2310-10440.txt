Monotonicity of positive solutions for an indefinite logarithmic Laplacian equation
Baiyu Liu and Shasha Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate a nonlocal equation involving the logarithmic Laplacian with indefinite nonlinearities: { L Δ ​ u ​ ( x ) = a ⁡ ( x n ) ​ f ​ ( u ) , x ∈ Ω , u ⁡ ( x ) = 0 , x ∈ ℝ n \ Ω . \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{\Delta}u(x)=a(x_{n})f(u),&x\in\Omega,\\ u(x)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega.\end{array}\right. 0=-2\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}(x^{0})
, i.e.

(33)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}(x^{0})=0.

Dividing both side of (
9
) by
\delta_{k}=|\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n}|=\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n}
and using Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

(34)

\qquad 0>\varlimsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{L_{\Delta}w_{\lambda_{k}}(x^{k})}{\delta_{k}}\geq\varlimsup_{k\to\infty}a(x_{n}^{k})M(\lambda_{k},x^{k})\frac{w_{\lambda_{k}}(x^{k})}{\delta_{k}}.

Noticing that as
k\to\infty
, we have
\delta_{k}\to 0
,
x^{k}\to x^{0}
,
a(x_{n}^{k})M(\lambda_{k},x^{k})\to a(\lambda_{0})M(\lambda_{0},x^{0})
and

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{w_{\lambda_{k}}(x^{k})}{\delta_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u((x^{k})^{\prime},2\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n})-u((x^{k})^{\prime},x^{k}_{n})}{|\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}((x^{k})^{\prime},\theta^{k})\cdot 2(\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n})}{\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}((x^{k})^{\prime},\theta^{k})\to 2\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}(x^{0})=0\quad\textrm{as}k\to\infty,

in which
\theta^{k}
is between
2\lambda_{k}-x^{k}_{n}
and
x^{k}_{n}
, and also we have used (
33
).

Putting (
35
) into the right side of (
34
) will lead to a contradiction.

Therefore, we have
\lambda_{0}=+\infty.

∎

3.
Non-existence of the positive bounded solutions

In the previous section, we have proved that the positive bounded solutions of (
2
) is monotone increasing along
x_{n}
direction. Based on this result, in this section, we will prove the non-existence of positive bounded solutions for equation (
2
).

The following maximum principle will be needed in our proof. It has been obtained in
[
17
]
(Proposition 4.1).
We include the proof for readers’ convenience.
Let
B_{1}(Re_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the unit ball centered at
(0,\dots,0,R)
.

Proposition 3.1

(Strong Maximum Principle in a unit ball)
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{B_{1}(Re_{n})})
satisfying

(36)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{\Delta}u(x)=\left((-\Delta)^{L}+\rho_{n}\right)u(x)\geq 0,&x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}),\\
u(x)>0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}(Re_{n}).\end{array}\right.

Then
u>0
in
B_{1}(Re_{n}).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose for contradiction that there is
x_{0}\in B_{1}(Re_{n})
such that

(37)

u(x_{0})=\min\limits_{x\in B_{1}(Re_{n})}u(x)\leq 0.

Denote
A=B_{1}(x_{0})\cap B_{1}(Re_{n})
,
D=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash(B_{1}(x_{0})\cup B_{1}(Re_{n}))
.
By using the definition of
(-\Delta)^{L}
, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{L}u(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}P.V.\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy+C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}(x_{0})}\frac{-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}P.V.\int_{A}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy+C_{n}\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle-C_{n}\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy-C_{n}\int_{D}\frac{u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy.

Since
u>0
in
D
and in
B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A
, there holds

(38)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{L}u(x_{0})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{n}P.V.\int_{A}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy+C_{n}\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A}\frac{u(x_{0})}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle+C_{n}\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy-C_{n}\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{u(x_{0})}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}P.V.\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})}\frac{u(x_{0})-u(y)}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy+C_{n}u(x_{0})\left(\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy-\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}u(x_{0})\left(\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy-\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy\right),

where we have used (
37
).

Obviously, for those
y\in B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A
,
|x_{0}-y|<1
. Also, for
y\in B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A
,
|x_{0}-y|>1.

Hence, we can infer that

(39)

\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy-\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A}\frac{1}{|x_{0}-y|^{n}}dy>|B_{1}(x_{0})\backslash A|-|B_{1}(Re_{n})\backslash A|=0.

Using the fact that
\rho_{n}>0
and putting (
39
) into (
38
),
we arrive at
\left((-\Delta)^{L}+\rho_{n}\right)u(x_{0})\leq(-\Delta)^{L}u(x_{0})<0
,
which contradicts with (
36
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the aforementioned maximum principle remains valid on a bounded domain
\Omega
, provided that the condition

\int_{B_{1}(x)\backslash\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}dy-\int_{\Omega\backslash B_{1}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}dy+\rho_{n}\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in\Omega,

holds, which is precisely Proposition 4.1 in
[
17
]
.
Additionally, the result holds for
u(x)
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{L}u(x)\geq 0,&x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}),\\
u(x)>0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}(Re_{n}).\end{array}\right.

The proofs for these assertions follow a similar reasoning as the one presented above.

We shall now establish the proof of Theorem
1.2
, following the approach presented in
[
14
]
.
Consider
\phi(x)
as the first eigenfunction associated with
L_{\Delta}
in
B_{1}(Re_{n})
, that is, the function defined as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{\Delta}\phi(x)=\lambda_{1}\phi(x),&x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}),\\
\phi(x)=0,&x\in B_{1}^{c}(Re_{n}).\end{array}\right.

According to Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 1.11 in
[
17
]
, we can deduce that
\phi(x)
is strictly positive in
B_{1}(Re_{n})
,
\lambda_{1}
is positive, and
\phi(x)
belongs to
C\big(\overline{B_{1}(Re_{n})}\big)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume for contradiction that
u\in L_{0}\cap C_{loc}^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
is a positive bounded solution of problem (
2
). By Theorem
1.1
,
u(x)
is monotone increasing in
x_{n}
direction in
\Omega
.

It follows from that assumption that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz coercive epigraph, we know that there is
R_{1}>0
so that
B_{1}(R_{1}e_{n})\subset\Omega
.
Since
u(x)
is positive in
\Omega
,

\xi_{0}:=\min_{x\in B_{1}(R_{1}e_{1})}u(x)>0.

By the fact that
u(x)
is monotone increasing in
x_{n}
direction in
\Omega
, we get for all
R>R_{1}
,
u(x)>\xi_{0}
,
\forall x\in B_{1}(Re_{n})
. Since
a(t)
satisfies (i) and (iv), it is positive somewhere and nondecreasing. Hence, we choose
R>R_{1}
sufficiently large such that
a(t)>0
in
(R-1,+\infty)
.

Denote
m_{0}=\frac{f(\xi_{0})}{\sup\limits_{R^{n}}u}>0.

It follows from assumptions (i) and (iii)

L_{\Delta}u(x)=a(x_{n})f(u(x))\geq a(R-1)f(\xi_{0})\geq a(R-1)m_{0}u(x),\quad\forall x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}).

Taking assumption
(iv)
into account, we have

(40)

L_{\Delta}u(x)\geq\lambda_{1}u(x),\quad\forall x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}).

for sufficiently large
R
.

Next we construct a auxiliary functions. Define

M=\max\limits_{x\in B_{1}(Re_{n})}\frac{\phi}{u}(x),\quad\textrm{and}\quad v(x)=Mu(x)\in C_{loc}^{1,1}\cap L_{0}.

By using (
40
) and a direct calculation, we have for all
x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}),

\displaystyle L_{\Delta}v(x)

\displaystyle=ML_{\Delta}u(x)\geq M\lambda_{1}u(x)=\left(\max\limits_{x\in B_{1}(Re_{n})}\frac{\phi}{u}(x)\right)\lambda_{1}u(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\phi(x)}{u(x)}\lambda_{1}u(x)=\lambda_{1}\phi(x)=L_{\Delta}\phi(x),

which implies

(41)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{\Delta}(v(x)-\phi(x))\geq 0,&x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}),\\
v(x)-\phi(x)>0,&x\in B_{1}^{c}(Re_{n}).\end{array}\right.

Applying Proposition
3.1
to problem (
41
), we have

v(x)>\phi(x),\quad\forall x\in B_{1}(Re_{n}).

This contradicts the definition of
v,
because at a maximum point
x^{0},
we have

v(x^{0})=\frac{\phi(x^{0})}{u(x^{0})}u(x^{0})=\phi(x^{0}).

Therefore, equation (
2
) does not possess any positive solution, and hence we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

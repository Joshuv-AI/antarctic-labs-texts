The C 0 C^{0} -convergence at the Neumann boundary for Liouville equations Thanks: Lei Liu was supported in part by National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12101255).
Yuchen Bi, Jiayu Li, Lei Liu, Shuangjie Peng

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the blow-up analysis for a sequence of solutions to the Liouville type equation with exponential Neumann boundary condition. For interior case, i.e. the blow-up point is an interior point, Li [ 9 ] gave a uniform asymptotic estimate. Later, Zhang [ 13 ] and Gluck [ 6 ] improved Li’s estimate in the sense of C 0 C^{0} -convergence by using the method of moving planes or classification of solutions of the linearized version of Liouville equation. If the sequence blows up at a boundary point, Bao-Wang-Zhou [ 1 ] proved a similar asymptotic estimate of Li [ 9 ] . In this paper, we will prove a C 0 C^{0} -convergence result in this boundary blow-up process. Our method is different from [ 13 , 6 ] .



1.
introduction

The compactness of a sequence of solutions to a nonlinear equation plays an important role in the study of the existence problem. For most of interesting geometric partial differential equations, one can not use variational methods directly to get the existence result, such as harmonic maps, minimal surface, Liouville equation and so on. The main reason lies in the lack of compactness of the solution space. To overcome this type of problem, an important method called blow-up analysis was employed to provide better information of solution space.

The blow-up analysis for a sequence of solutions to Liouville type equation has been widely studied since the work of Breziz-Merle
[
3
]
where a concentration-compactness phenomenon of solutions was revealed. Later, Li-Shafrir
[
10
]
initiated to study the blow-up value at the blow-up point, which is quantized, i.e. at each blow-up point, the blow-up value is
8\pi m
for some positive integer
m
. We emphasize that there is no boundary condition in Li-Shafrir
[
10
]
. If certain boundary assumption is imposed, for example the oscillation on the boundary is uniformly bounded, then Li
[
9
]
proved that there is only one bubble at each blow-up point, which implies that the blow-up value is
8\pi
. Roughly speaking, let
u_{k}
be a sequence solutions to Liouville equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u=e^{u},\ \ in\ \ B_{1}(0)

with uniformly bounded energy

(1.2)

\int_{B_{1}(0)}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx\leq C

and boundary condition

(1.3)

osc_{\partial B_{1}(0)}u_{k}\leq C.

Suppose
x=0
is the only blow-up point for this sequence. Then Li
[
9
]
proved that there exists a sequence of points
x_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, such that passing to a subsequence, there holds

(1.4)

\left\|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(x_{k})-v\Big(\frac{x-x_{k}}{e^{-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x_{k})}}\Big)\right\|_{C^{0}(B_{1}(0))}\leq C,\ \ \forall\ k,

where
v(x)=-2\log(1+\frac{1}{8}|x|^{2})
. Furthermore, if we assume a stronger boundary condition that

osc_{\partial B_{1}(0)}u_{k}=o(1),

Chen-Lin
[
4
]
, Zhang
[
13
]
and Gluck
[
6
]
established a type of
C^{0}
-convergence result of (
1.4
) as follows

(1.5)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(x_{k})-v\Big(\frac{x-x_{k}}{e^{-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x_{k})}}\Big)\right\|_{C^{0}(B_{1}(0))}=0.

Motivated by the question of prescribed geodesic curvature, it is also interesting to study the Liouville equation with a Neumann boundary condition. For the blow-up analysis of Liouville type equation near the Neumann boundary, Guo-Liu
[
7
]
proved Brezis-Merle type concentration phenomenon and Li-Shafrir type quantization property. Later, Bao-Wang-Zhou
[
1
]
extended Li’s work
[
9
]
to the boundary case. More precisely, they proved that there is only one bubble near a boundary blow-up point and a similar convergence result of (
1.4
) also holds near the boundary if the oscillation on the boundary is uniformly bounded.

In this paper, we want to establish a similar
C^{0}
-convergence of (
1.5
) near a boundary blow-up point. Before stating the main result, we make some notations.

Let
B_{r}(x_{0})
be the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with radius
r
centered at
x_{0}
. Let
\partial B_{r}(x_{0})
be the boundary of
B_{r}(x_{0})
. Denote

B^{+}_{r}(x_{0}):=\{y=(y^{1},y^{2})\in B_{r}(x_{0})\ |\ y^{2}>0\}

and

\partial^{+}B^{+}_{r}(x_{0}):=\{y=(y^{1},y^{2})\in\partial B_{r}(x_{0})\ |\ y^{2}>0\},\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{r}(x_{0}):=\{y=(y^{1},y^{2})\in B_{r}(x_{0})\ |\ y^{2}=0\}.

Denote

\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}:=\{y=(y^{1},y^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ y^{2}>-a\}

for some
a\geq 0
and

\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}:=\{y=(y^{1},y^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ y^{2}=-a\}.

For simplicity of notations, we always denote
B_{1}(0)
,
B^{+}_{1}(0)
,
B_{R}(0)
,
B^{+}_{R}(0)
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{0}
by
B
,
B^{+}
,
B_{R}
,
B^{+}_{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
respectively.

Now we consider the following equation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u=e^{u},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial\vec{n}}=e^{\frac{u}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+},\end{cases}

where
\vec{n}
is the unit outer normal vector on the boundary.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.6
) with uniformly bounded energy

(1.7)

\int_{B}e^{u_{k}}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}e^{\frac{u_{k}}{2}}ds_{x}\leq C<\infty

and
0
be its only blow-up point in
B^{+}_{1}(0)
, i.e.

(1.8)

\displaystyle\max_{K\subset\subset\overline{B^{+}}\setminus\{0\}}u_{k}\leq C(K),\ \max_{\overline{B^{+}}}u_{k}\to+\infty.

Assume the boundary condition as

(1.9)

osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}}u_{k}=o(1).

Our main result is as follows

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.6
) with conditions (
1.7
), (
1.8
) and (
1.9
). Then there exists a sequence of points
\{x_{k}\}\subset\overline{B^{+}}
such that, passing to a subsequence, there hold:

(1)

x_{k}\to 0
and
u_{k}(x_{k})=\sup_{B^{+}}u_{k}(x)\to+\infty
as
k\to\infty
;

(2)

Denote
\lambda_{k}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x_{k})}
and
\Omega_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ x_{k}+\lambda_{k}x\in B^{+}\}
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{dist(x_{k},\partial^{0}B^{+})}{\lambda_{k}}=a<+\infty

and
\Omega_{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}
as
k\to\infty
.

(3)

Denote
v_{k}(x)=u_{k}(x_{k}+\lambda_{k}x)+2\log\lambda_{k}
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|v_{k}(x)-v(x)\right\|_{C^{1}(B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega_{k})}=0,\ \ \forall R>0,

where

v(x)=\log\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{\Big[\lambda^{2}+(x^{1}+s_{0})^{2}+\Big(x^{2}+a+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{2}},\ \ x=(x^{1},x^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

for some
\lambda>0
and
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, which satisfies
v(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v=e^{v}\ \ &in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}_{a},\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{v}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}.\end{cases}

(4)

The
C^{0}
-convergence:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(x_{k})-v\Big(\frac{x-x_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\right\|_{C^{0}(B^{+})}=0.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we focus on establishing the
C^{0}
-convergence in the blow-up process, i.e. the fourth conclusion of Theorem
1.1
since conclusions
(1)-(3)
are standard now by
[
7
,
1
,
14
]
.

For conclusion
(4)
, by a standard blow-up analysis, it is not hard to see that we only need to show

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(x_{k})-v\Big(\frac{x-x_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\right\|_{osc(B^{+}\setminus B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k}))}=0,

for some
R_{k}\to+\infty
. We divide the oscillation estimate into two parts:
B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k})
and
B^{+}\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{k})
. For the first part, we will prove that both the tangential and radial oscillation are small. To control the tangential oscillation, we will use Green’s formula to derive a pointwise estimate of
\nabla v_{k}
, where there appears a bad term

\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{3/2}}.

One may see that this term is not bounded if
x
goes to the boundary even if
|x|
is big. To overcome this obstacle, we will use the method of integration and the symmetry of Green’s function for the unit ball, see Corollary
3.3
. To control the radial oscillation, the key point is to derive the following type energy decay

\alpha(t)=4+\frac{O(1)}{t^{p}},\ \ t\in\Big[R_{k},\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}\Big]

for some
p>0
, where
\alpha(t)
is the energy defined by (
2.13
). This will be derived by combining Pohozaev’s type identity, the method of integration and above point estimate of
\nabla v_{k}
, see Lemma
3.4
. With the help of energy decay estimate, we will use an ODE method to get the control of radial oscillation, see Proposition
3.5
. To estimate the second part, we will use the PDE’s theory including
L^{p}
-theory, Green’s formula and maximal principle to prove the oscillation is small.

The rest of paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we will prove some basic lemmas including a bounded oscillation lemma and a fast decay lemma near the boundary which will be used in our later proof. In Sect.
3
, we first derive a point estimate of
\nabla v_{k}
. Then additionally by using Pohozaev’s type identity and the method of integration, we will establish a energy decay lemma which is crucial in our proof. Finally, we will prove Theorem
1.1
at the end of this section.

2.
Some basic lemmas

In this section, we will recall some classical blow-up analysis for Liouville type equation, and prove some lemmas which will be used in our later proof, such as a bounded oscillation estimate near the boundary, a fast decay lemma near the boundary and so on.

We start by recalling some standard theory in the blow-up analysis of Liouville type equation. See
[
3
,
10
,
9
,
1
,
7
]
. Let
x_{k}\in\overline{B^{+}}
be the point such that

u_{k}(x_{k})=\sup_{B^{+}}u_{k}(x).

By (
1.8
), it is easy to check that

x_{k}\to 0\ \ and\ \ u_{k}(x_{k})\to+\infty\ \ as\ \ k\to\infty.

Set
\lambda_{k}:=e^{-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x_{k})}
and

v_{k}(x):=u_{k}(x_{k}+\lambda_{k}x)+2\log\lambda_{k}.

Denote

\Omega_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|x_{k}+\lambda_{k}x\in B^{+}\}

and

d_{k}:=dist(x_{k},\partial^{0}B^{+}).

Then we have the following two cases.

Case 1:
\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}=\infty
.

In this case, it is easy to check that
v_{k}(x)
is well defined in the domain
\Omega_{k}
which converges to the whole plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By the standard theory of Liouville equation, it is well known that
v_{k}(x)\to v(x)
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
v(x)
satisfies Liouville equation

(2.1)

-\Delta v(x)=e^{v},\ \ in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}\ \ with\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v}dx\leq C<+\infty.

It follows from the classification result in
[
5
]
that

v(x)=-2\log\Big(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{8}\Big),\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v}dx=8\pi.

Case 2:
\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}=a<\infty
.

In this case, the domain
\Omega_{k}
converges to
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}:=\{x=(x^{1},x^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|\ x^{2}\geq-a\}
. Since

v_{k}(0)=0,\,\,v_{k}(x)\leq 0,\ \ \forall\,x\in\Omega_{k}

and

|\Delta v_{k}(x)|\leq C,\ \ \Big|\frac{\partial v_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}(x)\Big|\leq C,

by a standard elliptic theory involving a Harnack inequality near a Neumann-type boundary (see Lemma A.2 in
[
8
]
), we claim that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{R})}\leq C(R)\ \ uniformly\ \ in\ \ B^{+}_{R}.

In fact, denoting
\tilde{x}_{k}=(0,-\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}})
and setting

\tilde{v}_{k}(x):=v_{k}(x+\tilde{x}_{k}),

we can check that
\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq 0,\ \ \tilde{v}_{k}(-\tilde{x}_{k})=0
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{v}_{k}=e^{\tilde{v}_{k}},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Let
w_{k}
be the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{k}=-\Delta\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{2R},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{2R},\\
w_{k}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{2R}.\end{cases}

Extend
w_{k}
evenly, then
\|w_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{2R})}\leq C(R)
. Set
\eta_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k}-w_{k}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\eta_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{2R},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{2R},\\
\eta_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{2R}.\end{cases}

By the Harnack inequality of Lemma A.2 in
[
8
]
) and the fact that
|\eta_{k}(-\tilde{x}_{k})|\leq C<+\infty
, we have
\|\eta_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{\frac{3}{2}R})}\leq C(R)
, which implies the claim (
2.2
) immediately.

By (
2.2
) and the standard elliptic theory, we know that
v_{k}
converges in
C^{2}(B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega_{k}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a})
to a function
v
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v=e^{v}\ \ &in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}_{a},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{v}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a},\end{cases}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}}e^{v}dx+\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}}e^{\frac{v}{2}}ds_{x}\leq C<\infty.

Using the classification result in
[
11
]
, we know

v(x)=\log\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{\Big[\lambda^{2}+(x^{1}+s_{0})^{2}+\Big(x^{2}+a+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{2}},\ \ x=(x^{1},x^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

for some
\lambda>0
and
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}}e^{v}dx+\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a}}e^{\frac{v}{2}}ds_{x}=4\pi.

Moreover, we can choose a sequence
R_{k}\to\infty
such that
\lambda_{k}R_{k}\to 0
and passing to a subsequence, there holds

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|v_{k}(x)-v(x)\|_{C^{1}(B_{2R_{k}}\cap\Omega_{k}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{a})}=o(1)\ \ and\ \ \int_{B_{R_{k}}\cap\Omega_{k}}e^{v_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{B_{R_{k}}\cap\partial^{0}\Omega_{k}}e^{\frac{1}{2}v_{k}(x)}ds_{x}=4\pi+o(1),

where
\partial^{0}\Omega_{k}:=\{x=(x^{1},x^{2})\in\overline{\Omega}_{k}\ \ |\ \ x^{2}=-\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, case
(1)
will not happen, i.e. only case
(2)
holds. Moreover, we have

\int_{\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{v_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{\frac{1}{2}v_{k}(x)}ds_{x}=o(1).

Proof.

It follows from the results in
[
1
]
or
[
14
]
that

(2.4)

\int_{B^{+}}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}e^{\frac{u_{k}(x)}{2}}ds_{x}=4\pi+o(1).

If case
(1)
holds, then it is easy to get that

\int_{B^{+}}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx\geq 8\pi,

which is a contradiction. Thus case
(1)
will not happen. Since case
(2)
implies (
2.3
), the second conclusion of the lemma follows immediately from (
2.4
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

So far, one can see that we have proved conclusions
(1)-(3)
of Theorem
1.1
. In the sequel, we focus on the proof of conclusion
(4)
.

Since the solution has only one bubble, by the selection of bubbling areas in
[
10
]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

u_{k}(x)+2\log|x-x_{k}|\leq C.

Proof.

From (
2.3
), we see

v_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq C,\ \ |x|\leq 2R_{k},\ x\in\Omega_{k},

which implies

u_{k}(x)+2\log|x-x_{k}|\leq C,\ \ |x-x_{k}|\leq 2\lambda_{k}R_{k},\ x\in B^{+}.

Now we claim

u_{k}(x)+2\log|x-x_{k}|\leq C,\ \ x\in B^{+}.

Otherwise, there exists
y_{k}\in\overline{B^{+}}
such that
|y_{k}-x_{k}|\geq 2\lambda_{k}R_{k}
and

u_{k}(y_{k})+2\log|y_{k}-x_{k}|\to+\infty.

Denote
t_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}|x_{k}-y_{k}|
and

\phi_{k}(x):=u_{k}(x)+2\log(t_{k}-|x-y_{k}|).

Let
p_{k}
be the maximal point such that

\phi_{k}(p_{k})=\max_{x\in\overline{B^{+}_{t_{k}}(y_{k})}}\phi_{k}(x).

It is easy to check that

\phi_{k}(p_{k})\geq\phi_{k}(y_{k})\to+\infty,

which also implies
u_{k}(p_{k})\to+\infty
.

Denote

s_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}(t_{k}-|p_{k}-y_{k}|)\ \ and\ \ \epsilon_{k}:=e^{-\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(p_{k})}.

Since
\phi_{k}(p_{k})\to+\infty
, we have

\frac{s_{k}}{\epsilon_{k}}\to+\infty.

Noting that for any
x\in B^{+}_{s_{k}}(p_{k})
, there hold

u_{k}(x)+2\log|t_{k}-|x-y_{k}||\leq\phi_{k}(p_{k})=u_{k}(p_{k})+2\log(2s_{k})

and

t_{k}-|x-y_{k}|\geq t_{k}-|x-p_{k}|-|p_{k}-y_{k}|\geq s_{k},

we get

u_{k}(x)\leq u_{k}(p_{k})+2\log 2,\ \ \forall\ x\in B^{+}_{s_{k}}(p_{k}).

Set

w_{k}(x)=u_{k}(p_{k}+\epsilon_{k}x)+2\log\epsilon_{k},p_{k}+\epsilon_{k}x\in B^{+}.

It is easy to check that

p_{k}+\epsilon_{k}x\in B^{+},w_{k}(0)=0,\,w_{k}(x)\leq 2\log 2,\ \ \forall\,\ |x|\leq\frac{s_{k}}{\epsilon_{k}}.

Now, we distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1:
\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{dist(p_{k},\partial^{0}B^{+})}{\epsilon_{k}}=+\infty
.

In this case, we see that
w_{k}(x)
is well defined in a domain which converges to the whole plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. Then by the standard theory of Liouville type equation, we know that
w_{k}(x)\to w(x)
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
w(x)
satisfies Liouville equation (
2.1
). Moreover, we can select a sequence
R^{1}_{k}\to\infty
such that
R^{1}_{k}\epsilon_{k}=o(1)s_{k}
and passing to a subsequence, there holds

\|w_{k}(x)-w(x)\|_{C^{0}(B_{R^{1}_{k}})}\to 0.

It is easy to check that

B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k})\cap B_{\epsilon_{k}R^{1}_{k}}(p_{k})=\emptyset.

Hence

\int_{B^{+}}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx\geq\int_{B_{\epsilon_{k}R^{1}_{k}}}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx=8\pi+o(1),

which is a contradiction.

Case 2:
\lim_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{dist(p_{k},\partial^{0}B^{+})}{\epsilon_{k}}=b<+\infty
.

In this case, the domain
\Omega^{1}_{k}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ p_{k}+\epsilon_{k}x\in B^{+}\}
converges to
\mathbb{R}_{b}^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. By a similar argument as before, we know that
w_{k}(x)\to w(x)
in
C^{1}(B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega^{1}_{k})
, where
w(x)
is a solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w=e^{w}\ \ &in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}_{b},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{w}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{b}.\end{cases}

Moreover, we can select a sequence
R^{1}_{k}\to\infty
such that
R^{1}_{k}\epsilon_{k}=o(1)s_{k}
and passing to a subsequence, there holds

\|w_{k}(x)-w(x)\|_{C^{1}(B_{R^{1}_{k}}\cap\Omega^{1}_{k})}\to 0.

It is easy to check that

B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k})\cap B^{+}_{\epsilon_{k}R^{1}_{k}}(p_{k})=\emptyset.

As a result,

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}e^{\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x)}ds_{x}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k})}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}R_{k}}(x_{k})}e^{\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x)}ds_{x}+\int_{B^{+}_{\epsilon_{k}R^{1}_{k}}(p_{k})}e^{u_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\epsilon_{k}R^{1}_{k}}(p_{k})}e^{\frac{1}{2}u_{k}(x)}ds_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi+o(1),

which is also a contradiction.

We proved the lemma.

∎

With the help of Lemma
2.3
, we have the following bounded oscillation estimate near the boundary.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u_{k}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.6
) satisfying (
1.7
)-(
1.8
). Then

osc_{B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}d_{k}(x)}(x)}u_{k}\leq C,\ \ \forall x\in B^{+}\setminus\{x_{k}\},

where
d_{k}(x)=|x-x_{k}|
and
C
is a universal constant independent of
k
and
x
.

Proof.

Let
w_{k}
be the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{k}=-\Delta u_{k},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+},\\
w_{k}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}.\end{cases}

Define

(2.5)

\phi_{k}(x):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}\log|x-y|e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y},\ \ x\in B^{+}.

Then we can check

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ \ B^{+},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{u_{k}(x)}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}.\end{cases}

Letting
\eta_{k}=u_{k}-w_{k}-\phi_{k}
, there holds

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\eta_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ \ B^{+},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+},\\
\eta_{k}=u_{k},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}.\end{cases}

Extending
\eta_{k}
evenly, by maximal principle, we have

osc_{B^{+}}\eta_{k}(x)\leq osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}}u_{k}(x)=o(1).

Extending
w_{k}
and
u_{k}
evenly (we still use the same notations), by Green’s formula, we have

w_{k}(x)=\int_{B_{1}(0)}G(x,y)e^{u_{k}(y)}dy,

where

G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x-y|+H(x,y)

is the Green’s function on
B_{1}
with respect to the Dirichlet boundary and
H(x,y)
is a smooth harmonic function.

For any
x\in B^{+}\setminus\{x_{k}\}
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}d_{k}(x)}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle w_{k}(p_{1})-w_{k}(p_{2})+\phi_{k}(p_{1})-\phi_{k}(p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(G(p_{1},y)-G(p_{2},y)\right)e^{u_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{u_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}(x)}(x)}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{u_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}(x)}^{+}(x)}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B\setminus B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}(x)}(x)}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{u_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}(B^{+}\setminus B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}(x)}^{+}(x))}\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}+O(1).

We first estimate
\mathbf{II}
. Noting that
|y-x|\geq\frac{3}{4}d_{k}(x)
, we find

\left|\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}\right|\leq C,

which implies
\mathbf{II}=O(1)
.

For
\mathbf{I}
, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}}(x)}\left|\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}\right|e^{u_{k}(y)}dy+C\int_{\partial^{0}B_{\frac{3}{4}d_{k}}^{+}(x)}\left|\log\frac{|p_{1}-y|}{|p_{2}-y|}\right|e^{\frac{u_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\left|\log\frac{|p_{1}-x-d_{k}(x)y|}{|p_{2}-x-d_{k}(x)y|}\right|e^{u_{k}(x+d_{k}(x)y)}d^{2}_{k}(x)dy

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\partial^{0}B_{\frac{3}{4}}^{+}(0)}\left|\log\frac{|p_{1}-x-d_{k}(x)y|}{|p_{2}-x-d_{k}(x)y|}\right|e^{\frac{u_{k}(x+d_{k}(x)y)}{2}}d_{k}(x)ds_{y}

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}\left|\log\frac{|\frac{p_{1}-x}{d_{k}(x)}-y|}{|\frac{p_{2}-x}{d_{k}(x)}-y|}\right|e^{u_{k}(x+d_{k}(x)y)}d^{2}_{k}(x)dy+C\int_{\partial^{0}B_{\frac{3}{4}}^{+}(0)}\left|\log\frac{|\frac{p_{1}-x}{d_{k}(x)}-y|}{|\frac{p_{2}-x}{d_{k}(x)}-y|}\right|e^{\frac{u_{k}(x+d_{k}(x)y)}{2}}d_{k}(x)ds_{y}.

Noting

|x+d_{k}(x)y-x_{k}|\geq|x-x_{k}|-|d_{k}(x)y|\geq\frac{1}{4}d_{k}(x),\ \ \forall y\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0),

by Lemma
2.3
, we have

u_{k}(x+d_{k}(x)y)\leq C-2\log|x+d_{k}(x)y-x_{k}|\leq C-2\log d_{k}(x),\ \ \forall y\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0).

From

\left|\frac{p_{1}-x}{d_{k}(x)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2},\ \ \left|\frac{p_{2}-x}{d_{k}(x)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2},

one can easily find that
\mathbf{I}=O(1)
, which implies the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

At the end of this section, we prove the following fast decay lemma, which will be used in next section to estimate a upper bound of solutions and to control the radial oscillation of solutions. This type of fast decay definition was firstly introduced in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a number sequence
\{N_{k}\}
which tends to
+\infty
as
k\to\infty
, such that

v_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-N_{k},\ \ |x|\geq R_{k},\ \ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), we know

v_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,\ \ \forall\ R_{k}\leq|x|\leq 2R_{k},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Denote
\tilde{x}_{k}:=(0,-\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}})
and

(2.8)

\tilde{v}_{k}(x)=v_{k}(x+\tilde{x}_{k}),\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.

Noting that
|\tilde{x}_{k}|\leq C
, it is easy to check that

(2.9)

\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,\ \ \forall x\in B_{2R_{k}}^{+}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}.

Set

(2.10)

\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(r):=\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{r}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)d\theta,\ \ 0<r\leq\frac{3}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

On one hand, by Lemma
2.4
, we have

(2.11)

osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{r}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq osc_{\frac{1}{2}r\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{2}r,\ x\in\Omega_{k}}v_{k}(x)\leq C,\ \ \forall\ r\in\Big[R_{k},\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}\Big].

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.11
), we obtain

(2.12)

\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(2R_{k})+2\log(2R_{k})\leq-2\log R_{k}+C.

On the other hand, defining

(2.13)

\alpha(r):=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\int_{B^{+}_{r}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)ds_{x}\right)

and making a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{B^{+}_{r}}\Delta\tilde{v}_{k}(x)dx-\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)ds_{x}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{B^{+}_{r}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(x)}dx-\frac{1}{\pi r}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)ds_{x}:=-\frac{1}{r}\alpha(r).

Thus, for any
t\in[2R_{k},\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}]
, there holds

\displaystyle\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(t)+2\log t

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(2R_{k})+2\log(2R_{k})+\int_{2R_{k}}^{t}\frac{d}{dr}\left(\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(r)+2\log r\right)dr

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(2R_{k})+2\log(2R_{k})+\int_{2R_{k}}^{t}\frac{2-\alpha(r)}{r}dr

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(2R_{k})+2\log(2R_{k})\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,

where the last second inequality follows from

\alpha(r)\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(\int_{B^{+}_{R_{k}}}e^{v_{k}(x)}dx+\int_{B_{R_{k}}\cap\partial^{0}\Omega_{k}}e^{\frac{1}{2}v_{k}(x)}ds_{x}\Big)=\frac{4\pi+o(1)}{\pi}=4+o(1)

and the last inequality follows from (
2.12
).

Combining this with (
2.11
) and (
2.9
), we get

\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,\ \ \forall x\in B_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}^{+}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}},

and hence

v_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,\ \ \forall x\in B_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Combining this with the fact that

osc_{x\in B_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{-1}_{k}},\ x\in\Omega_{k}}v_{k}\leq C,

and using Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that

v_{k}(x)+2\log|x|\leq-2\log R_{k}+C,\ \ \forall x\in B_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Therefore the conclusion of the lemma follows immediately by taking
N_{k}=2\log R_{k}+C
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we first derive a uniformly upper bound of
v_{k}
in its whole definition domain and then prove a pointwise estimate of
\nabla v_{k}
when
|x|
is large , which yields the control of tangential oscillation. We will also derive a energy decay lemma which is crucial in our proof. The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given at the end of this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have

v_{k}(x)\leq-(4-2\delta)\log|x|+C,\ \ x\in\Omega_{k},\ |x|\geq 2,

for
k
large enough .

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is more or less standard now. See for example
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. For reader’s convenience and our later proof, here we give a detailed proof.

By Lemma
2.4
, we know

osc_{\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}v_{k}(x)\leq C.

So it suffices to prove that

v_{k}(x)\leq-(4-2\delta)\log|x|+C,\ \ 2\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

By (
2.3
), we know

v_{k}(x)\leq-4\log|x|+C,\ \ 2\leq|x|\leq R_{k},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Hence, we only need to show that

v_{k}(x)\leq-(4-2\delta)\log|x|,\ \ R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}_{k},\ x\in\Omega_{k}.

Considering
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{k}}{\lambda_{k}}=a<\infty
, we find that the above estimate is equivalent to

(3.1)

\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq-(4-2\delta)\log|x|,\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-1}_{k}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}},

where
\tilde{v}_{k}(x)
is defined by (
2.8
) which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{v}_{k}(x)=e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(x)},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}(x)}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{\tilde{v}_{k}(x)}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq 0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Let
w_{k}
be the solution of

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w_{k}=-\Delta\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
w_{k}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Define

(3.3)

\phi_{k}(x):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log|x-y|e^{\frac{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}{2}}ds_{y},\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.

It is easy to see that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=e^{\frac{\tilde{v}_{k}(X)}{2}},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Letting
\eta_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k}-w_{k}-\phi_{k}
, there holds

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\eta_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ \ B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\eta_{k}}{\partial\overrightarrow{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\\
\eta_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ &on\ \ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.\end{cases}

Extending
\eta_{k}
evenly, by maximal principle, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\eta_{k}(x)-\eta_{k}(0)\leq osc_{B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\eta_{k}(x)\leq osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq osc_{B^{+}\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u_{k}(x)\leq C,

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
.

Now we show that

(3.7)

w_{k}(x)+\phi_{k}(x)-w_{k}(0)-\phi_{k}(0)\leq-(4-2\delta)\log|x|+O(1),\ \ R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1},

which implies (
3.1
) since
\tilde{v}_{k}(0)\leq C
.

Extending
w_{k}
evenly, by Green’s formula, we have

\displaystyle w_{k}(x)=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}G(2\lambda_{k}x,2\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy,\ \ \forall\ x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle w_{k}(x)+\phi_{k}(x)-w_{k}(0)-\phi_{k}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log|x|\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+O(1)

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}\Big)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+O(1),

where
\alpha(r)
is defined by (
2.13
).

We claim that

(3.9)

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy=o(1)\log|x|,\ \ \forall\,R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}

and

(3.10)

-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}=o(1)\log|x|,\ \ \forall\,R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

In fact,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\ |\ |y|\geq 2|x|\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+\int_{\{y\in B_{2|x|}\ |\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq R_{k}\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+\int_{\{|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}+\mathbf{I}_{3}+\mathbf{I}_{4}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\ |\ |y|\geq 2|x|\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\ |\ |y|\leq 2|x|,\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq R_{k}\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle+\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\ |\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+\int_{\{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\ |\ |y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{1}+\mathbf{II}_{2}+\mathbf{II}_{3}+\mathbf{II}_{4}.

For
\mathbf{I}_{1}
and
\mathbf{II}_{1}
, since
|y|\geq 2|x|
, it holds

\frac{1}{2|x|}\leq\frac{1}{|x|}-\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\leq\frac{1}{|x|}+\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{3}{2|x|},

which implies

|\mathbf{I}_{1}|\leq C\log|x|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy=o(1)\log|x|

and

|\mathbf{II}_{1}|\leq C\log|x|\int_{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y|\geq R_{k}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}=o(1)\log|x|.

For
\mathbf{I}_{2}
and
\mathbf{II}_{2}
, since
|y|\leq 2|x|
and
|y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|
, it holds

\frac{1}{2|y|}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\leq\frac{1}{|x|}+\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{3}{|y|},

which implies

\left|\log\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\right|\leq C\log|y|\leq C\log|x|.

Hence,

|\mathbf{I}_{2}|\leq C\log|x|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy=o(1)\log|x|

and

|\mathbf{II}_{2}|\leq C\log|x|\int_{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ \ |y|\geq R_{k}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}=o(1)\log|x|.

For
\mathbf{I}_{3}
and
\mathbf{II}_{3}
, since
|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|
, it holds that
\frac{1}{2}|x|\leq|y|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x|
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|e^{v_{k}(y)}dy-\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}(\log|x|+\log|y|)e^{v_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|e^{v_{k}(y)}dy+o(1)\log|x|

\displaystyle=\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|\frac{o(1)}{|y|^{2}}dy+o(1)\log|x|

\displaystyle=\frac{o(1)}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|dy+o(1)\log|x|=o(1)\log|x|

where we have used the fact that
v_{k}(y)+2\log|y|\leq-N_{k}
which follows from Lemma
2.5
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}-\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}(\log|x|+\log|y|)e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+o(1)\log|x|

\displaystyle=\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|\frac{o(1)}{|y|}ds_{y}+o(1)\log|x|

\displaystyle=\frac{o(1)}{|x|}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},\ |y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\log|x-y|ds_{y}+o(1)\log|x|=o(1)\log|x|.

For
\mathbf{I}_{4}
and
\mathbf{II}_{4}
, since
|y|\leq R_{k}
and
|x|\geq 2R_{k}
, we have

\frac{1}{2|y|}\leq\frac{1}{|y|}-\frac{1}{|x|}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{|x||y|}\leq\frac{1}{|x|}+\frac{1}{|y|}\leq\frac{3}{2|y|}.

Since
\tilde{v}_{k}(x)\leq 0
, we get

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\big|\log|y|\big|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{|y|\leq 2\}}\big|\log|y|\big|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+C\int_{\{2\leq|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log|y|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{|y|\leq 2\}}\big|\log|y|\big|dy+C\int_{\{2\leq|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log|y|\frac{1}{|y|^{4}}dy\leq C

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{II}_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\big|\log|y|\big|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq 2\}}\big|\log|y|\big|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},2\leq|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log|y|e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq 2\}}\big|\log|y|\big|ds_{y}+C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},2\leq|y|\leq R_{k}\}}\log|y|\frac{1}{|y|^{4}}ds_{y}\leq C.

As a result, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) hold true. Therefore, by (
3
), we get

w_{k}(x)+\phi_{k}(x)-w_{k}(0)-\phi_{k}(0)=-\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}\Big)\log|x|+o(1)\log|x|+O(1),\ \ R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1},

which yields (
3.7
) since

\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}\Big)=4+o(1)

by Lemma
2.1
. We proved the lemma.
∎

With the help of Lemma
3.1
, we get a pointwise estimate of first derivative as follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}(x)-\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(2\lambda_{k}x,2\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\alpha(|x|)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{5/4}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{3/2}}+o(1)\lambda_{k},\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}

and

\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}(x)=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\alpha(|x|)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{5/4}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{3/2}}+O(1)\lambda_{k},\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}.

Proof.

We just need to show the first conclusion, since the second one follows from the fact

\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(2\lambda_{k}x,2\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy=O(1)\lambda_{k},\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}.

We use the notations as in Lemma
3.1
. Let
w_{k}(x),\ \phi_{k}(x),\ \eta_{k}(x)
be defined as before. Extending
\eta_{k}
evenly and by using Green’s formula, we have

\eta_{k}(x)-\eta_{k}(0)=\int_{\partial B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(G(2\lambda_{k}x,2\lambda_{k}y)\right)\left(\eta_{k}(y)-\eta_{k}(0)\right)ds_{y},

which implies

(3.11)

\nabla\eta_{k}(x)=o(1)\lambda_{k},\ \ \forall|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda^{-1}_{k}.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\nabla w_{k}(x)+\nabla\phi_{k}(x)-\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(\lambda_{k}x,\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{|y|\leq 2|x|,\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq 2|x|,\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x^{s}|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbf{III}_{1}+\mathbf{III}_{2}+\mathbf{III}_{3}+\mathbf{III}_{4},

where
s\in(0,1)
is a constant which will be chosen later.

For
\mathbf{III}_{1}
, since
|y|\geq 2|x|
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\left|\int_{\{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}\int_{\{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{4-2\delta}}dy\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{3-2\delta}}

and

\left|\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\geq 2|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2-\delta}}ds_{y}\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2-\delta}},

which implies

\mathbf{III}_{1}=\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-\delta}}.

For
\mathbf{III}_{2}
, since
|y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|
, by Lemma
3.1
, we see

\left|\int_{\{|y|\leq 2|x|,\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}\int_{\{|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq 2|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{4-2\delta}}dy\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{1+(2-2\delta)s}}

and

\left|\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq 2|x|,\ |y-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|,\ |y|\geq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq 2|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2-\delta}}ds_{y}\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{1+(1-\delta)s}},

which implies

\mathbf{III}_{2}=\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1+(1-\delta)s}}.

For
\mathbf{III}_{3}
, since
|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|
, it holds
\frac{1}{2}|x|\leq|y|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x|
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, we get

\left|\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\right|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{4-2\delta}}\int_{\{|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y-x|}dy\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{3-2\delta}}

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2-\delta}}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y-x|}ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2-\delta}}\left(|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|+\log|x|\right),

which implies

\mathbf{III}_{3}=\frac{O(1)\log|x|}{|x|^{2-\delta}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{2-\delta}}.

For
\mathbf{III}_{4}
, since

\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}=\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}},

we find

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|^{s}\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\left(\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\int_{\{|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\right)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1+(2-2\delta)s}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}

and

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|^{s}\}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{2}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|^{s}\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\left(\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}-\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}\right)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1+(1-\delta)s}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}},

where we have used the fact that

\int_{\{|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy\leq C\int_{\{|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{4-2\delta}}dy=\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{(2-2\delta)s}}

and

\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}\leq C\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|x|^{s}\leq|y|\leq|x|\}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2-\delta}}ds_{y}=\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{(1-\delta)s}}.

Thus,

\mathbf{III}_{4}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1+(1-\delta)s}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}.

Combining these together, we get

\displaystyle\nabla w_{k}(x)+\nabla\phi_{k}(x)-\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(\lambda_{k}x,\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}dy-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}},|y|\leq|x|\}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1+(1-\delta)s}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-s}}+\frac{O(1)\log|x|}{|x|^{2-\delta}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{2-\delta}}.

Taking
\delta=s=\frac{1}{2}
, we deduce

\nabla w_{k}(x)+\nabla\phi_{k}(x)-\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(\lambda_{k}x,\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\alpha(|x|)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{5/4}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{3/2}},

which implies

\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}(x)-\nabla\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(\lambda_{k}x,\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\alpha(|x|)+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{5/4}}+O(1)\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{|x|^{3/2}}+o(1)\lambda_{k}.

∎

As a corollary of above lemma, we can control the tangential oscillation as follows.

Corollary 3.3
.

We have

\sup_{R_{k}\leq t\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}osc_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\tilde{v}_{k}=o(1).

Proof.

For any
R_{k}\leq t\leq\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and any two points
(t,\theta_{1}),(t,\theta_{2})\in\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}
, by Lemma
3.2
, it holds

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{1})-\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\theta_{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta)}{\partial\theta}d\theta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\left|\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}{\partial\theta}\right|ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{t^{5/4}}+\frac{|\log dist(y,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{t^{3/2}}+\lambda_{k}\right)ds_{y}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\frac{|\log dist(y,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{t^{3/2}}ds_{y}+o(1).

Taking the coordinate notation
y=(y^{1},y^{2})
and noting that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}}|\log dist(y,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|ds_{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}}|\log|y^{2}||ds_{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{y\in\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t},|y^{2}|\leq 1}|\log|y^{2}||ds_{y}+\int_{y\in\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t},|y^{2}|\geq 1}|\log|y^{2}||ds_{y}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|\log y^{2}|dy^{2}+C\int_{y\in\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}}\log tds_{y}\leq Ct\log t,

we get

(3.13)

\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{1})-\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{2})=o(1),\ \ R_{k}\leq t\leq\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For any
\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}
and any two points
z_{1}=(t,\theta_{1}),z_{2}=(t,\theta_{2})\in\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}
, set

F(x)=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}H(2\lambda_{k}x,2\lambda_{k}y)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy.

On one hand, by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{1})-F(z_{1})-\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{2})+F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\theta_{2}}^{\theta_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta)-F(x))d\theta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\left|\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(\tilde{v}_{k}(x)-F(x))\right|ds_{x}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{t^{5/4}}+\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{t^{3/2}}+o(1)\lambda_{k}\right)ds_{x}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=C\int_{\partial^{+}B_{t}^{+}}\frac{|\log dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})|}{t^{3/2}}ds_{x}+o(1)=o(1).

On the other hand, by the formula of Green’s function for the unit ball, we know

H(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\left||x|\Big(y-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\Big)\right|.

Hence, we see

\displaystyle|F(z_{1})-F(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\left(H(2\lambda_{k}z_{1},2\lambda_{k}y)-H(2\lambda_{k}z_{2},2\lambda_{k}y)\right)e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy\right|+C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left|\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\log\frac{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{1}|}{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{2}|}e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy\right|+C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\left|\log\frac{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{1}|}{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{2}|}\right|e^{\tilde{v}(y)}dy+C\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\setminus B_{R_{k}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{4-2\delta}}dy=o(1),

where we have used Lemma
3.1
and the following fact that

\left|\log\frac{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{1}|}{|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y-z_{2}|}\right|=o(1)

as
k\to\infty
since
\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}
,
|4\lambda_{k}^{2}t^{2}y|\leq CR_{k}
and
\lambda_{k}R_{k}=o(1)
.

By (
3
) and (
3
), we get

\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{1})-\tilde{v}_{k}(t,\theta_{2})=o(1),\ \ \lambda_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

Combining this with (
3.13
), we proved the corollary.
∎

With the help of Lemma
3.2
, using Pohozaev’s type identity, we get the following energy decay estimate.

Lemma 3.4
.

There holds

\displaystyle\alpha(|x|)=4+\displaystyle\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{\frac{1}{4}}},\ \ \forall\,R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

Proof.

By the Pohozaev identity, we have

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{B^{+}_{t}}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}}dx+\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{t}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}}ds_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\int_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t}}\left(\Big|\frac{\partial\tilde{v}_{k}}{\partial r}\Big|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\tilde{v}_{k}|^{2}+e^{\tilde{v}_{k}}\right)ds_{x}+2te^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(t,0)}+2te^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(-t,0)},\ \ \forall\,0<t\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

Taking
t=|x|
in above equality, by Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.1
and (
3
), we find

\displaystyle 2\pi\alpha(|x|)=\frac{1}{2}\pi\alpha^{2}(|x|)+\alpha(|x|)\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{\frac{1}{4}}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{2-2\delta}}+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{1-\delta}}.

Then taking
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, it holds

(3.16)

\alpha(|x|)=4+\frac{O(1)}{|x|^{\frac{1}{4}}},\ \ \forall\,R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}.

∎

Using the above energy decay estimate, we can control the oscillation of radial part as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}(x)-v(x)\|_{C^{0}(B_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}\cap\Omega_{k})}=0.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), one can see that the proposition follows from the following estimate

\lim_{k\to 0}osc_{R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1},x\in\Omega_{k}}(v_{k}(x)-v(x))=0.

Noting that

v(x)+4\log|x|=o(1),\ \ \forall|x|\geq R_{k},

we just need to show that

\lim_{k\to 0}osc_{R_{k}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1},x\in\Omega_{k}}(v_{k}(x)+4\log|x|)=0,

which is equivalent to

(3.17)

\lim_{k\to 0}osc_{x\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda^{-1}_{k}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}}(\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+4\log|x|)=0,

where
\tilde{v}_{k}
was defined by (
2.8
).

In fact, for any two points
p_{1}=(t_{1},\theta_{1}),p_{2}=(t_{2},\theta_{2})\in B^{+}_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda^{-1}_{k}}\setminus B^{+}_{R_{k}}
(without loss of generality, we assume
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
), there holds

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{v}_{k}(p_{1})+4\log|p_{1}|-\tilde{v}_{k}(p_{2})-4\log|p_{2}|\right|

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(t_{1})+4\log|p_{1}|-\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(t_{2})-4\log|p_{2}|\right|+osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t_{1}}}\tilde{v}_{k}+osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{t_{2}}}\tilde{v}_{k},

where
\tilde{v}_{k}^{*}
was defined by (
2.10
).

It follows from (
2
) and Lemma
3.4
that

\frac{d\tilde{v}_{k}^{*}}{dr}=-\frac{4}{r}+\frac{O(1)}{r^{5/4}},

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(t_{1})+4\log|p_{1}|-\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(t_{2})-4\log|p_{2}|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{d}{dr}(\tilde{v}^{*}_{k}(r)+4\log r)dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{1}{r^{5/4}}dr\leq\frac{C}{R_{k}^{1/4}}=o(1).

Combining this with (
3
) and Corollary
3.3
, we get (
3.17
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.5
, we need to show that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}(x)-v(x)\|_{C^{0}(\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}})}=0,

which is equivalent to

(3.19)

\lim_{k\to\infty}osc_{\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}}\left(v_{k}(x)+4\log|x|\right)=0.

Noting that
\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{\frac{3}{8}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(0)\subset\Omega_{k}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(-\frac{x_{k}}{\lambda_{k}})
, one can see that (
3.19
) is a consequence of the following fact

(3.20)

\lim_{k\to\infty}osc_{B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\big(\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+4\log|x|\big)=0,

where
\bar{x}_{k}:=-\frac{(x^{1}_{k},0)}{\lambda_{k}}
and
\tilde{v}_{k}
was defined by (
2.8
).

Let
w^{1}_{k}
be the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w^{1}_{k}=-\Delta\tilde{v}_{k},\ \ &in\ B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{1}_{k}}{\partial\vec{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\\
w^{1}_{k}=0,\ \ &on\ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\cup\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}).\end{cases}

Extending
w^{1}_{k}
evenly, by standard elliptic theory and Lemma
3.1
, we can check

\displaystyle\|w^{1}_{k}\|_{C^{0}(B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\lambda_{k}^{-2}e^{\tilde{v}_{k}(\lambda_{k}^{-1}(x+\lambda_{k}\bar{x}_{k}))}\|_{L^{2}(B^{+}_{1}(0)\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\lambda_{k}^{-2}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{k}^{-1}(x+\lambda_{k}\bar{x}_{k})|^{4-2\delta}}\|_{L^{2}(B^{+}_{1}(0)\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\lambda_{k}^{2-2\delta}\frac{1}{|x-(x^{1}_{k},0)|^{4-2\delta}}\|_{L^{2}(B^{+}_{1}(0)\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}}(0))}\leq C\lambda_{k}^{2-2\delta}=o(1),

since
x_{k}=(x^{1}_{k},x^{2}_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Denote

w^{2}_{k}(x):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\log|x-y|g(y)ds_{y},\ \ x\in B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),

where
g(y)=0
if
y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})
and
g(y)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}
if
y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})
.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w^{2}_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{2}_{k}}{\partial\vec{n}}=g,\ \ &on\ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}).\end{cases}

Moreover, we claim

\|w^{2}_{k}\|_{C^{0}(B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}))}=o(1).

In fact, for any
x\in B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})
, if
dist(x,\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})>1
, then by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle|w^{2}_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\log\lambda_{k}^{-1}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}|g(y)|ds_{y}

\displaystyle=C\log\lambda_{k}^{-1}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{v}_{k}(y)}ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\log\lambda_{k}^{-1}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\frac{1}{|y|^{2-\delta}}ds_{y}

\displaystyle=C\lambda_{k}^{1-\delta}\log\lambda_{k}^{-1}=o(1).

If
dist(x,\partial^{0}\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\leq 1
, from Lemma
3.1
, it holds

\displaystyle|w^{2}_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\log|x-y|g(y)ds_{y}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{k}^{2-\delta}\int_{\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\log|x-y|ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{k}^{2-\delta}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\ |y-x|\leq 1\}}\log|x-y|ds_{y}

\displaystyle\quad+C\lambda_{k}^{2-\delta}\int_{\{y\in\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\ |y-x|\geq 1\}}\log|x-y|ds_{y}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{k}^{2-\delta}(1+\lambda_{k}^{-1}\log\lambda_{k}^{-1})=o(1),

which implies the claim.

Now let
w^{3}_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k}+4\log|x|-w^{1}_{k}(x)-w^{2}_{k}(x)
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w^{3}_{k}=0,\ \ &in\ B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{3}_{k}}{\partial\vec{n}}=0,\ \ &on\ \partial^{0}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus\partial^{0}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}),\\
w^{3}_{k}=\tilde{v}_{k}+4\log|x|-w^{2}_{k}(x),\ \ &on\ \partial^{+}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\cup\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}).\end{cases}

Extend
w^{3}_{k}
evenly and note that

\displaystyle osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}(\tilde{v}_{k}+4\log|x|-w^{2}_{k}(x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}}u_{k}(x)+osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}\log|x|+osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}w^{2}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1)

and

\displaystyle osc_{\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}(\tilde{v}_{k}+4\log|x|-w^{2}_{k}(x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+4\log|x|\big\|_{C^{0}(\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}))}+\|w^{2}_{k}(x)\|_{C^{0}(\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1),

where we have used the fact that
\big\|\tilde{v}_{k}(x)+4\log|x|\big\|_{C^{0}(\partial^{+}B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k}))}=o(1)
, which follows from Proposition
3.5
.

By maximal principle, we deduce

osc_{B^{+}_{\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})\setminus B^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}(\bar{x}_{k})}w^{3}_{k}=o(1).

Combining these together, we get (
3.20
).

Consequently, we complete the proof.
∎

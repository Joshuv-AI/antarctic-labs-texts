Derivation of Solar Position Formulae
Ross Ure Anderson

Abstract




Abstract.
Derivation of the following formulae for solar position as seen from orbiting planet based on a simplified model: sunrise direction formula, solar declination formula, sunrise equation, daylight duration formula, solar altitude formula, solar azimuth formula. Use of notion of effective axial tilt, and Rodrigues Rotation Formula, and reflections of the orbital quadrants to reduce the general case to the simpler case of the day of the winter solstice. Derivation of equations for solar time to clock time conversion, and sunrise, sunset, and solar noon times. Implementation of an analemma calculator. Comparison of the sunrise direction formula with 304 point dataset of actual sunrise data from Earth, obtaining average accuracy of
1.25^{\circ}
, and estimate of Earth’s axial tilt of
23.52^{\circ}
— within
0.1^{\circ}
of the currently accepted value
23.44^{\circ}
.

This article first derives a formula for the sunrise direction for a planet orbiting a central sun, in terms of the day of the year and the latitude, under a simplified model. Then, building on the method of proof used a number of further formulae relating to the solar position

The term ‘winter solstice’ refers to the standard notion of ‘winter’ of the northern hemisphere, even though towards the equator the terms winter and summer lose their normal meanings — for example on the equator maximal solar radiation is received at the equinoxes and is minimal at both summer and winter solstices

Though astronomical terminology is used throughout, the problems of the solar position under the simplified model are purely geometric involving a sphere on an elliptical path intersected by parallel rays from the direction of one of the path’s foci. For example the point of sunrise/sunset on a planet is simply when these rays become parallel to the tangent plane at the point. The desired sunrise/sunset direction is then the direction of the rays wrt true north

A chief interest of the sunrise direction formula is how in relating the above quantities measurable by naked eye from the Earth using only primitive instruments, the hypothesis of a spherical Earth in a circular orbit and rotating about a fixed axis is arrived at using only a geometric argument — since the close correlation with the actual data would be highly unlikely to occur unless the above simplified model was a good approximation in practice. Thus it is one method from which the ancients could have determined the Earth shape and motions from only naked eye observations and geometry, without the benefit of modern astronomy.

For non-pole latitudes beyond one of the arctic circles such non-diurnal sunrises and sunsets also occur at certain times of the year — when periods of perpetual day or night are entered. Outside those periods the sunrise and sunset are diurnal. These changes occur as the effective axial tilt changes so the actual latitude travels above and below the effective arctic circle (the latter becoming a point at the pole on the spring equinox). In the sunrise direction formula the periods of perpetual day and night correspond with the

In this section we relate the clock time CT (or civil time) to the solar time for the Earth
4
4

4

We shall assume a 24 hour clock.
. The solar time is based on the daily passage of the Sun, with a day defined as the time between successive solar noons (§
4

[
29

0^{\circ}

[

[

]

]

Analemma

One form of analemma
[
43
]
is a graph of the Sun’s position in the sky as seen from a fixed location on Earth, as altitude plotted against azimuth, at the same local clock time CT (ex-DST) each day for a period of a year. With equal horizontal and vertical scales this will have the same shape as a trace of the Sun’s actual positions over these times, for example as captured in analemma photographs. The irregular figure 8 shape of the analemma (Figure
8
) is a result of the deviation throughout the year of the AST from the MST, as given by the equation of time (§
5
). In the present simplified model the analemma is given by equations (
24
), (
38
), and (
39
) together with equation (
18
) which for a fixed daily local clock time CT, produces a varying solar hour angle
\tau
for the day
d
, which is then entered in equations (
24
), (
38
), and (
39
) to produce the altitude
\mu
and azimuth
\phi
. The parameter
\lambda
also varies daily, according to equation (
10
). If the solar day length were constant throughout the year so that MST = AST, and
EOT(d)=0\;\forall d
, then
\tau
would be constant for clock time CT each day, and the analemma would be a simple curve without any loops because the solar declination parameter
\lambda
retraces its values in
[0,180^{\circ}]
in the return journey from
180^{\circ}
to
360^{\circ}
due to the
\cos\psi
term in equation (
10
).

The Perl script
calc-analemma.pl
in Appendix
C.3
calculates the analemma for a given location and time using the above formulae, producing a table of altitude and azimuth values over a year that can be directly input into the TIKZ/pgfplots package to produce a graph (eg by compiling the file
analemma-graph.tex
). The script uses the circular orbit/constant speed model to calculate an approximate orbital angle
\psi
for each day of the year. To use the script specify the desired local clock time CT (ex-DST), the latitude
\delta
, and the difference
\Delta L^{\circ}
in degrees between the longitude of the location and the longitude of the defining meridian for the time zone
7
7

7

Note the longitude itself is not sufficient information as a single longitude can span more than one time zone, eg the UK and Spain span time zones UTC and UTC+1.
.

Uncommenting a line in the script shows the effect of a constant solar day length, and other lines can be uncommented to show the separate effects of the eccentricity and obliquity components of the EOT.

Note that an analemma in the tropics will become ill-defined towards solar noon because at such locations at certain times of the year the Sun is directly overhead at solar noon (§
7.1
) and the azimuth is then no longer well-defined. As we approach solar noon at such locations the azimuth equations (
38
) and (
39
) approach
0/0
. Note because of equation (
24
), which implies
\mu
is maximal at
\tau=0
, the Sun directly overhead can only occur at solar noon (and likewise the nadir position only at solar midnight).

38

Appendix D
Rodrigues Rotation Formula

Rodrigues Rotation Formula

In the following EV denotes the set of all Euclidean vectors. Rotations about an axis follow the right hand rule convention.

Theorem.

Given a unit vector
\underline{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathrm{EV}
then the rotation
\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}
of vector
\underline{\mathbf{r}}\in\mathrm{EV}
about the axis
\underline{\mathbf{u}}
is given by :

\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}=(\cos\theta)\,\underline{\mathbf{r}}+(\sin\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\times\underline{\mathbf{r}})+(1-\cos\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathbf{r}})\,\underline{\mathbf{u}}

(45)

Proof.

As shown in Figure
D.1
express
\underline{\mathbf{r}}
as a sum of components parallel and perpendicular to
\underline{\mathbf{u}}
:

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\hskip 2.5pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}+\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp},

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\hskip 20.00003pt\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\hskip 2.5pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathbf{r}})\,\underline{\mathbf{u}}\>,

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{r}}-\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\hskip 2.5pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}=\underline{\mathbf{r}}-(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathbf{r}})\,\underline{\mathbf{u}}\>.

Since we are using the right hand rule convention the image of
\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}
under the rotation is given by :

\displaystyle{\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}_{\hskip-3.0pt\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta)\,\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}+(\sin\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\times\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos\theta)\,\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}+(\sin\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\times\underline{\mathbf{r}})\>,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{since}\ \underline{\mathbf{u}}\times\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\hskip 2.5pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}=\underline{\mathbf{0}}\>,

and clearly
{\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}=\underline{\mathbf{r}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\hskip 2.5pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}
, thus :

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}_{\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\hskip 2.0pt\rule{0.5pt}{6.0pt}}+{\underline{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}}_{\hskip-3.0pt\rule{0.0pt}{9.0pt}\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathbf{r}})\,\underline{\mathbf{u}}+(\cos\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{r}}-(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\underline{\mathbf{r}})\,\underline{\mathbf{u}})+(\sin\theta)\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\times\underline{\mathbf{r}})

which readily rearranges to (
45
). QED

On the dual positive cones and the algebraicity of compact Kähler manifolds
Hsueh-Yung Lin

Abstract




Abstract.
We investigate the algebraicity of compact Kähler manifolds admitting a positive rational Hodge class of bidimension
(1,1)
. We prove that if the dual Kähler cone of a compact Kähler manifold
X
contains a rational class as an interior point, then its Albanese variety is projective. As a consequence, we answer the Oguiso–Peternell problem for Ricci-flat compact Kähler manifolds. We also study related algebraicity problems for threefolds.

Keywords.
Compact Kähler manifolds, algebraic dimension, positive cones, curve classes

1. Introduction

1.1. Dual statements of the Kodaira embedding theorem

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. The celebrated Kodaira embedding theorem asserts that if the Kähler cone
\mathscr{K}(X)
of
X
contains a rational cohomology class, then
X
is projective;
cf.

[
Kod54
]
. Now consider the
dual Kähler cone

\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}=\left\{\ \alpha\in H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})\mathrel{:=}H^{n-1,n-1}(X)\cap H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{R})\ \big|\ \mathopen{\langle}\alpha,\omega\mathclose{\rangle}\geq 0\text{ for every }\omega\in\mathscr{K}(X)\ \right\}

of
X
, where

\mathopen{\langle}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \mathclose{\rangle}\colon H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})\otimes H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}

is the perfect pairing defined by Poincaré duality. As the closure
\overline{\mathscr{K}(X)}
of
\mathscr{K}(X)
in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
is a salient cone, the dual
\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}
has non-empty interior
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
in
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
. The following problem was first asked and studied by Oguiso and Peternell
[
OP00
,
OP04
]
in search of a dual statement of the Kodaira embedding theorem.

Problem 1.1

(Oguiso–Peternell)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
such that
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
contains an element of
H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
. How algebraic is
X
? For instance, what are the possible algebraic dimensions of
X
?

Here is another problem dual to the Kodaira embedding theorem that we can formulate. The closure
\overline{\mathscr{K}(X)}
of the Kähler cone is called the
nef
cone of
X
. Thanks to Demailly and Păun, we know that the Poincaré dual of
\overline{\mathscr{K}(X)}
is the closed convex cone in
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
generated by the classes of closed positive currents of type
(n-1,n-1)
;
cf.

[
BDP+13
, Theorem 2.1]
. The analog of such a cone in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
is the
pseudoeffective cone

\mathrm{Psef}(X)
, defined as the closed convex cone in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
generated by the classes of closed positive currents of type
(1,1)
. From this point of view, if
\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\subset H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
denotes the Poincaré dual of
\mathrm{Psef}(X)
and
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\right)
its interior in
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
, then the following can also be considered as a dual problem to the Kodaira embedding theorem.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
such that
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\right)
contains an element of
H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
. Is
X
always projective? If not, how algebraic is
X
?

We say that a compact Kähler manifold
X
of dimension
n
satisfies the
dual Kodaira condition (K)
if
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
contains an element of
H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
. The
dual Kodaira condition (P)
is defined similarly with
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
replaced by
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\right)
.

We can compare the dual Kodaira conditions (K) and (P), together with the condition in the Kodaira embedding theorem, as follows. On the one hand if
\omega\in\mathscr{K}(X)
, then
\omega^{n-1}\in\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\right)
, so condition (P) is weaker than the condition in the Kodaira embedding theorem. On the other hand since
\mathscr{K}(X)\subset\mathrm{Psef}(X)
, condition (P) is stronger than condition (K). It is still unknown whether these conditions are equivalent.

In
[
OP04
]
, a similar problem had also been studied by Oguiso and Peternell.

Problem 1.3

(Oguiso–Peternell)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
such that
X
contains a smooth curve with ample normal bundle. How algebraic is
X
?

The two Oguiso–Peternell problems (Problems
1.1
and
1.3
) could be related by the conjecture that if
C\subset X
is a smooth curve with ample normal bundle, then
[C]\in\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
;
cf.

[
OP04
, Conjecture 0.3]
. All the problems introduced above aim at understanding the algebraicity of compact Kähler manifolds containing some positive rational Hodge class of
bidimension

(1,1)
.

1.2. Main results

In this article, we study the aforementioned problems. Our first result provides the following partial answer to the Oguiso–Peternell problem (and also to Problem
1.2
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K), then the Albanese torus of
X
is projective.

For a compact Kähler manifold
X
as in Problem
1.1
, Theorem
1.4
gives a lower bound of the algebraic dimension
a(X)
of
X
, namely the dimension of its Albanese image. In particular, if
X
has maximal Albanese dimension (namely, if the Albanese map is generically finite onto its image), then
X
is projective.

Remark 1.5
.

If we replace condition (K) in Theorem
1.4
with the existence of a smooth curve
C\subset X
such that
N_{C/X}
is ample (hence in the context of Problem
1.3
), then the projectivity of
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
simply follows from the surjectivity of
\mathrm{Alb}(C)\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)
;
cf.

[
Ott16
, Lemma 12]
.

The essential part of the proof of Theorem
1.4
consists in answering Problem
1.1
for complex tori; see Proposition
5.1
for the precise statement. As a consequence of Proposition
5.1
(together with Proposition
6.1
about hyper-Kähler manifolds), we also answer Problems
1.1
for Ricci-flat compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with
c_{1}(X)=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K), then
X
is projective.

For a compact Kähler surface
S
, Huybrechts
[
Huy99
,
Huy03b
]
and independently Oguiso–Peternell
[
OP00
]
proved that if
S
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K), then
S
is projective. This completely answers Problem
1.1
and Problem
1.2
in dimension 2. In this article, we answer Problem
1.2
in dimension 3 except for simple non-Kummer threefolds, which presumably do not exist (see Remark
1.9
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth compact Kähler threefold. Assume that
X
is not a simple non-Kummer threefold. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (P), then
X
is projective.

The study of the Oguiso–Peternell problem in the threefold case was initiated by Oguiso and Peternell in
[
OP04
]
. Suppose that
X
is a compact Kähler threefold which is not simple non-Kummer. They proved that if
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
contains an element of
H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
which is a curve class, then
X
has algebraic dimension
a(X)\geq 2
. We improve their result by removing the curve class assumption.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth compact Kähler threefold. Assume that
X
is not a simple non-Kummer threefold. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K), then
a(X)\geq 2
.

Remark 1.9
.

In both Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
, the existence of simple non-Kummer threefolds would be excluded if the abundance conjecture holds for compact Kähler threefolds (see
[
HP18
, Proof of Theorem 6.2]
). This is proven in a very recent paper by Das and Ou
[
DO23
]
.

If we replace the dual Kodaira condition in Theorem
1.8
with the existence of a smooth curve in
X
with ample normal bundle (thus in the context of Problem
1.3
), then the same conclusion
a(X)\geq 2
holds; this was proven by Oguiso and Peternell
[
OP04
, Theorem 0.5]
. Oguiso and Peternell have also outlined a strategy to construct a non-algebraic threefold
X
such that
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)
contains a curve class (or such that
X
contains a smooth curve with ample normal bundle). An explicit construction of such an example is still missing.

While we are still unable to solve Problems
1.1

and
1.3
in dimension 3, we can relate these problems to
a problem about 1-cycles in threefolds. We postpone the discussion to
Section
1.4
, after we give an outline of the proofs of
Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
about
threefolds.

1.3. Outline of the proofs of Theorems
1.7
and
1.8

To prove Theorem
1.8
, we have to show that if
X
is a compact Kähler threefold
X
of algebraic dimension
a(X)\leq 1
which is not simple non-Kummer, then
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)\cap H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})=\emptyset
. Essentially based on Fujiki’s descriptions of algebraic reductions of compact Kähler threefolds
[
Fuj83
]
, those threefolds are bimeromorphic to one of the following (see Proposition
2.13
):

(i)

a threefold which dominates a surface,

(ii)

a fibration in abelian varieties over a curve,

(iii)

a finite quotient of a smooth isotrivial torus fibration over a curve without multi-sections,

(iv)

a finite quotient of a 3-torus
T
.

According to the bimeromorphic invariance of the emptiness of
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)\cap H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
(see Proposition
3.2
), it suffices to prove that
\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee}\right)\cap H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})=\emptyset
for the above four types of varieties. The first case is an immediate consequence of
[
OP04
, Proposition 2.6]
. The proof in cases (
ii
), (
iii
), and (
iii
) will be carried out in Sections
4
,
7
, and
5
, respectively.

Since
\mathscr{K}(X)\subset\mathrm{Psef}(X)
, Theorem
1.8
also implies that a compact Kähler threefold
X
as in Theorem
1.7
satisfies
a(X)\geq 2
. Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.7
for
X
, it suffices to exclude the possibility that
a(X)=2
. Compact Kähler threefolds of algebraic dimension 2 are bimeromorphic to elliptic fibrations over a projective surface, and we will prove Theorem
1.7
for elliptic threefolds in Section
8
.

1.4. A question about 1-cycles and the Oguiso–Peternell problem

We now relate Problem
1.1
to a question about 1-cycles in compact Kähler threefolds, which we first formulate.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and
Y\subset X
a complex subvariety of codimension
l
. Let
\alpha\in H^{k,k}(X,\mathbf{Q})
be a Hodge class which vanishes in
H^{2k}(X\backslash Y,\mathbf{Q})
. Since
H^{2k}(X,\mathbf{Q})
carries a pure Hodge structure,
\alpha
belongs to the image of
\imath_{*}\colon H^{2k-2l}(\tilde{Y},\mathbf{Q})\to H^{2k}(X,\mathbf{Q})
, where
\imath\colon\tilde{Y}\to X
is the composition of a Kähler desingularization
\tilde{Y}\to Y
of
Y
with
Y\hookrightarrow X
(see
e.g.
Lemma
2.7
). If we moreover assume that
X
is projective, then based on the existence of polarization on the underlying
\mathbf{Q}
-Hodge structure of (the summands of the primitive decomposition of)
H^{2k}(X,\mathbf{Q})
, the class
\alpha
is even the image of a Hodge class
\beta\in H^{k-l,k-l}(\tilde{Y},\mathbf{Q})
;
cf.

[
Voi14
, Remark 2.30]
. Without the projectivity assumption on
X
, it is yet unknown whether the latter property still holds, even for 1-cycles in threefolds.

Question 1.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth compact Kähler threefold, and let
Y\subset X
be a surface
(
possibly singular with several irreducible components
)
. Let
\imath\colon\tilde{Y}\to X
be the composition of a desingularization
\tilde{Y}\to Y
of
Y
with the inclusion
Y\hookrightarrow X
. Given a Hodge class
\alpha\in H^{2,2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
which vanishes in
H^{4}(X\backslash Y,\mathbf{Q})
, does there exist a
\beta\in H^{1,1}(\tilde{Y},\mathbf{Q})
such that
\imath_{*}\beta=\alpha
?

So far, Question
1.10
can be answered in the affirmative if
Y
is irreducible (see Lemma
8.3
). Under the assumption that Question
1.10
has a positive answer, we are able to answer Problems
1.1
and
1.3
in dimension
3
(except for simple non-Kummer threefolds; see Remark
1.9
).

Corollary 1.11
.

Assume that Question
1.10
has a positive answer. Let
X
be a smooth compact Kähler threefold which is not simple non-Kummer. If
X
satisfies the conditions in Problems
1.1
or
1.3
, then
X
is projective.

1.5. The existence of connecting families of curves and Problem
1.2

We finish this introduction by discussing how one could expect Problem
1.2
to have a positive answer. Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. The
movable cone

\mathscr{M}(X)\subset H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
of
X
is defined as the closed convex cone generated by classes of the form
\mu_{*}(\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{n-1})
, where
\mu\colon\tilde{X}\to X
is a bimeromorphic morphism from a compact Kähler manifold
\tilde{X}
and the
\omega_{i}
are Kähler classes on
\tilde{X}
. Let

\mathscr{M}(X)_{\mathrm{NS}}\mathrel{:=}\mathscr{M}(X)\cap\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbf{R}},

where
\mathrm{NS}(X)_{\mathbf{R}}
denotes the
\mathbf{R}
-span of
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})
in
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
. As a consequence of
[
BDP+13
, Theorem 2.4]
, if we assume that
X
is projective, then
\mathscr{M}(X)_{\mathrm{NS}}
coincides with the closed convex cone generated by the classes of
connecting families
of curves
(C_{t})_{t\in T}
(which means that for every general pair of points
x,y\in X
, there exist
t_{1},\ldots,t_{l}\in T
such that
x,y\in C_{t_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{t_{l}}
and
C_{t_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{t_{l}}
is connected). One can ask whether this property remains true without the projectivity assumption.

Question 1.12
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. Does the Néron–Severi part
\mathscr{M}(X)_{\mathrm{NS}}
of the movable cone coincide with the closed convex cone generated by the classes of a connecting family of curves?

Conjecturally,
\mathscr{M}(X)
is the dual cone of
\mathrm{Psef}(X)
;
cf.

[
BDP+13
, Conjecture 2.3]
. If we assume this conjecture and that Question
1.12
has a positive answer, then a compact Kähler manifold
X
satisfying
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee})\cap H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})\neq\emptyset
would be algebraically connected (see Section
2.6
for the definition). By Campana’s projectivity criterion (Theorem
2.9
), a compact Kähler manifold
X
as in Problem
1.2
would then be projective. Together with the evidence provided by threefolds (Theorem
1.7
) and Ricci-flat manifolds (Theorem
1.6
), this leads us to conjecture the following.

Conjecture 1.13
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (P), then
X
is projective.

2. Preliminaries

2.1. Convention and terminology

In this article, compact complex manifolds and varieties are assumed to be irreducible (but subvarieties can be reducible). A
fibration
is a surjective proper holomorphic map
f\colon X\to B
with connected fibers. We say that a compact complex variety
X
is in the Fujiki class
\mathscr{C}
if
X
is meromorphically dominated by a compact Kähler manifold.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold. The hypercohomology of a bounded complex of sheaves of abelian groups
\mathscr{F}^{\bullet}
on
X
is denoted by
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathscr{F}^{\bullet})
. For any subring
R\subset\mathbf{C}
, we define
H^{k,k}(X,R)
as the kernel of the composition

H^{2k}(X,R)\longrightarrow H^{2k}(X,\mathbf{C})\simeq\mathbf{H}^{2k}(X,{}_{X}^{\bullet})\longrightarrow\mathbf{H}^{2k}\left(X,{}_{X}^{\bullet\leq k-1}\right),

where
{}_{X}^{\bullet}
is the holomorphic de Rham complex. Note that we have

H^{k,k}(X,\mathbf{Q})\simeq H^{k,k}(X,\mathbf{Z})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{Q}

(which does not hold in general if
\mathbf{Q}
is replaced by other rings,
e.g.

\mathbf{R}
). When
X
is a compact Kähler manifold,
H^{k,k}(X,R)
is the sub-
R
-module of
H^{2k}(X,R)
consisting of elements whose image in
H^{2k}(X,\mathbf{C})
is of type
(k,k)
with respect to the Hodge decomposition. The weight filtration of a mixed Hodge structure
H
is denoted by
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}H
.

We follow
[
Dem12
]
for the definition and convention of positive cones, and the reader is referred to
op. cit.
for related basic properties.

2.2. Kähler forms on normal complex spaces

Let
X
be a normal complex space (for instance, the quotient of a complex manifold by a finite group). A smooth function on
X
is a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbf{R}
such that for some open cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
, there exist holomorphic embeddings
U_{i}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{N}
such that each
f_{|U_{i}}
extends to a smooth function on a neighborhood of
U_{i}
in
\mathbf{C}^{N}
. The sheaf of germs of smooth functions on
X
is denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\infty}
. Similarly, a strictly plurisubharmonic (or psh for short) function on
X
is an upper semi-continuous function with values in
\mathbf{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
which extends to a strictly psh function on a neighborhood of a local embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{N}
.

Let
\mathrm{PH}_{X}=\Re\mathscr{O}_{X}\subset\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\infty}
be the subsheaf of pluriharmonic functions (
i.e.
, the real part of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
). A
Kähler metric
on
X
is a collection of smooth strictly psh functions
\Set{\phi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathbf{R}}_{i\in I}
, where
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an open cover of
X
such that
{\phi_{i}}_{|U_{i}\cap U_{j}}-{\phi_{j}}_{|U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\in\mathrm{PH}_{X}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
. In particular, a Kähler metric on
X
is an element of
H^{0}(X,\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{X}/\mathrm{PH}_{X})
. The short exact sequences

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{PH}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{X}/\mathrm{PH}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

and

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\xrightarrow{\times\sqrt{-1}}\mathscr{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{2\cdot\Re}\mathrm{PH}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

give the composition

[\bullet]\colon H^{0}(X,\mathscr{C}_{X}^{\infty}/\mathrm{PH}_{X})\longrightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathrm{PH}_{X})\longrightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R}),

and a Kähler class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
is the image of a Kähler metric
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\mathscr{C}^{\infty}/\mathrm{PH}_{X})
. The Kähler classes form a convex cone
\mathscr{K}(X)\subset H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
, and elements in the closure
\overline{\mathscr{K}(X)}
are called
nef
classes of
X
.

The following two lemmas are presumably well known; they are direct consequences of Varouchas’ work
[
Var84

Var89
]

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a finite morphism between compact complex manifolds. If
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
is a Kähler class, then
f_{*}[\omega]\in H^{2}(Y,\mathbf{R})
is also a Kähler class.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler form which represents
[\omega]
. For every
y\in Y

Lemma 2.4

3. Dual positive cones under dominant meromorphic maps

3.1. Dual Kähler cones

First we prove that the existence of rational classes in the interior of the dual Kähler cone is invariant under bimeromorphic modifications (see Proposition

for a more general statement). The statement can be reduced to the special case of blow-ups along smooth centers.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X

along a submanifold

Let

be the exceptional divisor, and let

and

(\nu^{*}\alpha+q\ell)(\nu^{*}\gamma-rE)>0

and

Note that if

Let

Let
M<0

f_{*}\omega\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(Y)}
. It follows that
f^{*}\alpha\cdot\omega=\alpha\cdot f_{*}\omega>0
. Hence
f^{*}\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee})
.
∎

3.2. Dual pseudoeffective cones

We start with the easy observation that the interior of the dual pseudoeffective cone is stable under pushforwards by surjective morphisms.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective map between compact Kähler manifolds. If
\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee})
, then
f_{*}\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(Y)^{\vee})
.

Proof.

For every
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(Y)\backslash\{0\}
, since
\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee})
and
f^{*}\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)\backslash\{0\}
, we have
(f_{*}\alpha)\cdot\gamma=\alpha\cdot f^{*}\gamma>0
. Hence
f_{*}\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(Y)^{\vee})
.
∎

The following result is the analog of Lemma
3.1
for pseudoeffective cone.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\nu\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be the blow-up of
X
along a submanifold
Y\subset X
. If
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee})\cap H_{2}(X,\mathbf{Q})\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})^{\vee})\cap H_{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbf{Q})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee})\cap H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
. Let
E=\nu^{-1}(Y)
be the exceptional divisor, and let
\ell
be a line in
\nu^{-1}(y)
for some
y\in Y
. Then every element
\tilde{\gamma}\in H^{1,1}(\tilde{X},\mathbf{R})
is of the form
\tilde{\gamma}=\nu^{*}\gamma+rE
for some
\gamma\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
and
r\in\mathbf{R}
. If moreover
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})
, then
\gamma=\nu_{*}\tilde{\gamma}
is also pseudoeffective. Therefore to prove the proposition, it suffices to find a
q\in\mathbf{Q}_{>0}
such that
(\nu^{*}\alpha-q\ell)\cdot(\nu^{*}\gamma+rE)>0
for every
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)
and
r\in\mathbf{R}
such that
\nu^{*}\gamma+rE\in\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})\backslash\{0\}
.

Fix a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
, and let

\mathrm{Psef}(X)_{1}=\mathrm{Psef}(X)\cap\left\{\gamma\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})\mid\|\gamma\|=1\right\}.

For every
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)_{1}
, let
r_{\gamma}=\inf\left\{r\in\mathbf{R}\mid\nu^{*}\gamma+rE\in\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})\right\}
. Since
\nu^{*}\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})
, we have
r_{\gamma}\leq 0
. As
\alpha\cdot\gamma>0
for every
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)_{1}
and both
\gamma\mapsto\alpha\cdot\gamma
and
\gamma\mapsto r_{\gamma}
are continuous functions defined on the compact set
\mathrm{Psef}(X)_{1}
, there exists a
q\in\mathbf{Q}_{>0}
such that

\alpha\cdot\gamma+qr_{\gamma}>0

for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)_{1}
.

Now let
\gamma\in\mathrm{Psef}(X)
and
r\in\mathbf{R}
be such that
\nu^{*}\gamma+rE\in\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})\backslash\{0\}
. If
\gamma=0
, then
r>0
, so

(\nu^{*}\alpha-q\ell)\cdot(rE)=qr>0.

If
\gamma\neq 0
, then
\frac{r}{\|\gamma\|}\geq r_{\gamma/\|\gamma\|}
, so we also have

(\nu^{*}\alpha-q\ell)\cdot(\nu^{*}\gamma+rE)=\alpha\cdot\gamma+qr=\|\gamma\|\left(\alpha\cdot\frac{\gamma}{\|\gamma\|}+q\frac{r}{\|\gamma\|}\right)\geq\|\gamma\|\left(\alpha\cdot\frac{\gamma}{\|\gamma\|}+qr_{\gamma/\|\gamma\|}\right)>0.\qed

Remark 3.6
.

In the setting of Proposition
3.5
, it is not true that
\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}\left(\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}\right)
implies
\nu^{*}\alpha\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})^{\vee})
(which is already false when
\nu
is the blow-up of
\mathbf{P}^{2}
along a point). However, note that for any generically finite surjective morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between compact Kähler manifolds, since
f_{*}\mathrm{Psef}(X)\subset\mathrm{Psef}(Y)
, we always have
f^{*}\mathrm{Psef}(Y)^{\vee}\subset\mathrm{Psef}(X)^{\vee}
.

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a dominant meromorphic map between compact Kähler manifolds. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (P), then so does
Y
.

Proof.

Let
X\xleftarrow{\nu}\tilde{X}\xrightarrow{\tilde{f}}Y
be a resolution of
f
by a sequence of blow-ups
\nu\colon\tilde{X}\to X
along smooth centers. By Proposition
3.5
, there exists an
\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(\tilde{X})^{\vee})\cap H_{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbf{Q})
. We conclude by Lemma
3.4
that
\tilde{f}_{*}\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Int}(\mathrm{Psef}(Y)^{\vee})\cap H_{2}(Y,\mathbf{Q})
.
∎

4. Smooth torus fibrations

In this section, we study the Oguiso–Peternell problem for smooth torus fibrations. The argument involves the Deligne cohomology in an essential way, and the reader is referred to,
e.g.
,
[
EZZ84
, Sections 2 and 3]
for a reference. See also
[
Cla18
, Section 2]
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth torus fibration such that
X
and
B
are compact Kähler manifolds, and let
g=\dim X-\dim B
. Recall that the (absolute) Deligne complex
\underline{D}_{X}(g)
is defined as

\cdots 0\longrightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{X}\xrightarrow{\times(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{g}}\mathscr{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}{}_{X}^{1}\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}\cdots\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}{}_{X}^{g-1}\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\cdots,

where
\mathbf{Z}_{X}
is placed at the
0^{\mathrm{th}}
degree.
The Deligne cohomology group of degree
2g
is defined by

H^{2g}_{D}(X,\mathbf{Z}(g))=\mathbf{H}^{2g}(X,\underline{D}_{X}(g))
.
We have the short exact sequence of complexes

(4.1)

0\longrightarrow{}_{X}^{\bullet\leq g-1}[-1]\longrightarrow\underline{D}_{X}(g)\longrightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

which gives rise to the regulator map

(4.2)

\mathrm{cl}\colon H^{2g}_{D}(X,\mathbf{Z}(g))=\mathbf{H}^{2g}(X,\underline{D}_{X}(g))\longrightarrow H^{2g}(X,\mathbf{Z})

with image equal to

\ker\left(H^{2g}(X,\mathbf{Z})\longrightarrow\mathbf{H}^{2g}(X,{}_{X}^{\bullet\leq g-1})\right)=H^{g,g}(X,\mathbf{Z}).

Similarly, the relative Deligne complex
\underline{D}_{X/B}(g)
is defined as

\cdots 0\longrightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{X}\xrightarrow{\times(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{g}}\mathscr{O}_{X/B}\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}{}_{X/B}^{1}\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}\cdots\xrightarrow{\;\;d\;\;}{}_{X/B}^{g-1}\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\cdots,

and we have the short exact sequence of complexes

(4.3)

0\longrightarrow{}_{X/B}^{\bullet\leq g-1}[-1]\longrightarrow\underline{D}_{X/B}(g)\longrightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{X}\longrightarrow 0.

Applying
Rf_{*}
to (
4.3
), together with the vanishing
R^{2g}f_{*}{}^{\bullet\leq g-1}_{X/B}=0
(because
f
is smooth of relative dimension
g
), we obtain a short exact sequence

(4.4)

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{J}\longrightarrow R^{2g}f_{*}\underline{D}_{X/B}(g)\longrightarrow R^{2g}f_{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{B}\longrightarrow 0,

where

\mathscr{J}\mathrel{:=}\mathrm{coker}\left(R^{2g-1}f_{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}\longrightarrow R^{2g-1}f_{*}{}^{\bullet\leq g-1}_{X/B}\right).

The natural map
{}_{X}^{\bullet}\to{}_{X/B}^{\bullet}
of de Rham complexes induces a morphism of exact sequences from (
4.1
) to (
4.3
), which further induces the commutative diagram

(4.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2g}_{D}(X,\mathbf{Z}(g))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{g,g}(X,\mathbf{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B,R^{2g}f_{*}\underline{D}_{X/B}(g))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}\left(B,\mathbf{Z}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(B,\mathscr{J})\hbox to0.0pt{,\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cl}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

where the vertical arrow on the left is the composition of
H^{2g}_{D}(X,\mathbf{Z}(g))\to\mathbf{H}^{2g}(X,\underline{D}_{X/B}(g))
induced by the natural map
\underline{D}_{X}(g)\to\underline{D}_{X/B}(g)
with
\mathbf{H}^{2g}(X,\underline{D}_{X/B}(g))\to H^{0}(B,R^{2g}f_{*}\underline{D}_{X/B}(g))
and the second row is an exact sequence induced by (
4.4
).

Finally, the sheaf
\mathscr{J}
is isomorphic to the sheaf of germs of sections of the Jacobian fibration
p\colon J\to B
associated to
f
. Set
\eta(f)\mathrel{:=}\delta(1)\in H^{1}(B,\mathscr{J})
. This defines a bijection

(4.6)

\eta\colon\Set{\text{Isomorphism classes of }J\text{-torsors}}\xrightarrow{\;\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip-3.0pt}\;}H^{1}(B,\mathscr{J}),

and
\eta(f)
is torsion if and only if
f
has a multi-section (which can be chosen étale over
B
);
cf.

[
Cla18
, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth torus fibration of relative dimension
g
over a compact complex manifold. If
f_{*}\colon H^{g,g}(X,\mathbf{Q})\to H^{0}(B,\mathbf{Q})
is surjective, then
f
​ has an étale multi-section.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\eta(f)
is torsion. Since
f_{*}\colon H^{g,g}(X,\mathbf{Q})\to H^{0}(B,\mathbf{Q})
is surjective, there exists an
\alpha\in H^{g,g}(X,\mathbf{Z})
such that
f_{*}\alpha\in H^{0}(B,\mathbf{Z})
is non-zero. By (
4.5
),
f_{*}\alpha
lifts to an element of
H^{0}(B,R^{2g}f_{*}\underline{D}_{X/B}(g))
, so
\delta(f_{*}\alpha)=0
. Hence
\eta(f)=\delta(1)
is torsion.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X_{0}
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
such that
\mathrm{Int}(\mathscr{K}(X_{0})^{\vee})
contains a rational class
\alpha\in H^{2n-2}(X_{0},\mathbf{Q})
. Assume that
X_{0}
is bimeromorphic to
X/G
, where
G
is a finite group and
X
is the total space of a
G
-equivariant smooth torus fibration
f\colon X\to B
over a smooth curve
B
. Then
f
​ has an étale multi-section.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}\leftarrow\tilde{X}\to X/G
be a resolution of a bimeromorphic map
X_{0}\dashrightarrow X/G
by a sequence of blow-ups
\tilde{X}\to X_{0}
along smooth centers. By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathrm{Int}(\mathscr{K}(\tilde{X})^{\vee})
contains a rational class
\beta
. Let
X\xleftarrow{p}\tilde{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\tilde{X}
be a resolution of the meromorphic map
X\to X/G\dashrightarrow\tilde{X}
by a compact Kähler manifold
\tilde{X}^{\prime}
. The situation is summarized in the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B/G\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{f}}

With the notation therein,

r_{*}f_{*}p_{*}q^{*}\beta=\tilde{f}_{*}q_{*}q^{*}\beta=\deg(q)\cdot\tilde{f}_{*}\beta\neq 0\in H^{0}(B/G,\mathbf{Q}),

where the non-vanishing follows from
[
OP04
, Proposition 2.5]
. In particular, if
\alpha\mathrel{:=}p_{*}q^{*}\beta
, then
f_{*}\alpha\neq 0
in
H^{0}(B,\mathbf{Q})
. Therefore,
f_{*}\colon H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})\to H^{0}(B,\mathbf{Q})
is surjective, and we apply Lemma
4.1
to conclude.
∎

5. Algebraicity of the Albanese torus

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.4
, answering Problem
1.1
in terms of the Albanese torus for every compact Kähler manifold.

First we study the Oguiso–Peternell problem for complex tori (and their finite quotients for later use).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T
be a complex torus of dimension
n
and
G
a finite group acting on
T
. If there exists a
\beta\in H^{n-1,n-1}(T,\mathbf{Q})^{G}
such that
\beta\cdot\omega\neq 0
for every
\omega\neq 0\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(T)}^{G}
, then
T
is projective.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Poincaré’s formula)
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on the complex torus
T
of dimension
n
. Assume that

d\mathrel{:=}\frac{c_{1}(L)^{n}}{n!}\neq 0,

and let

c_{L}\mathrel{:=}\frac{c_{1}(L)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!d}\in H^{2n-2}(T,\mathbf{Q}).

Then for every integer
p\in[0,n]
, we have

\frac{c_{1}(L)^{p}}{p!}=d\frac{c_{L}^{\star n-p}}{(n-p)!}\in H^{2p}(T,\mathbf{Q}),

where
\star
is the Pontryagin product on
H^{\bullet}(T,\mathbf{Q})
.

This lemma is well known; we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n}
be a symplectic basis of
H_{1}(T,\mathbf{Z})
for
L
(see
[
BL04
, Section 3.1]
), and let
dx_{1},\ldots,dx_{n},dy_{1},\ldots,dy_{n}
be the associated dual basis of
H^{1}(T,\mathbf{Z})
. Assume that
L
is of type
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. By
[
BL04
, Lemma 3.6.4]
, we have

(5.1)

c_{1}(L)=-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\cdot dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}

and also

(5.2)

d=(-1)^{s}d_{1}\cdots d_{n}

by
[
BL04
, Theorem 3.6.1]
, where
s
is number of negative eigenvalues of the Hermitian form associated to
L
. A computation thus shows that

(5.3)

c_{L}=\frac{c_{1}(L)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!d}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{s+n-1}}{d_{i}}\widehat{dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}},

where

\widehat{dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}}\mathrel{:=}(dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(dx_{i-1}\wedge dy_{i-1})\wedge(dx_{i+1}\wedge dy_{i+1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(dx_{n}\wedge dy_{n}).

Let
\mathrm{PD}\colon H^{\bullet}(T,\mathbf{Z})\to H_{2n-\bullet}(T,\mathbf{Z})
denote the Poincaré duality morphism. For every subset
I
of
[1,n]\cap\mathbf{Z}
, we deduce from
[
BL04
, Lemmas 3.6.5 and 4.10.1]
that

(5.4)

\mathrm{PD}\left(\bigwedgeop\displaylimits_{i\in I}(dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i})\right)=(-1)^{n+s}\underset{{i\in I^{\circ}}}{{{\bigstar}}}(\lambda_{i}\star\mu_{i}),

where
I^{\circ}\mathrel{:=}[1,n]\cap\mathbf{Z}\backslash I
. It follows from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) that

\mathrm{PD}\left(\frac{c_{L}^{\star n-p}}{(n-p)!}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{PD}(c_{L})^{\star n-p}}{(n-p)!}=(-1)^{n-p}\sumop\displaylimits_{I}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in I}d_{i}\right)^{-1}\underset{{i\in I}}{{{\bigstar}}}(\lambda_{i}\star\mu_{i}),

where
I
in the sum runs through all subsets of
[1,n]\cap\mathbf{Z}
of cardinal
n-p
, and thus

\frac{c_{L}^{\star n-p}}{(n-p)!}=(-1)^{s-p}\sumop\displaylimits_{I}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in I}d_{i}\right)^{-1}\bigwedgeop\displaylimits_{i\in I^{\circ}}(dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}),

again by (
5.4
).
Hence

d\cdot\frac{c_{L}^{\star n-p}}{(n-p)!}=(-1)^{p}\sumop\displaylimits_{I}\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in I^{\circ}}d_{i}\right)\bigwedgeop\displaylimits_{i\in I^{\circ}}(dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i})=\frac{c_{1}(L)^{p}}{p!}

by (
5.2
) and (
5.1
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

We can assume that the group action is trivial. Indeed, if
\beta
satisfies the assumption of Proposition
5.1
, then the same
\beta
satisfies

\beta\cdot\omega=\frac{1}{|G|}\sumop\displaylimits_{g\in G}g_{*}\beta\cdot\omega=\beta\cdot\frac{1}{|G|}\sumop\displaylimits_{g\in G}g^{*}\omega\neq 0

for every
\omega\neq 0\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(T)}
. Up to replacing
\beta
with some non-zero multiple of it, we can also assume that
\beta
is the image of some element of
H^{n-1,n-1}(T,\mathbf{Z})
(still denoted by
\beta
).

We first prove Proposition
5.1
assuming
T
is a simple torus (in the sense that
T
does not contain any non-trivial sub-torus). Let
n=\dim T
, and let
\hat{T}
be the dual of
T
. Let

\mathscr{F}\colon H^{2n-k}(T,\mathbf{Z})\xrightarrow{\;\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip-3.0pt}\;}H^{k}(\hat{T},\mathbf{Z})

be the Fourier transformation. Up to sign,
\mathscr{F}
is the composition of the Poincaré duality morphism
H^{2n-k}(T,\mathbf{Z})\xrightarrow{\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip-3.0pt}}H^{k}(T,\mathbf{Z})^{\vee}
with the isomorphism
H^{k}(T,\mathbf{Z})^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\sim$}\vskip-3.0pt}}H^{k}(\hat{T},\mathbf{Z})
induced by the natural perfect pairing
H^{1}(T,\mathbf{Z})\otimes H^{1}(\hat{T},\mathbf{Z})\to\mathbf{Z}
,
cf.

[
Bea83a
, Proposition 1]
, and it follows that

\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{1}\star\gamma_{2})=\pm\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{1})\cdot\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2})=\pm\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{1})\star\mathscr{F}(\gamma_{2}).

The map
\mathscr{F}
is an isomorphism of Hodge structures, so there exists a line bundle
L
on
\hat{T}
such that
c_{1}(L)=\mathscr{F}(\beta)
; let
\phi_{L}\colon\hat{T}\to T
be the homomorphism induced by
L
. Since
T
is simple,
\ker(\phi_{L})
is either
\hat{T}
or finite. In other words, either
c_{1}(L)=0
, or
\phi_{L}
is finite;
cf.

[
BL04
, Lemma 2.4.7]
. As
\beta\neq 0
, we have
c_{1}(L)=\mathscr{F}(\beta)\neq 0
. It follows from
[
BL04
, Corollary 3.6.2 and Theorem 3.6.3]
that
\left(\frac{c_{1}(L)^{n}}{n!}\right)^{2}=\deg\phi_{L}\neq 0
. So
c_{1}(L)^{n}\neq 0
, and
\beta^{\star n}=\pm\mathscr{F}^{-1}(c_{1}(L)^{n})\neq 0
. In particular,
\beta^{\star(n-1)}\neq 0
.

Let
L^{\prime}
be a line bundle over
T
such that
c_{1}(L^{\prime})=\beta^{\star(n-1)}
. By the same argument, we have
c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n}\neq 0
, so the Hermitian form
h
on
H_{1}(T,\mathbf{C})
which corresponds to
c_{1}(L^{\prime})
is non-degenerate. Let
(p,q)
be the signature of
h
. There exist
dz_{1},\ldots,dz_{n}\in H^{1}(T,\mathbf{C})
such that
dz_{1},\ldots,dz_{n},d\bar{z}_{1},\ldots,d\bar{z}_{n}
form a basis of
H^{1}(T,\mathbf{C})
and

c_{1}(L^{\prime})=\sqrt{-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\,dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}\in H^{1}(T,\mathbf{R})

with
c_{1},\ldots,c_{p}>0
and
c_{p+1},\ldots,c_{n}<0
. Define

\omega\mathrel{:=}\sqrt{-1}\left(q\cdot\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\,dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}-p\cdot\sumop\displaylimits_{j=p+1}^{n}c_{j}\,dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}\right),

which is a Kähler form.

Assume that
p,q\neq n
. Then
\omega\cdot c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n-1}=0
by an elementary computation. Since
c_{1}(L^{\prime})=\beta^{\star(n-1)}
and
\beta^{\star n}\neq 0
, we have
\hat{\mathscr{F}}(c_{1}(L^{\prime}))=\pm\hat{\mathscr{F}}(\beta)^{n-1}
and
\hat{\mathscr{F}}(\beta)^{n}\neq 0
, where
\hat{\mathscr{F}}\colon H^{\bullet}(\hat{T},\mathbf{Z})\to H^{\bullet}(T,\mathbf{Z})
denotes the Fourier transform of
\hat{T}
. So by Lemma
5.2
, there exists a
C\in\mathbf{Q}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\hat{\mathscr{F}}(c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n-1})=\pm\hat{\mathscr{F}}(c_{1}(L^{\prime}))^{\star(n-1)}=\pm\left(\hat{\mathscr{F}}(\beta)^{n-1}\right)^{\star(n-1)}=C\hat{\mathscr{F}}(\beta),

and therefore

c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n-1}=C\beta.

It follows that

\omega\cdot c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n-1}=C\left(\omega\cdot\beta\right)\neq 0,

where the non-vanishing follows from the assumption on
\beta
and
\omega\in\mathscr{K}(T)
. This contradicts the equality
\omega\cdot c_{1}(L^{\prime})^{n-1}=0
. Hence either
p=n
, or
q=n
, so either
L^{\prime}
or
L^{\prime\vee}
is ample. Thus
T
is projective.

Now we prove Proposition
5.1
by induction on
\dim T
. When
\dim T=1
,
T
is always projective. Assume that
\dim T>1
and that Proposition
5.1
is proven for every complex torus of dimension strictly less than
\dim T
endowed with a finite group action. By what we have proven, we can assume that
T
is not simple. Then
T
is the total space of a smooth isotrivial torus fibration
\pi\colon T\to T^{\prime}
over a simple complex torus
T^{\prime}
with
0<\dim T^{\prime}<\dim T
. For every
\omega^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(T^{\prime})}\backslash\{0\}
, we have
\pi^{*}\omega^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(T)}\backslash\{0\}
, so

\pi_{*}\beta\cdot\omega^{\prime}=\beta\cdot\pi^{*}\omega^{\prime}\neq 0

by assumption. Thus
T^{\prime}
is projective by the induction hypothesis. Moreover,
\pi_{*}\beta\neq 0\in H_{2}(T^{\prime},\mathbf{Z})
, and since
T^{\prime}
is simple, the previous argument showing that
\beta^{\star n}\neq 0
(when
T
is simple) also proves that

\pi_{*}(\beta^{\star\dim T^{\prime}})=(\pi_{*}\beta)^{\star\dim T^{\prime}}\neq 0\in H^{0}(T^{\prime},\mathbf{Z}),

so

has an étale multi-section

by Lemma

, together with a surjective homomorphism

over a fiber

of

. For every

, we have

Thus

is projective as well by the induction hypothesis. Since

is a fibration with a multi-section such that

and the fibers of

∎

Before we prove Theorem

, let us prove some immediate consequences of Proposition

Corollary 5.3
.

Let

be a compact Kähler manifold which is bimeromorphic to the quotient

of a complex torus

by a finite group

Let

Corollary 5.4
.

Let

be compact Kähler manifold which satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K). If

.

It is equivalent to show that

We finish this section with a proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

Claim

For every

, and let

∎

Now let

6. Ricci-flat manifolds

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
, answering Problem
1.1
for Ricci-flat compact Kähler manifolds. Let us start with the special case of hyper-Kähler manifolds.

6.1. Hyper-Kähler manifolds

In this article, a compact hyper-Kähler manifold is a simply connected compact Kähler manifold
X
such that
H^{0}(X,{}_{X}^{2})
is generated by a nowhere degenerate holomorphic
2
-form. The reader is referred to
[
Huy03a
]
for basic results about compact hyper-Kähler manifolds.

Let
X
be a compact hyper-Kähler manifold. Let
q_{X}
be the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki quadratic form on
H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
, and let

\Phi_{X}\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})\simeq H^{2n-2}(X,\mathbf{R})

be the isomorphism (of Hodge structures) sending
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})
to the Poincaré dual of
q_{X}(\alpha,\cdot)\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{R})^{\vee}
. For every cone
C\subset H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
, its dual with respect to
q_{X}
is denoted by

C^{*}\mathrel{:=}\left\{\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})\mid q_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\geq 0\text{ for every }\beta\in C\right\}.

We have

\Phi_{X}(C^{*})=C^{\vee},

where
C^{\vee}\subset H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{R})
is the Poincaré dual of
C
.

For a compact hyper-Kähler manifold
X
, there are two other natural positive cones that we can define in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
: the birational Kähler cone
\mathscr{B}\mathscr{K}(X)
and the positive cone
\mathscr{C}(X)
. Recall that

\mathscr{B}\mathscr{K}(X)\mathrel{:=}\bigcupop\displaylimits_{f\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}}f^{*}\mathscr{K}(X^{\prime})\subset H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R}),

where the union runs through all bimeromorphic maps
f
from
X
to another compact hyper-Kähler manifold and
\mathscr{C}(X)
is defined to be the connected component of

\left\{\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})\mid q_{X}(\alpha,\alpha)>0\right\}

containing
\mathscr{K}(X)
.

We are able to answer Problem
1.1
for hyper-Kähler manifolds based on Huybrechts’ description of their nef cones.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a compact hyper-Kähler manifold. If
X
satisfies the dual Kodaira condition (K), then
X
is projective.

Proof.

Let
n\mathrel{:=}\dim X
. We show that if
X
is not projective, then
\mathrm{Int}(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee})\cap H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})=\emptyset
. Let
V\subset H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
be the subspace generated by
\Phi_{X}^{-1}(H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q}))
. Since
X
is assumed to be non-projective, we have
q_{X}(\alpha,\alpha)\leq 0
for every
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{Q})
;
cf.

[
Huy03a
, Proposition 26.13]
. In particular,
{q_{X}}_{|V}
is negative semi-definite. As the signature of
q_{X}
on
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})
is
(1,h^{1,1}(X))
, there exists an
\omega\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbf{R})\backslash\{0\}
such that
q_{X}(\omega,\omega)\geq 0
and
q_{X}(\omega,\alpha)=0
for every
\alpha\in V
. It follows that
\omega\cdot\beta=0
for every
\beta\in H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})
.

Since
q_{X}(\omega,\omega)\geq 0
, up to replacing
\omega
with
-\omega
, we can assume that
\omega\in\overline{\mathscr{C}(X)}
. For every rational curve
C\subset X
, since
[C]\in H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})
, we have
\omega\cdot C=0
. It follows from
[
Huy03a
, Proposition 28.2]
that
\omega\in\overline{\mathscr{K}(X)}
. Since
\omega\cdot\beta=0
for every
\beta\in H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})
, we conclude that
\mathrm{Int}(\mathscr{K}(X)^{\vee})\cap H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbf{Q})=\emptyset
.
∎

6.2. Ricci-flat manifolds

We finish this section with a proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
n\mathrel{:=}\dim X
. By
[
Bea83b
, Théorème 2]
, there exists a finite étale cover

such that

, where

is a complex torus,

, and each

is a hyper-Kähler manifold. By Lemma

, we have

Lemma 8.4

Let

-Hodge structures. Assume that

Finally, we prove Corollary
1.11
for Problem
1.3
. Before we start the proof, let us first recall and prove some statements about subvarieties with ample normal bundles. The first one is a theorem due to Fulton and Lazarsfeld, asserting that a subvariety with ample normal bundle intersects non-negatively with other subvarieties.

Theorem 10.3

(Fulton–Lazarsfeld
[
Laz04b
, Corollary 8.4.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold, and let
Y\subset X
be a local complete intersection subvariety of dimension
k
. Assume that
N_{Y/X}
is ample. Then for every subvariety
Z\subset X
of codimension
k
, we have
Y\cdot Z\geq 0
. Moreover, if
Y\cap Z\neq\emptyset
, then
Y\cdot Z>0
.

While the pullback of an effective cycle does not necessarily remain effective, in some situations the ampleness of the normal bundle of a subvariety
Y\subset X
ensures that the pullback of the cycle class of
Y
is still effective.

Lemma 10.4
.

Let
\mu\colon Y\to X
be a bimeromorphic morphism between smooth compact Kähler threefolds, and let
C\subset X
be a smooth irreducible curve. If
N_{C/X}
is ample, then
\mu^{*}[C]\in H^{4}(Y,\mathbf{Q})
is an effective curve class. More precisely, there exists an irreducible curve
\tilde{C}
on
Y
such that
\mu(\tilde{C})=C
and
m\mu^{*}[C]=[\tilde{C}]+[C^{\prime}]
for some
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
and some effective curve class
[C^{\prime}]\in H^{4}(Y,\mathbf{Q})
.

We need to first prove a technical lemma before proving Lemma
10.4
. Let
\mathscr{X}
be a compact complex variety. A curve
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{X}
is called
displaceable
if for every point
x\in\mathscr{X}
and every irreducible component
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{C}
, we can find a curve
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathscr{X}
such that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
is deformation equivalent to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
and
x\notin\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
. For any subvariety
\mathscr{Y}\subset\mathscr{X}
, we say that
\mathscr{C}
is
displaceable in
\mathscr{Y}
if
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{Y}
and in the previous definition,
\mathscr{C}^{\prime\prime}
is deformation equivalent to
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{Y}
.

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
\nu\colon\tilde{\mathscr{X}}\to\mathscr{X}
be the blow-up of a compact Kähler threefold
\mathscr{X}
along an irreducible smooth center
Z\subset\mathscr{X}
. Let
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{X}
be a displaceable curve. Then
\nu^{*}[\mathscr{C}]\in H^{4}(\tilde{\mathscr{X}},\mathbf{Q})
can be represented by a displaceable curve
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
.

If moreover
\mathscr{C}
has an irreducible component
\mathscr{C}_{0}
which is displaceable in some surface
S\subset\mathscr{X}
, then
\nu^{*}[\mathscr{C}]\in H^{4}(\tilde{\mathscr{X}},\mathbf{Q})
can be represented by a displaceable curve
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
which contains an irreducible component
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
such that
\nu(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{0})
is deformation equivalent to
\mathscr{C}_{0}
in
S
and
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
is displaceable in the strict transform
\tilde{S}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
of
S
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{0},\ldots,\mathscr{C}_{k}
be the irreducible components of
\mathscr{C}
. The blow-up center
Z
is either a point or a curve. If
Z
is a point (resp. a curve), then since
\mathscr{C}
is displaceable, we can choose a general deformation
\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{X}
of
\mathscr{C}_{i}
such that
\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}\cap Z=\emptyset
(resp.
\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}\cap Z=\emptyset
or
\dim\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}\cap Z=0
). For the irreducible component
\mathscr{C}_{0}
, we choose a general deformation
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}_{0}
of
\mathscr{C}_{0}
which moreover remains in
S
. By the blow-up formula
[
Ful98
, Theorem 6.7]
, we have

\nu^{*}[\mathscr{C}]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\nu^{*}[\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}]=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}[\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{i}^{\prime}]+m[F]\in H^{4}(\tilde{\mathscr{X}},\mathbf{Q})

for some
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
, where
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{i}^{\prime}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{X}}
is the strict transform of
\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}
and
F
is a fiber of
\nu^{-1}(Z)\to Z
.

Clearly,
F
is displaceable. Since each
\mathscr{C}_{i}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathscr{C}_{0}^{\prime}
) is a general deformation of the displaceable curve
\mathscr{C}_{i}
(resp.
\mathscr{C}_{0}
), the strict transform
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{i}^{\prime}
is displaceable (resp. displaceable in
\tilde{S}
). Finally, since
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{0}^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
{\mathscr{C}}^{\prime}_{0}
under
\nu
, the image
\nu(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{0}^{\prime})={\mathscr{C}}^{\prime}_{0}
is deformation equivalent to
\mathscr{C}_{0}
in
S
by assumption.
∎

The Complement of the Djoković-Winkler Relation
Marc Hellmuth, Bruno J. Schmidt, Guillaume E. Scholz, Sandhya Thekkumpadan Puthiyaveedu

Abstract
The Djoković-Winkler relation Θ \Theta is a binary relation defined on the edge set of a given graph that is based on the distances of certain vertices and which plays a prominent role in graph theory. In this paper, we explore the relatively uncharted “reflexive complement” Θ ¯ \overline{\Theta} of Θ \Theta , where ( e , f ) ∈ Θ ¯ (e,f)\in\overline{\Theta} if and only if e = f e=f or ( e , f ) ∉ Θ (e,f)\notin\Theta for edges e e and f f . We establish the relationship between Θ ¯ \overline{\Theta} and the set Δ e ​ f \Delta_{ef} , comprising the distances between the vertices of e e and f f and shed some light on the intricacies of its transitive closure Θ ¯ ∗ \overline{\Theta}^{*} . Notably, we demonstrate that Θ ¯ ∗ \overline{\Theta}^{*} exhibits multiple equivalence classes only within a restricted subclass of complete multipartite graphs. In addition, we characterize non-trivial relations R R that coincide with Θ ¯ \overline{\Theta} as those where the graph representation is disconnected, with each connected component being the (join of) Cartesian product of complete graphs. The latter results imply, somewhat surprisingly, that knowledge about the distances between vertices is not required to determine Θ ¯ ∗ \overline{\Theta}^{*} . Moreover, Θ ¯ ∗ \overline{\Theta}^{*} has either exactly one or three equivalence classes.



Keywords:
distances, diameter, equivalence relation, complete multipartite graph, block graph, Cartesian product.

,

,

We study here the reflexive complement

being or not being in relation

characterize certain graph classes, including block graphs, trees or graphs of diameter less than
three or greater than two. In particular, there is a close connection of the relation

\overline{\Theta}
and the size of the set

understanding the conditions under which an arbitrary relation

coincides with
\overline{\Theta}
for
some graph. Under the assumption that
\overline{\Theta}^{*}
has more than one equivalence class,
R=\overline{\Theta}

for some graph if and only if the graph representation of

has three connected components, and
each connected component is the Cartesian product of complete graphs or the join of the Cartesian

distance function

in

\overline{\Theta}^{*}

and that
\overline{\Theta}^{*}
has either exactly one or three equivalence classes.

Theorem 3.14

For a given relation

there is a connected graph

and
for which
\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}
is not
1
-trivial if and only if

\mathscr{G}_{R}
has exactly three connected components

precisely one of the following conditions is satisfied:

1.

There are integers

such that

,
H_{2}\simeq K_{p}\Box K_{r}
, and
H_{3}\simeq K_{q}\Box K_{r}

Proof.

Suppose that

\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}

,

and

and
y\neq y^{\prime}
or
x\neq x^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}

4
Summary and Outlook

In this paper, we explored structural properties of the irreflexive complement
\overline{\Theta}_{G}

of the Djoković-Winkler Relation
\Theta_{G}
. In particular, we characterized those

\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}
having exactly one, respectively, more than one equivalence class. Moreover,
we showed that determining
\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}
does not require explicit knowledge about
distances in the underlying graph
G
. In addition, we provided novel characterizations of block
graphs, trees, graphs with diameter less than three, and more than two.

In view of future research, it might be of interest to establish results similar to
Theorem
3.14
assuming that
R=\overline{\Theta}_{G}
and
\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}
is
1
-trivial.
Moreover, can one characterize those
\Theta
-relations for which
\Theta_{G}^{*}
has
k>1
equivalence
classes or for which connected components are Cartesian products similar to
Theorem
3.14
? In addition, one may ask which graphs
G
satisfy
\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}=\Theta_{G}^{*}
. By Lemma
3.3
, this can only happen if both
\overline{\Theta}_{G}^{*}
and
\Theta_{G}^{*}

are
1
-trivial.

The parabolic quaternionic Calabi-Yau equation on hyperkähler manifolds Thanks: This work was supported by GNSAGA of INdAM
Lucio Bedulli, Giovanni Gentili and Luigi Vezzoni

Abstract
. We show that the parabolic quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation on a compact hyperkähler manifold has always a long-time solution which once normalized converges smoothly to a solution of the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation. This is the same setting in which Dinew and Sroka [ 13 ] prove the conjecture of Alesker and Verbitsky [ 3 ] . We also introduce an analogue of the Chern-Ricci flow in hyperhermitian manifolds.

1.
Introduction

A hypercomplex manifold is a real
4n
-dimensional smooth manifold equipped with three complex structures
I,J,K
satisfying the quaternionic-type relations
IJ=-JI=K
. A hyperhermitian metric
g
is determined either by a form
\Omega
of type
(2,0)
with respect to
I
or by a positive real form
\omega
of type
(1,1)
with respect to
I
such that
J\omega=-\omega
. A hyperhermitian metric is called HKT (hyperkähler with torsion) if
\partial\Omega=0
(the operators
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
will be always taken with respect to the complex structure
I
throughout the paper). HKT metrics were first introduced in
[
25
]
with motivations coming from theoretical physics and further studied from the purely geometric point of view (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
34
,
35
,
43
,
45
,
47
]
and the references therein).

In hyperhermitian geometry the role of plurisubharmonic functions is usually replaced by smooth real functions
\varphi
satisfying
\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi>0
(
quaternionic plurisubharmonic
functions), where the positivity is in the sense of (2,0)-forms (see the preliminaries) and
\partial_{J}=J^{-1}\bar{\partial}J
is the
twisted
\bar{\partial}
operator
. Equivalently a function
\varphi
is quaternionic plurisubharmonic if
\omega+\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-J\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)
is positive as a
(1,1)
-form.

In
[
3
]
Alesker and Verbitsky introduced an analogue of the complex Monge-Ampère equation on hyperhermitian manifolds by considering for a given smooth function
f
the
quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation

(1)

(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi)^{n}={\rm e}^{f+b}\,\Omega^{n}\,,\qquad\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi>0\,,\qquad\sup_{M}\varphi=0\,,

for a real-valued function
\varphi
and a constant
b
. The equation can be reformulated in terms of real
2
-forms as

(2)

(\omega+\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-J\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi))^{2n}={\rm e}^{2(f+b)}\,\omega^{2n}\,,\qquad\omega+\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-J\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)>0\,,\qquad\sup_{M}\varphi=0\,.

In
[
3
]
it is conjectured that the equation is always solvable on compact HKT manifolds at least under the extra assumption that the canonical bundle of
(M,I)
is holomorphically trivial.

Following the strategy of Yau for proving the Calabi conjecture
[
46
]
, the natural approach for studying the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation is via the continuity method and the hard part in this direction is, as usual, the proof of a priori estimates. Fortunately some important results have been established in this direction. The
C^{0}
estimate is now proved in the most general case. A first proof of the
C^{0}
estimate was given in
[
3
]
under the assumptions on
g
to be HKT and on the canonical bundle of
(M,I)
to be holomorphically trivial. Furthermore the
C^{0}
estimate was improved in
[
2
]
by removing the condition on the canonical bundle and recently also the HKT assumption was removed by Sroka in
[
35
]
applying a recent method of Guo, Phong and Tong
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. The higher order estimates have been established so far only under extra assumptions. In
[
1
]
Alesker confirmed the conjecture on compact flat hyperkähler manifolds. More recently Dinew and Sroka drastically improved Alesker’s result by proving the conjecture also for non-flat hyperkähler manifolds
[
13
]
. Other partial confirmations to the conjecture can be found in
[
17
,
18
]
.

In the present paper we first observe that in the most general case an upper bound of the Laplacian of the solution to (
1
) combined with the
C^{0}
estimate implies all the other a priori estimates (Theorem
3.1
). In particular this allows us to simplify part of the proof of the Dinew-Sroka Theorem in
[
13
]
(see Remark
3.2
). We further prove an analogous result for the quaternionic parabolic Monge-Ampère equation

(3)

\dot{\varphi}=2\log\frac{(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi)^{n}}{\Omega^{n}}-2f\,,\qquad\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi>0\,,\qquad\varphi(\cdot,0)=0\,,

introduced in
[
6
,
47
]
(see Theorem
3.3
). This leads to the following theorem which we prove in section
4

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,I,J,K,g)
be a compact hyperhermitian manifold. Assume that
(I,J,K)
admits a compatible hyperkähler metric
\hat{g}
. Then for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
equation (
3
) has a unique long-time solution
\varphi(t)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
. The normalization

\tilde{\varphi}:=\varphi-\frac{1}{\int_{M}\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}}\int_{M}\varphi\,\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}

converges to a solution of (
1
) with a suitable choice of
b
.

Theorem
1.1
is the natural generalization of the main theorem in
[
6
,
47
]
and the parabolic version of the main result in
[
13
]
.

Given a solution
\varphi(t)
to (
3
), the associated
(1,1)
-form
\omega(t):=\omega+\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)-J\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))
satisfies the evolution equation

(4)

\dot{\omega}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm Ric}(\omega(t))-J{\rm Ric}(\omega(t))-\beta+J\beta\right)

where
\beta={\rm Ric}(\omega)-2i\partial\bar{\partial}f
and
{\rm Ric}
is the Chern-Ricci form with respect to
I
. Long-time existence and convergence of (
3
) would imply the fact that for every representative
\beta
of
c_{1}^{\rm BC}(M,I)
, there is a unique hyperhermitian metric of the form
\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+\tfrac{i}{2}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-J\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)
with
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{\varphi})=\beta
. From this point of view Theorem
1.1
is in the spirit of Cao’s theorem
[
8
]
.

Equation (
4
) suggests to consider the geometric flow

(5)

\dot{\omega}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm Ric}(\omega(t))-J{\rm Ric}(\omega(t))\right)\,,\quad\omega(0)=\omega\,,

since it preserves the compatibility with the hypercomplex structure and plays the role of the Ricci-flow in Kähler geometry and of the Chern-Ricci flow in Hermitian geometry
[
41
]
. We think that a study of flow (
5
) in the same spirit of
[
41
]
could give new insights in hyperhermitian geometry. In section
5
we collect some preliminary observations on the flow.

2.
Preliminaries

A
hypercomplex manifold

(M,I,J,K)
is an even-dimensional complex manifold
(M,I)
equipped with two additional complex structures
(J,K)
satisfying the quaternionic-type relations
IJ=-JI=K
. A Hermitian metric
g
on
(M,I,J,K)
is
hyperhermitian
if it is compatible with each
I,J,K
.
Any hyperhermitian metric
g
induces a corresponding form

\Omega:=\frac{1}{2}\left(g(J\cdot,\cdot)+ig(K\cdot,\cdot)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega_{J}+i\omega_{K}\right)\in\Lambda^{2,0}_{I}

which satisfies the following properties

•

\Omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\bar{\Omega}
(
\Omega
is q-real);

•

\Omega(Z,J\bar{Z})>0
for every non-zero
Z\in T^{1,0}M
(
\Omega
is positive).

Conversely, any q-real and positive
\Omega\in\Lambda_{I}^{2,0}
induces a hyperhermitian metric
g
via the relation

(6)

g=2\mathrm{Re}(\Omega(\cdot,J\cdot))\,.

Hence we have a one-to-one correspondence between
q
-real positive
(2,0)
-forms and hyperhermitian metrics. If
\Omega
is
\partial
-closed we say that
g
is
hyperkähler with torsion
(HKT). We further denote by
\omega
the fundamental form of
(g,I)
. We have the following relation

(7)

\frac{\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}}{(n!)^{2}}=\frac{\omega^{2n}}{(2n)!}\,,

(see e.g.
[
36
, Section 4.3]
).

Let
\partial
be the
\partial
-operator with respect to
I
and
\partial_{J}:=J^{-1}\bar{\partial}J\colon\Lambda^{r,0}_{I}\to\Lambda^{r+1,0}_{I}
. Then

\partial\partial_{J}=-\partial_{J}\partial\,,

see
[
13
]
.
Every
\Omega_{\varphi}:=\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi>0
induces a hyperhermitian metric
g_{\varphi}
. We further denote by
\omega_{\varphi}
the
(1,1)
-form of
(g_{\varphi},I)
.

The following useful lemma follows from
[
35
, Remark 4.1]
and
[
13
, Proposition 2.15]
but we prove for the reader’s convenience

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

\partial\partial_{J}\varphi(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(X,JY)+\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(JX,Y)\right)^{2,0}\,.

Moreover,

\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+\frac{i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-iJ\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi}{2}

and

\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g=2n-\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi\,,

where
\Delta_{\varphi}
is the Chern-Laplacian operator with respect to
g_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement can be easily proved locally by using
I
-holomorphic coordinates such that
\partial_{i}J_{s}^{\bar{r}}=\partial_{s}J_{i}^{\bar{r}}

[
13
, Remark 2.13]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\partial\partial_{J}\varphi

\displaystyle=-\partial J\bar{\partial}\varphi=-\partial J\varphi_{\bar{r}}d\bar{z}^{r}=-\partial(\varphi_{\bar{r}}J^{\bar{r}}_{s}dz^{s})

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{i\bar{r}}J^{\bar{r}}_{s}dz^{i}\wedge dz^{s}-\varphi_{\bar{r}}\partial_{i}J^{\bar{r}}_{s}dz^{i}\wedge dz^{s}=-\varphi_{i\bar{r}}J^{\bar{r}}_{s}dz^{i}\wedge dz^{s}\,;

hence

(8)

\partial\partial_{J}\varphi(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(X,JY)+\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(Y,JX)\right)^{2,0}

as required. Moreover, from (
6
) and (
8
) we have

\displaystyle g_{\varphi}(X,Y)=2\mathrm{Re}(\Omega_{\varphi}(X,JY))=g(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(KX,JY)-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(X,IY)\right)

and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{\varphi}(X,Y)=g_{\varphi}(IX,Y)=\omega(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(JX,JY)-i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(X,Y)\right)\,.

Finally, since
g_{\varphi}
is
J
-Hermitian we have

\mathrm{tr}_{g_{\varphi}}g=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\omega=2n-\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{\varphi}}\frac{i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi-iJ\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi}{2}=2n-\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi

as claimed.
∎

The following Lemma essentially follows from
[
45
]

Lemma 2.2
.

If
g
is HKT, then

d\Omega^{n}=\vartheta\wedge\Omega^{n}

where
\vartheta=-Id^{*}\omega
is the Lee form of
(\omega,I)
. In particular
{\rm Ric}(\omega)=dd^{*}\omega
and in the compact case

d\Omega^{n}=0\iff\omega\mbox{ is balanced }\iff{\rm Ric}(\omega)=0\,.

Proof.

It is quite easy to observe that

*\Omega=\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,n!}\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n-1}

and

*\alpha=-\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,n!}J\alpha\wedge\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n-1}

for every
1
-form
\alpha
of type
(1,0)
with respect to
I
. The HKT condition implies
\vartheta=-Jd^{*}\omega_{J}=-Kd^{*}\omega_{K}

[
26
]
, hence

\begin{split}J\vartheta&=d^{*}\omega_{J}=d^{*}(\Omega+\bar{\Omega})=-*d*(\Omega+\bar{\Omega})=-\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,n!}*d\left(\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n-1}+\Omega^{n-1}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,n!}*\left(d\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n-1}+\Omega^{n-1}\wedge d\bar{\Omega}^{n}\right)\,.\end{split}

Clearly there exists a
(0,1)
-form
\beta
such that
d\Omega^{n}=\beta\wedge\Omega^{n}
, but from these computations it follows that

J\vartheta=-\frac{1}{(n-1)!\,n!}*\left(\beta\wedge\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n-1}+\bar{\beta}\wedge\Omega^{n-1}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}\right)=J(\beta+\bar{\beta})

and thus
\beta\wedge\Omega^{n}=\vartheta\wedge\Omega^{n}
as claimed. Moreover since
\omega
is HKT it is Bismut-Ricci flat and formula (2.7) in
[
4
]
together implies
{\rm Ric}(\omega)=dd^{*}\omega
. The last statement is trivial.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
5
]
it is proved that on a compact nilmanifold
(N/\Gamma,I,J,K)
a left-invariant HKT metric is always balanced. This fact can be also deduced from Lemma
2.2
taking into account that the Chern-Ricci form of a left-invariant Hermitian metric on a complex nilmanifold is always zero (see e.g.
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
).

3.
From a bound of the Laplacian to
C^{2,\alpha}
-estimates

In
[
39
]
it is proved a general theorem for deducing
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates of a solution of an elliptic equation from a bound on the Laplacian of the solution. The theorem is applied in
[
39
]
to a large class of
equations in Hermitian geometry. In this section we observe that it can be also applied to the quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(M,I,J,K,g)
ba a compact hyperhermitian manifold and
\varphi
a solution to (
1
) such that

(9)

\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}}\leq C\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\mathrm{tr}_{g}g_{\varphi}\leq C\,,

for some constant
C>0
. Then for
0<\alpha<1
there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
depending only on
(M,I,J,K,g)
,
f
,
\alpha
and
C
such that

\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha}\,.

Proof.

Choose
I
-holomorphic local coordinates
(z^{1},\dots,z^{2n})
in a chart, which for simplicity we identify with the unit ball
B_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. Consider also the underlying real coordinates
(x^{1},\dots,x^{4n})
given by
z^{k}=x^{k}+ix^{2n+k}
for
k=1,\dots,2n
and the usual real representation of complex matrices defined as

\iota(H):=\begin{pmatrix}\,\,\,\,\mathrm{Re}(H)&\mathrm{Im}(H)\\
-\mathrm{Im}(H)&\mathrm{Re}(H)\end{pmatrix}\,.

Let
\mathrm{Herm}(2n)
and
\mathrm{Sym}(4n)
be the spaces of
2n\times 2n
Hermitian matrices and
4n\times 4n
real symmetric matrices respectively. Notice that
\iota
sends
\mathrm{Herm}(2n)
to
\mathrm{Sym}(4n)
. We define the following functions

•

\mathcal{F}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(4n)\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\mathcal{F}(N):=\frac{1}{2}\log\det(N)
;

•

\mathcal{S}\colon B_{1}\to\mathrm{Sym}(4n)
given by
\mathcal{S}(x):=\iota(g(x))
;

•

\mathcal{T}\colon\mathrm{Sym}(4n)\times B_{1}\to\mathrm{Sym}(4n)
given by
\mathcal{T}(N,x):=\frac{1}{4}(p(N)+\iota(\,^{t}\!J(x))p(N)\iota(J(x)))
, where
p
is the projection
p(N):=\frac{1}{2}(N+\,^{t}\!INI)
.

Here, we are writing
g(x)
and
J(x)
for the complex matrices of
g
and
J
at the point
x
in the coordinates
(z^{1},\dots,z^{2n})
. For simplicity we set
\tilde{J}=\iota(J)
. Since
p(D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}u(x))=2\iota(D^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}u(x))
(here
D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
D^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
are the real and complex Hessian, respectively) for any function
u\colon B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\det(\iota(H))=\det(H)^{2}
for any Hermitian matrix
H
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}(x)+\mathcal{T}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\det\left(\iota(g_{i\bar{j}})+\frac{1}{2}\iota(\varphi_{i\bar{j}})+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{J}_{i}^{\bar{s}}\iota(D^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi)_{r\bar{s}}\tilde{J}_{\bar{j}}^{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\det\iota\left(g_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{s}}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi)_{r\bar{s}}J_{\bar{j}}^{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\log\det\left(g_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}+\frac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{s}}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi)_{r\bar{s}}J_{\bar{j}}^{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=2f(x)+2b+\log\det g(x)\,.

The arithmetic-geometric means inequality gives
\mathrm{tr}_{g}{g_{\varphi}}\geq 2n(\frac{\det g_{\varphi}}{\det g})^{1/2n}=2n\mathrm{e}^{(f+b)/n}\geq C^{\prime}
, because
|b|
is bounded by
\sup|f|
using a standard maximum principle argument directly on equation (
1
). Since also
\mathrm{tr}_{g}g_{\varphi}\leq C
by assumption (
9
), we then have

C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{i\bar{j}})\leq g_{i\bar{j}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{s}}(x)\varphi_{r\bar{s}}(x)J_{\bar{j}}^{r}(x)\leq C_{0}(\delta_{i\bar{j}})

for
x\in B_{1}
and a constant
C_{0}>0
. Since
\iota
preserves (semi)positivity, i.e.
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
if and only if
\iota(H_{1})\leq\iota(H_{2})
, we deduce

C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{ij})\leq\mathcal{S}(x)+\mathcal{T}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x))\leq C_{0}(\delta_{ij})\,.

Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the compact convex subset

\mathcal{E}:=\{N\in\mathrm{Sym}(4n)\mid C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{ij})\leq N\leq C_{0}(\delta_{ij})\}\,.

We check that all the assumptions
\mathbf{H1}-\mathbf{H3}
of
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
are satisfied.

•

It is well-known that
\mathcal{F}
is uniformly elliptic and concave on
\mathcal{E}
(conditions
\mathbf{H1}.(1)
and
\mathbf{H1}.(2)
of
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
). Moreover
\mathbf{H1}.(3)
is trivial for
\mathcal{F}
since it does not depend on
x
.

•

Next we verify conditions
\mathbf{H2}
in
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
. Condition
\mathbf{H2}.(1)
is easily checked and
\mathbf{H2}.(2)
is straightforward. We just need to show that also
\mathbf{H2}.(3)
holds. For any positive semidefinite
N\in\mathrm{Sym}(4n)
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4n}
we have

\begin{split}\quad\qquad\langle\mathcal{T}(N,x)v,v\rangle&=\frac{1}{8}\left(\langle Nv,v\rangle+\langle\,^{t}\!INIv,v\rangle+\langle\,^{t}\!\tilde{J}N\tilde{J}v,v\rangle+\langle\,^{t}\!\tilde{J}\,^{t}\!INI\tilde{J}v,v\rangle\right)\!(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{8}\left(\langle Nv,v\rangle+\langle NIv,Iv\rangle+\langle N\tilde{J}v,\tilde{J}v\rangle+\langle NI\tilde{J}v,I\tilde{J}v\rangle\right)\!(x)\geq 0\,.\end{split}

We may assume without loss of generality that
J(0)
is orthogonal and we get

\frac{1}{8}\|N\|\leq\|\mathcal{T}(N,0)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|N\|\,,

where
\|A\|=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\langle Av,v\rangle
. Possibly shrinking the ball, we may assume that
J(x)
is close to
J(0)
and
\mathbf{H2}.(3)
is satisfied.

•

Condition
\mathbf{H3}
obviously holds.

Since the assumptions
\mathbf{H1}-\mathbf{H3}
of
[
39
, Theorem 1.2]
are verified the theorem follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem
3.1
was already proved by Alesker in the case of compact locally flat HKT manifolds
[
1
]
. Our version allows to simplify the proof of the main theorem of
[
13
]
. Indeed the proof of the Alesker-Verbitsky conjecture on hyperkähler manifolds is obtained in
[
13
]
proving independently the
C^{1}
estimate and a bound for the Laplacian and then combining them in order to obtain the second order estimate. Hence the proof of the Dinew and Sroka theorem can be alternatively obtained bypassing the gradient estimate and using our Theorem
3.1
. We also note that Theorem
3.1
does not need
g
to be HKT.

Next we focus on the “parabolic counterpart”of Theorem
3.1
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
,
\varphi(t)
,
t\in[0,T)
, a solution to (
3
) such that

(10)

\osc_{M}\varphi\leq C\,,\quad\|\dot{\varphi}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C\,,\quad\mathrm{tr}_{g}g_{\varphi}\leq C\,,

for some positive constant
C
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
.
Then
\varphi(t)
satisfies the following a priori estimate

\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha}

in
[\varepsilon,T)
, where
C_{\alpha}>0
depends only on
(M,I,J,K,g)
,
f
,
\alpha
,
C
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Here we apply the general result of Chu
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
, in the same fashion as
[
12
, Lemma 6.1]
. In the same notations of the previous theorem, with
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
chosen in the same way, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}(x,t)-\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}(x)+\mathcal{T}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t)))=-2f(x)-\log\det g(x)\,.

From (
10
), the arithmetic-geometric means inequality and Lemma
4.2
we get
C\geq\mathrm{tr}_{g}{g_{\varphi}}\geq 2n(\frac{\det g_{\varphi}}{\det g})^{1/2n}=2n\mathrm{e}^{(\dot{\varphi}+2f)/2n}\geq C^{-1}
. We then have

C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{i\bar{j}})\leq g_{i\bar{j}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{i\bar{j}}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}J_{i}^{\bar{s}}(x)\varphi_{r\bar{s}}(x,t)J_{\bar{j}}^{r}(x)\leq C_{0}(\delta_{i\bar{j}})

for
(x,t)\in B_{1}\times(0,1]
and a uniform constant
C_{0}>0
. We then infer

C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{ij})\leq\mathcal{S}(x)+\mathcal{T}(D^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x,t))\leq C_{0}(\delta_{ij})\,.

Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the compact convex subset

\mathcal{E}:=\{N\in\mathrm{Sym}(4n)\mid C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{ij})\leq N\leq C_{0}(\delta_{ij})\}\,.

All the assumptions
\mathbf{H1}-\mathbf{H3}
of
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
are easily checked as in the previous theorem. Nonetheless, at this point, we cannot directly apply
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
since
\varphi
does not necessarily satisfy a

and is focused on studying the heat-type equation

(16)

In

a Harnack inequality is obtained and applied to

\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}
decays exponentially. This allows to deduce the convergence of

to a smooth function. We emphasise that for us the dependence of

\tilde{\varphi}

\dot{\tilde{\varphi}}(t)=2\log\frac{(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\tilde{\varphi}(t))^{n}}{\Omega^{n}}-2f-\frac{2}{\int_{M}\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}}\int_{M}\left(\log\frac{(\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\tilde{\varphi}(t))^{n}}{\Omega^{n}}-f\right)\Omega^{n}\wedge\bar{\Omega}^{n}

5.
The adapted Chern-Ricci flow

In this section we consider flow (
5
) on hypercomplex manifolds.

Given a compact hyperhermitian manifold
(M,I,J,K,g)
and a covariant
2
-tensor
S
, we denote by

S^{-}:=\frac{1}{2}(S-JS)

its
J
-anti-invariant part in order to rewrite (
5
) as

\dot{\omega}(t)=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega(t))\,,\quad\omega(0)=\omega\,.

Analogously to the Kähler and the Hermitian case the flow is equivalent to a scalar one

(17)

\dot{\varphi}(t)=\log\frac{(\omega-t{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)+(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))^{-})^{2n}}{\omega^{2n}}\,,\quad\varphi(0)=0\,.

Indeed, if
\varphi
solves (
17
), then
\omega(t):=\omega-t{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)+(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))^{-}
solves (
5
) since

\dot{\omega}(t)=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)+(i\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}(t))^{-}=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)+\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{\omega(t)^{2n}}{\omega^{2n}}\right)^{-}=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega(t))\,.

Conversely if
\omega(t)
solves (
5
), then we define

\varphi(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\log\frac{\omega(s)^{2n}}{\omega^{2n}}\,ds

and we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\omega(t)-\omega+t{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)-(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))^{-})

\displaystyle=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega(t))+{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)-(i\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}(t))^{-}

\displaystyle=-{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega(t))+{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)-\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{\omega(t)^{2n}}{\omega^{2n}}\right)^{-}=0

which implies
\omega(t)=\omega-t{\rm Ric}^{-}(\omega)+(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))^{-}
for all
t
.

According to the Kähler
[
38
]
and the Hermitian case
[
41
]
, it is quite natural to introduce the following conjecture

Conjecture 5.1
.

There exists a unique maximal solution to the flow (
5
) on

[0,T)
, where

T=\sup\,\{t\geq 0\,:\,\mbox{ there exists }\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)\mbox{ s.t. }\omega-t{\rm Ric}(\omega)^{-}+i(\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{-}>0\}\,.

Note that, if
c_{1}^{\rm BC}(M,I)=0
, then flow (
5
) is equivalent to the parabolic quaternionic Monge-Ampère equation (
3
). In particular Theorem
1.1
implies that if
(M,I,J,K)
has an underlying hyperkähler metric, then (
5
) has a long-time solution and the conjecture in this special case is verified. In order to prove the conjecture in the general case we need a priori estimates on the solution
\varphi
to (
17
). We can observe that estimates can be obtained working as in the complex case
[
41
, Lemma 4.1]
except for the estimate for the second order derivatives which we leave open.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,I,J,K,g)
be a compact hyperhermitian manifold and
\omega(t)
a solution of (
5

such that

C^{-1}\omega\leq\omega(t)\leq C\omega\,,

then Conjecture
5.1
holds.

Flow (
17
) fits in the following quite general class of parabolic problems:

let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold and

F_{t}\colon C^{2}_{+}(M)\to C^{0}(M)\,,\quad t\in[0,T)\,,

C^{2}_{+}(M)
of
C^{2}(M)
. Assume that
-F_{t}\colon C^{2}_{+}(M)\to C^{0}(M)
is strongly elliptic for every
t\in[0,T)
. Assume further that

F_{t}(\psi+C)=F_{t}(\psi)

for every
\psi\in C^{2}_{+}(M)
,
t\in[0,T)
and constant
C
. Then we consider the parabolic flow

(18)

\dot{\varphi}(t)=F_{t}(\varphi(t))\,,\quad\varphi(0)=0\,.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that there exists a continuous map
\Lambda\colon M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}

\lambda\colon M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any

and
\psi\in C^{2}(M\times[0,T^{\prime}])

F_{t_{0}}(\psi({t_{0}}))(x_{0})\geq\lambda(x_{0},t_{0})\mbox{ if $(x_{0},t_{0})$ is a minumum point of }\psi\,,

) satisfy a uniform lower bound.

Let
\varphi\in C^{2}(M\times[0,T^{\prime}))

and consider

, where

is a positive constant. Let
(x_{0},t_{0})
be a maximum point of
\psi
in

6.
Remarks and Further Developments

From the geometric point of view the conjecture of Alesker and Verbitsky consists in prescribing the
J
-anti-invariant part of the Chern-Ricci tensor of an HKT metric in a fixed quaternionic Bott-Chern cohomology class. The existence of Chern-Ricci flat HKT metrics on a hypercomplex manifold implies that the canonical bundle is holomorphically trivial. When the canonical bundle is not holomorphically trivial, it is quite natural to study the existence of hyperhermitian metrics
\omega
satisfying the Einsten-type condition

(19)

{\rm Ric}-J{\rm Ric}=\lambda\omega\,,\mbox{ for some constant }\lambda\,,

or, more generally, of hyperhermitian metrics with constant Chern-scalar curvature (this research project has been suggested to the second named author by Misha Verbitsky). Equation (
19
)
can be rewritten in terms of
\Omega
as

(20)

\partial_{J}\vartheta^{1,0}=\lambda\Omega\,,

where
\vartheta
is the Lee form.
In the compact case (
20
) forces
\lambda
to be non-negative and on Joyce homogeneous examples
[
28
]
(which are the simplest examples of compact HKT manifolds where the canonical bundle is not holomorphically trivial) the canonical metric satisfies (
20
)
[
16
]
. Since
\lambda
is non-negative, in analogy with the Kähler case, we expect that it is possible to find obstructions to the existence of HKT metrics satisfying (
20
).

In order to study the existence of HKT metrics having constant Chern scalar curvature it is quite natural to consider the following analogue of the Calabi-flow on HKT manifolds

(21)

\dot{\varphi}(t)=s_{\varphi}(t)\,,\quad\varphi(0)=0\,,

where
s_{\varphi}(t)
is the Chern-scalar curvature of
\Omega_{\varphi}:=\Omega+\partial\partial_{J}\varphi(t)
and
\Omega
is a fixed HKT metric.
When the canonical bundle is holomorphically trivial (
21
) is the gradient flow of the following Mabuchi-type functional

\mathcal{M}(\varphi)=\int_{M}\log\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\Omega^{n}}\,\Omega_{\varphi}^{n}\wedge\bar{\Theta}-\int_{M}h\,\Omega_{\varphi}^{n}\wedge\bar{\Theta}\,,

where
h
is a
\partial\partial_{J}
-potential of
\partial_{J}\vartheta^{1,0}
and
\Theta
is a holomorphic volume form.

Cavity magnonics with domain walls in insulating ferromagnetic wires
Mircea Trif, Yaroslav Tserkovnyak

Abstract
Magnetic domain walls (DWs) are topological defects that exhibit robust low-energy modes that can be harnessed for classical and neuromorphic computing. However, the quantum nature of these modes has been elusive thus far. Using the language of cavity optomechanics, we show how to exploit a geometric Berry-phase interaction between the localized DWs and the extended magnons in short ferromagnetic insulating wires to efficiently cool the DW to its quantum ground state or to prepare nonclassical states exhibiting a negative Wigner function that can be extracted from the power spectrum of the emitted magnons. Moreover, we demonstrate that magnons can mediate long-range entangling interactions between qubits stored in distant DWs, which could facilitate the implementation of a universal set of quantum gates. Our proposal relies only on the intrinsic degrees of freedom of the ferromagnet, and can be naturally extended to explore the quantum dynamics of DWs in ferrimagnets and antiferromagnets, as well as quantum vortices or skyrmions confined in insulating magnetic nanodisks.



Introduction.
—Magnetic domain walls (DWs) are stable topological magnetic configurations that are robust against local deformations

;

Miniaturization naturally raises the question of the importance of quantum effects in future DW-based devices. Indeed, several works have proposed recently to harness their quantum properties
in order to encode and process quantum information at the nanoscale

;

In this work, we answer these questions affirmatively by proposing a ground-state cooling method that exploits the intrinsic magnons (or spin waves) in insulating ferromagnetic devices hosting DWs.

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}a_{p}(t)+i\frac{P_{p}(Z_{0})P_{\Phi}}{M_{\Phi}}a_{p}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}a_{p,\rm in}(t)\,,

where
a_{p,\rm in}(t)
represents the input field in the probe mode,
\kappa_{p,ex}
the decay rate of mode
p
into the leads, and all operators are in the Heisenberg picture. Eq. (
Cavity magnonics with domain walls in insulating ferromagnetic wires

g_{p}
. Assuming
\Gamma_{jk}^{p}\ll\Gamma^{\rm tot}_{jk}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p}(\omega)\approx\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{\pi\kappa_{p}}\sum_{j,k}\frac{\Gamma^{p}_{jk}\Gamma^{\rm eff}_{jk}}{(\omega-\Omega_{p}-\epsilon_{jk})^{2}+(\Gamma^{\rm eff}_{jk})^{2}}p_{j}\,,

\Delta_{n}=-\epsilon_{q}

\mathcal{S}_{p}(\Omega_{p}-\epsilon_{q})/\mathcal{S}_{p}(\Omega_{p}+\epsilon_{q})\approx p_{1}/p_{2}

Magnons can mediate interactions between two such DWs, facilitating the implementation of two-qubit gates. Indeed, assuming that both DWs are cooled down to their respective ground states, and sufficient nonlinearity in the spectrum, their dynamics are restricted to the subspace spanned by the lowest two states,
\{|1_{a}\rangle,|2_{a}\rangle\}

\displaystyle J_{1-2}=\sum_{a=1,2}\frac{g_{n,1}g_{n,2}|\langle 1_{1}|P_{1,\Phi}|2_{1}\rangle||\langle 1_{2}|P_{2,\Phi}|2_{2}\rangle|}{\Delta_{n}-\epsilon_{q,a}}\,,

(14)

where we assumed identical DWs, and
\sigma_{a}^{+}=|2_{a}\rangle\langle 1_{a}|
(
\sigma_{a}^{-}=|1_{a}\rangle\langle 2_{a}|

a=1,2

\propto\sigma_{1}^{+}\sigma_{2}^{+},\sigma_{1}^{-}\sigma_{2}^{-}

Rips and Hartmann 2013

H_{1-2}
for
t_{g}=\pi/4J_{1-2}

\sqrt{i{\rm SWAP}}

Barenco et al. 1995

ac

Conclusions
.—In summary, we have shown that magnons in short insulating ferromagnetic wires can be leveraged to initialize, entangle and detect quantum states encoded in macroscopic DWs. These features are enabled by universal (and intrinsic) Berry-phase mediated interactions between the magnons and the DW, rendering our approach promising in terms of scalability, since it does not require extrinsic degrees of freedom (e.g. photons) to be integrated with the ferromagnetic wire. By the same token, it could be naturally extended to 2D topological objects, such as skyrmions, or even to other magnetic materials, such as ferrimagnets or antiferromagnets, which can exhibit faster time scales for manipulations.

Isolated DW dynamics

As described in the main text, the isolated dynamics of a DW positioned far from the boundaries, i.e.
L\gg Z
, is governed by the Lagrangian
Tatara et al. 2008
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{dw}\approx-2S_{dw}Z\dot{\Phi}-\widetilde{K}_{h}(\sin^{2}\Phi+\tilde{b}\sin\Phi)-V_{\rm pin}(Z)\,,

(S1)

where
S_{dw}=s\lambda
,
\widetilde{K}_{h}=K_{h}\lambda
, and
\tilde{b}=\pi b/2K_{h}
. Furthermore, the pinning potential
V_{\rm pin}(Z)
(around
Z=Z_{0}
) may be due to inhomogeneous magnetic anisotropies in the sample (e.g., reduced local
K_{e}
that can be controlled electrically). The dissipation function, on the other hand, becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{dw}=\frac{S_{dw}\alpha}{2}\left(\dot{Z}^{2}+\dot{\Phi}^{2}\right)\,.

(S2)

The equations of motion engendered by the Lagrangian stated in Eq. (
S1
) are

\displaystyle P_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{dw}}{\partial\dot{\Phi}}=-2S_{dw}Z\,,\,\,\,\,P_{Z}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{dw}}{\partial\dot{Z}}=0\,,

(S3)

which indicates that
\{Z,\Phi\}
are Hamiltonian canonically conjugate variables. With dissipation, the generalized Euler-Lagrange equations of motion read:

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{h}[\sin{(2\Phi)}+\tilde{b}\cos{\Phi}]

\displaystyle-2S_{dw}\dot{Z}=-\alpha S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}\,,

\displaystyle 2S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}+\frac{\partial V_{\rm pin}(Z)}{\partial Z}

\displaystyle=-\alpha S_{dw}\dot{Z}\,.

(S4)

By replacing
\dot{Z}
in the second line and taking the time-derivative of the equation that follows, we can obtain (while only considering the Rayleigh dissipation of
\Phi
in the case of strong pinning):

\displaystyle 2S_{dw}\ddot{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\rm pin}(Z)}{\partial Z^{2}}\left(\widetilde{K}_{h}(\sin{(2\Phi)}+\tilde{b}\cos{\Phi})+\alpha S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}\right)/2S_{dw}=0\,,

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\ddot{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{K}_{h}\left(\sin(2\Phi)+\tilde{b}\cos\Phi\right)+\alpha S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}=0\,,

(S5)

where

M_{\Phi}=(2S_{dw})^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\rm pin}(Z)}{\partial Z^{2}}\right)^{-1}

(S6)

is the inertia of the DW associated with rigid rotations. Assuming

\displaystyle V_{\rm pin}(Z)\approx\frac{\delta K_{e}(Z-Z_{0})^{2}}{2}\,,

(S7)

results in the mass
M_{\Phi}=(2S_{dw})^{2}/\delta K_{e}
. The effective Lagrangian describing the dynamics of the angle
\Phi
only (in the absence of dissipation) becomes

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}^{2}-\underbrace{\widetilde{K}_{h}(\sin^{2}\Phi+\tilde{b}\sin\Phi)}_{\displaystyle U(\Phi)}\,,

(S8)

which in turn pertains to the following effective Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2M_{\Phi}}P_{\Phi}^{2}+U(\Phi)\,,

(S9)

which is depicted in Eq. (1) in the main text.

We can now promote the momentum
P_{\Phi}
to its operator version,
P_{\Phi}\rightarrow-i\partial_{\Phi}
, which eventually allows determining the Hamiltonian that describes the quantum dynamics of the isolated DW.

To make progress on that end, it is beneficial to identify the classical potential minima of
U(\Phi)
, which implicate solving

\displaystyle\cos\Phi_{eq}\left(\sin\Phi_{eq}-\tilde{b}\right)=0\,,

(S10)

along with the local maximum condition

2\cos 2\Phi_{eq}+\tilde{b}\sin\Phi_{eq}\geq 0\,.

(S11)

We see that
\Phi_{eq}=\pi/2
becomes an inflection point at
\tilde{b}_{c}=1
(rendering the effective potential quartic in
\Phi
), while the two minima before reaching this point are located at
\{\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi_{eq}^{2}\}=\{-\pi+\arcsin{\tilde{b}},-\arcsin{\tilde{b}}\}
.

There are three regimes for the operation of the DW: (
i
)
0<\tilde{b}<1
, (
ii
)
\tilde{b}=1
(the case studied in detail in this work), and (
iii
)
\tilde{b}>1
.

I.0.1
Double-well potential limit:
0\leq\tilde{b}<1

The first case corresponds to a double-well potential and has been discussed in many previous works (see, for example,
Zou et al. 2023
). To determine the spitting of the lowest levels, we calculate the amplitude of the transition between the two minima,
G(\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi_{eq}^{2})
, using the Euclidian path integral approach by switching to the imaginary time representation
t\rightarrow i\tau
. Thus, we write:

\displaystyle G(\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi_{eq}^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\Phi_{eq}^{1}|e^{-\tau H_{\Phi}}|\Phi_{eq}^{2}\rangle=\int_{\Phi(0)=\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi(\tau)=\Phi_{eq}^{2}}{\mathcal{D}}\Phi\,e^{-\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\mathcal{L}}_{\Phi}(\Phi,\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\Phi)}\,,

(S12)

where

{\mathcal{L}}_{\Phi}(\Phi,\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}^{2}+U(\Phi)

(S13)

represent the Lagrangian for the DW particle moving in the inverted potential
-U(\Phi)
.
The corresponding classical equations of motions hence read as follows

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\ddot{\Phi}_{cl}-\frac{\partial U(\Phi_{cl})}{\partial\Phi_{cl}}=0\,,

(S14)

so that the classical trajectories
\Phi_{cl}
will satisfy:

\frac{1}{2}M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}_{cl}^{2}-U(\Phi_{cl})={\rm const}\,.

(S15)

We are looking for solutions that do not allow the particle to “escape” the maxima of the inverted potential, that is,
\dot{\Phi}|_{\Phi_{eq}^{1,2}}=0
, so we get const
=U(\Phi_{eq}^{1,2})\equiv U_{c}
. We can define a new (shifted) potential
U^{\prime}(\Phi_{cl})=U(\Phi_{cl})-U_{c}
such that
U^{\prime}(\Phi_{eq}^{1,2})=0
(note that adding a constant term does not influence the dynamics). Thus, the classical trajectory Lagrangian is given by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\Phi}(\Phi_{cl},\partial_{\tau}\Phi_{cl})=M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}_{cl}^{2}\equiv\sqrt{2M_{\Phi}U^{\prime}(\Phi_{cl})}\dot{\Phi}_{cl}
. Since the lowest barrier between the two minima is
\Phi=-\pi/2
, we can retain in the action only the trajectories that involve this barrier. The classical action is then obtained:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{cl}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}{\mathcal{L}}_{\Phi}(\Phi_{cl},\partial_{\tau}\Phi_{cl})=\int_{\Phi_{eq}^{1}}^{\Phi_{eq}^{2}}d\Phi_{cl}\sqrt{2M_{\Phi}U^{\prime}(\Phi_{cl})}=2\sqrt{2M_{\Phi}}\left[\sqrt{1-\tilde{b}^{2}}-\tilde{b}\arccos{\tilde{b}}\right]\,.

(S16)

Following the discussion in
Altland and Simons 2010
, we find the following.

\displaystyle G(\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi_{eq}^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle\approx Ce^{-\omega\tau}\sum_{n\in{\rm odd}}\frac{(\tau K)^{n}}{n!}e^{-n\mathcal{S}_{cl}}=Ce^{-\omega\tau}\sinh{\left[\tau Ke^{-\mathcal{S}_{cl}}\right]}\,,

(S17)

where
\omega
is the oscillation frequency associated with one of the wells only

\omega=\sqrt{\frac{2\widetilde{K}_{h}(1-\tilde{b}^{2})}{M_{\Phi}}}=\frac{1}{S_{dw}}\sqrt{\frac{\delta K_{e}\widetilde{K}_{h}(1-\tilde{b}^{2})}{2}}\,,

(S18)

while
C
is a constant that can be extracted from treating an isolated well analytically
Altland and Simons 2010
. Moreover,

K=\gamma\omega\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{S}_{cl}}{2\pi}}\,,

(S19)

where
\gamma\approx 1
. Physically, the two initially degenerate minima at
\Phi_{eq}^{1,2}
become mixed due to macroscopic quantum tunneling, and the states split into symmetric
|S\rangle
and antisymmetric
|A\rangle
configurations of the wave functions. Projecting the transition probability onto this subspace, we can write:

\displaystyle G(\Phi_{eq}^{1},\Phi_{eq}^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle\approx\langle\Phi_{eq}^{1}|\left(|S\rangle e^{-\epsilon_{S}\tau}\langle S|+|A\rangle e^{-\epsilon_{A}\tau}\langle A|\right)|\Phi_{eq}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{2}e^{-\bar{\epsilon}\tau/}\left(e^{-\Delta\epsilon\tau/2}-e^{\Delta\epsilon\tau/2}\right)=Ce^{-\bar{\epsilon}\tau}\sinh{(\tau\Delta\epsilon)}\,,

(S20)

with
\bar{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{A}+\epsilon_{S})/2
,
\Delta\epsilon=\epsilon_{A}-\epsilon_{S}
, and
C=2|\langle\Phi_{eq}^{1,2}|S\rangle|^{2}
. Comparing the above expression with the quasi-classical calculation, we obtain the following.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{S,A}=\frac{\omega}{2}\pm Ke^{-\mathcal{S}_{cl}}=\frac{\omega}{2}\pm\gamma\omega\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{S}_{cl}}{2\pi}}e^{-\mathcal{S}_{cl}}\,,

(S21)

and thus the splitting between the two states (qubit splitting) is:

\Delta\epsilon=2\gamma\omega\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{S}_{cl}}{2\pi}}e^{-\mathcal{S}_{cl}}\,.

(S22)

Above, we assumed
\langle\Phi_{eq}^{1}|\Phi_{eq}^{2}\rangle\approx 0
, i.e. the two states localized at the potential minima have negligible overlap (such a condition only applies for large barriers).

I.1
Dissipation via Gilbert damping

Here, we discuss briefly how to account for the dissipation introduced by the Gilbert damping in the quantized Hamiltonian. We start by writing the classical equation of motion pertaining to the DW Lagrangian in the presence of the damping (
\mathcal{R}_{\Phi}=\alpha S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}^{2}/2
):

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\ddot{\Phi}+\widetilde{K}_{h}[\sin(2\Phi)+\tilde{b}\cos\Phi]+\alpha S_{dw}\dot{\Phi}=\xi(t)\,,

(S31)

where
\xi(t)
is the Langevin noise that is subject to the fluctuation-dissipation condition
Takei et al. 2017

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{-i\omega t}\langle\xi(t)\xi(0)\rangle=\alpha\omega S_{dw}\coth(\omega/2k_{B}T)\,.

(S32)

In the quantum Hamiltonian description, this in turn can be included as:

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}\rightarrow H_{\Phi}-\Phi\hat{\xi}+H_{\xi}\,,

(S33)

where
\hat{\xi}=\sum_{p}g_{p}(\xi_{p}+\xi_{p}^{\dagger})
describe the bosonic environment with
\xi_{p}
(
\xi_{p}^{\dagger}
) being the annihilation (creation) operators satisfying
[\xi_{p},\xi_{p^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{pp^{\prime}}
, while
H_{\xi}
dictates the environment dynamics. Following the approach described in one of the following sections, we can write the evolution of the DW density matrix as follows:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\Phi}=-i[H_{\Phi},\rho_{\Phi}]+\sum_{j,k}\gamma_{jk}\mathcal{D}(A_{jk})\rho_{\Phi}\,,

(S34)

where
A_{jk}=|k\rangle\langle j|
,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O})\rho_{\Phi}=\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\rho_{\Phi}\mathcal{O}-\{\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O},\rho_{\Phi}\}/2
is the dissipator, while

\displaystyle\gamma_{jk}=2\alpha S_{dw}|\langle j|\Phi|k\rangle|^{2}(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})[1+n_{B}(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})]=\gamma_{kj}e^{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})/k_{B}T}\,,

(S35)

are the resulting rates that satisfy the detailed balance condition in thermal equilibrium. Above,

\displaystyle n_{B}(\omega)=\frac{1}{e^{\omega/k_{B}T}-1}\,,

(S36)

is the Bose-Einstein distribution.

II
Description of the magnons

To address the magnons’ dynamics and their coupling to the DW, we first analyze the case when the latter is static. This will allow us to express the dynamical solutions in terms of the instantaneous basis for a given position
Z
and orientation
\Phi
of the DW.

II.1
Magnons around a static DW at
K_{h}=b=0

The fluctuations around the DW magnetization texture
{\bm{n}}_{0}(z)=(\sin\theta_{0}\cos\Phi,\sin\theta_{0}\sin\Phi,\cos\theta_{0})\equiv{\bm{n}}_{0}(z-Z)
can be described as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\delta n_{1}^{2}+\delta n_{2}^{2}}}({\bm{n}}_{0}+{\bm{e}}_{1}\delta n_{1}+{\bm{e}}_{2}\delta n_{2})\approx\left(1-\frac{\delta n_{1}^{2}+\delta n_{2}^{2}}{2}\right){\bm{n}}_{0}(z)+{\bm{e}}_{1}\delta n_{1}+{\bm{e}}_{2}\delta n_{2}\,,

(S37)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{e}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial{\bm{n}}_{0}}{\partial\theta_{0}}=(\cos\theta_{0}\cos\Phi,\cos\theta_{0}\sin\Phi,-\sin\theta_{0});\,\,{\bm{e}}_{2}=\frac{\partial{\bm{n}}_{0}}{\sin\theta_{0}\partial\Phi}=(-\sin\Phi,\cos\Phi,0)

(S38)

are the unit vectors that are perpendicular to the DW magnetization at position
z
(and mutually perpendicular), while
\delta n_{1,2}\equiv\delta n_{1,2}(z,t)
are their amplitudes with respect to the DW position
Z
. Note that in the regime
\delta n_{1,2}(z,t)\ll 1
, we have retained only terms up to the second order in the fluctuations.

The resulting instantaneous magnon Lagrangian becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{sw}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int dz[2S_{dw}\delta n_{1}\partial_{t}\delta n_{2}+\delta n_{1}\mathcal{H}_{0}\delta n_{1}+\delta n_{2}\mathcal{H}_{0}\delta n_{2}]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{K}_{e}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+1-2{\rm sech}^{2}(z-Z)\right)\,,

(S39)

which means magnons moving in a Pösch and Teller potential well
U(z)=-2{\rm sech}^{2}(z-Z)
, having the property that in an infinite system, the magnons pass through it without any reflection. In the following, we first determine the magnon solutions and spectrum for an infinite wire and then utilize these solutions to find the eigenmodes in the finite wire. We can formally write:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{K}_{e}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+1-2{\rm sech}^{2}(z-Z)\right)=\widetilde{K}_{e}a^{\dagger}a\,,

(S40)

with

a\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\tanh{(z-Z)},\,\,\,\,a^{\dagger}\equiv-\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\tanh{(z-Z)}\,.

(S41)

The Euler-Lagrange equations in the absence of damping lead to the following equations of motion for the fields
\delta n_{1,2}
:

\displaystyle is\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\dot{n}_{1}\\
\delta\dot{n}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&i\mathcal{H}_{0}\\
-i\mathcal{H}_{0}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta n_{1}\\
\delta n_{2}\end{array}\right)\,,

which can be solved efficiently by using the techniques of super-symmetric quantum mechanics
Kim et al. 2018
. We briefly present the rationale behind this approach. Let us first define another Hamiltonian density:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{K}_{e}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+1\right)=\widetilde{K}_{e}aa^{\dagger}\,,

(S48)

which is translation invariant and allow for plane wave solutions
\delta n_{0}(z,t)\propto e^{i(kz-i\omega t)}
. The solutions to the initial problem are then simply found from
\psi(z)=a^{\dagger}\psi_{0}(z)
, while the two Hamiltonians share the same spectrum
\omega_{k}
. Let us thus solve the simpler problem:

\displaystyle\omega\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta n_{1}^{0}(\omega,k)\\
\delta n_{2}^{0}(\omega,k)\end{array}\right)=\frac{K_{e}(k^{2}+1)}{s}\sigma_{y}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta n_{1}^{0}(\omega,k)\\
\delta n_{2}^{0}(\omega,k)\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\sigma_{y}
is a Pauli matrix acting in the space of the two orthogonal fluctuations. By applying the operator
\sigma_{y}
again on the right, we obtain the magnons’ spectrum and the two types of eigenvectors:

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{K_{e}}{s}(k^{2}+1)\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\pm}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\pm i\end{array}\right)\,,

with
\pm
corresponding to
\pm\omega_{k}
. While in a classical theory we can choose either sign of the frequency, the quantum description requires more caution. Let us calculate the energy of the mode:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\delta n_{1,s}^{0}H_{0}\delta n_{1,s}^{0}+\delta n_{2,s}^{0}H_{0}\delta n_{2,s}^{0})\propto\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{s}^{\dagger}H_{0}\Psi_{s}=\omega_{k}\,,

(S55)

and therefore each of the negative and positive eigenvalue solution correspond to an energy
|\omega_{k}|
. Therefore, the fields
\delta n_{1,2}^{0}(z,t)
can be presented in a compact way as:

\displaystyle\delta{\bm{n}}^{0}(z,t)=[\delta n_{1}^{0}(z,t),\delta n_{2}^{0}(z,t)]^{T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k,s=\pm}a_{k,\pm}(t)\Psi_{\pm}\psi_{k}^{0}(z)e^{\pm i\omega_{k}t}\,,

(S56)

and, imposing the condition that the fields are real, we find:

\displaystyle a_{k,\pm}=(a_{-k,\mp})^{*}\equiv a_{k}\,.

(S57)

We can conclude that
\delta n_{1}^{0}(z,t)
and
\delta n_{2}^{0}(z,t)
are related to the Re and Im parts of the same function
\delta n^{0}(z,t)=\delta n_{1}^{0}(z,t)+i\delta n_{2}^{0}(z,t)
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\delta n^{0}(z,t)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k}a_{k}(t)\psi_{k}^{0}(z)e^{i\omega_{k}t}\,,

(S58)

where

a_{k}(t)=\int dz\delta n^{0}(z,t)(\psi^{0}_{k})^{*}(z)\,,

(S59)

is the amplitude of the spin wave with momentum
k
that oscillates with frequency
\sim\omega_{k}
, and
\psi_{k}^{0}(z)=e^{ikz}
is the associated wave-function. This in turn allows us to find the actual fields
\psi_{k}(z)=a^{\dagger}\psi_{k}^{0}(z)
, that is,

\psi_{k}(z,Z)=\frac{\tanh(z-Z)-ik}{\sqrt{s(1+k^{2})}}e^{ikz}\equiv\psi^{*}_{-k}(z)\,.

(S60)

The factor
1/\sqrt{s(1+k^{2})}
was chosen to ensure that the following normalization condition is maintained:

s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\psi^{*}_{k}(z)\psi_{k^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+k^{2})(1+k^{\prime 2})}}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\left[e^{i(k-k^{\prime})z}(1+kk^{\prime})-\frac{d}{dz}\left(e^{i(k-k^{\prime})z}\tan(z-Z)\right)\right]=2\pi\delta(k-k^{\prime})\,,

(S61)

where we neglected the last term that gives a highly oscillatory contribution when
L\rightarrow\infty
. Finally, a general solution can be written
as follows

\displaystyle\delta n(z,t)=\sum_{k}a_{k}(t)\psi_{k}(z,Z)\,,

(S62)

leading to the Lagrangian describing an infinite system:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0,m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(ia^{*}_{k}\dot{a}_{k}-\omega_{k}a^{*}_{k}a_{k})\,.

(S63)

In a finite wire of length
2L
, the general solution for a magnon of a given energy
\omega_{k}
can be written as:

\displaystyle\delta n(z,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k>0}a_{k}(t)\Psi_{k}(z,t)\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\psi_{k}(z,Z)+\beta_{k}\psi_{-k}(z,Z)\,,

(S64)

with the coefficients
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
determined from the assumption of vanishing spin current at the boundaries,
{\bm{j}}_{s}(\pm L,t)=0
. That is equivalent to imposing von Neumann boundary conditions, or

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\delta n_{1,2}(z\rightarrow\pm L,t)=0\,,

(S65)

resulting in the following implicit equations:

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k}e^{\pm ikL}+\beta_{k}e^{\mp ikL}){\rm sech}^{2}{(\pm L-Z)}+k\left[k(\alpha_{k}e^{\pm ikL}+\beta_{k}e^{\mp ikL})+i(\alpha_{k}e^{\pm ikL}-\beta_{k}e^{\mp ikL})\tanh{(\pm L-Z)}\right]=0\,.

(S66)

When
|Z|\ll L
, we can ignore the term proportional to
{\rm sech}^{2}(\pm L-Z)
, and the boundary conditions can be simplified to:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(k+i)e^{ikL}

\displaystyle+\beta_{k}(k-i)e^{-ikL}=0\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(k-i)e^{-ikL}

\displaystyle+\beta_{k}(k+i)e^{ikL}=0\,,

(S67)

from which we can extract the allowed wave-vectors
k_{n}\equiv k(n)
and the associated eigen-energies
\omega_{n}=\omega(k_{n})
. Specifically, we find the following transcendental equation

\displaystyle\tan(2k_{n}L)=\frac{2k_{n}}{1-k_{n}^{2}}\,,

(S68)

which encodes two types of alternating solutions:
\tan(k_{n}L)=k_{n}
and
\tan(k_{n}L)=-1/k_{n}
, that yields
\exp(2ik_{n}L)=\pm(i-k_{n})/(i+k_{n})
, respectively. Moreover, from

\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n}\frac{i+k_{n}}{i-k_{n}}e^{ik_{n}L}\equiv\pm\alpha_{n}

(S69)

we can write

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z,Z)\approx C_{n}\left[\psi_{k_{n}}(z,Z)\pm\psi_{-k_{n}}(z,Z)\right]\equiv\pm\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\,,

(S70)

where
C_{n}\approx 1/\sqrt{2L}
is found from the normalization of the wave-functions.
In the representation
\Psi_{n}(z,Z)
, the fluctuating magnonic fields can be formally written as:

\displaystyle\delta n_{1}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}[\Psi_{n}(z,Z)a_{n}(t)+\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{n}(t)]\,,

\displaystyle\delta n_{2}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n}[\Psi_{n}(z,Z)a_{n}(t)-\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{n}(t)]\,,

(S71)

where
a_{n}(t)
are the amplitudes of the spin waves with the quantum number
n
.
Consequently, the free magnon Lagrangian becomes identical to that in Eq. (S27), with
k\rightarrow n
, with the corresponding Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{0,m}=\sum_{n}\omega_{n}a_{n}^{*}a_{n}\,.

(S72)

Figure S2:
The energies
\omega_{n}
and the wave-functions
\Psi_{n}(z)
of the first five magnonic modes (
n=1,\dots,5
) in the presence of a DW located at different positions
Z_{0}
in the ferromagnetic wire of length
2L=20
. The arrows depict the spin orientation in each of the wire segment. All energies are expressed in terms of the magnonic energy gap
\omega_{0}=K_{e}/s
.

Let us briefly discuss the approximate spectrum at small momenta
k_{n}\ll 1
(that is, for wavelengths much larger than the DW size
\lambda
). In that case, we can write
\tan(2k_{n}L)\sim 2k_{n}
and, together with
\tan(\theta+n\pi)=\tan(\theta)
,
n\in\mathcal{N}
, allows us to write
k_{n}=(r+n\pi/2)/L
, or

\tan(2r)\sim 2r=2k\,.

(S73)

Therefore, we find that the solutions are:

k_{n}\approx\frac{\pi n}{2(L-1)}\,,

(S74)

with
n=0,1,2,\dots
. We can easily infer that
n=2k
(
n=2k+1
), with
k=0,1,\dots
pertaining to the first (second) type of magnon solution.

II.2
Berry phase mediated interaction between the DW and magnons

In the preceding section, the wave-functions
\Psi_{n}(z,Z)
form a complete basis and can therefore be utilized to represent the magnonic fields
\delta n_{1,2}(z,t)
also when
Z
and
\Phi
become time-dependent.

The Berry phase contribution to the Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}

\displaystyle=-s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\frac{{\bm{d}}\times{\bm{n}}}{1-{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{d}}}\,\dot{\bm{n}}\,,

(S75)

where
{\bm{d}}
is the (arbitrary) direction of the Dirac string. In the following, we choose
{\bm{d}}=-{\bm{e}}_{z}
, and expand
\mathcal{L}_{B}
to the second order in the fluctuations. Retaining only the terms that contain the magnons, we obtain
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}^{B}=\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm m},1}+\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm m},2}+\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm int}}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm m},1}

\displaystyle=s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\left(\delta n_{1}\dot{\Phi}\sin\theta_{0}+\delta n_{2}\dot{Z}\partial_{z}\theta_{0}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm m},2}

\displaystyle=-s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\delta n_{2}\partial_{t}\delta n_{1}\,,

(S76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{B}_{{\rm int}}

\displaystyle=s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\left(-\dot{Z}\delta n_{2}\partial_{Z}\delta n_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(\delta n_{1}^{2}+\delta n_{2}^{2})\cos\theta_{0}\dot{\Phi}\right)\equiv\dot{Z}P_{\rm m}^{Z}+\dot{\Phi}P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}\,,

where

\displaystyle P_{\rm m}^{Z}

\displaystyle=-s\int_{-L}^{L}dz\delta n_{2}\partial_{Z}\delta n_{1}\,,

(S77)

\displaystyle P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dz(\delta n_{1}^{2}+\delta n_{2}^{2})\cos\theta_{0}\,.

(S78)

Above,
P_{\rm m}^{Z}
and
P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}
represent the pressure of the magnons on the DW along the
Z
and
\Phi
directions, respectively
Kim et al. 2018
. These terms occur because the basis states become time-dependent: sliding and rotating along with the DW, respectively. The last term, in particular, pertain to a fictitious magnetic field
\dot{\Phi}
along the wire axis, which in the case of a uniform magnet does not have any physical effects (it shifts the zero of energy by
\dot{\Phi}
). However, as demonstrated later, it can cause physical effects when the wire contains DW(s). Importantly, the linear coupling terms vanish due to the orthogonality of the magnon modes with the DW zero modes encoded by
\{Z,\Phi\}
.

Writing explicitly the expressions for the magnonic fields we find for the second term:

\displaystyle P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{8}\sum_{n,m}\int_{-L}^{L}dz([\Psi_{n}(z,Z)a_{n}(t)+\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{n}(t)][\Psi_{m}(z,Z)a_{m}(t)+\Psi_{m}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{m}(t)]

\displaystyle-[\Psi_{n}(z,Z)a_{n}(t)-\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{n}(t)][\Psi_{m}(z,Z)a_{m}(t)-\Psi_{m}^{*}(z,Z)a^{*}_{m}(t)])\cos(\theta_{0})\approx\sum_{n,m}P_{nm}^{\Phi}a_{n}^{*}(t)a_{m}(t)\,,

(S79)

\displaystyle P_{nm}^{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\Psi_{m}(z,Z)\cos(\theta_{0})\,.

(S80)

In the last line, we disregard the terms
\propto a_{n}(t)a_{m}(t)
and
\propto a_{n}^{*}(t)a_{m}^{*}(t)
as they relate to high-energy processes that are not effective in the low-energy theory describing the DW. Of particular importance in our work are the diagonal terms, that is

\displaystyle P_{nn}^{\Phi}\equiv P_{n}(Z_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\Psi_{n}(z,Z)\cos(\theta_{0})\,,

(S81)

which will be discussed in more detail in the following sections.

II.3
Equations of motion in the presence of magnons

The equation of motion for the magnon field
a_{n}(t)
reads (neglecting the Hamiltonian terms because they scale as
1/L
, as discussed later):

\displaystyle i\dot{a}_{n}=\omega_{n}a_{n}-\sum_{m}(\dot{Z}P^{Z}_{nm}+\dot{\Phi}P^{\Phi}_{nm})a_{m}\,,

(S82)

while the DW dynamics in the absence of Gilbert damping becomes:

\displaystyle\partial_{\Phi}U(\Phi)

\displaystyle-2s\dot{Z}+\dot{P}^{\Phi}_{\rm m}=0\,,

\displaystyle 2s\dot{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial V_{\rm pin}(Z)}{\partial Z}+\dot{P}_{\rm m}^{Z}=0\,.

(S83)

Eliminating
\dot{Z}
from the above equations:

\dot{Z}=\frac{1}{2s}\left(\partial_{\Phi}U(\Phi)+\dot{P}_{\rm m}^{\Phi}\right)\,,

(S84)

we obtain

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\ddot{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\partial_{\Phi}U(\Phi)+\dot{P}_{\rm m}^{\Phi}+\frac{M_{\Phi}}{2SN_{dw}}\ddot{P}_{\rm m}^{Z}\approx M_{\Phi}\ddot{\Phi}+\partial_{\Phi}U(\Phi)+\dot{P}_{\rm m}^{\Phi}=0\,,

(S85)

where in the last line we neglected the
P_{\rm m}^{Z}
terms which become irrelevant for large wires and strongly pinned DW.
This in turn pertains to the total Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=\frac{M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}^{2}}{2}-U(\Phi)+\sum_{n}(ia_{n}^{*}\dot{a}_{n}-\omega_{n}a_{n}^{*}a_{n})+\dot{\Phi}P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}\,.

(S86)

Dropping the
\Phi
index from
P_{\rm m}^{\Phi}
from here on and utilizing
P_{\Phi}=\partial_{\dot{\Phi}}\mathcal{L}_{\rm tot}=M_{\Phi}\dot{\Phi}+P_{\rm m}
, we can readily obtain the corresponding (quantum) Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{\rm tot}=\frac{(P_{\Phi}-P_{\rm m})^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+\sum_{n}\omega_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}+U(\Phi)\,,

(S87)

which is the DW-magnon Hamiltonian displayed in the main text.

II.4
Uniform ferromagnetic lead coupled to the magnetic cavity

In a fixed frame, the magnetic lead is described by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int dz[2s\delta n_{l,1}\partial_{t}\delta n_{l,2}+\delta n_{l,1}\mathcal{H}_{l}\delta n_{l,1}+\delta n_{l,2}\mathcal{H}_{l}\delta n_{l,2}]\,,

(S88)

where
n_{l,1(2)}
are the amplitudes along the two orthogonal directions. More generally, the magnetization fluctuations read:

\displaystyle{\bm{n}}_{l}(z)={\bm{e}}_{1,l}n_{1,l}(z)+{\bm{e}}_{2,l}n_{l,2}(z)\,,

(S89)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{e}}_{1,l}

\displaystyle=(-1,0,0);\,\,{\bm{e}}_{2,l}=(0,1,0)\,.

(S90)

Consequently, the lead can be aligned either ferro- or anti-ferromagnetically with the cavity. The magnonic fields read (assuming Neumann boundary conditions at the interface with the cavity):

\displaystyle\delta n_{l}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{L_{l}}}\cos{[k(z+L)]}b_{k}\,,

(S91)

where
b_{k}
are the amplitudes of the magnons with momentum
k\geq 0
and
L_{l}
the size of the lead. The exchange coupling between the magnetic cavity and the lead at position
z=-L
reads:

\displaystyle H_{\rm c}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}{\bm{n}}_{l}(-L)\cdot{\bm{n}}(-L)\approx\mathcal{T}({\bm{e}}_{1,l}\delta n_{1,l}+{\bm{e}}_{2,l}\delta n_{2,l})({\bm{e}}_{1}\delta n_{1}+{\bm{e}}_{2}\delta n_{2})=\frac{\mathcal{T}}{2}\left(\delta n_{l}^{*}\delta ne^{-i\Phi}+\delta n_{l}\delta n^{*}e^{i\Phi}\right)\,,

(S92)

where
\mathcal{T}
is the coupling strength, which we assumed to be isotropic, while
\delta n=\delta n_{1}+i\delta n_{2}
(and similarly for the lead). Above, we kept only the leading-order contributions in the magnonic fields (the coupling along the
z
direction only renormalizes the magnonic Hamiltonian for each ferromagnetic side with opposite signs for the two alignments). Performing the substitution:

\displaystyle\delta n\rightarrow\delta ne^{i\Phi}\,,

(S93)

the tunneling Hamiltonian becomes independent on
\Phi
, but it affects the effective magnetic field felt by the magnons,
P_{n}=\dot{\Phi}P_{\rm m}
, which now becomes:

\displaystyle P_{\rm m}=\frac{s}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dz(\cos{\theta}_{0}+1)(\delta n_{1}^{2}+\delta n_{2}^{2})\,.

(S94)

This leads to the modified total Hamiltonian, including the lead:

\displaystyle H_{\rm tot}=\frac{(P_{\Phi}-P_{\rm m})^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+\sum_{n}\omega_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}+U(\Phi)+\sum_{k,n}\mathcal{T}_{kn}(b_{k}^{\dagger}a_{n}+{\rm h.c.})+\sum_{k}\omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}\,,

(S95)

and in the following, we retain only the Berry phase mediated interactions between the DW and the magnons (since the Hamiltonian terms scale as
1/L
and are negligible for a large cavity). If we focus on the dynamics around one of the cavity modes,
\omega_{n}
, then
P_{\rm m}\approx P_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}
, with
P_{n}\equiv P_{n}(Z_{0})
being the result of integration for a given position
Z_{0}
of the DW in the wire.

In Figs.
S3
(a) and (b) we show
P_{n}
as a function of
Z_{0}
and
L
, respectively, for the first five magnonic modes. We see that the qualitative behavior follows:

\displaystyle P_{n}(Z_{0})\approx 1+Z_{0}/L\,,

(S96)

up to corrections due to interference effects associated with the standing waves. However, this expression becomes exact for magnons that satisfy
k_{n}L\gg 1
.

II.5
Hamiltonian induced DW-magnon couplings

The Hamiltonian part of the Lagrangian can be found similarly, by expanding again up to second order in the fluctuations
\delta n_{1,2}
. Retaining only the second order corrections in the magnonic fields (since the first order correspond to high-energy processes), the interaction with the DW becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{H}_{int}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\left(\delta n_{1}V_{1}\delta n_{1}+\delta n_{2}V_{2}\delta n_{2}+\delta n_{1}V_{3}\delta n_{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=K_{h}\cos(2\theta_{0})\sin^{2}\Phi-\frac{b}{2}\sin\theta_{0}\sin\Phi\,,

(S97)

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=K_{h}(\cos^{2}\Phi-\sin^{2}\theta_{0}\sin^{2}\Phi)-\frac{b}{2}\sin\theta_{0}\sin\Phi\,,

\displaystyle V_{3}

\displaystyle=K_{h}\cos\theta_{0}\sin(2\Phi)\,.

We can further write:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{H}_{int}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n,m}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\Psi_{m}(z,Z)(V_{1}+V_{2})a_{n}^{*}a_{m}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n,m}(K_{h}J_{nm}\sin^{2}\Phi+bF_{nm}\sin\Phi)a_{n}^{*}a_{m}\,,

(S98)

\displaystyle J_{nm}\equiv J_{nm}(Z_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\Psi_{m}(z,Z)\sin^{2}\theta_{0}\,,

\displaystyle F_{nm}\equiv F_{nm}(Z_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{-L}^{L}dz\Psi_{n}^{*}(z,Z)\Psi_{m}(z,Z)\sin\theta_{0}\,,

where we disregarded terms containing
a_{n}^{*}a_{m}^{*}
and
a_{n}a_{m}
as they do not conserve the number of magnons and correspond to high energy process (note that
V_{3}
only contains such types of process, and therefore does not contribute to the low-energy theory). Furthermore, we also disregarded the terms that only affect the magnons independently on the DW degrees of freedom.

II.6
Effect of the pinning potential on the magnons

Here we scrutinize the effect of the pinning potential on the magnons. Assuming that the DW is strongly pinned at
Z_{0}
, the scattering of the magnons can be extracted by solving the pinning potential at position
Z_{0}
. That is, we need to solve the following Schrodinger equation:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{H}_{0}+V_{0}\delta(z-Z_{0})]\Psi_{n}(z,Z_{0})=\omega_{n}\Psi_{n}(z,Z_{0})\,,

(S99)

where the (dimensionless) scattering strength is
V_{0}=\delta K_{e}/(2K_{e})
, and the wave-function is subject to the boundary conditions
\partial_{z}{\Psi}_{n}(z=\pm L)=0
(vanishing spin current). We seek for solutions of the form:

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{L,k}\psi_{k}(z,Z_{0})+\beta_{L,k}\psi_{-k}(z,Z_{0})&-L<z<Z_{0}\,,\\
\\
\alpha_{R,k}\psi_{k}(z,Z_{0})+\beta_{R,k}\psi_{-k}(z,Z_{0})&Z_{0}<z<L\,,\end{array}\right.

so that the connection between the two across the scatterer reads:

\displaystyle-[\Psi^{\prime}_{n}(Z_{0}^{+},Z_{0})-\Psi^{\prime}_{n}(Z_{0}^{-},Z_{0})]+V_{0}\Psi_{n}(Z_{0},Z_{0})=0\,,

(S103)

as well as
\Psi_{n}(Z_{0}^{+},Z_{0})=\Psi_{n}(Z_{0}^{-},Z_{0})
, stemming from the continuity of the wave-function. Hence, the equations determining the spectrum and the wave-functions, respectively, read:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k,R}(k+i)e^{ikL}

\displaystyle+\beta_{k,R}(k-i)e^{-ikL}=0\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{k,L}(k-i)e^{-ikL}

\displaystyle+\beta_{k,L}(k+i)e^{ikL}=0\,,

(S104)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k,L}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k,R})e^{ikZ_{0}}-(\beta_{k,L}-\beta_{k,R})e^{-ikZ_{0}}=0\,,

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k,L}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k,R})e^{ikZ_{0}}+(\beta_{k,L}-\beta_{k,R})e^{-ikZ_{0}}+\frac{ikV_{0}}{1+k^{2}}(\alpha_{L,k}e^{ikZ_{0}}-\beta_{L,k}e^{-ikZ_{0}})=0\,.

The resulting eigen-momentum equation becomes:

\displaystyle 2(1+k^{2})\left[2k\cos(2kL)-(1-k^{2})\sin(2kL)\right]-kV_{0}\left[(1-k^{2})\cos(2kL)+2k\sin(2kL)-(1+k^{2})\cos(2kZ_{0})\right]=0\,.

(S105)

Although we cannot find an exact solution for
k_{n}
analytically, we can determine approximate solutions in the limit
L\rightarrow\infty
. We can write

\displaystyle k_{n}\approx\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}+a_{n}\right)\,,

(S106)

with
n=0,1,\dots
, and
a_{n}
needs to be found perturbatively. In doing so, we find:

\displaystyle a_{n}=\frac{n\pi}{8L}[4-V_{0}(1+(-1)^{n}\cos(\pi nZ_{0}/L)]\,.

(S107)

Hence, we can deduce that magnons experience minimal scattering at low
k_{n}
values, and the impact of potential pinning can be safely ignored.

III
Derivation of the reduced density matrix describing the DW evolution

In this section, we provide additional information regarding the dynamics of the domain wall (DW) in the presence of driven magnons in the finite magnetic wire system.

III.1
Linearised wire Hamiltonian in the original frame

We assume that the magnetic cavity is driven at a frequency
\Omega_{n}\sim\omega_{n}
. In a frame rotating at this frequency, the total Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle H_{\rm tot}=\frac{(P_{\Phi}-P_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n})^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+U(\Phi)+\Delta_{n}a^{\dagger}_{n}a_{n}+h_{n}(a_{n}^{\dagger}+a_{n})\,,

(S108)

where
\Delta_{n}=\omega_{n}-\Omega_{n}
and
h_{n}=\sqrt{W_{n}\kappa_{n,ex}/\omega_{n}}
is the amplitude of the
ac
magnetic field driving the mode
n
from the left port, with
\kappa_{n,ex}
being the corresponding decay rate and
W_{n}
the injected power. Note that here we disregarded the other (non-driven) magnonic modes, as they are not resonant with the DW states and therefore do not participate in the dynamics. The evolution of the combined system density matrix
\rho_{\rm S}
can be captured by the following Lindblad equation:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\rm S}=-i[H_{\rm tot},\rho_{\rm S}]

\displaystyle+\kappa_{n}\mathcal{D}(a_{n})\rho_{\rm S}+\sum_{j,k}\gamma_{jk}\mathcal{D}(A_{jk})\rho_{\rm S}\,,

(S109)

where we recall that
A_{jk}=|j\rangle\langle k|
. Next, we can expand the magnonic cavity field operators around their steady-state value,
\alpha_{n}
, that is,
a_{n}\rightarrow\alpha_{n}+a_{n}
. The average value
\alpha_{n}
is found by requiring that the linear terms in
a_{n}
and
a_{n}^{\dagger}
that do not involve DW operators vanish, leading to the following equation

\displaystyle h_{n}=\alpha_{n}\left[i\kappa_{n}-\left(\Delta_{n}+\frac{P^{2}_{n}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{M_{\Phi}}\right)\right]\,.

(S110)

The resulting density matrix equation is the same as above, but the unitary part is described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=\frac{(P_{\Phi}-P_{n}|\alpha_{n}|^{2})^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}-\frac{P_{n}P_{\Phi}(\alpha_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}+\alpha_{n}^{*}a_{n})}{M_{\Phi}}+\underbrace{\left(\Delta_{n}-\frac{P^{2}_{n}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}}{M_{\Phi}}\right)}_{\Delta_{n}^{\prime}}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}+U(\Phi)\,,

(S111)

In the limit of small power, or
|\alpha_{n}|\ll 1
, we can disregard all terms
\propto|\alpha_{n}|^{2}
above, which results in the Hamiltonian in Eq. (3) in the main text.

III.2
Total wire Hamiltonian in the rotated frame

The gauge field introduced by the magnons can be removed by a unitary transformation
\mathcal{U}(\Phi)=e^{iP_{n}\Phi}
, so that the total Hamiltonian becomes:

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\rm tot}\equiv\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}(\Phi)H_{\rm tot}\mathcal{U}(\Phi)=\frac{P_{\Phi}^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+U(\Phi)+\Delta_{n}a^{\dagger}_{n}a_{n}+h_{n}(a_{n}^{\dagger}e^{-iP_{n}\Phi}+a_{n}e^{iP_{n}\Phi})\,,

(S112)

which shows that the entire effect of the coupling is lumped into the driving term. Note that in effect this transformation also alters the DW dissipator. However, since the rates are already small compared to the level spacing, we disregard any such modifications induced by this transformation.
We can further displace the magnons by a quantity that, in turn, removes the linear terms that are independent of
\Phi
. That is:

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{\Phi}^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+U^{\prime}(\Phi)+\Delta_{n}a^{\dagger}_{n}a_{n}+h_{n}\left[a_{n}^{\dagger}(e^{-iP_{n}\Phi}-1)+a_{n}(e^{iP_{n}\Phi}-1)\right]\,,

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=U(\Phi)+h_{n}\left[\alpha_{n}^{*}(e^{-iP_{n}\Phi}-1)+\alpha_{n}(e^{iP_{n}\Phi}-1)\right]\,.

(S113)

In the limit
\kappa_{n}\ll\Delta_{n}
(which is needed for efficient cooling), we can approximate
h_{n}\approx-\alpha_{n}\Delta_{n}
, so that:

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{\Phi}^{2}}{2M_{\Phi}}+U^{\prime}(\Phi)+\Delta_{n}a^{\dagger}_{n}a_{n}-\alpha_{n}\Delta_{n}\left[a_{n}^{\dagger}(e^{-iP_{n}\Phi}-1)+a_{n}(e^{iP_{n}\Phi}-1)\right]\,,

\displaystyle U^{\prime}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=U(\Phi)-2\bar{n}\Delta_{n}\left[\cos(P_{n}\Phi)-1\right]\,,

(S114)

where
\bar{n}=|\alpha_{n}|^{2}
is the average number of magnons in mode
\omega_{n}
. As in the previous subsection, if
|\alpha_{n}|\ll 1
, we can disregard the modifications of the potential
U(\Phi)
by the magnons.

III.3
Effective DW density matrix evolution

The total density matrix evolution of the combined system in the lab frame is described by the following Liouville equation:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\rm S}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}+\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}+\mathcal{L}_{\Phi-{\rm m}})\rho_{\rm S}\,,

(S115)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}\rho_{\rm S}

\displaystyle=-i\Delta_{n}[a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n},\rho_{\rm S}]+\kappa_{n}\mathcal{D}(a_{n})\rho_{\rm S}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}\rho_{\rm S}

\displaystyle=-i[H_{\Phi},\rho_{\rm S}]+\sum_{j,k}\gamma_{jk}\mathcal{D}(A_{jk})\rho_{\rm S}\,,

(S116)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Phi-{\rm m}}\rho_{\rm S}

\displaystyle=-ig_{n}[P_{\Phi}X_{n},\rho_{\rm S}]\,,

where
X_{n}=(\alpha_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}+\alpha_{n}^{*}a_{n})/|\alpha_{n}|
and we recall that
g_{n}=|\alpha_{n}|P_{n}(Z_{0})/M_{\Phi}
.
Let us define
\rho_{\Phi}(t)={\rm Tr}_{\rm m}[\rho_{\rm S}(t)]
as the reduced density matrix that describes the dynamics of the DW only, and adiabatically eliminate the magnons. Then, in the interaction picture and within the Born-Markov approximation we obtain
Gardiner and Zoller 2000
:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\Phi}(t)

\displaystyle=-g_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau{\rm Tr}_{\rm m}\{[P_{\Phi}(t)X_{n}(t),[P_{\Phi}(t-\tau)X_{n}(t-\tau),\rho_{\Phi}(t)\otimes\rho_{\rm m}]]\}

\displaystyle=-g_{n}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\langle X_{n}(t)X_{n}(t-\tau)\rangle[P_{\Phi}(t),[P_{\Phi}(t-\tau),\rho_{\Phi}(t)]]+\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau\langle[X_{n}(t),X_{n}(t-\tau)]\rangle[P_{\Phi}(t),\rho_{\Phi}(t)P_{\Phi}(t-\tau)]\right)

(S117)

where
P_{\Phi}(t)=\sum_{j,k}\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle e^{i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})t}A_{jk}
, and
Gardiner and Zoller 2000

\displaystyle\langle X_{n}(t)X_{n}(t-\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\rm m}[X_{n}e^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}\tau}X_{n}\rho_{\rm m}(t)]\equiv\langle a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)a_{n}(t-\tau)\rangle+\langle a_{n}(t)a_{n}^{\dagger}(t-\tau)\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\langle[X_{n}(t),X_{n}(t-\tau)]\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\rm m}[X_{n}e^{\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}\tau}[X_{n},\rho_{\rm m}(t)]]\equiv\langle[a_{n}^{\dagger}(t),a_{n}(t-\tau)]\rangle+\langle[a_{n}(t),a_{n}^{\dagger}(t-\tau)]\,,

(S118)

with
\rho_{\rm m}
being the (thermal) density matrix describing the magnons in mode
\omega_{n}
around the steady state solution
\alpha_{n}
. The correlators above can be evaluated by employing the quantum regression theorem
Gardiner and Zoller 2000
, which gives:

\displaystyle\langle a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)a_{n}(t-\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-(-i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}\langle a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)a_{n}(t)\rangle=e^{-(-i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}n_{B}(\omega_{n})\,,

\displaystyle\langle a_{n}(t)a_{n}^{\dagger}(t-\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-(i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}\langle a_{n}(t)a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)\rangle=e^{-(i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}[1+n_{B}(\omega_{n})]\,,

(S119)

\displaystyle\langle a_{n}(t-\tau)a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-(-i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}\langle a_{n}(t)a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)\rangle=e^{-(-i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}[1+n_{B}(\omega_{n})]\,,

\displaystyle\langle a_{n}^{\dagger}(t-\tau)a_{n}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-(i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}\langle a_{n}^{\dagger}(t)a_{n}(t)\rangle=e^{-(i\Delta_{n}+\kappa_{n}/2)\tau}n_{B}(\omega_{n})\,.

Therefore:

\displaystyle\langle X_{n}(t)X_{n}(t-\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[e^{-i\Delta_{n}\tau}(1+n_{B}(\omega_{n}))+e^{i\Delta_{n}\tau}n_{B}(\omega_{n})\right]e^{-\kappa_{n}\tau/2}\,,

\displaystyle\langle[X_{n}(t),X_{n}(t-\tau)]\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(e^{i\Delta_{n}\tau}-e^{-i\Delta_{n}\tau}\right)e^{-\kappa_{n}\tau/2}\,.

(S120)

We can insert the above equations in the expression for the density matrix evolution to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\Phi}(t)=

\displaystyle-g_{n}^{2}\sum_{j,k}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\tau}\left[e^{-i\Delta_{n}\tau}(1+n_{B}(\omega_{n}))+e^{i\Delta_{n}\tau}n_{B}(\omega_{n})\right]e^{-\kappa_{n}\tau/2}[A_{jk},[A_{jk}^{\dagger},\rho_{\Phi}(t)]]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\tau}\left(e^{-i\Delta_{n}\tau}-e^{i\Delta_{n}\tau}\right)e^{-\kappa_{n}\tau/2}[A_{jk},\rho_{\Phi}(t)A_{jk}^{\dagger}]\right)

\displaystyle=g_{n}^{2}\sum_{j,k}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\bigg(\frac{\kappa_{n}}{2}\left[\frac{1+n_{B}(\omega_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}+\frac{n_{B}(\omega_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\right]\mathcal{D}[A_{jk}]\rho_{\Phi}(t)

\displaystyle+i\left[\frac{(1+n_{B}(\omega_{n}))(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}+\frac{n_{B}(\omega_{n})(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+\Delta_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\right][A_{jk}^{\dagger}A_{jk},\rho_{\Phi}(t)]\bigg)

\displaystyle\equiv i\sum_{j}(\epsilon_{j}+\delta\epsilon_{j})[A_{jk}^{\dagger}A_{jk},\rho_{\Phi}(t)]+\sum_{j,k}(\Gamma_{jk}^{n}+\gamma_{jk})\mathcal{D}[A_{jk}]\rho_{\Phi}(t)\,,

(S121)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}_{jk}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{n}^{2}\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\kappa_{n}}{2}\left[\frac{1+n_{B}(\omega_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}+\frac{n_{B}(\omega_{n})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\right]\approx\frac{1}{2}\frac{g_{n}^{2}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\kappa_{n}}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\,,

(S122)

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon^{n}_{j}

\displaystyle=g_{n}^{2}\sum_{k}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\left[\frac{(1+n_{B}(\omega_{n}))(\Delta_{n}-\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{j})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}+\frac{n_{B}(\omega_{n})(\Delta_{n}+\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j})}{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}+\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k}\frac{g_{n}^{2}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}(\Delta_{n}-\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{j})}{(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta_{n})^{2}+(\kappa_{n}/2)^{2}}\,.

(S123)

In the last lines of each above equation, we assumed
k_{B}T\ll\omega_{n}
such that
n_{B}(\omega_{n})\approx 0
.
Note that
\delta\epsilon_{j}
is the Lamb shift of the DW levels due to interaction with the magnons. We can now establish the stationary density matrix pertaining to the DW, that is,
\dot{\rho}_{\Phi}=0
, and
\rho_{\Phi}=\sum_{j}p_{j}|j\rangle\langle j|
, with
p_{j}
the (out-of-equilibrium) occupation probability of the DW state of energy
\epsilon_{j}
. They obey the following detailed balance equation:

\displaystyle p_{j}\sum_{k\neq j}\underbrace{(\gamma_{kj}+\Gamma^{n}_{kj})}_{\displaystyle{\Gamma_{kj}^{\rm tot}}}-\sum_{k\neq j}p_{k}\underbrace{(\gamma_{jk}+\Gamma^{n}_{jk})}_{\displaystyle{\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm tot}}}=0\,,

(S124)

and subject to the normalization condition
\sum_{j}p_{j}=1
. In the main text, we find
p_{j}
numerically by including the first 20 DW levels in the above equation.

III.4
Wigner function (or quasi-probability distribution)

The Wigner function is defined as
Haroche and Raimond 2006
:

\displaystyle W(\Phi,P_{\Phi})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int d\Phi^{\prime}\langle\Phi-\Phi^{\prime}|\rho_{\Phi}|\Phi+\Phi^{\prime}\rangle e^{2iP_{\Phi}\Phi^{\prime}}\,,

(S125)

and it contains all possible information
about the DW state. The density operator
\rho_{\Phi}
can be deduced from it and hence the expectation value of all the possible DW observables.
Its physical interpretation can be traced back to the expectation values of the operators. Let
A
be an operator in the phase space
\Phi-P_{\Phi}
. Then, its expectation value reads:

\displaystyle\langle A\rangle=\int\int d\Phi dP_{\Phi}W(\Phi,P_{\Phi})A\,.

(S126)

Although naively one could interpret
W(\Phi,P_{\Phi})
as a probability distribution in the phase space, this is not, in fact, a classical probability distribution. A feature that distinguishes the Wigner function from a classical probability distribution is the fact that the former can take negative values. Generally, negativities in the Wigner function are a good indication of the nonclassical nature of a quantum state
Haroche and Raimond 2006
.

At the origin, i.e.
\Phi=P_{\Phi}=0
, we find:

W(0,0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int d\Phi^{\prime}\langle-\Phi^{\prime}|\rho_{\Phi}|\Phi^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j}p_{j}\int d\Phi^{\prime}\Psi_{j}^{*}(-\Phi^{\prime})\Psi_{j}(\Phi^{\prime})\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j+1}p_{j}\,,

(S127)

which is the expression employed in the main text.

III.5
Detection of the DW levels occupation from the emitted power spectrum

In experimental settings, the population of the DW levels can be determined by examining the power spectrum of the magnons that are emitted. This involves applying a separate laser, which is weakly driven, to the magnetic cavity at one of the ports. This laser is tuned to resonate with one of the magnonic modes, denoted as
\omega_{p}
(distinct from the laser used to manipulate occupations). The resulting magnonic radiation is subsequently analyzed.

Let us establish the equations of motion for the quantum fluctuations of the cavity operators
a_{p}
s around the average value
\alpha_{p}
:

\displaystyle\dot{a}_{p}=-\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}a_{p}

\displaystyle-i\frac{P_{n}(Z_{0})}{M_{\Phi}}P_{\Phi}(a_{p}+\alpha_{p})+\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}a_{p,\rm in}(t)+\sqrt{(\kappa_{p}-\kappa_{p,ex})}b_{p,\rm in}(t)\,,

(S128)

where
\kappa_{p}
and
\kappa_{p,ex}
are the total decay rate and the decay rate associated with the vacuum noise of the laser field, respectively. Note that we can write the input contribution as
a_{p,\rm in}(t)\rightarrow h_{p}(t)+a_{p,\rm in}
, which is the sum of the coherent part of the input and fluctuations, respectively. The output power spectrum associated with a mode driven by the laser frequency
\omega_{L}=\omega_{p}
is given by
Wilson-Rae et al. 2008
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\langle a_{p,\rm out}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{p,\rm out}(t)\rangle\,,

(S129)

where the output field,
a_{p,\rm out}(t)
, is related to the input one,
a_{p,\rm in}(t)
, via the input-output relations:

\displaystyle a_{p,\rm out}(t)=\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}[a_{p}(t)+\alpha_{p}]+h_{p}(t)+a_{p,\rm in}(t)\,,

(S130)

and
\langle\dots\rangle
represents the expectation value over the stationary state. We are hence left with finding the cavity field evolution, which can be formally integrated as:

\displaystyle a_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{p,0}(t)+i\frac{P_{n}(Z_{0})}{M_{\Phi}}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau e^{-\kappa_{p}(t-\tau)/2}P_{\Phi}(\tau)[a_{p}(\tau)+\alpha_{p}]

\displaystyle\approx a_{p,0}(t)+i\frac{P_{n}(Z_{0})}{M_{\Phi}}\sum_{j,k}\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle\int_{0}^{t}d\tau e^{-\kappa_{p}(t-\tau)/2}e^{-i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})(t-\tau)}A_{jk}(t)[a_{p}(\tau)+\alpha_{p}]

\displaystyle\approx a_{p,0}(t)-\underbrace{\frac{\alpha_{p}P_{n}(Z_{0})}{M_{\Phi}}}_{\displaystyle g_{p}}\sum_{j,k}\frac{\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle}{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}-i\kappa_{p}/2}A_{jk}(t)\,,

(S131)

where
a_{p,0}(t)
is the solution in the absence of the coupling to the DW, i.e. for
P_{n}(Z_{0})=0
, and we kept the leading order terms in the Dyson series. Hence, the noise can be written as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\bigg\langle\left[\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}a_{p}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)+a_{p,\rm in}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)+\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}\alpha_{p}^{*}-\frac{ih_{p}^{*}}{\sqrt{k_{p,ex}}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}a_{p}(t)+a_{p,\rm in}(t)+\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}\alpha_{p}+\frac{ih_{p}}{\sqrt{\kappa_{p,ex}}}\right]\bigg\rangle\,.

(S132)

This can be expressed as the sum of elastic scattering (which does not involve DW) and the inelastic contribution (or side-band transitions), that is,
S_{p}(\omega)\equiv S_{p,\rm el}(\omega)+S_{p,\rm in}(\omega)
. The former is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p,\rm el}(\omega)\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\left[\frac{1}{\kappa_{p,ex}}|h_{p}|^{2}+\kappa_{p,ex}|\alpha_{p}|^{2}+i(\alpha_{p}^{*}h_{p}-\alpha_{p}h_{p}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|h_{p}|^{2}}{\pi\kappa_{p,ex}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\left[1+\frac{8\kappa_{p,ex}^{2}}{\kappa_{p}^{2}}-\frac{8\kappa_{p}\kappa_{p,ex}}{\kappa_{p}^{2}}\right]=\frac{W_{p}}{\omega_{p}}\left[1-\frac{8\kappa_{p,ex}(\kappa_{p}-\kappa_{p,ex})}{\kappa_{p}^{2}}\right]\delta(\omega-\omega_{L})\,,

(S133)

with the delta function highlighting the elastic processes. The second term, which takes on the behavior of the DW, is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p,\rm in}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\langle a_{p}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{p}(t)\rangle=\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}{\mathcal{R}e}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\langle a_{p}^{\dagger}(t+\tau)a_{p}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}g_{p}^{2}{\mathcal{R}e}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\bigg\langle\int_{0}^{t+\tau}d\tau_{1}e^{-\kappa_{p}(t+\tau-\tau_{1})/2}P_{\Phi}(\tau_{1})\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{2}e^{-\kappa_{p}(t-\tau_{2})/2}P_{\Phi}(\tau_{2})\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}g_{p}^{2}{\mathcal{R}e}\sum_{j,j^{\prime};k,k^{\prime}}|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\bigg\langle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau_{1}e^{-\kappa_{p}\tau_{1}/2}e^{-i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\tau_{1}}A_{jk}(t+\tau)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau_{2}e^{-\kappa_{p}\tau_{2}/2}e^{-i(\epsilon_{j^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{k^{\prime}})\tau_{2}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}g_{p}^{2}{\mathcal{R}e}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\sum_{j,k}\frac{|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}}{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})^{2}+(\kappa_{p}/2)^{2}}\langle A_{jk}(t+\tau)A_{kj}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}g_{p}^{2}{\mathcal{R}e}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{L})\tau}\sum_{j,k}\frac{|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}}{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})^{2}+(\kappa_{p}/2)^{2}}e^{-M_{jk}\tau}p_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{p,ex}}{2\pi}g_{p}^{2}\sum_{j,k}\frac{|\langle j|P_{\Phi}|k\rangle|^{2}}{(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})^{2}+(\kappa_{p}/2)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}}{(\omega-\omega_{L}-\epsilon_{j}+\epsilon_{k})^{2}+(\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff})^{2}}p_{j}\,,

(S134)

where
\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}={\mathcal{I}m}M_{jk}=\sum_{p}(\Gamma_{pk}^{n}+\Gamma_{pj}^{n}+\gamma_{pk}+\gamma_{pj})=\Gamma_{kj}^{\rm eff}
, as demonstrated below. Therefore, for
\omega\neq\omega_{L}
, the total contribution to the noise is due to side-band transitions, as mentioned in the main text.

III.6
The DW correlators

Here, we provide the derivation of the evolution of the various expectation values and correlators utilized above. We start with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle A_{jk}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\langle A_{jk}(t)\rangle-\sum_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{n}+\gamma_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}){\rm Tr}\{A_{jk}(A_{k^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\rho_{\Phi}+\rho_{\Phi}A_{k^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}})\}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}(\Gamma_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{n}+\gamma_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}){\rm Tr}\{A_{k^{\prime}j^{\prime}}A_{jk}A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\rho_{\Phi}\}=[i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})-\underbrace{\sum_{p}(\Gamma_{pk}^{n}+\gamma_{pk}+\Gamma_{pj}^{n}+\gamma_{pj})]}_{\displaystyle\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}}\langle A_{jk}(t)\rangle\,,

(S135)

so that

\displaystyle\langle A_{jk}(t)\rangle=e^{i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})t}e^{-\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}t}\langle A_{jk}(0)\rangle\,.

(S136)

Hence, we can use the quantum regression theorem
Gardiner and Zoller 2000
to extract the correlators:

\displaystyle\langle A_{jk}(t+\tau)A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle=e^{i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\tau}e^{-\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}\tau}\langle A_{jk}(t)A_{j^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\equiv e^{i(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\tau}e^{-\Gamma_{jk}^{\rm eff}\tau}p_{j}\,,

(S137)

where in the last line we assumed the long-time (or stationary) limit density matrix
\rho_{\Phi}=\sum_{j}p_{j}|j\rangle\langle j|
.

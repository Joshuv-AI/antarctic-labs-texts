Meijer’s G G -Function and Euler’s Differential Equation Revisited
Fritz Gesztesy, Markus Hunziker

Abstract
. We consider the generalized eigenvalue problem for the classical Euler differential equation and demonstrate its intimate connection with Meijer’s G G -functions. In the course of deriving the solution of the generalized Euler eigenvalue equation we review some of the basics of generalized hypergeometric functions and Meijer’s G G -functions and some of its special cases where the underlying Mellin-type integrand exhibits higher-order poles.

Introduction

While Euler’s

(1.1)

\Bigg(z^{-N}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[z\frac{d}{dz}-\lambda_{j}\bigg]\Bigg)y(z)=0,\quad\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,1\leq j\leq N,\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\},

\Bigg(z^{-N}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[z\frac{d}{dz}-\lambda_{j}\bigg]\Bigg)y(z)=f(z),\quad\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,1\leq j\leq N,\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\},

has received enormous attention in the ODE literature, see, for instance,
[

, p. 122–123, 131, ]
,
[

, p. 85]
,
[
9
, p.343–344]
,
[

, p. 141–143, 202]
,
[

, p. 26–27, 53–54]
,
[

, p. 116–117]
,
[

, and our observations in Section

, the associated generalized eigenvalue differential equation,

(1.3)

\Bigg(z^{-N}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[z\frac{d}{dz}-\lambda_{j}\bigg]\Bigg)y(\mu,z)=\mu y(\mu,z),\quad\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},\,1\leq j\leq N,\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\},

with
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
the spectral parameter, seems to have escaped scrutiny, at least, we were not able to find pertinent references.

This paper aims at filling this gap and demonstrate that the solutions of (
1.3
) can be expressed in terms of Meijer’s
G
-function, and, generically in terms of generalized hypergeometric functions (see Theorems
4.1
and
4.3
).

In Section

we recall some of the basic facts on generalized hypergeometric functions and Meijer’s
G
-functions, relevant in our context and, in the generic case of the parameters involved (see (
2.5
) and (
2.39
), describe a fundamental system of solutions for the generalized hypergeometric differential equation (
2.4
) and a fundamental system of the Meijer

-function differential equation (
2.22
).

The case of fundamental systems in the general (non-generic) case for Meijer’s
G
-function

is incredibly complex and beyond the scope of this note. Instead, in Section
3
, we only discuss the special situation
G^{m,0}_{0,q}
, a case that is tailored precisely to the generalized Euler eigenvalue problem at hand. In particular, we permit higher order poles in the integrand in the Mellin-type integral for
G^{m,0}_{0,q}
. We have not been able to locate this result, even in the present special case considered, in the literature.

The results of Section
3
are then applied in our principal Section
4
to derive the solutions of (
1.3
).

Finally, in Section
5
we add some remarks on the nonhomogeneous
N
th-order Euler differential equation (
1.2
).

3.

In this section we take a close look at a fundamental system of solutions of then differential equation (

) in the absence of condition (

To analyze the right-hand side of (
2.18
) in the case the integrand has higher-order poles we start with some preparations:

{\bf(I)}
Let
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\Gamma(c-\,\cdot\,)
has the partial fraction expansion (see, e.g.,
[
26
, p. 162]
)

(3.1)

\Gamma(c-\omega)=\underbrace{\int_{1}^{\infty}dx\,e^{-x}x^{c-1-\omega}}_{\text{entire w.r.t. $\omega$}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!(\omega-c-n)},\quad\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{c+{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}.

{\bf(II)}
Let
b\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\Gamma(b-\omega)
has first order poles precisely at the points
\omega\in b+{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and is analytic otherwise on
{\mathbb{C}}
. The residues of
\Gamma(b-\omega)
at
\omega\in b+{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are given by

(-1)^{n+1}/\Gamma(n+1)
, that is,

(3.2)

\lim_{\omega\to b+n}(\omega-b-n)\Gamma(b-\omega)=(-1)^{n+1}/(n!),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(We use the usual convention
0!=1
.)

{\bf(III)}
Let
b\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
1/\Gamma(1-b+\omega)
is entire with respect to
\omega
with simple zeros at
\omega\in b-{\mathbb{N}}
; there are no other zeros in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Consequently, the poles of
\Gamma(b_{j}-\,\cdot\,)
and zeros of
1/\Gamma(1-b_{j^{\prime}}+\,\cdot\,)
,
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq q
, cannot interfere (and possibly cancel each other).

{\bf(IV)}
The map
{\mathbb{C}}\ni\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{\omega}=e^{\omega\operatorname{ln}(\zeta)}
has a cut in
\zeta
(connecting
\zeta=0
with infinity), but no cut in
\omega
.

{\bf(V)}
Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
be open and connected,
\omega_{0}\in\Omega
, and
f:\Omega\backslash\{\omega_{0}\}\to{\mathbb{C}}
analytic, with
\omega_{0}
a pole of
f
of order
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the residue of
f
at
\omega_{0}

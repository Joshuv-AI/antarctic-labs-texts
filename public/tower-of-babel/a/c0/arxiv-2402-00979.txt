Analysis of weak Galerkin mixed FEM based on the velocity–pseudostress formulation for Navier–Stokes equation on polygonal meshes
Zeinab GharibiMehdi Dehghan

Abstract
The present article introduces, mathematically analyzes, and numerically validates a new weak Galerkin (WG) mixed-FEM based on Banach spaces for the stationary Navier–Stokes equation in pseudostress-velocity formulation. More precisely, a modified pseudostress tensor, called 𝝈 {\boldsymbol{\sigma}} , depending on the pressure, and the diffusive and convective terms has been introduced in the proposed technique, and a dual-mixed variational formulation has been derived where the aforementioned pseudostress tensor and the velocity, are the main unknowns of the system, whereas the pressure is computed via a post-processing formula. Thus, it is sufficient to provide a WG space for the tensor variable and a space of piecewise polynomial vectors of total degree at most ’k’ for the velocity. Moreover, in order to define the weak discrete bilinear form, whose continuous version involves the classical divergence operator, the weak divergence operator as a well-known alternative for the classical divergence operator in a suitable discrete subspace is proposed. The well-posedness of the numerical solution is proven using a fixed-point approach and the discrete versions of the Babuška–Brezzi theory and the Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem. Additionally, an a priori error estimate is derived for the proposed method. Finally, several numerical results illustrating the method’s good performance and confirming the theoretical rates of convergence are presented. Keywords : Weak Galerkin. Pseudostress-velocity formulation. Mixed finite element methods. Navier–Stokes equation. Well-posedness. Error analysis. Mathematics Subject Classification: 34B15, 65L60, 65M70.

Introduction

1.1
Scope

The weak Galerkin method (WG), introduced in
[
31
]
for second-order elliptic equations, approximates the differential operators in the variational formulation through a framework that emulates the theory of distributions for piecewise polynomials. Additionally, the usual regularity of the approximating functions in this method is compensated by carefully-designed stabilizers. In turn, the WG methods have been studied for solving various models (cf. (cf.
[
28
,
36
,
36
,
29
,
31
,
32
,
11
,
25
,
27
,
12
,
21
,
13
]
)), demonstrating their substantial potential as a formidable numerical method in scientific computing.
The key distinction between WG methods and other existing finite element techniques lies in their utilization of weak derivatives and weak continuities in formulating numerical schemes based on conventional weak forms for the underlying PDE problems.
Because of their significant structural pliability, WG methods are aptly suited for a broad class of PDEs, ensuring the requisite stability and accuracy in approximations.

On the other hand, the development of certain mixed finite element methods has become a new research field aimed at solving linear and nonlinear problems using velocity-pressure, stress-velocity-pressure, and pseudostress-velocity-based formulations. A principal benefit of stress-based and pseudostress-based formulations is the immediate calculation of physical quantities, such as stress, without the need for velocity derivatives, preventing the degradation of precision. Despite the extensive research on the weak Galerkin FEM, there are few publications on the weak Galerkin mixed-FEM in the literature.
Specifically, and up to our knowledge,
[
32
]
and
[
21
]
are the only two works in which the weak Galerkin mixed-FEM for linear and nonlinear PDEs based on the dual formulation are proposed and analyzed, including corresponding optimal errors estimates.
Although many research works have been studied and analyzed on the weak Galerkin method for the Navier–Stokes problem (cf.
[

,
35
,
33
,
34
,
24
]
), most existing schemes are extracted by the Stokes-type (or primal-mixed) formulation, where the velocity and pressure are principle unknowns.

According to the above discussion, the objective of the present paper is to develop and design a new mixed FEM based on the nonstandard Banach spaces drawn by Gatica et al.
[
18
]
within the framework of the weak Galerkin method to solve the stationary Navier–Stokes equation.
More precisely, we are particularly interested in the development of mixed formulations not involving any augmentation procedure (as done, e.g. in
[
7
,
6
]
). To this end, we now extend the applicability of the approach employed in
[
8
]
for the fluid model, in the framework weak Galerkin method.
In fact, instead of using the primal or dual-mixed method (see e.g.
[
32
,
21
]
), we now employ a modified mixed formulation and adapt the approach from
[
22
]
to propose, up to our knowledge by the first time, a weak Galerkin mixed-FEM for Navier–Stokes, which consists of introducing the gradient of velocity and a vector version of the Bernoulli tensor as further unknowns. In this way, and besides eliminating the pressure, which can be approximated later on via postprocessing, the resulting mixed variational formulation does not need to incorporate any augmented term, and it yields basically the same Banach saddle-point structure.

2
The model problem and its continuous formulation

Consider a spatial bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) with a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
with outward-pointing unit normal
\mathbf{n}
.
We focus on solving the Navier–Stokes equation with viscosity
\nu
, where, given the body force term
\textbf{f}\in\textbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and suitable boundary data
\mathbf{g}\in\textbf{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, the objective is to find a velocity field
\mathbf{u}
and a pressure field
p
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\nu\,\boldsymbol{\Delta}{\mathbf{u}}+{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p&=&\,\textbf{f}&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[8.61108pt]
{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbf{u}})&=&\,0&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[8.61108pt]
{\mathbf{u}}&=&\,\mathbf{g}&{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\partial\Omega\,,\end{array}

(2.1)

In addition, in order to guarantee uniqueness of the pressure, this unknown will be sought in the space

L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\,:=\,\Big\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega):\quad\int_{\Omega}p\,=\,0\Big\}\,.

(2.2)

Note that, due to the incompressibility of the fluid (cf. second row of (
2.1
)),
\mathbf{g}
must satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\,=\,0\,.

For the subsequent analysis we assume that coefficient
\nu
is piecewise constant and positive.

Next, to obtain a velocity–pseudostress formulation, the first step is to rewrite equation (
2.1
) so that stress and velocity are only unknowns of equation. To achieve this, we introduce a tensor field denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
, represented as

\boldsymbol{\sigma}\,:=\,\nu\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}-(p+r_{\mathbf{u}})\mathbb{I}\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,

(2.3)

where

r_{\mathbf{u}}\,:=\,-c_{r}(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}),1)_{0,\Omega}\,=\,-c_{r}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})_{0,\Omega}\qquad\text{with}~~c_{r}\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2|\Omega|}\,.

In this way, applying the trace operator to both sides of (
2.3
), and utilizing the incompressibility condition
\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{u})=0
, one arrives at

p\,=\,-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})\right)-r_{\mathbf{u}}\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,.

(2.4)

which allows us to eliminate the pressure variable from the formulation. In turn, according
to (
2.4
), the assumption (
2.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{tr}}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,=\,0\,.

(2.5)

Hence, after replacing back (
2.3
) in (
2.1
),
gathering the resulting equation and (
2.5
), we have the following problem, which contains unknowns
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
and
{\mathbf{u}}
.

Problem 1

(Model problem)
.

Find
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{u}
such that

\begin{cases}\hskip 2.84544pt\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathtt{d}}+(\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})^{\mathtt{d}}\,=\,\nu\,\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{u}&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\hskip 36.98866pt\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\,=\,-\mathbf{f}&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\hskip 31.2982pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{tr}}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,=\,0\,.\end{cases}

supplied with the following boundary condition

\mathbf{u}\,=\,\mathbf{g}\quad{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\partial\Omega\,.

Next, in order to derive a velocity–pseudostress based-mixed formulation for Problem
1
, we let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbf{Y}
be corresponding test spaces, and then proceed to
multiply the first and second equations of Problem
1
by
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}
, respectively, and use the fact that
{\mathrm{tr}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{\mathtt{d}})=0
, to get

\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{\mathtt{d}}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{\Omega}({\mathbf{u}}\otimes{\mathbf{u}})^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\,=\,\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\mathbf{u}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}\,,

(2.6)

and

\int_{\Omega}{\mathbf{div}}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\cdot{\mathbf{v}}\,=\,-\int_{\Omega}\textbf{f}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}\qquad\forall\,{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Y}\,,

(2.7)

it is easy to notice that, thanks to Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality, the first term on the left hand side of (
2.6
) makes sense for
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
In turn, regarding the term on the right hand side of (
2.6
), assuming originally that
{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, and given
t,t^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
, conjugate to each other,
we can integrate by parts with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{H}({\mathbf{div}}_{t};\Omega)
, so that using the Dirichlet boundary conditions provided in Problem
1
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\mathbf{u}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\,=\,-\int_{\Omega}{\mathbf{u}}\cdot{\mathbf{div}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})+\langle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}{\mathbf{n}},\mathbf{g}\rangle\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{H}({\mathbf{div}}_{t};\Omega)\,,

(2.8)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
stands for the duality

(\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\big)
.
Now, from the first term on the right hand side of the foregoing equation, along with the Sobolev embedding

\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\subset\mathbf{L}^{t^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, we realize that it actually suffices to look for

{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{t^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. However, it is clear from (
2.6
)
that its second term is well defined if

At this point, in order to deal with the null mean value of

(cf. third row of Problem

\mathbb{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{4/3};\Omega)\,:=\,\Big\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}:\quad\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{tr}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,=\,0\Big\}\,.

Then, testing the new (

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{R}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,:=\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{\mathtt{d}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{X}\times\mathbf{Y}\rightarrow\mathrm{R}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{\tau}},{\mathbf{v}})\,:=\,\int_{\Omega}{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\operatorname{\textbf{div}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot):\mathbf{Y}\times\mathbf{Y}\times\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{R}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{C}(\textbf{w},{\mathbf{v}};{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,:=\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\int_{\Omega}(\textbf{w}\otimes{\mathbf{v}})^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\,,

\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,:=\,\int_{\partial\Omega}\textbf{g}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathbf{n}\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{v}})\,:=\,-\int_{\Omega}\textbf{f}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}\quad\forall\,{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Y}\,.

Then, with these forms at hand, the variational formulation of Problem

reads as follows:

(variational problem)
.

Find the tensor
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathbb{X}
and the velocity
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{Y}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})+\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u};{\boldsymbol{\tau}})+\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{\tau}},\mathbf{u})\,=\,\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 119.50148pt\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathbf{v})\,=\,\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v})\quad\forall\,{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Y}\,.\end{array}\right.

The solvability result concerning Problem (
2
) is established as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\delta>0
be constant related to the inf-sup condition of the linear part of the left-hand side of Problem

(cf. Ref.

) and

be the upper bound of

, define the ball

\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\,:=\,\Big\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbf{Y}:\quad\quad\|\mathbf{z}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\,\leq\,\dfrac{\delta\nu}{2}\Big\}\,,

and assume that the given data satisfy

\big(\dfrac{\nu\delta}{2}\big)^{-2}\Big(c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}\Big)\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\,.

Then, there exists a unique solution
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}},\mathbf{u})\in\mathbb{X}\times\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}
for Problem

, and there holds the following stability estimate

\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\|_{\mathbb{X}}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\,\leq\,\dfrac{2}{\delta}\Big(c_{g}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}\Big)\,.

Proof.

See
[

3
Weak Galerkin approximation

This section aims to introduce the weak Galerkin spaces and the discrete bilinear form essential for introducing a weak Galerkin mixed FEM scheme. We focus on the construction of method in the 2D case for simplicity.

3.1
Various tools in weak Galerkin method

A fundamental aspect of the weak Galerkin method lies in employing uniquely defined weak derivatives instead of traditional derivative operators. Our emphasis here centers on the weak divergence operator, a crucial step in introducing our Weak Galerkin technique. To facilitate this discussion, we begin by offering an overview of the mesh structure.

Mesh notation
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}=\{K\}
be a shape regular mesh of domain
\Omega
that consists of arbitrary polygon elements, where the mesh size
h=\max\{h_{K}\}
,
h_{K}
is the diameter of element
K
.
The interior and the boundary of any element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, are represented by
K^{0}
and
\partial K
, respectively.
Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the set of all edges in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\partial\Omega
be the set of all interior edges. Here is a set of normal directions on
\mathcal{E}_{h}
:

\mathrm{D}_{h}\,:=\,\bigg\{\mathbf{n}_{e}:~~~~\mathbf{n}_{e}~\text{is unit and outward normal to}~e,~~~~\text{for all}~e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\bigg\}\,.

(3.1)

Weak divergence operator and weak Galerkin space
.
It is well known that the weak divergence operator is well-defined for weak matrix-valued functions

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\}
on the element
K
such that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\mathbf{n}_{e}\in\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\partial K)
, where
\mathbf{n}_{e}\in\mathrm{D}_{h}|_{K}
. Components
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}
can be understood as the value of function
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
in
K^{0}
and on
\partial K
, respectively.
We follow
[
32
]
, and introduce for each
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
the local weak tensor space

\mathbb{X}_{\mathtt{w}}(K)\,:=\,\Big\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\}:~~~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(K){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\mathbf{n}_{e}\in\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\partial K)\quad\forall\,\mathbf{n}_{e}\in\mathrm{D}_{h}|_{K}\Big\}\,.

(3.2)

The global space
\mathbb{X}_{\mathtt{w}}
is defined by gluing together all local spaces
\mathbb{X}_{\mathtt{w}}(K)
for any
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
. Now, we define
the weak divergence operator for matrix-valued functions as follows.

Definition 3.1

(
[
31
]
)
.

For any weak matrix-valued function
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{\mathtt{w}}(K)
and element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, the weak divergence operator, denoted by
\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w}}
, is defined as the unique vector-valued function
\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(K)
satisfying

({\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}),\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})_{0,K}\,:=\,-({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0},\,\nabla{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})_{0,K^{0}}\,+\,\langle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\mathbf{n}_{e},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle_{0,\partial K}\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(K)\,.

(3.3)

Our focus will be on a subspace of

in which
({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}|_{e})=({\boldsymbol{\tau}}|_{e}\mathbf{n}_{e})\mathbf{n}_{e}

Then, given

\begin{split}\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\,:=\,\Big\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}&=\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\}\in\mathbb{X}_{\mathtt{w}}(K):\quad{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\\
&~~~~~~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}|_{e}=\tau_{b}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{e}\,,\,\,\tau_{b}\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(e)\,,\,\,\forall\,e\subset\partial K\,,\,\,\forall\,\mathbf{n}_{e}\in\mathrm{D}_{h}\Big\}\,.\end{split}

In addition, the global finite dimensional space

, associated with the partition

\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)

Definition 3.2

(
[
31
]
)
.

For any
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)
and element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, the discrete weak divergence operator, denoted by
\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}
, is defined as the unique vector-valued polynomial
\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\in\mathbf{P}_{k+1}(K)
satisfying

({\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}),\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})_{0,K}\,:=\,-({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h},\,\nabla{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})_{0,K^{0}}\,+\,\langle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\rangle_{0,\partial K}\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\in\mathbf{P}_{k+1}(K)\,.

(3.4)

On the other hand, for approximating the velocity unknowns we simply consider the piecewise polynomial space of degree up to
k+1

\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,:=\,\bigg\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}:\quad\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbf{P}_{k+1}(K)\quad\text{for all}~K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\bigg\}\,.

2
-orthogonal projections and approximation properties.
For any

K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}

\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0,r}^{K}:\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{r}(K)

\int_{K}\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0,r}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}):\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{r}=\int_{K}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{r}\quad{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\quad\int_{\partial K}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,r}^{K}(\mathbf{v})\cdot\mathbf{q}_{r}=\int_{\partial K}\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{r}\,,

for all
({\boldsymbol{\tau}},\mathbf{v})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(K)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\partial K)
and

(\widehat{\mathbf{q}}_{r},\mathbf{q}_{r})\in\mathbb{P}_{r}(K)\times\mathbf{P}_{r}(\partial K)

Now, we introduce projection operator
\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}^{K}

as:

\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\,:=\,\left\{\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0,k}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}\,,\,{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}^{K}(\tau_{b})\otimes\mathbf{n}_{e}\right\},\qquad\text{for all}~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\,.

Also, for each element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
and function
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}
, the global projection operator
\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}
on the space
\mathbb{X}_{h}
is defined by

\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})|_{K}\,=\,\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}^{{K}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}|_{K})\,.

The approximation properties of
\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a finite element partition of
\Omega
satisfying the shape regularity assumptions
\mathbf{A1}
-
\mathbf{A4}
stated in
[

]

k,s,m\in\mathrm{N}_{0}
such that
m\in\{0,1\}
there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}
, independent on the mesh size
h
, such that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}-\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\|_{m,0;K}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,C_{1}h^{2(s-m)}|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}|_{s}^{2}\qquad s\leq k\,,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\mathbf{v}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}^{K}(\mathbf{v})\|_{0;K}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,C_{2}h^{2s}|\mathbf{v}|_{s}^{2}\qquad~~~s\leq k+1\,.

(3.7)

3.2
Weak Galerkin scheme

In order to define our weak Galerkin scheme for Problem
2
, we now introduce, when necessary, the discrete versions of the bilinear forms and functionals involving the weak spaces.
Following the usual procedure in the WG setting, the construction of them is based on the weak derivatives to ensure computability for all weak functions.

Notice that, for each
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}
and
{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}},{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, the quantities

\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,,\qquad\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}};{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,,\qquad\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{v})\,,

are computable, while the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}|_{\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}
is not computable because it involve the divergence operator which cannot be evaluated for weak functions. To overcome this matter, employing Definition
3.2
we define the discrete bilinear form

\mathcal{B}_{h}^{K}:\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\times\mathbf{P}_{k+1}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
by

\mathcal{B}_{h}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}},{\mathbf{v}})\,:=\,\displaystyle\int_{K}{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\qquad\forall\,({\boldsymbol{\tau}},{\mathbf{v}})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\times\mathbf{P}_{k+1}(K)\,.

(3.8)

On the other hand, despite the computability of
\mathcal{A}|_{\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbb{X}_{h}}
, this form needs an additional stabilizer term to achieve the well-posedness of our weak Galerkin scheme. More precisely, we define the corresponding discrete bilinear form as follows:

\mathcal{A}_{h}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,:=\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\displaystyle\int_{K}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}^{\mathtt{d}}\,+\,\rho\,\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\,,

(3.9)

where
\rho
is the piecewise constant on
\mathcal{K}_{h}

and the stabilization form
\mathcal{S}^{K}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\times\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
is given by

\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\,:=\,h_{K}\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0}\mathbf{n}-{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{b}\mathbf{n},\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0}\mathbf{n}-{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{b}\mathbf{n}\right\rangle_{0,\partial K}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(K)\,.

(3.10)

In addition, the global bilinear forms
\mathcal{A}_{h}
and
\mathcal{B}_{h}
can be derived by adding the local contributions, that is,

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)\,:=\,\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\mathcal{A}_{h}^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)\,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\,\mathcal{B}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)\,:=\,\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\mathcal{B}_{h}^{K}(\cdot,\cdot)\,.

Finally, let us introduce the subspace of
\mathbb{X}_{h}
as

\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\,:=\,\bigg\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}=\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}:\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{tr}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h})\,=\,0\bigg\}\,.

Referring to the above space, the discrete bilinear form (
3.9
) and (
3.8
),
the discrete weak Galerkin problem reads as follow.

Problem 3

(WG problem)
.

Find
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}
and
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{u}}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{h})\,=\,\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\hskip 150.79968pt\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\,=\,\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{v}}_{h})\qquad\forall\,{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.\end{array}\right.

4
Solvability analysis

This section goals to examine the solvability of Problem
3
. Initially, we delve into the discussion of the stability properties inherent in the discrete forms of Problem
3
. Subsequently, we employ the Banach fixed-point and classical Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorems to establish the well-posedness of the discrete scheme, assuming an appropriate smallness condition on the data.

4.1
Stability properties

We begin by equipping the approximate pair spaces
\mathbb{X}_{h}
and
\mathbf{Y}_{h}+\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
with the following discrete norms, respectively (see e.g.,
[
32
]
)

\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}^{2}\,:=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\big]\qquad\text{for all}~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,,

and

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\,:=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}|\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{1,K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2}\qquad\quad\text{for all}~\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}+\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)\,,

Next, we provide the following result which is a counterpart of
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
for the weak divergence operator.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
\hat{c}_{\Omega}>0
depends on
\Omega
but independent of mesh size, such that

\hat{c}_{\Omega}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0}^{2}\,\leq\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}\|_{0}^{2}+\|\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\|_{0,4/3}^{2}\quad\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,.

(4.1)

Proof.

See
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

Then, some properties of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
(cf. (
3.9
)) are established as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

The discrete bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
defined in (
3.9
), satisfy the following properties:

•

consistency
: for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have that

\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi})\,=\,\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\xi})\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,.

•

stability and boundedness
: there exists positive constant
c_{\mathcal{A}}
, independent of
K
and
h
, such that:

\big|\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\big|\,\leq\,c_{\mathcal{A}}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,,

(4.2)

and let
\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{h}
be the discrete kernel of the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{h}
. Then, there exists constant
\alpha>0
, independent of
K
, such that

\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\,\geq\,\alpha\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}^{2}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{h}\,.

(4.3)

Proof.

By considering
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}
and employing the definition of the discrete form
\mathcal{A}_{h}
(cf. (
3.9
)), along with the observation that the stabilization term (cf. (
3.10
)) vanishes when one of the components is sufficiently regular, we deduce the ”consistency” property. Next, to verify the boundedness of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and virtue of

\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\,=\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\operatorname{tr}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h})\|_{0,K}^{2}\,\leq\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\,.

(4.4)

This gives

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\big|

\displaystyle\,=\,\bigg|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\nu}\displaystyle\int_{K}{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}+\rho\,\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\nu}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}+\rho\left(\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\right)^{1/2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{\nu},\rho\right\}\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,.

We now aim to establish the ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
on the discrete kernel of
\mathcal{B}_{h}|_{\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}
, that is,

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{h}\,:=\,\bigg\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}:\quad\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}\,=\,0\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}\bigg\}\,,

which, together with fact
\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
(cf. Definition
3.2
) implies that

\widetilde{\mathbb{X}}_{h}\,:=\,\Big\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}:\quad{\mathbf{div}}_{\mathtt{w,h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\,=\,0\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega\,\Big\}\,.

(4.5)

Therefore, employing Lemma
4.1
and the fact that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}
is weak divergence-free, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\nu}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\rho\,\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\Big)

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\dfrac{\hat{c}_{\Omega}}{\nu}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\rho\,\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\Big)

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\min\left\{\dfrac{\hat{c}_{\Omega}}{\nu},\rho\right\}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}^{2}\,,

which together with setting
c_{\mathcal{A}}\,=\,\max\left\{\dfrac{1}{\nu},\rho\right\}
and
\alpha\,=\,\min\left\{\dfrac{1}{\nu},\rho\right\}
completes the proof.
∎

To establish the discrete inf-sup condition for the bilinear form

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}
and

\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\,=\,\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\langle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e},\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\rangle_{e}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{0,K}\,.

Proof.

It straightforwardly follows from the definition of

).

We are now in a position to establish the discrete inf-sup condition for the bilinear from
\mathcal{B}_{h}

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a positive constant
\widehat{\beta}
independent of
h
such that

\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}}\dfrac{\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}{\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}}\,\geq\,\widehat{\beta}\,\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.

(4.6)

Proof.

In what follows, we proceed similarly to the proof of
[
32
, Lemma 3.3]
. In fact, given
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, we set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\,=\,-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\qquad\qquad\qquad~\text{in}~K\,,\\[5.69054pt]
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\,=\,h_{e}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\otimes\mathbf{n}_{e}\qquad\text{on}~e\,,\end{array}\right.

which satisfies

\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\,=\,\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\langle{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket,{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\rangle_{e}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{0,K}\,=\,\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\,.

(4.7)

On the other hand, we use the definition of the discrete norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H},h}
to the above chosen
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\,:=\,\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\}
and the trace inequlaity, to obtain

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}^{2}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\mathcal{S}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\Big)

\displaystyle\,=\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\Big(\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\|(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h})\mathbf{n}+h_{e}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{tr}})\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b,k}\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2}\,\leq\,\max\{1,(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{tr}})\}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\,.

Combining the above result with (
4.7
) concludes the desired inequality (
4.6
) with setting
\widehat{\beta}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\max\{1,(1+\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{tr}})\}}}
.
∎

The boundedness properties of the discrete bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{h}
(cf. (
3.8
)) is established as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

The discrete bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
. In other words, there exists a positive constant
c_{\mathcal{B}}
such that

\big|\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\big|\,\leq\,c_{\mathcal{B}}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}\qquad\forall\,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.

(4.8)

Proof.

An application of Lemma
4.3
and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, yields

\begin{array}[]{c}\big|\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\big|\,\leq\,\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}\,\|\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,e}+\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}\,\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\leq\,\left(\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\|\llbracket\mathbf{v}_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,\Omega}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\leq\,\bigg\{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,\Omega}\bigg\}\,\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,.\end{array}

(4.9)

Our aim is now to show that

\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,\lesssim\,\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,.

To attain the aforementioned inequality, we employ the trace inequality, resulting

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\Big(h_{e}\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}-{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h})\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}+h_{e}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\,\leq\,2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}h_{e}\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bh}-{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h})\mathbf{n}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}+2\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathtt{tr}}^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\,.

Substituting the above result back into (
4.9
) finishes the proof of lemma.
∎

satisfies:

\sup_{\textbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\Lambda_{h}[({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})]}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbf{Y}}}\,\geq\,\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}\,\|({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\|_{\mathbb{X}\times\mathbf{Y}}\,,

(4.11)

where

is the positive constant dependent on

.

Furthermore, the boundedness of the trilinear form
\mathcal{C}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathbf{Y}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\times\mathbb{X}_{h}
can be readily inferred by utilizing the Hölder inequality, the Sobolev embedding
\mathbf{H}^{1}\subset\mathbf{L}^{4}
and inequality (
4.4
), as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}\big|\mathcal{C}(\textbf{w}_{h};{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\big|\,=\,\big|\dfrac{1}{\nu}\left((\textbf{w}_{h}\otimes{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{\mathtt{d}},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}\right)_{0,\Omega}\big|\\[8.61108pt]
\,=\,\big|\dfrac{1}{\nu}\left((\textbf{w}_{h}\otimes{\mathbf{v}}_{h}),{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}\right)_{0,\Omega}\big|\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\|\textbf{w}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}\|_{0,\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\leq\,\dfrac{c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}{\nu}\|\textbf{w}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,.\end{array}

(4.12)

4.2
The fixed-point strategy

We begin by introducing the associated fixed-point operator for any
\textbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
as
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{z}_{h})\,=\,{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}
, where
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
is the solution of the linearized version of Problem
3
, that is,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})&=&\,\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})&\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})&=&\,\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{v}}_{h})&\quad\forall\,{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

Equivalently, given
{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, introducing the bilinear form
\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}:(\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h})\times(\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h})\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
given by

\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\textbf{w}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,:=\,\Lambda_{h}[({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\textbf{w}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,+\,\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h};\textbf{w}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\,,

(4.14)

and the linear functional
\mathrm{F}:\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\rightarrow\mathrm{R}
as

\mathrm{F}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\,:=\,\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{v}}_{h})\,.

(4.15)

for all
({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, the linearized problem (
4.13
) can be rewritten as

\Lambda_{\textbf{z}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},\textbf{u}_{\star,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,=\,\mathrm{F}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\qquad\forall\,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.

(4.16)

It can be observed that solving Problem
3
is equivalent to seeking a fixed point of
\textbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
, that is: Find
\textbf{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
such that

\textbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{u}_{h})\,=\,\textbf{u}_{h}\,.

Next, we utilize the classical Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem (see, for instance
[
14
, Theorem 2.6]
) to demonstrate that, for any arbitrary
\textbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, the problem (
4.13
) (or equivalently (
4.16
)) is well-posed, implying the well-definedness of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
.

The following lemma demonstrates the global discrete inf-sup condition for form
\Lambda_{{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}
on pair-space
\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
\mathbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
such that

\|\mathbf{z}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{\nu\,\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}\,,

(4.17)

there holds

\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})]}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\|_{h}}\,\geq\,\dfrac{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2}\,\|({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\|_{h}\qquad\forall\,({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,.

(4.18)

where
\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}
is the positive constant in (
4.11
).

Proof.

Bearing in mind the definition of
\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}
(cf. (
4.14
)) for each
{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
,
and combining (
4.11
) with the
effect of the extra term given by
\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{z}}_{h};\cdot,\cdot)
, which means invoking the upper bound provided by (
4.12
),
we arrive at

\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})]}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\|_{h}}\,\geq\,\Big\{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}-\dfrac{c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}{\nu}\,\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\Big\}\|(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h})\|_{h}\quad\forall~({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\mathbf{w}}_{h})\in\mathcal{V}_{h},

from which, under the assumption (
4.17
) we arrive at (
4.18
), which ends the proof.
∎

Now we ready to show that mapp
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
is well-defined or equivalently problem (
4.13
) is uniquely solvable.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let the assumption of Lemma
4.6
be satisfied. Then, there exists a unique
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
solution to problem (
4.13
). In addition, there holds

\|\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathbf{z}_{h})\|_{1,h}\,=\,\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}\|_{1,h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{2}{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}+c_{g}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\,.

(4.19)

Proof.

A straightforward application of the classical Babuška–Brezzi theory and Lemma
4.6
implies that problem (
4.13
) is well-posed. For the second part of proof, by combining (
4.16
) and Lemma
4.6
with considering
({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})\,:=\,({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})
,
we readily obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2}\,\|({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})]}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sup_{\mathbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\mathrm{F}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\|_{h}}\,\leq\,\left(c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}\right)\,,

where the boundness of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{F}
was used in the last step, and this finishes the proof.
∎

We continue the analysis by establishing sufficient conditions under which
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
maps a closed ball of

\mathbf{Y}_{h}
into itself.
Let us define the set

\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}_{h}\,:=\,\bigg\{\textbf{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}:\quad\|\textbf{z}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{\nu\,\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}\bigg\}\,,

and state the following result.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that the data are sufficiently small so that

\left(c_{g}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}\right)\,\leq\,\dfrac{\nu\,\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}^{2}}{4c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}\,,

Then
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}_{h})\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}_{h}
.

Proof.

It deduces straightly from a priori estimate stated by (
4.19
).
∎

We now address the continuity properties of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
\mathbf{z}_{h},\mathbf{y}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}
, there holds

\|\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathbf{z}_{h})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\mathbf{y}_{h})\|_{1,h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{4c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}{\nu\,\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}^{2}}\,\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,3/4}+c_{g}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\|\mathbf{z}_{h}-\mathbf{y}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,.

(4.20)

Proof.

Given
{\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\in\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}_{h}
we let
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{z}_{h})={\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}
and
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{y}_{h})={\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}
, where
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h})
and
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h})
are the corresponding solutions of equation (
4.16
). It follows from (
4.16
) that

\Lambda_{h,\textbf{z}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,=\,\Lambda_{h,\textbf{y}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{y}}_{\circ,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,,\quad\quad\forall\,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,,

from which, according to the definitions of
\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}}
and
\Lambda_{h,{\mathbf{y}}_{h}}
(cf. (
4.14
)), we have

\Lambda_{h}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,=\,\mathcal{C}(\textbf{y}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})-\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\,.

This result, combined with (
4.10
) by setting
({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},\textbf{w}_{h}):=({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h})
, yields

\begin{array}[]{c}\Lambda_{h,\textbf{z}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\,=\,\Lambda_{h}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\\[8.61108pt]
\,=\,\mathcal{C}(\textbf{y}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})-\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}(\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\\[8.61108pt]
\,=\,-\mathcal{C}({\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{\mathbf{y}}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\,.\end{array}

Now, we apply the discrete global inf–sup condition (
4.18
) to the left-hand side of
the above equation and utilize the estimates (
4.12
) and (
4.19
), to get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2}\|({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\sup_{\textbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\Lambda_{\textbf{z}_{h}}[({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}),({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})]}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\sup_{\textbf{0}\neq({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}}\dfrac{\mathcal{C}(\textbf{y}_{h}-\textbf{z}_{h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})}{\|({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\dfrac{c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}{\nu}\,\|\textbf{z}_{h}-\textbf{y}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\dfrac{2c_{\mathtt{em}}^{2}}{\nu\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}\left(\|\textbf{f}\|_{0,3/4}+c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\|\textbf{z}_{h}-\textbf{y}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,.

So, thanks to the above inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{z}_{h})-\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}(\textbf{y}_{h})\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\,=\,\|{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\|({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\star,h}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\circ,h},{\mathbf{u}}_{\star,h}-{\mathbf{u}}_{\circ,h})\|_{h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{4}{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}^{2}\nu}\left(\|\textbf{f}\|_{0,3/4}+c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\|\textbf{z}_{h}-\textbf{y}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,,

and thus ends the proof.
∎

The main result of this section is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Assume that the hypothesis of Lemma
4.8
hold.
Then, there exists
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}
solution for Problem
3
. Moreover, there holds

\|({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\|_{h}\,\leq\,\dfrac{2}{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}\,\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}+c_{g}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\,.

(4.21)

Proof.

The compactness of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
on space
\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}_{h}
and the Lipschitz-continuity of
\mathbf{S}_{\mathtt{d}}
are guaranteed by Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
, respectivly. Hence, by applying the Banach fixed-point theorem directly to Problem
3
, one can conclude its existence and uniqueness. Furthermore, the stability result (

4.19

∎

\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H},h}\,,\quad\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1,h}\,,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

•

For any
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}
, from the first equation of Problem

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\theta_{h})\,=\,\big[\mathcal{A}_{h}(\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\mathbf{u})\big]-\big[\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\big]\\[8.61108pt]

against
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}

\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0}
,

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\theta_{h})\,=\,\dfrac{1}{\nu}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{0}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})^{\mathtt{d}}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{0h}^{\mathtt{d}}+\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{h}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},~{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\mathbf{u})+\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h};\mathbf{u}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\\[8.61108pt]

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume that the data satisfy

\dfrac{4}{\nu\alpha_{\Lambda}\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}\left(\|\textbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}+c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{2}\,,

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h};\theta_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})-\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u};\mathbf{u},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h};\mathbf{u}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\,=\,\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u};\mathbf{u},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})\\[8.61108pt]

(cf. Lemma

(cf. (

and

(cf. Lemma

\mathcal{C}_{2}

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}}{2}\|({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},\theta_{h})\|_{h}\,\leq\,\left(\dfrac{4}{\nu\min\{\alpha_{\Lambda},\alpha_{\Lambda,\mathtt{d}}\}}\|\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}+\dfrac{2}{\alpha_{\Lambda}\nu}\|({\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},\theta_{h})\|_{h}\right)\left(\|\textbf{f}\|_{0,4/3}+c_{g}\|\textbf{g}\|_{1/2,\partial\Omega}\right)\\[10.33327pt]

5.9

5.2
Optimal convergence

To obtain an optimal order error estimate for the vector component
\theta_{h}=\mathbf{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}_{h}\textbf{u}

in the usual
\mathbf{L}^{2}
-norm, we consider a dual problem that seeks
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}
and
\Phi
satisfying

\begin{cases}\hskip 2.84544pt\boldsymbol{\vartheta}^{\mathtt{d}}+(\mathbf{u}\otimes\Phi)^{\mathtt{d}}\,=\,\nu\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\hskip 28.45274pt-\operatorname{\mathbf{div}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})\,=\,\theta_{h}&{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{tr}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta})=0{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\Phi\,=\,\mathbf{0}&{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\partial\Omega\,,\end{cases}

(5.13)

Assume that the usual
\mathrm{H}^{2}
-regularity is satisfied for the dual problem; i.e., for any

\theta_{h}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\Phi)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\|_{1}+\|\Phi\|_{2}\,\leq\,C_{\mathtt{reg}}\|\theta_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,.

(5.14)

Next, we give an error estimate for
\theta_{h}

Theorem 5.6

\begin{array}[]{c}\big|\mathrm{I}_{7}\big|\,=\,\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\bigg|\langle\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n}-{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n}),\theta_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}\bigg|=\bigg|\langle\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n}-{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n}),\theta_{h}-\bar{\theta}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}\bigg|\\[4.30554pt]
\,\leq\,\left(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n}-{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{b}^{K}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\mathbf{n})\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\|\theta_{h}-\bar{\theta}_{h}\|_{0,\partial K}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\[4.30554pt]
\,\leq\,Ch\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\|_{1}\left(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\big[h_{K}^{-2}\|\theta_{h}-\bar{\theta}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\big]\right)^{1/2}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\leq\,Ch\|\theta_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\|_{1}\,.\end{array}

Step 3: error estimate.
We now insert the bounds on
\mathrm{I}_{1}
-
\mathrm{I}_{7}
in (
5.17b
), yielding

\|\theta_{h}\|_{0}\,\leq\,\Big(Ch^{k+2}+h(\|\theta_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{\mathbb{H},h})\Big)(\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\|_{1}+\|\Phi\|_{2})\,.

(5.29)

The sought result follows from Theorem
5.5
and employing regularity (
5.14
).
∎

We end this section by establishing the error estimate for the pressure.
By proceeding as in
[
17
, Theorem 5.5, eqs. (5.38) and (5.39)]

(see also
[
18
, eq. 5.14]
), we deduce the existence of a positive constant
C
,
independent of
h
, such that

\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,\Big\{\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,+\,\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\Big\}\,.

(5.30)

Therefore, thanks to Theorems
5.5
and
5.6
we get

\|p-p_{h}\|_{0}\leq C\,h^{k+1}\left(\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\|_{k+1}+\|\mathbf{u}\|_{k+2}\right).

(5.31)

Numerical Results

The purpose of this section is to illustrate the efficiency of the WG pseudostress-based mixed-FEM for solving the Brinkman problem.
Like in Ref.
[
18
]
, we use a real Lagrange multiplier to impose the zero integral mean condition for
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}
of the discrete scheme. As a result, Poblem
3
is rewritten as follow:
find
(({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}),\lambda)\in\mathbb{X}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{Y}_{h}\times\mathrm{R}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathcal{A}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h};{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})+\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{tr}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h})&=&\mathcal{G}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}),&\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\mathcal{B}_{h}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h},{\mathbf{v}}_{h})&=&\mathcal{F}({\mathbf{v}}_{h}),&\quad\forall{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{Y}_{h}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
\xi\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{tr}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h})&=&0,&\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathrm{R}\,.\end{array}\right.

(6.1)

In addition, the individual errors associated to the main unknowns and the postprocessed pressure are
denoted and defined, as usual, by

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{\tt e}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\,:=\,\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}\|_{{\mathbf{div}}_{4/3};\Omega}\,,\quad{\tt e}(\mathbf{u})\,:=\,\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{0,4;\Omega}\,,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}{\tt e}(p)\,:=\,\|p-\widehat{p}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,.\end{array}

In turn, for all
\star\in\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\mathbf{u}},p\}
,
we let
{\tt r}(\star)~:=~\dfrac{\text{log}({\tt e}(\star)/{\tt e}^{\prime}(\star))}{\text{log}(h/h^{\prime})}
be the experimental
rates of convergence, where
h
and
h^{{}^{\prime}}
denote two consecutive mesh sizes with
errors
{\tt e}(\star)
and
{\tt e}^{{}^{\prime}}(\star)
, respectively.

In addition,
a fixed point strategy with a fixed tolerance
\mathtt{Tol}=1
e-6 is utilized for the solving of the nonlinear equation (
6.1
). To that end, we begin with vector all zeros as an initial guess and stop iterations when the velocity’s error between two concessive iterations is adequately small,

\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{m}-{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{h}^{m}\|_{0}+\|\mathbf{u}^{m}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{m}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\leq\mathtt{Tol}\,.

In the first example, we mainly verify the accuracy of the constructed numerical scheme. Examples 2 and 3 is utilized to evaluate the effectiveness of the discrete scheme by simulation of practical problems for which no analytical solutions. We performed our computations using the MATLAB 2020 b software on an Intel Core i7 machine with 32 GB of memory.

Example 4: Fluid flows with heterogeneous porous inclusions

Mathematical modeling and simulation of fluid flows in the presence of single or multiple obstacles have been topics of interest for several decades due to their wide applicability in various practical circumstances across disciplines. Flow past solid bodies such as cylinders and airfoils has been investigated
broadly for a long time (see for instance,
[
15
,
26
,
30
]

We study the time-dependent Navier-Stokes equation, which is dependent on the porosity parameter, within the exterior flow domain (
\Omega_{f}
) characterized by large porosity and in the porous subdomains (
\Omega_{p}
) characterized by small porosity.
A typical computational domain in the present problem is illustrated in Fig.

\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}
and the final time by
t_{F}

\phi
using the following non-dimensionalization

Limit theorems for mixed - norm sequence spaces with applications to volume distribution
Michael L. Juhos, Zakhar Kabluchko, Joscha Prochno

Abstract
Let p , q ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ] p,q\in(0,\infty] and ℓ p m ​ ( ℓ q n ) \ell_{p}^{m}(\ell_{q}^{n}) be the mixed-norm sequence space of real matrices x = ( x i , j ) i ≤ m , j ≤ n x=(x_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n} endowed with the (quasi-)norm ∥ x ∥ p , q := ∥ ( ∥ ( x i , j ) j ≤ n ∥ q ) i ≤ m ∥ p \lVert x\rVert_{p,q}:=\bigl\lVert\bigl(\lVert(x_{i,j})_{j\leq n}\rVert_{q}\bigr)_{i\leq m}\bigr\rVert_{p} . We shall prove a Poincaré–Maxwell–Borel lemma for suitably scaled matrices chosen uniformly at random in the ℓ p m ​ ( ℓ q n ) \ell_{p}^{m}(\ell_{q}^{n}) -unit balls 𝔹 p , q m , n \mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n} , and obtain both central and non-central limit theorems for their ℓ p ​ ( ℓ q ) \ell_{p}(\ell_{q}) -norms. We use those limit theorems to study the asymptotic volume distribution in the intersection of two mixed-norm sequence balls. Our approach is based on a new probabilistic representation of the uniform distribution on 𝔹 p , q m , n \mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n} . Keywords . Introduction and main results

The asymptotic theory of convex bodies is intimately linked to probability theory whose methods and ideas have been key elements in obtaining numerous deep results of both analytic and geometric flavour. It has led to the development of a quite powerful quantitative methodology in geometric functional analysis and allowed to form a qualitatively new picture of high-dimensional spaces and structures. The role of convexity in high
-
dimensional spaces is similar to the role of independence in probability and guarantees a certain regularity of the otherwise complex structure of a high-dimensional space. One of the most classical results of stochastic-geometric and high
-
dimensional flavor is probably the Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel lemma, which asserts that any fixed number of coordinates of a vector chosen uniform at random from the boundary of the unit Euclidean ball
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{n}
is approximately Gaussian (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
), and in the more modern spirit there is the pioneering work of V. D. Milman on the concentration
-
of
-
measure phenomenon, which has led to several major breakthroughs (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
18
]
). The arguably most prominent example of the last two decades is Klartag’s central limit theorem for convex bodies, showing that the marginals of a high
-
dimensional isotropic and log
-
concave random vector are approximately Gaussian distributed
[
16
]
.
Besides Klartag’s central limit theorem for convex bodies, a number of other (weak) limit theorems have been obtained for various geometric quantities in the last decades, demonstrating their regularity and universality; we refer to the survey
[
22
]
for references. Several of those results have led to a deeper understanding of the volume distribution in high
-
dimensional convex bodies.

The motivation of the present paper is essentially twofold and will be elaborated upon in view of classical and preceding works before presenting our main results.

Motivation 1: Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel type results.
Having its roots in kinetic gas theory, and going back to Maxwell and later Poincaré and Borel, it is observed that the first
k
coordinates of a random point on the
(n-1)
-
dimensional Euclidean sphere
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}
are asymptotically independent and Gaussian as
n
tends to infinity; to be precise,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dTV\bigl(\Vertl(\sqrt{n}(X_{i}^{n})_{i\leq k}),\Vertl((Z_{i})_{i\leq k})\bigr)=0,

where
\dTV
denotes the total variation distance,
(X_{i}^{n})_{i\leq n}
is sampled uniformly from
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{n-1}
and
Z_{1},\dotsc,Z_{k}
are independent standard Gaussian variables, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed. We refer to Diaconis and Freedman
[
5
, Section 6]
for a more detailed account and give a more detailed statement in Proposition
10
below. In
[
5
]
, Diaconis and Freedman prove an analogous result for the simplex and exponential distribution. Generalizations to the
\ell_{p}
-
sphere were obtained by Mogul
′
skiĭ
[
19
]
, where the point was distributed according to the normalized Hausdorff measure, and by Rachev and Rüschendorf
[
23
]
for the cone probability measure. The latter authors exploited a probabilistic representation relating a
p
-
generalized Gaussian distribution to the
\ell_{p}
-
balls, allowing one to make a transition from a random vector with dependent coordinates to one with independent ones. Naor and Romik
[
20
]
showed that the normalized Hausdorff measure and the cone probability measure are asymptotically equal (their equality for
p\in\{1,2,\infty\}
irrespective of dimension being long known prior), thereby unifying the previous results. A further generalization to Orlicz balls (and even beyond) was undertaken recently by Johnston and Prochno
[
9
]
. We stress that all results cited have been proved for the total variation distance of probability measures. In the present article we only consider the weak topology on probability measures (equivalently: convergence in distribution of random variables).

Motivation 2: Schechtman
–
Schmuckenschläger type results.
Instigated by a question of V. D. Milman, Schechtman and Zinn
[
27
]
found an upper bound on the volume left over from an
\ell_{p}
-
ball after cutting out a dilated
\ell_{q}
-
ball; incidentally the authors utilized the same stochastic representation as did Rachev and Rüschendorf (see above). A few years after, Schechtman and Schmuckenschläger
[
26
]
used that probabilistic representation in order to investigate the limit of the volume of the cut
-
out portion in the very same setting as before, revealing the following threshold behaviour: below a certain critical dilation factor depending only on
p
and
q
the limit is zero, and above that it is one, provided the
\ell_{p}
-
ball has unit volume. More formally, writing
\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}
for the
n
-
dimensional unit
-
volume
\ell_{p}
-
ball,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\vol_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}\cap t\mathbb{D}_{q}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }tA_{p,q}<1,\\
1&\text{if }tA_{p,q}>1.\end{cases}

About a decade later, Schmuckenschläger
[
28
,
29
]
determined the asymptotics at the threshold itself and found the limit to be
1/2
by proving a central limit theorem that revealed this behaviour. We refer to Proposition
11
below for the precise statement. More recently, Kabluchko, Prochno, and Thäle
[
12
,
14
]
revisited the results of Schechtman and Schmuckenschläger, providing a unified framework and also generalizing the previous works in various directions, yet still treating
\ell_{p}
-
balls and using the probabilistic representation. A further step was taken by Kabluchko and Prochno
[
11
]
, studying the intersections of Orlicz balls and observing a similar thresholding behaviour; here much finer tools from large deviations theory and statistical mechanics where required, and it is not even known whether the limit at the threshold itself exists. Another generalization from
\ell_{p}
-
balls to
\ell_{p}
-
ellipsoids, i.e., axis
-
parallel
-
scaled balls (a case not covered by Orlicz balls), was recently obtained by Juhos and Prochno in
[
10
]
; the phenomenon of the threshold emerges again. The case of intersections of unit balls from classical random matrix ensembles has been treated by Kabluchko, Prochno, and Thäle in
[
13
]
. Let us point out that understanding the asymptotic volume of intersections of scaled unit balls naturally appears, for instance, when studying the curse of dimensionality for high
-
dimensional numerical integration problems
[
8
]
.

Suspecting a universal behaviour among symmetric convex bodies, we tackle another generalization, namely finite
-
dimensional sequence spaces with mixed
\ell_{p}
-
norms, and consider the asymptotic volume of the intersection of two balls: the thresholding behaviour is found to be valid also in this case, and for a wide range of parameters the limit in the critical case is determined; little surprisingly, owing to the larger set of parameters as compared to the
\ell_{p}
-
balls, this limit’s value is much more varied and the overall analysis is considerably more delicate.

Let us point out that the study of mixed
-
norm spaces is a classical one in approximation theory and geometric functional analysis and we refer, for instance, to the work of Schütt regarding the symmetric basis constant of these spaces
[
30
]
, the characterization of mixed
-
norm subspaces of
L_{1}
by Prochno and Schütt
[
21
]
and Schechtman
[
24
]
, the work on non
-
existence of greedy bases for the mixed
-
norm spaces by Schechtman
[
25
]
and the study of volumetric properties of these spaces by Kempka and Vybíral
[
15
]
as well as the recent work of Mayer and Ullrich on the order of entropy numbers of mixed
-
norm unit balls
[
17
]
.

We would like to add that, naturally, it would be interesting to consider even more general norms. The main hindrance, though, is that each of the results referenced in the motivation above, and others more, has required tools tailored to the specific problems; to our best knowledge there is no unified theory yet that would allow us to assess such questions “in one fell swoop.” Current research is conducted, e.g., for Schatten norms of not necessarily square matrices.

The mathematical setup

In order to be able to present our main results, we shall briefly introduce the most essential setup; more details can be found in Section
2
on notation and preliminaries.

For
p,q\in(0,\infty]
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
define the finite
-
dimensional mixed
-
norm sequence space
\ell_{p}^{m}(\ell_{q}^{n})
to be the space
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
endowed with the
(m\cdot n)
-
dimensional Lebesgue measure
\vol_{mn}
and the quasinorm

\lVert x\rVert_{p,q}:=\bigl\lVert\bigl(\lVert(x_{i,j})_{j\leq n}\rVert_{q}\bigr)_{i\leq m}\bigr\rVert_{p},

where
x=(x_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{p}
is the usual
\ell_{p}
-
norm, that is,

\lVert(x_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p}:=\begin{cases}\bigl(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lvert x_{i}\rvert^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
\max\limits_{i\leq n}\lvert x_{i}\rvert&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular, we consider the unit balls

\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\,:\,\lVert x\rVert_{p,q}\leq 1\big\};

the
\ell_{p}
-
unit ball and sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are written
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, respectively;
\omega_{p}^{n}
denotes the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
.

We seek to characterize
\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n})
, the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}
. Given a random matrix
X=(X_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n})
, define

R_{i}:=\lVert(X_{i,j})_{j\leq n}\rVert_{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta_{i}:=(\Theta_{i,j})_{j\leq n}:=\Bigl(\frac{X_{i,j}}{R_{i}}\Bigr)_{j\leq n}\quad\text{for }i\in[1,m];

(1)

then clearly
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m}
,
\Theta_{i}
is almost surely well-defined and
\Theta_{i}\in\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}
.

The notations
R_{i}
and
\Theta_{i}
,
\Theta_{i,j}
are used throughout this article with the meaning given in (
1
); note that they actually depend on the parameters
p,q,m,n
, but we suppress this in our notation.

For
p\in(0,\infty]
the
p
-
generalized Gaussian distribution,
or
p
-
Gaussian distribution
for short, is defined to be the probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
with Lebesgue
-
density

x\mapsto\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{p}+1)}\,\mathsf{e}^{-\lvert x\rvert^{p}/p}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{2}\chF_{[-1,1]}(x)&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

1.1
Main results
—
a Schechtman
–
Zinn probabilistic representation

The first main result, which facilitates all computations and is essential to our proofs, is a probabilistic representation of the uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}
, generalizing the classical result of Schechtman and Zinn
[
27
]
and Rachev and Rüschendorf
[
23
]
. Given the numerous applications of the classical probabilistic representation, the following result clearly is of independent interest.

Proposition 1
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
X\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n})
.

(a)

The distribution of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
has Lebesgue-density

f_{R_{1},\dotsc,R_{m}}(r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m})=\frac{(2n)^{m}}{\omega_{p/n}^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}^{n-1}\cdot\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m}}(r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}).

Therefore,
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
can be represented as

(R_{i})_{i\leq m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}U^{1/(mn)}\,\bigl(\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}}{(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{k}\rvert^{p/n})^{1/p}}\bigr)_{i\leq m}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq m}&\text{if }p=\infty,\end{cases}

where
U,\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{m}
are independent random variables with
U
distributed uniformly on
[0,1]
, and
\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{m}
are
\frac{p}{n}
-
Gaussian.

(b)

The random vectors
(R_{i})_{i\leq m},\Theta_{1},\dotsc,\Theta_{m}
are all independent, each
\Theta_{i}
is distributed according to the cone measure on
\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}
, for
i\in[1,m]
, and therefore can be represented as

\Theta_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\Bigl(\frac{\eta_{i,j}}{\lVert(\eta_{i,l})_{l\leq n}\rVert_{q}}\Bigr)_{j\leq n},

where
(\eta_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}
is an array of independent
q
-
Gaussian random variables.

(c)

The components
X_{i,j}
of
X
have the representation

\begin{split}X_{i,j}&=R_{i}\Theta_{i,j}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}U^{1/(mn)}\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}}{(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{k}\rvert^{p/n})^{1/p}}\,\frac{\eta_{i,j}}{\lVert(\eta_{i,l})_{l\leq n}\rVert_{q}}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\,\frac{\eta_{i,j}}{\lVert(\eta_{i,l})_{l\leq n}\rVert_{q}}&\text{if }p=\infty,\end{cases}\end{split}

(2)

where
U,\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{m},\eta_{1,1},\dotsc,\eta_{m,n}
are as before.

1.2
Main results
—
Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principles

One type of limit theorem which we are considering is a Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principle, that is, a statement about the limiting distribution of the first few coordinates of a random vector. In the following two theorems, we shall always assume
(X_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n})
.

Owing to the nature of the space
\ell_{p}^{m}(\ell_{q}^{n})
, having two parameters for dimension, in the sequel limit theorems will usually be considered for three different regimes: firstly, letting
m\to\infty
while keeping
n
fixed; secondly, vice versa, keeping
m
fixed while letting
n\to\infty
; and thirdly, letting
n\to\infty
while treating
m
as dependent on
n
and going to infinity as well.

In order to keep the amount of case distinctions at a minimum, for the case of the parameter value
p=\infty
we agree on these conventions:

\frac{c}{p}:=0\text{ for any }c\in\mathbb{R},\quad\frac{p}{c}:=\infty\text{ for any }c\in(0,\infty),\quad p^{1/p}:=1.

For the formulation of our results we introduce the following quantities (whose superscripts denote indices, not powers):

\displaystyle M_{p}^{\alpha}:=\frac{p^{\alpha/p}}{\alpha+1}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha+1}{p}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p}+1)}

\displaystyle\text{for }p\in(0,\infty]\text{ and }\alpha\in(0,\infty),

(3)

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}:=1

\displaystyle\text{for }p=\infty,

and

\displaystyle C_{p}^{\alpha,\beta}:=M_{p}^{\alpha+\beta}-M_{p}^{\alpha}\,M_{p}^{\beta},\quad V_{p}^{\alpha}:=C_{p}^{\alpha,\alpha}\quad\text{for }p\in(0,\infty]\text{ and }\alpha,\beta\in(0,\infty),

\displaystyle C_{p}^{p,\beta}:=V_{p}^{p}:=0\quad\text{for $p=\infty$ and $\beta\in(0,\infty)$.}

We can now formulate the first Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principle for the case where
m\to\infty
while
n
is fixed. By
\Vertl(X)
we denote the distribution, or law, of a random variable
X
.

Theorem A

(
m\to\infty
,
n
constant)
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
, let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed, and let
\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{k}
be independent
\frac{p}{n}
-
Gaussian random variables.

(a)

The following weak convergence holds true,

(m^{1/p}\,X_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq n}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\text{d}}(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\,\Theta_{i})_{i\leq k}.

(b)

The empirical measures satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,R_{i}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\Vertl\bigl(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\bigr),

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,(X_{i,j})_{j\leq n}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\Vertl\bigl(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\,\Theta_{1}\bigr).

The convergence is to be understood as convergence in probability in the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively, endowed with the Lévy
–
Prokhorov metric; cf. Lemma
22
.

We now formulate the second Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principle for
n\to\infty
while
m
is either fixed or tends to infinity with
n
.

Theorem B

(
n\to\infty
)
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed or let
m=m(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, let
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
(
k\leq m
if necessary), and let
(\eta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}
be an array of independent
q
-
Gaussian random variables.

(a)

The following weak convergence holds true,

(m^{1/p}\,n^{1/q}\,X_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}(\eta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}.

(b)

The empirical measure satisfies

\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,n^{1/q}\,X_{i,j}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\Vertl(\eta_{1,1}).

The convergence is to be understood as convergence in probability in the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
endowed with the Lévy
–
Prokhorov metric; cf. Lemma
22
.

1.3
Main results
—
weak limit theorems

Here we present three weak limit theorems for
\lVert X\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}
, where
X\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
and
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
in general (for
(p_{1},q_{1})=(p_{2},q_{2})
see Remark
3
below). We start with the case
m\to\infty
while
n
is fixed.

Theorem C

(
m\to\infty
,
n
fixed)
.

Let
p_{1},q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\infty]
and
p_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
let
X^{m}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
. Then

\Biggl(\sqrt{m}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}])^{1/p_{2}}}\,\lVert X^{m}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}-1\biggr)\Biggr)_{m\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\sigma N,

where
N
is a standard Gaussian random variable, and

\sigma^{2}:=\frac{1}{np_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{1}/n,p_{2}/n}}{p_{1}p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}+\frac{M_{p_{1}/n}^{2p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{2p_{2}}]}{(p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}])^{2}}.

Remark 2
.

It can be shown that
\sigma^{2}=0
iff
(p_{1},q_{1})=(p_{2},q_{2})
.

The following weak limit theorem covers the case where
n\to\infty
while
m
is fixed. We obtain both central and non
-
central limit behaviour, depending on the relation/values of the parameters
p_{1}
,
q_{1}
, and
q_{2}
.

Theorem D

(
m
fixed,
n\to\infty
)
.

Let
p_{1},q_{1}\in(0,\infty]
and
p_{2},q_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
X^{n}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
.

(a)

If
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
, then

\Biggl(\sqrt{n}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}-1\biggr)\Biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\sigma N,

where
N
is a standard Gaussian random variable, and

\sigma^{2}:=\frac{1}{m}\biggl(\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{1}}}{q_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{q_{1}}^{q_{1},q_{2}}}{q_{1}q_{2}M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}+\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}{(q_{2}M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{2}}\biggr).

(b)

If
q_{1}=q_{2}
and
p_{1}<\infty
, then

\bigl(mn(1-m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}})\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}E+\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2p_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2},

where
E
is an exponentially distributed random variable with mean
1
,
N_{1},\dotsc,N_{m-1}
are standard Gaussian random variables, and
E,N_{1},\dotsc,N_{m-1}
are independent.

(c)

If
q_{1}=q_{2}
and
p_{1}=\infty
, then

\bigl(mn(1-m^{-1/p_{2}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}})\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i},

where
E_{1},\dotsc,E_{m}
are independent, exponentially distributed random variables with mean
1
.

Remark 3
.

Statement (b) above remains true even if
p_{1}=p_{2}
and
m=m(n)\to\infty
; this is because then
\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{1},q_{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}U^{1/(mn)}
, and
\bigl(mn(1-U^{1/(mn)})\bigr)_{n\to\infty}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}E
.

The third weak limit theorem now treats the case where both
m
and
n
tend to infinity.

Theorem E

(
m,n\to\infty
)
.

Let
p_{1},q_{1}\in(0,\infty]
and
p_{2},q_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
, let
m=m(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, let
N
be a standard Gaussian random variable, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
X^{n}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
.

(a)

If
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
, then

\Biggl(\sqrt{mn}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}])^{1/p_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}-1\biggr)\Biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\sigma N,

where

\sigma^{2}:=\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{1}}}{q_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{q_{1}}^{q_{1},q_{2}}}{q_{1}q_{2}M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}+\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}{(q_{2}M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{2}}.

(b)

If
q_{1}=q_{2}
and

, then

\Biggl(\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}-1\biggr)\Biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\frac{\lvert p_{2}-p_{1}\rvert}{\sqrt{2}\,p_{1}}N.

(c)

If
q_{1}=q_{2}
and
p_{1}=\infty
, then

\Biggl(\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}-1\biggr)\Biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}N.

—

Kempka and Vybíral

Clearly

also depends on

, but since those parameters are fixed, and since we wish to keep the notation simple, we will suppress them.

Corollary 4

(
m\to\infty
,
n
fixed)
.

Let
p_{1},q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\infty]
and
p_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
, let

be fixed, and let

. Define

A_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}:=\biggl(\frac{\omega_{q_{1}}^{n}\,\Gamma(\frac{n}{p_{1}}+1)}{\omega_{q_{2}}^{n}\,\Gamma(\frac{n}{p_{2}}+1)}\biggr)^{1/n}\Bigl(\frac{\mathsf{e}}{n}\Bigr)^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\frac{p_{1}^{1/p_{1}}}{p_{2}^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{1}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}.

Then

\lim_{m\to\infty}V^{m,n}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}<1,\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}=1,\\
1&\text{if }tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}>1.\end{cases}

In order to formulate the next result we remind the reader of the gamma
-
distribution

, defined for shape parameter
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and scale parameter

by its Lebesgue

density,

x\mapsto\frac{x^{\alpha-1}\,\mathsf{e}^{-x/\beta}}{\beta^{\alpha}\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\chF_{(0,\infty)}(x).

Corollary 5

(
m
fixed,
n\to\infty
)
.

Let
p_{1},q_{1}\in(0,\infty]
and
p_{2},q_{2}\in(0,\infty)
with
(p_{1},q_{1})\neq(p_{2},q_{2})
, let

be fixed, and let

. Define

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{m,n}(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}<1,\\

In the case

0&\text{if }q_{1}=q_{2}\text{ and }p_{1}=\infty,\end{cases}

and in the case

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{m,n}(t)&=\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{m-1}{2},2\max\Bigl\{1,\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\Bigr\}\Bigr)\Biggl(\biggl(0,\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}\biggr]\Biggr)\\
&\quad+\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{m-1}{2},2\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\Bigr\}\Bigr)\Biggl(\biggl(\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}})}{p_{1}-p_{2}},\infty\biggr)\Biggr).\end{split}

Remark 6
.

1.

We stress that

does not depend on any of

,
p_{2}
,

-

, resp.

The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is endowed with its Borel
\sigma
-
algebra and the
n
-

-
volume
\vol_{n}
. For a Borel set

with

let
\Unif(A)
stand for the uniform distribution on
A
with respect to
\vol_{n}

The (measure

\chF_{A}

For nonempty sets

2.2
The
\ell_{p}
- and mixed
-
norm sequence spaces

\ell_{p}
-
spaces

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(0,\infty]
let
\ell_{p}^{n}
denote the
n
-
dimensional
\ell_{p}
-
space, that is,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the quasinorm

\lVert(x_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p}:=\begin{cases}\bigl(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lvert x_{i}\rvert^{p}\bigr)^{1/p}&\text{for }p<\infty,\\
\max\limits_{i\leq n}\lvert x_{i}|&\text{for }p=\infty;\end{cases}

this is a norm iff
n=1
or
p\geq 1
. The unit ball and unit sphere are written
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, resp.; the former’s volume is
\omega_{p}^{n}:=\vol_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})=\frac{(2\,\Gamma(1/p+1))^{n}}{\Gamma(n/p+1)}
. On the sphere we introduce the normalized cone measure
\kappa_{p}^{n-1}(A):=\frac{\vol_{n}([0,1]A)}{\omega_{p}^{n}}
, for Borel sets
A\subset\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
; it is the unique probability measure such that the following polar integration formula is valid (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Prop. 1]
): for any measurable map
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=n\omega_{p}^{n}\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}}r^{n-1}\,h(r\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\kappa_{p}^{n-1}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r.

The uniform distribution on
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
has a nice stochastic representation in terms of independent random variables with known distributions, having its roots in
[
27
]
and independently
[
23
]
. In order to formulate it, let
\Gaussp_{p}
denote the
p
-
generalized Gaussian distribution on
\mathbb{R}
; recall from the introduction that it is defined via its Lebesgue density

\frac{\mathrm{d}\!\Gaussp_{p}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2p^{1/p}\,\Gamma(1/p+1)}\,\mathsf{e}^{-\lvert x\rvert^{p}/p}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
\frac{1}{2}\chF_{[-1,1]}(x)&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular,
\Gaussp_{2}=\Nvert(0,1)
and
\Gaussp_{\infty}=\Unif([-1,1])
. An easy calculation shows
M_{p}^{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\!\Gaussp_{p}(x)
for
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, where
M_{p}^{\alpha}
has been defined in (
3
). Now let
X
be a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p\in(0,\infty)
, then
X\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})
iff there exist independent random variables
U\sim\Unif([0,1])
and
Y_{1},\dotsc,Y_{n}\sim\Gaussp_{p}
such that

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}U^{1/n}\,\frac{(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}}{\lVert(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p}}.

(4)

Obviously
\frac{(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}}{\lVert(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p}}\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{n-1}
, and actually its distribution is
\kappa_{p}^{n-1}
. Notice that
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}=[-1,1]^{n}
and hence
\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n})=\Gaussp_{\infty}^{\otimes n}
, therefore the coordinates of
X\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n})
already are independent.

In order for the reader to compare the known results for
\ell_{p}
-
balls with the new ones for
\ell_{p}(\ell_{q})
-

For the Poincaré
–

Borel principle recall the notion of

of probability measures: let
(E,\mathcal{E})
be a measurable space and let

and

be probability measures on

\dTV(\mu,\nu):=2\sup_{A\in\mathcal{E}}\lvert\mu(A)-\nu(A)\rvert

can be shown. Convergence w.r.t.

Proposition 12

.

is almost surely constant, then
\bigl(f(X_{n},Y_{n})\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}f(X,Y)
.

The second allows us to handle remainder terms in Taylor expansions, hence we will call it the ‘remainder lemma.’ In general it appears to be well
-
known and widely used; nevertheless, as we cannot find a good reference, and for the convenience of the reader we also provide a proof.

Lemma 13

(remainder lemma)
.

Let
d,l\in\mathbb{N}
, let
R\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function for which there exist
M,\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
\lvert R(x)\rvert\leq M\lVert x\rVert^{l}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\lVert x\rVert\leq\delta
, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; let
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be real sequences such that
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}=\BigO(1)
and
\beta_{n}=\BigO(\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert^{l})
, and let
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-
valued random variables such that
(\alpha_{n}Z_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mathbf{0}
. Then
\bigl(\beta_{n}R(Z_{n})\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
, then for all those
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
\beta_{n}\neq 0
(for all others the following probability is zero already),

\displaystyle\Wsk\bigl[\lvert\beta_{n}R(Z_{n})\rvert\geq\varepsilon\bigr]

\displaystyle=\Wsk\bigl[\lvert\beta_{n}R(Z_{n})\rvert\geq\varepsilon\wedge\lVert Z_{n}\rVert\leq\delta\bigr]+\Wsk\bigl[\lvert\beta_{n}R(Z_{n})\rvert\geq\varepsilon\wedge\lVert Z_{n}\rVert>\delta\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\Wsk\bigl[\lvert\beta_{n}\rvert\,M\,\lVert Z_{n}\rVert^{l}\geq\varepsilon\bigr]+\Wsk\bigl[\lVert Z_{n}\rVert\geq\delta\bigr]

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[\lVert\alpha_{n}Z_{n}\rVert\geq\Bigl(\frac{\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert^{l}}{\lvert\beta_{n}\rvert}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{M}\Bigr)^{1/l}\biggr]+\Wsk\bigl[\lVert\alpha_{n}Z_{n}\rVert\geq\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert\delta\bigr].

By the premises there exist
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C\in(0,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert}\leq C
and
\lvert\beta_{n}\rvert\leq C\lvert\alpha_{n}\rvert^{l}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, and this implies

\Wsk\bigl[\lvert\beta_{n}R(Z_{n})\rvert\geq\varepsilon\bigr]\leq\Wsk\biggl[\lVert\alpha_{n}Z_{n}\rVert\geq\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon}{CM}\Bigr)^{1/l}\biggr]+\Wsk\biggl[\lVert\alpha_{n}Z_{n}\rVert\geq\frac{\delta}{C}\biggr].

Because of
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}Z_{n}=\mathbf{0}
in probability, the claim follows.
∎

In the remainder of this subsection we gather diverse auxiliary results together with their proofs.

Lemma 14
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
and let
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\sim\Gaussp_{p}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
. If either
q<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
, then

\bigl(n^{-1/q}\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{q}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}(M_{p}^{q})^{1/q}.

Proof.

Case
q<\infty
:
We have

n^{-1/q}\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{q}=\biggl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{q}\biggr)^{1/q},

and the claim follows from the SLLN.

Case
p=q=\infty
:
Here
\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{\infty}=\max\{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\,:\,i\leq n\}=:M_{n}
. First we show
(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}1
, then, because 1 is constant, convergence in probability follows. Clearly
M_{n}\in[0,1]
, hence for any
x\in[0,1]
we get

\displaystyle\Wsk[M_{n}\leq x]

\displaystyle=\Wsk[\forall i\in[1,n]\colon\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\leq x]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Wsk[\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert\leq x]=\Wsk[\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\leq x]^{n}

\displaystyle=x^{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\chF_{[1,\infty)}(x),

where we have used independence for the second equality and identical distribution for the third. This proves
(M_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}1
.

Via the Borel
–
Cantelli lemma it suffices to show

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Wsk[\lvert M_{n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]<\infty

for any
\varepsilon>0
in order to strengthen convergence in probability to almost sure convergence. So let
\varepsilon>0
, w.l.o.g.
\varepsilon<1
, then

\displaystyle\Wsk[\lvert M_{n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=\Wsk[M_{n}\leq 1-\varepsilon]+\Wsk[M_{n}\geq 1+\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)^{n},

where recall
M_{n}\leq 1
. But
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-\varepsilon)^{n}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}<\infty
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
q,r\in(0,\infty)
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\xi_{n}\sim\Gaussp_{p/n}
.

1.

We have the following asymptotics, as
n\to\infty
:

(a)

If
p<\infty
,

\Erw[|\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}]=M_{p/n}^{q/n}=1+\frac{q(q-p)}{2p}\,\frac{1}{n}+\Bigl(\frac{q^{2}}{8p^{2}}-\frac{5q}{12p}+\frac{3}{8}-\frac{p}{12q}\Bigr)\frac{q^{2}}{n^{2}}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\Bigr)

and

C_{p/n}^{q/n,r/n}=\frac{qr}{p}\,\frac{1}{n}+\Bigl(\frac{q^{2}+qr+r^{2}}{2p^{2}}-\frac{q+r}{p}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\frac{qr}{n^{2}}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\Bigr).

(b)

If
p=\infty
,

M_{\infty}^{q/n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)^{k}}{n^{k}}=1-\frac{q}{n}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)

and

C_{\infty}^{q/n,r/n}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\Bigl(\binom{k}{l}-1\Bigr)q^{l}r^{k-l}\,\frac{1}{n^{k}}=\frac{qr}{n^{2}}\Bigl(1-\frac{2(q+r)}{n}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr),

and for any
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
we have (here
E\sim\Exp(1)
)

\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{\infty}^{q/n}\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]=\Bigl(\frac{q}{n}\Bigr)^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert E-1\rvert^{\alpha}](1+\smallO(1)).

2.

We have the distributional limits:

(a)

If
p<\infty
,

\bigl(\sqrt{n}(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-1)\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\frac{q}{\sqrt{p}}\,N,

where
N\sim\Nvert(0,1)
.

(b)

If
p=\infty
,

\bigl(n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}qE,

where
E\sim\Exp(1)
.

Proof.

1.

(a)

Recall the formula in Equation (
3
),

M_{p/n}^{q/n}=\frac{\bigl(\frac{p}{n}\bigr)^{(q/n)/(p/n)}}{\frac{q}{n}+1}\,\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{q/n+1}{p/n}+1\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{p/n}+1\bigr)}=\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{q/p}\,\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{q+n}{p}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n}{p}\bigr)},

and subsequently
C_{p/n}^{q/n,r/n}=M_{p/n}^{(q+r)/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}M_{p/n}^{r/n}
. The result now is a simple consequence of Stirling’s formula,
\Gamma(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,z^{z-1/2}\,\mathsf{e}^{-z}\,\mathsf{e}^{R(z)}
for
z\in(0,\infty)
, where we know
R(z)=\frac{1}{12z}+\BigO(\frac{1}{z^{3}})
.

(b)

Note that of course
\frac{\infty}{n}=\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and thus
\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\sim\Unif([0,1])
, hence by direct calculation

M_{\infty}^{q/n}=\int_{0}^{1}x^{q/n}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{1+\frac{q}{n}};

the result then follows from the geometric series. Therewith we also get

C_{\infty}^{q/n,r/n}=M_{\infty}^{(q+r)/n}-M_{\infty}^{q/n}M_{\infty}^{r/n}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{q+r}{n}}-\frac{1}{\bigl(1+\frac{q}{n}\bigr)\bigl(1+\frac{r}{n}\bigr)},

and we use the geometric series again and the Cauchy product of series.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. It suffices to show
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert n(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{\infty}^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]<\infty
; then the convergence of moments follows together with

n(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{\infty}^{q/n})=n(1-M_{\infty}^{q/n})-n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}q-qE=q(1-E),

where we have anticipated 2.(b), whose proof is independent. We may restrict ourselves to
\alpha\geq 1
, then
\lvert x+y\rvert^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha-1}(\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}+\lvert y\rvert^{\alpha})
by Hölder’s inequality, and so

\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert n(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{\infty}^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\bigl(\bigl\lvert n(1-M_{\infty}^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}+\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]\bigr);

now since
\bigl\lvert n(1-M_{\infty}^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}
converges and hence is bounded, we must ensure
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]<\infty
. Exploiting Taylor expansion of the exponential function we have

\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}=\mathsf{e}^{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert/n}=1+\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}+R\Bigl(\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\Bigr),

where we know
R(x)=\frac{\mathsf{e}^{y}}{2}\,x^{2}
with some
y
between
0
and
x
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
(Lagrangian form of remainder term). In our case, since
\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\sim\Unif([0,1])
, we have
\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\leq 0
almost surely, thus we can estimate

\Bigl\lvert R\Bigl(\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert\leq\frac{q^{2}\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{2}}{2n^{2}}.

In particular we can write
E:=-\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\sim\Exp(1)
, then we get

\displaystyle\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert qE-nR\Bigl(\frac{-qE}{n}\Bigr)\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\biggl(q^{\alpha}\Erw[E^{\alpha}]+n^{\alpha}\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert R\Bigl(\frac{-qE}{n}\Bigr)\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\biggr]\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha-1}\biggl(q^{\alpha}\Erw[E^{\alpha}]+\frac{q^{2\alpha}\Erw[E^{2\alpha}]}{2^{\alpha}\,n^{\alpha}}\biggr),

and clearly this last expression remains bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

(a)

We show the result for
q=p
first. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable and nonnegative, then

\displaystyle\Erw[h(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\bigl(\frac{p}{n}\bigr)^{n/p}\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n}{p}+1\bigr)}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(\lvert x\rvert^{p/n})\mathsf{e}^{-\lvert x\rvert^{p/n}/(p/n)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bigl(\frac{p}{n}\bigr)^{n/p}\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n}{p}+1\bigr)}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\mathsf{e}^{-x/(p/n)}\,\frac{n}{p}\,x^{n/p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bigl(\frac{p}{n}\bigr)^{n/p}\,\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n}{p}\bigr)}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\,x^{n/p-1}\,\mathsf{e}^{-x/(p/n)}\,\mathrm{d}x;

this shows that
\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}
follows a gamma distribution with shape parameter
\frac{n}{p}
and scale parameter
\frac{p}{n}
. Then because of the semigroup and scaling properties of the gamma distribution, there exists a sequence
(g_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of independent random variables, each having a gamma distribution with shape
\frac{1}{p}
and scale
p
, such that

\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}.

The classical CLT yields

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(g_{j}-1)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}\sqrt{p}\,N

with
N\sim\Nvert(0,1)
, where we have used
\Erw[g_{1}]=1
and
\Var[g_{1}]=p
. Since

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(g_{j}-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\sqrt{n}(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1),

this concludes the case
q=p
.

For general
q
call
\Xi_{n}:=\sqrt{n}(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1)
, then we have

\sqrt{n}(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-1)=\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\Bigl(1+\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)^{q/p}-1\Bigr).

Taylor expansion gives

\sqrt{n}(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-1)=\sqrt{n}\Bigl(1+\frac{q}{p}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)-1\Bigr)=\frac{q}{p}\,\Xi_{n}+\sqrt{n}\,R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr),

where the remainder satsafies
\lvert R(x)\rvert\leq Mx^{2}
with some
M>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small. From the case
q=p
we know
(\Xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\sqrt{p}N
, so by Slutsky’s theorem
n^{1/4}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=n^{-1/4}\,\Xi_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0
; thus by the remainder lemma
\sqrt{n}\,R\bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0
, and another application of Slutsky’s theorem leads to the desired statement.

(b)

Using Taylor expansion of the exponential function as in the proof of 1.(b),

\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}=\mathsf{e}^{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert/n}=1+\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}+R\Bigl(\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\Bigr),

where the remainder satisfies
\lvert R(x)\rvert\leq Mx^{2}
with some
M>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small. Rearrange,

n(1-\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n})=-q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert-nR\Bigl(\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\Bigr).

As before we know
\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\sim\Unif([0,1])
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
; this implies
-\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\sim\Exp(1)
. Also
\sqrt{n}\,\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}=qn^{-1/2}\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0
, so the remainder lemma yields
nR\bigl(\frac{q\log\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert}{n}\bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0
. Thus follows the claim. ∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty)
with
p\neq q
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\xi_{n}\sim\Gaussp_{p/n}
; define

Z_{n}:=n\biggl(\frac{\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}-\frac{\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1}{p}\biggr).

Then

(Z_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}\frac{q-p}{2p}\,(N^{2}-1),

where
N\sim\Nvert(0,1)
; and for any
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
we have

\sup\bigl\{\Erw[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}]\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\bigr\}<\infty,

in particular convergence of moments holds true, i.e.,
(\Erw[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}])_{n\geq 1}\to\bigl\lvert\frac{q-p}{2p}\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert N^{2}-1\rvert^{\alpha}]
.

Proof.

First we prove the claimed weak convergence. From the proof of Lemma
15
, 2.(a), we know

\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}

with
(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}\sim\Gamma(\frac{1}{p},p)^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
; we also know

\Xi_{n}:=\sqrt{n}\bigl(\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}-1)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}\sqrt{p}\,N

with
N\sim\Nvert(0,1)
. Then we have
\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}=1+\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}
, and via Taylor expansion of
x\mapsto(1+x)^{q/p}
we get

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle=n\biggl(\frac{1}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\Bigl(1+\frac{q}{p}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{q(q-p)}{2p^{2}}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}^{2}}{n}+R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\Bigr)-\frac{\Xi_{n}}{p\sqrt{n}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}+\frac{\sqrt{n}(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{pM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\,\Xi_{n}+\frac{q-p}{2p^{2}M_{p/n}^{q/n}}\,\Xi_{n}^{2}+\frac{n}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\,R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr),

where the remainder term satsifies
\lvert R(x)\rvert\leq M\lvert x\rvert^{3}
for all
\lvert x\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
with some
M>0
. From Lemma
15
, 1.(a), we know
M_{p/n}^{q/n}=1+\frac{q(q-p)}{2pn}+\BigO(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
; this means both

\frac{n(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}-\frac{q-p}{2p}

and

\frac{\sqrt{n}(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

the latter also implies via Slutsky’s theorem

\frac{\sqrt{n}(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\,\Xi_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0.

Equally by Slutsky’s theorem we get

n^{1/3}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}=n^{-1/6}\,\Xi_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0,

thence with the remainder lemma,

\frac{n}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\,R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}0,

and another application of Slutsky’s theorem leads to

(Z_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}-\frac{q-p}{2p}+\frac{q-p}{2p^{2}}(\sqrt{p}\,N)^{2}=\frac{q-p}{2p}(N^{2}-1).

Now we prove the boundedness of moments. Let
\alpha>0
, w.l.o.g. such that
\alpha\geq 1
and
\frac{2\alpha q}{p}\geq 1
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\bigr]=\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]}\bigr]+\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert\geq n^{1/4}]}\bigr],

(6)

and we are going to show that either term on the right
-
hand side remains bounded as
n\to\infty
. For the first expectation on the right
-
hand side of (
6
) we use the same Taylor expansion as before and additionally apply the inequality
\bigl\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\leq k^{\alpha-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lvert a_{i}\rvert^{\alpha}
(which is a direct consequence of Hölder’s inequality), that is,

\displaystyle\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\alpha-1}\Erw\biggl[\biggl(\biggl\lvert\frac{n(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}+\biggl\lvert\frac{\sqrt{n}(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{pM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mspace{70mu}+\biggl\lvert\frac{q-p}{2p^{2}M_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}+\biggl(\frac{n}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr)^{\alpha}\Bigl\lvert R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\biggr)\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\alpha-1}\biggl(\biggl\lvert\frac{n(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}+\biggl\lvert\frac{\sqrt{n}(1-M_{p/n}^{q/n})}{pM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle\mspace{70mu}+\biggl\lvert\frac{q-p}{2p^{2}M_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr\rvert^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}]+\biggl(\frac{n}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}\biggr)^{\alpha}\Erw\biggl[\Bigl\lvert R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]}\biggr]\biggr).

We already know that the first three deterministic coefficients converge in
\mathbb{R}
; because of
\Erw[\lvert g_{1}\rvert^{\beta}]<\infty
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and of
[
2
, Theorem 2]
also
\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}]
and
\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}]
converge as
n\to\infty
. Finally if
\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}
, then
\bigl\lvert\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr\rvert<n^{-1/4}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
; hence eventually for all
n
, on the event
[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]
we have
\bigl\lvert\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and then

n^{\alpha}\Erw\biggl[\Bigl\lvert R\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\Bigr\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert<n^{1/4}]}\biggr]\leq n^{\alpha}\Erw\biggl[M^{\alpha}\biggl\lvert\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr\rvert^{3\alpha}\biggr]=n^{-\alpha/2}\,M^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{3\alpha}]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

because also
\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert^{3\alpha}]
converges as
n\to\infty
.

Now we attend to the second expectation on the right
-
hand side of (
6
). First we apply Hölder’s inequality,

\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert\geq n^{1/4}]}\bigr]\leq\Erw[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}]^{1/2}\Wsk[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert\geq n^{1/4}]^{1/2}.

(7)

The first factor on the right
-
hand side of (
7
) is dealt with rather crudely, we simply estimate

\displaystyle\Erw[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha}\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}}{qM_{p/n}^{q/n}}-\frac{\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1}{p}\biggr\rvert^{2\alpha}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq n^{2\alpha}\,3^{2\alpha-1}\biggl(\frac{\Erw[\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha q/n}]+(M_{p/n}^{q/n})^{2\alpha}}{(qM_{p/n}^{q/n})^{2\alpha}}+\frac{\Erw[\lvert\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1\rvert^{2\alpha}]}{p^{2\alpha}}\biggr);

for the individual summands we see

\Erw[\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha q/n}]=\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\biggr\rvert^{2\alpha q/p}\biggr]\leq\frac{n^{2\alpha q/p-1}}{n^{2\alpha q/p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Erw[\lvert g_{i}\rvert^{2\alpha q/p}]=\Erw[\lvert g_{1}\rvert^{2\alpha q/p}],

and

\Erw[\lvert\lvert\xi_{n}\rvert^{p/n}-1\rvert^{2\alpha}]=\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}-1)\biggr\rvert^{2\alpha}\biggr]\leq\frac{n^{2\alpha-1}}{n^{2\alpha}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Erw[\lvert g_{i}-1\rvert^{2\alpha}]=\Erw[\lvert g_{1}-1\rvert^{2\alpha}],

so we have

\Erw[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{2\alpha}]^{1/2}=\BigO(n^{\alpha}).

The second factor on the right
-
hand side of (

) equals
\Wsk\bigl[\bigl\lvert n^{-3/4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}-1)\bigr\rvert\geq 1\bigr]^{1/2}
; because the moment generating function of

is finite in a neighbourhood of
0
, the series
\sum_{n\geq 1}(g_{n}-1)
satisfies a moderate deviations principle, hence by
[
4
, Theorem 3.7.1]
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\log\Wsk\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{n^{3/4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}-1)\biggr\rvert\geq 1\biggr]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1^{2}}{\Var[g_{1}-1]}=-\frac{1}{2p}.

This implies that eventually,

\Wsk\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{n^{3/4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}-1)\biggr\rvert\geq 1\biggr]\leq\mathsf{e}^{-\sqrt{n}/(4p)},

and in total we obtain

\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}\chF_{[\lvert\Xi_{n}\rvert\geq n^{1/4}]}\bigr]\leq Cn^{\alpha}\,\mathsf{e}^{-\sqrt{n}/(8p)}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.\qed

Lemma 17
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
q\in(0,\infty)
, either let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed or let
m=m(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
(\xi_{n,i})_{i\leq m}\sim\Gaussp_{p/n}^{\otimes m}
. Then

\biggl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}\biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1.

Proof.

Actually we are going to show that convergence is in
L_{2}
, that is,

\Erw\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-1\biggr)^{2}\biggr]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

First note

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-1=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr)+\bigl(M_{p/n}^{q/n}-1\bigr),

and from Lemma
15
we know
(M_{p/n}^{q/n})_{n\geq 1}\to 1
, hence it suffices to prove

\Erw\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr)\biggr)^{2}\biggr]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

So let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the random variables
\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}
,
i\in[1,m]
, are i.i.d. and centred, hence

\displaystyle\Erw\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr)\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\Var\biggl[\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\lvert\xi_{n,i}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\Var\bigl[\lvert\xi_{n,1}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr].

In any case we have
\frac{1}{m}\leq 1
and from Lemma
15
again we get

\Var\bigl[\lvert\xi_{n,1}\rvert^{q/n}-M_{p/n}^{q/n}\bigr]=\Var\bigl[\lvert\xi_{n,1}\rvert^{q/n}\bigr]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

and this finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
and
q\in(0,\infty)
, and let
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\sim\Gaussp_{p}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}

This follows easily from

and using the tail
-

and if

a.s. and hence
\Wsk[\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\geq x]=0

Lemma 19
.

Let

and

with
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
, and either let

Then

(Y_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}p\sigma N,

where

\sigma^{2}:=\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{1}}}{q_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{q_{1}}^{q_{1},q_{2}}}{q_{1}q_{2}M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}+\frac{V_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}}{q_{2}^{2}(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{2}}.

\alpha\in[1,\infty)
,

(8)

Therefore
\bigl(\Erw[\lvert Y_{n}\rvert^{\alpha}]\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\to p^{\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}\Erw[\lvert N\rvert^{\alpha}]
, and if
\alpha
is integer,
(\Erw[Y_{n}^{\alpha}])_{n\geq 1}\to p^{\alpha}\sigma^{\alpha}\Erw[N^{\alpha}]

\bigl(\Erw\bigl[n^{p(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})}\lVert X_{n}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p}\bigr]\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\to(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{p/q_{2}}.

(9)

Proof.

Concerning convergence of
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
for
p=1
, the only case still open is
X_{n}\sim\kappa_{\infty}^{n-1}
and
q_{2}<\infty
. Let

\lVert X_{n}\rVert_{q_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{q_{2}}}{\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{\infty}}=\frac{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{q_{2}}\bigr)^{1/q_{2}}}{\lVert(\xi_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{\infty}}.

.
Furthermore, as has already been glimpsed in the proof of Lemma

where

s.t.

in distribution and hence in probability; from the latter and the remainder lemma (with
l=1

(\mathrm{H}_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
. Now we may write

\displaystyle\lVert X_{n}\rVert_{q_{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{\bigl(nM_{\infty}^{q_{2}}+\sqrt{n}\,\Xi_{n}\bigr)^{1/q_{2}}}{1-\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}

\displaystyle=n^{1/q_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}\frac{\bigl(1+\frac{1}{M_{\infty}^{q_{2}}}\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)^{1/q_{2}}}{1-\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}}

\displaystyle=n^{1/q_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{q_{2}M_{\infty}^{q_{2}}}\,\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+R_{2}\Bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\biggr)

where we have employed the Taylor expansion

. Notice

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
; since

converges in distribution, Slutsky’s theorem implies
\bigl(n^{1/4}\bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(0,0)
, and with the remainder lemma we infer
\bigl(\sqrt{n}R_{2}\bigl(\frac{\Xi_{n}}{\sqrt{n}},\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
. Another application of Slutsky’s theorem finally yields the desired convergence

\displaystyle Y_{n}=\sqrt{n}\biggl(1+p\,\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+R_{3}\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)-1\biggr)=pZ_{n}+\sqrt{n}R_{3}\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr);

again the remainder term satisfies
\lvert R_{3}(x)\rvert\leq Mx^{2}

. Statement (

) follows from the relation

n^{p(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})}\lVert X_{n}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p}=(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{p/q_{2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{Y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)

and the fact
(\Erw[Y_{n}])_{n\geq 1}\to p\sigma\Erw[N]=0
.
∎

3
Proofs of the Poincaré–Maxwell–Borel principles

In this section we present the proofs of the Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principles, that is, Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
. We shall start with the probabilistic representation of Schechtman
–
Zinn type, which facilitates computations.

3.1
Proof of the probabilistic representation

In this subsection we present the proof of Proposition
1
, which provides us with a probabilistic representation of the uniform distribution on the unit balls in mixed
-
norm sequence spaces.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to[0,\infty)
be an arbitrary measurable function, then

\displaystyle\Erw[h(X)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{p,q}^{m,n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}h(x)\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

or writing
x
in terms of its rows
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}
,

\displaystyle\Erw[h(X)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{p,q}^{m,n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dotsi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})\,\mathrm{d}x_{m}\dotsm\mathrm{d}x_{1}.

Introduce polar coordinates for each row separately, that is
x_{i}=r_{i}\theta_{i}
with
r_{i}\in[0,\infty)
and
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}

—
notice that this corresponds to our decomposition
(X_{i,j})_{j\leq n}=R_{i}\Theta_{i}
introduced in (
1
)
—
to get

\displaystyle\Erw[h(X)]

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{m}(\omega_{q}^{n})^{m}}{\omega_{p,q}^{m,n}}\int_{[0,\infty)}r_{1}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}}\dotsi\int_{[0,\infty)}r_{m}^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1}}h(r_{1}\theta_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}\theta_{m})

\displaystyle\hskip 64.58313pt\cdot\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}}(r_{1}\theta_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}\theta_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\kappa_{q}^{n-1}(\theta_{m})\,\mathrm{d}r_{m}\dotsm\mathrm{d}\kappa_{q}^{n-1}(\theta_{1})\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}.

Finally use
(r_{i}\theta_{i})_{i\leq m}\in\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}
iff
(r_{i})_{i\leq m}\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}
, plug in
\omega_{p,q}^{m,n}=2^{-m}\omega_{p/n}^{m}(\omega_{q}^{n})^{m}
(see Equation (
5
)), and gather terms to arrive at

\displaystyle\Erw[h(X)]

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,\infty)^{m}}\frac{(2n)^{m}}{\omega_{p/n}^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}^{n-1}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}}(r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m})

\displaystyle\hskip 43.05542pt\cdot\int_{(\mathbb{S}_{q}^{n-1})^{m}}h(r_{1}\theta_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}\theta_{m})\,\mathrm{d}(\kappa_{q}^{n-1})^{\otimes m}(\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{m})\,\mathrm{d}(r_{1},\dotsc,r_{m}).

Now we recognize that the last integral proves the claimed density of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
, the claimed independence and the claimed distribution of the
\Theta_{i}
,
i\in[1,m]
.

It remains to show the representation of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
. To that end define
(S_{i})_{i\leq m}:=(R_{i}^{n})_{i\leq m}
, hence
R_{i}=S_{i}^{1/n}
for each
i\in[1,m]
; this yields the Jacobian
n^{-m}\,\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{1/n-1}
and
(S_{i})_{i\leq m}
has density

\displaystyle f_{S_{1},\dotsc,S_{m}}(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{(2n)^{m}}{\omega_{p/n}^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(s_{i}^{1/n})^{n-1}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m}}(s_{1}^{1/n},\dotsc,s_{m}^{1/n})n^{-m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{1/n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{m}}{\omega_{p/n}^{m}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p/n}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m}}(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{m}).

Therefore
(S_{i})_{i\leq m}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p/n}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m})
, and by Schechtman and Zinn it can be written

(S_{i})_{i\leq m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}U^{1/m}\bigl(\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert}{(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{k}\rvert^{p/n})^{n/p}}\bigr)_{i\leq m}&\text{if }p<\infty,\\
(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert)_{i\leq m}&\text{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

Transforming back to
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
concludes the proof.

3.2
Proofs of the Poincaré
–
Maxwell
–
Borel principles

Lemma 20
.

Let
p,q\in(0,\infty]
. Then for any fixed
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
(
k\leq m
,
l\leq n
where necessary):

(a)

(m^{1/p}\,R_{i})_{i\leq k}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\text{d}}(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq k}
for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(b)

(m^{1/p}\,R_{i})_{i\leq k}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}(1)_{i\leq k}
for either fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
or
m=m(n)\to\infty
, and

(c)

(n^{1/q}\,\Theta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}(\eta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}
.

Proof.

(a)

Case
p<\infty
:
We use Proposition
1
, (a),

(m^{1/p}\,R_{i})_{i\leq k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{U^{1/(mn)}}{\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p/n}\bigr)^{1/p}}(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq k}.

(14)

By the SLLN,
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p/n}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}M_{p/n}^{p/n}=1
, hence the right-hand-side converges a.s. to
(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq k}
, and convergence in distribution follows.

Case
p=\infty
:
Obvious because of
(R_{i})_{i\leq k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq k}
.

(b)

Case
p<\infty
:
By Lemma
17
we know both
\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p/n}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1
and
(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1
for each
i\in[1,k]
(apply the lemma with
q=1
and
m=1
), hence the right-hand-side of (
14
) converges to
1
in probability, therefore
(m^{1/p}\,R_{i})_{i\leq k}
converges in distribution towards a constant and thus also in probability.

Case
p=\infty
:
Now
(R_{i})_{i\leq k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})_{i\leq k}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}(1)_{i\leq k}
via Lemma
17
.

(c)

We have by Proposition
1
, (b),

(n^{1/q}\,\Theta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\biggl(\frac{\eta_{i,j}}{n^{-1/q}\lVert(\eta_{i,j^{\prime}})_{j^{\prime}\leq n}\rVert_{q}}\biggr)_{i\leq k,j\leq l}.

(15)

By Lemma
14

(n^{-1/q}\lVert(\eta_{i,j^{\prime}})_{j^{\prime}\leq n}\rVert_{q})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}1
, so the right-hand-side of (
15
) converges to
(\eta_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}
almost surely. ∎

Remark 21
.

Statements (a) and (c) of Lemma
20
can be seen as consequences of Proposition
10
; this is immediate for (c), and for (a) recall from the proof of Proposition
1
that
(R_{i}^{n})_{i\leq m}\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p/n}^{m}\cap[0,\infty)^{m})
.

For a separable metric space
E
let
\Masz_{1}(E)
denote the convex set of probability measures on
(E,\Borel(E))
endowed with the topology of weak convergence of measures; then
\Masz_{1}(E)
is separable too. This topology on
\Masz_{1}(E)
may be metrized by, e.g., the Lévy
–
Prokhorov metric
\dLP
. We denote by
\LipB(E)
the space of bounded, Lipschitz-continuous functions on
E
, equipped with the norm
\lVert f\rVert_{\Lip}:=\max\{\lVert f\rVert_{\infty},\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}\}
where
\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}
is the Lipschitz-constant of
f
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
\Masz_{1}(E)
-
valued random measures and
\mu\in\Masz_{1}(E)
a deterministic measure. Then

(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mu\Longleftrightarrow\forall f\in\LipB(E)\colon\biggl(\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\biggr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Proof.

\Rightarrow
: Let
f\in\LipB(E)
, then the map
\nu\mapsto\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu
is continuous at
\mu
w.r.t.
\dLP
, hence for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
\nu\in\Masz_{1}(E)
,

\dLP(\mu,\nu)<\delta\Longrightarrow\biggl\lvert\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr\rvert<\varepsilon.

Now let
\varepsilon>0
, then

\Wsk\biggl[\biggl\lvert\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}-\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr\rvert\geq\varepsilon\biggr]\leq\Wsk[\dLP(\mu_{n},\mu)\geq\delta]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

\Leftarrow
: Let
\varepsilon>0
. The open ball
B_{\varepsilon}^{\text{LP}}(\mu)
is open in the weak topology, thus there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{m}\in\LipB(E)
and
\delta>0
such that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\biggl\{\nu\in\Masz_{1}(E)\,:\,\biggl\lvert\int_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr\rvert<\delta\biggr\}\subset B_{\varepsilon}^{\text{LP}}(\mu).

The union-bound then implies

\displaystyle\Wsk[\dLP(\mu_{n},\mu)\geq\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=\Wsk[\mu_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}^{\text{LP}}(\mu)^{\mathsf{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq\Wsk\biggl[\mu_{n}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\biggl\{\nu\in\Masz_{1}(E)\,:\,\biggl\lvert\int_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr\rvert\geq\delta\biggr\}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Wsk\biggl[\biggl\lvert\int_{E}f_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}-\int_{E}f_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu\biggr\rvert\geq\delta\biggr]\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.\qed

Proof of Theorem
A
.

(a)

We have

(m^{1/p}\,X_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq n}=(m^{1/p}R_{i}\Theta_{i})_{i\leq k}.

The claim follows from Lemma
20
, (a), together with the independence of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
from
\Theta_{1}
,…,
\Theta_{m}
.

(b)

Case
p<\infty
:
The stochastic representation of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
implies

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,R_{i}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{U^{1/(mn)}\,(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{k}\rvert^{p/n})^{-1/p}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}}.

For the sake of legibility call
C_{m}:=U^{1/(mn)}\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p/n}\bigr)^{-1/p}
, then
(C_{m})_{m\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}1
. Now it suffices to prove

\biggl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}}\biggr)_{m\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\Vertl(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}),

since then
\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,R_{i}}\bigr)_{m\geq 1}\to\Vertl(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n})
in distribution and, because the latter is constant in
\Masz_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, also in probability.

We apply Lemma
22
. Let
f\in\LipB(\mathbb{R})
, then

\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n})]\biggr\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})\biggr\rvert+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n})]\biggr\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\lvert f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})\bigr\rvert+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n})]\biggr\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}\lvert C_{m}-1\rvert\,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n})]\biggr\rvert;\end{aligned}

the last line converges a.s. to zero because the sums obey the SLLN, and thus also in probability.

Essentially the same argument is valid for
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{m^{1/p}\,X_{i}}
. Write
m^{1/p}\,X_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i}
with
C_{m}
as before, and let
f\in\LipB(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where the Lipschitz constant is taken with respect to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{q}
, then

\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{1})]\biggr\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})\biggr\rvert\\
&\quad+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{1})]\biggr\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\lvert f(C_{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})\bigr\rvert+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{1})]\biggr\rvert\\
&\leq\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}\lvert C_{m}-1\rvert\,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{i})-\Erw[f(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{1/n}\Theta_{1})]\biggr\rvert;\end{aligned}

again the sums obey the SLLN and hence the desired convergence is implied.

Case
p=\infty
:
Notice that by the stochastic representation we are dealing with independent random variables and thus the convergence is immediate. ∎

Proof of Theorem
B
.

(a)

Recall

(m^{1/p}\,n^{1/q}\,X_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}=(m^{1/p}R_{i}\cdot n^{1/q}\Theta_{i,j})_{i\leq m,j\leq n}.

Lemma
20
, (b) and (c), imply the convergence in distribution of
(m^{1/p}\,n^{1/q}\,X_{i,j})_{i\leq k,j\leq l}
as claimed, where the joint convergence of
(R_{i})_{i\leq m}
,
\Theta_{1}
,…,
\Theta_{m}
may be argued either by their independence or by Slutsky’s theorem.

(b)

Case
p<\infty
:
Write
C_{m,n}:=U^{1/(mn)}\bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p/n}\bigr)^{-1/p}
and
D_{i,n}:=\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{1/n}(n^{-1/q}\lVert(\eta_{i,j})_{j\leq n}\rVert_{q})^{-1}
, then

(C_{m,n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1\quad\text{and}\quad(D_{i,n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1.

Now take any
f\in\LipB(\mathbb{R})
and consider

\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\biggr\rvert;

we have to show that the probability of this expression being smaller than any positive number converges to one. So let
\varepsilon>0
. Define
B_{m,n,\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chF_{[\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]}
, then
B_{m,n,\varepsilon}
is binomially distributed with parameters
m
and
\Wsk[\lvert D_{1,n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]
, and there holds
(\frac{1}{m}\,B_{m,n,\varepsilon})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
: indeed, let
\delta>0
, then

\displaystyle\Wsk\Bigl[\Bigl\lvert\frac{1}{m}B_{m,n,\varepsilon}\Bigr\rvert\geq\delta\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}\,\delta^{2}}\Var[B_{m,n,\varepsilon}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m\,\delta^{2}}\Wsk[\lvert D_{1,n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]\Wsk[\lvert D_{1,n}-1\lvert<\varepsilon],

and because of
(D_{1,n})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1
the latter converges to zero as
n\to\infty
, irrespective of whether
m
is fixed or diverges. We also have the laws of large numbers
\bigl(\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]
and
\bigl(\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lvert\eta_{i,j}\rvert\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}M_{q}^{1}
. Now there exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
n\geq n_{0}
, the probability of the event that
\lvert C_{m,n}-1\rvert\leq\varepsilon
and
2\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\frac{1}{m}B_{m,n,\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon
and
\bigl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\bigr\rvert\leq\varepsilon
and
\bigl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lvert\eta_{i,j}\rvert-M_{q}^{1}\bigr\rvert\leq\varepsilon
all hold true is at least, say,
1-\varepsilon
. Let
n\geq n_{0}
, then on the same event, for any
i\in[1,m]
such that
\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert<\varepsilon
, we have

\lvert C_{m,n}D_{i,n}-1\rvert\leq\lvert C_{m,n}\rvert\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert+\lvert C_{m,n}-1\rvert\leq(1+\varepsilon)\varepsilon+\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon,

and therewith

\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\biggr\rvert\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})\biggr\rvert\\
&\quad+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\biggr\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl\lvert f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-f(\eta_{i,j})\bigr\rvert+\varepsilon\\
&=\varepsilon+\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chF_{[\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert<\varepsilon]}\bigl\lvert f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-f(\eta_{i,j})\bigr\rvert\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chF_{[\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]}\bigl\lvert f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-f(\eta_{i,j})\bigr\rvert\\
&\leq\varepsilon+\frac{\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chF_{[\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert<\varepsilon]}\lvert C_{m,n}D_{i,n}-1\rvert\lvert\eta_{i,j}\rvert\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\chF_{[\lvert D_{i,n}-1\rvert\geq\varepsilon]}\bigl(\lvert f(C_{m,n}D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})\rvert+\lvert f(\eta_{i,j})\rvert\bigr)\\
&\leq\varepsilon+\frac{\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}}{mn}(\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lvert\eta_{i,j}\rvert+\frac{2\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}B_{m,n,\varepsilon}}{m}\\
&\leq\varepsilon+\lvert f\rvert_{\Lip}(\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon)(M_{q}^{1}+\varepsilon)+\varepsilon.\end{aligned}

Because this estimate holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
, convergence in probability is established.

Case
p=\infty
:
Again let
f\in\LipB(\mathbb{R})
, then with the same notation and techniques as before,

\displaystyle\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+\biggl\lvert\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(\eta_{i,j})-\Erw[f(\eta_{1,1})]\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigl\lvert f(D_{i,n}\eta_{i,j})-f(\eta_{i,j})\bigr\rvert+o(1).

The remaining argument is the same as in the case
p<\infty
, only formally
C_{m,n}=1
throughout. ∎

Proof of Theorem

Case
p_{1}<\infty

\lVert X^{m}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}U^{1/(mn)}\,\frac{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}\lVert\Theta_{i}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{1}/n}\bigr)^{1/p_{1}}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{2p_{2}/q_{2}}}{n^{2p_{2}(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})}}\,\frac{\Var[Y_{n}]}{n}=\frac{p_{2}^{2}\sigma^{2}(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{2p_{2}/q_{2}}}{n^{2p_{2}(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})+1}}\bigl(1+\smallO(1)\bigr);

these lead to

\Var\biggl[\frac{Z_{n}^{3}}{m}\biggr]=\frac{1}{m}\,\frac{\Var[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]}{\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]^{2}}=\frac{p_{2}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{mn}\bigl(1+\smallO(1)\bigr),

and therewith also to

\Var\biggl[\frac{Z_{n}^{4}}{m}\biggr]=\frac{1}{m}\,\frac{\Var[\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}]\Var[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{2}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]^{2}}=\BigTheta\Bigl(\frac{1}{mn^{2}}\Bigr).

We further note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n,i}^{3}\rvert^{3}]

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]^{3}}\Erw\biggl[\frac{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{3p_{2}/q_{2}}}{n^{3p_{2}(1/q_{1}-1/q_{2})}}\,\biggl\lvert\frac{Y_{n}-\Erw[Y_{n}]}{\sqrt{n}}\biggr\rvert^{3}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n^{3/2}}p_{2}^{3}\sigma^{3}\Erw[\lvert N\rvert^{3}]\bigl(1+\smallO(1)\bigr).

But then the array
((\Xi_{n,i}^{3})_{i\leq m})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies Lyapunov’s condition (
16
) with
\delta=1
since

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Erw[\lvert\Xi_{n,i}^{3}\rvert^{3}]}{\Var[Z_{n}^{3}]^{3/2}}=\frac{\BigTheta(mn^{-3/2})}{\BigTheta(m^{3/2}n^{-3/2})}=\BigTheta\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigr),

and hence we get the CLT
(\Var[Z_{n}^{3}]^{-1/2}Z_{n}^{3})_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}N
, or rather

\biggl(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{3}\biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}p_{2}\sigma N.

On the other hand there is still
\Var\bigl[\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{4}\bigr]=\BigTheta(\frac{1}{n})
, and a little bit less obviously (employ Lemma
15
again; alternatively, Lemma
16
with weaker asymptotics),

\Var\biggl[\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)\biggr]=n\biggl(\frac{V_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}{(p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{2}}-\frac{2C_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{1}/n,p_{2}/n}}{p_{1}p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}+\frac{V_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{1}/n}}{p_{1}^{2}}\biggr)=\frac{(p_{2}-p_{1})^{2}}{2p_{1}^{2}\,n}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)

wherefore by Čebyšëv’s inequality both
\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\bigr)
and
\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{4}
converge to zero in probability. For the remainder term we consider

(mn)^{1/4}\biggl(\frac{\log(U)}{mn},\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{3}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{4}}{m}\biggr)=\biggl(\frac{\log(U)}{(mn)^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{1}}{m^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{2}}{m^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{3}}{m^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{4}}{m^{3/4}}\biggr).

Again
((mn)^{-3/4}\log(U))_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}0
is obvious. For
k\in\{1,2,3\}
we have
\Var[m^{-3/4}n^{1/4}Z_{n}^{k}]=\BigTheta((mn)^{-1/2})
, and also
\Var[m^{-3/4}n^{1/4}Z_{n}^{4}]=\BigTheta(m^{-1/2}n^{-3/2})
; hence the respective components converge to zero in probability via Čebyšëv’s inequality. This establishes that the conditions of the remainder lemma are met, and finally the remainder term converges to zero in probability.

Case
p_{1}=\infty
:
There is no need to iterate the argument in its entirety; for one, omit
U
and
\Xi_{n}^{1}
from the previous case, and for another, we have different asymptotics for the variances; to wit, referring to Lemma
15
, 1.(b),

\Var\biggl[\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\biggr]=\frac{1}{m}\,\frac{V_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}{(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{2}}=\frac{\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}}\bigl(1+\BigO\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\bigr)\bigr)}{m\bigl(1+\BigO\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\bigr)^{2}}=\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{mn^{2}}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr),

and correspondingly,

\Var\biggl[\frac{Z_{n}^{4}}{m}\biggr]=\frac{p_{2}^{4}\sigma^{2}}{mn^{3}}\bigl(1+\smallO(1)\bigr).

These imply
\Var\bigl[\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{2}\bigr]=\BigTheta(\frac{1}{n})
and
\Var\bigl[\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{4}\bigr]=\BigTheta(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
, hence
\bigl(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{2}\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
and
\bigl(\sqrt{\frac{n}{m}}\,Z_{n}^{4}\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
via Čebyšëv’s inequality. For the remainder term we consider

(mn)^{1/4}\biggl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{3}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{4}}{m}\biggr)=\biggl(\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{2}}{m^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{3}}{m^{3/4}},\frac{n^{1/4}\,Z_{n}^{4}}{m^{3/4}}\biggr).

We have
\Var[m^{-3/4}n^{1/4}Z_{n}^{2}]=\BigTheta(m^{-1/2}n^{-3/2})
and also
\Var[m^{-3/4}n^{1/4}Z_{n}^{4}]=\BigTheta(m^{-1/2}n^{-5/2})
, and
\Var[m^{-3/4}n^{1/4}Z_{n}^{3}]=\BigTheta((mn)^{-1/2})
remains unchanged; hence the respective components converge to zero in probability via Čebyšëv’s inequality. The remainder lemma does the rest.

(b)

Define
\Xi_{n,i}^{1}
,
\Xi_{n,i}^{2}
,
Z_{n}^{1}
, and
Z_{n}^{2}
as in (a), then we have the probabilistic representation

\displaystyle\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}U^{1/(mn)}\,\frac{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{1}/n}\bigr)^{1/p_{1}}}

\displaystyle=U^{1/(mn)}\,\frac{\bigl(mM_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}+M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}Z_{n}^{2}\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}{(m+Z_{n}^{1})^{1/p_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}\,U^{1/(mn)}\,\frac{\bigl(1+\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(1+\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m}\bigr)^{1/p_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}\biggl(1+\frac{\log(U)}{mn}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}m}+\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}m}+R\Bigl(\frac{\log(U)}{mn},\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\Bigr)\biggr),

where we have used the same Taylor expansion as in (a), but evaluated at
z_{3}=z_{4}=0
, and the remainder term satsifies
\lvert R(u,z_{1},z_{2})\rvert\leq M\lVert(u,z_{1},z_{2})\rVert_{2}^{2}
with some
M>0
in a neighbourhood of
(0,0,0)
. Rearranging yields

\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}\,\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}-1\biggr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{\log(U)}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{n}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)+\sqrt{m}\,nR\Bigl(\frac{\log(U)}{mn},\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\Bigr).

We know
(m^{-1/2}\log(U))_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}0
; in order to apply the remainder lemma we have to ensure

m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\Bigl(\frac{\log(U)}{mn},\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}(0,0,0),

but we have
m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\frac{\log(U)}{mn}=m^{-3/4}n^{-1/2}\log(U)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0
, and with reference to the proof of (a),
\Var\bigl[m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\frac{Z_{n}^{k}}{m}\bigr]=m^{1/2}n\BigTheta(\frac{1}{mn})=\BigTheta(m^{-1/2})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0
for
k\in\{1,2\}
, hence via Čebyšëv’s inequality
m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{m},\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}(0,0)
.

It remains to show that
\frac{n}{\sqrt{m}}\bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\bigr)
converges to the claimed distribution. Written out, the term in parentheses reads

\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\biggl(\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}{p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}-\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{1}/n}-1}{p_{1}}\biggr);

call
Z_{n,i}:=\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}{p_{2}M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}}-\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{1}/n}-1}{p_{1}}
, then
\Erw[Z_{n,i}]=0
, and we are going to demonstate that the array
((Z_{n,i})_{i\leq m})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies Lyapunov’s condition. Let
\delta>0
, then from Lemma
16
we get, for any
i\in[1,m]
,

\displaystyle\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n,i}\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr]=n^{-2-\delta}\Erw\bigl[\lvert nZ_{n,i}\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr]=n^{-2-\delta}\Bigl\lvert\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{2p_{1}}\Bigr\rvert^{2+\delta}\Erw\bigl[\lvert N^{2}-1\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr](1+\smallO(1)),

and as we have established already in the proof of (a),

s_{n}^{2}:=\Var\biggl[\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{n,i}\biggr]=\frac{m(p_{2}-p_{1})^{2}}{2p_{1}^{2}n^{2}}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr).

These now show

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2+\delta}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Erw\bigl[\lvert Z_{n,i}\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{mn^{-2-\delta}\Bigl\lvert\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{2p_{1}}\bigr\rvert^{2+\delta}\Erw\bigl[\lvert N^{2}-1\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr](1+\smallO(1))}{m^{1+\delta/2}n^{-2-\delta}\bigl\lvert\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{\sqrt{2}\,p_{1}}\bigr\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigl(1+\BigO\bigl(\frac{1}{n}\bigr)\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{\delta/2}}\,\frac{\Erw\bigl[\lvert N^{2}-1\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr]}{2^{1+\delta/2}}\,(1+\smallO(1))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

that is, Lyapunov’s condition is satisfied, and hence we have the CLT

\frac{1}{s_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{n,i}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,p_{1}}{\lvert p_{2}-p_{1}\rvert}\,\frac{n}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}N\sim\Nvert(0,1),

equivalently,

\frac{n}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}}-\frac{Z_{n}^{1}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{d}}\frac{\lvert p_{2}-p_{1}\rvert}{\sqrt{2}\,p_{1}}\,N,

which concludes this part’s proof.

(c)

Keeping
Z_{n}^{2}
as before, we have

\displaystyle\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}\biggr)^{1/p_{2}}

\displaystyle=m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\biggr)^{1/p_{2}}

\displaystyle=m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}\biggl(1+\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{p_{2}m}+R\biggl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\biggr)\biggr),

equivalently

\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}-1\biggr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\frac{n}{p_{2}\sqrt{m}}\,Z_{n}^{2}+\sqrt{m}\,nR\biggl(\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\biggr),

where the remainder fulfils
\lvert R(x)\rvert\leq Mx^{2}
in some neighbourhood of zero. Like in the proof of (a), case
p_{1}=\infty
, we use Lemma
15
, 1.(b), to see

\Var\biggl[m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\,\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\biggr]=\sqrt{m}\,n\,\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{mn^{2}}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)=\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{\sqrt{m}\,n}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr),

and via Čebyšëv’s inequality this immediately implies
\bigl(m^{1/4}n^{1/2}\frac{Z_{n}^{2}}{m}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0
, and thus by the remainder lemma the remainder term converges to zero.

Since
Z_{n}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}{M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}
, we are going to prove that the triangular array
\bigl(\bigl(\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}{M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}\bigr)_{i\leq m}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}
satisfies Lyapunov’s condition with any
\delta\in(0,\infty)
(the components are centred already); so let
\delta\in(0,\infty)
. Employing Lemma
15
, 1.(b), again we have, for any
i\in[1,m]
,

\Erw\biggl[\biggl\lvert\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}{M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}\biggr\rvert^{2+\delta}\biggr]=\frac{\bigl(\frac{p_{2}}{n}\bigr)^{2+\delta}\Erw[\lvert E-1\rvert^{2+\delta}](1+\smallO(1))}{(1+\smallO(1))^{2+\delta}}=\frac{p_{2}^{2+\delta}\Erw[\lvert E-1\rvert^{2+\delta}]}{n^{2+\delta}}(1+\smallO(1)),

so together with the by now well
-
known
\Var[Z_{n}^{2}]=\frac{mp_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{1}{n})\bigr)
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Erw\bigl[\bigl\lvert\frac{\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{p_{2}/n}-M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}{M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n}}\bigr\rvert^{2+\delta}\bigr]}{\Var[Z_{n}^{2}]^{1+\delta/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{mn^{-2-\delta}p_{2}^{2+\delta}\Erw[\lvert E-1\rvert^{2+\delta}](1+\smallO(1))}{m^{1+\delta/2}n^{-2-\delta}p_{2}^{2+\delta}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{1}{n})\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Erw[\lvert E-1\rvert^{2+\delta}]}{m^{\delta/2}}\,(1+\smallO(1))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

So this shows
\bigl(\Var[Z_{n}^{2}]^{-1/2}\,Z_{n}^{1}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}=\bigl(\frac{n}{p_{2}\sqrt{m}}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{1}{n})\bigr)Z_{n}^{2}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}N\sim\Nvert(0,1)
, and finally

\biggl(\frac{n}{p_{2}\sqrt{m}}\,Z_{n}^{2}\biggr)_{n\geq 1}\xrightarrow{\text{d}}N,

and through an application of Slutsky’s theorem the proof is finished. ∎

4.2
Proofs of the corollaries

The key observation is the following: let
X\sim\Unif(\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
, then
(r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,X\sim\Unif((r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})
, and therewith, for any
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\vol_{m,n}\bigl((r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,\mathbb{B}_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}\cap t(r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,\mathbb{B}_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\bigl[(r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,X\in t(r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n})^{-1}\,\mathbb{B}_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}\bigr]=\Wsk\biggl[\lVert X\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}\leq\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}\,t\biggr].

(17)

Proof of Corollary
4
.

We use Theorem
C
, thus, continuing from Equation (
17
),

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[\sqrt{m}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigl)^{1/p_{2}}}\lVert X^{m}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}-1\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq\sqrt{m}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigl)^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}\,t-1\biggr)\biggr].

The last random variable converges in distribution to a centred nondegenerate normally distributed random variable
N
, hence we must determine the limit of the right-hand-side. Recall the definition of the radii
r_{p_{i},q_{i}}^{m,n}
at the beginning of Section
1.4
; expanding the gamma
-
functions in the volumes
\vol_{mn}(\mathbb{B}_{p_{i},q_{i}}^{m,n})
(which see Equation (
5
)) using Stirling’s formula we arrive at

\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}=\begin{cases}A_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{1}{m})\bigr)&\text{if }p_{1}<\infty,\\
A_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{\log(m)}{m})\bigr)&\text{if }p_{1}=\infty.\end{cases}

(18)

This implies

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}=A_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n},

and therewith the cases
tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}<1
or
tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}>1
are immediately accounted for. In the threshold case
tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}=1
we need the information in Equation (
18
) that the correction
-
term is of order
\smallO(m^{-1/2})
in either case; this yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{m}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{\bigl(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n}\Erw[\lVert\Theta_{1}\rVert_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}]\bigr)^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}\,t-1\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m}\Bigl(tA_{p_{1},q_{1};p_{2},q_{2};n}\Bigl(1+\smallO\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigr)\Bigr)-1\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m}\smallO\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Bigr)=\smallO(1),

and finally

\lim_{m\to\infty}V^{m,n}(t)=\Wsk[N\leq 0]=\frac{1}{2}.\qed

Proof of Corollary
5
.

Case
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
:
Here we invest Theorem
D
, that is,

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[\sqrt{n}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{2}}-1\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sqrt{n}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}\,t-1\biggr)\biggr].

Like in the proof of Corollary
4
we use asymptotic expansion; to wit, this reads

\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}=\begin{cases}A_{q_{1},q_{2}}\bigl(1+\BigO(\frac{1}{n})\bigr)&\text{if }q_{1}<\infty,\\
A_{q_{1},q_{2}}\bigl(1+\BigO\bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\bigr)\bigr)&\text{if }q_{1}=\infty.\end{cases}

(19)

On the one hand this yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}=A_{q_{1},q_{2}},

and therefore the result is immediate for
tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}<1
or
tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}>1
. On the other hand, for the threshold
-
case
tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}=1
Equation (
19
) tells us that either way we are off by at most
\smallO(n^{-1/2})
. Thus

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}\,n^{1/q_{1}-1/q_{2}}}{(M_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}})^{1/q_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{2}}^{m,n}}\,t-1\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\Bigl(tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}\Bigl(1+\smallO\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)\Bigr)-1\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}\smallO\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr)=\smallO(1),

and the conclusion follows as in the proof of Corollary
4
.

Case
q_{1}=q_{2}
and
p_{1}<\infty
:
The starting point is similar to the above,

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[mn\biggl(1-\frac{m^{1/p_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}\biggr)\geq mn\biggl(1-\frac{m^{1/p_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}\,t\biggr)\biggr].

The asymptotic reads

\frac{m^{1/p_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}=1-\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2mn}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr);

(20)

this implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{m^{1/p_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}=1=A_{q_{1},q_{1}},

and again the result follows easily for
tA_{q_{1},q_{1}}=t<1
or
t>1
. In the threshold case
t=1
there follows from Equation (
20
),

mn\biggl(1-\frac{m^{1/p_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}\,t\biggr)=\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr),

and therefore

\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{m,n}(1)=\Wsk\biggl[E+\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2p_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}\biggr].

For
m=1
this simplifies to

\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{1,n}(1)=\Wsk[E\geq 0]=1.

In the case
m\geq 2
note that
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2}\sim\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2},2)
(the chi
-
squared distribution with
m-1
degrees of freedom). Because
E
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2}
are independent we can compute the given probability explicitly as follows, where we have to distinguish whether
p_{1}<p_{2}
or
p_{1}>p_{2}
. Here we treat only the former in detail; the latter is done analogously. First we have

\Wsk\biggl[E+\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2p_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}\biggr]\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{2^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}\mathsf{e}^{-x/2}\int_{\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}-\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})x}{2p_{1}}}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{-y}\chF_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x;

then for any
x>0
we have
\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}-\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})x}{2p_{1}}\geq 0
iff
x\geq\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}
, and since
p_{1}<p_{2}
this last expression is positive. Hence for
x<\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}
the inner integral is
1
, and else it is

\int_{\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}-\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})x}{2p_{1}}}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{-y}\chF_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}+\frac{(p_{1}-p_{2})x}{2p_{1}}}=\Bigl(\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)^{(m-1)/2}\,\mathsf{e}^{\frac{x}{2}-\frac{p_{2}x}{2p_{1}}}.

So by splitting up the outer integral we reach

\displaystyle\Wsk\biggl[E+\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{2p_{1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}N_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{2^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}\mathsf{e}^{-x/2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{2^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}\mathsf{e}^{-x/2}\Bigl(\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)^{(m-1)/2}\,\mathsf{e}^{\frac{x}{2}-\frac{p_{2}x}{2p_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x;

now the first summand obviously equals

\int_{0}^{\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{2^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}\mathsf{e}^{-x/2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{m-1}{2},2\Bigr)\Biggl(\biggl(0,\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}\biggr]\Biggr),

and the second summand evaluates to

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{2^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}

\displaystyle\mathsf{e}^{-x/2}\Bigl(\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}\Bigr)^{(m-1)/2}\,\mathsf{e}^{\frac{x}{2}-\frac{p_{2}x}{2p_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(p_{1}/p_{2})}{p_{1}-p_{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{(m-3)/2}}{\bigl(\frac{2p_{1}}{p_{2}}\bigr)^{(m-1)/2}\Gamma(\frac{m-1}{2})}\mathsf{e}^{-\frac{x}{2p_{1}/p_{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{m-1}{2},\frac{2p_{1}}{p_{2}}\Bigr)\Biggl(\biggl(\frac{p_{1}(m-1)\log(\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}})}{p_{1}-p_{2}},\infty\biggr)\Biggr),

and because of
\min\{1,\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\}=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}
,
\max\{1,\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\}=1
this equals the claimed expression.

Case
q_{1}=q_{2}
and
p_{1}=\infty
:
Now we are working with

V^{m,n}(t)=\Wsk\biggl[mn(1-m^{-1/p_{2}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}})\geq mn\biggl(1-\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{m^{1/p_{2}}\,r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}\,t\biggr)\biggr].

This time we have

\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{m^{1/p_{2}}\,r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}=1-\frac{(m-1)\log(n)}{2mn}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\Bigr),

(21)

whence we see

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{m^{1/p_{2}}\,r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}=1=A_{q_{1},q_{1}},

and the noncritical cases follow as before. For the threshold
-
value
t=1
, Equation (
21
) results in

mn\biggl(1-\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{m^{1/p_{2}}\,r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}\biggr)=\frac{(m-1)\log(n)}{2}+\BigO(1),

which leads us to

\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{m,n}(1)=\Wsk\biggl[\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}\geq\infty\biggr]=0.\qed

Case
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
:
Referring to Theorem

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

and from these follow the limit values for the cases

and
tA_{q_{1},q_{2}}>1
. In the remaining case

Case
q_{1}=q_{2}

Again we rewrite

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{m^{1/p_{1}-1/p_{2}}}{(M_{p_{1}/n}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}-1\biggr)

and since the logarithm is strictly concave and

hence the limit as

\displaystyle V^{m,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Wsk\biggl[\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{\lVert X^{n}\rVert_{p_{2},q_{1}}}{m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}-1\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\frac{1}{m^{1/p_{2}}(M_{\infty}^{p_{2}/n})^{1/p_{2}}}\,\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}\,t-1\biggr)\biggr].

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\infty,q_{1}}^{m,n}}{m^{1/p_{2}}\,r_{p_{2},q_{1}}^{m,n}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\log(2\pi n/p_{2})}{2n}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi mn/p_{2})}{2mn}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(mn)^{2}}{m^{2}n^{2}}\Bigr)

\begin{aligned} &=\sqrt{m}\,n\biggl(\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{n}+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{\log(2\pi n/p_{2})}{2n}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi mn/p_{2})}{2mn}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(mn)^{2}}{m^{2}n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)-1\biggr)\\

&\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi mn/p_{2})}{2mn}\Bigl(1+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(n)}{n}\Bigr)\Bigr)+\BigO\Bigl(\frac{\log(mn)^{2}}{m^{2}n^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr)\\

and the conclusion easily follows. Notice that this result is consistent with the previous case

Appendix A
Appendix: Higher
-
order mixed
-
norm spaces

It is not difficult to generalize the idea of mixed norms to higher orders in the following sense. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{p}_{k}=(p_{j})_{j\leq k}\in(0,\infty]^{k}
and
\mathbf{n}_{k}=(n_{j})_{j\leq k}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, then on
\mathbb{R}^{\times\mathbf{n}_{k}}:=\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\dotsb\times n_{k}}
define the
k
th
-
order mixed norm with exponents
p_{1},\dotsc,p_{k}
recursively by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}:=\lVert\cdot\rVert_{p_{1}}
, and for
k\geq 2
,

\lVert(x_{i})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k}}\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}:=\bigl\lVert\bigl(\lVert(x_{i,i_{k}})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}}\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}\bigr)_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\bigr\rVert_{p_{k}}.

We call
\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}:=(\mathbb{R}^{\times\mathbf{n}_{k}},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{k}})
the (real)
finite
-
dimensional
k
th
-
order mixed
-
norm sequence space,
then we have the recursion
\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}=\ell_{p_{k}}^{n_{k}}(\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})
. Let
\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
be its closed unit ball, whose (
n_{1}\dotsm n_{k}
)
-
dimensional Lebesgue volume we denote by
\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
, and
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
its unit sphere with associated cone measure
\kappa_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
. Via the usual identification
(\mathbb{C},\lvert\cdot\rvert)\cong(\mathbb{R}^{2},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2})
we may also subsume complex mixed
-
norm spaces under the real ones, to wit

\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}(\mathbb{C})\cong\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}(\ell_{2}^{2})=\ell_{(2,\mathbf{p}_{k})}^{(2,\mathbf{n}_{k})}.

Notice that compared to the definition in Section
2.2
we have reversed the order of indices here in order to write the recursion in a more natural manner (append new indices at end, not at beginning), so what we have notated
\mathbb{B}_{p,q}^{m,n}
there, corresponds to
\mathbb{B}_{(q,p)}^{(n,m)}
here.

The recursive structure of
\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
lends itself well to calculate
\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}
in a recursive way as well. For the following assume
k\geq 2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times\mathbf{n}_{k}}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{n_{k}}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}}\bigl(((x_{i,i_{k}})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x.

On each of the
n_{k}
component spaces introduce
\ell_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}}
-
polar coordinates, i.e.,
(x_{i,i_{k}})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}}=r_{i_{k}}\theta_{i_{k}}
with
r_{i_{k}}\in[0,\infty)
and
\theta_{i_{k}}\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}}
for each
i_{k}\in[1,n_{k}]
; therewith we get

\lVert((x_{i,i_{k}})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}=\bigl\lVert\bigl(\lVert r_{i_{k}}\theta_{i_{k}}\rVert_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}\bigr)_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\bigr\rVert_{p_{k}}=\lVert(r_{i_{k}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\rVert_{p_{k}}

and hence
\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}}\bigl(((x_{i,i_{k}})_{i\in\times\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\bigr)=\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}}^{n_{k}}}((r_{i_{k}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}})
. This yields

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\bigl(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}}\bigr)^{n_{k}}\int_{[0,\infty)^{n_{k}}}\int_{(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{n_{k}}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}}^{n_{k}}}(r)\prod_{i_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}r_{i_{k}}^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}-1}\,\mathrm{d}(\kappa_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{\otimes n_{k}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=(\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{n_{k}}\int_{[0,\infty)^{n_{k}}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}}^{n_{k}}}(r)\prod_{i_{k}=1}^{n_{k}}\bigl(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}r_{i_{k}}^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}-1}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Now transform
s_{i_{k}}:=r_{i_{k}}^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}}
for each
i_{k}\in[1,n_{k}]
; recall
\lVert(x_{i})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p}=\lVert(\lvert x_{i}\rvert^{\alpha})_{i\leq n}\rVert_{p/\alpha}^{1/\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, which gives

\lVert r\rVert_{p_{k}}=\lVert(r_{i_{k}}^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1}})_{i_{k}\leq n_{k}}\rVert_{p_{k}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}^{1/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}=\lVert s\rVert_{p_{k}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}^{1/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})},

hence
\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}}^{n_{k}}}(r)=\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}^{n_{k}}}(s)
and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{n_{k}}\int_{[0,\infty)^{n_{k}}}\chF_{\mathbb{B}_{p_{k}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}^{n_{k}}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k-1}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k-1}})^{n_{k}}\,\omega_{p_{k}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{k-1})}^{n_{k}}}{2^{n_{k}}},

which is the desired recursive formula. Via induction on
k
this leads to the explicit formula

\omega_{\mathbf{p}_{k}}^{\mathbf{n}_{k}}=2^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(\omega_{p_{j}/(n_{1}\dotsm n_{j-1})}^{n_{j}})^{n_{j+1}\dotsm n_{k}}}{2^{n_{j}\dotsm n_{k}}}=2^{n_{1}\dotsm n_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n_{1}\dotsm n_{j-1}}{p_{j}}+1)^{n_{j}\dotsm n_{k}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n_{1}\dotsm n_{j}}{p_{j}}+1)^{n_{j+1}\dotsm n_{k}}}.

In the special case of
\ell_{(p,q)}^{(m,n)}(\mathbb{C})
this yields

\omega_{(2,p,q)}^{(2,m,n)}=2^{2mn}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+1)^{2mn}\,\Gamma(\frac{2}{p}+1)^{mn}\,\Gamma(\frac{2m}{q}+1)^{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{2}{2}+1)^{mn}\,\Gamma(\frac{2m}{p}+1)^{n}\,\Gamma(\frac{2mn}{q}+1)}=\pi^{mn}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2}{p}+1)^{mn}\,\Gamma(\frac{2m}{q}+1)^{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{2m}{p}+1)^{n}\,\Gamma(\frac{2mn}{q}+1)},

and this equals the value given in
[
15
, Theorem 5]
.

A connecting theorem for geodesic flows on the 2-torus
Stefan Klempnauer

Abstract
We use a result of J. Mather on the existence of connecting orbits for compositions of monotone twist maps of the cylinder to prove the existence of connecting geodesics on the unit tangent bundle S ​ T 2 ST^{2} of the 2-torus in regions without invariant tori.

1
Introduction and main result

Let
T^{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
denote the 2-torus with universal covering
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to T^{2}
. The tangent bundle
is given by
TT^{2}\cong T^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In the following we will recall the definition of a Finsler metric. For a general
overview of Finsler geometry see
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
Finsler metric
on
T^{2}
is a map

F:TT^{2}\to[0,\infty)

with the following properties

1.

(Regularity)
F
is
C^{\infty}
on
TT^{2}-0

2.

(Positive homogeneity)
F(x,\lambda y)=\lambda\cdot F(x,y)
for
\lambda>0

3.

(Strong convexity) The hessian

(g_{ij}(x,v))=\left(\partial_{v_{i}v_{j}}\frac{1}{2}F(x,v)^{2}\right)

is positive-definite for every
(x,v)\in TT^{2}-0
.

A Finsler metric is called
reversible
if
F(x,v)=F(x,-v)
for every
(x,v)\in TT^{2}
. The

unit tangent bundle

ST^{2}\cong T^{2}\times S^{1}
is given by
ST^{2}=F^{-1}(\{1\})
.

We define the
length

l_{F}(c)
of a piecewise differentiable curve
c:[a,b]\to T^{2}
via

l_{F}(c)=\int_{a}^{b}F(\dot{c}(t))dt

Lifting the Finsler metric
F
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
allows us to define the length of a curve
c:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

in the universal covering in the same manner.
The
geodesic flow

\phi^{t}:ST^{2}\to ST^{2}
is the restriction of the Euler-Lagrange flow of the
Lagrangian
L_{F}
, with

L_{F}=\frac{1}{2}F^{2}

to the unit tangent bundle. A
geodesic
we call either a trajectory of the Euler-Lagrange flow, or
its projection to
T^{2}
, i.e. a geodesic is a curve
t\mapsto c(t)\subset T^{2}
satisfying the Euler-Lagrange equation

\partial_{x}L_{F}(c,\dot{c})-\partial_{t}(\partial_{v}L_{F}(c,\dot{c}))=0

In general we will assume a geodesic
c
to be parametrized by arclength, i.e.
\dot{c}\subset ST^{2}
.
Sometimes it can be convenient to consider lifts
\widetilde{c}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\dot{\widetilde{c}}:\mathbb{R}\to T\mathbb{R}^{2}

of a geodesic to the universal cover. These can be seen as
the geodesics of the lifted
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic Finsler metric
\widetilde{F}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A subset
\Lambda\subset ST^{2}
is called an
invariant torus
if
\Lambda
is the graph
of a continuous map
X:T^{2}\to S^{1}
and
\phi^{t}
-invariant.
An invariant torus (or more generally any
\phi^{t}
-invariant set)
\Lambda
has
bounded direction

(with respect to
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}-\{0\}
), if the lifts
\tilde{c}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of geodesics in
\Lambda
are graphs over
the euclidean line
\mathbb{R}v
.

In analogy to a Birkhoff region of instability we define the following notion of an instability region
of the geodesic flow.

Definition 1.3
.

An
instability region
is a compact invariant subset
U\subset ST^{2}
with boundary being
the disjoint union of two invariant tori
\Lambda_{-},\Lambda_{+}
, such that every invariant torus

\Lambda\subset U
is equal to
\Lambda_{-}
or
\Lambda_{+}
.

The main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
F
be a Finsler metric on
T^{2}
and let
U\subset ST^{2}
be an instability region with bounded direction
(with respect to
e_{1}
). Furthermore, we assume that there are no closed geodesics
in the boundary of
U
.
Then there exists an
F
-geodesic
c:\mathbb{R}\to T^{2}
that connects the two boundary components of
U
(i.e. there exist sequences
t_{n}\to\infty
and
s_{n}\to-\infty
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\dot{c}(t_{n})\in\Lambda_{+}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dot{c}(s_{n})\in\Lambda_{-}
, where
\Lambda_{-}
and
\Lambda_{+}
are the two boundary components of
U
).

Return maps of the geodesic flow on
T^{2}
and twist maps

2.1
Return maps as Lagrangian time-1 maps

Let
F
be a Finsler metric on the 2-torus
T^{2}
with
\widetilde{F}
denoting the lifted metric on the universal cover

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For a vector
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we will write
w^{\perp}
for the vector

w^{\perp}=(-w_{2},w_{1})
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a Finsler metric
F
and a prime element
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
we define a time-dependent Lagrangian
on
\mathbb{R}
via

\widetilde{L}(t,x,r)=\widetilde{F}(tv+xv^{\perp},v+rv^{\perp})

which is 1-periodic in the time
t
and
1-periodic in the first component
x
. The periodicity is a direct consequence of the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodicity
of
\widetilde{F}
. Since
\widetilde{L}
is periodic in
x
it can be interpreted as a time-periodic Lagrangian
L
on
S^{1}
.
Note that
\widetilde{L}
is strictly convex, i.e.
\partial_{rr}\widetilde{L}>0
because of the strict convexity of the
Finsler metric
\widetilde{F}
.

We have a correspondence of lifted geodesics, which are graphs over
v\mathbb{R}
and solutions of
L
in the
following theorem by J.P. Schröder. A special case can be found in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F
and
L
as above and let
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.
Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the curve given by

\gamma(t)=tv+\theta(t)v^{\perp}

Then
\gamma
is a reparametrization of an
\widetilde{F}
-geodesic if and only if
\theta
is an
Euler-Lagrange solution of
\widetilde{L}
.

Proof.

Observe that we have the following relation between the Lagrangian action
A_{\widetilde{L}}
and the Finsler length
l_{\widetilde{F}}
.

\displaystyle A_{\widetilde{L}}(\theta|_{[a,b]})

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\widetilde{L}(t,\theta(t),\theta^{\prime}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\widetilde{F}(tv+\theta(t)v^{\perp},v+\theta^{\prime}(t)v^{\perp})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))dt

\displaystyle=l_{\widetilde{F}}(\gamma|_{[a,b]})

Assume now that
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the reparametrization of an
\widetilde{F}
-geodesic,
i.e.
\partial_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\gamma_{s})=0
for any
proper variation of
\gamma
. Let
\theta_{s}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a proper variation of
\theta
.
From
\theta_{s}
we
construct a proper variation
\gamma_{s}
of
\gamma
via

\gamma_{s}(t)=tv+\theta_{s}(t)v^{\perp}

Then we have
A_{\widetilde{L}}(\theta_{s})=l_{\widetilde{F}}(\gamma_{s})
for every
s
, and hence we have

\partial_{s}|_{s=0}A_{\widetilde{L}}(\theta_{s})=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\gamma_{s})=0.

This proves one direction.
To prove the other direction assume now that
\theta:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is critical with respect to the Lagrangian action and
let
X:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a vector field along
\gamma
with
X(a)=X(b)=0
. Since

\dot{\gamma}(t)=v+\theta^{\prime}(t)v^{\perp}
the pair of vectors
\{\dot{\gamma}(t),v^{\perp}\}
always forms a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Thus, we can rewrite the vector field
X
as

X(t)=\underbrace{\lambda(t)\dot{\gamma}(t)}_{A(t)}+\underbrace{\mu(t)v^{\perp}}_{B(t)}

for functions
\lambda,\mu
with
\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=\mu(a)=\mu(b)=0
.
Let
\gamma_{s}
be a proper variation of
\gamma
corresponding to the variational vector field
X
and let
\beta_{s}

be a proper variation of
\gamma
corresponding to
B
, i.e.

\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\gamma_{s}(t)=X(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\beta_{s}(t)=B(t)

Observe that for small
|s|
the curve
\eta_{s}:t\mapsto\gamma(t+s\lambda(t))
is a reparametrization of
\gamma

and hence has length independent of
s
. Thus we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\eta_{s})

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\int_{a}^{b}\widetilde{F}(\eta_{s}(t),\partial_{t}\eta_{s}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\widetilde{F}(\eta_{s}(t),\partial_{t}\eta_{s}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\eta_{0}(t),\partial_{t}\eta_{0}(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\eta_{s}(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\eta_{0}(t),\partial_{t}\eta_{0}(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\partial_{t}\eta_{s}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\partial_{t}\gamma(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\eta_{s}(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\partial_{t}\gamma(t))\partial_{t}\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\eta_{s}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\partial_{t}\gamma(t))A(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\partial_{t}\gamma(t))\dot{A}(t)dt

Hence it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{s}|_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\gamma_{s})

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\int_{a}^{b}\widetilde{F}(\gamma_{s}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{s}(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma_{0}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{0}(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\gamma_{s}(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma_{0}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{0}(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\partial_{t}\gamma_{s}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\gamma_{s}(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\partial_{t}\partial_{s}|_{s=0}\gamma_{s}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))X(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\dot{X}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))(A(t)+B(t))+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))(\dot{A}(t)+\dot{B}(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\partial_{1}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))B(t)+\partial_{2}\widetilde{F}(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\dot{B}(t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}|_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\beta_{s})

Consequently, the curve
\gamma
is critical with respect to the Finsler length if

\partial_{s}|_{s=0}l_{\widetilde{F}}(\beta_{s})=0

for every proper variation
\beta_{s}
, which varies
\gamma
only in
v^{\perp}
direction, i.e.
\beta_{s}
is of the form

\beta_{s}(t)=tv+\theta_{s}(t)v^{\perp}

For those variations we have already computed that if
\theta_{s}
is critical with respect to the
\widetilde{L}
-action then

\beta_{s}
is critical with respect to the Finsler length.

∎

Remember that
T\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
To establish a connection between return maps of the geodesic flow and the time-1 map
of the Lagrangian
\widetilde{L}
we define the following sets

\widetilde{V}=\{(x,w)\in T\mathbb{R}^{2}:\langle w,v\rangle>0,\widetilde{F}(w)=1\}

with sections
\widetilde{V}_{i}
, where
i\in\mathbb{Z}

\widetilde{V}_{i}=\{(iv+bv^{\perp},w)\in T\mathbb{R}^{2}:\langle w,v\rangle>0,\widetilde{F}(w)=1,b\in\mathbb{R}\}

In general the Euler-Lagrange solutions of
\widetilde{L}
will not exist for all times. However, because
of theorem
2.2
we have the following

Proposition 2.3
.

If the Euler-Lagrange flow of
\widetilde{L}
is complete then the set
\widetilde{V}
is invariant and every orbit
starting in a section
\widetilde{V}_{i}
will consecutively pass through the sections
\widetilde{V}_{i+j}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Completeness of the Euler-Lagrange flow means that the Euler-Lagrange solutions of
\widetilde{L}
exist
for all times. Let
(x,w)\in\widetilde{V}
.
We write
(x,w)=(av+bv^{\perp},rv+hv^{\perp})
for coefficients
a,b,h,r\in\mathbb{R}
with
r>0
. Let

\widetilde{c}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the geodesic with
\widetilde{c}(a)=x
and
\dot{\widetilde{c}}(a)=\tfrac{1}{r}w
. To show
that
\widetilde{V}
is invariant we have to show that
\langle\dot{\widetilde{c}}(t),v\rangle>0
for every

t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Euler-Lagrange solution of
\widetilde{L}
with

\theta(a)=b
and
\theta^{\prime}(a)=\tfrac{h}{r}
. Since
\theta
exists for all times it follows from
Theorem
2.2
that the curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

\gamma(t)=tv+\theta(t)v^{\perp}
is a reparametrization of a geodesic.
We have
\gamma(a)=av+bv^{\perp}=x
and
\dot{\gamma}(a)=v+\tfrac{h}{r}v^{\perp}=\tfrac{1}{r}w
and hence
\gamma
is a reparametrization of the geodesic
\widetilde{c}
.
Since
\langle\dot{\gamma}(t),v\rangle=\|v\|^{2}
for every

\langle\dot{\widetilde{c}}(t),v\rangle>0
for every
t
.
With the same construction one sees that if
(x,w)
was chosen in
\widetilde{V}_{i}
the constructed
reparametrization
\gamma
will pass through
\widetilde{V}_{i+j}
at times
i+j
.
∎

By
\widetilde{R}_{0}:\widetilde{V}_{0}\to\widetilde{V}_{1}
we denote the map that maps an element
(x,w)\in\widetilde{V}_{0}

to the point of intersection of
\widetilde{V}_{1}
with the orbit passing through
(x,w)
. We define the projected
section
V\subset ST^{2}
via

V:=\Pi(V_{0})

Since the geodesic
flow on
T\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-invariant, the map
\widetilde{R}_{0}
descends to a map

R:V\to V
, which is the
n
-th return map of the section
V
, with respect to the geodesic flow on
ST^{2}
. Here,
n-1
is equal
to
\|v\|^{2}-1
(

is equal to the number of deck-transformations of
\mathbb{R}v^{\perp}
, that lie
in between
\mathbb{R}v^{\perp}
and
v+\mathbb{R}v^{\perp}
, which corresponds to the number of integer points

v

v

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

equal to
\|v\|^{2}-1

Proposition 2.4
.

If the Euler-Lagrange flow of
\widetilde{L}
is complete
the return map
R
is conjugated to the time-1 map
\varphi_{L}^{0,1}:Z\to Z
of the
Lagrangian
L
.

Proof.

Let
(x,w)\in\widetilde{V}_{0}
. We can rewrite
(x,w)
as

(x,w)=\left(\frac{\langle x,v^{\perp}\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}v^{\perp},\frac{\langle w,v\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}v+\frac{\langle w,v^{\perp}\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}v^{\perp}\right)

Define real numbers
b,h\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
, such that

(x,w)=(bv^{\perp},rv+hv^{\perp})
.
Since the Euler-Lagrange flow is complete there
exists an Euler-Lagrange solution
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of
\widetilde{L}
with

\theta(0)=b\quad\text{and}\quad\theta^{\prime}(0)=\frac{h}{r}

From theorem
2.2
it follows that there exists a reparametrization

\gamma(t)=tv+\theta(t)v^{\perp}

of an
\widetilde{F}
-geodesic. It follows then that

\widetilde{R}_{0}(x,w)=\left(\gamma(1),\frac{\dot{\gamma}(1)}{\widetilde{F}(\gamma(1),\dot{\gamma}(1))}\right)

We define the scaled sections
\widetilde{W}_{i}\subset T\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
via

\widetilde{W}_{0}=\{(bv^{\perp},v+dv^{\perp})\in T\mathbb{R}^{2}:b,d\in\mathbb{R}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{W}_{i}=iv+\widetilde{W}_{0}

together with a map
\widetilde{g}:T\mathbb{R}^{2}-0\to T\mathbb{R}^{2}-0
with

\widetilde{g}(x,w)=\left(x,\frac{w}{\widetilde{F}(x,w)}\right)

Observe that the restrictions
\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{i}}:\widetilde{W}_{i}\to\widetilde{V}_{i}

are diffeomorphisms (check that
(x,w)\mapsto(x,\tfrac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle v,w\rangle}w)
is the inverse
of
\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{i}}
and both are differentiable) and that

\left(\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{1}}\right)^{-1}\circ\widetilde{R}_{0}\circ\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{0}}=\widetilde{P}_{0}

for the map
\widetilde{P}_{0}:\widetilde{W}_{0}\to\widetilde{W}_{1}
with

\widetilde{P}_{0}(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))=(\gamma(1),\dot{\gamma}(1))

Define diffeomorphisms
\widetilde{l}_{i}:\widetilde{W}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

\widetilde{l}_{i}(x,w)=\left(\frac{\langle x,v^{\perp}\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}},\frac{\langle w,v^{\perp}\rangle}{\|v\|^{2}}\right)

Then we have

\widetilde{l}_{1}\circ\widetilde{P}_{0}\circ\left(\widetilde{l}_{0}\right)^{-1}=\varphi_{\widetilde{L}}^{0,1}

The conjugacy of the return map

and the time-1 map

follows
from the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\widetilde{F}

Example 2.5
.

Let
\widetilde{F}
be the flat metric, i.e.

\widetilde{F}(x,w)=\|w\|=\sqrt{\langle w,w\rangle}

and
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
prime. The Lagrangian
\widetilde{L}
(see definition
2.1
)
is then given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{L}(t,x,r)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{F}(tv+xv^{\perp},v+rv^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\|v\|\sqrt{1+r^{2}}

We obtain that a function
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an Euler-Lagrange solution if and only if

\underbrace{\partial_{x}\widetilde{L}(t,\theta(t),\theta^{\prime}(t))}_{=0}-\partial_{t}(\partial_{r}\widetilde{L}(t,\theta(t),\theta^{\prime}(t)))=0

which is equivalent to

\|v\|\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}}{\sqrt{1+\theta^{\prime 2}}^{3}}=0

and thus
\theta
is an Euler-Lagrange solution if and only if
\theta^{\prime\prime}(t)=0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently the solutions are of the form

\theta(t)=at+b\quad\text{for }a,b\in\mathbb{R}

Thus, we have for the time-1 map
\varphi_{\widetilde{L}}^{0,1}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the Lagrangian
\widetilde{L}

\varphi_{\widetilde{L}}^{0,1}(x,y)=(x+y,y)

To determine the map
\widetilde{R}_{0}
let
(x,w)\in\widetilde{V}_{0}
, i.e.

(x,w)=(bv^{\perp},rv+hv^{\perp})
for
b,h\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
, such that
\|rv+hv^{\perp}\|=1
. The Euler-Lagrange solution
\theta
with
\theta(0)=b
and
\theta^{\prime}(0)=\tfrac{h}{r}

is given by

\theta(t)=\frac{h}{r}t+b

This yields the following reparametrization
\gamma
of an
\widetilde{F}
-geodesic

\gamma(t)=tv+\left(\frac{h}{r}t+b\right)v^{\perp}

We calculate

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{0}(x,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma(1),\frac{\dot{\gamma}(1)}{\|\dot{\gamma}(1)\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(v+(\tfrac{h}{r}+b)v^{\perp},\frac{v+\tfrac{h}{r}v^{\perp}}{\|v+\tfrac{h}{r}v^{\perp}\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x+\frac{1}{r}w,\frac{w}{\|w\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x+\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle w,v\rangle}w,w\right)

To determine the map
\widetilde{P}_{0}
recall that

\widetilde{P}_{0}=\left(\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{1}}\right)^{-1}\circ\widetilde{R}_{0}\circ\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{0}}

with

\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{i}}(x,w)=\left(x,\tfrac{w}{\|w\|}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{i}}\right)^{-1}(x,w)=\left(x,\tfrac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle w,v\rangle}w\right)

For
(x,w)\in\widetilde{W}_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{0}(x,w)

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{1}}\right)^{-1}\circ\widetilde{R}_{0}\left(x,\tfrac{w}{\|w\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{g}|_{\widetilde{W}_{1}}\right)^{-1}\left(x+\tfrac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle w,v\rangle}w,\tfrac{w}{\|w\|}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x+\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle w,v\rangle}w,\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle w,v\rangle}w\right)

\displaystyle=(x+w,w)

with the last equation following from
(x,w)\in\widetilde{W}_{0}
.
To conclude the example we calculate further

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{1}\circ\widetilde{P}_{0}\circ(\widetilde{l}_{0})^{-1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{l}_{1}\circ\widetilde{P}_{0}(xv^{\perp},v+yv^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{l}_{1}(xv^{\perp}+v+yv^{\perp},v+yv^{\perp})

\displaystyle=(x+y,y)

with the last line being equal to the time-1 map of
\widetilde{L}
.

In this example the time-1 map of
\widetilde{L}
is a twist map (the so-called
shear map
).
We will see later that in general the strict convexity of
L
guarantees that the time-1 map

\phi_{L}^{0,1}
is conjugated to a finite composition of positive twist maps. This will allow us to apply the

2.2
Twist maps of the cylinder and Mather’s theorem

In this section we will briefly recall some of the theory on finite compositions of monotone twist maps of the
cylinder and Mather’s theorem about connecting orbits in Birkhoff regions of instability.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
f:Z\to Z
be a map of the cylinder with a lift
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

F(x,y)=(X(x,y),Y(x,y))
is a diffeomorphism with

1.

F
is isotopic to the identity

2.

Twist condition: The map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,X(x,y))
is a diffeomorphism for every
y

3.

Exact symplectic:
F^{*}(\lambda)-\lambda=dS
for some function
S:Z\to\mathbb{R}
.

Then
f:Z\to Z
is called a
monotone twist map of the cylinder.
The map
f
is called
positive

if
\partial_{y}X>0
everywhere. Here,
\lambda=ydx
is the Liouville form on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

For further reading on twist maps we refer the reader to
[

Observe that the Euler-Lagrange solutions of

Proposition 3.3

The time-1 map

Since

\theta_{r_{n}}(t_{n})
is also unbounded. This contradicts the fact that

. Since we have

. Analogously we obtain
\theta_{p}(t)=s
. As above it follows that

.
To see that
g

h_{t_{n}}(s_{n})=h_{t_{n}}(\theta_{r_{n}}(t_{n}))=\theta_{r_{n}}^{\prime}(t_{n})

and hence it follows from the continuity of

Thus we have proven that
g:(s,t)\mapsto h_{t}(s)
is continuous.
To construct an invariant graph in
ST^{2}
, observe that since the reparametrized geodesics
\gamma_{s}
with

\gamma_{s}(t)=tv+\theta_{s}(t)v^{\perp}
can not

Furthermore,

We define a map

.

\displaystyle\gamma_{s}(t)+v^{\perp}

\displaystyle=tv+\theta_{s}(t)v^{\perp}+v^{\perp}

\displaystyle=tv+(\theta_{s}(t)+1)v^{\perp}

\displaystyle=tv+\theta_{s+1}(t)v^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{s+1}(t)

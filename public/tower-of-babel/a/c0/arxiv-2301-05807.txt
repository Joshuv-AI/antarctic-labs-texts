Clarkson-McLeod solutions of the fourth Painlevé equation and the parabolic cylinder-kernel determinant
Jun Xia, Shuai-Xia Xu, Yu-Qiu Zhao

Abstract
The Clarkson-McLeod solutions of the fourth Painlevé equation behave like κ ​ D α − 1 2 2 ​ ( 2 ​ x ) \kappa D_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(\sqrt{2}x) as x → + ∞ x\rightarrow+\infty , where κ \kappa is some real constant and D α − 1 2 ​ ( x ) D_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(x) is the parabolic cylinder function. Using the Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method, we derive the asymptotic behaviors for this class of solutions as x → − ∞ x\to-\infty . This completes a proof of Clarkson and McLeod’s conjecture on the asymptotics of this family of solutions. The total integrals of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions and the asymptotic approximations of the σ \sigma -form of this family of solutions are also derived. Furthermore, we find a determinantal representation of the σ \sigma -form of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions via an integrable operator with the parabolic cylinder kernel.

Keywords and phrases:
The fourth Painlevé equation, asymptotic expansion, Riemann-Hilbert problem, Deift-Zhou method, Hamiltonian, total integral, Fredholm determinant.

1
Introduction and statement of results

This paper is concerned with the fourth Painlevé (PIV) equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}q}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}=\frac{1}{2q}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}q^{3}+4xq^{2}+2(x^{2}-2\alpha)q,

(1.1)

where the parameter

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We are interested in the real solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying the asymptotic condition

q(x)\rightarrow 0\quad\mathrm{as}\quad x\rightarrow+\infty.

(1.2)

A result due to Bassom
et al.

[
6
]
shows that any real solution of the PIV equation (
1.1
), fulfilling the boundary condition (
1.2
), possesses the following asymptotic behavior

q(x)\sim\kappa D_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(\sqrt{2}x)\quad\mathrm{as}\quad x\to+\infty

(1.3)

for some constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. Here,
D_{\mu}(x)
denotes the parabolic cylinder function,
which is the solution of Weber’s equation (cf.
[
24
, Chapter 12]
)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}D_{\mu}(x)}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}=\left(\frac{1}{4}x^{2}-\mu-\frac{1}{2}\right)D_{\mu}(x),

uniquely characterized by the asymptotic property

D_{\mu}(x)=x^{\mu}e^{-\frac{1}{4}x^{2}}\left(1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\quad x\to+\infty.

(1.4)

Conversely, for any constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique solution to the PIV equation (
1.1
) asymptotic to
\kappa D_{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}^{2}(\sqrt{2}x)

as
x\to+\infty
. For convenience, we denote such solutions by
q(x)=q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
.

Concerning the asymptotic behaviors of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
as
x\rightarrow-\infty
, there has been the following conjecture.

Conjecture (Clarkson-McLeod
[
9
]
)

There exists a constant
\kappa^{*}>0
such that:

(a) When
0<\kappa<\kappa^{*}
and as
x\rightarrow-\infty
, we have

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)\sim c_{n}\,2^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}x^{2\alpha-1}e^{-x^{2}}

(1.5)

if
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}=n+1\in\mathbb{N}
with
\mathbb{N}
being the set of all positive integers; and

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)\sim-\frac{2x}{3}+\frac{4d_{1}}{\sqrt{3}}\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{4d_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln(\sqrt{2}|x|)+d_{2}+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right)+O\left(x^{-1}\right)

(1.6)

if
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, where the constants
c_{n}
,
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
are dependent on
\kappa
.

(b) When
\kappa=\kappa^{*}
,
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
is asymptotic to
-2x
as
x\rightarrow-\infty
.

(c) When
\kappa>\kappa^{*}
,
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
has a pole at some point
x
on the real axis.

The solutions
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
in this class are now called the
Clarkson-McLeod solutions
of the PIV equation (
1.1
).
In the case
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
of part (a), the asymptotic formula (
1.5
) has been proven in
[
6
,
9
]
. Therein, the values of
c_{n}
and
\kappa^{*}
were explicitly evaluated by

c_{n}=\frac{\kappa}{1-\sqrt{\pi}\,n!\,\kappa},\qquad\kappa^{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\,n!}.

(1.7)

While
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, the value of
\kappa^{*}
in
[
9
]
was conjectured to be

\kappa^{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

(1.8)

For
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, each solution
q(x)=q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
of the PIV equation (
1.1
) can be explicitly expressed in terms of the classical special functions. The behavior of the solution as
x\to-\infty
can then be determined in a straightforward manner. For example, when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, the exact solution is given by (see
[
6
, Equation (4.13)]
)

q\left(x;{1}/{2},\kappa\right)=\frac{2\kappa\,e^{-x^{2}}}{2-\kappa\sqrt{\pi}\,\mathrm{erfc}(x)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.9)

where
\mathrm{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt
is the complementary error function; cf.
[
24
, Chapter 7]
.
It is readily verified that
\kappa^{*}=1/\sqrt{\pi}
. The approximation (
1.5
) is valid since
\mathrm{erfc}(x)
is strictly monotone decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
with range
(0,2)
. Similarly, it holds that
q\left(x;{1}/{2},1/\sqrt{\pi}\right)\sim-2x
as
x\to-\infty
. For
\kappa\sqrt{\pi}>1
, the solution has a real pole which is the zero of the denominator in
(
1.9
).
For general
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, the solutions and their asymptotic approximations as given in (
1.5
) can be obtained by using (
1.9
) and the Bäcklund transformation; see
[
6
, Equation (5.9)]
, or
[
4
, Equation (3.24)]
.

The solutions
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
with positive half-integers
\alpha

find prominent applications in the theory of orthogonal polynomials with the discontinuous Hermite weight
[
8
]
, and in random matrix theory
[
15
,
25
]
.
It is well known that for the Gaussian unitary ensemble of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices,
the probability of having no eigenvalues in the interval
(x,+\infty)
can be expressed in terms of the Fredholm determinant

\det\left(\mathbf{I}-K_{n,x}\right).

(1.10)

Here,
K_{n,x}
is the integrable operator acting on
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
with the classical Hermite kernel

K_{n,x}(\lambda,\mu)=e^{-\frac{(x+\lambda)^{2}+(\mu+x)^{2}}{2}}\gamma_{n-1}^{2}\frac{\pi_{n}(\lambda+x)\pi_{n-1}(\mu+x)-\pi_{n-1}(\lambda+x)\pi_{n}(\mu+x)}{\lambda-\mu},

(1.11)

where
\pi_{n}(\lambda)
is the
n
-th monic Hermite polynomial
determined by the orthogonal relation

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\pi_{n}(x)\pi_{m}(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx=\gamma_{n}^{-2}\delta_{n,m},

(1.12)

with the normalization constant

\gamma_{n-1}^{2}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(n)};

(1.13)

see
[
24
, Table 18.3.1]
.
It is found by Tracy and Widom in
[
25
]
that

\frac{d}{dx}\ln\det(\mathbf{I}-K_{n,x})=\sigma_{n}(x)

(1.14)

where
\sigma_{n}(x)
is the unique solution of the
\sigma
-form of the PIV equation
[
20
]
with the parameter
\nu=n

\left(\sigma_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}+4\left(\sigma_{\nu}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\left(\sigma_{\nu}^{\prime}+2\nu\right)-4\left(x\sigma_{\nu}^{\prime}-\sigma_{\nu}\right)^{2}=0,

(1.15)

characterized by the boundary condition

\sigma_{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{2^{\nu-1}x^{2\nu-2}e^{-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}(\nu-1)!},\quad x\to+\infty.

(1.16)

It is worth mentioning that
\sigma_{\nu}(x)
is closely related to the Hamiltonian for the PIV equation (
1.1
); see Remark
1.7
below and
[
20
]
.

For general
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
the asymptotic formula (
1.6
) in part (a) was proved by Abdullayev
[
1
]
using the integral equation method. The connection formulas, that is, the explicit expressions of the parameters
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
in (
1.6
), in terms of the parameter
\kappa
, were derived later by Its and Kapaev
[
18
]
, and by Wong and Zhang
[
27
]
, using respectively the isomonodromy method and the uniform asymptotic approach.
Moreover, according to the numerical investigations performed
in
[
5
]
,
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
might blow up at finite
x
if
\alpha<-1/2
. While
\alpha>-1/2
, the same numerical results allow us to expect the absence of the real poles of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
.
The asymptotic behavior of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
and the connection formulas in part (c) were recently derived by the current authors in
[
28
]
.
While, to the best of our knowledge, the asymptotic result in part (b) of the Clarkson-McLeod conjecture has not been confirmed.
It is also desirable to know whether there exists a determinantal representation of the
\sigma
-form of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions similar to (
1.14
), for general
\alpha
not being a half-integer.

In the present paper, we derive the asymptotic approximations and the connection formulas for the Clarkson-McLeod solutions as
x\to-\infty
by using the Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. Particularly, we prove part (b) and revisit part (a), (c) of the Clarkson-McLeod conjecture.
We also show that the
\sigma
-form of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions of the PIV equation (
1.1
) with general parameter
\alpha
can be represented by the Fredholm determinant of an integrable operator whose kernel is expressed in terms of the classical parabolic cylinder functions, thus generalizing the result (
1.14
) of Tracy and Widom.
Furthermore, the asymptotics of the Hamiltonian of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions and the evaluations of total integrals of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions are also obtained.

1.1
Statement of results

Asymptotics of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions

Our first result is the following complete description of the asymptotic behaviors of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions to the PIV equation (
1.1
) when the parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\kappa\neq 0
be a given real number and
\kappa^{*}
be the constant defined by (
1.8
). For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique real solution
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
to the PIV equation (
1.1
) satisfying the following asymptotic behavior

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)=\kappa\,2^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}x^{2\alpha-1}e^{-x^{2}}\left(1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right)\quad\mathrm{as}\quad x\to+\infty.

(1.17)

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, the solution
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
possesses the following asymptotic behaviors as
x\rightarrow-\infty
.

(1) If
\kappa(\kappa-\kappa^{*})<0
, then

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\frac{2}{3}x+\frac{2\sqrt{6}\,b_{1}}{3}\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln(2\sqrt{3}x^{2})+\psi_{1}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(1.18)

(2) If
\kappa=\kappa^{*}
, then

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-2x+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(1.19)

(3) If
\kappa(\kappa-\kappa^{*})>0
, then

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\frac{2}{3}x+\frac{2x}{2\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{b_{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln\left(2\sqrt{3}x^{2}\right)+\psi_{2}\right)+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(1.20)

The error term in the asymptotic expansion (
1.20
) is uniform for
x
bounded away from the singularities appearing on the right-hand side of the asymptotic expansion. Moreover, the corresponding connection formulas are respectively given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} b_{1}^{2}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\ln(1-\left|\rho\right|^{2}),\quad b_{1}\geq 0,\\
\psi_{1}&=-\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{2\pi}{3}\alpha-\arg\Gamma\left(-\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}i\right)-\arg\rho,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.21)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} b_{2}&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\ln(|\rho|^{2}-1),\\
\psi_{2}&=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\alpha-\arg\Gamma\left(-\frac{b_{2}}{\sqrt{3}}i+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\arg\rho,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.22)

where

\rho=1-\frac{2\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{e^{\pi i\alpha}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\right)}\kappa.

(1.23)

When
\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds
\kappa^{*}=0
, and
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
possesses the asymptotic behavior (
1.20
)
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

as
x\rightarrow-\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the remaining case
\kappa=0
corresponds to the trivial solution
q(x;\alpha,0)=0
. As aforementioned,
the asymptotic formula (
1.18
) and connection formulas (
1.21
) have been derived in
[
18
,
27
]
. In this paper, we give alternative proofs of these formulas by performing asymptotic analysis of the Riemann-Hilbert (RH, for short) problem for PIV equation. Furthermore, we accomplish the case
b_{1}=0
in (
1.18
) and (
1.21
) which is not covered in
[
18
, Equation (1.10)]
and
[
27
, Equation (1.5)]
. We also show that (
1.20
) is true for negative half-integer
\alpha
, a case not considered in our previous work
[
28
]
.
We also provide a novel proof of (
1.19
). Theorem
1.1
, along with the case with positive half-integer
\alpha
solved in terms of the special functions in
[
6
,
9
]
, fully confirms the conjecture of Clarkson and McLeod. Minor extension allows
\kappa^{*}
to be negative or zero.

The asymptotic analysis of the RH problem for the PIV equation enables us to derive simultaneously the asymptotics of the Hamiltonian associated with the Clarkson-McLeod solutions and the total integrals of Clarkson-McLeod solutions along the real axis, which are of independent interests.

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{N}
, the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)
of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions, defined by (
2.14
) below,
has the following asymptotic behavior as
x\rightarrow+\infty
:

\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\kappa\,2^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}x^{2\alpha-1}e^{-x^{2}}\left(1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\right).

(1.24)

While for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)
satisfies the following asymptotic behaviors as
x\rightarrow-\infty
.

(1) If
\kappa(\kappa-\kappa^{*})<0
, then

\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\frac{8x^{3}}{27}+\frac{4}{3}\left(\alpha+b_{1}^{2}\right)x-\frac{2\sqrt{2}\,b_{1}}{3}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln(2\sqrt{3}x^{2})+\psi_{1}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right),

(1.25)

(2) If
\kappa=\kappa^{*}
, then

\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)=4\alpha x+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right),

(1.26)

(3) If
\kappa(\kappa-\kappa^{*})>0
, then

\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\frac{8x^{3}}{27}+\frac{4}{3}(\alpha+b_{2})x+\frac{\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}x\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{b_{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln\left(2\sqrt{3}x^{2}\right)+\psi_{2}\right)}{2\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{b_{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\ln\left(2\sqrt{3}x^{2}\right)+\psi_{2}\right)+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(1.27)

The parameters
b_{1}
,
\psi_{1}
and
b_{2}
,
\psi_{2}
are the same as in (
1.21
) and (
1.22
), respectively. The error term in the asymptotic expansion (
1.27
) is uniform for
x
bounded away from the singularities appearing on the right-hand side of the asymptotic expansion.

Moreover, when
\frac{1}{2}-\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, for any non-vanishing

\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)
possesses the same asymptotic behavior (
1.27
) as
x\rightarrow-\infty
.

. Similar results for the integrals of the Painlevé II transcendents have been derived in

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathrm{P.V.}
denote the Cauchy principal value. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we evaluate the total integrals of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
as follows.

(1) If
\kappa(\kappa-\kappa^{*})<0
, take
c<0<d
such that all real poles of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
lie in the interval
(c,d)
,

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg\{\int^{c}_{-\infty}\left(q(t;\alpha,\kappa)+\frac{2t}{3}-\frac{2\alpha}{t}\right)dt+\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{c}^{d}q(t;\alpha,\kappa)\,dt+\int^{+\infty}_{d}q(t;\alpha,\kappa)\,dt\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{(-1)^{N_{+}-N_{-}}\sqrt{\pi}e^{\frac{c^{2}}{3}}|c|^{-2\alpha}3^{2\alpha}(1-\rho)e^{\pi i\alpha}}{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha)(1-|\rho|^{2})^{\frac{2}{3}}},

(1.28)

where
\rho
is related to
\kappa
by (
1.23
) and
N_{\pm}
denote the numbers of real poles
of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
in the interval
(c,d)
of residues
\pm 1
, respectively.

(2) If
\kappa=\kappa^{*}
, take
c<0<d
such that all real poles of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
lie in the interval
(c,d)
,

\displaystyle\exp\Bigg\{\int^{c}_{-\infty}\left(q(t;\alpha,\kappa)+2t+\frac{2\alpha}{t}\right)dt+\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{c}^{d}q(t;\alpha,\kappa)\,dt+\int^{+\infty}_{d}q(t;\alpha,\kappa)\,dt\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{(-1)^{N_{+}-N_{-}}\sqrt{\pi}e^{-c^{2}}|c|^{-2\alpha}2^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)},

(1.29)

where
N_{\pm}
denote the numbers of real poles
of
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
of residues
\pm 1
in the interval
(c,d)
.

Remark 1.5
.

According to the numerical analysis performed in
[
5
]
, it is expected that
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
is pole free on the real axis when

. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no rigorous proof of the numerical evidence.

Determinantal representation of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions

Let
\gamma K_{\nu,x}
be the integrable operator acting on
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
with the parabolic cylinder kernel

\gamma K_{\nu,x}(\lambda,\mu)=\gamma\frac{D_{\nu}(\sqrt{2}(\lambda+x))D_{\nu-1}(\sqrt{2}(\mu+x))-D_{\nu-1}(\sqrt{2}(\lambda+x))D_{\nu}(\sqrt{2}(\mu+x))}{\lambda-\mu},

(1.30)

where
D_{\nu}
is the parabolic cylinder function with the parameter
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma
is a real parameter. Then, the Fredholm determinant of the operator is related to the
\sigma
-form of the Clarkson-McLeod solutions as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma K_{\nu,x}
is the integrable operator acting on
L^{2}(0,+\infty)
with the kernel (
1.30
). We have

\frac{d}{dx}\ln\det(\mathbf{I}-\gamma K_{\nu,x})=\sigma_{\nu}(x;\gamma),

(1.31)

where
\sigma_{\nu}(x;\gamma)
is the unique solution of the
\sigma
-form (
1.15
) of the PIV equation,
determined by the boundary condition near positive infinity

\sigma_{\nu}(x;\gamma)\sim\sqrt{2}\gamma D_{\nu-1}^{2}(\sqrt{2}x).

(1.32)

Remark 1.7
.

It is seen from
[
20
, Equations (C.34)-(C.36)]
and (
2.14
) below that the
\sigma
-form of the PIV equation is related to
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
and the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)
by

\sigma_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(x;\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\left(q(x;\alpha,\kappa)-\mathcal{H}(x;\alpha,\kappa)\right),

(1.33)

and conversely

q(x;\alpha,\kappa)=-\frac{\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(x;\gamma)+2x\sigma_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x;\gamma)-2\sigma_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(x;\gamma)}{2\sigma_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}(x;\gamma)+4\alpha+2}.

(1.34)

It follows from the approximation (
1.32
) and the relation (
1.34
) that
q(x;\alpha,\kappa)
actually satisfies the boundary condition (
1.17
),
and the parameters
\kappa
and
\gamma
are related by

\kappa=\sqrt{2}\gamma.

(1.35)

In view of (
1.33
), the asymptotic behavior near negative infinity of the solution of the
\sigma
-form (
1.15
) of the PIV equation subject to the boundary condition (
1.32
) can be obtained by using Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

Remark 1.8
.

For
\nu=n\in\mathbb{N}
, the parabolic cylinder function is reduced to the Hermite polynomial
[
24
, (12.7.2)]

D_{n}(\sqrt{2}x)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}2^{\frac{n}{2}}\pi_{n}(x),

(1.36)

where
\pi_{n}(x)
is the monic Hermite polynomial defined through
(
1.12
). Therefore, for given
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, the kernel
\gamma^{*}K_{\nu,x}(\lambda,\mu)
with

\gamma^{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\Gamma(\nu)}

(1.37)

is reduced to the classical Hermite kernel defined in (
1.11
).
Applying Theorem
1.6
, we recover (
1.14
)-(
1.16
), which was obtained first by Tracy and Widom.

2
Riemann-Hilbert problem for the Painlevé IV equation

In this section, we review the RH problem for the PIV equation (
1.1
), a detailed description can be found in

Taking into account the lower and upper triangular structures of the jump matrices, the properties of

and the neighborhoods of the origin

RH problem for

\displaystyle f(z)

When

for

but

In the case
|s_{*}|>1
, we have

. From the definition (
3.22

, it follows that

{\beta}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ln(1-|s_{*}|^{2})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ln(|s_{*}|^{2}-1)-\frac{1}{2}:={\beta}_{0}-\frac{1}{2},\quad{\beta}_{0}\in i\mathbb{R}.

(4.1)

This, together with (
3.44
) and (

as

. Therefore, the matching conditions (
3.35
) and (
3.45
) are no longer fulfilled.

Similar problems have also occurred in deriving singular asymptotics for the PII transcendents
[
7
,
16
]
. In
[
7
]
, Bothner and Its develop a certain “dressing” technique to tackle this problem. However, we find their method does not apply to our case. Instead, our idea is to modify the global parametrix

, so as to satisfy the matching condition. Similar technique was first used in
[
30
]
to derive the uniform asymptotics of the Pollaczek polynomials and subsequently to derive the uniform asymptotics of a system of Szegő class polynomials
[

]
.

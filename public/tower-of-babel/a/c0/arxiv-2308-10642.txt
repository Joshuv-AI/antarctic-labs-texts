Frobenius trace fields of cohomologically rigid local systems
Raju Krishnamoorthy, Yeuk Hay Joshua Lam

Abstract
. Let X / ℂ X/\mathbb{C} be a smooth variety with simple normal crossings compactification X ¯ \bar{X} , and let L L be an irreducible ℚ ¯ ℓ \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell} -local system on X X with torsion determinant. Suppose L L is cohomologically rigid. The pair ( X , L ) (X,L) may be spread out to a finitely generated base, and therefore reduced modulo p p for almost all p p ; the Frobenius traces of this mod p p reduction lie in a number field F p F_{p} , by a theorem of Deligne. We investigate to what extent the fields F p F_{p} are bounded , meaning that they are contained in a fixed number field, independent of p p . We prove a host of results around this question. For instance: • assuming L L has totally degenerate unipotent monodromy around some component of Z Z , then L L admits a spreading out such that the F p F_{p} ’s are bounded; • without any local monodromy assumptions, we show that the F p F_{p} ’s are bounded as soon as they are bounded at one point of X X . We also speculate on the relation between the boundedness of the F p F_{p} ’s, and the local system L L being strongly of geometric origin, a notion due to Langer-Simpson.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate an arithmetic property of rigid local systems on a complex variety
X
. More precisely, to such a local system
L
and a prime number
p
, there is a naturally associated number field, which is by definition the field generated by the traces of Frobenius elements at
p
. Our motivating question is whether these number fields are independent of
p
. Our strongest result is
A
, which says that the
F_{p}
’s are indeed independent of
p
, under an assumption on the local monodromy at infinity.

1.1.

For a smooth complex variety

Definition 1.1.1

Let

Remark 1.1.5

In the very special situation when the local system

Remark 1.1.9

In response to Theorems

Or, as a sanity check, whether such a statement is implied by “standard conjectures” in this area, such as Simpson’s motivicity conjecture, the Hodge conjecture, the Tate conjecture, etc. This will be discussed at length in this introduction: as we will see in Remark

1.2.
Discussions

The motivicity conjecture of Simpson
[
Sim92
, p.9]
states that each
L\in\text{CohRig}_{d}^{n,v}(X^{\text{an}},\mathbb{C})
is
of geometric origin
(also referred to as
motivic
in the following): this roughly means that after restricting to a dense open subset
U\subset X
, it arises as a subquotient (or even summand) in the cohomology of a family of smooth projective varieties. Note that if
L
is of geometric origin, with family
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow U
and
i\geq 0
such that
L|_{U}
is a summand of
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}
, then it is very well possible that
L|_{U}
is
not cut out by an algebraic cycle
.

Therefore, there is an a priori more restrictive notion, namely that
L
is cut out by algebraic cycles from the cohomology of a family of varieties; this notion was introduced by Langer-Simpson
[
LS18
]
(see the end of Section 2.5 of loc.cit.), and we follow their terminology and call such local systems
strongly of geometric origin
, or
strongly motivic
. It is straightforward to see that if
L
is strongly motivic, then a spreading out with bounded Frobenius traces does exist.

Question 1.2.1
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth complex variety, and
L
a local system on
X
. Does the existence of a spreading out
(\mathfrak{X},\bar{\mathfrak{X}},S,\mathcal{L},\iota)
with bounded Frobenius traces imply that
L
is strongly of geometric origin?

Remark 1.2.2
.

Unfortunately, it seems hard to probe Question
1.2.1
since there are no known examples of local systems of geometric origin which are not strongly so. We note that local systems which are strongly motivic admit spreading outs satisfying other properties, such as being
de Rham
at all places above
\ell
, which a priori seems independent from having bounded Frobenius traces. Indeed, the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture
[
LZ17
]
together with the Tate conjecture imply that a local system is strongly motivic if it admits a spreading out which is de Rham at
\ell
.

Note also that, conjecturally, the subtlety between geometric and strongly geometric local systems disappear once we consider the projectivized local system. Indeed, in his beautiful work, Petrov
[
Pet23
]
shows that the adjoint of any geometrically irreducible arithmetic
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-local system is de Rham at places above
\ell
, and hence this adjoint local system satisfies the assumptions of the relative Fontaine-Mazur conjecture, and therefore is conjecturally strongly motivic.

Remark 1.2.3

(Assuming all conjectures)
.

Langer-Simpson say the following
[
LS18
, p.1544]
: “(If we) assume the Hodge conjecture, then one might be able to show that

of weak geometric origin implies that some

is of strong geometric origin.”

The Hodge conjecture implies that the Hodge realization functor
\textbf{Mot}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\textbf{HS}_{\mathbb{Q}}
from motives to Hodge structures is fully faithful. More generally, it is a folklore conjecture that if
S/\mathbb{C}
is a smooth variety, then the functor:

\textbf{Mot}(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\textbf{-VHS}(S)

from

S
(appropriately defined) to
\mathbb{Q}
-VHS is fully faithful. Therefore, for any finite extension

, the base-changed functor
\textbf{Mot}(S)_{E}\rightarrow E\textbf{-VHS}(S)
is conjecturally also fully-faithful.

Suppose now that
L
is a
\mathbb{C}
-local system on
S
of geometric origin. Then there exists

•

a number field

such that

may be conjugated to have coefficients in

•

a open dense

, a smooth projective family

Combining this with Simpson’s motivicity conjecture, we can therefore expect that, for

1.4.
Sketch of proof of Theorem
D

For this proof sketch we assume

, i.e. that our local systems, when restricted to the geometric fundamental group, have trivial determinant. The overall strategy is to analyze the ramification behavior of the trace field
F_{s}
at each prime

by looking at the
m
-adic companions.

For point (1) note that the degree of the trace field is the number of
\ell
-companions; that
\ell
-companions of cohomologically rigid local systems are again cohomolgically rigid was proven by Esnault-Groechenig, and we may conclude since there are only finitely many such local systems with fixed rank and trivial determinant.

Let us write
p=\text{char}(s)
and
k(s)
for the residue field at
s
. For the proof of point (2), we let
\ell_{0}=\ell_{0}(X,n,d)
be a prime such that for all prime numbers
m>\ell_{0}
, the topological, cohomologically rigid, local systems on
X^{an}
all have coefficients unramified at
m
. For a spreading out
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{L},S,\iota)
, a point

, and

, let

be any
m
-companion of
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathfrak{X}_{s}}
, which is an
m
-adic local system. Then

also has unramified coefficients. We then show that, after possibly discarding finitely many

’s, we may descend this local system to

itself, with

: indeed, it is immediate that there is a descent as a Weil sheaf on

(i.e. a representation of the Weil group), but to get an étale sheaf we need an argument involving absolute irreducibility of residual representations. This descent will not, in general, be the local system we started with: rather, it is a

.

For point (3) of Theorem

, as mentioned above, we proceed by showing that

is also unramified at

, at least under some assumptions on the point

As mentioned above, the work most closely related to ours is perhaps that of Klevdal-Patrikis

(\mathfrak{X},S,\mathcal{L},\iota)
such that
{\rm ad}(\mathcal{L})
has bounded Frobenius traces.

The following is the key lemma.

Lemma 6.1.2
.

Let
Y/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a smooth variety and let

be an irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
local system with finite order determinant on

. Then the Frobenius trace field of
{\rm ad}(L)
is a subfield of the stable trace field of

.

Proof.

Set

to be the stable trace field inside of the Frobenius trace field of
L
and
K^{\prime}\subset K\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
to be the stable trace field inside of the Frobenius trace field of
{\rm ad}(L)
. Of course,
K\subset E
and
K^{\prime}\subset E^{\prime}
, and our goal is to prove that
K\subset E^{\prime}
.

If
E=E^{\prime}
, then we are done, so we may assume that
E^{\prime}
strictly contains
E
; we may suppose that
E^{\prime}
is in fact the Frobenius trace field of the restriction of
L
to

for some power

of

. Furthermore, we may assume that there is
\lambda\in K\subset E
which is not in

.

Then there exists
\tau\in\text{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
with the following properties:

(1)

{}^{\tau}L
is not isomorphic to
L
and
{}^{\tau}{\rm ad}(L)
is not isomorphic to
{\rm ad}(L)

(2)

{}^{\tau}L
and
L
are isomorphic on

, and similarly with
{\rm ad}(L)
.

Note that the operation of
\tau
-companions commutes with tensor product and dual, so

.

On the other hand, as
{}^{\tau}L
and

are isomorphic over

, it follows that there exists a character

such that

. It follows that

,
which is a contradiction. Therefore, such a

∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1.1
.

By Theorem
D
, there exists a spreading out
(\mathfrak{X},S,\mathcal{L},\iota)
and a number field

∎

6.2.
Boundedness for

The goal of this section is the prove the following.

Theorem 6.2.1
.

For

, there exists an integer

We first require the following lemma, analogous to Lemma
6.1.2
.

Lemma 6.2.2

Let

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
local system with determinant finite order. Let

. Then the Frobenius trace field of

is a subfield of the stable trace field of

.

The idea to consider the tensor product

is essentially due to Kerz
[
Pet23
, Lemma 6.2]

Proof.

The proof is analogous to Lemma
6.1.2
. Let

be the stable trace field inside of the Frobenius trace field of
L
. Then the trace field of

is a subfield

, and it is our goal to prove that

.

As before, we may assume that

. Suppose for contradiction that

. Take

that is the identity on

but non-trivial on

Note that

-local system on

, as

and

Therefore,

, it follows that

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2.1
.

Set
h=n
, where

is the rank of
L
. Let
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{L},S,\iota)
be a spreading out as in Theorem
D
(
3
). After possibly replacing

by a scheme étale over it, we may assume that the map
\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow S
has a section

. Consider

. (Here,

is a local system on

, and we view it as a local system on

∎

6.3.
Rigidifying

Recall that it cannot be true that the field of Frobenius traces of an
arbitrary
spreading-out of a rigid local system is bounded; indeed, twisting

preserve this boundedness. Esnault suggested to us a formulation to rigidify the problem.

Setup 6.3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}/S

, where the structure map

Theorem 6.3.2
.

Notation as in Setup
6.3.1
. Suppose further that

(1)

has bounded stable Frobenius trace field, i.e., there exists a number field

(2)

Then there exists an étale map

the base-change of

to

We first construct

when

. The local system

corresponds to a representation

is an
\ell
-adic local field. By compactness, we may (non-canonically) pick a stable lattice to obtain a representation

We replace

Pick a closed point

of

(with residue field denoted by

∎

6.4.
The case of bad reduction

In this subsection, we study the case of local systems with bad reduction, specifically with reference to Definition
A.7.3
. To explain our results, we need to recall what it means for a local system to be
de Rham at
\ell
.

Definition 6.4.1
.

Let
X/k
be a smooth variety over a finitely generated field of characteristic 0 and let
L
be an
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-local system on
X
. We say that
L
is
de Rham at all places above
\ell
(or, more simply,
de Rham at
\ell
) if for every
\ell
-adic local field

and for every embedding
k\hookrightarrow K
, the restriction
L|_{X_{K}}
is de Rham at
\ell
in the sense of
[
Pet23
, Definition 2.2]
.

We prove two main results: if
\mathcal{L}
is an (geometrically irreducible) arithmetic
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-local system whose monodromy around a boundary component has a Jordan block of multiplicity 1 and generalized eigenvalue
\in\pm 1
, then there is a twist
\mathcal{L}(\chi)
such that

(1)

the stable Frobenius trace field coincides with the Frobenius trace field; and

(2)

\mathcal{L}(\chi)
is geometric in the sense of Fontaine-Mazur, i.e., is de Rham at all places above
\ell
.

An example of the above boundary condition is a local system with totally degenerate unipotent reduction. These two results suggest to us that arithmetic local systems with such conditions at the boundary are in fact strongly of geometric origin.

In particular, this shows us that cohomologically rigid local systems that have maximal unipotent degeneration admit a spreading out with bounded Frobenius traces.

Setup 6.4.2
.

Let

.

(1)

First, shrink

Lemma 6.4.4

Let

Appendix A
Companions

In this section, we briefly recall the theory of (
\ell
-adic) companions, as conjectured by Deligne and proven by Lafforgue, Drinfeld, and Deligne, and record several consequences that we will use in this article. (For a panoramic summary of the theory of companions, see
[
Ked22
]
.) We also explain a strong local-global compatibility of
\ell
-adic companions at the cusps, which was observed in
[
Del12

and is a simple corollary of local-global compatibility in the Langlands correspondence.

For the rest of this section, let
Y/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a smooth, geometrically connected variety over a finite field of characteristic
p
.

A.1.
\ell
-adic companions

Definition A.1.1
.

Let
L
be an lisse
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
Weil sheaf on
Y
. We say that
L
is

if for all closed points

. We say that

has

if there exists integers

, such that

.

Definition A.1.2
.

Let
L
be an algebraic lisse
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
Weil sheaf on
Y
. Let
\ell^{\prime}\neq p
be a prime number (possibly equal to
\ell
). Let
\tau\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
be a (possibly non-continuous) field isomorphism; abusing notation, let
\tau
also denote the induced isomorphism
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[t]\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell^{\prime}}[t]

-companion to

is a lisse
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
-Weil sheaf

on

, such that for all closed points

\tau(P_{y}(L,t))=P_{y}(L^{\prime},t)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell^{\prime}}[t].

Remark A.1.3
.

We make two important remarks on
\tau
-companions.

(1)

As
\tau
is generally

, we cannot simply construct a sheaf by applying

to the corresponding matrices: the resulting representation will no longer be continuous and hence there will be no corresponding lisse Weil sheaf.

(2)

Note that the behavior of the
\tau
-companion is only specified on Frobenius conjugacy classes; in particular, the definition does not directly imply any constraint for the characteristic polynomial of other element of
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(Y)

The mathematical content of the following theorem is due to Lafforgue (for curves) and Deligne and Drinfeld (for higher dimensional varieties).

Theorem A.1.4

Let

(2)

Suppose

has fractional cyclotomic determinant. Then

-companions.

Proof.

We provide references to
[

]
for these results. The first follows from Corollary 3.10 and Theorem 4.28 of

The second follows from the fact that companions is preserved under tensorial operations, together with the fact that there exists a

has finite order determinant, and is hence

-plain by Theorem 1.3. (For the definition of

.)
∎

Remark A.7.2

ABJ Correlators with Weakly Broken Higher Spin Symmetry


Abstract
We consider four-point functions of operators in the stress tensor multiplet of the 3d 𝒩 = 6 \mathcal{N}=6 U ​ ( N ) k × U ​ ( N + M ) − k U(N)_{k}\times U(N+M)_{-k} or S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) 2 ​ k × U ​ S ​ p ​ ( 2 + 2 ​ M ) − k SO(2)_{2k}\times USp(2+2M)_{-k} ABJ theories in the limit where M M and k k are taken to infinity while N N and λ ∼ M / k \lambda\sim M/k are held fixed. In this limit, these theories have weakly broken higher spin symmetry and are holographically dual to 𝒩 = 6 \mathcal{N}=6 higher spin gravity on A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} , where λ \lambda is dual to the bulk parity breaking parameter. We use the weakly broken higher spin Ward identities, superconformal Ward identities, and the Lorentzian inversion formula to fully determine the tree level stress tensor multiplet four-point function up to two free parameters. We then use supersymmetric localization to fix both parameters for the ABJ theories in terms of λ \lambda , so that our result for the tree level correlator interpolates between the free theory at λ = 0 \lambda=0 and a parity invariant interacting theory at λ = 1 / 2 \lambda=1/2 . We compare the CFT data extracted from this correlator to a recent numerical bootstrap conjecture for the exact spectrum of U ​ ( 1 ) 2 ​ M × U ​ ( 1 + M ) − 2 ​ M U(1)_{2M}\times U(1+M)_{-2M} ABJ theory (i.e. λ = 1 / 2 \lambda=1/2 and N = 1 N=1 ), and find good agreement in the higher spin regime.

2.2
The
\mathfrak{so}(6)
Pseudocharge

Having reviewed the properties of conserved current multiplets in
{\mathcal{N}}=6

1/c_{T}
. We will follow the strategy employed in
[
26

and use the weakly broken higher spin symmetries to constrain three-point functions. Unlike that paper however, which studies the non-supersymmetric case and so considers the symmetries generated by a spin 4 operator, we will instead focus on the spin 1 operator
H_{1}^{\mu}(\vec{x},X)
. While itself not a higher spin conserved current, it is related to the spin 3 current
T_{3}(\vec{x})
by supersymmetry.

We begin by using
H_{1}(x)
to define a pseudocharge:

\tilde{\delta}(X)\mathcal{O}(0)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|x|=r}dS\cdot H_{1}(\vec{x},X)\,\mathcal{O}(0)\bigg|_{\text{finite as }r\rightarrow 0}\,.

(2.11)

The action of
\tilde{\delta}(X)
is fixed by the 3-points functions
\langle H_{1}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\rangle
. Because
H_{1}^{\mu}
has spin
1
, it must act in the same way on conformal primaries as would any other spin
1
conserved current. In particular, it relates conformal primaries to other conformal primaries with the same spin and conformal dimension.

Now consider the action of
\tilde{\delta}(X)
on an arbitrary three-point function. We can use the divergence theorem to write:

\begin{split}\tilde{\delta}(X)\big\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{y}_{1})&\mathcal{O}_{2}(\vec{y}_{2})\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{y}_{3})\big\rangle\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{r}}d^{3}x\left\langle\nabla\cdot H_{1}(\vec{x},X)\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{y}_{1})\mathcal{O}_{2}(\vec{y}_{2})\mathcal{O}_{3}(\vec{y}_{3})\right\rangle\bigg|_{\text{finite }r\rightarrow 0}\,,\end{split}

(2.12)

where
\mathcal{R}_{r}
is the set of
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
for which
|\vec{x}-\vec{y}_{i}|>r
for each
y_{i}
. If the operator
H_{1}^{\mu}(x,X)
were conserved, the right-hand side of this expression would vanish and we would find that correlators were invariant under
\tilde{\delta}(X)
. When the higher spin symmetries are broken, however,
\nabla\cdot H_{1}
will no longer vanish and so (
2.12
) will gives us a non-trivial identity.

In the infinite

\nabla\cdot H_{1}
will become a conformal primary distinct from
H_{1}^{\mu}
. In order to work out what this primary is, we can use the
{\mathcal{N}}=6
multiplet recombination rules
[
28

38
]

\begin{split}\text{Long}_{\Delta,0}^{[000]}&\underset{\Delta\rightarrow 1}{\longrightarrow}(A,\text{cons})^{[100]}_{1,0}\oplus(B,1)_{2,0}^{[200]}\,,\\
\text{Long}_{\Delta,\ell}^{[000]}&\underset{\Delta\rightarrow\ell+1}{\longrightarrow}(A,\text{cons})^{[100]}_{\ell+1,\ell}\oplus(A,1)_{\ell+3/2,\ell-1/2}^{[100]}\quad\text{ for }\ell>0\,.\end{split}

(2.13)

From this we see that, unlike the other conserved current multiplets, the scalar conserved current multiplet recombines with a B-type multiplet, the
(B,1)_{2,0}^{[200]}

\mathcal{N}=6
CFT at infinite
c_{T}
is the double-trace operator
S^{[a}{}_{[b}S^{c]}{}_{d]}
, whose descendants are also double-traces of stress tensor operators. From this we deduce that

\begin{split}\nabla\cdot H_{1}(\vec{x},X)&=-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{c_{T}}}\Phi(\vec{x},X)+\text{fermion bilinears}+O(c_{T}^{-1})\\
\text{ with }\Phi(\vec{x},X)&=X^{a}{}_{b}\left(S^{b}{}_{c}(\vec{x})P^{c}{}_{a}(\vec{x})-P^{b}{}_{c}(\vec{x})S^{c}{}_{a}(\vec{x})\right)\,,\end{split}

(2.14)

and where

\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}\rangle=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi\sqrt{c_{T}}}\int d^{3}x\,\langle\Phi(\vec{x},X)\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}\rangle+\text{fermion bilinears}+O({c_{T}}^{-{3/2}})\,,

(2.15)

where we have left the regularization of the right-hand integral implicit.

We will begin by considering the case where
\mathcal{O}_{1}
,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
\mathcal{O}_{3}
are any three bosonic conserved currents. In this case,

(2.16)

We thus find that, at leading order in the

. This is a strong statement, allowing us to import statements about conserved currents and apply them to

Consider now the

\mathfrak{so}(6)\times\mathfrak{so}(6)

\begin{split}\lambda_{PP\mathcal{J}^{L}_{\ell}}&=\left(\frac{\lambda_{SSS}-\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{\lambda_{SSS}+\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}\right)\ell\lambda_{SS\mathcal{J}^{L}_{\ell}}\,,\qquad\lambda_{PP\mathcal{J}^{R}_{\ell}}=\left(\frac{\lambda_{SSS}+\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{\lambda_{SSS}-\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}\right)\ell\lambda_{SS\mathcal{J}^{R}_{\ell}}\,.\end{split}

(2.40)

We can then use the superconformal blocks (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to find that

\lambda_{PPH_{\ell}}=(-1)^{\ell}\ell\lambda_{SSH_{\ell}}\,,\qquad\lambda_{PPJ_{\ell}}=(-1)^{\ell+1}\ell\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell}}\,,

(2.41)

and, from (
2.22
), we see that

\displaystyle\lambda_{SS\mathcal{J}^{L}_{\ell}}=\lambda_{SSH_{\ell}}\cos\theta_{\ell}+\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell}}\sin\theta_{\ell}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\lambda_{SS\mathcal{J}^{R}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{SSH_{\ell}}\sin\theta_{\ell}+\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell}}\cos\theta_{\ell}

(2.42)

\displaystyle\lambda_{PP\mathcal{J}^{L}_{\ell}}=\lambda_{PPH_{\ell}}\cos\theta_{\ell}+\lambda_{PPJ_{\ell}}\sin\theta_{\ell}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\lambda_{PP\mathcal{J}^{R}_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{PPH_{\ell}}\sin\theta_{\ell}+\lambda_{PPJ_{\ell}}\cos\theta_{\ell}\,.

Combining (
2.40
), (
2.41
), and (
2.42
) together, we have 8 equations which are linear in the 8 OPE coefficients. Generically, the only solution to these equations will be the trivial one where all of OPE coefficients vanish. However, if we set

\theta_{\ell}=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{n\pi}{2}\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(2.43)

then we find that the equations become degenerate, allowing non-trivial solutions. By suitably redefining the conserved currents
H_{\ell}\rightarrow-H_{\ell}
we can always fix
n=0
so that
\lambda_{SSH_{\ell}}\geq 0
, and can then solve the equations to find that

\lambda_{SSH_{\ell}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda_{SSS}}{\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell}}&\ell\text{ is even}\\
\frac{\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{\lambda_{SSS}}\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell}}&\ell\text{ is odd}\,.\\
\end{cases}

(2.44)

To complete our derivation, we simply note that from the superblocks (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that

\lambda_{SSJ_{\ell+2}}=\lambda_{SSH_{\ell+1}}\,,\qquad\lambda_{SSJ_{1}}=\lambda_{SSS}\,,\qquad\lambda_{SSH_{1}}=\lambda_{SSB_{0}}\,,

(2.45)

so that

\lambda_{SSH_{\ell+1}}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{SSS}&\ell\text{ is even}\\
\lambda_{SSB_{0}}&\ell\text{ is odd}\,.\\
\end{cases}

(2.46)

Let us now apply (
2.46
) to two special cases: free field theory and parity preserving theories. In free field theory the higher spin currents remain conserved, so that
\alpha=0
and hence, using (
2.35
), we find that
\lambda_{SSB_{0}}=\lambda_{SSS}
. We thus find that each conserved current supermultiplet contributes equally to
\langle SSSS\rangle
.

For parity preserving theories, supersymmetry requires that
S
is a scalar but that
P
is a pseudoscalar. As we see from (
2.14
), the operator
H_{1}^{\mu}
eats a pseudoscalar, and so is also a pseudovector rather than a vector. Parity preservation then requires that
\lambda_{SSB_{0}}=0
, and so we conclude that for parity preserving theories only conserved current supermultiplet with odd spin contribute to
\langle SSSS\rangle
. Note that this does not apply to free field theory (which is parity preserving), because
H_{1}^{\mu}
remains short.

We will conclude by noting that, unlike all previous calculations in this section, the variations
\tilde{\delta}\langle SSP\rangle
and
\tilde{\delta}\langle SPB_{0}\rangle
involve the double trace operators in (
2.24
). In Appendix
A.2
we use these variations to compute
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, and find that

\kappa_{1}=\frac{\alpha\lambda_{SSS}}{4\sqrt{c_{T}}}\,,\qquad\kappa_{2}=-\frac{\alpha\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{4\sqrt{c_{T}}}\,.

(2.47)

2.4
The
\langle SSSS\rangle
Four-Point Function

In the previous section we showed that the OPE coefficient between two
S
operators and a conserved current is completely fixed by
H_{1}^{\mu}
pseudo-conservation in terms of
\lambda_{SSS}
and
\lambda_{SSB_{0}}
. Our task now is to work out the implications of this for the
\langle SSSS\rangle
four-point function.

Conformal and R-symmetry invariance imply that
[
8
]

\langle S(\vec{x}_{1},X_{1})S(\vec{x}_{2},X_{2})S(\vec{x}_{3},X_{3})S(\vec{x}_{4},X_{4})\rangle=\frac{1}{x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}{\cal S}^{i}(U,V){\cal B}_{i}\,,\\

(2.48)

where we define the R-symmetry structures

\begin{split}{\cal B}_{1}&=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{2})\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{3}X_{4})\,,\quad{\cal B}_{2}=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{3})\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{2}X_{4})\,,\quad{\cal B}_{3}=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{4})\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{2}X_{3})\,,\\
{\cal B}_{4}&=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{4}X_{2}X_{3})+\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{3}X_{2}X_{4}X_{1})\,,\quad{\cal B}_{5}=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4})+\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{4}X_{3}X_{2}X_{1})\,,\\
{\cal B}_{6}&=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{1}X_{3}X_{4}X_{2})+\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(X_{2}X_{4}X_{3}X_{1})\,,\end{split}

(2.49)

and where
{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(U,V)
are functions of the conformally-invariant cross-ratios

U\equiv\frac{x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}x_{24}^{2}}\,,\qquad V\equiv\frac{x_{14}^{2}x_{23}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}x_{24}^{2}}\,.

(2.50)

Crossing under
1\leftrightarrow 3
and
2\leftrightarrow 3
relates the different
{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(U,V)
:

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(U,V)&=U{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{U},\frac{V}{U}\right)\,,\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{3}(U,V)=\frac{U}{V}{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(V,U)\,,\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{5}(U,V)&=U{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{U},\frac{V}{U}\right)\,,\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{6}(U,V)=\frac{U}{V}{\mathcal{S}}^{4}(V,U)\,,\end{split}

(2.51)

so that
\langle SSSS\rangle
is uniquely specified by
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(U,V)
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{4}(U,V)
. The
{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(U,V)
furthermore satisfy certain differential equation imposed by the supersymmetric Ward identities, computed in
[
8
]
.

Another useful basis for the R-symmetry structures corresponds to the
\mathfrak{so}(6)

S\times S
tensor product

\begin{split}{\bf 15}\otimes{\bf 15}&={\bf 1}_{s}\oplus{\bf 15}_{a}\oplus{\bf 15}_{s}\oplus{\bf 20^{\prime}}_{s}\oplus({\bf 45}_{a}\oplus{\bf\overline{45}}_{a})\oplus{\bf 84}_{s}\,,\end{split}

(2.52)

where
s/a
denotes if the representation is in the symmetric/antisymmetric product. We define
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf{r}}
to receive contributions only from operators in the
s
-channel OPE that belong to
\mathfrak{so}(6)_{R}
irrep
\bf{r}
. This is related to the basis
{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(U,V)
by the equation
[
8
]

\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{5}\\
{\mathcal{S}}^{6}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-1&2&0&\frac{4}{15}\\

0&0&0&6&4&4\\
0&0&0&-6&0&4\\
0&1&1&-3&-1&-\frac{2}{3}\\
0&-1&1&-3&1&-\frac{2}{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 1}_{s}}\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 15}_{a}}\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 15}_{s}}\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 20}^{\prime}_{s}}\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 45}_{a}\oplus{\overline{\bf 45}}_{a}}\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\bf 84}_{s}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.53)

Each
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf r}(U,V)
can be expanded as a sum of conformal blocks

\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf r}(U,V)=\sum_{\text{conformal primaries ${\cal O}_{\Delta,\ell,{\bf r}}$}}\lambda^{2}_{SS\mathcal{O}}g_{\Delta,\ell}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(2.54)

where
{\cal O}_{\Delta,\ell,{\bf r}}
is a conformal primary in
S\times S
with scaling dimension
\Delta
, spin
\ell
, and
\mathfrak{so}(6)
irrep
\bf r
,
\lambda_{SS\mathcal{O}}
is its OPE coefficient, and
g_{\Delta,\ell}(U,V)
are conformal blocks normalized as in
[
41
]
. Note that due to
1\leftrightarrow 2
crossing symmetry, even (odd) spin operators contribute to
{\mathcal{S}}_{\bf r}
only if
{\bf r}
appears symmetrically (anti-symmetrically) in (

).

Our task is to write down the most general ansatz for
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(U,V)
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{4}(U,V)
compatible with both supersymmetry and with the constraints from weakly broken higher spin symmetry computed in the previous section. As shown in
[
22
]
using the Lorentzian inversion formula
[
21
]
,
{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(U,V)
is fully fixed by its double discontinuity up to a finite number contact interactions in AdS. More precisely, we can write:

\mathcal{S}^{i}(U,V)=\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{GFFT}}(U,V)+\frac{1}{c_{T}}\Big(\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{exchange}}(U,V)+\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{contact}}(U,V)\Big)+O(c_{T}^{-2})\,,

(2.55)

where the generalized free field theory correlator is

\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{GFFT}}(U,V)=\begin{pmatrix}1&U&\frac{U}{V}&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.56)

The
\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{exchange}}(U,V)
term is any CFT correlator with the same single trace exchanges as
\mathcal{S}^{i}(U,V)
, and with good Regge limit behavior so that the Lorentzian inversion formula holds. Finally,
\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{contact}}(U,V)
is a sum of contact interactions in AdS with at most six derivatives, which contribute to CFT data with spin two or less. We will focus on each of these two contributions in turn.

Let us begin with the exchange term. In higher spin
\mathcal{N}=6
theories the only single trace operators are conserved currents, and their contributions to
\langle SSSS\rangle
are fixed by the OPE coefficients computed in the previous section. Let us define the
s
-channel superconformal blocks

\begin{split}\mathfrak{G}_{\text{stress tensor}}^{i}(U,V)&=\mathfrak{g}_{S}^{i}(U,V)+\mathfrak{g}_{J_{1}}^{i}(U,V)+\mathfrak{g}_{T_{2}}^{i}(U,V)\,,\\

\begin{split}\frac{1}{c_{T}}\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{exchange}}(U,V)&=\lambda_{SSS}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{G}_{\text{stress tensor}}^{i}(U,V)+\sum_{\text{odd }\ell}\mathfrak{G}_{\text{cons},\ell}^{i}(U,V)\right)\\

(2.60)

where we define

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\text{free}}^{i}(U,V)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\frac{U}{\sqrt{V}}&\sqrt{\frac{U}{V}}&\sqrt{U}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

(2.61)

to be the connected correlator in the

is a correlator in a unitary CFT, it is guaranteed to have the necessary Regge behavior required for the Lorentzian inversion formula.

Having derived an expression for the sum of odd and even conserved current superblocks, let us turn to the difference. Note that for

,

,

, and

On their own, the difference of two conformal blocks does not have good Regge behavior. We can however replace these conformal blocks with scalar exchange diagrams in AdS. Such exchange diagrams do have good Regge behavior, and the only single trace operators that appears in their OPE have the same quantum numbers as the exchanged particle. Using the general scalar exchange diagram computed in

Now that we have an expression for the exchange terms, let us now turn to the contact terms. As already noted,

must be a sum of contact Witten diagrams that contribute to CFT data of spin two or less. Furthermore, because our theory is supersymmetric these contact Witten diagrams must also preserve

\mathcal{S}^{4}_{\text{cont}}(U,V)&=4U\left(\bar{D}_{1,1,1,3}(U,V)-\frac{4}{3}D_{1,1,2,2}(U,V)\right)\,.\end{split}

(2.66)

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}^{i}&(U,V)=\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\text{GFFT}}(U,V)\\

The

5
Discussion

The main result of this paper is the expression for tree level, i.e. leading large
c_{T}
,
\langle SSSS\rangle
for any higher spin
{\mathcal{N}}=6
theory in terms of just two free parameters. For the
U(N)_{k}\times U(N+M)_{-k}
and
SO(2)_{2k}\times USp(2+2M)_{-k}
ABJ theories, we used localization to fix these parameters in terms of
\lambda
, which is
M/k
for the former theory and
(M+1/2)/k
for the latter theory.
30
30

30

We find that the tree level result is independent of
N
.
We then successfully compared the CFT data extracted from this tree level correlator at
\lambda=1/2
to the large
c_{T}\sim M
regime of the conjectured non-perturbative numerical bootstrap solution to
\langle SSSS\rangle
for the
U(1)_{2M}\times U(1+M)_{-2M}
theory
[
28
]
. On the way to deriving these results, we derived superconformal Ward identities for
\langle SSSP\rangle
as well as an integrated relation between this correlator and
\partial_{m_{\pm}}^{3}\partial_{m_{-}}F\big|_{m_{\pm}=0}
which can be computed using supersymmetric localization.

It is instructive to compare our
\mathcal{N}=6
correlators
\langle SSSS\rangle
in (
1.1
) and
\langle PPPP\rangle
in (
1.3
) to the tree level correlator of the scalar single trace quasibosonic
{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}
and quasifermionic
{\mathcal{O}}_{qf}
operators for non-supersymmetric vector models in
[
22
]
:

\begin{split}\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}&(\vec{x}_{1}){\mathcal{O}}_{qb}(\vec{x}_{2}){\mathcal{O}}_{qb}(\vec{x}_{3}){\mathcal{O}}_{qb}(\vec{x}_{4})\rangle=\frac{1}{x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}}\frac{8}{c_{T}}\Bigg[\sqrt{U}+\sqrt{\frac{U}{V}}+\frac{U}{\sqrt{V}}\\
&-\frac{2}{\pi^{\frac{5}{2}}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\lambda_{qb}}{2}\right)\left(U\bar{D}_{1,1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(U,V)+U\bar{D}_{1,1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(V,U)+\bar{D}_{1,1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{U},\frac{V}{U}\big)\right)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(5.1)

and

\begin{split}\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{qf}(\vec{x}_{1})&{\mathcal{O}}_{qf}(\vec{x}_{2}){\mathcal{O}}_{qf}(\vec{x}_{3}){\mathcal{O}}_{qf}(\vec{x}_{4})\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{x_{12}^{4}x_{34}^{4}}\frac{2}{c_{T}}\Bigg[\frac{U^{2}(U-V-1)}{V^{3/2}}-\sqrt{U}(U-V+1)-\frac{\sqrt{{U}}(U+V-1)}{V^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(5.2)

For concreteness, we set
\frac{1}{\tilde{N}}=\frac{2}{c_{T}}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{qb}=\tan(\frac{\pi\lambda_{qb}}{2})
in
[
22
]
as in
[
51
]
for a
U(N_{qb})_{k_{qb}}
Chern-Simons matter theory with
\lambda_{qb}\equiv N_{qb}/k_{qb}
and one complex scalar for the quasibosonic case, or one complex fermion in the quasifermionic case. The quasibosonic case should be naturally compared to
\langle SSSS\rangle
, as both
S
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}
are scalars with
\Delta=1
at tree level, while the quasifermionic case should be compared to
\langle PPPP\rangle
, as both
P
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{qf}
are pseudoscalars with
\Delta=2
at tree level. For all cases, the contact terms allowed by the Lorentzian inversion formula vanish. For the quasiboson and
\langle SSSS\rangle
, the tree level correlator consists of a connected free theory term and a scalar exchange term, while for the quasifermion and
\langle PPPP\rangle
, only a connected free term appears. In our case both
\langle SSSS\rangle
and
\langle PPPP\rangle
depend on
\lambda
as
\sin^{2}(\pi\lambda)
, while in the nonsupersymetric case only the quasiboson depends on
\lambda_{qb}
, and has the slightly different periodicity
\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi\lambda_{qb}}{2})
.
31
31

31

The factor of two discrepancy in the periodicity between the ABJ case and the non-supersymmetric case is discussed in Section 6.2 of
[
6
]
.
For both the quasiboson and
\langle SSSS\rangle
, the exchange terms are given simply by scalar exchange Witten diagrams, though the physical origin is quite different in each case. In the quasibosonic case,
[
26
]
showed that for spin
\ell
single trace operators
J_{\ell}
, all tree level
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}J_{\ell}\rangle
were the same as the free theory except for
J_{0}\equiv{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}
, which depends on
\lambda_{qb}
. The scalar exchange then appears so as to compensate for the fact that tree level
\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}{\mathcal{O}}_{qb}\rangle
is not given by the free theory result. In our
\mathcal{N}=6
case, we found that the tree level three-point functions between two
S
’s and a higher spin multiplet were given by the free theory result only for odd
\ell
, while for even
\ell
they are all proportional to the same
\lambda
dependent coefficient. The contribution of the exchange diagrams for the even and odd spin single trace long multiplets, which at tree level coincide with conserved supermultiplets, exactly canceled so that only the scalar exchange diagrams remained.

We showed that the contact terms allowed by the Lorentzian inversion formula for
\langle SSSS\rangle
vanished by combining localization with the
\langle SSSP\rangle
four-point function computed using the weakly broken Ward identity. There is in fact a possible alternative argument that only uses
\mathcal{N}=6
superconformal symmetry, and so would apply to any
\mathcal{N}=6
higher spin theory. Note that
\mathcal{N}=6
superconformal symmetry only allows a single contact term with four or less derivatives, which thus contributes to spin two or less as allowed by the large
M
Lorentzian inversion formula
[
21
]
. In
[
8
]
, we used flat space amplitude arguments to show that this four derivative contact term for
\langle SSSS\rangle
actually becomes a six derivative contact term in other stress tensor multiplet correlators like
\langle SSJJ\rangle
, where
J
is the R-symmetry current, that are related to
\langle SSSS\rangle
by supersymmetry. Since six derivative contact terms generically contribute to spin three CFT data in correlators of non-identical operators
[
52
]
, they would be disallowed by the Lorentzian inversion formula for correlators with spin
[
53
]
, which would then disallow the putative four derivative
\langle SSSS\rangle
contact term. In fact, the
\langle SSSS\rangle
contact term contributes to a scalar long multiplet that contains a spin three descendant. This happens to not contribute to the
\langle SSSS\rangle
superblock
[
28
]
, but could well appear in the
\langle SSJJ\rangle
superblock. It would be interesting to derive the superconformal Ward identity that explicitly relates
\langle SSJJ\rangle
to
\langle SSSS\rangle
, so that we could verify this alternative argument for the vanishing of the contact term. Our tree level result would then just be fixed in terms of a single free parameter, as in the non-supersymmetric case of
[
22
,
23
]
.

There are several ways we could improve our comparison between tree level higher spin
\langle SSSS\rangle
and the numerical bootstrap results in
[
28
]
for the
U(1)_{2M}\times U(1+M)_{-2M}
theory. Numerically, it would be good to improve the precision of the numerics and compute predictions for more CFT data. In particular, we will likely need much higher precision to probe the single trace multiplets with superprimaries of odd spin, since their OPE coefficients squared scale as
O(c_{T}^{-1})
and so are hard to see numerically, unlike the even spin case that scales as
O(c_{T}^{0})
. Analytically, we will need to generalize the large
c_{T}
analytic calculation of
\langle SSSS\rangle
to order
1/c_{T}^{2}
if we want to extract anomalous dimensions of the odd single trace higher spin multiplets even at tree level, due to the
O(c_{T}^{-1})
scaling of their OPE coefficients squared. This 1-loop calculation can in principle be computed from tree level CFT data
[
54
]
, but would require one to unmix the double trace tree level CFT data, which is difficult even in the non-supersymmetric case
[
55
]
. This unmixing would similarly be required if we want to compare to numerical results for unprotected double trace operators with non-lowest twist, which are degenerate.

We could also generalize our tree level higher spin correlator calculation to a wider class of theories. For instance, the ABJ quadrality of
[
9
]
considered not just the 3d
\mathcal{N}=6

SO(2)_{2k}\times USp(2+2M)_{-k}
theory considered in this work, but the wider class of
\mathcal{N}=5

O(N_{1})_{2k}\times USp(N_{2})_{-k}
ABJ theories, which also have approximately broken higher spin symmetry when
N_{1},k
are large and
N_{2},\lambda\equiv N_{1}/k
are finite (similarly for
N_{1}\leftrightarrow N_{2}
), and so are conjecturally related to
\mathcal{N}=5
higher spin gravity on
AdS_{4}
. From the string theory perspective, these theories are obtained by orientifolding the brane construction of the
U(N)_{k}\times U(N+M)_{-k}
theory, so that the
O(N_{1})_{2k}\times USp(N_{2})_{-k}
theories are dual to type IIA string theory on
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In the string or M-theory limit, orientifolding changes the single trace spectrum, such that certain tree level correlators vanish, and the 1-loop corrections are suitably modified
[
56
]
. In the higher spin limit, however, the orientifold does not affect the single trace spectrum aside from reducing the supersymmetry when
N_{1}\neq 2
from
\mathcal{N}=6
to
\mathcal{N}=5
, so we expect that the general structure of the
\mathcal{N}=5
tree level correlator should be very similar to our
\mathcal{N}=6
result. The precise dependence on
\lambda
could still be different, as that depends on the Lagrangian of the specific theory, as well as the the specific form of the
\mathcal{N}=5
version of the
\mathcal{N}=6
integrated constraints discussed in this work. It is possible one might also need to consider integrated constraints involving the squashed sphere, which can also be computed using supersymmetric localization as in
[
57
,
42
,
58
,
10
]
.

The numerical bootstrap has been used so far to find conjectured numerical solutions to the
U(1)_{2M}\times U(1+M)_{-2M}
and
U(N)_{2}\times U(N+1)_{-2}
theories, which at large
c_{T}
are dual to higher spin gravity and M-theory, respectively. These
\mathcal{N}=6
and
\mathcal{N}=8

, which is related to a certain integral of

as shown in (

[

17
,

. Once we can study ABJ theory for any

N
and large

disappear as

All the discussion so far has concerned the CFT side of the higher spin AdS/CFT duality. This is mostly because supersymmetric higher spin gravity is still poorly understood. The only known formulation so far is in terms of Vasiliev theory

[

Pseudocharge Action on

Our task in this section is to derive the action of
\tilde{\delta}(X)
on the scalars
S

. In Appendix

we derive (
2.24
), which gives expressions for
\tilde{\delta}(X)S(y,Y)
and
\tilde{\delta}(X)P(y,Y)

A.1
Constraining the Pseudocharge Action

Our task is to derive (
2.24
). We begin with
\tilde{\delta}(X)S(\vec{y},Y)
, which we can compute by evaluating

\langle\tilde{\delta}(X)S(0,Y)\mathcal{O}(\hat{e}_{3})\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left.\int_{|x|=r}dS\cdot\left\langle H_{1}(\vec{x},X)S(0,Y)\mathcal{O}(\hat{e}_{3})\right\rangle\right|_{\text{finite as }r\rightarrow\infty}

(A.1)

for general operators
\mathcal{O}(\hat{e}_{3})
located at
\hat{e}_{3}=(0,0,1)
. We first note that the right-hand side of (
A.1
) is only non-zero if
\mathcal{O}
is a scalar with conformal dimension 1. For this special case, conformal invariance implies that

\begin{split}\left\langle H_{1}(\vec{x},X)S(0,Y)\mathcal{O}(\hat{e}_{3})\right\rangle&=f_{S\mathcal{O}H_{1}}(X,Y)\mathcal{C}_{1,1,1}^{\mu}(0,\hat{e}_{3},\vec{x})\\
&=\frac{f_{S\mathcal{O}H_{1}}(X,Y)}{4}\left(\frac{(\hat{e}_{3}-x)^{\mu}}{|\hat{e}_{3}-x|^{2}}-\frac{x^{\mu}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{|x||x-\hat{e}_{3}|}\,,\end{split}

(A.2)

where
f_{S\mathcal{O}H_{1}}(X,Y)
is a function of
X
and
Y
whose exact form depends on the
\mathfrak{so}(6)_{R}
properties of
\mathcal{O}
. Substituting this into (
A.1
), we find that

\langle\tilde{\delta}(X)S(0,Y)\mathcal{O}(\hat{e}_{3})\rangle=-\frac{1}{4}f_{S\mathcal{O}H_{1}}(X,Y)\,.

(A.3)

The only two dimension
1
scalars in higher spin
\mathcal{N}=6
theories are
S(\vec{y},Y)
itself and
B_{0}(\vec{x})
. For
S
, we apply (
A.3
) with
{\mathcal{O}}(\hat{e}_{3})=S(\hat{e}_{3},Z)
to find that

\begin{split}\langle\tilde{\delta}(X)S(0,Y)S(\hat{e}_{3},Z)\rangle&=-\frac{\lambda_{SSH_{1}}\text{tr}([Y,Z]X)}{4}=-\frac{\lambda_{SSH_{1}}}{4}\left\langle S(0,[X,Y])S(\hat{e}_{3},Z)\right\rangle\,,\end{split}

(A.4)

while for
B_{0}
we find that

\begin{split}\langle\tilde{\delta}(X)S(0,Y)B_{0}(\hat{e}_{3})\rangle&=-\frac{\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}\text{tr}(XY)}{4}=-\frac{\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}\text{tr}(XY)}{4}\langle B_{0}(0)B_{0}(\hat{e}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(A.5)

However, as we will now show,
\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}=0
. To see this, we compute:

\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle S(0,Y)SH_{1}\rangle=\langle\tilde{\delta}(X)S(0,Y)SH_{1}\rangle+\dots=-\frac{\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}\text{tr}(XY)}{4}\langle B_{0}(0)S_{0}H_{1}\rangle+\dots\,,

(A.6)

where the additional terms come from the variations of the second
S
and
H_{1}
, and from the multiplet recombination. Note that
\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle S(0,Y)SH_{1}\rangle
contains a term proportional to
\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}^{2}\text{tr}(XY)
. But it is straightforward to check that no additional term appears in either
\left\langle S(0,Y)\tilde{\delta}(X)\left(SH_{1}\right)\right\rangle
or in
\langle S\tilde{P}H_{1}\rangle
with the right
R
-symmetry structure needed to cancel such a contribution, and so conclude that
\lambda_{SB_{0}H_{1}}=0
. Having exhausted the possible operators that could appear in
\tilde{\delta}(X)S(y,Y)

\tilde{\delta}(X)S(\vec{y},Y)=-\frac{\lambda_{SSH_{1}}}{4}S(y,[X,Y])=-\frac{\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{4}S(y,[X,Y])\,.

(A.7)

We can constrain
\tilde{\delta}(X)P(\vec{y},Y)
in much the same way, except now we have to consider not only the single trace operators
P
and
C_{0}
, but also double-trace operators built from
S
and
B_{0}
. The most general expression we can write is

\begin{split}\tilde{\delta}(X)P(\vec{y},Y)&=\kappa_{0}P(\vec{y},[X,Y])+\kappa_{1}S^{2}(\vec{y},[X,Y])+\kappa_{2}SB_{0}(\vec{y},[X,Y])\\
&+\mu_{1}\text{tr}(XY)\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{y})+\mu_{2}\mathcal{O}_{2}(\vec{y},\{X,Y\})+\mu_{3}S(\vec{y},X)S(y,Y)\,,\end{split}

(A.8)

where
\mathcal{O}_{1}(y)
is some linear combination of
S^{a}{}_{b}S^{b}{}_{a}
and
B_{0}^{2}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is some linear combination of
P
,
S^{2}
and
SB_{0}
. By computing

\kappa_{0}=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{PPH_{1}}=\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{SSH_{1}}\,.

(A.9)

If we instead consider

\mu_{i}
are proportional to OPE coefficients

A.2
Computing
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}

We now compute
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
using the variations
\tilde{\delta}\langle SSP\rangle
and
\tilde{\delta}\langle SPB_{0}\rangle
. Let us begin with
\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle SSP\rangle
. As listed in equation (
2.8
), supersymmetry forces both

and
\langle SPP\rangle
to vanish. Expanding the left-hand side of the higher spin Ward identity, we thus find that

\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle SSP(y_{3},Y_{3})\rangle=\kappa_{1}\langle SSS^{2}(\vec{y}_{3},[X,Y_{3}])\rangle\,,

(A.10)

while expanding the right-hand side we instead find that

\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle SSP(y_{3},Y_{3})\rangle=-\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{c_{T}}}\langle SS\tilde{S}(\vec{y}_{3},[X,Y_{3}])\rangle\,.

(A.11)

Equating these two expressions, we conclude that

\kappa_{1}=\frac{\alpha\lambda_{SSS}}{4\sqrt{c_{T}}}\,.

(A.12)

The variation
\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle SB_{0}P\rangle
is a little trickier, as

does not vanish at

. It is instead related by supersymmetry to the three-point function

\frac{\lambda_{SPH_{1}}}{\lambda_{SPB_{0}}}=\sqrt{\frac{2(\Delta+1)}{\Delta}}\underset{\Delta\rightarrow 1}{\longrightarrow}2\,,

where
\Delta

\lambda_{SPH_{1}}
to

using the multiplet recombination formula (

), and so find that

\lambda_{SPB_{0}}=-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{SPH_{1}}=\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{c_{T}}}\,.

(A.14)

Now that we have computed
\lambda_{SPB_{0}}
, let us turn to
\tilde{\delta}\langle SPB_{0}\rangle
. Expanding this using (
2.24
), we find that

\begin{split}\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle&S(\vec{y}_{1},Y_{1})P(\vec{y}_{2},Y_{2})B_{0}(\vec{y}_{3})\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{SSB_{0}}\langle S(\vec{y}_{1},Y_{1})P(\vec{y}_{2},[X,Y_{2}])B_{0}(\vec{y}_{3})\rangle+\kappa_{2}\langle S(\vec{y}_{1},Y_{1})SB(\vec{y}_{2},[X,Y_{2}])B_{0}(\vec{y}_{3})\rangle\,.\end{split}

(A.15)

But if we instead use the multiplet recombination rule, we find that

\tilde{\delta}(X)\langle S(\vec{y}_{1},Y_{1})P(\vec{y}_{2},Y_{2})B_{0}(\vec{y}_{3})\rangle=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{c_{T}}}\left\langle S(\vec{y}_{1},Y_{1})\tilde{S}(\vec{y}_{2},[X,Y_{2}])B_{0}(\vec{y}_{3})\right\rangle\,,

(A.16)

where we dropped

\kappa_{2}=-\frac{\alpha\lambda_{SSB_{0}}}{4\sqrt{c_{T}}}\,.

(A.17)

\bar{D}_{1,\frac{1}{2},1,\frac{1}{2}}(U,V)&=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\sqrt{\pi}U^{m}(1-V)^{n}\Gamma\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma(2m+2n+1)}{4^{m+n}m!n!\Gamma\left(2m+n+\frac{3}{2}\right)}\Big(2\psi\left(2m+n+\frac{3}{2}\right)\\
&-2\psi(2m+2n+1)-\psi\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\psi(m+1)+\log(4)\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\log U\Bigg]\,,\\
\end{split}

(E.3)

where
\psi(x)
is the Digamma function, and note the
\log U
dependence in the last two expressions. It is then straightforward to compare these expansions to the superblock expansion around
U\sim 0
and
V\sim 1
to get the CFT data given in the main text.

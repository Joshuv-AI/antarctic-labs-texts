Abstract
. In a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} over time interval ( 0 , T ) (0,T) , we consider mean field game equations whose principal coefficients depend on the time and state variables with a general Hamiltonian. We attach the non-zero Robin boundary condition. We first prove the Lipschitz stability in Ω × ( ε , T − ε ) \Omega\times(\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon) with given ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 for the determination of the solutions by Dirichlet data on arbitrarily chosen subboundary of ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . Next we prove the Lipschitz stability for an inverse problem of determining spatially varying factors of source terms and a coefficient by extra boundary data and spatial data at an intermediate time.

with the homogeneous Robin boundary condition

(1.2)

\nabla u\cdot\nu=\nabla(av)\cdot\nu=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$},

and given
u(\cdot,T)
and
v(\cdot,0)
in

We mainly consider a linearized equation of (
1.1
), which is formulated
as follows.

We set

\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq d
and

\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
and for
\gamma:=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{d})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{d}
we define

\partial_{x}^{\gamma}:=\partial_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\partial_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}
and

|\gamma|:=\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}
,
and introduce the following functional spaces

\displaystyle C^{1,0}(\overline{Q}):=\{u\in C(\overline{Q});\,\nabla u\in C(\overline{Q})\},\quad C^{1}(\overline{Q}):=\{u\in C(\overline{Q});\,\nabla u,\partial_{t}u\in C(\overline{Q})\},

\displaystyle C^{2,1}(\overline{Q}):=\{u\in C(\overline{Q});\,\nabla u,\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u,\partial_{t}u\in C(\overline{Q}),\,\,1\leq i,j\leq d\},

\displaystyle H^{2,1}(Q):=\{u\in L^{2}(Q);\,\nabla u,\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u,\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(Q),\,\,1\leq i,j\leq d\}\quad\mbox{with the norm}:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2,1}(Q)}:=\left(\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Throughout this article, we consider the following partial differential
operators of the second order with
(x,t)
- dependent coefficients defined
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&A(t)u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x,t)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}u+a_{0}(x,t)u,\\
&B(t)v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(x,t)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}v+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{j}(x,t)\partial_{j}v+b_{0}(x,t)v,\\
&A_{0}(t)u:=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq 2}\widetilde{a}_{\gamma}(x,t)\partial_{x}^{\gamma}u,\end{array}\right.

where

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&a_{ij},b_{ij}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q}),\quad a_{ij}=a_{ji},\,\,b_{ij}=b_{ji}\quad\mbox{ for}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq d,\cr\\
&a_{k},b_{k}\in L^{\infty}(Q)\quad\mbox{ for}\quad 0\leq k\leq d,\quad\widetilde{a}_{\gamma}\in L^{\infty}(Q)\quad\mbox{for}\quad|\gamma|\leq 2,\end{array}\right.

and it is assumed that there exists a constant

\chi>0
such that

(1.4)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\chi\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\chi\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{j}^{2}

for all
(x,t)\in Q
and
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
.

We define

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(\partial_{j}u)\nu_{i},\quad\partial_{\nu_{B}}v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{ij}(\partial_{j}v)\nu_{i}\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

We consider a linearized mean field game system:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u+A(t)u=c_{0}(x,t)v+F(x,t),\\
&\partial_{t}v-B(t)v=A_{0}(t)u+G(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q\end{array}\right.

with the Robin boundary condition

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{\nu_{A}}u(x,t)-p(x,t)u(x,t)=g(x,t),\\
&\partial_{\nu_{B}}v(x,t)-q(x,t)v(x,t)=h(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{array}\right.

where we assume

(1.7)

p,q\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega\times[0,T])\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{0}\in L^{\infty}(Q).

We emphasize that general Hamiltonians also can be considered. Such Hamiltonians produce
the second-order partial differential operator

A_{0}(t)u
after the linearization.

Our key is a classical Carleman estimate for a single parabolic equation
with singular weight function by Imanuvilov
[
8
]
.
The linearized mean field game equations (
1.5
) have two features:

•

The equation in
u
is backward and the one in
v
is forward.

•

The equation in
v
contains the second-order spatial derivatives

A_{0}(t)u
of
u
.

The mixed forward and backward equations in (
1.5
),
makes the forward problem such as initial boundary
value problem difficult, but thanks to the symmetry of the time
variable in the weight function, this does not matter for
Carleman estimates, our main tool.

Systems with coupled principal parts usually cause
difficulty for establishing relevant Carleman estimates.
However, in our case, although the second equation in (
1.5
) is
coupled with the second-order terms
A_{0}(t)u
of
u
, the first one in
(
1.5
) is coupled only
with zeroth order term of
v
, which enables us to execute a typical argument
of absorbing the second-order terms of
u
by taking large parameters
of the Carleman estimate, as is described in Section 2.

This article is composed of five sections. In Section
2
, we prove
a Carleman estimate (Theorem
2.5
) for the linearized mean field game
equations and complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
4
, we
consider the state determination problem for the original nonlinear
mean value field equations (
1.1
).
Section
5
is devoted to the proof of Lemma

: the key Carleman
estimate for the case
of the non-homogeneous Robin boundary condition.

2.
Carleman estimate for the mean field game equations and the proof
of Theorem
1.1

Let
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
be an arbitrarily given subboundary.
We choose a subboundary
\Gamma_{1}\subset\partial\Omega
such that

\overline{\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma}\subset\Gamma_{1}
. Then it is known
(e.g., Fursikov and Imanuvilov
[
6
]
, Imanuvilov
[
8
]
) that
there exists a function
\eta\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\eta(x)>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad\eta|_{\Gamma_{1}}=0,\quad\nabla\eta\neq 0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\overline{\Omega}.

Let function
\mu=\mu(t)
satisfy

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\mu\in C^{\infty}[0,T],\quad\mu(t)=t^{2}\quad\mbox{for $0\leq t\leq\frac{T}{4}$},\\
&\mbox{$\mu(t)$ is monotone increasing in $\left[0,\,\frac{T}{2}\right]$},\quad\mu(t)=\mu(T-t)\quad\mbox{for $0\leq t\leq T$}.\end{array}\right.

For arbitrarily chosen sufficiently large constant
\lambda>0
, we set

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{\mu(t)},\quad\alpha(x,t)=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{2\lambda||\eta||_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}}{\mu(t)},\quad(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times(0,T).

We recall the assumptions (
1.3
) and (
1.7
), and
further set

(2.2)

M:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}||a_{ij}||_{C^{1}(\overline{Q})}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}||a_{j}||_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+||c_{0}||_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\quad M_{0}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}||a_{ij}||_{C^{1}(\overline{Q})}.

We consider a
boundary value problem

(2.3)

\partial_{t}u+A(t)u=F\quad\mbox{in $Q$}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\partial_{t}u-B(t)u=F\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

and

(2.4)

\partial_{\nu_{A}}u-p(x,t)u=g\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.

Now we state the key Carleman estimate for a parabolic equation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and
p\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega\times[0,T])
.
We choose sufficiently large
\lambda>0
.
Let

,

Then there exist constants

and

such that

(2.5)

\int_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{{s}\varphi}\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|^{2}\right)+s\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|u|^{2}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\biggl(\int_{Q}|F|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt+\int_{(\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma)\times(0,T)}\left(\frac{|\partial_{t}g|^{2}}{s^{2}\varphi^{2}}+\frac{1}{\root \of{s\varphi}}|g|^{2}\right)e^{2{s}\alpha}dSdt+\|ge^{s\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}\biggr)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{\Gamma\times(0,T)}\left(s\varphi|\nabla u|^{2}+s^{3}\varphi^{3}|u|^{2}+\frac{|\partial_{t}u|^{2}}{s\varphi}\right)e^{2s\alpha}dSdt

) and (

,

,

Here and henceforth, by

In the case where

Moreover, we set

\|g\|_{*}:=\|g\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma))}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}.

We note

\partial_{i}\varphi=\lambda(\partial_{i}\eta)\varphi,\quad\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi=(\lambda\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\eta+\lambda^{2}(\partial_{i}\eta)(\partial_{j}\eta))\varphi,

&|\nabla\alpha|\leq C\varphi,\quad|\partial_{t}\alpha|\leq C\varphi^{2}\quad\mbox{in $Q$}\cr\end{array}\right.

In order to rewrite the norms appearing in (

.

\sup_{s\geq 1}\sup_{(x,t)\in Q}|\varphi(x)^{\rho}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}|<\infty.

and

\|\varphi^{\rho}\psi ge^{s\alpha}\|_{*}\leq C_{\rho}s\|g\|_{*}.

.

(i) First we have

On the other hand, for

where

C_{1}:=2(e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}-e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}})>0.

Noting that
\xi:=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}
,
t\in(0,T)
varies
[c_{0},\infty)

with some constant

c_{0}>0
and
\sup_{\xi\geq c_{0}}\xi^{\rho}e^{-C_{1}\xi}<\infty
,
we see (

) for all
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii) By (

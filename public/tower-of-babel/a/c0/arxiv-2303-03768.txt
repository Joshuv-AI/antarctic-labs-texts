Weyl sums with multiplicative coefficients and joint equidistribution
Matteo Bordignon, Cynthia Bortolotto, Bryce Kerr

Abstract
. In this paper we generalize a result of Montgomery and Vaughan regarding exponential sums with multiplicative coefficients to the setting of Weyl sums. As applications, we establish a joint equidistribution result for roots of polynomial congruences and polynomial values and obtain some new results for mixed character sums.



1.
Introduction

Let
A\geqslant 1
and
f
a multiplicative function satisfying
|f(p)|\leqslant A
for any prime
p
and
\sum_{n\leq N}|f(n)|^{2}\leqslant A^{2}N
for all natural numbers
N
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
set

S(\alpha):=\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(\alpha n),

where
e(x)=\exp(2\pi ix)
.

These sums first appear to be considered by Daboussi
[
11
]
, who showed that if
|\alpha-a/q|\leqslant 1/q^{2}
,
(a,q)=1
and
3\leqslant q\leqslant(N/\log N)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then

S(\alpha)\ll\frac{N}{(\log\log q)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

and implied constant depending only on
A
.

This result was improved by Montgomery and Vaughan
[
32
, Corollary 1]
who show that assuming
|\alpha-a/q|\leqslant 1/q^{2}
,
(a,q)=1
and
2\leqslant R\leqslant q\leqslant N/R
, we have

(1)

S(\alpha)\ll\frac{N}{\log N}+\frac{N(\log R)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

We refer the reader to
[
32
, Section 7]
for a demonstration that the term
N/\log{N}
is sharp.

The optimal dependence on
R
in (
1
) is an open problem and has been the subject of a number of works, see for example
[
1
,
4
]
, and it is expected the estimate (
1
) may be improved to

(2)

S(\alpha)\ll\frac{N}{\log N}+\frac{N}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Recently, Bretéche and Granville
[
5
]
have studied in detail the sums
S(\alpha)
on minor arcs. Their estimates suggest the following conjecture (see
[
5
, Equation 1.4]
)

S(\alpha)\ll\frac{N}{\log N}+\frac{N}{q^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\beta|x)},\quad\alpha=\frac{a}{q}+\beta,\quad(a,q)=1.

We also note that
[
5
]
contains some nice applications to circle-method type problems.

The estimate (
1
) has important applications to Dirichlet L-functions. For example, Montgomery and Vaughan
[
32
]
have shown how it may be combined with the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) to obtain a sharp upper bound for Dirichlet L-functions at the point
s=1
. One may also combine (
1
) with progress around the Burgess bound to obtain unconditional variants of Montgomery and Vaughan’s result and we refer the reader to
[
14
,
18
,
19
]
for progress in this direction.

Since the work of Montgomery and Vaughan, exponential sums with multiplicative coefficients have appeared in a number of different contexts and a variety of techniques have been developed to facilitate the reduction to exponential sums over bilinear forms. Some examples include Karatsuba’s work on short Kloosterman sums
[
24
]
, which has been refined by Korolev, see for example
[
26
]
. Bourgain, Sarnak and Ziegler
[
2
]
have established a finite version of Vinogradov’s bilinear sum inequality. Gong and Jia
[
15
]
have considered shifted character sums with multiplicative coefficients and Korolev and Shparlinski
[
27
]
dealt with sums over trace functions with multiplicative coefficients.

In this paper, we revisit the approach of Montgomery and Vaughan and generalise into the setting of sums of the form

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)e(g(n)),

where
g
is a polynomial with real coefficients and
f
is a multiplicative function satisfying

\displaystyle f(p)=O(1),\quad\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|=O(N),\quad\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|^{2}=O(N(\log{N})^{A}),

for any
A\geq 0
.

Problems of this sort have previously been considered by Jiang, Lü and Wang
[
23
]
, who showed that one may replace an assumption on the
\ell_{2}
norm

\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|^{2}\ll N,

with an assumption of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant N\\
p\ \text{prime}\end{subarray}}|f(p)||f(p+h)|\ll\frac{h}{\phi(h)}\frac{N}{(\log{N})^{2}}.

Such a relaxation is significant in the context of
\text{GL}_{m}

L
-functions in the absence of progress towards the Ramanujan conjectures.

Matthiesen
[
29
]
has considered sums of the form (
3
) over polynomial nilsequences with slightly weaker conditions on
f
than Montgomery and Vaughan. These results were later applied to linear correlations of multiplicative functions
[
30
]
. We also mention the recent work
[
28
]
which considers exponential sums with multiplicative functions over nilsequences on average over short intervals.

2.
Main results

Our first result is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A>0
and
c>0
be real numbers and
f
be a multiplicative function satisfying

(4)

\displaystyle|f(p)|\leqslant C\quad\text{for each prime $p$},

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|=O(N),

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|^{2}=O(N(\log{N})^{A}).

Let
F
be a polynomial of degree
d\geqslant 1
with real coefficients given by

F(x)=\alpha_{d}x^{d}+\dots+\alpha_{1}x.

Let
R\geqslant 1
and suppose there exist integers
l,a,q
with
1\leqslant q\leqslant R
and
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant d
,
(a,q)=1
and

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{\ell}-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{Rq},

Denote
C=\frac{A}{2r}
. Then for any
r>d(d+1)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(F(n))

\displaystyle\ll N\left(\frac{1}{(\log{N})^{1-C}}+(\log{N})^{C}\left(\frac{q}{N^{\ell}}+\frac{1}{q}\right)^{1/4r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+(NR^{1/\ell})^{1/2},

where the implied constant depends on
A
and
r
.
In particular, if we suppose that

(\log{N})^{4r^{2}}\leqslant q\leqslant\frac{N^{\ell}}{(\log{N})^{4r^{2}}},

then

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(F(n))\ll\frac{N}{(\log{N})^{1-C}}.

To demonstrate the precision of the above estimate, in Section
10
we prove that for any polynomial
F
and for all
N
, there exists
f=f_{F,N}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(F(n))\right|\geqslant\frac{1}{10}\frac{N}{\log{N}}.

The proof of Theorem
1
follows the outline of Montgomery and

Vaughan
[
32
]
and starts with a combinatorial decomposition of multiplicative functions based on Möbius inversion and reduces the problem to estimating bilinear forms over polynomials with summation restricted to points under the hyperbola. We then use Montgomery and Vaughan’s partition of the parabola into disjoint rectangles to which techniques related to the Vinogradov Mean Value theorem may be applied. It will be fundamental to develop a version of Vinogradov Mean Value theorem for primes in large translated intervals, this is Lemma
5
. We should note that we introduced the general condition (
6
) with the aiming of using Theorem
1
in the proof of Theorem
2
.

3.
Applications

3.1.
Joint equidistribution

As an application of Theorem
1
, we prove a joint distribution result. Throughout this section, we let
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
of degree
e\geqslant 2
and we consider the ratios
v/n
, where
v
are the roots of the polynomial
p
modulo
n

\displaystyle p(v)\equiv 0\bmod{n}.

Consider the sequence
(g_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
of these ratios so that the corresponding denominators are in ascending order.
Hooley proved, in
[
22
, Theorem 2]
, that this sequence is equidistributed in
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We now let
F(x)=\alpha_{1}x+\ldots+\alpha_{d}x^{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
with
d\geqslant 1
and with an irrational coefficient and define

\displaystyle A(F,p)_{k}=(g_{k},F(k^{\prime}))_{k\geqslant 1},

where
g_{k}
is as above and for
g_{k}=v/n
,
p(v)\equiv 0\bmod{n}
, we take
k^{\prime}=n
. We prove the following

Theorem 2
.

The sequence
(A(F,p)_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is equidistributed in
({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
.

This indicates that the sequence
(F(n))_{n\in\geqslant 1}
is somehow not correlated with the sequence
(g_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}.

3.2.
Mixed character sums

We next explain how Theorem
1
is related to sums considered by Enflo
[
12
]
, Chang
[
7
]
and Heath-Brown and Pierce
[
17
]
.
Theorem
1
implies large exponential sums must correspond to pretentious multiplicative functions.

Corollary 1
.

With notation and conditions as in Theorem
1
, let
r\geqslant d(d+1)
and suppose that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(F(n))\right|\gg\frac{N}{(\log{N})^{1-A/2r}}.

There exists an integer

k\leqslant(\log{N})^{d(4r^{2}+4rA)},

and a multiplicative character
\psi\mod{k}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(F(n))\ll(\log{N})^{4r^{2}+4rA}\max_{u\leqslant N}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant u}\psi(n)f(n)\right|.

Corollary
1
implies one may bound character sums mixed by polynomials by reducing to pure character sums, we refer the reader to Section
8
for more precise results.

Corollary 2
.

Let
F(n)
a polynomial of degree
d
with real coefficients and
\chi
a primitive characters modulo
q
. Suppose that
\delta,\varepsilon
satisfy

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\ll(\log{q})^{100d^{3}}\\
\psi\mod{k}\\
u\leqslant N\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant u}\psi(n)\chi(n)\right|\leqslant Nq^{-\varepsilon}\quad\text{provided}\quad N\geqslant q^{\delta}.

Then we have

(8)

\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\ll\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1-1/d(d+1)}}.

In particular, the estimate (
8
) holds under the following conditions:

•

For arbitrarily small
\delta
assuming the Generalised Riemann hypothesis

•

For
\delta=1/3
and arbitrary integer
q
, which follows from the Burgess bound, see for example
[
16
]
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be small. Enflo
[
12
]
has previously established that

\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\ll N^{1-\delta},\quad\text{provided}\quad N\geqslant q^{1/4+\varepsilon},

and we refer the reader to
[
7
,
17
]
for quantitative improvements on Enflo’s result.
Chang
[
8
]
has shown

\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\ll N^{1-\delta},\quad\text{provided}\quad N\geqslant q^{\varepsilon},

provided

Corollary
2
provides some new instances where one may bound mixed character sums nontrivially. We refer the reader to Section
8
for more details.

4.1.
Reduction to bilinear forms

We proceed in a similar fashion to
[
32
, Section 2]
, which reduces the proof of Theorem
1
to bounding bilinear
forms under the hyperbola.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f
be a multiplicative function satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1
,
g
be any real valued function and let
\epsilon>0
. Then for any integer
N
we have

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant N}f(n)e(g(n))\right|\ll\\
&\frac{N}{\log{N}^{1-\epsilon}}+\frac{1}{\log{N}}\left|\sum_{1\leqslant np\leqslant N}f(n)f(p)(\log{p})e(g(np))\right|.\end{split}

Proof.

We follow the argument from
[
32
, Section 2]
with some modifications to deal with the condition

\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|^{2}\ll N(\log{N})^{A}.

Consider

S=\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)e(g(n))\log N/n.

Since

S=\log{N}\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)e(g(n))-\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)(\log{n})e(g(n)),

it is sufficient to show

(10)

\displaystyle S\ll_{\epsilon}N(\log{N})^{\epsilon},

and

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)

\displaystyle(\log{n})e(g(n))\ll_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{1\leqslant np\leqslant N}f(n)f(p)(\log{p})e(g(np))\right|+N(\log{N})^{\epsilon}.

Let
r
be a large real number and apply Hölder’s inequality, (
5
) and (
6
) to get

\displaystyle|S|^{2r}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{n\leqslant N}(\log{N/n})^{2r}\right)\left(\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|\right)^{2r-2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{n\leqslant N}(\log{N/n})^{2r}\right)N^{2r-1}(\log{N})^{A}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}(\log{N/n})^{2r}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\\
1\leqslant 2^{j}\leqslant N\end{subarray}}j^{2r}\sum_{N/2^{j+1}\leqslant n\leqslant N/2^{j-1}}1

\displaystyle\ll N\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\frac{j^{2r}}{2^{j}}\ll N,

we obtain (
10
) after taking
r
sufficiently large.
Since

\log n=\sum_{d|n}\Lambda(d),

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)(\log{n})e(g(n))

\displaystyle=\sum_{nm\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)f(nm)e(g(nm))

\displaystyle=\sum_{mn\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)f(n)f(m)e(g(nm))

\displaystyle+O\left(\sum_{mn\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)|f(nm)-f(n)f(m)|\right).

Using (
4
) and (
5
)

\displaystyle\sum_{mn\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)f(n)f(m)e(g(nm))

\displaystyle=\sum_{pn\leqslant N}(\log{p})f(p)f(n)e(g(nm))

\displaystyle+O\left(N\sum_{k\geqslant 2}\sum_{p^{k}\leqslant N}\frac{|f(p^{k})|}{p^{k}}.\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{pn\leqslant N}(\log{p})f(p)f(n)e(g(nm))

\displaystyle+O(N),

since the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
6
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant 2}\sum_{p^{k}\leqslant N}\frac{|f(p^{k})|}{p^{k}}\ll\left(\sum_{k\geqslant 2}\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p^{0.8k}}\right)\left(\sum_{n}\frac{|f(n)|^{2}}{n^{1.2}}\right)=O(1).

Hence it remains to show

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{mn\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)|f(nm)-f(n)f(m)|=O(N(\log{N})^{\epsilon}).

From (
5
)

\displaystyle\sum_{mn\leqslant N}\Lambda(m)|f(nm)-f(n)f(m)|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\sum_{p^{k}\leqslant N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant N/p^{k}\\
p|n\end{subarray}}|f(p^{k})||f(n)|+|f(p^{k}n)|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k,j\geqslant 1}\sum_{p^{k+j}\leqslant N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant N/p^{k+j}\\
(n,p)=1\end{subarray}}(|f(p^{k+j})|+|f(p^{k})||f(p^{j})|)|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll N\sum_{k,j\geqslant 1}\sum_{p}\frac{|f(p^{k})||f(p^{j})|+|f(p^{k+j})|}{p^{k+j}}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
6
) and partial summation to summation over
p,k,j
as above, we establish (
12
) which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let notation and conditions be as in Theorem
1
. Suppose
s
is a parameter and for each

write

\displaystyle J_{i}=\min\left(i+1,\lfloor\log_{2}{N}\rfloor-i+1,\lfloor\log_{2}(64N/s)/2\rfloor\right).

Define rectangles

(14)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{i}=(0,2^{i}]\times\left(\frac{N}{2^{i+1}},\frac{N}{2^{i}}\right],\quad 0\leqslant i\leqslant\log_{2}{N},

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{R}}_{i,j,k}=&\left(\frac{2^{i+j}}{k},\frac{2^{i+j+1}}{2k-1}\right]\times\left(\frac{(k-1)N}{2^{i+j}},\frac{(2k-1)N}{2^{i+j+1}}\right],\\
&0\leqslant i\leqslant\log_{2}{N},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant J_{i},\quad 2^{j-1}\leqslant k\leqslant 2^{j}.\end{split}

Then each

is a rectangle of the form
(P,P^{\prime}]\times(N,N^{\prime}],
with

satisfying

\displaystyle P^{\prime}-P\geqslant\frac{1}{4},\quad N^{\prime}-N\geqslant\frac{1}{4},\quad(P^{\prime}-P)(N^{\prime}-N)\gg s.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
denote the set of points
(p,n)
satisfying

which do not lie in any of the rectangles (
14
) or (
15
). Then, for any fixed
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}}f(p)(\log{p})

\displaystyle f(n)e(g(pn))\ll

\displaystyle\ll(\log N)^{\epsilon}(N+(Ns)^{1/2}\log({2N/s})\log{s}).

Proof.

Our proof is similar to that of
[
32
, Section 3]
with some minor modifications. We first partition

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}}f(p)(\log{p})f(n)e(g(pn))\ll\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})

\displaystyle+\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})+\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p}),

4

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})\right)^{2r}\ll

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}|f(n)|\right)^{2r-2}\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}|f(n)|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}(\log{p})^{2r}\right).

For each prime
p

\displaystyle\#\{n\ :\ (p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\}\ll\frac{N}{p^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}(\log{p})^{2r}\ll N\sum_{p}\frac{(\log{p})^{2r}}{p^{2}}\ll N,

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{n\leqslant N}|f(n)|\ll N,

Consider next
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})\right)^{2}\ll\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}|f(n)|^{2}\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}(\log{p})^{2}.

Finally consider
{\mathcal{E}}_{3}
. By Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})\right)^{2r}\ll

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N}{s}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant p\leqslant(Ns)^{1/2},

and for each

Using the Brun-Titchmarsh inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})\right)^{2r}\ll(Ns)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{(N/s)^{1/2}\leqslant n\leqslant(Ns)^{1/2}}|f(n)|^{2r/(2r+1)}\frac{\log{2N/n}}{n\log{2Ns/n^{2}}}\right)^{2r-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{(N/s)^{1/2}\leqslant p\leqslant(Ns)^{1/2}}\frac{\log{p}}{p}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{(N/s)^{1/2}\leqslant p\leqslant(Ns)^{1/2}}\frac{\log{p}}{p}\ll\log{s},

\displaystyle\ll(\log(2N/s))^{2r-1}\left(\sum_{(N/s)^{1/2}\leqslant n\leqslant(Ns)^{1/2}}\frac{|f(n)|}{n}\right)^{2r-2}

Which after taking

sufficiently large gives

\displaystyle\sum_{(p,n)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{3}}|f(p)||f(n)|(\log{p})\ll(Ns)^{1/2}(\log{N})^{\varepsilon}(\log{s})\log{(2N/s)}.

Sums over bilinear forms

5.1.
The Vinogradov mean value theorem

Given integers
r,d,V
we let
J_{r,d}(V)
count the number of solutions to the system of equations

\displaystyle v_{1}^{j}+\dots-v_{2r}^{j}=0,\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant d,

with variables satisfying

1\leqslant v_{1},\dots,v_{2r}\leqslant V.

We will use a consequence of Bourgain, Demeter and Guth’s work on the Vinogradov mean value theorem, see
[
3
, Section 5]
.

Lemma 3
.

Assume
d\geqslant 2
and
r>d(d+1)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle J_{r,d}(V)\ll_{k}V^{2r-d(d+1)/2}.

Combining Lemma
3
with the fact that the Vinogradov system is translation invariant, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3
.

Let
d\geqslant 2
,
r>d(d+1)
be integers and
{\mathcal{M}}(k),1\leqslant k\leqslant K
disjoint intervals satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(k)=(M^{\prime}(k),M^{\prime\prime}(k)],\quad M^{\prime\prime}(k)-M^{\prime}(k)\leqslant Y.

Then the number of solutions to the system of equations

(16)

\displaystyle n_{1}^{j}+\dots-n_{2r}^{j}=0,\quad n_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}(k),\quad 1\leqslant k\leqslant K,

is bounded by

O(KY^{2r-d(d+1)/2}).

We will also require an estimate for the number of solutions to the Vinogradov system with prime variables in translated intervals, here it will be fundamental that the intervals will not be ’too short’.
To obtain such a result we need the following intermediary lemma that follows directly from
[
21
, Theorem 10]
, here appears clear why it is important that the intervals we work with are quite large compared to their starting point. We use
L=\log P
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
0<Q\leqslant c_{1}(k)L^{\sigma_{1}}
,
X\gg 1
and

S(X,P)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<p\leqslant X+P\\
p\equiv t\pmod{Q}\end{subarray}}e(f(p))

in which

P\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{M}},

for any
M\gg 1
and

f(x)=\frac{h}{q}x^{k}+\alpha_{1}x^{k-1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k},\quad(h,q)=1,

the number
\alpha
being real. Suppose that
L^{\sigma}<q\leqslant P^{k}L^{-\sigma}
. For arbitrary
\sigma_{0}>0
, when
\sigma\leqslant 2^{6k}(\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{1}+1)
, we always have

|S(X,P)|\leqslant c_{2}(k)\frac{P}{QL^{\sigma_{0}-M}}.

This lemma allows us to prove the following estimate for the number of solutions to the Vinogradov system with prime variables in large translated intervals.

Lemma 5
.

Let
d\geqslant 2
be an integer and
X,Y\gg 1
. If
r>d(d+1)
, the number of solutions to the equation

\displaystyle p_{1}^{j}+\dots-p_{2r}^{j}=0,\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant d,\quad Y\leqslant p_{i}\leqslant Y+X,

\displaystyle X\gg\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{M}},\quad p_{i}\quad\text{prime},

for any
M\gg 1
, is bounded by

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{X^{2r-d(d+1)/2}}{(\log{X})^{2r}}\right).

Proof.

The result follows in a straightforward way from
[
21
, Theorem 16]
re-defining in part 1)

S(\alpha_{k},\cdots,\alpha_{1})=\sum_{Y\leqslant p\leqslant Y+X}e(f(p)),\quad f(x)=\alpha_{k}x^{k}+\cdots+\alpha_{1}x,

and introducing the the two following changes which account for the translation in the set of primes and optimize the range of
r
in Hua’s result.
In part 3) of the proof we use our Lemma
4
instead of
[
21
, Theorem 10]
, this is possible as
s_{1}
in
[
21
, Lemma 10.8]
is of arbitrary size. While in part 5) of the proof we need to substitute
[
21
, Theorem 15]
with
[
3
, Theorem 1]
. Doing this we need to be careful to isolate, in
[
21
, pag. 144]
,
\left|S(\alpha_{k},\cdots,\alpha_{1})\right|^{d(d+1)-\epsilon}
instead of
\left|S(\alpha_{k},\cdots,\alpha_{1})\right|^{s_{0}-1}
and, in
[
21
, pag. 145]
,
\left|S(\alpha_{k},\cdots,\alpha_{1})\right|^{s-\epsilon}
instead of
\left|S(\alpha_{k},\cdots,\alpha_{1})\right|^{s-1}
. Here
\epsilon>0
it is such that
s=d(d+1)+2\epsilon
.
∎

5.2.
Bounding bilinear forms

It is well known that one may use Lemma
3
to estimate bilinear forms with Weyl sums over rectangles. We next show how one may obtain sharper results by averaging bilinear forms over a family of disjoint rectangles.

Lemma 6
.

Let
F\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
be a polynomial of degree
d\geqslant 2
of the form

F(x)=\alpha_{d}x^{d}+\dots+\alpha_{1}x.

Let
M,N
be integers and
\alpha(n),\beta(m)
two sequences of complex numbers satisfying

\displaystyle|\beta(m)|\leqslant 1,

and

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}|\alpha(n)|\ll N,\quad\sum_{n\leqslant N}|\alpha(n)|^{2}\ll N(\log{N})^{A},

for
A\geqslant 0

For
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
let

(18)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(k)={\mathcal{L}}(k)\times{\mathcal{M}}(k),

be a rectangle of the form

{\mathcal{L}}(k)=(Q^{\prime}(k),Q^{\prime\prime}(k)],\quad{\mathcal{M}}(k)=(M^{\prime}(k)\times M^{\prime\prime}(k)],

such that
{\mathcal{L}}(k)\subseteq(0,Q)
, with
Q\gg 1
, are disjoint and satisfy

(19)

Q^{\prime\prime}(k)-Q^{\prime}(k)\leqslant X,

or

(20)

Q^{\prime\prime}(k)-Q^{\prime}(k)=X\gg\frac{Q^{\prime}(k)}{(\log Q^{\prime}(k))^{M}}

and
{\mathcal{M}}(k)\subseteq(0,M]
are disjoint and satisfy

M^{\prime\prime}(k)-M^{\prime}(k)\leqslant Y,\quad M^{\prime\prime}(k)\leqslant 2M^{\prime}(k),

and
K\ll M
.
Let
I
denote the sum

(21)

\displaystyle I=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(p,n)\in{\mathcal{R}}(k)\end{subarray}}\alpha(n)\beta(p)e(F(np)).

Let
R\geqslant 1
and suppose there exists
q\leqslant R
and
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant d
such that

(22)

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{\ell}-\frac{a}{q}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{qR},

for some
(a,q)=1
. For
r>d(d+1)
, we have

\displaystyle I^{4r^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll m(X)(\log{M})^{2rA}M^{4r^{2}}\left(\frac{X}{\log{X}}\right)^{4r^{2}}\left(\frac{M}{Y}\right)^{d(d-1)/2}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Q}{X}\right)^{d(d-1)/2}\left(\frac{q}{XQ^{\ell-1}YM^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{XQ^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{YM^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{q}\right),

where

m(x)=\begin{cases}(\log{x})^{4r}&{when} \eqref{eq:cond1} {holds},\\
1&{when} \eqref{eq:cond2} {holds}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that

F(x)=\alpha_{d}x^{d}+\dots+\alpha_{1}x,

and define

(23)

\displaystyle S_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(p,n)\in{\mathcal{R}}(k)\end{subarray}}\alpha(n)\beta(p)e(F(np)),

so that

(24)

\displaystyle I=\sum_{k=1}^{K}S_{k}.

Fix some
1\leqslant k\leqslant K
and consider (
23
). Recalling (
18
), by Hölder’s inequality, for any integer
r\geqslant 1

\displaystyle S_{k}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}|\alpha(n)|^{2r/(2r-1)}\right)^{1-1/2r}\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}\left|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{L}}(k)}\beta(p)e(F(np))\right|^{2r}\right)^{1/2r}.

After another application of Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle I^{2r}\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}|\alpha(n)|^{2r/(2r-1)}\right)^{2r-1}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}\left|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{L}}(k)}\beta(p)e(F(np))\right|^{2r}.

Hence from assumptions on the
{\mathcal{M}}(k)

\displaystyle I^{2r}\ll\left(\sum_{n\leqslant M}|\alpha(n)|^{2r/(2r-1)}\right)^{2r-1}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}\left|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{L}}(k)}\beta(p)e(F(np))\right|^{2r},

which combined with (
17
) implies that

\displaystyle I^{2r}\ll M^{2r-1}(\log{M})^{A}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}\left|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{L}}(k)}\beta(p)e(F(np))\right|^{2r}.

Write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})
and let
J_{k}(\lambda)
count the number of solutions to

p_{1}^{j}+\dots-p_{2r}^{j}=\lambda_{j},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant d,\quad p_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}(k),\quad p_{i}\ \ \text{prime}.

Note by assumptions on
{\mathcal{L}}(k)
, if
p_{1},\dots,p_{2r}\in{\mathcal{L}}(k)

p_{1}^{j}+\dots-p_{2r}^{j}=\lambda_{j},

Ignoring the condition that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{\lambda}J_{k}(\lambda)^{2}\ll Km_{1}(X),

since

(26)

\displaystyle I^{4r^{2}}\ll m(X)M^{4r^{2}-2r}(\log{M})^{2rA}K^{2r-1}\left(\frac{X}{\log{X}}\right)^{4r^{2}}\frac{I_{0}}{X^{d(d+1)/2}},

\displaystyle\widehat{S}(x)=\max\{0,1-|x|\},

There exists a constant
c_{0}

\displaystyle I_{0}\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{j}\ll XQ^{j-1}\\
j\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S\left(\frac{c_{0}\lambda_{\ell}}{XQ^{\ell-1}}\right)\left|\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{M}}(k)}e(\alpha_{d}\lambda_{d}n^{d}+\dots+\alpha_{1}\lambda_{1}n)\right|^{2r}.

Expanding the
2r
-th power and interchanging summation

\displaystyle I_{0}\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant K\\

Using that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{XQ^{\ell-1}YM^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{XQ^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{YM^{\ell-1}}+\frac{1}{q}\right),

a^{\prime}=\frac{a}{d},\quad k^{\prime}=\frac{k}{d},

so that

S(a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant u\\
n\equiv a^{\prime}\bmod{k^{\prime}}\\
n\equiv 0\bmod{d}\end{subarray}}f(n)=\frac{1}{\phi(k^{\prime})}\sum_{\psi\mod k^{\prime}}\overline{\psi}(a^{\prime})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant u\\
n\equiv 0\bmod{d}\end{subarray}}\psi(n)f(n).

If
(d,q)\neq 1
then
S(a)=0
. If
(d,q)=1
, then

|S(a)|\leqslant\frac{1}{\phi(k^{\prime})}\sum_{\psi\mod k^{\prime}}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant u/d}\psi(n)f(n)\right|.

Thus, observing that

\operatorname{lcm}(s_{d},\cdots,s_{1})\leqslant\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}s_{i}\leqslant(\log{N})^{d(4r^{2}+4rA)}

we conclude the proof.

8.
Short mixed character sums

We next use Corollary
1
to show how one may estimate short mixed character sums assuming GRH.

Corollary 4
.

Let
F(n)
a polynomial of degree
d
with real coefficients given by

F(x)=\alpha_{d}x^{d}+\dots+\alpha_{1}x

and taken
\chi
a primitive characters modulo
q
. Then assuming GRH, uniformly for all primitive characters, polynomials
F
of degree
d
and

(32)

\log{N}\geqslant B\log\log{q},

for a suitable fixed constant
B
, we have

\left|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\right|\ll\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1-\varepsilon}},

for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

We need the following lemma on convolution of Dirichlet characters, that follows from Theorem 2 in
[
13
]
and Corollary 1.3 in
[
20
]
, remembering that there they have
u=\log x/\log y
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\chi
be a non-principal character modulo
q
. Assuming the Generalised Riemann hypothesis, for any
x
such that

\frac{\log x}{\log\log q}\geqslant B,

for a sufficiently large constant
B
,
we have

\left|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\chi(n)\right|\ll x(\log q)^{-(1+o(1))\frac{\log x\log\left(\frac{\log x}{2\log\log q}\right)}{2(\log\log q)^{2}}},

We can thus prove Corollary
4
in a similar fashion to
[
18
, Lemma 3]
and
[
25
, Lemma 7]
.

Proof of Corollary
4
.

Clearly we may suppose

\left|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\right|\gg\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1-\varepsilon}}.

For any
\psi
with modulus smaller than or equal to
k:=lcm(s_{d},\cdots,s_{1})
, by Lemma
7
, we have

{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}\max_{u\leqslant N}}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant u}\psi(n)\chi(n)\right|\ll N(\log q)^{-(1+o(1))\frac{\log N\log\left(\frac{\log N}{2\log\log q}\right)}{2(\log\log q)^{2}}}.

Thus, by Corollary
1
and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\leqslant N}\chi(n)e(F(n))\right|

\displaystyle\ll N(\log{x})^{d^{5}}(\log q)^{-(1+o(1))\frac{\log N\log\left(\frac{\log N}{2\log\log q}\right)}{2(\log\log q)^{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{N}{(\log N)^{1-\varepsilon}},

and this concludes the proof.
∎

9.
On the correlation between roots of polynomial congruences and polynomial values

We now prove Theorem
2
.
Let
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
be irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
of degree
e\geqslant 2
and consider the ratios
v/n
, where
v
are the roots of the polynomial
p
modulo
n

\displaystyle p(v)\equiv 0\bmod{n}.

Define the sequence
(g_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
of these ratios so that the corresponding denominators are in ascending order.

Hooley
[
22
, Theorem 1]
proved that
(g_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is equidistributed in
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Even stronger than that, he showed

(33)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}\\
p(v)\equiv 0\bmod{n}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{hv}{n}\right)\right|=0,

for every
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\}
.
To prove Theorem
2
, by the Weyl equidistribution criteria, it suffices to prove that
for any
(h_{1},h_{2})\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}e\left(h_{1}F(n)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}p}(v)\equiv 0\bmod{n}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{h_{2}v}{n}\right)=o(N).

If
h_{2}\neq 0
this is true by equation (
33
). Thus, the only cases that need to be considered are
h_{2}=0
and
h_{1}\neq 0
. If we let

(34)

\displaystyle\varrho(n)=|\{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}};{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}p}(v)\equiv{0}\bmod{n}\}|,

then the problem is therefore to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}\varrho(n)e\left(F(n)\right)=o(N).

We first prove some properties of
\varrho
.

Lemma 8
.

The function
\varrho
is multiplicative and satisfies:

(1)

For all
\epsilon>0
,
\varrho(n)\leqslant_{\epsilon}n^{\epsilon}
, for
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(2)

There exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho(n)\sim\lambda N.

(3)

There exists a constant
A\geqslant 0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho(n)^{2}\ll N(\log{N})^{A},

for
N\geqslant 2
.

(4)

There exists a constant
D\geqslant 1
such that

\varrho(mn)\leqslant D^{\operatorname{disc}(f)}\varrho(m)\varrho(n),

for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

Proof.

The Chinese Remainder Theorem implies that
\varrho
is a multiplicative function. From Wirsing’s theorem
[
34
, Satz 1]
we conclude the second item. The proof of the third one is standard (see, for e.g
[
10
, Lemma 2.7]
).

To conclude the fourth item, we observe that

\varrho(p^{\alpha})=\varrho(p)
for all
p
primes,
p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(f)
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 1}
and
\varrho(p^{\alpha})\leqslant D
for all
p
primes and a constant
D>0
(for a proof, see, for e.g.
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
). We also note that if
p|\operatorname{disc}(f)
and
\varrho(p^{\alpha})\neq 0
then
\varrho(p^{\beta})\neq 0
for all
\beta\leqslant\alpha
and we can conclude the result by factoring
m
and
n
in primes. Note this also implies the first item, since if
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots p_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
then

\varrho(n)\ll D^{k}\ll D^{\log{n}/{\log\log{n}}}.

∎

From now on, we fix
r^{\ast}=2\max(d(d+1),A)
and
B=4{r^{\ast}}^{2}+4rA+1
and the constants
\lambda
,
D
and
A
as in Lemma
8
.

From Dirichlet’s Theorem, we can find
a_{i}
and
1\leqslant q_{i}\leqslant\frac{N^{i}}{(\log{N})^{B}}
coprime integers satisfying

(35)

\left|\alpha_{i}-\frac{a_{i}}{q_{i}}\right|\leqslant\frac{(\log{N})^{B}}{q_{i}N^{i}},

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
. We also let

q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}).

Proposition 1
.

If there exists an
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
q_{l}
satisfies
q_{l}\geqslant(\log{N})^{B}
then

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho(n)e(F(n))\ll\frac{N}{(\log{N})^{1/2}}.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
1
applied to
f=\rho
,
r=r^{\ast}
and
R=N^{\ell}/(\log{N})^{B}
.
∎

We next focus on establishing the following result.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose that
q_{i}<(\log{N})^{B}
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
. Then it holds that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varrho(n)e(F(n))\ll\frac{N}{q^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}-\epsilon}}.

Combining Proposition
2
with Proposition
1
one may deduce Theorem
2
. Indeed, since at least one of the
\alpha_{i}
is irrational, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, the integers
q_{i}
in (
35
) must satisfy
q_{i}\rightarrow\infty
which implies

\sum_{n=1}\varrho(n)e(F(n))=o(N).

To prove Proposition
2
we will proceed with an algebraic approach. We split the task in four subsections: first we make some reductions and provide a proof of the proposition, conditional to further analysis of a different sum. This new sum will be analysed using a Dirichlet series. In the next subsection, we write the Dirichlet series that we want to analyse in terms of better understood L-functions and in the third subsection we state some of its properties. At last, we gather all the results and conclude the proof.

9.1.
First reductions

We assume
q_{i}<(\log{N})^{B}
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
and we set, for
r|q
, and
Q\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]

\displaystyle S_{r}(N,Q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,q)=r\end{subarray}}^{N}\varrho(n)e(Q(n)),

and we omit
r
from the notation when the sum is over all integers

.

We can split
S(N,F)
in two sums as follows

(36)

\displaystyle S(N,F)=\sum_{r|q}S_{r}(N,F)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r<q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}S_{r}(N,F)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r\geqslant q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}S_{r}(N,F).

We can bound trivially the second sum of the right hand side of equation (
36
) using Lemma
8
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r\geqslant q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}S_{r}(N,F)\right|\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r\geqslant q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,q)=r\end{subarray}}^{N}\varrho(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r\geqslant q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,q/r)=1\end{subarray}}^{N/r}\varrho(rn)

(37)

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}\frac{N}{q^{1/d(d+1)-\epsilon}}.

We turn our attention to

when

. Let
b_{j}=\frac{a_{j}\cdot q}{q_{j}},
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant d
,
\tilde{F}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{b_{j}}{q_{j}}x^{j}
and

. Observe that integration by parts yields

(38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{r}(N,F)&=\ e\left(Q(N)\right)S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})\\
&-2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{d}j\beta_{j}\int_{1}^{N}u^{j-1}e\left(Q(u)\right)S_{r}(u,\tilde{F})du,\end{split}

where
\beta_{j}
is defined by

(39)

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}=\frac{a_{j}}{q_{j}}+\beta_{j}.

In particular, from (
35
)

(40)

\displaystyle|\beta_{j}|\leqslant\frac{(\log{N})^{B}}{q_{j}N^{j}}.

The above reduces to analysing
S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})
instead of
S_{r}(N,F)
.

Observe that since we are summing over multiples of
r
, we can rewrite
e(\tilde{F}(n))
as
e(\tilde{F_{r}}(n))
, where
\tilde{F_{r}}(n):=\tilde{F}(rn)
, so that the latter is a periodic function modulo
q/r
. We let

and we decompose
S_{r}(u,\tilde{F_{r}})
using Dirichlet characters modulo

as follows:

(41)

\begin{split}S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})&=\sum_{x\bmod{r^{\prime}}}e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leqslant N/r\\
(m,r^{\prime})=1\\
m\equiv x\bmod{r^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\varrho(rm)\\
&=\frac{1}{\varphi(r^{\prime})}\sum_{x\bmod{r^{\prime}}}e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))\sum_{\chi\bmod{r^{\prime}}}\overline{\chi(x)}\sum_{m\leqslant N/r}\chi(m)\varrho(rm).\end{split}

We will prove:

Proposition 3
.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character
\bmod{r^{\prime}}
. There exist constants
c>0
,
0\leqslant\delta(\chi)\leqslant\lambda r
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leqslant N/r}\chi(m)\varrho(rm)=\delta(\chi)\frac{N}{r}+O(Ne^{-\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{\log{N}}}).

Moreover, if
\delta(\chi)\neq 0
then
\chi
has conductor
h|\operatorname{disc}(p)
.

We next show that Proposition
3
implies we can conclude the proof of Proposition
2
. Indeed, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})=\frac{N}{r\varphi(r^{\prime})}\sum_{x\bmod{r^{\prime}}}e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))\sum_{\chi\bmod{r^{\prime}}}\overline{\chi(x)}\delta(\chi)+O(Ne^{-\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\sqrt{\log{N}}}),

which substituted into (
38
) and using (
40
) implies

\displaystyle S_{r}(N,F)

\displaystyle=\ e\left(Q(N)\right)S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})

\displaystyle-C\int_{1}^{N}u\left(2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{d}j\beta_{j}u^{j-1}\right)e\left(Q(u)\right)du+O(Ne^{-\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{\log{N}}}),

with

\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{r\varphi(r^{\prime})}\sum_{x\bmod{r^{\prime}}}e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))\sum_{\chi\bmod{r^{\prime}}}\overline{\chi(x)}\delta(\chi).

Note that integrating by parts

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{N}u\left(2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{d}j\beta_{j}u^{j-1}\right)e\left(Q(u)\right)du\ll N,

and since if
\delta(\chi)\neq 0
then
\chi
has conductor
h|\operatorname{disc}(p)
, the above implies

(42)

\displaystyle S_{r}(N,F)\ll\frac{N}{r}\frac{1}{\phi(r^{\prime})}\sum_{x\mod{r^{\prime}}}\chi(x)e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))+O(Ne^{-\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{\log{N}}}),

for some
\chi
with conductor
h|\operatorname{disc}(p)
.

Recall that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{r}(x)=\tilde{F}(rx)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{b_{j}r^{j}}{q_{j}}x^{j}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}(qb_{j}r^{j}/q_{j})x^{j}}{q}.

Let
q^{\prime}
denote the smallest divisor of
q
such that the polynomial

P(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(qb_{j}r^{j}/q_{j})x^{j},

is constant mod
q/q^{\prime}
. Note that for each
1\leqslant j\leqslant d

\frac{qb_{j}r^{j}}{q_{j}}\equiv 0\mod{q/q^{\prime}}.

Since
(b_{j},q_{j})=1
, this implies

q^{\prime}>\frac{q_{j}}{r^{j}}\geqslant\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{q_{j}}{r^{j}}\right)^{1/d}\geqslant\frac{q^{1/d}}{r^{(d+1)/2}}\geqslant q^{1/2d},

provided

It follows from work of Cochrane and Zheng
[
9
]
and the Chinese remainder theorem that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\mod{r^{\prime}}}\chi(x)e(\tilde{F}_{r}(x))\ll\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}(q^{\prime})^{1-1/d}\ll\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{1/2d(d+1)}}.

Combining the above with (
42
) gives

S_{r}(N,F)\leqslant\frac{N}{q^{1/2d(d+1)}}\quad\text{provided}\quad r\leqslant q^{1/d(d+1)}.

Summing over
r<q^{1/d(d+1)}
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|q\\
r<q^{1/d(d+1)}\end{subarray}}S_{r}(N,\tilde{F})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{N}{q^{1/2d(d+1)+o(1)}}.

Together with (
36
) and (
9.1
) this concludes the proof of Proposition
2
. We will now focus on the proof of Proposition
3
.
In order to understand the sum

(43)

\sum_{n\leqslant N/r}\varrho(rn)\chi(n),

we study the Dirichlet series for
\chi\bmod{r^{\prime}}

D_{r}(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varrho(rn)\chi(n)n^{-s}.

9.2.
Decomposing
D_{r}(s,\chi)

Obverse that
D_{r}(s,\chi)
is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.
Our next goal is to extend
D_{r}(s,\chi)
to the left of
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
so we can use contour integration to estimate the sum (
43
). We will do this by decomposing
D_{r}
in terms of well-known Artin L-functions. With this in mind, we fix some notation.

Denote by
K_{f}
the splitting field of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}
, by
G
the Galois group of
K_{f}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and by
{\mathbb{Q}}(e(1/r^{\prime}))
the cyclotomic field generated by
r^{\prime}
-roots of unity. Observe that the latter is a Galois extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, with Galois group
C_{r^{\prime}}
isomorphic to
({\mathbb{Z}}/r^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
.

We also consider the compositum of
K_{f}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}(e(1/r^{\prime}))
and denote it by
K_{f,r^{\prime}}
and its Galois group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
by
G_{r^{\prime}}
. Observe that there is a natural injection

\displaystyle G_{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow G\times C_{r^{\prime}}\simeq G\times({\mathbb{Z}}/r^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}.

Moreover, observe that from the extension theorem for field automoprhism, it follows that the projections
p_{1}:G_{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow G
and
p_{2}:G_{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow({\mathbb{Z}}/r^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
are surjective.

We denote by
\hat{G}
the finite set of isomorphism classes of complex irreducible representations of
G
and for
\pi\in\hat{G}
we write
\chi_{\pi}
for the character of
\pi
.

Note that
\chi
can be viewed as a character of
({\mathbb{Z}}/r^{\prime}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, and consequently as a character
\eta:C_{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. It is known that
\eta(\sigma_{p})=\chi(p)
for
p\nmid r^{\prime}
and
\sigma_{p}
the Frobenius automorphism at
p
.

So, we consider the representations
p_{1}^{\ast}\pi=\pi\circ p_{1}
and
p^{\ast}_{2}\eta=\eta\circ p_{2}
of

and observe that their tensor product satisfies

(44)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta(\sigma_{p}))=\chi_{\pi}(\sigma_{p})\chi(p),

for

and

.

Proposition 4
.

D_{r}(s,\chi)
has an expression

\displaystyle D_{r}(s,\chi)=F_{r}(s,\chi)\prod_{\pi\in\hat{G}}E_{r,\pi}(s,\chi)(L(s,K_{f,r^{\prime}}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta))^{m_{\pi}},

for
m_{\pi}\geqslant 0
an integer and, for any
\epsilon>0
,
F_{r}\ll_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}

and
E_{r,\pi}\ll q^{\epsilon}
for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geqslant 3/4
.

To prove the proposition we will need the following lemma:

Lemma 9
.

Denote by
\pi_{f}
the permutation representation of
G
acting on the set of roots of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}
and let

\displaystyle\pi_{f}=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\hat{G}}m_{\pi}\pi

be its decomposition in irreducible representations, where
m_{\pi}\geqslant 0
are integers. For all
p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(f)
it holds

(45)

\displaystyle\varrho(p)=\sum_{\pi}m_{\pi}\cdot\chi_{\pi}(\sigma_{p}),

for
\sigma_{p}\in G
the Frobenius automorphism at
p
.

Proof.

The proof follows by noting that
\varrho(p)
is the number of fixed points of the Frobenius automorphism
\sigma_{p}
at
p
, which is also the character at
\sigma_{p}
of the permutation representation.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4
.

First we observe that
D_{r}
can be decomposed the following way

\displaystyle D_{r}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e|r^{\infty}}\sum_{(k,r)=1}\chi(ek)\varrho(erk)(ek)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e|r^{\infty}}\varrho(er)\chi(e)e^{-s}\sum_{(k,r)=1}\chi(k)\varrho(k)k^{-s},

where the notation

means that
e
runs over all possible products of powers of the primes that divide
r
.

We denote by

\displaystyle E_{r}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e|r^{\infty}}\varrho(er)\chi(e)e^{-s}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(k,r)=1}\chi(k)\varrho(k)k^{-s},

and using Lemma
8
and the property
\varrho(p^{n})\leqslant C,
for all
n\geqslant 1
and
p
prime, we observe that
E_{r}(s,\chi)\ll r^{\epsilon}
uniformly for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geqslant 3/4
and that
\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)
converges absolutely for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
.

To be able to explore the properties of
\varrho
, we factor the primes that divide the discriminant of
f
in
\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)
and, since

, we also factor the remaining primes that divide
q
. This yields

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)=E_{1,r}(s,\chi)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(f)\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\varrho(p^{k})\chi(p)^{k}p^{-ks},

where

is entire and bounded for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geqslant 3/4
by
C(\epsilon)q^{\epsilon}
.

We proceed in a similar manner and factor out the prime powers with exponent bigger than
2
, obtaining

(46)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)=E_{1,r}(s,\chi)E_{2}(s,\chi)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(f)\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}(1+\varrho(p)\chi(p)p^{-s}),

for

holomorphic and bounded in vertical strips by
C(\epsilon)q^{\epsilon}
for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geqslant 3/4
.

Now we can use Lemma
9
and a further decomposition to write

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{r}(s,\chi)=E_{1,r}(s,\chi)E_{2}(s,\chi)E_{3,r}(s,\chi)\prod_{\pi\in\hat{G}}\prod_{p}(1+\chi_{\pi}(\sigma_{p})\chi(p)p^{-s})^{m_{\pi}},

for

holomorphic and uniformly bounded in vertical strips by

for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geqslant 3/4
. Set
F_{r}=E_{1,r}E_{2}E_{3,r}
. To conclude, we use equality (

\displaystyle\prod_{p}(1+\chi_{\pi}(\sigma_{p})\chi(p)p^{-s})=E_{r,\pi}(s,\chi)L(s,K_{f,q}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta).

∎

Observe that now we are dealing with Artin L-functions, which are better understood than the original Dirichlet series. Since
p_{1}
is surjective and
\pi
is irreducible it follows that
p_{1}^{\ast}\pi
is an irreducible representation. Furthermore, since
p_{2}^{\ast}\eta
is of dimension

we can conclude that
p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta
is also an irreducible representation. Artin’s conjecture states that this L-function is entire except if the representation is trivial - which can occur if
p_{1}^{\ast}\pi
is the inverse of
p_{2}^{\ast}\eta
.

To avoid assuming Artin’s conjecture we will use the Brauer induction theorem (see, e.g,
[
33
, Theorem 19]
) instead. It states that for every subgroup
H
of
G
and 1-dimensional character
\beta_{H}:H\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, there exist integers
n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}=\sum_{H}\sum_{\beta_{H}}n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}\cdot{\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}}\beta_{H}.

Observe that this implies that
L(s,K_{f,q}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\pi)
can be represented as follows

\displaystyle L(s,K_{f,q}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\pi)=L(s,K_{f}/{\mathbb{Q}},\pi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{H}\prod_{\beta_{H}}L(s,K_{f}/{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}\beta_{H})^{n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{H}\prod_{\beta_{H}}L(s,K_{f}/K_{H},\beta_{H})^{n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}},

where
K_{H}\subset K_{f}
is the subfield fixed by
H
- which implies that
H
is the Galois gorup of
K_{f}
over
K_{H}
.

We can now introduce the twist by the Dirichlet character. We denote by
H^{\prime}=p_{1}^{-1}(H)
and observe that we can write

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\ast}\pi=\bigoplus_{H}n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}\operatorname{Ind}_{H^{\prime}}^{G_{r^{\prime}}}(p_{1}^{\ast}\beta_{H}),

and thus

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta=\bigoplus_{H}\bigoplus_{\beta_{H}}n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}\operatorname{Ind}_{H^{\prime}}^{G_{r^{\prime}}}(p_{1}^{\ast}\beta_{H}\otimes\operatorname{Res}_{H^{\prime}}^{G_{r^{\prime}}}p_{2}^{\ast}\eta).

So denoting
\theta_{\beta_{H}}=p_{1}^{\ast}\beta_{H}\otimes\operatorname{Res}_{H^{\prime}}^{G_{r^{\prime}}}p_{2}^{\ast}\eta
, we can finally write

\displaystyle L(s,K_{f,q}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\chi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta)=\prod_{H}\prod_{\beta_{H}}L(s,K_{f,q}/K_{H},\theta_{\beta_{H}})^{n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}},

thus
D_{r}
has the following representation

(47)

\displaystyle D_{r}(s,\chi)=F_{r}(s,\chi)\prod_{\pi\in\hat{G}}E_{r,\pi}(s,\chi)\prod_{H}\prod_{\beta_{H}}L(s,K_{f,q}/K_{H},\theta_{\beta_{H}})^{n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}}.

Now
L(s,K_{f,q}/K_{H},\theta_{\beta_{H}})^{n_{\pi,\beta_{H}}}
are Artin L-functions of dimension one, which we know that are entire except for a pole in
s=1
if
\theta_{\beta_{H}}
is the trivial one dimensional character.

Observe that Mellin inversion implies that

(52)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,q)=r\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\varrho(n)\chi(n)\phi(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}D_{r}(s,\chi)\hat{\phi}(s)ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}F_{r}(s,\chi)\prod_{\pi\in\hat{G}}E_{r,\pi}(s,\chi)(L(s,K_{f,r^{\prime}}/{\mathbb{Q}},p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta))^{m_{\pi}}\hat{\phi}(s)ds\\

Our goal is to shift the contour of integration and use the information on the L-functions
L(s,K_{f,q}/K^{\prime}_{H},\theta_{\beta_{H}})
to obtain good bounds for
S_{r}(s,\tilde{F}_{r})
. Observe that we can find

such that all the conditions of Proposition

are satisfied for

Moving the contour of integration in equation (

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\

&+\tilde{\alpha}\operatorname{Res}(D_{r}(s,\chi),\beta)\cdot N^{\beta},\\
\end{split}

where

if there is
\pi\in\hat{G}
, for which
p_{1}^{\ast}\pi\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}\eta
is the principal character in
K_{f,r^{\prime}}/{\mathbb{Q}}
with

and

otherwise. Likewise,
\tilde{\alpha}=1
if
L(s,K_{f,q}/K^{\prime}_{H},\theta_{\beta_{H}})
has an exceptional zero
\beta
and

, and
\tilde{\alpha}=0
otherwise. Note from Proposition
4
that there is at most one trivial character

10.
Sharpness of Theorem
1

Following the approach of
[
32
]
, we will construct a completely multiplicative function
f=f_{N,F}
, with
|f(n)|\leqslant 1
, such that

(56)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)e\left(F(n)\right)\right|\geqslant\frac{1}{10}\frac{N}{\log{N}}.

We first observe that the function
G:{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
given by

\displaystyle G(z)=\sum_{n\leqslant N}z^{\Omega(n)}e(F(n))+\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<p\leqslant N}(1-ze(F(p)))

is entire and its value at zero satifies

\displaystyle G(0)=\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<p\leqslant N}1\geqslant\frac{1}{10}\frac{N}{\log{N}},

for
N
sufficiently large.
Thus, by the maximum modulus principle, there exists a
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
|z_{0}|=1
, such that
|G(z_{0})|\geqslant|G(0)|
.

Define the completely multiplicative function
f
by
f(p)=z_{0}
, for
p\leqslant N/2
, and
f(p)=e(-F(p))
, for
p>N/2
. To conclude that equation (
56
) is satisfied, we just observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant N}f(n)e\left(F(n)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leqslant N}z_{0}^{\Omega(n)}e(F(n))+\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<p\leqslant N}(1-z_{0}e(F(p)))

\displaystyle=G(z_{0})\gg\frac{N}{\log{N}}.

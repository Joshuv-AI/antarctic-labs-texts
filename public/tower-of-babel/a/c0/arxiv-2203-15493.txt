On the stable Harbourne conjecture for ideals defining space monomial curves
Kosuke Fukumuro and Yuki Irie

Abstract
For the ideal 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} in k ⁡ [ x , y , z ] {k}[{x},{y},{z}] defining a space monomial curve, we show that 𝔭 ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) ⊆ 𝔪 ​ 𝔭 n {\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}} for some positive integer n {n} , where 𝔪 {\mathfrak{m}} is the maximal ideal ( x , y , z ) ({x},{y},{z}) . Moreover, the smallest such n {n} is determined. It turns out that there is a counterexample to a claim due to Grifo, Huneke, and Mukundan, which states that 𝔭 ( 3 ) ⊆ 𝔪 ​ 𝔭 2 {\mathfrak{p}}^{(3)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{2} if k {k} is a field of characteristic not 3 3 ; however, the stable Harbourne conjecture holds for space monomial curves as they claimed.

\displaystyle\frac{{l}_{{R}}\left({R}/{\mathfrak{q}}+({z})\right)}{{a}_{1}{b}}

\displaystyle=(r+3)+(r+2)(r-1)+(r+1)r+\sum_{{i}=1}^{r}{i}(r-1)

\displaystyle=2r^{2}+3r+1+(r-1)\frac{(r+1)r}{2}

\displaystyle=(r+1)\Bigl(2r+1+\frac{r(r-1)}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(r+1)\binom{r+2}{2}.

Therefore
{\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{p}}^{(r+1)}
.

(iii)

Let

{\mathfrak{q}}=({D}_{2}{D}_{r},{G}^{2}{D}_{r},{G}{H}{D}_{r},{H}^{2}{D}_{r})+({G}^{{i}}{H}^{{j}}:{i}+{j}=r+2,\ 0\leq{i}\leq r).

Since

\displaystyle{D}_{2}{D}_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y})^{r+4}

\displaystyle\pmod{{z}},

\displaystyle{G}^{2}{D}_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y})^{r+3}{x}^{2{a}_{1}}

\displaystyle\pmod{{z}},

\displaystyle{G}{H}{D}_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y})^{r+2}{x}^{(r+1){a}_{1}}

\displaystyle\pmod{{z}},

\displaystyle{H}^{2}{D}_{r}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y})^{r+1}{x}^{2r{a}_{1}}

\displaystyle\pmod{{z}},

\displaystyle{G}^{{i}}{H}^{{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y})^{{i}}{x}^{(r(r+2)-(r-1){i}){a}_{1}}

\displaystyle\pmod{{z}}\text{{\ }\ for \ }0\leq{i}\leq r,

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{{l}_{{R}}\left({R}/{\mathfrak{q}}+({z})\right)}{{a}_{1}{b}}

\displaystyle=(r+4)\cdot 2+(r+3)(r-1)+(r+2)(r-1)+(r+1)r+\sum_{{i}=1}^{r}{i}(r-1)

\displaystyle=2r+8+r^{2}+2r-3+r^{2}+r-2+r^{2}+r+(r-1)\frac{(r+1)r}{2}

\displaystyle=3r^{2}+6r+3+(r-1)\frac{(r+1)r}{2}

\displaystyle=(r+1)\Bigl(3r+3+\frac{r^{2}-r}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(r+1)\binom{r+3}{2}.

Therefore
{\mathfrak{q}}={\mathfrak{p}}^{(r+2)}
.

∎

4
The stable Harbourne conjecture

To prove Theorem
1.6
, we show the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{M}
be a matrix of type
1^{\prime}
. For
1\leq{l}\leq r{{}_{M}}+1
,

\begin{split}{x}^{-\alpha_{{l}}}{z}^{-\gamma_{{l}}}{D}_{{l}}&={z}^{-{c}_{2}}({H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}})\\
&={x}^{-{a}_{1}}({G}{D}_{{l}-1}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-1){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}).\\
\end{split}

(4.1)

Moreover,
{x}^{({l}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}
and
{z}^{({l}-1){c}_{2}}{D}_{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}
.

Proof.

We show the lemma by induction on
{l}
.
Note that
\alpha_{{l}}=\gamma_{{l}}=0
for
{l}\leq r{{}_{M}}
.
If
{l}=1
, then
{D}_{1}={F}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
and

{x}^{{a}_{1}}{F}=-{Y}{G}-{z}^{{c}_{1}}{H},

so (
4.1
) holds.
Suppose that
{l}\geq 2
. Since

{x}^{-\alpha_{l}}{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma_{l}}{D}_{{l}}={H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}},

it suffices to show that

{x}^{{a}_{1}}\bigl({H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}\bigr)={z}^{{c}_{2}}\bigl({G}{D}_{{l}-1}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-1){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}\bigr).

(4.2)

Since
\alpha_{{l}-1}=\gamma_{{l}-1}=0
, it follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle{D}_{{l}-1}

\displaystyle={z}^{-{c}_{2}}\bigl({H}{D}_{{l}-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle={x}^{-{a}_{1}}\bigl({G}{D}_{{l}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}-1}\bigr).

This implies that

\displaystyle{x}^{{a}_{1}}\bigl({H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}\bigr)

\displaystyle={H}\bigl({G}{D}_{{l}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}-1}\bigr)-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}

\displaystyle={G}{H}{D}_{{l}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}

and

\displaystyle{z}^{{c}_{2}}\bigl({G}{D}_{{l}-1}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-1){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}\bigr)

\displaystyle={G}\bigl({H}{D}_{{l}-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}

\displaystyle={G}{H}{D}_{{l}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}.

Therefore (
4.2
) and (
4.1
) hold.
By the induction hypothesis,

{x}^{({l}-2){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}-1}
and
{z}^{({l}-2){c}_{2}}{D}_{{l}-1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}-1}
,
so

\displaystyle{x}^{({l}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}}

\displaystyle={x}^{({l}-1){a}_{1}+\alpha_{{l}}}{z}^{\gamma_{{l}}}\bigl({x}^{-{a}_{1}}({G}{D}_{{l}-1}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-1){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}})\bigr)

\displaystyle={z}^{\gamma_{{l}}}{G}{x}^{({l}-2){a}_{1}+\alpha_{{l}}}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{({l}-2){a}_{1}+\alpha_{{l}}}{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-1){c}_{2}+\gamma_{{l}}}{H}^{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}

and

\displaystyle{z}^{({l}-1){c}_{2}}{D}_{{l}}

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha_{{l}}}{z}^{({l}-1){c}_{2}+\gamma_{{l}}}\bigl({z}^{-{c}_{2}}({H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}})\bigr)

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha_{{l}}}{H}{z}^{({l}-2){c}_{2}+\gamma_{{l}}}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}+\alpha_{{l}}}{z}^{({l}-2){c}_{2}+\gamma_{{l}}}{G}^{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If
{M}
is a matrix of type
1^{\prime}
and
0\leq{l}\leq r{{}_{M}}+1
, then

{D}_{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta_{{l}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lfloor\frac{{l}+1}{2}\rfloor},

(4.3)

where

\delta_{{l}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{\ \ if \ }{l}\text{\ is even or\ }{l}=r{{}_{M}}+1,\\
0&\text{\ \ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Write
{l}=2{n}-\epsilon
with
\epsilon\in\set{0,1}
.
Then

\left\lfloor\frac{{l}+1}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{2{n}-\epsilon+1}{2}\right\rfloor={n}.

We show that
{D}_{{l}}={D}_{2{n}-\epsilon}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta_{{l}}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
by induction on
{l}
.
Note that
r{{}_{M}}=\lfloor{a}_{2}/{a}_{1}\rfloor+1\geq 2
.
If
{l}=1
, then
{n}=1
and
{D}_{1}={F}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
.
If
{l}=2
, then
{n}=1
and
{D}_{2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
2.2
.
Suppose that
{l}=3
; then
{n}=2
.
By Remark
2.5
,
{D}_{3}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
.
If
{l}=3=r{{}_{M}}+1
, then
r{{}_{M}}=2
and
{a}_{2}<2{a}_{1}
,
so
\alpha_{3}=\max\set{0,2{a}_{1}-{a}_{2}}>0
and
{D}_{3}\in{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
by Theorem
1.3
.
Suppose that
{l}\geq 4
, and let
\alpha=\alpha_{{l}}
,
\gamma=\gamma_{{l}}
, and
\delta=\delta_{{l}}
.
Note that
{l}\geq{n}+2
since
{l}-{n}-2={n}-\epsilon-2\geq 0
.

Step 1.

We show that

({x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}}+{Y}^{2}){x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}.

(4.4)

We can see that

{x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}.

(4.5)

Indeed, if
{l}\leq r{{}_{M}}
, then
\delta=\delta_{{l}}=\delta_{{l}-2}
and
{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}
by the induction hypothesis,
so
{x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}
;
if
{l}=r{{}_{M}}+1
, then
\alpha\geq 1
, so
{x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}={\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}
.
Therefore (
4.5
) holds.
Hence

{F}{x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}=({Y}^{2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}}){x}^{\alpha}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}.

By Lemma
4.1
,
{x}^{({l}-3){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}-2}
.
Since
{a}_{2}\geq(r{{}_{M}}-1){a}_{1}>({l}-3){a}_{1}
, it follows that

{x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}-2}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}.

Therefore (
4.4
) holds.

Step 2.

Let

{C}_{0}={x}^{\alpha}\bigl(x^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}}{D}_{{l}-2}-x^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr).

We show that if
{C}_{0}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
, then
{D}_{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
.
By Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle{x}^{-\alpha}{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{D}_{{l}}

\displaystyle={H}{D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}

\displaystyle=({x}^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}}-{Y}{z}^{{c}_{2}}){D}_{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}}

\displaystyle={x}^{{a}_{2}}\bigl({G}{D}_{{l}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-{Y}\bigl({H}{D}_{{l}-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}\bigl({z}^{{c}_{1}+{c}_{2}}-{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}}\bigr){G}^{{l}-1}

\displaystyle=({x}^{{a}_{2}}{G}-{Y}{H}){D}_{{l}-2}+2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-{x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-1){a}_{1}}{z}^{{c}_{1}+{c}_{2}}{G}^{{l}-1}.

Notice that
{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}\geq{c}_{2}-\gamma
since

({c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2})-({c}_{2}-\gamma)\geq{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}-{c}_{2}+({l}-1){c}_{2}-{c}_{1}=0.

Thus

{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{D}_{{l}}\equiv{x}^{\alpha}\bigl(({x}^{{a}_{2}}{G}-{Y}{H}){D}_{{l}-2}+2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}}.

Since

\displaystyle{x}^{{a}_{2}}{G}-{Y}{H}

\displaystyle={x}^{{a}_{2}}\bigl({z}^{{c}_{1}+{c}_{2}}-{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}})-{Y}({x}^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}}-{Y}{z}^{{c}_{2}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=({x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}}+{Y}^{2}){z}^{{c}_{2}}-2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}},

it follows from (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{D}_{{l}}

\displaystyle\equiv{x}^{\alpha}\bigl(({x}^{{a}_{2}}{z}^{{c}_{1}}+{Y}^{2}){z}^{{c}_{2}}{D}_{{l}-2}-2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}}{D}_{{l}-2}+2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv{x}^{\alpha}\bigl(-2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}+{a}_{2}}{D}_{{l}-2}+2{Y}{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv-2{Y}{C}_{0}\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}}.

Thus if
{C}_{0}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
, then
{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{D}_{{l}}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
,
so
{D}_{{l}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{\delta}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
.

Step 3.

For
1\leq{j}\leq{n}
, let

{C}_{{j}}={x}^{\alpha}\bigl(x^{{a}_{2}-({j}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-{j}-2}-x^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-{j}-1}\bigr).

By induction on
{j}
, we show that
if
{C}_{{j}}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-{j}}
, then
{C}_{0}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
.
If
{j}=0
, then it is obvious.
Suppose that
{j}\geq 1
. By Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle{C}_{{j}-1}

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha}\bigl(x^{{a}_{2}-({j}-2){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-{j}-1}-x^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-{j}}\bigr)

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha}\bigl(x^{{a}_{2}-({j}-1){a}_{1}}({G}{D}_{{l}-{j}-2}-{z}^{{c}_{1}-({l}-{j}-2){c}_{2}}{H}^{{l}-{j}-1})-x^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-{j}}\bigr).

Since
{c}_{1}-({l}-{j}-2){c}_{2}\geq{c}_{1}-({l}-2){c}_{2}\geq{c}_{2}-\gamma
, it follows that

\displaystyle{C}_{{j}-1}

\displaystyle\equiv{x}^{\alpha}{G}(x^{{a}_{2}-({j}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-{j}-2}-x^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-{j}-1})

\displaystyle\equiv{G}{C}_{{j}}\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-{j}+1}}.

Therefore if
{C}_{{j}}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-{j}}
, then
{C}_{j-1}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-{j}+1}
,
so
{C}_{0}\in{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
by the induction hypothesis.

Step 4.

We show that
{C}_{{n}}\in({z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma})
.
Note that

\displaystyle{C}_{{n}}

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha}\bigl({x}^{{a}_{2}-({n}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{l}-{n}-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-({l}-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{l}-{n}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha}\bigl({x}^{{a}_{2}-({n}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{n}-\epsilon-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-(2{n}-\epsilon-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\bigr).

By Lemma
3.3
,

{D}_{{n}-\epsilon-2}\equiv(-{Y})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{1}-({n}-\epsilon-3){c}_{2}}}.

We see that
{c}_{1}-({n}-\epsilon-3){c}_{2}\geq{c}_{2}-\gamma
, so

{D}_{{n}-\epsilon-2}\equiv(-{Y})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}}.

Since

\displaystyle{G}^{{n}-\epsilon-1}

\displaystyle=({z}^{{c}_{1}+{c}_{2}}-{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}

\displaystyle\equiv(-{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}},

it follows that

\displaystyle{C}_{{n}}

\displaystyle={x}^{\alpha}\bigl({x}^{{a}_{2}-({n}-1){a}_{1}}{D}_{{n}-\epsilon-2}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-(2{n}-\epsilon-2){a}_{1}}{G}^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv{x}^{\alpha}\bigl({x}^{{a}_{2}-({n}-1){a}_{1}}(-{Y})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}-{x}^{{a}_{2}-(2{n}-\epsilon-2){a}_{1}}(-{Y}{x}^{{a}_{1}})^{{n}-\epsilon-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{{z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma}}.

Therefore
{C}_{{n}}\in({z}^{{c}_{2}-\gamma})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.
We first show that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{l}-1)}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}
for
1\leq{l}\leq{n}-1
.
Since
{n}=\lfloor(r{{}_{M}}+1)/2\rfloor+1\leq(r{{}_{M}}+1)/2+1
,
we see that
2{l}-1\leq 2{n}-3\leq r{{}_{M}}
.
By Lemma
3.3
,
{D}_{2{l}-1}\equiv(-{Y})^{2{l}}\pmod{{x}}
or
{D}_{2{l}-1}\equiv(-{Y})^{2{l}}\pmod{{z}}
,
so
{D}_{2{l}-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}
. Therefore
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{l}-1)}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{l}}
by Theorem
1.5
.

We next show that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}
.
First, suppose that
r{{}_{M}}
is odd; then
r{{}_{M}}=2{n}-3
.
Note that
{n}\geq 2
.
By Theorem
1.5
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-3}+({D}_{2{n}-3}),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}{\mathfrak{p}}+({D}_{2{n}-2}),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}.

It follows from Lemmas
2.2
and
4.2
that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-3}+({D}_{2{n}-3})\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-3}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}={\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1},

and

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}{\mathfrak{p}}+({D}_{2{n}-2})\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}+{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}={\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-3)}{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}.

Next, suppose that
r{{}_{M}}
is even; then
r{{}_{M}}=2{n}-2
.
By Theorem
1.5
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-2}+({D}_{2{n}-2}),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}{\mathfrak{p}}+({D}_{2{n}-1}).

By Lemma
4.2
,

{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}={\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-2}+({D}_{2{n}-2})\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{2{n}-2}+{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}={\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}

and

{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-1)}={\mathfrak{p}}^{(2{n}-2)}{\mathfrak{p}}+({D}_{2{n}-1})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}-1}{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}={\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}^{{n}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

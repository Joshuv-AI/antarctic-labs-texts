Goldman form, flat connections and stable vector bundles
Leon A. Takhtajan

Abstract
. We consider the moduli space 𝒩 \mathscr{N} of stable vector bundles of degree 0 0 over a compact Riemann surface and the affine bundle 𝒜 → 𝒩 \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{N} of flat connections. Following the similarity between the Teichmüller spaces and the moduli of bundles, we introduce the analogue of the quasi-Fuchsian projective connections — local holomorphic sections of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} — that allow to pull back the Liouville symplectic form on T ∗ ​ 𝒩 T^{*}\mathscr{N} to 𝒜 \mathscr{A} . We prove that the pullback of the Goldman form to 𝒜 \mathscr{A} by the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence coincides with the pullback of the Liouville form. We also include a simple proof, in the spirit of Riemann bilinear relations, of the classic result – the pullback of Goldman symplectic form to 𝒩 \mathscr{N} by the Narasimhan-Seshadri connection is the natural symplectic form on 𝒩 \mathscr{N} , introduced by Narasimhan and Atiyah & Bott.

Introduction

There is a close similarity between the theory of projective connections on Riemann surfaces, and the theory of flat connections in vector bundles over a Riemann surface (more generally, connections with central curvature), and it is worthwhile to describe it in some detail.

Namely, the main object in the first theory is a holomorphic affine bundle
\mathscr{P}_{g}\to T_{g}
of projective connections over the Teichmüller space

of compact Riemann surfaces of genus

\mathscr{P}_{g}

T_{g}

[
13

The Teichmüller space

2.
Moduli space of flat vector bundles

2.1.
Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g>1
, and let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
and degree
d
. Denote by

\mu(E)=d/n
the slope of
E
. The bundle
E
is said to be stable, if for every proper holomorphic subbundle
F
of
E
we have

\mu(F)<\mu(E).

In what follows we will consider stable bundles of degree
0
— flat bundles. The theorem of Narasimhan-Seshadri — an analogue of the Fuchsian uniformization for vector bundles — states that every stable flat bundle over a Riemann surface
X
of genus
g>1
arises from an irreducible unitary representation of
the fundamental group
\pi_{1}
of
X
.

Namely, let
X\simeq\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
,
where

\mathbb{H}=\{z=x+\sqrt{-1}y\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\}

is the Lobachevsky (hyperbolic) plane, and
\Gamma
is a co-compact Fuchsian group uniformizing the Riemann surface
X
, so that
\Gamma\simeq\pi_{1}
.
Let
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(n)
be an irreducible unitary representation of
\Gamma
, and let
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
be the trivial bundle with the following
\Gamma
-action:

(z,v)\mapsto(\gamma z,\rho(\gamma)v),\quad\text{where}\quad z\in\mathbb{H},\;v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma.

Denote by
E_{\rho}
the corresponding quotient bundle
\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}\simeq X
— a holomorphic flat bundle over
X
.
We have the following key result.

Theorem

(Narasimhan-Seshadri)
.

A holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
of rank
n
and degree
0

is stable if and only if it is isomorphic to a bundle
E_{\rho}
, where
\rho
is an irreducible unitary
representation of the group
\Gamma
. The bundles
E_{\rho_{1}}
and
E_{\rho_{2}}
are isomorphic if
and only if the representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are equivalent.

The standard Hermitian metric in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defines a metric in the trivial bundle
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
. This metric is
\Gamma
-invariant and determines the Hermitian
metric
h_{E}
in the quotient bundle
E
, and the Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{End}\,E}
in the endomorphism bundle
\mathrm{End}\,E
. Corresponding canonical connection
\nabla_{E}=d+A_{E}
in the Hermitian bundle
(E,h_{E})
— a unique connection, compatible with the metric
h_{E}
and with the
holomorphic structure in
E
— is called the
Narasimhan-Seshadri connection
. It is associated with the trivial connection
\nabla=d+0
in the trivial bundle
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}
, and has zero curvature. Conversely, it was proved by Donaldson
[
5
]
, that
an indecomposable degree
0
holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
is stable, if there exists a Hermitian metric in
E
such that the corresponding canonical connection has zero curvature.

The metric
h_{\mathrm{End}\,E}
determines a Hodge
\ast
-operator in the vector space
\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
of smooth
(0,1)
-forms on
X
with values in
\mathrm{End}\,E
. The Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
is the completion
of
\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
with respect to the Hodge inner product

(2.1)

\langle\mu,\nu\rangle=\int_{X}\mu\wedge\ast\nu,

where
\wedge
is a composition of the exterior product of differential forms on
X
and the fiber-wise trace map
\mathrm{tr}
in
\mathrm{End}\,E
.
Denote by
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
the zero eigenspace of the corresponding
\bar{\partial}
-Laplace operator in
\mathfrak{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
, the subspace of harmonic

\mathrm{End}\,E
-valued
(0,1)
-forms on
X
, and by

P:\mathfrak{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)\to\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)

— the corresponding orthogonal projection operator.

In case
E=E_{\rho}
the vector space
\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
is naturally identified with the space of smooth
\mathrm{End}\,\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{H}
satisfying

(2.2)

\mu(\gamma z)\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}=\mathrm{Ad}\,\rho(\gamma)\mu(z)\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

where
\mathrm{Ad}\,\rho
is the adjoint representation of the group
\Gamma
in the vector space
\mathrm{End}\,\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Correspondingly,
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
is the space of antiholomorphic functions
\mu(z)
satisfying (
2.2
). The Hodge
\ast
-operator is
\ast\mu=-\sqrt{-1}\mu^{\ast}
, where

\mu^{\ast}=\bar{\mu}^{\mathrm{t}}
is the Hermitian conjugation of a
n\times n
-matrix
\mu
, and the Hodge inner product (
2.1
) becomes

\langle\mu,\nu\rangle=\sqrt{-1}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left(\mu(z)\nu(z)^{\ast}\right)dz\wedge d\bar{z},

where
F
is a fundamental domain for
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{H}
.

2.2.
The moduli space

The moduli space of
stable vector bundles of rank
n
and degree
0
over a compact Riemann surface
X

of genus
g>1
is the set
\mathscr{N}
of isomorphism classes
\{E\}
of stables bundles
E
over
X
. By the Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem,
\mathscr{N}
coincides with the
\mathrm{U}(n)
-character
variety

\mathscr{K}=\mathrm{Hom}_{0}(\pi_{1},\mathrm{U}(n))/\mathrm{U}(n),

where it is understood that
\pi_{1}\simeq\Gamma
, and the subscript
0
stands for the irreducible representations. The moduli space is a
complex manifold of dimension
d=n^{2}(g-1)+1
(see
[
16
,
17
]
).

Specifically, according
to the Kodaira-Spencer theory of deformation of complex structures
4
4

4

See
[
16
,
17
]
) for the details in connection with vector bundles.
,
the holomorphic tangent space
T_{E}\mathscr{N}
to the manifold
\mathscr{N}
at the point
\{E\}
corresponding
to a stable bundle
E
is identified with the complex vector space
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
of harmonic

(0,1)
-forms.
The holomorphic cotangent space
T^{*}_{E}\mathscr{N}
is then identified with the complex vector space
\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
of harmonic
(1,0)
-forms, the space of Higgs fields.
The natural pairing between these vector spaces is given by the integration:

\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)\otimes\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)\ni\theta\otimes\mu\mapsto\int_{X}\theta\wedge\mu\in\mathbb{C}.

The Hermitian inner product (
2.1
) in the vector spaces
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
determines a natural Hermitian metric
ds^{2}
in
\mathscr{N}
, introduced by Narasimhan
[
18
]
in 1969, and later by Atiyah and Bott
[
3
]
. This metric is an analogue of the Weil-Petersson metric in Teichmüller spaces, and also is Kähler
[
18
,
3
]
. We call it the
Narasimhan-Atiyah-Bott metric
, and denote by
\omega_{NAB}
its symplectic form, the associated
(1,1)
-form on
\mathscr{N}
,

\omega_{NAB}=-\frac{1}{2}\im ds^{2}.

2.3.
Complex coordinates

It was shown in
[
23
]
, that is possible to describe the complex structure of the moduli space
\mathscr{N}
by introducing complex coordinates in a neighborhood of each point. These coordinates are analogous of the Bers coordinates in Teichmüller spaces, and are convenient for performing local computations.

Namely, realize a stable bundle
E
as the quotient bundle
E_{\rho}
corresponding to the irreducible unitary representation
\rho
of the group
\Gamma
, and
\mu\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
— as
\mathrm{End}\,\mathbb{C}^{n}
-function on
\mathbb{H}
, satisfying (
2.2
). The analog of the Beltrami equation in the Teichmüller theory is the following
\bar{\partial}
-problem

(2.3)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}f(z)=f(z)\mu(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{H}.

The next result was proved in
[
23
]
. For convenience of the reader, we present here its simple proof.

Proposition 1
.

If
\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
is sufficiently close to zero, then equation (
2.3
) has is a unique solution
f^{\mu}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})

with the following properties.

(i)

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma

f^{\mu}(\gamma z)=\rho^{\mu}(\gamma)f^{\mu}(z)\rho(\gamma)^{-1},

where
\rho^{\mu}
is an irreducible representation of the group
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{U}(n)
.

(ii)

For a fixed
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
(say
z_{0}=\sqrt{-1}
) the matrix
f^{\mu}(z_{0})
is positive definite and
\det f^{\mu}(z_{0})=1
.

Proof.

Since
\mu\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
is an anti-holomorphic matrix-valued function on
\mathbb{H}
, the ordinary differential equation

(2.4)

\frac{df}{d\bar{z}}=f(z)\mu(z)

has a unique anti-holomorphic solution — the
n\times n
matrix-valued function
f_{\mu}(z)
normalized by
f_{\mu}(z_{0})=I
, where
I
is the identity matrix. It follows from (
2.2
) that

f_{\mu}(\gamma z)=\rho_{\mu}(\gamma)f_{\mu}(z)\rho(\gamma)^{-1}\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

where
\rho_{\mu}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
is a representation of the group
\Gamma
.
For sufficiently small
\mu
the quotient bundle

E_{\rho_{\mu}}=\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})
associated with the representation
\rho_{\mu}
is stable, so representation
\rho_{\mu}
is irreducible. According to the Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem, there
is an irreducible representation
\rho^{\mu}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(n)
such that
E_{\rho_{\mu}}\simeq E_{\rho^{\mu}}
. In other words, there is holomorphic mapping

g^{\mu}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
, such that

g^{\mu}(\gamma z)=\rho^{\mu}(\gamma)g^{\mu}(z)\rho_{\mu}(\gamma)^{-1}\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

and we put
f^{\mu}(z)=g^{\mu}(z)f_{\mu}(z)
.

The proof of uniqueness is also easy. Suppose
f_{1}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)
are two solutions of (
2.3
) satisfying property (i) with unitary representations
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
. Then
h(z)=f_{1}(z)f_{2}(z)^{-1}
is a holomorphic function satisfying
h(\gamma z)=\rho_{1}(\gamma)h(z)\rho_{2}(\gamma)^{-1}
. It determines an isomorphism
E_{\rho_{1}}\simeq E_{\rho_{2}}
, and by the Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem,
h(z)
is a scalar
multiple of a unitary matrix; it follows form the property (ii) that
h(z)=I
.
∎

Now for each stable bundle
E=E_{\rho}
choose a basis
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{d}
in the vector space
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
, and put

\mu=\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{d}\mu_{d}
. According to Proposition
1
, this introduces complex coordinates
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})
in the coordinate chart at the point
\{E\}\in\mathscr{N}
— a neighborhood
U
of
\{E\}
— determined by the condition that
\mu
is sufficiently close to zero. Indeed, for different
\mu
representations
\rho^{\mu}
are not equivalent, as it follows from the general Kodaira-Spencer theory. It can be also verified directly using Corollary
1
, as in
[
2
, Ch. VI D]
for the Teichmüller space. These coordinates transform holomorphically and endow
\mathscr{N}
with the structure of a complex manifold.

Specifically, the differential of such coordinate
transformation in the intersection of the coordinate charts at
\{E_{\rho}\}
and
\{E_{\rho^{\mu}}\}
is a linear mapping of vector spaces
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})
and
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho^{\mu}})
, explicitly given by the formula

\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})\ni\nu\mapsto P_{\mu}(\mathrm{Ad}f^{\mu}(\nu))\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho^{\mu}}).

Here
P_{\mu}
is the orthogonal projection operator onto
\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho^{\mu}})
, and

\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{\mu}
is a fiberwise linear mapping
\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho}\to\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho^{\mu}}
, where
\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{\mu}(\nu)=f^{\mu}\cdot\nu\cdot(f^{\mu})^{-1}
.
Corresponding vector fields
\partial/\partial\varepsilon_{i}
on
U
at each point
\{E_{\rho^{\mu}}\}\in U

are given by

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{i}}\right|_{\mu}=P_{\mu}(\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{\mu}(\mu_{i}))\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho^{\mu}}),\quad i=1,\dots,d.

In complex coordinates we have the following expression for the Narasimhan-Atiyah-Bott symplectic form,

(2.5)

\omega_{NAB}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mu}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\varepsilon}_{\nu}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\langle\mu,\nu\rangle,

where
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mu}}
and
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\varepsilon}_{\nu}}
are the holomorphic and antiholomorphic tangent vectors at
\{E\}\in\mathscr{N}
corresponding to
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
respectively.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that unitary representations

Lemma 1

A(\gamma z)\gamma^{\prime}(z)=\mathrm{Ad}\,\rho(\gamma)A(z)\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma.

The parallel transport equation

\frac{dg}{dz}+Ag=0

has a unique holomorphic solution: a non-degenerate matrix-valued function
g(z)
on
\mathbb{H}
, satisfying
g(z_{0})=I
and

(4.1)

g(\gamma z)=\rho(\gamma)g(z)\sigma(\gamma)^{-1}\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,

thus specifying a representation
\sigma:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
The map
g
is an isomorphism
E_{\sigma}\simeq E_{\rho}
of the quotient bundles, so
the representation
\sigma
is irreducible and determines constant transition functions for the bundle
E
.

Conversely, a choice of constant transitions functions for the bundle
E
determines a representation
\rho
, and the image of the fiber
\mathscr{A}(E)
under the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence is the set of equivalence classes of constant transition functions of the stable
vector bundle
E
.

4.2.
Holomorphic representations

Generalizing Remark
2.1
in §
2.3
, here we define, for each coordinate chart, a holomorphic family of irreducible representations. Namely, choose
\{E\}\in\mathscr{N}
and
\nabla=d+A\in\mathscr{A}(E)
and, using the map
g
above, realize the bundle
E=E_{\rho}

as
E_{\sigma}
. The quotient bundle
E_{\sigma}
is a local system with de Rham differential, so Dolbeault cohomology group
H^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
can be realized
as
H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
—the space of antiholomorphic matrix-valued functions
\mu(z)
on
\mathbb{H}
,
satisfying

\mu(\gamma z)\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}=\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(\gamma)\mu(z)\quad\text{for all}\quad\gamma\in\Gamma.

The solution
f(z)
of the ordinary differential equation

(4.2)

\frac{df}{d\bar{z}}(z)=f(z)\mu(z)

normalized by
f(z_{0})=I
satisfies

(4.3)

f(\gamma z)=\sigma_{\mu}(\gamma)f(z)\sigma(\gamma)^{-1},

and for small enough
\mu
determines a family
\sigma_{\mu}
of irreducible representations. Choose a basis
\mu_{i}
in
H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
and define complex coordinates in a neighborhood of
E_{\sigma}
by
\mu=\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{d}\mu_{d}
. These coordinates are the same as in Proposition
1
, and we obtain a holomorphic
family of representations parameterized by
\sigma
; construction in Remark
2.1
corresponds to the case
A=0
.

Indeed, it is sufficient to verify that the map

H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})\ni\mu\to\tilde{\mu}=P(\hat{\mu})\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho}),\quad\hat{\mu}=\mathrm{Ad}\,g(\mu),

is injective, and hence is an isomorphism. Namely, if
\tilde{\mu}=0
, then we have for all
\theta\in\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})
,

0=\int_{X}\tilde{\mu}\wedge\theta=\int_{X}\hat{\mu}\wedge\theta=\int_{X}\mu\wedge\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}(\theta).

However, since
g:E_{\sigma}\to E_{\rho}
is an isomorphism of holomorphic vector bundles, the map

\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}:\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})\to H^{1,0}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})

is an isomorphism, so
\mu=0
.

The inner product in
H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
is determined by requiring that the isomorphism
H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})\simeq\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})
is an isometry.

Holomorphic sections

. Clearly, this definition does not depend on the choice of section

.

Remark 4.1
.

Here the pairing between

The bundle
\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{N}

However, the bundle

). Indeed, if
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}

-form on
\mathscr{N}
. Since in this case

, which is obviously a contradiction

6

Namely, for a coordinate chart

centered at

use the representation

, where

), and realize each bundle in

, where

.

be a connection in

\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}

In analogy with the Teichmüller theory, when corresponding projective connections come

For a smooth map

\mathcal{A}(f)=f^{-1}(z)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(z)dz.

The operator

\mathcal{A}(gh)=\mathrm{Ad}\,h^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{A}(h),

Lemma 3
.

The

\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{NS}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}
on

\left.(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{NS}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma})\right|_{E_{\sigma_{\mu}}}=\mathcal{A}(g^{\mu})\in\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma_{\mu}}),\quad\{E_{\sigma_{\mu}}\}\in U.

The Narasimhan-Seshadri connection in

4.4.
Quasi-unitary reciprocity

Our next result is a vector bundle analogue of the quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity: the difference between Fuchsian and quasi-Fuchsian projective structures is a
\partial
-closed
(1,0)
-form on the Teichmüller space
[
14

20
]
. Namely, we have the following statement.

Proposition 2

(Quasi-unitary reciprocity)
.

\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}

.
Since each
\{E\}\in U

E

2.3

\partial\vartheta(\mu,\nu)=0

\mu,\nu\in H^{0,1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
. Denote by
L_{\mu}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{\mu}}
and
L_{\nu}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{\nu}}
corresponding vector fields on

.
Since
[L_{\mu},L_{\nu}]=0
,
we have by Cartan formula

\partial\vartheta\left(L_{\mu},L_{\nu}\right)=L_{\mu}\vartheta(L_{\nu})-L_{\nu}\vartheta(L_{\mu}).

Now consider commutative diagram (
4.4
) with
\varepsilon\mu
instead of
\mu
. Using Lemma
3
, we have at
\{E_{\sigma_{\varepsilon\mu}}\}\in U
,

\displaystyle\vartheta(L_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathcal{A}(g^{\varepsilon\mu})\wedge\mathrm{Ad}\,f_{\varepsilon\mu}(\nu)=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{-1}_{\varepsilon\mu}\cdot\mathcal{A}(g^{\varepsilon\mu})\wedge\nu.

It follows from (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) that

(4.7)

\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{-1}_{\varepsilon\mu}\cdot\mathcal{A}(g^{\varepsilon\mu})=\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{A}(f^{\varepsilon\hat{\mu}})+\mathcal{A}(g)-\mathcal{A}(f_{\varepsilon\mu}).

Differentiate, in a complex-analytic sense, equation (
4.7
) with respect to
\varepsilon
and set
\varepsilon=0
. Since
f_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)
is holomorphic in
\varepsilon
and antiholomorphic in
z
,
we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}f_{\varepsilon\mu}(z)\right)=0,

so

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\left(\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{-1}_{\varepsilon\mu}\cdot\mathcal{A}(g^{\varepsilon\mu})\right)=\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{+}}{\partial z}\right).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\vartheta\left(L_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{+}}{\partial z}\right)dz\wedge\nu=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\frac{\partial\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{+}}{\partial z}dz\wedge\hat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\frac{\partial\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{+}}{\partial z}(z)\hat{\nu}(z)\right\}dz\wedge d\bar{z}.

Using Hodge decomposition

\hat{\mu}=P(\hat{\mu})+\bar{\partial}F,\quad\hat{\nu}=P(\hat{\nu})+\bar{\partial}G,

where
P(\hat{\mu}),P(\hat{\nu})\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})
and
F,G\in\Omega^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\rho})
, and taking into account Corollary
2
, we obtain

\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{+}=\mathscr{E}+F,

where
\mathscr{E}
is the Eichler integral for
P(\hat{\mu})
. Since
\mathscr{E}
is antiholomorphic and
F
and
G
are
\Gamma
-automorphic functions with representation
\mathrm{Ad}\,\rho
, by Stokes theorem we have

\displaystyle L_{\mu}\vartheta(L_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}(z)\left(P(\hat{\nu})+\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)\right)\right\}dz\wedge d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}(z)\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)\right\}dz\wedge d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\frac{\partial G}{\partial z}(z)\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)\right\}dz\wedge d\bar{z}=L_{\nu}\vartheta(L_{\mu}).

For convenience of the reader here is the proof of the second statement, which is Theorem 1 in
[
23
]
. Since
\vartheta
is a
(1,0)
-form and
[L_{\nu},L_{\bar{\mu}}]=0
,
Cartan formula simplifies

\bar{\partial}\vartheta\left(L_{\nu},L_{\bar{\mu}}\right)=-L_{\bar{\mu}}\vartheta(L_{\nu}).

Differentiating, in a complex-analytic sense, equation (
4.7
) with respect to
\bar{\varepsilon}
and setting
\varepsilon=0
, we obtain

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\varepsilon}}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\left(\mathrm{Ad}\,f^{-1}_{\varepsilon\mu}\cdot\mathcal{A}(g^{\varepsilon\mu})\right)=\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{f}^{\hat{\mu}}_{-}}{\partial z}\right).

It follows from the Hodge decomposition and Corollary
2
that
\dot{f}_{-}^{\hat{\mu}}=-\mathscr{E}^{*}
, so

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\vartheta\left(L_{\nu},L_{\bar{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}\mathrm{Ad}\,g^{-1}\cdot P(\hat{\mu})^{*}\wedge\nu=\sqrt{-1}\int_{X}P(\hat{\mu})^{*}\wedge\hat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\langle P(\hat{\nu}),P(\hat{\mu})\rangle,

and it follows from (
2.5
) that
\bar{\partial}\vartheta=-2\sqrt{-1}\,\omega_{NAB}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Formula
L_{\mu}\vartheta(L_{\nu})=L_{\nu}\vartheta(L_{\mu})
is a precise analogue of the quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity (see
[
14
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
20
, Proposition 4.1]
).
Our commutative diagram (
4.4
) is analogous to the commutative diagram (3.2) in
[
20
]
. However, for weight 2 automorphic forms we no longer can use series over the group
\Gamma
,
as in
[

20
]
for the forms of weight 4, so we replace this argument by using the Hodge decomposition.

5.
Liouville form on
\mathscr{A}

5.1.
Holomorphic symplectic form

The total space of the holomorphic cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
of a complex manifold
M
carries a natural holomorphic symplectic
(2,0)
-form
\omega_{L}
— the Liouville symplectic form.
Namely,
\omega_{L}=d\theta_{L}
, where the holomorphic
(1,0)
-form
\theta_{L}
— the Liouville 1-form — is defined by

\theta_{L}(u)=p(\pi_{*}u),\quad\text{where}\quad u\in T_{(p,q)}T^{*}M,\;\;p\in T^{*}_{q}M,

and
\pi:T^{*}M\to M
is the canonical projection.
It is convenient to use on
T^{*}U
local coordinates

(\bm{p},\bm{q})=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q^{1},\dots,q^{n}),

where
\bm{q}=(q^{1},\dots,q^{n})
— are complex coordinates on
U\subset M
, and complex coordinates
\bm{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
correspond to the basis
dq^{1},\dots,dq^{n}
in
T^{*}_{q}M
for
q\in U
. In this notation, commonly used by physicists, we have

\theta_{L}=\bm{p}d\bm{q}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}dq^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{L}=d\bm{p}\wedge d\bm{q}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}dp_{k}\wedge dq^{k}.

The following statement is trivial, but rather useful (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
).

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha
be a
(1,0)
-form on
M
, considered as a section of
\pi:T^{*}M\to M
. Then

\alpha^{*}(\theta_{L})=\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha^{*}(\omega_{L})=d\alpha.

Proof.

In physics notation, this is a tautology. Namely, in local coordinates

\alpha=\bm{a}(\bm{q})d\bm{q}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{i}(q^{1},\dots,q^{n})dq^{i},

so
M\ni q\mapsto\alpha_{q}=\bm{a}(\bm{q})\in T^{*}_{q}M

and
\alpha^{*}(\theta_{L})=\alpha
. A formal argument is also trivial: since
\pi\circ\alpha=\id_{M}
, then for
v\in T_{q}M
we have
\alpha_{\ast}(v)\in T_{(\alpha_{q},q)}T^{\ast}M
and

\alpha^{\ast}(\theta)(v)=\theta(\alpha_{\ast}(v))=\alpha_{q}(\pi_{\ast}(\alpha_{\ast}v))=\alpha(v).\qed

5.2.
Pullback of Liouville form

Here we apply results in §
5.1
to the case
M=\mathscr{N}
. The Liouville form
\omega_{L}
at a point
(\theta,E)\in T^{*}\mathscr{N}
, where
\theta\in\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
, evaluated at the tangent vectors
(\theta_{1},\mu_{1}),(\theta_{2},\mu_{2})\in T_{(\theta,E)}T^{*}\mathscr{N}
,
can be written as

(5.1)

\omega_{L}((\theta_{1},\mu_{1}),(\theta_{2},\mu_{2}))=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{X}(\theta_{1}\wedge\mu_{2}-\theta_{2}\wedge\mu_{1}),

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{H}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathscr{H}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
.

As discussed in §
4.3
,
every smooth section
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}:\mathscr{N}\to\mathscr{A}
determines an isomorphism
\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}}:\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\sim}T^{\ast}\mathscr{N}
by

\mathscr{A}(E)\ni A\mapsto\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}}(A)=A-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}(E)\in T^{*}_{E}\mathscr{N}

and turns
\mathscr{A}
into a symplectic manifold with the symplectic form

\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}}^{*}(\omega_{L})
. If the section
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}
was holomorphic,
\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}}^{*}(\omega_{L})
would be a holomorphic symplectic form on
\mathscr{A}
, but the bundle
\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{N}
has no global holomorphic sections.

Nevertheless, one can use local holomorphic sections to pull back the Liouville symplectic form
\omega_{L}
to
\mathscr{A}
. The following simple statement (cf.
[
13
]
)
guarantees when such local symplectic forms on
\mathscr{A}
agree.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}
and
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}
be holomorphic sections of
\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{N}
over an open
U\subset\mathscr{N}
. The equality
\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}}^{*}(\omega_{L})=\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}}^{*}(\omega_{L})
on
\mathscr{A}_{U}
— a restriction of
\mathscr{A}
over
U
—
is equivalent to the condition
\pi^{*}(\partial(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}))=0
, where
\pi:T^{*}\mathscr{N}\to\mathscr{N}
is the canonical projection.

Proof.

We have for
A\in\mathscr{A}(E)
,
\{E\}\in U
,

\displaystyle(\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}}-\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}})(A)

\displaystyle=A-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}(E)-(A-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}(E))

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}(E)-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}(E),

so
\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}}-\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}}=(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1})\circ\pi
. It follows from Lemma
4
that

\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}}^{*}(\theta_{L})-\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}}^{*}(\theta_{L})=\pi^{*}((\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1})^{*}\theta_{L})=\pi^{*}(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}).

and

\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}}^{*}(\omega_{L})-\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}}^{*}(\omega_{L})=\pi^{*}(d(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}))=\pi^{*}(\partial(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{1}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{2})).\qed

Now over each coordinate chart
U
use a quasi-unitary section
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}
of the bundle
\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{N}
to define a holomorphic
(2,0)
symplectic form

\omega_{U,\sigma}=\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}}^{*}(\omega_{L})\quad\text{over}\quad\mathscr{A}_{U}.

We have the following result.

Proposition 3
.

Holomorphic
(2,0)
-forms
\omega_{U,\sigma}
determine a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
on
\mathscr{A}
. The form
\omega
does not depend on the choices of local quasi-unitary sections.

Proof.

We need to show that
\omega_{U_{1},\sigma_{1}}=\omega_{U_{2},\sigma_{2}}
on
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq\emptyset
. By Proposition
2
, we have

\partial(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{NS}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma_{1}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{NS}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma_{2}})=0

in coordinate charts
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, so that
\partial(\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma_{1}}-\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma_{2}})=0
on
U_{1}\cap U_{2}
. Now the result follows form
Lemma
5
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

in

2
and Lemma

\jmath^{*}_{\sigma}(\omega_{L})=-2\sqrt{-1}\,\omega_{NAB}.

6.
Goldman form on
\mathscr{A}

Let
\nabla(t)
be a smooth curve in the complex manifold
\mathscr{A}
of zero curvature connections,
\nabla(0)=\nabla\in\mathscr{A}(E)
for some
\{E\}\in\mathscr{N}
, and let
v=\dot{\nabla}
be its tangent vector at
t=0
, considered as a vector in the holomorphic tangent space to
\mathscr{A}
at
\nabla
. Let
\sigma=\mathscr{Q}(\nabla)
be the holonomy of the connection
\nabla
in
E
. Under the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, we have a curve
\mathscr{Q}(\nabla(t))
in the character variety
\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
with the holomorphic tangent vector
\mathscr{Q}_{*}(v)\in T_{\sigma}\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
at
t=0
.

On the other hand, over a neighborhood
U
we have an isomorphism
\imath=\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}}:\mathscr{A}_{U}\xrightarrow{\sim}T^{*}U
(see §§
4.2
-
4.3
and §
5.2
) and we can consider, for small
t
,
the image of the curve
\nabla(t)
in
T^{*}U
with the holomorphic tangent vector
\imath_{*}(v)
.
According to Proposition
3
, the pullback of the Liouville form
\omega_{L}
to
\mathscr{A}_{U}

does not depend on a choice of the isomorphism
\imath
.

6.1.
Differential of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence

Here we explicitly describe the differential of the map
hol=\mathscr{Q}\circ\imath^{-1}:T^{*}U\to\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
for
\imath=\imath_{\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}}}{\scalebox{.4}[.4]{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{S}$}}_{\sigma}}
corresponding to the isomorphism
E\simeq E_{\sigma}
, as in §§
4.2
-
4.3
.

Namely, let
E(t)
be the projection of the curve
\nabla(t)
in
\mathscr{A}
to
U
. Each bundle in
U
is realized as
E_{\sigma_{\mu}}
, and the curve

E(t)
at
t=0
has a tangent vector
\mu\in H_{\mathrm{DR}}^{0,1}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
. Under the isomorphism
\imath:\mathscr{A}_{U}\xrightarrow{\sim}T^{*}U

we have

\nabla(t)=d+A_{t},\quad\text{where}\quad A_{t}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma_{t\mu}}),\quad A_{0}=0,

and without changing the tangent vector
v=\dot{\nabla}
we can assume that
E(t)=E_{\sigma_{t\mu}}
.
Put

B_{t}=\mathrm{Ad}f^{-1}_{t\mu}\cdot A_{t}\in\Omega^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma}).

Then

T_{\nabla}\mathscr{A}\ni v\mapsto\imath_{*}(v)=(P(\dot{B}),\mu)\in T_{(0,E_{\sigma})}(T^{*}\mathscr{N}),

where

\dot{B}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}B_{t}\in\Omega^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma}),

and
P
is the projection operator onto
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1,0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E_{\sigma})
.

The image of the curve
A_{t}
under the map
hol=\mathscr{Q}\circ\imath^{-1}
is a curve
\sigma_{t}
in the character variety
\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
— a family of irreducible representations
\sigma_{t}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
, defined by

g_{t}(\gamma z)=\sigma_{t\mu}(\gamma)g_{t}(z)\sigma_{t}(\gamma)^{-1},

where the functions
g_{t}
satisfy the parallel transport equation

(6.1)

\frac{dg_{t}}{dz}(z)+A_{t}(z)g_{t}(z)=0,

and
A_{t}=A_{t}(z)dz
.
The holomorphic tangent vector to
\sigma_{t}
at
t=0
is
\chi\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma})
, where

(6.2)

\chi(\gamma)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\sigma_{t}(\gamma)\sigma(\gamma)^{-1},

so

hol_{*}(P(\dot{B}),\mu)=\chi.

Cocycle
\chi
admits the following expression in terms of
\dot{B}
and
\mu
.
Put
h_{t}(z)=f_{t\mu}^{-1}(z)g_{t}(z)
. It follows from (
2.4
) and (
6.1
) that the functions
h_{t}(z)
satisfy a system of partial differential equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial z}(z)

\displaystyle=-B_{t}(z)h_{t}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{t}}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)

\displaystyle=-t\mu(z)h_{t}(z),

where
B_{t}=B_{t}(z)dz
,
and have the property

(6.3)

h_{t}(\gamma z)=\sigma(\gamma)h_{t}(z)\sigma_{t}(\gamma)^{-1}.

Differentiating this system with respect to
t
at
t=0
and putting

\dot{h}(z)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}h_{t}(z),

we get the system

(6.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{h}}{\partial z}(z)

\displaystyle=-\dot{B}(z),

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{h}}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\mu(z),

and it follows from (
6.3
) that

(6.6)

\chi(\gamma)=\sigma(\gamma)\dot{h}(z)\sigma(\gamma)^{-1}-\dot{h}(\gamma z).

Thus we expressed
\chi=hol_{*}(P(\dot{B}),\mu)
in terms of the solution of the system (
6.4
)–(
6.5
).

6.2.
Pullback of Goldman form

Here we prove our main result.

Theorem 6.1
.

The pullback to
\mathscr{A}
of the Goldman symplectic form
\omega_{G}
on
\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
by the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence is
-2\sqrt{-1}
times the pullback to
\mathscr{A}
of the Liouville symplectic form

\omega_{L}
on
T^{*}\mathscr{N}
by the quasi-unitary connections.

Proof.

Let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be holomorphic tangent vectors to
\mathscr{A}
at a point
A\in\mathscr{A}(E)
, zero curvature connection in
E
with the holonomy
\sigma\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{C}}
, and let

\imath_{*}(v_{1})=(P(\dot{B}_{1}),\mu_{1})
and
\imath_{*}(v_{2})=(P(\dot{B}_{2}),\mu_{2})
. We have, using (
5.1
) and equations (
6.4
)–(
6.5
),

\displaystyle\omega_{L}(\imath_{*}(v_{1}),\imath_{*}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{X}\left\{P(\dot{B}_{1})\wedge\mu_{2}-P(\dot{B}_{2})\wedge\mu_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left(\dot{B}_{1}(z)\mu_{2}(z)-\dot{B}_{2}(z)\mu_{1}(z)\right)dz\wedge d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{h}_{1}}{\partial z}(z)\frac{\partial\dot{h}_{2}}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)-\frac{\partial\dot{h}_{2}}{\partial z}(z)\frac{\partial\dot{h}_{1}}{\partial\bar{z}}(z)\right)dz\wedge d\bar{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\iint_{F}\mathrm{tr}(d\dot{h}_{1}\wedge d\dot{h}_{2})=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{\partial F}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{h}_{1}d\dot{h}_{2}).

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we continue, using (
3.8
) and (
6.6
),

\displaystyle\int_{\partial F}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{h}_{1}d\dot{h}_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\left\{\int_{S_{i}}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{h}_{1}d\dot{h}_{2})-\int_{\lambda_{i}(S_{i})}\mathrm{tr}(\dot{h}_{1}d\dot{h}_{2})\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\int_{S_{i}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\chi_{1}(\lambda_{i})\,\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(\lambda_{i})\cdot d\dot{h}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{2g}\mathrm{tr}\left(\chi_{1}(\lambda^{-1}_{i})\int_{S_{i}}d\dot{h}_{2}\right).

Using again (
6.6
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{S_{k}}d\dot{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{R_{k-1}z_{0}}^{R_{k-1}a_{k}z_{0}}d\dot{h}_{2}=\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k-1})\cdot\int_{z_{0}}^{a_{k}z_{0}}d\dot{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k-1})\cdot\chi_{2}(a_{k})+(\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k-1}a_{k})-\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k-1}))\cdot\dot{h}_{2}(z_{0})

and

\displaystyle\int_{S_{k+g}}d\dot{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{R_{k}z_{0}}^{R_{k}b_{k}z_{0}}d\dot{h}_{2}=-\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k})\cdot\int_{z_{0}}^{b_{k}z_{0}}d\dot{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k})\cdot\chi_{2}(b_{k})-(\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k}b_{k})-\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k}))\cdot\dot{h}_{2}(z_{0}),

Using (
3.9
) and (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\chi_{1}(\lambda^{-1}_{k})\int_{S_{k}}d\dot{h}_{2}\right\}=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left(\chi_{1}(\alpha_{k})\,\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k-1})\cdot\chi_{2}(a_{k})\right)+I_{1}

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\left(\chi_{1}(a_{k}^{-1}R_{k-1}^{-1}\alpha_{k})-\chi_{1}(a_{k}^{-1}R_{k-1}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(R_{k-1}^{-1}\alpha_{k})+\chi_{1}(R_{k-1}^{-1})\right)\dot{h}_{2}(z_{0})\right\}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\chi_{1}(\lambda^{-1}_{k})\int_{S_{k+g}}d\dot{h}_{2}\right\}=-\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left(\chi_{1}(\beta_{k})\,\mathrm{Ad}\,\sigma(R_{k})\cdot\chi_{2}(b_{k})\right)+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\left(\chi_{1}(b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k})-\chi_{1}(b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(R_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k})+\chi_{1}(R_{k}^{-1})\right)\dot{h}_{2}(z_{0})\right\}.

Finally, using (
3.4
)–(
3.6
) and relations
R_{k}b_{k}a_{k}=R_{k-1}a_{k}b_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{g}\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\left(\chi_{1}(a_{k}^{-1}R_{k-1}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(a_{k}^{-1}b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}^{-1})+\chi_{1}(b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(R_{k-1}^{-1})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\chi_{1}(b_{k}^{-1}a_{k}^{-1}R_{k-1}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}^{-1})-\chi_{1}(a^{-1}_{k}R_{k-1}^{-1})+\chi_{1}(R_{k}^{-1})\right)\dot{h}_{2}(z_{0})\right\}=0,

so by (
3.7
)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial F}\mathrm{tr}(h_{1}dh_{2})=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\omega_{G}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}).\qed

6.3.
Generalizations

It is straightforward to generalize the obtained results to the moduli space
\mathscr{N}(n,d)
of stable vector bundles of arbitrary rank
n
and degree
d
on
X
(it is sufficient to consider
-n<d\leq 0
). As in
[
17
]
, we realize
X\simeq\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
as an orbifold Riemann surface with conical singularity of order
n
at a given point
x_{0}\in X
, so the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
contains additional elliptic generator
\gamma_{0}
of order
n
with the fixed point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
that projects on
x_{0}
. The Narasimhan-Seshadri theorem says that a stable bundle
E
of rank
n
and degree
d
is obtained from an irreducible representation
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(n)
such that
\rho(\gamma_{0})=\zeta^{-d}I
, where
\zeta
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity.

Correspondingly, a fiber
\mathscr{A}(E)
of the bundle
\mathscr{A}
is the affine space of connections in
E
compatible with the holomorphic structure and having a constant central curvature as in
[
5
]
, or equivalently, connections
\nabla
in
E
such that their curvature (considered as a current) satisfies

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\int_{X}f\wedge\nabla^{2}=\mu(E)\mathrm{tr}\,\!f(x_{0}),

for all
f\in\Omega^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}\,E)
, as in
[
23
]
. Concretely, realizing
E
as a quotient bundle
E_{\rho}
, such connections are
d+A
, where
A
is a meromorphic matrix-valued automorphic form of weight
2
for
\Gamma
with the representation
\mathrm{Ad}\,\rho
, having only simple poles at
\Gamma\cdot z_{0}
with residue
-\mu(E)I
, where
I
is
n\times n
identity matrix. Corresponding
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-character variety consists of equivalence classes of irreducible representations
\sigma:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
such that
\sigma(\gamma_{0})=\zeta^{-d}I
. All results in the paper and their proofs generalize verbatim to this case.

Finally, obtained results can be also generalized to the moduli spaces of parabolic bundles. In this case, one needs to use parabolic cohomology as in
[
15
,
22
]
, the matrix-valued cusp forms of weight
2

as in
[
21
,
15
]
, and define a character variety by specifying conjugacy classes for the parabolic generators of
\Gamma
, as in
[
9
]
.

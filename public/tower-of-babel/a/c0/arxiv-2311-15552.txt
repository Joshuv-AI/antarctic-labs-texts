Role of partial functionals in the study of variational systems Journal: Applied Mathematical Letters
Andrei Stan

Abstract
Applying techniques originally developed for systems lacking a variational structure, we establish conditions for the existence of solutions in systems that possess this property but their energy functional is unbounded both lower and below. We show that, in general, our conditions differ from those in the classical mountain pass approach by Ambroseti-Rabinovitz when dealing with systems of this type. Our theory is put into practice in the context of a coupled system of Stokes equations with reaction terms, where we establish sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution. The systems under study are intermediary between gradient-type systems and Hamiltonian systems.



1
Introduction and Preliminaries

Many real-world processes can be represented by equations or systems of equations. However, solving these problems can be quite challenging. Over time, various techniques have been developed, with the critical point technique being one of the most significant. This technique is important because it simplifies the task of solving an equation to demonstrating that a specific function has a critical point.

In the recent papers
Precup and Stan 2023
;
Precup 2014
;
Stan 2021
;
Stan 2023
, systems of the form

\begin{cases}E_{11}(u,v)=0\\
E_{22}(u,v)=0,\end{cases}

were considered, where
E_{1},E_{2}
are certain
C^{1}
functionals. Such systems have the property that they lack a variational structure as a whole but possess it individually on each component.

In this paper we consider systems of the form

\begin{cases}E_{u}(u,v)=0\\
E_{v}(u,v)=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
E
is a
C^{1}
functional. In the literature there are many tools to establish the existence of critical points for
E
. However, if
E
has no upper and lower bounds, or is not well behaved, such methods may fail. Our aim is to use the techniques developed in
Precup and Stan 2023
;
Precup 2014
to prove the existence of critical points for
E
, using some partial functionals
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, which may not necessarily be related to
E
.

The novelty of this paper consist in obtaining different conditions then the ones typically used in the classical mountain pass approach by Ambroseti-Rabinovitz for the existence of a solution for the system (
1.1
).

Our theorey is applied to an abstract system from
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
as well as a system of Stokes equations. The latter system comes in the study of fluid dynamics and it is obtained neglecting the nonlinear term from the Navier-Stokes equations, which is an Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg elliptic and linear system. We send to
Galdi 2011
;
Teman 2001
;
Sohr 2001
for further details.

In the following section, we will review some important results from functional analysis, matrices converging to zero, and the Stokes system. These concepts will be used in the upcoming material.

1.1
Ekeland variational principle

The proof of our main result (
Theorem

2.3
) is essentially based on the weak form of
Ekeland’s variational principle (see, e.g.,
De Figueiredo 1989
).

Lemma 1.1

(Ekeland Principle - weak form)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and let
\Phi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a lower semicontinuous and
bounded below functional. Then, given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
u_{\varepsilon}\in X
such that

\Phi(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\inf_{X}\Phi+\varepsilon

and

\Phi(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\Phi(u)+\varepsilon d(u,u_{\varepsilon}),

for all
u\in X.

Abstract linear operator

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, be a bounded open set. Let
A\colon H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
be a continuous and strongly monotone operator, that is, there exists
\theta>0
such that

\langle Au,u\rangle\geq\theta|u|_{H_{0}^{1}}^{2},\text{ for all }u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(1.2)

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stand for the dual pairing between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
We observe that for every
h\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, Riesz representation theorem guarantees that there exists a unique element
u_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

Au_{h}=h,

i.e.,
A
is a bijective, where
A^{-1}h=u_{h}
. If
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that

\langle Au_{h},v\rangle=(h,v)_{L^{2}},\text{ for all }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

and thus
\left(u_{h},v\right)_{H_{0}^{1}}=(h,v)_{L^{2}}
.

If we identify
H^{-1}(\Omega)
with
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the operator
L

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{A}
and the norm
|\cdot|_{A}
, given by

\left(u,v\right)_{A}:=\langle Au,v\rangle

and

|u|_{A}:=\sqrt{\langle Au,u\rangle},

for all
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

From the strong monotony of
A
given in (
1.2
), we immediately deduce the following
Poincaré inequality

|u|_{L^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\theta}|u|_{A},\text{ for all }u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Matrices convergent to zero

A square matrix
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n\times n}\left(\mathbb{R}+\right)
is considered to be "convergent to zero" if its power
M^{k}
tends to the zero matrix as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Other equivalent characterizations include the requirement that the spectral radius of the matrix is less than one, or if the inverse of
I-A
(where
I
is the identity matrix) is both invertible and has nonnegative entries (see, e.g.,
Berman and Plemmons 1979
).

The following result, concerning matrices convergent to zero, holds true:

Lemma 1.2

(
(
Stan 2021
, Lemma 2.2)
)
.

Let
\left(x_{k,p}\right)_{k\geq 1},\ \left(y_{k,p}\right)_{k\geq 1}\ \
be two sequences of vectors in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
(column vectors) depending on a parameter
p,
such that

x_{k,p}\leq Ax_{k-1,p}+y_{k,p}

for all
k
and
p,
where
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a matrix
convergent to zero. If the sequence
\left(x_{k,p}\right)_{k\geq 1}
is bounded uniformly with respect to

and
y_{k,p}\rightarrow 0_{n}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
uniformly with respect to

then
x_{k,p}\rightarrow 0_{n}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
uniformly with respect
to
p.

1.4
Stationary Stokes-type equation

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\leq 3)
be an open and bounded domain and let

\textbf{f}\in H^{-1}(\Omega^{\prime})^{N}
. We recall some results related to the Stokes-type problem (see, e.g.,
Kohr and Precup 2023
;
Brumar 2023
),

\begin{cases}-\Delta\textbf{v}+\mu\textbf{v}+\nabla p=\textbf{f}\text{ in }\Omega^{\prime}\\
\operatorname{div}\textbf{v}=0\text{ in }\Omega^{\prime}\\
\textbf{v}=0\text{ on }\Omega^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

A solution is sought in the Sobolev space

V=\left\{\textbf{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})^{N}\,:\,\operatorname{div}\textbf{v}=0\right\}.

We endow
V
with the scalar product

(\textbf{v},\textbf{w})_{V}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\textbf{v}\cdot\nabla\textbf{w}+\int_{\Omega}\mu\textbf{v}\cdot\textbf{w}

and the corresponding norm
|\textbf{v}|_{V}=\sqrt{(\textbf{v},\textbf{v})_{V}}
.
One has the Poincare’s inequality (see, e.g.,
Kohr and Precup 2023
),

|\textbf{v}|_{(L^{2})^{N}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\mu}|\textbf{v}|_{V},\text{ for all }\textbf{v}\in V,

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem
-\Delta\textbf{v}=\lambda\textbf{v}
in
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\textbf{v}=0
on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
.

For
(\textbf{v},p)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})^{N}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, the variational formulation of the system (
1.4
) is:

(\textbf{v},\textbf{w})_{(H_{0}^{1})^{N}}+\mu(\textbf{v},\textbf{w})_{(L^{2})^{N}}-(p,\operatorname{div}\textbf{w})_{L^{2}}=\langle\textbf{f},\textbf{w}\rangle,\text{ for all }\textbf{w}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})^{N}

If
\textbf{v}\in V
, the above relation becomes,

(\textbf{v},\textbf{w})_{V}=\langle\textbf{f},\textbf{w}\rangle,\text{ for all }\textbf{w}\in V.

(1.5)

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the dual pairing between
V^{\prime}
and
V
.

Remark 1.3
.

If we find a solution
\textbf{v}\in V
to (
1.5
), the pressure
p\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
is guaranteed by De Rham’s Theorem (see, e.g.,
Teman 2001
;
Girault and Raviart 1986
).

From Riesz’s representation theorem, there exists a unique weak solution
\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}\in V
of the problem (
1.5
), that is, there is only one
\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}\in V
such that

\left(\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}},\textbf{w}\right)_{V}=\langle\textbf{f},\textbf{w}\rangle,\text{ for all }\textbf{w}\in V.

Moreover, one has the inequality,

|\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}|_{V}^{2}=(\textbf{f},\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}})\leq|\textbf{f}|_{V^{\prime}}\left|\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}\right|_{V},

i.e.,
|\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}|_{V}\leq|\textbf{f}|_{V^{\prime}}.

Thus, we may define the solution operator
S\colon V^{\prime}\to V
,
S(\textbf{f})=\textbf{v}_{\textbf{f}}
. Clearly, it is an isomorphism between
V^{\prime}
and
V
.

2
Main results

Let
H
be a Hilbert space together with the scalar product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{H}
and the induced norm
|\cdot|_{H}
.
We consider the system of the type

\begin{cases}u=N_{u}(u,v)\\
-v=N_{v}(u,v),\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
N\colon H\times H\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous operator.

Remark 2.1
.

The structure of the system (
2.1
) that we have considered situates it as an intermediary between gradient-type systems and Hamiltonian systems. Clearly, it admits a variational structure given by the functional

E(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{H}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v).

However, in general, this functional is unbounded from both above and below.

To the system (
2.1
), we associate the partial functionals
E_{1},E_{2}\colon H\times H\to\mathbb{R}
given by

E_{1}(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v),

and

E_{2}(u,v)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v).

One easily sees that both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are Fréchet differentiable and moreover,

\displaystyle E_{11}(u,v):=(E_{1})_{u}=u-N_{u}(u,v),

(2.2)

\displaystyle E_{22}(u,v):=(E_{2})_{v}=-v-N_{v}(u,v).

We say that an point
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})\in H\times H
is a
partial critical point
for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
if it satisfies

E_{11}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=0\text{ and }E_{22}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=0.

Obviously, any partial critical point for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
is a solution to the system (
2.1
).

The subsequent result establishes a relation between the critical points of the functional
E
and the partial critical points of the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

A pair
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})\in H\times H
is a critical point of
E
if and only if it is a partial critical point for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Proof.

The result is immediate if we observe that for any
u,v\in H
, the following relations hold:

E_{u}(u,v)=u-N_{u}(u,v)=E_{11}(u,v)

and

E_{v}(u,v)=-v-N_{v}(u,v)=E_{22}(u,v).

∎

2.1
Existence of a partial critical point

Now we are prepared to present our main result, which essentially involves establishing sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of at least one partial critical point for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the previous established setting, we additionally assume:

(h1)

One has the growth conditions

-\underline{\alpha}|v|_{H}^{2}-C\leq N(u,v)\leq\overline{\alpha}|u|_{H}^{2}+C,\text{ for all }u,v\in H,

(2.3)

where
0\leq\overline{\alpha},\underline{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
such that
\overline{\alpha}+\underline{\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}
and
C>0
.

(h2)

There are nonegative real numbers
m_{ij}\,(i,j\in\{1,2\})
such that the following monotony conditions hold true:

\left(N_{u}(u,v)-N_{u}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)\leq m_{11}|u-\overline{u}|^{2}_{H}+m_{12}|u-\overline{u}|_{H}|v-\overline{v}|_{H},

\left(N_{v}(u,v)-N_{v}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),v-\overline{v}\right)\geq-m_{22}|v-\overline{v}|^{2}_{H}-m_{21}|u-\overline{u}|_{H}|v-\overline{v}|_{H},

for all
u,v,\overline{u},\overline{v}\in H
.

(h3)

The matrix
M=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
is convergent to zero.

Then, there exists a partial critical point
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})\in H\times H
for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Proof.

For better comprehension, we structure our proof into several steps.

Step 1:
Boundedness from below and upper of the functionals
E_{1},E_{2}
.

Let
u,v\in H
. The growth conditions (
2.3
) yields

\displaystyle E_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\overline{\alpha}\right)|u|^{2}_{H}-C\geq-C,

and

\displaystyle E_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\underline{\alpha}\right)|v|^{2}_{H}+C\leq C.

Step 2:
Construction of an approximation sequence
(u_{k},v_{k})
.

We employ a method similar to the one described in
Precup 2014
. For an
v_{0}
arbitrarily chosen, using Ekeland’s variational principle within a recursive procedure, we generate a sequence
(u_{k},v_{k})\in H\times H
such that

\displaystyle E_{1}(u_{k},v_{k-1})\leq\inf_{H}E_{1}(\cdot,v_{k-1})+\tfrac{1}{k}\text{ },\ \ \ \text{ }E_{2}(u_{k},v_{k})\geq\sup_{H}E_{2}(u_{k},\cdot)-\tfrac{1}{k},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left|E_{11}(u_{k},v_{k-1})\right|_{H}\leq\tfrac{1}{k},\text{ \ \ \ }\left|E_{22}(u_{k},v_{k})\right|_{H}\leq\tfrac{1}{k}.\text{ }

(2.5)

Step 3:
Boundedness of the sequence
u_{k}
.

From (
2.3
) and the second relation from (
2.4
), we infer

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}|u_{k}|^{2}_{H}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(u_{k},v_{k-1}\right)+\inf_{H}E_{1}(\cdot,v_{k-1})+\tfrac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\leq N\left(u_{k},v_{k-1}\right)+E_{1}(0,v_{k-1})+1

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\alpha}|u_{k}|^{2}_{H}+\underline{\alpha}|v_{k-1}|^{2}_{H}+2C+1.

Hence,

|u_{k}|_{H}^{2}\leq\frac{\underline{\alpha}}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\overline{\alpha}}|v_{k-1}|_{H}^{2}+C_{1},

(2.6)

for some constant
C_{1}
.
Under similar computations, from the second relation of (
2.4
) we obtain

\tfrac{1}{2}|v_{k}|^{2}_{H}\leq\overline{\alpha}|u_{k}|^{2}_{H}+\underline{\alpha}|v_{k}|^{2}_{H}+2C+1,

which yields

|v_{k}|_{H}^{2}\leq\frac{\overline{\alpha}}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\underline{\alpha}}|u_{k}|_{H}^{2}+C_{2},

(2.7)

for some constant
C_{2}
.
Now, we combine inequalities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to deduce

|u_{k}|_{H}^{2}\leq\mu|u_{k-1}|^{2}_{H}+C_{3},

where

\mu=\frac{\overline{\alpha}\,\underline{\alpha}}{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\overline{\alpha}\right)\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\underline{\alpha}\right)}.

From (h1), we easily see that
\mu<1
, which guarantees that
u_{k}
is bounded.

Step 4:
Convergence of the sequences
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
.

Let
p>0
. From the monotony conditions (h2), we have

\displaystyle|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(u_{k+p}-N_{u}(u_{k+p},v_{k+p-1})-u_{k}+N_{u}(u_{k},v_{k-1}),u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(N_{u}(u_{k+p},v_{k+p-1})-N_{u}(u_{k},v_{k-1}),u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{k+p}+\frac{1}{k}\right)|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}+m_{11}\left|u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+m_{11}\left|u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right|_{H}\left|v_{k+p-1}-v_{k-1}\right|_{H}.

Thus,

|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}\leq\tfrac{2}{k}+m_{11}\left|u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right|_{H}+m_{11}\left|v_{k+p-1}-v_{k-1}\right|_{H}.

(2.8)

For the sequence
(v_{k})
, we similarly obtain

\displaystyle|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(v_{k+p}-v_{k},-v_{k}-N_{v}(u_{k},v_{k})+v_{k+p}+N_{v}(u_{k+p},v_{k+p})\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(v_{k+p}-v_{k},N_{v}(u_{k+p},v_{k+p})-N_{v}(u_{k},v_{k})\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\leq|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}\left(\frac{1}{k+p}+\frac{1}{k}\right)+m_{11}|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+m_{11}|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}^{2}.

Hence,

|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}\leq\frac{2}{k}+m_{11}\left|v_{k+p}-v_{k}\right|_{H}+m_{11}\left|u_{k+p}-u_{k}\right|_{H}.

(2.9)

If we write the relations (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) in matrix form, we infer

\begin{bmatrix}|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}\\
|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}\end{bmatrix}\leq\begin{bmatrix}m_{11}&0\\
m_{11}&m_{11}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}|u_{k+p}-u_{k}|_{H}\\
|v_{k+p}-v_{k}|_{H}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&m_{11}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}|u_{k+p-1}-u_{k-1}|_{H}\\
|v_{k+p-1}-v_{k-1}|_{H}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2}{k}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Since
u_{k}
is bounded and the matrix
M
converges to zero, we can conclude from
Lemma

1.2
that both
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
converge. Let us denote their limits as
u^{\ast}
and
v^{\ast}
.

Step 5:
Passing to limit.

Since
u_{k}\to u^{\ast}
and
v_{k}\to v^{\ast}
, the conclusion follows immediately if we pass to limit in (
2.5
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The partial critical point obtained in
Theorem

2.3
has the additional property of being a Nash equilibrium for the functionals
E_{1}
and
-E_{2}
(see, e.g.,
Precup 2014
;
Stan 2023
for further details on Nash equilibrium). This relationship is a result of taking the limit in (
2.4
), which gives

\displaystyle E_{1}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\inf_{H}E_{1}(\cdot,v^{\ast}),

\displaystyle E_{2}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\sup_{H}E_{2}(u^{\ast},\cdot).

2.2
Relation with the classical mountain pass approach

The well-known approach to obtain critical points for functionals that lack upper or lower bounds is to employ the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz results, which guarantee the existence of mountain pass points (as seen in
(
Ambrosetti and Rabinowitz 1973
, Theorem 2.1)
). The typical conditions imposed on the functional
E
are:

(I1)

There exists
\tau>0
such that

E(u,v)\geq\alpha>E(0,0),\text{ for all }|(u,v)|_{H\times H}=\tau.

(I2)

There exists
e\in H\times H
with
|e|>\tau
such that

E(e)<\inf_{|(u,v)|=\tau}E(u,v).

(I3)

The functional
E
has the Palais-Smale property, i.e., if
e_{k}
is a sequence such that

E(e_{k})\text{ is bounded}

and

\nabla E(e_{k})\to 0,

then
e_{k}
admits a convergent subsequence.

In the following, we will explore how these conditions align with our hypotheses (h1)-(h3).

Condition (I1):

Let
(u,v)\in H\times H
such that
|(u,v)|_{H\times H}=\tau
, i.e.,
|u|_{H}+|v|_{H}=\tau
. We compute,

E(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{H}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{H}^{2}-N(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(|u|_{H}+|v|_{H}\right)\left(|u|_{H}-|v|_{H}\right)-N(u,v).

Thus, for the relation
E(u,v)\geq\alpha
to hold, we need

N(u,v)<\tfrac{\tau}{2}\left(|u|_{H}-|v|_{H}\right)-\alpha,\text{ for all }|(u,v)|_{H\times H}=\tau.

(2.10)

On the other hand,
E(0,0)<\alpha
implies that

-N(0,0)<\alpha,\text{ i.e., }-\alpha<N(0,0).

Hence, relation (
2.10
) becomes

N(u,v)<-\tfrac{\tau}{2}\left(|u|_{H}-|v|_{H}\right)+N(0,0),\text{ for all }|(u,v)|_{H\times H}=\tau,

that is

N(u,v)-N(0,0)<\tfrac{\tau}{2}\left(|u|_{H}-|v|_{H}\right),\text{ for all }|(u,v)|_{H\times H}=\tau.

(2.11)

In our main result such a condition is not required, which enables us to encompass a broader range of situations in which the system (
2.1
) is solvable. It is clear that there might be cases where our result is not applicable, but the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz theorem is, and vice versa.

Condition (I2)
.

This condition is satisfied; for instance, one can take
(0,\gamma e)
, where
\gamma
is a sufficiently large real number, and
e
is a fixed element from
H
distinct from the origin of the space. Indeed,

\displaystyle E(0,\gamma e)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|e|^{2}-N(0,\gamma e)

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\underline{\alpha}\right)\gamma^{2}|e|^{2}+C\to-\infty,\text{ as }\gamma\to\infty.

Condition (I3)

Let
e_{k}=((e_{1})_{k},(e_{2})_{k})
be a sequence such that

E(e_{k})\text{ is uniformly bounded},

and
\nabla E(e_{k})\to 0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(e_{1})_{k}-N_{u}(e_{k})\to 0,

(2.12)

\displaystyle-(e_{2})_{k}-N_{v}(e_{k})\to 0.

(2.13)

Let
k_{0}
large enough such that
|(e_{1})_{k}-N_{u}(e_{k})|\leq 1
, for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Consequently, when taking a scalar product in (
2.12
) with
(e_{1})_{k}
for
k\geq k_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left((e_{1})_{k}-N_{u}\left(e_{k}\right),(e_{1})_{k}\right)_{H}\leq

\displaystyle\left|(e_{1})_{k}\right|_{H}.

From the monotony conditions (h2) we deduce

\displaystyle\left((e_{1})_{k}-N_{u}\left(e_{k}\right),(e_{1})_{k}\right)_{H}

\displaystyle=\left((e_{1})_{k},(e_{2})_{k}\right)_{H}-\left(N_{u}(e_{k}),(e_{1})_{k}\right)_{H}

\displaystyle=\left|(e_{1})_{k}\right|_{H}^{2}-\left(N_{u}(e_{k})-N_{u}(0),(e_{1})_{k}\right)_{H}-\left(N_{u}(0),(e_{1})_{k}\right)_{H}

\displaystyle\geq(1-m_{11})|(e_{1})_{k}|^{2}_{H}-m_{11}|(e_{1})_{k}|_{H}|(e_{2})_{k}|_{H}-|N_{u}(0,0)|_{H}|(e_{1})_{k}|_{H}.

Hence,

(1-m_{11})|(e_{1})_{k}|^{2}_{H}-m_{11}|(e_{1})_{k}|_{H}|(e_{2})_{k}|_{H}\leq\left(|N_{u}(0,0)|_{H}+1\right)|(e_{1})_{k}|_{H}.

(2.14)

Following a similar reasoning, from (
2.13
) we have

(1-m_{11})|(e_{2})_{k}|^{2}_{H}-m_{11}|(e_{1})_{k}|_{H}|(e_{2})_{k}|_{H}\leq\left(|N_{v}(0,0)|_{H}+1\right)|(e_{2})_{k}|_{H}.

(2.15)

Therefore, the above two relations (
2.14
), (
2.15
) yields

\beta|(e_{2})_{k}|_{H}\leq D,

where
D
is some constant and

\beta=1-m_{11}-\frac{m_{11}m_{11}}{1-m_{11}}.

Given that the matrix
M
is convergent to zero, we immediately deduce that

\beta=1-m_{11}-\frac{m_{11}m_{11}}{1-m_{11}}>0,

which guarantees the boundedness of
(e_{2})_{k}
. From this, is clear that
(e_{1})_{k}
is also bounded.

The boundedness of the sequence
e_{k}
guarantees the existence of a weakly convergent subsequence. However, establishing the strong convergence of this subsequence solely under hypotheses (h1)-(h3), remains an open question. Thus, we can formulate the following problem:

Open Question
.

Given only the assumptions (h1)-(h3), does the functional
E
satisfy the Palais-Smale condition?

Nonetheless, under certain additional assumptions, this result is valid.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that the operator
K:=\nabla N=(N_{u},N_{v})
is compact. Then the functional
E
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Proof.

Note that
\nabla E=I-K
. Given the compactness of
K
and the boundedness of
e_{k}
, it follows that there exists a subsequence, also denoted as
e_{k}
, such that
K(e_{k})
converges to a point
\tilde{e}
in
H\times H
. Thus,

|e_{k}-\tilde{e}|-|\tilde{e}-K(e_{k})|\leq|e_{k}-K(e_{k})|=|\nabla E(e_{k})|.

Now, the conclusion is immediate since
|\nabla E(e_{k})|\to 0
and
|K(e_{k})-\tilde{e}|\to 0.

∎

3
Applications

In this section, we present two application for the results obtained in
Theorem

2.3
.

3.1
Abstract system on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

Let us consider the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}Au=F_{u}(u,v)\\
-Av=F_{y}(u,v)\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\,(n\geq 3)
is a bounded open set,
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
functional and the operator
A
is defined in
Section

1.2
. Here,
F_{u}
and
F_{v}
stand for the partial derivatives of
F
with respect to the first and second component, respectively.
We use
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
|\cdot|
to denote the scalar product and the corresponding norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The Hilbert space
H
is considered to be the Sobolev space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
equipped with the scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{A}
and the corresponding norm
|\cdot|_{A}
.
Clearly, the system (
3.1
) admits a variational given by the energy functional
E\colon H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
,

E(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{A}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}_{A}-\int_{\Omega}F(u,v).

The partial functionals
E_{1},E_{2}\colon H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated to the system (
3.1
) are given by

\displaystyle E_{1}(u,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|u|_{A}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u,v),

\displaystyle E_{2}(u,v)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|v|_{A}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}F(u,v).

If we denote

\begin{cases}f_{1}(u,v)=F_{u}(u,v)\\
f_{2}(u,v=F_{v}(u,v),\end{cases}

the identification of
H^{-1}(\Omega)
with
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
via
A^{-1}
, yields to the representation

\nabla E(u,v)=\left(u-A^{-1}f_{1}(u,v),v-A^{-1}f_{2}(u,v)\right)=\left(E_{11}(u,v),E_{22}(u,v)\right),

where
E_{11},E_{22}
stand for the partial Fréchet derivatives of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
with respect to the first and second component, respectively.
Consequently, the operator
N
is given by

N(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}F(u,v)

and its derivatives are the Nemytskii’s operators

N_{u}(u,v)=A^{-1}f_{1}(u,v)\text{ and }N_{v}(u,v)=A^{-1}f_{2}(u,v).

On the potential
F
, we assume the following conditions:

(H1)

There exist real numbers
0\leq\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leq\frac{1}{4\theta}
and
C>0
, such that the following conditions hold

-\tau_{1}|x|^{2}-C\leq F(x,y)\leq\tau_{2}|y|^{2}+C,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Related to the gradient of
F
, let us assume:

(H2)

There are nonegative real numbers
\overline{m}_{ij}
such that, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, one has the monotony conditions:

\displaystyle\left(f_{1}(x,y)-f_{1}(\overline{x},\overline{y}),x-\overline{x}\right)\leq\overline{m}_{11}|x-\overline{x}|^{2}+\overline{m}_{12}|x-\overline{x}||y-\overline{y}|

and

\left(f_{2}(x,y)-f_{2}(\overline{x},\overline{y}),x-\overline{x}\right)\geq-\overline{m}_{22}|y-\overline{y}|^{2}-\overline{m}_{21}|x-\overline{x}||y-\overline{y}|.

Finally, the constants specified in (H2) are such that:

(H3)

The matrix

M:=\theta\begin{bmatrix}\overline{m}_{11}&\overline{m}_{12}\\
\overline{m}_{21}&\overline{m}_{22}\end{bmatrix}

is convergent to zero.

In the subsequent, we prove that conditions (H1)-(H3) are sufficient to ensure the existence of a partial critical point for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (H1)-(H3) hold true. Then, there exists a pair of points
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that it is a critical point for the functional
E
.

Furthermore, it has the additional property that

\displaystyle E_{1}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\inf_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}E_{1}(\cdot,v^{\ast}),

\displaystyle E_{2}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\sup_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}E_{2}(u^{\ast},\cdot).

Proof.

We verify that all conditions from
Theorem

2.3
are satisfied.

Check of the condition (h1)
.
Let
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, for some constant
C_{1}>0
, using the Poincaré inequality (
1.3
), we deduce

\displaystyle N(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}F(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{2}|u|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{2}\theta|u|_{A}^{2}+C_{1},

and

\displaystyle N(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}F(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq-\tau_{1}|v|_{L^{2}}^{2}-C_{1}

\displaystyle\geq-\theta\tau_{1}|u|_{A}^{2}-C_{1}.

The conclusion is immediate since
\theta\,\tau_{1}<\frac{1}{4}
and
\theta\,\tau_{2}<\frac{1}{4}
.

Check of the condition (h2)
.
For any
u,v,\overline{u},\overline{v}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, one has

\displaystyle\left(N_{u}(u,v)-N_{u}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{A}

\displaystyle=\left(A^{-1}f_{1}(u,v)-f_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{A}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{1}(u,v)-f_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{m}_{11}|u-\overline{u}|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\overline{m}_{12}|u-\overline{u}|_{L^{2}}|v-\overline{v}|_{L^{2}}

From the Poincaré inequality (
1.3
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\left(N_{u}(u,v)-N_{u}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{A}\leq\theta\,\overline{m}_{11}|u-\overline{u}|_{A}^{2}+\theta\,\overline{m}_{12}|u-\overline{u}|_{A}|v-\overline{v}|_{A}.

Similar estimates are obtained for
N_{v}
,

\displaystyle\left(N_{v}(u,v)-N_{v}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{A}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{2}(u,v)-f_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{v}),u-\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\overline{m}_{22}|v-\overline{v}|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\overline{m}_{21}|u-\overline{u}|_{L^{2}}|v-\overline{v}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\theta\,\overline{m}_{22}|v-\overline{v}|_{A}^{2}-\theta\,\overline{m}_{21}|u-\overline{u}|_{A}|v-\overline{v}|_{A}.

Consequently, condition (h2) is satisfied with
m_{ij}=\theta\,\overline{m}_{ij}
,
(i,j=\{1,2\})
.

Check of the condition (h3)
. This condition is immediate from (H3).

Thus, all hypothesis of
Theorem

2.3
are satisfied and consequently, there exists a partial critical point
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})
for the pair of functionals
(E_{1},E_{2})
such that

\displaystyle E_{1}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\inf_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}E_{1}(\cdot,v^{\ast}),

\displaystyle E_{2}(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})=\sup_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}E_{2}(u^{\ast},\cdot).

Moreover, from
Lemma

2.2
, the pair
(u^{\ast},v^{\ast})
is a critical point for the functional
E
.
∎

3.2
Stokes-type coupled system

We consider the Stokes-type coupled system

\begin{cases}-\Delta\textbf{u}_{1}+\mu\textbf{u}_{1}+\nabla p_{1}=F_{\textbf{u}_{1}}(\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2})\text{ in }\Omega^{\prime}\\
-\Delta\textbf{u}_{2}+\mu\textbf{u}_{2}+\nabla p_{2}=-F_{\textbf{u}_{2}}(\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2})\text{ in }\Omega^{\prime}\\
\operatorname{div}\textbf{u}_{i}=0\text{ in }\Omega^{\prime}\\
\textbf{u}_{i}=0\quad(i=1,2)\text{ on }\partial\Omega^{\prime},\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
\mu>0
and
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{2N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
functional. Here,
F_{\textbf{u}_{1}},F_{\textbf{u}_{2}}
represent for the partial derivatives of
F
with respect to the first and second component, respectively.

Our problem (
3.2
) is equivalent with the fixed point equation

\begin{cases}\textbf{u}_{1}=S^{-1}F_{\textbf{u}_{1}}\\
\textbf{u}_{2}=S^{-1}F_{\textbf{u}_{2}},\end{cases}

(3.3)

where
\left(\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\right)\in V\times V
.

Now, we can apply
Theorem

2.3
, where
H=V
and

N\left(\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\right)=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}F\left(\textbf{u}_{1},\textbf{u}_{2}\right).

The verification of conditions (h1)-(h3) follows a similar process to the previous application. This is done under the assumption that
F
satisfies (H1)-(H3), where by
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
|\cdot|
we understand the usual scalar product and norm in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, while
\theta
is replaced by
\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\mu}
.

Therefore,
Theorem

2.3
ensures the existence of a pair
({\bf u}_{1}^{\ast},{\bf u}_{2}^{\ast})\in V\times V
, which, according to De Rham’s Lemma, further guarantees the pressures
(p_{1},p_{2})\in L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})\times L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
such that
(({\bf u}_{1}^{\ast},p_{2}),({\bf u}_{2}^{\ast},p_{2}))\in\left(V\times L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})\right)^{2}
solves the system (
3.2
).

Leptonic and semileptonic decays of mesons in the Domain model of QCD vacuum
Vladimir Voronin

Abstract
The leptonic and semileptonic decays of mesons are investigated within the Domain model of QCD vacuum and hadronization. The Domain Model is the mean-field approach based on the statistical ensemble of almost everywhere homogeneous Abelian (anti-)self-dual gluon fields which reproduces main features of low-energy QCD and allows to deduce a nonlocal effective meson action. Using this meson action, the leptonic decay constants, form factors and branching ratios of semileptonic decays are evaluated simultaneously with masses of mesons. The results are compared to experimental data or other approaches.



I
Introduction

The leptonic and semileptonic decays are most easily accessed processes, both theoretically and experimentally, which involve quark flavor transformation due to weak interaction. This makes them ideal for extracting the magnitudes of elements of Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix
V
from available experimental data.
While the CKM matrix
V
concerns mixing of quarks, the experimental data are given for hadrons, which poses difficulties in extracting CKM elements, from the theoretical point of view.
A multitude of methods can be employed in order to address this problem (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
and references therein). Among them are Lattice QCD, heavy quark effective theory, Dyson-Schwinger equations, sum rules and various quark models.

In this work, leptonic and semileptonic decays of mesons are investigated within the Domain Model of QCD vacuum and hadronization
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
which describes the composite nature of mesons with nonlocal meson-quark interaction.
The Domain Model of QCD vacuum and hadronization consistently describes main features of low-energy QCD.
The translation-invariant parts of gluon and quark propagators are entire analytical functions of complex momentum which can be interpreted as confinement of dynamical quarks
[
6
]
. It was shown in Ref.
[
13
]
that the vacuum ensemble also provides the area law for the Wilson loop, that is the confinement of static quarks. The vacuum also provides chiral symmetry breaking and resolution of
U_{A}(1)
problem
[
8
]
. The mean-field model of hadronization in the presence of Abelian (anti-)self-dual vacuum gluon fields developed in Refs.
[
6
,
7
,
9
]
allows to deduce an effective meson action via hadronization of one-gluon exchange of quark currents. The resulting collective colorless excitations describe extended (non-pointlike) mesons. It was shown that masses of mesons in the model exhibit Regge character at large orbital and radial quantum numbers
[
6
]
. The model describes masses of light, heavy-light mesons and heavy quarkonia
[
7
]
, leptonic decay constants of pseudoscalar mesons and electromagnetic transition constants of vector mesons
[
9
]
, decay constants of vector mesons into a couple of pseudoscalar ones
[
10
]
, electromagnetic transition form factors of pseudoscalar mesons
[
10
]
, dipole polarizabilities of pseudoscalar mesons
[
14
]
. The model was also applied to the anomalous magnetic moment of muon, in particular to dominating contributions due to strong interactions
[
15
]
. The present work adds leptonic decay constants of vector mesons and semileptonic form factors to the list of phenomena investigated with the Domain Model of QCD vacuum and hadronization. See also Refs.
[
16
,
17
]
summarizing results concerning weak interactions of mesons which were obtained within a related nonlocal model.

The leptonic decays of mesons are considered in Section
III
, and semileptonic decays in Section
IV
. The results are summarized in Section
V

Description of the Domain Model

The effective meson action of the Domain Model of QCD vacuum and hadronization
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
is given by the following formulas in Euclidean space-time:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle={\cal N}\int D\phi_{\cal Q}\exp\left\{-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{2}\frac{h^{2}_{\cal Q}}{g^{2}C^{2}_{\mathcal{Q}}}\int d^{4}x\phi^{2}_{\cal Q}(x)-\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}W_{k}[\phi]\right\},

(1)

\displaystyle W_{k}[\phi]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{k}}h_{{\cal Q}_{1}}\dots h_{{\cal Q}_{k}}\int d^{4}x_{1}\dots\int d^{4}x_{k}\Phi_{{\cal Q}_{1}}(x_{1})\dots\Phi_{{\cal Q}_{k}}(x_{k})\Gamma^{(k)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{k}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}),

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\cal Q}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ipx}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}(p)\tilde{\phi}_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}(p),\quad C_{\mathcal{Q}}=C_{J},\quad C^{2}_{S/P}=2C^{2}_{V/A}=\frac{1}{9}.

Here the index
\mathcal{Q}\equiv\{aJLn\}
stands for all quantum number of a meson.
\Lambda
is the strength of background gluon field related to the condensate
\langle g^{2}F^{2}\rangle
.
Auxiliary fields
\Phi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
introduced during hadronization are transformed into physical meson fields
\phi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
by an orthogonal matrix
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}
, so the quadratic term
W_{2}[\phi]
in Eq. (
2
) becomes diagonal.

Inverting the quadratic part of the effective action, one finds corresponding propagators of meson fields
\phi_{\mathcal{Q}}
:

D_{\mathcal{Q}}(p^{2})=h_{\mathcal{Q}}^{-2}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{g^{2}C^{2}_{\mathcal{Q}}}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{\mathcal{Q}}(p^{2})\right)^{-1}

(3)

where
\tilde{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{\cal Q}
is the two-point correlation function diagonalized with respect to all quantum numbers and
g
is strong coupling constant.
The constants
h_{\mathcal{Q}}
defined by formula

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=h^{2}_{\cal Q}\left.\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{\cal Q}(p^{2})\right|_{p^{2}=-M^{2}_{\cal Q}}

provide that the residue at the pole of meson propagator (
3
) is equal to unity.

The general function
\Gamma^{(k)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{k}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
includes “connected” and “disconnected” terms correlated by background gluon field. For example,
\tilde{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{\cal QQ^{\prime}}(p)
is given by

\Gamma^{(2)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}{\cal Q}_{2}}=\overline{G^{(2)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}{\cal Q}_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})}-\Xi_{2}(x_{1}-x_{2})\overline{G^{(1)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}}G^{(1)}_{{\cal Q}_{2}}},

(4)

Here
\Xi
is correlation function of the background field which characterizes the statistical ensemble of the almost everywhere homogeneous Abelian (anti-)self-dual fields.

G^{(k)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{k}}
are quark loops averaged over background field with measure
d\sigma_{B}
:

\displaystyle\overline{G^{(k)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{k}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}=\int d\sigma_{B}{\rm Tr}V_{{\cal Q}_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)S\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\dots V_{{\cal Q}_{k}}\left(x_{k}\right)S\left(x_{k},x_{1}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\overline{G^{(l)}_{{\cal Q}_{1}\dots{\cal Q}_{l}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{l})G^{(k)}_{{\cal Q}_{l+1}\dots{\cal Q}_{l+k}}(x_{l+1},\dots,x_{l+k})}=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma_{B}{\rm Tr}\left\{V_{{\cal Q}_{1}}\left(x_{1}\right)S\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\dots V_{{\cal Q}_{k}}\left(x_{l}\right)S\left(x_{l},x_{1}\right)\right\}\times

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left\{V_{{\cal Q}_{l+1}}\left(x_{l+1}\right)S\left(x_{l+1},x_{l+2}\right)\dots V_{{\cal Q}_{l+k}}\left(x_{l+k}\right)S\left(x_{l+k},x_{l+1}\right)\right\}.

Here
S(x,y)
is a quark propagator in background gluon field, and
V_{{\cal Q}}
is a nonlocal meson-quark vertex.

It is possible to find analytical expressions for propagators and vertices if one approximates the ensemble of of almost everywhere homogeneous (anti-)self-dual Abelian background gluon field with just homogeneous field. The averaging over the ensemble is then implemented by averaging the quark loops (
5
) over configurations of homogeneous gluon field. These include self-dual and anti-self-dual fields with different directions in Euclidean and color spaces. The averaging over spatial directions can be found with the help of generating formula

\int d\sigma_{B}\exp(if_{\mu\nu}J_{\mu\nu})=\frac{\sin\sqrt{2\left(J_{\mu\nu}J_{\mu\nu}\pm J_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{J}_{\mu\nu}\right)}}{\sqrt{2\left(J_{\mu\nu}J_{\mu\nu}\pm J_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{J}_{\mu\nu}\right)}},

(6)

where
J_{\mu\nu}
is an arbitrary antisymmetric tensor.
Tensor
f_{\mu\nu}
stands for an Abelian (anti-)self-dual background field with strength
\Lambda
:

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{\mu}=-\frac{1}{2}\hat{n}B_{\mu\nu}x_{\nu},\ \hat{n}=t^{3}\cos\xi+t^{8}\sin\xi,

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}B_{\alpha\beta}=\pm B_{\mu\nu},\ \hat{B}_{\rho\mu}\hat{B}_{\rho\nu}=4\upsilon^{2}\Lambda^{4}\delta_{\mu\nu},

(7)

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\hat{n}}{2\upsilon\Lambda^{2}}B_{\alpha\beta},\ \upsilon=\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3}\right),\ f_{\mu\alpha}f_{\nu\alpha}=\delta_{\mu\nu}.

The upper sign in “
\pm
” should be taken for self-dual field, and the lower for anti-self-dual field.

Nonlocal vertices
V^{aJln}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{l}}
are given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle V^{aJln}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{l}}={\cal C}_{ln}\mathcal{M}^{a}\Gamma^{J}F_{nl}\left(\frac{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\cal D}}^{2}\!\!\!(x)}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)T^{(l)}_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{l}}\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\cal D}}\!(x)}{\Lambda}\right),

(8)

\displaystyle{\cal C}^{2}_{ln}=\frac{l+1}{2^{l}n!(n+l)!},\quad F_{nl}(s)=s^{n}\int_{0}^{1}dtt^{n+l}\exp(st),

\displaystyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{\mathcal{D}}}}\vphantom{D}^{ff^{\prime}}_{\mu}=\xi_{f}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\mu}-\ \xi_{f^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\mu},\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\mu}\hskip-3.00003pt(x)=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}+\ i\hat{B}_{\mu}(x),\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\mu}\hskip-3.00003pt(x)=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{\partial}}_{\mu}-\ i\hat{B}_{\mu}(x),

\displaystyle\xi_{f}=\frac{m_{f^{\prime}}}{m_{f}+m_{f^{\prime}}},\ \xi_{f^{\prime}}=\frac{m_{f}}{m_{f}+m_{f^{\prime}}}.

Here
\mathcal{M}^{a}
is a flavor matrix for a given meson,
\Gamma^{J}
is corresponding Dirac matrix

\Gamma^{S}=1,\ \Gamma^{P}=i\gamma_{5},\ \Gamma^{V}_{\mu}=\gamma_{\mu},\ \Gamma^{A}_{\mu}=\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu},

\xi_{f},\xi_{f^{\prime}}
provide that
x
is a center of mass for quarks with flavors
f
and
f^{\prime}
, and
n,l
are radial and orbital quantum numbers, correspondingly. Function
F_{nl}
is defined by the propagator of gluons charged with respect to the background field,
T^{(l)}
are irreducible tensors of four-dimensional rotation group.
Propagator of the quark with mass
m_{f}
in the presence of the homogeneous Abelian (anti-)self-dual gluon field has the form

\displaystyle S_{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2}\hat{n}x_{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}y_{\nu}\right)H_{f}(x-y),

(9)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{f}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\upsilon\Lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dse^{(-p^{2}/2\upsilon\Lambda^{2})s}\left(\frac{1-s}{1+s}\right)^{m_{f}^{2}/4\upsilon\Lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\vphantom{\frac{s}{1-s^{2}}}p_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}\pm is\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\alpha}f_{\alpha\beta}p_{\beta}+m_{f}\left(P_{\pm}+P_{\mp}\frac{1+s^{2}}{1-s^{2}}-\frac{i}{2}\gamma_{\alpha}f_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\beta}\frac{s}{1-s^{2}}\right)\right],

where anti-Hermitean representation of Dirac matrices is used, and “
\pm
” is the same as in formula (
7
),
P_{\pm}=(1\pm\gamma_{5})/2
is the chirality projector. The translation-invariant part
H_{f}
of the propagator is an analytical function in finite momentum plane which is interpreted as confinement.

A common way of extracting matrix
V
from experimental data is via matrix elements of quark currents. For example, leptonic decay constants of mesons are found from matrix element

\langle 0|\bar{q}^{\prime}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}q|H\rangle.

(10)

III
Leptonic decays of mesons

The amplitude of leptonic decays of pseudoscalar mesons can be parametrized as

A(H(p)\to\ell(k)\bar{\nu}_{\ell}(k^{\prime}))=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}M_{H}^{\mu}=i\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}f_{H}p^{\mu}

and for vector mesons as

A(H(p)\to\ell(k)\bar{\nu}_{\ell}(k^{\prime}))=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}M_{H}^{\mu\nu}\vec{e}_{\nu}(p)=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}Mf_{H}\vec{e}^{\mu}(p),

where
p^{2}=M^{2},\quad k^{2}=m^{2},\quad k^{\prime 2}=0
.
Here
V_{qq^{\prime}}
is the element of CKM matrix corresponding to a given decay,
M
is the mass of decaying meson,
m
is the mass of final lepton, and
f_{H}
is the constant which parametrizes hadronic part
M_{H}
of the corresponding amplitude.

IV
Semileptonic decays of mesons

The amplitude of semileptonic decay
P\to P^{\prime}\ell\bar{\nu}_{\ell}
with a pseudoscalar meson in the final state

A(H(p)\to H^{\prime}(p^{\prime})\ell\bar{\nu}_{\ell})=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}M_{HH^{\prime}}^{\mu},

can be parametrized as

M_{HH^{\prime}}^{\mu}=F_{+}(q^{2})P^{\mu}+F_{-}(q^{2})q^{\mu},

(15)

where
P=p+p^{\prime},q=p-p^{\prime}
.
The hadronic part of the amplitude
P\to V\ell\bar{\nu}_{\ell}
with a vector meson in the final state

A(H(p)\to H^{\prime}(p^{\prime})\ell\bar{\nu}_{\ell})=\vec{e}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{qq^{\prime}}\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{\ell}M_{HH^{\prime}}^{\mu\alpha},

where
\vec{e}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
is polarization of vector meson, can be represented in the form suggested in Ref.
[
31
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vec{e}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}M_{HH^{\prime}}^{\mu\alpha}=&-(M+M^{\prime})\vec{e}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}g^{\mu\alpha}A_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{\vec{e}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}q^{\alpha}}{M+M^{\prime}}P^{\mu}A_{2}(q^{2})\\

\displaystyle F_{\pm}^{D^{0}\to\pi^{-}}

V
Summary and outlook

The Domain model of QCD vacuum and hadronization is a mean-field approach that allows unified description of basic low-energy properties of QCD and meson physics. In the present work, the model was applied to the leptonic and semileptonic decays of mesons. The numerical results for leptonic decay constants, semileptonic form factors and branching ratios are presented. The phenomenological description is reasonable with the exception of branching ratios
\mathcal{B}\left(P\to V\ell\bar{\nu}_{\ell}\right)
for large-width mesons
V
.

When one consistently introduces the electroweak interactions into the model, the corresponding amplitudes of these decays get contributions due to vertices in Fig.
1
in addition to the impulse approximation. The impulse approximation for leptonic decays of vector mesons in not even meaningful on its own. The Domain Model is obviously an effective model with limited precision, but the findings of the present work indicate that vertices in Fig.
1
should be taken into account when extracting CKM matrix from the experimental data. It is plausible that the tension between determination of CKM matrix elements from inclusive and exclusive semileptonic decays, leptonic decays and semileptonic decays can be related to contributions in Fig.
1
, as well as deviation from unitarity of CKM matrix (see e.g. review
[
4
]
).

The decays involving
\eta

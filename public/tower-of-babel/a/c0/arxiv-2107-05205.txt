Connectedness of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties for unramified groups
Sian Nie

Abstract
. For unramified reductive groups, we determine the connected components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties in the partial affine flag varieties. Based on the work of Hamacher-Kim and Zhou, this result allows us to verify, in the unramified group case, the He-Rapoport axioms, the “almost product structure” of Newton strata, and the precise description of mod p p isogeny classes predicted by the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture, for the Kisin-Pappas integral models of Shimura varieties of Hodge type with parahoric level structure.



Introduction

0.1.

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean local field with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is a power of some prime
p
. Denote by
{\breve{F}}
the completion of a maximal unramified extension of
F
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group defined over
F
, and let
\sigma
be the Frobenius automorphism of
G({\breve{F}})
. Fix an element
b\in G({\breve{F}})
, a geometric cocharacter
\lambda
of
G
, and a
\sigma
-stable parahoric subgroup
K\subseteq G({\breve{F}})
. The attached affine Deligne-Lusztig variety is defined by

X(\lambda,b)_{K}=X^{G}(\lambda,b)_{K}=\{g\in G({\breve{F}})/K;g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in K{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)K\},

where
{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
is the admissible set associated to the geometric conjugacy class of
\lambda
. If
F
is of equal characteristic,
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
is a locally closed and locally finite-type subvariety of the partial affine flag variety
G({\breve{F}})/K
. If
F
is of mixed characteristic,
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
is a perfect subscheme of the Witt vector partial affine flag variety, in the sense of Bhatt-Scholze
[
1
]
and Zhu
[
37
]
.

The variety
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
, first introduced by Rapoport
[
32
]
, encodes important arithmetic information of Shimura varieties. Let
(\mathbf{G},X)
be a Shimura datum with
G=\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and
\lambda
the inverse of the Hodge cocharacter. Suppose there is a good integral model for the corresponding Shimura variety with parahoric level structure. Langlands
[
26
]
, and latter refined by Langlands-Rapoport
[
27
]
and Rapoport
[
32
]
, conjectured a precise description of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points of the integral model in terms of the varieties
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
. In the case of PEL Shimura varieties,
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
is also the set of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points of a moduli space of
p
-divisible groups define by Rapoport-Zink
[
33
]
.

0.2.

The main purpose of this paper is to study the set
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})
of connected components of
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
. Notice that
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
only depends on
\lambda
and the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
of
b
. Thanks to He
[
11
]
,
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
is non-empty if and only if
[b]
belongs to the set of “neutral acceptable”
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of
G({\breve{F}})
with respect to
\lambda
.

Let
\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}
be the set of coinvariants of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G)
under the Galois group
\Gamma_{0}={\rm Gal}(\overline{{\breve{F}}}/{\breve{F}})
. There is a natural map
\eta_{G}:G({\breve{F}})/K\to\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}
. To compute
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})
we can assume that
G
is adjoint and hence simple by the following Cartesian diagram (see
[
18
, Corollary 4.4]
)

\textstyle{\pi_{0}(X^{G}(\lambda,b)_{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{G}}
\textstyle{\pi_{0}(X^{G_{{\rm ad}}}(\lambda_{{\rm ad}},b_{{\rm ad}})_{K_{{\rm ad}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{G_{{\rm ad}}}}
\textstyle{\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi_{1}(G_{{\rm ad}})_{\Gamma_{0}}.}

The map
\eta_{G}
gives a natural obstruction to the connectedness of
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
. Another more technical obstruction is given by the following Hodge-Newton decomposition theorem.

Theorem 0.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 4.17]
)
.

Suppose
G
is adjoint and simple. If the pair
(\lambda,b)
is Hodge-Newton decomposable (with respect to some proper Levi subgroup
M
) in the sense of
[
6
, §2.5.5]
, then
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
is a disjoint union of open and closed subsets, which are isomorphic to affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties attached to
M
.

By Theorem
0.1
and induction on the dimension of
G
, it suffices to consider the Hodge-Newton indecomposable case. This means that either
\lambda
is a central cocharacter or the pair
(\lambda,b)
Hodge-Newton irreducible, see
[
36
, Lemma 5.3]
. In the former case,

X(\lambda,b)_{K}\cong\mathbb{J}_{b}/(K\cap\mathbb{J}_{b})

is a discrete subset with
\mathbb{J}_{b}
the
\sigma
-centralizer of
b
. In the latter case, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 0.1

(see
[
36
, Conjecture 5.4]
)
.

Assume
G
is adjoint and simple. If
(\lambda,b)
is Hodge-Newton irreducible, then the map
\eta_{G}
induces a bijection

\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})\cong\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}^{\sigma},

where
\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}^{\sigma}
is the set of
\sigma
-fixed point of
\pi_{1}(G)_{\Gamma_{0}}
.

If
G
is unramified (that is,
G
extends to a reductive group over
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}
) and
K
is hyperspecial, Conjecture
0.1
is established by Viehmann
[
35
]
, Chen-Kisin-Viehmann
[
2
]
, and the author
[
29
]
. If
b
is basic, it is proved by He-Zhou
[
18
]
. If
G
is split or
G={\rm Res}_{E/F}{\rm GL}_{n}
with
E/F
a finite unramified field extension, it is proved by L. Chen and the author in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 0.2
.

Conjecture
0.1
is true if
G
is unramified.

In particular, Theorem
0.2
completes the computation of connected components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties for unramified groups.

0.3.

We discuss some applications. Assume
p\neq 2
. Let
(\mathbf{G},X)
be a Shimura datum of Hodge type with parahoric level structure such that
p\nmid|\pi_{1}(\mathbf{G}_{\rm der})|
,
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is tamely ramified, and the parahoric subgroup
K
at
p
is a connected parahoric. Let
\mathscr{S}_{K}=\mathscr{S}_{K}(G,X)
be the Kisin-Pappas integral model of the corresponding Shimura variety constructed in
[
23
]
. Let
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
G=\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, and
\lambda
be the inverse of the Hodge cocharacter.

Remark 0.3
.

In
[
31
]
, Pappas and Rapoport obtained a new construction of integral models for Hodge type Shimura varieties with parahoric level structure, without the the tameness assumption on
G
. It would be desirable to extend the applications discussed below to their integral models in the unramified group case.

0.3.1.

A major motivation to study
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})
comes from the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture mentioned in §
0.1
. In the hyperspecial level structure case, the conjecture is proved by Kottwitz
[
25
]
for PEL Shimura varieties of types
A
and
C
, and by Kisin
[
21
]
for his integral models
[
20
]
of Shimura varieties of abelian type. Using the Kisin-Pappas integral models
[
23
]
for Hodge type Shimura varieties with parahoric level structure
K
, Zhou
[
36
]
proved that each mod
p
isogeny class has the predicted form when
G
is residually split.

One of the key ingredients in the proofs of Kisin and Zhou is to construct certain lifting map from
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
to an isogeny class of
\mathscr{S}_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
(see also
[
14
, Axiom A]
), which uses in a crucial way descriptions of
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})
in
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
respectively. Combining
[
36
, Proposition 6.5]
with Theorem
0.2
, we deduce that such a lifting map always exists if
G
is unramified.

Proposition 0.4
.

If
G
is unramified, then the Rapoport-Zink uniformisation map admits a unique lift on
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points

X(\lambda,b)_{K}\to\mathscr{S}_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}),

which respects canonical crystalline Tate tensors on both sides.

If
G
is unramified and
K
is hyperspecial, Proposition
0.4
is proved by Kisin
[
21
]
. If
b
is basic or
G
is residually split, it is proved by Zhou
[
36
]
. If
G
is quasi-split and
K
is absolutely special, it is proved by Zhou in
[
12
, Theorem A.4.3]
.

As an application, one can extend
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
to the unramified group case, by combining the methods in
[
36
]
and Proposition
0.4
. This is pointed out to us by Zhou.

Corollary 0.5
.

If
G
is unramified, then the isogeny classes in
\mathscr{S}_{K}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
has the form predicted by the Langlands-Rapoport conjecture. Moreover, each isogeny class contains a point which lifts to a special point in the corresponding Shimura variety.

Corollary
0.5
was first proved by Pol van Hoften
[
12
]
using a different approach.

0.3.2.

In
[
17
]
, He and Rapoport formulated five axioms on Shimura varieties with parahoric level structure, which provide a group-theorectic way to study certain characteristic subsets (such as Newton strata, Ekedahl-Oort strata, Kottwitz-Rapoport strata, and so on) in the mod
p
reductions of Shimura varieties. Based on this axiomatic approach, Zhou
[
36
]
proved that all the expected Newton strata are non-empty (see
[
22
]
using a different approach). For more applications of these axioms, we refer to
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
36
]
and
[
34
]
. Combining
[
36
, Theorem 8.1]
with Proposition
0.4
we have

Corollary 0.6
.

All the He-Rapoport axioms hold if
G
is unramified.

These axioms are verified by He-Rapoport
[
17
]
in the Siegel case, and by He-Zhou
[
18
]
for certain PEL Shimura varieties (unramified of types
A
and
C
and odd ramified unitary groups). In
[
36
]
, Zhou verified all the axioms except the surjectivity of
[
17
, Axiom 4 (c)]
in the general case, and all of them in the case that
G
is residually split.

0.3.3.

In
[
28
]
, Mantovan established a formula expressing the
l
-adic cohomology of proper PEL Shimura varieties in terms of the
l
-adic cohomology with compact supports of the Igusa varieties and of the Rapoport-Zink spaces for any prime
l\neq p
. This formula encodes nicely the local-global compatibility of the Langlands correspondence. A key part of its proof is to show that the products of reduced fibers of Igusa varieties and Rapoport-Zink spaces form nice “pro-étale covers up to perfection” for the Newton strata, of PEL Shimura varieties with hyperspecial level structure. This is referred as the “almost product structure” of Newton strata. In
[
14
]
, Hamacher-Kim extended Mantovan’s results to the Kisin-Pappas integral models under some mild assumptions. Combining
[
14
, Theorem 2]
with Proposition
0.4
we have

Corollary 0.7
.

The “almost product structure” of Newton strata holds if
G
is unramified.

When
G
is unramified and
K
is hyperspecial, the “almost product structure” of Newton strata is established by Mantovan
[
28
]
for PEL Shimura varieties. The general case is proved by Hamacher-Kim provided the lifting property
[
14
, Axiom A]
holds.

0.4.

We outline the strategy of the proof. First we show the
\sigma
-centralizer
\mathbb{J}_{b}
acts transitively on
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})
. Then we show the stabilizer of each connected component is the normal subgroup
\mathbb{J}_{b}\cap\ker(\eta_{G})
. Combining these two results one deduces that
\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K})\cong\mathbb{J}_{b}/(\mathbb{J}_{b}\cap\ker(\eta_{G}))\cong\pi_{1}(G)^{\sigma}
as desired.

The stabilizers can be determined by adapting the computations in
[
30
]
. The crucial part is to the show the transitivity of the
\mathbb{J}_{b}
action. Our starting point is the following natural surjection (see Theorem
2.1
)

\sqcup_{{\tilde{w}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}}\mathbb{J}_{b,{\tilde{w}}}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{0}(X(\lambda,b)_{K}),

where
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}
is the set of semi-standard elements (see §
1.4
) contained in
{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
and
[b]
, and
\mathbb{J}_{b,{\tilde{w}}}=\{g\in G({\breve{F}});g^{-1}b\sigma(g)={\tilde{w}}\}
on which
\mathbb{J}_{b}
acts transitively. So it remains to connect all the subsets
\mathbb{J}_{b,{\tilde{w}}}K/K
in
X(\lambda,b)_{K}
. To this end we consider the following decomposition

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}=\sqcup_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}^{+}}\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x},

where
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}^{+}
consists of standard elements in
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x}
consists of elements in
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}
that are
\sigma
-conjugate to
x\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}^{+}
under the Weyl group of
G
. Note that
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}^{+}
can be naturally identified with a subset of cocharacters dominated by
\lambda
. So we can adapt the connecting algorithm in
[
30
]
to connect
\mathbb{J}_{b,x}K/K
for
x\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b}^{+}
with each other. In contrast, the structure of
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x}
is much more mysterious, which makes it an essential difficulty to connect
\mathbb{J}_{b,{\tilde{w}}}K/K
for
{\tilde{w}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x}
with each other. To overcome it, we show that each set
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x}
contains a unique (distinguished) element
x_{\rm dist}
which is of minimal length in its Weyl group coset, and then connect
\mathbb{J}_{b,{\tilde{w}}}K/K
with
\mathbb{J}_{b,x_{\rm dist}}K/K
for all
{\tilde{w}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,b,x}
. This new connecting algorithm is motivated from the partial conjugation method by He in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
.

1.
Preliminaries

In the body of the paper we assume that
G
is unramified, simple, and adjoint. Without loss of generality, we assume further that
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
. Then
{\breve{F}}=\mathbb{k}((t))
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}=\mathbb{k}[[t]]
and residue field
\mathbb{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

1.1.

Let
T\subseteq B
be a maximal torus and a Borel subgroup defined over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
\mathcal{R}=(Y,\Phi^{\vee},X,\Phi,\mathbb{S}_{0})
be the root datum associated to the triple
(T\subseteq B\subseteq F)
, where
X
and
Y
are the character and cocharacter groups of
T
respectively equipped with a perfect pairing
\langle,\rangle:Y\times X\to\mathbb{Z}
;
\Phi=\Phi_{G}\subseteq X
(resp.
\Phi^{\vee}\subseteq Y
) is the set of roots (resp. coroots);
\mathbb{S}_{0}
is the set of simple roots appearing in
B
. For
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
s_{\alpha}
the reflection which sends
\mu\in Y
to
\mu-\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle\alpha^{\vee}
, where
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
denotes the coroot of
\alpha
. Via the bijection
\alpha\leftrightarrow s_{\alpha}
, we also denote by
\mathbb{S}_{0}
the set of simple reflections.

Let
W_{0}=N_{T}({\breve{F}})/T({\breve{F}})
be the Weyl group of
G
, where
N_{T}
is the normalizer of
T
in
G
. The Iwahori-Weyl group of
G
is given by

\tilde{W}_{G}=\tilde{W}=N_{T}({\breve{F}})/T(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})=Y\rtimes W_{0}=\{t^{\mu}w;\mu\in Y,w\in W_{0}\}.

We can view
\tilde{W}
as a subgroup of affine transformations of
V:=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, where the action of
{\tilde{w}}=t^{\mu}w
is given by
v\mapsto\mu+w(v)
. Let
\Phi^{+}=\Phi\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\mathbb{S}_{0}
be the set of positive roots and let
\mathbb{a}=\{v\in Y_{\mathbb{R}};0<\langle\alpha,v\rangle<1,\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
be the base alcove.

Let
\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{\Phi}_{G}=\Phi\times\mathbb{Z}
be the set of (real) affine roots. Let
{\tilde{\alpha}}=\alpha+k\in\tilde{\Phi}
. Then
\tilde{\alpha}
is an affine function on
V
such that
\tilde{\alpha}(v)=-\langle\alpha,v\rangle+k
. The induced action of
\tilde{W}
on
\tilde{\Phi}
is given by
{\tilde{w}}(\tilde{\alpha})(v)=\tilde{\alpha}({\tilde{w}}^{-1}(v))
. Let
s_{\tilde{\alpha}}=t^{k\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}\in\tilde{W}
be the corresponding affine reflection. Then
\{s_{\tilde{\alpha}};\tilde{\alpha}\in\tilde{\Phi}\}
generates the affine Weyl group

W^{a}=W^{a}_{G}=\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}\rtimes W_{0}=\{t^{\mu}w;\mu\in\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee},w\in W_{0}\}.

Moreover, we have
\tilde{W}=W^{a}\rtimes\Omega
, where
\Omega=\Omega_{G}=\{\omega\in\tilde{W};\omega(\mathbb{a})=\mathbb{a}\}
. Set
\tilde{\Phi}^{+}=\tilde{\Phi}_{G}^{+}=\{\tilde{\alpha}\in\tilde{\Phi};\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{a})>0\}
and
\tilde{\Phi}^{-}=-\tilde{\Phi}^{+}
. Then
\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{\Phi}^{+}\sqcup\tilde{\Phi}^{-}
. Note that
\Phi^{\pm}\subseteq\tilde{\Phi}^{\mp}
. Let
\ell:\tilde{W}\to\mathbb{N}
be the length function given by
\ell({\tilde{w}})=|\tilde{\Phi}^{-}\cap{\tilde{w}}(\tilde{\Phi}^{+})|
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{a}=\{s_{\tilde{\alpha}};\tilde{\alpha}\in\tilde{\Phi},\ell(s_{\tilde{\alpha}})=1\}
be the set of simple affine reflections. Notice that
(W^{a},\mathbb{S}^{a})
is a Coxeter system, and let
\leq
be the associated Bruhat order on
\tilde{W}=W^{a}\rtimes\Omega
.

For
{\tilde{\alpha}}=(\alpha,k)\in\tilde{\Phi}
, let
U_{{\tilde{\alpha}}}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\to LG
be the corresponding affine root subgroup, where
LG
denotes the loop group associated to
G
. More precisely,
U_{{\tilde{\alpha}}}(z)=u_{\alpha}(zt^{k})
for
z\in\mathbb{k}
, where
u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\to G
is the root subgroup of
\alpha
. We set

I=I_{G}=T(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})\prod_{{\tilde{\alpha}}\in\tilde{\Phi}^{+}}U_{{\tilde{\alpha}}}(\mathbb{k})=T(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}u_{\alpha}(t\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}})\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}}u_{-\beta}(\mathcal{O}_{\breve{F}}),

which is called an Iwahori subgroup of
G({\breve{F}})
.

1.2.

Let
v\in V=Y\otimes\mathbb{R}
. We say
v
is dominant if
\langle v,\alpha\rangle\geqslant 0
for each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, and denote by
\bar{v}
the unique dominant
W_{0}
-conjugate of
v
. Let
Y^{+}
and
V^{+}
be the set of dominant vectors in
Y
and
V
respectively. For
v,v^{\prime}\in V
we write
v^{\prime}\leqslant v
if
v-v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}(\Phi^{+})^{\vee}
.

Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius automorphism of
G({\breve{F}})
. We also denote by
\sigma
the induced automorphism on the root datum
\mathcal{R}
. Then
\sigma
acts on
V
as a linear transformation of finite order which preserves
\mathbb{a}
. For
{\tilde{w}}\in\tilde{W}
there exists a nonzero integer
m
such that
({\tilde{w}}\sigma)^{m}=t^{\xi}
for some
\xi\in Y
. Define
\nu_{\tilde{w}}=\xi/m\in V
, which does not depend on the choice of
m
.

Let
b\in G(L)
. We denote by
[b]=[b]_{G}=\{g^{-1}b\sigma(g);g\in G(L)\}
the
\sigma
-conjugate class of
b
. By
[
24
]
, the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
is determined by two invariants: the Kottwitz point
\kappa_{G}(b)\in\pi_{1}(G)_{\sigma}
and the Newton point
\nu_{G}(b)\in(V^{+})^{\sigma}
. Here
\kappa_{G}:G({\breve{F}})\to\pi_{1}(G)_{\sigma}=\pi_{1}(G)/(\sigma-1)\pi_{1}(G)
is the natural projection. To define
\nu_{G}(b)
, we note that there exists
{\tilde{w}}\in\tilde{W}
such that
{\tilde{w}}\in[b]
. Then
\nu_{G}(b)=\bar{\nu}_{{\tilde{w}}}
, which does not depend on the choice of
{\tilde{w}}
. For
b^{\prime}\in G({\breve{F}})
we set

\mathbb{J}_{b,b^{\prime}}=\mathbb{J}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{G}=\{g\in G({\breve{F}});g^{-1}b\sigma(g)=b^{\prime}\},

and put
\mathbb{J}_{b}=\mathbb{J}_{b,b^{\prime}}
if
b=b^{\prime}
.

For
\lambda\in Y^{+}
and
b\in G({\breve{F}})
we define

X(\lambda,b)=X^{G}(\lambda,b)_{I}=\{g\in G({\breve{F}})/I;g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in I{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)I\},

where
{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
is the
\lambda
-admissible set defined by

{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)=\{x\in\tilde{W};x\leq t^{w(\lambda)}\text{ for some $w\in W_{0}$}\}.

Note that
\mathbb{J}_{b}
acts on
X(\lambda,b)
by left multiplication. By
[
11
]
,
X(\lambda,b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\kappa_{G}(t^{\lambda})=\kappa_{G}(b)
and
\nu_{G}(b)\leqslant\lambda^{\diamond}
, where
t^{\lambda}:=\lambda(t)
and
\lambda^{\diamond}
is the
\sigma
-average of
\lambda
. We say the pair
(\lambda,b)
is Hodge-Newton irreducible if
X(\lambda,b)\neq\emptyset
and
\lambda^{\diamond}-\nu_{G}(b)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}(\Phi^{+})^{\vee}
.

1.3.

Let
M\supseteq T
be a (semi-standard) Levi subgroup of
G
. Then
B\cap M
is a Borel subgroup of
M
. By replacing the triple
(T,B,G)
with
(T,B\cap M,M)
, we can define, as in previous subsections,
\Phi_{M}^{+}
,
\tilde{W}_{M}

. Then
s{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}<s{\tilde{w}}s^{\prime}
(as
s{\tilde{w}}s^{\prime}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
), that is,
s{\tilde{w}}s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}(\alpha_{s}))=s{\tilde{w}}(\alpha_{s})\in\tilde{\Phi}^{+}
. Since
{\tilde{w}}(\alpha_{s})\in\tilde{\Phi}^{-}
(as
{\tilde{w}}\in\tilde{W}^{R}
), we have
{\tilde{w}}(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{s}
. This means
s{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}={\tilde{w}}s^{\prime}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, a contradiction. Notice that
s{\tilde{w}}s^{\prime}s\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
by Lemma
A.3
.

Suppose
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. Then
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}<s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}
. If
s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}<s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s
, then
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
by Lemma
A.3
. Otherwise, by Lemma
A.1
we have
s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s={\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}
(since
{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}<{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s
) and hence
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s=s{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. So we always have
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. By Lemma
A.2
we have
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{s}
, that is,
{\tilde{w}}(\alpha_{s^{\prime}})=-(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s^{\prime}})
, which is impossible as
{\tilde{w}}\in\tilde{W}^{R}
.

Suppose
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. Then
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s<s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s
. Since
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, by Lemma
A.2
we have
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s(\alpha_{s})=\alpha_{s}
, that is,
{\tilde{w}}(\alpha_{s^{\prime}})=\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{s^{\prime}}
. This means
ss^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}s=s^{\prime}{\tilde{w}}ss^{\prime}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, a contradiction.

Now we show the “if” part, that is,
u{\tilde{w}}u^{-1}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
for
u\in W_{R}
. We argue by induction on the length
u
. If
u=1
, the statement is true. Let
u=su_{1}>u_{1}
with
u_{1}\in W_{R}
and
s\in R
. We assume
u_{1}{\tilde{w}}u_{1}^{-1}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
by induction hypothesis. It remains to show that
u{\tilde{w}}u^{-1}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. Otherwise, we have
\ell(u{\tilde{w}}u^{-1})=\ell(u_{1}{\tilde{w}}u_{1}^{-1})+2
and
u_{1}{\tilde{w}}u^{-1}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
by Lemma
A.2
(1) & (3), which contradicts the “only if” part.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let
{\tilde{w}}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
and
s\in\mathbb{S}_{0}
such that
s{\tilde{w}}s\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
and
s{\tilde{w}}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\{\alpha_{s}\}
such that
{\tilde{w}}s_{\alpha}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
. Then
s{\tilde{w}}s_{\alpha}s\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)

Proof.

Suppose
s{\tilde{w}}s_{\alpha}s\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, then
s{\tilde{w}}s_{\alpha}\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
by Lemma
A.2
. As
s{\tilde{w}}\notin{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, we have

. On the other hand, as

,

and
s{\tilde{w}}s\in{\rm{Adm}}(\lambda)
, we have
s{\tilde{w}}(\alpha)\in\tilde{\Phi}^{-}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

.

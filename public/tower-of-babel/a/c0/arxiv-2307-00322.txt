Spanning trees in the square of pseudorandom graphs
Matías Pavez-Signé

Abstract
We show that for every Δ ∈ ℕ \Delta\in\mathbb{N} , there exists a constant C C such that if G G is an ( n , d , λ ) (n,d,\lambda) -graph with d / λ ≥ C d/\lambda\geq C and d d is large enough, then G 2 G^{2} contains every n n -vertex tree with maximum degree bounded by Δ \Delta . This answers a question of Krivelevich.



1
Introduction

A pseudorandom graph
G
on
n
vertices is a sparse graph that “resembles” many of the properties that are typically present in the binomial random graph
G(n,p)
with edge density
p=e(G)/\binom{n}{2}
. Arguably, the most crucial characteristic of random graphs that pseudorandom graphs try to capture is the
uniform edge distribution
property, that is, that all large subsets of vertices span approximately the expected number of edges that appear in the truly random case.

In this paper, we will take a widely used approach to pseudorandom graphs based on a
spectral gap
condition. Say that a graph
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph if
G
is an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph such that all of the non-trivial eigenvalues of
G
are bounded by
\lambda
in absolute value, in which case, the so-called
expander mixing lemma
implies that
G
enjoys the uniform edge distribution property. We refer to the excellent paper by Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
19
]
for a comprehensive survey about pseudorandom graphs. In this article, we are interested in the following extremal question.

Question 1.1
.

For an
n
-vertex graph
H
, how large
d/\lambda
must be so that every
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph contains a copy of
H
.

One of the most important directions here is when
H
is a Hamilton cycle, in which case we have the following beautiful conjecture posed by Krivelevich and Sudakov nearly 20 years ago.

Conjecture 1.2

(Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
18
]
)
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that the following holds. If
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
, then
G
contains a Hamilton cycle.

Krivilevich and Sudakov
[
19
]
proved that
d/\lambda\geq C\log n^{1-o(1)}
is enough to guarantee Hamiltonicity in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs, and quite recently Glock, Munhá Correira, and Sudakov
[
10
]
improved this result by showing that
d/\lambda\geq C(\log n)^{1/3}
is sufficient to force Hamiltonicity. Moreover, they showed that Conjecture
1.2
is true when
d\geq n^{\alpha}
for some fixed
\alpha>0
.

Besides Hamiltonicity, probably the most natural problem here is to study when
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs contain all
n
-vertex trees with bounded maximum degree. If we believe that Conjecture
1.2
is correct, then we should expect to find Hamiltonian paths in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs, as long as
{d}/{\lambda}
is large enough, and, therefore, it is plausible to believe that all bounded degree spanning trees appear as well. Indeed, this was explicitly asked in 2007 by Alon, Krivelevich, and Sudakov
[
1
]
.

Question 1.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Is it true that for any
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Delta)
such that if
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
, then
G
contains a copy of every spanning tree
T
with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
.

As pointed out by Glock, Munhá Correira, and Sudakov
[
10
]
, it is not even known how to find paths of length longer than
n-O(\frac{\lambda n}{d})
in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs when
d/\lambda\geq C
for some large constant
C
. Therefore, looking for general spanning trees in optimal pseudorandom graphs seems to be a quite challenging problem. For almost-spanning trees, however, Alon, Krivelevich, and Sudakov
[
1
]
showed that for any
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,\Delta)
such that if
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
, then
G
contains a copy of each tree with maximum degree bounded by
\Delta
and at most
(1-\varepsilon)n
vertices. The dependency of the constant
C=C(\varepsilon,\Delta)
was improved in subsequent works by Balogh, Csaba, Pei, and Samotij
[
5
]
and by Montgomery, Pavez-Signé, and Yan
[
21
]
when
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small. Regarding spanning trees, an unpublished work of Dellamonica Jr
[
6
]
shows that
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs contain a specific type of spanning tree of maximum degree
3
when
d/\lambda
is large, and a recent result by Han and Yang
[
11
]
establishes that
d\geq 2\lambda\Delta^{5\sqrt{\log n}}
is enough for an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph to contain all
n
-vertex trees with maximum degree bounded by
\Delta
.

A new twist to this problem was recently introduced by Krivelevich
[
16
]
, who considered a weakened version of Question
1.1
by replacing the
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
with its square
1
1

1

The square of a graph
G
, denoted
G^{2}
, is the graph obtained from
G
by adding edges between every pair of vertices at distance
2
in
G
.
. Krivelevich
[
16
]
proved that if
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
for some large constant
C
, then it contains a spanning subgraph which consists of a linear length cycle together with linearly many non-adjacent leaves attached to it, which he called
crown
, which implies that
G^{2}
is Hamiltonian. He also asked whether a similar result for spanning trees holds or not.

Question 1.4

(
[
16
]
)
.

Is it true that for every
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Delta)
such that if
d/\lambda\geq C
and
T
is an
n
-vertex tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
, then the square
G^{2}
of an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph

contains a copy of
T
.

Our first result is a positive answer to Question
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the following holds for every sufficiently large
d\in\mathbb{N}
. If
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
, then
G^{2}
contains a copy of every
n
-vertex tree with maximum degree at most
\Delta
.

A well-known result says that trees contain either a large collection of leaves or many vertex-disjoint induced paths of some fixed length, in compensation. If we are given a tree
T
with few leaves, and therefore many long induced paths, we can then define a new tree
\tilde{T}
which is obtained from
T
by replacing a single edge from each of those paths in
T
with a
spike
. This new tree
\tilde{T}
has the following two main features. Firstly,
\tilde{T}
has bounded maximum degree and contains many leaves and, secondly, if
G
contains a copy of
\tilde{T}
, then
G^{2}
contains a copy of
T
. Therefore, we will deduce Theorem
1.5
from the following result, which might be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.6
.

For every
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the following holds for every sufficiently large
d\in\mathbb{N}
. If
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d/\lambda\geq C
, then
G
contains a copy of every
n
-vertex tree with maximum degree at most
\Delta
and at least
\alpha n
leaves.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we give an overview of the proof of Theorem
1.6
. In Section
3
we introduce the main tools that we need here, and we prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
in Section
4
. Lastly, we give some concluding remarks in Section
5
.

2
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.6

Suppose we are given an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
and a tree
T
which contains a set of leaves
L
of size
|L|\geq\alpha n
. In order to embed
T
, we will follow a similar approach as it has been done before for trees with many leaves (see
[
3
,
11
,
15
,
20
]
for instance). Roughly speaking, the idea is to first embed
T-L
and then find a matching between the image of the parents of
L
and the unoccupied vertices in
G
.

Assume for a moment that we can actually embed
T-L
and let us discuss how to complete the embedding of
T
. When the host graph is a truly random graph, this can be easily done by just
sprinkling
a few more edges and then showing that with high probability there exists a matching between the set of parents and the rest of the uncovered vertices in the graph. However, when working with pseudorandom graphs, this strategy is not possible anymore. To overcome this issue, we will use the idea of
matchmakers
as introduced by Montgomery
[
20
]
and recently implemented by Krivelevich
[
16
]
.

We first pick pairwise disjoint small random subsets
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\subset V(G)
, called matchmakers, and show that with positive probability every vertex in
V(G)
has
\Omega(\lambda)
neighbours in each of the
V_{i}
’s (this is done by using the Lovász’s local lemma). This property will then imply that small sets of vertices expand into each of the
V_{i}
’s (see Lemma
3.8
). We will use each of these sets
V_{1},V_{2}
and
V_{3}
for different purposes. Firstly, we use
V_{3}
to show that even after removing
V_{1}
from
G
, we still have good expansion properties. Secondly, we prove that if we embed
T-L
outside
V_{1}
, then we can use the properties of
V_{1}
to show that the image of the set of parents of
L
will expand into the set of unoccupied vertices in
G
. Lastly, we will use
V_{2}
to show that the set of unused vertices in
G
also have good expansion properties in the image of the parents of
L
. To perform this last step, however, we need to embed
T-L
while ensuring that the image of the parents of
L
covers every vertex from
V_{2}
. We will explain now how to do this.

The main tool that we use to embed trees is a powerful embedding technique, sometimes called
extendability methods
or
tree embeddings with rollbacks
, which was first introduced by Friedman and Pippenger
[
8
]
in 1987 and subsequently improved by Haxell
[
12
]
in 2001. Here we will use a modern reformulation of this technique which is attributed to Glebov, Johannsen, and Krivelevich
[
9
]
, and that has played a major role in the solution of several problems in the last few years (see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
17
,
16
,
20
,
21
]
for instance). Roughly speaking, the extendability method (Lemma
3.13
) says that if we are given a subgraph
S_{i}\subset G
which is ’extendable’ and
G
has good expansion properties, then we can extend
S_{i}
by adding an edge
e_{i}
with one of its endpoints in
V(S_{i})
and other in
V(G)\setminus V(S_{i})
so that
S_{i}+e_{i}
remains extendable. In
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs, this method works smoothly as long as
|S_{i}|\leq|G|-\Theta(\frac{\Delta\lambda n}{d})
, and therefore, since
|L|\geq\alpha n\gg\frac{\lambda n}{d}
, we will be able to iterate this process until we embed all of
T-L
. The main issue here, however, is that we need to ensure that the matchmaker
V_{2}
is completely contained in the image of the parents of
L
.

In order to cover
V_{2}
, we will use some further ideas from the work of Montgomery
[
20
]
. We first take a large set
Q
of parents of leaves which are far apart from each other in the tree (this is possible as
T
has bounded degree). Using extendability methods, we will embed
T^{\prime}
in rounds so that at each round we cover more and more of
V_{i}
using only vertices from
Q
at each step. After this is completed, every vertex from
V_{2}
will be covered by vertices from
Q
and then we just finish the embedding of
T-L
using extendability methods. To complete the embedding of
T
, we use Hall’s theorem to find a matching between the image of the parents of leaves and the leftover vertices in the graph. The properties of the matchmakers will guarantee that Hall’s matching criteria is satisfied and thus we can complete the embedding of
T
.

3
Preliminaries

We will use standard graph theory notation. For a graph
G
, we denote by
V(G)
and
E(G)
the set of vertices and edges of
G
, respectively, and write
|G|=|V(G)|
and
e(G)=|E(G)|
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
N(v)
the set of neighbours of
v
and let
d(v)=|N(v)|
denote the degree of
v
. Given a subset
S\subset V(G)
, the set of neighbours of
S
is
\Gamma(S)=\bigcup_{s\in S}N(s)
and the external neighbourhood of
S
is
N(S)=\Gamma(S)\setminus S
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
and sets
U,S\subset V(G)
, we write
d(v,U)=|N(v)\cap U|
and
N(S,U)=N(S)\cap U
. When working with more than one graph, we will use a subscript to specify which graph are we working with. For example, if
H
is a subgraph of
G
and
v\in V(H)
, then
d_{H}(v)
denotes the degree of
v
in
H
. Given a subset
S\subset V(G)
, we write
G[S]
to denote the graph
induced
by
S
, that is, the graph with vertex set
S
and all the edges from
G
with both endpoints in
S
, and we write
G-S
for the graph
G[V(G)\setminus S]
. For two sets
A,B\subset V(G)
, we let
e(A,B)
denote the number of edges with one endpoint in
A
and the other endpoint in
B
, and, if
A
and
B
are disjoint, we let
G[A,B]
denote the bipartite graph induced by
A
and
B
in
G
, in which case
e(A,B)
is just the number of edges in
G[A,B]
. For a graph
H
and an edge
e\not\in E(H)
, we let
H+e
denote the graph obtained from
H
by adding the edge
e
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. We will use the standard hierarchy notation, that is, for real numbers
a,b\in(0,1]
, we will write
a\ll b
to mean that given
b
fixed, there exists
a_{0}>0
such that if
a\leq a_{0}
then all the subsequent relevant statements hold. If
1/x
appears in such a hierarchy, we will assume that
x
is a natural number, and hierarchies with more constants are defined in a similar way and are to be read from right to left.

3.1
Probabilistic tools

We will use the following standard probabilistic results (see
[
13
, Corollary 2.3]
and
[
2
, Corollary 5.1.2]
).

Lemma 3.1

(Chernoff’s bound)
.

Let
X
be binomial random variable. Then, for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{3}{2}
,

\mathbb{P}(\big|X-\mathbb{E}[X]\big|\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[X])\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{3}\mathbb{E}[X]\right).

Lemma 3.2

(Lovász’s local lemma)
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be events in a probability space. Suppose that each event
A_{i}
is independent of all the other events
A_{j}
but at most
d
. If
\mathbb{P}(A_{i})\leq p
for all
i\in[n]
and
ep(d+1)\leq 1
, then
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\overline{A_{i}})>0
.

3.2
Dividing trees

Given a tree
T
, say that two subtrees
S_{1},S_{2}\subset T
divide
T
if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
share exactly one vertex and
T=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
20
, Proposition 3.19]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree and let
Q\subset V(T)
be a fixed subset. Then, there exists subtrees
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
that divide
T
and
|V(S_{1})\cap Q|,|V(S_{2})\cap Q|\geq|Q|/3
.

For a tree
T
, say that a subset
X\subset V(T)
is
k
-separated if every pair of vertices from
X
are at distance at least
k
in
T
. The following result says that large subsets of bounded degree trees contain a large separated subset.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
20
, Corollary 3.16]
)
.

Let
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\geq 0
. Let
T
be a tree with
\Delta(T)\leq\Delta
which contains a subset
X\subset V(T)
of size
|X|\geq 3\Delta^{k}
. Then, there exists a subset
Q\subset X
which is
(2k+2)
-separated in
T
and
|Q|\geq|X|/(8k+8)\Delta^{k}
.

3.3
Expansion properties of pseudorandom graphs

In this section, we collect properties of
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs that we will use throughout the paper. The main ingredient that we use is the well-known expander mixing lemma (see
[
19
, Theorem 2.11]
for a proof).

Lemma 3.5

(Expander Mixing Lemma)
.

Let
G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph. Then, for every pair of (not necessarily disjoint) sets
A,B\subset V(G)
, we have

\left|e(A,B)-\tfrac{d}{n}|A||B|\right|<\lambda\sqrt{|A||B|}.

Definition 3.6
.

Say that a graph
G
is
m
-joined if for every pair of disjoint subsets
A,B\subset V(G)
, each of size
m
, there exists at least one edge between them.

A direct consequence of the expander mixing lemma is that
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs are
\frac{\lambda n}{d}
-joined.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph, then
G
is
\frac{\lambda n}{d}
-joined.

Proof.

Given two disjoint subsets
A,B\subset V(G)
of size
|A|=|B|=\lambda n/d
, by the Expander Mixing Lemma we have

e(A,B)>\frac{d}{n}|A||B|-\lambda\sqrt{|A||B|}=0,

as
|A||B|=(\lambda n/d)^{2}
.
∎

The following lemma states that in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs we can translate minimum degree conditions into an expansion property for small sets.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
D\geq 1
and let
d,\lambda>0
satisfy
d/\lambda>2D
. Let
G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph which contains a subset
X\subset V(G)
such that every vertex
v\in V(G)
has at least
2D\lambda
neighbours in
X
. Then, every subset
S\subset V(G)
of size
|S|\leq\lambda n/d
satisfies
|N(S)\cap X|\geq D|S|
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a subset
S\subset V(G)
of size
1\leq|S|\leq\lambda n/d
such that
|N(S)\cap X|<D|S|
. Let
Y=N(S)\cap X
. Using the Expander Mixing Lemma, we have

2\lambda D|S|\leq e(S,Y)<\frac{d}{n}|S|\cdot|Y|+\lambda\sqrt{|S||Y|}\leq\lambda\cdot D|S|+\lambda|S|\sqrt{D},

and thus

2\lambda D<\lambda D+\lambda\sqrt{D}\leq 2\lambda D,

which is a contradiction.
∎

The last result we need is a lower bound on the second largest eigenvalue in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs.

Lemma 3.9
.

Every
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph satisfies
\lambda\geq\sqrt{d\cdot\frac{n-d}{n-1}}
.

3.4
Finding matchmakers

The following result finds, in an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
, a collection of pairwise disjoint small subsets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}\subset V(G)
so that each vertex in
G
has a large number of neighbours in each of the
V_{i}
’s.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
1/d\ll 1/C\ll 1/t\ll 1/\ell
and let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>0
satisfy
d/\lambda\geq C
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
d\leq 3n/5
and let
G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph. Then, there exist disjoint subsets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}\subset V(G)
, each with at most
4\lambda tn
vertices, such that every vertex
v\in V(G)
has at least
t\lambda/4
neighbours in each of the
V_{i}
’s.

Proof.

Letting
k=\lfloor{d/t\lambda}\rfloor
, note that
k\geq 1
as
d/\lambda\geq C>t
. We colour each vertex from
G
uniformly at random with an element from
[k]
, making all choices independently. For
v\in V(G)
and a colour
i\in[k]
, let
A_{v,i}
denote the event that
v
has less than
d/2k
neighbours in colour
i
. Then, using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{v,i})\leq 2\exp(-d/12k).

Note that the event
A_{v,i}
is not independent only of those events
A_{u,j}
such that either
u
and
v
have common neighbours or
u=v
, and the number of such events is at most
2kd^{2}
. Using Lemma
3.9
and that
d\leq 3n/5
, we get

kd^{2}e^{-d/12k}\leq td\lambda e^{-\lambda t/24}\leq d^{2}e^{-t\sqrt{d}/100}\ll 1

whenever
d
is large enough. Therefore, from Lemma
3.2
we deduce that
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge_{i\in[n]}\overline{A_{i}})>0
, and thus there is a colouring of
V(G)
so that every vertex has at least
d/2k\geq t\lambda/4
neighbours in each colour. Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
be the
\ell
smallest colour classes and suppose that
|V_{1}|\leq\ldots\leq|V_{\ell}|
. Then, using that
\ell\leq k/2
if
t
is large enough, we get

|V_{i}|\leq|V_{\ell}|\leq\frac{n}{k-\ell}\leq\frac{2n}{k}\leq\frac{4\lambda tn}{d}.

∎

3.5
The extendability method

Here we state some of the main tools of the extendability method, we refer to
[
9
,
20
]
for a more comprehensive exposition of this technique.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
D,m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
D\geq 3
. For a graph
G
and a subgraph
S\subset G
, say that
S
is
(D,m)
-extendable in
G
if
S
has maximum degree at most
D
and for all
U\subset V(G)
with
1\leq|U|\leq 2m
one has

|\Gamma_{G}(U)\setminus V(S)|\geq(D-1)|U|-\sum_{u\in U\cap V(S)}(d_{S}(u)-1).

(3.1)

The following lemma says that it is enough to control the external neighbourhood of small sets in order to verify extendability.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
D,m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
D\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
. Let
G
be a graph and let
S\subset G
be a subgraph with
\Delta(S)\leq D
. If for all
U\subset V(G)
, with
1\leq|U|\leq 2m
, we have

|N(U,V(G)\setminus V(S))|\geq D|U|,

then
S
is
(D,m)
-extendable in
G
.

The next result states that we can add leaves to an extendable subgraph while remaining extendable.

Lemma 3.13

(
[
20
, Corollary 3.5]
)
.

Let
D,m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
D\geq 3
, and let
G
be an
m
-joined graph. Let
S
be a
(D,m)
-extendable subgraph of
G
such that
|G|\geq|S|+(2D+3)m+1
. Then for every
s\in V(S)
with
d_{S}(s)\leq D-1
, there exists
y\in N_{G}(s)\setminus V(S)
such that
S+sy
is
(D,m)
-extendable.

A direct consequence of Lemma
3.13
is that we can embed large trees and remain extendable.

Corollary 3.14

(
[
20
, Corollary 3.7]
)
.

Let
D,m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
D\geq 3
, and let
G
be an
m
-joined graph. Let

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we solved a question of Krivelevich (Question
1.4
) about whether the square of
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs contain spanning bounded degree trees. While doing so, we also made progress towards a question of Alon, Krivelevich, and Sudakov (Question
1.3
), giving an affirmative answer provided the tree has linearly many leaves. Actually, in Theorem
4.2
we can deal with trees with
\Theta(\frac{\lambda n}{d})
leaves which, because of Lemma
4.5
, implies that it only remains to solve the case when
T
has a collection of
\Theta(\frac{\lambda n}{d})
vertex-disjoint bare paths, each of length
\Theta(\frac{d}{\lambda})
. If
d/\lambda=\text{polylog}(n)
, then it seems plausible that one can use the absorption approach introduced by Montgomery
[
20
]
to deal with trees with
\Omega(n/\text{polylog}(n))
bare paths, but, if
d/\lambda
is much smaller than
\log n
, then this question seems to be out of reach at the moment. It would be quite interesting, though, to answer Question
1.3
when
d
is large in terms of
n
, as in the recent work of Glock, Munha Correia, and Sudakov
[
10
]
for Hamilton cycles.

Question 5.1
.

Is it true that for any
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that if
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph, with
d/\lambda\geq C

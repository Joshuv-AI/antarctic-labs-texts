Abstract
A graph G G is k k -path-coverable if its vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) can be covered by k k or fewer vertex disjoint paths. In this paper, using the Q Q -index of a connected graph G G , we present a tight sufficient condition for G G with fixed minimum degree and large order to be k k -path-coverable.



Key words:

Q
-index; minimum degree;
k
-path-coverable.

We only consider simple connected graphs throughout paper. The notations we used are standard from
[
2
]

G

V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
such that
|V(G)|=n
and
|E(G)|=e(G)
. We use

to denote the degree of a vertex

in
G

\delta(G)=\delta

G\cup H
is denoted to be the disjoint union of
G
and
H,

G\vee H
the join of
G
and
H,
which is obtained from

and
H.

We use the symbol

to denote the vertices
i
and
j

otherwise.

The

of

. The

of

The largest eigenvalue of
Q(G)
, denoted by
q(G)
, is called the
Q
-
index
(or the signless Laplacian spectral radius) of

.

2
Preliminary Results

We first present some basic notations and results.
For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{T}\neq 0
, according to the Rayleigh’s principle, we have

q(G)=\max_{x}\frac{\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}=\max_{x}\frac{x^{T}Q(G)x}{x^{T}x}.

By the definition of
Q(G),
we have

\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle=\sum_{i\in V(G)}d(i)x_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{i\sim j}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{i\sim j}(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}.

If
{h}
is the unit positive eigenvector of
q(G)
,
then

Q(G)h=q(G)h.

If we take the
i
-th entry of both sides and rearranging terms, we get

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i))h_{i}=\sum_{i\sim j}h_{j}.

(1)

Let
N(i)
denote the neighbor of
i,

N[i]=N(i)\cup\{i\}
. From the above, we have

Lemma 2.1

[
21
]

For any
i,j\in V(G),
we have

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i))(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N(j)}h_{k}-\sum_{l\in N(j)\setminus N(i)}h_{l}.

We now present some graph notations we will use.
The concept of stability was introduced by Bondy and Chvátal
[
1
]
.
Let
P
be a property defined on all graphs of order
n
.
Let
k
be a non-negative integer. The property
P
is said to be

-stable
if whenever
G+uv
has property
P
and

d_{G}(u)+d_{G}(v)\geq k,

where

, then

itself has property
P
.
Among all the graphs
H
of order
n
such that
G\subseteq H
and

d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(v)<k

for all
uv\notin E(H)
, there is a unique smallest on with respect to size. We shall call this graph
the

-
closure
of G, and denote it by
\mathrm{cl}_{k}(G)
. Obviously,
\mathrm{cl}_{k}(G)

can be obtained from

by recursive procedure which consists of joining non-adjacent vertices
with degree-sum at least

. This concept plays a prominent role in any structual graph theory problems.

3
Proof of the Main Results

In order to prove our main result, we need the following stability result.

Lemma 3.1

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 8\delta+6k+7
, where
k\geq 1
.
If
\delta(G)\geq\delta\geq 1
and

e(G)>\frac{1}{2}(n-k-\delta-1)(n-k-\delta-2)+(\delta+k+1)(\delta+1),

then
G
is
k
-path-coverable unless
G\subseteq B(n,k,\delta)
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{cl}_{n-k}(G)
. If
G^{\prime}
is
k
-path-coverable, then so is
G

by the
(n-k)
-stablity of
k
-path-coverable. We now assume that
G^{\prime}
is not
k
-path-coverable,
note that
\delta(G^{\prime})\geq\delta(G)\geq\delta
and
e(G^{\prime})\geq e(G)
. Next, we show that

\omega(G^{\prime})=n-\delta-k.

Let
C
be the vertex set of a maximum clique of
G^{\prime}
, since every two vertices whose degree sum is
at least
n-k
are adjacent, then
C
contains all vertices whose degree is at least
(n-k)/2
.
Let
H=G^{\prime}-C
and
t=|C|
.

Suppose first that
1\leq t\leq(n+k)/3+\delta+4/3
, observe that
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\leq(n-k-1)/2

for every
v\in V(H)
, if otherwise, we may assume
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)>(n-k-1)/2
,
because
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)
is a positive integer, then
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\geq(n-k-1)/2+1/2=(n-k)/2
,
so
v
is contained in
C
, a contradiction. Clearly,
d_{C}(v)\leq t-1
,
so some tedious manipulation yields that

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e(G^{\prime}[C])+e(H)+e(V(H),C)

\displaystyle=e(G^{\prime}[C])+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{v\in V(H)}d_{G^{\prime}}(v)+\sum\limits_{v\in V(H)}d_{C}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\leq{t\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-t)\left(\frac{n-k-1}{2}+t-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+k+1}{4}t+\frac{n(n-k-3)}{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+k+1}{4}\left(\frac{n+k}{3}+\delta+\frac{4}{3}\right)+\frac{n(n-k-3)}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}n^{2}+\frac{3\delta-k-4}{12}n+(k+1)(3\delta-k+3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-k-\delta-1)(n-k-\delta-2)+(\delta+1)(\delta+k+1),

which leads to a contradiction (The last inequality follows due to
n\geq 8\delta+6k+7
).

Suppose second that
(n+k)/3+\delta+4/3<t\leq n-\delta-k-1
,
note that
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\leq n-k-t
for every
v\in V(H)
,
if otherwise, we assume
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\geq n-k-t+1
, then
v
will be adjacent to every vertex in
C
.
Therefore

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e(G^{\prime}[C])+e(H)+e(V(H),C)

\displaystyle\leq e(G^{\prime}[C])+\sum\limits_{v\in V(H)}d_{G^{\prime}}(v)

\displaystyle\leq{t\choose 2}+(n-t)(n-k-t)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}t^{2}+(-2n+k-\frac{1}{2})t+n(n-k)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\left(n-\delta-k-1\right)^{2}+(-2n+k-\frac{1}{2})(n-\delta-k-1)+n(n-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n-k-\delta-1)(n-k-\delta-2)+(\delta+1)(\delta+k+1),

also a contradiction.

Finally, suppose that
t\geq n-\delta-k+1
, since
G^{\prime}
is not a clique, then
V(H)
is not empty.
Note that
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\geq\delta
for every
v\in V(H)
and
d_{G^{\prime}}(u)\geq t-1\geq n-k-\delta

for every
u\in C
, this means that every vertex of
V(H)
is adjacent to every vertex of
C
,
this contradicts the fact that
C
is a maximum clique.
Consequently, we now infer that
\omega(G^{\prime})=n-\delta-k
.

We call that a vertex in
C
is a
frontier
vertex if it has degree at least
n-\delta-k
in
G^{\prime}
,
that is to say, it has at least one neighbour in
H
, and denote
F=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{s}\}
by
the set of frontier vertices. Since
\omega(G^{\prime})=n-\delta-k
, then
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)\geq n-\delta-k-1

for every
v\in C
, we can see by the
(n-k)
-stablity that every vertex in
H
has degree exactly

\delta
in
G^{\prime}
, furthermore, every vertex in
H
is adjacent to every frontier vertex in
G^{\prime}
, and
then we have
1\leq s\leq\delta
. Since
C
is a clique
K_{n-\delta-k}
,
then we can choose a path
P
in
C-F
with two end-vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{s}
.

We assert that
s=\delta
.

If
s=1
, then every vertex in
H
has degree
\delta-1
in
H
.
According to Dirac’s theorem,

H
has a path of order at least
\delta
.
First, we assume that
H
has a path
P^{\prime}
of order
\delta+1
,
and let
x,x^{\prime}
be the two end-vertices of
P^{\prime}
, then
G^{\prime}
can be covered by one path

x^{\prime}P^{\prime}xu_{1}P
and the rest
(k-1)
vertices in
H
, so
G^{\prime}
is
k
-path-coverable, a contradiction.
We now assume that
H
has no paths of order more than
\delta
,
let
P^{\prime}
be a path of order
\delta
in
H
and
V(H-P^{\prime})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
.
Observe that
x
has no neighbour in
V(H-P^{\prime})
and
d_{H}(x)=\delta-1
, which implies that

xx^{\prime}\in E(H)
. Since
H
has no paths of order more than
\delta
, then every vertex in
V(H-P^{\prime})

has no neighbour in
P^{\prime}
, so
H-P^{\prime}
has an edge, say,
v_{1}v_{2}
. Then
G^{\prime}
can be covered by two paths

x^{\prime}P^{\prime}xu_{1}P,v_{1}v_{2}
and the rest
(k-2)
vertices in
H
, Also a contradiction.

If
2\leq s\leq\delta-1
, so every vertex in
H
has degree
\delta-s
in
H
. By Dirac’s theorem,

H
has a path of order at least
\delta-s+1
. We first assume that
H
has a path
P^{\prime}
of order

\delta-s+2
and
V(H-P^{\prime})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{s+k-2}\}
, then

v_{1}u_{1}v_{2}u_{2}\cdots v_{s-1}u_{s-1}x^{\prime}P^{\prime}xu_{s}P
is path of order

, so
G^{\prime}
is

k
-path-coverable, a contradiction. Now we assume that
H
has no paths of order more than

\delta-s+1
, let
P^{\prime}
be a path of order
\delta-s+1
in
H
and
V(H-P^{\prime})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k+s-1}\}
.
Observe that
x
has no neighbour in
V(H-P^{\prime})
and
d_{H}(x)=\delta-s
, which implies that

xx^{\prime}\in E(H)
. Since
H
has no paths of order more than
\delta-s-1
, then every vertex in
V(H-P^{\prime})

has no neighbour in
P^{\prime}
, so

has an edge, say,

. Then

v_{s}u_{1}v_{1}v_{2}u_{2}v_{3}u_{3}\cdots v_{s-1}u_{s-1}x^{\prime}P^{\prime}xu_{s}P
is a path of order

,
so

is
k
-path-coverable, this also leads to a contradiction.

We have thus proved that

, as we claimed.
Then
H
is an independent vertices
\overline{K_{\delta+k}}
, i.e.,

, we have

. The proof is now complete.

Lemma 3.2

For each graph
G\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(n,k,\delta)
,
we have
q(G)\geq 2(n-k-\delta-1)
.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(n,k,\delta).

Note that
q(K_{n-k-\delta}\cup\overline{K_{\delta+k}})=2(n-k-\delta-1)
,
and we define a characteristic vector
x
,
where
x_{i}=1
if
i\in Y\cup Z
and
x_{j}=0
if
j\in X
.
Obviously
x
is the eigenvector corresponding to
q(K_{n-k-\delta-1}\cup\overline{K_{\delta+k+1}})
.
Then we get

\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle-\langle Q(K_{n-k-\delta}\cup\overline{K_{\delta+k}})x,x\rangle=\delta(\delta+k)-4|E^{\prime}|\geq 0.

By Rayleigh’s principle, we have
q(G)\geq 2(n-k-\delta-1).

Lemma 3.3

For each graph
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
, we have
q(G)>2(n-k-\delta-1)-1.

Proof.

Let
x
be the column vector defined in the proof of Lemma
3.2
. We obtain that

\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle-\langle Q(K_{n-\delta-k}\cup\overline{K_{k+\delta}})x,x\rangle=\delta(\delta+k)-4|E^{\prime}|\geq-4.

Similarly, we have
q(G)\geq 2(n-k-\delta-1)-\frac{4}{\|x\|^{2}}>2(n-k-\delta-1)-1.

Let
G
be a graph of
\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
with the largest signless Laplacian spectral radius,
and furthermore we may assume that the induced graph

contains the largest number of edges.
Let

.
We assume further that
\max_{i\in V(G)}h_{i}=1

Lemma 3.4

Assume
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
.
For each
i\in X
, we have
h_{i}\leq\frac{\delta}{q(G)-\delta}
.

Proof.

Applying equation (

) at

Since

and
\max_{i\in V(G)}h_{i}=1

(q(G)-\delta)h_{i}\leq\delta

Next, we divide

\displaystyle Y_{1}=\{i\in Y:d(i)=n-1\},\quad Y_{2}=\{i\in Y:d(i)\leq n-2\},

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\{i\in Z:d(i)=n-\delta-k-1\},\quad Z_{2}=\{i\in Z:d(i)\leq n-k-\delta-2\}.

3.5

We already know the upper bound of

.
Therefore we get

To obtain the difference between

and

Lemma 3.5

Assume
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
as defined above.

(1)

If

, then

for all

and

.

(2)

If

, then
h_{i}>h_{j}
for all
i\in Z_{1}
and
j\in Z_{2}
.

(3)

If

(1) By contradiction, we assume that there are some

.
Let

(2) By Lemma

, for each

j\in Z_{2}
, we have

(q(G)-d(i))(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N(j)}h_{k}-\sum_{l\in N(j)\setminus N(i)}h_{l}.

We notice that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\}
, equivalently,

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}.

Note that
d(i)>d(j),
the proof is completed.

(3) If
Y_{1}\neq\emptyset
. For each
i\in Y_{1}
and

, by Lemma

, we have

(q(G)-d(i))(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N(j)}h_{k}-\sum_{l\in N(j)\setminus N(i)}h_{l}.

Note that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\}
, rearranging the last equation, we obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}.

(3)

Since
d(i)>d(j),
the proof is completed.

The key step for proving Lemma
3.7
is to show Lemma
3.6
.

Lemma 3.6

Assume
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
as defined above. We have

\max\limits_{i\in V(G)}h_{i}-\min\limits_{j\in Y\cup Z}h_{j}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Proof.

We distinguish it into two cases.

Case 1
:

Y_{1}\neq\emptyset.
Notice that
\max_{i\in V(G)}h_{i}
is attained by vertices from
Y_{1}
by the above analysis. Hence
d(i)=n-1
, i.e., the vertex
i
is adjacent to all other vertices in
G
.

Subcase 1.1
:
If
j\in Y_{2},
we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\}
.
Thus we have
d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1

and
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1
.
Note that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\}
, applying (4), we get

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}\leq\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+2.

Since
i\in Y_{1}
, then

\displaystyle h_{i}-h_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}=\frac{(\delta+k)\delta+4}{2(q(G)-n+2)}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 1.2
:
If
j\in Z_{1}
, we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=X
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=\emptyset
.
Then
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|=\delta+k+1
. Meanwhile, note that
d(i)-d(j)=\delta+k+1
. So

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})

\displaystyle=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}-\sum_{l\in X}h_{l}

\displaystyle\leq(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\delta+k+\delta+k.

\displaystyle=2(\delta+k).

Since
i\in Y_{1}
, we easily have

\displaystyle h_{i}-h_{j}<\frac{2(\delta+k)}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}=\frac{4(\delta+k)}{2(q(G)-n+2)}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 1.3
:
If
j\in Z_{2}
, we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in X\cup Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\}
,
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=\emptyset
. Thus we have

|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\delta+k+\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1
.
Then, note that
d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1+\delta+k
.
Therefore, we get

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})

\displaystyle=(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}-\sum_{l\in X}x_{l}

\displaystyle\leq(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+2(\delta+k)+2.

\displaystyle=\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)}{2}+2.

Since
i\in Y_{1},
we easily have

\displaystyle h_{i}-h_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}=\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+2)}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Case 2
:

Y_{1}=\emptyset
. Notice that
\max_{i\in V(G)}h_{i}
is attained by vertices from
Z_{1}
.
And for each
i\in Z_{1},d(i)=n-\delta+k-1
,
hence the vertex
i
is adjacent to all other vertices in
Y\cup Z
.

Subcase 2.1
:
If
j\in Z_{2}
, we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\}
.
Thus we get
d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1

and
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1
.
Note that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\}
, applying (4), we get

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}\leq\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+2.

Since
i\in Z_{1},
we easily have

\displaystyle h_{i}-h_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-\delta-k-1)+1}=\frac{(\delta+k)\delta+4}{2(q(G)-n+\delta+k+2)}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 1.2
:
If
j\in Y_{2}
, we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\}
,
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=X
.
Thus we have
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+1
.
We also observe that
|d(i)-d(j)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{4}\right\rfloor+\delta+k+1
.
Similarly, we get

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(h_{i}-h_{j})

\displaystyle=(d(i)-d(j))h_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}h_{k}-\sum_{l\in X}h_{l}

\displaystyle\leq(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\delta+k)\delta}{2}+\delta+k+1+1.

\displaystyle=\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+2)}{2}+2.

Since
i\in Z_{1},
we easily have

\displaystyle h_{i}-h_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+2)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-\delta-k-2)+1}=\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+2)+4}{2(q(G)-n+\delta+k+2)}<\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

The proof is completed.

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, we also need another lemma.

Lemma 3.7

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq F(k,\delta)
and minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq\delta\geq 1
.
For each graph
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
, we have
q(G)<2(n-\delta-k-1)
.

Proof.

We first assume
G\in\mathcal{B}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
such that
G
has the largest signless Laplacian spectral radius and
G[Y]
contains the largest number of edges.
Let
q(G)
be the largest eigenvalue of
Q(G)
and
h
be the eigenvector corresponding to
q(G)
.
Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
together with

|E^{\prime}|=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta(\delta+k)}{4}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{\delta(\delta+k)+1}{4}
imply that

\displaystyle\langle Q(G)h,h\rangle-\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta+k}}\cup K_{n-\delta-k})h,h\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in X,j\in Y}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{2}-\sum_{{i,j}\in E^{\prime}}(h_{i}+h_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|X||Y|\left(\max_{i\in X}h_{i}+\max_{j\in Y}h_{j}\right)^{2}-|E^{\prime}|\left(2\min_{j\in Y\cup Z}h_{j}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta(k+\delta)\left(1+\frac{\delta}{q(G)-\delta}\right)^{2}-[\delta(\delta+k)+1]\left(1-\frac{(\delta+k)(\delta+4)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}\right)^{2}.

Since
q(G)>2(n-\delta-k-1)-1
by Lemma
3.3
,
then we can choose for our purpose that

n\geq(\delta^{2}+k\delta+7\delta+6k+9)(\delta^{2}+k\delta+1)
,
to make sure the following holds.

\langle Q(G)h,h\rangle-\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta+k}}\cup K_{n-\delta-k})h,h\rangle<0.

According to Rayleigh’s principle,

\frac{\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta+k}}\cup K_{n-\delta-k})h,h\rangle}{\langle h,h\rangle}\leq q(\overline{K_{\delta+k}}\cup K_{n-\delta-k})=2(n-\delta-k-1).

Therefore, we have
q(G)=\frac{\langle Q(G)h,h\rangle}{\langle h,h\rangle}<2(n-\delta-k-1)
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

2(n-\delta-k-1)\leq q(G)\leq\frac{2e(G)}{n-1}+n-2.

We obtain

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(n-2\delta-2k)(n-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n-k-\delta-1)(n-k-\delta-2)+(\delta+1)(k+\delta+1)

\displaystyle\quad+n-\frac{1}{2}(k+\delta+1)(3\delta+k+2)-1

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}(n-k-\delta-1)(n-k-\delta-2)+(\delta+1)(k+\delta+1),

the last inequality holds as
n\geq\frac{1}{2}(k+\delta+1)(3\delta+k+2)+1
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
G
is
k
-path coverable unless
G\subseteq{B}(n,k,\delta)
.
Together with Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.7
, the result follows.

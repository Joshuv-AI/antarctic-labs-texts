θ \theta -derivations on convolution algebras
M. Eisaei 1 and Gh. R. Moghimi 1

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate θ \theta -derivations on Banach algebra L 0 ∞ ​ ( w ) ∗ L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*} . First, we study the range of them and prove the Singer-Wermer conjucture. We also give a characterization of the space of all θ \theta -derivations on L 0 ∞ ​ ( w ) ∗ L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*} . Then, we prove automatic continuity and Posner’s theorems for θ \theta -derivations.

\theta
-derivations, Singer-Wermer conjucture, automatic continuity, Posner’s theorems.

Introduction

Let
A
be a Banach algebra with the center
Z(A)
and the right annihilator; i.e.,

Z(A)=\{a\in A:~~ax=xa~~~\text{for all}~~~x\in A\}

and

\hbox{ran}(A)=\{r\in A:~~ar=0~~~\text{for all}~~~a\in A\}.

Let
D:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
D
is called
k
-centralizing
if for every
m\in A
, we have

[D(m),m^{k}]:=D(m)m^{k}-m^{k}D(m)\in Z(A).

In particular, if
[D(m),m^{k}]=0
, then
D
is called

k
-commuting
.
Assume now that
\theta:A\rightarrow A
is a homomorphism. Then
D
is called a
\theta
-derivation
if

D(mn)=D(m)\theta(n)+\theta(m)D(n)

for all
m,n\in A
.
If
\theta
is the identity map, then
D
is called a
derivation
.

Let us recall that a continuous function
w:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[1,\infty)

is called a
weight function
if
w(0)=1
and for all
x,y\in[0,\infty)

w(x+y)\leq w(x)w(y).

Let
L^{1}(w)
be the Banach space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
[0,\infty)
such that
wf\in L^{1}([0,\infty))
, the Banach algebra of all Lebesque integrable functions on
[0,\infty)
.
It is well-known that
L^{1}(w)
is a Banach algebra with the convolution product

\varphi*\psi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(y)\psi(x-y)d(y),~~~~(\varphi,\psi\in L^{1}([0,\infty))

and the norm

\|\varphi\|_{w}=\int_{0}^{\infty}w(x)|\varphi|(x)dx~~~(\varphi\in L^{1}([0,\infty));

see [4, 15].
Let also
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)
be the Banach space of all Lebesgue measure functions
f
on
[0,\infty)
such that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\text{ess sup}\bigg\{\frac{f(y)\chi_{(x,\infty)}(y)}{w(y)}:y\geq 0\bigg\}=0,

where
\chi_{(x,\infty)}
is the characteristic function of
(x,\infty)
on
[0,\infty)
.
It is well-known that the dual of
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)
, represented by

L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
, is a Banach algebra with the first Arens product defined by

\langle mn,f\rangle=\langle m,nf\rangle,

where
\langle nf,\varphi\rangle=\langle n,f\varphi\rangle
, in which

f\varphi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x+y)\varphi(y)dy

for all
m,n\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
,
f\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)
,
\varphi\in L^{1}(w)
and
x\geq 0
; see [7, 8, 11, 12].
Note that every element
\varphi\in L^{1}(w)
can be regarded as an element of
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
,

\langle\varphi,f\rangle=\int\varphi(x)f(x)dx

for all
f\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)
.
We denoted by

\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
the set of all right identities of

L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
with bounded one.
For every
u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and

m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
, we have
m-um\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and

m=um+(m-um).

It follows that

L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}=uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}\oplus\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}).

One can prove that the radical of
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
is equal to

\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
; see [13].

Derivations and
\theta
-derivations were studied by several authors [1-3, 9, 10, 13, 14].
For example, derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
investigated in [13].
They proved that the range of a derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
is contained into
\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.
They also showed that the zero map is the only
k
-centralizing derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.

In this paper, we investigate
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
. In the case where,
\theta
is an isomorphism, we prove that the range of
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
is contained into the radical of
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
. If
\theta
is also continuous, then
D
is continuous if and only if
D|_{\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})}
is continuous. In this case,
D|_{uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}}
is always continuous.
Finally, we study Posner first and second theorems for
\theta
-derivations on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.

2
Main Results

Singer and Wermer [16] showed that the range of a continuous derivation on a
commutative Banach algebra is a subset of its radical. They conjectured that the continuity requirement for the derivations can be removed.
Thomas [17] proved the conjecture. In the sequal, we investigate this conjecture for
\theta
-derivation on non-commutative Banach algebra
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\theta
be a homomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
and

D
be a
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

D
maps
\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and
\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
into
\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.

(ii)

If
\theta
is an isomorphism, then
D
maps

L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
into
\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.

Proof.

(i)
First, note that if

u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and
r\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
, then

\theta(r)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and

\displaystyle\theta(u)=u+r_{0}

for some
r_{0}\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
;
see Lemma 2.1 of [5].
So for every
k\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle kD(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(u+r_{0}).D(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\theta(u)D(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k[D(ur)-D(u)\theta(r)]=0.

This shows that
D(r)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.
We also have

\displaystyle D(uu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(u)\theta(u)+\theta(u)D(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(u)+\theta(u)D(u).

Hence
\theta(u)D(u)=0
.
It follows that

\displaystyle kD(u)=k\theta(u)D(u)=0

for all
k\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
Therefore,
D(u)\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.

(ii)
Let
\theta
be an isomorphism. Then
\theta^{-1}D
is a derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
By Theorem 2.1 of [5],

\displaystyle\theta^{-1}D(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})\subseteq\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}).

Thus

\displaystyle D(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})\subseteq\theta(\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}))\subseteq\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}),

as claimed.
∎

A mapping
T:L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}\rightarrow L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
is called
spectrally bounded
if there exists
c\geq 0
such that
r(T(m))\leq\alpha r(m)
for all
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
, where
r(m)
denotes the spectral radius of
m
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\theta
be a continuous isomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
and
D
be a
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

D|_{uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}}
is always continuous for all
u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.

(ii)

D
is continuous if and only if

D|_{\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})}
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\theta
be a continuous isomorphism on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
and
D
be a
\theta
-derivation on
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
Then
D
maps
L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
into
\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and so

\displaystyle D(um)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(u)\theta(m)+\theta(u)D(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(u)\theta(m)=D(u)\theta(u)\theta(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(u)\theta(um)

for all
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
and
u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.
Thus

\displaystyle\|D(um)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D(u)\theta(um)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|D(u)\|\|\theta(um)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|D(u)\|\|\theta\|\|um\|.

For (ii), let
D
be continuous on
\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.
Then there exist
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0

such that for every
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
,

u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
and
r\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
,

\|D(r)\|\leq c_{1}\|r\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|D(um)\|\leq c_{2}\|um\|.

Assume that
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
. Then

m=um+r,

where

r=m-um\in\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
. So by (i), we obtain

\displaystyle\|D(m)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D(um)+D(r)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|D(um)\|+\|D(r)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\|um\|+c_{1}\|r\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\|u\|\|m\|+c_{1}(\|m\|+\|u\|\|m\|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(c_{2}+2c_{1})\|m\|.

That is,
D
is continuous. The converse is clear.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\theta
be an isometrically isomorphism on

L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
and
u\in\Lambda(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
. Then

\emph{Der}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})=\emph{Der}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*},uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})\oplus\emph{Der}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*},\emph{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}))

Proof.

Let
D\in\emph{Der}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
.
We define
d:L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}\rightarrow uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}

and
T:L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}\rightarrow L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
by

d(m)=D(um)\quad\text{and}\quad T(m)=D(m-um)

for all
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
. Note that

\displaystyle D(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um+(m-um))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um)+D(m-um)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(m)+T(m)

for all
m\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
.
Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}
. Since
\theta
is an isometrically isomorphism,
\theta(u)=u
.
Thus

\displaystyle d(m_{1}m_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um_{1}um_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um_{1})\theta(um_{2})+\theta(um_{1})D(um_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um_{1})\theta(u)\theta(m_{2})+\theta(u)\theta(m_{1})D(um_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(um_{1})\theta(m_{2})+\theta(m_{1})D(um_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(m_{1})\theta(m_{2})+\theta(m_{1})d(m_{2}).

Hence
d\in\emph{Der}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*},uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})
. On the other hand,

uL_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*}\cap\hbox{ran}(L_{0}^{\infty}(w)^{*})=\{0\}.

These facts prove the result.
∎

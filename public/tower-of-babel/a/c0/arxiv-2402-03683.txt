Gambling-Based Confidence Sequences for Bounded Random Vectors
J. Jon Ryu, Gregory W. Wornell

Abstract
A confidence sequence (CS) is a sequence of confidence sets that contains a target parameter of an underlying stochastic process at any time step with high probability. This paper proposes a new approach to constructing CSs for means of bounded multivariate stochastic processes using a general gambling framework, extending the recently established coin toss framework for bounded random processes. The proposed gambling framework provides a general recipe for constructing CSs for categorical and probability-vector-valued observations, as well as for general bounded multidimensional observations through a simple reduction. This paper specifically explores the use of the mixture portfolio, akin to Cover’s universal portfolio, in the proposed framework and investigates the properties of the resulting CSs. Simulations demonstrate the tightness of these confidence sequences compared to existing methods. When applied to the sampling without-replacement setting for finite categorical data, it is shown that the resulting CS based on a universal gambling strategy is provably tighter than that of the posterior-prior ratio martingale proposed by Waudby-Smith and Ramdas.



1
Introduction

Time-uniform (or anytime-valid) confidence sets, which is often called a
confidence sequence
(CS), is a sequence of sets that contains a target parameter of the underlying stochastic process
at any time
with high probability.
The time-uniformity guarantee implies that a practitioner can make a decision by continuously acquiring new data in a sequential manner, without the concern of the so-called p-hacking problem with the classical (non-time-uniform) confidence sets
(
Ramdas et al. 2020
)
.
The notion of time-uniform guarantee dates back to
Hoeffding 1963
;
Darling & Robbins 1967
;
Lai 1976
, but the idea has regained attention only recently with the new modern applications such as A/B testing
(
Evidently 2023
)
and election audits
(
Waudby-Smith et al. 2021
)
.
As tight confidence sequences can directly lead to saving various resources in practical sequential decision making applications, there has been a surge of interest in the research community, e.g.,
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
;
Howard et al. 2021
;
Orabona & Jun 2024
)
, to name a few.

While the field is rapidly evolving with new theories and constructions for CS
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
;
Howard et al. 2021
;
Jun & Orabona 2019
;
Orabona & Jun 2024
;
Manole & Ramdas 2023
;
Shekhar & Ramdas 2023
)
, we remark that most of the development has been especially for scalar-valued processes until recently; a few exceptions include
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2020
)
for categorical observation in without replacement sampling, and
(
Whitehouse et al. 2023
;
Chugg et al. 2023
)
for unbounded vectors.

The goal of this paper is to develop a set of new methods for constructing CSs for
bounded, vector-valued
stochastic processes.
Note that there exist natural bounded vector-valued data in practice, a canonical example of which is a categorical data.
For bounded scalar-valued processes,
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
proposed a coin-toss-based framework, studying several gambling strategies and properties of the resulting CSs.
In the same framework,
Orabona & Jun 2024
considered applying the celebrated algorithm of Cover’s universal portfolio
(
Cover 1991
)
in information theory, proposed its tight outer approximations, and established analytical properties of their algorithms.
A subsequent work of
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
further explored the idea of universal gambling, arguing that the coin-toss framework can be understood as the continuous two-horse race, which admits more natural interpretation and closes the conceptual gap between CS and universal gambling.

Shekhar & Ramdas 2023
recently established near-optimality of gambling-based CSs.

Given the elegance of the coin-toss framework for bounded, scalar-valued processes and its empirical tightness, especially in the small-sample regime
(
Orabona & Jun 2024
)
, it is tempting to ask whether an analogous gambling framework exists for bounded, vector-valued processes. In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer: starting from the two-horse race formulation of
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
, we extend the existing coin-toss framework
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
)
to a general gambling framework for bounded, vector-valued processes. The key observation that leads to the multivariate extension is that the coin-toss framework essentially constructs confidence sequences for binary probability vectors, each of which happens to be characterized by a single number.
Going forward, we explore the resulting CSs from this framework when we use a universal gambling strategy such as
Cover 1991
’s universal portfolio as in
(
Orabona & Jun 2024
;
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
)
for scalar-valued processes, and study their theoretical properties and empirical performance.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we gradually build up the idea for the general gambling framework, by starting from the standard definitions, basic tools, and the coin-toss framework for scalar-valued processes.
In the next two sections, we study the resulting CSs for the categorical observation (Section
3
) and probability-valued observation (Section
4
), showing the nice properties of CSs resulting from universal gambling strategies.
In Section
4.2
, we briefly describe how the general, bounded vector processes can be handled as probability-vector valued observations.
We conclude with remarks in Section
5
.

2
General Gambling Framework

In this section, we illustrate the key idea of the paper on how to construct a confidence sequence for vector-valued observations via gambling.

2.1
Preliminaries

We start with a few definitions and notation.
We use a boldface variable, e.g.,
{\bf x}
, to denote a vector-valued observation.
We use a capital letter
X_{t}
(or
{\bf X}_{t}
) to denote a random variable (or a random vector) and a small letter
x_{t}
(or
{\bf x}_{t}
) for its realization.
We use the shorthand notation
{\bf x}_{i}^{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(x_{i},\ldots,x_{j})
and
{\bf x}^{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\bf x}_{1}^{t}
.
For an underlying stochastic process
{\bf Y}^{t}
,
let
\mathcal{F}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sigma({\bf Y}^{t})
be the sigma-field generated by
{\bf Y}^{t}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the trivial sigma-field.
We call the sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
the
canonical filtration
.

We assume that there exists an underlying, bounded vector-valued stochastic process
({\bf Y}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
such that
\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf Y}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]={\bm{\mu}}
for any
t\geq 1
for some
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Our goal is to construct
time-uniform
confidence sets, so that the target mean parameter
{\bm{\mu}}
is to be contained in the sequence at any time, with high probability.
Formally, we wish to find a sequence of set-valued functions, each of which maps the observations
{\bf y}^{t}
until then to a
convex
subset
\mathcal{C}({\bf y}^{t};\delta)\subset\mathbb{R}^{K}
, such that

\P\{{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{C}({\bf Y}^{t};\delta),\forall t\geq 1\}\geq 1-\delta,

for any given desired parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Our development follows the standard technique based on the following inequality by
Ville 1939
for supermartingales, which is a cornerstone of the most known confidence sequences.
Different methods plug in different supermartingales given their own problem settings and considerations.
A stochastic process
(W_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
is said to be
supermartingale
if
(W_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
is adapted to the canonical filtration and satisfy

\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[W_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq W_{t-1}

for every
t\geq 1
. It is said to be
martingale
, if the inequality holds with equality for any
t\geq 1
.

Theorem 1

(Ville’s inequality)
.

For a nonnegative supermartingale sequence
(W_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
with
W_{0}>0
, for any
\delta>0
,

\P\Bigl\{\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{W_{t}}{W_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\Bigr\}\leq\delta.

A general recipe to derive confidence sequences based on Ville’s inequality can be briefly summarized as follows.
First, construct a sequence
(W_{t}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m}))_{t=1}^{\infty}
as a function of
{\bf y}^{t}
and a candidate parameter
{\bf m}
at each time
t\geq 1
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}({\bf y}^{t};\delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}\frac{W_{t}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m})}{W_{0}(\varnothing;{\bf m})}<\frac{1}{\delta}\Bigr\}.

(1)

If the induced stochastic process
(W_{t}({\bf Y}^{t};{\bf m}))_{t=1}^{\infty}
whose randomness comes from
{\bf Y}^{t}
is (super)martingale for
{\bf m}={\bm{\mu}}
, then it readily follows from Ville’s inequality that
(\mathcal{C}({\bf Y}^{t};\delta))_{t=1}^{\infty}
is a confidence sequence for
{\bm{\mu}}
of coverage
1-\delta
.
1
1

1

We note in passing that the running intersection
(\cap_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{C}_{i}({\bf y}^{i};\delta))_{t=1}^{\infty}
is always tighter or equal to the original CS and also a valid CS, and thus this operation is always implicitly assumed.

Since we expect the confidence sequence to be shrinking to the singleton that contains
{\bm{\mu}}
as fast as possible while maintaining the desired coverage, the sequence
(W_{t}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m}))_{t=1}^{\infty}
needs to be carefully constructed.
Ideally, the stochastic process
(W_{t}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m}))_{t=1}^{\infty}
should diverge quickly for all
{\bf m}\neq{\bm{\mu}}
, so that any
{\bf m}\neq{\bm{\mu}}
can be excluded from
(\mathcal{C}({\bf Y}^{t};\delta))_{t=1}^{\infty}
, eventually letting it converge to the singleton
\{{\bm{\mu}}\}
.
In summary, the (super)martingale property of the process for
{\bf m}={\bm{\mu}}
guarantees the constructed sequence to be a valid confidence sequence, and
the speed of the divergence of the stochastic process for
{\bf m}\neq{\bm{\mu}}
will govern the speed of concentration to the target parameter.

2.2
Coin-Toss Framework for
[0,1]
-Valued Processes

One natural and elegant way of constructing a martingale sequence is to consider the wealth process of
gambling
.
The high-level idea is that if a gambling is
subfair
(i.e., statistically not favorable to gamblers), then a gambler’s wealth as a stochastic process must be supermartingale, that is, the gambler cannot earn money out of the gambling.
The idea of using gambling has been extensively studied by a line of recent works for bounded, scalar-valued stochastic processes
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
;
Orabona & Jun 2024
;
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
;
Shekhar & Ramdas 2023
)
.
Below, we briefly overview the existing formulation and motivate our idea for the extension to multivariate processes.

We start from the standard betting framework introduced in
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
)
for a conditionally mean-
\mu
stochastic process
(Y_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
for some
\mu\in(0,1)
.

Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
introduced the definition of a
capital process

\mathsf{K}_{t}(m)
for any
m\in(0,1)
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{t}(y^{t};m)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1+\lambda_{i}(m)\cdot(y_{i}-m)),

(2)

where
(\lambda_{t}(m))_{t=1}^{\infty}
is a
[-1/(1-m),1/m]
-valued predictable sequence, i.e.,
\lambda_{t}(m)
only depends on
Y^{t-1}
, but not on the future
Y_{t}^{\infty}
.
It is clear that the capital process is martingale for
m=\mu
, since

\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[\mathsf{K}_{t}(Y^{t};\mu)\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\mathsf{K}_{t}(Y^{t-1};\mu)\cdot(1+\lambda_{i}(m)\cdot(\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[Y_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]-\mu))=\mathsf{K}_{t}(Y^{t-1};\mu)
.
Here,
(Y_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
can be viewed as the stochastic outcomes of coin betting, and the predictable sequence
(\lambda_{t}(m))_{t=1}^{\infty}
can be understood as a causal betting strategy.
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
studied various different betting strategies and their properties in terms of the resulting confidence sequences.
We remark, however, that it is nontrivial to extend this betting framework to vector-valued processes, as also pointed out in the discussion of
Li et al. 2023
.
One critical complication in this convention is that the range of the bet
\lambda_{t}(m)
depends on the candidate parameter
m
.

More recently,
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
proposed an equivalent, yet alternative formulation of the betting framework, connecting it to the standard language of gambling in information theory
(
Cover & Thomas 2006
, Ch. 6, 16)
.
The alternative formulation is based on the (continuous) two-horse race interpretation of the capital process (
2
).
The rule of the standard horse race is as follows.
There are two horses in the race, say horse 1 and horse 2, associated with
odds

o_{1}>0
and
o_{2}>0
, respectively. These numbers indicate that if the horse
i
wins, a $1 bet on the horse results in a payoff of $
o_{i}
.
At each round
t\geq 1
, a gambler bets over the (yet unseen) outcome of the horse race with two horses. The bet is characterized by a single number
b_{t}\in[0,1]
, which is to distribute the current wealth
\mathsf{W}_{t}
of the gambler to the two horses as $
b_{t}\mathsf{W}_{t}
and $
(1-b_{t})\mathsf{W}_{t}
.
After the race, the per-round multiplicative gain of the gambler’s wealth can be written as

\displaystyle(o_{1}b_{t})^{Y_{t}}(o_{2}(1-b_{t}))^{1-Y_{t}},

(3)

where
Y_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{I}\{Z_{t}=1\}\in\{0,1\}
and
Z_{t}\in\{1,2\}
denotes the index of the winning horse.
The continuous variation of the horse race allows the outcome
Y_{t}
to be
[0,1]
-valued (hence continuous) and defines the per-round gain as

\displaystyle o_{1}b_{t}Y_{t}+o_{2}(1-b_{t})(1-Y_{t}).

(4)

Note that (
4
) is equivalent to (
3
) for discrete outcomes
Y_{t}\in\{0,1\}
.

Ryu & Bhatt 2022
noted that the capital process (
2
) can be viewed as the wealth process from the continuous two-horse race setting with odds
o_{1}=\frac{1}{m}
,
o_{2}=\frac{1}{1-m}
and a strategy
(b_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
, as the per-round gain can be written as

\frac{1}{m}b_{t}y_{t}+\frac{1}{1-m}(1-b_{t})(1-y_{t})=1+\lambda_{t}(m)(y_{t}-m),

if we define the
scaled bet

\lambda_{t}(m)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{b_{t}}{m(1-m)}-\frac{1}{1-m}\in\Bigl[-\frac{1}{1-m},\frac{1}{m}\Bigr].

Compared to (
2
), this reformulation separates out the amount of bet
b_{t}
from
m
and thus the range of the bet is simply
[0,1]
, being
m
-independent.

2.3
Extension for Multivariate Processes: Overview

We are now ready to introduce an extension of the betting framework for vector-valued processes following the convention of
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
.
To proceed, we first introduce a general abstract formulation of the gambling as a multiplicative game.
Without loss of generality, we assume a unit dollar for the initial wealth of a gambler, i.e.,
\mathsf{W}_{0}=\$1
.
We assume that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}
is given as a set of
odds vectors
.
For each round
t\geq 1
, a gambler chooses a bet
\mathbf{b}_{t}=\mathbf{b}_{t}({\bf x}^{t-1})\in\Delta^{K-1}
as a function of the previous odds vectors
{\bf x}^{t-1}\in\mathcal{M}^{t-1}
, where
we use
\Delta^{K-1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{K}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}p_{1}+\ldots+p_{K}=1\}
to denote the
(K-1)
-dimensional probability simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
After each round, the odds vector
{\bf x}_{t}\in\mathcal{M}
is revealed, and the gambler’s wealth is multiplied by
\mathbf{b}_{t}^{\intercal}{\bf x}_{t}
,
and thus the multiplicative gain after round
t
is

\mathsf{W}_{t}({\bf x}^{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\intercal}{\bf x}_{i}.

As alluded to earlier, it is easy to show that the wealth process
(\mathsf{W}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
attained by any causal strategy is a supermartingale, if the sequence of odds
({\bf X}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}

subfair
, i.e.,
\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf X}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\leq\mathds{1}_{K}
for any
t\geq 1
, where the inequality holds coordinatewise for
\mathds{1}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[1,\ldots,1]\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.

We remark that this abstract setting subsumes the two-horse race
\mathsf{HR}_{2}
and continuous two-horse race
\mathsf{CHR}_{2}
as special cases, which correspond to certain restricted sets of odds vectors
\mathcal{M}=\{o_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1},o_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{M}=\{o_{1}y\mathbf{e}_{1}+o_{2}(1-y)\mathbf{e}_{2}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}y\in[0,1]\}
, respectively.
For
K\geq 2
horses, we can consider the
K
-horse race
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
and continuous
K
-horse race
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
, where
\mathcal{M}=\{o_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,o_{K}\mathbf{e}_{K}\}
and
\mathcal{M}=\{o_{1}y_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\ldots+o_{K}y_{K}\mathbf{e}_{K}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}{\bf y}\in\Delta_{K}\}
are assumed, respectively.
The most general scenario is when the odds vector
{\bf x}_{t}
can take any value from the positive orthant, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
. In this case, the gambling becomes equivalent to the
stock market investment

(
Cover 1991
)
.

The key idea of this paper is to observe that one can consider
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
and
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
to derive CSs for
K
-categorical data and
\Delta^{K-1}
-valued stochastic processes.
Recall that
\mathsf{HR}_{2}
and
\mathsf{CHR}_{2}
can be used to derive CSs of the mean parameter for
\{0,1\}
-valued and
[0,1]
-valued stochastic processes, respectively.
The extension is natural, once a single number
Y_{t}
is mapped into the form of a binary probability vector
[Y_{t},1-Y_{t}]
lying in the probability simplex
\Delta^{1}
.
In general, for a
\Delta^{K-1}
-valued process
({\bf Y}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
such that
\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf Y}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]={\bm{\mu}}
, the gambling with odds vector sequence
({\bf X}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
, which is defined as

{\bf X}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{{\bf Y}}{{\bm{\mu}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl(\frac{Y_{t1}}{\mu_{1}},\ldots,\frac{Y_{tK}}{\mu_{K}}\Bigr),

is fair,
since
\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf X}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\mathds{1}
by construction.
Note that the odds vector (
2.3
) corresponds to
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
if
{\bf Y}_{t}\in\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{K}\}
(i.e., multinomial, or categorical observations) and
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
if
{\bf Y}_{t}\in\Delta_{K}
in general.
The embedding of the mean vector parameter in (
2.3
) is the essence of the multivariate extension.

In the rest of this paper, we build up this idea more concretely.
In Section
3
, we start by categorical observations, where our key ideas and the application of universal gambling strategies are illustrated with the simple setting of
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
. In this special case, we also show that when reduced to the sampling without replacement setting, the derived CS yields a provably tighter CS than that of
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2020
)
.
We then present its natural continuous extension for probability-vector-valued observations in Section

, where the underlying gambling becomes
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
, a special case of the stock market investment.
We also present how a general
[0,1]^{K-1}
-valued observations can be handled by a simple reduction to
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
.
All proofs are deferred to Appendix

3.1
Krichevsky–Trofimov CS

Since any causal gambling strategy results in a valid confidence sequence, a natural question is then which gambling strategy should be used to derive tight confidence sequences.
In this section, we consider applying a celebrated yet simple
mixture
betting strategy from universal gambling.
The resulting CS is easy to compute, naturally convex, and always nonvacuous even after observing one sample.
As a shorthand notation, for two vectors
\mathbf{a}
and
{\bf b}
of same dimension
K
, we denote
\mathbf{a}^{{\bf b}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{j=1}^{K}a_{j}^{b_{j}}
.

The celebrated idea of universal gambling from information theory is essentially to utilize a continuous mixture of the wealth processes of constant bettors.
Consider the cumulative wealth attained by a constant bettor
{\bf b}
from
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
(\mathbf{o})
:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}^{{\bf b}}({\bf z}^{t};\mathbf{o})}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\prod_{j=1}^{K}(o_{j}b_{j})^{\mathbb{I}\{z_{ti}=1\}}=(\mathbf{o}\odot{\bf b})^{\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})}.

Here, we define the cumulative count vector as

\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{I}\{z_{ij}=1\}\Bigr)_{j\in[K]}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{\bf z}_{i}.

While any mixture of this wealth process induces a valid wealth process,
we specifically consider the so-called Krichevsky–Trofimov (KT) strategy
(
Krichevsky & Trofimov 1981
)
,
which takes a continuous mixture of the wealth of constant bettors with respect to the the Dirichlet distribution
\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{B(\bm{\alpha})}\prod_{i=1}^{K}b_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1}
. Here,
B(\bm{\alpha})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\prod_{i=1}^{K}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}))/\Gamma(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i})
denotes the multivariate beta function and
\Gamma(\alpha)
denotes the gamma function.
That is,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};{\bf m}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Delta^{K-1}}{\mathsf{W}^{{\bf b}}({\bf z}^{t};{\bf m}^{-1})}\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\bf b}

\displaystyle={\bf m}^{-\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})}\frac{B(\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})+\bm{\alpha})}{B(\bm{\alpha})}.

(8)

The KT mixture specifically sets
\bm{\alpha}=\frac{\mathds{1}_{K}}{2}
.
This specific choice is the Jeffreys prior for multinomial distributions
(
Yang & Berger 1996
)
.

Krichevsky & Trofimov 1981
proved, in the equivalent language of universal coding, that the KT mixture wealth can achieve the optimal wealth achieved by the best constant bettor in hindsight with a minimax optimal rate in the regret in log wealth ratios under a stochastic market assumption.
It is also later shown that the optimality even holds for individual sequences
(
Xie & Barron 2000
)
.

This wealth process is easy to compute for any given
{\bf m}
, since it is only a function of the count vector
\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})
, which can be viewed as the sufficient statistics.
We can further manipulate this expression to better understand the behavior of this wealth process.
First, we define the empirical mean vector
\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{t}{\bf z}_{i}=\frac{\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})}{t}
.
Second, we define
q^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf x})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{B({\bf x}+\bm{\alpha})}{B(\bm{\alpha})}
for
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{K}
.
Then, the corresponding confidence set
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};\delta)
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};\delta)

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}^{\intercal}\log\frac{1}{{\bf m}}<\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{1}{q^{\mathsf{KT}}(t\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t})}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}D(\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}~\|~{\bf m})<\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{e^{-tH(\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}})}{q^{\mathsf{KT}}(t\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t})}\Bigr\},

where
D(\mathbf{p}~\|~\mathbf{q})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\log\frac{\mathbf{p}}{\mathbf{q}}
denotes the Kullback–Leibler divergence between
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\Delta^{K-1}
and
H(\mathbf{p})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\log\mathbf{p}
denotes the Shannon entropy for a probability vector
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^{K-1}
.
The following properties are now immediate.

Theorem 3

(KT CS)
.

Pick any
\delta\in(0,1)
.

(a)

(Convexity)
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};\delta)\subset\Delta^{K-1}
is always convex.

(b)

(Always-non-vacuous guarantee) If
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};\delta)
,

m_{j}>\frac{k_{j}({\bf z}^{t})}{t}(\delta q^{\mathsf{KT}}(\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})))^{\frac{1}{t}}

for any
j\geq 1
.
In particular, for any
j\in[K]
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf z}^{t};\delta)
is strictly bounded away from the hyperplane
\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}m_{j}=0\}
as soon as
\min_{i\in[t]}z_{ij}=1
.

(c)

(Large-sample behavior)
For
t
sufficiently large,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf Z}^{t};\delta)
asymptotically behaves as

\displaystyle\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}D(\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}~\|~{\bf m})<\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{K-1}{2t}\log t+o(1)\Bigr\},

where
o(1)
is a vanishing term as
t\to\infty
.

3.1.1
Baselines

Instead of the multi-horse-race approach,
one can construct a confidence set for categorical data by properly
aggregating
confidence intervals constructed for each coordinate, e.g., using the existing gambling techniques for scalar-valued processes.
Below, we empirically demonstrate the benefit of the proposed approach compared to such baselines.

More concretely, for each coordinate
j\in[K]
, we can construct a confidence set with level
1-\delta^{\prime}
for
\mu_{j}
, say
\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime}}^{(j)}
, based on a binary sequence
(\mathbb{I}\{z_{ij}=1\})_{i=1}^{t}
.
To construct a single confidence set for
{\bm{\mu}}
from such
K
confidence sets,
one straightforward choice is the well-known
Bonferroni correction
, which takes the intersection of
K
CSs constructed with level
1-\delta^{\prime}
with
\delta^{\prime}=\frac{\delta}{K}
for each coordinate:

\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{bonf}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcap_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{C}_{\frac{\delta}{K}}^{(j)}.

By a union bound, it readily follows that the resulting CS is a CS with level
1-\delta
.

For gambling-based (or martingale-based) CSs, there exists yet another natural way to aggregate CSs from different wealth sequences. Suppose that we are given
K
wealth sequences
(\mathsf{W}_{t}^{(j)})_{t=0}^{\infty}
for
j\in[K]
, each of which induces a level-
(1-\delta^{\prime})
CS as

\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime}}^{(j)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}\mathsf{W}_{t}^{(j)}({\bf m})\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}\Bigr\}.

We can then aggregate the
K
sets by a
mixture wealth
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{mix}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathsf{W}_{t}^{(j)}({\bf m})\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\Bigr\}.

Then, it is straightforward that the Bonferroni correction is provably not smaller than the mixture-based aggregation:

Proposition 4
.

\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{mix}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{bonf}}
.

Proof.

If
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{mix}}
, then
\mathsf{W}_{t}^{(j)}({\bf m})\leq\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{K}\mathsf{W}_{t}^{(j^{\prime})}({\bf m})\leq\frac{K}{\delta}
for every
j\in[K]
, which implies
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\sf{bonf}}
.
∎

We note that a concurrent work by
Cho et al. 2024
essentially discusses these two baselines and argues the tightness of the mixture-based aggregation, while we independently rediscovered this during the revision of our manuscript.

\displaystyle\P\Bigl\{D(\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}~\|~{\bm{\mu}})<\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{K}{t}\log(t+1)\Bigr\}\geq 1-\delta.

3.2
CS for Sampling Without Replacement

In this section, we specifically consider the sampling
without replacement
(WoR) setup, where we aim to construct a tight, vanishing confidence sequence to the mean of finite samples with sampling WoR.
The main result in this section is that if one adapts the KT CS for this setting via a general reduction to be described below, the resulting CS is always tighter than the posterior-prior martingale proposed by
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2020
.

Suppose that there exists a finite set of
N
multinomial observations
\{{\bf z}_{1},\ldots,{\bf z}_{N}\}\subset\mathcal{V}_{K}
, which are fixed and nonrandom.
The underlying population is now fixed, and the goal here is to estimate the population mean
{{\bm{\mu}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\bf z}_{i}
by sequentially drawing an observation from the population uniformly at random WoR.
Formally, the probabilistic model for the sampling WoR procedure is

{\bf Z}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}{\bf Z}^{t-1}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\{{\bf z}_{1},\ldots,{\bf z}_{N}\}\backslash\{{\bf Z}_{1},\ldots,{\bf Z}_{t-1}\}).

We note that this setup has important practical applications; for example, a tight WoR confidence sequence can lead to a fast decision in the real-world auditing for votes
(
Waudby-Smith et al. 2021
)
.

As argued in
(
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2024
)
, there exists a general reduction that maps any CS for bounded random processes to CS for sampling WoR.
For each
t\geq 1
, after having observed
{\bf Z}^{t-1}
, the mean of the remaining samples is

{\bm{\mu}}_{N,t}^{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t-1})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf Z}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}^{t-1}]=\frac{N{\bm{\mu}}-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}{\bf Z}_{i}}{N-(t-1)}.

For any
{\bf m}\in\Delta^{K-1}
, we define an analogous transform

{\bf m}_{N,t}^{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t-1})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{N{\bf m}-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}{\bf Z}_{i}}{N-(t-1)}.

To testing if the underlying population mean is
{\bf m}
,
we can plug-in
{\bf m}_{N,t+1}^{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t})
in place of
{\bf m}
to the definition of any valid confidence sequence
(\mathcal{C}({\bf z}^{t};\delta))_{t=1}^{\infty}
for bounded vectors; we defer the statement Proposition
10
to Appendix
B
.

Concretely,
we can adapt the KT CS to this setup by only plugging-in
{\bf m}_{N,t+1}^{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t})
in place of
{\bf m}
in the definition of the wealth process (
8
), which leads to

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{WoR}}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf Z}^{t};{\bf m})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{KT}}({\bf Z}^{t};{\bf m}_{N,t+1}^{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t}))

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\mathbf{K}_{t}+\bm{\alpha})}{B(\bm{\alpha})}\Bigl(\frac{N{\bf m}-\mathbf{K}_{t}}{N-t}\Bigr)^{-\mathbf{K}_{t}},

where we define
\mathbf{K}_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{k}({\bf Z}^{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{\bf Z}_{i}
as a shorthand.
Here, we allow
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
to be a hyperparameter.

Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2020
proposed a CS for categorical observations based on the process

\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{WoR}}({\bf Z}^{t};{\bf m})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{B(\mathbf{K}_{t}+{\bm{{\alpha}}})}{B({\bm{{\alpha}}})}\frac{\binom{N}{N{\bf m}}}{\binom{N-t}{N{\bf m}-\mathbf{K}_{t}}}

{\bf m}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,N}
, i.e.,
{\bf m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{K}
and
{\bf m}/N\in\Delta^{K-1}
,
where
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{K}
is again a hyperparameter.
This sequence is derived as a posterior-prior ratio (PPR) under a hypergeometric observation model, and thus we refer to the resulting CS as PPR CS.
Interestingly, KT CS is always tighter than or equal to PPR CS with probability 1 as implied by the following theorem.
Recall that a CS becomes tighter as the underlying sequence diverges faster.

Theorem 5
.

Pick any
0\leq t\leq N
and
\widetilde{{\bf z}}^{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{t}
.
For any
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,N}
, we have

\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{WoR}}^{\mathsf{KT}}(\widetilde{{\bf z}}^{t};{\bf m})\geq\mathsf{R}_{\mathsf{WoR}}(\widetilde{{\bf z}}^{t};{\bf m}).

4.1
Universal-Portfolio CS

As previously for categorical observations and the HR-based CS construction, any causal gambling strategy leads to a valid CS.
In the previous section, we demonstrated some nice properties of the KT CS, which is induced by a universal gambling strategy based on the KT mixture.
Analogously, we will also explore the idea of universal gambling for probability-vector-valued observations.
The idea of universal gambling in this continuous case is same: aiming to achieve the best wealth attained by any constant bettor, consider a mixture of the wealth of all constant bettors.
If we consider the KT mixture with
\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})
with
\bm{\alpha}=\frac{\mathds{1}_{K}}{2}
, the universal gambling strategy is Cover’s universal portfolio (UP) algorithm
(
Cover 1991
;
Cover & Ordentlich 1996
)
.
For the scalar case
(K=2)
, constructing a CS using UP was studied and its excellent empirical performance especially in a small-sample regime was demonstrated by
(
Orabona & Jun 2024
;
Ryu & Bhatt 2022
)
. Here, we demonstrate a similar property in the multidimensional case.

:
A similar experiment to Fig.
1
for i.i.d. Dirichlet observations for
K\in\{3,4\}
.

While the conceptual development is exactly the same, the difference arises in the definition of the wealth.
We start by considering the cumulative wealth attained by a constant bettor
{\bf b}
from
\mathsf{CHR}_{K}
(\mathbf{o})
for a
\Delta_{K}
-valued sequence
{\bf y}^{t}
:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}^{{\bf b}}({\bf y}^{t};\mathbf{o})}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{K}o_{j}b_{j}y_{tj}.

(12)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle{(\star)}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle{(\star)}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{(\star)}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{(\star)}$\hss}}}}{{=}}\sum_{\eta^{t}\in[K]^{t}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(o_{\eta_{i}}b_{\eta_{i}})y_{i\eta_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf z}^{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{t}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}(\mathbf{o}\odot{\bf b}\odot{\bf y}_{i})^{{\bf z}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,t}}{\bf y}^{t}[\mathbf{k}](\mathbf{o}\odot{\bf b})^{\mathbf{k}}.

(13)

Here, noting that the wealth (
12
) is a product of summations, we rewrite it as a summation of products by applying the distributive law in
(\star)
. We then further simplify the expression with the notations
\mathcal{V}_{K}
and
\mathcal{G}_{K,t}
defined in Table
1
.
For
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,t}
, we also define in (
13
)

{\bf y}^{t}[\mathbf{k}]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{{\bf z}^{t}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}^{t}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}\mathbf{k}({\bf z}^{t})=\mathbf{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{t}{\bf y}_{i}^{{\bf z}_{i}}.

Note that
{\bf z}_{i}
’s are one-hot vectors and thus
{\bf y}_{i}^{{\bf z}_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{K}y_{i,z_{ij}}
simply reads out the coordinate of
{\bf y}_{i}
where
{\bf z}_{i}
has 1.
As a sanity check, if
{\bf y}\in\mathcal{V}_{K}
, the wealth simplifies to
\mathsf{W}^{\bf b}({\bf y}^{t};\mathbf{o})=(\mathbf{o}\odot{\bf b})^{\mathbf{k}({\bf y}^{t})}
as in
\mathsf{HR}_{K}
(\mathbf{o})
.

Now, if we consider the mixture of the wealth of constant bettors
{\bf b}
with respect to a Dirichlet distribution
\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})
, the resulting wealth is the wealth achieved by Cover’s UP:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf y}^{t};\mathbf{o})}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Delta^{K-1}}{\mathsf{W}_{t}^{\bf b}({\bf y}^{t};\mathbf{o})}\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\bf b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,t}}{\bf y}^{t}[\mathbf{k}]\mathbf{o}^{\mathbf{k}}\frac{B(\mathbf{k}+\bm{\alpha})}{B(\bm{\alpha})},

since
\int_{\Delta^{K-1}}{\bf b}^{\mathbf{k}}\mathrm{Dir}({\bf b};\bm{\alpha})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\bf b}=B(\mathbf{k}+\bm{\alpha})/B(\bm{\alpha})
.
Note that the summation is over
\mathcal{G}_{K,t}
which has cardinality
\binom{t+K-1}{t}=O(t^{K-1})
, and thus the summation can be computed in
O(t^{K-1})
with memory complexity
O(t^{K-1})
at each round
t
, by maintaining and updating the statistics
{\bf y}^{t}[\mathbf{k}]
for
\mathbf{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{K,t}
by

{\bf y}^{t}[\mathbf{k}]=\sum_{j=1}^{K}y_{tj}{\bf y}^{t-1}[\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{e}_{j}]\mathbb{I}\{k_{j}\geq 1\}.

after observing
{\bf y}_{t}
at round
t\geq 1
.
We note that this dynamic programming argument was essentially discussed in
Cover & Ordentlich 1996
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf Y}^{t};\delta))_{t=1}^{\infty}
denote the resulting
(1-\delta)
-CS from the UP wealth
\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m}^{-1})
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf y}^{t};\delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Bigl\{{\bf m}\in\Delta_{K}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}{\mathsf{W}({\bf y}^{t};{\bf m}^{-1})}<\frac{1}{\delta}\Bigr\}.

We formally state the properties of UP CS as in Theorem
3
:

Theorem 7

(UP CS)
.

Pick any
\delta\in(0,1)
.

(a)

(Convexity)
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf y}^{t};\delta)\subset\Delta^{K-1}

is always convex.

(b)

(Always-non-vacuous guarantee) For any
j\in[K]
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf y}^{t};\delta)
is strictly bounded away from the hyperplane
\{{\bf m}\mathchoice{\colon}{\colon}{\mathchar 58\relax\mspace{1mu}}{\mathchar 58\relax}m_{j}=0\}
as soon as
\max_{i\in[t]}y_{ij}>0
.

(c)

\widehat{{\bm{\mu}}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathsf{UP}}({\bf Y}^{t})
with probability 1.

Orabona & Jun 2024
established a similar property to (b) for UP CS in the scalar-valued case; (c) is a generalization of

by a simple reduction described below.
Suppose that we observe
{\bf Y}_{t}=(Y_{t1},\ldots,Y_{t,K-1})\in[0,1]^{K-1}
for each
t\geq 1
, which satisfies
\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[{\bf Y}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\equiv{\bm{\mu}}
for some
{\bm{\mu}}\in[0,1]^{K-1}
.
To construct a confidence sequence for
{\bm{\mu}}
, we can consider its probability-vector representation
\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{t}=({\widetilde{Y}}_{t1},\ldots,{\widetilde{Y}}_{tK})\in\Delta^{K}
, which is defined as
as

\displaystyle{\widetilde{Y}}_{tK}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1-\frac{1}{K-1}(Y_{t1}+\ldots+Y_{t,K-1})

(16)

and
{\widetilde{Y}}_{tj}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Y_{tj}/(K-1)
for
j\in[K-1]
.
Note that
\widetilde{\bf Y}_{t}
lies on the
K
-dimensional probability simplex
\Delta^{K-1}
by construction and now its conditional mean is

\mathop{\mathsf{E}}[\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{t}\mathchoice{\mspace{1mu}\mspace{1mu}}{}{}{}\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{{\bm{\mu}}}{K-1}\\
1-\frac{\mathds{1}_{K-1}^{\intercal}{\bm{\mu}}}{K-1}\end{bmatrix}\in\Delta^{K-1}.

Now we can apply the UP CS on the sequence
(\widetilde{{\bf Y}}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
to construct a confidence sequence for
{\bm{\mu}}
.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we presented a general gambling framework for constructing confidence sequences and established some theoretical properties of CSs from universal gambling strategies.
Our simulations show that compared to the baseline CSs constructed with Bonferroni correction and averaged wealth, the multivariate gambling CSs are more conservative (i.e., of higher coverage for each time instance), provide tighter confidence sets in the small-sample regime, and the performance margin increases as the dimensionality increases.
Such a tight confidence set construction may lead to faster decisions in sequential decision-making problems, i.e.,, as we demonstrated in Section
3.2
.
We remark that one can consider applying the KT CS for multi-arm bandits as demonstrated in
(
Malloy et al. 2020
, Section 4.4)
.

While UP CS provides extremely tight confidence sets with guaranteed coverage even with small samples, the prohibitive computational complexity
O(T^{K})
for
K
-dimensional probability vectors of length
T
makes UP CS less applicable in practice for high-dimensional data.
Efficient approximation of UP has been an open problem in information theory and theoretical computer science for decades
(
van Erven et al. 2020
)
.
For example,
(
Kalai & Vempala 2002
)
proposed efficient algorithms for approximate computation of UP based on Monte Carlo Markov chain methods, and several developments have been made recently, including
(
Luo et al. 2018
;
van Erven et al. 2020
;
Mhammedi & Rakhlin 2022
;
Zimmert et al. 2022
;
Jézéquel et al. 2022
;
Tsai et al. 2024
)
.
The exploration of efficient approximate UP methods and the computational-statistical trade-off is left for future research. In the context of CSs, finding an efficient yet tight approximation of UP in the small-sample regime would be of great practical importance.

An experienced reader may wonder if the proposed gambling technique is applicable to sequential kernel regression (or kernel bandit)
(
Vakili et al. 2021
)
, or even to simpler linear bandits
(
Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011
)
. While this extension is intriguing, nontrivial technicalities arise. First, the proposed methods in this paper only apply to bounded vectors, requiring a suitable problem setting in such an extension. Second, the UP-based confidence sets are implicitly defined, needing a tight analytical outer bound or an efficient numerical procedure for implementation. We leave this direction for future work.

There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.

Lemma 8

Posterior-Prior Ratio Interpretation of PPR CS

As alluded to earlier, the PPR sequence (

Note that we can write

More precisely, if we assume
